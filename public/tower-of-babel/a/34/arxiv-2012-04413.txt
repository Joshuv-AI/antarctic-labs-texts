On spectral and orbital stability for the Klein–Gordon equation coupled to an anharmonic oscillator
Andrew Comech

Abstract
We obtain explicit characterization of spectral and orbital stability of solitary wave solutions to the 𝐔 ⁡ ( 1 ) \mathbf{U}(1) -invariant Klein–Gordon equation in one spatial dimension coupled to an anharmonic oscillator. We also give the complete analysis of the spectrum of the linearization at a solitary wave.



1
Introduction

In the present article we study the spectral stability
of the
\mathbf{U}(1)
-invariant
Klein–Gordon equation on a line with a concentrated nonlinearity:

\ddot{\psi}(x,t)=\partial_{x}^{2}\psi(x,t)-m^{2}\psi(x,t)+\delta(x)a(|\psi(0,t)|^{2})\psi(0,t),\qquad\psi(x,t)\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}},

(1.1)

where
m>0
.
Above,
a(\cdot)
is a real-valued
differentiable function,
so that the model is
\mathbf{U}(1)
-invariant.
The equation is understood in the sense of distributions.
Physically, equation (
1.1
) describes the Klein–Gordon field
coupled to a nonlinear oscillator located at
x=0
,
with

a(|\psi|^{2})\psi
being the oscillator force.
Equation (
1.1
)
admits finite energy solutions of the form

\phi(x,\omega)e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}
,
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
called
solitary waves
.
The solitary waves form a two-dimensional
solitary manifold

in the Hilbert space of finite energy states of the system.

We will
determine conditions for orbital and spectral stability of solitary waves,
formulating the results in terms of values of
a
and
a^{\prime}
evaluated
at
|\phi(0,\omega)|^{2}
;
see Theorem
3.6
below.
Then, in Theorem
4.5
,
we give the complete description of the spectrum of the
linearization at a solitary wave.

Equation (
1.1
)
was proposed to model electron’s transitions
between Bohr’s
quantum orbits
,
which is one of the fundamental problems of Quantum Mechanics.
More precisely,
(
1.1
) models the interaction of the electron
(represented by the anharmonic oscillator)
with the electromagnetic
radiation (represented by the Klein–Gordon field).
The global attraction of any finite energy solution
to the set of all
solitary waves in local energy norms,
which was established in
[
KK07
]
,
can be interpreted as the
relaxation of a perturbed electron
to the Bohr orbit, where it no longer loses the energy
via the radiation,
in the complete agreement with Bohr’s postulate
on quantum jumps.

We pursue further properties of the model (
1.1
)
since it is a convenient playground
for establishing asymptotic stability results,
similarly to

[
BKKS08
,
KKS12
]
,
allowing one to explicitly check
all the spectral properties of the linearized equation.
In the present article,
we will obtain the spectral and orbital stability results.
Moreover, we obtain the complete description of the spectrum
of the linearized equation:
these results will be needed for the subsequent proof
of asymptotic stability.
We point out that many pieces of
the spectral analysis which we develop in the present article
can be carried over essentially verbatim to models similar to (
1.1
),
such as the models where the concentrated nonlinearity
is substituted by its regularized versions,
such as the self-interaction based on the mean field
[
KK09
]
.

We mention that the local and global well-posedness
of (
1.1
) has already been proved in
[
KK07
]
.
In
[
Kop09
,
Kop10
]
,
asymptotic stability of solitary waves was obtained for discrete
Schrödinger and Klein-Gordon equations.
Let us also mention that related results
on local well-posedness,
orbital stability, and linear instability
of solitary waves in the nonlinear Klein–Gordon equation
in the external
\delta
-function potential
were obtained in
[
CGOR19
]
.

The paper is organized as follows.
The model and its solitary wave solutions are described
in Section
2
.
The linearization at a solitary wave is
carried out in Section
3
,
where the standard properties of the linearized operator
are obtained.
The detailed structure of the spectrum
of the linearized operator
is derived in
Section
4

(see Theorem
4.5
).

2
The model

We define

\varPsi(x,t)=\begin{bmatrix}\psi(x,t)\\
\pi(x,t)\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}

and rewrite (
1.1
) in the vector form:

\dot{\varPsi}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2}&0\end{bmatrix}\varPsi(t)+\delta(x)\begin{bmatrix}0\\
a(|\psi|^{2})\psi\end{bmatrix},\qquad\varPsi=\begin{bmatrix}\psi\\
\pi\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2},\qquad\varPsi\big|_{t=0}=\varPsi_{0}:=\begin{bmatrix}\psi_{0}\\
\pi_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

(2.1)

The oscillator force
a(|\psi|^{2})\psi

admits a real-valued potential,

a(|\psi|^{2})\psi=-\nabla_{\,\mathrm{Re}\,\psi,\,\mathrm{Im}\,\psi}U(\psi),\qquad\psi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2},\qquad U\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

(2.2)

where
U(\psi)=u(|\psi|^{2})
, with

u(\tau)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\tau}a(s)\,ds
.
Then (
2.1
) is formally a Hamiltonian system
with the Hamiltonian functional

{{\cal H}}(\varPsi)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\Big(|\pi|^{2}+|\partial_{x}\psi|^{2}+m^{2}|\psi|^{2}\Big)\,dx+U(\psi(0)),\qquad\varPsi=\begin{bmatrix}\psi(x)\\
\pi(x)\end{bmatrix}\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\times L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

(2.3)

which is conserved for sufficiently regular finite energy solutions.
Equation (
1.1
) is
\mathbf{U}(1)
-invariant:
if
\psi(x,t)
is a solution, then so is
e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\psi(x,t)

for any
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The Nöther theorem implies the
charge
conservation:
the value of the functional

\mathcal{Q}(\varPsi)=-\frac{1}{2}\,\mathrm{Im}\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\big(\overline{\psi}(x)\pi(x)-\overline{\pi}(x)\psi(x)\big)\,dx,\qquad\varPsi=\begin{bmatrix}\psi(x)\\
\pi(x)\end{bmatrix},

(2.4)

is conserved for solutions to (
1.1
).

The local and global existence result for the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
proved in
[
KK07
, Theorem 2.1]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that the potential is represented by

U(\psi)=u(|\psi|^{2})
with
u\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

and that
U
satisfies the inequality

U(\psi)\geq A-B|\psi|^{2}\quad\mbox{for}\quad\psi\in{\mathbb{C}},\qquad\mbox{where}\quad A\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad 0\leq B<m.

Then:

1.

For every
\varPsi_{0}\in{\mathcal{E}}:=H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\oplus L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

the Cauchy problem (
2.1
)
has a unique solution

\varPsi\in C_{b}({\mathbb{R}},{\mathcal{E}})
.

2.

The energy of
\varPsi
is conserved:
for all
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\cal H}(\varPsi(t))={\cal H}(\varPsi(0))
.

3.

There exists
\varLambda(\varPsi_{0})>0
such that

\sup\limits_{t\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|{\varPsi(t)}\|_{\mathcal{E}}\leq\varLambda(\varPsi_{0})<\infty
.

The main subject of this paper is the analysis of the spectral and orbital
stability of
“quantum stationary states”, or
solitary waves

[
GSS87
]
,
which are finite energy solutions of the form

\varPsi(x,t)=\varPhi(x)e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t},\qquad\omega\in{\mathbb{R}},\qquad\varPhi(x)=\begin{bmatrix}\phi(x)\\
-\mathrm{i}\omega\phi(x)\end{bmatrix},\qquad\phi\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}).

(2.5)

Above,

H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
is the Sobolev space of complex-valued measurable functions
satisfying
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}(|\partial_{x}\psi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\bigr)\,dx<\infty
.
By (
2.1
),
the frequency
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}

and the amplitude
\phi(x)
solve the following

nonlinear eigenvalue problem
:

\omega^{2}\phi(x)=-\partial_{x}^{2}\phi(x)+m^{2}\phi(x)-\delta(x)a(|\phi(0)|^{2})\phi(0),\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(2.6)

Definition 2.2
.

The solitary manifold
{\cal S}
is the set of all amplitudes

\varPhi(\cdot,\omega)=\begin{bmatrix}\phi(\cdot,\omega)\\
-\mathrm{i}\omega\phi(\cdot,\omega)\end{bmatrix}
,
where
\phi(x,\omega)e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega t}
is a nonzero solitary wave solution to (
1.1
),
with all possible
\omega\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

The set
{\cal S}
is given by

{\cal S}=\Bigl\{\varPhi(x,\omega)e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}=\begin{bmatrix}\phi(x,\omega)\\
-\mathrm{i}\omega\phi(x,\omega)\end{bmatrix}e^{\mathrm{i}\theta},\qquad\phi(x,\omega)=Ce^{-\varkappa|x|},\qquad\theta\in[0,2\pi]\Bigr\},

where
|\omega|<m
,

\varkappa=\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}>0
,
and
C>0
satisfies the relation

\displaystyle a(C^{2})=2\varkappa.

(2.7)

If
a^{\prime}(C^{2})\neq 0
, then
C
is locally a
C^{1}
-function of
\omega
.

Proof.

Equation (
2.6
) implies that

\partial_{x}^{2}\phi(x,\omega)=(m^{2}-\omega^{2})\phi(x,\omega)
,
x\neq 0
,
and hence
\phi(x,\omega)=C_{\pm}e^{-\varkappa_{\pm}|x|}
for
\pm x>0
, where
\varkappa_{\pm}

satisfy
\varkappa_{\pm}^{2}=m^{2}-\omega^{2}
.
Since
\phi(\cdot,\omega)\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})
,
we conclude that
\varkappa_{\pm}>0
,

|\omega|<m
, and that
\varkappa_{\pm}=\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}>0
.
Since the function

\phi(x,\omega)
is continuous at
x=0
,
one has

C_{-}=C_{+}=C
.
Thus, the solitary waves are solutions of the form

\phi(x,\omega)=Ce^{-\varkappa|x|},\qquad\varkappa=\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}>0,\qquad C\not=0.

(2.8)

For our convenience,
we assume that
C>0
,
and write solitary wave solutions in the form

\phi(x,\omega)=e^{\mathrm{i}\theta}Ce^{-\varkappa|x|},\qquad\varkappa=\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}>0,\qquad C>0,\quad\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}\mod 2\pi.

(2.9)

The algebraic equation satisfied by the constant
C>0

is obtained by collecting coefficients
at
\delta(x)
in (
2.6
):

0=\partial_{x}\phi(0+,\omega)-\partial_{x}\phi(0-,\omega)+a(|\phi(0,\omega)|^{2})\phi(0,\omega).

(2.10)

This implies that
0=-2\varkappa C+a(C^{2})C
,
hence
2\varkappa=a(C^{2})
.

We note that there is the following relation:

\displaystyle-2\omega=\frac{d}{d\omega}\varkappa^{2}=\frac{d}{d\omega}\frac{a(C^{2})^{2}}{4}=a(C^{2})a^{\prime}(C^{2})C\frac{dC}{d\omega},

(2.11)

which shows that
C
is locally a
C^{1}
-function of
\omega

as long as
a^{\prime}(C^{2})\neq 0
.
∎

Remark 3.2

Lemma 3.3

(Spectrum of
L_{\kappa}
)
.

Let
\omega\in(-m,m)
,

.
The operator

is selfadjoint and satisfies

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(L_{\kappa}(\omega))=[\varkappa^{2},+\infty),

\displaystyle\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(L_{\kappa}(\omega))=\begin{cases}\emptyset,&\kappa\leq-1/2,\\
\Lambda_{\kappa}(\omega):=-4\varkappa^{2}(\kappa+\kappa^{2}),&\kappa>-1/2,\end{cases}

where
\varkappa=\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}
.
The eigenvalue

Proof.

Solving the equation

(-\partial_{x}^{2}-\alpha(1+2\kappa)\delta(x)+\varkappa^{2})\psi=\Lambda\psi,\qquad\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}),

we find that

\displaystyle\psi(x)=Ce^{-|x|\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}-\Lambda}},\qquad x\in{\mathbb{R}},\quad C\neq 0,

while

is obtained from the jump condition at

\alpha(1+2\kappa)=2\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}-\Lambda}.

Taking into account that

,

\varkappa^{2}(1+2\kappa)^{2}=\varkappa^{2}-\Lambda

Lemma 3.4
.

For any

\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}

Recall that

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega&1&0\\
\omega&0&0&1\\
\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2}+(1+2\kappa)\alpha\delta(x)&0&0&-\omega\\
0&\partial_{x}^{2}-m^{2}+\alpha\delta(x)&\omega&0\end{bmatrix},\qquad\alpha=2\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}.

(4.1)

For
x\neq 0
,
substituting
\Psi(x)=\bm{w}e^{-\nu|x|}

with some
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}
,
\,\mathrm{Re}\,\nu\geq 0
,
into the equation
(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)-\lambda)\Psi=0
, we get

\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda&-\omega&1&0\\
\omega&-\lambda&0&1\\
\nu^{2}-m^{2}&0&-\lambda&-\omega\\
0&\nu^{2}-m^{2}&\omega&-\lambda\end{bmatrix}\bm{w}=0,\qquad\nu\in{\mathbb{C}},\quad\bm{w}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4},\quad\bm{w}\neq 0,

(4.2)

which,
via the Schur complement idea,
is equivalent to

\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{cc}-\lambda&-\omega\\
\omega&-\lambda\end{array}&\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\\
S(\omega,\lambda,\nu)&\begin{array}[]{cc}0&0\\
0&0\end{array}\end{bmatrix}\bm{w}=0,\qquad\bm{w}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4},\quad\bm{w}\neq 0,

(4.3)

with

S(\omega,\lambda,\nu)\in\mathop{\mathrm{End}}({\mathbb{C}}^{2})

the Schur complement
of the top right block
I_{2}
:

\displaystyle S(\omega,\lambda,\nu)=\begin{bmatrix}\nu^{2}-m^{2}&0\\
0&\nu^{2}-m^{2}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda&-\omega\\
\omega&-\lambda\end{bmatrix}^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}\nu^{2}-m^{2}+\omega^{2}-\lambda^{2}&-2\lambda\omega\\
2\lambda\omega&\nu^{2}-m^{2}+\omega^{2}-\lambda^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(4.4)

The condition to have nonzero solution
\bm{w}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}

to (
4.3
)
is equivalent to

\det S(\omega,\lambda,\nu)=(m^{2}-\omega^{2}+\lambda^{2}-\nu^{2})^{2}+4\lambda^{2}\omega^{2}=0.

This gives

m^{2}-\omega^{2}+\lambda^{2}-\nu^{2}=-2\mathrm{i}\lambda\omega,\qquad m^{2}-\omega^{2}+\lambda^{2}-\nu^{2}=2\mathrm{i}\lambda\omega,

m^{2}-(\omega-\mathrm{i}\lambda)^{2}=\nu^{2},\qquad m^{2}-(\omega+\mathrm{i}\lambda)^{2}=\nu^{2},

allowing one to express
\nu\in{\mathbb{C}}

in terms of
\omega
and
\lambda
:

\nu=\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)^{2}}.

We choose the cuts in the complex plane
\lambda
from the branching points to
infinity:

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{+}:=(-\mathrm{i}\infty,-\mathrm{i}(m-\omega)]\cup[\mathrm{i}(m+\omega,\mathrm{i}\infty),

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{-}:=(-\mathrm{i}\infty,-\mathrm{i}(m+\omega)]\cup[\mathrm{i}(m-\omega,\mathrm{i}\infty),

defining

\displaystyle\nu_{\pm}(\omega,\lambda)=\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)^{2}},

(4.5)

with

\,\mathrm{Re}\,\nu_{\pm}(\omega,\lambda)>0,\qquad\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\pm}.

(4.6)

The zero eigenvalue of the Schur complement
S

from (
4.4
)
corresponds to
two eigenvectors

u_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
,
depending on the choice
\nu=\nu_{\pm}
;
these eigenvectors
are given by

\begin{bmatrix}2\lambda\omega\\
-m^{2}+\omega^{2}+\nu_{\pm}^{2}-\lambda^{2}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2\lambda\omega\\
-m^{2}+\omega^{2}+(m^{2}-\omega^{2}+\lambda^{2}\mp 2\mathrm{i}\lambda\omega)-\lambda^{2}\end{bmatrix}=2\lambda\omega\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mp\mathrm{i}\end{bmatrix},

so we can use

u_{\pm}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mp\mathrm{i}\end{bmatrix}
.
By (
4.3
),
the corresponding vector
from the null space of

\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)-\lambda I

is then

\bm{w}_{\pm}=\begin{bmatrix}u_{\pm}\\
v_{\pm}\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}
,
with

v_{\pm}=-\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda&-\omega\\
\omega&-\lambda\end{bmatrix}u_{\pm}=\begin{bmatrix}\lambda\mp\mathrm{i}\omega\\
-\omega\mp\mathrm{i}\lambda\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}.

Thus, one has

\bm{w}_{\pm}=\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\pm\mathrm{i}\\
\lambda\mp\mathrm{i}\omega\\
\omega\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda\end{bmatrix}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{4}.

Therefore, an eigenfunction
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}

is of the form

\displaystyle\Psi(x,\omega,\lambda)=A\bm{w}_{+}e^{-\nu_{+}|x|}+B\bm{w}_{-}e^{-\nu_{-}|x|}=A\begin{bmatrix}1\\
\mathrm{i}\\
\lambda-\mathrm{i}\omega\\
\omega+\mathrm{i}\lambda\end{bmatrix}e^{-\nu_{+}|x|}+B\begin{bmatrix}1\\
-\mathrm{i}\\
\lambda+\mathrm{i}\omega\\
\omega-\mathrm{i}\lambda\end{bmatrix}e^{-\nu_{-}|x|},

(4.7)

with
A,\,B\in{\mathbb{C}}
not simultaneously zeros and
\,\mathrm{Re}\,\nu_{\pm}>0

(when the corresponding coefficient is nonzero).
The values of
A
and
B
are obtained from the jump
conditions at
x=0
:
substituting
\Psi
into

(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)-\lambda)\Psi=0

and collecting the terms with
\delta
-function
gives:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(-2\nu_{+}+\alpha(1+2\kappa))A+(-2\nu_{-}+\alpha(1+2\kappa))B=0,\\
(-2\nu_{+}+\alpha)\mathrm{i}A+(-2\nu_{-}+\alpha)(-\mathrm{i}B)=0.\end{cases}

(4.8)

The condition to have

A,\,B\in{\mathbb{C}}
not simultaneously zeros,

\det\begin{bmatrix}-2\nu_{+}+\alpha(1+2\kappa)&-2\nu_{-}+\alpha(1+2\kappa)\\
-2\nu_{+}+\alpha&2\nu_{-}-\alpha\end{bmatrix}=0,

takes the form

-8\nu_{+}\nu_{-}+4(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})\alpha(1+\kappa)-2\alpha^{2}(1+2\kappa)=0,

which we rewrite as

\displaystyle D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)=0

(4.9)

with

\displaystyle D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)=\alpha^{2}(1+\kappa)^{2}-2(\nu_{+}+\nu_{-})\alpha(1+\kappa)+4\nu_{+}\nu_{-}-\alpha^{2}\kappa^{2},

(4.10)

with

\alpha=2\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}

from (
3.5
)
and
\nu_{\pm}(\omega,\lambda)=\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega\pm\mathrm{i}\lambda)^{2}}

from (
4.5
).
The function
D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)
is analytic
in
\lambda

in
{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{-}\cup\mathcal{C}_{+})
.
Since there are two possible
values for each of the square roots in
the definition (
4.5
) of
\nu_{\pm}
,

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)

can be continuied analytically
through the cuts
\mathcal{C}_{-}
and
\mathcal{C}_{+}

to an analytic function
on the four-sheet cover of
{\mathbb{C}}
,
which we also denote by
D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)
.
We call
the sheet defined by conditions
(

) the

of

.

4.1
Embedded eigenvalues and virtual levels

We start by studying embedded eigenvalues and virtual levels of the operator
\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)
.
Before formulating our results,
let us mention that
a
virtual level

(also known as a
threshold resonance
)
can be defined as a limit point of an eigenvalue family
which corresponds to values of a perturbation parameter in an interval
when this limit point no longer corresponds to a square-integrable
eigenfunction.
The virtual levels usually occur
at thresholds of the essential spectrum
(the endpoints of the essential spectrum or
the points where the continuous spectrum
changes its multiplicity).
For more on the phenomenon of virtual levels,
see e.g.
[
JK79
,
JN01
,
Yaf10
,
GN20
,
EGT19
]
.

Lemma 4.1

(Embedded eigenvalues and virtual levels of

\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)
)
.

1.

There are embedded eigenvalues
\lambda=\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}
if and only if
\kappa=0
and
|\omega|\geq m/3
.

2.

For any
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\omega\in(-m,m)\setminus 0
there are no virtual levels
at the embedded thresholds
\lambda=\pm\mathrm{i}(m+|\omega|)
.

3.

There are virtual levels at
\lambda=\pm\mathrm{i}(m-|\omega|)

if and only if
\kappa\in\big[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\big)

and
\omega=\pm\mathcal{T}_{\kappa}
,
where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\kappa}=m\frac{(1+2\kappa)^{2}}{3+4\kappa};

(4.11)

in particular,
\lambda=\pm\mathrm{i}m
are virtual levels if and only if
\kappa=-1/2
and
\omega=0
.
By Part
1
,
these are genuine virtual levels
(with non-
L^{2}
eigenfunction)
if and only if
\kappa\neq 0
.

Proof.

Because of the symmetry with respect to the sign
of
\omega
,
we will assume that

0\leq\omega<m.

For Part
(
1
)
,
it suffices to
consider
\lambda=\mathrm{i}\Lambda
with
\Lambda\geq m-\omega
.
In this case

\nu_{-}=\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega+\Lambda)^{2}}\in\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}};

therefore,
in the expression for
the corresponding eigenfunction (
4.7
)
one would need to take
B=0
.
There are two cases to consider:

•

If
\kappa\neq 0
,
the system (
4.8
)
shows that one would also have
A=0
;
hence,
\lambda
can not be an eigenvalue;

•

If
\kappa=0
and
\omega\in[m/3,m)
,
there is an embedded eigenvalue
\lambda=2\omega\mathrm{i}

since the system
(
4.8
)
is satisfied for
B=0
and any
A\in{\mathbb{C}}
.
(Note that in this case
\Lambda=2\omega\geq m-\omega

and

\nu_{+}=\varkappa=\alpha/2
.)

For Part
(
2
)
,
it is enough to consider

\omega\in(0,m)
,
\lambda=\mathrm{i}(m+\omega)
.
At this value of
\lambda
, one has

\nu_{+}=0
,

\nu_{-}=\sqrt{m^{2}-(m+2\omega)^{2}}\in\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\}
.
Thus,

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\mathrm{i}(m+\omega))=\alpha^{2}(1+2\kappa)-2\nu_{-}\alpha(1+\kappa)

can not be zero since
\alpha>0
.
In the case
\omega=0
, one has
\nu_{\pm}=0
,
and then
D_{\omega,\kappa}(\mathrm{i}m)=0
if and only if
\kappa=-1/2
.

For Part
(
3
)
,
we need to consider

\omega\in(0,m)
,
\lambda=\mathrm{i}(m-\omega)
.
In this case

\nu_{-}=0,\quad\nu_{+}=\sqrt{m^{2}-(2\omega-m)^{2}}=2\sqrt{\omega(m-\omega)}>0

by (
4.6
). We need to solve:

0=D_{\omega,\kappa}(\mathrm{i}(m-\omega))=\alpha^{2}(1+2\kappa)-2\nu_{+}\alpha(1+\kappa).

The equation has no solution for
\kappa=-1
. In the case
\kappa\not=-1
, it is equivalent to

\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}\frac{1+2\kappa}{1+\kappa}=2\sqrt{\omega(m-\omega)},

which leads to

\displaystyle\omega=\mathcal{T}_{\kappa}=m\frac{(1+2\kappa)^{2}}{3+4\kappa}=m\Big(\kappa+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4(3+4\kappa)}\Big).

(4.12)

We note that
0\leq\mathcal{T}_{\kappa}<m
as long as

\kappa\in[-1/2,1/\sqrt{2})
.
∎

4.2
Discrete spectrum

Now we will study the discrete spectrum
of the operator
(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa))
.
Recall that for any
\kappa\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\omega\in(-m,m)
the operator has zero eigenvalue of of multiplicity
2
.
We start with the special cases
\kappa=0
and
\omega=0
.

Lemma 4.2

(Eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}(\omega,0)
,
\omega\neq 0
)
.

\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{A}(\omega,0))=\{0,\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}\}\quad 0<|\omega|<m,

with eigenvalues

\lambda=\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}
being simple.
The only eigenvalue of
\mathbf{A}(0,0)

is
\lambda=0
of algebraic multiplicity
4

and geometric multiplicity
2
.

Proof.

Considering
for which values
\lambda

the matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(\omega,0)-\lambda I_{4}=\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda&-\omega&1&0\\
\omega&0-\lambda&0&1\\
-L_{0}(\omega)-\omega^{2}&0&-\lambda&-\omega\\
0&-L_{0}(\omega)-\omega^{2}&\omega&-\lambda\end{bmatrix}

(4.13)

has eigenvalue zero
reduces to studying this question
for the Schur complement of the top right block
I_{2}
,

\displaystyle S=\begin{bmatrix}-L_{0}(\omega)-\omega^{2}&0\\
0&-L_{0}(\omega)-\omega^{2}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda&-\omega\\
\omega&-\lambda\end{bmatrix}^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}-L_{0}(\omega)-\lambda^{2}&-2\lambda\omega\\
2\lambda\omega&-L_{0}(\omega)-\lambda^{2}\end{bmatrix}.

(4.14)

By Lemma
3.3
,

L_{0}(\omega)
has the only eigenvalue
0
;
we conclude that
\lambda
can be obtained from the equation

\det\begin{bmatrix}-\lambda^{2}&-2\lambda\omega\\
2\lambda\omega&-\lambda^{2}\end{bmatrix}=0,

which gives
\lambda=0
and
\lambda=\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}
.
Recall that the eigenvalues
\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}

are embedded into the essential spectrum
if
|\omega|\geq m/3
.
In the case
\omega=0
,
the geometric multiplicity could be obtained from substituting

\lambda=0
,
\omega=0
, and
\kappa=0
into (
4.13
)
and taking into account that

\Lambda_{0}(\omega)=0
is a simple eigenvalue of
L_{0}(\omega)

(Lemma
3.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}(0,\kappa)
)
.

\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{A}(0,\kappa))=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0,\quad\quad\kappa\leq-1/2,\\
\pm 2m\sqrt{\kappa(1+\kappa)},\quad\kappa>-1/2.\end{array}\right.

The eigenvalues
\lambda=\pm 2m\sqrt{\kappa(1+\kappa)}
are simple
(real if
\kappa>0
and purely imaginary if
-1/2<\kappa<0
).

Proof.

In the case
\omega=0
,
one has
\nu_{\pm}=\sqrt{m^{2}+\lambda^{2}}
and
\alpha=2m
. Then

\displaystyle D_{0,\kappa}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4m^{2}(1+2\kappa)-8m(1+\kappa)\sqrt{m^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+4(m^{2}+\lambda^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 8m^{2}(1+\kappa)+4\lambda^{2}-8m(1+\kappa)\sqrt{m^{2}+\lambda^{2}}.

In the case
\kappa=-1
,
D_{0,\kappa}(\lambda)
has the only root
\lambda=0

of multiplicity
2
. For
\kappa\not=-1
,

D_{0,\kappa}(\lambda)=0
if and only if

2m^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\kappa}>0~~{\rm and}~~\Big(2m^{2}+\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\kappa}\Big)^{2}=4m^{2}(m^{2}+\lambda^{2}).

(4.15)

The second condition gives
\lambda^{2}=0
and
\frac{\lambda^{2}}{1+\kappa}=4m^{2}\kappa
.
Using the first condition of (
4.15
),
we conclude that
\kappa>-1/2
, and then
\lambda=2m\sqrt{\kappa(1+\kappa)}
.
∎

Denote

\displaystyle K_{\omega}=\frac{\sqrt{m^{2}-(2|\omega|-m)^{2}}-\varkappa}{2\varkappa-\sqrt{m^{2}-(2|\omega|-m)^{2}}}=\frac{2\sqrt{\frac{|\omega|}{m+|\omega|}}-1}{2-2\sqrt{\frac{|\omega|}{m+|\omega|}}},\qquad\omega\in(-m,m).

(4.16)

We note that

K_{\omega}\leq 0~~{\rm for}~~\omega\leq m/3,\quad{\rm and}~~K_{\omega}\geq 0~~{\rm for}~~\omega\geq m/3.

(4.17)

We note also that for
\omega\in[0,m)
the function

\omega\mapsto K_{\omega}

is the inverse to the function

\kappa\mapsto\mathcal{T}_{\kappa}

defined in (
4.11
).

Lemma 4.4
.

For
\omega\in(-m,m)
and
\kappa\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,K_{\omega})
,

\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa))=\{0\}
.

Proof.

For
\omega=0
, the statement follows from Lemma
4.3
;
because of the symmetry with respect to the sign of
\omega
,
it suffices to consider

\omega\in(0,m)
.
Due to Lemma
3.7

(
3
)
,
it suffices to prove
that

\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa))\cap\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}=\{0\},\qquad\kappa\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,K_{\omega}),\quad\omega\in(0,m).

It suffices to prove that

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)\neq 0,\quad\lambda\in\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0\},\qquad\kappa\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,K_{\omega}),\quad\omega\in(0,m),

(4.18)

with
D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)
defined in (
4.10
).
We rewrite
D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)
as

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)=(\alpha-2\nu_{+})(\alpha-2\nu_{-})+2\kappa\alpha(\alpha-\nu_{+}-\nu_{-}).

(4.19)

Note that

\lambda=\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}
are not roots of

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)\neq 0
for
\omega>0
and
\kappa\neq 0
.
Indeed, for
\lambda=2\omega\mathrm{i}
one has
\alpha-2\nu_{+}=0
, and then

D_{\omega,\kappa}(2\omega\mathrm{i})=\alpha\kappa(\alpha-2\nu_{-})=\alpha\kappa\Big(2\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-2\sqrt{m^{2}-9\omega^{2}}\Big)\neq 0;

the case
\lambda=-2\omega\mathrm{i}
is treated similarly.

For
\lambda\neq\{0,\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}\}
,
we may rewrite (
4.19
) as

D_{\omega,\kappa}(\lambda)=(\alpha-2\nu_{+})(\alpha-2\nu_{-})\Big(1+\kappa\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha-2\nu_{+}}+\frac{1}{\alpha-2\nu_{-}}\Big)\Big).

It remains to prove that for

\omega\in(0,m)
and

\kappa\in(-\infty,0)\cup(0,K_{\omega})
, the equation

1+\kappa\alpha\Big(\frac{1}{\alpha-2\nu_{+}}+\frac{1}{\alpha-2\nu_{-}}\Big)=0

has no solutions
\lambda\in\mathrm{i}{\mathbb{R}}\setminus\{0,\pm 2\omega\mathrm{i}\}
.
Denoting
\Lambda=\mathrm{i}\lambda
, we rewrite the above equation as

1+\kappa\varkappa Q(\Lambda)=0,

(4.20)

where

\displaystyle Q(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega+\Lambda)^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega-\Lambda)^{2}}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Lambda}\biggr[\frac{\varkappa+\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}-\lambda^{2}-2\omega\Lambda}}{\Lambda+2\omega}+\frac{\varkappa+\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}-\Lambda^{2}+2\omega\Lambda}}{\Lambda-2\omega}\biggr].

Due to (
4.17
) it suffices to prove that there are no solution to (
4.20
) in the following domains:

(1)

0<\omega<m/3
,
\ \kappa<T_{\kappa}(\omega)<0
,

\ 2\omega<\Lambda<m-\omega
;

(2)

0<\omega<m/3
,
\ \kappa<T_{\kappa}(\omega)<0
,
\ 0<\Lambda<2\omega
;

(3)

m/3<\omega<m
,
\ 0<\kappa<T_{\kappa}(\omega)
,
\ 0<\Lambda<m-\omega
.

(1)
Note that
Q(\Lambda)>0
for
2\omega<\Lambda<m-\omega
, and

Q^{\prime}(\Lambda)=-\sum\limits_{\pm}\frac{\Lambda\pm\omega}{\sqrt{m^{2}-(\Lambda\pm\omega)^{2}}\,\Big(\varkappa-\sqrt{m^{2}-(\Lambda\pm\omega)^{2}}\Big)^{2}}<0.

Hence,

\displaystyle\inf\limits_{(2\omega,m-\omega)}Q(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Q(m-\omega)=\frac{1}{\varkappa}+\frac{1}{\varkappa-\sqrt{m^{2}-(2\omega-m)^{2}}}=\frac{1}{\varkappa}\frac{2\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-2\sqrt{\omega(m-\omega)}}{\sqrt{m^{2}-\omega^{2}}-2\sqrt{\omega(m-\omega)}}

(4.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varkappa}\frac{2\sqrt{m+\omega}-2\sqrt{\omega}}{\sqrt{m+\omega}-2\sqrt{\omega}}=\frac{1}{\varkappa}\frac{2-2\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{m+\omega}}}{1-2\sqrt{\frac{\omega}{m+\omega}}}=\frac{1}{\varkappa}|K_{\omega}|^{-1}>0.

and

|\kappa|\varkappa Q(\Lambda)>|K_{\omega}|\varkappa Q(m-\omega)=1.

Therefore (
4.20
) has no solutions in the first domain.

(2)
Note that
0<\Lambda<\min\{2\omega,m-\omega\}
in the second and the third domains. Hence

|\Lambda(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)|<\varkappa^{2}
, and the following expansion holds:

\displaystyle\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega\pm\Lambda)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\varkappa^{2}-\Lambda(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)}=\varkappa\bigg(1-\frac{\Lambda(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)}{2\varkappa^{2}}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-3)!!\Lambda^{n}(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)^{n}}{n!2^{n}\varkappa^{2n}}\bigg).

The above leads to

\frac{\varkappa+\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega\pm\Lambda)^{2}}}{\Lambda(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)}=\frac{2\varkappa}{\Lambda(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)}-\frac{1}{2\varkappa}-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-3)!!\Lambda^{n-1}(\Lambda\pm 2\omega)^{n-1}}{n!2^{n}\varkappa^{2n-1}},

and then we derive from (
4.21
):

\displaystyle Q(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\varkappa+\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega+\Lambda)^{2}}}{\Lambda(\Lambda+2\omega)}+\frac{\varkappa+\sqrt{m^{2}-(\omega-\Lambda)^{2}}}{\Lambda(\Lambda-2\omega)}

(4.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\varkappa}-\frac{4\varkappa}{4\omega^{2}-\Lambda^{2}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!\Lambda^{n}\big((\Lambda+2\omega)^{n}+(\Lambda-2\omega)^{n}\big)}{(n+1)!2^{n+1}\varkappa^{2n+1}}<0

since each summand in
\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}
is positive for
0<\Lambda<2\omega
.
This immediately implies that (
4.20
) has no solution in the second domain.

(3)
In the third domain
m-\omega<2\omega
. Let us show that
Q(\Lambda)
decreases monotonically on
(0,m-\omega)
. Indeed, (
4.23
) implies

\displaystyle Q^{\prime}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{8\Lambda\varkappa}{(4\omega^{2}-\Lambda^{2})^{2}}-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!n\Lambda^{n-1}\big((\Lambda+2\omega)^{n}+(\Lambda-2\omega)^{n}\big)}{(n+1)!2^{n+1}\varkappa^{2n+1}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(2n-1)!!n\Lambda^{n}\big((\Lambda+2\omega)^{n-1}+(\Lambda-2\omega)^{n-1}\big)}{(n+1)!2^{n+1}\varkappa^{2n+1}}<0.

Hence, similarly to (
4.22
),

\sup\limits_{(0,m-\omega)}|Q(\Lambda)|=|Q(m-\omega)|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{m^{2}-(2\omega-m)^{2}}-\varkappa}-\frac{1}{\varkappa}=\frac{1}{\varkappa}K_{\omega}^{-1},

and

\kappa\varkappa|Q(\Lambda)|<K_{\omega}\varkappa|Q(m-\omega)|=1.

Hence (
4.20
)
has no solutions
in this domain.
∎

Theorem 4.5

(Eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa)
)
.

1.

For
\kappa=\omega^{2}/m^{2}
,
one has

\sigma_{\mathrm{p}}(\mathbf{A}(\omega,\kappa))=\{0\}

of algebraic multiplicity

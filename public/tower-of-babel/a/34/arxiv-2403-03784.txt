Abstract
Let
\Omega
be a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and
p(\cdot)
be a local Lipschitz funcion in
\Omega
with
1<p(x)<\infty
in
\Omega
.
We build up an interior
quantitative
second order Sobolev regularity for
the normalized
p(\cdot)
-Laplace equation
-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
in
\Omega
as well as the corresponding inhomogeneous equation
-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=f
in
\Omega
with
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
. In particular, given
any viscosity solution
u
to
-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
in
\Omega
, we prove the following:

(i)

in dimension
n=2
, for any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
and any
\beta\geq 0
, one has

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in L^{2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
with a
quantitative
upper bound, and moreover, the map
(x_{1},x_{2})\to|Du|^{\beta}(u_{x_{1}},-u_{x_{2}})
is quasiregular in
U
in the sense that

|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq-C\det D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{a.\,e. in $U$}.

(ii)

in dimension
n\geq 3
, for any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
with

\inf_{U}p(x)>1
and
\sup_{U}p(x)<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, one has

D^{2}u\in L^{2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
with a
quantitative
upper bound, and also with a pointwise upper bound

|D^{2}u|^{2}\leq-C\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}[u_{x_{i}x_{j}}u_{x_{j}x_{i}}-u_{x_{i}x_{i}}u_{x_{j}x_{j}}]\hskip 9.24994pt\mbox{a.\,e. in $U$}.

Here constants
\delta>0
and
C\geq 1
are independent of
u
. These extend the related results obtaind by Adamowicz-Hästö
[
1
]
when
n=2
and
\beta=0
.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a domain (open connected set) of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with
n\geq 2
. Suppose that

p:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a continuous function with
1<p(x)<\infty
for all
x\in\Omega
; for short,
p\in C^{0}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
.
In general, the function
p
is not necessary to be a constant; to emphasis this,
we write
p(\cdot)
or
p(x)
. Denote by

-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}
the normalized
p(\cdot)
-Laplacian, that is,

\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u:=\Delta u+(p(\cdot)-2)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}u}{|Du|^{2}},

where
\Delta_{\infty}
is the infinity Laplacian given by

\Delta_{\infty}u:=D^{2}uDu\cdot Du.

Observe that
\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u
is not of divergence form.
Consider the normalized
p(\cdot)
-Laplace equation

(1.1)

-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=0\quad\mbox{ in $\Omega$,}

and also, for any
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
,
the corresponding inhomogeneous equation

(1.2)

-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=f\quad\mbox{ in $\Omega$.}

Viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) and (
1.2
)
are given in
Definition
2.1
.

This paper aims to build up a
quantitative
second order Sobolev regularity
for viscosity solutions to the equations (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) under an additional assumption

p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
, that is,
p(\cdot)
is local Lipschitz in
\Omega

in the sense that

(1.3)

{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,Lip}}(p(\cdot),U)=\sup_{x,y\in U}\frac{|p(x)-p(y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty\ \mbox{for any subdomain $U\Subset\Omega$. }

Throughout the whole paper,
U\Subset\Omega
means that
U
is a bounded domain and its closure
\overline{U}\subset\Omega
.
For convenience, we write

p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
whenever

p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
and
1<p(x)<\infty
for all
x\in\Omega
.

We also use
p_{\pm}^{E}
to denote the supremum/infimum of
p(\cdot)
in the set
E\subset\Omega
,
that is,

p^{E}_{+}=\sup\{p(x)|x\in E\}\ \mbox{and}\ p^{E}_{-}=\min\{p(x)|x\in E\}.

Obviously,
1<p^{U}_{-}\leq p^{U}_{+}<\infty
for any domain
U\Subset\Omega
.
Moreover, we use
C(a,b,\cdots)
to denote a positive constant which depending only on the parameters
a,b,\cdots
, but whose value may vary from line to line.

Regards to the normalized
p(\cdot)
-Laplace equation (
1.1
), our main results are stated in Theorem 1.1 when
the dimension
n=2
and in Theorem 1.2 when the dimension
n\geq 3
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a domain,
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
and
\beta\in[0,\infty)
.
For any viscosity solution
u
to the equation (
1.1
), the following hold.

(i)

One has
|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
, and in particular,
D^{2}u\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
. Moreover,
the map
(x_{1},x_{2})\to|Du|^{\beta}(u_{x_{1}},-u_{x_{2}})
is locally quasiregular in
\Omega
, that is,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\mbox{ for any domain $U\Subset\Omega$, one has }|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq-C(p^{U}_{\pm},\beta)\det D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]\quad{\rm a.e.\ in}\ U.

(ii)

Given any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
,
one has

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}^{1,2+\delta}(U)
for some
\delta=\delta(p^{U}_{\pm},\beta)>0
with a
quantitative
upper bound

(1.5)

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{4}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(p^{U}_{\pm},\beta)\frac{1}{R}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\quad\forall\ B=B(z,R)\Subset U.

To state our main results for the
equation
(
1.1
) in dimension
n\geq 3
,
we need some extra but necessary notation and notions concerning
p(\cdot)
and
n
.
Firstly, we work with
\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{\beta}Du])
as a natural
substitute

of the determinant
-\det D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]
as considered in Theorem 1.1 (i) in dimension
n=2
,
where
\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{\beta}Du])=-\det D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]
.
We refer to
[
13
]
for some reasons to use
\sigma_{2}
in higher dimensions.

Recall that,

\sigma_{2}(M):={-}\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}[m_{ii}m_{jj}-m_{ij}m_{ji}]\quad\mbox{ for any $n\times n$ matrix $M=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}$}.

Obviously, when
n=2
,
\sigma_{2}(M)=-\det M
.
Next, for any
t>1
, set

(1.6)

\displaystyle\beta_{\star}(n,t):=-1+\frac{n-2}{{2}(n-1)}(t-1).

Note that
\beta_{\star}(2,t)\equiv-1
in dimension
n=2
.
In dimension
n\geq 3
,
\beta_{\star}(n,t)
is strictly increasing in
t
,

\mbox{$\beta_{\star}(n,t)>-1$ for all $t>1$, and $\beta_{\star}(n,t)=0$ if and only if }\ t=3+\frac{2}{n-2}.

Moreover, write

\Omega^{\ast}_{n}:=\left\{x\in\Omega|p(x)<3+\frac{2}{n-2}\right\}.

Obviously,
\Omega^{\ast}_{2}=\Omega
in dimension
n=2
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain with
n\geq 3
and
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
.
For any viscosity solution
u
to the equation (
1.1
), the following hold.

(i)

One has
D^{2}u\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega^{\ast}_{n})
. Given any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
, one has

D^{2}u\in L^{2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
for some
\delta=\delta(p^{U}_{\pm},n)>0
, and moreover,
a pointwise upper bound

(1.7)

\displaystyle|D^{2}u|^{2}\leq C(p^{U}_{\pm},n)\sigma_{2}(D^{2}u)\quad\mbox{a.\,e. in $U$}.

(ii)

Given any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
and any
\beta\in(\beta_{\star}(n,p^{U}_{+}),\infty)\cap[0,\infty)
,
one has
|Du|^{\beta}Du\in{W}^{1,{2+\delta}}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
for some
\delta=\delta(p^{U}_{\pm},n,\beta)>0
, a
quantitative
upper bound (
1.5
) with
the constant
C(p^{U}_{\pm},\beta)
therein replaced by
C(p^{U}_{\pm},n,\beta)
,
and moreover, a pointwise upper bound

|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq C(p^{U}_{\pm},n,\beta)\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{{\beta}}Du])\quad\mbox{a.\,e. in $U$}.

Our main results to the inhomogeneous normalized
p(\cdot)
-Laplace equation (

) are given in Theorem 1.3 when

n=2
and in Theorem 1.4 when
n\geq 3
.

.

Let

. Let

For any viscosity solution

to the equation (

(i)

One has

.

(ii)

Given any subdomain

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{4}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}

(1.8)

\displaystyle\leq C(p^{U}_{\pm},\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}|Du|^{2\beta+2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}(|Du|^{\beta}|f|)^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\right]\quad\forall B=B(z,R)\Subset U.

.

,

.

.

,

Before sketching the ideas to prove Theorem 1.1–Theorem 1.4, we recall several well-known studies in this direction in the literature.

In the special case that the exponent
p(\cdot)
is a constant, we denote
p(\cdot)
by
p
. For
1<p<\infty
,
the normalized
p
-Laplacian reads as

\Delta^{N}_{p}u=\Delta u{+}(p-2)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}u}{|Du|^{2}}.

The normalized
p
-Laplace
equation
-\Delta^{N}_{p}u=0
and its inhomogeneous equation

-\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
with
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
are partially motivated by the tug-of-war;
see
[
30
]
. Viscosity solutions to them in the sense of Crandall-Lions are well-defined.
Multiplying
\Delta^{N}_{p}u
by
|Du|^{p-2}
,
one gets formally the
p
-Laplacian
\Delta_{p}u
, that is,

\Delta_{p}u:={\rm div}(|Du|^{p-2}Du)=|Du|^{p-2}\Delta^{N}_{p}u.

Thanks to the divergence structure in
\Delta_{p}u
, weak solutions to
\Delta_{p}u=0
in
\Omega
are well-defined in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and, usually, called as
p
-harmonic functions in
\Omega
.
It was well-known that
u
is a

p
-harmonic function in
\Omega
if and only if
u
is a viscosity solution to the equation
\Delta_{p}u=0
in
\Omega
,
and also if and only if
u
is a viscosity solution to the equation
\Delta_{p}^{N}u=0
in
\Omega
; see
[
23
,
22
,
30
]
.
The existence and uniqueness of
p
-harmonic functions are standard.
For the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of
p
-harmonic functions, we refer to
[
38
,
37
,
26
,
15
,
16
]
.
For their second order regularity, when
{\gamma}>\beta_{\star}(n,p)
, each
p
-harmonic function
u
satisfies
D[|Du|^{\gamma}Du]\in L^{2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}

for some
\delta>0
depending on
n,p,{\gamma}
; see for example
[
13
,
33
]
.
When
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, since
\beta_{\star}(n,p)<0
, one may take
{\gamma}=0
here so to get
D^{2}u\in L^{2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
, which was originally proved in
[
29
]
via the Cordes condition.
In dimension 2,
p
-harmonic functions enjoy
C^{k,\alpha}
and
W^{k+2,q}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity
with sharp ranges of
k,\alpha,q
, and moreover,
for any
\beta>-1

the map
(x,y)\to|Du|^{\beta}(u_{x},-u_{y})
was shown to be quasiregular,
that is,

|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq-K_{p,\beta}\det D[|Du|^{\beta}Du];

see
[
21
,
3
,
9
,
27
]
.
Regards of the inhomogeneous equation
-\Delta^{N}_{p}u=f
with
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
,
we refer to
[
30
,
32
]
for the existence of viscosity solutions.
The uniqueness was established when

f
has constant sign.
For the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions we refer to Attouchi et al
[
5
]
.
When
1<p<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, viscosity solutions were shown in
[
6
]

via Cordes conditions to enjoy
W^{2,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
-regularity.

In general, assume that the exponent
p(\cdot)\in C^{0}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
is not a constant.
Multiplying
\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u
by
|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}
, one gets formally the
strong
p(x)
-Laplacian
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u
as introduced by Adamowicz-Hästö
[
1
,
2
]
, that is,

\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}\Delta u+(p(\cdot)-2)|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}\frac{\Delta_{\infty}u}{|Du|^{2}}.

Note that, under certain Sobolev regularity in
p(\cdot)
,
one would write

\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}

as

(1.9)

\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=-{\rm div}(|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}Du)+|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}\log(|Du|)Du\cdot Dp.

Since it consists of the divergence term
-{\rm div}(|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}Du)
and other term involving gradients,
weak solutions can be defined to the equation
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
.

We say

u\in W_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to the equation
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
in
\Omega
provided that

\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}Du\cdot D\phi\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|Du|^{p(\cdot)-2}\log(|Du|)Du\cdot Dp\phi\,dx=0\quad\forall\phi\in W_{0}^{1,p(\cdot)}(\Omega).

Assuming
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
in addition,
Adamowicz-Hästö
[
1
]
built up
an existence of such weak solutions their interior Hölder continuity;
and moreover, in dimension
n=2
they showed any such weak solution
u
satisfies
D^{2}u\in L^{2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
and
the map
(x_{1},x_{2})\to(u_{x_{1}},-u_{x_{2}})
is quasiregular in the sense
that

|D^{2}u|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\bigg(p(\cdot)-1+\frac{1}{p(\cdot)-1}\bigg)\det D^{2}u\quad\mbox{a. e. in}\ \Omega.

When
p(\cdot)
is log-Hölder continuous and satisfies
Dp\in L^{n}\log L^{n}
or
Dp\in L^{q(x)}
where
q\geq\max\{p,n\}+\delta
for some
\delta>0
, Zhang-Zhou
[
40
]
obtained the
C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of weak solutions to
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
. The uniqueness of weak solutions to the equation
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
remains unknown.

Under merely
p(\cdot)\in C^{0}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
, an existence of viscosity solutions to
\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
in
\Omega
was established in
[
4
]
.
Assuming
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
in addition, Siltakoski
[
35
]
proved that viscosity solutions to (
1.1
) actually coincide with weak solutions to
\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
in
\Omega
.
Siltakoski
[
36
]
also obtained
Lipschitz estimate and

C^{1,\alpha}
-regularity of viscosity solutions to
-\Delta_{p(\cdot)}^{N}u=f
whenever
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
. The uniqueness of viscosity solutions to
-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
and
-\Delta^{N}_{p(\cdot)}u=f
remains unknown.

Under the assumption
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
, in Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 we establish a
quantitative
second order regularity for viscosity solutions to the
equation
(
1.1
), and hence by Siltakoski
[
35
]
, for
weak solutions to the
equation

\Delta^{S}_{p(\cdot)}u=0
.
Moreover, in dimension
n=2
, Theorems 1.1 gives the quasiregular property of the map

(x_{1},x_{2})\to|Du|^{\beta}(u_{x_{1}},-u_{x_{2}})
for all
\beta\geq 0
and in dimension
n\geq 3
, Theorem 1.2 bounds
\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{\beta}Du])
via
|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}
from below. These extend the above results in dimension
n=2
and
\beta=0
obtained by Adamowicz-Hästö
[
1
]
.
In Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 we further build up a
quantitative
second order regularity for viscosity solutions to the inhmogenous
equation
(
1.2
).
Note that, when
f\neq 0
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4 are new.

Now we sketch the idea to prove Theorem 1.1–Theorem1.4, which is motivated by
[
13
,
33
,
36
]
. Let
1<t_{-}\leq p(x)\leq t_{+}<\infty
for all
x\in U
,
and
\beta\in(\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+}),\infty)
.
Firstly, we prove the following key pointwise inequality

(1.10)

\displaystyle|D[(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}Dv]|^{2}\leq C_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\sigma_{2}(D[(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{{\beta/2}}Dv])+\widetilde{C}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}(g-v)^{2}\quad\mbox{in $B$}

for any viscosity solution
v
to the equation

-\Delta v-(p(\cdot)-2)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{\left|Dv\right|^{2}+\varepsilon}=g-v\quad{\rm in}\ B,

where
p
is smooth in any given ball
B\Subset\Omega
.
The proof is partially motivated by
[
13
,
33
]
; see Lemma 3.1 in Section 3 for more details.

Next, given any viscosity solution
u
to the equation (
1.2
),
following
[
36
]
we approximate
u
by smooth functions
\{v^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})}
for some small
\varepsilon_{B}>0
. Here for any such
\varepsilon
,
v^{\varepsilon}

solves the Dirichlet probelm

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta v^{\varepsilon}-(p^{\varepsilon}(x)-2)\frac{\langle D^{2}v^{\varepsilon}Dv^{\varepsilon},Dv^{\varepsilon}\rangle}{|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon}=f^{\varepsilon}+u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}&\displaystyle\mbox{in $B$}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle v^{\varepsilon}=u&\displaystyle\mbox{on $\partial B$},\end{array}\right.

where
f^{\varepsilon}
and
p^{\varepsilon},u^{0,\varepsilon}
are smooth approximation of

f,p,u
. Applying (
1.10
) to
v^{\varepsilon}
, using the divergence structure of
\sigma_{2}
and sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, we are able to show that

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
with a
quantitative
upper bound

(1.13)

\displaystyle\left\|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{1}{4}B)}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{6}C_{\sharp}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R}\left\||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{1}{2}B)}+\Big\||Dv|^{\beta}f\Big\|_{L^{2}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\right]\quad\forall B=B(z,R)\Subset U

and also with a pointwise upper bound

(1.14)

\displaystyle|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq C_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{\beta}Du])+\widetilde{C}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)|Du|^{2\beta}f^{2}\quad\mbox{in $U$}.

See Theorem 4.1 for details.

Finally, applying Sobolev’s imbedding and Gehring’s lemma, we get

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W^{1,2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
and also the desired
quantitative
upper bound;
see Lemma 4.1. Theorem 1.1–Theorem 1.4 then follow from Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.1.

Remark 1.1

(i) The restriction

If the uniqueness of viscosity solutions to (

Remark 1.2
.

As referee pointed out, via using the known regularity theory of quasiregular mappings, the range
\beta\in[0,\infty)
in Theorem 1.1 can be relaxed to
\beta\in(-1,\infty)
so that

. Note that

[

[

Precisely, by Theorem 1.1 with
\beta=0
, we know that the mapping

is locally quasiregular. Given any
\beta\in(0,1)
,
denote by

the corresponding radial stretching in plane, that is,

) and the divergence of

\displaystyle\ \ +\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\frac{|D^{2}vDv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ \ -\bigg(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta\bigg)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(\Delta v)^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ -\bigg[1+(n-1+2\eta){\beta}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg]\frac{\Delta v\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ \ -\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}v)^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)}.

Step 2.

Therefore,

(3.10)

\displaystyle{\bf K}

\displaystyle\geq\eta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}

\displaystyle\ \ +\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\frac{|D^{2}vDv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ \ +\bigg\{-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)(p(x)-2)^{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +(p(x)-2)\bigg[1+(n-1+2\eta){\beta}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg]-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}}\bigg\}\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}v)^{2}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\ \ +{\bf K}_{1},

where

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{1}=

\displaystyle-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg[(g-v)^{2}+2(p(x)-2)(g-v)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{\left|Dv\right|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg]

\displaystyle+\bigg[1+(n-1+2\eta){\beta}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg]\frac{(g-v)\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}.

Step 3.

To bound
{\bf K}_{1}
from below,

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{1}=

\displaystyle\bigg[-(n-2+2\eta)(p(x)-2)+(n-1+2\eta)\beta\bigg](g-v)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+(g-v)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2}.

Recalling

|D^{2}v||Dv|^{2}\geq|\Delta_{\infty}v|.

Applying

\displaystyle(g-v)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq-|g-v||D^{2}v|\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{8}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}-C(\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2}

and, by

in

\displaystyle\bigg[-(n-2+2\eta)(p(x)-2)+(n-1+2\eta)\beta\bigg](g-v)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}v}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\geq-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)|g-v||D^{2}u|\frac{|Dv|^{4}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{8}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta,\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{6}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{3}}(g-v)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{8}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta,\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2}.

Therefore,

(3.11)

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta,\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2}.

To bound the second term in the right hand side of (

), observe that

(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}>[(n-1+2\eta)\beta+(n-1)]\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}.

If
\beta\geq 0
, for any
\eta>0

(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}>0

and
if

and hence

(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}>0.

Since the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields

\frac{|D^{2}vDv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\geq\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}v)^{2}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}},

we have

\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\frac{|D^{2}vDv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\geq\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}v)^{2}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}}.

From this, (
3.11

3.10

(3.12)

\displaystyle{\bf K}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta}{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}+{\bf K}_{2}\frac{(\Delta_{\infty}v)^{2}}{(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{2}}-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta,\eta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2},

where

\displaystyle{\bf K}_{2}=

\displaystyle\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}}

\displaystyle-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)(p(x)-2)^{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+(p(x)-2)\bigg[1+(n-1+2\eta){\beta}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}}.

Step 4.

Now we choose suitable
\eta>0
so that
{\bf K}_{2}\geq 0.

Set

Then

reads as

\displaystyle\bigg[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{1}{\tau}+\frac{n}{2}+\frac{n-2}{2}\bigg]\tau

\displaystyle-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)(p(x)-2)^{2}\frac{1}{\tau}

\displaystyle{+}(p(x)-2)\bigg[1+(n-1+2\eta)\beta\frac{1}{\tau}\bigg]

\displaystyle-\frac{n-2}{2}\tau.

Multiplying both sides by

, we have

\tau{\bf K}_{2}=\big[(n-1+2\eta)\beta\tau+\frac{n}{2}\tau^{2}\big]-(\frac{n-2}{2}+\eta)(p(x)-2)^{2}+(p(x)-2)\bigg[\tau+(n-1+2\eta)\beta\bigg].

Observe that

\frac{n}{2}\tau^{2}=(n-1)\tau^{2}+\frac{2-n}{2}\tau^{2}

and

(p(x)-2)\tau=(p(x)-2)(n-1)\tau+(p(x)-2)(2-n)\tau.

We have

\displaystyle\tau{\bf K}_{2}=

\displaystyle(n-1+2\eta)\beta\tau+(n-1+2\eta)(p(x)-2)\beta

\displaystyle+(n-1)\tau^{2}+(p(x)-2)(n-1)\tau

\displaystyle+\frac{2-n}{2}\tau^{2}+(p(x)-2)(2-n)\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}(p(x)-2)^{2}

\displaystyle-\eta(p(x)-2)^{2}.

Observe that

(n-1+2\eta)\beta\tau+(n-1+2\eta)(p(x)-2)\beta=(n-1+2\eta)\beta(p(x)-2+\tau),

(n-1)\tau^{2}+(p(x)-2)(n-1)\tau=(n-1)\tau[p(x)-2+\tau],

and

\displaystyle\frac{2-n}{2}\tau^{2}+(p(x)-2)(2-n)\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}(p(x)-2)^{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-2}{2}\big[\tau^{2}+2(p(x)-2)\tau+(p(x)-2)^{2}\big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{n-2}{2}(p(x)-2+\tau)^{2}.

One writes

\displaystyle\tau{\bf K}_{2}=

\displaystyle(p(x)-2+\tau)\big[(n-1+2\eta)\beta+(n-1)\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}(p(x)-2+\tau)\big]-\eta(p(x)-2)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n-1)(p(x)-2+\tau)\big[\frac{n-1+2\eta}{n-1}\beta+\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-2+\tau}{n-1}\big]-\eta(p(x)-2)^{2}.

Note that
t_{-}\leq p(x)\leq t_{+}
in
B
implies

(p(x)-2)^{2}\leq\max\{(t_{-}-2)^{2},(t_{+}-2)^{2}\}.

Since
\tau>1
and

, it follows that

(p(x)-2+\tau)>t_{-}-1>0.

Moreover, by
\tau>1
again one has

\displaystyle\frac{n-1+2\eta}{n-1}\beta+\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-2+\tau}{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{n-1+2\eta}{n-1}\beta+\frac{n}{2(n-1)}\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-2}{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad>\frac{n-1+2\eta}{n-1}\beta+\frac{n}{2(n-1)}-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-2}{n-1}

\displaystyle\quad=\beta+1-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-1}{n-1}+\frac{2\eta}{n-1}\beta.

Since
p(x)\leq t_{+}
,
it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{n-1+2\eta}{n-1}\beta+\tau-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{p(x)-2+\tau}{n-1}>

\displaystyle\beta+1-\frac{n-2}{2}\frac{t_{+}-1}{n-1}+\frac{2\eta}{n-1}\beta=\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})+\frac{2\eta}{n-1}\beta,

where we recall that

Thank to
\beta>\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})
, if
\beta\geq 0
, for any
\eta>0

\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})+\frac{2\eta}{n-1}\beta>0,

and hence

\displaystyle\tau{\bf K}_{2}\geq(n-1)(t_{-}-1)\big[\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})\big]-\eta\max\{(t_{-}-2)^{2},(t_{+}-2)^{2}\}.

If
\beta<0
, we choose

\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{1+\beta,\frac{n-1}{-2\beta}[\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})]\right\}.

\displaystyle\tau{\bf K}_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(t_{-}-1)\big[\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})\big]-\eta\max\{(t_{-}-2)^{2},(t_{+}-2)^{2}\}.

Finally, we choose
\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\in(0,1/2)

\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\in(0,\widetilde{\eta}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta))

\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\max\{(t_{-}-2)^{2},(t_{+}-2)^{2}\}<\frac{1}{2}(n-1)(t_{-}-1)\big[\beta-\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})\big].

Consequently, for any
\eta\in(0,\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta))
, we have
\tau{\bf K}_{2}\geq 0

and hence

Step 5.

Taking
\eta=\frac{1}{2}\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)
in (

\displaystyle{\bf K}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)}{4}\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}|D^{2}v|^{2}-C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\frac{|Dv|^{2}}{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}(g-v)^{2}.

Multiplying both sides by

considering the definition of

given in (

\displaystyle\frac{4[n-1+{\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)}]}{\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)}\sigma_{2}(D[(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{{\beta/2}}Dv])

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{4}{\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)}(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}\frac{|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon}{|Dv|^{2}}{\bf K}

\displaystyle\quad\geq(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}|D^{2}v|^{2}-\frac{4}{\eta_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)}C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)(|Dv|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}(g-v)^{2}.

Obviously (

determind completely by

,

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain,

and

.
Let

be any viscosity solution to the equation (

Given any

with

in

then one has

with a
quantitative
upper bound

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{1}{2}B)}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{6}C_{\sharp}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R}\left\||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}\right\|_{L^{2}(\frac{3}{4}B)}+\Big\||Dv|^{\beta}f\Big\|_{L^{2}(\frac{3}{4}B)}\right]\quad\forall B=B(z,R)\Subset U

Here

\{f^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})}
,
\{p^{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})}
and
\{u^{0,\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})}
as standard smooth modifications
of

and

respectively, that is,

\mbox{$f^{\varepsilon}=f\ast\eta_{\varepsilon},\,p^{\varepsilon}=p\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}$ and $u^{0,\varepsilon}=u\ast\eta_{\varepsilon}\quad\forall\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})$},

where
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B(0,1))
is the standard smooth mollifier and
\eta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\eta(z/\varepsilon)
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})
.
Observe that

uniformly in
\overline{B}

For

,
||Dp^{\varepsilon}||_{L^{\infty}(B)}\leq||Dp||_{L^{\infty}((1+\varepsilon_{B})B)}

For

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta w-(p^{\varepsilon}(\cdot)-2)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}w}{|Dw|^{2}+\varepsilon}=f^{\varepsilon}+u^{0,\varepsilon}-w&\displaystyle\mbox{in $B$}\\[8.53581pt]

Note that

is a second order partial differnetial operator satisfying the uniformly elliptic condition.

equations
,
for each

and integrating, one has

(4.8)

\displaystyle\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}]|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sigma_{2}(D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{{\beta/2}}Dv])\,\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\widetilde{C}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}(f+u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon})^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

Since

D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}]\to D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]
weakly in
L^{2}({\frac{1}{2}}B)
one has

\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\psi\,dx\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}]|^{2}\psi\,dx.

Noting that
(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}\to|Du|^{\beta}Du

in
L^{2}({\frac{3}{4}}B)
and weakly in
W^{1,2}({\frac{1}{2}}B)
,
by (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) one has

\displaystyle\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sigma_{2}(D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{{\beta/2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}])\phi\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[}(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}v^{\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}-c_{i}][(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}v^{\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}]_{x_{j}}\phi_{x_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[}(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}v^{\varepsilon}_{x_{i}}-c_{i}][(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}v^{\varepsilon}_{x_{j}}]_{x_{i}}\phi_{x_{j}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\to\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[}|Du|^{\beta}u_{x_{i}}-c_{i}][|Du|^{\beta}u_{x_{j}}]_{x_{j}}\phi_{x_{i}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}[}|Du|^{\beta}u_{x_{i}}-c_{i}][|Du|^{\beta}u_{x_{j}}]_{x_{i}}\phi_{x_{j}}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{\beta}Du])\,\phi\,dx.

Moreover, noting
u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\to u-u=0
in
C^{0}({\frac{1}{2}}\overline{B})
, by
\beta\geq 0
we have

(4.9)

\displaystyle\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}(f^{\varepsilon}+u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon})^{2}(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}\phi\,dx\to\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}f^{2}|Du|^{\beta}\phi\,dx.

Combinging these, letting
\varepsilon\to 0
in (
4.8
), one has

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D[|Dv|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{{\beta}}Du])\phi^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\widetilde{C}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}(|Du|^{2\beta}f^{2}\phi^{2}\,dx\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\frac{1}{2}}B).

This implies that

|D[|Dv|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\leq C_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\sigma_{2}(D[|Du|^{{\beta}}Du])+\widetilde{C}_{\star}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)(|Du|^{2\beta}f^{2}\ \mbox{a.\,e. in}\ \frac{1}{4}B.

Taking over all balls
B\Subset U
, we obtain (
4.2
) as desired.
∎

Next we use the Gehring lemma to improve the integrability of
D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a domain,
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega;(1,\infty))
and
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
.
Let
u
be any viscosity solution to the equation (
1.2
).

Given any
t_{\pm}\in(1,\infty)
and any subdomain
U\Subset\Omega
with
t_{-}\leq p(x)\leq t_{+}
in
U
, if
\beta\in(\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+}),\infty)\cap[0,\infty)
,
then

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W^{1,2+\delta}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)

for some constant
\delta=\delta(t_{\pm}n,\beta)>0
with

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{4}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}(|Du|^{\beta}|f|)^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\right]\quad\forall B=B(z,R)\Subset U.

Note that
\delta(t_{\pm},n,\beta)>0
and
C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\geq 1
are constants depending only on
t_{\pm},n
and
\beta
.

Proof.

Note that
Du\in L^{\infty}(U)
. By Theorem 4.1,

|Du|^{\beta}Du\in W^{1,2}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(U)
.
For any ball
B=B(z,R)\Subset U
, (
4.1
) implies

(4.12)

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{4}}B}|D[|Du|^{\beta}Du]|^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle 2^{6}C_{\sharp}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R^{2}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|^{2}\,dx+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|Du|^{2\beta}f^{2}\,dx\right],

where
\vec{c}_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}=\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|Du|^{\beta}Du\,dx
.
Applying the Sobolev-Poincaré
inequality, we have

\frac{1}{R^{2}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}|^{2}\,dx\leq C(n)\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{4}}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2}\,dx\leq

\displaystyle C_{\sharp}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)C(n)\left[\Big(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}\,dx\Big)^{\frac{n+2}{n}}+\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|Du|^{2\beta}f^{2}\,dx\right].

Note that
|Du|^{\beta}|f|\in L^{\infty}(U)
and hence
|Du|^{\beta}|f|\in L^{s}(U)
for all
s>1
.
Applying Lemma
2.5
with
r=\frac{n+2}{n}
,
F=|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}
and
G=[|Du|^{\beta}|f|]^{\frac{2n}{n+2}}
, there exists
\delta=\delta(t_{\pm},n,\beta)>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{4}}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}(|Du|^{\beta}|f|)^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\right].

for any ball
B=B(z,R)\Subset U
.

Applying (
1.8
) again we have

\displaystyle\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\frac{1}{4}B}|D(|Du|^{\beta}Du)|^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\left[\frac{1}{R}\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{3}{4}}B}||Du|^{\beta}Du-\vec{c}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}{+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{\frac{3}{4}B}(|Du|^{\beta}f)^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}}+\left(\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$}}\kern-4.86108pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$}}\kern-3.43057pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.908pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$}}\kern-2.76045pt}}\!\int_{{\frac{1}{2}}B}(|Du|^{\beta}|f|)^{2+\delta}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2+\delta}}\right]

for any ball
B=B(z,R)\Subset U
, which together with
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality yields (
4.11
) as desired.
∎

We are ready to prove Theorem 1.1-theorem 1.4.

Proofs of Theorems 1.1 and 1.3.

In dimension
n=2
, one has
\beta_{\star}(2,t_{+})=-1
for all
t_{+}>1
.
Hence,

\beta\geq 0
always implies that
\beta>\beta_{\star}(2,t_{+})
. Take
t_{\pm}=p^{U}_{\pm}
.
Applying Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.1 with
f=0
, we get the desired Theorem 1.1.
Applying Theorem 4.1 and Lemma 4.1 with general
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, we get the desired Theorem 1.3.
∎

Proofs of Theorems 1.2 and 1.4.

Take
t_{\pm}=p^{U}_{\pm}
.
Applying Lemma 4.1 with
f=0
, we get the desired Theorem 1.2 (ii) directly.
Applying Lemma 4.1 with general
f\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, we get the desired Theorem 1.4 (ii) directly.

To see Theorem 1.2 (i), note that for any
U\Subset\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
, one has

1<p^{U}_{-}\leq p^{U}_{+}<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
. Thus

\beta_{\star}(n,p^{U}_{+})<\beta_{\star}(n,3+\frac{2}{n-2})=-1+\frac{n-2}{n-1}(3+\frac{2}{n-2}-1)=0.

Applying Theorem 1.2 (ii) to any
U\Subset\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
, taking
\beta=0
we get Theorem 1.2 (i).
Applying Theorem 1.4 (ii) to any
U\Subset\Omega_{n}^{\ast}
, taking
\beta=0
we get Theorem 1.4 (i).
∎

We end this section by the following remark.

Remark 4.1
.

(i)
In Theorem 4.1, the adiditonal assumption
\beta\geq 0
is used to guarantee the convergnece (
4.9
).
If
p^{U}_{+}\geq 3+\frac{2}{n-2}
we must have
t_{+}\geq 3+\frac{2}{n-2}
and hence

\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})\geq\beta_{\star}(n,3+\frac{2}{n-2})=0.

So
\beta>\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})
implies
\beta\geq 0
.
If
p^{U}_{-}<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, it may happen that
p^{U}_{+}\leq t_{+}<3+\frac{2}{n-2}
, which implies

\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})<\beta_{\star}(n,3+\frac{2}{n-2})=0.

When
\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})<\beta<0,

it is not clear whether the convergence (
4.9
) holds or not.
Indeed, in the case
f=0
and hence
f^{\varepsilon}=0
,
(
4.9
) becomes

(4.13)

\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta}(u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon})^{2}\,dx\to 0.

Since the convergence rate of
u^{0,\varepsilon}-v^{\varepsilon}\to 0
is not clear, we
do not know whether
(
4.13
) holds or not.
In the case
f\neq 0
, say
f=1
, to get
(
4.9
), we need apriori that
|Du|^{2\beta}\in L^{1}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}
, which we do not know yet.

(ii) Recall that the uniquess of viscosity solutions to
equations
(
1.1
) and (
1.2
) remains open.
This is the reason that, in the proof of Theorem 4.1, we use approximation equations (
4
) so that
their smooth solutions
v^{\varepsilon}
approximate the given solution
u
to the equation (
1.2
) and, when
f=0
, to the
equation
(
1.1
).

In the case
f=0
, suppose that the uniquess of viscosity solutions to equations (
1.1
) holds, we
may
remove the additional assumption
\beta\geq 0
in Theorem 4.1 and hence Lemma 4.1 via a different approximation argument as below.
For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{B})
, instead of (
4
), let
w^{\varepsilon}
be the smooth solution to the equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle-\Delta w^{\varepsilon}-(p^{\varepsilon}(x)-2)\frac{\Delta_{\infty}w^{\varepsilon}}{|Dw^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon}=0&\displaystyle\mbox{in $B$}\\[8.53581pt]
\displaystyle w^{\varepsilon}=u&\displaystyle\mbox{on $\partial B$}.\end{array}\right.

The uniquess for viscosity solutions to
equations
(
1.1
) will guarantee that
w^{\varepsilon}\to u
in
C^{0}(B)
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
For any
\beta>\beta_{\star}(n,t_{+})
, which maybe less than
0
,
instead of (
4.7
), we will get

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\frac{1}{2}B}|D[(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\frac{\beta}{2}}Dv^{\varepsilon}]|^{2}\,dx\leq 2^{6}C_{\sharp}(t_{\pm},n,\beta)\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{\frac{3}{4}B}|(|Dv^{\varepsilon}|^{2}+\varepsilon)^{\beta/2}Dv^{\varepsilon}-\vec{c}|^{2}\,dx

for any
\vec{c}\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and repeating above argument,
we will get (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) with
f=0
therein. Repeating the proof of
Lemma 4.1 we also have (
4.11
) with
f=0
.

(iii) The assumption
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
in Theorem 4.1 and hence in Lemma 4.1 is used to get the uniform

C^{1,\alpha}
-regualrity of
\{v^{\varepsilon}\}
so to get the convergence of
Dv^{\varepsilon}\to Du
locally uniformly.
Moreover,
p(\cdot)\in C^{0,1}(\Omega)
also implies that
Du\in L^{\infty}_{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}(\Omega)
.

Under merely
p(\cdot)\in C^{0}(\Omega)
, which is the most natural assumption to define viscosity solutions,
it is not clear whether the above argument can be adaped so to get the same conclusions as Theorem 4.1.

Data structures for connectivity and cut queries
Zeev Nutov

Abstract
Let κ ⁡ ( s , t ) \kappa(s,t) denote the maximum number of internally disjoint s ​ t st -paths in an undirected graph G G . We consider designing a compact data structure that answers k k -bounded node connectivity queries: given s , t ∈ V s,t\in V return min ⁡ { κ ⁡ ( s , t ) , k + 1 } \min\{\kappa(s,t),k+1\} . A trivial data structure has space O ⁡ ( n 2 ) O(n^{2}) and query time O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) . A data structure of Hsu & Lu [ 8 ] has space O ⁡ ( k 2 ​ n ) O(k^{2}n) and query time O ⁡ ( log ⁡ k ) O(\log k) , and a randomized data structure of Iszak & Nutov [ 9 ] has space O ⁡ ( k ​ n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(kn\log n) and query time O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ n ) O(k\log n) . We extend the Hsu-Lu data structure to answer queries in time O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) . In parallel to our work, Pettie, Saranurak & Yin [ 19 ] extended the Iszak-Nutov [ 9 ] data structure to answer queries in time O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) . Our data structure is more compact than that of [ 19 ] for k < log ⁡ n k<\log n , and our query time is always better. We then augment our data structure by a list of cuts that enables to return a pointer to a minimum s ​ t st -cut in the list (or to a cut of size ≤ k \leq k ) whenever κ ⁡ ( s , t ) ≤ k \kappa(s,t)\leq k . A trivial data structure has cut list size n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) / 2 n(n-1)/2 , and cut query time O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) , while the Pettie, Saranurak & Yin [ 19 ] data structure has list size O ⁡ ( k ​ n ​ log ⁡ n ) O(kn\log n) and cut query time O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) O(\log n) . We show that O ⁡ ( k ​ n ) O(kn) cuts suffice to return an s ​ t st -cut of size ≤ k \leq k , and a list of O ⁡ ( k 2 ​ n ) O(k^{2}n) cuts contains a minimum s ​ t st -cut for every s , t ∈ V s,t\in V . In the case when S S is a node subset with κ ⁡ ( s , t ) ≥ k \kappa(s,t)\geq k for all s , t ∈ V s,t\in V , we show that 3 ​ | S | 3|S| cuts suffice, and that these cuts can be partitioned into O ⁡ ( k ) O(k) laminar families. Thus using space O ⁡ ( k ​ n ) O(kn) we can answers each connectivity and cut queries for s , t ∈ S s,t\in S in O ⁡ ( 1 ) O(1) time, generalizing and substantially simplifying the proof of a result of Pettie and Yin [ 20 ] for the case | S | = V |S|=V .

We start by describing a simple data structure for answering edge connectivity queries.

b\in\partial\mathbb{A}
, or (see Fig.

(a)):

a,b\in A\cap B
,
A^{*}\cap B^{*}=\emptyset
, and both

are non-empty.

Proof.

Assume that
a\notin\partial\mathbb{B}
and
b\notin\partial\mathbb{A}
. We will show that then the case in Fig.
1
(a) holds.

Suppose that
a\in A\cap B
; the analysis of the case
b\in A\cap B
is similar.
Then
A^{*}\cap B^{*}=\emptyset
;
otherwise,
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
cross and (by property 2 in Lemma

)
we get
\mathbb{A}\cap\mathbb{B}\in{\cal F}
, contradicting the minimality of
\mathbb{A}
.
Furthermore, if

\frac{|S|-k}{2}<|A|\leq|A^{*}|=|\partial\mathbb{B}\cap A^{*}|\leq k
, contradicting that
|S|\geq 3k
.
By a similar argument,
A\cap B^{*}\neq\emptyset
.
If
a\in A\cap B
and

(Fig.

(b)), then
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
co-cross
(by property 3 in Lemma

) and thus
\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{A}\in{\cal F}
;
this contradicts the minimality of
\mathbb{B}
.

If none of
a,b
is in
A\cap B
, then (see Fig.

(c))
a\in A\cap B^{*}
and

.
Consequently,
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
-co-cross, and thus (by property 3 in Lemma

)

\mathbb{A}\setminus\mathbb{B},\mathbb{B}\setminus\mathbb{A}\in{\cal F}
, contradicting the minimality of
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}
.

Thus the only possible case is the one in Fig.

From Lemma

, by a proof identical to that as in Lemma

we get the following.

Corollary 21
.

The family of large bisets in
{\cal C}
can be partitioned in polynomial time
into at most
2k+1
parts such that
any two bisets
\mathbb{A}\in{\cal C}_{a}
and
\mathbb{B}\in{\cal C}_{b}
that belong to the same part

are either laminar, or have the following property:

a,b\in A\cap B
and

.

Thus the following lemma finishes the proof of Lemma
19
, and also of Theorem
3
.

Lemma 22
.

Let
{\cal P}
be one of the
2k+1
parts as in Corollary
21
;
in particular, if
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in{\cal P}
are not laminar then
A\cap B\neq\emptyset
and
A^{*}\cap B^{*}=\emptyset
.
Then
{\cal P}
can be partitioned in polynomial time into at most
3
laminar families.

Proof.

Let
{\cal M}
be the family of maximal members in
{\cal P}
. We will show later that
the set family
{\cal H}=\{A^{*}:\mathbb{A}\in{\cal M}\}
has a hitting set
U
of size
|U|\leq 3
. Now note that:

•

For every
v\in S
the family
{\cal P}_{v}=\{\mathbb{A}\in{\cal P}:v\in A^{*}\}
is laminar, since

v\in A^{*}\cap B^{*}
for any
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in{\cal P}_{v}
,
while
A^{*}\cap B^{*}=\emptyset
for any non-laminar
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B}\in{\cal P}
.

•

Since
U
is a hitting set of
{\cal H}
, for any
\mathbb{A}\in{\cal P}
there is
v\in U\cap A^{*}
, and then
\mathbb{A}\in{\cal P}_{v}
.

Summarizing, each one of the families
{\cal P}_{v}
is laminar and
\cup_{v\in U}{\cal P}_{v}={\cal P}
.
By removing bisets that appear more than once, we get a partition of
{\cal P}
into
|U|\leq 3
laminar families.

It remains to show that
{\cal H}
has a hitting set of size
\leq 3
.
A
fractional hitting set
of
{\cal H}
is a function
h:S\rightarrow[0,1]
such that

h(A)=\sum_{v\in A}h(v)\geq 1
for all
A\in{\cal H}
.
For
v\in S
let
{\cal H}_{v}
be the family of sets in
{\cal H}
that contain
v
, and let
{\cal M}_{v}=\{\mathbb{A}\in{\cal M}:A^{*}\in{\cal H}_{v}\}
.
Note that:

•

h(v)=\frac{2}{|S|-k+1}
for all
v\in S
is a fractional hitting set of
{\cal H}
and
h(S)=\frac{2|S|}{|S|-k+1}<3
.

•

No two bisets in
{\cal M}_{v}
intersect and
|{\cal H}_{v}|\leq|{\cal M}_{v}|
.
This implies
|{\cal H}_{v}|\leq|{\cal M}_{v}|\leq\frac{2|S|}{|S|-k+1}<3
, so
|{\cal H}_{v}|\leq 2
for all
v\in S
.

Since
|{\cal H}_{v}|\leq 2
for all
v\in S
, computing a minimum hitting set of
{\cal H}

reduces to the minimum Edge-Cover problem, and
{\cal H}
has a hitting set
U
of size
|U|\leq\frac{4}{3}\cdot h(S)

(
\frac{4}{3}
is the integrality gap of the Edge-Cover problem).
Since
\frac{4}{3}\cdot h(S)<\frac{4}{3}\cdot 3
,
{\cal H}
has a hitting set
U
of size
|U|\leq 3
.
∎

This concludes the proof of Lemma
19
and thus also the proof of Theorem
3
is complete.

We can slightly improve the bound on the cut list sizes by a direct short proof, as follows.

Lemma 23
.

There exists a list of at most
(2k+1)n

st
-cut

\leq k

Proof.

The second part of the lemma easily follows from the first part.
Note that the union of list as in the first part for every

includes a minimum

-cut for any

.
The size of this list is
\sum_{k^{\prime}=1}^{k}(2k^{\prime}+1)n=k(k+2)n
.

We now prove the first part of the lemma. For a biset
\mathbb{A}
let
\psi(\mathbb{A})=|\partial\mathbb{A}|+|\delta(\mathbb{A})|
.
Here we will say that

is an

It is known that if

5
Decomposition of node connectivity into element connectivity

Recall that given a set
S\subseteq V
of terminals,
the element connectivity between
s,t\in S
is the maximum number of
pairwise element disjoint
st
-paths, where elements are the edges and the nodes in
V\setminus S
.
Let
\kappa^{S}_{G}(s,t)
denote the
st
-element connectivity in
G
.
By Menger’s Theorem,
\kappa^{S}_{G}(s,t)
equals the minimum size
|C|
of a set

C
of elements with
C\cap S=\emptyset
such
G\setminus C
has not
st
-path.
It is easy to see that
\kappa_{G}^{S}(s,t)\geq\kappa_{G}(s,t)
,
and that an equality holds iff there exists a minimum
st
-cut
C
with
C\cap S=\emptyset
.
We thus will consider the following problem: given a family
{\cal C}
of subsets of
V
,
find a “small” family
{\cal S}
of subsets of
V
such that for every
s,t\in V
and
C\in{\cal C}
with
s,t\notin C
,
there is
S\in{\cal S}
with
s,t\in S
and
C\cap S=\emptyset
; following
[
2
]
, we will call such a family
{\cal C}
-resilient
.
The objective can be also to minimize
\sum_{S\in{\cal S}}|S|
.
Chuzhoy and Khanna
[
2
]
showed that if
\{C\subset V:|C|\leq k\}
is the family of all subsets of size
\leq k
,
then there exists a
{\cal C}
-resilient family
{\cal S}
of size
|{\cal S}|=O(k^{3}\ln n)
. They also gave a randomized polynomial time algorithm for finding such
{\cal S}
.
The number of subsets of size
\leq k
is
\approx n^{k}
, while
the relevant family in our case – as in Lemma
23
, has a much smaller size
|{\cal C}|\leq k(k+2)n
.
We will consider the case of an arbitrary family
{\cal C}\subseteq\{C\subseteq V:|C|\leq k\}
, and prove the following.

Lemma 24
.

Let
{\cal C}
be a family of sets of size at most
k
each on a groundset
V
of size
n
.
Then there exists a
{\cal C}
-resilient family
{\cal S}
of
O(k^{2}\ln(n|{\cal C}|))
subsets of
V
of size
r=\frac{n-k}{k+1}
each.
Furthermore, assigning to each set in
{\cal A}=\{S\subseteq V:|S|=r\}
probability
\Delta=1/{n-k-2\choose r-2}

and applying randomized rounding
4\ln(n|{\cal C}|)
times gives such
{\cal S}
w.h.p.

Proof.

If
n\leq 3k+1
, then the subsets of
V
of size
2
is a family as required of size
\frac{3k(3k+1)}{2}
,
so assume that
n\geq 3k+2
.

Let
{\cal A}=\{S\subseteq V:|S|=r\}
and
{\cal B}=\{(\{s,t\},C):s,t\in V,C\in{\cal C}\}
.
Define a bipartite graph with sides
{\cal A},{\cal B}
by connecting
S\in{\cal A}

to
(\{s,t\},C)\in{\cal B}
if
s,t\in S
and
C\cap S=\emptyset
;
in this case we will say that
S
covers
(\{s,t\},C)
.
This defines an instance of the
Set Cover
problem,
where
{\cal A}
are the sets and
{\cal B}
are the elements.
The lemma says that there exists a cover
{\cal S}\subseteq{\cal A}
of
{\cal B}
that has size
|{\cal S}|=O(k^{2}\log(n|{\cal C}|))
.

A
fractional cover
of
{\cal B}
is a function
h:{\cal A}\longrightarrow[0,1]
such that

\sum\{h(S):S\in{\cal A}\mbox{ covers }(\{s,t\},C)\}\geq 1\ \ \ \ \ \forall(\{s,t\},C)\in{\cal B}\ .

The
value
of a fractional cover
h
is
\sum_{S\in{\cal A}}h(S)
.
It is known that if there is a fractional cover of value
\tau
, then there is a cover of size
\tau(1+\ln|{\cal B}|)
.
We have
|{\cal B}|=\frac{n(n-1)}{2}|{\cal C}|
, hence
\ln|{\cal B}|\leq 2\ln(n|{\cal C}|)-\ln 2
and
\lceil 2\ln|{\cal B}|\rceil\leq 4\ln(n|{\cal C}|)
.

Our next goal is to show that there is a fractional cover of value
O(k^{2})
.
We have
|{\cal A}|={n\choose r}
.
The number of sets in
{\cal A}
that cover a given member
(\{s,t\},C)\in{\cal B}

is
\Delta={n-k-2\choose r-2}
,
which is the number of choices of a set
S\setminus\{s,t\}
of size
r-2
from
the set
V\setminus(C\cup\{s,t\})
of size
n-k-2
.
Defining
h(S)=1/\Delta
for all
S\in{\cal A}
gives a fractional cover of value
|{\cal A}|/\Delta
. Denote
m=n-k
. Then:

\frac{|{\cal A}|}{\Delta}=\frac{{n\choose r}}{{m-2\choose r-2}}=\frac{m(m-1)}{r(r-1)}\cdot\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}\cdot\frac{(m-r)!}{m!}\leq\frac{m^{2}}{(r-1)^{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{n-i+1}{m-i+1}\ .

Note that for
1\leq i\leq r
we have

\frac{n-i+1}{m-i+1}=1+\frac{n-m}{m-i+1}\leq 1+\frac{k}{n-k-r}
.
Let us choose
r
such that
\frac{k}{n-k-r}=\frac{1}{r}
, so
r=\frac{n-k}{k+1}
;
assume that
r
is an integer, as adjustment to floors and ceilings
only affects by a small amount the constant hidden in the
O(\cdot)
term.
Since
{(1+1/r)}^{r}\leq e
we obtain

\prod_{i=1}^{r}\frac{n-i+1}{m-i+1}\leq{\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)}^{r}\leq e\ .

Since we assume that
n\geq 3k+2
, we have
\frac{n-k}{k+1}\geq 2
and thus
\frac{m}{r-1}\leq 2(k+1)
.
Consequently, we get that

\frac{|{\cal A}|}{\Delta}\cdot(1+\ln|{\cal B}|)=O(k^{2}\ln(n|{\cal C}|))
.
This implies that a standard greedy algorithm for
Set Cover
,
produces the required family of size
O\left(k^{2}\ln n|{\cal C}|\right)
.
There is a difficulty to implement this algorithm in time polynomial in
n
(unless
r=\frac{n-k}{k+1}
is a constant),
since
|{\cal A}|={n\choose r}
may not be polynomial in
n
.
Thus we use a randomized algorithm for
Set Cover
, by rounding each entry to
1
with probability
determined by our fractional cover.
It is known that repeating this rounding
2\lceil\ln|{\cal B}|\rceil\leq 4\ln(n|{\cal C}|)
times gives a cover w.h.p.,
and clearly its expected size is
2\lceil\ln|{\cal B}|\rceil
times the value of the fractional hitting set.
In our case,
h(S)=1/\Delta=1/{n-k-2\choose r-2}
for all
S\in{\cal A}
.
Thus we just need to assign to each set in
{\cal A}
probability
1/\Delta
,
and apply randomized rounding
4\ln(n|{\cal C}|)
times.
∎

Applying Lemma
24
on the family
{\cal C}
as in as in Lemma
23
, that has size
|{\cal C}|\leq k(k+2)n
,
we get that that there exists a
{\cal C}
-resilient family
{\cal S}
of
O(k^{2}\ln(n|{\cal C}|))=O(k^{2}\ln n)
subsets of
V

of size
r\approx\frac{n-k}{k+1}
each.
On the other hand, if
|{\cal C}|
is the family of all subsets of
V
of size
k
,
then
|{\cal C}|={n\choose k}<(ne/k)^{k}
and we get the bound
O(k^{2}\ln(n|{\cal C}|))=O(k^{3}\ln n)
of Chuzhoy and Khanna
[
2
]
.

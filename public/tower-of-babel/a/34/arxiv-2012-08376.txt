Abstract
. Given a quaternionic slice regular function f f , we give a direct and effective way to compute the coefficients of its spherical expansion at any point. Such coefficients are obtained in terms of spherical and slice derivatives of the function itself. Afterwards, we compare the coefficients of f f with those of its slice derivative ∂ c f \partial_{c}f obtaining a countable family of differential equations satisfied by any slice regular function. The results are proved in all details and are accompanied to several examples. For some of the results, we also give alternative proofs.

1.
Introduction

In basic complex analysis, if
D\subset\mathbb{C}
is an open domain and
f:D\to\mathbb{C}
is a holomorphic function, then for any
z_{0}\in D
there exists a neighborhood
U\subset D
of
z_{0}

where
f
can be written in the following form

f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 0}(z-z_{0})^{n}c_{n}.

A natural way to obtain the coefficient
c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
is to compute the
n
-th complex derivative
f
and evaluate it at
z_{0}
, i.e.
c_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}(\partial_{z}^{n}f)(z_{0})
.
In particular, if
f
is a polynomial of degree
m
, then
c_{N}=0
for all
N>m
. Moreover by comparing the coefficients
c_{n}
of
f
with those
c_{n}^{\prime}

of
\partial_{z}f
we get that
c_{n}^{\prime}=(n+1)c_{n+1}
, i.e. the following silly equation
\partial_{z}^{k}(\partial_{z}f)=\partial_{z}^{k+1}f
. In this paper we will look for
proper generalizations of the previous basic features in the setting of quaternionic slice regularity.

The concept of slice regular function was introduced in
[
11
]
to build a theory of quaternionic regular functions that resemble somehow complex holomorphy
and to contain quaternionic polynomials with right (or left) coefficients.
Quite immediately a Taylor-like expansion centered at real points was obtained and later, with
suitable modifications on the domain of convergence, also at non-real points (see
[
14
, Chapter 2]
and references therein).
At a later time, C. Stoppato
[
26
]
showed the possibility to expand any slice regular function on an Euclidean open subset of its domain near any
point, using suitable polynomials (see also
[
14
, Chapter 8.1]
).
What we will perform in this paper is to show a new effective way to compute the coefficients of such expansion. We will show several examples and obtain, as a byproduct, a
countable family of differential equations satisfied by slice regular functions.

We start with some standard material setting down the notations, the main definitions and results.
The algebra of quaternions will be denoted by
\mathbb{H}
. Any element
x\in\mathbb{H}
is of the form
x=x_{0}+x_{1}i+x_{2}j+x_{3}k
, where
i,j
and
k
, follow the usual quaternionic
multiplicative rules. For any
x\in\mathbb{H}
we set
x^{c}=x_{0}-(x_{1}i+x_{2}j+x_{3}k)
to be its
conjugate
and
{\sf Re}(x)=(x+x^{c})/2
,
{\sf Im}\,(x)=(x-x^{c})/2
to be its
real and imaginary part, respectively. The norm of any quaternion
x
is defined to be
|x|=\sqrt{x\cdot x^{c}}\in\mathbb{R}
and, if
x\neq 0
its inverse is given by

x^{-1}=x^{c}/|x|^{2}
.
Now,
i,j
and
k
are not the only imaginary units in
\mathbb{H}
, indeed, for any
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}=1
,
we have that
\alpha_{1}i+\alpha_{2}j+\alpha_{3}k
is an imaginary unit as well. For this reason we introduce the following notation

\mathbb{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,x^{2}=-1\}=\{\alpha_{1}i+\alpha_{2}j+\alpha_{3}k\,|\,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}\in\mathbb{R},\,\alpha_{1}^{2}+\alpha_{2}^{2}+\alpha_{3}^{2}=1\}.

Thanks to the previous observation, it is easy to see that

\mathbb{H}=\bigcup_{I\in\mathbb{S}}\mathbb{C}_{I},

where
\mathbb{C}_{I}=\{\alpha+\beta I\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}\}
, showing the
slice
nature of
\mathbb{H}
.
In particular, any
x\in\mathbb{H}
can be written as

x={\sf Re}(x)+{\sf Im}\,(x)={\sf Re}(x)+\frac{{\sf Im}\,(x)}{|{\sf Im}\,(x)|}|{\sf Im}\,(x)|=\alpha+I\beta,

where
\alpha={\sf Re}(x)
,
\beta=|{\sf Im}\,(x)|
,
I={\sf Im}\,(x)/|{\sf Im}\,(x)|
and, if
{\sf Im}\,(x)=0
, then of course
\beta=0
and
I
is any element in
\mathbb{S}
.

We now pass to define the class of slice regular functions following the approach of
[
16
]
.
Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open domain such that
D=\bar{D}
and
\Omega_{D}
its circularization defined as

\Omega_{D}:=\{\alpha+I\beta\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,\alpha+i\beta\in D,\,I\in\mathbb{S}\}.

Sets of the form
\Omega_{D}
will be called
symmetric sets
.
The definition of slice regularity needs one last preliminary observation: the real tensor product
\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}
inherits from
\mathbb{C}
a natural complex structure, namely if

\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}=\{x+\sqrt{-1}y\,|\,x,y\in\mathbb{H}\},

then such a complex structure is exactly the multiplication by
\sqrt{-1}
. Analogously, the standard conjugation in
\mathbb{C}
induces the following conjugation in
\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}
:
we have
\overline{x+\sqrt{-1}y}=x-\sqrt{-1}y
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open domain such that
D=\bar{D}
and
\Omega_{D}\subset\mathbb{H}
denotes its circularization.

(1)

A function
F=F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1}:D\to\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}
is said to be a
stem function
if
F(\bar{z})=\overline{F(z)}
, for any
z\in D
.

(2)

Any stem function
F=F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1}:D\to\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}
induces a (unique)
left slice function

f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
in the following way

f(\alpha+I\beta)=F_{0}(\alpha+i\beta)+IF_{1}(\alpha+I\beta).

We will write
f=\mathcal{I}(F)
to indicate that
F
induces
f
. The set of slice functions defined on
\Omega_{D}
of class
\mathcal{C}^{k}
, with
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\omega\}
, will be denoted
by
\mathcal{S}^{k}(\Omega_{D})
.

(3)

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F)\in\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Omega_{D})
be any slice function of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. We say that
f
is
left slice regular
if its defining stem function
is holomorphic with respect to the standard complex structure in
\mathbb{C}
and the one previously defined in
\mathbb{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. The set of slice regular functions defined on
\Omega_{D}

will be denoted by
\mathcal{SR}(\Omega_{D})
.

In what follows we will omit the adjective “left” when talking of sliceness or slice regularity. The word “unique” in the parenthesis of point
(2)
is justified by the fact that the map
F\mapsto\mathcal{I}(F)
is bijective
[
16
]
. An analogous theory of
right
slice regular function can be obviously defined.
This definition of regularity is equivalent to the one given in
[
11
,
14
]
on domains
\Omega=\Omega_{D}
, with

D\cap\mathbb{R}\neq\emptyset
. New types of domains are considered in
[
8
,

,

,
12
,
13
]
. The theory has also been generalized to the several variable case
[

,
19
]
. Very recently the definition of regularity was further

[

]
and in
[

.

We now introduce differential operators related to slice regularity.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}
be an open domain such that
D=\bar{D}
and
\Omega_{D}\subset\mathbb{H}
denotes its circularization,
x=\alpha+I\beta\in\Omega_{D}
and
z\in D
be such that
z=\alpha+i\beta
.

(1)

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1}):\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be any slice function. We define the
spherical value
of
f
as the slice function
v_{s}f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
defined by

(v_{s}f)(x)=\mathcal{I}(F_{0})(x)=\frac{f(x)+f(x^{c})}{2}=F_{0}(\alpha+i\beta).

We define the
spherical derivative
of
f
as the slice function
\partial_{s}f:\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}
defined by

(\partial_{s}f)(x)=\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{2iF_{1}}{z-\bar{z}}\right)(x)=(2{\sf Im}\,(x))^{-1}(f(x)-f(x^{c}))=\frac{F_{1}(\alpha+i\beta)}{\beta}.

(2)

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F)\in\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Omega_{D})
be any slice function of class
\mathcal{C}^{1}
. We define the functions
\bar{\partial}_{c}f,\partial_{c}f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
as

(\bar{\partial}_{c}f)(x)=\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\bar{z}}\right)(x),\qquad(\partial_{c}f)(x)=\mathcal{I}\left(\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}\right)(x).

The function
\partial_{c}f
is called the
slice derivative
of
f
.

It was proven in
[
25
]
that the spherical derivative operator is indeed a differential operator when applied to slice functions. It is in fact equal to some other differential operator
related to the operators
\bar{\partial}_{c},\partial_{c}
and to the Cauchy-Dirac operator. Clearly, a differentiable slice function
f
is slice regular if and only if
\bar{\partial}_{c}f
vanishes identically.

Regarding the notation, in recent works the spherical value, the spherical derivative and the slice derivatives are often written as

v_{s}f=f_{s}^{\circ},\qquad\partial_{s}f=f_{s}^{\prime},\qquad\bar{\partial}_{c}f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{c}},\qquad\partial_{c}f=\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}.

We will use the older notations because they fit better in the computations we are going to perform.

It is well known that the notion of slice regularity is not closed under pointwise product: it is enough to consider the functions
f(x)=xa
and
g(x)=x
for a suitable
a\in\mathbb{H}
;
the point wise product
(fg)(x)=f(x)g(x)=xax
it is not even a slice function in general. However, a natural modification of the pointwise product that preserves regularity can be given.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F)
and
g=\mathcal{I}(G)
be two slice functions defined on the same domain
\Omega_{D}
. We define the
slice product
of
f
and
g
as
the slice function
f\cdot g:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
defined as

(f\cdot g)(x)=\mathcal{I}(FG)(x).

We have that if
f
and
g
are slice regular functions then their slice product is regular too.
Some classes of slice functions play a remarkable role in relation with the slice product. These are the so called slice preserving and one-slice preserving functions.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1}):\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be any slice function. We say that
f
is a
slice preserving
function if
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are real valued functions,
while, if
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
take their values in some fixed
\mathbb{C}_{J}\subset\mathbb{H}
, for some
J\in\mathbb{S}
, then
f
is said to be a
one-slice preserving
function or a
\mathbb{C}_{J}
-
preserving
function.

Let
f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be any slice function. Then, clearly if
f
is
\mathbb{C}_{J}
-preserving, for some
J
, then

f(\Omega_{D}\cap\mathbb{C}_{J})\subset\mathbb{C}_{J}
, while if
f
is slice preserving, then
f(\Omega\cap\mathbb{C}_{I})\subset\mathbb{C}_{I}
, for any
I\in\mathbb{S}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F)
and
g=\mathcal{I}(G)
be two slice functions defined on the same domain
\Omega_{D}
. If
f
is slice preserving we have that
f\cdot g=g\cdot f=fg
.
If
f
and
g
are both
\mathbb{C}_{J}
-preserving, for some
J
, then
f\cdot g=g\cdot f
and
(f\cdot g)_{|\Omega_{D}\cap\mathbb{C}_{J}}=fg
.

Algebraic properties of such classes of functions are widely studied in
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
6
]
.

We now turn back to the main object of this paper. At the beginning of this introduction we talked about a Taylor-like expansion obtained for slice regular functions.
This was obtained by means of the use of the polynomials
(x-q_{0})
and its slice powers
(x-q_{0})^{\cdot n}
. The other approach exploits instead the following polynomials.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
q_{0}=\alpha+I\beta
be any non-real quaternion. We define the
characteristic polynomial
of
q_{0}
as the slice regular polynomial
\Delta_{q_{0}}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}
defined
by

\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)=(x-q_{0})\cdot(x-q_{0}^{c})=x^{2}-x(q_{0}+q_{0}^{c})+q_{0}q_{0}^{c}=x^{2}-2{\sf Re}(q_{0})x+|q_{0}|^{2}=x^{2}-2x\alpha+\alpha^{2}+\beta^{2}.

For any
q_{0}=\alpha+I\beta\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
we have that
\Delta_{q_{0}}
is a slice preserving function which identically vanishes on the
2
-sphere
\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}=\alpha+\mathbb{S}\beta
.

We recall the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
26
, Theorem 4.1]

Let
f
be a quaternionic slice regular function on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
such that
\Omega_{D}\cap\mathbb{R}\neq\emptyset
and let
q_{0}=\alpha_{0}+I_{0}\beta_{0}\in\Omega
and
R>0
be such that

U(q_{0},R):=\{x\in\mathbb{H}\,|\,|\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)|<R^{2}\}\subset\Omega_{D}.

Then there exist
s_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
such that

(1.1)

f(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n}(x)[s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1}],

for all
x\in U(q_{0},R)
.

Sets of type
U(q_{0},R)
are called
Cassini balls
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be any slice regular function and
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. The expression on the right hand side of Formula (
4.1
)
is called
spherical expansion
of
f
at
q_{0}
and its coefficients
s_{n}\in\mathbb{H}
are called
spherical coefficients
of
f
at
q_{0}
.

Such an expansion was later obtained also in other, more general, contexts
[
12
,
18
,
21
,
22
]
allowing to drop
the hypothesis
\Omega_{D}\cap\mathbb{R}\neq\emptyset
.
However, in none of these work
an immediate and clear representation of the spherical coefficients in terms of some differential operator applied to

was given. We shortly review some results.
At first
[

(R_{\bar{q}_{0}}R_{q_{0}})^{n}f(q_{0})

defined as follows

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(q_{0})+(x-q_{0})\cdot R_{q_{0}}f(x)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(q_{0})+(x-q_{0})\cdot R_{q_{0}}f(\bar{q}_{0})+[(x-\alpha_{0})^{2}+\beta_{0}^{2}]R_{\bar{q}_{0}}R_{q_{0}}f(x)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=f(q_{0})+(x-q_{0})\cdot R_{q_{0}}f(\bar{q}_{0})+[(x-\alpha_{0})^{2}+\beta_{0}^{2}]R_{\bar{q}_{0}}R_{q_{0}}f(q_{0})+[(x-\alpha_{0})^{2}+\beta_{0}^{2}](x-q_{0})R_{q_{0}}R_{\bar{q}_{0}}R_{q_{0}}f(x)

\displaystyle\dots

This representation was sufficient to obtain Theorem

Later, a quite standard integral representation

In particular the authors write the

Another relation between spherical coefficients and slice derivatives was recently given in

In what follows we will give a new way to obtain the spherical coefficients of a slice regular function
f
defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
that is somehow more convenient in explicit computations. This method simply involves spherical and slice derivatives of a slice regular functions, computed alternately. We will
be more clear in a moment. Firstly we describe the structure of the paper.

\partial_{s}

are needed to kill specific slice regular polynomials.

In Section

to obtain the exact values of
((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x))
,

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x))
,
((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)(x-q_{0}))
and of

at

, for

and any

\begin{cases}s_{2k}=\frac{1}{k!}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0}),\\

Moreover we also show explicit computations of some interesting example

The work done in the previous sections allows us to compare the spherical coefficients of a slice regular function

with those of its first slice derivative
\partial_{c}f

\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f
for

After an appendix with the aforementioned flow chart of the results in Section

2.
Preliminaries

We recall that, for any slice function
f
defined on a symmetric domain we have the following equalities

(2.1)

\partial_{s}^{2}f\equiv 0,\quad\partial_{s}v_{s}f\equiv 0,\quad v_{s}\partial_{s}f=\partial_{s}f,\quad v_{s}^{2}f=v_{s}f.

For the first two equalities see
[
16
, p. 1671]
, while the third and fourth equalities are true because
the spherical derivative and the spherical value of a slice function are slice functions induced by stem functions with zero
\sqrt{-1}
-part (see Definition
1.2
).

Moreover, if
f
and
g
are slice functions on a symmetric domain, then
[
14
, Propositions 1.40 and 1.41]
,
[
16
, Section 5 and Proposition 11]

(2.2)

\partial_{s}(f\cdot g)=\partial_{s}f\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\partial_{s}g,\qquad\partial_{c}(f\cdot g)=\partial_{c}f\cdot g+f\cdot\partial_{c}g.

If we add the regularity hypothesis, we have the following immediate result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f
be a slice regular function defined on a symmetric domain. Then

\partial_{c}v_{s}f=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{c}f.

Proof.

The proof is quite straightforward and relies on the following computation.
If
f=\mathcal{I}(F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1})
, then
v_{s}f=\mathcal{I}(F_{0})
. Therefore, if
x=\alpha+I\beta

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}v_{s}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-I\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)F_{0}(\alpha,\beta)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}F_{0}(\alpha,\beta)+\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta)\right)+\frac{I}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}F_{1}(\alpha,\beta)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}F_{0}(\alpha,\beta)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-I\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)(F_{0}(\alpha,\beta)+IF_{1}(\alpha,\beta))\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{c}f(x).

∎

Thanks to the previous lemma we are able to compute the value of the second order differential operator
\partial_{c}\partial_{s}
, applied to the product of two slice regular functions
and even to understand its behaviors under following iterations.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f
and
g
be two slice regular functions defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
. Then for any
x\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
we have

\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)(f\cdot g)\right)(x)=(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g)(x)+\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{s}f\cdot\partial_{c}g)(x)+(\partial_{c}f\cdot\partial_{s}g)(x)\right]+(v_{s}f\cdot\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right))(x).

Proof.

The proof is obtained thanks to the equalities in Formula (
2.2
) and to Lemma
2.1
.
∎

By
[
25
, Theorem 6.3]
, if
f
is a slice regular function, then
\partial_{c}\partial_{s}f

equals
-1/4
the four dimensional real Laplacian of
f
. From this point of view the formula in the previous lemma is related to the standard
formula for the Laplacian of a product.

Recalling now the equalities given in Formula (
2.1
), we are able to compute the next iterations of the operator
\partial_{c}\partial_{s}
applied to
the product of two slice regular functions. For instance we have the following result

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f
and
g
be two slice regular functions defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
. Then for any
x\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
we have

(2.3)

\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}(f\cdot g)=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}g\right)\\
+\frac{1}{2}\Big[\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)f\cdot\partial_{c}g+\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)f\right)\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g\right)+\partial_{s}f\cdot\left(\partial_{c}\left(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g\right)\right)\\
+\left(\partial_{c}\left(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f\right)\right)\cdot\partial_{s}g+\left(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f\right)\cdot\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right)+\partial_{c}f\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right)\right)\Big],

where in the equality we have omitted the evaluation at
x\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

By applying the spherical derivative operator to the terms in the equality in Lemma
2.2
, thanks to Formulas (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)(f\cdot g)\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{s}\left[(\partial_{s}f\cdot\partial_{c}g)+(\partial_{c}f\cdot\partial_{s}g)\right]+\partial_{s}(v_{s}f\cdot\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right))

\displaystyle=(\left(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f\right)\cdot v_{s}g)+\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{s}f\cdot(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g))+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f)\cdot\partial_{s}g)\right]+(v_{s}f\cdot(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right))).

Again, thanks to equalities in Formulas
2.1
and
2.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}(f\cdot g)\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}(\left(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f\right)\cdot v_{s}g)+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{c}\left[(\partial_{s}f\cdot(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g))+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f)\cdot\partial_{s}g)\right]+\partial_{c}(v_{s}f\cdot(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right)))

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+\left(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\partial_{c}g+\frac{1}{2}\partial_{c}f\cdot(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g\right)))+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{2}g\right)+

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\frac{1}{2}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g))+(\partial_{s}f\cdot\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}g))+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f)\cdot\partial_{s}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}g))\right],

which, up to a reordering of the terms, gives the thesis.
∎

After a couple of others iteration one realizes that a non-trivial pattern arises. The following main result exposes such pattern.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
f
and
g
be two slice regular functions defined on a symmetric domain, then, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}g)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]\right\}.

Proof.

The proof is done by induction. The base cases (
k=1,2
) are given in Lemmas
2.2
and
2.3
. Assume now that the equality is true for

k\in\mathbb{N}
. We will prove it for
k+1
. Firstly, by spending immediately the inductive hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}(f\cdot g))

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\left\{\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}g)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]\right\}\right\}.

Let us apply the spherical derivative
\partial_{s}
, taking into account formulas (
2.1
): we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}\left\{\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}g)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\left.\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]\right\}\right\}.

Using now the Leibniz rule for
\partial_{c}
and Lemma
2.1
we get

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot\partial_{c}g+\partial_{c}f\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)\right.

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}g)+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}g)\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,+\left.(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}g)\right]\Big\}.

We now reorder the previous sum in a more convenient way as follows:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot\partial_{c}g+\partial_{c}f\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-(h+1)}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=0}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}(\left[(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}g)\right]\Big\}.

If we set
h=p-1
in the two summations starting from
h=0
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}f\right)\cdot\partial_{c}g+\partial_{c}f\cdot\left(\partial_{s}\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k}g\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{p-1}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\binom{k-1}{p-1}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p+1}g)\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{h=1}^{k-1}\binom{k-1}{h}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{h}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-h+1}g)\right]\Big\}.

Now let us substitute
p
for
h
. We recall that, for all
p\in\{1,\ldots,k-1\}
, it holds

\binom{k-1}{p-1}+\binom{k-1}{p}=\binom{k}{p},

and
\binom{k-1}{k-1}=1=\binom{k}{k}
. Thus, we get the thesis

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}(f\cdot g)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}f\right)\cdot v_{s}g+v_{s}f\cdot\left(\left(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\right)^{k+1}g\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\sum_{p=0}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p}f)\cdot(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}g)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}f)\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-p}g)\right]\right.

\displaystyle\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\left.+\sum_{p=1}^{k}\binom{k}{p}\left[((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{(k+1)-p}f)\cdot((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}g)+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{p}f)\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{(k+1)-p}g)\right]\right\}.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Let
f=\mathcal{I}(F_{0}+\sqrt{-1}F_{1})
be any slice function such that its components
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
are homogeneous polynomials of degree
k
in
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Then, clearly,
\partial_{s}f
and
\partial_{c}f
are slice functions such that their respective stem functions are homogeneous polynomials of degree
k-1
.

Thanks to the previous remark, we can state the following Lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We have the following equalities.

•

If
n>0
, then

\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{n}x^{2n}=\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{n}x^{2n}\equiv 0.

•

If
n\geq 0
, then

(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{n+1}x^{2n+1}=(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{n+1}x^{2n+1}\equiv 0.

Proof.

Thanks to Remark
2.5
, by applying to
x^{k}
an alternate sequence of the operators
\partial_{s}
and
\partial_{c}
, it turns out that the
k
-th iteration returns a constant
and hence the
(k+1)
-th returns zero.
∎

Before stating the following corollary we recall from Definition
1.6
that, for any
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
the characteristic
polynomial of
q_{0}
is the quadratic quaternionic polynomial given by
\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)=(x-q_{0})\cdot(x-q_{0}^{c})
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
be any quaternion and
x=\alpha+I\beta
. Then we have the following equalities.

(1)

\partial_{s}(x-q_{0})=\partial_{c}(x-q_{0})\equiv 1,\qquad v_{s}(x-q_{0})=\alpha-q_{0},\qquad(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})(x-q_{0})=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})(x-q_{0})\equiv 0.

(2)

\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}=2(\alpha-{\sf Re}(q_{0})),\qquad\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}=2(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})),\qquad v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}=\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}-2\alpha{\sf Re}(q_{0})+|q_{0}|^{2}.

Notice that
(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})(q_{0})=(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})(q_{0})=0
. Moreover

(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}\equiv 1,\qquad(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}\equiv 2.

(3)

For any integer
k\geq 0
, we have

\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}=\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}\equiv 0,\qquad(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k+1}(\Delta^{k}_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0}))=(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k+1}(\Delta^{k}_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0}))\equiv 0.

Proof.

Points
(1)
and
(2)
are straightforward consequences of the definitions of
\Delta_{q_{0}}
and of the differential operators
\partial_{s}
and
\partial_{c}
. For point

(3)
, just notice that
\Delta^{k}_{q_{0}}
is a quaternionic polynomial of degree
2k
, while
\Delta^{k}_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0})
is a quaternionic polynomial of degree
2k+1
.
Hence, by Lemma
2.6
, we get the thesis.
∎

3.
Alternate actions of
\partial_{s}
and
\partial_{c}
on a characteristic polynomial

In this section we will give a series of technical results needed to prove our first main theorem. Notice that the order of the result contained in this section is not completely arbitrary. In fact as the reader might notice, all such results are somehow linked one to another
as showed in the flow chart given in Appendix
A
.

We start from the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for any
0\leq k<p
(and so for any
k\in\mathbb{\mathbb{N}}
) we have

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=0.

Proof.

First of all, for
k=0
, the thesis follows from Corollary
2.7
.
Assume now that
k>0
and
p>k
. By Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})

\displaystyle=((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}))

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\bigg\{((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}+v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left[\binom{k-1}{0}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}+\binom{k-1}{k-1}(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\,\,\,\quad\left.\left.+\binom{k-1}{1}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot 2+\binom{k-1}{k-1}((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}+v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}\cdot(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left.+(k-1)(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot 2\right].

Now, by using again the fact that
(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}=2
and that, for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m})(q_{0})=(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m})(q_{0})=0
, for
x=q_{0}
, we obtain

((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=k(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0}).

Iterating the previous computations and argument
k
times, we get

((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=k\cdot(k-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot 2\cdot 1(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-k})(q_{0})

but, as
p>k
, by the previous case
k=0
, we have
((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=0
.
∎

We now evaluate at
q_{0}
the
k
-th iteration of the operator
(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})
applied to the
p
-th power of the characteristic polynomial
\Delta_{q_{0}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for any
0\leq k\leq p
we have

((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=\begin{cases}2\cdot k!,\qquad\mbox{ if }k=p,\\
0,\qquad{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k=0
the result is obvious, since
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(q_{0})=0
. If
k=1<p
we have

((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(x)=\partial_{s}(2p\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))=2p[(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))+(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))],

but again, the previous expression vanishes when evaluated at
x=q_{0}
as
(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})=(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})=0
.
Assume now that
k>1
and that
p>k
. By Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}(2p\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))))=2p\partial_{s}\bigg\{((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x))\cdot v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\frac{1}{2}\left[\binom{k-2}{0}\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-2}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)+\binom{k-2}{k-2}\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-2}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=2p\left\{(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x))\cdot v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))+\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)\right\},

but since
(v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))_{|_{x=q_{0}}}=0
, then we have

((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=p((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0}).

Iterating the same computations and argument
k
times, we get

((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=p\cdot(p-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot 2\cdot 1((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-k})(q_{0}),

and, thanks to the previous computation, we get the thesis.
If instead
p=k
, we proceed by induction.

The base case, for
k=1
is done in Corollary
2.7
, point
(2)
.
Assume now that the result is true for
k-1\in\mathbb{N}
and set
x=\alpha+I\beta
. Then,

\displaystyle((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k})(x)

\displaystyle=((\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k})(x)=(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1})(k\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)((x-q_{0})+(x-\bar{q}_{0})))

\displaystyle=2k(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1})(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))).

We now apply the formula contained in Proposition
2.4
, and, recalling the equalities in Lemma
2.6
and Corollary
2.7
, obtain

\displaystyle((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k})(x)

\displaystyle=2k(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1})(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))=2k\partial_{s}\bigg\{(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)(\alpha-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))

\displaystyle\left.\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left[\binom{k-2}{0}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-2})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)+\binom{k-2}{k-2}(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-2})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=2k\left\{\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)\right\}=k\cdot 2\cdot(k-1)!=2\cdot k!,

where in the last line of the previous sequence of computations, we have used the inductive hypothesis.
∎

Thanks to the previous results we are now able to prove our first main result.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for any
0\leq k\leq p
we have

((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=\begin{cases}k!,\qquad\mbox{ if }k=p,\\
0\qquad\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

If
k=0
the result is obvious, since
(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=0
. If
k=1<p
we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}[(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})+(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})]

\displaystyle=((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})+(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}})+(\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}})\right],

but again, the previous expression vanishes when evaluated at
x=q_{0}
as
(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})=(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})=0
.
Assume now that
k>1
and that
p>k
. By Proposition
2.4
, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}\Delta_{q_{0}})

\displaystyle=((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}+(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\binom{k-1}{0}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}+\binom{k-1}{k-1}(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad+\binom{k-1}{1}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\cdot 2+\binom{k-1}{k-1}((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})\right\}.

Therefore, for
x=q_{0}
, using Lemma
3.1
, Lemma
3.2
and the fact that
(\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m})(q_{0})=(v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m})(q_{0})=0
, we get

((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=(k-1)((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0}).

Iterating the same computation and argument
k-1
times, we obtain

((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=(k-1)\cdot(k-2)\cdot\ldots\cdot 2\cdot 1((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-(k-1)})(q_{0}).

But as
p>k
, thanks to the previous case
k=1
, we get the thesis.
If instead
k=p
, then we proceed by induction. The base case, for
k=1
is addressed in Corollary
2.7
, point
(2)
.
Assume now that
k
is greater or equal than
2
and that the result is true for
k-1\in\mathbb{N}
and set
x=\alpha+I\beta
. Thanks to Proposition
2.4
, and the fact that
\Delta_{q_{0}}
has degree
2
, we have,

\displaystyle(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}})

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}+v_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left\{\binom{k-1}{0}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}+\binom{k-1}{k-1}(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\quad\quad\quad+\binom{k-1}{1}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})\Delta_{q_{0}})+\binom{k-1}{k-1}((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}})\right\}.

Now, thanks to Corollary
2.7
we have

(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{(k-1)((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})\cdot 2+((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1})\right\}.

Using the inductive hypothesis and Lemma
3.2
, we finally have

(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{(k-1)(k-1)!\cdot 2+2(k-1)!\right\}=(k-1)![(k-1)+1]=k!,

proving the thesis.
∎

We now consider a slight modification of Lemma
3.2
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for any
0\leq k\leq p-1
we have

((\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})=\begin{cases}2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})k!,\qquad\mbox{ if }k=p-1,\\
0,\,\,\,\quad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

For
k=0
and any
p>1
we have that
(\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(x)=2p\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))
, which clearly vanishes for
x=q_{0}
.
Assume now that
k>0
and
p>k+1
. By Proposition
2.4
and Corollary
2.7
, it holds

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(x)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}(2p\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))

\displaystyle=2p\left\{((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(x)\cdot v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))+\frac{1}{2}\left[\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1}(x)+(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(x)\right]\right\}

Now, for
x=q_{0}
we have that
(v_{s}(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))_{|_{x=q_{0}}}=0
and, by Lemma
3.1

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})=0
. Hence, the only piece that survives is
p(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-1})(q_{0})
. Iterating the same computation and argument
k-1
times, we obtain

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p})(q_{0})

\displaystyle=p\cdot(p-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(p-(k-1))(\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-k})(q_{0})

\displaystyle=p\cdot(p-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot(p-(k-1))\Big[2(p-k)\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p-k-1}(q_{0})(q_{0}-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))\Big](q_{0})=0

Assume now that
k=p-1
. For
k=1
we get

\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}=2(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})),

which, for
x=q_{0}
is equal to
2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})
.
Now, for a general

, repeating the previous computation, we get

((\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k})(q_{0})=k\cdot(k-1)\cdot\ldots\cdot 1(\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}})(q_{0})=2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})k!,

which gives the thesis.
∎

We now pass to study
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0})
. First of all we saw in Corollary
2.7
that, when
k=0
,
\partial_{s}(x-q_{0})\equiv 1
.
We will prove a suitable generalization in the following result.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
and
p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. Then, for any

we have

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)(x-q_{0})))_{|_{x=q_{0}}}=\begin{cases}k!,\qquad\mbox{ if }k=p,\\

Proof.

The result is trivially true when

as the spherical value and derivative of
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}
vanish at

.
Let now
k>0
and
p>k

, we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k})(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)(x-q_{0}))

\displaystyle=\partial_{s}\Big\{((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x))v_{s}(x-q_{0})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left[\binom{k-1}{0}\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)+\binom{k-1}{k-1}\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k-1}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)\right]\Big\}.

and thanks to Lemmas

Coefficients of the spherical expansion

Thanks to the results of the previous section, we are able to give a differential representation of the coefficients of the spherical expansion of a slice regular function.
First of all recall that if
f\in\mathcal{SR}(\Omega_{D})
and
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, then there exists a symmetric domain, namely a Cassini ball
U(q_{0},r)\subset\Omega_{D}
, such that,
for any
x\in U(q_{0},r)
we have

(4.1)

f(x)=s_{0}+(x-q_{0})s_{1}+\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)s_{2}+\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0})s_{3}+\dots+\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)s_{2k}+(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{2k+1}+\dots.

Now, it is well known (see
[
2
,
18
,
26
]
) that
s_{0}=f(q_{0})
,
s_{1}=(\partial_{s}f)(q_{0})
and that
s_{2}=((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f)(q_{0})
. We are able to describe in the same way all the other coefficients.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
f
be a slice regular function defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
and let
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Then, we have that the coefficients of the spherical expansion (
4.1
) are given as follows

\begin{cases}s_{2k}=\frac{1}{k!}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0}),\\
s_{2k+1}=\frac{1}{k!}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0}).\end{cases}

Proof.

As the result is trivially true for
k=0
, we start from
k=1
. Now, even for
k=1
we already know that
s_{2}=((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f)(q_{0})
. We then work on
s_{3}
.
If we apply the differential operator
\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})
to
f
as represented in Formula (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f)(x)=

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{3}+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{2}(x))s_{4}

\displaystyle+\dots+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x))s_{2k}+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0})))s_{2k+1}+\dots.

But now, thanks to Theorem
3.5
and to Lemma
3.1
we have

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f)(q_{0})=s_{3}.

Assume now that
k>1
. We start from the even coefficients. If we apply the differential operator
(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}
to
f
as represented in Formula (
4.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(x)=

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x))s_{2k}+((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{2k+1}

\displaystyle+\dots+((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x))s_{2p}+((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)(x-q_{0})))s_{2p+1}+\dots.

But now, thanks to Theorem
3.3
and to Lemma
3.6
we have

((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0})=k!s_{2k}.

Analogously we have

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(x)=

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{2k+1}+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k+1}(x))s_{2k+2}

\displaystyle+\dots+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x))s_{2p}+(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{p}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{2p+1}+\dots,

and thanks to Theorem
3.5

(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0})=k!s_{2k+1},

concluding the proof.

We give a first corollary about slice preserving and one-slice preserving functions.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be a slice regular function,
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
and let
s_{n}(q_{0})
be the spherical coefficients of
f
at
q_{0}
.
Then the following facts hold true:

(1)

the function
f
is slice preserving if and only if, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
s_{2k+1}(q_{0})\in\mathbb{R}
and, if
q_{0}\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
,
s_{2k}(q_{0})\in\mathbb{C}_{I}
.

(2)

the function
f
is
\mathbb{C}_{J}
-preserving if and only if
s_{2k+1}(q_{0})\in\mathbb{C}_{J}
and, for any
q_{0}\in\mathbb{C}_{J}
,

s_{2k}(q_{0})\in\mathbb{C}_{J}
.

Proof.

The necessity follows from the expression of the spherical coefficients given in Theorem
4.1
while the sufficiency follows directly just by looking at
s_{0}
and
s_{1}
.
∎

In view of the next corollary we recall some basic fact about zeros of slice regular functions. Let
f:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
be a slice regular function defined on a symmetric
domain and
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
. Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}\in\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}
(with
q_{i}\neq\bar{q}_{i+1}
for all
i=1,\dots,n-1
) be such that

f(x)=\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)(x-q_{1})\cdot(x-q_{2})\cdot\dots\cdot(x-q_{n})\cdot g(x),

for some slice regular function
g:\Omega_{D}\to\mathbb{H}
which does not have any zeros in
\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}
. We then say that
2m
is the spherical multiplicity
of
\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}
and that
n
is the isolated multiplicity of
q_{1}
.
As it was pointed out in
[
26
]
and in
[
14
, Remark 8.11]
, if
f
is a slice regular function on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}

and
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, then if
s_{2n}
or
s_{2n+1}
is the first non vanishing coefficient in the spherical expansion (
4.1
),
then
2n
is the spherical multiplicity of
f
at
\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}
. Moreover,
q_{0}
has a positive isolated multiplicity if and only if
s_{2n}=0
. These
information can be translated in new formulas thanks to our results.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
f
be a slice regular function defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
and let
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Then, if for any
k=0,\dots,n-1
we have that
((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0})=(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}f)(q_{0})=0
and

((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{n}f)(q_{0})\neq 0
or
(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{n}f)(q_{0})\neq 0

then
2n
is the spherical multiplicity of
f
at
\mathbb{S}_{q_{0}}
. Moreover,
q_{0}
has a positive isolated multiplicity if and only if
((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{n}f)(q_{0})=0
.

Having now these new nice representations of the spherical coefficients of a slice regular functions, we are able to effectively compute examples.

Example 4.4
.

We give an explicit example for the previous corollary. Consider the quaternionic polynomial

P(x)=x^{3}-x^{2}(i+j+k)+x(i-j+k)+1=(x-i)\cdot(x-j)\cdot(x-k).

We want to compute its spherical coefficients in general (i.e. at a non-real point) and at
x_{0}=i
.
If
s_{n}(x)
denote the
n
-th spherical coefficient at
x=\alpha+I\beta\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, then we have

\displaystyle s_{0}(i)=0,

\displaystyle s_{1}(x)=(\partial_{s}P)(x)=3\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}-2\alpha(i+j+k)+i-j+k,

\displaystyle s_{1}(i)=-1+i-j+k,

\displaystyle s_{2}(x)=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}P)(x)=3\alpha-(i+j+k)+I\beta,

\displaystyle s_{2}(i)=-j-k,

\displaystyle s_{3}(x)=(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}\partial_{s}P)(x)=1

\displaystyle s_{3}(i)=1,

and so, for any
n>3
, we have
s_{n}(x)=0
.
Therefore, near
x_{0}=i
, we have

P(x)=(x-i)(-1+i-j+k)+\Delta_{i}(x)(-j-k)+\Delta_{i}(x)(x-i).

We now deal with basic idempotent functions
[
5
,
7
]
.

Definition 4.5
.

We define the slice regular function
\mathcal{J}:\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}
as
\mathcal{J}(x)=\frac{{\sf Im}\,(x)}{|{\sf Im}\,(x)|}
, for all
x\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Sometimes, if
x=\alpha+I\beta
, we will also write
\mathcal{J}(x)=I
.

Let now
J\in\mathbb{S}
and
x=\alpha+I\beta\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, with
\beta>0
. We define
\ell^{+,J}:\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}
,
\ell^{-,J}:\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}
as

\ell^{+,J}(x)=\frac{1-\mathcal{J}(x)J}{2},\qquad\ell^{-,J}(x)=\frac{1+\mathcal{J}(x)J}{2}

As explained in
[
5
,
7
]
these functions
\ell^{\pm,J}
are idempotents for the slice product and any slice regular function
f
defined on a symmetric domain without real points can be written
as
f=f_{+}\cdot\ell^{+,J}+f_{-}\cdot\ell^{-,J}
, where
f_{+}
and
f_{-}
are slice regular functions defined on the same domain of
f
(this is called Peirce decomposition).
The important role of idempotent function was also exploited in
[
24
]
in order to characterize some relevant space of measurable regular functions.
Before going into the details, recall that, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
, the notation
N!!
stands for the double factorial, i.e.

N!!=\prod_{k=0}^{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil-1}(N-2k)=N(N-2)(N-4)\cdots

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
J\in\mathbb{S}
be any imaginary unit and
x=\alpha+I\beta\in\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}
, with
\beta>0
. Then the spherical coefficients of
\ell^{+,J}
at
x
are the following

s_{0}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1-IJ}{2},\quad\begin{cases}s_{2k}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k}}IJ,\\
s_{2k+1}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}}J.\end{cases}

Analogously, the spherical coefficients of
\ell^{-,J}
at
x
are the following

s_{0}^{-}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1+IJ}{2},\quad\begin{cases}s_{2k}^{-}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k}}IJ,\\
s_{2k+1}^{-}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}}J.\end{cases}

Proof.

We will prove the result for
\ell^{+,J}
as the argument for
\ell^{-,J}
is completely analogous.
For
s_{0}^{+}
there is nothing to prove. We have

s_{1}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=(\partial_{s}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta)=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{J}{\beta},

and so

s_{2}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=(\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta)=\partial_{c}\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{J}{\beta}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\alpha}-I\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)\left(-\frac{1}{2}\frac{J}{\beta}\right)=-\frac{1}{2^{2}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}IJ.

We now compute
s_{3}

s_{3}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}\partial_{s}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta)=\partial_{s}\left(-\frac{1}{2^{2}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}IJ\right)(\alpha+I\beta)=-\frac{1}{2^{2}}\frac{1}{\beta^{3}}J.

Let now
k>1
be fixed and assume that the result is true for all integers lower than
k
.
From Theorem
4.1
we know that

\begin{cases}s_{2k}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1}{k!}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta),\\
s_{2k+1}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=\frac{1}{k!}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta),\end{cases}

therefore

\displaystyle k!s_{2k}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta)=(\partial_{c}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k-1}\ell^{+,J}))(\alpha+I\beta)

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}\left((k-1)!s_{2(k-1)+1}(\alpha+I\beta)\right)

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}\left(-\frac{(2(k-1)-1)!!}{2^{(k-1)+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2(k-1)+1)}}J\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(-I\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\right)\left(-\frac{(2(k-1)-1)!!}{2^{(k-1)+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k-1}}J\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{(2(k-1)-1)!!}{2^{(k-1)+1}}\left(-\frac{2k-1}{\beta^{2k}}\right)IJ\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k}}IJ.

Analogously, we have

k!s_{2k+1}^{+}(\alpha+I\beta)=(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{+,J})(\alpha+I\beta)=\partial_{s}\left(-\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k}}IJ\right)=-\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}\frac{1}{\beta^{2k+1}}J.

∎

Remark 4.7
.

In
[
18
, Example 5.6]
the authors gave a description of spherical coefficients
\tilde{s}_{n}
for the function
2\ell^{+,J}
at
x=J
. They found

\tilde{s}_{0}=2,\qquad\begin{cases}\tilde{s}_{2k+1}=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}(-J),\quad k\geq 0,\\
\tilde{s}_{2k}=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k},\quad k\geq 1.\end{cases}

Our result is consistent as, at
x=J
(and
\beta=1
), we have

2s_{0}^{+}(J)=2\cdot\frac{1-J\cdot J}{2}=2=\tilde{s}_{0},\quad\begin{cases}2s_{2k+1}^{+}(J)=2\cdot(-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}J)=-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}J,\\
2s_{2k}^{+}(J)=2\cdot(-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k+1}}JJ)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}.\end{cases}

Now, thanks to standard properties of the double factorial, we have that

(2k-1)!!=\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!},

and hence

\begin{cases}2s_{2k+1}^{+}(J)=-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}J=-\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!}J=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}(-J)=\tilde{s}_{2k+1},\\
2s_{2k}^{+}(J)=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\frac{(2k)!}{2^{k}k!}=4^{-k}\binom{2k}{k}=\tilde{s}_{2k}.\end{cases}

Remark 4.8
.

Notice that, even though the functions
\ell^{\pm,J}
are slice constant (see
[
1
]

slice polynomial functions, i.e. slice regular functions of the form
P=P_{+}\cdot\ell^{+,J}+P_{-}\cdot\ell^{-,J}

P_{+}
and
P_{-}
are regular polynomials.
Using the linearity of the operators
(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}
and
\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}
and the formula in Proposition
2.4
one can compute all the coefficients
and, since those of
\ell^{\pm,J}
are infinite, then also those of
P
are, in general, infinite.
However, with some more information, it is possible to simplify a lot all the computation as the following examples show.

Example 4.9

Example 4.10
.

Let
R:\mathbb{H}\setminus\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{H}

\displaystyle s_{0}(x_{0})=x_{0}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}i,

\displaystyle((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}R)(x)

\displaystyle=v_{s}(-x^{2})((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{+,i})(x)+v_{s}(x)((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\ell^{-,i})(x)

\displaystyle(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}R)(x)

5.
Spherical coefficients of the slice derivative

Thanks to the results of the previous section, we are able to compute the coefficients of the spherical expansion of the slice derivative of a slice regular function.
Starting from Equation (
4.1
) we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{c}f(x)

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}(x))s_{2}+\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)(x-q_{0}))s_{3}+\dots+\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x))s_{2k}+\partial_{c}((\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}(x)(x-q_{0})))s_{2k+1}+\dots

\displaystyle=s_{1}+(2(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))s_{2}+((x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}+2\Delta_{q_{0}}(x))s_{3}+\dots

\displaystyle\quad\dots+(2k\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})))s_{2k}+\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k-1}(x)\left[k(x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}+(k+1)\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)\right]s_{2k+1}+\dots,

Now, if we denote by
s_{h}^{\prime}
the spherical coefficients of
\partial_{c}f
, we already know, from Theorem
4.1
, that

\begin{cases}s_{2k}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{k!}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\partial_{c}f)(q_{0}),\\
s_{2k+1}^{\prime}=\frac{1}{k!}(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k}\partial_{c}f)(q_{0}).\end{cases}

Therefore we have that

s_{0}^{\prime}=(\partial_{c}f)(q_{0})=s_{1}+2(q_{0}-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))s_{2}=s_{1}+2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2},

and that

s_{1}^{\prime}=(\partial_{s}\partial_{c}f)(q_{0})=2s_{2}-2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{3}.

We can now state our result.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
f
be a slice regular function defined on a symmetric domain
\Omega_{D}
and let
q_{0}\in\Omega_{D}\setminus\mathbb{R}
.
Then, we have that the coefficients
s_{n}^{\prime}
of the spherical expansion (
5.1
) are given as follows

\begin{cases}s^{\prime}_{2k}=(2k+1)s_{2k+1}+(k+1)2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2k+2},\\
s^{\prime}_{2k+1}=(2k+2)s_{2k+2}-(k+1)2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2k+3}.\end{cases}

Proof.

To prove the theorem is sufficient to compare the spherical expansion of the function
\partial_{c}f
near
q_{0}

(5.2)

\partial_{c}f=\sum_{n\geq 0}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n}(x)[s_{2n}^{\prime}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1}^{\prime}],

with that obtained by deriving the expansion of
f
, i.e.

\partial_{c}f=\sum_{n\geq 0}\partial_{c}\left(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n}(x)[s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1}]\right).

We proceed to compute this last derivative:

\displaystyle\partial_{c}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\partial_{c}\left(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n}[s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1}]\right)

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\left[n\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n-1}2(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0}))(s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1})+\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n}s_{2n+1}\right]

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\left\{\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n-1}\left[2n(x-q_{0})(s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1})+2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})(s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1})+\Delta_{q_{0}}s_{2n+1}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=s_{1}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\left\{\Delta_{q_{0}}^{n-1}\left[2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})(2ns_{2n}-2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n+1})+\Delta_{q_{0}}(2n+1)s_{2n+1}\right]\right\}

(5.3)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\Delta_{q_{0}}^{k}\left[(2k+1)s_{2k+1}+(k+1)2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2k+2}+(x-q_{0})((2k+2)s_{2k+2}-(k+1)2{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2k+3})\right],

where we have used that

\displaystyle 2n(x-q_{0})(s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1})+2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})(s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})s_{2n+1})+\Delta^{n}_{q_{0}}(x)s_{2n+1}=

\displaystyle 2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})(2ns_{2n}+2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n+1})+(x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}2ns_{2n+1}+\Delta^{n}_{q_{0}}(x)s_{2n+1}=

\displaystyle 2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})(2ns_{2n}+2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n+1})+(x-q_{0})\cdot(x-q_{0}^{c}+q_{0}^{c}-q_{0})2ns_{2n+1}+\Delta^{n}_{q_{0}}(x)s_{2n+1}=

\displaystyle 2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n}+(x-q_{0})(2ns_{2n}-2n{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})s_{2n+1})+\Delta^{n}_{q_{0}}(x)(2n+1)s_{2n+1}.

Therefore, comparing Formula (
5.2
) with Formula (
5.3
), by the uniqueness of the spherical expansion, we obtain the thesis.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

Notice that, from the Formulas in Theorem
5.1
, if
{\sf Im}\,(q_{0})

.

Let

((\partial_{s}\partial_{c})^{k+1}f)(x)=2(k+1)v_{s}((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k+1}f)(x)=2(k+1)[(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k+1}f)(x)-{\sf Im}\,(x)(\partial_{s}(\partial_{c}\partial_{s})^{k+1}f)(x)].\end{cases}

Proof.

It is immediate from Theorems
4.1
and
5.1
.
∎

Notice that in
[
2
]
, we found the first of such relations, namely

(\partial_{c}f)(x)=(\partial_{s}f)(x)+2{\sf Im}\,(x)((\partial_{c}\partial_{s})f)(x),

.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
q_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
be any quaternion. For any integer
m\geq 1
, we have the following equalities

\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x))=2m\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})),\qquad\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)(x-q_{0}))=\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)\left[m(x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}+(m+1)\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)\right].

Proof.

Both equalities are direct consequences of the Leibniz rule for the slice derivative
\partial_{c}
. In particular

\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x))=m\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)\partial_{c}\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)=m\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)[(x-q_{0})+(x-\bar{q}_{0})]=2m\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)(x-{\sf Re}(q_{0})),

while

\displaystyle\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)(x-q_{0}))

\displaystyle=\partial_{c}(\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x))(x-q_{0})+\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)=m\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)[(x-q_{0})+(x-\bar{q}_{0})]\cdot(x-q_{0})+\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)\left[m(x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}+(m+1)\Delta_{q_{0}}(x)\right].

∎

Hence, while the slice derivative of
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m}(x)
is the product of
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)
times a polynomial of degree
1
, the slice derivative
of

can be written as the product of
\Delta_{q_{0}}^{m-1}(x)
times the degree
2
polynomial

(B.1)

\mathfrak{P}_{q_{0},m}(x):=m(x-q_{0})^{\cdot 2}+(m+1)\Delta_{q_{0}}(x).

For future convenience, we collect in the next Lemma some of the properties of

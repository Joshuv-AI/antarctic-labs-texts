The localized Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture
Rufei Ren

Abstract
. Gouvêa–Mazur [ GM ] made a conjecture on the local constancy of slopes of modular forms when the weight varies p p -adically. Since one may decompose the space of modular forms according to associated residual Galois representations, the Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture makes sense for each such component. We prove the localized Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture when the residual Galois representation is irreducible and its restriction to Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ p / ℚ p ) \Gal(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}) is reducible and generic.



1.
Introduction

Let
p
be a prime number,
N
be a positive integer coprime to
p
. We normalize the
p
-adic valuation of elements in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}
so that
v_{p}(p)=1
.
For any
r\in\mathbb{Q}
and a space
S
of modular forms with
U_{p}
-action, we write
d(S,r)
for the cardinality of the number of generalized eigenvalues of the
U_{p}
-action with
p
-adic valuation
r
.
Let
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Np))
be the space of cusp forms with weight
k
and level
Np
.

Statement 1.1
.

There is a polynomial
M(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
such that
for any
r\in\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
, if
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
are integers such that

(1)

k_{1}\geq 2r+2
and
k_{2}\geq 2r+2
;

(2)

k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\pmod{(p-1)p^{m}}
for an integer
m\geq M(r)
,

then
d\left(S_{k_{1}}(\Gamma_{0}(Np)),r\right)=d\left(S_{k_{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(Np)),r\right)
.

Based on the numerical data from Mestre, Gouvêa and Mazur made the following conjecture in
[
GM
]
.

Conjecture 1.2

(Gouvêa–Mazur Conjecture)
.

Statement
1.1
is true with
M(x)=x
.

In
[
BC
]
, Buzzard and Calegari disproved this conjecture by giving an explicit counterexample.
However, it is still believed to be true that
M
can be taken to be a
linear polynomial. So far, the only definite result
concerning Statement
1.1
is due to
[
Wan
]
, in which Wan proved Statement
1.1
with a quadratic
M
. Recently, Hattori proved in
[
Hat
]
an analogue of Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture for
Drinfeld cuspforms of level
\Gamma_{1}(t)
.

In this paper, we prove that if we localize
S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Np))
at some maximal
Hecke ideal associated to a Galois representation
\bar{r}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}:=\Gal\left(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}\right)\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\bar{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
(under certain conditions on
\bar{r}
), Statement
1.1
is true with
M(x)=x+5
(see Theorem
1.3
). Our work
is mainly based on
[
LTXZ-1
]
and
[
LTXZ-2
]
from Liu, Truong, Xiao and Zhao, in which they solved the so-called local ghost conjecture from
[
BP
]
. To present our main theorem, we introduce some notations first.

Let
E
be a finite field extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
that contains
\sqrt{p}
, and denote by
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathbb{F}
its
ring of integers and residue field, respectively. Let
\Delta:=(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
For any character
\psi:\Delta\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
and any Galois representation
\bar{r}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F})
, we denote by

S_{k}\left(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}}
the localization of
S_{k}\left(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi\right)
at the maximal Hecke ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}
associated to
\bar{r}
.
Since
S_{k}\left(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi\right)
has finite dimension, it can be decomposed into a direct sum
\bigoplus_{\bar{r}}S_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}}
over a finite set of (semisimple) residual Galois representations
\bar{r}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL_{2}\left(\mathbb{F}\right)
.
Therefore, it makes sense to study a similar question as Conjecture
1.2
about each
S_{k}\left(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}}
. In consideration of the following two remarks, we prove this result in Theorem
1.3
under some certain assumptions.

(1)

The proof of our main theorem relies heavily on the local ghost conjecture, whose key requirements are that
\bar{r}
is absolutely irreducible, and
\bar{r}|_{\mathrm{I}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}
is reducible and generic, i.e. there is a unique pair of integers
(a,b)
such that

(1.1)

\bar{r}|_{\mathrm{I}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}\simeq\begin{bmatrix}\omega_{1}^{a+b+1}&*\\
0&\omega_{1}^{b}\end{bmatrix}\quad\text{ for some~}2\leq a\leq p-5\text{ and }0\leq b\leq p-2,

where
I_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
and
\omega_{1}
are the inertial group and the first fundamental character of
\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}
, respectively.

(2)

For
\bar{r}
in (1), we have

S_{k}\left(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi\right)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}}\neq\emptyset\ \textrm{if and only if}\ \psi(\bar{\alpha})=\omega(\bar{\alpha})^{a+2b-k+2}\ \textrm{for every}\ \bar{\alpha}\in\Delta.

Theorem 1.3

(Main theorem)
.

Assume that
p\geq 11
.
Let
\bar{r}:\Gal_{\mathbb{Q}}\rightarrow\GL_{2}(\mathbb{F})
be an absolutely irreducible residual Galois representation such that

\bar{r}|_{\mathrm{I}_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}}}

is reducible and generic, i.e.
2\leq a\leq p-5
; and
\psi:(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\bar{\mathbb{Q}}^{\times}
be a character.

For any integers
m\geq 4
and
k_{1},k_{2}
such that

(1)

k_{1}>m-3
and
k_{2}>m-3
,

(2)

k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\pmod{(p-1)p^{m}}
,

(3)

\psi(\bar{\alpha})=\omega(\bar{\alpha})^{a+2b-k_{1}+2}=\omega(\bar{\alpha})^{a+2b-k_{2}+2}
for every
\bar{\alpha}\in\Delta
,

we have
d\left(S_{k_{1}}(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}},m-4\right)=d\left(S_{k_{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(Np),\psi)_{\mathfrak{m}_{\bar{r}}},m-4\right)
.

Remark 1.4
.

(1)

The conditions
p\geq 11
and
2\leq a\leq p-5
are from one of the key ingredients, local ghost conjecture (see
[
LTXZ-2
, Theorem 1.3]
), to the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

(2)

The integer
m
in Theorem
1.3
plays the same role to the rational number
r
in Statement
1.1
.

We note that Theorem
1.3
is a direct consequence of the following theorem, which is our main technical result. See details in §
2
.

Theorem
2.3
.

Assume that
p\geq 11,

2\leq a\leq p-5
,
0\leq b\leq p-2
, and that
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{1}\times\varepsilon_{1}\omega^{k_{\varepsilon}-2}
is
\bar{\rho}
-relevant.
For any integers
m\geq 4
,
k_{1}\geq 2
and
k_{2}\geq 2
such that
k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\equiv k_{\varepsilon}\pmod{p-1}
and

k_{1}\equiv k_{2}\pmod{p^{m}}
, the multiset of
U_{p}
-slopes with slope value
\leq m-4
of
G_{\bar{\rho}}^{(\varepsilon)}(w_{k_{1}},-)
is same to the one of
G_{\bar{\rho}}^{(\varepsilon)}(w_{k_{2}},-)
.

It is important to clarify that our paper does not conclusively prove the Gouvêa–Mazur conjecture, instead indicating a discrepancy of an additive factor of
-4
. In contrast, the example offered by Buzzard and Calegari shows a deviation of an additive factor of
-1
. By synthesizing these two findings, we have managed to reduce the accurate factor in our studied case to the interval
[-4,-1]
. We trust that with a more rigorous estimation for (
3.7
) or Lemma
3.8
, we can confine the factor even further within a smaller interval.

Lemma 4.1

d_{k}^{\mathrm{ur}}=n_{0}
, then
v_{p}(k-k_{0})=0
, a contradiction. This completes the proof of (
4.10
).

Combining (
4.10
) with the assumption on
k_{0}
in this proposition, we obtain that for every
k\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}\backslash\{k_{0}\}
, we have

(4.11)

v_{p}(k-\tilde{k})<m.

Note that

(4.12)

v_{p}\left(w_{k_{1}}-w_{k_{2}}\right)\geq 1\textrm{~for all~}k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathcal{K}.

Combining this with (
4.8
), we have

(4.13)

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}(v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{\tilde{k}})-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}}))\geq v_{p}(w_{k_{0}}-w_{\tilde{k}})-1+\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}(1-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}})),

and by (
3.3
),

(4.14)

k_{0\bullet}\leq\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)(p+1).

From
k^{\prime}<k_{0}
and (
4.10
), for every

k\in\mathcal{K}^{\prime}

we have

(4.15)

v_{p}(k_{\bullet}-k^{\prime}_{\bullet})\leq\log_{p}(|k_{\bullet}-k^{\prime}_{\bullet}|)\leq\log_{p}(k_{0\bullet})\leq\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)+\log_{p}(p+1).

If
k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}
, by definition, we have

d_{k}^{\mathrm{ur}}\leq n_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}d_{k}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-1
,
and hence by Lemma
3.3
(1),

n_{0}\leq k_{\bullet}\leq\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)(p+1).

Combined with (
4.15
) and Lemma
4.1
with
r=\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)+\log_{p}(p+1)
, this restriction of
k_{\bullet}
implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}(1-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}}))

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\sum_{n_{0}\leq k_{\bullet}\leq\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)(p+1)}v_{p}(k_{\bullet}-k^{\prime}_{\bullet})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)(p+1)-n_{0}+1}{p-1}\right)-\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)-\log_{p}(p+1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-\frac{n_{0}}{2}-\frac{p+2}{p-1}-\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)-\log_{p}(p+1).

Plugging it into (
4.13
), we have

(4.16)

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{+}}(v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{\tilde{k}})-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}}))\\
\geq v_{p}(w_{k_{0}}-w_{\tilde{k}})-\frac{n_{0}}{2}-\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)-\frac{2p+1}{p-1}-\log_{p}(p+1).

On the other hand, by (
4.12
) again, we have

(4.17)

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}(v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{\tilde{k}})-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}}))\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}(v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{\tilde{k}})-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}v_{p}(k-\tilde{k}).

Note that
by (
4.11
), we have

v_{p}(k-\tilde{k})=v_{p}(k-k_{0})
, and hence

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}v_{p}(k-\tilde{k})=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}v_{p}(k-k_{0})=\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}v_{p}(k_{\bullet}-k_{0\bullet}).

Since every
k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
satisfies
k<k_{0}
, we have

(4.18)

v_{p}(k-\tilde{k})\leq\log_{p}(k_{0\bullet}-k_{\bullet}).

On the other hand, for every
k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}
,
by definition, we have

\tfrac{1}{2}d_{k}^{\mathrm{Iw}}+1\leq n_{0}\leq d_{k}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-d_{k}^{\mathrm{ur}}\leq d_{k}^{\mathrm{Iw}},

and hence by Lemma
3.3
(1) again,

\frac{n_{0}}{2}-1\leq k_{\bullet}\leq n_{0}-1.

Combined with (
4.18
),
this restriction of
k_{\bullet}
implies that

(4.19)

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}v_{p}(k-\tilde{k})\leq\sum_{\frac{n_{0}}{2}-1\leq k_{\bullet}\leq n_{0}}v_{p}(k_{0\bullet}-k_{\bullet}).

By (
4.14
), we estimate
\log_{p}(k_{0\bullet}-k^{\prime}_{\bullet})
by

\log_{p}(k_{0\bullet}-k^{\prime}_{\bullet})\leq\log_{p}\left(\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)(p+1)-\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}-1\right)\right)=\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right).

Combining it with Lemma
4.1
with
r=\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right)
, we obtain

\sum_{\frac{n_{0}}{2}-1\leq k_{\bullet}\leq n_{0}-1}v_{p}(k_{0\bullet}-k_{\bullet})\leq\frac{n_{0}-\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1}{p-1}+\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right).

Plugging it into (
4.19
) and then (
4.17
), we have

\sum_{k\in\mathcal{K}^{-}}(v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{\tilde{k}})-v_{p}(w_{k}-w_{k^{\prime}}))\leq\frac{n_{0}+2}{2(p-1)}+\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right).

Plugging it with (
4.16
) into (
4.7
), we prove

(4.20)

\frac{1}{L}\left(v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}+L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)-v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)\right)\\
\begin{aligned} \geq\frac{k^{\prime}-2}{2}+v_{p}(w_{k_{0}}-w_{\tilde{k}})-\frac{n_{0}}{2}-\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)-\frac{2p+1}{p-1}-\log_{p}(p+1)&\\
-\left(\frac{n_{0}+2}{2(p-1)}+\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right)\right)&.\end{aligned}

Note that
n_{0}\leq\frac{1}{2}d^{\mathrm{Iw}}_{k^{\prime}}
. By Lemma
3.3
(1), we have
n_{0}-1\leq k_{\bullet}^{\prime}=\frac{k^{\prime}-k_{\varepsilon}}{p-1}
, and hence

k^{\prime}-2\geq k_{\varepsilon}-2+(p-1)\left(n_{0}-1\right)\geq(p-1)\left(n_{0}-1\right).

Combined with the trivial bound
k^{\prime}\geq 2
, this inequality gives

k^{\prime}\geq\max\{(p-1)(n_{0}-1),0\}+2.

Plugging it into
(
4.20
), we obtain

\frac{1}{L}\left(v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}+L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)-v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)\right)\\
\begin{aligned} \geq\frac{\max\{(p-1)(n_{0}-1),0\}}{2}+m+1-\frac{n_{0}}{2}-\log_{p}\left(\frac{n_{0}}{2}+1\right)-\frac{2p+1}{p-1}&\\
-\log_{p}(p+1)-\left(\frac{n_{0}+2}{2(p-1)}+\log_{p}\left(\frac{pn_{0}}{2}+p+2\right)\right)&.\end{aligned}

By calculation and our assumption
p\geq 11
, the term on the right hand side in the above equation reaches its minimum when
p=11
and
n_{0}=1
, which is

\displaystyle m+\frac{1}{2}-\log_{p}(\tfrac{3}{2})-\frac{2p+1}{p-1}-\log_{p}(p+1)-\left(\frac{3}{2(p-1)}+\log_{p}\left(\frac{3p}{2}+2\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4p+5}{2(p-1)}-\log_{p}(\tfrac{3}{2})-\log_{p}(p+1)-\log_{p}\left(\frac{3p}{2}+2\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle m-4.37.

Note that by (
4.7
), we have

\frac{1}{L}\left(v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}+L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)-v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)\right)\in\frac{\mathbb{Z}}{2}.

This implies that

\frac{1}{L}\left(v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}+L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)-v_{p}\left(g_{\frac{1}{2}(d_{k^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Iw}}-L),\widehat{k^{\prime}}}(w_{\tilde{k}})\right)\right)\geq m-4,

and hence
completes the proof.
∎

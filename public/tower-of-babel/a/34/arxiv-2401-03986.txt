Probabilistic derangement numbers and polynomials
Taekyun Kim, Dae San Kim

Abstract
. Let Y Y be a random variable such that the moment generating function of Y Y exists in a neighborhood of the origin. The aim of this paper is to study probabilistic versions of the derangement polynomials, the derangement polynomials of type 2 and the r r -derangement numbers, namely the probabilistic derangement polynomials associated with Y Y , the probabilistic derangement polynomials of type 2 associated with Y Y and the probabilistic r r -derangement numbers associated Y Y , respectively. We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers. In addition, we consider the special case that Y Y is the gamma random variable with parameters α , β > 0 \alpha,\beta>0 .

1.
Introduction

In combinatorics, a derangement is a permutation that has no fixed points. The number of derangements of an
n
element set is called the
n
th derangement number. For
0\leq r\leq n
, the
n
th
r
-derangement number is the number of derangements of an
n+r
element set under the restriction that the first
r
elements are in disjoint cycles.

Let
Y
be a random variable satisfying the moment condition (see (
11
)). The aim of this paper is to study the probabilistic derangement polynomials associated with
Y
, the probabilistic derangement polynomials of type 2 associated with
Y
and the probabilistic
r
-derangement numbers associated
Y
, as probabilistic versions of the derangement polynomials, the derangement polynomials of type 2 and the
r
-derangement numbers, respectively. We derive some properties, explicit expressions, certain identities and recurrence relations for those polynomials and numbers. In addition, we consider the special case that
Y
is the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta>0
.

The outline of this paper is as follows. In Section 1, we recall the derangement numbers, the
r
-derangement numbers, the derangement polynomials
D_{n}(x)
and the derangement polynomials of type 2. We remind the reader of the Fubini polynomials
F_{n}(x)
, the Stirling numbers of the first kind, the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind and the Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n\brace k}
. Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
,
E[e^{tY}]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}E[Y^{n}],\quad(|t|<r)
, exists for some
r>0
. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\,\,(k\geq 1)
,   with
S_{0}=0
. Then we recall the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
, the probabilistic Bell polynomials associated with
Y
,
\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)
, and the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\,\beta>0
. Section 2 is the main results of this paper. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1},\,\,S_{k},\,\,(k=0,1,\dots)
be as in the above. Then we first define the probabilistic derangement polynomials associated
Y
,
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
. In Theorem 2.1, we find two explicit expressions for
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
. We derive a recurrence relation for
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
in Theorem 2.2. In Theorem 2.3, we express
\phi_{n}^{Y}(1-x)
as a finite sum involving
D_{k}(x)
. We define the probabilistic Euler numbers
E_{n}^{Y}
in a natural manner. In Theorem 2.4, we express the convolution
\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\phi_{m}^{Y}(1-x)E_{n-m}^{Y}
, as an infinite sum involving
D_{m}(x)
. We define the probabilistic
r
-derangement numbers
D_{n}^{(r,Y)}
. We derive finite sum expressions for
D_{n}^{(r,Y)}
in Theorems 2.5 and 2.6. In Theorem 2.7, the
n
th moment of
Y
is expressed as a finite sum involving
D_{k+r}^{(r,Y)}
. We express
D_{n+r}^{(r,Y)}
as a finite sum involving
D_{n-l}^{Y},\,\,(0\leq l\leq n)
in Theorem 2.8. We define the probabilistic derangement polynomials of type 2,
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
. In Theorem 2.9, we find an explicit expression and a recurrence relation for
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
. In Theorem 2.10, we derive a finite sum identity which is given by
\sum_{m=0}^{n}d_{m}^{Y}(x){n\brace m}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}{m\brace l}\binom{n}{m}E[Y^{l}]F_{n-m}(x)
. We deduce an explicit expression for
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
in Theorem 2.11. Finally, when
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
, we derive an expression of
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
as a finite sum involving
D_{l}(1-x),\,\,(0\leq l\leq n)
, in Theorem 2.12. For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For
n\geq 0
, the derangement numbers
D_{n},\ (n\geq 0)
are given by

(1)

D_{n}=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(n-k)!(-1)^{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [5,6,8,10,18,19,21,24]).

From (
1
), we easily derive the following equation

(2)

\frac{1}{1-t}e^{-t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18,19,21,24,29]).

For
0\leq r\leq n
, the
r
-derangement numbers
D_{n}^{(r)}
are given by

(3)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{(r)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}e^{-t},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18,19,21,24]).

The derangement polynomials are defined by

(4)

\frac{e^{xt}}{1-t}e^{-t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [11,18,19,21,24,30]),

and the derangement polynomials of type 2 are given by

(5)

\frac{1}{1-xt}e^{-t}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [18]).

Note that

D_{n}(0)=D_{n}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad d_{n}(1)=D_{n},\ (n\geq 0).

It is well known that the Fubini polynomials are defined by

(6)

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}k!x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,16,17,20,23,30]),

where
{n\brace k}
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind given by

(7)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(e^{t}-1\big)^{t}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-32]).

From (
6
), we have

(8)

\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}F_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,17,23]).

The Stirling numbers of the first kind are given by

(9)

\frac{1}{k!}\big(\log(1+t)\big)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}S_{1}(n,k)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6-30]).

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, the unsigned Stirling numbers of the first kind are defined by

(10)

{n\brack k}=(-1)^{n-k}S_{1}(n,k),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6-30]).

Let
Y
be a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y

(11)

E\big[e^{tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r)\quad\textrm{exists for some $r>0$.}

Assume that
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually independent copies of
Y
, and that
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1),\ S_{0}=0
, (see [2,3,14,31]). The probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with random variable
Y
are defined by

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[S_{j}^{n}\big],\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [2,3,14,22,32]).

Equivalently, they are given by

(12)

\frac{1}{k!}(E[e^{tY}]-1)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n\brace k}_{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1,\ {n\brace k}_{Y}={n\brace k},\ (n\geq k\geq 0)
. The probabilistic Bell polynomials associated with random variable
Y
are defined by

(13)

\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,14,32]).

From (
13
), we have

(14)

e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

A continuous random variable
X
whose density function is given by

f(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{\beta}{\Gamma(\alpha)}e^{-\beta x}(\beta x)^{\alpha-1},&\textrm{if $x\geq 0$,}\\
0,&\textrm{if $x<0$,}\end{array}\right.

for some
\alpha,\beta>0
is said to be the gamma random variable with parameters
\alpha,\beta
, which is denoted by
X\sim\Gamma(\alpha,\beta),
(see [28,31]).

In particular, if
X\sim\Gamma(1,1)
, then we have

\displaystyle E\Big[e^{(X-1+p)t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(x-1+p)t}f(x)dx=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(x-1+p)t}e^{-x}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-t}}{1-t}e^{pt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}(p)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(t<1).

Thus, we get

E\big[(X-1+p)^{n}\big]=D_{n}(p),\quad(n\geq 0).

2.
Probabilistic derangement numbers and polynomials associated with random variables

Let
(Y_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

S_{0}=0,\,\,S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\geq 1).

Now, we define the
probabilistic derangement polynomials associated with
Y
by

(15)

\frac{e^{xt}}{1-t}E\big[e^{-tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

In particular, for
x=0,\ D_{n}^{Y}=D_{n}^{Y}(0)
are called the
probabilistic derangement numbers associated with
Y
.

When
Y=1
, we have
D_{n}^{Y}(x)=D_{n}(x)
,
(n\geq 0)
. From (
15
), we have

(16)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{E\big[e^{-tY}\big]}{1-t}e^{xt}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}D_{l}^{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}D_{l}^{Y}x^{n-l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
15
), we also have

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{E\big[e^{-tY}\big]}{1-t}e^{xt}=\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e^{t(x-Y)}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}t^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[(x-Y)^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{E\big[(x-Y)^{m}\big]}{m!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
16
) and (
17
), we obtain
the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{n}^{Y}(x)=n!\sum_{m=0}^{n}\frac{E\big[(x-Y)^{m}\big]}{m!}=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}D_{l}^{Y}x^{n-l}.

From (
15
), we note that

(18)

\displaystyle E\big[e^{t(x-Y)}\big]

\displaystyle=(1-t)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big(D_{n}^{Y}(x)-nD_{n-1}^{Y}(x)\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}+1.

On the other hand, by Taylor expansion, we also get

(19)

\displaystyle E\big[e^{t(x-Y)}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E\big[(x-Y)^{n}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}E\big[(x-Y)^{n}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
18
) and (
19
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

For
n\geq 1
, we have

D_{n}^{Y}(x)-nD_{n-1}^{Y}(x)=E\big[(x-Y)^{n}\big].

In particular, for
x=0
, we get

D_{n}^{Y}-nD_{n-1}^{Y}=(-1)^{n}E[Y^{n}].

Replacing
t
by
1-E[e^{Yt}]
in (
4
), we get

(20)

\displaystyle e^{(1-x)(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{k}(x)\frac{1}{k!}\big(1-E[e^{Yt}]\big)^{k}E[e^{Yt}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{k}(x)(-1)^{k}\sum_{l=k}^{\infty}{l\brace k}_{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{l}D_{k}(x)(-1)^{l}{l\brace k}_{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{n}{l}{l\brace k}_{Y}D_{k}(x)(-1)^{l}E[Y^{n-l}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
14
) and (
20
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{n}^{Y}(1-x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\binom{n}{l}{l\brace k}_{Y}D_{k}(x)(-1)^{l}E[Y^{n-l}].

The
probabilistic Euler numbers
are given by

(21)

\frac{2}{E[e^{tY}]+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
,
E_{n}^{Y}=E_{n},\ (n\geq 0)
, where
E_{n}
are the ordinary Euler numbers given by

\frac{2}{e^{t}+1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E_{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [30]).

Replacing
t
by
-E[e^{tY}]
in (
4
), we have

(22)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+E[e^{Yt}]}e^{(1-x)E[e^{Yt}]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m}(x)\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}\Big(E[e^{Yt}]\Big)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m}(x)\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}E\Big[e^{(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{m})t}\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m}(x)\frac{(-1)^{m}}{m!}E[S_{m}^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
21
), we get

(23)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1+E[e^{Yt}]}e^{(1-x)E[e^{Yt}]}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\frac{2}{1+E[e^{Yt}]}e^{(1-x)(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}E_{l}^{Y}\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}^{Y}(1-x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{1-x}}{2}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\phi_{m}^{Y}(1-x)E_{n-m}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
22
) and (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}\phi_{m}^{Y}(1-x)E_{n-m}^{Y}=2e^{x-1}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{m}(x)}{m!}(-1)^{m}E[S_{m}^{n}].

For
0\leq r\leq n
, we consider the
probabilistic
r
-derangement numbers associated with
Y
, which are defined by

(24)

\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}D_{n}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
,
D_{n}^{(r,Y)}=D_{n}^{(r)},\ (n\geq 0)
. From (
24
), we have

(25)

\displaystyle\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+r}{r}t^{k+r}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}\binom{k}{r}t^{k}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k}{r}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{E[Y^{n-k}]}{(n-k)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
24
) and (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n\geq r\geq 0
, we have

D_{n}^{(r,Y)}=n!\sum_{k=r}^{n}\binom{k}{r}(-1)^{n-k}\frac{E[Y^{n-k}]}{(n-k)!}.

From (
24
), we note that

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}D_{n}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{t}{1-t}\bigg)^{r}\frac{1}{1-t}E[e^{-Yt}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=r}^{\infty}\binom{l-1}{r-1}t^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m}^{Y}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=r}^{\infty}n!\sum_{l=r}^{n}\binom{l-1}{r-1}\frac{D_{n-l}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

By comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n\geq r\geq 0
, we have

D_{n}^{(r,Y)}=n!\sum_{l=r}^{n}\binom{l-1}{r-1}\frac{D_{n-l}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}.

By (
24
), we get

(27)

\displaystyle E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{r+1}\sum_{k=r}^{\infty}D_{k}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k-r}}{k!}

\displaystyle=(1-t)^{r+1}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{k+r}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k}}{(k+r)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}\binom{r+1}{l}t^{l}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}D_{k+r}^{(r,Y)}\frac{t^{k}}{(k+r)!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{D_{k+r}^{(r,Y)}}{(k+r)!}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{r+1}{n-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
27
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.7
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

E\big[Y^{n}\big]=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{D_{k+r}^{(r,Y)}}{(k+r)!}(-1)^{k}\binom{r+1}{n-k}.

Now, we observe that

(28)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t^{r}}\frac{t^{r}}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e^{-tY}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{n+r}^{(r,Y)}}{(n+r)!}t^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{n+r}^{(r,Y)}}{\binom{n+r}{r}r!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by binomial expansion, we get

(29)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-t)^{r+1}}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=\bigg(\frac{1}{1-t}\bigg)^{r}\frac{1}{1-t}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\binom{r+l-1}{l}t^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}D_{m}^{Y}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{r+l-1}{l}\frac{D_{n-l}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
28
) and (
29
), we get the following theorem.

Theorem 2.8
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{n+r}^{(r,Y)}=(n+r)!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{r+l-1}{l}\frac{D_{n-l}^{Y}}{(n-l)!}.

Now, we define the
probabilistic derangement polynomials of type 2 associated with
Y
as

(30)

\frac{1}{1-xt}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

When
Y=1
, we have
d_{n}^{Y}(x)=d_{n}(x),\ (n\geq 0)
. In particular, for
x=1
,
d_{n}^{Y}(1)=D_{n}^{Y},\ (n\geq 0)
.

From (
30
), we have

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-xt}E[e^{-Yt}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}x^{m}t^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E[Y^{k}]t^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E\big[Y^{k}\big]x^{n-k}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

and

(32)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]=(1-xt)\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=d_{0}^{Y}(x)+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigg(d_{n}^{Y}(x)-nxd_{n-1}^{Y}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Thus, by (
32
), we get

(33)

d_{0}^{Y}(x)=1,\quad d_{n}^{Y}(x)=nxd_{n-1}^{Y}(x)+(-1)^{n}E[Y^{n}],\quad(n\geq 1).

Therefore, by (
31
) and (
33
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.9
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

d_{n}^{Y}(x)=n!\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}E[Y^{k}]x^{n-k}.

Moreover, we have

d_{0}^{Y}(x)=1,\quad d_{n}^{Y}(x)=nxd_{n-1}^{Y}(x)+(-1)^{n}E[Y^{n}],\quad(n\geq 1).

Replacing
t
by
e^{t}-1
in (
30
), we get

(34)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}E\big[e^{-Y(e^{t}-1)}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}d_{m}^{Y}(x)\frac{1}{m!}(e^{t}-1)^{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}d_{m}^{Y}(x){n\brace m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, by (
8
), we obtain

(35)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-x(e^{t}-1)}E\big[e^{-Y(e^{t}-1)}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}E[Y^{l}]\frac{(e^{t}-1)^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}E[Y^{l}]\sum_{m=l}^{\infty}{m\brace l}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}F_{k}(x)\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}E[Y^{l}]{m\brace l}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}\binom{n}{m}{m\brace l}E[Y^{l}]F_{n-m}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
34
) and (
35
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.10
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\sum_{m=0}^{n}d_{m}^{Y}(x){n\brace m}=\sum_{m=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{m}(-1)^{l}{m\brace l}\binom{n}{m}E[Y^{l}]F_{n-m}(x).

From (
30
), we note that

(36)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}d_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=e^{-\log(1-xt)}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}\big(\log(1-xt)\big)^{l}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{j}{j\brack l}x^{j}\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}E[Y^{m}]\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}{j\brack l}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}(-1)^{n-j}E[Y^{n-j}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.11
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

d_{n}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{j}{j\brack l}\binom{n}{j}x^{j}(-1)^{n-j}E[Y^{n-j}].

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. Then, for
t>-1
, we have

(37)

\displaystyle E\big[e^{(x-Y)t}\big]

\displaystyle=e^{xt}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-yt}e^{-y}dy=\frac{1}{1+t}e^{t}e^{(x-1)t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}D_{l}(1-x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}.

From (
37
), we have

(38)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}D_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{xt}}{1-t}E\big[e^{-Yt}\big]=\frac{1}{1-t}\frac{1}{1+t}e^{t}e^{(x-1)t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-t}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{l}D_{l}(1-x)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}D_{l}(1-x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
38
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
. For
n\geq 0
, we have

D_{n}^{Y}(x)=n!\sum_{l=0}^{n}\frac{(-1)^{l}}{l!}D_{l}(1-x).

3.
Conclusion

By means of generating functions we studied probabilistic versions of the derangement polynomials, the
r
-derangement numbers and the derangement polynomials of type 2, namely the probabilistic derangement polynomials
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
associated with
Y
, the probabilistic
r
-derangement numbers
D_{n}^{(r,Y)}
associated
Y
and the probabilistic derangement polynomials of type 2
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
associated with
Y
. Here
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin. In more detail, we derived an explicit expression for
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorem 2.1) and that in terms of derangement polynomials
D_{l}(x)
for the special case of
Y\sim\Gamma(1,1)
(see Theorem 2.12). We deduced a recurrence relation for
D_{n}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorem 2.2). We expressed
\phi_{n}^{Y}(1-x)
and the convolution of that with the probabilistic Euler numbers
E_{n}^{Y}
in terms of
D_{k}(x)
(see Theorems 2.3, 2.4). We found explicit expressions for
D_{n}^{(r,Y)}
(see Theorems 2.5, 2.6) and two identities involving those numbers (see Theorems 2.7, 2.8). We obtained explicit expressions for
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorems 2.9, 2.11), a recurrence relation for
d_{n}^{Y}(x)
(see Theorem 2.9) and an identity involving those polynomials (see Theorem 2.10).

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue to study probabilistic versions of many special polynomials and numbers and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

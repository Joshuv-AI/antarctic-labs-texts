Fingerprints of anti-Pfaffian topological order in quantum point contact transport
Jinhong Park, Christian Spånslätt, Alexander D. Mirlin

Abstract
Despite recent experimental developments, the topological order of the fractional quantum Hall state at filling ν = 5 / 2 \nu=5/2 remains an outstanding question. We study conductance and shot noise in a quantum point contact device in the charge-equilibrated regime and show that, among Pfaffian, particle-hole Praffian, and anti-Pfaffian (aPf) candidate states, the hole-conjugate aPf state is unique in that it can produce a conductance plateau at G = ( 7 / 3 ) ​ e 2 / h G=(7/3)e^{2}/h by two fundamentally distinct mechanisms. We demonstrate that these mechanisms can be distinguished by shot noise measurements on the plateaus. We also determine distinct features of the conductance of the aPf state in the coherent regime. Our results can be used to experimentally single out the aPf order.



Introduction.—
The fractional quantum Hall (FQH) state at filling
\nu=5/2

Willett et al. 1987
is the prototypical candidate for a non-Abelian phase of matter
Moore and Read 1991
. This state has attracted immense attention as a tentative platform for topological quantum computations
Nayak et al. 2008
. However, to experimentally verify the realized topological order at this filling remains an outstanding problem in condensed-matter physics
Ma and Feldman 2019
;
Ma et al. 2022
.

To describe the
\nu=5/2
state, several candidate states were proposed, most prominently the Pfaffian (Pf)
Moore and Read 1991
, anti-Pfaffian (aPf)
Levin et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2007
and particle-hole Pfaffian (phPf)
Fidkowski et al. 2013
;
Zucker and Feldman 2016
;
Antonić et al. 2018
states, all with non-Abelian orders. To date, numerical simulations favor either the aPf or Pf state
Wan et al. 2008
;
Rezayi 2017
;
Rezayi et al. 2021
, while in GaAs/AlGaAs devices, recent measurements of the thermal conductance
Banerjee et al. 2018
;
Dutta et al. 2022a
;
Paul et al. 2023
and upstream noise
Dutta et al. 2022b
point towards the phPf state, supported by edge theory
Park et al. 2020
;
Hein and Spånslätt 2023
;
Yutushui and Mross 2023
.
Moreover, despite recent observations of several even-denominator states in novel 2D materials
Falson et al. 2015
;
Zibrov et al. 2017
;
Li et al. 2017
;
Kim et al. 2019
;
Shi et al. 2020
;
Huang et al. 2022
;
Hossain et al. 2023
;
Chen et al. 2023
, detailed transport experiments at
\nu=5/2
in these materials remain elusive.

In this paper, we address the
\nu=5/2
conundrum by analyzing edge transport through a quantum point contact (QPC) device. Our main goal is to identify hallmarks of the aPf order related to its hole-conjugate nature, i.e., the presence of
counterpropagating
bosonic edge modes. In the regime of equilibrated charge transport, the aPf state is expected to display a highly non-trivial plateau in the two-terminal conductance,
G=7/3
(in units of
e^{2}/h
), when the QPC is continuously tuned by the corresponding gate voltage. This plateau arises when the local QPC filling factor is
\nu_{\rm QPC}=3

[Fig.
1
(a)
], i.e., is
higher
than the bulk filling
\nu_{B}=5/2
, see Ref.
Lai and Yang 2013
for a discussion of related quantum-dot and line-junction setups. Among the non-Abelian candidates, this exotic mechanism of plateau formation is operative only for the aPf state due to its unique hole-conjugate character. However,
a
G=7/3
plateau may form by another mechanism for
any

\nu=5/2
candidate state. This happens if the QPC instead
lowers
the local density to the stable FQH filling
\nu_{\rm QPC}=7/3
, i.e., for
\nu_{\rm QPC}<\nu_{B}

Spånslätt et al. 2020
. We demonstrate that these two kinds of
G=7/3
plateaus can be distinguished by electrical shot noise measurements, which thereby provide a unique fingerprint for the aPf state. Further, we explore the evolution of
the
G=7/3
plateau arising from
\nu_{\rm QPC}=3
in the aPf state in the regime of coherent charge transport, which can be reached for the lowest temperatures and bias voltages. We show that, due to disorder,
G
then generically fluctuates with changing QPC gate voltage within the range
35/17\leq G\leq 3
. Among the three non-Abelian candidate states,
G>5/2
is reachable only for the aPf state, which thus provides a complementary fingerprint of this topological order in the QPC transport.

QPC conductance plateaus.—
The unusual situation with
\nu_{\rm QPC}>\nu_{B}
is a feature common to all hole-conjugate states, i.e., for fillings satisfying
n-1/2\leq\nu<n
, with
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Indeed, all such states can be viewed as FQH liquids formed by condensation of hole-like quasiparticles on top of an integer number
n
of filled Landau levels
Haldane 1983
;
Halperin 1984
;
MacDonald 1990
. As a consequence, hole-conjugate states naturally accommodate local regions with
\nu(r)=n>\nu_{B}
[see Fig.
1
(b)
]. This property suggests the possibility of a local region with integer-valued filling
\nu_{\text{QPC}}=n
, despite the application of a negative gate voltage that normally
lowers
the local density. Having
\nu_{\rm QPC}>\nu_{B}
, together with the assumption of fully equilibrated charge transport, leads to non-trivial conductance plateaus
Sup

\displaystyle G=\frac{\nu_{B}\nu_{\text{QPC}}-(2\nu_{B}-\nu_{\text{QPC}})\nu_{T}}{2\nu_{\text{QPC}}-\nu_{B}-\nu_{T}},\,\,\,\text{for}\,\,\,\nu_{\text{QPC}}>\nu_{B}\,,

(1)

see Fig.

Here,
\nu_{T}
is the total filling factor discontinuity associated with fully transmitted modes (without any coupling to the other modes). Equation (
1

Nakamura et al. 2023
;
Fauzi et al. 2023
;
Yan et al. 2023
. In particular, for the
\nu_{B}=2/3
state (with
\nu_{\text{QPC}}=1
and
\nu_{T}=0
), a
G=1/2
plateau was recently observed
Nakamura et al. 2023
;
Fauzi et al. 2023
. Also FQH states in higher Landau levels were observed to display non-trivial plateaus classified by Eq. (

Yan et al. 2023
.

Crucially, among the
\nu=5/2
candidate states, it is only the aPf state that is hole-conjugate. Hence, only the aPf can produce a conductance plateau by the mechanism governing Eq. (

\nu_{B}=5/2

\nu_{T}=2

\nu_{\text{QPC}}=3

However, conductance plateaus may also arise for a reduced density in the QPC region

\displaystyle G=\nu_{\text{QPC}},\quad\text{for}\,\,\,\nu_{\text{QPC}}<\nu_{B}\,.

(2)

In contrast to the plateaus (
1

Bhattacharyya et al. 2019

We see that, according to Eq. (

\nu_{\text{QPC}}=7/3

APf edge theory.—
The edge consists of three modes in the second Landau level: two counter-propagating bosonic modes
\phi_{1}

(red solid lines in Fig.
2
) and
\phi_{\frac{1}{2}}
(blue solid lines) associated with the filling factor discontinuities
\delta\nu=1
and
\delta\nu=-1/2
, respectively,
as well as one charge-neutral Majorana mode
\chi=\chi^{\dagger}
(blue dashed lines)
Levin et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2007
. We disregard two integer modes of the lowest Landau levels, assuming that they are decoupled and simply give a contribution 2 to
G
. The edge action is
S=\int dt(\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\text{dis}})
with

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}=\int\frac{dx}{4\pi}\big[\big(-\partial_{x}\phi_{1}(\partial_{t}+v_{1}\partial_{x})\phi_{1}+2\partial_{x}\phi_{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{t}-v_{\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{x})\phi_{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-2v_{\text{int}}\partial_{x}\phi_{1}\partial_{x}\phi_{\frac{1}{2}}\big)-i\chi(\partial_{t}-v_{M}\partial_{x})\chi\big]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{dis}}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}\int dx\chi(x)\big(\xi(x)e^{i(\phi_{1}+2\phi_{\frac{1}{2}})}-h.c.\big)\,.

(3)

Here,
v_{1}
,
v_{\frac{1}{2}}
, and
v_{M}
are the mode speeds,
v_{\rm int}
is the inter-mode interaction strength, and
a
is an ultraviolet length cutoff. The term
\mathcal{L}_{\text{dis}}
describes disorder-induced electron tunneling with the random complex amplitude
\xi(x)
. The scaling dimension of the disorder term evaluated with respect to
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is
\Delta=1/2+(3/2-2x)/\sqrt{1-2x^{2}}
, where
x=v_{\text{int}}/(v_{1}+v_{\frac{1}{2}})
.

When
\mathcal{L}_{\text{dis}}
is relevant in the renormalization group (RG) sense, i.e., for
\Delta<3/2

Giamarchi and Schulz 1988
, the edge is driven to a disordered fixed point where
\Delta=1

;
Lee et al. 2007
. At this point, the edge hosts three decoupled modes: one charge mode
\phi_{c}\equiv\sqrt{2}(\phi_{1}+\phi_{\frac{1}{2}})
, one neutral mode
\phi_{n}\equiv\phi_{1}+2\phi_{\frac{1}{2}}
, and the remaining Majorana mode
\chi
. These are described by
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fix}}=\mathcal{L}_{c}+\mathcal{L}_{n}
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c}=\int\frac{dx}{4\pi}[-\partial_{x}\phi_{c}(\partial_{t}+v_{c}\partial_{x})\phi_{c}]\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{n}=\int dx\Big[\frac{1}{4\pi}\partial_{x}\phi_{n}(\partial_{t}-\bar{v}_{n}\partial_{x})\phi_{n}-i\chi(\partial_{t}-\bar{v}_{n}\partial_{x})\chi

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi a}}\left(\xi(x)e^{i\phi_{n}}-h.c.\right)\chi\,\Big]\,.

(4)

Near the fixed point, all possible terms (e.g.,
\phi_{c}\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}\phi_{n}
interactions and the velocity anisotropy) are RG irrelevant
Levin et al. 2007
;
Lee et al. 2007
. Still, these terms cause decoherence which leads to inter-mode equilibration governed by the characteristic charge equilibration length
\ell_{\text{eq}}^{c}\sim T^{-2}
, with
T
the temperature. This length defines two distinct charge transport regimes: the
coherent
regime
L\ll\ell_{\text{eq}}^{c}
and the
incoherent
regime
L\gg\ell_{\text{eq}}^{c}

denotes the length of the arms of the QPC (see Fig.
2
). In the following, we separately discuss transport properties in these two regimes.

Shot noise in the incoherent regime.—
We now show that the two types of
G=7/3
plateaus for the aPf generate very distinct (dc) shot noise characteristics as they correspond to two distinct QPC configurations: (i)
\nu_{\rm{QPC}}=3>\nu_{B}=5/2

\nu_{\rm{QPC}}=7/3<\nu_{B}

We therefore start by establishing the relevant hierarchy of these length scales. Motivated by recent experimental observations in both GaAs/AlGaAs and graphene devices, we assume

\displaystyle\ell_{\text{eq}}^{c}\ll L_{\text{QPC}}\ll\ell_{\text{eq}}^{h}\ll L.

To describe incoherent transport along a FQH edge segment, we model it as a line junction consisting of multiple counter-propagating edge modes. Edge mode

\delta\nu_{i}

In the incoherent regime, we can define a local, effective electrochemical potential (related to a local voltage) and a local, effective temperature for each individual mode. These local quantities are determined by solving a set of transport equations defined below in Eq. (
S5
) and (

To simplify the problem, we next assume that modes propagating in the same direction share the same local voltage and temperature. \nu_{d}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_{d}}\nu_{i}
and the central charge

\nu_{u}=\sum_{i=n_{d}+1}^{n_{d}+n_{u}}\nu_{i}
and

,

,

, and

In this section, we compute the noise on the conductance plateau

The geometry of the arms of the QPC is essentially identical to the line junction setup in Fig.
S1
considered in Sec.

, upon the identification
\nu_{d}=1
,
\nu_{u}=1/2
,
c_{d}=1
, and

We compute the voltages

,

,

,

,

T_{R}
,

in Fig.

by solving the differential equations (

) and (

) for the electric and energy current, respectively, along each edge segment in the device. As boundary conditions for the two contacts we use
V_{S}=V
,
V_{D}=0

\displaystyle V_{T}=\frac{2V}{3},\quad V_{R}=\frac{2V}{3},\quad V_{B}=\frac{V}{3},\quad V_{L}=\frac{V}{3}\,.

(S25)

Let us now focus on regime (i), i.e.,
\ell_{\text{eq}}^{h}\ll L

SA
) and (
S13
), we obtain the following relations between the temperatures
T_{U}
,
T_{D}
,

\displaystyle T_{T}^{2}=T_{L}^{2}+\frac{(c_{d}+c_{u})}{18c_{d}c_{u}L_{0}}V^{2}\,,\quad T_{R}^{2}=\frac{c_{d}}{c_{u}}T_{U}^{2}\,,\quad T_{B}^{2}=T_{R}^{2}+\frac{(c_{d}+c_{u})}{18c_{d}c_{u}L_{0}}V^{2}\,,\quad T_{L}^{2}=\frac{c_{d}}{c_{u}}T_{B}^{2}\,,

In regime (ii), i.e., for

We next compute the noise by applying Eq. (

) to the arm regions by identifying
G_{d}=e^{2}/(2h)
and
G_{u}=0

\displaystyle\Delta V_{T}=\frac{\Delta V_{L}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\Delta V_{B}=\frac{\Delta V_{R}}{2}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{e^{2}}{2h}(\Delta V_{T}-\Delta V_{R})=\delta I_{\text{line},\text{RU}}\,,

\displaystyle\quad\frac{e^{2}}{2h}(\Delta V_{B}-\Delta V_{L})=\delta I_{\text{line},\text{LD}}

3
in the main text), located at the upper, right (lower, left) corner, respectively.
Then, the total current fluctuations measured in the drain

\displaystyle\Delta I

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{2}}{h}(\Delta V_{T}-\Delta V_{B})=\frac{2}{3}(\delta I_{\text{line},\text{RU}}-\delta I_{\text{line},\text{LD}})\,,

(S31)

and thus the noise measured in

\displaystyle S=\overline{(\Delta I)^{2}}=\frac{4}{9}(\overline{\delta I_{\text{line},\text{RU}}^{2}}+\overline{\delta I_{\text{line},\text{LD}}^{2}})=\frac{8}{9}S_{\text{NS}}\,.

(S32)

In the final equality, we used
\overline{\delta I_{\text{line},\text{RU}}^{2}}=\overline{\delta I_{\text{line},\text{LD}}^{2}}

to the total noise

measured in
D
, as discussed in the main text. Next, by using Eq. (

in the two distinct thermally equilibrated regimes (i) and (ii). In the thermally equilibrated regime (i), we obtain the noise

\displaystyle S=\frac{8}{9}S_{\text{NS}}\approx 0.12\frac{e^{3}V}{h}\,.

(S33)

A corresponding Fano factor can be defined as

\displaystyle F\equiv\frac{S}{2eI_{\text{imp}}T_{\text{eff}}(1-T_{\text{eff}})}\approx 0.53\,,

(S34)

upon identification of the effective transmission
T_{\text{eff}}=(1/3)/(1/2)=2/3
of the edge modes in the second Landau level, and the impinging current
I_{\text{imp}}=e^{2}V/(2h)
in the incoherent regime. The definition (
S34
) has been used in several QPC experiments in the FQH regime, see e.g., Refs.
Bid et al. 2009
;
Bhattacharyya et al. 2019
;
Biswas et al. 2022

\displaystyle S=\frac{8}{9}S_{\text{NS}}\approx 0.029\frac{e^{3}V}{h}\,,\quad F\approx 0.13\,.

(S35)

This value of

is given as the green, dashed line in Fig.

in the main text.
We stress that the Fano factor (

heats the system to an effective temperature
\Delta T\sim V
, and this elevated temperature leads to noise

.

We finish this section by commenting on the noise on the conductance plateau produced for
\nu_{B}>\nu_{\text{QPC}}
. As shown in the main text, the noise on this plateau scales as

does not
agree with Ref.
Manna et al. 2023b
, in which the noise approaches a constant value in the limit of no thermal equilibration, see Eq. (38) in Ref.
Manna et al. 2023b

We now identify the two values of

\displaystyle H_{\theta=\pi,\text{fix}}=

\displaystyle\int_{x<\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}L/2}\frac{dx}{4\pi}[v_{1}(\partial_{x}\phi_{1})^{2}+2v_{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{x}\phi_{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}]+\int_{x>L/2}\frac{dx}{4\pi}[v_{1}(\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi}_{1})^{2}+2v_{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial_{x}\tilde{\phi}_{\frac{1}{2}})^{2}]

\displaystyle+\int_{|x|<L/2}\frac{dx}{4\pi}[v_{c}(\partial_{x}\phi_{c})^{2}+\bar{v}_{n}(\partial_{x}\phi_{n})^{2}]+\int_{-\infty}^{L/2}dx\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}i\bar{v}_{n}\chi\partial_{x}\chi\right)+\int_{L/2}^{\infty}dx\left(\scalebox{0.75}[1.0]{$-$}i\bar{v}_{n}\tilde{\chi}\partial_{x}\tilde{\chi}\right)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{c}^{\text{(ii)}}\\
\phi_{n}^{\text{(ii)}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{T}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\text{(i)}}\\

\sqrt{2}\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(iii)}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{T}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{c}^{\text{(ii)}}\\
\phi_{n}^{\text{(ii)}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\penalty\ \mathcal{T}=\frac{1}{T}\begin{pmatrix}1&R\\
R&1\end{pmatrix}\,,

(S38)

with

. These values come from the transformation between the “bare mode” basis and the “charge-neutral” basis. For
\theta=\pi

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{c}^{\text{(ii)}}\\
\phi_{n}^{\text{(ii)}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{T}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}^{\text{(i)}}\\
\sqrt{2}\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(i)}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{\phi}_{1}^{\text{(iii)}}\\
\sqrt{2}\tilde{\phi}_{1/2}^{\text{(iii)}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{T}^{-1}_{\theta=\pi}\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{c}^{\text{(ii)}}\\
\phi_{n}^{\text{(ii)}}\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\penalty\ \mathcal{T}_{\theta=\pi}=\frac{1}{T}\begin{pmatrix}1&-R\\

Note that for
\theta=\pi

\mathcal{T}_{\theta=\pi}\equiv\sigma_{z}\mathcal{T}\sigma_{z}

captures the additional minus sign that
\phi_{n}
accumulates in the disordered region (ii). At the same time,
\phi_{c}
remains unaffected. Next, we combine
\mathcal{T}
with
\mathcal{T}_{\theta=\pi}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{T}\begin{pmatrix}1&Re^{i\theta}\\

(S40)

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{\text{tot}}=\mathcal{T}_{\theta}^{-1}\mathcal{T}_{\text{dyn}}\mathcal{T}\,,\quad\text{with}\,\,\,\mathcal{T}_{\text{dyn}}=\begin{pmatrix}e^{i\omega L/v_{c}}&0\\

bridges the modes in region (i) and the modes in the region (iii), and hence connects the incoming modes
\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(i)}}
and
\phi_{1}^{\text{(iii)}}
, from
|x|\rightarrow\infty
to the outgoing modes,
\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(iii)}}
and
\phi_{1}^{\text{(i)}}
, propagating to
|x|\rightarrow\infty

\mathcal{T}_{\text{dyn}}

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{c}(x=0)\\

\sqrt{2}\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(i)}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

\langle\phi_{1}^{\text{(iii)}}(\omega,0)\phi_{1}^{\text{(iii)}}(-\omega,0)\rangle=\pi/\omega
, and
\phi_{1/2}^{\text{(i)}}
and
\phi_{1}^{\text{(iii)}}

), we are ready to perform the RG analysis close to

\displaystyle S_{n}=\frac{1}{2}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{|\phi_{n}(\omega,x=0)|^{2}}{|g_{n}^{\theta=0}(\omega,x=0)|}-\int d\tau\frac{2i\theta\bar{v}_{n}}{\sqrt{\pi a}}\sin(\phi_{n}(x=0,\tau))\chi(x=0,\tau)\,.

\displaystyle\frac{d\theta}{d\ln\mathcal{L}}=\left(1-\Delta_{\theta=0}\right)\theta\,,\quad\Delta_{\theta=0}=\left(\frac{T^{2}(1+R^{2})}{2(1+R^{4}-2R^{2}\cos(\omega\tau))}+\frac{1}{2}\right)\,.

(S46)

Here, the running length scale

. Furthermore,
\Delta_{\theta=0}

SF
Conductance range in the QPC geometry

In this section, we use a thermodynamic argument to show that the two terminal conductance
G
, for the QPC configuration depicted in Fig.
2
in the main text, is bound to range from
G=2+1/17=35/17
to
G=2+1=3
(in units of
e^{2}/h
). These bounds agree with the results of calculation for QPC conductance at fixed points, see Eq. (
Fingerprints of anti-Pfaffian topological order in quantum point contact transport
) of the main text and Sec.
SE
of this Supplemental Material.

We begin with considering a setup shown in Fig.
S4
(a)
. In contrast with the previous setups considered in this paper, the
\delta\nu=1
and
\delta\nu=-1/2
modes are here each connected to fully independent reservoirs, characterized by the voltages
V_{1,\text{L}}
,
V_{1,\text{R}}
,
V_{2,\text{L}}
, and
V_{2,\text{R}}
. The upper and lower edge modes are completely disconnected to each other.
The currents collected in the contact are then given by (we set here
e^{2}/h=1
)

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I_{1,\text{L}}\\
I_{2,\text{L}}\\
I_{2,\text{R}}\\
I_{1,\text{R}}\end{pmatrix}=\mathcal{G}\begin{pmatrix}V_{1,\text{L}}\\
V_{2,\text{L}}\\
V_{2,\text{R}}\\
V_{1,\text{R}}\end{pmatrix}\,,\quad\text{with}\,\,\mathcal{G}=\begin{pmatrix}-1&g_{\text{D}}&0&1-g_{\text{D}}\\
g_{\text{U}}&-1/2&1/2-g_{\text{U}}&0\\
0&1/2-g_{\text{D}}&-1/2&g_{\text{D}}\\
1-g_{\text{U}}&0&g_{\text{U}}&-1\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S57)

Here,
g_{\text{U}}
and
g_{\text{D}}
are dimensionless coefficients that characterize back-scattering on the upper and lower edges, respectively. The total Joule heating power dissipated in all contacts is then given by
P=\sum_{i}I_{i}V_{i}=\sum_{i,j}\mathcal{G}_{ij}V_{i}V_{j}=\sum_{i,j}(\mathcal{G}_{ij}+\mathcal{G}_{ji})V_{i}V_{j}/2
. The condition
P>0
enforces that the symmetrized conductance matrix
\mathcal{G}_{s}\equiv(\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{G}^{T})/2
must be positive semi-definite, i.e., it has only non-negative eigenvalues. Explicitly, the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{G}_{s}
read
0,g_{\text{U}}+g_{\text{D}},(3-(g_{\text{U}}+g_{\text{D}})\pm\sqrt{1+(g_{\text{U}}+g_{\text{D}})^{2}})/2
. The condition of positive semi-definite
\mathcal{G}_{s}
constrains
0\leq g_{\text{U}}+g_{\text{D}}\leq 4/3
. Since
g_{\text{U}}
and
g_{\text{D}}
are fully independent, they can in principle be tuned independently, which leads to
0\leq g_{\text{U}},g_{\text{D}}\leq 2/3
.

We note here that the constraint

, explicitly predicts that Andreev reflection-like behavior can occur in this system. To see this, we consider the single line junction setup depicted in Fig.

(a)
. When the
\delta\nu=1
and
\delta\nu=-1/2

V_{1}

and

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}I_{1}\\

V_{2}\end{pmatrix}\,,\,\,\text{with}\,\,\mathcal{G}=\begin{pmatrix}1-g&g\\

(S58)

Here,

is a dimensionless conductance parameter that follows the condition

as above. From Secs.

-

SG
Kubo formula

In this section, we use linear response theory to derive a Kubo formula for the electric conductance of a FQH edge segment involving multiple edge modes. The derived form of Kubo formula is used in Sec.
SE
to calculate the QPC conductance in the coherent regime.

We consider
N
“bare” edge modes described by the bosonic fields
\hat{\phi}_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq N
. The generic two-terminal conductance setup consists of three parts: (i) a left lead for
x<L/2
, (ii) the actual system in
|x|<L/2
, and (iii) the right lead
x>L/2
. The clean edge Hamiltonian

\displaystyle H_{0}=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int dx\;u_{ij}\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}_{j}(x),

(S61)

includes both the kinetic terms with velocities
v_{i}\equiv u_{ii}
and short-range density-density interactions
u_{ij}
for
i\neq j
.

We next analyze the electric current flowing in response to the time-dependent voltage
V(x,t)
. To properly model a two-terminal setup, we take this voltage to be constant in
x
in the lead regions, i.e.,
\partial_{x}V(x,t)=0
for
|x|>L/2
. More specifically, we take
V(x,t)=V_{L}(t)
for
x<-L/2
and
V(x,t)=V_{R}(t)
for
x>L/2
. The voltage couples to the total, local charge densities

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)\,,

(S62)

by adding to
\hat{H}_{0}
the term

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{V}(t)

\displaystyle=-e\int dx\hat{\rho}(x)V(x,t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{e}{2\pi}\int dx\partial_{x}\hat{\phi}_{i}V(x,t)=-\frac{e}{2\pi}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx(-\partial_{x}V(x,t))\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{\phi}_{i}(x)\,.

(S63)

Within linear response, the average electric current
j(x,t)
is given by

\displaystyle j(x,t)=-i\int dt^{\prime}\theta(t-t^{\prime})\langle[\hat{j}(x,t),\hat{H}_{V}(t^{\prime})]\rangle_{\hat{H}_{0}}=\frac{ie}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{\prime}\theta(t-t^{\prime})(-\partial_{x^{\prime}}V(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\langle[\hat{j}(x,t),\hat{\phi}_{i}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})]\rangle_{H_{0}}\,,

(S64)

from which we can identify the conductivity kernel
\sigma(x,x^{\prime};t-t^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle\sigma(x,x^{\prime};t-t^{\prime})=\frac{ie}{2\pi}\theta(t-t^{\prime})\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle[\hat{j}(x,t),\hat{\phi}_{i}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\rangle_{H_{0}}\,.

(S65)

Here, we used the definition of the conductivity kernel

\displaystyle j(x,t)\equiv\int_{-L/2}^{L/2}dx^{\prime}\int dt^{\prime}\sigma(x,x^{\prime};t-t^{\prime})(-\partial_{x^{\prime}}V(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}))\,.

(S66)

In the frequency domain, the conductivity kernel (
S65
) becomes

\displaystyle\sigma(x,x^{\prime};\omega)=-\frac{e}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\omega_{1}-\omega+i\eta}\langle[\hat{j}(x,\omega_{1}),\hat{\phi}_{i}(x^{\prime},-\omega_{1})]\rangle_{H_{0}}\,.

(S67)

Importantly,
\sigma(x,x^{\prime})
does not depend on
x
and
x^{\prime}
in the dc limit (
\omega\rightarrow 0
) due to charge conservation. This independence allows us to move
\sigma
outside the integration over
x^{\prime}
in Eq. (
S66
), which results in the formula

\displaystyle j(\omega)=\sigma(\omega)(V_{L}(\omega)-V_{R}(\omega))\,.

(S68)

In the dc limit
\omega\rightarrow 0
, the dc conductance
G
becomes

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\equiv\lim_{\omega\rightarrow 0}\sigma(\omega)=-\frac{e}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\omega_{1}+i\eta}\langle[\hat{j}(x,\omega_{1}),\hat{\phi}_{i}(x^{\prime},-\omega_{1})]\rangle_{H_{0}}\,.

(S69)

Since again
G
does not depend on
x
and
x^{\prime}
, we can use this freedom to choose
x
and
x^{\prime}
as we wish and thus choose
x=x^{\prime}=0
. Finally, we identify the current operator,
\hat{j}
, from the continuity equation
\partial_{t}\hat{\rho}+\partial_{x}\hat{j}=0
as

\displaystyle\hat{j}(x,t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{\partial_{t}\hat{\phi}_{i}}{2\pi}\,\xrightarrow{FT}\hat{j}(x,\omega)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{i\omega}{(2\pi)}\hat{\phi}_{i}(x,\omega)\,,

(S70)

which upon insertion into Eq. (
S69
) relates
G
to bosonic correlation functions via

\displaystyle G=\frac{ie^{2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{\omega_{1}}{\omega_{1}+i\eta}\langle[\hat{\phi}_{i}(x=0,\omega_{1}),\hat{\phi}_{j}(x=0,-\omega_{1})]\rangle_{H_{0}}\,.

(S71)

The remaining procedure to obtain
G
is thus to compute the local correlation function
\langle[\hat{\phi}_{i}(x=0,\omega_{1}),\hat{\phi}_{j}(x=0,\omega_{1})]\rangle_{H_{0}}
. This can be done by setting up transfer matrices that connect regions (i)-(iii). These matrices permit us to write
\hat{\phi}_{i}(x=0,\omega_{1})
in terms of the bosonic modes incoming from
|x|\rightarrow\infty
. In turn, this relation allows us to express the local correlation functions in terms of correlation functions of the incoming bosonic modes. We do this explicitly in Sec.
SE
above.

Which Metrics Are Consistent with a Given Pseudo-Hermitian Matrix?
Joshua Feinberg, Miloslav Znojil

Abstract
Given a diagonalizable N × N N\times N matrix 𝑯 \boldsymbol{H} , whose non-degenerate spectrum consists of p p pairs of complex conjugate eigenvalues and additional N − 2 ​ p N-2p real eigenvalues, we determine all metrics 𝑴 \boldsymbol{M} , of all possible signatures, with respect to which 𝑯 \boldsymbol{H} is pseudo-hermitian. In particular, we show that any compatible 𝑴 \boldsymbol{M} must have p p pairs of opposite eigenvalues in its spectrum so that p p is the minimal number of both positive and negative eigenvalues of 𝑴 \boldsymbol{M} . We provide explicit parametrization of the space of all admissible metrics and show that it is topologically a p p -dimensional torus tensored with an appropriate power of the group ℤ 2 {\mathbb{Z}}_{2} .

\langle{\bf A}_{1}|\boldsymbol{H}{\bf A}_{2}\rangle_{M}=\langle\boldsymbol{H}{\bf A}_{1}|{\bf A}_{2}\rangle_{M}\,,

II
Solving for
\boldsymbol{M}

We shall concentrate on finding invertible solutions
\boldsymbol{M}
to (

I

\boldsymbol{H}

\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{S}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{m}\boldsymbol{S}.

(8)

Thus,
\boldsymbol{m}

are congruent, and by Sylvester’s Law of Inertia

By substituting (

or equivalently, by taking the

\boldsymbol{m}
into four blocks as

\boldsymbol{m}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\boldsymbol{m}_{RR}&\boldsymbol{m}_{RC}\\
\boldsymbol{m}_{RC}^{\dagger}&\boldsymbol{m}_{CC}\end{array}\right)

(11)

Upon substituting in (
10

\mu,\nu\in R

IV
Discussion

Each choice of an element
\boldsymbol{M}\in{\mathcal{M}}
corresponds to an inner product vector space
{\mathcal{V}}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
, possibly of indefinite signature, which accommodates the given PH matrix
\boldsymbol{H}
as an element of the linear space (with real coefficients) of PH matrices which act on its vectors. Each such PH matrix

\boldsymbol{\Phi}
can be expressed uniquely
FR-review
;
FR-B
;
carlson
as
3
3

3

This result appeared in the physics literature first in
JK
, in the context of SQH matrices.

\boldsymbol{\Phi}=\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{M},\quad\boldsymbol{A}^{\dagger}=\boldsymbol{A},

(26)

provided
\det\boldsymbol{M}\neq 0
, which is what we assume in the first place.

The special case in which both
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{M}
are real symmetric matrices leads to the real pseudo-symmetric matrices constructed in
KA
. On the other hand, a generic diagonalizable real matrix
\boldsymbol{H}
, whose spectrum automatically consists of either real or pairs of complex conjugate eigenvalues, will be diagonalized by a complex matrix
\boldsymbol{S}
as in (
3
), and is of no exception to the general analysis in this paper. As was observed in
KA
, such an
\boldsymbol{H}
fulfills the intertwining relation
\boldsymbol{H}^{T}(\boldsymbol{S}^{T}\boldsymbol{S})=(\boldsymbol{S}^{T}\boldsymbol{S})\boldsymbol{H}
, meaning that
\boldsymbol{H}
and
\boldsymbol{H}^{T}
are similar. However,
\boldsymbol{S}^{T}\boldsymbol{S}
is in general not hermitian and therefore cannot serve as metric.

For the special case in which
\boldsymbol{H}
is SQH, that is, has purely real spectrum of eigenvalues (
4
), we have
r=N
,
p=0
and (
25
) becomes simply
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{N}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{\rm global}
. The simplest element in this set obviously corresponds to
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}=\boldsymbol{1}_{N}
, in which case we obtain from (
24
) the well-known factorization
\boldsymbol{M}=\boldsymbol{S}^{\dagger}\boldsymbol{S}
. (Cf. Eq.(13) in
Mostafazadeh1
, or equivalently, Eq.(22) in
Mostafazadeh2
.)

However, note that even for this special case of an SQH matrix
\boldsymbol{H}
, there are exponentially many more possibilities for
\boldsymbol{m}_{0}
with all possible sign assignment, all of which lead to vector spaces
{\mathcal{V}}_{\boldsymbol{M}}
of indefinite metric, which accommodate
\boldsymbol{H}
as an observable.

As a final remark for this paper, the next step along the lines presented here, is to extend our results to non-diagonalizable matrices
\boldsymbol{H}
with a given Jordan form.

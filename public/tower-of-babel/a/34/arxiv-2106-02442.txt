Large mass rigidity for a liquid drop model in 2D with kernels of finite moments
Benoit Merlet, Marc Pegon

Abstract
. Motivated by Gamow’s liquid drop model in the large mass regime, we consider an isoperimetric problem in which the standard perimeter P ⁡ ( E ) P(E) is replaced by P ⁡ ( E ) − γ ​ P ε ​ ( E ) P(E)-\gamma P_{\varepsilon}(E) , with 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 and P ε P_{\varepsilon} a nonlocal energy such that P ε ​ ( E ) → P ​ ( E ) P_{\varepsilon}(E)\to P(E) as ε \varepsilon vanishes. We prove that unit area minimizers are disks for ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 small enough. More precisely, we first show that in dimension 2 2 , minimizers are necessarily convex, provided that ε \varepsilon is small enough. In turn, this implies that minimizers have nearly circular boundaries, that is, their boundary is a small Lipschitz perturbation of the circle. Then, using a Fuglede-type argument, we prove that (in arbitrary dimension n ⩾ 2 n\geqslant 2 ) the unit ball in ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} is the unique unit-volume minimizer of the problem among centered nearly spherical sets. As a consequence, up to translations, the unit disk is the unique minimizer. This isoperimetric problem is equivalent to a generalization of the liquid drop model for the atomic nucleus introduced by Gamow, where the nonlocal repulsive potential is given by a radial, sufficiently integrable kernel. In that formulation, our main result states that if the first moment of the kernel is smaller than an explicit threshold, there exists a critical mass m 0 m_{0} such that for any m > m 0 m>m_{0} , the disk is the unique minimizer of area m m up to translations. This is in sharp contrast with the usual case of Riesz kernels, where the problem does not admit minimizers above a critical mass.

\left\lvert t\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+(n-1)\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right\rvert\leqslant Ct^{3}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u\rvert^{3}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

and

(A.2)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-(n-1)\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

Proof.

Since
\lvert E_{t}\rvert=\lvert B\rvert
, we have

n\lvert E_{t}\rvert=\int_{\partial B}(1+tu(x))^{n}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=\int_{\partial B}1\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}=n\lvert B\rvert.

Thus, writing

(1+tu(x))^{n}-\left(1+ntu(x)+\frac{(n-1)}{2}t^{2}u(x)^{2}\right)=\sum_{k=3}^{n}\binom{n}{k}t^{k}u(x)^{k},

we deduce
Equation

A.1
, choosing e.g.
t_{1}=1
and
C
depending only on
n
.
There remains to show
Equation

A.2
.
We decompose
u
in spherical harmonics

(A.3)

u=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d(k)}a_{k}^{i}(u)\,Y_{k}^{i},

so that

\lVert u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d(k)}a_{k}^{i}(u)^{2},\qquad\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}=\sum_{k=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{d(k)}l_{k}\,a_{k}^{i}(u)^{2}.

Recall that
d(0)=1
,
Y_{0}^{1}
is constant,
d(1)=n
, and that
Y_{1}^{i}
is colinear to

x\mapsto x_{i}
.
Since
l_{1}=n-1
and
l_{k}\geqslant 2n
for
k\geqslant 2
, it follows

(A.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-(n-1)\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-\frac{(n+1)}{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{1}^{i}(u)^{2}\right)-(n-1)a_{0}^{1}(u)^{2}.

On one hand,

a_{0}^{1}(u)=\frac{1}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rvert}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}u\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

so that by
Equation

A.1
, we have

(A.5)

\lvert a_{0}^{1}(u)\rvert\leqslant\frac{2t}{\lvert\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rvert}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2\sqrt{n-1}}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})},

up to choosing
t_{1}=t_{1}(n)
small enough, and using
t<t_{1}
and

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
.
On the other hand, the barycenter condition

\int_{E_{t}}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x=0

gives

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}x_{i}(1+tu(x))^{n}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{x}^{n-1}=0,\qquad\forall i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}.

Using the binomial formula again and
Y_{1}^{i}(x)=\lvert B_{1}\rvert^{-1/2}x_{i}
, we obtain, for

i\in\{1,\dotsc,n\}
,

a_{1}^{i}(u)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lvert B_{1}\rvert}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}x_{i}u(x)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{x}^{n-1}=-\frac{1}{n\sqrt{\lvert B_{1}\rvert}}\sum_{k=2}^{n}\binom{n}{k}t^{k}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}x_{i}u(x)^{k}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{x}^{n-1}.

Next, using
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and

n\lvert B_{1}\rvert=\lvert\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rvert
, we get

\lvert a_{i}(u)\rvert\leqslant\frac{2^{n}}{n}t^{2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}.

Whence, choosing
t_{1}
even smaller, but still depending only on
n
, we may assume

(A.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\penalty\ a_{1}^{i}(u)^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}.

Gathering
Equations

A.4
,
A.5
and
A.6
, we find

\frac{1}{2}\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}-(n-1)\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}^{2},

which proves
Equation

A.2
and concludes the proof.
∎

We establish a technical lemma to control terms of the form

\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{x}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{y},

by the
H^{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
norm of
u
, where
\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)
is a small perturbation of

(x-y)
, and
k_{\varepsilon}
are suitable rescalings of a nonnegative kernel.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
k:(0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
be a measurable function such that
k(r)\leqslant C_{0}r^{-(n+1)}

on
(R_{0},+\infty)
for some positive constants
C_{0}
,
R_{0}
, and

I_{k}^{1}\coloneqq\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}}\lvert x\rvert k(\lvert x\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x<+\infty.

Let us define the rescaling
k_{\varepsilon}(r)\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-(n+1)}k(\varepsilon^{-1}r)
,
r,\penalty\ \varepsilon>0
.
For
v\in{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
, we define a map
\Phi_{v}:\left(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\right)^{2}\times(0,1)^{2}
by

(A.7)

\Phi_{v}(x,y,r,\rho)=(x-y)+\Big[\big(rv(x)+(1-r)v(y))\big)x-\big(\rho v(y)+(1-\rho)v(x)\big)y\Big].

Then there exist positive constants
\overline{t}_{1}=\overline{t}_{1}(n)
,

\overline{C}_{0}=\overline{C}_{0}(n,C_{0},I_{k}^{1})
and
\overline{\varepsilon}_{6}=\overline{\varepsilon}_{6}(n,R_{0})
such that the
following holds.
For any
u\in{\mathrm{Lip}}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
such that
\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
,

\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
, and any
t\in(0,\overline{t}_{1})
, we have

(A.8)

\iint_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{x}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{y}\\
\leqslant\overline{C}_{0}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right).

Proof.

Let
\overline{t}_{1}>0
to be chosen later. In the proof, we assume that
0<t<\overline{t}_{1}
.
We work in local coordinates, proceeding in two steps.

Step 1.

Let us denote by
D_{r}(x)
the open ball of radius
r
centered at
x
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. We first show
that for any
\widetilde{u}\in{\mathrm{Lip}}(D_{2})
such that
\lVert\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}+\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}\leqslant M
, we have

(A.9)

\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\leqslant C\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x,

for all
\varepsilon>0
, provided
t<\overline{t}_{1}
, and
\overline{t}_{1}
is chosen small enough depending only on

n
and
M
, and
C=C(n,I_{k}^{1})
. Here, by a slight abuse of notation,
\Phi_{v}
denotes the map from

\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\right)^{2}\times(0,1)^{2}
whose expression is given by
Equation

A.7
.
For
v\in{\mathrm{Lip}}(D_{2})
, let us define the map
E_{v}:\left({\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\right)^{2}\times(0,1)^{2}
by

E_{v}(x,y,r,\rho)\coloneqq\big(rv(x)+(1-r)v(y))\big)x-\big(\rho v(y)+(1-\rho)v(x)\big)y,

so that
\Phi_{v}(x,y,r,\rho)=x-y+E_{v}(x,y,r,\rho)
.
Due to the
L^{\infty}
bound on
\widetilde{u}
and
\nabla\widetilde{u}
, we easily see that the
maps
E_{t\widetilde{u}}
converge uniformly to
0
on
(D_{2})^{2}\times(0,1)^{2}
as
t
vanishes.
In fact, we have

(A.10)

\begin{array}[]{rl}\left\lvert E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\right\rvert&\leqslant t\left(2\lVert\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}+\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}\right)\lvert x-y\rvert\\[4.0pt]
&\leqslant 2M\overline{t}_{1}\lvert x-y\rvert\end{array}\qquad\forall x,y\in D_{2},\,\forall r,\rho\in(0,1).

In particular, choosing
\overline{t}_{1}<1/(4M)
,

(A.11)

\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert}{2}\leqslant\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert\leqslant 2\lvert x-y\rvert,\quad\forall x,y\in D_{2},\,\forall r,\rho\in(0,1).

Integrating on lines, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Fubini’s theorem, we have

(A.12)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(\widetilde{u}(x)-\widetilde{u}(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+s(y-x))\rvert^{2}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}s\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho.

Now let us focus on the integral on
D_{2}\times D_{2}
, fixing
s\in(0,1/2)
and

r,\rho\in(0,1)
. By
Equation

A.11
, our choice of
\overline{t}_{1}
, and the fact that

0<t<\overline{t}_{1}
, it follows

(A.13)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+s(y-x))\rvert^{2}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leqslant 4\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+s(y-x))\rvert^{2}\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y.

We wish to make the change of variables

\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x,y)=(\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho),(1-s)x+sy)\eqqcolon(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}).

First, let us remark that
\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}
is an injection whenever

\overline{t}_{1}<1/(4M)
. Indeed,

(A.14)

\displaystyle\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x_{1},y_{1})=\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x_{2},y_{2})

\displaystyle\iff\begin{cases}x_{1}-x_{2}=y_{1}-y_{2}+E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{2},y_{2})-E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{1},y_{1})\\
(1-s)(x_{1}-x_{2})=s(y_{2}-y_{1})\end{cases}

\displaystyle\iff\begin{cases}x_{1}-x_{2}=s\left[E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{2},y_{2})-E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{1},y_{1})\right]\\
(1-s)(x_{1}-x_{2})=s(y_{2}-y_{1}).\end{cases}

We compute

\displaystyle E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{2},y_{2})-E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{1},y_{1})

\displaystyle=t\Big[\big(r\widetilde{u}(x_{2})+(1-r)\widetilde{u}(y_{2})\big)x_{2}-\big(r\widetilde{u}(x_{1})+(1-r)\widetilde{u}(y_{2})\big)x_{1}\Big]

\displaystyle-t\Big[\big(\rho\widetilde{u}(y_{2})+(1-\rho)\widetilde{u}(x_{2})\big)y_{2}-\big(\rho\widetilde{u}(y_{1})+(1-\rho)\widetilde{u}(x_{1})\big)y_{1}\Big]

\displaystyle=t\Big[\big(r\widetilde{u}(x_{2})+(1-r)\widetilde{u}(y_{2})\big)(x_{2}-x_{1})+\big(r(\widetilde{u}(x_{2})-\widetilde{u}(x_{1}))+(1-r)(\widetilde{u}(y_{1})-\widetilde{u}(y_{2}))\big)x_{1}\Big]

\displaystyle-t\Big[\big(\rho\widetilde{u}(y_{2})+(1-\rho)\widetilde{u}(x_{2})\big)(y_{2}-y_{1})+\big(\rho(\widetilde{u}(y_{2})-\widetilde{u}(y_{1}))+(1-\rho)(\widetilde{u}(x_{2})-\widetilde{u}(x_{1}))\big)y_{1}\Big],

thus, using the inequality
\lVert\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}+\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}\leqslant M

and the fact that
x_{1},y_{1}\in D_{2}
, we find

(A.15)

\left\lvert E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{2},y_{2})-E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{1},y_{1})\right\rvert\leqslant t\left(\lVert\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}+2\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}\right)\left(\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert+\lvert y_{2}-y_{1}\rvert\right)\\
\leqslant 2M\overline{t}_{1}\left(\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert+\lvert y_{2}-y_{1}\rvert\right).

If
(1-s)(x_{1}-x_{2})=s(y_{2}-y_{1})
,
Equation

A.15
implies

s\left\lvert E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{2},y_{2})-E_{t\widetilde{u}}(x_{1},y_{1})\right\rvert\leqslant 2M\overline{t}_{1}\lvert x_{2}-x_{1}\rvert,

and plugging this into
Equation

A.14
, we see that
\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}
is
injective if
\overline{t}_{1}<1/(4M)
. Then, a simple computation gives

D\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x,y)=\begin{pmatrix}A(x,y)&-B(x,y)\\
(1-s)I_{n}&sI_{n}\end{pmatrix},

where
I_{n}
denotes the identity matrix in
{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}
, and for almost every
x,y\in D_{2}
,

A(x,y)\coloneqq I_{n}+t\Big[\big(r\widetilde{u}(x)+(1-r)\widetilde{u}(y)\big)I_{n}+\big(rx-(1-\rho)y\big)\otimes\nabla\widetilde{u}(x)\Big]

and

B(x,y)\coloneqq I_{n}+t\Big[\big(\rho\widetilde{u}(y)+(1-\rho)\widetilde{u}(x)\big)I_{n}+\big(\rho y-(1-r)x\big)\otimes\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)\Big].

Since
\det D\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}=\det(sA+(1-s)B)
and

sA(x,y)+(1-s)B(x,y)=I_{n}+t\Big[I_{n}+s\big(rx-(1-\rho)y\big)\otimes\nabla\widetilde{u}(x)+(1-s)\big((1-r)x-\rho y\big)\otimes\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)\Big],

we see that, choosing
\overline{t}_{1}
even smaller if needed, depending only on
n
and
M
, we have

\lvert\det D\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x,y)\rvert\geqslant\frac{1}{2}.

Hence, making the change of variables
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(x,y)
in
Equation

A.13

yields

(A.16)

\displaystyle\int_{D_{2}}\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(x+s(y-x))\rvert^{2}\lvert x-y\rvert^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\iint_{\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(D_{2}\times D_{2})}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(y^{\prime})\rvert^{2}\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert x^{\prime}\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\leqslant 8\left(\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}(y)\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}y\right)\left(\int_{0}^{8}t^{n}k_{\varepsilon}(t)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}t\right),

where we used the fact that
\Psi_{t\widetilde{u},r,\rho,s}(D_{2}\times D_{2})\subseteq D_{8}\times D_{2}
for
the last inequality, in view of
Equation

A.11
.
Plugging
Equation

A.16
into
Equation

A.12
and making the change of variable
t^{\prime}=t/\varepsilon

gives
Equation

A.9
, by the assumptions on
k
, which concludes this step.

Step 2.

We split the domain of integration in the left-hand side of
Equation

A.8
in

\left\{x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \lvert x-y\rvert>1/4\right\}
and

\left\{x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \lvert x-y\rvert\leqslant 1/4\right\}
. We first treat the contribution of
distant pairs
(x,y)
.
Choosing
\overline{t}_{1}
small enough depending only on
n
, we have

\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert\geqslant\frac{\lvert x-y\rvert}{2},\qquad\forall x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1},\,\forall r,\rho\in(0,1),

so that, by the squared triangle inequality and by symmetry,

(A.17)

\displaystyle\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\\
\{\lvert x-y\rvert>\frac{1}{4}\}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{x}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{y}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u(x)\rvert^{2}\Bigg(\int\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\\
\{\lvert x-y\rvert>\frac{1}{4}\}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{y}\Bigg)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{x}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\lvert\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\rvert\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}\right)\left(\sup_{r>\frac{1}{8}}\penalty\ k_{\varepsilon}(r)\right).

Then, choosing
\overline{\varepsilon}_{6}=1/(8R_{0})
, and using that

k(r)\leqslant C_{0}r^{-(n+1)}
for all
r\in(R_{0},+\infty)
, for any
0<\varepsilon<\overline{\varepsilon}_{6}
,

Equation

A.17
implies

(A.18)

\iint\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\\
\{\lvert x-y\rvert>\frac{1}{4}\}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{x}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1}_{y}\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\lvert u\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

for some
C=C(n,C_{0})
.

There remains to estimate the integral over the domain

\mathcal{M}\coloneqq\left\{x,y\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\penalty\ :\penalty\ \lvert x-y\rvert<1/4\right\}
. For this, we cover

\mathcal{M}
by a finite number
N(n)
of
\mathcal{M}_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}(x_{i})\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}(x_{i})
, where for each
i\in\{1,\dotsc,N\}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
, and
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}(x_{i})

denotes the hemisphere with center
x_{i}
. Using the stereographic projection
\Pi_{i}
with
respect to
-x_{i}
, we map
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}(x_{i})
to
D_{2}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
. By the changes of
variables
\xi=\Pi_{i}(x)
,
\zeta=\Pi_{i}(y)
, setting
\widetilde{u}_{i}\coloneqq u\circ\Pi_{i}^{-1}
, we
have
\lVert\widetilde{u}_{i}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}+\lVert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{i}\rVert_{L^{\infty}(D_{2})}\leqslant C(n)
since

\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
and
\lVert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})}\leqslant 1
.
Applying Step 1 with
\widetilde{u}=\widetilde{u}_{i}
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(u(x)-u(y))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{tu}(x,y,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{x}^{n-1}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}_{y}^{n-1}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\iint_{D_{2}\times D_{2}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}(\widetilde{u}_{i}(\xi)-\widetilde{u}_{i}(\zeta))^{2}k_{\varepsilon}(\lvert\Phi_{t\widetilde{u}_{i}}(\xi,\zeta,r,\rho)\rvert)\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}r\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\rho\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\leqslant C\int_{D_{2}}\lvert\nabla\widetilde{u}_{i}\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}x\leqslant C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}_{+}(x_{i})}\lvert\nabla_{\tau}\,u\rvert^{2}\mathop{}\mathopen{}\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{n-1},

whenever
t<\overline{t}_{1}
, where
\overline{t}_{1}=\overline{t}_{1}(n)
and
C=C(n,I_{k}^{1})
.
Summing these estimates over
i\in\{1,\dotsc,N(n)\}
and using
Equation

A.18
, we conclude the
proof.
∎

1.
Introduction

1.1.
H^{p}
spaces and analytic and co-analytic projections

Let
\mathbf{D}=\{z\in\mathbf{C}:|z|<1\}
be the unit disk and let
\mathbf{T}=\{z\in\mathbf{C}:|z|=1\}
be the unit circle. For
0<p\leq\infty

we denote by
L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
the Lebesgue space on
\mathbf{T}
. The space
H^{p}(\mathbf{T})
contains all
f\in L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
for
which all negative Fourier coefficients are equal to zero, i.e.,

\hat{f}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(e^{it})e^{-int}dt=0\quad\text{for every integer}\ n<0.

The Riesz projection operator
P_{+}:L^{p}(\mathbf{T})\to H^{p}(\mathbf{T})
, and the co-analytic projection operator
P_{-}=I-P_{+}
, where
I
is the
identity operator on
L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
and
\quad f(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}\hat{f}(n)\zeta^{n},
are defined as

(1)

P_{+}f(\zeta)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\hat{f}(n)\zeta^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{-}f(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{-1}\hat{f}(n)\zeta^{n}.

For a function
f
on
\mathbf{D}
and
r\in(0,1)
we denote by
f_{r}
the function
f_{r}(\zeta)=f(r\zeta)
,
\zeta\in\overline{\mathbf{D}}
. The
harmonic Hardy space
h^{p}
,
0<p\leq\infty
, consists of all harmonic complex-valued functions
f
on
\mathbf{D}
for which the integral mean

M_{p}(f,r)=\left\{\int_{\mathbf{T}}|f_{r}(\zeta)|^{p}\frac{|d\zeta|}{2\pi}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p}}

remains bounded as
r
approaches
1
. Since
|f|^{p}
,
1\leq p<\infty
is subharmonic on
\mathbf{D}
, the integral mean
M_{p}(f,r)
is increasing in

r
. The norm on
h^{p}
in this case is given by

\|f\|_{p}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1}M_{p}(f,r).

The analytic Hardy space
H^{p}
is the subspace of
h^{p}
that contains analytic functions. For the theory of Hardy spaces in the unit disk we refer to
classical books
[
6
,
8
,
24
]
and to the more recent Pavlović book
[
34
]
for new results on the Hardy space theory in the unit disc. Since
|f|^{p}
is subharmonic for every
0<p<\infty
if
f
is holomorphic, the (quasi)norm on the Hardy space
H^{p}
may be introduced as

\|f\|_{p}=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1}M_{p}(f,r).

For
p=\infty
the space
H^{\infty}
contains all bounded analytic functions in the unit disc.

It is well known that for
f\in H^{p}
the radial boundary value
f^{\ast}(\zeta)=\lim_{r\rightarrow 1-}f(r\zeta)
exists for almost every

\zeta\in\mathbf{T}
,
f^{\ast}
belongs to the space
H^{p}(\mathbf{T})
, and we have the isometry relation
\|f^{\ast}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})}=\|f\|_{H^{p}}
. Moreover, given
\varphi\in H^{p}(\mathbf{T})
, the Cauchy (and the Poisson) extension of it gives a function
f
in
H^{p}
such
that
f^{\ast}(\zeta)=\varphi(\zeta)
for almost every
\zeta\in\mathbf{T}
. Therefore, one may identify the spaces
H^{p}(\mathbf{T})
and
H^{p}

via the isometry
f\rightarrow f^{\ast}
. Let us say, also, that after identifying these two spaces, the Riesz operator
P_{+}

may be represented as the Cauchy integral (with density
f^{\ast}
)

P_{+}f(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\mathbf{T}}\frac{f^{\ast}(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\zeta,\quad z\in\mathbf{D}.

The Riesz operator is not bounded on
L^{1}(\mathbf{T})
; actually there does not exist a bounded projection of
L^{1}(\mathbf{T})
onto

H^{1}(\mathbf{T})
. This was shown by Newman (
[
31
]
) and generalized by Rudin (
[
38
]
). From the Parseval’s identity it follows that the operator
P_{+}
is an
orthogonal projection of
L^{2}(\mathbf{T})
onto
H^{2}(\mathbf{T})
. Therefore, the norm of
P_{+}
as an operator from
{L^{2}(\mathbf{T})}

onto
H^{2}(\mathbf{T})
is equal to
1
. If
p=\infty
, then the Riesz projection operator
P_{+}
is also unbounded.
The question for
p\neq 2
is more subtle. It is a classical result that the Riesz projection
P_{+}
is bounded if it is considered as an operator
on
L^{p}(\mathbf{T})
for every
1<p<\infty
,
[
37
]
. There are many proofs of this fact; for example, a proof that uses Hardy-Stein identity can be found in
[
34
]
. However, finding the accurate norm of this operator has turned out to be a more delicate problem.
Some partial results on the norm of the Riesz operator
P_{+}
were obtained by Gohberg and Krupnik. It was done in the case when
p=2^{n}
,
n\in\mathbf{N}
). They also made a conjecture on its value that has been proved by Hollenbeck and Verbitsky,
[
16
]
. Many other results that preceded the final solution of Gohberg-Krupnik conjecture can be found in
[
9
,

,
11
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
33
,
42
]
. Best constants inequalities concerning Hilbert transform, Riesz projection or some other singular integral operators using purely analytic or probabilistic methods are considered by many authors, let us mention also
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

In
[
16
]
, Hollenbeck and Verbitsky proved slightly stronger result than that stated in Gohberg - Krupnik conjecture:

(2)

\|\max\{|P_{+}(f)|,|P_{-}(f)|\}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})}\leq\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})},\quad f\in{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})}.

The proof uses the method of plurisubharmonic minorants. In fact, they proved the ”elementary” inequality:

(3)

\max\{|z|^{p},|w|^{p}\}\leq\frac{1}{\sin^{p}\frac{\pi}{p}}|z+\overline{w}|^{p}-E(z,w),

where
E(z,w)=\frac{2|\cos\frac{\pi}{p}|^{1-\frac{p}{2}}}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}\operatorname{Re}(zw)^{\frac{p}{2}},1<p\leq 2
is plurisubharmonic. Then, integrating this inequality with
z=P_{+}f(\zeta)
and
w=\overline{P_{-}f(\zeta)}
over
\zeta\in\mathbb{T}
the desired inequality is established. This implies the result on the norm of the Riesz projection on the circle for
1<p\leq 2,
while the conclusion for
p>2
follows from the duality arguments. The paper
[
16
]
also contains the analogous result for the real line and certain weighted
L^{p}
spaces on
\mathbb{R}.

In
[
17
]
, an inequality similar to (
3
) is proved for
p>2,
with the different choice of
E(z,w).
This concluded the proof of the inequality (
2
) for
p>2.

1.2.
Statement of the problem and recent progress

In the same paper,
[
17
]
, the authors posed a problem of finding the optimal constant
A_{p,s}
in the inequality

(4)

\|(|P_{+}f|^{s}+|P_{-}f|^{s})^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})}\leq A_{p,s}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})}

for
f\in{L^{p}(\mathbf{T})},\,1<p<\infty,\,0<s<\infty.
They conjectured that

(5)

A_{p,s}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2\cos\frac{\pi}{2p}},\quad 1<p<2,\ 0<s<\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}

and

(6)

A_{p,s}=\frac{2^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2p}},\quad 2<p<\infty,\ 0<s<\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}.

In terms of the above notation, Hollenbeck-Verbitsky results from
[
16
]
and
[
17
]
reads as

A_{p,\infty}=\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}},\quad 1<p<\infty.

It is an easy consequence of Verbitsky’s result from
[
42
]
that the best constant in the inequality (
4
) for real-valued functions is given by (
5
) and (
6
) for all positive
s.
However, for
s>\max\{\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\}
in the complex case the same does not hold, i.e.
A_{p,s}
depends in a more substantial way on
s.

Let us describe the motivation of introducing the conjectural value
A_{p,s}
and the existence of cutoff’s for
s.
For the family
f_{\gamma}(z)=\alpha\operatorname{Re}g_{\gamma}(z)+\imath\beta\operatorname{Im}g_{\gamma}(z)
, where
g_{\gamma}(z)=\bigl(\frac{1+z}{1-z}\bigr)^{\gamma}
, we easily find

P_{+}f_{\gamma}(z)=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}(z)+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad P_{-}f_{\gamma}(z)=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\big(\overline{g_{\gamma}}(z)-1\big).

Thus,

\frac{\|\big(|P_{+}f_{\gamma}|^{s}+|P_{-}f_{\gamma}|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}{\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}=\frac{\|\big(|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}|^{s}+|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}{\|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}.

For
s\geq 1,
by Minkowski inequalities for sequences and integrals, respectively, we find:

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}-2^{\frac{1}{s}-1}\big|\alpha-\beta\big|\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}-2^{\frac{1}{s}-1}\big|\alpha-\beta\big|.

For
s<1,
we have
\frac{p}{s}>1,
hence, using the inequality
|x\pm y|^{s}\geq|x|^{s}-|y|^{s}
and Minkowski integral inequality with exponent
\frac{p}{s}
we get:

\displaystyle\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg\|\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\bigg|^{s}-2^{1-s}\big|\alpha-\beta\big|^{s}\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{p}{s}}({\mathbb{T}})}^{\frac{1}{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg[\bigg\|\bigg|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\bigg|^{s}\bigg\|_{L^{\frac{p}{s}}({\mathbb{T}})}-2^{1-s}\big|\alpha-\beta\big|^{s}\bigg]^{\frac{1}{s}}.

Similarly we obtain the analogous inequalities with the sign
"\leq"
changing the signs in front of
2^{\frac{1}{s}-1}|\alpha-\beta|
and
2^{1-s}|\alpha-\beta|^{s}
from the minus to the plus sign.
By the fact that
\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\frac{1}{p}}\|g_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}=+\infty,
we arrive at

\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\|\big(|P_{+}f_{\gamma}|^{s}+|P_{-}f_{\gamma}|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}{\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}=\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow\frac{1}{p}}\frac{\|\big(|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}|^{s}+|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}{\|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}},

which is, by
|\operatorname{Im}g_{\gamma}|=\tan\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\operatorname{Re}g_{\gamma},
equal to
\frac{\big(|\alpha+\beta|^{s}+|\alpha-\beta|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2\big(\alpha^{2}\cos^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}+\beta^{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}.

Putting
\alpha+\beta=1,\alpha-\beta=e^{-y},

y\geq 0,
we get:

(7)

A_{p,s}\geq\max_{y\geq 0}\frac{2^{\frac{1}{s}}\cosh^{\frac{1}{s}}\frac{sy}{2}}{\big(\cosh y\pm\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}},

where we choose the plus sign for
1<p\leq 2
and the minus sign for
p\geq 2.
More details on these estimates from below and calculations of this maximum can be found in
[
30
]
or later on in Lemma
2
. Here, as we will see, the range of
0<s\leq\max\{\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\}
, i.e. equalities (
5
) and (
6
) corresponds to the case when the above maximum is achieved for
y=0.

Also, note that, since
\max\{a,b\}=\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\big(a^{s}+b^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}},
for
a,b>0,
it is expected that
\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}A_{p.s}=A_{p,\infty}
for all
p>1.
This is proved in
[
30
]
using (
3
) and its analog for
p>2
with explicit calculations of the lower bound for
A_{p,s}
.

In the paper
[
21
]
, Kalaj proved that
A_{p,2}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}\sin\frac{\pi}{2p^{\prime}}},
where
p^{\prime}=\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\},
confirming Hollenbeck-Verbitsky conjecture in case
s=2
. He also proved the reverse estimate to (
4
) for
s=2
i.e. the best constant inequality where the left- and right-hand sides of (
4
) are replaced by each other. The application of these inequalities to the isoperimetric inequality for harmonic functions are also given. We will see at the end of this paper that better estimates can be proved without using them.

The author of this paper found an approach of proving the appropriate ”elementary” inequalities used in the proof of (
4
) and its reversed counterparts for
s=2
, in
[
28
]
, which significantly shorten their proofs. The idea is to analyze stationary points of the appropriate function inside a rectangle and reducing the proof to checking the non-negativity of this function on its sides. It has turned out that this method works for
s\leq\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}
and in
[
30
]
the conjecture is proved for
p\geq 2
and
s\leq p
and
1<p\leq\frac{5}{4}
and
s=4.
Also,
s\leq\max\{p,\frac{p}{p-1}\}
is the maximum range of
s
where this approach can be applied.

Using the monotonicity of the power means, it is easy to see that if (
5
) or (
6
) is proved for some
s_{0}\leq\max\{\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\},
then it holds for
s\in(0,s_{0}]
(see also
[
30
]
). Hence to confirm (
5
) and (
6
) it is enough to prove it for the case
s=\max\{\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\}.
This is the largest value for which
A_{p,s}
has the form as (
5
) and (
6
), depending on whether
1<p\leq 2
of
p>2,
by
[
30
]
. We will call it critical value and, by this observation, concentrate only on the range
s\geq\max\{\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\}.

2.
Main results and corollaries

In this paper, we will prove (
5
) for
1<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
and (
6
) for all
p\geq 2.
Therefore, the main result reads as follows:

Theorem 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
and
s>0
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
P_{+}f
and
P_{-}f
its analytic and co-analytic projections. Then the following sharp inequality holds

(8)

\|\big(|P_{+}f|^{s}+|P_{-}f|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq A_{p,s}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})},

where
A_{p,s}=2^{\frac{1}{s}}\max\limits_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{1}{s}}\frac{sy}{2}}{\sqrt{\cosh y-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}}.

Specially, for
s\leq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
we have:

(9)

\|\big(|P_{+}f|^{s}+|P_{-}f|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2\sin\frac{\pi}{2p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

If
1<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
and
s\leq\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
, then we have:

(10)

\|\big(|P_{+}f|^{s}+|P_{-}f|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}\leq\frac{2^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2\cos\frac{\pi}{2p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}.

The sharpness of these constants is clear from (
7
). Also, note that the main result from
[
17
]
can be inferred from our Theorem 1 and hence, by duality the value of the exact norm of Riesz projection from
[
16
]
.

Using main results from this theorem as granted, we will now mention several corollaries of them.

Corollary 1
.

Let
f
be an
L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
function and
\tilde{f}(\zeta)=-\imath\sum_{n=-\infty}^{+\infty}(\sgn n)\hat{f}(n)\zeta^{n}
its harmonic conjugate. Then the following sharp estimate holds:

(11)

\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{f+\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{f-\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leq A_{p,s}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

with the same constant
A_{p,s}
for the values of
p
and
s
as in Theorem
1
.

Proof.

Using inequalities (
15
) and (
17
) with
z=\frac{f+\imath\tilde{f}}{2}
and
\overline{w}=\frac{f-\imath\tilde{f}}{2}
and integrating over the unit circle:

\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{f+\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{f-\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}+2^{\frac{p}{s}-p}D_{p,s}\big|f(0)\big|^{p}\big|\cos\frac{p\pi}{2}\big|\leq A_{p,s}^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p},

for
p\geq 2
and
s>0.
Similarly, for
1<p\leq\frac{4}{3}
and
s\leq\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}

we get:

\bigg\|\bigg(\bigg|\frac{f+\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}+\bigg|\frac{f-\imath\tilde{f}}{2}\bigg|^{s}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{s}}\bigg\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}+2^{\frac{p}{s}-p}D_{p,s}\big|f(0)\big|^{p}\leq A_{p,s}^{p}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

To see that
A_{p,s}
is sharp, we use the same family of functions
f_{\gamma}.

Indeed, since
\frac{f_{\gamma}+\imath\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}{2}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}
and
\frac{f_{\gamma}-\imath\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}{2}=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}},
from
|\operatorname{Im}g_{\gamma}|=\tan\frac{\pi\gamma}{2}\operatorname{Re}g_{\gamma}
we obtain:

\frac{\big\|\big(\big|\frac{f_{\gamma}+\imath\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}{2}\big|^{s}+\big|\frac{f_{\gamma}-\imath\tilde{f}_{\gamma}}{2}\big|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\big\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}}{\|f_{\gamma}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T})}}=\frac{\|\big(|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}|^{s}+|\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}|^{s}\big)^{\frac{1}{s}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}{\|\frac{\alpha+\beta}{2}g_{\gamma}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2}\overline{g_{\gamma}}\|_{L^{p}({\mathbb{T}})}}.

Now the result follows by the arguments already used in the above considerations.
∎

with the constant as in (

Finally, Section 9 is reserved for a remark on an isoperimetric inequality for harmonic functions.

3.
Theorem

via inequalities with plurisubharmonic minorants

In this section we will state the appropriate ”elementary” inequalities with plurisubharmonic minorants used in proof of our results. It can be seen that they are equal to that used in

[

]
, up to a multiplicative constant. This problem of finding a good minorant is an example a plurisubharmonic obstacle problem and an interesting introduction to this topic is given in (
[

Lemma 1
.

For
p\geq 2
and
s>0

(15)

\left(\frac{|z|^{s}+|w|^{s}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}\leq C_{p,s}|z+\overline{w}|^{p}-(rR)^{\frac{p}{2}}D_{p,s}v_{p}\bigg(\frac{u+t}{2}\bigg),

where
u=\arg z,

t=\arg w,

r=|z|,R=|w|
and

v_{p}(t)=\begin{cases}-\cos(p(\frac{\pi}{2}-|t|)),&\mbox{if}\ \frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{p}<|t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2};\\
\max\{|\cos(p(\frac{\pi}{2}-t))|,|\cos(p(\frac{\pi}{2}+t))|\},&\mbox{if}\ |t|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{\pi}{p}.\end{cases}

and
v_{p}(t)=v_{p}(\pi-|t|)
for
\frac{\pi}{2}\leq|t|\leq\pi
.
Constants
C_{p,s}
and
D_{p,s}
are defined in Section 7.

Specially, for
s\leq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
we have:

(16)

\left(\frac{|z|^{s}+|w|^{s}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}\leq\frac{|z+\overline{w}|^{p}}{2^{p}\sin^{p}\frac{\pi}{2p}}-(rR)^{\frac{p}{2}}\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}v_{p}\bigg(\frac{u+t}{2}\bigg),

For
s\leq\sec^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
and
1<p<\frac{4}{3}
there holds

(17)

\left(\frac{|z|^{s}+|w|^{s}}{2}\right)^{\frac{p}{s}}\leq\frac{|z+\overline{w}|^{p}}{2^{p}\cos^{p}\frac{\pi}{2p}}-\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\operatorname{Re}(zw)^{\frac{p}{2}},\quad z\in\mathbf{C},\,w\in\mathbf{C}.

We will define
C_{p,s}
and
D_{p,s}
later. In this section, we will just use that they are both positive.

It is well-known that if
U(re^{\imath t})=r^{p}v_{p}(t)
is subharmonic in
\mathbb{D}
, then
E(z,w):=(rR)^{\frac{p}{2}}v_{p}(\frac{u+t}{2})
where
u=\arg z,

t=\arg w,

r=|z|,R=|w|
is plurisubharmonic function (this can be found in

]
or
[

]
). The proof of the subharmonicity of

can be found in
[

]
, where this function was originally defined. The minorant from (

[

]
and
[

]
, respectively. Further, putting

and

we infer the sharp norm estimates from Theorem

A_{p,s}=2^{\frac{1}{s}}C_{p,s}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

In case of

We will describe some reductions of these inequalities for the sake of completeness even it was done in
[

]
. First, these inequalities are invariant under the transformation

, so we can set

-\bigg(\frac{1+r^{s}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}+C_{p,s}\big(1+r^{2}+2r\cos t\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}-D_{p,s}r^{\frac{p}{2}}v_{p}\bigg(\frac{t}{2}\bigg)\geq 0,

for

-\bigg(\frac{1+r^{s}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}+\frac{(1+r^{2}+2r\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{2^{p}\cos^{p}\frac{\pi}{2p}}-r^{\frac{p}{2}}\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\geq 0,

for

Multiplying both of them with

we see that they are non-negative for

Hence, it is enough to prove them for
0<r\leq 1.
The function on the left-hand side in the inequality (

so we can assume

Now, we reproduce the approach from (
[

]
) to show that for the inequality (

t\in[\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p},\pi].
Indeed, since for
t\in[0,\pi-\frac{2\pi}{p}],

-\cos(\frac{p}{2}(\pi-t))
decreases from

to

Therefore, since

we easily see

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{1+r^{s}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}+C_{p,s}\big(1+r^{2}+2r\cos t\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}-D_{p,s}r^{\frac{p}{2}}v_{p}\bigg(\frac{t}{2}\bigg)\geq

\displaystyle-\bigg(\frac{1+r^{s}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}+C_{p,s}\big(1+r^{2}+2r\cos\theta\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}-D_{p,s}r^{\frac{p}{2}}v_{p}\bigg(\frac{\theta}{2}\bigg)\geq 0.

Similar reduction, that continues to hold here, has been done in

Finally, replacing

-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)+C_{p,s}\big(\cosh y-\cos t\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}+D_{p,s}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\geq 0,

for
y\geq 0
,

and
p\geq 2.
Similarly,
in case

and

we have to prove

(22)

-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)+\frac{(\cosh y+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\geq 0,

for
y\geq 0
and

We will now restate the results from Section 2 in
[

]
which we will be useful later in proving (

) and (
22
). Also, we will give a shorter proof with new notation.

Lemma 2
.

Let
s>0,

p\geq 2
and let
C_{p,s}
denotes the maximum of the function

(23)

K(y):=\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}{\big(\cosh y-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}}.

Then:

•

For
s\leq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},
the maximum is attained at
y=0
and its value is

C_{p,s}=\big(\frac{s}{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}};

•

For
s>\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},
the maximum is attained at some
\tilde{y}>0.
Specially, for
p=2
, the supremum is equal to the limit in
+\infty
i.e.
1.

Proof.

Let
p>2.
From

K^{\prime}(y)=\frac{p\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{2\big(\cosh y-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}+1}}\bigg(\frac{\sinh\big(\frac{(s-2)y}{2}\big)}{\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\bigg)

we see that it is enough to examine the sign of
\frac{\sinh\big(\frac{(s-2)y}{2}\big)}{\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}
. Since its derivative is equal to
\frac{a\cosh(ay)\sinh(by)-b\cosh(by)\sinh(ay)}{\sinh^{2}(by)}
and
\big(a\cosh(ay)\sinh(by)-b\cosh(by)\sinh(ay)\big)^{\prime}=(a^{2}-b^{2})\sinh(ay)\sinh(by)\leq 0,
where
a=\frac{s-2}{2}
and
b=\frac{s}{2},
we conclude that
\frac{\sinh\big(\frac{(s-2)y}{2}\big)}{\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}
decreases in
y
. If
s\leq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},
then
\frac{\sinh\big(\frac{(s-2)y}{2}\big)}{\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}
has value in
0
is
\leq 0
, hence
K(y)
is decreasing and attains its maximum in
0
equal
C_{p,s}=\big(\frac{s}{2}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}}.
For
s>\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
considered expression is positive in
0
and tends to
-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}<0
when
y\rightarrow+\infty.
Therefore, it has unique zero
\tilde{y}
such that
K^{\prime}(y)>0
for
(0,\tilde{y})
and
K^{\prime}(y)<0
for
(\tilde{y},+\infty),
i.e.
\max_{y\in\mathbb{R}}K(y)=K(\tilde{y})=C_{p,s}.
We leave the case of
p=2
to the reader as an easy exercise.
∎

We can proceed similarly in the case
1<p<2,
see
[
30
]
.
In the upcoming sections we will prove Lemma 1 in the range specified above.

4.
Proof of the Lemma 1 for
s=\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
and
p\geq 2

Now, we concentrate on the proof of the inequality (
21
) for
s=\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
. In this section we describe the method used in its proof, while some steps will be postponed for the next two sections.
Let us consider the following function:

(24)

\Phi(y,t)=-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)+\frac{(\cosh y-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}+\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\cos\frac{tp}{2}.

Our aim is to prove its non-negativity for
y\geq 0
and
t\in[0,\frac{2\pi}{p}].

We easily find that

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\big(\cosh y-\cos t\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sin t\bigg(\frac{\big(1-\cos t\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin t}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\sin t\bigg(\frac{\big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\frac{\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg)=0,

by the claim from the proof of Lemma
3
. Therefore,
\Phi(y,t)
increases in
t
for
t\in[\frac{\pi}{p},\frac{2\pi}{p}]
and
\Phi(y,t)\geq\Phi(y,\frac{\pi}{p})\geq 0.
We are now reduced to
t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{p}].

Validity of (
21
) for
y=0
follows from the known results (
[
21
]
,
[
30
]
).
From

\frac{2}{p}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}(y,t)=-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)+\sinh y\frac{(\cosh y-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}

for
y>0
we will prove that, for every fixed
t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{p})
there exists a
y_{p}(t)\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}<0
for
y\in(0,y_{p}(t)),
while we have
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}>0
for
y\in(y_{p}(t),+\infty).

The function
y_{p}(t)
is well defined, differentiable and monotone (Section 6) on its domain and we can consider the function
\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)
.
The crux of the proof is the fact that
\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)\leq 0.
Hence, our function is decreasing and
\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)\geq\Phi(y_{p}(\frac{\pi}{p}),\frac{\pi}{p})=\Phi(0,\frac{\pi}{p})=0
.
We have

\frac{2}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)=

y^{\prime}_{p}(t)\bigg[-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\bigg(\frac{sy_{p}(t)}{2}\bigg)\sinh\bigg(\frac{sy_{p}(t)}{2}\bigg)+\sinh y_{p}(t)\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\bigg]+

\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2}=\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2},

since the expression inside the brackets is equal to zero (
y_{p}(t)
is the zero of
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}
for fixed
t
).

Therefore, it is enough to prove that

\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\leq\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin t}

for
t\in(0,\frac{\pi}{p}).
The following lemma is the first step in its proof:

Lemma 3
.

We have the following inequality:

\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin t}\geq E_{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}t

for
0\leq t\leq\frac{\pi}{p},

p\geq 2
with
E_{p}=\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}}.

Proof.

Let us consider the function
f(t)=\log\sin\frac{tp}{2}-\log\sin t-(\frac{p}{2}-1)\log\cos t.

We find the derivative

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{p}{2}\cos\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}-\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}+\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\frac{\sin t}{\cos t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}\sin t\cos t}\bigg[\frac{p}{2}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\sin t\cos t-\cos^{2}t\sin\frac{tp}{2}+\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\sin^{2}t\sin\frac{tp}{2}\bigg].

Note that the conclusion of our lemma follows from
f^{\prime}(t)\leq 0,
so we have to show that

h(t)=\frac{p}{2}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\sin t\cos t-\cos^{2}t\sin\frac{tp}{2}+\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\sin^{2}t\sin\frac{tp}{2}=\frac{p}{4}(\sin 2t\cos\frac{tp}{2}-\cos 2t\sin\frac{tp}{2})+(\frac{p}{4}-1)\sin\frac{tp}{2}=\frac{p}{4}\sin(\frac{p}{2}-2)t+(\frac{p}{4}-1)\sin\frac{tp}{2}\leq 0.

This is equivalent to
\frac{\sin(\frac{p}{2}-2)t}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}\geq\frac{p-4}{p}
for
p>4
and to
\frac{\sin(2-\frac{p}{2})t}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}\geq\frac{4-p}{p}
for
2\leq p\leq 4.
Both of these estimates follows from the next statement:

Claim 1
.

If
0<\alpha<\beta
and
t\in(0,\frac{\pi}{\beta}),
then:

g(t)=\frac{\sin\alpha t}{\sin\beta t}

is monotone increasing function on
(0,\frac{\pi}{\beta}).

Indeed,
g^{\prime}(t)=\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\beta t}\big(\alpha\cos\alpha t\sin\beta t-\beta\cos\beta t\sin\alpha t\big)
which is not smaller then zero, since the expression inside the brackets is
0
at
0
and its derivative on
t
is
(\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2})\sin\alpha t\sin\beta t\geq 0
.
∎

Remark
. For
2\leq p\leq 4
, it can be proved that the exponent
\frac{p}{2}-1
in the previous lemma can be replaced with not greater than
\cot^{2}\frac{\pi}{p},
but this is still good and more convenient for our needs.

Using Lemma
3
we are able to handle further with the inequality (
21
) working with the
y-
variable. It is sufficient to show the estimate:

\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)-\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\leq\frac{\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}t}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}}.

Now, the left-hand side of the last inequality is equal to
\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}
; the right-hand side is, from the condition
\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}(y_{p}(t),t)=0
, equal to

\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg[\cosh y-\big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)\bigg(\big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}-1}.

Hence, we want to show that

\displaystyle\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg[\cosh y-\big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)\bigg(\big(1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}\big)\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\bigg]^{\frac{p}{2}-1},

which, after multiplying by
{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}},
taking the
\frac{2}{p-2}
th power of both sides and using
\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{\pi}{p}\cos^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\frac{\pi}{p}=\big(2\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\big)\big(1-2\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}=\big(\frac{2}{s}\big)\big(1-\frac{2}{s}\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}
and
1-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}=\frac{2}{s}
reduces to the inequality

f(y)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\leq\cosh y,

for

f(y)=\frac{2}{s}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}.

Remark
. Note that, after realizing that
y_{p}(t)
is well defined (which is easy in this case),
for
s=2
the last inequality becomes the identity. Hence, we get a very quick proof of the inequality used in
[
21
]
to prove the result when
s=2.

5.
Main estimate for hyperbolic functions

Both existence of
y_{p}(t)
and the conclusion on the monotonicity of the function
\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)
on
(0,\frac{\pi}{p})
depends on the inequality

(25)

\frac{2}{s}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}\leq\cosh^{\frac{p}{2}-1}y

for some range of
y.

The next lemma will give a crucial step in estimating range of
y^{\prime}
s for
p\geq 4.
We will work with all
s\geq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p},
hence the Lemma will be useful also in general setting.

Lemma 4
.

For
p\geq 4
and
s\geq\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
there holds the estimate:

\cosh y-\bigg(\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{C_{p,s}\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}>1

for
y\geq\cosh^{-1}\big(\frac{1}{\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}\big).

Specially, for this range of
y
and
s=\csc^{2}\frac{\pi}{2p}
this reads as

\cosh y-\frac{2}{s}\bigg(\frac{2}{s}\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}>1.

Proof.

Since
\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}(\frac{sy}{2})\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y}<\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y},
it is enough to prove

\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)<C_{p,s}\sinh y\big(\cosh y-1\big)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}.

If we rewrite it as

\bigg(\frac{e^{\frac{sy}{2}}+e^{-\frac{sy}{2}}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}<C_{p,s}\bigg(\frac{e^{y}-e^{-y}}{2}\bigg)\bigg(\frac{e^{y}+e^{-y}}{2}-1\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}-1},

after dividing both sides by
e^{\frac{py}{2}},
we get

(26)

\bigg(\frac{1+e^{-sy}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}<C_{p,s}2^{-\frac{p}{2}}\big(1-e^{-y}\big)^{p-1}\big(1+e^{-y}\big).

Note that the left-hand side is monotone decreasing, while the right-hand side is increasing function, so the inequality follows if we prove it for
y=\cosh^{-1}\big(\frac{1}{\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}\big).
Since

e^{-y}=\frac{1-\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}
, we easily calculate that

\big(1-e^{-y}\big)^{p-1}\big(1+e^{-y}\big)=\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{2^{p}\sin^{p}\frac{\pi}{2p}}{(1+\sin\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}.
In order to estimate the left-hand side of (
26
), we will prove:

(27)

\phi(x)=\cosh x\cos x\leq 1,\quad\text{where}\quad x=\frac{\pi}{p}<1.

Indeed, by the power series expansion, we have:

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\bigg(1+\frac{x^{4}}{4!}+\frac{x^{8}}{8!}+\dots\bigg)^{2}-\bigg(\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\frac{x^{6}}{6!}+\frac{x^{10}}{10!}+\dots\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(1+\frac{x^{4}\psi(x)}{12}\bigg)^{2}-x^{4}\psi^{2}(x)=1+\frac{x^{4}\psi(x)}{6}-x^{4}\psi^{2}(x)+\frac{x^{8}\psi^{2}(x)}{144},

where
\psi(x)=\frac{1}{2!}+\frac{x^{4}}{6!}+\frac{x^{8}}{10!}+\dots
Since
\psi(0)=\frac{1}{2}\leq\psi(x)\leq\psi(1)\leq 1
, we conclude

\phi(x)\leq 1+\frac{x^{4}}{6}+\frac{x^{8}}{144}-\frac{x^{4}}{4}=1-\frac{x^{4}}{12}+\frac{x^{8}}{144}\leq 1.

By convexity of the power-mean function and inequality (
27
), we estimate the left-hand side as

\bigg(\frac{1+e^{-sy}}{2}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{s}}\leq 2^{-\frac{p}{s}}\big(1+e^{-py}\big)\leq 2^{-\frac{p}{s}}\big(1+e^{-\pi}\big).

We estimate
C_{p,s}
in the following way:

Straightforward calculation gives:

Hence, it is enough to prove:

\frac{\cot\frac{\pi}{2p}}{\cos^{p}\frac{\pi}{2p}(1+\sin\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\geq 1+e^{-\pi}.

We introduce the change of variable

and consider the function

We will prove that it is decreasing, hence

increases in

and the conclusion follows from

From

we find

Now, using estimate

we get

we have

=0

∎

Lemma 5
.

Let

Specially,

attains its minimum on an interval

Proof.

From
f^{(k_{0}+1)}(x)=\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{+\infty}a_{k}x^{k}<0,
we conclude that

is monotone decreasing function and, since

and

we infer that

is increasing till some point and then decreasing. If we continue with this argument, by using that

, it is easily seen that the same holds for

and we are done.

Lemma 6
.

The function
\varphi(y)
is monotone increasing for

From

\varphi^{\prime}(y)=\sinh y-\frac{2}{s}\frac{2}{p-2}f(y)^{\frac{2}{p-2}-1}f^{\prime}(y)

we have:

\varphi^{\prime}(y)=\sinh y-\frac{4}{s(p-2)}\frac{f(y)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}}{\sinh y\sinh\big(sy\big)}\bigg[p\sinh^{2}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh y+s\sinh y-\cosh y\sinh\big(sy\big)\bigg].

It is obvious that if

we have
\varphi^{\prime}(y)\geq 0,
while in the other case we use
the estimate
f(y)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\leq\cosh y
to get:

\varphi^{\prime}(y)=\sinh y-\frac{4}{s(p-2)}\frac{\cosh y}{\sinh y\sinh\big(sy\big)}\bigg[p\sinh^{2}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh y+s\sinh y-\cosh y\sinh\big(sy\big)\bigg].

We see that a proof of our lemma depends on the main estimate for hyperbolic functions and that is why we have the restriction for the domain in case
p>4.

Therefore,
\varphi^{\prime}(y)\geq 0
follows from

p\sinh(2y)\frac{\cosh(sy)-1}{2}+s\sinh(2y)-\sinh(sy)\cosh(2y)-\sinh(sy)

\leq\frac{s}{2}\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\big(\cosh(2y)-1\big)\sinh(sy).

This is equivalent with

-1+\frac{p}{2}\frac{\cosh y\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y\cosh(\frac{sy}{2})}+\frac{s\sinh(2y)-2\sinh(sy)}{\sinh(sy)(\cosh(2y)-1)}\leq\frac{s}{2}\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big).

\frac{\cosh y\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y\cosh(\frac{sy}{2})}

is monotone decreasing (its derivative is equal to
\\
\frac{s\sinh(2y)-2\sinh(sy)}{4\sinh^{2}y\cosh^{2}(\frac{sy}{2})}\leq 0
), we conclude

(28)

\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\frac{\cosh y\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y\cosh(\frac{sy}{2})}\leq\frac{s}{2}\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big).

Therefore, it is enough to prove

(29)

-1+\frac{\cosh y\sinh(\frac{sy}{2})}{\sinh y\cosh(\frac{sy}{2})}+\frac{s\sinh(2y)-2\sinh(sy)}{\sinh(sy)(\cosh(2y)-1)}\leq 0.

After multiplying both sides by

we get

\displaystyle-\sinh(sy)-\sinh((s-2)y)+(s-1)\sinh(2y)

\displaystyle=2\cosh y\bigg((s-1)\sinh(y)-\sinh((s-1)y)\bigg)\leq 0.

In any case, we can conclude the main result of this section using Lemma
4

6

\geq 0,

What we have to prove is now equivalent with

\displaystyle\cosh y

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)}{C_{p,s}\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}\bigg(1+\frac{\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}{\cosh\tilde{y}-\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\cosh\tilde{y}\bigg(\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh\big(\frac{sy}{2}\big)\sinh\tilde{y}}{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\big(\frac{s\tilde{y}}{2}\big)\sinh\big(\frac{s\tilde{y}}{2}\big)\sinh y}\bigg)^{\frac{2}{p-2}}

G(y)\leq G(\tilde{y}),\quad\text{for}\quad\tilde{y}\leq y\leq y^{\prime},

where

This restriction of values for

while for

Thus, we need to prove that the function

Proceeding similarly as in the case of

G(y)\geq G(\tilde{y}),\quad\text{for some range of $y^{\prime}$s}.

In the next subsection we will see that we have to prove (

8.
Proof of the Lemma 1 for

Now, we prove (

and

Our proof is very similar to that of

but technically more delicate at some places. Our method works only in this range as can be seen from the proof, but the inequality that we intend to prove also does not hold for

Our main inequality is

(37)

\Phi(y,t)=-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}}\frac{sy}{2}+\frac{(\cosh y+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}}}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\geq 0,

for
y\geq 0
and
t\in[0,\pi],
as we already mention in Section 3.
Since

\displaystyle\frac{2}{p}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial t}(y,t)

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\cosh y+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}+\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-\frac{(1+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}+\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2},

non-negativity of the last expression for
t\in[\frac{\pi}{p},\pi]
follows from the next statement.

Claim 2
.

The function

\phi(t)=\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin\frac{t}{2}\cos^{p-1}\frac{t}{2}}

is increasing and
\phi(t)\geq\phi(\frac{\pi}{p})
for
t\in[\frac{\pi}{p},\pi].

Proof.

We easily calculate

\big(\log\phi(t)\big)^{\prime}=\frac{p}{2\sin t\sin\frac{tp}{2}}\bigg[\frac{\sin(1-\frac{p}{2})t}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}-\frac{2-p}{p}\bigg].

By the Claim 1 we see that
\frac{\sin(1-\frac{p}{2})t}{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}
increases on
[0,\pi]
and hence
\geq\frac{2-p}{p}.
So,
\phi(t)
increases and we are done.
∎

Now, we are reduced to the case of
t\in[0,\frac{\pi}{p}].
For a fixed
t,
we have

\frac{2}{p}\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial y}(y,t)=\frac{(\cosh y+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sinh y}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\frac{sy}{2}\sinh\frac{sy}{2}

and by Lemma
10
, we see that there exists a unique

such that

(38)

\varphi(y)=\bigg[\frac{\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\frac{sy}{2}\sinh\frac{sy}{2}}{\sinh y}(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}\bigg]^{\frac{2}{p-2}}-\cosh y=\cos t.

This means that the function
\Phi(y,t)
satisfies

therefore, it is enough to prove that
\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)
has its minimal value for
t=\frac{\pi}{p}.
In fact, we will show that it is decreasing on
[0,\frac{\pi}{p}]
. From

\frac{2}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\Phi(y_{p}(t),t)=

y^{\prime}_{p}(t)\bigg[-\cosh^{\frac{p}{s}-1}\bigg(\frac{sy_{p}(t)}{2}\bigg)\sinh\bigg(\frac{sy_{p}(t)}{2}\bigg)+\sinh y_{p}(t)\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}\bigg]-

\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}+\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2}=-\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}\sin t}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}+\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\sin\frac{tp}{2},

we infer that it is a consequence of the inequality:

(39)

\tan\frac{\pi}{2p}\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin t}\leq\frac{(\cosh y_{p}(t)+\cos t)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}}{(1+\cos\frac{\pi}{p})^{\frac{p}{2}}}.

As in the case
p\geq 2,
we will estimate the left-hand side in such a way that we can work with the appropriate range of
y^{\prime}
s. The first step is given by the next

Lemma 7
.

For
1<p\leq 2
and

there holds the following inequality

\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}}{\sin t}\leq\frac{1}{\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg(\frac{c_{p}+\cos t}{c_{p}+\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg)^{\frac{p}{2}-1},

where
c_{p}=\frac{\frac{p}{2}\sin^{2}\frac{\pi}{p}-1}{\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}.

Proof.

Let us consider the function

f(t):=\log\frac{\sin\frac{tp}{2}\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}{\sin t}-\big(\frac{p}{2}-1\big)\log\bigg(\frac{c_{p}+\cos t}{c_{p}+\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}\bigg).

Since
f(\frac{\pi}{p})=0,
it is enough to prove
f^{\prime}(t)=\frac{p\cos\frac{tp}{2}}{2\sin\frac{tp}{2}}-\frac{\cos t}{\sin t}+(\frac{p}{2}-1)\frac{\sin t}{c_{p}+\cos t}\geq 0.

From

\displaystyle F(t)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(t)\sin\frac{tp}{2}\sin t(c_{p}+\cos t)

\displaystyle=\frac{p}{2}\cos\frac{tp}{2}\sin t\big(c_{p}+\cos t\big)-\sin\frac{tp}{2}\cos t\big(c_{p}+\cos t\big)+(\frac{p}{2}-1)\sin^{2}t\sin\frac{tp}{2}

\displaystyle=\big(\frac{p}{4}-1\big)\sin\frac{tp}{2}+\frac{p}{4}\sin\big(2-\frac{p}{2}\big)t+c_{p}\bigg(\frac{p-2}{4}\sin\big(1+\frac{p}{2}\big)t+\frac{p+2}{4}\sin\big(1-\frac{p}{2}\big)t\bigg)

we infer

F^{\prime}(t)=\frac{p}{2}\big(1-\frac{p}{4}\big)\bigg(\cos\big(2-\frac{p}{2}\big)t-\cos\frac{tp}{2}\bigg)+c_{p}\frac{4-p^{2}}{8}\bigg(\cos\big(1-\frac{p}{2}\big)t-\cos\big(1+\frac{p}{2}\big)t\bigg)\leq 0,

since

\cos\big(2-\frac{p}{2}\big)t-\cos\frac{tp}{2}=-2\sin t\sin(1-\frac{p}{2})t\leq 0,

\cos\big(1-\frac{p}{2}\big)t-\cos\big(1+\frac{p}{2}\big)t=-2\sin t\sin\frac{tp}{2}\leq 0

and
F(\frac{\pi}{p})=0
. Hence,

and the Lemma
7
follows. ∎

The inequality (

It is obvious that
\psi(0)=0.

) for some range of

corresponding to

. Our proof will be given through the following four steps:

•

is increasing till some point and then decreases,

•

Then

•

8.1.
Step 1

By direct calculation we find

we find:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}(p)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2\cos\frac{\pi}{p}}{(2-p)^{2}}-\frac{\pi\sin\frac{\pi}{p}}{p(2-p)}-\frac{13}{3}\sinh\frac{13(p-1)}{5}

\displaystyle<\frac{2\cos\frac{3\pi}{13}}{(\frac{2}{3})^{2}}-\frac{\frac{10\pi}{13}\sin\frac{10\pi}{13}}{2-\frac{13}{10}}-\frac{13}{5}\sinh\frac{39}{50}=\frac{9\cos\frac{3\pi}{13}}{2}-\frac{100\pi\sin\frac{3\pi}{13}}{91}-\frac{13}{5}\sinh\frac{39}{50}

\displaystyle<\frac{27}{8}-\frac{942}{455}-\frac{13\cdot 39}{250}<0

which gives

\sigma(p)\geq\sigma(\frac{4}{3})=\sqrt{2}-\cosh\frac{13}{15}>0.

∎

9.
A remark on an isoperimetric inequality for harmonic functions

In this section we will make some remarks on an isoperimetric inequality for harmonic functions in the unit disk. Namely, in
[
22
]
it is proved that there is a constant
K_{p}
such that

(43)

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2p}dA(z)\leq K_{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{\imath t})|^{p}dt\bigg)^{2}

for every function
f
harmonic in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
. Here,
f(e^{\imath t})
denotes the value of radial limit of a function
f.

We will prove this inequality with constant
C_{p}
better than that in
[
21
]
, following the approach from
[
29
]
, where the problem earlier considered in
[
23
]
is completely solved. In
[
13
]
, the authors considered similar problem in half-space setting using variational approach.

By the basics of harmonic function theory, see
[
34
]
, it can be easily seen that (
43
) is equivalent to

\int_{\mathbb{D}}|P_{z}f(e^{\imath t})|^{2p}dA(z)\leq K_{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{\imath t})|^{p}dt\bigg)^{2},

where
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
P_{z}f(e^{\imath t})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos(\theta-t)+r^{2}}f(e^{\imath t})dt
,
z=re^{\imath\theta}.

Since the kernel of the integral operator
P_{z}f
is positive, we infer that the constant
K_{p}
has the same value for real and complex valued functions
f.
In next lemma we give an easy observation on
K_{p}.

Lemma 12
.

The best constant in the inequality (
43
)

K_{p}=C_{p}^{p}

decreases as a function on
p.

Proof.

Let
q>p>1.
From Jensen’s inequality and the definition of
K_{p}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2q}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos(\theta-t)+r^{2}}|f(e^{\imath t})|dt\bigg)^{2p\cdot\frac{q}{p}}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{D}}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos(\theta-t)+r^{2}}|f(e^{\imath t})|^{\frac{q}{p}}dt\bigg)^{2p}dA(z)

\displaystyle\leq K_{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{\imath t})^{\frac{q}{p}}|^{p}dt\bigg)^{2}=K_{p}\bigg(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(e^{\imath t})|^{q}dt\bigg)^{2}

which gives
K_{p}\geq K_{q}.

∎

In
[
22
]
it is proved that
C_{2}\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{2}+1}{\sqrt{2}}}
, while the maximum principle for harmonic functions gives
C_{\infty}=1
. Therefore, by our Lemma
12
we get, for
p>2:

C_{p}\leq\bigg(\frac{\sqrt{2}+1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Note that the Riesz-Thorin interpolation theorem for the Poisson extension operator gives the same estimate!

For
1<p<2,
in
[
20
]
it is proved that

C_{p}\leq\bigg(\frac{\cos\frac{\pi}{4p}}{\cos\frac{\pi}{2p}}\bigg)^{2}.

We are not able to improve it further; however, we can see that the constant is the same in case of the real- and complex-valued harmonic functions.

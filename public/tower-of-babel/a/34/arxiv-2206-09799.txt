Unified approach to the nonlinear Rabi models Journal: New J. Phys.
Liwei Duan

Abstract
An analytical approach is proposed to study the two-photon, two-mode and intensity-dependent Rabi models. By virtue of the su(1,1) Lie algebra, all of them can be unified to the same Hamiltonian with 𝒵 2 \mathcal{Z}_{2} symmetry. There exist exact isolated solutions, which are located at the level crossings between different parities and correspond to eigenstates with finite dimension. Beyond the exact isolated solutions, the regular spectrum can be achieved by finding the roots of the G-function. The corresponding eigenstates are of infinite dimension. It is noteworthy that the expansion coefficients of the eigenstates present an exponential decay behavior. The decay rate decreases with increasing coupling strength. When the coupling strength tends to the spectral collapse point g → ω / 2 g\rightarrow\omega/2 , the decay rate tends to zero which prevents the convergence of the wave functions. This work paves a way for the analysis of novel physics in nonlinear quantum optics.



Keywords
: nonlinear Rabi model, su(1,1) Lie algebra, exact solution

Among them, two-photon, two-mode and intensity-dependent Rabi models are three typical ones which introduce different forms of nonlinear interactions.

The two-photon and two-mode Rabi models describe the transitions of the two-level system accompanied by emitting or absorbing two photons in single- and two-mode bosonic field respectively. They can be used to describe the second-order process with consequently small coupling strengths in different physical setups

[

Pioneered by Buck and Sukumar, they proposed an intensity-dependent Jaynes-Cummings model, namely the Buck-Sukumar model, to study the collapse and revival behavior of the two-level system

Although the Hamiltonians of the two-photon, two-mode and intensity-dependent Rabi models are quite different, they share some common features: (i) One can introduce the su(1,1) Lie algebra to describe the bosonic parts of three Hamiltonians. The paper is structured as follows. In section
2

4

The condition to achieve exact isolated solutions is given. Beyond the exact isolated solutions, the regular spectrum is achieved by solving the G-function. The energy spectrum and the behavior of the eigenstates can be found in section
5

6

2
SU(1,1) group

The group theory has been employed in various branches in quantum optics
[
34
,
35
]
.
We begin by briefly reviewing the basic properties of the SU(1,1) group and its associate su(1,1) algebra.
The SU(1,1) group is non-compact. The generators associated with SU(1,1) group satisfy

\displaystyle\left[\hat{K}_{0},\hat{K}_{\pm}\right]=\pm\hat{K}_{\pm},\quad\left[\hat{K}_{+},\hat{K}_{-}\right]=-2\hat{K}_{0}.

(1)

The corresponding Casimir
\hat{C}
operator can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{C}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{0}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{K}_{+}\hat{K}_{-}+\hat{K}_{-}\hat{K}_{+}\right),

(2)

which commutes with all the elements of the su(1,1) Lie algebra. One can choose the basis state
\left|{}k,m\right>
, which satisfies the following relations,

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{0}\left|{}k,m\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(k+m)\left|{}k,m\right>,

(3a)

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{+}\left|{}k,m\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{(m+1)(m+2k)}\left|{}k,m+1\right>,

(3b)

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{-}\left|{}k,m\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{m(m+2k-1)}\left|{}k,m-1\right>,

(3c)

\displaystyle\hat{C}\left|{}k,m\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(k-1)\left|{}k,m\right>,

(3d)

with
m=0,1,2,\dots
All states can be obtained from the lowest one
\left|{}k,0\right>
by successive actions of the raising operator
\hat{K}_{+}
according to

\displaystyle\left|{}k,m\right>=\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(2k)}{m!\Gamma(2k+m)}}\hat{K}_{+}^{m}\left|{}k,0\right>.

(3d)

The number
k
is known as the Bargmann index which separates different irreducible representations.

3
Nonlinear Rabi model

A general nonlinear Rabi model with an su(1,1) coupling scheme can be written as

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega\hat{K}_{0}+g\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left(\hat{K}_{+}+\hat{K}_{-}\right),

(3e)

where
\epsilon
and
\omega
correspond to the frequency of the two-level system and bosonic field respectively,
g
is the coupling strength. Like the linear Rabi model, the nonlinear one has
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
symmetry. The parity operator can be defined as
\hat{\Pi}=-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\otimes\hat{T}
with
\hat{T}=\exp\left[\rmi\pi\left(\hat{K}_{0}-k\right)\right]
.
We can easily verify that

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\hat{\sigma}_{z}\hat{\Pi}^{\dagger}=\hat{\sigma}_{z},\quad\hat{\Pi}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\hat{\Pi}^{\dagger}=-\hat{\sigma}_{x},\quad\hat{\Pi}\hat{K}_{0}\hat{\Pi}^{\dagger}=\hat{K}_{0},\quad\hat{\Pi}\hat{K}_{\pm}\hat{\Pi}^{\dagger}=-\hat{K}_{\pm},

which leads to
\hat{\Pi}\hat{H}\hat{\Pi}^{\dagger}=\hat{H}
and
\left[\hat{H},\hat{\Pi}\right]=0
.
The parity operator
\hat{\Pi}
has eigenvalues
\Pi=\pm 1
, and it can separate the whole Hilbert space into two subspaces with even and odd parities respectively. Unlike the linear Rabi model, the nonlinear one also commutes with the Casimir operator
\hat{C}
, which separates the whole Hilbert space into different subspaces indexed by the Bargmann index
k
.

Such a Hamiltonian has been studied by Penna
et al
[
31
]
who mainly focused on the two-mode and Holstein-Primakoff realizations of the su(1,1) algebra. Depending on the choice of the realizations,
\hat{H}
can be expressed in different forms.

3.1
Two-photon Rabi model

In the one-mode bosonic realization, the generators can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad\hat{K}_{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)^{2},\quad\hat{K}_{-}=\frac{1}{2}\hat{a}^{2},

(3f)

where
\hat{a}
(
\hat{a}^{\dagger}
) is the bosonic annihilation (creation) operator. The corresponding Bargmann index is
k=\frac{1}{4}
or
\frac{3}{4}
. Given the Fock states
\left|{}n\right>_{a}
which satisfies
\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}\left|{}n\right>_{a}=n\left|{}n\right>_{a}
, the basis state
\left|{}k,m\right>
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left|{}k,m\right>=\left|{}2\left(m+k-\frac{1}{4}\right)\right>_{a}.

(3g)

Therefore, the number of bosons is even and odd for
k=\frac{1}{4}
and
\frac{3}{4}
respectively.

One can obtain the well-known two-photon Rabi model
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
by substituting
\hat{K}_{0}
and
\hat{K}_{\pm}
in (
3e
) with those in (
3f
), which leads to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{2p}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}-\frac{\omega_{\mathrm{2p}}}{2},

(3h)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega_{\mathrm{2p}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+g_{\mathrm{2p}}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left(\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\right)^{2}+\hat{a}^{2}\right),

with
\omega_{\mathrm{2p}}=\omega/2
,
g_{\mathrm{2p}}=g/2
.

3.2
Two-mode Rabi model

In the two-mode bosonic realization, the generators can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}+1\right),\quad\hat{K}_{+}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger},\quad\hat{K}_{-}=\hat{a}\hat{b}.

(3i)

The corresponding Bargmann index is
k=\frac{1}{2},~1,~\frac{3}{2},~\dots
Given the Fock states
\left|{}n\right>_{s}
(
s=a,b
) which satisfies
\hat{s}^{\dagger}\hat{s}\left|{}n\right>_{s}=n\left|{}n\right>_{s}
, the basis state
\left|{}k,m\right>
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left|{}k,m\right>=\left|{}m+2k-1\right>_{a}\otimes\left|{}m\right>_{b}.

(3j)

Therefore, the Bargmann index are related with the number difference between two modes.

One can obtain the two-mode Rabi model
[
27
,
31
,
36
]
by substituting
\hat{K}_{0}
and
\hat{K}_{\pm}
in (
3e
) with those in (
3i
), which leads to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{2m}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}-\omega_{\mathrm{2m}}

(3k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega_{\mathrm{2m}}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\right)+g_{\mathrm{2m}}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left(\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}\hat{b}\right),

with
\omega_{\mathrm{2m}}=\omega/2
and
g_{\mathrm{2m}}=g
.

3.3
Intensity-dependent Rabi model

In the Holstein-Primakoff realization, the generators can be expressed as

\displaystyle\hat{K}_{0}=\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+k,\quad\hat{K}_{+}=\sqrt{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+2k-1}\hat{a}^{\dagger},\quad\hat{K}_{-}=\hat{a}\sqrt{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+2k-1},

(3l)

where
k
is the Bargmann index. In this case, the basis state
\left|{}k,m\right>
is nothing but the Fock state, namely,
\left|{}k,m\right>=\left|{}m\right>_{a}
.

One can obtain the intensity-dependent Rabi model
[
31
,
37
]
by substituting
\hat{K}_{0}
and
\hat{K}_{\pm}
in (
3e
) with those in (
3l
), which leads to

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{I}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{H}-k\omega_{\mathrm{I}}

(3m)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{2}\hat{\sigma}_{z}+\omega_{\mathrm{I}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+g_{\mathrm{I}}\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left(\sqrt{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+2k-1}\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a}\sqrt{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+2k-1}\right),

with
\omega_{\mathrm{I}}=\omega
and
g_{\mathrm{I}}=g
. The well-known Buck-Sukumar model
[
29
]
is recovered if we choose
k=\frac{1}{2}
and perform the RWA.

4
Methods

Up to a global shift, three nonlinear Rabi models can be expressed in a united form
\hat{H}
(
3e
) in terms of the su(1,1) generators. Instead of solving three nonlinear Rabi models individually, we only need to find the analytical solutions to
\hat{H}
itself.

In the basis state of
\{\left|{}\pm\right>\}
which satisfy
\hat{\sigma}_{x}\left|{}\pm\right>=\pm\left|{}\pm\right>
, Hamiltonian (
3e
) can be written in a matrix form,

\hat{H}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\omega\hat{K}_{0}+g\left(\hat{K}_{+}+\hat{K}_{-}\right)&-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\\
-\frac{\epsilon}{2}&\omega\hat{K}_{0}-g\left(\hat{K}_{+}+\hat{K}_{-}\right)\end{array}\right).

(3n)

Since diagonal elements only consist of the linear combination of the su(1,1) generators, one can easily achieve its eigenstates and eigenvalues which satisfy

\left[\omega\hat{K}_{0}\pm g\left(\hat{K}_{+}+\hat{K}_{-}\right)\right]\left|{}k,m\right>_{\pm}=\beta\left(k+m\right)\left|{}k,m\right>_{\pm},

(3o)

with
\beta=\sqrt{\omega^{2}-4g^{2}}
,
r=\mathrm{arctanh}\left(\frac{2g}{\omega}\right)
and

\displaystyle\left|{}k,m\right>_{\pm}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{S}(\mp r)\left|{}k,m\right>,

(3p)

\displaystyle\hat{S}(\mp r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\left[\mp\frac{r}{2}\left(\hat{K}_{+}-\hat{K}_{-}\right)\right].

(3q)

For the one-mode and two-mode realizations,
\hat{S}(\mp r)
corresponds to the well-known Bogoliubov transformation or squeezing operator
[
2
]
. It should be noted that when
g\rightarrow\omega/2
,
\beta
tends to zero, which leads to spectral collapse
[
15
,
24
,
26
,
28
,
31
]
. Beyond the spectral collapse point (
g>\omega/2
), the nonlinear Rabi model becomes no longer self-adjoint
[
28
]
. Therefore, we only focus on
0<g<\omega/2
in this paper.

To achieve the eigenstates of
\hat{H}
(
3n
), one can construct an ansatz which is written as a superpostion of
\{\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\}
, namely,

\displaystyle\left|{}\psi\right>=\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\\
d_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\end{array}\right).

From the Schrödinger equation
\hat{H}\left|{}\psi\right>=E\left|{}\psi\right>
, the expansion coefficients
c_{m}
and
d_{m}
should satisfy

\displaystyle\beta\left(k+m\right)c_{m}-\frac{\epsilon}{2}d_{m}=Ec_{m},

(3u)

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon}{2}c_{m}+\beta\left[\cosh 2r\left(k+m\right)d_{m}-\frac{\sinh 2r}{2}\left(\sqrt{m(m+2k-1)}d_{m-1}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.+\sqrt{(m+1)(m+2k)}d_{m+1}\right)\right]=Ed_{m}.

(3v)

Equation (
3u
) leads to

\displaystyle c_{m}=\frac{\epsilon/2}{\beta\left(k+m\right)-E}d_{m}.

(3w)

Substituting the above equation into (
3v
), we can obtain a three-term recurrence relation for
d_{m}
, namely,

\displaystyle d_{m+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{m}d_{m}-R_{m-1}d_{m-1}.

(3x)

with

\displaystyle T_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\left(\beta\cosh 2r\left(k+m\right)-\frac{\epsilon^{2}/4}{\beta\left(k+m\right)-E}-E\right)}{\beta\sinh 2r\sqrt{(m+1)(m+2k)}},

(3y)

\displaystyle R_{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{(m+1)(m+2k)}{(m+2)(m+2k+1)}}.

(3z)

In the limit of
m\rightarrow+\infty
, we find that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{d_{m+1}}{d_{m}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\tanh r=\frac{2g}{\omega}<1\\
\\
\coth r=\frac{\omega}{2g}>1\end{array}\right..

Therefore, equation (
3x
) has two linearly independent solutions
d_{m}^{(0)}
and
d_{m}^{(1)}
with the following limit behaviors respectively,

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{d_{m+1}^{(0)}}{d_{m}^{(0)}}=\frac{2g}{\omega},\quad\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{d_{m+1}^{(1)}}{d_{m}^{(1)}}=\frac{\omega}{2g}.

(3ae)

Generally,
d_{m}
can be written as

\displaystyle d_{m}=A^{(0)}d_{m}^{(0)}+A^{(1)}d_{m}^{(1)}.

(3af)

Due to
0<g<\omega/2
, we expect that the eigenstates correspond to
A^{(1)}=0
, otherwise it will lead to divergence.

4.1
Exact isolated solutions

Special attention should be paid to (
3u
). When the energy satisfies
E=\beta\left(k+M\right)
with
M=1,2,3,\dots
, the expansion coefficient
c_{m=M}
and the corresponding wavefunction will not diverge only if
d_{m=M}=0
.

E=\beta\left(k+M\right)
is called the baseline energy
[
38
,
39
]
.
From the three-term recurrence relation (
3x
),
d_{M}=0
corresponds to

\displaystyle\left|\begin{array}[]{ccccc}-T_{0}&1\\
R_{0}&-T_{1}&1\\
&R_{1}&-T_{2}&1\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&R_{M-2}&-T_{M-1}\end{array}\right|=0,

which gives a relation between the system parameters
\epsilon
,
\omega
and
g
. When this relation is satisfied,
E=\beta\left(k+M\right)
is the eigenenergy, also known as the exact isolated solutions. The eigenstates corresponding to the exact isolated solutions can be reduced to a closed form as follows,

\displaystyle\left|{}\psi\right>=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\sum_{m=0}^{M}c_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\\
\sum_{m=0}^{M-1}d_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\end{array}\right),

where
d_{m<M}
and
c_{m<M}
are determined by (
3x
) and (
3w
) respectively.
c_{M}
is determined by (
3v
) with
d_{M}=d_{M+1}=0
, namely,

\displaystyle-\frac{\epsilon}{2}c_{M}-\beta\frac{\sinh 2r}{2}\sqrt{M(M+2k-1)}d_{M-1}=0.

(3ap)

Such kinds of exact isolated solutions were first discovered by Judd in the Jahn-Teller model
[
38
]
, which correspond to the level crossings in the energy spectrum. Soon after that, Reik
et al
found them in the linear Rabi model
[
39
]
. The exact isolated solutions for two-photon and two-mode Rabi models were also brought into the spotlight
[
25
,
27
,
26
,
28
]
. A new type of exact isolated solutions, also known as the dark-like state, were found when generalizing it to the multi-qubit cases
[
40
,
41
,
42
]
. However, less attention has been paid to those in the intensity-dependent Rabi model.

4.2
G-functions

Beyond the exact isolated solutions, one can set
d_{0}=1
in general, while
d_{m>0}
as a function of
E
can be achieved from the three-term recurrence relation (
3x
) successively. Due to the parity symmetry, the eigenstates of
\hat{H}
should also be those of the parity operator
\hat{\Pi}
, namely,
\hat{\Pi}\left|{}\psi\right>=\Pi\left|{}\psi\right>
. The left-hand side corresponds to

\displaystyle\hat{\Pi}\left|{}\psi\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\hat{\sigma}_{z}\otimes\hat{T}\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\\
d_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}d_{m}(-1)^{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{-}\\
c_{m}(-1)^{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{-}\end{array}\right),

while the right-hand side corresponds to

\displaystyle\Pi\left|{}\psi\right>

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}c_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\\
d_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}\end{array}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}d_{m}(-1)^{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+},

(3ay)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}c_{m}(-1)^{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{-}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Pi\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}d_{m}\left|{}k,m\right>_{+}.

(3az)

It should be noted that (
3ay
) and (
3az
) are equivalent, since they can be transformed to each other by the unitary transformation
\hat{T}
. Note that
[
34
,
35
]

\displaystyle\langle{}k,0|{}k,m\rangle_{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}\langle{}k,0|{}k,m\rangle_{-}

(3ba)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)^{k}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(2k+m)}{m!\Gamma(2k)}}\xi^{m},

with
\xi=\tanh\frac{r}{2}
.
Projecting (
3ay
) onto
\left|{}k,0\right>
, we achieve the G-function as follows,

\displaystyle G_{k}^{\Pi}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}d_{m}(-1)^{m}\langle{}k,0|{}k,m\rangle_{-}-\Pi c_{m}\langle{}k,0|{}k,m\rangle_{+}

(3bb)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{+\infty}\Xi_{k,m}^{\Pi}(E)\xi^{m},

with

\displaystyle\Xi_{k,m}^{\Pi}(E)=\left(d_{m}-\Pi c_{m}\right)\left(1-\xi^{2}\right)^{k}\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma(2k+m)}{m!\Gamma(2k)}},

(3bc)

where we have employed (
3ba
).
G_{k}^{\Pi}(E)
can be regarded as a power series in
\xi
. From (
4
), we can find that

\displaystyle\lim_{m\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\Xi_{k,m+1}^{\Pi}}{\Xi_{k,m}^{\Pi}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\coth r,

(3bd)

which indicates that the radius of convergence satisfies
R\geq\tanh r
. Therefore,
G_{k}^{\Pi}(E)
is well-defined and will always converge due to
\xi<R
. The roots of
G_{k}^{\Pi}(E)=0
determine the eigenenergies, with which we can obtain the eigenstates according to (

).

The G-functions of the two-photon and two-mode Rabi models

have been analyzed separately, whereas few studies focused on the intensity-dependent Rabi model. In this paper, three nonlinear Rabi models can be described by a unified G-function (

6
Summary

In the last decades, exploring the strong and nonlinear coupling between light and matter has achieved great processes. The interest in the nonlinear Rabi models has blossomed both experimentally and theoretically. In this paper, we focus on three typical nonlinear Rabi models: two-photon, two-mode and intensity-dependent Rabi models, and propose a unified analytical approach.

Previous studies mainly dealt with three models individually, and their common behaviors didn’t receive sufficient attention. By virtue of different realizations of the su(1,1) Lie algebra, three models can be described by the same Hamiltonian with
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
symmetry. By choosing appropriate basis states, we construct an ansatz to describe the eigenstates, whose expansion coefficients satisfy a three-term recurrence relation.
Of special significance is the baseline energy identifying the exact isolated solutions at the level crossings between different parities, for which the eigenstates can be reduced to a closed form.
We reproduce the exact isolated solutions in the two-photon and two-mode Rabi models, whereas those in the intensity-dependent Rabi models are first achieved.

Beyond the exact isolated solutions, we propose a unified G-function based on the
\mathcal{Z}_{2}
symmetry, whose roots give the regular spectrum. The expansion coefficients of the eigenstates present an exponential decay behavior in the limit of
m\rightarrow+\infty
, and the decay rate
\gamma_{\mathrm{d}}
can be achieved analytically. With increasing coupling strength
g/\omega
, the decay rate
\gamma_{\mathrm{d}}
decreases and it tends to zero in the spectral collapse point
g\rightarrow\omega/2
.

With the unified analytical approach, we achieve the eigenstates and eigenenergies of three nonlinear Rabi models, and their common behaviors are addressed. The nonlinear Rabi model introduces new physical mechanisms which cannot be captured by the linear one. The squeezing effect is inherent in the nonlinear Rabi model, compared to the linear one which is obvious only if the frequency of the two-level system is large enough
[
43
]
. The exotic nonlinear phenomena, the squeezing effect, and their applications in quantum information deserve further consideration, which are left to future research.

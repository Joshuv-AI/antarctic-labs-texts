Pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone Boson Production from Inflaton Coupling during Reheating
Kunio Kaneta, Sung Mook Lee, Kin-ya Oda, Tomo Takahashi

Abstract
The existence of pseudo-Nambu-Goldstone boson (pNGB) fields is a common feature in many models beyond the Standard Model, characterized by their exclusive derivative couplings. This paper investigates a scenario where a pNGB is coupled to the inflaton field during the reheating phase of the early universe. We calculate the perturbative decay rate of a coherently oscillating inflaton into pNGBs on a general basis, considering both constant and field-dependent couplings with monomial potentials at the minimum. As a concrete application, we explore the production of axions when the radial mode of the Peccei-Quinn (PQ) scalar serves as the inflaton, particularly in the presence of a large gravitational non-minimal coupling. Our findings suggest that the presence of pNGBs during reheating can lead to significant non-thermal relics, offering new constraints on inflationary reheating models and providing potential observational signatures in the form of dark radiation.

2
Inflaton Decay to pNGB during the Reheating

The inflation and reheating stages are characterized by the dynamics of a coherently oscillating inflaton field
\phi
during the reheating phase following inflation.
Due to its large occupation number, this coherent field configuration can be treated as a classical field
[
33
]
.

Depending on the potential
V(\phi)
, the field evolves according to the Klein-Gordon equation in an expanding universe:

\displaystyle\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+\frac{dV}{d\phi}=0,

(2)

where
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter with a dot being derivative with respect to time
t
.
Broadly speaking, the Hubble friction term causes the amplitude of the field
\phi
to gradually decrease (slow mode), while the
dV/d\phi
term induces fast oscillations in the inflaton field (fast mode).

In this study, we assume that the potential minimum during reheating can be approximated as a monomial function
V\propto\phi^{m}
, specifically:

\displaystyle V(\phi)=\begin{cases}\frac{m_{\phi}^{2}}{2}\phi^{2}&(m=2)\\
\frac{\lambda}{4}\phi^{4}&(m=4)\\
\frac{\kappa}{6}\phi^{6}&(m=6)\end{cases}

(3)

where
m_{\phi}
is the mass of the field
\phi
, and
\lambda
and
\kappa
are self-coupling constants. Note that
\kappa
has a mass dimension of
[\kappa]=-2
.

For definite examples, the field
\phi
has approximate solutions of the form:

\displaystyle\phi(t)\simeq\begin{cases}\phi_{0}\cos(m_{\phi}t)&(m=2)\\
\phi_{0}\,\text{cd}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\lambda\phi_{0}^{2}}{2}}t,-1\right)&(m=4)\end{cases}

(4)

where
\phi_{0}
is the overall amplitude (envelope) of the field
\phi
, and ‘
cd
’ is one of the Jacobi elliptic functions.
There is no explicit analytic solution for
m=6
.
Neglecting the expansion of the universe (equivalent to setting the second term of Eq. (
2
) to zero),
\phi_{0}
remains constant but generally decreases slowly with the universe’s expansion, which we refer to as the slow mode.

For later convenience, we introduce the effective mass parameters:

\displaystyle(m_{\phi}^{\rm eff})^{2}\equiv\left.\frac{\partial^{2}V}{\partial\phi^{2}}\right|_{\phi=\phi_{0}}=\begin{cases}m_{\phi}^{2}&(m=2)\\
3\lambda\phi_{0}^{2}&(m=4)\\
5\kappa\phi_{0}^{4}&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(5)

We also decompose the field as:

\displaystyle\phi(t)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{n}e^{-in\omega t}.

(6)

where
\omega\equiv 2\pi/T
is the leading, fundamental frequency with the period
T

[
34
,
35
]
.
Explicitly,

\displaystyle\omega=m_{\phi}^{\rm eff}\times\begin{cases}1&(m=2)\\
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{6}}\frac{\Gamma(3/4)}{\Gamma(5/4)}&(m=4)\\
\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{15}}\frac{\Gamma(2/3)}{\Gamma(7/6)}&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(7)

For instance, in the
m=2
case,
\phi_{\pm 1}=1/2
and zero otherwise. In the
m=4
case,

\displaystyle\phi_{n}=\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma(3/4)}{\Gamma(5/4)}\frac{e^{-n\pi/2}}{1+e^{-n\pi}}&(n~\text{odd})\\
0&(n~\text{even})\end{cases}

\displaystyle(m=4).

(8)

In what follows, we will derive the decay rates of
\phi
to
\chi
,
\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}
, from the
\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}
and
\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}
couplings by treating the
\phi
field as a classical, external field.

2.1
\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}
Coupling

As an explicit starting point, let us consider the following interaction:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-g\phi(\partial\chi)^{2},

(9)

where the coupling
g
has mass dimension
[g]=-1
.
Treating
\phi
as an external current, this can be interpreted as an interaction term in the Hamiltonian:

\displaystyle V(t)=g\phi(t)\int d^{3}\vec{x}\,(\partial\chi)^{2}.

(10)

The production rate of the
\chi
field is then given by:

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi}\omega^{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{4}|\phi_{n}|^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{32\pi}\langle\ddot{\phi}^{2}\rangle

(11)

where
\langle\,\cdot\,\rangle
denotes the time average. See Appendix
A
for details of the calculation.
By comparing the energy loss of the inflaton field
\phi
and the energy gain of the
\chi
field, i.e.,
\rho_{\phi}\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}\Delta t=E_{\chi}\Gamma\Delta t
for some infinitesimal time interval
\Delta t
where
E_{\chi}
is the mean energy of the two-particle state, the decay rate of the inflaton energy density is given by:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}=\Gamma\frac{E_{\chi}}{\rho_{\phi}}

(12)

with

\displaystyle E_{\chi}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{\sum_{n}\int d^{3}\vec{p}\,d^{3}\vec{q}\,\delta(\vec{p}+\vec{q})E_{f}\delta(E_{f}-n\omega)|\mathcal{M}_{n}|^{2}}{\sum_{n}\int d^{3}\vec{p}\,d^{3}\vec{q}\,\delta(\vec{p}+\vec{q})\delta(E_{f}-n\omega)|\mathcal{M}_{n}|^{2}}

(13)

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n\omega)^{5}|\phi_{n}|^{2}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n\omega)^{4}|\phi_{n}|^{2}}=\omega\begin{cases}1&(m=2)\\
1.290&(m=4)\\
1.700&(m=6)\end{cases}

where we used the matrix element for mode
n
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}=-4\pi ig\phi_{n}p

(14)

for the massless pNGB with momentum
p=|\vec{p}|
. Additionally,

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\ddot{\phi}^{2}\rangle}{\rho_{\phi}}=\begin{cases}m_{\phi}^{2}&(m=2)\\
0.365(m_{\phi}^{\rm eff})^{2}&(m=4)\\
0.230(m_{\phi}^{\rm eff})^{2}&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(15)

Using these results, we obtain the inflaton decay rate as:

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{\mathcal{A}_{m}g^{2}(m_{\phi}^{\rm eff})^{3}}{32\pi},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{m}\equiv\begin{cases}1&(m=2)\\
0.231&(m=4)\\
0.130&(m=6)\end{cases}

(16)

where we introduce
\mathcal{A}_{m}
for later convenience.

For
g=1/f_{\chi}
, the decay rate is given by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}=\frac{m_{\phi}^{3}}{32\pi f_{\chi}^{2}}

(17)

in the quadratic case, which is what one would obtain using perturbative QFT calculations.
For
m\geq 4
cases, there is a suppression compared to naive particle-picture results, as indicated by
\mathcal{A}_{m}<1
.

2.2
\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}
Coupling

Our previous calculation can be straightforwardly generalized to the case of the coupling

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-y\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2},

(18)

where the coupling
y
has mass dimension
[y]=-2
.
First, we introduce
\zeta_{n}
parameters as

\displaystyle\phi^{2}(t)-\langle\phi^{2}\rangle=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\zeta_{n}e^{-in\omega t}.

(19)

where we subtract time-independent
\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
factor. By replacing
\phi_{n}
with
\zeta_{n}
from Eq. (
11
) and Eq. (
13
), we have

\displaystyle\Gamma=\frac{y^{2}}{16\pi}\omega^{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{4}|\zeta_{n}|^{2}.

(20)

The mean energy
E_{\chi}
is given by:

\displaystyle E_{\chi}=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n\omega)^{5}|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(n\omega)^{4}|\zeta_{n}|^{2}}=\omega\begin{cases}1&(m=2)\\
1.007&(m=4)\\
1.019&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(21)

This results in

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}=\mathcal{B}_{m}\frac{y^{2}}{2\pi}m_{\phi}^{\rm eff}\rho_{\phi},

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{m}=\begin{cases}1&(m=2)\\
1.237&(m=4)\\
0.355&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(22)

3
PNGB Abundance

The energy density of the inflaton field is described by the Boltzmann equation
2
2

2

Notice that this choice of the definition for
\Gamma_{\phi\to{\rm all}}
corresponds to introducing the dissipation term in the equation of motion by
\ddot{\phi}+(3H+\Gamma_{\phi\to{\rm all}}/(1+w_{\phi}))\dot{\phi}+dV/d\phi=0
. See Ref.
[
36
]
for comparison.
We choose this definition to make the computation of the inflaton decay rate simpler.

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\phi}+3H(1+w_{\phi})\rho_{\phi}\simeq-\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\text{all}}\rho_{\phi}

(23)

where
w_{\phi}
is the effective equation of state given by

\displaystyle w_{\phi}\equiv\frac{\langle\rho_{\phi}\rangle}{\langle p_{\phi}\rangle}=\frac{m-2}{m+2}=\begin{cases}0&(m=2)\\
1/3&(m=4)\\
1/2&(m=6)\end{cases}.

(24)

based on the virial theorem,
\langle\dot{\phi}^{2}\rangle=m\langle V\rangle
. Here,
\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\text{all}}
is the total decay rate of the inflaton field.
In this paper, we assume that reheating ends when the Hubble rate at that time becomes comparable to the total decay rate, i.e.,
\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\text{all}}\simeq H
.

During the earlier stages of reheating, with
H\gg\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\text{all}}
, we can neglect the decay term, and the energy density follows a simple power law

\displaystyle\rho_{\phi}=\rho_{e}\left(\frac{a}{a_{e}}\right)^{-3(1+w_{\phi})}

(25)

where
\rho_{e}
and
a_{e}
are the energy density of the inflaton field and the scale factor at the end of inflation (i.e., the start of reheating), respectively. This implies
\phi_{0}\propto a^{-6/(m+2)}
.

Simultaneously, the evolution of the energy density of the relativistic pNGB field
\rho_{\chi}
is governed by another Boltzmann equation

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\chi}+4H\rho_{\chi}\simeq\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}\rho_{\phi}

(26)

where the decay rate of the inflaton to the axion,
\Gamma_{\phi\rightarrow\chi}
, has been obtained in Section
2
.
We neglect the backreaction of the
\chi
field on the dynamics of the
\phi
field, which is valid as long as
\rho_{\chi}\ll\rho_{\phi}
.

In this section, we consider the amount of non-thermal, relativistic pNGB remnant for each coupling
\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}
and
\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}
.
The results depend significantly on whether we assume constant coupling or field-dependent coupling.
Specifically, we consider cases where
g\propto\phi_{0}^{-1}
in Eq. (
9
) and
y\propto\phi_{0}^{-2}
in Eq. (
18
).

Also, it is convenient to change the variable from cosmological time
t
to the scale factor
a
.
In terms of
a
, the left-hand side of Eq. (
26
) is rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{\chi}+4H\rho_{\chi}=\frac{1}{a^{4}}\frac{d}{dt}(a^{4}\rho_{\chi})=H_{e}\frac{1}{a^{3}}\left(\frac{a_{e}}{a}\right)^{3(1+w_{\phi})/2}\frac{d}{da}(a^{4}\rho_{\chi})

(27)

where we used
H=H_{e}\left(a_{e}/a\right)^{3(1+w_{\phi})/2}
with
H_{e}
being the Hubble parameter at the end of the inflation.

\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}

(28)

•

\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}
Coupling

\displaystyle\rho_{\chi}=\begin{cases}\frac{y^{2}m_{\phi}}{\pi}\frac{\rho_{e}^{2}}{H_{e}}\left[\left(\frac{a_{e}}{a}\right)^{4}-\left(\frac{a_{e}}{a}\right)^{9/2}\right]&(m=2)\\
\frac{y^{2}}{2\sqrt{3}\pi}\mathcal{B}_{4}\frac{\rho_{e}^{2}\phi_{e}\sqrt{\lambda}}{H_{e}}\left[\left(\frac{a_{e}}{a}\right)^{4}-\left(\frac{a_{e}}{a}\right)^{7}\right]&(m=4)\\

(29)

Note that only the
\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}
coupling with

\propto a^{-4}

\phi

\displaystyle V_{E}(\phi)\simeq\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{2}}{6\xi^{2}}\left(\phi-\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\xi f_{\chi}^{2}}{M_{P}}\right)^{2}.

(36)

At first, we can neglect the vacuum expectation value (vev) of the inflaton field
\phi_{\rm vev}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\xi f_{\chi}^{2}}{M_{P}}
but in the end
\phi
stabilizes to
\phi_{\rm vev}
.
Note that, while the vev of Jordan frame field
\varphi
is
f_{\chi}
, going to Einstein field changes the field value of the vev different from
f_{\chi}
.

Therefore, we can approximate the behavior of the inflaton field
\phi
as

\displaystyle\phi\simeq\begin{cases}\phi_{0}\cos(m_{\phi}t)&(\phi_{0}>\phi_{\rm vev})\\
\phi_{\rm vev}&(\phi_{0}<\phi_{\rm vev})\end{cases}.

(37)

where
m_{\phi}=\sqrt{\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{2}}{3\xi^{2}}}
, and slowly time varying envelop
\phi_{0}=\phi_{e}\left(a/a_{e}\right)^{-3/2}
. Also, from the Lagrangian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\ni\frac{1}{2}\varphi^{2}(\partial\theta)^{2}\simeq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{M_{P}}{\xi}\phi(\partial\theta)^{2},

(38)

we normalize
\theta

\displaystyle\chi\equiv\left(\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{M_{P}}{\xi}\right)^{1/2}\theta\times\begin{cases}\phi_{0}^{1/2}&(\phi_{0}\geq\phi_{\rm vev})\\
\phi_{\rm vev}^{1/2}&(\phi_{0}<\phi_{\rm vev}).\end{cases}

(39)

Here, we define
a_{\rm tr}
with the condition
\phi_{0}(a_{\rm tr})=\phi_{\rm vev}
, hence
a_{\rm tr}=a_{e}\left(\phi_{e}/\phi_{\rm vev}\right)^{2/3}
. The deformation of the potential and its coupling to the radial mode due to the non-minimal gravitational coupling, even at the vev, is a novel feature that arises when considering a large axion decay constant,
f_{\chi}>M_{P}/\xi
. In the case of Higgs inflation, the electroweak scale is always much smaller than
M_{P}/\xi
, so we do not expect significant modifications from the non-minimal coupling near the vacuum.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the potential implications of the reheating phase following inflation, focusing specifically on the decay of the inflaton field into pNGBs, including axions. We derived general formulas for the decay rates of inflaton to pNGBs under both constant and field-dependent coupling scenarios, assuming a monomial potential
V(\phi)\propto\phi^{m}
with
m=(2,4,6)
.

Our analysis revealed that the presence of pNGBs during reheating, which appear universally in many BSM models, can lead to the production of non-thermal relics. These relics may manifest as dark radiation and influence the effective number of neutrino species
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
, providing potential observational signatures. This connection offers opportunities to detect BSM remnants from the reheating phase in the early universe.

We applied our general results to a specific model where the inflaton is identified with the radial mode of the PQ scalar field, which possesses a large non-minimal coupling to gravity
\xi
. Our findings indicate that the bounds on
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
impose significant constraints on the reheating temperature, particularly when the axion decay constant
f_{\chi}
is smaller than
M_{P}/\xi
.

Our work highlights that the reheating stage, often considered a black box, may have rich phenomenological consequences associated with the coupling between inflaton and pNGBs.

Appendix A
Born Approximation

In this section, we present the details of the calculation of the decay rate using the Born approximation, which we omitted in the main text.

As a definite case, let us consider the following interaction first:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-g\phi(\partial\chi)^{2}.

(47)

Here, we treat
\phi(t)
as a classical field, and only promote
\chi
field as a quantum field
\hat{\chi}
:

\displaystyle\hat{\chi}=\int\frac{d^{3}\vec{p}}{(2\pi)^{3/2}\sqrt{2E_{p}}}\left(e^{ipx}a_{\vec{p}}+e^{-ipx}a_{\vec{p}}^{\dagger}\right).

(48)

For the brevity, we will omit hat hereafter. In the Hamiltonian, this gives the interaction

\displaystyle V(t)=g\phi(t)\int d^{3}\vec{x}\,(\partial\chi)^{2}.

(49)

From this interaction, what we are interested in is the transition amplitude from the vacuum
|0\rangle
to the final state with two
\chi
s with momentum
\vec{k}_{1}
and
\vec{k}_{2}
, i.e.
|\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2}\rangle
:

\displaystyle\langle\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2}|g\phi(t)(\partial_{\mu}\chi)(\partial^{\mu}\chi)|0\rangle

(50)

\displaystyle=-g\int d^{4}x\,\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{3}\sqrt{E_{k_{1}}E_{k_{2}}}}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\left(E_{k_{1}}E_{k_{2}}-\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}_{2}\right)e^{i(E_{k_{1}}+E_{k_{2}}-n\omega)t}e^{i(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\cdot\vec{x}}

(51)

\displaystyle=-2\pi g\delta(\vec{k}_{1}+\vec{k}_{2})\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\phi_{n}\frac{E_{k_{1}}^{2}+|\vec{k}_{1}|^{2}}{E_{k_{1}}}\delta(2E_{k_{1}}-n\omega)

(52)

Then the production rate of
\chi
field becomes

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{2\pi}(2\pi g)^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\phi_{n}|^{2}\int\frac{d^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(2E_{k}-\omega)\frac{(E_{k}^{2}+|\vec{k}|^{2})^{2}}{E_{k}^{2}}

(53)

\displaystyle=4\pi g^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\phi_{n}|^{2}\int\frac{4\pi k^{2}\,dk}{(2\pi)^{3}}\delta(2k-n\omega)\cdot k^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{16\pi}\omega^{4}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{4}|\phi_{n}|^{2}=\frac{g^{2}}{32\pi}\langle\ddot{\phi}^{2}\rangle

This corresponds to Eq. (
11
) in the main text. The results for the
\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}=-y\phi^{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}
case can be obtained using the same steps.

Appendix B
Inflation with Large-Non-minimal Coupling

In this section, we summarize the essential results of inflation with large gravitational non-minimal coupling
[
40
,
60
,
8
]
, which we use in Section
4
. The main purpose is to clarify many details used in the inflation model.

Let us first consider the Lagrangian in Jordan frame with field
\varphi

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}\sqrt{-g_{J}}\left[-\frac{M^{2}+\xi\varphi^{2}}{2}R_{J}+\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}-V(\varphi)\right]

(54)

with
M^{2}\equiv M_{P}^{2}-\xi f_{\chi}^{2}
and
g_{J}\equiv\det g_{J\mu\nu}
.

We redefine the metric

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}\equiv\Omega^{2}g_{J\mu\nu},

\displaystyle\Omega^{2}\equiv\frac{M_{P}^{2}+\xi(\varphi^{2}-f_{\chi}^{2})}{M_{P}^{2}}.

(55)

Then, the transformed action becomes

\displaystyle S=\int d^{4}\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{M_{P}}{2}R+\frac{1}{2}\Pi(\varphi)(\partial\varphi)^{2}-\frac{V(\varphi)}{\Omega^{4}}\right]

(56)

where

\displaystyle\Pi(\varphi)\equiv\frac{\Omega^{2}+6\xi^{2}\varphi^{2}/M_{P}^{2}}{\Omega^{4}}

(57)

motivating us to introduce the canonicalized field
d\phi/d\varphi=\sqrt{\Pi(\varphi)}
. This corresponds to Eq. (
33
). This expression can be integrated analytically
[
43
,
60
]
. Here, instead of presenting full expressions, we provide approximated ones with comparison to the exact one:

\displaystyle\phi\simeq\begin{cases}\varphi&\left(\varphi\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{M_{P}}{\xi}\right)\\
\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\xi\varphi^{2}}{M_{P}}&\left(\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{M_{P}}{\xi}\lesssim\varphi\ll\frac{M_{P}}{\sqrt{\xi}}\right)\\
6M_{P}\log\left(\frac{\sqrt{\xi}\varphi}{M_{P}}\right)&\left(\varphi\gg\frac{M_{P}}{\sqrt{\xi}}\right)\end{cases}

(58)

During the inflationary regime with large field value, this implies that

\displaystyle V_{E}(\phi)\simeq\frac{\lambda M_{P}^{4}}{4\xi^{2}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\phi}{M_{P}}\right)\right]^{2}.

(59)

The end of inflation is defined when one of the slow-roll parameters,
\epsilon_{V}\simeq\frac{M_{P}^{2}}{2}\left(\frac{V_{E}^{\prime}}{V_{E}}\right)^{2},
becomes unity, providing the field value at the end of inflation,
\phi_{e}\simeq 0.94M_{P}
. This is also regarded as the initial field value at the beginning of the reheating stage.

CMB pivot scale corresponds to where the expansion happens about
\log{(a_{e}/a_{*})}\equiv N_{e}\simeq 50-60
where
a_{i}
is the scale factor of the universe when the modes corresponding to CMB observations leave the horizon. In terms of the potential, it is approximated as

\displaystyle N_{e}\simeq\int_{\phi_{*}}^{\phi_{e}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\epsilon_{V}}}\frac{d\phi}{M_{P}}

(60)

implying
\phi_{*}\simeq 5M_{P}
.

The observational result on the amplitude of the scalar perturbations
A_{s}\simeq 2.1\times 10^{-9}

[
38
]
can be interpreted as a bound on the scale of inflation

\displaystyle A_{s}\simeq\frac{H_{\rm inf}}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon_{V*}M_{P}^{2}}

(61)

where
\epsilon_{V*}\equiv\epsilon_{V}(\phi_{*})\simeq 3/(4N_{e}^{2})
. This dictates the normalization
\xi^{2}/\lambda\simeq 2.5\times 10^{9}

[
40
]
. This condition is used throughout the main text.

Appendix C
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
Bound

When there exists an extra relativistic degree of freedom
X
, this would also add extra energy density
\rho_{X}
. This amount is usually parameterized by
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
, as the ratio with respect to the energy density of the neutrinos,

\displaystyle\Delta N_{\rm eff}\equiv\frac{\rho_{X}}{\rho_{\nu}}.

(62)

In terms of the energy density of the photon,
\rho_{X}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\rho_{X}(a_{0})=\Delta N_{\text{eff}}\cdot\frac{7}{8}\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})

(63)

with
a_{0}
being scale factor of today. The Planck constraints on
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
is
\Delta N_{\rm eff}\lesssim 0.3

[
9
]
.

The goal of this appendix is to derive the bound on the energy density at the time of the reheating from
\Delta N_{\rm eff}
bound. In the main text, we specify
X
as
\chi
.

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{0})}{\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})}=\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{\rm reh})}{\rho_{r}(a_{\rm reh})}\cdot\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{0})/\rho_{X}(a_{\rm reh})}{\rho_{r}(a_{0})/\rho_{r}(a_{\rm reh})}\cdot\frac{\rho_{r}(a_{0})}{\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})}

(64)

where
\rho_{r}
is the energy density of the total radiation, i.e.
\rho_{r}=\rho_{\gamma}+\rho_{\nu}+\rho_{X}
although the contribution from
\rho_{X}
is negligible. Here, assuming that
X
decouples all other degrees of freedom,
\rho_{X}\propto a^{-4}
, while the total radiation receive corrections by having different effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom
g_{*}(T)
as the temperature decreases:

\displaystyle\rho_{r}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{*}(T)T^{4}.

(65)

On the other hand, from the entropy conservation, we have
g_{*s}(T)T^{3}a^{3}=\text{const}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{0})/\rho_{X}(a_{\rm reh})}{\rho_{r}(a_{0})/\rho_{r}(a_{\rm reh})}=\frac{g_{*}(T_{\text{reh}})^{-1/3}}{g_{*}(T_{0})g_{*s}(T_{0})^{-4/3}}.

(66)

where we assumed
g_{*}(T_{\rm reh})=g_{*s}(T_{\rm reh})
at high temperature.

Finally, this implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{\rm reh})}{\rho_{r}(a_{\rm reh})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{X}(a_{0})}{\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})}\left(\frac{\rho_{r}(a_{0})}{\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})}\right)^{-1}\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\text{reh}})^{-1/3}}{g_{*}(T_{0})g_{*s}(T_{0})^{-4/3}}\right)^{-1}

(67)

\displaystyle=\Delta N_{\text{eff}}\cdot\frac{7}{8}\left(\frac{4}{11}\right)^{4/3}\left(\frac{\rho_{\gamma}(a_{0})}{\rho_{r}(a_{0})}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{0})}{g_{*s}(T_{0})^{4/3}}\right)g_{*}(T_{\text{reh}})^{1/3}

(68)

\displaystyle\simeq 0.10\left(\frac{\Delta N_{\rm eff}}{0.3}\right)\left(\frac{g_{*}(T_{\rm reh})}{106.75}\right)^{1/3}

(69)

where we used
g_{*}(T_{0})=3.38
,
g_{*s}(T_{0})=3.94
in the last line.

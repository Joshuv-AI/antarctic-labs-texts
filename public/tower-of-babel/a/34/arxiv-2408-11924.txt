On reduced basis methods for eigenvalue problems, and on its coupling with perturbation theory
Louis Garrigue, Benjamin Stamm

Abstract
. In this article, we study eigenvalue problems associated to self-adjoint operators and their approximation obtained by subspace projection, as used in the reduced basis method for instance. We provide error bounds between the exact eigenmodes and the approximated ones and also consider degenerate cases in the analysis. When the operator depends on a parameter, we apply the bounds assuming that the reduced space contains the derivatives of the eigenfunction with respect to the parameter. Finally, we provide some numerical examples that reflect the analytical results.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Projected space methods for eigenvalue problems

A classical issue in eigenvalue problems is to reduce the number of degrees of freedom of the studied systems by extracting only the relevant ones, leading to subspace methods, the full considered Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
being too large to be addressed in its exact form.
In particular, reduced basis method (RBM) approximations aim at approximating
\mathcal{H}
by a well-chosen low-dimensional subset
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, created via an orthogonal projector
\mathcal{P}
.
Denoting the exact self-adjoint operator by
H
, then the approximated operator of interest is

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}},

as the restriction of
\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}
to
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, whose eigenmodes we are interested in. Among other works, reduced basis problems have been investigated in the context of eigenvalue problems in
[

,

,

,

,

,

,
8
,

,
43
]
, the case of several eigenvalues was examined in
[
31
,
12
,
7
]
where more details about the RBM are considered, while here, we only focus on the approximation results. Moreover, in the previously mentioned works,
a posteriori
bounds were derived, while we provide

ones in this document. See also
[
1
,
70
,
28
,
2
,

,

]
for other mathematical documents about the approximation of eigenvalue problems.

In Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
, we first provide bounds enabling to estimate the error between the eigenmodes of the exact operator
H
and the ones of the approximated operator
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
in a general setting. We treat the degenerate (i.e. when several eigenvalues are equal) and almost-degenerate cases by using the formalism of density matrices and clusters of eigenmodes, and we treat the non-degenerate case with an eigenvector formalism. We sought to provide general bounds which could be applied to diverse settings, for instance to derive effective operators in quantum mechanics, or to discretize the Hilbert space for numerical purposes. In the vector case, it is showed that those errors are controlled by the key quantity
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi
, where
\phi
is the exact eigenvector of
H
. Since
\mathcal{P}
is chosen so that
\phi
is expected to be “almost” contained in
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi
is small in norm. A similar quantity also controls the error in the case of clusters of eigenmodes.

2.
Definitions and assumptions

for the general reduced basis method

We choose a standard but general mathematical setting which can address common operators involved in quantum mechanics, including Dirac operators, many-body Schrödinger operators and Bloch transforms of periodic operators.

2.1.
Hilbert space and operators

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space, endowed with the complex scalar product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
and the corresponding norm
\|\cdot\|
.
As a convention, we will denote elements
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
as vectors even if
\mathcal{H}
is infinite-dimensional and can be viewed as functions.

The object of study in the article is a closed self-adjoint operator
H
acting on
\mathcal{H}
, and we want to approximate some of its eigenmodes by using a subspace or reduced basis method.

For linear operators
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, we will denote by

\displaystyle\|B\|:=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in\mathcal{H}\backslash\{0\}\end{subarray}}{\text{{sup}}}\;\frac{\|B\psi\|}{\|\psi\|}

the canonical operator norm.
Further, let us take a closed self-adjoint operator
A
of
\mathcal{H}
, possibly unbounded, which will serve to define the energy norm.
We assume that
A
has a dense domain and a dense form domain and we will always assume that

\displaystyle c_{A}:=\|A^{-1}\|<+\infty,\qquad c_{H}:=\|A^{-1}HA^{-1}\|<+\infty.

(2)

For vectors
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, the energy norm is then defined by

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{e}:=\|A\varphi\|.

(3)

This norm is stronger than the norm
\|\cdot\|
. We will also make use of the notation
\|\cdot\|_{e,0}:=\|\cdot\|
and
\|\cdot\|_{e,1}:=\|\cdot\|_{e}
, so that for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
and
\delta\in\{0,1\}
, there holds
\|\varphi\|_{e,\delta}=\|A^{\delta}\varphi\|
.

For example, consider the case of the Schrödinger operator
H=-\Delta+v
acting on
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. It is then natural to choose
A=\sqrt{-\Delta}
and
\|\cdot\|_{e}
is equivalent to the Sobolev norm
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Further, for any operators
B,D
on
\mathcal{H}
, the Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product is denoted by
\left(B,D\right)_{\text{2}}:=\tr B^{*}D
, its norm
\|B\|_{2}:=\sqrt{\tr B^{*}B}
, and the corresponding normed space is the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, denoted by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}_{2}:=\{B:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H},\|B\|_{2}<+\infty\}.

(4)

For
\delta\in\{0,1\}
and any
B\in\mathfrak{S}_{2}
, we use the notation

\displaystyle\|B\|_{2,\delta}:=\|A^{\delta}B\|_{2}.

(5)

The norm
\|\cdot\|_{2,1}
, called the energy norm, is the natural one for density matrices (i.e. operators), as
\|\cdot\|_{e}
is the natural norm for vectors.

2.2.
Density matrices

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, we denote by
P_{\varphi}
the orthogonal projector onto
\mathbb{C}\varphi=\Span\{\varphi\}
. For any orthogonal projection
P
, we will use the notation
P^{\perp}:=1-P
.

The notion of the density matrices is a key-concept to formalize eigenvalue problems with degeneracies and cluster of eigenvalues.
They are analogous objects as eigenvectors, but for problems considering several eigenvectors and consist of the spectral projector onto the eigenspace spanned by those eigenvectors. For any set
{\bm{\varphi}}:=(\varphi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}^{\nu}
of orthonormal vectors, we define the corresponding density matrix

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}:=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left|\varphi_{\alpha}\right>\left<\varphi_{\alpha}\right|=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}P_{\varphi_{\alpha}},

(6)

using the convenient bra-ket notation. By orthonormality,
\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
is an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{H}
, that is
\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}^{2}=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}^{*}=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
. We denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}_{\nu}:=\{U\in\mathbb{C}^{\nu\times\nu}\;|\;U^{*}U=1\}

the group of unitary matrices of dimension
\nu
and for any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
we define its action
U{\bm{\varphi}}:=((U{\bm{\varphi}})_{\alpha})_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}
on
\mathcal{H}^{\nu}
where
(U{\bm{\varphi}})_{\alpha}:=\sum_{\beta=1}^{\nu}U_{\alpha\beta}\varphi_{\beta}
. Then, there holds
\mathcal{D}_{U{\bm{\varphi}}}=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
for all
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
and set
{\bm{\varphi}}\in\mathcal{H}^{\nu}
of orthonormal vectors.
Therefore,
\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\varphi}}}
is independent on
{\bm{\varphi}}
but only depends on the space that
{\bm{\varphi}}
spans.

2.3.
Space reduction by projection

Let us take an orthogonal projection
\mathcal{P}
on
\mathcal{H}
, we assume that
\mathcal{P}
is neither the identity nor the null projection to avoid the trivial cases, and we recall that
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=1-\mathcal{P}
. The reduced space is
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, it can, but does not need to, be infinite-dimensional and we will need to assume that

\displaystyle c_{\mathcal{P}}:=\|A\mathcal{P}A^{-1}\|<+\infty.

(7)

Our central object will be
\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}:\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, which is the restriction of
\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}
to
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, hence it is an operator of
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, while
\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}
is an operator of
\mathcal{H}
. If
d:=\dim\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
is finite, we can represent this operator by a
d\times d
matrix. We consider
\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
as an approximation of
H
, in the sense that its eigenmodes will well approximate the ones of
H
we are interested in. Remark that since
\mathcal{P}\neq 1
,
\sigma\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)=\sigma\big(\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\big)\cup\{0\}
because
\im\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\subset\Ker\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}
.

2.4.
Target set of eigenmodes

For a given
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, we focus in this work on an arbitrary set of
\nu
eigenvalues

\displaystyle\{E_{\mu}\}_{1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu}\subset\sigma(H)

in the spectrum of
H
. They are counted with multiplicity and their eigenvectors are denoted by
\phi_{\mu}
, normed such that
\|\phi_{\mu}\|=1
, and grouped into
{\bm{\phi}}:=\left(\phi_{\mu}\right)_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
. We denote the associated density matrix by

\displaystyle\Gamma:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\phi_{\mu}\right>\left<\phi_{\mu}\right|=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\phi}}}.

The purpose of taking clusters of eigenvalues, i.e.
\nu\geqslant 2
, is to be able to treat the almost-degenerate and degenerate cases, i.e. when eigenvalues are close or even equal. If the eigenvalues are not close, one can take the non-degenerate case
\nu=1
since no singular quantity in the following analysis will appear. Note that the eigenvalues
E_{\mu}
are not necessarily sorted in increasing order and not necessarily on the bottom of the spectrum.

For any operator
B
, we denote by
\sigma_{\textup{d}}(B)
the discrete spectrum of
B
, and we assume that
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
has at least
\nu
eigenvalues in its discrete spectrum.
We consider
\nu
of them, we denote them by

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\}_{1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu}\subset\sigma_{\textup{d}}\big(\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\big),

(8)

and the corresponding normalized eigenvectors by
\psi_{\mu}
, grouped into
{\bm{\psi}}:=\left(\psi_{\mu}\right)_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
. We define the associated density matrix by

\displaystyle\Lambda:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\psi_{\mu}\right>\left<\psi_{\mu}\right|=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\psi}}}.

Note that
\Lambda\mathcal{P}=\Lambda
and thus
\Lambda\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=0
.

We will study the closeness between
\phi_{\mu}
and
\psi_{\mu}
for any
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, so each level
\mu
of
H
corresponds to a level
\mu
of
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
. But they are not sorted in increasing order, so for instance if we follow a variational approach, the label
\mu
can denote the
3^{\text{rd}}
level of
H
and the
5^{\text{th}}
level of
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
, and
\phi_{\mu}-\psi_{\mu}
can be small. For example Figure
1
illustrates this principle, where
H
depends on a parameter.

2.5.
Definition of partial inverses

Finally, we need to introduce a technical definition combined with a further assumption.
For any self-adjoint operator
B
, if
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}\subset\sigma_{\textup{d}}(B)
, then there exists
\kappa_{B}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\sigma(B)\backslash\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}\right)\cap\big(\cup_{\mu=1}^{\alpha}\;]e_{\mu}-\kappa_{B},e_{\mu}+\kappa_{B}[\;\big)=\varnothing.

(9)

In addition to (
8
) we will also assume that

\displaystyle\dim\left(\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\cap\bigoplus_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\Ker\big(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}-\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\big)\right)=\nu,

(10)

to ensure that all the eigenvectors associated to the approximated eigenvalues
\{\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
are taken into account. For any

\displaystyle z\in\{\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\cup\left(\mathbb{C}\backslash\sigma\big(\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\big)\right)

we define

\displaystyle\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\right)^{-1}&\mbox{on }\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H},\\
0&\mbox{on }\Lambda\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{H},\end{array}\right.

extended by linearity on
\mathcal{H}
. Note that we used the space decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\Lambda\mathcal{H}\oplus\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}
.
We also define

\displaystyle R_{\mu}:=\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}.

(13)

Then, by (
8
) and (
9
) there exists
\kappa_{\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}}>0
such that
\|R_{\mu}\|\leqslant\kappa_{\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}}^{-1}
for any
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
.

3.
Main results on the general reduced basis method

In this section we present our main result, which is a bound on the difference between exact and approximated eigenmodes. This quantifies how close the approximation is from the exact quantity. It is a generic estimate that does not yet consider the parametrized setting, which is left for Section
5
. We use the notations as introduced in Section
2
. The case of clusters of eigenvalues is treated in Theorem
3.1
and the non-degenerate case is treated in Proposition
3.2
.

3.1.
Clusters of eigenmodes

The following result is adapted to the degenerate or almost-degenerate case, that is when several eigenvalues that we take into account are equal or close. Our goal is to quantify the difference between the eigenvectors of
H
and the ones of
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
, we use the formalism of density matrices recalled in Section
2.2
.

Theorem 3.1

(Error between exact eigenmodes and reduced basis eigenmodes)
.

Take a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and a closed self-adjoint operator
A
which is chosen to form the energy norm defined in (
5
). Take a closed self-adjoint operator
H
which eigenmodes will be approximated. Consider an orthogonal projector
\mathcal{P}
, assume that
H
and
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
have at least
\nu
eigenvalues (counted with multiplicity). We consider
\nu
eigenmodes of respectively
H
and
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
, denoted by respectively
(E_{\mu},\phi_{\mu})
and
(\mathcal{E}_{\mu},\psi_{\mu})
, where
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
,
{\|\phi_{\mu}\|=\|\psi_{\mu}\|=1}
and we assume (
8
) and (
10
) to hold. We define
{{\bm{\phi}}:=(\phi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}}
,
{{\bm{\psi}}:=(\psi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}}
, and the corresponding density matrices
{\Gamma:=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\phi}}}}
,
\Lambda:=\mathcal{D}_{{\bm{\psi}}}
. We assume that
c_{\mathcal{P}},c_{A}<+\infty
, where those quantities are defined in Section
2
, and that all the quantities involved in the following are finite. For
\delta\in\{0,1\}
, we have

\displaystyle\Gamma-\Lambda=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left((1+HR_{\mu})\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{\psi_{\mu}}+P_{\psi_{\mu}}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}(1+R_{\mu}H)\right)+\Omega,

(14)

where

\|\Omega\|_{2,\delta}\leqslant c_{A}^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}^{2}+\big(1+\left(c_{A}c_{\mathcal{P}}^{2}\right)^{\delta}\big)\left(1+c_{A}(1+c_{A})\|A\Lambda\|\right)^{2\delta}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}^{2}\\
+2\left(c_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}\hskip-2.84544pt+\hskip-2.84544pt\nu c_{A}^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\Lambda\|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\hskip-2.84544pt\|A^{\delta}R_{\mu}\|\right)\left(c_{A}\left(1+c_{A}\|A\Lambda\|\right)\right)^{\delta}\hskip-2.84544pt\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}\hskip-2.84544pt\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}.

(15)

A proof is given in Section
7
. Since
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Lambda=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}=\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}A^{-\delta}A^{\delta}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\|_{2,\delta}\leqslant(1+c_{\mathcal{P}})^{\delta}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta},

hence we see in (
15
) that
\Omega
is quadratic in
\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}
, and thus negligible in (
14
) when
\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}
is small. The leading term for
\Gamma-\Lambda
is thus
\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left((1+HR_{\mu})\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma P_{\psi_{\mu}}+\textup{adj.}\right)

Proposition 3.2

(More detail in the non-degenerate case)
.

Consider the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
for
\nu=1

such that

is an eigenmode of

, so that
{H\phi=E\phi}
and
{\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\psi=\mathcal{E}\psi}

\displaystyle=\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\psi+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)R\left(\phi-\psi\right),

(16)

\displaystyle E-\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>+(E-\mathcal{E})\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}

(17)

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt-\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\re\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,(H-E)(\phi-\psi)\right>+\left(E-\mathcal{E}\right)^{2}\left<\phi-\psi,R\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>.

Remark 3.3

All the quantities involved in (

Remark 3.4

(Error in individual eigenvectors and in sum of eigenvalues of the cluster)

From Theorem
3.1
, for
\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}
small enough and

\displaystyle\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}\leqslant 4\nu\left(c_{A}\|A\Lambda\|\right)^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|A^{\delta}(1+R_{\mu}H)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}A^{-\delta}\|,

(18)

see Section

, there exists a rotation

for some constant

, and yet for another constant

, the error in the sums of eigenvalues is quadratic, that is

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\right)\right|\leqslant c\|A\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2}^{2}.

(20)

Hence the errors in individual eigenvectors (left-hand side of (

)) are controlled by the key quantity

.

Remark 3.5

(Main consequences of Proposition

)
.

If

is small enough, then Proposition

yields

\displaystyle\|\phi-\psi\|_{e,\delta}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\left(1+c_{H}\|ARA\|\right)^{\delta}\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\|,

(21)

\displaystyle\left|E-\mathcal{E}\right|

Remark 3.6

(Scaling of the different terms in (
16
) and (
17
))
.

The term
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi=\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\left(\phi-\psi\right)
is controlled by
\|\phi-\psi\|_{e}
in norm.
When
\|\phi-\psi\|_{e}
is small, the leading term in (
16
) is
\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi
. Then, the second leading term is of order 2 and
\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)R\left(\phi-\psi\right)
is of order 3 in the eigenvector error.
In (

), the leading term is
\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>
(which is of order 2), the second term is of order 4, the third term is of order 4 and the last one of order 6, all measured in the eigenvector error

\displaystyle\phi-\psi=(1+RH)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi+O_{\|\phi-\psi\|_{e,\delta}\rightarrow 0}\left(\|\phi-\psi\|_{e,\delta}^{2}\right).

Remark 3.7

(General approximability)
.

Consider the vector case corresponding to Proposition
3.2
. We numerically see that making
\mathcal{P}
larger by adding more vectors to
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
decreases the error, in general. This can be expected from the form of the leading term
\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi
, in which, for any vector
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
,
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\varphi
decreases. However, as will be seen in Section
6
, there are some exceptional cases where making
\mathcal{P}
larger increases the error.

Remark 3.8

(Variational formulation)
.

One can choose a variational approach. For instance if
\mu\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, and
E_{\mu}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
are defined as the
\mu^{\text{th}}
lowest eigenvalues (which we assume to exist) of resp.

\displaystyle E_{\mu}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset\mathcal{H}\\
\dim B=\mu+1\end{subarray}}{\text{{inf}}}\;\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in B\\
\|\varphi\|=1\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\left<\varphi,H\varphi\right>\leqslant\mathcal{E}_{\mu}=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}B\subset\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\\
\dim B=\mu+1\end{subarray}}{\text{{inf}}}\;\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\varphi\in B\\
\|\varphi\|=1\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\left<\varphi,H\varphi\right>.

(23)

Depending on
\mathcal{P}
, it is not necessarily
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}
which is close to
E_{\mu}
, but it can be
\mathcal{E}_{\nu}
for

. The drawback of this approach is that it is not able to treat eigenmodes being above the continuous spectrum.

Moreover, in the physics literature, the reduced basis method is often called the variational approximation, as we will discuss in Appendix
23
. However, we do not used this last terminology because of the fact that the variational point of view (

Note that the variational formulation can also refer to the fact that
\left<v,H\phi\right>=E\left<v,\phi\right>
for any

and
\left<u,H\psi\right>=\mathcal{E}\left<u,\psi\right>
for any

.

Remark 3.9

(Comparison to the Feshbach-Schur operator)
.

Using the effective operator
\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
can be put in perspective with the Feshbach-Schur technics, see
[

3

]
and
[

, in which the effective operator is
\left(\mathcal{P}(H+Hr_{\perp}H)\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
, where

.

5.
Main results on RBM+PT

We now apply the results of the previous section to a parameter dependent operator

5.1.
Heuristics

Let us first explain briefly the idea behind RBM+PT, at order
\ell\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Consider an eigenvector
\phi(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\phi^{n}
of
H(\lambda)
. If
\phi^{0},\phi^{1},\dots,\phi^{\ell}\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
, then
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
contains
\varphi(\lambda):=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\lambda^{n}\phi^{n}
, i.e.
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
is able to produce this perturbative approximation vector, so we can expect RBM+PT to be at least as good as PT. By (
16
) (or (
21
)) we have that
\phi(\lambda)-\psi(\lambda)
is controlled by the key quantity

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi^{n}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi^{n}\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\\
\forall 0\leqslant n\leqslant\ell\end{subarray}}{=}\;\sum_{n=\ell+1}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi^{n}=\lambda^{\ell+1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi^{\ell+1}+O(\lambda^{\ell+2}).

So the leading order of the error in RBM+PT is controlled by
\lambda^{\ell+1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi^{\ell+1}
, while the leading order of the error in PT is
\lambda^{\ell+1}\phi^{\ell+1}
. The acceleration factor of RBM+PT with respect to PT is thus approximately
\|\phi^{\ell+1}\|/\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi^{\ell+1}\|
.

Locally around
\lambda=0
adding the derivatives of
\phi(\lambda)
at
\lambda=0
is the optimal way of making
\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
grow, in the goal of modeling
\phi(\lambda)
.

Remark 5.4

A consequence of (

Remark 5.7

(Intermediate normalization)
.

Intermediate normalization is reviewed in Appendix

. Instead of building the reduced space

Remark 5.8

(Comparison to perturbation theory)

We provide a comparison of RBM+PT with PT in Section

Remark 5.9

(Generalization to higher-dimensional parameter space)

Corollary

5.5.
Vectors in the degenerate case

The bounds of Corollary
5.1
do not enable to obtain bounds on individual eigenvectors and individual eigenvalues in the degenerate case. Nevertheless, following a different strategy of proof can lead to such bounds and this is the purpose of this section.

5.5.1.
Assumptions on derivatives

Let us define

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}:=\left(\Gamma(0)H^{1}\Gamma(0)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}},

the restriction of
\Gamma(0)H^{1}\Gamma(0)
as an operator of
\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}
. Let us make the following hypothesis on the
E_{\mu}(\lambda)
’s, but we could make them on the
\mathcal{E}_{\mu}(\lambda)
’s, this is equivalent since
\Gamma(0)H^{1}\Gamma(0)=\Lambda(0)H^{1}\Lambda(0)
. We assume that

\displaystyle\forall\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\dots,\nu\},\;E_{\alpha}(0)=E_{\beta}(0),

(46)

i.e. the system is exactly degenerate. For any
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, we define

\displaystyle E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(0):=\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\lambda}E_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},

and it is well-known from first-order perturbation theory (see
[
34
]
for instance) that the

’s are the eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{H}
. We take
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
and we make the assumption that

\displaystyle\text{the eigenvalue }E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)\text{ is non-degenerate for $\mathfrak{H}$},

(47)

implying that for any
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}\backslash\{\nu\}
,
E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)\neq E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)
. Thus there exists
\kappa_{\mathfrak{H}}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left(\sigma\left(\mathfrak{H}\right)\backslash\{E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)\}\right)\cap\,]E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)-\kappa_{\mathfrak{H}},E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)+\kappa_{\mathfrak{H}}[\,=\varnothing,

and we can define

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(0):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left(E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)-\mathfrak{H}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0muP_{\phi_{\mu}(0)}^{\perp}\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}\rightarrow P_{\phi_{\mu}(0)}^{\perp}\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}}\right)^{-1}&\hskip-8.5359pt\mbox{on }P_{\phi_{\mu}(0)}^{\perp}\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H},\\
0&\hskip-8.5359pt\mbox{on }\Gamma(0)^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathbb{C}\phi_{\mu}(0),\end{array}\right.

extended by linearity on all of
\mathcal{H}
. More explicitely, we have

\displaystyle G_{\mu}(0)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\nu\\
\alpha\neq\mu\end{subarray}}\left(E_{\mu}^{\prime}(0)-E_{\alpha}^{\prime}(0)\right)^{-1}P_{\phi_{\alpha}(0)}.

We then define, for
\delta\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\xi^{\textup{deg}}_{\textup{RBM+PT},\mu,\ell}:=\|\left(1+G_{\mu}(0)H^{1}\right)\left(1+R_{\mu}(0)H^{0}\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi_{\mu}^{\ell+1}(0)\|_{e,\delta}.

7.
Proof of Theorem
3.1

We recall that for any self-adjoint operators

of
\mathcal{H}
,

\displaystyle\|BC\|_{2}\leqslant\|B\|\|C\|_{2}.

(54)

Moreover, for any

, we have

\displaystyle\|\left|u\right>\left<v\right|\|_{2}=\|\left|u\right>\left<v\right|\|=\|u\|\|v\|.

(55)

7.1.
Plan of the proof

We decompose the error
\Gamma-\Lambda
into several terms, which will be possible to handle individually. It is based on the space decomposition
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{H}
. First, we have
\phi_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
for all
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, so

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\Lambda=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\mathcal{P}\phi_{\alpha}\right>\left<\phi_{\alpha}\right|=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\phi_{\alpha}\right>\left<\phi_{\alpha}\right|=\Lambda,

hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\Lambda=\Lambda\mathcal{P}=\Lambda,\qquad\qquad\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Lambda=\Lambda\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=0.

(56)

Then we can decompose the error
\Gamma-\Lambda
in the following way

\displaystyle\Gamma-\Lambda

\displaystyle\hskip-4.26773pt\hskip-5.69046pt\hskip-4.26773pt\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1=\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\end{subarray}}{=}\hskip-5.69046pt\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}+\mathcal{P}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}+\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:commu}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}+\mathcal{P}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}+\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}.

(57)

We will follow those steps :

•

in Section
7.2
we show how to treat the first terms
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
,
\mathcal{P}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}
, and
\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\mathcal{P}
,

•

in Section
7.3
we present a first way of treating the term

Let us first present a well-known and basic estimate showing that the errors between eigenvalues can be expressed as the square of the error between eigenvectors. We give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 8.2

Take two self-adjoint operators

\displaystyle E-\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle=\left<\phi-\psi,(E-H)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>,

\displaystyle\left|E-\mathcal{E}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A^{-1}(H-E)A^{-1}\|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in[0,2\pi[\end{subarray}}{\text{{min}}}\;\|A(\phi-e^{i\theta}\psi)\|^{2}.

\displaystyle\left|E-\mathcal{E}\right|\leqslant\left(\|A^{-1}\|^{2}\left|E\right|+\|A^{-1}HA^{-1}\|\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\theta\in[0,2\pi[\end{subarray}}{\text{{min}}}\;\|A(\phi-e^{i\theta}\psi)\|^{2}.

\displaystyle\leqslant\|A^{-1}(E-H)A^{-1}\|\|A(\phi-\psi)\|^{2}.

To conclude, we change

.
∎

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}(\mathcal{P}H-\mathcal{E})\psi=0\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)R\phi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)R\phi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)R\phi+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\psi

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\mathcal{P}P_{\psi}^{\perp}\phi+\left(E-\mathcal{E}\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}HR\phi

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\psi.

(79)

Similarly as in (
76
), using
(E-H)\phi=0
and
\|\psi\|=1
, we have

\displaystyle\qquad E-\mathcal{E}=\left<\phi-\psi,(E-H)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>

\displaystyle=\left<\phi-\psi,(\mathcal{E}-H)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>+(E-\mathcal{E})\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:equality_diff}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>-\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\left<\psi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>

\displaystyle\qquad+\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\left<R\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>+(E-\mathcal{E})\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}.

(80)

We now compute each of those terms. First, by (
79
) we have

\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,(\mathcal{E}-H)(\phi-\psi)\right>\\
=\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>\\
+(\mathcal{E}-E)\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}P_{\psi}^{\perp}\phi\right>+(E-\mathcal{E})\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}^{\perp}HR\phi\right>\\
+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\psi\right>.

Then using
R\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=0
and (
78
), we get

\left<\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,(\mathcal{E}-H)(\phi-\psi)\right>=\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>\\
+(\mathcal{E}-E)\left<RH\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}P_{\psi}^{\perp}\phi\right>+(E-\mathcal{E})\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}^{\perp}HR\phi\right>\\
+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\psi\right>\\
\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}P_{\psi}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}R=R\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>+\tfrac{1}{2}\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,H\psi\right>,

giving the first term of (
80
). Using (
79
), the second term of (
80
) comes from

\left<\psi,(\mathcal{E}-H)(\phi-\psi)\right>=\left<\mathcal{P}\psi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>\\
=\left<\mathcal{P}\psi,(\mathcal{E}-H)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>+\left<\mathcal{P}\psi,(\mathcal{E}-H)RH\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{E}-H)R\\
=P_{\psi}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\end{subarray}}{=}\;-\left<H\psi,\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>.

The third term of (
80
) comes from

\left<R\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:interm_eq},\eqref{eq:intermios}\\
R\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=0\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left<R\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\mathcal{P}P_{\psi}^{\perp}\phi\right>\\
\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}R\mathcal{P}P_{\psi}^{\perp}=R\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\left<\phi,R\phi\right>\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}R\psi=0\end{subarray}}{=}\;\left(\mathcal{E}-E\right)\left<\phi-\psi,R\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>.

Summing all the terms of (
80
) yields

E-\mathcal{E}=\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>+\left(E-\mathcal{E}\right)^{2}\left<\phi-\psi,R\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>\\
+(E-\mathcal{E})\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}+\|\phi-\psi\|^{2}\re\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,H\psi\right>.

Moreover,
\big((\mathcal{E}-H)(1+RH)\big)^{*}=(1+HR)(\mathcal{E}-H)=(\mathcal{E}-H)(1+RH)
is self-adjoint so
\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,\left(\mathcal{E}-H\right)\left(1+RH\right)\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi\right>\in\mathbb{R}
,
\left<\phi-\psi,R\left(\phi-\psi\right)\right>\in\mathbb{R}
. To conclude, we use that
\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,H\psi\right>=-\left<\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\phi,(H-E)(\phi-\psi)\right>
.

9.2.
A preliminary bound on “Cauchy squares”

First we will need the following result, which is a bound on a series that we can call the “Cauchy square” series.

Lemma 9.1

(Upper bound on the Cauchy square series)
.

Take
\alpha,\beta>0
and let us define
x_{1}:=\alpha
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle x_{n}:=\beta\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}x_{n-s}x_{s}.

Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle x_{n}\leqslant\alpha\frac{\left(2\zeta\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\alpha\beta\right)^{n-1}}{n^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(81)

where
\zeta
is Riemann’s zeta function so
2\zeta\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)\simeq 5.2248...

Remark 9.2
.

We made a numerical study giving evidence that

\displaystyle\frac{x_{n}}{\alpha\pi^{-\frac{1}{2}}(4\alpha\beta)^{n-1}n^{-\frac{3}{2}}}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\rightarrow+\infty\end{subarray}}{\longrightarrow}\;1.

Proof.

First, we show that proving the result with
\beta=1
enables to show it for any
\beta>0
. Take a general
\beta>0
. By using
y_{n}:=\beta x_{n}
, we have
y_{1}=\alpha\beta
and
y_{n}=\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}y_{n-s}y_{s}
. We use the result for
\beta=1
on
y_{n}
, which yields the claimed result for
x_{n}
.

Hence without loss of generality we can take
\beta=1
. Let us prove (
81
) by induction. We define
\xi:=2\zeta\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)
, for any
x\in]0,n[
we define
g(x):=\left(x(n-x)\right)^{-\frac{3}{2}}
, we extend it on
\mathbb{C}\backslash\{0,n\}
, and we define
S_{n}:=\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}g(s)
for any
n\geqslant 2
.

For
n=1
the right hand side of (
81
) is
\alpha
so the initial step is valid. Take
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geqslant 2
, such that for any
s\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
,
x_{s}\leqslant\alpha(\xi\alpha)^{s-1}s^{-\frac{3}{2}}
. Then

\displaystyle x_{n}\leqslant\alpha^{2}\left(\xi\alpha\right)^{n-2}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{\left(s(n-s)\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}}=\alpha^{2}\left(\xi\alpha\right)^{n-2}S_{n}.

(82)

Defining
G(x):=\frac{n-2x}{\sqrt{x(n-x)}}
we have
G^{\prime}(x)=-\frac{n^{2}}{2}g(x)
so

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{n-1}g=\frac{2}{n^{2}}\left(G(1)-G(n-1)\right)=\frac{4(n-2)}{n^{2}\sqrt{n-1}}.

Moreover,
g(\overline{z})=\overline{g(z)}
and by the Abel-Plana formula,

\displaystyle S_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{1}^{n-1}g(s){\rm d}s+\tfrac{1}{2}g(1)+\tfrac{1}{2}g(n-1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-2\im\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{g(1+iy)-g(n-1+iy)}{e^{2\pi y}-1}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{5n^{2}-12n+8}{(n-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}n^{2}}-4\im\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{g(1+iy)}{e^{2\pi y}-1}{\rm d}y

hence

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\rightarrow+\infty\end{subarray}}{\text{{lim}}}\;n^{\frac{3}{2}}S_{n}=5-4\im\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{(1+iy)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{e^{2\pi y}-1}{\rm d}y=2\zeta\left(\tfrac{3}{2}\right)=\xi,

where we used the Abel-Plana formula again. Moreover,
(n^{3/2}S_{n})_{n\geqslant 2}
is an increasing sequence, thus
S_{n}\leqslant\xi/n^{3/2}
. Then (
82
) enables to conclude.
∎

9.3.
Bound on the Rayleigh-Schrödinger series : the non-degenerate case

We are now ready to obtain a bound on
E^{n}_{\mu}
and
\Phi^{n}_{\mu}
. In particular, this provides a bound on the convergence radius of the perturbation series. We take the non-degenerate case, that is
\nu=1
, so
\mu=1
.

We recall that
K_{\mu}(0)
is defined in (
4.2
). For any
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

\displaystyle h^{m}_{\mu}:=H^{m}-E^{m}_{\mu},\qquad\qquad Q^{n}_{\mu}:=h^{n}_{\mu}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}h^{n-s}_{\mu}K_{\mu}(0)Q^{s}_{\mu}.

(83)

Then, by a classical result which can be found in
[
34
,
30
]
for instance, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{\mu}^{n}=K_{\mu}(0)Q^{n}_{\mu}\Phi_{\mu}^{0},\qquad\forall n\geqslant 1.

(84)

Moreover, we can compute

\displaystyle E^{n}_{\mu}=\left<\Phi_{\mu}^{0},\left(Q^{n}_{\mu}+E^{n}_{\mu}\right)\Phi_{\mu}^{0}\right>.

(85)

Lemma 9.3

(Bound on
E^{n}
,
\phi^{n}
and
\Phi^{n}
, non-degenerate case)
.

Let us consider the Hamiltonian family
H(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}H^{n}
under the assumptions of Sections
4.1
and
4.2
, with
\nu=1
. The non-degenerate eigenmode is denoted by
(E_{\mu}(\lambda),\phi_{\mu}(\lambda))
, we fix the phasis of
\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)
such that
\left<\phi_{\mu}^{0},\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the intermediate normalization eigenvector is
\Phi_{\mu}(\lambda):=\frac{\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)}{\left<\phi_{\mu}^{0},\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>}
and the Taylor series are written

\displaystyle E_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}E_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},\phi_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},\Phi_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\Phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0}.

Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\left|E^{n}_{\mu}\right|+\|\phi_{\mu}^{n}\|_{e}+\|\Phi_{\mu}^{n}\|_{e}

\displaystyle\leqslant ab^{n},

(86)

where
a,b\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
are independent of
n
.

Proof.

For clarity, we drop the subscripts 1, so
E:=E_{1}
,
\phi:=\phi_{1}
,
\Phi:=\Phi_{1}
,
K:=K_{1}(0)
,
Q^{n}:=Q_{1}^{n}
,
h^{n}:=h_{1}^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We define

\displaystyle c_{H,\infty}:=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\end{subarray}}{\text{{sup}}}\;\|A^{-1}H^{n}A^{-1}\|,\qquad c_{K}:=\|AKA\|,\qquad c_{A}:=\|A^{-1}\|.

(87)

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
let us define

\displaystyle q^{n}:=H^{n}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}h^{n-s}KQ^{s}.

(88)

From (
83
) we have
Q^{n}=q^{n}-E^{n}
, from (
85
) we have
E^{n}=\left<\Phi^{0},q^{n}\Phi^{0}\right>
, and we recall that
h^{n}=H^{n}-E^{n}
hence

\displaystyle\left|E^{n}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\left<A\Phi^{0},A^{-1}q^{n}A^{-1}A\Phi^{0}\right>\right|\leqslant\|q^{n}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2},

\displaystyle\|Q^{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|q^{n}\|_{p}+c_{A}^{2}\left|E^{n}\right|\leqslant\|q^{n}\|_{p}\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right),

\displaystyle\|h^{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{n}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}.

(89)

Thus from (
88
) we have

\displaystyle\|q^{n}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+c_{K}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\|h^{n-s}\|_{p}\|Q^{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+c_{K}\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\left(c_{H,\infty}+\|q^{n-s}\|_{p}c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\|q^{s}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+\beta\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\left(1+\|q^{n-s}\|_{p}\right)\|q^{s}\|_{p}

where

\displaystyle\beta:=c_{K}\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\max\left(c_{H,\infty},c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right).

Defining
y_{n}:=\|q^{n}\|_{p}+1
, we have

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+c_{H,\infty}+\beta\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}y_{n-s}(y_{s}-1)\leqslant(1+c_{H,\infty}+\beta)\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}y_{n-s}y_{s}.

(90)

By (
84
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{n}\|_{e}\leqslant c_{K}\|Q^{n}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}\leqslant c_{K}\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}y_{n}.

(91)

We now show the bound for
\|\phi^{n}\|_{e}
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we define
Y^{n}
and
X^{n}
as in (
133
), and

\displaystyle u_{n}:=\max\left(\left|Y^{n}\right|,\left|X^{n-1}\right|,y_{n}\right).

We have

\displaystyle\left|Y^{n}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\left|\left<\Phi^{n-k},\Phi^{k}\right>\right|+\left|Y^{n-k}\right|\left|Y^{k}\right|\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{n-k}\|_{e}\|\Phi^{k}\|_{e}+u_{n-k}u_{k}\right)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:bphI}\end{subarray}}{\leqslant}\;g\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}u_{n-k}u_{k},

where

\displaystyle g:=\max\left(1,c_{A}c_{K}\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\|\Phi^{0}\|_{e}\right)^{2}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle X^{n-1}=-\sum_{s=0}^{n-3}X^{s}Y^{n-1-s}=-\sum_{s=1}^{n-2}X^{s-1}Y^{n-s}\quad\text{so}\quad\left|X^{n-1}\right|\leqslant\sum_{s=1}^{n-2}u_{n-s}u_{s}.

We deduce that

\displaystyle u_{n}\leqslant(1+c_{H,\infty}+\beta+g)\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}u_{n-s}u_{s}.

Using Lemma
9.1
, we deduce that there are
a,b>0
such that
u_{n}\leqslant ab^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We can propagate this result for
\|q^{n}\|_{p}
,
\left|E^{n}\right|
,
\|Q^{n}\|_{p}
using (
89
), for
\|\Phi^{n}\|_{e}
by using (
91
), and for
\|\phi^{n}\|_{e}
by using (
134
).
∎

Remark 9.4

(On the radius of convergence of the perturbative approximation)
.

Defining the perturbation approximation in the intermediate normalization
\varphi(\lambda):=\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\lambda^{n}\phi_{\mu}^{n}
, (
86
) yields

\displaystyle\|\varphi(\lambda)\|_{e}\leqslant a\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\left(\left|\lambda\right|b\right)^{n}=a\frac{1-\left(\left|\lambda\right|b\right)^{\ell+1}}{1-\left|\lambda\right|b}\leqslant\frac{a}{1-\left|\lambda\right|b},

and the radius of convergence of the right-hand side is

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)-\varphi(\lambda)\|_{e}\leqslant\frac{a}{1-\left|\lambda\right|b}\left(\left|\lambda\right|b\right)^{\ell+1}.

(92)

9.4.
Bound on the Rayleigh-Schrödinger series : the degenerate case, when degeneracy is lifted at first order

To show Theorem
5.10
, we will need a similar lemma as Lemma
9.3
but for the degenerate case. We consider a degenerate case, so for all
\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
,
E^{0}_{\alpha}=E^{0}_{\beta}
. As before,
\Gamma(0)
is the orthogonal projector onto
\oplus_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\Ker\left(H^{0}-E^{0}_{\mu}\right)
. It is well-known that the
\nu
eigenvalues of
\left(\Gamma(0)H^{0}\Gamma(0)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}}
, as an operator of
\Gamma(0)\mathcal{H}
, are

\displaystyle E_{\mu}^{1}=\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\lambda}E_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0}=\left<\phi^{0}_{\mu},H^{1}\phi^{0}_{\mu}\right>.

Here we assume that degeneracy is lifted at first order for some
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, meaning that for any
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}\backslash\{\mu\}
,
E_{\alpha}^{1}\neq E_{\mu}^{1}
.

9.4.1.
Pseudo-inverses

In the degenerate case, we need to introduce two kinds of partial inverse operators. The “zeroth order” partial inverse
K_{\mu}(0)
was defined in (
4.2
), and we set

\displaystyle K^{0}_{\mu}:=K_{\mu}(0).

We take some
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
and assume that degeneracy is lifted at first order. We also set

\displaystyle K^{1}_{\mu}:=G_{\mu}(0),

where
G_{\mu}(0)
was defined in (
5.5.1
).

9.4.2.
Series

We present degenerate perturbation theory as in the work of Hirschfelder
[
30
, Section 4.A]
, in the case where all degeneracies are lifted at first order. We define
h^{n}_{\mu}:=H^{n}-E^{n}_{\mu}
, the operators

\displaystyle q^{n}_{0,\mu}:=H^{n}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}h^{s}_{\mu}K^{0}_{\mu}Q^{n-s}_{0,\mu},\qquad q^{n}_{1,\mu}:=q^{n}_{0,\mu}+\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}Q^{s}_{0,\mu}K^{1}_{\mu}Q^{n-s+1}_{1,\mu}

(93)

and
Q^{n}_{i,\mu}=q^{n}_{i,\mu}-E^{n}_{\mu}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
. Then for any
m\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\displaystyle E^{m}_{\mu}=\left<\phi^{0}_{\mu},q^{m}_{1,\mu}\phi^{0}_{\mu}\right>,\qquad\Phi^{n}_{\mu}=\left(K^{0}_{\mu}Q^{n}_{1,\mu}+K^{1}_{\mu}Q^{n+1}_{1,\mu}\right)\phi^{0}_{\mu}.

(94)

More precisely, in
[
30
, Section 4.A]
, the first term of
\Phi^{n}_{\mu}
is not the same as in (
94
) but it can be showed that there is equality between them.

Lemma 9.5

(Bound on
E^{n}_{\mu}
,
\phi^{n}_{\mu}
and
\Phi^{n}_{\mu}
, degenerate case)
.

Let us consider the Hamiltonian family
H(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}H^{n}
under the assumptions of Sections
4.1
and
4.2
. We take
\mu\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
and an eigenmode denoted by
(E_{\mu}(\lambda),\phi_{\mu}(\lambda))
. We consider the degenerate case, where the degeneracy is lifted at first order, as described in Section
5.5
, i.e.
E^{0}_{\mu}=E^{0}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
and
E^{1}_{\mu}\neq E^{1}_{\alpha}
for all
\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}\backslash\{\mu\}
. We fix the phasis of
\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)
such that
\left<\phi_{\mu}^{0},\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
, the intermediate normalization eigenvector is
\Phi_{\mu}(\lambda):=\frac{\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)}{\left<\phi_{\mu}^{0},\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>}
and the Taylor series are written

\displaystyle E_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}E_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},\phi_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},\Phi_{\mu}^{n}:=\tfrac{1}{n!}\left(\tfrac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\Phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0}.

Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\left|E^{n}_{\mu}\right|+\|\phi^{n}_{\mu}\|_{e}+\|\Phi^{n}_{\mu}\|_{e}\leqslant ab^{n},

(95)

where
a,b>0
are independent of
n
and
\mu
, and depend polynomially on
\|AK^{0}_{\mu}A\|
.

Proof.

We recall that
c_{H,\infty}
was defined in (
87
). From (
94
) we have

\displaystyle\left|E^{n}_{\mu}\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\|q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2},

\displaystyle\|h^{n}_{\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+c_{A}^{2}\left|E^{n}_{\mu}\right|\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2},

(96)

and for
i\in\{0,1\}
,

\displaystyle\|Q^{n}_{i,\mu}\|_{p}\leqslant\|q^{n}_{i,\mu}\|_{p}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}.

(97)

Next,

\displaystyle\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+\|K^{0}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\|h^{s}_{\mu}\|_{p}\|Q^{n-s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{H,\infty}+\|K^{0}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\big(c_{H,\infty}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\left(\|q^{n-s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{n-s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}\right).

We define

\displaystyle C:=\max\left(c_{H,\infty},\|K^{0}_{\mu}\|_{ee},\|K^{1}_{\mu}\|_{ee},1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}\right),

and we have

\displaystyle\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}\leqslant C+C^{3}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\big(1+\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\big)\left(\|q^{n-s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|q^{n-s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\right).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\|q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\leqslant\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|K^{1}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}\|Q^{s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}\|Q^{n-s+1}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle+\left(1+c_{A}^{2}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\|K^{1}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}\left(\|q^{s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+c_{A}^{2}\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}^{2}\right)\|q^{n-s+1}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+C^{3}\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}\left(\|q^{s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\right)\|q^{n-s+1}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}.

We define
x_{n}:=\|q^{n}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}+1
and estimate

\displaystyle x_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C+2C^{3}\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\big(1+\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\big)\left(\|q^{n-s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|q^{n-s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad+C^{3}\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}\left(\|q^{s}_{0,\mu}\|_{p}+\|q^{s}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\right)\|q^{n-s+1}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\left(1+C^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}x_{s}x_{n-s}+\sum_{s=2}^{n-1}x_{s}x_{n-s+1}\right)

\displaystyle=2C(1+C^{2})\left(x_{1}x_{n-1}+\sum_{s=1}^{n-2}\left(x_{s}+x_{s+1}\right)x_{n-s}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 4C(1+C^{2})\sum_{s=1}^{n-2}\left(x_{s}+x_{s+1}\right)x_{n-s}.

Then with
y_{n}:=x_{n}+x_{n+1}
, we have

\displaystyle y_{n}

\displaystyle\leqslant x_{n}+4C(1+C^{2})\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}\left(x_{s}+x_{s+1}\right)x_{n+1-s}

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{n-1}+4C(1+C^{2})\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}y_{s}y_{n-s}\leqslant(1+4C(1+C^{2}))\sum_{s=1}^{n-1}y_{s}y_{n-s},

where we used that
1/y_{1}\leqslant 1
in the last inequality. Using Lemma
9.1
, we deduce that there are
a,b>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
y_{n}\leqslant ab^{n}
, and then
\|q^{n}_{i,\mu}\|_{p}\leqslant ab^{n}
for
i\in\{0,1\}
. We propagate this property for
\left|E^{n}_{\mu}\right|
,
\|Q^{n}_{i,\mu}\|_{p}
using (
96
) and (
97
) and for
\|\Phi^{n}_{\mu}\|_{e}
by using

\displaystyle\|\Phi^{n}\|_{e}\leqslant\left(\|K^{0}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\|Q^{n}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}+\|K^{1}_{\mu}\|_{ee}\|Q^{n+1}_{1,\mu}\|_{p}\right)\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e}.

As we see in
C
, we can bound
a
and
b
using polynomials in

\displaystyle c_{H,\infty},\|K^{0}_{\mu}\|_{ee},\|K^{1}_{\mu}\|_{ee},\|\Phi^{0}_{\mu}\|_{e},c_{A}.

The bound on
\|\phi^{n}_{\mu}\|_{e}
can be deduced with the same method as in Lemma
9.3
.
∎

10.
Coefficients in density matrix perturbation theory

In this section we present how to compute the coefficients of the perturbative series in density matrix perturbation theory. We use the Liouvillian operator and its partial inverse, a classical tool in perturbation theory, see
[
34
,
67
]
, used in
[
4
,
46
]
, with a detailed exposition in
[
14
, Section 5.1]
. See also for instance
[
69
]
.

10.1.
Definitions

We choose the same context and notations as in Section
2
, in particular, we consider a series of operators

\displaystyle H(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}H^{n}.

We consider a cluster of eigenmodes
\left(E_{\mu}(\lambda),\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
of
H(\lambda)
, where all those maps are analytic in
\lambda\in]-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}[
. Let us take
\lambda_{0}
small enough so that there is
\kappa_{H}>0
, independent of
\lambda
, such that for any
\lambda\in]-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0}[
,

\displaystyle\big(\sigma(H)\backslash\{E_{\mu}(\lambda)\}_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\big)\cap\big(\cup_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\;]E_{\mu}(\lambda)-\kappa_{H},E_{\mu}(\lambda)+\kappa_{H}[\;\big)=\varnothing.

Take
(\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda))_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}^{\nu}
such that

\displaystyle\left<\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda),\varphi_{\alpha}(\lambda)\right>=\delta_{\mu\alpha},\qquad\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda)\in\Ker\left(H(\lambda)-E_{\mu}(\lambda)\right),

(98)

the density matrix corresponding to those eigenmodes is

\displaystyle\Gamma(\lambda):=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>\left<\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right|=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\Gamma^{n},\quad\text{where}\quad\Gamma^{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\Gamma(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},

and is independent of the choice of the frame
\varphi_{\mu}(\lambda)
, as long as it respects (
98
).

10.2.
Statement

Let us take
(\varphi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\in\mathcal{H}^{\nu}
such that
\varphi_{\mu}\in\Ker\left(H(0)-E_{\mu}(0)\right)
and
\left<\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\alpha}\right>=\delta_{\mu\alpha}
for any
\mu,\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}
, so
\Gamma^{0}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\varphi_{\mu}\right>\left<\varphi_{\mu}\right|
. Let us define
A_{0}:=\Gamma^{0}
,
B_{0}:=0
,
C_{0}:=0
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n\geqslant 1
,

A_{n}:=-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(A_{n-k}A_{k}+B_{n-k}^{*}B_{k}\right),\qquad C_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(C_{n-k}C_{k}+B_{n-k}B_{k}^{*}\right)\\
b_{n}:=\left(\Gamma^{0}\right)^{\perp}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(H^{n-k}\left(A_{k}+B_{k}\right)-\left(B_{k}+C_{k}\right)H^{n-k}\right)\Gamma^{0}\\
B_{n}:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}K_{\mu}(0)b_{n}P_{\varphi_{\mu}},

(99)

where
\sum_{m=a}^{b}:=0
if
b<a
, and
K_{\mu}(0)
is defined in (
4.2
). We see that
A_{n}
and
C_{n}
are self-adjoint. The following result is classical and comes from
[
44
]
. See also
[
69
]
for other methods of computing
\Gamma^{n}
.

Proposition 10.1

(Coefficients in density matrix perturbation theory,
[
44
]
)
.

Let us consider a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, a self-adjoint energy operator
A
, an analytic family of self-adjoint operators
H(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}H^{n}
, we make the assumptions of Sections
4.1
and
4.2
. Take
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, consider a set of eigenmodes
\left(E_{\mu}(\lambda),\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right)_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
, analytic in
\lambda
, and the corresponding density matrix
\Gamma(\lambda):=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left|\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right>\left<\phi_{\mu}(\lambda)\right|=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\Gamma^{n}
. Then for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}=A_{n}+B_{n}+B_{n}^{*}+C_{n},

(100)

where the involved operators are defined in (
99
).

For the sake of completeness, we give a rigorous proof in Section
10.3
.

Remark that
\Gamma^{n}
is invariant under the gauge change
(\varphi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}=:{\bm{\varphi}}\rightarrow U{\bm{\varphi}}
, for any unitary
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
, as long as (
98
) is respected. Hence
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
are also invariant under this transformation. So one does not need to compute the exact
\phi_{\mu}(0)
’s, which are notoriously hard to obtain.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle A_{n}=\Gamma^{0}\Gamma^{n}\Gamma^{0},\;\;\;B_{n}=(\Gamma^{0})^{\perp}\Gamma^{n}\Gamma^{0},\;\;\;B_{n}^{*}=\Gamma^{0}\Gamma^{n}(\Gamma^{0})^{\perp},\;\;\;C_{n}=(\Gamma^{0})^{\perp}\Gamma^{n}(\Gamma^{0})^{\perp},

and we can rewrite

A_{n}=-\Gamma^{0}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma^{n-k}\Gamma^{k}\Gamma^{0},\qquad C_{n}=\left(\Gamma^{0}\right)^{\perp}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma^{n-k}\Gamma^{k}\left(\Gamma^{0}\right)^{\perp}\\
b_{n}=\left(\Gamma^{0}\right)^{\perp}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}[H^{n-k},\Gamma^{k}]\Gamma^{0},\qquad B_{n}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}K_{\mu}(0)b_{n}P_{\varphi_{\mu}}.

We recall that we defined in Section
2.1
an operator
A
enabling to implement the energy norm, and we recall that
\|\cdot\|_{2}
is the Hilbert-Schmidt norm. Then to prove (
41
) we will need to following bound on the
\Gamma^{n}
series.

Proposition 10.2

(Bound for the coefficients of density matrix perturbation theory)
.

There exist
a,b>0
, independent of
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\|A\Gamma^{n}A\|_{2}\leqslant ab^{n}.

(101)

10.3.
Proof of Proposition
10.1

10.3.1.
First relations

The Euler-Lagrange equation
[H(\lambda),\Gamma(\lambda)]=0
gives that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left[H^{n-k},\Gamma^{k}\right]=0.

(102)

Moreover,
\Gamma(\lambda)^{*}=\Gamma(\lambda)
and
\Gamma(\lambda)^{2}=\Gamma(\lambda)
so for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
\left(\Gamma^{n}\right)^{*}=\Gamma^{n}
and

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Gamma^{n-k}\Gamma^{k}=\Gamma^{n}.

(103)

10.3.2.
Decomposition of the projection

We define

P:=\Gamma^{0}
,
P^{\perp}:=1-\Gamma^{0}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}:=P\Gamma^{n}P,\qquad\mathcal{B}_{n}:=P^{\perp}\Gamma^{n}P,\qquad\mathcal{C}_{n}:=P^{\perp}\Gamma^{n}P^{\perp}

so
\mathcal{A}_{n}^{*}=\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}^{*}=\mathcal{C}_{n}
and

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}+\mathcal{B}_{n}+\mathcal{B}_{n}^{*}+\mathcal{C}_{n}

(104)

so we want to compute the series
\mathcal{A}_{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
,
\mathcal{C}_{n}
.

10.3.3.
Formulas for
\mathcal{A}_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}

We define the Liouvillian

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}:=\left[H^{0},\cdot\right],

and
\mathfrak{S}_{2}
denotes the space of Hilbert-Schmidt operators, defined in (
4
). For any
B,F\in\mathfrak{S}_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}B,F)_{2}=\tr\left(\mathcal{L}B\right)^{*}F=\tr[H^{0},B]^{*}F=\tr B^{*}H^{0}F-\tr H^{0}B^{*}F

\displaystyle\qquad=\tr B^{*}H^{0}F-\tr B^{*}FH^{0}=\tr B^{*}[H^{0},F]=\tr B^{*}\mathcal{L}F=(B,\mathcal{L}F)_{2},

hence
\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint, or in other words,
\mathcal{L}^{*}=\mathcal{L}
. We define

\displaystyle X_{n}:=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left[H^{n-k},\Gamma^{k}\right],\qquad Y_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma^{n-k}\Gamma^{k}.

(105)

Then (
102
) transforms to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\Gamma^{n}=X_{n}

and (
103
) transforms to

\displaystyle\Gamma^{n}P+P\Gamma^{n}-\Gamma^{n}=-Y_{n}.

(106)

From (
104
) we can compute
\Gamma^{n}P+P\Gamma^{n}-\Gamma^{n}=\mathcal{A}_{n}-\mathcal{C}_{n}
so (
106
) implies
\mathcal{A}_{n}-\mathcal{C}_{n}=-Y_{n}
and

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}=-PY_{n}P\qquad\qquad\mathcal{C}_{n}=P^{\perp}Y_{n}P^{\perp}.

(107)

So we can develop

Y_{n}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:dec_gamma}\\
\eqref{eq:def_XY}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n-k}+\mathcal{B}_{n-k}+\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{*}+\mathcal{C}_{n-k}\right)\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}^{*}+\mathcal{C}_{k}\right)\\
=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\mathcal{A}_{n-k}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}^{*}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{n-k}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}^{*}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{n-k}^{*}\left(\mathcal{C}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}\right)+\mathcal{C}_{n-k}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}+\mathcal{C}_{k}\right).

Applying
P
on the left and on the right, and applying
P^{\perp}
on the left and on the right, together with (
107
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}=-\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n-k}\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}^{*}_{n-k}\mathcal{B}_{k}\right),\qquad\mathcal{C}_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{n-k}\mathcal{C}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{n-k}\mathcal{B}_{k}^{*}\right).

(108)

10.3.4.
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}_{n}

We have
H^{0}P=PH^{0}=PH^{0}P
and
H^{0}P^{\perp}=P^{\perp}H^{0}=P^{\perp}H^{0}P^{\perp}
hence for
Q,G\in\{P,P^{\perp}\}
and for any operator
F\in\mathfrak{S}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\left(QFG\right)=Q\left(\mathcal{L}F\right)G.

(109)

By taking
F=X_{n}
,
Q=P^{\perp}
,
G=P
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}_{n}=P^{\perp}X_{n}P\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:dec_gamma}\\
\eqref{eq:def_XY}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}+\mathcal{C}_{k}\right)H^{n-k}P-P^{\perp}H^{n-k}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}\right).

(110)

10.3.5.
Partial inverse of the Liouvillian

We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}:=\{L\in\mathfrak{S}_{2}\;|\;L=P^{\perp}LP\}.

Let us take
(\varphi_{\mu})_{\mu=1}^{\nu}
such that
\varphi_{\mu}\in\Ker\left(H(0)-E_{\mu}(0)\right)
and
\left<\varphi_{\mu},\varphi_{\alpha}\right>=\delta_{\mu\alpha}
for any
\mu,\alpha\in\{1,\dots,\nu\}

and the operator of
\mathfrak{S}_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{+}:\hskip 14.22636pt\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathfrak{S}_{2}&\longrightarrow&\mathfrak{S}_{2}\\
F&\longmapsto&-\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}K_{\mu}(0)FP_{\varphi_{\mu}}.\end{array}

For any
F\in\mathfrak{S}_{2}
, we compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}F\hskip-1.42271pt=\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271pt\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}K_{\mu}(0)[H^{0},F]P_{\varphi_{\mu}}\hskip-1.42271pt=\hskip-1.42271pt\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}K_{\mu}(0)FH^{0}P_{\varphi_{\mu}}\hskip-1.42271pt-\hskip-1.42271ptK_{\mu}(0)H^{0}FP_{\varphi_{\mu}}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}E_{\mu}(0)K_{\mu}(0)FP_{\varphi_{\mu}}+P^{\perp}FP_{\varphi_{\mu}}-E_{\mu}(0)K_{\mu}(0)FP_{\varphi_{\mu}}=P^{\perp}FP,

(113)

where we used that
K_{\mu}(0)\left(H^{0}-E_{\mu}(0)\right)=-P^{\perp}
. So if
F\in\mathcal{O}
, then
\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}F=F
. By a similar computation, we have
\mathcal{L}\mathcal{L}^{+}=\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}
. Moreover,
(\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L})^{2}=\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}
and
(\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L})^{*}=\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}
, where the dual operator is taken with respect to the scalar product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\text{2}}
. Hence
\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{O}
, and
\mathcal{L}^{+}
is a partial inverse.

10.3.6.
Formula for
\mathcal{B}_{n}

Since
\mathcal{B}_{n}\in\mathcal{O}
, and since
\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{O}
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}^{+}\mathcal{L}\mathcal{B}_{n}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:LB}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\mathcal{L}^{+}\big(\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}+\mathcal{C}_{k}\right)H^{n-k}P\big)-\mathcal{L}^{+}\big(P^{\perp}H^{n-k}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}\right)\big)

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:Lplus_full}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}K_{\mu}(0)\left(H^{n-k}\left(\mathcal{A}_{k}+\mathcal{B}_{k}\right)-\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}+\mathcal{C}_{k}\right)H^{n-k}\right)P_{\varphi_{\mu}}.

(114)

10.3.7.
Conclusion

The recursive relations (
108
) and (
114
) respected by
\mathcal{A}_{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}
are the same as the ones respected by
A_{n}
,
B_{n}
and
C_{n}
. Thus from
\mathcal{A}_{0}=A_{0}
,
\mathcal{B}_{0}=B_{0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{0}=C_{0}
, we conclude that
\mathcal{A}_{n}=A_{n}
,
\mathcal{B}_{n}=B_{n}
and
\mathcal{C}_{n}=C_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

10.4.
Proof of Proposition
10.2

We recall that

\|B\|_{2,ee}:=\|ABA\|_{2},\qquad c_{K}:=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|AKA\|,\\
c_{H,\infty}:=\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|A^{-1}H^{n}A^{-1}\|.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, we define

\displaystyle v_{n}:=\max\left(\|A_{n}\|_{2,ee},\|B_{n}\|_{2,ee},\|C_{n}\|_{2,ee}\right).

Let us take
n\in\mathbb{N}
and. We have

AB_{n}A=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}AK_{\mu}(0)A\\
\times\left(A^{-1}H^{n-k}A^{-1}A\left(A_{k}+B_{k}\right)A^{-1}-A^{-1}\left(B_{k}+C_{k}\right)AA^{-1}H^{n-k}A^{-1}\right)\\
\times AP_{\varphi_{\mu}}A.

Moreover, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and any
L\in\{A_{k},B_{k},C_{k}\}
,

\displaystyle\|ALA^{-1}\|=\|ALAA^{-2}\|\leqslant c_{A}^{2}\|ALA\|\leqslant c_{A}^{2}\|L\|_{2,ee}\leqslant c_{A}^{2}v_{k},

and

\displaystyle\|AP_{\varphi_{\mu}}A\|_{2}=\|A\Gamma^{0}P_{\varphi_{\mu}}\Gamma^{0}A\|_{2}\leqslant\|A\Gamma^{0}\|\|P_{\varphi_{\mu}}\|_{2}\|\Gamma^{0}A\|=\|A\Gamma^{0}\|^{2},

In the physics literature, the reduced basis method or subspace projections methods are called variational approximation. A number of approaches relating perturbation theory and reduced basis methods were developped, they are often called “variational perturbation theory” but are not equivalent to RBM+PT that we studied in this document. We present some of them in this section. Other contributions using variational methods and perturbation theory are present in
[
64
,
63
,
17
]
.

General reduced basis developped in
\lambda

In
[
60
]

H(\lambda)
depending on the real parameter
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. They take a general basis of
m
functions
(g_{a})_{1\leqslant a\leqslant m}
, forming
\Span(g_{a})_{1\leqslant a\leqslant m}=\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}
where
\mathcal{P}
is an orthogonal projection. They minimize the quadratic form associated to
H(\lambda)
on this basis, which provides
m
eigenvectors

is formed by the derivatives of the exact eigenvector with respect to
\lambda
, but we did not see them following this path in their developments. They called this method the perturbation-variational Rayleigh-Ritz (PVRR) approach, which was used in
[
56
,
61
,
55
,
54
,
57
,
58
,
59
]
for instance.

Appendix C
Intermediate normalization

In this section, we show several results about intermediate normalization, which is aimed to be applied to Rayleigh-Schrödinger series eigenvectors in another part of this document, for both degenerate and non-degenerate cases.

C.1.
Unit normalization

We consider a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
with scalar product
\left<\cdot,\cdot\right>
and norm
\|\cdot\|
, and a map
\phi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
depending on one real parameter
\lambda
. We consider that

\displaystyle\|\phi(\lambda)\|=1

for any
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, which is called unit normalization. We assume that
\phi
is analytic at
0
so we can expand it

\displaystyle\phi^{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\phi(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},\qquad\phi(\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{n}\phi^{n}.

C.2.
Definition of intermediate normalization

Let us define

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda):=\frac{\phi(\lambda)}{\left<\phi^{0},\phi(\lambda)\right>},\qquad\qquad\Phi^{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}\Phi(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0}.

We then define
Z(\lambda):=\frac{1}{\left<\phi^{0},\phi(\lambda)\right>}
. Let us denote by
P_{\phi^{0}}
the projector onto
\mathbb{C}\phi^{0}
, so
P_{\phi^{0}}\phi(\lambda)=\left<\phi^{0},\phi(\lambda)\right>\phi^{0}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\lambda)

\displaystyle=Z(\lambda)\left(P_{\phi^{0}}\phi(\lambda)+\big(1-P_{\phi^{0}}\big)\phi(\lambda)\right)=\phi^{0}+\big(1-P_{\phi^{0}}\big)Z(\lambda)\phi(\lambda),

Thus
\Phi^{0}=\phi^{0}=\Phi(0)=\phi(0)
, and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Phi^{n}\in\big(1-P_{\phi^{0}}\big)\mathcal{H}=\{\Phi^{0}\}^{\perp}
. We conclude that

\displaystyle\Phi^{n}\perp\Phi^{0},\qquad\forall n\geqslant 1.

(131)

The normalization of
\Phi(\lambda)
is called the intermediate normalization. It is not a unit vector for all
\lambda\neq 0
in general, but has the convenient property (
131
). For instance in the case of families of eigenvectors, it is computable as recalled in Section
9
. For this reason, this is usually the one that is computer first in eigenvalue problems depending on one parameter.

C.3.
From standard normalization to unit normalization

Once
\Phi^{n}
is computed, or once one has proved properties on it, one can need to work with
\phi^{n}
again. One way of going from intermediate normalization to unit normalization is to fix the phasis gauge of
\phi(\lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\left<\phi^{0},\phi(\lambda)\right>\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then
\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\|\Phi(\lambda)\|}=\left(\|\Phi(\lambda)\|\left<\phi^{0},\phi(\lambda)\right>\right)^{-1}\phi(\lambda)
so
\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\|\Phi(\lambda)\|}
and
\phi(\lambda)
have the same phasis, and since they both have unit normalization they are equal,

\displaystyle\phi(\lambda)=\frac{\Phi(\lambda)}{\|\Phi(\lambda)\|}.

(132)

The next result shows how to obtain the series
\phi^{n}
from the
\Phi^{n}
’s.

Lemma C.1

(Obtaining the unit normalization series from the intermediate normalization one)
.

We define
Y^{0}:=X^{0}:=1
,
Y^{1}:=X^{1}:=0
and, recursively, for any
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle Y^{n}:=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\left<\Phi^{n-k},\Phi^{k}\right>-Y^{n-k}Y^{k}\right),\qquad X^{n}:=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}X^{k}Y^{n-k}.

(133)

Then
\phi^{0}=\Phi^{0}
,
\phi^{1}=\Phi^{1}
and for any
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\phi^{n}=\Phi^{n}+\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}X^{n-k}\Phi^{k}.

(134)

We remark that
\phi^{2}=\Phi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\phi^{1}\|^{2}\phi^{0}
.

Proof.

We define
y(\lambda):=\|\Phi(\lambda)\|
and consider its Taylor series

\displaystyle y^{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}y(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0},

the relation
y(\lambda)^{2}=\|\Phi(\lambda)\|^{2}
gives, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n}y^{n-k}y^{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\left<\Phi^{n-k},\Phi^{k}\right>

hence, using
y^{0}=\|\phi^{0}\|^{-2}=1
and (
131
), we get a recursive way of obtaining the
y^{n}
’s, which is
y^{1}=0
and for any
n\geqslant 2
, via

\displaystyle y^{n}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\left(\left<\Phi^{n-k},\Phi^{k}\right>-y^{n-k}y^{k}\right),

so
y^{n}=Y^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. We then define
x(\lambda):=1/y(\lambda)
, and its Taylor series
x^{n}:=\frac{1}{n!}\left(\frac{{\rm d}^{n}}{{\rm d}\lambda^{n}}x(\lambda)\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\lambda=0}
. The relation
x(\lambda)y(\lambda)=1
gives
\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}y^{n-k}=\delta_{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, yielding
x^{0}=1
,
x^{1}=0
and for any
n\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle x^{n}=-\sum_{k=0}^{n-2}x^{k}y^{n-k},

so
x^{n}=X^{n}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Finally, from (
132
) we have
\phi(\lambda)=\Phi(\lambda)x(\lambda)
hence we deduce (
134
).
∎

\displaystyle\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{{\bm{\phi}}}\right)\|^{2}-\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left|E_{\alpha}+a\right|\left(\|\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha}\|^{2}-2\|\Lambda^{\perp}\left(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha}\right)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left|E_{\alpha}+a\right|\left(\|\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha}\|^{2}-\|\Lambda^{\perp}\left(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha}\right)\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left|E_{\alpha}+a\right|\|\Lambda\left(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha}\right)\|^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\left|E_{\alpha}+a\right|\left|\left<\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha},\phi_{\mu}\right>\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:tcomp}\end{subarray}}{\leqslant}\;\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\left|\left<\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\alpha},\phi_{\mu}-\psi^{{\bm{\phi}}}_{\mu}\right>\right|^{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\|{\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{{\bm{\phi}}}\|_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\qquad\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:error_dm_vecs}\end{subarray}}{\leqslant}\;\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2}^{4}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\|\left(H+a\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2}^{2}\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\|_{2}^{2}.

Then

\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{{\bm{\phi}}}\right)\|\leqslant\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\|\left(H+a\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\times\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\|_{2}.

(139)

Next,

\displaystyle\|A\big({\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{{\bm{\phi}}}\big)\|\leqslant\|A\left|H+a\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left({\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{{\bm{\phi}}}\right)\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|A\left|H+a\right|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\left(1+\frac{1}{4}\lambda_{\textup{max}}\|\left(H+a\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\times\|\left|H+a\right|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\|_{2},

and we deduce (
135
) by using (
139
).

Let us now show (
136
). For any
U\in\mathcal{U}_{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left<U\psi_{\alpha},HU\psi_{\alpha}\right>=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu,\beta\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\overline{U}_{\alpha\mu}U_{\alpha\beta}\left<\psi_{\mu},H\psi_{\beta}\right>

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu,\beta\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\overline{U}_{\alpha\mu}U_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}\left<\psi_{\mu},\psi_{\beta}\right>=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu,\beta\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\overline{U}_{\alpha\mu}U_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{E}_{\beta}\delta_{\mu-\beta}

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha,\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}\overline{U}_{\alpha\mu}U_{\alpha\mu}\mathcal{E}_{\mu}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}U^{*}_{\mu\alpha}U_{\alpha\mu}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\left(U^{*}U\right)_{\mu\mu}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\mathcal{E}_{\mu}.

Hence similarly as in (
76
),

\displaystyle\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left<\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha},\left(E_{\alpha}-H\right)\left(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha}\right)\right>=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left<\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha},\left(E_{\alpha}-H\right)\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha}\right>

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}E_{\alpha}-\left<U^{{\bm{\phi}},{\bm{\psi}}}\psi_{\alpha},HU^{{\bm{\phi}},{\bm{\psi}}}\psi_{\alpha}\right>=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left(E_{\alpha}-\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left(E_{\alpha}-\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\right)\right|\leqslant\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\left|\left<A\big(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha}\big),A^{-1}\big(E_{\alpha}-H\big)A^{-1}A\big(\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha}\big)\right>\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|A^{-1}\left(E_{\alpha}-H\right)A^{-1}\|\right)\sum_{\alpha=1}^{\nu}\|\phi_{\alpha}-\psi^{\bm{\phi}}_{\alpha}\|_{e}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\left(c_{H}+c_{A}^{2}\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\alpha\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\left|E_{\alpha}\right|\right)\|A\big({\bm{\phi}}-{\bm{\psi}}^{\bm{\phi}}\big)\|^{2}.

∎

In this section, we provide another way of bounding

\displaystyle\left(z-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left(z-H\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Gamma^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\perp}\mathcal{H}}\right)^{-1}&\mbox{on }\Gamma^{\perp}\mathcal{H},\\

Proposition E.1

(Alternative bound for
\Gamma-\Lambda
)
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
3.1
, and if further the following gap assumptions

\displaystyle\dist\left(\{\mathcal{E}_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\nu},\sigma_{\textup{d}}\big(H_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Gamma^{\perp}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Gamma^{\perp}\mathcal{H}}\big)\right)

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle\dist\left(\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{\nu},\sigma_{\textup{d}}\big(\left(\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\Lambda^{\perp}\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\big)\right)

\displaystyle>0,

(142)

hold, then

\|\Gamma-\Lambda\|_{2,\delta}\leqslant c_{A}^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}^{2}+\nu c_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\mathcal{P}\Lambda\|\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|A^{\delta}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|\\
+\left(c_{A}c_{\mathcal{P}}\|A\Gamma\|\right)^{\delta}\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta}\left(2+\nu\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leqslant\mu\leqslant\nu\end{subarray}}{\text{{max}}}\;\|\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\|\right).

and

E.1.
Proof of Proposition
E.1

In this section we present another way of treating
\Gamma-\Lambda
.

For any
z\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\sigma\big((\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\big)
, we define the partial inverse

\displaystyle\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\mkern 1.0mu\vrule height=6.02777pt\mkern 2.0mu\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\right)^{-1}&\mbox{on }\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H},\\
0&\mbox{on }\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{H},\end{array}\right.

extended by linearity on
\mathcal{H}
. By the definition (
E.1
),

\displaystyle\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\varphi=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varphi&\mbox{if }\varphi\in\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H},\\
0&\mbox{if }\varphi\in\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{H},\end{array}\right.

hence

\displaystyle\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)\left(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{P}.

(146)

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\mathcal{P}-(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}-(z-H)(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}-(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}+\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}-H)(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\eqref{eq:res_PHP}\end{subarray}}{=}\;\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}(H-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}},

where we used that
(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{P}(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}
in the last step.
We now use that

\displaystyle\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}P_{\phi_{\mu}}(z-H)^{-1}=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}P_{\phi_{\mu}}(z-E_{\mu})^{-1}

to deduce
\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma^{\perp}=\Gamma^{\perp}(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma=0
and
\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}=\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma
, so we can write

\displaystyle(z-H)^{-1}

\displaystyle\qquad=\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma+\Gamma^{\perp}(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma+\Gamma(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma^{\perp}+\Gamma^{\perp}(z-H)^{-1}\Gamma^{\perp}

\displaystyle\qquad=(z-H)^{-1}_{\perp}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}P_{\phi_{\mu}}(z-E_{\mu})^{-1}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\Lambda(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}+\Lambda^{\perp}(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\Lambda^{\perp}

\displaystyle=(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\perp}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}P_{\psi_{\mu}}(z-\mathcal{E}_{\mu})^{-1}.

The operators
(z-H)^{-1}_{\perp}
and
(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\perp}
are holomorphic in the interior of
\mathcal{C}
so they will “participate passively” to the Cauchy integral. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}(z-E_{\mu})^{-1}(z-\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})^{-1}{\rm d}z=\delta_{E_{\mu}\neq\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}}\left((\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}-E_{\mu})^{-1}+(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})^{-1}\right)=0.

(147)

We are ready to compute the Cauchy integral

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\left(\Gamma-\Lambda\right)\mathcal{P}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\left(\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\mathcal{P}-(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}\right){\rm d}z

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{P}(z-H)^{-1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H(z-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P})^{-1}_{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{H}}{\rm d}z

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{\mu=1}^{\nu}\left(\mathcal{P}P_{\phi_{\mu}}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}+\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}HP_{\psi_{\mu}}\right).

As for inequalities, we have

\displaystyle\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}P_{\phi_{\mu}}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}A^{-\delta}A^{\delta}\Gamma P_{\phi_{\mu}}\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}A^{-\delta}\|\|A^{\delta}\Gamma\|\|P_{\phi_{\mu}}\|_{2}\|\Gamma\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\|\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\left(c_{A}c_{\mathcal{P}}\|A\Gamma\|\right)^{\delta}\|\left(E_{\mu}-\mathcal{P}H\mathcal{P}\right)_{\perp}^{-1}H\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\|\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}\Gamma\|_{2,\delta},

and

\displaystyle\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}HP_{\psi_{\mu}}\|_{2}=\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}A^{-\delta}A^{\delta}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\Lambda P_{\psi_{\mu}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\|A^{\delta}\mathcal{P}A^{-\delta}\|\|A^{\delta}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\Lambda\|\|P_{\psi_{\mu}}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant c_{\mathcal{P}}^{\delta}\|A^{\delta}\left(\mathcal{E}_{\mu}-H\right)_{\perp}^{-1}\|\|\mathcal{P}^{\perp}H\Lambda\|,

and also using the inequalities of Section
7.2
, we can deduce (
143
) of Proposition
E.1
.

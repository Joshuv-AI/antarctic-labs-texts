Abstract
. The Shapley value provides a natural means of quantifying the contributions of facts to database query answers. In this work, we seek to broaden our understanding of Shapley value computation (SVC) in the database setting by revealing how it relates to Fixed-size Generalized Model Counting (FGMC), which is the problem of computing the number of sub-databases of a given size and containing a given set of assumed facts that satisfy a fixed query. Our focus will be on explaining the difficulty of SVC via FGMC, and to this end, we identify general conditions on queries which enable reductions from FGMC to SVC. As a byproduct, we not only obtain alternative explanations for existing hardness results for SVC, but also new complexity results. In particular, we establish FP - # ​ P \#\textsf{P} complexity dichotomies for constant-free unions of connected CQs and connected homomorphism-closed graph queries. We also consider some variants of the SVC problem, by disallowing assumed facts or quantifying the contributions of constants rather than facts.

3.1.
Shapley Value Computation

The
Shapley value
has been introduced by Lloyd Shapley in 1952
(
Shapley 1953
)
to distribute the wealth of a
cooperative game
, and it follows three axioms of good behaviour.
Intuitively, these axioms state that the value of two isomorphic
games
is the same, that the sum of all players values equals the total wealth of the
game
and that the value of a sum of two
games
equals the sum of the individual games’ values.
Remarkably, there is only one function satisfying these axioms: the Shapley value.

A
cooperative game
is given by a finite set of players
P
and a
wealth function

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}:\wp(P)\to\mathbb{Q}
that represents the wealth generated by every possible coalition of players, such that
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\varnothing)=0
.
To define the
Shapley value
, assume players arrive one by one in a random order, and each earns the difference between the coalition’s wealth before she arrives and after.
The
Shapley value
of a player
p\in P
is her expected earnings in this scenario, which can be expressed as:

(1)

\immediate\immediate\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},p)\mathrel{\hat{=}}\frac{1}{|P|!}\sum_{\sigma\in{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbb{P}}(P)}\left({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p}\cup\{p\})-{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p})\right)

where
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\mathbb{P}}(P)
denotes the set of permutations of
P
and
\sigma_{<p}
the set of players that appear strictly before
p
in the permutation
\sigma
. The following equivalent formula – more convenient for our proofs – can be obtained by grouping together the
\sigma
having the same
\sigma_{<p}
.

(2)

\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},p)=\sum_{B\subseteq P\setminus\{p\}}\!\!\!\frac{|B|!(|P|-|B|-1)!}{|P|!}\left({\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(B\cup\{p\})-{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(B)\right)

The
cooperative games
that will interest us are those associated with a
Boolean query

q
and a
partitioned database

\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{n}}}},\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{x}}}})
, where the set of players is the set
\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{n}}}}
of

facts
, and the
wealth function

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\mathbf{v}_{q}}
assigns
v_{S}-v_{\textup{{x}}}
to each subset

, where
v_{S}=1
(resp.
v_{\textup{{x}}}=1
) if

(resp. if
\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{x}}}}\models q
), and

otherwise.

The main focus of this paper is to understand the complexity of computing the

of facts in such

We will use

3.3.
Probabilistic Query Evaluation

A
tuple-independent probabilistic database
is a pair
\mathcal{D}=(S,\pi)
where

is a finite set of

and
\pi:S\to(0,1]
is a probability assignment.
We
associate
to every such database a

. For a
Boolean query

\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{Pr}}(\mathcal{D}\models q)

\alpha

For a fixed
Boolean query

tuple-independent probabilistic database

\mathcal{D}

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{Pr}}(\mathcal{D}\models q)

\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{PQE}_{q}}

We consider also restrictions of
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{PQE}_{q}}
where the input

(S,\pi)
is such that the image

has certain characteristics.

(1)

If

we obtain
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{PQE}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)}
.

(2)

If

we obtain
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{PQE}_{q}\left(\frac{1}{2};1\right)}
.

(3)

If
Im(\pi)=\{p\}
for some
p\in(0,1]
, we obtain
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{SPQE}_{q}}
: the
single probability query evaluation
problem.

(4)

If
Im(\pi)=\{p,1\}
for some
p\in(0,1]
, we obtain
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{SPPQE}_{q}}

4.
Main Results

Our main results are reductions from

(ii)

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q\land q^{\prime}}}

(iii)

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}

6.1.
Purely Endogenous Databases

Having the possibility of fixing some

However, often we will be presented with an unpartitioned database, in which case all

Unfortunately,

how

Let us start by observing that the proof of
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}

fact
to the input database, meaning it cannot prove

immediately. However, the following lemma shows that a constant number of

is not a problem under

.

.

Fix

6.2.
Queries with Negation

PQE
and
SVC
have been explored beyond
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}C}
-hom-closed
queries, by considering queries with some form of negation. An
FP
/
\#\textsf{P}
dichotomy has been established for
SVC
over
self-join-free conjunctive queries with safe negations
(sjf-
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
) in
(
Reshef et al. 2020
, Theorem 3.1)
, and another dichotomy for
PQE
has been shown for the larger class of self-join-free
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{1RA}^{-}}
queries
(
Fink and Olteanu 2016
, Theorem 1.1)
. In fact, it follows from the proof of
(
Fink and Olteanu 2016
)
that this latter dichotomy is also valid for
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{PQE}\left(\frac{1}{2};1\right)}
, since no other probability is used.

Briefly, a sjf-
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
query is a
sjf-CQ
that may have some negative atoms, with the restriction that their variables are present in the positive part. The
hierarchical
queries are defined as before (except the atoms can be negative), and they also characterize the tractable queries.

A slight adaptation of our proof technique yields the following:

Proposition
6.0
.

Let
q
be a sjf-
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
, where
q^{+}
and
q^{-}
are its positive and negative atoms, respectively.
Let
q_{vc}^{+}
be any maximal
variable-connected
subquery of
q^{+}
, and
q_{vc}^{-}
be the atoms of
q^{-}
whose every variable is in
q^{+}_{vc}
. Then,
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q_{vc}^{+}\land q_{vc}^{-}}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}
.

The preceding result does not cover the full dichotomy of sjf-
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
because of the basic
non-hierarchical
query
A(x)\land\lnot S(x,y)\land B(y)
. However, it does cover all queries with a
variable-connected
positive part,
as well as sjf-
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
queries with “component-guarded negation”,
defined as the sjf-
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{CQ}^{\lnot}}
queries such that the set of variables of every negative atom appears in the same maximal
variable-connected
subquery of the positive part.

6.3.
Maximum Shapley Value

A main reason for considering the Shapley value is to identify the most important facts for a given query. One might therefore consider
the problem of computing the top contributor and its
Shapley value
.
Concretely, let
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{max-SVC}_{q}}
be the problem of, given a
database

\mathcal{D}
, outputting any
fact

\alpha\in\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{n}}}}
and its
Shapley value

v_{\alpha}
for
q
, such that
v_{\alpha}
is the maximum value among all (
endogenous
)
facts
.
It turns out that all of our reductions to show
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}
can be adapted to show
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{max-SVC}_{q}}
, leading to the conjecture that
max-SVC
might be equivalent to
SVC
.

Proposition 6.0.

For any pair of queries
q,q^{\prime}
for which we obtain a reduction
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q^{\prime}}}
as a consequence of
Lemma

4.1
,
4.7
or
4.9
, we have
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\leq_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{max-SVC}_{q^{\prime}}}
.

The proof of Proposition
6.5
utilizes the following lemma that shows that the Shapley value of a fact that is a
generalized support
on its own is always maximal. This property holds for the class of games that are monotone (
B\subseteq B^{\prime}\Rightarrow{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(B)\leq{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(B^{\prime})
) and binary (
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}
has image
\{0,1\}
).

Lemma 6.0.

Let
(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}})
be a monotone binary Shapley
game
that contains some
s\in P
such that
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\{s\})=1
. Then for every
p\in P
,
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},p)\leq{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},s)
.

Proof.

For every
p\in P
, denote
\mathcal{W}_{p}\mathrel{\hat{=}}\{\sigma\in{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbb{P}}(P)\mid{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p}\cup\{p\})-{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p})=1\}
. Since the
game
is monotone and binary,
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\varnothing)=0
and
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}_{q}}{\{s\}}=1
, the
\mathcal{W}_{p}
form a partition of
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbb{P}}(P)
, and by
Equation

1

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},p)
is proportional to
|\mathcal{W}_{p}|
.

Now let
p\in P
and
\sigma\in\mathcal{W}_{p}
. Since the
game
is binary, then necessarily we must have
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p})=0
, which implies
s\not\in\sigma_{<p}
because the
game
is monotone. Therefore, if
\tau
denotes the function that swaps the positions of
s
and
p
in a permutation, then
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\tau(\sigma)_{<s}\cup\{s\})-{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\tau(\sigma)_{<s})=1
because
\tau(\sigma)_{<s}\subseteq\sigma_{<p}
and
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\sigma_{<p})=0
. This means that
\tau_{|\mathcal{W}_{p}}
injects in
\mathcal{W}_{s}
hence
|\mathcal{W}_{p}|\leq|\mathcal{W}_{s}|
. It follows that
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},p)\leq{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(P,{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}},s)
.∎

Proof of Proposition
6.5
.

Note that if we assume that
\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{x}}}}\nvDash q
(otherwise every fact has a zero hence maximal Shapley value), a fact
\mu
that is a
generalized support
on its own indeed verifies
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}}(\{s\})=1
. Now if we go back to the constructions used to prove
Lemmas

4.1
,
4.7
and
4.9
, they are equally valid if we take
S^{0}\mathrel{\hat{=}}S
and
S^{-}\mathrel{\hat{=}}\varnothing
(the only point of
S^{-}
was to limit the number of
exogenous
facts). If we do so, then
\mu
, which is the only fact the
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}
oracle is called on, is a singleton
generalized support
and therefore has a maximal Shapley value.
∎

Observe that
max-SVC
is not the same problem as the “maximum
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}
contributor problem”, that is, the problem of finding a
fact
with maximum
Shapley value
. We believe this is a relevant problem which may, in principle, be simpler to address than
SVC
.

6.4.
Shapley Value of Constants

Instead of computing the
Shapley values
of
facts
, one could consider a setting in which the players are a set of “endogenous constants”. Likewise, one could consider supports consisting of constants, or assign independent probabilities to the
constants
instead of
facts
.

As an example, consider a simple
schema
containing two binary relations
Publication(authorID, paperID)
and
Keyword(paperID, keywordStr)
containing publications and related keywords, and the query
q^{*}=\exists x,y~\textsf{Publication}(x,y)\land\textsf{Keyword}(y,\texttt{`Shapley'})
testing the existence of
‘Shapley’
-related papers.
One could then extract the experience level of authors on this topic by computing the
Shapley value
of author
constants
,
treating all
constants
as
exogenous
except those in the
authorID
column. Note that the Shapley value for facts would be much less informative as an author’s expertise may be split across several facts.

This idea gives rise to variants of
PQE
,
FGMC
and
SVC
.
For any
database

\mathcal{D}
and
C\subseteq{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{Const}}
, let
\mathcal{D}|_{C}
denote the
database
induced by the
constants

C
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{D}|_{C}\mathrel{\hat{=}}\{\alpha\in\mathcal{D}\mid{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(\alpha)\subseteq C\}
.
For a monotone query
q
,
let
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{SVC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
be the task of computing, for
a
database

\mathcal{D}
,
a partition
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(\mathcal{D})=C_{\textup{{n}}}\mathop{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\uplus}}C_{\textup{{x}}}
, and
a
constant

c\in C_{\textup{{n}}}
,
the
Shapley value
in the
cooperative game
where the set of players is
C_{\textup{{n}}}

and the
wealth function
assigns
1
to any subset
C\subseteq C_{\textup{{n}}}
such that
\mathcal{D}|_{C\cup C_{\textup{{x}}}}\models q
and
\mathcal{D}|_{C_{\textup{{x}}}}\not\models q
, or
0
otherwise.
Similarly, let
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
be the task of computing, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
database

\mathcal{D}
and
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(\mathcal{D})=C_{\textup{{n}}}\mathop{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\uplus}}C_{\textup{{x}}}
, the number of sets
C\subseteq C_{\textup{{n}}}
of size
k
such that
\mathcal{D}|_{C\cup C_{\textup{{x}}}}\models q
.
The variants
\immediate\immediate\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{SVC}^{\textsf{{n}},\textit{const}}_{q}}
and
\immediate{\color[rgb]{0.3438,0.0195,0.0273}\textup{FMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
(and the probabilistic variants) can be defined analogously.

By a simple adaptation of our proof of
Lemma

4.1
, we can show a polynomial equivalence result for these variants:

Proposition 6.0.

For every
hom-closed
query
q
, we have

{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\equiv_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
and
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textsf{{n}},\textit{const}}_{q}}\mathrel{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\equiv_{\textit{poly}}}}{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
.

Proof.

The reduction from
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
(resp.
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textsf{{n}},\textit{const}}_{q}}
) to
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
(resp.
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
) can be obtained by directly applying the proofs of
Proposition

3.3
and
Corollary

6.2
. For the other direction, we shall prove the stronger result that for any
C
-hom-closed
query
q
,
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}(\mathcal{D})
(resp.
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}(\mathcal{D})
) can be computed in polynomial time from an oracle to
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
(resp.
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textsf{{n}},\textit{const}}_{q}}
) provided that
\mathcal{D}\cap C\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{x}}}}
. The argument is a simple adaptation of the proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an input database such that
\mathcal{D}\cap C\subseteq\mathcal{D}_{{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{{x}}}}
. If all
minimal supports
of
q
have their constants in
C
, then either all coalitions are
generalized supports
or none are, but either way the computation of
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}(\mathcal{D})
is trivial. Otherwise, take a
minimal support

S
of
q
such that
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(S)\nsubseteq C
, and by
C
-
homomorphically
renaming it if necessary we can assume
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(S)\setminus C
is reduced to a singleton
\{a_{\mu}\}
with
a_{\mu}\notin{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textit{const}}(\mathcal{D})
.
Since the players are the constants,
S
is essentially a
duplicable singleton support
(see
Corollary

4.6
): in the formula that defines
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathrm{Sh}}(C_{\textup{{n}}}\cup\{a_{\mu}\},{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\mathbf{v}_{q}},a_{\mu})
, any coalition that contains
a_{\mu}
will satisfy the query, and any that does not will not be contained in the input database.

Finally, note that no
exogenous
element (
constants
instead of
facts
in this setting) is added since
S
, the minimal support used in the construction, only contains a single
constant
.
∎

In fact, this proposition can be extended to apply more generally to
C
-hom-closed
queries provided the constants in
C
are
exogenous
– in particular, capturing the example query
q^{*}
.

Wenote that in the
graph database
setting these variants are equivalent to considering
Shapley values
of
nodes
instead of
edges
. This problem was considered in
(
Khalil and Kimelfeld 2023
, §6)
, where the authors showed that
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
is
\#\textsf{P}
-hard for any full
CRPQ
with a “non-redundant” atom whose language contains a word of length at least four.

7.
Discussion

After having identified
FGMC
as the suitable counting analog of
SVC
,
we have exhibited reductions from
FGMC
to
SVC
.
As the hypotheses of our reductions are formulated generally, they
can be combined with existing hardness results for model counting or probabilistic query evaluation
to establish
\#\textsf{P}
-hardness for
SVC

for a range of query classes. In particular, we have obtained an
FP
/
\#\textsf{P}
dichotomy
for constant-free unions of connected CQs, a relevant stepping stone towards
the conjectured dichotomy for UCQs.
In some cases, our reductions allow us not only to transfer hardness results but also
to establish the polynomial-time equivalence between
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}_{q}}

and
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}_{q}}
, providing the strongest evidence as of yet that Shapley value computation is, from a complexity perspective, nothing other
than a counting problem.

As is apparent from Figure
1(b)
, most of our new results concern constant-free queries, as the presence of constants in queries make reductions from
FGMC
considerably more difficult. While there are known methods for eliminating constants from queries in the probabilistic setting (most notably, the so-called query “shattering”
(
Dalvi and Suciu 2012
, §2.5)
), they interact badly with our hypotheses. For example, the shattering of a connected

variable-connected
query may become disconnected, and not even
pseudo-connected
, as illustrated by
.
It is therefore remains an important question for future work how best to eliminate or otherwise handle constants when reducing
FGMC
to
SVC
.

Aside from extending our reductions to larger classes of queries, there are other interesting questions
to explore related to the variants from Section
6
.
In particular, we would like to know whether the purely endogenous variant
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{SVC}^{\textsf{{n}}}}
is as hard as
SVC
, which appears to be related to the open question of whether there exist queries for which
MC
is tractable but not
GMC
.
We are also eager to understand better the Shapley value of constants, which we find natural. Due to
Proposition

6.7
,
we can alternatively consider the model counting analog
{\color[rgb]{0.0195,0.2109,0.2539}\textup{FGMC}^{\textit{const}}_{q}}
,
which is conceptually simpler.

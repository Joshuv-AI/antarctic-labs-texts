Calderón-Zygmund theory with noncommuting kernels via H 1 c H_{1}^{c}
Antonio Ismael Cano-Mármol, Éric Ricard

Abstract
. We study an alternative definition of the H 1 H_{1} -space associated to a semicommutative von Neumann algebra L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) ​ ⊗ ¯ ​ ℳ L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M} , first studied by Mei. Introduction

This paper is related to the theory of semicommutative
Calderón-Zygmund operators. This is a research line that takes
advantage of the hybrid nature of certain vector valued
L_{p}
-spaces. Let
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
be a von Neumann algebra of
operators on a separable Hilbert space, equipped with a normal
semifinite faithful trace
\tau
. Denote by

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

the weak operator closure of the space of essentially bounded
(strongly measurable) functions

f:\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
. For the sake of
exposition, we will restrict ourselves to dimension 1, even
though our arguments extend trivially to any finite dimension namely for
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The
von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}
can be identified with the
tensor product

L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
equipped with
the trace

\displaystyle\varphi(f)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau(f(x))\ dx.

The noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces associated with
\mathcal{A}
are indeed vector-valued
L_{p}
-spaces: indeed,
[
22
, Chapter 3]

L_{p}(\mathcal{A})=L_{p}(\mathbb{R};L_{p}(\mathcal{M})),

for
1\leq p<\infty
. We are interested in endpoint estimates for
operators acting on
L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
, and in particular in the
boundedness of operators from the operator-valued version of the Hardy
space
H_{1}
into
L_{1}
. This question was widely studied in the
classical setting for scalar-valued functions
[
20
,
19
]
as
well as for vector-valued functions
[
7
,
13
]
, where the
existence of the atomic decomposition plays an essential role. This
technique does not seem to have been exploited as often in the
noncommutative setting except perhaps in
[
11
]
. Mei
[
18
]
was the first to introduce the
so-called
operator-valued Hardy space

H_{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
in this context via noncommutative
equivalents of the Poisson integral, the Lusin area integral and the
Littlewood-Paley
g
function. These techniques allowed Mei to identify
the dual space of
H_{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, which is denoted by

\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, in the spirit of the classical
argument by Fefferman and Stein
[
6
]
. Moreover, some maximal
inequalities, and several interpolation results via a martingale
approach were established. Mei’s fundamental contribution has been a key
in the development of noncommutative forms of Calderón-Zygmund
theory, both in the semicommutative context and in fully
noncommutative ones via transference techniques. For the first one,
the semicommutative Calderón-Zygmund theory was initiated in

[
21
]
with the obtaining of weak-
L_{1}
endpoint inequalities
for singular integrals, with an argument that was simplified in recent
years
[
3
,
2
]
. The second line has many instances, among
which are
[
10
,
15
]
.

The initial motivation for the present work was to obtain new
interpolation consequences of endpoint estimates of the type

L_{\infty}(\mathcal{A})
-
\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
which rely, by
duality, on the structure of the Hardy space

H_{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
. Our goal led to two main tasks to
tackle: a completely explicit description of

\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, and the study of the boundedness of
Calderón-Zygmund operators on the Hardy space via its atomic
decomposition. This may seem common but another of our goals is to provide complete proofs; for instance, in many papers the extension from atoms to the whole Hardy space is overlooked but requires more delicate arguments than the main estimates (see
[
1
]
). We also want to point out that up to now there is no notion of noncommutative distributions, thus one has to be careful when defining Calderón-Zygmund operators.

The operator-valued
\mathsf{BMO}
-space introduced by
Mei,
\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, is defined as the
intersection of a column space and a row space,

\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})

respectively. The reason for considering both a column and a row space
is a ubiquitous phenomenon in noncommutative analysis (see
[
17
]

for an outstanding example), and by symmetry we shall limit our
discussion to the column case.
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
is set
to be the subspace of the column Hilbert-valued space (
[
14
]
)

L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
on which
the seminorm

(MBMO)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}I\subseteq\mathbb{R}\\
|I|\ \mathrm{finite}\end{subarray}}\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}\big|g-g_{I}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{\mathcal{M}}

makes sense, where
g_{I}=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}g
. The
\mathsf{BMO_{r}}
seminorm is
\|g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}=\|g^{*}\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}
. The space
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
denotes the closure of
\mathcal{M}\otimes L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
with respect to the weak
∗
topology of the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
. There is no guarantee that
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
is a space of
\mathcal{M}-
valued functions (in the Bochner sense), and so the integral in (
MBMO
) may not be well-defined. Indeed,

L_{2}\big(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}};\mathcal{M}\big)\subset L_{\infty}\big(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})\big),

but the reverse inclusion fails in general,
a priori
preventing us from defining
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}
as a space of functions. In section 1 and the first part of section 2, we propose a general construction of
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
which recovers Mei’s description given by (
MBMO
). We will also study a predual of
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
(resp.
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
). The novelty here is that this predual space, which we denote as
H_{1}^{r}(\mathcal{A})
(resp.
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
), will be a row (resp. column) Hardy space which is exclusively constructed in terms of “new” atomic decompositions, which extend the work of the second author PhD Thesis
[
23
]
.

The key to our approach is the
H_{1}
-
\mathsf{BMO}
duality product when elements in the former space are described in terms of atoms. In the classical case, it is a well-known fact that the norm of
g\in\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
can be characterized through the expression

(atBMO)

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}}=\sup_{a}\big|\int ga\big|,

so that the supremum is taken over
L_{2}
-atoms
[
8
,
20
,
19
]
. An analogous formula for
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
may shed light on the structure of atoms in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
. This is exactly what we achieve. First, it turns out that it is enough to consider only elements
g\in\mathcal{A}\cap\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
. The expression (
MBMO
) is meaningful for
g
, and so duality yields

\displaystyle\|g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}

\displaystyle=\|g^{*}\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}=\sup_{I}\Big\|\Big(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}\big|g^{*}-(g^{*})_{I}\big|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{I,\|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1}\Big(\int_{I}\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}h(g-g_{I})\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\Big)^{1/2}.

with the supremum taken over
h
in the unit ball of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. Now, recalling that
g-g_{I}
has zero integral over
I
, it follows that

\|g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}=\sup_{I,h}\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}h(g-g_{I})\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\big\|_{L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{I,h}\sup_{\|f\|_{L_{2}}\leq 1}\big|(\tau\circ\int)(h(g-g_{I})\frac{f\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}}{\sqrt{|I|}})\big|.

Comparing the latter expression with (
atBMO
) suggests that an atom in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
should be an operator of the form
a=bh
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{A})
, where
h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
with
\|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1
and
b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is supported on some interval
I
and has additional cancellation over
I
that we will make precise later.
In what follows,
a
will be called a
c
-atom
. Then, define the column Hardy space
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
as the Banach subspace of
L_{1}(\mathcal{A})
of those operators
f
such that

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \mathrm{in}\ L_{1}(\mathcal{A}),\ \mathrm{for\ some}\ (a_{i})_{i}\ c\textrm{-atoms},\ (\lambda_{i})_{i}\in\ell_{1},

which becomes a Banach space with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{1}^{c}}=\inf\big\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\ :\ f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\big\}.

The row space
H_{1}^{r}(\mathcal{A})
is defined analogously and
H_{1}(\mathcal{A})=H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})+H_{1}^{r}(\mathcal{A})
. By symmetry, it suffices to show
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}=\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
. The proof of this duality result strongly relies on the extension of a well-known argument by Meyer
[
20
]
: the space

\displaystyle L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt)=\big\{f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt)\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}f=0\big\}

is a dense subspace of the classical atomic Hardy space

H_{1}(\mathbb{R})
. A further characterization of the column
Hilbert-valued spaces
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};H^{c})
will be the key
to establish an analogous result in our context. We will briefly
explain how to get that the column Hardy space
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
coincides with the
one introduced by Mei in
[
18
]
in section
4
. This
will in particular allow us to indeed use interpolation as usual.

It is worth noting that Mei’s work already contains a description
of
H_{1}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
in terms of certain atomic
decompositions. However, the one included in the present paper
seems more useful to deal with Calderón-Zygmund operators and to
make connections with vector valued harmonic analysis. Indeed, it
allows us to consider singular integrals with noncommuting
kernels, something that looks difficult to reach at the
weak-
L_{1}
level
[
2
]
. Let
\mathcal{M}

be a von Neumann algebra over a separable Hilbert space, we
denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of compactly supported essentially
bounded functions

\mathbb{R}\to L_{\infty}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
( measurable with values in
L_{1}
); it
will play the role of test functions.
Let
T
be a
bounded operator on
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
for which there exists a
kernel

K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\ \setminus\ \{x=y\}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}
such that

\displaystyle\int T(f)(x)g(x)\ dx=\int\int K(x,y)f(y)g(x)\ dx\ dy

holds for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
satisfying that the distance between the supports
\mathrm{supp}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}
and
\mathrm{supp}\|g\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}
is strictly positive. In that case, We say that
T
is a Calderón-Zygmund operator with kernel
K
. Also, assume that
T
fulfills

•

a right-modularity condition:
T(fh)=T(f)h
for any
f\in L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
with compact support and
h\in\mathcal{M}
; we say that
T
is a left Calderón-Zygmund operator,

•

the
Hörmander condition

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\geq 2|y^{\prime}-y|}\|K(x,y)-K(x,y^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ dx<\infty.

Under these assumptions,
T
extends to a bounded map
T:H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow L_{1}(\mathcal{A})
. The proof of this statement is inspired by
[
19
]
and is divided in two steps. The first one consists in obtaining an universal constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|T(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{A})}\leq C\ \textrm{for \ any \ $c$-atom}\ a.

In order to set up this statement, we first check that the extension of
T
to
c
-atoms satisfies

\displaystyle T(a)=T(b)h\ \textrm{for \ any \ $c$-atom}\ a=bh.

This allows us to exploit the boundedness of
T
on
L_{2}(\mathcal{A})
.
We rely on
an approximation of
T
and
K
by certain uniformly bounded kernels, which also implies that
T
extends to the whole
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
. Once we have done that, we obtain that whenever
K
is scalar and modularity works on both sides, then
T
extends to a bounded operator from
H_{1}(\mathcal{A})
into
L_{1}(\mathcal{A})
. This is somehow a dual statement of a result in
[
16
]
where
L_{\infty}
-BMO estimates are given and in spirit close to
[
11
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In section 1, we
shall introduce the column and row Hilbert-valued noncommutative

-spaces. This enables us to define

\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R,\mathcal{M}})

Column/row Hilbert-valued
L_{p}
-spaces

In this section we collect some basic facts on classical vector-valued
L_{p}
-spaces
[

-spaces

Before giving definitions, some facts about vector-valued functions and von Neumann algebras should be tackled (see
[
5
, Chapter 2]
and
[
24

\mathbb{X}
be a Banach space, and let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space (or more generally, a localizable measure space
[
25
]
). Then, a function
f:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{X}
is said to be
\mu
-measurable
whenever there exists a sequence of simple functions
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}

f_{n}=\sum_{i}x_{i}^{n}\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{A_{i}^{n}}
for some
x_{i}^{n}\in\mathbb{X}
and
\mu-
measurable sets
A_{i}^{n}
, such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\|f_{n}-f\|_{\mathbb{X}}=0\ \mu\mathrm{-almost\ everywhere}.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, one can then define

L_{p}(\Omega,\mu;\mathbb{X})
as the space of
\mu
-measurable
functions
f
for which
\|f(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\in L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)

with the norm

\|f\|_{p}=\|\|f(\cdot)\|_{\mathbb{X}}\|_{L_{p}(\Omega,\mu)}
.
In our situation, we recall the classical identities:

\displaystyle L_{1}(\Omega,\mu;L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))=L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})=L_{1}(\Omega,\mu)\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}L_{1}(\mathcal{M});

\displaystyle L_{2}(\Omega,\mu;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))=L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})=L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)\otimes_{2}L_{2}(\mathcal{M}),

where
\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}
and
\otimes_{2}
are the Banach space
projective tensor product and the Hilbert space tensor product. On
the other hand, the identification of the von Neumann algebra

L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
as a space of
vector-valued functions requires a more subtle construction. In
general, a function
f:\Omega\longrightarrow\mathbb{X}^{*}
is
said to be
weak
∗

\mu
-measurable
if
J_{x}\circ f
is
measurable for each
x\in\mathbb{X}
, where
J_{x}
denotes the
continuous functional on
\mathbb{X}^{*}
given by

J_{x}(x^{*})=x^{*}(x)
for every
x^{*}
in
\mathbb{X}^{*}
. Consider

\mathbb{X}=L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
(which is assumed to be separable to avoid measurability issues) and define

L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu;\mathcal{M})
as the Banach space of all

\mathcal{M}
-valued weak
∗

\mu-
measurable functions which are
essentially bounded, that is,

\displaystyle{\mathrm{ess}\sup}_{t\in\Omega}\|f(t)\|_{\mathcal{M}}<\infty.

Then,
L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu;\mathcal{M})
is a von Neumann algebra under the pointwise multiplication and the map

\displaystyle f\otimes m\mapsto f(t)m,\quad f\in L_{\infty}(\Omega),\ m\in\mathcal{M},

can be extended to a weak
∗
isomorphism of
L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
onto
L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu;\mathcal{M})
.

Let
H
be a separable
Hilbert space.

first one. Moreover, elements of
H
viewed in the dual Hilbert
space
H^{*}
will be represented with an overlined letter. We mean
that
\overline{h}
will denote the continuous functional

\displaystyle\overline{h}:k\in H\mapsto\langle h,k\rangle\ \mathrm{for\ any\ }k\in H.

Given two elements
\xi
and
\eta
of
H
, we consider
the
rank-one operator

\xi\otimes\overline{\eta}
acting on
H
as

\displaystyle(\xi\otimes\overline{\eta})(h)=\langle\eta,h\rangle\ \xi\ \mathrm{for\ any\ }h\in H.

In the following, let
\mathds{1}
denote a fixed element of
H
with
\|\mathds{1}\|_{H}=1
, and let

-space

\displaystyle L_{p}(\mathcal{M};H^{c})=L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(H))(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}}).

for any

. Identify

as a subspace of

via the map
m\mapsto m\otimes p_{\mathds{1}}=(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}})(m\otimes\mathbf{1}_{B(H)})(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}})

\displaystyle L_{p}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}})L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(H))(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}}).

Then, given an element

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};H^{c})}=\|f\|_{\ L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(H))}=\|(f^{*}f)^{1/2}\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}

on

. Analogously, the

L_{p}
-space
is

\displaystyle L_{p}(\mathcal{M};H^{*r})=(\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{M}}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}})L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(H)).

Given elements

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};H^{c})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|g\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};H^{*r})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\Big(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle h_{i},h_{j}\rangle_{H}\ m_{i}m_{j}^{*}\Big)^{1/2}\Big\|_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M})}.

In the last statement, one needs a conjugation because the
∗
on the left is for the Banach space adjoint whereas the right one is the Hilbert space sense. Of course similarly statements hold for row spaces considering bounded maps
T:H^{*}\to K^{*}
.

1.1.
Noncommutative spaces
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{c}_{2}(\Omega))

Let
(\Omega,\mu)
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space. A remarkable setting for noncommutative Hilbert-valued column/row
L_{p}
-spaces is the case
H=L_{2}(\Omega):=L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. Notice that under these conditions, the duality bracket (
1
) is given by the expression

\displaystyle\langle m_{1}\otimes\overline{f}_{1},m_{2}\otimes f_{2}\rangle_{r,c}

\displaystyle=\tau_{\mathcal{M}}(m_{1}m_{2})\ \int_{\Omega}\overline{f_{1}}f_{2}\ d\mu.

In particular, identifying
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)^{*}
and
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
and using the bilinear pairing
(f,g)\mapsto\int_{\Omega}fg\ d\mu
, then

\displaystyle L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\Omega,\mu))=L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega,\mu))^{*}\ \mathrm{for\ }1\leq p<\infty.

This duality identity will allow us to drop the conjugation or the involution
*
from now on. Moreover, for

F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L_{2}(\Omega)
with
p<\infty
:

\|F\|^{p}_{L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega,\mu))}=\tau\Big(\int_{\Omega}\Big|\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(t)m_{i}\Big|^{2}\Big)^{p/2}

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(t)m_{i}
can be interpreted as
F(t)
, it is very tempting to consider elements
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
as functions. Indeed for
p\leq 2
(see
[
14
, Proposition 2.5]
):

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
1\leq p\leq 2
, the identity on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L_{2}(\Omega)

extends to an injective contraction
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))\to L_{2}(\Omega;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Proof.

First note that by definition

L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{2}L_{2}(\Omega)=L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))=L_{2}(\Omega;L_{2}(\mathcal{M})).

Let
p<2
and set
q
be so that

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q}
. Take

F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L_{2}(\Omega)\subset L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
, it
corresponds to an element of

L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(L_{2}(\Omega)))
. Its
modulus
|F|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}|F(t)|^{2}d\mu
falls into
L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})
, thus
using its polar decomposition it can be written as
F=ab
with

b\in L_{q}(\mathcal{M})
and

a\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
with

\|a\|_{2}\cdot\|b\|_{q}=\|F\|_{p}
. Actually
a
is also a simple function and

\int_{\Omega}\|F(t)\|_{p}^{2}d\mu\leq\|b\|_{q}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\|a(t)\|_{2}^{2}d\mu=\|F\|_{p}^{2}.

This shows that the identity is indeed a contraction and thus extends to a contraction
\iota
by density.

To show the injectivity, consider
m\in L_{q}(\mathcal{M})
and

h\in L_{2}(\Omega)
. The linear form associated to

m\otimes h
satisfies

\langle F,m\otimes h\rangle_{c,r}=\int_{\Omega}\tau(\iota(F)(t)m)h(t)d\mu
. Indeed, this is clear for

F\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\otimes_{2}L_{2}(\Omega)
, and for all
F
by density.
Since
L_{q}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L_{2}(\Omega)
is norm-dense in
L_{q}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\Omega))
, we can conclude.
∎

As a consequence, we can identify elements in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
with a.e. Bochner measurable functions from
\Omega
to
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})
when
1\leq p\leq 2
. This will be convenient for some identifications. Unfortunately, when
p>2
we have no way to consider elements in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
as functions. Indeed, in the following sections, the case
p=\infty
will be specially relevant. For that reason, the extension of some useful operators on
L_{2}(\Omega)
to
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\Omega))
will be carefully studied.

Lemma 1.4
.

Let
A,B
be two measurable sets, and let
w
and
w^{\prime}
be strictly positive functions belonging to
L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)
. Consider the following operators on
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
:

\displaystyle T_{w}

\displaystyle:f\mapsto w^{1/2}f,

\displaystyle P_{A}

\displaystyle:f\mapsto\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{A}f.

These maps extend to bounded operators on
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L^{t}_{2}(\Omega,\mu))
, for
t=c,r
and any
1\leq p\leq\infty
such that
\|\widetilde{T}_{w}\|=\|w\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)}
,
\|\widetilde{P}_{A}\|=\|\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{A}\|_{L_{\infty}(\Omega,\mu)}
. Moreover, they satisfy the following relations:

(1)

\widetilde{T}_{w}\widetilde{T}_{w^{\prime}}=\widetilde{T}_{w^{\prime}}\widetilde{T}_{w}
,

(2)

\widetilde{P}_{A}\widetilde{T}_{w}=\widetilde{T}_{w}\widetilde{P}_{A}
,

(3)

whenever
w^{-1}
is bounded,
\widetilde{T}_{w}\widetilde{T}_{w^{-1}}=\mathrm{Id}=\widetilde{T}_{w^{-1}}\widetilde{T}_{w}
.

(4)

\widetilde{P}_{A}=\widetilde{P}_{A}\widetilde{P}_{B}=\widetilde{P}_{B}\widetilde{P}_{A}
whenever
A\subseteq B
,

(5)

\widetilde{P}_{B\setminus A}=\widetilde{P}_{B}-\widetilde{P}_{A}
whenever
A\subseteq B
.

Proof.

By Corollary
1.1
, the extension operators
\widetilde{T}_{w}
,
\widetilde{P}_{A}
are bounded as long as the original ones are bounded on
L_{2}(\Omega,\mu)
. The maps
T_{w}
and
P_{A}
are bounded with norm
\|w^{1/2}\|_{L_{\infty}}
and
1
respectively, since they are pointwise multiplication operators. Claims (1)-(4) follow from Lemma
1.2
, while linearity of the map
T\mapsto\widetilde{T}
implies (5).
∎

2.
Duality between Hardy spaces and
\mathsf{BMO}
-spaces

Consider the measure space
(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
. Set
\omega(t)=1+t^{2}
. Then,
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle g,f\rangle_{1/\omega}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{g(t)}\ f(t)\ \frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}.

We will consider the associated column space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
for
0<p\leq\infty
. We will choose
\mathds{1}
to be the constant function
1/\sqrt{\pi}
, which satisfies the condition
\|\mathds{1}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})}=1
. For the sake of exposition, we define some operators on
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
which can be described in terms of the maps appearing in Lemma
1.4
. Let
A
be a measurable set with nonzero measure and define the map

R_{A}=\frac{1}{|A|}\widetilde{T}_{\omega}\widetilde{P}_{A}

so that
R_{A}
is the extension of the operator acting on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
as follows:

f\mapsto\frac{(1+t^{2})^{1/2}}{\sqrt{|A|}}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{A}f.

On the other hand, denote by
a_{A}
the extension to
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
of the map

a_{A}:f\mapsto f_{A}\ 1=\Big(\frac{1}{|A|}\int_{A}f\Big)\ 1.

Similarly the linear form
f\mapsto f_{A}
, has a weak
∗
extension
from
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
to

\mathcal{M}=L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\mathbb{C}^{c})
. Thus we may also use the notation
f_{A}
for
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
.

Some other Hilbert spaces over the real line will be considered through this work. Since the distinguished vectors are not relevant, we will always write them
\mathds{1}
in all spaces.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A
be a measurable set. Then, the pre-adjoint maps for
R_{A}
and
a_{A}
on
L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},dt/(1+t^{2})))
act as follows on any operator
m\otimes f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})\otimes L_{2}(\mathbb{R},dt/(1+t^{2}))

\displaystyle(R_{A})_{*}

\displaystyle:m\otimes f\longmapsto m\otimes\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}{\sqrt{|A|}}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}f

\displaystyle(a_{A})_{*}

\displaystyle:m\otimes f\longmapsto m\otimes\langle 1,f\rangle_{1/\omega}\frac{1+t^{2}}{|A|}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{A}.

Moreover, the identity operator

\begin{array}[]{cccc}V&:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))&\longmapsto&L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))\\
&m\otimes f&\longmapsto&m\otimes f,\end{array}

is contractive and admits a pre-adjoint

\begin{array}[]{cccc}V_{*}&:L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))&\longmapsto&L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))\\
&m\otimes f&\longmapsto&m\otimes\frac{f}{1+t^{2}}.\end{array}

Proof.

These are consequences of the same results on Hilbert spaces using Proposition
1.2
. One just need to care of conjugations which actually play no role here.
∎

Now, we are ready to define the column and row
\mathsf{BMO}
-spaces.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
with n.s.f. trace
\tau
, set the
column
\mathsf{BMO}
-space
, denoted as
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, to be the subspace of operators
f
in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
satisfying

(2)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}:=\sup_{I\subseteq\mathbb{R}}\big\|R_{I}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})f\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R};\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}<\infty,

where the supremum is considered over finite intervals
I
of
\mathbb{R}
. Likewise, the row
\mathsf{BMO}
-space,
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
, is the subspace of elements in
L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
for which the norm
\|f\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}:=\|f^{*}\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}
is finite.

It is clear that
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}
is a norm modulo

\mathcal{M}
. This expression will be convenient for abstract
questions. We point out that it admits a much more tractable form.
The multiplication by
\sqrt{1+t^{2}}
is just an isometry from

L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
. For a finite interval
I
,
let
\widetilde{\iota}_{I}
denote the extension of the map

f\mapsto\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}f
from
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, then clearly:

(3)

\displaystyle\Big\|R_{I}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})f\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}=\Big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\big(\widetilde{\iota}_{I}\big(f-(f_{I}\otimes 1)\big)\big)\Big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}

In particular, for operators in
\mathcal{M}\otimes L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
, we recover the expression which determines the definition for the
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}
-norm introduced in
[
18
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

For any operator
f
in
\mathcal{M}\otimes L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
it holds

\|f\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}=\sup_{I\subseteq\mathbb{R}}\Bigg\|\Bigg(\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}|f-f_{I}|^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Bigg\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

Along the next section, the study of the boundedness of Calderón-Zygmund operators on operator-valued Hardy spaces will require a concrete formulation in terms of atomic decompositions. In order to justify introducing these spaces, we will check that the dual of this new description of the column (resp. row) Hardy space coincides with
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
(resp.
\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra with n.s.f. trace. A
c
-atom
is a function
a\in L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
which admits a factorization of the form
a=bh
for some function
b:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and an norm-one operator
h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, satisfying

(1)

\mathrm{supp}_{\mathbb{R}}(b)\subseteq I
for some interval
I
,

(2)

\int_{I}b=0
,

(3)

\|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}
.

Then, the
column Hardy space

H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
is defined to be the subspace of elements in
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
of the form

(4)

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \mathrm{where\ }(\lambda_{i})_{i}\in\ell_{1}\ \mathrm{and}\ (a_{i})_{i}\ c\mbox{-}\mathrm{atoms}

with respect to the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|=\inf\{\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\ :\ f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \mathrm{in\ }L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}),\ (\lambda_{i})_{i}\in\ell_{1},\ (a_{i})_{i}\ c\mbox{-}\mathrm{atoms}\}.

Under the above definition, any
c
-atom satisfies

\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq 1

since, by the Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\|a\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\ \|h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq|I|^{-1/2}\ |I|^{1/2}=1.

Therefore,
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
is contractively contained into
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
.

We choose to give the explicit decomposition of atoms as
a=bh
rather than just saying
a\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
with norm less than
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}
and mean zero. We do so as this makes explicit the connection with vector valued harmonic analysis and will make all the proofs transparent. We leave the following to the reader:

Proposition 2.5
.

The column Hardy space
(H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A}),\|\cdot\|_{H_{1}^{c}})
is a Banach space.

Given a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
, let
L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})\ dt;\mathbb{X})
denote the subspace of functions
f
in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})\ dt;\mathbb{X})
satisfying

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(t)\ dt=0.

Then, the classical argument by Meyer
[
20
, Chapter 5, Proposition 1]
extends to the Banach-valued setting yielding the inclusion as a subspace of
L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})\ dt;\mathbb{X})
into the vector-valued Hardy space
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})

[
13
]
. More clearly, given
f\in L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
, there exists a sequence of atoms
(b_{i})_{i}\subseteq H_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
and
(\lambda_{i})_{i}\in\ell_{1}
such that

(5)

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}b_{i}\ \mathrm{in}\ L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\ \mathrm{and}\ \sum_{i=0}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}.

Since any
c
-atom
a=bh
is the product of an
L_{2}
-atom
b
in
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
and an element
h
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, the argument by Meyer still works in the semicommutative case.

Proposition 2.6
.

The formal identity map

L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt)\otimes L_{1}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{1}(\mathbb{R})\otimes L_{1}(\mathcal{M})

extends to an injective and
contractive map
Q:L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L^{\circ,c}_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))\to H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

First note that simple tensors

f\otimes m\in L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt)\otimes L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
are both in

H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
and in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R})\otimes L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. Indeed by
Meyer’s result in the scalar case,
f=\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
where

a_{i}
are scalar valued atoms. Choose

\alpha,\beta\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
with
m=\alpha\beta
and

\|\alpha\|_{2}=1
. Then
a_{i}\otimes\alpha
are atoms in

H_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
, so
a_{i}\otimes m
is a
multiple of a
c
-atom and
f\otimes m
is in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
.

The end of the argument is as in Proposition
1.3
. Any
simple

x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}\otimes m_{i}\in L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt)\otimes L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
can be
written as
x=F\beta
, with
\beta\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and a
simple tensor

F\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L^{\circ,c}_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))=L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
such that

\|F\|_{2}\cdot\|\beta\|_{2}=\|x\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L^{\circ,c}_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))}
. Using Meyers’
decomposition (
5
) for
F
gives the norm estimate. One
concludes to the boundedness of the extension by density. The injectivity follows by the weak
∗
density of simple tensors in the dual spaces as in
1.3
.
∎

Consequently any linear form on
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
induces another one on

L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L^{\circ,c}_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))
. Precomposing it
with the map
V_{*}
of Lemma
2.1
(which obviously
has dense range), this allows us to represent
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}
as a subspace of

L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L^{\circ,c}_{2}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))^{*}\subset L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Given a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, we have a contractive inclusion

H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}\subseteq\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M}).

Proof.

Let
g\in H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}
, we already explained that
VQ^{*}(g)\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))
. We estimate its
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}
norm,

\displaystyle\|VQ^{*}g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{I\subseteq\mathbb{R}}\|R_{I}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})(VQ^{*}g)\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{I}\sup_{f}|\langle f,R_{I}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})(VQ^{*}g)\rangle|

where the supremum is taken over
f
in the unit ball of
L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
.
We can as well take it on simple tensors by density (we could
as well consider it as a function with values in

L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
). Moreover by the factorization argument
of Proposition
1.3
, we can even write it as

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}h\otimes f_{i}
with

f_{i}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
,

m_{i},h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
such that

\|h\|_{2}=\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}=1
.
Using the
formulas of Lemma
2.1
,

QV_{*}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})_{*}R_{I*}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}h\otimes F_{i}

where for
i=1,...,n

\displaystyle F_{i}=\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}}{\sqrt{|I|}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}f_{i}

\displaystyle-\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}}{|I|}\langle 1,\frac{\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}{\sqrt{|I|}}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}f_{i}\rangle_{1/\omega}\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt).

Set
b=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes F_{i}\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt))
. Then, clearly
\mbox{supp}_{\mathbb{R}}(b)\subseteq I
,
\int_{I}b=0
since
\int_{I}F_{i}=0
. Let
G=\frac{\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}}{\sqrt{|I|}\sqrt{1+t^{2}}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}
. Using that
b
is the projection of
G
onto the orthogonal of constant functions on
I
, one gets

\Big(\int_{I}\big\|b(t)\big\|_{2}^{2}\ dt\Big)^{1/2}\leq\big\|G\big\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}\Big\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}.

In other words,
QV_{*}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})_{*}R_{I*}(f)
is a
c
-atom, hence

\|VQ^{*}g\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}\leq\|g\|_{H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}}.

∎

The reverse inclusion
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})\subseteq H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}
is more involved. We need to check that every operator in
\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
induces a continuous functional on
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
. The starting point of our argument is that every operator
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
induces a functional on the algebraic vector space
\mathcal{H}
generated by the
c
-atoms in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})\subset L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Recall that
L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},dt))
can be also
be interpreted as a space of functions (defined a.e.) with values
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. By definition, as a function any
c
-atom

a
is the pointwise product of a compactly supported function in

L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
with a constant element

h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, thus it is an element in

L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},dt))
and moreover

\|a\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},dt))}\leq 1
. To
emphasize, we will denote the inclusion by

\gamma:\mathcal{H}\to L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},dt))
. The arguments we just
gave also say that it is continuous for the norm on
\mathcal{H}

given by

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\inf\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\lambda_{i}|\;:\;f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\;,N\geq 1,\;\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}\textrm{ and }a_{i}\;c\textrm{-atoms}\}.

We will denote by
A_{g}
the pointwise multiplication by
g
on
a.e. functions with values in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
or

L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
. It is well defined on all elements that
have compact support in the spaces we consider. The same kind
arguments justify that

A_{\omega}(a)\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))

and

A_{\omega}:\mathcal{H}\to L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))
is of course
linear.
A_{\omega}
is not continuous but if
a
is an atom supported on
I

then
A_{\omega}(a)=\widetilde{M}_{\omega}^{I}(\gamma(a))
, where
M_{\omega}^{I}:L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L_{2}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt)
is the multiplication by
\omega\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}
(which is bounded).

This allows us to define a duality pairing for
\mathcal{H}
and

\mathsf{BMO}_{r}
by
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
,
f\in\mathcal{H}
:

\langle\varphi,f\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}=\langle\varphi,A_{\omega}(f)\rangle_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})),L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}.

This definition is made so that if

\varphi=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}\otimes f_{i}\in\mathcal{M}\otimes L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})
then

\langle\varphi,f\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\tau(f(t)m_{i})f_{i}(t)dt.

So, we recover the classical duality pairing for functions.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
. If
a
is a
c
-atom in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
, there holds

\displaystyle|\langle\varphi,a\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}|\leq\ \|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}.

Proof.

Let
I
be the interval for which
a=bh
satisfies the
definition of a
c
-atom. Then
A_{\omega}(a)=|I|(R_{I*})^{2}(a)
and
the mean zero condition exactly means that
a_{I*}(A_{\omega}(a))=0
. Thus, there holds

\langle\varphi,a\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}=\langle R_{I}(\mathrm{Id}-a_{I})\varphi,|I|R_{I*}(a)\rangle_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})),L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}.

It remains to show that
\||I|R_{I*}(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}\leq 1
, but this comes from the factorization property in terms of operators

|I|R_{I*}(a)=(\sqrt{|I|}A_{\sqrt{\omega}}(b))\cdot h

where
\|\sqrt{|I|}A_{\sqrt{\omega}}(b)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}=\sqrt{|I|}\ \|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},{dt}))}\leq 1

and
h
is a norm-one operator.
∎

The second step in the proof is to extend the duality pairing to
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
. This will require some care and approximations.

Given a compactly supported continuous function
\xi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we denote by
R_{\xi}
the convolution on

L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
against
\xi
. We could consider its
extension
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}
to any
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
. On the other hand, using vector-valued integration, as the convolution against
\xi
in
L_{p}(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}};L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
is feasible; we denote it by
\xi*x
. When
p=2
, we have
\widetilde{R}_{\xi}(x)=\xi*x
using the identification
L_{2}(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))=L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
. Moreover, we consider here a third possible extension which will be used along this section.

Lemma 2.9
.

Assume
\xi
is a compactly supported continuous function on
\mathbb{R}
. Given any
f\in L_{1}(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
, then
\xi*f\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
(viewed as a function
space). Moreover, this induces a continuous linear map

C_{\xi}:L_{1}(\mathbb{\mathbb{R}};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))\to L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
.

Proof.

Recall that
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
is the
projective tensor product

L_{1}(\mathbb{R})\widehat{\otimes}_{\pi}L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. It
suffices to prove the statement for a simple tensor

f=g\otimes m
with
g\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R})
and

m\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. Then the function
\xi*f

corresponds to the operator

m\otimes(\xi*g)\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))
. Factorizing
m=rs
with
r,s\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and

\|r\|_{2}=\|s\|_{2}=\|m\|_{1}^{1/2}
, we obtain a factorization in
term of operators
m\otimes(\xi*g)=(r\otimes(\xi*g))s

so that
\|r\otimes(\xi*g)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}=\|r\|_{2}\|\xi*g\|_{2}
. Thus, we can conclude due to the Young inequality that

\|m\otimes(\xi*g)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\|r\|_{2}\|s\|_{2}\|\xi*g\|_{2}\leq\|m\|_{1}\|g\|_{1}\|\xi\|_{2}.

We get that the norm of
C_{\xi}
is controlled by
\|\xi\|_{2}
.
∎

Let fix some non-negative even continuous function
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
with support in
(-1,1)
such that
\int_{\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}}\phi(t)dt=1
and denote
\phi_{n}(x)=n\phi(nx)
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Given a
c
-atom
a=bh
supported on
I
, the following holds:

(1)

\frac{1}{2}\phi_{n}*a
is a finite convex combination of
c
-atoms

(2)

If
n\geq\frac{2}{|I|}
,
\phi_{n}*a-a=\lambda_{n}a_{n}
,
where
a_{n}
is a
c
-atom supported on
2I
,
\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
with

\lim_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}=0
.

(3)

We have
\gamma(\phi_{n}*a)=C_{\phi_{n}}(a)
for all
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

For (1), cut
(-\frac{1}{n},\frac{1}{n})
into

N
disjoint intervals
K_{j}^{n}
whose lengths are less than
|I|
(we
can have
N=1
) and consider
b_{j}^{n}=(\phi_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{j}^{n}})*b
. For
all
j
,
b_{j}^{n}
is supported on an interval of length
2|I|
, has mean 0 and
L_{2}
-norm less than

\frac{2\|\phi_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{j}^{n}}\|_{1}}{\sqrt{|I|}}
. Thus
b_{j}^{n}h
is
an atom up to a factor
2\|\phi_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{j}^{n}}\|_{1}
and

\phi_{n}*a=\sum_{j=1}^{N}b_{j}^{n}h
. But there holds

\sum_{j=1}^{N}2\|\phi_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{j}^{n}}\|_{1}=2
, it yields (1).

Similarly, for
(2)
,
\phi_{n}*b-b
has support in
2I
with mean

0
. Its
L_{2}
-norm goes to
0
giving the result.

There are many ways to check (3). One can identify them as
a.e. functions from
\mathbb{R}
to
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. Another one is to
note that point (1) implies that the convolution by
\phi_{n}
is
continuous for
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Moreover, atoms of the form

a=bh
with
b
a
L_{2}
-atom which is a simple tensor in

L_{2}(\mathbb{R})\otimes L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
and

h\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
are dense in
\mathcal{H}
for

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. On such atoms, the formula is obvious
and then extends by continuity.
∎

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
\psi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
satisfying

\widetilde{P}_{J}\psi=\psi
for some finite interval

J
. Then for any family of atoms
(a_{i})
and

(\lambda_{i})\in\ell_{1}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}=0
in
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
there holds

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\ \langle\psi,a_{i}\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}=0.

Proof.

First, by Lemma
2.10
,
\phi_{n}*a_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
for all
n,i\geq 1
. We come back to the definition

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\phi_{n}*a_{i}\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\widetilde{P}_{J}\psi,\widetilde{P}_{J}A_{\omega}(\phi_{n}*a_{i})\rangle_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}})),L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))}.

Using the projection
\widetilde{P}_{J}
, we get

\widetilde{P}_{J}A_{\omega}(\phi_{n}*a_{i})=\widetilde{P}_{J}\widetilde{M}_{\omega}^{J}\gamma(\phi_{n}*a_{i})
and since
\gamma(\phi_{n}*a_{i})=C_{\phi_{n}}(a_{i})
:

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\phi_{n}*a_{i}\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r},\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle(\widetilde{M}_{\omega}^{J})^{*}\psi,C_{\phi_{n}}(a_{i})\rangle_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R})),L_{1}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle C_{\phi_{n}}^{*}(\widetilde{M}_{\omega}^{J})^{*}\psi,a_{i}\rangle_{\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),L_{1}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}))}.

We can conclude as
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}=0
in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
that
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\psi,\phi_{n}*a\rangle=0
.

Next, by Lemma
2.8
, the duality
pairing is continuous for the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}}
. Lemma

2.10
(1) and (2) mean that

\|\phi_{n}*a_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\leq 2\|a_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}
and

\|\phi_{n}*a_{i}-a_{i}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. We can conclude to
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\psi,a_{i}\rangle=0
by the Lebesgue Theorem.
∎

In order to conclude the argument, it remains to show that any
operator
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
induces a
well-defined functional on
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
. The estimates obtained for
compactly supported
\psi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
will
yield a general result under another suitable approximation
argument from Garnett’s book
[
9
, p. 261]
as stated in
[
18
, Lemma 1.5]

in the semi-commutative case. See Appendix
B
for its proof.

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
and suppose
J
is an interval such that
\varphi_{J}=0
. Let
3J
be the interval concentric with
J
having length
3|J|
. Then there exist some universal
C>0
,
\psi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{c}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
such that

\widetilde{P}_{3J}\psi=\psi,\qquad\widetilde{P}_{J}(\psi-\varphi)=0,\qquad\|\psi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}\leq C\ \|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}.

Theorem 2.13
.

Given a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{M}
, there is a contractive inclusion

\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})\subseteq H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})^{*}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
and let

f\in H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
admit an atomic decomposition

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
. We want to define the duality pairing by

\langle\varphi,f\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\varphi,a_{i}\rangle_{\mathsf{BMO},\mathcal{H}}.

We start by showing that this well defined algebraically, that
is if
f=0
in

L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
then
the right hand side is also 0.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, let
N\geq 1
such that

\sum_{i>N}|\lambda_{i}|<\varepsilon
and let
J
be a finite
interval satisfying for
i=1,...,N
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{i})\subseteq J.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\varphi_{J}=0
, so by Lemma
2.12
, there exists some
\psi\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
satisfying
\widetilde{P}_{3J}\psi=\psi
,
\widetilde{P}_{J}(\varphi-\psi)=0
and

\|\psi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}

for some universal constant
C>0
. Therefore, as a consequence of Proposition
2.11
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\varphi,a_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\varphi,a_{i}\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\langle\psi,a_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{i}\ \langle\varphi-\psi,a_{i}\rangle+\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\ \langle\varphi-\psi,a_{i}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\,\langle\varphi-\psi,a_{i}\rangle.

We get to

\displaystyle|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\ \langle\varphi,a_{i}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=N+1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|(1+C)\|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}<(1+C)\varepsilon\|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}}.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrarily small, the duality pairing is well defined. To conclude the norm estimate, one just need to use Lemma
2.8
.
∎

This last theorem completes the proof of the isometric identity

\displaystyle(H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A}))^{*}=\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M}).

Therefore, a new description for a predual of

\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
has been obtained just in terms of
a new atomic decomposition, which will be crucial for the study of
the boundedness of Calderón-Zygmund operators from
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
to

L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
.

We end this section by briefly mentioning that there is another approach
to define the duality bracket
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})-\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
.
One can easily show that there is a natural isometric embedding

\pi:L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{r}(\mathbb{R},1/(1+t^{2})dt))\subset B\big(L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}\otimes_{2}L_{2}(\mathcal{M})),L_{2}(\mathcal{M})\big).

Then for an atom
a=bh
and
f\in\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})
,
one can set
\langle a,f\rangle=\tau(\pi(f)(b)h)
, without regard to the decomposition of
a
. Then, one can rely on Hilbert valued
H_{1}
and
\mathsf{BMO}
noting that
\|f\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{r}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{\|h\|_{2}\leq 1}\|\pi(f)^{*}(h)\|_{\mathsf{BMO}(\mathbb{R},L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}
. Nevertheless, one
still need all the above approximation arguments.

3.
Calderón-Zygmund operators with operator-valued kernels

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra over a separable Hilbert space. Through this section we establish the conditions under which a kernel
K
, defined outside the diagonal with values in
\mathcal{M}
, will induce a Calderón-Zygmund operator from
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
into
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
. As one could expect, the resulting operator will be defined up to a pointwise multiplication operator. Recall the identification

L_{\infty}(I\times J)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}=L_{\infty}(I\times J;\mathcal{M})
where we consider weak
∗
-measurable
\mathcal{M}
-valued functions.

Recall that we denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of compactly supported essentially bounded functions
\mathbb{R}\to L_{\infty}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
that are measurable with values in
L_{1}
. Note that
B_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}\cap\mathcal{S}
is norm-dense in
B_{L_{p}(\mathcal{A})}
when
1\leq p<\infty
and weak
∗
-dense for
p=\infty
. Indeed
if
f\in L_{p}(\mathcal{A})
with polar decomposition
f=u|f|
, take
p_{n}
a sequence of finite projections strongly going to 1 in
\mathcal{M}
and consider

f_{n}=u\max\{|f|,n\}\ p_{n}\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{[-n;n]}\in\mathcal{S}
. It is clear that
\|f_{n}\|_{p}\leq\|f\|_{p}
and
f_{n}\to f
in norm in
L_{p}
(weak
∗
-convergence if
p=\infty
).

Definition 3.1
.

Assume that
T
is a bounded operator on
L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R}){\overline{\otimes}}\mathcal{M})
. We say that
T
is a
left Calderón-Zygmund operator
if there exists some function

\displaystyle K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\ \setminus\ \{x=y\}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}

such that for every pair of intervals
I,J
satisfying
d(I,J)>0
, there exist
K_{I,J}\in L_{\infty}(I\times J)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
such that

•

{K}_{I,J}(t)=K(t)
for almost every
t\in I\times J
,

•

for any
f,g\in\mathcal{S}
supported respectively on
J
and
I
:

(6)

\displaystyle\Big(\tau\circ\int\Big)(g\ Tf)=\langle f(y)g(x),K_{I,J}\rangle_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(I\times J)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}),L_{\infty}(I\times J)\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}}

We say that
T
is a
left Calderón-Zygmund operator
if it also satisfies that for any compactly supported
f\in L_{2}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
and
h\in\mathcal{M}
:

\displaystyle T(fh)=T(f)h.

Let
\lambda>0
, we say that the kernel
K
(or
T
) satisfies the
Hörmander condition
for
\lambda
whenever

(7)

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y^{\prime}|\geq\lambda|y-y^{\prime}|}\|K(x,y^{\prime})-K(x,y)\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ dx\leq C_{\lambda}

for some constant
C_{\lambda}\geq 0
.

We choose
\mathcal{S}
in order to have a perfectly defined duality in (
6
) as
f(y)g(x)\in L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
. Moreover, the space
\mathcal{S}
is an algebra for the pointwise product. We could also have chosen to state (
6
) for
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
instead of
\mathcal{S}
without significant modifications.

The following argument constitutes our main estimate it can be considered as an arrangement of the scalar case to the semicommutative setting.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
T
be a left Calderón-Zygmund operator which is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
and has associated kernel

K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\ \setminus\ \{x=y\}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}.

If
K
satisfies the Hörmander condition for some
\lambda\geq 1
and moreover

\displaystyle T(a)=T(b)h\ \textrm{\rm for any $c$-atom}\ a=bh,

then there holds

\displaystyle\|T(a)\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\max\{C_{\lambda},\lambda^{1/2}\|T\|\}.

Proof.

Let
a=bh
be a
c
-atom in
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
with support contained in the interval
I=[y_{0}-d,y_{0}+d]
and let
\lambda I=[y_{0}-\lambda d,y_{0}+\lambda d]
. Then, the norm

\displaystyle\|T(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}=\int_{\lambda I}\|T(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}+\int_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\|T(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}

can be bounded in two steps. First, the continuity of
T
on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
implies that

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda I}\|T(a)

\displaystyle\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}=\int_{\lambda I}\|T(b)h\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\leq\Big(\int_{\lambda I}\|T(b)\|^{2}_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\Big)^{1/2}\ \Big(\int_{\lambda I}\|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|T(b)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\ |\lambda I|^{1/2}\|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\|T:L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\|\ \|b\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}\ |\lambda I|^{1/2}\ \|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{1/2}\ \|T:L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\rightarrow L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\|.

To deal with the second term, we have

\|T(a)\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\|_{1}=\|T(b)h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\|_{1}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\mathcal{S},\|g\|_{\infty}\leq 1\\
\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\lambda I)^{c}}g=g\end{subarray}}\big|\tau\int T(b)hg\big|.

Assume for the moment that
b\in\mathcal{S}
and
h\in L_{\infty}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
. Let
y_{0}
be the center of
I
. Since
I
and
(\lambda I)^{c}
are disjoint measurable sets and
b
has integral zero, it follows using (
6
) and the Hörmander condition that

\displaystyle\|T(a)

\displaystyle\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\|_{1}\leq\sup_{g}\big|\tau\int_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\int_{I}K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y)\ b(y)hg(x)\ dxdy\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{g}\big|\tau\int_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\int_{I}(K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y)-K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y_{0}))\ b(y)hg(x)\ dx\ dy\big|

\displaystyle=\big\|\int_{I}(K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y)-K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y_{0}))\ b(y)h\ dy\big\|_{L_{1}((\lambda I)^{c};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\int_{I}\|(K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y)-K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y_{0}))\ b(y)h\|_{L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}\ dx\ dy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\int_{I}\|(K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y)-K_{(\lambda I)^{c},I}(x,y_{0}))\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ \|b(y)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ \|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ dx\ dy

\displaystyle\leq C_{\lambda}\ \int_{I}\|b(y)\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ \|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ dy\leq C_{\lambda}\|b\|_{2}\ \|h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\|_{2}\leq C_{\lambda}.

The general case follows by approximation. Indeed let

b_{n}\in\mathcal{S}
going to
b
with
\|b_{n}\|_{2}\leq\|b\|_{2}
as
explained above and
h_{n}\in L_{\infty}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{M})

going to
h
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
with

\|h_{n}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq 1
. Centering
b_{n}
on
I

decreases its
L_{2}
-norm, so we can as well assume it has mean
0. Let
J_{k}=[-k,k]\cap(\lambda I)^{c}
. Then for any
k\geq 1
,

T(b_{n})h_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{J_{k}}\to T(b)h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{J_{k}}
in
L_{1}
as

n\to\infty
by the
L_{2}
-continuity of
T
. It follows that

\|T(b)h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{J_{k}}\|_{1}\leq\limsup_{n}\|T(b_{n})h_{n}\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{J_{k}}\|_{1}\leq C_{\lambda}
. Letting

gives
\|T(b)h\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\lambda I)^{c}}\|_{1}\leq C_{\lambda}
.
∎

In order to extend a left Calderón-Zygmund operator to the whole Hardy space

]
. A family of bounded kernels will be constructed, yielding a bounded family of Calderón-Zygmund operators that will approximate the original operator.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be a left Calderón-Zygmund operator whose associated

satisfies the Hörmander condition (for
\lambda=2
)
(

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y^{\prime}|\geq 4|y-y^{\prime}|}\|K_{m}(x,y^{\prime})-K_{m}(x,y)\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ dx\leq C^{\prime}.

Let

be an even smooth function supported on

, and let

be the operator defined on

where
\phi_{m}(x)=m\phi(mx)
. We define the function
K_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathcal{M}
by duality. Assume

z\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
is decomposed as
z=ab
with
a,b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
, and define the quantity

(9)

\displaystyle\langle K_{m}(x,y),z\rangle_{\mathcal{M},L_{1}(\mathcal{M})}=\int\tau\big(T((\tau_{y}\phi_{m})(t)a)\tau_{x}({\phi}_{m})(t)b\big)dt,

a

Linearity is proved in a similar way. We also get

\displaystyle\|K_{m}(x,y)\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}{a,b\in L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\\
\|a\|_{2}=\|b\|_{2}=1\end{subarray}}|(\int\circ\tau)\big(T(\tau_{y}\phi_{m}a)\ \tau_{x}{\phi}_{m}b\big)|

The formula extends to

by continuity since

is bounded on the whole

is the kernel for

in the sense of Definition
3.1
. Moreover,

is a continuous function

by continuity of translations on

as

\displaystyle\|K_{m}(x,y)-K_{m}(x,y^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{M}}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\ \|\phi_{m}\|_{2}\ \|(\tau_{y}-\tau_{y^{\prime}})\phi_{m}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\ m^{1/2}\ \|\phi\|_{2}\ \big(m^{3/2}{|y-y^{\prime}|}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\big)

(10)

\displaystyle=m^{2}{|y-y^{\prime}|}\|T\|\|\phi\|_{2}\|\phi^{\prime}\|_{\infty}

A similarly estimate holds for
\|K_{m}(x,y)-K_{m}(x^{\prime},y)\|_{\mathcal{M}}
.

On the other hand, the kernel

satisfies the Hörmander condition for
\lambda=4
. First, consider
x,y,y^{\prime}
such that
|x-y|\geq 4|y^{\prime}-y|

and

. Then, the support of

is

and
\tau_{y^{\prime}}\phi_{m}

and

9

for

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\geq 4|y^{\prime}-y|\vee\frac{4}{m}}\|K_{m}(x,y)-K_{m}(x,y^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|x-y|\geq 4|y^{\prime}-y|\vee\frac{4}{m}}\int\int\big\|{K}(x-u,y-{v})-{K}(x-{u},y^{\prime}-{v})\big\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ {\phi_{m}}(u)\phi_{m}(v)\ du\ dv\ dx.

\int_{|x-y|\geq 4|y^{\prime}-y|\vee\frac{4}{m}}\|K_{m}(x,y)-K_{m}(x,y^{\prime})\|_{\mathcal{M}}\ dx\leq C.

On the other hand, whenever

satisfies condition (

\displaystyle\|T_{m}(f)-T(f)\|_{L_{2}}

Before proving that a left Calderón-Zygmund operator extends to a bounded map from
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
into
L_{1}(\mathcal{A})
, a fundamental property is included.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra and let
T
be a left Calderón-Zygmund operator which is bounded on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
whose kernel is zero. Then
T
corresponds to the left multiplication by some operator
F\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
such that
\mbox{supp}\|f\|_{2}
is compact and contained in some interval
J
. Then, we claim that for any
g,g^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}
there holds

(11)

\displaystyle\langle g^{\prime},T(gf)\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}=\langle g^{\prime},T(g)f\rangle_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}.

As a consequence of Definition
3.1
, given
m\in\mathcal{M}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{M})
and
I
an arbitrary interval of positive length and
\lambda<1
, it holds

\displaystyle\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes 1),T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes m))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g(y)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes m)(y)g^{\prime}(x)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes 1)(x),K_{(\overline{I})^{c},\lambda I}\rangle=0,

\displaystyle\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1),T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes m))\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle g(y)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes m)(y)g^{\prime}(x)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1)(x),K_{\lambda I,(\overline{I})^{c}}\rangle=0.

It follows that

\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1),T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I^{c}}\otimes m))\rangle=\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1),(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes 1)T(g(1\otimes m))\rangle.

Using modularity, we get

\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1),T(g)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m)\rangle=\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\lambda I}\otimes 1),T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m))\rangle.

By continuity, this also holds for
\lambda=1
and similarly

\displaystyle\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes 1),T(g)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m)\rangle

\displaystyle=0=\langle g^{\prime}(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{(\overline{I})^{c}}\otimes 1),T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m))\rangle,

yielding

\langle g^{\prime},T(g)(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m)\rangle=\langle g^{\prime},T(g(\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m))\rangle
. By continuity of
T
, this can be extended to any
g,g^{\prime}\in L_{2}
. And finally, this identity also remains valid when replacing

\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{I}\otimes m
by any
f
by weak
∗
-density of elementary tensors (and Kaplansky’s theorem) and by weak
∗
continuity of all involved maps. Therefore, claim (
11
) follows.

We have shown that
T
commutes with right multiplications by
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
, thus it has to be a left multiplication by an element in
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
as the algebra
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
acts in standard form on
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))
.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a von Neumann algebra. Let
T
be a left Calderón-Zygmund operator with associated kernel
K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\setminus\{x=y\}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
. If
K
satisfies Hörmander condition (
7
), then
T
extends to a bounded operator from
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
into
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))
.

Proof.

First the operator
T
can be defined on
c
-atoms. This is
where we use the modularity. Indeed, given a
c
-atom with
decomposition
a=bh
supported on
I
,
T(b)h
is well defined
in
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})

but it may depend on the
decomposition. Let us justify that it does not. First, for any

m\geq 1
, since
K_{m}(x,\cdot)
belongs to

L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
(actually it is continuous), we have the formula

\displaystyle T_{m}(a)(x)=\int K_{m}(x,y)\ b(y)h\ dy=\int K_{m}(x,y)\ b(y)\ dt\cdot h=T_{m}(b)(x)\cdot h.

Consider another decomposition
a=b^{\prime}h^{\prime}
supported on
J
. Then, we have
T_{m}(b)h=T_{m}(b^{\prime})h^{\prime}
in
L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})
. Thus,
T(a)
can indeed be defined as
T(a)=T(b)h=T(b^{\prime})h^{\prime}
since the norm of the difference

\displaystyle\|T(b)h-

\displaystyle T(b^{\prime})h^{\prime}\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq\|T(b)h-T_{m}(b)h\|_{L_{1}}+\|T_{m}(b^{\prime})h^{\prime}-T(b^{\prime})h^{\prime}\|_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{|I|}\ \|h\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ \|T(b)-T_{m}(b)\|_{L_{2}}+\sqrt{|J|}\ \|h^{\prime}\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M})}\ \|T(b^{\prime})-T_{m}(b^{\prime})\|_{L_{2}}

goes to zero as
m\to\infty
by Lemma
3.3
.

In particular
T
and
T_{m}
satisfy the assumptions of Lemma
3.2
and we can conclude that for some universal
C\geq 0
(due to (
8
)):

(12)

\displaystyle\|T(a)-T_{m}(a)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}\leq C.

Moreover,
R_{m}a=\phi_{m}*a
and
\phi_{m}*a-a
are multiples of
c
-atoms by Lemma
2.10
and
\|\phi_{m}*a-a\|_{H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
. Thus

\displaystyle\|T(a)-T_{m}(a)\|_{L_{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|R_{m}TR_{m}(a)-R_{m}T(a)\|_{L_{1}}+\|R_{m}T(a)-T(a)\|_{L_{1}}

(13)

\displaystyle\leq\|T(R_{m}a-a)\|_{L_{1}}+\|(R_{m}-\mathrm{Id})Ta\|_{L_{1}},

which goes to
0
with
m\to\infty
.

In order to define
T
on
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
, assume
f
is decomposed as

f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}
where
a_{i}
’s are

c
-atoms and
(\lambda_{i})\in\ell_{1}
. Then, we check that

T(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}T(a_{i})\in L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})

does not depend on the decomposition. Indeed, assume that
f=0
. Then, the extension above is well defined and

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}T_{m}(a_{i})(x)=\int K_{m}(x,y)\ \sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}(y)\ dy=0

holds since
K_{m}(x,\cdot)
belongs to

L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
. Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\ T(a_{i})\Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}\ (T(a_{i})-T_{m}(a_{i}))\ \Big\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\ \|T(a_{i})-T_{m}(a_{i})\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\ \|T(a_{i})-T_{m}(a_{i})\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{1}(\mathcal{M}))}.

The former series tends to
0
as
m\to\infty
as consequence of (
12
) and (
13
). The boundedness of
T
is then clear as a consequence of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We chose to write the results for Calderón-Zygmund defined from

into itself. Actually, they extend without any modification to maps from

to

with
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
-valued kernels as long as

is an inclusion of von Neumann algebras with
\widehat{\tau}_{|\mathcal{M}}=\tau
and

has the right modular property with respect to
\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.7
.

As we already mentioned in the Introduction, all of our results hold
for functions in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. They also extend to
more general measure metric spaces so long as the underlying measure
is doubling, that is, if the condition

\mu(B(x,2r))\leq C_{\mu}\mu(B(x,r),

holds for all
x\in\mathrm{supp}(\mu)
and all
r>0
and that one has suitable approximations (such as in Lemma
2.12
or
3.3
). When
\mu
is
a measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
which fails the doubling condition the
definition of the appropriate
\mathsf{BMO}
-type space and a predual is more
involved and due to Tolsa in the classical case
[
26
]
. A
semicommutative definition in that context can be found in

[
4
]
. We let the interested reader to try to carry on all our

4.
An example

We sketch how to use Theorem
3.5
to
recover some results in
[
18
]
. A similar approach was used in

[
27
]
to obtain characterizations of some Hardy spaces relying on
column-Hilbert spaces Calderón-Zygmund kernels, and indeed constitutes a particular case of our work.

Let
(\mathcal{N},\tau)
be a semifinite von Neumann algebra and consider

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\overline{\otimes}B(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt)^{2})

with its natural tensor product trace. We fix
\mathds{1}
a function of norm 1 in
L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt)^{2}
.

Let
P(x,y)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{y}{x^{2}+y^{2}}
be the Poisson kernel for the
upper half plane. We can define a continuous convolution kernel

K:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\ \setminus\ \{x=y\}\to\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\overline{\otimes}B(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt)^{2})
by
K(x,v)=\mathcal{K}(x-v)
where
\mathcal{K}
is defined as

\mathcal{K}(x)=1_{\mathcal{N}}\otimes\Big(\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}P(x,\cdot),\frac{\partial}{\partial y}P(x,\cdot)\big)\otimes\mathds{1}\Big)

for
x\neq 0
. The map
\mathcal{K}
is continuous and bounded on each closed interval not
containing 0. Since
\mathcal{N}
does not play any role,
\mathcal{K}

actually takes values in a column Hilbert space. It is a standard fact that it satisfies the Mikhlin condition. All this implies that
K
satisfies the Hörmander condition.

The Calderón-Zygmund operator
T
associated to
K
can be defined. At
the
L_{2}
-level, it acts only on a column subspace of the
S_{2}(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt)^{2})
component. Thus, by homogeneity of hilbertian operator spaces, we deduce
that it is bounded from the classical result with values in Hilbert spaces (i.e. going from
L_{2}(\mathbb{R})
to
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt))
). Since
T
is right-modular with respect to

\mathcal{N}
inside the tensor algebra
\mathcal{N}\overline{\otimes}B(L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},t\ dt)^{2})

(with
n\mapsto n\otimes p_{\mathds{1}}
which preserves the traces), we indeed have a left Calderón-Zygmund operator from
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N}
to
L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M}
by Remark
3.6
.

Let
f
be a compactly supported simple function on
\mathbb{R}
with values
in
\mathcal{N}\cap L_{1}(\mathcal{N})
. If
F

denotes the harmonic extension of
f
to the upper half plane, the
column Littlewood-Paley function associated to
f
is

G_{c}(f)(x)=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}|\nabla F(x,y)|^{2}y\ dy\Big)^{1/2}.

We can see
f
as a function
with values in
\mathcal{M}
by considering

f\otimes(\mathds{1}\otimes\mathds{1})
as we already did. Some easy computations yield

\big|T(f\otimes p_{\mathds{1}})\big|^{2}=G_{c}(f)^{2}\otimes p_{\mathds{1}}.

After giving suitable definitions for all the objects, Theorem
3.5
yields that there is a constant
C
so that there holds

(14)

\|G_{c}(f)\|_{L_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{M})}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{c}_{1}(L_{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{N})}.

for every
f\in H^{c}_{1}(\mathcal{A})
. In the same way, one can also get (
14
) with the Lusin square function
S_{c}(f)
instead of the Littlewood-Paley one.

Consequently our definition for
H_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{A})
matches that of
[
18
]
: we have just proved an inclusion and the dual spaces are the same (see Theorem 2.4 (i) and Corollary 2.7 there).

As another direct application, we recover a dual result of
[
16
]
and close to
[
11
]
: for scalar
kernels satisfying the Hörmander condition, the associated
Calderón-Zygmund operators are bounded on

L_{p}(\mathbb{R};L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
when
1<p<\infty
.

Appendix A
Vector-valued Hardy spaces

This appendix contains an explicit argument for the construction of the map

\displaystyle Q:L_{2}^{\circ}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\longrightarrow H_{1}(\mathbb{R};L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))

as stated along the discussion preceding Proposition
2.6
. Actually, the map
Q
can be considered whenever
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})
is replaced by any other Banach space. Moreover, a brief study of
molecules
, which give an additional description of the vector-valued Hardy space, is included.

Given a Banach space
\mathbb{X}
we say that a function
a
belonging to

L_{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
-atom in
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
whenever it satisfies the following conditions:

•

\mathrm{supp}(a)\subseteq I
for some interval
I
,

•

\int_{I}a=0
.

•

\|a\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}
,

Then,
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
is defined as the subspace of those functions
f
in
L_{1}
admitting a decomposition

(15)

\displaystyle f=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\quad\mathrm{in\ }L_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})

for some absolutely summable sequence
(\lambda_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}

and some family of
L_{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
-atoms

(a_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
. According to
[
13
]
, the definition
of
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
can be given via maximal functions the
same way it is done in the scalar-valued case. This justifies
which sort of convergence is considered at
(
15
). It can be checked that the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})}=\inf\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|\lambda_{i}|\ :\ f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{i}a_{i}\ \mathrm{for\ some\ }(\lambda_{i})_{i}\in\ell_{1}\ and\ L_{2}\textrm{-atoms}\ (a_{i})_{i}\}

is equivalent to the one defined via maximal functions with values in arbitrary Banach spaces. On the other hand, we include below the characterization via
molecules
of
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
which streamlines the proof for the classical case
[
20
, Ch. 5 Sec. 5]
.

Let
\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}
be a Banach space and consider as before
the function
\omega(x)=1+x^{2}
. Then, the space of Bochner measurable functions

\displaystyle L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega\ dx;\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})=\big\{f\in L_{0}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}}\ \omega(x)\ dx\ <\infty\big\}

is contained in
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})
. We will consider the subspace

\displaystyle M(\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})=\big\{f\in L_{2}(\mathbb{R},\omega\ dx;\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})\ :\ \int_{\mathbb{R}}f=0\big\}.

Lemma A.1
.

M(\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})
is a dense linear subspace of
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})
.

Proof.

Let
f\in M(\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})
and define

\displaystyle f_{0}=f\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\{|x|\leq 1\}}\ \mathrm{and}\ f_{j}=f\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{\{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}\}}\ \mathrm{for\ any\ }j>0.

These functions satisfy

\displaystyle\|f_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{\mathbb{X}})}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}\|f(x)\|_{\mathbb{\mathbb{X}}}^{2}\ dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}\|f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}\ \omega(x)\ dx\Big)^{1/2}\ 2^{-(j-1)}=:R_{j}\ 2^{-j}

so that the sequence
(R_{j})_{j\geq 0}
belongs to
\ell_{2}
. Let
I_{j}
be the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f_{j}
. Then, by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and a similar computation, it follows that, for
j\geq 0

\displaystyle\|I_{j}\|_{\mathbb{X}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}\|f(x)\|^{2}_{\mathbb{X}}\ \omega(x)\ dx\Big)^{1/2}\ \Big(\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}\omega(x)^{-1}\ dx\Big)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq R_{j}\ 2^{-j/2}.

Therefore, this yields some estimates for
S_{j}=\sum_{k\geq j}I_{j}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|S_{j}\|_{\mathbb{X}}\leq c\sum\limits_{k\geq j}R_{k}\ 2^{-k/2}.

Now, let’s replace the functions
f_{j}
by some
perturbed
atoms
a_{j}
given by

\displaystyle a_{j}(x)=f_{j}(x)+S_{j+1}\ 2^{-(j+2)}\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{|x|\leq 2^{j+1}}(x)-S_{j}\ 2^{-(j+1)}\ \mbox{\large$\chi$}_{|x|\leq 2^{j}}(x).

Then, the sequence
(a_{j})_{j\geq 0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}a_{j}(x)\ dx

\displaystyle=\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}f(x)\ dx+S_{j+1}-S_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{2^{j-1}<|x|\leq 2^{j}}f(x)\ dx-I_{j}=0.

Moreover, it is easy to check that, by hypothesis, the support of
a_{j}
is contained in
[-2^{j+1},2^{j+1}]
and using the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\|a_{j}\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})}

\displaystyle\leq R_{j}\ 2^{-j}+2\sum\limits_{k\geq j}R_{k}\ 2^{-k/2}2^{-(j+1)/2}=\lambda_{j}.

Then
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\ell_{1}
by previous computations and since

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k\geq j}2^{-(j+1)/2}R_{j}\ 2^{-k/2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}R_{j}2^{-j}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+t^{2})dt;L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))}.

Moreover, redefining
a_{i}
as
a_{i}/\lambda_{i}
, we obtain the expression

\displaystyle f=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{i}\ a_{i}

where each
a_{i}
is an atom with support
[-2^{j+1},2^{j+1}]
and convergence holds in the sense of
L_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
. In conclusion,
f
belongs to
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.
∎

Definition A.2
.

A
molecule

f
with values in
\mathbb{X}
, centered at
x_{0}
and of width
d>0
, is defined to be a function belonging to
M(\mathbb{X})
which is normalized by

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|f(x)\|_{\mathbb{X}}^{2}\ \Big(1+\frac{|x-x_{0}|^{2}}{d^{2}}\Big)\ dx\Big)^{1/2}\leq d^{-1/2}.

The
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
-norm is invariant by translations and
homogeneous for composition with homothethies. It follows that a
molecule
f
sits in
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})-
with a norm
controlled by an absolute constant. One can check that an atom is a
molecule. Thus, one can use molecules instead of atoms in the
definition of
H_{1}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{X})
.

Appendix B
Proof of Lemma
2.12

The justification for Lemma
2.12
is included in this
appendix. This result was stated in the commutative setting by
Garnett
[
9
]
, and also by Mei without proof
[
18
]
. For
that reason, a general version of the argument is developed
here. Before giving the explicit construction, some preliminary
classical results are showed. We will use the easier form
(
3
) and to lighten the notation we drop
\otimes\mathds{1}
.

Lemma B.1
.

Suppose
f\in L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R},\frac{dt}{1+t^{2}}))
and let
I\subset J
be finite intervals. Then, there holds

\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\widetilde{\iota}_{I}(f-f_{I})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}\leq\sqrt{\frac{|J|}{|I|}}\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\widetilde{\iota}_{J}(f-f_{J})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Proof.

This is clear because one goes from
\iota_{J}(f-f_{J})

just by restricting and centering with respect to
I
and all these operations are contractions at the
L_{2}
-level.
∎

Let
J=(a,b]
be a finite interval and let
c_{J}=\frac{a+b}{2}
denote the center of
J
. Write

\displaystyle J=J_{0}\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{n}\cup\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}J_{n}^{\prime}

where
d(J_{n},\partial J)=|J_{n}|
for
n\geq 0
and
d(J_{n}^{\prime},\partial J)=|J_{n}^{\prime}|
for
n\geq 1
. Then
J_{0}
coincides with the middle third of
J
, that is,

\displaystyle J_{0}=\frac{1}{3}J=\big(c_{J}-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{3}|J|,c_{J}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{3}|J|\big],

while for any
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle J_{n}

\displaystyle=\big(c_{J}+\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}},c_{J}+\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\big]

\displaystyle J_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\big(c_{J}-\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}},c_{J}-\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\big].

Thus,
|J_{n}|=|J_{n}^{\prime}|=\frac{|J|}{3}\frac{1}{2^{n}}
. Set
K_{n}
(respectively
K_{n}^{\prime}
) as the reflection of
J_{n}
(respectively
J_{n}^{\prime}
) across
b
(respectively
a
). Then,

\displaystyle K_{n}

\displaystyle=\big[b+\frac{|J|}{3}-\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}},b+\frac{|J|}{3}-\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\big),

\displaystyle K_{n}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\big[a-\frac{|J|}{3}+\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{2^{k}},a-\frac{|J|}{3}+\frac{|J|}{3}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{2^{k}}\big),

so
|K_{n}|=|K_{n}^{\prime}|=|J_{n}|=|J_{n}^{\prime}|
. Finally, define
L=[b+\frac{|J|}{3},\infty)
and
L^{\prime}=(-\infty,a-\frac{|J|}{3})
. This construction yields the desired operator
\psi
, which is given by the following expression assuming the normalization
\varphi_{J}=0
(recall that
\varphi
is defined up to a constant factor):

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle=\widetilde{P}_{J}\varphi+\sum_{n\geq 1}\varphi_{J_{n}}\otimes\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{n}}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\varphi_{J_{n}^{\prime}}\otimes\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{n}^{\prime}}.

Let
J^{+}=\cap_{n\geq 1}J_{n}
and
J^{-}=\cap_{n\leq 1}J_{n}
. It is worth
to mention that if
\mathbb{E}^{+}
denotes the conditional
expectation on
L_{2}(J^{+})
corresponding to the partition

\{J_{n};n\geq 1\}
, then

\sum_{n\geq 1}\varphi_{J_{n}}\otimes\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{n}}
is nothing but
\widetilde{R}_{+}(\widetilde{\mathbb{E}^{+}})(\widetilde{P}_{J^{+}}\varphi)
where
R_{+}
is the reflection with respect to
b
. A similar formula holds for
\sum_{n\geq 1}\varphi_{J_{n}^{\prime}}\otimes\mbox{\large$\chi$}_{K_{n}^{\prime}}
.

We turn to the proof of Lemma
2.12
. We need to bound, for all finite intervals
I
, the quantity

\big\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}.

We distinguish according to
I
:

Case 1
:
I\subset J
; there is nothing to do
as
\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})=\iota_{I}(\varphi-\varphi_{I})
.

Case 2
:
b\in I
.

Using Lemma (
B.1
), we can assume that
I
is symmetric around
b
losing a factor
\sqrt{2}
.

If
a\notin I
, then
I\subset\cup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}(J_{n}\cup K_{n})
with
J_{n_{0}-1}\cap I=\emptyset
. Similarly, by enlarging it and losing another factor
\sqrt{2}
, we can assume it is has the form
\cup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}(J_{n}\cup K_{n})
because
|J_{n_{0}}|\leq|I|
. Then
\psi_{I}=\psi_{I^{+}}=\psi_{I^{-}}=\varphi_{I^{-}}
where
I^{-}=\cup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}J_{n}
and
I^{+}=\cup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}K_{n}
,
|I|=2|I^{-}|
. Note that
\widetilde{R}_{+}
yields an isometry and

\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I^{+}|}}\iota_{I^{+}}(\psi-\psi_{I^{+}})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}=\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I^{-}|}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}^{+}}\iota_{I^{-}}(\varphi-\varphi_{I^{-}})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}.

Thus using that
\widetilde{\mathbb{E}^{+}}
is a contraction commuting with
\iota_{I^{-}}
, we get that

\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}\leq\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I^{+}|}}\iota_{I^{+}}(\psi-\psi_{I^{+}})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}^{2}\leq\|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}.

If
a\in I
, then we can simply replace it with
3J
which has a comparable size. then a similarly reasoning as above also gives a control by
\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\iota_{J}(\varphi-\varphi_{J})\big\|_{L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};L_{2}^{c}(\mathbb{R}))}
.

Case 3
:
a\in I
. One can proceed as in case 1.

Case 4
:
I\subset(b,\infty)
.

If
I\supset K_{n}
for some
n
, then
|I|\geq|K_{n}|
and losing a factor
\sqrt{2}
we can replace it by the bigger interval
\cup_{k\geq n}K_{k}\cup I
which has size less than
2|I|
. We are then back to case 2.

If
I\subset K_{n}
for some
n
there is noting to do as
\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})=0
.

If
I\subset K_{n}\cup K_{n+1}
for some
n\geq 1
. By losing a constant factor we can assume that
I
is symmetric with respect to left border of
K_{n}
say
b_{n}
.
If the size of
I
is smaller than
2|K_{n+1}|
, then
\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})
is a simple function taking two values
\pm\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{K_{n}}-\psi_{K_{n+1}})
. Then the norm of
\frac{1}{\sqrt{|I|}}\iota_{I}(\psi-\psi_{I})
is

\|\frac{1}{2}(\psi_{K_{n}}-\psi_{K_{n+1}})\|
. It the same as if we consider

(b_{n}-|K_{n+1}|,b_{n}+|K_{n+1}|)
and we are back to situation where
K_{n+1}\subset I
(or we can use the argument of case 2).

The only remaining situation is when
I\subset K_{1}\cup L
. Then an easy calculation gives that the involved norm is controlled
by
\|\sqrt{2}\psi_{K_{1}}\|=\|\sqrt{2}\varphi_{J_{1}}\|
. This is where we use that
\varphi_{J}=0
to say that (recall
6|J_{1}|=|J|
)

\|\varphi_{J_{1}}\|\leq\sqrt{6}\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{|J|}}\widetilde{\mathbb{E}^{+}}i_{J}(\varphi-0)\|\leq\sqrt{6}\|\varphi\|_{\mathsf{BMO}_{c}}.

Case 5
:
I\subset(-\infty,a)
. It can be done similarly.

We have dealt with all possible situations for
I
. This conclude Lemma
2.12
with at most
C=2\sqrt{6}
.

Rational approximation of holomorphic semigroups revisited Thanks: This work was partially supported by an NCN grant UMO- 2023/49/B/ST1/01961.
Charles Batty, Alexander Gomilko, Yuri Tomilov

Abstract
. Using a recently developed ℋ \mathcal{H} -calculus we propose a unified approach to the study of rational approximations of holomorphic semigroups on Banach spaces. We provide unified and simple proofs to a number of basic results on semigroup approximations and substantially improve some of them. We show that many of our estimates are essentially optimal, thus complementing the existing literature.

Primary 47A60, 41A20, 41A25; Secondary 47D03, 30E10, 65J08

1.
Introduction

The theory of rational appoximations of operator semigroups is an established area
intimately related to the numerical analysis of PDEs,
and also of value in probability theory and function theory.
The paper offers a new, functional calculus approach to the study
of rational approximations
arising from discretisation of an abstract Cauchy problem

(1.1)

x^{\prime}(s)=-Ax(s),\qquad s\in[0,t],\quad x(0)=x_{0},

on a Banach space
X
,
where
A
belongs to the set of sectorial operators
{\rm Sect}(\theta)

on
X
of angle
\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
. Since
-A
generates a
bounded holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
,
mild solutions to (
1.1
)
are given by
x(s)=e^{-sA}x_{0},x_{0}\in X
.
When considering a division of
[0,t]
into equal time steps
t/n

and discretising (
1.1
) accordingly, one approximates
a bounded holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}

by
\{r^{n}(tA/n):n\in\mathbb{N},t\geq 0\}
as propagator, where
r
is an appropriate rational function.

There are two main problems arising in the study of rational approximations
r^{n}(tA/n)

and their generalizations. The first is to find the best possible operator norm bounds for

r^{n}(tA/n)
and, ideally, to show that
\sup_{n\geq 1,t\geq 0}\|r^{n}(tA/n)\|<\infty
, i.e. to prove stability of
r^{n}(tA/n)
. If the stability does not hold, then
the convergence of
r^{n}(tA/n)x
to
e^{-tA}x
as
n\to\infty
for all
t\geq 0
may not take place
for all
x\in X
. However sharp bounds for
\|r^{n}(tA/n)\|

help to deal with the second problem
of obtaining the convergence of
e^{-tA}x-r^{n}(tA/n)x
for, at least, sufficiently regular initial data
x
,
and to equip this convergence with optimal approximation rates.

The two problems above lead to the two corresponding classes of rational functions which are basic in numerical analysis.
To study stability of rational approximations of
e^{-tA}
it is natural and necessary (even if
X=\mathbb{C}
)
to restrict attention to
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational functions
r
, that is
r
satisfying

|r(\lambda)|\leq 1
for
\lambda
from
\Sigma_{\psi}:=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:|{\rm arg}\,\lambda|<\psi\}

with
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
. If
\psi=\pi/2
, then
r
is said to be
\mathcal{A}
-stable.
The study of the approximation rates requires dealing with rational functions which approximate

e^{-tz}
at zero well enough: a rational function
r
is said to be
an approximation of order
q\in\mathbb{N}
to the exponential
e^{-z}
if

(1.2)

e^{-z}-r(z)=\mbox{O}(|z|^{q+1}),\qquad z\to 0,

or, in other words,
r^{(k)}(0)=(-1)^{k}
for all
0\leq k\leq q
.

Note that the approximation problems for (
1.1
) are often considered
with variable step sizes
\mathcal{K}_{n}=\{k_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}\subset(0,\infty)
,

k_{1}+\cdots+k_{n}=t
, and it is of interest to obtain
norm bounds for
r(k_{1}A)\cdots r(k_{n}A)

independent of
t
. While approximation rates can be studied in such a setting as well, and
results similar to the case of constant steps can also be obtained (see
[
27
, p. 95, Eq. (7)]
),
most of the literature has concentrated on approximation rates for constant step sizes, and we also follow this line of research.

Rational approximations of
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
can be treated by variety
of means. One efficient approach, not requiring precise information
on the structure of
A
, relies on employing functional calculi for
A
.
In
[
21
]
Hersh and Kato proposed an approach to the study of rational approximations of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups on Banach spaces based on the Hille-Phillips calculus, and thus on function estimates in the algebra of Laplace transforms of bounded measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This direction was further developed by Brenner and Thomee in
[
8
]
and a number of subsequent papers, see
[
5
]
,
[
11
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
22
]
, and
[
23
]
as examples, and many relevant approximation bounds got their optimal form.
However, in the setting of (sectorially) bounded holomorphic semigroups
the estimates offered by the Hille-Phillips calculus appear to be rather crude.
Thus, the existing arguments were mostly based on case by case studies
via the holomorphic Riesz-Dunford calculus. The arguments relied on appropriate choices of integration contours
and estimates for Riesz-Dunford integrals. They were rather technical,
and had to be adjusted to each problem accordingly.

The aim of the paper is twofold. First, using a new functional calculus, we provide a unified approach
to a variety of norm estimates for rational approximations of holomorphic semigroups obtained in the literature, and as a byproduct, improve some of them. Given
A\in{\rm Sect}(\theta),\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
,
we use a bounded homomorphism from an appropriate algebra
of holomorphic functions
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
on a sector
\Sigma_{\psi}
,

\psi\in(\theta,\pi/2]
, constructed in
[
6
]

and called
\mathcal{H}
-calculus, see Section
2
for more details.
The
\mathcal{H}
-calculus is compatible with the standard holomorphic functional calculus,
and since all the relevant rational functions arising in our considerations
belong to
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
, it reduces the study of rational approximations
to suitable
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-norm estimates. In this way we develop a technique for the
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-norm estimates of rational functions based on fine control of their oscillations along the rays and sharp asymptotic estimates of approximation errors in a sector around zero.

In particular, using the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus, we prove two stability estimates for

\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational approximations
r
and variable step sizes
\mathcal{K}_{n}=(k_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
.
The first one, originally due to Bakaev
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
(see also
[
26
, Corollary 1.2]
),
provides a general operator norm estimate for
P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}(A)=r(k_{1}A)\cdots r(k_{n}A)

in terms of the maximum ratio of
k_{j}
.
If
\max_{j}k_{j}\leq C\min_{j}k_{j}
for some
C>0
, in particular if all
k_{j}
are the same, then the estimate shows stability of
P_{{\mathcal{K}_{n}},r}(A)
, and, if
r
approximate
e^{-z}
with certain order, this leads to convergence of
P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}
on
X
as remarked in
[
27
, p. 95]
.
The result was preceded by important stability estimates in
[
9
]
and
[
26
]

yielding stability for the family
\{r^{n}(tA/n):n\in\mathbb{N},t\geq 0\}
.
Our second estimate, which is in a weaker form due to Bakaev
[
2
]
and Palencia
[
26
, Theorem 1]
(see also
[
4
]
where the result is reproved), shows stability of
P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}(A)

if
|r(\infty)|<1
. Both authors assume that
r
is a first order approximation to the exponential,
their proofs are rather involved and they rely essentially on this assumption.
The
\mathcal{H}
-calculus approach is direct, and it allows us to show that the approximation order of
r
is irrelevant, and thus the result holds in greater generality.
These estimates serve as illustrations of our technique, and other applications such as the study of multi-step
approximations as in
[
27
]
could probably be handled similarly. We leave this to the interested reader.
For an abstract approach to variable stepsize approximation and other related results one may consult
[
1
, Section II.8]
.

As the second aim of the paper (related to the first one),
we present a new,
\mathcal{H}
-calculus approach for obtaining
rational approximation rates for holomorphic semigroups and strengthen
several well-known statements in the literature.
Let us first recall
several basic bounds on approximation rates in the setting of holomorphic semigroups.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational approximation of order
q
to the exponential, and let

A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in[0,\theta)
and
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(\theta)
such that the following hold for all
s\in[0,q]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}

and
t>0
:

(i)

If
|r(\infty)|=1
, then

(1.3)

\|e^{-tA}x-r^{n}(tA/n)x\|\leq CM_{\theta}(A)t^{s}n^{-s}\|A^{s}x\|,\qquad x\in\operatorname{dom}(A^{s}).

(ii)

If
|r(\infty)|<1
, then

(1.4)

\|e^{-tA}-r^{n}(tA/n)\|\leq CM_{\theta}(A)/n^{q}.

Theorem
1.1
(i) was apparently first remarked in
[
8
, p. 694]
for
s=q

as a consequence of arguments given by Le Roux
[
25
]
, and it was repeated in
[
9
, Theorems 3 and 4]
again for
s=q
(mentioning also
[
14
]
and
[
28
]
).
See also
[
29
, Theorem 9.2 and Theorem 9.3]
, where Theorem
1.1
with
s=q
in (i)
was given with a full proof.
Both parts (i) and (ii) of Theorem
1.1
, for integer
s
and invertible
A
were proved in
[
24
, Theorem 4.2]
and
[
24
, Theorem 4.4]
, while in
[
27
, p. 95]
it was noted that that the argument in
[
24
]
can be adjusted to get rid of invertibility of
A
and to consider even variable step approximations, see also
[
20
, Theorem 2.1]
.
(Concerning lifting the invertibility of
A
see the remark following
[
9
, Theorem 5]
).
Generalisations of Theorem
1.1
for invertible
A
, integer
s
and variable time steps were obtained in
[
31
, Theorem 2.1 and Theorem 2.6]
. (See also
[
31
]
and
[
32
]
for other related results on rational approximation.)
Note
[
13
, Theorem 2.2 and Theorem 2.5]
, where Theorem
1.1
for integer
s
was obtained
assuming that
A
admits a bounded
H^{\infty}
-calculus. Finally, Theorem
1.1
in the present general formulation was proved in
[
17
, Theorem 9.2.3 and Corollary 9.2.9]
.

In the form of Theorem
1.1
(with varying generality) the result on rational approximation
of holomorphic semigroups has been functioning in the numerical analysis community since the
1980
s, and it became standard (e.g. in
[
22
]
). In this paper, we show that Theorem
1.1
(i) can, in fact, be substantially improved up to its optimal form. To this aim we introduce the notion of
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stability of a rational function. Let
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
and let
r
be a non-constant
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function. Then
r(\infty)=\lim_{z\to\infty}r(z)
exists in
\mathbb{C}
,
|r(\infty)|\leq 1
, and
there exist
a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that

(1.5)

r(z)=r(\infty)-\frac{a}{z^{m}}+\mbox{O}(|z|^{-(m+1)}),\qquad z\to\infty.

If
r
satisfies (
1.5
), then we say that
r
is
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable
.
Observe that if
\epsilon>0
and
r_{\epsilon}(z)=r(z+\epsilon)
, then
r_{\epsilon}
is
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable,
with the same value of
a
in (
1.5
).
Moreover, if
r
is
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable, then
there exists
c_{r}>0
such that

(1.6)

|r^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{c_{r}}{(1+|z|)^{2}},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\psi}.

Assuming that
r
is an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable approximation of order
q
to
e^{-z}
,
with
|r(\infty)|=1
, and setting for simplicity
t=1
, we prove in Theorem
5.1
that if
s\in[0,mq/(m+1))
, then the approximation rates
n^{-s}
in (
1.3
) can be replaced by strictly
sharper rates
n^{-s(1+1/m)}
. If
s\in[mq/(m+1),q+1]
, we improve again the rates
n^{-s}
given by (
1.3
) and replace them with the rates
n^{-q}
. The latter rates are in fact the fastest possible when
s
varies through the whole of
[0,q+1]
as we show in in Theorem
7.3
.
Our results have an especially transparent form if
r
is one of the diagonal Padé approximations to
e^{-z}
. Then
m=1
, and Corollary
5.3
yields the maximal convergence rate
n^{-q}
on quite a large interval
[q/2,q+1]
containing, in particular, many integers for large
q
.
For
s\in[q,q+1]
we do not get any improvements for approximation rates thus observing
a “saturation” effect, and this interval for
s
seems not to have been discussed in the literature.
These phenomena can be explained by the fact that the
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-norm estimates for
r^{n}(\cdot/n)

also do not improve when
s\in[q,q+1]
.

If
|r(\infty)|<1
, then we show that (
1.4
) holds in a slightly generalised form
with the right hand side replaced by
\|A^{s}x\|/n^{q}
for
s\in[0,q+1]
and all

x\in\operatorname{dom}(A^{s})
. One gets (
1.4
) if
s=0
,
but, on the other hand,
\|A^{s}x\|
and
\|x\|
are, in general, not comparable for
x\in\operatorname{dom}(A^{s})
if
A
is not invertible.

An important feature of our abstract approach is that we are able to show that
the obtained approximation rates are optimal in a strong sense. Namely, by an operator-theoretical construction of independent interest, we prove that if
r
is
an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximation of order
q
to the exponential then
for all
\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
and
s\in[0,q+1]
, there exist a Banach space
X
, an
operator
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
on
X
, and a vector
x\in\operatorname{dom}(A^{s})

such that the lower bounds for

\|e^{-tA}x-r(A/n)^{n}x\|

match the approximation rates obtained in Theorem
5.1
up to a constant depending on
\theta

and a sequence vanishing at infinity. Optimality issues are very
natural in approximation theory, and they have been addressed thoroughly for rational approximations
of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups, but we have not been able to find any similar results in the literature. A typical result would obtain optimality only in a certain (operator norm) sense, see e.g.
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
,

[
7
, Chapter 5, Theorems 4.3 and 4.4]
,
[
23
, p. 285]
,
[
15
, Corollary 7.5]
or
[
16
, Prop. 4.8]
. This was a starting point of our research which led to unexpected consequences discussed above.

Conventions and notation

In this paper, we will consider an individual rational function
r
.
It will be implicit that some functions and constants that are introduced will depend on
r

and other parameters associated solely with
r
(for example, the order
q
).
They may depend on other parameters which are independent of
r
, and
we will try to make clear which parameters are involved by writing
C=C(n,s)
or
C_{n,s}
,
if the constants depend on
n
and
s
.

For readers’ convenience, we list here various functions derived from
r
. The variables are
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}=[0,\infty)
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
. The following notation for relevant functions
will be used frequently:

•

r_{n}(z)=r^{n}(z/n)
,

•

r_{n,s}(z)=r_{n}(z)/z^{s}
,

•

\Delta_{n}(z)=e^{-z}-r_{n}(z)
,

•

\Delta_{n,s}(z)=\Delta_{n}(z)/z^{s}
.

We will also denote

\mathbb{C}_{+}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm Re}\,z>0\}
,
\mathbb{D}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}
,

\mathbb{D}_{R}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<R\}
, and

\Sigma_{\theta}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|{\rm arg}\,z|<\theta\}
,
\theta\in(0,\pi)
.
The set of holomorphic functions on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
will be denoted by
{\rm Hol}(\Omega)
,
and
H^{\infty}(\Omega)
will stand for the space of
f\in{\rm Hol}(\Omega)
which are bounded in
\Omega
.

If
X
is a Banach space, then the space of bounded linear operators on
X
will be denoted by
L(X)
,
and
\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
will stand for the set of sectorial operators on
X
.

2.
The HP-calculus and the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus

Our considerations will be based on the so-called
\mathcal{H}
-functional calculus
constructed recently in
[
6
]
.
To put the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus into a proper context, we first
recall another functional caclulus, which was used in the study of rational approximations of semigroups, see
[
8
]
.

Let
{\rm M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
denote the Banach algebra of bounded Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and let

\mathcal{LM}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
be the Banach algebra of Laplace transforms
\mathcal{L}\mu
, where
\mu\in{\rm M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
equipped with the norm

(2.1)

\|\mathcal{L}\mu\|_{\mathcal{LM}}:=\|\mu\|_{{\rm M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

If
-A
generates a bounded
C_{0}
-semigroup
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
on a Banach space
X
,
and
M:=\sup_{t\geq 0}\|e^{-tA}\|
,
then the Hille-Phillips (HP-) calculus for
A
is defined as the bounded homomorphism
\Psi_{A}:\mathcal{LM}\to L(X)

given by the operator version of the Laplace transform:

\Psi_{A}(\mathcal{L}\mu)x=\mathcal{L}\mu(A)x=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-tA}x\,d\mu(t),\qquad x\in X,\quad\mathcal{L}\mu\in\mathcal{LM},

so that

(2.2)

\|\mathcal{L}\mu(A)\|\leq M\|\mu\|_{{\rm M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

The HP-calculus is one of the basic functional calculi used in the literature. However, it often leads to rather crude estimates and ignores specific aspects of important subclasses of the class of bounded
C_{0}
-semigroups, such as sectorially bounded holomorphic semigroups. In the studies of rational approximations for holomorphic semigroups, another (holomorphic) functional calculus based on Riesz-Dunford integrals is used frequently. However, in this case, the arguments depend on delicate choices of contours and involved estimates which depend on particular contexts.

To produce sharp approximation norm bounds,
finer calculi are required. One such calculus was
developed in
[
6
]
, and we recall some of its features now.
Let
H^{1}(\Sigma_{\psi})
stand for the Hardy space of functions

f\in{\rm Hol}(\Sigma_{\psi})
such that

\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\psi})}:=\sup_{|\theta|<\psi}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(|f(te^{i\theta})|+|f(te^{-i\theta})|\right)\,dt.

It was shown in
[
6
, Theorem 4.6]
that if
f\in H^{1}(\Sigma_{\psi})
, then the boundary values

f(te^{\pm i\psi})=\lim_{\varphi\to\pm\psi}f(te^{it\varphi})
exist for a.e.
t

and
\|f\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\psi})}=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Sigma_{\psi})}
.

The Hardy-Sobolev space
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
,

\psi\in(0,\pi)
, is defined as the space of
f\in{\rm Hol}(\Sigma_{\psi})

such that

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}}:=\|f^{\prime}\|_{H^{1}(\Sigma_{\psi})}<\infty.

By
[
6
, Theorem 4.8]
, if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
then
f\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\psi})
,
f
extends continuously to
\partial\Sigma_{\psi}
, and there exists

f(\infty)=\lim_{z\to\infty,z\in\Sigma_{\psi}}f(z)
. Note that by the above,

(2.3)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}}=\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Sigma_{\psi})}

Moreover, by
[
6
, Lemma 4.9]
,
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
is a Banach algebra equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}:=\|f\|_{\infty}+\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}}
.
If
f\in{\rm Hol}(\Sigma_{\theta})
, then

(2.4)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\theta}},\quad f\in\mathcal{H}_{\theta},\quad 0<\psi<\theta.

The expression

\|f\|^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}:=|f(\infty)|+\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}},\qquad f\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi},

defines an equivalent norm on
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
, which is not a Banach algebra norm. In particular,
[
6
, Theorem 4.8(iii)]
shows that, if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
and
f(\infty)=0
, then

(2.5)

\|f\|_{\infty}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}.

The Banach algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
for
\psi\in(0,\pi)
are isometrically isomorphic to each other, via fractional powers.
Let
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in(0,\pi)
and
\gamma=\psi_{1}/\psi_{2}
. If
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi_{1}}
and
f_{\gamma}(z)=f(z^{\gamma})
, it is easy to check that
f_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi_{2}}
and
\|f_{\gamma}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi_{2},0}}=\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi_{1},0}}
. See
[
6
, Lemma 4.9]
.

Moreover, it is instructive to observe that if
f\in\mathcal{LM}
, then (the restriction to
\Sigma_{\psi}
of)
f
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
for any
\psi\in(0,\pi/2)
. Indeed, if
f=\mathcal{L}\mu
and
|\theta|<\psi
, then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|f^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\,dt\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t\tau\cos\theta}\,dt\,\tau|\mu|(d\tau)=\frac{1}{\cos\theta}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|\mu|(d\tau),

so that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\|f\|^{*}_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}\leq|\mu(\{0\})|+\frac{2}{\cos\psi}\int_{(0,\infty)}|\mu|(d\tau)\leq\frac{2}{\cos\psi}\|f\|_{\mathcal{LM}}.

As shown in
[
6
]
, the algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
allow one to define a functional calculus
for a sectorial operator
A
on a Banach space
X
, which takes into account the angle of sectoriality
of
A
and leads to fine operator-norm estimates for functions of
A
.
More precisely, recall that the operator
A
on
X
is said to be sectorial of angle
\theta

if
\sigma(A)\subset\overline{\Sigma}_{\theta}
, and for every
\psi\in(\theta,\pi)

there exists
M_{\psi}(A)>0
such that

M_{\psi}(A):=\sup\{\|\lambda R(\lambda,A)\|:\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi-\psi}\}<\infty.

As in the introduction, we let
\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
stand for the class of sectorial operators of angle
\theta
,
for
\psi\in[0,\pi)
and
\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi-\psi}
denote

\rho_{\lambda}(z):=(z+\lambda)^{-1}
.
If
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
,
\psi\in(\theta,\pi)
,
then by
[
6
, Theorem 8.6]
there is a unique bounded homomorphism

\Psi_{A}:\mathcal{H}_{\psi}\to L(X)
,
\Psi_{A}(f)=f(A)
,
such that
\Psi_{A}(\rho_{\lambda})=(A+\lambda)^{-1},\lambda\in\Sigma_{\pi-\psi}
,
called the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus for
A
.
The calculus is given by
the formula:

(2.7)

f(A)=f(\infty)-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(f^{\prime}(e^{it\psi})+f^{\prime}(e^{-it\psi})\right)\arccot(A^{\gamma}/t^{\gamma})\,dt,

where the operator kernel
\arccot(A^{\gamma}/t^{\gamma})
is defined by a separate
formula involving the logarithmic function as explained in
[
6
, Section 8.2]
.
From (
2.7
) it follows that

(2.8)

\|f(A)\|\leq|f(\infty)|+\frac{M_{\psi}(A)}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}}\leq M_{\psi}(A)\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi}}.

Thus, if
f(\infty)=0
,

(2.9)

\|f(A)\|\leq\frac{M_{\psi}(A)}{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\psi,0}}.

The formula (
2.7
) appears to depend on
\psi
, but the definitions of
f(A)
agree provided that
\psi\in(\theta,\pi)
and
f
is holomorphic on
\Sigma_{\psi}
. Recall that
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta),\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
, if and only if
-A
generates a (sectorially bounded) holomorphic
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
,
so that the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus applies, in fact, to the negative generators of holomorphic semigroups.

We emphasize two crucial properties of the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus, which are implicit
in
[
6
]
in view of a somewhat indirect approach to the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus developed there.
Note that by
[
6
, Theorems 5.10, 11.1]
, the algebras
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}

are invariant under shifts, i.e., if
f\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
,
\epsilon>0
, and
f_{\epsilon}(z)=f(z+\epsilon)
,
then
f_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
, and the map
f\mapsto f_{\varepsilon}
from
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
to
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
is bounded.
Thus, it is easy to see that if
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
, then
A+\varepsilon
has a bounded
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-calculus given by

\Phi_{A+\epsilon}(f)=\Phi_{A}(f_{\epsilon}).

Since the
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-calculus for
A+\varepsilon
is unique, it follows that

(2.10)

f(A+\epsilon)=f_{\epsilon}(A).

Since
-A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
on
X
,
and
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
contains the functions
e^{-t\cdot}
for
t\geq 0
, it is natural to expect
that

e^{-t\cdot}(A)=T(t),\qquad t\geq 0.

This follows from the fact that if
t\geq 0
then
e^{-t\cdot}
is the limit in
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
of
\rho_{1}(t\cdot/n)^{n},\,n\to\infty
, proved in Remark
5.2
below.

We will be concerned only with the exponential functions
(e^{-tz})_{t\geq 0}
and rational functions
r
which are
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable, and functions derived from these, applied in situations
where
\psi\in(0,\pi/2)
, and
A
is sectorial of angle
\theta\in[0,\psi)
. The operators
(e^{-tA})_{t\geq 0}
form a
C_{0}
-semigroup which extends to a bounded holomorphic semigroup on a sector. The operators
r(A)
have the natural definition for rational functions.

3.
Estimates for rational approximations of the exponential

3.1.
Stability estimates

In this section we extend known results about the stability of certain classes of rational approximations to the exponential function with variable stepsizes. In the next section we will apply these results to sectorial operators. For
R>0
, recall the notation

\mathbb{D}_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<R\}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
f
be a holomorphic function on
\mathbb{D}_{R}
which is non-constant,
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, and let
f_{\theta}(t):=|f(te^{i\theta})|,\,0<t<R
. Then
there exist
R_{0}\in(0,R)
and a finite (possibly empty) set

\Theta_{f}\subset(-\pi,\pi]
such that for every
\theta\in(\pi,\pi]\setminus\Theta_{f}
,

(3.1)

0<|f^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\leq C_{\theta}|f^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|,\quad t\in(0,R_{0}),\quad

for some
C_{\theta}>0
.

Proof.

By adjusting
R
if necessary, we may assume that
f^{\prime}
has no zeroes in
\mathbb{D}_{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
From the Taylor series for
f
, there exist
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
b=|b|e^{i\varphi}\neq 0
such that

f(z)=f(0)+bz^{k}+\mbox{O}(z^{k+1}),\qquad z\to 0,\quad z\in\mathbb{D}_{R},

and

(3.2)

|f^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|=k|b|t^{k-1}+\mbox{O}(t^{k}),\qquad t\to 0,\quad\theta\in(-\pi,\pi].

If
f(0)=0
,
then

f_{\theta}(t)=|b|t^{k}+\mbox{O}(t^{k+1}),\qquad f^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)=k|b|t^{k-1}+\mbox{O}(t^{k}),

uniformly in
\theta
, and in view of (
3.2
), the inequality (
3.1
) holds
for some
R_{0}>0
and every
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
.

If
f(0)\not=0
, we may assume, without loss of generality, that

f(0)=1
. A simple calculation reveals that

f_{\theta}^{2}(t)=1+2|b|t^{k}\cos(\varphi+k\theta)+\mbox{O}(t^{k+1}),\\

uniformly in
\theta
.
Hence, for every
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]
,

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=1+{\rm O}(t^{k}),

\displaystyle f_{\theta}(t)f^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)

\displaystyle=k|b|t^{k-1}\cos(\varphi+k\theta)+{\rm O}(t^{k}).

Taking into account (
3.2
), we infer that (
3.1
) is satisfied
for some
R_{0}>0
and

\Theta_{f}:=\{\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]:\,\cos(\varphi+k\theta)=0\}.\qed

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
r
be a rational function which is non-constant, holomorphic at zero and bounded at infinity.
Then there exist a finite (possibly empty) set

\Theta_{r}\subset(-\pi,\pi]

and
R_{1}>R_{0}>0
such that
for every
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi]\setminus\Theta_{r}

(3.3)

0<|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\leq c_{\theta}|r^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|,\qquad t\in(0,R_{0})\cup(R_{1},\infty),

for some
c_{\theta}>0
.

Proof.

Employing Proposition

3.1
we infer that there is
R_{0}>0
such that the inequality in (
3.3
) holds for
t\in(0,R_{0})
and all except finitely many
\theta
.
Passing then to
r^{*}(z):=r(1/z)
and employing Proposition
3.1
again
we obtain
R_{1}>0
such that the inequality in (
3.3
) also
holds for
t>R_{1}
and all except finitely many
\theta
.
∎

A consequence of (
3.3
) is that
r_{\theta}^{\prime}
has no zero in
(0,R_{0})
or
(R_{1},\infty)
. This has the consequence that if
g
is a non-negative measurable function on an interval
I
contained in
(0,R_{0})
then

(3.4)

\int_{I}|r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|g(t)\,dt=\left|\int_{I}r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)g(t)\,dt\right|.

Example 3.3
.

We illustrate Proposition
3.1
by considering a Cayley transform.
Let
z_{0}=\tau e^{i\varphi}\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and

r(z)=\frac{z-z_{0}}{z+\overline{z}_{0}},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

We will show that
for every
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
,

(3.5)

r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)<0,\quad t\in(0,\tau),\qquad\text{and}\qquad r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)>0,\quad t\in(\tau,\infty),

and for every
\delta\in(0,1)
there exists
c_{\delta}>0
such that

(3.6)

|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\leq c_{\delta}\frac{|r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)|}{\cos\theta},\quad t\in(0,(1-\delta)\tau)\cup((1+\delta)\tau,\infty).

Indeed, note that

|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|=\frac{2\tau\cos\varphi}{t^{2}+2t\tau\cos(\theta+\varphi)+\tau^{2}},

and

r^{2}_{\theta}(t)=\frac{t^{2}-2t\tau\cos(\theta-\varphi)+\tau^{2}}{t^{2}+2t\tau\cos(\theta+\varphi)+\tau^{2}}.

Differentiation gives

r_{\theta}(t)r_{\theta}^{\prime}(t)=\frac{2\tau(t^{2}-\tau^{2})\cos\theta\cos\varphi}{(t^{2}+2t\tau\cos(\theta+\varphi)+\tau^{2})^{2}}=\frac{(t^{2}-\tau^{2})\cos\theta}{t^{2}+2t\tau\cos(\theta+\varphi)+\tau^{2}}|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta}|.

Since
r_{\theta}(t)>0
and
r_{\theta}(t)\leq 1
, this shows that (
3.5
) holds. Moreover,

\displaystyle|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|

\displaystyle=\frac{t^{2}+2t\tau\cos(\theta+\varphi)+\tau^{2}}{|t^{2}-\tau^{2}|\cos\theta}|r_{\theta}(t)r^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(t+\tau)^{2}}{|t^{2}-\tau^{2}|\cos\theta}|r^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|=\frac{t+\tau}{|t-\tau|\cos\theta}|r^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|,

so (
3.6
) holds with
c_{\delta}=2\delta^{-1}-1
.

In Example
3.3
,

r\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
and (
3.3
) holds for all
\theta\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
.
In the general case, when
r\in H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\psi})
and
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
,
there may be exceptional values of
\theta
for which
(
3.3
) does not hold as the following two examples show.

Example 3.4
.

Fix

\varphi\in(0,\pi/2)
and consider the rational function

r(z):=\frac{z-e^{-i\varphi}}{z+e^{-i\varphi}}.

Note that
r\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
. If

z=ie^{-i\varphi}t=te^{i(\pi/2-\varphi)},t>0,

then
z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
and

|r(ie^{-i\varphi}t)|=\left|\frac{it-1}{it+1}\right|=1,\qquad t>0.

Hence

\frac{d}{dt}|r(ie^{-i\varphi}t)|\equiv 0,\quad t>0,

and so (
3.3
) does not hold for
\theta=\pi/2-\varphi
.

Example 3.5
.

For a fixed
\varphi\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
define (a “shifted Cayley
transform”)

r(z):=\frac{z+1-e^{-i\varphi}}{z+1+e^{i\varphi}}

and note that

\|r\|_{{H}^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}_{+})}=1
and
r(\infty)=1
. Moreover, for every
t>0
,

|r(t)|^{2}=\frac{(t+1)^{2}-2(t+1)\cos\varphi+1}{(t+1)^{2}+2(t+1)\cos\varphi+1},

By simple calculations,

\left.\frac{d}{dt}|r(t)|\right|_{t=0}=0,\qquad|r^{\prime}(0)|=\frac{2}{|1+e^{i\varphi}|^{2}}\not=0.

This implies that (
3.3
) does not hold for
\theta=0
and small
t>0
.

Now we turn our attention towards rational approximations schemes involving
products of a single function over variable stepsizes.
Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function,
and
let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}=(k_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
be a sequence in
(0,\infty)
of length
n
,

K_{0}=\min_{j}\,\{k_{j}\}
and
K_{1}=\max_{j}\,\{k_{j}\}
.
Let

(3.7)

P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(z):=\prod_{j=1}^{n}r(k_{j}z),\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\psi}.

By (
1.6
) and (
2.3
),
we have
P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}\in\mathcal{H}_{\theta,0}
and

(3.8)

\|P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\theta,0}}=\|P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Sigma_{\theta})}.

We will give two estimates for the right-hand side of (
3.8
), firstly an estimate which is valid for all
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational functions, but the estimate depends on
\mathcal{K}_{n}
as well as
r
and
\theta
, and secondly an estimate which is independent of
\mathcal{K}_{n}
provided that
|r(\infty)|<1
.

In the statements and proofs of these two theorems, the notation
C
denotes a constant which may depend on
r
,
\psi
and
\theta
, but does not depend on
\mathcal{K}_{n}
. The value of
C
may change from place to place.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function, where
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
, and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
There is a constant
C
such that

(3.9)

\|P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\theta,0}}\leq C\left(1+\log\left(\frac{K_{1}}{K_{0}}\right)\right)

for all finite sequences
\mathcal{K}_{n}
in
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
R_{0}
,
R_{1}
and
\Theta_{r}
be as in Corollary
3.2
, and

a:=\frac{R_{0}}{K_{1}},\qquad b:=\frac{R_{1}}{K_{0}}.

By the maximum principle, it suffices to consider
\theta\in(0,\psi)
, sufficiently close to
\psi
that (
3.3
) holds for
\pm\theta
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}
and
P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}
be as above.
Since
\|P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}\|_{H^{\infty}(\Sigma_{\psi})}\leq 1
,
there is a constant
C
(depending only on
\theta
and
\psi
) such that

(3.10)

|P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{C}{|z|},\qquad z\in\overline{\Sigma}_{\theta},\,z\neq 0.

Using (
3.3
), (
3.10
) and (
3.4
), we have

\displaystyle\hskip-15.0pt\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})\right|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}te^{i\theta})|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r(k_{l}te^{i\theta})|\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\hbox{}+\int_{a}^{b}\left|P_{{\mathcal{K}}_{n},r}^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})\right|\,dt+\int_{b}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}te^{i\theta})|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r(k_{l}te^{i\theta})|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg|\int_{0}^{a}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d}{dt}|r(k_{j}te^{i\theta})|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r(k_{l}te^{i\theta})|\,dt\Bigg|

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\hbox{}+C\int_{a}^{b}\frac{dt}{t}+C\Bigg|\int_{b}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d}{dt}|r(k_{j}te^{i\theta})|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r(k_{l}te^{i\theta})|\,dt\Bigg|

\displaystyle=C\Bigg|\int_{0}^{a}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}r(k_{j}te^{i\theta})\Big|\,dt\Bigg|+C\log\left(\frac{b}{a}\right)+C\Bigg|\int_{b}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dt}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}r(k_{j}te^{i\theta})\Big|\,dt\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq C+C\log\left(\frac{R_{1}K_{1}}{R_{0}K_{0}}\right)\leq C\left(1+\log\left(\frac{K_{1}}{K_{0}}\right)\right).

The equality sign is valid by the sign argument in (
3.4
).
The same argument also works for
-\theta
, and hence (
3.9
) follows.
∎

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function
with
|r(\infty)|<1
,
and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
There exists
C
such that

(3.11)

\|P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\leq C

for every finite sequence
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subseteq(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
R_{0}=1
; otherwise consider
r(zR_{0})
in place of
r(z)
, noting that this change of variable does not change
\|P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\theta,0}}
.
Thus

(3.12)

0<|r^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\leq C|r^{\prime}_{\theta}(t)|,\qquad t\in(0,1).

By the maximum principle there exists
\kappa_{r}\in(0,1)
such that

(3.13)

|r(z)|\leq\kappa_{r},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\theta},\;|z|\geq 1.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}
be a sequence of length
n
in
(0,\infty)
.
We have shown in Theorem
3.6
that
P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}\in{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}
.
To obtain (
3.11
), we will use (
3.13
) to improve the estimate of the
L^{1}
-norm of
P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}

on the boundary of
\Sigma_{\theta}
, where
\theta\in(0,\psi)
and
(\ref{CP1D})
holds for
\pm\theta
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{n}=(k_{j})_{j=1}^{n}
, where
0<k_{1}\leq k_{2}\leq\dots\leq k_{n}
without loss of generality, and let

(3.14)

t_{0}=0,\;\quad t_{j}=\frac{1}{k_{n-j+1}},\;j=1,\dots,n,\quad\;t_{n+1}=\infty.

Let

S_{r,m}(\mathcal{K}_{n};\theta):=\int_{t_{m-1}}^{t_{m}}\big|P^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(te^{i\theta})\big|\,dt,\qquad m=1,\dots,n+1.

Then

(3.15)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|P^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(te^{i\theta})\big|\,dt=\sum_{m=1}^{n+1}S_{r,m}(\mathcal{K}_{n};\theta).

Taking into account that

k_{j}t\leq 1,\quad j=1,\dots,n,\quad t\in(0,1/k_{n}),

and applying (
3.14
) and (
3.4
),
we have

\displaystyle S_{r,1}(\mathcal{K}_{n};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\int_{0}^{1/k_{n}}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}e^{i\theta}t)|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\int_{0}^{1/k_{n}}\left|\frac{d}{dt}r_{\theta}(k_{j}t)\right|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=C\left|\int_{0}^{1/k_{n}}\frac{d}{dt}|P_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(te^{i\theta})|\,dt\right|\leq C.

For
2\leq m\leq n
, we have

\displaystyle k_{j}t

\displaystyle\leq 1,\quad j=1,\dots,n-m+1,\quad t\leq\frac{1}{k_{n-m+1}},

\displaystyle k_{l}t

\displaystyle\geq 1,\quad l=n-m+2,\dots,n,\quad t\geq\frac{1}{k_{n-m+2}}.

Using (
3.12
), (
1.6
), (
3.13
) and (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle S_{r,m}(\mathcal{K}_{n};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\int_{1/k_{n-m+2}}^{1/k_{n-m+1}}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}e^{i\theta}t)|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa_{r}^{m-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n-m+1}k_{j}\int_{1/k_{n-m+2}}^{1/k_{n-m+1}}\left|\frac{d}{dt}r_{\theta}(k_{j}t)\right|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n-m+1}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\hbox{}+\sum_{j=n-m+2}^{n}k_{j}\int_{1/k_{n-m+2}}^{1/k_{n-m+1}}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}e^{i\theta}t)|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle=C\kappa_{r}^{m-1}\left|\int_{1/k_{n-m+2}}^{1/k_{n-m+1}}\frac{d}{dt}|P_{n-m+2}(te^{i\theta})|\,dt\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 12.0pt\hbox{}+c_{r}\kappa_{r}^{m-2}\sum_{j=n-m+2}^{n}\int_{1/k_{n-m+2}}^{1/k_{n-m+1}}\frac{k_{j}}{(1+k_{j}t)^{2}}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa_{r}^{m-1}+c_{r}(m-1)\kappa_{r}^{m-2}.

For
m=n+1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{r,n+1}(\mathcal{K}_{n};\theta)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}\int_{1/k_{1}}^{\infty}|r^{\prime}(k_{j}e^{i\theta}t)|\prod_{l=1,\;l\not=j}^{n}|r_{\theta}(k_{l}t)|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq c_{r}\kappa_{r}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{1/k_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{k_{j}}{(1+k_{j}t)^{2}}\,dt\leq c_{r}n\kappa_{r}^{n-1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|P^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(te^{i\theta})\big|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq C+\sum_{m=2}^{n}(C\kappa_{r}^{m-1}+c_{r}(m-1)\kappa_{r}^{m-2})+c_{r}n\kappa_{r}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=C\sum_{m=1}^{n}\kappa_{r}^{m-1}+c_{r}\sum_{m=1}^{n}m\kappa_{r}^{m-1}\leq\frac{C}{1-\kappa_{r}}+\frac{c_{r}}{(1-\kappa_{r})^{2}}.

and completely similarly

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\big|P^{\prime}_{\mathcal{K}_{n},r}(te^{-i\theta})\big|\,dt\leq\frac{C}{1-\kappa_{r}}+\frac{c_{r}}{(1-\kappa_{r})^{2}}.

Now (
3.11
) is immediate.
∎

3.2.
Approximation rate estimates

In this section, we prove several auxiliary lemmas about
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximations of the exponential function, which lead to the improved version of Theorem
1.1
by means of
the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus. Recalling the definition of
A(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximations from the Introduction, we start with a simple example of an
\mathcal{A}(\pi/6,2)
-stable function which approximates
e^{-z}
with
q=1
and
|r(\infty)|=1
, illustrating this new sharper notion of stability.

Example 3.8
.

Let

r(z)=\frac{1-4z^{3}}{1+z+4z^{3}},\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Then
r(\infty)=-1
, and

r(z)+1=\frac{2+z}{1+z+4z^{3}}=\frac{1}{4z^{2}}+{\rm O}(|z|^{-3}),\qquad z\to\infty.

Moreover,

r(0)=1,\qquad r^{\prime}(0)=-1,\qquad r^{\prime\prime}(0)=2,

so
r
approximates
e^{-z}
with exact order
q=1
. The poles of
r
are at
-1/2
and at

\frac{1\pm i\sqrt{7}}{4}=\frac{e^{\pm i\varphi}}{\sqrt{2}},\quad\varphi=\arctan\sqrt{7}\in(\pi/3,\pi/2).

Thus
r
is holomorphic on
\Sigma_{\pi/6}
. If
t>0
and
z=te^{i\pi/6}=(\sqrt{3}+i)t/2
, then
z^{3}=it^{3}
and

\displaystyle|r(te^{\pm i\pi/6})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{16t^{6}+1}{(4t^{3}+t/2)^{2}+(\sqrt{3}t/2+1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{16t^{6}+4}{16t^{6}+4t^{4}+t^{2}+\sqrt{3}t+4}<1.

Thus
r
is
\mathcal{A}(\pi/6,2)
-stable.

For the study of approximation rates for rational approximations

r(t\cdot/n)^{n}
to the exponential,
it is crucial to have sharp estimates for the size
of the rational function
r
near infinity. The following lemma
provides such an estimate in terms the Taylor expansion of
r

at infinity.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational function with
|r(\infty)|=1
.
Then for every
\theta\in(0,\psi)
there exist
b_{1},b_{2}>0
and
R\geq 1
,
such that

(3.16)

e^{-b_{2}/|z|^{m}}\leq|r(z)|\leq e^{-b_{1}/|z|^{m}}

for all
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
with
|z|\geq R
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
r(\infty)=1
.
If
a=|a|e^{i\beta}
,
\beta\in(-\pi,\pi]
, then it follows from

\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stability that

|r(te^{i\varphi})|^{2}=1-2|a|t^{-m}\cos(\beta-m\varphi)+\mbox{O}(t^{-m-1}),\qquad t\to\infty,\quad|\varphi|\leq\psi.

It follows that

\cos(\beta\pm m\varphi)\geq 0,\qquad|\varphi|\leq\psi,

and then

|\beta\pm m\psi|\leq\pi/2,

and finally

|\beta|+m\psi=\max\{|\beta-m\psi|,|\beta+m\psi|\}\leq\pi/2.

Let
z=te^{i\varphi}
, with
t>0
,
|\varphi|\leq\theta
, and let

\omega:=\max\,\{|\beta-m\theta|,|\beta+m\theta|\}<\pi/2.

We infer from (
1.5
) that there exists
c>0
such that

(3.17)

|r(te^{i\varphi})|\leq\left|1-\frac{|a|e^{i\beta}e^{-im\varphi}}{t^{m}}\right|+\frac{c}{t^{m+1}}\leq\left|1-\frac{|a|e^{i\omega}}{t^{m}}\right|+\frac{c}{t^{m+1}}.

If

(3.18)

t\geq(|a|/\cos\omega)^{1/m},

we may apply an elementary inequality

|1-z|\leq 1-\frac{{\rm Re}\,z}{2}\qquad\mbox{if}\qquad|z|^{2}\leq{\rm Re}\,z,

and conclude that

(3.19)

|r(te^{i\varphi}|\leq 1-\frac{|a|\cos\omega}{2t^{m}}+\frac{c}{t^{m+1}}.

If in addition to (
3.18
),

(3.20)

t\geq\left(\frac{4c}{|a|\cos\omega}\right)^{1/(m+1)},

then it follows from (
3.19
) and the inequality
1-\tau\leq e^{-\tau}
that

(3.21)

|r(te^{i\varphi})|\leq 1-\frac{|a|\cos\omega}{4t^{m}}\leq e^{-|a|\cos\omega/(4t^{m})}.

Thus the second inequality in (
3.16
) holds with
b_{1}=(|a|\cos\omega)/4
and

t\geq R_{1}:=\max\,\left\{\left(\frac{|a|}{\cos\omega}\right)^{1/m},\left(\frac{4c}{|a|\cos\omega}\right)^{1/(m+1)}\right\}.

For the first inequality, there exists
c^{\prime}>0
such that

(3.22)

|r(te^{i\varphi})|\geq 1-\frac{|a|}{t^{m}}-\frac{c^{\prime}}{t^{m+1}},\qquad t>0.

Let

(3.23)

R_{2}:=\max\,\{c^{\prime}/|a|,(8|a|)^{1/m}\}.

If
t\geq R_{2}
, then the elementary bound
1-\tau/2\geq e^{-\tau}
,
\tau\in[0,1]
, shows that

|r(te^{i\varphi})|\geq 1-\frac{|a|}{t^{m}}-\frac{c^{\prime}}{t^{m+1}}\geq 1-\frac{2|a|}{t^{m}}\geq e^{-4|a|/t^{m}}.

Hence the first inequality in (
3.16
) is true with with
b_{2}=4|a|
and
t\geq R_{2}
as in (
3.23
).
So (
3.16
) holds with
R=\max(R_{1},R_{2},1)
.
∎

Given an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function
r
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
define

r_{n}(z):=r^{n}(z/n).

For
\varphi\in[-\psi,\psi]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
s\geq 0
,
\epsilon\geq 0
and
R>0
, let

(3.24)

\displaystyle Q^{\epsilon}_{R}(\varphi,n,s):=\int_{Rn}^{\infty}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{r_{n}(te^{i\varphi})}{(te^{i\varphi}+\epsilon)^{s}}\right)\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n^{s}}\int_{R}^{\infty}\left|n(ue^{i\varphi}+\epsilon)r^{\prime}(ue^{i\varphi})-sr(ue^{i\varphi})\right|\,|r^{n-1}(ue^{i\varphi})|\,\frac{du}{|ue^{i\varphi}+\epsilon|^{s+1}},

by a change of variable
t=ne^{i\varphi}u
.

The next rather technical lemma is basic for obtaining our improvements of approximation rates
for rational approximations.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational function such that
|r(\infty)|=1
,
and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
and
T>0
.
Then there exist
R\geq 1
and
C_{1}=C_{1}(\theta,T)>0
and
C_{2}=C_{2}(\theta,\epsilon,T)>0

such that for all
\varphi\in[-\theta,\theta]
,
s\in[0,T]
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.25)

Q^{0}_{R}(\varphi,n,s)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{n^{(1+m^{-1})s}},

and, for every
\epsilon\geq 0
,

(3.26)

Q^{\epsilon}_{R}(\varphi,n,s)\geq\frac{C_{2}}{n^{(1+m^{-1})s}}.

Proof.

Let
b_{1}
,
b_{2}
and
R\geq 1
be as in Lemma
3.9
. Let
\varphi\in[-\theta,\theta]
, and
s\geq 0
. Note that
|r(te^{i\varphi})|\leq 1
and, from (
1.5
), that

(3.27)

|r^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{c_{\theta}}{|z|^{m+1}},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\theta}.

With the changes of variables
\tau=t^{-m}
and
v=(n-1)\tau
for
n\geq 2
in (
3.24
),
we can estimate as follows for
\epsilon=0
:

\displaystyle Q^{0}_{R}(\varphi,n,s)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n^{s}}\int_{R}^{\infty}\,(c_{\theta}nt^{-m}+s)|r^{n-1}(te^{i\varphi})|\,\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{mn^{s}}\int_{0}^{1/R^{m}}(c_{\theta}n\tau+s)\,e^{-b_{1}(n-1)\tau}\tau^{s/m-1}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\theta,m,b_{1})}{n^{s}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(n\tau+s)\tau^{s/m-1}\,e^{-b_{1}(n-1)\tau}\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\theta,m,b_{1})}{(n-1)^{s/m}n^{s}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(2v+s)v^{s/m-1}\,e^{-b_{1}v}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{C(\theta,m,b_{1})(2+mb_{1})}{b_{1}}\frac{\Gamma(1+s/m)}{b_{1}^{s/m}{(n-1)^{s/m}n^{s}}},

where
\Gamma
stands for the gamma function. For
T>0
, this estimate is bounded by an expression of the form on the right-hand side of (
3.25
), where
C_{1}
is independent of
s\in[0,T]
and
n\geq 2
.

For
n=1
, we have

Q^{0}_{R}(\varphi,1,s)\leq\frac{1}{m}\int_{0}^{1/R^{m}}(c_{\theta}\tau+s)\tau^{s/m-1}\,d\tau,

which is easily seen to be finite and bounded for
s\in[0,T]
, so there is an upper bound as in (
3.25
), valid for all
\varphi\in[-\theta,\theta]
,
s\in[0,T]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Now we will justify the lower bound in (
3.25
). Let
\epsilon\geq 0
be fixed. There exists
\tilde{c}_{\theta}>0
such that

(3.28)

|r^{\prime}(z)|\geq\frac{\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{|z|^{m+1}},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\theta},\quad|z|\geq R.

Let
T>0
, and

R_{T}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{c}_{\theta}}{2T}\right)^{1/m}.

Let
s\in[0,T]
.
Let
n\geq(R/R_{T})^{m}
and
z=te^{i\varphi}
, where
|\varphi|<\theta
and
R_{T}n^{1/m}\leq t\leq 2R_{T}n^{1/m}
. Then

n|z+\epsilon|\geq n|z|\,|r^{\prime}(z)|\geq 2T\geq 2s.

Using (
3.28
), (
3.16
) the inequalities

e^{-b_{2}(n-1)/t^{m}}\geq e^{-b_{2}/R_{T}^{m}},\qquad t\geq R_{T}n^{1/m},

and

|te^{i\varphi}+\epsilon|\leq(1+\epsilon)t,\qquad t\geq 1,

we infer that

\displaystyle(1+\epsilon)^{s+1}Q^{\epsilon}_{R}(\varphi,n,s)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{n^{s}}\int_{R_{T}n^{1/m}}^{2R_{T}n^{1/m}}\,(nt|r^{\prime}(te^{i\varphi})|-s)|r^{n-1}(te^{i\varphi})|\,\frac{dt}{t^{s+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2n^{s}}\int_{R_{T}n^{1/m}}^{2R_{T}n^{1/m}}\,|r^{\prime}(te^{i\varphi})||r^{n-1}(te^{i\varphi})|\,\frac{dt}{t^{s}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2n^{s}}\int_{R_{T}n^{1/m}}^{2R_{T}n^{1/m}}e^{-b_{2}(n-1)/t^{m}}\,\frac{dt}{t^{s+m+1}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{2n^{s}}\int_{R_{T}n^{1/m}}^{2R_{T}n^{1/m}}e^{-b_{2}/R_{T}^{m}}\,\frac{dt}{t^{s+m+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1-2^{-(s+m)}\right)e^{-b_{2}/R_{T}^{m}}}{2(s+m)R_{T}^{s+m}}\,n^{-(1+m^{-1})s}.

By taking the minimum value of the term for
s\in[0,T]
, we obtain a lower bound of the form (
3.26
) for all but a finite set of
n
. For each of the remaining values of
n
, the function

(\varphi,s)\mapsto n^{(1+m^{-1})s}Q^{\epsilon}_{R}(\varphi,n,s)

is continuous on
[-\theta,\theta]\times[0,T]
, so it attains a positive minimum value.
Thus a lower bound as in (
3.25
) can be obtained for all
\varphi\in[-\theta,\theta]
,
s\in[0,T]
, and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
∎

For an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function
r
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\geq 0
, we define

\Delta_{n,s}(z)=\frac{e^{-z}-r_{n}(z)}{z^{s}},\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\psi},\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

A somewhat tedious proof of the next auxiliary estimate is postponed to the Appendix.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
r
be an
A(\psi)
-stable rational approximation of order
q
to
the exponential, and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
and
R>0
. Then there exist
C>0
and

\alpha>0
depending on
r,\theta
and
R
such that

(3.29)

\left|\Delta_{n,s}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq C\left(q+1-s+|z|\right)\frac{|z|^{q-s}}{n^{q}}e^{-\alpha|z|}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
z\in\Sigma_{\theta}
,
|z|<Rn
, and
s\in[0,q+1]
.

We also need the following estimate, under the assumptions of Lemma
3.11
and the additional assumption that
R\geq 1
:

(3.30)

\displaystyle\int_{Rn}^{\infty}\left|\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{e^{-te^{i\varphi}}}{(te^{i\varphi}+\epsilon)^{s}}\right)\right|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{Rn}^{\infty}\left(\frac{s}{t^{s+1}}+\frac{1}{t^{s}}\right)e^{-t\cos\varphi}\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{q+2}{\cos\varphi}e^{-Rn\cos\varphi}.

Remark 3.12
.

It should be noted that Lemmas
3.9
and
3.10
as well as the estimate in (
3.30
) apply in exactly the same form for the shifts of
r
, i.e., the functions
r_{\epsilon}(z)=r(z+\epsilon)
, with possibly different values for
C=C(\epsilon)
depending on
\epsilon
.

Lemmas
3.10
and
3.11
lead to the following function-theoretical result
on rational approximations, which after applying the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus,
will transform into a statement on rational approximation rates
for holomorphic semigroups.

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational
approximation of order
q
to
the exponential, and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
Then the following hold.

(i)

If
|r(\infty)|=1
, then there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that

(3.31)

\|\Delta_{n,s}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{\delta_{s}}}

for all
s\in[0,q+1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\delta_{s}:=\min\,\{(1+m^{-1})s,q\}
.

(ii)

If
|r(\infty)|<1
, then there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that

(3.32)

\|\Delta_{n,s}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\leq\frac{C}{n^{q}}

for all
s\in[0,q+1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

We start with a proof of (i). Let
s\in[0,q+1]
be fixed.
Let
R\geq 1
be given by Lemma
3.10
with
T=q+1
, and fix
C
and
\alpha
given
by Lemma
3.11
for that value of
R
.
Using (
3.29
), (
3.30
) and Lemma
3.10
,
we obtain
that

(3.33)

\displaystyle\|\Delta_{n,s}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{n^{q}}\int_{0}^{Rn}(q+1-s+t)t^{q-s}e^{-\alpha t}\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\hbox{}+2\int_{Rn}^{\infty}\left|\Delta^{\prime}_{n,s}(te^{i\theta})-r^{\prime}_{n,s}(te^{i\theta})\right|\,dt+2\int_{Rn}^{\infty}\,|r_{n,s}^{\prime}(te^{i\theta})|\,dt

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{n^{q}}\int_{0}^{\infty}(q+1-s+t)t^{q-s}e^{-\alpha t}\,dt

\displaystyle\hskip 15.0pt\hbox{}+\frac{2(q+2)}{\cos\theta}e^{-Rn\cos\theta}+2Q^{0}_{R}(\theta,n,s)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(\frac{1}{n^{q}}+\frac{1}{n^{(1+m^{-1})s}}\right)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{n^{\delta_{s}}},

for appropriate constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, so (i) is proved.

Now assume that
|r(\infty)|<1
. We use the same approach as for (i), taking
R=1
in Lemma
3.11
. We follow the argument for (i) but Lemma
3.10
is inapplicable, and we need to estimate
Q_{1}^{0}(\varphi,n,s)
in a different way. By the assumptions and the maximum principle, we observe that, for

with
|z|>1
,

(3.34)

|r(z)|\leq\kappa,\qquad|r^{\prime}(z)|\leq\frac{c_{0}}{|z|^{2}},

for some
\kappa\in(|r(\infty)|,1)
,
c_{0}=c_{0}(\theta)
.
Setting

and using (
3.34
) in (
3.24
), we infer that there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that for every
\varphi\in(0,\theta)
,

Q_{1}^{0}(\varphi,n,s)\leq\frac{\kappa^{n-1}}{n^{s}}\int_{n}^{\infty}\left(\frac{c_{0}n^{2}}{t^{s+2}}+\frac{s}{t^{s+1}}\right)\,dt\leq\frac{C}{n^{q}}.

Applying this estimate to replace Lemma
3.10
and Lemma
3.11
with
R=1
,
and arguing as in (
3.33
),
we obtain (
3.32
).
∎

4.
Stability of operator approximations

Now we are ready to formulate our stability estimates for rational approximations
of (sectorially) bounded holomorphic semigroups.
Since the negative generators of such semigroups coincide with sectorial operators of angle less
than
\pi/2,
we employ the language of sectorial operators,
and deduce our results as direct corollaries
of the
\mathcal{H}
-calculus, using (
2.8
) along with Theorems
3.6

and
3.7
. Firstly, we obtain
the following general statement
on stability of variable stepsize rational approximations first proved in
[
3
]

and containing a well-known, constant-step stability result from
[
9
]
,
[
24
]
(see also
[
29
]
and
[
17
]
).
Recall that for a finite sequence
\mathcal{K}_{n}:=(k_{j})_{j=1}^{n}\subset(0,\infty)
, we let
K_{0}=\min\{k_{j}\}
and
K_{1}=\max\{k_{j}\}
.
The proof is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.6
and (
2.8
).

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function, where
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
, and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
Then there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that,
for every operator
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in(0,\theta)
and every finite sequence
\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset(0,\infty)
,

\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}r(k_{j}A)\bigg\|\leq CM_{\theta}(A)\left(1+\log\left(\frac{K_{1}}{K_{0}}\right)\right).

In particular, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\|r^{n}(A/n)\right\|\leq CM_{\theta}(A).

Secondly, our methods allow us to strengthen substantially
a well-known result on variable stepsize approximation due to Palencia
[
27
]
and Bakaev
[
2
]
(reproved in
[
4
]
) and to obtain the next statement.
It is an immediate consequence of Theorem
3.7
and (
2.8
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational function with
|r(\infty)|<1
, where
\psi\in(0,\pi/2]
, and let
\theta\in(0,\psi)
.
Then there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that,
for every operator
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in(0,\theta)
and every finite sequence

\mathcal{K}_{n}\subset(0,\infty)
,

\bigg\|\prod_{j=1}^{n}r(k_{j}A)\bigg\|\leq CM_{\theta}(A).

The assumptions in
[
2
]
and
[
27
]
(and in
[
4
]
) included not only that
|r(\infty)|<1
, but also that
r
approximates
e^{-z}
with order
1
. This assumption is indispensable in their proofs which are rather technical. For results related to Theorem
4.2
see also
[
5
]

where the first order of approximation also appears to be crucial.

5.
Rates for operator approximations

The
\mathcal{H}
-calculus also leads to sharp estimates of approximation rates,
in particular to a refined version of Theorem
1.1
.
It might appear to be a mere improvement of Theorem
1.1
,
taking into account fine structure of
r
.
However Theorem
5.1
(i) gives a substantially better estimate than Theorem
1.1
(i)
when
s\in(0,q)

(and it is essentially vacuous when
s=0
).
Theorem
5.1
(ii) for
s=0
gives the result in Theorem
1.1
(ii),
and extends it slightly for smooth vectors
x
.
Moreover, the bounds given by Theorem
5.1
are optimal as we prove in Section
7
.
The proofs are of a common form, based on the
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-calculus
and its compatibility with the extended holomorphic calculus.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational
approximation of order
q
, and
let
\varphi\in(0,\psi)
and
\theta\in(\varphi,\psi)
.
Then there exists a constant
C=C(\theta)
such that the following holds for all operators
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\varphi)
for some
\varphi\in[0,\theta)
:

(i)

If
|r(\infty)|=1
,
s\in[0,q+1]
, and
\delta_{s}:=\min\{(1+m^{-1})s,q\}
, then for all
x\in{\rm dom}(A^{s})
,
t>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.1)

\|e^{-tA}x-r^{n}(tA/n)x\|\leq\frac{CM_{\theta}(A)t^{s}}{n^{\delta_{s}}}\|A^{s}x\|.

(ii)

If
|r(\infty)|<1
and
s\in[0,q+1]
, then for all
x\in{\rm dom}(A^{s})
,
t>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(5.2)

\|e^{-tA}x-r^{n}(tA/n)x\|\leq\frac{CM_{\theta}(A)t^{s}}{n^{q}}\|A^{s}x\|.

Proof.

Replacing
A
by
tA
for
t>0
, we may assume that
t=1
.
For
s\in[0,q+1]
, let
g_{s}(z)=z^{-s}
and

\Delta_{n}(z):=e^{-z}-r_{n}(z),\qquad z\in\Sigma_{\psi}.

Then
g_{s}\Delta_{n}=\Delta_{n,s}\in\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, the extended holomorphic calculus can be applied to the invertible operator
A+\varepsilon
.
In that calculus,
g_{s}(A+\varepsilon)=(A+\varepsilon)^{-s}
in the standard theory of fractional powers.
Moreover,
g_{s}
is in the domain of the extended
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-calculus for
A+\varepsilon
,
and
\Delta_{n}(A+\varepsilon)=e^{-(A+\varepsilon)}-r_{n}(A+\varepsilon)
in both calculi. Then

\Delta_{n,s}(A+\varepsilon)=(A+\varepsilon)^{-s}\big(e^{-(A+\varepsilon)}-r_{n}(A+\varepsilon)\big)

in the extended holomorphic calculus. Since the two calculi are compatible (see
[
6
, Proposition 8.1(iv)]
) this also holds in the
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}
-calculus. Hence, for
x\in\operatorname{dom}{(A^{s})}=\operatorname{dom}((A+\varepsilon)^{s})
,

\displaystyle\big\|\big(e^{-(A+\varepsilon)}-r_{n}(A+\varepsilon)\big)x\big\|

\displaystyle=\left\|\Delta_{n,s}(A+\varepsilon)(A+\varepsilon)^{s}x\right\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Delta_{n,s}(A+\varepsilon)\|\,\|(A+\varepsilon)^{s}x\|.

If
s>0
, then we can use the estimates from Theorem
3.13
and (
2.8
) to obtain (
5.1
) or (
5.2
) with
A
replaced by
A+\varepsilon
. Letting
\varepsilon\to 0
, and using the relation that
\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}M_{\theta}(A+\varepsilon)=M_{\theta}(A)
and
[
17
, Proposition 3.1.9 c)]
, we obtain (
5.1
) or (
5.2
) for
A
. For
s=0
,
\Delta_{n,0}(\infty)=-r(\infty)
and there is an additional term
|r(\infty)|^{n}
in the estimate for
\|\Delta_{n,0}(A+\varepsilon)\|
. This can be covered by changing the constant
C
.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

The case
s=0
of Theorem
5.1
(ii) shows that for every
t\geq 0
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|e^{-tA}-r^{n}(tA/n)\|=0.

Let
\rho_{1}(z)=(z+1)^{-1}
.
Then
\rho_{1}
is
\mathcal{A}(\pi/2,1)
-stable rational approximation of order
1
, with
\rho_{1}(\infty)=0
.
If
A\in{\rm Sect(\pi/2)}
then
-A
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
, and it is known that
\rho_{1}^{n}(tA/n)
converges strongly to
T(t)
for each
t\geq 0
(
[
12
, Corollary III.5.5]
).
Hence
T(t)=e^{-tA}
.

The following corollary provides a natural example of a function
r
satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
5.1
(i).

Corollary 5.3
.

Let
r=P_{k}/Q_{k}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, be the diagonal Padé approximation of order
2k
to

e^{-z}
, and let
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\varphi),\varphi\in[0,\pi/2)
and
\theta\in(\varphi,\pi/2)
.
Then
there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that, for all
s\in[0,q+1]
,

,

and

where

Proof.

By
[

(or

), the diagonal Padé approximations of

are
\mathcal{A}(\pi/2)

6.
Optimality in terms of the algebra
\mathcal{H}_{\psi}

In this and the next section we address the optimality of Theorem
5.1
,
and show that the approximation rates provided by this theorem are sharp.
We define

(6.1)

\Delta_{n,s,1}(z):=\Delta_{n,s}(z+1),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximation. Then the following hold.

(i)

If
r
is of exact order
q
,
then there exists
C>0

such that, for all
\theta\in(0,\psi)
,
s\in[0,q+1]
and

,

(6.2)

\|\Delta_{n,s}\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\theta,0}}\geq|\Delta_{n,s}(1)|\geq\frac{C}{n^{q}}.

(ii)

If
r
is of order
q
and

|r(\infty)|=1
, then
there exists
C=C(\theta)
such that, for all
\theta\in(0,\psi)
,
s\in[0,q+1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(6.3)

\|\Delta_{n,s,1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\geq\frac{C}{n^{(1+m^{-1})s}}.

Proof.

The first inequality in (
6.2
) is immediate from (
2.5
),
so we only need to prove the second inequality.
Since
r
is of exact order
q
, there exists

such that

r(z)=e^{-z}+az^{q+1}+\mbox{O}(|z|^{q+2}),\qquad z\to 0.

Let

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle:=r(z)e^{z}-1-az^{q+1}=\mbox{O}(|z|^{q+2}),

\displaystyle h(z)

\displaystyle:=r(z)e^{z}-1=g(z)+az^{q+1}=\mbox{O}(|z|^{q+1}).

There exist
R>0
and
c
such that

and

are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}_{R}
and

(6.4)

|g(z)|\leq c|z|^{q+2},\qquad|h(z)|\leq\min\{\tfrac{1}{2},c|z|^{q+1}\},\qquad z\in\mathbb{D}_{R}.

Let
z\in\mathbb{D}_{R}
and

\eta(z)=\log(1+h(z))-az^{q+1},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}_{R}.

Using the elementary inequality

|\log(1+\zeta)-\zeta|\leq|\zeta|^{2},\qquad|\zeta|\leq 1/2,

for

, we obtain

|\eta(z)|\leq|h(z)|^{2}+|g(z)|\leq c_{1}|z|^{q+2},

and

Now let

and

,
so that

. Then

\Delta_{n,s}(z)=\frac{e^{-z}-r_{n}(z)}{z^{s}}=\frac{e^{-z}(1-e^{az^{q+1}/n^{q}}e^{\eta_{n}(z)})}{z^{s}},

Setting

in (

) and letting

,
we see that

, and
we infer that

\displaystyle|\Delta_{n,s}(1)|=e^{-1}|1-\exp\left(a/n^{q}+\eta_{n}(1)\right)|=\frac{|a|}{en^{q}}+\mbox{O}(n^{-(q+1)}),\quad n\to\infty,

uniformly for

.
Thus (
6.2
) holds.

Now we will prove (
6.3
) under the additional assumption that
|r(\infty)|=1
.
Let
s\in[0,q+1]
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Using (

) with

, we note that

\|\Delta_{n,s,1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\geq Q_{1}^{1}(\theta,n,s)-\frac{q+2}{\cos\theta}e^{-Rn\cos\theta}.

Taking into account Remark
3.12
and applying the bound (

and

in place of

,
we infer that there exists
C=C(\theta)>0

such that

\|\Delta_{n,s,1}\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\theta,0}}\geq\frac{C}{n^{(1+m^{-1})s}}.

Thus (
6.3
) holds.
∎

For
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
s\geq 0
, and

Let
r

Let

7.
Optimality for semigroup generators

The estimates in Theorem
5.1
for semigroup generators are optimal, as we will show in this section.
We start by showing that Theorem
5.1
(ii) is optimal. Given the estimate in Theorem
6.1
(ii), the optimality of (
5.2
) is shown in the following trivial example.

Example 7.1
.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi)
-stable rational approximation of exact order
q
,
and let
A
be the identity operator
I
on any Banach space
X
, so
A
is sectorial of angle
0
.
Then
A^{s}
is the identity operator and
\Delta_{n,s}(A)
is the operator of multiplication by the scalar
\Delta_{n,s}(1)
on
X
.
Thus it is immediate from Theorem
6.1
(i) that there exists
C>0
such that

\|(e^{-A}-r^{n}(A/n))x\|=|\Delta_{n,s}(1)|\,\|A^{s}x\|\geq\frac{C}{n^{q}}\|x\|

for all
s\in[0,q+1]
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
x\in X
. Thus the estimate (
5.2
) in Theorem
5.1
is optimal for semigroup generators (up to a multiplicative constant).

Next we will show that Theorem
5.1
(i) is optimal, by first considering a specific example of a semigroup of multiplication operators.

Example 7.2
.

For
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, let
\mathcal{L}f
be the Laplace transform of
f
. Consider the Banach space

X=\mathcal{L}L_{1}:=\big\{\mathcal{L}f:f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\big\},

with the norm given by

(7.1)

\|\mathcal{L}f\|_{X}:=\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},\qquad\mathcal{L}f\in X,

cf. (
2.1
).
For
t\geq 0
, let

(T(t)\mathcal{L}f)(z)=e^{-tz}(\mathcal{L}f)(z),\qquad\mathcal{L}f\in X,\;z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

The map
f\mapsto\mathcal{L}f
is a surjective isometry from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to
X
which transforms the
C_{0}
-semigroup of right shifts on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
into the multiplication operators on
X
.
Let
-D
be the generator of
(T(t))_{t\geq 0}
.
By the HP-calculus, for all
\mathcal{L}f\in X
and
\mathcal{L}\mu\in\mathcal{LM}
,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{L}\mu(D)\mathcal{L}f)(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}(T(t)\mathcal{L}f)(z)\,d\mu(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zt}\mathcal{L}f(z)\,\,d\mu(t)=\mathcal{L}\mu(z)\mathcal{L}f(z)=\mathcal{L}(f*\mu)(z).

Then, by
[
10
, Proposition 4.7.43(i)]
(cf. (
2.2
)),

(7.2)

\|\mathcal{L}\mu(D)\|=\|\mathcal{L}\mu\|_{\mathcal{LM}}.

For
\mathcal{L}f\in X
and
z,\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
,

\big((\lambda+D)^{-1}\mathcal{L}f\,\big)(z)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}e^{-zt}(\mathcal{L}f)(z)\,dt=\frac{(\mathcal{L}f)(z)}{\lambda+z}.

Hence
D
is of the form

(7.3)

\displaystyle{\rm dom}(D)

\displaystyle=\big\{\mathcal{L}f\in X:\,z(\mathcal{L})f(z)\in X\big\},

\displaystyle(D\mathcal{L}f)(z)

\displaystyle=z(\mathcal{L}f)(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

The considerations above are variations of the elementary
transference principle considered in
[
18
, Example 5.2]
, for example.

Let
\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
,
\gamma=2\theta/\pi
and
A:=D^{\gamma}
,
where
D
is given by (
7.3
).
By the extended HP (or holomorphic) functional calculus,

A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
, and

(Af)(z)=z^{\gamma}f(z),\qquad f\in X,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}_{+}.

Let
r
be an
\mathcal{A}(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximation of order
q
with
|r(\infty)|=1
,
and let
s\in[0,q+1]
.
By the composition rule for the extended holomorphic calculus
[
18
, Theorem 2.4.2]
,

\Delta_{n,s,1}(A)={\Delta}^{[\gamma]}_{n,s,1}(D).

It follows from Corollary
6.2
that

(7.4)

\|\Delta_{n,s,1}(A)\|=\|\Delta^{[\gamma]}_{n,s,1}(D)\|=\|\Delta^{[\gamma]}_{n,s,1}\|_{\mathcal{LM}}\geq\frac{C}{n^{\delta_{s}}}

for some
C=C(\theta)>0

and all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
s\in[0,q+1]
, where
\mathbf{\delta_{s}=}\min\,\{(1+m^{-1})s,q\}
.

Next we show that (
5.1
) is optimal when
|r(\infty)|=1
and
(1+m^{-1})s<q
. The case when
s\in[mq/(m+1),q+1)
has already been covered in Example
7.1
.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
r
be an
A(\psi,m)
-stable rational approximation of order
q
with
|r(\infty)|=1
.
Then for every
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset(0,\infty)

and

\mathcal{X}
, an operator
\mathcal{A}\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)
on
\mathcal{X}
,

\|e^{-\mathcal{A}}x-r^{n}(\mathcal{A}/n)x\|\geq C\alpha_{n}n^{-(1+m^{-1})s},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we can assume that
(\alpha_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset(0,1/2)
.
Let
\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
be fixed, and let
X
and
A\in\operatorname{Sect}(\theta)

be as in Example
7.2
.

Define a Banach space
\mathcal{X}=c_{0}(X)
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}:=

\displaystyle\{(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subset X:\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\,\|x_{n}\|=0\},

\displaystyle\|x\|_{\mathcal{X}}:=

\displaystyle\sup_{n\geq 1}\,\|x_{n}\|,\qquad x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{X},

and consider the linear operator

\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{X}

given by

\displaystyle{\rm dom}(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle:=\{(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{X}:x_{n}\in{\rm dom}(A),\,\,(Ax_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathcal{X}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}x

\displaystyle:=((A+I)x_{n})_{n\geq 1},\qquad x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\rm{dom}(\mathcal{A}).

It is straightforward to show that

.

Let

.
By Example

there exists
C=C(\theta)>0
such that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\|\Delta_{n,s,1}(A)\|\geq{C}{n^{-(1+m^{-1})s}}.

Hence there exist

satisfying

, the operators

Moreover, the operators
\mathcal{A}^{-s}

Let

Thus

.
Using the product rule for

we infer that, for every

\|\Delta_{n}(\mathcal{A})x\|_{\mathcal{X}}\geq\|\Delta_{n}(A+I)x_{n}\|=\|\Delta_{n,1}(A)x_{n}\|\\
=\|\Delta_{n,s,1}(A)y_{n}\|\geq 2^{-1}C\alpha_{n}n^{-(1+m^{-1})s}.\qed

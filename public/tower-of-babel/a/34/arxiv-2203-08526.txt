Cycle Integrals of the Parson Poincaré Series and Intersection Angles of Geodesics on Modular Curves
Alessandro Lägeler and Markus Schwagenscheidt

Abstract
. We prove a geometric formula for the cycle integrals of Parson’s weight 2 ​ k 2k modular integrals in terms of the intersection angles of geodesics on modular curves. Our result is an analog for modular integrals of a classical formula for the cycle integrals of certain hyperbolic Poincaré series, due to Katok. On the other hand, it extends a recent geometric formula of Matsusaka and Duke, Imamoḡlu, and Tóth for the cycle integrals of weight 2 modular integrals.

2.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The proof is similar to the proof of Katok’s formula (
1.2

[
6

Up to interchanging
\gamma
with
\gamma^{-1}
and
\sigma
with
\sigma^{-1}
we may assume that the lower left entries of
\gamma
,
\sigma
are positive. First, we rewrite

\int_{\sigma^{n}.z_{0}}^{\sigma^{n+1}.z_{0}}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz=\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}(c_{n}z+d_{n})^{-2k}F_{k,\gamma}(\sigma^{n}.z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz,

where we put
\sigma^{n}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
c_{n}&d_{n}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. A direct computation shows that

(2.1)

\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}\circ\sigma^{-n}(1,0))\to\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))\;\;\mathrm{as}\;n\to+\infty;

see
[
9
, Lemma 2.7]
. If the two geodesics
S_{\gamma}
and
S_{\sigma}
intersect, we have
\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))=-\mu_{p}(\gamma,\sigma)
.
Now the Parson Poincaré series becomes

\displaystyle(c_{n}z+d_{n})^{-2k}F_{k,\gamma}(\sigma^{n}.z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{D_{\gamma}^{k-1/2}}{\pi}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)}{(c_{n}z+d_{n})^{2k}(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(\sigma^{n}.z,1)^{k}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{D_{\gamma}^{k-1/2}}{\pi}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}\circ g\sigma^{-n}))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}

\displaystyle\to-\frac{D_{\gamma}^{k-1/2}}{\pi}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}},\;\mathrm{as}\;n\to+\infty,

where in the last line we applied (
2.1
). The resulting series is modular of weight
2k
for the group
\Gamma_{\sigma}=\{\pm\sigma^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. Hence, the cycle integral

\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

is independent of the choice of
z_{0}
.

Now a typical unfolding argument yields

\displaystyle\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma/\Gamma_{\sigma}}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\sigma^{m}.z_{0}}^{\sigma^{m+1}.z_{0}}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma/\Gamma_{\sigma}}\int_{S_{\sigma}}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz.

Subtracting the complex conjugate from the last equation, gives the sum of integrals

\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma/\Gamma_{\sigma}}\displaystyle\int_{C(\sigma)}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

over the circle
C(\sigma)
through the roots of
Q_{\sigma}(z,1)=0
. The integrands are all meromorphic, with poles only at the real roots of
(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)
. Hence, by Cauchy’s theorem, they vanish if the geodesic connecting the roots of
(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)
does not intersect
S_{\sigma}
. Thus, we are left with

\frac{D_{\gamma}^{k-1/2}}{\pi}\sum_{p\in[S_{\gamma}]\cap[S_{\sigma}]}\mu_{p}(\gamma,\sigma)\displaystyle\int_{C(\sigma)}\frac{1}{(Q\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz,

as
\mu_{p}(\gamma,\sigma)=-\mathrm{sign}((Q\circ g)(w_{\sigma}^{\prime},1))
if the geodesics
S_{\sigma}
and
S_{g^{-1}\gamma g}
intersect.

The integrals evaluate to

\displaystyle\int_{C(\sigma)}\frac{1}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz=2\pi iD_{\gamma}^{-k/2}D_{\sigma}^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\mu_{p}^{k}P_{k-1}(\cos\theta_{p});

see
[
6

. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.1

2.2.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

The proof is a careful application of
[
6
, Lemma 2]
, which asserts that for odd
k\in\mathbb{Z},k\geq 3
, and any
A,B,C\in\mathbb{R}
with
D=B^{2}-4AC>0
, we have

(2.2)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k-1}}{(-At^{2}+Bit+C)^{k}}dt=\begin{cases}0,&AC>0,\\
(-1)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}\mathrm{sign}(A)2\pi D^{-k/2}P_{k-1}\left(\frac{B}{\sqrt{D}}\right),&AC<0.\end{cases}

Consider the integral
\displaystyle\int_{-\frac{d}{c}}^{i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)(cz+d)^{k-1}dz
. A standard argument gives that

\int_{-\frac{d}{c}}^{i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)(cz+d)^{k-1}dz=\left(\frac{c}{2\pi}\right)^{k}\Gamma(k)\frac{i^{k}}{c}L_{\gamma,k}\left(k,-\frac{d}{c}\right).

The imaginary part of the integral is equal to

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\int_{-\frac{d}{c}}^{i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)(cz+d)^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle=c^{k-1}\mathrm{Im}\int_{0}^{i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}\left(z-\frac{d}{c}\right)z^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle=-c^{k-1}\frac{D_{\gamma}^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{\pi}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma}\mathrm{Im}\left(\int_{0}^{i\infty}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)z^{k-1}}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z-d/c,1)^{k}}dz\right),

where we can exchange sum and integral by Fubini’s theorem, as the integrand is absolutely convergent.

Fix
g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma
for the moment and write
Az^{2}+Bz+C
for
(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)
. The quadratic form

(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z-d/c,1)=Az^{2}+\left(B-2Ad/c\right)z+\left(A\left(d/c\right)^{2}-Bd/c+C\right)=A^{\prime}z^{2}+B^{\prime}z+C^{\prime}

intersects with the non-compact geodesic
S_{-d/c}
if and only if

A^{\prime}C^{\prime}=A\left(A\left(d/c\right)^{2}-Bd/c+C\right)<0.

With (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{Im}\left(\int_{0}^{i\infty}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(A^{\prime})z^{k-1}}{\left(A^{\prime}z^{2}+B^{\prime}z+C^{\prime}\right)^{k}}dz\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{\frac{k-1}{2}}}{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\mathrm{sign}(A^{\prime})t^{k-1}}{\left(-A^{\prime}t^{2}+B^{\prime}it+C^{\prime}\right)^{k}}dt

\displaystyle=-\pi D_{\gamma}^{-k/2}P_{k-1}\left(\frac{B^{\prime}}{\sqrt{D_{\gamma}}}\right)

if and only if the geodesic
S_{g\gamma g^{-1}}
intersects the non-compact geodesic
S_{-d/c}
associated to the quadratic form
cz+d
. Otherwise, the integral evaluates to zero by (
2.2
). By
[
11
, Proposition 2.2]
, the intersection angle
\theta_{p}
between these two geodesics is given by
\cos\theta_{p}=\frac{Bc-2Ad}{c\sqrt{D_{\gamma}}}
. This finishes the proof.

3.

In this section, we present some further properties and possible applications of the homogenized cycle integrals and periods of Parson’s modular integrals
F_{k,\gamma}(z)
.

3.1.
Explicit representation of the cycle integral

As the Parson Poincaré series is no longer modular, the cycle integral
\displaystyle\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz
depends on the choice of the point
z_{0}
and is a complicated function in terms of
z_{0}
. Only when taking the homogenization we get a conjugacy class invariant object independent of the choice of
z_{0}
, which has the nice representation on the right hand side of (
1.8
).

Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz=\int_{i\infty}^{\sigma.i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle+\sum_{n=0}^{2k-2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\setminus\Gamma,\\
w_{g\gamma g^{-1}}^{\prime}<\sigma^{-1}.i\infty<w_{g\gamma g^{-1}}\end{subarray}}\rho_{n,g,\gamma,\sigma}(z_{0})\;_{2}F_{1}\left(k,2k-1-n;2k;1-\frac{z_{0}-w_{Q}}{z_{0}-w_{Q}^{\prime}}\right),

where
\rho_{n,g,\gamma,\sigma}(z_{0})
is a rational function given by
\rho_{n,g,\gamma,\sigma}(z_{0})=\frac{\Gamma(2k-n-1)}{\Gamma(2k)}\frac{\partial_{z}^{n}Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}|_{z=z_{0}}}{(z_{0}-w_{g\gamma g^{-1}}^{\prime})^{n-2k-1}}
. One can also see from this representation that the integral converges as
z_{0}\to w_{\sigma}
.

To prove this, we use

\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz=\int_{i\infty}^{\sigma.i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz+\int_{i\infty}^{z_{0}}r_{k,\gamma}(\sigma,z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz.

This follows from differentiating both sides in
z_{0}
and observing that both sides are equal at
z_{0}\to i\infty
. Rewriting the polynomial
Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{2k-2}a_{n,\sigma}(z_{0})(z-z_{0})^{n}
in its Taylor expansion about
z_{0}
, we obtain a finite sum of integrals

\int_{i\infty}^{z_{0}}r_{k,\gamma}(\sigma,z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz=\sum_{n=1}^{2k-2}a_{n,\sigma}(z_{0})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\sim Q_{\gamma},\\
w_{Q}^{\prime}<\sigma^{-1}.i\infty<w_{Q}\end{subarray}}\int_{z_{0}}^{i\infty}\frac{(z-z_{0})^{n}}{(z-w_{Q})^{k}(z-w_{Q}^{\prime})^{k}}dz,

which can be solved. Standard integral transformations give

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{z_{0}}^{i\infty}\frac{(z-z_{0})^{n}}{(z-w_{Q})^{k}(z-w_{Q}^{\prime})^{k}}dz=\\
&\frac{\Gamma(2k-n-1)\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(2k)}(z-w_{Q}^{\prime})^{n-2k+1}\ _{2}F_{1}\left(k,2k-n-1,2k;1-\frac{z-w_{Q}}{z-w_{Q}^{\prime}}\right).\end{split}

That the right hand side of (
3.1
) is symmetric in
w_{Q}^{\prime}
and
w_{Q}
also follows from the identity
\ {}_{2}F_{1}(c-a,b,c;z/(z-1))=(1-z)^{b}\ _{2}F_{1}(a,b,c;z)
.

The integral evaluation (
3.1
) can also be used to prove that the weight
2-2k
cocycle

\displaystyle R_{\gamma}(\sigma,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{(-2\pi i)^{2k-1}D^{k-1/2}}{\pi(2k-1)!\binom{2k-2}{k-1}}\sum_{w_{Q}^{\prime}<-\frac{d}{c}<w_{Q}}\frac{1}{|Q(1,0)|(z-w_{Q}^{\prime})}\ _{2}F_{1}\bigg(k,1,2k;1-\frac{z-w_{Q}}{z-w_{Q}^{\prime}}\bigg)

\displaystyle+\frac{(-2\pi i)^{2k-1}}{(2k-2)!}\frac{i}{c^{2k-1}}\sum_{n=0}^{2k-2}\binom{2k-2}{n}i^{n}\left(\frac{c}{2\pi}\right)^{n+1}\Gamma(n+1)L_{k,\gamma}(n+1,a/c)(cz+d)^{n}

for
\sigma=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\Gamma
and
Q
running over the equivalence class
[Q_{\gamma}]
is a
(2k-1)
-th primitive of
r_{\gamma}(g,z)
, i.e.
\mathcal{D}^{2k-1}R_{\gamma}(g,z)=r_{\gamma}(g,z)
with
\mathcal{D}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
. The Fourier coefficients

can be explicitly calculated as in
[

, Theorem 3]
.

3.2.
Periods of modular integrals

In this subsection we let
\Gamma=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Kohnen and Zagier
[
8
]
studied the periods of the cusp forms
f_{k,\gamma}(z)
defined in (
1.1
), and showed that certain linear combinations of these periods are rational numbers.

A natural follow-up question to Theorem
1.1
would be to study the periods of the Parson Poincaré series, i.e.
p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(it)t^{n}\;dt
for
0\leq n\leq 2k-2
. As in
[
8
]
we define the symmetrizations

F_{k,\gamma}^{+}=F_{k,\gamma}+F_{k,\gamma^{\prime}}(z),\qquad F_{k,\gamma}^{-}=i(F_{k,\gamma}(z)-F_{k,\gamma^{\prime}}(z)),

where
\gamma^{\prime}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\gamma\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. We split the period polynomial

p(F_{k,\gamma})(z)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{i\infty}F_{k,\gamma}(z)(x-z)^{2k-2}dz=\sum_{n=0}^{2k-2}i^{-n+1}\binom{2k-2}{n}p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma})x^{2k-2-n}

of
F_{k,\gamma}
into its even and odd part as
p(F_{k,\gamma})=ip^{+}(F_{k,\gamma})+p^{-}(F_{k,\gamma})
with

\displaystyle p^{+}(F_{k,\gamma})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq n\leq 2k-2\\
n\text{ even}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{n/2}\binom{2k-2}{n}p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma})x^{2k-2-n},

\displaystyle p^{-}(F_{k,\gamma})(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<n<2k-2\\
n\text{ odd}\end{subarray}}(-1)^{(n-1)/2}\binom{2k-2}{n}p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma})x^{2k-2-n}.

By closely following the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 5]
, one obtains

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&p^{+}(F_{k,\gamma}^{+})(x)+p^{-}(F_{k,\gamma}^{-})(x)\\
&\quad\doteq-2\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a,b,c]\in[Q_{\gamma}]\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}(ax^{2}-bx+c)^{k-1}-\frac{2D^{k-1/2}\zeta_{Q_{\gamma}}(k)}{\binom{2k-2}{k-1}(2k-1)\zeta(2k)}(x^{2k-2}-1),\end{split}

where
\zeta_{Q_{\gamma}}(s)
is the
\zeta
-function associated with
Q_{\gamma}
as in
[
8
, p. 222]
,
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann
\zeta
-function, and
\doteq
means equality up to a non-zero multiplicative constant
5
5

5

The formulas (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) are correct if we normalize
F_{k,\gamma}
as in
[
8
]
. Since our normalization of
F_{k,\gamma}
is different, we get some simple but unpleasant extra factors.
.
In particular, the periods
p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma}^{+})
for even
0<n<2k-2
and the periods
p_{n}(F_{k,\gamma}^{-})
for odd
0<n<2k-2
are rational.

We let
F_{k,D}(z)=\sum_{D_{\gamma}=D}F_{k,\gamma}(z)
where the sum ranges over a system of representatives
\gamma
of the conjugacy classes of primitive hyperbolic elements in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with discriminant
D_{\gamma}=D
. From (
3.2
) it follows that for odd
k\in\mathbb{Z}
we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}p^{+}(F_{k,D})&\doteq-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}[a,b,c]\in\mathcal{Q}_{D}\\
a<0<c\end{subarray}}(ax^{2}+bx+c)^{k-1}-\frac{D^{k-1/2}\zeta(k)L_{D}(k)}{\binom{2k-2}{k-1}(2k-1)\zeta(2k)}(x^{2k-2}-1),\end{split}

where
L_{D}(s)
is the Dirichlet
L
-function associated to the Kronecker symbol
\left(\frac{D}{\cdot}\right)
. Formula (
3.3
) is the analog of
[
8

. In particular, the periods
p_{n}(F_{k,D})
for even
0<n<2k-2
are rational and satisfy the symmetry
p_{2k-2-n}(F_{k,D})=p_{n}(F_{k,D})
.

3.3.
The homogenized cycle integral as an ”inner product”

By unfolding and
[
7
, Proposition 7]
, one can also show that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\sigma^{n}.z_{0}}^{\sigma^{n+1}.z_{0}}F_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz

\displaystyle\qquad\doteq\int_{\Gamma\setminus\mathbb{H}}\sum_{g\in\Gamma_{\gamma}\backslash\Gamma}\sum_{h\in\Gamma_{\sigma}\backslash\Gamma}\frac{\mathrm{sign}((Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(w_{h\sigma h^{-1}}^{\prime},1))}{(Q_{\gamma}\circ g)(z,1)^{k}(Q_{\sigma}\circ h)(\overline{z},1)^{k}}y^{2k}\frac{dxdy}{y^{2}}.

Since
w_{h\sigma h^{-1}}^{\prime}=h^{-1}.w_{\sigma}^{\prime}
, the integrand is
\Gamma
-invariant.

One should compare this with the fact that the cycle integral
\displaystyle\int_{z_{0}}^{\sigma.z_{0}}f_{k,\gamma}(z)Q_{\sigma}(z,1)^{k-1}dz
is up to constants equal to the Petersson inner product
\langle f_{k,\sigma},f_{k,\gamma}\rangle
of the hyperbolic Poincaré series (
1.1
).

3.4.
Equidistribution of intersection angles

A possible application of Theorem
1.1
could be to give another proof the fact that the intersection angles
\theta_{p}
of a fixed geodesic
[S_{\gamma}]
with the geodesics of discriminant
D
(for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
) equidistribute to the measure
\frac{1}{2}\sin\theta\;d\theta
as
D\to+\infty
. This was conjectured by Rickards
[
11
, Conjecture 4.2]
and recently proved by Jung and Sardari
[
5
]
. To show this, it suffices to prove that
\cos\theta_{p}
equidistribute to the Lebesgue measure on
[-1,1]
. The Legendre polynomials
P_{k-1}
with
k\geq 1
form a complete orthonormal system of the space of continuous functions on
[-1,1]
. For
k>1
even, the Weyl sum on the right hand side of (
1.2
) can be estimated using the Shimura-theory of cusp forms
[
7
]
and non-trivial bounds on the Fourier coefficients of half-integral weight cusp forms
[
4
]
. A Siegel-type bound for the number of intersections can be obtained using (
1.5
),
[
9
, Theorems 3.3 and 4.7, Remark 4.8]
, and some elementary considerations on continued fractions. For
k>1
odd, this approach does not work due to the appearance of the
\mu_{p}
-factor, so the Weyl sum here is given by the right hand side in (
1.8
). However, it appears to be difficult to estimate the traces of the homogenized cycle integrals. We plan to come back to this in the future.

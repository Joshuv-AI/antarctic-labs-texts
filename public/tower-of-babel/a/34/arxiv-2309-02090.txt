Abstract
. In this paper, we deal with the notions of naturality from category theory and definablity from model theory and their interactions. In this regard, we present three results. First, we show, under some mild conditions, that naturality implies definablity. Second, by using reverse Easton iteration of Cohen forcing notions, we construct a transitive model of ZFC in which every uniformisable construction is weakly natural. Finally, we show that if F F is a natural construction on a class 𝒦 \mathcal{K} of structures which is represented by some formula, then it is uniformly definable without any extra parameters. Our results answer some questions by Hodges and Shelah.

In this paper, naturality is in the sense of Eilenberg and Mac Lane

\mathcal{K}
of structures, the constriction
A\mapsto F(A)
, for

A\in\mathcal{K}
and every automorphism

of

of

such that the following diagram is commutative

\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{a}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{F(A)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f(a)}
\textstyle{A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}

The challenging initial example for naturality was the dual of vector spaces, see

[
3
]
.
In
[
7
, page 11]

Lambek observed that divisible hulls of
abelian groups are not natural. Despite this, he pointed out that divisible hulls of torsion-free
abelian groups are natural, as it is left adjoint to the inclusion functor.

Hodges and Shelah
[

observed that the construction of
algebraic closure of fields and the construction of divisible hulls are not natural. Also, they showed that
any left adjoint to any functor gives naturality. This may be considered as a source to

According to
[
1

in a category with injective hulls and a cogenerator, the injective hulls is not natural, unless all objects are injective.

Naturality says that there is an splitting for
{\rm Aut}(F(A))\to{\rm Aut}(A)

[
9

Hodges and Shelah
[
8
]
asked:

Question 1.1

When does naturality imply definablity?

We use two-sorted models in order to deal with the question. This approach is not new. For example,
Harvey Friedman
[
4
]

already has considered some constructions in algebra (e.g., direct product construction of pairs of groups) as operations from relational

many sorted relational type.
Also, Hodges and Shelah
[
9

interpreted Question

In sum,

In §2, and for the convenience of the readers, we review the concept of many sorted models.
According to its definition, any 2-sorted model
{\mathfrak{B}}
gives us two groups

If the restriction map

The new concept of uni-construction problem will play the role of weak naturality. Then we introduce the concept of solvability (see Definition

Concerning Question
1.1
, in §3, we discuss some special kinds of 3-sorted models, and prove the following as our first main result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\chi
be a cardinal and let
{\mathbf{c}}
be a uni-construction problem. If
{\mathbf{c}}
has no lifting, then in a forcing extension,
{\mathbf{c}}
has no

\chi

In
[
9

, Hodges and Shelah constructed

(See
[

The cardinality restriction can be removed.

In §4 we settle the conjecture, by iterating the main

[

using reverse Easton iteration of suitable Cohen forcing notions. It may be nice to note that
the use of forcing techniques in the naturality problems
comes back to
Friedman
[

where he applied Easton product Cohen-forcing, though the term naturalness in
[

Let
M
be a model of set theory.
Suppose some formula represents the construction

on the class
\mathcal{K}
in

(ii)

Recall that left adjoint defines a natural construction.

2.
The uni-construction problem

In this section we define the concepts of uni-construction problem, lifting, weak lifting and solvability.
We use many sorted models, to unify and simplify our treatment.

Let us start by defining
n
-sorted model structures, where
n\geq 2
is a natural number.
We only work with finite structures.

Definition 2.1
.

An
n
-sorted model structure
\mathcal{M}
is of the form

\mathcal{M}=\big(\{M_{1};\ldots;M_{n}\};R_{1};\ldots;R_{m};f_{1};\ldots;f_{k};c_{1};\ldots;c_{l}\big)

where

(a)

the universes
M_{1};\ldots;M_{n}
are nonempty,

(b)

the relations
R_{1};\ldots;R_{m}
are between elements of the universes. In other words, for each
i
, there is
some sequence
\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{s}\rangle
from
\{1,\ldots n\}
such that

R_{i}\subseteq M_{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times M_{i_{s}},

(c)

the functions
f_{1};\ldots;f_{k}
are between elements of the universes. In other words, for each
i
,
there is some sequence
\langle i_{1},\ldots,i_{s},r\rangle
from
\{1,\ldots n\}
such that

f_{i}:M_{i_{1}}\times\ldots\times M_{i_{s}}\to M_{r},

(d)

the distinguished constants
\{c_{1};\ldots;c_{l}\}
are in the universes.

Notation 2.2
.

Given an
n
-sorted model structure
\mathcal{M}
as above, for any
1\leq i\leq n,
there is a natural model structure with the universe
M_{i}
, that we denote by
{\rm sort}_{i}(\mathcal{M})
.

In what follow, we only work with the concepts of 2-sorted and 3-sorted model structures.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
H
be a group which is not necessarily abelian.

i)

By
\mathcal{Z}(H)
we mean the center of
H
.

ii)

Since
\mathcal{Z}(H)
is a normal subgroup of
H
, it induces a group structure on
H/\mathcal{Z}(H)
. By
\pi_{H}:H\rightarrow H/\mathcal{Z}(H)
we mean the natural group homomorphism from
H
onto
H/\mathcal{Z}(H)
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
H
and
G
be two groups, which are not necessarily abelian.
Assume
\varphi:H\twoheadrightarrow G
is an surjective homomorphism of groups.

1)

We say
\psi\in{\rm Hom}(G,H)
splits
\varphi
, when
\varphi\circ\psi={\rm id}_{G}
.

2)

We say
\psi
weakly splits
\varphi
provided:

(a)

\psi
is a function from
G
into
H
,

(b)

\varphi\circ\psi={\rm id}_{G}
,

(c)

the composite mapping
\pi_{H}\circ\psi
belongs to

{\rm Hom}(G,H/\mathcal{Z}(H))
,

(d)

\psi(x^{-1})=(\psi(x))^{-1}
and
\psi(1_{G})=1_{H}
.

3)

We say
\varphi
splits (resp. weakly splits), if some
\psi

splits (resp. weakly splits) it.

We now define the concept of uni-construction problem, which plays a key role in this paper.

Definition 2.5
.

1) We say
{\mathbf{c}}
is a uni-construction problem (
{\rm ucp}
in short),
when

{\mathbf{c}}=\langle{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}},{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}},H_{{\mathbf{c}}},K_{{\mathbf{c}}},G_{{\mathbf{c}}},\varphi_{{\mathbf{c}}},\psi_{{\mathbf{c}}}\rangle=\langle{\mathfrak{B}},{\mathfrak{A}},H,K,G,\varphi,\psi\rangle

and it satisfies the following
conditions:

(a)

{\mathfrak{B}}
is a two-sorted model,

(b)

{\mathfrak{A}}:={\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}}),

(c)

H={\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{B}}),K=\mathcal{Z}(H)
and
G={\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{A}})
,

(d)

\varphi
is the natural restriction map from
H
into
G
,
i.e.
\varphi(f)=f{\restriction}{\mathfrak{A}}
, furthermore,
\varphi
is a group homomorphism:

\textstyle{{\mathfrak{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\scriptstyle{f{\restriction}{\mathfrak{A}}}
\textstyle{{\mathfrak{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{{\mathfrak{B}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{f}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{B}}

(e)

\varphi
is onto
G
. So, for any
g:{\mathfrak{A}}\to{\mathfrak{A}}
there is an
f
such that the following diagram commutes

\textstyle{{\mathfrak{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{{\mathfrak{A}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\subseteq}
\textstyle{{\mathfrak{B}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\exists{f}}
\textstyle{\mathfrak{B}}

(f)

\psi

.

2) We say
{\mathbf{c}}
is a weak uni-constructive problem, if
{\mathbf{c}}
is as above and it satisfies items (a)-(e) above.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
{\mathbf{c}}
be a uni-construction problem.
Then the classes
K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
and
K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
are defined as follows:

(a)

K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}:=\{{\mathfrak{A}}:{\mathfrak{A}}
isomorphic
to
{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}}\}
,

(b)

K^{2}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}:=\{{\mathfrak{B}}:{\mathfrak{B}}
isomorphic to
{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}}\}
.

Remark 2.7
.

The assignment
{\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle F}}{{\mapsto}}{\mathfrak{B}}
on the domain of
K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
is not in general
single-valued; but by clause (e) from Definition
2.5
it is single-valued up to isomorphism over

{\mathfrak{B}}
. In particular, suppose

{\mathfrak{A}}\cong{\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}})
. Then

F({\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}}))={\mathfrak{B}}\cong F({\mathfrak{A}}).

Definition 2.8
.

Let
{\mathbf{c}}
be a uni-construction problem.

(a)

We say
{\mathbf{c}}
is solvable, when there is a class function
F:K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}\to K^{2}_{{\mathfrak{c}}}
, such that for each

{\mathfrak{B}}\in K^{2}_{{\mathfrak{c}}},

F({\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}}))={\mathfrak{B}}
.

(b)

Let
F
be as in clause (a). We say
F

is
definable, if there is a formula
\theta
such that

F({\mathfrak{A}})={\mathfrak{B}}\Leftrightarrow\theta({\mathfrak{A}},{\mathfrak{B}}),

for all two sorted models
{\mathfrak{B}}
with
{\mathfrak{A}}:={\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{B}})
.

(c)

We say
{\mathbf{c}}
is purely solvable, when there is an
F
as above which is definable using only

({\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}},{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}})
as a parameter.

(d)

We say
{\mathbf{c}}
is
\chi
-solvable, when there is
F
as above, which is
definable by some parameter
a\in{\mathscr{H}}(\chi)
.

Let us present with the following easy observation.

Observation 2.9
.

Let
{\mathbf{c}}
and
{\mathbf{d}}
be two uni-construction problems, satisfying the following conditions:

(a)

{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}}={\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{d}}}
,

(b)

{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{d}}}
expands
{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
.

If
{\mathbf{d}}
is solvable, then
{\mathbf{c}}
is solvable.

Proof.

Assume
{\mathbf{d}}
is solvable, and let

F_{{\mathbf{d}}}:K^{1}_{{\mathbf{d}}}\to K^{2}_{{\mathbf{d}}}
witness it. Let
{\mathfrak{A}}\in K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
. Since
K^{1}_{{\mathbf{d}}}=K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
, we can define
F_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\mathfrak{A}})
.
Let
F_{{\mathbf{c}}}({\mathfrak{A}})
be the reduction of
F_{{\mathbf{d}}}({\mathfrak{A}})
into the language of
{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
. Then

F_{{\mathbf{c}}}:K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}}\to K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}}
is well-defined, and it witnesses that
{\mathbf{c}}
is solvable.
∎

3.
From uni-construction to naturality

The main result of this section is Theorem
3.9
, which shows that for a given cardinal
\chi,
if a uni-construction
problem has no lifting, then it has no
\chi
-solution in some forcing extension of the universe.

Hypothesis 3.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}
be a 3-sorted model
such that
{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2},{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3},{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}
are weak
uni-construction problems, where:

(1)

{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}={\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{C}}),{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}={\rm sort}_{1,2}({\mathfrak{C}})
,

(2)

{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}={\rm sort}_{1,2}({\mathfrak{C}}),{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}={\mathfrak{C}}
,

(3)

{\mathfrak{A}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}={\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{C}}),{\mathfrak{B}}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}={\mathfrak{C}}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(Transitivity)

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}
be as in Hypothesis
3.1
.
If
{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}
and
{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}
are solvable, then
{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}
is solvable.

Proof.

According to Definition
2.6

there are definable class functions

F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}:K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}\to K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}
, and

F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}:K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}\to K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}
.

Recall from Hypothesis

that the following three equalities are satisfied

(i)

K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}={\rm sort}_{1}({\mathfrak{C}})=K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}
,

(ii)

K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}={\rm sort}_{1,2}({\mathfrak{C}})=K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}
,

(iii)

K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}={\mathfrak{C}}=K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}
.

This enables us to get the composition
F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}=F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}\circ F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}
.
Let us summarize things in the following commutative diagram:

\textstyle{K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}}
\scriptstyle{=}
\textstyle{K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}}
\textstyle{K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}}
\textstyle{K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{=}
\textstyle{K^{2}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{=}
\textstyle{K^{1}_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}}

Then the function

witnesses that
{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}
is solvable.
∎

Conclusion 3.3
.

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}
be as in Hypothesis

and let

If
{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}

.

Let
{\mathfrak{C}}

{\mathbf{c}}_{1,2}

Fix an infinite cardinal

.
As
\varphi_{{\mathbf{c}}_{2,3}}
has a lifting, and in the light of

-solvable.

\varphi_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}
has no weak lifting, thus by

, we can find a generic extension

of the universe, in which
\varphi_{{\mathbf{c}}_{1,3}}

-solvable. In view of Conclusion

Definition 3.5
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be a group, and let
\psi
be an automorphism of
G^{\prime}
.
Let
G
be a
(\mathbb{Z},G^{\prime})
-construction.
We are going to use the multiplicative structure of
\mathbb{Z}
. In this regard, we identify
\mathbb{Z}
with
\{y^{n}:n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
. For simplicity, we denote
(y^{n},x)
with
y^{n}x
.
So, as a set we have

G:=\{y^{n}x:n\in\mathbb{Z}\emph{and}x\in G^{\prime}\}.

The multiplication of
x\in G^{\prime}
from left hand side is defined via the role

x\times y:=y\psi(x)\in G.

An easy induction shows that

x\times y^{n}:=y^{n}\psi^{n}(x)\in G
, and consequently,
(G,\times)
becomes a group, denoted by
\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\psi}G^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be two groups and suppose there is an onto homomorphism
\varphi_{1,2}:G_{2}\twoheadrightarrow G_{1}
that has no lifting but has a weak lifting.
Then, there are
G_{3}
and
\varphi_{2,3}
, such that:

(a)

G_{3}
is a group;

(b)

\varphi_{2,3}:G_{3}\twoheadrightarrow G_{2}
is a surjective homomorphism
which has a lifting;

(c)

\varphi_{1,3}=\varphi_{1,2}\circ\varphi_{2,3}\in{\rm Hom}(G_{3},G_{2})
is a surjective homomorphism with no weak lifting.

Proof.

(a):
First, we introduce the auxiliary group
G^{\prime}_{3}:=\bigoplus\{G_{2,n}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, where for each

G_{2,n}\cong G_{2}
. Let also
\psi_{n}

denote the corresponding isomorphism
\psi_{n}:G_{2}\to G_{2,n}

(1)

(2)

G_{2,n}\cap\Sigma\{G_{2,k}:k\in{\mathbb{Z}}\backslash\{n\}\}=\{1_{G^{\prime}_{3}}\}
, and

(3)

the family of groups

\{G_{2,n}:n\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}

Let
\psi_{*}
be the automorphism of
G^{\prime}_{3}
such that, for each

\psi_{*}{\restriction}G_{2,m}=\psi_{m+1}\circ\psi^{-1}_{m}.

Set
G_{3}:=\mathbb{Z}\ltimes_{\psi_{*}}G_{3}^{\prime}
. Recall from Definition

is generated by

, subject to the following relations

(b):
Let
\varphi_{2,3}:G_{3}\rightarrow G_{2}
be the unique homomorphism
from
G_{3}
onto
G_{2}
such that:

\bullet_{1}

\varphi_{2.3}{\restriction}G_{2,n}=\psi^{-1}_{n}\text{ for }n\in{\mathbb{Z}}

\bullet_{2}

\varphi_{2.3}(y)=1_{G_{2}}

Now

, i.e. clause (b)

(c)
: In order to prove clause (c), we first prove the following claim.

Claim 3.7
.

G_{3}
has trivial center.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in G^{\prime}_{3}\backslash\{1_{G^{\prime}_{3}}\}
. Clearly

). Suppose

. By Definition

,

has the form

with

and

n\neq 0\vee x_{1}\neq 1_{G^{\prime}_{3}}
. Let
x_{2}\in G^{\prime}_{2}\backslash\{1_{G^{\prime}_{2}}\}
be such that

\ell:=\min({\rm supp}(x_{2}))>\max({\rm supp}(x_{1}))+n.

Suppose on the way of contradiction that
y^{n}x_{1}
and

commute with each other. This yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}y^{n}(x_{1}x_{2})&=(y^{n}x_{1})x_{2}\\
&=x_{2}(y^{n}x_{1})\\
&=x_{2}(yy^{n-1}x_{1})\\
&=(x_{2}y)y^{n-1}x_{1}\\
&=(y\psi_{*}(x_{2}))y^{n-1}x_{1}\\
&=y^{n}(\psi_{*}^{n}(x_{2})x_{1}).\end{array}

By definition, this implies

\psi_{*}^{n}(x_{2})x_{1}=x_{1}x_{2}\quad(+)

Recall that
\psi_{*}{\restriction}G_{2,m}=\psi_{m+1}\circ\psi^{-1}_{m}
.
By applying this to

, we see some coordinate of its left hand side is nonzero, but the corresponding coordinate in the right hand side is nonzero.
This contradiction shows that
G_{3}
has trivial center, as claimed.
∎

By definition
\varphi_{1,3}\in{\rm Hom}(G_{3},G_{2})
is onto. We show that it has no weak lifting. Suppose towards contradiction that there is a function
\psi:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{3}
such that
\varphi_{1,3}\circ\psi={\rm id}_{G_{1}}

and the composite mapping
\pi_{H}\circ\psi
belongs to

{\rm Hom}(G_{1},G_{3}/\mathcal{Z}(G_{3}))
, where

\pi_{H}:G_{3}\rightarrow G_{3}/\mathcal{Z}(G_{3})
is the canonical homomorphism.
But, according
to Claim

,

G_{3}
has trivial center.
So
\psi
is a homomorphism from
G_{1}
into
G_{3}
, hence
\varphi_{2,3}\circ\psi
is a homomorphism from
G_{1}
into
G_{2}
; in fact, is one
to one as
\varphi_{1,3}\circ\psi
is. This contradicts our assumption that

\varphi_{1,2}
has no lifting.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\chi
be a cardinal.
Suppose

is onto, and does not split. Then there exists a weak uni-construction problem

,
such that in some forcing extension,

{\mathbf{c}}

-solvable.

Proof.

Let
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
be as above and set
\varphi_{1,2}:=\varphi
. By Proposition

, we can find a group

and two homomorphisms

and
\varphi_{1,3}
, which fit in the following diagram

\textstyle{G_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{1,2}}
\textstyle{G_{1}}
\textstyle{G_{3}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{2,3}\rightsquigarrow\emph{withnoweaklifting}}
\scriptstyle{\emph{withlifting}\rightsquigarrow\varphi_{1,3}}

Without loss of generality  the groups
G_{1},G_{2}

and
G_{3}

are pairwise disjoint. We define the three sorted model
{\mathfrak{C}}
as

(*)

(a)

the set of elements of

is the set of

Now, we show that the assumptions of Corollary

hold.
To this end, note that we consider the sorts only as a set of elements and forget about

. The entire structure is given by the functions.
We claim that

(i)

and
G_{3}\cong{\rm Aut}({\mathfrak{C}})

(ii)

For clause (i), we only show that

In order to see

to

\varphi_{2,3}(c)&\mbox{if }g\in G_{2}\\
\varphi_{1,3}(c)&\mbox{if }g\in G_{1}\end{array}\right.

is well-defined. Let

. First we show that

is a homomorphism of
{\mathfrak{C}}
. Consider

, and show it behaves well with respect to

\sigma_{c}(F^{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{a,3}(b))=c\cdot b\cdot a=\sigma_{c}(b)\cdot a=F^{{\mathfrak{C}}}_{a,3}(\sigma_{c}(b)),

To prove clause (ii),

In the light of Proposition

∎

&=g_{2,s,s(*)}(b_{2,s(*)})\\

i.e.,

, from which the equivalence

∎

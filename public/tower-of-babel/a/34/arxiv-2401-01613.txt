Synthetically enhanced sensitivity using higher-order exceptional point and coherent perfect absorption
Yao-Dong Hu, Yi-Pu Wang, Rui-Chang Shen, Zi-Qi Wang, Wei-Jiang Wu, J. Q. You

Abstract
Sensors play a crucial role in advanced apparatuses and it is persistently pursued to improve their sensitivities. Recently, the singularity of a non-Hermitian system, known as the exceptional point (EP), has drawn much attention for this goal. Response of the eigenfrequency shift to a perturbation ϵ \epsilon follows the ϵ 1 / n \epsilon^{1/n} -dependence at an n n th-order EP, leading to significantly enhanced sensitivity via a high-order EP. However, due to the requirement of increasingly complicated systems, great difficulties will occur along the path of increasing the EP order to enhance the sensitivity. Here we report that by utilizing the spectral anomaly of the coherent perfect absorption (CPA), the sensitivity at a third-order EP can be further enhanced owing to the cooperative effects of both CPA and EP. We realize this synthetically enhanced sensor using a pseudo-Hermitian cavity magnonic system composed of two yttrium iron garnet spheres and a microwave cavity. The detectable minimum change of the magnetic field reaches 4.2 × 10 − 21 4.2\times 10^{-21} T. It opens a new avenue to design novel sensors using hybrid non-Hermitian quantum systems.

Pseudo-Hermitian cavity magnonic system

2.1
Experimental setup

The cavity magnonic system consists of a three-dimensional (3D) rectangular cavity (
50\times 25\times 7.5~\rm{mm}^{3}

The power ratio and phase difference of the two signals are controlled by a variable attenuator (VA) and a variable phase shifter (VPS), respectively. Here, the CPA is realized by precisely adjusting the VA and VPS to achieve zero output signals from the cavity. The dissipation rates of the cavity ports are tuned by changing the lengths of antenna pins inserted into the cavity (

2.2

The non-Hermitian Hamiltonian of the system with CPA can be written, in the rotating frame associated with the cavity frequency (details in

H/\hbar=\left(\begin{matrix}i\kappa_{c}&g_{1}&g_{2}\\
g_{1}&\Delta_{1}-i\gamma_{1}&0\\
g_{2}&0&\Delta_{2}-i\gamma_{2}\\
\end{matrix}\right),

(2)

\kappa_{c}\equiv\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{\rm int}>0

(

\gamma_{1}
(
\gamma_{2}

In the symmetric case, we have two identical YIG spheres (

\Omega^{3}+c_{1}\Omega+c_{0}=0,

3.2
Perturbation-induced eigenfrequency shift

The perturbation of the external magnetic field can induce frequency change of the Kittel mode in each YIG sphere. In our experiment, we use two identical YIG spheres, so these frequency changes can be nearly identical. By considering the magnetic-field perturbation, the pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian, i.e., the Hamiltonian (
3
) with
\Delta=\sqrt{g^{2}-\gamma^{2}}
, can be converted to

H^{\prime}/\hbar=\begin{pmatrix}2i\gamma&g&g\\
g&\sqrt{g^{2}-\gamma^{2}}+\Delta_{B}-i\gamma&0\\
g&0&-\sqrt{g^{2}-\gamma^{2}}+\Delta_{B}-i\gamma\\
\end{pmatrix},

(18)

where
\Delta_{B}
is the frequency change of the Kittel mode induced by the magnetic-field perturbation. From the eigenvalue equation
{\rm Det}\left(H^{\prime}-\Omega^{\prime}I\right)=0
, we can obtain the relation between the eigenvalue
\Omega^{\prime}(\Delta_{B})\equiv\omega^{\prime}(\Delta_{B})-\omega_{c}
and the frequency change
\Delta_{B}
, which is

\frac{2\Delta_{B}-\Omega^{\prime}}{g^{2}}+\frac{4\left(g^{2}-\gamma^{2}\right)\left(\Omega^{\prime}-\Delta_{B}\right)}{\left[{\left(\Omega^{\prime}-\Delta_{B}\right)^{2}+g^{2}}\right]^{2}-4\left(g^{2}-\gamma^{2}\right)\left(\Omega^{\prime}-\Delta_{B}\right)^{2}}=0,

(19)

where
\Omega^{\prime}(\Delta_{B})\equiv\omega^{\prime}(\Delta_{B})-\omega_{c}
reduces to the eigenvalue
\Omega\equiv\omega-\omega_{c}
of the pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian when
\Delta_{B}=0
.

For a sufficiently small perturbation
\Delta_{B}
, we can ignore the higher-order terms
O\left(\Delta_{B}^{2}\right)
and write the relation between
\Delta_{B}
and
\Omega^{\prime}
as

\Delta_{B}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime 3}\left(\Omega^{\prime 2}+g^{2}\right)-\left(3g^{2}-4\gamma^{2}\right)\left(\Omega^{\prime 2}+g^{2}\right)\Omega^{\prime}}{g^{4}+2\Omega^{\prime 2}\left(\Omega^{\prime 2}+2g^{2}\right)-\left(3g^{2}-4\gamma^{2}\right)\left(4\Omega^{\prime 2}+g^{2}\right)}.

(20)

Based on equation (
20
), we can study the response of the eigenfrequency to the perturbation.

We define the perturbation-induced eigenfrequency shift as

\Delta_{\omega}\equiv\omega^{\prime}\left(\Delta_{B}\right)-\omega=\Omega^{\prime}\left(\Delta_{B}\right)-\Omega.

(21)

Here we focus on the
central
branch of the eigenvalues of the pseudo-Hermitian Hamiltonian, i.e.,
\Omega=\Omega^{(0)}=0
[cf. equation (
6
)].

(i) At the EP3, i.e.,
g=g_{\rm EP3}\equiv 2\gamma/\sqrt{3}
, equation (
20
) reduces to

\Delta_{B}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime 3}\left(\Omega^{\prime 2}+g_{\rm EP3}^{2}\right)}{g_{\rm EP3}^{4}+2\Omega^{\prime 2}\left(\Omega^{\prime 2}+2g_{\rm EP3}^{2}\right)}.

(22)

For a small perturbation, we can assume that
\Omega^{\prime}
is close to
\Omega=\Omega^{(0)}=0
such that
\Omega^{\prime}\ll g
. Then, it follows from equation (
22
) that

\Delta_{B}=\frac{\Omega^{\prime 3}}{g_{\rm EP3}^{2}}=\frac{\Delta_{\omega}^{3}}{g_{\rm EP3}^{2}},

(23)

i.e.,

\Delta_{\omega}=g_{\rm EP3}^{2/3}{\Delta_{B}}^{1/3},

(24)

where the
{\Delta_{B}}^{1/3}
dependence is characteristic of the EP3.

From equation (
24
), we obtain the sensitivity factor at the EP3:

G_{\rm EP3}\equiv\left(\frac{\Delta_{\omega}}{\Delta_{B}}\right)_{\rm EP3}=\frac{g_{\rm EP3}^{2/3}}{{\Delta_{B}}^{2/3}}.

(25)

It tends to infinity when
\Delta_{B}\rightarrow 0
, indicating the singular behavior at the EP3. In figure
3
(d), we numerically show the relation between the sensitivity factor
{G}_{\rm EP3}
and the perturbation
\Delta_{B}
.

(ii) Away from the EP3, there is
g\neq g_{\rm EP3}\equiv 2\gamma/\sqrt{3}
. For a small perturbation, we can still assume that
\Omega^{\prime}\ll g
and then equation (
20
) is reduced to

\Delta_{B}=-\frac{\left(3g^{2}-4\gamma^{2}\right)\Omega^{\prime}}{g^{2}-\left(3g^{2}-4\gamma^{2}\right)},

(26)

which gives a
linear
dependence of the eigenfrequency shift on the perturbation
\Delta_{B}
,

\Delta_{\omega}=\left(1-\frac{g^{2}}{3g^{2}-4\gamma^{2}}\right)\Delta_{B}.

(27)

Figure
3
(c) shows the frequency shift
\Delta_{\omega}
of the central eigenvalue versus
|\Delta_{B}|
. Theoretically, at the EP3, this eigenfrequency shift is
\Delta_{\omega}=g^{2/3}\Delta_{B}^{1/3}
, while
\Delta_{\omega}\sim\Delta_{B}
away from the EP3. These theoretical results fit well with the experimental data. The sensitivity factor at the EP3 [figure

G_{\rm EP3}\equiv\left(\frac{\Delta_{\omega}}{\Delta_{B}}\right)_{\rm EP3}=g^{2/3}\Delta_{B}^{-2/3}.

(28)

Compared with the case away from the EP3, the eigenvalue shift
\Delta_{\omega}

\Delta_{B}
, our system has been tuned to exhibit the CPA, and it can exhibit nearly CPA under a small
\Delta_{B}
. In decibels, the output spectrum of the system with CPA has an extremely sharp dip, but a less sharp dip occurs in the system nearly with CPA [cf. figure
3
(e)].In the present experiment, we focus on the central branch of the three eigenvalues, i.e.,
\Omega=\Omega^{(0)}=0
. We can define a sensitivity factor related to the CPA:

G_{\rm{CPA}}\equiv 2\pi\frac{\Delta|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}|^{2}}{\Delta_{\omega}}=\frac{10{\rm log_{10}}|{S^{\prime}}_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(\Delta_{\omega})|^{2}-10{\rm log_{10}}|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(0)|^{2}_{\rm CPA}}{(\Delta_{\omega}/2\pi)},

(34)

where
|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(0)|^{2}_{\rm CPA}
is the minimum value of the output spectrum in the presence of CPA, where
\Omega=0
, and
|{S^{\prime}}_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(\Delta_{\omega})|^{2}
is the minimum value of the corresponding output spectrum under the perturbation
\Delta_{B}
, where
\Omega^{\prime}=\Delta_{\omega}
.
Because
|{S^{\prime}}_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(\Delta_{\omega})|^{2}\neq 0
and
|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(0)|^{2}_{\rm CPA}=0
,
10\log_{10}|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(0)|^{2}_{\rm CPA}\rightarrow-\infty
, giving rise to
G_{\rm{CPA}}\rightarrow\infty
in the ideal case. However, in a practical experiment,
10\log_{10}|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}(0)|^{2}_{\rm{CPA}}
cannot reach
-\infty
, but it can reach
-91.5
dB here at the EP3, resulting from the limited measurement precision of the VNA and the limited adjusting accuracy of the system parameters. Nevertheless, this minimum value can still produce a significantly large enhancement factor
G_{\rm CPA}
at the EP3 [cf. figure
3
(f)], especially in the case of small
\Delta_{\omega}
.

3.4
Synthetic sensitivity in the presence of both CPA and EP3

Based on the experimental data of the output spectra, we obtain the observed changes of the minimum values of the output spectra
\Delta|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}|^{2}
at the EP3 versus the perturbation
\Delta_{B}
, as shown in figure
4
(a). From this relationship between
\Delta|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}|^{2}
and
\Delta_{B}
, we can define the synthetically enhanced sensitivity of the system in the presence of both CPA and EP3,
G_{\rm syn}\equiv 2\pi\Delta|S_{\rm tot}^{({\rm min})}|^{2}/\Delta_{B}
, which is readily given by
G_{\rm syn}=G_{\rm CPA}G_{\rm EP3}
. Figure
4
(b) shows the obtained
G_{\rm syn}
versus the perturbation
\Delta_{B}
, in comparison with the corresponding
G_{\rm CPA}
and
G_{\rm EP3}
. Because the enhancements related to the CPA and EP3 are combined in a
product
form, the synthetic enhancement factor becomes significantly greater than the respective enhancement factors [figure
4
(b)]. This clearly demonstrates the distinct advantage of using both CPA and EP3 in a single non-Hermitian system to produce a sensor with high sensitivity.

3.5
Detectable minimum change of the magnetic field

Finally, we discuss the limit of the minimum magnetic-field change that can be detected by the current synthetically enhanced sensor. For the YIG sphere, the relation between the applied magnetic field and the frequency of the uniform magnon mode is

\omega_{m}=\gamma_{e}|\vec{B}|+\omega_{m,0},

(35)

where
\gamma_{e}/2\pi=28
GHz/T denotes the gyromagnetic ratio and
\omega_{m,0}
is determined by the anisotropy field.
In the synthetically enhanced sensor, the adjustment of the magnetic field is limited by the smallest change of the current supply, i.e.,
1\times 10^{-4}
A, in our electromagnet. The measured frequency change of the magnon mode is
\Delta_{B}/2\pi=(\omega^{\prime}_{m}-\omega_{m})/2\pi\approx 0.025~{\rm MHz}
, where
\omega^{\prime}_{m}
(
\omega_{m}
) is the frequency of the magnon mode with (without) the perturbation. It follows from
\Delta_{B}=\gamma_{e}\delta{B}
that the corresponding
magnetic-field change is
\delta{B}\approx 9\times 10^{-7}
T.

According to equation (
24
), the frequency shift of the eigenvalue at EP3 is

\Delta_{\omega}={g_{\rm EP3}}^{2/3}{\Delta_{B}}^{1/3}=\left(2\gamma/\sqrt{3}\right)^{2/3}\times(2\pi\times 0.025)^{1/3}\approx 2\pi\times 0.67~({\rm MHz}),

(36)

where
\gamma/2\pi\approx 3
MHz is the measured damping rates of the two nearly identical YIG spheres.

In our experiment, the minimum value of the total output spectrum changes 21.5 dB around the EP3 when, e.g.,
\Delta_{B}/2\pi=0.025
MHz, as illustrated in figure
3
(e). The synthetic sensitivity factor is

G_{\rm syn}=21.5/0.025=860
. Correspondingly, the CPA-related sensitivity factor and the sensitivity factor at the EP3 are
G_{\rm CPA}=32.1
and
G_{\rm EP3}=26.8
, respectively. Indeed, as shown in figure
4
(b), the synthetic sensitivity factor is much larger than the respective sensitivity factors. Note that the smallest spectrum change that can be resolved by the vector network analyzer used in our experiment (KEYSIGHT PNA-L Network Analyzer N5232B) is
\delta A\sim 1\times 10^{-13}
dB. From the definition of the synthetic sensitivity factor, it follows that the detectable minimum change of the magnetic field
\delta B_{\rm min}
is determined by

G_{\rm syn}=2\pi\frac{\delta A}{\gamma_{e}\delta B_{\rm min}}.

(37)

Thus, we have

\delta B_{\rm min}=\frac{2\pi\times 10^{-13}}{(2\pi\times 28\times 10^{3})\times 860}\approx 4.2\times 10^{-21}~({\rm T}),

(38)

which indicates that the detectable minimum change of the magnetic-field can reach the magnitude
\sim 10^{-21}
T.

4
Conclusions

In summary, we have demonstrated a synthetically enhanced magnetic sensor using both CPA and EP3 in a pseudo-Hermitian cavity magnonic system. The sensitivity enhancements associated with these two mechanisms are superimposed in a product manner, yielding a greatly amplified synthetic sensitivity. This synthetically enhanced sensitivity offers a new way to explore promising applications of precision metrology via hybrid non-Hermitian quantum systems.

Appendix B
Non-Hermitian Hamiltonian of the system in the presence of coherent perfect absorption

In the rotating frame associated with the cavity frequency
\omega_{c}
, the Hamiltonian can be written as

H_{s}/\hbar=\Delta_{1}b_{1}^{\dagger}b_{1}+\Delta_{2}b_{2}^{\dagger}b_{2}+g_{1}\left(a^{\dagger}b_{1}+ab_{1}^{\dagger}\right)+g_{2}\left(a^{\dagger}b_{2}+ab_{2}^{\dagger}\right),

(39)

where
\Delta_{1(2)}=\omega_{1(2)}-\omega_{c}
is the frequency detuning between the Kittel mode in the YIG sphere 1(2) and the cavity mode. The Langevin equations of the coupled system can now be written as

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[a,H_{s}\right]-\left(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{\rm int}\right)a+\sqrt{2\kappa_{1}}a_{1}^{({\rm in})}+\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}}a_{2}^{({\rm in})},

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{1}\!

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{1},H_{s}\right]-\gamma_{1}b_{1},

(40)

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{2}\!

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{2},H_{s}\right]-\gamma_{2}b_{2},

where
\kappa_{1}
,
\kappa_{2}
and
\kappa_{\rm int}
are the dissipation rates of the cavity mode associated with port 1, port 2 and the intrinsic loss of the cavity, respectively, while
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are the dissipation rates of the Kittel modes in the two YIG spheres. The input and output fields of the
i
th port,
a_{i}^{({\rm in})}
and
a_{i}^{(\rm out)}
, obey the input-output relation

a_{i}^{({\rm in})}+a_{i}^{({\rm out})}=\sqrt{2\kappa_{i}}a,~~~~i=1,2.

(41)

When the coherent perfect absorption (CPA) occurs,
a_{1}^{({\rm out})}=a_{2}^{({\rm out})}=0
, so

a_{1}^{({\rm in})}=\sqrt{2\kappa_{1}}a,~~~~a_{2}^{({\rm in})}=\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}}a.

(42)

The quantum Langevin equations in equation (
B
) become

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[a,H_{s}\right]+\left(\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{\rm int}\right)a,

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{1}\!

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{1},H_{s}\right]-\gamma_{1}b_{1},

(43)

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{2}\!

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{2},H_{s}\right]-\gamma_{2}b_{2},

which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\dot{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[a,H_{\rm eff}\right],

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{1}\!

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{1},H_{\rm eff}\right],

(44)

\displaystyle\dot{b}_{2}\!

\displaystyle=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{2},H_{\rm eff}\right],

where

H_{\rm eff}/\hbar=i\kappa_{c}a_{1}^{\dagger}a_{1}+(\Delta_{1}-i\gamma_{1})b_{1}^{\dagger}b_{1}+(\Delta_{2}-i\gamma_{2})b_{2}^{\dagger}b_{2}+g_{1}\left(a^{\dagger}b_{1}+ab_{1}^{\dagger}\right)+g_{2}\left(a^{\dagger}b_{2}+ab_{2}^{\dagger}\right),

(45)

with
\kappa_{c}\equiv\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}-\kappa_{\rm int}
. When
\kappa_{c}>0
, the CPA provides an effective gain to the cavity.
In the matrix form, this non-Hermitain Hamiltonian can be written as

H_{\rm eff}/\hbar=\begin{pmatrix}{i\kappa_{c}}&g_{1}&g_{2}\\
g_{1}&{\Delta_{1}-i\gamma_{1}}&0\\
g_{2}&0&{\Delta_{2}-i\gamma_{2}}\\
\end{pmatrix},

(46)

.

Appendix C
Formulism of the output spectrum

The quantum Langevin equations of the cavity magnonic system have been illustrated in equation (
B
). It notes that when applying the Fourier transformations

x(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\!\!x(\omega)e^{-i\omega t}d\omega,~~~x=a,b_{1},b_{2},

(47)

we obtain the Langevin equations in the frequency domain,

\displaystyle-i\omega a(\omega)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[{a(\omega),H_{s}(\omega)}\right]-\left({\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{\rm int}}\right)a(\omega)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{2\kappa_{1}}a_{1}^{({\rm in})}(\omega)+\sqrt{2\kappa_{2}}a_{2}^{({\rm in})}(\omega),

\displaystyle-i\omega b_{j}(\omega)=-\frac{i}{\hbar}\left[b_{j}(\omega),H_{s}(\omega)\right]-\gamma_{j}b_{j}(\omega),~~j=1,2.

(48)

Using the input-output relation

a_{j}^{({\rm in})}(\omega)+a_{j}^{({\rm out})}(\omega)=\sqrt{2\kappa_{j}}a(\omega),~~j=1,2,

(49)

we can obtain the general relations between the output fields, i.e.,
a_{1}^{({\rm out})}
and
a_{2}^{({\rm out})}
, and the input fields, i.e.,
a_{1}^{({\rm in})}
and
a_{2}^{({\rm in})}
:

\displaystyle a_{1}^{({\rm out})}=r_{11}a_{1}^{({\rm in})}+t_{12}a_{2}^{({\rm in})},

\displaystyle a_{2}^{({\rm out})}=r_{22}a_{2}^{({\rm in})}+t_{21}a_{1}^{({\rm in})},

(50)

with the transmission and reflection coefficients

\displaystyle t_{12}(\omega)=t_{21}(\omega)=-\frac{2\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}}{m+in},

\displaystyle r_{jj}(\omega)=-1-\frac{2\kappa_{j}}{m+in},~~j=1,2,

(51)

where

\displaystyle m=-\left({\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{2}+\kappa_{\rm int}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{g_{j}^{2}\gamma_{j}}{\left({\omega-\omega_{j}}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{j}^{2}},

\displaystyle n=\left({\omega-\omega_{c}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{2}\frac{g_{j}^{2}\left({\omega-\omega_{j}}\right)}{\left({\omega-\omega_{j}}\right)^{2}+\gamma_{j}^{2}}.

(52)

Note that the output field of each port is the sum of the reflection signal at that port and the transmission signal from the other port. The amplitudes of the output fields at the two ports of the cavity are

\displaystyle{\rm S}_{1}(\omega)=r_{11}(\omega)\sqrt{p}e^{-i\phi}+t_{12}(\omega),

\displaystyle{\rm S}_{2}(\omega)=r_{22}(\omega)+t_{21}(\omega)\sqrt{p}e^{-i\phi},

(53)

where
p
and
\phi
denote, respectively, the power ratio and phase difference between the two input fields.

The total output spectrum is obtained by summing the output-field intensities at the two ports:

\displaystyle\left|{\rm S_{\rm tot}(\omega)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\equiv\left|{\rm S_{1}(\omega)}\right|^{2}+\left|{\rm S_{2}(\omega)}\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left({p+1}\right)\left|{\left[{m(\omega)+{{2\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}e^{i\phi}}/\sqrt{p}}+2\kappa_{1}}\right]+in(\omega)}\right|^{2}}{{m(\omega)}^{2}+{n(\omega)}^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\left({\sqrt{p}e^{-i\phi}-\sqrt{\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{2}}}\right)k(\omega)}{{m(\omega)}^{2}+{n(\omega)}^{2}},

(54)

where

\displaystyle k(\omega)

\displaystyle=4\left({\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}+\kappa_{2}e^{i\phi}/\sqrt{p}}\right)^{2}\left({\sqrt{p}e^{-i\phi}-\sqrt{\kappa_{1}/\kappa_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+4\left(\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}+\kappa_{2}e^{i\phi}/\sqrt{p}\right)\left({m+2\sqrt{\kappa_{1}\kappa_{2}}e^{i\phi}/\sqrt{p}+2\kappa_{1}}\right).

(55)

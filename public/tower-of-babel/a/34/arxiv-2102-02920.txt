Twenty Vertex model and domino tilings of the Aztec triangle
Philippe Di Francesco

Abstract
. We show that the number of configurations of the 20 Vertex model on certain domains with domain wall type boundary conditions is equal to the number of domino tilings of Aztec-like triangles, proving a conjecture from [ DFG20 ] . The result is based on the integrability of the 20 Vertex model and uses a connection to the U-turn boundary 6 Vertex model to re-express the number of 20 Vertex configurations as a simple determinant, which is then related to a Lindström-Gessel-Viennot determinant for the domino tiling problem. The common number of configurations is conjectured to be 2 n ⁡ ( n − 1 ) / 2 ​ ∏ j = 0 n − 1 ( 4 ​ j + 2 ) ! ( n + 2 ​ j + 1 ) ! = 1 , 4 , 60 , 3328 , 678912 ​ … 2^{n(n-1)/2}\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{(4j+2)!}{(n+2j+1)!}=1,4,60,3328,678912... The enumeration result is extended to include refinements of both numbers.

Partition function of the 20V model

2.1.
The 20V model: integrable weights

Given a domain
D
of the two-dimensional triangular lattice, we consider the set of orientations of all lattice edges within
D
that satisfy the ice rule,
namely that
each vertex is incident to exactly 3 incoming and 3 outgoing edges

As mentioned before, the 20V model configurations are alternatively described by osculating Schröder paths on edges of the domain
D
that travel right and down. The boundary edges of
D
are fixed to be occupied/empty as part of the definition of the model. The occupied boundary edges split into equal numbers of starting and endpoints of the paths.

Consider a triple intersection between a horizontal, vertical and diagonal line, and translate slightly the diagonal, say to the right:

Transformation to a U-turn boundary 6V model

Figure 10.
The U-turn boundary 6V model inhomogeneous partition function (a) reduces when all
z_{i}=z=p^{-2}q^{-2}
to the configurations
of (b) where all the arrow orientations at the U-turns point down, and where the top vertices are all frozen in a b-type configuration. Erasing the latter and cutting the U-turns leads to the partition function (c), in which horizontal spectral parameters alternate between
z
and
z^{-1}
from bottom to top, while the vertical ones are
w_{n},...,w_{2},w_{1}
from left to right.

The U-turn boundary 6V model was independently studied by Tsuchiya
[
Tsu98
]
and Kuperberg
[
Kup02
]
. The latter uses the 6V weights

(A,B,C)(qz,q^{-1}w)/\sqrt{zw}
(and up to a renaming of variables
x=\sqrt{z/w}
,
a=q
,
b=p
).
Here we drop the overall factor of
1/\sqrt{zw}
in the definition of the weights, which amounts to using the 6V weights (
2.3
)
of the sublattices 2 or 3.

Let

Z_{n}^{6V-U}[z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n};w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n};p]\equiv Z_{n}^{6V-U}[{\mathbf{z}};{\mathbf{w}};p]

denote the partition function of the inhomogeneous 6V model
on a rectangular grid of size
2n\times n
with alternating horizontal spectral parameters
z_{1},z_{1}^{-1},...,z_{n},z_{n}^{-1}

(from bottom to top), vertical spectral parameters
w_{n},w_{n-1},...,w_{1}
(from left to right), with entering arrows along the W boundary, exiting arrows along the N and S boundaries, and U-turn
boundaries on the E, as described in Fig.
10
(a).
The marked dot on the U-turn indicates that the
spectral parameter changes from
z_{i}
(bottom) to
z_{i}^{-1}
(top) and that the configuration of the corresponding U-turn
receives a weight
Y(z_{i})
, where

(2.7)

Y(z)=\left\{\begin{matrix}pq^{-1}-p^{-1}qz&\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{\hbox{\epsfbox}}\\
\ &\ \\
pqz-p^{-1}q^{-1}&\raisebox{-8.5359pt}{\hbox{\epsfbox}}\end{matrix}\right.

These weights obey Sklyanin’s Reflection Yang-Baxter equation
[
Skl88
]
.
We now relate the partition function of this model to that of the mixed 6V model of previous section.

Setting all
z_{i}=z=p^{-2}q^{-2}
forces each U-turn to have an arrow pointing down, as indicated in Fig.
10
(b).
However this imposes no constraint on the vertical spectral parameters
w_{i}
, which we keep arbitrary.
In the resulting semi-homogeneous partition function, we
may then cut out all the marked dots on the U-turns, and moreover use the ice rule to dispose of the top-most
horizontal line,
which gives rise to
n
vertices with trivial b-type configurations. Erasing
these while keeping track of their weight leaves us with the semi-homogeneous partition function
Z_{n}^{6V}[z;{\mathbf{w}}]
corresponding to
Fig.
10
(c), with alternating uniform horizontal spectral parameters
z,z^{-1}
, and arbitrary vertical spectral parameters
{\mathbf{w}}=(w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n})
:

(2.8)

Z_{n}^{6V}[z;{\mathbf{w}}]:=\frac{Z_{n}^{6V-U}[z;{\mathbf{w}};p]}{(p^{2}-p^{-2})^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(p-p^{-1}w_{i})}\ ,\quad p=z^{-1/2}q^{-1},

where we have divided by the top row b-type weights
q^{-1}z^{-1}-qw_{i}=qp(p-p^{-1}w_{i})
and by the U-turn weights

pq^{-1}-p^{-1}qz=p^{-1}q^{-1}(p^{2}-p^{-2})
, using the relation
z=p^{-2}q^{-2}
.

The partition function
Z_{n}^{6V}[z;{\mathbf{w}}]
(
2.8
) may now be identified with
Z_{n}^{m6V}[z,t;{\mathbf{w}}]
of the previous section as follows.
Note first that a rotation of
180^{\circ}

maps the configurations of Fig.
10
(c) onto those of Fig.
9
(d) for general size
n
, and then
that this rotation flips all the local arrow orientations of the vertices, which therefore keep the same weights (
2.2
).
However a discrepancy subsists between the weights in
Z_{n}^{m6V}[z,t;{\mathbf{w}}]
and those in
Z_{n}^{6V}[z;{\mathbf{w}}]
.
Indeed, the 6V weights for
Z_{n}^{m6V}[z,t;{\mathbf{w}}]
alternate between those of the sublattice 1 on odd rows and 3 on even rows.
The weights on the even rows are those of the sublattice 3 in both models, and we must therefore identify their spectral parameters by setting
t=z^{-1}
.
On odd rows, using the relation
(a_{1},b_{1},c_{1})(z,w_{i})=(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})(q^{-1}z,qw_{i})=q(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})(q^{-2}z,w_{i})
, we may identify these weights with

(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})(z,w_{i})
up to the overall factor
q
, provided we redefine odd row parameters
z\to q^{2}z
(while keeping
t=z^{-1}
on even rows).
This results in the following relation between the semi-homogeneous partition functions:

Z_{n}^{m6V}[q^{2}z,z^{-1};{\mathbf{w}}]=q^{n^{2}}Z_{n}^{6V}[z;{\mathbf{w}}]

with
z=p^{-2}q^{-2}
.
Together with (
2.6
), this leads to the relation:

(2.9)

Z_{n}^{20V}[q^{2}z,z^{-1};{\mathbf{w}}]=\frac{q^{n^{2}}a_{2}(q^{2}z,z^{-1})^{n(3n-1)/2}\,\prod_{i=1}^{n}b_{1}(q^{2}z,w_{i})^{i-1}\,a_{3}(z^{-1},w_{i})^{i}}{(p^{2}-p^{-2})^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}(p-p^{-1}w_{i})}\,Z_{n}^{6V-U}[z;{\mathbf{w}};p]

with
p=z^{-1/2}q^{-1}
.

To reach the combinatorial point (
2.5
), let us pick uniform
20V spectral parameters so that
(q^{2}z,z^{-1},w_{i})=\delta(q^{6},1,q^{-6})
, namely
z=q^{2},\delta=q^{-2},p=q^{-2}

and
w_{i}=q^{-8}
for all
i
,
and finally set
q=e^{i\pi/8}
. Using the homogeneity of the weights, this allows to relate:

(2.10)

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{20V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{{\alpha}}{\delta}\right)^{\frac{3n(3n-1)}{2}}\,Z_{n}^{20V}[q^{2}z,z^{-1},w]\big|_{z=q^{2};w=q^{-8}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{b_{1}(q^{4},q^{-8})^{\frac{n(n-1)}{2}}a_{2}(q^{4},q^{-2})^{\frac{n(3n-1)}{2}}a_{3}(q^{-2},q^{-8})^{\frac{n(n+1)}{2}}q^{n^{2}}}{\left(2^{5/6}q^{2}\right)^{\frac{3n(3n-1)}{2}}(q^{-4}-q^{4})^{n}(q^{-2}-q^{-6})^{n}}\,Z_{n}^{6V-U}[q^{2},q^{-8};q^{-2}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{-\frac{n(5n+3)}{4}}\,q^{-5n}\,Z_{n}^{6V-U}[q^{2},-1;q^{-2}]

with
{\alpha}
as in (
2.5
), and
where the latter partition function
Z_{n}^{6V-U}[q^{2},-1;q^{-2}]
corresponds to taking uniform values
z_{i}=q^{2}
and

w_{i}=w=q^{-8}=-1
for all
i
, and
p=q^{-2}

in the U-turn boundary partition function
Z_{n}^{6V-U}[{\mathbf{z}},{\mathbf{w}};p]
.

Remark 2.2
.

We note that neither
Z_{n}^{6V-U}[q^{2},-1;q^{-2}]
nor
Z_{n}^{6V}[q^{2},-1]
are “combinatorial” in the sense that they count objects. Indeed, they are weighted countings of configurations of the corresponding models, including irrational weights (such as
\sqrt{2}
).
For instance, we may choose to normalize the weights of the
6V
model by an overall factor
\rho_{o}=q\sqrt{2}

on odd rows such that
\rho_{o}(A,B,C)(qz,q^{-1}w)=(1,\sqrt{2},1)
and
\rho_{e}=q^{-1}\sqrt{2}
on even rows, such that
\rho_{e}(A,B,C)(qz^{-1},q^{-1}w)=(\sqrt{2},1,1)
for
z=q^{2}
and
w=-1
. In that case, the
6V
partition function
{\bar{Z}}_{n}^{6V}
with these new weights reads
{\bar{Z}}_{n}^{6V}=\rho_{o}^{n^{2}}\,\rho_{e}^{n(n-1)}\,Z_{n}^{6V}[q^{2},-1]
. Yet, using the above relations at the combinatorial point, we find that

{\bar{Z}}_{n}^{6V}=2^{n(n-1)/4}\,Z_{n}^{20V}
. So we should think of the
6V
partition functions at hand as combinatorial tools rather than counting combinatorial objects.

2.5.
Inhomogeneous determinant formula

The result for the partition function of the U-turn boundary 6V model
[
Tsu98
,
Kup02
]
reads as follows for our choice of weights.
For short, we denote by
(a,b,c)(z,w):=(a_{3},b_{3},c_{3})(z,w)=(A,B,C)(qz,q^{-1}w)
.
Let
M_{U}(n)
be the
n\times n
matrix with entries

(2.11)

[M_{U}(n)]_{i,j}=\frac{1}{a(z_{i},w_{j})b(z_{i},w_{j})}-\frac{1}{a(1,z_{i}w_{j})b(1,z_{i}w_{j})}

Then the inhomogeneous partition function is given by:

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{6V-U}[z_{1},z_{2},...,z_{n};w_{1},w_{2},...,w_{n};p]

(2.12)

\displaystyle\qquad=\det\big(M_{U}(n)\big)\times{\scriptstyle\frac{\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(p-p^{-1}w_{i})a(qz_{i},q^{-1}z_{i}^{-1})c(z_{i},w_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{i,j=1}^{n}a(z_{i},w_{j})b(z_{i},w_{j})a(1,z_{i}w_{j})b(1,z_{i}w_{j})\right\}}{\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}^{n-1}\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}-z_{j})(w_{j}-w_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(1-z_{i}z_{j})(1-w_{i}w_{j})\right\}}}

The aim of the following sections is to extract a homogeneous limit from this, with relevant values of spectral parameters, to finally get a compact formula for the partition function of the
20V
model.

2.6.
Homogeneous limit

Let us denote by
\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};w_{1},...,w_{n}]
the quantity:

(2.13)

\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};w_{1},...,w_{n}]:=\frac{\det\big(M_{U}(n)\big)}{\left\{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}-z_{j})(w_{j}-w_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(1-z_{i}z_{j})(1-w_{i}w_{j})\right\}}

In view of the determinant formula (
2.5
),
to deduce the homogeneous limit
Z_{2n\times n}^{6V-U}[z,w;p]
(
2.8
), we must now compute the limit

\Delta_{n}[z,w]:=\lim_{z_{i}\to z\atop w_{i}\to w}\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};w_{1},...,w_{n}]

In fact, we are only interested in the special combinatorial point values
z=q^{2},w=q^{8}=-1
of the homogeneous parameters.
In this section, we start by computing
\Delta_{n}[z,-1]
.

Notation:
Throughout the paper, for any function
f(u,v)
with a power series expansion around
(0,0)
,
we denote by
f(u,v)|_{u^{i}v^{j}}
the coefficient of
u^{i}v^{j}
in the series expansion of
f
, in other words

f(u,v)|_{u^{i}v^{j}}:=\frac{\partial_{u}^{i}}{i!}\frac{\partial_{v}^{j}}{j!}f(0,0)
.

Theorem 2.3
.

We have:

(2.14)

\Delta_{n}[z,-1]=\frac{(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(f_{U}(z;u,v)\Big|_{u^{i}v^{2j+1}}\right),

where
f_{U}(z;u,v)
denotes the function:

(2.15)

\displaystyle f_{U}(z;u,v)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{a(z+u,v-1)b(z+u,v-1)}-\frac{1}{a(1,(z+u)(v-1))b(1,(z+u)(v-1))}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\scriptstyle\frac{1}{(q^{-2}-q^{2})(z+u)}\left\{\frac{q^{-1}}{a(z+u,v-1)}-\frac{q}{b(z+u,v-1)}-\frac{q^{-1}}{a(\frac{1}{z+u},v-1)}+\frac{q}{b(\frac{1}{z+u},v-1)}\right\}}

Proof.

The theorem is proved in two steps. We first keep
z_{i}
generic, and compute for
w=-1
:

\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};-1]:=\lim_{w_{i}\to-1\atop i=1,2,...,n}\frac{\det\big(M_{U}(n)\big)}{\left\{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(w_{j}-w_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(1-w_{i}w_{j})\right\}}

Setting
f[z;v]:=f_{U}(z;0,v)
, and setting
w_{i}=-1+v_{i}
, we may rewrite:

\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};-1]:=\lim_{v_{i}\to 0\atop i=1,2,...,n}\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\big(f[z_{i};v_{j}]\big)}{\left\{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(v_{j}-v_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(v_{i}+v_{j})\right\}}

where in the last factors
1-w_{i}w_{j}=v_{i}+v_{j}-v_{i}v_{j}
we have dropped the higher order term
v_{i}v_{j}
as the
v
’s tend to
0
.

For each
i
, we may expand
f[z_{i};v_{j}]
in power series of
v_{j}
:

f[z_{i};v_{j}]=\sum_{m\geq 0}f_{m}[z_{i}]v_{j}^{m}
, where the coefficients read:

(2.16)

f_{m}[z_{i}]={\scriptstyle\frac{1}{(q^{-2}-q^{2})z_{i}}\left\{\left(\frac{1}{q^{2}z_{i}+1}\right)^{m+1}+\left(\frac{q^{2}z_{i}}{q^{2}z_{i}+1}\right)^{m+1}-\left(\frac{1}{q^{-2}z_{i}+1}\right)^{m+1}-\left(\frac{q^{-2}z_{i}}{q^{-2}z_{i}+1}\right)^{m+1}\right\}}

Let
{\mathbf{F}}[v]
(resp.
{\mathbf{F}}_{m}
) denote the column vector with entries
f[z_{i};v]
(resp.
f_{m}[z_{i}]
)
i=1,2,...,n
. We have

{\mathbf{F}}[v]=\sum_{m\geq 0}v^{m}\,{\mathbf{F}}_{m}
.
Moreover, the columns
{\mathbf{F}}_{m}
are not linearly independent. More precisely we have the following.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
c_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
be the coefficients in the series expansion:

(2.17)

\frac{2}{1+e^{-x}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}\,\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

namely
c_{0}=1
,
c_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
,
c_{2}=0
,
c_{3}=-\frac{1}{4}
,
c_{4}=0
,
c_{5}=\frac{1}{2}
,
c_{6}=0
,
c_{7}=\frac{17}{8}
, …
Then for all
n\geq 0
we have:

(2.18)

{\mathbf{F}}_{2n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{2i+1}{2n+1\choose 2i+1}{\mathbf{F}}_{2n-2i-1}

Proof.

For any function
f({\alpha})
(say polynomial), introduce the shift operator
S:f\mapsto Sf
, such that
(Sf)({\alpha})=f({\alpha}-1)
.
Using the Taylor expansion around
{\alpha}=0
, we may express
S
as
\exp(-\frac{d}{d{\alpha}})
. In particular,
the identity
(1+\exp(-\frac{d}{d{\alpha}}))f({\alpha})=f({\alpha})+f({\alpha}-1)
is easily inverted using the series expansion (
2.17
) in which
x
is substituted with the differential operator
\frac{d}{d{\alpha}}
:

f({\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 0}c_{n}\frac{1}{n!}\frac{d^{n}}{d{\alpha}^{n}}\big(f({\alpha})+f({\alpha}-1)\big)

Let us apply this to the polynomial
f({\alpha})={\alpha}^{2n+1}
for
n\geq 0
. We get:

{\alpha}^{2n+1}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{{\alpha}^{2n+1}+({\alpha}-1)^{2n+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{2i+1}{2n+1\choose 2i+1}({\alpha}^{2n-2i}+({\alpha}-1)^{2n-2i})\right\}

which implies:

{\alpha}^{2n+1}+(1-{\alpha})^{2n+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{2i+1}{2n+1\choose 2i+1}({\alpha}^{2n-2i}+(1-{\alpha})^{2n-2i})\ .

Up to the overall prefactor
1/((q^{-2}-q^{2})z_{i})
, the
i
-th row of the desired identity (
2.18
) follows immediately from this, as it takes precisely the form:

\displaystyle{\alpha}^{2n+1}+(1-{\alpha})^{2n+1}-(\beta^{2n+1}+(1-\beta)^{2n+1})

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}c_{2i+1}{2n+1\choose 2i+1}(({\alpha}^{2n-2i}+({\alpha}-1)^{2n-2i})-(\beta^{2n-2i}+(\beta-1)^{2n-2i}))

where
{\alpha}=\frac{1}{q^{2}z_{i}+1}
and
\beta=\frac{1}{q^{-2}z_{i}+1}
.
∎

Note that as
{\mathbf{F}}_{0}=0
we have the series expansion
{\mathbf{F}}[v]=\sum_{n\geq 1}{\mathbf{F}}_{n}v^{n}=:v{\mathbf{G}}[v]
.
The matrix with entries
f[z_{i};v_{j}]
is made of successive columns
v_{1}{\mathbf{G}}[v_{1}],...,v_{n}{\mathbf{G}}[v_{n}]
,
therefore the determinant of this matrix is
v_{1}v_{2}\cdots v_{n}\det({\mathbf{G}}[v_{1}]{\mathbf{G}}[v_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{G}}[v_{n}])
.
Consequently we rewrite:

\frac{\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\big(f[z_{i};v_{j}]\big)}{\left\{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(v_{j}-v_{i})\right\}\left\{\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(v_{i}+v_{j})\right\}}=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{G}}[v_{1}]{\mathbf{G}}[v_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{G}}[v_{n}])}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2})}=:D_{n}[{\mathbf{z}};{\mathbf{v}}]

Let us now successively take
v_{1}\to 0
,
v_{2}\to 0
, …
v_{n}\to 0
. The first limit
v_{1}\to 0
amounts to taking
{\mathbf{G}}[0]={\mathbf{F}}_{1}
as first column in the determinant and set
v_{1}=0
in the denominator. To perform the second, we must expand
{\mathbf{G}}[v_{2}]={\mathbf{F}}_{1}+{\mathbf{F}}_{2}v_{2}+{\mathbf{F}}_{3}v_{2}^{2}+O(v_{2}^{3})
up to order
2
, as both constant and linear terms are columns proportional to
{\mathbf{F}}_{1}
by Lemma
2.4
. When we reach
v_{k}\to 0
, we must expand
{\mathbf{G}}[v_{k}]
up to order
2k
, as all previous terms are linear combinations of the previous columns in the determinant, as a consequence of Lemma
2.4
.
This leads successively to:

\displaystyle\lim_{v_{1}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{z}};{\mathbf{v}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{F}}_{1}{\mathbf{G}}[v_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{G}}[v_{n}])}{\prod_{i=2}^{n}v_{i}^{2}\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq n}(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2})}

\displaystyle\lim_{v_{1},v_{2}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{z}};{\mathbf{v}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{F}}_{1}{\mathbf{F}}_{3}{\mathbf{G}}[v_{3}]\cdots{\mathbf{G}}[v_{n}])}{\prod_{i=3}^{n}v_{i}^{4}\prod_{3\leq i<j\leq n}(v_{j}^{2}-v_{i}^{2})}

\displaystyle\quad\vdots\

\displaystyle\lim_{v_{1},v_{2},...,v_{n}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{z}};{\mathbf{v}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{n}}\det({\mathbf{F}}_{1}{\mathbf{F}}_{3}\cdots{\mathbf{F}}_{2n-1})=\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};-1]

We are now left with the second step, namely sending all
z_{i}
’s to
z
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}[z,-1]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{z_{i}\to z\atop i=1,2,...,n}\frac{\Delta_{n}[z_{1},...,z_{n};-1]}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(z_{i}-z_{j})\prod_{1\leq i\leq j\leq n}(1-z_{i}z_{j})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{i}\to 0\atop i=1,2,...,n}\frac{1}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{F}}_{1}{\mathbf{F}}_{3}\cdots{\mathbf{F}}_{2n-1})}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})}

where we have set
z_{i}=z+u_{i}
, and
the column vector
{\mathbf{F}}_{2j+1}
has entries
f_{2j+1}[z+u_{i}]
,
i=1,2,...,n
. Let us represent the matrix

({\mathbf{F}}_{1}{\mathbf{F}}_{3}\cdots{\mathbf{F}}_{2n-1})
by its rows, in the form
({\mathbf{R}}[u_{1}]{\mathbf{R}}[u_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{R}}[u_{n}])^{t}
. Here, the column vector
{\mathbf{R}}[u_{i}]
is the transpose of the
i
-th row vector of the original matrix, and
{\mathbf{R}}[u]
has entries
f_{2j+1}[z+u]
,
j=0,1,...,n-1
.
As before, we write the series expansion
{\mathbf{R}}[u]=\sum_{n\geq 0}{\mathbf{R}}_{n}u^{n}
, and perform the successive limits
u_{1}\to 0
,
u_{2}\to 0
, …
u_{n}\to 0
in the quantity:

D_{n}[{\mathbf{u}}]:=\frac{1}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{R}}[u_{1}]{\mathbf{R}}[u_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{R}}[u_{n}])^{t}}{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})}

The vectors
{\mathbf{R}}_{i}
turn out to be linearly independent, so that each vector
{\mathbf{R}}[u_{j}]
only has to be expanded up to order
j-1
,
and we end up with the formulas:

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{1}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{u}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{R}}_{0}{\mathbf{R}}[u_{2}]\cdots{\mathbf{R}}[u_{n}])^{t}}{\prod_{i=2}^{n}u_{i}\prod_{2\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})}

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{1},u_{2}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{u}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n-1+n-2}}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\frac{\det({\mathbf{R}}_{0}{\mathbf{R}}_{1}{\mathbf{R}}[u_{3}]\cdots{\mathbf{R}}[u_{n}])^{t}}{\prod_{i=3}^{n}u_{i}^{2}\prod_{3\leq i<j\leq n}(u_{i}-u_{j})}

\displaystyle\quad\vdots

\displaystyle\lim_{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{n}\to 0}D_{n}[{\mathbf{u}}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{n(n-1)/2}}{2^{n}(1-z^{2})^{n(n+1)/2}}\det({\mathbf{R}}_{0}{\mathbf{R}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{R}}_{n})^{t}=\Delta_{n}[z;-1]

The identity (
2.14
) follows by identifying the entries
i+1,j+1
(
i,j=0,1,...,n-1
) of the matrix
({\mathbf{R}}_{0}{\mathbf{R}}_{1}\cdots{\mathbf{R}}_{n})^{t}
with the coefficient of
u^{i}v^{2j+1}
of the series expansion of
f_{U}(u,v)
around
(0,0)
. Indeed,
({\mathbf{F}}_{2j+1})_{i+1}
corresponds to the coefficient of
v^{2j+1}
in the series expansion of
{\mathbf{F}}[v]
around
v=0

3.1.

The Aztec triangle

where the length of the vertical West boundary is

tiling configurations of this domain by means of

of
n

(0,2i)
,

(2j+1,2j+1)

These paths are Schröder paths, with three types of allowed steps on

(1,1)

(1,-1)
and horizontal
(2,0)

Let

denote the total number of domino tiling configurations of the Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
The latter is easily computed by direct application of the Lindström-Gessel-Viennot determinant formula
[
Lin73
,
GV85
]
.
Denoting by

the number of configurations of a single path from the starting point
(0,2i)
to the endpoint

(2j+1,2j+1)

Lemma 3.1
.

The partition function for domino tilings of the Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n}
reads:

Z_{n}^{DT}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(M_{i,j}\right)

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
M_{i,j}
is independent of

, as the shape of the Aztec triangle domain imposes no further constraint
on the paths apart from their fixed starting and endpoints. The Lemma
3.1
states that

, where
the

may be viewed as the finite truncation
to the first

with entries

,

.

3.2.
Generating functions and determinant formula

The generating function
f_{M}(u,v):=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}v^{i}u^{j}\,M_{i,j}
is given by the following.

Theorem 3.3
.

We have

f_{M}(u,v)=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\infty}v^{i}u^{j}\,M_{i,j}=\frac{1+u}{1-v-4uv-u^{2}v+u^{2}v^{2}}

Proof.

We start from the generating series:

(3.1)

\sigma(u,v)=\frac{1}{1-u-v-uv}

We may interpret
\sigma(u,v)
as the partition function for arbitrary finite length Schröder paths from the origin, with weight
u
per up step,
v
per down step, and
uv
per horizontal step. The position of the endpoint of the path
reads
(U+D+2H,U-D)
where
U,D,H
denote respectively the total numbers of up, down, horizontal steps in the path.
Such a path receives a weight
u^{U}v^{D}(uv)^{H}=u^{U+H}v^{D+H}
. The number
\Sigma_{i,j}
of paths
from the origin with fixed endpoint
(i,j)
, with
i-j
even, therefore reads:

\Sigma_{i,j}=\sigma(u,v)|_{u^{\frac{i+j}{2}}v^{\frac{i-j}{2}}}
. Equivalently:

(3.2)

\Sigma_{i,j}=\oint\frac{dx}{2i\pi x}\frac{dy}{2i\pi y}\,\frac{1}{x^{\frac{i+j}{2}}y^{\frac{i-j}{2}}}\,\frac{1}{1-x-y-xy}

where the contour integral picks the residues at
0
. By a trivial translation of the starting point, we may now interpret
M_{i,j}
as the total number of paths from the origin to
(2j+1,2j+1)-(0,2i)=(2j+1,2j+1-2i)
, hence

M_{i,j}=\Sigma_{2j+1,2j+1-2i}
, or:

M_{i,j}=\oint\frac{dx}{2i\pi x}\frac{dy}{2i\pi y}\,\frac{1}{x^{2j+1-i}y^{i}}\,\frac{1}{1-x-y-xy}

Changing integration variables to
u=y/x,v=x
, we get

M_{i,j}=\oint\frac{du}{2i\pi u}\frac{dv}{2i\pi v}\,\frac{1}{v^{2j+1}u^{i}}\,\frac{1}{1-v-uv-uv^{2}}=\frac{1}{1-v-uv-uv^{2}}\Big|_{u^{i}v^{2j+1}}

Finally, we remark that for any series
f(v)
, and any
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}
, we have:

(3.3)

f(v)|_{v^{2j+1}}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{v}}\left(f(\sqrt{v})-f(-\sqrt{v})\right)\Big|_{v^{j}}

and the theorem follows from the identity

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{v}}\left(\frac{1}{1-(1+u)\sqrt{v}-uv}-\frac{1}{1+(1+u)\sqrt{v}-uv}\right)=\frac{1+u}{1-v-4uv-u^{2}v+u^{2}v^{2}}

∎

This allows to translate the result of Lemma
3.1
into the following determinant formula.

Theorem 3.4

The partition function for domino tilings of the Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(f_{DT}(u,v)\Big|_{u^{i}v^{j}}\right)

(3.5)

\displaystyle f_{DT}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+u}{1-v-4uv-u^{2}v+u^{2}v^{2}}

4.1.
Determinant relations

We wish to compare the results of Theorems
2.7
and
3.4
.
Using Remarks
2.8
and
3.2
, we see that both enumeration results are expressed as the determinant of a finite
truncation of an infinite matrix. We have the following
[
DFL18
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
L
(resp.
U
) be arbitrary infinite lower (resp. upper) triangular matrices, with diagonal entries
1
, and

any infinite matrix.
Then we have the following properties:

(1)

For all

, the truncation
(LAU)_{n}
of the infinite matrix
LAU
to its first
n
rows and columns is equal to
L_{n}A_{n}U_{n}
, the product of the corresponding truncations of

respectively.

(2)

\det(A_{n})=\det\big((LAU)_{n}\big)

Proof.

We have for all
i,j\in[0,n-1]
:

((LAU)_{n})_{i,j}=\sum_{\ell,m\geq 0}L_{i,\ell}A_{\ell,m}U_{m,j}
. But
L_{i,\ell}=0
for
\ell>i
hence in particular for all
\ell\geq n
, as
i\leq n-1
. Similarly,
U_{m,j}=0
for
m\geq n
, and therefore we may rewrite

((LAU)_{n})_{i,j}=\sum_{\ell,m=0}^{n-1}L_{i,\ell}A_{\ell,m}U_{m,j}=((L_{n}A_{n}U_{n})_{i,j}
, and (1) follows. The statement (2) follows from

\det(L_{n}A_{n}U_{n})=\det(A_{n})
as
\det(L_{n})=\det(U_{n})=1
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
A,B
be two infinite matrices with entries generated by the series
f_{A}(u,v)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}u^{i}v^{j}A_{i,j}

and
f_{B}(u,v)=\sum_{i,j\geq 0}u^{i}v^{j}B_{i,j}
. Then the product
AB
has for generating series the convolution of
f_{A}
and
f_{B}
,
namely:

f_{AB}(u,v)=f_{A}*f_{B}(u,v)=\oint\frac{dt}{2i\pi t}f_{A}(u,t^{-1})f_{B}(t,v)

where the contour integral picks up the residue at

.

Proof.

By direct calculation.
∎

Theorem 4.3

.

Proof.

By Remarks
2.8
and
3.2
, we have
Z_{n}^{20V}=\det(P_{n})
,

in terms of truncations of infinite

The property (1) follows from noticing that we have a series identity of the form

for refined domino tiling partition functions of the Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
We have the following:

Theorem 6.1
.

The generating function

reads:

(6.1)

\displaystyle Z^{DT}(t)

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(f_{DT}(t;u,v)\Big|_{u^{i}v^{j}}\right)

(6.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{DT}(u,v)+v^{n-1}\frac{{\alpha}_{+}(u)^{2n}}{\sqrt{1+6u+u^{2}}}\,\frac{(t-1)u}{{\alpha}_{+}(u)-tu}

Proof.

Let us first compute

.
By direct application of the Lindström-Gessel-Viennot theorem

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2n-1-k,2n-1-2i+k}

\displaystyle\oint\frac{dx}{2i\pi x}\frac{dy}{2i\pi y}\frac{1}{x^{2n-1-i}y^{i-k}}\frac{1}{1-x-y-xy}

Theorem 7.2
.

The number of configurations of the 20V DWBC3 model on the pentagon
\mathcal{P}_{n,n-3}
is identical to the number of domino tilings of the truncated Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n,n-3}
, namely:

Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-3})=Z^{DT}({\mathcal{T}}_{n,n-3})

Proof.

We proceed like in the case
k=2
. For the 20V model on
\mathcal{P}_{n,n-3}
, let us start from the partition function

Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-2})
. It splits into four parts according to the local configuration of the two bottom vertices, as shown in Figs.
16
(a), (c), (d) and (e).

The first contribution (a) can be further split according to the configuration of the rightmost column: there are two ways in which the path can leave the second rightmost vertical line at an exit point say at height
k
(by a horizontal or diagonal step, followed by vertical steps until the endpoint). Removing the last column, and inserting vertical steps up to the point of exit at height
k
into the second vertical, we get a 2 to 1 mapping to refined configurations of the 20V on
\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}
that reach the last column at or above height
k
, resulting in a contribution
\sum_{j\geq k}Z_{n-1,j}^{20V}
for each

, hence a total of
2\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}\sum_{j\geq k}Z_{n-1,j}^{20V}=\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}2k\,Z_{n-1,k}^{20V}
.

The case (c) corresponds
to the contributions to the refined partition function
Z_{n,2}^{20V\backslash}
such that a path goes vertically through the left vertex. We must therefore subtract from
Z_{n,2}^{20V\backslash}
the contributions with the two rightmost paths ending diagonally before their last vertical step, in bijection with 20V configurations on
\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}
. This gives a net contribution of

Z_{n,2}^{20V\backslash}-Z_{n-1}^{20V}
. The case (d) contributes

Z_{n,2}^{20V-}
. Finally the case (e) contributes
Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-3})
. Collecting all the terms and using

Z_{n,2}^{20V\backslash}+Z_{n,2}^{20V-}=Z_{n,2}^{20V}
, we get:

Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-2})=\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}2k\,Z_{n-1,k}^{20V}+Z_{n,2}^{20V}-Z_{n-1}^{20V}+Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-3})

5.6

6.3

\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}2k\,Z_{n-1,k}^{20V}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}2k(Z_{n-1,k}^{20V}+Z_{n-1,2n-2-k}^{20V})=2(n-1)\sum_{k=1}^{2n-3}Z_{n-1,k}^{20V}=2(n-1)Z_{n-1}^{20V}

(7.1)

Z^{20V}({\mathcal{P}}_{n,n-3})=Z^{20V}_{n}-Z^{20V}_{n,2}-2(n-1)Z^{20V}_{n-1}

8.
Discussion and Conclusion

In this paper we have investigated the 20V model with DWBC3 boundary conditions on the family of pentagons
\mathcal{P}_{n,n-k}
and their relation to the domino tilings of the family of Aztec triangles
\mathcal{T}_{n,n-k}
, and proved the conjectured identity between their partition functions for the cases
k=1
,
2
,
3
. In the case
k=1
of the quadrangle
\mathcal{Q}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{n,n-1}
we have extended the result to refined partition functions. Our proofs for this case have relied mainly on the choice of integrable weights for the 20V model, allowing for transforming the original problem into a 6V model with U-turn boundaries. The further truncations of the domain
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
for
k=2,3
were treated in a purely combinatorial manner, by relating the partition functions to the
k=1
case and its refinements.

We now discuss a conjecture for the exact number
Z_{n}^{20V}=Z_{n}^{DT}
and alternative formulas for these quantities. We also show how to introduce some extra weight in the domino tiling problems, before making some concluding remarks.

Y_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}=(n-1,n-1,n-1,n-2,n-2,n-2,...,1,1,1,0,0)
has dimension

\delta_{n}^{\prime\prime\prime}=2^{n-1}\,B_{2n-1}
. Recalling that the in 6V case
[
Str06
,
RS04b
]
the argument involved some non-trivial
{\mathbb{Z}}_{3}
symmetry of the partition function at the combinatorial point, we expect a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
symmetry to play an analogous role here. We intend to return to this question in a future publication.

8.2.
Alternative expressions for
Z_{n}^{20V}

The results of this paper allow us to derive several alternative determinant formulas for the number
Z_{n}^{20V}
.
Using Theorem
2.7
, we may express
Z_{n}^{20V}
as follows.

Theorem 8.2
.

The number
Z_{n}^{20V}
of configurations of the
20V
model on the quadrangle
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
reads:

(8.2)

Z_{n}^{20V}=\det_{0\leq i\leq j\leq n-1}\left(2^{i}\,{i+2j+1\choose 2j+1}-{i-1\choose 2j+1}\right)

with the convention
{m\choose p}=0
for all
-1\leq m<p
.

Proof.

We start from the determinant formula
Z_{n}^{20V}=\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}(f_{P}(u,v)|_{u^{i}v^{j}})=\det(P_{n})
of Theorem
2.7
, where:

f_{P}(u,v)=g_{20V}(u,v)=\frac{\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}{1-v\,\left(\frac{1+u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}-\frac{u^{2}}{1-v\,u^{2}}

and
P_{n}
is the finite truncation of the infinite matrix
P
generated by
f_{P}
.
Using the same technique as in Sections
4.1
-
4.2
, let us consider the infinite matrix
\Lambda
with generating function:

f_{\Lambda}(u,v)=\frac{1-u}{1-u-vu}

Then (1)
\Lambda
is lower triangular and (2) the diagonal matrix elements of
\Lambda
are all
1
. We deduce
the truncation
(\Lambda\,P)_{n}=\Lambda_{n}\,P_{n}
, and therefore
\det((\Lambda\,P)_{n})=\det(P_{n})
. Explicitly computing:

f_{\Lambda\,P}(u,v)=(f_{\Lambda}*f_{P})(u,v)=f_{P}\left(\frac{u}{1-u},v\right)=\frac{\left(\frac{1}{1-2u}\right)^{2}}{1-v\,\left(\frac{1}{1-2u}\right)^{2}}-\frac{\left(\frac{u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}{1-v\,\left(\frac{u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}

we identify the entries

(\Lambda P)_{i,j}=\left\{\left(\frac{1}{1-2u}\right)^{2j+2}-\left(\frac{u}{1-u}\right)^{2j+2}\right\}\Bigg|_{u^{i}}=2^{i}\,{i+2j+1\choose 2j+1}-{i-1\choose 2j+1}

and the theorem follows.
∎

A slight variant of the formula consists of using the analytic continuation of binomial coefficients.

Corollary 8.3
.

The number
Z_{n}^{20V}
of configurations of the
20V
model on the quadrangle
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
reads:

(8.3)

Z_{n}^{20V}=\frac{2^{n(n-1)/4}}{2}\,\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\Big(\theta_{2j+1}(i)+\theta_{2j+1}(-i)\Big),\quad\theta_{m}(x)=\frac{2^{x/2}}{m!}(x+1)(x+2)...(x+m)

This latter, more symmetric form allows to derive the following constant term identity.

Theorem 8.4
.

The number
Z_{n}^{20V}
of configurations of the
20V
model on the quadrangle
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
reads:

Z_{n}^{20V}=CT_{x_{1},...,x_{n}}\left\{\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(1+x_{i}+x_{j}-x_{i}x_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2i-1}\,(1-x_{i})^{n}}\right\}

where the symbol
CT_{x_{1},...,x_{n}}
stands for the constant coefficient in the Laurent series expansion around
x_{i}=0
for all
i
.

Proof.

Let us rewrite the function
\theta_{m}(x)
of (
8.3
) as:

\theta_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{m!}\partial_{u}^{m}\,u^{m+x}\big|_{u=\sqrt{2}}=CT_{t}\left\{e^{t\,\partial_{u}}t^{-m}\,u^{m+x}\right\}\big|_{u=\sqrt{2}}=CT_{t}\left\{t^{-m}\,(\sqrt{2}+t)^{m+x}\right\}

where we have used the Taylor expansion to interpret the operator
e^{t\,\partial_{u}}
as
the shift map
S_{t}:f(u)\mapsto f(u+t)
. We now use multilinearity of determinants to rewrite

Z_{n}^{20V}=\frac{2^{n(n-1)/4}}{2}\,CT_{t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n}}\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{t_{j}}\right)^{2j-1}\,\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\left((\sqrt{2}+t_{j})^{i-1}+(\sqrt{2}+t_{j})^{1-i}\right)\right\}

Next we use the standard formula

\det_{1\leq i,j\leq n}(x_{j}^{i-1}+x_{j}^{1-i})=2\,\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{i}-x_{j})(1-x_{i}x_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{n-1}}

to rewrite:

(8.4)

Z_{n}^{20V}=2^{n(n-1)/4}\,CT_{t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n}}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{t_{i}}\right)^{2i-1}\,\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(t_{j}-t_{i})\left(1-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2}+t_{i})(\sqrt{2}+t_{j})}\right)\right\}

We now use the obvious property of the constant term that
CT_{\mathbf{x}}(f({\mathbf{x}}))=CT_{\mathbf{x}}({\rm Sym}(f({\mathbf{x}}))
, where for any function
of the variables
{\mathbf{x}}=(x_{1},...,x_{n})
we define the symmetrization map

{\rm Sym}:f({\mathbf{x}})\mapsto{\rm Sym}(f)({\mathbf{x}}):=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}f(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(n)}).

We also need the antisymmetrization map

{\rm ASym}:f({\mathbf{x}})\mapsto{\rm ASym}(f)({\mathbf{x}}):=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}{\rm sgn}(\sigma)\,f(x_{\sigma(1)},...,x_{\sigma(n)})

with the main property

{\rm Sym}\left(\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{j}-x_{i})f(x_{1},...,x_{n})\right)=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(x_{j}-x_{i})\,{\rm ASym}\left(f(x_{1},...,x_{n})\right).

Symmetrizing the argument of the constant term in (
8.4
), we get:

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{20V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n(n-1)/2}}{n!}CT_{t_{1},t_{2},...,t_{n}}\left\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{t_{i}}\right)\right.

\displaystyle\quad\times\,\left.\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\left(1-\frac{1}{(\sqrt{2}+t_{i})(\sqrt{2}+t_{j})}\right)(t_{j}-t_{i})\left(\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{t_{j}}\right)^{2}-\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{2}}{t_{i}}\right)^{2}\right)\right\}.

Let us now change variables to
x_{i}=\frac{t_{i}}{t_{i}+\sqrt{2}}
. Viewing the constant term in
t_{j}
as a contour integral

\oint\frac{dt_{j}}{2\pi i\,t_{j}}=\oint\frac{dx_{j}}{2\pi i\,x_{j}(1-x_{j})}
, we easily find:

\displaystyle Z_{n}^{20V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n!}CT_{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}}\left\{\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1+x_{i}+x_{j}-x_{i}x_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})(x_{j}^{-2}-x_{i}^{-2})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\,(1-x_{i})^{n}}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle CT_{x_{1},x_{2},...,x_{n}}\left\{{\rm Sym}\left(\frac{\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq n}(1+x_{i}+x_{j}-x_{i}x_{j})(x_{i}-x_{j})}{\prod_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}^{2i-1}\,(1-x_{i})^{n}}\right)\right\}.

The theorem follows.
∎

The constant term identity of Theorem
8.4
is reminiscent of constant term expressions for TSSCPP derived by Zeilberger
[
Zei96
]
in his famous proof of the ASM conjecture.

8.3.
Introducing step weights in the domino tiling problem

In this paper we have found compact determinant formulas for both the number of tilings of the Aztec triangle
\mathcal{T}_{n}
and its refinements, involving the truncation of infinite matrices generated respectively by the series
f_{DT}
(
3.5
) and
f_{DT}^{\rm ref}
(
6.2
).
We note that a very simple decoration of the generating function
f_{DT}(u,v)
allows to include a non-trivial extra step weight for the non-intersecting Schröder paths. In general, one would want to associate weights
{\alpha},\beta,\gamma
respectively to up, down and horizontal steps. However, by simple rescaling of
u,v
we may restrict without loss of generality to only a weight
\gamma
per horizontal step.

Theorem 8.5
.

The partition function for domino tilings of the Aztec triangle of order
n
, with an extra weight
\gamma
per horizontal step in the non-intersecting Schröder path formulation reads:

(8.5)

\displaystyle Z^{DT,\gamma}_{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(f_{DT,\gamma}(u,v)\Big|_{u^{i}v^{j}}\right)

(8.6)

\displaystyle f_{DT,\gamma}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1+u}{1-v-2(1+\gamma)uv-u^{2}v+\gamma^{2}u^{2}v^{2}}

Proof.

We adapt the proof of Theorem
3.3
. By the Lindström-Gessel-Viennot theorem, the partition function function of the non-intersecting Schröder paths with the new weights is given by
Z^{DT,\gamma}_{n}=\det((M_{\gamma})_{n})
, where the subscript
n
indicates the truncation to the first
n
rows and columns of the infinite matrix
M_{\gamma}

whose entries
(M_{\gamma})_{i,j}
are the partition functions for a single path form
(0,2i)
to
(2j+1,2j+1)
,
counted with a multiplicative weight
\gamma
per horizontal step. We simply have to show that these entries are generated by:

f_{M_{\gamma}}(u,v)=\frac{1+u}{1-v-2(1+\gamma)uv-u^{2}v+\gamma^{2}u^{2}v^{2}}=f_{DT,\gamma}(u,v)

To show this, we start with the function
\sigma_{\gamma}(u,v):=\frac{1}{1-u-v-\gamma uv}
the partition function for arbitrary finite length Schröder paths from the origin, with weight
u
per up step,
v
per down step, and
\gamma uv
per horizontal step. Accordingly the partition function
(\Sigma_{\gamma})_{i,j}
of paths from the origin
with fixed endpoint
(i,j)
, with
i-j
even, is given by

(\Sigma_{\gamma})_{i,j}=\sigma_{\gamma}(u,v)|_{u^{\frac{i+j}{2}}v^{\frac{i-j}{2}}}
, or equivalently by a straightforward generalization of (
3.2
). Noting again that
(M_{\gamma})_{i,j}=(\Sigma_{\gamma})_{2j+1,2j+1-2i}
, we get

(M_{\gamma})_{i,j}=\oint\frac{dx}{2i\pi x}\frac{dy}{2i\pi y}\,\frac{1}{x^{2j+1-i}y^{i}}\,\frac{1}{1-x-y-\gamma xy}=\oint\frac{du}{2i\pi u}\frac{dv}{2i\pi v}\,\frac{1}{v^{2j+1}u^{i}}\,\frac{1}{1-v-uv-\gamma uv^{2}}

after again changing integration variables to
u=y/x,v=x
.
Using the same trick (
3.3
) as in previous section, we conclude that:

f_{M_{\gamma}}(u,v)=\frac{\frac{1}{1-(1+u)\sqrt{v}-\gamma uv}-\frac{1}{1+(1+u)\sqrt{v}-\gamma uv}}{2\sqrt{v}}=\frac{1+u}{(1-\gamma uv)^{2}-v(1+u)^{2}}

and the Theorem follows.
∎

The result above is easily extended to the refined domino tiling of Section.
6
.
The partition function
Z_{n}^{DT,\gamma}(t):=\sum_{k=1}^{n}Z^{DT,\gamma}_{n,k}\,t^{k-1}
now including a multiplicative weight
\gamma
per horizontal step in the non-intersecting Schröder path formulation of the refined tiling problem, can be directly calculated as follows (we leave the proof to the reader as a straightforward exercise).

Theorem 8.6
.

The generating function
Z^{DT}(t,\gamma)
reads:

\displaystyle Z^{DT}(t,\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det_{0\leq i,j\leq n-1}\left(f_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)\Big|_{u^{i}v^{j}}\right)

\displaystyle f_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{DT,\gamma}(u,v)+v^{n-1}\frac{{\alpha}_{+}(\gamma;u)^{2n}}{\sqrt{1+2(1+2\gamma)u+u^{2}}}\,\frac{(t-1)u}{{\alpha}_{+}(\gamma;u)-tu}

where
{\alpha}_{+}(\gamma,u)=\frac{1}{2}\big(1+u+\sqrt{1+2(1+2\gamma)u+u^{2}}\big)
and
f_{DT,\gamma}(u,v)
as in (
8.6
).

It is interesting to notice that the upper triangular matrix
L
involved in the proofs of Theorems
4.3
and
6.2
can be modified to obtain an alternative expression for the partition function generating function
Z^{DT,\gamma}(t)

above.
Indeed, let
L_{\gamma}
be the infinite matrix generated by:

f_{L_{\gamma}}(u,v):=\frac{1+2\gamma u-\gamma u^{2}}{1-u-vu(1+\gamma u)}

Writing
Z^{DT}(t,\gamma)=\det\big((L_{\gamma}{\tilde{M}}_{\gamma}^{(n)}(t))_{n}\big)
, where
{\tilde{M}}_{\gamma}^{(n)}(t)
is generated by
f_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)
above, we finally get:

Theorem 8.7
.

The generating function
Z^{DT}(t,\gamma)
reads:

\displaystyle Z^{DT}(t,\gamma)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det\Big({\bar{f}}_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)\big|_{u^{i}v^{j}}\Big)

\displaystyle{\bar{f}}_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(1+\gamma u^{2})(1+2\gamma u-\gamma u^{2})}{(1-\gamma^{2}u^{2}v)((1-u)^{2}-v(1+\gamma u)^{2})}+v^{n-1}\left(\frac{1+\gamma u}{1-u}\right)^{2n}\frac{(t-1)u}{1-tu}

Proof.

We use the infinite matrix
{\bar{M}}_{\gamma}^{(n)}(t):=L_{\gamma}{\tilde{M}}_{\gamma}^{(n)}(t)
, whose generating function is easily computed via the formula

f_{L_{\gamma}\,{\tilde{M}}_{\gamma}^{(n)}(t)}(u,v)=\frac{1+2\gamma u-\gamma u^{2}}{1-u}f_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}\left(u\frac{1+\gamma u}{1-u},v\right)={\bar{f}}_{DT,\gamma}^{\rm ref}(u,v)
.
∎

Note also that

\frac{(1+\gamma u^{2})(1+2\gamma u-\gamma u^{2})}{(1-\gamma^{2}u^{2}v)((1-u)^{2}-v(1+\gamma u)^{2})}=\frac{\left(\frac{1+\gamma u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}{1-v\left(\frac{1+\gamma u}{1-u}\right)^{2}}-\frac{\gamma^{2}u^{2}}{1-v\gamma^{2}u^{2}}

This
\gamma
-deformation of (
2.22
) is very suggestive, however we have not been able to find a counterpart of the parameter
\gamma
in the 20V model.

8.4.
Conclusion

Despite the great progress on proving the conjectures of Ref.
[
DFG20
]
, we are still only understanding part of the sequence of correspondences between the 20V model on the domains
\mathcal{P}_{n,k}
and the domino tiling of the domains
\mathcal{T}_{n,k}
. One way to gain a better global understanding of these would be to include deformation (spectral?) parameters in the correspondence of partition functions on
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
and
\mathcal{T}_{n}
respectively. However, the number of deformation parameters at hand is limited. For instance, the trick we used with the last
w_{n}=w
vertical spectral parameter cannot be adapted to horizontal spectral parameters: these are indeed all frozen to the same value
z=p^{-2}q^{-2}
in order for the relation to the U-turn boundary 6V partition function to hold. One may however try to keep more non-trivial values of the vertical spectral parameters
w_{n},w_{n-1},...
etc.

Another direction of study regards large
n
asymptotics. We expect the typical large configurations of the 20V model on
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
to exhibit an arctic curve phenomenon, such as that observed in
[
DDFG20
]
for the 20V DWBC1,2 models, and derived analytically using the tangent method of Colomo and Sportiello
[
CS16
]
. We intend to address this question in a future publication.

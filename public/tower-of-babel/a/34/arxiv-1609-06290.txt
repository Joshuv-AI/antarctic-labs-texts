Nested Words for Order-2 Pushdown Systems Partially supported by LIA InForMeL
C. Aiswarya, Paul Gastin, Prakash Saivasan

Abstract
We study linear time model checking of collapsible higher-order pushdown systems (CPDS) of order 2 (manipulating stack of stacks) against MSO and PDL (propositional dynamic logic with converse and loop) enhanced with push/pop matching relations. To capture these linear time behaviours with matchings, we propose order-2 nested words. These graphs consist of a word structure augmented with two binary matching relations, one for each order of stack, which relate a push with matching pops (or collapse) on the respective stack. Due to the matching relations, satisfiability and model checking are undecidable. Hence we propose an under-approximation, bounding the number of times an order-1 push can be popped. With this under-approximation, which still allows unbounded stack height, we get decidability for satisfiability and model checking of both MSO and PDL. The problems are ExpTime-Complete for PDL.

We propose propositional dynamic logic with loop and converse (
\mathsf{LCPDL}
) and
monadic second-order logic (
\mathsf{MSO}
) over order-2 nested words to specify

.
\mathsf{LCPDL}

These logics are very expressive since they can use the nesting relations. We

Our results are quite surprising, since they differ from the established results under classical semantics. Strikingly

•

Model checking and satisfiability problems of
\mathsf{MSO}
and

turn out to be
undecidable, even when the

is non-collapsible. Further, in our
undecidability proof, the height of the order-2 stack is bounded by 2. On the
other hand, MSO over non-collapsible 2-HOPDS under classical semantics is decidable

,
24

.

•

The satisfiability and model checking problems described above can be
reduced to that of non-collapsible
\textsf{2-CPDS}/\textsf{2-NWs}

fact that, when considering HOPDS under classical semantics

•

In

With this restriction we gain decidability for satisfiability and model checking
of
\mathsf{MSO}

\mathsf{LCPDL}

ExpTime
-Complete.

We establish decidability by showing that bounded-pop

Bounded split-width

Order-2 pushdown systems with collapse

Order-2 stacks.

An order-2 stack is a stack of stacks. In a
collapsible order-2 stack, the stack symbols may in addition contain a pointer
to some stack in the stack of stacks. Let
S
be a finite set of stack
symbols. An order-2 stack is of the form
W=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]]
, where
each
[u_{i}]
is a stack over
S
with collapse-pointers to stacks below.
Thus we may see the contents
u_{i}
of the
i
-th stack as a word from
S\times\{1,2,\dots,{i-1}\}
where the second component of an entry indicates
the index of the stack to which the collapse-link points. The empty order-2
stack is denoted
[[]]
, where the order-2 stack contains an empty stack. We
have the following operations on order-2 stacks:

•

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}
: duplicates the topmost stack in the order-2 stack.
That is,
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}][u_{n+1}]]
with
u_{n+1}=u_{n}
.

•

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}
: pops the topmost stack from the order-2 stack.
That is,
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n-1}]]
.
Notice that
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}([[u_{1}]])
is undefined.

•

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}^{s}}}
: pushes a symbol
s
to the top of the topmost stack. Further the pushed symbol contains a ‘‘collapse link’’ to the topmost but one stack of the order-2 stacks.
1
1

1

Collapse links to order-1 stack symbols, or pushes without collapse links are not considered for simplicity. These are, however, easy to simulate thanks to
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}
and the top-test.

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}^{s}}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}(s,n-1)]]
.

•

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}
: removes the topmost element from the topmost stack.

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u^{\prime}_{n}]]
, if
u_{n}=u^{\prime}_{n}(s,i)
.
Notice that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[]])
is undefined.

•

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}
:
the collapse operation pops the stacks in the order-2 stack until the stack
pointed-to by the link in the topmost symbol of the previously topmost stack
becomes the topmost stack.
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{i}]]
, if
u_{n}=u^{\prime}_{n}(s,i)
.

•

\mathsf{top}(s)
: checks if the topmost symbol of the topmost stack is
s
.

Hence,
\mathsf{top}(s)([[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]])=[[u_{1}][u_{2}]\dots[u_{n}]]
, if
u_{n}=u^{\prime}_{n}(s,i)
. It is undefined otherwise. Also, we can check whether the
topmost stack is empty by
\mathsf{top}(\bot)
.

The above defined operations form the set
\mathsf{Op}(S)
.

2-CPDS

is a finite state system over a finite alphabet

\Sigma
equipped with an order-2 stack. Formally it is a tuple
\mathcal{H}=(Q,S,\Delta,q_{0},F)
where

Q
is the finite set of states,
S
is the set of stack
symbols/labels,
q_{0}
is the initial state,
F
is the set
of accepting states, and
\Delta\subseteq Q\times\Sigma\times\mathsf{Op}(S)\times Q
is the set of transitions. On
the right, we have a
2-CPDS

\mathcal{H}_{1}
with collapse and a second one

\mathcal{H}_{2}
without collapse operations.

A
configuration
is a pair
C=(q,W)
where
q\in Q
is a state and
W
is an order-2 stack.
The
initial
configuration
C_{0}=(q_{0},[[]])
.
A configuration
C=(q,W)
is
accepting

if
q\in F
.
We write
C\xRightarrow{\tau}C^{\prime}
for configurations

C=(q,W)
,
C^{\prime}=(q^{\prime},W^{\prime})
and transition

\tau=(q,a,\mathsf{op}(s),q^{\prime})
, if
W^{\prime}=\mathsf{op}(s)(W)
.
A
run

\rho
of
\mathcal{H}
is an alternating sequence of configurations and
transitions, starting from the initial configuration, and conforming to the
relation
\xRightarrow{}
, i.e.,
\rho=C_{0}\xRightarrow{\tau_{1}}C_{1}\xRightarrow{\tau_{2}}C_{2}\ldots\xRightarrow{\tau_{n}}C_{n}
.
We say that
\rho
is an
accepting run
if
C_{n}
is accepting.

2-CPDS
as order-2

2-NW
). For instance,

and

Order-2 nested words (
2-NW
).

We propose words augmented with nesting relations to capture the behaviours of

2-CPDS
, analogous to nested words for pushdown systems. We have two nesting
relations
\curvearrowright^{1}
and
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
, for order-1 stacks and order-2 stacks
respectively. For each position
j
executing
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}
, we find the
position
i
at which the popped symbol was pushed and we link these matching
positions with
i\curvearrowright^{1}j
. Notice that since a stack may be duplicated multiple times, a

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}}}
event may have multiple
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}
partners. For instance, in
\mathcal{N}_{2}

above, the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}}}
at position 4 is matched by the
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}
at positions 6,12,19.
Similarly,
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
links
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}
events with matching
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}
events.
Every
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}
event may have at most one
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
partner, since each
pushed stack is popped at most once. A
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}
event is seen as popping
several stacks in one go, hence, several
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}
events may be linked to single

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}
event by
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
relation. For instance, in
\mathcal{N}_{1}
above
the collapse at position 11 pops the stacks pushed at positions 2,4,6.
Thus an order-2 nested word (
2-NW
) over an alphabet
\Sigma
is a tuple

\mathcal{N}=\langle w,\curvearrowright^{1},\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}\rangle
where
w
is a word over

\Sigma
, and
\curvearrowright^{1}
and
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
are binary relations over
positions of
w
.

The language of a
2-CPDS
over an alphabet
\Sigma
is a set of
2-NW

over
\Sigma
generated by accepting runs. It is denoted
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
. When depicting
2-NW
s, we
sometimes do not indicate the labelling by the finite alphabet, but often
indicates the type of the stack operation. When we express the letter of the
alphabet and the operation, we just write them next to each other. For example

a{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}^{s}}}
would mean that the label is
a
, and that position performs
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}^{s}}}
.

Given a generating sequence
\mathsf{op}_{0}\mathsf{op}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{n}\in\mathsf{Op}^{+}
of operations,
either it is not valid, or there are unique
\curvearrowright^{1}
and
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}

which conform to the order-2 stack policy. To characterize these relations, we
define the position
\mathsf{push}_{1}(n)
at which the current (after
\mathsf{op}_{n}
) top stack
symbol was pushed and the position
\mathsf{Push}_{2}(n)
at which the current top order-1
stack was pushed/duplicated. We let
\mathsf{push}_{1}(n)=-1
if the top (order-1) stack
is empty. We let
\mathsf{Push}_{2}(n)=-1
if the order-2 stack contains only one order-1
stack.
For instance, with the sequence generating
\mathcal{N}_{2}
we have

\mathsf{Push}_{2}(5)=5=\mathsf{Push}_{2}(7)
,
\mathsf{Push}_{2}(4)=-1=\mathsf{Push}_{2}(8)
,

\mathsf{push}_{1}(4)=4=\mathsf{push}_{1}(5)=\mathsf{push}_{1}(8)
and
\mathsf{push}_{1}(0)=0=\mathsf{push}_{1}(3)=\mathsf{push}_{1}(6)

and
\mathsf{push}_{1}(2)=-1=\mathsf{push}_{1}(7)
. Also, in the
2-NW

\mathcal{N}_{1}
we have

\mathsf{push}_{1}(11)=1
and
\mathsf{Push}_{2}(11)=0
.

Surprisingly,
\mathsf{push}_{1}
and
\mathsf{Push}_{2}
can be characterized by a context-free
grammar. We denote by
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
the languages defined by the
non-terminals
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
of the following grammar:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}S_{1}&\rightarrow&{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}}}\mid S_{1}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}\mid S_{1}S_{1}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}\mid S_{1}S_{2}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}\mid S_{1}S_{2}S_{1}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}\\
S_{2}&\rightarrow&{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\uparrow_{2}}}\mid S_{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\uparrow_{1}}}\mid S_{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}{\downarrow_{1}}}\mid S_{2}S_{2}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\downarrow_{2}}}\mid S_{2}S_{2}S_{1}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}{\Downarrow}}\,.\end{array}

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathsf{op}_{0}\mathsf{op}_{1}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{n}\in\mathsf{Op}^{+}
be a valid push/pop/collapse sequence.
Then, for all
0\leq i\leq j\leq n
we have (proof in
Appendix
A
)

P1.

\mathsf{push}_{1}(j)=i
iff
\mathsf{op}_{i}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{j}\in L_{1}
,

P2.

\mathsf{Push}_{2}(j)=i
iff
\mathsf{op}_{i}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{j}\in L_{2}
,

P3.

\mathsf{push}_{1}(j)=-1
iff
\mathsf{op}_{k}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{j}\notin L_{1}
for
all
0\leq k\leq j
,

P4.

\mathsf{Push}_{2}(j)=-1
iff
\mathsf{op}_{k}\cdots\mathsf{op}_{j}\notin L_{2}
for

.

This characterization will be crucial in the rest of the paper, to justify correctness of both formulas in Section
3

(

PDL and MSO over order-2 nested words

We introduce two logical formalisms for specifications over
2-NW
. The first
one is propositional dynamic logic which essentially navigates
through the edges of a
2-NW
, checking positional properties on the way. The
second one is the yardstick monadic second-order logic, which extends MSO over
words with the
\curvearrowright^{1}
and
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
binary relations.

Propositional dynamic logic was originally introduced in
[
18
]
to study
the branching behaviour of programs. Here we are not interested in the
branching behaviour. Instead we study the linear time behaviours (words)
enriched with the nesting relations. Since these are graphs, we take advantage
of the path formulas of PDL based on regular expressions to navigate in the

2-NWs
. This is in the spirit of

[
22
,
10
,
4
,
3
]
where PDL was used to specify
properties of graph structures such as message sequence charts or multiply
nested words.

Propositional Dynamic Logic with converse and loop (
\mathsf{LCPDL}
)

can
express properties of nodes (positions) as boolean combinations of the existence
of paths and loops. Paths are built using regular expressions over the edge
relations (and their converses) of order-2 nested words.
The syntax of the node formulas
\varphi
and path formulas
\pi
of
\mathsf{LCPDL}
are
given by

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle a\mid\varphi\vee\varphi\mid\neg\varphi\mid\langle\pi\rangle\varphi\mid\mathsf{Loop}(\pi)

\displaystyle\pi

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\{\varphi\}?\mid{\mathop{\rightarrow}}\mid{\leftarrow}\mid{\curvearrowright^{1}}\mid{\curvearrowleft^{1}}\mid{\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}}\mid{\mathrel{\red\curvearrowleft^{2}}}\mid\pi\cdot\pi\mid\pi+\pi\mid\pi^{\ast}

where
a\in\Sigma
. The node formulas are evaluated on positions of an order-2 nested word, whereas
path formulas are evaluated on pairs of positions.
We give the semantics below (
i,j,i^{\prime},j^{\prime}
vary
over positions of a
2-NW

\mathcal{N}=\langle w,\curvearrowright^{1},\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}\rangle
):

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models a

if

\displaystyle i\text{th letter of $w$ is $a$}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi_{1}\vee\varphi_{2}

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi_{1}\text{ or }\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models\neg\varphi

if

\displaystyle\text{ it is not the case that }\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models\langle\pi\rangle\varphi

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models\pi\text{ and }\mathcal{N},j\models\varphi\text{ for some j}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i\models\mathsf{Loop}(\pi)

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,i\models\pi

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models\{\varphi\}?

if

\displaystyle i=j\text{ and }\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\mathop{\rightarrow}}

if

\displaystyle j\text{ is the successor position of $i$ in the word $w$}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\leftarrow}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},j,i\models\mathop{\rightarrow}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\curvearrowright^{1}}

if

\displaystyle i\curvearrowright^{1}j\text{ in the $\textsf{2-NW}$ }\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\curvearrowleft^{1}}

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},j,i\models{\curvearrowright^{1}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}}

if

\displaystyle i\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}j\text{ in the $\textsf{2-NW}$ }\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\mathrel{\red\curvearrowleft^{2}}}

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},j,i\models{\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\pi_{1}\cdot\pi_{2}}

if

\displaystyle\text{ there is a position $k$ such that }\mathcal{N},i,k\models\pi_{1}\text{ and }\mathcal{N},k,j\models{\pi_{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\pi_{1}+\pi_{2}}

if

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models\pi_{1}\text{ or }\mathcal{N},i,j\models{\pi_{2}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{N},i,j\models\pi^{\ast}

if

there exist positions
i_{1},\dots,i_{n}
for some
n\geq 1

such that
i=i_{1}
,
j=i_{n}
and
\mathcal{N},i_{m},i_{m+1}\models\pi
for all
1\leq m<n

An
\mathsf{LCPDL}

sentence
is a boolean combination of atomic sentences of the form

\mathsf{E}\varphi
. An atomic
\mathsf{LCPDL}
sentence is evaluated on an order-2

\mathcal{N}
. We have
\mathcal{N}\models\mathsf{E}\varphi

of
\mathcal{N}
such that
\mathcal{N},i\models\varphi
.

We use abbreviations to include
\mathsf{true}
,
\mathsf{false}
, conjunction, implication,

) etc. We

We give now path formulas corresponding to the functions

In all of this section, by

we mean order-2 nested words
without

\textsf{2-NW}(\beta)

the core of our decision procedures.

A Split-
2-NW

is a
2-NW
in which the underlying word has been split in several factors.
Formally, a split-
2-NW
is a tuple

\overline{\mathcal{N}}=\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{m},\curvearrowright^{1},\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}\rangle

is a
2-NW
.
The number
m
of factors in a split-
2-NW
is called it’s width.
A
2-NW
is a split-
2-NW
of width one.

A split-
2-NW
can be seen as a labelled graph whose vertices are the
positions of the underlying word (concatenation of the factors) and we have order-1 edges
\curvearrowright^{1}
,
order-2 edges
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
and successor edges
\mathop{\rightarrow}
between consecutive
positions
within
a factor.
We say that a split-
2-NW
is connected if the underlying graph is
connected
.
If a split-
2-NW
is not connected, then its connected components form a
partition of its factors.

Example 11
.

Consider the split-
2-NW
on the right.
It has two connected components, and its width is five.

.

The split game

is a two-player turn based game
\mathcal{G}=\langle V=V_{\forall}\uplus V_{\exists},E\rangle
where
Eve’s positions
V_{\exists}
consists of (connected or not)

2-NWs
, and
Adam’s positions

consists of non connected split-
2-NWs
.
The edges

\mathcal{G}
reflect the moves of the players.
Eve’s moves consist in removing some successor
edges in the graph, i.e., splitting some factors, so that the resulting graph
is not connected.
Adam’s moves amounts to choosing a connected component.
A split-
2-NW
is atomic if it is connected and all
its factors are singletons. An atomic split-
2-NW
contains either a single
internal event, or, a single push with all its corresponding pops.
A play on a split-
2-NW

\overline{\mathcal{N}}
is path in
\mathcal{G}
starting from
\overline{\mathcal{N}}
to
an atomic split-
2-NW
. The cost of the play is the maximum width of any
split-
2-NW
encountered in the path. Eve’s objective is to minimize the cost
and Adam’s objective is to maximize the cost.

A strategy for Eve from a split-

8
Conclusion

In this paper we study the linear behaviour of a
2-CPDS
by giving extra structure to words. The specification formalisms can make use of this structure to describe properties of the system. This added structure comes with the cost of undecidable verification problems. We identify an under approximation that regains decidability for verification problems. Our decision procedure makes use of the split-width technique.

This work is a first step towards further questions that must be investigated.
One direction would be to identify other under-approximations which are
orthogonal / more lenient than bounded-pop for decidability. Whether similar
results can be obtained for order-n CPDS is also another interesting future
work. The language theory of CPDS where the language consists of nested-word
like structures is another topic of interest.

First, we construct a tree automaton

.

Proposition 14

. The tree

Proposition 15
.

For each
\mathsf{LCPDL}
formula
\phi
we can construct a tree automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi}
of
size
2^{\mathsf{Poly}(\beta,|\phi|)}
such that

\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi})=\{\tau\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}})\mid G_{\tau}\in\mathcal{L}(\phi)\}
.

Proof.

The idea is to translate the
\mathsf{LCPDL}
formula
\phi
to an alternating two-way tree
automaton (A2A) of size
\mathsf{Poly}(\beta,|\phi|)
. Due to the specific form of the

\mathsf{STTs}
accepted by
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}}
, it is easy to encode the nesting relations

\curvearrowright^{1}
and
\mathrel{\red\curvearrowright^{2}}
with a walking automaton. We can also easily
build a walking automaton for the successor relation
\mathop{\rightarrow}
by tracking the
colors until we reach a node labelled
\mathop{\mathsf{Add}_{i,j}^{\mathop{\rightarrow}}}
. One main
difficulty is to cope with loops of
\mathsf{LCPDL}
.
Here we use the result of
[
19
]
for PDL with converse and
intersection. In general, this can cause an exponential blow-up in the size of
the A2A. But loop is a special case with bounded intersection-width and hence
still allows a polynomial sized A2A.
Finally, the A2A for
\phi
is translated to the normal tree automaton
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi}
,
causing an exponential blow-up.
∎

We deduce that the bounded-pop satisfiability problem of
\mathsf{LCPDL}
can be solved in
exponential time by checking emptiness of
\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi}
.
Also, the bounded-pop model checking problem of
2-CPDS
against
\mathsf{LCPDL}
can be
solved in exponential time by checking emptiness of

\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\mathcal{H}}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\neg\phi}
.

Similarly, for each
\mathsf{MSO}
formula
\phi
, we can construct a tree automaton

\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}}\cap\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\phi})=\{\tau\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{A}^{\beta}_{\textsf{2-NW}})\mid G_{\tau}\in\mathcal{L}(\phi)\}
. We deduce the decidability of the bounded-pop
satisfiability problem of
\mathsf{MSO}
and of the bounded-pop model checking problem
of
2-CPDS
against
\mathsf{MSO}
. This concludes the proof of Theorem

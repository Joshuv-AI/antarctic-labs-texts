Hypergeometric identities related to Ruijsenaars systems


Abstract
We present a proof of hypergeometric identities which play a crucial role in the theory of Baxter operators in the Ruijsenaars model.

1
Introduction

1.1
Statement

In the paper
[
R1
]
S. Ruijsenaars showed that the crucial properties of his
kernel function
, which serves for the solution of the Ruijsenaars system
[
HR1
,
HR2
]
, are given by certain functional identity (now known as “kernel function identity”) found in
[
KN
]
by Y. Kajihara and M. Noumi. It states that for any odd function
s(z)
of a complex variable
z
, satisfying the Riemann relation

\begin{split}s(x+y)s(x-y)s(u+v)s(u-v)=&\,s(x+u)s(x-u)s(y+v)s(y-v)-\\
&\,s(x+v)s(x-v)s(y+u)s(y-u),\end{split}

(1.1)

and any complex parameter
\alpha
the following identity holds

\begin{split}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{r}\subset[n]\\
|I_{r}|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I_{r}}\left(\prod_{j\in[n]\setminus I_{r}}\frac{s(z_{i}-z_{j}-\alpha)}{s(z_{i}-z_{j})}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{s(z_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{s(z_{i}-y_{a})}\right)=\\
\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A_{r}\subset[n]\\
|A_{r}|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{a\in A_{r}}\left(\prod_{b\in[n]\setminus A_{r}}\frac{s(y_{a}-y_{b}+\alpha)}{s(y_{a}-y_{b})}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{s(z_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{s(z_{i}-y_{a})}\right).\end{split}

(1.2)

Here
[n]
denotes the set

[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}\,.

In
[
BDKK
]
, studying the Baxter operators in hyperbolic Ruijsenaars system, we found that the fundamental properties of these Baxter operator are governed by another functional identities of hypergeometric type, generalizing (
1.2
) in rational (
s(z)=z
) and trigonometric cases (
s(z)=\sin\beta z
). Following the terminology of
[
KN
]
, one can name them as certain “duality transformations for multiple hypergeometric series”.
In rational case they read as

\begin{split}\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(1+\alpha)_{k_{i}}}{k_{i}!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x_{i}-x_{j}-k_{j}-\alpha)_{k_{i}}}{(x_{i}-x_{j}-k_{j})_{k_{i}}}\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{a}+\alpha)_{k_{j}}}{(x_{j}-y_{a})_{k_{j}}}=\\
\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{(1+\alpha)_{k_{a}}}{k_{a}!}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(y_{a}-y_{b}-k_{a}-\alpha)_{k_{b}}}{(y_{a}-y_{b}-k_{a})_{k_{b}}}\prod_{j,a=1}^{n}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{a}+\alpha)_{k_{a}}}{(x_{j}-y_{a})_{k_{a}}}.\end{split}

(1.3)

Here the sum is taken over all
n
-tuples

\bm{k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),\qquad k_{i}\geq 0,\qquad k_{1}+\ldots+k_{n}=K

(1.4)

of non-negative integers such that their sum equals
K
, and

(x)_{n}=x(x+1)\cdots(x+n-1)

is the Pochhammer symbol.

The trigonometric version we write down with a help of
q
-analogs
(z;q)_{n}
of the Pochhammer symbol,

(z;q)_{n}=(1-z)(1-qz)\cdots(1-q^{n-1}z).

(1.5)

Then using the same notation (
1.4
) for the summation we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{{|\bm{k}|=K}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;q)_{k_{i}}}{(q;q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q)_{k_{i}}}{(q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{j}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{j}}}=\\
&\sum_{{|\bm{k}|=K}}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;q)_{k_{a}}}{(q;q)_{k_{a}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q)_{k_{b}}}{(q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q)_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{a}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};q)_{k_{a}}}.\end{split}

(1.6)

A sketch of the proof of (
1.6
) is given in
[
BDKK
]
. In this note we present the complete proof with all necessary technical details.

[

K

Proof

The proofs of (
1.3
) and (
1.6
) are similar. In fact, the identity (
1.3
) can be obtained as the limit of (
1.6
), so we prove (
1.6
). For the proof
it is more convenient to rewrite the identity (
1.6
) in terms of symmetric
q
-analogs of the Pochhammer symbols,

[z;q]_{n}=(z^{1/2}-z^{-1/2})(q^{1/2}z^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}z^{-1/2})\cdots(q^{(n-1)/2}z^{1/2}-q^{(-n+1)/2}z^{-1/2}).

(2.1)

Then (
1.6
) becomes

\begin{split}&\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{[qt;q]_{k_{i}}}{[q;q]_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q]_{k_{i}}}{[q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q]_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{j}}}{[u_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{j}}}=\\
&\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{[qt;q]_{k_{a}}}{[q;q]_{k_{a}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{a}}}{[u_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{a}}}.\end{split}

(2.2)

The proof uses the standard arguments from the complex analysis: we check in a rather tricky way
that the difference between the left and right hand sides has zero residues at all possible simple poles. Thus, both sides are the polynomials symmetric over the variables
u_{i}
and over the variables
v_{j}
. Then the asymptotic analysis of these polynomials shows that their difference is actually equal to zero.

The crucial step — calculation of the residues of both sides of the equality — divides into two parts. First we show that each side is regular at the diagonals
u_{i}=q^{p}u_{j}
and
v_{a}=q^{s}v_{b}
between the variables of the same group, see Lemma
1
. In this calculation we actually observe the canceling of terms grouped in corresponding pairs. Then we show that residues at mixed diagonals
u_{i}=q^{p}v_{a}
vanish. This is done by induction, using the nontrivial relation between such residues stated in Lemma
2
.

During the calculations we use the following properties of symmetric
q
-Pochhammer symbols

\displaystyle[q^{p}u;q]_{m}\times[u]_{n}=[q^{p}u]_{n-p}\times[u]_{m+p},

(2.3)

\displaystyle[qu;q]_{m}\times[q^{-(m+p)}u^{-1};q]_{n}=(-1)^{p}[qu;q]_{m+p}\times[q^{-m}u^{-1};q]_{n-p}

(2.4)

which are valid for any
u
and integer
m,n,p
.
Here we assume that

[z;q]_{-n}=(q^{1/2}z^{1/2}-q^{-1/2}z^{-1/2})^{-1}\cdots(q^{n/2}z^{1/2}-q^{-n/2}z^{-1/2})^{-1},\qquad n>0.

(2.5)

It is not difficult to verify that all the poles in (
2.2
) are simple (for generic parameter values). Consider the left hand side of (
2.2
) as the function of
u_{1}
and calculate the residue of this function at the point

u_{1}=u_{2}q^{p},\qquad p\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.6)

For each
\bm{k}
,
\sum_{j=1}^{n}k_{j}=K
denote by
\operatorname{U}_{\bm{k}}=\operatorname{U}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
the corresponding summand of the left hand side of (
2.2
), and by
\operatorname{V}_{\bm{k}}=\operatorname{V}_{\bm{k}}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
the corresponding summand of the right hand side of (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\operatorname{U}_{\bm{k}}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{[qt;q]_{k_{i}}}{[q;q]_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q]_{k_{i}}}{[q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};q]_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{j}}}{[u_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{j}}},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\operatorname{V}_{\bm{k}}=\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{[qt;q]_{k_{a}}}{[q;q]_{k_{a}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{a}}}{[u_{j}/v_{a};q]_{k_{a}}}.

(2.8)

The summands
\operatorname{U}_{\bm{k}}
, which contribute to the residue at the point (
2.6
), are divided into two groups.
The denominators of the terms
\operatorname{U}_{\bm{k}}
from the group

contain Pochhammer symbol

which vanishes at the point (

). It happens when

(2.21)

where

C=-\frac{[tq;q]_{k_{1}}}{[q;q]_{k_{1}}}\cdot\frac{[tq^{1-p};q]_{k_{1}}}{[q^{1-p};q]_{p-1}[q;q]_{k_{1}-p}}.

(2.22)

Here we decomposed two fractions of Pochhammers into the products of four fractions. In this presentation there are two products which do not depend on
k
indices. Put them in the front and use the equality
v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}
. Then the residue (
2.21
) looks as

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Res}_{v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}}\frac{1}{v_{1}}\operatorname{V}_{k_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{u};\bm{v})=C\cdot\operatorname{V}^{\prime}\cdot\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[tu_{1}/v_{b};q]_{p}}{[v_{1}/v_{b}]_{p}}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{1};q]_{p}}{[u_{j}/u_{1};q]_{p}}\times\\
&\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{b}}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}}}{[q^{-k_{b}}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}}}\cdot\frac{[tqv_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}-p}}{[qv_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}-p}}\times\\
&\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{1}}v_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[q^{-k_{1}}v_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{[tqu_{j}/u_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[qu_{j}/u_{1}]_{k_{1}-p}}.\end{split}

(2.23)

Now we use (
2.4
) in the second line of (
2.23
) together with the relation
v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}
.
We get

\begin{split}&\operatorname{Res}_{v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}}\frac{1}{v_{1}}\operatorname{V}_{k_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{u};\bm{v})=C\cdot\operatorname{V}^{\prime}\cdot\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[tu_{1}/v_{b};q]_{p}}{[v_{b}/v_{1}]_{p}}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{1};q]_{p}}{[u_{1}/u_{j};q]_{p}}\times\\
&\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[tqv_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[qv_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\cdot\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{b}+p}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[q^{-k_{b}+p}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}\times\\
&\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{1}}v_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[q^{-k_{1}}v_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{[tqu_{j}/u_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[qu_{j}/u_{1}]_{k_{1}-p}}.\end{split}

(2.24)

Set

v^{*}_{1}=q^{-1}u_{1},\qquad u^{*}_{1}=qv_{1}.

(2.25)

Then we can read two last lines in (
2.24
) as

\begin{split}\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[tu^{*}_{1}/y_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[u^{*}_{1}/y_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\cdot\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{1}+p}v^{*}_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}{[q^{-k_{1}+p}v^{*}_{1}/v_{b};q]_{k_{b}}}\cdot\frac{[t^{-1}q^{-k_{b}}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[q^{-k_{b}}v_{b}/v_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}\cdot\prod_{j\not=1}\frac{[tu_{j}/v^{*}_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[u_{j}/v^{*}_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}.\end{split}

(2.26)

One can recognize in (
2.26
) the factor of the product
\operatorname{V}_{k_{1}-p,\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u^{*}_{1},\bm{u}^{\prime};v^{*}_{1},\bm{v}^{\prime})
with missing constant

C^{\prime}=\frac{[tq;q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[q;q]_{k_{1}-p}}\times\frac{[tu^{*}_{1}/v^{*}_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[u^{*}_{1}/v^{*}_{1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}=\frac{[tq;q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[q;q]_{k_{1}-p}}\times\frac{[tq^{p+1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}{[q^{p+1};q]_{k_{1}-p}}.

(2.27)

We conclude that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}_{v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}}\frac{1}{v_{1}}\operatorname{V}_{k_{1},\bm{k}^{\prime}}(\bm{u};\bm{v})=

\displaystyle\frac{C}{C^{\prime}}\cdot\prod_{b\not=1}\frac{[tu_{1}/v_{b};q]_{p}}{[v_{b}/v_{1}]_{p}}\cdot\prod_{j=2}^{n}\frac{[tu_{j}/v_{1};q]_{p}}{[u_{1}/u_{j};q]_{p}}\times\operatorname{V}_{k_{1}-p,\bm{k}^{\prime}}(u^{*}_{1},\bm{u}^{\prime};v^{*}_{1},\bm{v}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle(-1)^{p}\frac{[tq;q]_{2p}}{[q;q]_{p}[q;q]_{p-1}}\operatorname{V}_{k_{1}-p,\bm{k}^{\prime}}(qv_{1},\bm{u}^{\prime};q^{-1}u_{1},\bm{v}^{\prime}).

(2.28)

The proof of (
2.19
) is analogous. One can get it by combining the involution (
2.16
) with the
previous arguments.
\Box

Proof of the identity (
2.2
).
Now we are ready to prove (
2.2
) by induction over
K
. Denote the difference between the left and right hand sides of (
2.2
) by
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
. Assume that
W_{K}(\bm{u},\bm{v})=0
for all
K<N
and any
m
-tuples of
variables
\bm{u}=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{m})
,
\bm{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{m})
for arbitrary
m
.
Summing up the difference of (
2.19
) and (
2.18
) over all
\bm{k}
with
|\bm{k}|=K
we get the
relation

\operatorname{Res}_{v_{1}=q^{p-1}u_{1}}\frac{1}{v_{1}}W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})=\varphi_{p}(\bm{u};\bm{v})\times W_{K-p}(\bm{u}^{*},\bm{v}^{*}),

(2.29)

where

\bm{u}^{*}=(qv_{1},\bm{u}^{\prime}),\qquad\bm{v}^{*}=(q^{-1}u_{1},\bm{v}^{\prime})

(2.30)

and by the induction assumption the right hand side of (
2.29
) equals zero. Taking in mind the symmetry of
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
with respect to permutation of
u_{i}
and of
v_{j}
we conclude that it has no poles at all. Since
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
is a homogeneous rational function of the variables
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
of total degree zero, it is equal to a constant, which could depend on
q
and
t
. To compute this constant, we consider the behavior of this function in asymptotic zone

u_{1}\ll u_{2}\ll\ldots\ll u_{n}\ll v_{n}\ll v_{n-1}\ll\ldots\ll v_{1}.

(2.31)

Here both sides of (
2.2
) tend to

\sum_{{|\bm{k}_{n}|=K}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{[qt;q]_{k_{i}}}{[q;q]_{k_{i}}}\times t^{\frac{1}{2}\big((n-1)k_{1}+(n-3)k_{2}+\ldots+(3-n)k_{n-1}+(1-n)k_{n}\big)}\times t^{-\frac{nK}{2}}.

(2.32)

Thus,
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
tends to zero in this asymptotic zone and so equals zero identically.
Another way to verify the vanishing of the constant value of
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
is to consider

W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
at the plane

u_{i}=t^{-1}v_{i},\qquad i=1,\ldots,n,

(2.33)

where it is identically zero due to the last products in each summands.
This completes the induction step and the proof of the identity (
2.2
) and thus of (
1.6
).
\Box

Comments

1
. Note first that the trigonometric kernel function identity (
1.2
) is a particular limit of the trigonometric hypergeometric identity (
1.6
), as well as the rational kernel function identity is a particular limit of the rational hypergeometric identity (
1.3
).

Rescale simultaneously all the variables
x_{j}
,
y_{a}
and
\alpha
in (
1.3
)

x_{j}\to\varepsilon x_{j},\qquad y_{a}\to\varepsilon y_{a},\qquad\alpha\to\varepsilon\alpha,

and tend the rescaling constant
\varepsilon
to zero. In this limit the relation (
1.3
) becomes

\begin{split}\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
k_{i}\not=0,\,k_{j}=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x_{i}-x_{j}-\alpha)}{(x_{i}-x_{j})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,j=1\\
k_{j}\not=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{(x_{j}-y_{a})}-\\
\sum_{|\bm{k}|=K}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
k_{a}=0,\,k_{b}\not=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(y_{a}-y_{b}-\alpha)}{(y_{a}-y_{b})}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,a=1\\
k_{a}\not=0\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(x_{j}-y_{a}+\alpha)}{(x_{j}-y_{a})}=0.\end{split}

(3.1)

Denote by
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
the left hand side of (
3.1
) and by
{\mathcal{K}}_{r}
the left hand side of the
r
-th rational kernel function identity:

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}I_{r}\subset[n]\\
|I_{r}|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{i\in I_{r}}\left(\prod_{j\in[n]\setminus I_{r}}\frac{x_{i}-x_{j}-\alpha}{x_{i}-x_{j}}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha}{x_{i}-y_{a}}\right)-\\
&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}A_{r}\subset[n]\\
|A_{r}|=r\end{subarray}}\prod_{a\in A_{r}}\left(\prod_{b\in[n]\setminus A_{r}}\frac{y_{a}-y_{b}+\alpha}{y_{a}-y_{b}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{x_{i}-y_{a}+\alpha}{x_{i}-y_{a}}\right)=0.\end{split}

(3.2)

We see that
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}={\mathcal{K}}_{1}
, that is the relation (
3.1
) for
K=1
coincides with the relation (
3.2
) for
r=1
. Next

{\mathcal{H}}_{2}={\mathcal{K}}_{2}+{\mathcal{K}}_{1}

(3.3)

where the first term in right hand side of (
3.3
) corresponds to partitions
\bm{k}=(1,1,0,\ldots,0)
and their permutations while the second to the partitions
\bm{k}=(2,0,\ldots,0)
. Thus we get (
3.2
) for
r=2
.
Going further we represent each
{\mathcal{H}}_{K}
as a sum of
{\mathcal{K}}_{K}
and of
{\mathcal{K}}_{r}
with
r<K
taken with some combinatorial coefficients. By induction we get all the relations
(
3.2
) from (
3.1
).

In trigonometric case we put

u_{i}=e^{2\imath\beta x_{i}},\qquad v_{a}=e^{2\imath\beta y_{a}},\qquad t=e^{2\imath\beta\alpha},\qquad q=e^{L}

and tend in (
2.2
) the positive constant
L
to infinity. By the same arguments we get (
1.2
) for
s(z)=\sin\beta z
.
Note that the original Ruijsenaars identity (
1.7
) on a single tuple of variables could not be derived from the identity (
1.2
) on the two tuples of variables. Probably, the same negative statement holds for the identity (
1.6
).

2
. The hypergeometric identities (
1.6
) remains valid, if we replace the
q
-Pochhammer symbol (
1.5
) by its elliptic analog

(z;p,q)_{k}=\theta(z;p)\theta(qz;p)\cdots\theta(q^{k-1}z;p)

(3.4)

where
|p|<1
and

\theta(z;p)=\prod_{n\geq 0}(1-p^{n}z)\prod_{m>0}(1-p^{m}z^{-1})

(3.5)

is the modified theta function, so that the identity (
1.6
) takes the form

\begin{split}&\sum_{{|\bm{k}|=K}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;p,q)_{k_{i}}}{(q;p,q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j=1\\
i\not=j\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};p,q)_{k_{i}}}{(q^{-k_{j}}u_{i}/u_{j};p,q)_{k_{i}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};p,q)_{k_{j}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};p,q)_{k_{j}}}=\\
&\sum_{{|\bm{k}|=K}}\prod_{a=1}^{n}\frac{(qt;p,q)_{k_{a}}}{(q;p,q)_{k_{a}}}\times\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}a,b=1\\
a\not=b\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{(t^{-1}q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};p,q)_{k_{b}}}{(q^{-k_{a}}v_{a}/v_{b};p,q)_{k_{b}}}\times\prod_{a,j=1}^{n}\frac{(tu_{j}/v_{a};p,q)_{k_{a}}}{(u_{j}/v_{a};p,q)_{k_{a}}}.\end{split}

(3.6)

The difference
W_{K}(\bm{u};\bm{v})
between the left and right hand sides of (
3.6
) satisfies quasiperiodicity conditions

\begin{split}W_{K}(u_{1},\ldots,pu_{i},\ldots,u_{n};\bm{v})&=t^{-K}W_{K}(u_{1},\ldots,u_{i},\ldots,u_{n};\bm{v}),\\
W_{K}(\bm{u};v_{1},\ldots,pv_{i},\ldots,v_{n})&=t^{K}\ W_{K}(\bm{u};v_{1},\ldots,v_{i},\ldots,v_{n}).\end{split}

(3.7)

By using (

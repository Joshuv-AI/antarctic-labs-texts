A proof of the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture for monotone and MDS-constructible Ferrers diagrams
Alessandro Neri, Mima Stanojkovski

Abstract
Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes were introduced by Etzion and Silberstein in 2009. In their work, they proposed a conjecture on the largest dimension of a space of matrices over a finite field whose nonzero elements are supported on a given Ferrers diagram and all have rank lower bounded by a fixed positive integer d d . Since stated, the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture has been verified in a number of cases, often requiring additional constraints on the field size or on the minimum rank d d in dependence of the corresponding Ferrers diagram. As of today, this conjecture still remains widely open. Using modular methods, we give a constructive proof of the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture for the class of strictly monotone Ferrers diagrams, which does not depend on the minimum rank d d and holds over every finite field. In addition, we leverage on the last result to also prove the conjecture for the class of MDS-constructible Ferrers diagrams, without requiring any restriction on the field size. Keywords : Rank-metric codes, Ferrers diagrams, Etzion-Silberstein conjecture, monotone diagrams, convex diagrams, MDS-constructible diagrams. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification : 11T71, 15A03, 16S35, 94B05, 94B60.

Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field and let
1\leq d\leq n
and
1\leq k\leq n^{2}
be integers. Let, moreover,
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
denote the collection of
n\times n
matrices with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}
. Then the map

\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\quad(A,B)\longmapsto\rk(A-B)

defines a metric on
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
. An
[n\times n,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}

rank-metric code
is a
k
-dimensional linear subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
such that
d
is the
minimum rank
among the nonzero elements of
\mathcal{C}
.
Rank-metric codes have a long mathematical history
[
6
,
9
]
and have become very popular in the field of algebraic coding theory since the discovery of their applications to network coding
[
18
]
.
Thanks to the work of Delsarte
[
6
]
, the parameters of an
[n\times n,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
rank-metric code are related by the following Singleton-like Bound:

(1)

k\leq n(n-d+1).

Codes meeting the last bound with equality are called
maximum rank distance codes
(MRDs, for short) and constructions of such codes are known over finite fields and can be found in
[
6
,
9
]
.

If rank-metric codes allow for codes within the entire matrix space
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
, the more general
Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes
are rank-metric codes with prescribed support. These were introduced in 2009 by Etzion and Silberstein
[
8
]
motivated by the application of subspace codes in network coding and arise from subspace codes in a fixed Schubert cell. The study of Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes has been carried on since by various authors
[
2
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
13
,
15
,
16
]
.

Let
[n]=\{1,\ldots,n\}
and let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[n]^{2}

be a top-right justified
Ferrers diagram
(cf.
Definition

2.2
) outside of which the elements of the code will have entries equal to
0
.
A
[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}

Ferrers diagram rank-metric code
is an
[n\times n,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
rank-metric code
\mathcal{C}
such that if
A=(a_{i,j})\in\mathcal{C}
and
(x,y)\in[n]^{2}\setminus\mathcal{D}
, then
a_{x,y}=0
.
Two immediate examples are:

(1)

If
\mathcal{D}=[n]^{2}
, then a
[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
Ferrers diagram rank-metric code is the same as an
[n\times n,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
rank-metric code.

(2)

If
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{T}_{n}=\{(i,j):i,j\in[n]\textup{ with }i\leq j\}
, then a
[\mathcal{T}_{n},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
Ferrers diagram rank-metric code is an
[n\times n,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
rank-metric code that is contained in the space of upper triangular
n\times n
matrices with coefficients over
\mathbb{F}
.

Adding constraints to the support of the matrices in the code clearly influences the relation between its intrinsic parameters. In
[
8
]
, the upper bound on the dimension given in (
1
) is generalized to an explicit upper bound
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)
taking into account the prescribed support
\mathcal{D}
; cf. (
6
) for the exact value of
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)
. For instance:

(1)

If
\mathcal{D}=[n]^{2}
, then
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=n(n-d+1)
recovers the bound in (
1
).

(2)

If
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{T}_{n}
, then
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)
is equal to

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{n},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}i=\binom{n-d+2}{2}=\frac{(n-d+1)(n-d+2)}{2}.

A
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
Ferrers diagram rank-metric code is called a
maximum Ferrers diagram code
(MFD, for short): Etzion and Silberstein conjectured that these codes exist for every choice of
d
and
\mathcal{D}
, and over every finite field
\mathbb{F}
. This is now known as the
Etzion-Silberstein conjecture
and, as of today, it is still open in its full generality.
In particular and to the best of our knowledge, not even the case of upper triangular matrices has been tackled completely. The instances of existing
[\mathcal{T}_{n},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{T}_{n},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD codes that are known to us and that do not impose any restriction on the field
\mathbb{F}
are the following:

•

The case where
d=1
, which consists of all the upper triangular matrices.

•

The case where
d=2
, for which an MFD code is given by the subspace of upper triangular matrices where every diagonal sum is zero.

•

The case where
d=3
, which is settled by
[
2
, Corollary 3.10]
.

•

The case where
d=n-1
, which is given by
[
2
, Theorem 5.3]
(for a constructive proof see
[
1
, Section 2.4]
).

All other cases are settled with the additional assumption that
|\mathbb{F}|\geq n-1
, as shown in
[
7
]
. This is due to the fact that the pair
(\mathcal{T}_{n},d)
is
MDS-constructible
for every
d
and
n
; cf.
Remark

2.13
.

In this paper, we fill the gap by providing a constructive proof of the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture for the class of MDS-constructible Ferrers diagrams without imposing any condition on the field size; cf.
Theorem

4.22
.
We do this by first proving the conjecture for the subclass of
strictly monotone Ferrers diagrams
; cf
Theorem

4.10
. These are Ferrers diagrams that do not allow two nonempty columns to have the same number of elements, and include therefore the upper triangular Ferrers diagrams
\mathcal{T}_{n}
as a special subclass. By adjunction, we also prove the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture for
initially convex
Ferrers diagrams; cf.
Definition

4.4
.

We conclude this introduction with a short account of our methods. To produce MFD codes for strictly monotone Ferrers diagrams, we extend Delsarte-Gabidulin constructions of MRD codes, which are realized through the identification of
\mathbb{F}^{n\times n}
with a skew algebra
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
, where
\mathbb{L}
is an auxiliary cyclic field extension of
\mathbb{F}
of degree
n
with Galois group generated by
\sigma
.
In
Theorem

3.4
, for a given finite field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
and
n=p^{m}
, we determine a skew algebra representation of the space of matrices supported on a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[n]^{2}
, when
\mathcal{D}
is
p
-monotone
; cf.
Definition

2.22
. The crucial properties used are that
\mathbb{L}
has degree
p^{m}
over
\mathbb{F}
and that
\sigma-\id
is a nilpotent
\mathbb{F}
-linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{L}
. We subsequently employ
Theorem

3.4
to prove the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture for
p
-monotone and
p
-convex Ferrers diagrams over finite fields of characteristic
p
and where
n
is a power of
p
; cf.
Theorem

4.2
.
For strictly monotone (and thus initially convex) Ferrers diagrams, we are able to move away from the modular case by showing that the property of being strictly monotone is preserved by embeddings.
Finally, we further extend our results to the class of MDS-constructible Ferrers diagrams, relying on the combinatorial features of their diagonals.

2
Notation and preliminaries

Let
n
be a positive integer and let
\mathbb{F}
be a field with a cyclic Galois extension
\mathbb{L}
of degree
n
. Fixing a generator
\sigma
of the Galois group
\Gal(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{F})
, the
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
(\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{L}),+,\circ)
can be represented as a skew group algebra
(\mathbb{L}[\sigma],+,\cdot)
. This is defined as the set

\mathbb{L}[\sigma]=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\,:\,a_{i}\in\mathbb{L}\right\}

endowed with usual addition, while the multiplication is defined on monomials via

(a\sigma^{i})\cdot(b\sigma^{j})=a\sigma^{i}(b)\sigma^{i+j},\qquad\mbox{ for }a,b\in\mathbb{L},

and extended by distributivity to
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
. The map

\mathbb{L}[\sigma]\rightarrow\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{L})
given by

(2)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{L}[\sigma]&\longrightarrow&\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{L})\\
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\sigma^{i}&\longmapsto&\Big(\alpha\longmapsto\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}\sigma^{i}(\alpha)\Big)\end{array}

is an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra isomorphism as a consequence of Artin’s Theorem of linear independence of characters.
By choosing any
\mathbb{F}
-basis
\mathcal{B}
of
\mathbb{L}
, one obtains an induced
\mathbb{F}
-algebra isomorphism

(3)

{\phi_{\mathcal{B}}:}\ \mathbb{L}[\sigma]\ \ \longrightarrow\ \ \mathbb{F}^{n\times n},

where the columns of the matrix represent the images of the basis vectors, per usual Linear Algebra practice (in contrast with the common coding-theoretic costume of using row vectors).
This representation lies at the heart of the most prominent constructions of families of optimal rank-metric codes, as we will see later in
Section

2.1
.

Throughout, we identify every element
p(\sigma)\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
with its image in
\End_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{L})
under (
2
): we denote its evaluation in
\alpha\in\mathbb{L}
by
p(\sigma)(\alpha)
. In addition, the
\sigma
-degree
(or simply
degree
) of a nonzero element
p(\sigma)=a_{0}\id+a_{1}\sigma+\ldots+a_{n-1}\sigma^{n-1}\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
is defined in the usual way as

\deg p(\sigma)=\max\{i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\,:\,a_{i}\neq 0\}.

Artin’s theorem of linear independence of characters ensures then that, for any nonzero
\sigma
-polynomial
p(\sigma)=a_{0}\id+a_{1}\sigma+\ldots+a_{n-1}\sigma^{n-1}\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
, the following holds:

(4)

\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\ker p(\sigma))\leq\deg p(\sigma).

This last statement can be found for instance in
[
12
, Theorem 5]
, although it can also be derived from earlier works; see e.g.
[
6
,
14
]
.

2.1
Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes

Rank-metric codes were first introduced by Delsarte in
[
6
]
and then reintroduced by Gabidulin in
[
9
]
. They have gained in popularity thanks to their application to network coding
[
18
]
and for further applications in distributed storage
[
4
]
and cryptography
[
10
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field and
k,m,n,d
positive integers. An
[n\times m,k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}

rank-metric code
is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}^{n\times m}
, where
d=\min\{\rk(A):A\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\}\}
is called the
minimum rank distance
of
\mathcal{C}
.

The parameters
k,m,n,d
of a rank-metric code depend from each other through the well-known Singleton-like bound, originally proved by Delsarte in
[
6
]
and reading:

(5)

k\leq\min\{m(n-d+1),n(m-d+1)\}.

Codes meeting the bound of (
5
) with equality are called
maximum rank distance codes
, or simply
MRD codes
. When
\mathbb{F}
admits a degree
\max\{n,m\}
cyclic extension field
\mathbb{L}
, there is a well-known construction of MRD codes for every choice of
d,n,m
with
1\leq d\leq\min\{m,n\}
, which is due to Delsarte
[
6
]
for finite fields and to Guralnick
[
14
]
for general fields. Given
\sigma
a generator of
\Gal(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{F})
, this construction goes as follows.

If
n=m
, then the
\mathbb{L}
-subspace

\mathbb{L}[\sigma]_{n-d}=\left\{\sum_{i=0}^{n-d}a_{i}\sigma^{i}\,:\,a_{i}\in\mathbb{L}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{L}[\sigma].

has
\mathbb{F}
-dimension
n(n-d+1)
and can be seen as an
[n\times n,n(n-d+1),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
code, due to the isomorphism in (
3
). Furthermore, by (
4
) it is clear that the minimum rank distance of
\mathcal{C}
is
d
, implying that
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]_{n-d}
is an MRD code.

If
m<n
, then by the last case we know that
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]_{m-d}
is an
[n\times n,n(m-d+1),d+n-m]_{\mathbb{F}}
MRD code. Projecting
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]_{m-d}
to
\mathbb{F}^{n\times m}
by erasing any set of
n-m
columns, we then obtain an
[n\times m,n(m-d+1),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MRD code.

If
m>n
, one reduces to the previous case by interchanging the values
n
and
m
and transposing the matrices.

In 2009, motivated by the application of subspace codes to network coding, Etzion and Silberstein initiated the study of Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes
[
8
]
. These are rank-metric codes whose elements have entries which are identically zero outside of a given Ferrers diagram, and arise from subspace codes in a fixed Schubert cell.

Definition 2.2
.

A (top-right justified)
Ferrers diagram of order
n
is
a subset
\mathcal{D}
of
[n]^{2}

satisfying the following properties:

•

If
(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}
and
j^{\prime}\in\{j,\ldots,n\}
, then
(i,j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{D}
.

•

If
(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}
and
i^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,i\}
, then
(i^{\prime},j)\in\mathcal{D}
.

Every Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
can be graphically represented as a top-right justified grid of dots. We will often use this representation in order to help the reader graphically catch the features of the Ferrers diagrams that we will deal with in this paper. For compactness, however, we will also identify a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
with the vector
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
, where
c_{i}\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
is the number of dots in the column
i
of the grid: in view of
Definition

2.2
it then holds that
0\leq c_{1}\leq\ldots\leq c_{n}\leq n
.

Remark 2.3
.

We have already seen that, if a Ferrers diagram is given as a subset of
[n]^{2}
, then its vector representation
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is obtained as

c_{j}=|\{i\in[n]\,:\,(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}\}|.

Conversely, if
\mathcal{D}
is given by a vector
(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
, then its representation as a subset of
[n]^{2}
is derived as

\mathcal{D}=\{(i,j)\in[n]^{2}\,:\,i\leq c_{j}\}.

These maps are inverses to each other and we will use the two representations of a Ferrers diagram interchangeably.

There is a natural notion of adjunction for Ferrers diagrams, which corresponds to the conjugate partition of an integer.

Let

If

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
. A
[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}

Ferrers diagram rank-metric code
is a
k
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
\mathcal{C}
of
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
endowed with the rank distance. The parameter
d
is the
minimum rank distance
of
\mathcal{C}
and is defined as

d=\min\{\rk(A)\,:\,A\in\mathcal{C},A\neq 0\}.

In their seminal paper
[
8
]
, Etzion and Silberstein showed the following relation between the parameters of a
[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
code
\mathcal{C}
, relying on the classical argument that
\mathcal{C}
trivially intersects any subspace of
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
whose elements have rank at most
d-1
.

Proposition 2.9

(
[
8
, Theorem 1]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
, and let
\mathcal{C}
be a
[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}
code. Then

k\leq{\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n-j}\max\{0,c_{i}-d+1+j\}\ :\ j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\right\}}.

The quantity on the right-hand side of the bound in Proposition
2.9
, which we denote

(6)

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)={\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n-j}\max\{0,c_{i}-d+1+j\}\ :\ j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\right\}},

represents, informally speaking, the minimum, among all the
j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
of the number of dots remaining in the Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
after removing the first
d-j-1
rows and the last
j
columns from
\mathcal{D}
. From this interpretation, it is immediately clear that
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\top},d)
.

To lighten the notation, in the remaining part of the paper, we will write
\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D},d)
meaning

\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-j}\max\{0,c_{i}-d+1+j\}

yielding in turn that
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\min\{\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D},d)\ :\ j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\}
.

Conjecture 2.11

(
[
8

)
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
, and let

. If
\mathbb{F}

[\mathcal{D},k,d]_{\mathbb{F}}

A code meeting the bound from Proposition

maximum Ferrers diagram (MFD) code
. Thus, the Etzion-Silberstein conjecture (Conjecture

. Since

Though Conjecture
2.11
is still widely open, there is a number of cases in which it has been confirmed; see
[
15

The first one allows to completely settle the case where

[
8
, Theorem 2]
,
[

, Corollary 19]
), and
[
2
, Theorem III.1]
.
For

, the structure of MRD codes has been exploited in various ways for the construction of MFD codes, often with additional restrictions on the minimum rank
d
depending on the Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
. This method can already be found in Etzion and Silberstein’s seminal paper
[
8
]
, and it has been improved and refined ever since (see e.g.
[
2
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
16
]
). A summary of the MRD-subcodes constructions can be found in
[
16
, Theorem 2.8]
.

The second general method uses the theory of MDS codes in the Hamming metric, but only allows to prove Conjecture
2.11
over “large enough” finite fields and for Ferrers diagrams with the property of being
MDS-constructible
; cf.
Definition

2.12
. In the following, for
i\in[n]
, let

\Delta_{i}^{n}=\{(j,j+i-1):j\in[n-i+1]\}

be the

-th diagonal of an

Definition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq[n]^{2}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
, and let

be a positive integer. The pair
(\mathcal{D},d)
is
MDS-constructible
if

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\}.

We remark that, since
|\Delta_{i}^{n}|=n-i+1
, for an MDS-constructible pair
(\mathcal{D},d)
one has

(7)

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\}.

The notion of MDS-constructible pairs was introduced in
[
2
]
, but it was already shown in
[
7
, Theorem 7]
that
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD codes exists (and can be explicitly constructed) whenever
|\mathbb{F}|\geq\max\{|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-1\,:\,i\in[n]\}
. The rough idea is to embed MDS codes of length
|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|
and Hamming distance
d
into
\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}
and then take the direct sum of these spaces. For a detailed description of this construction we refer the reader to
[
7
, Construction 1]
.
We remark that a similar diagonal construction for rank metric codes was already given in
[
17
, Theorem 3]
.
Recently, existence results for MFD codes over some MDS-constructible pairs were derived in
[
13
]
, with partial improvements on the field size.

Remark 2.13
.

It is readily seen that the upper triangular Ferrers diagrams fall into this category. In other words, for positive integers
n
and
1\leq d\leq n
, if
\mathcal{T}_{n}=\{(i,j)\,:\,i,j\in[n],i\leq j\}
denotes the upper-triangular
n\times n
Ferrers diagram, then
the pair
(\mathcal{T}_{n},d)
is MDS-constructible.

In
Section

4
we prove
2.11
for the family of MDS-constructible Ferrers diagrams over any finite field. To get rid of the
restriction on the field size, we exploit
MRD-subcode constructions. The novelty of our approach is the use of an auxiliary extension field whose degree is a power of the characteristic.

2.2
Monotone and convex Ferrers diagrams

Two special families of Ferrers diagrams which we will focus on, are those of
p
-monotone and
p
-convex Ferrers diagrams, where
p
is a prime number. We define them both in this section.

Definition 2.14
.

A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
of order
n
is called
monotone
if, whenever
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
is such that
0<c_{i}<n
, one has
c_{i+1}>c_{i}
.

Example 2.15
.

The following are examples of monotone Ferrers diagrams of order
n=5
.

\mathcal{D}=(0,0,1,3,4),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(1,2,4,5,5),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(2,3,5,5,5),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(0,1,4,5,5)

Remark 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n
, and define

\ell=\min\{i\,:\,c_{i}\neq 0\}\ \ \textup{ and }\ \ r=\max\{i\,:\,c_{i}\neq n\}.

Then, for every
i,j\in\{\ell,\ell+1,\ldots,r+1\}
with
i<j
, one has
c_{j}-c_{i}\geq j-i
.

Remark 2.17
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n
. If
i,j<n
are such that
(i,j)\in\mathcal{D}
, then monotonicity yields
(i+1,j+1)\in\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 2.18
.

A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
of order
n
is called
convex
if, for all
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, one has that
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\leq 1
.

The reader can easily verify that the empty diagram
\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
and the full diagram
\mathcal{D}=[n]^{2}
are both monotone and convex.

Example 2.19
.

The following are examples of convex Ferrers diagrams of order
n=5
.

\mathcal{D}=(0,1,2,2,3),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(2,3,3,4,5),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(3,3,4,5,5),\;\;\mathcal{D}=(2,3,3,3,4)

We remark that convex Ferrers diagrams are adjoints of monotone Ferrers diagrams and the other way around, as one can see from
Example

2.15
and
Example

2.19
.

Definition 2.20
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
. Let
h
be the largest nonnegative integer with the following properties:

(1)

The power
p^{h}
divides
n
.

(2)

For every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the power
p^{h}
divides
c_{i}
.

(3)

If
r,s\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
are such that there exists a nonnegative integer
Q
with the property that
r,s\in\{Qp^{h},Qp^{h}+1,\ldots,(Q+1)p^{h}-1\}
, then
c_{n-r}=c_{n-s}
.

The number
h
is called the
p
-height
of
\mathcal{D}
and the
p
-contraction
of
\mathcal{D}
is the Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}^{(p)}=(c_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,c_{n/p^{h}}^{\prime})
of order

that is defined by

c_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{c_{p^{h}i}}{p^{h}},\quad i\in\{1,\ldots,n/p^{h}\}.

We remark that, in the context of
Definition

2.20
, the value

satisfies
(1)
,
(2)
, and
(3)
so the

-height of

is well defined.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{D}
is a Ferrers diagram of order
n
of

-height

, then the
p
-contraction of
\mathcal{D}
is equal to
\mathcal{D}
.
Roughly speaking, as the following example suggests, if

is the
p
-height of a Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
, then
[n]^{2}
can be partitioned into blocks of size
p^{h}\times p^{h}
where the intersection of
\mathcal{D}
with any block is either full or empty.

Example 2.21
.

The Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(4,4,4,4,8,8,8,8)
of order
8
has
2
-height equal to
2
. Its
2
-contraction is the Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}^{(2)}=(1,2)
of order
2
.

Definition 2.22
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
. The diagram
\mathcal{D}
is called
p
-monotone
if its

-contraction is monotone.
Similarly,
\mathcal{D}
is
p
-convex

We remark that, with this new definition, monotone Ferrers diagrams are a special instance of the larger family of

Example 2.23
.

The following are examples of
2
-monotone Ferrers diagrams of order

.

\mathcal{D}=(0,0,2,2),\qquad\mathcal{D}=(2,2,4,4,6,6),\qquad\mathcal{D}=(0,0,4,4,8,8,8,8,8)

The following straightforward result ensures that
p
-convex Ferrers diagrams and

-monotone Ferrers diagrams are adjoint to each other.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
. Then the following hold:

(1)

The diagrams
\mathcal{D}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\top}
have the same
p
-height.

(2)

One has
(\mathcal{D}^{\top})^{(p)}=(\mathcal{D}^{(p)})^{\top}
.

(3)

The diagram
\mathcal{D}
is
p
-monotone if and only if
\mathcal{D}^{\top}
is
p
-convex.

In the next section, we will describe a very useful representation of Ferrers diagram matrix spaces
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
when
\mathcal{D}
is
p
-monotone. Furthermore, by adjunction – that is, transposing with respect to the antidiagonal – we automatically obtain also a representation of
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
when
\mathcal{D}
is
p
-convex.

The following remark generalizes
Remark

2.16
to the case of
p
-monotone Ferrers diagrams.

Remark 2.25
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a
p
-monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n
with
p
-height equal to
h
. Set

\ell=\min\{i\,:\,c_{i}\neq 0\}
and
r=\max\{i\,:\,c_{i}\neq n\}.

It follows from
p
-monotonicity that

\ell=1+sp^{h}\ \ \textup{and}\ \ r=tp^{h},\ \ \textup{ for some }s,t\in\{0,\ldots,n/p^{h}\}.

The Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
being
p
-monotone, we derive that, if
a,b\in\{s,\ldots,t+1\}
, then

a>b\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ c_{ap^{h}}-c_{bp^{h}}\geq(a-b)p^{h}.

Remark 2.26
.

We note that, in the special case where
\mathcal{D}
is a
p
-monotone Ferrers diagram, for every choice of an integer
1\leq d\leq n
,
one has that
\nu_{0}(\mathcal{D},d)\geq\nu_{1}(\mathcal{D},d)\geq\ldots\geq\nu_{d-1}(\mathcal{D},d)

and thus the value of
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)
can be rewritten as

{\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\min\{\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D},d)\ :\ j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}\}=\nu_{d-1}(\mathcal{D},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}c_{i}.}

In other words,
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)
can be computed by exclusively deleting columns from the Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
. This is a direct consequence of
Remark

2.25
.

3
Flags and modular skew algebras

In this section we fix a prime
p
, a positive integer
n=p^{m}
, and a field
\mathbb{F}
of characteristic
p
with a cyclic Galois extension
\mathbb{L}
of degree
n
.
We fix a generator
\sigma
of
\Gal(\mathbb{L}/\mathbb{F})
, and define
\bar{\sigma}=\sigma-\id\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
. Since the characteristic of the field divides the order of
\sigma
, resembling the classical group algebra setting, the skew algebra
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
is called
modular
. Note that, in this case the endomorphism
\bar{\sigma}
is nilpotent of order
n
, since

\bar{\sigma}^{n}=(\sigma-\id)^{n}=(\sigma-\id)^{p^{m}}=\sigma^{p^{m}}-\id,

which maps every element of
\mathbb{L}
to
0
.
We define

(8)

\mathcal{F}_{i}=\ker(\bar{\sigma}^{i}),\quad\mbox{ for every }i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}.

In particular, we have that
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{0\}
,
\mathcal{F}_{1}=\mathbb{F}
, and
\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathbb{L}
.
We work under these assumptions until the end of the present section. The following result collects some immediate properties of the endomorphisms
\bar{\sigma}^{i}
and of the
\mathbb{F}
-subspaces
\mathcal{F}_{i}
of
\mathbb{L}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The following hold:

(1)

The set
\{\bar{\sigma}^{i}:i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}\}
is an
\mathbb{L}
-basis of
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
.

(2)

For each
i\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, one has
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}\mathcal{F}_{i}=i
.

(3)

For each
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, one has
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{i}
.

(4)

For every
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
, one has
\bar{\sigma}^{j}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\mathcal{F}_{\max\{0,i-j\}}
.

A direct consequence of Lemma
3.1
is that
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n})
is a
full flag
of the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\mathbb{L}
, that is,
\{0\}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{n-1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{n}=\mathbb{L}
is a chain of
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces and the
\mathbb{F}
-dimension of each quotient
\mathcal{F}_{i}/\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
is equal to
1
.

Definition 3.2
.

An
\mathbb{F}
-basis
\mathcal{B}=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n})
of
\mathbb{L}
is called
\mathcal{F}
-compatible
if, for every index
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, the
\mathbb{F}
-span
\langle\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{i}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}}
is equal to
\mathcal{F}_{i}
.

The following result on the multiplicative behaviour of the flag
\mathcal{F}
will be crucial in the proof of
Theorem

3.4
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
a,b,h\geq 0
be integers satisfying
p^{h}(a+b)\leq n
. Then one has

\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}a}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}b}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a+b-1)}.

Proof.

We work by induction on
a+b
. If
a=0
or
b=0
, then the claim is true since
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\{0\}
. We assume thus that
a,b\geq 1
. Let
x\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}a}
and
y\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}b}
be arbitrary and write

u=\bar{\sigma}^{p^{h}}(x)=\sigma^{p^{h}}(x)-x\ \ \textup{ and }\ \ v=\bar{\sigma}^{p^{h}}(y)=\sigma^{p^{h}}(y)-y.

Lemma

3.1

(
4
)

implies that
u\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a-1)}
and
v\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(b-1)}
.
We compute

\sigma^{p^{h}}(xy)=\sigma^{p^{h}}(x)\sigma^{p^{h}}(y)=(x+u)(y+v)=xy+xv+yu+uv,

from which we derive that

\bar{\sigma}^{p^{h}}(xy)=\sigma^{p^{h}}(xy)-xy=xv+yu+uv\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}a}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(b-1)}+\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}b}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a-1)}+\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a-1)}\cdot\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(b-1)}.

It follows from the induction hypothesis that
\bar{\sigma}^{p^{h}}(xy)\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a+b-2)}
and so
xy\in\mathcal{F}_{p^{h}(a+b-1)}
.
∎

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n=p^{m}
. Throughout we write
\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]
for the
\mathbb{F}
-subspace

\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}\bar{\sigma}^{i-1}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\bar{\sigma}^{i-1}\,:\,\lambda_{i}\in\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}\right\}

of
\mathbb{L}[\sigma]
defined by
\mathcal{D}
with respect to the flag
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a
p
-monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n=p^{m}
. Let
\mathbb{L}
be a cyclic Galois extension of
\mathbb{F}
of order
n
, whose Galois group is generated by
\sigma
.
Write
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{0},\ldots,\mathcal{F}_{n})
, where
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is as in (
8
), and let
\mathcal{B}
be an
\mathcal{F}
-compatible
\mathbb{F}
-basis of
\mathbb{L}
. Then, the map
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
in (
3
) maps
\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]
isomorphically into
\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
.

Proof.

Since
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}
is injective, the
\mathbb{F}
-vector space
\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]
is mapped to a space of the same
\mathbb{F}
-dimension. Now,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}])=c_{1}+\ldots+c_{n}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}})
, so to prove that
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}])=\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
, it suffices to show that
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}])\subseteq\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
. Doing this is equivalent to proving that

(9)

f(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}\qquad\quad\mbox{ for every }f\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]\textup{ and }i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}.

We proceed thus to prove (
9
).
To this end, let
h
be the
p
-height of
\mathcal{D}
. This means that
p^{h}
divides every entry
c_{k}
of
\mathcal{D}
and, for every
k\in\{0,\ldots,n/p^{h}-1\}
, that
c_{1+kp^{h}}=c_{2+kp^{h}}=\ldots=c_{(k+1)p^{h}}
.
Since, for each index
k
, one has

\mathcal{F}_{1+kp^{h}}\subset\ldots\subset\mathcal{F}_{(k+1)p^{h}}\ \ \textup{ and }\ \ \mathcal{F}_{c_{1+kp^{h}}}=\ldots=\mathcal{F}_{c_{(k+1)p^{h}}},

checking (
9
) is equivalent to checking (
9
) for indices
i
of the form
i=ap^{h}
with
a\in\{1,\ldots n/p^{h}\}
.
Let thus
a\in\{1,\ldots n/p^{h}\}
and write
i=ap^{h}
. Let, moreover,
f\in\mathbb{L}[\sigma;\mathcal{D}]
:
leveraging on linearity, we assume without loss of generality that
f=\lambda_{j}\bar{\sigma}^{j-1}
, for some
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
and
\lambda_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{c_{j}}
. Applying the same argument as before, we assume without loss of generality that
j=bp^{h}
with
b\in\{1,\ldots,n/p^{h}\}
. Let
s
and
t
be as in
Remark

2.25
.

If
b<s
, then
\mathcal{F}_{j}=\{0\}
and we derive trivially
f(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\{0\}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}
. In a dual way, if
a>t
, then
\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}=\mathbb{L}
and thus we have
f(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}
. Moreover, if
a\leq b
, then Lemma
3.1

(
4
)

yields that
\bar{\sigma}^{j-1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\{0\}
and, once again, we get
f(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}
.

We assume now that
s\leq b<a\leq t
and write
c_{i}=cp^{h}
and
c_{j}=dp^{h}
.
Remark

2.25
ensures that
c-d\geq a-b
and so we have
p^{h}(d+a-b+1)\leq p^{h}(c+1)\leq n
.
With the aid of
Lemma

3.1

(
4
)

and
Proposition

3.3
we compute

f(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\lambda_{j}\bar{\sigma}^{j-1}(\mathcal{F}_{i})=\lambda_{j}\mathcal{F}_{i-j+1}=\lambda_{j}(\mathcal{F}_{ap^{h}-bp^{h}+1})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{dp^{h}}\mathcal{F}_{(a-b+1)p^{h}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{(a-b+d)p^{h}}.

and thus
f(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{(a-b+d)p^{h}}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{cp^{h}}=\mathcal{F}_{c_{i}}
.
∎

In this section we give a constructive proof of Conjecture

4.2
Strictly monotone and initially convex Ferrers diagrams

Among monotone Ferrers diagrams, a distinguished subfamily is given by strictly monotone ones, whose adjoints are the initially convex Ferrers diagrams.

Definition 4.4
.

A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is called
strictly monotone
if, whenever
c_{i}>0
, one has
c_{i+1}>c_{i}
.
A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is called
initially convex
if, for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, one has
c_{1}\leq 1
and
c_{i+1}-c_{i}\leq 1
.

It turns out that a Ferrers diagram is initially convex if and only if it is
in canonical form
in the sense of
[
5
, Definition 3.6]
. Although the two notions coincide, they appear
in different contexts and for apparently unrelated purposes. We remark that, in contrast with the case of monotone and convex Ferrers diagrams, the empty diagram
\mathcal{D}=\emptyset
is strictly monotone and initially convex, while the full diagram
\mathcal{D}=[n]^{2}
is neither (unless
n=1
).

Definition 4.5
.

Let
p
be a prime number. A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is called
strictly
p
-monotone
if its
p
-contraction is strictly monotone.
A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
is called
initially
p
-convex
if its
p
-contraction is initially convex.

Remark 4.6
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathcal{D}
be
a strictly monotone Ferrers diagram. If
\mathcal{D}
is nonempty, then the
p
-height of
\mathcal{D}
is equal to
0
and so
\mathcal{D}^{(p)}=\mathcal{D}
. In particular, strictly monotone Ferrers diagrams are strictly
p
-monotone for every choice of a prime
p
.

The following result is straightforward.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
p
be a prime number.
A Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}
is strictly
p
-monotone if and only if
\mathcal{D}^{\top}
is initially
p
-convex.

The following results ensures, up to iteration, that the property of being strictly
p
-monotone is preserved via top-right embeddings of Ferrers diagrams into larger square grids.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a strictly
p
-monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n
of
p
-height
h
. Then

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\underbrace{\,0\ldots,0\,}_{p^{h}\textup{ times}},c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})

is a strictly
p
-monotone Ferrers diagram of order
n+p^{h}
and
p
-height
h
.

Observe that Lemma
4.8
is not valid if we only assume the Ferrers diagram to be monotone.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
p
be a prime number and let
d,n
be positive integers with
1\leq d\leq n
. Then the following hold:

(1)

Conjecture
2.11
holds true for strictly
p
-monotone Ferrers diagrams of order
n
over any finite field of characteristic
p
.

(2)

Conjecture
2.11
holds true for initially
p
-convex Ferrers diagrams of order
n
over any finite field of characteristic
p
.

Proof.

We only prove the result for strictly
p
-monotone Ferrers diagrams: the one for initially
p
-convex Ferrers diagrams follows then from
Lemma

4.7
. Let
\mathcal{D}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})
be a strictly

-monotone Ferrers diagram of

-height

be a finite field of characteristic

Since

precisely
(p^{m}-n)/p^{h}

4.3
Extension to MDS-constructible pairs

We conclude the paper by extending Theorem

We start by showing that strictly monotone Ferrers diagrams and their adjoints are MDS-constructible with respect to any sensible distance.

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a strictly monotone or initially convex Ferrers diagram of order
n
. Then, for every integer
2\leq d\leq n
, the pair
(\mathcal{D},d)
is MDS-constructible.

Proof.

We start by assuming that
\mathcal{D}
is strictly monotone so, thanks to Remark
2.26
, we have that
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=|\mathcal{D}\cap([n]\times[n-d+1])|
. Moreover, for every
i\in[n]
, strict monotonicity implies
c_{i}\leq i
and thus
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{n}
.
With a slight abuse of notation identifying
\mathcal{T}_{n-d+1}
with its image in
[n]^{2}
, this means that

(10)

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n-d+1}|.

Set
R=[n]\times([n]\setminus[n-d+1])
and, for each
i\in[n-d+1]
, write
r_{i}=|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n-d+1}|
. Assuming that
r_{i}>0
,
Remark

2.17
yields that

(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap R)\subseteq\mathcal{D}
and thus

(11)

r_{i}=|\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n-d+1})|=|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-|\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap R)|=|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1.

Combining (
10
) and (
11
) we finally obtain

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n-d+1}|=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}r_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\},

which shows that the pair
(\mathcal{D},d)
is MDS-constructible. This also concludes the proof as initially convex Ferrers diagrams are adjoints of strictly monotone ones and MDS-constructibility is preserved under adjunction.
∎

Definition 4.17

i\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
we have

\displaystyle\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)\leq\nu_{i}(\mathcal{D},d)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{D}|-|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,i}|=|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,i}|

(12)

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|+|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|-|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,i}|=\nu_{i}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)+|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|.

On the other hand, by hypothesis
(\mathcal{D},d)
is
j
-Singleton, and thus we have

(13)

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D},d)=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=|\mathcal{D}|-|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}|=\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)+|\mathcal{D}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|.

Combining (
4.3
) with (
13
), we get
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)=\nu_{j}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)
.
∎

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
and let
d\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
,
j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
. Assume that
(\mathcal{D},d)
is MDS-constructible and
j
-Singleton. Then the following hold:

(1)

One has
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n,d,j}
.

(2)

One has
\{i\in[n-d+1]\,:\,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|\geq d\}=\{i\in[n-d+1]\,:\,\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}\neq\emptyset\}
.

(3)

If
i\in[n-d+1]
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}\neq\emptyset
, then

\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\supseteq\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}
.

Proof.

Define the subsets
X
and
Y
of
[n-d+1]
as

X=\{i\in[n-d+1]\,:\,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|\geq d\}\ \ \textup{ and }\ \ Y=\{i\in[n-d+1]\,:\,\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}\neq\emptyset\}.

We claim that
X\subseteq Y
. To see this, let
i\in X
and note that
|\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=n-d-i+2
. Since
|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|\geq d
and both
\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}
and
\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}
are contained in the set
\Delta_{i}^{n}
of cardinality
n-i+1
, they must intersect nontrivially. Since
i
was chosen arbitrarily, the claim is proven.

We proceed by first proving
(1)
and then
(2)
and
(3)
together.

(1)
Since
(\mathcal{D},d)
is
j
-Singleton, we have that

(14)

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|.

On the other hand,
(\mathcal{D},d)
is MDS-constructible and thus we have

\displaystyle\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in X}(|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1)=\sum_{i\in X}(|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-|\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}|)

(15)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in X}(|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-|\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j})|)=\sum_{i\in X}(|\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j})|)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in Y}(|\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j})|)=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n,d,j}|.

Combining (
14
) and (
4.3
), we derive that
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{n,d,j}
.

(2)
-
(3)
Thanks to the proof of
(1)
, we know that the inequalities in (
4.3
) are in fact all equalities and so, for each
i\in X
we have
\mathcal{D}\cap(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j})=\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}
. Furthermore, by the definition of
Y
and using the fact that the second to last inequality in (
4.3
) is an equality, we deduce that
Y=X
and the proof is complete.
∎

Example 4.21
.

Let
\mathcal{D}=(0,2,2,3,3,5,6,8)
be a Ferrers diagram of order
8
and let
d=4
. It is easy to verify that
(\mathcal{D},4)
is MDS-constructible and
j
-Singleton for
j=1
. We can see that
\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{8,4,1}=\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{T}_{8,4,1}
. Moreover, for every index
2\leq i\leq 5
we have that
\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{8}\cap\mathcal{T}_{8,4,1}\neq 0
and indeed, for each such index, it holds that
\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{8}\supseteq\Delta_{i}^{8}\cap\mathcal{L}_{8,4,1}
.

The following is the main result of this section, which ensures in particular that Conjecture
2.11
holds true for every MDS-constructible pair
(\mathcal{D},d)
.

Theorem 4.22
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
and let
2\leq d\leq n
be an integer. If
(\mathcal{D},d)
is MDS-constructible, then there exists a
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code over any finite field
\mathbb{F}
.

Before giving a proof of Theorem
4.22
, we recall a useful criterion to construct MFD codes on a given Ferrers diagram from MFD codes on related Ferrers diagrams. This strategy was first adopted in the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 7]
and the explicit statement can be found in
[
2
, Remarks II.12 & II.14]
.

Proposition 4.23
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
and let
2\leq d\leq n
be an integer.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
such that
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a
[\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code, then it is also a
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{D}
be a Ferrers diagram of order
n
such that
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)-|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\setminus\mathcal{D}|
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a
[\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code, then
\mathcal{C}\cap\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}}
is a
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code.

We give here the proof of
Theorem

4.22
and we illustrate its steps explicitly in
Example

4.24
.

Proof of
Theorem

4.22
.

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a finite field, let
(\mathcal{D},d)
be an MDS-constructible pair and let
j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
be such that
(\mathcal{D},d)
is
j
-Singleton.
Define

Y=\{i\in[n-d+1]\,:\,\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}\neq\emptyset\}

and note that, as a consequence of (
7
) and
Lemma

4.20
(2)
, one has

\displaystyle\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n-d+1}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in Y}\max\{0,|\mathcal{D}\cap\Delta_{i}^{n}|-d+1\}.

If
Y=\emptyset
, then
\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)=0
and there is nothing to prove. Hence, assume
Y\neq\emptyset
. By the definition of
Y
, there exists
\ell\in[n-d+1]
such that
Y=\{i\,:\,\ell\leq i\leq n-d+1\}
. Fix such
\ell
and define the following new Ferrers diagram

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}=(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j})\cup\Big(\bigcup_{i=\ell}^{n}(\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j})\Big).

By
Lemma

4.20
(3)
, for every
\ell\leq i\leq n-d+1
we have that
\Delta_{i}^{n}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
while the same is satisfied for
i\geq n-d+2
thanks to the fact that
\mathcal{D}
is a Ferrers diagram. It follows in particular that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Moreover, it also holds that

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}=\bigcup_{i=\ell}^{n}\Delta_{i}^{n}.

The Ferrers diagram
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is a copy of
\mathcal{T}_{n-\ell+1}
(considered as embedded into
[n]^{2}
) and hence it is strictly monotone and
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime},d)
is
j^{\prime}
-Singleton for every
j^{\prime}
. In particular, it is
j
-Singleton. Furthermore,
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
have the same intersection with
\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}
.
Thus, Lemma
4.19
yields that
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)
is also
j
-Singleton.
Since
\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}
is strictly monotone, by Theorem
4.10
there exists a
[\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}
, which we fix. Since
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)
and
(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime},d)
are both
j
-Singleton and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathcal{L}_{n,d,j}
, we compute

\displaystyle\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime},d)

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=|(\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j})|+|(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|

\displaystyle=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)+|(\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime})\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)+|\mathcal{D}^{\prime\prime}\setminus\mathcal{D}^{\prime}|.

Proposition

4.23
(2)
yields that
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\mathcal{C}^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathbb{F}^{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}
is a
[\mathcal{D}^{\prime},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code. Finally, we have

\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D}^{\prime},d)=|\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=|\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{S}_{n,d,j}|=\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d)

and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. By Proposition
4.23
(1)
, the code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is thus also a
[\mathcal{D},\nu_{\min}(\mathcal{D},d),d]_{\mathbb{F}}
MFD code.
∎

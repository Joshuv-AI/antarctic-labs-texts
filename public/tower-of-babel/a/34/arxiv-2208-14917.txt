Abstract
. We prove a uniform version of Varadhan decomposition for shift-invariant closed uniform forms associated to large scale interacting systems on general crystal lattices. In particular, this result includes the case of translation invariant processes on Euclidean lattices ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with finite range. Our result generalizes the result of [ BKS20 ] which was valid for systems on transferable graphs. In subsequent research [ BS22L2 ] , we will use the result of this article to prove Varadhan’s decomposition of closed L 2 L^{2} -forms for large scale interacting systems on general crystal lattices.

1.
Introduction

Varadhan’s Decomposition of shift-invariant closed
L^{2}
-forms

has played an important role in the strategy
of proof of the hydrodynamic limit for

non-gradient
models
[
KL99
]
*§7.
See the introduction of
[
BKS20
]
for a short overview of hydrodynamic limits
and Varadhan’s strategy.
In this article, we prove a
uniform version
of such decomposition
for large scale interacting systems on a general
crystal lattice
.
In particular, our result is valid for various translation invariant
processes on Euclidean lattices
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with finite range.
This generalizes our result in
[
BKS20
]
for systems on locales which are transferable
or weakly transferable.
The simple case of the exclusion process was previously announced in
[
KS16
]
.
However, whilst the rough outline of proof remains similar, many new ideas, such as the
origin of the conserved quantities and the notion for an interaction to be irreducibly quantified,
as well as the strategy
to reduce the proof of the key theorem
Theorem

4.5
to the
case of essentially Euclidean crystal lattices
were required to grasp and generalize many of the arguments in
[
KS16
]
which a priori relied on properties
specific to the exclusion process.
Due to such conceptual advancements, the result of this article is valid for far general interactions, including models with multiple conserved quantities.
Although there has been some research concerning the hydrodynamic
limit on crystal lattices (
[
T12
]
, more recently
[
F20
,
G20
]
),
the known cases for interacting systems on non-Euclidean lattices are all for the gradient case
– there are no known examples in the non-gradient case.

A
crystal lattice
, or a
topological crystal

in the terminology of
[
Sun13
]
*§6.2,
is any connected locally finite multi-graph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
for the set of vertices
X

and set of edges
E
, with a free action of a finitely generated abelian group
G

of rank
>0
such that the quotient graph
\mathcal{X}/G=(X/G,E/G)
is a strictly symmetric finite multi-graph.
See §
2.1
for terminology concerning graphs.
The dimension
\dim\mathcal{X}
is defined to be the rank of
G
.
The
Euclidean lattices
,
triangular lattice
,
hexagonal lattice
,
and the
diamond lattice
are all examples of crystal lattices.

We consider a non-empty set
S
which we call the
set of states
, and
S^{X}\coloneqq\prod_{x\in X}S

the
configurations
on
X
. We fix a map
\phi\colon S\times S\rightarrow S\times S

called an
interaction
satisfying a certain symmetry condition which specifies the
interaction of states on adjacent vertices.
Models such at the
generalized exclusion process
or the

multi-species exclusion process
may be described using such interaction
(see
[
BKS20
]
*Example 2.18 for other examples).
One important invariant of the interaction is the
conserved quantity
,
which is a normalized map
\xi\colon S\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying

\xi(s^{\prime}_{1})+\xi(s^{\prime}_{2})=\xi(s_{1})+\xi(s_{2}),

where
(s^{\prime}_{1},s^{\prime}_{2})=\phi(s_{1},s_{2})
for any
(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S
.
We denote by
{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
the
\mathbb{R}
-linear space of conserved quantities.
Then
c_{\phi}\coloneqq\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
gives the number of independent
conserved quantities of our large scale interacting system.

Via the interaction, for any edge
e\in E
, a configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
transitions
to a configuration
\eta^{e}=(\eta^{e}_{x})\in S^{X}
whose component
differs only on the origin and target of the edge
e
.
Such transitions give a structure of a multi-graph on
S^{X}
.
We denote by
C(S^{X})
and
C^{1}(S^{X})
the space of
\mathbb{R}
-valued functions and forms on
S^{X}
,
where a form is a system of functions
\omega=(\omega_{e})_{e\in E}

satisfying some minor conditions, where
\omega_{e}
for each
e\in E
is a function on
S^{X}
.

We may define the differential
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\colon C(S^{X})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X})

by
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f\coloneqq(\nabla_{\!e}f)_{e\in E}
, where

\nabla_{\!e}f(\eta)\coloneqq f(\eta^{e})-f(\eta),\qquad\forall\eta\in S^{X}.

Functions and forms are
local
if it depends only on states at a finite number of
vertices of
X
. In our previous work
[
BKS20
]
we extended the notion of
local functions to that of
uniform functions
, which allow for infinite sums of
normalized local functions. Let
C_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
and
C^{1}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
be the space
of uniform functions and forms. If we denote by
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})

the space of closed uniform form, consisting of uniform forms whose integration
over a path depends only on the origin and the target of the path,
then the differential induces the differential

\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\colon C_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})\rightarrow Z^{1}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X}).

The group
G
acts naturally on
C_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
and
Z^{1}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
, and
the action is compatible with the differential
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}
.
We let

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}

\displaystyle\coloneqq Z^{1}_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})^{G},

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\partial_{\mathcal{X}}(C_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})^{G})

the space of shift-invariant closed uniform forms and
shift-invariant uniform exact forms.

We say that an interaction is
irreducibly quantified

if the following condition holds for any finite
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
:
If two configurations
\eta=(\eta_{x}),\eta^{\prime}=(\eta^{\prime}_{x})\in S^{X}

satisfy
\sum_{x\in X}\xi(\eta_{x})=\sum_{x\in X}\xi(\eta^{\prime}_{x})
for any
conserved quantity
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, then there exists a path from
\eta
to
\eta^{\prime}

in
S^{X}
. We say that an interaction is
simple
, if
c_{\phi}=1
,
and for any non-zero
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
, the monoid generated by

\xi(S)\subset\mathbb{R}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
.

Our main theorem is as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(=
Theorem

4.3
)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
be a crystal lattice of dimension
d=\dim\mathcal{X}
,
and consider an interaction which is irreducibly quantified.
We assume in addition that the interaction is simple if
d=1
.
Then we have a decomposition

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{E}\oplus\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V},

where
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{C}
is a certain subspace given in
Lemma

4.2

of dimension
c_{\phi}d
, where
c_{\phi}=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
.

Theorem

4.3
is a generalization of
[
BKS20
]
*Theorem 5,17 to the
case of an arbitrary crystal lattice. In a subsequent article
[
BS22L2
]
, will use this result
to prove the decomposition of shift-invariant closed
L^{2}
-forms
on models over a general crystal lattice, generalizing
the result of
[
BS21L2
]
for the case of the Euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}^{d})
.
Such result will play a crucial role in the proof of the hydrodynamic limit
for large scale interacting systems on crystal lattices.
In particular, we
expect this result
would allow for the proof of the hydrodynamic limit for
translation invariant processes on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with finite range, extending
results which were up until now
known only for nearest neighbor interactions.

Crystal Lattices

We will then introduce the notion of uniform functions and forms

2.1.
Multigraphs and Crystal Lattices

Our main reference is

Since crystal lattices are in general multi-graphs instead of graphs,
we extend our previous works

A

*§3.1 corresponds to a

We will always assume that a multi-graph is symmetric.

Definition 2.1
.

We define a
symmetric directed multi-graph

\mathcal{X}=(X,E_{X},o,t,\iota)

We say that
\mathcal{X}
is

\bar{e}\neq e
for any

.

We will often denote
E_{X}
by
E
for simplicity.
Suppose
\mathcal{X}=(X,E,o,t,\iota)
is a multi-graph. For any edge

e\in E
, we call
o(e)
and
t(e)
the origin and target of
e
.
In literature, the map
(o,t)\colon E\rightarrow X\times X
is referred to as an
incidence map
.
We will often denote
(X,E,o,t,\iota)
simply as
(X,E)
.
We say that a multi-graph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)

is locally finite
if for any
x\in X
,
the set
E_{x}\coloneqq\{e\in E\mid o(e)=x\}
is finite for any
x\in X
,

is finite
if its set of vertices
X
and the set of edges
E
are both finite,
and
is infinite
if it is not finite.
If a multi-graph
(X,E)
is locally finite, then it is finite if and only if the set of vertices
X
is finite.

We define a
path
on a multi-graph

\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
to be a finite sequence
\vec{p}\coloneqq(e^{1},\ldots,e^{N})

of edges satisfying
t(e^{i})=o(e^{i+1})
for any
0<i<N
.
We say that
\vec{p}
is a path from
o(\vec{p})\coloneqq o(e^{1})
to
t(\vec{p})\coloneqq t(e^{N})
of
length
{\mathrm{len}}(\vec{p})\coloneqq N
.
For any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, we let
P(x,x^{\prime})
the set of paths from
x
to
x^{\prime}
.
We define the
distance
from
x
to
x^{\prime}
by

d_{\mathcal{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\coloneqq\inf_{\vec{p}\in P(x,x^{\prime})}{\mathrm{len}}(\vec{p})

if
P(x,x^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
and
d_{\mathcal{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\coloneqq\infty
otherwise.
For any
Y\subset X
, we say that
Y
is
connected
, if for any
y,y^{\prime}\in Y
,
there exists a path from
y
to
y^{\prime}
consisting of edges whose origin and target are all elements of
Y
.
If
X
is connected, then we say that the multi-graph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
is connected.

Any
e\in E
such that
o(e)=t(e)
is referred to as a
loop
.
A multi-graph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
is a
graph
, if the incidence
map
E\rightarrow X\times X
is injective.
For the case of graphs, we will identify
E
with its image in
X\times X
so that
E\subset X\times X
,
and the maps
o
and
t
are simply the first and second projections.
Using this convention,
the inversion of a graph
\mathcal{X}
coincides with the bijection given by exchanging the components of
E\subset X\times X
, and any loop
e\in E
satisfies
\bar{e}=e
.
A
simple graph
is a graph without any loops.
Graphs are referred to as a combinatorial graph in
[
Sun13
]
.
We may associate to a multigraph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
a graph
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=(X,E^{\prime})
by taking
E^{\prime}\subset X\times X

to be the image of the incidence map, which we refer to a
graph associated to
\mathcal{X}
.
The
simple graph
\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}
associated to
\mathcal{X}

is the simple graph obtained by removing the loops from
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
.

Definition 2.2
.

We let the

2.2.
Configuration Space and Uniform Functions

In order to consider the large scale interacting system on a locale, we next consider the configuration
space with transition structure.

Definition 2.8
.

We define an
interaction
to be a pair
(S,\phi)
consisting of a set
S

which we call the
set of states
and a
map

\phi\colon S\times S\rightarrow S\times S
satisfying

(2)

\bar{\phi}\circ\phi(s_{1},s_{2})=(s_{1},s_{2})

for any
(s_{1},s_{2})\in S\times S
such that
\phi(s_{1},s_{2})\neq(s_{1},s_{2})
.
Here,
\bar{\phi}\coloneqq\iota\circ\phi\circ\iota
for the bijection

\iota\colon S\times S\rightarrow S\times S
obtained by exchanging the components of

S\times S
.

In what follows, we let
(S,\phi)
be an interaction.
For any multi-graph
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
,
we define the
configuration space
on
\mathcal{X}
by

S^{X}\coloneqq\prod_{x\in X}S.

We call any
\eta\in S^{X}
a
configuration
.
For any configuration
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
and
e\in E
, we denote by
\eta^{e}

the element
\eta^{e}=(\eta^{e}_{x})\in S^{X}
such that

(\eta^{e}_{o(e)},\eta^{e}_{t(e)})\coloneqq\phi(\eta_{o(e)},\eta_{t(e)})
if
o(e)\neq t(e)
,
and
\eta^{e}_{x}=\eta_{x}
if
x\neq o(e),t(e)

or
x=o(e)=t(e)
.
Furthermore, we let

\Phi_{X}\coloneqq S^{X}\times E,

and we define the maps
o,t\colon\Phi_{X}\rightarrow S^{X}\times S^{X}
by

(3)

\displaystyle o((\eta,e))

\displaystyle=\eta,

\displaystyle t((\eta,e))

\displaystyle=\eta^{e}

for any
(\eta,e)\in\Phi_{X}
. We have the following.

Lemma 2.9
.

For any
e\in E
and
\eta\in S^{X}
, if
\eta^{e}\neq\eta
, then
(\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}}=\eta
.

Proof.

Let
e\in E
and
\eta=(\eta_{x})\in S^{X}
.
Note that
(\eta_{o(e)},\eta_{t(e)})\in S\times S
.
Suppose that
\eta^{e}\neq\eta
.
Then
\phi(\eta_{o(e)},\eta_{t(e)})\neq(\eta_{o(e)},\eta_{t(e)})
.
By definition of
(\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}}
, we see that

((\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}}_{o(e)},(\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}}_{t(e)})=\bar{\phi}\circ\phi(\eta_{o(e)},\eta_{t(e)}).

Our assertion now follows from (
2
).
∎

By
Lemma

2.9
, we have
(\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}}=\eta
if
\eta^{e}\neq\eta

for any
(\eta,e)\in\Phi_{X}
.
We define the inversion
\iota\colon\Phi_{X}\rightarrow\Phi_{X}
by

(4)

\iota((\eta,e))\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\eta,e)&\eta^{e}=\eta\\
(\eta^{e},\bar{e})&\eta^{e}\neq\eta\end{cases}

for any
(\eta,e)\in\Phi_{X}
.
Then by construction, if
\eta^{e}=\eta
, then
\iota\circ\iota(\eta,e)=\iota(\eta,e)=(\eta,e)

and if
\eta^{e}\neq\eta
, then
\iota\circ\iota(\eta,e)=\iota(\eta^{e},\bar{e})=((\eta^{e})^{\bar{e}},e)=(\eta,e)
.
This shows that
\iota\colon\Phi_{X}\rightarrow\Phi_{X}
is a bijection
such that if we denote
\bar{\varphi}=\iota(\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in\Phi_{X}
, then
we have
o(\bar{\varphi})=t(\varphi)
and
t(\bar{\varphi})=o(\varphi)
.
This shows that
(S^{X},\Phi_{X},o,t,\iota)
is a multi-graph.

Definition 2.10
.

The multi-graph

often denoted simply as

defines a multi-graph

associated

.

We say that a function

The most basic example of uniform functions is given by the conserved quantites.

Definition 2.14
.

We say that a map

for the pair
(S,\phi)
, if
\xi(*)=0
for the base state

.

.

gives an injective

\mathbb{R}
-linear homomorphism

.

Let
(\Lambda,E_{\Lambda})
be a finite locale.
In this case, we have
\xi_{\Lambda}=\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\xi_{x}\in C(S^{\Lambda})
.
Consider the canonical map

(6)

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda}\colon S^{\Lambda}\rightarrow{\mathrm{Hom}}_{\mathbb{R}}({\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S),\mathbb{R}),\qquad\eta\mapsto(\xi\mapsto\xi_{\Lambda}(\eta)).

Note that a choice of basis of

if and only if
\xi_{\Lambda}(\eta)=\xi_{\Lambda}(\eta^{\prime})

for any conserved quantity
\xi\in{\mathrm{Consv}}^{\phi}(S)
.

.

We say that an interaction
(S,\phi)
is

(\Lambda,E_{\Lambda})
and

from

to
\eta^{\prime}
.

Irreducibly quantified is an important assumption for our main results.

2.3.
Forms and Uniform Forms

In this subsection, we review the notion of forms and uniform forms.
We keep the notation of §
2.2
.
Let

\Phi_{X}^{*}\coloneqq\{\varphi\in\Phi_{X}\mid o(\varphi),t(\varphi)\in S^{X}_{*}\},

so that

for maps

again a multi-graph.
We let

C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})\coloneqq{\mathrm{Map}}^{\mathrm{alt}}(\Phi_{X}^{*},\mathbb{R})

be the
\mathbb{R}
-linear space of maps
\omega^{*}\colon\Phi_{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

that
\omega(\bar{\varphi})=-\omega(\varphi)
for any
\varphi\in\Phi_{X}^{*}
.
We call such
\omega
a
form
on
S^{X}_{*}
.
We have a differential

\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\colon C(S^{X}_{*})\rightarrow C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})

defined by
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f(\varphi)\coloneqq f(\eta^{e})-f(\eta)
for any
\varphi=(\eta,e)\in\Phi^{*}_{X}

For any

, a form
\omega\in C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})
defines a map
\omega_{e}\colon S^{X}_{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

by
\omega_{e}(\eta)\coloneqq\omega((\eta,e))

(7)

C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})\hookrightarrow\prod_{e\in E}C(S^{X}_{*}),\qquad\omega\mapsto(\omega_{e})_{e\in E}.

A system
(\omega_{e})_{e\in E}
is a form if and only if

(8)

\omega_{e}(\eta)=\begin{cases}-\omega_{\bar{e}}(\eta^{e})&\eta^{e}\neq\eta,\\
0&\eta^{e}=\eta.\end{cases}

If
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
, then
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f=(\nabla_{\!e}f)_{e\in E}
, where
\nabla_{\!e}f
is the function in
C(S^{X}_{*})

given by
\nabla_{\!e}f(\eta)\coloneqq f(\eta^{e})-f(\eta)
for any
\eta\in S^{X}_{*}
.

For any path
\vec{\gamma}=(\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{N})
in
S^{X}_{*}
, we define the integral of

\omega\in C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})

with respect to
\vec{\gamma}
by

\int_{\vec{\gamma}}\omega\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\omega(\varphi_{i}).

We say that a form
\omega\in C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})
is
closed
, if

\int_{\vec{\gamma}}\omega=0
for any closed path
\vec{\gamma}\in S^{X}_{*}
.
We denote by
Z^{1}(S^{X}_{*})
the space of closed forms.
As in
[
BKS20
]
*Lemma 2.15 and 2.16, we may prove the following.

.

A form
\omega\in C^{1}(S^{X}_{*})
is closed if and only if it is exact, in other words,
if there exists

such that
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f=\omega
.
In particular, the differential
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}
gives an isomorphism

C(S^{X}_{*})/{\mathrm{Ker}\,}\partial_{\mathcal{X}}\cong Z^{1}(S^{X}_{*})
.

For any

.

.

Let
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
such that
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f\in C^{1}_{R}(S^{X})
for some
R>0
.
Then for any finite connected
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset X
such that
d_{\mathcal{X}}(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})>R
,
there exists a function
h^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}_{f}\colon\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{1}|}\times\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{2}|}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

such that

(10)

\iota^{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}}f(\eta)-\iota^{\Lambda_{1}}f(\eta)-\iota^{\Lambda_{2}}f(\eta)=h^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}_{f}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{1}}(\eta),\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{2}}(\eta))

for all
\eta\in S^{X}_{*}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to prove the statement for
\eta=\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}}
.
Let
Y=\Lambda_{1}
and
W=B(\Lambda_{1},R)
.
Since
\Lambda_{2}\cap W=\emptyset
, we have
\eta|_{W}=\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}}
and

(f-\iota^{W}f)(\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}})=f(\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}})-f(\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}})=\iota^{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}}f(\eta)-\iota^{\Lambda_{1}}f(\eta).

This equality and
Lemma

2.18
show that the left hand side of (
10
)
depends only on
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{1}}(\eta)
and
\eta|_{\Lambda_{2}}
.
By replacing the roles of
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
, we see that
the left hand side of (
10
) depends only on

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{1}}(\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{1}|}
and
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{2}}(\eta)\in\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{2}|}
.
∎

By definition, for any finite connected
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}\subset X
and

\alpha\in\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{1}|}
and
\beta\in\mathcal{M}_{|\Lambda_{2}|}
,
we have

(11)

h^{\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}}_{f}(\alpha,\beta)=h^{\Lambda_{2},\Lambda_{1}}_{f}(\beta,\alpha).

3.
Criterion for Uniformity

In this section, we introduce the notion of an essentially Euclidean crystal lattice,
and to prove a criterion
Proposition

3.7
for ensuring that a function on
S^{X}_{*}

for such crystal lattice is uniform.
This criterion will be used in the next section
to prove
Theorem

4.8
concerning the surjectivity of the differential
from the space of uniform functions to closed uniform forms.
In §
3.3
, we will give examples of essentially Euclidean crystal
lattices.

3.1.
Pairings for Essentially Euclidean Crystal Lattices

In what follows, let
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
be a crystal lattice for the action of a finitely generated abelian group
G
.
In this subsection, we introduce the notion of

essentially Euclidean crystal lattices
,
and construct certain pairings associated to each function
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
whose differential is uniform.
We first introduce a Euclidean type coordinate on
\mathcal{X}
, which we call a
block coordinate
.

We let
F\subset G
be a free subgroup of finite index in
G
.
Note that in this case, the rank of
F
coincides with that of
G
.
We say that

\Lambda_{F}
is a
fundamental domain
of
\mathcal{X}
for the action of
F

\Lambda_{F}

is connected, and
\Lambda_{F}
gives a representative of the orbits of
X
with respect to the action of
F
,
so that for any
x\in X
, there exists a unique
\sigma\in F
such that
x\in\sigma(\Lambda_{F})
.
Note that
\Lambda_{F}
is finite since
\mathcal{X}/F
is finite.
We let
\mathcal{S}^{+}=\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{d}\}
be a free generator of
F
.
We call the pair
(\Lambda_{F},\mathcal{S}^{+})
a
coordinate
of
X
.
For any
\tau\in F
, we let
(n_{1}(\tau),\ldots,n_{d}(\tau))\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be such that

\tau=\sigma_{1}^{n_{1}(\tau)}\cdots\sigma_{d}^{n_{d}(\tau)}
.
We define the
block coordinate
of
x\in X
for
(\Lambda_{F},\mathcal{S}^{+})

to be
(n_{1}(\tau),\ldots,n_{d}(\tau))\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
where
\tau\in F
is such that
x\in\tau(\Lambda_{F})
.
We let
u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\colon X\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}

mapping any
x\in X
to its block coordinate.
For the set of edges

(12)

\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\{(\bar{x},\bar{x}^{\prime})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\mid\exists e\in E,u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(o(e))=\bar{x},u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(t(e))=\bar{x}^{\prime}\},

the pair
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}})
form a connected locally finite graph
such that the block coordinate gives a morphism of multi-graphs
u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

We define the
block distance
on
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
to be the function

d_{\mathcal{S}}\colon X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

given by

d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,x^{\prime})\coloneqq d_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}}(u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(x),u_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(x^{\prime}))

for any
x,x^{\prime}\in X
, where
d_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}}
denotes the graph distance on
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}
.
Note that by definition, we have

d_{\mathcal{X}}(x,x^{\prime})\leq{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda_{F})d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,x^{\prime}).

The block distance is not a distance in the usual sense since we may have
d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,x^{\prime})=0

even if
x\neq x^{\prime}
.

(ii)

We define the
block ball
in
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)

with center
x\in X
and radius
R\geq 0
by

\mathscr{B}(x,R)\coloneqq\{x^{\prime}\in X\mid d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,x^{\prime})\leq R\},

which is a finite connected subset of
X
.

(iii)

For any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{X}
, we define the
block diameter
of
\Lambda
by

{\mathrm{diam}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\inf_{x,x^{\prime}\in\Lambda}d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,x^{\prime}).

Definition 3.2
.

We say that a crystal lattice
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
is
essentially Euclidean
for the action of
G
,
if there exists a free subgroup
F\subset G
of finite index with
coordinate
(\Lambda_{F},\mathcal{S}^{+})
such that for the graph
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}_{\mathcal{S}})

(13)

d_{\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}}=d_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, where
d_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
denotes the graph distance on the Euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}^{d})
.
In this case,
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}
coincides with
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
except for possibly
simple loops in
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a crystal lattice which is essentially Euclidean for the action of
G
,
and if we fix a coordinate
(\Lambda_{F},\mathcal{S}^{+})

then by definition, the topology of
\mathbb{Z}_{\mathcal{S}}
corresponds to that of the
Euclidean lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}=(\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{E}^{d})

Any subset
\bar{\Lambda}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is connected
if and only if the inverse image

\Lambda\coloneqq u^{-1}_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(\bar{\Lambda})

is connected in
\mathcal{X}
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{X}

∎

For the rest of this section, we assume that

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

for some
R>0
.
For any finite connected

Then the functions

\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}=\Lambda_{1}\cap\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}=\emptyset
, there exists configurations

\eta,\eta^{\prime}\in S^{X}_{*}

\displaystyle\eta

\displaystyle=\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}},

\displaystyle\eta^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\prime}|_{\Lambda_{1}\cup\Lambda_{2}^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\eta|_{\Lambda_{1}}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\prime}|_{\Lambda_{1}},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!\Lambda_{2}}(\eta)

Proposition 3.6

Then the pairing

for any

Proof.

Take

sufficiently large so that
\alpha,\beta,\gamma\in\mathcal{M}_{k}

(0,\ldots,0)
,

(2R+2k+2,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\boldsymbol{\xi}_{\!D_{3}}(\eta)=\gamma

\displaystyle D_{1}\cup D_{2}

\displaystyle\subset D_{12}\subset X\setminus\mathscr{B}(D_{3},R),

\displaystyle D_{2}\cup D_{3}

3.2.
Criterion for Uniform Locality

In this subsection, we prove a criterion for uniformity.
Let
\mathcal{X}=(X,E)
be a crystal lattice for the action of a group
G
which is essentially Euclidean,
and we fix a coordinate
(\Lambda_{F},\mathcal{S}^{+})
satisfying (
13
).
Assume that
(S,\phi)
is an interaction which is irreducibly quantified.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
such that
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f\in C^{1}_{R}(S^{X})
for some
R>0
.
If the pairing
h_{f}\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfies
h_{f}\equiv 0
,
then
f
is uniform.

We will give a proof of
Proposition

3.7
at the end of this section.
We first give a condition for a function to be uniform.

Lemma 3.8
.

Suppose
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
.
If there exists integer
R>0
such that for any finite
\Lambda\subset X
and
x\in X
,
we have

(16)

\iota^{\Lambda}f-\iota^{\Lambda\setminus\mathscr{B}(x,0)}f=\iota^{\Lambda\cap\mathscr{B}(x,R)}f-\iota^{\Lambda\cap\mathscr{B}^{*}(x,R)}f,

then
f
is a uniform function in
C_{\mathrm{unif}}(S^{X})
.
Here,
\mathscr{B}^{*}(x,R)\coloneqq\mathscr{B}(x,R)\setminus\mathscr{B}(x,0)
.

Proof.

First observe that for any
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
, we have

\displaystyle\iota^{\Lambda}f

\displaystyle=\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda\setminus\mathscr{B}(x,0)}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda,\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\iota^{\Lambda\setminus\mathscr{B}(x,0)}f+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda,\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}.

If (
16
) holds, then we have

(17)

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda,\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda\cap\mathscr{B}(x,R),\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}.

Let
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{X}\mid f_{\Lambda}\neq 0,{\mathrm{diam}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)>R\}
,
and assume that
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset
.
We take
\Lambda
to be a minimal element in
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}
with respect to inclusions, so that
if
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda
, then we have
f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=0
or
{\mathrm{diam}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda^{\prime\prime})\leq R
.
For such
\Lambda
, take
x\in\Lambda
such that there exists
y\in\Lambda
with
d_{\mathcal{S}}(x,y)>R
.
Then we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda,\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=f_{\Lambda}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\Lambda,\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}=f_{\Lambda}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\subset\Lambda\cap\mathscr{B}(x,R),\\
\Lambda^{\prime\prime}\cap\mathscr{B}(x,0)\neq\emptyset\end{subarray}}f_{\Lambda^{\prime\prime}}.

Comparing this with equality (
17
), we see that
f_{\Lambda}\equiv 0
.
This contradicts the fact that
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}^{\prime}
, hence that
\mathscr{I}^{\prime}=\emptyset
.
In other words, any
\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{X}
such that
f_{\Lambda}\neq 0
satisfies
{\mathrm{diam}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)\leq R
.
If we let
R^{\prime}\coloneqq{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda_{0})R
,
then
{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda)\leq{\mathrm{diam}}(\Lambda_{0}){\mathrm{diam}}_{\mathcal{S}}(\Lambda)\leq R^{\prime}
.
We see that

f=\sum_{\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{X},R^{\prime}}}f_{\Lambda},

which proves that
f
is uniform as desired.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
f\in C(S^{X}_{*})
such that
\partial_{\mathcal{X}}f\in C^{1}_{R}(S^{X})
for some

.
If the pairing

,
then for any

\Lambda\in\mathscr{I}_{X}

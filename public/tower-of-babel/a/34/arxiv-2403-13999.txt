On the ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -valued index of elliptic odd symmetric operators on non-compact manifolds
Maxim Braverman and Ahmad Reza Haj Saeedi Sadegh

Abstract
. We investigate elliptic operators with a symmetry that forces their index to vanish. We study the secondary index, defined modulo 2. We examine Callias-type operators with this symmetry on non-compact manifolds and establish mod 2 versions of the Gromov-Lawson relative index theorem, the Callias index theorem, and the Boutet de Monvel’s index theorem for Toeplitz operators. Résumé. Nous étudions des opérateurs elliptiques avec une symétrie qui force leur indice à s’annuler. Nous étudions l’indice secondaire, défini modulo 2. Nous examinons les opérateurs de type Callias avec cette symétrie sur des variétés non compactes et établissons des versions mod 2 du théorème de l’indice relatif de Gromov-Lawson, du théorème de l’indice de Callias, et du théorème de l’indice de Boutet de Monvel pour les opérateurs de Toeplitz.

1.
Introduction

In many cases, when symmetry causes the index of a Fredholm operator to vanish, a secondary invariant taking values in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
can be defined. The first example of this was the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of a skew-adjoint operator on a real Hilbert space, discovered by Atiyah and Singer almost 50 years ago,
[
4
]
. Since then, many other examples of symmetries have been considered, drawing on applications in physics, cf.
[
30
,
21
]
and references therein (see also
[
24
,
27
]
).

This paper focuses on the case of
odd symmetric operators
, studied in
[
30
,
20
,
21
]
and relevant to physical systems with time-reversing symmetries. From a mathematical perspective, this translates to the existence of an anti-unitary anti-involution, i.e., an anti-linear map
\tau
with
\tau^{2}=-1
. A Fredholm operator
D
is called
odd symmetric
if
\tau D\tau^{-1}=D^{*}
.

The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index (or
\tau
-index) of
D
is defined as

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ :=\ \dim\ker D\qquad\text{modulo}\quad 2.

This definition resembles the Atiyah-Singer index for skew-adjoint operators, but here
D
is not skew-adjoint. The reason for this definition is that the
\tau
-index is invariant under continuous homotopy (
[
20
,
21
]
) and shares many other properties with the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index. Moreover, it is shown in
[
30
]
, that the space
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
of odd symmetric Fredholm operators on an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space,
H
, is the classifying space for the
KO^{-2}
functor. In particular,
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H))=KO^{-2}(\textit{pt})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Therefore operators
A,B\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
belong to the same connected component of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
if and only if they have the same
\tau
-index (see Remark
2.7
for more details).

This paper investigates the
\tau
-index of first-order elliptic odd symmetric differential operators on both compact and non-compact manifolds. We obtain analogs of several results for the usual index in the odd symmetric case. The proofs often closely resemble those in the classical case, although some modifications are necessary. Firstly, to ensure that all constructions respect the
\tau
-action, and secondly, the notion of a topological index and the Atiyah-Singer-type index theorem for the
\tau
-index is currently absent.

One of the applications of the
\tau
-index is in computing the bulk index in the theory of topological insulators with time-reversal symmetry, particularly for 2D topological insulators of type AII
[
24
,
27
]
. Graf and Porta
[
24
]
established a bulk-edge correspondence for these insulators, comparing the
\tau
-valued index with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued spectral flow. We address this problem in
[
10
]
and establish a generalization of the Graf-Porta result to a broader class of operators. This is similar to how the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued bulk-edge correspondence is generalized in
[
15
]
.

Let us now provide a more detailed description of our main results and constructions.

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold and let
\theta:M\to M
be a metric preserving involution. The pair
(M,\theta)
is referred to as an involutive manifold. A
quaternionic bundle
over
M
is a complex vector bundle
E
together with an anti-unitary anti-involution, which covers
\theta
,
[
23
,
19
,
27
]
. By this, we mean that for every
x\in M
we are given a map

\theta^{E}_{x}:E_{x}\to E_{\theta x}
, such that
\theta^{E}_{\theta x}\circ\theta^{E}_{x}=-1
. We define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
by

\tau:\,f(x)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{E}f(\theta x),\qquad f\in\Gamma(M,E),

and consider an elliptic first-order operator
D
which is odd symmetric with respect to
\tau
. Suppose that
D
is Fredholm. This is, for example, the case when
M
is compact. But we are also interested in the case when
M
is complete, but
D
is still Fredholm, e.g. when
D
is of Callias type, cf.
[
3
,
2
,
17
]
).

A quaternionic Dirac bundle above in the case of the trivial involution
\theta:M\to M
gives the setting for the so-called
\textbf{Cl}_{2}
-Dirac operator (
[
29
]
) which was originally studied by Atiyah and Singer (
[
5
]
). See Remark
3.2

In Section
3
, we study the basic properties of the
\tau
-index of an odd symmetric elliptic operator on a quaternionic vector bundle. We also compute the
\tau
-index in several geometric examples. One of those examples corresponds to the bulk index of 2d Topological Insulator of type AII, cf.
[
24
,
27
]
.

In Section
4
, we consider the case when
M
is a complete non-compact manifold and the operator is Callias-type (another name for it is a Dirac-Schrödinger operator,
[
16
]
). This means that we consider an operator
B=D+F
, where
D
is a first-order elliptic operator with a uniformly bounded principal symbol, and
F
is a bundle map, chosen so that
B
is Fredholm (cf. Section
4.1
). We establish an analog of the Gromov-Lawson relative index theorem,
[
25
]
.

In Section
5
, we specify to the case when
\not{\partial}
is an odd symmetric generalized Dirac operator on a complete involutive
odd-dimensional
manifold
M
. Here, we introduce a Callias-type operator
B=\not{\partial}+i\Phi
and establish a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued variant of the Callias index theorem,
[
3
,
17
]
. This theorem expresses the
\tau
-index of
B
as the
\tau
-index of a particular generalized Dirac operator on a hypersurface. Although our proof is similar to
[
9
]
, we needed to adapt several steps to maintain
\tau
-equivariance in our constructions. Moreover, we found several significant simplifications, which can also be applied to simplify the proof in the classical case.

In Section
7
, we apply the Callias index theorem to show that cobordisms preserve the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index.

In Section
8
, we prove a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the generalized Boutet de Monvel’s index theorem for Toeplitz operators,
[
8
]
. Boutet de Monvel established his result for a Toeplitz operator on a pseudo-convex domain. Guentner and Higson,
[
26
]
, reinterpreted it in terms of the index of a Callias-type operator. Bunke,
[
18
]
, generalized their result to Callias-type operators on general manifolds. In this section, we present a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of this generalized Boutet de Monvel theorem of Bunke.

2.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index of an odd symmetric operator

In this section, we consider a (possibly) unbounded Fredholm operator
D
which is symmetric with respect to a given anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
. Such operators are called
odd symmetric
,
[
30
,
20
,
21
]
. The index of such an operator vanishes due to its symmetry. Following
[
20
,
21
]
, we defined the
secondary invariant
–- the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-index of
D
by
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D:=\dim\ker D
modulo 2. This is similar to the Atiyah-Singer construction of the index of a skew-adjoint operator (though
D
is not skew-adjoint). We show that the
\tau
-index is invariant under homotopy and study other properties of this index. We also compute it in an example. Most of the material in this section is taken from
[
30
]
, but, since we work with unbounded operators, some minor changes are necessary.

2.1.
An anti-unitary anti-involution

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space and let
\tau:H\to H
be an
anti-linear
bounded map satisfying
\tau^{*}\tau=1
. Here we denote by
\tau^{*}
the unique anti–linear operator satisfying

\langle\tau x,y\rangle_{H}\ =\ \overline{\langle x,\tau^{*}y\rangle_{H}}\qquad\text{for all}\quad x,y\in H.

We also assume
\tau^{*}=-\tau
so that
\tau^{2}=-1
. We refer to such
\tau
as an
anti-unitary anti-involution
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\tau:H\to H
is an anti-unitary anti-involution. Then
\tau e
is perpendicular to
e
for every
e\in H
. Further, if
L\subset H
is a
\tau
-invariant subspace and
e\in H
is a vector perpendicular to
L
, then
\tau e
is also perpendicular to
L
.

Proof.

Since
\tau^{*}=-\tau
, we have

\langle\tau e,e\rangle_{H}\ =\ \overline{\langle e,\tau^{*}e\rangle_{H}}\ =\ -\,\langle\tau e,e\rangle_{H}.

Hence,
\langle\tau e,e\rangle_{H}=0
.

Suppose
L
is a
\tau
-invariant subspace and let
e\in H
be a vector perpendicular to
L
. For any
x\in L
,

\langle\tau e,x\rangle_{H}\ =\ \overline{\langle e,\tau^{*}x\rangle_{H}}\ =\ -\,\langle\tau x,e\rangle_{H}\ =\ 0.

∎

The following lemma is a version of the
Kramers’ degeneracy
, cf.
[
28
]
(see also
[
20
, Lemma 5.1]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

An anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
does not have non-zero fixed vectors, i.e.
\tau x=x
implies
x=0
. Further, if
\tau
acts on a finite-dimensional space
H
, then
\dim H
is even. Moreover, there exists a subspace
L\in H
such that
\tau L
is perpendicular to
L
and
H
decomposes into an orthogonal direct sum
H=L\oplus\tau L
.

Proof.

If
\tau x=x
, then
-x=\tau^{2}x=x
, hence,
x=0
.

Suppose now that
\dim H<\infty
. We will construct an orthogonal basis of
H
by induction. Choose any non-zero vector
e_{1}\in H
. Let
e_{2}=\tau e_{1}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
e_{1}\perp e_{2}
.

Suppose we already found
2k
linearly independent vectors
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2k}\}
in
H
, such that
e_{2j}=\tau e_{2j-1}
for all
j=1,\ldots,k
. Then
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2k}\}
is a
\tau
-invariant space. Choose any non-zero vector
e_{2k+1}
perpendicular to
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2k}\}
and set
e_{2k+2}:=\tau e_{2k+1}
. By Lemma
2.1
,
e_{2k+2}
is perpendicular to both
e_{2k+1}
and
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2k}\}
. Hence vectors
\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2k+2}\}
are mutually orthogonal.

By induction, we obtain an orthogonal basis of
H

\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}\}
such that
e_{2j+2}:=\tau e_{2j+1}
, which has an even number of vectors. Hence,
\dim H
is even. Set
L:=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},e_{3},\ldots,e_{2n-1}\}
. Then
\tau L=\operatorname{span}\{e_{2},e_{4},\ldots,e_{2n}\}
and
H=L\oplus\tau L
.
∎

2.2.
Odd symmetric operators

Fix an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau:H\to H
.

Definition 2.3
.

A closed linear operator
D:H\to H
is called
odd symmetric
(cf.
[
20
,
21
]
) if

(2.1)

\tau\,D\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ D^{*}.

Let
W\subset H
be a dense subspace and let
D:H\to H
be an unbounded closed linear operator on
H
whose domain is
W
. Then
W
is a Hilbert space with the inner product

\langle x,y\rangle_{W}\ =\ \langle x,y\rangle_{H}\ +\ \langle Dx,Dy\rangle_{W}

and the operator
D:W\to H
is a bounded. We denote by
{F}_{\tau}(W,H)
the space of Fredholm operators on
H
with domain
W
, i.e., odd symmetric operators with closed range and finite-dimensional kernel and cokernel. The space
{F}_{\tau}(W,H)
is endowed with the norm operator topology.

Remark 2.4
.

In our geometric applications,
W
will be a version of the first Sobolev space
W^{1,2}
. Hence the notation.

2.3.
Graded odd symmetric operators

Assume now that
H=H^{+}\oplus H^{-}
is a graded Hilbert space and that
D
is
odd with respect to this grading
, i.e.
D:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. Let
W^{\pm}:=W\cap H^{\pm}
. Then
W=W^{+}\oplus W^{-}
. We denote
D^{\pm}
the restriction of
D
to
H^{\pm}
and write
D=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}
. We are mostly interested in the case when
D
is self-adjoint. Then
(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
.

Assume that the anti-involution
\tau
is also odd with respect to the grading,
\tau:H^{\pm}\to H^{\mp}
. If
D
is odd symmetric, then (
2.1
) implies that
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=D^{\mp}
. In this situation, we say that
D
is a
graded odd symmetric operator
.

We denote the space of graded Fredholm odd symmetric self-adjoint operators by
\widehat{{F}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.
If
D\in\widehat{{F}}_{\tau}(W,H)
, then
\tau D^{\pm}\tau^{-1}=D^{\mp}=(D^{\pm})^{*}
. Then we say that
the operators
D^{\pm}
are odd symmetric
. Notice, that in this case
\tau:W\to W
.

2.4.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index

Let
D
be a Fredholm odd symmetric operator. It follows from (
2.1
) that
\operatorname{ind}D=0
. Following
[
30
]
, we define the
secondary

\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued invariant – the
\tau
-index of
D
by

(2.2)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D\ :=\ \dim\ker D\qquad\mod 2.

This is similar to the Atiyah-Singer index of skew-adjoint Fredholm operators,
[
4
]
.

If
D\in\widehat{{F}}_{\tau}(W,H)
is a graded Fredholm odd symmetric self-adjoint operator on a graded Hilbert space
H
, we define

(2.3)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ :=\ \dim\ker D^{+}\qquad\mod 2.

The reason for these definitions is that the
\tau
-index is stable under homotopy of
D
:

Theorem 2.5
.

(i)

The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D
is constant on the connected components of
{F}_{\tau}(W,H)
.

(ii)

The
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}
is constant on the connected components of
\widehat{{F}}_{\tau}(W,H)
.

Proof.

We prove the graded case. The proof in the ungraded case is practically the same.

Let
D_{s}
(
s\in[0,1]
) be a continuous path in
\widehat{{F}}_{\tau}(W,H)
. It suffices to show that for every
s_{0}\in[0,1]
there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{s}^{+}
is independent of
s
modulo 2 for
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
.

We have

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{s}^{+}\ =\ \dim\ker D_{s}^{+}\ =\ \dim\ker D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}.

For
s_{0}\in[0,1]
, there exists
\epsilon,\delta>0
such that
\delta
is not in the spectrum of
D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}
for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)
and the intersection of the spectrum of
D_{s_{0}}^{-}D_{s_{0}}^{+}
with the interval
[0,\delta]
is equal to
\{0\}
.

For
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, let
E_{s,\lambda}\subset H^{+}
denote the eigenspace of
D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}
with eigenvalue
\lambda
. Our assumptions on
\epsilon
and
\delta
imply that, for all
s\in(s_{0}-\epsilon,s_{0}+\epsilon)

\sum_{0\leq\lambda<\delta}\dim E_{s,\lambda}\ =\ \dim\ker D_{s_{0}}^{-}D_{s_{0}}^{+}.

To prove the homotopy invariance of
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{s}^{+}
it now suffices to show that the dimension of
E_{s,\lambda}
is even for all
0<\lambda<\delta
.

Using (
2.1
) and
\tau^{-1}=-\tau
, we conclude that

D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}\,\tau D_{s}^{+}\ =\ \tau D_{s}^{+}\,D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}.

It follows that
\tau D_{s}^{+}(E_{s,\lambda})\subset E_{s,\lambda}
. For
\lambda>0
consider the operator

{E}_{s,\lambda}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\tau D_{s}^{+}:\,E_{s,\lambda}\ \to E_{s,\lambda}.

Then

{E}_{s,\lambda}^{2}\ =\ \frac{1}{\lambda}\,\tau D_{s}^{+}\tau D_{s}^{+}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\lambda}\tau D_{s}^{+}\tau^{-1}D_{s}^{+}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\lambda}D_{s}^{-}D_{s}^{+}\ =\ -\operatorname{Id}:\,E_{s,\lambda}\ \to E_{s,\lambda}.

Thus
{E}_{s,\lambda}:\,E_{s,\lambda}\ \to E_{s,\lambda}
is an anti-unitary anti-involution. From Kramers’ degeneracy Lemma
2.2
we conclude now that the dimension of
E_{s,\lambda}
is even for all
\lambda\in(0,\delta)
.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
D\in{F}_{\tau}(W,H)
and let
K:W\to H
be a compact odd symmetric operator. Then the operator
D+K
and
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(D+K)=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D
.

Remark 2.7
.

In
[
30
]
, it is shown that the space
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
of odd symmetric Fredholm operators on an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space,
H
, is the classifying space for the
KO^{-2}
functor; that is for a compact Hausdorff space
X
, there is a bijection

(2.4)

KO^{-2}(X)\simeq[X,\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)].

In particular,
\pi_{0}(\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H))=KO^{-2}(\textit{pt})\simeq\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. Therefore, by Theorem
2.5
, the
\tau
-index defines an isomorphism
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}:KO^{-2}(\textit{pt})\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In particular, operators
A,B\in\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
belong to the same connected component of
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}(H)
if and only if they have the same
\tau
-index. Below, we briefly recall how the correspondence (
2.4
) is achieved in
[
30
]
.

Let
J:H\to H
denote the multiplication by the scalar
\sqrt{-1}
and by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle:H\times H\to\mathbb{C}
its inner product. Using Lemma
2.2
, one can show that there is a ‘complex conjugation’ operator
C:H\to H
and a ‘real’ unitary operator
I:H\to H
such that
I^{2}=-1
and
\tau=CI
(by real we mean
CIC=I
). One can see that the correspondence
T\mapsto IT
gives a bijection from
\mathcal{F}_{\tau}
to the space of antisymmetric Fredholm operators
\mathcal{F}_{as}(H)=\{T\in\mathcal{F}(H):T^{t}=-T\}
, where
T^{t}:=CT^{*}C
is the transpose of
T
. Now the correspondence
B\mapsto BC
gives a bijection from
\mathcal{F}_{as}(H)
to
\mathcal{F}^{2}(H_{\mathbb{R}})
where
H_{\mathbb{R}}
is the real Hilbert given by the inner product
(\cdot,\cdot):=\textit{Re}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
H
, and
\mathcal{F}^{2}(H_{\mathbb{R}})
is the space of real-linear skew-adjoint operators
A:H_{\mathbb{R}}\to H_{\mathbb{R}}
with the property
AJ=-JA
, cf.
[
4
]
. Now the space
\mathcal{F}^{2}({H_{\mathbb{R}}})
is a Classifying space for the
KO^{-2}
functor as shown in
[
4
]
.

2.5.
Example: A first-order differential operator on a line

Let

\theta_{\mathbb{C}}:\mathbb{C}^{2}\ \to\ \mathbb{C}^{2},\qquad\theta_{\mathbb{C}}:\,(z_{1},z_{2})\ \mapsto\ (\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1}).

Clearly,
\theta
is anti-linear, and
\theta_{\mathbb{C}}=-\theta_{\mathbb{C}}^{*}=-\theta_{\mathbb{C}}^{-1}
.

Let
H:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}^{2})
be the space of square-integrable functions with values in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
and let
W:=W^{1,2}(\mathbb{R},H)
, where
W^{1,2}
denotes the Sobolev space of square-integrable functions, whose first derivative is also square-integrable. We write functions in
H
as
f(t)=\big(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\big)

We define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau_{\mathbb{C}}:H\to H
by

(2.5)

\tau:\,f(t)\ \mapsto\ \theta_{\mathbb{C}}f(-t).

Consider the operator
D:W\to H
defined by the formula

(2.6)

D\ :=\ \frac{d}{dt}\ +\ \begin{pmatrix}\arctan(t)&0\\
0&-\arctan(t)\end{pmatrix}.

One readily sees that
D
is Fredholm and
\tau
-symmetric.

Lemma 2.8
.

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D=1.

Proof.

If
f(t)=\big(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\big)
is in the kernel of
D
, then

\dot{f}_{1}(t)\ =\ -\arctan(t)\,f_{1}(t),\qquad\dot{f}_{2}(t)\ =\ \arctan(t)\,f_{2}(t).

Hence,

f_{1}(t)=\ C\,e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\arctan(s)ds},\qquad f_{2}(t)=\ C\,e^{\int_{0}^{t}\arctan(s)ds}.

Clearly,
f_{1}(t)
is square-integrable, while
f_{2}(t)
is not square-integrable unless
C=0
. Thus, the kernel of
D
consists of vectors
C\big(e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\arctan(s)ds},0\big)
and, hence, has dimension one.
∎

3.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of an odd symmetric elliptic operator

In this section, we study the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued index of odd symmetric elliptic operators on a manifold with involution and we compute it in several geometric examples.

3.1.
Quaternionic vector bundles

Let
M
be a Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
. Usually, we will assume that
M
is either closed or complete. Throughout the paper, we identify the tangent and the cotangent bundles to
M
using the Riemannian metric and write
TM
for both bundles.

Let
\theta:M\to M
be a metric preserving involution,
\theta^{2}=1
. The pair
(M,\theta)
is called an
involutive Riemannian manifold
.

Definition 3.1
.

A
quaternionic vector bundle
over an involutive manifold
M
is a hermitian vector bundle
E
endowed with an anti-linear anti-unitary bundle map
\theta^{E}:E\to\theta^{*}E
, such that
(\theta^{E})^{2}=-1
. This means that for every
x\in M
there exists an anti-linear map
\theta^{E}_{x}:E_{x}\to E_{\theta(x)}
, which depends smoothly on
x
and satisfies
\theta^{E}_{\theta(x)}\,\theta^{E}_{x}=-1
.

If
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
is a graded vector bundle, we say that
\theta^{E}
is
odd (with respect to the grading)
if
\theta^{E}(E^{\pm})=E^{\mp}
. In this situation, we say that
(E,\theta^{E})
is a
graded quaternionic bundle
.

Given a quaternionic vector bundle
(E,\theta^{E})
over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta)
we define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
on the space
\Gamma(M,E)
of sections of
E
by

(3.1)

\tau:\,f(x)\ \mapsto\ \theta^{E}f(\theta x),\qquad f\in\Gamma(M,E).

Remark 3.2
.

When
M
is of dimension
8k+2
compact spin manifold with the involution
\theta:M\to M
the identity map, the complex spinor bundle is a quaternionic bundle. In this setting, the usual index of the Dirac operator vanishes as it is odd symmetric, and its associated
\tau
-index equals the complex dimension of the space of the harmonic spinors (see
[
5
]
and
[
29
]
).

3.2.
Odd symmetric elliptic operators

Let
(E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-},\theta^{E})
be a graded quaternionic vector bundle over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta)
. Let
D:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
be a self-adjoint odd symmetric elliptic differential operator on
M
, which is odd with respect to the grading, i.e.
D:\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})\to\Gamma(M,E^{\mp})
. We set
D^{\pm}:=D|_{\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})}
. Then

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

with respect to the decomposition
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
. Our assumptions imply that
\tau\,D^{\pm}\,\tau^{-1}=(D^{\pm})^{*}=D^{\mp}
.

Suppose now that
D
is Fredholm. In particular, this is always the case when
M
is compact. Then the
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is defined by (
2.2
).
The goal of this paper is to study the properties of this index.

Definition 3.3
.

We denote by
W^{1,2}(M,E)
the domain of the closure of
D
viewed as a Hilbert space (this is the space which we denoted by
W
in Section
2
). We refer to
W^{1,2}(M,E)
as the
Sobolev space
.

The operator
D
is a bounded operator
D:W^{1,2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
D
be a Fredholm odd symmetric elliptic differential operator on a graded quaternionic bundle
E
. Suppose
V:E\to E
is an odd symmetric bundle map which is odd with respect to the grading,
V:E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
. Assume that the support of
V
is compact in
M
. Then
D+V
is Fredholm and

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}(D^{+}+V^{+})\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+},

where
V^{+}
denotes the restriction of
V
to
E^{+}
.

Proof.

The Fredholmness follows from
[
2
, Theorem 2.1(vi)]
. The operator
V:W^{1,2}(M,E)\to L^{2}(M,E)
is a compact by Rellich’s Lemma. The lemma follows now from Corollary
2.6
.
∎

Next, we consider several examples of Dirac-type operators with non-trivial
\tau
-index.

3.3.
Example 1: An operator on a real line

This example is a reformulation of the example in Section
2.5
. Let
M=\mathbb{R}
and let
\theta(t):=-t
. Then
(M,\theta)
is an involutive manifold of dimension 1. We identify the tangent space to
M
at any point
t
with
\mathbb{R}
. Let
E:=M\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
and consider the operator
D^{+}:=i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
.

Set

V^{+}\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}i\arctan(t)&0\\
0&-i\arctan(t)\end{pmatrix}.

Define the anti-involution
\theta^{E}
by
\theta^{E}(z_{1},z_{2})=(\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1})
. Then,

\tau:\,\big(\sigma_{1}(t),\sigma_{2}(t)\big)\ \mapsto\ \big(\bar{\sigma}_{2}(-t),\bar{\sigma}_{1}(-t)\big).

One readily checks that the operator
D_{V}^{+}=D+V^{+}
is
\tau
-symmetric.

Then
D_{V}^{+}=iD
where
D
is given by (
2.6
). Hence, by Lemma
2.8
, we have

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{V}^{+}\ \equiv\ \dim\ker D_{V}^{+}\ =\ 1

where
\equiv
denotes equality modulo 2.

3.4.
Example 2: A torus with the trivial involution

Let
M=S^{1}\times S^{1}
be a two-dimensional torus with the product metric. We write a point
x\in M
as
x=(e^{it},e^{is})
, where
t,s\in[-\pi,\pi]
and let
\theta:M\to M
be the identity map. Let
E=M\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
be the trivial bundle over
M
, endowed
with the trivial metric and connection. We identify sections of
E
with two-component functions
(f_{1},f_{2}):M\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
.

Consider the operator

D\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}0&-\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial s}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
D^{+}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial s}
.

Define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\theta^{E}:M\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\to M\times\mathbb{C}^{2}
by
\theta^{E}:(x;z_{1},z_{2})=(x;\bar{z}_{2},-\bar{z}_{1})
, where
x\in M,\ z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Then
\tau\big(f_{1}(x),f_{2}(x)\big)=\big(\bar{f}_{2}(x),-\bar{f}_{1}(x)\big)
. One readily sees that
\tau
commutes with
D
and, hence,
D
is odd symmetric. Then the kernel of
D^{+}
consists of functions
f:M\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\ +\ i\frac{\partial f}{\partial s}\ =\ 0.

It follows that
f
is harmonic on
M=S^{1}\times S^{1}
:

\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial t^{2}}\ +\ \frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial s^{2}}\ =\ 0.

Hence,
f
is a constant, i.e. the kernel of
D^{+}
is one-dimensional and

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ \equiv\ \dim\ker D^{+}\ =\ 1\ \in\mathbb{Z}_{2}.

3.5.
Example 3: A a torus with time-reversal involution

Our next example is a reformulation of the bulk index of 2d Topological Insulator of type AII, cf.
[
24
,
27
]
.

Let
M=\mathbb{C}/\{m+in:m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\}
be a complex torus. Topologically it is
S^{1}\times S^{1}
. We write a point in
M
as
z=s+it
,
(-\pi\leq t,s\leq\pi)
and define a holomorphic involution
\theta:M\to M
, by
\theta:s+it\mapsto-s-it
.

Let
L_{n}
be a complex line bundle over
M
with Chern class
n\in\mathbb{Z}\simeq H^{2}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. It can be described as the quotient of the trivial bundle
[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]\times\mathbb{C}
over
[-\pi,\pi]\times[-\pi,\pi]
by the equivalence relations

(-\pi,s,v)\ \sim\ (\pi,s,v),\qquad(t,-\pi,v)\ \sim\ (t,\pi,ve^{int}).

To see that the Chern class of
L_{n}
is indeed
n
, let us consider the connection
\nabla
on
L_{n}
, which at the point
s+it\in M
has the form

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}\ =\ \frac{\partial}{\partial s},\qquad\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\ =\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{in}{2\pi}s.

Notice that at
s=\pm\pi
we have
\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mp\frac{in}{2}
. Hence,

\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\big|_{s=-\pi}\ =\ \frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\frac{in}{2}\ =\ e^{-int}\,\nabla_{\frac{\partial}{\partial t}}\big|_{s=\pi}\,e^{int},

which shows that
\nabla
is a smooth connection on
L_{n}
. The curvature of
\nabla
is the form
\omega=-\frac{in}{2\pi}dt\wedge ds
. Thus the representative of the Chern class of
L_{n}
in Chern-Weil theory is
\frac{i}{2\pi}\omega=\frac{n}{(2\pi)^{2}}dt\wedge ds
. Since the integral of this form over
M
is
n
, we conclude that
L_{n}
is indeed a bundle with Chern class
n
.

The curvature of the pull-back bundle
\theta^{*}L_{n}
is also equal to
\omega=\theta^{*}\omega
. So
\theta^{*}L_{n}
is also a line bundle with Chern class
n
and, hence, is isomorphic to
L_{n}
.

We view the bundle
L_{-n}
as the complex conjugate of
L_{n}
and define a bundle map
\alpha_{n}:L_{n}\to\theta^{*}L_{n}
by

\alpha_{n}:\,(t,s,v)\ \mapsto\ (-t,-s,\bar{v}),\qquad-\pi\leq t,s\leq\pi.

One readily sees that this formula defines a smooth map over
M
(i.e., satisfies the correct periodic conditions on the boundary). It is an anti-linear map and
\alpha_{n}\circ\alpha_{-n}=\operatorname{Id}
.

Let
\Lambda^{0,j}M
(
j=0,1
) denote the bundle of anti-holomorphic exterior forms on
M
. The space of sections
\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{0,j}M\otimes L_{n})
is the space
\Omega^{0,j}(M,L_{n})
of anti-holomorphic differential forms with values in
L_{n}
. Set

E^{+}\ :=\ \big(\Lambda^{0,0}M\otimes L_{n}\big)\oplus\big(\Lambda^{0,0}M\otimes L_{-n}\big);\qquad E^{-}\ :=\ \big(\Lambda^{0,1}M\otimes L_{-n}\big)\oplus\big(\Lambda^{0,1}M\otimes L_{n}\big).

Thus the space of sections of
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
is equal to
\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n})
.

Let
c:TM\to\operatorname{End}\Lambda^{0,*}M
be the standard Clifford action. It is defined as follows (see
[
14
, §2]
for more details): for a tangent vector
v\in TM
we write it as
v=v^{1,0}\oplus v^{0,1}
according to the decomposition
T_{\mathbb{C}}M=T^{1,0}M\oplus T^{0,1}M
of the tangent bundle to the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic part. Using the Riemannian metric we transform
v^{1,0}
to a cotangent vector
Iv^{1,0}
. Then

c(v):\,\alpha\ \mapsto\ \sqrt{2}\,\big(\,Iv^{1,0}\wedge\alpha\ -\ \iota_{v^{0,1}}\alpha\,\big).

Then
c(v)^{2}=-|v|^{2}
. We also denote by
c(v)
the induced operators on
\Lambda^{0,*}M\otimes\big(L_{n}\oplus L_{-n}\big)
and
\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n})
.

Since
\partial/\partial s=\frac{\partial/\partial z+\partial/\partial\bar{z}}{2}
and
\partial/\partial t=\frac{\partial/\partial z-\partial/\partial\bar{z}}{2i}
, we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle c(\partial/\partial s):\,\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\ \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\,\big(\,d\bar{z}\wedge\alpha\ -\ \iota_{\partial/\partial\bar{z}}\alpha\,\big).

\displaystyle c(\partial/\partial t):\,\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\ \frac{1}{i\sqrt{2}}\,\big(\,-d\bar{z}\wedge\alpha\ -\ \iota_{\partial/\partial\bar{z}}\alpha\,\big).

We define an anti-linear anti-involution on
E
by

\theta\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}0&\alpha_{-n}\\
\alpha_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}\circ c(\partial/\partial s)

and let
\tau:\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n})\to\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n})
be as in (
3.1
).

Consider the Dolbeault-Dirac operators

D_{\pm n}\ :=\ c(\partial/\partial s)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial s}\ +\ c(\partial/\partial t)\,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}:\,\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{\pm n})\ \to\ \Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{\pm n}).

By (
3.2
),
c(\partial/\partial s)
commutes and
c(\partial/\partial t)
anti-commutes with complex conjugation and, hence, with
\begin{pmatrix}0&-\alpha_{n}\\
\alpha_{n}&0\end{pmatrix}
.
Also
c(\partial/\partial s)
commutes and
c(\partial/\partial t)
anti-commutes with
c(\partial/\partial s)
. Therefore,

\tau\circ D_{\pm n}\circ\tau^{-1}\ =\ D_{\mp n}.

In particular, setting
D_{\pm n}^{+}:=D_{\pm n}\big|_{\Omega^{0,0}(M,L_{\pm n})}
and
D_{\pm n}^{-}:=D_{\pm n}\big|_{\Omega^{0,1}(M,L_{\pm n})}
, we obtain

(3.3)

\ker D_{-n}^{+}\ =\ \ker D_{n}^{-}.

Set

D\ :=\ D_{n}\oplus D_{-n}:\,\Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n})\ \to\ \Omega^{0,*}(M,L_{n}\oplus L_{-n}).

Then
\tau\circ D\circ\tau^{-1}=D
, i.e.,
D
is odd-symmetric. By (
3.3
),

(3.4)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}\ =\ \dim\ker D^{+}\ =\ \dim\ker D_{n}^{+}\ +\ \dim\ker D_{-n}^{-}\\
=\ \dim\ker D_{n}^{+}\ +\ \dim\ker D_{n}^{-}\equiv\ \operatorname{ind}D_{n},

where the last equality holds modulo 2.

Since the Todd class of
M
is trivial, it follows from the Atiyah-Singer index theorem that the index of
D_{n}
is equal to the Chern class of
L_{n}
. We conclude that

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}=0\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}

if
n
is even and
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D^{+}=1\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
n
is odd.

4.
The relative index theorem

In this section, we study the
\tau
-index of odd symmetric Callias-type operators on non-compact manifolds and prove a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the Gromov-Lawson relative index theorem. In the next section, we prove an analog of the Callias-type index theorem.

This section’s structure and many constructions are borrowed from
[
9
]
.

4.1.
Callias–type operators

Consider a complete Riemannian manifold
M
and let
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
be a graded complex vector bundle over
M
. We consider a formally self-adjoint first order elliptic differential operator
D

D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}\\
D^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}

acting on smooth sections of
E
. Here
D^{-}
and
D^{+}
are differential operators associated with the negative and positive parts of
E
, respectively. We make the following

Assumption 4.1
.

The principal symbol of
D
is uniformly bounded from above, i.e. there exists a constant
b>0
such that

(4.1)

\big\|\sigma(D)(x,\xi)\big\|\ \leq\ b\,|\xi|,\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in M,\ \xi\in T^{*}_{x}M.

Here
|\xi|
denotes the length of
\xi
defined by the Riemannian metric
,
\sigma(D)(x,\xi):E^{\pm}_{x}\rightarrow E^{\mp}_{x}
is the leading symbol of
D
, and
\|\sigma(D)(x,\xi)\|
is its operator norm.

Under this assumption, the operator
D
is essentially self-adjoint by
[
25
]
.
We note that Assumption
4.1
is automatically satisfied if
D
is a Dirac-type operator.

Let
F^{+}:E^{+}\rightarrow E^{-}
be a bundle map. Let
F^{-}=(F^{+})^{*}
be the formal adjoint of
F^{+}
and set

F\ :=\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&F^{-}\\
F^{+}&0\end{array}\right).

Definition 4.2
.

The endomorphism
F
is called
admissible
if there exists a compact set
K\subset M
and a constant
c>0
such that

(i)

the restriction of the
anticommutator

\{D,F\}\ :=\ D\,F\ +\ F\,D\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}D^{-}F^{+}+F^{-}D^{+}&0\\
0&D^{+}F^{-}+F^{+}D^{-}\end{pmatrix}

to
M\setminus K
is an operator of order
0
, i.e. a bundle map;

(ii)

for every
x\in M\setminus K
we have

(4.2)

F(x)^{2}\ \geq\ \big\|\{D,F\}(x)\big\|\ +\ c,

where
\big\|\{D,F\}(x)\big\|
denotes the operator norm of the bounded operator
\{D,F\}(x):E_{x}\to E_{x}
.

In this case, we refer to the compact set
K
as an
essential support
of
F
.

Definition 4.3
.

A (graded)
Callias-type operator
is an operator of the form

(4.3)

\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{B}_{F}\ :=\ D\ +\ F\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}+F^{-}\\
D^{+}+F^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
F
is an admissible endomorphism. We set
\mathcal{B}^{\pm}:=D^{\pm}+F^{\pm}
.

A Callias-type operator is Fredholm, cf.
[
3
, Proposition 1.4]
.

Suppose now that we are given an involution
\theta:M\to M
and that
(E,\theta^{E})
is a graded quaternionic vector bundle over
(M,\theta)
, cf. Section
3.1
. Assume that
D
and
F
are odd symmetric. Then
\mathcal{B}
is an odd symmetric Callias-type operator, and its
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}
is defined. The rest of this section is dedicated to the study of this index.

Lemma 4.4
.

If the essential support of
F
is empty, then
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}=0
.

Proof.

If (
4.2
) holds everywhere on
M
, then

\mathcal{B}^{2}\ =\ D^{2}\ +\ \{D,F\}\ +\ F^{2}\ \geq\ D^{2}\ +\ c\ >\ 0.

Hence,
\ker\mathcal{B}=\{0\}.

∎

4.2.
The relative index theorem

Suppose
(E_{j},\theta^{E_{j}})
(
j=0,1
) are graded quaternionic bundles over complete involutive Riemannian manifolds
(M_{j},\theta_{j})
and
\mathcal{B}_{j}=D_{j}+F_{j}
are odd symmetric Callias-type operators. Let
\tau_{j}:\Gamma(M_{j},E^{j})\to\Gamma(M_{j},E^{j})
be as in (
3.1
).

We say that a bundle map
\Psi:E_{j}\to E_{k}
(
j,k=0,1)
is
\tau
-equivariant
if
\Psi\,\tau_{j}=\tau_{k}\,\Psi
. We say that
\Psi
is graded if
\Psi:E_{j}^{\pm}\to E_{k}^{\pm}
.

Suppose there are partitions
M_{j}=W_{j}\cup_{N_{j}}V_{j}
into
\theta_{j}
-invariant submanifolds, such that
N_{j}
are
compact

\theta_{j}
-invariant hypersurfaces.
We make the following

Assumption 4.5
.

There exist
\theta_{j}
-invariant tubular neighborhoods
U(N_{j})
of
N_{j}
(
j=0,1
) and a
\tau
-invariant diffeomorphism
\psi:U(N_{0})\rightarrow U(N_{1})
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

The restriction of
\psi
to
N_{0}
is a diffeomorphism onto
N_{1}
;

(ii)

there exists a
\tau
-equivariant isomorphism of graded quaternionic bundles
\Psi:E_{0}|_{U(N_{0})}\rightarrow E_{1}|_{U(N_{1})}
covering
\psi
;

(iii)

the restrictions
D_{0}|_{U(N_{0})}
and
D_{1}|_{U(N_{1})}
are conjugated through the isomorphism
\Psi
.

We cut
M_{j}
along
N_{j}
and use the map
\psi
to glue the pieces together interchanging
V_{0}
and
V_{1}
, cf. Figure
1

In this way, we obtain the manifolds

M_{2}:=W_{0}\cup_{N}V_{1},\qquad M_{3}:=W_{1}\cup_{N}V_{0},

where
N\cong N_{0}\cong N_{1}
. We refer to
M_{2}
and
M_{3}
as
manifolds obtained from
M_{0}
and
M_{1}
by cutting and pasting
. They are naturally involutive manifolds.

and such that

.

F_{0}

such that:

(i)

)
.

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{0}^{+}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{+}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{+}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}.

.

4.3.
The proof of Theorem
4.6

Set
M:=M_{1}\sqcup M_{2}\sqcup M_{3}\sqcup M_{4}
and let
\theta
be the involution on
M
induced by
\theta_{j}

(j=0,1,2,3,4)
.
We use
E_{1}
,
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
to construct the bundle

(4.4)

E:=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}^{\textrm{op}}\oplus E_{4}^{\textrm{op}}

over
M
, where the superscript “op” means that we consider the opposite grading on the fibers of
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
. Then
\theta^{E_{j}}
induces an anti-involution
\theta^{E}
on
E
, which gives
E
a structure of a graded quaternionic vector bundle over
M
.

Consider the Callias-type operator

(4.5)

\mathcal{B}\ :=\ \mathcal{B}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{3}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{4}

acting on smooth sections of
E
. Our grading agreement (
4.4
) implies that

\mathcal{B}^{+}\ =\ \mathcal{B}_{1}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{B}_{2}^{+}\oplus\ \mathcal{B}_{3}^{-}\oplus B_{4}^{-}.

Since the operators
\mathcal{B}_{j}
are odd symmetric we have
\dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{j}^{+}=\dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{j}^{-}
. Thus we have the following chain of equalities in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
:

(4.6)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}^{+}\ :=\ \dim\ker\mathcal{B}^{+}\ =\ \dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{1}^{+}\ +\ \dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{2}^{+}\ +\ \dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{3}^{-}\ +\ \dim\ker\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}\\
=\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{1}^{+}+\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{2}^{+}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{3}^{+}\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}_{4}^{+}\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}.

Hence, Theorem
4.6
is equivalent to the equality
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}^{+}=0
.

For
j=1,2
, let
\alpha_{j}
,
\beta_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M_{j})
be the cut-off functions defined in Section
4.2
. Using these function in
[
9
, §3.3]
the authors constructed a self-adjoint bundle map
U\in C^{\infty}(M,\operatorname{End}(E))
with the following properties:

(i)

U^{2}=\operatorname{Id}_{E}
;

(ii)

U:E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
is odd with respect to the grading (to have this property we need to define the grading in
E
by (
4.4
));

(iii)

the anticommutator
\{\mathcal{B},U\}:=\mathcal{B}U+U\mathcal{B}
is an even compactly supported endomorphism of the bundle
E
.

This operator is given by an explicit formula in terms of the cut-off functions
\alpha_{j}
,
\beta_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M_{j})
(
j=0,1
). One readily sees that, since these functions are
\theta_{j}
-invariant, the operator
U
is odd symmetric. Thus so is the operator
U\mathcal{B}U
. By Condition (
i
),
U^{-1}=U
.

From Condition (
iii
) we see that
W:=U\mathcal{B}U+\mathcal{B}
is an odd symmetric bundle map with compact support. Consider the operator

(4.7)

\frac{\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U}{2}\ =\ \mathcal{B}\ -\ \frac{W}{2}.

Let
(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}
denote the restriction of
\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U
to
E^{+}
. By Lemma
3.4
,

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}^{+}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\frac{(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}}{2}.

Since
U
is odd symmetric and self-adjoint,
U\,\tau=\tau\,U
. Hence, the map
\tau\,U:\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E)
is an anti-unitary anti-involution of even grading degree, i.e.
\tau\,U:\Gamma(M,E^{+})\to\Gamma(M,E^{+})
. Since
\mathcal{B}
and U are odd symmetric, we have

(\tau\,U)\,\frac{(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}}{2}\,(\tau\,U)^{-1}\ =\ -\,\frac{(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}}{2}.

Hence,
\tau\,U
acts on the kernel of
(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}
. By Lemma
2.2
, the dimension of the kernel of
(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}
is even. Thus, by (
4.7
)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{B}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\frac{(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}}{2}\ \equiv\ \dim\ker\frac{(\mathcal{B}-U\mathcal{B}U)^{+}}{2}\ =\ 0\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}_{2},

as required.
\square

5.
The Callias-type theorem

In this section, we consider the case of a Dirac-type Callias-type operator and prove a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the Callias index theorem,
[
3
,
17
]
.

5.1.
A (generalized) Dirac operator

Suppose now that
E
is a
Dirac bundle
over a complete oriented Riemannian manifold
M
, cf.
[
29
, Definition II.5.2]
. By this, we mean that it is endowed with
Clifford action

c:TM\to\operatorname{End}(E)
, such that
c(v)^{2}=-|v|^{2}
(
v\in TM
), a Hermitian metric
h^{E}
with respect to which the operators
c(v)
are skew-adjoint, and a Hermitian
Clifford connection

\nabla^{E}
which satisfies the
Leibniz rule

(5.1)

\big[\nabla^{E},c(v)\big]\ =\ c(\nabla^{LC}v),

where
\nabla^{LC}
denote the Levi-Civita connection on
TM
. Then, the
(generalised) Dirac operator
is defined by

(5.2)

\not{\partial}\ :=\ \sum_{1\leq j\leq n}\,c(e_{i})\nabla^{E}_{e_{j}}:\,\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E),

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
is an orthonormal frame of
TM
. Then
\not{\partial}
is a formally self-adjoint differential operator, which is independent of the choice of the frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
, cf.
[
6
, §3.3]
.

Consider the operator

(5.3)

\Gamma\ :=\ i^{[\frac{n+1}{2}]}c(e_{1})\cdot c(e_{2})\cdots c(e_{n}),

where
[x]
denotes the integer part of
x
. Then,
[
6
, Lemma 3.17]
,
\Gamma
is an involution
\Gamma^{2}=1
which is independent of the choice of the frame
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n})
.

Consider, first, the case when
n
is even. Then,
[
6
, Lemma 3.17]
,
\Gamma
anti-commmutes with
c(v)
. Let
E^{+}
and
E^{-}
denote the eigenspaces of
\Gamma
with eigenvalues 1 and
-1
respectively. Then
E=E^{+}\oplus E^{-}
and
c(v):E^{\pm}\to E^{\mp}
. The space of sections of
E
is also graded by

\Gamma(M,E)\ =\ \Gamma(M,E^{+})\oplus\Gamma(M,E^{-}),

and the Clifford connection
\nabla^{E}
preserves the grading. It follows that the Dirac operator
\not{\partial}
is
odd with respect to the grading
, i.e.,
\not{\partial}:\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})\to\Gamma(M,E^{\mp})
. We denote by
\not{\partial}^{\pm}
the restriction of
\not{\partial}
to
\Gamma(M,E^{\pm})
. Then,
\not{\partial}^{\pm}
is the formal adjoint of
\not{\partial}^{\mp}
, and, with respect to the grading, the Dirac operator has the form

(5.4)

\begin{pmatrix}0&\not{\partial}^{-}\\
\not{\partial}^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}.

If
n
is odd, then
\Gamma
commutes with
c(v)
(
v\in TM
) and does not define a useful grading on
E
. So we consider
E
as an ungraded vector bundle.

5.2.
An odd symmetric Dirac operator

Let
(E,\theta^{E})
be a quaternionic bundle over an involutive manifold
(M,\theta)
, which is also a Dirac bundle (no connections between the two structures on
E
yet). If
n
is even, we assume, in addition, that
\theta^{E}
is odd with respect to the grading.

Let
\tau
be as in (
3.1
). Suppose
\not{\partial}
is odd symmetric,
\tau\,\not{\partial}\,\tau^{-1}=\not{\partial}
. If
n
is even, that means that
\not{\partial}^{\pm}=\tau\,\not{\partial}^{\mp}\,\tau^{-1}
.

5.3.
A relationship between the quaternionic and the Clifford structures

Let
\theta_{*}:T_{x}M\to T_{\theta x}M
denote the differential of
\theta
. Since
\theta
is an involution, the
\theta_{*}=\theta_{*}^{-1}=\theta^{*}
, where
\theta^{*}
is the pull-back map of on the cotangent bundle, which we identified with
TM
. Comparing the leading symbols of
\not{\partial}
and
\tau\,\not{\partial}\,\tau^{-1}
we conclude that

(5.5)

\theta^{E}\,c(\theta_{*}\xi)\,(\theta^{E})^{-1}\ =\ c(\xi).

One then readily sees that the equation
\tau\not{\partial}\tau^{-1}=\not{\partial}
implies that

(5.6)

\theta^{E}\,\nabla^{E}_{\theta_{*}\xi}\,(\theta^{E})^{-1}\ =\ \nabla^{E}_{\xi}.

5.4.
A potential

Let
V:E\to E
be an odd symmetric bundle map. If
n
is even, we assume that
V
is odd with respect to the grading, i.e.

(5.7)

V\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&V^{-}\\
V^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Consider the Dirac-type operator (or
generalized Dirac operator with potential
)

(5.8)

D_{V}^{+}\ :=\ \begin{cases}\not{\partial}^{+}\ +\ V^{+},\qquad&\text{if $n$ is even}\\
\not{\partial}\ +\ V,\qquad&\text{if $n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Then
D_{V}^{+}
is odd symmetric.

We have a “+” in the notation for the Dirac-type operator in the odd-dimensional case to be able to study both even and odd-dimensional cases simultaneously. To this end, if
n
is odd, we set
D_{V}^{-}:=(D_{V}^{+})^{*}
and consider the graded operator

D_{V}\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D_{V}^{-}\\
D_{V}^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&\not{\partial}+V^{*}\\
\not{\partial}+V&0\end{pmatrix}:\,\Gamma(M,E\oplus E)\ \to\ \Gamma(M,E\oplus E).

5.5.
The
\tau
-index of an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator

Suppose now that
D_{V}
is Fredholm. In particular, this is always the case when
M
is compact. Then the
\tau
-index
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{V}^{+}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
is defined by (
2.2
).

If
M
is compact, then it follows from Lemma
3.4
, that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{V}^{+}=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\not{\partial}^{+}
. But in this section, we will be mostly interested in the non-compact case. However, the following analog of the classical result for the usual
\mathbb{Z}
-valued index will play a role:

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose
M
is a compact involutive manifold of odd dimension and let
D_{V}^{+}=\not{\partial}+V
be an odd symmetric Dirac-type operator on a quaternionic bundle
(E,\theta^{E})
over
M
. Then
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{V}^{+}=0
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\not{\partial}=0
. Since
\not{\partial}
is odd symmetric and self-adjoint, it commutes with
\tau
. It follows that
\tau
acts on
\ker\not{\partial}
. Hence, by Lemma
2.2
, the dimension of the kernel of
\not{\partial}
is even. Thus

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}D_{V}^{+}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\not{\partial}\ \equiv\ \dim\ker\not{\partial}\ =\ 0\ \in\ \mathbb{Z}_{2}.

∎

Next, we consider several examples of the Dirac-type operators with non-trivial
\tau
-index.

5.6.
A ungraded Callias-type operator of Dirac-type

Let
(M,\theta)
be a complete involutive manifold
of odd dimension

n
. Let
(E,\theta^{E})
be a quaternionic Dirac bundle over
(M,\theta)
and let
\not{\partial}
be the corresponding generalized Dirac operator. We assume that
\not{\partial}
is odd symmetric. Let
V:E\to E
be an odd symmetric bundle map and consider the odd symmetric Dirac-type operator

(5.9)

D^{+}_{V}\ =\ \not{\partial}\ +\ V:\,\Gamma(M,E)\to\Gamma(M,E).

As before, we set
D^{-}_{V}:=(D^{+}_{V})^{*}
and consider the operator
D_{V}=\begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}_{V}\\
D^{+}_{V}&0\end{pmatrix}
.

Let
\Phi:E\to E
be a self-adjoint odd symmetric bundle map. Then the operator

B_{\Phi}\ :=\ D_{V}^{+}\ +\ i\Phi

is odd symmetric. To connect it to the graded Callias-type operator of Section
4.1
, we define

F\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&F^{-}\\
F^{+}&0\end{pmatrix}\ :=\ \begin{pmatrix}0&-i\Phi\\
i\Phi&0\end{pmatrix}

and set

\mathcal{B}_{F}\ :=\ D_{V}\ +\ F\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&B_{\Phi}^{*}\\
B_{\Phi}&0\end{pmatrix}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&D^{-}_{V}-i\Phi\\
D^{+}_{V}+i\Phi&0\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\mathcal{B}_{F}
is a graded odd symmetric operator acting on
\Gamma(M,E\oplus E)
. As usual, we set
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{+}:=B_{\Phi}
,
\mathcal{B}_{F}^{-}:=B_{\Phi}^{*}
.

Definition 5.2
.

We say that
B_{\Phi}=D^{+}_{V}+i\Phi
is an
ungraded Callias-type operator of Dirac type
if
\mathcal{B}_{F}
is a Callias-type operator in the sense of Definition
4.3
.

The admissibility conditions of Definition
4.2
become, in this case,

(i)

the restriction of the commutator
[D_{V}^{+},\Phi]\ :=\ D_{V}^{+}\,\Phi\ -\ \Phi\,D_{V}^{+}
to
M\setminus K
is an operator of order
0
;

(ii)

there exist a constant
c>0
such that

(5.10)

\Phi(x)^{2}\ \geq\ \big\|[D_{V}^{+},\Phi](x)\big\|\ +\ c,\qquad\text{for all}\quad x\in M\setminus K.

The compact set
K
is called an
essential support of
\Phi
.

Note that Condition (i) is equivalent to

(5.11)

[c(\xi),\Phi]\ =\ 0,\qquad\text{for all}\quad\xi\in T_{x}M,\ x\in M\setminus K.

In this section, we study the
\tau
-index of the operator
B_{\Phi}=B_{F}^{+}
.

5.7.
The
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued Callias-type theorem

Suppose that there is a
\theta
-invariant partition

M=M_{-}\cup_{N}M_{+}
, where
N=M_{-}\cap M_{+}
is a smooth compact hypersurface and
M_{-}
is a
compact
submanifold, whose interior contains an essential support
K
of
F
, cf. Figure
2
. The
\theta
-invariance of the partition meas that
M_{\pm}
are
\theta
-invariant submanifolds of
M
. Then
N

is also
\theta
-invariant.

Figure 2
.
A
\theta
-invariant partition of
M

We denote by
n
the unit normal vector field to
N
pointing in the direction of
M_{+}
, cf. Figure
2
. Since
M_{-}
is compact and
M_{+}
is not compact,
\theta:M_{\pm}\to M_{\pm}
. It follows that
\theta_{*}n=n
. Hence, (
5.5
) implies that
c(n)

\theta^{E}\,c\big(n(x)\big)\,(\theta^{E})^{-1}\ =\ c\big(n(\theta x)\big).

or, equivalently,

(5.12)

\tau\,c(n)\,\tau^{-1}\ =\ c(n).

Let
E_{N}
be the restriction of
E
to
N\subset M
. Since
N
is
\theta
-invariant submanifold,
E_{N}
is a
\theta^{E}
-invariant and, hence, is a quaternionic bundle over
(N,\theta)
.

By Condition (ii) of Definition
4.2
, zero is not in the spectrum of
\Phi(x)
for all
x\in N
. Therefore we have a direct sum decomposition

(5.13)

E_{N}=E_{N+}\oplus E_{N-},

where the fiber of
E_{N+}
(resp.
E_{N-}
) over

is the image of the spectral projection of
\Phi(x)
corresponding to the interval
(0,\infty)
(resp.
(-\infty,0)
).
By (
5.11
) the endomorphism
\Phi
commutes with the Clifford multiplication. Hence
c(\xi):E_{N\pm}\to E_{N\pm}
for all
\xi\in TM
. It follows that both bundles,
E_{N+}
and
E_{N-}
, inherit the Clifford action of
TM
. Also, since
\Phi
commutes with
\tau
, both bundles
E_{N+}
and
E_{N-}
are quaternionic bundles over
N
.

Recall that we denote by
n
the unit normal vector field pointing at the direction of
M_{+}
. The operator
c(n)
defines an endomorphism
\gamma:E_{N\pm}\to E_{N\pm}
.
Since
\gamma^{2}=-1
, the endomorphism
\alpha:=-i\gamma
is an involution and induces a grading

(5.14)

E_{N\pm}\ =\ E_{N\pm}^{+}\oplus E_{N\pm}^{-},

where
E_{N\pm}^{\pm}\subset E_{N\pm}
is the span of the eigenvectors of
\alpha
with eigenvalues
\pm 1
. The Clifford action of
TN
on
E_{N\pm}
is graded with respect to this grading, i.e.
c(\xi):E_{N\pm}^{\pm}\to E_{N\pm}^{\mp}
for all
\xi\in TN
. By (
5.12
),
\tau
is odd with respect to this grading (recall that
\tau
is anti-linear and, hence, anti-commutes with
\alpha=-ic(n)
). Hence,
E_{N\pm}
are graded quaternionic vector bundles over
N
.

Let
\nabla^{E_{N}}
be the connection on
E_{N}
obtained by restricting the connection on
E
. It does not, in general, preserve decomposition (
5.13
).
We define a connection
\nabla^{E_{N\pm}}
on the bundle
E_{N\pm}
by

(5.15)

\nabla^{E_{N\pm}}_{\xi}s^{\pm}\ =\ \operatorname{pr}_{E_{N\pm}}\left(\nabla^{E_{N}}_{\xi}s^{\pm}\right),\qquad s^{\pm}\in C^{\infty}(N,E_{N\pm}),\ \xi\in TN,

where
\operatorname{pr}_{E_{N\pm}}
is the projection onto the bundle
E_{N\pm}
. Since
\gamma
commutes with both, the connection
\nabla^{E_{N}}
and the projection
\operatorname{pr}_{E_{N\pm}}

\nabla^{E_{N\pm}}
.
By
[
1

, it is a Hermitian Clifford connection. We denote by
\not{\partial}_{N}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&\not{\partial}_{N-}\\
\not{\partial}_{N+}&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
the Dirac operator on
N
associated with these connections:

(5.16)

\not{\partial}_{N\pm}\ :=\ \sum_{i=1}^{n-1}c(e_{i})\,\nabla^{E_{N\pm}}_{e_{i}}.

where
(e_{1},\ldots,e_{n-1})
is an orthonormal frame of
TN
. The operators
\not{\partial}_{N\pm}
are odd symmetric and are odd with respect to the grading (
5.14
), i.e. they have the form

\not{\partial}_{N\pm}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&\not{\partial}_{N\pm}^{-}\\
\not{\partial}_{N\pm}^{+}&0\end{pmatrix},

where
\not{\partial}_{N\pm}^{+}
(respectively
\not{\partial}_{N\pm}^{-}
) is the restriction of
\not{\partial}_{N\pm}
to
E_{N\pm}^{+}
(respectively
E_{N\pm}^{-}
).

The next theorem is a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the Callias index theorem,

[

, Theorem 1.5]
and
[

, Theorem 2.9]
.

Theorem 5.3

(
Callias-type theorem for odd symmetric operators
)
.

(5.17)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}B_{\Phi}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\not{\partial}_{N+}^{+}.

We prove the theorem in the next section. We finish this section with a couple of immediate corollaries.

Corollary 5.4
.

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{}B_{\Phi}=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{}\not{\partial}_{N-}^{+}
. Hence,

(5.18)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{}\not{\partial}_{N+}^{+}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{}\not{\partial}_{N-}^{-}.

Proof of Corollary
5.4
.

Applying Theorem
5.3
to the operator
B_{-\Phi}
we get

(5.19)

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}B_{-\Phi}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}{}\not{\partial}_{N-}^{+}.

Since

is odd symmetric we have

B_{-\Phi}\ =\ D_{V}^{+}-i\Phi\ =\ (D_{V}+i\Phi)^{*}\ =\ B_{\Phi}^{*}\ =\ \tau\,B_{\Phi}\,\tau^{-1}.

Hence,
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}B_{\Phi}=\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}B_{-\Phi}
and the Corollary follows from (

∎

Remark 5.5
.

At first glance, the bundles
E_{N+}
and

are not related. However, both operators,

and

If
\Phi
is an admissible endomorphism of

then so is

we obtain the following

6.
The proof of Theorem
5.3

In this section, we prove Theorem
5.3

6.1.
The plan of the proof of Theorem
5.3
.

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
5.3
. The proof is essentially the same as in the classical case, cf.
[
3
,

]
. We mostly follow the exposition in
[

and carefully check that all construction can be made

Step 1

First, we consider the case when
M=N\times\mathbb{R}
is a cylinder and assume that all the structures on
M
are products of the structures on
N
and
\mathbb{R}
. We set

as in Section

and show that (

Step 2

We consider a manifold

to prove that the

Step 3

We consider the general manifold

. We identify a small neighborhood

of the hypersurface
N
with the product

and deform all the structures in

to that considered in Step 1. The

Step 4

Finally, we apply the Relative index theorem to reduce the general case to the case of a manifold with a cylindrical end (see Figure

).

The rest of this section is occupied with the details of this proof.

6.2.
Step 1. The case of a cylinder

First, we consider the case when

7.
Cobordism invariance of the
\tau
-index

In this section, we show that the Callias-type Theorem
5.3
implies that the
\tau
-index of a Dirac-type operator on a compact even-dimensional manifold is preserved by a cobordism. To simplify the notation we only consider the case of
null-cobordism
, i.e. the cobordism between a manifold and the empty set. We prove that the
\tau
-index of a null-cobordant operator vanishes. A standard argument, cf., for example,
[
11
, Remark 2.10]
, shows that this is equivalent to the cobordism invariance of the index.

A lot of formulations and constructions of this section are borrowed from
[
11
]
and
[
9
, §7]
.

8.
A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the Boutet de Monvel index theorem

Boutet de Monvel,
[
8
]
, discovered an index theorem for a Toeplitz operator on a pseudo-convex domain. Guentner and Higson,
[
26
]
, reinterpreted it in terms of the index of a Callias-type operator. Bunke,
[
18
]
, generalized their result to Callias-type operators on general manifolds. In this section, we present a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-valued analog of the result of Bunke. The proof is practically the same, but easier, than in
[
18
]
. We present it here for completeness.

8.1.
The gap condition

Let

be a complete even-dimensional Riemannian manifold, and let

be a graded Dirac bundle over

, cf. Section
5.1
.
Let
\not{\partial}
be the associated generalized Dirac operator on

.

We make the following fundamental

Assumption 8.1

(
Gap condition
)
.

Zero is an isolated point of the spectrum of
\not{\partial}
.

Remark 8.2
.

Let
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a strongly pseudo-convex domain endowed with the Bergman metric, cf.
[
31
, §7]
. Then
M
is a complete Riemannian manifold. Let
\not{\partial}=\bar{\partial}+\bar{\partial}^{*}
be the Dolbeault-Dirac operator on the space
\Omega^{n,*}(M,\mathbb{C}^{k})
of
(n,*)
-forms on
M
with values in the trivial bundle
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

It follows from
[
22
, §5]
that
\not{\partial}
satisfies the gap condition. This relates the results of this section to the original theorem of Boutet de Monvel.

Fix an integer
k
and consider the graded bundle

E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\ =\ \big(E^{+}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\big)\oplus\big(E^{-}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}\big).

over

. The structure of a graded Dirac bundle on

naturally extends to

and the corresponding generalized Dirac operator

satisfies the gap condition
8.1
.

8.2.
The quaternionic structure

Let
\theta:M\to M
be a metric preserving involution. Let

\theta^{E}:\,E\ \to\ \theta^{*}E

be an anti-linear map that preserves the Hermitian metric and such that
(\theta^{E})^{2}=1
. Thus it is an anti-unitary involution. Note that, contrary to other parts of the paper, we consider an involution, not an anti-involution and we don’t impose any conditions on its grading degree. In particular, in Example
8.3
it will be even with respect to the grading.

Let
\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}:\mathbb{C}^{k}\to\mathbb{C}^{k}
be an anti-unitary anti-involution (By Lemma

. It makes

a graded quaternionic bundle.

An important example of this situation appears as follows. Let
M
be a pseudo-convex domain in

and let
\theta:M\to M
be an anti-holomorphic involution. As in Remark
8.2
we endow
M
with the Bergman metric and consider the bundle
E=\Lambda^{n,*}T^{*}M
of
(n,*)
-forms on
M
. We define an anti-linear
involution
on
E
by

\theta^{E}:\,\omega\ \mapsto\ \overline{\theta^{*}\omega}.

Here bar stands for the complex conjugation and
\theta^{*}
is the pull-back of differential forms, which sends
(n,*)
-forms to
(*,n)
-forms (since
\theta
is anti-linear).

One readily checks that the Dolbeault-Dirac operator
D=\not{\partial}\otimes 1
is odd-symmetric with respect to the anti-unitary anti-involution
\theta^{E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}}:=\theta^{E}\otimes\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}
.

If
M
is a unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
, this example is related to the Graf-Porta model for topological insulators with a times reversal symmetry
[
24
]
. See
[
10
]
for details.

8.4.
Matrix valued functions

Let
Mat(k)
denote the space of complex
k\times k
-matrices. Let
BC(M,k)
denote the Banach algebra of continuous bounded functions
f(x)
on
M
with values in
Mat(k)
. We define an anti-unitary anti-involution
\tau
on
BC(M,k)
by

(8.1)

(\tau f)(x)\ =\ \theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}}\,f(\theta x)\,(\theta^{\mathbb{C}^{k}})^{-1}.

Let
BC(M,k)^{\tau}
denote the set of
\tau
-invariant elements of
BC(M,k)
. Let
C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}
denote the closure of the subalgebra of functions with compact support in
BC(M,k)^{\tau}
.

Let
C_{g}^{\infty}(M,k)^{\tau}\subset BC(M,k)^{\tau}
denote the space of smooth
\tau
-invariant functions with values in
Mat(k)
which are bounded and such that
df
vanishes at infinity. Then
C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}\subset C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
.

Let
C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
denote the closure of
C_{g}^{\infty}(M,k)^{\tau}
inside
BC(M,k)
. This is a commutative
C^{*}
-algebra. For
f\in C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
we denote by
[f]
its image in the quotient algebra
C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}/C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}
and write
f\in[f]
.

Definition 8.3
.

We say that
f
is
invertible at infinity
if
[f]
is invertible in
C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}/C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}
. Equivalently, this means that there exists a compact set
K\subset M
and
C>0
such that
f(x)
is an invertible and
\|f(x)^{-1}\|<C
for all
x\not\in K
.

8.5.
The Toeplitz operator

We now assume that the generalized Dirac operator

D\ :=\ \not{\partial}\otimes 1:\,\Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\ \to\ \Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}).

is odd symmetric and that
\not{\partial}
and, hence,
D
satisfy the gap condition, cf. Assumption
8.1
.

Let
{H}:=\ker D\subset L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
and let
P:L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to{H}
denote the orthogonal projection.

For
f\in C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
we denote by
M_{f}:\Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to\Gamma(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
the operator of multiplication by
f
. Then,
M_{f}
is an odd symmetric operator. We write
M_{f}^{\pm}
for the restriction of
M_{f}
to
E^{\pm}
.

Definition 8.4
.

Let
f\in C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
, the Toeplitz operator is the operator

T_{f}\ :=\ P\,M_{f}\,P:\,{H}\ \to\ {H}.

This is a bounded odd symmetric operator.

8.6.
The Fredholmness

By
[
18
, Lemma 2.6]
, if
D
satisfies Assumption
8.1
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
is invertible at infinity then
T_{f}
is Fredholm. The goal of this section is to compute
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}T_{f}
.

Lemma 8.5
.

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}T_{f}
depends only on the class
[f]\in C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}/C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}
.

Proof.

Let
f_{1}\in[f]
. Then
f_{1}-f\in C_{0}(M,k)^{\tau}
.

Let
W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
denote the domain of the closure of operator
D
, viewed as a Hilbert space. Then
D:W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})\to L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
is a bounded operator and
{H}\subset W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
.
The lemma follows now from Corollary
2.6
.
∎

8.7.
The Callias-type operator

Let
f\in C_{g}(M,k)^{\tau}
be invertible at infinity. Consider the ungraded odd symmetric operator

\mathcal{C}\ :=\ D\ +\ i\,M_{f}:\,\Gamma(M,E)\ \to\Gamma(M,E).

Lemma 8.6
.

The potential
M_{f}
satisfies the ungraded admissibility condition of Definition
5.2
. Hence,
\mathcal{C}
is a Callias-type operator. In particular, it is Fredholm.

Proof.

It follows from the Leibniz rule (
5.1
) that the commutator

[D,M_{f}]\ =\ c(df).

Thus, condition (i) of Definition
5.2
holds.

Since
F
is invertible at infinity and
df
vanishes at infinity, there exists a constant
c
and a compact set
K\subset M
such that
f^{2}(x)>2c
and
\|df\|<c
. This implies condition (ii) of Definition
5.2
.
∎

The main result of this section is the following analog of Proposition 2.6 of
[
18
]
.

Theorem 8.7
.

\displaystyle\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{C}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}T_{f}
.

Proof.

Recall that
P:L^{2}(M,E)\to{H}
denotes the orthogonal projection. Set
Q:=1-P
. The image of
Q
is the orthogonal complement
{H}^{\perp}
of
{H}
.
With respect to decomposition
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}={H}\oplus{H}^{\perp}
we have

\mathcal{C}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}P\mathcal{C}P&P\mathcal{C}Q\\
Q\mathcal{C}P&Q\mathcal{C}Q\end{pmatrix}

Recall that we denote by
W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
the domain of the closure of
D
.
By Lemma 2.4 of
[
18
]
the operators
PM_{f}Q
and
QM_{f}P
are compact as operators from
W^{1,2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
to
L^{2}(M,E\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k})
. All these operators are odd symmetric since
P
is odd symmetric. Hence, by Corollary
2.6
,

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{C}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\begin{pmatrix}P\mathcal{C}P&0\\
0&Q\mathcal{C}Q\end{pmatrix}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P\mathcal{C}P\ +\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}Q\mathcal{C}Q.

Consider the family
\mathcal{C}_{t}:=D+itM_{f}
(
t\in[0,1]
. For all
t>0
, the operator
\mathcal{C}_{t}
is of Callias type and, hence, Fredholm. So
Q\mathcal{C}_{t}Q
is also Fredholm. The operator
Q\mathcal{C}_{0}Q=D\big|_{(\ker D)^{\perp}}
is invertible. Thus, we have a continuous family
Q\mathcal{C}_{t}Q
of operators connecting
Q\mathcal{C}Q
with an invertible operator. It follows that
\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}Q\mathcal{C}Q=0
.

Finally,
P\mathcal{C}P=T_{f}
. Hence,

\operatorname{ind}_{\tau}\mathcal{C}\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}P\mathcal{C}P\ =\ \operatorname{ind}_{\tau}T_{f}.

∎

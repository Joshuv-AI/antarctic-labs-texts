Extremal edge general position sets in some graphs
Jing Tian, Sandi Klavžar, Elif Tan

Abstract
A set of edges X ⊆ E ⁡ ( G ) X\subseteq E(G) of a graph G G is an edge general position set if no three edges from X X lie on a common shortest path. The edge general position number gp e ​ ( G ) {\rm gp}_{\rm e}(G) of G G is the cardinality of a largest edge general position set in G G . Graphs G G with gp e ​ ( G ) = | E ⁡ ( G ) | − 1 {\rm gp}_{\rm e}(G)=|E(G)|-1 and with gp e ​ ( G ) = 3 {\rm gp}_{\rm e}(G)=3 are respectively characterized. Sharp upper and lower bounds on gp e ​ ( G ) {\rm gp}_{\rm e}(G) are proved for block graphs G G and exact values are determined for several specific block graphs.



Keywords:
general position set; edge general position set; cut-vertex; diametral path; block graph

Let

be a graph. Then we say that

is an

P

The main results of the seminal paper
[
10

are the formulas for the edge general position number of hypercubes and of Cartesian grids. As a point of interest, we would like to add that the general position number of hypercubes is a notorious difficult problem, cf.
[

Theorem 2.2

Let
G
be a (connected) graph with
n(G)\geq 4
. Then
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=m(G)-1
if and only if
G\in{\cal G}_{1}\cup{\cal G}_{2}
.

Proof.
Assume first that
G\in{\cal G}_{1}\cup{\cal G}_{2}
. Then
\diam(G)=3
, and hence
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\leq m(G)-1
. If
G\in{\cal G}_{1}
, then it is clear that
E(G)\setminus\{e\}
is an edge general position set of
G
, where
e
is the special edge of
G
. Let next
G\in{\cal G}_{2}
. Then we claim that
E(G)\setminus\{x_{1}x_{2}\}
is an edge general position set of
G
, where
x_{1}x_{2}
is the central edge of
G
. Indeed, this follows by the fact that every shortest path of
G
of length
3
passes through
x_{1}x_{2}
. In both cases we have
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq m(G)-1
and we may conclude that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=m(G)-1
.

Conversely, assume that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=m(G)-1
.

Claim 1
:
\diam(G)=3
.

By Lemma
2.1
we have
\diam(G)\geq 3
. Moreover, if
\diam(G)\geq 4
, then at least two edges of an arbitrary diametral path of
G
do not lie in an arbitrary edge general position set, hence
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\leq m(G)-2
. We conclude that
\diam(G)=3
.

Claim 2
: There exists an edge
e\in E(G)
which lies on every diametral path of
G
.

Let
R_{1},\ldots,R_{\ell}
be the diametral paths of
G
. If
V(R_{i})\cap V(R_{j})=\emptyset
, then every edge general position set of
G
misses at least one edge of
R_{i}
and at least one edge of
R_{j}
. Hence each two diametral paths share at least one edge.

Consider now arbitrary three diametral paths
R_{i}
,
R_{j}
and
R_{k}
. We claim that they have at least one common edge. Suppose on the contrary that this is not the case and let
X
be a largest edge general position set of
G
. Assume that
E(R_{i})\cap E(R_{j})=\{e\}
,
E(R_{i})\cap E(R_{k})=\{f\}
, and
E(R_{j})\cap E(R_{k})=\{g\}
. If
|\{e,f,g\}|\leq 2
, where we without loss of generality have
f=g
, then the edge
f
lies on all three paths. Hence we must have
|\{e,f,g\}|=3
. Recall that at least one edge of
R_{i}
(as well as of
R_{j}
and of
R_{k}
) is not in
X
. If this edge is
e
, then at least one additional edge of
R_{j}
is also not in
X
. Similarly, if
f\notin X
, then at least one additional edge of
R_{k}
is also not in
X
. Finally, if the unique edge from
V(R_{i})\setminus\{f,g\}
is not in
X
, then on each of
R_{j}
and
R_{k}
one additional edge is not in
X
. Hence in all the cases we have
\gp_{\rm e}(G)<m(G)-1
, a contradiction. We conclude that
E(R_{i})\cap E(R_{j})\cap E(R_{k})\neq\emptyset
.

Let
e\in E(R_{i})\cap E(R_{j})\cap E(R_{k})
and let
R_{k^{\prime}}
be an arbitrary additional diametral path. We are going to show that all these four paths have a common edge. If
e\in E(R_{k^{\prime}})
, there is nothing to prove. Assume hence that
e\notin E(R_{k^{\prime}})
. We now consider two cases. If
e\in X
, then on each of the paths
R_{i}
,
R_{j}
,
R_{k}
and
R_{k^{\prime}}
there lies at least one edge which is not in
X
. If this is the same edge, we are done. Otherwise there exist two different edges not in
X
, which is not possible as we have assumed that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=m(G)-1
. In the second case
e\notin X
. Since
e\notin E(R_{k^{\prime}})
, the path
R_{k^{\prime}}
contains an edge different from
e
not in
X
, which is again not possible. By induction we now conclude the truth of Claim 2.

By Claim 2,
G
has an edge
e=uv
which lies on all diametral paths of
G
.

Assume first that
e
is the first edge of some diametral path with the first vertex of it being
u
. Then
e
must be the first edge of every diametral path, for otherwise we would have
\diam(G)\geq 4
or there would exist another diametral path which would not contain
e
. Moreover, by the same reason we see that
\deg_{G}(u)=1
. Let
H=G-u
. Then
\diam(H)=2
and hence, since
\diam(G)=3
, it follows that
\deg_{H}(v)\leq n(H)-2
. We conclude that
G\in{\cal G}_{1}
.

Assume second that
e=uv
is the middle edge of some diametral path
P
, let
P=xuvy
. We claim that
u
is a cut-vertex of
G
. Suppose this is not the case and let
Q
be a shortest
x,y
-path in
G-u
. Since
\diam(G)=3
and
Q
does not contain the edge
uv
, its length is at least
4
. Consider the subpath
Q^{\prime}
of
Q
induced by its last four vertices, say
y_{1}
,
y_{2}
,
y_{3}
, and
y_{4}=y
. It is possible that
y_{3}=v
. We claim that
Q^{\prime}
is a shortest path also in
G
. Since we have assumed that
Q^{\prime}
is shortest in
G-u
, the only way
Q^{\prime}
is not shortest in
G
would be that there exists a shortest path in
G
between
y_{1}
and
y_{4}=y
using the vertex
u
. But this would mean that
uy\in E(G)
, which is not possible as
P
is a diametral path. Hence
Q^{\prime}
is a shortest path in
G
of length
3
which does not contain
uv
. This contradiction proves the claim, that is,
u
is a cut-vertex. By symmetry of
P
we also deduce that
v
is a cut-vertex.

Consider now the block
B
of
G
containing the vertices
u
and
v
. Let
w\neq u,v
be an arbitrary vertex from
B
. We first show that at least one of the edges
wu
and

wv
exist. Suppose not. Then assume without loss of generality that
d_{G}(w,u)\leq d_{G}(w,v)
. If
P
is a shortest
w,u
-path, then this path (which must be of length
2
) together with an arbitrary edge between
u
and a vertex not in
B
is a diametral path which does not pass
uv
, a contradiction. Suppose next that
wu\in E(G)
but
wv\notin E(G)
. Since
B
is a block and
wv\notin E(G)
, there is another
w,v
-path in
B
which does not pass
u
. Assuming that this is a shortest possible such path, the last two edges of it together with the edge between
v
and a vertex adjacent to
v
not from
B
again gives a diametral path not containing
uv
. It follows that each of
u
and
v
is adjacent to every vertex of
B
. Then
B\setminus\{u,v\}
is the graph
G_{0}
required to show that
G\in{\cal G}_{2}
. Moreover, let
G_{1}
be the graph induced by the vertices of
G
which are not in
B
and are closer to
u
than to
v
. Then
u
must be adjacent to each vertex of
G_{1}
because otherwise we would have
\diam(G)\geq 4
. Analogously we can define
G_{2}
with respect to
v
and conclude that
v
must be adjacent to each vertex of
G_{2}
. We conclude that
G\in{\cal G}_{2}
.

\square

In the second part of this section we consider graphs with small edge general position number.

From Proposition
1.1
(ii) we get that if

\gp_{\rm e}(T)=2
if and only if

G
is not a tree, then considering a shortest cycle in

which is necessarily isometric, we infer that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq 3
. Hence we have the following easy result.

Proposition 2.3

If
G
is a graph with
m(G)\geq 2
, then
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=2
if and only if
G
is a path.

We further have:

Proposition 2.4

Let
G
be a graph. Then
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=3
if and only if
G
is
K_{3}
or a tree with three leaves.

Proof.
Clearly,
\gp_{\rm e}(K_{3})=3
. If
G
is a tree with three leaves, then
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=3
by Proposition
1.1
(ii).

Conversely, assume that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=3
. If
G
is a tree, then the conclusion follows from Proposition
1.1
(ii). Hence assume in the rest that
G
is not a tree. Let
C
be a shortest cycle of
G
. If
n(C)\geq 4
, then since
C
is isometric, Proposition
1.1
(i) implies that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq 4
. Hence
C
must be a triangle. Let
V(C)=\{x,y,z\}
. If
n(G)\geq 4
, then we may without loss of generality assume that
\deg_{G}(x)\geq 3
. Let
x^{\prime}
be adjacent to
x
, where
x^{\prime}\not\in\{y,z\}
. Then
\{xy,yz,xz,xx^{\prime}\}
is an edge general position set of
G
. We conclude that
G=K_{3}
.

\square

For graphs
G
with
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=4
we have the following.

Proposition 2.5

If
G
is a graph with
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=4
, then the following holds.

(i)

\Delta(G)\leq 4
.

(ii)

If
\Delta(G)=4
, then

We know from (
1
) that (i) holds. To prove (ii), consider an arbitrary vertex

of

. We conclude that the distance levels of

Edge general position sets in block graphs

While investigating in the previous section the graphs with extremal edge general position number, it turned out that cut-vertices are ubiquitous. In this section we thus consider the edge general position number in block graphs. To state our results, some preparation is needed.

We say that a block
B
of a graph
G
is
thick
if
n(B)\geq 3
and that
B
is
pendant
if it contains exactly one cut-vertex of
G
.
A graph is a
block graph
if every block of it is complete. (See
[
1
,
2
,
8
]
for some current research on block graphs.)

A vertex of a graph is
simplicial
if its neighbourhood induces a complete subgraph. The set of simplicial vertices in a graph
G
will be denoted by
S(G)
and the cardinality of
S(G)
by
s(G)
.
A block
B
of a block graph is
simplicial
if
B
has at least one simplicial vertex.
An edge of a block graph is a
simplicial edge
if it is incident with at least one simplicial vertex. The set of simplicial edges of
G
will be denoted by
S^{\prime}(G)
and its cardinality by
s^{\prime}(G)
.

Let
G
be a block graph and let
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
be its simplicial blocks. Set
b_{i}=n(B_{i})
and
s_{i}=|S(G)\cap V(B_{i})|
for
i\in[k]
. Since an edge of
B_{i}
is not simplicial if and only if its both endvertices are not simplicial, we infer that

s^{\prime}(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\left[\binom{b_{i}}{2}-\binom{b_{i}-s_{i}}{2}\right].

(2)

Let
G
be a graph and let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}
be the consecutive vertices of a path
P
in
G
, where
k\geq 2
. We say that
P
is an
internal path
of
G
if
\deg_{G}(p_{1})\geq 3
,
\deg_{G}(p_{k})\geq 3
, and
\deg_{G}(p_{i})=2
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
. We say that
P
is a
pendant path
of
G
if
\deg_{G}(p_{1})\geq 3
,
\deg_{G}(p_{k})=1
, and
\deg_{G}(p_{i})=2
for
2\leq i\leq k-1
.

Let
G
be a block graph. Then the
reduction

R(G)
of
G
is constructed from
G
as follows. For every internal path
P
of
G
, identify the end vertices of
P
(and remove all the inner vertices of
P
). Moreover, replace every pendant path
P
by a pendant edge attached to
R(G)
in the same vertex as
P
is attached to
G
. Note that if
G
is a path, then
R(G)=G
.

Theorem 3.1

If
G
is block graph, then
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=\gp_{\rm e}(R(G))
.

Proof.
It is clear that an edge general position set of
R(G)
yields an edge general position set of
G
of the same cardinality. It follows that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq\gp_{\rm e}(R(G))
. In the rest we thus need to prove the reverse inequality.

If
G
is a path, then
R(G)=G
and there is nothing to prove. Assume in the following that
G
is a block graph which is not a path. Then
\deg_{G}(v)\geq 3
for some
v\in V(G)
. It follows that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq 3
.
Let
X
be an arbitrary
\gp_{\rm e}
-set of
G
.
Let
{\cal P}
be the set consisting of all internal and pendant paths of
G
. If
P\in{\cal P}
, then
P
is isometric subgraph of
G
and hence
|X\cap E(P)|\leq 2
. We hence distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1.

|X\cap E(P)|\leq 1
for any
P\in{\cal P}
.

It is obvious that
X
is also an edge general position set of
R(G)
if
|X\cap E(P)|=0
for any path
P\in{\cal P}
. Next, assume that
|X\cap E(P^{\prime})|=1
for some path
P^{\prime}\in{\cal P}
and let
e\in X\cap E(P^{\prime})
. If
P^{\prime}
is a pendant path, then
e
can be replaced by the pendant edge of
R(G)
corresponding to
P^{\prime}
, which keeps the property of being in edge general position. If
P^{\prime}
is an internal path, then let
u
and
v
be the end-vertices of
P^{\prime}
. Let
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
be the respective neigbors of
u
and
v
which do not lie on
P^{\prime}
. Then at most one of the edges
uu^{\prime}
and
vv^{\prime}
lie in
X
. Assume without loss of generality that
uu^{\prime}\notin X
. Then
(X\setminus\{e\})\cup\{uu^{\prime}\}
is also an edge general position set. Repeating this procedure we end up with an edge general position set
X^{\prime}
of
G
in which no edge from the paths from
{\cal P}
lies in
X^{\prime}
. Since this set is also an edge general position set of
R(G)
we conclude that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\leq\gp_{\rm e}(R(G))
.

Case 2.

|X\cap E(P)|=2
for some path
P\in{\cal P}
.

Let
e,e^{\prime}\in X\cap E(P)
. Let
e=xy
and
e^{\prime}=x^{\prime}y^{\prime}
.
Since
G
is not a path, there exists a simplicial vertex
v
such that
v\notin V(P)
. Let
v^{\prime}
be the neighbor of
v
such that the edges
e
,
e^{\prime}
, and
vv^{\prime}
lie on a common shortest path. Since
e,e^{\prime}\in X
, we clearly have
vv^{\prime}\notin X
. Now the set
X^{\prime}=(X\setminus\{e\})\cup\{vv^{\prime}\}
is an edge general position set of
G
. If
vv^{\prime}
is a pendant edge of a pendant path
P^{\prime}
of
G
, then
E(P^{\prime})\cap X=\emptyset
, for otherwise an edge from
E(P^{\prime})\cap X
would lie on a shortest path together with
e
and
e^{\prime}
. It follows that when
X^{\prime}
is constructed, the number of pendant paths which contain two edges from an edge general position set is reduced by one. The same holds if
P
is an internal path. Hence after repeating this procedure for every path from
{\cal P}
with two edges from
X
we end up with an edge general position set
X^{\prime\prime}
of
G
such that
|X^{\prime\prime}\cap E(P)|\leq 1
holds for any
P\in{\cal P}
. Now we can proceed as in Case 1.

\square

Next we prove general bounds for block graphs.

Theorem 3.2

If
G
is a block graph, then
s^{\prime}(G)\leq\gp_{\rm e}(G)\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1
.
Moreover, the bounds are sharp.

Proof.
Let
c_{i}
be the number of cut-vertices from
B_{i}
in
G
and let
E_{i}
be the set of simplicial edges from
B_{i}
for
i\in[k]
.
Set
X=\bigcup\limits_{i=1}^{k}E_{i}
. Recall that
s^{\prime}(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\big[\binom{b_{i}}{2}-\binom{b_{i}-s_{i}}{2}\big]
. We claim that
X
is an edge general position set of
G
.

Consider an arbitrary shortest
u,u^{\prime}
-path
P
from
G
and let
u=u_{0},\ldots,u_{\ell}=u^{\prime}
be its consecutive vertices. Clearly,
P
does not contain three edges of
X
if
\ell\leq 2
. Hence we may assume that
\ell\geq 3
. Since
G
is a block graph, the shortest path
P
is the unique shortest
u,u^{\prime}
-path. If
u\in S(G)
, then it follows that
|E(P)\cap X|\leq 2
. Hence
X
is an edge general position set of
G
.
Similarly, we also get the same result if
u^{\prime}\in S(G)
.
If
u,u^{\prime}\not\in S(G)
, then
|E(P)\cap X|=0
and
X
also is an edge general position set of
G
.
In consequence, we conclude that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq s^{\prime}(G)
.

To prove the upper bound we use induction on
t
, the number of blocks of
G
. If
t=1
, then
G
is a complete graph, then hence
\gp_{\rm e}(G)=\binom{s(G)}{2}
and the assertion holds.

Let now
t\geq 2
and let
B
be a pendant block of
G
. Let
G^{\prime}
be the block graph obtained from
G
by removing the block
B
but keeping the corresponding cut-vertex. Let
R
be an edge general position set of
G
of order
\gp_{\rm e}(G)
and let
R^{\prime}
be the restriction of
R
to
G^{\prime}
. By the induction hypothesis,
\gp_{\rm e}(G^{\prime})\leq\binom{s(G^{\prime})}{2}+1
.
Let
v
be the vertex connecting
G^{\prime}
with
B
in
G
and let
s(B)
be the set of simplicial vertices of
B
in
G
. Then
s(B)=n(B)-1
.
We distinguish the following two cases.

Case 1.

v
is not simplicial in
G^{\prime}
.

In this case
s(G)=s(G^{\prime})+s(B)
and hence
s(G^{\prime})=s(G)+1-n(B)
. Since
n(B)<s(G)
, we can now estimate as follows:

\displaystyle|R|

\displaystyle\leq|R^{\prime}|+|E(B)|

\displaystyle\leq\binom{s(G^{\prime})}{2}+1+|E(B)|

\displaystyle=\binom{s(G)+1-n(B)}{2}+1+\binom{n(B)}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s(G)+1-n(B))(s(G)-n(B))}{2}+\frac{n(B)(n(B)-1)}{2}+1

\displaystyle=\binom{s(G)}{2}+1+(n(B)-s(G))(n(B)-1)

\displaystyle\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1\,.

Case 2.

v
is simplicial in
G^{\prime}
.

In this case
s(G)=s(G^{\prime})-1+s(B)
, which means that
s(G^{\prime})=s(G)-n(B)+2
.
If
n(B)\geq 3
, then

\displaystyle|R|

\displaystyle\leq|R^{\prime}|+|E(B)|

\displaystyle=\binom{s(G^{\prime})}{2}+1+|E(B)|

\displaystyle=\binom{s(G)+2-n(B)}{2}+1+\binom{n(B)}{2}

\displaystyle=\binom{s(G)}{2}+1+(s(G)-n(B))(2-n(B))+1\,.

Since
n(B)\geq 3
, we have
2-n(B)<0
. Moreover, because
s(G)-n(B)\geq 0
, we can continue the above estimation with
|R|<\binom{s(G)}{2}+2-1
, hence
|R|\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1
.

If
n(B)=2
, it follows that
s(G)=s(G^{\prime})
.
Assume, without loss of generality, that
V(B)=\{v,v^{\prime}\}
.
If further
vv^{\prime}\not\in R
, then it is obvious that
|R|=|R^{\prime}|\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1
.
Hence we may assume that
vv^{\prime}\in R
.
If
k=2
, we see that
s(G)=n(G)-1
and we can conclude that
|R|\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1
.
If
k\geq 3
, let
v^{\prime\prime}
be a neighbor of
v
in
G^{\prime}
.
Then there exists one shortest
v,x
-path
P
in
G^{\prime}
such that
|E(P)\cap R^{\prime}|=2
.
Otherwise
R^{\prime}\cup\{vv^{\prime\prime}\}
is an edge general position set of
G^{\prime}
contradicting the maximality of
R^{\prime}
in
G^{\prime}
.
Hence we have
|R|\leq|R^{\prime}|\leq\binom{s(G)}{2}+1
, completing the proof of the upper bound.

The lower bound is sharp on trees. Indeed, if
T
is a tree, then
\gp_{\rm e}(T)
is the number of leaves of
T
, and this is equal to
s^{\prime}(T)
. To show that the upper bound is sharp, consider a graph
G
obtained from the complete graph
K_{n}
,
n\geq 2
, by attaching selecting
k\in[n]
vertices of
K_{n}
and add a separate pendant path (of arbitrary length) to each of the selected vertices. (See Fig.
3
, where the graph
G^{\prime\prime}
is such a graph.) Then we infer that
s(G)=n
. Moreover, the edges of
K_{n}
together with an arbitrary additional edge of
G
form a largest edge general position set of
G
. Indeed, this set is clearly an edge general position set. To see that it is largest, we can argue that if an edge general position set
X
of
G
contains at least two edges from
E(G)\setminus E(K_{n})
, then all but one of these edges can be replaced by a unique edge from
E(K_{n})\setminus X
keeping the property of being an edge general position set.

\square

We follow with a large class of block graphs which attain the lower bound of Theorem

. It is defined as follows. We say that a graph
G
is a
thick-leaved tree
if
G
is obtained from a tree
T
by a sequence of the following operations. Let
v
be a leaf of
T
and let
v^{\prime}
be its unique neighbor. Then replace the vertex
v
by a complete graph
K
, and replace the edge
v^{\prime}v
by an edge between
v^{\prime}
and one vertex of
K
. See Fig.

Proposition 3.3

If
G
is a thick-leaved tree and
B_{1},\ldots,B_{k}
are its simplicial blocks, then

\gp_{\rm e}(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n(B_{i})}{2}.

Proof.
By Theorem
3.2
, we know that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\geq s^{\prime}(G)
. Since
s^{\prime}(G)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n(B_{i})}{2}
, the lower bound follows.

To prove that
\gp_{\rm e}(G)\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}\binom{n(B_{i})}{2}
, consider an arbitrary edge general position set
X
of
G
and suppose
e=uv\in X
, where
u
and
v
are cut-vertices of
G
. Let
G_{u}
and
G_{v}
be the two components of
G-e
, where
u\in G_{u}
and
v\in G_{v}
. Then it follows that
X\cap E(G_{u})=\emptyset
or
X\cap E(G_{v})=\emptyset
. Indeed, otherwise there exist edges
e^{\prime}\in X\cap E(G_{u})
and
e^{\prime\prime}\in X\cap E(G_{v})
, but then
e^{\prime}
,
e
, and
e^{\prime\prime}
would lie on the same shortest path in
G
.
Hence we may assume, without loss of generality, that
X\cap E(G_{u})=\emptyset
.
Let
f
be an arbitrary simplicial edge of
G_{u}
. Note that
f
may be adjacent to
e
.
Then it is clear that
(X\setminus\{e\})\cup\{f\}
also is an edge general position set of
G
. Repeating this process we end up with an edge general position set of
G
which contains only simplicial edges and has the same cardinality as
X
. We conclude that
|X|\leq s^{\prime}(G)
.

\square

Abstract

For p = 3 p=3 , our polytopes are the regular toroidal maps { 3 , 6 } \{3,6\} . Introduction

An
abstract polytope

\mathcal{P}
is a partially-ordered set endowed with a rank function, satisfying certain conditions that arise naturally from a geometric setting. A

chain
of
\mathcal{P}
is a totally ordered subset of
\mathcal{P}
, and a maximal chain is called a
flag
of
\mathcal{P}
. An
automorphism
of a polytope
\mathcal{P}
is an incidence-preserving bijection from
\mathcal{P}
to itself. The group of automorphisms of
\mathcal{P}
is denoted by
\Gamma(\mathcal{P})
.
The automorphism group of a polytope always acts freely on flags, and in the case where this action is transitive as well, the polytope is said to be
regular
. The study of abstract regular polytopes has a rich history and been described comprehensively in the book by McMullen and Schulte
[
28
]
.

It is a natural question to try to describe all pairs
(\mathcal{P},G)
, where
\mathcal{P}
is a regular polytope and
G
is the automorphism group
acting transitively on the flags of
\mathcal{P}
.
The atlas
[
9
]
contains information about all regular polytopes with automorphism group of order at most 2000.
An interesting case is constituted by the pairs
(\mathcal{P},G)
with
G
simple or almost simple.
The atlas
[
24
]
contains all regular polytopes whose automorphism group
G
is an almost simple group such that
S\leq G\leq\hbox{\rm Aut}(S)
and
S
is a simple group of order less than one million,
and more striking results have been obtained for the symmetric groups
S_{n}
and alternating groups
A_{n}
. Fernandes
et al.

[
10
,
14
,
13
]
classified abstract regular polytopes of ranks
n-1
,
n-2
,
n-3
and
n-4
for
S_{n}
respectively, and
Cameron
et al.

[
5
]
showed that the highest rank of an abstract regular polytope for
A_{n}
is
\lfloor(n-1)/2\rfloor
when
n\geq 12
, which was known to be sharp by Fernandes
et al.

[
11
,
12
]
.
For a prime power
q
, more results about regular polytopes were obtained for the linear groups
\hbox{\rm PSL}(2,q)
in
[
22
]
,
\hbox{\rm PGL}(2,q)
in
[
23
]
,
\hbox{\rm PSL}(3,q)
and
\hbox{\rm PGL}(3,q)
in
[
3
]
,
\hbox{\rm PSL}(4,q)
in
[
4
]
, and for the Suzuki simple groups
\hbox{\rm Sz}(q)
in
[
20
]
. Furthermore, Connor
et al.

[
6
]
classified abstract regular polytopes for almost simple groups
G
with
\hbox{\rm PSL}(2,q)\leq G\leq\hbox{\rm P\mbox{$\Gamma$}L}(2,q)
. For more knowledge about this area, reader can refer the recent survey
[
21
]
.

Another interesting case is constituted by the pairs
(\mathcal{P},G)
with
G
solvable, in this case,
G
must have even order.
When it comes to solvable groups, except for abelian groups,
the first thing that come to mind are groups of orders
2^{n}
or
2^{n}p^{m}
, where
p
is an odd prime. Schulte and Weiss
[
29
]
talked that the order
2^{n}
and
2^{n}p^{m}
proved to be more difficult than others.

For
2
-groups, Conder
[
8
]
constructed an infinite families of regular polytopes of type
\{4,4,\cdots,4\}
of order
2^{n}
, where
n
depends on the rank.
The first author in this paper, Feng and Leemans
[
17
,
18

showed that all possible Schläfli types can be achieved for regular polytopes of order
2^{n}
for
n\geq 5
. More recently, Gomi
et al

[

.
For the groups of order

, some of small order and families of regular 3-polytopes arising from
the so-called tight regular
3
-polytopes, namely those with Schläfli type
\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
and order

[

[

constructed an infinite families of regular
3
-polytopes of order
2^{n}p
with type
\{2^{l_{1}},2^{l_{2}}p\}
where

and

. They also given a family of regular
3
-polytopes of order
3\cdot 2^{n}

\{6,6\}

.

Naturally, we wish to construct regular polytopes
\mathcal{P}
of order

.
Note that if
\mathcal{P}
has rank at least

.

has rank

3
-polytopes of order

with type

.

To state the result, we need to know some information about groups of order
p^{m}
. Let
G
be a group and
H_{p}(G)
the
subgroup of
G
generated by all the elements of order different from
p
.
H_{p}(G)
is called a

subgroup
of
G
(see
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
).
A group
G
of order
p^{m}
and nilpotency class
m-1
is said to be maximal class if
m\geq 3
. The basic
material about these groups can be found in Blackburn
[
2
]
or Huppert
[
16
, Chapter 3]
.
If
G
has an abelian maximal subgroup
A
, and
A=H_{p}(G)
, then, by
[
31
, Chapter 8, Example 8.3.3]

G=\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{r},s_{r+1},\cdots,s_{p-1},\beta\rangle\ \mbox{with}\ \mbox{the}\ \mbox{following}\ \mbox{relations}

s_{1}^{p^{e}},s_{2}^{p^{e}},\cdots,s_{r}^{p^{e}},s_{r+1}^{p^{e-1}},\cdots s_{p-1}^{p^{e-1}},\beta^{p},

[s_{i},s_{j}],\ {\rm for}\ 1\leq i,j\leq p-1,\ \mbox{and}\ s_{k+1}=[s_{k},\beta],\ {\rm for}\ 1\leq k\leq p-2,

[s_{p-1},\beta]=s_{1}^{-\tbinom{p}{1}}s_{2}^{-\tbinom{p}{2}}\cdots s_{p-2}^{-\tbinom{p}{p-2}}s_{p-1}^{-\tbinom{p}{p-1}},

where
\tbinom{l_{1}}{l_{2}}
denotes the binomial coefficient indexed by
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
, and

1\leq e,1\leq r\leq p-1,\ m=er+(e-1)(p-r-1)+1\geq 3.

Moreover, for any
x\notin A=\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1}\rangle
,
x
has order
p
.

Obviously,
G=\langle s_{1},\beta\rangle
. Define

\sigma:

s_{1}\mapsto s_{1}

and
\beta\mapsto\beta^{-1}
,

\tau:

s_{1}\mapsto s_{1}^{-1}

and
\beta\mapsto\beta
.

In this paper we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

Let
R=G\rtimes\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle
. Then
R
can be the automorphism groups of a regular
3
-polytope of order
4p^{m}
with type
\{p,2p\}
, where
m\geq 3
and
p
is an odd prime.

2
Background results

2.1
Abstract regular polytopes and String C-groups

We know that abstract regular polytopes and string C-groups are the same mathematical objects. The link between them may be found for instance in
[
28
, Chapter 2]
.
We take here the viewpoint of string C-groups because it is the easiest and most efficient one to define abstract regular polytopes.

Let
G
be a group and
S=\{\rho_{0},\cdots,\rho_{d-1}\}
a generating set of involutions of
G
.
For
I\subseteq\{0,\cdots,d-1\}
, let
G_{I}
denote the group generated by
\{\rho_{i}:i\in I\}
.
Suppose that

*

for any
i,j\in\{0,\ldots,d-1\}
with
|i-j|>1
,
\rho_{i}
and
\rho_{j}
commute (the
string
property
);

*

for any
I,J\subseteq\{0,\cdots,d-1\}
,

G_{I}\cap G_{J}=G_{I\cap J}\ \ (\mbox{the \emph{intersection property}})
.

Then the pair
(G,S)
is called a
string C-group of rank

d
and the
order
of
(G,S)
is simply the order of
G
. The
(Schläfli) type
of
(G,S)
is the ordered set
\{p_{1},\cdots,p_{d-1}\}
, where
p_{i}
is the order of
\rho_{i-1}\rho_{i}
. If
(G,S)
only satisfies the string property, then it is called a
string group generated by involutions
or
sggi
. By the intersection property,
S
is a minimal generating set of
G
. It is known that string
C
-groups are same as automorphism groups of regular polytopes
[
28
, Section 2E]
. In this paper we always assume that each
p_{i}
is at least 3, otherwise, the generated group is a direct product of two smaller groups. If that happens, the string C-group (and the corresponding abstract regular polytope) is called
degenerate
.
The following proposition is
straightforward, and for details, one may see
[
MC
]
.

Proposition 2.1

The intersection property for a string
C
-group
(G,S)
of rank
3
is equivalent to that
S
is a minimal generating set of
G
and
\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\rangle\cap\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle=\langle\rho_{1}\rangle
.

2.2
Group theory

We use standard notation for group theory, as in
[
30
]
for example.
In this subsection we briefly describe some knowledge of group theory we need.

Let
G
be a group. We define the
commutator

[x,y]
of elements
x
and
y
of
G

by
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
. The following result are elementary and so we give them without proof.

Proposition 2.2

Let
G
be a group. Then, for any
x,y,z\in G
,

(1)

[xy,z]=[x,z]^{y}[y,z]
,
[x,yz]=[x,z][x,y]^{z}
;

(2)

[x,y^{-1}]^{y}=[x,y]^{-1}
,
[x^{-1},y]^{x}=[x,y]^{-1}
;

(3)

[x^{-1},y^{-1}]^{xy}=[x,y]
.

2.3
Combinatorial Enumeration

In this subsection, we list some known results about combinatorial enumeration which be used in the sequel (see
[
1
, Chapter 1]
).

Proposition 2.3

Let
n,m,k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(1)

\tbinom{n}{m}=\tbinom{n}{n-m}
;

(2)

\tbinom{n+2}{m+1}=\tbinom{n+1}{m+1}+\tbinom{n+1}{m}
;

(3)

\tbinom{n}{m}\tbinom{m}{k}=\tbinom{n}{k}\tbinom{n-k}{m-k}
;

(4)

\sum\limits_{k=0}^{m}(-1)^{k}\tbinom{n}{k}=(-1)^{m}\tbinom{n-1}{m}
;

Furthermore, if
m<0
, then
\tbinom{n}{m}=0
.

3
The proof of Theorem 1.1

Assume
G
,
\sigma
and
\tau
as assumption above.

Let
s_{k+1}=[s_{k},\beta]
for
k=p-1
and
p
. Then

s_{p}=[s_{p-1},\beta]=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}
, and

s_{p+1}=[s_{p},\beta]=s_{p}^{-1}s_{p}^{\beta}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{-1})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{\beta})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}

=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{-1}s_{i}^{\beta})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}[s_{i},\beta]^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i+1}^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}.

In order to prove Theorem 1.1, we need to prove the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1

For any
1\leq k\leq p
, we have

(1)

s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}}=\prod\limits_{i=k}^{p+1}s_{i}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{i-k}}
;

(2)

s_{k}^{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}},&k\mbox{ is odd,}\\
(s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}})^{-1},&k\mbox{ is even.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.
(1) Since
s_{k+1}=[s_{k},\beta]
, we have
s_{k}^{\beta}=s_{k}s_{k+1}
.
Since
o(\beta)=p
and
k-1\leq p
, we have
p-k+1\geq 0
.
It follows that

s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}}=s_{k}^{\beta^{p+1-k}}=(s_{k}s_{k+1})^{\beta^{p-k}}=(s_{k}s_{k+1}^{2}s_{k+2})^{\beta^{p-k-1}}=\cdots

=s_{k}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{0}}s_{k+1}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{1}}s_{k+2}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{2}}\cdots s_{p}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{p-k}}s_{p+1}^{\tbinom{p+1-k}{p+1-k}}.

(2) By induction on
k
. For
k=1
, we have
s_{1}^{\sigma}=s_{1}
. For
k=2
, by Proposition
2.2
, we have

s_{2}^{\sigma}=[s_{1},\beta]^{\sigma}=[s_{1},\beta^{-1}]=([s_{1},\beta]^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}}=(s_{2}^{-1})^{\beta^{1-2}}.

Now this lemma is true for
k=1
and
2
. By induction and Proposition
2.2
,
if
k
is odd, then

s_{k}^{\sigma}=[s_{k-1}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}]=[(s_{k-1}^{\beta^{2-k}})^{-1},\beta^{-1}]=[s_{k-1}^{-1},\beta^{-1}]^{\beta^{2-k}}=([s_{k-1},\beta]^{\beta^{-1}s_{k-1}^{-1}})^{\beta^{2-k}}.

Notice that
A=\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1}\rangle
is an abelian maximal subgroup of
\mathcal{G}
and hence
A\unlhd\mathcal{G}
.
It follows that
s_{k}\in A
and
s_{k}^{\beta}\in A
for any
k\geq 1
. Thus,

s_{k}^{\sigma}=([s_{k-1},\beta]^{\beta^{-1}s_{k-1}^{-1}})^{\beta^{2-k}}=s_{k}^{(s_{k-1}^{-1})^{\beta}\beta^{1-k}}=s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}}.

If
k
is even, then

s_{k}^{\sigma}=[s_{k-1}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}]=[s_{k-1}^{\beta^{2-k}},\beta^{-1}]=[s_{k-1},\beta^{-1}]^{\beta^{2-k}}=(([s_{k-1},\beta]^{-1})^{\beta^{-1}})^{\beta^{2-k}}=(s_{k}^{\beta^{1-k}})^{-1}.

Lemma 3.2

\sigma,\tau\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
, and
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Proof.
Obviously,
\sigma^{2}=\tau^{2}=1
and
\sigma\tau=\tau\sigma
. Then
\langle\sigma,\tau\rangle\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. In the following we prove

\sigma,\tau\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
.

(1) We prove that
\sigma\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
. Obviously,
\langle s_{1}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}\rangle=\langle s_{1},\beta^{-1}\rangle=G
. Thus, we only need to show that the generating set
\{s_{1}^{\sigma},s_{2}^{\sigma},\cdots,s_{p-1}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}\}

of
G
satisfy
the same relations as
\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1},\beta\}
.

Recall that
A=\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1}\rangle
is abelian and
A\unlhd G
. By lemma
3.1
(2),

s_{i}^{\sigma}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}s_{i}^{\beta^{1-i}},&i\mbox{ is odd,}\\
(s_{i}^{\beta^{1-i}})^{-1},&i\mbox{ is even.}\end{array}\right.

Then
o(s_{i})=o(s_{i}^{\sigma})
,
[s_{i}^{\sigma},s_{j}^{\sigma}]=1
for
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
.
Also, it is easy to see that
s_{k+1}^{\sigma}=[s_{k}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}]
for
1\leq k\leq p-2
. Then we only need to show that

[s_{p-1}^{\sigma},\beta^{\sigma}]=(s_{1}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{1}}(s_{2}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{2}}(s_{3}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{3}}\cdots(s_{p-1}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{p-1}},

that is,
s_{p}^{\sigma}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}.

By Lemma
3.1
(1), we have

s_{1}^{\sigma}=s_{1},s_{2}^{\sigma}=\prod\limits_{i=2}^{p+1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p-1}{i-2}},s_{3}^{\sigma}=\prod\limits_{i=3}^{p+1}s_{i}^{\tbinom{p-2}{i-3}},\cdots,s_{p-1}^{\sigma}=\prod\limits_{i=p-1}^{p+1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{2}{i-(p-1)}}.

Then
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}=s_{1}^{u_{1}}s_{2}^{u_{2}}\cdots s_{p+1}^{u_{p+1}}
, where
u_{i}
comes from the following table

\begin{bmatrix}\begin{array}[]{c | c|cccccc}*&(s_{1}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{1}}&(s_{2}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{2}}&(s_{3}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{3}}&(s_{4}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{4}}&\cdots&\cdots&(s_{p-1}^{\sigma})^{-\tbinom{p}{p-1}}\\
\hline\cr u_{1}&-\tbinom{p}{1}&0&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
\hline\cr u_{2}&0&\tbinom{p-1}{0}\tbinom{p}{2}&0&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\
u_{3}&0&\tbinom{p-1}{1}\tbinom{p}{2}&-\tbinom{p-2}{0}\tbinom{p}{3}&0&\cdots&\cdots&0\\

u_{5}&0&\tbinom{p-1}{3}\tbinom{p}{2}&-\tbinom{p-2}{2}\tbinom{p}{3}&\tbinom{p-3}{1}\tbinom{p}{4}&\cdots&\cdots&0\\

Obviously,
u_{1}=-\tbinom{p}{1}
. By Proposition
2.3
(1), we have

u_{p+1}=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{p-1}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{p}{j}=\tbinom{p}{p-1}+\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\frac{p-3}{2}}\left[(-1)^{j}\tbinom{p}{j}+(-1)^{p-j}\tbinom{p}{p-j}\right]=\tbinom{p}{p-1}=p.

For
2\leq i\leq p-1
, we have
u_{i}=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{i}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{p+1-j}{i-j}\tbinom{p}{j}
.
For
i=p
, we have

u_{p}-\tbinom{1}{0}\tbinom{p}{p}=u_{p}-1=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{p}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{p+1-j}{p-j}\tbinom{p}{j}.

By Proposition
2.3
(2)
(3)
, we have

\tbinom{p+1-j}{i-j}\tbinom{p}{j}=\left[\tbinom{p-j}{i-j}+\tbinom{p-j}{i-j-1}\right]\tbinom{p}{j}=\tbinom{p-j}{i-j}\tbinom{p}{j}+\tbinom{p-j}{i-j-1}\tbinom{p}{j}=\tbinom{p}{i}\tbinom{i}{j}+\tbinom{p}{i-1}\tbinom{i-1}{j}.

Together with Proposition
2.3
(4), for
2\leq i\leq p
, we have

\sum\limits_{j=2}^{i}(-1)^{j}\left[\tbinom{p}{i}\tbinom{i}{j}+\tbinom{p}{i-1}\tbinom{i-1}{j}\right]

=\tbinom{p}{i}\left[i-1+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{i}{j}\right]+\tbinom{p}{i-1}\left[i-2+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}\tbinom{i-1}{j}\right]

=\tbinom{p}{i}\left[i-1+(-1)^{i}\tbinom{i-1}{i}\right]+\tbinom{p}{i-1}\left[i-2+(-1)^{i}\tbinom{i-2}{i}\right]

=\tbinom{p}{i}(i-1)+\tbinom{p}{i-1}(i-2)

=-\tbinom{p}{i}+(p-i+1)\tbinom{p}{i-1}+(i-2)\tbinom{p}{i-1}

=-\tbinom{p}{i}+(p-1)\tbinom{p}{i-1},

and hence

u_{p}=-\tbinom{p}{p}+(p-1)\tbinom{p}{p-1}+1=(p-1)\tbinom{p}{p-1},

u_{i}=-\tbinom{p}{i}+(p-1)\tbinom{p}{i-1},\quad 2\leq i\leq p-1.

By Lemma
3.1
and

The result is now immediate since

=s_{1}^{-\tbinom{p}{1}}\left[\prod\limits_{i=2}^{p-1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p}{i}+(p-1)\tbinom{p}{i-1}}\right]s_{p}^{(p-1)\tbinom{p}{p-1}}s_{p+1}^{p}

=\left[\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}\right]\left[(\prod\limits_{i=2}^{p}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p}{i-1}})^{1-p}\right]s_{p+1}^{p}

=s_{p}s_{p+1}^{1-p}s_{p+1}^{p}=s_{p}s_{p+1}=s_{p}^{\sigma}.

(2) We prove that
\tau\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
. Since
A
is abelian and
s_{i+1}=[s_{i},\beta]
for

, by Proposition

s_{i+1}^{\tau}=[s_{i},\beta]^{\tau}=[s_{i}^{-1},\beta]=(([s_{i},\beta])^{-1})^{s_{i}^{-1}}=(s_{i+1}^{-1})^{s_{i}^{-1}}=s_{i+1}^{-1}.

\{s_{1}^{-1},s_{2}^{-1},\cdots,s_{p-1}^{-1},\beta\}
of
G
satisfy
the same relations as
\{s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1},\beta\}
.
Obviously,
o(s_{i})=o(s_{i}^{\tau})
,
[s_{i}^{\tau},s_{j}^{\tau}]=1
for
1\leq i,j\leq p-1
.
Also, we have

[s_{k},\beta]^{\tau}=[s_{k}^{\tau},\beta^{\tau}]=[s_{k}^{-1},\ \beta]=([s_{k},\beta]^{-1})^{s_{k}^{-1}}=s_{k+1}^{-1}=s_{k+1}^{\tau}\ \mbox{for}\ 1\leq k\leq p-2.

Now we only need to show that

[s_{p-1}^{\tau},\beta^{\tau}]=(s_{1}^{\tau})^{-\tbinom{p}{1}}(s_{2}^{\tau})^{-\tbinom{p}{2}}(s_{3}^{\tau})^{-\tbinom{p}{3}}\cdots(s_{p-1}^{\tau})^{-\tbinom{p}{p-1}}.

It is easy to see that

[s_{p-1}^{\tau},\beta^{\tau}]=[s_{p-1}^{-1},\beta]=([s_{p-1},\beta]^{-1})^{s_{p-1}^{-1}}=(s_{p}^{-1})^{s_{p-1}^{-1}}=s_{p}^{-1}.

On the other hand,

\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{\tau})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}(s_{i}^{-1})^{-\tbinom{p}{i}}=(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p-1}s_{i}^{-\tbinom{p}{i}})^{-1}=s_{p}^{-1}.

It follows that
\tau\in\hbox{\rm Aut}(G)
.

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

Let
\rho_{0}=s_{1}\tau\sigma,\rho_{1}=\beta\tau\sigma,\rho_{2}=\sigma
. Since
o(\beta)=p,o(\tau)=2
and
[\beta,\tau]=1
, we have

(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{p}=(\beta\tau)^{p}=\tau
, and hence
\beta=(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{p+1}
. It follows that
R=\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle
.

Note that

\rho_{0}^{2}=s_{1}\tau\sigma s_{1}\tau\sigma=s_{1}s_{1}^{\tau\sigma}=s_{1}s_{1}^{-1}=1,

\rho_{1}^{2}=\beta\tau\sigma\beta\tau\sigma=\beta\beta^{\sigma\tau}=\beta\beta^{-1}=1,

(\rho_{0}\rho_{2})^{2}=(s_{1}\tau\sigma\sigma)^{2}=s_{1}s_{1}^{\tau}=s_{1}s_{1}^{-1}=1.

Then
\{R,\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}\}
is an
sggi
. We shall prove
\{R,\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}\}
is a string C-group.

Note that for any
x\notin\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1}\rangle
, we have
o(x)=p
.
Since

\rho_{0}\rho_{1}=s_{1}\tau\sigma\beta\tau\sigma=s_{1}\beta^{-1}\notin\langle s_{1},s_{2},\cdots,s_{p-1}\rangle,

o(\rho_{0}\rho_{1})=p
.
Since
(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{2}=(\beta\tau\sigma\tau)^{2}=(\beta\sigma)^{2}=\beta^{2}
,
o(\rho_{1}\rho_{2})=2p
.
If follows that
|\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\rangle|=2p
and
|\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle|=4p
.

Finally, if
\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\rangle\cap\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle>\langle\rho_{1}\rangle
, then
\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\rangle\leq\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle
.
It means that
G=\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle
, which is impossible because
|G|=4p^{m}>4p
.
Thus,
\langle\rho_{0},\rho_{1}\rangle\cap\langle\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\rangle=\langle\rho_{1}\rangle
.
By Proposition
2.1
,
\{R,\{\rho_{0},\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\}\}
is a string C-group
with type
\{p,2p\}
. That means
R
can be the automorphism groups of a regular
3
-polytope of order
4p^{m}
with type
\{p,2p\}
for
m\geq 3
.

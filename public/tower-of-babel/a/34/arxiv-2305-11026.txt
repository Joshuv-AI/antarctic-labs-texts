Prosaic Abelian Varieties Bad at One Prime
Armand Brumer, Kenneth Kramer

Abstract
. We say that an abelian variety A / ℚ A_{/{\mathbb{Q}}} of dimension g g is prosaic if it is semistable and its points of order 2 2 generate a 2 2 -extension of ℚ {\mathbb{Q}} . We focus on prosaic abelian varieties A A with good reduction outside one prime p p , whose ring of endomorphisms End ℚ ⁡ ( A ) \operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{Q}}(A) is ℤ {\mathbb{Z}} or an order in a totally real field of degree g g . 5.

If the conductor

of and abelian variety

is odd, then

is a finite flat group scheme over

Lemma 5.1
.

There is an abelian variety

isogenous to

, such that

has no subgroup scheme isomorphic to

Proof.

For each

be the maximal subgroup scheme of

that is multiplicative over

. We wish to show that some

is

, by which we mean that

. Suppose that

and

are isogenous to

. Thus there is an isogeny

If no

is obstructed, then we can create an arbitrarily long chain of

and

are isomorphic, say to

. Indeed, the multiplicative and étale constituents

and

have the same size over

. (See
[

, Lemma 3.2.1]
.)

Proposition 7.7
.

For integers

, let

)

\displaystyle F(x)=(4x+4a+1)\,(Q(x)-2i)\,(Q(x)+2i).

.
If
\gcd(m,n)=1
, then

is prosaic, with conductor equal to

.

Proof.

If

and

, then

\operatorname{res}(h,\bar{h})=-16,\quad{\mathfrak{m}}=\Delta_{h}=1-4b+8i,\quad\hskip 4.0pt\operatorname{res}(4x+4a+1,G)=n.

Since
m={\mathfrak{m}}\,\overline{{\mathfrak{m}}}
, formulas (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) give
\Delta_{F}=2^{8}mn^{2}
and
\Delta_{C}=mn^{2}
.

Assume that
\gcd(m,n)=1
and let
\ell
be a prime of bad reduction for
C
. Necessarily,
\ell
splits in
{\mathbb{Z}}[i]
and a choice of prime
\pi
over
\ell
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[i]
determines the image of
F(x)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}[x]
.

Case 1:
\ell|m
. By symmetry, we may assume that
\pi|{\mathfrak{m}}
, so
h
has a double root modulo
\pi
, say
x=\alpha
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. Since
\pi
does not divide

\gcd(\Delta_{h},\Delta_{\bar{h}})=\gcd(1-4b+8i,1-4b-8i)=1,

\alpha
is not a root of
\bar{h}
. But
m
and
n
are relatively prime, so
\pi
does not divide
\operatorname{res}(4x+4a+1,G)=n
. Thus,
\alpha
is not a root of

over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
. Hence
\alpha
is a node of
C
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}
.

Case 2:
\ell|n
. Then
\beta=-(4a+1)/4
is a common root of
4x+4a+1
and
G(x)
modulo
\ell
. Since
\ell

\Delta_{G}=\Delta_{h}\,\Delta_{\bar{h}}\,\operatorname{res}(h,\bar{h})^{2}=256m,

\beta

{\mathbb{F}}_{\ell}

Hence

is semistable, with squarefree conductor

-field unless

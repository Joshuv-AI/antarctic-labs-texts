1.
Introduction and statement of main results

To describe the branching of capillary sprouts during angiogenesis, Orme & Chaplain
[
31
]
proposed the following reaction-advection-diffusion system

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\xi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\Delta v+\xi_{2}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)+\alpha u-\beta v,\\
w_{t}=d_{3}\Delta w+\gamma u-\delta w,\end{cases}

(1.1)

with positive parameters
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3},\chi,\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\beta,\delta,\alpha,\gamma
,
where
u,v
and
w
denote the density of endothelial cells (ECs), adhesive sites, and the matrix (including fibronectin, laminin, and collagen IV), respectively. Different from the classical mathematical models of tumor angiogenesis with chemotaxis as the principle mechanism of cell motion (
[
8
,
35
]
), the model (
1.1
) was proposed based on the experimental observations that during angiogenesis process ECs secrete a matrix consisting of fibronectin, laminin and collagen IV
[
33
]
and the movement of ECs is effected by the distribution of adhesive sites on this matrix. More specifically, the following two processes are essentially incorporated in (
1.1
) (see
[
31
,
33
]
):

•

ECs secret matrix and adhesive sites;

•

The spreading of matrix with the convection of ECs and adhesive sites with it.

That is, the movement of ECs is governed by a combination of random motility, chemotaxis and convection.

Before proceeding to our motivation and main results, we first recall some most relevant results to the system (
1.1
) under homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions (IBVP) and nonnegative initial conditions.

(i)

\xi_{1}=\xi_{2}=0
: In this case, the first two components of (
1.1
) reduce to the well-known classical (minimal) Keller-Segel chemotaxis model:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\Delta v+\alpha u-\beta v,\\
\end{cases}

(1.2)

whose solution behaviors have been extensively studied in various perspectives in the past five decades including boundedness, blow-up, large time behavior and pattern formation. One can find more details from survey articles
[
10
,
6
,
5
]
and the references therein. More precisely, the boundedness and blowup of solutions for (
1.2
) have been established in two or higher dimensions
[
30
,
42
,
40
,
11
]
based on the following Lyapunov energy functional:

\mathcal{E}_{1}(u,v)=d_{1}\int_{\Omega}u\ln u-\chi\int_{\Omega}uv+\frac{\beta\chi}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}+\frac{\chi d_{2}}{2\alpha}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}.

(ii)

\xi_{2}=0
: This case means that the convection effect of matrix on the adhesive sites is neglected, and then
the system (
1.1
) reduces to the following widely studied attraction-repulsion Keller-Segel (ARKS) model

\begin{cases}u_{t}=d_{1}\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\xi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),\\
v_{t}=d_{2}\Delta v+\alpha u-\beta v,\\
w_{t}=d_{3}\Delta w+\gamma u-\delta w.\end{cases}

(1.3)

The ARKS model (
1.3
) has been proposed to describe the aggregation of
Microglia
in Alzheimer’s disease in
[
27
]
and to describe quorum effect in chemotaxis
[
32
]
. In one dimensional space, the existence of global boundedness of classical solution
[
26
,
14
]
and time-periodic patterns and steady states patterns
[
24
]
were established. In high dimensional spaces (
n\geq 2
), it has been found that the sign of
\Theta:=d_{2}\xi_{1}\gamma-d_{3}\chi\alpha
plays an important role in determining the solution behavior of (
1.3
). More precisely, if
\Theta\geq 0
(i.e., repulsion dominates or cancel attraction), the 2D fully parabolic ARKS model
[
13
,
25
]
or higher D parabolic-elliptic-elliptic simplification of the ARKS model
[
39
]
admits only global bounded solutions. However, if
\Theta<0
(i.e., attraction prevails over repulsion), based on the availability of Lyapunov functional, a critical mass phenomenon has been found (see
[
20
,
9
,
15
]
for more details). Recently, in the repulsion dominated case, i.e,
\Theta\geq 0
, the global stability of constant steady state has been studied in
[
22
,
17
]
.

(iii)

\xi_{1},\xi_{2}>0
: Due to the strong coupling of chemotaxis and convection in a cascade-like manner, which increases the complexity of mathematical analysis, the Lyapunov functional as constructed for the system (
1.3
) does not work anymore. To the best of our knowledge, the existing results on the system (
1.1
) seem at a rather rudimentary stage: in one dimensional space, the global existence of classical solution was very recently established in
[
21
]
based on semigroup estimate technique. Furthermore, based on an appropriate energy functional, the 1D bounded solution was shown to converge to constant steady state under some restrictions on the model parameters like
\xi_{2}
is small
[
18
]
. Very recently, for a parabolic-parabolic-elliptic simplified model in a bounded
convex
domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(1\leq n\leq 3)
, Tao & Winkler
[
38
]
used a Moser-type iteration to derive the global boundedness of classical solution for large
\xi_{1}
without qualitative information.

In summary of the above related results, some interesting questions naturally arise:

(Q1)

It follows from
[
38
]
that large repulsive convection prevents any blow-up phenomenon in
\leq 3
D (lower dimensional)
convex
domains. Hence, it is nature to ask whether or not large repulsive convection can prevent blow-up of solution in any dimensional bounded smooth but not necessarily convex domains.

(Q2)

In one dimensional space, due to nice Sobolev embeddings, a small
\xi_{2}(\in(0,1])
-independent upper bound of solution is available. This makes the small
\xi_{2}
-global stability toward constant equilibrium possible
[
18
]
. However, in higher dimensions, solution bounds typically depend on
\xi_{2}
(actually with a complex relation containing
\xi_{2}
and its inverse
\xi_{2}^{-1}
, cf. (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) for instance), and thus the method used in
[
18
]
does not work anymore. Hence, it is challenging to study long time dynamics of bounded solutions in higher dimensions.

(Q3)

Although there may be no significant increase in the rate of ECs mitosis during the first stages of angiogenesis, the mitosis occurs after the first spouts have formed
[
31
]
. Moreover, cell division also plays an essential role when repairing and remodelling of large wounds
[
33
]
. Thus, it is interesting and practically needed to study the effect of mitosis for the system (
1.1
).

To study the impact of convection more deeply and to provide relatively complete answers for the three questions above, for simplicity and clarity, based on the assumption that matrix diffuses much faster than adhesive sites and endothelial cells, we shall use a quasi-stationary approximation procedure as in
[
12
,
41
]
(
\tilde{w}=w-\bar{w}
, and then the
w
-equation becomes
d_{3}^{-1}\tilde{w}_{t}=\Delta\tilde{w}+\gamma d_{3}^{-1}(u-\bar{u})-\delta d_{3}^{-1}\tilde{w}
; then assuming
\gamma
has the same order as
d_{3}
and
\delta
has lower order than
d_{3}
, and finally, sending
d_{3}\rightarrow\infty
and dropping the tilde notation) to lead to the following version of parabolic-parabolic-elliptic problem:

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\xi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w)+u(a-\mu u^{\theta}),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
v_{t}=d\Delta v+\xi_{2}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)+u-v,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
0=\Delta w+u-\bar{u},\ \ \int_{\Omega}w=0,\ \ \bar{u}:=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u,&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\
\frac{\partial u}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial\nu}=\frac{\partial w}{\partial\nu}=0,&x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),~v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.4)

where

is a bounded domain with smooth boundary. Here we keep the parameters

as above and simplify other parameters in an obvious way for convenience. The kinetic term

with

is incorporated to see the effect of ECs mitosis. We shall henceforth assume that

(u_{0},v_{0})\in C^{0}(\bar{\Omega})\times W^{1,\infty}~~\text{with}~~u_{0}\geq 0,v_{0}\geq 0,~\text{and}~u_{0}\not\equiv 0.

(1.5)

Then our main findings on qualitative boundedness and convergence are stated as follows.

Theorem 1.1

(
Qualitative boundedness).

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 2)
be a bounded and smooth domain, the model parameters
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},d,\theta>0,a,\chi,\mu\geq 0
, and, let the initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
satisfy the regularity (

\xi_{0}
and
\mu_{0}
depending on

such that the IBVP (
1.4
) admits a unique global classical solution
provided one of the following conditions holds:

•

(1)
\xi_{1}\geq\xi_{0}\chi^{2}
; (2)
\theta=1,\ \mu\geq\max\left\{1,\ \chi^{\frac{8+2n}{5+n}}\right\}\mu_{0}\chi^{\frac{2}{5+n}}
; (3)
\theta>1,\mu>0
.

Moreover, the global solution is qualitatively bounded in the following way:

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}+\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}\leq M,\quad\quad\quad\forall t>0,

(1.6)

where, up to a multiplier depending only on

and
\Omega
, the positive constant

is explicitly expressible in terms of the model parameters

, see Lemmas

,
3.5
,

and

&:=M_{0},\end{split}

(1.7)

and

\|\nabla w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K_{2}\left(1+\left(1+d_{\Omega}M_{0}^{2(n+1)}\right)d^{-1}\chi^{2}M_{0}^{1-n}+M_{1}^{c}(n)\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}},

the function

M_{\mu}(\theta)=\left(1+\xi_{1}(\frac{1}{\mu})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}+(\frac{1}{\mu})^{\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)(\frac{1}{\mu})^{\frac{n+1}{\theta}},\ \ d_{\Omega}=d1_{\Omega}=\begin{cases}0,&\text{ if }\Omega\text{ is convex},\\
d,&\text{ if }\Omega\text{ is non-convex}.\end{cases}

(

The global bounded classical solution
(u,v,w)
obtained from Theorem

enjoys the following convergence properties:

(C1)

In the case of

(1.12)

(C2)

In the case of

All solutions

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}(n\geq 1)
be a bounded and smooth domain and let
g,h\in C^{2}(\bar{\Omega})
with
\frac{\partial g}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{\partial h}{\partial\nu}|_{\partial\Omega}=0
. Then, for all
p\geq 1
,

\left|\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot\nabla(\nabla g\cdot\nabla h)\right|\leq\left(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2p}+1\right)\|\nabla g\|^{2p}_{L^{2(p+1)}}\|D^{2}h\|_{L^{p+1}},

(2.8)

and

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h\nabla\cdot(|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g)\right|\\
&\leq\left(2(p-1)+\sqrt{n}\right)\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla g\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p+1)}}\|\Delta h\|_{L^{p+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}|D^{2}g|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(2.9)

as well as

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p+1)}\leq\left(2p+\sqrt{n}\right)^{2}\|g\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p-1)}|D^{2}g|^{2}.

(2.10)

Proof.

Using the following two facts

\nabla(\nabla g\cdot\nabla h)=D^{2}g\cdot\nabla h+D^{2}h\cdot\nabla g,

and

\nabla|\nabla g|^{2p}=p|\nabla g|^{2(p-1)}\nabla|\nabla g|^{2}=2p|\nabla g|^{2(p-1)}D^{2}g\cdot\nabla g,

(2.11)

we thus use the symmetry of
D^{2}g
and integration by parts formula to derive that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot\nabla(\nabla g\cdot\nabla h)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot\left(D^{2}g\cdot\nabla h+D^{2}h\cdot\nabla g\right)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}(D^{2}g\cdot\nabla g)\cdot\nabla h+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot(D^{2}h\cdot\nabla g)\\
&=\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\Omega}\nabla|\nabla g|^{2p}\cdot\nabla h+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot(D^{2}h\cdot\nabla g)\\
&=-\frac{1}{2p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p}\cdot\Delta h+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot(D^{2}h\cdot\nabla g).\end{split}

(2.12)

Noting the fact
|\Delta h|\leq\sqrt{n}|D^{2}h|
and using Hölder’s inequality, from (
2.12
) we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g\cdot\nabla(\nabla g\cdot\nabla h)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p}|D^{2}h|+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p}|D^{2}h|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2p}+1)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p}|D^{2}h|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2p}+1)\|\nabla g\|^{2p}_{L^{2(p+1)}}\|D^{2}h\|_{L^{p+1}},

which is our desired estimate (
2.8
). Similarly, we infer that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h\nabla\cdot(|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\nabla g)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h\nabla(|\nabla g|^{2p-2})\cdot\nabla g+\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\Delta g\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|2(p-1)\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h|\nabla g|^{2(p-2)}\nabla g\cdot(D^{2}g\cdot\nabla g)+\int_{\Omega}g\Delta h|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\Delta g\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2(p-1)+\sqrt{n})\int_{\Omega}|g|\cdot|\Delta h|\cdot|\nabla g|^{2p-2}\cdot|D^{2}g|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle(2(p-1)+\sqrt{n})\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Delta h\|_{L^{p+1}}\|\nabla g\|^{p-1}_{L^{2(p+1)}}\||\nabla g|^{p-1}|D^{2}g|\|_{L^{2}},

which gives rise to (
2.9
).

Finally, we use (
2.11
) to estimate the term
\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p+1)}
as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p+1)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2p}\nabla g\cdot\nabla g

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla g|^{2p}\Delta g-\int_{\Omega}g\nabla(|\nabla g|^{2p})\cdot\nabla g

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla g|^{2p}\Delta g-2p\int_{\Omega}g|\nabla g|^{2(p-1)}\nabla g\cdot(D^{2}g\cdot\nabla g)

\displaystyle\leq(2p+\sqrt{n})\int_{\Omega}|g|\cdot|\nabla g|^{2p}\cdot|D^{2}g|

\displaystyle\leq(2p+\sqrt{n})\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p+1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla g|^{2(p-1)}|D^{2}g|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

which upon simple algebraic manipulations entails (
2.10
).

Lemma 3.1

Proof.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}+\frac{4d(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}+\xi_{2}(p-1)\int_{\Omega}uv^{p}+p\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\\

(3.2)

\varepsilon

\begin{split}p\int_{\Omega}uv^{p-1}&\leq\xi_{2}(p-1)\int_{\Omega}uv^{p}+\xi_{2}^{-(p-1)}\int_{\Omega}u\\

(3.4)

3.3

3.4

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}+\frac{4d(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}+p\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\leq\frac{\xi_{2}m_{1}(p-1)}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}+m_{1}\xi_{2}^{1-p}.\end{split}

(3.5)

For
\varepsilon>0

depending only on

\|U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\|\nabla U\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+c_{1}(1+\varepsilon^{-\frac{n}{2}})\|U\|_{L^{1}}^{2}.

(3.6)

We choose
U=v^{\frac{p}{2}}
and
\varepsilon=\frac{4d|\Omega|}{p\xi_{2}m_{1}}
in (
3.6
) to derive that

\begin{split}&\frac{\xi_{2}m_{1}(p-1)}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\\
&\leq\frac{4(p-1)d}{p}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v^{\frac{p}{2}}|^{2}+\frac{\xi_{2}m_{1}c_{1}(p-1)}{|\Omega|}\left(1+\left(\frac{\xi_{2}m_{1}p}{4d|\Omega|}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}v^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2}\\

where

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}v^{p}+p\int_{\Omega}v^{p}\leq 2c_{2}p(1+p)^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}v^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2}+m_{1}\xi_{2}^{1-p},\end{split}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \left(\int_{\Omega}v^{p}(x,t)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(3.12)

for all

, where

(3.13)

Since

(3.14)

where

M_{1}(p):=\begin{cases}\xi_{1}m_{1}^{p+1}\frac{(p-1)}{p+1}\cdot\left(\frac{2p}{|\Omega|(p+1)}\right)^{p}+\left(\frac{1}{\mu}\right)^{\frac{p}{\theta}}\left(\frac{2ap}{p+\theta}\right)^{\frac{p}{\theta}}\frac{ap\theta}{p+\theta}|\Omega|,&{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}a,\mu>0,}\\[7.11317pt]
\xi_{1}m_{1}^{p+1}\frac{(p-1)}{p+1}\cdot\left(\frac{2p}{|\Omega|(p+1)}\right)^{p},&a=\mu=0.\end{cases}

(3.15)

Proof.

For
p>1
, multiplying the first equation of (
1.4
) by
u^{p-1}
and integrating the result over
\Omega
by parts, we conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{\theta+p}

\displaystyle=\chi(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\xi_{1}(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w+a\int_{\Omega}u^{p}

\displaystyle=\chi(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\Delta w+a\int_{\Omega}u^{p},

which together with the fact
\Delta w=\bar{u}-u
gives

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{p}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\mu\int_{\Omega}u^{\theta+p}+\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)}{p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1}\\
=~&\chi(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v+\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)}{p}\bar{u}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}+a\int_{\Omega}u^{p}.\end{split}

(3.16)

Applications of Hölder’s inequality and Young’s inequality yield that

\begin{split}\chi(p-1)\int_{\Omega}u^{p-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v&\leq\frac{(p-1)}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{p-2}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{\chi^{2}(p-1)}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}|\nabla v|^{2},\end{split}

(3.17)

and noting from
\bar{u}=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u\leq\frac{m_{1}}{|\Omega|}
that

\begin{split}\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)}{p}\bar{u}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}&\leq\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)m_{1}}{p|\Omega|}\int_{\Omega}u^{p}\\
&\leq\frac{\xi_{1}(p-1)}{2p}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+1}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\xi_{1}m_{1}^{p+1}\frac{(p-1)}{p(p+1)}\left(\frac{2p}{|\Omega|(p+1)}\right)^{p},}\end{split}

(3.18)

as well as, in the case of
a,\mu>0
,

\begin{split}a\int_{\Omega}u^{p}&\leq\frac{\mu}{2}\int_{\Omega}u^{p+\theta}+\left(\frac{2ap}{\mu(p+\theta)}\right)^{\frac{p}{\theta}}\frac{{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}a}\theta}{p+\theta}|\Omega|.\\
\end{split}

(3.19)

Substituting (
3.17
), (
3.18
) and (
3.19
) into (
3.16
) and noting the definition of
M_{1}(p)
in (
3.15
), we end up with (
3.14
) directly, thus proving this lemma.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

For

, the local-in-time classical solution of (

satisfies, for

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}+2k\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}+2kd\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}|D^{2}v|^{2}\\

Proof.

(3.21)

\displaystyle\;\;\;+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla u-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}.

\displaystyle=\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\nabla v\cdot\nabla w+v\Delta w)

\displaystyle=\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\nabla v\cdot\nabla w)-\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}v\Delta w\nabla\cdot(|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v).

\displaystyle=-2(k-1)\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla v|^{2k-4}\nabla v\cdot(D^{2}v\cdot\nabla v)-\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\Delta v

\displaystyle\leq(2(k-1)+\sqrt{n})\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla v|^{2k-2}|D^{2}v|.

A substitution of (

) and (
3.1
) into (
3.1
) shows (

Lemma 3.4
.

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}+2k\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k}+\frac{k}{2}(3d-d_{\Omega})\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}|D^{2}v|^{2}\\

where

and
\Omega
and

is defined by (

Proof.

By (
2.8
) and (
2.10

in (

\displaystyle J_{1}:

\displaystyle=2k\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v\cdot\nabla(\nabla v\cdot\nabla w)

\displaystyle=2k\xi_{2}(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2k}+1)\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2(k+1)}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\|D^{2}w\|_{L^{k+1}}

(3.26)

\displaystyle\leq 2k\xi_{2}(\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2k}+1)(2k+\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}\|v\|^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}_{L^{\infty}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2(k-1)}|D^{2}v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\|D^{2}w\|_{L^{k+1}}

for all

(3.27)

the well-known

[

2

19

, indeed,

where

and
\Omega
. Then we substitute (

J_{1}\leq 2c_{5}k\xi_{2}(\frac{1}{2k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})(2k+\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}{M_{0}}^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2(k-1)}|D^{2}v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{k}{k+1}}\|u\|_{L^{k+1}}.

(3.29)

Furthermore, using (
2.9
), (
2.10
) and the fact (
3.28
), one can derive

\begin{split}J_{2}&:=-2k\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}v\Delta w\nabla\cdot(|\nabla v|^{2k-2}\nabla v)\\

(3.30)

In addition, applying the Hölder’s inequality together with (
2.10
), we have

\begin{split}J_{3}:&=2k(2(k-1)+\sqrt{n})\int_{\Omega}u|\nabla v|^{2k-2}|D^{2}v|\\
&\leq 2k(2(k-1)+\sqrt{n})\left(\int_{\Omega}u^{2}|\nabla v|^{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2(k-1)}|D^{2}v|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\\

\begin{split}c_{6}&=2c_{5}k\xi_{2}(\frac{1}{2k}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}})(2k+\sqrt{n})^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}{M_{0}}^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}\\
&\ \ +2c_{5}k\xi_{2}(2(k-1)+\sqrt{n})(2k+\sqrt{n})^{\frac{k-1}{k+1}}{M_{0}}^{\frac{2k}{k+1}}\\

).

), Young’s inequality with

in (

&\leq\frac{k}{2}d_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2(k-1)}|D^{2}v|^{2}+c_{8}d_{\Omega}M_{0}^{2k}.\end{split}

(3.37)

Finally, substituting (

4.
Higher order regularity of solutions

In section 3, we have established the qualitative boundedness and thus global existence of solutions. To study large time behavior of global bounded solutions, we need further to enhance regularity properties of bounded solutions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(u,v,w)
be the global and bounded classical solution of (

) obtained in Theorem

. Then there exist

(4.1)

Proof.

In light of Theorem
1.1
, the global classical solution
(u,v,w)
satisfies

We now rewrite the first equation of (

) in the following form:

u_{t}=\nabla\cdot D(x,t,\nabla u)+R(x,t),\quad\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty),

where

and

In view of the boundedness in (

) and the Young inequality, we readily deduce

\begin{cases}D(x,t,\eta)\cdot\eta\geq\frac{1}{2}|\eta|^{2}-\frac{(\chi+\xi_{1})^{2}M^{4}}{2},\ \ |D(x,t,\eta)|\leq|\eta|+(\chi+\xi_{1})M^{2},\\[7.11317pt]

Then (
4.1
) follows from the Hölder regularity for parabolic equations
[
34
, Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2

There exists a constant

such that the global bounded solution of (

(4.3)

Proof.

In easier ways, we again use the boundedness (

(4.12)

For

(4.15)

At last, the term

5.

Given the enhanced regularity properties in the preceding section, we shall examine the long time dynamics of bounded solutions as obtained in Theorem

5.1.

In this case, the system (

\begin{cases}u_{t}=\Delta u-\chi\nabla\cdot(u\nabla v)+\xi_{1}\nabla\cdot(u\nabla w),&x\in\Omega,t>0,\\

&=2\chi\int_{\Omega}\Delta w\cdot\Delta v-\chi\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)\Delta v.\end{split}

(5.2)

Proof.

Multiplying the first equation of (
5.1
) by
\ln u-\ln\bar{u}
, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}u\ln\frac{u}{\bar{u}}+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}=\chi\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\xi_{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w.

(5.3)

We multiply the second equation of (

) by

to obtain

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}+d\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)\Delta v.

(5.4)

Then multiplying (
5.4
) by
\chi
and adding it to (

), and using the facts

and

due to homogeneous boundary conditions, we obtain

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}u\ln\frac{u}{\bar{u}}+\frac{\chi}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\right)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\nabla u|^{2}}{u}+d\chi\int_{\Omega}|\Delta v|^{2}+\chi\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\\
&=2\chi\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla v-\xi_{1}\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla w-\chi\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)\Delta v\\
&=2\chi\int_{\Omega}\Delta w\cdot\Delta v-\xi_{1}\int_{\Omega}|\Delta w|^{2}-\chi\xi_{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(v\nabla w)\Delta v,\end{split}

which gives rise to (

).
∎

In the limiting case of
\chi=0
, there is no bad terms to be estimated by (
5.2
), we can directly jump to (
5.15
) below, and hence its proof is simpler. We proceed to treat the hard case of

, for that purpose, we denote

A=A(d)=\sup_{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)}v(x,t),\quad\quad\quad B=B(d)=\sup_{(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)}|\nabla w(x,t)|.

(5.5)

Then by the qualitative bounds for
v
and
\nabla w
in (
1.7
) and (
1.8
) with (
1.9
) and (

\theta\geq 1
, by examining monotonicity, one easily sees that

\sup_{z\in(0,b)\cup(b,\infty)}\frac{(z-b)^{2}}{(z-b)(z^{\theta}-b^{\theta})}=\frac{1}{b^{\theta-1}}\Longleftrightarrow-(z-b)(z^{\theta}-b^{\theta})\leq-b^{\theta-1}(z-b)^{2},\ \ \forall z>0.

Applying this inequality to (
5.47
), we immediately arrive at

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u-b-b\ln\frac{u}{b})+\frac{b\chi^{2}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}(v-b)^{2}\right)+\frac{b\chi^{2}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}(v-b)^{2}\\
&\ \ +b^{\theta-1}\left[\mu-\frac{\left[\left(1+\frac{C_{p}^{2}\xi_{2}^{2}M_{0}^{2}}{d}\right)\frac{\chi^{2}}{d}+C_{p}^{2}\xi_{1}^{2}\right]}{2b^{\theta-2}}\right]\int_{\Omega}(u-b)^{2}\leq 0.\end{split}

(5.48)

We first integrate (
5.48
) from
1
to
t
and then we use our derived condition (
5.46
) and the elementary algebraic fact that
z-b-b\ln\frac{z}{b}\geq 0
for
z>0
to obtain that

\int_{1}^{t}\int_{\Omega}(u-b)^{2}<c_{81},\ \ \forall t\geq 1.

(5.49)

Given (
5.49
), using the same arguments as in Lemma
5.3
for
u
, one can easily show that

\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{\infty}}\to 0\ \ \mathrm{as}\ t\to\infty.

(5.50)

Next, observing the following elementary fact that

\lim_{z\rightarrow b}\frac{(z-b)^{2}}{z-b-b\ln\frac{z}{b}}=2b,

we infer from (
5.50
) there exists
t_{2}>0
such that

b\left[u(\cdot,t)-b-b\ln\frac{u(\cdot,t)}{b}\right]\leq\left[u(\cdot,t)-b\right]^{2}\leq 3b\left[u(\cdot,t)-b-b\ln\frac{u(\cdot,t)}{b}\right],\ \forall t\geq t_{2}.

(5.51)

Substituting (
5.51
) into (
5.48
), we end up with an important ODI:

\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\left(\int_{\Omega}(u-b-b\ln\frac{u}{b})+\frac{b\chi^{2}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}(v-b)^{2}\right)\\
&\ \ +\sigma\left(\int_{\Omega}(u-b-b\ln\frac{u}{b})+\frac{b\chi^{2}}{2d}\int_{\Omega}(v-b)^{2}\right)\leq 0,\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2},\end{split}

(5.52)

where, due to (
5.46
),

\sigma:=\min\left\{1,\ \ \ b^{\theta}\left[\mu-\frac{\left[\left(1+\frac{C^{2}_{p}\xi_{2}^{2}M_{0}^{2}}{d}\right)\frac{\chi^{2}}{d}+C^{2}_{p}\xi_{1}^{2}\right]}{2b^{\theta-2}}\right]\right\}>0.

Solving the ODI (
5.52
), we simply get the following exponential decay estimate:

\int_{\Omega}(u-b-b\ln\frac{u}{b})+\int_{\Omega}(v-b)^{2}\leq c_{82}e^{-\sigma(t-t_{2})},\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2},

which combined with (
5.51
) gives

\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{2}}+\|v(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{2}}\leq c_{83}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2}(t-t_{2})},\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2}.

(5.53)

Now, it is easy to improve this
L^{2}
-convergence to
L^{\infty}
-convergence as in Lemma
5.3
. Indeed, by the boundedness of
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2n}}
in (
4.3
) and the GN inequality, (
5.53
) entails

\begin{split}\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{\infty}}&\leq c_{84}\left(\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|^{\frac{n}{n+1}}_{L^{2n}}\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|^{\frac{1}{n+1}}_{L^{2}}+\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq c_{85}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2(n+1)}t},\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2}.\end{split}

(5.54)

Similarly, using the
W^{1,\infty}
-boundedness of
v
guaranteed by Theorem
1.1
, we use the GN inequality to improve the
L^{2}
-convergence of
v
in (
5.53
) to its
L^{\infty}
-convergence as follows:

\begin{split}\|v(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{86}\|v(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}}^{\frac{n}{n+2}}\|v(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{n+2}}&\leq c_{87}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{n+2}(t-t_{2})},\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2}.\end{split}

(5.55)

As for the
W^{2,\infty}
-convergence of
w
, applying the
W^{2,\infty}
-estimate to (
3.27
) and using (
5.54
), we see that
w
decays to zero exponentially in
W^{2,\infty}
-norm:

\begin{split}\|w(\cdot,t)\|_{W^{2,\infty}}&\leq c_{88}\|\Delta w\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{88}\left(\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{\infty}}+|b-\bar{u}|\right)\\
&\leq 2c_{88}\|u(\cdot,t)-b\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq c_{89}e^{-\frac{\sigma}{2(n+1)}t},\ \ \ \forall t\geq t_{2}.\end{split}

(5.56)

Our desired exponential convergence (
5.45
) follows from (
5.54
), (
5.55
) and (
5.56
).
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

The respective of convergence and exponential convergence in (C1) and (C2) of Theorem
1.3
have been fully contained in Lemmas
5.3
,
5.4
, and
5.8
.

A strongly convergent inertial inexact proximal-point algorithm for monotone inclusions with applications to variational inequalities
M. Marques Alves, J. E. Navarro Caballero, M. Geremia, R. T. Marcavillaca

Abstract
We propose an inertial variant of the strongly convergent inexact proximal-point (PP) method of Solodov and Svaiter (2000) for monotone inclusions. We prove strong convergence of our main algorithm under less restrictive assumptions on the inertial parameters when compared to previous analysis of inertial PP-type algorithms, which makes our method of interest even in finite-dimensional settings. We also performed an iteration-complexity analysis and applied our main algorithm to variational inequalities for monotone operators, obtaining strongly convergent (inertial) variants of Korpolevich’s extragradient, forward-backward and Tseng’s modified forward-backward methods. Preliminary numerical experiments indicate that our strongly convergent variant of Tseng’s modified forward-backward method performs well on certain matrix game problems. 2000 Mathematics Subject Classification: 47H05, 47N10, 90C33. Key words: Monotone inclusions, inertial algorithms, strong convergence, proximal-point, variational inequalities.

Introduction

We consider the
monotone inclusion problem

\displaystyle 0\in T(x)

(1)

where
T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is a (set-valued) maximal monotone operator as well as the (structured) inclusions

\displaystyle 0\in F(x)+B(x)

(2)

and

\displaystyle 0\in F(x)+N_{C}(x)

(3)

where
F(\cdot)
is a (single-valued) monotone map,
B(\cdot)
is (set-valued) maximal monotone and

N_{C}(\cdot)
is the normal cone of the convex set
C
(more details on notation will be given in Subsection
1.1
below). Under mild assumptions on the operators
F(\cdot)
and
B(\cdot)
, and the set
C
, problems (
2
) and (
3
) are instances of the general problem (
1
), where
T=F+B
and
T=F+N_{C}
, respectively.
Problem (
1
) and its structured versions (
2
) and (
3
) appear in a wide range of applications, including convex optimization, economics, machine learning, among others (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
and references therein).
Using the definition of
N_{C}(\cdot)
, it is simple to check that (
3
) is also equivalent to the problem of
finding
x\in C
satisfying

\displaystyle\langle F(x)\,,\,y-x\rangle\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;y\in C,

(4)

which is traditionally known as the (Stampacchia)
variational inequality problem
(VIP) for
F(\cdot)
and
C
.
Since in this paper the (monotone inclusion) formulation (
3
) is more convenient, from now on we will also refer to
(
3
) as a VIP.

Our focus will be on solving (
1
), (
2
) and (
3
) numerically. Specifically, we aim to design and analyze numerical schemes capable of computing approximate solutions to these problems.
Regarding (
1
), the most popular algorithm for computing its approximate solutions is the

proximal-point
(PP)

method
, which can be described as follows: given
x_{0}\in{\mathcal{H}}
, define the sequence
\{x_{k}\}
iteratively as

\displaystyle x_{k+1}\approx(\lambda_{k}T+I)^{-1}x_{k}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\geq 0,

(5)

where
\lambda_{k}>0
. In (
5
), “
\approx
” means that
x_{k+1}
is an approximation to the (exact)
iterate
(\lambda_{k}T+I)^{-1}x_{k}
, defining, in this way, inexact versions of the PP method.
In the seminal paper
[
28
]
, Rockafellar studied the
weak
convergence of (
5
) under summable error criteria and applied the obtained results to the justification of augmented Lagrangian algorithms for convex programming.
The
strong
convergence of sequence
\{x_{k}\}
as in (
5
) was left as an open problem, later on solved negatively by Güller in
[
17
]
.

The problem of forcing the strong convergence of the PP algorithm (
5
) (by conveniently modifying
the iterative process) was addressed by Solodov and Svaiter
[
32
]
in the setting of relative-error inexact PP methods,
which we briefly discuss next. Relative-error inexact PP-type methods appeared as an alternative to proximal algorithms employing summable error criteria for subproblems; the first methods of this type were proposed in
[
30
,
31
]
and subsequently studied, e.g., in
[
4
,
20
,
22
,
23
]
.
The key idea consists in observing that the exact PP iteration
x_{+}=(\lambda T+I)^{-1}x
can be decoupled as an
inclusion-equation system

\displaystyle v\in T(x_{+}),\quad\lambda v+x_{+}-x=0,

(6)

and then relaxing (
6
) within relative-error tolerance criteria – see Definition
2.2

and Eq. (
18
) below.

The starting point for this contribution is the paper
[
32
]
: we will introduce
inertial effects
on the iteration of
[
32
, Algorithm 1]
, obtaining in this way a strongly convergent inertial (relative-error) inexact PP method for solving (
1
). We will also study the iteration-complexity of our algorithm (see Algorithm
1
below) and apply the results of strong convergence and iteration-complexity to (
2
) and (
3
), through the derivation of
strong convergent inertial variants of the Korpolevich extragradient method, for solving (
3
), as well as of the Tseng’s modified forward-backward and forward-backward methods for (
2
).

Proximal algorithms with inertial effects for monotone inclusions and related problems were first proposed in the seminal paper
[
1
]
and subsequently developed in various research directions also by other authors (see, e.g.,

[
2
,
5
,
6
,
7
,
8
,
11
,
14
]
and references therein).
The main idea consists in at a current iterate, say
x_{k}
, introduce an “inertial effect” by extrapolating
x_{k}
in the direction

x_{k}-x_{k-1}
:

w_{k}=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),

where
\alpha_{k}\geq 0
, and then update the current information from
w_{k}
instead of
x_{k}
– see (
17
) and (
18
) below.
As it was mentioned earlier, our algorithms will benefit from inertial effects on the iteration (see also the comments and remarks following Algorithm
1
for more details).

General notation

Throughout this paper
{\mathcal{H}}
denotes a (possibly infinite-dimensional) real Hilbert space with inner product

\langle\,,\,\rangle
and induced norm
\|{\cdot}\|=\sqrt{\langle\cdot\,,\,\cdot\rangle}
.
For a set-valued map
T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
, the
effective domain
and
graph
of
T
are

\Dom T=\{x\mid T(x)\neq\emptyset\}
and
\Gra T=\{(x,v)\mid v\in T(x)\}
, respectively.
The
inverse
of
T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
T^{-1}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
defined at
any
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
by
v\in T^{-1}(x)
if and only if
x\in T(v)
.
The
sum
of two set-valued maps
T,S\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
T+S\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
, defined by
the usual Minkowski sum
(T+S)(x)=\{u+v\mid u\in T(x),v\in S(x)\}
.
For
\lambda>0
, we also define
\lambda T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
by

(\lambda T)(x)=\lambda T(x)=\{\lambda v\mid v\in T(x)\}
.
Whenever necessary, we will also identify single-valued maps
F\colon\Dom F\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
with its set-valued representation
F\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
by
F(x)=\{F(x)\}
.
A set-valued map
T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be a
monotone operator

if
\langle x-y\,,\,u-v\rangle\geq 0
for all
(x,u)
,
(y,v)\in\Gra T
, and
maximal monotone
if it is monotone and its graph
\Gra T
is not properly contained in the graph of any other monotone operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
.
A single-valued map
F\colon\Dom F\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is monotone
if
\langle x-y\,,\,F(x)-F(y)\rangle\geq 0
for all
x,y\in\Dom F
. The
resolvent
of a maximal monotone operator

T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is
J_{T}=(T+I)^{-1}
, where
I
denotes the identity operator in
{\mathcal{H}}
.
We will also denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of zeroes of the maximal monotone operator
T
, i.e.,
\mathcal{S}=T^{-1}(0)=\{x\mid 0\in T(x)\}
.

Let
f\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
be an extended real-valued function. The
domain
and
epigraph
of
f

are
\dom f=\{x\mid f(x)<+\infty\}
and
\epi f=\{(x,\mu)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}\mid\mu\geq f(x)\}
, respectively.
Recall that
f
is
proper
if
\dom f\neq\emptyset
and
convex
(resp.
lower semicontinuous
) if
\epi f
is a convex (resp. closed) subset of

{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}
.
For
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the
\varepsilon
-subdifferential
of
f
is

\partial_{\varepsilon}f\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
defined as

\partial_{\varepsilon}f(x)=\{v\mid f(y)\geq f(x)+\langle y-x\,,\,v\rangle-\varepsilon,\;\;\mbox{for all}\;y\in{\mathcal{H}}\}
.
When
\varepsilon=0
, the maximal monotone
[
27
]
operator
\partial_{0}f
is denoted by
\partial f
and is called the
subdifferential
of
f
.
For a nonempty closed and convex set
C
, the
indicator function
of
C
is
\delta_{C}
defined by

\delta_{C}(x)=0
if
x\in C
and
\delta_{C}(x)=+\infty
otherwise. The
normal cone
operator of
C
is
the set-valued map
N_{C}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
defined as

N_{C}(x)=\{v\mid\langle v\,,\,y-x\rangle\leq 0,\;\;\mbox{for all}\,y\in C\}
(we set
N_{C}(x)=\emptyset
if
x\notin C
);
it is easy to check that
N_{C}=\partial\delta_{C}
.
The
orthogonal projection
onto
C
is denoted by
P_{C}
, i.e.,
P_{C}(x)
is the unique element in
C
such that
\|{x-P_{C}(x)}\|\leq\|{x-y}\|
for all
y\in C
.

By
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\mathbb{R}_{++}
we denote the set of nonnegative and (strictly) positive reals, respectively.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
(the set of natural numbers), we let
[n]\coloneqq\{0,\dots,n\}
.
Finally, strong and weak convergence of sequences will be denoted by the usual symbols
\to
and
\rightharpoonup
, respectively.
For more details on notation and basis results on convex analysis and monotone operators we refer the reader
to
[
10
,
26
,
29
]
.

Preliminaries and basic results

In this section, we state some basic and preliminary results, mainly related to the notion of (relative-error) inexact solutions for the proximal subproblems we will employ in this paper. To this end, we begin by recalling some basic facts about
\varepsilon
-enlargements of set-valued maps.

For a set-valued map
T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the
\varepsilon
-enlargement
T^{\varepsilon}\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
of
T
is defined as

\displaystyle T^{\varepsilon}(x)=\left\{v\mid\langle u-v,y-x\rangle\geq-\varepsilon\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;(y,u)\in\Gra T\right\}.

(7)

If
T
is monotone, then the inclusion
T(x)\subset T^{\varepsilon}(x)
trivially holds (this justifies the name “enlargement” of
T
).
For
N_{C}
, i.e., for the normal cone of a (nonempty) closed and convex set, we have

N_{C}^{\varepsilon}=\partial_{\varepsilon}\delta_{C}
.
Other relevant and useful properties of
T^{\varepsilon}
(for this work) are summarized in the next proposition
(for a proof see, e.g.,
[
13
, Lemma 3.1 and Proposition 3.4(b)]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
T,S\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
be set-valued maps. Then,

(a)

If
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{2}
, then

T^{\varepsilon_{1}}(x)\subset T^{\varepsilon_{2}}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
.

(b)

T^{\varepsilon_{1}}(x)+S^{\,\varepsilon_{2}}(x)\subset(T+S)^{\varepsilon_{1}+\varepsilon_{2}}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
and

\varepsilon_{1},\varepsilon_{2}\geq 0
.

(c)

T
is monotone if and only if
\Gra T\subset\Gra T^{0}
.

(d)

T
is maximal monotone if and only if
T=T^{0}
.

(e)

If
f\colon{\mathcal{H}}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
is proper lower semicontinuous and convex, then

\partial_{\varepsilon}f(x)\subset(\partial f)^{\varepsilon}(x)
for
all
x\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
\varepsilon\geq 0
.

(f)

If
T
is maximal monotone,
\{(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})\}
is
such that
v_{k}\in T^{\varepsilon_{k}}(y_{k})
,
y_{k}\rightharpoonup y
,

v_{k}\to v
and
\varepsilon_{k}\to\varepsilon
, then

v\in T^{\varepsilon}(y)
.

(g)

If
u\in\partial f(x)
and
y\in\dom f
, then
u\in\partial_{\varepsilon}f(y)
, where

\varepsilon=f(y)-\left(f(x)+\langle y-x\,,\,u\rangle\right)
.

As we discussed in the introduction, the notion of relative-error criterion for the subproblems
of the exact PP iteration
x_{+}=(\lambda T+I)^{-1}x
can be derived by observing that the iteration can be decoupled as the inclusion-equation system (
6
), namely,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v\in T(y),\\[2.84526pt]
\lambda v+y-x=0\end{cases}

(8)

where
y=x_{+}
.
The following concept of approximate solutions for (
8
) was introduced for the first time in
[
33
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

A triple
(y,v,\varepsilon)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is an
\sigma
-approximate solution of the proximal system (
8
) at
(x,\lambda)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}_{++}
if
\sigma\in[0,1)
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v\in T^{\varepsilon}(y),\\[5.69054pt]
\|{\lambda v+y-x}\|^{2}+2\lambda\varepsilon\leq\sigma^{2}\left(\|{\lambda v}\|^{2}+\|{y-x}\|^{2}\right).\end{cases}

(9)

Note that if we take
\sigma=0
in (
9
), then it trivially follows that

\varepsilon=0
and
\lambda v+y-x=0
, which is to say, in view of Proposition
2.1
(d),
that
v\in T(y)
and
\lambda v+y-x=0
, or in other words,
y=(\lambda T+I)^{-1}x
. Hence the above definition of inexact solution for the subproblems of the PP algorithm gives
y
as the exact iteration
x_{+}=(\lambda T+I)^{-1}x
in the (exact) case
\sigma=0
. Moreover, the error in both the inclusion (given by the
\varepsilon
-enlargement) and the equation in (
8
) is “relative” to the displacement
y-x
and
\lambda v
.

We next state some basic properties of approximate solutions defined above.
The following lemma was proved in
[

Lemma 2.3
.

A triple
(y,v,\varepsilon)
is an
\sigma
-approximate solution of the proximal system (

) at
(x,\lambda)

(
in the sense of
Definition

)

if and only if

\displaystyle\langle x-y,v\rangle-\varepsilon\geq\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2\lambda}\left(\|{\lambda v}\|^{2}+\|{x-y}\|^{2}\right).

Furthermore, the following statements are equivalent

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v=0,\\
x\in T^{-1}(0),\\

and any of them implies

\|x-P_{C}(x)\|\leq\|x-y\|

A strongly convergent inertial inexact proximal-point algorithm

In this section, we consider the general (monotone) inclusion problem (
1
), i.e.,

\displaystyle 0\in T(x)

(16)

where the following assumptions are assumed to hold:

A1.

T\colon{\mathcal{H}}\rightrightarrows{\mathcal{H}}
is a maximal monotone operator.

A2.

The
solution set

\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)
of (
16
) is nonempty.

For numerically solving (
16

1
below and the comments following it). The main results are Theorems
3.6
and
3.7

Algorithm
1
consists of the following main ingredients:

Inertial effects

Inertial effects
on the iteration produced by two “control” sequences

\{\alpha_{k}\}
and
\{\beta_{k}\}
– see (
17
).

Relative-error criterion for subproblems

A flexible
relative-error criterion
for the proximal subproblems, allowing errors both in the inclusion and in the equation of the proximal system – see Definition
2.2
and Eq. (
18
).

Orthogonal projection onto half-spaces

Definition of the next iterate

as the
orthogonal projection
onto the intersection of the half-spaces
H_{k}
and
W_{k}
– see (

) and (

).

Next comes the algorithm:

Algorithm 1

A strongly convergent inertial inexact PP algorithm for solving (
16
)

Input:

x_{0}=x_{-1}\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
\sigma\in[0,1)

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

Choose
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}\geq 0
and set

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} w_{k}&=x_{k}+\alpha_{k}(x_{k}-x_{k-1}),\\[5.69054pt]
\widetilde{w}_{k}&=w_{k}+\beta_{k}(w_{k}-x_{0}).\end{aligned}

(17)

Choose
\lambda_{k}>0
and compute an
\sigma
-approximate solution (in the sense of Definition

) at
(\widetilde{w}_{k},\lambda_{k})
, i.e., compute
(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \begin{cases}v_{k}\in T^{\varepsilon_{k}}(y_{k}),\\[5.69054pt]
\|{\lambda_{k}v_{k}+y_{k}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|^{2}+2\lambda_{k}\varepsilon_{k}\leq\sigma^{2}\left(\|{\lambda_{k}v_{k}}\|^{2}+\|{y_{k}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|^{2}\right).\end{cases}\end{aligned}

(18)

Define

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{k}&=\{z\mid\langle z-y_{k},v_{k}\rangle\leq\varepsilon_{k}\},\\[5.69054pt]
W_{k}&=\{z\mid\langle z-x_{k},x_{0}-x_{k}\rangle\leq 0\}.\end{aligned}

(19)

Set

\displaystyle x_{k+1}=P_{H_{k}\cap W_{k}}(x_{0}).

(20)

(ii)

is a generalization (an inertial version) of
[
32
, Algorithm 1]
and many results of this work are inspired by the latter reference. The relative-error condition (
18
) is more general than the corresponding one in
[
32
]
, more precisely
[
32
, Definition 1]
; actually (
18
) appeared for the first time in
[
33
, Definition 1]
for a different version of the PP algorithm. As we mentioned in the introduction, relative-error criteria similar to (
18
) for subproblems of the PP algorithm and its variants have been employed in different contexts and by many authors (see, e.g.
[
4
,
20
,
22
,
23
,
33
]
and references therein).

(iii)

Note that if we set
\sigma=0
in (
18
) we obtain
\varepsilon_{k}=0
,

\lambda_{k}v_{k}+y_{k}-\widetilde{w}_{k}=0
and
v_{k}\in T^{0}(y_{k})
, which is to say that

y_{k}=(\lambda_{k}T+I)^{-1}(\widetilde{w}_{k})
and
v_{k}=\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k})
(we recall that, since
T
is maximal monotone, we have
T^{0}=T
). Hence, assuming that the resolvent operator
(\lambda T+I)^{-1}
is easy to evaluate, it follows that the triple

(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})\coloneqq\left((\lambda_{k}T+I)^{-1}(\widetilde{w}_{k}),\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}}(\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}),0\right)
always satisfies the relative-error condition (
18
).
In this paper, we don’t make such an assumption and are interested in the opposite cases, namely those cases where
(\lambda T+I)^{-1}
is hard to compute. Prescriptions on how to compute a triple
(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})
satisfying (
18
) in the general case
\sigma\in[0,1)
will depend on the particular structure of
T(\cdot)
in (
16
) – see, for instance, Sections
4
and
5
.

(iv)

The half-spaces
H_{k}
and
W_{k}
as in (
19
) are exactly the same as in
[
32
, Algorithm 1]
. They are crucial to ensure the strong convergence of the sequences generated by Algorithm
1
. As it was discussed in
[
32
]
, the
definition of
x_{k+1}
as in (
20
) depends on the explicit computation of the orthogonal projection onto

H_{k}\cap W_{k}
, which essentially reduces (at most) to the solution of a
2\times 2
linear system (see
[
32
, pp. 195 and 196]
and

[
10
, Propositions 28.18 and 28.19]
). The well-definedness of Algorithm
1
– regarding (
20
) – will be discussed in
Appendix
A
below.

(v)

Algorithm
1
can be used both as a practical method, when an inner algorithm is available to compute a triple
(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})
satisfying (
18
), and as a framework for the design and analysis of other more concrete schemes. The latter idea will be further explored in Sections
4
and
5
for designing strongly convergent inertial versions of the Korpelevich, Tseng’s modified forward-backward and forward-backward methods for variational inequalities and structured monotone inclusions.

3.1
Convergence analysis

In this subsection, we study the (asymptotic) strong convergence of Algorithm

; the main result is
Theorem

below. Propositions

and

Proposition 3.1
.

Consider the sequences evolved by
Algorithm
1
and let
d_{0}
denote the distance of
x_{0}

to the solution set
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
of (
16
). Then the following statements hold:

(a)

For all
k\geq 0
,

\|{x_{k+1}-x_{0}}\|^{2}\geq\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|^{2}+\|{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}\|^{2}.

(b)

For all
k\geq 0
,

\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|\leq d_{0}.

(c)

For all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|{x_{k+1}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2}\max\big\{\|{\lambda_{k}v_{k}}\|,\|{\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}}\|\big\}.

(21)

Proof.

(a)
By the definition of
W_{k}
, we have
x_{k}=P_{W_{k}}(x_{0})
. Applying (
14
) with
C=W_{k}
,
x=x_{k+1}
and
y=x_{0}
, and taking into account that
x_{k+1}=P_{W_{k}}{(x_{k+1})}

(because
x_{k+1}\in W_{k}
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|{P_{W_{k}}(x_{k+1})-P_{W_{k}}(x_{0})}\|^{2}\leq\|{x_{k+1}-x_{0}}\|^{2}-\|{\underbrace{\left[x_{k+1}-P_{W_{k}}(x_{k+1})\right]}_{0}-\left[x_{0}-P_{W_{k}}(x_{0})\right]}\|^{2}.

Then

\displaystyle\|{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}\|^{2}\leq\|{x_{k+1}-x_{0}}\|^{2}-\|{x_{0}-x_{k}}\|^{2},

which is clearly equivalent to the inequality in item
(a)
.

(b)
From (
20
) and (
66
) below, for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|{x_{k+1}-x_{0}}\|

\displaystyle=\|{P_{H_{k}\cap W_{k}}(x_{0})-x_{0}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{P_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})-x_{0}}\|

\displaystyle=d_{0},

where
P_{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the projection onto
\mathcal{S}
. Note that the desired inequality is trivial when
k=0
.

(c)
Since
x_{k+1}\in H_{k}
, using the definition of
P_{H_{k}}(\widetilde{w}_{k})
we find

\displaystyle\|{x_{k+1}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|\geq\|{P_{H_{k}}(\widetilde{w}_{k})-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|.

On the other hand, from Lemma
2.4
and the fact that
(y_{k},v_{k},\varepsilon_{k})
is an
\sigma
-approximate solution at
(\widetilde{w}_{k},\lambda_{k})
– see (
18
)–, it follows that

\displaystyle\|{P_{H_{k}}(\widetilde{w}_{k})-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|\geq\frac{1-\sigma^{2}}{2}\max\{\|{\lambda_{k}v_{k}}\|,\|{\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}}\|\},

which, in turn, finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

From now on in this paper we assume that the following conditions hold on the sequences
\{\alpha_{k}\}

and
\{\beta_{k}\}
given in Algorithm

.

Assumption 3.2
.

(a)

The sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}
is bounded, i.e.,

\overline{\alpha}\coloneqq\sup_{k\geq 0}\,\alpha_{k}<+\infty.

(b)

The sequence
\{\beta_{k}\}
belongs to
\ell_{2}
, i.e.,

\overline{s}\coloneqq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\beta^{2}_{k}<+\infty.

We will also use the notation

\overline{\beta}\coloneqq\sup_{k\geq 0}\,\beta_{k}<+\infty.

Remark 3.3
.

We emphasize that even if we set
\beta_{k}\equiv 0
, in which case
\overline{s}=\overline{\beta}=0
, the inertial effect produced by the sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}
on Algorithm

is still in consonance with the typical extrapolation introduced in the current literature on inertial-type algorithms. More than that, Assumption
3.2
(a)
, on the boundedness of the sequence
\{\alpha_{k}\}
, represents a much more flexible assumption when compared to what is usually imposed – in the seminal paper
[
1
]
, for instance, it is assumed that
\overline{\alpha}<1/3
. See also
[
3
, Section 2]
for an additional discussion. This latter fact makes the results of this paper of interest even in the finite-dimensional setting, where of course weak and strong convergence coincide.

Proposition 3.4
.

Consider the sequences evolved by
Algorithm
1
, let
d_{0}
denote the distance of
x_{0}

to the solution set
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
of (
16
) and let
\overline{\alpha}
,
\overline{\beta}
and
\overline{s}
be as in
Assumption
3.2
. Then

(a)

we have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\|{x_{k+1}-x_{k}}\|^{2}\leq d_{0}^{2}.

(b)

We have

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\|{x_{k+1}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|^{2}\leq(1+\overline{\alpha})\Big[(1+\overline{\beta})\big[1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\big]+\overline{s}\Big]d_{0}^{2}.

(c)

We have

x_{k}-y_{k}\to 0.

Proof.

(a)
The result follows directly from items
(a)
and
(b)
of Proposition
3.1
combined with a telescopic sum argument.

(b)
We claim that

\displaystyle\|{w_{k}-z}\|^{2}=(1+\alpha_{k})\|{x_{k}-z}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}(1+\alpha_{k})\|{x_{k}-x_{k-1}}\|^{2}-\alpha_{k}\|{x_{k-1}-z}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\;z\in{\mathcal{H}}.

(22)

Indeed, from the definition of
w_{k}
as in (
17
), we have

x_{k}-z=\frac{1}{1+\alpha_{k}}(w_{k}-z)+\frac{\alpha_{k}}{1+\alpha_{k}}(x_{k-1}-z)

and
w_{k}-x_{k-1}=(1+\alpha_{k})(x_{k}-x_{k-1})
, which
combined with Lemma
B.1
(a) in Appendix
B
and some simple algebra yields (
22
).

Note now that using Lemma
B.1
(b) and (
17
), we have

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-{\widetilde{w}}_{k}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k+1}-w_{k}-\beta_{k}(w_{k}-x_{0})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k+1}-w_{k}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}^{2}\|w_{k}-x_{0}\|^{2}+2\beta_{k}\langle w_{k}-x_{k+1},w_{k}-x_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=(1+\beta_{k})\|w_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\|w_{k}-x_{0}\|^{2}-\beta_{k}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}.

Using (
22
) (with
z=x_{k+1}
and
z=x_{0}
) in the above identity, we find

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-{\widetilde{w}}_{k}\|^{2}=

\displaystyle(1+\beta_{k})\left[(1+\alpha_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}(1+\alpha_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\alpha_{k}\|x_{k-1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle+\beta_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\left[(1+\alpha_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{0}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}(1+\alpha_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}-\alpha_{k}\|x_{k-1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle-\beta_{k}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1+\beta_{k})(1+\alpha_{k})\Big[\|x_{k}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|x_{k}-x_{0}\|^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle-\Big[\alpha_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\left(\|x_{k-1}-x_{k+1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|x_{k-1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\beta_{k}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\Big].

(23)

On the other hand, since
\|x_{k+1}-x_{k-1}\|+\|x_{k-1}-x_{0}\|\geq\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|
,
using Lemma
B.2
below, we get

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|x_{k-1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\geq\frac{\beta_{k}}{1+\beta_{k}}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2},

which in turn implies

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\left(\|x_{k+1}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}+\beta_{k}\|x_{k-1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\right)+\beta_{k}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\geq\beta_{k}(1+\alpha_{k})\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}.

(24)

Combining (
23
) and (
24
), and using the fact that

\|x_{k}-x_{0}\|\leq\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|
– see Proposition
3.1
(a) –, we get

\displaystyle\|x_{k+1}-{\widetilde{w}}_{k}\|^{2}\leq

\displaystyle(1+\alpha_{k})\bigg[(1+\beta_{k})\big[\|x_{k+1}-x_{k}\|^{2}+\alpha_{k}(1+\beta_{k})\|x_{k}-x_{k-1}\|^{2}\big]+\beta_{k}^{2}\|x_{k+1}-x_{0}\|^{2}\bigg],

which when combined with item (a) and Proposition
3.1
(b), and Assumption
3.2
, yields the desired result.

(c)
Note first that

\displaystyle\|{x_{k}-y_{k}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{x_{k}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|+\|{\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{x_{k}-x_{k+1}}\|+\|{x_{k+1}-\widetilde{w}_{k}}\|+\|{\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}}\|.

The desired result now follows from items (a) and (b) and Proposition
3.1
(c).
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
be generated by
Algorithm
1
and assume that there exists
\underline{\lambda}>0

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}\geq\underline{\lambda}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\geq 0.

Note first that Proposition
3.1
(b) implies that
\{x_{k}\}
is bounded. From Proposition
3.4
(b),

\displaystyle x_{k+1}-\widetilde{w}_{k}\rightarrow 0,

(26)

which, in turn, when combined with (
21
) yields

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}v_{k}\rightarrow 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\widetilde{w}_{k}-y_{k}\rightarrow 0.

Consequently, in view of the inequality in (

) and

\lambda_{k}\geq\underline{\lambda}>0

for all

\displaystyle v_{k}\to 0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\varepsilon_{k}\rightarrow 0.

Let
\{x_{k_{j}}\}

. Using Proposition

On the other hand, from (

v_{k_{j}}\to 0
and

.
Altogether, we have

Next we will prove the strong convergence of any sequence
\{x_{k}\}
generated by Algorithm

Theorem 3.6

(Strong convergence of Algorithm
1
)
.

Let
\{x_{k}\}
,
\{y_{k}\}
and
\{\lambda_{k}\}
be generated by
Algorithm 1
and assume that

\{\lambda_{k}\}
is bounded away from zero, i.e., that condition (
25

Suppose also that
Assumption
3.2
holds on the sequences
\{\alpha_{k}\}
and
\{\beta_{k}\}
.
Then
\{x_{k}\}
and
\{y_{k}\}
converge strongly to

.

Proof.

In view of Proposition
3.1
(b) and the fact that
d_{0}=\|{x^{*}-x_{0}}\|
, we have

\displaystyle\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|\leq\|{x^{\ast}-x_{0}}\|\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\geq 0.

(28)

Then
\{x_{k}\}
is bounded and, by Corollary
3.5
, its weak cluster points belong to
\mathcal{S}
.
Let
\{x_{k_{j}}\}
be a subsequence of
\{x_{k}\}
and
\overline{x}\in\mathcal{S}
be such that

\displaystyle x_{k_{j}}\rightharpoonup\overline{x}.

Since
\|{\cdot}\|
is lower semicontinuous in the weak topology, we obtain

\displaystyle\|{\overline{x}-x_{0}}\|\leq\liminf\|{x_{k_{j}}-x_{0}}\|\leq\limsup\|{x_{k_{j}}-x_{0}}\|\leq\|{x^{\ast}-x_{0}}\|,

(29)

28

, as a direct consequence of (

. So,
x_{k}\rightharpoonup x^{*}
, and from (

\displaystyle\|{x^{*}-x_{0}}\|\leq\liminf\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|\leq\limsup\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|\leq\|{x^{\ast}-x_{0}}\|

\|{x_{k}-x_{0}}\|\to\|{x^{\ast}-x_{0}}\|

(c).
∎

3.2
Iteration-complexity analysis

In this subsection, we study the (pointwise) iteration-complexity of Algorithm
1
.
The main result is Theorem
3.7
below.
Motivated by the complexity analyzes of the HPE method presented in
[
20
]
, we study the iteration-complexity of Algorithm
1
in the following sense: for a given tolerance
\rho>0
, we wish to estimate the number of iterations needed to compute a triple
(y,v,\varepsilon)\in{\mathcal{H}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that

\displaystyle v\in T^{\varepsilon}(y)\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\max\{\|{v}\|,\varepsilon\}\leq\rho.

(30)

In this case,
y
is considered to be a
\rho
-approximate solution of (
16
). Note that if we set

\rho=0
in (
30
), then
v=0
and
\varepsilon=0
, in which case
0\in T^{0}(y)
, i.e,

0\in T(y)
(in other words
y
is a solution of (
16
)).

Next is the main theorem regarding the iteration-complexity of Algorithm
1
.

Theorem 3.7

(Pointwise iteration-complexity of Algorithm
1
)
.

Consider the sequences evolved by
Algorithm 1
and assume that
\{\lambda_{k}\}
is bounded away from zero, i.e., assume that condition (
25
) holds. Let
d_{0}
denote the distance of
x_{0}
to the solution set
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
of (
16
) and suppose

Assumption
3.2
holds. Then, for every
k\geq 0
, there exists
j\in[k]
such that

\displaystyle v_{j}\in T^{\varepsilon_{j}}(y_{j}),

(31a)

\displaystyle\|{v_{j}}\|\leq\dfrac{2d_{0}}{\sqrt{k+1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{(1+\overline{\alpha})\Big[(1+\overline{\beta})\big[1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\big]+\overline{s}\Big]}}{(1-\sigma^{2})\underline{\lambda}}\right),

(31b)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\leq\frac{d_{0}^{2}}{k+1}\left(\frac{4\sigma^{2}(1+\overline{\alpha})\Big[(1+\overline{\beta})\big[1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\big]+\overline{s}\Big]}{(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}\underline{\lambda}}\right).

(31c)

Proof.

Using Proposition
3.1
(c) and the inequality in (
18
), we find for all

,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\|{\lambda_{i}v_{i}}\|^{2},\frac{\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\leq\dfrac{4}{(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}}\|{x_{i+1}-\widetilde{w}_{i}}\|^{2}.

Hence, from Proposition
3.4
(b),

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}\,\max\left\{\|{\lambda_{i}v_{i}}\|^{2},\frac{\lambda_{i}\varepsilon_{i}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\leq\dfrac{4(1+\overline{\alpha})\Big[(1+\overline{\beta})\big[1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\big]+\overline{s}\Big]d_{0}^{2}}{(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}}

and so there exists
j\in[k]
such that

\displaystyle(k+1)\,\left(\max\left\{\|{\lambda_{j}v_{j}}\|^{2},\frac{\lambda_{j}\varepsilon_{j}}{\sigma^{2}}\right\}\right)\leq\dfrac{4(1+\overline{\alpha})\Big[(1+\overline{\beta})\big[1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\big]+\overline{s}\Big]d_{0}^{2}}{(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}},

which combined with (
25
) yields (
31b
) and (
31c
). To finish the proof of the theorem, note that
(
31a
) follows directly from the inclusion in (
18
).
∎

We now make some remarks regarding Theorem

.

(i)

If we set
\beta_{k}\equiv 0
in Algorithm

– see Remark

and

given in (

(ii)

). In this case, Theorem

guarantees that, for a given tolerance

, Algorithm

finds a triple

(y,v,\varepsilon)
satisfying (

\displaystyle O\left(\left\lceil\left(\dfrac{1+\overline{\alpha}}{1-\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}\max\left\{\left(\dfrac{d_{0}}{\underline{\lambda}\rho}\right)^{2},\dfrac{\sigma^{2}d_{0}^{2}}{\underline{\lambda}\rho}\right\}\right\rceil\right)

(33)

iterations.

4
A strongly convergent inertial variant of the Korpolevich extragradient method

In this section, we consider the VIP (
4
), i.e., the problem of finding
x\in C
such that

\displaystyle\langle F(x)\,,\,y-x\rangle\geq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad y\in C,

(34)

where the following assumptions are assumed to hold:

B1.

C\subset\Dom F
is a nonempty closed and convex subset of
{\mathcal{H}}
.

B2.

F\colon\Dom F\subset{\mathcal{H}}\to{\mathcal{H}}
is monotone and
L
-Lipschitz continuous, i.e.,
F
is monotone (on
\Dom F
) and there exists
L>0
such that

\displaystyle\|{F(x)-F(y)}\|\leq L\|{x-y}\|\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x,y\in\Dom F.

(35)

B3.

The solution set
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq(F+N_{C})^{-1}(0)
of (
34
) is nonempty.

Note that under the above assumptions on
C
and
F(\cdot)
the operator
F+N_{C}
is maximal monotone
[
16
, Proposition 12.3.6]
.
Moreover, as we discussed in the introduction, problem (
34
) is equivalent to the monotone inclusion
0\in T(x)
with

T=F+N_{C}
; this fact allow us to apply the results of Section
3
to (
34
).

For numerically solving (
34
), we propose and study a strongly convergent inertial variant of the celebrated Korpolevich extragradient method
[
18
]
(see Algorithm
2
). The main results regarding strong convergence and iteration-complexity are summarized in Theorem
4.2
below.

Algorithm 2

A strongly convergent inertial variant of the Korpolevich extragradient method for solving (
34
)

Input:

x_{0}=x_{-1}\in{\mathcal{H}}
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\lambda=\sigma/L

for

k=0,1,2,\dots

do

Choose
\alpha_{k},\beta_{k}\geq 0

5
Strongly convergent inertial variants of Tseng’s modified forward-backward and forward-backward methods

In this section, we consider the structured monotone inclusion problem (

Assuming that the resolvent

•

A strongly convergent inertial variant of the Tseng’s forward-backward method
[

•

A modification of the forward-backward method for the case where

\displaystyle\langle x-y\,,\,F(x)-F(y)\rangle\geq\dfrac{1}{L}\|{F(x)-F(y)}\|^{2}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad x,y\in{\mathcal{H}},

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{j}\leq\frac{d_{0}^{2}L}{k+1}\left(\frac{4\sigma^{2}(1+\overline{\alpha})\left[(1+\overline{\beta})\left(1+\overline{\alpha}(1+\overline{\beta})\right)+\overline{s}\right]}{2(1-\sigma^{2})^{2}\sigma^{2}}\right).

(61c)

Proof.

The proof follows the same outline of Theorem

Appendix A
Well-definedness of Algorithm
1

In this appendix, we will prove the well-definedness of Algorithm
1
(note that
x_{k+1}
as in (
20
) depends on the nonemptyness of the set
H_{k}\cap W_{k}
).
Recall that we are assuming that the solution
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)
of (
16
) is nonempty.
The proof of the following proposition follows the same outline of
[
32
, Proposition 3]
.

Proposition A.1
.

Suppose
Algorithm
1
reaches the iteration
k
and
x_{k}\in M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})
, where

\displaystyle M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})\coloneqq\left\{x\mid\langle z-x\,,\,x_{0}-x\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\;\;z\in\mathcal{S}\right\}.

(65)

Then the following statements hold:

(a)

\mathcal{S}\subset H_{k}\cap W_{k}
.

(b)

x_{k+1}
is well-defined and
x_{k+1}\in M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})
.

Proof.

(a)
Since Algorithm
1
reaches iteration
k
, it follows that
H_{k}
and
W_{k}
as in (
19
)
are well-defined. Moreover, using the inclusion in (
18
) and the definition of
T^{\varepsilon}
as in (
7
) we conclude that
\mathcal{S}\subset H_{k}
.
Since, by assumption,
x_{k}\in M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})
, it follows from (
65
) that

\langle z-x_{k}\,,\,x_{0}-x_{k}\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad z\in\mathcal{S},

which is to say that
\mathcal{S}\subset W_{k}
(see (
19
)). Therefore,
\mathcal{S}\subset H_{k}\cap W_{k}
.

(b)
Since
\mathcal{S}\neq\emptyset
, from item (a) we obtain
H_{k}\cap W_{k}\neq\emptyset
, and so the next iterate
x_{k+1}
is well-defined (
H_{k}\cap W_{k}
is trivially closed and convex).
Using the fact that
x_{k+1}
is the projection of
x_{0}
onto
H_{k}\cap W_{k}
, from (
15
) we have

\langle z-x_{k+1}\,,\,x_{0}-x_{k+1}\rangle\leq 0\quad\mbox{for all}\quad z\in H_{k}\cap W_{k}.

As
\mathcal{S}\subset H_{k}\cap W_{k}
, the above inequality then holds for all
z\in\mathcal{S}
, implying that

x_{k+1}\in M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})
. This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

As a consequence of the proposition above, we have that the whole algorithm is well-defined.

Corollary A.2

(Well-definedness of Algorithm
1
)
.

We have that
Algorithm 1
is well-defined and generates “infinite” sequences.
Furthermore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}\subset H_{k}\cap W_{k}\quad\mbox{for all}\quad k\geq 0,

(66)

where
\mathcal{S}\coloneqq T^{-1}(0)\neq\emptyset
is the solution set of (
16
).

Proof.

Note that
x_{0}\in M_{\mathcal{S}}(x_{0})
, apply induction on
k\geq 0
, and use Proposition
A.1
.
∎

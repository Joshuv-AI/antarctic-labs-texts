The subgroup structure of pseudo-reductive groups
Michael Bate, Ben Martin, Gerhard Röhrle, Damian Sercombe

Abstract
. Let k k be a field. We investigate the relationship between subgroups of a pseudo-reductive k k -group G G and its maximal reductive quotient G ′ G^{\prime} , with applications to the subgroup structure of G G . Let k ′ / k k^{\prime}/k be the minimal field of definition for the geometric unipotent radical of G G , and let π ′ : G k ′ → G ′ \pi^{\prime}:G_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime} be the quotient map. We first characterise those smooth subgroups H H of G G for which π ′ ​ ( H k ′ ) = G ′ \pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})=G^{\prime} . We next consider the following questions: given a subgroup H ′ H^{\prime} of G ′ G^{\prime} , does there exist a subgroup H H of G G such that π ′ ​ ( H k ′ ) = H ′ \pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})=H^{\prime} , and if H ′ H^{\prime} is smooth can we find such a H H that is smooth? We find sufficient conditions for a positive answer to these questions. In general there are various obstructions to the existence of such a subgroup H H , which we illustrate with several examples. Finally, we apply these results to relate the maximal smooth subgroups of G G with those of G ′ G^{\prime} .



1.
Introduction

A fundamental problem in group theory is to describe the subgroup structure of a given group. In this paper we study the subgroup structure of pseudo-reductive groups over a field
k
.
Pseudo-reductive groups have received considerable attention over the last decade and a half
[
BRSS
]
,
[
CGP
]
,
[
CP
]
,
[
CP1
]
.

Let
G
be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group. Let
\overline{k}
be an algebraic closure of
k
, and let
k^{\sep}
be the separable closure of
k
in
\overline{k}
. Let
k^{\prime}/k
be the minimal field of definition for the geometric unipotent radical
\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})
. Then
\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})
descends to
k^{\prime}
, and
G^{\prime}:=G_{k^{\prime}}/\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{k^{\prime}})
is a reductive
k^{\prime}
-group. We denote the canonical projection by
\pi^{\prime}:G_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime}
, and the associated map under the adjunction of extension of scalars and Weil restriction by
i_{G}:G\rightarrow R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
. Given a (not necessarily smooth) subgroup
H
of
G
, we obtain a subgroup
H^{\prime}:=\pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})
of the reductive
k^{\prime}
-group
G^{\prime}
. This gives a mapping
{\mathcal{S}}
from the set of subgroups of
G
to the set of subgroups of
G^{\prime}
defined by
H\mapsto\pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})
.

We investigate the relationship between subgroups
H
of
G
and subgroups
H^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
. Our motivation is that it is easier to understand subgroups of a reductive group than those of a pseudo-reductive group; for example, a great deal is known about the reductive subgroups of a simple exceptional group when
k=\overline{k}

[
LS2
]
. The mapping
{\mathcal{S}}
allows us to pass from subgroups of
G
to subgroups of
G^{\prime}
. To reverse this process, we need the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
G^{\prime}
. A subgroup
H
of
G
satisfying
\pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})=H^{\prime}
is called a
levitation
of
H^{\prime}
in
G
; we say that
H^{\prime}

levitates
to
H
in
G
. If such a subgroup

exists then we say that
H^{\prime}

levitates
or
is levitating
in
G
. If, in addition,

(and hence
H^{\prime}
) is smooth then

is called a
smooth levitation
of
H^{\prime}
in
G
, and we say that
H^{\prime}

smoothly levitates
or is
smoothly levitating
(to
H
) in
G
.

Usually
{\mathcal{S}}
is not injective: that is, a given subgroup of
G^{\prime}
can admit more than one levitation. We show, however, that if
H^{\prime}
levitates then
H^{\prime}
has a unique largest levitation, and if
H^{\prime}
levitates smoothly then
H^{\prime}
has a unique largest smooth levitation (Proposition
4.1
). An obvious question is whether
{\mathcal{S}}
is surjective: that is, does every subgroup of
G^{\prime}
levitate? This is more delicate. We prove some results showing that certain subgroups of
G^{\prime}
levitate under certain additional assumptions, and we give a collection of examples that exhibit various obstructions to levitation. Some of these subtleties can occur only when the root system of
G
is non-reduced, so they are restricted to characteristic 2.

We start with the case
H^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
. Here is our first main result. Recall that a subgroup
L
of
G
is called a
Levi subgroup
if it satisfies
\smash{G_{\overline{k}}=\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})\rtimes L_{\overline{k}}}
(scheme-theoretically). We introduce a slightly weaker notion. A smooth subgroup
L
of
G
is an
almost Levi subgroup
if
\smash{G(\overline{k})=\mathscr{R}_{u}(G(\overline{k}))\rtimes L(\overline{k})}
(as abstract groups). Given an almost Levi subgroup
L
of
G
, if
L
is not a Levi subgroup then
k
has characteristic 2 and the root system of
G_{k^{\sep}}
is non-reduced.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group. Let
H
be a smooth subgroup of
G
, and denote
H^{\prime}:=\pi^{\prime}(H_{k^{\prime}})
.

(i)

Suppose

. Then
H^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
if and only if

contains an almost Levi subgroup of
G
.

(ii)

Suppose the root system of
G_{k^{\sep}}
is reduced. If

then the restriction map
\pi^{\prime}|_{H_{k^{\prime}}}:H_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime}
is smooth.

Next we turn our attention to tori.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let

be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group. Suppose the root system of

is reduced. The following are equivalent.

(a)

i_{G}(G)
contains
\mathscr{D}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime}))
.

(b)

contains every torus of

(c)

of

(d)

\pi^{\prime}(S_{k^{\prime}})=S^{\prime}

(e)

We find an example where a maximal torus of

does not levitate (Example

), and an example where a maximal torus of

).

Things are murkier when we move away from the case when

is a torus, even when the root system of

On the other hand, the following result shows that the relationship between subgroups of

is quite well-behaved if we restrict ourselves to regular smooth subgroups. (Recall that a subgroup of

Theorem 1.4
.

Let

be a pseudo-reductive

be a smooth subgroup of

. Suppose there exists a maximal torus

.

(i)

There exists a largest smooth subgroup

. Moreover

(ii)

We finish the paper by considering maximal smooth subgroups of

and

. It is not true in general that

, or that a smooth levitation of a maximal smooth subgroup of

: for instance, in Example

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
G
be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group.

(i)

Let

be a maximal smooth subgroup of

. Suppose at least one of the following conditions hold:

, or

.

(ii)

2.1.
Some structure theory

Let

If

Now assume that

[

The notions of a root system and root groups are particularly important; see

of

(resp.

) of

[

The

\mathscr{R}_{us}(G)
of

is the largest smooth connected split unipotent normal subgroup of

. If
\mathscr{R}_{us}(G)
is trivial then

is

. The group
G
is called

if it contains a split maximal torus, and

if it is non-commutative and has no nontrivial smooth connected proper normal subgroup. We say that
G
is

if

is pseudo-simple. There are natural notions of pseudo-semisimple groups and pseudo-simple components.

The geometric unipotent radical of
G
is the group
\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})
. If this group is trivial then we say that
G
is
reductive
. Note our convention is that reductive and pseudo-reductive groups are connected.

A subgroup
L
of
G
satisfying
\smash{G_{\overline{k}}=\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})\rtimes L_{\overline{k}}}
is called a

of

[

, 3.4.6]

[

[

, A.6]
), or if

[

, 7.2.2]
). We introduce a related notion.

Definition 2.1
.

A smooth subgroup
L
of

is an

if
G(\overline{k})=\mathscr{R}_{u}(G(\overline{k}))\rtimes L(\overline{k})
(as abstract groups).

Fix a cocharacter
\lambda:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow G
. For any

-algebra

, we say that
\Lim_{t\rightarrow 0}\lambda(t)g\lambda(t)^{-1}

Now specialise to the case where

and subgroups of

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose

is pseudo-reductive and

is the map just defined.
Let

be a subgroup of

. A subgroup

of

is called a

of

in

; we say that
H^{\prime}

levitates
to
H
in

. If such a subgroup

levitates
or

in

. If, in addition,

(and hence

) is smooth then

of

in

2.3.
Subgroups of a Weil restriction

In this subsection we prove two useful lemmas which relate the subgroup structure of a smooth algebraic

-group

to that of its Weil restriction

.
Keep the assumption that
k^{\prime}/k
is a finite field extension. Let

be a smooth algebraic
k^{\prime}
-group (not necessarily affine), and let
G:=R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
. If
G^{\prime}
is reductive then

is pseudo-reductive
[
CGP
, Prop. 1.1.10]
, if in addition

is purely inseparable then

is the minimal field of definition for
\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})
and

is an isomorphism
[

, Thm. 1.6.2(2)]
.
Note that most pseudo-reductive groups are obtained from a group of the form

via the so-called standard construction
[
CGP
, Thm. 1.5.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be a subgroup of
G^{\prime}
. There is a canonical inclusion
q_{G^{\prime}}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}})\subseteq H^{\prime}
, with equality if
H^{\prime}
is smooth. Consequently, if
H^{\prime}
is smooth and
R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})=R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
then
H^{\prime}=G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi^{\prime}:H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
be the inclusion. We may interpret
\smash{R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}}}
as an inclusion. Consider the
k^{\prime}
-homomorphism

\nu^{\prime}:=q_{G^{\prime}}\circ R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}}:R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime}.

That is,
\nu^{\prime}
is the restriction of
q_{G^{\prime}}
to
R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}}
. By the functoriality of
q_{G^{\prime}}
(i.e., Diagram (
7
)), we have that

\phi^{\prime}\circ q_{H^{\prime}}=q_{G^{\prime}}\circ R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}}=\nu^{\prime}.

Taking the image of this map
\nu^{\prime}
and leaving the inclusion maps as implicit gives us a canonical inclusion
q_{G^{\prime}}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}})\subseteq H^{\prime}
. If
H^{\prime}
is smooth then
q_{H^{\prime}}
is smooth and surjective, so indeed
q_{G^{\prime}}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}})=H^{\prime}
. The second assertion then follows immediately.
∎

The inclusion
q_{G^{\prime}}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})_{k^{\prime}})\subseteq H^{\prime}
in Lemma
2.3
may be strict if
H^{\prime}
is not smooth; for an example with reductive

see Example

.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
H
be a subgroup of
G
. Then there are canonical inclusions
H\subseteq R_{k^{\prime}/k}(q_{G^{\prime}}(H_{k^{\prime}}))\subseteq G
.

Proof.

Consider the subgroup
H^{\prime}:=q_{G^{\prime}}(H_{k^{\prime}})
of
G^{\prime}
. Let
\rho^{\prime}:H_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow H^{\prime}
be the
k^{\prime}
-homomorphism obtained by restricting the domain of
q_{G^{\prime}}
to
H_{k^{\prime}}
, and then restricting the codomain to its image
H^{\prime}
. Consider the
k
-homomorphism

\iota:H\rightarrow R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})

associated to
\rho^{\prime}
under the bijection in
(\ref{adjunctbij})
.

Let
\phi:H\hookrightarrow G
be the inclusion, and let
\phi^{\prime}:H^{\prime}\hookrightarrow G^{\prime}
be the inclusion induced by
\phi
. By definition
\phi^{\prime}\circ\rho^{\prime}=q_{G^{\prime}}\circ\phi_{k^{\prime}}
. The universal property of

(i.e., Diagram
(\ref{universalpropertyofjG})
) says that
\iota=R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\rho^{\prime})\circ j_{H}

(\ref{secondcounitunitequation})
that

is the identity map on

(i.e., Diagram

), we have that
R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi_{k^{\prime}})\circ j_{H}=j_{G}\circ\phi

R_{k^{\prime}/k}(\phi^{\prime})\circ\iota=\phi.

Since
\phi

∎

3.
Overgroups of almost Levi subgroups

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
, and then we illustrate it with some examples. We will need the following easy lemmas about reductive groups.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be a reductive
k
-group. Let
N
be a unipotent normal subgroup of
G
. Then
N
is infinitesimal.

Proof.

Since
G
is reductive, the subgroup of
\smash{G_{\overline{k}}}
generated by
N(\overline{k})
is finite, and so it is central in
G
. But
Z(G)
is of multiplicative type as
G
is reductive, hence
N(\overline{k})
is trivial. That is,
N
is infinitesimal.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:G_{1}\rightarrow G_{2}
be an isogeny between reductive
k
-groups. Suppose
f
restricts to an isomorphism between each corresponding pair of simple components and between maximal tori. Then
f
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Since
G_{1}
is reductive, its center
Z(G_{1})
is of multiplicative type. Hence
Z(G_{1})\cap\ker f
is trivial, as it is contained in every maximal torus of
G_{1}
. Assume
\ker f
is non-trivial. Then by assumption
\ker f
must be a non-central diagonally embedded subgroup of at least two of the simple factors of
G_{1}
. However these simple factors are all pairwise commuting, so
\ker f
centralises all of them, so it must be central. We have a contradiction. ∎

Henceforth let
G
be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group, let
k^{\prime}/k
be the minimal field of definition for
\mathscr{R}_{u}(\smash{G_{\overline{k}}})
, and let
\pi^{\prime}:G_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G_{k^{\prime}}/\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{k^{\prime}})=:G^{\prime}
be the natural projection. Recall that a smooth subgroup
L
of
G
is an almost Levi subgroup if
G(\overline{k})=\mathscr{R}_{u}(G(\overline{k}))\rtimes L(\overline{k})
(Definition
2.1
). The following result shows that this notion is only slightly more general than that of a Levi subgroup.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
L
be an almost Levi subgroup of
G
.
Then
L
is reductive, and
\pi^{\prime}
restricts to an isomorphism
Z(L_{k^{\prime}})^{\circ}\rightarrow Z(G^{\prime})^{\circ}
.
Let
S
be a pseudo-simple component of
G_{k^{\sep}}
.
Then either

(i)

L_{k^{\sep}}\cap S
is a Levi subgroup of
S
, or

(ii)

\Char(k)=2
and there exists
n:=n(S)\geq 1
such that
S
is of type
BC_{n}
,
L_{k^{\sep}}\cap S\cong\mathrm{SO}_{2n+1}
and

\smash{L_{\overline{k}}\cap S_{\overline{k}}\cap\ker\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}\cong(\alpha_{2})^{2n}}
.

In particular, if the root system of
G_{k^{\sep}}
is reduced – for instance if

– then

is a Levi subgroup of

.

Proof.

Without loss of generality we can assume that
k=k^{\sep}
,
since being a Levi subgroup or an almost Levi subgroup is invariant under base change by
k^{\sep}/k
.
Thus we may assume that
G
is pseudo-split.

We first show that
L
is reductive. By definition of an almost Levi subgroup and since

is smooth, the restriction map
\smash{\pi^{\prime}|_{L_{k^{\prime}}}:L_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G^{\prime}}
is bijective on
\smash{\overline{k}}
-points and its kernel is infinitesimal and unipotent. In other words,
\pi^{\prime}|_{L_{k^{\prime}}}
is a unipotent isogeny. Then

is connected, since
\smash{\ker\pi^{\prime}|_{L_{k^{\prime}}}}
is connected and as connectedness is preserved by group extensions. Since
\smash{\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{\overline{k}})\cap L_{\overline{k}}}
has trivial
\smash{\overline{k}}
-points and as
\smash{\pi^{\prime}_{\overline{k}}(\mathscr{R}_{u}(L_{\overline{k}}))\subseteq\mathscr{R}_{u}(G^{\prime}_{\overline{k}})=1}
, it follows that

is reductive.

Next consider the central torus
Z(L_{k^{\prime}})^{\circ}
of
L_{k^{\prime}}
. Since
\smash{\pi^{\prime}|_{L_{k^{\prime}}}}
is an isogeny between reductive
k^{\prime}
-groups, it restricts to an isogeny
Z(L_{k^{\prime}})^{\circ}\rightarrow\pi^{\prime}(Z(L_{k^{\prime}})^{\circ})=Z(G^{\prime})^{\circ}
. But
\ker\pi^{\prime}
is unipotent,
so
Z(L_{k^{\prime}})^{\circ}\rightarrow Z(G^{\prime})^{\circ}
is an isomorphism.

Let
S
be a pseudo-simple component of
G
. Since
\smash{\pi^{\prime}|_{L_{k^{\prime}}}}
is an isogeny between split reductive
k^{\prime}
-groups, it induces a bijection between the respective sets of simple components of
L_{k^{\prime}}
and
G^{\prime}
. Consider the simple component
S^{\prime}:=\pi^{\prime}(S_{k^{\prime}})
of
G^{\prime}
, and let

be the simple component of

satisfying
\pi^{\prime}((S_{0})_{k^{\prime}})=S^{\prime}
. Certainly
S_{0}\subseteq S
. Let us define
\smash{\upsilon:(S_{0})_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow S^{\prime}}
to be the composition of the inclusion
(S_{0})_{k^{\prime}}\hookrightarrow S_{k^{\prime}}
with
\pi^{\prime}|_{S_{k^{\prime}}}
. If

is an isomorphism then

is a Levi subgroup of

.

Now assume that
\upsilon
is not an isomorphism; then it is a unipotent isogeny between split simple
k
-groups.
Such isogenies were classified in
[

, Lem. 2.2]
and
[

, Thm. 2.2]
.
These results tell us that
\Char(k)=2
and there exists

such that

,

and
\ker\upsilon=(S_{0})_{k^{\prime}}\cap\ker\pi^{\prime}\cong(\alpha_{2})^{2n}
. Let

be the (direct) product of the first Frobenius kernels of the short root subgroups of

. Then

by
[

, Lem. 2.2]

(certainly

It remains to show that

That is, the inclusion

It remains to prove the second assertion of the proposition. Suppose the root system of

.

We can now prove Theorem

Recall that

is a pseudo-reductive

.

in

.

4.
Levitating subgroups

Let
G
be a pseudo-reductive
k
-group with minimal field of definition
k^{\prime}/k
for its geometric unipotent radical, and quotient map
\pi^{\prime}:G_{k^{\prime}}\rightarrow G_{k^{\prime}}/\mathscr{R}_{u}(G_{k^{\prime}}):=G^{\prime}
.
In this section we investigate the following general question:
which subgroups of
G^{\prime}
levitate in
G
, and which smooth subgroups of
G^{\prime}
levitate smoothly in
G
?

Along the way we prove Theorem
1.3
.
Before proceeding, we give a brief overview of the results below to help with navigation. Our basic idea is to consider three progressively more general cases: first, where
G=R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
is a Weil restriction; second, where
i_{G}(G)
contains
\smash{\mathscr{D}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime}))}
; finally, we consider the general case, for arbitrary pseudo-reductive
G
.

In the first case, if
H^{\prime}
is a smooth subgroup of
G^{\prime}
then Lemma
2.3
tells us that
H^{\prime}
levitates in
G
, its largest levitation being
R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})
, which moreover is smooth. However Example
4.18
shows that a non-smooth subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
need not levitate in
G
.

In the second case, it turns out that the condition that
i_{G}(G)
contains
\smash{\mathscr{D}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime}))}
is equivalent to requiring that all tori of
G^{\prime}
levitate in
G
.
If, in addition, the root system of
G_{k^{\sep}}
is reduced, this condition is equivalent to requiring that all tori of
G^{\prime}

smoothly
levitate in
G
.
This is a consequence of Theorem
1.3
and its proof. A similar statement holds if we require that
H^{\prime}
is generated by tori; see Corollary
4.11
. However, unlike when
G=R_{k^{\prime}/k}(G^{\prime})
, a smooth subgroup
H^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
need not levitate in
G
; refer to Example
4.19
.

Finally consider the general case, for arbitrary pseudo-reductive
G
. If
H^{\prime}
is a smooth connected normal subgroup of
G^{\prime}
then it smoothly levitates in
G
by
[
CGP
, 3.1.6]
. However in the absence of normality a torus of
G^{\prime}
need not levitate in
G
, and even if it does, it need not smoothly levitate in
G
. Refer to Examples
4.6
and
4.8
, respectively.

4.1.
Basic results and examples

Our first basic result shows that in case a subgroup does levitate, there is a largest levitation.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
H^{\prime}
be a subgroup of

in

in

: namely
\smash{i_{G}^{-1}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime}))}

\smash{(i_{G}^{-1}(R_{k^{\prime}/k}(H^{\prime})))^{\Sm}}

Proof.

Assume that

in

.

is the largest levitation of

in

is smooth), then

in

is

, and its largest smooth levitation is étale.

Now consider the (abstract group) homomorphism given by composing the canonical inclusion

We give a basic criterion for subgroups of

5.
Maximal subgroups

We turn to a study of maximal smooth subgroups of pseudo-reductive groups, using the results from the previous sections as a tool. There is a long history of studying maximal subgroups of a simple linear algebraic group over an algebraically closed field; in this context, “subgroup” is taken to mean “smooth subgroup”. It is known that
G
has only finitely many conjugacy classes of maximal smooth subgroups of positive dimension and there are explicit lists of these due to various authors including Borel-de Siebenthal
[
BD
]
, Dynkin
[
Dy
,
Dy1
]
, Seitz
[
Se
,
Se1
]
, Liebeck-Seitz
[
LS
,
LS1
,
LS2
]
and Testerman
[
Te
]
. This gives us a relatively good understanding of the maximal smooth subgroups of an arbitrary reductive group over an algebraically closed field. Over an imperfect field the situation is less clear – in Example
5.2
we demonstrate that a simple
k
-group can admit infinitely many isomorphism classes of positive-dimensional maximal smooth subgroups.

The smoothness requirement is indeed essential, as the following remark shows.

Remark 5.1
.

The notion of maximality is not very well-behaved if we allow non-smooth subgroups. For instance, let
G
be a split semisimple
group defined over the finite field
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
and let
H
be any proper subgroup of
G
. For
r\geq 1
, let
G_{r}
be the
r
th Frobenius kernel of
G
, a normal subgroup of
G
. Since each
G_{r}
is infinitesimal,
\dim HG_{r}=\dim H<\dim G
. Let
U
be a root group of
G
that is not contained in
H
(one must exist, since
G
is generated by its root groups). Let
n
be the smallest integer such that the finite group
U\cap H
does not contain a copy of
\alpha_{p^{n}}
. Since
U\cap G_{r}\cong\alpha_{p^{r}}
for all
r\geq 1
, we have an ascending chain of proper subgroups of
G

HG_{n}\subseteq HG_{n+1}\subseteq\cdots

which never becomes stationary. On the other hand, if
G
is a pseudo-simple group then any positive-dimensional smooth subgroup
H
of
G
lies in a maximal smooth subgroup. To see this, let
M
be a smooth proper subgroup of
G
such that
M\supseteq H
and
M
has maximal dimension. If
K
is a proper smooth subgroup of
G
and
M\subseteq K
then
K^{0}=M^{0}
, so
K\subseteq N_{G}(M^{0})
, so
K\subseteq N_{G}(M^{0})^{\Sm}
. Note that
N_{G}(M^{0})^{\Sm}
is proper as
\dim(M)>0
and
G
is simple. It follows that
N_{G}(M^{0})^{\Sm}
is a maximal smooth subgroup.

Hence we restrict ourselves to smooth subgroups in the study of maximality.

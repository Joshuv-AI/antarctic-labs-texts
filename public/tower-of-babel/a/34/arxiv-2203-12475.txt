Introduction

1.
Let
\pi

GL(m,\mathbb{A})
where
\mathbb{A}
is the ring of adèles of
\mathbb{Q}
. Let
L(s,\pi)
denote its
L
–function. More generally, we may consider a representation
\pi
of the form
\pi_{1}\times\pi_{2}\times\cdots\times\pi_{r}
where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{r}
,
\pi_{i}
is a cuspidal unitary representation of
GL(m_{i},\mathbb{A})
and

denotes induction by blocks. Thus

L(s,\pi)=L(s,\pi_{1})L(s,\pi_{2})\cdots L(s,\pi_{r}).

We will assume that
L(s,\pi)
has at most a simple pole, at
s=1
. Thus there is at most one occurence of
m_{i}=1
,
\pi_{i}(x)=|x|^{c}

(x\in\mathbb{A}^{\times})
and we then assume that
c=0

L(s,\pi_{i})=\zeta(s)
. (We can in fact consider more general
L
–functions, coming from a finite extension
F/\mathbb{Q}
. See § 2.1)

Assume
s
is a zero of
L(s,\pi)
in the critical strip :
s=\sigma+i\tau
,
0<\sigma<1
. We first assume that
\pi
contains no “zêta” factor. In this Introduction we assume that
s
is on the critical line :
\sigma=1/2
.

Theorem A.
(
\pi_{i}=\mathbb{C}

does not occur
in
\pi
)
(\sigma=1/2)

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)}{\frac{1}{2}+it-s}\Big|^{2}dt>2\pi\log 2.

In the case where
L(s,\pi)
has a zêta factor, the result is slightly

different :

Theorem B.

(\sigma=1/2)

(i)\\
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)}{\frac{1}{2}+it-s}\Big|^{2}dt>2\pi\Big(\log 2-\frac{2|\kappa|}{|1-s|}\Big)

where
\kappa
is the residue of
L(s,\pi)
at
s=1
.

(ii)\\
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)}{\frac{1}{2}+it-s}\Big|^{2}dt>\pi\log 2.

Obviously Theorem B contains Theorem A. Note that
\kappa\ll D^{\varepsilon}
for small
\varepsilon>0
,
D
being the conductor of
\pi
and the implicit constant being uniform (for
m
fixed) if
\pi
satisfies the Ramanujan hypothesis. See Iwaniec–Kowalski
[
11
, p. 160]
. Thus the first lower bound is effective.

The two next results were suggested by Peter Sarnak. They correspond to
(s=0)
in the previous statements; note that
L(s,\pi)
does not necessarily vanish at
(s=0).

Theorem C.
(
\pi_{i}=\mathbb{C}

does not occur
in
\pi
)

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)}{\frac{1}{2}+it}\Big|^{2}dt>\pi.

This theorem implies a uniform ’pseudo-
\Omega
-result’:

Corollary.

Under the same assumptions, for any

\varepsilon<1/2
,

Sup\\
_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)|}{|\frac{1}{2}+it|^{\varepsilon}}>\sqrt{2}/2(1+O(\epsilon))

where the remainder in
O(\epsilon)
is independent of
m
and
\pi
.

Of course this is
not
an
\Omega
-result, as we do not obtain the inequality for arbitrarily large values of
t
. Note that the Sup is finite according to the Lindelöf conjecture. The proof follows directly by bounding the integral by the product of the supremum (squared) and of the integral of
|\frac{1}{2}+it|^{-2+2\varepsilon}
. By considering the powers
L(s,\pi)^{r}
,
r\geq 1
(cf. the argument in the next paragraph), we can even deduce:

Corollary.

Under the same assumptions,

lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\\
Sup\\
_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\frac{|L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)|}{|\frac{1}{2}+it|^{\varepsilon}}\geq 1

uniformy with respect to
m
and
\pi
.

In the case where a residue is present, we obtain a slightly weaker result:

Theorem D.

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\Big|\frac{L(\frac{1}{2}+it,\pi)}{\frac{1}{2}+it}\Big|^{2}dt>\pi/2.

(x\rightarrow\infty)

\mathcal{M}H_{s}(w)=\frac{L(1-w,\pi)}{1-s-w}

is defined by
(0.2)

L(s,\pi)

The relevant Conjecture here is due to Friedlander–Iwaniec in the same paper
[

One interesting aspect of the duality introduced by Conjecture E is that it gives a relation between the growth of
L(s,\pi)
in vertical lines (controlled by the Lindelöf hypothesis) and the growth of

The Tate kernel for number fields, and its Fourier and Mellin transforms

1.1

Let
F
be a number field. We denote by
v
a place of
F
. Let
F_{\infty}=\prod\limits_{v|\infty}F_{v}
. The Fourier transform on
F_{\infty}
is defined place by place : on
F_{v}\cong\mathbb{R}
,

\mathcal{F}f(y)=\hat{f}(y)=\int f(x){e}^{-2i\pi xy}dy.

On
F_{v}\cong\mathbb{C}
, variables
z=x+iy
,
w=\xi+i\eta
,

\mathcal{F}f(w)=\hat{f}(w)=\int f(z)e^{-4i\pi Re(zw)}dz

where
dz=2dxdy
. (This is Tate’s self–dual normalisation, cf
[
23
]
). This also defines the Fourier transform for distributions on
F_{\infty}
.

For
x=(x_{v})\in F_{\infty}
, we write
X=\prod\limits_{v}|x_{v}|
where the absolute value is normalised
[
23
]
. Let
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{F}
be the integers of
F
,
\mathcal{D}=\mathcal{D}_{F}
its different and
\mathcal{D}^{-1}
the inverse different. Let
D=|D_{F}|
the absolute value of the discriminant ; thus
D=|N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{D}|
.

We denote by
\mathfrak{a}
a non–zero fractional ideal in
F
, and by
N\mathfrak{a}
its norm. Let
\kappa=\frac{2^{r_{1}}(2\pi)^{r_{2}}hR}{w\sqrt{|D|}}
be the residue at 1 of the zêta function
\zeta_{F}
. We define the following functions on
F_{\infty}
(depending only on
X
) for
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
s\not=0,1
:

Definition 1.1
.

For
x\in F_{\infty}
,
X=|x|
,

H_{s}(x)=X^{s-1}\sum_{N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{-s}-\frac{\kappa}{1-s}

\mathfrak{a}
runs over non zero

\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{O}
.

A_{s}(X)=\sum_{N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{-s}.

Definition 1.2
.

For

,

K_{s}(x)=D^{-1/2}X^{s-1}\sum_{\scriptstyle\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{D}^{-1}\atop\scriptstyle N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{-s}-\kappa\frac{D^{1/2}}{1-s}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume

h=h_{\infty}\otimes h_{f}

\mathcal{D}_{v}

\hat{h}_{v}=|\mathcal{D}_{v}|^{-1/2}ch(\mathcal{D}_{v}^{-1})

\begin{array}[]{rl}Z(h,s)&=\displaystyle\int_{I^{+}}h(x)|x|^{s}d^{\times}x+\int_{I^{+}}\hat{h}(x)|x|^{1-s}d^{\times}x\\
\vskip 5.69054pt\cr&-\dfrac{\kappa}{1-s}\hat{h}(0)-\dfrac{\kappa}{s}h(0).\end{array}

Here

(v\mid\infty)

(1.2)

\int_{I^{+}}h(x)|x|^{s}d^{\times}x-\frac{\kappa}{1-s}\hat{h}(0)+\int_{I^{+}}\hat{h}(x)|x|^{1-s}d^{\times}x-\frac{\kappa}{s}h(0)=0.

In this formula, we may replace the finite part of the measure

\prod\limits_{v}\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times}:=\mathcal{O}_{f}^{\times}

(and

[

(1.3)

\int_{I^{+}}h(x)|x|^{s}\mathbf{d}^{\times}x-\dfrac{\kappa D^{1/2}}{1-s}\hat{h}(0)+\int_{I^{+}}\hat{h}(x)|x|^{1-s}\mathbf{d}^{\times}x-\dfrac{\kappa D^{1/2}}{s}h(0)=0.

We now write the first part in classical terms. By (1.1)
D^{1/2}\hat{h}_{f}(0)=1
. We have

\int_{I^{+}}h(x)|x|^{s}\mathbf{d}^{\times}x=\int_{F_{\infty}}h_{\infty}(x_{\infty})|x_{\infty}|^{s-1}dx_{\infty}\int_{I_{f}}^{X}h_{f}(x_{f})|x_{f}|^{s}\mathbf{d}^{\times}x_{f}

where the second integral runs over

\{x_{f}\in I_{f}\mid|x_{f}|\geq X^{-1}\}.

The integrand is invariant by
\mathcal{O}_{f}^{\times}

(1.4)

I_{f}=\coprod_{\alpha=(\alpha_{v})}\Big(\prod_{v}\varpi_{v}^{\alpha_{v}}\mathcal{O}_{v}^{\times})

where the

If

|x_{f}|=\prod\limits_{v}q_{v}^{-\alpha_{v}}

\prod q_{v}^{\alpha_{v}}\leq X

\prod q_{v}^{-\alpha_{v}s}

\mathfrak{a}=\prod\mathfrak{p}_{v}^{\alpha_{v}}
, we see that the inner integral is

\sum_{N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{-s}.

Taking into account the residue term in (1.3), we see that the first two terms in (1.3) yield

(1.5)

\int_{F_{\infty}}h_{\infty}(x_{\infty})H_{s}(x_{\infty})dx_{\infty}.

We compute similarly the second part of (1.3). The integral is

\int_{F_{\infty}}\hat{h}_{\infty}(x_{\infty})|x_{\infty}|^{-s}dx_{\infty}\int_{I_{f}}^{X}\hat{h}_{f}(x_{f})|x_{f}|^{1-s}\mathbf{d}^{\times}x_{f}

D^{-1/2}\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{D}^{-1}\atop N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{s-1}.

Finally, the second part of (1.3) is equal to the integral of

D^{-1/2}X^{-s}\sum_{\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathcal{D}^{-1}\atop N\mathfrak{a}\leq X}N\mathfrak{a}^{s-1}-\frac{D^{1/2}\kappa}{s}.

. We have
z(\tau+h)=hz^{\prime}(\tau)+\frac{h^{2}}{2}(z_{re}^{\prime\prime}(\tau+\theta_{1}h)+iz_{im}^{\prime\prime}(\tau+\theta_{2}h))
where we have decomposed
z^{\prime\prime}
in its real and imaginary parts.
If
h\ll\tau
and
\mu
is an order of
\zeta
on (
\sigma=1/2
), for example
\mu=1/6
,
z^{\prime\prime}
, which is essentially
\zeta^{\prime\prime}
, is dominated by
\tau^{\mu+\varepsilon}
by the residue formula. Replacing
\varepsilon
by
2\varepsilon
and taking
\tau
sufficiently large, we deduce that for
u\leq h
:

|\frac{z(\tau+u)}{u}|\geq|z^{\prime}(\tau)|-\frac{u}{2}\tau^{\mu+\varepsilon},

whence

|\frac{z(\tau+u)}{u}|^{2}\geq|z^{\prime}(\tau)|^{2}-u\tau^{\mu+\varepsilon}|z^{\prime}(\tau)|

and

\int_{0}^{h}\Big|\frac{z(\tau+u)}{u}\Big|^{2}du\geq|z^{\prime}(\tau)|(h|z^{\prime}(\tau)|-\frac{h^{2}}{2}\tau^{\mu+\varepsilon}).

Choosing
h=\frac{|z^{\prime}(\tau)|}{\tau^{\mu+\varepsilon}}
, so clearly
h\ll\tau
, we see that the integral is larger than

\frac{1}{2}|z^{\prime}(\tau)|^{3}\tau^{-\mu-\varepsilon}.
Since this is uniformly bounded, we deduce

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume I(s) is uniformly bounded for s a zero on the critical line. Then, for any such zero
s=1/2+i\tau
,

\zeta^{\prime}(s)\ll\tau^{\mu/3+\varepsilon}.

Of course the zeroes are too sparse to use this argument to improve the estimate on
\zeta(s)
on the critical line. Although the estimate on
\zeta^{\prime}
is undoubtedly true, it seems unlikely that it can be so inferred.

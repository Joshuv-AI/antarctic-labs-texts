1.
Introduction

The Fourier coefficients of automorphic forms contain many
mysterious properties, especially its oscillation properties,
which have been intensively studied by many number theorists.
Let
\lambda_{F}(n)
be the normalized
Fourier coefficients of an automorphic
form
F
on
\rm GL_{n}
.
Among the many criteria for evaluating the nature of oscillation,
the summation function
\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{F}(n)
is certainly the most basic one,
and the related exponential sum
\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{F}(n)e\left(g(n)\right)
, where
as usual,
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
and
g(n)
is a real-valued function, is another important target,
with obvious application indications. In this paper, we are concerned with the oscillating behavior of the Fourier coefficients of
cusp forms on the full modular group. More precisely,
let
f
be a holomorphic Hecke cusp form of weight
k
or a Hecke-Maass cusp form
of Laplace eigenvalue
\lambda_{f}(\Delta)=1/4+\mu^{2}

(\mu>0)
for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
normalized Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
.
Then for
f
holomorphic, one has the Fourier expansion

\displaystyle f(z)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)n^{(k-1)/2}e(nz),\quad\operatorname{Im}(z)>0,

while for
f
a Maass form, we can write its Fourier expansion as

\displaystyle f(z)=\sqrt{y}\sum_{n\neq 0}\lambda_{f}(n)K_{i\mu}(2\pi|n|y)e(nx),

where
K_{i\mu}
is the modified Bessel function of the third kind.
The famous Ramanujan–Petersson conjectures assert that

\lambda_{f}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
.
This was proved by Deligne
[
2
]
for
f
holomorphic.
For
f
a Maass cusp form, the best result is
\lambda_{f}(n)\ll_{\varepsilon}n^{7/64+\varepsilon}

due to Kim and Sarnak
[
18
]
.
By Rankin–Selberg theory (see
[
3
, Proposition 19.6]
), we have the following average result

(1.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}|\lambda_{f}(n)|^{2}\ll_{\varepsilon}X(X|\mu|)^{\varepsilon}.

The Rankin-Selberg’s estimate in (
1.1
) shows that the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)

behave like constants on average. However, as
n
grows
\lambda_{f}(n)
in fact vary greatly
in sign, which can be seen in the estimate (see
[
4
,
7
]
)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)e(n\alpha)\ll_{f}X^{1/2}\log(2X),

which holds uniformly in
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.
It’s worth noting that the above estimate depends on the form
f
, whereas in certain applications
such the subconvexity problem, one may need an explicit dependence on the form
f
.
To this end, Godber
[
6
]
proved that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}

and any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(n\alpha\right)\ll X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\lambda_{f}(\Delta)^{1/4+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
. This is an improvement over the
bound
X^{1/2+\varepsilon}\lambda_{f}(\Delta)^{1/2+\varepsilon}
in
[
12
, Section 8.3]
.
For the associated quadratic exponential sums, Pitt
[
24
]
first proved
for any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}

and any
\varepsilon>0
,

(1.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n\right)\ll X^{15/16+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on the form
f
and
\varepsilon
.
Later, Liu and Ren
[
19
]
improved the above bound to
X^{7/8+\varepsilon}
.

It is interesting and useful to prove an estimate for the exponential sum in
(
1.3
), which does not depend on the form
f
. So the first aim of our paper is
to prove the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\lambda_{f}(n)
be the normalized Fourier coefficients
of a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{f}(\Delta)=1/4+\mu^{2}
.
For any
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and any
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n\right)\ll X^{7/8+\varepsilon}\lambda_{f}(\Delta)^{1/2+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
.

Remark 1
.

The methods of proving Theorem
1.1
can also be adopted for
holomorphic forms. Let
f
be a holomorphic cusp form of weight
k
for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with normalized Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
. It would have,
uniformly in
f
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n\right)\ll X^{7/8+\varepsilon}k^{1+\varepsilon}.

The proof for Theorem
1.1
is based on the ideas introduced by

[
24
]
. That is, taking two different approaches according to the
Dirichlet approximation of
\alpha
.
Let
Q\geq 1
be any given number. By Dirichlet’s theorem,
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a reduced rational number
\ell/q
with
1\leq q\leq Q

such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left|\alpha-\frac{\ell}{q}\right|\leq\frac{1}{qQ}.

For “larger”
q
, i.e., the oscillation of the exponential function is large,
we separate the Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
and the exponential function by the
\delta
-method
as in Pitt
[
24
]
and deal the resulting sum using techniques in Liu and Ren
[
19
]
. However, with the demand that we shall make clear the dependence on
f
in mind,
we need to adopt a different form of the
\delta
-method, i.e., the
\delta
-method due to
Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec (see Section 2.5). For “smaller”
q
, we deal with it as in
Pitt
[
24
]
, except for applying the result of Godber in (
1.2
).

Another purpose of our paper is to consider the summation function of
\lambda_{f}(n)
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n).

For holomorphic cusp forms, this was first studied by
Hecke
[
9
]
in 1927.
Later, Walfisz
[
26
]
proved the following estimate,
i.e.,

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll_{f,\vartheta}X^{\frac{1+\vartheta}{3}},

where
\vartheta
is exponent towards the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, i.e.,

|\lambda_{f}(n)|\leq n^{\vartheta}
.
Then by Deligne
[
2
]
, one has the estimate
O_{f,\varepsilon}(X^{1/3+\varepsilon})

for any
\varepsilon>0
.
Subsequently, Hafner and Ivić
[
8
]

removed the factor
X^{\varepsilon}
of Deligne’s result
and obtained the bound
O_{f}(X^{1/3})
.
The current best record is
O_{f}\left(X^{1/3}(\log X)^{-0.1185}\right)
due to Wu
[
27
]
.
For the case of Maass cusp forms, there are fewer results. Assuming the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture, one also has
the estimate (see for example
[
5
]
)

(1.5)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll_{f,\varepsilon}X^{1/3+\varepsilon}.

Other interesting results without assuming the Ramanujan-Petersson conjecture
but weaker than (
1.5
) can be found in
Hafner and Ivić
[
8
]
, Lü
[
20
]
,
Jiang and Lü
[
14
]
and some references therein.

Notice that all the above mentioned estimates depend on the form
f
.
So the second purpose of this paper is to strengthen the estimate in (
1.5
)
by making the dependence on the form
f
explicit. Our result is the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\lambda_{f}(n)
be the normalized Fourier coefficients
of a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with Laplacian eigenvalue
\lambda_{f}(\Delta)=1/4+\mu^{2}
.
Under the Ramanujan conjecture, we have, for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\sum_{n\leq X}\lambda_{f}(n)\ll X^{1/3+\varepsilon}\lambda_{f}(\Delta)^{4/9+\varepsilon},

where the implied constant depends only on
\varepsilon
.

Notation.

Throughout the paper,
\varepsilon
and
A
are arbitrarily small and arbitrarily large positive
numbers, respectively, which may be different at each occurrence. As usual,

e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}
and the symbol
n\sim X
means
X<n\leq 2X
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Maass cusp forms for
\rm GL_{2}

Let
f
be a Hecke-Maass cusp form for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

with Laplace eigenvalue
1/4+\mu^{2}
, with the normalized Fourier coefficients
\lambda_{f}(n)
.
For
\operatorname{Re}(s)>1
, the
L
-function associated to
f
is given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle L(s,f)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(n)}{n^{s}},

which satisfies the functional equation

(2.2)

\displaystyle L(1-s,f)=(-1)^{\eta}\gamma(s)L(s,f),

where
\eta=0
or 1 according as
f
is even or odd, and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\gamma(s)=\pi^{1-2s}\prod_{\pm}\Gamma\left(\frac{s+\eta\pm i\mu}{2}\right)\Gamma\left(\frac{1-s+\eta\pm i\mu}{2}\right)^{-1}.

2.2.
Summation formulas

We first recall the Poisson summation formula over an arithmetic progression.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\beta\in\mathbb{Z}

and
c\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. For a Schwartz function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}n\in\mathbb{Z}\\
n\equiv\beta\bmod c\end{subarray}}{\sum}f(n)=\frac{1}{c}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}\left(\frac{n}{c}\right)e\left(\frac{n\beta}{c}\right),

where
\widehat{f}(y)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e(-xy)\mathrm{d}x

is the Fourier transform of
f
.

Proof.

See e.g.
[
13
, Eq.(4.24)]
.
∎

We have the following
Voronoi formula for
\rm SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
21
, Eqs. (1.12), (1.15)]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\varphi(x)
be a smooth function compactly supported on
\mathbb{R^{+}}
.
Let
a,\overline{a},c\in\mathbb{Z}
with
c\neq 0,(a,c)=1
and

a\overline{a}\equiv 1\;(\text{{\rm mod }}c)
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)e\left(\frac{am}{c}\right)\varphi(m)=c\sum_{\pm}\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{\lambda_{f}(m)}{m}e\left(\pm\frac{\overline{a}m}{c}\right)\Psi_{\pm}\left(\frac{m}{c^{2}}\right),

where for
\sigma>-1
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Psi_{\pm}(x)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}i}\int_{(\sigma)}(\pi^{2}x)^{-s}\rho_{f}^{\pm}(s)\widetilde{\varphi}(-s)\mathrm{d}s,

with

(2.5)

\displaystyle\rho_{f}^{\pm}(s)=\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+s\pm i\mu}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{-s\pm i\mu}{2})}\pm\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2+s\pm i\mu}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1-s\pm i\mu}{2})}.

Here
\widetilde{\varphi}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(u)u^{s-1}\mathrm{d}u
is the Mellin transform of
\varphi
.

2.3.
Stirling’s formula

By Stirling asymptotic formula (see
[
23
, Section 8.4, Eq. (4.03)]
),
for
|\arg s|\leq\pi-\varepsilon
,
|s|\gg 1
and any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\ln\Gamma(s)=\left(s-\frac{1}{2}\right)\ln s-s+\frac{1}{2}\ln(2\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{K_{1}}\frac{B_{2j}}{2j(2j-1)s^{2j-1}}+O_{K_{1},\varepsilon}\left(\frac{1}{|s|^{2K_{1}+1}}\right),

where
B_{j}
are Bernoulli numbers. Thus for
s=\sigma+i\tau
,
\sigma
fixed and
|t|\geq 2
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\Gamma(\sigma+i\tau)=\sqrt{2\pi}(i\tau)^{\sigma-1/2}e^{-\pi|\tau|/2}\left(\frac{|\tau|}{e}\right)^{i\tau}\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{K_{2}}\frac{c_{j}}{\tau^{j}}+O_{\sigma,K_{2},\varepsilon}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\tau|^{K_{2}+1}}\bigg)\right),

where the constants
c_{j}
depend on
j,\sigma
and
\varepsilon
.
Thus for
\sigma\geq-1/2
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\rho_{f}^{\pm}(\sigma+i\tau)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{1+\sigma+i(\tau\pm\mu)}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{-\sigma-i(\tau\pm\mu)}{2})}\pm\prod_{\pm}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{2+\sigma+i(\tau\pm\mu)}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{1-\sigma-i(\tau\pm\mu)}{2})}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle(|\tau+\mu||\tau-\mu|)^{\sigma+1/2}.

2.4.
Estimates for exponential integrals

Let

I=\int_{\mathbb{R}}w(y)e^{i\varrho(y)}dy.

We need the following evaluation for exponential integrals
which are
Lemma 8.1 and Proposition 8.2 of
[
1
]
in the language of inert functions
(see
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
).

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an index set,
Y:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1}
and under this map

T\mapsto Y_{T}

be a function of
T\in\mathcal{F}
.
A family
\{w_{T}\}_{T\in\mathcal{F}}
of smooth
functions supported on a product of dyadic intervals in
\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d}

is called
Y
-inert if for each
j=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}

we have

\displaystyle C(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})=\sup_{T\in\mathcal{F}}\sup_{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}^{d}}Y_{T}^{-j_{1}-\cdots-j_{d}}\left|y_{1}^{j_{1}}\cdots y_{d}^{j_{d}}w_{T}^{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{d})}(y_{1},\ldots,y_{d})\right|<\infty.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
w=w_{T}(y)
is a family of
Y
-inert functions,
with compact support on
[Z,2Z]
, so that

w^{(j)}(y)\ll(Z/Y)^{-j}
. Also suppose that
\varrho
is
smooth and satisfies
\varrho^{(j)}(y)\ll H/Z^{j}
for some

H/Y^{2}\geq R\geq 1
and all
y
in the support of
w
.

(1)

If
|\varrho^{\prime}(y)|\gg H/Z
for all
y
in the support of
w
, then

I\ll_{A}ZR^{-A}
for
A
arbitrarily large.

(2)

If
\varrho^{\prime\prime}(y)\gg H/Z^{2}
for all
y
in the support of
w
,
and there exists
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\varrho^{\prime}(y_{0})=0
(note
y_{0}
is
necessarily unique), then

I=\frac{e^{i\varrho(y_{0})}}{\sqrt{\varrho^{\prime\prime}(y_{0})}}F(y_{0})+O_{A}(ZR^{-A}),

where
F(y_{0})
is an
Y
-inert function (depending on
A
) supported
on
y_{0}\asymp Z
.

We also need the
second derivative test (see
[
11
, Lemma 5.1.3]
).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\varrho(x)
be real and twice
differentiable on the open interval
[a,b]

with
\varrho^{\prime\prime}(x)\gg\lambda_{0}>0
on
[a,b]
. Let
w(x)

be real on
[a,b]
and let
V_{0}
be its total
variation on
[a,b]
plus the maximum modulus of
w(x)
on
[a,b]
.
Then

\displaystyle I\ll\frac{V_{0}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{0}}}.

2.5.
The circle method

Let
\delta:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow\{0,1\}
be defined as

\delta(0)=1
and
\delta(n)=0
for
n\neq 0
.
We will use a version of the
\delta
-method by
Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec (see
[
13
, Chapter 20]
)
which states that for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
C\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

(2.8)

\displaystyle\delta(n)=\frac{1}{C}\sum_{1\leq c\leq C}\;\frac{1}{c}\;\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{c}}e\left(\frac{na}{c}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}g(c,\zeta)e\left(\frac{n\zeta}{cC}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where the
\star
on the sum indicates
that the sum over
a
is restricted to
(a,c)=1
.
The function
g
has the following properties
(see
[
13
, (20.158), (20.159)]
and
[
10
, Lemma 15]
)

(2.9)

\displaystyle g(c,\zeta)\ll|\zeta|^{-A},\;\;\;\;\;\;g(c,\zeta)=1+O\left(\frac{C}{c}\left(\frac{c}{C}+|\zeta|\right)^{A}\right)

for any
A>1
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{j}}{\partial\zeta^{j}}g(c,\zeta)\ll|\zeta|^{-j}\min\left(|\zeta|^{-1},\frac{C}{c}\right)\log C,\quad j\geq 1.

2.6.
Some estimates

We quote the following results from
Pitt (see
[
24
, Section 2]
),
Tolev (see
[
25
, Section 2]
)
and Liu and Ren (see
[
19
, Lemma 3.3]
) respectively.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
c_{1}
be the largest square-free factor of
c\in\mathbb{N}
such that

c=c_{1}c_{2}
,
(c_{1},c_{2})=1
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\sim X}S(m,n,c)e(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n)\ll(Xc)^{1/2+\varepsilon}+\mathbf{T}^{1/2},

where

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}=(m,c)^{1/2}\tau^{2}(c_{1})c_{1}^{1/2}c_{2}\sum_{c=c_{3}c_{4}}c_{4}^{1/2}\sum_{u\bmod c_{3}\atop(u,c_{3})=1}\sum_{1\leq h<X}\min\left\{X,\left(2\left\|2\alpha h+\frac{u}{c_{3}}\right\|\right)^{-1}\right\}.

Here
\|x\|
denote the distance from
x
to the nearest integer.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
M\geq 2
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a smooth function

G(M,x)
, which is periodic with period one and satisfies

\begin{split}\min(M,\|x\|^{-1})\leq G(M,x).\end{split}

Moreover,
G(M,x)
has a Fourier expansion

\begin{split}G(M,x)=\sum_{n}b(n)e(nx)\end{split}

with coefficients satisfying
b(n)\ll\log M
and

\begin{split}\sum_{|n|>M^{1+\varepsilon}}|b(n)|\ll_{A,\varepsilon}M^{-A}\end{split}

for any constant
A>0
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
f(n)
denote the largest square-free factor of
n
such that
(f(n),n/f(n))=1
. Then we have

\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq X}f^{-1/4}(n)\ll X^{3/4}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\sum_{n\leq X}f^{-1/2}(n)\ll X^{1/2}\log X.\end{split}

We also need the following estimate (see Karatsuba
[
16
, Chapter VI, §2, Lemma 5]
).

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

\displaystyle\alpha=\frac{\ell}{q}+\frac{\theta}{q^{2}},\quad(\ell,q)=1,\quad q\geq 1,\quad|\theta|\leq 1.

Then for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
U>0
and
P\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{x=1}^{P}\min\left\{U,\left\|\alpha x+\beta\right\|^{-1}\right\}\leq 6\left(\frac{P}{q}+1\right)(U+q\log q).

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We assume
\mu>X^{\varepsilon}
, otherwise Theorem
1.1
follows from Liu and Ren
[
19
]
.
By dyadic subdivision it suffices to prove the required estimate for the sum

\displaystyle S(X,\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{n\sim X}\lambda_{f}(n)e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n\right).

Let
V(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(3/4,9/4)
be identically one
on
[1,2]
with derivatives satisfying
V^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
for any integer
j\geq 0
.
Then we can write
S(X,\alpha,\beta)
as

(3.1)

\displaystyle S(X,\alpha,\beta)=\sum_{n\sim X}e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n\right)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)V\left(\frac{m}{X}\right)\delta(n-m),

where
\delta(n)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &1\,\textit{ if }n=0,\\
&0\,\textit{ if }n\neq 0\end{aligned}\right.
is the Kronecker delta function.

Plugging the identity (
2.8
) for

\delta(n)
into (
3.1
) and
exchanging the order of integration and summations,
we get

\displaystyle S(X,\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sum_{1\leq c\leq C}\frac{g(c,\zeta)}{c}\sum_{n\sim X}e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n+\frac{n\zeta}{cC}\right)\;

\displaystyle\times\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{c}}e\left(\frac{na}{c}\right)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)e\left(-\frac{ma}{c}\right)V\left(\frac{m}{X}\right)e\left(-\frac{m\zeta}{cC}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta,

where
C>1
is a parameter to be chosen later.
Note that the contribution from
|\zeta|\leq X^{-G}
for
G>0
sufficiently large
is negligible.
Moreover, by the first property in (
2.9
), we can restrict
\zeta
in the range

|\zeta|\leq X^{\varepsilon}
up to an negligible error. So we can
insert a smooth partition of unity for the
\zeta
-integral and write
S(X,\alpha,\beta)
as

\displaystyle S(X,\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{X^{-G}\ll\Xi\ll X^{\varepsilon}\atop\text{dyadic}}\frac{1}{C}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varpi\left(\frac{\zeta}{\Xi}\right)\sum_{1\leq c\leq C}\frac{g(c,\zeta)}{c}\sum_{n\sim X}e\left(\alpha n^{2}+\beta n+\frac{n\zeta}{cC}\right)\;

\displaystyle\times\sideset{}{{}^{\star}}{\sum}_{a\bmod{c}}e\left(\frac{na}{c}\right)\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{f}(m)e\left(-\frac{ma}{c}\right)V\left(\frac{m}{X}\right)e\left(-\frac{m\zeta}{cC}\right)\mathrm{d}\zeta+O_{A}(X^{-A}),

where
\varpi(x)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(1,2)

satisfying
\varpi^{(j)}(x)\ll_{j}1
for any integer
j\geq 0
.

By dyadic subdivision, we only need to estimate the sum

Assume the Ramanujan–Petersson conjecture
\lambda_{f}(n)\ll n^{\varepsilon}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\sim X}\lambda_{f}(n)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\lambda_{f}(n)h\left(\frac{n}{X}\right)+O\big(\eta X^{1+\varepsilon}\big).

By Mellin inversion, one has

h(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{h}(s)x^{-s}\mathrm{d}s,

where
\widetilde{h}(s)=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(x)x^{s-1}\mathrm{d}x
.
It follows that

(4.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\sim X}\lambda_{f}(n)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(2)}\widetilde{h}(s)L(s,f)X^{s}\mathrm{d}s+O\big(\eta X^{1+\varepsilon}\big).

By shifting the line of integration in (
4.1
) to

(s)=-\varepsilon
, we get

(4.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\sim X}\lambda_{f}(n)=\frac{X^{-\varepsilon}}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}X^{it}\widetilde{h}(-\varepsilon+it)L(-\varepsilon+it,f)\mathrm{d}t+O\big(\eta X^{1+\varepsilon}\big).

Repeated integration by parts shows that for any integer
j\geq 1
,

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{h}(s)=\frac{(-1)^{j}}{s(s+1)\cdots(s+j-1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}h^{(j)}(x)x^{s+j-1}\mathrm{d}x\ll\eta(\eta|s|)^{-j}.

Thus for

\displaystyle\int_{|t|\geq T}X^{it}\widetilde{h}(-\varepsilon+it)L(-\varepsilon+it,f)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\lambda_{f}(\Delta)^{1/2+\varepsilon}\eta T^{2}(\eta T)^{-j},

where we have used the convexity bound (see

\displaystyle L(\sigma+it,f)\ll_{\sigma,\varepsilon}(\left(1+|t+\mu|\right)\left(1+|t-\mu|\right))^{(1-\sigma)/2+\varepsilon}.

Consequently, by taking

Moreover, by taking

in (

),

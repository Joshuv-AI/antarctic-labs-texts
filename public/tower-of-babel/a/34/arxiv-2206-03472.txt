Explicit construction of 𝑵 = 𝟐 N=2 SCFT orbifold models. Spectral flow and mutual locality Dedication: In memory of Yaroslav Pugai
Alexander Belavin, Vladimir Belavin, Sergey Parkhomenko

Abstract
In this work we present a new approach to constructing Calabi-Yau orbifold models required for compactification in superstring theory. We use the connection of CY orbifolds with the class of exactly solvable N=2 SCFT models to explicitly construct a complete set of fields in these models using the twisting of the spectral flow and the requirement of mutual locality of the fields.



1
Introduction

As is known ten-dimensional superstring theory is a candidate for unification of the Standard Model and quantum gravity.
To get a four-dimensional theory with Space-Time supersymmetry
(which is necessary for phenomenological reasons), we must compactify the 6 of 10 dimensions on the Calabi-Yau manifold, as shown by Candelas et al.
CHSW
.
Another equivalent approach is the compactification of 6 dimensions into
N=2
Superconformal field theory with the central charge
c=9
, as shown by D. Gepner
Gep
.

Each of these two equivalent approaches has its own merits.
Gepner’s approach makes it possible to use exactly solvable N=2 SCFT minimal models to construct new ones that are also exactly solvable.
The idea is to use the connection of CY-orbifold models with the class of exactly solvable minimal
N=2
SCFT models to explicitly construct a complete set of fields in the models
using Spectral flow transformation
SS
and the requirement of mutual locality of fields.

The large class of CY manifolds can be defined
in the weighted projective space

\mathbb{P}_{n_{1},...,n_{5}}
in terms of zeros of the quasi-homogeneous polynomials of Fermat type

\displaystyle W(x_{i}):=\sum_{i=1}^{5}x_{i}^{k_{i}+2},and\ W(\lambda^{n_{i}}x_{i})=\lambda^{d}W(x_{i}),\ \text{where}\ d=\sum_{i}n_{i}.

(1)

The last equality, which is the CY condition, can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{5}{1\over k_{i}+2}=1.

(2)

One can also obtain new CY-manifolds as orbifolds of the previous ones by quotienting them over the so-called admissible group defined as follows

\displaystyle G_{adm}\subset G_{tot}=\prod_{i}\mathbb{Z}_{k_{i}+2},\ x_{i}\rightarrow\omega_{i}^{w_{i}}x_{i},\ \text{where}\ \omega_{i}^{k_{i}+2}=1,

(3)

\displaystyle\ \sum_{i}{w_{i}\over k_{i}+2}\in\mathbb{Z},\ \text{and the element}\ (1,1,1,1,1)\in G_{adm}

It is important that
G_{adm}
preserves the product of
x_{i}
, which is equivalent to preserving a nonvanishing holomorphic
3
-form on the CY manofold.

The geometric approach to Calabi-Yau of Fermat class is equivalent to
N=2
SCFT approach with
c=9
, realized in terms of the product
\prod_{i=1}^{5}M_{k_{i}}
,
where
M_{k_{i}}
is Minimal model of
N=2
SCFT with
c_{i}={3k_{i}\over k_{i}+2}
and

\displaystyle c=\sum_{i}c_{i}=9.

(4)

The last equality coincides with CY condition above.

Thus, Gepner’s construction, along with the theory of free fields of transverse degrees of freedom of a superstring in 4-dimensional Minkowski space also contains a product of
N=2
Minimal models.

In this regard, it makes sense to separate the space-time degrees of freedom from the degrees of freedom of the compact sector and study orbifolds of products of
N=2
superconformal minimal models as
N=2

\sigma
-models
of SCFT on Calabi-Yau manifolds. This problem has been discussed in a number of works (see for example
EOTY
), but in our opinion it remains largely unexplored.
A recent paper
BP
provides some examples of such models built using a new explicit field construction approach to Calabi-Yau orbifolds of Fermat
type.

In this paper we continue the investigation of the orbifolds of tensor products of
N=2
minimal models.
We present a more general construction of orbifold model fields based on a different point of view than that used in
BP
. Namely, we show how to use such products of exactly solvable

N=2
minimal models in order to explicitly construct a set of fields in the orbifold models and thus to obtain new exactly solvable
N=2
SCFT models with the central charge equal to
9
.

For a few examples, we construct chiral and anti-chiral rings and show that their dimensions are consistent with the dimensions of the cohomology groups of mutually mirror CY-manifolds, predicted by D. Gepner
DG
,

COGP
and discovered in
CLS
,
GrPl
, (see also
LS
).

The plan of the paper is as follows.

In Section
2
we consider some known facts about the representation theory
of the
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra,
N=(2,2)
superconfomal field theory models, and
N=(2,2)
minimal models needed in what follows.
We also present the construction of all primary fields of the minimal models by twisting the spectral flow from chiral primary fields.
In section
3
we consider the compositions of the
N=(2,2)
minimal models and introduce the so called admissible group for the orbifold construction.
Then we generalize the construction of the spectral flow of primary fields defined in the previous section to the case of composite models
and construct explicitly the complete set of fields of the orbifold model from the requirement of mutual locality.

In section
4
we prove that OPE of the orbifold model is closed and that all other conformal bootstrap axioms hold including the modular invariance of the partition function.
In section
5
the spectral flow construction (defined in the previous section) is specified to find all
(c,c)
and
(a,c)
chiral rings of the model.
In section
6
we find these rings for several examples of orbifolds and show that they coincide with corresponding components of the cohomology groups of mutually mirrored CY-manifolds.
The last fact confirms the connection of the constructed orbifold models with the corresponding
\sigma
-models on CY-manifolds.
Section
7
is a conclusion in which we discuss some open questions.

2
Preliminaries

The
N=2
super Virasoro algebra and its representations.

Here we recall some general (quite known) properties of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra and its representations, which are necessary for our further construction.

The commutation relations for the generators
L_{n},J_{n},G^{\pm}_{r}

of
N=2
Super-Virasoro algebra have the form

\displaystyle[L_{n},L_{m}]=(n-m)L_{n+m}+{c\over 12}(n^{3}-n)\delta_{n+m,0},

(5)

\displaystyle[J_{n},J_{m}]={c\over 3}n\delta_{n+m,0},

\displaystyle[L_{n},J_{m}]=-mJ_{n+m},

\displaystyle\{G^{+}_{r},G^{-}_{s}\}=L_{r+s}+{r-s\over 2}J_{r+s}+{c\over 6}(r^{2}-{1\over 4})\delta_{r+s,0},

\displaystyle[L_{n},G^{\pm}_{r}]=({n\over 2}-r)G^{\pm}_{r+n},

\displaystyle[J_{n},G^{\pm}_{r}]=\pm G^{\pm}_{r+n}.

The representations of the
N=2
superalgebra split into two sectors. In the first sector, which is called the Nevew-Schwartz sector (
NS
)
the values of the modes
r,s
of the fermionic generators are half-integers. In the second so called Ramond sector (
R
) the modes
r,s
of the fermionic currents take integer values.

Note that the second and third commutation relations mean that
J_{n}
are the Fourier modes of the free bosonic
U(1)
-current
J(z)
,
which can be written as

\displaystyle J(z)=\imath\sqrt{c\over 3}{\partial\phi\over\partial z},

(6)

where
\phi(z)
is free massles scalar field.

In both sectors there exist highest weight representations.
In the
NS
sector, the highest weight representation is generated from the highest weight vector
\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}
, which is called the primary state in standard terminology.
This state is determined by the relations

\displaystyle L_{n}\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}=0,\ J_{n}\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}=0,\ n\geq 1,\ G^{\pm}_{r}\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}=0,\ r\geq{1\over 2},

(7)

\displaystyle L_{0}\Phi_{(\Delta,Q)}=\Delta\Phi_{(\Delta,Q)},\ J_{0}\Phi_{(\Delta,Q)}=Q\Phi_{(\Delta,Q)}.

Thus,
\Delta
and
Q
are the conformal dimension and
U(1)
charge of the primary state
\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}
.
The descendant states are generated from the primary state above by the creation generators of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra.
We form in this way a Verma module which is reducible in general.
The corresponding irreducible highest weight representation
{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}
is given by a quotient of the Verma module by its submodules.

The primary state (highest weight vector)
\Phi^{R}_{\Delta,Q}
in the
R
sector has similar properties, but the annihilation conditions for fermionic generators are slightly different:

\displaystyle G^{\pm}_{n}\Phi^{R}_{\Delta,Q}=0,\ n\geq 1,\quad G^{+}_{0}\Phi^{R}_{\Delta,Q}=0.

(8)

Applying to the state
\Phi^{R}_{\Delta,Q}
the creation generators of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra we form a Verma module. The corresponding highest weight representation
{\cal{H}}^{R}_{\Delta,Q}
is certain quotient of the Verma module by its submodules.
The structure of Verma modules of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra has been analyzed in
VPY
.

A special class of representations in
NS
sector arise for primary states which are subject to an additional constraint

\displaystyle G^{+}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}=0.

(9)

Such states are called chiral primary states.

Similarly, if the primary state, in addition to (
7
), satisfies

\displaystyle G^{-}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}=0,

(10)

then it is called anti-chiral primary state.

Chiral and anti-chiral primary states, which were first discussed in
LVW
, have special properties. Namely, due to the anti-commutators
from (
5
) the dimension and charge of the chiral primary state are not independent:

\displaystyle Q=2\Delta.

(11)

For the anti-chiral primary state

\displaystyle Q=-2\Delta.

(12)

It is possible to continuously connect
NS
sector to the
R
sector using one-parametric family of automorphisms of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra

LVW
known as spectral flow:

\displaystyle\tilde{G}^{\pm}_{r}=U^{-t}G^{\pm}_{r}U^{t}=G^{\pm}_{r\pm t},

(13)

\displaystyle\tilde{J}_{n}=U^{-t}J_{n}U^{t}=J_{n}+{c\over 3}t\delta_{n,0},

\displaystyle\tilde{L}_{n}=U^{-t}L_{n}U^{t}=L_{n}+tJ_{n}+{c\over 6}t^{2}\delta_{n,0}.

So, one can see that for
t\in\mathbb{Z}+{1\over 2}
the spectral flow interpolates between
NS
and
R
sectors and for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
it takes
NS
to
NS
and
R
to
R
. It makes sense to emphasize that as a result of the spectral flow transformation , we obtain a new, so-called twisted representation

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}^{t}_{\Delta,Q}=U^{t}{\cal{H}}_{\Delta,Q}.

(14)

It allows in particular to define
R
sector representations as the
{1\over 2}
-twisted
NS
sector representations:

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}^{R}_{\Delta_{R},Q_{R}}=U^{{1\over 2}}{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\Delta_{NS},Q_{NS}},

(15)

where

\displaystyle\Delta_{R}=\Delta_{NS}+{Q_{NS}\over 2}+{c\over 24},Q_{R}=Q_{NS}+{c\over 6}.

(16)

For the construction that will be discussed in what follows it is important that not only
U(1)
current can be expressed by the free scalar (
6
), but the generator of the spectral flow
U
is also expressed in terms of the free scalar field
\phi(z)
:

\displaystyle U=\exp{(\imath\sqrt{c\over 3}\phi(z))}.

(17)

Minimal representations.

In the case when the central charge takes one of the values

\displaystyle c={3k\over k+2},\ k=0,1,2,....

(18)

there exists a finite set of irreducible integrable unitary representations of
N=2
super Virasoro, numerated by pairs of numbers
l,q

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{l,q},\ {\cal{H}}^{R}_{l,q},\ l=0,1,...,k,\ q=-l,-l+2,...,l.

(19)

We will call them minimal representations.
The conformal dimensions and charges of the corresponding primary
states
\Phi^{NS}_{l,q}
in
NS
sector are

\displaystyle\Delta_{l,q}=\frac{l(l+2)-q^{2}}{4(k+2)},\ \ Q_{l,q}=\frac{q}{k+2}.

(20)

In
R
sector the dimensions and charges of primary states
\Phi^{R}_{l,q}
are given by

\displaystyle\Delta^{R}_{l,q}=\frac{l(l+2)-(q-1)^{2}}{4(k+2)}+\frac{1}{8},\quad\bar{Q}^{R}_{\l,q}=Q^{R}_{l,q}=\frac{q-1}{k+2}+\frac{1}{2}.

(21)

Among the primary states the chiral primary states appear when
q=l
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{c}_{l}\equiv\Phi^{NS}_{l,l},\quad\Phi^{a}_{l}\equiv\Phi^{NS}_{l,-l},

(22)

while anti-chiral primary states appear when
q=-l
:

\displaystyle\Phi^{a}_{l}\equiv\Phi^{NS}_{l,-l}.

(23)

Spectral flow construction of primary states for minimal representations.

As we already mentioned the spectral flow automorphism allows one to relate
NS
representations to
R
representations.
For the minimal representations (
19
) the spectral flow play much more prominent role
FST
.
We now show that all primaries from (
19
) can be explicitly constructed from chiral primaries (
22
) using spectral flow automorphism (
13
).

We start by the spectral flow construction an anti-chiral primary state. It uses the so called extremal vector of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra representation.
The extremal vector we are dealing with is a special descendant state in the irreducible representation of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra generated from the chiral primary state
\Phi^{c}_{l}
as

\displaystyle E^{-}_{l}=G^{-}_{{1\over 2}-l}...G^{-}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{c}_{l},

(24)

\displaystyle G^{+}_{r}E^{-}_{l}=0,\,\,\ r\geq{1\over 2}+l,\quad G^{-}_{r}E^{-}_{l}=0,\ \ r\geq-{1\over 2}-l,

\displaystyle J_{n}E^{-}_{l}=L_{n}E^{-}_{l}=0,\ \ n\geq 1.

The extremal vector
E^{-}_{l}
is associated with one of the possible Borel subalgebras in the
N=2
Super-Virasoro algebra generated by

\displaystyle G^{+}_{r},r\geq{1\over 2}+l,\quad G^{-}_{r},r\geq-{1\over 2}-l,\quad J_{n},\ L_{n},n\geq 0.

(25)

The vector
E^{-}_{l}
is isomorphic to the anti-chiral primary
state
\Phi^{a}_{l}
.
The isomorphism is given by the following spectral flow construction

\displaystyle U^{l}E^{-}_{l}=\Phi^{a}_{l}.

(26)

This two-stage procedure can be applied to construct all primary states of the
NS
sector.
Let us consider an example of the descendant state
G^{-}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{c}_{l}
.
By direct calculations one can show that the state

\displaystyle UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{c}_{l}

(27)

gives the spectral flow realization of the primary state
\Phi_{l,l-2}
.

More generally, the state

\displaystyle\Phi_{l,t}=U^{t}G^{-}_{-t+{1\over 2}}...G^{-}_{-{1\over 2}}\Phi^{c}_{l},\quad 0\leq t\leq l.

(28)

gives spectral flow realization of the primary state
\Phi_{l,q}
, where
q=l-2t
. Using (
13
) this expression can be represented in the following form

\displaystyle\Phi_{l,t}=(UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}})^{t}\Phi^{c}_{l},\quad 0\leq t\leq l.

(29)

For the purposes of the orbifold construction we need to extend this formula.
Namely, we define the vector

\displaystyle\Phi_{l,t}=(UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}})^{t-l-1}U(UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}})^{l}\Phi^{c}_{l},\quad l+1\leq t\leq k+1.

(30)

It can be checked that the state
\Phi_{l,t}
gives the spectral flow realization of the primary state
\Phi_{\tilde{l},\tilde{q}}
, where
\tilde{l}=k-l
,
\tilde{q}=k+2+l-2t
.

In fact, this formula can be extended. Indeed, if consider the vector

\displaystyle U(UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}})^{k-l}U(UG^{-}_{-{1\over 2}})^{l}\Phi^{c}_{l}(z),

(31)

then it is easy to verify that in this way we obtain another realization of the initial chiral primary state
\Phi^{c}_{l}
.
Thus, making
k+2
steps, we return to the original representation of
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra.
So, for the spectral flow the following periodicity property holds

\displaystyle U^{k+2}\approx 1.

(32)

In what follows we will use the spectral flow parameter
t
instead of
q
in order to stress the spectral flow realization of primary states
(
29
), (
30
).

The construction of the spectral flow of the primary states in the
R
-sector can be obtained by applying the operator
U^{1\over 2}
to the expressions (
29
), (
30
).

The characters.

As was shown in
Gep
it is convenient to split the space of each irreducible representation of
N=2
algebra into two subspace that are representations of the subalgebra generated by an even number of the fermionic generators
G^{\pm}
.

Then for the minimal representations (
19
) we can define the characters

\displaystyle NS_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(-\imath 2\pi\epsilon)}Tr_{{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{l,q}}(\exp{(\imath 2\pi(L_{0}-{c\over 24})\tau+\imath 2\pi J_{0}\theta)}),

(33)

\displaystyle\tilde{NS}_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(-\imath 2\pi\epsilon)}Tr_{{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{l,q}}((-1)^{F}\exp{(\imath 2\pi(L_{0}-{c\over 24})\tau+\imath 2\pi J_{0}\theta)}),

\displaystyle R_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(-\imath 2\pi\epsilon)}Tr_{{\cal{H}}^{R}_{l,q}}(\exp{(\imath 2\pi(L_{0}-{c\over 24})\tau+\imath 2\pi J_{0}\theta)}),

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(-\imath 2\pi\epsilon)}Tr_{{\cal{H}}^{R}_{l,q}}((-1)^{F}\exp{(\imath 2\pi(L_{0}-{c\over 24})\tau+\imath 2\pi J_{0}\theta)}),

where
(-1)^{F}
is fermionic number operator.

The modular transformation properties of characters, which we use below, were obtained in
Gep
, look as follows:

\displaystyle NS_{l,q}(\tau+1,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(\imath 2\pi(\Delta_{l,q}-{c\over 24}))}\tilde{NS}_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

(34)

\displaystyle\tilde{NS}_{l,q}(\tau+1,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(\imath 2\pi(\Delta_{l,q}-{c\over 24}))}NS_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle R_{l,q}(\tau+1,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(\imath 2\pi(\Delta^{R}_{l,q}-{c\over 24}))}R_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{l,q}(\tau+1,\theta,\epsilon)=\exp{(\imath 2\pi(\Delta^{R}_{l,q}-{c\over 24}))}\tilde{R}_{l,q}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle NS_{l,q}(-{1\over\tau},{\theta\over\tau},\epsilon+{c\theta^{2}\over 6\tau})=\sum_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}S^{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{l,q}NS_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\tilde{NS}_{l,q}(-{1\over\tau},{\theta\over\tau},\epsilon+{c\theta^{2}\over 6\tau})=\sum_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}S^{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}-1}_{l,q}R_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle R_{l,q}(-{1\over\tau},{\theta\over\tau},\epsilon+{c\theta^{2}\over 6\tau})=\sum_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}S^{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{l,q-1}\tilde{NS}_{l^{\prime}q^{\prime}}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

\displaystyle\tilde{R}_{l,q}(-{1\over\tau},{\theta\over\tau},\epsilon+{c\theta^{2}\over 6\tau})=-\imath\sum_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}S^{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}-1}_{l,q-1}\tilde{R}_{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}(\tau,\theta,\epsilon),

where the S-matrix

\displaystyle S^{l^{\prime},q^{\prime}}_{l,t}=\sin{(\pi{(l+1)(l^{\prime}+1)\over k+2})}\exp{(\imath\pi{qq^{\prime}\over k+2})}.

(35)

It follows that the characters of the
N=2
minimal model form a unitary representation of the modular group
Gep
.

Models of
N=(2,2)
Superconformal Field Theory.

Like any models of CFT, the
N=(2,2)
superconformal models are characterized by two copies of their symmetry algebra.

The space of states of
N=(2,2)
superconformal field theory consists of the local fields, which form the products of representations of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic
N=2
Virasoro superalgebras. So the total space of states can be written in the form

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}={\cal{H}}^{NS}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}\oplus{\cal{H}}^{R}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{R},

(36)

where

\displaystyle{\cal{\displaystyle H}}^{NS}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}=\oplus_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}\oplus_{Q,\bar{Q}}{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q}},

(37)

\displaystyle{\cal{\displaystyle H}}^{R}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{R}=\oplus_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}\oplus_{Q,\bar{Q}}{\cal{H}}^{R}_{\Delta,Q}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{R}_{\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q}}

and
{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\Delta,Q}
(
\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q}}
) is a highest weight
(\Delta,Q)
(
\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q}
) representation of the holomorphic (anti-holomorphic)
N=2
superalalgebra Virasoro in
NS
(
\overline{NS}
) sector considered above. The similar meaning has the expansion for
{\cal{H}}^{R}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{R}
.

The primary fields
\Psi_{(\Delta,Q)(\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q})}(z,\bar{z})
are the primary states w.r.t the holomorphic and anti-holomorphic
N=2
Virasoro superalgebras. These fields can be considered as a proper products of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic factors:

\displaystyle\Psi_{(\Delta,Q)(\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q})}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi_{\Delta,Q}(z)\bar{\Phi}_{\bar{\Delta},\bar{Q}}(\bar{z}).

(38)

The rest local fields are generated from the primary fields by applying creation generators of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic
N=2
Virasoro superalgebras. OPE of the local fields have to be closed and associative as required by Conformal Bootstrap axioms
BPZ
.

Among the primary fields there are special ones, which corresponds to the chiral and anti-chiral primary states. Thus, in general
N=(2,2)
superconformal model there are 4 types of special primary fields depending on whether their holomorphic and anti-holomorphic factors are the chiral or anti-chiral states:

\displaystyle\Psi^{cc}_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{c}_{\Delta}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{c}_{\bar{\Delta}}(\bar{z}),\ \Psi^{ac}_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{a}_{\Delta}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{c}_{\bar{\Delta}}(\bar{z}),\

(39)

\displaystyle\Psi^{ca}_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{c}_{\Delta}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{a}_{\bar{\Delta}}(\bar{z}),\ \Psi^{aa}_{\Delta,\bar{\Delta}}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{a}_{\Delta}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{a}_{\bar{\Delta}}(\bar{z}).

These fields form 4 rings in the general
N=(2,2)
SCFT, as shown in
LVW
.
In the geometric approach, in which
N=2
SCFT is interpreted as a
\sigma
-models on CY manifolds, these rings are related to the cohomology classes of CY-manifold.

A minimal model
M_{k}
of SCFT with
N=(2,2)
symmetry, is labeled by positive integer
k
. Its space of local fields can be decomposed into the sum of products of minimal representations (
19
).
In general, the set of local fields of the minimal models have an
A-D-E
classification, but in this paper we consider only
A
series. Then the primary fields of the model are given by diagonal pairing of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic factors:

\displaystyle\Psi^{NS}_{l,q}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{NS}_{l,q}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{NS}_{l,q}(\bar{z}),

(40)

\displaystyle\Psi^{R}_{l,q}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{R}_{l,q}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{R}_{l,q}(\bar{z}),

\displaystyle\text{where}\quad l=0,...,k,\quad q=-l,-l+2,...,l.

The factors
\Phi^{NS}_{l,q}(z)

\bar{\Phi}^{NS}_{l,q}(\bar{z})
,
\Phi^{R}_{l,q}(z)
,
\bar{\Phi}^{R}_{l,q}(\bar{z})
are the primary states of the minimal representations (
19
)
so that their conformal dimensions and
U(1)
charges are given by (
20
) and
(
21
) respectively.

Because of (
22
), (
23
) we see that in the Minimal model there are only chiral-chiral primary fields

\displaystyle\Psi^{cc}_{l}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{c}_{l}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{c}_{l}(\bar{z}),

and antichiral-antichiral primary fields

\displaystyle\Psi^{aa}_{l}(z,\bar{z})=\Phi^{a}_{l}(z)\bar{\Phi}^{a}_{l}(\bar{z})

All diagonally composed fields from
NS
sector are mutually local.

The
NS
sector fields are mutually quasi-local with
R
sector fields, i.e., when one field goes around the other, there is the phase factor
(-1)^{F+\bar{F}}
, where
F
and
\bar{F}
are the fermionic numbers of the
NS
field.
The
R
sector fields are mutually quasi-local, i.e., when one field goes around the other, there is the phase factor
(-1)^{F_{1}+\bar{F}_{1}+F_{2}+\bar{F}_{2}}
, where
F_{1,2}
and
\bar{F}_{1,2}
are the fermionic numbers of the
R

This mutual quasi-locality structure is a particular case of the general picture valid for any
N=(1,1)
superconformal theory.

From the total set of fields of the minimal model
M_{k}
one can single out the subset of mutually local fields

\displaystyle{\cal{H}}_{loc}={\cal{H}}^{NS}_{0}\oplus{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{2}\oplus{\cal{H}}^{R}_{1}\oplus{\cal{H}}^{R}_{3},

(43)

where
{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{0}
is the subspace of
{\cal{H}}^{NS}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}
with even fermion numbers
F
and
\bar{F}
,
{\cal{H}}^{NS}_{2}
is the subspace of
{\cal{H}}^{NS}\otimes\bar{\cal{H}}^{NS}
with odd fermion numbers
F
and

4
Fulfilling the Bootstrap axioms

It is easy to see that OPE of the primary fields of the orbifold is closed. Indeed, let us first consider the OPE of an arbitrary pair of the orbifold fields
\Psi_{\vec{l}_{1},\vec{t}_{1},\vec{w}_{1}}(z,\bar{z})

of the original fields since
U(1)
-charge conservation is performed for each minimal model
M_{k_{i}}

On the other hand, the twist
\vec{w}
of the resulting field is equal to the sum the
\vec{w}_{1}+\vec{w}_{2}
of twists of the original fields.
Hence, the resulting field will again satisfy the mutual locality equations
(

These arguments can be directly extended to include orbifold fields from the

63

It is also easy to check that the OPE of all (including quasi-local) orbifold fields (
52

63

Let us now consider the OPE of a mutually local subsector of the space of all quasilocal fields considered above.
The fields from this sector are selected by the equations
(
64
).
We have just discussed the consistency of OPE with the first equation of this pair.
So let us consider the second equation from (
64

\displaystyle\exp{[\imath\pi\sum_{i}(J_{0,i}-\bar{J}_{0,i})]}=1,

(65)

That is, if both fields entering to OPE, obey the second equation from (

Hence, the OPE for the mutually local subsector of fields is closed.

Using the obtained subset of the strongly mutually local fields,

\displaystyle Z_{G_{adm}}={1\over|G_{adm}|}\sum_{\vec{w}\in G_{adm}}\sum_{\vec{l},\vec{t}}\prod_{a=0}^{K-1}\delta(Q^{\vec{\beta}_{a}}_{\vec{l},\vec{t}}-\sum_{i}{\beta_{ai}w_{i}\over k_{i}+2})

Conclusion

In this paper we presented the explicit construction of the fields in a certain class of
N=(2,2)
superconformal models with
c=9
, which are the orbifolds of the products of
N=(2,2)
Minimal models.

We have shown how to use the products of the exactly solvable

N=2
Minimal models, the structure of the Minimal
N=2
Virasoro superalgebra representations and the spectral flow automorphism to explicitly construct the total set of fields in the orbifold models.

In this way we have obtained a new class of exactly solvable models of
N=2
SCFT with a central charge equal to
9
, since these models are defined in terms of the known exactly solvable CFT models, namely, in terms of the parafermionic field theory
ZF
and the theory of free bosonic field.

We explicitly constructed chiral and antichiral rings for several concrete examples of orbifolds and showed that their dimensions agree with the dimensions of the cohomology groups of mutually mirror CY-manifolds. Tables of the results of this calculation are given below in the section
A
.
These results agree with those obtained in
GrPl
and thus confirm the correctness of our approach.

We note that there are several interesting questions regarding our construction.

First of all, comparing the results of the explicit construction in the table (49,5) with the results in
BE
, we find that the elements of
H^{2,1}
appearing in twisted sectors cannot be realized as polynomials in the geometric approach.
Therefore, an interesting question arises, what is the geometric interpretation of the
H^{2,1}
elements that arise in the twisted sectors.

It would be interesting to generalize the considered
spectral flow twisting approach
to include boundary states
and to consider N = (2,2) SCFT of orbifolds with boundaries. This problem has been partially studied already in
P
, but it makes sense to explore such models using the more general constructions developed in this paper.

Finally, it would be interesting to generalize our approach to the cases of the Landau-Ginzburg
N=2
orbifold models with the more general Berglund-Hubsch type superpotentials
BH
, taking into account that some of them connected with Fermat type
N=2
orbifold models, as it was shown recently in
BBK
.

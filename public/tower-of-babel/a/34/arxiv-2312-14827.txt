On the smooth locus of affine Schubert varieties
Georgios Pappas, Rong Zhou

Abstract
. We give a simple and uniform proof of a conjecture of Haines–Richarz characterizing the smooth locus of Schubert varieties in twisted affine Grassmannians. Our method is elementary and avoids any representation theoretic techniques, instead relying on a combinatorial analysis of tangent spaces of Schubert varieties.

1.
Introduction

Schubert subvarieties of flag varieties, classical or affine, and their singularities have been a subject of considerable interest for some time. A basic problem is to characterize the Schubert varieties which are smooth or, more generally, identify their smooth locus; see, for example,
[
BL00
]
for many results in the classical case. In the affine case, this problem also relates,
via the theory of local models
[
PRS13
]
, to the study of singularities of reductions of Shimura varieties modulo primes. For example, understanding when affine Schubert varieties are smooth leads to a classification of Shimura varieties with parahoric level at a prime, whose reduction at that prime is smooth, see
[
HPR20
]
.

It turns out that this problem is simpler for Schubert varieties in affine Grassmannians.
Indeed, it was first shown by Evens–Mirkovic
[
EM99
]
that, for Schubert varieties in affine Grassmannians of split groups, the smooth locus is simply the ‘obvious’ open orbit. This result follows from their calculation of the characteristic cycle of the intersection cohomology sheaf of the Schubert variety and easily implies a characterization of the Schubert varieties which are smooth. Affine Grassmannians are also objects of Kac-Moody theory: The split case above is obtained from ‘untwisted’ affine Kac–Moody Lie algebras but one can equally consider the general (twisted) affine case, cf.
[
Kum02
, Ch. 13]
. The corresponding (twisted) affine Grassmannians can also be constructed using loop groups obtained from reductive groups over the field
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
of Laurent power series, see
[
PR08
]
, also for the comparison between the two points of view. In this group-theoretic set-up, Haines and Richarz gave a classification of all Schubert varieties in twisted affine Grassmannians which are smooth and also conjectured a generalization of the Evens–Mirkovic result
[
HR20
, Conjecture 5.4]
. The result of Haines and Richarz was used in the classification of Shimura varieties with smooth parahoric reduction of
[
HPR20
]
.

In this paper, we give a simple combinatorial proof of both the original result of Evens–Mirkovic and of its extension conjectured by Haines–Richarz. Let us now give some details. We formulate our results using the theory of reductive groups over the discretely valued field
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group over
F=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
. Associated to any special vertex
\mathfrak{s}
in the Bruhat–Tits building for
G
, we have an associated group scheme
\mathcal{G}
over
\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
. We let
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}:=LG/L^{+}\mathcal{G}
denote the twisted affine Grassmannian associated to
\mathcal{G}
, see
[
PR08
]
.
We fix
T\subset B\subset G
a maximal torus and Borel pair in
G
defined over
F
which are in good position relative to
\mathfrak{s}
, and let
I=\mathrm{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)
. Then to each dominant element
\mu\in X_{*}(T)_{I}^{+}
, we have an associated point
t^{\mu}\in\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathbb{C})
and the associated (affine) Schubert variety
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
. By definition
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is the closure of the
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-orbit
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\mu}
of
t^{\mu}
. Our main theorem is the following, which verifies
[
HR20
, Conjecture 5.4]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{s}
be an absolutely special vertex. Then the smooth locus of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\mu}
.

As we mentioned above, the result is well-known in the case of split groups due to work of Evens–Mirkovic
[
EM99
]
, see also Malkin–Ostrik–Vybornov
[
MOV05
, Cor. B]
. When the group
G
is not split, the picture is complicated by a phenomenon which was first observed in the theory of local models for integral models of Shimura varieties by Richarz (
[
Arz09
, Proposition 4.6]
): If the vertex
\mathfrak{s}
is special but not absolutely special, it can happen that the smooth locus of a Schubert variety is larger than the open stratum. This implies the existence of certain Shimura varieties with ‘exotic good reduction’ (i.e. Shimura varieties with non-hyperspecial level at a prime number
p
which, nevertheless, have good reduction at
p
, see
[
HPR20
, §5]
). Our result verifies the expectation that this does not happen in any other case. Implicitly, it also simplifies the proofs of the classification result of
[
HPR20
]
.

This conjecture has also been considered in work of Besson–Hong
[
BH
]
, who verified it when
G_{\mathrm{ad}}
does not have factors of type
C-BC_{n}
. Their method is very indirect and relies on difficult representation theoretic techniques. In particular, it crucially uses an extension of a result of Zhu
[
Zhu09
]
which gives a geometric interpretation of the Frenkel–Kac–Segal isomorphism between basic representations of affine Lie algebras and lattice vertex algebras. Furthermore, in the case of
E_{6}
, the proof requires computer assistance. Combined with Richarz’s thesis, which analyses the
C-BC_{n}
case explicitly, this can be used to deduce the conjecture in general. In contrast, our method is simpler, avoiding any use of representation theory and computer aided computations, and can be applied uniformly to all cases. Specialized to the case of split groups, it gives a new proof of the result in
[
EM99
]
and
[
MOV05
]
. Moreover, Theorem
1.1
, combined with now standard techniques, can be used to classify the smooth locus of twisted affine Grassmannians when
\mathbb{C}
is replaced by an algebraically closed field of any characteristic, at least when the group is tamely ramified (cf.
[
HR20
, Corollary 6.1]
.)

We now give some details of the proof. Since the smooth locus is open, it suffices to show that when
\mathfrak{s}
is absolutely special,
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is singular along the irreducible components of the boundary. These are given by
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\lambda}
, where
\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
is a minimal degeneration (cf. §
4.1
).
To prove this, we consider a lower bound for the dimension of the tangent space to
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
at
t^{\lambda}
. We obtain such a bound by considering the tangent vectors arising from smooth curves in the Schubert variety coming from affine root groups; we call these
root curves
. We show that for a minimal degeneration, the lower bound we obtain is equal to the dimension of the Schubert variety; here we use Stembridge’s classification of minimal degenerations and some simple computations using the échelonnage root system. When
\mathfrak{s}
is absolutely special, conjugating one of the root curves by an element of the opposite unipotent gives an extra tangent vector which makes the point non-smooth; this extra tangent direction has a component ‘along the Cartan direction’ (cf. Proposition
2.9
). The assumption that
\mathfrak{s}
is absolutely special is crucial here; in the case of exotic good reduction the shape of the neighbouring affine root groups for the special but not absolutely vertex prevents us from producing extra tangent directions. From this point of view, one can think of the singularities of minimal degenerations as being caused by extra tangent directions along the Cartan.
Roughly speaking, the moral of the main Theorem of
[
Zhu09
]
is similar: Properties of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
can be studied by restriction to the
0
-dimensional ind-subscheme
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{T}}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
, where
\mathcal{T}\subset\mathcal{G}
is a maximal torus.

We now give an outline of the paper. In section 2, we give an explicit description of the tangent space of partial affine flag varieties at the base point in terms of affine roots. This should be well-known to experts, but we provide the details here as they are needed for later. In section 3, we use the tangent vectors from root curves to give a lower bound on the dimension of tangent spaces of Schubert varieties, and in section 4 we apply the bound to the boundary components of minimal degenerations to prove the main result. At the end of the section, we give an alternative, less combinatorial, argument for the proof. This does not even need the full classification of minimal degenerations due to Stembridge but uses some additional algebraic geometric input. We finish with an example of the method applied to the case of the quasi-minuscule Schubert variety in the ramified triality, which gives a short proof of a result of Haines–Richarz
[
HR20
, §8]
.

2.
Tangent spaces of partial affine flag varieties

We give a description of the tangent space of partial affine flag varieties at the base point in terms of the associated affine root system. Such a description can also be deduced from the relationship between the affine flag varieties and Kac–Moody algebras as in
[
HLRar
, Proposition 4.5]
. However our construction of the isomorphism in Proposition
2.9
and its relation with affine root groups will be useful for our purposes.

2.1.

Let
F=\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
and
G/F
be an adjoint, absolutely simple reductive group.
Let
H
be the split Chevalley form of
G
and let
F^{\prime}:=\mathbb{C}(\!(u)\!)/F
be the minimal splitting field for
G
of degree
e
with
u^{e}=t
. Let
\tau
be a generator of
I:=\mathrm{Gal}(F^{\prime}/F)
; then
\tau(u)=\zeta u
where
\zeta
is an
e^{\mathrm{th}}
root of unity. Since
G
is absolutely simple, we have
e=1,2
or
3
. For any affine group scheme
X
over
F
, we write
LX
for the loop group scheme over
\mathbb{C}
associated to
X
. Similarly for
\mathcal{X}
an affine group scheme over
\mathcal{O}_{F}:=\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
, we write
L^{+}\mathcal{X}
for the positive loop group scheme over
\mathbb{C}
associated to
\mathcal{X}
, see
[
PR08
]
.

Let
H
denote the split Chevalley form over
\mathbb{C}
of
G
. Upon fixing a Chevalley pinning
(B_{H},T_{H},\{X_{\alpha^{\prime}}\})
of
H
, we may realize
G
as the functor whose value on an
F
-algebra
R
is given by

G(R):=\{g\in H(R\otimes_{F}F^{\prime})\,|\,\sigma(g)=g\}

where
\sigma=\sigma_{0}\circ\tau
, and
\sigma_{0}
is a diagram automorphism of the Dynkin diagram of
H
, which we consider as an element of
\mathrm{Aut}(H)
via the pinning.

The torus
T_{H}
determines a maximal torus
T
of
G
, and we let
S
denote the maximal
F
-split subtorus of
T
, which is a maximal
F
-split torus of
G
. We let
B\supset T
be the Borel subgroup defined over
F
determined by
B_{H}
and let
X_{*}(T)_{I}^{+}\subset X_{*}(T)_{I}
denote the set of dominant elements with respect to the choice of
B
.

2.2.

We recall the description of the affine root system for
G
and
H
following
[
PR09
]
. Let
\Phi
denote the set of relative roots for
G
and
\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
the set of affine roots. Similarly, we let
\Phi_{H}
denote the set of absolute roots for
H
, and
\Phi_{H,\mathrm{af}}
the set of affine roots. Let
\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)
(resp.
\mathcal{A}(H,T_{H},F^{\prime})
) denote the apartment for
S
over
F
(resp.
T_{H}
over
F^{\prime}
). Then the affine roots
\Phi_{\mathrm{af}},\Phi_{H,\mathrm{af}}
are affine functions on
\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)
,
\mathcal{A}(H,T_{H},F^{\prime})
respectively.

Definition 2.2.1
.

A vertex
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)
is said to be
absolutely special
if its image under the simplicial embedding
\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}(H,T_{H},F^{\prime})
is a special (equivalently hyperspecial) vertex.

Remark 2.2.2
.

This is a correction of
[
HR20
, Definition 5.1]
. It is necessary to take
F^{\prime}
the
minimal
splitting field of
G
since for
G={\rm PU}_{2n+1}
, the vertex labelled ‘s’ in
[
HR20
, equation (5.1)]
becomes special over a degree 4 ramified extension of
F
. We refer to Remark
4.6.1
for the definition of absolutely special vertex in general.

The action of
\sigma
on
H_{F^{\prime}}
induces an action on
\mathcal{A}(H,T_{H},F^{\prime})
which fixes the hyperspecial vertex corresponding to
H_{\mathcal{O}_{F^{\prime}}}
, and hence descends to a point
\mathfrak{s}_{0}\in\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)
. Then
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
is an absolutely special vertex in the sense above.
The choice of
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
induces identifications

\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)\cong X_{*}(T)_{I}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}\cong X_{*}(S)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R},

\mathcal{A}(H,T_{H},F^{\prime})\cong X_{*}(T_{H})\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}.

Using these identifications, we can write affine roots
a\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}},a^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H,\mathrm{af}}
as
a=\alpha+m
,
a^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}+m^{\prime}
, where
\alpha\in\Phi
and
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}
and
m,m^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Q}
. We normalize the valuation
v
on
F^{\prime}
so that
v(u)=\frac{1}{e}
. Then we have

\Phi_{H,\mathrm{af}}=\{\alpha^{\prime}+m^{\prime}\,|\,\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H},m^{\prime}\in\frac{1}{e}\mathbb{Z}\}.

For
a^{\prime}=\alpha^{\prime}+m^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H,\mathrm{af}}
, the corresponding root subgroup
\mathcal{U}^{\prime}_{a^{\prime}}\subset LH
over
\mathbb{C}
corresponds to the subspace
\mathbb{C}X_{\alpha^{\prime}}u^{m}
of the Lie algebra of the loop group
LH
, where
X_{\alpha^{\prime}}\in\mathrm{Lie}H
is our fixed Chevalley generator.

Let
\alpha\in\Phi
be a relative root, then
\alpha
determines a
\sigma
-orbit
C_{\alpha}\subset\Phi_{H}
of absolute roots. We write
d_{\alpha}=|C_{\alpha}|
. We define

R_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{d_{\alpha}}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $\frac{1}{2}\alpha\notin\Phi$,}\\
\frac{1}{2d_{\alpha}}+\frac{1}{d_{\alpha}}\mathbb{Z}&\text{if $\frac{1}{2}\alpha\in\Phi$}.\end{cases}

Then by
[
PR09
, Proposition 2.2]
, we have

\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}=\{\alpha+m\,|\,\alpha\in\Phi,m\in R_{\alpha}\}.

2.3.

For
\alpha\in\Phi
we write
U_{\alpha}\subset G
for the corresponding relative root subgroup over
F
, and
for
a=\alpha+m\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
, we let
\mathcal{U}_{a}\subset LU_{\alpha}
denote the corresponding root subgroup over
\mathbb{C}
defined in
[
PR08
]
. For each
a\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
we recall the description of
\mathcal{U}_{a}
below. We then use this to define a morphism
\tilde{f}_{a}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{a}
and a corresponding tangent vector
e_{a}
in the Lie algebra of
LH
fixed by
\sigma
.

Case (1):
\frac{1}{2}{\alpha},2\alpha\notin\Phi
. Then all elements of
C_{\alpha}
are orthogonal to one another, and our choice of
X_{\alpha^{\prime}}
determines an isomorphism
x_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}\mathbb{G}_{a}
for
K/F
of degree
d=d_{\alpha}=[K:F]
, where
d|e
. Under our assumptions, we have
d=1
or
d=e
, and hence
K=F
or
K=F^{\prime}
.
Let
a=\alpha+m\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
. Then
m\in\frac{1}{d}\mathbb{Z}
, and

x_{\alpha}(\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbb{C}))=\mathbb{C}u^{em}.

We define an isomorphism
\tilde{f}_{a}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{U}_{a}
such that its composition with
x_{\alpha}
is given by
r\mapsto ru^{em}.
The corresponding subspace of the Lie algebra of
LH
is generated by

e_{a}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}X_{\sigma_{0}^{i}(\alpha^{\prime})}(\zeta^{i}u)^{em},

where
\alpha^{\prime}\in C_{\alpha}
.

Case (2):
\alpha,2\alpha\in\Phi
. In this case
F^{\prime}/F
is a degree 2 extension, and we have an isomorphism
x_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\cong M_{F^{\prime},F}
where
M_{F^{\prime},F}
is the group scheme with
F
-points given by

M_{F^{\prime},F}(F)=\{(c,d)\,|\,c\sigma(c)+d+\sigma(d)=0\}.

Moreover,
U_{2\alpha}
is the subgroup of
U_{\alpha}
which corresponds under
x_{\alpha}
to

\{(0,d)\,|\,d+\sigma(d)=0\}\subset M_{F^{\prime},F}.

(2a) Let
a=\alpha+m\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
. Then
m\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, and we have

x_{\alpha}(\mathcal{U}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}))=\{(ru^{2m},\frac{(-1)^{2m+1}}{2}r^{2}u^{4m})|r\in\mathbb{C}\}.

We let
\tilde{f}_{a}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{U}_{a}
be such that

x_{\alpha}\circ\tilde{f}_{a}(r)=(ru^{2m},\frac{(-1)^{2m+1}}{2}r^{2}u^{4m}).

The corresponding subspace of the Lie algebra of
LH
is generated by

e_{a}=u^{2m}(X_{\alpha^{\prime}}+(-1)^{2m}X_{\sigma_{0}(\alpha^{\prime})}),

where
\alpha^{\prime}\in C_{\alpha}
.

(2b) Let
a=2\alpha+m
. Then
m\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
, and we have

x_{2\alpha}(\mathcal{U}_{a}(\mathbb{C}))=\{(0,ru^{2m})|r\in\mathbb{C}\}.

We let
\tilde{f}_{a}:\mathbb{A}^{1}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{U}_{a}
be such that

x_{\alpha}\circ\tilde{f}_{a}(r)=(0,ru^{2m}).

The corresponding element of the Lie algebra of
LH
is

e_{a}=u^{2m}X_{\alpha^{\prime}}

for
\alpha^{\prime}\in C_{2\alpha}
.

2.4.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
denote the Lie algebra of
H
which decomposes as

\mathfrak{h}\cong(\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}}\mathbb{C}X_{\alpha^{\prime}})\oplus\mathfrak{t}_{H},

where
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
is the Lie algebra of
T_{H}
. This gives a corresponding decomposition of the vector space

\mathfrak{h}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]=\left(\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]X_{\alpha^{\prime}}\right)\oplus(\mathfrak{t}_{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}])\subset\mathrm{Lie}LH.

This space is equipped with an action of
\sigma
, which preserves
\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]X_{\alpha^{\prime}}
and
\mathfrak{t}_{H}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]
.

Proposition 2.5
.

The vector space
(\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}}\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]X_{\alpha^{\prime}})^{\sigma}
has a basis given by the set
\mathcal{B}=\{e_{a}\}_{a\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}}
.

Proof.

Clearly, the
e_{a}
are
\sigma
-invariant and linearly independent.

Note that the vector space
\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}]X_{\alpha^{\prime}}
has a basis given by

\{X_{\alpha^{\prime}}u^{m}|\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H},m\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Suppose
v\in(\bigoplus_{\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{H}}X_{\alpha^{\prime}}\otimes\mathbb{C}[u,u^{-1}])^{\sigma}
has a non-zero component
X_{\alpha^{\prime}}u^{m}
; upon scaling we may assume this coefficient is 1. Let
\alpha\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
denote the associated relative root. We show
v
is spanned by the
e_{a}
by induction on the number of non-zero coefficients.

Case (1):
\frac{1}{2}{\alpha},2\alpha\notin\Phi
. Let
d=|C_{\alpha}|
as above. Then by
\sigma^{d}
-invariance, we have
\frac{e}{d}|m
, and the coefficient of
X_{\sigma_{0}^{i}(\alpha^{\prime})}u^{m}
in
v
is
\zeta^{im}
for
i=1,\dotsc,d
. Set
a=\alpha+\frac{1}{e}m\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
. Then
v-e_{a}
is spanned by
\mathcal{B}
by induction.

Case (2a):
2\alpha\in\Phi
. In this case
C_{\alpha}=\{\alpha^{\prime},\sigma_{0}(\alpha^{\prime})\}
, and the coefficient of
X_{\sigma_{0}(\alpha^{\prime})}u^{m}
is
(-1)^{m}u^{m}
. Then for
a=\alpha+\frac{1}{2}m
,
v-e_{a}
is spanned by
\mathcal{B}
by induction.

Case (2b):
\frac{1}{2}\alpha\in\Phi
. In this case
C_{\alpha}=\{\alpha^{\prime}\}
, and the
\sigma
-invariance implies
m
is odd. Set
a=\alpha+m
; then
v-e_{a}
is spanned by
\mathcal{B}
by induction. ∎

2.6.

The following computation will be used in §
4
to produce extra tangent directions along the Cartan.

Proposition 2.7
.

(1)

Let
a=\alpha+m
with
\frac{1}{2}\alpha,2\alpha\notin\Phi
. Set
b=-\alpha-m-\frac{1}{d_{\alpha}}\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
and
h=\tilde{f}_{b}(1)
. Then
\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}
has a non-zero component in
(\mathfrak{t}_{H}\otimes u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}])^{\sigma}
.

(2)

Let
a=2\alpha+m
with
\alpha\in\Phi
so that
2m
is an odd integer. Set
b=-\alpha-\frac{m}{2}-\frac{1}{2}\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
and
h=\tilde{f}_{b}(1)
. Then
\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}
has a non-zero component in
(\mathfrak{t}_{H}\otimes u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}])^{\sigma}
.

Proof.

In each case let
G_{\alpha}
denote the simply-connected cover of the subgroup of
G
generated by
U_{\alpha}
and
U_{-\alpha}
. Then
G_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{Res}_{K/F}\mathrm{SL}_{2}
for some
K/F
finite in (1) and
G_{\alpha}\cong\mathrm{SU}_{3}
the special unitary group for a degree 2 ramified extension
F^{\prime}/F
in (2). Since the affine root groups contained in
U_{\alpha},U_{-\alpha}
arise from
G_{\alpha}
, and the natural map
LG_{\alpha}\rightarrow LG
induces an injection on tangent spaces preserving Cartan spaces, it suffices to prove (1) and (2) for the group
G_{\alpha}
. In these cases, we compute using the standard matrix representations of these groups. Moreover, upon possibly changing the choice of absolutely special vertex
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
, we may assume
m=0
in (1) and
m=-\frac{1}{2}
in (2).

(1) Suppose
K=F(u^{\prime})
, so that
u^{\frac{e}{d_{\alpha}}}=u^{\prime}
. We have

e_{a}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\text{ and }h=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
u^{\prime-1}&1\end{matrix}\right).

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
u^{\prime-1}&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
-u^{\prime-1}&1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}-u^{\prime-1}&1\\
-u^{\prime-2}&u^{\prime-1}\end{matrix}\right).

The diagonal component of
\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}
gives a non-zero component in
(\mathfrak{t}_{H}\otimes u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}])^{\sigma}
.

(2) In this case,
e=d_{\alpha}=2
. Then we have

e_{a}=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&u^{-1}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\text{ and }h=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
1&1&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}&-1&1\end{matrix}\right).

Then we compute

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
1&1&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}&-1&1\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}0&0&u^{-1}\\
0&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}1&0&0\\
-1&1&0\\
-\frac{1}{2}&1&1\end{matrix}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{matrix}-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}&u^{-1}&u^{-1}\\
-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}&u^{-1}&u^{-1}\\
-\frac{u^{-1}}{4}&-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}&-\frac{u^{-1}}{2}\end{matrix}\right).

The diagonal of
\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{a}
gives a non-zero component in

There is an isomorphism

Proof.

By

(1)

(2)

4.
Tangent spaces for minimal degenerations

4.1.

The bound obtained in Corollary
3.8
can be made more explicit when applied to boundary components of minimal degenerations.
We keep the notation of the previous section so that
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
is the twisted affine Grassmannian associated to an adjoint absolutely simple reductive group over

and a choice of special parahoric corresponding to a point
\mathfrak{s}\in\mathcal{A}(G,S,F)

For

\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
is a minimal degeneration if

(1)

\mu\succcurlyeq\lambda

\mu\neq\lambda

(2)

If

We have the following classification of minimal degenerations due to Stembridge
[

, Theorem 2.8]

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
be a minimal degeneration. Then one of following holds:

(1)

\mu-\lambda
is a simple coroot.

(2)

\mu-\lambda
is the short dominant coroot of
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
, and
\lambda=0
on
\mu-\lambda
.

(3)

\mu-\lambda
is the short dominant coroot of
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
;
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
is of type
C_{n}
and
\lambda

on
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
is

0\,\rule[2.84544pt]{11.38092pt}{0.5pt}\,0\,\rule[2.84544pt]{11.38092pt}{0.5pt}\,\cdots\,\rule[2.84544pt]{11.38092pt}{0.5pt}\,0\xLeftarrow{\ \ \ }1\ .

(4)

\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
is of type
G_{2}
, and
\lambda=(2{\mathrel{\to}^{\ \ \ }}0)
,
\mu=(1{\mathrel{\to}^{\ \ \ }}1)
.

(5)

\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
is of type
G_{2}
, and
\lambda=(1{\mathrel{\to}^{\ \ \ }}0)
,
\mu=(0{\mathrel{\to}^{\ \ \ }}1)
.

Here the integer at a vertex of the Dynkin diagram represents the pairing with the simple root corresponding to that vertex.

4.3.

Let
\Sigma^{+}
denote the subset of positive roots, and we let
\rho
denote the half sum of positive roots in
\Sigma

Proposition 4.4

Let
\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
be a minimal degeneration. Then

\dim(T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu})^{\mathrm{root}}\geq\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}.

Proof.

By
[
Ric13
, Corollary 2.10]
, we have
\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}=\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle
. Thus by Corollary
3.8
, it suffices to show

(4.4.1)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma}k_{\alpha}\geq\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle.

By Proposition
3.6
, we have
k_{\alpha}=k_{-\alpha}+\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle
for any
\alpha\in\Sigma
. Substituting this in for each
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
, equation (
4.4.1
) becomes

(4.4.2)

\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}2k_{-\alpha}+\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle\geq\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle.

We consider the fives cases in Proposition
4.2
separately. Note that in each case
\mu-\lambda
is a positive coroot in
\Sigma^{\vee}
which we call
\beta^{\vee}
. Hence it suffices to show that

\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}2k_{-\alpha}\geq\langle\beta^{\vee},2\rho\rangle.

(1)

In this case,
\beta
is a simple root. Then
\langle\beta^{\vee},2\rho\rangle=2
since
\rho
is equal to the sum of the fundamental weights for
\Sigma
(cf.
[
Bou68
, Chap. VI]
). Thus it suffices to find some
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
with
k_{-\alpha}\geq 1
. It is easy to see that
k_{-\beta}\geq 1
works.

(2)

Let
\Sigma_{J}
denote the sub-root system
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
, and
W_{J}\subset W(\Sigma)
its Weyl group. If
\gamma^{\vee}
is a short positive coroot in
\Sigma_{J}^{\vee}
, then there is a
w\in W_{J}
such that
w(\gamma^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee}
. Then
w(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})=\lambda+\beta^{\vee}=\mu
and hence
(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dom}}\preccurlyeq\mu
, i.e.
k_{-\gamma}\geq 1
. We write
\Sigma_{J,\mathrm{short}}^{\vee,+}
for the set of short positive coroots in
\Sigma_{J}^{\vee}
. Then we have

(\sum_{\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}}2k_{-\alpha})\geq 2|\Sigma_{J,\mathrm{short}}^{\vee,+}|

and hence it suffices to show that

2|\Sigma_{J,\mathrm{short}}^{\vee,+}|\geq\langle\beta^{\vee},2\rho\rangle.

Recall that
\beta^{\vee}
is the short dominant coroot in
\Sigma_{J}
. We write
\beta^{\vee}
as a sum of simple coroots which necessarily lie in
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
, and we let
N_{\beta}
be the number of simple coroots appearing (counted with multiplicity). Then
\langle\beta^{\vee},2\rho\rangle=2N_{\beta}
. We thus reduce to showing

|\Sigma_{J,\mathrm{short}}^{\vee,+}|\geq N_{\beta}.

Note that this only depends on the root system
\Sigma_{J}
.

To show this last inequality, let
\gamma^{\vee}
be a short simple coroot and
v
a minimal length element in
W_{J}
such that
v(\gamma^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee}
. We let
s_{n}s_{n-1}\dotsc s_{1}
be a reduced word decomposition for
v
and write
v_{i}=s_{i}\dotsc s_{1}
for
i=1,\dotsc,n
. Then
n+1=N_{\beta}
, and
\gamma^{\vee},v_{1}(\gamma^{\vee}),\dotsc,v_{n}(\gamma^{\vee})
give
n+1
distinct elements of
\Sigma_{J,\mathrm{short}}^{\vee,+}
.

(3)

Let
\Sigma_{J}
denote the sub-root system
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)
which is of type
C_{n}
. Let
\gamma^{\vee}
be a positive short root. Then there is a
w\in W_{J}
such that
w(\lambda)=\lambda
and
w(\gamma^{\vee})=\beta^{\vee}
. We can prove this explicitly as follows.

We identify the coweight lattice of
\Sigma_{J}
with a submodule of
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Let
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Then the coroots are given by

\pm e_{i},i=1,\dotsc,n\text{ and }\pm e_{i}\pm e_{j},1\leq i<j\leq n.

We choose the ordering so that the positive coroots are given by
e_{i},i=1,\dotsc,n
and
e_{i}\pm e_{j},1\leq i<j\leq n
. Thus the short positive coroots are given by
e_{1},\dotsc,e_{n}
with
\beta^{\vee}=e_{1}
, and each can be made dominant using
w\in S_{n}\subset W_{J}
. By the condition on
\lambda
, the subgroup
S_{n}
fixes
\lambda
. Thus
w(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})=\lambda+\beta^{\vee}=\mu
and hence
(\lambda+\gamma^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dom}}\preccurlyeq\mu
, i.e.
k_{-\gamma}\geq 1
. The same argument as case (2) proves the desired inequality.

(4)+(5)

Let
\mathrm{supp}(\mu-\lambda)=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
with
\alpha_{1}
the short root. Then in both cases, we have

\mu-\lambda=\beta^{\vee}=\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+\alpha_{2}^{\vee}.

Thus
\langle\beta^{\vee},2\rho\rangle=4
and it suffices to show that there exist two positive coroots
\gamma^{\vee}_{1}
and
\gamma^{\vee}_{2}
such that
(\lambda+\gamma_{1}^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dom}}=(\lambda+\gamma_{2}^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dom}}=\mu
, in which case we have
k_{-\gamma_{1}}\geq 1
and
k_{-\gamma_{2}}\geq 1
. Clearly we can take
\gamma^{\vee}_{1}=\beta^{\vee}
. Note that
s_{\alpha_{1}}(\lambda)=\lambda
, and
s_{\alpha_{1}}(\alpha_{2}^{\vee})=\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+\alpha_{2}^{\vee}
. Thus
(\lambda+\alpha_{2}^{\vee})_{\mathrm{dom}}=\mu
and we can take
\gamma_{2}^{\vee}=\alpha_{2}^{\vee}
.

∎

4.5.

We now prove our main theorem which verifies a conjecture of Haines–Richarz
[
HR20
, Conjecture 5.4]
.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
be the twisted affine Grassmannian associated to an absolutely special vertex
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
. Let
\mu\in X_{*}(T)^{+}_{I}
. Then the smooth locus of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\mu}
.

Proof.

Clearly
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\mu}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is smooth. For the converse, note that the smooth locus is open, hence if
\lambda\preccurlyeq\mu
is such that
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\lambda}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}^{\mathrm{sm}}
, then
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\nu}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}^{\mathrm{sm}}
for
\lambda\preccurlyeq\nu\preccurlyeq\mu
. Thus it suffices to show for
\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
a minimal degeneration that
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\lambda}
does not lie in the smooth locus of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
. Using the
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
-action, it suffices to show
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is singular at
t^{\lambda}
, i.e. that

\dim T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}>\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{\preccurlyeq\mu}=\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle.

By Proposition
4.4
, we have

\dim(T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu})^{\mathrm{root}}\geq\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}.

Thus it suffices to show there exists
X\in T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
with non-zero component in
(u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}]\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}
.

Note that the restriction of the
L^{+}\mathcal{G}
action on
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
to the subgroup

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}:=\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])\cap t^{\lambda}\mathcal{G}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])t^{-\lambda}

preserves the point
t^{\lambda}
. Thus we obtain an action
\star
of
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
on
T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
. Under the identification (
3.7.1
), the action on the right is given by composing the natural adjoint action with the map
g\mapsto t^{-\lambda}gt^{\lambda}
.
Now since
\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle>\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle
, we have
k_{-\alpha}\geq 1
for some
\alpha\in\Sigma^{+}
and hence
e_{a}\in T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
for
a=\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(-\alpha-1)
.We set
h^{\prime}:=\tilde{f}_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(\alpha+\langle\lambda,\alpha\rangle)}(1)\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
and
h:=t^{-\lambda}h^{\prime}t^{\lambda}=\tilde{f}_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(\alpha)}(1)
. We claim that

X:=h^{\prime}\star e_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(-\alpha-1)}=\mathrm{Ad}(h)e_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(-\alpha-1)}\in T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}

has non-zero component along
(u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}]\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}
.

Note that since
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
is absolutely special, we have
\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(\alpha)=\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi_{\mathrm{af}}
with
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Phi
a relative root and
\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi
(cf. §
3.2
). Then

\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(-\alpha-1)=\begin{cases}\alpha^{\prime}-\frac{1}{d_{\alpha^{\prime}}}&\text{if $2\alpha^{\prime}\notin\Phi$,}\\
2\alpha^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then by Proposition
2.7
,
X
has a non-zero component in
(u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}]\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}
as desired.
∎

Remark 4.6.1
.

A standard argument can be used to extend the theorem to a general reductive group
G
over
\mathbb{C}(\!(t)\!)
. We write
G_{\mathrm{ad}}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\mathrm{Res}_{F_{i}/F}G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is an adjoint absolutely simple group over a finite extension
F_{i}
of
F
. In this case a vertex
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
in the building
\mathcal{B}(G,F)
for
G
over
F
is said to be absolutely special if it maps to an absolutely special vertex in
\mathcal{B}(\mathrm{Res}_{F_{i}/F}G_{i},F)\cong\mathcal{B}(G_{i},F_{i})
for all
i
. Then if
\mathcal{G}/\mathbb{C}[\![t]\!]
corresponds to an absolutely special vertex
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
, any Schubert variety of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
is isomorphic to a product of Schubert varieties in
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}_{i}}
, where
\mathcal{G}_{i}
corresponds to the image of
\mathfrak{s}_{0}
in
\mathcal{B}(G_{i},F_{i})
. It follows that the smooth locus is the open stratum.

4.7.

Here we explain an alternative proof of Theorem
4.6
that uses some extra algebro-geometric inputs, but which avoids the combinatorics in Proposition
4.4
.

Let
\mu\rightsquigarrow\lambda
be a minimal degeneration and set
Y:=\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\mu}\cup\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\lambda}\subset\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G}}
. Let
T^{\prime}:=T_{H}^{\sigma}
be the fixed point scheme of
T_{H}
over
\mathbb{C}
under the action of
\sigma
. Then
T^{\prime}\subset L^{+}\mathcal{G}
and hence acts on
Y
. By
[
BH
, Theorem 4.2, Corollary 4.3]
, the fixed point scheme
Y^{T^{\prime}}

has reduced locus
(Y^{T^{\prime}})_{\rm red}
which is
0
-dimensional and consists of just the points
t^{\mu}
and
t^{\lambda}
.

Suppose now that
Y
is smooth. Then
Y^{T^{\prime}}
is smooth by
[
Ive72
]
, and hence reduced and equal to
\{t^{\mu},t^{\lambda}\}
, so
t^{\lambda}
is an isolated
T^{\prime}
-fixed point in
Y
. Then, the tangent space

T_{t^{\lambda}}Y=T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is a
T^{\prime}
-module whose weight zero direct summand
(T_{t^{\lambda}}Y)_{0}
is trivial. This implies that
T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}=(T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu})^{\rm root}
.
Now
\mu-\lambda=\beta^{\vee}
, for some
\beta\in\Sigma^{+}
, and hence
k_{-\beta}^{(\lambda,\mu)}\geq 1
. Then as in the proof of Theorem
4.6
, taking
h^{\prime}=\tilde{f}_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(\beta+\langle\lambda,\beta\rangle)}(1)
, we obtain an element

X=h^{\prime}\star e_{\psi_{\mathfrak{s}_{0}}(-\beta-1)}\in T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}

whose component in
(u^{-1}\mathbb{C}[u^{-1}]\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}
is non-trivial. Then
X\not\in(T_{t^{\lambda}}\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu})^{\rm root}
which gives a contradiction.

4.8.
Example: The ramified triality

We explain how our computations can be used to give a lower bound on the dimension of the tangent space at the worst stratum of the quasi-minuscule Schubert variety for the ramified triality. This recovers a result of Haines–Richarz
[
HR20
, Theorem 8.1]
.

In our notation we can take
H=\mathrm{Spin}_{8}
. The absolute root system is of type
D_{4}
with simple roots
\alpha_{i},i=1,2,3,4
; here we use the notation from Bourbaki
[
Bou68
]
. Then
\sigma_{0}
fixes
\alpha_{2}
and acts as the cycle
1\rightarrow 3\rightarrow 4\rightarrow 1
on the other roots. The
\sigma_{0}
-orbits on positive roots can be listed as follows:

\{\alpha_{2}\},\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}\},\{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}\},

\{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3},\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4},\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}\},

\{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}\},\{\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}\}.

The simple roots for the échelonnage root system
\Sigma
are the modified norms of the simple absolute roots, so a basis for
\Sigma
is the pair
\alpha=\alpha_{2},\beta:=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}
which is of type
G_{2}
. The highest root is
\tilde{\alpha}=3\alpha+2\beta
.

The coroots are given by

\alpha^{\vee}=\alpha_{2}^{\vee},\quad\beta^{\vee}=\frac{\alpha_{1}^{\vee}+\alpha_{3}^{\vee}+\alpha_{4}^{\vee}}{3}

and we have
\tilde{\alpha}^{\vee}=\alpha^{\vee}+\beta^{\vee}
. Now take
\mu=\tilde{\alpha}^{\vee}
and
\lambda=0
.
We obtain 6 tangent directions of
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
at the base point
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\lambda}=\{e\}
by using the root curves

x\mapsto f_{\pm\beta-1}(x):=U_{\alpha_{1}}(u^{-1}x)U_{\alpha_{3}}(u^{-1}\zeta x)U_{\alpha_{4}}(u^{-1}\zeta^{2}x),

x\mapsto f_{\pm(\beta+3\alpha)-1}(x):=U_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}(u^{-1}x)U_{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}(u^{-1}\zeta x)U_{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}}(u^{-1}\zeta^{2}x),

x\mapsto f_{\pm(2\beta+3\alpha)-1}(x):=U_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}}(u^{-1}x)U_{\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{3}+\alpha_{4}}(u^{-1}\zeta x)U_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{4}}(u^{-1}\zeta^{2}x),

where
\zeta
is a third root of unity.

Let
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
be a basis for the Cartan
\mathfrak{t}_{H}
so that
e_{i}-e_{i+1}
corresponds to the root
\alpha_{i}
. Then conjugating the root curve
x\mapsto f_{-\beta-1}(x)
by
h:=\tilde{f}_{\beta}(1)
gives an extra tangent direction along the Cartan given by

u^{-1}((e_{1}-e_{2})+\zeta(e_{3}-e_{4})+\zeta^{2}(e_{3}+e_{4}))\in(u^{-1}\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}.

In fact
(u^{-1}\mathfrak{t}_{H})^{\sigma}
is 1-dimensional and the tangent direction along the Cartan given by conjugating the other two root curves above will be the same. Thus we get a lower bound of 7 for the dimension of the tangent space. On the other hand
\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle=6
, hence
\dim\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}=6
. So,
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\preccurlyeq\mu}
is singular at the point
\mathrm{Gr}_{\mathcal{G},\lambda}=\{e\}
.

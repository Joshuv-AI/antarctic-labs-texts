Dimensions of Riesz products and pluriharmonic measures
Evgueni Doubtsov

Abstract
. We estimate the energy and Hausdorff dimensions of the Riesz products on the unit sphere of ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} , n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . Also, we obtain similar results for the pluriharmonic measures on the torus.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 42A55; Secondary 28A78, 31C10, 43A85

1.
Introduction

The present paper is motivated by the following general question:
how does the spectrum of a measure
\mu
affect the size of support of
\mu
?
We primarily
consider Riesz product measures and related objects
on the unit sphere
S=S_{n}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|\zeta|=1\}
,
n\geq 2
,
therefore, we start by introducing the spaces
H(p,q)
,
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
.

1.1.
Complex spherical harmonics

Let
\mathcal{U}(n)
denote the group of unitary operators on the Hilbert space
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
.
Observe that
S_{n}=\mathcal{U}(n)/\mathcal{U}(n-1)
, hence,
S_{n}
is a homogeneous space.
General constructions of abstract harmonic analysis are explicitly implemented
on
S_{n}
in terms of the spaces
H(p,q)
,
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix a dimension
n
,
n\geq 2
.
Let
H(p,q)=H(p,q;n)
denote the space of all homogeneous harmonic polynomials
of bidegree
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}
.
By definition, this means that the polynomials under consideration have degree

p
in
z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}
, degree
q
in

\overline{z}_{1},\overline{z}_{2},\dots,\overline{z}_{n}
,
and have total degree
p+q
.

For the restriction of
H(p,q)
on
S
, one uses the same symbol.
The elements of
H(p,q)
are often called
complex
spherical harmonics.

1.2.
Riesz products on the sphere

Zygmund’s dichotomy
[
15
]
suggests that the homogeneous holomorphic polynomials introduced by
Ryll and Wojtaszczyk
[
14
]
could provide examples
of singular Riesz-type products on the sphere.
We say that
\{R_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

is a Ryll–Wojtaszczyk sequence with a constant
\delta\in(0,1)
if

•

R_{j}\in H(j,0)
, that is,
R_{j}
is a homogeneous holomorphic polynomial of degree
j
,

•

\|R_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(S)}=1
,

•

\|R_{j}\|_{L^{2}(S)}\geq\delta
for all
j=1,2,\dots
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
R=\{R_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a Ryll–Wojtaszczyk sequence,

J=\{j_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{N}
,
j_{k+1}/j_{k}\geq 3
,
and
a=\{a_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{D}
.
Then
(R,J,a)
is called a Riesz triple.

Each Riesz triple
(R,J,a)
generates a (standard) Riesz product.
Namely, the Riesz product

\Pi(R,J,a)
is defined by the formal equality

\Pi(R,J,a)=\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\overline{a}_{k}\overline{R}_{j_{k}}}{2}+1+\frac{a_{k}R_{j_{k}}}{2}\right),

where the product converges in the weak*-sense (see
[
7
]
,
[
6
]
for details).

There exists a fairly rich set of singular Riesz products on the sphere.
Indeed, if
(R,J,a)
is a Riesz pair with
a\notin\ell^{2}
, then Corollary 1 from
[
6
]
provides
a sequence
U=\{U_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
,
U_{j}\in\mathcal{U}(n)
, such that
\Pi(R\circ U,J,a)

is singular with respect to Lebesgue measure on
S_{n}
.

For a singular Riesz product, it is natural to ask about its dimension.
In the present paper, we estimate the energy and Hausdorff dimensions of the Riesz products
on the sphere.
Let
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi
denote the Hausdorff dimension of a measure
\Pi
.
We obtain, in particular, the following result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(R,J,a)
be a Riesz triple.
Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi(R,J,a)\geq 2n-1-\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2\log j_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}|a_{\ell}|^{2}\right).

1.3.
Organization of the paper

Auxiliary results, including definitions and basic facts of harmonic analysis on
S_{n}
,
are collected in Section
2
.
Estimates of the energy and Hausdorff dimensions of the Riesz products
on the unit sphere are obtained in Section
3
.
A related problem about
Hausdorff dimensions of the pluriharmonic measures on the torus is discussed in Section
4
.

1.4.
Notation

For
F,G>0
, we write
F\lesssim G
provided that
F\leq CG
for a constant
C>0
.
If
F\lesssim G
and
G\lesssim F
, then we write
F\approx G
.

2.
Auxiliary results

2.1.
Basics of harmonic analysis on
S_{n}

Let
\sigma=\sigma_{n}
denote the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit sphere
S_{n}
. Observe that

(2.1)

L^{2}(\sigma)=\operatornamewithlimits{\oplus}_{(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}}H(p,q).

Specific aspects of the harmonic analysis on
S
are illustrated by the following
multiplication rule for the spaces
H(p,q)
: if
f\in H(p,q)
and

g\in H(r,s)
, then

fg\in\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}H(p+r-\ell,q+s-\ell),

where
L=\min(p,s)+\min(q,r)
.
See
[
13
, Chapter 12]
for the proofs of the above facts and
for further results.

Let
M(S_{n})
denote the space of complex Borel measures on the unit sphere
S_{n}
.
Let
K_{p,q}(z,\zeta)
denote the reproducing kernel for the Hilbert space

H(p,q)\subset L^{2}(S)
. The polynomial

\mu_{p,q}(z)=\int_{S}K_{p,q}(z,\zeta)\,d\mu(\zeta),\quad z\in S,

is called the
H(p,q)
-projection of
\mu\in M(S)
.
For
\mu\in M(S)
, the spectrum
\textrm{spec}(\mu)
in terms of
H(p,q)
is defined by

\textrm{spec}(\mu)=\left\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}:\,\mu_{p,q}\neq\mathbf{0}\right\}.

2.2.
Disintegration into slice-products

Let
\mathbb{T}=S_{1}
denote the unit circle.
Given a
\xi\in S_{n}
,
the slice-product

\Pi_{\xi}(R,J,a)(\lambda):=\Pi(R(\lambda\xi),J,a),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{T},

is the classical Riesz product

(2.2)

\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\overline{a}_{k}\overline{R}_{j_{k}}(\xi)\overline{\lambda}^{j_{k}}}{2}+1+\frac{{a}_{k}R_{j_{k}}(\xi){\lambda}^{j_{k}}}{2}\right),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{T}.

In particular,
\Pi_{\xi}(R,J,a)
is a correctly defined probability measure.

Let
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
denote
the complex projective space of dimension
n-1
,
that is, the collection of all one-dimensional
linear subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Let
\pi=\pi_{n}
denote the canonical projection from

S_{n}
onto
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
.
Observe that
\Pi_{\lambda\xi}(w)=\Pi_{\xi}(\lambda w)
for
\xi\in S_{n}
,
\lambda,w\in\mathbb{T}
.
Therefore, the probability slice-measure
\Pi_{\zeta}
is correctly defined for
\zeta\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
as
an element of
M_{+}(S_{n})
supported by the unit circle
\pi^{-1}(\zeta)\subset S_{n}
.

Let
\widehat{\sigma}_{n}
denote the unique probability measure on
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}

invariant with respect to all unitary transformations of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The following lemma shows that
\Pi(R,J,a)
is the integral of its slices.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
, Lemma 1]
)
.

Let
(R,J,a)
be a Riesz triple on the sphere
S_{n}
,
n\geq 2
,
and let
\Pi(R,J,a)
denote the corresponding Riesz product. Then

\Pi(R,J,a)=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}}\Pi_{\zeta}(R,J,a)\,d\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(\zeta)

in the following weak sense:

\int_{S_{n}}f\,d\Pi(R,J,a)=\int_{\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}}\int_{S_{n}}f\,d\Pi_{\zeta}(R,J,a)\,d\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(\zeta)

for all
f\in C(S_{n})
.

3.
Dimensions of Riesz products

3.1.
Energy dimension

Let
M_{+}(S_{n})
denote the set of all finite positive Borel measures on
S_{n}
.
Given a measure
\mu\in M_{+}(S)
, its
t
-energy
I_{t}(\mu)
,
t>0
, is defined as

I_{t}(\mu):=\int_{S}\int_{S}\frac{d\mu(x)d\mu(y)}{|x-y|^{t}}.

We say that
d
is the energy dimension of
\mu

and we write
\dim_{\mathcal{E}}\!\mu=d
if

d=\sup\left\{t:I_{t}(\mu)<\infty\right\}.

To estimate the energy dimension of
\Pi(R,J,a)
,
we need the following theorem.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
)
.

For each
t
,
0<t<2n-1
, there are constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}I_{t}(\mu)\leq\|\mu_{0,0}\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}j^{t-2n+1}\sum_{p+q=j}\|\mu_{p,q}\|_{2}^{2}\leq C_{2}I_{t}(\mu)

for all
\mu\in M_{+}(S_{n})
with

n\geq 2
.

In
[
11
]
, an analog of Theorem
3.2
for the unit circle is used
to compute the energy dimension of a classical Riesz product on
\mathbb{T}
.
In the following result, we apply Theorem
3.1

to estimate from below the energy dimension of a Riesz product on the sphere.
In fact, modulo technical details, the principal argument below is similar to that from the proof
of Theorem 3.1 from
[
11
]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(R,J,a)
be a Riesz triple.
Then

\dim_{\mathcal{E}}\!\Pi(R,J,a)\geq 2n-1-\alpha_{0},

where

(3.1)

\alpha_{0}=\max\left[0,\ \limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{\log\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\log\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}|^{2}}{2}\right)}{\log j_{k}}\right)\right].

In particular,

\dim_{\mathcal{E}}\!\Pi(R,J,a)\geq 2n-1-\limsup_{k\to\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2\log j_{k}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}|a_{\ell}|^{2}\right).

Proof.

Let

\Pi:=\Pi(R,J,a)
.
For
k=1,2,\dots
, let

\Gamma_{k}:=\left\{\pm j_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}j_{\ell}:\varepsilon_{\ell}=0,\pm 1\right\}.

Suppose that
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
. Without loss of generality, assume that
\gamma>0
.
Then there exists exactly one representation

\gamma=j_{k}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\varepsilon_{\ell}j_{\ell}.

By the multiplication rule for the spherical harmonics (see Section
2.1
),

\sum_{p-q=\gamma}\Pi_{p,q}=R_{j_{k}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}R_{j_{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{\ell}),

where

\begin{split}R_{j_{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{\ell})&=R_{j_{\ell}},\quad\varepsilon_{\ell}=1,\\
R_{j_{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{\ell})&=1,\quad\quad\varepsilon_{\ell}=0,\\
R_{j_{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{\ell})&=\overline{R}_{j_{\ell}},\quad\varepsilon_{\ell}=-1.\end{split}

Applying (
2.1
), we obtain

\sum_{p-q=\gamma}\|\Pi_{p,q}\|_{2}^{2}=\left\|R_{j_{k}}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}R_{j_{\ell}}(\varepsilon_{\ell})\right\|^{2}_{2}\leq\left(\frac{|a_{k}|}{2}\prod_{\ell:\,\varepsilon_{\ell}\neq 0}\frac{|a_{\ell}|}{2}\right)^{2},

where the empty product is equal to one.

Now, fix an
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
. Since
p+q\geq p-q
, we have

(3.2)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}|p+q|^{-\alpha}\sum_{p-q=\gamma}\|\Pi_{p,q}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}|\gamma|^{-\alpha}\sum_{p-q=\gamma}\|\Pi_{p,q}\|_{2}^{2}.

If
\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}
, then
|\gamma|\approx j_{k}
. Thus,

(3.3)

\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma_{k}}|\gamma|^{-\alpha}\sum_{p-q=\gamma}\|\Pi_{p,q}\|_{2}^{2}\lesssim j_{k}^{-\alpha}\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}|^{2}}{2}\right).

Combining (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we obtain

(3.4)

I_{2n-1-\alpha}(\Pi)\lesssim 1+j_{k}^{-\alpha}\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}|^{2}}{2}\right)

by Theorem
3.1
.

We have
\log j_{k}\geq(k-1)\log 3
, thus,
selecting an
A
,
0<A<1
, sufficiently close to
1
,
we deduce from the inequality
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
that

\alpha\geq\frac{\log\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}+\sum_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\log\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}|^{2}}{2}\right)+k|\log A|}{\log j_{k}}

or, equivalently,

j_{k}^{-\alpha}\frac{|a_{k}|^{2}}{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k-1}\left(1+\frac{|a_{\ell}|^{2}}{2}\right)\leq A^{k}.

Combining the above property and (
3.4
), we conclude that
I_{2n-1-\alpha}(\Pi)<\infty
.
Since
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\dim_{\mathcal{E}}\!\Pi\geq 2n-1-\alpha_{0}
, as required.
∎

3.2.
Hausdorff dimension

For a Borel set
E
, let
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!E
denote its Hausdorff dimension.
The Hausdorff dimension of a measure
\mu\in M_{+}(S_{n})
is defined as

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\mu=\inf\{\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!E:E\ \textrm{is a Borel set with}\ \mu(E)>0\}.

For properties of the Hausdorff dimension of a measure see
[
9
, Chapter 10]
.

Given a Riesz product
\Pi=\Pi(R,J,a)
, direct inspection shows that there exists a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that

\Pi_{p,q}=\mathbf{0}
provided that

\left|\frac{p}{q}-1\right|<\varepsilon,\quad(p,q)\neq(0,0).

Therefore, Theorem 1.1 from
[
3
]
applies to
\Pi
and guarantees that

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi\geq 2n-2
.
In fact, applying Theorem
3.2
, we obtain a more precise estimate.

3.2.1.
Application of Theorem
3.2

If
I_{t}(\mu)<\infty
, then
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\mu>t
(cf.
[
8
, Section 4.3]
); thus, the Hausdorff dimension of a
measure is always at least the energy dimension.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
(R,J,a)
be a Riesz triple.
Then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi(R,J,a)\geq 2n-1-\alpha_{0},

where
\alpha_{0}
is defined by (
3.1
).

Proof.

Since
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi\geq\dim_{\mathcal{E}\!}\Pi
, it suffices to apply Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Clearly, Theorem
1.3
is a particular case of the above corollary.

3.2.2.
Reduction to slice-products

An alternative approach to Corollary
3.3
is to consider the slice-products
of
\Pi(R,J,a)
.
We need the following theorem which is a consequence of
[
10
, Sections 2.10.2 and 2.10.17]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{T}
be a compact set and
K_{\zeta}=\{w\in\mathbb{T}:(\zeta,w)\in K\}
.
Assume that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!K_{\zeta}>\beta
for
\zeta\in X\subset\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
.
If
\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(X)>0
, then

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!K\geq 2n-2+\beta.

Fix an
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
. It suffices to prove that

(3.5)

\dim_{\mathcal{H}\!}\Pi(R,J,a)\geq 2n-1-\alpha.

Assume that the above estimate does not hold. Then there exists a compact
E\subset S_{n}

such that
\Pi(E)>0
and

\dim_{\mathcal{H}\!}E<2n-1-\alpha.

We identify
S_{n}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{T}
, and we consider the sets

E_{\zeta}=\{w\in\mathbb{T}:(\zeta,w)\in E\}
.
We claim that

(3.6)

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!E_{\zeta}\leq 1-\alpha\quad\textrm{
for $\widehat{\sigma}_{n}$-almost all $\zeta\in\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}$.}

Indeed, if this is not the case, then
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!E_{\zeta}>1-\alpha

for
\zeta\in X
, where
\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(X)>0
.
Applying Theorem
3.5
, we obtain
\dim_{\mathcal{H}\!}E\geq 2n-1-\alpha
,
a contradiction. Thus, (
3.6
) holds.

Since
\Pi(E)>0
, Lemma
2.1
guarantees that
\Pi_{\zeta}(E_{\zeta})>0

for
\zeta\in Y_{0}
with
\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(Y_{0})>0
.
Combining this fact and (
3.6
), we obtain

(3.7)

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi_{\zeta}\leq 1-\alpha\quad\textrm{
for $\zeta\in Y$ with $\widehat{\sigma}_{n}(Y)>0$}.

Now, recall that
\Pi_{\zeta}(R,J,a)
is the classical Riesz product defined by (
2.2
).
Therefore,

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\Pi_{\zeta}(R,J,a)\geq 1-\alpha_{0}>1-\alpha
by
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
.
This contradicts (
3.7
). Thus, (
3.5
) holds true
for any
\alpha>\alpha_{0}
, as required.

In other words, consideration of slice-products allows to recover Corollary
3.3

from
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, the corresponding result for the classical Riesz products.

4.
Hausdorff dimensions of pluriharmonic measures

This section is motivated by analogs of Lemma
2.1
and their applications
in the setting of pluriharmonic measures.

4.1.
Pluriharmonic measures on the unit sphere

A measure
\mu\in M(S_{n})
is called pluriharmonic if

\textrm{spec}(\mu)\subset\{(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{2}:pq=0\}
.
An analog of Lemma
2.1
is known for the pluriharmonic measures on
S_{n}
.
This fact implies that
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\mu\geq 2n-2
for any pluriharmonic measure
\mu\in M(S_{n})
,
see
[
1
,
3
]
for further details.
However, to the best of the author’s knowledge, the sharpness of this estimate is an open problem.

4.2.
Pluriharmonic measures on the torus

A measure
\mu\in M(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is called pluriharmonic if

\hat{\mu}(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})=0
for

(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus(\mathbb{Z}_{-}^{n}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{+}^{n})
.
It is well known that
\mu\in M(\mathbb{T}^{n})
is pluriharmonic if and only if the Poisson integral of
\mu

is a pluriharmonic function in the polydisk
\mathbb{D}^{n}
.
Therefore, an analog of Lemma
2.1
holds true for every pluriharmonic measure

\mu
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.
See
[
2
, Proposition 2.1]
for the case of
\mu
on the unit sphere
S_{n}
,
see also
[
5
]
, where
pluriharmonic measures on the Shilov boundary of a bounded symmetric domain are considered.
Similar to the case of
S_{n}
, we conclude that

(4.1)

\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\mu\geq n-1;

cf.
[
5
, Corollary 3.3]
.
For
n=2
, estimate (
4.1
) and its sharpness were earlier proved in
[
4
]
.
Simple examples show that estimate (
4.1
) is sharp for all
n\geq 2
.
Indeed, put

\mu=\delta_{1}(\xi_{1})\otimes m(\xi_{2})\otimes\cdots\otimes m(\xi_{n}),

where
\delta_{1}
denotes the Dirac measure at
1\in\mathbb{T}

and
m=\sigma_{1}
denotes the normalized Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{T}
.
Clearly,
\mu
is a pluriharmonic measure and
\dim_{\mathcal{H}}\!\mu=n-1
.

Computability of magnetic Schrödinger and Hartree equations on unbounded domains
Simon Becker, Jonathan Sewell, Euan Tebbutt

Abstract
. We study the computability of global solutions to linear Schrödinger equations with magnetic fields and the Hartree equation on ℝ 3 \mathbb{R}^{3} . We show that the solution can always be globally computed with error control on the entire space if there exist a priori decay estimates in generalized Sobolev norms on the initial state. Using weighted Sobolev norm estimates, we show that the solution can be computed with uniform computational runtime with respect to initial states and potentials. We finally study applications in optimal control theory and provide numerical examples.



1.
Introduction

In this article, we address for the first time the question under which conditions the solution to linear Schrödinger equations with external magnetic field or the Hartree equation can be reduced to an effective dynamics on a bounded domain that can then be numerically computed. We show that in both cases, this is possible if the initial state can be controlled in a generalized Sobolev-type norm that controls both the regularity and the decay of the initial state. The linear Schrödinger equation with external magnetic field describes a single-particle system subject to an external magnetic field. The Hartree equation naturally occurs as a mean-field limit of many-particle systems and in quantum transport theory
[
IZL94
,
BM91
]
. Due to their relevance in physics, we focus on magnetic Schrödinger equations or Hartree equations with bilinear interaction potential on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Most of the literature dealing with numerical approximation schemes on unbounded domains, has been concerned with the existence of transparent or absorbing boundary conditions, see e.g.
[
KG18
,
JG07
]
and references therein, for electro-magnetic time-dependent Schrödinger equations. In
[
KG18
]
spatial restrictions are imposed on the electromagnetic vector potential, to avoid undesired boundary effects. In this work, we circumvent such undesired restrictions by studying a somewhat different question:
Can we forget about the unboundedness of the domain and just restrict the dynamics to a bounded domain whose size is uniform in the input parameters (initial state, potentials, control) of our problem?

Similar to the above results for linear Schrödinger equations, a mode decomposition for nonlinear Schrödinger equations has been thoroughly addressed on bounded domain by Soffer and Stucchio in
[
SS07
,
SS09
]
.

The linear Schrödinger equation is often numerically discretized by a simple Crank-Nicholson method, as this one preserves the
L^{2}
-norm. To numerically study the non-linear dynamics, there exist many results on splitting methods for nonlinear Hartree equations (also called Schrödinger-Poisson equation)
[
F04
,
L08
]
and even relativistic
[
BD11
]
and fractional Hartree equations
[
ZWW19
]
.

Thus, even though there exists a vast literature on convergent discretization schemes, an implementation on a physical computer must not rely on infinitely many variables. For instance, a convergent algorithm that assumes a uniform discretization of the entire space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
cannot be implemented in practice.

In Section
5
, we also discuss applications of our work to optimal control problems.

We illustrate our results with some basic numerical examples in Section
6
.

We emphasize at this point that all the constants appearing in this work could be made fully explicit. But for the sake of presentation, we do not specify them explicitly.

Let us now describe the precise set-up of the equations we consider, starting with the magnetic linear Schrödinger equation:

1.1.
The magnetic Schrödinger equation

Let
H_{0}
be a self-adjoint magnetic Schrödinger operator
H_{0}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+V:D(H_{0})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
where
V
is a time-independent
pinning potential

V
. We consider the evolution of a particle under the influence of an external
control potential

V_{\operatorname{con}}
with control function
u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{pcw}}(0,T)
. Writing
V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t):=u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}
for the full time-dependent potential, we study time-dependent linear Schrödinger equations of the form

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\psi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\\
\psi(\bullet,0)&=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

(1.1)

The assumptions we impose on the potentials are as follows

Assumption 1
.

We demand that the static pinning potential can be written as a sum of a regular (possibly unbounded at infinity) and singular part (possible local singularities)
V=W_{\operatorname{reg}}+W_{\operatorname{sing}}
. For these two potentials and the control potential
V_{\operatorname{con}}
with control
u
, we impose the assumptions that

•

\|\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}V_{\operatorname{con}}\|_{\infty}<\infty
and
\|\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}W_{\operatorname{reg}}\|_{\infty}<\infty,

•

W_{\operatorname{sing}}\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}),
and

•

u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{pcw}}(0,T).

For the magnetic vector potential we either assume a constant magnetic field
B_{0}>0
with associated vector potential
A=\frac{B_{0}}{2}(-x_{2},x_{1},0)
or a vector potential
A\in W^{1,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.

Remark 1
.

Our choice of singular and regular part allows us to treat standard physical examples of potentials such as the Coulomb potential
x\mapsto 1/|x|
(singular part) and the harmonic potential
x\mapsto|x|^{2}
(regular part and control potential).

The Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) with linear control potential appears naturally in the study of static physical systems with Hamiltonian
H_{0}
, under the influence of a time-dependent bilinear electric potential.

In this article, we build upon techniques, introduced in
[
B05
,
BKP05
]
, to prove existence of solutions to Schrödinger equations in certain weighted Sobolev spaces that ensure additional spatial decay. These spaces are essential in our study of global numerical algorithms that provide solutions to (
1.1
) on unbounded domains.

1.2.
Hartree equation

In the case of the Hartree equation, we consider a single particle described by the Hartree equation with Schrödinger operator
H_{0}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+V:D(H_{0})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and static
pinning potential

V
and
control potential

V_{\operatorname{con}}
with time-dependent control function
u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
such that
V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t):=u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\psi(x,t)+\left(|\psi(\bullet,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\psi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\\
\psi(\bullet,0)&=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

The potentials and magnetic fields are assumed to satisfy the conditions in Assumption
1
.

We denote by

We sometimes abuse the notation for spatial integrals in

2.
The magnetic linear Schrödinger equation

We start by recalling the following classical Lemma
[
BH20
,
BKP05
]
that shows that the free Schrödinger equation is well-posed in the weighted Sobolev space
H^{2}_{2}
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}
.
There exists a
\psi\in C([0,T];H^{2}_{2})
solving

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi&=-\Delta\psi\text{ with initial condition }\psi(x,0)=\varphi_{0}(x)\end{split}

such that
\left\lVert\psi(\bullet,t)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\leq C_{T}\left\lVert\varphi_{0}(\bullet)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

As a first step to study the magnetic linear Schrödinger equation, we show the existence of solutions to a regularized version of the magnetic linear Schrödinger equation with discrete derivatives, for some regularization parameter
h>0,

(\nabla_{h}\psi)(x):=\left(\frac{\psi(x+he_{1})-\psi(x)}{h},..,\frac{\psi(x+he_{3})-\psi(x)}{h}\right)

(2.1)

coupled to a bounded magnetic vector potential and regularized potentials.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
V\in L^{\infty}((0,T);C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
and
A\in W^{2,\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{3};{\mathbb{R}}^{3})
.
For initial states
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, the equation

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=-\Delta\psi(x,t)+V(x,t)\psi(x,t)+iA(x)\cdot(\nabla_{h}\psi)(x,t)+i(\nabla_{-h}\cdot(A\psi))(x,t)\\
\psi(x,0)&=\varphi_{0}(x)\end{split}

(2.2)

has a unique mild solution
\psi\in C([0,T];H^{2}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
.
Furthermore, if
\rho=\left\lVert V\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}
,
\mu=\left\lVert A\right\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}
, then there exists
C_{T,\rho,\mu,h}
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,

\left\lVert\psi(\bullet,t)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\leq C_{T,\rho,\mu,h}\left\lVert\varphi_{0}(\bullet)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

Proof.

Let
Y=C([0,T];H^{2}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
with norm
\lVert\psi\rVert_{Y}=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}e^{-\lambda t}\left\lVert\psi(\bullet,t)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}
for some
\lambda>0
to be specified later. In terms of the one-parameter group
S(t)=e^{i\Delta t}
, solving the equation (
2.2
) is equivalent to solving the fixed-point equation

\psi(t)=S(t)\varphi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\left(\psi(s)V(s)+(iA\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi)(s)+(i\nabla_{-h}\cdot(A\psi))(s)\right)ds.

Let
B_{R}=\{\psi\in Y:\lVert\psi\rVert_{Y}\leq R\}
, for some
R
to be specified later, and define a map
\Phi:B_{R}\to B_{R}
by

\Phi(\psi(t))=S(t)\varphi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\left(\psi(s)V(s)+(iA\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi)(s)+(i\nabla_{-h}\cdot(A\psi))(s)\right)ds.

To see that
\Phi
maps
B_{R}
into itself, first notice that

\begin{split}\left\lVert\psi(s)V(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\Delta(\psi(s)V(s))\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert(1+|\bullet|^{2})\psi(\bullet,s)V(\bullet,s)\right\rVert_{2}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert\Delta\psi(s)V(s)+\nabla\psi(s)\cdot\nabla V(s)+\psi\Delta V\right\rVert_{2}+\rho\lVert\psi(s)\rVert_{H_{2}}\leq C_{\rho}\left\lVert\psi(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

We then use in addition that
[\nabla_{h},\Delta]=0

\begin{split}\left\lVert A\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\Delta(A\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi(s))\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert(1+|\bullet|^{2})A(\bullet)\cdot\nabla_{h}\psi(\bullet,s)\right\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq C_{\mu,h}\left\lVert\psi(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\left\lVert\nabla_{-h}\cdot(A\cdot\psi)(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}&\lesssim\left\lVert\Delta(\nabla_{-h}\cdot(A\cdot\psi)(s))\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert(1+|\bullet|^{2})\nabla_{h}\cdot(A\cdot\psi)(s)\right\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq C_{h}(\left\lVert\Delta(A\cdot\psi)(s)\right\rVert_{2}+\mu\lVert\psi(s)\rVert_{H_{2}})\leq C_{\mu,h}\left\lVert\psi(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

Let
C_{T}
be the constant from Lemma
2.1
, let
2(C_{\rho}+C_{\mu,h})C_{T}\leq\lambda
, and let
R\geq 2C_{T}\left\lVert\varphi_{0}\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

Hence, we find that,
\Phi
is indeed a contraction:

\begin{split}\lVert\Phi(\psi_{1})-\Phi(\psi_{2})\rVert_{Y}&=\sup_{t}\left(e^{-\lambda t}\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}S(t-s)\left(V(s)+iA\cdot\nabla_{h}+i\nabla_{-h}\cdot A\right)(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(s)ds\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\right)\\
&\leq C_{T}(C_{\rho}+C_{\mu,h})\sup_{t}\left(e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda s}e^{-\lambda s}\left\lVert(\psi_{1}-\psi_{2})(s)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}ds\right)\\
&\leq C_{T}(C_{\rho}+C_{\mu,h})\lVert\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\rVert_{Y}\sup_{t}\left(e^{-\lambda t}\int_{0}^{t}e^{\lambda s}ds\right)\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi_{1}-\psi_{2}\rVert_{Y}.\end{split}

Therefore
\Phi
has a unique fixed point in
Y
.

The previous estimate immediately implies that
\left\lVert\psi(\bullet,t)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\leq C_{T,\rho,\mu,h}\left\lVert\varphi_{0}(\bullet)\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}
.
∎

Remark 3
.

We want to mention at this point that both Lemma

and Lemma

hold in

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{W^{1,1}(0,T)}=\alpha

there exists a constant

such that for any
\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}_{1}

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi(x,t)+V\psi(x,t)\\
\psi(x,0)&=\varphi_{0}(x)\end{split}

has a unique solution
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{1})
, and
\psi
satisfies
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{1}_{1})}\leq C_{T,\alpha,\rho,\mathcal{A}}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}.

Proof.

First, approximate the potentials by elements of
L^{\infty}(0,T;C_{b}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
in the following way:

Let
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, where
0\leq\chi
, chosen symmetric
\chi(x)=\chi(-x)
and normalized
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\chi(x)dx=1
. We define the rescaled function
\zeta_{\varepsilon}(x)=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{3}}\chi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right).
Using the truncation map
(\operatorname{Trunc}_{\varepsilon}f)(x)=\operatorname{sign}(f(x))\max\left(\frac{1}{\varepsilon},f(x)\right)
, we define for
i\in\{\operatorname{reg},\operatorname{con}\}

V_{i}^{\varepsilon}(x):=(\zeta_{\varepsilon}*\operatorname{Trunc}_{\varepsilon}(V_{i}))(x)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\operatorname{Trunc}_{\varepsilon}(V_{i}(x+\varepsilon y))\chi(y)dy.

(2.3)

Finally, let
W_{\operatorname{sing}}^{\varepsilon}:=\zeta_{\varepsilon}*W_{\operatorname{sing}}.

By properties of convolutions it can be shown that

\begin{split}\left\lVert(1+|\bullet|^{2})^{-1}W_{\operatorname{reg}}^{\varepsilon}(\bullet)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}&\leq\rho\text{ and }\left\lVert(1+|\bullet|^{2})^{-1}V_{\operatorname{con}}^{\varepsilon}(\bullet)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\rho.\end{split}

For the discrete derivative

D_{h}\psi=-i\nabla_{h}\psi,

(2.4)

with
\nabla_{h}
as in (
2.1
), we have by Lemma
2.2
, that for all
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})
satisfying

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)_{\varepsilon}&=(H_{B}^{\varepsilon}+V^{\varepsilon})\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\\
\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,0)&=\varphi_{0}(x)\end{split}

where
H_{B}^{\varepsilon}=-\Delta-AD_{\varepsilon}-D_{-\varepsilon}A+A\cdot A
is the regularized magnetic Schrödinger operator.
We then intend to bound the
H^{1}_{1}
norm of
\psi_{\varepsilon}
uniformly in
\varepsilon
.

We multiply the preceding equation by
(1+|x|^{2})\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(x)
and integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}i\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)+(1+|x|^{2})(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}H_{B}^{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)+(1+|x|^{2})V^{\varepsilon}(x)|\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx=0.

Thus by taking the imaginary part, we find

\Imag\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}i\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)-(1+|x|^{2})(\psi_{\varepsilon}H_{B}^{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)\ dx=0.

In the first term, we note that
\Imag(i\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})=\Real(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon})=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}.
This implies that

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})|\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx&=2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}H_{B}^{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)\ dx\\
&=2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\left(-\Delta-AD_{\varepsilon}-D_{-\varepsilon}A\right)\psi_{\varepsilon})(x,t)\ dx.\end{split}

Integration by parts yields

\begin{split}-2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon})(x)\ dx&=2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\underbrace{(1+|x|^{2})|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)|^{2}}_{\in\mathbb{R}}\ dx+2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}2\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(x)x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(x)\ dx\\

Hence, using that
ab\leq\frac{a^{2}}{2}+\frac{b^{2}}{2}
, this implies

\begin{split}-2\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx&\leq 2\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}dx\right).\end{split}

In terms of
F(x)=(1+|x|^{2})A(x)
, we find

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})\overline{\psi(x)}((AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi)(x)\ dx&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi(x)}((FD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}F)\psi)(x)\ dx\\
&\quad+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi(x)}([(1+|x|^{2}),D_{-\varepsilon}]A\psi)(x)\ dx.\end{split}

We now observe that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi(x)}((FD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}F)\psi)(x)\ dx\in{\mathbb{R}}
which is because

g(x)=(f(x-he_{1})(D_{-\varepsilon})_{1}x^{2},..,f(x-he_{1})(D_{-\varepsilon})_{3}x^{2})

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(t)&=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+\lambda\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx\\
&\leq\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon})-|A\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-V^{\varepsilon}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+|u^{\prime}(t)|\left\lVert\frac{V_{\operatorname{con}}^{\varepsilon}(\bullet)}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}\\
&\quad+2\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+2\lambda\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx\\
&\leq\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))-|A\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-V^{\varepsilon}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+C\left(1+|u^{\prime}(t)|\right)E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(t)\end{split}

(2.9)

where
C
is a constant that depends only on

,
\rho,
and
\lambda
.
Let
\beta(t)=1+|u^{\prime}(t)|
, integration of (

) from

\begin{split}E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(t)&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}(t))-|A\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}-V^{\varepsilon}(t)|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)|^{2}\ \\
&\quad+\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}(0))-|A\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)|^{2}-V^{\varepsilon}(0)|\psi_{\varepsilon}(0)|^{2}\ \right|\\
&\quad+C\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(s)\ ds+E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(0).\end{split}

Notice that by (

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|W_{\operatorname{sing}}^{\varepsilon}(x)||\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)^{2}W_{\operatorname{sing}}(x-\varepsilon y)\chi(y)|\ dy\ dx\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\chi(y)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\psi_{\varepsilon}(x+\varepsilon y,t)^{2}W_{\operatorname{sing}}(x)|\ dx\ dy\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\chi(y)\left\langle|W_{\operatorname{sing}}(\bullet)|\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet+\varepsilon y,t),\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet+\varepsilon y,t)\right\rangle_{L^{2}}\ dy.\end{split}

Therefore, by the relative zero-boundedness of the singular potential with respect to the Laplacian,
[

, it follows that for all
\delta>0

such that for all

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|W_{\operatorname{sing}}^{\varepsilon}(x)||\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(\delta\lVert\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet+\varepsilon y,t)\rVert_{2}^{2}+C_{\delta}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet+\varepsilon y,t)\rVert_{2}^{2}\right)\chi(y)\ dy\\
&=\delta\lVert\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet,t)\rVert_{2}^{2}+C_{\delta}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(\bullet,t)\rVert_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Notice also that straight from the assumptions on the magnetic vector potential and potentials

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|W_{\operatorname{reg}}^{\varepsilon}(x)||\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\leq\rho\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\rVert_{H_{1}}^{2}\text{ and }\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|A(x)|\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\leq\lVert A\rVert^{2}_{\infty}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\rVert_{H_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Then it follows that for all
\kappa>0

\begin{split}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\bar{\psi}_{\varepsilon}(x,t)(A(x)D_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A(x))\psi_{\varepsilon}(x,t))\ \right\rvert&\lesssim\frac{\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{2}}{\kappa}+\kappa\mathcal{A}\|\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\end{split}

since both

and

for suitable

Putting all these together, picking
\delta,\kappa
sufficiently small, and evaluating

E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+C_{\alpha,\mathcal{A},\rho,T}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{1}}^{2}+C_{\alpha,\mathcal{A},\rho,\lambda,T}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}^{2}+C\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)E_{\lambda}^{\varepsilon}(s)\ ds.

Then, by Gronwall’s inequality, we get
\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}\lesssim\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}.

∎

As our first theorem shows, the solution even exists in

Theorem 1
.

With the same notation and same assumptions as in Lemma

\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(-i\nabla-A(x))^{2}\psi(x,t)+V(x,t)\psi(x,t)\\

Proof.

We start by deriving a bound on

Lemma 2.4
.

There exists
C
independent of
\varepsilon
and
t

\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{2}}\leq C\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{2}}+C\lVert\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L_{2}}

Using some of the previously established results we see that

Proof.

Directly from the Schrödinger equation, we obtain

\begin{split}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{2}}&\leq\lVert\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+2\mathcal{A}\lVert D_{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+(\mathcal{A+A}^{2})\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+\rho(|u(t)|+1)\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{1}}+\lVert W_{\operatorname{sing}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+(2\mathcal{A}+\delta)\lVert\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+(\mathcal{A+A}^{2}+\rho|u(t)|+\rho+C_{\delta})\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{1}}.\end{split}

However, from
\lVert\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\leq\sqrt{\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\kappa}\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+\kappa\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{2}}

independent of
\varepsilon
and

\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{2}}\leq C\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{2}}+C\lVert\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{L_{2}}.

∎

We now continue with the energy estimates:
Starting from
i\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}=H_{B}^{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}+V^{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}
, multiply by
|x|^{4}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}
, integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and take the imaginary part

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon})\cdot\nabla(|x|^{4}\psi_{\varepsilon})-|x|^{4}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx\\
&\leq 2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx-\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx.\end{split}

Note that

\begin{split}\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx&=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(|x|^{4}AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}|x|^{4}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx\\
&\quad+\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(|x|^{4}D_{-\varepsilon}A-D_{-\varepsilon}|x|^{4}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx.\end{split}

The first term of the RHS is in
{\mathbb{R}}
by self-adjointness. Therefore

\begin{split}&\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(AD_{\varepsilon}+D_{-\varepsilon}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}(|x|^{4}D_{-\varepsilon}A-D_{-\varepsilon}|x|^{4}A)\psi_{\varepsilon}\ dx\\
&\quad-\sum_{j}(|x-\varepsilon e_{j}|^{4}A_{j}(x-\varepsilon e_{j})\psi_{\varepsilon}(x-\varepsilon e_{j})-|x|^{4}A_{j}(x)\psi_{\varepsilon}(x))\Bigg)\ dx\\
&=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\frac{i}{\varepsilon}\sum_{j}\left((|x-\varepsilon e_{j}|^{4}-|x|^{4})A_{j}(x-\varepsilon e_{j})\psi_{\varepsilon}(x-\varepsilon e_{j})\right)\ dx\lesssim\mathcal{A}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx\end{split}

for some constant

Therefore, we have shown that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx&\leq 4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+(2C\mathcal{A}+4)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx.\end{split}

Now notice that

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx&=-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\overline{\psi_{\varepsilon}}\left(|x|^{2}\Delta\psi_{\varepsilon}+2x\cdot\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}\right)\ dx\\
&\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|x|^{2}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}A\cdot A\ dx\\
&\quad+\rho(|u(t)|+1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}(|x|^{2}+|x|^{4})|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\ dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|x|^{4}|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\psi_{\varepsilon}|^{2}|W_{\operatorname{sing}}|\ dx\\
&\quad+\lVert|\bullet|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\lVert AD_{\varepsilon}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}+\lVert|\bullet|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\lVert D_{-\varepsilon}A\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}\\
&\lesssim\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H_{2}}^{2}+\lVert\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{H^{1}}^{2}+\lVert\partial_{t}\psi_{\varepsilon}\rVert_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

By Lemma
2.4
and the conservation of

and
\varepsilon

Lemma 2.6
.

With the same notation and constants as in Theorem
1
, but now for any initial state
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
on
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary conditions, the equation

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi(x,t)+V\psi(x,t)\\
\psi(x,0)&=\varphi_{0}(x)\end{split}

(2.16)

has a unique solution
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})
, and this
\psi
satisfies
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})}\leq C_{T,\alpha,\rho}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

Similarly, under the same notation and constants as in Lemma
2.3
, we have for the above equation
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})}\leq C_{T,\alpha,\rho,\mathcal{A}}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

The difference
\xi=\psi-\psi^{D}
on
\Omega
solves
i\partial_{t}\xi=(H_{0}^{B}+V_{\operatorname{TD}})\xi.

Multiplying by
\bar{\xi}
, taking imaginary parts and integration by parts gives for
n
the unit normal

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\xi(x)|^{2}\ dx&=-\Imag\left(\int_{\partial\Omega}(\overline{\xi}\nabla_{n}\xi)(x)\ dS\right)+\int_{\partial\Omega}|\psi(x)|^{2}(A\cdot n)(x)\ dS.\end{split}

If
\Omega=B_{R}(0)
and
A=\frac{B_{0}}{2}(-x_{2},x_{1},0),
the potential of a homogeneous field, then
A\cdot n=0.
While instead if
A
is a bounded function with
\lVert A\rVert_{\infty}\leq\rho
, then

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}|\psi(x)|^{2}(A\cdot n)(x)\ dS(x)\right|&=\left\lvert\int_{B_{R}(0)}\nabla\cdot(|\psi|^{2}A)(x)\ dx\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\rho\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\psi(x)|^{2}+2|(\psi\nabla\psi)(x)|\ dx\right\rvert\\
&\leq\frac{\rho}{R}\left(3R^{-4}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|x|^{4}|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx+2\int_{B_{R}(0)}|x||\psi(x)||\nabla\psi(x)|\ dx\right)\\
&\leq\frac{\rho}{R}\Bigg(3R^{-4}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|x|^{4}|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx+R^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|x|^{4}|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx\\
&+R^{-1}\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx\Bigg)\lesssim R^{-2}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

Now notice by Lemma
2.5

\begin{split}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\overline{\xi(x)}\nabla_{n}\xi(x)\ dS(x)&\lesssim\frac{\lVert\psi-\psi^{D}\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}}{R^{2}}\lesssim\frac{\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}+\lVert\varphi_{0}^{D}\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}}{R^{2}}.\end{split}

Thus, we have proven the following Theorem

Theorem 3
.

Let
\psi
be the solution to the linear Schrödinger equation with magnetic field (
1.1
) with potentials and magnetic vector potentials satisfying Assumption
1
. Then, there is some absolute constant
C_{\rho,T}
such that for any
R
sufficiently large, where
\psi^{D}
is the solution to the Dirichlet problem (
2.16
)
on
B_{R}(0),

\|\psi-\psi^{D}\|_{2}\leq C_{\rho,T}/R^{2}.

3.
Hartree equation

We now turn to the evolution of an effective single particle described by the Hartree equation with Schrödinger operator
H_{0}=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+V:D(H_{0})\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\rightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, static
pinning potential

V
,
control potential

V_{\operatorname{con}},
and time-dependent control function
u\in W^{1,1}(0,T)
such that
V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t):=u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}:

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\psi(x,t)+\left(|\psi(\bullet,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\psi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\\
\psi(\bullet,0)&=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

(3.1)

under Assumption
1
on the potentials.

Remark 4
.

Since we discussed the magnetic field and its complications already in the first part of this article, we shall neglect it in the treatment of the Hartree equation in the sequel to simplify the presentation.

3.1.
Local Existence of Solutions to the Hartree equation

We first need to show that equation (
3.1
) has a unique solution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\times[0,T]
. To do this we start by showing that a unique local solution exists and use an energy estimate to show the local solution can be extended into a unique global solution.

To show the existence of the local solution we collect in the following Lemma some basic estimates on the Hartree non-linearity, as in
[
B05
, Lemma
2.3
]
, that we shall frequently use throughout this section:

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a domain and
\psi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we then define
F_{\Omega}(\psi)(x)=\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\psi(x)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\ dy\ \psi(x)
and just write
F:=F_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}.

Then, there are constants
c,C_{F}>0
independent of
\Omega
such that

(1)

For all
\psi,\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
we have
||F_{\Omega}(\psi)-F_{\Omega}(\phi)||_{2}\leq c(||\psi||^{2}_{H^{1}}+||\phi||^{2}_{H^{1}})||\psi-\phi||_{2}.

(2)

There is
C_{F}>0
such that for all
\psi,\phi\in H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)

\begin{split}||F_{\Omega}(\psi)-F_{\Omega}(\phi)||_{H^{2}_{2}}&\leq C_{F}(||\psi||^{2}_{H^{1}}+||\phi||^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}})||\psi-\phi||_{H^{2}_{2}}\text{ and }\\
||F_{\Omega}(\psi)||_{H^{2}_{2}}&\leq C_{F}||\psi||^{2}_{H^{1}}||\psi||_{H^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

For the linear part of the nonlinear evolution equation (
3.1
), Theorem
1
and Lemma
2.6
guarantee the existence of evolution operators associated with the time-dependent Hamiltonian

H(t)=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+W_{\operatorname{sing}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}

which is a family
\{U(t,s):t,s\in[0,T]\}
on
H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that for all
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
the following properties hold

(1)

U(t,s)U(s,r)\psi_{0}=U(t,r)\psi_{0}
and
U(t,t)\psi_{0}=\psi_{0},

(2)

(t,s)\mapsto U(t,s)\psi_{0}
is strongly continuous in
L^{2}
on
[0,T]^{2}
and
U(t,s)
is an isometry on
L^{2}(\Omega),

(3)

For all
s,t\in[0,T],U(t,s)\in\mathcal{L}(H^{2}_{2})
and
(t,s)\mapsto U(t,s)\psi_{0}
is weakly continuous from
[0,T]^{2}
to
H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover there is

M=M(T,||u||_{W}^{1,1},||W_{\operatorname{sing}}||_{L^{p}},||\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}W_{\operatorname{reg}}||_{L^{\infty}},||\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}V_{\operatorname{con}}||_{L^{\infty}})

such that
||U(t,s)\psi||_{H^{2}_{2}}\leq M||\psi||_{H^{2}_{2}}.

(4)

In the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-sense, we have
i\partial_{t}U(t,s)\psi_{0}=H(t)U(t,s)\psi_{0}
and
i\partial_{s}U(t,s)\psi_{0}=U(t,s)H(t)\psi_{0}.

The evolution operators exist by Theorem
1
.

Now we can prove the existence and uniqueness of a local solution using a simple contraction argument on
\mathbb{R}^{3}:

Lemma 3.2
.

For
T_{0}>0
small enough, there exists a unique solution in
H^{2}_{2}(\Omega)
to

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\psi(x,t)+\left(|\psi(\bullet,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\psi(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T_{0})\\
\psi(\bullet,0)&=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

Proof.

Consider the functional
\Lambda:\psi\mapsto U(\bullet,0)\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{\bullet}U(\bullet,s)F(\psi(s))ds

and the set
B=\{\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\tau);H^{2}_{2}),||\psi||_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;H^{2}_{2})}\leq 2M||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}\}.

We want
\tau
to be sufficiently small that
\Lambda
is a contraction on
B
, i.e. for
\psi\in B
and Lemma
3.1

\displaystyle||\Lambda(\psi)(t)||_{H^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert U(t,0)\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t,s)F(\psi(s))ds\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq M||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}+8\tau M^{4}C_{F}||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}.

So we need to choose
\tau>0
sufficiently small that
8\tau C_{F}M^{3}||\psi_{0}||^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}<1
, and then

\begin{split}||\Lambda(\psi)||_{L^{\infty}(0,\tau;H^{2}_{2})}\leq 2M||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}.\end{split}

So
\Lambda(\psi)\in B
, and thus
\Lambda
maps
B
into itself. Now, for
\psi,\phi\in B
then for all times
t\in[0,\tau]
, using again Lemma
3.1

\displaystyle||\Lambda(\psi)(t)-\Lambda(\phi)(t)||_{H^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\int_{0}^{t}U(t,s)(F(\psi(s))-F(\phi(s)))\ ds\right\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 8\tau C_{F}M^{3}||\psi_{0}||^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}||\psi-\phi||_{L^{\infty}([0,\tau];H^{2}_{2})}.

So as
8\tau C_{F}M^{3}||\psi_{0}||^{2}_{H^{2}_{2}}<1
,
\Lambda
is a contraction on
B
and so there is a unique solution to

\begin{split}\psi(t)=U(t,0)\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t,s)F(\psi(s))ds.\end{split}

So if we apply
\partial_{t}
to this equation, and using that
i\partial_{t}U(t,s)\psi_{0}=H(t)U(t,s)\psi_{0}
we get

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\psi&=i\Delta\psi-iW_{\operatorname{reg}}\psi-iW_{\operatorname{sing}}\psi-iu(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}\psi-iF(\psi(t))\end{split}

and so
\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\tau);H^{2}_{2})
means that
F(\psi)\in L^{\infty}((0,\tau);H^{2}_{2})
and
\Delta\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,\tau);L^{2})
.
We then recall the elementary bounds, which follow from Assumption
1
,

\begin{split}||W_{\operatorname{reg}}\psi(t)||_{2}&\leq\left\lVert\frac{W_{\operatorname{reg}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(t)||_{H_{2}}\text{ and }||u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}\psi(t)||_{2}\leq\left\lVert\frac{u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(t)||_{H_{2}}.\end{split}

Now using
[
BH20
, Remark 3.8]

W_{\operatorname{sing}}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is infinitesimally bounded with respect to the negative Laplacian. Thus, for any
\varepsilon>0
there is
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\begin{split}||W_{\operatorname{sing}}\psi(t)||_{2}\leq\varepsilon||-\Delta\psi(t)||_{2}+C_{\varepsilon}||\psi(t)||_{2}.\end{split}

This implies that
||W_{\operatorname{sing}}\psi(t)||_{2}\lesssim||\psi(t)||_{H^{2}_{2}}.

By combining the previous bounds, we find
||\partial_{t}\psi(t)||_{2}\leq C_{2}||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}.

So it remains to prove that the solution is unique. Let
\psi
and
\psi^{\prime}
be two solutions and
\phi=\psi-\psi^{\prime}
. Then
\phi(0)=0
and by subtracting the Schrödinger equation for each of the solutions, multiplying by
\overline{\phi}
, integrating over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and taking the imaginary part we get

\begin{split}\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}i(\partial_{t}\phi)\overline{\phi}\ dx&=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left((-\Delta+W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})\phi+F(\psi)-F(\psi^{\prime})\right)\overline{\phi}\ dx.\end{split}

This shows that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}||\phi||^{2}_{2}&\lesssim||\phi||_{2}(||\psi||^{2}_{H^{1}}+||\psi^{\prime}||^{2}_{H^{1}})||\psi-\psi^{\prime}||_{2}\lesssim||\phi||^{2}_{2}.\end{split}

Then by Gronwall’s inequality
||\phi||^{2}_{2}=0
so
\psi=\psi^{\prime}
in
L^{2}
and so the solution is unique.
∎

3.2.
Energy Estimate

To extend our local solution to a global one we need an energy estimate of the solution of the Hartree equation for any arbitrary time
T
and show that the energy is bounded on our interval
[0,T]
.

Multiplying the equation by
\partial_{t}\overline{\psi}
, integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and taking the real part shows

\Real\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi|^{2}\ dx+\Real\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{1}{2}(W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})\partial_{t}(|\psi|^{2})+F(\psi)\partial_{t}\overline{\psi}\ dx=0.

Now to deal with the last term, we calculate

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}F(\psi)\overline{\psi}\ dx&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\psi|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(y)\partial_{t}|\psi(y,t)|^{2}\ dy+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\psi|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\partial_{t}|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\\
&=4\Real\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left(|\psi|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\psi(x,t)\partial_{t}\overline{\psi(x,t)}\ dx.\end{split}

From this we conclude that

\begin{split}2\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx+2\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})\partial_{t}(|\psi|^{2})\ dx+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}F(\psi)\overline{\psi}\ dx=0.\end{split}

This in turn leads to

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\nabla\psi|^{2}+(W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})|\psi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}F(\psi)\overline{\psi}\right)\ dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}u^{\prime}(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}|\psi|^{2}\ dx\\
&\leq\left\|\frac{u^{\prime}(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}||\psi||_{H_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Now by taking (
3.1
) and multiplying it by
(1+|x|^{2})\overline{\psi}
, we can take the imaginary part to get

\begin{split}\Imag i\partial_{t}\psi(1+|x|^{2})\overline{\psi}&=-\Imag(1+|x|^{2})\overline{\psi}\Delta\psi\text{ and }(1+|x|^{2})\partial_{t}(|\psi|^{2})=-\Imag(1+|x|^{2})\overline{\psi}\Delta\psi.\end{split}

Integrating over
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we thus get

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx&\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx.\end{split}

So now we can define
E
at time
t\in[0,T]
by

\begin{split}E(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(x,t)|^{2}+\lambda(1+|x|^{2})|\psi(x,t)|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(|\psi(t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx.\end{split}

Here,
\lambda>0
is a constant to be determined later in such a way to allow
E(t)
to be bounded

\begin{split}E^{\prime}(t)&\leq\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}||\psi||_{H_{1}}^{2}+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})(x)|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\\
&\quad+\lambda C\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|x|^{2}|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\right)\\
&\leq\left(\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\lambda C\right)E(t)+\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(W_{\operatorname{sing}}+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}})(x)|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx.\end{split}

Integrating over
(0,t)
we get

\begin{split}E(t)&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(s)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\lambda C\right)E(s)\ ds\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(W_{\operatorname{sing}}(x)+W_{\operatorname{reg}}(x)+u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}(x)|\psi(x,t)|^{2}-u(0)V_{\operatorname{con}}(x)|\psi(x,0)|^{2})\ dx.\end{split}

(3.2)

Now we use again the infinitesimal boundedness with respect to the negative Laplacian, together with
[
RS75
, Theorem X.18]
to find that for all
\varepsilon>0

\begin{split}|\langle\psi,W_{\operatorname{sing}}\psi\rangle_{2}|&\leq\varepsilon|\langle\psi,\Delta\psi\rangle_{2}|+C_{\varepsilon}\langle\psi,\psi\rangle_{2}=\varepsilon||\nabla\psi||_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}||\psi||_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

From this and (
3.2
), we obtain the estimate

\begin{split}E(t)&\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(s)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\lambda C\right)E(s)\ ds+\varepsilon||\nabla\psi(0)||_{2}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}||\psi(0)||_{2}^{2}\\
&+\left\lVert\frac{W_{\operatorname{reg}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(0)||_{H_{1}}^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{u(0)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(0)||_{H_{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon||\nabla\psi(t)||_{2}^{2}\\
&+C_{\varepsilon}||\psi(t)||_{2}^{2}+\left\lVert\frac{W_{\operatorname{reg}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(t)||_{H_{1}}^{2}+\left\|\frac{u(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}||\psi(t)||_{H_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

Taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
we find by the preservation of the
L^{2}
norm of
\psi
, and since
u\in W_{\operatorname{pcw}}^{1,1}[0,T]
that

\begin{split}E(t)&\leq C+\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(s)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\lambda C\right)E(s)\ ds+\frac{||\nabla\psi(t)||_{2}^{2}}{2}+C^{\prime}||\psi(t)||_{H_{1}}^{2}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Noting that
E(0)\lesssim||\psi_{0}||^{2}_{H^{1}\cap H_{1}}+||\psi_{0}||_{H^{1}}||\psi_{0}||_{2}^{3},
we define
G
for times
t\in[0,T]
by

\begin{split}G(t)&:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\nabla\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(1+|x|^{2})|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\psi(t,\bullet)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)|\psi(t,x)|^{2}dx\end{split}

then taking
\lambda
to be
C^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}
we can subtract
\frac{1}{2}||\nabla\psi(t)||_{2}^{2}+C^{\prime}||\psi(t)||_{H_{1}}^{2}
from (
3.3
) to see that

\begin{split}G(t)\leq C+2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(s)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty}+\lambda C\right)G(s)\ ds.\end{split}

Therefore, by Gronwall’s inequality
G(t)\lesssim\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\beta(s)ds\right),
where
\beta(t)=\left\lVert\frac{u^{\prime}(t)V_{\operatorname{con}}}{1+|\bullet|^{2}}\right\rVert_{\infty},

and so there exists
C_{T,\psi_{0}}>0
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
:

\begin{split}||\psi(t)||^{2}_{H^{1}\cap H_{1}}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left(|\psi(x,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)|\psi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx\leq C_{T,\psi_{0}}.\end{split}

3.3.
Global Existence

Now that we have an estimate for the energy we can use that the equation is equivalent to the integral equation

\begin{split}\psi(t)=U(t,0)\psi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}U(t,s)F(\psi(s))\ ds.\end{split}

Thus, the claim follows from Grönwall’s inequality applied to

\begin{split}||\psi(t)||_{H^{2}_{2}}&\lesssim||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}||F(\psi(s))||_{H^{2}_{2}}\ ds\\
&\lesssim||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}||\psi(s)||_{H^{1}}^{2}||\psi(s)||_{H^{2}_{2}}\ ds\lesssim 1+\int_{0}^{t}||\psi(s)||_{H^{2}_{2}}\ ds.\end{split}

Thus, we have shown that

Theorem 4
.

With the same notation and same assumptions as in Lemma
2.3

there exists a constant
C_{T}
such that for any
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}
, equation (
3.1
)

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)&=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi(x,t)+V\psi(x,t)\text{ with }\psi(\bullet,0)=\varphi_{0}\end{split}

has a unique solution
\psi\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})
satisfying
\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{2}_{2})}\leq C_{T,\alpha,\rho}\lVert\varphi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}.

3.4.
Existence and uniqueness of a solution on a ball

We now look on a bounded open domain with piecewise smooth boundary
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}.

To approximate the global dynamics on a bounded domain, we consider now the operator
H_{0}=-\Delta+V:D(H_{0})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
and take potentials as before, but now restricted to the domain
\Omega
with Dirichlet boundary condition. We then study the equation

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}u(x,t)&=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))u(x,t)+\int_{\Omega}\frac{|u(y,t)|^{2}}{|x-y|}\ dy\ u(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T)\\
u(x,t)&=0\quad(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T)\text{ such that }u(\bullet,0)=u_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).\end{split}

(3.4)

It is easy to see that Theorem
4
holds true with the same constant
C_{T,\alpha,\rho}
for the Dirichlet problem.

3.5.
Reduction to bounded domains

Now we need to show that the solution on the bounded domain tends in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
to the solution on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
as the radius of the ball tends to
\infty
. So first we prove the equivalent of
[
BH20
, Lemma 7.1]
for our version of the Schrödinger equation.

Lemma 3.3
.

(Perturbation of singular potentials & initial states). Let
\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}},W_{\operatorname{sing}},W_{\operatorname{reg}},V_{\operatorname{con}},u
satisfy the assumptions in Assumption
1
, and
\widetilde{\psi_{0}},\psi_{0}\in H_{2}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
be two initial states. Then the solution to

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)&=(-\Delta+\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}+\widetilde{V}_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)+\left(|\widetilde{\psi}(\bullet,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x)\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)\\
\widetilde{\psi}(\bullet,0)&=\widetilde{\psi_{0}}.\end{split}

converges in
L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
to the solution of

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}{\psi}(x,t)&=(-\Delta+{W_{\operatorname{sing}}}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t)){\psi}(x,t)+\left(|{\psi}(\bullet,t)|^{2}*\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)(x){\psi}(x,t)\\
{\psi}(\bullet,0)&=\psi_{0}\end{split}

as
\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}\to_{2}W_{\operatorname{sing}}
,
\widetilde{V}_{\operatorname{con}}\to_{2}V_{\operatorname{con}}
, and
\widetilde{\psi_{0}}\to_{2}\psi_{0}
. Furthermore there is

\begin{split}C=C(T,||\psi||_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))},||\widetilde{\psi}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))})\end{split}

such that

\begin{split}||\psi-\widetilde{\psi}||_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leq C\left(||W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+||V_{\operatorname{con}}-\widetilde{V}_{\operatorname{con}}||_{2}+||\psi_{0}-\widetilde{\psi_{0}}||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right).\end{split}

Proof.

Let
\xi=\psi-\widetilde{\psi}
and subtract the two equations to get

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\xi=\left(-\Delta+W_{\operatorname{reg}}+\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t)\right)\xi+(W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}})\psi+(F(\psi)-F(\widetilde{\psi}))\end{split}

with initial condition
\xi(0)=\psi_{0}-\widetilde{\psi_{0}}
. Multiply by
\overline{\xi}
, integrate over
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and take the imaginary part to get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}|\xi|^{2}&=\Imag\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{3}}\left((W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}})\psi+F(\psi)-F(\widetilde{\psi})\right)\overline{\xi}\\
&\leq||\xi||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\left(\left\lVert(W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}})\psi\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\left\lVert F(\psi)-F(\widetilde{\psi})\right\rVert_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\right).\end{split}

Now note that

\begin{split}\left\lVert(W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}})\psi\right\rVert_{2}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}&\leq\left\lVert W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}\right\rVert_{2}^{2}\lVert\psi\rVert^{2}_{H^{2}}/2+\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}^{2}/2.\end{split}

Integrating in time and using that
\psi\in L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))
, Lemma
3.1
yields

\begin{split}||\xi(t)||^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}&\leq||\xi(0)||^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+\left\lVert W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}\int_{0}^{t}||\psi(s)||^{2}_{H^{2}}ds+\int_{0}^{t}C||\xi(s)||_{2}^{2}ds\\
&\leq||\xi(0)||^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}+Ct\left\lVert W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+\int_{0}^{t}C||\xi(s)||_{2}^{2}ds\end{split}

where the constants depend on
||\psi||_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))},||\widetilde{\psi}||_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2})}
.

Now Grönwall’s inequality implies that

\begin{split}||\xi||_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\lesssim\left\lVert W_{\operatorname{sing}}-\widetilde{W}_{\operatorname{sing}}\right\rVert^{2}_{2}+||\psi_{0}-\widetilde{\psi_{0}}||_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}.\end{split}

The control potential can be included in a similar way.
∎

Since
u\in W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{pcw}}
it suffices to treat from now on the case that
u
is constant, since
u
can be approximated by piecewise constant functions.

Now, we are ready to prove the reduction to a boundary value problem on a bounded domain.

Theorem 5
.

Let
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}_{2}(B_{R}(0))\cap H^{1}_{0}(B_{R}(0))
be an initial state,
T\in(0,\infty)
and
V,V_{\operatorname{con}},u
as in Assumption
1
. Then the equation with solution
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
can be approximated by
\psi^{D}
, the solution to the Dirichlet boundary value problem on
B_{R}(0)
for
R>0
. In particular, the difference
\xi=\psi-\psi^{D}
satisfies, for some constant

C=C(T,||u||_{W^{1,1}_{\operatorname{pcw}}(0,T)},||W_{\operatorname{sing}}||_{2},||\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}W_{\operatorname{reg}}||_{L^{\infty}},||\langle\bullet\rangle^{-2}V_{\operatorname{con}}||_{L^{\infty}})

we have

\begin{split}||\xi||^{2}_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3}))}\leq C\left|\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\xi\nabla\bar{\xi}\ dS\right|\lesssim\frac{||\psi_{0}||_{H^{2}_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}}{R^{2}}.\end{split}

Proof.

First we use Lemma
3.3
to reduce the initial state to an initial state with support in
B_{R}(0)
. So we separate the solution
\psi
into its behaviour outside the ball
B_{R}(0)
, where the solution to the boundary value problem is zero, and it’s behaviour inside
B_{R}(0)
.

To control the difference inside the ball, take the difference
\xi=\psi-\psi^{D}
such that

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\xi(t)&=(-\Delta+V+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t))\xi(t)+F(\psi(t))-F(\psi^{D}(t))\text{ with }\xi(0)=0.\end{split}

Multiplying by
\overline{\xi}
, integrate over
B_{R}(0)
, and taking the imaginary part yields

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}&\int_{B_{R}(0)}|\xi(x,t)|^{2}dx=\Imag\left(\int_{B_{R}(0)}-\overline{\xi}\Delta\xi+(F(\psi)-F(\psi^{D}))\overline{\xi}\ dx\right)\\
&\leq\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\overline{\xi}\nabla\xi\ dS\right|+\int_{B_{R}(0)}|F(\psi)-F(\psi^{D})||\overline{\xi}|\ dx\\
&\lesssim\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\overline{\xi}\nabla\xi\ dS\right|+||\xi||_{L^{2}(B_{R}(0))}^{2}.\end{split}

Grönwall’s inequality implies then that
||\xi(t)||^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{R}(0))}\lesssim C\left|\int_{\partial B_{R}(0)}\overline{\xi}\nabla\xi\ dS\right|.
Combining the two bounds above with Lemma
2.5
yields then the bound of this Lemma.
∎

3.6.
Approximation of Coulomb kernel

Next we aim to replace the Coulomb kernel,
1/|x|,
with
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
as we need smooth functions in order to compute the solution.
It is easy to see that Theorem
4

1/|x|
replaced by
f.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\psi
be the solution to the Hartree equation and
\widetilde{\psi}
be the solution with
1/|x|
replaced by
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)\cap L^{2}(\Omega)
.

The difference of the two solutions
\xi_{:}=\widetilde{\psi}-\psi
then satisfies the PDE

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\xi=(H_{0}+V_{\operatorname{TD}})\xi+\int_{\Omega}f(\bullet-y)|\psi_{n}(y)|^{2}dy\ \widetilde{\psi}-\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\psi(y)|^{2}dy\ \psi\end{split}

(3.5)

and has the property that
\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq C_{T}\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}
.

Proof.

Now multiply (
3.5
) by
\overline{\xi}
, take the imaginary part and integrate over
\Omega
to get

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\xi\|^{2}_{2}&=\Imag\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\left(f(x-y)|\widetilde{\psi}(y)|^{2}-\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\psi(y)|^{2}\right)dy\ \psi(x)\overline{\xi}(x)dx\\
&\leq\left\lVert\int_{\Omega}f(\bullet-y)|\widetilde{\psi}(y)|^{2}-\frac{1}{|\bullet-y|}|\psi(y)|^{2}dy\right\rVert_{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\xi\|_{2}\\
&=\left\lVert\int_{\Omega}\left(f(\bullet-y)-\frac{1}{|\bullet-y|}\right)|\widetilde{\psi}(y)|^{2}+\frac{1}{|\bullet-y|}\left(|\widetilde{\psi}(y)|^{2}-|\psi(y)|^{2}\right)dy\right\rVert_{2}\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\xi\|_{2}.\end{split}

We can bound the first term in the preceding equation by Young’s convolution inequality

\begin{split}&\left\lVert\ \left(f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right)*|\widetilde{\psi}(\bullet)|^{2}\right\rVert_{2}\leq\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}\lVert|\widetilde{\psi}|^{2}\rVert_{2}\lesssim\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}\lVert\widetilde{\psi}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}^{2}\end{split}

and similarly for the second term

\begin{split}\left\lVert\frac{1}{|\bullet|}*\left(|\widetilde{\psi}(\bullet)|^{2}-|\psi(\bullet)|^{2}\right)\right\rVert_{2}&\leq\left\lVert\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert|\widetilde{\psi}(\bullet)|^{2}-|\psi(\bullet)|^{2}\right\rVert_{1}\\
&=C\int_{\Omega}|\psi(x)+\xi(x)|^{2}-|\psi(x)|^{2}\ dx\\
&\lesssim\int_{\Omega}|\psi(x)||\xi(x)|+|\widetilde{\psi}(x)||\xi(x)|\ dx\\
&\lesssim\left(\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert\widetilde{\psi}\rVert_{2}\right)\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}.\end{split}

Now using these bounds and that
\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}},\lVert\widetilde{\psi}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lesssim\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{2}&\lesssim\left(\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}^{2}+\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}\right)\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}\\
&\lesssim\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}^{3}+C\lVert\psi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}^{2}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}^{2}.\end{split}

Integrating with respect to time gives and an application of Grönwall’s inequality yields

\begin{split}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert_{2}^{2}\lesssim\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}t\exp{Ct}.\end{split}

Hence,
\lVert\xi\rVert^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})}\leq C_{T}\left\lVert f-\frac{1}{|\bullet|}\right\rVert_{1}
.
∎

4.1.1.
Perturbation of the potential

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
V,U\in L^{2}(\Omega)
be potentials and denote by
\psi_{V},\psi_{U},
two solutions to the Hartree equation (
3.4
) with the respective time-independent potential with Dirichlet boundary conditions and some initial state
\varphi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, then for some constant
C(T)>0

\|\psi_{V}-\psi_{U}\|_{2}\leq C(T,\varphi_{0})\|V-U\|_{2}.

Proof.

Looking at the difference solution

, we find using Lemma
3.1

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|\xi(x,t)|^{2}\ dx&=\Imag\left(\int_{\Omega}((V-U)\psi_{V}\bar{\xi})(x)+F(\psi_{U}(x))-F(\psi_{V}(x))\ dx\right)\\
&\leq\|V-U\|_{2}\|\psi_{V}\|_{\infty}\|\xi\|_{2}+C_{F}(\|\psi_{U}\|_{H^{2}_{2}}+\|\psi_{V}\|_{H^{2}_{2}})\|\xi\|^{2}_{2}\\
&\leq\|V-U\|_{2}^{2}\|\psi_{V}\|_{\infty}^{2}+(1+C_{F}(\|\psi_{U}\|_{H^{2}_{2}}+\|\psi_{V}\|_{H^{2}_{2}}))\|\xi\|_{2}^{2}\end{split}

which after a straightforward application of Gronwalls lemma implies the claim.
∎

4.1.2.
Bounding the convolution

To estimate the convolution in the Hartree nonlinearity, we need the following auxiliary lemma whose proof follows from elementary estimates that we shall leave to the reader:

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a domain. For
u,v,w\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, and

\begin{split}\lVert(f*(uv))w\rVert_{2}\leq\|f\|_{\infty}\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}.\end{split}

For

we have

\begin{split}\lVert(f*(uv))w\rVert_{H^{1}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}(\|u\|_{H^{1}}\|v\|_{2}\|w\|_{2}+\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{H^{1}}\|w\|_{2}+\|u\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}\|w\|_{H^{1}})\end{split}

and for
u,v,w\in H^{2}(\Omega)

\begin{split}\lVert(f*(uv))w\rVert_{H^{2}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l,m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
k+l+m=2\end{subarray}}\|u\|_{H^{k}}\|v\|_{H^{l}}\|w\|_{H^{m}}.\end{split}

Our global convergence proof now follows from combining a stability estimate in Subsection
4.1.3
, together with error propagation estimate in Subsection
4.1.4
. This will lead us to (
4.6
) which shows the convergence of full solution to a time-discretized Splitting scheme. We then study in Subsection

.

4.1.3.
Stability of the splitting scheme

For the time-evolution in the following Lemma, we introduce the magnetic Sobolev norm

\|\psi\|_{H^{2}_{B}}:=\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

which is clearly equivalent to the usual
H^{2}
norm on bounded domains for magnetic vector potentials as specified in Assumption
1
.

Lemma 4.3

(
L^{2}
and
H^{2}
stability)
.

We consider the time-discrete strang splitting scheme on some bounded domain
\Omega
for initial states
\psi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
with a magnetic field as in Assumption
1

\begin{split}\psi_{n+1/2}^{-}&=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\tau(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}\psi_{n}\\
\psi_{n+1/2}^{+}&=e^{-i\tau\left(\left(f*|\psi_{n+1/2}^{-}|^{2}\right)+V\right)}\psi_{n+1/2}^{-}\\
\psi_{n+1}&=e^{-\frac{i}{2}\tau(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}\psi_{n+1/2}^{+}.\end{split}

We then introduce the time-evolution operator by
\Phi_{\tau}\psi_{n}:=\psi_{n+1}.

Let
\psi,\phi\in H^{2}
be two states with
\|\psi\|_{H^{2}},\|\phi\|_{H^{2}}\leq M_{1}
, then

\begin{split}\|\Phi_{\tau}(\psi)-\Phi_{\tau}(\phi)\|_{2}&\leq e^{c_{0}\tau}\|\psi-\phi\|_{2}\text{ and }\|\Phi_{\tau}(\psi)-\Phi_{\tau}(\phi)\|_{H^{2}_{B}}\leq e^{c_{1}\tau}\|\psi-\phi\|_{H^{2}_{B}}.\end{split}

Proof.

As
e^{-\frac{i}{2}\tau(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}
preserves the
L^{2}
and
H^{2}_{B}
norm, we only need to consider the evolution by
e^{-i\tau\left(\left(f*|\psi|^{2}\right)+V\right)}\psi.

We shall continue with the
L^{2}
bound and observe that
e^{-i\tau\left(\left(f*|\psi|^{2}\right)+V\right)}\psi=\theta(\tau)
and
e^{-i\tau\left(\left(f*|\phi|^{2}\right)+V\right)}\phi=\eta(\tau)
where
\theta
and
\eta
are solutions to

\begin{split}i\dot{\theta}&=(f*|\psi|^{2}+V)\theta,\quad\theta(0)=\psi\\
i\dot{\eta}&=(f*|\psi|^{2}+V)\eta,\quad\eta(0)=\phi.\end{split}

So taking the difference

\begin{split}i(\dot{\theta}-\dot{\eta})&=(f*|\psi|^{2}+V)\theta-(f*|\phi|^{2}+V)\eta\\
&=(f*(\psi-\phi)\overline{\psi})\theta+(f*(\overline{\psi}-\overline{\phi})\phi)\theta+(f*|\phi|^{2})(\theta-\eta)+V(\theta-\eta).\end{split}

Integrating this identity in time, we find by the unit
L^{2}
norm of
\phi
and
\psi
, and the initial state of
\theta(0)=\psi
,
\eta(0)=\phi
that

\begin{split}\lVert\theta(t)-\eta(t)\rVert_{2}\leq\lVert\psi-\phi\rVert_{2}+K_{0}\lVert\psi-\phi\rVert_{2}(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}}+\lVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}})t\\
\quad+\left(K_{0}\rVert\phi\rVert_{H^{1}}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\right)\int_{0}^{t}\lVert\theta(s)-\eta(s)\rVert_{2}\ ds.\end{split}

By Grönwall’s inequality

\begin{split}\lVert\theta(t)-\eta(t)\rVert_{2}\leq\|\psi-\phi\|_{2}(1+K_{0}(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}})t)\exp\left(\left(K_{0}\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}\right)t\right)\end{split}

and thus, writing
\mathcal{V}[\psi]:=f*|\psi|^{2}+V
,

\begin{split}\|e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}[\psi]}\psi-e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}[\phi]}\phi\|_{2}&=\|\theta(\tau)-\eta(\tau)\|_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert\psi-\phi\rVert_{2}(1+2K_{0}M_{1}\tau)\exp((K_{0}M_{1}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}})\tau)\\
&\leq\lVert\psi-\phi\rVert_{2}e^{c_{0}\tau}.\end{split}

The proof of the
H^{2}_{B}
estimate follows in a similar fashion.
∎

4.1.4.
Error propagation

Using a Lie algebraic description, that is largely inspired by the analysis in
[
L08
]
, we want to bound the error in one step in the splitting scheme. First we let
\hat{T},\hat{V}
be vector fields defined by

\begin{split}\hat{T}(\psi)&=-i(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi=i\Delta\psi+i\langle\nabla,A\psi\rangle+i\langle A,\nabla\psi\rangle+i|A|^{2}\psi,\text{ and }\\
\hat{V}(\psi)&=-i(f*|\psi|^{2}+V)\psi,\end{split}

(4.2)

then their Lie commutator is

\begin{split}[\hat{T},\hat{V}](\psi)=&\hat{T}^{\prime}|_{\psi}(\hat{V}(\psi))-\hat{V}^{\prime}|_{\psi}(\hat{T}(\psi))\\
=&i\Delta(-i(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)\psi)+i(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)i\Delta\psi+i(f*i\Delta\psi\overline{\psi})\psi+i(f*\psi\overline{i\Delta\psi})\psi\\
&+i\langle\nabla,-iA(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)\rangle\psi+i\langle A,-i\nabla(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)\rangle\psi+2(f*|A|^{2}|\psi|^{2})\psi\\
&+if*(\langle\nabla,-iA\psi\rangle\overline{\psi}+\overline{\langle\nabla,-iA\psi\rangle}\psi)\psi+if*(\langle A,-i\nabla\psi\rangle\overline{\psi}+\overline{\langle A,-i\nabla\psi\rangle}\psi)\psi\\
=&(\langle 2f*\nabla\psi,\nabla\overline{\psi}\rangle+2f*\psi\Delta\overline{\psi}+\Delta V)\psi+2\langle(f*\nabla\psi\ \overline{\psi}+f*\psi\ \nabla\overline{\psi}+\nabla V),\nabla\psi\rangle\\
&+i\langle\nabla,-iA(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)\rangle\psi+i\langle A,-i\nabla(f*\psi\overline{\psi}-V)\rangle\psi+2(f*|A|^{2}|\psi|^{2})\psi\\
&+if*(\langle\nabla,-iA\psi\rangle\overline{\psi}+\overline{\langle\nabla,-iA\psi\rangle}\psi)\psi+if*(\langle A,-i\nabla\psi\rangle\overline{\psi}+\overline{\langle A,-i\nabla\psi\rangle}\psi)\psi.\end{split}

We thus have for the
L^{2}
norm of the commutator that

\begin{split}\lVert[\hat{T},\hat{V}](\psi)\rVert_{2}&\leq C\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+C\lVert V\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}.\end{split}

(4.3)

In the following, we write
\psi(\tau)=:\operatorname{exp}(\tau D_{K})\psi_{0}
to denote the solution to the equation

\psi^{\prime}(t)=K(\psi(t)),\quad\psi(0)=\psi_{0}.

By applying the variation of constants formula twice, we find for the solution

\begin{split}\psi(\tau)&=\exp(\tau D_{H})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\\
&=\exp(\tau D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})+\int_{0}^{\tau}\exp((\tau-s)D_{H})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\ ds\\
&=\exp(\tau D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})+\int_{0}^{\tau}\exp((\tau-s)D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\ ds+r_{1}(\psi_{0}),\end{split}

with remainder term

\begin{split}r_{1}(\psi_{0})=\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{0}^{\tau-s}\exp((\tau-s-\sigma)D_{H})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(\sigma D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\ d\sigma\ ds.\end{split}

We can also write the time-discretization of the Strang splitting (
4.1
) on continuous space
\Omega
as

\begin{split}\psi_{1}=\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\exp(\tau D_{\hat{V}})\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0}).\end{split}

By Taylor expansion, we have
\exp(\tau D_{\hat{V}})=I+\tau D_{\hat{V}}+\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)\exp(\theta\tau D_{\hat{V}})D_{\hat{V}}^{2}d\theta

\begin{split}\psi_{1}=\exp(\tau D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})+\tau\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})+r_{2}(\psi_{0})\end{split}

with remainder
r_{2}(\psi_{0})=\tau^{2}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\theta)\exp(\frac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\exp(\theta\tau D_{\hat{V}})D_{\hat{V}}^{2}\exp(\frac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})d\theta.

Therefore, the error between the actual solution and the time-discretization becomes

\begin{split}\psi_{1}-\psi(\tau)&=\tau\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(\tfrac{\tau}{2}D_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\exp((\tau-s)D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})ds+(r_{2}-r_{1})(\psi_{0}).\end{split}

Let
f(s):=\exp((\tau-s)D_{\hat{T}})D_{\hat{V}}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})
, then we have

\psi_{1}-\psi(\tau)=\int_{0}^{\tau}f(\tau/2)-f(s)\ ds+(r_{2}-r_{1})(\psi_{0}).

(4.4)

Thus, apart from the two remainder expressions, we are left to understand the operator associated with the mid-point rule
L(g)=\tau g(\frac{1}{2}\tau)-\int_{0}^{\tau}g(s)ds
. Using Peano’s kernel theorem with the Peano kernel

\begin{split}k_{1}(\theta)&=L[(\bullet-\theta)\operatorname{1\hskip-2.75ptl}_{\bullet\geq\theta}]=\tau\left(\theta-\frac{\tau}{2}\right)\operatorname{1\hskip-2.75ptl}_{\tau/2\leq\theta}-\theta^{2}/2.\end{split}

we are then left to study an operator, which by integration by parts, since
k_{1}(0)=k_{1}(\tau)=0
, satisfies

\begin{split}Lf&=\int_{0}^{\tau}k_{1}(\theta)f^{(2)}(\theta)d\theta=-\int_{0}^{\tau}k_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)f^{\prime}(\theta)d\theta.\end{split}

Now, clearly
|k_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)|=|\tau\operatorname{1\hskip-2.75ptl}_{\tau/2\leq\theta}-\theta|\leq\tau
and

\begin{split}f^{\prime}(s)&=-\exp((\tau-s)D_{\hat{T}})[D_{\hat{T}},D_{\hat{V}}]\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\\
&=\exp((\tau-s)D_{\hat{T}})D_{[\hat{T},\hat{V}]}\exp(sD_{\hat{T}})\operatorname{id}(\psi_{0})\\
&=e^{-is(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}[\hat{T},\hat{V}](e^{-i(\tau-s)(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}\psi_{0}).\end{split}

which we can bound, using (
4.3
), as

\lVert f^{\prime}(s)\rVert_{2}\leq C(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}).

This implies that

\lVert Lf\rVert_{2}\leq C\tau^{2}(\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{2}_{2}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{2,\infty}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{H^{1}_{1}}).

(4.5)

So now estimating the remainder terms in (
4.4
) using

\begin{split}\lVert\hat{V}(\psi)\rVert_{2}&\leq C\|f\|_{\infty}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}^{3}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{\infty}}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}\\
\lVert\hat{V}^{\prime}(\psi)\phi\rVert_{2}&\leq C\|f\|_{\infty}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}^{2}\|\phi\|_{2}+\lVert V\rVert_{W^{\infty}}\lVert\phi\rVert_{2},\end{split}

we infer that
\lVert r_{1}\rVert_{2}+\lVert r_{2}\rVert_{2}\leq C_{2}\tau^{2}
and hence by combining the remainder estimates with (
4.5
)
\lVert\psi_{1}-\psi(\tau)\rVert_{2}\leq C_{4}\tau^{2}.

By the usual Lady Windermere’s fan argument, this implies that

\lVert\psi_{N}-\psi(N\tau)\rVert_{2}\leq C(T)\tau.

(4.6)

This shows the explicit global convergence of our time-discretized but space-continuous splitting scheme. Our next goal is to obtain a space-discretized version of (
4.6
).

4.2.
Space-Discrete Splitting scheme

To define a convergent numerical discretization scheme, we employ a
cubic discretization
.

Cubic Discretization:

Consider a lattice of side length
h
with lattice points
(x_{j})_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}
and a family of cubes
Q_{x_{j}}=\times_{i=1}^{3}[x_{j}^{i}-\tfrac{h}{2},x_{j}^{i}+\tfrac{h}{2})
with
j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}
that form a disjoint decomposition of
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
up to a set of measure zero. The cubic approximation of a function
f\in L_{loc}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{3},\mathbb{C})
is defined by

\begin{split}f_{Q}(x):=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{3}}\frac{1}{\vol(Q_{x_{j}})}\int_{Q_{x_{j}}}f(s)\ ds\operatorname{1\hskip-2.75ptl}_{Q_{x_{j}}}(x).\end{split}

Now we need to introduce the discrete derivative, so let
(\tau_{h}^{i}f)(x)=f(x-h\hat{e_{i}})
be the translation by
h
and
\delta^{i}_{h}=(\tau^{i}_{-h}-\tau^{i}_{h})/(2h)
the discretized symmetric derivative in direction
i
with step
h>0
. Then we can define the discretized Laplacian
\Delta^{h}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(\delta^{i}_{h})^{2}
and discretized gradient
\nabla^{h}=(\delta_{h}^{i})_{i}
.

Then it is easy to verify that
(\delta^{i}_{h})^{*}=-\delta_{h}^{i}
and also the product rule holds

(\delta_{h}fg)(x)=f(x+h)(\delta_{h}g)(x)+g(x-h)(\delta_{h}f)(x).

Moreover, by
[
BH20
, Prop. 8.9]
, for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+},k\in{1,2,3},f\in W^{n,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
p\in[1,\infty)
we have

\begin{split}\lVert((\delta_{h}^{k})^{n}-\partial_{k}^{n})f\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=o(1)\operatorname{as}h\to 0\end{split}

and for
f\in W^{n+1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
and
p\in[1,\infty]
there is a constant
C>0
independent of
f
and
h
such that

\begin{split}\lVert(\delta_{h}^{k})^{n}f_{Q}-\partial^{n}_{k}f\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{W^{n+1,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}h\ \operatorname{and}\ \lVert(\delta_{h}^{k})^{n}f\rVert_{L^{p}}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{W^{n,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}.\end{split}

In addition, for
p\in(1,\infty)
,
f\in W^{n+\varepsilon,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})
, and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
it follows that

\lVert(\delta_{h}^{k})^{n}f_{Q}-\partial_{k}^{n}f\rVert_{L^{p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}=\mathcal{O}(\lVert f\rVert_{W^{n+\varepsilon,p}({\mathbb{R}}^{3})}h^{\varepsilon}).

We can now directly use
[
BH20
, Lemma 8.10]
that for our Schrödinger equation using the discretized derivative there is a constant
C=C(T)
such that
\lVert\psi^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T),H^{2}(\Omega))}\leq C\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}(\Omega)}.

Now we need the equivalent of
[
BH20
, Lemma 8.11]
, whose proof is a simple adaptation of the proof given in
[
BH20
]
, but with a
H^{2}_{h}
norm and a magnetic Schrödinger operator, instead.

Lemma 4.4
.

For an initial state
\phi_{0}\in H^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
the difference between
\psi
, the solution to the Schrödinger equation
i\partial_{t}\psi=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\psi
with initial state
\phi_{0},

and the solution
\psi^{h}
, to the discretized Schrödinger equation
i\partial_{t}\psi^{h}=H^{h}_{\operatorname{lin}}\psi^{h}
with initial state
\psi^{h}(0)=(\phi_{0})_{Q}
and Hamiltonian
H^{h}_{\operatorname{lin}}:=(-i\nabla^{h}-A^{h})^{2}

satisfies for some constant
C=C(T,\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2+\varepsilon}})

\begin{split}\lVert\psi-\psi^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2})}\leq Ch^{\varepsilon}.\end{split}

To analyze the second step of the splitting scheme, where we ought to compare the dynamics induced by
V[\psi]=f*|\psi|^{2}+V=\int_{\Omega}f(x-y)|\psi(y)|^{2}\ dy+V
with the one in which
f
is replaced by
f_{Q}
and
V
by
V_{Q}

Lemma 4.5
.

For an initial state
\phi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, potential
V\in W^{3,\infty}(\Omega)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
then there is a constant
C=C(\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}},\lVert V\rVert_{2},T)
such that if
\psi
and
\psi^{h}
are respectively the solutions to

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\psi&=(f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}+V)\psi\text{ with }\psi(0)=\phi_{0}\text{ and }\\
i\partial_{t}\psi^{h}&=(f_{Q}*|\phi_{0}|^{2}_{Q}+V_{Q})\psi^{h}\text{ with }\psi^{h}_{0}=(\phi_{0})_{Q},\end{split}

then
\lVert\psi-\psi^{h}\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2})}\leq Ch.

Proof.

Let
\xi=\psi-\psi^{h}
. Then due to

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\xi&=(f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}+V-f_{Q}*|\phi_{0}|_{Q}^{2}-V_{Q})\psi+(f_{Q}*|\phi_{0}|_{Q}^{2}+V_{Q})\xi\end{split}

we find that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}&\leq\lVert(f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}+V-f_{Q}*|\phi_{0}^{h}|^{2}-V_{Q})\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert(f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}+V_{Q})\xi\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq\lVert f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}+V-f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}-V_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\psi\rVert_{2}+\lVert f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}+V_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\xi\rVert_{2}\end{split}

and hence by Gronwall’s inequality

\begin{split}\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert_{2}&\leq\left(\lVert\phi_{0}-\phi_{0}^{h}\rVert_{2}+\lVert f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}+V-f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}-V_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\psi\rVert_{L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{2})}T\right)\\
&\quad\times\exp\left(\lVert f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}-V_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}T\right).\end{split}

Now we need to use
[
BH20
, Proposition 8.9]
and the mean value theorem to have, for constants
C

\lVert\phi_{0}-\phi_{0}^{h}\rVert_{2}\leq C\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}}h\text{ and }\lVert V-V_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}}h.

Finally for the convolution
\lVert f*|\phi_{0}|^{2}-f_{Q}*(|\phi_{0}|^{2})_{Q}\rVert_{\infty}\leq C\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert^{2}_{2}h.
Hence, there is
C=C(\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{1}},\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}},\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}},T)
such that

\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\lVert\xi(t)\rVert_{2}\leq Ch.

∎

Now we want to put all this together to get a scheme for calculating an approximation to the solution.

As an intermediate step, we use a continuous (in space) Strang splitting scheme

\begin{split}\psi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=e^{-\frac{i\tau(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}{2}}\psi_{S}(t_{k})\\
\psi^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=\exp\left(-i\tau\mathcal{V}(\psi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right)\psi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\\
\psi_{S}(t_{k+1})&=e^{-\frac{i\tau(-i\nabla-A)^{2}}{2}}\psi^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

(4.7)

where
\psi_{S}(0)=\phi_{0}
and
\mathcal{V}(\psi):=f*|\psi|^{2}+V
. Finally, we want to approximate this scheme using the cubic discretization and a Crank-Nicholson method for the propagation of the linear kinetic operator
(-i\nabla-A)^{2}.
In particular,

\mathcal{C}^{\tau}_{h}\phi:=\left(1-\frac{i\tau}{2}H^{h}_{\operatorname{lin}}\right)\left(1+\frac{i\tau}{2}H^{h}_{\operatorname{lin}}\right)^{-1}\phi.

Recall that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
t\in[0,T]
there is
C_{T}>0
such that

\left|e^{-it\lambda}-\left(\frac{1-i\frac{t}{2}\lambda}{1+i\frac{t}{2}\lambda}\right)\right|\leq C_{T}t^{3}|\lambda|^{3},

we see, using functional calculus that there is (Taylor expansion) some constant
C_{T}>0
independent of
h
and
\tau\in[0,T]
such that

\begin{split}\left\lVert\left(e^{-iH_{\operatorname{lin}}^{h}\tau}-\mathcal{C}^{\tau}_{h}\right)\psi^{h}(t_{k})\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{T}\tau^{3}/h^{6}.\end{split}

(4.8)

This yields a Strang splitting scheme

\begin{split}\phi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=\mathcal{C}_{h}^{\tau/2}\phi_{S}(t_{k})\\
\phi^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}&=\exp_{K}\left(-i\tau\mathcal{V}_{Q}(\phi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}})\right)\phi^{-}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\\
\phi_{S}(t_{k+1})&=\mathcal{C}_{h}^{\tau/2}\phi^{+}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}\end{split}

(4.9)

where
\psi_{S}(0)=(\phi_{0})_{Q}
and
\mathcal{V}_{Q}(\psi_{Q}):=f_{Q}*(|\psi|^{2})_{Q}+V_{Q}
.

Theorem 6
.

Given an initial state
\phi_{0}\in H^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and
V,f\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
. There is a constant
C=C(T,\tau,\lVert\phi_{0}\rVert_{H^{2}},\lVert V\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}},\lVert f\rVert_{W^{1,\infty}})
such that the solution
\phi_{S}
obtained from the Strang splitting scheme (
4.9
) and the full solution
\psi
satisfy

\max_{k;0\leq k\leq\lfloor T/\tau\rfloor}\rVert\psi(\tau k)-\psi_{S}(\tau k)\rVert_{2}\leq C\lVert\phi_{0}-(\phi_{0})_{Q}\rVert_{2}.

(4.10)

Proof.

We have already shown the convergence to the continuous Strang splitting scheme (
4.7
) in Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
. It therefore suffices to approximate the continuous splitting scheme by the discrete one
.
The
L^{2}
-convergence to the discrete approximation by the Crank-Nicholson method in the discrete Strang splitting scheme (
4.9
), follows already from (
4.8
). Thus, it only remains to prove the convergence of the propagation of the non-linearity:

\begin{split}\lVert&\left(e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}(\psi^{-}_{k+1/2})}-e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}_{Q}(\phi^{-}_{k+1/2})}\right)\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq\left\lVert\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}-\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\right\rVert_{2}+\left\lVert\left(e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}(\psi^{-}_{k+1/2})}-e^{-i\tau\mathcal{V}_{Q}(\phi^{-}_{k+1/2})}\right)\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\right\rVert_{2}\\
&\leq\left\lVert\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}-\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\right\rVert_{2}+\tau\|f*|\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}|^{2}-f_{Q}*|\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}|^{2}\|_{\infty}\\
&\leq\left\lVert\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}-\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\right\rVert_{2}+\tau\|f*(|\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}|^{2}-|\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}|^{2})\|_{\infty}+\tau\left\lVert f-f_{Q}\right\rVert_{\infty}\\
&\leq(1+2\tau\|f\|_{\infty})\left\lVert\psi^{-}_{k+1/2}-\phi^{-}_{k+1/2}\right\rVert_{2}+\tau\left\lVert f-f_{Q}\right\rVert_{\infty}.\end{split}

So we have enough for global Lipschitz estimate (
4.10
) of the discrete Strang splitting scheme.

∎

\mathcal{I}(u):=\left\langle\psi(T),S\psi(T)\right\rangle+\kappa\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{1}_{0}(0,T)}^{2}

for a positive operator

Examples of such optimal control problems are numerous

Lemma 5.1
.

The functional
\mathcal{I}
has a minimizer in
H^{1}_{0}(0,T)
.

Proof.

The Banach-Alaoglu theorem implies the existence of a
H^{1}_{0}(0,T)
-weakly convergent subsequence
u_{n}\overset{}{\rightharpoonup}u
such that
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{I}(u_{n})=\inf_{w\in H^{1}_{0}(0,T)}\mathcal{I}(w).

Since

is weakly convergent, the sequence is bounded. Hence, the sequence
\psi_{n}
of solutions to (

and joint initial value

is bounded in

As

is compact in
H_{2-\varepsilon}^{2-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
the Aubin-Lions lemma implies that for yet another subsequence of
(\psi_{n})

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left\lVert\psi_{n}-\psi\right\rVert_{C((0,T);H_{2-\varepsilon}^{2-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}=0

where
\psi
is the solution to the Schrödinger equation with initial value

and control

Weak lower semicontinuity of the

The

\begin{split}i\partial_{t}\zeta&=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}\zeta+V\zeta+V_{\operatorname{TD}}(t)\zeta,\ (x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,T)\\

. For the linear Schrödinger equation this equation reads

-2\kappa u^{\prime\prime}(t)=\left\langle\zeta(t),V_{\operatorname{con}}\psi(t)\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},\ u\in H^{1}_{0}(0,T),

the solution to the adjoint problem.
Various algorithms
[

]

1.1
), adjoint equation (

Our findings of the previous sections showed that it is possible to compute the solution to the Schrödinger equation by restricting it to an auxiliary BVP. The following example shows that this is not possible for optimal control problems.

Example 2
.

Consider an odd control potential
V
and an even initial state
\varphi_{0}
to the Schrödinger equation

\begin{split}i\psi_{u}^{\prime}(t,x)=\left(-\partial_{x}^{2}+u(t)V\right)\psi_{u}(t,x),\quad\psi(0)=\varphi_{0}.\end{split}

We also take
S
to be an even function in the window that the algorithm samples such that there exists a non-zero optimal control with
L^{2}
distance to any other optimal control of at least
2\varepsilon.
Our assumptions guarantee that if
u
is an optimal control, then so is
-u
as the solution satisfies
\psi_{u}(x)=\psi_{u}(-x)
and
S
is assumed to be an even function in the window the algorithm samples.

It suffices now to continue
S
in such a way that the control functional (
5.1
) favors
u
over
-u
. The algorithm necessarily has to include both
u
and
-u
and will therefore be off by a Hausdorff distance
2\varepsilon
at least.

Thus, it is impossible to numerically approximate optimal controls numerically by sampling only a finite amount of data. However, it is possible to approximate the value of the control functional for a positive Schrödinger operator
S=(-i\nabla-A)^{2}+U
with magnetic potential
A
and control potential
U
(possibly different from the potentials governing the evolution of the equation) satisfying the conditions of the static potential in Assumption
1
.

Proof.

The result follows straight from the result we have already established in previous sections:
We first observe that by Theorems
2
and
6
, we can compute up to any precision the value of the control functional (
5.1
) for any fixed control
u
in the domain of the computational problem.
Thus, by computing the value of the control functional for any fixed control, we obtain an upper bound on the minimal value of (
5.1
) and thus also on the
H^{1}_{0}
bound of any optimal control. This yields a bound on the absolute value of Fourier coefficients on the optimal control. This implies an error that does not exceed
\varepsilon
in the optimal control functional, as the solution is easily seen to be Lipschitz continuous with respect to controls in
L^{1}(0,T)
.∎

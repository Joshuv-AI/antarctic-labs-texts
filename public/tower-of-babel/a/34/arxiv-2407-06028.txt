Optical reflection signature of an axion dielectric with magnetic current
Pedro D. S. Silva a, Ronald A. Pereira a, Manoel M. Ferreira Jr. a,b

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the reflection properties on the interface between an ordinary dielectric medium and a dielectric supporting a magnetic current (equivalent to a dielectric governed by axion electrodynamics). Considering the usual Maxwell equations and constitutive relations, we derive the general Fresnel coefficients for reflection for an incident wave with s s and p p polarization, assuming an isotropic magnetic current on a dielectric substrate. We determine all total internal reflection and critical angles (Brewster angles) conditions, which are given by strict relations between all relevant electromagnetic quantities of the system and the frequency. For s s - and p p -polarized incident waves, total internal reflection can occur under certain conditions on the constitutive parameters (for each propagating mode) at specific frequency windows and certain incidence angle intervals. All possible conditions to define critical angles for null reflection are determined. This scenario allows polarization changes by reflection. Considering a p p -polarized incident wave, the frequency and the Brewster angle, allowing null reflection for both propagating modes, are determined. The Goos-Hänchen shift and the complex Kerr rotation are also evaluated. The Kerr ellipticity angle presents a frequency-dependent behavior, also reported in Weyl semimetals, having maximum values η K ​ e ​ r ​ r = ± π / 4 \eta_{Kerr}=\pm\pi/4 for specific values of frequency, which may work as a signature of this axion chiral dielectric.

Another scenario studied in the last few years is dielectric matter supporting magnetic currents, in connection with the chiral magnetic effect (CME). Such an effect consists of a macroscopic magnetic current law,

In this sense, the present work is devoted to analyzing the optical reflection aspects in an interface between a usual dielectric and a dielectric endowed with magnetic current,

Chiral dielectric endowed with magnetic conductivity

Classical electromagnetic and optical properties of a dielectric medium in the presence of a magnetic current density have been recently addressed
[
56
,
57
,
58
]

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf D}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!0\;,\hskip 8.5359pt\hskip 8.5359pt\nabla\times{\bf E}+\frac{\partial{\bf B}}{\partial t}={\bf 0}\;,

(1a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf B}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!0\;,\hskip 8.5359pt\hskip 8.5359pt\nabla\times{\bf H}-\frac{\partial{\bf D}}{\partial t}={\bf J}\;,

(1b)

where
{\bf J}
is the current density. The system is ruled by conventional constitutive relations,

{\bf{D}}=\epsilon{\bf{E}},\,\,\,{\bf{B}}=\mu{\bf{H}},

(2)

where
\epsilon
is the electric permittivity and
\mu
is the magnetic permeability.
The magnetic current constitutive relation can be written in terms of the conductivity tensor,
\sigma^{B}_{ij}
,

{J}^{i}={\sigma}_{ij}^{B}\,{B}^{j}\;

(3)

which replaced in Maxwell´s equations, in the plane-wave
ansatz
, yields

({\bf{k}}\times{\bf{k}}\times{\bf{E}})^{i}+\mu\,\epsilon\,\omega^{2}\,{{E}^{i}}+\mathrm{i}\,\mu\,{\sigma}_{ij}^{B}({\bf{k}}\times{\bf{E}})^{j}=0\,.

(4)

The wave equation for the electric field amplitude is

\displaystyle\left[\,{\bf k}^{2}\,\delta_{ij}-k_{i}\,k_{j}-\omega^{2}\mu\,\overline{\epsilon}_{ij}(\omega)\,\right]E^{j}=0\;,

(5)

with the effective permittivity tensor

\displaystyle\overline{\epsilon}_{ij}(\omega)=\epsilon(\omega)\,\delta_{ij}-\frac{i}{\omega^{2}}\,\sigma^{B}_{ia}\,\epsilon_{abj}\,k_{b}\;.

(6)

For an isotropic magnetic conductivity,
\sigma^{B}_{ij}=\Sigma\,\delta_{ij}
, where
\Sigma
is a real parameter, one obtains a Hermitian permittivity,

{\bar{\epsilon}}_{ij}(\omega)=\epsilon\,{\delta}_{ij}+{\frac{{\mathrm{i}\Sigma}}{{\omega}^{2}}}\,\epsilon_{ijb}\,k_{b}\,,

(7)

which assures energy conservation and the absence of dissipation. Relation (
5
) may also be written as

{M}_{ij}E^{j}=0,

(8)

with

{M}_{ij}={\bf k}^{2}\,\delta_{ij}-k_{i}\,k_{j}-\omega^{2}\mu\,\overline{\epsilon}_{ij}(\omega)\;.

(9)

The dispersion relations of the model are the non-trivial solutions of
Eq. (
8
)
, obtained requiring
\det[{M_{ij}}]=0
, which provides distinct real refractive indices,

n_{\pm}=\sqrt{\mu\epsilon+\left(\frac{\mu\Sigma}{2\omega}\right)^{2}}\pm\frac{\mu\Sigma}{2\omega}\;.

(10)

These indices were first carried out in Refs.
[
56
,
57
]
, defining a dispersive non-absorbing medium endowed with birefringence. The propagation modes for the indices (
10
), obtained from the relation
M_{ij}E_{j}=0
, are

\mathbf{E}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{n\sqrt{2(n_{1}^{2}+n_{3}^{2})}}\begin{pmatrix}nn_{3}\mp\mathrm{i}n_{1}n_{2}\\
\pm\mathrm{i}(n_{1}^{2}+n_{3}^{2})\\
\mp\mathrm{i}n_{2}n_{3}-nn_{1}\\
\end{pmatrix}\;,

(11)

which for a wave propagating at the
{\cal Z}
-axis,

\mathbf{n}=(0,0,n_{3})
, yields

\mathbf{E}_{\pm}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\pm\mathrm{i}\\
0\\
\end{pmatrix}\,.

(12)

The circular birefringence is quantified by the specific rotatory power
\delta

[
60
,
59
,
61
]

\delta=-\frac{\omega}{2}[\mathrm{Re}(n_{+})-\mathrm{Re}(n_{-})]\;,

which measures the rotation of the oscillation plane of linearly polarized light per unit traversed length in the medium, with

and
n_{-}
associated with left and right-handed circularly polarized waves, respectively. For the refractive indices (
10

\delta=-\frac{\mu\Sigma}{2}\;.

(14)

There exists a relevant interplay between the CME and the Maxwell-Carroll-Field-Jackiw (MCFJ) electrodynamics
[
62
,
63
]
, which contains non-dynamical axion terms. In fact, the magnetic current term appears in the axion Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=-\frac{1}{4}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}+\theta(\mathbf{E}\cdot\mathbf{B)},

where the axion field

\displaystyle\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=\rho-\mathbf{\nabla}\theta\cdot\mathbf{B},

(16)

\displaystyle\mathbf{\nabla}\times\mathbf{B}-\partial_{t}\mathbf{E}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{j}+(\partial_{t}\theta)\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{\nabla}\theta\times\mathbf{E}.

The axion gradient composes the anomalous charge density term,

[

,

,

and Casimir effect
[

V.1

When the incidence angle is greater than

\displaystyle\bar{\theta}_{i}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{arc}\sin\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}\left(\sqrt{\mu_{2}\epsilon_{2}+\frac{\mu_{2}^{2}\Sigma^{2}}{4\omega^{2}}}\pm\frac{\mu_{2}\Sigma}{2\omega}\right)\,\right],

(47)

where
\lambda
is the wavelength of the incident light and

2
2

2

In a standard reflection scenario between two usual dielectrics (
\Sigma=0

.

which happens for most usual materials
[
39
]
. Implementing
Eq. (
53
)
in
Eq. (
56
)
, it provides

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{1}\mu_{2}n_{1}\left(n_{2+}-n_{2-}\right)}{\mu_{1}^{2}n_{2+}n_{2-}-\mu_{2}^{2}n_{1}^{2}}.

(59)

Replacing
n_{1}=\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}
and
Eq. (
19
)
in
Eq. (
59
)
, one finds

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}}\frac{\mu_{2}\Sigma}{\omega}.

(60)

Finally, by applying the real result (
60
) in
Eq. (
57
)
and
Eq. (
58
)
, we obtain
\theta_{K}=0
and

\displaystyle\sin(2\eta_{K})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}}\frac{\mu_{2}\Sigma}{\omega}\,\frac{1}{1+\frac{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}{(\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1})^{2}}\left(\frac{\mu_{2}\Sigma}{\omega}\right)^{2}}.

(61)

The latter represents a frequency-dependent ellipticity Kerr angle (a consequence of non-null magnetic conductivity
\Sigma
), which is illustrated in Fig.
16
in terms of the dimensionless parameter
x=\omega/\Sigma
. The maximum value of
\eta_{Kerr}=\pm\pi/4
happens for specific values of frequency, given by

\displaystyle x^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}{\pm\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}\mp\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}}.

(62)

The plus (minus) sign of
\eta_{Kerr}
indicates the handedness of the reflected wave, which will be left- (right-) handed elliptically polarized
[
1
]
. The plus (minus) sign of
x^{\pm}
corresponds to the cases where
\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}
is positive (negative), which leads to
\eta_{Kerr}>0
(
\eta_{Kerr}<0
). Furthermore, the possible maximum values for Kerr ellipticity are
\pm\pi/4
regardless of the values of permeabilities (
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
) and permittivities (
\epsilon_{1},\epsilon_{2}
). In the special case of
\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}=0
, one finds
\eta_{Kerr}\rightarrow 0
, meaning that the reflected wave will be linearly polarized.

In the very low-frequency regime, the Kerr ellipticity angle (
61
) behaves linearly with the frequency,

\displaystyle\left.\eta_{Kerr}\right|_{low}\simeq\frac{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}}{\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}\frac{\omega}{\Sigma},

while for very high frequencies it goes as

\displaystyle\left.\eta_{Kerr}\right|_{high}\simeq\frac{\mu_{2}\sqrt{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{1}}}{\mu_{1}\epsilon_{2}-\mu_{2}\epsilon_{1}}\frac{\Sigma}{\omega}.

(64)

We point out that the general frequency behavior of the Kerr ellipticity (
61
), depicted in Fig.
16
, is analogous to the frequency-dependent Kerr effect observed in Weyl semimetals
[
40
]
, where the optical activity is caused by the axion term. The evaluation of such an optical quantity in exotic materials endowed with magnetic conductivity may work as a tool to measure the magnitude of
\Sigma
.

VI
Final Remarks

In this work, we have analyzed the reflection at the interface between an ordinary dielectric and a dielectric endowed with magnetic conductivity, starting from the Fresnel coefficients for
s
and
p
polarizations, presented in Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
), respectively.

For both polarizations, two frequency-dependent reflection coefficients were obtained,
R^{\pm}
, in considering the refractive indices
n_{+}
and
n_{-}
. The behavior of the reflection coefficients was examined in terms of the incidence angle and frequency. The dispersive character is a novelty that brings several interesting new reflection properties.

For the polarization-s, the two reflection coefficients are
R^{\pm}_{\perp}
. As for
R^{+}_{\perp}

2
and Fig.
3

4

\Sigma
.

Concerning the reflection coefficient

, its behavior in terms of frequency is shown in Figs.
5
and
6
, being substantially distinct from the one of
R^{+}_{\perp}
. For non-null
\Sigma
values, the coefficient

becomes equal to 1 in the low-frequency interval

given by
Eq. (

)
, representing a scenario of total reflection induced by the dependence on the frequency of
n_{2-}
. Indeed, for

, there occurs
n_{2-}<n_{1}
, and the Snell’s law explains the total reflection. An additional interesting property is the fact of

for a specific value of frequency,
\omega_{0}
, given by
Eq. (
33
)
, as shown in Figs.
5
and
6
, representing a partial Breswter angle for this polarizarion. The behavior of

in terms of the incidence angle
\theta_{1}
was also examined in Fig.
7

, with

given by
Eq. (
34
)
. This total reflection region is also a consequence of the dispersive behavior of

, which does not occur in a conventional dielectric (see the dashed black line in Fig.
7

For incident
p
-polarization, the reflection coefficients

are given in

and

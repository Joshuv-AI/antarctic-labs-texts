1.4.
Outlook

The study of Galois representations attached to Drinfeld modules suggests a number of distribution problems that merit further investigation. One natural step is to investigate the full adelic Galois image of Drinfeld modules of specific ranks. However, achieving such results remains challenging. Even if the residual Galois representation is surjective modulo two distinct primes
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
, the image modulo
\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q}
may still be strictly smaller than
\mathrm{GL}_{r}(A/\mathfrak{p}\mathfrak{q})
due to arithmetic entanglements between the corresponding torsion fields. Put differently, the division fields attached to different primes need not be linearly disjoint, and controlling such intersections requires a much finer analysis than what is currently available. The argument of Jones proving that
100\%
of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
have surjective adelic Galois image cannot be adapted to the Drinfeld setting, since it relies on analytic estimates with no known analogue over global function fields. Following Duke, the method uses a large sieve to control exceptional primes, and its key input is an estimate for weighted sums of Hurwitz class numbers in arithmetic progressions, obtained via the Fourier expansion of half-integral weight modular forms together with the Ramanujan bound for their coefficients
[
5
, Lemma 3]
. For Drinfeld modules, these methods do not carry over.

A related problem that has been of interest is that of constructing examples of Drinfeld modules of general rank
r
with maximal Galois image. This question is still unresolved. Another potential direction of inquiry is to characterize the mod-
T
(and
T
-adic) Galois images of Drinfeld modules over rational function fields.

Drinfeld Modules and

Definition 2.1
.

An

-algebra homomorphism

If

We set

A Drinfeld module is a faithful
A
-module structure on the additive group scheme. In order to make this notion precise, we introduce the

-algebra of non-commutative twisted polynomials over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra

is defined by the rules:

•

Addition is done term-wise.

•

Multiplication is defined according to the rule
(a\tau^{i})(b\tau^{j})=ab^{q^{i}}\tau^{i+j}

For a twisted polynomial

One thus gets a polynomial that is

•

•

One defines the

Note that the degrees of

and

are related by
\operatorname{deg}_{x}f(x)=q^{\operatorname{deg}_{\tau}(f)}
. Consider the derivative map
\partial\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}K\{\tau\}\rightarrow K

\partial\left(\sum_{i=0}a_{i}\tau^{i}\right)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=a_{0},

and note that

The

Let

Thus the Galois group

v(w_{i})\geq 0
. It follows from Lemma
5.4
that
v(e_{\Lambda}(w_{i}))=v(w_{i})\geq 0
. We find that

\varphi_{b}(x)=cx+d\varphi_{T}(x)=bx+db_{1}x^{q}+\dots+db_{(r-1)}x^{q^{(r-1)}},

and therefore,

v\left(\varphi_{b}(e_{\Lambda}(z))\right)=q^{(r-1)}v(e_{\Lambda}(z))=\begin{cases}q^{(r-1)}v(z)&\text{ if }d\neq 0;\\
v(z)&\text{ if }d=0.\end{cases}

Therefore, we have shown that if
b\neq 0
,

v\left(e_{\Lambda}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1}+bz)\right)=v\left(\varphi_{b}(e_{\Lambda}(z))\right)=q^{(r-1)i}v(z),

where
i\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\operatorname{deg}_{T}(b)
.

When
b=0
, it follows from Lemma
5.4
that

v\left(e_{\Lambda}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1})\right)=v(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1}).

∎

We conclude this section with the proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Proof of Proposition
5.2
.

Applying Lemma
5.5
, we simplify the right hand side of (
5.3
). First, we note that

\begin{split}&\sum_{a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1},b;b\neq 0}v\left(e_{\Lambda}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1}+bz)\right)\\
=&q^{2(r-1)}\left(\sum_{b,\operatorname{deg}b=0}v(z)+\sum_{b,\operatorname{deg}b=1}q^{(r-1)}v(z)\right)\\
=&q^{2(r-1)}v(z)\left((q-1)+q(q-1)q^{(r-1)}\right)\\
=&q^{2(r-1)}(q-1)v(z)(1+q^{r}).\end{split}

On the other hand,

\begin{split}&\sum_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})\neq 0}v\left(e_{\Lambda}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1})\right)\\
=&\sum_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})\neq 0}v(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1})\\
=&v\left(\prod_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{r-1})\neq 0}(a_{1}w_{1}+a_{2}w_{2}+\dots+a_{r-1}w_{r-1})\right)\\
=&v\left(\prod_{0\neq w\in\phi[T^{2}]}w\right)=v(T^{2})=0.\end{split}

For the final relation, we use the fact that
\prod_{0\neq w\in\varphi[T^{2}]}w
is the constant term of
\varphi_{T^{2}}(x)/x
and therefore equals
T^{2}
.
Thus the right hand side of (
5.5
) equals

q^{2(r-1)}(q-1)v(z)(1+q^{r}).

Thus, we have shown that

v(z)=-\frac{v(g_{r})}{(q-1)q^{2(r-1)}}.

Since
p\nmid v(g_{r})
, we find that
q^{2(r-1)}
divides the ramification index of
F_{\mathfrak{l}}(z)/F_{\mathfrak{l}}
. This implies that
q^{2(r-1)}
divides
\#\rho_{2}(\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{l}})
. Note that
\rho_{2}(\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{l}})
is contained in
\mathcal{U}_{2}
and
\#\mathcal{U}_{2}=(q-1)q^{2r-1}
. Also note that
\#\mathcal{U}_{1}=(q-1)q^{r-1}
and
\#\mathcal{W}=q^{r}
. We deduce that
q^{r-1}
divides
\#\left(\rho_{2}(\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{l}})\cap\mathcal{W}\right)
. This proves the result.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

One has the following inclusion of sets:

\Pi_{r}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime\prime}.

Proof.

Let
\vec{g}\in\Pi_{r}
, then by assumption there is a prime
\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}
such that
\mathfrak{l}\neq(T)
and

•

v(g_{r-1})=0
,

•

p\nmid v(g_{r})
.

Proposition
5.2
implies that
\rho_{2}(\operatorname{I}_{\mathfrak{l}})
contains a nontrivial element in
\mathcal{W}
. Note that
\mathcal{W}
does not contain any scalar matrices. Therefore, it follows that
\mathcal{H}(\vec{g})^{[1]}
contains a non-scalar matrix. In other words,
\vec{g}\in\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

6.
Density results

In this section we prove that
\mathfrak{d}(\mathcal{F}_{r})=1
. We begin by showing that
\Pi_{r}
defined in the previous section has density
1
.

Proposition 6.1
.

With respect to notation above,
\mathfrak{d}(\Pi_{r})=1
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega_{r}
be the subset of
\mathcal{C}_{r}
consisting of
\vec{g}=(g_{1},\dots,g_{r})
such that for all primes
\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}\setminus(T)
, one has that
v(g_{r})=0
or
v(g_{r})\geq p
. It is clear that the complement of
\Pi_{r}
is contained in
\Omega_{r}
. We show that
\mathfrak{d}(\Omega_{r})=0
and deduce that
\mathfrak{d}(\Pi_{r})=1
. Let
\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathfrak{l})
consist of all tuples in
(A/\mathfrak{l}^{p})^{r}
and consider the
\mathfrak{l}^{p}
-reduction map

\pi_{\mathfrak{l}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{C}_{r}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathfrak{l}).

Let
\Omega_{r}(\mathfrak{l})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\pi_{\mathfrak{l}}\left(\Omega_{r}\right)
, consisting of all tuples
(\bar{g}_{1},\bar{g}_{2},\dots,\bar{g}_{r})
such that
\mathfrak{l}\nmid\bar{g}_{r}
or
\bar{g}_{r}=0
. Therefore, we find that
\#\Omega_{r}(\mathfrak{l})=q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p(r-1)}\left(q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}-q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p-1}+1\right)
, where
q_{\mathfrak{l}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=q^{\operatorname{deg}(\mathfrak{l})}
. Define

\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{l})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{\#\Omega_{r}(\mathfrak{l})}{\#\mathcal{C}_{r}(\mathfrak{l})}=\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}}+\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}}\right)

and given a finite set of primes
S\subset\Omega_{A}\backslash(T)
set
\mathfrak{d}_{S}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in S}\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{l})
. Define
\Omega_{r}^{S}
to be the set of all
\vec{g}\in\mathcal{C}_{r}
such that for all primes
\mathfrak{l}\in S
,

v(g_{r})=0\text{ or }v(g_{r})\geq p.

Since
\Omega_{r}^{S}
is defined by finitely many congruence conditions, it is easy to see that

\Omega_{r}^{S}\sim\mathfrak{d}_{S}q^{rX}.

On the other hand,
\Omega_{r}\subset\Omega_{r}^{S}
and thus for all
S
,

\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(\Omega_{r})\leq\mathfrak{d}(\Omega_{r}^{S})=\left(\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in S}\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{l})\right).

Since
S
is an arbitrary finite subset of
\Omega_{A}\backslash(T)
, it follows that

\overline{\mathfrak{d}}(\Omega_{r})\leq\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}\backslash(T)}\mathfrak{d}(\mathfrak{l})\leq\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}\backslash(T)}\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}}+\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}}\right).

Lemma
6.2
shows that

\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}\backslash(T)}\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}}+\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}}\right)=0

and thus it follows that
\mathfrak{d}(\Omega_{r})=0
. This implies that
\mathfrak{d}(\Pi_{r})=1
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

With respect to notation above,

\prod_{\mathfrak{l}\in\Omega_{A}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}}+\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}}\right)=0.

Proof.

Note that

\prod_{\mathfrak{l}}\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}}+\frac{1}{q_{\mathfrak{l}}^{p}}\right)=\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{n}}+\frac{1}{q^{np}}\right)^{c_{n}},

where
c_{n}
is the number of irreducible polynomial of degree
n
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. This product converges to
0
if and only if

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}\operatorname{log}\left(1-\frac{1}{q^{n}}+\frac{1}{q^{np}}\right)

goes to
-\infty
. By the limit comparison test, this is equivalent to

\sum_{n}\frac{c_{n}}{q^{n}}

goes to
+\infty
. We have that

c_{n}=q^{n}/n+O(q^{n/2}/n)

[
12
, Theorem 2.2]
the result follows.
∎

Theorem 6.3
.

Let
r\geq 2

\mathcal{F}_{r}

Proof.

Noting that
\mathcal{F}_{r}=\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime}\cap\mathcal{C}_{r}^{\prime\prime}

6.1

∎

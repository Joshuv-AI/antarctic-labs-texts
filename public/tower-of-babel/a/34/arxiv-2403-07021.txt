Stochastic Quantum Dynamics Stabilization: A Lyapunov-Based Control Approach with Homodyne-Mediated Filtering
Nahid Binandeh Dehaghani, A. Pedro Aguiar, Rafal Wisniewski

Abstract
Efficient control of stochastic dynamics in quantum systems is pivotal for various applications, including quantum information processing and metrology. This paper introduces a Lyapunov-based control approach with homodyne-mediated filtering. We employ a modified extended Kalman filtering method to directly estimate the evolution of the quantum density operator ρ \rho , considering sequential homodyne current measurements. Our method explicitly addresses the dynamics of a stochastic master equation with correlated noise, ensuring by construction the quantum properties of the estimated state variable ρ ^ \hat{\rho} . Moreover, our proposed switching based Lyapunov control scheme that is fed with ρ ^ \hat{\rho} , guarantees noise-to-state practically stable in probability of the desired stationary target set with respect to the estimation error variance. We demonstrate our approach’s efficacy in stabilizing a qubit coupled to a leaky cavity under homodyne detection.

with the aim of
steering the system’s state
\rho(t)
from an initial condition
\rho_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
towards a predetermined stationary point within the orbit
O_{f}(\rho_{0}):=\{\hat{X}\rho_{0}\hat{X}^{\dagger}\mid\hat{X}\in U(n)\}
, where any target state
\rho_{f}
within
O_{f}(\rho_{0})
is characterized by its commutation with the drift Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{d}
, that is,
[\rho_{f},\hat{H}_{d}]=0
. Here,
U(n)
denotes the group of
n\times n
unitary matrices. The aim is to identify states that are unitarily equivalent to
\rho_{0}
, implying these states share the same spectrum as the initial state.
Prior to delving into the control design, we introduce the following stability definitions pertinent to the stochastic quantum system problem at hand, drawing upon the frameworks in
[
20
,
21
]
.

Definition 1

(Asymptotically Practically Stable in Probability):
Let
\chi
be a closed and invariant set under the dynamics governed by the stochastic differential equation in Itô form

d\rho_{c}=f(\rho_{c})dt+h(\rho_{c})\sigma(t)dW(t)
,
where
\rho_{c}
denotes the state in
\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
,
\sigma(t)
is the intensity of noise at time
t
, and
dW(t)
denotes the increment of a 1-dimensional standard Wiener process. The set
\chi
is asymptotically practically stable in probability if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a function
\beta\in\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
and a non-negative real number
b\geq 0
such that for every initial state
\rho_{c}(0)\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
, the following condition holds:

\mathbb{P}\left\{d(\rho_{c}(t),\chi)<\beta\left(d(\rho_{c}(0),\chi),t\right)+b\right\}\geq 1-\varepsilon,\quad\forall t\geq 0

where
d(\rho_{c},\chi)
denotes a distance from state
\rho_{c}
to the set
\chi
, measured in an appropriate metric. If
b=0
,
\chi
is asymptotically stable in probability. If
\beta(r,t)=cre^{-\lambda t}
for some
c>0
and
\lambda>0
,
\chi
is exponentially stable in probability.

Definition 2

(Noise-to-State Practically Stable in Probability):
Adopting the conditions from Definition
1
, the set
\chi
is noise-to-state practically stable (NSpS) in probability if, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a function
\beta\in\mathcal{K}\mathcal{L}
, a function
\gamma\in\mathcal{K}
, and a non-negative real number
b\geq 0
, ensuring that for every initial state
\rho_{c}(0)\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}
, the following holds with probability at least
1-\varepsilon
, for every
t\geq 0

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\hbox{$\left\{\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}d(\rho_{c}(t),\chi)<

\displaystyle\beta\left(d(\rho_{c}(0),\chi),t\right)+\gamma\left(\sup_{\tau\in[0,t]}\sigma^{2}(\tau)\right)+b\hbox{$\left\}\vbox to0.0pt{}\right.$}\geq 1-\varepsilon

If
b=0
,
\chi
is noise-to-state stable (NSS) in probability.

Definition
2
is introduced to measure the resilience of a quantum system’s stability in the face of noise. It ensures that, probabilistically, the system’s state stays within a practical vicinity of the invariant set
\chi
, notwithstanding the influence of quantum noise. The extent of this vicinity is contingent upon the intensity of the noise, as characterized by the variance
\sigma^{2}
.

Design of the Lyapunov function

Drawing inspiration from the concepts presented in
[
22
]
, we begin by considering a state
\rho_{f}\in O_{f}(\rho_{0})
, and
select a matrix
\hat{P}=I-\rho_{f}
.
This selection leads us to define the Lyapunov function as

V(\rho)=\Tr(\hat{P}\rho).

(9)

It is important to note that
\hat{P}
is Hermitian, ensuring that it possesses real eigenvalues. Furthermore,
\hat{P}
is positive semi-definite, which guarantees that the Lyapunov function
V(\rho)
is non-negative for all states
\rho
, i.e.,
V({\rho}(t))\geq 0
.
Additionally, a significant characteristic of
\hat{P}
is its commutativity with the final state
\rho_{f}
, that is,
[\rho_{f},\hat{P}]=0
.

Remark 2

Stationary points of the Lyapunov function
V(\rho)
are identified as states
\bar{\rho}
that commute with
\rho_{f}
, see
[
22
]
, i.e.,
[\bar{\rho},\rho_{f}]=0
. Given the definition of
\hat{P}=I-\rho_{f}
, this commutation property implies
[\bar{\rho},\hat{P}]=0
.

We initially derive a controller to minimize
V(\rho)
, assuming access to the actual state
\rho(t)
. Subsequently, we adapt this approach for situations lacking direct access to
\rho(t)
, employing an estimated state
\hat{\rho}(t)
.

Theorem 2

Consider the quantum system (
5
) and let
\chi_{f}=\{\rho\in\mathcal{H}_{\rho}:[\rho,\hat{P}]=0\}
be the desired stationary set. The feedback control strategy is implemented as

\Omega(t)=\begin{cases}-i\frac{\Upsilon_{0}}{\Upsilon_{1}}&\text{ for }t\in[t_{k-1},t_{k}),\ k=1,3,5,\ldots\\
\quad 0&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

(10)

where
{{\Upsilon}_{0}}=\gamma\Tr\left(\hat{P}\mathcal{D}\left[{\hat{a}}\right]{{\rho}_{c}}\right)+\alpha\Tr\left(\hat{P}{{\rho}_{c}}\right)
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, and
{{\Upsilon}_{1}}=\Tr\left(\hat{P}\left[{{{\hat{H}}}_{l}},\rho_{c}\right]\right)
. The control is adjusted at discrete switching times
\!\{t_{0},t_{1},\ldots\!\}
, ​​​​
with
t_{0}
the initial time and
t_{k}
as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}t_{k}=\min\limits_{t}\{t\geq t_{k-1}+\Delta_{1}:\Upsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon\}&\text{for }k\text{ odd},\\
t_{k}=\min\limits_{t}\{t\geq t_{k-1}+\Delta_{2}:\Upsilon_{1}\geq\varepsilon\}&\text{for }k\text{ even},\end{array}\right.

for some appropriate dwell times

\Delta_{1},\,\Delta_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
Then, the resulting closed-loop system exhibits the property that the set
\chi_{f}
is practically exponentially stable in probability.

Proof:

Given that
\rho_{f}
is selected to commute with
\hat{P}
, it follows that
\rho_{f}
becomes a stationary point of the Lyapunov function
V(\rho)
, as discussed in
[
22
]
. Furthermore, according to Remark
2
, the set
\chi_{f}
is identified as a stationary set. To investigate its stability, we consider the infinitesimal generator of
\rho(t)
applied to
V(\rho)
. This analysis involves the diffusion process described in (
5
) and leverages the Ito formula, resulting in

L(V(\rho))=\Tr(\hat{P}^T(\Delta(\rho) + \Epsilon(\rho)\Omega(t)))+\frac{1}{2}\Tr\left(Z(\rho)H_{V}Z(\rho)^{T}\right)

where the Hessian matrix
H_{V}
of
V(\rho)
is zero in this context. Considering the commutation relation
[\rho_{f},\hat{H}_{d}]=0
and the definition
\hat{P}=I-\rho_{f}

[\hat{P},\hat{H}_{d}]=0
. By employing the trace property
\Tr(A[B,C])=\Tr(C[A,B])

\Tr(\hat{P}[\hat{H}_d, \rho])=0

L(V(\rho))=-i\Omega(t)\Tr(\hat{P}[\hat{H}_l, \rho_c])+\gamma\Tr(\hat{P}\mathcal{D}[\hat{a}]\rho_c).

(11)

At this point, our objective is to identify a control function
\Omega(t)
that satisfies
L(V(\rho))\leq-\alpha V(\rho)
, ensuring an exponential decrease in
V(\rho)
with a rate
\alpha>0
. Among the potential solutions, we opt for the one that minimizes the control effort
\Omega^{2}(t)
pointwise in time. Hence, we seek
\Omega(t)
that solves the optimization problem:

\min\Omega^{2}(t)
subject to
-i\Omega(t)\Upsilon_{1}+\Upsilon_{0}\leq 0
.
The solution to this optimization problem is precisely the control law presented in (
10
). Caution is advised when
\Upsilon_{1}
approaches zero to prevent computational inaccuracies and excessive control signal magnitudes. As a preventive measure, the control strategy switches
\Omega(t)
to zero once
\Upsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon
. To avoid Zeno behavior, characterized by an infinite number of switches within a finite time, we enforce a minimum dwell time between switching,
t_{i}-t_{i-1}>\Delta

Taking now into account the results and reasoning presented in
[
23

for switching systems with stable and unstable modes, and acknowledging that

, for some class
\mathcal{K}
function
\eta
and constant
\eta_{0}\geq 0
.

VI
Conclusion

This study presents a Lyapunov-based control framework, integrated by homodyne-mediated filtering, tailored for quantum systems. By employing a Kalman-based filtering, exploiting adequately the properties of a coherence-vector representation, and combining with input to state concepts, our approach maintains inherent quantum characteristics, which efficiently handle stochastic dynamics ensuring practical stability against noise. Demonstrated through qubit state stabilization in a leaky cavity, our method paves the way for robust quantum information processing.

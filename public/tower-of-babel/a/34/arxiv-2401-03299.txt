Discrete delayed perturbation of Mittag-Leffler function and its application to linear fractional delayed difference system Journal: Journal
Mustafa AYDIN, Nazim I. MAHMUDOV

Abstract
The linear nonhomogeneous fractional difference system with constant coefficients is introduced. An explicit solution to the system is acquired by proposing a newly discrete retarded perturbation of the nabla Mittag-Leffer-type function containing such matrix equations that provide non-permutability. A couple of special cases obtained from our results are discussed.

Lemma 7

Let
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\beta-1
and
\alpha+\beta-1
are nonnegative integers. Then

\nabla_{a}^{-\alpha}H_{\beta-1}(k,a)=H_{\alpha+\beta-1}(k,a),\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{a},

and

\nabla_{a}^{\alpha}H_{\beta-1}(k,a)=H_{\alpha-\beta-1}(k,a),\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{a}.

Proof.
We firstly find an equality of
H_{\beta-1}(k,a)
using (generalized) binomial coefficients.

H_{\beta-1}(k,a)=\frac{(k-a)^{\overline{\beta-1}}}{\Gamma(\beta)}=\frac{\Gamma(k-a+\beta-1)}{\Gamma(k-a)\Gamma(\beta)}=\binom{k-a-1+\beta-1}{k-a-1}.

Using the above equality and the definition of the (nabla) fractional sum, we acquire

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}^{-\alpha}H_{\beta-1}(k,a)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{k}H_{\alpha-1}(k,\rho(s))H_{\beta-1}(s,a)\nabla s

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=a+1}^{k}H_{\alpha-1}(k,\rho(s))H_{\beta-1}(s,a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=a+1}^{k}\binom{k-s+\alpha-1}{k-s}\binom{s-a-1+\beta-1}{s-a-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{k-a-1}\binom{k-a-1-s+\alpha-1}{k-a-1-s}\binom{s+\beta-1}{s},

Applying Chu-Vandermonde identity to the above expression, we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}^{-\alpha}H_{\beta-1}(k,a)

\displaystyle=\int_{a}^{k}H_{\alpha-1}(k,\rho(s))H_{\beta-1}(s,a)\nabla s

\displaystyle=\binom{k-a-1+(\alpha+\beta-1)-1}{k-a-1}=H_{\alpha+\beta-1}(k,a).

The second one can be proved in the similar manner.

The composition of the (nabla) fractional sum and the (nabla) Riemann-Liouville fractional difference is given in the next lemma. A special case of the lemma is examined in reference
[
28
]
and it is proved via integration by parts. So we prove it in a different way.

Lemma 8

For
0<\beta<1
and
t\in\mathbb{N}_{a+1}
, we have

\nabla_{a}^{-\beta}\nabla_{\rho(a)}^{\beta}z(k)=z(k)-H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))z(a).

Proof.
By using definitions of
\nabla_{a}^{-\beta}
and
\nabla_{a}^{\beta}
, we acquire

\displaystyle\nabla_{a}^{-\beta}\nabla_{\rho(a)}^{\beta}z(k)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{a}^{-\beta}\left(\sum_{s=a}^{k}H_{-\beta-1}(k,\rho(s))z(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=a+1}^{k}H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(s))\sum_{i=a}^{s}H_{-\beta-1}(s,\rho(i))z(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=a}^{k}H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(s))\sum_{i=a}^{s}H_{-\beta-1}(s,\rho(i))z(i)

\displaystyle\ \ -H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))\sum_{i=a}^{a}H_{-\beta-1}(a,\rho(i))z(i)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=a}^{k}z(i)\sum_{s=i}^{k}H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(s))H_{-\beta-1}(s,\rho(i))

\displaystyle\ \ -H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))H_{-\beta-1}(a,\rho(a))z(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=a}^{k}z(i)\int_{\rho(i)}^{k}H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(s))H_{-\beta-1}(s,\rho(i))\nabla s

\displaystyle\ \ -H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))z(a)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=a}^{k}H_{-1}(k,\rho(i))z(i)-H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))z(a)

\displaystyle=z(k)-H_{\beta-1}(k,\rho(a))z(a),

where
H_{-1}(k,\rho(i))=0
for
a\leq i\leq k-1
and
H_{-1}(k,\rho(k))=1

4
The explicit solution of RL fractional retarded difference system

In this section, our aim is to investigate an explicit solution to the linear Riemann Liouville fractional retarded difference system by dividing into three subsections.

We share a couple of main theorems to achieve our objective. When it comes to most of their proofs related to natural numbers, we use the method of mathematical induction for them, which is the most useful and powerful proof technique on the natural numbers.

4.1
Homogeneous case:

In this subsection, we firstly consider the following homogeneous Riemann Liouville fractional delayed difference system

\left\{\begin{array}[c]{l}\nabla_{-r}^{\alpha}z\left(k\right)=Mz\left(k\right)+Nz\left(k-r\right),\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{1},\\
\ \ \ \ z\left(k\right)=\mathbb{E}_{M,\alpha,\alpha-1}\left(k,-r\right),\ \ k\in\mathbb{N}_{1-r}^{0},\ \ r>0,\\
\end{array}\right.

where
\nabla_{-r}^{\alpha}
is the Riemann-Liouville fractional difference of order

is a retardation,

The discrete DPML matrix function

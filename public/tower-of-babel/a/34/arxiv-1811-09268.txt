1.
Introduction

The present work is concerned with existence of positive solutions for a class of fractional equation involving a Kirchhoff term and singular potential. More precisely we consider the problem

{(\mathcal{P}_{s,q})}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}M\bigg(\displaystyle\iint_{{I\!\!R}^{N}\times{I\!\!R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\,dx\,dy\bigg)(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2s}}+f(x,u)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>&0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\Omega\subset{I\!\!R}^{N}
is a bounded domain containing the origin,
s\in(0,1)
,
q\in(1,2]
with
N>2s
,
\lambda>0
and
f
is a measurable nonnegative function with suitable hypotheses.

M
is a positive function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Here, for
0<s<1
, the fractional Laplacian

(-\Delta)^{s}
is defined by

(1.1)

(-\Delta)^{s}u(x):=a_{N,s}\mbox{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy},

where

a_{N,s}:=2^{2s-1}\pi^{-\frac{N}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{N+2s}{2})}{|\Gamma(-s)|}

is the normalization constant such that the identity

(-\Delta)^{s}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(|\xi|^{2s}\mathcal{F}u),\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

holds for all
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

where
\mathcal{F}u
denotes the Fourier transform of
u
and

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the Schwartz class of tempered functions.

The problem
(\mathcal{P}_{s,q})
is related to the following Hardy inequality, proved in
[
5
]
and
[
16
]
,

(1.2)

\frac{a_{N,s}}{2}\int\int_{D_{\Omega}}{\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}}\,dx\,dy\geq\Lambda_{N,s}\int_{\Omega}{\frac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}\,dx},\,u\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}({I\!\!R}^{N}),

where

{D_{\Omega}}:={I\!\!R}^{N}\times{I\!\!R}^{N}\setminus\big(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\mathcal{C}\Omega\big)\,.

and

(1.3)

\Lambda_{N,s}=2^{2s}\dfrac{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N+2s}{4})}{\Gamma^{2}(\frac{N-2s}{4})}.

Notice that
\Lambda_{N,s}
is optimal and not attained.

Nonlocal problems related to the Hardy potential were widely studied in the last years. Before staring our main result, let us begin by some previous results. In the case where
M\equiv 1
, then it is well known that problem
(\mathcal{P}_{s,q})
has a solution if and only if
\lambda\leq\Lambda_{N,s}
. Moreover, for
\lambda\in(0,\Lambda_{N,s}]
fixed and taking into consideration the singular behavior near the origin of the Hardy potential, it holds that a natural condition is imposed on
f
in order to get the existence of a solution. More precisely, if
f
does not depends on
u
(
f(x,u):=f(x))
, then
(\mathcal{P}_{s,q})
has a solution if and only if
\displaystyle\int\limits_{\Omega}|x|^{-\gamma_{\lambda}}f(x)\,dx<\infty
where
\gamma_{\lambda}
will be explicitly stated in the next section.

If
f(x,u)=u_{+}^{p}
, then existence of solution holds if and only if
p<p_{+}(\lambda)<2s
. We refer for instance to
[
2
]
and
[
10
]
for more details.

Where
M
is not identically constant, then for the local case
s=1
and without the Hardy potential, the problem can be expressed in the general form

{(\mathcal{P}_{K})}\quad-M(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx)\Delta u=g(x,u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega.

(\mathcal{P}_{K})
is well known as Kirchhoff type problem and is widely studied in the literature. The first motivation was the study of the stationary solution to the classical equation

\rho\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial t^{2}}-\left(\frac{p_{0}}{h}+\frac{E}{2L}\int_{0}^{L}\left|\dfrac{\partial u}{\partial x}\right|^{2}dx\right)\dfrac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}=0.

This equation is an extension of the classical d’Alembert’s wave equation by considering the effects of the changes in the length of the string during the vibrations. In this equation,
L
is the length of the string,
h
is the area of the cross-section,
E
is the Young modulus of the material,
\rho
is the mass density and
P_{0}
is the initial tension. The stationary multidimensional Kirchhoff equation is given by

{(\mathcal{P}_{K})}\quad-M(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}dx)\Delta u=g(x,u)\quad\text{in}\quad\Omega,\quad u=0\quad\text{on}\quad\partial\Omega,

which received much attention after the work of Lions
[
18
]
, where a functional setting was proposed to the problem.

The Kirchhoff function
M
is assumed Lipschitz continuous, but not always monotone, even if the model proposed by Kirchhoff in 1883
M(t)=a+bt^{\beta}
where
a,b\geq 0
and
\beta\geq 0
is monotone, see discussion in
[
20
]
and
[
22
]
. If
M(0)=0
, the Kirchhoff problem is called degenerate, we may refer to
[
8
]
and references therein for more details on this case.

In
[
3
]
, using variational arguments the authors proved the existence of a positive solution of
{(\mathcal{P}_{K})}
if
M
is a positive non-increasing function and
g
satisfies the Ambrosetti-Rabinowitz condition. The case where
M
is a non-decreasing function was considered in
[
4
]
. In
[
19
]
, the author collected some related problems to
{(\mathcal{P}_{K})}
and unified the presentation of the results. Especially, for
g
such that
g(x,u)u\leq a\left|u\right|^{2}+b\left|u\right|
(
a,b>0
) he showed both existence and uniqueness of solutions. In the case where
g(x,u)
is asymptotically linear at infinity with respect to
u
, some existence results are proved in
[
4
]
. The singular case was considered in
[
12
]
.

The nonlocal case
s\in(0,1)
, was considered recently by several authors, including the critical and singular cases. We refer to
[
20
,
21
,
23
]
and references therein for further details.

The novelty in our paper is to get some interactions between the Kirchhoff term and the Hardy potential in order to get the existence of non negative solution for a largest class of the datum
f
and without any restriction on the parameter
\lambda
. We will show that, under some growth hypothesis on the Kirchhoff term, it is possible to break any resonance effect of the Hardy term. The resut covers also the local case
s=1
.

Through this paper we will assume that
M
is a continuous function such that:

(M1)
M:{I\!\!R}^{+}\to{I\!\!R}^{+}
is increasing.

(M2)
\exists m_{0}>0;\quad M(t)\geq m_{0}\quad\forall t\in{I\!\!R}^{+}.

(M3)
\underset{t\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}M(t)=+\infty

We begin by analyzing the case where
f
does not depend on
u
, namely
f(x,u)=:=f(x)
. Then for
q=2
and hence the problem has a variational structure, we are able to proof the next result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is such that
f\gneqq 0
, then for all
\lambda>0
, problem
(\mathcal{P}_{s,2})
has a unique positive solution
\bar{u}\in H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
.

We mention here the strong regularity enjoyed by the solution. In the case where
M\equiv 1
, such a regularity is false including for
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
where
\lambda
is closed to the Hardy constant
\Lambda_{N,s}
.

In the general case
q<2
, we get the existence of a solution for a large class of the datum
f
. More precisely we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{I\!\!R}^{N}
be a bounded domain such that
0\in\Omega
and
1<q<\frac{N}{N-s}
. Suppose that
f\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is such that
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f|x|^{-\theta}dx<c
for some positive constant
\theta
. Then for all
\lambda>0
, the problem
(\mathcal{P}_{s,q})
has a minimal solution
\bar{u}
such that
\bar{u}\in W^{s,\sigma}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<\frac{N}{N-s}
.

In the second part of the paper we treat the case where
f(x,u)=u_{+}^{p}+\mu g
. In this case and under suitable hypotheses on
\mu
and
g
, we will show the existence of a solution without any restriction on
\lambda
.

This paper is organized as follow. In Section
2
, we give some results related to fractional Sobolev spaces and some functional inequalities. The sense for which solutions are defined is also presented, namely energy solutions and weak solutions.

The proofs of the main existence results are given in Section
3
. We begin by the case of variational structure and at the end of this section, we use approximating argument to treat the case of non variational structure.

2.
Preliminaries and functional setting

Let
\Omega\subset{I\!\!R}^{N}
, for
s\in(0,1)
and
1\leq p<+\infty
, the fractional Sobolev
spaces
W^{s,p}(\Omega)
, are defined by

W^{s,p}(\Omega)\equiv\Big\{\phi\in L^{p}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy<+\infty\Big\}

W^{s,p}(\Omega)
is a Banach space endowed with the following norm

\|\phi\|_{W^{s,p}(\Omega)}=\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\phi(x)|^{p}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}dxdy\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We define the space
W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)
as

W_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega):=\left\{u\in W^{s,p}({I\!\!R}^{N}):u=0\textup{ in }{I\!\!R}^{N}\setminus\Omega\right\}.

If
\Omega
is a bounded regular domain, then using Poincaré type inequality, we can endow
W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with the equivalent norm

\|u\|_{W^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega)}:=\left(\iint_{D_{\Omega}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+sp}}dxdy\right)^{1/p},

where

D_{\Omega}:=({I\!\!R}^{N}\times{I\!\!R}^{N})\setminus(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\mathcal{C}\Omega)=(\Omega\times{I\!\!R}^{N})\cup(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\Omega).

The next Sobolev inequality is proved in
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Fractional Sobolev inequality)
Assume that
0<s<1,p>1
in ordrer to get
ps<N
. Then, there exists a positive constant
S\equiv S(N,s,p)
such that for all

v\in C_{0}^{\infty}({I\!\!R}^{N})
,

\iint_{{I\!\!R}^{2N}}\dfrac{|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{N+ps}}\,dxdy\geq S\Big(\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|v(x)|^{p_{s}^{*}}dx\Big)^{\frac{p}{p^{*}_{s}}},

where
p^{*}_{s}=\dfrac{pN}{N-ps}
.

In the particular case
p=2
,
H^{s}(\Omega):=W^{s,2}(\Omega)
turns out to be a Hilbert spaces. If
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{N}
, the Fourier transform provides yet an alternative definition, more precisely, the Plancherel identity leads to

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\xi|^{2s}|\mathcal{F}(u)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi={\frac{a_{N,s}}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{2}}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}dxdy.

The operator
(-\Delta)^{s}
defined for

u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
by

(-\Delta)^{s}u:=a_{N,s}\mbox{ P.V. }\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dy},\,\,\qquad s\in(0,1),

can be extended by density from
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In this way, the associated scalar product can be reformulated as

\begin{split}\langle u,v\rangle_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}&:=\langle(-\Delta)^{s}u,v\rangle+(u,v)\\
&:=P.V.\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\times\mathbb{R}^{N}}{\frac{(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))}{|x-y|^{N+2s}}\,dx\,dy}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}uv\,dx.\end{split}

In the case of bounded domain if we denote by
H^{-s}(\Omega):=[H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)]^{*}
the dual space
of
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
, then

(-\Delta)^{s}:H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\rightarrow H^{-s}(\Omega),

is a continuous operator.

We state, by the next definition, the sense in which the solution is defined.

Definition 2.2
.

Assume that
h\in L^{1}(\Omega)
and consider the problem

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&h&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{ in }{I\!\!R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\\
\end{array}\right.

we say that
u\in L^{1}(\Omega)
is a weak solution to (
2.1
) if
u=0
in
{I\!\!R}^{N}\backslash\Omega
and for all
\psi\in\mathbb{X}_{s}
, we have

\int\limits_{\Omega}u((-\Delta)^{s}\psi)dx=\int\limits_{\Omega}h\psi dx.

where

\mathbb{X}_{s}\equiv\Big\{\psi\in\mathcal{C}({I\!\!R}^{N})\,|\,\text{supp}(\psi)\subset\overline{\Omega},\,\,(-\Delta)^{s}\psi(x)\hbox{ pointwise defined and }|(-\Delta)^{s}\psi(x)|<C\hbox{ in }\Omega\Big\}.

The next existence result is proved in
[
17
]
,
[
11
]
and
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Assume that
h\in L^{1}(\Omega)
, then problem (
2.1
) has a unique weak solution
u
obtained as the limit of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where
u_{n}
is the unique solution of the approximating problem

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}&=&h_{n}(x)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u_{n}&=&0&\mbox{ in }{I\!\!R}^{N}\backslash\Omega,\end{array}\right.

with

and
T_{n}(\sigma)=\max(-n,\min(n,\sigma))
. Moreover,

(2.3)

T_{k}(u_{n})\to T_{k}(u)\hbox{ strongly in }H^{s}_{0}(\Omega),\quad\forall k>0,

(2.4)

u\in L^{\theta}(\Omega)\,,\qquad\forall\ \theta\in\big[1,\frac{N}{N-2s}\big)\,

and

Furthermore

(2.6)

As a subsequent of this existence result, we get a compactness result which will be systematically used in the sequel.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume that

be fixed and consider the operator
D:L^{1}(\Omega)\to W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)
defined by
D(h)=u
where

is the unique solution to the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&h(x)&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\

then

is a compact operator, moreover, there exists a positive constant

such that

||u||_{W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq C||h||_{L^{1}(\Omega)}.

Since we are considering problem involoving the Hardy potential, we have tospecify the behavior of any solution in the neighborhood of the singular point
0
.

For

fixed, we define

by the identity

(2.7)

\lambda=\lambda(\alpha)=\lambda(-\alpha)=\dfrac{2^{2s}\,\Gamma(\frac{N+2s+2\alpha}{4})\Gamma(\frac{N+2s-2\alpha}{4})}{\Gamma(\frac{N-2s+2\alpha}{4})\Gamma(\frac{N-2s-2\alpha}{4})}.

Denote

then

and

is the unique solution to the equation

Form
[

Consider the problem

3.
Existence results in the case
f(x,u):=f(x)
.

In what follows and for the sake of simplicity we set

||u||_{s,q}^{q}=\iint_{D_{\Omega}}\dfrac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{q}}{|x-y|^{N+qs}}\,dxdy,

where

D_{\Omega}:=({I\!\!R}^{N}\times{I\!\!R}^{N})\setminus(\mathcal{C}\Omega\times\mathcal{C}\Omega).

Along this section we will assume that the function
M
satisfies the conditions
(M1),(M2)

Recall that we are considering the next problem

{(\mathcal{P}_{s,2})}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2s}}+f(x)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>&0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Our first existence result consists in the following.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
is such that
f\gneqq 0
, then for all
\lambda>0
, the problem
(\mathcal{P}_{s,2})

\bar{u}

Proof
.  Let
f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and fix
\lambda>0
, we will proceed by approximation.

Consider the truncated problem

{(\mathcal{P}^{n}_{s})}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}&=&\dfrac{\lambda T_{n}(u_{n}^{+})}{|x|^{2s}+\frac{1}{n}}+f(x)&\text{ in }\Omega,\\

u_{n}&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega,\end{array}\right.

It is clear that solutions of problem
(\mathcal{P}^{n}_{s})
are critical points of the functional
J_{n}
given by

J_{n}(v)=\dfrac{1}{2}\widehat{M}(\left\|v\right\|_{s}^{2})-\lambda\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\widehat{T}_{n}(v^{+})}{\left|x\right|^{2s}+\frac{1}{n}}dx-\int_{\Omega}fvdx,

where

\widehat{M}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}M(\tau)d\tau,\qquad\widehat{T}_{n}(t)=\int^{t}_{0}T_{n}(\tau)d\tau.

Taking into consideration the behavior of
M
and setting

D_{n}=\inf_{H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)\backslash\{0\}}J_{n}(u),

we reach that
D_{n}
is achieved and then we get the existence of
u_{n}\in H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
that solves
(\mathcal{P}^{n}_{s})
.
We claim that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. We argue by contradiction. Assume that
||u_{n}||_{s,2}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
.
Taking
u_{n}
as a test function in
(\mathcal{P}^{n}_{s})
and using Hardy and Hölder inequalities, it follow that

(3.1)

\begin{array}[]{ll}M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2}\leq\lambda\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left|u_{n}\right|^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}fu_{n}dx\\
\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{N,s}}\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{2}_{s,2}+\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}||u||_{s,2}.\end{array}

Thus

(3.2)

||u||^{2}_{s,2}\left(M\left(||u||^{2}_{s,2}\right)-\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{N,s}}\right)\leq 2^{-1}\left(||u||^{2}_{s,2}+\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right),

from which we have,

(3.3)

2||u_{n}||^{2}_{s,2}(M(||u||^{2}_{s,2})-\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{N,s}}-1)\leq\left\|f\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

By
(M2)
, we deduce that

M(||u_{n}||^{2}_{s,2})-\frac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{N,s}}-1\to\infty\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Hence we reach a contradiction with (

). Thus we conclude that
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in

and the claim follows.

Therefore, we get the existence of
\bar{u}\in H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
weakly in
H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
, and

strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega)
for all

.

We claim that there exists a positive constant

such that up to a subsequence denoted by
u_{n}
, we have

(3.4)

\frac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}<\Lambda_{N,s}-\rho\mbox{ for all }n.

By virtue of

and since

, we obtain,

\liminf_{n\rightarrow\infty}M(||u||_{s,2}^{2}):=\mu>0.

Therefore, we get the existence of a subsequence denoted by

such that,

M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})\rightarrow\mu\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Obviously,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
weakly in

, thus by passing to the limit in the problem of

\mu(-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}=\lambda\frac{\bar{u}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}+f.

Thus

, satisfies

in
\Omega

From the result of

, we conclude that

.

Let us show that

If

, then
\bar{u}
is solution of the equation

(-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}=\Lambda_{N,s}\dfrac{\bar{u}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}+\dfrac{f(x)}{\mu}\quad\text{in}\hskip 4.26773pt\Omega,

Using again the result of

2
]
, one can see that
\bar{u}(x)\geq C|x|^{-\frac{N-2s}{2}}
in a small ball

. Thus

\int_{B_{\eta}(0)}\bar{u}^{2^{*}_{s}}dx\geq C\int_{B_{\eta}(0)}|x|^{-N}dx=\infty,

which is a contradiction with the fact that
\bar{u}\in H_{0}^{s}(\Omega)
. Hence we get the existence of a positive constant
\rho
such that

(3.5)

\frac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}<\Lambda_{N,s}-\rho.

To conclude, we prove the strong convergence of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
in
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

It is clear that
u_{n}
solves

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}=\dfrac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}\dfrac{u_{n}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}+\dfrac{f(x)}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}.

Using
u_{n}-\bar{u}
as a test function, we obtain that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}(u_{n}-\bar{u})dx=\dfrac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}(u_{n}-\bar{u})}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx+\dfrac{1}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f(x)(u_{n}-\bar{u})dx.

Since the term
\dfrac{1}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}
is bounded away from zero and
u_{n}-\bar{u}\to 0
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\dfrac{1}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}\int_{\Omega}f(x)(u_{n}-\bar{u})dx\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

We deal now with the term
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx
.

We have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}(u_{n}-\bar{u})}{(|x|+n^{-1})^{2s}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)^{2}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\bar{u}\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)^{2}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx+\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}-\bar{u}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{s}}\dfrac{\bar{u}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{s}}dx.

Taking into consideration that
\dfrac{\bar{u}}{{(|x|+n^{-1})^{s}}}\to\dfrac{\bar{u}}{|x|^{s}}
strongly in
L^{2}(\Omega)
and that

\dfrac{u_{n}-\bar{u}}{{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{s}}}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{2}\left(\Omega\right),
(as
n\to\infty
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}-\bar{u}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{s}}\dfrac{\bar{u}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{s}}dx\to 0\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Consequently, and using again the Hardy inequality, we get

(3.7)

\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx=\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\left(u_{n}-\bar{u}\right)^{2}}{\left(\left|x\right|+n^{-1}\right)^{2s}}dx+o(1)\leq\dfrac{1}{\Lambda_{N,s}}\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}+o(1).

Now, by the weak convergence, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta)^{s}u_{n}(u_{n}-\bar{u})dx=\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}+o(1).

Therefore, by estimates (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), it follows that

\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}\leq\dfrac{\lambda}{\Lambda_{N,s}M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}+o(1).

Recall that by (
3.5
), we have
\dfrac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,2}^{2})}<\Lambda_{N,s}-\rho
for all
n
. Thus

\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}<\displaystyle\dfrac{\Lambda_{N,s}-\rho}{\Lambda_{N,s}}\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}+o(1).

Since

, then
\left\|u_{n}-\bar{u}\right\|_{s,2}^{2}\longrightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
and then

strongly in
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

As a consequence it follows that
M(||u_{n}||_{s,2})\to M(||u||_{s,2})

solves
(\mathcal{P}_{s,2})

It remains to proof yet the uniqueness part.

Assume that

Assume by contradiction that

u_{1}=M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})u
and

(-\Delta)^{s}v_{1}=\frac{\lambda}{M(||v||_{s,2}^{2})}\,\dfrac{v_{1}}{|x|^{2s}}+f(x),

and

(-\Delta)^{s}u_{1}=\frac{\lambda}{M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})}\,\dfrac{u_{1}}{|x|^{2s}}+f(x).

Since
\frac{\lambda}{M(||v||_{s,2}^{2})}<\frac{\lambda}{M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})}
and

in

in

, thus

in
\Omega
. Going back to the equation of

M(||v||_{s,2}^{2})||v||_{s,2}^{2}=\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{v^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}fvdx\leq\lambda\int\limits_{\Omega}\dfrac{u^{2}}{|x|^{2s}}dx+\int\limits_{\Omega}fudx=M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})||u||_{s,2}^{2}

Since

is a strict monotone function, we conclude that

.

Using
u_{n}
as a test function in (
3.13
) and integrating over
\Omega
, it holds that

\beta\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{n}dx=\dfrac{\lambda}{M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{u_{n}\varphi}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\dfrac{1}{M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\varphi dx.

Then,

(\beta-\dfrac{\lambda}{M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})})\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{n}dx\leq\dfrac{1}{m_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\varphi dx\leq\dfrac{1}{m_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f_{n}\left|x\right|^{-\gamma_{\beta}}dx.

Now, since
M(||u_{n}||_{s,q}^{q})\longrightarrow+\infty
, we can choose
n_{0}
large enough such that

\beta-\dfrac{\lambda}{M(||u_{n}||_{s,q}^{q})}>\varepsilon_{0}>0
if
n\geq n_{0}
.
Hence, for
n\geq n_{0}
,

\beta_{0}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{n}dx\leq\dfrac{1}{m_{0}}\int_{\Omega}f\left|x\right|^{-\theta_{\varepsilon}}dx.

Recall that
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}f\left|x\right|^{-\theta}dx}<c
, then we fix now
\beta
small enough such that
\theta_{\varepsilon}\leq\theta
. Thus
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}f\left|x\right|^{-\theta_{\beta}}<\infty
.
Thus

(3.15)

\beta_{0}\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\int_{\Omega}u_{n}dx<m^{-1}_{0}c.

From which we deduce that
\bigg\|\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}<m^{-1}_{0}c
and the claim follows.

Now going back to (
3.11
) and using the fact that the sequence
\{g_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
L^{1}(\Omega)
, we reach that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in

W^{s,\sigma}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<\frac{N}{N-s}
, in particular for
\sigma=q
. Thus
\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{s,q}<C_{1}
and then
M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})<\bar{C}
, a contradiction with the main hypothesis. Thus
\{M(||u_{n}||_{s,q}^{q})\}_{n}
is bounded in
{I\!\!R}^{+}
.

Hence, we get the existence of
\bar{u}\in W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)
such that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\rightharpoonup\bar{u}
weakly in
W^{s,q}_{0}(\Omega)
,

u_{n}\to\bar{u}
strongly in
L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<q^{*}_{s}
and
u_{n}\to\bar{u}\>\>a.e\mbox{ in }\Omega
.

Setting
\alpha_{n}:=M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{q}_{s,q})
, then we can assume that
\alpha_{n}\longrightarrow\bar{\alpha}\geq m_{0}.

Let us show that
\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\to\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2s}}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
. It is clear that
\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\to\dfrac{\bar{u}}{|x|^{2s}}
a.e. in
\Omega.
Using the fact that
u_{n}\to\bar{u}
strongly in
L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<q^{*}_{s}
, we reach that

\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\to\dfrac{\bar{u}}{|x|^{2s}}\mbox{ strongly in }L^{1}(\Omega\backslash B_{R}(0)\mbox{ for all }R>0.

Hence to conclude we have just to show that

\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\to\dfrac{\bar{u}}{|x|^{2s}}\mbox{ strongly in }L^{1}(B_{R}(0))\mbox{ for some }R>0.

Recall that from (
3.15
), we have
\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx<C
for all
n
. By the fact that
\varphi\geq C_{\beta}|x|^{-\theta_{\varepsilon}}
in
B_{R}(0)
, it holds that

C_{\beta}R^{-\theta_{\beta}}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}\leq C\mbox{ for all }n.

Fix
R>0
to be chosen later and consider
E\subset B_{R}(0)
a measurable set. Then

\displaystyle\int_{E}\dfrac{u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{E\cap B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx+\int_{E\backslash B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{\beta}R^{\theta_{\beta}}+\int_{E\backslash B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx.

Let
\varepsilon>0
, then we can choose
\delta>0
such that if
|E|<\delta
and
n\geq n_{0}
, then

\int_{E\backslash B_{R}(0)}\dfrac{\varphi u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

It is clear that we can choose
R
small enough such that

C_{\beta}R^{\theta_{\beta}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Combining the above estimates, it holds that for
n\geq n_{0}
and if
|E|\leq\delta
, then

\int_{E}\dfrac{u_{n}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}dx\leq\varepsilon.

Hence, using Vitali’s lemma, we reach that

\dfrac{u_{n}}{|x|^{2s}}\to\dfrac{u}{|x|^{2s}}
strongly in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.

Reminding the definition of
g_{n}
in (
3.12
) and using the compactness result in Theorem
2.4
we conclude, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to\bar{u}
strongly in
W^{\sigma,q}_{0}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<\frac{N}{N-s}
. Thus

M(\left\|u_{n}\right\|^{q}_{s,q})\to M(\left\|\bar{u}\right\|^{q}_{s,q})\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

Therefore we conclude that
\bar{u}
satisfies

(-\Delta)^{s}\bar{u}=\dfrac{\lambda}{M(\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})}.\dfrac{\bar{u}}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}+\dfrac{f}{M(\left\|\bar{u}\right\|_{s,q}^{q})},

and then
\bar{u}
is solution of Problem
({P}^{\lambda}_{s,q})
.

Remark 1
.

If
f\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
for
\sigma>1
, then using Hölder inequality we can prove that the condition stated in Theorem
3.7
holds for
f
for some
\theta>0
. Thus problem
({P}^{\lambda}_{s,q})
has a solution for all
f\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
\sigma>1
and for all
\lambda>0
.

4.
Existence results in the case
f(x,u):=u^{p}+g(x)
.

Let consider now the nonlinear problem

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}M(\left\|u\right\|_{s,2}^{2})(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda\dfrac{u}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}+u^{p}+\mu g&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&>&0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=&0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

In the case where
M\equiv 1
, then, as in the local case, for
\lambda<\Lambda_{N,s}
fixed, it is possible to show the existence of a critical value
p_{*}>2^{*}_{s}-1
such that the problem (
4.1
) has a nonnegative super-solution if and only if
p<p_{*}
. This can be obtained using suitable radial computation, according the behavior of any super-solution near the origin and a suitable test function, see
[
6
]
and
[
10
]
.

The goal of this section is to check that, under suitable hypothesis on
g
and
p
, the existence of a solution to problem (
4.1
) for all
\lambda>0
.
The first existence result is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
g\in L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
with
\sigma\geq\frac{2N}{N+2s}
and suppose that
p<2^{*}_{s}-1
, then there exists
\mu^{*}>0
such that for all
\lambda>0
and for all
\mu<\mu^{*}
, problem (
4.1
) has a solution
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

In the case
M=1
, existence of solution is proved under the condition
\lambda\leq\Lambda_{N,s}
and the comparison principle using a suitable radial supersolution. However this argument is not applicable in our case and we have to use a different argument in order to show the existence of a solution. The main idea is to use a suitable fixed point argument.

Fix
p<r<2^{*}_{s}
, we can show the existence of
\mu^{*}>0
such that if
\mu<\mu^{*}
, then the algebraic equation

C(l+\lambda^{\ast}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)})=l^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
C
is a universal constant that will be chosen later.

Define the set

E:=\left\{\varphi\in W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega):||\varphi||_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\leq l^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\},

Then
E
is a closed convex set of
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
.

Now, we define
T:E\to W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
by
T(\varphi)=u
, where
u
is the weak solution to

(4.2)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}M(||u||_{s,2}^{2})(-\Delta)^{s}u&=&\lambda\dfrac{u}{\left|x\right|^{2s}}+\varphi_{+}^{p}+\mu g(x)\,,&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\,,&\mbox{ in }{I\!\!R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{array}\right.

It is clear that if
u=T(u)
, then
u
solves problem (
4.1
). Hence, to prove Theorem
4.1
, we shall show that
T
has fixed point belonging to
W_{0}^{s,2}(\Omega)\cap\mathcal{C}^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)
for some
\alpha>0
.

The proof will be given in several steps.

Step 1:
T
is well defined.

Since
\varphi\in E
, then
\varphi_{+}^{p}+g\in L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)
. Thus using Theorem
3.1
and the Remark
3.2
, we get the existence and the uniqueness of
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
u
solves the problem (
4.2
). Hence
u\in W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
and then
T
is well defined.

Step 2:
T(E)\subset E
.

Let
\varphi\in E
, we define
u=T(\varphi)
. By the regularity result in Proposition
3.3
, it follows that

(4.3)

\displaystyle||u||_{L^{r}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\varphi_{+}^{p}+\lambda g(x)\big\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C(||\varphi||^{\frac{p}{r}}_{L^{r}(\Omega)}+\lambda^{\ast}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)})

\displaystyle\leq C(l+\lambda^{\ast}\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)})\leq l^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since, we have proved previously that
u\in W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
, then using the definition of
l
, we conclude that
u\in E
and so, that
T(E)\subset E
.

Step 3:
T
is continuous.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
be such that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
in
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
. Consider
u_{n}=T(\varphi_{n})
and
u=T(\varphi)
.
Define

h_{n}(x):=(\varphi_{n})_{+}^{p}+\lambda h(x),

then to get the desired result, we have just to show that
h_{n}\to h
strongly in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)
where

h(x):=(\varphi)_{+}^{p}+\lambda g(x).

Using Sobolev inequality in
W^{s,1}_{0}(\Omega)
and since
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
strongly in
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
, then

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
strongly in
L^{\frac{N}{N-1}}(\Omega)
. Since
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}
is bounded in
L^{r}(\Omega)
, then using an interpolation arguments it holds that
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi
strongly in
L^{\sigma}(\Omega)
for all
\sigma<r
and in particular in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)
. Hence
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
and then the continuity of
T
follows.

Step 4:
T
is compact.

Let
\{\varphi_{n}\}\subset E
be a bounded sequence in
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
and define
u_{n}=T(\varphi_{n})
. We will prove that, up to a subsequence,
u_{n}\to u
in
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
for some
u\in W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
.

Recall that
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}\subset E
, then as in the proof of the
Step 3
, we reach that
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)
. Now, setting

h_{n}(x):=(\varphi_{n})_{+}^{p}+\lambda g(x),

we have that
\{h_{n}\}
is a bounded sequence in
L^{\frac{2N}{N+2s}}(\Omega)
. The result then follows from the compactness result in Proposition
2.4
.

As a conclusion, and since
E
is a closed convex set of
W_{0}^{s,1}(\Omega)
and, by the previous steps, we can apply the Schauder fixed point Theorem to get the existence of
u\in E
such that
T(u)=u
. Thus, we conclude that problem (
4.1
) has a weak solution for all
0<\lambda\leq\lambda^{\ast}
. It is clear that
u\in H^{s}_{0}(\Omega)
.

∎

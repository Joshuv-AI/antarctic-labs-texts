Vector-Vector Scattering on the Lattice
\firstname Fernando \lastname Romero-López \fnsep, \firstname Carsten \lastname Urbach, \firstname Akaki \lastname Rusetsky \fnsep

Abstract
In this work we present an extension of the Lüscher formalism to include the interaction of particles with spin, focusing on the scattering of two vector particles. The derived formalism will be applied to Scalar QED in the Higgs Phase, where the U(1) gauge boson acquires mass.

Introduction

The study of scattering in Lattice Field Theory starts with the original work of Lüscher
Luscher
. In this first work, he derived equations for the scattering length and phase shift of spinless particles in the rest frame. The formalism has been extended to include moving frames
Gottt
,
\pi-N
scattering
Bernard:2008ax
,
N-N
scattering
Briceno:2013lba
, different masses
Bernard:2012bi
;
ZiwenFu
, moving frames with different masses and one particle with spin
1/2

Gockeler:2012yj
and any multichannel system with arbitrary spin, momentum and masses
Briceno:2014oea
.

In this work, we derive a general Lüscher equation for scattering of particles with arbitrary spin through the matching to a non-relativistic effective theory. The results obtained here are in agreement with Ref.
Briceno:2014oea
. We will focus on the case of two identical vector particles and we will make use of the spatial symmetries of the lattice to factorize the Lüscher equation. By means of operators that transform under a certain irreducible representation of the spatial symmetry group, we gain access to the different phase shifts of the theory. The equations will be tested in Scalar QED, for which first numerical results will be shown.

2
Scattering of two vector particles

2.1
Derivation of Lüscher Equation for Arbitrary Spin

Let us consider a system of two particles with masses
M_{i}
,
i=1,2
and described by the effective non-relativistic Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}=\phi_{1}^{\dagger}2W_{1}(i\partial_{t}-W_{1})\phi_{1}+\phi_{2}^{\dagger}2W_{2}(i\partial_{t}-W_{2})\phi_{2}+\mathcal{L}_{I}.

(1)

Here,
\phi_{i}
are the non relativistic fields with spin
s_{i}
and
W_{i}=(M_{i}^{2}-\nabla^{2})^{1/2}
. The corresponding non-relativistic propagators, with
\omega_{i}=(M_{i}^{2}+\mathbf{p}^{2})^{1/2}
, read

S_{i}(p)=\frac{1}{2\omega_{i}(\mathbf{p})}\frac{1}{\omega_{i}(\mathbf{p})-p^{0}-i\epsilon},

(2)

The scattering T matrix is defined through the Lippman-Schwinger (LS) equation:

T(z)=(-H_{I})+(-H_{I})(-G_{0}(z))T(z),

(3)

where
G_{0}(z)=(z-H_{0})^{-1}
is the free resolvent and the two particle states coupled to a spin S are

\displaystyle\ket{\mathbf{k_{1}},\mathbf{k_{2}},S,\nu}\equiv\ket{\mathbf{P},\mathbf{k},S,\nu},

(4)

\displaystyle\braket{\mathbf{P^{\prime}},\mathbf{k^{\prime}},S^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}|\mathbf{P},\mathbf{k},S,\nu}=4\omega_{1}\omega_{2}(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{P^{\prime}}-\mathbf{P})(2\pi)^{d}\delta^{d}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}}-\mathbf{k})\delta_{S^{\prime}S}\delta_{\nu^{\prime}\nu},

(5)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}=\mathbf{k_{1}}+\mathbf{k_{2}},\ \ \ \mathbf{k}=\mu_{2}\mathbf{k_{1}}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{k_{2}},\ \ \ \mu_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\pm\frac{m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2}}{P^{2}}\right),

(6)

where
S
,
\nu
label the total spin of the two particle system and
\mathbf{P}
,
\mathbf{k}
are the total and relative momentum in the “laboratory frame”. Now define the matrix elements:

t^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P},z)=\int\frac{d^{d}\mathbf{P^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\braket{\mathbf{P^{\prime}},\mathbf{k^{\prime}},S^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}|T(z)|\mathbf{P},\mathbf{k},S,\nu},

(7)

h^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P})=\int\frac{d^{d}\mathbf{P^{\prime}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\braket{\mathbf{P^{\prime}},\mathbf{k^{\prime}},S^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}|(-H_{I})|\mathbf{P},\mathbf{k},S,\nu}.

(8)

Additionally,
G_{0}
can be written in terms of an elementary diagram using the Feynman rules and
one can perform the integration over
q^{0}
using the Cauchy integration formula. One may rewrite the Lippman-Schwinger Equation in terms of matrix elements using Equations
7
and
8
:

\displaystyle t^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P},z)=h^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P})+\int\frac{d^{d}\mathbf{q_{1}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\sum_{\nu^{\prime\prime}}\frac{h^{S^{\prime}S^{\prime\prime}}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{q_{1}},\mathbf{P})t^{S^{\prime\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{q_{1}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P},z)}{4\omega_{1}(\mathbf{q_{1}})\omega_{2}(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{q_{1}}).(\omega_{1}(\mathbf{q_{1}})+\omega_{2}(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{q_{1}})-z)},

(9)

where
\mathbf{q}=\mu_{2}\mathbf{q_{1}}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{q_{2}}
, as in Equation
6
. A key point here is that the elementary bubble is diagonal in spin, because also the single particle propagators are. However, the scattering amplitude need not be diagonal.
Now define the projectors to the partial waves in the CM frame, whose momenta are
\mathbf{k^{*}}
:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}^{A^{\prime}A}(\mathbf{k^{\prime*}},\mathbf{k^{*}})=\sum_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}U^{S}_{\nu^{\prime}\rho^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k^{\prime*}})^{*}U^{S}_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{k^{*}})(\mathcal{Y}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k^{\prime*}},\rho^{\prime}))^{*}\mathcal{Y}_{JlS\mu}(\mathbf{k^{*}},\rho),

(10)

\displaystyle A=(J,l,S,\mu),\ \ A^{\prime}=(J^{\prime},l^{\prime},S^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}),

(11)

where
U^{S}_{\nu\rho}(\mathbf{k^{*}})
is the unitary transformation of the spin indices under a boost and the spin spherical harmonics, with
\mathbf{\hat{k}}=\mathbf{k}/|\mathbf{k}|
, read

\mathcal{Y}_{JlS\mu}(\mathbf{k},\nu)=\sum_{m,\sigma}\braket{lmS\sigma|J\mu}|\mathbf{k}|^{l}Y_{lm}(\mathbf{\hat{k}})\chi^{S}_{\sigma}(\nu)\equiv|\mathbf{k}|^{l}Y_{JlS\mu}(\mathbf{\hat{k}},\nu).

(12)

Using the projectors, Equations
7
and
8
take the form

\displaystyle t^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P},z)

\displaystyle=4\pi\sum_{A^{\prime},A}\Pi_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}^{A^{\prime}A}(\mathbf{k^{\prime*}},\mathbf{k^{*}})t_{A^{\prime}A}(s,\mathbf{P},z),

(13)

\displaystyle h^{S^{\prime}S}_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}(\mathbf{k^{\prime}},\mathbf{k},\mathbf{P})

\displaystyle=4\pi\sum_{A^{\prime},A}\Pi_{\nu^{\prime}\nu}^{A^{\prime}A}(\mathbf{k^{\prime*}},\mathbf{k^{*}})h_{A^{\prime}A}(s,\mathbf{P}).

(14)

If the system is placed in a box, the integral may be replaced by an sum:

\int\frac{d^{d}\mathbf{q_{1}}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{\mathbf{q_{1}}},

(15)

and by plugging Equations
13
and
14
into Equation
9
on the mass-shell, one arrives to

\displaystyle t_{A^{\prime}A}(s,\mathbf{P})-h_{A^{\prime}A}(s,\mathbf{P})=\frac{k^{*}}{8\pi\sqrt{s}}\sum_{B,B^{\prime}}h_{A^{\prime}B}(s,\mathbf{P})((\mathbf{k^{*}})^{l+l^{\prime}}i^{l-l^{\prime}}\delta_{S_{B}S_{B^{\prime}}}\mathcal{M}_{BB^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{P}))t_{B^{\prime}A}(s,\mathbf{P}),

(16)

with
s=P^{2}
and
S_{B}
being the spin of the multi-index
B
as in Equation
11
. Now, using unitarity of the transformation of the spin indices, one arrives at

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{JlS\mu,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}(s,\mathbf{P})=\frac{32\pi^{2}}{\mathbf{k^{*}}}\frac{\sqrt{s}}{L^{3}}i^{l^{\prime}-l}\delta_{S^{\prime}S}\sum_{q_{1}}\frac{\sum_{\nu}({Y}_{JlS\mu}(\mathbf{\hat{q}^{*}},\nu))^{*}{Y}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S\mu^{\prime}}(\mathbf{\hat{q}^{*}},\nu)}{4\omega_{1}(\mathbf{q_{1}})\omega_{2}(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{q_{1}})(\omega_{1}(\mathbf{q_{1}})+\omega_{2}(\mathbf{P}-\mathbf{q_{1}})-P_{0})}.

(17)

This matrix can be related to its equivalent for scalar particles by using Equation
12
:

\mathcal{M}_{JlS\mu,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}=\delta_{S^{\prime}S}\sum_{m,m^{\prime},\sigma}\braket{lm,S\sigma|J\mu}\braket{l^{\prime}m^{\prime},S\sigma|J^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{lm,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}},

(18)

where we used the identity
Gasser:2011ju
;
Bernard:2012bi
(with
\mathbf{q}=\mathbf{q_{1}}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{P}
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{1}{4\omega_{1}\omega_{2}(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-P_{0})}=\frac{1}{2P_{0}}\frac{1}{\mathbf{q}^{2}-\frac{(\mathbf{q}\mathbf{P})^{2}}{P_{0}^{2}}-(\mathbf{k^{*}})^{2}}\\
&+\frac{1}{4\omega_{1}\omega_{2}}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+P_{0}}-\frac{1}{\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}+P_{0}}-\frac{1}{\omega_{2}-\omega_{1}+P_{0}}\right),\end{split}

(19)

kept only the divergent part (first term in Equation
19
) and used
(\mathbf{q^{*}})^{2}=\mathbf{q}^{2}-\frac{(\mathbf{qP})^{2}}{P^{2}_{0}}
.
This way, and up to exponentially suppressed terms (
\mathbf{q^{*}}\rightarrow\mathbf{k^{*}}
),
\mathcal{M}_{lm,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}
is given by (see
Bernard:2012bi
)

\mathcal{M}_{lm,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\mathbf{k^{*}},s)=\frac{(-1)^{l}}{\pi^{3/2}\gamma}\sum_{j=|l-l^{\prime}|}^{l+l^{\prime}}\sum_{s=-j}^{j}\frac{i^{j}}{\eta^{j+1}}Z^{d}_{js}(1,s)^{*}C_{lm,js,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}},\ \eta=\frac{|\mathbf{k^{*}}|L}{2\pi},

(20)

where

C_{lm,js,l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}=(-1)^{m^{\prime}}i^{l-j+l^{\prime}}\sqrt{(2l+1)(2l^{\prime}+1)(2j+1)}\begin{pmatrix}l&j&l^{\prime}\\
m&s&-m^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}l&j&l^{\prime}\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix},

(21)

Z^{d}_{lm}(1,s)=\sum_{\mathbf{r}\in P_{d}}\frac{|\mathbf{r}|^{l}Y_{lm}(r)}{\mathbf{r}^{2}-\eta^{2}},\ \ \ P_{d}=\{\mathbf{r_{||}}=\gamma^{-1}(\mathbf{n_{||}}-\mu_{1}\mathbf{d}),\mathbf{r_{\perp}}=\mathbf{n_{\perp}}\},

(22)

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. One can see that Equation
16
is a matrix equation, and the poles in
t_{A^{\prime}A}
arise when

\det\left(\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{(\mathbf{k^{*}})^{l+l^{\prime}+1}}(h^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}})^{-1}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}-\delta_{S,S^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{JlS\mu,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S\mu^{\prime}}\right)=0,

(23)

where it is already implied that
J
and
\mu
are conserved in scattering processes in infinite volume and mixings can be present in
l
and
S
,
h_{JlS\mu,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}}=h^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}
. In order to express this equation in a more compact way, one uses the standard definition of the
S
matrix (see
Briceno:2013bda
, for nucleon-nucleon scattering),
S=e^{2i\delta(s)}
, in terms of the phase shift
\delta(s)
. This way, one can express
h^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}
in terms of
\delta
:

h^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S}=\frac{8\pi\sqrt{s}}{(\mathbf{k^{*}})^{l+l^{\prime}+1}}(\tan\delta)^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}},

(24)

and plugging it in Equation
23
, we arrive at

\det\left((\cot\delta)^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}-\delta_{SS^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}_{JlS\mu,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S\mu^{\prime}}\right)=0.

(25)

2.3
Reduction of Lüscher Equation

The basis vector labeled by
\alpha
of a symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
, in a certain irreducible representation
\Gamma
, are constructed (up to normalization) applying the projector

(P^{\Gamma,J,l}_{\alpha\beta})_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}=\sum_{i\in G}(R_{i}^{\Gamma})^{*}_{\alpha\beta}\tilde{D}^{J,l}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(R_{i}),

(27)

to the basis vectors in the continuum,
\ket{J,S,l,\mu}
, for a fixed
\beta
and
\mu
:

\ket{\Gamma,\alpha,J,S,l,n}\propto\sum_{\mu^{\prime}}(P^{\Gamma,J,l}_{\alpha\beta})_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\ket{J,S,l,\mu^{\prime}},

(28)

where
n
labels the number of occurrences of
\Gamma
in
J
,
l
. In the previous equations
\tilde{D}^{J,l}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(R_{i})=(-1)^{l}{D}^{J}_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}(R_{i})
if the element
i
includes inversion, or just the standard Wigner matrix if not. The basis vectors of the irreducible representations of the symmetry group of the lattice can be expressed in terms of the one of the continuum:

\ket{\Gamma,\alpha,J,l,S,n}=\sum_{\mu}c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}\ket{JlS\mu},

(29)

where the coefficients
c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}
are to be read from basis vector tables in References
Gockeler:2012yj
and
Bernard:2008ax
, for example. The matrix
\mathcal{M}
can be partially diagonalized in this basis:

\displaystyle\braket{\Gamma^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},J^{\prime},l^{\prime},S,n^{\prime}|\mathcal{M}|\Gamma,\alpha,J,l,S,n}=\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}Sn^{\prime},JlSn}\delta_{\Gamma^{\prime}\Gamma}\delta_{\alpha^{\prime}\alpha},

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}Sn^{\prime},JlSn}=\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}(c^{\Gamma n^{\prime}\alpha}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}})^{*}c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}\mathcal{M}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S\mu^{\prime},JlS\mu}.

(31)

Moreover, the matrix
\cot\delta
has to be brought to the same basis as
\mathcal{M}
:

(\cot\delta)^{\Gamma}_{JlSn,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}n^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu^{\prime}\mu}(c^{\Gamma n^{\prime}\alpha}_{J^{\prime}l^{\prime}\mu^{\prime}})^{*}c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}(\cot\delta)^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}\delta_{\mu\mu^{\prime}}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}=\sum_{\mu}(c^{\Gamma n^{\prime}\alpha}_{Jl^{\prime}\mu})^{*}c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}(\cot\delta)^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}.

(32)

The coefficients
c^{\Gamma n\alpha}_{Jl\mu}
just depend on the total angular momentum
J
and the behaviour under spatial inversions
(-1)^{l}
. Moreover, mixing can only occur between states with same
J
and
(-1)^{l}
, so the sum over the index
\mu
yields either 1 or the matrix
\cot\delta
is trivially zero due to the symmetry considerations. Spin does not enter here at all, because it does not influence the coefficients. This way,
(\cot\delta)^{\Gamma}_{JlSn,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}S^{\prime}n^{\prime}}
is diagonal in
J
and
n

and the determinant factorizes:

\prod_{S=\begin{subarray}{c}even\\
odd\end{subarray}}\ \ \prod_{\Gamma}\ \det\left((\cot\delta)^{J}_{lS,l^{\prime}S^{\prime}}\delta_{JJ^{\prime}}\delta_{nn^{\prime}}-\delta_{SS^{\prime}}\mathcal{M}^{\Gamma}_{JlSn,J^{\prime}l^{\prime}Sn^{\prime}}\right)=0.

(33)

3
Toy Model: Scalar QED

In order to test the formalism, we use Scalar QED with a Higgs mechanism, since the vector state needs to be massive. The continuum Euclidean Lagrangian of such a theory reads

\mathcal{L}_{E}=(D_{\mu}\phi_{c})^{\dagger}D_{\mu}\phi_{c}+m^{2}_{0}|\phi_{c}|^{2}+\lambda_{c}|\phi_{c}|^{4},\quad D_{\mu}\phi_{c}=\partial_{\mu}\phi_{c}+igA_{\mu}\phi_{c}.

(34)

The discretized action is (See
SCALARQED
)

\displaystyle S=\sum_{x}

\displaystyle\Big(-\frac{\beta}{2}\sum_{\mu<\nu}(U_{\mu\nu}+U_{\mu\nu}^{*})-\kappa\sum_{\mu}(\phi_{x}^{*}U_{x,\mu}\phi_{x+\mu}+cc)+\lambda(|\phi_{x}|^{2}-1)^{2}+|\phi_{x}|^{2}\Big),

(35)

with

\displaystyle\lambda_{c}=\frac{\lambda}{\kappa^{2}},\quad(am_{0})^{2}=\frac{1-2\lambda-8\kappa}{\kappa},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{g^{2}}.

(36)

The basic operators for the scalar and real particle are

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{A_{1}}=\sum_{i}\operatorname{Re}(\phi^{\dagger}(x,t)U_{i}(x)\phi(x+ae_{i},t)),

(37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}^{T_{1}^{-}}_{n,i}=\operatorname{Im}(\phi^{\dagger}(x,t)\left(\prod_{n=0}^{N-1}U_{i}(x+ne_{i})\right)\phi(x+Ne_{i},t)),

(38)

respectively. Notice that the second one is highly non-local, since this have been seen to improve significantly the signal. In particular, with the vector operator, one can build one and two particle operators that transform under certain irreducible representations of the spatial symmetry group, in rest and moving frames. A one particle operator transforming under a certain irrep
\Gamma
with invariant momentum under the spacial symmetry group
p\in\mathcal{G}
can be built from an arbitrary operator
\mathcal{O}(x,t)
as follows:

\mathcal{O}^{\Gamma}(p,t)=\sum_{x}e^{ipx}\sum_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{\Gamma}(S_{i})\ (S_{i}\mathcal{O})(S_{i}^{-1}x,t)

(39)

Equivalently, for a two particle operator:

\mathcal{O}^{\Gamma}(p,q,t)=\sum_{x,y}\left(\sum_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}e^{ipx+S_{i}q(y-x)}\right)\sum_{S_{i}\in\mathcal{G}}\chi^{\Gamma}(S_{i})\ (S_{i}\mathcal{O})(S_{i}^{-1}x,S_{i}^{-1}y,t),

(40)

where
p
is the total momentum that belonging to the spatial symmetry group
\mathcal{G}
,
q
is a relative momentum that the two particles have and
\mathcal{O}(x,y,t)
is an arbitrary two particle operator.

5
Summary and Outlook

We have shown that the study on the Lattice of scattering of vector particles is possible by applying the derived framework to the toy model Scalar QED. The complete numerical results, together with the group theoretical derivations, will be published soon in a longer, more detailed version. On the long term, we expect to apply the ideas of this work to study the possibility of the Higgs boson to be a resonance of two
W
bosons. This is the case for a model proposed by Frezzotti
et al.

Frezzotti:2014wja
;
Frezzotti:2016bes
, where a “superstrong interaction” together with superstrongly interacting particles are present.

A class of non-reversible hypercube long-range random walks and Bernoulli autoregression
Andrea Collevecchio, Robert C. Griffiths

Abstract
We study a large class of long-range random walks which take values on the vertices of an N N dimensional hypercube. These processes are connected with multivariate Bernoulli autoregression. Keywords Bernoulli Autoregression, Krawtchouk Polynomials, General Random Walks on the Hypercube. MSC 60Gxx, 62Mxx

Introduction

Collevecchio and Griffiths 2021
study a class of reversible long-range random walks
(X_{t})_{t}
on the hypercube
{\cal V}_{N}=\{0,1\}^{N}
. The random walk is characterized by having an
N
-product Bernoulli
(p)
stationary distribution and the property that

\mathbb{P}(X_{t}(B)\in C\mid X_{t-1})=\mathbb{P}(X_{t}(B)\in C\mid X_{t-1}(B)),

where
B\subseteq[N]=\{1,2,\ldots,N\}
,
C\subseteq\{0,1\}^{B}
and
X_{t}(B)
denotes a restriction to the coordinates in
B
. The characterization, under Conditions
1
and
2
in Section
2
, is equivalent to a Lancaster characterization of the eigenvalues in the spectral expansion of the transition functions with tensor product eigenfunctions constructed from products of orthogonal functions on the marginal distributions. Knowing the spectral expansion allows a calculation of cut-off rates as
N\to\infty
. These mixing time rates are very fast in some cases, taking only two or three steps. In this paper we consider an extension of the processes in
Collevecchio and Griffiths 2021
to non-stationary long-range random walks. In the reversible model if
p_{xy}
,
x,y\in\{0,1\}
is the marginal transition probability at a coordinate and
p_{0}=1-p,p_{1}=p
, then there is a reversibility property that
p_{x}p_{xy}=p_{y}p_{yx}
. Here this condition is relaxed somewhat in Condition
1
so that
if
a coordinate
X_{t}[k]
has a Bernoulli
(\gamma)
distribution,
then

X_{t+1}[k]
is distributed as Bernoulli
(\varphi)
, where
\gamma,\phi
are specific parameters of the model. This extended concept of reversibility may seem unfamiliar, but commonly holds in
AR(1)
series. Further comments about this assumption can be found in Section
2
.
It is possible to construct
(X_{t})_{t}
with state space
\{0,1\}^{\infty}
which has exchangeable coordinates such that for any
N
coordinates
(X_{t}^{(N)})_{t}
belongs to the class studied. This is an application of de Finetti’s representation for countably infinite exchangeable random variables.

Existing literature and structure of the paper

Characterization and spectral expansion

We first focus on the transition of a single coordinate from
x
to

\gamma

\varphi

\varphi_{1}=\varphi

p_{xy}=\varphi_{y}\{1+\kappa(1-x/\gamma)(1-y/\varphi)\},

(1)

where

This is a spectral expansion in one transition using the orthogonal function set on

\{1,1-y/\varphi\}
and similarly for
X

always has an orthogonal function expansion

\phi_{y}\gamma_{x}\big\{1+\kappa_{01}(1-y/\varphi)+\kappa_{10}(1-x/\gamma)+\kappa(1-x/\gamma)(1-y/\varphi)\big\}

(\varphi)

\kappa_{01}=0
and similarly
\kappa_{10}=0
since

is Bernoulli
(\gamma)

Let

is equivalent to

if
\varphi+\gamma\geq 1

if
\varphi+\gamma<1

\varphi+\gamma<1

might be exchanged for
\bar{x},\bar{y}
and
\varphi,\gamma

\varphi=\gamma

Any non-degenerate markov chain on
\{0,1\}
is reversible and ergodic.
In our case, where the transition probabilities are determined by (
1

\frac{1-\kappa(1-\varphi)}{1-(1-\varphi-\gamma)\kappa/\gamma}.

(2)

Coordinates of the hypercube that we study are generally not independent, the usual reversibility property does not hold jointly for the coordinates, although it does hold marginally for the coordinates, and the stationary distribution is not a product distribution unless
\varphi=\gamma

2

\otimes_{k=1}^{N}\{1,1-y[k]/\varphi\}

x

3
Bernoulli autoregressive system

Corollary 4
.

(X_{t})_{t}
satisfies a Bernoulli autoregressive system

X_{t}[k]=A_{t}[k]X_{t-1}[k]+B_{t}[k](1-X_{t-1}[k]),\quad k\in[N],

where
A_{t},B_{t}
depend on
Z_{t}
such that

(A_{t}[k],B_{t}[k])=\begin{cases}\begin{cases}(I_{U(t,k)\leq\varphi/\gamma},0)&\mbox{ if }\varphi\leq\gamma\\
(1,I_{U(t,k)>\bar{\varphi}/\gamma})&\mbox{ if }\varphi>\gamma\end{cases}&\text{in the case~}Z_{t}[k]=0,\\
(I_{U(t,k)>\bar{\varphi}/\gamma},1)&\text{in the case ~}Z_{t}[k]=1.\end{cases}

It is straightforward to show that

\text{Cov}\big(A_{t}[k],B_{t}[k]\big)=\begin{cases}-(\bar{\gamma}/\gamma)\text{Var}\big(Z_{t}[k]\big)&\varphi\leq\gamma\\
-(\bar{\varphi}^{2}/(\gamma\bar{\gamma}))\text{Var}\big(Z_{t}[k]\big)&\varphi>\gamma.\end{cases}

A_{t}
and
B_{t}
are independent if and only if
\max\{\varphi,\gamma\}=1
, when the coefficients of
\text{Var}\big(Z_{t}[k]\big)
are zero, or
Z_{t}
is a constant in
{\cal V}_{N}
with probability 1.

The conditional means and variances in a transition are now calculated. From (
12

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[k]-\varphi\mid X_{t-1},Z_{t}\big]=-\bar{\varphi}/(\bar{\alpha}\gamma)(Z_{t}[k]-\alpha)(X_{t-1}[k]-\gamma),

(19)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[k]\mid X_{t-1}\big]=(\varphi-\alpha)/\bar{\alpha}+(\bar{\varphi}/\bar{\alpha})\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{t}[k]\big]+(\alpha\bar{\varphi})/(\bar{\alpha}\varphi)X_{t-1}[k]-\bar{\varphi}/(\bar{\alpha}\gamma)\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{t}[k]\big]X_{t-1}[k],

\displaystyle\text{Var}\big(X_{t}[k]\mid X_{t-1}\big)=\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[k]\mid X_{t-1}\big]\Big(1-\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[k]\mid X_{t-1}\big]\Big).

For
k\neq l

\displaystyle\text{Cov}\big(X_{t}[k],X_{t}[l]\mid X_{t-1})

\displaystyle=0+\text{Cov}\big(\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[k]\mid X_{t-1},Z_{t}\big],\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}[l]\mid X_{t-1},Z_{t}\big]\big)

\displaystyle=\bar{\varphi}/(\bar{\alpha}\gamma)^{2}\text{Cov}\big(Z_{t}[k],Z_{t}[l]\big)(X_{t-1}[k]-\gamma)(X_{t-1}[l]-\gamma)

In a process where
Z_{t}
is time homogeneous and
(X_{t})_{t}
is stationary denote

\displaystyle m_{Z}=\mathbb{E}\big[Z_{t}],~m_{X}=\mathbb{E}\big[X_{t}],

\displaystyle C_{Z}=\big(\text{Cov}(Z[k],Z[l])\Big)_{k,l\in[N]},~C_{X}=\big(\text{Cov}(X[k],X[l])\Big)_{k,l\in[N]}.

From (
19
)

\displaystyle m_{X}[k]

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi-\alpha+\bar{\varphi}m_{Z}[k]}{\bar{\alpha}+(\bar{\varphi}/\gamma)(m_{Z}[k]-\alpha)}.

(20)

The denominator in (

For the covariance matrix,

\displaystyle C_{X}(k,k)

\displaystyle=m_{X}[k](1-m_{X}[k])

\displaystyle C_{X}(k,l)

\displaystyle=\frac{\varphi^{2}A_{Z}(k,l)(m_{X}[k]-\gamma)(m_{X}[l]-\gamma)}{\bar{\alpha}^{2}\gamma^{2}-\varphi^{2}A_{Z}(k,l)},\qquad\mbox{for $k\neq l$,}

where

Proposition 6

(Properties of the Krawtchouk
polynomials)
.

The Krawtchouk polynomials
\{Q_{n}(\zeta;N,\alpha)\}_{n=0}^{N}
that we use are orthogonal on the Binomial
(N,\alpha)
distribution, scaled so that
Q_{n}(0;N,\alpha)=1
, and

\mathbb{E}\big[Q_{n}(\zeta;N,\alpha)^{2}\big]^{-1}={N\choose n}\Big(\frac{\alpha}{\bar{\alpha}}\Big)^{n}.

Their generating function is

\sum_{n=0}^{N}{N\choose n}Q_{n}(\zeta;N,\alpha)s^{n}=(1-(\bar{\alpha}/\alpha)s)^{\zeta}(1+s)^{N-\zeta}.

If
\zeta=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\xi_{l}
, where
\xi_{l}\in\{0,1\}
,
l=1,\ldots,N
, then
Q_{n}(\zeta;N,\alpha)
is the
n
th elementary symmetric function in
\{1-\xi_{i}/\alpha\}_{i=1}^{N}
. That is

Q_{n}(\zeta;N,\alpha)={N\choose n}^{-1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\prod_{l=1}^{n}(1-\xi_{\sigma(l)}/\alpha),

where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group of order
n
.

For a proof of Proposition
6
and a probabilistic description of these polynomials see
Diaconis and Griffiths 2012
.

Estimation from a sample path

Let
(X_{t})
be time homogeneous and assume that the coordinates of
X_{t}
are exchangeable.
The data is a sample path
(x_{t})_{t=0}^{T}
. Let
n_{ij}^{t}

i,j\in\{0,1\}
denote the number of transitions
i\to j
in transition

Estimation of
\varphi,\gamma

If the coordinates of
X_{t-1}
have an independent
N
-Bernoulli
(\gamma)
distribution then the coordinates of
X_{t}
have an independent
N
-Bernoulli
(\varphi)
distribution. This is always true and does not depend on
(Z_{t})
.
The two parameters can be estimated by a least squares approach by minimizing

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\Big(\bar{\gamma}\widehat{p}_{01}[t]+\gamma\widehat{p}_{11}[t]-\varphi\Big)^{2},

(41)

where
\widehat{p}_{ij}[t]=n_{ij}^{t}/n^{t-1}_{i\cdot}
, assuming that
n^{t-1}_{i\cdot}\geq 1
. Denote

d[t]=\widehat{p}_{01}[t]-\widehat{p}_{11}[t]
,
\bar{p}_{ij}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widehat{p}_{ij}[t]/T
,
\bar{d}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}d[t]/T
.
Suppose
\varphi\neq\gamma
. The least squares estimates satisfy

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}+\widehat{\gamma}\bar{d}=\bar{p}_{01},\qquad\mbox{ and }\qquad\widehat{\varphi}\bar{d}+\widehat{\gamma}\sum_{t=1}^{T}d[t]^{2}/T=\sum_{t=1}^{T}d[t]\widehat{p}_{01}[t]/T.

(42)

Solving (
42
)

\widehat{\gamma}=\frac{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(d[t]-\bar{d})(\widehat{p}_{01}[t]-\bar{p}_{01})}{\sum_{t=1}^{T}(d[t]-\bar{d})^{2}},

with
\widehat{\varphi}
determined by the first equation in (
42
).

If
\varphi=\gamma
minimizing (
41
),

\widehat{\varphi}=\frac{\bar{p}_{01}}{1+\bar{d}}.

Estimating the distribution of
Z_{t}

Estimation of the distribution of
Z_{t}
in a simple exchangeable model is now worked through. We assume that
\varphi
and
\gamma
are known, but if not they can be estimated by the proceedure above. At each transition, conditional on an observation
\theta_{t}
from a measure
\nu
take the
N
coordinates of

to be
i.i.d.

(\theta_{t})
. We assume that
\nu
is continuous with support a subset or equal to
(0,1)
and a density with respect to Lebesgue measure.
X_{t}
is observed but
Z_{t}
is not.
This is a Bayesian approach where in each transition the probability in the Bernoulli distribution of coordinates is a random variable.
The likelihood in transition

\theta_{t}
, when
\{n_{ij}^{t}\}
is observed is

\prod_{i=0}^{1}\prod_{j=0}^{1}{n_{i\cdot}\choose n_{ij}}p_{t}(i,j;\theta_{t})^{n_{ij}^{t}},

Maximum likelihood can now be used to obtain an estimate
\widehat{\theta}_{t}
in each transition. If
N
is large this estimate will be approximately normally distributed with a variance proportional to
N^{-1}
. Information about
\nu
is then obtained by the estimates
\widehat{\theta_{t}}
over transitions.
There are two independent equations in the following in each transition when

\displaystyle p_{0}(\theta_{t})

\displaystyle:={\bar{\varphi}}\Big\{1+\frac{{\alpha}}{{\bar{\alpha}}}\big(1-\frac{{\theta_{t}}}{{\alpha}}\big)\Big\}=\begin{cases}1-\theta_{t}&\varphi\leq\gamma\\
\frac{\bar{\varphi}}{\bar{\gamma}}(1-\theta_{t})&\varphi>\gamma\end{cases}

\displaystyle p_{1}(\theta_{t})

\displaystyle:={\bar{\varphi}}\Big\{1-\frac{{\alpha}}{{\bar{\alpha}}}\big(1-\frac{{\theta_{t}}}{{\alpha}}\big)\frac{{\bar{\gamma}}}{{\gamma}}\Big)\Big\}=\begin{cases}1-\varphi/\gamma+\theta_{t}\bar{\gamma}/\gamma&\varphi\leq\gamma\\
\frac{\bar{\varphi}}{\gamma}\theta_{t}&\varphi>\gamma.\end{cases}

An estimate

\displaystyle 0=c_{0}(\varphi,\gamma)\Big(-n^{t}_{00}\frac{1}{p_{0}(\theta_{t})}+n^{t}_{01}\frac{1}{1-p_{0}(\theta_{t})}\Big)+c_{1}(\varphi,\gamma)\Big(n^{t}_{10}\frac{1}{p_{1}(\theta_{t})}-n^{t}_{11}\frac{1}{1-p_{1}(\theta_{t})}\Big)

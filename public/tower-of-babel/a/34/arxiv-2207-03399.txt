Notes on the arithmetic of Hecke L L -functions
A. Raghuram

Abstract
. This is an expository article that concerns the various related notions of algebraic idèle class characters, the Größencharaktere of Hecke, and cohomological automorphic representations of GL ⁡ ( 1 ) {\rm GL}(1) , all under the general title of algebraic Hecke characters. The first part of the article systematically lays the foundations of algebraic Hecke characters. The only pre-requisites are: basic algebraic number theory, familiarity with the adelic language, and basic sheaf theory. Observations that play a crucial role in the arithmetic of automorphic L L -functions are also discussed. The second part of the article, on the ratios of successive critical values of the Hecke L L -function attached to an algebraic Hecke character, concerns certain variations on a theorem of Harder [ 6 ] , especially drawing attention to a delicate signature that apparently has not been noticed before.

2.
Hecke characters

We set up some general notation for this article:

\mathbb{Q}
is the rational number field, with closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
inside complex numbers
\mathbb{C}
;

F
is a number field, i.e., a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}
;

we do not assume that
F
is contained in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}\subset\mathbb{C}
;

d_{F}=d=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
;

\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the ring of integers of
F
;

U_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
is the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
;

\Sigma_{F}={\rm Hom}(F,\mathbb{C})
, all embeddings of
F
into
\mathbb{C}
; note:
{\rm Hom}(F,\bar{\mathbb{Q}})={\rm Hom}(F,\mathbb{C});

S_{\infty}
= set of all archimedean places of
F
;

S_{\infty}=S_{r}\cup S_{c}
the real and complex places, respectively;

r_{1}=\#S_{r},

r_{2}=\#S_{c}
;
d_{F}=r_{1}+2r_{2};

\mathfrak{p}
a finite prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
or an infinite place;

F_{\mathfrak{p}}
the completion of
F
at
\mathfrak{p};

\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the ring of integers of
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
at a finite prime
\mathfrak{p};

\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a uniformizer at a finite prime
\mathfrak{p};
hence
\mathfrak{p}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}};

U_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for a finite prime
\mathfrak{p}.

2.1.
Class groups

We start with some preliminaries on class groups, towards which we need some more notation:

\mathbb{J}_{F}
is the group of all fractional ideals of
F;

\mathbb{P}_{F}
is the group of all principal fractional ideals
(x)=x\mathcal{O}_{F}
for
x\in F^{\times};

x\gg 0
means that
x\in F
is totally positive, i.e.,
\rho(x)>0
for all
\rho\in{\rm Hom}(F,\mathbb{R})
;

\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}=\{(x)\in\mathbb{P}_{F}\ :\ x\gg 0\};

\mathcal{C}l_{F}=\mathbb{J}_{F}/\mathbb{P}_{F}
is the class group of
F
;

\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}=\mathbb{J}_{F}/\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}
is the narrow class group of
F
.

The adjective ‘narrow’ is to suggest narrow conditions in
\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}
that we divide by to get
\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}
; the narrow class group surjects onto the class group; we have the exact sequence:

(1)

0\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{P}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}}\longrightarrow 0.

Observe that

\frac{\mathbb{P}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}}\ \simeq\ \frac{F^{\times}/U_{F}}{F^{\times}_{+}/U_{F}^{+}}\ \simeq\ \frac{F^{\times}}{F^{\times}_{+}U_{F}},

where
F^{\times}_{+}=\{x\in F^{\times}:x\gg 0\}
and
U_{F}^{+}=U_{F}\cap F^{\times}_{+}
is the group of totally positive units.
We have an exact sequence:

(2)

0\longrightarrow\frac{U_{F}}{U_{F}^{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{F^{\times}}{F^{\times}_{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{F^{\times}}{F^{\times}_{+}U_{F}}\longrightarrow 0.

Splicing the two exact sequences, we get a four-term exact sequence relating the narrow class group to the class group:

(3)

0\longrightarrow\frac{U_{F}}{U_{F}^{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{F^{\times}}{F^{\times}_{+}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathcal{C}l_{F}\longrightarrow 0.

The narrow class group surjects onto the class group and the ratio of their orders is a power of
2
since

[F^{\times}:F^{\times}_{+}]=2^{r_{1}}.
(This suggests an interesting exercise in basic number theory:
given
r_{1},t_{1}\in\mathbb{Z},
with
r_{1}\geq 1
and
0\leq t_{1}\leq r_{1}
, construct a number field
F
with exactly
r_{1}
real embeddings and such that the order of the group
U_{F}/U_{F}^{+}
is
2^{t_{1}},
equivalently, the kernel of
\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}\rightarrow\mathcal{C}l_{F}
is
2^{r_{1}-t_{1}}.
)

To discuss the class groups in terms of idèles, here are some more notations:

\mathbb{A}_{F}
is the adèle ring of
F;

\mathbb{I}_{F}
is the group of idèles of
F
;

C_{F}=\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}
is the group of idèle-classes of
F
;

U_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathcal{O}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
for finite
\mathfrak{p}
as before, but moreover

U_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
for
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{r},
and

U_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathbb{C}^{\times}
for
\mathfrak{p}\in S_{c};

\mathbb{I}_{F}^{S}
= group of
S
-idèles for any finite set
S
of places;

F_{\infty}\ :=\ F\otimes\mathbb{R}\ \simeq\ \prod_{v\in S_{r}}F_{v}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}F_{w}\ \simeq\ \prod_{v\in S_{r}}\mathbb{R}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\mathbb{C};

F_{\infty+}=\{x=(x_{\lambda})\in F_{\infty}\ :\ x_{v}>0,\ \forall v\in S_{r}\}.

There is a canonical map

\bm{\upiota}:\mathbb{I}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{J}_{F}

which sends an idèle
a=(a_{\mathfrak{p}})
to the ideal
\bm{\upiota}(a):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}\mathfrak{p}^{{\rm ord}_{\mathfrak{p}}(a_{\mathfrak{p}})}.
It is clear that

{\rm Kernel}(\bm{\upiota})=F_{\infty}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}.

The map
\bm{\upiota}
induces an isomorphism which gives an idèle-theoretic description of the class group of
F
:

(4)

\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}(F_{\infty}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})}\ \simeq\ \frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}}\ =\ \mathcal{C}l_{F}.

Similarly, one has for the narrow class group:

\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F_{+}^{\times}(F_{\infty}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})}\ \simeq\ \frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}}\ =\ \mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}.

Weak-approximation gives that
F^{\times}
is dense in
F_{\infty}^{\times}
; hence

F_{+}^{\times}F_{\infty}^{\times}=F^{\times}F_{\infty+}^{\times};
whence:

(5)

\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}(F_{\infty+}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})}\ \simeq\ \frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}}\ =\ \mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}.

The four-term long exact sequence relating the class group and the narrow class group described using idèles takes the form:

(6)

0\longrightarrow\frac{U_{F}}{U_{F}^{+}}\longrightarrow\frac{F_{\infty}^{\times}}{F_{\infty+}^{\times}}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}(F_{\infty+}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}(F_{\infty}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})}\longrightarrow 0.

To compare the second term from the left in (
3
) with that in (
6
) we note that weak-approximation says that the canonical map

F^{\times}/F_{+}^{\times}\rightarrow F_{\infty}^{\times}/F^{\times}_{\infty+}

induced by the diagonal inclusion
F^{\times}\hookrightarrow F_{\infty}^{\times}

is an isomorphism.

2.2.
Basics of Hecke characters

A Hecke character is a continuous homomorphism

\chi\ :\ \mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\ \rightarrow\ \mathbb{C}^{\times}.

We do not ask
\chi
to be unitary. Some people prefer calling a unitary Hecke character as a Hecke character, and what we call a Hecke character, as a Hecke quasi-character. It is painful to keep writing ‘quasi-character’ all the time, and so, with a possible abuse of terminology, we will work with the above definition. This is not so serious as we now explain: Consider the norm of an idèle
\alpha\in\mathbb{I}_{F}
defined as
|\!|\alpha|\!|:=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}|\alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}|_{\mathfrak{p}}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
runs over all valuations (finite and archimedean), and each valuation is normalized.
Then
|\!|\ |\!|:\mathbb{I}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
is a surjective homomorphism and let
{}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}
denote its kernel. The product formula says that
|\!|a|\!|=1
for all
a\in F^{\times}
, i.e.,

F^{\times}\subset{}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}.
We have the following exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow\frac{{}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}}\longrightarrow\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\longrightarrow 0.

This sequence splits. There are several splittings; for example: map
t\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
into the idèle which is
t
at a particular real infinite place and
1
elsewhere, or map it to
t^{1/2}
at a particular complex infinite place and
1
elsewhere. But this depends on a choice of an infinite place. Instead, using
d=d_{F}=[F:\mathbb{Q}]
,
we will always consider the following splitting:

t\mapsto(t^{1/d},\dots,t^{1/d};1,1,\dots),

where the idèle has the positive
d
-th root of
t
at every infinite place and
1
at every finite place. (Note that it is indeed a splitting, because for any complex place, the normalized valuation satisfies
|x|_{\mathbb{C}}=|x|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}.
) This splitting gives

\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\ \simeq\ {}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}.

It is a fundamental fact that
{}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}
is compact (Neukirch
[
10
, Theorem VI.1.6]
).
A continuous homomorphism of
{}^{0}\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}
into
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
has compact image and so lands in
S^{1}.

Further, any homomorphism
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is of the form
x\mapsto|x|^{w}
for a complex number

w=\sigma+i\varphi
. (See
2.5.1
below.) Putting these remarks together,
any Hecke character
\chi
can be uniquely factored as

(7)

\chi\ =\ {}^{0}\chi\otimes|\!|\ |\!|^{\sigma}

for a unitary Hecke character
{}^{0}\chi:\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\rightarrow S^{1}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R};
sometimes
{}^{0}\chi
is denoted also as
\chi^{u}.

2.3.
Dirichlet characters: Hecke characters of finite order

A continuous homomorphism
\chi:\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

with finite image and which is unramified everywhere (i.e.,
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\chi|_{F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}}
is trivial on the units
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
for all finite
\mathfrak{p}
) gives a
character of
\frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}(F_{\infty+}^{\times}\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}})},
and by (
5
), is a character of the
narrow class group
\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}.
This generalizes by introducing some level structure giving us a description of Hecke characters of finite order in terms of
characters of narrow ray class groups with level structure. Here is some more notation:

\mathfrak{m}
will be an integral ideal with prime-factorization
\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}\mathfrak{p}^{m_{\mathfrak{p}}};

U_{\mathfrak{p}}(m_{\mathfrak{p}})
will be defined as:

U_{\mathfrak{p}}(m_{\mathfrak{p}})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1+\mathfrak{p}^{m_{\mathfrak{p}}},&{\rm if}\ \mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty},\ \mathfrak{p}\,|\,\mathfrak{m},\\
U_{\mathfrak{p}},&{\rm if}\ \mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty},\ \mathfrak{p}\not|\,\mathfrak{m},\\
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+},&{\rm if}\ \mathfrak{p}\in S_{r},\\
\mathbb{C}^{\times},&{\rm if}\ \mathfrak{p}\in S_{c}.\end{array}\right.

\mathbb{U}_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}(m_{\mathfrak{p}});

\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}):=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}(m_{\mathfrak{p}});

C_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathbb{U}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})F^{\times}/F^{\times}
is called the congruence subgroup mod
\mathfrak{m}
in
C_{F};

C_{F}/C_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathbb{I}_{F}/\mathbb{U}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})F^{\times}
is the idèle-theoretic narrow ray class group mod
\mathfrak{m}
;

\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is the group of all fractional ideals relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m};

\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is the group of all principal fractional ideals
(x)
with
x\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}.

\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
is the subgroup of
\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
consisting of those
(x)
with
x\gg 0;

\mathcal{C}l_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is the ray class group mod
\mathfrak{m}
;

\mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})
is the narrow ray class group mod
\mathfrak{m}
.

Proposition 8
.

The canonical homomorphism
w:\mathbb{I}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{J}_{F}
induces an isomorphism:

\frac{C_{F}}{C_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}\ \simeq\ \frac{\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}{\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m})}.

Proof.

Define:

\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\ :=\ \{\alpha\in\mathbb{I}_{F}\ :\ \alpha_{\mathfrak{p}}\in U_{\mathfrak{p}}(m_{\mathfrak{p}}),\ \forall\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}\cdot\infty\}.

Weak approximation implies:
\mathbb{I}_{F}=F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m}).
Hence

C_{F}=\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}=\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})/(F^{\times}\cap\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})).

Note that
\bm{\upiota}
maps
\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
onto
\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
and similarly, maps
F^{\times}\cap\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
onto
\mathbb{P}_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m}).
Further,

{\rm Ker}\left(\bm{\upiota}:\mathbb{I}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\right)\ =\ \mathbb{U}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
. The rest is easy.
∎

A fundamental property of these congruence subgroups
C_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is captured by

Proposition 9
.

For any integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, the congruence subgroup
C_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
is a subgroup of the idèle class group
C_{F}
of finite index. Conversely, any subgroup of finite index of
C_{F}
contains
C_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
for some
\mathfrak{m}.

Proof.

See Neukirch
[
10
, Proposition VI.1.8]
.
∎

The moral of the above discussion is that Hecke characters of finite order are exactly the characters of narrow ray class groups:

Corollary 10

(Finite order Hecke characters)
.

Consider a continuous homomorphism

\chi\ :\ \mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\ \longrightarrow\ \mathbb{C}^{\times}

whose image is of finite order. Then there is an integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\chi
factors as

\textstyle{\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}=C_{F}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\chi}
\textstyle{\mathbb{C}^{\times}}
\textstyle{C_{F}/C_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

The smallest such
\mathfrak{m}
is called the conductor of
\chi
and will be denoted
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}.

A Dirichlet character of
F
is a (necessarily unitary) character of a narrow ray class group of
F
with level structure,
i.e., it is a homomorphism

\chi:\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})^{+}\ \rightarrow\ S^{1},

for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}.
By Prop.
8
, Prop.
9
, and Cor.
10
it follows
that Dirichlet characters are exactly the Hecke characters of finite order. The algebraic Hecke characters that we want to understand are in general not of finite order.

An easy exercise is to take
F=\mathbb{Q}
,
\mathfrak{m}=N\mathbb{Z}
to see that a Dirichlet character for
\mathbb{Q}
modulo
N\mathbb{Z}
is exactly a homomorphism

(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times};
such characters were considered by Dirichlet in his proof of infinitude of primes in arithmetic progressions; this exercise justifies the terminology.

2.4.
Local components at finite places and conductor

Fix any place
\mathfrak{p}
of
F
. Then
F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
embeds into
\mathbb{I}_{F}
as
x_{\mathfrak{p}}\mapsto(1,\dots,1,x_{\mathfrak{p}},1\dots),

the idele having
x_{\mathfrak{p}}
at the place corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}
and
1
elsewhere.
The restriction
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\chi
to this copy of
F_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\times}
is called the local character at
\mathfrak{p}
. Sometimes we use
v
for a place instead of
\mathfrak{p}.

For almost all finite
\mathfrak{p}
, the character
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}
is unramified, i.e., it is trivial on
U_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This may be seen by considering the restriction of
\chi
to
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S_{\infty}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}
, a compact and totally disconnected group the image of which under a continuous homomorphism into
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
must be finite by the so-called ‘no small-subgroups argument’; see, for example, Bump
[
2
, Exercise 3.1.1]
. One can write
\chi=\otimes^{\prime}_{v}\chi_{v}
as a restricted tensor product.
By the same token
\chi
is trivial on
\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})
for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}.

The smallest ideal
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\chi
is trivial on
\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})
is called conductor of
\chi
and
will be denoted
\mathfrak{f}_{\chi}.

2.5.
The character at infinity of a Hecke character

2.5.1.
Characters of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}

Any continuous homomorphism
\chi:\mathbb{R}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is of the form

\chi(x)\ =\ {\rm sgn}(x)^{n}\,|x|^{w}\ =\ \left(\frac{x}{|x|}\right)^{n}\,|x|^{w},

with
n\in\{0,1\}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}.
Such a character
\chi
is unitary if and only if
w=\mathfrak{i}\varphi\in\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}.

2.5.2.
Characters of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}

For
z=x+\mathfrak{i}y\in\mathbb{C}
, define
|z|:=|z|_{\mathbb{R}}:=\sqrt{x^{2}+y^{2}},
and the normalised valuation on

\mathbb{C}
as
|z|_{\mathbb{C}}:=|z|_{\mathbb{R}}^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}.
A continuous homomorphism
\chi:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
is of the form

\chi(z)\ =\ \left(\frac{z}{|z|}\right)^{n}\,|z|_{\mathbb{C}}^{w},

with
n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}.
If
z=re^{\mathfrak{i}\theta}
with
r\in\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, then
\chi(z)=r^{2w}e^{\mathfrak{i}n\theta}.

Such a character
\chi
is unitary if and only if
w=\mathfrak{i}\varphi\in\mathfrak{i}\mathbb{R}.

2.5.3.
Description of
\chi_{\infty}

For a Hecke character
\chi
, let
\chi_{\infty}=\chi\,|_{F_{\infty}^{\times}}
where
F_{\infty}^{\times}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{I}_{F}.
To describe this character at infinity explicitly, we introduce some more notation:

\lambda
is any infinite place;
\lambda\in S_{\infty};

v
is any real place;
v\in S_{r}
and
F_{v}\simeq\mathbb{R}
canonically;

w
is any complex place;
w\in S_{c}
and
F_{w}\simeq\mathbb{C}
non-canonically;

|x_{\infty}|_{\infty}=\prod_{\lambda}|x_{\lambda}|_{\lambda}
for
x_{\infty}\in F_{\infty}
is the product of normalized valuations.

Keeping in mind that a Hecke character factorizes as
\chi={}^{0}\chi\otimes|\ |^{\sigma}
(see (
7
)),
one can write the character at infinity
\chi_{\infty}
on
x_{\infty}\in F_{\infty}^{\times}
as

(11)

\chi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\left(\prod_{\lambda\in S_{\infty}}\left(\frac{x_{\lambda}}{|x_{\lambda}|}\right)^{n_{\lambda}}|x_{\lambda}|_{\lambda}^{i\varphi_{\lambda}}\right)\,|x_{\infty}|_{\infty}^{\sigma}.

where
n_{v}\in\{0,1\}
,
n_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R},
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

3.
Größencharaktere
mod
\mathfrak{m}

3.1.
Definition

We follow the treatment in Neukirch
[
10
, Section VII.6]
, except that for us these characters can take values in
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, i.e., they need not be unitary. Before getting started, some further notation that will be useful:

U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}):=\{u\in U_{F}\ |\ u\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}\}
are the units congruent to
1
mod
\mathfrak{m}
;

U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})^{+}:=\{u\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\ |\ u\gg 0\};

\mathcal{O}_{F}(\mathfrak{m}):=\{a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}\ |\ (a,\mathfrak{m})=1\};
nonzero integers in
F
relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m};

F(\mathfrak{m}):=\{x\in F\ |\ (x,\mathfrak{m})=1\};
nonzero elements of
F
relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m};

F^{1}(\mathfrak{m}):=\{x\in F\ |\ x\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}\};
elements of
F
congruent to
1
mod
\mathfrak{m}
.

By a
Größencharakter
mod
\mathfrak{m}

we mean a homomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
for which there exists a pair of characters
(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
with

\psi_{f}:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \psi_{\infty}:F_{\infty}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},

such that for all
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
, we have

(12)

\psi((a))\ =\ \psi_{f}(a\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m})\,\psi_{\infty}(a),

where in the left hand side
(a):=a\mathcal{O}_{F}
is the principal ideal generated by
a
.
We call
\psi_{f}
the finite part and
\psi_{\infty}
the infinite part of
\psi
; they satisfy the compatibility condition:
if
u\in U_{F}=\mathcal{O}_{F}^{\times}
is a unit then
\psi((u))=1
and so we require:

\psi_{f}(u\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m})\,\psi_{\infty}(u)=1.

Further, if
\epsilon\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}):=\{u\in U_{F}\ |\ u\equiv 1\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m}\}
then the above requirement implies that

\psi_{\infty}(\epsilon)=1.

The following proposition explains the relation between
\psi
and
(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
.

Proposition 13
.

A Größencharakter
\psi
mod
\mathfrak{m}
uniquely determines
\psi_{f}
and
\psi_{\infty}
; indeed, the restriction of
\psi
to the group
\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
uniquely determines
\psi_{f}
and
\psi_{\infty},
and these satisfy the above compatibility conditions.

Conversely, given homomorphisms
\psi_{f}^{\prime}:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and
\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}:F_{\infty}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}

satisfying the compatibility condition
\psi_{f}^{\prime}(u\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m})\,\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}(u)=1
for all
u\in U_{F}

(and so necessarily
\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}(\epsilon)=1
for all
\epsilon\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
),
there exists a Größencharakter
\psi
mod
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\psi_{f}=\psi_{f}^{\prime}
and
\psi_{\infty}=\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}.

Finally, if

\xi
is another Größencharakter mod
\mathfrak{m}
such that
\xi_{f}=\psi_{f}
and
\xi_{\infty}=\psi_{\infty}
, then

\psi\xi^{-1}
is a character of
\mathcal{C}l_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
–the ray class group mod
\mathfrak{m}.

Proof.

Using
\lx@xy@svg{\hbox{\raise 2.5pt\hbox{\kern 17.20837pt\hbox{\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\hbox{\vtop{\halign{\entry@#!@&&\entry@@#!@\cr&\crcr}}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern-17.20837pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces{}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}\ignorespaces{\hbox{\kern 41.20837pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern-3.0pt\lower 0.0pt\hbox{\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}\lx@xy@tip{1}\lx@xy@tip{-1}}}}}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\lx@xy@droprule}}{\hbox{\kern 41.20837pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 0.0pt\raise 0.0pt\hbox{\hbox{\kern 3.0pt\raise-2.5pt\hbox{$\textstyle{\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}$}}}}}}}\ignorespaces}}}}\ignorespaces\subset\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
we see that a
Größencharakter

\psi
mod
\mathfrak{m}

determines via restriction a character of
F^{1}(\mathfrak{m}).
By weak approximation,
F^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
is dense in
F_{\infty}^{\times}
; thus
\psi
restricted to

\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
uniquely determines
\psi_{\infty}.

Clearly,
\psi
and
\psi_{\infty}
uniquely determine
\psi_{f}
by
(
12
). For the converse, given
\psi_{f}^{\prime}
and
\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}
, define
\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{O}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
by

\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}((a))\ :=\ \psi_{f}^{\prime}(a\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m})\,\psi_{\infty}^{\prime}(a).

The compatibility conditions say that
\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}(a)
is well-defined. This
\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}
extends uniquely to
F(\mathfrak{m})
, and being trivial on
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}),
we get
a character
\psi^{\prime}:\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
as
\psi^{\prime}((a))=\tilde{\psi}^{\prime}(a).
Now induce
\psi^{\prime}
to
\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
, i.e., consider the induced representation

{\rm Ind}_{\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}^{\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}(\psi^{\prime}).

An easy exercise with Mackey theory says that this representation is a multiplicity free direct sum of characters
\psi
of

\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
such that
\psi|_{\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}=\psi^{\prime}
. Moreover, fix such a
\psi
, then

{\rm Ind}_{\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}^{\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}(\psi^{\prime})\ =\ \psi\otimes{\rm Ind}_{\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}^{\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}(1\!\!1)\ =\ \bigoplus_{\chi}\psi\otimes\chi

with
\chi
running over the characters of
\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})/\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})=\mathcal{C}l_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
the ray class group modulo
\mathfrak{m}
. Hence, if
\xi
is a

Größencharakter
with same finite and infinite parts as
\psi
then
\xi=\psi\otimes\chi.

∎

Following the German language, the plural of
Größencharakter
will be
Größencharaktere
.

3.2.
The correspondence between Hecke characters and
Größencharaktere

The domain of definition of a
Größencharakter
surjects onto the domain of definition of a Hecke character as in the
following exact sequence:

Proposition 14
.

We have an exact sequence

1\ \ \longrightarrow\ \frac{F(\mathfrak{m})}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varkappa}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \ \mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\times\frac{F_{\infty}^{\times}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\ \ \stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varrho}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ \ \frac{\mathbb{I}_{F}}{F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})}\ \ \longrightarrow\ \ 1,

where

(1)

\varkappa(a)=((a)^{-1},\ a\ {\rm mod}\ \mathfrak{m},\ a\ {\rm mod}\ U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}))
for all
a\in F(\mathfrak{m})
;

(2)

\varrho=\alpha\otimes\beta\otimes\gamma^{-1}
where

(a)

\alpha:\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}))
is induced by the map
\mathbb{J}_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}_{F}

that sends a prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}
to
the idèle that has
\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}}
at the place corresponding to
\mathfrak{q}
and
1
’s elsewhere;

(b)

\beta:(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}))
is induced by the map that sends
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
to the idèle
that has
a
at all infinite places and also at all finite places
\mathfrak{p}
not dividing
\mathfrak{m}
, and is
1
at all
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}
; and

(c)

\gamma:F_{\infty}^{\times}/U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\rightarrow\mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}))
is induced by the inclusion

F_{\infty}^{\times}\subset\mathbb{I}_{F}
; check that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
is mapped into
F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}).

See Neukirch
[
10
, Prop. VII.6.13]
for a proof but it is an instructive exercise to fix a proof for oneself. Let us denote an
a\in\mathbb{I}_{F}
as
(a_{\infty};\dots a_{\mathfrak{p}}\dots;\dots a_{\mathfrak{q}}\dots)
for
\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}
and
\mathfrak{q}\!\not|\,\mathfrak{m}
, and
[a]
the class of the idèle
a
in

\mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})).
Note that:

(1)

\alpha(\mathfrak{q})=[(\dots 1\dots;\ \dots 1\dots;\ 1,\dots 1,\varpi_{\mathfrak{q}},1,\dots)]
for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{q}\!\not|\,\mathfrak{m}
.

(2)

\beta(a)=[((\dots 1\dots;\ a^{-1},\dots,a^{-1};\ \dots 1\dots)].

(3)

\gamma(x_{\infty})=[(x_{\infty};\ \dots 1\dots;\ \dots 1\dots)].

The rest of the details are left to the reader.

A Hecke character
\chi
trivial on
\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})
will be called a Hecke character mod
\mathfrak{m}
; necessarily the conductor of
\chi
divides

\mathfrak{m}.
From the exact sequence, the dictionary between
Größencharaktere
mod
\mathfrak{m}
and Hecke characters mod
\mathfrak{m}

is clear: Given a
Größencharakter

\psi
mod
\mathfrak{m}
, with
\psi_{f}
and
\psi_{\infty}
its finite and infinite parts, we get a character

\psi\otimes\psi_{f}\otimes\psi_{\infty}
on
\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\times(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}\times(F_{\infty}^{\times}/U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}))
and
(
12
) says that this is trivial on the image of
\varkappa
and hence we get a character
\chi=\chi_{\psi}

on
\mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\cdot\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})),
i.e.,
\chi
is a Hecke character mod
\mathfrak{m}
. Conversely, given such a
\chi
, we inflate
it via
\varrho
to get
\psi\otimes\psi_{f}\otimes\psi_{\infty}=\chi\circ\varrho.

3.3.
The infinity type of a
Größencharakter

Given a
Größencharakter

\psi=(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
mod
\mathfrak{m}
, by using the above dictionary and (
11
), one sees that
the character at infinity, which is a continuous character
\psi_{\infty}:F_{\infty}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},
is of the form:

\psi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\left(\prod_{\lambda\in S_{\infty}}\left(\frac{x_{\lambda}}{|x_{\lambda}|}\right)^{n_{\lambda}}|x_{\lambda}|_{\lambda}^{i\varphi_{\lambda}}\right)\,|x_{\infty}|_{\infty}^{\sigma},

where
n_{v}\in\{0,1\}
,
n_{w}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\varphi_{\lambda}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
; furthermore,
\psi_{\infty}
being trivial on
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
imposes
certain restrictions on the infinity type, i.e., the collection
((n_{\lambda},\varphi_{\lambda})_{\lambda},\sigma)
of exponents, that can occur.

Lemma 15
.

Let
\psi
be a Größencharakter mod
\mathfrak{m}
with the above notations for
\psi_{\infty}
. Then:

(i)

there are no restrictions on
n_{\lambda}
for any
\lambda\in S_{\infty}
, and on
\sigma
; however,

(ii)

2\varphi_{v}=\varphi_{w}=\varphi
(say), for all
v\in S_{r}
and all
w\in S_{c}
.

Hence, the character at infinity takes the shape:

\psi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\left(\prod_{\lambda\in S_{\infty}}\left(\frac{x_{\lambda}}{|x_{\lambda}|}\right)^{n_{\lambda}}\right)\,|x_{\infty}|_{\infty}^{\sigma+i\varphi}.

We adumbrate how one proves this lemma. Write the character at infinity as:

\psi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\ =\ \prod_{v\in S_{r}}{\rm sgn}(x_{v})^{n_{v}}|x_{v}|^{\sigma+i\varphi_{v}}\cdot\prod_{w\in S_{c}}x_{w}^{\sigma+n_{w}/2+i\varphi_{w}/2}\,\bar{x}_{w}^{\,\sigma-n_{w}/2+i\varphi_{w}/2}

For brevity, denote

f_{v}:=\sigma+i\varphi_{v},\ f_{w}:=\sigma+n_{w}/2+i\varphi_{w}/2,\ f_{\bar{w}}:=\sigma-n_{w}/2+i\varphi_{w}/2.

As
\psi_{\infty}
is trivial on any
u\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})^{+}
(totally positive global units that are
1
mod
\mathfrak{m}
) we get

\prod_{v\in S_{r}}v(u)^{f_{v}}\prod_{w\in S_{c}}w(u)^{f_{w}}\bar{w}(u)^{f_{\bar{w}}}=1.

We will go through the rest of the proof, which uses the ingredients of the proof in Dirichlet’s unit theorem, in detail in the proof of Lem.
17
below
(the main focus of this article is the notion of an algebraic Hecke character to which that lemma particularly applies) giving us the constraint:

2f_{v}=f_{w}
, from which the above lemma follows.

4.
Algebraic Hecke characters

4.1.
Definition and purity

There is a canonical map
\Sigma_{F}\rightarrow S_{\infty}.
Let
\lambda\in S_{\infty}
.
If
\lambda=v\in S_{r}
, i.e.,
\lambda
is a real place
v
then it corresponds to a unique
real embedding
\tau_{v}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, and if
\lambda=w\in S_{c}
, i.e.,
\lambda
is a complex place
w
then it corresponds to a conjugate pair of complex embeddings

\{\tau_{w},\bar{\tau}_{w}\}
with the understanding that the choice of
\tau_{w}:F\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is not canonical; we use
\tau_{w}
to identify
F_{w}\simeq\mathbb{C}.

Recall, as a reminder of our notational artifice, that

F_{\infty}=F\otimes\mathbb{R}\simeq\prod_{\lambda\in S_{\infty}}F_{\lambda}\simeq\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\mathbb{R}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\mathbb{C}.
One may write

x_{\infty}\in F_{\infty}
simply as
x_{\infty}=(x_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in S_{\infty}},
or more elaborately as

x_{\infty}=((x_{v})_{v\in S_{r}},(z_{w})_{w\in S_{c}})
; both notations have their intrinsic virtue.
Let
\chi
be Hecke character of
F
, and
\chi_{\infty}
its character at infinity; see (
11
). We say that
\chi
is an

algebraic Hecke character
if for every
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
there exists an integer
n_{\tau}
such that for
x_{\infty}\in F_{\infty}^{\times}
we have:

(16)

\chi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\ =\ \prod_{v\in S_{r}}x_{v}^{n_{\tau_{v}}}\prod_{w\in S_{c}}z_{w}^{n_{\tau_{w}}}\bar{z}_{w}^{\,n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}}.

By separating, the right hand side into unitary and non-unitary parts, while noting that
z^{p}\bar{z}^{q}=\left(\tfrac{z}{|z|}\right)^{p-q}|z|_{\mathbb{C}}^{(p+q)/2}
,
we have:

\chi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\ =\ \left(\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\left(\frac{x_{v}}{|x_{v}|}\right)^{n_{\tau_{v}}}\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\left(\frac{z_{w}}{|z_{w}|}\right)^{n_{\tau_{w}}-n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}}\right)\left(\prod_{v\in S_{r}}|x_{v}|_{v}^{n_{\tau_{v}}}\prod_{w\in S_{c}}|z_{w}|_{w}^{(n_{\tau_{w}}+n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}})/2}\right).

If we compare this with (
11
), we see
v\in S_{r},

w\in S_{c},
and
\lambda\in S_{\infty}
that:

n_{v}\equiv n_{\tau_{v}}\ {\rm mod}\ 2,\ \ n_{w}=n_{\tau_{w}}-n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}},\ \ \varphi_{\lambda}=0,\ \ 2\sigma=2n_{\tau_{v}}=n_{\tau_{w}}+n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}

Especially note the constraint:
2n_{\tau_{v}}=n_{\tau_{w}}+n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}
for all
v\in S_{r},

w\in S_{c}.
Now, let us suppose the
algebraic Hecke character has modulus
\mathfrak{m}.
Then this constraint gets significantly tighter by the following:

Lemma 17

(Purity)
.

For each
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
, suppose we are given
n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Suppose for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
F
we have

(18)

\prod_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}}\tau(u)^{n_{\tau}}\ =\ 1,\quad\forall u\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}).

Then, there exists
{\sf w}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(i)

if
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
then
n_{\tau}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\tau_{F},
and

(ii)

if
S_{r}=\emptyset
then
n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).

Proof.

The proof uses the ingredients of the proof in Dirichlet’s unit theorem. Note that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
has finite index in
U_{F}
.
Enumerate the real embeddings of
F
as
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r_{1}}\}
and the complex embeddings as

\{w_{1},\bar{w}_{1},\dots,w_{r_{2}},\bar{w}_{r_{2}}\}.

With a minor abuse of notation, for brevity, write
\tau_{v_{i}}=v_{i}
, and
\tau_{w_{j}}=w_{j}.

Let
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
be the hyperplane given by the sum of all coordinates being
0.

The Minkowski map
\mathfrak{l}:U_{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is given by:

\mathfrak{l}(u)\ =\ ({\rm log}|v_{1}(u)|_{\mathbb{R}},\,\dots,\,{\rm log}|v_{r_{1}}(u)|_{\mathbb{R}},\,{\rm log}|w_{1}(u)|_{\mathbb{C}},\dots,\,{\rm log}|w_{r_{2}}(u)|_{\mathbb{C}}).

In (
18
), apply
{\rm log}|\ |_{\mathbb{C}}
to get:

(19)

2n_{v_{1}}{\rm log}|v_{1}(u)|_{\mathbb{R}}+\cdots+2n_{v_{r_{1}}}{\rm log}|v_{r_{1}}(u)|_{\mathbb{R}}+\\
(n_{w_{1}}+n_{\bar{w}_{1}}){\rm log}|w_{1}(u)|_{\mathbb{C}}+\cdots+(n_{w_{r_{2}}}+n_{\bar{w}_{r_{2}}}){\rm log}|w_{r_{2}}(u)|_{\mathbb{C}}\ =\ 0,

for all
u\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})^{+}.

The proof of Dirichlet’s unit theorem gives that
\Gamma=\mathfrak{l}(U_{F})
is a lattice in
\mathcal{H}
; hence
\Gamma_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}):=\mathfrak{l}(U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}))
is also a lattice
in
\mathcal{H}
; whence, there exists
u_{1},\dots,u_{t-1}\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
such that

\{v_{1}:=\mathfrak{l}(u_{1}),\dots,v_{t-1}:=\mathfrak{l}(u_{t-1})\}
is an
\mathbb{R}
-basis for
\mathcal{H},
where, for brevity, we denote
t=r_{1}+r_{2}.

Let us write
v_{i}=(a_{i1},\dots,a_{it})
as a vector
in
\mathbb{R}^{t},
and consider the
(t-1)\times t
sized matrix
A=[a_{ij}].
The rank of
A
is
t-1
and hence its nullity is
1.
Since each
v_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
we know

\sum_{j}a_{ij}=0
, we deduce that for any
X\in\mathbb{R}^{t\times 1}
, if
AX=0
then all the coordinates of
X
are equal. Now
(
19
) applied to
u_{1},\dots,u_{t-1}
gives a solution to the system of equations
AX=0
from which we get

2n_{v_{1}}=\cdots=2n_{v_{1}}=n_{w_{1}}+n_{\bar{w}_{1}}=\cdots=n_{w_{r_{2}}}+n_{\bar{w}_{r_{2}}}.

(The reader may pause here to note that the above argument proves Lem.
15
.)
Let us denote the common value by
{\sf w}^{\prime}.
Now, take any
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, apply
\gamma^{-1}
to (
18
) to get

1\ =\ \prod_{\tau\in\tau_{F}}\gamma^{-1}(\tau(u))^{n_{\tau}}\ =\ \prod_{\tau\in\tau_{F}}\tau(u)^{n_{\gamma\circ\tau}},

and we go through the name argument to get
n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\overline{\tau}}={\sf w}^{\prime}
for all
\tau\in\tau_{F}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).
If
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
, then since
{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
acts transitively on
\Sigma_{F}
we see that
n_{\tau}=n_{v_{1}}
for all
\tau
; then
take
{\sf w}=n_{v_{1}}={\sf w}^{\prime}/2
. If
S_{r}=\emptyset
then take
{\sf w}={\sf w}^{\prime}.

∎

Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character of
F
mod
\mathfrak{m}
and with infinity type
(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}}.
Then the exponents
n_{\tau}
satisfy the
conditions of Lem.
17
. The integer
{\sf w}
is called the
purity weight
of
\chi
. It is worth noting that when
F
is totally real or totally imaginary then there is no constraint on the parity of the purity weight
{\sf w}
, but when
F
has both real and complex embeddings then the purity weight
{\sf w}
is necessarily an even integer. Note the following easy consequence of purity:

Corollary 20
.

Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character of
F
mod
\mathfrak{m}
and with infinity type
(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}}
and purity weight
{\sf w}.

(i)

If
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
then
\chi=\chi^{\circ}|\!|\ |\!|^{\sf w}
for a Dirichlet character
\chi^{\circ}
of
F
mod
\mathfrak{m}
.

(ii)

If
S_{r}=\emptyset
then
\chi=\chi^{u}|\!|\ |\!|^{{\sf w}/2}
for a unitary Hecke character
\chi^{u}
of
F
mod
\mathfrak{m}
.

In case (ii), let us observe that the character at infinity of
\chi^{u}
has the following shape:

\chi^{u}_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\ =\ \prod_{w\in S_{c}=S_{\infty}}\left(x_{w}/\bar{x}_{w}\right)^{n_{w}-{\sf w}/2}.

4.2.
The
L
-function of an algebraic Hecke character

4.2.1.
Definition(s) of Hecke
L
-function

Hecke considered the class of
Größencharaktere
mod integral ideals as the optimal class of characters for which one may study the analytic properties of
L
-functions, called Hecke
L
-functions, that we now define.
Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character, and
\psi=(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
the associated
Großencharakter
. Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}
is the conductor of

\chi
, and so also
\mathfrak{m}
is the conductor of
\psi
. The (finite part of the) Hecke
L
-function of
\chi
may be defined in two possible ways:

(1)

As defined by Hecke in the form of a Dirichlet series:

L_{f}(s,\chi)\ =\ \sum_{\mathfrak{a}}\frac{\psi(\mathfrak{a})}{N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a})^{s}},

the summation running over integral ideals
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
that are relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}
.

(2)

As defined by Tate in the form of an Euler product:

L_{f}(s,\chi)\ =\ \prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\,\left(1-\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})^{-s}\right)^{-1},

the product is over prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{F}
that are relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}.

Here,
N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})
is defined as
|\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{p}|,
and is multiplicatively extended to define
N_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{a}).

One has the usual analytic properties: absolute convergence in a half-plane, analytic continuation, and functional equation. In the form of Hecke’s definition, we refer the reader to Neukirch
[
10
]
; in the form of Tate’s definition, we refer the reader to Tate’s thesis
[
15
]
and Weil’s book
[
18
]
.

4.2.2.
The coefficients of the
L
-function of an algebraic Hecke character

The main reason to consider algebraic Hecke characters (called characters of type
A_{0}
by Weil
[
17
]
) is the following proposition which
puts us in the right context to study the arithmetic properties of Hecke
L
-functions.

Proposition 21
.

Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character, and
\psi=(\psi_{f},\psi_{\infty})
the associated Großencharakter of modulus, say,
\mathfrak{m}.

Then the coefficients of the Dirichlet series giving the finite part of the Hecke

L
-function
L_{f}(s,\chi)
are contained in a number field, i.e., there is a finite extension
E
of
\mathbb{Q}
such that
\psi(\mathfrak{a})\in E
(resp.,
\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})\in E
) for all integral ideals
\mathfrak{a}
(resp., prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
) relatively prime to
\mathfrak{m}.

Proof.

By (
12
), it follows that for
a\in\mathcal{O}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
, and hence also for
a\in F(\mathfrak{m})
,

\psi((a))
takes values in a finite extension
E_{1}
of
\mathbb{Q}
inside
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, since
(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
is a finite group,
and since
\psi_{\infty}
is algebraic (
16
) we see that

\psi_{\infty}(a)
takes values in the compositum of all the conjugates of
F
inside
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}.
Hence the values of
\psi
on
\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
lie in
E_{1}
. Since,

\mathbb{P}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
has finite index in
\mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
we see that the values of
\psi
are in a finite extension
E
of
E_{1}
contained inside
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}.

By Prop.
14
,
\psi(\mathfrak{p})=\chi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\varpi_{\mathfrak{p}})
for all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}\!\not|\,\mathfrak{m}
.
∎

4.2.3.
The rationality field of an algebraic Hecke character

The smallest subfield of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
that contains all the ‘values’ of an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
, denoted
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
,
is called its
rationality field
. More precisely,
with notations as in the above proposition, suppose
\chi
has conductor
\mathfrak{m}
, we may define:

(22)

\mathbb{Q}(\chi)\ =\ \mathbb{Q}(\{\chi(\mathfrak{p}):\mbox{for all prime ideals $\mathfrak{p}$ relatively prime to $\mathfrak{m}$}\})\\
=\mathbb{Q}(\{\psi(\mathfrak{a}):\mbox{for all integral ideals $\mathfrak{a}$ relatively prime to $\mathfrak{m}$}\}).

As Weil comments in
[
17
]
,
\mathbb{Q}(\chi)
need not contain
F
; consider, for example, a quadratic character of any number field
F
.

4.2.4.
The critical values of the
L
-function of an algebraic Hecke character

Let us recall the shape of the functional equation satisfied by
L_{f}(s,\chi),
in as much as we recall the precise recipe for the
\Gamma
-factors

L_{\infty}(s,\chi)
necessary to define the completed
L
-function:

L(s,\chi)\ :=\ L_{\infty}(s,\chi)L_{f}(s,\chi),

where, if
\chi
has infinity type
(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}}
,
n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfying the purity condition of Lem.
17
, then we define:

L_{\infty}(s,\chi)\ =\ \prod_{v\in S_{r}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s+n_{v}+\epsilon_{v})\cdot\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\left(s+\frac{n_{w}+n_{\bar{w}}}{2}+\frac{|n_{w}-n_{\bar{w}}|}{2}\right),

where
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2),

\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
, and
\epsilon_{v}\in\{0,1\}
with

\epsilon_{v}\equiv n_{v}
mod
2
. When
\chi
is nontrivial, the completed
L
-function admits an analytic continuation to an entire function
of
s\in\mathbb{C}
, and a beautiful aspect of
L
-functions is the functional equation:

L(s,\chi)\ =\ \varepsilon(s,\chi)L(1-s,\chi^{-1}),

where
\varepsilon(s,\chi)
is an exponential function. (See
[
10
]
or
[
18
]
.)
An integer
m
is said to be
critical
for the Hecke
L
-function
L(s,\chi)
if
both
L_{\infty}(s,\chi)
and
L_{\infty}(1-s,\chi^{-1})
are regular (i.e., finite) at
s=m.

We may say that the
\Gamma
-factors on either side of the functional equation do not have poles at
s=m
.
The set of all critical integers for
L(s,\chi)
, denoted
{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))
, is given by the following

Proposition 23
.

Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character of a number field
F
with infinity type
(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}}
,
n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z}
, satisfying the purity condition of Lem.
17
with purity weight
{\sf w}
. Then the critical set of integers for
L(s,\chi)
is given by:

(i)

F
is totally real (
S_{c}=\emptyset
): Recall that
n_{\tau}={\sf w}
for all
\tau
.

(a)

If there exists
v_{1},v_{2}\in S_{r}
, such that
\epsilon_{v_{1}}\neq\epsilon_{v_{2}}
then
{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))=\emptyset.

(b)

If
\epsilon_{v}=0
for all
v\in S_{r}
then
{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))
is given by:

\{\dots,\,-{\sf w}+1-2k,\,\dots,\,-{\sf w}-3,\,-{\sf w}-1;\,-{\sf w}+2,\,-{\sf w}+4,\,\dots,\,-{\sf w}+2k,\,\dots\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}.

The critical set is centered at
\tfrac{1}{2}-{\sf w}
.

(c)

If
\epsilon_{v}=1
for all
v\in S_{r}
then
{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))
is given by:

\{\dots,-{\sf w}-2k,\dots,-{\sf w}-2,-{\sf w};\,-{\sf w}+1,-{\sf w}+3,\dots,-{\sf w}+1+2k,\dots\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}}.

The critical set is centered at
\tfrac{1}{2}-{\sf w}
.

(ii)

F
is totally imaginary (
S_{r}=\emptyset
): Define the width of
\chi
as the non-negative integer:

\ell=\ell(\chi)=\min\{|n_{w}-n_{\bar{w}}|\,:\,w\in S_{c}\}.
Then
{\sf w}\equiv\ell\ {\rm mod}\ 2.

We have:

{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))\ =\ \left\{m\in\mathbb{Z}\ :\ 1-\frac{\sf w}{2}-\frac{\ell}{2}\ \leq\ m\ \leq\ -\frac{\sf w}{2}+\frac{\ell}{2}\right\}.

The critical set, centered at
\tfrac{1-{\sf w}}{2}
, is a finite set of cardinality
\ell
.

(iii)

If
F
has real and complex places (
S_{r}\neq\emptyset\neq S_{c}
), then

{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))=\emptyset.

We omit the tedious proof of the above proposition and just add a few comments after which it is an extended exercise that is left to the reader.
Recall that the poles of
\Gamma(s)
are simple and located at non-positive integers, and furthermore that
\Gamma(s)\neq 0
for all
s
. In case (i)(a), one sees the presence of a product

\Gamma(s/2)\Gamma((1+s)/2)
because of which no integer can be critical.
In case (i)(b), up to shifting by
-{\sf w},

it is the same as
{\rm Crit}(\zeta(s))
, the critical set of the Riemann zeta function. In case (i)(c), up to shifting by
-{\sf w},
it is the same as the critical set of an odd classical Dirichlet character. For case (ii), the reader is referred to
[
12
, Sect. 3.1]
where the details are explicated.
Case (iii), is like the intersection of cases (i) and (ii); by Lem.
17
,
n_{\tau}={\sf w}
for all
\tau
;
\ell=0
where
\ell
is defined using only
S_{c}
, forcing no critical points if there are real and complex places.

There has been a huge amount of work on the nature of the critical values of
L(m,\chi)
. Deligne’s conjecture
[
4
]

on the special values of motivic
L
-functions sets the stage for the sort of theorem one seeks. The interested reader is referred to
Blasius
[
1
]
, Harder
[
6
]
, and Schappacher
[
13
]
, and all the references therein. My reason for
writing this article is that while I was working on the critical values of automorphic
L
-functions (
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
),
I had to work through the results of this article frequently; and these results, although well-known to experts, did not seem to be readily available in the literature in the way I needed them. There are some very deep sign problems, apparently hitherto not well-appreciated, that appear in these works which have their roots – to paraphrase Weil
[
17
]
– in the remarkable properties of such algebraic Hecke characters.

The above proposition begets the terminology of a
critical algebraic Hecke character
by which we mean an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
such that

{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))\neq\emptyset.
Existence of a critical algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of a number field
F
implies first of all that
F
is totally real or
totally imaginary, and furthermore, when
F
is totally real then the parities
\epsilon_{v}
of all the local archimedean characters are equal.
We will now discuss
the existence of critical algebraic Hecke characters with prescribed infinity type subject to the purity condition.

4.3.
Existence of (critical) algebraic Hecke characters

For a given number field
F
we discuss the problem of existence of algebraic Hecke characters that may or may not be critical. The purity lemma
naturally leads us to consider two cases (i)
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
and (ii)
S_{r}=\emptyset
; we will see that case (ii) when
F
is totally imaginary is
in some sense far more interesting.

4.3.1.
When
F
has a real place (
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
)

From Cor.
20
, (i), we know that an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
is necessarily of the form

\chi\ =\ \chi^{\circ}|\!|\ |\!|^{\sf w},

for a character
\chi^{\circ}
of finite order and for an integer
{\sf w}.
We may fine-tune our question a little further to
note that we can arrange for
\chi^{\circ}
to have any prescribed signature at infinite places: for any (possibly empty)
S\subset S_{r}
, by weak approximation, we can find
x\in F^{\times}
such that
v(x)>0
for
v\in S
, and
v(x)<0
for
v\in S_{r}\setminus S.
Take
E=F(\sqrt{x})
, and

\chi=\omega_{E/F}
the quadratic character of
F
corresponding to
E
by class field theory. Then
\chi_{v}(-1)=1
for
v\in S
and

\chi_{v}(-1)=-1
for
v\in S_{r}\setminus S.

In particular, when
F
is a totally real field, by taking
S=\emptyset
or
S=S_{r}=S_{\infty}
, we
see that there exists critical algebraic Hecke characters of any prescribed parity (totally even or totally odd)
and any prescribed purity weight
{\sf w}.

4.3.2.
When
F
is totally imaginary (
S_{r}=\emptyset
)

Consider an infinity type:
{\bf n}:=(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}},

n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z},
satisfying the purity condition

n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
and all
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
The question we wish to address is whether there exists an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of
F
with infinity type
{\bf n}.
As
Weil says in
[
17
]
, Artin pointed out to him that this is essentially an exercise in Galois theory.

First, consider the case when
F
is a CM field, by which one means that
F
is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field
F^{+}.

Let us note an easy

Lemma 24
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field
F^{+}
.
Let
\tau:F\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
and let
\mathfrak{c}
stand for complex conjugation as an element of
{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
.
Then

\gamma\circ\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau\ =\ \mathfrak{c}\circ\gamma\circ\tau,

i.e., complex conjugation and any automorphism of
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
commute on the image of a CM field. This also holds for a totally real field.

Proof.

Let
\tau_{1}=\gamma\circ\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau
and
\tau_{2}=\mathfrak{c}\circ\gamma\circ\tau.
Then it is easy to that
\tau_{1}|_{F^{+}}=\tau_{2}|_{F^{+}}
(since
F^{+}
is totally real). This means that
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}
or
\tau_{1}=\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau_{2}
; if the latter, then
\gamma\circ\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau=\gamma\circ\tau
, whence

\mathfrak{c}\circ\tau=\tau
or that
\tau
lands inside
\mathbb{R}
which is not possible. Hence
\tau_{1}=\tau_{2}.
The last assertion for a totally real field is obvious.
∎

For a CM field
F
, consider an infinity type
{\bf n}:=(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}},

n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z},
satisfying the purity condition

n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
. The above lemma gives that
n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}=n_{\overline{\gamma\circ\tau}}
. Hence, purity for a CM field is the same as asking
n_{\tau}+n_{\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}.

Proposition 25
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field
F^{+}
. Let
{\bf n}:=(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}},

n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z},
satisfying the purity condition
n_{\tau}+n_{\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}.
Then there exists an algebraic Hecke character (of some modulus) with that infinity type.

Proof.

If such a character exists, then the character at infinity would have the shape:

\chi_{\infty}(x_{\infty})\ =\ \left(\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\left(\frac{x_{w}}{\bar{x}_{w}}\right)^{\tfrac{n_{\tau_{w}}-n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}}{2}}\right)|x_{\infty}|_{\infty}^{{\sf w}/2},

and also for some integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
,
\chi_{\infty}
needs to be trivial on
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}).

Put
p_{w}=n_{\tau_{w}}-n_{\bar{\tau}_{w}}
, and write
x_{w}
in polar form as
r_{w}e^{i\theta_{w}}.
Then we need to construct a unitary
Hecke character
\chi^{u}:\mathbb{I}_{F}/F^{\times}\rightarrow S^{1}
, such that
\chi^{u}_{\infty}(x_{\infty})=\prod_{w}e^{ip_{w}\theta_{w}},
and
\chi^{u}_{\infty}

needs to be trivial on
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}).
We can then take
\chi=\chi_{u}|\!|\ |\!|^{{\sf w}/2}.
Note that
F_{\infty}^{\times}=(S^{1}\times\cdots\times S^{1})\times(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}).

The units
U_{F}
(via the proof of Dirichlet’s unit theorem) intersects with the compact part in roots of unity
\mu_{F}
in
F^{\times}
. Take an integral ideal

\mathfrak{m}
such that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\cap\mu_{F}=\{1\}
; hence,

U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\cap(S^{1}\times\cdots\times S^{1})=\{1\}
. Define a unitary character
\psi^{u}_{\infty}
that maps

x_{\infty}
to
\prod_{w}e^{ip_{w}\theta_{w}}.
Then
\psi^{u}_{\infty}
defines a character of
F_{\infty}^{\times}/U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}).
Take any character

\psi^{u}_{f}
of
(\mathcal{O}_{F}/\mathfrak{m})^{\times}
, and by Prop.
13
we get a
Größencharakter

\psi^{u}
mod
\mathfrak{m}
with the necessary infinity type.
Take
\chi^{u}
to be the corresponding Hecke character as in the paragraph after Prop.
14
.
∎

Next, consider the case of a general totally imaginary field
F
. There is a maximal CM or totally real subfield
F_{1}
of
F
that plays an important role.
Following Weil
[
17
]
, let
F_{0}
be the largest totally real subfield of
F
. Then
F_{0}
admits at most one totally imaginary quadratic extension contained inside
F_{0}
. If
F
does admit such a CM subfield, denote it as
F_{1}
; and if there is no such extension inside
F
, then put
F_{1}=F_{0}.

A basic property of a pure infinity type of a totally imaginary field is that it is the base-change of a pure infinity type of
F_{1}
as explained by the following

Proposition 26
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary field. Consider an infinity type
{\bf n}:=(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}},

n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z},
satisfying the purity condition

n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
and all
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).
Then
n_{\tau}
, as a function of

\tau
depends only on the restriction of
\tau
to
F_{1},
i.e., there exists a necessarily pure infinity type
{\bf m}:=(m_{\tau_{1}})_{\tau_{1}\in\Sigma_{F_{1}}}

for the field
F_{1}
, such that if
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
with
\tau_{1}=\tau|_{F_{1}}
then
n_{\tau}=m_{\tau_{1}}.
(In this situation, we may say
that
{\bf n}
is the base-change of
{\bf m}
.)

Proof.

(I learnt of the following proof from Deligne; it appears in a different context in
[
5
]
.) The purity condition
n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w},
for all

\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
and all
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}),
also implies that

n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\overline{\gamma\circ\tau}}={\sf w}
, from which deduce

n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}\ =\ n_{\overline{\gamma\circ\tau}},\quad\forall\tau,\ \forall\gamma.

Consider the function
n:{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\times\Sigma_{F}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, defined as
n(\gamma,\tau)=n_{\gamma\circ\tau}.
For any
x\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
we have

n(\gamma x,\tau)=n(\gamma,x\tau).
The above displayed equation reads

n(\gamma,\mathfrak{c}\tau)\ =\ n(\mathfrak{c},\gamma\tau).

Replacing
\tau
by
\gamma^{-1}\tau
, we also have:

n(\mathfrak{c},\tau)\ =\ n(\gamma,\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1}\tau)\ =\ n(\gamma\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1},\tau).

Replacing
\tau
by
\mathfrak{c}\tau
we get

n(1,\tau)\ =\ n(\gamma\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1}\mathfrak{c},\tau).

Now, take any
x\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, and we see using the above relations (for all
\gamma
and
\tau
) that:

n(x\gamma\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1}\mathfrak{c}x^{-1},\tau)\ =\ n(x\gamma\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1}x^{-1}x\mathfrak{c}x^{-1},\tau)\ =\ n((x\gamma)\mathfrak{c}(x\gamma)^{-1},x\mathfrak{c}x^{-1}\tau)\ =\\
n(\mathfrak{c},x\mathfrak{c}x^{-1}\tau)\ =\ n(x\mathfrak{c}x^{-1},\mathfrak{c}\tau)\ =\ n(x\mathfrak{c}x^{-1}\mathfrak{c},\tau)\ =\ n(1,\tau).

In other words,
n_{h\tau}=n_{\tau}
for all
h
in the normal subgroup
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{G}:={\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
generated by
\{\gamma\mathfrak{c}\gamma^{-1}\mathfrak{c}:\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\}
.
Note that
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
is the largest quotient in which
\mathfrak{c}
is central.
We see that
\mathcal{G}
acts transitively on
\Sigma_{F};
the transitive action of
\mathcal{G}
on
\Sigma_{F_{1}}
is via
\mathcal{G}/\mathcal{N}
(Lem.
24
);
and
\mathcal{N}
acts transitively on the fiber over
\tau_{1}\in\Sigma_{F_{1}}
in the restriction map
\Sigma_{F}\rightarrow\Sigma_{F_{1}}.
In particular,
if
\tau,\tau^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{F}
with
\tau_{1}=\tau|_{F_{1}}=\tau^{\prime}|_{F_{1}}
, and suppose
h\in\mathcal{N}
is such that
h\tau=\tau^{\prime}
, then

n_{\tau^{\prime}}=n_{h\tau}=n_{\tau},
and we call this common value as
m_{\tau_{1}}.

∎

We are now ready to state the basic fact about the existence of algebraic Hecke characters for a totally imaginary field in the following

Proposition 27
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary field. Consider an infinity type
{\bf n}:=(n_{\tau})_{\tau\in\Sigma_{F}},

n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z},
satisfying the purity condition

n_{\gamma\circ\tau}+n_{\gamma\circ\bar{\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
and all
\gamma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q}).

Then, there exists an algebraic Hecke character of infinity type
{\bf n}.

(i)

If
F_{0}=F_{1}
(this is the case when
F
has no CM subfield), then there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
n_{\tau}=n
for all
\tau\in\Sigma_{F}
. Any
algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of infinity type
{\bf n}
is of the form
\chi=\chi^{\circ}|\!|\ |\!|^{n}
for a Dirichlet character
\chi^{\circ}
of
F
.

(ii)

If
F_{1}
is a CM field, then
{\bf n}
is the base-change of an infinity type
{\bf m}
for
F_{1}
(Prop.
26
); any
algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of
F
of infinity type
{\bf n}
is of the form

\chi\ =\ \chi_{1}\circ N_{F/F_{1}}\otimes\chi^{\circ},

for some algebraic Hecke character
\chi_{1}
of
F_{1}
with infinity type
{\bf m}
and some Dirichlet character
\chi^{\circ}
of
F
.

Proof.

In both cases from Prop.
26
there exists an infinity type
{\bf m}
over
F_{1}
whose base-change to
F
is
{\bf n}
.
In case (i), since

F_{1}=F_{0}
is totally real, all the
m_{\tau_{1}}
are equal, and hence
n_{\tau}
are all equal, to say,
n
. If there is an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of
this infinity type then necessarily we have
\chi|\!|\ |\!|^{-n}
is a finite-order character, say,
\chi^{\circ}.
To construct
\chi
, take any finite-order Hecke character

\chi^{\circ}
of
F
, and put
\chi=\chi^{\circ}|\!|\ |\!|^{n}
.
In case (ii), from Prop.
25
, there exists an algebraic Hecke character
\chi_{1}
of

with infinity type

Consider

to

Going backwards, construct the required

∎

I have found it helpful to keep some examples in mind while thinking of a totally imaginary field

and

Example 28
.

When

Example 29
.

Take a CM field
F^{\rm cm}
, and a number field
L
that is linearly disjoint from
F^{\rm cm}
, and put
F=L\cdot F^{\rm cm}.

This is a particularly pleasant example of a totally imaginary field. If

is also of CM type.
For concreteness, take

, then

Example 30
.

For a totally imaginary field not of the above form, take
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
, and put
F_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})
and

(This example is used in

.)

Example 31
.

For a totally imaginary field with no CM subfield, take
F_{0}
to be a cubic totally real field; for example,
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{3}+\zeta_{3}^{-1}).

Choose non-squares
a
and

in

, with conjugates

and

Remark 32
.

There is a slightly different way to organise one’s thoughts about the subfields
F_{0}
and
F_{1}
of a totally imaginary field
F
, and which
at times is useful to keep in mind.
Recall that
\mathfrak{c}
denote the complex conjugation on
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
F_{1}
be maximal among all subfields
K
of
F
that have an involution
\iota_{K}:K\rightarrow K
such that
\mathfrak{c}\circ\sigma=\sigma\circ\iota_{K}
for

\sigma:K\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}.
In particular,
F_{1}
has its involution
\iota_{F_{1}},
and let

There are two cases:

5.
Cohomological automorphic representations of
{\rm GL}_{1}

So far, we have treated Hecke characters as complex valued functions on adèlic spaces, and one might say that this is from the point of view of

automorphic forms
on
{\rm GL}(1).
We may also treat them as
algebro-geometric
objects; see, for example, Deligne
[
3
]
or Serre
[
14
]
. Of course, these points of view are inter-related for which the reader is recommended to see Schappacher
[
13
, Chap. 0]
for a lucid summary. In this section we consider Hecke characters from the point of view often taken in the
cohomology of arithmetic groups
.
We begin by setting up the context, adopting the treatment in
[
9
, Chap. 2]
.

5.1.
Locally symmetric spaces for
{\rm GL}_{1}/F

Let
G={\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}({\rm GL}_{1}/F)
. Then:

G(\mathbb{Q})={\rm GL}_{1}(F)=F^{\times};

G(\mathbb{A})={\rm GL}_{1}(\mathbb{A}_{F})=\mathbb{I}_{F};

G(\mathbb{A}_{f})=\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}
the group of finite idèles;

G(\mathbb{R})={\rm GL}_{1}(F\otimes\mathbb{R})=F_{\infty}^{\times}=\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\mathbb{C}^{\times};

G(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}=F_{\infty+}^{\times}
, the connected component of the identity in
G(\mathbb{R});

K_{\infty}=\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\{\pm 1\}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}S^{1},
the maximal compact subgroup of
G(\mathbb{R});

K_{\infty}^{\circ}\simeq\prod_{w\in S_{c}}S^{1}
, the connected component of the identity in
K_{\infty};

A={\rm GL}_{1}/\mathbb{Q}\hookrightarrow G/\mathbb{Q}
is the canonical inclusion in
{\rm Hom}({\rm GL}_{1}/\mathbb{Q},{\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}({\rm GL}_{1}/F));

A(\mathbb{R})
is the diagonal copy of
\mathbb{R}^{\times}
inside
G(\mathbb{R})=\prod_{v\in S_{r}}\mathbb{R}^{\times}\times\prod_{w\in S_{c}}\mathbb{C}^{\times};

A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}=\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\times}
, the connected component of the identity in
A(\mathbb{R});

\mathfrak{m}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathfrak{p}^{m_{\mathfrak{p}}}
, an integral ideal of
F
, which gives us a level structure, by which we mean:

K_{\mathfrak{m}}\subset G(\mathbb{A}_{f}),
an open compact subgroup:
K_{\mathfrak{m}}:=\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}|\mathfrak{m}}(1+\mathfrak{p}^{m_{\mathfrak{p}}})\times\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\not\,|\mathfrak{m}}U_{\mathfrak{p}}.

There are various quotients of
\mathbb{I}_{F}
that one may consider depending on how much to divide by inside
G(\mathbb{R})
. Here are five possible situations:

(33a)

\displaystyle S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\ :=\ G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(33b)

\displaystyle X^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\ :=\ G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}K_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(33c)

\displaystyle Y^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\ :=\ G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/K_{\infty}^{\circ}K_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(33d)

\displaystyle T^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\ :=\ G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}K_{\infty}^{\circ}K_{\mathfrak{m}}.

(33e)

\displaystyle C^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle\ :=\ G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A})/G(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}K_{\mathfrak{m}}.

Of course,
G
being abelian, the double-coset-spaces are all quotients of
\mathbb{I}_{F}
; for example

C^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ =\ G(\mathbb{A})/(G(\mathbb{Q})G(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}K_{\mathfrak{m}})\ =\ \mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}\mathbb{U}_{F}(\mathfrak{m}))\ \approx\ \mathcal{C}l^{+}_{F}(\mathfrak{m}),

the narrow ray class group modulo
\mathfrak{m}.
Writing as a double-coset space is simply to suggest how things would generalize if
G
is taken to be a general reductive group over
\mathbb{Q}
. There are the canonical projection maps:

(34)

\textstyle{S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Y^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{T^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{C^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\approx\mathcal{C}l^{+}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})}

The bottom most is a finite-abelian group being a narrow ray class group.
To get a feel for the space
S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
at the top, think of

G(\mathbb{A})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}\ =\ G(\mathbb{R})\times(G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}),

as the product of
G(\mathbb{R})
with the totally disconnected space
G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}
;
on which the discrete subgroup
G(\mathbb{Q})
–it is discrete in
G(\mathbb{A})
–acts diagonally, and taking the quotient by that action we have

G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash(G(\mathbb{R})\times(G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{\mathfrak{m}})).
Note
that
G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}
is a finite set:

G(\mathbb{Q})\backslash G(\mathbb{A}_{f})/K_{\mathfrak{m}}\ =\ \mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}/(F^{\times}\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}))\ \hookrightarrow\ \mathbb{I}_{F}/(F^{\times}(F_{\infty+}^{\times}\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})))\ =\ \mathcal{C}l_{F}^{+}(\mathfrak{m}).

There exists a finite set
\{x_{1,f},\dots,x_{k,f}\}
of finite idèles such that
\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}=\cup_{i=1}^{k}x_{i,f}F^{\times}\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}).

Using these representatives a bijection

S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ \simeq\ \coprod_{i=1}^{k}(F_{\infty}^{\times}/U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}))x_{i,f}

can described as follows: for
x=(x_{\infty},x_{f})\in\mathbb{I}_{F}=F_{\infty}^{\times}\times\mathbb{A}_{F,f}^{\times}
, there is a unique
i
such that
x_{f}=x_{i,f}\,\gamma\,u_{f}
for
some
\gamma\in F^{\times}
and
u_{f}\in\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})
; the bijection is given by:

S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}\ni x(F^{\times}\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m}))\ \mapsto\ (\gamma^{-1}x_{\infty}U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}),x_{i,f})\in(F_{\infty}^{\times}/U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m}))x_{i,f}.

Let us describe the connected components of
S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, which are all copies of

\frac{F^{\times}_{\infty+}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\ \simeq\ \frac{(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+})^{r_{1}}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r_{2}}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}.

Let
{}^{0}F^{\times}_{\infty+}
denote the kernel of norm at infinity
|\!|\ |\!|_{\infty}:F^{\times}_{\infty+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}.
Then

F^{\times}_{\infty+}\ =\ {}^{0}F^{\times}_{\infty+}\times A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}.

By the product formula,
F^{\times}_{\infty+}
contains
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
. Note that if

u\in U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\cap A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}
then
\sigma(u)=\sigma^{\prime}(u)
for all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{F}
, hence
u\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
; whence
u\in\{\pm 1\}.

Assume the mild condition that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\cap\{\pm 1\}=\{1\}.
Hence we get

\frac{F^{\times}_{\infty+}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\ =\ A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\times\frac{{}^{0}F^{\times}_{\infty+}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}.

Next, under the identification
F^{\times}_{\infty+}\simeq(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+})^{r_{1}}\times(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r_{2}}
, after writing
\mathbb{C}^{\times}=\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\times S^{1}
, and noting that
K(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}=(S^{1})^{r_{2}}
which is the product of the
S^{1}
’s coming from the complex places, we have:

{}^{0}F^{\times}_{\infty+}\ \simeq\ K(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\times{}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+})^{r_{1}+r_{2}},

where,
{}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+})^{r_{1}+r_{2}}
consists of tuples
(t_{v})_{v\in S_{\infty}}
such that
\sum_{v}{\rm log}|t_{v}|_{v}=0.

Assume furthermore that
\mathfrak{m}
is deep enough so that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})\cap K(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}
, which
a priori
is finite being discrete and compact in
F^{\times}_{\infty+}
,
is in fact trivial, i.e., it amounts to assuming that
U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
has no roots of unity. Then, by the proof of Dirichlet’s unit theorem using the Minkowski map
(see the notations in the proof of Lem.
17
) one has:

\frac{{}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+})^{r_{1}+r_{2}}}{U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\approx\frac{\mathcal{H}\approx\mathbb{R}^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}}{\Gamma_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})}\approx(S^{1})^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1}.

To summarise, any connected component of
S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, for
\mathfrak{m}
deep enough, is homeomorphic to

A(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\times K(\mathbb{R})^{\circ}\times(S^{1})^{r_{1}+r_{2}-1},

which in turn is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{\times}_{+}\times(S^{1})^{r_{1}+2r_{2}-1}.

Note that

{\rm dim}(S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}})=r_{1}+2r_{2}=d_{F}=[F:\mathbb{Q}].

Similarly, the other spaces in (
33
) can be described. We can grant ourselves the license to call
any of these as a ‘locally symmetric space’ for
{\rm GL}_{1}
over
F
with level
\mathfrak{m}.

5.2.
Sheaves on locally symmetric spaces

Take a field
E,
which is assumed to be a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that takes a copy of
F
; hence

{\rm Hom}(F,E)={\rm Hom}(F,\bar{E}).

This field
E
may be called the field of coefficients. Consider an infinity type
{\bf n}=(n_{\tau})_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}
with
n_{\tau}\in\mathbb{Z};
one may also denote this as

{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
.
This is like an infinity type from before, except that it is now parametrised over
{\rm Hom}(F,E)
instead of
{\rm Hom}(F,\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
as before.
An embedding
\iota:E\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
gives an identification
\iota_{*}:{\rm Hom}(F,E)\rightarrow{\rm Hom}(F,\bar{\mathbb{Q}})
as

\tau\mapsto\iota_{*}\tau=\iota\circ\tau.
Such an
{\bf n}
gives us an algebraic representation
\vartheta_{\bf n}
on a one-dimensional

E
-vector space
\mathcal{M}_{{\bf n},E}
denoted:

\vartheta_{\bf n}:G\times E\ \longrightarrow\ {\rm GL}_{1}(\mathcal{M}_{{\bf n},E}).

Since
G\times E={\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}({\rm GL}_{1}/F)\times E=\prod_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}{\rm GL}_{1}\times_{F,\tau}E,
and on the component indexed by
\tau
, the
representation is
x\mapsto\vartheta_{n^{\tau}}(x):=x^{n_{\tau}}
for
x\in{\rm GL}_{1}(E).

For
a\in G(\mathbb{Q})=F^{\times}
, we have

\vartheta_{\bf n}(a)\ =\ \prod_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}\vartheta_{n_{\tau}}(\tau(a))\ =\ \prod_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}\tau(a)^{n_{\tau}}.

Given an infinity type
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
, and the associated representation
(\vartheta_{\bf n},\mathcal{M}_{{\bf n},E})
, there is a sheaf
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}
of
E
-vector spaces on
S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
constructed as follows. Consider the canonical projection map of dividing by
F^{\times}
:

\pi\ :\ \mathbb{I}_{F}/\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})\ \longrightarrow\ F^{\times}\backslash\mathbb{I}_{F}/\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})=S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}.

For any open subset
V\subset S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
, define the sections of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}
over
V
as the
E
-vector space:

(35)

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}(V)\ =\ \{s:\pi^{-1}(V)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{{\bf n},E}\ :\ s(ax)=\vartheta_{\bf n}(a)s(x),\ \ \forall a\in F^{\times},\ \forall x\in\pi^{-1}(V)\}.

Checking the sheaf axioms is a routine exercise. The purity lemma (Lem.
17
) can be recast as the following lemma:

Lemma 36

(Purity)
.

Given
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
, the sheaf
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}
of
E
-vector spaces on
S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}}
is non-zero if and only if:
there exists
{\sf w}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(i)

if
S_{r}\neq\emptyset
then
n_{\tau}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in{\rm Hom}(F,E),
and

(ii)

if
S_{r}=\emptyset
then
n_{\iota\circ\tau}+n_{\overline{\iota\circ\tau}}={\sf w}
for all
\tau\in{\rm Hom}(F,E)
and for all

\iota\in{\rm Hom}(E,\bar{\mathbb{Q}}).

Proof.

A sheaf is nonzero if and only if it admits a nonzero stalk. A stalk over a point
y\in V
, consists of all
s:\pi^{-1}(y)\rightarrow\mathcal{M}_{{\bf n},E}
, such that

s(ax)=\vartheta_{\bf n}(a)s(x),
for all
a\in F^{\times}
and all
x\in\pi^{-1}(y).
This is possible if and only if
\vartheta_{\bf n}(a)=1
for
all
a\in F^{\times}\cap\mathbb{U}_{F,f}(\mathfrak{m})=U_{F}^{1}(\mathfrak{m})
. After passing to
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
via an
\iota
, the rest is basically the same as proof of Lem.
17
.
∎

Analogously, one may construct sheaves on the other spaces in (

); one may pull-back or push-forward the so constructed sheaves, and explicate the mutual relations between them.

5.3.
Cohomology of
{\rm GL}_{1}
and algebraic Hecke characters of
F
with coefficients in
E

Given an integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, and an infinity type
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
satisfying the purity conditions of Lem.
36
,
a fundamental object of interest is

H^{\bullet}(S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}},\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}),

the sheaf-cohomology of a locally symmetric space for
{\rm GL}_{1}/F

with coefficients in the sheaf
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}.
One may also consider

H^{\bullet}_{c}(S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}},\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},E}),

which is the cohomology with compact supports. It will take us way too much of an effort without substantial dividends for the purposes of this article to develop this systematically.
Any reader who might feel this is brewing heavy weather is recommended to look into Harder
[
6
, § 2.5]
on the cohomology of tori, where the torus at hand is the diagonal torus in
{\rm GL}_{2}
, which, up to the easily controlled centre, is basically the above kind of cohomology group. This context naturally arises in the theory of Eisenstein cohomology where the cohomology of the Levi subgroups (in particular, tori) play a crucial role. The reader is referred to Harder’s
forthcoming book
[
7
]
where this point of view is treated in great detail.

Let us suffice here to note that an algebraic Hecke character
\chi
of
F
modulo
\mathfrak{m}
,
taking values in the algebraic closure
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
of
\mathbb{Q}
inside
\mathbb{C}
, and with infinity type
{\bf n}
,
contributes to
H^{0}(S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}},\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}})
, and by duality also to the top-degree cohomology with compact supports:

H^{d_{F}}_{c}(S^{G}_{\mathfrak{m}},\,\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{{\bf n},\bar{\mathbb{Q}}}).
For arithmetic applications, it is better to work with an arbitrary coefficient field
E
, from which
one can then map into
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
or
\mathbb{C}
; this necessitates clarifying the meaning of an algebraic Hecke character of
F
with
coefficients in
E
.

The definition below follows Deligne
[
3
, §5]
; see also Schappacher
[
13
, Chap. 0]
.
Whereas in
[
3
]
and
[
13
]

E
is any number field with no relation to
F
, we will continue with our
restriction that
E
is a finite Galois extension of
\mathbb{Q}
that contains a copy of
F.
Given an integral ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
F
,
and an infinity type
{\bf n}\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)],

define an
algebraic Hecke character of
F
with values in
E
of modulus
\mathfrak{m}
and infinity type
{\bf n}
as a homomorphism

\chi:\ \mathbb{J}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})\ \rightarrow\ E^{\times}

such that for any principal ideal
(a)\in\mathbb{P}^{+}_{F}(\mathfrak{m})
(recall:
a\gg 0
and
a\equiv 1\pmod{\mathfrak{m}}
) one has

\chi((a))\ =\ \prod_{\tau\in{\rm Hom}(F,E)}\tau(a)^{n_{\tau}}.

Note the similarity to Hecke’s definition of a
Größencharakter
mod
\mathfrak{m}
, but with values in
E^{\times}
instead of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}
, and
while asking for the character at infinity to be of algebraic type.
To see the relation with our previous treatment of algebraic Hecke characters, following
[

6.
Ratios of critical values of Hecke
L
-functions

Let
\chi
be an algebraic Hecke character of
F
with values in
E
of modulus
\mathfrak{m}
and infinity type
{\bf n}=\sum_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}n_{\tau}\tau.

For any embedding
\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, consider the algebraic Hecke character
{}^{\iota}\chi
as in Prop.
40
. The rest
of this article concerns the special values of the
\mathbb{C}
-valued

L(s,\iota,\chi)\ :=\ L(s,{}^{\iota}\chi),

the right hand side is the usual Hecke
L
-function; see
4.2.1
.
It is sometimes convenient to consider the

-valued

\mathbb{L}(s,\chi)\ :=\ \{L(s,\iota,\chi)\}_{\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}},

where

is identified with
\prod_{\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}.
From Prop.
23
and Lem.
49
below
it follows that the
critical set of

is independent of
\iota
; one may therefore allude to the critical set for

Furthermore, we are interested in the rationality results for the ratios of successive

L

[

]
.
Henceforth, we assume that

is a totally imaginary number field, in which case it follows from Prop.

, then we are guaranteed the existence of two consecutive integers

6.1.
A result of Harder on ratios of critical
L
-values

The relative discriminant
\delta_{F/K}
of any extension
F/K
of number fields is defined as: suppose
{\rm Hom}(F,\bar{K})=\{\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r}\}
, and

\{\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{r}\}
is a
K
-basis of
F
, then define
\delta_{F/K}\ :=\ \det([\sigma_{i}(\omega_{j})])^{2}
;
taking the square makes it invariant under
{\rm Gal}(\bar{K}/K)
, and hence
\delta_{F/K}
is in
K^{\times}
, and as an element of
K^{\times}/K^{\times}{}^{2}
it is
independent of the choice of the basis
\{\omega_{j}\}
. The absolute discriminant of
F
is
\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}.

Harder proves in
[
6
, Cor. (4.2.2)]
that if
m
and
m+1
are critical for
\mathbb{L}(s,\chi),
then the number

(41)

C({}^{\iota}\chi)\ :=\ |\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}

is in
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
, and for any
\varsigma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
, one has

(42)

\varsigma(C({}^{\iota}\chi))\ =\ C({}^{\varsigma\circ\iota}\chi).

Let us note that we work with the completed
L
-function, and not with just the finite part of the
L
-function as in
loc. cit.
In particular, the power of

\bm{\uppi}
in
[
6
, Cor. (4.2.2)]
is subsumed by the ratio of archimedean local-factors. Also, the result is stated therein for
m=-1
; for a general critical integer
m
(with
m+1
also critical) follows by taking Tate-twists of
\chi.

Note that (
41
) is asserting that the ratio of
L
-values therein is algebraic, since
|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}
is algebraic; but if we only look
at the ratios of
L
-values then the reciprocity law becomes obscured. At any rate, the algebraicity result as stated in (
41
)
together with the reciprocity law in (
42
) gives that:

(43)

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}\ \in\iota(E).

We contend that whereas just the algebraicity result in (
41
) is correct, but restated as the stronger statement in (
43
) via the reciprocity law it is not correct as it stands by producing an example when
F
is a totally imaginary field that is not of CM type.
The reason turns out to be, and this is the
raison d’être
for
writing this article, that the reciprocity law in (
42
) needs to be modified by a sign; this sign is rather complicated, and turns out to be trivial when

F
is a CM field, and can be nontrivial for a general totally imaginary field.
We will first discuss this example in Sect.
6.2
that says that (
43
) is not stable under base-change making it incorrect as it stands in general. In Sect.
6.3
, we will see how to rectify the reciprocity law in (
42
) using, interestingly enough, some other passages in
[
6
]
itself.

6.2.
An example: stability under base-change

Consider the fields in Example
30
:
F_{0}=\mathbb{Q}
, and put
F_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})
and
F=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i},\sqrt{4+\mathfrak{i}}).
To emphasize, this
F
is totally imaginary but not CM, and
F_{1}
is a CM subfield. Changing notations mildly to make it more suggestive we denote:

\textstyle{F=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i},\sqrt{4+\mathfrak{i}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{\rm cm}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{F^{+}=\mathbb{Q}}

Let us compute
\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}.
First of all,

\delta_{F/F^{\rm cm}}\ =\ \delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i},\sqrt{4+\mathfrak{i}})/\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})}\ =\ 4(4+\mathfrak{i}),

computed with respect to the basis
\{1,\sqrt{4+\mathfrak{i}}\}
; hence:

N_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F^{\rm cm}})\ =\ N_{\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})/\mathbb{Q}}(4(4+\mathfrak{i}))\ =\ 16\cdot 17.

Also, using the basis
\{1,\mathfrak{i}\}
for
\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})
, we have:

\delta_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}=\delta_{\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})/\mathbb{Q}}=-4.

Using the well-known formula for the discriminant of a tower of fields, we get:

\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\ =\ \delta_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[F:F^{\rm cm}]}\cdot N_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F^{\rm cm}})\ =\ (-4)^{2}\cdot 16\cdot 17\ =\ 2^{8}\cdot 17.

Let
\psi
be an algebraic Hecke character of
F^{\rm cm}=\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})
; as a continuous homomorphism
\psi:\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}(\mathfrak{i})^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times}
,
with the character at infinity,
\psi_{\infty}:\mathbb{C}^{\times}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{\times},
being of the form
\psi_{\infty}(z)=z^{a}\bar{z}^{b},
for
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
. We may take (without loss of much generality) that
a\geq b
. The critical set for
L(s,\psi)
is given by:

{\rm Crit}(L(s,\psi))\ =\ \{1-a,\,2-a,\,\dots,\,-b\}.

Let us further assume that
a-b\geq 2
so that there are at least two critical points; let
m,m+1\in{\rm Crit}(L(s,\psi)).
Then,
(
43
) will say that

|\delta_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ \frac{L(m,\psi)}{L(m+1,\psi)}\ \in\mathbb{Q}(\psi),

where
\mathbb{Q}(\psi)
is the number field generated by the values of the finite part
\psi_{f}
of
\psi.
(We could have set up the context so that
\psi
takes values in a coefficient field
E
, and then used an

\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and to assert that the left hand side, for
L(s,\iota,\psi)
in place of
L(s,\psi),
lies in
\iota(E).
) Since

|\delta_{F^{\rm cm}/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}=|-4|^{1/2}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
we have:

(44)

\frac{L(m,\psi)}{L(m+1,\psi)}\ \in\ \mathbb{Q}(\psi).

Suppose
\omega
is a quadratic Dirichlet character of
F^{\rm cm}
, then
\omega_{\infty}
is trivial, and (
44
) applied to
\psi\omega
will give:

(45)

\frac{L(m,\psi\omega)}{L(m+1,\psi\omega)}\ \in\ \mathbb{Q}(\psi\omega)\ =\ \mathbb{Q}(\psi).

Now let
\chi
be the base-change of
\psi
to give a Hecke character of
F
:

\chi\ :=\ \psi\circ N_{F/F^{\rm cm}}.

Note that
F_{\infty}=\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
,
F^{\rm cm}_{\infty}=\mathbb{C}
, and
N_{F_{\infty}/F^{\rm cm}_{\infty}}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}z_{2}
. Hence:

\chi_{\infty}(z_{1},z_{2})=(z_{1}z_{2})^{a}\overline{(z_{1}z_{2})}^{b}=z_{1}^{a}z_{2}^{a}\bar{z_{1}}^{b}\bar{z_{2}}^{b},
which is to say

\chi_{\infty}=\psi_{\infty}\otimes\psi_{\infty}
, whence
L_{\infty}(s,\chi)=L_{\infty}(s,\psi)^{2}
, from which we deduce:

{\rm Crit}(L(s,\chi))\ =\ {\rm Crit}(L(s,\psi)).

Also, a basic property of base-change is that:

(46)

L(s,\chi)\ =\ L(s,\psi)L(s,\psi\omega),

where
\omega=\omega_{F/F^{\rm cm}}
is the quadratic character of
F^{\rm cm}
attached by class field theory to the quadratic extension
F/F^{\rm cm}.

From (
46
), (
44
), and (
45
) we deduce:

(47)

\frac{L(m,\chi)}{L(m+1,\chi)}\ \in\ \mathbb{Q}(\psi)=\mathbb{Q}(\chi).

But, on the other hand, one may directly apply (
43
) to
L(s,\chi)
to get:

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ \frac{L(m,\chi)}{L(m+1,\chi)}\ \in\mathbb{Q}(\chi).

For the discriminant term:
|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}=(2^{8}\cdot 17)^{1/2}=\sqrt{17}\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}.
Hence:

(48)

\sqrt{17}\,\frac{L(m,\chi)}{L(m+1,\chi)}\ \in\ \mathbb{Q}(\chi).

Of course, (
47
) and (
48
) are contradictory, because in general
\sqrt{17}\notin\mathbb{Q}(\chi).
We may say that
that the formal statement in (
43
) is not stable under base-change. The reader can readily appreciate that the above example can be generalized.

Variations on a result of Harder

In this subsection we state the main theorem of this article; see Theorem
56
. Before doing so, we need to introduce notions and notations that lead to a delicate signature that appears in that theorem. For the rest of this subsection we will let
F
be a totally imaginary number field, and
E
a number field that is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
and contains a copy of
F
. Let

{\bf n}=\sum_{\tau}n_{\tau}\tau\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
be an infinity type satisfying the purity condition of Lem.
36
,
with purity weight
{\sf w}.

Suppose

\chi
is an algebraic Hecke character of
F
with values in
E
of infinity type
{\bf n}.
Take any embedding
\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
and
let
{}^{\iota}\chi
be the character as in Prop.
40
.

Recall from Prop.
23
that the width of
{\bf n},
for a given
\iota,
is defined as:

\ell({}^{\iota}\chi)\ =\ \ell({}^{\iota}{\bf n})\ :=\ \min_{\tau:F\rightarrow E}\{|n_{\iota\circ\tau}-n_{\overline{\iota\circ\tau}}|\}.

The Hecke
L
-function
L(s,{}^{\iota}\chi)
has
\ell({}^{\iota}\chi)
many critical points. Note the following simple but important lemma:

Lemma 49
.

\ell({}^{\iota}{\bf n})
is independent of
\iota
, and depends
only on the pure infinity type
{\bf n}.

Proof.

If
F
has no CM subfield (
F_{1}=F_{0}
is totally real), then from Prop.
27

is follows that
\ell({}^{\iota}{\bf n})=0,
which proves the lemma; but this case is not interesting because we would like to have critical points.
Assume henceforth that
F
is totally imaginary field that contains a (maximal)

F_{1},
in which case
{\bf n}
is the base-change from a pure infinity type

over

. It is clear that

\ell({}^{\iota}{\bf n})=\ell({}^{\iota}{\bf m}).
Suppose
{\rm Hom}(F_{1},E)=\{\tau_{1},\tau_{1}^{\prime},\dots,\tau_{k},\tau_{k}^{\prime}\}
, where
2k=[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}]
,
and
\tau_{j}
and

of

. Then it follows from (the proof of)

\ell({}^{\iota}{\bf m})\ =\ \min_{j}\{|m_{\tau_{j}}-m_{\tau_{j}^{\prime}}|\}.

∎

6.3.1.
A combinatorial lemma

The following is a special case
of a general ‘combinatorial lemma’ that is at the heart of what makes the theory of Eisenstein cohomology work so well for studying the special values
to automorphic
L
-functions.

Lemma 50
.

The following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

s=-1
and
s=0
are critical for
L(s,{}^{\iota}\chi).

(2)

-\ell\leq{\sf w}\leq-4+\ell.

(3)

For each
\tau:F\rightarrow E
and
\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, there exists
w_{\iota\circ\tau}
in the Weyl group of
{\rm GL}(2)
such that

(a)

l(w_{\iota\circ\tau})+l(w_{\overline{\iota\circ\tau}})=1,
and

(b)

w_{\iota\circ\tau}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n_{\iota\circ\tau}&\\
&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is dominant.

The reader is referred to
[
12
, Sect. 3.2]
for a proof; to compare Statement (2) above to the corresponding statement in
[
12
, Lem. 3.16]
, note
that
N
,
\ell(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
, and
a(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
of
[
12
]
take the values

2
,
\ell
, and
{\sf w}/2,
respectively in this article; Statement (3) and its consequences need to be elaborated upon.
Given
n_{1},n_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, by
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n_{1}&\\
&n_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)
one means the algebraic character of the diagonal torus of

{\rm GL}(2)
given by
{\rm diag}(t_{1},t_{2})\mapsto t_{1}^{n_{1}}t_{2}^{n_{2}}.
Such a character is said to be dominant if
n_{1}\geq n_{2}
(where the choice of
the Borel subgroup
B
is taken to be the upper-triangular matrices in
{\rm GL}(2)
).
The Weyl group
W
of
{\rm GL}(2)
, which has order
2
, acts on this torus and hence
on its characters. The twisted action of the nontrival element
w_{0}\in W
on such a character
is explicitly given by:

w_{0}\cdot\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n_{1}&\\
&n_{2}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}n_{2}-1&\\
&n_{1}+1\end{smallmatrix}\right).

Of course the length
l(w)=1
if
w=w_{0}
, and
l(w)=0
if
w
is the trivial element. Now the statement in (3) makes sense.
Furthermore, in each pair

\{\iota\circ\tau,\,\overline{\iota\circ\tau}\}
of conjugate embeddings
\Sigma_{F}
, by (a), exactly one of the Weyl group elements is nontrivial and so having length
1
, and
the other is trivial; this defines a CM-type for
F
(recall that by a CM-type
\Phi
for

one means
\Phi\subset\Sigma_{F}
such that

\Sigma_{F}=\Phi\amalg\bar{\Phi}

(51)

\Phi({\bf n},\iota)\ :=\ \left\{\eta\in\Sigma_{F}\ :\ l(w_{\eta})=1\right\},

and a bijection
\beta_{{\bf n},\iota}:\Phi({\bf n},\iota)\rightarrow S_{\infty}
with the set of archimedean places
of
F.
Statement (3) also implies:

(52)

\eta\in\Phi({\bf n},\iota)\implies n_{\eta}\leq-2,\quad{\rm and}\quad\eta\notin\Phi({\bf n},\iota)\implies n_{\eta}\geq 0.

For each
\eta\in\Sigma_{F}
we have (

and
n_{\bar{\eta}}\geq 0
) or (
n_{\eta}\geq 0
and
n_{\bar{\eta}}\leq-2

Let us digress for a moment: what condition (3) really says is that the automorphic representation

{}^{\rm a}{\rm Ind}_{B(\mathbb{A}_{F})}^{{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}({}^{\iota}\chi,1\!\!1),
that is algebraically and parabolically induced from the Borel subgroup,
contributes to the cohomology in degree
[F:\mathbb{Q}]/2
of the

\partial_{B}S^{G}
of a locally symmetric space

for

to deduce results on the special values of

.

from which an analogous rationality result follows for the ratios of other successive pair of critical values by allowing ourselves

\chi

of critical values,
no more and no less!

for
F
is deduced from the CM-type

\Phi({\bf m},\iota)
for
F_{1}
under the canonical map
\Sigma_{F}\rightarrow\Sigma_{F_{1}};
and for a CM-field such as
F_{1}
, a Galois conjugate of a CM-type is
a CM-type; hence we deduce that
(\varsigma\circ-)(\Phi({\bf n},\iota))=\Phi({\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota)
is a CM-type. The sign of the permutation

\pi_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
defines
the signature:

(53)

\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\ :=\ {\rm sgn}(\pi_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)).

Let us digress for another moment: the key idea in the theory of Eisenstein cohomology is to see the map induced in cohomology by
the standard intertwining operator:

T_{\rm st}(s)|_{s=-1}\ :\ {}^{\rm a}{\rm Ind}_{B(\mathbb{A}_{F})}^{{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}({}^{\iota}\chi,1\!\!1)\ \longrightarrow\ {}^{\rm a}{\rm Ind}_{B(\mathbb{A}_{F})}^{{\rm GL}_{2}(\mathbb{A}_{F})}(1\!\!1(1),{}^{\iota}\chi(-1))

is realised in terms of the restriction map from total cohomology of
S^{G}
to the cohomology of the isotypic component cut out by these induced representations in the cohomology of the Borel–Serre boundary
\partial_{B}S^{G}
. (See
[
9
, pp. 82–83]
.)
The infinity type of the inducing representation
{}^{\iota}\chi\otimes 1\!\!1
in the domain of
T_{\rm st}(s)|_{s=-1}
corresponds to the weight

{}^{\iota}\underline{\bf n}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}{}^{\iota}{\bf n}&\\
&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
; similarly
the infinity type of the inducing representation
1\!\!1(1)\otimes{}^{\iota}\chi(-1)
for the codmain is the weight

{}^{\iota}\tilde{\underline{\bf n}}:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&\\
&{}^{\iota}{\bf n}+1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
.

Complementary to
\Phi({\bf n},\iota)
, we define the CM-type

(54)

\Phi(\tilde{\bf n},\iota)\ :=\ \left\{\eta\in\Sigma_{F}\ :\ l(w_{\eta})=0\right\}\ =\ \left\{\eta\in\Sigma_{F}\ :n_{\eta}\geq 0\right\}.

Note that
\Phi({\bf n},\iota)=\overline{\Phi}(\tilde{\bf n},\iota),
and the bijection

\beta_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}:\Phi(\tilde{\bf n},\iota)\rightarrow S_{\infty}
is the complex-conjugate of
\beta_{{\bf n},\iota}.

For each
\varsigma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
define a permutation
\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
of
S_{\infty}
by the diagram:

\textstyle{\Phi(\tilde{\bf n},\iota)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}}
\scriptstyle{\varsigma\circ-}
\textstyle{S_{\infty}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)}
\textstyle{\Phi(\tilde{\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\beta_{\tilde{\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota}}
\textstyle{S_{\infty},}

the sign of which defines the signature

(55)

\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\ :=\ {\rm sgn}(\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)).

6.3.3.
The main theorem on
L
-values

We are now ready to state the main theorem which is a variation of a result due to Harder:

Theorem 56
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary number field, and
E
a number field that is Galois over
\mathbb{Q}
and containing a copy of
F
. Let

{\bf n}=\sum_{\tau}n_{\tau}\tau\in\mathbb{Z}[{\rm Hom}(F,E)]
be an infinity type satisfying the purity condition of Lem.
36
,
with purity weight
{\sf w}.

Suppose

\chi
is an algebraic Hecke character of
F
with values in
E
of infinity type
{\bf n}.
Take any embedding
\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
and
let
{}^{\iota}\chi
be the character as in Prop.
40
.
Assume the condition on
{\bf n}
from Lem.
50
:

-\ell\leq{\sf w}\leq-4+\ell.

Hence
\ell\geq 2
and
{\bf n}
is the base-change to
F
from an infinity-type of a maximal CM-subfield of
F
.
Suppose
m,m+1\in{\rm Crit}(\mathbb{L}(s,\chi));
see Prop.
23
.
Then:

(57)

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}\ \in\iota(E),

and, furthermore, for every
\varsigma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
we have the reciprocity law:

(58)

\varsigma\left(|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}\right)\ =\ \varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\frac{L(m,{}^{\varsigma\circ\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\varsigma\circ\iota}\chi)}.

6.3.4.
Remarks on the proof

The above theorem is a special case of a more general theorem on Rankin–Selberg
L
-functions
for
{\rm GL}(n)\times{\rm GL}(n^{\prime})
over a totally imaginary field; see
[
12
, Thm. 5.16, (1), (2)]
.
When
F
is a CM field, one has
\varsigma\circ\mathfrak{c}\circ\eta=\mathfrak{c}\circ\varsigma\circ\eta
(see Lem.
24
),
hence,

\pi_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)=\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
an equality of the permutations of
S_{\infty}
; whence,

\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)=\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma).
In particular,
the reciprocity law in (
58
) is the same as (
42
).
As will be seen below this is no longer the case for a general total imaginary field; even though one has an equality of sets

\mathfrak{c}(\Phi({\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota))=\varsigma(\mathfrak{c}(\Phi({\bf n},\iota))),
the permutations
\pi_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
and
\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)

can be distinct, and the signature
\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
can be nontrivial. (This is in essence the explanation of the counterexample in Sect.
6.2
.)
Even though Thm.
56
and especially (
58
),
for a general totally imaginary field, are not stated like this
in Harder
[
6
]
, its proof follows by putting together the proof of

[
6
, Cor. 4.2.2]
amplified by the discussion in
[
6
, Sect. 2.4]
of a rational system of generators for the unipotent cohomology of the coefficient system on
{\rm GL}(2).
The signature
\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
is described in
[
6
, (2.4.1)]
.

6.4.
Compatibility with Deligne’s Conjecture

In this subsection we recall Deligne’s celebrated conjecture on the special values of motivic
L
-functions, as applied to ratios of successive critical values,
and also recall a result of Deligne on the ratios of motivic periods
that gives a motivic explanation to the ratio of successive critical
L
-values for a Hecke
L
-function.

6.4.1.
Statement of Deligne’s Conjecture

We will freely use the notation of
[
4
]
; a motive
M
over
\mathbb{Q}
with coefficients in a field
E
will be thought in terms of its Betti, de Rham, and
\ell
-adic realizations. For a critical motive
M
we have its periods
c^{\pm}(M)\in(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}
as in
loc. cit.,
that are well-defined in
(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}/E^{\times}.
We begin with a relation between the two periods over a totally imaginary base field
F
; recall that
F_{0}
is the largest totally real subfield of
F
; then
F_{0}
admits at most one totally imaginary quadratic extension contained inside
F
; if
F
does admit such a CM subfield, we denote it as
F_{1}
; and if there is no such extension inside
F
, then we put
F_{1}=F_{0}.
If
F_{1}
is indeed a CM field, and suppose
F_{1}=F_{0}(\sqrt{D})
for a totally negative
D\in F_{0}
, then define

\Delta_{F_{1}}\ :=\ \sqrt{N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)},\quad\Delta_{F}\ :=\ \Delta_{F_{1}}^{[F:F_{1}]}.

If
F_{1}=F_{0}
is totally real, then define

\Delta_{F_{1}}\ :=\ 1,\quad\Delta_{F}\ :=\ \Delta_{F_{1}}^{[F:F_{1}]}=1.

The following proposition is proved in
[
5
]
:

Proposition 59
.

Let
M_{0}
be a pure motive of rank
1
over a totally imaginary number field
F
with coefficients in a number field
E
. Put
M={\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(M_{0}),
and suppose that
M
has no middle Hodge type. Let
c^{\pm}(M)
be the periods defined in
[
4
]
.
Then

\frac{c^{+}(M)}{c^{-}(M)}\ =\ 1\otimes\Delta_{F},\quad\mbox{in $(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}/E^{\times}$.}

Let us also note that
1\otimes\Delta_{F}
is
\pm 1
in each component of
(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}/E^{\times}
, since its square is trivial.
Based on Prop.
59
, Deligne’s conjecture
[
4
]
for the ratios of successive critical values of the completed
L
-function of a rank-one motive may be stated as:

Conjecture 60

(Deligne)
.

Let
M_{0}
be a pure motive of rank
1
over a totally imaginary
F
with coefficients in
E
. Put
M={\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}(M_{0}),
and suppose that

M
has no middle Hodge type. For
\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
let
L(s,\iota,M)
denote the completed
L
-function attached to
(M,\iota).
Put

L(s,M)=\{L(s,\iota,M)\}_{\iota:E\rightarrow\mathbb{C}}
for the array of
L
-functions taking values in
E\otimes\mathbb{C}.
Suppose
m
and
m+1
are critical
integers for
L(s,M)
, and assuming that
L(m+1,M)\neq 0
, we have

\frac{L(m,M)}{L(m+1,M)}\ =\ 1\otimes\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F},

where the equality is in
(E\otimes\mathbb{C})^{\times}/E^{\times}.

A word of explanation is in order, since, in
[
4
]
, Deligne formulated his conjecture for critical values of
L_{f}(s,M)
–the finite-part of the
L
-function attached to
M;
which takes the shape:

\frac{L_{f}(m,M)}{L_{f}(m+1,M)}\ =\ (1\otimes(2\bm{\uppi}\mathfrak{i})^{-d^{\pm}(M)})\,\frac{c^{\pm}(M)}{c^{\mp}(M)},\quad\mbox{in $E\otimes\mathbb{C}$}.

The assumption on
M
that there is middle Hodge type gives
d^{\pm}(M)=d(M)/2=d_{F}/2.

Knowing the
L
-factor at infinity we have:

L_{\infty}(m,M)/L_{\infty}(m+1,M)=1\otimes(2\bm{\uppi})^{d_{F}/2}.

For the completed
L
-function, we get:

(61)

\frac{L(m,M)}{L(m+1,M)}\ =\ (1\otimes\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2})\,\frac{c^{\pm}(M)}{c^{\mp}(M)}.

It is clear now that (
61
) and Prop.
59
give Conj.
60
.

Using the conjectural correspondence between an algebraic Hecke character of
F
with values in
E
, and a rank-one motive over
F
with coefficients
in
E
, Conj.
60
may be restated as the following:

Conjecture 62

(Deligne’s Conjecture for ratios of critical values of Hecke
L
-functions)
.

Let the notations and hypothesis be as in Thm.
56
.
Then:

(63)

\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}\,\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}\ \in\iota(E),

and, furthermore, for every
\varsigma\in{\rm Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})
we have the reciprocity law:

(64)

\varsigma\left(\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}\,\frac{L(m,{}^{\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\iota}\chi)}\right)\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}\,\frac{L(m,{}^{\varsigma\circ\iota}\chi)}{L(m+1,{}^{\varsigma\circ\iota}\chi)}.

6.4.2.
Thm.
56

\implies
Conj.
62

This follows from the following:

Proposition 65
.

Let the notations and hypothesis be as in Thm.
56
. Then:

\frac{\varsigma(\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F})}{\mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}}\ =\ \frac{\varsigma(|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2})}{|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2})}\cdot\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma).

Proof.

When
F
is a CM-field, we have
\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)=1
from
6.3.4
; the proof will follow from the following lemma:

Lemma 66
.

Let
F
be a CM field. Then, as elements of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, we have:

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\cdot\Delta_{F}.

Proof of Lem.
66
.

Let
F_{0}
be the maximal totally real subfield of
F;
suppose
F=F_{0}(\sqrt{D})
, for a totally
negative
D\in F_{0};
then
\Delta_{F}=\sqrt{N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)}.

For the absolute discriminant of
F
, using the well-known formula for the discriminant of a tower of fields, we have:

\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\ =\ \delta_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[F:F_{0}]}\cdot N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}})\ =\ \delta_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}^{2}\cdot N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}}).

Using the basis
\{1,\sqrt{D}\}
we get the relative discriminant of
F/F_{0}
as
\delta_{F/F_{0}}=4D.
Hence, taking square-root
of the absolute value of
\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}
we get:

\sqrt{|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|}\ =\ \sqrt{|N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)|}\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}.

Since
D
is totally negative, the sign of
N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)
is the same as
(-1)^{[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]}=(-1)^{d_{F}/2},
i.e.,

|N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)|=(-1)^{d_{F}/2}N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D).
Hence:

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ =\ \sqrt{(-1)^{d_{F}/2}N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)}\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\sqrt{N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)}\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}.

∎

Suppose now that
F
is a totally imaginary field; the hypothesis of Thm.
56
guarantees that
F
contains a CM subfield
F_{1}
and its maximal
totally real subfield
F_{0}.
Lem.
66
generalizes to the following lemma:

Lemma 67
.

Let
F
be a totally imaginary field, and
F_{1}
its maximal CM subfield.
Then, as elements of
\mathbb{C}^{\times}/\mathbb{Q}^{\times}
, we have:

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\cdot\Delta_{F}\cdot\left(N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})\right)^{1/2}.

Proof of Lem.
67
.

Begin by noting that

\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}\ =\ \delta_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}^{[F:F_{0}]}\cdot N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}}),
and since
[F:F_{0}]=2[F:F_{1}]
is even, we get

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ =\ |N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}})|^{1/2}\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}.

Next, since
\delta_{F/F_{0}}\ =\ \delta_{F_{1}/F_{0}}^{[F:F_{1}]}\cdot N_{F_{1}/F_{0}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}});
using the
F_{0}
-basis
\{1,\sqrt{D}\}
for

F_{1}
, one has
\delta_{F_{1}/F_{0}}=4D;
therefore,

N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}})\ =\ N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(4D)^{[F:F_{1}]}\cdot N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(N_{F_{1}/F_{0}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}}))\\
=\ N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)^{[F:F_{1}]}\cdot N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}{}^{2}}.

Since
F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}
is a CM-extension,
N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})>0
; hence, we get

|\delta_{F/\mathbb{Q}}|^{1/2}\ =\ |N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{0}})|^{1/2}\ =\ |N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)|^{[F:F_{1}]/2}\cdot\left(N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})\right)^{1/2}\pmod{\mathbb{Q}^{\times}}.

Since
D\ll 0
in
F_{0}
, we see that
(-1)^{[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]}N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)>0
. Hence,

|N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D)|^{[F:F_{1}]/2}\ =\ ((-1)^{[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]}N_{F_{0}/\mathbb{Q}}(D))^{[F:F_{1}]/2}\ =\ (\mathfrak{i}^{[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}]}\Delta_{F_{1}})^{[F:F_{1}]}\\
=\ \mathfrak{i}^{[F_{0}:\mathbb{Q}][F:F_{1}]}\Delta_{F}\ =\ \mathfrak{i}^{d_{F}/2}\Delta_{F}.

∎

Lemma 68
.

With notations as in Lem.
67
and Thm.
56
, we have the equality of signatures:

\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\cdot\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)\ =\ \frac{\varsigma\left(N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})^{1/2}\right)}{N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})^{1/2}}.

Proof of Lem.
68
.

For the left hand side,
recall that
\varepsilon_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
(resp.,
\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
)
is the sign of the permutation
\pi_{{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
(resp.,
\pi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}(\varsigma)
)
induced by the map

\varsigma\circ-:\Phi_{{\bf n},\iota}\rightarrow\Phi_{{\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota}

(resp.,
\Phi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}\rightarrow\Phi_{\tilde{\bf n},\varsigma\circ\iota}
). For brevity, let
\Phi=\Phi_{{\bf n},\iota}
, then

\bar{\Phi}=\Phi_{\tilde{\bf n},\iota}
. Recall from Prop.
26
that
{\bf n}
is the base-change of
{\bf m}
, hence
the CM-type
\Psi=\Phi_{{\bf m},\iota}
for the CM-field
F_{1}
has the property that
\Phi\rightarrow\Psi
under the restriction map; also

\bar{\Phi}\rightarrow\bar{\Psi}.
One has the following relation with respect to conjugating by a Galois element
\varsigma
:

\textstyle{\Phi\cup\bar{\Phi}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varsigma\circ-}
\textstyle{\varsigma(\Phi)\cup\varsigma(\bar{\Phi})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\Psi\cup\bar{\Psi}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varsigma\circ-}
\textstyle{\varsigma(\Psi)\cup\varsigma(\bar{\Psi})}

Fixing an ordering on
S_{\infty}(F_{1})
, the bottom row involves two permutations corresponding to

\Psi\rightarrow\varsigma(\Psi)
and
\bar{\Psi}\rightarrow\varsigma(\bar{\Psi})
, but from

24
it follows that they are equal, hence
\varepsilon_{{\bf m},\iota}(\varsigma)=\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf m},\iota}(\varsigma).

However, the two permutations and their signatures corresponding to
\Phi\rightarrow\varsigma(\Phi)
and
\bar{\Phi}\rightarrow\varsigma(\bar{\Phi})
need not be equal.

For the right hand side: let
\Sigma_{F_{1}}=\{\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{d_{1}}\};

d_{1}=[F_{1}:\mathbb{Q}];
then for any

one has

N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}=\prod_{j=1}^{d_{1}}\theta_{j}(x)>0.
Also, suppose
\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k}\}
denotes all embeddings of
F
into
\bar{F_{1}}
over
F_{1},

and

is an

Putting together, we get

N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})\ =\ \prod_{\theta\in\Sigma_{F_{1}}}\theta(\det[\rho_{i}(\omega_{j})]^{2})\ =\ \prod_{\theta\in\Sigma_{F_{1}}}\det[\rho_{i}^{\theta}(\omega_{j})]^{2},

where

F
into
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
that restrict to
\theta:F_{1}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbb{Q}}.
Hence,

N_{F_{1}/\mathbb{Q}}(\delta_{F/F_{1}})^{1/2}\ =\ \pm\det\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}[\rho_{i}^{\theta_{1}}(\omega_{j})]&&&\\
&[\rho_{i}^{\theta_{2}}(\omega_{j})]&&\\
&&\ddots&\\
&&&[\rho_{i}^{\theta_{d_{1}}}(\omega_{j})]\end{array}\right],

where the appropriate sign
\pm

the

.

then the blocks along

which is the same as

and
\bar{\Psi}\rightarrow\varsigma(\bar{\Psi}),

and since
\varepsilon_{{\bf m},\iota}(\varsigma)=\varepsilon_{\tilde{\bf m},\iota}(\varsigma).

∎

.

We conclude this article by drawing attention to following piquant detail in the special case when

is its maximal totally real subfield. Suppose

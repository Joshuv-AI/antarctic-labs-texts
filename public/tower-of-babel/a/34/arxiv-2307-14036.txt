Abstract
. We present a new encoding of the Battle of Hercules and Hydra as a rewrite system with AC symbols. Unlike earlier term rewriting encodings, it faithfully models any strategy of Hercules to beat Hydra. To prove the termination of our encoding, we employ type introduction in connection with many-sorted semantic labeling for AC rewriting and AC-MPO, a new AC compatible reduction order that can be seen as a much weakened version of AC-RPO.

1.
Introduction

The mythological monster Hydra is a dragon-like creature with multiple
heads. Whenever Hercules in his fight chops off a head, more and more new
heads can grow instead, since the beast gets increasingly angry. Here we
model a Hydra as an unordered tree. If Hercules cuts off a leaf
corresponding to a head, the tree is modified in the following way: If the
cut-off node
h
has a grandparent
n
, then the branch from
n
to the
parent of
h
gets multiplied, where the number of copies
corresponds to the
number of decapitations so far. Hydra dies if there are no heads left, in
that case Hercules wins. The following sequence shows an example fight:

✂
0

✂
1

✂
2

✂
3

4

Though the number of heads can grow considerably in one step, it turns out
that the fight always terminates, and Hercules will win independent of his
strategy. Proving termination of the Battle is challenging since
Kirby and Paris proved in their landmark paper
[
KP82
]
that
termination for an arbitrary (computable) strategy is independent of
Peano arithmetic.
In
[
KP82
]
a termination argument based on ordinals is used.

Starting with
[
DJ90
, p. 271]
, several TRS encodings of the Battle of
Hercules and Hydra have been proposed and studied

[
Buc06
,
DM07
,
FZ96
,
Mos09
,
Tou98
]
.
Touzet
[
Tou98
]
was the first to give a
rigorous termination proof and in
[
ZWM15
]
the automation of ordinal
interpretations is discussed.
In this article we present yet another
encoding. In contrast to earlier TRS encodings that model a specific
strategy, it uses AC matching to represent
arbitrary
battles.
To prove its termination, we adapt existing termination
methods for AC rewriting.

The remainder of the article is organized as follows. After
recalling some basic definitions in Section
2
, we present
our new encoding of the Battle in Section
3
. We give a
rigorous proof that our encoding faithfully represents the Battle.
In Section
4
we present
many-sorted semantic labeling for AC rewriting and apply it to our
encoding. This results in an infinite AC rewrite system, which
can be shown terminating by Rubio’s AC-RPO
[
Rub02
]
.
As a matter of fact, we do not need the full power of AC-RPO. Inspired
by Steinbach’s AC-KBO
[
Ste90
]
, in Section
5
we introduce AC-MPO,
a much weakened version of AC-RPO, and show that it is powerful enough
for our purpose. Some of the properties of AC-MPO are proved in the
appendix.

Related work is discussed in
Section
6
. In particular, we comment on earlier encodings of the
Battle. We conclude in Section
7
with suggestions for future
research.

A preliminary version of this article appeared in the proceedings of the
8th International Conference on Formal Structures for Computation and
Deduction
[
HM23
]
. AC-MPO is a new result. New examples
provide further illustration of the simulation of the Battle of Hercules
and Hydra.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a set of
sorts
. An
\mathcal{S}
-
sorted signature

\mathcal{F}

consists of function symbols
f
having a sort declaration

S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{n}\to S
. Here
{S_{1}},\dots,{S_{n}}
and
S
are sorts in

\mathcal{S}
and
n
is the
arity
of
f
. By
f^{(n)}
we indicate
that
f
has arity
n
. Let
\mathcal{V}
be a countably infinite set of
variables, where every variable has its own sort.
We assume the existence of infinitely many variables of each sort.

Terms
of sort
S
are inductively defined as usual: Every
variable of sort
S
is a term of sort
S
and if
f
has sort
declaration
S_{1}\times\cdots\times S_{n}\to S
and
t_{i}
is a term of
sort
S_{i}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
then
f({t_{1}},\dots,{t_{n}})
is a term
of sort
S
.

Ground terms
are terms without variables.
The
root symbol

\mathsf{root}(t)
of a term
t
is
t
if it is a
variable, and
f
if
t=f({t_{1}},\dots,{t_{n}})
.
For every sort
S
we introduce a fresh constant
\Box_{S}
, called the

hole
. A term over
\mathcal{F}\uplus\{\Box_{S}\mid S\in\mathcal{S}\}
is a

context
over
\mathcal{F}
if it contains exactly one hole. Given a
context
C
and a term
t
, we write
C[t]
for the term resulting from
replacing the hole in
C
by
t
.
We write
s\trianglelefteqslant t
if
t=C[s]
for some context
C
. We write

s\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}t
if
s\trianglelefteqslant t
and
s\neq t
.
A mapping
\sigma
that associates each variable to a
term of the same sort is a
substitution
if its domain

\{\hskip 0.85358ptx\in\mathcal{V}\mid\sigma(x)\neq x\hskip 0.85358pt\}
is finite. The application

t\sigma
of
\sigma
to a term
t
is defined as
\sigma(t)
if
t
is a
variable and
f(t_{1}\sigma,\dots,t_{n}\sigma)
if
t=f({t_{1}},\dots,{t_{n}})
.
A binary relation
\to
on terms is
closed under substitutions
if

s\sigma\to t\sigma
whenever
s\to t
, for all substitutions

\sigma
. It is

closed under contexts
if
C[s]\to C[t]
whenever

s\to t
, for all contexts
C
.
It has the
subterm property
if the inclusion

{\rhd}\subseteq{\to}
holds.
Moreover, the relation
\to
is said to be a
rewrite relation

if it is closed under contexts and substitutions.

Rewrite orders
are rewrite relations that are strict orders,
and
reduction orders
are rewrite orders that are well-founded.

A
rewrite rule

\ell\rightarrow r
consists of two terms
\ell
and

r
of the same sort such that all variables in
r
occur in
\ell
.
A (many-sorted)
term rewrite system
(TRS) is a set of rewrite
rules. We denote by
\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}
the smallest rewrite relation that
contains the pairs of the TRS
\mathcal{R}
.
A rule
\ell\rightarrow r
is
non-collapsing
if
r
is
not a variable. A TRS is called
non-collapsing
if all rules
are non-collapsing.
A TRS
\mathcal{R}
is
terminating
if
\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}
is well-founded.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{AC}}
be a subset of the binary function symbols in

\mathcal{F}
that have sort declarations of the form
S\times S\to S
. We
denote by
\mathsf{AC}
the set of equations

\displaystyle f(f(x,y),z)

\displaystyle\approx f(x,f(y,z))

\displaystyle f(x,y)

\displaystyle\approx f(y,x)

expressing the associativity and commutativity of each
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{AC}}
.
Since equations in
\mathsf{AC}
are rewrite
rules, we can view
\mathsf{AC}
as a TRS. Using this fact, we
define the relation
=_{\mathsf{AC}}

as the reflexive, transitive, and symmetric closure of
\rightarrow_{\mathsf{AC}}
.
Let
\mathcal{R}
be a TRS. The relation
=_{\mathsf{AC}}\cdot\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}}\cdot=_{\mathsf{AC}}

is called AC rewriting and abbreviated by
\to_{\mathcal{R}/\mathsf{AC}}
. We
say that
\mathcal{R}
is
AC terminating
if

\to_{\mathcal{R}/\mathsf{AC}}
is well-founded.
A reduction order
>
is
AC-compatible
if the inclusion

{=_{\mathsf{AC}}\cdot>\cdot=_{\mathsf{AC}}}\subseteq{>}
holds. AC termination of a
TRS
\mathcal{R}
can be shown by finding an AC-compatible reduction order such
that
\mathcal{R}\subseteq{>}
holds.

The above definitions specialize to the usual unsorted setting
when the set of sorts is a singleton set.

Finally, we recall two order extensions. Let
>
be a strict order on a
set
A
. The
lexicographic extension

>^{\mathsf{lex}}
of
>
is defined on
tuples over
A
as follows:
({a_{1}},\dots,{a_{m}})>^{\mathsf{lex}}({b_{1}},\dots,{b_{n}})
if
n=m
and
there exists an index
1\leqslant k\leqslant n
such that
a_{k}>b_{k}

and
a_{i}=b_{i}
for all
i<k
. The
multiset extension

>^{\mathsf{mul}}
of

>
is defined on multisets over
A
as follows:
M>^{\mathsf{mul}}N
if there
exist multisets
X
and
Y
such that
N=(M-X)\uplus Y
,

\varnothing\neq X\subseteq M
, and every
b\in Y
admits an element

a\in X
with
a>b
.

3.
Encoding

First we give a formal account of the Hydra Battle.

{defi}

To represent Hydras, we use a signature containing a constant
symbol
\mathsf{h}
representing a head, a binary symbol
\mathbin{|}
for siblings, and
a unary function symbol
\mathsf{i}
representing the internal nodes. We use infix
notation for
\mathbin{|}
and declare it to be an AC symbol.
We write
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
for the set of ground terms over
\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{h},\mathsf{i},{\mathbin{|}}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
.

Encodings
of Hydras are terms
t
in
\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
with

\mathsf{root}(t)\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt\mathsf{h},\mathsf{i}\hskip 0.85358pt\}
.

To improve readability we omit parentheses in terms with nested

\mathbin{|}
symbols in examples.

{exa}

The Hydras in the above example fight are represented by the terms

\displaystyle H_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})))\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})

\displaystyle H_{1}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}))\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})

\displaystyle H_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}))\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})

\displaystyle H_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}))\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})

\displaystyle H_{4}

\displaystyle=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}))\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h}\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})

and they are encodings of Hydras. The term
\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}
is included in

\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{H}}
but not regarded as an encoding of a Hydra.

{defi}

Let
n
be a natural number. The TRS
\mathcal{R}_{n}
operates on
encodings of Hydras and consists of the following four rules:

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 1$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}^{n+2})

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 3$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}^{n+2}\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 2$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(x)^{n+2})

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}x)\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 4$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(x)^{n+2}\mathbin{|}y)

Here
t^{k}
for
k\geqslant 1
is defined inductively as follows:

\displaystyle t^{k}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}t&\text{if $k=1$}\\
t^{k-1}\mathbin{|}t&\text{if $k>1$}\end{cases}

The transition relation
\Rightarrow_{n}
on encodings of Hydras is
defined as follows:
H\Rightarrow_{n}H^{\prime}
if

(1)

H=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})
and
H^{\prime}=\mathsf{h}
, or

(2)

H=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}t)
and
H^{\prime}=\mathsf{i}(t)
for some term
t
, or

(3)

H\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}_{n}/\mathsf{AC}}H^{\prime}
.

That
H
and
H^{\prime}
are encodings of successive Hydras at stages
n
and

n+1
in a battle is expressed as
H\Rightarrow_{n}H^{\prime}
. So fights with
Hydras are represented by finite or infinite sequences of the form

H_{0}\Rightarrow_{0}H_{1}\Rightarrow_{1}H_{2}\Rightarrow_{2}\cdots
.

{exa}

[continued from Example
3
]
We have the following sequence:

H_{0}\,\Rightarrow_{0}\,H_{1}\,\Rightarrow_{1}\,\cdots\,\Rightarrow_{3}\,H_{4}

For instance, the first step is verified as follows: Let
\ell\to r

be the third rule in
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, and let

C=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{i}(\Box)\,\mathbin{|}\,\mathsf{h})
and

\sigma=\{\hskip 0.85358pty\mapsto\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\hskip 0.85358pt\}
. Since
H_{0}=C[\ell\sigma]
and

H_{1}=_{\mathsf{AC}}C[r\sigma]
hold, we have
H_{0}\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}_{0}/\mathsf{AC}}H_{1}
and so

H_{0}\Rightarrow_{0}H_{1}
is obtained.

Now we present our TRS encoding of the Hydra Battle. We represent
natural numbers
n
by
\mathsf{s}^{n}(\mathsf{0})
, which is abbreviated to
\mathsf{n}
.

{defi}

Let
\mathcal{F}
be the signature consisting of the constant
\mathsf{0}
, the
unary symbol
\mathsf{s}
, the five binary symbols
\mathsf{A}\,\text{--}\,\mathsf{E}
as
well as the three symbols
\mathsf{h}
,
\mathsf{i}
, and
\mathbin{|}
in Definition
3
.
The TRS
\mathcal{H}
over
\mathcal{F}
consists of the following 14
rewrite rules:

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 1$}\,}\,\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{s}(n),\mathsf{h})

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(x)))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 8$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(x)))

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 2$}\,}\,\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{s}(n),\mathsf{i}(x))

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(x)\mathbin{|}y))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 9$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(x))\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(n,\mathsf{i}(x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 3$}\,}\,\mathsf{B}(n,\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{s}(n),\mathsf{i}(x)))

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}x)\mathbin{|}y))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 10$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(n,\mathsf{i}(x))\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{0},x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 4$}\,}\,\mathsf{E}(x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}x)))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 11$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(n,\mathsf{i}(x)))

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(n),x)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 5$}\,}\,x\mathbin{|}\mathsf{C}(n,x)

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}y))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 12$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(n,\mathsf{h})\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{E}(x)\mathbin{|}y)

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 6$}\,}\,\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(x\mathbin{|}y))

\displaystyle\mathsf{D}(n,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 13$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(n,\mathsf{h}))

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{E}(x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 7$}\,}\,\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(x))

\displaystyle\mathsf{B}(n,\mathsf{E}(x))

\displaystyle\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 14$}\,}\,\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{s}(n),x)

The Battle is started with the term
\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{0},H)
where
H

is the encoding of the initial Hydra. Rule 1 takes care of the dying Hydra

.
An application of the rule ends the battle with a term of
the form
\mathsf{A}(n,\mathsf{h})
.
The
\mathsf{h}
denotes here a dead Hydra; a Hydra with
only one head is represented by the term
\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h})
.
Rule 2 cuts a head without grandparent node, and so no
copying takes place. Due to the power of AC matching, the removed
head need not be the leftmost one. With rule 3, the search for locating a
head with grandparent node starts. The search is performed with the
auxiliary symbol
\mathsf{D}
and involves rules 8–13. When the head
to be cut is located (in rules 10–13), copying begins with the
auxiliary symbol
\mathsf{C}
and rules 4 and 5. The end of the copying phase
is signaled with
\mathsf{E}
, which travels upwards with rules 6 and 7.
Finally, rule 14 creates the next stage of the Battle. Note that we make
extensive use of AC matching to simplify the search process.

It is important to note that the TRS
\mathcal{H}
defined above is

unsorted
and we establish in this article the result that it is AC
terminating on all terms. When simulating a battle, like in the statement
of the Theorem
1
, we deal with well-behaved terms adhering to
the sort discipline introduced shortly. The restriction to sorted terms is
crucial for our termination proof, but entails no loss of generality. This
is due to the following result, which is a special case of

[
MO00
, Corollary 3.9]
.

Theorem 2
.

A non-collapsing
TRS
over a many-sorted signature is

AC
terminating if and only if the corresponding
TRS
over
the unsorted version of the signature is
AC
terminating.
∎

The idea of using sorts to simplify termination proof goes back to
Zantema
[
Zan94
]
.
The TRS
\mathcal{H}
can be seen as a TRS over the many-sorted signature
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
:

\displaystyle\mathsf{h}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\mathsf{i},\mathsf{E}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{O}\to\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle{\mathbin{|}}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{O}\times\mathsf{O}\to\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\mathsf{A},\mathsf{B}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{O}\to\mathsf{S}

\displaystyle\mathsf{0}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{N}

\displaystyle\mathsf{s}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{N}\to\mathsf{N}

\displaystyle\mathsf{C},\mathsf{D}

\displaystyle:\mathsf{N}\times\mathsf{O}\to\mathsf{O}

where
\mathsf{N}
,
\mathsf{O}
and
\mathsf{S}
are sort symbols. Since
\mathcal{H}
is
non-collapsing, Theorem
2
guarantees that AC termination of

\mathcal{H}
follows from AC termination of well-sorted terms over
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
.

In the remainder of this section we present a proof of
Theorem
1
and its converse.

Lemma 3
.

If
n>0
then
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{n},t)\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}t^{n}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t)
for all
terms
t
.

Proof 3.1
.

We use induction on
n
. If
n=1
then

\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{n},t)\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 5$}\,}\,t\mathbin{|}\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{0},t)\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 6$}\,}\,t\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t)=t^{n}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t)

Suppose the result holds for
n\geqslant 1
and consider
n+1
. The
induction hypothesis yields
\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{n},t)\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}t^{n}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t)
. Hence

\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),t)\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 5$}\,}\,t\mathbin{|}\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{n},t)\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}t\mathbin{|}(t^{n}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t))=_{\mathsf{AC}}t^{n+1}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(t)

Lemma 4
.

Let
n
be a natural number. If
H\Rightarrow_{n}H^{\prime}

then
\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),H)\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}\mathsf{E}(H^{\prime})
.

Proof 3.2
.

We use structural induction on
H
and consider the following two cases.

•

First suppose
H\rightarrow_{\mathcal{R}_{n}/\mathsf{AC}}H^{\prime}
is a root step. If the first rule of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
is
used then
H=\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}))
and
H^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{i}(h^{n+2})
. We have

\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),H)\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 13$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),\mathsf{h}))
. Using
Lemma
3
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),\mathsf{h}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}^{n+1}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{h}))=_{\mathsf{AC}}\cdot\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 6$}\,}\,\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{h}^{n+1}))=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{E}(H^{\prime})

If the second rule of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
is used then
H=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{h}\mathbin{|}t))
and

H^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(t)^{n+2})
for some term
t
. We have

\mathsf{D}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),H)=_{\mathsf{AC}}\cdot\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 11$}\,}\,\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),\mathsf{i}(t)))
. Using
Lemma
3
we obtain

\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{C}(\mathsf{s}(\mathsf{n}),\mathsf{i}(t)))\rightarrow_{\mathcal{H}/\mathsf{AC}}^{*}\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(t)^{n+1}\mathbin{|}\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(t)))=_{\mathsf{AC}}\cdot\,\xrightarrow{\,\makebox[5.69054pt]{$\scriptstyle 6$}\,}\,\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(\mathsf{i}(t)\mathbin{|}\mathsf{i}(t)^{n+1}))=_{\mathsf{AC}}\mathsf{E}(H^{\prime})

If the third rule of
\mathcal{R}_{n}
is used then

Related Work

In an influential survey paper, Dershowitz and

[

, p. 270]

introduced a 5-rule rewrite system to simulate the Hydra Battle. The
proposed rewrite system was later shown to be erroneous. A corrected

, see also Moser

. Earlier,

It is worth noting that our rewrite system

is not even simply
terminating on unsorted terms. In fact, we have the following cyclic
sequence with respect to

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}(\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{i}(x)),\mathsf{i}(x))

The rewrite systems referred to above model the so-called

standard
battle, which corresponds to a specific strategy for
Hercules. In this regard it is interesting to quote Kirby and

[
KP82
]
, who introduced the battle as an accessible example of

A

is a function which determines for Hercules which head
to chop off at each stage of any battle. It is not hard to find a

Theorem 2. (i)

[\dots]

Theorem 2. (ii)

In a recent paper
[
EKO21
, Section 6]
, rules are
presented to slay Hydras, independent of the strategy. These rules
do not constitute a term rewrite system in the usual sense

Earlier, Ferreira and Zantema
[

7.
Conclusion

We presented a new TRS encoding of the Battle of Hydra and Hercules.
Unlike earlier encodings, it makes use of AC symbols. This allows us
to faithfully model any strategy of Hercules, as envisaged in the
paper by Kirby and Paris
[
KP82
]
in which the Battle was first
presented. To prove the termination of the encoding we
employed many-sorted rewriting modulo AC and we extended semantic
labeling modulo AC to many-sorted TRSs. The infinite TRS produced
by semantic labeling was proved terminating by suitably instantiating
AC-RPO.

One of the reviewers for this article pointed out that the Hydra
battle can still be simulated even if rule
6
in
\mathcal{H}
is replaced
by the simpler
\mathsf{E}(x)\mathbin{|}y\rightarrow\mathsf{E}(x\mathbin{|}y)
. While we expect
that this variant also has the termination property, the presented
termination methods are not applicable. In fact, the rule cannot be
ordered by AC-MPO and the AC symbol
\mathbin{|}
cannot be labeled.
The reviewer also suggested an alternative encoding of Hydras that
omits
\mathsf{i}
from

in

this encoding. While this simplifies representations of Hydras, it seems
difficult to construct an ordinal interpretation of

for semantic
labeling. Further investigations of AC termination techniques are
required.

{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(u)
we infer
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(u)

by the induction hypothesis, the AC-compatibility of
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
, and the
transitivity of
=_{\mathsf{AC}}
. Hence
s>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}u
by case (3).

The subterm property is next.

Lemma 19
.

The relation
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
has the subterm property.

Proof A.3
.

Let
t=f({t_{1}},\dots,{t_{n}})
. Fix
i\in\{\hskip 0.85358pt1,\dots,n\hskip 0.85358pt\}
. We show

t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t_{i}
. The subterm property is then obtained by induction and
the transitivity of
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
(Lemma
18
).
We distinguish two cases.

(1)

If
t_{i}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
then
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}\{\hskip 0.85358ptt_{i}\hskip 0.85358pt\}

and thus
t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t_{i}
by case (1).

(2)

If
t_{i}\notin{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
then
f
is an AC symbol,
n=2
and

\mathsf{root}(t_{i})=f
. Since

{\triangledown_{\!}}(t_{i})\subsetneq{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
,
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t_{i})
holds and
thus
t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t_{i}
by case (3).

The preceding lemmata are used to prove irreflexivity.

Lemma 20
.

The relation
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
is irreflexive.

Proof A.4
.

Assume to the contrary
t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
. We derive a
contradiction by induction on
t
. We distinguish three cases.

(1)

Suppose
t^{\prime}\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
for some
t^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. Since

t\rhd t^{\prime}
, the subterm property (Lemma
19
)
yields
t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
. So
t^{\prime}\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
, and thus

t^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
is obtained by the transitivity (Lemma
18
) or
AC compatibility (Lemma
17
) of
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
.
The induction hypothesis yields the desired contraction.

(2)

If
t>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
is derived by case (2) then
\mathsf{root}(t)>\mathsf{root}(t)
, which
contradicts the irreflexivity of the precedence
>
.

(3)

Suppose
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. Let
U
be the set of all
proper subterms of

t
, and
\succ
the restriction of
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
to
U\times U
. The
multiset extension
\succ^{\mathsf{mul}}
coincides with the restriction of
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}

to finite multisets over
U
. Hence
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)\succ^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
follows from

{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. The relation
\succ
is irreflexive according to
the induction hypothesis. Moreover,
\succ
inherits transitivity from

>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
(Lemma
18
). Hence
\succ
is a strict order and
thus so is its multiset extension
\succ^{\mathsf{mul}}
. Since
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
is a finite
multiset over
U
,
{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)\succ^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
cannot hold, yielding the
desired contradiction.

In the proof of closure under substitutions we use the fact that
for an
f
-rooted term
t
and a substitution
\sigma
the multiset

{\triangledown_{\!}}(t\sigma)
is the multiset sum of
{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
for all

t^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
.

Lemma 21
.

The relation
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
is closed under substitutions.

Proof A.5
.

Suppose
s>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
and let
\sigma
be a substitution.
We show
s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
by induction on
|s|+|t|
.
We distinguish three cases.

(1)

Suppose
s^{\prime}\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
for some
s^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
. If
s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t

then we obtain
s^{\prime}\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
from the induction hypothesis.
If
s^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}t
then we obtain

s^{\prime}\sigma=_{\mathsf{AC}}t\sigma
from the closure under substitutions of
=_{\mathsf{AC}}
.
So in both cases we have
s^{\prime}\sigma\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
.
If
s^{\prime}\sigma\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s\sigma)
then

{\triangledown_{\!}}(s\sigma)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}\{\hskip 0.85358ptt\sigma\hskip 0.85358pt\}

and thus
s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
by case (1).
If
s^{\prime}\sigma\notin{\triangledown_{\!}}(s\sigma)
then
s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}
and
thus
s^{\prime}=t
follows from
s^{\prime}\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
.
Hence
s^{\prime}\sigma=t\sigma
.
Since
s^{\prime}\sigma\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}s\sigma
we obtain

s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
from the subterm property
(Lemma
19
).

(2)

Suppose
\mathsf{root}(s)>\mathsf{root}(t)
and
\{\hskip 0.85358pts\hskip 0.85358pt\}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. Clearly

\mathsf{root}(s\sigma)=\mathsf{root}(s)>\mathsf{root}(t)=\mathsf{root}(t\sigma)
. Consider an arbitrary
term
u\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t\sigma)
. Let
f=\mathsf{root}(t)
. There exists a term

t^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
such that
u\in{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
. The
induction hypothesis yields
s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}\sigma
.
Since

u\in{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
satisfies
u\trianglelefteqslant t^{\prime}\sigma
,
we have

t^{\prime}\sigma\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}u
by the subterm property
(Lemma
19
)
and thus
s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}u
by transitivity (Lemma
18
).
Hence
\{\hskip 0.85358pts\sigma\hskip 0.85358pt\}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t\sigma)
and thus

s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
by case (2).

(3)

Suppose
\mathsf{root}(s)=\mathsf{root}(t)
and
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
.
We write
f
for
\mathsf{root}(s)
.
Let
s^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
and
t^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
such that

s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
or
s^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}t^{\prime}
is used in
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
.

•

We first consider
s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
.
Since
s^{\prime}\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}s
and
t^{\prime}\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}t
,
the induction hypothesis yields
s^{\prime}\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}\sigma
.
From
s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
we infer
s^{\prime}\notin\mathcal{V}
and thus

s^{\prime}\sigma\notin\mathcal{V}
. Because of
s^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
, we have

\mathsf{root}(s^{\prime})\neq f
and thus
\mathsf{root}(s^{\prime}\sigma)\neq f
. Hence

{\triangledown_{\!f}}(s^{\prime}\sigma)=\{\hskip 0.85358pts^{\prime}\sigma\hskip 0.85358pt\}
. Consider a term

u\in{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
. Since
u\trianglelefteqslant t^{\prime}\sigma
, we
obtain
t^{\prime}\sigma\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}u
by the subterm property
(Lemma
19
) and thus
s^{\prime}\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}u
by
transitivity
(Lemma
18
). Hence

{\triangledown_{\!f}}(s^{\prime}\sigma)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
follows.

•

Suppose
s^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}t^{\prime}
. Since
=_{\mathsf{AC}}
is closed under substitutions, we
have
s^{\prime}\sigma=_{\mathsf{AC}}t^{\prime}\sigma
and thus

{\triangledown_{\!f}}(s^{\prime}\sigma)=_{\mathsf{AC}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t^{\prime}\sigma)
.

It follows that the derivation of
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)

can be simulated, resulting in
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s\sigma)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t\sigma)
.
Hence
s\sigma>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t\sigma
by case (3).

The following technical result is used in the proof that AC-MPO is

Lemma 22
.

If
f\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathsf{AC}}
is minimal in

s=f(s_{1},s_{2})>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t

{\triangledown_{\!f}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!f}}(t)

.

(2)

(3)

.

The relation

.

Incrementality is the final property in Theorem

.

Lemma 24
.

The relation
>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
is incremental.

Proof A.8
.

Let
>
and
\sqsupset
be precedences with
{>}\subseteq{\sqsupset}
.
Suppose
s>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
. We show
s\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
by induction on

|s|+|t|
. We distinguish three cases.

(1)

If
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)\geqslant_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}\{\hskip 0.85358ptt\hskip 0.85358pt\}
then
s^{\prime}=_{\mathsf{AC}}t
or
s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t

for some
s^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
. In the latter case, the induction hypothesis
yields
s^{\prime}\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
. Hence in both cases

s\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
by case (1).

(2)

If
\mathsf{root}(s)>\mathsf{root}(t)
and
\{\hskip 0.85358pts\hskip 0.85358pt\}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
then

\{\hskip 0.85358pts\hskip 0.85358pt\}\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
by the induction hypothesis. Hence,

s\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t
is obtained by case (2).

(3)

Suppose
\mathsf{root}(s)=\mathsf{root}(t)
and
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. Let

s^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
and
t^{\prime}\in{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)
be a
term pair
such that
s^{\prime}>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
is used in
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
. Since

s^{\prime}\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}s
and
t^{\prime}\mathrel{\vartriangleleft}t
, the induction hypothesis yields

s^{\prime}\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}t^{\prime}
. Thus, the derivation of

{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)>_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
can be simulated by
\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}
.
Therefore,
{\triangledown_{\!}}(s)\sqsupset_{\mathsf{acmpo}}^{\mathsf{mul}}{\triangledown_{\!}}(t)
, and hence

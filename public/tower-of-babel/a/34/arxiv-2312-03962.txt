Lyapunov exponents and shear-induced chaos for a Hopf bifurcation with additive noise
Peter H. Baxendale

Abstract
This paper considers the effect of additive white noise on the normal form for the supercritical Hopf bifurcation in 2 dimensions. The main results involve the asymptotic behavior of the top Lyapunov exponent λ \lambda associated with this random dynamical system as one or more of the parameters in the system tend to 0 or ∞ \infty . This enables the construction of a bifurcation diagram in parameter space showing stable regions where λ < 0 \lambda<0 (implying synchronization) and unstable regions where λ > 0 \lambda>0 (implying chaotic behavior). The value of λ \lambda depends strongly on the shearing effect of the twist factor b / a b/a of the deterministic Hopf bifurcation. If b / a b/a is sufficiently small then λ < 0 \lambda<0 regardless of all the other parameters in the system. But when all the parameters except b b are fixed then λ \lambda grows like a positive multiple of b 2 / 3 b^{2/3} as b → ∞ b\to\infty .



Keywords: random dynamical system, Lyapunov exponent, stochastic Hopf bifurcation, shear-induced chaos.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 37H10, 37H15, 60H10, 60J60.

Introduction

Consider the stochastic differential equation (SDE) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
:

dX_{t}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}X_{t}+\|X_{t}\|^{2}\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}X_{t}\right)dt+\sigma\begin{bmatrix}dW_{t}^{1}\\
dW_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}

(1)

for constants
\mu,\omega,a,b
and
\sigma
, where
\{W_{t}^{i}:t\geq 0\}
for
i=1,2
are independent standard Brownian motions. Assume
\sigma>0
, and to ensure the system is recurrent assume
a>0
. The other constants
\mu,\omega,b
can be arbitrary real numbers. Since the noise is additive the Itô and Stratonovich versions of (
1
) agree.

When
\sigma=0
the equation reduces to the normal form for the supercritical Hopf bifurcation with fixed point
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and bifurcation parameter
\mu
. See for example Guckenheimer and Holmes

[
19
]
. If
\mu<0
then the fixed point
0\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
is globally stable, and if
\mu>0
then
0
is unstable and there is stable limit cycle centered at
0
with radius
\sqrt{\mu/a}
. The parameter
a
determines the local stability of the limit cycle and the parameter
b
determines the shearing effect of the system near the limit cycle.

The stochastic system with
\sigma>0
has been studied from a theoretical viewpoint in DeVille, Namachchivaya and Rapti
[
14
]
, Doan, Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
16
]
, Chemnitz and Engel
[
12
]
and Breden and Engel
[
11
]
. See also Wieczorek
[
31
]
in relation to stochastic bifurcation of noise-driven lasers, and Dijkstra, Frankcombe and von der Heydt
[
15
]
and Tantet, Chekroun, Dijkstra and Neelin
[
29
]
in relation to models in oceanography.

We consider how the stability of the system depends on the parameters
\mu
,
\omega
,
a
,
b
and
\sigma
. That is, we wish to describe the stochastic bifurcation scenario. In one dimension, Crauel and Flandoli
[
13
]
show that “Additive noise destroys a pitchfork bifurcation.”
The two-dimensional nature of the Hopf bifurcation makes the stability analysis more complicated. Monotonicity arguments are not available.
Instead, the extra dimension allows the possibility of shearing vector fields, and it turns out that the size of the parameter
b
controlling the strength of the shearing is important in determining the stability of the system.

The term “shear induced chaos” refers to the tendency of shear vector fields to cause chaotic behavior, and this has been well-studied in both the deterministic and stochastic settings.
Of particular relevance is the study of periodically and stochastically “kicked” limit cycles, see Wang and Young
[
30
]
and Lin and Young
[
24
]
, and Lu and Wang and Young
[
25
]
.
When
\mu>0
the deterministic system underlying (
1
) has a stable limit cycle, and the white noise acts as random “kicking” in the two coordinate directions.
Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
17
]
consider a special case of stochastic kicking of a limit cycle, and their formula for the associated Lyapunov exponent plays an important role in the recent result of Chemnitz and Engel
[
12
]
for the system (
1
).

With additive white noise the one-point motion
\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is positive recurrent on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with invariant density with respect to Lebesgue measure of the form

\frac{1}{Z}\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2\sigma^{2}}\bigl(\|X\|^{2}-\mu/a\bigr)^{2}\right)

for some normalizing constant
Z
. The shape of the invariant density varies with
\mu
, and there is a P-bifurcation (see Arnold
[
4
]
) at
\mu=0
, but there is no essential qualitative change in the dynamical behavior of the one-point motion. In this setting it is more interesting to consider the associated random dynamical system, and in particular the issue of stability along trajectories.
Suppose that
\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
and
\{Y_{t}:t\geq 0\}
are both strong solutions of (
1
)
with distinct initial conditions
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
. It is a non-trivial question to determine whether or not

\|Y_{t}-X_{t}\|\to 0
almost as
t\to\infty
. If
\|Y_{t}-X_{t}\|\to 0
almost surely for all distinct
X_{0}
and
Y_{0}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
we say that the system (
1
) has stability along trajectories. In applications involving oscillators (see for example
[
31
,
23
,
27
]
) this is often referred to as synchronization, or phase locking.

In this paper we study the closely related concept of linearized stability along trajectories. Linearizing (
1
) along a trajectory
\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
gives the process
\{U_{t}:t\geq 0\}
satisfying

dU_{t}=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}U_{t}+\|X_{t}\|^{2}\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}U_{t}+2\langle X_{t},U_{t}\rangle\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}X_{t}\right)dt.

(2)

Here
U_{t}
should be regarded as a vector at the point
X_{t}
. Intuitively, if
U_{0}=Y_{0}-X_{0}
and
\|Y_{0}-X_{0}\|
is small then the processes
\{U_{t}:t\geq 0\}
and
\{Y_{t}-X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
should evolve in a
similar manner as long as
\|Y_{t}-X_{t}\|
remains small. The long time rate of exponential growth or decay of the process
\{U_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is given by the (top) Lyapunov exponent

\lambda=\lambda(\mu,\omega,a,b,\sigma)=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log\|U_{t}\|.

(3)

For the system (
1
,
2
) standard arguments show that the limit in (
3
) exists almost surely and takes the same value for all initial points
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
and all non-zero initial vectors
U_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Doan, Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
16
]
show that the system (
1
) gives rise to a random dynamical system in the sense of Arnold
[
4
]
, and they discuss the relationship between the sign of
\lambda
and the nature of the random attractor associated with the random dynamical system. In particular they show, applying a result of Flandoli, Gess and Scheutzow
[
18
]
, that
\lambda<0
for (
1
) implies synchronization, so that the random attractor is almost surely a singleton. In this paper we concentrate on the evaluation of
\lambda
and especially the determination of regions in parameter space
(\mu,\omega,a,b,\sigma)
where
\lambda
is negative (indicating stability along trajectories) or positive (indicating sensitive dependence on initial conditions, and chaos).

Remark 1.1
.

The bifurcation scenario is very different if the additive noise in (
1
) is replaced by multiplicative noise, so that the deterministic fixed point
0
remains fixed under the perturbation. For example, consider the effect of varying the parameter
\mu
in the Stratonovich SDE with linear white noise:

dX_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}X_{t}dt+\|X_{t}\|^{2}\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}X_{t}dt+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}A_{\alpha}X_{t}\circ dW_{t}^{\alpha}

(4)

for some given matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}
(and assume at least one of the
A_{\alpha}
is not skew-symmetric). Linearizing (
4
) at 0 gives

dU_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}U_{t}dt+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}A_{\alpha}U_{t}\circ dW_{t}^{\alpha}.

(5)

Let
-\mu_{c}
denote the top Lyapunov exponent for the SDE

dV_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}0&-\omega\\
\omega&0\end{bmatrix}V_{t}dt+\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}A_{\alpha}V_{t}\circ dW_{t}^{\alpha}

satisfied by
V_{t}=e^{-\mu t}U_{t}
. Then the top Lyapunov exponent for (
5
) is
\lambda=\mu-\mu_{c}
. If
\mu<\mu_{c}
then
0
is a stable fixed point and
X_{t}\to 0
almost surely for all
X_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. But if
\mu>\mu_{c}
then
0
is an unstable fixed point and
X_{t}\to\nu
in distribution for all
X_{0}\neq 0
, where
\nu
is an invariant probability measure for
\{X(t):t\geq 0\}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
. Moreover
\nu
is close to 0 in the sense that for each
p>0
there exists
C_{p}
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}}\|X\|^{p}d\nu(X)\sim C_{p}(\mu-\mu_{c})
as
\mu\searrow\mu_{c}
. This is an application of Baxendale
[
7
, Theorem 2.13]
, which applies also to more general multiplicative noise systems in arbitrary dimensions. The issue of stability along trajectories for the system in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\}
is addressed in Baxendale
[
8
]
.

1.1
Evaluation of
\lambda

The rotational invariance of the system (
1
) simplifies the computation of
\lambda
considerably. The Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula (see Arnold
[
4
]
) gives

\lambda=\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q(r_{s},\psi_{s})ds=\int_{M}Q(r,\psi)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)

(6)

where

\displaystyle Q(r,\psi)

\displaystyle=\mu-2ar^{2}+r^{2}\bigl(b\sin 2\psi-a\cos 2\psi\bigr)

\displaystyle=\mu-ar^{2}+2r^{2}\cos\psi\bigl(b\sin\psi-a\cos\psi\bigr)

(7)

and
\widetilde{\nu}
is the unique invariant probability measure on
M\equiv(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/(\pi\mathbb{Z})
for the diffusion
\{(r_{t},\psi_{t}):t\geq 0\}
with generator
{\cal L}
, say, given by

\displaystyle dr_{t}

\displaystyle=\bigl(\mu r_{t}-ar_{t}^{3}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r_{t}}\bigr)dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{r},

(8)

\displaystyle d\psi_{t}

\displaystyle=2r_{t}^{2}\cos\psi_{t}\bigl(b\cos\psi_{t}+a\sin\psi_{t}\bigr)dt-\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}dW_{t}^{\phi},

(9)

where
\{W_{t}^{r}:t\geq 0\}
and
\{W_{t}^{\phi}:t\geq 0\}
are independent standard Brownian motions. The derivation of these formulas is given in Section
4
. A version with a slightly different parametrization for
\psi
is given in
[
14
, Section 3.1]
.

The rotation parameter
\omega
does not appear in the equations (
7
,
8
,
9
), and so
\lambda
does not depend on
\omega
. The underlying reason for the lack of dependence on
\omega
is that the system (
1
) is equivariant in distribution under the group
SO(2)
of rigid rotations of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Henceforth we write

\lambda=\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)\quad\mbox{where}\quad(\mu,a,b,\sigma)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty).

Also the function
Q(r,\psi)
is invariant under the transformation
(b,\psi)\mapsto(-b,-\psi)
and the system (
8
,
9
) is invariant in distribution under the same transformation. It follows that

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\lambda(\mu,a,-b,\sigma).

(10)

In this case the underlying reason is that the system (
1
) is equivariant in distribution under the reflection
\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
x_{2}\end{bmatrix}\to\begin{bmatrix}x_{1}\\
-x_{2}\end{bmatrix}
. Because of this symmetry we will assume without loss of generality that
b\geq 0
. We note that
[
16
]
and
[
12
]
use
-b
where
[
14
]
and this paper use
b
; happily, because of (
10
) this is not a source of confusion.

There is no explicit formula for the density of
\widetilde{\nu}
, so it is hard to evaluate
\lambda
exactly. However there is an explicit formula (
76
) for the density for the
r
marginal
\nu
, say, of
\widetilde{\nu}
and the methods in this paper will make much use of this fact.
The results in this paper will describe the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda
as one or more of the parameters tends to 0 or infinity. These will lead to results giving regions in parameter space for which
\lambda>0
or
\lambda<0
.

Previous results

2.1
An upper bound on
\lambda

This section contains results in DeVille, Namachchivaya and Rapti
[
14
]
with extensions by Doan, Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
16
]
. The upper bound

Q(r,\psi)\leq\mu+(\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}-2a)r^{2}

(11)

and the explicit formula for
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2}d\nu(r)
given in
[
14
, eqn (26)]
, see (
77
) in the Appendix, together imply

\displaystyle\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\int_{M}Q(r,\psi)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)

\displaystyle<\mu+(\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}-2a)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2}d\nu(r)

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2a\sigma^{2}}J\left(\frac{\mu}{\sqrt{2a\sigma^{2}}},\frac{b}{a}\right)

(12)

where

J(z,b)=z+(-2+\sqrt{1+b^{2}})\left(z+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{e^{-z^{2}}}{{\rm erfc}(-z)}\right).

(13)

The condition
J(z,b)\leq 0
is equivalent to the condition
|b|\leq\widehat{J}(z)
where

\widehat{J}(z)=\sqrt{\dfrac{1}{1+\sqrt{\pi}ze^{z^{2}}{\rm erfc}(-z)}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\sqrt{\pi}ze^{z^{2}}{\rm erfc}(-z)}+2\right)}>0.

(14)

It follows from elementary analysis that
\widehat{J}(z)
is strictly decreasing with
\widehat{J}(z)\sim 2z^{2}
as

and
\widehat{J}(z)\sim 1/\sqrt{\sqrt{\pi}ze^{z^{2}}}
as

. The graph of
\mu\mapsto\widehat{J}(\mu/\sqrt{2})
appears as part of Figure
1
. The inequality (
12
) gives the following result.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
0\leq b\leq a\widehat{J}\left(\dfrac{\mu}{\sqrt{2a\sigma^{2}}}\right)
then
\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)<0
. In particular, given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
and
\sigma>0
there exists
b_{0}>0
such that
\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)<0
whenever
0\leq b\leq b_{0}
.

This includes the result
\lambda(\mu,a,0,\sigma)<0
given by Flandoli, Gess and Scheutzow
[
18
]
.

The upper bound (

2.2
Result of Chemnitz and Engel

Let
\Psi(\zeta)
denote the top Lyapunov exponent for the SDE

dY_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\
\zeta^{1/3}&0\end{bmatrix}Y_{t}dt+\begin{bmatrix}0&\zeta^{1/6}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}Y_{t}dW_{t}.

(15)

Imkeller and Lederer
[
22
]
show that

\Psi(\zeta)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{6}u^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u\right)\right)du}{\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{-1/2}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta}\left(\frac{1}{6}u^{3}-\frac{1}{2}u\right)\right)du}-1\right).

(16)

Engel, Lamb and Rasmussen
[
17
]
show there exists
c_{\ast}>0
such that
\Psi(\zeta)<0
for
0<\zeta<c_{\ast}
and
\Psi(\zeta)>0
for
\zeta>c_{\ast}
. Numerically,
c_{\ast}\approx(0.2823)^{-1}=3.543
.

Theorem 2.1
.

(Chemnitz and Engel
[
12
]
)
Fix
\mu>0
,
a>0
,
b\geq 0
and
\sigma>0
. Then

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\lambda(\mu,a,b/\varepsilon,\varepsilon\sigma)=2\mu\Psi\left(\frac{b^{2}\sigma^{2}}{2\mu^{2}a}\right).

In particular, if
b^{2}\sigma^{2}>2c_{\ast}\mu^{2}a
then
\lambda(\mu,a,b/\varepsilon,\varepsilon\sigma)>0
for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
.

This gives the first proof that
\lambda>0
is possible in this system.

3
Main results

3.1
Scaling

We have already seen that
\lambda
does not depend on
\omega
or the sign of
b
. The next result reduces the effective parameter space to 2 dimensions.

Proposition 3.1
.

For all
A>0
and
B>0
we have

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\frac{1}{A}\lambda\left(A\mu,\frac{Aa}{B^{2}},\frac{Ab}{B^{2}},\sqrt{A}B\sigma\right).

(17)

In particular

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\sigma\sqrt{a}\,\lambda\left(\frac{\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{a}},1,\frac{b}{a},1\right)

(18)

and if
\mu\neq 0
then

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=|\mu|\lambda\left(\frac{\mu}{|\mu|},a,b,\frac{\sigma}{|\mu|}\right).

(19)

Proof.

Rescaling the system (
1
) in time by a factor
A
gives an equivalent system with parameters
(A\mu,Aa,Ab,\sqrt{A}\sigma)
, so that
A\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\lambda(A\mu,Aa,Ab,\sqrt{A}\sigma)
. Also, rescaling the system (
1
) in space by a factor
B
gives an equivalent system with parameters
(\mu,a/B^{2},b/B^{2},B\sigma)
, so that
\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\lambda(\mu,a/B^{2},b/B^{2},B\sigma)
. Together we have (
17
). Taking
A=\sigma\sqrt{a}
and
B=\sqrt{Aa}
gives (
18
), and taking
A=1/|\mu|
and
B=\sqrt{A}
gives (
19
). ∎

It follows from (
18
) that the sign of
\lambda
depends only on the dimensionless ratios
b/a
and
\mu/(\sigma\sqrt{a})
. The ratio
b/a
is the “twist factor” or “twist number” of the deterministic Hopf bifurcation obtained by putting
\sigma=0
in (
1
), see for example
[
30
,
25
,
29
]
. When
\mu>0
it determines the angle of transversality as the deterministic isochrons intersect the limit cycle of radius
\sqrt{\mu/a}
. The interpretation of
\mu/(\sigma\sqrt{a})
is less clear. Adopting the argument in
[
12
]
for the case of positive
\mu>0
and small noise intensity
\sigma
, we have radius
=\sqrt{\mu/a}
, contraction
=2\mu
and effective noise = noise/radius
=\sigma\sqrt{a/\mu}
. Then

\frac{\rm contraction}{({\rm effective\,\,noise})^{2}}=\frac{2\mu}{\sigma^{2}a/\mu}=2\left(\frac{\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{a}}\right)^{2}.

But this interpretation is not valid when
\mu
is negative or when
\sigma
is not small.

3.2

Let

.
Write

\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,

and
\sigma>0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
and

4.1
Adjoint method

The essential idea in the adjoint method, introduced by Arnold, Papanicolaou and Wihstutz
[
5
]
, is to replace the evaluation of the integral
\int_{M}Q(r,\psi)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)
in the Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula by the search for a function
F(r,\psi)
and a constant
\lambda
satisfying
{\cal L}F(r,\psi)=Q(r,\psi)-\lambda
, or equivalently
Q(r,\psi)-{\cal L}F(r,\psi)=\lambda
. Under suitable integrability and growth conditions, see Proposition
B.1
, we have
\int_{M}{\cal L}F(r,\psi)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)=0
and so

\int_{M}Q(r,\psi)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)=\int_{M}\bigl(Q(r,\psi)-{\cal L}F(r,\psi)\bigr)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)=\int_{M}\lambda\,d\widetilde{\nu}(r,\psi)=\lambda.

When applying the method it is often impossible to find a function
F
for which
Q(r,\psi)-{\cal L}F(r,\psi)
is constant, but it is frequently possible to find a function
F
for which
Q(r,\psi)-{\cal L}F(r,\psi)
is easier to analyze than the original
Q(r,\psi)
. The proof of Theorem
3.1
uses the adjoint method twice. The first step uses a function
F
motivated by the heuristic argument in Section
5.1
to exhibit the
b^{2/3}
growth rate, see Proposition
6.1
, and the second step uses a function
G
to remove the
\psi
dependence from the problem, see Lemma
6.5
and Proposition
6.2
. The proof of Theorem
3.4
uses a very similar two step method, starting with exactly the same
F
as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
. In contrast, the proof of Theorem
3.3
starts in a more conventional manner, looking for an asymptotic expansion
F(r,\psi)=F_{0}(r,\psi)+\sigma^{2}F_{2}(r,\psi)+\cdots
in powers of the small parameter
\sigma^{2}
. The novelty here is in the construction of the functions
F_{0}
and
F_{2}
. Unlike the original setting of
[
5
]
involving an elliptic operator on a compact state space, the equations (
65
,
66
) for
F_{0}
and
F_{2}
involve a first order differential operator on the non-compact space
M=(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}/(\pi\mathbb{Z})
. The functions
F_{0}
and
F_{2}
have singularities at the hyperbolic fixed point of the corresponding vector field, and the proof requires an
a priori
estimate on the invariant probability measure
\widetilde{\nu}
near this fixed point as
\sigma\to 0
, see Lemma
9.2
.

Heuristic arguments

Before presenting the rigorous proofs in Section
6
onwards, we give here some heuristic arguments for the results in Theorems
3.1
-
3.4
. These are seen most easily when the derivative process
U_{t}
is written in terms of the rotating frame given by the unit radial and tangential vectors
\begin{bmatrix}\cos\phi_{t}\\
\sin\phi_{t}\end{bmatrix}
and
\begin{bmatrix}-\sin\phi_{t}\\
\cos\phi_{t}\end{bmatrix}
at the point
X_{t}
.
For
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
define
R_{\tau}=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\tau&-\sin\tau\\
\sin\tau&\cos\tau\end{bmatrix}
, corresponding to rotation through the angle
\tau
. Note that
R^{\prime}_{\tau}=JR_{\tau}
where
J=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
.
The components of
U_{t}
relative to the rotating frame are given by the vector
V_{t}
, say, where

U_{t}=R_{\phi_{t}}V_{t}.

So
V_{t}=R_{-\phi_{t}}U_{t}
and then

\displaystyle dV_{t}

\displaystyle=R_{-\phi_{t}}dU_{t}-JR_{-\phi_{t}}U_{t}d\phi_{t}-\frac{1}{2}R_{-\phi_{t}}U_{t}(d\phi_{t})^{2}

\displaystyle=R_{-\phi_{t}}\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}+r_{t}^{2}\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2+\cos 2\phi_{t}&\sin 2\phi_{t}\\
\sin 2\phi_{t}&2-\cos 2\phi_{t}\end{bmatrix}\right)R_{\phi_{t}}V_{t}dt

\displaystyle\quad-(\omega+br_{t}^{2})JV_{t}dt-\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}JV_{t}dW_{t}^{\phi}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r_{t}^{2}}V_{t}dt.

(31)

Since

R_{-\phi_{t}}\begin{bmatrix}\cos 2\phi_{t}&\sin 2\phi_{t}\\
\sin 2\phi_{t}&-\cos 2\phi_{t}\end{bmatrix}R_{\phi_{t}}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}\mbox{ and }R_{-\phi_{t}}J\begin{bmatrix}\cos 2\phi_{t}&\sin 2\phi_{t}\\
\sin 2\phi_{t}&-\cos 2\phi_{t}\end{bmatrix}R_{\phi_{t}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}

the right side of (
31
) simplifies and we get

\displaystyle dV_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{bmatrix}\mu&-\omega\\
\omega&\mu\end{bmatrix}+2r_{t}^{2}\begin{bmatrix}-a&-b\\
b&-a\end{bmatrix}-ar_{t}^{2}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{bmatrix}+br_{t}^{2}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}\right)V_{t}dt

\displaystyle\quad-(\omega+br_{t}^{2})\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}V_{t}dt-\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}V_{t}dW_{t}^{\phi}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r_{t}^{2}}V_{t}dt

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mu-3ar_{t}^{2}-\sigma^{2}/2r_{t}^{2}&0\\
2br_{t}^{2}&\mu-ar_{t}^{2}-\sigma^{2}/2r_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}V_{t}dt+\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{bmatrix}V_{t}dW_{t}^{\phi}.

(32)

This equation appears in Stratonovich form as
[
12
, eqn (2.12)]
.

Remark 5.1
.

Since
V_{t}
is obtained from
U_{t}

. Applying the methods of equations (

,
27
) to the Itô SDE for
V_{t}
in place of the ordinary differential equation for
U_{t}
gives an alternative method for deriving the function
Q(r,\psi)
and the equation (
9
) for
\{\psi_{t}:t\geq 0\}
. The calculation is a direct application of Itô’s formula, and is similar to those carried out for Stratonovich versions in
[
22
, eqns (5,6)]
and
[
12
, Lemma 3.2]
.

5.1
Heuristic argument for Theorem
3.1

Fix
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
a>0
and
\sigma>0
. As
b\to\infty
we see the emergence of a nilpotent structure in the drift matrix of (
32
), and this suggests the transformation used by Pinsky and Wihstutz
[
28
]
for nilpotent linear SDEs. In a linear SDE with constant coefficients we would consider
\widetilde{V}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}A&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}V_{t}
where
A=Bb^{1/3}
for a suitable constant
B
. This gives

\displaystyle d\widetilde{V}_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}\mu-3ar_{t}^{2}-\sigma^{2}/2r_{t}^{2}&0\\
2b^{2/3}r_{t}^{2}/B&\mu-ar_{t}^{2}-\sigma^{2}/2r_{t}^{2}\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{t}dt

\displaystyle\hskip 107.63855pt+\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}\begin{bmatrix}0&Bb^{1/3}\\
-1/(Bb^{1/3})&0\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{t}dW_{t}^{\phi}

(33)

\displaystyle=b^{2/3}\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
2r_{t}^{2}/B&0\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{t}dt+b^{1/3}\begin{bmatrix}0&B\sigma/r_{t}\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}\widetilde{V}_{t}dW_{t}^{\phi}

\displaystyle\qquad+\mbox{ terms of lower order in }b\mbox{ as }b\to\infty.

(34)

Recall

dr_{t}=\bigl(\mu r_{t}-ar_{t}^{3}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r_{t}}\bigr)dt+\sigma dW_{t}^{r}.

We see a separation of time scales:
\widetilde{V}_{t}
moves at a rate proportional to
b^{2/3}
whereas
r_{t}
moves at rate independent of
b
. Proceeding under the heuristic that the fast motion in (
34
) can be analysed while treating
r_{t}
as a constant, we choose
B
so that
2r^{2}/B=(B\sigma/r)^{2}
. This gives
B=(2r^{4}/\sigma^{2})^{1/3}
and then two leading terms in (
34
) correspond to a time changed version of the equation (
20
):

dY_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}Y_{t}dt+\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}Y_{t}dW_{t}

with time change factor
b^{2/3}\times(2r^{2}/B)=(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}
. The time change gives a Lyapunov exponent
(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}\gamma_{0}
. This calculation was carried out under the assumption that
r
is fixed. The final stage in this heuristic is to average over
r
with respect to the invariant measure
\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}
. Thus we would get

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)\sim(2b\sigma)^{2/3}\gamma_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2/3}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)

as
b\to\infty
. However this is non-rigorous. The fact that
B
is a function of
r=r_{t}
introduces extra terms into (
33
) and hence extra lower order terms in (
34
). In the setting of the Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula, there are lower order terms arising from the
Q
function, and there are lower order terms in the generator
{\cal L}
. All of these remainder terms need to be controlled, but the analysis of the remainder terms involves functions of
r
which are not integrable near 0. Instead of pursuing this idea directly, we use it as motivation for the proof of Theorem
3.1
in Section
6
.

More precisely, if we make the change from
V
to
\widetilde{V}=\begin{bmatrix}A(r)&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}V
with
A(r)=(2br^{4}/\sigma^{2})^{1/3}
and then apply the method of Khasminskii to the SDE for
\widetilde{V}_{t}
then the integrand in the Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula changes from

Q(r,\psi)
to

Q(r,\psi)+{\cal L}\log\left(\frac{\|\widetilde{V}\|}{\|V\|}\right)=Q(r,\psi)+\frac{1}{2}{\cal L}\Bigl(\log\bigl(A(r)^{2}\cos^{2}\psi+\sin^{2}\psi\bigr)\Bigr).

However the function
(r,\psi)\mapsto\log\bigl(A(r)^{2}\cos^{2}\psi+\sin^{2}\psi\bigr)
blows up as
r\to 0
and
\psi\to 0
. In Section
6
we keep the original Furstenberg-Khasminskii formula (
6
), and then use a variation of the adjoint method by replacing
Q(r,\psi)
with
Q(r,\psi)-{\cal L}F(r,\psi)
where the function

F(r,\psi)=-\frac{1}{2}\log\bigl(A(r)^{2}\cos^{2}\psi+1\bigr)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+\tan^{2}\psi}{A(r)^{2}+1+\tan^{2}\psi}\right)

is better behaved as
r\to 0
. The change of variable from
z=\tan\psi
to
w=z/A(r)
used in Section
6
corresponds exactly to the change from
V
to
\widetilde{V}=\begin{bmatrix}A(r)&0\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}V
.

\displaystyle\qquad+\mbox{ terms with a factor }(r_{t}-\sqrt{\mu/a})\,\,+\mbox{ terms of lower order in }\sigma.

(35)

The two leading terms in (

) correspond to a time changed version of the equation (

):

As

The top Lyapunov exponent for (

) is

. There is no proof here that approximation

6
Proof of Theorem
3.1

6.1
Tangent substitution

Following the methods of Imkeller and Lederer
[
21
,
22
]
we reparametrize the set
\mathbb{R}/(\pi\mathbb{Z})
using the new variable
z=\tan\psi
. Thus
z
takes values in the set
\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
consisting of the extended real line
[-\infty,\infty]
with the points
-\infty
and
\infty
identified. The advantage of this reparametrization is that trigonometric functions in
\psi
are replaced by rational functions of
z
. Writing
z_{t}=\tan\psi_{t}
we have

\displaystyle dz_{t}

\displaystyle=\sec^{2}\psi_{t}\,d\psi_{t}+\frac{1}{2}(2\sec\psi_{t})(\sec\psi_{t}\tan\psi_{t})(d\psi_{t})^{2}

\displaystyle=\sec^{2}\psi_{t}\left(2r_{t}^{2}\cos\psi_{t}(b\cos\psi_{t}+a\sin\psi_{t})dt-\frac{\sigma}{r_{t}}dW_{t}^{\phi}\right)+\sec^{2}\psi_{t}\tan\psi_{t}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}}{r_{t}^{2}}\right)dt

\displaystyle=\left(2r_{t}^{2}(b+az_{t})+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{r_{t}^{2}}(1+z_{t}^{2})z_{t}\right)dt-\frac{\sigma}{r}(1+z_{t}^{2})\,dW_{t}^{\phi}.

(39)

The behavior of
z_{t}
near
\pm\infty
is determined by the behavior of
1/z_{t}
near 0, and the SDE for
1/z_{t}
can be obtained from (
39
) by Itô’s formula.

We will abuse notation by writing
Q(r,z)
in place of
Q(r,\psi)
and by using
\widetilde{\nu}
to denote the unique invariant measure for the process
\{(r_{t},z_{t}):t\geq 0\}
with values in
(0,\infty)\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
given by (
8
,
39
). Thus

\lambda(\mu,a,b,\sigma)=\int_{M}Q(r,z)d\widetilde{\nu}(r,z)

where now
M=(0,\infty)\times\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
and

Q(r,z)=\mu-ar^{2}+r^{2}\Bigl(b\sin 2\psi-a(1+\cos 2\psi)\Bigr)=\mu-ar^{2}+2r^{2}\left(\frac{bz}{1+z^{2}}-\frac{a}{1+z^{2}}\right).

We will also abuse notation by letting
{\cal L}
denote the generator for the process
\{(r_{t},z_{t}):t\geq 0\}
. It may be written
{\cal L}={\cal L}_{r}+{\cal L}_{z}
where

{\cal L}_{r}=\left(\mu r-ar^{3}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial r}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial r^{2}}

and

{\cal L}_{z}=\left(r^{2}(2b+2az)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{r^{2}}(1+z^{2})z\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r^{2}}(1+z^{2})^{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial z^{2}}.

6.2
First application of the adjoint method

Here our choice of the function
F
and the variable
w=z/A(r)
is based on the heuristic argument in Section
5.1
.

Definition.
For a function
A=A(r)
to be chosen later, define

F(r,z)=\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\frac{1+z^{2}}{A^{2}+1+z^{2}}\right).

(40)

Note that we can treat
A
as a constant for the purpose of calculating
{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

\displaystyle Q(r,z)-{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2br^{2}z}{A^{2}+1+z^{2}}-\frac{A^{2}\sigma^{2}(1+z^{2})(A^{2}+1-z^{2})}{2r^{2}(A^{2}+1+z^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu-ar^{2}-2ar^{2}\frac{A^{2}+1}{A^{2}+1+z^{2}}.

(41)

Proof.
This is a direct calculation. We omit the details. ∎

Writing
w=z/A
in the right side of (
41
) gives

\displaystyle Q(r,z)-{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{2br^{2}}{A}\frac{w}{(1+A^{-2}+w^{2})}-\frac{\sigma^{2}A^{2}}{r^{2}}\frac{(A^{-2}+w^{2})(1+A^{-2}-w^{2})}{2(1+A^{-2}+w^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\mu-ar^{2}-2ar^{2}\frac{1+A^{-2}}{(1+A^{-2}+w^{2})}.

(42)

At this point we make the choice that

2br^{2}/A=\sigma^{2}A^{2}/r^{2}
, so that

A=A(r)=\left(\frac{2br^{4}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{1/3}.

Then
2br^{2}/A=\sigma^{2}A^{2}/r^{2}=(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}
, and equation (
42
) becomes

\displaystyle Q(r,z)-{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)

\displaystyle=(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}\left(\frac{w}{1+A^{-2}+w^{2}}-\frac{(A^{-2}+w^{2})(1+A^{-2}-w^{2})}{2(1+A^{-2}+w^{2})^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+\mu-ar^{2}-2ar^{2}\frac{1+A^{-2}}{(1+A^{-2}+w^{2})}.

(43)

Note that
A
is now an explicit function of
r
, and that
A\to\infty
as
b\to\infty
for each
r>0
but
A\to 0
as
r\to 0
for each
b
. The next two results quantify the effect of the
A^{-2}
terms in the right side of (
43
) and bound the contribution of
{\cal L}_{r}F(r,z)
towards
{\cal L}F(r,z)
.

Definition.
For
w\in\widehat{\mathbb{R}}
, define

\Phi(w)=\frac{w}{1+w^{2}}-\frac{w^{2}(1-w^{2})}{2(1+w^{2})^{2}}.

Note that

|\Phi(w)|\leq\frac{|w|}{1+w^{2}}+\frac{w^{2}|1-w^{2}|}{2(1+w^{2})^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}=1.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume
b>0
. With
A(r)=(2br^{4}/\sigma^{2})^{1/3}
and
w=z/A(r)
we have

\left|Q(r,z)-{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)-(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}\Phi(w)+\frac{2ar^{2}}{1+w^{2}}\right|\leq\frac{3\sigma(2b\sigma)^{1/3}}{4r^{2/3}}+\frac{a\sigma r^{2/3}}{(2b\sigma)^{1/3}}+|\mu-ar^{2}|.

(44)

Proof.
Notice first that
1+\delta+w^{2}\geq 1+\delta\geq 2\sqrt{\delta}
, so that
\delta/(1+\delta+w^{2})\leq\sqrt{\delta}/2
. Then

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(\delta+w^{2})(1+\delta-w^{2})}{2(1+\delta+w^{2})^{2}}-\frac{w^{2}(1-w^{2})}{2(1+w^{2})^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta(1+w^{4}+2w^{6})+\delta^{2}(1+w^{2}+2w^{4})}{2(1+\delta+w^{2})^{2}(1+w^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\delta(1+w^{2})^{3}+\delta^{2}(1+w^{2})^{2}}{(1+\delta+w^{2})^{2}(1+w^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta}{1+\delta+w^{2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2}.

Similarly

\left|\frac{w}{1+\delta+w^{2}}-\frac{w}{1+w^{2}}\right|=\frac{\delta|w|}{(1+\delta+w^{2})(1+w^{2})}\leq\frac{\delta}{2(1+\delta+w^{2})}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{4}

and

\left|\frac{1+\delta}{1+\delta+w^{2}}-\frac{1}{1+w^{2}}\right|=\left|\frac{\delta w^{2}}{(1+\delta+w^{2})(1+w^{2})}\right|\leq\frac{\delta}{1+\delta+w^{2}}\leq\frac{\sqrt{\delta}}{2}.

Using these inequalities with
\delta=A(r)^{-2}
in (
43
) gives

\displaystyle\left|Q(r,z)-{\cal L}_{z}F(r,z)-(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}\Phi(w)+\frac{2ar^{2}}{1+w^{2}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq(2b\sigma r)^{2/3}\left(\frac{1}{4A(r)}+\frac{1}{2A(r)}\right)+2ar^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2A(r)}\right)+|\mu-ar^{2}|,

and (
44
) follows directly. ∎

Lemma 6.3
.

With
A(r)=(2br^{4}/\sigma^{2})^{1/3}
we have

|{\cal L}_{r}F(r,z)|\leq\frac{4}{3}|\mu-ar^{2}|+\frac{8\sigma(2b\sigma)^{1/3}}{9r^{2/3}}.

(45)

Proof.
We have

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{r}F(r,z)

\displaystyle=\left(\mu r-ar^{3}+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r}\right)\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}(r,z)+\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\cdot\frac{\partial^{2}F}{\partial r^{2}}(r,z)

\displaystyle=-(\mu r-ar^{3})\frac{A}{(A^{2}+1+z^{2})}\frac{\partial A}{\partial r}-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2r}\cdot\frac{A}{(A^{2}+1+z^{2})}\frac{\partial A}{\partial r}

\mu>0
and
a>0
and
\sigma_{0}>0
. Then the integrals

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{-2/3}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)
are bounded above and the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2/3}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)

is bounded away from
0
for
0<\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}
.

Proof.
For
\mu>0
we have
\mbox{erfc}(-\mu/\sqrt{2a\sigma^{2}})>1
. Using (
77
)we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)\leq\frac{\mu}{a}+\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\pi a}}\leq\frac{\mu}{a}+\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma_{0}^{2}}{\pi a}}

for all
\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}
. Next, if
r\leq\sqrt{\mu/2a}
then

\rho(r)\leq\frac{2r\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[r^{2}-\frac{\mu}{a}\right]^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi\sigma^{2}}{2a}}}\leq\frac{2r}{\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2a}}}\cdot\frac{e^{-\mu^{2}/8a\sigma^{2}}}{\sigma}\leq kr

for all
\sigma>0
, where
k
is a constant depending on
\mu
and
a
. Therefore for all
\sigma>0

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{-2/3}d\nu_{\mu,a,\sigma}(r)\leq\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\mu/2a}}r^{-2/3}d\nu_{\mu,a,\sigma}(r)+\left(\frac{2a}{\mu}\right)^{1/3}\leq k\int_{0}^{\sqrt{\mu/2a}}r^{1/3}dr+\left(\frac{2a}{\mu}\right)^{1/3}.

Finally since
\mbox{erfc}(-\mu/\sqrt{2a\sigma^{2}})<2
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}r^{2/3}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\sqrt{\mu/a}}^{\infty}r^{2/3}\cdot\frac{2r\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[r^{2}-\frac{\mu}{a}\right]^{2}\right)}{2\sqrt{\frac{\pi\sigma^{2}}{2a}}}dr

\displaystyle=\int_{\mu/a}^{\infty}s^{1/3}\cdot\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[s-\frac{\mu}{a}\right]^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}/a}}ds

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{\mu}{a}\right)^{1/3}\int_{\mu/a}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left(-\frac{a}{2\sigma^{2}}\left[s-\frac{\mu}{a}\right]^{2}\right)}{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^{2}/a}}ds=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\mu}{a}\right)^{1/3},

and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
\mu>0
. For all measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(r^{2})d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)\leq 2\mathbb{E}[f(\chi)]

(78)

where
\chi
is a normal random variable with mean
\mu/a
and variance
\sigma^{2}/a
. In particular

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left(r^{2}-\frac{\mu}{a}\right)^{2}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)\leq\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{a}

(79)

and

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{\gamma(r^{2}-\mu/a)^{2}}d\nu_{(\mu,a,\sigma)}(r)\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{1-2\gamma\sigma^{2}/a}}

(80)

for all
\gamma<a/(2\sigma^{2})
.

Proof.
The inequality (
78
) is proved by Chemnitz and Engel
[
12
, Lemma 3.11]
, and then (
79
) and (
80
) use standard facts about the normal random variable. ∎

Appendix B
Estimates involving
\int_{M}{\cal L}F(x)dm(x)

Suppose
{\cal L}
is the generator of a diffusion process on a manifold
M
with invariant probability measure
m
and let
F\in C^{2}(M)
. It is not always true that
\int_{M}{\cal L}F(x)dm(x)=0
. For example let
{\cal L}_{r}
be the generator for
\{r_{t}:t\geq 0\}
given by (
8
) above and let
F(r)=\log r
. Then
{\cal L}F(r)=\mu-ar^{2}
, but
\int r^{2}d\nu(r)\neq\mu/a
, see (
77
) above. In order to apply the adjoint method on a non-compact space we will use the following strengthening of
[
9
, Prop 3]
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
\{X_{t}:\ t\geq 0\}
be a non-explosive
diffusion process on a
\sigma
-compact manifold
M
with
invariant probability measure
m
. Let
{\cal L}
be an operator acting
on
C^{2}(M)
functions that agrees with the generator of
\{X_{t}:\ t\geq 0\}
on
C^{2}
functions with compact support. Let
F\in C^{2}(M)
. Suppose
F
and
{\cal L}F
are
m
integrable. Suppose also there exists a positive
V\in C^{2}(M)
satisfying

{\cal L}V(x)\leq kV(x)
for some
k<\infty
such that
|F(x)|/V(x)\to 0
as
|F(x)|\to\infty
. Then

\int_{M}{\cal L}F(x)\,dm(x)=0.

Proof.
Let
\mathbb{P}^{x}
denote the law of the process

\{X_{t}:t\geq 0\}
with
X_{0}=x
. Itô’s formula implies that

N_{t}:=F(X_{t})-F(x)-\int_{0}^{t}{\cal L}F(X_{s})\,ds

is a local martingale with respect to the measure
\mathbb{P}^{x}
for all
x\in M
. More precisely, if
\{K_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence
of compact subsets of
M
such that
K_{n}\nearrow M
and

\delta_{n}=\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\ X_{t}\notin K_{n}\right\}
, then

\delta_{n}\nearrow\infty
and
N_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}
is a
martingale with respect to the measure
\mathbb{P}^{x}
for all
x\in M
and
all
n
. In particular

\mathbb{E}^{x}(F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}))-F(x)=\mathbb{E}^{x}\left(\int_{0}^{t\wedge\delta_{n}}{\cal L}F(X_{s})ds\right).

(81)

A similar calculation applied to the function
e^{-kt}V(y)
shows
that
e^{-k(t\wedge\delta_{n})}V(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}})
is a

\mathbb{P}^{x}
supermartingale for all
x\in M
and all
n
. It follows
that

\mathbb{E}^{x}(V(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}))\leq e^{kt}V(x).

Given
\varepsilon>0
choose
c
so that
|F(y)|\geq c
implies
|F(y)|/V(y)<\varepsilon
. Then

\mathbb{E}^{x}\left(|F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}})|1_{|F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}})|\geq c}\right)\leq\varepsilon\mathbb{E}^{x}(V(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}))\leq\varepsilon e^{kt}V(x).

Thus for each
t
and
x
the family
\{F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}):n\geq 1\}
is
\mathbb{P}^{x}
uniformly integrable, and so

\mathbb{E}^{x}(F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}))\to\mathbb{E}^{x}(\lim_{n\to\infty}F(X_{t\wedge\delta_{n}}))=\mathbb{E}^{x}(F(X_{t})).

Also

\left|\int_{0}^{t\wedge\delta_{n}}{\cal L}F(X_{s})ds\right|\leq\int_{0}^{t}|{\cal L}F(X_{s})|ds

and, using Fubini’s theorem and the invariance of
m
,

\int_{M}\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{t}|{\cal L}F(X_{s})|ds\right)dm(x)=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{M}\bigl(\mathbb{E}^{x}|{\cal L}F(X_{s})|\bigr)dm(x)\right)ds=t\int_{M}|{\cal L}F(x)|dm(x)<\infty

for each
t>0
. For fixed
t
there exists a measurable subset
M_{0}\subset M
with
m(M_{0})=1
such that

\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{t}|{\cal L}F(X_{s})|ds<\infty

for all
x\in M_{0}
. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
81
) and using uniform integrability on the left and the dominated convergence theorem on the right, we get

E^{x}(F(X_{t}))-F(x)=E^{x}\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\cal L}F(X_{s})ds\right)\quad\mbox{ for all }x\in M_{0}.

Integrating with respect to
m
and using the invariance of
m
again we obtain

0=\int_{M}F(x)\,dm(x)-\int_{M}F(x)\,dm(x)=t\int_{M}{\cal L}F(x)\,dm(x)

and the proof is complete.

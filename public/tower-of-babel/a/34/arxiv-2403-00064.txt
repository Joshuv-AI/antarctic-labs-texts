Splitting links by integer homology spheres
Marco Golla, Marco Marengon

Abstract
. For every n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 , we construct 2-component links in S n + 1 S^{n+1} that are split by an integer homology n n -sphere, but not by S n S^{n} . In the special case n = 3 n=3 , i.e. that of 2-links in S 4 S^{4} , we produce an infinite family of links L ℓ L_{\ell} and of integer homology spheres Y ℓ Y_{\ell} such that the link L ℓ L_{\ell} is (topologically or smoothly) split by Y ℓ Y_{\ell} and by no other integer homology sphere in the family.



1.
Introduction

A link in
S^{n+1}
is the image of an embedding of a disjoint union of
(n-1)
-spheres, which we consider up to ambient isotopy.
We recall that a link is called (
smoothly
or
topologically
)
split
if there is a (smooth or locally-flat) embedding of
S^{n}
in
S^{n+1}
that separates the link, i.e. each component of
S^{n+1}\setminus S^{n}
contains at least one component of the link.
More generally, given a closed
n
-manifold
Y
, we say that a link in
S^{n+1}
is (
smoothly
or
topologically
)
split by
Y
if there exists a (smooth or locally-flat) embedding of
Y
in
S^{n+1}
that separates the link.

Moreover, we call links which are (smoothly or topologically) split by an integer homology sphere (smoothly or topologically)
integrally split
.
In this paper we show that there exist integrally split links which are not split by the standard
n
-sphere, answering a question raised (in the case
n=3
) by Peter Kronheimer during Maggie Miller’s talk at the Kronheimer birthday conference in Oxford.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
n\geq 3
, there exists a
2
-component link
L
in
S^{n+1}
which is smoothly integrally split, but not topologically split by
S^{n}
.

We note that in the case
n=2
(i.e. the case of classical links in
S^{3}
) the generalised notion of “split by
Y
” is not very useful, because every link with at least
2
components is separated by a torus, which is the simplest possible surface after a 2-sphere.
On the other hand, such a notion is highly non-trivial whenever
n\geq 3
.
A simple homological argument provides an obstruction to being split by a rational homology sphere: if
L
is split by a rational homology sphere, then the (single-variable) Alexander polynomial vanishes (see Remark
2.5
).
Homological arguments can also be used to produce examples of triples
(L,Y,Z)
, where
Y
and
Z
are rational homology spheres and
L
is a link split by
Y
but not by
Z
(see Remark
2.4
; these examples have
H_{1}(Y)\neq H_{1}(Z)
).
Exhibiting such triples where
Y
and
Z
are
integer
homology spheres, as in Theorem
1.1
, is more subtle, and requires arguments that go beyond homology.

We also remark (see Remark
3.2
) that there is no real hope to strengthen Theorem
1.1
by splitting a non-split link by a homotopy sphere: in higher dimensions, there are no embeddings at all of non-trivial homotopy spheres into
S^{n+1}

[
Sma62
]
, and in lower dimensions the only non-trivial case would be when
n=4
, but the existence of non-trivial homotopy
4
-spheres is still open.

While Theorem
1.1
states the existence of
one
link for every
n\geq 3
, we will actually exhibits
infinitely many
integrally split links that are not split by
S^{n}
. Moreover, in the case
n=3
we can produce an infinite family of links and an infinite family of integer homology
3
-spheres such that each link in the family is split by exactly one such integer homology
3
-sphere.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exist an infinite family
\{L_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
of
2
-component links in
S^{4}
and an infinite family
\{Y_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
of integer homology
3
-spheres such that
L_{k}
is smoothly split by
Y_{k}
but not topologically split by any other
Y_{\ell}
.

The proofs of both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
is based on a flexible construction of 2-component links
L
in
S^{n+1}
which are split by an integer homology
n
-sphere
Y
such that the double branched cover
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is a mapping torus on the same
Y
.

In terms of regularity (smooth vs topological), Theorem
1.2
is already the best possible one (the construction is smooth, the obstruction is topological). Nonetheless, the topological obstruction from Theorem
1.2
is quite specific to a certain family of integer homology spheres
\{Y_{\ell}\}_{\ell\in\mathbb{N}}
, so we also prove a smooth obstruction (using Heegaard Floer homology
[
OS04b
]
) which applies more broadly.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
L
be a
2
-component link in
S^{4}
which is smoothly split by an integer homology
3
-sphere
Y
, and such that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{4},L)
is a mapping torus on
Y
.
If
L
is smoothly split also by an integer homology
3
-sphere
Z
, then
\HF_{\mathrm{red}}(Y)
is a direct summand of
\HF_{\mathrm{red}}(Z)
.

We use Theorem
1.3
to produce another infinite family
\{L^{\prime}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
of 2-component links in
S^{4}
and another infinite family
\{Y^{\prime}_{\ell}\}_{\ell\geq 1}
of
\mathbb{Z}HS^{3}
such that
L^{\prime}_{k}
is smoothly split by
Y^{\prime}_{k}
but not by any other
Y^{\prime}_{\ell}
(see Proposition
5.1
).

Ideally we would like to apply the obstruction from Theorem
1.3
to a case when we know that the link is topologically split, thus detecting exotic splitness. Therefore, we conclude with the following questions.

Question 1.4
.

Does there exist a
2
-component link in
S^{4}
that is topologically but not smoothly (integrally) split?

Question 1.5
.

Given an integer homology sphere
Y
, does there exist a
2
-component link in
S^{4}
that is topologically but not smoothly split by
Y
?

Organisation

In Section
2
we present a flexible construction of 2-component links
L
in
S^{n+1}
which are smoothly split by an integer homology sphere
Y
. By our construction, the double branched cover
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is a mapping torus on
Y
.
In Section
3
we show that none of such links are split by
S^{n}
, and we provide infinitely many explicit examples, hence proving Theorem
1.1
.
In Section
4
we prove Theorem
1.2
, and in Section
5
we prove the smooth obstruction (Theorem
1.3
). We conclude with an appendix recapping some well-known properties of Heegaard Floer homology.

Proposition 2.1
.

For every knot

, there is an open book decomposition of

\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\setminus B^{n}.

Sp_{1}(K)\subset S^{n+1}

By

Thus, the double cover
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},Sp_{1}(K))

S^{n+1}

S^{n+1}

Moreover, we can lift the trivial open book decomposition to the double branched cover, thus obtaining a new open book decomposition of
S^{n-1}
.
Its page
P_{\vartheta}
is the double cover of
B^{n}_{\vartheta}
branched over (a tangle whose closure is)
K

\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\setminus B^{n}

From such an open book decomposition we deduce the existence of an integrally split 2-component link, as in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.2
.

For every knot
K
, there exists a
2
-component link
L_{K}
in
S^{n+1}
which is split by the
n
-manifold

Y_{K}:=\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\#\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},m(K)),

and such that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L_{K})
is a mapping torus on
Y_{K}
.

Here and below,
m(K)
denotes the mirror of the knot
K
, and therefore
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},m(K))
is diffeomorphic to
-\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)
.

Proof.

Consider the open book decomposition of
S^{n+1}
from Proposition
2.1
, and let
S
denote its binding.
By gluing together two opposite pages
P_{\vartheta}
and
P_{-\vartheta}
along their common boundary
S
, we obtain an
n
-manifold

Y_{K}:=\Sigma_{2}(K\#m(K))=\Sigma_{2}(K)\#\Sigma_{2}(m(K)),

which splits
S^{n+1}
in two diffeomorphic parts.
That is, we have exhibited a splitting of
S^{n+1}
by
Y_{K}
, i.e.
S^{n+1}=W^{+}_{K}\cup_{Y_{K}}W^{-}_{K}
.
See Figure
1
.

We define a link
L_{K}\subset S^{n+1}
by taking the disjoint union of two push-offs of the binding
S
, one in each normal direction to
Y_{K}
. Call these two
(n-1)
-spheres

S^{+}_{K}\subset W^{+}_{K}
and
S^{-}_{K}\subset W^{-}_{K}
. Note that in this way, by construction, the link is split by
Y_{K}
.

We now show that the double cover
p\colon\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L_{K})\to S^{n+1}
of
S^{n+1}
branched over
L_{K}
is a mapping torus on
Y_{K}
. In the remaining part of the proof we drop the subscript
K
for ease of notation.

Note that
S^{n+1}
can be viewed as the union of a regular neighbourhood
N\cong D^{2}\times S
of
S
containing
L
(this is possible since each component of
L
is a push-off of
S
) and the exterior
E
of
S
, which is a mapping torus on
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\setminus B^{n}
.

Since each loop in
E
winding once around
S
loops twice around
L
, the restriction of the double cover
p
to
E
is the (trivial) disconnected cover. On the other hand, since
N\cong D^{2}\times S
, the double cover of
N
is the product of the identity of
S
and the double cover of
D^{2}
branched over two points, so
p^{-1}(N)
is the product of
S^{n-1}
and an annulus.

Thus,
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is obtained by gluing the two copies of
E
to the two boundary components of
(I\times S^{1})\times S^{n-1}
, in such a way that the
S^{n-1}
-fibration induced from the mapping torus
E
on its boundary
\partial E
is identified with the standard
S^{n-1}
-fibration of
S^{1}\times S^{n-1}
, viewed in the boundary of
(I\times S^{1})\times S^{n-1}
.

It follows that the manifold
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is also a mapping torus. More precisely, since
E
is the mapping torus of a self-diffeomorphism of
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\setminus B^{n}
, it follows that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is the mapping torus of a self-diffeomorphism of
Y
.
∎

From Proposition
2.2
we also obtain an obstruction to the link being split by another integer homology sphere
Z
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
L
be a
2
-component link in
S^{n+1}
which is (smoothly or topologically) split by an integer homology
n
-sphere
Y
, and such that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is a mapping torus on
Y
.

If
L
is (smoothly or topologically) split by another integer homology
n
-sphere
Z
, then there is a (smooth or topological) invertible cobordism from
Y
to
Z
.

Recall that a cobordism
W
from
Y
to
Z
is called
invertible
if there is another cobordism
W^{\prime}
from
Z
to
Y
such that the composition
W^{\prime}\circ W
is isotopic to
I\times Y
relative to the boundary. Note that if
Y
and
Z
are integer homology spheres, then both
W
and
W^{\prime}
must necessarily be integer homology cobordisms.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a (smooth or topological) embedding of
Z
in
S^{n+1}
that splits
L
.
If we restrict the branched double cover
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)\to S^{n+1}
to the complement of
L
, we obtain a regular Abelian cover. Since
H_{1}(Z;\mathbb{Z})=0
, we can lift the embedding
Z\hookrightarrow S^{n+1}
to
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
, which is a mapping torus on
Y
, and the fact that
Z
splits
L
implies that
Z
lifts as a homological section of
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
.

The long exact sequence in homotopy associated with the fibration
Y\to\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)\to S^{1}
shows that the image of
\pi_{1}(Y)
inside
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L))
is the commutator subgroup, hence the map

\mathbb{R}\times Y\to\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)

obtained by unwinding the mapping torus is in fact the maximal Abelian cover of
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
. Again, since
H_{1}(Z;\mathbb{Z})=0
, we can lift
Z
to
\mathbb{R}\times Y
, again as a homological section. By choosing two parallel lifts of
Y
on either sides of
Z
we obtain an identity cobordism
I\times Y
which is split by
Z
. One of the two components of
(I\times Y)\setminus Z
gives a cobordism from
Y
to
Z
, and the other part is the left inverse cobordism.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

With some homological observations, the arguments in this section can be used to produce a link
L
and two rational homology spheres
Y
and
Z
such that
L
is split by
Y
but not by
Z
.
First use Proposition
2.2
on a knot
K
with
\det K\neq 1
to produce a link
L=L_{K}
split by a rational homology sphere
Y=Y_{K}
with
H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})\neq 0
. Now let
Z
be another rational homology sphere, with
H_{1}(Z;\mathbb{Z})
of order coprime with that of
H_{1}(Y;\mathbb{Z})
, and assume by contradiction that
Z
splits
L
, too.

We can then follow the argument of Lemma
2.3
. First one shows that
Z
lifts from
S^{n}\setminus L
to
\Sigma_{2}(L)\setminus\pi^{-1}(L)
, using the fact that the two-fold cover is Abelian, and that the torsion
\mathbb{Z}
-module
H_{1}(Z)
must map trivially to the free
\mathbb{Z}
-module
H_{1}(S^{n}\setminus L)
. Then, the fact that
\Sigma_{2}(L)
is a mapping torus over
Y
implies via a Mayer–Vietoris sequence that
H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(L))\cong\mathbb{Z}\oplus Q
, where
Q
is a quotient of
H_{1}(Y)
.
Thus, the map
H_{1}(Z)\to H_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(L))
vanishes, and thus
Z
lifts to the Abelian cover
I\times Y
. Moreover,
Z
is homologically primitive in
I\times Y
, by the same argument as in Lemma
2.3
, so
[Y]=\pm[Z]
in
H_{3}(I\times Y)\cong\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, restricting the projection gives a degree-one map from
Z
to
Y
, which must induce a surjection in homology, and this gives the desired contradiction.

Remark 2.5
.

If one wants to construct links that are not split by any rational homology sphere, then there is a simple obstruction coming from the single-variable Alexander polynomial: if
L
is split by
Y
and
b_{1}(Y)=0
, then
\Delta_{L}(t)=0
.
To see this, note that
Y
lifts to the infinite cyclic cover
\tilde{X}
of
L
associated to the morphism that sends each meridian
\mu_{i}
of
L
to a generator of
\mathbb{Z}
(here we are taking
L
with an orientation), and so the preimage of
Y
to
\tilde{X}
is
\coprod_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{m}\,Y_{0}
. Consider a loop
\gamma
in
S^{4}
in the homology class
[\mu_{1}]-[\mu_{2}]
that intersects
Y
geometrically twice.
See Figure
2
.

\gamma
also lifts to a loop
\gamma_{0}
in

\langle Y_{0},\gamma_{0}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}}t^{m}\langle t^{-m}Y_{0},\gamma_{0}\rangle=\pm(t-1).

Thus, the
\mathbb{Z}[t^{\pm 1}]
-module
H_{1}(\tilde{X};\mathbb{Z})
is not torsion, and therefore its first Alexander ideal vanishes.

3.
Higher-dimensional links

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
L
be a
2
-component link in
S^{n+1}
which is split by an integer homology
n
-sphere
Y
with
\pi_{1}(Y)\neq 1
, and such that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is a mapping torus on
Y
.
Then
L
is not topologically split by
S^{n}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
there is an invertible topological cobordism from
Y
to
S^{n}
, or, equivalently, there is a locally-flat embedding of
S^{n}
in
\mathbb{R}\times Y
as a homological section. Composing with the projection, this would give a degree-1 map from
S^{n}
to
Y
. A degree-1 map always induces an epimorphism on
\pi_{1}
, but this is impossible under the assumption that
\pi_{1}(Y)\neq 1
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Due to the presence of the hypothesis
\pi_{1}(Y)\neq 1
in the statement of Proposition
3.1
, the reader may wonder if it is possible to find a 2-component link in
S^{n+1}
which is split by an
n
-dimensional
homotopy
sphere, but not by
S^{n}
.
The answer is always negative.

When
n\neq 4
it is impossible to even embed an
n
-dimensional exotic sphere
Y
in
S^{n+1}
. Indeed, an embedding
Y\hookrightarrow S^{n+1}
would give an
h
-cobordism between
Y
and
S^{n}
(after removing a small ball from
S^{n+1}
), hence showing that
Y
is diffeomorphic to
S^{n}

[
Sma62
]
. (Note that
n\geq 7
because
Y
is assumed to be exotic.)

When
n=4
, even if the existence of exotic 4-spheres is open, one can still show that a homotopy sphere
Y^{4}
splits a link
L
if and only if the standard sphere
S^{4}
does.
This can be deduced from the fact that there is a unique smooth
h
-cobordism between any two homotopy 4-spheres
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
, up to diffeomorphism rel boundary
[
Law88
,
Kre01
]
.
Given the unique
h
-cobordism
W\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
and its inverse
-W\colon Y^{\prime}\to Y
, the composition
(-W)\circ W
must be diffeomorphic to
I\times Y
, by uniqueness.
It follows that
Y^{\prime}
sits in
I\times Y
as a homological section.
In other words, a regular neighbourhood
\nu(Y)
of a homotopy 4-sphere
Y\subset S^{5}
contains every other homotopy 4-sphere
Y^{\prime}
as a homological section, so
L
is split by a homotopy 4-sphere if and only if it is split by all of them.

Theorem 3.3
.

For every
n\geq 3
, there exists a
2
-component link
L
in
S^{n+1}
which is smoothly split by an integer homology
n
-sphere
Y
but is not topologically split by
S^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
J
be a non-trivial knot in
S^{3}
with
\det J=1
, so that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{3},J)
is an integer homology sphere with
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(S^{3},J))\neq 1
.
(Concretely, we can choose an infinite family of such knots by taking
J
to be any torus knot
T(p,q)
with
p,q>1
both odd, in which case
\Sigma_{2}(S^{3},J)
is the Brieskorn sphere
\Sigma(2,p,q)
.)

We define a knot
K
in
S^{n}
by taking the
0
-spun knot
n-3
times:

K:=Sp_{0}\left(\cdots\left(Sp_{0}(J)\right)\cdots\right).

From the
0
-spinning construction, it is straightforward to check that if
J
is a knot whose double branched cover is an integer homology sphere, then so is
Sp_{0}(J)
. Thus, iterating this reasoning, we have that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)
is an integer homology sphere.
Moreover, since
0
-spinning preserves the knot group, we have that
\pi_{1}(S^{n}\setminus K)\cong\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus J)
, and therefore
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K))\cong\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(S^{3},J))
, being their unique index-
2
subgroups: thus,
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)
is also an integer homology sphere with
\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K))\neq 1
.

By Proposition
2.2
, there exists a link
L_{K}
in
S^{n+1}
which is split by the manifold
Y_{K}=\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},K)\#\Sigma_{2}(S^{n},m(K))
(which by the previous paragraph is an integer homology sphere with
\pi_{1}(Y_{K})\neq 1
), and such that
\Sigma_{2}(S^{n+1},L)
is a mapping torus over
Y_{K}
.
It then follows from Proposition
3.1
that
L_{K}
is not topologically split by
S^{n}
.
∎

4.
Locally-flatly splitting in dimension
4

In this section we prove the following more precise version of Theorem
1.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For a positive torus knot
T=T(p,q)\neq T(3,5)
with
p,q
both odd, let
L_{T}
and
Y_{T}
denote the link and the integer homology spheres given by Proposition
2.2
. If
T,T^{\prime}
are two positive torus knots as above, then
L_{T}
is smoothly split by
Y_{T}
but not topologically split by
Y_{T^{\prime}}
.

We are indebted to Michel Boileau for suggesting the idea of the proof.

Recall that if
T=T(p,q)
is a torus knot with
p
,
q
both odd, then
\det T=1
and the double cover
\Sigma_{2}(S^{3},T)
is the Brieskorn sphere
\Sigma(2,p,q)
, which is a small Seifert fibred space. If, furthermore,
T\neq T(3,5)
, then
\pi_{1}(\Sigma(2,p,q))
is infinite. Since small Seifert fibred spaces are irreducible, by the sphere theorem the universal cover of
\Sigma(2,p,q)
has trivial
\pi_{2}
. Since the fundamental group of
\Sigma(2,p,q)
is infinite, the universal cover has trivial
H_{3}
, and by the Hurewicz theorem
\Sigma(2,p,q)
has trivial
\pi_{3}
too.

Moreover, there are no incompressible surfaces in

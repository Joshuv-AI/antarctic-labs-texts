Multidegrees, families, and integral dependence
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Claudia Polini, Bernd Ulrich

Abstract
. We study the behavior of multidegrees in families and the existence of numerical criteria to detect integral dependence. We show that mixed multiplicities of modules are upper semicontinuous functions when taking fibers and that projective degrees of rational maps are lower semicontinuous under specialization. We investigate various aspects of the polar multiplicities and Segre numbers of an ideal and introduce a new invariant that we call polar-Segre multiplicities. In terms of polar multiplicities and our new invariants, we provide a new integral dependence criterion for certain families of ideals. By giving specific examples, we show that the Segre numbers are the only invariants among the ones we consider that can detect integral dependence. Finally, we generalize the result of Gaffney and Gassler regarding the lexicographic upper semicontinuity of Segre numbers.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with
the behavior of multidegrees in families
and with
the search for criteria to detect integral dependence
.
Although these two themes are not typically studied together, the backbone of our work is the delicate interplay between them.
Multidegrees provide the natural generalization of the degree of a projective variety to a multiprojective setting, and their study goes back to classical work of van der Waerden
[
62
]
.
The notion of multidegrees (or mixed multiplicities) has become of importance in several areas of mathematics (e.g., algebraic geometry, commutative algebra and combinatorics; see
[

,
36
,
31
,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,
40
,
54
]
).
On the other hand, the idea of detecting integral dependence with numerical invariants was initiated with seminal work of Rees
[
50
]
.
Considerable effort has been made to extend Rees’ theorem to the case of arbitrary ideals, modules, and, more generally, algebras (see
[
38
,
39
,

,

,
60
,
4
,

,

,

,

,

).

Teissier’s Principle of Specialization of Integral Dependence (PSID) can be seen as the first indication of a fruitful connection between the behavior of multiplicities in families and the detection of integral dependence (see
[
56
]
,
[
55
, Appendice I]
).
Indeed, in an analytic setting, the original PSID states that for a family of zero-dimensional ideals with constant Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity, a section is integrally dependent on the total family if and only if it is integrally dependent on the fibers corresponding to a Zariski-open dense subset of the base.
For families of not necessarily zero-dimensional ideals (also in an analytic setting), the PSID was extended by Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
using Segre numbers.
This paper continues the line of research traced by the aforementioned works of Teissier, Gaffney and Gassler.

We now describe the results of this paper more precisely.

1.1.

The behavior of multidegrees and projective degrees of rational maps in families.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
T
be a finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra.
Let
X=\text{MultiProj}(T)
be the corresponding multiprojective scheme embedded in a product of projective spaces
{\mathbb{P}}={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.
For each
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=\dim(X)
, one denotes by
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
the
multidegree of
X
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
.
In classical geometric terms, if
\mathbb{k}
is algebraically closed, then
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X)
is equal to the number of points (counted with multiplicities) in the intersection of
X
with the product
L_{1}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}L_{p}\subset{\mathbb{P}}
, where
L_{i}\subseteq{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{i}}
is a general linear subspace of dimension
m_{i}-n_{i}
for each
1\leq i\leq p
.
More generally, given a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
T
-module
M
, one denotes by
e({\mathbf{n}};M)
the
mixed multiplicity of
M
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
, for each
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim(\text{Supp}_{++}(M))
.
Let
\Psi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be a rational map and
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be the closure of the graph of
\mathcal{F}
.
For each
0\leq i\leq r
, the
i
-th
projective degree
of
\Psi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
is given by the following multidegree

d_{i}(\Psi)\,=\,\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}}^{i,r-i}\left(\Gamma\right).

For more details on these notions, see
Section 2
.

In
Section 3
, we study the behavior of mixed multiplicities under the process of taking fibers with respect to a base ring.
The idea of studying the multiplicities of families is now classical (see, e.g.,
[
43
]
,
[
16
, Chapter 5]
), but the case of mixed multiplicities does not seem to have been considered before.
Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and
\mathscr{T}
be a finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded
A
-algebra.
Denote by
\mathscr{X}=\text{MultiProj}(\mathscr{T})
the corresponding multiprojective scheme. Let
\mathscr{M}
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
, and consider the finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded module
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
over the finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
-algebra
\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathscr{T}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.
Then, for all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
, we introduce a function

e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}\,:\,{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}

that naturally measures the mixed multiplicities of the fibers
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
(see
Definition 3.3
).
Our first main result is the following.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 3.4
)
.

For all
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
, the function
e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}
is upper semicontinuous.

A direct consequence of the above theorem is the upper semicontinuity of the respective functions

\deg_{\mathscr{X},{\mathbb{P}}_{A}}^{\mathbf{n}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}

measuring the multidegrees of the fibers
\mathscr{X}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))
(see
Definition 3.5
and
Corollary 3.6
).
On the other hand, under certain conditions, in
Corollary 3.7
we show that mixed multiplicities are lower semicontinuous under the process of taking specializations.

We study rational maps and their specializations in
Section 4
.
In particular, we are interested in the behavior of projective degrees with respect to specializations.
Since projective degrees are the mixed multiplicities of the corresponding Rees algebra (i.e., the multidegrees of the graph), this type of question can be traced back to the problem of specializing Rees algebras (see
[
19
,
37
]
).
More recently, specializations of rational maps were studied in
[
11
,
9
]
.
Let
A
be a Noetherian domain,
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring and
{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}=\text{Proj}(S)
.
Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
be a rational map with representative
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0}:\cdots:f_{s})
such that
\{f_{0},\ldots,f_{s}\}\subset S
are homogeneous elements of the same degree.
Denote by
I=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{s})\subset S
the base ideal of
\mathcal{F}
.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we get the rational map

\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}):{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{s}
with representative

\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbf{f})=\left(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}):\cdots:\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{s})\right)

where
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{i})
is the image of
f_{i}
under the natural map
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}:S\rightarrow S(\mathfrak{p})=S\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.
Then, we introduce the functions

\displaystyle{\rm degIm}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

(measures the degree of the image of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})
)

\displaystyle d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

(measures the projective degrees of
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})
)

\displaystyle j^{I}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\text{(measures the $j$-multiplicity of $I(\mathfrak{p})\subset S(\mathfrak{p})$)};

see
Definition 4.2
.
Our second main result deals with the behavior of the last three functions.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 4.3
)
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

{\text{degIm}}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function.

(ii)

d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(iii)

j^{I}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function.

In
Corollary 4.5
, we use
Theorem B
to give sharp upper bounds for the projective degrees of several families of rational maps (the list includes perfect ideals of height two and Gorenstein ideals of height three).
The basic idea is that for several families of rational maps under generic conditions we can compute projective degrees, and then
Theorem B
yields an upper bound for any specialization.

1.2.

Polar multiplicities, Segre numbers and integral dependence.

Our next objective is to study various aspects of the
polar multiplicities
and
Segre numbers
of an ideal and introduce a new invariant that plays an important role in our work.
One technical goal of our work is to extend several of the results of Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
from their analytic setting to an algebraic one over a Noetherian local ring.
Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\kappa)
be a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
and residue field
\kappa
.
Let
d=\dim(R)
,
X={\text{Spec}}(R)
and
I\subset R
be a proper ideal.
Consider the blow-up
\pi:P=\text{Proj}(\mathscr{R}(I))\rightarrow X
and the exceptional divisor
E=\text{Proj}(\text{gr}_{I}(R))
of
X
along
I
.
Following the general notion of polar multiplicities due to Kleiman and Thorup
[
38
,
39
]
, one defines

\text{(\emph{polar multiplicity}) \;}m_{i}(I,R)\;=\;m_{d}^{d-i}(\mathscr{R}(I))\quad\text{ and }\qquad\text{(\emph{Segre number}) \;}c_{i}(I,R)\;=\;m_{d-1}^{d-i}(\text{gr}_{I}(R))

as polar multiplicities of
\mathscr{R}(I)
and
\text{gr}_{I}(R)
, respectively.
It should be mentioned that the polar multiplicities of a standard graded
R
-algebra can be seen as the multidegrees of a biprojective scheme over the residue field
\kappa
.
We introduce the new invariant

\nu_{i}(I,R)\;=\;m_{i}(I,R)+c_{i}(I,R)

that we call
polar-Segre multiplicity
.

Section 5
is dedicated to establish several properties of the invariants
m_{i}(I,R)
,
c_{i}(I,R)
and
\nu_{i}(I,R)
.
Let
\delta=o(I)
be the order of
I
(see
Notation 5.7
).
In
Proposition 5.8
, we show the inequality

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;\leq\;m_{i}(I,R)+c_{i}(I,R)\;=\;\nu_{i}(I,R),

and that equality holds for all
1\leq i\leq d
if and only if
I
satisfies the
\mathscr{G}
-parameter condition generically
(see
Notation 5.7
).
Next, we express all these numbers as the multiplicities of the push-forward via
\pi
of certain cycles obtained by making general cuttings; thus following general tradition when studying local invariants (see, e.g.,
[
24
]
,
[
38
, §8]
).
Assume that
\kappa
is an infinite field,
\underline{H}=H_{1},\ldots,H_{d}
is a sequence of general hyperplanes, and denote by
\underline{g}=g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}
the associated sequence of elements in
I
(see
Notation 5.5
).
We introduce the following objects:

(
polar scheme
)

\displaystyle\qquad P_{i}(I,X)\;=\;P_{i}^{\underline{H}}(I,X)\;=\;\pi\left(H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i}\right)

(
Segre cycle
)

\displaystyle\qquad\Lambda_{i}(I,X)\;=\;\Lambda_{i}^{\underline{H}}(I,X)\;=\;\pi_{*}\left(\big[E\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1}\big]_{d-i}\right)\;\in\;Z_{d-i}(X)

(
polar-Segre cycle
)

\displaystyle\qquad V_{i}(I,X)\;=\;V_{i}^{\underline{H}}(I,X)\;=\;\pi_{*}\left(\big[H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1}\cap\pi^{*}D_{i}\big]_{d-i}\right)\;\in\;Z_{d-i}(X);

for more details, see
Setup 5.10
.
We have the following unifying result.

Theorem C

(
Theorem 5.12
)
.

(
\kappa
infinite
)
. The following statements hold:

(i)

m_{i}(I,R)=e_{d-i}\left(P_{i}(I,X)\right)
and

P_{i}(I,X)\;=\;{\text{Spec}}\left(R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty}\right).

(ii)

c_{i}(I,R)=e_{d-i}\left(\Lambda_{i}(I,X)\right)
and

\Lambda_{i}(I,X)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in V\left((g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty}\right)\\
\mathfrak{p}\in V(I),\;\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=d-i\end{subarray}}e\big(I,\,R_{\mathfrak{p}}/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1})R_{\mathfrak{p}}:_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}I^{\infty}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\big)\cdot\left[R/\mathfrak{p}\right]\;\in\;Z_{d-i}(X).

(iii)

\nu_{i}(I,R)=e_{d-i}(V_{i}(I,X))
and

V_{i}(I,X)\;=\;\left[P_{i}(I,X)\right]_{d-i}+\Lambda_{i}(I,X)\;=\;\big[{\text{Spec}}\left(R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty}+g_{i}R\right)\big]_{d-i}\;\in\,Z_{d-i}(X).

A particular consequence of the above theorem is that it recovers known formulas for the polar multiplicities and Segre numbers of an ideal (see
Remark 5.13
).

In
Section 6
, we introduce new numerical criteria for integral dependence.
From the main result of
[
48
]
, we know that Segre numbers detect integral dependence (also, in an analytic setting, see
[
24
]
).
In
Theorem 6.6
, we prove a PSID that is similar to the ones in
[
24
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
48
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Then a driving question for our work is:
can we detect integral dependence with the invariants
m_{i}(I,R)
and
\nu_{i}(I,R)
?

In the particular case of the polar multiplicities
m_{i}(I,R)
this has been a folklore question for many years.
Here we give a definitive and perhaps unfortunate answer:

•

“only Segre numbers detect integral dependence”
.

Indeed, in
Example 6.9
and
Example 6.10
, we provide examples where the invariants
m_{i}(I,R)
and
\nu_{i}(I,R)
do not detect integral dependence.
On the other hand, we have the following criterion where these two invariants can be used.

Theorem D

(
Theorem 6.7
)
.

Assume that
R
is equidimensional and universally catenary, and let
I\subset J
be two
R
-ideals.
Suppose the following two conditions:

(a)

o(I)=o(J)
.

(b)

I
satisfies the
\mathscr{G}
-parameter condition generically
(
see
Notation 5.7
)
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

J
is integral over
I
.

(ii)

m_{i}(I,R)=m_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
.

(iii)

\nu_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.

An interesting family of ideals where the above theorem applies is that of equigenerated ideals (see
Corollary 6.15
).

Finally, we study the behavior of Segre numbers in families.
More precisely, we generalize the result of Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
regarding the lexicographic upper semicontinuity of Segre numbers.
We now introduce a suitable algebraic notation to extend their original result expressed in an analytic setting.
Let
\iota:A\hookrightarrow R
be a flat injective homomorphism of finite type of Noetherian rings and assume that
\pi:R\twoheadrightarrow A
is a section of
\iota
.
Let
Q=\text{Ker}(\pi)
.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, consider the Noetherian local ring
S(\mathfrak{p})=R(\mathfrak{p})_{QR(\mathfrak{p})}
that we call the
distinguished fiber
of
\mathfrak{p}
(see
§6.1
).
Our last main result is the following.

Theorem E

(
Theorem 6.12
)
.

Assume that for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, the fibers
R(\mathfrak{p})
are equidimensional of the same dimension
d
and

\text{ht}(I(\mathfrak{p}))>0
.
Then the function

\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\;\mapsto\;\big(c_{1}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right),c_{2}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right),\ldots,c_{d}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right)\big)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d}

is upper semicontinuous with respect to the lexicographic order.

We give related lexicographic upper semicontinuity results for Segre numbers in
Corollary 6.4
and
Corollary 6.5
.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

Here we recall the concepts of mixed multiplicities, multidegrees and projective degrees.
We also set up the notation that is used throughout the paper.
Let
p\geq 1
be a positive integer and, for each
1\leq i\leq p
, let
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
be the
i
-th elementary vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}=\left(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0\right)
.
If
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}),\,{\mathbf{m}}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
are two vectors, we write
{\mathbf{n}}\geq{\mathbf{m}}
whenever
n_{i}\geq m_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq p
, and
{\mathbf{n}}>{\mathbf{m}}
whenever
n_{j}>m_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq p
.
We write
\mathbf{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
for the zero vector
\mathbf{0}=(0,\ldots,0)
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
T
be a finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded algebra over
\mathbb{k}
, that is,
\left[T\right]_{\mathbf{0}}=\mathbb{k}
and
T
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{k}
by elements of degree
{\mathbf{e}}_{i}
with
1\leq i\leq p
.
The multiprojective scheme
X=\text{MultiProj}(T)
corresponding to
T
is given by
the set of all multihomogeneous prime ideals in
T
not containing the irrelevant ideal
{\mathfrak{N}}:=\left([T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\right)\cap\cdots\cap\left([T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}\right)
, that is,

X=\text{MultiProj}(T):=\big\{\mathfrak{P}\in{\text{Spec}}(T)\mid\mathfrak{P}\text{ is multihomogeneous and }\mathfrak{P}\not\supseteq{\mathfrak{N}}\big\},

and its scheme structure is obtained by using multihomogeneous localizations (see, e.g.,
[
35
, §1]
).
We embed
X
as a closed subscheme of a multiprojective space
{\mathbb{P}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{1}}\times_{\mathbb{k}}\cdots\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{m_{p}}
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
T
-module.
A homogeneous element is said to be
filter-regular on
M
(with respect to
{\mathfrak{N}}
; see
[
53
, Appendix]
) if
z\not\in\mathfrak{P}
for all associated primes
\mathfrak{P}\in\text{Ass}_{T}(M)
of
M
such that
\mathfrak{P}\not\supseteq{\mathfrak{N}}
.
In terms of the multiprojective scheme
X
, this means that
zT_{\mathfrak{P}}
is a nonzerodivisor on
M_{\mathfrak{P}}
for all
\mathfrak{P}\in X
.
A sequence of homogeneous elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{m}
in
T
is said to be
filter-regular on
M
(with respect to
{\mathfrak{N}}
) if
z_{j}
is a filter-regular element on
M/\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{j-1}\right)M
for all
1\leq j\leq m
.
The relevant support of
M
is given by
\text{Supp}_{++}(M):=\text{Supp}(M)\cap X
.
There is a polynomial
P_{M}({\textbf{t}})=P_{M}(t_{1},\ldots,t_{p})\in\mathbb{Q}[{\textbf{t}}]=\mathbb{Q}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{p}]
, called the
Hilbert polynomial
of
M
(see, e.g.,
[
31
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
38
, §4]
), such that the degree of
P_{M}
is equal to
r=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}(M)\right)
and

P_{M}(\nu)=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([M]_{\nu}\right)

for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
such that
\nu\gg\mathbf{0}
.
Furthermore, if we write

P_{M}({\textbf{t}})=\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{p}\geq 0}e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\binom{t_{1}+n_{1}}{n_{1}}\cdots\binom{t_{p}+n_{p}}{n_{p}},

then
e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
for all
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{p}=r
.
From this, we obtain the following invariants:

Definition 2.1
.

(i)

For
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{n}}\rvert=\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}(M)\right)
,
e({\mathbf{n}};M):=e(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})
is the
mixed multiplicity of
M
of type
\mathbf{n}
.

(ii)

For
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
\lvert{\mathbf{n}}\rvert=\dim(X)
,
\deg_{\mathbb{P}}^{\mathbf{n}}(X):=e({\mathbf{n}};T)
is the
multidegree of
X=\text{MultiProj}(T)\subset{\mathbb{P}}
of type
{\mathbf{n}}
with respect to
{\mathbb{P}}
.

We recall the following basic concepts related to rational maps.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\Psi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be a rational map,
Y\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be the closure of the image of
\Psi
, and
\Gamma\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be the closure of the graph of
\Psi
.
The rational map
\Psi
is
generically finite
if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(i)

The field extension
K(Y)\hookrightarrow K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r})
is finite, where
K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r})
and
K(Y)
denote the fields of rational functions of
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
and
Y
, respectively.

(ii)

\dim(Y)=\dim({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r})=r
.

The
degree
of
\Psi
is defined as

\deg(\Psi):=\left[K({\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}):K(Y)\right]
when
\Psi
is generically finite.
Otherwise, by convention, we set
\deg(\Psi):=0
.
For each
0\leq i\leq r
, the
i
-th
projective degree
of
\Psi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
is given by

d_{i}(\Psi)\;:=\;\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\times_{\mathbb{k}}{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}}^{i,r-i}\left(\Gamma\right).

For more details on projective degrees, the reader is referred to
[
29
, Example 19.4]
and
[
17
, §7.1.3]
.
Of particular interest is the
0
-th projective degree
d_{0}(\Psi)
as it is equal to the product of the degree of the map times the degree of the image

(1)

d_{0}(\Psi)\;=\;\deg(\Psi)\cdot\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}}(Y)

(e.g., this follows from
[
12
, Theorem 5.4]
and
[
6
, Theorem 2.4]
).

When we work with families of ideals, we shall use the following notation.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
A
be a ring and
R
be an
A
-algebra.
For any prime
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
, and set
R_{\mathfrak{p}}:=R\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
,
R(\mathfrak{p}):=R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
and
I(\mathfrak{p}):=IR(\mathfrak{p})\subset R(\mathfrak{p})
for any ideal
I\subset R
.

The notion general element will be quite useful in our treatment, thus we recall the following definition.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring infinite residue field
\kappa
.
Let
I\subset R
be a proper ideal generated by elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in R
.

–

We say that a property
\mathscr{P}
holds for a
general element

g\in I
if there exists a dense Zariski-open subset
U\subset\kappa^{e}
such that whenever
g=a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}f_{m}
and the image of
(a_{1},...,a_{m})
belongs to
U
, then the property
\mathscr{P}
holds for
g
.

–

We say that
\underline{g}=g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
is a
sequence of general elements
in
I
if the image of
g_{i}
in the ideal
I/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1})\subset R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1})
is a general element for all
1\leq i\leq k
.
We also say that
\underline{g}=g_{1},\ldots,g_{k}
are
sequentially general elements
in
I
.

Given a multihomogeneous ideal
\mathcal{J}\subset T
, we say that an element
z\in T
is general in
\mathcal{J}
if its image is general in the localization
\mathcal{J}T_{\mathfrak{M}}
where
\mathfrak{M}:=\left([T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{1}}\right)+\cdots+\left([T]_{{\mathbf{e}}_{p}}\right)
.

3.
The behavior of mixed multiplicities

In this section, we study the behavior of mixed multiplicities under the processes of taking fibers and performing specializations, both with respect to a base ring.
This section revisits and generalizes some results from
[
16
, Chapter 5]
and
[
9
,
11
]
.
We fix the following setup throughout this section.

Setup 3.1
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian ring and
\mathscr{T}
be a finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded algebra over
A
.
Let
\mathscr{X}=\text{MultiProj}(\mathscr{T})
be the corresponding multiprojective scheme.

Given a topological space
Z
and a totally ordered set
(\mathfrak{S},<)
,
we say that a function
f:Z\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is
upper semicontinuous
if
\{z\in Z\mid f(z)\geq s\}
is a closed subset of
Z
for each
s\in\mathfrak{S}
;
on the other hand, a function
f:Z\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}
is said to be
lower semicontinuous
if
\{z\in Z\mid f(z)\leq s\}
is a closed subset of
Z
for each
s\in\mathfrak{S}
.

An important basic tool in this paper is the topological Nagata criterion (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 24.2]
).

Remark 3.2

(topological Nagata criterion for openness)
.

A subset
U\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
is open if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(i)

If
\mathfrak{q}\in U
, then
U
contains a nonempty open subset of
V(\mathfrak{q})\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
,
\mathfrak{p}\in U
and
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
, then
\mathfrak{q}\in U
.

Given a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module
\mathscr{M}
, we seek to study the behavior of mixed multiplicities
e({\mathbf{n}};\bullet)
when considering the family of modules
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
with
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Notice that
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
is a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})
-module and that
\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})
is a finitely generated standard
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
-graded algebra over the field
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.

Definition 3.3
.

We consider the functions

d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)\right)

and

e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\},\qquad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\begin{cases}e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|=d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\\
0&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|>d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\\
\infty&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|<d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\end{cases}

for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
.
We use the natural ordering on the set
{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}
.

The following result extends
[
16
, Theorem 5.13]
to a multigraded setting.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume
Setup 3.1
.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a finitely generated
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is an upper semicontinuous function.

(ii)

e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}
is an upper semicontinuous function for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
.

Proof.

We prove both statements by utilizing the topological Nagata criterion (see
Remark 3.2
) and Grothendieck’s Generic Freeness Lemma (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 24.1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 14.4]
).

Fix elements
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
{\mathbf{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{p})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
and
e\in{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}
.
We need to show that

U_{d}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\leq d\big\} \quad\text{ and }\quad V_{{\mathbf{n}},e}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid e_{{\mathbf{n}}}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\leq e\big\}

are open subsets of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

First, we verify condition (i) of
Remark 3.2
for both subsets
U_{d}
and
V_{{\mathbf{n}},e}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
and
\overline{A}:=A/\mathfrak{q}
.
The Generic Freeness Lemma applied to the module
\overline{\mathscr{M}}:=\mathscr{M}/\mathfrak{q}\mathscr{M}
gives a nonzero element
0\neq a\in\overline{A}
such that each graded component of
\overline{\mathscr{M}}_{a}
is a finitely generated free
\overline{A}_{a}
-module.
It follows that
P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}=P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in D(a)\subset V(\mathfrak{q})\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
, which verifies the validity of condition (i) of
Remark 3.2
for both
U_{d}
and
V_{{\mathbf{n}},e}
.

Next, we show that condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
also holds.
Due to Nakayama’s lemma, for any two primes
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
, we have that

\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right]_{\nu}\right)=\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right]_{\nu}\right)\geq\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{q}}\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
.
The dimension of the relevant support equals the degree of the Hilbert polynomial, and the latter can be read-off from the Hilbert function.
For any
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
, it follows that
P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(\nu)\geq P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}(\nu)
for all
\nu\gg\mathbf{0}
, and so
d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\geq d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{q})
.
This shows that condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
is satisfied for the subset
U_{d}
.

Given two primes
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
and
d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})>d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{q})
, we easily check from the definition of the function
e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}
that
e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})\geq e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{q})
.

Next, consider the case
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
and
d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})=d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{q})
.
(That
P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(\nu)-P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}(\nu)\geq 0
for all
\nu\gg\mathbf{0}
does not necessarily imply that the coefficients of the monomials of highest degree of
P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}
are bigger or equal than the ones of
P_{\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}
; for instance,
f(x,y)=(x-y)^{2}=x^{2}-2xy+y^{2}\in\mathbb{Q}[x,y]
.)

Let
r:=|{\mathbf{n}}|
.
We only need to consider the case where
r=d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})=d_{++}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{q})
.
Notice that we can reduce modulo
\mathfrak{q}
and localize at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Hence we assume
A
is a local domain with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}=0
.
By utilizing the faithfully flat extension
A\rightarrow A[y]_{\mathfrak{p}A[y]}
, we may assume that the residue field of
A
is not an algebraic extension of a finite field.
From
[
48
, Lemma 2.6]
, for any multihomogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathscr{T}
, we can choose an element
z\in\mathfrak{J}
whose image is general in both
\mathfrak{J}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mathfrak{J}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{q})
.
Therefore, by prime avoidance, there exists a sequence of homogeneous elements
z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}
in
\mathscr{T}

such that the following three conditions are satisfied:

(1)

each
z_{j}\in\mathscr{T}
has degree
\deg(z_{j})={\mathbf{e}}_{l_{j}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
where
1\leq l_{j}\leq p
;

(2)

n_{i}
equals the number
|\{j\mid 1\leq j\leq r\text{ and }l_{j}=i\}|
;

(3)

\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}\}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{q})
are filter-regular sequences on the modules
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})
, respectively.

To simplify notation, let
\mathscr{M}_{j,\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathscr{M}/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{j})\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})
.
By successively applying
[
12
, Lemma 3.9]
, we obtain

e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)=e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{p}}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\right)=e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{q}}\right).

We choose
\mathbf{0}\ll\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with the property that
e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{p}}\right]_{\nu}\right)
and
e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{q}}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{q}}\right]_{\nu}\right)
.
Finally, Nakayama’s lemma yields that

\displaystyle e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right)=e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{p}}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{p}}\right]_{\nu}\right)=\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\geq\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{q}}\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{q}}\right]_{\nu}\right)=e\left(\mathbf{0};\mathscr{M}_{r,\mathfrak{q}}\right)=e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\right).

Therefore, the subset
V_{{\mathbf{n}},e}
satisfies condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

We restate the above theorem for the case of multidegrees.
As before, we embed
\mathscr{X}
as a closed subscheme of a multiprojective space
{\mathbb{P}}_{A}:={\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{m_{1}}\times_{A}\cdots\times_{A}{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{m_{p}}
.
We seek to study the multidegrees of the fibers
\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\mathscr{X}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))=\text{MultiProj}(\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p}))\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathfrak{p}}:={\mathbb{P}}_{A}\times_{{\text{Spec}}(A)}{\text{Spec}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))
.

Definition 3.5
.

We define the functions

d^{\mathscr{X}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\quad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\dim\left(\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)

and

\deg_{\mathscr{X},{\mathbb{P}}_{A}}^{\mathbf{n}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\},\qquad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\begin{cases}\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\mathbf{n}}(\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}})&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|=\dim\left(\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\\
0&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|>\dim\left(\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\\
\infty&\text{ if }|{\mathbf{n}}|<\dim\left(\mathscr{X}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\end{cases}

for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
.

We have the following direct consequence of
Theorem 3.4
.

Corollary 3.6
.

Assume
Setup 3.1
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

d^{\mathscr{X}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is an upper semicontinuous function.

(ii)

\deg_{\mathscr{X},{\mathbb{P}}_{A}}^{\mathbf{n}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}\cup\{\infty\}
is an upper semicontinuous function for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
.

We quickly revisit the notion of specialization of a module.
Here we follow the same setting and notations as in
[
9
]
.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a finitely generated torsionless
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module, with a fixed injection
\iota:\mathscr{M}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{F}
into a free
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module of finite rank.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, the
specialization of
\mathscr{M}
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
is defined as

\mathbb{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathscr{M})\,:=\,\text{Im}\Big(\iota\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\Big).

We now consider the following function

\mathbb{S}e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto e\left({\mathbf{n}};\mathbb{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathscr{M})\right)

for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|\geq\dim\left(\text{Supp}_{++}\left(\mathbb{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathscr{M})\right)\right)
.

The following result deals with the behavior of mixed multiplicities with respect to specializations.

Corollary 3.7
.

Assume
Setup 3.1
and that each graded component of
\mathscr{T}
is a free
A
-module.
Let
\mathscr{M}
be a finitely generated torsionless
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module, with a fixed injection
\iota:\mathscr{M}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{F}
into a free
{\mathbb{Z}}^{p}
-graded
\mathscr{T}
-module of finite rank.
Let
r
be the common dimension
\dim(\text{Supp}_{++}(\mathscr{F}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})))
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Then the function

\mathbb{S}e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}

is lower semicontinuous for every
{\mathbf{n}}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{p}
with
|{\mathbf{n}}|\geq r
.

Proof.

For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we have the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathbb{S}_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathscr{M})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{M}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow 0.

From the additivity of mixed multiplicities, we get
e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{F}}(\mathfrak{p})=e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})+\mathbb{S}e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{M}}(\mathfrak{p})
.
Since
e_{\mathbf{n}}^{\mathscr{F}}
is a constant function by assumption and
e_{{\mathbf{n}}}^{\mathscr{F}/\mathscr{M}}
is upper semicontinuous by
Theorem 3.4
, the result of the corollary follows.
∎

4.
Rational maps and their specializations

Here we concentrate on a specialization process of rational maps.
The next setup is now in place.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
r<s
be two positive integers.
Let
A
be a Noetherian domain,
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring,
{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}=\text{Proj}(S)
, and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset S
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
be a rational map with representative
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0}:\cdots:f_{s})
such that
\{f_{0},\ldots,f_{s}\}\subset S
are homogeneous elements of degree
\delta>0
.

We specialize this rational map as follows.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we get the rational map

\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})\,:\,{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{s}

with representative

\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbf{f})=\left(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}):\cdots:\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{s})\right)

where
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{i})
is the image of
f_{i}
under the natural map
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}:S\rightarrow S(\mathfrak{p})
.

Let
I=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{s})\subset S
be the base ideal of the rational map
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
.
The closure of the graph of
\mathcal{F}
is given as
\Gamma={\text{BiProj}}(\mathscr{R}(I))\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\times_{A}{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
where
\mathscr{R}(I):=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}I^{n}T^{n}\subset S[T]
is the Rees algebra of
I
.
As customary,
\mathscr{R}(I)
is presented as a quotient of a standard bigraded polynomial ring
\mathscr{T}:=S\otimes_{A}A[y_{0},\ldots,y_{s}]
by using the
A
-algebra homomorphism

\mathscr{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{R}(I),\quad x_{i}\mapsto x_{i},\;y_{j}\mapsto f_{j}t.

We have the equalities
I(\mathfrak{p})=\left(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}),\ldots,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{s})\right)\subset S(\mathfrak{p})
and
(I^{k})(\mathfrak{p})=I(\mathfrak{p})^{k}\subset S(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
and
k\geq 0
.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
\Gamma(\mathfrak{p})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}\times_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{s}
and
Y(\mathfrak{p})\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{s}
be the closures of the graph and the image of the rational map
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})
.
We have that
\dim(\Gamma(\mathfrak{p}))\leq r
and
\dim(Y(\mathfrak{p}))\leq r
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
We also consider the
j-multiplicity
of ideal
I(\mathfrak{p})\subset S(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
(see
[
1
]
,
[
21
, §6.1]
).

Definition 4.2
.

We have the following three functions:

j^{I}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\;\mathfrak{p}\mapsto j\left(I(\mathfrak{p})\right),

{\text{degIm}}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\begin{cases}\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{s}}\left(Y(\mathfrak{p})\right)&\text{ if }\dim(Y(\mathfrak{p}))=r\\
0&\text{ if }\dim(Y(\mathfrak{p}))<r\end{cases}

and

d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\;d_{i}\left(\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})\right)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Our main result regarding these functions is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume
Setup 4.1
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

{\text{degIm}}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function.

(ii)

d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(iii)

j^{I}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}
is a lower semicontinuous function.

Proof.

By
[
42
, Theorem 5.3]
, we have that
j^{I}(\mathfrak{p})=\delta\cdot d_{0}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Thus, we only need to prove parts (i) and (ii) of the theorem.

Fix
0\leq i\leq r
,
e\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It remains to show that

D_{e}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid{\text{degIm}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})\geq e\big\} \quad\text{ and }\quad E_{i,h}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})\geq h\big\}

are open subsets of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Again, to prove this we utilize a combination of the topological Nagata criterion and the Generic Freeness Lemma.

First, we verify condition (i) of
Remark 3.2
for both
D_{e}
and
E_{i,h}
.
Let
\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, and set
\overline{A}=A/\mathfrak{q}
,
\overline{S}=S/\mathfrak{q}S
and
\overline{I}=I\overline{S}\subset\overline{S}
.
We use the version of the Generic Freeness Lemma given in
[
32
, Lemma 8.1]
applied to the inclusion of algebras
\mathscr{R}_{\overline{S}}\left(\overline{I}\right)\hookrightarrow\overline{S}[t]
, and we find a nonzero element
0\neq a\in\overline{A}
such that each graded component of
\overline{S}[t]/\mathscr{R}_{\overline{S}}\left(\overline{I}\right)\otimes_{\overline{A}}\overline{A}_{a}
is a finitely generated free
\overline{A}_{a}
-module (also, see
[
9
, Theorem 3.5]
).
For every
\mathfrak{p}\in D(a)\subset V(\mathfrak{q})\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we obtain that
\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[I(\mathfrak{p})^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[I(\mathfrak{q})^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)
for all
n\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Accordingly, condition (i) of
Remark 3.2
holds for both
D_{e}
and
E_{i,h}
.

Next, we show that condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
also holds for
D_{e}
and
E_{i,h}
.

For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we have that
{\text{degIm}}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})=e_{r+1}\left(L_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
and
d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})=e\left(i,r-i;\mathscr{R}_{S(\mathfrak{p})}(I(\mathfrak{p}))\right)
, where
L_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\left[\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{0}),\ldots,\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}(f_{s})\right]=\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\left[I(\mathfrak{p})^{n}\right]_{n\delta}
.
For each
n\geq 0
, we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow I(\mathfrak{p})^{n}\rightarrow S(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow S/I^{n}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\rightarrow 0.

Since we know that
\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[S/I^{n}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\right]_{\nu}\right)\geq\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[S/I^{n}\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})\right]_{\nu}\right)
for all
n\geq 0
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
, it follows that
D_{e}
satisfies condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
.

Fix two primes
\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{q}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
with
\mathfrak{p}\supseteq\mathfrak{q}
.
As in the proof of
Theorem 3.4
, we may assume that
\mathfrak{q}=0
and that
A
is a local domain with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
.
Moreover, by making a purely transcendental field extension,
we may assume that for any multihomogeneous ideal
\mathfrak{J}\subset\mathscr{T}
, we can choose an element
z\in\mathfrak{J}
whose image is general in both
\mathfrak{J}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{p})
and
\mathfrak{J}\mathscr{T}(\mathfrak{q})
(see
[
48
, Lemma 2.6]
).

By applying
[
12
, Proposition 5.6]
, we find a sequence
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}\subset S\subset\mathscr{T}
of homogeneous elements of degree one such that

d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})=e\left(0,r-i;\,\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(J_{\mathfrak{p}})\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{q})=e\left(0,r-i;\,\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(J_{\mathfrak{q}})\right)

where
R_{\mathfrak{p}}:=S(\mathfrak{p})/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i})S(\mathfrak{p})
,
R_{\mathfrak{q}}:=S(\mathfrak{q})/(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i})S(\mathfrak{q})
,
J_{\mathfrak{p}}:=IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
J_{\mathfrak{q}}:=IR_{\mathfrak{q}}\subset R_{\mathfrak{q}}
.
Since
i\leq r
, we may further assume that
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\}S(\mathfrak{p})
and
\{z_{1},\ldots,z_{i}\}S(\mathfrak{q})
are regular sequences on the polynomial rings
S(\mathfrak{p})
and
S(\mathfrak{q})
, respectively.

There exists a positive integer
m>0
such that

e\left(0,r-i;\,\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(J_{\mathfrak{p}})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(J_{\mathfrak{p}})\right]_{(m,n)}\right)}{n^{r-i}/(r-i)!}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[J_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\right]_{m+n\delta}\right)}{n^{r-i}/(r-i)!}

and

e\left(0,r-i;\,\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(J_{\mathfrak{q}})\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[\mathscr{R}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(J_{\mathfrak{q}})\right]_{(m,n)}\right)}{n^{r-i}/(r-i)!}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[J_{\mathfrak{q}}^{n}\right]_{m+n\delta}\right)}{n^{r-i}/(r-i)!}.

So, to show that
d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})\leq d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{q})
, it suffices to verify that
\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[J_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)\leq\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[J_{\mathfrak{q}}^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)
for all
n\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Notice that the Hilbert functions of
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
R_{\mathfrak{q}}
are equal.
Then, from Nakayama’s lemma we get

\displaystyle\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\left(\left[R_{\mathfrak{p}}/J_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\left[\frac{S}{\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i},I^{n}\right)S}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\right]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}\left(\left[\frac{S}{\left(z_{1},\ldots,z_{i},I^{n}\right)S}\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{q}}\right]_{\nu}\right)=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}\left(\left[R_{\mathfrak{q}}/J_{\mathfrak{q}}^{n}\right]_{\nu}\right)

for all
n\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Finally, this implies that
d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})\leq d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{q})
, and so it follows that condition (ii) of
Remark 3.2
holds for
E_{i,h}
.
So, we are done with the proof of the theorem.
∎

We single out an important corollary of
Theorem 4.3
.
When
r=s
, and we consider a rational map of the form
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}
, we have some control over the degree of the specialized rational maps
\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}):{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}
.

Corollary 4.4
.

Assume
Setup 4.1
.
Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}
be a rational map.
Then the function

\deg^{\mathcal{F}}:{\text{Spec}}(A)\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}},\qquad\mathfrak{p}\mapsto\deg\left(\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p})\right)

is lower semicontinuous.

Proof.

We have that
\deg^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})=d_{0}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Indeed, if
\dim(Y(\mathfrak{p}))<r
, both
\deg^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})
and
d_{0}^{\mathcal{F}}(\mathfrak{p})
are equal to zero; and if
\dim(Y(\mathfrak{p}))=r
,
\deg_{{\mathbb{P}}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}^{r}}(Y(\mathfrak{p}))=1
and so
d_{0}(\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}))=\deg(\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}))
according to
(1)
.
Thus, the result follows from
Theorem 4.3
.
∎

We now apply the above results to different families of rational maps.
We obtain generalizations of
[
11
, Theorems 6.3, 6.8]
, and we eliminate the conditions assumed there.
The following corollary yields significant upper bounds for the projective degrees of certain families of rational maps.
It should be mentioned that these inequalities are sharp for the general members of the considered families (see
[
12
, Theorems 5.7, 5.8]
).

Corollary 4.5
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring,
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
,
\Psi:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{s}
be a rational map with representative
\mathbf{g}=(g_{0}:\cdots:g_{s})
and base ideal
J=(g_{0},\ldots,g_{s})\subset R
, and suppose that
\delta=\deg(g_{j})>0
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

d_{i}(\Psi)\leq\delta^{r-i}
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(ii)

Suppose that
J
is a perfect ideal of height two with Hilbert-Burch resolution of the form

0\rightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}R(-\delta-\mu_{i})\rightarrow{R(-\delta)}^{s+1}\rightarrow J\rightarrow 0.

Then, for all
0\leq i\leq r
, we have

d_{i}(\Psi)\leq e_{r-i}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})

where
e_{r-i}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
denotes the elementary symmetric polynomial

e_{r-i}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})=\sum_{1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{r-i}\leq s}\mu_{j_{1}}\cdots\mu_{j_{r-i}}.

In particular, if
r=s
, then
\deg(\Psi)\leq\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{r}
.

(iii)

Suppose that
J
is a Gorenstein ideal of height three.
Let
D\geq 1
be the degree of every nonzero entry of an alternating minimal presentation matrix of
J
.
Then, for all
0\leq i\leq r
, we have

d_{i}(\Psi)\leq\begin{cases}D^{r-i}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\frac{s-r+i}{2}\rfloor}\binom{s-1-2k}{r-i-1}&\text{ if }0\leq i\leq r-3\\
\delta^{r-i}&\text{ if }r-2\leq i\leq r.\end{cases}

In particular, if
r=s
, then
\deg(\Psi)\leq D^{r}
.

Proof.

(i) For
0\leq j\leq s
, consider a set of variables
\mathbf{z}_{j}=\{z_{j,1},\ldots,z_{j,m}\}
over
\mathbb{k}
with
m=\binom{\delta+r}{r}
, and set
\mathbf{z}=\mathbf{z}_{0}\cup\cdots\cup\mathbf{z}_{s}
.
Let
A=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{z}]
,
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and consider the generic polynomials

G_{j}\,:=\,z_{j,1}x_{0}^{\delta}+z_{j,2}x_{0}^{\delta-1}x_{1}+\cdots+z_{j,m}x_{r}^{\delta}\in S.

Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
be a rational map with base ideal
I=(G_{0},\ldots,G_{s})\subset S
.
Let
\xi=(0)\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
be the generic point and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}=\left(\{z_{j,k}-\alpha_{j,k}\}_{j,k}\right)\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
be a rational maximal ideal such that
J=I({\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha})\subset R
.
It is known that the projective degrees of the morphism
\mathcal{F}(\xi):{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})}^{r}\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})}^{s}
(it is base point free as
I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})\subset S(\xi)=\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
is a zero-dimensional ideal) are equal to
d_{i}\left(\mathcal{F}(\xi)\right)=\delta^{r-i}
(see, e.g.,
[
42
, Observation 3.2]
).
By using
Theorem 4.3
, we obtain
d_{i}(\Psi)=d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}({\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha})\leq d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)=\delta^{r-i}
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(ii) For
1\leq j\leq s+1
and
1\leq k\leq s
, let

\mathbf{z}_{j,k}=\{z_{j,k,1},z_{j,k,2},\ldots,z_{j,k,m_{k}}\}
denote a set of variables over
\mathbb{k}
of cardinality
m_{k}=\binom{\mu_{k}+r}{r}
, and set
\mathbf{z}=\bigcup_{j,k}\mathbf{z}_{j,k}
.
Let
A=\mathbb{k}[\mathbf{z}]
,
S=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
and consider the generic
(s+1)\times s
Hilbert-Burch matrix

\mathcal{M}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}p_{1,1}&p_{1,2}&\cdots&p_{1,s}\\
p_{2,1}&p_{2,2}&\cdots&p_{2,s}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
p_{s+1,1}&p_{s+1,2}&\cdots&p_{s+1,s}\\
\end{array}\right)

where each polynomial
p_{j,k}\in S
is given by

p_{j,k}=z_{j,k,1}x_{0}^{\mu_{k}}+z_{j,k,2}x_{0}^{\mu_{k}-1}x_{1}+\cdots+z_{j,k,m_{k}}x_{r}^{\mu_{k}}.

Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
be a rational map with base ideal
I=I_{s}(\mathcal{M})\subset S
.
Let
\varphi\in R^{(s+1)\times s}
be the Hilbert-Burch presentation of
J
.
Let
\xi=(0)\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
be the generic point and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}=\left(\{z_{j,k,l}-\alpha_{j,k,l}\}_{j,k,l}\right)\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
be a rational maximal ideal such that
\varphi\in R^{(s+1)\times s}
is obtained by specializing
\mathcal{M}\in S^{(s+1)\times s}
via the map
S\twoheadrightarrow S/{\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha}\cong R
.
From
[
11
, Lemma 6.2]
the generic ideal
I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})\subset S(\xi)=\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
is perfect of height two and satisfies the condition
G_{r+1}
, and so
[
12
, Theorem 5.7]
implies that the projective degrees of
\mathcal{F}(\xi):{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}(\mathbf{z})}^{s}
are equal to
d_{i}(\mathcal{F}(\xi))=e_{r-i}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
.
Finally,
Theorem 4.3
yields that
d_{i}(\Psi)=d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}({\mathfrak{m}}_{\alpha})\leq d_{i}^{\mathcal{F}}(\xi)=e_{r-i}(\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{s})
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(iii) This part follows verbatim as part (ii), except that we
need to use
[
10
, Lemma 2.12]
and
[
12
, Theorem 5.8]
.
∎

From
Theorem 4.3
, we have that the projective degrees and the degree of the image of a rational map
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
behave as lower semicontinuous functions under specialization; accordingly, these invariants cannot increase under specialization.
However, it turns out that the degree of a rational map is a much more erratic invariant, and it seems that
Corollary 4.4
is the most general result one can hope for. Indeed, the following two examples show that the degree of a rational map can either increase or decrease under specialization.

Example 4.6

(The degree of a rational map can increase)
.

Let
\mathbb{Q}
be the field of rational numbers, and
A=\mathbb{Q}[a]
and
S=A[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
be polynomial rings.
Consider the following matrix

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}{x}_{0}&{x}_{1}&{x}_{0}^{2}\\
{x}_{1}&{x}_{0}&{x}_{1}^{2}\\
{x}_{0}+a\,{x}_{2}&{x}_{1}&{x}_{2}^{2}\\
0&{x}_{0}&0\end{pmatrix},

and let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{3}
be a rational map with base ideal
I=(f_{0},f_{1},f_{2},f_{3})\subset S
given by

I=I_{3}(\mathcal{M})={\small\begin{pmatrix}{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2}+a\,{x}_{0}{x}_{1}^{2}{x}_{2}-{x}_{0}{x}_{1}{x}_{2}^{2},\\
-{x}_{0}^{4}-a\,{x}_{0}^{3}{x}_{2}+{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2},\\
{x}_{0}^{3}{x}_{1}-{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2},\\
{x}_{0}^{4}-{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2}+a\,{x}_{0}^{3}{x}_{2}-a\,{x}_{1}^{3}{x}_{2}-{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2}+{x}_{1}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2}\end{pmatrix}}.

Let
\mathscr{T}=S\otimes_{A}A[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2},y_{3}]
be a standard bigraded polynomial ring over
A
and write the Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}(I)
as
\mathscr{R}(I)\cong\mathscr{T}/\mathcal{J}
.
The ideal
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathscr{T}
is equal to

\small\begin{pmatrix}\mathtt{{x}_{1}{y}_{0}+{x}_{0}{y}_{1}+{x}_{1}{y}_{2}+{x}_{0}{y}_{3}},\\
\mathtt{{x}_{0}{y}_{0}+{x}_{1}{y}_{1}+\left({x}_{0}+a\,{x}_{2}\right){y}_{2}},\\
\left({x}_{0}{x}_{1}-{x}_{1}^{2}\right){y}_{1}+\left({x}_{0}^{2}+a\,{x}_{0}{x}_{2}-{x}_{2}^{2}\right){y}_{2},\\
\mathtt{{x}_{2}{y}_{0}^{2}+\left(-a\,{x}_{0}+a\,{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}\right){y}_{1}^{2}+\left(a^{2}+2\right){x}_{2}{y}_{0}{y}_{2}-a\,{x}_{1}{y}_{1}{y}_{2}+{x}_{2}{y}_{2}^{2}+\left(-a\,{x}_{0}+a\,{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}\right){y}_{1}{y}_{3}},\\
{y}_{0}^{4}-2\,{y}_{0}^{2}{y}_{1}^{2}+{y}_{1}^{4}+\left(a^{2}+4\right){y}_{0}^{3}{y}_{2}-4\,{y}_{0}{y}_{1}^{2}{y}_{2}+a^{2}{y}_{1}^{3}{y}_{2}+\left(2\,a^{2}+6\right){y}_{0}^{2}{y}_{2}^{2}-2\,{y}_{1}^{2}{y}_{2}^{2}+\\
\left(a^{2}+4\right){y}_{0}{y}_{2}^{3}+{y}_{2}^{4}-2\,{y}_{0}^{2}{y}_{1}{y}_{3}+2\,{y}_{1}^{3}{y}_{3}-4\,{y}_{0}{y}_{1}{y}_{2}{y}_{3}+2\,a^{2}{y}_{1}^{2}{y}_{2}{y}_{3}-2\,{y}_{1}{y}_{2}^{2}{y}_{3}+{y}_{1}^{2}{y}_{3}^{2}+a^{2}{y}_{1}{y}_{2}{y}_{3}^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

This can be computed in a computer algebra system like
Macaulay2

[
27
]
.
The ideal
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathscr{T}
is generated by
5
bihomogeneous polynomials in
\mathscr{T}
.
The first, second and fourth generators of
\mathcal{J}
are linear in the variables
x_{i}
.
Let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{Q}(a)={\text{Quot}}(A)
and
\mathbb{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
be the generic rational map with base ideal
I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{L}\subset\mathbb{L}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
.
From
[
18
, Theorem 2.18]
, we can check that
\mathbb{G}
is birational, i.e.,
\deg(\mathbb{G})=1
.
Alternatively, we give a short direct argument.
We may assume that
\mathbb{L}
is algebraically closed.
By considering the generator
f_{2}=x_{0}^{3}x_{1}-x_{0}^{2}x_{1}^{2}=x_{0}^{2}x_{1}(x_{0}-x_{1})
of
I
, we obtain the morphism

h:D(f_{2})\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
.
Notice that
D(f_{2})
lies inside the affine patch
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
with
x_{0}=1
.
For any point
p=(p_{0}:p_{1}:p_{2}:p_{3})\in{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{3}
in the image of
h
, if
(1,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in h^{-1}(p)
, then we get the following linear system

\begin{array}[]{cccc}p_{1}\alpha_{1}&+&p_{2}a\alpha_{2}&=-(p_{0}+p_{2})\\
(p_{0}+p_{2})\alpha_{1}&&&=-(p_{1}+p_{3})\end{array}

that is derived from the two linear syzygies in
\mathcal{M}
; this system has a unique solution
(1,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
if the determinant
-ap_{2}(p_{0}+p_{2})
is non-zero.
It follows that for any
\alpha=(1,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})\in D(f_{2})\cap D(af_{2}(f_{0}+f_{2}))=D(f_{2}(f_{0}+f_{2}))
, the fiber
h^{-1}(h(\alpha))
has one element.
Thus
\mathbb{G}
is birational.

On the other hand, we make the specialization
a=0
, which gives the matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}{x}_{0}&{x}_{1}&{x}_{0}^{2}\\
{x}_{1}&{x}_{0}&{x}_{1}^{2}\\
{x}_{0}&{x}_{1}&{x}_{2}^{2}\\
0&{x}_{0}&0\end{pmatrix}.

Let
\mathbb{g}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
be a rational map with base ideal

J=I_{3}(M)=\left(-{x}_{0}^{4}+{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2}+{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2}-{x}_{1}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2},\,{x}_{0}^{3}{x}_{1}-{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2},\,{x}_{0}^{4}-{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}^{2},\,{x}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}^{2}-{x}_{0}{x}_{1}{x}_{2}^{2}\right).

In this case
\mathbb{g}
is not birational, indeed
\deg(\mathbb{g})=2
.

Therefore, under the above specialization, we obtain
\deg(\mathbb{g})=2>1=\deg(\mathbb{G})
.

Example 4.7

(The degree of a rational map usually decreases)
.

We recall an example
[
11
, Example 6.5]
where the degree of a rational map can decrease arbitrarily under specialization.
Let
m\geq 1
be an integer.
Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field, and
A=\mathbb{k}[a]
and
S=A[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
be polynomial rings. Consider the matrix

\mathcal{M}=\begin{pmatrix}x&zy^{m-1}\\
-y&zx^{m-1}+y^{m}\\
az&zx^{m-1}\end{pmatrix}

with entries in
S
.
Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{2}
be a rational map with base ideal
I=I_{2}(\mathcal{M})\subset S
.
Let
\mathbb{L}=\mathbb{k}(a)={\text{Quot}}(A)
and
\mathbb{G}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{L}}^{2}
be the generic rational map with base ideal
I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{L}\subset\mathbb{L}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
.
For any
\beta\in\mathbb{k}
, let
\mathfrak{n}_{\beta}=(a-\beta)\in A
and
\mathbb{g}_{\beta}:{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
be a rational map with base ideal
I(\mathfrak{n}_{\beta})\subset\mathbb{k}[x_{0},x_{1},x_{2}]
.
Then, we have that
\deg(\mathbb{G})=m
and

\deg(\mathbb{g}_{\beta})=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\beta=0\\
m&\text{ if }\beta\neq 0.\end{cases}

So, the specialization
a=0
gives an arbitrary decrease in degree
\deg(\mathbb{g}_{0})=1<m=\deg(\mathbb{G})
.

5.
Polar multiplicities, Segre numbers and new set of invariants

In this section, we prove several results regarding polar multiplicities and Segre numbers of an ideal, and we introduce a new related invariant.
These invariants are defined as a special case of the general notion of polar multiplicities due to Kleiman and Thorup
[
38
,
39
]
.
Here, an important goal for us is to extend several of the results of Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
from their analytic setting to an algebraic one over a Noetherian local ring.
The following setup is used throughout this section.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\kappa)
be a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
and residue field
\kappa
.
Let
d:=\dim(R)
and
X:={\text{Spec}}(R)
.
Let
I\subset R
be a proper ideal generated by elements
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in R
.
We consider the Rees algebra
B:=\mathscr{R}(I):=R[IT]=\bigoplus_{v\geq 0}I^{v}T^{v}\subset R[T]
of the ideal
I
.
We have a natural homogeneous presentation
W:=R[y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]\twoheadrightarrow\mathscr{R}(I)
,
y_{i}\mapsto f_{i}T
, where
W
is a standard graded polynomial ring over
R
.

Definition-Proposition 5.4

Let

Since we have

.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
S
-module with
\mathcal{F}\cong\widetilde{M}
and
\text{H}_{S_{+}}^{0}(M)=0
.
Notice that we may substitute

by

and

.
Therefore we assume that

and

, and that any minimal prime of

is relevant.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
be a minimal prime of

of dimension

.
Notice that any relevant prime of

contracting to

Thus the fiber

is finite, and so we can find an affine open neighborhood

[

,

]

[

We set

Since

We have the equality

\sum_{\mathfrak{q}}[\kappa(\mathfrak{q}):\kappa(\mathfrak{p})]\cdot{\rm length}_{A_{\mathfrak{q}}}(L_{\mathfrak{q}})\;=\;{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}}),

where the sum runs through the minimal primes of

.
Finally, notice that the right-hand side of the above equality is the coefficient in

Remark 5.6

(

Notation 5.7

Proposition 5.8
.

(
\kappa

)
.
Let
H\subset P
be a general hyperplane,
D=V(g)
be the associated hypersurface and
\ell\in B_{+}
be the associated linear form. Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\pi^{*}D
and

are effective Cartier divisors.

(ii)

\pi^{*}D=E+H
.

(iii)

Let
\delta=o(I)
.
Then we have the inequality

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;\leq\;m_{i}(I,R)+c_{i}(I,R)\;=\;\nu_{i}(I,R),

and equality holds for all

satisfies the
\mathscr{G}
-parameter condition generically
(

)
.

Proof.

(i) From
Remark 5.6
, we get that
\pi^{*}D
is an effective Cartier divisor.
We have that
H
is also an effective Cartier divisor by prime avoidance.

(ii) We write
g=a_{1}f_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}f_{m}
and
\ell=a_{1}y_{1}+\cdots+a_{m}y_{m}
.
Consider the affine open subscheme

.
As we mentioned before, the local equation of

on

is given by

.
On the other hand, the local equations of

and

This shows the equality

on each

, and so the equality holds globally on the whole blowup

.

(iii) First, we check that in the vacuous case

, we have that

,

,

.

As

.
Notice that
\big[\pi^{*}D\big]_{d-1}=\big[\widetilde{B/gB}\big]_{d-1}
and

, and thus part (ii) yields the equality of cycles

\big[\widetilde{B/gB}\big]_{d-1}\;=\;\big[\widetilde{B/\ell B}\big]_{d-1}+\big[\widetilde{G}\big]_{d-1}\;\in\;Z_{d-1}(P);

see, e.g.,
[

.
Hence the additivity of polar multiplicities (see
[

) implies that

m_{d-1}^{i}(B/gB)\;=\;m_{d-1}^{i}(B/\ell B)+m_{d-1}^{i}(G).

Due to

, we have
m_{d-1}^{i}(B/gB)\geq\delta\cdot m_{d}^{i+1}(B)
and an equality holds for all

if and only if

is a
\mathscr{G}
-parameter on

(in the sense of
[

).
Since

is a general element, by utilizing

.
Finally, by combining everything we get the inequality

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;=\;\delta\cdot m_{d}^{d-i+1}(B)\;\leq\;m_{d}^{d-i}(B)+m_{d-1}^{d-i}(G)\;=\;m_{i}(I,R)+c_{i}(I,R),

and an equality holds for all

if and only if
g
is a
\mathscr{G}
-parameter on
B
.
Since

is a general element of
I
, it follows that

is a
\mathscr{G}
-parameter on

satisfies the
\mathscr{G}
-parameter condition generically.

(i)

Since

Therefore, we get the following equality (as schemes)

(2)

H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i}\;=\;\text{Proj}(B/(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i})B)\;=\;\text{Proj}(\mathscr{R}_{R_{i}}(IR_{i}))\;=\;{\rm Bl}_{I}(X_{i})\;=:\;P_{i}.

(ii) First, set

By

in part (i), it follows that
\text{Supp}\left(\text{H}^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})\right)\subset V(I,0:_{R}I^{\infty})
.
Then we get the following equality

\left[\text{H}^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})\right]_{d-1}\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in V(I,0:_{R}I^{\infty})\\
\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=d-1\end{subarray}}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\text{H}^{0}(E,\mathcal{O}_{E})\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot[R/\mathfrak{p}]\;\in\;Z_{d-1}(X).

Fix a prime
\mathfrak{p}
in the above summation.
Let
E_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\text{Proj}\big(\text{gr}_{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}(R_{\mathfrak{p}})\big)
be the exceptional divisor of

the first equality holds for any

On the other hand, we have

Since
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i-1}

[
13
, Proposition 2.10]

c_{i}(I,R)=m_{d-1}^{d-i}(G)=m_{d-i}^{d-i}(G/(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i-1})G)=e_{d-i}\left(\text{H}^{0}(P,\mathcal{O}_{E\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1}})\right)

(2)

E\cap H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1}\;=\;(H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1})\times_{X}{\text{Spec}}(R/I)\;=\;P_{i-1}\times_{X}{\text{Spec}}(R/I)\;=\;E_{i-1}

along

(iii)
We have the following equalities

\displaystyle V_{i}(I,X)

\displaystyle\;=\;\pi_{*}\left(\left[H_{1}\cap\cdots\cap H_{i-1}\cap\pi^{*}D_{i}\right]_{d-i}\right)

by definition of

\displaystyle\;=\;\pi_{*}\left(\left[P_{i-1}\cap\pi^{*}D_{i}\right]_{d-i}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;\left[P_{1}(I,\overline{X}_{i-1})\right]_{d-i}+\left[\Lambda_{1}(I,\overline{X}_{i-1})\right]_{d-i}

by applying

\displaystyle\;=\;\left[P_{i}(I,X)\right]_{d-i}+\Lambda_{i}(I,X)

by

\dim(R/\mathfrak{p})=d-1\end{subarray}}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\text{H}^{0}(P,\mathcal{O}_{P}/g\mathcal{O}_{P})\otimes_{R}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot[R/\mathfrak{p}]\;\in\;Z_{d-1}(X).

\mathfrak{p}

the blowup of

6.
Integral dependence and specialization

In this section, we discuss how integral dependence can be detected by utilizing the invariants that we study in
Section 5
.
We also discuss when integral closure and polar schemes specialize.
Moreover, we generalize the result of Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
regarding the lexicographic upper semicontinuity of Segre numbers (see
Theorem 6.12
).
Throughout this section, we continue using
Setup 5.1
.

To deal with the case where
\text{ht}(I)=0
, we also need to consider an additional number that is denoted as
c_{0}(I,R)
.
Let
\mathscr{H}:=\text{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(G)=\text{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(\text{gr}_{I}(R)\right)
with standard bigrading
[\mathscr{H}]_{(v,n)}={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}G_{v}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}G_{v}
.
The first sum transform of the Hilbert function of
\mathscr{H}
with respect to
n
is equal to

H_{\mathscr{H}}^{1}(v,n):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dim_{\kappa}\left([\mathscr{H}]_{(v,n)}\right)={\rm length}_{R}\left(G_{v}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}G_{v}\right),

and it encodes the polar multiplicities of the standard graded algebra
G=\text{gr}_{I}(R)
(i.e., the Segre numbers
c_{1}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d}(I,R)
; see
Section 5
).
If we further consider the second sum transform
H_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}(v,n):=\sum_{k=0}^{v}H_{\mathscr{H}}^{1}(k,n)
, for
v\gg 0
and
n\gg 0
, we get a polynomial

H_{\mathscr{H}}^{2}(v,n)\;=\;\sum_{i=0}^{d}\;\frac{c_{i}(I,R)}{i!\,(d-i!)}\;v^{i}n^{d-i}\;+\;\text{(lower degree terms)},

that encodes the Segre numbers
c_{1}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d}(I,R)
defined in
Section 5
, but also the new number
c_{0}(I,R)
.
This leads to the following definition.

Definition 6.1

(Achilles–Manaresi
[
2
]
)
.

The
multiplicity sequence
of the ideal
I\subset R
is given by

\big(c_{0}(I,R),c_{1}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d}(I,R)\big)\;\in\;{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}.

In
Section 5
, we restricted ourselves to considering only the first sum transform
H_{\mathscr{H}}^{1}
(i.e., the polar multiplicities of
G
) because then we could use many desirable properties of polar multiplicities (see
[
13
]
,
[
38
, §8]
).

We now discuss when the polar schemes specialize module an element
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
that is part of a system of parameters of
R
(i.e.,
\dim(R/tR)=d-1
).
We say that the
i
-th polar scheme
P_{i}(I,X)

specializes
modulo the element
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
if
\dim\left(P_{i}(I,X)\cap V(t)\right)\leq d-i-1
and we have the equality of cycles

\big[P_{i}(I,X)\cap V(t)\big]_{d-i-1}\;=\;\big[P_{i}\left(I,X\cap V(t)\right)\big]_{d-i-1}\;\in\;Z_{d-i-1}(X).

We shall see that the question of whether polar schemes specialize is governed by the Segre numbers
c_{i}(I,R)
and
c_{i}(I,R/tR)
.
First, we have the following basic but useful observation.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume
Setup 5.10
.
Let
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be such that
\dim(R/tR)=d-1
.
If
\text{ht}\left(I,(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)\geq i+2
, then
P_{i}(I,X)
specializes modulo
t
.

Proof.

Let
\overline{R}:=R/\left((g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)
and notice that
I^{k}\overline{R}\cdot\left((t,g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty}\right)\overline{R}=0
for some
k>0
.
Thus the result follows as a direct consequence of
Theorem 5.12
(i).
∎

A very important technical result is the following theorem.
Our arguments below follow closely the ones in the proof of
[
48
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 6.3
.

Assume
Setup 5.10
and that
R
is equidimensional and catenary.
Let
1\leq i\leq d-1
.
Let
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be such that
\text{ht}\left((g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)\geq i
and
\text{ht}\left(I,(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)\geq i+1
.
Then the following statements hold:

(i)

\text{ht}\left((g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)\geq i+1
.

(ii)

c_{i}(I,R/tR)\geq c_{i}(I,R)
.

(iii)

If
c_{i}(I,R/tR)=c_{i}(I,R)
, then
\text{ht}\left(I,(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i}):_{R}I^{\infty},t\right)\geq i+2
.

Proof.

We may assume that
\dim(R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty})=d-i+1
because
R
is equidimensional and catenary (see
Remark 5.11
).
By utilizing
Theorem 5.12
(ii), we may assume
i=1
and substitute
R
by
R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i-1}):_{R}I^{\infty}
.
So, we are free to assume the conditions
i=1
,
\dim(R/tR)=d-1
,
0:_{R}I^{\infty}=0
and
\text{ht}(I,t)\geq 2
.
Hence
g_{1}
is a nonzerodivisor since it is a general element of
I
.
Set
g:=g_{1}
.
Since
g
is a general element of
I
, it follows that
\text{ht}((g):_{R}I^{\infty},t)\geq\text{ht}(g,t)\geq 2
; thus settling part (i).
We need to verify
c_{1}(I,R/tR)\geq c_{1}(I,R)
to prove part (ii).
Part (iii) follows by showing that if
c_{1}(I,R/tR)=c_{1}(I,R)
then
\text{ht}(I,(g):_{R}I^{\infty},t)\geq 3
.

The rest of the proof follows verbatim the arguments in
[
48
, page 962, proof of Theorem 3.3]
.
Consider the finite sets of primes
\Lambda:=\{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I,t)\mid\text{ht}(\mathfrak{p})=2\}
and
\Gamma:=\{\mathfrak{q}\in V(I)\mid\text{ht}(\mathfrak{q})=1\}
.
Thus, to prove the inequality
\text{ht}(I,(g):_{R}I^{\infty},t)\geq 3
, it suffices to show that for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda
we have
\mathfrak{p}\not\supset(g):_{R}I^{\infty}
or, equivalently,
IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\sqrt{gR_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
For each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda
, we define the finite sets

\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\mathfrak{q}\in{\rm Min}(g)\mid\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{p}\}\quad\text{ and }\quad\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}:=\{\mathfrak{q}\in V(I)\mid\mathfrak{q}\subset\mathfrak{p}\text{ and }\text{ht}(\mathfrak{q})=1\}.

Notice that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
and that an equality holds if and only if
IR_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset\sqrt{gR_{\mathfrak{p}}}
.
By utilizing the faithfully flat extension
R\rightarrow R[y]_{{\mathfrak{m}}R[y]}
, we may assume that the residue field of
R
is not an algebraic extension of a finite field, and thus we may assume that
g
is a general element of
IR_{\mathfrak{p}}
for each
\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda
(see
[
48
, Lemma 2.6]
).

We have the following

\displaystyle c_{1}(I,R/tR)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}e\left(I,R_{\mathfrak{p}}/tR_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

by
[
48
, Proposition 2.4(a)]
or
Theorem 5.12
(ii)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}e\left((g),R_{\mathfrak{p}}/tR_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

since
g
is general in
IR_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}e\left((t),R_{\mathfrak{p}}/gR_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

by
[
48
, Lemma 3.1]
since
g
is a nonzerodivisor

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/gR_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot e\left((t),R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

by the associativity formula

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda}\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/gR_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot e\left((t),R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

since
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\subseteq\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Gamma}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/gR_{\mathfrak{q}})\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in\Lambda,\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}}e\left((t),R_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{q}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{p})

by switching the summation

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Gamma}{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{q}}}(R_{\mathfrak{q}}/gR_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{q})

by
[
48
, Lemma 3.2]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Gamma}e((g),R_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{q})

by
[
21
, Corollary 1.2.12]

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\mathfrak{q}\in\Gamma}e(IR_{\mathfrak{q}},R_{\mathfrak{q}})\cdot e(R/\mathfrak{q})

since
g\in I

\displaystyle=c_{1}(I,R)

by
[
48
, Proposition 2.4(a)]
or
Theorem 5.12
(ii).

Therefore, we obtain the inequality
c_{1}(I,R/tR)\geq c_{1}(I,R)
and that
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{p}}=\Sigma_{\mathfrak{p}}
if an equality holds.
This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Important consequences of
Theorem 5.12
and
Theorem 6.3
are the following lexicographical upper-semicontinuity results.

Corollary 6.4
.

Assume that
R
is equidimensional and catenary, and let
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be such that
\dim(R/tR)=d-1
.
Suppose that
\text{ht}(I,0:_{R}I^{\infty},t)\geq 2
.
Then we get the inequality

\big(c_{1}(I,R/tR),c_{2}(I,R/tR),\ldots,c_{d-1}(I,R/tR)\big)\;\;\geq_{\rm lex}\;\;\big(c_{1}(I,R),c_{2}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d-1}(I,R)\big)

under the lexicographical order.

Corollary 6.5
.

Assume that
R
is equidimensional and universally catenary.
Let
I\subset J
be two
R
-ideals.
Then we get the inequality

\big(c_{0}(I,R),c_{1}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d}(I,R)\big)\;\;\geq_{\rm lex}\;\;\big(c_{0}(J,R),c_{1}(J,R),\ldots,c_{d}(J,R)\big)

under the lexicographical order.

Proof.

By replacing
R
by
R[y]_{({\mathfrak{m}},y)}
(where
y
is a new variable) and the ideals
I
and
J
by
(I,y)
and
(J,y)
, we may assume that
\text{ht}(I)\geq 1
and
\text{ht}(J)\geq 1
and that
c_{0}(I,R)=0
and
c_{0}(J,R)=0
; see
[
48
, Corollary 2.2(d)]
.

Consider the local ring
S=R[t]_{({\mathfrak{m}},t)}
(where
t
is a new variable).
Let
H=IS+tJS\subset S
.
Notice that
\text{ht}(H,0:_{S}H^{\infty},t)\geq\text{ht}(IS,t)\geq 2
,
H\cdot S/tS=I
and
HS_{{\mathfrak{m}}S}=JR[t]_{{\mathfrak{m}}R[t]}
.
Then we obtain the following inequalities

\displaystyle\big(c_{1}(I,R),c_{2}(I,R),\ldots,c_{d}(I,R)\big)

\displaystyle\;=\;\big(c_{1}(H,S/tS),c_{2}(H,S/tS),\ldots,c_{d}(H,S/tS)\big)

\displaystyle\;\geq_{\rm lex}\;\big(c_{1}(H,S),c_{2}(H,S/tS),\ldots,c_{d}(H,S)\big)

by
Corollary 6.4

\displaystyle\;\geq\;\big(c_{1}(H,S_{{\mathfrak{m}}S}),c_{2}(H,S_{{\mathfrak{m}}S}),\ldots,c_{d}(H,S_{{\mathfrak{m}}S})\big)

by
[
48
, Proposition 2.7]

\displaystyle\;=\;\big(c_{1}(J,R),c_{2}(J,R),\ldots,c_{d}(J,R)\big)

that settle the claim of the corollary.
∎

We now present a principle of specialization of integral dependence that we enunciate similarly to the ones in
[
24
, Theorem 4.7]
and
[
48
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 6.6

(Principle of Specialization of Integral Dependence – PSID)
.

Assume
Setup 5.1
and that
R
is equidimensional and universally catenary.
Let
t\in{\mathfrak{m}}
be such that
\dim(R/tR)=d-1
.
Suppose that
\text{ht}(I,t)\geq 2
and that
c_{i}(I,R/tR)=c_{i}(I,R)
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
.
Then, for any element
h\in R
, the following two conditions are equivalent:

(i)

h\in\overline{I}
.

(ii)

hR_{\mathfrak{p}}\in\overline{IR_{\mathfrak{p}}}
for all primes
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(R)
such that
t\not\in\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

Assume momentarily that
\dim(G/tG)<d
.
As
G
is equidimensional of dimension
d
(see
[
49
, Theorem 3.8]
,
[
51
, Lemma 2.2]
), it follows that the contraction of a minimal prime of
G
to
R
does not contain
t
.
This implies that no prime in the set
L(I):=\{\mathfrak{p}\in V(I)\mid\dim(R_{\mathfrak{p}})=\ell(IR_{\mathfrak{p}})\}
contains
t
.
Since every associated prime of
\overline{I}
belongs to
L(I)
(see
[
45
, 3.9 and 4.1]
), the result of the theorem follows.
Hence it remains to show
\dim(G/tG)<d
.

Since
\text{ht}(I)\geq 1
, it follows that
\dim(G/G_{+})=\dim(R/I)\leq d-1
and so no minimal prime of
G
contains
G_{+}
.
Set
E^{\prime}:=\text{Proj}(G/tG)
.
Thus
\dim(E^{\prime})<d-1
if and only if
\dim(G/tG)<d
.
From
[
13
, Proposition 2.10]
, we obtain
m_{d-1}^{d-1}(G/tG)=e_{d-1}(\text{H}^{0}(E^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{E^{\prime}}))
and so it follows that
m_{d-1}^{d-1}(G/tG)=0
because
\text{Supp}(\text{H}^{0}(E^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{E^{\prime}}))\subset V(I,t)
and by assumption we have the inequality
\text{ht}(I,t)\geq 2
.
We now assume the notation and assumptions of
Setup 5.10
.
From
[
13
, Proposition 2.10]
and
(3)
, we get

m_{d-1}^{d-1-i}(G/tG)\;=\;m_{d-1-i}^{d-1-i}\left(G/(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{i},t)G\right)\;=\;m_{d-1-i}^{d-1-i}(G_{i}/tG_{i}),

where
G_{i}=\text{gr}_{IR_{i}}(R_{i})
and
R_{i}=R/(g_{1},\ldots,g_{i})R
.
Therefore, by utilizing
Theorem 5.12
(ii) and
Theorem 6.3
, we can prove inductively that

m_{d-1}^{d-1-i}(G/tG)=0\quad\text{ for all }\quad 0\leq i\leq d-1.

Due to
[
13
, Lemma 2.3]
, we obtain
\dim(E^{\prime})<d-1
, and so it follows that
\dim(G/tG)<d
. ∎

The following theorem contains our new criteria for integral dependence in terms of the invariants
m_{i}(I,R)
and
\nu_{i}(I,R)
.

Theorem 6.7
.

Assume
Setup 5.1
and that
R
is equidimensional and universally catenary.
Let
I\subset J
be two
R
-ideals.
Suppose the following two conditions:

(a)

o(I)=o(J)
.

(b)

I
satisfies the
\mathscr{G}
-parameter condition generically
(
see
Notation 5.7
)
.

Then the following are equivalent:

(i)

J
is integral over
I
.

(ii)

m_{i}(I,R)=m_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
.

(iii)

\nu_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.

Proof.

Let
\delta:=o(I)=o(J)
be the order of
I
and
J
.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii) & (iii):
If
J
is integral over
I
, then it is known that
m_{i}(I,R)=m_{i}(J,R)
and
c_{i}(I,R)=c_{i}(J,R)
(see
[
59
, Corollary 3.8]
and
[
48
, Theorem 4.2]
).
This settles both implications (without assuming the conditions (a) and (b)).

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Suppose that
m_{i}(I,R)=m_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
.
Then
Proposition 5.8
yields

\nu_{i}(I,R)\;=\;\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;=\;\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(J,R)\;\leq\;\nu_{i}(J,R)

for all
1\leq i\leq d
.
This implies that
c_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(I,R)-m_{i}(I,R)\leq\nu_{i}(J,R)-m_{i}(J,R)=c_{i}(J,R)
for all
1\leq i\leq d
.
On the other hand, we have
c_{0}(I,R)=e(R)-m_{0}(I,R)=e(R)-m_{0}(J,R)=c_{0}(J,R)
.
Therefore,
J
is integral over
I
due to
[
48
, Theorem 4.2]
.

(iii)
\Rightarrow
(i): Suppose that
\nu_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.
From
Proposition 5.8
, we obtain

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;=\;\nu_{i}(I,R)\;=\;\nu_{i}(J,R)\;\geq\;\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(J,R)

for all
1\leq i\leq d
.
It follows that
c_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(I,R)-m_{i}(I,R)\leq\nu_{i}(J,R)-m_{i}(J,R)=c_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
.
We also have
c_{d}(I,R)=\nu_{d}(I,R)=\nu_{d}(J,R)=c_{d}(J,R)
.
Finally,
J
is integral over
I
by invoking
[
48
, Theorem 4.2]
once again.
∎

The next remark complements
[
59
]
*Corollary 4.3.

Remark 6.8
.

Let
R
be a regular local ring of dimension at least two and let
I
be an ideal of height one. Write
I=aH
where
H
is an ideal with
\text{ht}(H)\geq 2
.
Then
m_{1}(I,R)=o(H)
.

Proof.

We may assume that the residue field of
R
is infinite. Let
g
be a general element of
H
. According to
Theorem 5.12
(i), we have
m_{1}(I,R)=e(R/agR:_{R}I^{\infty}).

On the other hand,

agR:I^{\infty}\;=\;(agR:_{R}aH):_{R}(aH)^{\infty}\;=\;(gR:H):_{R}(aH)^{\infty}\;=\;(gR:_{R}H^{\infty}):_{R}(aR)^{\infty}\;=\;gR.

Finally,
e(R/gR)=o(g)=o(H)
.
∎

The remark above shows that the polar multiplicities of an ideal of height one in a two-dimensional regular local ring only depend on the order of the saturation of the ideal with respect to its unmixed part, and hence cannot detect integral dependence.

Example 6.9

(Polar multiplicities do not detect integral dependence)
.

Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},x_{1}]
be a polynomial ring over a field.
Consider the ideals
I=(x_{0}^{2},\,x_{0}x_{1}^{2})\,\subsetneq\,J=(x_{0}^{2},\,x_{0}x_{1})
.
We have the following table of values:

\begin{array}[]{l|r}\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{0}(I,R)=0&m_{0}(I,R)=1&\nu_{0}(I,R)=1\\
c_{1}(I,R)=1&m_{1}(I,R)=1&\nu_{1}(I,R)=2\\
c_{2}(I,R)=4&m_{2}(I,R)=0&\nu_{2}(I,R)=4\\
\end{array}&\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{0}(J,R)=0&m_{0}(J,R)=1&\nu_{0}(J,R)=1\\
c_{1}(J,R)=1&m_{1}(J,R)=1&\nu_{1}(I,R)=2\\
c_{2}(J,R)=2&m_{2}(J,R)=0&\nu_{2}(I,R)=2.\\
\end{array}\end{array}

Here the invariants
m_{i}
fail to detect the fact that
\overline{I}\subsetneq\overline{J}
.
However, both invariants
c_{i}
and
\nu_{i}
do detect this.

From the previous example, we see that the invariants
\nu_{i}
seem to carry more information related to the integral closure of an ideal.
Nevertheless, we have the following more delicate example where the invariants
\nu_{i}
fail to detect integral dependence.

Example 6.10

(Polar-Segre multiplicities do not detect integral dependence)
.

Let
{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}=\text{Proj}(\mathbb{k}[s,t])\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}=\text{Proj}(\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{3}])
,
(s:t)\mapsto(s^{4}:s^{3}t:st^{3}:t^{4})
be the parametrization of the rational quartic
\mathcal{C}\subset{\mathbb{P}}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
.
Let
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{3}]/\mathfrak{P}
be the homogeneous coordinate ring of
\mathcal{C}
.
Consider the ideals
I=(x_{0}^{2},\,x_{0}x_{2}x_{3},\,x_{1}^{2}x_{3}^{2})R\,\subsetneq\,J=(x_{0}^{2},\,x_{0}x_{2},\,x_{0}x_{3},\,x_{1}x_{3}^{2})R
.
We get the following table of values:

\begin{array}[]{l|r}\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{0}(I,R)=0&m_{0}(I,R)=4&\nu_{0}(I,R)=4\\
c_{1}(I,R)=5&m_{1}(I,R)=3&\nu_{1}(I,R)=8\\
c_{2}(I,R)=14&m_{2}(I,R)=0&\nu_{2}(I,R)=14\\
\end{array}&\begin{array}[]{lll}c_{0}(J,R)=0&m_{0}(J,R)=4&\nu_{0}(J,R)=4\\
c_{1}(J,R)=3&m_{1}(J,R)=5&\nu_{1}(I,R)=8\\
c_{2}(J,R)=14&m_{2}(J,R)=0&\nu_{2}(I,R)=14.\\

Therefore the invariants
\nu_{i}
fail to detect the fact that

.

6.1.
Lexicographic upper semicontinuity of Segre numbers

In this subsection, we show that Segre numbers have a lexicographic upper semicontinuous behavior in families.
This generalizes a result due to Gaffney and Gassler
[
24
]
.
We now use the following setup.

Setup 6.11
.

Let
\iota:A\hookrightarrow R
be a flat injective homomorphism of finite type of Noetherian rings.
Suppose that the inclusion
\iota
has a section; i.e., there is a homomorphism
\pi:R\twoheadrightarrow A
such that
\pi\circ\iota={\rm id}_{A}
.
Consider the ideal
Q:=\text{Ker}(\pi)\subset R
.

Notice that given prime
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, we always have that
QR(\mathfrak{p})
is a prime ideal in the fiber
R(\mathfrak{p})
.
Indeed, we get the inclusion
\iota_{\mathfrak{p}}:A/\mathfrak{p}\hookrightarrow R/\mathfrak{p}R
with section
\pi_{\mathfrak{p}}:R/\mathfrak{p}R\twoheadrightarrow A/\mathfrak{p}
, and since
\text{Ker}(\pi_{\mathfrak{p}})={Q\cdot R/\mathfrak{p}R}
, it follows that
{Q\cdot R/\mathfrak{p}R}
is a prime ideal satisfying
\left(R/\mathfrak{p}R\right)_{Q\cdot R/\mathfrak{p}R}=R(\mathfrak{p})_{QR(\mathfrak{p})}
.
Moreover
{QR(\mathfrak{p})}
is a maximal ideal in
R(\mathfrak{p})
.
Thus, although the fiber
R(\mathfrak{p})
might not be a local ring, we do get a distinguished local ring
R(\mathfrak{p})_{QR(\mathfrak{p})}
.
To simplify notation, we say that
S(\mathfrak{p}):=R(\mathfrak{p})_{QR(\mathfrak{p})}
is the
distinguished fiber
of
\mathfrak{p}
.

Our main result regrading the behavior of Segre numbers in families is the following.

Theorem 6.12
.

Assume
Setup 6.11
and let
I\subset R
be an ideal.
Assume that for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, the fibers
R(\mathfrak{p})
are equidimensional of the same dimension
d
and

\text{ht}(I(\mathfrak{p}))>0
.
Then the function

\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\;\mapsto\;\big(c_{1}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right),c_{2}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right),\ldots,c_{d}\left(I,S(\mathfrak{p})\right)\big)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{d}

is upper semicontinuous with respect to the lexicographic order.

Proof.

By the topological Nagata criterion (see
Remark 3.2
), it suffices to show the following two conditions:

(i)

Under the assumption that
A
is a domain, there is a dense open subset
U\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
where the above function is constant.

(ii)

If
\mathfrak{p}\supset\mathfrak{q}
, then
\big(c_{1}(I,S(\mathfrak{p})),\ldots,S(\mathfrak{p})\big)\geq_{\rm lex}\big(c_{1}(I,S(\mathfrak{q})),\ldots,c_{d}(I,S(\mathfrak{q}))\big)
.

First, we prove the claim (i).
We assume
A
is a domain, thus by Grothendieck’s Generic Freeness Lemma (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Theorem 24.1]
,
[
20
, Theorem 14.4]
) there is an element
0\neq a\in A
such that the bigraded components of
\text{gr}_{Q}(\text{gr}_{I}(R))\otimes_{A}A_{a}
and the graded components of
\text{gr}_{I}(R)\otimes_{A}A_{a}
are free
A_{a}
-modules.
Set
U:=D(a)\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Then, for any
\mathfrak{p}\in U
, we have the isomorphisms

\text{gr}_{Q}(\text{gr}_{I}(R))\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\;\cong\;\text{gr}_{Q}(\text{gr}_{I}(R)\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\;\cong\;\text{gr}_{Q}(\text{gr}_{I}(R(\mathfrak{p}))).

This shows shows that the above function is constant on
U
.

Next, we prove the claim (ii).
Notice that we can reduce modulo
\mathfrak{q}
and localize at
\mathfrak{p}
.
Thus we assume
A
is a local domain with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}=0
.
Let
K:={\text{Quot}}(A)
.
By
[
30
, Exercise II.4.11]
or
[
28
, Proposition 7.1.7]
, there is a discrete valuation ring
V
of
K
that dominates
A
; that is,
A\subset V
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{n}\cap A
where
\mathfrak{n}=(t)\subset V
is the closed point of
{\text{Spec}}(V)
.
Let
R_{V}:=R\otimes_{A}V
.
Notice that
\iota^{\prime}:V\hookrightarrow R_{V}
is flat and has a section
\pi^{\prime}:R_{V}\twoheadrightarrow V
with
\text{Ker}(\pi^{\prime})=QR_{V}
.
We get a field extension
\kappa(\mathfrak{p})\hookrightarrow\kappa(\mathfrak{n})
and the following isomorphisms

R_{V}(\mathfrak{n})\;\cong\;(R\otimes_{A}V)\otimes_{V}\kappa(\mathfrak{n})\;\cong\;R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{n})\;\cong\;(R\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p}))\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\kappa(\mathfrak{n})\;\cong\;R(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\kappa(\mathfrak{n}).

This gives the isomorphisms
I^{v}R_{V}(\mathfrak{n})\cong I^{v}R(\mathfrak{p})\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\kappa(\mathfrak{n})
and so it follows

(4)

\text{gr}_{Q}\left(\text{gr}_{I}\left(R_{V}(\mathfrak{n})\right)\right)\;\cong\;\text{gr}_{Q}\left(\text{gr}_{I}\left(R(\mathfrak{p})\right)\right)\otimes_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}\kappa(\mathfrak{n}).

Let
T:={(R_{V})}_{(t,Q)R_{V}}
.
From
(4)
, we have

(5)

c_{i}(I,S(\mathfrak{p}))\;=\;c_{i}(I,T/tT).

Since
T_{QT}=R(\mathfrak{q})_{QR(\mathfrak{q})}=S(\mathfrak{q})
, we also obtain

(6)

c_{i}(I,S(\mathfrak{q}))\;=\;c_{i}(I,T_{QT}).

To use
Corollary 6.4
, we now verify that the ideal
IT\subset T
satisfies the required assumptions.
By applying
Lemma 6.14
(i) to the ring
R(\mathfrak{p})
, we conclude that the fibers of
R_{V}
over
V
are equidimensional of dimension
d
.
Hence due to
Lemma 6.14
(ii),
T
is equidimensional of dimension
d+1
and universally catenary and
\dim(T/tT)=\dim(T)-1
.
Since
R(\mathfrak{p})\hookrightarrow T/tT
is a flat homomorphism,
\text{ht}((I,t)T/tT)\geq\text{ht}(I(\mathfrak{p}))\geq 1
, hence
\text{ht}(t,IT)\geq 2
because
t
is regular on
T
.
Now
(5)
and
Corollary 6.4
yield

\big(c_{1}(I,S(\mathfrak{p})),\ldots,c_{d}(I,S(\mathfrak{p}))\big)\;=\;\big(c_{1}(I,T/tT),\ldots,c_{d}(I,T/tT)\big)\;\geq_{\rm lex}\;\big(c_{1}(I,T),\ldots,c_{d}(I,T)\big).

Finally, from
[
48
, Proposition 2.7]
and
(6)
, we get

\big(c_{1}(I,T),\ldots,c_{d}(I,T)\big)\;\geq\;\big(c_{1}(I,T_{QT}),\ldots,c_{d}(I,T_{QT})\big)\;=\;\big(c_{1}(I,S(\mathfrak{q})),\ldots,c_{d}(I,S(\mathfrak{q}))\big).

This concludes the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 6.13
.

A basic situation where the assumptions of
Setup 6.11
hold is the following.
Assume
R=A[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
is a positively graded polynomial ring over
A
, and let
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
In this case, the natural projection
\pi:R\twoheadrightarrow A\cong R/{\mathfrak{m}}
is a section of the inclusion
\iota:A\rightarrow R
.
Therefore, given a prime
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, the distinguished fiber of
\mathfrak{p}
is the localization
R(\mathfrak{p})_{{\mathfrak{m}}R(\mathfrak{p})}
of the fiber
R(\mathfrak{p})\cong\kappa(\mathfrak{p})[x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}]
at the graded irrelevant ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}R(\mathfrak{p})
.

The following technical lemma was used in the proof of
Theorem 6.12
.

Lemma 6.14
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

Let
B
be a finitely generated algebra over a field
\mathbb{k}
.
If
B
is equidimensional, then
B\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}L
is equidimensional of the same dimension for any field extension
L
of
\mathbb{k}
.

(ii)

Let
V
be a discrete valuation ring with uniformizing parameter
t
.
Let
j:V\hookrightarrow B
be a flat injective homomorphism of finite type that has a section
\tau:B\twoheadrightarrow V
, and let
\mathfrak{Q}=\text{Ker}(\tau)
.
Let
T=B_{(t,\mathfrak{Q})}
.
Suppose that the fibers of
j
are equidimensional of dimension
e
. Then
T
is equidimensional of dimension
e+1
and universally catenary, and
\dim(T/tT)=e
.

Proof.

(i) By
[
44
, Theorem 23.2]
, we have
\text{Ass}\left(B\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}L\right)=\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}\in\text{Ass}(B)}\text{Ass}\left(B/\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}L\right)
.
The result follows because each minimal prime of
B/\mathfrak{p}\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}\mathbb{L}
has the same dimension as
B/\mathfrak{p}
(see
[
30
, Exercise II.3.20(f)]
).

(ii)
Let
K={\text{Quot}}(V)
.
Since
B
is
V
-flat, it follows that
t
is a nonzerodivisor on
B
.
Let
\mathfrak{P}\in{\text{Spec}}(B)
be a minimal prime contained in
(t,\mathfrak{Q})
.
Such
\mathfrak{P}
does not contain
t
, hence it corresponds to a minimal in the fiber
B\otimes_{V}K
.
By applying the dimension formula
[
44
, Theorems 15.5, 15.6]
to the inclusion
V\hookrightarrow B/\mathfrak{P}
, we obtain

\dim\left((B/\mathfrak{P})_{(t,\mathfrak{Q})}\right)\;=\;\dim(V)+{\rm trdeg}_{V}(B/\mathfrak{P})=1+e.

This implies that
T
is equidimensional of dimension
e+1
.
As
T
is finitely generated over a discrete valuation ring, it is universally catenary.
Observe that
\dim(T/tT)=e
since
t
is regular on
T
.
∎

6.2.
The case of equigenerated ideals

This brief subsection is dedicated to the family of equigenerated ideals, where our results are particularly satisfying.

Corollary 6.15
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R
be a finitely generated standard graded
\mathbb{k}
-algebra of dimension
d
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=R_{+}
be the graded irrelevant ideal.
Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal generated by elements of the same degree
\delta\geq 1
.
Then the following statements hold:

(1)

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(I,R)
for all
1\leq i\leq d
.

(2)

For any homogeneous ideal
J\subset R
containing
I
such that
o(J)=\delta
, the following are equivalent:

(i)

J
is integral over
I
.

(ii)

m_{i}(I,R)=m_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d-1
.

(iii)

\nu_{i}(I,R)=\nu_{i}(J,R)
for all
0\leq i\leq d
.

First proof.

We may assume that
\mathbb{k}
is infinite.
Notice that part (1) implies part (2) (indeed, by
Proposition 5.8
(iii), the statement (1) is equivalent to the condition (b) of
Theorem 6.7
).
Since
I
is equigenerated in degree
\delta
, we see the Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}(I)
as a standard bigraded algebra with
\big[\mathscr{R}(I)\big]_{(v,n)}=\big[I^{v}\big]_{n+v\delta}
.
Thus Nakayama’s lemma yields the isomorphisms

\big[\text{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}\left(\mathscr{R}(I)\right)\big]_{(v,n)}\;=\;\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}I^{v}}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}I^{v}}\;\cong\;\big[I^{v}\big]_{n+v\delta}\;=\;\big[\mathscr{R}(I)\big]_{(v,n)}.

Let
g
be a general element of
I
.
Since
\left[\left(0:_{\mathscr{R}(I)}g\right)\right]_{(v,*)}=0
for
v\gg 0
(see
Remark 5.6
), it follows that
\left[\left(0:_{\text{gr}_{\mathfrak{m}}(\mathscr{R}(I))}\text{in}(g)\right)\right]_{(v,*)}=0
for
v\gg 0
.
The result follows from
Proposition 5.8
(iii).
∎

Second proof.

Again, we see
\mathscr{R}(I)
as a standard bigraded algebra with
\big[\mathscr{R}(I)\big]_{(v,n)}=\big[I^{v}\big]_{n+v\delta}
.
Therefore
\text{gr}_{I}(I)\cong\mathscr{R}(I)/I\mathscr{R}(I)
is also naturally a standard bigraded algebra.
Notice that we have a short exact sequence

0\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(I)(1,-\delta)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}(I)\rightarrow\text{gr}_{I}(R)\rightarrow 0.

Then a basic computation with the polynomial functions
P_{\mathscr{R}(I)}(v,n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\mathscr{R}(I)]_{(v,k)}\right)
and
P_{\text{gr}_{I}(R)}(v,n)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\text{gr}_{I}(R)]_{(v,k)}\right)
gives the equality

\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R)\;=\;m_{i}(I,R)+c_{i}(I,R)\;=\;\nu_{i}(I,R)

for all
1\leq i\leq d
(see
[
13
, Remark 2.9]
).
∎

As a consequence, in the equigenerated case, we get an alternative proof of the lexicographic upper semicontinuity of Segre numbers (see
Theorem 6.12
).

Remark 6.16
.

Let
A
be a Noetherian domain and
R=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a standard graded polynomial ring.
Let
\mathcal{F}:{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{r}\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}_{A}^{s}
be a rational map with representative
\mathbf{f}=(f_{0}:\cdots:f_{s})
such that
f_{0},\ldots,f_{s}
are homogeneous elements of degree
\delta>0
.
Let
I=(f_{0},\ldots,f_{s})\subset R
and assume that
I(\mathfrak{p})\neq 0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Notice that
d_{i}(\mathcal{F}(\mathfrak{p}))=m_{r-i}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
By
Theorem 4.3
, each function
\mathfrak{p}\mapsto m_{i}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))
is lower semicontinuous.
Notice that we always have
m_{0}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))=1
.
Therefore, the equality
c_{i}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))=\delta\cdot m_{i-1}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))-m_{i}(I,R(\mathfrak{p}))
implies that the function

\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\;\mapsto\;\big(c_{1}\left(I,R(\mathfrak{p})\right),c_{2}\left(I,R(\mathfrak{p})\right),\ldots,c_{r+1}\left(I,R(\mathfrak{p})\right)\big)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}^{r+1}

is upper semicontinuous with respect to the lexicographic order.

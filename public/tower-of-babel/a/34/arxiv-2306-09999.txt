Sobolev spaces and uniform boundary representations
Kevin Boucher, Ján Špakula

Abstract
. We prove uniform boundedness of certain boundary representations on appropriate fractional Sobolev spaces W s , p W^{s,p} with p > 1 p>1 for arbitrary Gromov hyperbolic groups. These are closed subspaces of L p L^{p} and in particular Hilbert spaces in the case p = 2 p=2 . This construction allows us, for an appropriate choice of p p , to approximate the trivial representation through uniformly bounded representations. This phenomenon does not have analogue in the setting of isometric representations whenever the hyperbolic group considered has the Property (T) [ 4 ] . The key is the introduction of a notion of metrically conformal operator on a metric space endowed with a conformal structure à la Mineyev [ 56 ] and a metric analogue of the isomorphisms of Sobolev spaces induced by the Cayley transform [ 3 ] .

1.1.
Landscape

The theory of spherical representations deals with representations realised as spaces of ordinary functions over the flag manifold of semi-simple Lie groups.
In the broader context of geometric groups such as Gromov hyperbolic groups, the flag manifold is substituted by a certain type of boundary. We refer to these representations as

For readers familiar with the language of groupoids, the principal idea is the following. Any uniformly bounded representation of an

Starting with a uniformly bounded representation

We proceed “backwards”: by describing a convenient (metric) bundle over

The (metric) bundle we will consider is the collection

Example 2.3

Suppose that

3.2.
Metric measure spaces and Möbius maps

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a metric measure space, that is, a complete metric space
(Z,d)
without isolated points, and a Borel measure
\nu
on

.
We will exclusively work with ones which are also Ahlfors
D
-regular, i.e. for which we have
\nu(B(x,r))\asymp r^{D}
for

and

[

\nu

is
the
D

Recall that a Möbius map is a function
g:(Z,d)\to(Y,d^{\prime})
between metric space which preserves cross-ratios, i.e. the quantity
[x,y;z,w]=\frac{d(x,z)d(y,w)}{d(x,w)d(y,z)}
, whenever this expression makes sense. We denote by
\Mob(Z)
the group of self-homeomorphisms of
(Z,d)
. For any
g\in\Mob(Z)
there exists a
metric derivative
of
g
, that is, a Lipschitz function
|g^{\prime}|:Z\to(0,\infty)
satisfying the
geometric mean value property

(3.1)

d^{2}(g\xi,g\eta)=|g^{\prime}|(\xi)\,|g^{\prime}|(\eta)\,d^{2}(\xi,\eta)\qquad\text{for all }g\in\Mob(Z),\,\xi,\eta\in Z.

see e.g.
[
58
, Lemmas 6, 7]
(cf. also
[
63
]
). Now, if
\nu
is the
D
-dimensional Hausdorff measure which is also Ahlfors regular,

preserves the class of
\nu
and (a power of)

gives the Radon–Nikodym derivative of the action (see e.g.
[
58

(3.2)

\mathrm{d}\nu(g\xi)=|g^{\prime}|^{D}(\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\qquad\text{for all }g\in\Mob(Z)\text{ and }\xi\in Z.

We further have a cocycle formula

(3.3)

|(gh)^{\prime}|(\xi)=|g^{\prime}|(h\xi)\,|h^{\prime}|(\xi)\qquad\text{for all }g,h\in\Mob(Z)\text{ and }\xi\in Z.

It follows that the formula (
1.6
) in Theorem
2

).
Finally, the above formulas imply that we have the following formal adjoint relation (with respect to the

-pairing) for

(3.4)

\displaystyle\pi_{z}^{(p)}(g)^{*}=\pi_{-\overline{z}}^{(q)}(g^{-1}).

As defined in the Introduction, for

3.3.
Lorentz spaces

Recall that Lorentz spaces
[
52
,
41
]
are function spaces that interpolate between
L^{p}
-spaces. We recall the results we will need; for general theory see e.g. textbook
[
33
]
. Let
(Z,\nu)
be a non-atomic
\sigma
-finite measure space. For a measurable
f:Z\to\mathbb{C}
, denote its non-increasing rearrangement by
f^{*}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
f^{*}(t)=\inf\{s>0\mid\nu(\{|f|>s\})\leq t\}
. For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, define the
(p,q)
–Lorentz norm
by

\left\|f\,|L(Z;p,q)\right\|=\left\|t\mapsto t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\,|L^{q}(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}t}{t})\right\|.

We write also
L(p,q)
when the measure space is clear from the context. One has that
L(p,p)=L^{p}
, and
L(p,\infty)
is the
weak
L^{p}
-space
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
33
, Proposition 1.4.9]
)
.

If
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
, then

\left\|f\,|L(p,q)\right\|=p^{1/q}\left\|s\mapsto s\cdot\nu(\{|f|>s\})^{1/p}\,|L^{q}(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}s}{s})\right\|.

Proposition 3.2

(Inclusions
[
33
, Proposition 1.4.10]
)
.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
1\leq q<r\leq\infty
, one has
L(p,q)\subseteq L(p,r)
.

Proposition 3.3

(Hölder-type inequality
[
33
, Theorem 1.4.16]
)
.

Let
p,p^{\prime},q,q^{\prime}\in[1,\infty]
satisfy
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}
. Then for
f\in L(p,q)
and
h\in L(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})
, we have

\uprightintop\displaylimits|f(\xi)h(\xi)|d\xi\leq\|f\mid L(p,q)\|\cdot\|h\mid L(p^{\prime},q^{\prime})\|.

Lemma 3.4

(
[
33
, Remark 1.4.7]
)
.

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
,

For any

\left\||u|^{a}\mid L(p,1)\right\|=\left\|u\mid L(ap,a)\right\|^{a}.

4.
Fractional Sobolev spaces

For this section, let
(Y,d,\nu)
be an Ahlfors
D
-regular quasi-metric measure space.
Fix also
1\leq p<\infty
and
s\in(0,\infty)
.

Given a measurable function
\phi:Y\to\mathbb{C}
, recall from (
1.2
) that its
Gagliardo seminorm
is given by:

(4.1)

[\phi]_{s,p}=\left(\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Y\times Y}\frac{|\phi(\xi)-\phi(\eta)|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)\right)^{1/p}.

Recall from the Introduction that the
fractional Sobolev space

W^{s,p}(Y)
on
(Y,d,\nu)
is the function space

W^{s,p}(Y)=\{\phi\in L^{p}(Y,\nu):[\phi]_{s,p}<\infty\}
,
endowed with norm

\|\phi\,|\,W^{s,p}(Y)\|^{p}=\|\phi\,|\,L^{p}\|^{p}+[\phi]_{s,p}^{p}
,
also called the Gagliardo, or Aronszajn, or Slobodeckij norm
[
26
, Section 2]
.
We also need the homogeneous fractional Sobolev space
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Y)
, which is defined as the completion of the set of compactly supported, measurable functions
\phi:Z\to\mathbb{C}
with
[\phi]_{s,p}<\infty

in the semi-norm
[\cdot]_{s,p}
. Note that for instance constant functions have zero Gagliardo seminorm. For a discussion about homogeneous Sobolev spaces in the context of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
17
]
and references therein.

Remark 4.1
.

When
Y=\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 1
) with the Euclidean metric and Lebesgue measure,
W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{0\}
for
s\geq 1
, see the discussion after
[
26
, Proposition 2.2]
. In this case, for
s\in(0,1)
, we obtain the fractional Sobolev spaces that interpolate between
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and the Sobolev spaces
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of
L^{p}
-functions with
p
-integrable gradients
[
26
]
.

On the other hand if
Y
is totally disconnected, for instance a finite dimensional vector space over the
q
-adic numbers (
q
prime) viewed as a metric space
and its corresponding Hausdorff measure, then
W^{s,p}(Y)
is dense in
L^{p}(Y)
for all
s>0

[
8
]
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that both
W^{s,p}(Y)
and
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Y)
both contain compactly supported Lipschitz functions.
This is a classical consequence of
D
-Ahlfors regularity: The value of
[\phi]_{s,p}
for any compactly supported Lipschitz function
\phi:Y\to\mathbb{C}
is bounded from above by an integral for the form
\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{A\times A}C/d^{\alpha}(\xi,\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)
for some
0\leq\alpha<D
, a bounded set
A\subset Y
and a constant
0\leq C<\infty
. Any such integral is finite, cf. for example Lemma
6.1
.

Consequently,
W^{s,p}(Y)
is dense in
L^{p}(Y)
, and both
C_{c}(Y)\cap W^{s,p}(Y)
and
C_{c}(Y)\cap\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Y)
are dense in
C_{0}(Y)
. In fact, they are also dense in
W^{s,p}(Y)
and
\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Y)
respectively, i.e. our Sobolev spaces are
regular
. This has been communicated to us by D. Gerontogiannis (private communication; a trace theory argument).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
(Y,d,\nu)
be an Ahlfors
D

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

5.

The purpose of this section is to prove the following Theorem. In this section, the symbols

Theorem 5

Let

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a bounded Ahlfors
D
-regular metric measure space and consider
(Z_{a},d_{a},\nu_{a})
as defined in (

). Let

Proof.

We need to check that

is bounded. We can assume

\displaystyle\leq\uprightintop\displaylimits_{0}^{t^{-(2D)/(k\alpha)}}\hskip-15.00002pt\nu\left(\left\{d^{-2D}(\cdot,a)>s\right\}\right)\mathrm{d}s

∎

Theorem 6
.

Let
(Z,d,\nu)
be a bounded Ahlfors
D
-regular metric measure space, and for each

we consider
(Z_{a},d_{a},\nu_{a})
as defined in (

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, and

with

. Denote

The operator

\displaystyle\Omega_{z,p}(a)

\displaystyle:W^{s,p}(Z)\subset L^{p}(Z)\rightarrow\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Z_{a}),

\displaystyle\Omega_{z,p}(a)\phi(\xi)

Take

\displaystyle\left\|\Omega_{z,p}(a)\phi|\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Z_{a})\right\|^{p}

\displaystyle=\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}_{a}}\frac{\left|\Omega_{z,p}(a)\phi(\xi)-\Omega_{z,p}(a)\phi(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}_{a}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:C_a-deriv-formula}}}{{=}}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}_{a}}\frac{\left|d^{\frac{2D}{p}-2z}(\xi,a)\phi(\xi)-d^{\frac{2D}{p}-2z}(\eta,a)\phi(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}_{a}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\eta)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Z_a-d_a-nu_a-defn}}}{{=}}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\left|d^{2\left(\frac{D}{p}-z\right)}(\xi,a)\phi(\xi)-d^{2\left(\frac{D}{p}-z\right)}(\eta,a)\phi(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\cdot\frac{1}{d^{\left(\frac{D}{p}-s\right)p}(\xi,a)}\frac{1}{d^{\left(\frac{D}{p}-s\right)p}(\eta,a)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)

\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{\left|\frac{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\xi,a)}{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\eta,a)}\phi(\xi)-\frac{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\eta,a)}{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\xi,a)}\phi(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)\\
\leq 3^{p-1}\left\|\phi|W^{s,p}(Z)\right\|^{p}+2\cdot 3^{p-1}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\,\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\frac{\left|1-\frac{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\eta,a)}{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\xi,a)}\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\cdot|\phi(\eta)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta).

Using Lorentz–Hölder inequality (Proposition
3.3
), Lemma
3.4

6.4
(as

\displaystyle\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\,\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}

\displaystyle\frac{\left|1-\frac{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\eta,a)}{d^{{D}/{p}-z}(\xi,a)}\right|^{p}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\cdot|\phi(\eta)|^{p}\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)

\displaystyle\prec_{z,p}\left\|\phi\left|L\left(\tfrac{pD}{D-sp},p\right)\right.\right\|^{p}\prec_{s,p}\left\|\phi|W^{s,p}(Z)\right\|^{p}.

To finish the proof, we need to show that

is a limit (in
[\cdot]_{s,p}
) of compactly supported functions on
Z_{a}
. First, we can assume that
\phi
(and hence also
\Omega_{z,p}(a)\phi
) is a bounded function, by considering cut-offs of
\phi
, by the argument at the beginning of the proof of
[
26
, Theorem 6.5]
. Next, we prove the following statement: if

is a bounded measurable function with
[\psi]_{s,p}<\infty
, then it is a
[\cdot]_{s,p}
-limit of boundedly supported ones. For this, fix

, and consider functions

for

, such that

,

, and

Observe that for

\left|\psi(\xi)f_{R}(\xi)-\psi(\eta)f_{R}(\eta)\right|\leq|\psi(\xi)-\phi(\eta)|+K\|\psi|L^{\infty}\|\cdot d_{a}(\xi,\eta).

Consequently (cf. Remark

independently of

Then

∎

In this Section, we deduce Theorem

from Theorem

much like we explained in Section

Let us recall the setup of Theorem
2
. Let
(Z,d)
be a complete metric space without isolated points, let
\nu
be the
D
-dimensional Hausdorff measure on

(Z,d,\nu)
is Ahlfors
D
-regular. Let

,

and

with

. Denote

.
Recall that we are considering the bundle

, and

,
\Pi^{(p)}_{z}(g;a)\phi=|g^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{p}-z}(a)\,g^{*}\phi
, are isometries.

(8.1)

\left\|\Pi_{z}^{(p)}(g;a)\phi\left|\accentset{\mbox{\large.}}{W}^{s,p}(Z_{ga})\right.\right\|^{p}\\
\begin{aligned} &\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:defn-of-[]sp-intro}}}{{=}}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z_{ga}}\frac{\left|\Pi_{z}^{(p)}(g;a)\phi(\xi)-\Pi_{z}^{(p)}(g;a)\phi(\eta)\right|^{p}}{d_{ga}^{D+sp}(\xi\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(\eta)\\

Furthermore, they satisfy the relation of being an (isometric) representation of

A groupoid picture

Assume for simplicity that
G

Denote
\mathcal{Z}=Z\times Z
for the pair groupoid. The transformation groupoid
\mathcal{G}=G\ltimes Z

by
\mathcal{G}\to G\to\text{Aut}(\mathcal{Z})
. One can then define the
fibred semi-direct product groupoid
,
\mathcal{G}\rtimes\mathcal{Z}

As a topological space,
\mathcal{G}\ltimes\mathcal{Z}
is the fibre product

\mathcal{G}\ltimes\mathcal{Z}=_{\text{def.}}\{(g,z):t_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)=s_{\mathcal{G}}(g)\}\subset\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{Z},

with space of objects
Z
, and source and target map given respectively by

and

. For

denote

.
The maps

and

are continuous, and
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{Z}
respectively identify with subsets

and
\{(f_{t_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)},z):z\in\mathcal{Z}\}

The multiplication on
\mathcal{G}\ltimes\mathcal{Z}
is the unique one that makes
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{Z}
(identified as above) into subgroupoids, and satisfies

(g,e_{s_{\mathcal{G}}(g)})(f_{t_{\mathcal{Z}}(z)},z)=(g,z)

.
In particular every representation of

Concretely, let

\gamma\in G

, and induce a class of representations of

\pi_{a}(\gamma)=_{\text{def.}}c(a,\gamma a)\Pi(a;\gamma)=\Pi(\gamma^{-1}a;\gamma)c(\gamma^{-1}a,a)

.

On the other hand every uniformly bounded representation of

:

is a complete metric space without isolated points, and
\nu
is an Ahlfors
D
-regular Hausdorff
D
-measure on
(Z,d)
.

Calculation B.1
.

We verify the conformal identity for
\Delta_{a}^{s}
from (
2.1
), i.e. that
\Delta_{ga}^{s}(g^{*}\phi)=|g^{\prime}|^{2s}(a)\cdot g^{*}(\Delta_{a}^{s}\phi)
. Here
g\in\Mob(Z)
,
a\in Z
,
\phi\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
, and
\xi\in Z_{a}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{ga}^{s}(g^{*}\phi)(\xi)

\displaystyle=-\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{ga}}\frac{g^{*}\phi(\eta)-g^{*}\phi(\xi)}{d^{D+2s}_{ga}(\eta,\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(\eta)

\displaystyle=-\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{ga}}\frac{\phi(\omega)-g^{*}\phi(\xi)}{d_{ga}^{D+2s}(g\omega,\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(g\omega)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:change-of-var-nu_a}\eqref{eq:GMV-for-d_a}}}{{=}}-\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{a}}\frac{\phi(\omega)-\phi(g^{-1}\xi)}{d_{a}^{D+2s}(\omega,g^{-1}\xi)}\cdot|g^{\prime}|^{D+2s}(a)\cdot\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\omega)}{|g^{\prime}|^{D}(a)}

\displaystyle=|g^{\prime}|^{2s}(a)\cdot\Delta_{a}^{s}\phi(g^{-1}\xi)=|g^{\prime}|^{2s}(a)\cdot g^{*}(\Delta_{a}^{s}\phi)(\xi).

Calculation B.2
.

We verify that for an
s
-conformal family
(\mathcal{D}_{a},T_{a})_{a\in Z}
,
g\in\Mob(Z)
,
a\in Z
,
p\geq 2
, and
z=s+it\in\mathbb{C}
, the operator
\Pi_{z}(g;a):\mathcal{H}_{T_{a}}\to\mathcal{H}_{T_{ga}}
is unitary. Recall that

\Pi_{z}(g;a)\phi=|g^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}-z}(a)\,g^{*}\phi
.
Here
\phi,\psi\in\mathcal{D}_{a}
:

\displaystyle\langle T_{ga}\Pi_{z}(g;a)\phi,\Pi_{z}(g;a)\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(Z_{ga})}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Pi_s_g_a-defn}}}{{=}}|g^{\prime}|^{D-z-\overline{z}}(a)\langle T_{ga}(g^{*}\phi),g^{*}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{ga}}

\displaystyle=|g^{\prime}|^{D-2s}(a)\langle T_{ga}(g^{*}\phi),g^{*}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{ga}}

\displaystyle=|g^{\prime}|^{D}(a)\langle g^{*}(T_{a}\phi),g^{*}\psi\rangle_{L^{2}_{ga}}

\displaystyle=|g^{\prime}|^{D}(a)\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{g}a}T_{a}\phi(g^{-1}\xi)\overline{\psi(g^{-1}\xi)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{ga}}T_{a}\phi(\omega)\overline{\psi(\omega)}\,|g^{\prime}|^{D}(a)\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{ga}(g\omega)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:change-of-var-nu_a}}}{{=}}\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z_{a}}T_{a}\phi(\omega)\overline{\psi(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{a}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\langle T_{a}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(Z_{a})}.

Appendix C
A remark on tangent spaces and conformal rescaling of metric operators

In this Appendix, we offer a heuristic for thinking about
(Z_{a},d_{a})
as tangent spaces of
(Z,d)
, and how to interpret the conformal operators on the bundle
(Z_{a},d_{a})_{a\in Z}
as a rescaling of operators on
Z
.

Throughout this section, we suppose that
(X,\rho)
is a roughly geodesic, strongly hyperbolic metric space (cf.
[
56
,
59
]
for background). Fix a basepoint
o\in X
, and let
Z=\partial X
. This setup has been explained before, cf. Theorem
2
and Section
9
: denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{o}
the Gromov product (which continuously extends to the compactification
X\cup Z
), by
d(\xi,\eta)=\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}\langle\xi,\eta\rangle_{o})
(for a suitable
\varepsilon_{0}>0
) a metric on
Z
, by
D
the Hausdorff dimension of
(Z,d)
, and by
\nu
the (Ahlfors
D
-regular) measure on
Z
.

Suppose that a group
\Gamma
acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly by isometries on
(X,\rho)
(and hence
\Gamma<\Mob(Z)
).
We shall also need that in this situation, for
g\in\Gamma
and
\xi\in Z
, we have
|g^{\prime}|(\xi)=\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}\beta(\xi,go,o))
, where for
x\in X
we denote
\beta(\xi,x,o)=-2\langle\xi,x\rangle_{o}+\rho(x,o)
the Busemann function
[
59
, Section 3]
.

C.1.
Metric tangent spaces

Fix
a\in Z
. Roughly speaking, we explain below that any fixed ball
B(z_{0},R)
in
(Z_{a},d_{a})
is a (Gromov–Hausdorff) limit of rescaled small balls in
Z
“in the direction of
a
”.

In this subsection, let us use the notation
B_{d}(z,R)
to denote (open) balls centred at
z
, with radius
R
, with respect to the metric
d
.

Suppose that we fix a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Gamma
such that
g_{n}o\to a
in the compactification
X\cup Z
, as
n\to\infty
. Denote
\lambda_{n}=\exp(\varepsilon_{0}\rho(g_{n}o,o))
; then necessarily
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
.

Fix a basepoint
z_{0}\in Z_{a}
, and
R>0
. We now show that the balls
B_{\lambda_{n}d}(g_{n}z_{0},R)
“converge” to the ball
B_{d_{a}}(z_{0},R)
in
(Z_{a},d_{a})
in the following sense: there exist functions
f_{n}:B_{\lambda_{n}d}(g_{n}z_{0},R)\to Z_{a}
, such that

•

f_{n}(g_{n}z_{0})=z_{0}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

•

for any
\delta>0
,
f_{n}
are eventually surjective onto
B_{d_{a}}(z_{0},R-\delta)
, and

•

the restrictions of the metrics to the balls converge uniformly to the metric on
B_{d_{a}}(z_{0},R)
, i.e.

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\xi,\eta\in B_{d_{a}}(z_{0},R)}\{|\lambda_{n}d(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})-d_{a}(\xi,\eta)|:f_{n}(\xi_{n})=\xi,f_{n}(\eta_{n})=\eta\}=0.

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi\in B_{\lambda_{n}d}(g_{n}z_{0},R)
, we set
f_{n}(\xi)=g_{n}^{-1}\xi
. Then the above properties follow from the calculation below. Let
\xi_{n},\eta_{n}\in B_{\lambda_{n}d}(g_{n}z_{0},R)
, and denote
\xi=f_{n}(\xi_{n})
,
\eta=f_{n}(\eta_{n})
. Then

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}d(\xi_{n},\eta_{n})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}d(g_{n}\xi,g_{n}\eta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:GMV}}}{{=}}\lambda_{n}\cdot|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\xi)\cdot|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{1}{2}}(\eta)\cdot d(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\exp(\varepsilon_{0}\rho(g_{n}o,o))\cdot\exp(\varepsilon_{0}(\langle\xi,g_{n}o\rangle_{o}-\rho(g_{n}o,o)/2))\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\exp(\varepsilon_{0}(\langle\eta,g_{n}o\rangle_{o}-\rho(g_{n}o,o)/2))\cdot d(\xi,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{d(\xi,\eta)}{\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}\langle\xi,g_{n}o\rangle_{o})\cdot\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}\langle\xi,g_{n}o\rangle_{o})}

\displaystyle\longrightarrow_{n\to\infty}\frac{d(\xi,\eta)}{d(\xi,a)d(\eta,a)}=d_{a}(\xi,\eta).

This fits into the framework of weak tangent spaces from
[
19
]
(see also
[
24
]
). One may also extend this to the framework of measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence to include the measures
\nu
and
\nu_{a}
.

C.2.
Rescaling of operators

We continue in the setup above. Suppose we have an operator
T
on
L^{2}(Z)
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(T)\subset L^{2}(Z)
stable under the action of
\Gamma
. We say that an operator
(\mathcal{D}(T_{a}),T_{a})
on
L^{2}(Z_{a})
is a
rescale
of
T
, if there is a sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset(0,\infty)
with
\lambda_{n}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty
, and a sequence
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\Gamma
with
g_{n}o\to a
in
X\cup Z
, such that

\displaystyle\phi\in\mathcal{D}(T_{a})\implies g_{n}^{*}\phi\in\mathcal{D}(T)\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\langle T_{a}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(Z_{a})}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\langle T(\lambda_{n}g_{n}^{*}\phi),\lambda_{n}g_{n}^{*}\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(Z)},

for
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(T_{a})
,
\psi\in L^{2}(Z_{a})
.

We shall check that for
s\in(0,1)
, the fractional Laplacian (
2.1
) is a rescale of

\Delta^{s}\phi(\xi)=-\uprightintop\displaylimits_{Z}\frac{\phi(\eta)-\phi(\xi)}{d^{D+2s}(\xi,\eta)}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta),\quad\xi\in Z,

with the domain
\mathcal{D}(\Delta^{s})
consisting of compactly supported Lipschitz functions on
Z
.
We fix any
(g_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
as above (i.e. such that
g_{n}o\to a
), denote
\lambda_{n}=\exp(\varepsilon_{0}(D-sp)\rho(g_{n}o,o)/2)
.

For notational convenience, we shall write
d(\xi,x)=\exp(-\varepsilon_{0}\langle\xi,x\rangle_{o})
for
\xi\in Z
and
x\in X
. Although this is not a metric, we have that
d(\xi,x)\to d(\xi,a)
for
x\to a
in
X\cup Z
. Similarly, we write
\mathrm{d}\nu_{x}(\xi)=d^{-2D}(x,\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)
; these are measures on
Z
such that
\nu_{x}\to\nu_{a}
as
x\to a
. Finally, we write
d_{x}(\xi,\eta)=d(\xi,\eta)/(d(\xi,x)d(\eta,x))
.

As a preparatory step, we calculate for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\xi,\eta\in Z
:

(C.1)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lambda_{n}^{2}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}-s}(\xi)|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}-s}(\eta)&=\exp(\varepsilon_{0}(D-2s)\rho(g_{n}o,o))\cdot\\
&\qquad\cdot\exp\!\left(\varepsilon_{0}\!\left(\tfrac{D}{2}-s\right)\!\left(2\langle\xi,g_{n}o\rangle-\rho(g_{n}o,o)\right)\right)\cdot\\
&\qquad\cdot\exp\!\left(\varepsilon_{0}\!\left(\tfrac{D}{2}-s\right)\!\left(2\langle\eta,g_{n}o\rangle-\rho(g_{n}o,o)\right)\right)\\
&=d^{-D+2s}(\xi,g_{n}o)\,d^{-D+2s}(\eta,g_{n}o).\end{aligned}

Now we calculate

\left\langle\Delta^{s}(\lambda_{n}g_{n}^{*}\phi),\lambda_{n}g_{n}^{*}\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(Z)}\\
\begin{aligned} &=\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{2}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{(\phi(g_{n}^{-1}\xi)-\phi(g_{n}^{-1}\eta))\overline{(\psi(g_{n}^{-1}\xi)-\psi(g_{n}^{-1}\eta))}}{d^{D+2s}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:change-of-var},\eqref{eq:GMV}}}{{=}}\frac{\lambda_{n}^{2}}{2}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\eta))\overline{(\psi(\xi)-\psi(\eta))}}{d^{D+2s}(\xi,\eta)}|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}-s}(\xi)|g_{n}^{\prime}|^{\frac{D}{2}-s}(\eta)\,\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta)\\
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{localeq:prep-for-rescale-calculation}}}{{=}}\frac{1}{2}\uprightiintop\displaylimits_{Z^{2}}\frac{(\phi(\xi)-\phi(\eta))\overline{(\psi(\xi)-\psi(\eta))}}{d_{g_{n}o}^{D+2s}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu_{g_{n}o}(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{g_{n}o}(\eta)\\
&\longrightarrow\langle\Delta_{a}^{s}\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(Z_{a})},\end{aligned}

Note that a similar calculation works more generally for the fractional
p
-Laplacian (
p\geq 1
), which is a
(p-1)
-homogeneous nonlinear operator
\Delta_{p}^{2}:W^{s,p}(Z)\to W^{s,p}(Z)^{\prime}
, defined by

\langle\Delta_{p}^{s}(u),v\rangle_{(W^{s,p})^{\prime},W^{s,p}}=\frac{1}{2}\uprightiintop\displaylimits\frac{|u(\xi)-u(\eta)|^{p-2}(u(\xi)-u(\eta))\overline{(v(\xi)-v(\eta))}}{d^{D+sp}(\xi,\eta)}\mathrm{d}\nu(\xi)\mathrm{d}\nu(\eta),

for
u,v\in W^{s,p}(Z)
.

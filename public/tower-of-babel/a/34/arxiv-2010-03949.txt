An Optimum Algorithm for Quantum Search
Jiang Liu

Abstract
This paper discusses an improvement to Grover’s algorithm for searches where target states are Hamming weight eigenstates and search space is not ordered. It is shown that under these conditions search efficiency depends on the smaller number of 0’s and 1’s, not the total length, of binary string of target state, and that Grover’s algorithm can be improved whenever number of 0’s and number 1’s are not equal. In particular, improvement can be exponential when number of 0’s or number of 1’s is very small relative to binary string length. One interesting application is that Dicke state preparation, which in Grover’s algorithm is P on average, can be made poly-efficient in all cases. For decision making process, this improvement won’t improve computation efficiency, but can make implementation much simpler.

I
Introduction

Quantum search is a process of looking for a target state
|k\rangle
, where
0\leq k<2^{n}
and
n
is the number of qubits of the system, out of a mixture of
2^{n}
un-ordered states

\displaystyle|\psi_{\theta}\rangle\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}\sum_{j=0}^{2^{n}-1}|j\rangle\longrightarrow|k\rangle

(1)

\displaystyle\sin\theta=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n}}}

(2)

Efficiency of quantum search plays an important role in the design of collision-resistance cryptography
Katz and Lindell 2008
;
Kim et al. 2018a
;
Hosoyamada et al. 2019
;
Brassard et al. 1997
.

In the absence of coherent interference all states in the mixture are independent. Number of trials of success of finding
|k\rangle
on average is the same as that in classical search, for which query complexity is
\bm{O}(2^{n})
. With coherent interference in quantum computing, Grover
Grover 1996
discovered that search efficiency can be sped-up quadratically, reducing query complexity to
\bm{O}(2^{n/2})
.

Grover’s algorithm is generated, in part, by rotating qubit along polar axes of Bloch sphere
Bloch 1946
by
\pi/2
.
Qubit rotation plays an important role in quantum computing
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
. The most famous example so far is Quantum Fourier Transformation (QFT)
Deutsch 1985
;
Coppersmith 1994
;
Griffiths and Niu 1996
;
Cleve et al. 1998
;
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
and its applications such as Shor’s algorithm
Shor 1994
for factorization and Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd algorithm
Harrow et al. 2009
for certain linear system of equations. QFT is generated by rotating qubit along azimuthal axes of Bloch sphere.

It is well known that Grover’s algorithm is optimum
Zalka 1999
;
Bennett et al. 1997
when qubit polar angle is
\pi/2
, i.e., when Hadamard matrix is unbiased. It is, however, less clear what would happen if Hadamard matrix is biased. The idea of using biased Hadamard transformation is not new
Childs et al. 2002
. The purpose of this paper is to explore if Grover’s algorithm can be improved under certain conditions when Hadamard transformation is biased.

In section II, dynamics of Grover’s algorithm is recast to oscillation. Although Grover’s algorithm can be viewed from different angles
Bennett et al. 1997
;
Boyer et al. 1998
;
Brassard et al. 2002
, viewing from oscillation angle
Pontecorvo 1958
;
Maki et al. 1962
;
Wolfenstein 1978
;
Mikheyev and Smirnov 1985
can provide useful insight into where and how improvement may be made.

Following oscillation view, section III discusses an optimum search algorithm, referred to as Grover-Plus henceforth for convenience, for Grover’s original database search, where both target
|k\rangle
and its oracle are given but search list
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
is unordered. In this case, the only difference between Grover-Plus and Grover’s algorithm is that qubit polar angle is a variable of Hamming weight
Hamming 1950
and that polar angle is determined by optimization.

It will be shown that while Grover-Plus and Grover’s algorithm are identical when Hamming weight of target state equals
n/2
, Grover-Plus is always more efficient in any other cases. In particular, Grover-Plus is exponentially more efficient when Hamming weight of target state is either very small or very close to
n
.

Section IV discusses an interesting application of Grover-Plus: Dicke states
Dicke 1954
production. Dicke states play a special role in quantum entanglement and quantum computing (for details see
Childs et al. 2002
;
Ionicioiu et al. 2008
;
Bergmann and Gühne 2013
;
Chakraborty et al. 2014
;
Bärtschi and Eidenbenz 2019
and references therein). Grover-Plus turns out to be simpler and more efficient than existing methods

Childs et al. 2002
;
Ionicioiu et al. 2008
;
Chakraborty et al. 2014
;
Bärtschi and Eidenbenz 2019
. Query complexity of Grover-Plus is
\bm{O}\left((\Delta_{0,k}(1-\Delta_{0,k}/n))^{1/4}\right)
, where
\Delta_{0,k}
is the Hamming weight of Dicke state.

Section V compares Grover-Plus to Grover searches where searching space can be limited to states of equal Hamming weight. In this case, Grover’s algorithm and Grover-Plus have similar computation efficiency. Implementation of Grover-Plus turns out to be much simpler.

Conclusion of this paper is summarized in section VI. Some details of calculation are organized in the Appendix.

II
Quantum Search and Quantum Oscillation

Casting quantum search to quantum oscillation has two advantages: First, it makes the proof of completeness of Grover’s search
Bennett et al. 1997
;
Boyer et al. 1998
;
Brassard et al. 2002
much simpler. Second, it openly reveals which part of Grover’s algorithm is already optimized and where else it can potentially be improved.

Let’s start by re-deriving Grover’s algorithm from oscillation point of view.

The
2^{n}
dimensional Hilbert space of Eq. (
1
) can be partitioned to a coarse-grained two dimensional subspace supported by search target state
|k\rangle
and its negation
|\tilde{k}\rangle
, defined by

|\tilde{k}\rangle\langle\tilde{k}|=1-|k\rangle\langle k|
,

|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=\cos\theta|\tilde{k}\rangle+\sin\theta|k\rangle,

(3)

where
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
and
\theta
are given by Eqs. (
1
) and (
2
).

Oscillation takes place between
(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}
and

(|0\rangle,|\tilde{0}\rangle)^{T}
bases connected by

H^{\otimes{n}}\begin{pmatrix}|\tilde{k}\rangle\\
|k\rangle\end{pmatrix}=U^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}|0\rangle\\
|\tilde{0}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(4)

where
|\tilde{0}\rangle
is the negation of

|0\rangle
,
H

is Hadamard gate and

U_{\theta}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta&\sin\theta\\
-\sin\theta&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}

(5)

is a bases mixing matrix. That
(|0\rangle,|\tilde{0}\rangle)^{T}
comes into play is because
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle=H^{\otimes{n}}|0\rangle
.

Evolution of
(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}
is given by

\begin{pmatrix}|\tilde{k}\rangle\\
|k\rangle\end{pmatrix}_{t}=\left(T_{\theta}\right)^{t}\begin{pmatrix}|\tilde{k}\rangle\\
|k\rangle\end{pmatrix}_{0},

(6)

where
t
is the number of oscillation cycles and

T_{\theta}=U_{\theta}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}U_{\theta}^{\dagger}\begin{pmatrix}-1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

Diagonal matrices of
T_{\theta}
are referred to as Grover’s oracle in the literature. They are oscillation matrices of bases eigenvectors. That they are traceless implies that oscillation is maximum. One important consequence of maximum oscillation is

\left(T_{\theta}\right)^{t}=T_{t\theta}.

(8)

resulting in

Pr\left(k|\psi_{\theta}\right)_{t}=\sin^{2}\left(\theta+2t\theta\right).

(9)

This is Grover’s result re-derived from the oscillation point of view. Query complexity of Eq. (
9
) is determined by
t=\lfloor(\pi/\theta-2)/4\rfloor=\bm{O}(2^{n/2})
.

Dynamics of Grover’s algorithm is similar to that of neutrino oscillation
Wolfenstein 1978
;
Mikheyev and Smirnov 1985
;
Blennow and Smirnov 2013
in that both involve bases mixing and oscillation. In particular,
(|0\rangle,|\tilde{0}\rangle)^{T}
and
(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}

play similar roles as mass and interaction bases
Wolfenstein 1978
in neutrino oscillation. In Grover’s algorithm, mixing and oscillation take place at different time and location. By contrast, mixing and oscillation of neutrinos in interaction-bases are modeled at the same time and at the same location.

Oscillation efficiency is driven by three factors
Pontecorvo 1958
;
Wolfenstein 1978
;
Mikheyev and Smirnov 1985
;
Blennow and Smirnov 2013
: (1) structure of oscillation matrix, (2) bases-mixing and (3) choice of oscillation bases. In view of oscillation physics, Grover’s algorithm is already optimum with respect to the structure of oscillation matrix. However, issues related to bases-mixing and oscillation bases were not addressed. These are the areas where improvement may potentially be made.

III
A Generalization of Grover’s Algorithm

Following oscillation view, Grover’s computation model
Grover 1996
;
Brassard and Hoyer 1997
;
Boyer et al. 1998
;
Brassard et al. 2002
may be generalized by allowing arbitrary polar angle and arbitrary intermediate bases, while keeping maximum oscillation feature unchanged, i.e.,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}|0\rangle\\
|\tilde{0}\rangle\end{pmatrix}\rightarrow\begin{pmatrix}|j\rangle\\
|\tilde{j}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(10)

\displaystyle H\rightarrow H_{\zeta}=\sigma_{z}\cos\frac{\zeta}{2}+\sigma_{x}\sin\frac{\zeta}{2},

(11)

where
\sigma_{x}
and
\sigma_{z}
are the Pauli matrices,
H_{\zeta}
is a generalized Hadamard gate with
0\leq\zeta\leq\pi
.
Changing intermediate basis is not necessary for the optimum search algorithm, Grover-Plus, discussed below, but is indispensable for algorithms requiring a multiple-step evolution path.

Optimizing bases-mixing involves two parts. First, determining a polar angle
\zeta
that optimizes bases-mixing. Second, setting an initial condition that connects optimized oscillation bases to
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
so that results of optimization can be applied to searches described by Eq. (
1
).

Let’s start from the first part. Operationally,
H_{\zeta}^{\otimes{n}}
turns a pure state
|j\rangle
to a superposition. Weight of
|k\rangle
in the superposition represents mixing between
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
. It is shown in the Appendix that
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
mixing depends on
\zeta
and on number of bit-flippers between
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
. If binary strings of
|j\rangle=|j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}\rangle
and
|k\rangle=|k_{n}\cdots k_{2}k_{1}\rangle
are viewed as members of ordered set, number of bit-flippers is their Hamming distance
Hamming 1950

\Delta_{j,k}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}\left(j_{\alpha}-k_{\alpha}\right)^{2}.

(12)

When
j=0
,
\Delta_{j,k}=\Delta_{0,k}
becomes the Hamming weight of
|k_{n}\cdots k_{2}k_{1}\rangle
.

If target state is an eigenstate of
\Delta_{j,k}
, optimum solution to polar angle
\zeta
turns out to be (see Appendix for detail)

\sin^{2}\frac{\zeta}{2}=\frac{\Delta_{j,k}}{n},

(13)

and

\sin^{2}\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}=\left(\frac{\Delta_{j,k}}{n}\right)^{\Delta_{j,k}}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{j,k}}{n}\right)^{n-\Delta_{j,k}},

(14)

where
\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}
is the mixing angle between
(|j\rangle,|\tilde{j}\rangle)^{T}
and
(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}
bases, and that probability of finding
|k\rangle
from
|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}\rangle
after
t
cycles of oscillation is

Pr\left(k|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}\right)_{t}=\sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}+2t\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}\right),

(15)

where
|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}\rangle=H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}|0\rangle
is the analog of

|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
of Eq. (
3
).

Notice, polar angle described by Eq. (
13
) is exactly the same as that suggested, heuristically, by
Childs et al. 2002
for Dicke state
Dicke 1954
production. This paper shows that the choice of
Childs et al. 2002
is optimal.

To apply these results to Grover’s search space, one must connect
|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}\rangle
to

|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
. This is handled by the second part of Grover-Plus discussed below.

Permissible initial states in generalized Grover’s computation model are

H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta^{\prime}}|0\rangle
for arbitrary
\zeta^{\prime}
.
Among them,
H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}|0\rangle
with
\zeta
determined by Eq. (
13
) has largest mixing with
|k\rangle
.

\zeta
is also the same angle that maximizes oscillation.
The natural choice of initial state for

|k\rangle

is therefore

H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}|0\rangle
. All other initial states can be transformed to
H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}|0\rangle

by an unitary base-shift operator
H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta^{\prime}}
.

To apply Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
) to Grover’s search space, one simply needs to add a base-shift operator ahead of oscillation, i.e.,

\left(T_{\theta}\right)^{t}\rightarrow\left(T_{\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}}\right)^{t}H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}H^{\otimes{n}}=T_{t\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}}H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}H^{\otimes{n}}.

(16)

Probability of finding
|k\rangle
from
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
after
t
cycles of oscillation now becomes

Pr\left(k|\psi_{\theta}\right)_{t}=\sin^{2}\left(\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}+2t\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}\right).

(17)

Eqs. (
13
), (
14
), (
16
) and (
17
) define Grover-Plus when target state is an eigenstate of Hamming weight and search space is given by Eq. (
1
). They are the major results of this paper. They have the following interesting features.

When
\Delta_{0,k}=n/2
, Grover-Plus and Grover’s algorithm are identical. In this case,
\zeta=\pi/2
and
\sin\theta_{n/2}=\sin\theta=2^{-n/2}
. In other words, Grover’s algorithm is a special case of Grover-Plus, and is optimum when
\Delta_{0,k}=n/2
.

When
\Delta_{0,k}\neq n/2
, Grover-Plus is more efficient than Grover’s algorithm because bases-mixing in Grover-Plus is larger than that in Grover’s algorithm.
Asymptotically, one can show from Eq. (
14
) that

\theta_{\Delta{{}_{j,k}}}\sim\begin{cases}\left(\delta_{j,k}/n\right)^{\delta_{j,k}/2}&\quad\text{when }\delta_{j,k}\ll n/2\\
\left(\delta_{j,k}/n\right)^{\delta_{j,k}}&\quad\text{when }\delta_{j,k}\sim n/2\end{cases}

(18)

where

\delta_{j,k}\equiv\min\left[\Delta_{j,k},n-\Delta_{j,k}\right],

(19)

is the smaller of
\Delta_{j,k}
and
n-\Delta_{j,k}
.
\delta_{j,k}=n/2
corresponds to the case for Grover’s algorithm.

Query complexity of Grover-Plus is determined by

t=\lfloor(\pi/\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}})-2)/4\rfloor
, which leads to

\bm{O}\left(c^{n/c}\right)
, when
\delta_{j,k}\sim n/c
where
c\geq 2
, and
\bm{O}\left(n^{\delta_{j,k}/2}\right)
when
\delta_{j,k}\ll n/2
. In all these cases, Grover-Plus is always more efficient than Grover’s algorithm whose query complexity is always equal to
\bm{O}\left(2^{n/2}\right)
.

In particular, when
\delta_{j,k}\ll n
, query complexity of Grover-Plus is polynomial, which is exponentially more efficient than Grover’s algorithm.

Of special interest are cases where target is
|0\rangle
or

|2^{n}-1\rangle
.
In these cases
\Delta_{0,k}=0
or
n
. Grover-Plus solution is
\zeta=0
or
\pi
,
\theta_{\Delta_{0,0}}=\theta_{\Delta_{0,2^{n}-1}}=\pi/2
and
t=0
. No oscillation is needed, i.e.,
t=0
, base-shift
H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}H^{\otimes{n}}
alone can do the job. In Grover’s algorithm, by contrast, one has to query Grover’s Oracle
2^{n/2}
times to get the same results.

Another interesting example is the process
where
\delta_{0,k}=1
. Grover-Plus solution is
\sin^{2}\frac{\zeta}{2}=n^{-{1/2}}
or
1-n^{-1/2}
, query computational complexity is
\bm{O}\left(\sqrt{n}\right)
. Compared to Grover’s algorithm, speed-up of Grover-Plus is exponential.

Implementation of Grover-Plus is straightforward. Once search target
|k\rangle
is specified, Hamming weight of
|k\rangle
is known. All one needs to do is (1) dial qubit polar angle to that described by Eq. (
13
), (2) add base-shift gate in front of oscillation oracles, and (3) make measurement after
\lfloor(\pi/\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}-2)/4\rfloor
queries.

IV
Dicke State Preparation

This section discusses an interesting application of Grover-Plus: Dicke state preparation. A Dicke state is an equally weighted superposition of all states of the same Hamming weight

|D^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\rangle\equiv{{n\choose\Delta_{0,k}}^{-1/2}}\sum_{j}|j\rangle.

(20)

Producing a Dicke state from
|00\cdots 0\rangle
is equivalent to Hamming weight search in Grover’s unsorted search space
|\psi_{\theta}\rangle
.

Classically,
probability density of finding a
\Delta_{0,k}
in an unstructured dataset is

\rho\left(\Delta_{0,k}\right)=2^{-n}{n\choose\Delta_{0,k}}.

(21)

Computational complexity of Eq. (
21
) varies from worst case of
\bm{O}(2^{n}/n)
, which is NP informally, when
\delta_{0,k}\ll n/2
, where
\delta_{0,k}
is given by Eq. (
19
), to
\bm{O}(\sqrt{n})
, which is P, when
\delta_{0,k}\to n/2
. On average
Levin 1986
;
Arora and Barak 2009
, this process is P because states with
\delta_{0,k}=\Delta_{0,k}=n/2
are most populous.

Grover’s algorithm can provide a quadratical speedup to classical search,but cannot change the nature of computational complexity.

Grover-Plus is applicable to
|D^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\rangle
search. The only changes are (1) Grover’s Oracle acts on all member state of
|D^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\rangle
equall, and (2) replacing bases-mixing of a pure state,
\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}
, by bases-mixing of

|D^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\rangle
,
\theta^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}
. One can show

\sin^{2}\theta^{n}_{\Delta_{0},k}={n\choose\Delta_{0,k}}\sin^{2}\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}.

(22)

Hamming-weight search is in NP only when
\delta_{0,k}\ll n/2
. This is exactly the same place where Grover-Plus is exponentially more efficient than Grover’s algorithm. As a result, we expect that Grover-Plus is able to make Hamming-weight search poly-efficient for all cases.

Indeed, from Stirling approximation

{n\choose\Delta_{0,k}}\approx\left[2\pi\Delta_{0,k}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{0,k}}{n}\right)\right]^{-1/2}\sin^{-2}\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}\

(23)

where

\sin^{2}\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}

is given by Eq. (
14
), one finds

\sin^{2}\theta^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\approx\left[2\pi\Delta_{0,k}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{0,k}}{n}\right)\right]^{-1/2},

(24)

and

Pr\left(D^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}|\psi_{\theta}\right)_{t}\approx\sin^{2}\left(\theta^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}+2t\theta^{n}_{\Delta_{0,k}}\right),

(25)

where approximation follows from Eq. (
23
). Computational complexity of Eqs. (
24
) and (
25
) is determined by
t=\lfloor(\pi/\theta_{\Delta^{n}_{0,k}}-2)/4\rfloor=\bm{O}\left((\Delta_{0,k}(1-\Delta_{0,k}/n))^{1/4}\right)
, which is
in P for arbitrary
\Delta_{0,k}
.

As far as Dicke state production is concerned, Grover-Plus is a simple realization of computation model hypothesized in
Childs et al. 2002
. It provides a simple close-form description of Dicke state wave function of arbitrary Hamming weight at all time. In particular, it shows that the choice of biased Hadamard angle in
Childs et al. 2002
is optimal (see Eqs. (
13
)) and (
14
)).

Grover-Plus is poly-efficient in
\min[n,n-\Delta_{0,k}]
, while others

Childs et al. 2002
;
Ionicioiu et al. 2008
;
Chakraborty et al. 2014
;
Bärtschi and Eidenbenz 2019
are poly-efficient in
n
. Query complexity of these models, whenever available, vary from
\bm{O}(n),~\bm{O}(n^{1/2})
to
\bm{O}(n^{1/4})
. Grover-Plus is likely to be more efficient when
\Delta_{0,k}\neq 0,n,n/2
, because
\bm{O}\left((\Delta_{0,k}(1-\Delta_{0,k}/n))^{1/4}\right)\leq\bm{O}(n^{1/4})
. Grover-Plus does not require ancilla qubits.

V
Searching States of Equal Hamming Weight

When Hamming weight of the searching target is known, an alternative strategy is to use Grover’s algorithm and limit searching space to states whose Hamming weight are equal to that of the target state. For convenience, we refer to this strategy as modified Grover’s algorithm. Modified Grover’s algorithm is often used in decision making process as an equation solver. In this section, we are to compare Grover-Plus to modified Grover’s algorithm.

Modified Grover’s algorithm requires two conditions absent in Grover-Plus. One is that space
\left\{|k\rangle\right\},k=0,1,\cdots,2^{n}-1
, is ordered. The other is that qubit rotation and Grover’s Oracle act on different spaces.

The first condition is to makes sure that selection of equal Hamming weight states,
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
, from
\left\{|k\rangle\right\}
is possible and efficient. Here
\left\{|\Delta_{0,,k}\rangle\right\}
represents states whose Hamming weight is
\Delta_{0,k}
. When
\Delta_{0,k}=1
, for example, member states of
\left\{|\Delta_{0,K}\rangle\right\}
are
|0\cdots 01\rangle,|0\cdots 10\rangle,\cdots,|1\cdots 00\rangle
in binary representation and
|2^{k}\rangle
,
k=0,1,\cdots,n-1
, in digital representation.

The second condition rises because
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
is not a Hilbert subspace of
\left\{|k\rangle\right\}
with algebra
H^{\otimes n}_{\zeta}
.
H_{\zeta}^{\otimes n}|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle
will bring in states outside of
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
. As a consequence, applying Eq.(
7
) to
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
will not produce required coherent interference for amplitude amplification.

To work round, one can introduce a working registry of
n^{\prime}
qubits,
\left\{|k^{\prime}\rangle\right\},k^{\prime}=0,1,\cdots,2^{n^{\prime}}-1
, and map
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
to
\left\{|k^{\prime}\rangle\right\}
(with potentially trivial padding states in
|k^{\prime}\rangle
)

\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}\longleftrightarrow\left\{|k^{\prime}\rangle\right\}.

(26)

Size of
\left\{|\Delta_{0,k}\rangle\right\}
is binormially distributed. Required number of qubits of working registry,
n^{\prime}
, is determined by

{n\choose\Delta_{0,k}+1}>2^{n^{\prime}}\geq{n\choose\Delta_{0,k}}.

(27)

In this implementation, Grover’s Oracle is acting on
\left\{|k\rangle\right\}
as before, Grover’s phase shift,
|0\rangle\to-|0\rangle
, qubit polar rotation
H^{\otimes n^{\prime}}
and final measurement are on working registry
\left\{|k^{\prime}\rangle\right\}
.

In modified Grover’s algorithm, bases mixing is determined by

\sin\theta^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2^{n^{\prime}}}},

(28)

which can be orders of magnitude bigger than that in Grover algorithm if
n^{\prime}\ll n
. Accordingly, modified Grover’s algorithm can be orders of magnitude more efficient than Grover’s algorithm.

Comparing modified Grover algorithm to Grover-Plus, we have from Eqs.(
23
), (
27
) and (
28
)

\frac{\sin\theta^{\prime}}{\sin\theta_{\Delta_{0,k}}}\sim\left[2\pi\Delta_{0,k}\left(1-\frac{\Delta_{0,k}}{n}\right)\right]^{1/4},

(29)

i.e., bases mixings of modified Grover’s algorithm and Grover-Plus are of same order of magnitude for all Hamming weight. As a result, modified Grover algorithm and Grover-Plus are equally efficient.

Advantage of Grover-Plus is that its implementation is simpler. Grover-Plus works in the Grover’s database search Hilbert space
\left\{|k\rangle\right\}
. It does not require
\left\{|k\rangle\right\}
be ordered, nor need an extra working registry to map to for every iteration.

VI
Conclusion

Based on the observation that dynamics of quantum search is quantum oscillation, it is shown that Grover’s database search algorithm can be improved when search target is a Hamming weight eigenstate and Hamming weight of the target state is known.

Improvement rises from an optimum choice of qubit polar rotation angle determined by Hamming weight.
Magnitude of improvement varies from exponential when Hamming weight of target state is close to
0
or
n
to no improvement when Hamming weight is equal to
n/2
.

As far as computational complexity is concerned, choosing optimum qubit rotation angle is in general equivalent to limiting searching space to states of equal Hamming weight. It is shown, however, that choosing optimum rotation angle is much easier to implement.

Gilliam et al. 2020
on the same topic. Although our approaches and focuses are somewhat different, we independently reached the same results shown by
Eqs.(
13
) and (
14
).

Appendix A
Oscillation in Binary System

Relation between qubit polar rotation angle and bases mixing is most evident in binary presentation of quantum state, in which
|j\rangle
can be written as

\displaystyle|j\rangle=|j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}\rangle,

(30)

\displaystyle j=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}j_{\alpha}2^{\alpha-1},

(31)

where
j_{\alpha}\in\{0,1\}
and
\alpha=1,2,\cdots n
.
j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}
is referred to as binary string of
j
in literature. The length of
j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}
is
n
. In general, computational complexity of a process involving
|j\rangle
is measured by the length of binary string. Depending on the detail of processes, the result could be polynomial, exponential or others.

In binary presentation, differences between
j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}
and
k_{n}\cdots k_{2}k_{1}
may be measured by their intersection

I_{j,k}=\sum_{\alpha=1}^{n}j_{\alpha}k_{\alpha},

(32)

and symmetric difference
\Delta_{j,k}
, defined by Eq. (
12
), which counts the number of bit-flippers between

|j_{n}\cdots j_{2}j_{1}\rangle
and

|k_{n}\cdots k_{2}k_{1}\rangle
. For example, when
j=0
,
\Delta_{0,k}=\sum_{\alpha}k_{\alpha}
is the number of 1’s in binary string of
|k\rangle

From Eqs. (
11
) to (
12
) and binomial expansion, it can be shown that

H_{\zeta}^{\otimes n}|j\rangle=\sum_{k=0}^{2^{n}-1}\left(-1\right)^{I_{j,k}}\sin\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}|k\rangle,

(33)

where

\sin\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}=\left(\cos\frac{\zeta}{2}\right)^{n-\Delta_{j,k}}\left(\sin\frac{\zeta}{2}\right)^{\Delta_{j,k}}.

(34)

Oscillation bases involving
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
are
(|j\rangle,|\tilde{j}\rangle)^{T}
and

(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}
, where
|\tilde{j}\rangle
and

|\tilde{k}\rangle
are the negations of
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
, in the sense that
|\tilde{k}\rangle\langle\tilde{k}|=1-|k\rangle\langle k|

and that
|\tilde{j}\rangle\langle\tilde{j}|=1-|j\rangle\langle j|
, respectively. They are connected by

\begin{pmatrix}|j\rangle\\
|\tilde{j}\rangle\end{pmatrix}=U_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}H_{\zeta}^{\otimes{n}}\begin{pmatrix}|\tilde{k}\rangle\\
|k\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(35)

where
U_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}
is a generalization of Eq. (
5
) with
\theta
replaced by
\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}
of Eq. (
34
). Ignoring an over all phase of no physical significance, one can show

\displaystyle|\tilde{j}\rangle=\sum_{i\neq j}\left(-1\right)^{I_{i,k}-I_{j,k}}\frac{\sin\theta_{\Delta_{i,k}}}{\cos\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}|i\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle|\tilde{k}\rangle=\sum_{i\neq k}\left(-1\right)^{I_{j,i}-I_{j,k}}\frac{\sin\theta_{\Delta_{j,i}}}{\cos\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}|i\rangle.

(37)

For a given
\zeta
,
|j\rangle
and
|k\rangle
,
the
2^{n}
dimensional Hilbert space can be partitioned into a coarse-grained
two-dimensional subspace, in which
H^{\otimes{n}}_{\zeta}|j\rangle
is the
initial superposition
|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}}\rangle
,
|k\rangle
is the target to be searched. Evolution of
(|\tilde{k}\rangle,|k\rangle)^{T}
is similar to that in Grover’s algorithm with mixing
\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}
determined by
\Delta_{j,k}
and
\zeta
. Optimizing
Pr(k|\psi_{\theta_{\Delta_{j,k}}})
with respect to
\zeta
produces Eqs. (
13
) and (
14
).

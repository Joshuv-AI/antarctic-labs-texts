1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background and main result

Let
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
be compact Riemannian manifolds. Recall that a sufficiently regular map
v:M\to N
is called
harmonic
if its tension field

(1.1)

{\mathcal{T}}(v)={\operatorname{tr}}_{g}\nabla dv

vanishes identically. Equivalently,
v
is a critical point of the Dirichlet energy functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|du|^{2}\,dV.

We are chiefly concerned with the fundamental case
M=N=S^{2},
although some of our results are stated more generally.

A classical result due to Lemaire and Wood
[
13
, (11.5)]
states that any harmonic map
v:S^{2}\to S^{2}
must either be holomorphic or antiholomorphic; hence

(1.2)

E(v)=4\pi|\deg(v)|

and
v
attains the minimum allowable energy within its homotopy class. The proof relies on a famous trick: since the Hopf differential (a certain quadratic expression in the components of
du
) of a harmonic map is holomorphic, it necessarily vanishes, forcing the map
v
to be holomorphic or antiholomorphic.

It is natural to ask whether a quantitative version of this result holds true; specifically, whether the energy of a map with small tension must be close to a predetermined value. In his thesis
[
27
]
, Topping obtained a remarkable first result in this direction: any map
u:S^{2}\to S^{2}
with

(1.3)

E(u)<4\pi|\deg(u)|+\delta_{0},

must obey the estimate

(1.4)

E(u)\leq 4\pi|\deg(u)|+C\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2},

where
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}}.

For initial data satisfying (
1.3
), Topping used (
1.4
) to prove that Struwe’s weak solution of the harmonic map flow converges exponentially away from a finite set of singular points.

Without the assumption (
1.3
), one can no longer expect
a map with low tension to be nearly holomorphic; instead, one might expect such a map to be close to a
sum
of holomorphic and anti-holomorphic maps. This intuition was borne out by the work of Ding-Tian
[
12
]
, Qing
[
18
]
, and Wang
[
32
]
, establishing that a sequence of maps
u_{i}
with
\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|\to 0
must subconverge to a
bubble tree
of harmonic maps. The reader may see Theorem
5.4
of the present paper for a detailed statement of this theory, or recent work of Jendrej, Lawrie, and Schlag
[
14
]
for more precise results in the parabolic setting.

In fact, the energies
E(u_{i})
converge to the sum of the energies of the maps in the tree, which by (
1.2
), is an integer multiple of
4\pi:

E(u_{i})\to 4\pi n.

Another remarkable result of Topping
[
29
]
generalizes the estimate (
1.4
) to this setting: under certain assumptions on the nature of the bubble tree (described below), we have

(1.5)

|E(u_{i})-4\pi n|\leq C^{\prime}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|^{2}.

Topping was able to use (
1.5
), which he calls a “quantization estimate,” to again obtain exponential convergence of harmonic map flow on compact domains away from the singular set.

The mechanism of Topping’s second theorem is more complex than the first, incorporating a “repulsion” effect between the body map, assumed to be holomorphic, and any antiholomorphic bubbles—in other words, a quantification of the Hopf-differential trick.
To ensure a sufficiently strong repulsion effect, Topping makes the following assumptions: (1) holomorphic and antiholomorphic bubbles do not occur at the same points, and (2) the energy density of the body map does not vanish at any of the antiholomorphic bubble points—see
[
29
]
for precise statements. As shown later
[
28
]
, these hypotheses are necessary for an estimate of the form (
1.5
) to hold.

However, if one settles for a weaker estimate in place of (
1.5
), namely, a
Łojasiewicz(-Simon) inequality

(1.6)

|E(u)-4\pi n|\leq C\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{\alpha},

then it turns out that Topping’s assumptions can be removed. We shall prove (
1.6
) with
\alpha=1
in full generality and with
\alpha>1
assuming only that the body map is not identically constant. For a solution of harmonic map flow
u(t):S^{2}\to S^{2},
this allows us to conclude the following: if the weak limit along a given sequence of times tending to infinity is nonconstant, then
u(t)
must converge polynomially on compact domains away from the singular set. In particular, weak subsequential limits are unique in this case.

1.2.
Related work

Our main theorem can be compared with Łojasiewicz inequalities and uniqueness-of-subsequential-limit results that have appeared in similar contexts.

Assuming that
u
is
C^{2,\alpha}
-close to a fixed harmonic map, (
1.6
) follows directly from fundamental work of Leon Simon
[
25
]
. Historically, extensions of Simon’s theorem to singular contexts have been exceedingly rare, Topping’s theorems
[
27
,
29
]
being the best and perhaps earliest examples. Another instance is the work of Daskalopoulos and Wentworth
[
8
,
9
]
(generalized by Sibley
[
23
]
and Sibley-Wentworth
[
24
]
) establishing convergence modulo gauge and uniqueness of the bubbling set for Hermitian Yang-Mills flow on holomorphic bundles over compact Kähler manifolds. In his thesis
[
31
, Theorem 3.7-Corollary 3.8]
, the author also gave an infinite-time exponential convergence result for 4D Yang-Mills flow analogous to Topping’s first convergence theorem, although in that context the bubbling set turned out to be empty.

More recently, Malchiodi, Rupflin, and Sharp
[
17
]
obtained a Łojasiewicz inequality for the
H
-functional that applies near “simple bubble trees,” consisting of a single bubble on a constant body map (assuming that the domain is a surface of genus at least one). Rupflin
[
19
]
adapted this technique to the Dirichlet energy of harmonic maps and succeeded to work with simple bubble trees mapping into a general real-analytic target. Meanwhile, Rupflin
[
20
]
has also obtained Łojasiewicz inequalities for more general low-energy maps under realistic assumptions, including
[
20
, Corollary 1.6]
, which is a direct precedent of our estimate (
1.6
).
(For recent progress on the related question of closeness of
u
to a genuine holomorphic map under the hypothesis (
1.3
), see Bernand-Mantel, Muratov, and Simon
[
1
]
, Topping
[
30
]
, and Rupflin
[
21
]
.)

A related trend initiated by Colding and Minicozzi
[
5
]
aims to develop Łojasiewicz-type estimates suitable for proving uniqueness of blowup limits at “generic” singularities. These estimates are designed for analyzing type-I singularities of the mean curvature flow and have been remarkably successful on that front
[
6
,
7
,
2
,
3
,
4
]
. Lotay, Schulze, and Szekelyhidi
[
16
]
have also obtained a strong uniqueness result for certain singularities of Lagrangian mean curvature flow, which are of type II. As we shall demonstrate in forthcoming work, a version of the inequality (
1.6
) can also be used to analyze (type-II) finite-time singularities of 2D harmonic map flow.

Finally, we mention the recent Łojasiewicz inequality of Deruelle-Ozuch
[
10
]
for a version of Perelman’s
\lambda
-functional near a Ricci-flat ALE space, which has applications to infinite-time convergence of Ricci-DeTurck flow
[
11
]
.

1.3.
Łojasiewicz inequalities

The simplest version of our main result is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
k\in{\mathbb{N}}.
The following are true of any map
u\in W^{2,2}(S^{2},S^{2})
with
E(u)\leq 4\pi k.

(a) There exists an integer
n\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
such that

\left|E(u)-4\pi n\right|\leq C_{\ref{thm:loj}a}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|.

Here
\|\cdot\|=\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(S^{2})}
and
C_{\ref{thm:loj}a}
depends only on

(b)
There exist
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0,
depending only on

as follows; fix any
1<\alpha<\frac{2L+2}{2L+1}
and
0<\kappa<{\varepsilon}_{0}.

Suppose that there exist open sets
\Gamma\Subset\hat{\Gamma}\subset S^{2}
such that

(1.7)

E(u,\hat{\Gamma})<{\varepsilon}_{0}

and

(1.8)

E(u,\Gamma)\geq\kappa.

Then
u
obeys the Łojasiewicz inequality

\left|E(u)-4\pi n\right|\leq C_{\ref{thm:loj}b}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{\alpha}.

Here

depends on
k,\kappa,\alpha,
and the geometry of
\Gamma
and
\hat{\Gamma}.

We have also the following more quantitative versions. Given

denote the image in

of the disk of radius

We shall also write

Fix

Let

Applications to harmonic map flow

We can now state our harmonic-map-flow results.

.

Let

1.5.
Idea of proof

We follow a stategy of decomposing an almost-harmonic map into an alternating sum of almost-holomorphic and almost-antiholomorphic maps. The basic estimate of Topping’s first theorem can be applied on each map, modulo a boundary term; the goal is to make sure that these boundary terms are taken on annuli where the holomorphic and antiholomorphic energies are comparable and bounded below by a power of the radius. Our analysis starts from the observation that in this case, the boundary term can be controlled using Topping’s repulsion estimate, giving precisely a (pre-)Łojasiewicz inequality.

The main difficulty in exploiting this observation
is to obtain the required lower (and upper) bounds on the energy densities in the neck regions.
These ultimately follow from a basic three-annulus estimate and its generalization to a “multi-annulus estimate,” needed to pass the lower bounds across ghost bubbles; however, a tricky inductive argument over the bubble tree is required to put these together.

2.1.
Harmonic maps to Kähler manifolds

Let
\Sigma
be a Riemannian surface and
N
a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n.
The complexified tangent bundles decompose according to type:

(2.1)

\begin{split}T\Sigma^{\mathbb{C}}=T^{1,0}\Sigma\oplus T^{0,1}\Sigma,\qquad TN^{\mathbb{C}}=T^{1,0}N\oplus T^{0,1}N.\end{split}

Given a local holomorphic coordinate
z
on
\Sigma,
we have the local coordinate frames

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x}-i\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}y}\right),\qquad\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}x}+i\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}y}\right)

for
T^{1,0}\Sigma
and
T^{0,1}\Sigma,
respectively, as well as the dual frames

dz=dx+idy,\qquad d\bar{z}=dx-idy

for
T^{*}{}^{1,0}\Sigma
and
T^{*}{}^{0,1}\Sigma,
respectively.
Given local holomorphic coordinates
w^{\alpha},

\alpha=1,\ldots,n,
on
N,
we have similar formulae for the frames

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}w^{\alpha}},

\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\bar{w}^{\alpha}},

dw^{\alpha},
and
d\bar{w}^{\alpha}.

The metric tensor
g
on
\Sigma
may be extended complex-linearly to
TN^{\mathbb{C}}.
We obtain a hermitian metric by the formula

\left\langle v,w\right\rangle=g(v,\bar{w}),

which agrees with the real-valued metric on
TN\subset TN^{\mathbb{C}}.
If
z
is a local coordinate, we may let

\sigma(z)=\sqrt{2g\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}z},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\bar{z}}\right)}.

The metric tensor and Kähler form on
\Sigma
are given locally by

g=\frac{1}{2}\sigma^{2}\left(dz\otimes d\bar{z}+d\bar{z}\otimes dz\right),\qquad\omega_{\Sigma}=\frac{i}{2}\sigma^{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}.

Similarly, we may write

h_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}=h\left(\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}w^{\alpha}},\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}\bar{w}^{\beta}}\right),

which is a Hermitian matrix. The Kähler form on
N
then has the local expression

\omega_{N}=ih_{\alpha\bar{\beta}}dw^{\alpha}\wedge d\bar{w}^{\beta}.

See e.g.
[
26
, §2.4]
, for more details.

Now, given a map
u:\Sigma\to N,
its differential
du
is naturally a section of
\mathcal{E}=T^{*}M\otimes u^{*}TN.
The complexification
\mathcal{E}^{\mathbb{C}}=T^{*}M^{\mathbb{C}}\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\left(u^{*}TN\right)^{\mathbb{C}}
decomposes into four factors corresponding to the direct sum decompositions (
2.1
). The components of
du

du=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}{\partial}u&{\bar{\partial}}u\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{{\bar{\partial}}u}&\overline{{\partial}u}\end{array}\right),

where in local coordinates, we have

{\partial}u=w_{z}^{\alpha}dz\otimes\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}w^{\alpha}},\qquad{\bar{\partial}}u=w^{\alpha}_{\bar{z}}d\bar{z}\otimes\frac{{\partial}}{{\partial}w^{\alpha}}.

Here we have written

w^{\alpha}_{z}=\frac{{\partial}w^{\alpha}(u(z))}{{\partial}z},
etc.
The Dirichlet energy density of
u,

e(u)=\frac{1}{2}|du|^{2},

decomposes under the above splitting as

\begin{split}e(u)&=\frac{1}{2}\left(|{\partial}u|^{2}+|{\bar{\partial}}u|^{2}+|\overline{{\partial}u}|^{2}+|\overline{{\bar{\partial}}u}|^{2}\right)\\
&=|{\partial}u|^{2}+|{\bar{\partial}}u|^{2}\\
&=:e_{\partial}(u)+e_{\bar{\partial}}(u).\end{split}

Letting

E_{\partial}(u)=\int_{\Sigma}e_{\partial}(u)\,dV,\qquad E_{\bar{\partial}}(u)=\int_{\Sigma}e_{{\bar{\partial}}}(u)\,dV,

the Dirichlet energy splits as

E(u)=E_{\partial}(u)+E_{\bar{\partial}}(u).

Meanwhile, a brief calculation shows that

u^{*}\omega_{N}=\left(e_{\partial}(u)-e_{\bar{\partial}}(u)\right)\omega_{\Sigma}.

Since
d\omega_{N}=0,
the pullback is again closed and its integral is invariant under homotopy of
u.
We conclude that

E_{\partial}(u)-E_{\bar{\partial}}(u)=\int_{\Sigma}u^{*}\omega_{N}=:\kappa

is a constant depending only on the homotopy class of
u.
Rearranging, we obtain

(2.2)

E(u)=4\pi\kappa+2E_{\bar{\partial}}(u).

Note that if
N
also has complex dimension one, then
\kappa=4\pi\deg(u).

Recall that the tension field
{\mathcal{T}}(u)
(see (
1.1
)) is the negative
L^{2}
-gradient of the Dirichlet functional. We can decompose
{\mathcal{T}}(u)
as follows:

{\mathcal{T}}(u)=\tau(u)+\overline{\tau(u)}\in u^{*}T^{1,0}N\oplus u^{*}T^{0,1}N.

In view of (

),
\tau(u)
is half the
L^{2}

E_{\bar{\partial}}(u),
which can also be computed by integration-by-parts. This yields the formula

Here,

is the coupled
{\bar{\partial}}
-operator using the pullback of the Levi-Civita connection on

Similarly, we have

\tau(u)=-{\partial}^{*}{\partial}u.

By the Kähler identities, we also have

\tau(u)=-\Lambda{\partial}{\bar{\partial}}u=\Lambda{\bar{\partial}}{\partial}u.

In local coordinates, we have the formula

\tau(u)^{\alpha}=\sigma^{-2}(z)\left(w^{\alpha}_{z\bar{z}}+{}^{N}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}w^{\beta}_{z}w^{\gamma}_{\bar{z}}\right)

and

We have the

[
22
]
or

for a derivation.

(2.3)

\left\langle\alpha,{\bar{\partial}}{\bar{\partial}}^{*}\alpha\right\rangle\leq-2|\nabla\alpha|^{2}+\Delta|\alpha|^{2}+K_{\Sigma}|\alpha|^{2}+q_{1}({\partial}u,\alpha)+q_{2}({\bar{\partial}}u,\alpha),

2.2.
The 2-sphere

The round 2-sphere
S^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
carries a complex structure
I:TS^{2}\to TS^{2}
derived from the cross-product on
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}:

I_{u}(v)=u\times v,\quad v\perp u.

This agrees with the complex multiplication on tangent vectors coming from the identification
S^{2}\cong{\mathbb{C}}{\mathbb{P}}^{1},
given by the stereographic coordinate charts

\begin{split}z&\mapsto\frac{1}{1+|z|^{2}}\left(2\operatorname{Re}z,2\operatorname{Im}z,1-|z|^{2}\right)\\
w=z^{-1}&\mapsto\frac{1}{1+|w|^{2}}\left(2\operatorname{Re}w,-2\operatorname{Im}w,|w|^{2}-1\right).\end{split}

A similar chart centered at any point in
S^{2}
can be obtained by post-composing with an
{\mathrm{SO}}(3)
rotation. We have

\sigma(z)=\frac{2}{1+|z|^{2}}

and

\mathrm{Vol}_{S^{2}}=\sigma^{2}\mathrm{Vol}_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}.

Letting
z=x+iy,
and viewing
u:{\mathbb{C}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to S^{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
as a vector-valued function on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2},
we have

\begin{split}e_{\partial}(u)&=\frac{1}{4\sigma^{2}}|u_{x}-u\times u_{y}|^{2}\\
e_{\bar{\partial}}(u)&=\frac{1}{4\sigma^{2}}|u_{x}+u\times u_{y}|^{2}\\
{\mathcal{T}}(u)&=\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\left(\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u+|\nabla_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}u|^{2}u\right)\\
\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(S^{2})}&=\|\sigma{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}.\end{split}

See Topping
[
29
]
for these formulae.

Remark 2.1
.

In contrast with
[
29
]
, by default, we will take norms with respect to the metric and volume form of
S^{2}
rather than with respect to the Euclidean metric in the stereographic chart. For
D_{\rho}
with
\rho\leq C,
these are uniformly equivalent, so the distinction can almost always be ignored.

This section contains the first several ingredients of our proof. We will assume that the domain manifold is

although this is not always necessary. We take the target to be a general compact Riemannian manifold,

which we will usually assume to be Kähler and eventually to be
S^{2}.

Let

K\geq 1,
and
0<\beta<\frac{1}{2}.

Our estimates will involve the following small constants:

•

{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
- constant depending only on
N
and (later) on

or if the target is
N=S^{2},
then only on

•

{\varepsilon}_{1}>0
- constant depending on
K
and

•

c_{1}>0
- constant depending only on

•

•

3.1.
{\varepsilon}
-regularity

In this subsection, we give a standard epsilon-regularity result followed by two elementary but crucial variations needed later. Recall that
D_{\rho}(x)
denotes the image in
S^{2}
of the disk of radius
\rho
about the origin in the stereographic chart centered at
x.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0,
depending on the geometry of
N,
as follows. Let
\sigma>1
and
\rho\leq 1,
and put
D=D_{\rho}(x),\hat{D}=D_{\sigma\rho}(x).

(a) Given
u\in W^{2,2}(\hat{D},N)
satisfying
\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\hat{D})}\leq 1
and

E(u,\hat{D})<{\varepsilon}_{0},

we have

\rho^{2}\|\nabla du\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(D\right)}\leq C_{\sigma}\left(E(u,\hat{D})+\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\hat{D)})}^{2}\right).

(b) Supposing that
N
is Kähler, we also have

\rho^{2}\|\nabla{\partial}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(D\right)}\leq C_{\sigma}\left(E_{\partial}(u,\hat{D})+\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\hat{D})}^{2}\right)

and

\rho^{2}\|\nabla{\bar{\partial}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(D\right)}\leq C_{\sigma}\left(E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\hat{D})+\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\hat{D})}^{2}\right).

Similar statements hold after replacing
D\Subset\hat{D}
by any pair of precompact open sets
\Omega\Subset\hat{\Omega}.

Proof.

The proof of (
a
) is a minor adaptation of the proof by Ding-Tian
[
12
, Lemma 2.1]
, which we omit.

To prove (b), we apply (
2.3
) with
\alpha={\bar{\partial}}u,
to get

\left\langle{\bar{\partial}}u,{\bar{\partial}}\tau(u)\right\rangle\leq-2|\nabla{\bar{\partial}}u|^{2}+\Delta|{\bar{\partial}}u|^{2}+\left(K_{\Sigma}+C|du|^{2}\right)|{\bar{\partial}}u|^{2}.

The result follows by a similar argument.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
N
is Kähler. Let
\frac{4}{3}\leq q\leq 2,

K\geq 1,

0<\rho\leq 1,
and
x_{0}\in S^{2}.
Let
U=U_{\rho}^{2\rho}(x_{0})
and
\hat{U}=U_{\rho/2}^{4\rho}(x_{0})\subset S^{2}.
Suppose that
\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|\leq 1,

E(u,\hat{U})<{\varepsilon}_{0},
and

(3.1)

E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\hat{U})+\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\hat{U}\right)}\leq KE_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U).

Let
V\subset U
be any measurable set with

(3.2)

|V|\geq\left(1-(2CK)^{-2}\right)|U|,

where
C
is a sufficiently large universal constant. We then have

E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)\leq CK\rho^{2-\frac{4}{q}}\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(V)}.

Proof.

By scaling-invariance, we may assume without loss of generality that
\rho=1.
Let

p=\frac{q^{*}}{2}=\frac{q}{2(q-1)}\in\left[1,2\right].

By Lemma
3.1
b
and the Sobolev inequality, we have

(3.3)

\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{2}(U)}=\|{\bar{\partial}}u\|^{2}_{L^{4}(U)}\leq C\left(E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\hat{U})+\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}\left(\hat{U}\right)}\right)\leq CKE_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U),

where we have applied the assumption (
3.1
).
Then (
3.3
) and Hölder’s inequality give

(3.4)

\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C\|e_{\bar{\partial}}\|_{L^{2}(U)}\leq CKE_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U).

We have

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)&=E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,V^{c})+E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,V).\end{split}

Applying Hölder’s inequality on the first term and the interpolation inequality on the second, we obtain

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)&\leq|V^{c}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{2}(U)}+\|e_{\bar{\partial}}\|_{L^{p}(V)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(V)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

By (
3.2
), we have

(3.5)

|V^{c}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2CK}.

Inserting (
3.4
-
3.5
), we obtain

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)&\leq\frac{1}{2}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)+\left(CKE_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|e_{{\bar{\partial}}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(V)}^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

Rearranging and cancelling exponents, we have the desired estimate.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
N,U,
and
\hat{U}
be as in the previous lemma. Given
K\geq 1,
there exist
{\varepsilon}_{1}>0,
depending on
K
and
N,
and
c_{1}>0,
depending on
K,
as follows. Let
u:\hat{U}\to N
be a map that satisfies

E(u,\hat{U})<{\varepsilon}_{1},

(3.6)

\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\hat{U})}\leq{\varepsilon}_{1}E_{\partial}(u,U),

and

(3.7)

E_{\partial}(u,\hat{U})\leq KE_{\partial}(u,U).

Then

(3.8)

c_{1}\sup_{\rho\leq r\leq 2\rho}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}e_{\partial}\,d\theta\leq\inf_{\rho\leq r\leq 2\rho}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}e_{\partial}\,d\theta

and

(3.9)

\mathrm{Area}\left\{x\in U\mid\rho^{2}e_{\partial}(u)(x)\leq c_{1}E_{\partial}(u,U)\right\}\leq(2CK)^{-2}\mathrm{Area}(U).

Proof.

This will follow by applying a standard contradiction argument to the
(1,0)
-form
{\partial}u.
We may assume
\rho=1
without loss of generality. We view
U
and
\hat{U}
as subsets of the ball
B_{4}(0)\subset{\mathbb{C}}
by stereographic projection.

First let
\alpha=f(z)dz
be a
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued holomorphic 1-form on
U^{\prime}=U^{3}_{2/3},
satisfying

{\bar{\partial}}\alpha=0,\quad\int_{U}|\alpha|^{2}=1,\,\,\,\,\text{and}\,\,\,\,\int_{U^{\prime}}|\alpha|^{2}\leq K.

By elementary complex analysis,
\alpha
must obey estimates of the form

(3.10)

2c_{1}\sup_{1\leq r\leq 2}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|\alpha|^{2}\,d\theta\leq\inf_{1\leq r\leq 2}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|\alpha|^{2}\,d\theta

and

\mathrm{Area}\left\{x\in U\mid|\alpha(x)|^{2}\leq 2c_{1}\right\}\leq(2CK)^{-2}

for a small constant
c_{1}>0
depending on
K.
The same statements hold for a holomorphic 1-form valued in a flat holomorphic bundle over
U^{\prime}.

We can now prove (
3.8
) by contradiction. Assume that
u_{i}:\hat{U}\to N
is a sequence of nonconstant maps such that

E(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq\frac{1}{i},

E_{\partial}(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq KE_{\partial}(u_{i},U),

and

(3.11)

\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\hat{U})}\leq\frac{E_{\partial}(u_{i},U)}{i},

but for which

(3.12)

c_{1}\sup_{\rho\leq r\leq 2\rho}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|{\bar{\partial}}u_{i}|^{2}\,d\theta\geq\inf_{\rho\leq r\leq 2\rho}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|{\bar{\partial}}u_{i}|^{2}\,d\theta.

By Lemma
3.1
a,
we have

\|\nabla du_{i}\|_{L^{2}(U^{\prime})}^{2}\leq C\left(E(u_{i},\hat{U})+\frac{1}{i}E_{{\partial}}(u_{i},\hat{U})\right)\leq CE(u_{i},\hat{U})\to 0.

Passing to a subsequence, we may assume that the images
u_{i}\left(U^{\prime}\right)
are contained in a fixed holomorphic coordinate neighborhood inside
N.
We have
du_{i}\to 0
strongly in
W^{1,2}(U^{\prime}).

Now, by Lemma
3.1
b
and (
3.11
), we have

(3.13)

\|\nabla{\partial}u_{i}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(U^{\prime})}\leq C\left(1+\frac{1}{i}\right)E_{{\partial}}(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq CE_{{\partial}}(u_{i},U).

Let

\alpha_{i}=\frac{{\partial}u_{i}}{\sqrt{E_{\partial}(u,U)}}.

We then have

\int_{U}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}=1,

\int_{\hat{U}}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\leq K,

\int_{\hat{U}}|{\bar{\partial}}_{u_{i}}\alpha_{i}|^{2}=\frac{\int|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})|^{2}}{E_{\partial}(u,U)}\to 0,

and, by (
3.13
),

\|\nabla\alpha_{i}\|_{L^{2}(U^{\prime})}\leq C.

We may pass to a weak limit in
W^{1,2}(U^{\prime}),

\alpha_{i}\rightharpoonup\alpha,

which again satisfies

(3.14)

\begin{split}\int_{U}|\alpha|^{2}&=1,\\
\int_{U^{\prime}}|\alpha|^{2}&\leq K.\end{split}

In the holomorphic coordinates on
N,
we have

\left({\bar{\partial}}_{u_{i}}\alpha_{i}\right)^{\mu}={\bar{\partial}}\left(\alpha_{i}\right)^{\mu}+{}^{N}\Gamma(du_{i},du_{i})\#\alpha_{i}.

Since the left-hand side tends to zero in
L^{2},
while
du_{i}
tends to zero and
\alpha_{i}
is bounded in
L^{p}
for all
p,

{\bar{\partial}}(\alpha_{i})^{\mu}

must also tend to zero in
L^{2};
hence
{\bar{\partial}}\alpha=0.
Moreover, since
\alpha_{i}
converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(U^{\prime}),

\sqrt{\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|\alpha_{i}|^{2}\,d\theta}
converges weakly in
W^{1,2}(\left[\rho,2\rho\right])
as a function of
r,
and in particular strongly in
C^{0}(\left[\rho,2\rho\right]),
so (
3.12
) is preserved.
But, together with (
3.14
), this contradicts (
3.10
), establishing (
3.8
).

The proof of (
3.9
) is similar.
∎

Proposition 3.4

(
[
29
]
, Lemma 2.5b)
.

For

Proposition 3.5

(
[
29
]
, Lemma 2.3d)
.

Let
0<\rho\leq R<\infty,
with
R\geq 1,
and assume that
D_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})\subset D_{\rho}(x_{0})
for each
i.
Let

\Omega^{\prime}=D_{\rho}(x_{0})\setminus\cup_{i}\bar{D}_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i}),\quad\hat{\Omega}^{\prime}=D_{2R}(x_{0})\setminus\cup_{i}\bar{D}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}).

If

then we have

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega^{\prime})\leq C_{\ell,\beta}\left(\frac{\rho}{R}\right)^{\beta}\left(R^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}\left(\hat{\Omega}^{\prime}\right)}^{2}+E_{\bar{\partial}}\left(u,U^{2R}_{R}(x_{0})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{R}{\rho_{i}}\right)^{\beta}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))\right).\end{split}

Proof.

After homothetically rescaling so that
R=1,
the
E_{\bar{\partial}}
terms are unchanged and the
R^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}
term decreases; hence, we may assume without loss of generality that

Letting
\mu=1
in Topping’s notation, the proof of
[
29
, Lemma 2.3d]
on p. 486 gives

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega^{\prime})&\leq C\rho^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}\|u\|_{L^{\frac{2q}{2-q}}(\hat{\Omega}^{\prime})}\\
&\leq C_{q}\rho^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}}\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}\left(\hat{\Omega}^{\prime}\right)}+\|u\|_{L^{q}(U^{2}_{1})}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{\rho_{i}}\|u\|_{L^{q}(U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))}\right).\end{split}

The proof proceeds by applying Hölder’s inequality on the remaining terms, as in
[
29
]
, and letting

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume
0<\rho_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}
for
i=1,\ldots,\ell.
Write

\Omega=S^{2}\setminus\cup_{i}D_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i}),\quad\hat{\Omega}=S^{2}\setminus\cup_{i}D_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}),

U_{i}=U^{2\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i}),\quad\hat{U}_{i}=U^{4\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}).

Supposing that
E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\hat{\Omega})<{\varepsilon}_{0},
we have

E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega)\leq C_{\ell,\beta}\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(\hat{\Omega})}^{2}+\sum\rho_{i}^{-\beta}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))\right).

Proof.

Since
\rho_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}},
there exist two antipodal points
x_{0},\hat{x}_{0}\in S^{2}
such that

D_{1/\sqrt{\ell}}(x_{0})\cap D_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})=\emptyset=D_{1/\sqrt{\ell}}(\hat{x}_{0})\cap D_{\rho_{i}}(x_{i})

for
i=1,\ldots,\ell.
Let

\rho=\sqrt{\ell},\qquad R=(2C_{\ell,\beta})^{\frac{1}{\beta}}\rho,

where
C_{\ell,\beta}\geq 1
is the constant of the previous Proposition.
We apply the proposition twice, to obtain

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega\cap D_{\sqrt{\ell}}(x_{0}))\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(S^{2})}^{2}+E_{\bar{\partial}}\left(u,U^{2R}_{R}(x_{0})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\frac{R}{\rho_{i}}\right)^{\beta}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))\right),\end{split}

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega\cap D_{\sqrt{\ell}}(\hat{x}_{0}))\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(R^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(S^{2})}^{2}+E_{\bar{\partial}}\left(u,U^{2R}_{R}(\hat{x}_{0})\right)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{R}{\rho_{i}}\right)^{\beta}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))\right).\end{split}

Since
R^{2}\geq\ell,
we have
U^{2R}_{R}(x_{0})\subset D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}}(\hat{x}_{0}),
so
U^{2R}_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega\cap D_{\sqrt{\ell}}(\hat{x}_{0});
also
U^{2R}_{R}(\hat{x}_{0})\subset D_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\ell}}}(x_{0}),
so
U^{2R}_{R}(\hat{x}_{0})\subset\Omega\cap D_{\sqrt{\ell}}(x_{0}).
Therefore

E_{\bar{\partial}}\left(u,U^{2R}_{R}(\hat{x}_{0})\right)+E_{\bar{\partial}}\left(u,U^{2R}_{R}(x_{0})\right)\leq E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega).

We may add the above two inequalities together and rearrange, to obtain

\begin{split}&E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,\Omega)\leq 2\left(R^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|_{L^{2}(S^{2})}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(\frac{R}{\rho_{i}}\right)^{\beta}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u,U^{\rho_{i}}_{\rho_{i}/2}(x_{i}))\right).\end{split}

Finally, we absorb
R
into the constant to obtain the desired estimate.
∎

Theorem 3.8

(Pre-Łojasiewicz estimate)

{\varepsilon}_{3}

-

-

By Lemma

3.4

\begin{split}Ck\|{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}&\geq\|e_{\partial}e_{\bar{\partial}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(S^{2})}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{q}{2}}}\sum_{i}\|e_{\partial}e_{\bar{\partial}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(V_{i})}\\
&\geq\frac{c_{1}}{\ell^{\frac{q}{2}}}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{-2}E_{{\partial}}(u,U_{i})\|e_{\bar{\partial}}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}(V_{i})}\\
&\geq\frac{c_{1}}{MK\ell^{\frac{q}{2}}}\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{\frac{4}{q}-4}E_{{\bar{\partial}}}(u,U_{i})^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{c_{1}}{MK\ell^{\frac{q}{2}+1}}\left(\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{\frac{2}{q}-2}E_{{\bar{\partial}}}(u,U_{i})\right)^{2}.\end{split}

Rearranging and canceling squares, we get

\begin{split}\sqrt{\frac{CMKk\ell^{\frac{q}{2}+1}}{c_{1}}}\lambda^{3-\frac{4}{q}}\|{\mathcal{T}}\|&\geq\sum_{i}\rho_{i}^{1-\frac{2}{q}}E_{{\bar{\partial}}}(u,U_{i}).\end{split}

Combining (
3.22

3.21
).

Applying Proposition
3.6
, we obtain

The desired estimate now follows from Lemma
3.7
a.

(
b
) We may take
\lambda=\rho_{j}.
By our assumption (

) and (
a
), we have

\begin{split}\lambda^{m+\beta}\leq M\left(\lambda^{-\beta}E(u,U_{j})+\lambda^{2-\beta}\|{\mathcal{T}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\hat{U}_{j})}\right)&\leq C\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}\|+\lambda^{1-2\beta}\right)\|{\mathcal{T}}\|\end{split}

Adding

&=C\|{\mathcal{T}}\|^{\frac{m+\beta}{m+3\beta-1}}.\end{split}

The second estimate of (

4.
Estimates on energy density in neck regions

In this section, we prove the lower and upper bounds on the the (anti)holomorphic energy densities that we will later assemble into the needed estimates on an almost-holomorphic bubble tree.

4.1.
Three-annulus estimate

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
N
is Kähler. Let
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
with
|n|\leq L,

1<\tau\leq\sigma,
and
0<\beta\leq\frac{1}{2}
be such that

(4.1)

\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\sigma^{2}+1}<\sigma^{2\beta}.

There exists
\xi_{0}>0,
depending on
N,L,\tau,\sigma,
and
\beta,
as follows.

Given any
x_{0}\in S^{2}
and
0<\rho\leq 1,
let
\hat{U}=U^{\tau\sigma\rho}_{\sigma^{-1}\rho}(x_{0}).
Suppose that
u:\hat{U}\to N
is a nonconstant
W^{2,2}
map with

(4.2)

E(u,\hat{U})<\xi_{0}

and

(4.3)

\rho^{2}\|{\mathcal{T}}(u)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\hat{U})}\leq\xi_{0}E_{\partial}(u,\hat{U}).

Let

S(1)=\sup_{\sigma^{-1}\!\rho<r\leq\tau\sigma^{-1}\!\!\rho}r^{-n}\sqrt{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{S^{1}_{r}(x_{0})}e_{\partial}\,d\theta}.

S(2)=\sup_{\rho\leq r\leq\tau\!\rho}r^{-n}\sqrt{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{S^{1}_{r}(x_{0})}e_{\partial}\,d\theta}.

S(3)=\sup_{\sigma\!\rho\leq r<\tau\sigma\!\rho}r^{-n}\sqrt{\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{S^{1}_{r}(x_{0})}e_{\partial}\,d\theta}.

The following implications hold:

(
a
)

S(1)\leq\sigma^{1-\beta}S(2)\Rightarrow S(2)<\sigma^{\beta}S(3)

(
b
)

S(3)\leq\sigma^{1-\beta}S(2)\Rightarrow S(2)<\sigma^{\beta}S(1).

Proof.

We first check the implications when
{\partial}u=g(z)dz
is a nonzero holomorphic 1-form on a flat annulus
V=U^{\tau\sigma}_{\sigma^{-1}}(0)\subset{\mathbb{C}}.
More specifically, we prove:

(
a^{\prime}
)

S(1)\leq\sqrt{\sigma\left(1+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{2\sigma}\right)}S(2)\Rightarrow S(2)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{1+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{2\sigma}}}S(3)

(
b^{\prime}
)

S(3)\leq\sqrt{\sigma\left(1+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{2\sigma}\right)}S(2)\Rightarrow S(2)\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{1+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{2\sigma}}}S(1)

Under the assumption (
4.1
), these implications are strictly stronger than (
a
) and (
b
).

Dividing
g
by
S(2)z^{n},
we may reduce to the case
n=0
and
S(2)=1.

To prove (
a^{\prime}
) in this case, we use the Laurent expansion of
g,
which reads

g(z)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}.

This gives

(4.4)

F(r)^{2}:=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{S^{1}_{r}}|g|^{2}\,d\theta=\sum|a_{n}|^{2}r^{2n}.

Assume that the supremum
S(2)=1
is attained at
r_{0}\in\left[1,\tau\right],
so

(4.5)

F(r_{0})^{2}=1=\sum|a_{n}|^{2}r_{0}^{2n}.

We have the identity

1=\frac{\sigma+\sigma^{-1}}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}},

and, for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}},
the inequality

(4.6)

1\leq\frac{\sigma^{-2n+1}+\sigma^{2n-1}}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}}.

Inserting (
4.6
) into (
4.5
) for each
n\in{\mathbb{Z}},
we have

\begin{split}1=\sum|a_{n}|^{2}r_{0}^{2n}&\leq\frac{1}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}}\sum|a_{n}|^{2}r_{0}^{2n}\left(\sigma^{-2n+1}+\sigma^{2n-1}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}}\left(\sigma^{-1}F\left(\sigma^{-1}r_{0}\right)+\sigma F\left(\sigma r_{0}\right)\right)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}}\left(\sigma^{-1}S(1)^{2}+\sigma S(3)^{2}\right).\end{split}

We insert the assumption
S(1)^{2}\leq\sigma\left(1+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{2\sigma}\right),
and rearrange, to obtain

1\leq\frac{2\sigma}{2+\frac{(\sigma-1)^{2}}{\sigma}}S(3)^{2},

establishing (
a^{\prime}
). The proof of (
b^{\prime}
) is identical.

The proof of (
a
) and (
b
) now follows from a standard compactness-and-contradiction argument, similar to the proof of Lemma
3.3
. Suppose that that the implication (
a
) fails for any choice of constant
\xi_{0}.
Then we can choose a sequence of maps
u_{i}:\hat{U}\to N
with
E(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq\frac{1}{i}
and

(4.7)

\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\hat{U})}^{2}\leq\frac{E_{{\partial}}(u_{i},\hat{U})}{i},

but for which

(4.8)

\max\{\sigma^{\beta-1}S(1),\sigma^{\beta}S(3)\}\leq S(2)

for each
i,
where
S(j)
is defined with respect to
u_{i}.
Let
U^{\prime}
be any domain with

U:=U_{\tau\sigma^{-1}}^{\sigma}\Subset U^{\prime}\Subset U_{\sigma^{-1}}^{\tau\sigma}=\hat{U}.

We have

E_{\partial}(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq C_{\sigma}\left(S(1)^{2}+S(3)^{2}+E_{\partial}(u_{i},U)\right)\leq C_{\sigma}\left(S(2)^{2}+E_{\partial}(u_{i},U)\right).

But by Lemma
3.1
b,
we have

S(2)^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\left(\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|^{2}+E_{\partial}(u_{i},U^{\prime})\right),

where
C^{\prime}
depends on
\sigma
and
U^{\prime}.

Together with (
4.7
), this gives

E_{\partial}(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq C^{\prime}\left(\frac{E_{\partial}(u_{i},\hat{U})}{i}+E_{\partial}(u_{i},U^{\prime})\right).

For
i
sufficiently large, we may rearrange to obtain

E_{\partial}(u_{i},\hat{U})\leq C^{\prime}E_{\partial}(u_{i},U^{\prime}),

which by (
4.7
), gives

\|{\mathcal{T}}(u_{i})\|_{L^{2}(\hat{U})}^{2}\leq C^{\prime}\frac{E_{{\partial}}(u_{i},U^{\prime})}{i}.

The argument now proceeds by letting

\alpha_{i}=\frac{{\partial}u_{i}}{S(2)}

and passing to a subsequence to obtain a holomorphic limit on
U^{\prime},
as in the proof of Lemma
3.3
. One can then enlarge
U^{\prime}
and pass to a diagonal subsequence to obtain a holomorphic limit on
\hat{U},
which contradicts the implication (
a^{\prime}
). The proof of (
b
) is the same.
∎

the following holds.

\begin{split}\Lambda&=B_{1}(0)\setminus\cup_{i=1}^{\ell}\bar{B}_{\zeta_{i}}(z_{i}),\\

Suppose that

is a nonzero holomorphic function on

Let

S_{i}(1)=\sqrt{\left(\zeta^{\prime}_{i}\right)^{-2n_{i}}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.98003pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.26338pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.6363pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.45924pt}}\!\int_{S^{1}_{\zeta^{\prime}_{i}}\left(z^{\prime}_{i}\right)}|f(z)|^{2}\,d\theta}

The following implication holds:

\begin{split}\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}S_{i}(1)\leq\sigma^{1-\beta}S(2)\quad\Rightarrow\quad S(2)<\sigma^{\beta}S(3).\end{split}

Proof.

Dividing

for all

This only affects the estimates up to constants depending on

and
\zeta,
which can be absorbed into
\sigma_{0}.

We now use the following “multiple” Laurent expansion:

(4.19)

\begin{split}f(z)&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w|=\sigma}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}dw-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|w-z^{\prime}_{i}|=\zeta^{\prime}_{i}}\frac{f(w)}{w-z}dw\\
&=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{n,i}(z-z^{\prime}_{i})^{-n}\\
&=:f_{0}(z)+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}f_{i}(z).\end{split}

The expansion is obtained by inserting the identity
\frac{1}{w-z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{n}}{w^{n+1}}
in the first term and
\frac{1}{w-z}=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\frac{-(w-z^{\prime}_{i})^{n}}{(z-z^{\prime}_{i})^{n+1}}
in the remaining terms.
By construction,

are the nonnegative Fourier coefficients of

along the circle

and

are the negative Fourier coefficients of

along

We therefore have the bounds

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{2}\sigma^{2n}\leq S(3)^{2}

and

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n,i}|^{2}\left(\zeta_{i}^{\prime}\right)^{-2n}=(\zeta_{i}^{\prime})^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n,i}|^{2}\left(\zeta_{i}^{\prime}\right)^{2-2n}\leq S_{i}(1)^{2}

for
i=1,\ldots,\ell,
which gives

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n,i}|^{2}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\zeta_{i}}\right)^{2n-2}\leq\frac{\zeta_{i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}S_{i}(1)^{2}.

We have

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Lambda}|f_{0}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{B_{1}}|f_{0}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum|a_{n}|^{2}\int_{B_{1}}|z|^{2n}\,dV_{z}=4\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{|a_{n}|^{2}}{2n+2}\leq 2S(3)^{2}

and, for
i=1,\ldots,\ell,

\begin{split}\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.83337pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-6.11674pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.48965pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.31259pt}}\!\int_{\Lambda}|f_{i}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}\leq\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{U^{1}_{\zeta_{i}}(z_{i})}|f_{i}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}&=\frac{2}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{n,i}|^{2}\int_{U^{1}_{\zeta_{i}}(z_{i})}|z-z^{\prime}_{i}|^{-2n}\,dV_{z}\\
&\leq 4\left(|b_{1,i}|^{2}(-\log\zeta_{i})+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{|b_{n,i}|^{2}}{\zeta_{i}^{2n-2}(2n-2)}\right)\\
&\leq\frac{4\zeta_{i}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}S_{i}(1)^{2}\left(-\log\zeta_{i}+\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\right)\\
&\leq 2\frac{S_{i}(1)^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}.\end{split}

Putting these together, we have

\begin{split}S(2)=\sqrt{\int_{\Lambda}|f(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}}&\leq\sqrt{\int_{\Lambda}|f_{0}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sqrt{\int_{\Lambda}|f_{i}(z)|^{2}\,dV_{z}}\\
&\leq\sqrt{2}\left(S(3)+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}S_{i}}{\sigma}\right).\end{split}

This clearly implies the claimed inequality.
∎

\left|\int\left(e_{\bar{\partial}}(u(t_{2}))-e_{\bar{\partial}}(u(t_{1}))\right)\varphi\,dV\right|\leq C\sup|\nabla\varphi|\sqrt{k}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\delta(t)\,dt,

where
\varphi
is compactly supported. The same identity holds with
e_{{\partial}}
or
e
in place of
e_{\bar{\partial}}.
Let

U_{i}^{\prime}=U^{\frac{5}{2}\lambda_{i}}_{\frac{4}{5}\lambda_{i}}(x_{i}),\quad\hat{U}_{i}^{\prime}=U^{3\lambda_{i}}_{\frac{2}{3}\lambda_{i}}(x_{i}),\quad\Omega^{\prime}=S^{2}\setminus\cup\bar{D}_{\frac{4}{5}\lambda_{i}}(x_{i}).

We can apply (
6.12
) several times, to obtain:

(6.13)

\begin{split}E_{\bar{\partial}}(u(t),\Omega^{\prime})&<{\varepsilon}_{0}+\frac{C\sqrt{k}}{\min\lambda_{i}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\delta(t)\,dt,\end{split}

(6.14)

\begin{split}E(u(t),\hat{U}_{i}^{\prime})&<{\varepsilon}_{0}+\frac{C\sqrt{k}}{\min\lambda_{i}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\delta(t)\,dt,\end{split}

and

(6.15)

\begin{split}E_{\partial}(u(t),U^{\prime}_{i})&\geq\kappa-\frac{C\sqrt{k}}{\min\lambda_{i}^{2}}\int_{0}^{t}\delta(t)\,dt.\end{split}

We first prove (
6.11
) with coefficient
1
in place of
2
on both sides. Since the hypotheses of Theorem
1.4
are satisfied, we have

(6.16)

\Delta(t)\leq\delta(t)^{\alpha},

where we may ignore the constant by taking
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small. This gives us

\frac{d}{dt}\Delta(t)\leq-\Delta(t)^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

as long as
\Delta(t)\geq 0,
and

\frac{d}{dt}\Delta(t)\leq-(-\Delta(t))^{\frac{2}{\alpha}}

thereafter. The inequalities follow from Gronwall’s Theorem.

Next, we prove strict inequality in (
6.8
-
6.10
). By (
6.7
), we have

\frac{d}{dt}\Delta(t)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}=-\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\Delta(t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\delta^{2}(t).

Together with (
6.16
), this gives

(6.17)

\begin{split}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\delta(t)\,dt=\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\delta(t)^{2}\delta(t)^{-1}\,dt&\leq\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\delta(t)^{2}\Delta(t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,dt\\
&=-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\Delta(t)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\,dt\\
&\leq\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-1}\left(\Delta(t_{1})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}-\Delta(t_{2})^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\right).\end{split}

Apply (
6.17
) with
t_{1}=0
and
t_{2}=T_{*},
to obtain

(6.18)

\int_{0}^{T_{*}}\delta(t)\,dt\leq\frac{2\alpha}{\alpha-1}\delta_{0}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}.

Provided that
\delta_{0}
is sufficiently small, we may conclude from (
6.13
-
6.15
) that strict inequality holds in (
6.8
-
6.10
). This implies that in fact
T_{*}=T,
so the first estimate of Theorem
1.5
a
now follows from (
6.11
). The second estimate of Theorem
1.5
a
follows from (
6.17
) by integrating the harmonic map flow equation in time and applying the
L^{2}
norm, then using (
6.18
).

To obtain Theorem
1.5
b,
let
u_{\infty}
be the body map along any subsequence of times; by (
6.8
-
6.10
), this must be nonconstant and holomorphic. The stated

t_{2}\to\infty

6.17
) and inserting (
6.11
). The

(see e.g. Song-Waldron
[

1.6
, suppose that
u_{\infty}

\{x_{i}\}=\{z_{i}\}

for each

-

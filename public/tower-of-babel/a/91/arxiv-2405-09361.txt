Quantum operations for Kramers-Wannier duality
Maaz Khan, Syed Anausha Bin Zakir Khan, Arif Mohd

Abstract
We study the Kramers-Wannier duality for the transverse-field Ising lattice on a ring. A careful consideration of the ring boundary conditions shows that the duality has to be implemented with a proper treatment of different charge sectors of both the twisted and untwisted Ising and the dual-Ising Hilbert spaces. We construct a superoperator that explicitly maps the Ising operators to the dual-Ising operators. The superoperator naturally acts on the tensor product of the Ising and the dual-Ising Hilbert space. We then show that the relation between our superoperator and the Kramers-Wannier duality operator that maps the Ising Hilbert space to the dual-Ising Hilbert space is naturally provided by quantum operations and the duality can be understood as a quantum operation that we construct. We provide the operator-sum representation for the Kramers-Wannier quantum operations and reproduce the well-known fusion rules. In addition to providing the quantum information perspective on the Kramers-Wannier duality, our explicit protocol will also be useful in implementing the Kramers-Wannier duality on a quantum computer.



I
Introduction

Kramers-Wannier duality of the two dimensional Ising model on a square lattice
Kramers and Wannier 1941
is a paradigmatic example of duality. While originally formulated for the statistical Ising model it can be converted via the transfer function formalism (see, for e.g., Ref.
Shankar 2017
) to a duality of the quantum Ising lattice in one dimension with a dual-Ising lattice that lives on the links of the original lattice. The duality also offers a theoretical laboratory in which many concepts like non-invertible symmetries, defect operators, entanglement entropy can be demonstrated, understood, and generalized
(see, for e.g., the review article
Shao 2024
).

In this article our goal is to provide a quantum information perspective on the Kramers-Wannier duality for the transverse-field Ising lattice on a ring. We will show how the duality action on states can be understood as quantum operations and how the well known fusion rules can be derived through this perspective. Some articles in which similar ideas are discussed, implicitly or explicitly, are

Okada and Tachikawa 2024
;
Ashkenazi and Zohar 2022
;
Tantivasadakarn et al. 2021
;
Tantivasadakarn et al. 2023
;
Lootens et al. 2024
;
Lootens et al. 2023a
;
Lootens et al. 2023b
.

In order to frame our question sharply it will be convenient to phrase the statement of the duality in terms of bond algebras (see Ref.
Cobanera et al. 2011
). Let us denote the Ising operators/states with unhatted quantities and those of the dual-Ising with hatted quantities.
The Ising bond algebra is the algebra generated by the operators

X_{i}
and
Z_{i-1}Z_{i}
, where the index
i
runs over the Ising spins 1 to N and
X,Y,Z
are the usual Pauli operators. This algbera has global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry generated by the operator
Q=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}
.
The dual bond algebra, i.e., the dual-Ising operator algebra, is generated by the operators
\hat{X}_{i}
and
\hat{Z}_{i-1}\hat{Z}_{i}
, and acts on the dual spins residing on the dual lattice
as shown in Fig.
1
. The dual algebra also has a global
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, sometimes called the quantum symmetry, generated by the operator
\hat{Q}=\prod\limits_{i=1}^{N}\hat{X}_{i}
.

The Kramers-Wannier duality, in terms of bond-algebras, can now be phrased as a map from the Ising Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
to the dual-Ising Hilbert space

\hat{\mathcal{H}}
,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}},

(1)

such that,

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}

\displaystyle X_{i}=\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1}{\mathscr{D}},

(2a)

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}

\displaystyle Z_{i-1}Z_{i}=\hat{X}_{i}{\mathscr{D}}.

(2b)

We are however interested in the explicit mapping between the bond algebras themselves. It is clear that any operator that maps the bond algebras as in Eq. (
2
)
must itself contain both the hatted (i.e., Ising) and unhatted (i.e., dual-Ising) operators. It thus naturally acts on the tensor product of the Ising and the dual-Ising Hilbert spaces,

\mathcal{H}\otimes\hat{\mathcal{H}}
. Therefore the correct expression for the action of such an operator, now denoted by
\mathcal{U}
, is

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\left(X_{i}\otimes\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)=\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1}\right)\mathcal{U},

(3a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle\left(Z_{i-1}Z_{i}\otimes\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)=\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{X}_{i}\right)\mathcal{U},

(3b)

where the
\mathbbm{1}
and
\hat{\mathbbm{1}}
are the identity operators on the Ising and the dual-Ising Hilbert spaces, respectively.

One of our objectives is to find an explicit expression of
\mathcal{U}
. Another objective is to construct
{\mathscr{D}}
from this
\mathcal{U}
. We will show that

{\mathscr{D}}
acts on the space of states on the Ising Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(density matrices on
\mathcal{H}
) as
quantum operations
. Not every quantum operation
admits a description in terms of state vectors (wavefunctions). Remarkably,
{\mathscr{D}}
does! We will show this and use this to obtain the well-known fusion rules for the
Kramers-Wannier duality.

This article is organized as follows. In Sec.
II
, we introduce the quantum Ising lattice on the ring and we discuss the boundary conditions and the charge/twist sectors.
In Sec.
III
we construct the superoperator that implements the duality on the Ising bond algebra. In Sec.
IV
we give a quick review of quantum operations.
In Sec.
V
we show how the Kramers-Wannier duality is realized as a quantum operation. Sec.
VI
concludes the paper with a summary and outlook.

So far we have not mentioned a specific Ising Hamiltonian. The only requirement we have is that it be constructed out of the bond algebra. Although the specific Hamiltonian
is not needed for the purposes of this paper, it is nevertheless instructive to have an example to understand the effect of twists

. For the transverse-field Ising Hamiltonian we have

\displaystyle H=-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}.

(12)

Note that there are two theories here depending upon the boundary condition in Eq.
10
. These are called the twisted sectors.
Explicitly, the Hamiltonian in each sector is labelled by the twist
t\in\{0,1\}
as

\displaystyle H^{\{t\}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}Z_{i}Z_{i+1}-(-1)^{t}Z_{N}Z_{1}\right]-\sum_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}.

(13)

Now applying the duality transformation in Eq.
2
to the Ising Hamiltonian we get

\displaystyle{\mathscr{D}}H=\hat{H}{\mathscr{D}},

(14)

where the dual-Ising Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{X}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1}.

(15)

Just like the Ising twist sectors, here also we have two dual-Ising twist sectors that are governed by their own Hamiltonians labelled by
\hat{t}\in\{0,1\}
that we write explicitly as

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\{\hat{t}\}}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N}\hat{X}_{i}-\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1}+(-1)^{\hat{t}}\hat{Z}_{N}\hat{Z}_{1}\right].

(16)

Note that the effect of the duality transformation on the theory is to invert the coupling
\lambda
, wherein lies the power of the duality to understand a strongly coupled theory in terms of
a weakly coupled theory. We emphasize that it is
not
that that Kramers-Wannier duality is mapping the
t=0
Ising sector to some particular
\hat{t}
dual-Ising sector.
The duality action is more nuanced. The fusion rules in Eqns. (
8
) and (
11
) relate

. Modern way to interpret this table, say for the entry labelled
as
C
, is that the charge

states of Ising are mapped to charge 1 states of dual-Ising with a defect. Similarly, the entry labelled

says that the charge 1 states of the
defect Ising are mapped to the charge -1 states of dual-Ising. The twist, therefore, corresponds to the

-symmetry defect

III
Construction of the superoperator
\mathcal{U}

From Eq. (
3
) the action of
\mathcal{U}
on charges
Q
and
\hat{Q}
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\left(Q\otimes\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\hat{t}}\mathcal{U},

(17a)

\displaystyle(-1)^{t}\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{Q}\right)\mathcal{U},

(17b)

where we have also made use of the boundary conditions of Eqns. (
7
) and (
10
). We will construct
\mathcal{U}
in two steps.

We first define an operator
{\mathcal{S}}
that implements the duality transformation like Eq.
2
on a
single
Ising chain in the sense that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}\hat{X}_{i}

\displaystyle=\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1}{\mathcal{S}},

(18a)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}\hat{Z}_{i-1}\hat{Z}_{i}

\displaystyle=\hat{X}_{i}{\mathcal{S}}.

(18b)

The explicit expression for this operator (see also Ref.
Shao 2024
) is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{S}}=\hat{P}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\frac{(1+i\hat{X}_{i})}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{(1+i\hat{Z}_{i}\hat{Z}_{i+1})}{\sqrt{2}}\right]\frac{(1+i\hat{X}_{N})}{\sqrt{2}},

(19)

where the hatted projector
\hat{P}
is,

\displaystyle\hat{P}=\frac{1+(-1)^{t}\hat{Q}}{2}.

(20)

Next we define an operator
{\cal C}
that copies the Ising operators to the dual Ising operators just in the right way so that the duality is implemented when followed by
{\mathcal{S}}
in Eq. (
19
).
In particular,

\displaystyle{\cal C}\left(X_{i}\otimes\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{X}_{i}\right){\cal C},

(21a)

\displaystyle{\cal C}\left({Z}_{i-1}{Z}_{i}\otimes\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbbm{1}\otimes\hat{Z}_{i-1}\hat{Z}_{i}\right){\cal C}.

(21b)

The explicit expression of the copying operator is

\displaystyle{\cal C}=\left[\prod_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(SWAP\right)_{i}\right]\frac{(1+iX_{N}\hat{X}_{N})}{\sqrt{2}}\frac{(1+iY_{N}\hat{Y}_{N})}{\sqrt{2}}P,

(22)

where
(SWAP)_{i}
is the operator that swaps between the
i^{th}
Ising and dual-Ising spins,

\displaystyle(SWAP)_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+X_{i}\hat{X}_{i}+Y_{i}\hat{Y}_{i}+Z_{i}\hat{Z}_{i}\right),

(23)

and
P
is the projection,

\displaystyle P=\frac{1+(-1)^{\hat{t}}Q}{2}.

(24)

Finally, the superoperator that implements the duality transformation on the Ising operators in the sense of Eq.
3
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}:={\mathcal{S}}{\cal C}.

(25)

\mathcal{U}
is the key operator of this paper. Due the presence of projectors in
{\mathcal{S}}
and
{\cal C}
, the operator
\mathcal{U}
is non-invertible.
The projectors are there to ensure that the algebra in Eq. (
17
) is respected.
For later use we also define an operator
\tilde{\mathcal{U}}=P\mathcal{U}\hat{P}
and record the following relations,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{\dagger}\mathcal{U}

\displaystyle=\frac{P}{2},

(26a)

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{U}}^{\dagger}\tilde{\mathcal{U}}

\displaystyle=\frac{P\hat{P}}{2}.

(26b)

What is the relation between
\mathcal{U}
of Eq. (
3
) and
{\mathscr{D}}
of Eq. (
2
)? How do we get
{\mathscr{D}}
from
\mathcal{U}
? We claim that the answer to these questions is obtained by viewing the Kramers-Wannier duality through the lens of quantum information theory.
More precisely, we will show that action of
{\mathscr{D}}
is that of a non trace-preserving quantum channel, called quantum operations in general, acting on
the density matrices on the Ising Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and yielding density matrices on the dual-Ising Hilbert space
\hat{\mathcal{H}}
.
In the next section, Sec.
IV
, we quickly review the quantum operations before moving on to show how the Kramers-Wannier duality is framed in this language.

IV
A quick review of Quantum Operations

In this section we give a quick review, based on Ref.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
of the definition of quantum operations and the equivalent form in which we will use it in the next sections.
For details we refer the reader to Ref.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
.

A quantum operation
{\cal E}
is defined as a map from the density operators on
\mathcal{H}
to the density operators on

\hat{\mathcal{H}}

that satisfies the following three properties:

1.

tr
\left[{\cal E}(\rho)\right]\leq 1

2.

{\cal E}
is a convex linear map on the set of density matrices on
\mathcal{H}
.

3.

{\cal E}
is a completely positive map.

When
\mathrm{tr}\left[{\cal E}(\rho)\right]
is strictly equal to
1
quantum operation is referred to as quantum channel.
It can be proved (see, Ref.
Nielsen and Chuang 2010
) that a map
{\cal E}
satisfies the above three axioms if and only if it
admits an operator sum representation,

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\rho)=\sum_{i}E_{i}\rho E_{i}^{\dagger},

(27)

for some set of operators
\{E_{i}\}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\hat{\mathcal{H}}
, such that the sum

\displaystyle\sum_{i}E_{i}^{\dagger}E_{i}\leq\mathbbm{1}.

(28)

It is in this explicit
form that we will express the quantum operation implementing the Kramers-Wannier duality.

V.2
Why is
{\mathscr{D}}
the Kramers-Wannier duality?

The basic tenet of any duality is that it preserves the correlation functions, up to possibly an overall state and operator independent normalization.
The question is therefore if the quantum operation
{\mathscr{D}}

VI
Discussion

In this paper we have provided a quantum information perspective on the Kramers-Wannier duality of the Ising lattice on a ring. We showed that the duality acts on Ising states (density matrices) and on the Ising bond algebra via quantum operations. We gave an explicit construction of this quantum operation and reproduced through this perspective the duality action on the vector states and the well-known fusion rules in the literature
Li et al. 2023
;
Shao 2024
. Due to the charge superselection sectors and the inclusion of twisted Hilbert spaces the duality is not formulated on a fixed Hilbert space. This
is reflected in the non trace-preserving nature of our quantum operation implementing the duality. Nevertheless the duality is still useful because it maps the Ising correlators to the dual-Ising correlators as we have shown. This has an effect of inverting the coupling and therefore mapping a strongly coupled system to a weakly coupled system exemplifying the UV/IR nature of dualities. If one insists on formulating the duality on a fixed Hilbert space then one would have to restrict to the individual charge superselection sectors
Radičević 2018
, in which case we would have a trace preserving quantum channel.

Yet another way in which Kramers-Wannier duality can be derived is by gauging the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
charge of the Ising model
Aksoy et al. 2024
;
Kogut 1979
;
Savit 1980
. It will be interesting to see how the gauging approach can be seen from the quantum operations perspective (see, for e.g., Ref.
Ashkenazi and Zohar 2022
) and how it is related to our approach here which does not involve gauge fields. This may also pave the way to thinking about ‘t Hooft anomalies from the point of view of quantum information theory. Let us elaborate on this a little. In either the original argument of ‘t Hooft
’t Hooft 1980
or that of Callan and Harvey
Callan and Harvey 1985
, some ancillary degrees of freedom are coupled to the original system with a global symmetry. In the case of ‘t Hooft these degrees of freedom are the background gauge field for the global symmetry and chiral fermions coupled to this gauge field that cancel the gauge anomaly, while in the case of Callan-Harvey the ancillary degrees of freedom live in higher dimensions and cancel the gauge anomaly by the anomaly-inflow mechanism. In both cases, in the end the ancillary degrees of freedom are decoupled (traced out)
and one deduces that the ‘t Hooft anomaly should remain the same at any energy scale

Finally, as mentioned in the abstract, the explicit operators we have provided in this paper makes it possible to study the Kramers-Wannier duality experimentally on a quantum computer.
The gates appearing in our operator expressions are all two-qubit gates so it should be possible to implement the duality following our quantum operations, say, on an IBM quantum computer using qiskit.
Using our explicitly laid out protocol the transformation of ground states, entanglement entropy, critical coupling etc., can thus be studied experimentally.

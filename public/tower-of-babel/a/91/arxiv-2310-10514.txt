Diagonalization in a quantum kicked rotor model with non-analytic potential
Yunfeng Shi, Li Wen

Abstract
. In this paper we study the lattice quasi-periodic operators with power-law long-range hopping and meromorphic monotone potentials, and diagonalize the operators via a Nash-Moser iteration scheme. As applications, we obtain uniform power-law localization, uniform dynamical localization and Lipschitz continuity of the integrated density of states (IDS) for such operators. Our main motivation comes from investigating quantum suppression of chaos in a quantum kicked rotor model with non-analytical potential.



1.
Introduction

Quantum chaos aims to investigate the quantum mechanics of classically chaotic systems. One of the basic models in quantum chaos is the so called quantum kicked rotor, which was first introduced by
[
Cea79
]
as a quantum analog of the standard mapping. This model is defined by

\displaystyle\sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial\Psi(\theta,t)}{\partial t}=\left(\mathcal{H}_{0}+\check{\phi}(\theta)\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\delta(t-2n\omega)\right)\Psi(\theta,t),

(1.1)

where

\displaystyle\ (\theta,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R},\ \omega>0,\ \mathcal{H}_{0}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}\ {\rm or}\ \sqrt{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},

and
\check{\phi}:\ \mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is a potential
1
1

1

The original model in
[
Cea79
]
corresponds to (
1.1
) with
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\theta^{2}}
and
\check{\phi}(\theta)=\cos(2\pi\theta)
.
.
Typically, the motion in the quantum kicked rotor model is almost-periodic while the classical one is chaotic. This quantum suppression of chaos phenomenon was first discovered in
[
Cea79
]
, and well understood after the
remarkable work of Fishman, Grempel and Prange (cf.
[
FGP82
,
GFP82
]
), in which they mapped the quantum kicked rotor model onto lattice ergodic operators
H_{x}
given by

\displaystyle(H_{x}u)(n)=\sum_{m\neq n}\phi({n-m})u(m)+d_{n}(x)u(n),\ x\in\mathbb{R},\ n\in\mathbb{Z},

where

\displaystyle\phi(n)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}\tan\pi\left(\frac{\check{\phi}(\theta)}{2}\right)e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}n\theta}{d}\theta,

\displaystyle d_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\tan\pi\left(x-n^{2}\omega\right)\ {\rm or}\ \tan\pi\left(x-n\omega\right)\ \mbox{depending on}\ \mathcal{H}_{0}.

The parameter
x\in\mathbb{R}
represents the quasi-energy of the Floquet operator associated to (
1.1
) (cf.
[
FGP82
]
). It turns out that the almost-periodicity of the motion in (
1.1
) follows from the Anderson localization (i.e., pure point spectrum with exponentially decaying eigenfunctions) of
H_{x}
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}
(cf.
[
FGP82
,
SPK22
]
).

For the analytic potential
\check{\phi}
, the operator
H_{x}
admits an exponential hopping (i.e.,
|\phi(n)|\leq e^{-c|n|}
for some
c>0
). However, the non-analytic (even singular) potential
\check{\phi}
which could yield a power-law decay hopping (i.e.,
|\phi(n)|\leq{|n|^{-C}}
for some
C>0
) also appears naturally in some important physical models, e.g., the quantum Fermi accelerator (cf.
[
JC86
]
). The work
[
GGW05
]
has provided numerical evidence for the localization transition in quantum chaos with the singular potential
\check{\phi}(\theta)=(|\theta|^{\alpha}\mod 1
), which induces a hopping of
|\phi(n)|\sim|n|^{-1-\alpha}
. Note that the analytic derivation of the localization in
[
GGW05
]
relies on physical perspective of localization for random operators with power-law hopping (cf.
[
Rea03
]
). However, the on-site energy sequence
\{d_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is only pseudo-random and the localization properties of
H_{x}
should depend on arithmetic properties of
\omega
. Thus, a mathematically rigorous treatment of the localization for quasi-periodic operators with (realistic) power-law hopping becomes important. We would also like to mention that certain quantum kicked rotor models in higher dimensions (cf.
[
DF88
]
) or with a quasi-periodic potential (cf.
[
CGS89
]
) will give rise to higher dimensional lattice quasi-periodic operators with tangent potential.

The main purpose of this paper is to diagonalize certain lattice quasi-periodic operators with power-law decay hopping and meromorphic monotone potentials via the Nash-Moser iteration scheme. As applications, we prove uniform power-law localization, uniform dynamical localization and Lipschitz continuity of the IDS. More precisely, consider on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
the operators

\displaystyle H_{z}=\varepsilon T_{\phi}+f(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}},\ \varepsilon\in\mathbb{R},

(1.2)

where the off-diagonal part (i.e., the hopping term)
T_{\phi}
satisfies

\displaystyle(T_{\phi}u)({\bm{n}})=\sum_{\bm{m}\neq\bm{n}}\phi({\bm{n}-\bm{m}})u({\bm{m}}),\ \phi({\bm{0}})=0,\ |\phi({\bm{n}})|\leq|\bm{n}|^{-s}

with some
s>0
and
|\bm{n}|=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq d}|n_{i}|
. In the diagonal part we assume the potential
f
is a
1
-periodic meromorphic function defined on
{D}_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ |\Im z|<R\}
satisfying the monotonicity condition (cf. (
1.4
) for details), which includes
\tan(\pi z)
and
e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}
as the special cases. We call
\bm{\omega}\in[0,1]^{d}
the frequency and
z
the phase. Typically, we assume
\bm{\omega}
satisfies the Diophantine condition, namely, there exist
\tau>d
and
\gamma>0
so that

\displaystyle\|\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}\|_{\mathbb{T}}=\inf_{l\in\mathbb{Z}}|l-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}|\geq\frac{\gamma}{|\bm{n}|^{\tau}}\ {\rm for}\ \forall\ \bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}.

Obviously, if
\varepsilon=0
, then
H_{z}
exhibits the Anderson localization for all
z
such that
z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}
is not a pole of
f
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. So it is reasonable to expect the localization of
H_{z}
for
0<|\varepsilon|\ll 1
. A potential approach is to diagonalize
H_{z}
via some unitary transformation. However, it is not totally trivial to carry out such a method since the small divisors problem, i.e.,

\displaystyle\liminf_{|\bm{n}|\to\infty}|f(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})-f(z)|=0.

In 1983 Craig
[
Cra83
]
first proved an inverse Anderson localization result for some lattice almost-periodic Schrödinger operators by using a KAM type diagonalization method. This method of Craig is to fix a diagonal operator
d_{\bm{n}}\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}
(with
d_{\bm{n}}=g(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})
for some 1-periodic function
g
) satisfying

\displaystyle|d_{\bm{m}}-d_{\bm{n}}|>\frac{\gamma}{|\bm{m}-\bm{n}|^{\tau}}\ (\bm{m}\neq\bm{n}).

(1.3)

An additional condition is imposed on
g
so that
\{d_{\bm{n}}\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is almost-periodic. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, Craig constructed a unitary transformation
Q
and some 1-periodic function
g^{\prime}
so that

\displaystyle Q^{-1}(\varepsilon\Delta+g(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}+g^{\prime}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}})Q=g(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}},

where
\Delta
denotes the lattice Laplacian. The transformation
Q
is obtained via KAM iterations. It is important that the modulation operator
g^{\prime}(\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}
is constructed so that the small-divisors
d_{\bm{n}}-d_{\bm{m}}
do not change in the iterations. However, it is much more difficult to deal with the direct problem, i.e., to diagonalize
\varepsilon\Delta+d_{\bm{n}}\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}
via some unitary transformation. In this case one has to deal with small divisors of the form

\tilde{d}_{\bm{n}}-\tilde{d}_{\bm{m}}
with
\tilde{d}_{\bm{n}}
being the modulation of
d_{\bm{n}}
. Generally, it is hard to determine whether the modulated sequence
\{\tilde{d}_{\bm{n}}\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
still satisfies the non-resonant condition (
1.3
) or not. For this reason, Bellissard-Lima-Scoppola
[
BLS83
]
provided a class of meromorphic monotone functions which is stable under small analytic function perturbations. As a result, the non-resonant condition (
1.3
) can be essentially preserved under modulations. Then by using the KAM iteration method,
[
BLS83
]
proved the Anderson localization for (
1.2
) with
\phi
being exponentially decaying. Later, Pöschel
[
Pös83
]
developed a general approach to study both inverse and direct Anderson localization in the setting of translation invariant Banach algebra. He also presented new examples of limit-periodic Schrödinger operators that exhibit Anderson localization. We want to remark that the methods of
[
Cra83
,
BLS83
,
Pös83
]
can only handle operators with exponential decay hopping. Very recently, Shi
[
Shi22
]
developed a Nash-Moser iteration type diagonalization approach to deal with almost-periodic operators with power-law decay hopping. The key new ingredients of the proof in
[
Shi22
]
consist of introducing the smoothing operation in the iteration together with establishing the tame estimates for some operator norms induced by the power-law hopping. Consequently, Shi
[
Shi22
]
generalized the results of Pöschel
[
Pös83
]
to the power-law hopping case. Finally, we also refer to
[
FP84
,
Sim85
,
JL17
,
JK19
,
Kac19
,
KPS22
]
for important progress on the study of lattice quasi-periodic operators with monotone type potentials.

We would like to remark that while the method of Shi
[
Shi22
]
works for more general translation invariant Banach algebras, if one applies it to the operators (
1.2
), the power-law localization of
H_{z}
only holds if
|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
for some postive
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(z)
depending on
z
. This single-phase type localization result is obviously not satisfactory and one expects uniform (in
z
) localization results as that obtained in
[
BLS83
]
. In addition, if we want to investigate the dynamical localization and the regularity of the IDS, a uniform in
z
estimate of
\varepsilon_{0}
is required. In this paper we combine the Nash-Moser iteration diagonalization method of Shi
[
Shi22
]
with the meromorphic monotone function estimates of Bellissard-Lima-Scoppola
[
BLS83
]
to establish uniform power-law localization, uniform dynamical localization and Lipschitz continuity of the IDS for (
1.2
). One of the new ingredients of our proof is to introduce a norm which takes account of both
z\in{D}_{R}
and
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
directions. This new norm is actually the
\ell^{1}
-type one rather than the
\ell^{2}
-one as that in
[
Shi22
]
, which will lead to a significant simplification of the proof.

1.1.
Main results

We present our results in the language of holomorphic kernels algebra introduced by Bellissard-Lima-Scoppola
[
BLS83
]
.

We first introduce the function class of potentials. For
R>0
,
\mathscr{H}_{R}
denotes the set of period
1
holomorphic bounded functions on
{D}_{R}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ \left|\Im z\right|<R\}
,
equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{R}=\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}|f(z)|
.
Then
\mathscr{P}_{R}
denotes the set of period
1
meromorphic functions
g
on
{D}_{R}
such that, there is a constant
c>0
satisfying

\displaystyle\left|g(z)-g(z-a)\right|\geq c\|a\|_{\mathbb{T}}\ {\rm for}\ \forall\ a\in\mathbb{R}\ {\rm and}\ \forall\ z\in{D}_{R}

(1.4)

with
\|a\|_{\mathbb{T}}=\underset{k\in\mathbb{Z}}{\inf}|a-k|.

Then
|g|_{R}
is defined as the biggest possible value of
c
in (
1.4
). Let
\mathscr{P}=\underset{R>0}{\bigcup}{\mathscr{P}}_{R}
. We remark that both
g_{1}(z)=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}
(cf.
[
Sar82
]
) and
g_{2}(z)=\tan(\pi z)
are in
\mathscr{P}
(cf.
[
BLS83
]
for details).

We then consider the holomorphic kernels constituting the hopping operators. Let

\displaystyle\bm{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\cdots,\omega_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \bm{n}=(n_{1},\cdots,n_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}.

Denote
\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}n_{i}\omega_{i}
and
|\bm{n}|=\underset{1\leq i\leq d}{\max}|n_{i}|
. For
R>0
and
s\geq 0
, denote by
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}
the set of kernels
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}(z,\bm{n}))_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},z\in{D}_{R}}
with
\mathcal{M}(z,\bm{n})\in\mathscr{H}_{R}
for each
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}=\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}(z,\bm{n})|\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{s}<\infty,\ \langle\bm{n}\rangle=\max\{1,|\bm{n}|\}.

(1.5)

Then
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}
is a Banach space. Giving
\bm{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define an algebraic structure with respect to (w.r.t)
\bm{\omega}
by
2
2

2

This definition is also valid for elements that are not in
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}
, e.g.,
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{M}(z,\bm{n}))_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d},z\in{D}_{R}}
with
\mathcal{M}(z,\bm{n})\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
for each
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2})(z,\bm{n})=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{1}(z,\bm{l})\mathcal{M}_{2}(z-\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{n}-\bm{l}).

(1.6)

An involution w.r.t
\bm{\omega}
is given by

\displaystyle(\mathcal{M}^{*})(z,\bm{n})=\overline{\mathcal{M}(\bar{z}-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},-\bm{n})}.

(1.7)

By (
1.5
) and (
1.7
), we have
(\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2})^{*}=\mathcal{M}_{2}^{*}\mathcal{M}_{1}^{*}
and
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}=\|\mathcal{M}^{*}\|_{R,s}
. If
f
is defined in some domain of
\mathbb{C}
, we also write
f^{*}(z)=\bar{f}(\bar{z})
. Then
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}
with above two structures w.r.t
\bm{\omega}
denotes
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
.

Remark 1.1
.

We remark that

(i).

If
f:D_{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, then
f
can be considered as a diagonal kernel by defining

{f}(z,\bm{n})\equiv f(z)\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{0}}
. So if
V\in\mathscr{P}
, we can still define the product
V\mathcal{M}
via (
1.6
) for
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
. We say
V\in\mathscr{P}
is self-adjoint if
V^{*}=V.

(ii).

If
\bm{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathcal{U}_{\bm{e}}
is the kernel
\mathcal{U}_{\bm{e}}(z,\bm{n})=\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{e}},

and the Laplace kernel is then given by
\bm{\delta}=\sum\limits_{|\bm{e}|_{1}=1}\mathcal{U}_{\bm{e}},
where
|\bm{e}|_{1}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d}|e_{i}|
.

(iii).

The unit in
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
is denoted by
\bm{1}=\mathcal{U}_{\bm{0}}
. Then
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
is called unitary (resp. self-adjoint) if
\mathcal{M}\mathcal{M}^{*}=\mathcal{M}^{*}\mathcal{M}=\bm{1}
(resp.
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}^{*}
).

Finally, we define the Diophantine condition. Fix
\gamma>0
and
\tau>d
. We say
\bm{\omega}\in[0,1]^{d}
satisfies the
(\tau,\gamma)
-Diophantine condition, if

\displaystyle\|\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}\|_{\mathbb{T}}\geq\frac{\gamma}{\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{\tau}}\ {\rm for}\ \forall\ \bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}.

Denote by
{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}
the set of all
(\tau,\gamma)
-Diophantine frequencies.

Throughout this paper we always assume
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}.
This assumption is reasonable since the long-range hopping in our model has a power-law off-diagonal decay. More precisely, since we use a KAM type perturbation method, imposing Diophantine condition on the frequency allows a controllable loss of derivatives (cf. Lemma
3.3
in this paper) when solving the homological equations. Without this assumption, we can not have effective estimates on the solutions of homological equations, and the iteration scheme will become invalid. In contrast, in the work of
[
Cra83
,
Pös83
]
the Diophantine frequency can be relaxed to the Bruno-Rüssmann one
3
3

3

For example, this condition includes the case that the power-law bound in the Diophantine condition is replaced by a sub-exponential one.
since the hopping operators in
[
Cra83
,
Pös83
]
are of exponential off-diagonal decay. However, if the power-law lower bound in the Diophantine condition is replaced by an exponential one (i.e., the Liouville frequency case), to the best of our knowledge, there is simply no localization results for quasi-periodic operators on
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

We are able to state our main results.

1.1.1.
Diagonalization

We first introduce the diagonalization theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}
. Fix
\delta>0
,
\alpha_{0}>0
and
R>0
. Let

\displaystyle V\in\mathscr{P}_{R},\ \mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,\alpha+3\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}.

Then for

\displaystyle\alpha>\alpha_{0}+\tau+4\delta,

there is some
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R,\alpha,\alpha_{0},\gamma,\tau,|V|_{R},\delta)>0
such that the following holds true. If
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,\alpha+3\delta}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then there exist an invertible element
\mathcal{U}\in\mathscr{U}_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}
and some
\hat{V}\in\mathscr{P}_{\frac{R}{2}}
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(V+\mathcal{M})\mathcal{U}^{-1}=\hat{V},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}^{\pm 1}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\leq K_{1}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,\alpha+3\delta}^{\frac{3\delta}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}},

(1.9)

\displaystyle V-\hat{V}\in\mathscr{H}_{\frac{R}{2}},\ |\hat{V}|_{\frac{R}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|V|_{R},

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|V-\hat{V}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}\leq K_{2}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,\alpha+3\delta},

where
K_{1}>0
and
K_{2}>0
only depend on
\alpha_{0},\alpha,\delta
.
Moreover, if both
\mathcal{M}
and
V
are self-adjoint, then
\mathcal{U}
is unitary and
\hat{V}^{*}=\hat{V}
.

Remark 1.2
.

In this theorem it suffices to assume
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,\alpha}^{\bm{\omega}}
for
\alpha>\tau
since
\delta>0
and
\alpha_{0}>0
can be arbitrarily small.

1.1.2.
Power-law Localization

Now we can apply the above theorem to obtain the (uniform) power-law localization.

A representation of
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
in
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
is given by

\displaystyle\left(T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\psi\right)(\bm{n})=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})\psi(\bm{l}),

(1.11)

where
\psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
z\in{D}_{R}
.

Fix
V\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
. Define for
0\leq R^{\prime}\leq R
the set

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{R^{\prime}}=\bigcap_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ |\Im z|\leq R^{\prime}\ {\rm and}\ z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega}{\rm\ is\ not\ a\ pole\ of}\ V\}.

(1.12)

Since
V
is meromorphic, the set
D_{R}\setminus\mathcal{Z}_{R}
is at most countable. For
V\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
and
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{R}
, denote by
T_{V}(z)=V(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}
the multiplication operator.

Then we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}
. Fix
\delta>0
,
\alpha_{0}>0
and
R>0
. Let
V\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
and
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
with

\displaystyle s>\alpha_{0}+\tau+\frac{d}{2}+7\delta.

Then there is some
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R,\alpha_{0},\gamma,\tau,|V|_{R},\delta,s,d)>0
such that the following holds true. If
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then the operator
H_{z}=T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)+T_{V}(z)
has a complete set of eigenfunctions
\{\varphi_{\bm{n}}\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
obeying
|\varphi_{\bm{n}}(\bm{i})|\leq 2\langle\bm{n}-\bm{i}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}
for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{{R}/{2}}
.
In addition, if both
\mathcal{M}
and
V
are self-adjoint, then
H_{x}
is self-adjoint and its spectrum is equal to
\mathbb{R}
for
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}
.

Remark 1.3
.

We first mention that the perturbation strength
\varepsilon_{0}
is independent of
x
for
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}.

Remark 1.4
.

We have explicit descriptions of localization centers. Moreover, we establish in fact the uniform (power-law) localization (cf.
[
Jit97
]
for the definition of uniform localization).

Remark 1.5
.

Let
H_{x}=\varepsilon T_{\phi}+\tan\pi(x-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\delta_{\bm{n}\bm{n}^{\prime}}
for some sequence
\phi=\{\phi(\bm{n})\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}

satisfying
\phi(\bm{0})=0
, and
|\phi(\bm{n})|\leq|\bm{n}|^{-s}
for
\bm{n}\neq\bm{0}
. If
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma},\ s>d+\tau
and
|\varepsilon|\leq\varepsilon_{0}(s,\tau,\gamma,d)>0
, then
H_{x}
has uniform power-law localization for all
x\not\in\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}+\bm{\omega}\cdot\mathbb{Z}^{d}.
This extends the perturbative results of
[
BLS83
]
to the power-law hopping case.

1.1.3.
Uniform dynamical localization

In this section we apply our diagonalization theorem to study dynamical localization.
For
\psi\in\mathbb{C}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
and
s\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi(\bm{n})|^{2}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{2s}.

Let
\ell^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
denote the set of all
\psi=\{\psi(\bm{n})\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
satisfying
\|\psi\|_{s}<\infty
.

Given the family
(H_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{T}}
of self-adjoint operators defined on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we are interested in the estimate of

\displaystyle\|e^{-\sqrt{-1}tH_{x}}\psi\|_{q}\ {\rm for}\ \psi\in\ell^{2}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

We have

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}
. Fix
\delta>0
,
\alpha_{0}>0
,
R>0
and
q\geq 0
. Let both
V\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
and
\mathcal{M}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
be self-adjoint. Assume

\displaystyle s>\alpha_{0}+\tau+q+\frac{d}{2}+7\delta.

Then there is some
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(R,\alpha_{0},\gamma,\tau,|V|_{R},\delta,s,d,q)>0
such that the following holds true. If
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}<\varepsilon_{0}
, then for
\forall\ \psi\in\ell^{2}_{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|e^{-\sqrt{-1}tH_{x}}\psi\|_{q}<\infty,

where

3.
The Nash-Moser iteration

In this section we will prove a Nash-Moser iteration theorem. The main strategy is based on the iteration scheme outlined in
[
Shi22
]
combined with meromorphic function estimates of
[
BLS83
]
. The final transformation
\mathcal{U}
will be obtained as the limit of the product
\mathcal{U}_{l}=\prod\limits_{i=l}^{0}e^{\mathcal{W}_{i}}
with a sequence of transformations
{\mathcal{W}_{i}}
(
0\leq i\leq l
). More precisely, at the
l
-th iteration step we will find
\mathcal{W}_{l}\in\mathscr{U}_{R_{l},s}^{\bm{\omega}}
,
V_{l}\in\mathscr{P}_{R_{l}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{l}\in\mathscr{U}_{R_{l},s}^{\bm{\omega}}
so that

\displaystyle\prod_{i=l}^{0}e^{\mathcal{W}_{i}}\left(V+\sum_{i=0}^{l-1}\mathcal{M}^{(i)}\right)\prod_{i=0}^{l}e^{-\mathcal{W}_{i}}=V_{l}+\mathcal{R}_{l}

and
\|\mathcal{R}_{l}\|_{R_{l},s}=o(\|\mathcal{R}_{l-1}\|_{R_{l-1},s})
, where
\mathcal{M}^{(l)}
is the
l
-section of
\mathcal{M}
w.r.t
\{\theta_{l}=\theta_{0}\Theta^{l}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
with
\theta_{0}
,
\Theta>1
being specified later. The sequence
\{R_{l}\}_{l=0}^{\infty}
satisfies
R_{l}\searrow R_{\infty}\geq R_{0}/2.

To clarify the iteration scheme, we set

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{0}=\mathcal{W}_{0}=\mathcal{M}_{-1}=0,\ V_{0}=V_{-1}=V,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{l}=\mathcal{U}_{l}\mathcal{M}^{(l)}\mathcal{U}_{l}^{-1}+\mathcal{R}_{l}.

Equivalently, at the
l
-th iteration step we aim to find
\mathcal{W}_{l}\in\mathscr{U}_{R_{l},s}^{\bm{\omega}}
,
V_{l}\in\mathscr{P}_{R_{l}}
and
\mathcal{R}_{l}\in\mathscr{U}_{R_{l},s}^{\bm{\omega}}
so that

\displaystyle e^{\mathcal{W}_{l}}\left(V_{l-1}+\mathcal{M}_{l-1}\right)e^{-\mathcal{W}_{l}}=V_{l}+\mathcal{R}_{l},

with
\|\mathcal{R}_{l}\|_{R_{l},s}=o(\|\mathcal{R}_{l-1}\|_{R_{l-1},s})
. For this purpose, it needs to eliminate terms of order
O(\|\mathcal{R}_{l-1}\|_{R_{l-1},s})
, which leads to solvling the following homological equations

\displaystyle V_{l}(z)=V_{l-1}(z)+\mathcal{M}_{l-1}(z,\bm{0}),\ \mathcal{W}_{l}(z,\bm{0})=0,

\displaystyle(\mathcal{W}_{l}V_{l}-V_{l}\mathcal{W}_{l})(z,\bm{n})=-(S_{\theta_{l}}\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l-1})(z,\bm{n})\ {\rm for}\ \bm{n}\neq\bm{0},

where
\tilde{\mathcal{M}}_{l-1}=(I-S_{0})\mathcal{M}_{l-1}
.

To solve the homological equations, we have to address the following issues. First, the non-resonant property of
V_{l}
should be preserved, which requires the use of quantitative version of meromorphic monotone function estimates established by Bellissard-Lima-Scoppola
[
BLS83
]
. Second, since in the iteration steps we need to estimate the
\|\cdot\|_{R,s}
norm of products of elements in
\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
, the tame property of the norm (cf. (
2.1
)) plays an essential role.

This section is then organized as follows. We first provide some useful estimates in §3.1. The Nash-Moser iteration theorem is then proved in §3.2.

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},s}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\theta_{l}^{-3\delta}\leq C.

Then for
\forall\ \varepsilon>0
, there is
N(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
so that for all
n\geq N
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{4(1+C)K(\alpha)e^{K(\alpha)C}},

\displaystyle\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{4(1+C)K(\alpha)e^{K(\alpha)C}}.

Using (
3.28
) implies

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+p+1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{K(\alpha-\tau-4\delta)\sum\limits_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq e^{K(\alpha)\sum\limits_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}

\displaystyle\leq 1+e^{K(\alpha)\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n-1}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}.

As a result, we have

\displaystyle\ \ \ \|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+p+1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}

\displaystyle\leq e^{K(\alpha)\sum\limits_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\ \ +e^{K(\alpha)\sum\limits_{l=0}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}\left(\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}\right)\left(\sum_{l=n}^{n+p}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2K(\alpha)}.

Similarly, we get

\displaystyle\|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+p+1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2K(\alpha)}.

From (
3.11
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}_{n+p+1}-\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq K(\alpha-\tau-4\delta)\|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+p+1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}

\displaystyle\ \ +K(\alpha-\tau-4\delta)\|e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+p+1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{W}_{n+1}}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},0}\|\mathcal{U}_{n}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq K(\alpha)\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2K(\alpha)}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2K(\alpha)}\right)=\varepsilon,

which implies the product
\mathcal{U}_{l+1}
converges to some
\mathcal{U}\in\mathscr{U}_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}
as
\lambda\rightarrow\infty
. Similarly, one can show
\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\|\mathcal{U}_{l+1}^{-1}-\mathcal{U}^{-1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}=0
. In addition, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}^{\pm 1}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\right)e^{K(\alpha)\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\|\mathcal{W}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},0}}

\displaystyle\leq Ce^{K(\alpha)C}\theta_{0}^{-3\delta}=K_{1}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,\alpha+3\delta}^{\frac{3\delta}{\alpha-\alpha_{0}}},

where
K_{1}=K_{1}(\alpha_{0},\alpha,\delta)=Ce^{K(\alpha)C}
.

Next, considering
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{l}\mathcal{M}^{(j)}
, we have
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{l}\mathcal{M}^{(j)}=S_{\theta_{l}}\mathcal{M},

which implies

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}-\sum_{j=0}^{l}\mathcal{M}^{(j)}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle=\|(I-S_{\theta_{l}}\mathcal{M})\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}\leq\theta_{l}^{-\tau-7\delta}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{{R},\alpha+3\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{l}^{-\tau-7\delta}\rightarrow 0\ \text{(as $l\rightarrow\infty$)}.

Obviously, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{R}_{l+1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{R}_{l+1}\|_{R_{l+1},\alpha-\tau-4\delta}

\displaystyle\leq\theta_{l+1}^{-\tau-4\delta}\rightarrow 0\ \text{(as $l\rightarrow\infty$)}.

This finishes the proof of convergence of the iteration scheme.
Moreover, from (
3.32
), we have
|\hat{V}|_{\frac{R}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|V|_{R}.

The remaining is to show if both
V
and
\mathcal{M}
are self-adjoint, then
\mathcal{U}
can be improved to become unitary and
\hat{V}^{*}=\hat{V}
. Suppose now

\displaystyle V^{*}=V,\ \mathcal{M}^{*}=\mathcal{M}.

From Theorem
3.8
, we show
\mathcal{U}_{l+1}
is unitary and
V_{l}^{*}=V_{l}
for each
l\geq 0
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}^{*}\mathcal{U}=\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{U}_{l+1}^{*}\mathcal{U}_{l+1}=\bm{1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}^{*}=\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{U}_{l+1}\mathcal{U}_{l+1}^{*}=\bm{1},

\displaystyle\hat{V}^{*}=\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}V_{l}^{*}=\lim\limits_{l\rightarrow\infty}V_{l}=\hat{V},

which implies
\mathcal{U}
is unitary and
\hat{V}^{*}=\hat{V}
.

This completes the whole proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section we will prove the power-law localization (cf. Theorem
1.2
) by using Theorem
1.1
.

We begin with a useful lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For any
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}_{1},\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
and
z\in{D}_{R}
, we have

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(z)

\displaystyle=\left(T_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{z})\right)^{*},

(5.1)

\displaystyle T_{\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2}}(z)

\displaystyle=T_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}(z)T_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}(z).

(5.2)

If in addition
s>q+\frac{d}{2}
, then

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\|_{q}\leq X(s,q)\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s},

(5.3)

where

\displaystyle X(s,q)=\sqrt{K(2q)(Y^{2}(s)+Y^{2}(s-q))}>0,

(5.4)

K(s)
is given by (
3.9
),
Y(s)=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}}
and the norm of
T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)
denotes the standard operator norm on
\ell_{q}^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

Proof.

Let
(\cdot,\cdot)
denote the standard inner product on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
.

First, for
\forall\ \psi
and
\varphi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\left(T_{\mathcal{M}^{*}}(z)\psi,\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}^{*}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})\psi(\bm{l})\overline{\varphi(\bm{n})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\psi(\bm{l})\overline{\mathcal{M}(\bar{z}-\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{n}-\bm{l})\varphi(\bm{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(\psi,T_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{z})\varphi\right)=\left(\left(T_{\mathcal{M}}(\bar{z})\right)^{*}\psi,\varphi\right),

which shows (
5.1
).

Next, for
\forall\ \psi\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
\forall\ \bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2}}(z)\psi\right)(\bm{n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{1}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{k})\mathcal{M}_{2}(z-(\bm{n}+\bm{k})\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-(\bm{n}+\bm{k}))\psi(\bm{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{1}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{k})\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}\psi\right)(\bm{n}+\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}_{1}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{k}-\bm{n})\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}\psi\right)(\bm{k})

\displaystyle=\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}\psi\right)\right)(\bm{n})=\left(\left(T_{\mathcal{M}_{1}}T_{\mathcal{M}_{2}}\right)\psi\right)(\bm{n}),

which implies (
5.2
).

If in addition
s>q+\frac{d}{2}
for
\forall\ \psi\in\ell_{q}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
, we get

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\psi\|^{2}_{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left|\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})\psi(\bm{l})\right|^{2}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{2q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})||\psi(\bm{l})|\right)^{2}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{2q}.

Recalling (
1.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})|

\displaystyle\leq\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-s}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})||\psi(\bm{l})|\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})|\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\ \ \times\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{n})|\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-s}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{2}\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\psi\|^{2}_{q}\leq\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{2}\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{2q}\right).

According to (
3.8
), we have

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{2q}\right)

\displaystyle\leq K(2q)\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\left(\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\left(\langle\bm{l}\rangle^{2q}+\langle\bm{l}-\bm{n}\rangle^{2q}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=K(2q)\left(\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{-2s}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\langle\bm{l}\rangle^{2q}+\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\bm{n}\rangle^{-(2s-2q)}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\psi(\bm{l})|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=K(2q)(Y^{2}(s)\|\psi\|_{q}^{2}+Y^{2}(s-q)\|\psi\|_{0}^{2})

\displaystyle\leq K(2q)(Y^{2}(s)+Y^{2}(s-q))\|\psi\|_{q}^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\psi\|^{2}_{q}\leq\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{2}K(2q)(Y^{2}(s)+Y^{2}(s-q))\|\psi\|_{q}^{2},

that is

\displaystyle\|T_{\mathcal{M}}(z)\|_{q}\leq X(s,q)\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}.

∎

We can now prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We apply Theorem
1.1
with

\displaystyle\alpha=s-3\delta.

(5.5)

Since (
5.5
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}=\mathscr{U}_{R,\alpha+3\delta}^{\bm{\omega}},

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=s-3\delta>\alpha_{0}+\tau+4\delta+\frac{d}{2}>\alpha_{0}+\tau+4\delta.

Hence applying Theorem
1.1
implies that if
\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}\ll 1
, there are
\mathcal{U}\in\mathscr{U}_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}
and
\hat{V}\in\mathscr{P}_{\frac{R}{2}}
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(V+\mathcal{M})\mathcal{U}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\hat{V},

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{U}^{\pm 1}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{\frac{3\delta}{s-\alpha_{0}-3\delta}}.

Letting
U_{z}=T_{\mathcal{U}}(z)
, according to (
5.3
) and
s-\tau-7\delta>\frac{d}{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|U_{z}^{\pm 1}\|_{0}

\displaystyle\leq 1+\|U_{z}^{\pm 1}-I\|_{0}\leq 1+X(s-\tau-7\delta,0)\|\mathcal{U}^{\pm 1}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}

where
X(s-\tau-7\delta,0)
and
K_{1}
are given by (
5.4
) and (
1.9
) respectively. Therefore
U_{z}
is a bounded invertible operator. By (
5.2
), we get for
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{{R}/{2}}
(cf. (
1.12
)),

\displaystyle U_{z}H_{z}U_{z}^{-1}=T_{\hat{V}}(z).

Note that
T_{\hat{V}}(z)
is a diagonal operator for
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{R/2}
. Then the standard basis
\{\delta_{\bm{n}}\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
of
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})

with eigenvalues
\{\hat{V}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
for
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{R/2}
. Letting
\varphi_{\bm{n}}=U_{z}^{-1}\delta_{\bm{n}}

\displaystyle H_{z}\varphi_{\bm{n}}=\hat{V}(z-\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\omega})\varphi_{\bm{n}},

which combined with the boundedness of

will imply
\{\varphi_{\bm{n}}\}_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
is a
complete set of eigenfunctions
of
H_{z}
for
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{R/2}
. In fact, we have

\displaystyle\varphi_{\bm{n}}(\bm{i})=\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathcal{U}^{-1}(z-\bm{i}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{l}-\bm{i})\delta_{\bm{n}}(\bm{l})=\mathcal{U}^{-1}(z-\bm{i}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{n}-\bm{i}).

, we obtain

\displaystyle|\varphi_{\bm{n}}(\bm{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{U}^{-1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}\langle\bm{n}-\bm{i}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\|\mathcal{U}^{-1}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta})\langle\bm{n}-\bm{i}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}

\displaystyle\leq(1+K_{1}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{\frac{3\delta}{s-\alpha_{0}-3\delta}})\langle\bm{n}-\bm{i}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}

\displaystyle\leq 2\langle\bm{n}-\bm{i}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}

provided

\varphi_{\bm{n}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d}).

We then prove the

. Suppose for all
\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
(\psi,\varphi_{\bm{n}})=0

\psi=0

\forall\ \bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}

\displaystyle 0=(\psi,\varphi_{\bm{n}})=\psi(\bm{n})+(\psi,(U_{z}^{-1}-I)\delta_{\bm{n}}),

5.3
) and

\displaystyle\|T_{(\mathcal{U}^{-1}-\bm{1})^{*}}(\bar{z})\psi\|^{2}_{0}

of

and

\mathcal{U}
is unitary and

) and (

), we have for

\displaystyle U_{x}(U_{x})^{*}

\displaystyle=T_{\mathcal{U}}(x)T_{\mathcal{U}^{*}}(\bar{x})=T_{\mathcal{U}\mathcal{U}^{*}}(x)=I,

which implies

is a self-adjoint operator and

is equal to that of

. At this stage, we recall a result proven in

]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
g\in\mathscr{P}_{R}
,
g^{*}=g
, then there is a unique

such that the real poles of

are

\displaystyle\sigma(T_{\hat{V}}(x))=\mathbb{R},

Consequently, we obtain
\sigma(H_{x})=\mathbb{R}
for

.

This proves Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

1.1
, there exist
\mathcal{U}\in\mathscr{U}_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}
and
\hat{V}\in\mathscr{P}_{\frac{R}{2}}

7.
Proof of Theorem

In this section we prove Lipschitz continuity of the IDS by using Theorem
1.1
.
We first prove the invariance of IDS under unitary transformation that is also nearly identical.

For any self-adjoint operator
H
defined on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and
E\in\mathbb{R}
, let

\kappa_{H}(E)=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{(2L+1)^{d}}\text{tr}(\chi_{L}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(H)).

We have

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
H
be a self-adjoint operator on
\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
and let
UHU^{-1}=D
, where
U
is unitary and
D
is diagonal. Assume that the matrix elements
u_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}=(\delta_{\bm{i}},U\delta_{\bm{j}})
of
U
satisfy

\displaystyle|u_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}-\delta_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}|\leq c_{1}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{j}\rangle^{-r},

where
r>d
and
c_{1}>0
. Then

\displaystyle\kappa_{H}(E)=\kappa_{D}(E)

if one of them exists.

Proof.

By
UHU^{-1}=D
, we have

\displaystyle\text{tr}(\chi_{L}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(H))

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\chi_{L}U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D)U)

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(U\chi_{L}U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D))

\displaystyle=\text{tr}(\chi_{L}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D)+(U\chi_{L}-\chi_{L}U)U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D)).

Direct computations yield

\displaystyle(U\chi_{L}-\chi_{L}U)_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|>L\ \text{and}\ |\bm{j}|>L,\\
0&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|\leq L\ \text{and}\ |\bm{j}|\leq L,\\
-u_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|\leq L\ \text{and}\ |\bm{j}|>L,\\
u_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|>L\ \text{and}\ |\bm{j}|\leq L.\end{array}\right.

Denote
(U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D))_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}=c_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}
. Then
\sup_{\bm{i},\bm{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|c_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}|\leq c_{2}
for some
c_{2}>0
. Note also that

\displaystyle|((U\chi_{L}-\chi_{L}U)U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D))_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\sum\limits_{|\bm{k}|>L}|u_{\bm{i}\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\bm{j}}|&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|\leq L,\\
\sum\limits_{|\bm{k}|\leq L}|u_{\bm{i}\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}\bm{j}}|&\text{if}\ |\bm{i}|>L.\end{array}\right.

As a result, the decay property of
u_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}
allows us to obtain

\displaystyle|\text{tr}((U\chi_{L}-\chi_{L}U)U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D))|

\displaystyle\leq c_{2}\sum_{|\bm{i}|\leq L}\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}|u_{\bm{i}\bm{k}}|+c_{2}\sum_{|\bm{i}|>L}\sum_{|\bm{k}|\leq L}|u_{\bm{i}\bm{k}}|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{1}c_{2}\sum_{|\bm{i}|\leq L}\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}.

To control the above sum, we have the following two cases.

Case 1.

|\bm{i}|<L-\sqrt{L}
. In this case
\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle\geq\sqrt{L}+1
for
|\bm{k}|>L
.
Note that for any
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
t\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\#\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ \langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle=t,|\bm{k}|>L\}\leq(2t+1)^{d}-(2t-1)^{d}\leq 2d(3t)^{d-1}.

Hence, for a fixed
\bm{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
|\bm{i}|<L-\sqrt{L}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle={\sum_{t\geq\sqrt{L}+1}}\sum_{\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle=t,|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle={\sum_{t\geq\sqrt{L}+1}}\frac{\#\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ \langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle=t,|\bm{k}|>L\}}{t^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq{\sum_{t\geq\sqrt{L}+1}}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{r-d+1}}.

Since
r>d
and fixing any
\eta\in(0,r-d)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\bm{i}|\leq L-\sqrt{L}}\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle\leq\#\{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ |\bm{i}|\leq L\}{\sum_{t\geq\sqrt{L}+1}}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{t-d+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2L+1)^{d}}{\sqrt{L}^{\eta}}{\sum_{t\geq\sqrt{L}+1}}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{r-d-\eta+1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{(2L+1)^{d}}{\sqrt{L}^{\eta}}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{r-d-\eta+1}}=O(L^{d-\eta/2}).

Case 2.

L-\sqrt{L}\leq|\bm{i}|\leq L
. In this case we have

\displaystyle\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\sum_{\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle=t,|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{\#\{\bm{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ \langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle=t,|\bm{k}|>L\}}{t^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{r-d+1}}.

Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{L-\sqrt{L}\leq|\bm{i}|\leq L}\sum_{|\bm{k}|>L}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{k}\rangle^{-r}

\displaystyle\leq\#\{\bm{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\ L-\sqrt{L}\leq|\bm{i}|\leq L\}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\frac{2d3^{d-1}}{t^{t-d+1}}=O(L^{d-1/2}).

So combining
Case 1
and
Case 2
implies

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{L\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{(2L+1)^{d}}\text{tr}((U\chi_{L}-\chi_{L}U)U^{-1}\mathbb{P}_{(-\infty,E]}(D))=0,

which concludes the proof of this lemma.

∎

We are ready to prove Theorem
1.4
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

From Theorem
1.1
, there exist
\mathcal{U}\in\mathscr{U}_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}^{\bm{\omega}}
and
\hat{V}\in\mathscr{P}_{\frac{R}{2}}
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(V+\mathcal{M})\mathcal{U}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\hat{V},

\displaystyle U_{x}H_{x}U_{x}^{-1}

\displaystyle=T_{\hat{V}}(x),

where
U_{x}
is unitary and
T_{\hat{V}}(x)
is diagonal for
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{0}
. Recalling (
1.9
), we obtain

\displaystyle|(U_{x})_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}-\delta_{\bm{i}\bm{j}}|

\displaystyle=|(\mathcal{U}-\bm{1})(x-\bm{i}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{j}-\bm{i})|

\displaystyle\leq\|\mathcal{U}-\bm{1}\|_{\frac{R}{2},s-\tau-7\delta}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{j}\rangle^{-s+\tau+7\delta}

\displaystyle\leq K_{1}\|\mathcal{M}\|_{R,s}^{\frac{3\delta}{s-\alpha_{0}-3\delta}}\langle\bm{i}-\bm{j}\rangle^{-(s-\tau-7\delta)},

where
s-\tau-7\delta>d
. Denote by
\kappa_{0}(E)
the IDS of
T_{\hat{V}}(x)
. From Lemma
7.1
and
\bm{\omega}\in{\rm DC}_{\tau,\gamma}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\kappa(E)=\kappa_{0}(E)={\rm mes}\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}:\ \hat{V}(\theta)\leq E\},

(7.1)

where
{\rm mes}(\cdot)
denotes the Lebesgue measure.
Recalling Lemma
5.2
and
\hat{V}^{*}=\hat{V}
, we assume
\hat{V}
is non-decreasing in
(0,1)
without loss of generality. Then
\hat{V}
has an inverse function defined on
\mathbb{R}
which is denoted by
\hat{V}^{-1}
. Since
\hat{V}\in\mathscr{P}_{\frac{R}{2}}
and (
1.10
), we have

\displaystyle\inf_{x\in(0,1)}\frac{d\hat{V}(x)}{dx}\geq|\hat{V}|_{\frac{R}{2}}\geq\frac{1}{2}|V|_{R}>0,

which combined with (
7.1
) implies for
E_{1}<E_{2}
,

\displaystyle\left|\kappa(E_{2})-\kappa(E_{1})\right|

\displaystyle={\rm mes}\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}:\ E_{1}<\hat{V}(\theta)\leq E_{2}\}

\displaystyle={\rm mes}\{\theta\in\mathbb{T}:\ \hat{V}^{-1}(E_{1})<\theta\leq\hat{V}^{-1}(E_{2})\}

\displaystyle=\hat{V}^{-1}(E_{2})-\hat{V}^{-1}(E_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{|V|_{R}}(E_{2}-E_{1}).

This proves Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Appendix A

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Recalling (
1.5
) and (
3.8
), we have for any
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}\in\mathscr{U}_{R,s}^{\bm{\omega}}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{M}_{1}\mathcal{M}_{2}\|_{R,s}\leq

\displaystyle\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{1}(z,\bm{l})||\mathcal{M}_{2}(z-\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{n}-\bm{l})|\left(\langle\bm{l}\rangle+\langle\bm{n}-\bm{l}\rangle\right)^{s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(s)\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{\bm{n}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{1}(z,\bm{l})||\mathcal{M}_{2}(z-\bm{l}\cdot\bm{\omega},\bm{n}-\bm{l})|\left(\langle\bm{l}\rangle^{s}+\langle\bm{n}-\bm{l}\rangle^{s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(s)\left(\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{1}(z,\bm{l})|\langle\bm{l}\rangle^{s}\|\mathcal{M}_{2}\|_{R,0}+\underset{z\in{D}_{R}}{\sup}\sum_{\bm{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}|\mathcal{M}_{1}(z,\bm{l})|\|\mathcal{M}_{2}\|_{R,s}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle K(s)\left(\|\mathcal{M}_{1}\|_{R,s}\|\mathcal{M}_{2}\|_{R,0}+\|\mathcal{M}_{1}\|_{R,0}\|\mathcal{M}_{2}\|_{R,s}\right),

which implies Lemma
2.1
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Recalling (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{N}_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{N}_{k}^{l_{k}}\|_{R^{\prime},s}\leq(K(s))^{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}-1}\sum_{m=1}^{k}l_{m}\left(\prod_{j\neq m}\|\mathcal{N}_{j}\|_{R^{\prime},0}^{l_{j}}\right)\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},0}^{l_{m}-1}\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},s}.

which implies

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \left\|e^{\mathcal{N}_{1}}\cdots e^{\mathcal{N}_{k}}-\bm{1}\right\|_{R^{\prime},s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=l}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots\mathcal{N}_{k}^{l_{k}}}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{k}!}\right\|_{R^{\prime},s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}(K(s))^{l-1}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=l}\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{l_{m}\left(\prod_{j\neq m}\|\mathcal{N}_{j}\|_{R^{\prime},0}^{l_{j}}\right)\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},0}^{l_{m}-1}\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},s}}{l_{1}!\cdots l_{k}!}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\frac{(K(s))^{l-1}}{(l-1)!}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{N}_{j}\|_{R^{\prime},0}\right)^{l-1}\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},s}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{K(s)\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},0}\right)}\left(\sum\limits_{m=1}^{k}\|\mathcal{N}_{m}\|_{R^{\prime},s}\right).

∎

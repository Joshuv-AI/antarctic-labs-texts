Positivity and maximum principle preserving discontinuous Galerkin finite element schemes for a coupled flow and transport
Shihua Gong, Young-Ju Lee, Yukun Li, Yue Yu

Abstract
We introduce a new concept of the locally conservative flux and investigate its relationship with the compatible discretization pioneered by Dawson, Sun and Wheeler [ 11 ] . We then demonstrate how the new concept of the locally conservative flux can play a crucial role in obtaining the L 2 L^{2} norm stability of the discontinuous Galerkin finite element scheme for the transport in the coupled system with flow. In particular, the lowest order discontinuous Galerkin finite element for the transport is shown to inherit the positivity and maximum principle when the locally conservative flux is used, which has been elusive for many years in literature. The theoretical results established in this paper are based on the equivalence between Lesaint-Raviart discontinuous Galerkin scheme and Brezzi-Marini-Süli discontinuous Galerkin scheme for the linear hyperbolic system as well as the relationship between the Lesaint-Raviart discontinuous Galerkin scheme and the characteristic method along the streamline. Sample numerical experiments have also been performed to justify our theoretical findings.

[

The compatibility condition is a necessary condition for the flux to guarantee that the discrete system for the transports possesses the so-called zeroth-order accuracy. The zeroth-order accuracy means that the constant true solution can be recovered. We observe that the compatibility condition is basically the local conservation, which is a bit stronger than the standard local conservation. The standard local conservation refers to conservation of mass over a control volume or an element in a finite element or finite difference grid
[

14

In this paper, our concern is solely restricted to the discontinuous Galerkin finite element discretizations for both flow (pressure) and transports. For the sake of presentation, we shall denote the degree of polynomials used to approximate the pressure by
k_{p}

\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=f,\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega.

(1)

\int_{T}(\nabla\cdot{\bf U})w\,dx=\int_{T}fw\,dx,\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T),

T

is strongly conservative, then it is locally conservative of any degree. Namely, the newly proposed local conservation is a more general version of the standard local conservation
[
18

The first main result in the paper is that if the numerical flux

2.1
Continuous Maximum Principle

In this section, we shall establish the maximum principle for the continuous case. We shall see that the continuous maximum principle is strongly affected by the continuity equation:

\nabla\cdot{\bf u}=f.

(10)

First, we define a notation for a given function
\phi
as follows:

\phi_{{}_{+}}:=\max\{\phi,0\}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\phi_{{}_{-}}:=\max\{-\phi,0\}.

(11)

Secondly, we show that the following continuous weak maximum principle holds under the condition that
{\bf u}
satisfies the strong conservation.

Theorem 1
.

For the initial condition
c^{0}
, the boundary condition
c_{\rm I}
and specified source
\widetilde{c}
, we let
M=\max\{|c^{0}|,|c_{\rm I}|,|\widetilde{c}|\}
. Under the condition of the strong conservation (
3
), we can show that the solution to equations (
7
)–(
9
) satisfies
|c|\leq M
.

Proof.

By the equation (
7
), we have for
v\in H^{1}(\Omega)
,

\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}c\,v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot{\bf u}cvdx+\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla c\,v\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot(D({\bf u})\nabla c)v\,dx=\int_{\Omega}fc^{*}vdx.

(12)

Using the integration by parts and the strong conservation (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}c\,v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla c\,v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot\nabla v\,dx

(13)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(c^{*}-c)vdx+\int_{\partial\Omega}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot{\bf n}vdx.

We shall show that
c\leq M
by setting
v:=(c-M)_{{}_{+}}
in (
13
). We then observe that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla c\,v\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}{\bf u}\cdot\nabla c\,(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\nabla[(c-M)_{{}_{+}}]^{2}\cdot{\bf u}\,dx

(14)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}v^{2}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}fv^{2}dx.

Using this identity and the boundary condition
(
9
)
, the equation (
13
) can be shown to satisfy the following inequality:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|(c-M)_{{}_{+}}|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}D({\bf u})|\nabla(c-M)_{{}_{+}}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}f(c^{*}-c)vdx+\int_{\partial\Omega}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot{\bf n}\,v\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\partial\Omega}v^{2}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}fv^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\rm I}}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot{\bf n}\,v\,ds+\int_{\Gamma_{\rm O}}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot{\bf n}\,v\,ds+\int_{\Omega}f\left(c^{*}-c+\frac{1}{2}v\right)vdx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\rm I}}[(c-M)_{{}_{+}}]^{2}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\rm O}}[(c-M)_{{}_{+}}]^{2}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Gamma_{\rm I}}D({\bf u})\nabla c\cdot{\bf n}\,v\,ds-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Gamma_{\rm I}}[(c-M)_{{}_{+}}]^{2}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds+\int_{\Omega}f\left(c^{*}-c+\frac{1}{2}v\right)vdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Gamma_{\rm I}}\left(c-c_{\rm I}-\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\right)(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\,{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}f\left(c^{*}-c+\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\right)(c-M)_{{}_{+}}dx.

We shall assume
c>M
. Then, we have

c-c_{\rm I}-\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}=\frac{c}{2}+\frac{M}{2}-c_{\rm I}\geq M-c_{\rm I}\geq 0.

Now, we consider the following identity:

f\left(c^{*}-c+\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\right)(c-M)_{{}_{+}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}f\left(\tilde{c}-c+\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\right)(c-M)_{{}_{+}},\,\,\mbox{ for }f>0\\
\frac{1}{2}f(c-M)_{{}_{+}}(c-M)_{{}_{+}},\,\,\mbox{ for }f\leq 0.\end{array}\right.

This means

f\left(c^{*}-c+\frac{1}{2}(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\right)(c-M)_{{}_{+}}\leq 0.

Therefore, we conclude that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\Omega}|(c-M)_{{}_{+}}|^{2}\,dx\leq 0

(15)

and arrive that
c\leq M
if
c^{0}\leq M
. This is a contradiction to
c>M
and it proves that
c\leq M
. The proof to show
c\geq-M
can be done by setting
v:=(c+M)_{{}_{-}}
in (
13
). The rest of the process is similar and thus, we omit the details. This completes the proof that
|c|\leq M
.

\mathcal{T}_{h}
be the shape-regular triangulation by a family of partitions of

or tetrahedra/cubes in

),

and

.
Also we denote by
{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}
the set of all edges, and by
{{\mathcal{E}}^{o}_{h}}
and
{{\mathcal{E}}^{\partial}_{h}}
the collection of all interior and boundary edges, respectively.

We shall also introduce standard tools such as jumps and averages of scalar and vector valued functions across the edges of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. Let

be an interior edges shared by two elements

. We define the unit normal vectors
{\bf n}^{\pm}
on

pointing exterior to
T^{\pm}
, respectively. For a function
\phi
that is piecewise smooth on

, with
\phi^{\pm}=\phi|_{T^{\pm}}
, we define

\{\!\!\{\phi\}\!\!\}=\frac{1}{2}(\phi^{+}+\phi^{-})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad[\![\phi]\!]=\phi^{+}{\bf n}^{+}+\phi^{-}{\bf n}^{-}\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},

(16)

where
\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
is the set of interior edges
e
. For a vector-valued function
\bm{\tau}
, piecewise smooth on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
, with
\bm{\tau}^{\pm}=\bm{\tau}|_{T^{\pm}}
, we define

\{\!\!\{\bm{\tau}\}\!\!\}=\frac{1}{2}(\bm{\tau}^{+}+\bm{\tau}^{-})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad[\![\bm{\tau}]\!]=\bm{\tau}^{+}{\bf n}^{+}+\bm{\tau}^{-}{\bf n}^{-}\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}.

(17)

For a set of boundary edges
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}
, let

[\![\phi]\!]=\phi{\bf n},\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\{\!\!\{\bm{\tau}\}\!\!\}=\bm{\tau},\quad\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}.

(18)

The space
H^{s}(\mathcal{T}_{h})

(s\in\mathbb{R})
is the set of element-wise
H^{s}
functions on
\mathcal{T}_{h}
with
s\geq 1
, and
L^{2}({{\mathcal{E}}_{h}})
refers to the set of functions whose traces on the elements of
{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}
are square integrable. Let
\mathbb{P}_{k}(T)
denote the space of polynomials of degree at most

on

.
Define the space of piecewise discontinuous polynomials of degree

by

V_{h,k}:=\left\{p\in L^{2}(\Omega)|\ p_{|_{T}}\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T),\ \forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}.

(19)

We also use the following notations:

\displaystyle(v,w)

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}v\,wdx\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\,\,v,w\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}),

\displaystyle\langle v,w\rangle_{{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}}

\displaystyle:=\displaystyle\sum_{e\in{{\mathcal{E}}_{h}}}\int_{e}v\,w\,ds\qquad

3.1
DG formulation for the flow and the concept of the local conservation

In this section, we use
V_{h,k_{p}}
as the DG space for the pressure approximation and present the DG formulation for the flow equation, which is given as follows:

\displaystyle{\bf u}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\nabla p,\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(20a)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf u}

\displaystyle=f,\quad\mbox{ in }\Omega,

(20b)

which is subject to the boundary conditions (
5
). The interior penalty DG formulation
[
27
,
23
]
for (
20
) is then given as: Find
P\in V_{h,k_{p}}
such that

\displaystyle(\kappa\nabla P,\nabla w)-\langle\{\!\!\{\kappa\nabla P\}\!\!\},[\![w]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}}+\langle\sigma[\![P]\!],[\![w]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}+\langle\sigma(P-g_{D}),w\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}

(21)

\displaystyle+\theta_{f}\langle\{\!\!\{\kappa\nabla w\}\!\!\},[\![P]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}+\theta_{f}\langle\kappa\nabla w\cdot{\bf n},(P-g_{D})\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}=(f,w),\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{p}},

where
\sigma
is the penalty parameter and it is given as
\sigma=O(1/h)
. Note that the parameter
\theta_{f}
determines the type of DG, namely,
\theta_{f}=0
for IIPG,
\theta_{f}=-1
for SIPG and
\theta_{f}=1
for NIPG
[
12
]
.
We shall now discuss how to define the numerical flux. In fact, it is standard to define the flux using
P
, the solution to (
20
) in the following manner:

\displaystyle{\bf{U}}

\displaystyle=-\kappa\nabla P,\quad\mbox{ in }T,~\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h},

(22a)

\displaystyle{\bf{U}}

\displaystyle=-\{\!\!\{\kappa\nabla P\}\!\!\}+\sigma[\![P]\!],\quad\mbox{ on }e,~\forall e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o},

(22b)

\displaystyle{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n}

\displaystyle=g_{N},\quad\mbox{ on }e,~\forall e\in\Gamma_{N},

(22c)

\displaystyle{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n}

\displaystyle=-(\kappa\nabla P)\cdot{\bf n}+\sigma(P-g_{D}),~\mbox{ on }e,\quad\forall e\in\Gamma_{D},

(22d)

This means that the flux
U
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle-({\bf{U}},\nabla w)+\langle{\bf{U}},[\![w]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}+\langle{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n},w\rangle_{\partial\Omega}+\theta_{f}\langle\{\!\!\{\kappa\nabla w\}\!\!\},[\![P]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}

(23)

\displaystyle+\theta_{f}\langle\kappa\nabla w\cdot{\bf n},(P-g_{D})\rangle_{\Gamma_{D}}=(f,w),\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{p}}.

Necessarily, the flux satisfies the standard local conservation.

\int_{\partial T}{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n}\,ds=\int_{T}f\,dx.

(24)

On the other hand, if
\theta_{f}=0
, then the flux
{\bf{U}}
satisfies the following equation:

-({\bf{U}},\nabla w)+\langle{\bf{U}},[\![w]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}+\langle{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n},w\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=(f,w),\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{p}}.

(25)

Equivalently, for each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, the flux
{\bf U}
satisfies the following equation:

\int_{T}\nabla\cdot{\bf{U}}w\,dx=\int_{T}fw\,dx,\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{p}}.

(26)

We can view this identity as a stronger version of the local conservation since the standard local conservation is the case when the equation (
26
) holds for
w
being the piecewise constant function. Furthermore, if
k_{p}
is replaced by
k_{c}
, then it has been discussed as the compatibility condition in
[
11
]
, which will be discussed in more details in §
3.3
below. Thus, we are motivated to introduce this condition as a new concept of the local conservation.

We are in a position to state the main definition of the new local conservation, i.e., the local conservation of degree
k
.

Definition 2
.

The flux
{\bf U}
is said to be locally conservative of degree
k
with respect to the triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{h}
if and only if for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, it holds that

\int_{T}\nabla\cdot{\bf U}\,w\,dx=\int_{T}f\,w\,dx,\quad\forall w\in\mathbb{P}_{k}(T).

(27)

In §
4
, we discuss the importance of the local conservation, which include
L^{2}
norm stability, zeroth order accuracy, positivity and maximum principle preserving. We remark that if
\nabla\cdot{\bf U}=f
, i.e., strong conservation holds, then such a local conservation is necessarily true.

3.2
DG formulation for the transport

In this section, we present the DG formulation for transport, primarily based on the work of Brezzi, Marini, and Süli
[
6
]
for the hyperbolic part of the transport. The elliptic part follows the standard interior penalty method by Wheeler
[
27
]
, similar to the flow case.

In our discussion, we shall assume that the mesh is sufficiently small or the flux
{\bf U}
is sufficiently smooth so that

{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}_{e}|_{e}\mbox{ does not change its sign for all }e\in\mathcal{E}_{h},

(28)

where
{\bf n}_{e}
is the normal to the edge
e
. This is crucial to establish the relationship between Lesaint-Raviart DG
[
17
]
and Brezzi-Marini-Süli DG. Under this assumption, for any given edge
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
, which is the common edge of two triangles
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
, we can determine which triangle is upstream triangle
T^{-}
and which is the downstream triangle
T^{+}
. Specifically, if
{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}^{1}\geq 0
, then

3.3
Compatibility condition and its relationship to the local conservation

In this section, we shall review the compatibility condition pioneered by Dawson, Sun and Wheeler
[
11
]
. Basically, the compatibility condition is the minimial requirement imposed on the flux approximation to obtain meaningful solution to the transport. The meaningfulness of the solution to the transport was found at two requirements. One is the global conservation and the other is the zeroth-order accuracy. For the schemes in this paper, the requirement of the global conservation is generally independent of how
{\bf U}
is computed
[
11
]
. Thus, this does not seem to be closely related to the compatibility condition. Rather, it is related to the zeroth-order accuracy. The zeroth-order accuracy can be considered to be a weaker version of the maximum or minimum principle. Namely, if the transport scheme can not reproduce a constant solution, it means there arise spurious sources and sinks due to numerical inaccuracies. In relation to DG formulation (
33
) of the transport, the zeroth order accuracy is shown to hold under the assumption that the numerical flux satisfies the following compatibility condition
[
11
]
:

-({\bf{U}},\nabla w)_{\Omega}+\langle{\bf{U}},[\![w]\!]\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}+\langle{\bf{U}}\cdot{\bf n},w\rangle_{\partial\Omega}=(f,w)_{\Omega},\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{c}}.

(38)

Note that this is obtained by setting
C=C^{*}=c_{\rm I}=c^{0}
is a constant in the equation (
32
) and it is equivalent to the following statement, i.e., for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, it holds that

\int_{T}\nabla\cdot{\bf U}\,w\,dx=\int_{T}f\,w\,dx,\quad\forall w\in V_{h,k_{c}}.

(39)

We observe that the compatibility condition is the local conservation of degree
k_{c}
. This observation is summarized in the following theorem:

Theorem 3
.

For
k_{c}>0
, the local conservation of degree
k_{c}
can be achieved by using
k_{p}\geq k_{c}
for the flow equation with
\theta_{f}=0
only. For
k_{c}=0
, the local conservation of degree
k_{c}
can be achieved by using
k_{p}\geq k_{c}
with
\theta_{f}=-1,0,1
.

Proof.

We recall that the flux
{\bf U}
satisfies the equation (
23
). Thus, in order to obtain the local conservation of degree
k_{c}
, it is clear to use
k_{p}\geq k_{c}
. For
k_{c}>0
, only
\theta_{f}=0
can lead to the local conservation of degree
k_{c}
. On the other hand, if
k_{c}=0
, then any
\theta_{f}=-1,0,1
can lead to the local conservation of degree
k_{c}=0
. This completes the proof.

4.1
The Lesaint-Raviart DG method and its equivalence with the BMS-DG scheme

In this section, we shall introduce the Lesaint-Raviart DG formulation for the equation (
40

We note that similar to the streamline diffusion methods
[
7
,
11
]

40

\partial_{t}c+{\bf U}\cdot\nabla c+(\nabla\cdot{\bf U})c=fc^{*}.

(42)

After time discretization by Euler’s method, the Lesaint-Raviart formulation is designed using the discontinuous Galerkin finite element: Find
c_{h}\in V_{h,k_{c}}
such that
c_{h}=c_{\rm I}
on
\Gamma_{-}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}({\bf U}\cdot\nabla c_{h})w_{h}+(\nabla\cdot{\bf U})c_{h}w_{h}+\gamma c_{h}w_{h}\,dx

(43)

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)}(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+})\,ds+\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{-}}\int_{e}c_{h}|{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}|w_{h}\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}(fc^{*}+g)w_{h}\,dx+\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{-}}\int_{e}c_{\rm I}|{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}|w_{h}\,ds,\quad\forall w_{h}\in V_{h,k_{c}},

where
g=\gamma c_{h}^{old}
with
\gamma=\frac{1}{\Delta t}
,
\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)
represents the interior edge with
{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}<0
and
u^{\pm}=\lim\limits_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}u(\bm{x}\pm\epsilon{\bf U})
.
The following lemma basically establishes the equivalence relationship between the BMS-DG method and Lesaint-Raviart DG method.

Lemma 4
.

It holds true that

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}c_{h}w_{h}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)}(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+})\,ds

(44)

\displaystyle-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}c_{h}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}w_{h}\,ds=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{c_{h}\}\!\!\}\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}\frac{|{\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}|}{2}[\![c_{h}]\!]\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds.

Proof.

It is well known that
[
1
]

\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n})c_{h}w_{h}\,ds=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\bf U}c_{h}\}\!\!\}[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}[\![{\bf U}c_{h}]\!]\{\!\!\{w_{h}\}\!\!\}\,ds.

(45)

Thus, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}c_{h}w_{h}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n})\,ds-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)}(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+})\,ds-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}c_{h}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}w_{h}\,ds

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\bf U}c_{h}\}\!\!\}[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}[\![{\bf U}c_{h}]\!]\{\!\!\{w_{h}\}\!\!\}\,ds-\int_{\Gamma_{-}}c_{h}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}w_{h}\,ds

\displaystyle-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)}(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+})\,ds.

Since
[\![\phi]\!]=\phi\bm{n}
and
\{\!\!\{\bm{\tau}\}\!\!\}=\bm{\tau}
on
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\partial}
, we see that

\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\bf U}c_{h}\}\!\!\}[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds=\sum_{e\notin\Gamma_{-}}\int_{e}\{\!\!\{{\bf U}c_{h}\}\!\!\}[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds+\sum_{e\in\Gamma_{-}}\int_{e}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n})gw_{h}\,ds.

(46)

Thus, we only need to show that

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}[\![{\bf U}c_{h}]\!]\{\!\!\{w_{h}\}\!\!\}\,ds-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{\Gamma_{-}^{\circ}(T)}(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}({\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+})\,ds

(47)

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}}\int_{e}\frac{|{\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}|}{2}[\![c_{h}]\!]\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!]\,ds.

For any given
e\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{o}
, we see that

\displaystyle\quad[\![{\bf U}c_{h}]\!]\{\!\!\{w_{h}\}\!\!\}-(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}{\bf U}\cdot{\bf n}^{+}

\displaystyle=({\bf U}c_{h}^{+}\cdot{\bf n}^{+}+{\bf U}c_{h}^{-}\cdot{\bf n}^{-})\frac{w_{h}^{+}+w_{h}^{-}}{2}-(c_{h}^{+}-c_{h}^{-})w_{h}^{+}{\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}^{+}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}{\bf U}c_{h}^{+}w_{h}^{+}\bm{n}^{+}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf U}c_{h}^{+}w_{h}^{-}\bm{n}^{+}-\frac{1}{2}{\bf U}c_{h}^{-}w_{h}^{+}\bm{n}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}{\bf U}c_{h}^{-}w_{h}^{-}\bm{n}^{-}

\displaystyle=\frac{|{\bf U}\cdot\bm{n}|}{2}[\![c_{h}]\!]\cdot[\![w_{h}]\!].

This completes the proof.

By using the integral by parts for the first term in (
43

4

[
6
]
and Lesaint-Raviart DG scheme
[
21
]

Proof.

We can easily find that

Theorem 9
.

Assume that the flux satisfies the local conservation of degree
k\geq 2k_{c}=0
. The solution
C^{\eta}
to (
57

0\leq C^{\eta}\leq M
, where

M=\max\{|c^{0}|,|c_{\rm I}|,|\widetilde{c}|\}.

(71)

Proof.

Consider piecewise constant function in (
67

w_{h}=1
in
T
and
w_{h}=0
in others, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{T}C^{\eta,\ell+1}\,dx+\int_{T}(f_{{}_{+}}+\gamma)C^{\eta,\ell+1}\,dx

(72)

\displaystyle=\int_{T}f_{{}_{+}}\widetilde{c}\,dx+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{T}\widetilde{C}^{\eta,\ell}(x)\,dx+\int_{T}g\,dx.

Obviously, we can get
C^{\eta,\ell+1}\geq 0
by induction.
Additionally, the above equation yields

\displaystyle\int_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+f_{{}_{+}}+\gamma\right)C^{\eta,\ell+1}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{T}f_{{}_{+}}\widetilde{c}\,dx+\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{T}\widetilde{C}^{\eta,\ell}\,dx+\int_{T}g\,dx

(73)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+f_{{}_{+}}+\gamma\right)M\,dx.

Then we have,

\displaystyle\int_{T}\left(\frac{1}{\eta}+f_{{}_{+}}+\gamma\right)(C^{\eta,\ell+1}-M)\,dx\leq 0.

Thus,

6
Conclusions

In this paper, we introduce the new concept of the locally conservative flux, i.e., the local conservation of degree
k\geq 0
. This can be interpreted as the intermediate local conservation between the standard local conservation and the strong conservation. Under this condition,
we prove the
L^{2}
-stability for the transport equation.
Besides, we show how the positivity and maximum principle can be obtained when the piecewise constant function is used for approximating the transport. This resolves the long standing open question. Finally, several numerical experiments are carried out to verify the theoretical results.

Bismut-Stroock Hessian formulas and local Hessian estimates for heat semigroups and harmonic functions on Riemannian manifolds
Qin-Qian Chen 1 , Li-Juan Cheng 1 , Anton Thalmaier 2

Abstract
. In this article, we develop a martingale approach to localized Bismut-type Hessian formulas for heat semigroups on Riemannian manifolds. Our approach extends the Hessian formulas established by Stroock (1996) and removes in particular the compact manifold restriction. To demonstrate the potential of these formulas, we give as application explicit quantitative local estimates for the Hessian of the heat semigroup, as well as for harmonic functions on regular domains in Riemannian manifolds.

Introduction

Let
M
be a complete Riemannian manifold of dimension
n
with
Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
. We consider the operator

L=\frac{1}{2}\Delta
where
\Delta:={\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\,\nabla{\bf d}
is the
Laplace-Beltrami operator on
M
. Denote by
X{.}(x)
a Brownian
motion on
M
starting at
x\in M
with generator
L
and explosion
time
\zeta(x)
. The explosion time is the random time at which the
process leaves all compact subsets of
M
. Furthermore, let
B_{t}
be
the stochastic anti-development of
X{.}(x)
which is a Brownian
motion on
T_{x}M
, stopped at the lifetime
\zeta(x)
of
X{.}(x)
, and
denote by
P_{t}f
the associated minimal heat semigroup, acting on
bounded measurable functions
f
, which is represented in
probabilistic terms by the formula

\displaystyle(P_{t}f)(x)=\mathbb{E}\big[f(X_{t}(x))\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{t<\zeta(x)\}}\big].

Recall that for any
T>0
fixed,
(t,x)\mapsto(P_{t}f)(x)
is smooth
and bounded on
{(0,T]}\times M
with
P_{0}f=f
.

Let
Q_{t}\colon T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
be defined by

\displaystyle DQ_{t}=-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(Q_{t})\,dt,\quad Q_{0}={\mathop{\mathchoice{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}{\text{\rm id}}}\nolimits},

where
D:=/\!/_{t}\,d\,/\!/_{t}^{-1}
and

/\!/_{t}:=/\!/_{0,t}:T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}(x)}M
denotes
parallel transport along
X(x)
. The following formula is taken from

[
16
]
and gives a typical probabilistic derivative
formula for the semigroup
P_{t}f
; it extends earlier formulas of
Elworthy-Li
[
8
]
. Results in this direction are centered
around Bismut’s integration by parts formula
[
3
]
. To be
precise, let
D
be a fixed regular domain in
M
and
\tau_{D}
be the
first exit time of
X{.}(x)
from
D
when started at
x\in D
. A
regular domain is by definition an open, relatively compact connected
domain in
M
with non-empty boundary. Then for
v\in T_{x}M
,

\displaystyle\nabla P_{t}f(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{t}(x))\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{t<\zeta(x)\}}\int_{0}^{\tau_{D}\wedge t}\langle Q_{t}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}\,dB_{s}\rangle\right],

where the function
k\in L^{1,2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},[0,1])
satisfies the property
that
k(0)=0
and
k(s)=1
for all
s\geq\tau_{D}\wedge t
. An
explicit choice of the random function

in the above Bismut formula
can be used to derive sharp gradient estimates in various situations,
e.g. for heat semigroups, as well as for harmonic functions on regular
domains in Riemannian manifolds, see
[
17
,
20
]
for explicit
results. Recently, such formulas have also been applied to
quantitative estimates for the derivative of a
C^{2}
function
u
in
terms of local bounds on
u
and
\Delta u

[
6
]
.

A similar approach has been used by Arnaudon, Driver and Thalmaier

[
1
]
to approach Cheng-Yau type inequalities for the
harmonic functions. Actually, the effect of curvature on the
behavior of harmonic functions on a Riemannian manifold
M
is a
classical problem (see e.g.
[
21
,
13
]
), see also

[
7
]
for characterizations of gradient estimates in the
context of metric measure spaces. In this paper, we aim to clarify in
particular the effect of curvature on the Hessian of harmonic
functions on regular domains in Riemannian manifolds. To this end, we
establish a local version of a Bismut type formula for
{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{t}f

which is consistent with Stroock’s formula in
[
14
]
while
here we follow a martingale approach and allow the manifold to be
non-compact.

We start by giving some background on Bismut type formulas for second
order derivatives of heat semigroups. A first such formula appeared in
Elworthy and Li
[
8
,
9
]
for a non-compact manifold, however
under strong curvature restrictions. An intrinsic formula for

{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{t}f
was given by Stroock
[
14
]
for a compact
Riemannian manifold, while a localized intrinsic formula was obtained
by Arnaudon, Planck and Thalmaier
[
2
]
adopting a martingale
approach. A localized version of the Hessian formula (still with
doubly stochastic damped parallel translations) for the Feynman-Kac
semigroup was derived by Thompson
[
19
]
. The study of
the Hessian of a Feynman-Kac semigroup generated by a Schrödinger
operator of the type
\Delta+V
where
V
is a potential, has been
pushed forward by Li
[
11
,
10
]
. Very recently, Bismut-type
Hessian formulas have been used for new applications, see for instance
Cao-Cheng-Thalmaier
[
4
]
where
L^{p}
Calderón-Zygmund
inequalities on Riemannian manifolds have been established under
natural geometric assumptions for indices
p>1
.

The following formula is taken from
[
2
]
and gives a localized
intrinsic Bismut type formula for the Hessian of the heat semigroup.
For
v,w\in T_{x}M
define an operator-valued process

W_{t}(v,w):T_{x}M\rightarrow T_{X_{t}}M
as solution to the covariant
Itô equation

\displaystyle DW_{t}(v,w)=R\big(/\!/_{t}dB_{t},Q_{t}(v)\big)Q_{t}(w)-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(w))\,dt-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(W_{t}(v,w))\,dt

with initial condition
W_{0}(v,w)=0
. Here
R
denotes the Riemann
curvature tensor. It is easy to check that

W_{t}(v,w)=Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}\left(R\big(/\!/_{r}dB_{r},Q_{r}(v)\big)Q_{r}(w)-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(Q_{r}(v),Q_{r}(w))\,dr\right).

The operator
{\bf d}^{*}R
is defined by

{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1})v_{2}:=-{\operatorname{tr}}\kern 0.80002pt\nabla{.}R(\cdot,v_{1})v_{2}
and satisfies

\displaystyle\langle{\bf d}^{*}R(v_{1})v_{2},v_{3}\rangle=\langle(\nabla_{v_{3}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{1}),v_{2}\rangle-\langle(\nabla_{v_{2}}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp})(v_{3}),v_{1}\rangle

for all
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\in T_{x}M
and
x\in M
. Let
v,w\in T_{x}M
with

x\in D
,
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
and fix
0<S<T
. Suppose that
D_{1}

and
D_{2}
are regular domains such that
x\in D_{1}
and

\bar{D}_{1}\subset D_{2}\subset D
. Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be two
stopping times satisfying

0<\sigma\leq\tau_{D_{1}}<\tau\leq\tau_{D_{2}}
. Assume
k,\ell
are
bounded adapted processes with paths in the Cameron-Martin space

L^{1,2}([0,T];[0,1])
such that

•

k(0)=1
and
k(s)=0
for
s\geq\sigma\wedge S
;

•

\ell(s)=1
for
s\leq\sigma\wedge S
and
\ell(s)=0
for

s\geq\tau\wedge T
.

Let
{\operatorname{Hess}}=\nabla{\bf d}
. Then for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
, we have

\displaystyle({\operatorname{Hess}}_{x}P_{T}f)(v,v)=

\displaystyle-\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[f(X_{T})\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{T<\zeta(x)\}}\int_{0}^{T\wedge\tau}\langle W_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v,v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right]

(1.1)

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}\left[f(X_{T})\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{T<\zeta(x)\}}\int_{S}^{T}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{\ell}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\int_{0}^{S}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right].

This formula has the advantage to be concise with only two terms on
the right-hand side to characterize the Hessian of semigroup; the
martingale approach to this formula is direct and does not need
advanced path perturbation theory on Riemannian manifold. However, it
depends on the choice of two random test functions
k
and
\ell
.
For local estimates the construction of two different random times

\tau
and
\sigma
is required, which complicates the calculation of
the coefficients in the estimates, see
[
2
]
.

Let us compare formula (
1.1
) with the Hessian
formula from
[
14
]
where the manifold is supposed to be
compact. Stroock proved that in this case one deterministic function
test function
k\in C^{1}([0,T],[0,1])
satisfying
k(0)=1
and

k(t)=0
is sufficient in a Bismut-type formula for the Hessian of the
semigroup (see
[
15
, Eq. (1.6)]
and formula
(
2.3
) below). In what follows, we call it the
Bismut-Stroock Hessian formula. Although its proof relies on
perturbation theory of Brownian paths and more terms are involved in
the formula, the fact that only one test function
k
enters makes it
attractive for applications to the short-time behavior of the heat
kernel, see
[
15
]
. Recently, Chen, Li and Wu
[
5
]

adopted a perturbation of the driving force
B_{t}
with a second order
term for a new approach to this formula. The formula is derived with
localized vector fields and extends to a general (non-compact)
complete Riemannian manifold by introducing a family of cut-off
processes.

The described works motivate the following two questions:

(i)

Can one extend the Stroock-Bismut Hessian formula

[
14
]
to a local Bismut-type formula for the Hessian heat
semigroup by a direct construction of suitable martingales, and
achieve from it explicit local estimates of
{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{t}f
by a
proper choice of
k
?

(ii)

Can the localized Bismut-Stroock Hessian formula be
transformed into a formula for the Hessian of harmonic functions
on regular domains in Riemannian manifolds from where quantitative
estimates can be derived?

In the sequel we answer both questions positively. Note that for the
first question, it has been explained in
[
5
, Section 4]
that it
seems difficult to adopt the perturbation theory of
M
-valued
Brownian motions in order to replace the non-random vector field on
path space by a random one; the time reversed field will not be
adapted anymore and the Itô integral no longer be well defined. This
reason motivates our search for a different stochastic approach to the
problem in the form of a direct martingale argument. Actually,
establishing localized Bismut-Stroock Hessian formula of semigroup
relies on the proper construction of martingales which turns out to be
the main difficulty for the first question. As an application of our
formula, we can give explicit local Hessian estimates for the heat
semigroup: for any regular domain
D\subset M
and
x\in D
,

(1.2)

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{t}f|(x)\leq\inf_{\delta>0}\left\{\left(\frac{t}{2}\sqrt{K_{1}^{2}+\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{\delta}}+\frac{2}{t}\right)\exp\left(t\,\Biggl(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3)}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}\Biggl)\right)\right\}\|f\|_{D},

where
\delta_{x}=\rho(x,\partial D)
is the Riemannian distance of
x

to the boundary of
D
. Compared with known estimates,
e.g.
[
2
]
, the constants in (
1.2
) are explicit and
concise. The estimate is not essentially more complicated, compared
to the local gradient estimate
[
17
]
.

The second problem is then to extract Hessian estimates for harmonic
functions from the Bismut-Stroock Hessian formula which requires a
careful estimate of the terms involving
W{.}(v,w)
and
Q{.}(v)
,
along with a proper choice of the process
k
. Let
D\subset M
be a
regular domain. In Section
3
, we obtain that for a
bounded positive harmonic function
u
on
D
,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}\,u|(x)

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}0<\delta_{1}<1/2\\
\delta_{2}>0\end{subarray}}\Bigg\{\sqrt{(1+2\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{K_{0}^{-}\neq 0\}})\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{6}\,\Bigg(\frac{1}{2\delta_{2}}+3K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3+\delta_{1}^{-1})}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}\Bigg)\Bigg\}\sqrt{\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)},

where constants
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
are defined as in
(
2.10
)–(
2.12
) below; see also Theorem
3.5
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we
construct several local martingales to achieve local Hessian formulas
for the heat semigroup which are consistent with Stroock’s
construction
[
15
]
on compact manifolds with a
deterministic function
k
. For reader’s convenience, Section

3
provides the method to construct
k
which follows

[
17
]
. In Section 4 we give explicit local Hessian estimates
for harmonic functions. Finally, in Section 5, we apply our method
also to estimate the second derivative of heat semigroups and heat
kernels.

2.
Bismut-type formulas for the Hessian of
P_{t}f

We start by constructing our fundamental martingale which will be the
basis for our approach. To this end, for
v,w\in T_{x}M
let

\displaystyle W_{t}^{k}(v,w)=Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}R\big(/\!/_{r}dB_{r},Q_{r}(k(r)v)\big)Q_{r}(w)-\frac{1}{2}Q_{t}\int_{0}^{t}Q_{r}^{-1}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}\big(Q_{r}(k(r)v),Q_{r}(w)\big)\,dr.

It is easy to see that
W_{t}^{k}
solves the following equation:

\displaystyle DW^{k}_{t}(v,w)=R\big(/\!/_{t}\,dB_{t},Q_{t}(k(t)v)\big)Q_{t}(w)-\frac{1}{2}({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}\big(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(w)\big)\,dt-\frac{1}{2}{\operatorname{Ric}}^{\sharp}(W^{k}_{t}(v,w))\,dt,

and
W_{0}^{k}(v,w)=0
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
x\in M
and
D
be a relatively compact open domain in
M
such
that
x\in D
. Let
\tau
be a stopping time such that

0<\tau\leq\tau_{D}
where
\tau_{D}
denotes the first exit time of

X(x)
with starting point
x\in D
. Fix
T>0
and suppose that
k

is a bounded, non-negative and adapted process with paths in the
Cameron-Martin space
L^{1,2}([0,T];\mathbb{R})
such that
k(s)=0
for

s\geq T\wedge\tau
,
k(0)=1
. Then for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
and

v,w\in T_{x}M
,

\displaystyle({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-t}f)\big(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(k(t)v)\big)+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v))

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}^{k}(v,\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds\right)

is a local martingale on
[0,\tau\wedge T)
.

Proof.

First of all, by an approximation argument we may assume that

f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and is constant outside a compact set so that

|{\bf d}f|
and
\Delta f
are bounded.

Let

\displaystyle N_{t}(v,v)={\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(v),Q_{t}(v))+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}(v,v)).

Then
N_{t}(v,v)
is a local martingale, see for instance the proof of

[
19
, Lemma 2.7]
with potential
V\equiv 0
.
Furthermore, define

N^{k}_{t}(v,v)={\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(v))+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W^{k}_{t}(v,v)).

According to the definition of
W^{k}_{t}(v,v)
, resp.
W_{t}(v,v)
, and in
view of the fact that
N_{t}(v,v)
is a local martingale, it is easy to
see that

(2.1)

\displaystyle N_{t}^{k}(v,v)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(v))\,ds

is a local martingale as well. Replacing in
N_{t}^{k}(v,v)
the second
argument
v
by
k(t)v
, we further see that also

\displaystyle N_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(k(t)v))\,ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(k(s)v),Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(W^{k}_{s}(v,\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds

(2.2)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-r}f)(Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),Q_{r}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,dr\ ds

is a local martingale. Note that
N_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v)=N_{t}^{k}(v,v)\,k(t)
.
Exchanging the order of integration in the
last term shows that

\displaystyle N_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v)-\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(k(t)v))\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad-\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(k(s)v),Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(W^{k}_{s}(v,\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-r}f)(Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),Q_{r}((k(t)-k(r))v))\,dr

(2.3)

\displaystyle=N_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v)-\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(W^{k}_{s}(v,\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds-2\int_{0}^{t}{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(k(s)v),Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds

is a local martingale. Moreover, by the formula

(2.4)

\displaystyle P_{T-t}f(X_{t})=P_{T}f(x)+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(/\!/_{s}dB_{s})

and integration by parts,

(2.5)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}({\bf d}P_{T-s}f)(W_{s}^{k}(v,\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds-P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}^{k}(v,\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}\,dB_{s}\rangle

is a local martingale. Similarly, from the formula

\displaystyle{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v))={\bf d}P_{T}f(v)+\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(/\!/_{s}dB_{s},Q_{s}(k(s)v))+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds

it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(k(s)v))\,ds-{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(2.6)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds\,\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

is also a local martingale. Concerning the last term in
(
2
), we note that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\,ds\,\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),/\!/_{r}dB_{r}\rangle\,ds

is a local martingale. Combining this with (
2
) we
conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-s}f)(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),Q_{s}(k(s)v))\,ds-{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(2.7)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),/\!/_{r}dB_{r}\rangle\,ds

is a local martingale. Using the local martingales
(
2.5
) and (
2
) to replace the last two
terms in (
2.3
), we conclude that

\displaystyle({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-t}f)(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(k(t)v))+({\bf d}P_{T-t}f)(W_{t}^{k}(v,k(t)v))

\displaystyle\quad-P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\int_{0}^{t}\langle W_{s}^{k}(v,\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-2{\bf d}P_{T-t}f(Q_{t}(k(t)v))\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

(2.8)

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),/\!/_{r}dB_{r}\rangle\,ds

is a local martingale as well. On the other hand, by the product rule
for martingales, we have

(2.9)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds=2\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{r}(\dot{k}(r)v),/\!/_{r}dB_{r}\rangle\right)\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle

which along with (
2.4
) implies that

\displaystyle P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2\int_{0}^{t}{\bf d}P_{T-s}f(Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v))\int_{0}^{s}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\,ds

is a local martingale. Applying this observation to
Eq. (
2
), we finally see that

\displaystyle({\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T-t}f)\big(Q_{t}(k(t)v),Q_{t}(k(t)v)\big)

\displaystyle\ +P_{T-t}f(X_{t})\left(\left(\int_{0}^{t}\langle Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v),/\!/_{s}dB_{s}\rangle\right)^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}|Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds\right)

is local martingale. This completes the proof.

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)}\sqrt{\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)\,\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{c_{1}(f)s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{(2K_{0}^{-}-c_{1}(f))r}\,dr\,ds}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{6}f^{-2}(x)\sqrt{\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)}\,\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{c_{2}(f)s}\,ds\bigg).

It now suffices to choose a suitable function
h_{1}
.
By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{c_{1}(f)s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{(2K_{0}^{-}-c_{1}(f))r}\,dr\leq\operatorname{e}^{c_{1}s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{(2K_{0}^{-}-c_{1})r}\,dr\leq\operatorname{e}^{(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{-c_{1}r}\,dr\leq\frac{\operatorname{e}^{(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}}{c_{1}}.

We define

\displaystyle h_{1}(s)=\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K^{-}_{0})r}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K^{-}_{0})r}\,dr}.

If
\delta_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, then it is easy to see that

c_{2}(f)\leq c_{2}\leq c_{1}

and
c_{1}\geq K_{0}^{-}
,

\displaystyle|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,u)_{x}(v,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)}\sqrt{\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)\frac{c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-}}{c_{1}}}

\displaystyle\quad+f^{-2}(x)\sqrt{6\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)}(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{6\|u\|_{D}\,u(x)}\,\left((2\delta_{2})^{-1}+3K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3+\delta_{1}^{-1})}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}\right)

for

and
\delta_{2}>0
. We complete the proof of inequality (

) by taking the infimum with respect to

and
\delta_{2}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.5
(Part II).

Theorem
3.1
allows to control
|{\operatorname{Hess}}\,u(v,v)|
directly
in terms of
\|u\|_{D}
as follows:

\displaystyle|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,u)_{x}(v,v)|

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,P^{D}_{t}u)_{x}(v,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{D}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}|W_{s}^{k}(v,v)|^{2}\,\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{D}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}\|Q_{s}\|^{2}\,\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{D}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}|W_{s}^{k}(v,v)|^{2}\,\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]}

(3.16)

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{D}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}s}\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]={\rm I}+{\rm II}.

For the second term
{\rm II}
we observe

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau(t)}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}s}\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]=\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}\tau(s)}f^{-2}(X_{\tau(s)})\big]\,ds;

the first term has been dealt with in (
3.12
) above. We conclude from (
3.16
) that

\displaystyle\big|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,u)_{x}(v,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{D}\sqrt{\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[|W^{k}_{\tau(s)}(v,v)|^{2}f^{-2}(X_{\tau(s)})\right]\,ds}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|u\|_{D}\,\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}\tau(s)}f^{-2}(X_{\tau(s)})\big]\,ds.

Recall that by Lemma
3.3
,

\displaystyle c_{3}(f)

\displaystyle=\sup_{D}\left\{K_{0}^{-}f^{2}+3|\nabla f|^{2}-(f\Delta f)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3)}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}=:c_{3}<c_{1}.

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}^{x}\big[f^{-2}(X_{\tau(s)})\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}\tau(s)}\big]\leq f^{-2}(x)\operatorname{e}^{c_{3}(f)s}\leq\operatorname{e}^{c_{3}s},

and hence

\displaystyle\big|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,u)_{x}(v,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{c_{1}s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{(2K_{0}^{-}-c_{1})r}\,dr\,ds\right)}\,\|u\|_{D}

(3.17)

\displaystyle\qquad+2\left(\int_{0}^{t}\dot{h}_{1}(s)^{2}\operatorname{e}^{c_{3}s}\,ds\right)\|u\|_{D}.

Define

\displaystyle h_{1}(s)=\frac{\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}\,dr}{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}\,dr},

so that

\displaystyle\dot{h}_{1}(s)=\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}}{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}dr}.

The estimate

\displaystyle\operatorname{e}^{c_{1}s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{(2K_{0}^{-}-c_{1})r}\,dr\leq\operatorname{e}^{(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}\int_{0}^{s}\operatorname{e}^{-c_{1}r}\,dr\leq\frac{\operatorname{e}^{(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}}{c_{1}}

is immediate. Since

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-2(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}\operatorname{e}^{c_{3}s}}{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}dr\right)^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})s}}{\left(\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}dr\right)^{2}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{t}\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})r}dr}=\frac{c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-}}{1-\operatorname{e}^{-(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-})t}}.

Using these estimates to bound (
3.17
) from above and letting
t
tend to
\infty
, we deduce

\displaystyle|({\operatorname{Hess}}\,u)_{x}(v,v)|

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)\left(\frac{c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-}}{c_{1}}\right)}\,\|u\|_{D}+2\left(c_{1}+2K_{0}^{-}\right)\|u\|_{D}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{(1+2\hbox{\kern 3.75pt\vrule height=6.75972pt,depth=-0.43057pt,width=0.50003pt\kern-3.75pt\rm 1}_{\{K_{0}^{-}\neq 0\}})\left((\delta_{1}+1)K_{1}^{2}+\frac{\delta_{2}}{2}K_{2}^{2}\right)}\,\|u\|_{D}

\displaystyle\quad+2\left((2\delta_{2})^{-1}+3K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3+\delta_{1}^{-1})}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}\right){\|u\|_{D}}

for

and

∎

4.
Estimate of the Hessian of the semigroup

We are now going to apply our results to give explicit local Hessian estimates
for the heat semigroup on a Riemannian manifold.

Remark 4.1
.

Local Hessian estimates for the semigroup have been derived in

[
12
, Section 4.2]
by using the Hessian formula in
[
2
]
.
We can improve these results significantly by clarifying the coefficients.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
x\in M
and
D
be a relatively compact open domain such that
x\in D
.
Then for
f\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(M)
,

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T}f|(x)\leq\inf_{\delta>0}\left\{\left(\frac{T}{2}\sqrt{K_{1}^{2}+\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{\delta}}+\frac{2}{T}\right)\exp\left(T\left(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{\delta}{2}+\frac{\pi\sqrt{(n-1)K^{-}_{0}}}{2\delta_{x}}+\frac{\pi^{2}(n+3)}{4\delta_{x}^{2}}\right)\right)\right\}\|f\|_{D}.

Proof.

By Theorem
2.3
and Cauchy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\big|({\operatorname{Hess}}{P_{T}f})_{x}(v,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{D}\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{T}|W^{k}_{s}(v,\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds\right]\right)^{1/2}+2\|f\|_{D}\left(\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{T}|Q_{s}(\dot{k}(s)v)|^{2}\,ds\right)

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{D}\sqrt{\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau(T)}|W^{k}_{s}(v,v)|^{2}\,\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]}+2\|f\|_{D}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau(T)}|Q_{s}|^{2}\,\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]={\rm I}+{\rm II}.

Using a similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.5
(Part II) and Lemma
2.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|(

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Hess}}{P_{T}f})_{x}(v,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{D}\left(\frac{(2p-1)^{p}K_{1}^{2p}}{2\delta^{p-1}}+\frac{K_{2}^{2p}}{2\delta^{2p-1}}\right)^{1/(2p)}\operatorname{e}^{(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta)T}T^{1/p}\,\left[\mathbb{E}^{x}\int_{0}^{T}|\dot{k}(t)|^{2q}\,dt\right]^{1/(2q)}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|f\|_{D}\,\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\operatorname{e}^{-K_{0}s}\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{D}\left(\frac{(2p-1)^{p}K_{1}^{2p}}{2^{2}\delta^{p-1}}+\frac{K_{2}^{2p}}{2^{2}\delta^{2p-1}}\right)^{1/(2p)}\operatorname{e}^{\big(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta+\frac{\tilde{c}_{q}}{2q}\big)T}T^{3/(2p)-1/2}

\displaystyle\quad+2\|f\|_{D}\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}^{-}T}\mathbb{E}^{x}\left[\int_{0}^{T}\dot{k}(s)^{2}\,ds\right]

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{D}\left(\frac{(2p-1)^{p}K_{1}^{2p}}{2^{2}\delta^{p-1}}+\frac{K_{2}^{2p}}{2^{2}\delta^{2p-1}}\right)^{1/(2p)}\operatorname{e}^{\big(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta+\frac{\tilde{c}_{1}}{2}\big)T}T^{(3-p)/2p}+\|f\|_{D}\operatorname{e}^{(K_{0}^{-}+\tilde{c}_{1})T}\frac{2}{T}

for any
\delta>0
.
We complete the proof by substituting
\tilde{c}_{1}
defined in Lemma
2.2
into the above inequality and letting

.
∎

For global results (i.e. the case
D=M

holds with

and the Cameron-Martin valued process

If the manifold is non-compact,

,

\displaystyle K_{2}:=\sup\left\{|({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(v,v)|\colon v,w\in T_{x}M,\ x\in M,\ |v|=1,\,|w|=1\right\}<\infty,

In the following, we use a different argument by choosing

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
M
be a non-compact manifold. Assume
{\operatorname{Ric}}\geq K_{0}
and
K_{1}:=\|R\|_{\infty}<\infty

\displaystyle K_{2}=\sup\left\{|({\bf d}^{*}R+\nabla{\operatorname{Ric}})^{\sharp}(v,v)|\colon v,w\in T_{x}M,\ |v|=1,\,|w|=1\right\}<\infty.

Then,

(4.2)

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T}f|\leq\left(\left(\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{2\delta}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{2}{T}\right)\exp\left(\left(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)T\right)\|f\|_{\infty}

(4.3)

\displaystyle|{\operatorname{Hess}}P_{T}f|\leq\left(\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{\sqrt{2}}+\frac{2}{T}\right)\operatorname{e}^{K_{0}^{-}T}\|f\|_{\infty}.

Proof.

From (

\displaystyle\big|({\operatorname{Hess}}{P_{T}f})_{x}(v,v)\big|

If we choose

\displaystyle\big|({\operatorname{Hess}}{P_{T}f})_{x}(v,v)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\frac{K_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{2\delta}\right)^{1/2}+\frac{2}{T}\right)\exp\left(\left(K_{0}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}\delta\right)T\right)\|f\|_{\infty}

pyJac : analytical Jacobian generator for chemical kinetics Journal: Computer Physics Communications
Kyle E. Niemeyer, Nicholas J. Curtis, Chih-Jen Sung

Abstract
Accurate simulations of combustion phenomena require the use of detailed chemical kinetics in order to capture limit phenomena such as ignition and extinction as well as predict pollutant formation. However, the chemical kinetic models for hydrocarbon fuels of practical interest typically have large numbers of species and reactions and exhibit high levels of mathematical stiffness in the governing differential equations, particularly for larger fuel molecules. In order to integrate the stiff equations governing chemical kinetics, generally reactive-flow simulations rely on implicit algorithms that require frequent Jacobian matrix evaluations. Some in situ and a posteriori computational diagnostics methods also require accurate Jacobian matrices, including computational singular perturbation and chemical explosive mode analysis. Typically, finite differences numerically approximate these, but for larger chemical kinetic models this poses significant computational demands since the number of chemical source term evaluations scales with the square of species count. Furthermore, existing analytical Jacobian tools do not optimize evaluations or support emerging SIMD processors such as GPUs. Here we introduce pyJac , a Python-based open-source program that generates analytical Jacobian matrices for use in chemical kinetics modeling and analysis. In addition to producing the necessary customized source code for evaluating reaction rates (including all modern reaction rate formulations), the chemical source terms, and the Jacobian matrix, pyJac uses an optimized evaluation order to minimize computational and memory operations. As a demonstration, we first establish the correctness of the Jacobian matrices for kinetic models of hydrogen, methane, ethylene, and isopentanol oxidation (number of species ranging 13 to 360 13360 ) by showing agreement within 0.001 % 0.001\text{\,}\mathrm{\%} of matrices obtained via automatic differentiation. We then demonstrate the performance achievable on CPUs and GPUs using pyJac via matrix evaluation timing comparisons; the routines produced by pyJac outperformed first-order finite differences by 3 to 7.5 37.5 times and the existing analytical Jacobian software TChem by 1.1 to 2.2 1.12.2 times on a single-threaded basis. It is noted that TChem is not thread-safe, while pyJac is easily parallelized, and hence can greatly outperform TChem on multicore CPUs. The Jacobian matrix generator we describe here will be useful for reducing the cost of integrating chemical source terms with implicit algorithms in particular and algorithms that require an accurate Jacobian matrix in general. Furthermore, the open-source release of the program and Python-based implementation will enable wide adoption.

In parallel with the potential improvements in stiff implicit integrators used for chemical kinetics, algorithms tailored for high-performance hardware accelerators offer another route to reducing computational costs.
In the past, central processing unit (CPU) clock speeds increased regularly—i.e., Moore’s Law—but power consumption and heat dissipation issues disrupted this trend recently, slowing the pace of increases in CPU clock rates.
While multicore parallelism continues to raise CPU performance, single-instruction multiple data (SIMD) processors, e.g., graphics processing units (GPUs), recently emerged as a low-cost, low-power consumption, and massively parallel high-performance computing alternative.
GPUs—originally developed for graphics/video processing and display purposes—consist of hundreds to thousands of cores, compared to the tens of cores found on a typical CPU.
Recognizing that the SIMD parallelism model fits well with the operator-split chemistry integration that forms the basis of many reactive-flow codes

This provides significant motivation to provide the capability of evaluating analytical Jacobian matrices on GPUs as well as CPUs.

Thus, motivated by the potential cost reductions offered by analytical Jacobian matrix formulations, over the past five years a number of research groups developed analytical Jacobian generators for chemical kinetics, although as will be discussed the software package introduced here offers a number of improvements.

is the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind of degree
n-1
typically expressed as

\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{T}_{n-1}(x)=\cos\left((n-1)\arccos(x)\right)\quad\text{for }|x|\leq 1\;.

(41)

The reduced temperature
\tilde{T}
and pressure
\tilde{p}
are given by

\displaystyle\tilde{T}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2T^{-1}-T^{-1}_{\min}-T^{-1}_{\max}}{T^{-1}_{\max}-T^{-1}_{\min}}\quad\text{and}

(42)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{2\log_{10}p-\log_{10}p_{\min}-\log_{10}p_{\max}}{\log_{10}p_{\max}-\log_{10}p_{\min}}\;,

(43)

where
T_{\min}\leq T\leq T_{\max}
and
p_{\min}\leq p\leq p_{\max}
describe the ranges of validity for temperature and pressure.

3
Jacobian matrix formulation

Next, we detail the construction of the Jacobian matrix, and provide a simple method for evaluating it efficiently by calculating elements in the appropriate order.
More sophisticated approaches for reducing the cost of evaluating the matrix will be proposed in Section
.

3.1
Elements of the Jacobian matrix

Let
\mathcal{J}
denote the Jacobian matrix corresponding to the vector of ODEs given by Eq. (
6
).

\mathcal{J}
is filled by the partial derivatives
\partial f/\partial\Phi
, such that

\mathcal{J}_{i,j}=\frac{\partial f_{i}}{\partial\Phi_{j}}\;.

(44)

The first line of
\mathcal{J}
is filled with partial derivatives of the energy equation, or

\mathcal{J}_{1,j}=\frac{\partial\dot{T}}{\partial\Phi_{j}}\quad j=1,\dotsc,N_{\text{sp}}-1\;.

(45)

The components of
\mathcal{J}_{1,j}
are:

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial T}=\frac{-1}{c_{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial T}-h_{k}\frac{\partial c_{p}}{\partial T}\right)\frac{W_{k}\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}+\frac{h_{k}}{c_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{W_{k}\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}\right)\right],

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{1,j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{1}}{\partial Y_{j}}=\frac{-1}{c_{p}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial Y_{j}}-h_{k}\frac{\partial c_{p}}{\partial Y_{j}}\right)\frac{W_{k}\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}+\frac{h_{k}}{c_{p}}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}}\left(\frac{W_{k}\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}\right)\right],

(47)

for
j=1,\dotsc,N_{\text{sp}}-1
.

The remaining lines of
\mathcal{J}
are filled with the partial derivatives of the species equations, with the components

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k+1,1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k+1}}{\partial T}=\frac{W_{k}}{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\partial T}-\frac{\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial T}\right)\text{ and}

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{k+1,j+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{k+1}}{\partial Y_{j}}=\frac{W_{k}}{\rho}\left(\frac{\partial\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\partial Y_{j}}-\frac{\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\rho}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial Y_{j}}\right),

(49)

for
k=1,\dotsc,N_{\text{sp}}-1
and
j=1,\dotsc,N_{\text{sp}}-1
.

The partial derivatives of
\rho
,
c_{p}
,
h_{k}
, and
\dot{\omega}_{k}
with respect to temperature and mass fraction need to be evaluated.
The partial derivatives of density are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{pW}{\mathcal{R}T}\right)=-\frac{\rho}{T}\quad\text{and}

(50)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}}\left(\frac{pW}{\mathcal{R}T}\right)=-\rho W\left(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\right)\;.

(51)

The partial derivative of species specific enthalpy with respect to temperature is simply constant-pressure specific heat, and the partial derivative with respect to species mass fraction is zero:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=c_{p,k}

(52)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{k}}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=0\;.

(53)

The derivatives of specific heat are expressed by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial c_{p}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}Y_{k}\frac{\partial c_{p,k}}{\partial T}\;,

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial c_{p,k}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{R}}{W_{k}}\left(a_{1,k}+T\left(2a_{2,k}+T\left(3a_{3,k}+4a_{4,k}T\right)\right)\right)\;,\text{ and}

(55)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial c_{p}}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}Y_{k}c_{p,k}=c_{p,j}-c_{p,N_{\text{sp}}}\;.

(56)

Next, the derivatives of species rate-of-production
\dot{\omega}_{k}
are given by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{reac}}}\nu_{ki}\frac{\partial q_{i}}{\partial T}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{reac}}}\nu_{ki}\left(\frac{\partial c_{i}}{\partial T}R_{i}+c_{i}\frac{\partial R_{i}}{\partial T}\right)\text{ and}

(57)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\dot{\omega}_{k}}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{reac}}}\nu_{ki}\frac{\partial q_{i}}{\partial Y_{j}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{\text{reac}}}\nu_{ki}\left(\frac{\partial c_{i}}{\partial Y_{j}}R_{i}+c_{i}\frac{\partial R_{i}}{\partial Y_{j}}\right)\;.

(58)

The partial derivatives of
R_{i}
vary depending on whether the reaction is reversible.
For irreversible reactions,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{f,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial k_{f,i}}{\partial T}\frac{1}{k_{f,i}}R_{f,i}+k_{f,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime}}\right)\;\text{and}

(59)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{f,i}}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{f,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime}}\right)\;.

(60)

The partial derivatives of species concentration are

\displaystyle\frac{\partial[X_{i}]}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{i}}{W_{i}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial T}=-\frac{[X_{i}]}{T}

(61)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial[X_{i}]}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Y_{i}}{W_{i}}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial Y_{j}}+\frac{\rho}{W_{i}}\frac{\partial Y_{i}}{\partial Y_{j}}=-[X_{i}]W\left(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\right)+\left(\delta_{ij}-\delta_{iN_{\text{sp}}}\right)\frac{\rho}{W_{i}}\;,

(62)

where
i=1,\ldots,N_{\text{sp}}
,
j=1,\ldots,N_{\text{sp}}-1
, and
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker delta.
The partial derivatives of the molar concentration product terms are then

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{T}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime}}\right)\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ji}^{\prime}\quad\text{ and}

(63)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial Y_{j}}\left(\prod_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ki}^{\prime}\Biggl(-[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime}}W\left(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\right)

(64)

\displaystyle+\>\left(\delta_{kj}-\delta_{kN_{\text{sp}}}\right)\frac{\rho}{W_{k}}[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime}-1}\Biggr)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{l}]^{\nu_{li}^{\prime}}\;,

and the partial derivative of forward reaction rate coefficient is

\displaystyle\frac{\partial k_{f,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{f,i}}{T}\left(\beta_{i}+\frac{T_{a,i}}{T}\right)\;.

(65)

Inserting these into Eqs. (
59
) and (
60
) gives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{f,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{f,i}}{T}\left(\beta_{i}+\frac{T_{a,i}}{T}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ji}^{\prime}\right)\text{ and}

(66)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{f,i}}{\partial Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ki}^{\prime}\Biggl(-R_{f,i}W\left(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\right)

(67)

\displaystyle+\>\left(\delta_{kj}-\delta_{kN_{\text{sp}}}\right)k_{f,i}\frac{\rho}{W_{k}}[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{l}]^{\nu_{li}^{\prime}}\Biggr)\;.

For reversible reactions with explicit reverse Arrhenius coefficients,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial k_{f,i}}{\partial T}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime}}+k_{f,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime}}\right)

(68)

\displaystyle-\>\frac{\partial k_{r,i}}{\partial T}\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime\prime}}-k_{r,i}\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\prod_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{j}]^{\nu_{ji}^{\prime\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{R_{f,i}}{T}\left(\beta_{f,i}+\frac{T_{af,i}}{T}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ji}^{\prime}\right)-\frac{R_{r,i}}{T}\left(\beta_{r,i}+\frac{T_{ar,i}}{T}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ji}^{\prime\prime}\right)\;.

(69)

Similarly,
\frac{1}{[X_{j}]}\left(\nu_{ji}^{\prime}R_{f,i}-\nu_{ji}^{\prime\prime}R_{r,i}\right)
should be avoided since
[X_{j}]
could be zero.

The partial derivative of the reverse reaction rate coefficient, when evaluated using the forward rate coefficient and equilibrium constant using Eq. (

\displaystyle\frac{\partial k_{r,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial T}\left(\frac{k_{f,i}}{K_{c,i}}\right)=\frac{\frac{\partial k_{f,i}}{\partial T}K_{c,i}-\frac{\partial K_{c,i}}{\partial T}k_{f,i}}{K_{c,i}^{2}}

(70)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{f,i}}{K_{c,i}}\frac{1}{T}\left(\beta_{i}+\frac{T_{a,i}}{T}\right)-\frac{1}{K_{c,i}}\frac{\partial K_{c,i}}{\partial T}\frac{k_{f,i}}{K_{c,i}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial K_{c,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K_{c,i}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ki}\frac{\partial B_{k}}{\partial T}

(72)

\displaystyle\therefore\frac{\partial k_{r,i}}{\partial T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k_{r,i}\left(\frac{1}{T}\left(\beta_{i}+\frac{T_{a,i}}{T}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ki}\frac{\partial B_{k}}{\partial T}\right)\;,

(73)

where

\frac{\partial B_{k}}{\partial T}=\frac{1}{T}\left(a_{0,k}-1+\frac{a_{5,k}}{T}\right)+\frac{a_{1,k}}{2}+T\biggl(\frac{a_{2,k}}{3}+T\left(\frac{a_{3,k}}{4}+\frac{a_{4,k}}{5}T\right)\biggr)\;.

(74)

\displaystyle\frac{R_{f,i}}{T}\left(\beta_{i}+\frac{T_{a,i}}{T}-\sum_{j=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\nu_{ji}^{\prime}\right)

For all reversible reactions,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial R_{i}}{\partial Y_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{\text{sp}}}\Biggl(\nu_{ki}^{\prime}\biggl(

\displaystyle-R_{f,i}W\Bigl(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\Bigr)

(76)

l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{l}]^{\nu_{li}^{\prime}}\biggr)

\displaystyle-\>\nu_{ki}^{\prime\prime}\biggl(-R_{r,i}W\Bigl(\frac{1}{W_{j}}-\frac{1}{W_{N_{\text{sp}}}}\Bigr)

\displaystyle+\>\left(\delta_{kj}-\delta_{kN_{\text{sp}}}\right)k_{r,i}\frac{\rho}{W_{k}}[X_{k}]^{\nu_{ki}^{\prime\prime}-1}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{N_{\text{sp}}}[X_{l}]^{\nu_{li}^{\prime\prime}}\biggr)\Biggr)\;.

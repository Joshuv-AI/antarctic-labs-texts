Positive supersolutions for the Lane-Emden system with inverse-square potentials


Abstract
In this paper, we study the nonexistence of positive supersolutions for the following Lane-Emden system with inverse-square potentials { − Δ ​ u + μ 1 | x | 2 ​ u = v p in ​ Ω ∖ { 0 } , − Δ ​ v + μ 2 | x | 2 ​ v = u q in ​ Ω ∖ { 0 } \left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}-\Delta u+\frac{\mu_{1}}{|x|^{2}}u=v^{p}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt] -\Delta v+\frac{\mu_{2}}{|x|^{2}}v=u^{q}\quad{\rm in}\ \,\Omega\setminus\{0\}\end{array}\right. (0.1) for suitable p , q > 0 p,q>0 , μ 1 , μ 2 ≥ − ( N − 2 ) 2 / 4 \mu_{1},\mu_{2}\geq-(N-2)^{2}/4 , where Ω \Omega is a smooth bounded domain containing the origin in ℝ N \mathbb{R}^{N} with N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . Precisely, we provide sharp supercritical regions of ( p , q ) (p,q) for the nonexistence of positive supersolutions to system ( 0.1 ) in the cases − ( N − 2 ) 2 / 4 ≤ μ 1 , μ 2 < 0 -(N-2)^{2}/4\leq\mu_{1},\mu_{2}<0 and − ( N − 2 ) 2 / 4 ≤ μ 1 < 0 ≤ μ 2 -(N-2)^{2}/4\leq\mu_{1}<0\leq\mu_{2} . Due to the negative coefficients μ 1 , μ 2 \mu_{1},\mu_{2} of the inverse-square potentials, an initial blowing-up at the origin could be derived and an iteration procedure could be applied in the supercritical case to improve the blowing-up rate until the nonlinearities are not admissible in some weighted L 1 L^{1} spaces. In the subcritical case, we prove the existence of positive supersolutions for system ( 1.1 ) by specific radially symmetric functions.

Introduction and main results

Let the integer
N\geq 3
,
\mu\geq\mu_{0}:=-\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
and the Hardy operator be defined
\mathcal{L}_{\mu}=-\Delta+\mu|x|^{-2}
. Our concern of this paper is to consider nonexistence of positive supersolutions for Lane-Emden system

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1}}u=v^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{2}}v=u^{q}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
\mu_{1},\,\mu_{2}\geq\mu_{0}
,
p,\,q>0
,
\Omega
is a bounded
C^{2}
domain containing the origin in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

It is known that Hardy operator is related to the classical Hardy inequality stated as following: for a smooth bounded domain
\mathcal{O}
in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing the origin, there holds

\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\geq c_{N}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|x|^{-2}|u|^{2}dx,\quad\forall\,u\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathcal{O})

with the best constant
c_{N}=\frac{(N-2)^{2}}{4}
. The qualitative properties of Hardy inequality and its improved versions have been studied extensively, see for example
[
1
,
4
,
16
,
18
]
, motivated by great applications in the study of semilinear elliptic Hardy problem by variational method, see
[
12
,
13
,
15
]
and the reference therein. Due to the inverse-square potentials, the related semilinear elliptic and parabolic equations appear various peculiar phenomena and attract great attentions, such as parabolic equations
[
5
,
20
,
21
]
, singular solutions of Hardy problem with absorption nonlinearity
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
19
]
, Hardy problems with source nonlinearities
[
3
,
7
,
14
,
17
]
.

When
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
, the homogeneous problem

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\{0\}

(1.2)

has radially symmetric solutions with the formula that

\Phi_{\mu}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}&{\rm if}\quad\mu>\mu_{0}\\
|x|^{\tau_{-}(\mu)}(-\ln|x|)&{\rm if}\quad\mu=\mu_{0}\end{array}\right.\quad{\rm and}\quad\Gamma_{\mu}(x)=|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)},

(1.3)

where

\tau_{\pm}(\mu)=-(N-2)/2\,\pm\sqrt{\mu-\mu_{0}}

(1.4)

are two roots of
\mu-\tau(\tau+N-2)=0
. Note that
\tau_{+}(\mu)<0
if
\mu\in[\mu_{0},\,0)
, and any positive solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=u^{p}&{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\},\\[4.2679pt]
\quad\ \,u=0&{\rm on}\quad\partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(1.5)

blows up at least like
\Gamma_{\mu}
at the origin. Inversely, for the existence of positive solutions, the nonlinearity
u^{p}
must have an admissible singularity in some weighted
L^{1}
space. Inspired by this observation,
[
3
,
13
]
show that letting
p^{*}_{\mu}=1+\frac{2}{-\tau_{+}(\mu)}
for
\mu\in[\mu_{0},\,0)
, semilinear problem (
1.5
)
has no any positive solution for
p\geq p^{*}_{\mu}
. This nonexistence of (
1.5
) is
specific to Hardy problem with
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
.

Our interest in this paper is to obtain the nonexistence of positive supersolutions of (
1.1
) when at least one parameter of
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
is
in
[\mu_{0},0)
. Here a functions’ pair
(u,v)
is a positive supersolution of Lane-Emden system (
1.1
), if
(u,v)
satisfies the inequalities

\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{1}}u(x)\geq v(x)^{p}\quad{\rm and}\quad\mathcal{L}_{\mu_{2}}v(x)\geq u(x)^{q}\quad{\rm for\ any\ }x\in\Omega\setminus\{0\}.

(1.6)

The nonexistence results state as follows.

2
Preliminary results

Let us start this section with the Hardy problem (
1.7
). For
\mu_{0}\leq\mu<0
,

\Phi_{\mu}
is a regular solution of
(
1.5
) in the sense that
\mu|\cdot|^{-2}\Phi_{\mu}(\cdot)\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\Phi_{\mu}=0\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}\xi\,dx=0,\quad\forall\,\xi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

This normally distributional sense fails to express the singularity of
\Phi_{\mu}
. Furthermore, it even does not hold for
\Phi_{\mu}
by the strong singularity for
\mu>0
large.

To overcome this difficulty, the authors in
[
6
,
8
]
introduced a new distributional framework: function
\Phi_{\mu}
is a distributional solution of

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=c_{\mu}\delta_{0}\quad{\rm in}\quad\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\Phi_{\mu}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}\xi\,d\gamma_{\mu}=c_{\mu}\xi(0),\quad\forall\,\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

where
\delta_{0}
is Dirac mass at the origin,

d\gamma_{\mu}(x)=\Gamma_{\mu}(x)dx
,

\mathcal{L}^{*}_{\mu}=-\Delta-2\frac{\tau_{+}(\mu)}{|x|^{2}}\,x\cdot\nabla,

and
c_{\mu}>0
is the normalized constant.
With the help of this new distributional framework, the authors also obtain the classification of isolated singular
solutions for non-homogeneous Hardy problem (
1.7
).

Theorem 2.1

[
6
, Theorem 1.3]

Let
f
be a function in
C^{\gamma}_{loc}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\{0\})
for some
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

(I) Assume that

\int_{\Omega}|f|\,d\gamma_{\mu}<+\infty,

(2.1)

then problem (
1.7
), subject to
\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=k
with
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
has a unique solution
u_{k}
, which satisfies the distributional identity

\int_{\Omega}u_{k}\mathcal{L}_{\mu}^{*}(\xi)\,d\gamma_{\mu}=\int_{\Omega}f\xi\,d\gamma_{\mu}+c_{\mu}k\xi(0),\quad\forall\,\xi\in C^{1.1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.2)

(II) Assume that
f
verifies (
2.1
) and
u
is a nonnegative solution of (
1.7
), then
u
satisfies (
2.2
) for some
k\geq 0

and verifies that

\lim_{|x|\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=k.

(III) Assume that
f\geq 0
and

\lim_{r\to 0^{+}}\int_{\Omega\setminus B_{r}(0)}f\,d\gamma_{\mu}=+\infty,

(2.3)

then problem (
1.7
) has no nonnegative solution.

In order to obtain the nonexistence of positive supersolution of system (
1.1
), we need the following basic tools: comparison principle and basic estimate of singularities at the origin.

Lemma 2.1

Assume that
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
f_{1}\geq f_{2}
,
k_{1}\geq k_{2}
, functions
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
satisfy that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u_{1}\geq f_{1}\qquad{\rm in}\quad{\Omega}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\liminf_{|x|\to 0}u_{1}(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)\geq k_{1}\end{array}\right.

and

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u_{2}\leq f_{2}\qquad{\rm in}\quad{\Omega}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to 0}u_{2}(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)\leq k_{2}.\end{array}\right.

If
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
, then

u_{1}\geq u_{2}\quad{\rm in}\quad\Omega\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.
Under the assumptions,
w:=u_{1}-u_{2}
satisfies that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}w\geq 0\qquad{\rm in}\quad{\Omega}\setminus\{0\},\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{\mathcal{L}_{\mu}}w\geq 0\qquad{\rm on}\quad{\partial\Omega}\\[4.2679pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\liminf_{|x|\to 0}w(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)\geq 0.\end{array}\right.

By applying
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
, we obtain that
w\geq 0
and the proof ends.
\Box

Lemma 2.2

Assume that
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
r_{0}\in(0,1]
is such that
B_{2r_{0}}(0)\subset\Omega
and nonnegative function
u\in C^{1.1}(\Omega\setminus\{0\})
is a classical solution of (
1.7
) with
f\gneqq 0
.

Then
u
is positive in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and for some
c_{1}>0
,

u(x)\geq c_{1}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)},\quad\forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\}.

(2.4)

Proof.
Note that
u
is positive in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
by strong Maximum Principle, then

u(x)\geq c_{1}
on
\partial B_{r_{0}}(0)
and

\liminf_{|x|\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}(x)^{-1}\geq 0.

By the compactness of
\partial B_{r_{0}}(0)
, there exist
c_{2}>0
such that

u\geq c_{2}\Gamma_{\mu}\quad{\rm on}\ \,\partial B_{r_{0}}(0),

thus by Lemma
2.1
, we have that
u\geq c_{2}\Gamma_{\mu}
in
B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\}
, that is (
2.4
).
\Box

Lemma 2.3

Assume that
\mu\geq\mu_{0}
,
f
is a nonnegative continuous function in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and verifies that

f(x)\geq c_{3}|x|^{\tau-2},\quad\forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\},

(2.5)

where
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
c_{3}\geq 1
.

If
\tau\leq\tau_{-}(\mu)
, then

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mu}u=f\qquad{\rm in}\quad\ {\Omega}\setminus\{0\},\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{L_{\mu}\,}\displaystyle u=0\qquad{\rm on}\quad\ \partial{\Omega},\\[2.84526pt]
\phantom{}\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\to 0}u(x)\Phi_{\mu}^{-1}(x)=0\end{array}\right.

(2.6)

admits no positive solution

If
\tau>\tau_{-}(\mu)
and
f\in L^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu})
, problem (
2.6
) admits a unique solution
u_{f}
, which is positive and has following asymptotic behavior:

for
\tau_{-}(\mu)<\tau<\tau_{+}(\mu)
,

u_{f}(x)\geq c_{4}|x|^{\tau},\ \ \forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\};

(2.7)

for
\tau=\tau_{+}(\mu)
,

u_{f}(x)\geq c_{4}|x|^{\tau}(-\ln|x|),\ \ \forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\};

(2.8)

and for
\tau>\tau_{+}(\mu)
,

u_{f}(x)\geq c_{4}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)},\ \ \forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\},

(2.9)

where

.

Proof.
The nonexistence for
\tau\leq\tau_{-}(\mu)
and existence for
\tau>\tau_{-}(\mu)
follow directly from Theorem
2.1
. For asymptotic behavior at the origin, we shall construct suitable bounds for
u_{f}
. In the proof of this lemma, we assume that

r_{0}=1
, otherwise we just do a scaling to re-normalized it.

For
\tau_{-}(\mu)<\tau<\tau_{+}(\mu)
, we have that

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}|x|^{\tau}=c_{\tau}|x|^{\tau-2},

where
c_{\tau}>0
. From (

Then by Lemma

t_{1}(|x|^{\tau}-|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)})
in

.

For

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}(-\ln|x|))=(2\tau_{+}(\mu)+N-2)|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu)}.

The lower bound could be constructed by adjusting the coefficient
t
of

For

\mathcal{L}_{\mu}(|x|^{\tau})=(\mu-\tau(\tau+N-2))|x|^{\tau-2},

The proof is thus completed.
\Box

3
Nonexistence in sharp supercritical regions

3.1
Preliminary nonexistence property

For
\mu\in[\mu_{0},0)
, we note that
\Gamma_{\mu}
defined in (
1.3
) blows up at
the origin, which may cause that
u^{q}\not\in L^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu_{2}})
or

v^{p}\not\in L^{1}(\Omega,d\gamma_{\mu_{1}})
, and then a contradiction appears by (
2.3
) in Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 3.1

(i)
Let
\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1},\mu_{2}<0
, and
p,q>0
satisfy

{\rm either}\qquad p\geq\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})}{-\tau_{+}(\mu_{2})}\quad{\rm or}\quad q\geq\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu_{2})}{-\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})},

(3.1)

then system (
1.1
) admits no positive supersolution.

(ii)
Let
\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}<0\leq\mu_{2}
and
p,q
satisfy one of the following conditions:

\begin{array}[]{lll}(a)\qquad q\geq\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu_{2})}{-\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})};\\[17.07164pt]
(b)\qquad\frac{2}{-\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})}<q<\frac{N+\tau_{+}(\mu_{2})}{-\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})}\quad{\rm and}\quad\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})(pq+1)+2p+N\leq 0,\end{array}

(3.2)

then system (
1.1
) admits no positive supersolution.

Proof.
By contradiction, we assume that
(u,v)
is a positive solutions’ pair of system (
1.1
), then by Lemma
2.2
,
u,v
are positive and

u(x)\geq c_{5}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu_{1})}\ \ {\rm and}\ \ v(x)\geq c_{5}|x|^{\tau_{+}(\mu_{2})},\quad\forall\,x\in B_{r_{0}}(0)\setminus\{0\},

Capacity Bounds for Vertically-Drifted First Arrival Position Channels under a Covariance Constraint
Yun-Feng Lo, Yen-Chi Lee, Min-Hsiu Hsieh

Abstract
In this paper, we delve into the capacity problem of additive vertically-drifted first arrival position noise channel, which models a communication system where the position of molecules is harnessed to convey information. Drawing inspiration from the principles governing vector Gaussian interference channels, we examine this capacity problem within the context of a covariance constraint on input distributions. We offer analytical upper and lower bounds on this capacity for a three-dimensional spatial setting. This is achieved through a meticulous analysis of the characteristic function coupled with an investigation into the stability properties. The results of this study contribute to the ongoing effort to understand the fundamental limits of molecular communication systems.

First arrival models for absorbing receivers can be roughly categorized into three types: time modulation, position modulation, and joint position-time modulation. System Model

Figure 1:

This figure depicts a 3D MC system where both the transmitter and receiver have plane shapes. The communication takes place in a fluid medium with a vertical drift

In this paper, we investigate a diffusion-based molecular communication system, which employs the emission positions of MMs in a
D
-dimensional fluid medium to convey information. In practice,
\text{D}=2,3
are commonly used.
We consider a scenario where there is a constant drift
[
6
]
in the ambient space. The diffusion effects of MMs are captured by the diffusion coefficient
D=\sigma^{2}/2

[

,
13
]

Our MC system comprises a transmitter (Tx) and a receiver (Rx), which are modeled as parallel
D
-dimensional hyperplanes (hereafter referred to as

) separated by a distance
\lambda>0
. Without loss of generality, we can set the transmitter plane at coordinate
x_{\text{D}}=\lambda

x_{\text{D}}=0

In this study, we assume an ideal MC system that satisfies the following assumptions:

•

The transmitter has perfect control over the emission position of the molecules.

•

The transmitter plane is transparent, allowing MMs to move through it without experiencing any force after they are released.

•

The receiver can perfectly measure the first arrival positions of the molecules.

•

Upon first arrival at the receiver plane, molecules are captured and removed from the system.

•

The movement of every molecule is independent.

The positional information is
d
-dim, where
d=\text{D}-1

\boldsymbol{Y}
of a molecule released at position
\boldsymbol{X}
can be expressed as
\boldsymbol{Y}=\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{N}
, where
\boldsymbol{N}
denotes the deviation of the first arrival position. The probability density function of

is known as the

[

,
12
]

In order to express the FAP density, we denote the
D
-dim drift vector
\mathbf{v}=[v_{1},\ldots,v_{\text{D}}]^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}

\mathbf{v}=[\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{par}}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}},v_{\text{D}}]^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}

\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{par}}:=[v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}]^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}

is the drift component perpendicular to the transmitter and receiver planes. As a rule of thumb, we use bold fonts to stand for column vectors; slanted vectors are
d

D
-dim. The operator

We also introduce the notation

\mathbf{u}:=\frac{\mathbf{v}}{\sigma^{2}}

and similarly for
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{par}}
and

. These

In

12

14
]
, the 2D and 3D FAP densities were separately derived based on the above listed system assumptions.
By the methodology proposed in
[
13
]
, we can unify the two FAP densities (i.e.,
\text{D}=2,3
) into a single expression:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&f^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{n})\\
=\penalty\ &2\lambda\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{d+1}e^{\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{par}}^{\intercal}\boldsymbol{n}-u_{\text{D}}\lambda}\frac{K_{\tfrac{d+1}{2}}\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|\sqrt{\norm{\bs{n}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)}{\left(\|\mathbf{u}\|\sqrt{\norm{\bs{n}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)^{\tfrac{d+1}{2}}},\end{split}

(3)

where
K_{\nu}(\cdot)
denotes the order-
\nu

[
16

\|\cdot\|
denotes the Euclidean norm.

This paper focuses on a sub-family of FAP distributions with two specific properties:

1.

The

components are zero, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{v}_{\text{par}}=\boldsymbol{0}
.

2.

The
vertical drift
(VD) component points from the Tx to the Rx, i.e.,
v_{\text{D}}<0
. (Intuitively, this vertical drift helps the transmission of information.)

This sub-family is referred to as Vertically-Drifted First Arrival Position (VDFAP) distributions because only the vertical drift component is non-zero. We can set
\boldsymbol{u}_{\text{par}}=\boldsymbol{0}
so that
\|\mathbf{u}\|

3

|u_{\text{D}}|=-u_{\text{D}}
.
Abbreviating

\displaystyle\begin{split}f^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{n})=2\lambda\left(\frac{|u|}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\right)^{d+1}e^{\lambda|u|}\frac{K_{\tfrac{d+1}{2}}\left(|u|\sqrt{\norm{\bs{n}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)}{\left(|u|\sqrt{\norm{\bs{n}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)^{\tfrac{d+1}{2}}}.\end{split}

(4)

We can thus view
u
as a parameter of VDFAP distribution.
As a shorthand, we denote
\boldsymbol{X}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda)
if a
d
-dim random vector
\boldsymbol{X}
follows the VDFAP distribution defined by (
4
) with parameters
u<0
and
\lambda>0
.

Note that we refer to a random variable
X\in\mathbb{R}
as a “one-dimensional random vector” to maintain consistency in terminology.

III
The Characteristic Function of VDFAP Distribution

From probability theory, distributions can be characterized by its
characteristic function
(CF).
The CF viewpoint facilitates the computation of moments and the analysis of stability properties for VDFAP distributions.

For a random vector
\boldsymbol{N}
following
\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda)
, we denote its CF as

\displaystyle\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}):=\mathbb{E}[\exp(i\bs{\omega}^\tsp\bs{N})],\text{\ for\ }\boldsymbol{\omega}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(5)

We have derived a novel closed-form expression for (
5
) when
d=1,2
as detailed in Appendix
A
. The resulting closed-form expression for the CF (
5
) is

\displaystyle\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega};u,\lambda)=\exp\left(-\lambda\left(\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}-|u|\right)\right),

(6)

where we have included the parameters
u,\lambda
to emphasize the dependency.

It is important to note that letting
|u|\to 0
in the CF formula (
6
) recovers the
d
-dim Cauchy CF
[
17
,
18
]
:

\displaystyle\lim_{|u|\to 0}\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega};u,\lambda)=\exp\left(-\lambda\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|\right).

(7)

The parameters
u,\lambda
will be suppressed hereafter when the context allows for clear understanding.
The observation in (
7
) is consistent with
[
19
]
, which indicates that as the drift approaches zero, the density of FAP distribution given by (
3
) reduces to a Cauchy density.

Via the closed-form CF expression (
6
), we can obtain the first two moments, as well as a weak stability property of VDFAP distributions.

III-A

Mean Vector and Covariance Matrix

Based on (
5
), the mean vector of
\boldsymbol{N}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda)
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{N}]=-i\nabla\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\omega}=\boldsymbol{0}},

(8)

and the correlation matrix of
\boldsymbol{N}
as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]=-\nabla^{2}\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\bigg|_{\boldsymbol{\omega}=\boldsymbol{0}}.

(9)

Due to the radial symmetry of
\boldsymbol{N}
, its mean is zero, and thus the correlation matrix coincides with the covariance matrix.
We first calculate the gradient of the CF:

\displaystyle\nabla\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})=\frac{-\lambda\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}}\boldsymbol{\omega}.

(10)

Then we can proceed from (
10
) to calculate the Hessian of the CF:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla^{2}\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})&=\frac{-\lambda\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}}\mathbb{I}_{d}\\
&\penalty\ \penalty\ +\frac{\lambda\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})}{(\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2})^{\frac{3}{2}}}\left(1+\lambda\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}\right)\boldsymbol{\omega}\boldsymbol{\omega}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}},\end{split}

(11)

where
\mathbb{I}_{d}
denotes the
d\times d
identity matrix.
Hence, the covariance matrix of
\boldsymbol{N}
can be expressed as:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]=\frac{\lambda}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{d}.

(12)

It is interesting to note that the variance of each component of a random vector
\boldsymbol{N}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda)
is
\frac{\lambda}{|u|}
. Also, the components of
\boldsymbol{N}
are pairwise uncorrelated, but not independent, as the PDF (
4
) cannot be decomposed as the product of its marginal PDFs.

III-B

A Weak Stability Property

Cauchy distributions, along with Gaussian and Lévy distributions, are known to be examples of
stable
distributions. These distributions possess a specific form of characteristic function (CF)
[
20
]
, which is not satisfied by (
6
). Therefore, we can conclude that VDFAP distributions are
not
stable. However, despite not being strictly stable, VDFAP distributions still exhibit certain “weaker” stability properties.

One of the weaker stability properties of VDFAP distributions is that if
\boldsymbol{N}_{1}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda_{1})
and
\boldsymbol{N}_{2}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda_{2})
are two independent VDFAP random vectors with the same (normalized) drift
u
, then their sum
\boldsymbol{N}_{1}+\boldsymbol{N}_{2}
follows the
\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
distribution.

To prove this property, note that
\boldsymbol{N}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{N}_{2}
are independent. Therefore, the CF of their sum is the product of their respective CFs, i.e.,

\mathbb{E}[\exp(i\boldsymbol{\omega}^\tsp(\bs{N}_1+\bs{N}_2))]=\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}_{1}}(\boldsymbol{\omega})\Phi^{(d)}_{\boldsymbol{N}_{2}}(\boldsymbol{\omega}).

Using the CF formula (
6
) for
\boldsymbol{N}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{N}_{2}
, we obtain:

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}[\exp(i\boldsymbol{\omega}^\tsp(\bs{N}_1+\bs{N}_2))]\\
=\penalty\ &\exp\left(-\lambda_{1}\left(\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}-|u|\right)\right)\\
&\cdot\exp\left(-\lambda_{2}\left(\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}-|u|\right)\right)\\
=\penalty\ &\exp\left(-\left(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}\right)\left(\sqrt{\|\boldsymbol{\omega}\|^{2}+|u|^{2}}-|u|\right)\right).\end{split}

(13)

Since the CF uniquely characterizes the distribution, we can compare this result to the CF formula (
6
) again to conclude that
\boldsymbol{N}_{1}+\boldsymbol{N}_{2}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(d)}(u,\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2})
.

This
weak stability property
will be useful in deriving a lower bound for the capacity of VDFAP channels.

Borrowing the wisdom from vector Gaussian interference channels

[

,
21
]

as follows:

\displaystyle\begin{split}C=\sup_{f(\boldsymbol{X}):\penalty\ \mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]\preceq\Sigma}I(\boldsymbol{X};\boldsymbol{Y}),\end{split}

(14)

where
\Sigma
is a prescribed

Note that the channel output
\boldsymbol{Y}
equals to
\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{N}
, where the noise
\boldsymbol{N}
follows
\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda)
distribution, and is

\boldsymbol{X}
. The capacity appeared in (

.

Applying the additive channel structure

, equation (

\displaystyle\begin{split}C=\sup_{f(\boldsymbol{X}):\penalty\ \mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]\preceq\Sigma}h(\boldsymbol{Y})-h(\boldsymbol{N}),\end{split}

(15)

where
h(\cdot)
denotes the differential entropy.
We have calculated the differential entropy of a random vector
\boldsymbol{N}
following
\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda)
in closed-form:

\displaystyle\begin{split}h(\boldsymbol{N})&=\log\left(2\pi e^{3}\right)+2\log(\lambda)-\log(1+\lambda|u|)\\
&\penalty\ \penalty\ -\lambda|u|e^{\lambda|u|}\big(e\cdot\text{Ei}(-1-\lambda|u|)-3\cdot\text{Ei}(-\lambda|u|)\big),\end{split}

(16)

where the exponential integral function
\text{Ei}(\cdot)
is defined as
[
16
]
:

\displaystyle\text{Ei}(x):=-\int_{-x}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}\ \mathrm{d}t,\text{\ for\ }x<0.

(17)

The calculation of (
16
) is given in Appendix
B

IV-A

Lower Bound

Using the weak stability property of VDFAP distributions proved in
III-B
, we obtain a lower bound on the capacity (
14
) of the VDFAP channel:

\displaystyle C(u,\lambda,\Sigma)

\displaystyle\geq 2\log\Big( 1 + \tfrac{|u|}{\lambda}\sigma_\text{min} \Big)-\log\left(1+\tfrac{|u|^{2}}{1+\lambda|u|}\sigma_{\text{min}}\right)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ +\lambda|u|e^{\lambda|u|}\big(e\cdot\text{Ei}(-1-\lambda|u|)-3\cdot\text{Ei}(-\lambda|u|)\big)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ -\Big(\lambda+|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}\Big)|u|\exp\left\{\left(\lambda+|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}\right)|u|\right\}

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \times\bigg\{e\cdot\text{Ei}\Big(-1-\big(\lambda+|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}\big)|u|\Big)

\displaystyle\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ -3\cdot\text{Ei}\Big(-\big(\lambda+|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}\big)|u|\Big)\bigg\}

(18)

\displaystyle>0,

where
\sigma_{\text{min}}
denotes the minimum eigenvalue of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

To obtain a lower bound on the capacity, we pick particular input distributions
f(\boldsymbol{X})
satisfying the second-moment constraint
\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]\preceq\Sigma
, calculate
h(\boldsymbol{Y})-h(\boldsymbol{N})
, and then optimize the result via taking supremum.

Take
\boldsymbol{X}\sim\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda^{\prime})
for some positive
\lambda^{\prime}\leq|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}
. By (
12
), we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]=\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{2}\preceq\sigma_{\text{min}}\mathbb{I}_{2}\preceq\Sigma,

so
\boldsymbol{X}
satisfies the
covariance constraint.
Also, by weak stability property in
III-B
,
\boldsymbol{Y}=\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{N}
follows
\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})
. Then, a lower bound
C\geq h(\boldsymbol{Y})-h(\boldsymbol{N})
follows from (
15
).

To analyze this lower bound, we introduce an ancillary function
h_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{0}(s)&:=2\log(s)-\log(1+s)\\
&\quad-se^{s}\left(e\cdot\text{Ei}(-1-s)-3\cdot\text{Ei}(-s)\right),\text{\ for\ }s>0,\end{split}

(19)

so that the differential entropy (
16
) of a random vector
\boldsymbol{Z}
, which follows
\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda^{\prime\prime})
, can be written as

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{Z})=h_{0}(|u|\lambda^{\prime\prime})+\log(2\pi e^3)-2\log(|u|).

(20)

Therefore, the lower bound can be expressed via the function
h_{0}(\cdot)
as

C\geq h_{0}(|u|(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}))-h_{0}(|u|\lambda),

which holds for any
0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}
. Hence, we can maximize over
\lambda^{\prime}
to get
that

\displaystyle C\geq\sup_{0<\lambda^{\prime}\leq|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}}h_{0}(|u|(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime}))-h_{0}(|u|\lambda).

(21)

It is shown in Appendix
C
that
h_{0}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing.

Therefore, (
21
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle C\geq h_{0}\bigg(|u|\Big(\lambda+|u|\sigma_{\text{min}}\Big)\bigg)-h_{0}(|u|\lambda).

(22)

Plugging (
19
) into (
22
) yields (
18
).
The lower bound in (
22
), and hence (
18
), is strictly positive due to the two facts that
h_{0}(\cdot)
is strictly increasing, and that
\sigma_{\text{min}}
is strictly positive as
\Sigma\succ 0
.
∎

IV-B

Upper Bound

It is well-established in the literature that
the differential entropy is maximized by multivariate Gaussian
under a prescribed covariance matrix

[
22
, Chapter 12]
,
[
23
]
.
This fact can be applied to establish an upper bound on the capacity. Under the constraint in (
14
), the bound can be expressed as:

\displaystyle C(u,\lambda,\Sigma)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\log\left(\det\Big(
\tfrac{1}{\lambda^2}\Sigma+
\tfrac{1}{\lambda|u|}
\mathbb{I}_2
\Big)\right)+\log(1+\lambda|u|)

\displaystyle\quad+\lambda|u|e^{\lambda|u|}\left(e\cdot\text{Ei}(-1-\lambda|u|)-3\cdot\text{Ei}(-\lambda|u|)\right)

\displaystyle\quad-2,

(23)

where
\det(\cdot)
denotes the determinant.

Proof.

Because
\boldsymbol{X}
and
\boldsymbol{N}
are independent and that
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{N}]=\boldsymbol{0}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{Y}\boldsymbol{Y}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\penalty\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{N}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{N}\right)^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]

\displaystyle=\penalty\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}+\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}+\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}+\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(a)}{=}\penalty\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\right]^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}+\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\right]\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\right]^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}+\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(b)}{=}\penalty\

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{N}\boldsymbol{N}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{(c)}{\preceq}\penalty\

\displaystyle\Sigma+\tfrac{\lambda}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{2},

(24)

where (a) is due to independence, (b) is due to
\mathbb{E}[\boldsymbol{N}]=\boldsymbol{0}
, and (c) is due to the constraint
\mathbb{E}\left[\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{X}^{\mathsf{\scriptstyle{T}}}\right]\preceq\Sigma

and (
12
).

Under the
covariance constraint
(
24
) on the output
\boldsymbol{Y}
, its differential entropy
h(\boldsymbol{Y})
is maximized when
\boldsymbol{Y}
follows the bivariate Gaussian distribution
\mathcal{N}\Big(\mathbf{0},\Sigma+\tfrac{\lambda}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{2}\Big)
.
Hence, from (
15
) we have

\displaystyle C\leq h\bigg(\mathcal{N}\Big(\mathbf{0},\Sigma+\tfrac{\lambda}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{2}\Big)\bigg)-h\left(\text{VDFAP}^{(2)}(u,\lambda)\right).

(25)

Since the differential entropy of the bivariate Gaussian is

\displaystyle h\bigg(\mathcal{N}\Big(\mathbf{0},\Sigma+\tfrac{\lambda}{|u|}\mathbb{I}_{2}\Big)\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\log\Big(
(2\pi e)^2
\det\big(
\Sigma+
\tfrac{\lambda}{|u|}
\mathbb{I}_2
\big)
\Big),

(26)

together with (
16
) we can simplify (
25

23
).
∎

V
Conclusion

The concept of First Arrival Position (FAP) channels has been recently introduced in the field of diffusive molecular communication. This paper has advanced the understanding of this novel concept by addressing the capacity problem of additive vertically-drifted (VD) FAP noise channels, specifically under
the covariance matrix constraint.

The primary contribution of our research lies in providing explicit expressions for both the upper and lower bounds of this capacity as the spatial dimension equals
3
. To arrive at these expressions, we carried out a meticulous analysis of the characteristic function of VDFAP distributions.
Furthermore, our research has uncovered a novel stability property of VDFAP.

Our findings contribute to the ongoing efforts to comprehend the fundamental limits of molecular communication systems. They open new avenues for future research, including extending the analysis to other spatial dimensions and exploring the implications of the discovered stability property.

Appendix B

From (

\displaystyle\begin{split}c&:=\dfrac{\lambda}{\sqrt{2\pi^{3}}}|u|^{3}e^{\lambda|u|};\penalty\ f(r):=\dfrac{K_{3/2}\left(|u|\sqrt{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)}{\left(|u|\sqrt{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)^{3/2}}.\end{split}

(37)

\displaystyle h(\boldsymbol{N})=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}c\cdot f(r)\cdot(\log c+\log f(r))\cdot r\penalty\ \mathrm{d}r\penalty\ \mathrm{d}\theta,

where we have applied (

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho:=|u|\sqrt{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}};\penalty\ \penalty\ \mathrm{d}\rho=|u|\frac{r}{\sqrt{r^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\mathrm{d}r=|u|^{2}\frac{r}{\rho}\mathrm{d}r\end{split}

Observe that

,
where we introduce a function

\displaystyle g(s):=se^{s+1}\text{Ei}(-(s+1))-3se^{s}\text{Ei}(-s),\text{ for }s>0.

Taking the derivative of (

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(s):=\derivative{s}g(s)=\frac{s+1}{s}g(s)+\frac{s}{s+1}-3.

\displaystyle h_{0}^{\prime}(s):=\derivative{s}h_{0}(s)=\frac{s+1}{s}(2-g(s)).

Global Estimates for Generalized Forchheimer Flows of Slightly Compressible Fluids
Luan Hoang, Thinh Kieu

Abstract
This paper is focused on the generalized Forchheimer flows of slightly compressible fluids in porous media. They are reformulated as a degenerate parabolic equation for the pressure. The initial boundary value problem is studied with time-dependent Dirichlet boundary data. The estimates up to the boundary and for all time are derived for the L ∞ L^{\infty} -norm of the pressure, its gradient and time derivative. Large-time estimates are established to be independent of the initial data. Particularly, thanks to the special structure of the pressure’s nonlinear equation, the global gradient estimates are obtained in a relatively simple way, avoiding complicated calculations and a prior requirement of Hölder estimates.

1
Introduction

In studies of fluid dynamics in porous media, Darcy’s law is ubiquitously used.
However this linear relation between the velocity and pressure gradient is not always valid.
The deviation from Darcy’s law is well-known when the Reynolds number increases
[
16
,
2
]
. Such a deviation was even noticed in early works by Darcy
[
3
]
and Dupuit
[
6
]
.
Nonlinear alternatives were formulated by Forchheimer
[
7
,
8
]
, and were studied extensively afterward in physics and engineering, see
[
16
,
26
,
2
,
17
,
21
]
and references therein.
In contrast to the vast mathematical research of Darcy’s flows, see e.g.
[
25
]
,
existing mathematical papers on Forchheimer flows are much fewer and came much later.
Even rarer are the ones for compressible fluids.
(See, e.g.,
[
1
,
9
,
10
]
for more introduction to Forchheimer flows.)
Generalized Forchheimer equations were proposed
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
in order to cover a general class of fluid flows in porous media formulated from experiments.
In our previous paper
[
12
]
, we derive interior estimates for generalized Forchheimer flows of slightly compressible fluids. In this article, we focus on the spatially global estimates, i.e., on the entire domain.
Since the equation for pressure is of degenerate parabolic type, finding
L^{\infty}
-bounds for its gradient, in general, is a difficult task and requires much work, see, for e.g.,
[
24
,
18
,
19
,
5
]
.
We will show that the gradient estimates for the particular flows under the current study can be obtained in a relatively simple way thanks to their equations’ specific structure.

In this paper, we consider the initial boundary value problem for the pressure in a bounded domain with time-dependent Dirichlet boundary data.
Our goal is to obtain bounds for the pressure’s gradient and time derivative in terms of initial and boundary data.
Aiming at studying the long-term dynamics of the system, we also emphasize the bounds for large time.
The standard technique from
[
14
]
, see also
[
15
,
4
]
, requires a Hölder estimate first, which, by itself, is not a small task.
Moreover, the estimates obtained in
[
14
]
for degenerate equations depend on the initial value of the gradient. This will result in long-time estimates for the gradient that are dependent on the initial data.
That means that the long-time dynamics cannot be reduced to a much smaller set, say, the global attractor (c.f.
[
23
,
20
]
).
In this paper, we will demonstrate that these two setbacks can be overcome without overcomplicated calculations.
First, we extend the techniques for parabolic equations in
[
14
]
to our degenerate one in a suitable way. Such extension is possible due to the equation’s special structure. Second, by doing analysis on the entire domain, we avoid the spatially local consideration, and hence, the requirement for the Hölder estimates.
Finally, localizing the estimates in time and utilizing uniform Gronwall-type inequalities remove the estimates’ dependence on the initial data.

The paper is organized as follows.
In section
2
, we recall basic facts about generalized Forchheimer equations, and prove a global embedding of Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva type. It is different from the versions in
[
14
,
12
]
which are localizations at an interior or a boundary point.
This will contribute to simpler proofs for the global estimates in section
6
.
In section
3
, we review some relevant estimates from our previous works
[
11
,
12
]
which will be used repeatedly. In section
4
, we use De Giorgi’s iteration to obtain (spatially) global estimates.
Time-local inequality (
4.2
) is “quasi-homogeneous” in terms of
L_{x,t}^{\alpha}
-norm of the solution.
This improves estimates in our previous works, and is similar to a recent improvement on interior estimates in
[
22
]
. Explicit estimates in terms of initial and boundary data are established in Theorem
4.4
.
Section
5
is devoted to estimating the maximum of the gradient’s modulus on the boundary.
Unlike the results in
[
14
]
, we are able to localize the estimate in time in Theorem
5.2
, hence, it is independent of the initial gradient.
This is essential to finding the gradient bounds for large time, by combining it with certain uniform Gronwall-type estimates. This is demonstrated in Corollary
5.3
.
In section
6
, we establish global
L^{s}
-estimates for the gradient
for all

s>0
and all positive time. This requires only moderate regularity for the initial data, say,
L^{\alpha}
and
W^{1,2-a}
with an appropriate
\alpha>0
.
Section
7
contains
L^{\infty}
-estimates for the gradient.
Thanks to the special form of the resulting equations (
7.2
) for the gradient, it is possible to apply the De Giorgi technique, see Theorem
7.1
.
Section
8
has the same results as section
7
but for the time derivative. The main estimates are in Theorems
8.2
and
8.3
.

2
Preliminaries

Consider a fluid in a porous medium in space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For physics problem
n=3
, but here we consider any
n\geq 2
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
be the spatial and time variables.
The fluid flow has velocity
v(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, pressure
p(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
and density
\rho(x,t)\in[0,\infty)
.

The generalized Forchheimer equations studied in
[
1
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
are of the the form:

g(|v|)v=-\nabla p,

(2.1)

where
g(s)\geq 0
is a function defined on
[0,\infty)
.
When
g(s)=\alpha,\alpha+\beta s,\alpha+\beta s+\gamma s^{2},\alpha+\gamma_{m}s^{m-1}
, where
\alpha,\beta,\gamma,m,\gamma_{m}
are empirical constants, we have Darcy’s law, Forchheimer’s two-term, three-term and power laws, respectively.

In this paper, we study the case when the function
g
in (
2.1
) is a generalized polynomial with positive coefficients, that is,

g(s)=a_{0}s^{\alpha_{0}}+a_{1}s^{\alpha_{1}}+\ldots+a_{N}s^{\alpha_{N}}\quad\text{for}\quad s\geq 0,

(2.2)

where

N\geq 1
, the powers
\alpha_{0}=0<\alpha_{1}<\ldots<\alpha_{N}
are fixed real numbers (not necessarily integers), the coefficients
a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}
are positive.
This function
g(s)
is referred to as the Forchheimer polynomial in equation (
2.1
).

From (
2.1
) one can solve
v
implicitly in terms of
\nabla p
and
derives a nonlinear version of Darcy’s equation:

v=-K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p,

(2.3)

where the function
K:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

K(\xi)=\frac{1}{g(s(\xi))},\text{ with }s=s(\xi)\geq 0\text{ satisfying }sg(s)=\xi,\ \text{ for }\xi\geq 0.

(2.4)

In addition to (
2.1
), we have the continuity equation

\phi\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial t}+\nabla\cdot(\rho v)=0,

(2.5)

where number
\phi\in(0,1)
is the constant porosity.
Also, for slightly compressible fluids, the equation of state is

\frac{d\rho}{dp}=\frac{\rho}{\kappa},\quad\text{with }\kappa=const.>0.

(2.6)

From (
2.3
), (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) one derives a scalar equation for the pressure:

\phi\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\kappa\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p)+K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2}.

(2.7)

On the right-hand side of (
2.7
), the constant
\kappa
is very large for most slightly compressible fluids in porous media
[
16
]
, hence we neglect its second term and by scaling the time variable, we study the reduced equation

\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p).

(2.8)

Note that this reduction is commonly used in engineering.

Our aim is to study the initial boundary value problem for equation (
2.8
) in a bounded domain.
Here afterward
U
is a bounded, open, connected subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n=2,3,\ldots
with
C^{2}
boundary
\Gamma=\partial U
.
Throughout, the Forchheimer polynomial
g(s)
in (
2.2
) is fixed.
The following number is frequently used in our calculations:

a=\frac{\alpha_{N}}{1+\alpha_{N}}\in(0,1).

(2.9)

The function
K(\xi)
in (
2.4
) has the following properties (c.f.
[
1
,
9
]
): it is decreasing in
\xi
mapping
\xi\in[0,\infty)
onto
(0,1/a_{0}]
, and

\frac{d_{1}}{(1+\xi)^{a}}\leq K(\xi)\leq\frac{d_{2}}{(1+\xi)^{a}},

(2.10)

d_{3}(\xi^{2-a}-1)\leq K(\xi)\xi^{2}\leq d_{2}\xi^{2-a},

(2.11)

-aK(\xi)\leq K^{\prime}(\xi)\xi\leq 0,

(2.12)

where
d_{1}
,
d_{2}
,
d_{3}
are positive constants depending on
g
.
As in previous works, we use the function
H(\xi)
defined by

H(\xi)=\int_{0}^{\xi^{2}}K(\sqrt{s})ds\quad\hbox{for }\xi\geq 0.

It satisfies
K(\xi)\xi^{2}\leq H(\xi)\leq 2K(\xi)\xi^{2}
, thus, by (
2.11
),

d_{3}(\xi^{2-a}-1)\leq H(\xi)\leq 2d_{2}\xi^{2-a}.

(2.13)

The following parabolic Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities are needed for our study.
For each
T>0
, denote
Q_{T}=U\times(0,T)
.
We define a threshold exponent

\alpha_{*}=\frac{an}{2-a}.

(2.14)

Lemma 2.1

(cf.
[
12
]
, Lemma 2.1)
.

Assume

\alpha\geq 2\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha>\alpha_{*}.

(2.15)

Let

p=\alpha\Big(1+\frac{2-a}{n}\Big)-a.

(2.16)

Then

\|u\|_{L^{p}(Q_{T})}\leq C(1+\delta T)^{1/p}[[u]],

(2.17)

where
\delta=1
in general,
\delta=0
in case
u
vanishes on the boundary
\partial U
, and

[[u]]=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{[0,T]}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{\alpha}(U)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|u(x,t)|^{\alpha-2}|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2-a}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{\alpha-a}}.

(2.18)

The next is a particular embedding with spatial weights from Lemma 2.4 of
[
13
]
(see inequality (2.28) with
m=2
there).

Lemma 2.2

(cf.
[
13
]
, Lemma 2.4)
.

Given
W(x,t)>0
on
Q_{T}
.
Let
r
be a number that satisfies

\frac{2n}{n+2}<r<2.

(2.19)

Set

\varrho=\varrho(r)\mathbin{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mathbin{=\kern-2.0pt=}}\limits^{\rm def}}}4(1-1/r^{*}).

(2.20)

Then

\|u\|_{L^{\varrho}(Q_{T})}\leq C[[u]]_{2,W;T}\Big\{\delta T^{\frac{1}{\varrho}}+\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{t\in[0,T]}\Big(\int_{U}W(x,t)^{-\frac{r}{2-r}}\chi_{{\rm supp}\,u}(x,t)dx\Big)^{\frac{2-r}{\varrho r}}\Big\},

(2.21)

where
\delta=1
in general,
\delta=0
in case
u
vanishes on the boundary
\partial U
, and

[[u]]_{2,W;T}=\mathop{\mathrm{ess\,sup}}_{[0,T]}\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(U)}+\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}W(x,t)|\nabla u(x,t)|^{2}dxdt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(2.22)

Above,
{\rm supp}f
denotes the support set of a function
f
, and
\chi_{A}
denotes the characteristic functions of a set
A
.

The following embedding is a global version of Lemma 3.7 from
[
13
]
and Lemma 5.4 on page 93 in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
w
is a function on
\bar{U}
that satisfies
|\nabla w|\leq M
on
\Gamma
.

Let
v=\max\{|\nabla w|^{2}-M^{2},0\}
.
For each
s\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C>0
depending on
s
such that for any
k\in\mathbb{R}
,

\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s+1}dx\leq C\max|w-k|^{2}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}v^{s-1}dx+CM^{4}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}dx.

(2.23)

Proof.

Let
I=\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s+1}dx
.
Note that
v=0
on
\Gamma
.
First, we see that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}|\nabla w|^{2}dx=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}\partial_{i}w\partial_{i}(w-k)dx.

By integration by parts,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{U}\partial_{i}(K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}\partial_{i}w)\cdot(w-k)dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{U}\Big(K^{\prime}(|\nabla w|)\frac{\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w\partial_{j}w}{|\nabla w|}\Big)v^{s}\partial_{i}w\cdot(w-k)dx-\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}\Delta w\cdot(w-k)dx

\displaystyle\quad-s\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)\Big(v^{s-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}w\partial_{j}w\Big)\cdot\partial_{i}w\cdot(w-k)\chi_{\{v>0\}}dx.

From this and (
2.12
), it follows that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq a\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)|\nabla^{2}w|v^{s}|w-k|dx+\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}|\Delta w||w-k|dx

\displaystyle\quad+s\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}|\nabla^{2}w||\nabla w|^{2}|w-k|\chi_{\{v>0\}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}|\nabla^{2}w||w-k|dx+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)|\nabla^{2}w|v^{s-1}(v+M^{2})|w-k|dx.

Hence,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s}|\nabla^{2}w||w-k|dx+CM^{2}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)|\nabla^{2}w|v^{s-1}|w-k|dx

\displaystyle=C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{\frac{s+1}{2}}\cdot|\nabla^{2}w|v^{\frac{s-1}{2}}|w-k|dx+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}|\nabla^{2}w||w-k|\cdot M^{2}dx.

This last inequality and Cauchy’s inequality imply that

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)(\frac{v^{s+1}}{2}+C|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}v^{s-1}|w-k|^{2})dx+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}(|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}|w-k|^{2}+M^{4})dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}I+C\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}|\nabla^{2}w|^{2}|w-k|^{2}dx+CM^{4}\int_{U}K(|\nabla w|)v^{s-1}dx.

Therefore, we obtain (
2.23
).
∎

3
Previous results

We study the following initial boundary value problem (IBVP) for
p(x,t)
:

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(K(|\nabla p|)\nabla p)&\text{in }U\times(0,\infty),\\
p(x,0)=p_{0}(x)&\text{in }U,\\
p(x,t)=\psi(x,t)&\text{on }\Gamma\times(0,\infty).\end{cases}

(3.1)

In order to deal with the non-homogeneous boundary condition, the data
\psi(x,t)
with
x\in\Gamma
and
t>0
is extended to a function
\Psi(x,t)
with
x\in\bar{U}
and
t\geq 0
. Throughout, our results are stated in terms of
\Psi
instead of
\psi
. Nonetheless, corresponding results in terms of
\psi
can be retrieved as performed in
[
9
]
. The function
\Psi
is always assumed to have adequate regularities for all calculations in this paper.

Solutions.

It is proved in section 3 of
[
11
]
that (
3.1
) possesses a weak solution

for all
t>0
.
It, in fact, has more regularity in spatial and time variables, see
[
4
]
.
For the current study, we assume that solution
p(x,t)
has sufficient regularities both in
x
and
t
variables such that our calculations hereafter can be performed legitimately.
Specifically, we assume

p,\nabla p,p_{t}\in C(\bar{U}\times(0,\infty))
, the Hessian matrix of second spatial derivatives
\nabla^{2}p\in C(U\times(0,\infty))
,
and the function

is continuous from
[0,\infty)
to
W^{1,2-a}(U)\cap L^{\alpha}(U)
, for an appropriate

which will be determined for each type of estimate. Some particular estimates may require much less regularity such as the

-estimates in section

The obtained estimates also hold without the

(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{1/\alpha})^{\frac{1}{1/p_{1}+\delta_{1}}}\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(0,T))}^{\frac{\delta_{2}}{1/p_{1}+\delta_{1}}}+(\mathcal{E}_{2}^{1/\alpha})^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha/p_{1}+\delta_{1}(\alpha-a)}}\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(0,T))}^{\frac{\delta_{4}(\alpha-a)p_{1}}{\alpha+\delta_{1}(\alpha-a)p_{1}}}\Big\}.

(4.19)

Rewriting the powers in (
4.19
), noticing that

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\delta_{1}=1+\delta_{3},\quad\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-a}+\delta_{1}=1+\delta_{2},\quad\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)p_{1}}+\delta_{1}=1+\delta_{4},

and also

\mathcal{E}_{2}^{1/\alpha}\leq C(T^{\frac{\alpha-2}{2\alpha}}\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}+T^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}),

we obtain (
4.2
). The proof is complete.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In (
4.2
), the norm
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\infty}(U\times[T_{0}+\theta T,T_{0}+T])}
is estimated by a sum of homogeneous terms in
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}
. Therefore it is appropriate for both small and large
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}
. This improves our previous interior versions in Theorem 4.1 of
[
12
]
and Proposition 3.2 of
[
13
]
, where the bounds contain an additive constant, which make them more suitable for large value
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T)}
. For doubly nonlinear parabolic equations of p-Laplacian type, the interior estimate of this kind was obtained in
[
22
]
using Moser’s iteration.

To simplify our future estimates, we use the following weaker version of Theorem
4.1
.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\alpha
,
T_{0}
,
T
,
\theta
,
r_{1}
,
p_{1}
,
q_{1}
,
\delta_{1}
,
\delta_{2}
,
\delta_{3}
,
\delta_{4}
be as in Theorem

.
Then

\sup_{[T_{0}+\theta T,T_{0}+T]}\|{\bar{p}(t)}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq C\Big(1+(\theta T)^{-1/\delta_{1}}+T^{z_{1}}\Big)\\
\cdot\Big(1+\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}+\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}\Big)^{z_{2}}\\
\cdot\Big(1+\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U\times(T_{0},T_{0}+T))}\Big)^{z_{3}},

(4.20)

where

z_{1}=\frac{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})},\ z_{2}=\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})},\ z_{3}=\max\Big\{1,\frac{\delta_{2}}{1+\delta_{3}}\Big\}.

(4.21)

Proof.

We apply Young’s inequality in (
4.2
) to the terms involving
(\theta T)^{-1}
,
T
,
\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}}
,
\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}}
,
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}}
.

First, we note that
\delta_{2}>\delta_{1}\geq\delta_{3}
and
\delta_{2}\geq\delta_{4}>\delta_{3}
.

\bullet
For the power of
(\theta T)^{-1}
, we have
1/\delta_{1}>1/((\alpha-a)\delta_{1})
.

\bullet
The largest power of
T
is

\displaystyle z_{1}=\max\Big\{\frac{\alpha-2}{2\alpha(1+\delta_{1})},\frac{\alpha-2}{2(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{2})},\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha(1+\delta_{3})},\frac{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})}\Big\}.

Note that

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-2}{2\alpha(1+\delta_{1})}<\frac{\alpha-2}{2(\alpha+\delta_{1}(\alpha-a))}=\frac{\alpha-2}{2(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{2})}\leq\frac{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})},

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha(1+\delta_{3})}=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha(1/p_{1}+\delta_{1})}<\frac{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-a)(\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)p_{1}}+\delta_{1})}=\frac{\alpha-1}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})}.

Hence

is as in (
4.21
).

\bullet
The largest power of

and

Since

\frac{1}{1+\delta_{3}}=\frac{1}{1/p_{1}+\delta_{1}}<\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)(\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)p_{1}}+\delta_{1})}=\frac{\alpha}{(\alpha-a)(1+\delta_{4})},

we have
z_{2}
as in (
4.21
).

\bullet
The largest power of
\|\bar{p}\|_{L^{\alpha}}
is

z_{3}=\Big\{1,\frac{\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{1}},\frac{\delta_{2}}{1+\delta_{3}},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\delta_{1}},\frac{\delta_{3}}{1+\delta_{2}},\frac{\delta_{4}}{1+\delta_{4}}\Big\}.

Note that

\frac{\delta_{1}}{1+\delta_{1}},\frac{\delta_{3}}{\delta_{1}},\frac{\delta_{3}}{1+\delta_{2}},\frac{\delta_{4}}{\delta_{4}+1}<1.

Hence
z_{3}
is as in (
4.21
).
Therefore, we obtain (
4.20
) from (
4.2
).
∎

increasing in

5
Gradient estimates on the boundary

In this section, we estimate the maximum of
|\nabla p|
on the boundary
\Gamma\times(0,\infty)
.
It will be used in estimates of
L^{s}
-norms for the gradient in section
6
and its
L^{\infty}
-norm in section
7
.
We extend Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva’s technique
[
14
]
in order to have better estimates for large time.

We rewrite the PDE in (
3.1
) for
p
in the non-divergence form as

p_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ij}p_{x_{i}x_{j}}=0,

(5.1)

where

\mathcal{A}_{ij}=\mathcal{A}_{ij}(x,t)\mathbin{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mathbin{=\kern-2.0pt=}}\limits^{\rm def}}}K(|\nabla p(x,t)|)\delta_{ij}+\frac{K^{\prime}(|\nabla p(x,t)|)}{|\nabla p(x,t)|}p_{x_{i}}(x,t)p_{x_{j}}(x,t),\quad i,j=1,2,\ldots,n.

Thanks to (
2.10
)–(
2.12
) we find that

|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\leq(1+a)K(|\nabla p|),

(1-a)K(|\nabla p|)|y|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ij}y_{i}y_{j}\leq K(|\nabla p|)|y|^{2},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

By (
2.10
), we have more explicit relations:

|\mathcal{A}_{ij}|\leq(1+a)d_{2}(1+|\nabla p|)^{-a},

(5.2)

(1-a)d_{1}(1+|\nabla p|)^{-a}|y|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\mathcal{A}_{ij}y_{i}y_{j}\leq d_{2}(1+|\nabla p|)^{-a}|y|^{2},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(5.3)

We will establish boundary estimates in case the boundary is flat first.
For general boundary, we will flatten it out and hence transform equation (
5.1
) to a different, but similar, one.
To prepare for this transformation, we consider a more general PDE:

{\mathcal{L}}p\mathbin{\mathrel{\mathop{\kern 0.0pt\mathbin{=\kern-2.0pt=}}\limits^{\rm def}}}p_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}p_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\tilde{b}_{i}p_{x_{i}}=0,

(5.4)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}=\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}(x,t)
,
\tilde{b}_{i}=\tilde{b}_{i}(x,t)
for
i,j=1,2,\ldots,n
.

Define for
\xi\geq 0
the function

\tilde{K}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(1+\xi)^{a}}.

Similar to (
5.2
) and (
5.3
), we assume that
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}
satisfy

(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}|^{2})^{1/2}\leq c_{1}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|),

(5.5)

c_{2}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|y|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}y_{i}y_{j}\leq c_{3}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|y|^{2},\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

(5.6)

where
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
are positive constants.
In addition, we also assume that

|\tilde{b}(x,t)|\leq c_{4}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\text{ with }\tilde{b}=(\tilde{b}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{b}_{n}).

(5.7)

for some positive constant
c_{4}.

Note, particularly when
y=e_{k}
, a unit vector in the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have from (
5.6
) that

\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{kk}\geq c_{2}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|),\quad k=1,2,\ldots,n.

(5.8)

Denote the ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
centered at the origin with radius
R
by
B_{R}
, and the upper half ball by
B_{R}^{+}
, i.e.,
B_{R}^{+}=B_{R}\cap\{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n}):x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1},\ x_{n}>0\}
.
Also, denote
\Gamma_{R}=\partial B_{R}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}
-the flat portion of the boundary of
B_{R}^{+}
.

We start with estimates on a flat boundary.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
T>0
and
R>0
. Let
p(x,t)\in C^{1,0}(\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T])\cap C^{2,1}(B_{R}^{+}\times(0,T])

be a solution of (
5.4
) in
B_{R}^{+}\times(0,T]
, with
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}
and
\tilde{b}_{i}
satisfying (
5.5
)–(
5.7
) in
\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]
.
Then

\max_{\Gamma_{R/2}\times[\theta T,T]}|\nabla p(x,t)|\leq C(1+R^{-2})e^{C^{\prime}R}(1+(\theta T)^{-1})^{\mu_{0}}\exp\big(C^{\prime}\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[\theta T/2,T]}|\bar{p}|\big)\\
\cdot(1+\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}|\Psi_{t}|^{\mu_{0}}+\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}|\nabla\Psi|^{2}+\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}|\nabla^{2}\Psi|),

(5.9)

for any
\theta\in(0,1)
, where
C
,
C^{\prime}
are positive numbers independent of
R
,
T
,
\theta
, and

\mu_{0}=\frac{2}{2-a}.

(5.10)

Proof.

(a) Let
v=-1+e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}
with the constant
\kappa_{0}>0
chosen later. Then

v=0\text{ on }\partial B_{R}^{+}\cap\{x_{n}=0\}.

(5.11)

For
i,j=1,2,\ldots,n
, we have

v_{t}=\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\bar{p}_{t},\quad v_{x_{i}}=\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\bar{p}_{x_{i}},\quad v_{x_{i}x_{j}}=\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\bar{p}_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\bar{p}_{x_{i}}\bar{p}_{x_{j}}.

(5.12)

Substituting (
5.12
) into (
5.4
) gives

\kappa_{0}^{-1}e^{-\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}v_{t}+\Psi_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}\Big\{\kappa_{0}^{-1}e^{-\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}v_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\Psi_{x_{i}x_{j}}-\kappa_{0}\bar{p}_{x_{i}}\bar{p}_{x_{j}}\Big\}+\tilde{b}\cdot(\kappa_{0}^{-1}e^{-\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\nabla v+\nabla\Psi)=0.

Multiplying by
\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}
, it shows that
v(x,t)
solves the equation

v_{t}-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}v_{x_{i}x_{j}}+\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla v=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\bar{p}_{x_{i}}\bar{p}_{x_{j}}+\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}\Psi_{x_{i}x_{j}}-\Psi_{t}-\tilde{b}\cdot\nabla\Psi).

Thus, by (
5.6
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

{\mathcal{L}}v\leq-c_{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla\bar{p}|^{2}+\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\Big[(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}|^{2})^{1/2}(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\Psi_{x_{i}x_{j}}|^{2})^{1/2}+|\tilde{b}||\nabla\Psi|+|\Psi_{t}|\Big].

Using inequality
|\nabla\bar{p}|^{2}=|\nabla p-\nabla\Psi|^{2}\geq|\nabla p|^{2}/2-|\nabla\Psi|^{2}
, and inequalities (
5.5
), (
5.7
), we get

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}v&\leq-\frac{c_{2}}{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2}+c_{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla\Psi|^{2}\\
&\quad+\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\Big[\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)(c_{1}|\nabla^{2}\Psi|+c_{4}|\nabla\Psi|)+|\Psi_{t}|\Big].\end{split}

Let

\displaystyle M_{1}=\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}(c_{1}|\nabla^{2}\Psi|+c_{4}|\nabla\Psi|),\quad M_{2}=\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}|\Psi_{t}|,\quad M_{3}=c_{2}\max_{\bar{B}_{R}^{+}\times[0,T]}|\nabla\Psi|^{2}.

Then

{\mathcal{L}}v\leq-\frac{c_{2}}{2}\kappa_{0}^{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2}+\kappa_{0}(M_{3}\kappa_{0}+M_{1})e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)+\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}M_{2}.

(5.13)

Let
\varepsilon>0
. We write
M_{2}=\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\cdot M_{2}(1+|\nabla p|)^{a}
and estimate

M_{2}(1+|\nabla p|)^{a}\leq M_{2}(1+|\nabla p|^{a})\leq M_{2}+\varepsilon^{-a/(2-a)}M_{2}^{2/(2-a)}+\varepsilon|\nabla p|^{2}.

From this and (
5.13
), we find that

\begin{split}{\mathcal{L}}v&\leq-\big(\frac{c_{2}}{2}\kappa_{0}-\varepsilon\Big)\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla p|^{2}\\
&\quad+\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)(\kappa_{0}M_{3}+M_{1}+M_{2}+\varepsilon^{-a/(2-a)}M_{2}^{2/(2-a)}).\end{split}

(5.14)

(b) We localize the above calculations. Let
\zeta=\zeta(x,t)\in[0,1]
be a cut-off function on
B_{R}\times(0,T)
with
\zeta=0
on
(B_{R}\times[\theta T/2,T])\cup(\partial B_{R}\times[0,T])
, and
\zeta(x,t)=1
on
B_{R/2}\times(\theta T,T)
, and satisfy

|\nabla\zeta|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{R},\quad|\nabla^{2}\zeta|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{R^{2}},\quad|\zeta_{t}|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{\theta T},

where
C_{0}>0
is independent of
R
,
\theta
, and
T
.
Denote by
\chi=\chi(t)
the characteristic function of
[\theta T/T,T]
.

Let
w=v\zeta^{2}
. Then

{\mathcal{L}}w=\zeta^{2}{\mathcal{L}}v+v{\mathcal{L}}(\zeta^{2})-2\zeta(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\zeta.

(5.15)

For the last term, by (
5.5
) and (
5.12
):

\displaystyle|2\zeta(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\zeta|

\displaystyle\leq 2c_{1}\zeta\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla v||\nabla\zeta|\leq C_{1}\zeta\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)|\nabla\bar{p}|

\displaystyle\leq\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\big[C_{1}\zeta|\nabla p|\chi+C_{1}\zeta|\nabla\Psi|\big],

where
C_{1}=2c_{1}
.
Thus

|2\zeta(\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\zeta|\leq\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\big(\varepsilon\zeta^{2}|\nabla p|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-1}C_{1}^{2}\chi+C_{1}\zeta|\nabla\Psi|\big).

(5.16)

For the second term on the right-hand side of (
5.15
),

|v{\mathcal{L}}(\zeta^{2})|\leq|v|2\zeta|\zeta_{t}|+|v|(\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{ij}||(\zeta^{2})_{x_{i}x_{j}}|+2\zeta|\tilde{b}||\nabla\zeta|)\leq|v|2\zeta|\zeta_{t}|+C_{2}|v|\chi\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|),

(5.17)

where
C_{2}=C_{3}(1+R^{-2})
, with
C_{3}>0
independent of
R
,
\theta
,
T
.
Treating the term
2|v|\zeta|\zeta_{t}|
in (
5.17
) the same way as we did for
M_{2}
in (
5.13
):

\displaystyle 2|v|\zeta|\zeta_{t}|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}|v|\zeta\leq C_{4}|v|\zeta\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)(1+|\nabla p|^{a})\leq\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)(C_{4}|v|\zeta+C_{4}|v|\zeta|\nabla p|^{a}),

where

C_{4}=2C_{0}(\theta T)^{-1}.

Note that

C_{4}|v|\zeta|\nabla p|^{a}=C_{4}|v|\zeta^{1-a}[\varepsilon\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}]^{-a/2}\cdot[\varepsilon^{1/2}\kappa_{0}^{1/2}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}/2}\zeta|\nabla p|]^{a}.

Applying Young’s inequality with power
2/(2-a)
and
2/a
yields

2|v|\zeta|\zeta_{t}|\leq\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\Big[C_{4}|v|\zeta+(C_{4}|v|\zeta^{1-a})^{2/(2-a)}(\varepsilon\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}})^{-a/(2-a)}\Big]+\varepsilon\kappa_{0}e^{\kappa_{0}\bar{p}}\tilde{K}(|\nabla p|)\zeta^{2}|\nabla p|^{2}.

(5.18)

It follows from (
5.17
) and (

First, we establish the basic step for the Ladyzhenskaya-Uraltseva iteration.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
s>0
,
T_{0}\geq 0
, and
T>T^{\prime}\geq 0

Define

M_{b}=\max_{\Gamma\times[T_{0}+T^{\prime},T_{0}+T]}|\nabla p|\quad\text{and}\quad v=\max\{|\nabla p|^{2}-M_{b}^{2},0\}.

\sup_{[T_{0},T_{0}+T]}\int_{U}v^{s+1}(x,t)\zeta dx+\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}v^{s}\zeta dxdt\leq C\int_{T_{0}}^{T}\int_{U}v^{s+1}|\zeta_{t}|dxdt.

(6.1)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
T_{0}=0

Note that

on
\Gamma\times[0,T]

Denote
\chi=\chi_{\{v>0\}}

Multiplying the equation (
3.1
) by

, integrating the resultant over

and using integration by parts,
we obtain

\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta dx=-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\int_{U}\partial_{j}(K(|\nabla p|)\partial_{i}p)\,\partial_{i}(v^{s}\partial_{j}p\zeta)dx+\frac{1}{2s+2}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta_{t}dx.

(6.2)

Using the product rule for the first term in the right hand side of (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta dx=-\sum_{i,j,l=1}^{n}\int_{U}\Big[\partial_{y_{l}}(K(|y|)y_{i})\Big|_{y=\nabla p}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}p\Big]\partial_{j}\partial_{i}pv^{s}\zeta dx

\displaystyle\quad-s\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\sum_{l,m=1}^{n}\int_{U}\Big[\partial_{y_{l}}(K(|y|)y_{i})\Big|_{y=\nabla p}\partial_{j}\partial_{l}p\Big]\partial_{j}p\,(v^{s-1}\partial_{i}\partial_{m}p\partial_{m}p\chi)\,\zeta dx+\frac{1}{2s+2}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta_{t}dx.

We denote the three terms on the right-hand side by

,
I_{2}
, and

.
It follows from the calculations in Lemma 3.6 of
[

]
that

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle\leq-(1-a)\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla(\partial_{j}p)|^{2}v^{s}\zeta dx,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\leq-(1-a)s\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)\Big|\nabla\Big(\frac{1}{2}|\nabla p|^{2}\Big)\Big|^{2}v^{s-1}\chi\zeta dx\leq 0.

Combining these estimates, we find that

\frac{1}{2s+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta dx+(1-a)\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}v^{s}\zeta dx\leq\frac{1}{2s+2}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta_{t}dx.

(6.3)

Inequality (
6.1
) follows directly by integrating (
6.3
) from

to
T

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
s\geq 1
,
T_{0}\geq 0
,
T>0
, and
0<\theta^{\prime}<\theta<1
. Define

Then

\int_{T_{0}+\theta T}^{T_{0}+T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}dxdt\leq CT\mathcal{U}(s)+Cd_{0}^{\frac{s-1}{2-a}}\int_{T_{0}+\theta^{\prime}T}^{T_{0}+T}\int_{U}(1+|\nabla p|^{2-a})dxdt,

(6.4)

where constant
C>0
is independent of
T_{0}
,
T
,
\theta
, and
\theta^{\prime}
,

\mathcal{U}(s)=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }1\leq s\leq 3-a,\\
d_{0}+d_{0}^{\frac{s-1}{2-a}+\frac{1}{2}},&\text{if }s>3-a,\end{cases}

(6.5)

(6.6)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
T_{0}=0
.
The proof consists of three steps.

Step 1.
Let
\zeta(t)
be the cut-off function with
\zeta=0
for

\zeta=1
for

. For
s\geq 0
, by applying Lemma
2.3
with
k=0
, and
s+1
in place of
s
, multiplying (
2.23
) by

and integrating from

, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq C\max|p|^{2}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)|\nabla^{2}p|^{2}v^{s}\zeta^{2}dxdt+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta^{2}dxdt.

To estimate the first integral on the right-hand side, we use (
6.1
) with
\zeta^{2}
in place of
\zeta
, and find that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq CN_{0}^{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}v^{s+1}\zeta\zeta_{t}dxdt+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta^{2}dxdt.

(6.7)

Now using Young’s inequality we have

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt+CN_{0}^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K^{-1}(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta_{t}^{2}dxdt\\
+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta^{2}dxdt.

Thus

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt\leq CN_{0}^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K^{-1}(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta_{t}^{2}dxdt+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\zeta^{2}dxdt.

(6.8)

Note that

\displaystyle K^{-1}(|\nabla p|)v^{s}

\displaystyle\leq CK(|\nabla p|)(1+|\nabla p|)^{2a}v^{s}\leq CK(|\nabla p|)(1+(v+M_{b}^{2})^{a})v^{s}

\displaystyle\leq CK(|\nabla p|)(1+v^{s+a}+M_{b}^{2a}v^{s})\leq CK(|\nabla p|)(v^{s+a}+1+M_{b}^{2(s+a)}),

and

K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}\leq CK(|\nabla p|)(1+v^{s+a}).

Thus (
6.8
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}\zeta^{2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C[N_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}+M_{b}^{4}]\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+a}\chi_{\{\zeta>0\}}dxdt

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}[N_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}(1+M_{b}^{2(s+a)})+M_{b}^{4}]\chi_{\{\zeta>0\}}dxdt.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C[N_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}+M_{b}^{4}]\int_{\theta^{\prime}T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+a}dxdt

\displaystyle+CT[N_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}(1+M_{b}^{2})^{s+a}+M_{b}^{4}].

\displaystyle\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+2}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{0}\int_{\theta^{\prime}T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s+a}dxdt+CN_{1}M_{b}^{2(s+a)}+CN_{2}.

(6.9)

Equivalently, for

\displaystyle\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq Cd_{0}\int_{\theta^{\prime}T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{s-(2-a)}dxdt+CN_{1}(1+M_{b}^{2})^{s-(2-a)}+CN_{2}.

(6.10)

Step 2.

6.4
) for

.

Second, let

\displaystyle\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{3-a}dxdt\leq CN_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}\int_{\theta^{\prime}T}^{T}\int_{U}(1+|\nabla p|)^{a}v^{1-a}dxdt+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{\theta^{\prime}T}^{T}\int_{U}v^{1-a}dxdt

\displaystyle\leq CN_{0}^{4}((\theta-\theta^{\prime})T)^{-2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}(1+|\nabla p|)^{a}|\nabla p|^{2(1-a)}dxdt+CM_{b}^{4}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}|\nabla p|^{2(1-a)}dxdt.

Therefore,

\int_{\theta T}^{T}\int_{U}K(|\nabla p|)v^{3-a}dxdt\leq Cd_{0}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{U}(1+|\nabla p|^{2-a})dxdt

(6.12)

This implies (

) when

Third, when

\displaystyle\quad+CN_{1}\Big[M_{b}^{2(s-(2-a))}+d_{0}M_{b}^{2(s-2(2-a))}+d_{0}^{2}M_{b}^{2(s-3(2-a))}+\ldots+d_{0}^{m-1}M_{b}^{2(s-m(2-a))}\Big]

(6.14)

Then by Young’s inequality and (
6.29
) for
s=\tilde{s}_{2}
,

\displaystyle\lambda^{s_{1}/2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{t-1}^{t}\int_{U}(1+|\nabla p|)^{s_{2}}dxd\tau\Big)^{\nu_{1}/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}})^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(t)^{{\tilde{s}}_{2}\nu_{1}}\cdot\Big(1+\int_{t-2}^{t}G_{1}(\tau)d\tau\Big)^{\nu_{1}/2}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\exp\Big\{C^{\prime}(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U)})^{z_{3}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{z_{3}}(1+\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))}+\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))})^{z_{2}}\Big\}.

Combining this with (
8.7
) and (
3.11
) gives

\displaystyle\|p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq C(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}})^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(t)^{{\tilde{s}}_{2}\nu_{1}}\cdot\Big(1+\int_{t-2}^{t}G_{1}(\tau)d\tau\Big)^{\nu_{1}/2}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\exp\Big\{C^{\prime}(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U)})^{z_{3}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{z_{3}}(1+\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))}+\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))})^{z_{2}}\Big\}

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\Big(1+\int_{U}|\bar{p}_{0}(x)|^{\alpha}dx+[EnvA(\alpha,t)]^{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-a}}+\int_{t-2}^{t}G_{3}(\tau)d\tau+\int_{t-2}^{t}\int_{U}|\Psi_{t}|^{2}dxd\tau\Big)^{1/2}+\sup_{[t-1,t]}\|\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Using the facts
G_{1}(t)\leq G_{3}(t)
and
\int_{U}|\Psi_{t}(x,t)|^{2}dx\leq G_{3}(t)
, we infer that

\|p_{t}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}\leq C(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}})^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}+\alpha/2}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{\kappa_{3}\nu_{1}+\alpha/2}\tilde{\mathcal{K}}_{2}(t)^{{\tilde{s}}_{2}\nu_{1}}\Big(1+\int_{t-2}^{t}G_{3}(\tau)d\tau\Big)^{\nu_{1}/2+1/2}\\
\cdot\exp\Big\{C^{\prime}(1+\|\bar{p}_{0}\|_{L^{\alpha}(U)})^{z_{3}}\mathcal{K}_{1}(t)^{z_{3}}(1+\|\nabla\Psi\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))}+\|\Psi_{t}\|_{L^{\alpha q_{1}}(U\times(t-3,t))})^{z_{2}}\Big\}+\sup_{[t-1,t]}\|\psi_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}},

and obtain (
8.5
). The proof is complete.
∎

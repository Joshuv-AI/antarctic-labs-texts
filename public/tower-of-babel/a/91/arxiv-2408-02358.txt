On the geometrization of the p p -adic local Langlands correspondence
Pierre Colmez, Gabriel Dospinescu, Wiesława Nizioł

Abstract
Résumé We survey results related to our geometrization of a part of the p p -adic local Langlands correspondence for 𝐆𝐋 2 ​ ( 𝐐 p ) {\bf GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p}) .

Introduction

The
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
{\bf GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})
has a
very satisfactory shape but there is no other group for which the situation is as well
understood (not even
{\bf GL}_{3}({\bf Q}_{p})
or
{\bf GL}_{2}(F)
for
[F:{\bf Q}_{p}]<\infty
).
There exist though several competing approaches for remedying this situation. Here we will review
our proposal from
[
23
]

and some related research.

1
The
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
{\bf GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})

1.1
Notation

In what follows we fix a finite extension
L
of
{\bf Q}_{p}
(this will be the field
of coefficients for our representations of groups), with ring of integers

{\mathscr{O}}_{L}
and residue field
k_{L}
.

Let
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}\supset{\rm W}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
denote
the absolute Galois group of
{\bf Q}_{p}
and the Weil group of
{\bf Q}_{p}
.
Local class field theory gives an identification between continuous
characters of
{\rm W}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
and continuous characters of
{\bf Q}_{p}^{\boldsymbol{*}}
,
and a character of
{\rm W}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
extends to
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
if
and only if it is unitary (i.e., takes values in
{\mathscr{O}}_{L}^{\boldsymbol{*}}
) ;
under this identification the cyclotomic character
\chi:{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}\to{\mathscr{O}}_{L}^{\boldsymbol{*}}

corresponds to
x\mapsto x|x|
.

We set
G:={\bf GL}_{2}({\bf Q}_{p})
.

1.2
The correspondence

Let

{\rm Rep}_{L}\,G
be the category of continuous representations
of
G
on
L
-Banach spaces
\Pi
, that are unitary (there is a
G
-invariant
norm defining the topology
on
\Pi
), of finite length as topological
G
-modules (this implies that
\Pi
is
admissible, i.e. if
\Pi_{0}
is the unit ball for a
G
-invariant norm, the action
of
G
on
k_{L}\otimes_{{\mathscr{O}}_{L}}\Pi_{0}
is locally constant and, if
K
is an open subgroup of

G
, the
K
-fixed vectors are finite dimensional over
k_{L}
).

Let

{\rm Rep}_{L}\,{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
be the category of finite dimensional
L
-modules
with a continuous and linear action of
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
. If

n\geq 1
, let
{\rm Rep}^{(n)}_{L}\,{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
be the subset of representations
of dimension
n
.

\theoname
1.1
.

There exists :

(i)

a functor
\Pi\mapsto{\bf V}(\Pi)
,
from
{\rm Rep}_{L}\,G
to
{\rm Rep}_{L}\,{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
,

(ii)

a correspondence
V\mapsto\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)

from
{\rm Rep}^{(2)}_{L}\,{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
to
{\rm Rep}_{L}\,G
with :

\bullet

{\bf V}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V))=V
,

\bullet

\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
has no finite subobject,

\bullet

\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
is maximal for these two properties.

The construction of the functor
{\bf V}
goes through the theory of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules
introduced
[
46
]
by Fontaine to classify
L
-representations of
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
,
and is rather straightforward.
What is delicate is to show that one gets finite dimensional
representations of
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
and that any representation of dimension
2

is in the image.

1.3
Properties of the correspondence

The above correspondence enjoys a number of additional properties :

\bullet

Compatibility with class field theory : the central character of
\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)

and the determinant of
V
are related by the formula :

\omega_{\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)}=\chi^{-1}\det V.

\bullet

V\mapsto\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
induces a bijection between
2
-dimensional,
absolutely irreductible
V
’s, and
supercuspidal
\Pi
’s (i.e., absolutely irreducible, and not a Jordan-Hölder
factor of the induction of a character of the Borel).

If
V
is not irreducible, neither is
\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
; more precisely :

V^{\rm ss}=\delta_{1}\oplus\delta_{2}\Rightarrow\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)^{\rm ss}\sim{\rm Ind}_{B}^{G}(\delta_{2}\otimes\delta_{1}\chi^{-1})^{\rm ss}\oplus{\rm Ind}_{B}^{G}(\delta_{1}\otimes\delta_{2}\chi^{-1})^{\rm ss}

Moreover, all irreducible
\Pi
’s appear as a Jordan-Hölder factor of a
\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
.

\bullet

Relation to the classical local Langlands correspondence :

\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)^{\rm alg}\neq 0
if and only if
V
is de Rham,
with distinct Hodge-Tate weights
a<b
, and we have :

\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)^{\rm alg}={\rm LL}(D_{\rm pst}(V))\otimes{\rm Sym}^{b-a-1}\otimes{\det}^{a}

Moreover, there is also a recipe to recover
{\rm LL}(D_{\rm pst}(V))
from
\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
if
a=b
.

\bullet
Compatibility with a mod
p
local Langlands correspondence : in a semi-simplified
version, this mod
p
correspondence gives a bijection
{\mathscr{B}}\leftrightarrow\overline{\rho}_{\mathscr{B}}

between the blocks of
{\rm Rep}_{k_{L}}\,G
and the

2
-dimensional, semi-simple,
k_{L}
-representations of
{\rm Gal}_{{\bf Q}_{p}}
.

\bullet
Compatibility with global correspondence :

\boldsymbol{\Pi}(V)
appears in the completed cohomology of towers of modular curves.

One useful application of the correspondence is the following result explaining
how to recover
\Pi
from its locally analytic vectors.

\propname
1.2
.

(
[
22
]
)

The dual
\Pi^{\boldsymbol{*}}

of
\Pi
is the subspace of
(\Pi^{\rm an})^{{\boldsymbol{*}}}
of vectors which are
bounded under the action of
G
.

A natural question (Emerton) is whether this relation is true for
finite length
unitary admissible representations of
{\bf GL}_{n}(F)
,
[F:{\bf Q}_{p}]<\infty

(or more generally any reductive group in place of
{\bf GL}_{n}
).

The Lamperti transformation in the infinite-dimensional setting and the genealogies of self-similar Markov processes
Arno Siri-Jégousse, Alejandro H. Wences

Abstract
We propose a change in focus from the prevalent paradigm based on the branching property as a tool to analyze the structure of population models, to one based on the self-similarity property, which we also introduce for the first time in the setting of measure-valued processes. By extending the well-known Lamperti transformation for self-similar Markov processes to the Banach-valued case we are able to generalize celebrated results in population genetics that describe the frequency-process of measure-valued stable branching processes in terms of the subfamily of Beta-Fleming-Viot processes. In our work we describe the frequency process of populations whose total size evolves as any positive self-similar Markov process in terms of general Λ \Lambda -Fleming-Viot processes. Our results demonstrate the potential power of the self-similar perspective for the study of population models in which the reproduction dynamics of the individuals depend on the total population size, allowing for more complex and realistic models.



Keywords—
self-similarity, Lamperti transformation, measure-valued processes, coalescent processes.

Introduction and Main Results

In the same spirit as in
[

]

In the latter, we have removed the drift terms and the constants for simplicity. Also, the derivative

(resp.

(resp.

The genealogy of such models is described by means of characterizing the associated frequency process

\left(\frac{\mu_{t}}{\left\lVert\mu_{t}\right\rVert}\right)_{t\geq 0}
,

\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert\coloneqq\mu([0,1])

The authors show that, after the time change

c_{{\beta-1}}(t)=\inf\{s\geq 0\colon\int_{0}^{s}\left\lVert\mu_{u}\right\rVert^{1-\beta}du>t\},
the frequency process becomes Markov in itself and

\Lambda
-Fleming-Viot (FV)

[
4

\Lambda
-FV processes and
\Lambda
-coalescents
[
4
]

The family of
\Lambda
-FV (resp.
\Lambda
-coalescents) generalizes the standard FV process (resp. the Kingman coalescent) to capture the population genetics dynamics of a wider range of neutral populations, including populations with highly skewed offspring distributions (but also many more models, including models with selection).
Still,
[
8
]
proved that only the Beta subfamily can be obtained from a branching process by using their method
based on path-wise
random time changes. For the rest of the super-processes (measure-valued branching processes), the associated frequency process is not Markov in itself under any random time change that is written in terms of the total population size (see their Lemma 3.5 for further details). This apparent lack of a branching process counterpart for the rest of the Lambda family has motivated research seeking variants of the
main results in
[
8
]
to obtain different
\Lambda
-FV (resp.
\Lambda
-coalescents)
for the frequency process (resp. genealogy) of branching processes, but using different transformations. An example is the culling procedure in
[
10
]
(see also
[
21
]
) who work
with the two-dimensional counterparts of FV processes, and who approximate populations with constant size and in distribution, losing the path-wise quality. Another example is the work of
[
25
]

who study the genealogy of multi-type branching processes locally in time by computing
the corresponding coalescence rates, which notably depend on the size of the populations of each of the finitely-many types. In our case it has motivated a change of perspective by now focusing on the self-similarity property instead, enabling us to use robust path-wise tools such as
the Lamperti transformation in the infinite-type setting. This technique also allows us to incorporate
dependencies in the reproduction dynamics of the individuals, mainly through the total population size, and also
to accommodate in a simple way the effect of this size on the coalescence rates and the genealogy.

A Markov process

-SS) if

\forall a>0,\hskip 9.24994pt\{X_{t},\mathbbm{P}^{X}_{x}\}=\{aX_{a^{-\alpha}t},\mathbbm{P}^{X}_{a^{-1}x}\}.

(1)

On the other hand, a two-coordinate process
\left(Z_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(\rho_{t},\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

\zeta^{Z}

,

,

\mathbbm{E}^{Z}_{\theta,z}\left[f(\rho_{t+s},\xi_{t+s}-\xi_{t}),t+s<\zeta^{Z}|\mathcal{F}_{t}\right]=\mathbbm{E}^{Z}_{\rho_{t},0}\left[f(\rho_{s},\xi_{s}),s<\zeta^{Z}\right]\mathbbm{1}_{t<\zeta^{Z}}.

(2)

Writing

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix

-SS Markov process

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}F(\mu)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert^{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\kappa}F(\mu)+\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\mu)+\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}F(\mu)\right)

where, for

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\kappa}F(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\mu(da)\kappa F^{\prime}(\mu;a),

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\mu)

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert\int_{\mathcal{T}}\mu(da)\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\mu;a,a),\text{ and }

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}F(\mu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\mu(da)}{\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert}\int_{{(0,1)}}\frac{\Lambda(d\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}\bigg\{F\left(\mu+\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt-F(\mu)-\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert(\left\lvert\log(1-\zeta)\right\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{\zeta<1/2})F^{\prime}(\mu;a)\bigg\}.

-SS Markov process with non-negative jumps. Furthermore, letting

-Fleming-Viot process; whereas the second coordinate is the Lévy process with triplet

\zeta^{-2}\Lambda(d\zeta)

\zeta\to-\log(1-\zeta)

(0,1)

Through an appropriate choice of the parameters

\Lambda

The reproduction dynamics of the (sub)family of self-similar populations of Theorem
1.1
have the following interesting biological interpretation, which also motivates the study of the more general case: the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\kappa}+\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}+\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda},

(4)

is in fact the generator of a measure-valued Markov process describing a population whose total size evolves as the exponential of a Lévy process (see Theorem

Then, the extra scaling term

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(\frac{X_{c_{\alpha}(t)}}{\left\lVert X_{c_{\alpha}(t)}\right\rVert},\log(\left\lVert X_{c_{\alpha}(t)}\right\rVert)\right)_{t\geq 0}

Conversely, let
\left(\rho_{t},\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

\gamma_{\alpha}(t)\coloneqq\inf\left\{s>0\colon\int_{0}^{s}e^{\alpha\xi_{u}}du\geq t\right\},\hskip 9.24994ptt\in[0,\int_{0}^{\zeta^{\xi}}e^{\alpha\xi_{u}}du].

Our results show an unexplored link between the fields of mathematical population genetics and self-similar Markov processes in infinite dimensions, motivating new research and opening new questions in both of these fields separately, but also at their intersection.
For instance, the populations driven by

\mathcal{F}
above, characterized by four parameters (the Lévy triplet and the self-similarity index)
constitute only a sub-class of the entire family of measure-valued self-similar processes
which is yet to be fully described.
Whether one will need a new model to describe their corresponding genealogies, outside or extending the family of
\Lambda
(and more generally
\Xi
) coalescent processes, is still an open question. At the same time, measure-valued processes, together with the well-established analytic tools available in population genetics such as duality methods, may serve as a suitable template for the development of the theory of self-similar Markov processes and their Lamperti transformations
in infinite dimensions.

\displaystyle=P^{Y}_{t}(e^{z}\theta,{\Phi^{-1}(A)})=P^{Y}_{t}(e^{z+a}\theta,e^{a}{\Phi^{-1}(A)})

\displaystyle=P^{Z}_{t}((\theta,z+a),A+(0,a)).

Theorem 2.2

(Self-Similar Lamperti Time Change)
.

\text{a.s. }\forall t\geq 0,\hskip 9.24994ptY_{t}=X_{\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert Y_{u}\right\rVert^{\alpha}du\wedge\zeta^{X}}.

(7)

be a standard Markov process with trajectories

To ease notations, we will omit the index

By Propositions IV.1.6 and IV.1.13 in

(resp.

it follows that
\left(X_{c(t)}\right)_{t\geq 0}
(resp.
\left(Y_{\gamma(t)}\right)_{t\geq 0}

the mapping

, the space of bounded Borel functions on

, so that the process defined by

in
i)
)
is indeed a standard process; and similarly for the process defined by

in
iii)
).

We now show that the process

.

-SS property of

We proceed similarly to prove
iii)

For

Write
\psi(t)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert X_{s}\right\rVert^{-\alpha}ds

If

,
and
\zeta_{\infty}^{X}=\infty

\psi(\infty)=\infty
.
In any case
\psi
is monotonic and absolutely continuous on compact

with inverse function

and a.e. derivative

.
It follows that for any

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

\left(Y_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

\mathcal{S}_{a}\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{A})

iii)

The process

Preliminary objects of study

3.1
\Lambda
-coalescents

We expose the construction of coalescents with multiple merger from the seminal works of
[
34
,
35
]
.
For a positive integer
p
, let
[p]=\{1,\cdots,p\}
and
\mathscr{P}_{[p]}
be the space of partitions of
[p]
endowed with the discrete topology.
We call the elements of any partition
\pi\in\mathscr{P}_{[p]}
the blocks of
\pi
and denote its number by
\#\pi
.
Let
\Lambda
be a finite measure on
[0,1]
which can be decomposed as

\Lambda=\Lambda(\{0\})\delta_{0}+\mathbbm{1}_{{(0,1)}}\Lambda.

The
(p,\Lambda)
-coalescent process
\left(\Pi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a Markov jump process with values in
\mathscr{P}_{[p]}
that evolves through
“coagulations” or mergers. The latter consists of constructing a new coarser partition of
[p]
from
an initial
\pi\in\mathscr{P}_{[p]}

by taking the union of a collection of blocks that are present in
\pi
.
The coagulations of
\left(\Pi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
are directed by the measure
\Lambda
via the
following rules; at time
t\geq 0
:

Pairwise coagulations:

Any pair of blocks of
\Pi_{t}
coagulate at rate
\Lambda(\{0\})
.

Coin-flip coagulations:

Any collection of
2\leq i\leq j=\#\Pi_{t}
blocks of
\Pi_{t}
, coagulate
into a single block at rate
\beta_{j,i}^{(\Lambda)}\coloneqq\int_{{(0,1)}}\zeta^{i-2}(1-\zeta)^{j-i}\Lambda(d\zeta)
.

The first dynamics correspond to those of Kingman’s coalescent
[
26
]
.
The second dynamics have the following well-known interpretation: at rate
\zeta^{-2}{\Lambda(d\zeta)}

a value
\zeta\in{(0,1)}
is drawn; then, each block of
\Pi_{t}
decides to participate in the coagulation event
with probability
\zeta
.
This representation for the rates implies that those processes are consistent according to
p
and can thus be extended to
p=\infty
. In this case, we will talk about
\Lambda
-coalescents. In the present work we leave out the case when
\Lambda
has an atom at
1
, which corresponds to adding an additional rate
\Lambda({1})
at which all the blocks decide to coagulate.

A famous and important example of
\Lambda
-coalescent processes
is the family of Beta coalescents
[
37
,
20
]

in which
\Lambda(d\zeta)=c{\zeta^{1-\beta}(1-\zeta)^{\beta-1}}d\zeta
for
\beta\in 0,2)
.
See
[
2
]
for a thorough exposition of general coalescent processes.

3.2
The
\Lambda
-Fleming-Viot processes

We begin with a few remarks on the space
\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})
endowed with the topology of weak convergence.
By Theorem 1.14 in
[
30
]
the space
\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})
is locally-compact
whenever
\mathcal{T}
is compact.
In fact, following the proof of this theorem, the set

\mathtt{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T})=\{\mu\in\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})\colon\mu(\mathcal{T})\leq r\}
, for
r\geq 0
, is compact.

Let us write

\left\langle f,\mu\right\rangle\coloneqq\int\mu(da)f(a).

An important class of functions in
\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
is the algebra of polynomials

\mathtt{Pol}\left(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})\right)
which is the linear span of monomials of the form

F_{\phi,p}(\rho)=\left\langle\phi,\rho^{\otimes p}\right\rangle,\hskip 9.24994pt\phi\in\mathtt{\bar{C}}(\mathcal{T}^{p}).

By a straightforward extension of Lemma 2.1.2 in
[
13
]
(extending the arguments
therein to
\mathtt{\bar{C}}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
), the polynomials
\mathtt{Pol}\left(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})\right)
are
dense in the topology of uniform convergence on compact sets on
\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
, and convergence determining for
the topology of weak convergence in
\mathtt{M}(\mathtt{M}_{r}(\mathcal{T}))
for
every
r\geq 0
.

A function
F\in\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
is said to be differentiable if
its derivative in the direction of
a\in\mathcal{T}
(more precisely of
\delta_{a}
) given by

F^{\prime}(\mu;a)\coloneqq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\frac{F(\mu+\epsilon\delta_{a})-F(\mu)}{\epsilon},

exists and is continuous as a function of
a\in\mathcal{T}
. We denote by
F^{\prime\prime}(\mu;a,b)
the
second derivative of
F
, first in the direction of
a
and then in the direction of
b
; whereas
for higher derivatives
we write
F^{(\ell)}(\mu;a_{1},\cdots,a_{\ell})
for the corresponding sequential derivatives
in the directions of
a_{1},\cdots,a_{\ell}
.
By Lemma 2.1.2 in
[
13
]
, the polynomials
\mathtt{Pol}\left(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T})\right)
are infinitely differentiable. Their derivatives are given by

F_{\phi,p}^{\prime}(\mu;a)=\sum_{i=1}^{p}\left\langle\phi,\mu^{\otimes i-1}\otimes\delta_{a}\otimes\mu^{p-i-1}\right\rangle,

and recursively, for
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\cdots,a_{\ell})
,

F^{(\ell)}_{\phi,p}(\mu;\bm{a})=\begin{cases}\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{a},\mu,p)}\left\langle\phi,\otimes_{i=1}^{p}\sigma_{i}\right\rangle&\text{ if }\ell\leq p\\
0&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(9)

where the sum is over all the permutations
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(\bm{a},\mu,p)
, say
\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{p})
,
of the atomic measures
\delta_{a_{1}},\cdots,\delta_{a_{\ell}}
and
p-\ell
copies of
\mu
.
Note that in particular

\left\lvert F_{\phi,p}(\mu)+F_{\phi,p}^{\prime}(\mu;a)+F_{\phi,p}^{\prime\prime}(\mu;a,b)\right\rvert\leq C\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert(1+\cdots+\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert^{p})

for some
C
depending on
p
,
so that the polynomials
\mathtt{Pol}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
are contained in the set

\displaystyle D\coloneqq\Big\{

\displaystyle F\in\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))\colon\exists C>0,p\geq 1\text{ s.t. }

\displaystyle\left\lvert F(\mu)\right\rvert+\left\lvert F^{\prime}(\mu;a)\right\rvert+\left\lvert F^{\prime\prime}(\mu;a,a)\right\rvert\leq C(1+\dots+\left\lVert\mu\right\rVert^{p})\Big\},

(10)

which will appear in the domain of generators of measure-valued processes further ahead.

Now, let
\Lambda
and
\beta_{j,i}^{(\Lambda)}
be as in section
3.1
.
The
\Lambda
-Fleming-Viot process
[

Lemma 4.1
.

There exists a solution to the martingale problem for
\mathcal{H}
for any initial condition

.

Sketch of proof.

We only provide a sketch of proof and leave the details to the reader. We deal only with the dynamics driven by the measure
\Lambda
, the rest of the dynamics correspond to independent Kingman coalescent and Brownian components respectively, which are easy to incorporate.
Fix

as above. One way to construct the processes

Remark 3

Looking at

recursively as

\displaystyle\nu_{s}\coloneqq\begin{cases}e^{\hat{\kappa}s}\hat{\nu}_{s}\left(\nu\right)&\text{ if }0\leq s<t_{1},\\
e^{\hat{\kappa}(s-t_{i})}\hat{\nu}_{s-t_{i}}\left(\nu_{t_{i}-}+\left\lVert\nu_{t_{i}-}\right\rVert\frac{\zeta_{i}}{1-\zeta_{i}}\delta_{a_{i}}\right)&\text{ if }t_{i}\leq s<t_{i+1},\end{cases}

(31)

where the processes
\left(\hat{\nu}_{t}(\cdot)\right)_{t\geq 0}
that appear in each time interval
[t_{i},t_{i+1}]
are all independent and
the points
(a_{i})_{1\leq i\leq K}
are chosen independently
according to
\nu_{t_{i}-}/\left\lVert\nu_{t_{i}-}\right\rVert
.

Lemma 4.5
.

The process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is Markov with generator
given by
\mathcal{G}
in
(
4
)
and domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma})
containing
D_{1}
.

Proof.

First, when
\Lambda\equiv 0
, i.e. for the Markov process
\left(e^{\hat{\kappa}t}\hat{\nu}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
, a simple
computation yields

\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbbm{E}_{\nu}[F(e^{\hat{\kappa}t}\hat{\nu}_{t})]=\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\hat{\kappa}}F(\nu)+\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\nu).

To add the jumps, and compute the resulting generator, we use Theorem 2.4 in

[
36
]
.
For this, we set
(in their notation)
\phi_{t}=C_{\Lambda}t
with
C_{\Lambda}\coloneqq\int_{[0,1]}\zeta^{-2}\Lambda(d\zeta)
and the transition kernel
K(\nu,\cdot)

given by, for
F\in\mathtt{\bar{B}}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
,

K(\nu,F)=\begin{cases}\int_{{(0,1)}}\zeta^{-2}\frac{\Lambda(d\zeta)}{C_{\Lambda}}\int_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\nu}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}(da)F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a})&\text{ whenever }\nu\neq 0,\\
F(0)&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Then,
\phi_{t}
being of “Kac type”, according to Theorem 2.4 (and Example 1) in
[
36
]
,
the generator of the process in (
31
)
is given by

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\mathbbm{E}[F(e^{\hat{\kappa}t}\hat{\nu}_{t})]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\hat{\kappa}}F(\nu)+\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\nu)

\displaystyle+\int_{{(0,1)}}\zeta^{-2}\Lambda(d\zeta)\int_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\nu}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}(da)\left\{F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a})-F(\nu)\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\kappa}F(\nu)+\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\nu)+\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}F(\nu)

with domain
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma})
.
∎

4.3
A Weak Limit and Martingale Problem

In this section we choose a particular sequence of processes
\left\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\right\}_{n}

that will weakly approximate a process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
with generator
\mathcal{G}
as in (
4
), the latter will
be proven
to be a Feller process.
In the following Proposition
4.6
, and in order to simplify technical arguments, here we only focus on the construction of the process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
for which we impose stronger conditions than just the convergence of the generators.

In the following we set
\Lambda(\{0\})=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}
. Also, throughout this section,
we will work with the jumping measure
\mathbbm{1}_{0<\zeta\leq 1/2}\Lambda(d\zeta)
instead of
\Lambda(d\zeta)
for the
limit process; while
the approximating processes
\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
will have jumping measure

\Lambda_{n}(d\zeta)=\mathbbm{1}_{1/n<\zeta\leq 1/2}\Lambda(d\zeta).

(32)

Removing the atoms in which
\zeta>1/2
ensures that the jumps of
\left(\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}
are bounded,
which in turn
simplifies the proof of tightness for the family
\left\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\right\}_{n}
. This imposes
no loss of generality for the construction of the process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
with
generator of the general form
\mathcal{G}
since the finitely-many large jumps that
occur with intensity
\mathbbm{1}_{\zeta>1/2}\Lambda(d\zeta)
can always be “added back” via the
same argument as in Lemma
4.5
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
be constructed as in section
4.2
with
\Lambda_{n}
as in (
32
)
where, furthermore,
\nu^{(n)}_{0}\underset{\mathtt{d}}{\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}}\nu_{0}
and

\sup_{n}\mathbbm{E}[\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{0}\right\rVert^{p}]<\infty
for every
p\geq 1
.
Then, as
n\to\infty
,

\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\Rightarrow\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

in the Skorohod topology on
D([0,\infty);\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
.
The process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is SMH and Feller with generator of the form
\mathcal{G}
in
D_{2}
.
Furthermore,
\left(\nu_{t}/\left\lVert\nu_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a
(\Lambda+\sigma^{2}/2\delta_{0})
-Fleming-Viot process,
and
\left(\log\left\lVert\nu_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}
a Lévy process with triplet
(d+\kappa,\sigma,\Pi)
.

Proof.

We proceed in multiple steps: proving existence of solutions
to the
\mathcal{G}
-martingale problem through tightness of the
family of processes
\left\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\right\}_{n}
in the Skorohod topology for
D([0,\infty);\mathtt{M}\left(\mathcal{T}\right))
,
and the convergence of the approximating generators
\mathcal{G}_{n}
to
\mathcal{G}
;
identifying the limit; and showing that it is Feller.
The proof
of tightness rests on the well-known Aldous-Rebolledo criterion (e.g. Theorem 1.17 in
[
16
]
), whereas the characterization and the properties of the limiting
process will be a consequence of the duality relation described in Proposition
4.2
.

Step 1: Tightness.

The proof rests on Theorem III.9.1 in
[
17
]
.
Let us first prove that

\limsup_{n\geq 1}\mathbbm{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert\geq r\right)\leq\frac{1}{r}\mathbbm{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert]\overset{r\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

(33)

which implies the compact containment condition (eq. (9.1) therein) for our processes
since,

\mathcal{T}
being compact, the space
\mathtt{M}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)
is locally compact. In fact
the balls of radius
r
,

\mathtt{M}_{r}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)=\left\{\mu\in\mathtt{M}\left(\mathcal{T}\right)\colon\left\langle 1,\mu\right\rangle\leq r\right\},

are compact (see e.g. Theorem 1.14 in
[
30
]
).

Using Lemma
4.5
and plugging
F(\nu)=h(\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert)
in (
4
) (take e.g.
\phi\equiv 1

and
z=1
in (
18
) and (
4.1
))
we obtain that
\left(\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(e^{\xi^{(n)}_{t}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
in distribuion where
\left(\xi^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is the Lévy process

\xi^{(n)}_{t}=\log(\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{0}\right\rVert)+(\kappa+d)t+M^{(n)}_{t}.

Here

\left(M^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is its
martingale
component, given by

M^{(n)}_{t}=\sigma B^{(n)}_{t}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{1/n<\zeta<1/2}\left\lvert\log(1-\zeta)\right\rvert\tilde{}\mathcal{P}^{(n)}(ds,d\zeta),

where
\left(B^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a brownian motion and

\tilde{}\mathcal{P}^{(n)}
is a compensated Poisson random measure on
\mathbb{R_{+}}\times[1/n,1/2]
of intensity
ds\times\zeta^{-2}\Lambda(d\zeta)
.
Markov’s inequality followed by Hölder’s inequality yield, for any
p,q
with
1/p+1/q=1
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{P}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert\geq r\right)\leq\frac{e^{\left\lvert\kappa+d\right\rvert T}}{r}\mathbbm{E}\left[\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{0}\right\rVert\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}e^{M^{(n)}_{t}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{\left\lvert\kappa+d\right\rvert T}}{r}\mathbbm{E}\left[\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{0}\right\rVert^{q}\right]^{1/q}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}e^{pM^{(n)}_{t}}\right]^{1/p}.

(34)

The
\mathtt{L}^{q}
-norm of
\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{0}\right\rVert
above is uniformly bounded on
n
by hypothesis. We now bound, also uniformly on
n
, the second expectation in the r.h.s. above. Then taking
r\to\infty
in the last line above will yield (
33
).
By Doob’s maximal
inequality applied to the submartingale
\left(e^{pM^{(n)}_{t}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
we have for some constant
C_{p}
, and plugging Campbell’s formula in the second inequality below,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}e^{pM^{(n)}_{t}}]\leq C_{p}\mathbbm{E}[e^{pM^{(n)}_{T}}]

(35)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}e^{Tp^{2}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}}\exp\left\{T\int_{1/n}^{1/2}\frac{\Lambda(d\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}\left\{e^{-p\log(1-\zeta)}-1+p\log(1-\zeta)\right\}\right\}.

Since the integrand above is of order
\mathcal{O}(\zeta^{2})
as
\zeta\to 0
, we have

\int_{0}^{1/2}\frac{\Lambda(d\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}\left\{e^{-p\log(1-\zeta)}-1+p\log(1-\zeta)\right\}<\infty,

which gives a bound that is uniform on
n
for (
35
).

In view of Theorem III.9.1 in
[
17
]
,
and the fact that the polynomials
\mathtt{Pol}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
are dense in
\mathtt{\bar{C}}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
for
the topology of uniform convergence in compact sets;
it remains to prove that
\left\{\left(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})\right)_{t\geq 0}\right\}_{n}
is relatively
compact for every
F\in\mathtt{Pol}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))\subset D
, where we recall that
D
is as in (
3.2
).
For the latter, in turn, we will prove the conditions of the Aldous-Rebolledo criterion.

First, the tightness of
\{F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})\}_{n}
for fixed
t
follows directly from (
33
).

Second, by Lemma
4.5
,
N_{t}^{(n)}\coloneqq F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})ds
defines a martingale
so that the finite variation process of
\left(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})\right)_{t\geq 0}
is given by

V^{(n)}_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})ds.

On the other hand, the compensator of the process
\left((N_{t}^{(n)})^{2}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is given by

\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})ds

where
\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}
is the carré du champ operator associated to
\mathcal{G}_{n}
. The latter is given by

\mathbf{\Gamma}_{n}F=\mathcal{G}_{n}F^{2}-2F\mathcal{G}_{n}F

(see e.g. section 1.2.2 in
[
24
]
). The remaining
conditions of the Aldous-Rebolledo criterion on
\left(V^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
and
\left(\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
(which are given by (
37
) and (
38
) below) will follow
once we prove
that for
any
F\in\mathtt{Pol}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
,
\delta>0
, and any stopping time
0\leq\tau_{n}\leq T
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq\delta}\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+\theta}(\left\lvert F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert\vee 1)\left\lvert\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert ds\right]\leq\delta C

(36)

for some
C>0
depending only on
F
.
Indeed,
by Markov’s inequality we obtain, on the one hand,

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq\delta}\mathbbm{P}\left(\left\lvert V^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}+\theta}-V^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}}\right\rvert>\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq\delta}\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+\theta}\left\lvert\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert ds\right]\leq\frac{\delta C}{\epsilon}.

So that, taking
\delta=\epsilon^{2}/C
, (
36
) implies

\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq\delta}\mathbbm{P}\left[\left\lvert V^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}+\theta}-V^{(n)}_{\tau_{n}}\right\rvert>\epsilon\right]\leq\epsilon.

(37)

On the other hand,
for any
\delta>0
,

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}\left[\left\lvert\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{\tau_{n}+\delta}-\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{\tau_{n}}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+\delta}\left\lvert\mathcal{G}_{n}F^{2}(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert ds\right]+2\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+\delta}\left\lvert F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert ds\right],

so that now (
36
) applied to each term in the r.h.s. above,
and choosing
\delta
adequately,
yields

\sup_{n\geq n_{0}}\sup_{0\leq\theta\leq\delta}\mathbbm{P}\left[\left\lvert\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{\tau_{n}+\theta}-\left[N^{(n)}\right]_{\tau_{n}}\right\rvert>\epsilon\right]\leq\epsilon

(38)

again through Markov’s inequality.

Let us then prove (
36
).
On the one hand, by (
3.2
), the diffusion and drift terms of
\mathcal{G}_{n}F
are
uniformly bounded on
n\geq 1
by

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{G}^{(B)}_{\sigma}F(\nu)\right\rvert+\left\lvert\mathcal{G}^{(D)}_{\kappa}F(\nu)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\int_{\mathcal{T}}\nu(da)\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}\left\lvert F^{\prime\prime}(\nu;a,a)\right\rvert+\int_{\mathcal{T}}\nu(da)\kappa\left\lvert F^{\prime}(\nu;a)\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{1}}\right)

(39)

for some
C_{1}>0
and
p_{1}\geq 1
. On the other hand, for the jump component

\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda_{n}}F
built from the measure
\Lambda_{n}
defined in (
32
),
we have

\left\lVert\Lambda_{n}(d\zeta)-\mathbbm{1}_{0<\zeta\leq 1/2}\Lambda(d\zeta)\right\rVert_{TV}=\Lambda([0,\frac{1}{n}))\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(40)

Fix a measure
\nu
and regard

F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a})
as a function of
\zeta
. Then writing

\psi(\zeta)=\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}
so that
\psi^{\prime}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{(1-\zeta)^{2}}
and
\psi^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)=\frac{2}{(1-\zeta)^{3}}
,
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\zeta}F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a})

\displaystyle=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a}+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert(\psi(\zeta+h)-\psi(\zeta))\delta_{a})-F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a})}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert(\psi(\zeta+h)-\psi(\zeta))}

\displaystyle\times\lim_{h\to 0}\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\psi(\zeta+h)-\psi(\zeta)}{h}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert F^{\prime}(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a};a)\psi^{\prime}(\zeta),

and, similarly,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\zeta^{2}}F(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a})

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{2}F^{\prime\prime}(\nu+\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a};a,a)(\psi^{\prime}(\zeta))^{2}

\displaystyle+\hskip 9.24994pt\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert F^{\prime}(\nu+\psi(\zeta)\delta_{a};a)\psi^{\prime\prime}(\zeta).

Taylor’s expansion for
\zeta
near
0
and the assumption
F\in D
give, for some constant
C_{2}>0
and some

p_{2}\geq 1
,

\left\lvert F\left(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a}\right)-F(\nu)-\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\zeta F^{\prime}\left(\nu;a\right)\right\rvert\leq C_{2}\left(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{2}}\right)\frac{\zeta^{2}}{(1-\zeta)^{4}}.

Furthermore, using
that

\forall 0<\zeta<1,\hskip 9.24994pt\left\lvert\zeta-\left\lvert\log(1-\zeta)\right\rvert\right\rvert=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{k}}{k}\leq\frac{\zeta^{2}}{1-\zeta}

which is continuous and bounded on
\zeta\in[0,1/2)
,
we see that the function of
\zeta

\mathbbm{1}_{\zeta\in{(0,1)}}\left\langle\frac{F\left(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a}\right)-F(\nu)-\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\left\lvert\log(1-\zeta)\right\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{\zeta<1/2}F^{\prime}(\nu;a)}{\zeta^{2}},\frac{\nu(da)}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}\right\rangle

is bounded uniformly on
[0,1/2]
by

C_{3}(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{2}})

for some
C_{3}>0
depending only on
F
. Thus (
40
) yields

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{{\Lambda_{n}}}F(\nu)-\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}F(\nu)\right\rvert\leq C_{3}\left(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{2}}\right)\left\lVert\Lambda_{n}(d\zeta)-\Lambda(d\zeta)\right\rVert_{TV}

(41)

and, in particular,

\left\lvert\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{{\Lambda_{n}}}F(\nu)\right\rvert\leq C_{4}\left(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{2}}\right)

(42)

for some
C_{4}
.
Putting together (
4.3
) and (
42
),
and also using that
F(\nu)
satisfies the same type of inequality (by (
3.2
)),
we obtain for some
C>0
and
p\geq 1
,
and for any stopping time
0\leq\tau_{n}\leq T
and
0\leq\theta\leq\delta<1
,

\mathbbm{E}\left[\int_{\tau_{n}}^{\tau_{n}+\theta}(\left\lvert F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert\vee 1)\left\lvert\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})\right\rvert ds\right]\leq C\delta\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{t\in[0,T+\delta]}1+\dots+e^{p\xi^{(n)}_{t}}\right]

(43)

where we have again used
\left(\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(e^{\xi^{(n)}_{t}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
.
It remains to prove that the expectation in the r.h.s. above is uniformly bounded on
n\geq 1
. But this follows from
(
4.3
) and
(
35
).

Step 2: Limiting
martingale problem.

Assume that, along a subsequence
\left\{n_{i}\right\}_{i\geq 1}
(that we will simply denote by
n
to ease notation) we
have

\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\overset{n\to\infty}{\underset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}

in
D([0,\infty),\mathtt{M}\left(\mathcal{T}\right))
for some process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
. We now prove
that
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
must solve the martingale problem for (
4
) in the domain

\{F\in D\colon\mathcal{G}F\in\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))\}
.

We prove the latter via a mild adaptation of the proof of Lemma IV.5.1 in
[
17
]

to the case where
F
and
\mathcal{G}F
may not be bounded (but still continuous), and also where
the convergence of the generators
\mathcal{G}_{n}
to
\mathcal{G}
is uniform but only
on compact sets (taking advantage of the fact that we have already proved the compact containment condition (
33
)).
We only prove that the key equation (IV.5.3) therein (eq. (
4.3
) below)
also holds in our setting.

Let
h_{i},1\leq i\leq k,
be functions in
\mathtt{\bar{C}}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
. Also let
0\leq s_{i}\leq s<t

all belong to the set
\{u\colon\nu_{u}=\nu_{u-}\text{ a.s.}\}
.
Then, for
F\in D
such that
\mathcal{G}F\in\mathtt{C}(\mathtt{M}(\mathcal{T}))
, we have, by the continuous mapping theorem,

\displaystyle(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})-F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu^{(n)}_{u})du)\prod_{i}h_{i}(\nu^{(n)}_{s_{i}})

\displaystyle\overset{n\to\infty}{\underset{d}{\longrightarrow}}(F(\nu_{t})-F(\nu_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu_{u})du)\prod_{i}h_{i}(\nu_{s_{i}}).

Let us now prove that this convergence also holds in expectation. By Theorem 3.5 in
[
6
]
we need only verify
that the random variables in the first line above are uniformly integrable. Observe that, since
F\in D
, the first line above is bounded by

\displaystyle\left\lvert(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})-F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu^{(n)}_{u})du)\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\nu^{(n)}_{s_{i}})\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C(\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}1+\cdots+\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{u}\right\rVert^{p})\prod_{i=1}^{k}\left\lVert h_{i}\right\rVert_{{\infty}}

(44)

for some
C,p\geq 0
(where we have also used (
42
)). That the r.h.s.
is uniformly integrable, in turn, is a simple consequence of Theorem 3.6 in
[
6
]

together with the convergence

\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{u}\right\rVert^{p}\overset{n\to\infty}{\underset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\left\lVert\nu_{u}\right\rVert^{p},

and the bound in (
35
).
Indeed, the corresponding a.s. convergence ensured by Skorohod representation theorem, and the
bound in (
35
), together yield the convergence in expectation

\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{u}\right\rVert^{p}\right]\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathbbm{E}\left[\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\left\lVert\nu_{u}\right\rVert^{p}\right].

We thus conclude

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(F(\nu_{t})-F(\nu_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu_{u})du)\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\nu_{s_{i}})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbm{E}[(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})-F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu^{(n)}_{u})du)\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\nu^{(n)}_{s_{i}})].

(45)

We now approximate
\mathcal{G}
by
\mathcal{G}_{n}
in the above limit. Observe that, by (
41
), and since the other two components of
{\mathcal{G}}_{n}
are constant on
n
, we
have

\left\lvert{\mathcal{G}}_{n}F(\nu)-\mathcal{G}F(\nu)\right\rvert\leq C_{3}\left(1+\dots+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert^{p_{2}}\right)\Lambda((0,1/n))\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

uniformly for
\nu
on the compact sets
\mathtt{M}_{r}
,
r\geq 0
. Plugging the above bound
into (
4.3
),
together with the bound in (
4.3
)
and the uniform integrability of
\sup_{s\leq u\leq t}\left\lVert\nu^{(n)}_{u}\right\rVert^{p}
,
easily yield

\displaystyle\mathbbm{E}[(F(\nu_{t})-F(\nu_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}F(\nu_{u})du)\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\nu_{s_{i}})]

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbbm{E}[(F(\nu^{(n)}_{t})-F(\nu^{(n)}_{s})-\int_{s}^{t}\mathcal{G}_{n}F(\nu^{(n)}_{u})du)\prod_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}(\nu^{(n)}_{s_{i}})]=0,

(46)

where we have used Lemma
4.5
in the last equality.

Step 3: Identification of the limit and Feller property.

The identification of the limit along any weakly convergent subsequence
is a consequence of the previous Step 2 (limiting martingale problem) together with Proposition
4.2
where uniqueness of solutions to the martingale problem for
\mathcal{G}
is proved.

On the other hand, for the characterization of each of the coordinates in the limit,
observe that
computing
\mathcal{G}F
for functions of the form
F(\nu)=h(\log(\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert))
with
h\in\mathtt{C^{2}_{0}}(\mathbb{R})
, and

F(\nu)=H_{\pi}^{(\phi)}(\nu)=\left\langle\phi,\left(\frac{\nu}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}\right)^{\otimes p}\right\rangle
with
\phi\in\mathtt{\bar{B}}(\mathcal{T}^{p})
respectively
–using e.g. (
19
) and (
4.1
)–; we obtain
the generators of the Lévy process with triple
(d+\kappa,\sigma,\Pi)
and the
(\Lambda+\sigma^{2}/2\delta_{0})
-Fleming-Viot
respectively. The result then follows from the uniqueness of solutions to their corresponding martingale problems.

We now prove that

\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is Feller. First note that by Proposition 3.3 in
[
19
]
the Fleming-Viot process

\left(\rho_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(\nu_{t}/\left\lVert\nu_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}
is Feller, as well as the Lévy process
\left(\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}=\left(\log\left\lVert\nu_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}
.
Then, to prove the continuity in probability of
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
at
t=0
,
note that
\rho_{t}\overset{t\to 0}{\underset{{\operatorname{P}}}{\longrightarrow}}\rho_{0}
and

\xi_{t}\overset{t\to 0}{\underset{{\operatorname{P}}}{\longrightarrow}}\xi_{0}
imply
(\rho_{t},\xi_{t})\overset{t\to 0}{\underset{{\operatorname{P}}}{\longrightarrow}}(\rho_{0},\xi_{0})
,
so that
\nu_{t}=e^{\xi_{t}}\rho_{t}\overset{t\to 0}{\underset{{\operatorname{P}}}{\longrightarrow}}\nu_{0}
. We now prove the weak continuity of
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
as a function of the initial state
\nu_{0}
.
Consider a sequence of processes
\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\}_{n}
started at
\nu^{(n)}_{0}
where
\nu^{(n)}_{0}\overset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\nu_{0}
.
By the same argument as in Step 1 (Tightness) above, with
\Lambda_{n}
therein set to
\Lambda_{n}=\Lambda
,
the sequence
\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\}_{n}
is tight. Furthermore, now using Step 2 (limiting martingale problem), the limit along any weakly-convergent subsequence of
\{\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\}_{n}
is also identified in this case
to be the unique solution to the martingale problem for
\mathcal{G}
started at
\nu_{0}
. Then
\left(\nu^{(n)}_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}\overset{n\to\infty}{\underset{d}{\longrightarrow}}\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
follows.

Finally, the fact that
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is SMH follows from verifying item
iv)
in Proposition
2.3
. Given
(
15
), we only do this for the term
\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}
. This is simply given by
observing that, for
b>0
, we have
(\mathcal{S}_{b}F)^{\prime}(\nu;a)=bF^{\prime}(b\nu;a)
so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{S}_{b}F(\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{T}}\frac{\nu(da)}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}\int_{{(0,1)}}\frac{\Lambda(d\zeta)}{\zeta^{2}}\bigg\{F\left(b\nu+b\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\frac{\zeta}{1-\zeta}\delta_{a}\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt\hskip 9.24994pt-F(b\nu)-b\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert(\left\lvert\log(1-\zeta)\right\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{\zeta<1/2})F^{\prime}(b\nu;a)\bigg\}.

Then

\mathcal{S}_{b^{-1}}\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{S}_{b}F(\nu)=\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}\mathcal{S}_{b}F(b^{-1}\nu)=\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Lambda}F(\nu).

∎

Remark 6
.

Theorems
4.3
and
4.4
could be generalized in the following two directions using the same arguments in the proof. We do not include details in this paper to avoid such burden in notation in our main results.

i)

A general positive SS Markov process, see
[
3
]
, can be attained for the total mass process
\left(\left\lVert\mu_{t}\right\rVert\right)_{t\geq 0}

by adding negative jumps of the form
x\to x\zeta
,
0<\zeta<1,
with intensity
\Theta^{-}(d\zeta)
,
the pushforward of a Lévy measure
\Pi
on
(-\infty,0)
(i.e. a measure satisfying
\int_{-\infty}^{0}(\zeta^{2}\wedge 1)\Pi(d\zeta)<\infty
) under the
transformation
\zeta\to e^{\zeta}
.
A simple way to do this is to
allow for the underlying SMH process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
to have jumps of the form
\nu\to\nu\zeta
with intensity
\Theta^{-}(d\zeta)
.
In this sense,
a term of the form

\mathcal{G}^{(J-)}_{\Theta^{-}}F(\nu)=\int_{(0,1)}\Theta^{-}(d\zeta)\left\{F\left(\nu\zeta\right)-F(\nu)\right\}

is added to its generator.
Note that adding negative jumps in this way does not change the dynamics of the corresponding frequency process,
so that the arguments leading to the construction of the SMH process
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
can be easily generalized in this direction.

ii)

It is possible to extend our results to the

\Xi
-Fleming-Viot case with
\int_{[0,1]^{\mathbb{N}}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\zeta_{k}^{2}\Xi(d\zeta)<\infty
.
In this case
the generator of
\left(\nu_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is given by updating the jumping part
\mathcal{G}^{(J)}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{(J)}_{\Xi}F(\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\left(\mathcal{T}\right)^{\mathbb{N}}}\left(\frac{\nu}{\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert}\right)^{\otimes\mathbb{N}}(da)\int_{{(0,1)}^{\mathbb{N}}}\Xi(d\zeta)\bigg\{F\left(\nu+\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{i}}{1-\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}}}\delta_{a_{i}}\right)-F(\nu)

\displaystyle\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt\hskip 18.49988pt-\left\lVert\nu\right\rVert\left\lvert\log(1-\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}})\right\rvert\mathbbm{1}_{\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}}<1/2}F^{\prime}(\nu;a)\bigg\}.

Heuristically, jumps are of the form

\nu\to\nu+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta_{i}}{1-\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}}}\delta_{a_{k}}

where the positions of the new atoms
(a_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are
i.i.d with distribution
\frac{\nu_{t-}}{\left\lVert\nu_{t-}\right\rVert}
and
the atom sizes
\zeta=(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2},\dots)
, satisfying
\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}}<1
arrive with intensity
dt\otimes\Xi(d\zeta)
.

The process
\left(\rho_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
becomes the
\Xi
-Fleming-Viot process and
\left(\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0}
is a Lévy process with compensated jumps given by

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}\left\lvert\log(1-\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}})\right\rvert\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(ds,d\zeta)

where
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}(ds,d\zeta)=\mathcal{P}(ds,\zeta)-\mathbbm{1}_{\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{\mathtt{l}_{1}}<1/2}ds\otimes\Xi(d\zeta)

and
\mathcal{P}
is a Poisson point process on
\mathbb{R}^{+}\times(0,1)^{\mathbb{N}}
with intensity
ds\otimes\Xi(d\zeta)
.

Mixed-state topological order and the errorfield double formulation of decoherence-induced transitions
Yimu Bao, Ruihua Fan, Ashvin Vishwanath, Ehud Altman

Abstract
We develop an effective field theory characterizing the impact of decoherence on states with abelian topological order and on their capacity to protect quantum information. The decoherence appears as a temporal defect in the double topological quantum field theory that describes the pure density matrix of the uncorrupted state, and it drives a boundary phase transition involving anyon condensation at a critical coupling strength. The ensuing decoherence-induced phases and the loss of quantum information are classified by the Lagrangian subgroups of the double topological order. Our framework generalizes the error recovery transitions, previously derived for certain stabilizer codes, to generic topologically ordered states and shows that they stem from phase transitions in the intrinsic topological order characterizing the mixed state.



Recent experiments in many-body quantum simulation platforms, such as arrays of Rydberg atoms and superconducting qubit systems, have shown compelling evidence for the establishment of topological order
Semeghini et al. 2021
;
Bluvstein et al. 2022
;
Satzinger et al. 2021
;
Acharya et al. 2022
.
While these platforms offer a high degree of control, the state preparation is subject to inevitable decoherence, leading ultimately to a mixed state.
Experimental probes of topological order have a long history in the context of the fractional quantum Hall effect in semiconductors
Tsui et al. 1982
;
Bartolomei et al. 2020
;
Nakamura et al. 2020
.
In this case, it is understood that the imperfection due to non-vanishing temperature broadens the signatures of topological order
Wen 2017
;
Nussinov and Ortiz 2008
;
Nussinov and Ortiz 2009
;
Bravyi and Terhal 2009
;
Landon-Cardinal and Poulin 2013
;
Brown et al. 2016

However, the states prepared in the new platforms do not have time to reach thermal equilibrium before they are probed and therefore cannot be described as Gibbs states.
Instead, these systems are better modeled as a topologically ordered pure state corrupted by local channels describing decoherence for a finite time.
It is natural to ask how to characterize the topological order in such corrupted topological states.

Different perspectives on this problem suggest seemingly conflicting answers.
On one hand, the operation of the local quantum channels has an equivalent description as a finite-depth unitary process in an extended Hilbert space that includes an ancilla qubit for every system qubit.
This finite-depth unitary circuit cannot lead to a singular change in the expectation value of any conventional diagnostic used to probe topological order, such as Wilson loops or open string operators
Fradkin 2013
, which support in a finite region.
From this perspective, the topological order persists for any strength of decoherence, short of full dephasing or depolarization.

On the other hand, we can assess the effect of decoherence on the topological order through its ability to overwhelm the protection of quantum information encoded in the degenerate ground state subspace
Kitaev 2003
;
Bravyi and Kitaev 1998
.
In this vein, Dennis et al.
Dennis et al. 2002
calculate a finite error threshold for the Toric code subject to local Pauli
X
and
Z
errors.
These errors create pairs of syndromes (i.e. anyon excitations) detected by stabilizer measurements.
The recovery scheme is an algorithm for annihilating the syndromes in pairs, which fails when the error rate exceeds the threshold.
The existence of a recovery threshold strongly suggests that there is an underlying decoherence-induced transition in the intrinsic properties of the mixed state, which is not captured by standard probes used to detect ground state topological order.
We note, however, that the recovery fidelity itself is not an intrinsic property as it may depend on the recovery algorithm.
Furthermore, theories of recovery thresholds are limited to solvable topological stabilizer codes and do not offer insight into the effect of decoherence on topologically ordered states more generally.

In this Letter, we develop a universal description of topological order and decoherence-induced transitions in corrupted mixed states that is based on the underlying topological quantum field theory.
A key step is treating the density matrix as a state vector in a double (ket and bra) Hilbert space,
\mbox{$|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}=\mathcal{N}\,|\Psi_{0}\rangle\otimes|\Psi_{0}^{*}\rangle
.
The action of the decoherence channel
\mathcal{N}
, which couples the ket and bra states, can induce condensation of anyon pairs from these corresponding spaces.
We argue that this transition is described in terms of boundary criticality in close analogy to the effect of measurement on quantum ground states recently analyzed in Ref.
Garratt et al. 2022
.

To derive an effective theory of the decoherence-induced transition, we utilize the description of the state
|\Psi_{0}\rangle
as a (2+1)-dimensional topological quantum field theory (TQFT).
Then,
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
is given as a double TQFT coupled by the quantum channel
\mathcal{N}

\tau=0

Accordingly, the distinct decoherence-induced phases are classified by the possible boundary anyon condensates

;
Barkeshli et al. 2013
.
We note that the description in terms of boundary phases and phase transitions explains why the error threshold transitions in stabilizer models invariably map to classical statistical mechanics models in two dimensions despite representing a transition in a quantum state

Errorfield double state.
—
The basic subject for our analysis is a topologically ordered state
\ket{\Psi_{0}}
affected by local decoherence channels
\mathcal{N}_{i}
.
The resulting mixed state can be written as

\rho=\prod_{i}\mathcal{N}_{i}[\ket{\Psi_{0}}\bra{\Psi_{0}}]\,,

(1)

where
i
is a site index.
The local decoherence channels
\mathcal{N}_{i}[\cdot]:=\sum_{k}\mathcal{K}_{k,i}(\cdot)\mathcal{K}_{k,i}^{\dagger}
are defined by the Kraus operators
\mathcal{K}_{k,i}
, which satisfy
\sum_{k}\mathcal{K}_{k,i}^{\dagger}\mathcal{K}_{k,i}=\mathbb{1}
; they can be thought of as imparting different kinds of errors on the state.
For example, the decoherence channel that causes bit-flip errors consists of two Kraus operators
\mathcal{K}_{1,i}=\sqrt{1-p}\,\mathbb{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2,i}=\sqrt{p}X_{i}
, where
p
is the local error rate.

To understand the effect of decoherence on a quantum ground state, it is convenient to express the Kraus operators in terms of the quasiparticles they create. In particular, a Kraus operator acting on a topological state generally creates a superposition of different clusters of anyons, i.e.

\mathcal{K}_{k,i}=\sum_{\alpha}c_{k,\alpha}\,\mathcal{A}_{i}[\alpha]
, where
c_{k,\alpha}
are complex coefficients and
\mathcal{A}_{i}[\alpha]
create the clusters of anyons in the vicinity of site
i

1
1

1

Strictly speaking, such a decomposition is well-defined for fixed-point states where the sizes of anyons are zero. In more general situations, e.g., Laughlin states, one can regard this decomposition as a
definition
of phenomenological error models that we choose to work with.
.
Here we focus on Kraus operators that are dominated by a single term rather than a coherent superposition, so
\mathcal{K}_{k,i}
is approximately proportional to some
\mathcal{A}_{i}[\alpha]
. Such Kraus operators are said to create incoherent errors. Furthermore, the dominant incoherent errors usually consist of just a single pair, an anyon and its anti-particle on neighboring sites.
The resulting mixed state is an incoherent mixture of states with different anyon configurations.

An essential step in characterizing the phases of the corrupted state is to regard the density matrix
\rho
as a state vector in a double Hilbert space.
To this end, we write the density matrix as

\displaystyle\mbox{$|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}=

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}\ket{\Psi_{0}}\otimes\ket{\Psi_{0}^{*}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\prod_{i}\sum_{k}\mathcal{K}_{k,i}\overline{\mathcal{K}}_{k,i}\ket{\Psi_{0}}\otimes\ket{\Psi_{0}^{*}},

(2)

where we define barred operators as acting on the bra space, i.e. a super-operator
\mathcal{K}(\cdot)\mathcal{K}^{\dagger}
acting on a density matrix is denoted as
\mathcal{K}\,\overline{\mathcal{K}}
when acting on
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
.
We refer to
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
as an
errorfield double
(EFD) because of its analogy to the thermofield double state
Haag 1992
.

In absence of decoherence,
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
contains two decoupled copies of conjugate topologically ordered states.
The non-unitary evolution affected by the operator
\mathcal{N}
can induce phase transitions in
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
that can be diagnosed by expectation values of operators in the errorfield double state.
Up to normalization, these expectation values are of the form
\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\rho|$}O\,\overline{O}\mbox{$|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}
or in standard operator notation
\operatorname{tr}(\rho O\rho O^{\dagger})
. This object can be interpreted as an overlap between
\rho
and
\rho^{\prime}=O\rho O^{\dagger}
, which can serve as a metric of distinguishability between these two states.

Decoherence-induced transition as anyon condensation.
—
To begin, we consider the example of the Toric code
Kitaev 2003
subject to bit-flip errors.
The Kraus operator
X_{i}=m_{r}m_{r^{\prime}}
is incoherent and creates a pair of
m

r,r^{\prime}

In the EFD state, the corresponding super-operator creates two neighboring anyon pairs,
X_{i}\overline{X}_{i}=(m\overline{m})_{r}(m\overline{m})_{r^{\prime}}

m\overline{m}
creates an anyon of the double Toric code.
The EFD state produced by the errors is given by

\mbox{$|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}=\exp\left(\mu\sum_{\langle rr^{\prime}\rangle}(m\overline{m})_{r}(m\overline{m})_{r^{\prime}}\right)\mbox{$|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle$},

(3)

where
\mu=\tanh^{-1}[p/(1-p)]
.
Thus, the operation of the error channel generates an effective imaginary time evolution of the pure topological state.
As shown in the supplementary material
SOM

m\overline{m}
at
p_{c}^{(2)}=0.178
, with the transition in the 2D (classical) Ising universality class

The anyon condensation can be detected by the open string operator

\langle\!\langle W_{m\overline{m}}(\mathcal{P})\rangle\!\rangle:=\frac{\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\rho|$}W_{m\overline{m}}(\mathcal{P})\mbox{$|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}}{\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\rho|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}}=\frac{\operatorname{tr}\rho\,w_{m}(\mathcal{P})\,\rho\,w_{m}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{P})}{\operatorname{tr}\rho^{2}},

where

and therefore

m\overline{m}
at each end of the open path
\mathcal{P}
.
In the topological phase, this quantity decays exponentially with the distance between two endpoints.
In other words, the density matrix obtained by injecting an extra pair of far-separated anyons into

\rho
and hence distinguishable from it.
In this sense, the
m

On the other hand, once the paired anyon condenses,
the open string saturates to a non-vanishing value at a long distance.
We remark that the condensate can also be generally probed by the Fredenhagen-Marcu (FM) order parameter of

Other types of incoherent errors in the Toric code, i.e. Pauli-Y and -Z errors, can induce distinct phases with condensed fermion pairs
f\overline{f}
and electric anyon pairs
e\overline{e}
, respectively.
All of these transitions are mutually independent because the paired anyons are mutual bosons, and in the Toric code, they occur at the same
p_{c}^{(2)}
.
If two types of paired anyons condense simultaneously, it implies a condensate of the third type as well because each two are equivalent (i.e. fuse) to the third type, e.g.
e\times m=f
.

We remark that the anyon condensation can be also probed by the topological entanglement entropy (TEE) of the EFD state
Kitaev and Preskill 2006
;
Levin and Wen 2006
, which changes discontinuously at the transition.
In the toric code without decoherence the TEE is
2\log 2
, with each of the two states contributing
\log 2
.
The condensation of
m\overline{m}
reduces the TEE to
\log 2
and further condensing also
e\overline{e}
leads to vanishing of the TEE
SOM

Anyon condensation in the EFD affects the information encoding in the corrupted state.
In topological quantum memory, the information is manipulated by the logical operator
w_{\alpha}(\ell)
that moves an anyon
\alpha
along non-contractible loops
\ell=\ell_{1},\ell_{2}
on the torus.
These logical operators are non-commuting in the ground state subspace and therefore encode quantum information.

In the condensed phase of
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
,
W_{\alpha\overline{\alpha}}
acting on the EFD does not produce an orthogonal state, i.e. two states have a finite overlap, indicating the loss of information encoded in
w_{\alpha}
.
Furthermore, the condensation identifies
W_{\beta\overline{\beta}}
with
W_{\eta\overline{\eta}}
provided that
\alpha\times\beta=\eta
.
Thus, two logical operators
w_{\beta}(\ell)
and
w_{\eta}(\ell)
are no longer independent.
By analyzing the commutation relations between independent logical operators, one can determine the encoding ability.
If the remaining operators are non-commuting, the corrupted state remains a quantum memory, otherwise, only classical information can be encoded.
The memory is completely destroyed if no operators remain.

Effective field theory.—
To generalize the decoherence-induced transitions to other topologically ordered states and gain insight into the nature of the ensuing phases we formulate the problem within an effective field theory.
To this end, we employ the path integral representation of the normalization

\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\rho|\rho\rangle\!\rangle$}=\mbox{$\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|$}\,\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}\mathcal{N}\,\mbox{$|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle$}\,,

(5)

which plays the role of a generating function for expectation values in the EFD state.
Since
\mbox{$|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle$}=\ket{\Psi_{0}}\otimes\ket{\Psi_{0}^{*}}
is a double topologically ordered ground state, it is given as a double TQFT in the Euclidean half spacetime
\tau<0
.
Similarly
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|
is represented by a double TQFT in the half spacetime
\tau>0
.
Therefore, in absence of decoherence, the norm
\langle\!\langle\rho_{0}|\rho_{0}\rangle\!\rangle
is represented by two decoupled TQFT in the entire spacetime.
The decoherence, represented by
\mathcal{N}^{\dagger}\mathcal{N}
creates a temporal defect in the double TQFT, which couples the conjugate copies on the plane
\tau=0
, as shown in Fig.
1
(b).
We note that the effect of weak or partial measurement of a quantum ground state, discussed in Ref.
Garratt et al. 2022
is similarly represented by a temporal defect in the Euclidean action.

Since the defect cannot affect correlations in the bulk, the phase transition induced by the coupling at
\tau=0
must be a boundary phase transition occurring on the temporal defect.
Specifically, the coupling between the two TQFTs induces condensation of paired anyons
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
on the defect surface, which is markedly different from anyon condensation in the (2+1)D spacetime that drives ground state topological transitions
SOM
.
The latter involves proliferation of
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
loops of arbitrary size in the
(2+1)
D spacetime, while the former is marked by the proliferation of small loops of the condensed object
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
, fluctuating near
\tau=0
, as depicted in [Fig.
1
(b)].
As a consequence, there are crucial differences in the properties and signatures of the decoherence-induced anyon condensate.

The usual ground state condensation of the anyon
m\overline{m}
would lead to confinement of an anyon
e
that has non-trivial mutual statistics with
m\overline{m}

Burnell 2018
.
This would be detected, for example, by the Wilson loop
W_{e}(\mathcal{C}):=\prod_{i\in\mathcal{C}}Z_{i}\otimes\mathbb{1}
along a contractible loop
\mathcal{C}
.
Once
m\overline{m}
is condensed,
W_{e}(\mathcal{C})
is affected by a finite density of large
m\overline{m}
loops that pierce
\mathcal{C}
, leading to exponential decay of
W_{e}(\mathcal{C})
with an area law, i.e.
W_{e}(\mathcal{C})\sim e^{-b\text{Area}(\mathcal{C})}
.
By contrast, the decoherence-induced phase only involves the proliferation of small
m\overline{m}
loops that affect the Wilson loop only along its perimeter leading to perimeter-law scaling,
W_{e}(\mathcal{C})\sim e^{-a|\mathcal{C}|}
regardless of the error rate.
This indicates that the anyon condensation in the EFD does not lead to confinement, and the Wilson loop cannot probe the phase transition
SOM
.

The results in the above example can be readily extended to general Abelian topological orders subject to incoherent errors.
The paired anyon
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
in the EFD are self-bosons and may condense at a large error rate.
Moreover, different types of paired anyons,
\alpha\overline{\alpha}
and
\beta\overline{\beta}
, are always mutual bosons and can condense independently.

Discussion.—

We have introduced the errorfield double formulation to describe the breakdown of quantum memories described by Abelian topological orders, generalizing the seminal work by Dennis et al. on the surface code
Dennis et al. 2002
.
We showed that the physical picture governing the breakdown of quantum memories is a boundary transition in a replicated theory consisting of
n\geqslant 2
copies of the topological order and its conjugate.
We focused on the
n=2
case and incoherent errors, and classified boundary phases corresponding to the breakdown of the quantum memory.
Our results are ripe for multiple lines of future generalization.

One useful extension of our framework is to characterize the effect of quantum channels that include coherent errors
Bravyi et al. 2018
;
Huang et al. 2019
;
Iverson and Preskill 2020
;
Venn et al. 2022
.
A physically important example is provided by amplitude damping errors applied to the toric code
Darmawan and Poulin 2017
;
Darmawan and Poulin 2018
.
In this case, the decoherence involves terms that can drive condensation of anyons on a single copy (ket or bra).
The corresponding Lagrangian subgroup does not need to satisfy the fourth criterion, allowing for the establishment of additional decoherence-induced phases
SOM
.
Exploring the expanded phase diagram and interpreting the new phases in terms of the capacity to encode quantum information is an interesting direction for future work.

It would also be interesting to apply the framework developed in this paper to investigate the effect of decoherence in non-abelian topologically ordered states.
One complication is that in this case errors are intrinsically “coherent” as incoherent errors can generate anyons in the individual ket or bra copy due to non-abelian fusion rules.
Furthermore, in non-abelian states decoherence can affect not only the quantum memory but also the capacity to perform protected quantum computation.
It is worthwhile exploring if a single intrinsic transition governs both aspects of the non-abelian state.

The existence of an error recovery threshold suggests that the decoherence-induced phases and phase transitions can be characterized by information-theoretic properties.
In a separate work by the same authors we propose three information-theoretic diagnostics of the topological order in corrupted mixed states and demonstrate their consistency in the special case of the Toric code subject to incoherent errors
Fan et al. pear
.
The EFD framework allows calculations of quantities that are quadratic in the density matrix and therefore correspond to second Rényi versions of the true information theoretic quantities.
However, the framework is easily generalizable to a replicated theory of the 1+1 dimensional defect, allowing us to calculate the
n
-th moment of
\rho
(see supplementary material
SOM
).
The information theoretic quantities can then be obtained in the replica limit
n\to 1
.
We expect that the classification in Table
1
still holds in this limit.

Finally, the EFD formalism can be applied to the investigation of mixed states beyond topologically ordered systems.
Decoherence-induced transitions may occur in other states that encode information nonlocally, such as fracton systems
Haah 2011
;
Vijay et al. 2016
and quantum low-density parity check codes
Breuckmann and Eberhardt 2021
.
The recently proposed average symmetry-protected topological phases
Ma and Wang 2022
is another problem that would be natural to reframe in terms of mixed-states and formulate in the EFD framework developed in this paper.

The invariant subspaces of periodic Fourier multipliers with application to abstract evolution equations
Sebastian Król & Jarosław Sarnowski

Abstract
. By methods of harmonic analysis, we identify large classes of Banach spaces invariant of periodic Fourier multipliers with symbols satisfying the classical Marcinkiewicz type conditions. Such classes include general (vector-valued) Banach function spaces Φ \Phi and/or the scales of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces defined on the basis of Φ \Phi . We apply these results to the study of the well-posedness and maximal regularity property of an abstract second-order integro-differential equation, which models various types of elliptic and parabolic problems arising in different areas of applied mathematics. In particular, under suitable conditions imposed on a convolutor c c and the geometry of an underlying Banach space X X , we characterize the conditions on the operators A A , B B and P P on X X such that the following periodic problem ∂ P ​ ∂ u + B ​ ∂ u + A ​ u + c ∗ u = f in ​ 𝒟 ′ ​ ( 𝕋 , X ) \partial P\partial u+B\partial u+{A}u+c\ast u=f\qquad\textrm{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};X) is well-posed with respect to large classes of function spaces. The obtained results extend the known theory on the maximal regularity of such problem.

1.
Introduction

Fourier multipliers with operator-valued symbols have found many applications in the theory of abstract evolution equations, in particular, in connection with solvability (well-posedness) and regularity of integro-differential equations. A large class of such equations can be modelled by the following abstract, degenerated second-order problem with a convolution term:

(AP)

(Pu^{\prime})^{\prime}+Bu^{\prime}+Au+c\ast u=f.\\

Here,
A
,
B
,
P
denote closed linear operators on a Banach space
X
and

c
is an operator-valued function.

For particular forms of (
AP
), the studies of their well-posedness on diverse vector-valued function spaces have been increased with occurring two seminal papers by Amann
[
2
]
and Weis
[
69
]
, where operator-valued counterparts of classical multiplier theorems for Besov and Lebesgue-Bochner spaces on
\mathbb{R}
are provided.
Those results indicated a right form of multiplier conditions (see
[
31
]
,
[
30
]
), which have been further adapted to different situations; see, e.g. periodic multiplier results in
[
7
,
8
,
67
]
, which are relevant to this article.

In the literature one can extract two lines of research corresponding to such studies: namely, when (
AP
) is considered in the
euclidean
setting, that is, on
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and in the
periodic
one, that is, on
{\mathbb{T}}:=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}
(i.e., when the periodic conditions are imposed).
Each of these lines is represented by a long series of papers; to mention a few representative results, see for the first one, e.g.
[
2
,
69
,
59
,
6
,
28
,
11
,
27
,
5
,
4
,
51
]
, and for the second one, e.g.
[

(as well as the references therein).
Such studies correspond to the well-known research program formulated by Amman in
[
1

and labelled as
’pairs of maximal regularity’
.
In both settings, the basic idea for such studies is the same and relies on multiplier theorems.
Roughly, by the theory of vector-valued distributions, the well-posedness and regularity questions for (
AP
), reduce to checking if corresponding Fourier multipliers with operator-valued symbols are bounded in a space under consideration. Such Fourier multiplier operators arise naturally via the representation formula for corresponding solution operators associated to a given form of (
AP
).

In the euclidean setting, the so-called phenomenon of the
extrapolation of
L^{p}
-maximal regularity
, which can be simply considered as a special variant of the well-posedness with respect to various Banach function spaces, have been studied recently; see, e.g.
[
59
,
11
,
27
,
37
,
29

51

Beyond a natural theoretical interest in this phenomenon, the maximal regularity with respect to a more general function space is an important tool for the study of associated non-linear problems; see, e.g.
[

11

In the periodic case, the well-posedness and maximal regularity were addressed mainly in the context of the classical Lebesgue, Besov, Triebel-Lizorkin spaces; see, e.g. the corresponding series of the references mentioned above.
The aim of this article is to extend such results to a much wider context of general Banach function spaces; see the main results of this paper, Theorems
6.5
,
6.7
,
6.9
, and
6.12
. In particular, we clarify the phenomenon of the extrapolation of
L^{p}
-maximal regularity for several periodic evolution equations modelled by (
AP
); see Theorems
6.7
and
6.12
, as well as Section
7
. These results, in particular, provide counterpart of the euclidean line of research mentioned above for the periodic situation. In addition, we provide a convenient framework for such studies, which reveals an underlying structure, allows to simplify and unify technicalities mainly resulting from the fact that we deal with higher order Marcinkiewicz’s conditions, and allows to handle different questions (distributional or strong solvability, maximal regularity) in a unified manner. It is achieved with the help of two auxiliary results Theorem

To establish such results we make a revision of underlying multiplier results from
[

,
8
,
25
]
applied in the context of
L^{p}
setting.
Roughly, our main periodic multiplier results, see Theorems

Such classes include general Banach function spaces
\Phi

We conclude with a remark on the strategy of the proof of our abstract extrapolation result, Theorem

In fact, we show that such simplification is reflected mainly in the representation formulas for periodic Fourier multipliers; see Section

2.1.
Function spaces

We refer the reader to the monograph by Bennett and Sharpley
[
13
]
for the background on Banach function spaces. Here, we mention only several facts we use in the sequel.

Let
\Psi
be a Banach function space over
(G,\mathrm{d}t)
, where
G
denotes
\mathbb{R}
or
{\mathbb{T}}
equipped with the Lebesgue measure. It means that
\Psi
is a Banach space, which is an order ideal of
L^{0}:=L^{0}(G,\mathrm{d}t)
, i.e. for every
f\in L^{0}
and
g\in\Psi
if
|f|\leq|g|
, then
f\in\Psi
and
\|f\|_{\Psi}\leq\|g\|_{\Psi}
.
Here,
L^{0}
stands for the space of all complex measurable functions on
G
(as usual, any two functions equal almost everywhere are identified).
Moreover,
\Psi
has Fatou’s property, and by the Lorentz-Luxemburg theorem
[
13
, Theorem 2.7, p.10]
,
(\Psi^{\prime})^{\prime}=\Psi
with equal norms. Here,
\Psi^{\prime}
denotes the
(Köthe) dual
(or
associated space
) of
\Psi
; see
[
13
]
.

We define the vector-valued variant of Banach function spaces
\Psi
as follows. Let
X
be a Banach space with norm
|\cdot|_{X}
. Set

\Psi(G;{X}):=\{f:G\rightarrow X\textrm{ strongly measurable}:\;\;|f|_{X}\in\Psi\}

and
\|f\|_{\Psi(G;X)}:=\||f|_{X}\|_{\Psi}
for
f\in\Psi(X)
.
Throughout, the symbol
\Phi
is reserved to denote a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
. Note that if a function
e_{0}(\tau):=1

(\tau\in{\mathbb{T}})
is in
\Phi
, then by the ideal property of
\Phi
we get that
L^{\infty}({\mathbb{T}};X)\subset\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X)=:\Phi(X)
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}};X)
denotes the set of all
X
-valued polynomials on
{\mathbb{T}}
, i.e.

{\mathcal{P}}(X):={\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}};X):=\left\{\sum_{k=-N}^{N}e_{k}\otimes x_{k}:N\in\mathbb{N},x_{k}\in X\right\}

then
{\mathcal{P}}(X)\subset\Phi(X)
. Here,
(e_{k}\otimes x)(\tau):=e_{k}(\tau)x
, where
e_{k}(\tau):=\tau^{k}
(
\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
x\in X
).

Moreover, we introduce a variant of vector-valued Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces corresponding to a Banach function space
\Phi
.

Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};X):={\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{D}},X)
, where
{\mathcal{D}}:={\mathcal{D}}({\mathbb{T}})
is a space of all complex-valued infinitely differentiable functions on
{\mathbb{T}}
equipped in the usual locally convex topology. We refer to

]
for the backgrounds on the scalar distributions on

, and to

For instance, relying on

, it is readily seen that for each
\psi\in{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbb{R})
with the compact support, the operator
\psi(\Delta)
given by

\psi(\Delta)f:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{k}\otimes\psi(k)\hat{f}(k)\qquad(f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};X))

is in
{\mathcal{L}}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};X))
.

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the resolution of the identity on
\mathbb{R}
generated by a function
\psi\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\psi\equiv 1
on

and

, i.e.

\psi_{0}:=\psi,\qquad\psi_{j}:=\psi(2^{-j}\cdot)-\psi(2^{-j+1}\cdot)\quad\textrm{ for }j\in\mathbb{N}.

One can check that

Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space over

For

In the case when

In the both cases (

2.2.
Preliminary results on boundedness of periodic multipliers

For a polynomially bounded sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
we write
\check{m}
to denote the corresponding periodic distribution in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, i.e.

\check{m}:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{k}\otimes m(k).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
be such that
\check{m}
is in
L^{\infty}({\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
. Then, for every Banach function space
\Phi
over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
such that
L^{\infty}\subset\Phi\subset L^{1}
the operator
m(\Delta)
given by

m(\Delta)f:=\sum_{k}e_{k}\otimes m(k)\hat{f}(k)\quad f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)

is in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))
with
\|m(\Delta)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))}\leq c_{\Phi}\|\check{m}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\chi_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{\Phi}
, where
c_{\Phi}
denotes the norm of embedding operator from
\Phi
into
L^{1}
.

Proof.

A standard argument shows that
\Phi\hookrightarrow L^{1}
, i.e.
\|g\|_{L^{1}}\leq c_{\Phi}\|g\|_{\Phi}
(
g\in\Phi
). Since for every
\eta\in L^{\infty}
,
g\in\Phi
and
\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}
we have

|(\eta\ast g)(\tau)|\leq c_{\Phi}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|g\|_{\Phi}

we infer that

\|\eta\ast g\|_{\Phi}\leq c_{\Phi}\|\eta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\chi_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{\Phi}\|g\|_{\Phi},

where
\chi_{\mathbb{T}}
is the characteristic function of
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Since for every
f\in L^{1}(X)
we have

|(m(\Delta)f)(\tau)|_{Y}=|(\check{m}\ast f)(\tau)|_{Y}\leq\big(\|\check{m}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}\ast|f|_{X}\big)(\tau)\qquad(\tau\in{\mathbb{T}}),

the proof is complete.
∎

In particular, Lemma
2.1
shows that each operator
\psi_{j}(\Delta)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is bounded on
\Phi(X)
if
L^{\infty}\subset\Phi\subset L^{1}
. To show their uniform boundedness on
\Phi(X)
we need an additional assumption on the boundedness of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator on
\Phi
.

Recall that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operators
M_{\mathbb{T}}
and
M_{\mathbb{R}}
are defined by

M_{\mathbb{T}}f(\tau):=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\Gamma(\tau,\epsilon)}|f(\zeta)|\,|\mathrm{d}\zeta|\qquad(\tau\in{\mathbb{T}})

for
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}})
, where
\Gamma(\tau,\epsilon):={\mathbb{T}}\cap\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z-\tau|\leq\epsilon\}
, and

M_{\mathbb{R}}f(t):=\sup_{\epsilon>0}\frac{1}{2\epsilon}\int_{[t-\epsilon,t+\epsilon]}|f(s)|\mathrm{d}s\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R})

for
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
. In the view of the standard identification between the function
f
on
{\mathbb{T}}
with its
2\pi
-periodic extension
\widetilde{f}
on
\mathbb{R}
,
\widetilde{f}(t):=f(e^{it})
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists a constant
c>0
such that

c^{-1}(M_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{f})(t)\leq(M_{\mathbb{T}}f)(e^{it})\leq c(M_{\mathbb{R}}\widetilde{f})(t)\qquad(f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{T}}),t\in\mathbb{R}).

Note that the assumption that
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on a Banach function space
\Phi
implies that
L^{\infty}\subset\Phi\subset L^{1}
. Indeed, if
f\in\Phi\setminus\{0\}
, then there exists a constant
c>0
and a measurable subset

of
{\mathbb{T}}
such that
|f|\geq c\chi_{A}
, i.e.
\chi_{A}\in\Phi
. Hence, by the boundedness of

on

. Consequently, by the ideal property of

.

The following lemma provides a periodic counterpart of
[
66
, Chapter 2, (17) p.57]
.
For its proof we need a vector-valued variant of Fejér’s theorem, which asserts that for an arbitrary Banach space

and

S_{l}(g):=\sum_{|k|\leq l}e_{k}\otimes\hat{g}(k)

Its proof follows the lines of the proof of its scalar prototype almost verbatim.

Lemma 2.2
.

(i)

(ii)
Let

and set

,

(ii) Note that

for every

. Therefore, (

2.3.
Fundamental properties of generalized Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

Here, we collect the fundamental properties of such spaces, which play a role in our further studies; see Proposition

3.
Fourier multipliers

3.1.
The representation formula for periodic multipliers

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces.
By the representation result for the periodic distributions
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};X)
(see, e.g.
[
8
, Proposition 2.1]
, as well as
[
35
, Chapter 12]
or
[
63
, Section 3]
for its scalar-valued prototype), it is easily seen that for an arbitrary sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\mapsto{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
with a polynomial growth, the following operator

(9)

m(\Delta):{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}},X)\ni f\mapsto\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{k}\otimes m(k)\hat{f}(k)\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}},Y)

is well-defined and continuous (the series is convergent in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};Y)
).

Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
and

\mathbb{E}\in\{\Phi,B^{s,q}_{\Phi},F^{s,r}_{\Phi}:s\in\mathbb{R},q\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty)\}.

A sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
is called a
\mathbb{E}
-Fourier multiplier
if
m(\Delta)
restricts to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{E}({\mathbb{T}};X),\mathbb{E}({\mathbb{T}};Y))
. We set
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
to denote the space of all
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
-valued
\mathbb{E}
-Fourier multipliers, and we simply write
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
for
{\mathcal{M}}_{L^{p}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
.

Now, let
\Psi
be a Banach function space over
(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}t)
and

\mathbb{E}\in\{\Psi,B^{s,q}_{\Psi},F^{s,r}_{\Psi}:s\in\mathbb{R},q\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty)\}.

Let
m:\mathbb{R}\to{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
be a bounded measurable function and

D_{m}:=\{f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X):{\mathcal{F}}f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};X)\text{ and }m(\cdot){\mathcal{F}}f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};Y)\}

Then,
m
is called a
\mathbb{E}
-Fourier multiplier, if the following operator

m(D):D_{m}\cap\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{R};X)\ni f\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(m(\cdot){\mathcal{F}}f)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};Y)

extends to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{R};X),\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{R};Y))
. Similarly to the periodic case, we set
{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{E}}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
to denote the space of all
{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
-valued
\mathbb{E}
-Fourier multipliers, and we simply write
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
for
{\mathcal{M}}_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
.

Remark 3.1
.

Note that in a contrast to the representation formulas for Fourier multipliers on the real line, each periodic multiplier
has a (canonical) representation formula (cf.
[
51
, Section 4]
).
More precisely, since it is not obvious how to define, in general, the extension of the
pointwise multiplication
of a function
m\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
with a distribution
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X)
, only in special cases one can show that the operator

m(D):{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbb{R};X)\ni f\mapsto{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(m(\cdot){\mathcal{F}}f)\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};Y)

extends to a continuous operator on whole
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X)
. For instance, recall that by means of Schwartz’ kernel theorem, such extension holds for each
m\in{\mathcal{O}}_{M}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, that is, slowly increasing, smooth functions. In this case, we have a formal expression for such extension given for all
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X)
by

m(D)f={\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\Theta(m,{\mathcal{F}}f),

where
\Theta
is a hypocontinuous, bilinear map from
{\mathcal{O}}_{M}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\times{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};X)
into
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};Y)
; see, e.g. Amann
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Consequently, compared to the case of the real line, the study of periodic integro-differential equations is conceptually more straightforward at this point (cf. e.g. the presentation of
[
51
, Proposition 4.2]
with Theorem
5.5
below, see also
[
43
, Problems 3.2 and 3.3]
).

3.2.
The multiplier conditions

For the convenience of the reader we recall the notion of Marcinkiewicz’s type conditions used in the next sections.

Let
X
and
Y
denote Banach spaces and
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that a function
m:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
satisfies the
Marcinkiewicz condition of order

\gamma
(in short,
the
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}
-condition
), if
m\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\gamma}(\dot{\mathbb{R}};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
and

[m]_{\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}}:=\max_{l=0,...,\gamma}\sup_{t\neq 0}\|t^{l}m^{(l)}(t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}<\infty\quad\quad{(\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma})}.

Then, we write
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
,

Furthermore, let
\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
denote a subclass of
{\mathfrak{M}}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, which consists symbols
m
such that

[m]_{\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{\gamma}}:=\max_{l=0,...,\gamma}\sup_{t\neq 0}\|(1+|t|)^{l}m^{(l)}(t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}<\infty\quad\quad{(\widetilde{\mathfrak{M}}^{\gamma})}

and
m(0^{+})=m(0^{-})
(equivalently,
m
has continuous extension at
0
).

The discrete counterpart is defined analogously. Namely, a sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
satisfies
the
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}
-condition
, if

[m]_{\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}}:=\max_{l=0,...,\gamma}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|k^{l}(\Delta^{l}m)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}<\infty,

where
(\Delta^{l}m)(k)=\sum_{j=0}^{l}\binom{l}{j}(-1)^{l-j}m(k+j)
for
l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Similarly, we write
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
.

Furthermore, we say that
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
satisfies the
variational Marcinkiewicz condition
, if

[m]_{Var}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|m(k)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}+\sup_{j\geq 0}\sum_{2^{j}\leq|k|<2^{j+1}}\|m(k+1)-m(k)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}<\infty.

Set
Var(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
to denote the space of all sequences which satisfy the above condition. Note that
\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\subset Var(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
.

Finally, we say that
m:\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
satisfies the
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}_{{\mathcal{R}}}
-condition and write
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, if the sets
\{k^{l}\Delta^{l}m(k)\}
are
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded for all
l\in\{0,1,...,\gamma\}
. We refer the reader, e.g. to
[
52
]
or
[
34
]
, for the background on
{\mathcal{R}}
-boundedness.

4.
Transference results

In this section we provide some crucial ingredients of the results presented in the following sections. These ingredients allow to transfer directly some results known in the real-line setting to the periodic one, rather than rephrasing the proofs from
\mathbb{R}
to
{\mathbb{T}}
.
Such transference methods reveal some natural questions, which maybe of independent interest; see e.g. Remark
4.7
(a).

4.1.
The operator-valued counterpart of Jodeit’s theorem

The following result is an operator-valued counterpart of an extension of Jodeit’s theorem (
[
46
, Theorem 3.5]
) provided by Asmar et al; see
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z
be Banach spaces and
p\in[1,\infty)
. Let
\Lambda\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)
have a compact support. Then the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

\Lambda\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)
;

(ii)

For every
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
the function

(10)

e(\Lambda,m)(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda(t-n)m(n)

is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};X,Z)
.

Remark 4.2
.

Note that (according to the well-known results of Burkholder
[
26
]
and Bourgain
[
18
]
), if
Y
is not
U\!M\!D
space, then not for every compactly supported, scalar function
\lambda\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
, the function
\Lambda(t):=\lambda(t)I_{Y}
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(Y))

(here we assume that
Y=Z
and put
I_{Y}
for the identity operator on
Y
).
Moreover, since the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
is based on several non-trivial ingredients (mainly from
[
33
]
and
[
46
]
), for the convenience of the reader we outline how the above-stated operator-valued extension of
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
can be derived from its scalar counterpart.

Proof.

Following
[
10
]
we split the proof into two steps.
In the first one, we need to show that if
\Lambda
is a function in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)
with

\operatorname{supp}\Lambda\subset[-\frac{1}{4}2^{N},\frac{1}{4}2^{N}]
for some
N\in\mathbb{N}
, then the periodic extension of
\Lambda(2^{N}\cdot)
on
\mathbb{R}
from
[-1,1]
, i.e. the function

\Lambda^{\sharp}(t):=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Lambda(2^{N}(t-n))
, is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)
as well.

Since, as one can directly check the function
\Lambda(2^{N}\cdot)
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)
with

\|\Lambda(2^{N}\cdot)\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)}=\|\Lambda\|_{{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};Y,Z)},

the proof of the above statement would follow from a direct operator-valued variant of de Leeuw’s
[
33
, Theorem 4.5]
. Such variant can be obtained following essentially the lines of the proof of
[
33
, Theorem 4.5]
.
For the proof of one of its main ingredients, i.e.
[
33
, Proposition 4.2]
, it seems to be more convenient for a given function
\phi\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}(\mathbb{R};X)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
, to define
\lambda_{N}
as an element of
L^{p}(\mathbf{D};X)
by

\lambda_{N}(t):=N^{-1/p}\phi(t/N)\chi_{\frac{1}{N}\mathbb{Z}}(t)\qquad(t\in\mathbf{D}).

Here,
\mathbf{D}
stands for the discrete real line
\mathbb{R}
.
Then, if
\mu=(\mu(n)))_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is a sequence in
l^{p}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
with finitely many non-zero elements, which we consider as an element of
L^{p}(\mathbf{D};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, then

\displaystyle\|\mu\star\lambda_{N}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbf{D};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t\in\mathbf{D}}\left|\mu\star\lambda_{N}(t)\right|^{p}_{Y}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}N^{-1}\left|\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu(k)\phi(\frac{n}{N}-k)\right|^{p}_{Y}.

Moreover, in the context of
[
33
, Corollary 4.3]
, if we set
r(t):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu(k)e^{ikt}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, then

{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(r(\cdot){\mathcal{F}}\phi)(t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu(k)\phi(t-k)\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, it is readily seen that

\|\mu\star\lambda_{N}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbf{D};Y)}\rightarrow\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(r(\cdot){\mathcal{F}}\phi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R};Y)}\qquad\textrm{ as }N\rightarrow\infty

for every
{\mathcal{C}}_{c}(\mathbb{R};X)
.
It gives the desired claim about
\Lambda^{\sharp}
.

For the second step, we need the following operator-valued counterpart of Jodeit’s result,
[
46
, Theorem 3.5]
:

Let
\delta(t):=\max(0,1-|t|)
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then,
for arbitrary Banach spaces
X
and
Y
and every
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
the function

e(\delta,m)
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
.

The proof of this statement follows almost verbatim the lines of the proof of its scalar prototype
[
46
, Theorem 3.5]
(see also Remark
4.3
below).

Now the implication
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
, follows from this special variant as in the proof of
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
. Namely, since Fourier’s transform of the de la Vallée Poussin kernel
{V}
is a linear combination of the dilations of
\delta
, i.e.
{\mathcal{F}}V:=2\delta(2\cdot)-\delta(4\cdot)
, a direct rescaling argument shows that
e({\mathcal{F}}{{V}},m)
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
for every
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
.
Furthermore, since
e(\Lambda(2^{N}\cdot),m)=\Lambda^{\sharp}e({\mathcal{F}}{V},m)

is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{R};X,Z)
, again, a rescaling argument yields
(ii)
.

Finally, note that for
X=Y
and the sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(Y)
with
m(0):=I_{Y}
and
m(k):=0
for
k\neq 0
, then
e(\Lambda,m)=\Lambda
. Here,
I_{Y}
stands for the identity operator on
Y
. It gives the converse implication, that is
(ii)\Rightarrow(i)
, and completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

In the sequel, we apply Theorem
4.1
only in the case when
\Lambda=\lambda I_{Y}
and
Y=Z
for scalar functions
\lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
. It is natural to reduce the above proof only to its second step, i.e. to a direct proof of such special operator-valued variant of Jodeit’s theorem.
Compared to the proof of
[
46
, Theorem 3.5]
, where
\lambda=\delta
,
it is not clear how to show that the sum of
l^{1}
-norms of Fourier’s coefficients of the functions
h_{k}:{\mathbb{T}}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
given by
h_{k}(e^{it}):=({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\lambda)(t+\pi k)
for
t\in[-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
0
elsewhere, is finite, i.e.
\sum_{n,k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{h}_{k}(n)|<\infty
.

For the proof of our extrapolation result, Theorem
5.5
below, we need a refined version of the above operator-valued extension of Jodeit’s theorem. Roughly speaking, for a given multiplier sequence
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
satisfying the Marcinkiewicz condition
\mathfrak{M}^{3}
on
{\mathbb{T}}
, we are looking for a
summability kernel

\Lambda=\lambda(\cdot)I_{Y}
such that the function
e(\Lambda,m)
satisfies the corresponding Marcinkiewicz condition
\mathfrak{M}^{3}
on
\mathbb{R}
. As can be readily seen, not every function
\lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
yields such an extension of
m
, and a more suitable choice of
\lambda
has to be made.

An extension procedure which preserves the first and second order Marcinkiewicz conditions was provided in
[
41
, Theorem 5.7.9]
(piecewise affine extension, that is, corresponding to Jodeit’s summability kernel
\delta
) and in
[
6
, Lemma 3.5]
, respectively. The following result provides an alternative and unified argument for such extension procedures.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
X
and
Y
be arbitrary Banach spaces.
Then, for every
\gamma\in\{1,2,3\}
there exists a function
\lambda\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{C})
such that for each
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
the function
e(\lambda,m):=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\lambda(\cdot-k)m(k)
is an extension of
m
, which belongs to
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
.

Proof.

We give the proof for
\gamma=3
. The proofs for
\gamma=1
and
\gamma=2
can be readily extracted from that one (see also Remark
4.5
).

First, note that for any function
\lambda\in{\mathcal{C}}_{c}^{\infty}
with
\operatorname{supp}{\lambda}\subset[-1,3]
and any sequence
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
, the function
e:=e(\lambda,m)
can be expressed as follows:

e(s)=m(k_{s})\lambda(u_{s})+m(k_{s}-1)\lambda(u_{s}+1)+m(k_{s}-2)\lambda(u_{s}+2)+m(k_{s}-3)\lambda(u_{s}+3),

where
k_{s}:=\lfloor{s}\rfloor+1
,
u_{s}:=s-\lfloor{s}\rfloor-1\in[-1,0]
, and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, straightforward arguments show that

\displaystyle se^{\prime}(s)=

\displaystyle s(\Delta m)(k_{s}-1)\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s})+s(\Delta m)(k_{s}-2)\Big(\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s(\Delta m)(k_{s}-3)\Big(\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+1)+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+2)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s\,m(k_{s}-3)\Big(\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+1)+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+2)+\lambda^{\prime}(u_{s}+3)\Big),

\displaystyle s^{2}e^{\prime\prime}(s)=

\displaystyle s^{2}(\Delta^{2}m)(k_{s}-2)\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})+s^{2}(\Delta m)(k_{s}-3)\Big(2\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s^{2}m(k_{s}-2)\Big(3\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})+2\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}+1)+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}+2)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s^{2}m(k_{s}-3)\Big(-2\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s})-\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}+1)+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u_{s}+3)\Big),

and

\displaystyle s^{3}e^{\prime\prime\prime}(s)=

\displaystyle s^{3}(\Delta^{3}m)(k-3)\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s})+s^{3}m(k_{s}-1)\Big(3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s}+1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s^{3}m(k_{s}-2)\Big(-3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s}+2)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+s^{3}m(k_{s}-3)\Big(\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s})+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u_{s}+3)\Big).

Notice that there exist a constant
c>0
such that
|s|<c|(k_{s}-j)|
,
s^{2}<c(k_{s}-j)^{2}
and
|s|^{3}<c|(k_{s}-j)|^{3}
for
j=0,1,2
and
|s|
sufficiently large.

Therefore, if for all
u\in[-1,0]
the function
\lambda
satisfied

(11)

\lambda^{\prime}(u)+\lambda^{\prime}(u+1)+\lambda^{\prime}(u+2)+\lambda^{\prime}(u+3)=0,

then
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{3}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\subset\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
would clearly yield the boundedness of
e
and
(\cdot)e^{\prime}
.

Similarly, if for all
u\in[-1,0]
the function
\lambda
satisfied

(12)

\begin{cases}3\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u)+2\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+1)+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+2)=0,\\
-2\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u)-\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+1)+\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+3)=0,\end{cases}

then
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{3}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\subset\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
would imply the boundedness of
(\cdot)^{2}e^{\prime\prime}
.

Finally, if for all
u\in[-1,0]
the function
\lambda
satisfied

(13)

\begin{cases}3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+1)=0,\\
-3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+2)=0,\\
\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u)+\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+3)=0,\end{cases}

then
m\in\mathfrak{M}^{3}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
would give that
(\cdot)^{3}e^{\prime\prime\prime}
is bounded.

Notice that the conditions stated in (
11
), (
12
) and (
13
) are not mutually contradictory. Indeed, one can check that
\eqref{lambda 3}\Rightarrow\eqref{lambda 2}\Rightarrow\eqref{lambda 1}
. Because
e
must be an extension of
m
, we have to additionally ensure that
\lambda(0)=1
,
\lambda(1)=0
,
\lambda(2)=0
.

To construct a function
\lambda
, which obeys all of the above conditions, on the interval
[-1,0]
we take
\lambda
as any smooth function with

\lambda(-1)=0,\quad\lambda(0)=1,\quad\lambda^{\prime}(-1)=0,\quad\lambda^{\prime}_{-}(0)=3/2,\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime}_{-}(0)=1,\quad\lambda^{(j)}(k)=0

for
j\geq 3
and
k\in\{-1,0\}
. Then, on the interval
[0,3]
, we define
\lambda
in the following way:

\displaystyle\lambda(u+1)

\displaystyle:=-3\lambda(u)+\frac{(u+1)^{2}}{2}+\frac{3}{2}(u+1)+1,

\displaystyle\lambda(u+2)

\displaystyle:=3\lambda(u)-2\frac{(u+1)^{2}}{2}-2(u+1),

\displaystyle\lambda(u+3)

\displaystyle:=-\lambda(u)+\frac{(u+1)^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}(u+1)\qquad(u\in[0,1]).

Now it is straightforward to check that for every
u\in[-1,0]
we have

\lambda^{\prime}(u+1)=-3\lambda^{\prime}(u)+u+\frac{5}{2},\quad\lambda^{\prime}(u+2)=3\lambda^{\prime}(u)-2u-4,\quad\lambda^{\prime}(u+3)=-\lambda^{\prime}(u)+u+\frac{3}{2},

\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+1)=-3\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u)+1,\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+2)=3\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u)-2,\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u+3)=-\lambda^{\prime\prime}(u)+1,

\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+1)=-3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u),\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+2)=3\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u),\quad\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u+3)=-\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}(u).

Using the above expressions for
\lambda^{\prime}
,
\lambda^{\prime\prime}
and
\lambda^{\prime\prime\prime}
, it is easy to verify that such function
\lambda
satisfies (
11
), (
12
) and (
13
). The additional conditions imposed on derivatives of
\lambda
at
0
ensure that
\lambda
is smooth. It completes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The function
\lambda
which the existence is proven above for
\gamma=3
works as well for
\gamma=1
and
\gamma=2
. However, note that if we are interested merely in the case when
\gamma=1
or
\gamma=2

4.2.
De Leeuw’s couples

Let
\Phi=\Phi({\mathbb{T}})
and
\Psi=\Psi(\mathbb{R})
be two Banach function spaces over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
and
(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}t)
, respectively.
If for every
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\Psi}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
such that each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is a Lebesgue point of
m
its restriction to
\mathbb{Z}
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Phi}(\mathbb{Z};X,Y)
, then we call
(\Phi,\Psi)

de Leeuw’s couple
.

The classical de Leeuw theorem
[
33
]
(see also its operator-valued counterpart
[
41
, Theorem 5.93]
) shows that the couples
(L^{p}({\mathbb{T}};X),L^{p}(\mathbb{R};X))
,
p\in[1,\infty)
, have such property.
Moreover, some further extensions of de Leeuw’s result done in the literature provide other examples of such couples; see e.g.
[
15
]
and the references therein.
In particular, for our further purposes we need the following operator-valued variant of a weighted extension of de Leeuw’s theorem due to Berkson and Gillespie
[
14
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
. Let
\widetilde{w}
be a
2\pi
-periodic weight and
w(e^{it}):=\widetilde{w}(t)
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Then, the pair
(L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}}),L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R}))
is de Leeuw’s couple.

More precisely, for every Banach spaces
X
and
Y
, if
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}}(\mathbb{R};X,Y)
and each point
k\in\mathbb{Z}
is a Lebesgue point of
m
, then

\|m_{|\mathbb{Z}}(\Delta)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};X),L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};Y))}\leq\|m(D)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R};X),L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R};Y))}.

The proof can be obtained by an adaptation of the proof of its scalar counterpart; see e.g. the presentation given in
[
3
]
. Below, we propose a slightly different approach.

Proof.

Let
\phi(t):=e^{-\frac{\pi}{p}t^{2}}
and
\psi(t):=e^{-\frac{\pi}{q}t^{2}}
for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
.
As in
[
42
, Lemma 5.94]
one can show that

\int_{[-\pi,\pi]}\langle m(\Delta)f(t),g(t)\rangle_{Y,Y^{*}}\mathrm{d}t=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\epsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle m(D)(\phi(\epsilon\cdot)f)(t),(\phi(\epsilon\cdot)g)(t)\rangle_{Y,Y^{*}}\mathrm{d}t

for all trygonometric polynomials
f:{\mathbb{T}}\to X,g:{\mathbb{T}}\to Y^{*}
.
Furthermore, note that

\displaystyle\epsilon\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle m(D)

\displaystyle(\phi(\epsilon\cdot)f)(t),(\psi(\epsilon\cdot)g)(t)\rangle_{Y,Y^{*}}\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|m(D)(\phi(\epsilon\cdot)f)\|_{L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R};Y)}\|(\psi(\epsilon\cdot)g)\|_{L^{q}_{\widetilde{w}^{1-q}}(\mathbb{R};Y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\|m(D)\|\epsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}\|\phi(\epsilon\cdot)f)\|_{L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R};X)}\epsilon^{\frac{1}{q}}\|(\psi(\epsilon\cdot)g)\|_{L^{q}_{\widetilde{w}^{1-q}}(\mathbb{R};Y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\|m(D)\|\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}2\pi\epsilon\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\phi(\epsilon(2\pi n+t))|^{p}|f(t)|_{X}^{p}w(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}2\pi\epsilon\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\psi(\epsilon(2\pi n+t))|^{q}|g(t)|_{Y^{*}}^{q}w^{1-q}(t)\mathrm{d}t\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m(D)\|\|\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\|g\|_{L^{q}_{w^{1-q}}(Y^{*})}

\displaystyle\leq\|m(D)\|\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}\|g\|_{L^{q}_{w^{1-q}}(Y^{*})}.

Since
L^{q}_{w^{1-q}}(Y^{*})
is norming for
L^{p}_{w}(Y)
we have

\displaystyle\|m(\Delta)f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(Y)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|_{L^{q}_{w^{1-q}}(Y^{*})}=1}\quad\int_{[-\pi,\pi]}\langle m(\Delta)f(t),g(t)\rangle_{Y,Y^{*}}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\|m(D)\|\|f\|_{L^{p}_{w}(X)}.

It finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

(a) For our purposes (see, e.g. the proof of Theorem
5.5
), for a given Banach function space
\Phi
over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
we are interested in constructing a Banach function space
\Psi
over
(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{d}t)
such that
(\Phi,\Psi)
is a de Leeuw’s couple and, in addition,
\Psi
inherits some
analytic
properties of
\Phi
. For instance, we need to know that the Hardy-Littlewood operator
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded on
\Psi
if
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
. We do not know if such
\Psi
can be contracted for Banach function spaces
\Phi
, which are considered in the results of Section
5
. Here, we only mention that applying Wiener’s amalgam type construction we can define such a space
\Psi
under additional density and rearrangement type assumptions on
\Phi
.

It determines our strategy of the proofs of some results presented below (e.g. Theorems
5.3
and
5.5
), and does not allow to obtain some periodic variants of results already known in the
\mathbb{R}
-setting via a direct transference.

(b) However, by straightforward arguments (see also
[
16
, Theorem 2.10]
), one can show that for every Muckenhoupt weight
w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
its periodic extension
\widetilde{w}
on
\mathbb{R}
is in
A_{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore, by Muckenhoupt’s theorem and Lemma
4.6
, for every
w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
and
p\in(1,\infty)
the couple
(L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}}),L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R}))
is de Leeuw’s couple and
M_{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{\widetilde{w}}(\mathbb{R})
.
To get desired results for general
\Phi
’s we adopt the Rubio de Francia iteration algorithm to the periodic setting.

4.3.
The extrapolation theorem

The following extrapolation theorem is a crucial ingredient of results presented in the sequel.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
such that the Hardy-Littlewood operator
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
and its dual
\Phi^{\prime}
.

(i)

Let
X
be a Banach space. Then, for every
p\in(1,\infty)

\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X)\subset\bigcup_{w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};X)

(ii)

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and
p\in(1,\infty)
. Assume that
\{T_{j}\}_{j\in J}
is a family of linear operators
T_{j}:{\mathcal{P}}({\mathbb{T}};X)\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{T}};Y)
such that for every
{\mathcal{W}}\subset A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
with
\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}[w]_{A_{p}}<\infty

\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\sup_{j}\|T_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};X),L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};Y))}<\infty.

Then, each
T_{j}
extends to a linear operator
{\mathcal{T}}_{j}
on
\bigcup_{w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})}L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}};X)\subset L^{1}({\mathbb{T}};X)
and has the restriction to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X),\Phi({\mathbb{T}};Y))
. Moreover,

\sup_{j\in J}\|{\mathcal{T}}_{j}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X),\Phi({\mathbb{T}};Y))}<\infty.

The proof follows the lines of the proof of
[
51
, Theorem 3.1]
almost verbatim. Therefore, we leave it for the reader.

Remark 4.9
.

(a) Recall that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{w}({\mathbb{T}})
for all

and each Muckenhoupt weight

[

, Theorem 5.2, Corollary 5.3]
.

(b) By the reverse Hölder inequality for weights in

one can show that for every

; see the proof of

, Theorem 4.1]
.
In the context of a comment stated below

, Remark 4.4]
, note that for each

one can construct a weight

on
[0,2\pi)
, which is in the class
A_{p}([0,2\pi])
(as it is defined in
[
50
]
), but the function
{\mathbb{T}}\ni\tau\mapsto w(\arg\tau)
is not in
A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
.

By Lemma
4.8
(i) we get that

(14)

\bigcup\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X)=\bigcup_{p>1}L^{p}({\mathbb{T}};X),

where the first union is taken over all Banach function spaces
\Phi
over

such that

is bounded on

and

.

We conclude this section with the following consequence of Lemmas
4.6
and
4.8
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
X
be a Banach space with the UMD property. Then,
for every Banach function space
\Phi
over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
such that the Hardy-Littlewood operator
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
,
the family

\{\chi_{I}(\Delta):I\subset\mathbb{R}\textrm{ an interval}\}
is uniformly bounded in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X))
.

Proof.

Recall that the Hilbert transform
H_{\mathbb{R}}=-i\operatorname{sgn}(D)
is bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R};X)
and its kernel satisfies the Calderón-Zygmund conditions; see, e.g.
[
42
, Theorem 5.1, p. 374]
.
In particular, for each
{\mathcal{W}}\subset A_{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}[w]_{A_{2}}<\infty
,
\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\|H_{\mathbb{R}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R};X))}<\infty
; see, e.g.
[
62
]
or
[
45
]
. By Lemma
4.6
we infer that the periodic Hilbert transform
H_{\mathbb{T}}:=-i\operatorname{sgn}(\Delta)
has the analogues boundedness property as
H_{\mathbb{R}}
does.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.8
we get that
H_{\mathbb{T}}
is in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X))
.
Since for every
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
with
a<b
we have

\chi_{\{a,...,b\}}(k)=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(k-a)-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{sgn}(k-b)+\chi_{\{a\}}(k)+\chi_{\{b\}}(k)\qquad(k\in\mathbb{Z})

and
\|\operatorname{sgn}(\cdot-n)(\Delta)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X))}=\|H_{\mathbb{T}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X))}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we get the desired claim.
∎

5.
The boundedness results for periodic Fourier multipliers

This section provides the boundedness results for periodic Fourier multipliers on spaces introduced in Section
2
.
We treat the case of Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces separately to the case of Banach function spaces
\Phi
.

In the both subsections, our purpose is to show that the typical multiplier conditions, sufficient for the boundedness on
B^{s,q}_{L^{p}}
-spaces/
F^{s,q}_{L^{p}}
-spaces/
L^{p}
-spaces, ensure the boundedness on
B^{s,q}_{\Phi}
-spaces/
F^{s,q}_{\Phi}
-spaces/
\Phi
-spaces corresponding to general Banach functions spaces
\Phi
(see Theorems
5.3
and
5.5
below).

5.1.
Multipliers on generalized Besov and Triebel-Lizorkin spaces

In the case of Besov spaces, it is readily seen from the definition of their norms
\|\cdot\|_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}}
, that the problem whether a sequence
m
is a multiplier on
B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)
reduces to showing that its
dyadic parts

\psi_{j}m(\Delta)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, are uniformly bounded on the underlying Banach function space
\Phi(X)
.
Note that, in the contrast to the real line case, no extension procedures are needed in the periodic setting; see Subsection
3.1
.
Moreover, the boundedness of each dyadic part
m\psi_{j}(\Delta)
of
m(\Delta)
is automatic (see Lemma
2.1
), and the problem reduces to the uniform boundedness of their norms. For this reason we postpone the study of the boundedness of general multipliers on Banach function spaces
\Phi
to the next section.

The following lemma makes the above observation rigorous.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces. Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
. Then the following statements hold.

Let
m:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
be such that

(15)

\mu:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|(\psi_{j}m)(\Delta)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))}<\infty.

Then, for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
,
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
.

More precisely, the multiplier
m(\Delta)
restricts to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X),{\mathcal{L}}(B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y))
and for every
f\in{B}^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)

(16)

m(\Delta)f=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\psi_{j}m)(\Delta)f=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e_{k}\otimes\psi(2^{-N}k)m(k)\hat{f}(k)

with the convergence in
{B}^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y)
if
q<\infty
, and in the
B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(Y^{*})
-topology if
q=\infty
.

Moreover, if
q=\infty
, then the restriction of
m(\Delta)
to
{B}^{s,\infty}_{\Phi}(X)
is
\sigma({B}^{s,\infty}_{\Phi}(X),B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(X^{*}))
-to-
\sigma({B}^{s,\infty}_{\Phi}(Y),B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(Y^{*}))
-continuous.

Proof.

Since
\psi_{j}(\Delta)m(\Delta)f=(\chi_{j}m)(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f
for every
f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
\chi_{j}:=\psi_{j-1}+\psi_{j}+\psi_{j+1}
with
\psi_{-1}\equiv 0
, the condition (
15
) readily gives that
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
.
The point is to show the claimed representation formula for
m(\Delta)
and its continuity when
q=\infty
.

For
q<\infty
, since
\{\psi_{j}(\Delta)\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is the resolution of the identity operator on
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
and, by Lemma
2.3
,
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
is a dense subset of
B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)
, it is sufficient to show that the operators
\sum_{0\leq l\leq N}(\psi_{l}m)(\Delta)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, are uniformly in
{\mathcal{L}}(B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X),B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y))
. Let
f\in B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{0\leq l\leq N}(\psi_{l}m)(\Delta)f\right\|_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\left\|2^{js}\sum_{0\leq|l-j|\leq 1\atop 0\leq l\leq N}(\psi_{l}m)(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\right\|^{q}_{\Phi(Y)}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 3\mu\,\|f\|_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)}.

Let
q=\infty
. We start with the continuity statement. First note that, for all
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the adjoint operator to the operator
(\psi_{j}m)(\Delta)\in{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))
on functions
g\in\Phi^{\prime}(Y^{*})\subset[\Phi(Y)]^{*}
(see (
3
)) acts as the multiplier
m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)
. Here,
m(s)^{*}
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
stands for the adjoint of
m(s)\in{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)
. Let

f\in B^{s,\infty}_{\Phi}(X)
and
g\in B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(Y^{*})
. Since for
j,l\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

\langle\psi_{j}(\Delta)g,\psi_{l}(\Delta)[m(\Delta)f]\rangle_{\Phi^{\prime}(Y^{*}),\Phi(Y)}=\langle\psi_{j}(\Delta)[m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)g],\psi_{l}(\Delta)f\rangle_{\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*}),\Phi(X)},

it suffices to show that
m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)g
is in
B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(X^{*})
. For, since

\Phi(X)\subset[\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*})]^{*}
is a norming subspace of
\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*})
, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there exists
h_{j}\in\Phi(X)
such that

\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)[m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)g]\|_{\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*})}\leq\left|\langle\psi_{j}(\Delta)[m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)g],h_{j}\rangle_{\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*}),\Phi(X)}\right|+2^{sj-j}.

However,

\displaystyle|\langle\psi_{j}(\Delta)[m(-\cdot)^{*}(\Delta)g],h_{j}\rangle_{\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*}),\Phi(X)}|

\displaystyle=\left|\langle\psi_{j}(\Delta)g,(\chi_{j}m)(\Delta)h_{j}\rangle_{\Phi^{\prime}(X^{*}),\Phi(X)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\mu\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)g\|_{\Phi^{\prime}(Y^{*})},

which gives the desired claim. The convergence of the series in (
16
) in the

B^{-s,1}_{\Phi^{\prime}}(Y^{*})
-topology follows from similar arguments to those presented above. We omit it.
∎

In the context of Subsection
3.1
, it is interesting to compare the proof of the above
boundedness principle
for periodic Fourier multipliers with the proof of the corresponding result for
\mathbb{R}
, see
[
51
, Theorem 3.8]
; cf. also
[
44
, Problems 3.2 and 3.3]
.

We complete Lemma
5.1
with the following supplementary observation on the convergence of the series in (
16
) under additional assumptions on the geometry of the underlying spaces
X
and
Y
.

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces and suppose that
Y
has the
U\!M\!D
property. Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space on
({\mathbb{T}};\mathrm{d}t)
such that
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
and
\Phi^{\prime}
. Then, for every
q\in[1,\infty)
,
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{B^{s,q}_{\Phi}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
and
f\in B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)

(17)

m(\Delta)f=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{|k|\leq N}e_{k}\otimes m(k)\hat{f}(k)

with the convergence in
B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y)
.
If, in addition,
Y
is a Banach function space and
m(\Delta)f\in\Phi(Y)
(for instance, when
s>0
), then the above convergence holds pointwise almost everywhere on
{\mathbb{T}}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
and Proposition
4.10
, we infer that
\chi_{[-N,N]}(\Delta)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
, are uniformly in
{\mathcal{L}}(B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y))
. Since (
17
) holds for every
f\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
and
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
is dense in
B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)
(see Lemma
2.3
), by Lemma
5.1
, we get (
17
) for every
f\in B^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)

For the additional statement, note that by (
14
),
m(\Delta)f\in L^{p}(Y)
for some
p>1
. By Rubio de Francia’s vector-valued counterpart of Carleson’s theorem (see
[
61
]
), we get the pointwise convergence in (
17
). It completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces. Let
\Phi
denote a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
such that the Hardy-Littlewood operator
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
.

Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

For every
\mathbb{E}:={B}^{s,q}_{\Phi}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we have that

\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y).

(ii)

Let
X
and
Y
have the
U\!M\!D
property and, in addition,
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi^{\prime}
. Then, for every
\mathbb{E}:={B}^{s,q}_{\Phi}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in[1,\infty]
, we have that

Var(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\mathbb{E}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y).

(iii)

If, in addition,
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi^{\prime}
, then the above statements
(i)
and
(ii)
hold for every
\mathbb{E}=F^{s,q}_{\Phi}
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
q\in(1,\infty)
.

Proof.

(i)

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the resolution of the identity on
\mathbb{R}
.
By Lemma
4.4
there exists an extension
\widetilde{m}
of
m
on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\widetilde{m}\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{R};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
. Therefore, to get the uniform boundedness of the operators
\psi_{j}m(\Delta)=(\psi_{j}\widetilde{m})(\Delta)
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi(X),\Phi(Y))
,
by Lemma
2.2
, it is sufficient to show that there exists an even, radially decreasing functions
\phi_{j}
,
j\in\mathbb{N}
, on
\mathbb{R}
such that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\|\phi_{j}\|_{L^{1}}<\infty
and
\|{\mathcal{F}}^{-1}(\psi_{j}\widetilde{m})(t)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}\leq\phi_{j}(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
The fact that
(\psi_{0}m)(\Delta)=\psi_{0}\widetilde{m}(\Delta)
is in
{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi({\mathbb{T}};X),\Phi({\mathbb{T}};Y))
follows directly from, e.g. Lemma
2.1
.
But, the existence of such majorants it is exactly what the proof of
[
51
, Proposition 4.2(i)]
shows (
\phi_{j}(t)=\frac{C[\widetilde{m}]_{\mathfrak{M}^{2}}2^{j}}{1+2^{2j}t^{2}}

(t\in\mathbb{R})
). Therefore, by Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
5.1
we get the desired claim.

(ii) First note that for every
j\geq 1
and
f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
we can write

\displaystyle\chi_{[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]}(\Delta)m(\Delta)

\displaystyle\psi_{j}(\Delta)f

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=2^{j-1}}^{2^{j+1}}e_{l}\otimes m(2^{j+1})\psi_{j}(l)\hat{f}(l)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^{j+1}-1}\sum_{l=2^{j-1}}^{k}e_{l}\otimes[m(k)-m(k+1)]\psi_{j}(l)\hat{f}(l)

\displaystyle=m(2^{j+1})\chi_{[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]}(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^{j+1}-1}[m(k)-m(k+1)]\chi_{[2^{j-1},k]}(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big\|\chi_{[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]}(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)m(\Delta)f\big\|_{\Phi(Y)}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left\|m(2^{j+1})\right\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}\left\|\chi_{[2^{j-1},2^{j+1}]}(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\right\|_{\Phi(X)}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\sup_{2^{j-1}\leq k\leq 2^{j+1}-1}\|\chi_{[2^{j-1},k]}(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\|_{\Phi(X)}\Big\|\sum_{k=2^{j-1}}^{2^{j+1}-1}[m(k)-m(k+1)]\Big\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y)}.

Similarly, the analogous estimate holds for
\|\chi_{[-2^{j+1},-2^{j-1}]}(\Delta)m(\Delta)\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\|_{\Phi(Y)}
. Since
\operatorname{supp}\psi_{j}\subset\{2^{j-1}\leq|t|\leq 2^{j+1}\}
(
j\geq 1
), and
m\in Var(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
, by Proposition
4.10
, there exists a constant
\mu>0
such that for all
f\in\Phi(X)
and
j\geq 1
we have

\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)m(\Delta)f\|_{\Phi(Y)}\leq\mu[m]_{Var}\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\|_{\Phi(Y)}.

Therefore, Lemma
5.1
completes the proof.

(iii) The proof of this part follows arguments presented in the proof of Proposition
2.3
. We sketch some details.
First, since
F^{s,q}_{\Psi}(X)=B^{s,q}_{\Psi}(X)
for every
\Psi=L^{q}_{w}
(
q\in(1,\infty),w\in A_{p}({\mathbb{T}})
),
m(\Delta)
restricts to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(F^{s,q}_{\Psi}(X),F^{s,q}_{\Psi}(Y))
for such
\Psi^{\prime}s
. Its norm is bounded by

\mu_{w,q}:=\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\|(\psi_{j}m)(\Delta)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Psi(X),\Psi(Y))},

\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}\mu_{w,q}<\infty
for each
{\mathcal{W}}\subset A_{q}({\mathbb{T}})
with
\sup_{w\in{\mathcal{W}}}[w]_{A_{q}}<\infty
. Furthermore, for every
f\in F^{s,q}_{\Psi}(X)
and
j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(18)

\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)m(\Delta)f\|_{\Psi(Y)}\leq 3\mu_{w,q}\|\psi_{j}(\Delta)f\|_{\Psi(X)}.

Fix
\Phi
and
f\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
. Let

Gf:=\left(\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}|2^{sj}\psi_{j}(\Delta)f(\cdot)|^{q}_{X}\right)^{1/q}.

and similarly for
G(m(\Delta)f)
. Of course,
Gf
and
G(m(\Delta)f)
are in
\Phi
; see, e.g. (
4
).
Let
h\in\Phi^{\prime}
,
h\neq 0
, and set

w:=w_{Gf,h,q}:={\mathcal{R}}(Gf)^{1-q}{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}h.

Then
Gf\in L^{q}_{w}
, i.e.
f\in F^{s,q}_{L^{q}_{w}}({\mathbb{T}};X)
and
m(\Delta)f\in F^{s,q}_{L^{q}_{w}}({\mathbb{T}};X)
. By (
18
) and a similar argument as in the proof of Proposition
2.3
(ii) give

\displaystyle 3\mu_{w,q}\|{\mathcal{R}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi)}\|Gf\|_{\Phi}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi^{\prime})}\|h\|_{\Phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq 3\mu_{w,q}\|{\mathcal{R}}Gf\|_{\Phi}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}h\|_{\Phi^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq 3\mu_{w,q}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathcal{R}}(Gf){\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}h\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\mathcal{R}}(Gf){\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}h\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(m(\Delta)f)^{q}w\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}{\mathcal{R}}(Gf){\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}h\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathbb{T}}G(m(\Delta)f)h\,\mathrm{d}t

Since

\sup\left\{[w_{Gf,h,q}]_{A_{q}}:{f\in{\mathcal{P}}(X),h\in\Phi^{\prime}}\right\}<\infty,

\mu:=\sup\left\{3\mu_{w,q}:w=w_{Gf,h,q}\textrm{ with }f\in{\mathcal{P}}(X),h\in\Phi^{\prime}\right\}<\infty.

Therefore, for every
f\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)

\|m(\Delta)f\|_{F^{s,q}_{\Phi}({\mathbb{T}};Y)}\leq\mu\|{\mathcal{R}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi)}\|{\mathcal{R}}^{\prime}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(\Phi^{\prime})}\|f\|_{F^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X)}.

Since
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
is dense in
F^{s,q}_{\Phi}({\mathbb{T}};X)
(see Lemma
2.3
),
m(\Delta)
restricts to an operator in
{\mathcal{L}}(F^{s,q}_{\Phi}(X),F^{s,q}_{\Phi}(Y))
.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

Note that
[

on

5.2.
Multipliers on general Banach function spaces

The main aim of this section is to provide a result on the
extrapolation
of the boundedness of periodic Fourier multipliers; see Theorem
5.5
. More precisely, we identify the classes of multipliers
m
for which a priori knowledge that
m
is in
{\mathcal{M}}_{L^{p}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
implies that
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\Phi}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
for a large class of Banach function spaces
\Phi
.
In the next section, we apply this result to prove the phenomenon of the extrapolation of the
L^{p}
-maximal regularity for a large class of abstract evolution equations; see Theorems
6.7
,
6.9
and
6.12
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
X
and
Y
be Banach spaces.
Let
\Phi
be a Banach function space over
({\mathbb{T}},\mathrm{d}t)
such that
M_{\mathbb{T}}
is bounded on
\Phi
and its dual
\Phi^{\prime}
.
Then the following assertions hold.

(i)

Assume that
m\in{\mathfrak{M}}^{3}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Y))
and
m\in{\mathcal{M}}_{L^{p}}({\mathbb{T}};X,Y)
for some

6.
Applications

In this section we apply the results developed above to study the solvability and maximal regularity of an abstract second-order integro-differential equation, see (
AEE
) below. We start with some preliminaries.

Let
A,B,P
be closed operators on a Banach space
X
. By
D_{A},D_{B},D_{P}
we denote their domains equipped with the corresponding graph norms.
Let
{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}
denote the
evaluations
of operators
A
,
B
and
P
on
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{A})
,
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{B})
and
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{P})
, respectively. More precisely,
\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{L}({\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{A}),{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X))
is given by
(\mathcal{A}u)(\phi)=A(u(\phi))
for every
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{A})
and
\phi\in{\mathcal{D}}
. The operators
{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathcal{P}}
are defined in the similar manner.
Moreover, let
c\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
, where
Z
is a Banach space continuously embedded in
X
.

Let us consider the following abstract degenerated, second-order problem with the convolution term:

(AEE)

\partial\mathcal{P}\partial u+\mathcal{B}\partial u+\mathcal{A}u+c\ast u=f\quad(\text{in }{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X))

where
f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
is a given
X
-valued distribution. The convolution term
c\ast u
we interpret as the Fourier multiplier, i.e.
c\ast u:=\hat{c}(\Delta)u
.
For a given
f\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
, a distribution
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
is called the
distributional solution
of (
AEE
) if
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{A})\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(Z)
,
\partial u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{B})\cap{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(D_{P})
and (
AEE
) holds in
{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
.

Set

(19)

Y:=D_{A}\cap D_{B}\cap D_{P}\cap Z

with the norm

|y|_{Y}:=\max(|y|_{A},|y|_{B},|y|_{P},|y|_{Z})\quad(y\in Y).

Our first result shows how the structure of (
AEE
) affects the relation between the regularities of the symbols of Fourier multipliers, which are involved in the study of solvability of (
AEE
), which we address below.
To make this result applicable to the different situations (see Section
7
), we need an abstract joint multiplier condition on two symbols.
For
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
we say that a sequence
d:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X)
satisfies the
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}
-
condition with respect to a sequence

a:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z)
, if

[d]_{\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(a)}:=\max_{l=0,...,\gamma}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigl\|k^{l}(\Delta^{l}d)(k)a(k+l)\bigr\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X)}<\infty.\quad

We write
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(a)
, when the above holds.

Remark 6.1
.

Of course, if
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
and
a\in l^{\infty}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z))
, then
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(a)
. However, under further information on the boundedness of
a
one can provide more suitable conditions on
d
to show that
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(a)
. For example, if, in addition, we have that
(\cdot)a\in l^{\infty}(\mathcal{L}(X,Z))
, then
(\cdot)^{-1}d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
yields
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(a)
. To see it, notice that

\sup_{l=0,...,\gamma}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|k^{l-1}(\Delta^{l}d)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X)}<\infty\quad\textrm{ if and only if }\quad[(\cdot)^{-1}d]_{\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))}<\infty.

Furthermore, note that
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma-1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
implies
(\cdot)^{-1}d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
, but the converse does not hold in general. For instance, for
\gamma=2
and the sequence
d(k):=k
(
k\in\mathbb{Z}
) we have that
(\cdot)^{-1}d\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}
but
d
does not satisfy the
\mathfrak{M}^{1}
-condition.
We apply these observations below.

We say that a sequence
d:\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X)
satisfies the
variational Marcinkiewicz condition with respect to a sequence

a:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z)
, and write
d\in Var(a)
, if

[d]_{Var(a)}:=[d]_{\mathfrak{M}^{0}(a)}+\sup_{j\geq 0}\sum_{2^{j}\leq|k|<2^{j+1}}\|\Delta d(k)a(k+1)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(X)}<\infty

We say that a sequence
d:\mathbb{Z}\to{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X)
satisfies the
\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}_{{\mathcal{R}}}
-condition with respect to a sequence

a:\mathbb{Z}\rightarrow{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z)
(and write
d\in\mathfrak{M}^{\gamma}_{{\mathcal{R}}}(a)
), if for each
l=0,1,...,\gamma
the set
\{k^{l}(\Delta^{l}d)(k)a(k+l)\}_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded in
{\mathcal{L}}(X)
.

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A
,
B
,

,

and

be as stated above. Assume that for every

(ii)

(i) First we prove the statement for

For

(20)

Thus, by our assumptions on

For

For

For, note that for all

Remark 6.3
.

By the open mapping theorem, the assumption on the operators

We call a distributional solution of (

with

,

Recall that
W^{1,1}(X)\subset{\mathcal{C}}(X)
. Moreover, note that the existence of a strong solution of (

) requires that

, and then (
AEE
) reads as

(Pu^{\prime}(t))^{\prime}+Bu^{\prime}(t)+Au(t)+(c\ast u)(t)=f(t)\quad\textrm{ for a.e. }t\in\mathbb{R}

with

and

. The ’

and

refers to the classical derivative, which exists almost everywhere on

in the topology of

. In the context of Theorem

, note that the multiplier sequence

corresponds to the solution operator of (

, the sequences

and

correspond to the summands

In the following lemma we abstract some facts which allow to adapt multiplier results from the previous section to the study of the solvability of (

Lemma 6.4
.

(i) Assume that
A
,
B
and
P
are closed, linear operators on a Banach spaces

and

, where

. Then, the distributional solution

u,\,\partial u,\,\mathcal{P}\partial u,\,\partial\mathcal{P}\partial u,\,\mathcal{B}\partial u,\,\mathcal{A}u,\,c\ast u\in L^{1}(X).

(ii) Assume that for every function
f=e_{k}\otimes x
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in X
, the problem (
AEE
) has a unique distributional solution. Then, for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the operator
b(k)=-k^{2}P+ikB+A+\hat{c}(k)

Proof.

(i)
The necessity is readily seen. Combining Lebesgue’s differentiation theorem and closedness of operators
A
,
B
and

it is straightforward to show that this condition is sufficient. For instance, if
u
is a distributional solution of (

)
such that

and the distribution

is represented by

\int_{{\mathbb{T}}}v\phi\mathrm{d}t=({\mathcal{P}}\partial u)(\phi)=P\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}u^{\prime}\phi\mathrm{d}t\right),

and both integrals are convergent in

give that for a.e.

,

and

(\cdot)^{-2}\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
in (b), respectively.

It is worth noticing that according to Remark
6.1
, if
\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
, then
(\cdot)^{-1}\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
. Thus, for
c
such that
\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
the equivalence in Theorem
6.9
(a) also holds. This is a strictly stronger condition, but in some situations it might be easier to verify.

Remark 6.11
.

If we modify the notion of the well-posedness of (
AEE
) (which sometimes can be motivated by a special form of the convolution therm; see, e.g. (
28
)) by replacing the condition
u\in W^{1,1}(X)
by a stronger one, e.g.
u\in W^{1,1}(Z)
, where
Z
is a Banach space such that
Z\hookrightarrow X
, then the above characterizations, Theorems
6.7
and
6.9
(where
Z=X
) can be easily adjusted to such modified setting. The proofs of such modifications have the same pattern as those of Theorems
6.7
and
6.9
. Here, we do not provide such reformulation of the above characterization results, which corresponds to such stronger notion of well-posedness of (
AEE
).

For our further purposes we present a counterpart of Theorems
6.7
and
6.9
for a convolutor
c\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
with and arbitrary
Z\hookrightarrow X
, which we apply in the next section.

Theorem 6.12
.

Let
A
,
B
and
P
be closed, linear operators on a Banach space
X
. Let
c\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
, where
Z
is a Banach space
continuously embedded in
X
.

(a)
Assume that
\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
. Then, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The problem (
AEE
) has
\mathbb{E}
-maximal regularity (respectively, is
\mathbb{E}
-well-posed) for every
\mathbb{E}
such that

\mathbb{E}\in\bigl\{B^{s,q}_{\Psi},F^{s,r}_{\Phi}:s\in\mathbb{R},q\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty),\,\,\Phi,\Phi^{\prime},\Psi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{M}\bigr\}

(respectively, in addition,
\mathbb{E}\subset L^{1}
).

(ii)

The problem (
AEE
) has
B^{s,q}_{L^{p}}
-maximal regularity (respectively, is
B^{s,q}_{L^{p}}
-well-posed) for some
p\in(1,\infty)
and
q\in[1,\infty]
.

(iii)

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the operator
b(k):=-k^{2}P+ikB+A+\hat{c}(k)
is bijective and the sequences

\left(b(k)^{-1}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z),\,\,(kBb(k)^{-1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X),\,\,(k^{2}Pb(k)^{-1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)

are bounded (respectively, in addition,
\left(kb(k)^{-1}\right)_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)
is bounded).

(b)
Assume that
X
has the
U\!M\!D
property and
\hat{c}\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(Z,X))
. Then, the following assertions are equivalent.

(i)

The problem (
AEE
) has
\mathbb{E}
-maximal regularity (respectively, is
\mathbb{E}
-well-posed) for every
\mathbb{E}
such that

\mathbb{E}\in\bigl\{\Phi,\,B^{s,q}_{\Psi},\,F^{s,r}_{\Phi}:s\in\mathbb{R},q\in[1,\infty],r\in(1,\infty),\,\,\Phi,\Phi^{\prime},\Psi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{M}\bigr\}.

(respectively, in addition,
\mathbb{E}\subset L^{1}
).

(ii)

The problem (
AEE
) has
L^{p}
-maximal regularity (respectively, is
\mathbb{E}
-well-posed) for some
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(iii)

For every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the operator
b(k):=-k^{2}P+ikB+A+\hat{c}(k)
is bijective and the sequences

(b(k)^{-1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X,Z),\quad(kBb(k)^{-1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X),\quad(k^{2}Pb(k)^{-1})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)

are
{\mathcal{R}}
-bounded (respectively, in addition,

7.1.
Integro-differential equations

Here, we consider (
AEE
) for special classes of convolutors
c
, which arise in the (abstract) reformulation of the integro-differential equations describing physical processes in materials with fading memory.
We start with equations, where a convolutor
c
is given by the so-called
finite delay operators
, that is,
c
is of the form

(28)

c\ast u:=Hu_{\cdot}+Gu^{\prime}_{\cdot},

where
H,G\in{\mathcal{L}}(L^{p}({\mathbb{T}};X),X)
for some
p\in(1,\infty)
,
u_{t}(s):=u(t+s)
and, if, for instance,
u\in W^{1,p}({\mathbb{T}};X)
,
u^{\prime}_{t}(s)=u^{\prime}(t+s)
(
s\in[0,2\pi]
).
Note that if for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
x\in X
we set

H_{k}x:=H(e_{k}\otimes x)\quad\textrm{ and }\quad G_{k}x:=G(e_{k}\otimes x),

then
\widehat{Hu_{\cdot}}(k)=H_{k}\hat{u}(k)
and
\widehat{Gu_{\cdot}}(k)=G_{k}\hat{u}(k)
for all
u\in L^{p}(X)
. Consequently, the sequences

h:=(H_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
g:=(G_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
are in
{\mathcal{M}}_{p}(\mathbb{Z};X,X)
. It shows that one can extend the meaning of (
28
) to all
u\in{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}(X)
putting

c\ast u=h(\Delta)u+\partial g(\Delta)u=h(\Delta)u+g(\Delta)(\partial u),

that is,
\hat{c}(k)=h(k)+ikg(k)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

This abstract reformulation of (
28
) leads to the following characterisation of the maximal regularity and well-posedness for this class of equations.

Corollary 7.1
.

Let
A
,
B
and
P
be closed, linear operators on a Banach space
X
.
Let
(h(k))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}
,
(g(k))_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\subset{\mathcal{L}}(X)
.

(1)
Assume that
(\cdot)^{-\alpha}h\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
and
(\cdot)^{1-\alpha}g\in\mathfrak{M}^{2}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
for some
\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}
. Let
\hat{c}:=h+i(\cdot)g
.
Then, the following statements are true.

(i)

If
\alpha=0
, then the equivalence
(i)\Leftrightarrow(ii)\Leftrightarrow(iii)
of Theorem
6.7
(a) holds for (
AEE
) with
\hat{c}
.

(ii)

If
\alpha=1
, then the conclusion of Theorem
6.9
(a) holds, and if, in addition,
B=I
, then the conclusion of Theorem
6.7
(a) holds too.

(iii)

If
\alpha=2
and, in addition,
P=I
, then the conclusion of Theorem
6.7
(a) and Theorem
6.9
(a) hold.

(2)
Let
X
have the
U\!M\!D
property. Assume that
(\cdot)^{-\alpha}h\in\mathfrak{M}_{\mathcal{R}}^{1}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
and
(\cdot)^{1-\alpha}g\in\mathfrak{M}^{1}_{\mathcal{R}}(\mathbb{Z};{\mathcal{L}}(X))
for some
\alpha\in\{0,1,2\}
. Then the analogous statements to those of the point (1) hold, that is, the statements which are obtained by replacing the assertion (a) of Theorems
6.7
and
6.9
with the corresponding (b) therein.

.

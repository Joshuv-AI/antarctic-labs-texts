A method to compute the strength using bounds
Rikio Ichishima, Francesc A. Muntaner-Batle, Yukio Takahashi

Abstract
. A numbering f f of a graph G G of order n n is a labeling that assigns distinct elements of the set { 1 , 2 , … , n } \{1,2,\ldots,n\} to the vertices of G G . The strength str ⁡ ( G ) \mathrm{str}\left(G\right) of G G is defined by str ( G ) = min { str f ( G ) | f is a numbering of G } \mathrm{str}\left(G\right)=\min\left\{\mathrm{str}_{f}\left(G\right)\left|f\text{ is a numbering of }G\right.\right\} , where str f ( G ) = max { f ( u ) + f ( v ) | u v ∈ E ( G ) } \mathrm{str}_{f}\left(G\right)=\max\left\{f\left(u\right)+f\left(v\right)\left|uv\in E\left(G\right)\right.\right\} . A few lower and upper bounds for the strength are known and, although it is in general hard to compute the exact value for the strength, a reasonable approach to this problem is to study for which graphs a lower bound and an upper bound for the strength coincide. In this paper, we study general conditions for graphs that allow us to determine which graphs have the property that lower and upper bounds for the strength coincide and other graphs for which this approach is useless.

1.
Introduction

Only graphs without loops or multiple edges will be considered in this
paper. Undefined graph theoretical notation and terminology can be found in
[
2
]
unless otherwise specified.
The
vertex set
of a graph
G
is denoted by
V\left(G\right)
,
while the
edge set
of
G
is denoted by
E\left(G\right)
.

For the sake of notational convenience, we will use the notation
\left[a,b\right]
for the interval of integers
x
such that
a\leq x\leq b
.
A
numbering

f
of a graph
G
of
order
n
is a labeling that assigns distinct elements of the set
\left[1,n\right]
to the vertices of
G
. The
strength

\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)
of
G
is defined by

\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)=\min\left\{\mathrm{str}_{f}\left(G\right)\left|f\text{ is a numbering of }G\right.\right\}\text{,}

where
\mathrm{str}_{f}\left(G\right)=\max\left\{f\left(u\right)+f\left(v\right)\left|uv\in E\left(G\right)\right.\right\}
.
Since empty graphs
nK_{1}
do not have edges, this definition does not apply
to such graphs.
Consequently, we may define
\mathrm{str}\left(nK_{1}\right)=+\infty
for every positive integer
n
.
Therefore, for every nonempty graph
G
of order
n
,
it follows that
3\leq\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)\leq 2n-1
.
This type of numberings was introduced in
[
8
]
as a generalization of the
problem of finding whether a graph is super edge-magic or not (see
[
3
]
for the definition of a super edge-magic graph, and also consult either

[
1
]
or
[
4
]
for alternative and often more useful definitions of the same
concept).
A necessary and sufficient condition for a graph to be super edge-magic established by Figueroa-Centeno et al.
[
4
]

gives rise to the concept of the consecutive strength labeling of a graph (see
[
8
]
for the definition of a consecutive strength labeling of a graph),
which is equivalent to the concept of super edge-magic labeling.
For further knowledge on the strength of graphs and related concepts, the authors suggest that
the reader may consult the results in
[
6
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.

The
degree of a vertex

v
in a graph
G
is the number of edges of
G
incident with
v
. The
minimum degree
of
G
is the minimum degree among the vertices of
G
and is denoted by
\delta\left(G\right)
.

The next result found in
[
8
]
provides a lower bound for the strength of a graph in terms of its order and minimum degree.

Lemma 1.1
.

For every graph
G
of order
n
with
\delta\left(G\right)\geq 1
,

\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)\geq n+\delta\left(G\right)\text{.}

The lower bound given in Lemma
1.1
is sharp in the sense that there are infinitely many graphs
G
for which
\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)=\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+\delta\left(G\right)
(see
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
12
,
16
,
17
,
13
,
14
]
for a detailed list of such graphs and other sharp bounds).
In fact, it was shown in
[
11
]
that for every
k\in\left[1,n-1\right]
,
there exists a graph
G
of order
n
satisfying
\delta\left(G\right)=k
and

\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)=n+k
.

A set
S
of vertices in a graph
G
is
independent
if no two vertices in
S
are adjacent.
The maximum number of vertices in an independent set of vertices of
G
is called the
independence number
of
G
and is denoted by
\beta\left(G\right)
.
From the definition, it is immediate that if
G
and
H
are two graphs of the same order such that
G\subseteq H
, then
\beta\left(H\right)\leq\beta\left(G\right)
.

The next result provides lower and upper bounds for the strength of a graph in terms of its order and independence number.
The lower bound is due to Gao et al.
[
6
]
and the upper bound was found in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 1.2
.

For every graph
G
of order
n
,

2n-2\beta\left(G\right)+1\leq\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)\leq 2n-\beta\left(G\right)\text{.}

Since there is only one numbering
f
of the complete graph
K_{n}
(
n\geq 2
) and the label of the edge joining the vertices labeled
n
and
n-1
is
2n-1
, it follows that
\mathrm{str}_{f}\left(K_{n}\right)=\mathrm{str}\left(K_{n}\right)=2n-1
.
It is also clear that
\beta\left(K_{n}\right)=1
for all positive integers
n
.
Therefore, the lower and upper bounds given in Lemma
1.2
coincide when
G=K_{n}
for integers
n\geq 2
and this is the only case when these two bounds coincide.

There are other related parameters that have been studied in the area of
graph labelings. Excellent sources for more information on this topic are
found in the extensive survey by Gallian
[
5
]
, which also includes
information on other kinds of graph labeling problems as well as their
applications.

2.
Computing the strength of graphs

This section is devoted to study for which graphs the lower and upper bounds provided in Lemmas
1.1
and
1.2
coincide.
The next result shows that the lower bound given in Lemma
1.1
coincides with the upper bound given in Lemma
1.2
for complete
k
-partite graphs.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a complete
k
-partite graph of order
n
for some positive integer
k
.
Then

n+\delta\left(G\right)=2n-\beta\left(G\right)\text{.}

Proof.

Let the partite sets of
G
be
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
for some positive integer
k
.
Then

\beta\left(G\right)=\max\left\{\left|V_{i}\right|\left|i\in\left[1,k\right]\right.\right\}

and
n=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left|V_{i}\right|
.
Therefore,

\delta\left(G\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{k}\left|V_{i}\right|-\max\left\{\left|V_{i}\right|\left|i\in\left[1,k\right]\right.\right\}=n-\beta\left(G\right)\text{,}

implying that

n+\delta\left(G\right)=n+\left(n-\beta\left(G\right)\right)=2n-\beta\left(G\right)\text{.}

∎

The next result concerns with the partition of the vertex set of a connected bipartite graph.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph with partite sets
U
and
W
. Then the partition
U\cup W
is unique up to renaming of vertices.

A set of edges in a graph
G
is
independent
if no two edges in the set are adjacent in
G
.
The edges in an independent set of edges of
G
are called a
matching
in
G
.
If
M
is a matching in a graph
G
with the property that every vertex of
G
is incident with an edge of
M
, then
M
is a
perfect matching
in
G
.

In the following, we determine the independence number of a connected bipartite graph that contains a matching.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph having partite sets
U
and
W
, where
\left|U\right|=m
and
\left|W\right|=n
for some positive integers
m
and
n
with
m\geq n
.
If
G
contains a matching of order
2n
, then
\beta\left(G\right)=m
.

Proof.

Since
G
is a connected bipartite graph, it follows from Lemma
2.2
that the partition of
V\left(G\right)
is unique.
With this fact, we consider two cases.

Case 1.
For
m=n
, by hypothesis,
G
contains a perfect matching
M
of order
2m
.
It is clear that
\beta\left(M\right)=m
.
Since
\left|V\left(M\right)\right|=\left|V\left(G\right)\right|=2m
and
M\subseteq G
,
it follows that
\beta\left(G\right)\leq\beta\left(M\right)=m
.
The sets
U
and
W
are both independent sets of cardinality
m
, implying that

\beta\left(G\right)\geq m
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\beta\left(G\right)=m
.

Case 2.
For
m>n
, by hypothesis,
G
contains a matching
M
of order
2n
in
G
.
Since
M
has order
2n
, it follows that
n
vertices of
U
and
n
vertices of
W
are vertices of the matching.
Then the independent set may contain only
n
vertices belonging to the subgraph of
G
induced by the vertices of the matching
M
together with the remaining vertices of
W
not in
M
.
Thus, any independent set of
G
may contain at most
n+\left(m-n\right)
vertices.
On the other hand,
U
is an independent set of cardinality
m
in
G
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\beta\left(G\right)=m
.
∎

The next result provides a sufficient condition for a connected bipartite graph
G
that guarantees that

\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+\delta\left(G\right)\neq 2\left|V\left(G\right)\right|-\beta\left(G\right)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a connected bipartite graph having partite sets
U
and
W
, where
\left|U\right|=m
and
\left|W\right|=n
for some positive integers
m
and
n
with
m\geq n
.
If
G
is not the complete bipartite graph
K_{m.n}
and contains a perfect matching of order
2n
,
then

\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+\delta\left(G\right)\neq 2\left|V\left(G\right)\right|-\beta\left(G\right)\text{.}

Proof.

Assume the hypothesis of the theorem.
It then follows from Lemma
2.3
that

\beta\left(G\right)=m=\beta\left(K_{m,n}\right)\text{.}

Since
G
is not the complete bipartite graph
K_{m,n}
, it follows that

\delta\left(G\right)<n=\delta\left(K_{m,n}\right)\text{,}

implying by Lemma
2.1
that

\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+\delta\left(G\right)<\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+n=2\left|V\left(G\right)\right|-\beta\left(G\right)\text{.}

Therefore, the result follows.
∎

The preceding result is not necessarily true for
k
-partite graphs when
k\geq 3
.
To see this, let
G
be a complete
k
-partite graph
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{k}}
with partite sets
V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{k}
such that
\left|V_{i}\right|=n_{i}
for each
i\in\left[1,k\right]
, where
n_{1}=\max\left\{n_{i}\left|\right.i\in\left[1,k\right]\right\}
and
n_{1}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}
.
Consider the subgraph
H
of
G
induced by the set of vertices
\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V_{i}
, and let
H^{\prime}
be any subgraph of
H
.
If we consider any subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
with

V\left(G^{\prime}\right)=V\left(G\right)\text{ and }E\left(G^{\prime}\right)=E\left(G\right)-E\left(H^{\prime}\right)\text{,}

then we have the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
G^{\prime}
be any subgraph of the complete
k
-partite graph
G
as defined above.
Then
\mathrm{str}\left(G^{\prime}\right)=\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)
.

Proof.

First, observe that

\deg_{G}v=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}-n_{j}
for all
v\in V_{j}
, where
j\in\left[1,k\right]
.
Further, observe that
\left|V\left(G\right)\right|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}
,
\beta\left(G\right)=n_{1}
and
\delta\left(G\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}
.
This implies by Lemmas
1.1
and
1.2
that

\left|V\left(G\right)\right|+\delta\left(G\right)\leq\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)\leq 2\left|V\left(G\right)\right|-\beta\left(G\right)

or, equivalently,

2\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}-n_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}+\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}\leq\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)\leq 2\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}-n_{1}\text{.}

Consequently,
\mathrm{str}\left(G\right)=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}-n_{1}
.

Now, consider the graph
G^{\prime}
as defined above.
Since no edges
xy
, where
x\in V_{1}
have been removed and any
y\in V\left(H\right)-V_{1}
is adjacent in
G^{\prime}
to all vertices of
V_{1}
, it follows that all these vertices have degree at least
n_{1}
and
n_{1}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}
, implying that

\delta\left(G^{\prime}\right)=\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}
.
It is clear that
\beta\left(G^{\prime}\right)=n_{1}
since
n_{1}\geq\sum_{i=2}^{k}n_{i}
.
It is also true that
\left|V\left(G^{\prime}\right)\right|=\left|V\left(G\right)\right|
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\mathrm{str}\left(G^{\prime}\right)=2\sum_{i=1}^{k}n_{i}-n_{1}
.
∎

3.
Conclusions

The goal of this paper is to study graphs for which the lower bounds and the upper bounds for the strength coincide, and hence the strength of such graphs can be computed.
We have seen this in Theorem
2.2
.
We also find graphs for which we show that this approach does not work (see Theorem
2.1
).
Notice that Theorem
2.1
suggests the use of a well-known theorem due to Hall
[
7
]
and referred to as Hall’s Marriage Theorem to determine the existence of certain matchings in bipartite graphs.
Finally, we conclude this paper with the following two open problems that we think that may be interesting for future research.

Let
KG_{n,k}
denote the Kneser graph introduced by Lovász
[
19
]
to prove Kneser’s Conjecture (see
[
18
]
).
For positive integers
k
and
n
with
n=k+1
,
KG_{n,k}=nK_{1}
, it follows from the definition that
\mathrm{str}\left(KG_{n,k}\right)=+\infty
.
For positive integers
k
and
n
with
n\geq 2k
, we propose the following problem.

Problem 1
.

For every two positive integers
k
and
n
with
n\geq 2k
, determine the exact value of
\mathrm{str}\left(KG_{n,k}\right)
.

Let
\overline{KG_{n,k}}
denote the complement of
KG_{n,k}
.
For
k=1
and any positive integer
n
,
\overline{KG_{n,k}}=\overline{K_{n}}=nK_{1}
, and follows from the definition that
\mathrm{str}\left(\overline{KG_{n,k}}\right)=+\infty
.
For integers
k
and
n
with
n\geq 2k\geq 4
, we propose the following problem.

Problem 2
.

For every two integers
k
and
n
with
n\geq 2k\geq 4
, determine the exact value of
\mathrm{str}\left(\overline{KG_{n,k}}\right)
.

We find the above two problems interesting since Kneser graphs and their complements are very well studied graphs,
and hence any question related to them seems to be interesting.

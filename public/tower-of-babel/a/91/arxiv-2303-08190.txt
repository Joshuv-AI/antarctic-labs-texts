The i i -Graphs of Paths and Cycles
R. C. Brewster, C. M. Mynhardt ∗, L. E. Teshima

Abstract
The independent domination number i ⁡ ( G ) i(G) of a graph G G is the minimum cardinality of a maximal independent set of G G , also called an i ⁡ ( G ) i(G) -set. The i i -graph of G G , denoted ℐ ⁡ ( G ) \mathscr{I}(G) , is the graph whose vertices correspond to the i ⁡ ( G ) i(G) -sets, and where two i ⁡ ( G ) i(G) -sets are adjacent if and only if they differ by two adjacent vertices. Although not all graphs are i i -graph realizable, that is, given a target graph H H , there does not necessarily exist a source graph G G such that H ≅ ℐ ⁡ ( G ) H\cong\mathscr{I}(G) , all graphs have i i -graphs. We determine the i i -graphs of paths and cycles and, in the case of cycles, discuss the Hamiltonicity of these i i -graphs.

Introduction

Paths and cycles are among the simplest graph classes and one can easily determine their various domination-type numbers. We consider their minimum independent dominating sets to be the vertices of a new graph, called an “
i
-graph”, in which two vertices are adjacent whenever the symmetric difference of their corresponding sets consists of two vertices that are adjacent in the original graph. We also precisely determine those cycles whose
i
-graphs are Hamiltonian or traceable, i.e., have Hamiltonian paths.

The
i
-graph of a graph is an example of a “reconfiguration graph”. In graph theory, reconfiguration problems are often concerned with solutions to a specific problem that are
vertex/edge subsets of a graph. When this is the case, the reconfiguration problem can be viewed as
a token manipulation problem, where
a solution subset is represented by placing a token at each vertex or edge of the subset.
The reconfiguration step for vertex subsets can be of one of three variants (edge
subsets are handled analogously):

\vartriangleright

Token Slide (TS) Model
: A single token
is slid along an edge between adjacent vertices.

\vartriangleright

Token Jump (TJ) Model
: A single token
jumps from one vertex to another (without the vertices necessarily being
adjacent).

\vartriangleright

Token Addition/Removal (TAR) Model:
A
single token can either be added to a vertex or be removed from a vertex.

To represent the many possible solutions in a reconfiguration problem, each solution can
be represented as a vertex of a new graph, referred to as a
reconfiguration graph
, where adjacency between vertices follows one of the three token adjacency models, producing the

slide graph
, the
jump graph
, or the
TAR graph
, respectively. Here we consider the token slide model reconfiguration step applied to the minimum independent dominating sets of paths and cycles.
We refer the reader to
[
6
]
for a survey on reconfiguration of colourings and dominating sets in graphs.
See also the survey
[
8
]
for results on independent set reconfiguration.

We use the standard notation of
\alpha(G)
for the independence number and
\gamma(G)
for the domination number
(cardinality of a minimum dominating set) of a graph
G
.
The
independent domination number

i(G)
of
G
is the
minimum cardinality of a maximal independent set of
G
, or, equivalently,
the minimum cardinality of an independent domination set of
G
. An independent dominating set of
G
of cardinality
i(G)
is also called an
i
-
set
of
G
, or an
i(G)
-
set
. In general, we follow the notation of
[
4
]
.

i
-Graphs

The
i
-graph
of a graph
G
, denoted
\mathscr{I}(G)=(V(\mathscr{I}(G)),E(\mathscr{I}(G)))
, is the graph with vertices
representing the minimum independent dominating sets of
G
(that is, the
i
-sets of
G
), and where
u,v\in V(\mathscr{I}(G))
, corresponding to the
i(G)
-sets
S_{u}
and
S_{v}
, respectively, are
adjacent in
\mathscr{I}(G)
if and only if there exists
xy\in E(G)
such that
S_{u}=(S_{v}-x)\cup\{y\}
. That is, adjacency in
\mathscr{I}(G)
follows a token slide model.

We say
H

is an
i
-graph
, or is
i
-
graph realizable
, if there exists some graph
G
such that

\mathscr{I}(G)\cong H
. Moreover, we refer to
G
as the
seed graph
of the
i
-graph
H
.
Going forward, we mildly abuse notation to denote both the
i
-set
X
of
G
and its corresponding
vertex in
H
as
X
, so that
X\subseteq V(G)
and
X\in V(H)
.

We observe that only the token slide model is relevant for
i
-set reconfiguration. Imagine that there is a token on each vertex of an
i
-set
S
of
G
. Then
S
is adjacent, in
\mathscr{I}(G)
, to an
i(G)
-set
S^{\prime}
if and only if a single token can be slid along an edge of
G
to transform
S
into
S^{\prime}
.
A token is said to be
frozen
(in any reconfiguration model) if there are no available vertices to which it can slide.

In acknowledgment of the slide-action in
i
-graphs, given
i
-sets
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\}
and

Y=\{y_{1},x_{2},\dots x_{k}\}
of
G
with
x_{1}y_{1}\in E(G)
, we denote the adjacency of
X
and
Y
in
\mathscr{I}(G)

as
X\overset{x_{1}y_{1}}{\sim}Y
, where we imagine transforming the
i
-set
X
into
Y
by sliding the token at
x_{1}

along an edge to
y_{1}
.
More generally, we use
x\sim y
to denote the adjacency of vertices
x
and
y
(and
x\not\sim y
to
denote non-adjacency); this is used in the context of both the seed graph and the target graph.

The study of
i
-graphs was initiated by L. E. Teshima in
[
10
]
. In the paper
[
3
]
based on this
work, the authors investigated
i
-graph realizability and proved a number of results concerning the adjacency of
vertices in an
i
-graph and the structure of their associated
i
-sets in the seed graph. They presented the
three smallest graphs that are not
i
-graphs, namely the diamond graph
\mathfrak{D}=K_{4}-e
,
K_{2,3}
and the graph

\kappa
, which is
K_{2,3}
with an edge subdivided. They showed that several common graph classes, like trees
and cycles, are
i
-graphs. They demonstrated that known
i
-graphs can be used to construct new
i
-graphs and
applied these results to build other classes of
i
-graphs, such as block graphs, hypercubes, forests,
cacti, and unicyclic graphs.

The diamond
\mathfrak{D}
,
K_{2,3}
, and
\kappa
, mentioned above, are examples of
theta graphs
: graphs that
are the union of three internally disjoint nontrivial paths with the same two distinct end vertices.
The problem of characterizing theta graphs that are
i
-graph realizable was fully resolved in
[
10
]

and also reported in
[
2
]
.

Here we consider the opposing question: given a graph
G
, what is the structure of

?
The exact structure of the resulting

-graph can vary among families of graphs from the simplest
isolated vertex to surprisingly complex structures.
We illustrate this statement by examining the

paths and cycles. We count the number of distinct

and the cycle

in Sections

That is, we determine

and

for

. In Sections

and

we determine the

-graphs of paths and cycles, respectively. Then, in Section

We note that a description of the

3.1
The Number of
i
-Sets of Cycles

Our method for counting the
i
-sets of cycles is similar to our approach to paths. An
i
-set
X
of
C_{n}
again partitions
V(C_{n})-X
into sets
X_{1},X_{2},\dots,X_{t}
. Letting
x_{j}=|X_{j}|
, we have
1\leq x_{j}\leq 2
with exactly
0,1,
or
2
of the
x_{j}=1
when
n=3k,3k+2,
or
3k+1
respectively. The rotational symmetry of
C_{n}
however complicates the counting. For example, when
n=3k
each
x_{j}=2
uniquely determining the gaps between vertices of
X
, but there are 3
i
-sets of
C_{n}
.

Removing the edge
e=v_{0}v_{n-1}
of
C_{n}
gives the path
P_{n}=C_{n}-e=(v_{0},\dots,v_{n-1})
. Any
i
-set
X
of
C_{n}
corresponds to either an
i
-set of
P_{n}
or to a “near
i
-set of
P_{n}
”, in which

(i)

v_{2}
is the first vertex and
v_{n-1}
is the last vertex of
X
on
P_{n}
, or

(ii)

v_{0}
is the first vertex and
v_{n-3}
is the last vertex of
X
on
P_{n}
.

These three varieties of
i
-sets are pairwise disjoint; therefore, by counting the number of
i
-sets and near
i
-sets of
P_{n}
we obtain the number of
i
-sets of
C_{n}
. Recall
t=i(P_{n})+1
. Define
r=\sum_{j=1}^{t}x_{j}=n-i(P_{n})
.
Thus, the number of
i
-sets of
C_{n}
equals the sum of the number of integer solutions to the following equations:

\displaystyle x_{1}+\dots+x_{t}=r,

\displaystyle\text{ where }x_{1}=0\text{ and }1\leq x_{j}\leq 2\text{ for }j\in\{2,\dots,t\},

(1)

\displaystyle x_{1}+\dots+x_{t}=r,

\displaystyle\text{ where }x_{t}=0\text{ and }1\leq x_{j}\leq 2\text{ for }j\in\{1,\dots,t-1\},

(2)

\displaystyle x_{1}+\dots+x_{t}=r,

\displaystyle\text{ where }x_{1}=x_{t}=1\text{ and }1\leq x_{j}\leq 2\text{ for }j\in\{2,\dots,t-1\}.

(3)

The corresponding generating function is

\displaystyle(x+x^{2})^{t-1}+(x+x^{2})^{t-1}+x^{2}(x+x^{2})^{t-2}=2x^{t-1}(1+x)^{t-1}+x^{t}(1+x)^{t-2}.

(4)

\bullet

If
n=3k
, then
t=k+1
and
r=2k
. Thus, we require the coefficient of
x^{2k}
in
2x^{k}(1+x)^{k}+x^{k+1}(1+x)^{k-1}
, which is 3.

\bullet

If
n=3k+1
, then
t=i(P_{n})+1=k+2
and
r=2k
. Thus, we require the coefficient of
x^{2k}
in (
4
), that is, in
2x^{k+1}(1+x)^{k+1}+x^{k+2}(1+x)^{k}
, which is
2\binom{k+1}{k-1}+\binom{k}{k-2}=k(3k+1)/2
.

\bullet

If
n=3k+2
, then
t=k+2
and
r=2k+1
. Thus, we require the coefficient of
x^{2k+1}
in
2x^{k+1}(1+x)^{k+1}+x^{k+2}(1+x)^{k}
, which is
2\binom{k+1}{k}+\binom{k}{k-1}=3k+2=n
.

\displaystyle\left|V\left(\mathscr{I}(C_{n})\right)\right|=\begin{cases}3&\text{ if }\ n=3k\\

n&\text{ if }\ n=3k+2.\end{cases}

Lemma 3.3

For

,
\mathscr{I}(C_{3k+1})\cong\mathfrak{B}_{k}
.

To prove Lemma

, we first establish several lemmas, beginning with a formulation of the observation from page

, for the case

. Going forward, we assume that all arithmetic in the
i
-set notation is performed modulo
3k+1
.

Observation 3.4

Each
i
-set
X
of
C_{3k+1}
contains exactly two pairs of vertices, say
v_{j-1},v_{j+1}
and
v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell+1}
,
such that each pair has a common neighbour in
C_{3k+1}-X
; all other pairs of consecutive
vertices of
X
on
C_{3k+1}
are separated by two vertices of
C_{3k+1}-X
.

Lemma 3.5

Each vertex of
\mathscr{I}(C_{3k+1})
has degree
2
or
4
.

Proof.
For each
i
-set
X
of
C_{3k+1}
, we deduce from Observation
3.4
that
X
is one of two types (with notation as in Observation
3.4
):

Type 1:

\{v_{j-1},v_{j+1}\}\cap\{v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell+1}\}\neq\varnothing
; in this case

|\{v_{j-1},v_{j+1}\}\cap\{v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell+1}\}|=1
. Say
{j+1}={\ell-1}
. In our wide-angled notation, Type 1

i
-sets are of the form
X=\left\langle j,j+2\right\rangle
. Then the subpath
(v_{j-1},v_{j},v_{j+1},v_{j+2},v_{j+3})
of
C_{3k+1}
has tokens on
v_{j-1},v_{j+1},
and
v_{j+3}
. A
token on
v_{j-1}
can only slide counterclockwise to
v_{j-2}
, a token on
v_{j+3}
can only slide clockwise to
v_{j+4}
, and a token on
v_{j+1}
is frozen. Hence
X
has degree
2
in
\mathscr{I}(C_{3k+1})
(all other tokens are frozen).

Type 2:

\{v_{j-1},v_{j+1}\}\cap\{v_{\ell-1},v_{\ell+1}\}=\varnothing
. When
X
is a Type 2

i
-set, and
v_{j-1},v_{j+1},v_{\ell-1},
and
v_{\ell+1}
occur in this order in a clockwise direction on

C_{3k+1}
, then each of
v_{j-1}
and
v_{\ell-1}
is immediately preceded
(counterclockwise) by two vertices of
C_{3k+1}-X
, and each of
v_{j+1}
and
v_{\ell+1}
is
immediately followed (clockwise) by two vertices of
C_{3k+1}-X
. Hence tokens
on
v_{j-1}
and
v_{\ell-1}
can slide counterclockwise to
v_{j-2}

and
v_{\ell-1}
, respectively, while tokens on
v_{j+1}
and

v_{\ell+1}
can slide clockwise to
v_{j+2}
and

v_{\ell+2}
, respectively. Hence
X
has degree
4
in

\mathscr{I}(C_{3k+1})
.

.

Let

be an

Theorem 3.7

For

,

3.3
Hamiltonicity of
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})

In some of the figures presented in Section
3.2
, a Hamiltonian cycle or path is easily found; in others, it is not. This leads to the problem of determining the values of
n
for which
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is Hamiltonian or Hamilton traceable (i.e. has a Hamiltonian path). In most cases, this is not too difficult to determine, as we show next.

Theorem 3.8

For
n\geq 3
,

\bullet

If
n\equiv 0\ (\text{mod}\ 3)
and
n\neq 3
, then
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is disconnected.

\bullet

If
n\equiv 2\ (\text{mod}\ 3)
or
n=3
, then
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is trivially Hamiltonian.

\bullet

If
n\equiv 4\ (\text{mod}\ 6)
, then
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is neither Hamiltonian nor Hamilton traceable.

Proof.
The first two cases are trivial, and so assume that
n\equiv 4\ (\text{mod}\ 6)
. Since
\mathscr{I}(C_{4})=\overline{K_{2}}
, it is non-Hamiltonian;
hence, assume
n>4
, say
n=6k+4
,
k\geq 1
. With notation as above, we first count the
number of vertices
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
of
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
such that
j\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
. For
j=0
, the set of these
vertices is

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}_{0}=\{\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,8\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle 0,6k+2\right\rangle\}

and
|\mathscr{X}_{0}|=k+1
. Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathscr{X}_{j}=\{\left\langle j,j+2\right\rangle,\left\langle j,j+8\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle j,j+6k+2\right\rangle\}

and

\displaystyle\sum^{6k+3}_{j=0}|\mathscr{X}_{j}|=(k+1)(6k+4).

But each vertex
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle,j\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
occurs in exactly two sets, namely
\mathscr{X}_{j}
and
\mathscr{X_{\ell}}
. Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\bigcup_{j=0}^{6k+3}\mathscr{X}_{j}\right|=(k+1)(3k+2).

Since

\displaystyle|V(\mathscr{I}(C_{n}))|=\frac{n(n-1)}{6}=(3k+2)(2k+1),

\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
has
k(3k+2)
vertices
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
such that
j\not\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
.

We show next that each vertex
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
such that
j\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
is adjacent only to vertices
\left\langle j^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\right\rangle
such that
j^{\prime}\not\equiv\ell^{\prime}\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
, and vice versa.

Let
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
be any vertex of
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
such that
j\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
. Then, with arithmetic in
the subscripts performed modulo
n
,

\displaystyle N(\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle)=\begin{cases}\{\left\langle j,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle j,\ell-3\right\rangle,\left\langle j+3,\ell\right\rangle,\left\langle j-3,\ell\right\rangle\}&\text{ if }\ell-j\not\equiv\pm 2\ (\text{mod}\ n)\\
\{\left\langle j,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle j-3,\ell\right\rangle\}&\text{ if }\ell-j\equiv 2\ (\text{mod}\ n)\\
\{\left\langle j,\ell-3\right\rangle,\left\langle j+3,\ell\right\rangle\}&\text{ if }\ell-j\equiv-2\ (\text{mod}\ n),\\
\end{cases}

where, since
n
is even,
j\not\equiv\ell\pm 3\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
and
\ell\not\equiv j\pm 3\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
. Hence each vertex
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
of

\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
such that
j\equiv\ell\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
is adjacent only to vertices
\left\langle j^{\prime},\ell^{\prime}\right\rangle
such that
j^{\prime}\not\equiv\ell^{\prime}\ (\text{mod}\ 2)
.

Therefore,
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is bipartite with
(k+1)(3k+2)
vertices in one partite set, and
k(3k+2)
in the other. Since the cardinalities of the partite sets differ by more than one, the result follows.

The case for
n\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 6)
is more complicated. From Figures
7
and
9
given above,
\mathscr{I}(C_{7})
and
\mathscr{I}(C_{13})
are Hamiltonian:
\mathscr{I}(C_{7})
trivially so, and for
\mathscr{I}(C_{13})
, illustrated in red (dashed). For
n\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 6)
with
n\geq 19
, we claim that
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is not Hamiltonian. Consider
\mathscr{I}(C_{19})

, and so

is not Hamiltonian. A similar argument follows for larger

with

, as we show next.

Theorem 3.9

If
n\geq 19
and
n\equiv 1\ (\text{mod}\ 6)
, then
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})

Proof.
As shown in Lemma
3.6

(i)
, the neighbours of the
i
-set

X=\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
in
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
are

\left\langle-3,2\right\rangle
and
\left\langle 0,5\right\rangle
. On the
other hand, the neighbours of
\left\langle 0,5\right\rangle
are

\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
,
\left\langle 0,8\right\rangle
,

\left\langle 3,5\right\rangle
and
\left\langle-3,5\right\rangle
. Note
that
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
and
\left\langle 3,5\right\rangle
are
Type 1 vertices while
\left\langle 0,8\right\rangle
and
\left\langle-3,5\right\rangle
are Type 2. Similarly,
\left\langle-3,2\right\rangle

has Type 1 neighbours
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
and
\left\langle-3,-1\right\rangle
, and Type 2 neighbours
\left\langle-3,5\right\rangle

and
\left\langle-6,2\right\rangle
. A similar remark holds for any
i
-set

\left\langle j,j+5\right\rangle,\ j\in\{0,...,3k\}
. We call these
i
-sets

Type 2a

i
-sets.

Consider the
i
-set
X=\left\langle 0,2+3s\right\rangle
, where

s\in\{2,...,2k-3\}
(hence
k\geq 3
). As proved in Lemma
3.6

(ii)
, the neighbours of
X
in
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
are the Type 2

i
-sets

\left\langle 0,2+3(s+1)\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,2+3(s-1)\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,2+3s\right\rangle,\left\langle-3,2+3s\right\rangle.

Observe that for the given range of
s
, these are all Type 2
i
-sets. A
similar remark holds for any
i
-set
\left\langle j,j+2+3s\right\rangle
,
where
s\in\{2,...,2k-3\}
. We call these
i
-sets
Type 2b

i
-sets.

Therefore there are exactly
6k+1
Type 1
i
-sets of
C_{6k+1}
and the same
number of Type 2a
i
-sets, and each Type 1
i
-set has exactly two neighbours
in
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
, both of which are Type 2a
i
-sets, and,
conversely, each Type 2a
i
-set has two Type 1 neighbours in
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
. We deduce that the subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
induced by
its Type 1 vertices and their neighbours consists of only Type 1 and Type 2a
vertices, and is
2
-regular. Hence if
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
has Type 2b
vertices, that is, if
k\geq 3
, then
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
is
non-Hamiltonian.

3.4
Traceability of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
, where
k\geq 3
.

When
k\geq 3
and
n=6k+1
, we have previously established that
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
has no Hamiltonian cycle. We now instead prove that it has a Hamilton path.

Theorem 3.10

For
n=6k+1
,
k\geq 3
,
\mathscr{I}(C_{n})
is Hamilton traceable.

Proof.
Say
k\geq 3
, and consider
C_{6k+1}
and an
i
-set
\left\langle j,\ell\right\rangle
. The distance from
j
to
\ell
on
C_{6k+1}
is the length of the shorter path, thus
d(j,\ell)\in\{2,5,8,\dots,3k-1\}
.

\bullet

If
k
is even, say
k=2k^{\prime}
, then, in
C_{12k^{\prime}+1}
, we
see that
d(j,\ell)\in\{2,5,8,\dots,6k^{\prime}-1\}
. This set contains an equal
number of even and odd integers.

\bullet

If
k
is odd, say
k=2k^{\prime}+1
, then, in
C_{12k^{\prime}+7}
, we
see that
d(j,\ell)\in\{2,5,8,\dots,6k^{\prime}+2\}
. This set contains more even
than odd integers.

Consider, again, the subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
induced by its Type 1
vertices and their neighbours, which is
2
-regular (as above) and has order

2(6k+1)
. Denote this graph by
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
.
In Figure
11
,
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
is the subgraph induced by the vertices:

\displaystyle\{\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,5\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,5\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,8\right\rangle,\left\langle 6,8\right\rangle\dots,\left\langle 2,16\right\rangle\}

with the red (dashed) edges forming a cycle.
We argue below that in general

\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
is in fact connected; that is,
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
is a cycle.

Note that, with arithmetic modulo
6k+1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{2,5}=

\displaystyle\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,2+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,2+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,2+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,\left\langle 2\cdot 3,2+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\dots,\left\langle 3x,2+3x\right\rangle,\left\langle 3x,2+3(x+1)\right\rangle\dots

is a walk in
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
. When does
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle

recur? There are two cases to consider, each having two subcases.

Case 1
:

When
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
recurs for the first time, an even cycle is formed. Then

\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle=\left\langle 3x,2+3x\right\rangle
for some
integer
x
. Since
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle=\left\langle 2,0\right\rangle
, there are two subcases.

Case 1.1
:
3x\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
2+3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
, that is,

3x\equiv-2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. But then
2\equiv-2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
, which is impossible because
k>0
.

Case 1.2
:
3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
2+3x\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Since
\gcd(3,6k+1)=1
,
x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Then the first time

\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
recurs on
\mathcal{W}_{2,5}
is therefore
when
x=6k+1
. It follows that
\mathcal{W}_{2,5}
contains the cycle

\displaystyle\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,2+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,2+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,2+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,\left\langle 2\cdot 3,2+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,\dots,\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle

of length
2(6k+1)=|V(\mathcal{H}_{2,5})|
. Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}\cong C_{12k+2}
.

Case 2
:

When
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
recurs for the first time, an odd cycle is formed. Then

\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle=\left\langle 3x,2+3(x+1)\right\rangle
for
some integer
x
. Again there are two subcases.

Case 2.1
:

3x\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
2+3(x+1)\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Then
7\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
, which is impossible
because
k>1
.

Case 2.2
:

3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
2+3(x+1)\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. This is likewise impossible.

Therefore, we conclude in all cases that
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}\cong C_{12k+2}
.

In general, for fixed
\ell\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6)
and
2\leq\ell\leq 3k-1
, denote the subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
induced by the

i
-sets of the form
\left\langle j,j+\ell\right\rangle
and
\left\langle j,j+\ell+3\right\rangle
, where
j\in\{0,...,6k\}
, by
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
.

Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{\ell,\ell+3}

\displaystyle=\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,\ell+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,\left\langle 2\cdot 3,\ell+2\cdot 3\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\dots,\left\langle 3x,\ell+3x\right\rangle,\left\langle 3x,\ell+3(x+1)\right\rangle\dots

is a walk in
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
. When does
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle

recur? Again there are two cases to consider.

Case 3
:

When
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle
recurs for the first time, an
even cycle is formed. Then
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle=\left\langle 3x,\ell+3x\right\rangle
for some integer
x
. Since
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle=\left\langle\ell,0\right\rangle
, there are two
subcases.

Case 3.1
:

3x\equiv\ell\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
\ell+3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
,
that is,
3x\equiv-\ell\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Then
2\ell\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and, since
\gcd(2,6k+1)=1
,
\ell\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Since
2\leq\ell\leq 3k-1
, this is
impossible.

Case 3.2
:

3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
\ell+3x\equiv\ell\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
,
i.e.,
x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Therefore the first time

\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle
recurs on
\mathcal{W}_{\ell,\ell+3}
is when

x=6k+1
. It follows that
\mathcal{W}_{\ell,\ell+3}
contains the cycle

\displaystyle\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,\ell+3\right\rangle,...,\left\langle 3(x-1),\ell+3x\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle

of length
2(6k+1)=|V(\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3})|
. Therefore
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}\cong C_{12k+2}
.

Case 4
:

When
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle
recurs for the first time, an odd cycle is formed. Then

\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle=\left\langle 3x,\ell+3(x+1)\right\rangle
for
some integer
x
.

Case 4.1
:

3x\equiv\ell\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
\ell+3(x+1)\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Then
2\ell+3\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
, or
2\ell\equiv-3\equiv 6k-2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. This implies that
\ell\equiv 3k-1\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and the restrictions on
\ell
show that

\ell=3k-1
. That is, there is exactly one value of
\ell
for which these
congruences hold. Moreover,
6x+3\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
, i.e.,

2x\equiv-1\equiv 6k\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. Hence
x=3k
.

Therefore
\mathcal{W}_{\ell,\ell+3}
contains the cycle

\displaystyle\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,\ell+3\right\rangle,...,\left\langle 3x,\ell+3x\right\rangle,\left\langle\ell,0\right\rangle

of length
2x+1=6k+1
. Consider the distances
d(0,3k-1)
and
d(0,3k+2)
on

C_{6k+1}
. Observe that
d(0,\ell)=d(0,3k-1)=3k-1
and

d(0,\ell+3)=d(0,3k+2)=6k+1-(3k+2)=3k-1
. It follows that
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}

consists of all
i
-sets
\left\langle p,q\right\rangle
such that, on

C_{6k+1}
,
d(p,q)=3k-1
, and there are exactly
6k+1
such
i
-sets. Hence

|V(\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3})|=6k+1
, that is,
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
is exactly the
cycle
\left\langle 0,\ell\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,\ell+3\right\rangle,\left\langle 3,\ell+3\right\rangle,...,\left\langle 3x,\ell+3x\right\rangle,\left\langle\ell,0\right\rangle
.

Since
\ell=3k-1
and
\ell\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6)
, we deduce that
k
is
odd; say
k=2k^{\prime}+1
, where
k^{\prime}\geq 1
. Then
\ell=6k^{\prime}+2
.
The smallest cycle
C_{6k+1}
where
k\geq 3
for which this case occurs is

C_{19}
, in which case
\ell=8
(see Figure
11
).

Case 4.2
:

3x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
and
\ell+3(x+1)\equiv\ell\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
. From the first congruence,
x\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
,
and so, from the second congruence,
3\equiv 0\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6k+1)
.
This is impossible.

To summarize:

Fix
\ell\in\{2,5,8,...,3k-1\}
.

\bullet

If
k=2k^{\prime}
, then by (1) and Cases 3 and 4, the subgraphs

\mathcal{H}_{2,5},...,\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}-4,6k^{\prime}-1}
of

\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+1})
all have order
24k^{\prime}+2
, and

\mathcal{H}_{2,5}\cong\cdots\cong\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}-4,6k^{\prime}-1}\cong C_{24k^{\prime}+2}
.

\bullet

If
k=2k^{\prime}+1
, then by (2) and Cases 3 and 4, the subgraphs

\mathcal{H}_{2,5},...,\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}-4,6k^{\prime}-1}
of

\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+7})
all have order
24k^{\prime}+14
, and

\mathcal{H}_{2,5}\cong\cdots\cong\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}-4,6k^{\prime}-1}\cong C_{24k^{\prime}+14}
. However, the subgraph
\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}+2,6k^{\prime}+5}
of
\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+7})
has order

12k^{\prime}+7
and
\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}+2,6k^{\prime}+5}\cong C_{12k^{\prime}+7}
. In Figure
11
,
the subgraph
\mathcal{H}_{8,11}
of
\mathscr{I}(C_{19})
is shown with blue (dotted) edges.

In either case, each vertex of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
belongs to
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}

for some
\ell\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6)
.

Connecting the Subgraphs
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
to Form a
Hamilton Path of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})

Denote the subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
that consists of the union of
the cycles
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
by
\mathcal{H}
, and the set of edges of

\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
that do not belong to
\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{E}
.
Since each vertex of
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
belongs to
H_{\ell,\ell+3}
for some

\ell\equiv 2\ (\operatorname{mod}\ 6)
,
\mathcal{H}
is a spanning subgraph of

\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1})
. We consider two cases, depending on whether
k
is
even or odd.

Case 1:
k=2k^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}:\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,5\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,8\right\rangle,...,\left\langle 0,6k^{\prime}-4\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,6k^{\prime}-1\right\rangle

is a path in
\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+1})
whose edges belong alternately
to
\mathcal{H}
and to
\mathcal{E}
, beginning with the edge
(\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,5\right\rangle)
in
\mathcal{H}_{2,5}
and
ending with the edge
(\left\langle 0,6k^{\prime}-4\right\rangle,\left\langle 0,6k^{\prime}-1\right\rangle)
in
\mathcal{H}_{6k^{\prime}-4,6k^{\prime}-1}
. Moreover,
\mathcal{P}
contains at least one vertex of each
\mathcal{H}_{\ell,\ell+3}
. Let
\mathcal{T}
be the subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+1})
obtained by deleting all edges of
\mathcal{P}
from
\mathcal{H}
,
then adding the edges of
E(\mathcal{P})\cap\mathcal{E}
. Observe that

\mathcal{T}
is a spanning subgraph of
\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+1})
.
Since the edges of
\mathcal{P}
were alternately deleted and added, all
vertices of
\mathcal{T}
have degree
2
, except for
\left\langle 0,2\right\rangle
and
\left\langle 0,6k^{\prime}-1\right\rangle
, which
have degree
1
. Also, by construction,
\mathcal{T}
is connected. Therefore,

\mathcal{T}
is a Hamiltonian path of
\mathscr{I}(C_{12k^{\prime}+1})
.

Case 2:
k=2k^{\prime}+1
. The argument is similar.

Therefore, in all cases
\mathscr{I}(C_{6k+1}),k\geq 3
is Hamilton traceable.

This completes the characterization of which cycles have Hamiltonian or Hamiltonian
traceable
i
-graphs.

4
Open Problems

We conclude with a few open problems. Although the problems are stated here for
i
-graphs, many are relevant to other reconfiguration graphs pertaining to domination-type parameters and are also mentioned in
[
7
,
10
]
.

Problem 1

Determine the structure of
i
-graphs of various families of trees. For example, consider

(a)

caterpillars in which every vertex has degree
1
or
3
,

(b)

spiders (
K_{1,r}
with each edge subdivided).

Problem 2

Find more classes of
i
-graphs that are Hamiltonian, or Hamiltonian traceable.

Problem 3

Suppose
G_{1},G_{2},\dots
are graphs such that
\mathscr{I}(G_{1})\cong G_{2}
,
\mathscr{I}(G_{2})\cong G_{3}
,
\mathscr{I}(G_{3})\cong G_{4},\dots
. Under which conditions does there exist an integer
k
such that
\mathscr{I}(G_{k})\cong G_{1}
?

As a special case of Problem
3
, note that for any
n\geq 1
,
\mathscr{I}(K_{n})\cong K_{n}
, and that for
k\equiv 2\ (\text{mod}\ 3)
,
\mathscr{I}(C_{k})\cong C_{k}
.

Problem 4

Characterize the graphs
G
for which
\mathscr{I}(G)\cong G
.

Large values of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions
Pranendu Darbar and Gopal Maiti

Abstract
. Assuming the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), we utilize the long resonator method to derive Ω \Omega -results for the family of quadratic Dirichlet L L -functions L ⁡ ( σ , χ d ) L(\sigma,\chi_{d}) , where d d runs over all fundamental discriminants with | d | ≤ X |d|\leq X and σ ∈ [ 1 / 2 , 1 ] \sigma\in[1/2,1] is fixed. This study advances understanding of the maximum size of L ⁡ ( σ , χ d ) L(\sigma,\chi_{d}) within the segment σ ∈ [ 1 / 2 , 1 ] \sigma\in[1/2,1] . In particular, we improve upon Soundararajan’s results at the central point and provide a lower bound on the proportion of fundamental discriminants, uniformly within an expected order of magnitude, up to optimal values of the constant for a fixed σ ∈ ( 1 / 2 , 1 ] \sigma\in(1/2,1] .

1.
Introduction

The understanding of maximum size of
L
-functions poses a profound challenge in number theory. Significant advancements have been made in recent years, particularly regarding the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions in the conductor aspect. After the work of Balasubramanian and Ramachandra
[
5
]
employing the moment method for the Riemann zeta function, Soundararajan
[
26
]
applied a more versatile approach called the “resonance method”. The resonance method reframes the problem as an optimization problem of a quadratic form applicable to a broad family of
L
-functions. Notably, for the Riemann zeta function, Soundararajan
[
26
, Theorem 1]
established that

\max_{T\leq t\leq 2T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T}{\log_{2}T}}\right).

Using the long resonators introduced in
[
1
]
, Bondarenko and Seip
[
7
]
achieved significant advancements in
\Omega
-results for the Riemann zeta function on the critical line, where they accomplished this feat by estimating a specific type of greatest common divisor (GCD) sum. The efficacy of their long resonator technique extends beyond the critical line, encompassing the critical strip for both the Riemann zeta function and Dirichlet
L
-functions in the conductor aspect are explored in
[
3
,
4
,
8
,
28
]
.
In fact, Bondarenko and Seip
[
7
, Theorem 1]
proved that

\max_{0\leq t\leq T}|\zeta(1/2+it)|\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log T\log_{3}T}{\log_{2}T}}\right),

a result that was later refined by de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
9
]
, who enhanced the exponent by replacing
1
with
\sqrt{2}
through the optimization of a full GCD-sum problem. Furthermore, for Dirichlet
L
-functions, de la Bretèche and Tenenbaum
[
9
, Theorem 1.5]
showed that

\displaystyle\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in X_{q}^{+}\\
\chi\neq\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}|L(1/2,\chi)|\geq\exp\left((1+o(1))\sqrt{\frac{\log q\log_{3}q}{\log_{2}q}}\right),

where
X_{q}^{+}
denotes the set of all even characters modulo
q
.

In these families, key characteristics include full orthogonality, positive coefficients of both the resonator and
L
-functions, and a connection to the GCD sum, which constitute essential components of the long resonator technique. Though Soundararajan’s resonator technique can be extended to a broader class of
L
-functions and Dirichlet polynomials (refer to
[
2
,
27
]
for further details). The maximum sizes of
L
-functions over different families were conjectured by Farmer, Gonek and Hughes
[
13
]
.
One particularly intriguing example is the family of quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions, parameterized by fundamental discriminants. For
\sigma\in[1/2,1]
, this family is represented as

\{L(\sigma,\chi_{d}):d\text{ is fundamental discriminant}\}.

These
L
-functions feature coefficients that are not necessarily positive and do not exhibit full orthogonality, making the conventional use of long resonators challenging, as discussed by C. Aistleitner et al. in
[
4
]
. This question was initially posed in
[
4
]
following Theorem 1.2, and numerous attempts have been made to address it over the years.

In our previous work
[
11
]
, we established an
\Omega
-result of Bondarenko–Seip type for the quadratic Dirichlet
L
-function
L(1/2,\chi_{P})
over irreducible polynomials
P
associated with hyperelliptic curves of genus
g
over a fixed finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the large genus limit. These
L
-functions over function fields constituted the first family deviating from the full orthogonality property, where we focused on irreducible polynomials to avoid certain obstacles.

The main objective of this paper is to investigate the maximum sizes of
\{L(\sigma,\chi_{d}):|d|\leq X\}
for any fixed
\sigma\in[1/2,1]
. Additionally, for
\sigma\in(1/2,1]
, we aim to evaluate the proportion of fundamental discriminants that fall near the expected order of magnitude up to the precise values of constants.

Throughout this paper,
\sum^{\flat}
and
\log_{j}
represent the summation over fundamental discriminants and the
j
-th iterated natural logarithm, respectively. The notation
\square
indicates a perfect square. Also,
\varepsilon
is an arbitrarily small positive quantity which is not necessarily the same at each occurrence. Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and
\mathbb{P}
be the set of primes.

1.1.
Large values of
L(1/2,\chi_{d})

Soundararajan
[
26
, Theorem 2]
showed that

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\text{ fundamental discriminant }\\
X<|d|\leq 2X\end{subarray}}|L(1/2,\chi_{d})|\geq\exp\left(\left(1/\sqrt{5}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).

Note the typo in the statement of Theorem 2 in
[
26
]
. In this article, we use the long resonator technique of Bondarenko–Seip to study the large value of
L(1/2,\chi_{d})
under the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis (GRH), estimating character sums effectively to absorb long resonators within them. Our approach yields the following theorem, improving Soundararajan’s above result.

Theorem 1
.

For sufficiently large
X
, under GRH, we have

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\text{ fundamental discriminant }\\
X<|d|\leq 2X\end{subarray}}|L(1/2,\chi_{d})|\geq\exp\left(\left(\frac{1}{2}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{\log X\log_{3}X}{\log_{2}X}}\right).

1.2.
Large values of
L(1,\chi_{d})

The distribution of
L(1,\chi_{d})
offers an intriguing connection to various arithmetic entities, such as the class number of related quadratic number field. Granville–Soundararajan studied the distribution of values of
L(1,\chi_{d})
and established
[
14
, Theorem 4]
that the function
\Phi_{X}(\tau)
, the proportion of fundamental discriminants
d
satisfying
|d|\leq X
with
L(1,\chi_{d})\geq e^{\gamma}\tau
has the following doubly exponentially decaying:

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}(\tau)=\exp\left(-\frac{e^{\tau-C_{1}}}{\tau}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{A}+\frac{1}{\tau}\right)\right)\right)

uniformly in the range
\tau\leq\log_{2}X+\log_{4}X-\log_{2}A-20
unconditionally and
\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-\log_{2}A-20
under GRH, where
2\leq A\leq\log_{2}X
and
C_{1}
is given by (
2
). This result gives an asymptotic formula for
\Phi_{X}(\tau)
in (
1
) if
A=A(X)
is chosen as an arbitrary function of
X
such that
A(X)\to\infty
as
X\to\infty
. In
[
14
]
, the authors’ studied the distribution of
L(1,\chi_{d})
using a probabilistic model
L(1,Y)
for a suitable random variable
Y
and solved one of the conjectures posed by Montgomery–Vaughan
[
23
, Conjecture 1]
.

Moreover, Granville and Soundararajan predicted that if the asymptotic formula (
1
) holds to the edge of the viable range, i.e.,
\tau=\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X+C_{1}
then

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\text{ fundamental discriminant }\\
|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}L(1,\chi_{d})=e^{\gamma}\left(\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X+C_{1}+o(1)\right),

where
C_{1}
is derived from the underlying probabilistic model, specifically

(2)

\displaystyle C_{1}=\int_{0}^{1}\left(\tanh y\right)\frac{dy}{y}+\int_{1}^{\infty}(\tanh y-1)\frac{dy}{y}=0.8187\ldots.

Montgomery–Vaughan
[
23
, Conjecture 2]
also proposed the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1

(Montgomery–Vaughan)
.

The number of fundamental discriminants
|d|\leq X
with
L(1,\chi_{d})\geq e^{\gamma}\left(\log_{2}|d|+\log_{3}|d|\right)
is
>X^{\theta}
and
<X^{\Theta}
, where
0<\theta<\Theta<1
.

Assuming the GRH, Granville and Soundararajan proved the upper bound part of the Conjecture
1
as a consequence of
[
14
, Theorem 4]
. Also, the authors’
[
14
, Theorem 5a]
provided a lower bound towards the conjecture by showing that there are
\gg X^{1/2}
primes
\leq X
such that

(3)

\displaystyle L(1,\chi_{p})\geq e^{\gamma}\left(\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-\log(2\log 2)+o(1)\right).

In this paper, we employ a long resonator technique to show the following “existence” and “proportion” of large values of
|L(1,\chi_{d})|
, as
d
varies over all fundamental discriminants in a range
|d|\leq X
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
X
be sufficiently large, and suppose GRH holds. Then there exists a fundamental discriminant
d
with
|d|\leq X
such that

|L(1,\chi_{d})|\geq e^{\gamma}\left(\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-C_{2}+o(1)\right),

where

C_{2}=\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{\log 2}{2}+\log\left(3\log 2-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\approx 0.455967.

Notice that
-C_{2}\approx-0.455967
is slightly weaker than
-\log(2\log 2)\approx-0.3266
in (
3
), which is anticipated given that we are considering the full family. However, the following lower bound of the proportion must be compared to the double exponential decay of (
1
) as explained in Remark
1
. This result provides that the double exponential decay of type (
1
) might persist up to the edge of the viable range,
\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X+C
, for some constant
C>0
. Such information cannot be uniformly deduced from Theorem 5a of
[
14
]
within a constant factor of the expected order of magnitude
\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X
. More precisely, if (
1
) holds for
\tau=\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X+C_{1}-\eta
for some
\eta>0
, then our results are optimal up to a precise value of constant.

Theorem 3
.

Assume GRH holds. For every
\eta>0
, we define
\Phi_{X}(\eta)
to be the proportion of fundamental discriminants
d
with
|d|\leq X
, for which
|L(1,\chi_{d})|>e^{\gamma}\tau_{\eta,X}
, where
\tau_{\eta,X}=\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-C_{2}-\eta
, i.e.,

\Phi_{X}(\eta):=\frac{1}{\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq X}1}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\\
|L(1,\chi_{d})|>e^{\gamma}\tau_{\eta,X}\end{subarray}}1.

Then we have,

\Phi_{X}(\eta)\geq X^{-\frac{e^{-\eta}}{2}\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log_{2}X}}\right)\right)}.

Remark 1
.

Proportionally, shifting to the left of
-C_{2}
by a constant allows us to establish a lower bound on the proportion of fundamental discriminants that decay double exponentially, uniformly dependent on the parameter

\eta
from Theorem
2
. For example, setting
\eta=0.044
yields
\Phi_{X}(\eta)\geq X^{-0.478}
according to Theorem
2
. Moreover, if the asymptotic (
1
) holds for
\tau=\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-C_{1}-C_{2}-\eta
to the quality in Theorem
2
, then
\Phi_{X}(\eta):=\Phi_{X}(\tau)\approx X^{-0.279e^{-\eta}}
, which is better than our proportion
X^{-0.5e^{-\eta}}
from Theorem
2
. The expression (
1
) fails to discern this proportion, given that
A=A(X)\rightarrow\infty
.
We must mention that for the other families examined in
[
3
,
4
]
, the value of
C_{2}

exceeds 1. However, by employing a sieving method on perfect squares, we reduce this value to
0.455967
.

Remark 2
.

The subfamily
\{L(1,\chi_{p}):p\leq X\}
achieves large values, both in terms of the constant and proportion, comparable to those established in Theorem

1.3.
Large values of
L(\sigma,\chi_{d})
when
\sigma\in(1/2,1)

In this range, based on Diophantine approximation, Montgomery
[
22
]
established that for large
T
,

(4)

\displaystyle\max_{t\in[T,2T]}\log|\zeta(\sigma+it)|\geq c(\sigma)(\log T)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}T)^{-\sigma},

where
c(\sigma)=\frac{(\sigma-1/2)^{1/2}}{20}
unconditionally and
c(\sigma)=1/20
under RH. The constant
c(\sigma)
under RH was later improved by Yang
[
28
]
using the long resonator technique of Bondarenko–Seip. Also, a prediction on
c(\sigma)
was proposed by Lamzouri
[
19
, Remark 2]
.

In
[
19
]
, Lamzouri studied the logarithm of the absolute value of
L
-functions at
1/2<\sigma<1
across various families, showing it can reach values as large as
(\log X)^{1-\sigma}/\log_{2}X
, where
X
is the conductor of the family. This result can also be attained using Soundararajan’s resonance method
[
26
]
. However, proving Montgomery’s
\Omega
-result (
4
) for other families of
L
-functions remains challenging.

For
1/2<\sigma<1
, Lamzouri
[
19
, Theroem 1.8]
achieved Montgomery’s
\Omega
-result for the subfamily
\{L(\sigma,\chi_{p}):p\leq X\}
under GRH, using an idea by Granville and Soundararajan
[
14
, Theorem 5a]
. More precisely, there are
\gg X^{1/2}
primes
p\leq X
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\log|L(\sigma,\chi_{p})|\geq\left(\beta(\sigma)+o(1)\right)(\log X)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}X)^{-\sigma},

where
\beta(\sigma)=(2\log 2)^{1-\sigma}/(1-\sigma)>\sqrt{2/\log 2}\approx 1.698
.

Using the long resonator technique, we extend this result to the full family of fundamental discriminants and establish the following theorem, recovering the constant found in Lamzouri’s above result.

Theorem 4
.

Assume GRH holds. Let
1/2<\sigma<1
, and
0<b<1
be given. For sufficiently large
X
, there exists a fundamental discriminant
d
with
|d|\leq X
such that

\log|L(\sigma,\chi_{d})|\geq\left(\alpha(\sigma,b)+o(1)\right)(\log X)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}X)^{-\sigma},

where
\alpha(\sigma,b)=\frac{b}{1-\sigma}\left(\alpha(b)\right)^{\sigma-1}
and
\alpha(b)=2\log\left(\frac{(1+b)^{2}}{1+b^{2}}\right)
.

Observe that when
b
approaches
1
in Theorem
4
, we have
a(\sigma,b)>\sqrt{2/\log 2}\approx 1.698
, also found in (
5
). Here,
b
represents the coefficient of the resonator at primes.
Furthermore, the following theorem establishes a lower bound for the proportion within the expected range of uniformity
\asymp_{\sigma}(\log X)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}X)^{-\sigma}
, except the optimal value of constant that may depend on
\sigma
. This result should be comparable to Theorem 6 in
[
19
]
, as discussed in Remark
3
.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X,b,\alpha(b),\alpha(\sigma,b)
be as in Theorem
4
. Assume GRH holds. For every
0<\eta<1/\alpha(b),
let
\Phi_{X}(\sigma,\eta)
be the proportion of fundamental discriminants
d
such that
|d|\leq X
and
\log|L(\sigma,\chi_{d})|>\tau_{\eta,X}
, where
\tau_{\eta,X}=\alpha(\sigma,b)\left(1-\eta\alpha(b)\right)^{1-\sigma}(\log X)^{1-\sigma}(\log_{2}X)^{-\sigma}
. That is

\Phi_{X}(\sigma,\eta)=\frac{1}{\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq X}1}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\\
\log|L(\sigma,\chi_{d})|>\tau_{\eta,X}\end{subarray}}1.

Then we have,

\Phi_{X}(\sigma,\eta)\geq X^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\eta\alpha(b)\right)\left(1+O\left(1/\sqrt{\log_{2}X}\right)\right)}.

Remark 3
.

Theorem 6 in
[
19
]
establishes the existence of a constant
c_{2}(\sigma)>0
such that, uniformly in the range
\tau\leq c_{2}(\sigma)(\log X\log_{4}X)^{1-\sigma}/\log_{2}X

(6)

\displaystyle\Phi_{X}(\sigma,\tau)=\exp\left(-A_{2}(\sigma)\tau^{\frac{1}{1-\sigma}}(\log\tau)^{\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}}\left(1+O\left(1/\sqrt{\log\tau}+r(y,\tau)\right)\right)\right),

Theorem

provides a method to generate a lower bound for the proportion uniformly in an extended range

compared to the range in (

very close to

and a different error term instead of

Moreover, the theorem can be doable to the subfamily

Further Discussion

The arguments presented in Lemma
1
exhibit broad applicability, with potential analogues extendable to various other families of
L
-functions. For instance, cubic
L
-functions over rationals (see
[
10
]
) and quadratic Dirichlet
L
-functions in the hyperelliptic ensemble (see
[
21
]
) are notable examples. Furthermore, the methodologies employed in proving Theorems
1
–

1.5.
Strategy of the paper

For
\bm{\sigma=1/2}
:
We employ the resonance technique, as outlined in
[
7
]
, focusing on sub-GCD sums rather than full GCD sums as described in
[
9
]
. This entails analyzing
\sum_{|d|\leq X}L(1/2,\chi_{d})R_{d}^{2}
instead of
\sum_{|d|\leq X}L(1/2,\chi_{d})^{2}R_{d}^{2}
, because of the non-square contributions from the characters, the sum significantly increases in its second moment. Here,
R_{d}
is known as the resonator of
L(1/2,\chi_{d})
. In fact, the resonator
R_{d}
is chosen
as a Dirichlet polynomial, and it is often suitable to choose a function with multiplicative coefficients. It can be written as a finite Euler product. Bondarenko–Seip approach achieves superior results by introducing the concept of a “long resonator,” unlike Soundararajan’s methods.

The main idea is to decompose the sum
\sum_{|d|\leq X}L(1/2,\chi_{d})R_{d}^{2}
into components, distinguishing between square and non-square parts arising from the character sum. The character sum accumulates positive mass from the square terms where positivity applies, and we employ the GRH to take control of the error term by establishing bounds within the zero-free region of the corresponding
L
-functions. This is achieved using Lemma
1
, where
f(n_{0})
and
g(n_{1})
play a crucial role in mitigating the impact of the lengthy resonators.
Any sufficiently small exponent applied to the conductor in the error term of the character sum in Lemma
1
will disrupt the efficacy of the long resonator technique.

For
\bm{1/2<\sigma\leq 1}
:
In this interval, we use the resonator
R_{d}
as a short Euler product in an utterly multiplicative way because the corresponding
L
-function has a precise and effective short Euler product approximation. It is important to emphasize that the analysis of the resonator at the central point is distinct, as the resonator is not completely multiplicative.

The remaining analysis follows the same approach described in the second paragraph of the strategy for
\sigma=1/2
, with the additional task of handling non-square-free integers when bounding the functions
f
and
g
. For
\sigma=1
, we count the exact number of ways the product of three integers forms a square to optimize the constant
C_{2}
. Reducing
C_{2}
is challenging due to the difficulty of approximating the resonator coefficients of the form
r_{p}=1-p/z
. For
\sigma\in(1/2,1)
, we select the resonator coefficient as a constant close to
1
throughout the segment.

2.
A useful Lemma on character sum

This section establishes a crucial estimate regarding character sums that are pivotal for proving our main theorems.
We establish the following character sum using an approximation lemma for the logarithm of Dirichlet
L
-functions.

Lemma 1
.

Assume GRH holds.
Let
n=n_{0}n_{1}^{2}
be a positive integer with
n_{0}
square-free part of
n
. Then for any
\varepsilon>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{|d|\leq X}\chi_{d}(n)=\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{p|n}\left(\frac{p}{p+1}\right)\mathds{1}_{n=\square}+O\left(X^{1/2+\varepsilon}f(n_{0})g(n_{1})\right),

where
f(n_{0})=\exp\left((\log n_{0})^{1-\varepsilon}\right)
arises from the square-free component, while
g(n_{1})=\sum_{d\mid n_{1}}\frac{\mu^{2}(d)}{b^{1/2+\varepsilon}}
originates from the square component of the modulus, and
\mathds{1}_{n=\square}
indicates the indicator function of the square numbers.

Proof.

Since fundamental discriminants
d
are either
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
,
d
square-free, or
d=4N,N\equiv 2,3\pmod{4}
,
N
square-free, we can confine ourselves to the case
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
and the other cases can be handled similarly.

The contribution of the numbers
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
to the sum is equal to

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\psi_{4}\,\text{(mod 4)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X\\
(d,n_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mu^{2}(d)\psi_{4}(d)\chi_{n_{0}}(d)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\psi_{4}\,\text{(mod 4)}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}d\leq X\end{subarray}}\psi_{4}(d)\chi_{n_{0}}(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{2}\mid d\\
(a,n_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\sum_{b\mid(d,n_{1})}\mu(b)

(7)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\psi_{4}\,\text{(mod 4)}}\sum_{b\mid n_{1}}\mu(b)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{2}b\leq X\\
(a,n_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\mu(a)\sum_{d\leq\frac{X}{a^{2}b}}\psi_{4}(d)\chi_{n_{0}}(d),

where
\psi_{4}
is a character of modulus
4
and the two inner sums involving the Möbius function come from the condition that
d
is square-free and
(d,n_{1})=1
. To estimate the following character sum:

S(Z;n_{0}):=\sum_{d\leq Z}\chi(d),

where
\chi=\psi_{4}\chi_{n_{0}}
is a quadratic character of modulus
4n_{0}
and
Z:=X/(a^{2}b)
, we can start with the case
n_{0}>1
. Then,
\chi
is always a non-principal character. Applying the Perron’s formula, for every
\varepsilon>0
,

S(Z;n_{0})=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(1+\varepsilon)}L(s,\chi)Z^{s}\frac{ds}{s}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{1+\varepsilon-iT}^{1+\varepsilon+iT}L(s,\chi)Z^{s}\frac{ds}{s}+O\left(\frac{Z^{1+\varepsilon}}{T}\right),

where

L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\chi(n)}{n^{s}}.

We now extend an argument in the proof of Lemma 4.5 in
[
14
]
.
Using Lemma 8.2 of
[
15
]
, if
\sigma_{1}=\min\left(\sigma_{0}+\frac{1}{\log y},\frac{\sigma+\sigma_{0}}{2}\right)
, where
y\geq 2,|t|\geq y+3
, and
\sigma>\sigma_{0}\geq 1/2
, then

(8)

\displaystyle\log L(s,\chi)=\sum_{n=2}^{y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi(n)}{n^{s}\log n}+O\left(\frac{\log q}{(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0})^{2}}y^{\sigma_{1}-\sigma}\right).

For
|t|\leq T
and
y=(\log T)^{\beta}
, we get

|\log L(s,\chi)|\ll y^{1-\sigma}+\frac{\log T}{(\sigma_{1}-\sigma_{0})^{2}}y^{\sigma_{1}-\sigma}\ll(\log T)^{\beta(1-\sigma)}+(\log T)^{1+\beta(\sigma_{0}-\sigma)}\log_{2}T.

Inserting this bound after shifting the line of integration in the above Perron’s integral to
\Re(s)=\sigma>\sigma_{0}\geq\frac{1}{2}
,

S(Z;n_{0})\ll Z^{\sigma}\exp\left((\log T)^{\beta(1-\sigma)}+(\log T)^{1+\beta(\sigma_{0}-\sigma)}\log_{2}T\right)+\frac{Z^{1+\varepsilon}}{T}.

Due to the periodicity of characters, we may suppose that
Z\leq 4n_{0}
. Also equating
\beta(1-\sigma)
and
1+\beta(\sigma_{0}-\sigma)
, we can choose
\sigma_{0}=\frac{\beta-1}{\beta}
. Take
T=n_{0}
. Therefore, for
\sigma>\sigma_{0}\geq 1/2
,

S(Z;n_{0})\ll Z^{\sigma}f_{\sigma}(n_{0}),

where
f_{\sigma}(n_{0})=\exp\left((\log n_{0})^{\beta(1-\sigma)}\right)
.

Plugging in these estimates, for
\sigma\geq 1/2+\varepsilon
, we obtain

\eqref{contri}\ll X^{\sigma}f_{\sigma}(n_{0})\sum_{b\mid n_{1}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{2}b\leq X\\
(a,n_{1}=1)\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu^{2}(a)}{a^{2\sigma}}\ll X^{\sigma}f_{\sigma}(n_{0})g_{\sigma}(n_{1}),

where
g_{\sigma}(n_{1})=\sum_{b\mid n_{1}}\frac{\mu^{2}(b)}{b^{\sigma}}
.

When
n_{0}=1
, we isolate the principal and non-principal characters from the outermost sum, allowing us to manage the non-principal characters using the method outlined above. Therefore, in this case, the contribution of the numbers
d\equiv 1\pmod{4}
to the sum equals

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{2}\sum_{b\mid n_{1}}\frac{\mu(b)}{b}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a^{2}b\leq X\\
(a,n_{1})=1\end{subarray}}\frac{\mu(a)}{a^{2}}+O\left(X^{\sigma}f_{\sigma}(n_{0})g_{\sigma}(n_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{2\zeta(2)}\prod_{p\mid n_{1}}\left(\frac{p}{p+1}\right)+O\left(X^{\sigma}f_{\sigma}(n_{0})g_{\sigma}(n_{1})\right).

In particular, taking
\sigma=\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon
and
\beta=2
, we complete the proof.
∎

The Resonator for

The resonator is the Dirichlet polynomial

R_{d}:=\sum_{m\in\mathcal{R}}\psi(m)\chi_{d}(m),

with

Following section 2 of

Let

and
\delta\in(0,1)
be fixed real numbers.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be the set of all primes

\displaystyle\psi(p)=\sqrt{\frac{\log N\log_{2}N}{\log_{3}N}}p^{-1/2}\left(\log p-\log(\log N\log_{2}N)\right)^{-1}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{k}
be the set of all primes
p

be the set of positive integers numbers that have at least

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}_{k}
be the set of integers from
\mathcal{R}_{k}
that having all their prime divisors in
\mathcal{P}_{k}

Lemma 2
.

If

Proof.

Observe that,
\mathcal{R}
is the set of square-free numbers that have at most
\frac{a\log N}{k^{2}\log_{3}N}
prime divisors in each set
\mathcal{P}_{k}
.
So, if
n\in\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle\log n\leq\sum_{k=1}^{[(\log_{2}N)^{\delta}]}\frac{a\log N}{k^{2}\log_{3}N}\log\left(e^{k+1}\log N\log_{2}N\right)\ll\frac{\log N\log_{2}N}{\log_{3}N},

Lemma 8
.

Let
y=(\log X)^{180}

fundamental discriminants

\displaystyle L(1,\chi_{d})=L(1,\chi_{d};y)\left(1+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log X)^{10}}\right)\right).

\displaystyle\frac{S_{1}^{*}}{S_{2}^{*}}\geq e^{\gamma}\left(\log_{2}X+\log_{3}X-c_{3}-\log c+O\left(\frac{1}{\log_{2}X}\right)\right),

where

).
Also, from (
22
), we can choose

in such a way that

(26)

\displaystyle\log c=\log(2(3\log 2-\pi/2))+5\eta-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\log_{2}X}}.

The additional factor

. We define it this way to maintain uniformity, although any factor of the form

Hence, using Lemma

(

)
, we conclude that there exists a fundamental discriminant

6.
Proof of Theorem
4

Since the GRH is assumed, then equation (
8
) with
t=0
,
y=(\log X)^{4/(\sigma-1/2)}
and
\sigma_{0}=\sigma/2+1/4>1/2
, we obtain

(27)

\displaystyle\log L(\sigma,\chi_{d})=\sum_{n=2}^{y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{\sigma}\log n}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log X)^{2}}\right).

We assume that
1/2<\sigma<1
is fixed, and set
y=(\log X)^{4/(\sigma-1/2)}
.
Since

\sum_{k\geq 2}\sum_{n=p^{k}}^{y}\frac{\Lambda(n)\chi_{d}(n)}{n^{\sigma}}\ll 1,

it follows that

(28)

\displaystyle\log L(\sigma,\chi_{d})=S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)+O(1),

where
S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)=\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{\chi_{d}(p)}{p^{\sigma}}
. Consequently, to prove the theorem, it suffices to exhibit large values of
S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)
. To achieve this, we employ a long resonator technique to approximate
S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)
, similar to the approach taken in the previous section.

We set
Y=a\log X\log_{2}X
, where
a>0
to be optimized later. Furthermore, we set
r_{1}=1
and
r_{p}=0
for
p>Y
, and
r_{p}=b
for all primes
p\leq Y
, where
0<b<1
is fixed. We extend this in a completely multiplicative manner to obtain weights
r_{n}
for all
n\geq 1
. Define

\displaystyle R_{d}=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(1-r_{p}\chi_{d}(p)\right)^{-1}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}r_{n}\chi_{d}(n).

Similar to the previous sections, we consider the sums

\displaystyle S_{1}(\sigma):=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)R_{d}^{2}\quad\quad\text{ and }\quad\quad S_{2}(\sigma):=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}R_{d}^{2}.

Therefore

(29)

\displaystyle\max_{|d|\leq X}|S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)|\geq\frac{|S_{1}(\sigma)|}{S_{2}(\sigma)}.

Applying Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{1}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}S_{\chi_{d}}(\sigma,y)R_{d}^{2}=\sum_{m,n}r_{m}r_{n}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}(mnp)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mnp=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{m}r_{n}}{p^{\sigma}}\Bigg(\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\,prime\\
q\mid mnp\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)+O\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}g(k)\right)\Bigg)

\displaystyle+O\Bigg(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\varepsilon}{3}}\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
mnp=s_{0}s_{1}^{2}\\
\mu(s_{0})\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{n}f(s_{0})g(s_{1})\Bigg).

The previous section shows that it is enough to prove the second error term, where we use the bounds on
f
and
g
. Since
g(s_{1})\leq g(s_{0}s_{1}^{2})\leq g(m)g(n)g(p)
and
f(s_{0})\ll X^{\varepsilon/3}
, the second error term is bounded by

\displaystyle\ll X^{1/2+2\varepsilon/3}\left(\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p^{\sigma}}\right)\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\end{subarray}}r_{m}g(m)\right)^{2}.

Note that,
r_{m}
and
g(m)
are completely multiplicative and multiplicative functions with
r_{p}=b
and
g(p^{l})=g(p)=1+\frac{1}{p^{1/2+\varepsilon}}
for
l\geq 1
respectively. Again, using the prime number theorem and the choice of
Y
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 1\end{subarray}}r_{m}g(m)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(1+r_{p}g(p)+r^{2}_{p}g(p)+r^{3}_{p}g(p)+\ldots\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(1+\frac{r_{p}g(p)}{1-r_{p}}\right)=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(\frac{1}{1-b}+\frac{b}{(1-b)p^{1/2+\varepsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-(\log(1-b)+O(1/\log Y))\frac{Y}{\log Y}\right)=X^{-a\log(1-b)(1+O(1/\log_{2}X))}.

Also, for
1/2<\sigma<1
,

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{g(p)}{p^{\sigma}}=\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma}}+\sum_{p\leq y}\frac{1}{p^{\sigma+1/2+\varepsilon}}=(1+o(1))\frac{y^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)\log y}\ll X^{\varepsilon/3}.

Therefore, both the error terms in
S_{1}(\sigma)
are
O\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}-2a\log(1-b)+\varepsilon}\right).

The condition
mnp=\square
forces
p\leq Y
. Using positivity of the coefficients
r_{m}
, and collecting those primes
p
which divide
n
,

\displaystyle\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\sum_{p\leq y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mnp=\square\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{m}r_{n}}{p^{\sigma}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\,prime\\
q\mid mnp\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\sum_{p\leq Y}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mnp=\square\\
p\mid n\end{subarray}}\frac{r_{m}r_{n}}{p^{\sigma}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\,prime\\
q\mid mn\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\left(\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{r_{p}}{p^{\sigma}}\right)\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geq 1\\
ml=\square\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{l}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}q\,prime\\
q\mid ml\end{subarray}}\left(\frac{q}{q+1}\right)\Bigg),

where we substitute
n=pl
.
Again, the prime number theorem and the choice of
Y=a\log X\log_{2}X
yield

\displaystyle\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{r_{p}}{p^{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\leq Y}\frac{b}{p^{\sigma}}=(1+O(1/\log Y))\frac{bY^{1-\sigma}}{(1-\sigma)\log Y}

\displaystyle=\frac{ba^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}(1+O(1/\log_{2}X))\left(\log X\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{2}X\right)^{-\sigma}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle S_{1}(\sigma)=\frac{ba^{1-\sigma}(1+O(1/\log_{2}X))}{1-\sigma}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,l\geq 1\\
ml=\square\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{l}h(ml)\Bigg)\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\left(\log X\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{2}X\right)^{-\sigma}

\displaystyle+O\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}-2a\log(1-b)+\varepsilon}\right).

Similarly, again using Lemma
1
and following the estimate of
S_{1}(\sigma)
, we get

\displaystyle S_{2}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}R_{d}^{2}=\sum_{m,n}r_{m}r_{n}\sideset{}{{}^{\flat}}{\sum}_{\begin{subarray}{c}|d|\leq X\end{subarray}}\chi_{d}(mn)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mn=\square\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{n}h(mn)+O\Bigg(X^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\geq 1\\
mn=s_{0}s_{1}^{2}\\
\mu(s_{0})\neq 0\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{n}f(s_{0})g(s_{1})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{X}{\zeta(2)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mn=\square\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{n}h(mn)+O\left(X^{\frac{1}{2}-2a\log(1-b)+\varepsilon}\right).

The resonator sum can be evaluated as

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m,n\\
mn=\square\end{subarray}}r_{m}r_{n}h(mn)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\end{subarray}}r^{2}_{k}\,d(k^{2})h(k)=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(1-h(p)+h(p)(1-r_{p}^{2})^{-2}(1+r_{p}^{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\prod_{p\leq Y}\left(\frac{1+b^{2}}{(1-b^{2})^{2}}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p+1}+\frac{(1-b^{2})^{2}}{(1+b^{2})(p+1)}\right)

(30)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(\theta(b)\left(1+O(1/\log Y)\right)\frac{Y}{\log Y}\right)=X^{a\theta(b)\left(1+O(1/\log_{2}X)\right)},

where

.

We evaluated this resonator sum to find an optimal value of

as below.
Combining these estimates and comparing the error terms, we finally conclude

(31)

\displaystyle\frac{S_{1}(\sigma)}{S_{2}(\sigma)}\geq\frac{ba^{1-\sigma}}{1-\sigma}(1+O(1/\log_{2}X))\left(\log X\right)^{1-\sigma}\left(\log_{2}X\right)^{-\sigma},

provided that

\frac{1}{2}-2a\log(1-b)<1+a\,\theta(b)\iff a<\left(2\log\left(\frac{(1+b)^{2}}{1+b^{2}}\right)\right)^{-1}.

By choosing

with
\eta>0
arbitrary small, from (
28
), (
29
) and (
31
), we complete the proof.

Here we must mention that following the same path as of the Theorem

counting

to the sum

, one can obtain

instead of

in (

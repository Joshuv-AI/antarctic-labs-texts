Restarted Nonnegativity Preserving Tensor Splitting Methods via Relaxed Anderson Acceleration for Solving Multilinear Systems
Dongdong Liu, Ting HuXifu Liu

Abstract
Multilinear systems play an important role in scientific calculations of practical problems. In this paper, we consider a tensor splitting method with a relaxed Anderson acceleration for solving the multilinear systems. The new method preserves nonnegativity for every iterative step and improves the existing ones. Furthermore, the convergence analysis of the proposed method is given. The new algorithm performs effectively for numerical experiments.



Key words.
Tensor splitting, multilinear systems, Anderson acceleration, strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor

{{\bf{x}}_{k}}={(M{(\mathcal{E})^{-1}}\mathcal{F}{\bf{x}}_{k-1}^{l-1}+M{(\mathcal{E})^{-1}}{\bf{b}})^{[\frac{1}{{l-1}}]}},

(1.3)

The iterative solution of which is a fixed point of the following iterative function

19

20

[

\mathcal{M}
-tensor. Besides, the convergence analysis is given for preserving the nonnegativity. Furthermore, the proposed algorithm performs better than the existing preconditioned methods in numerical experiments.

Preliminaries

An order
l
and dimension
n
tensor
\mathcal{A}
with
n^{l}
elements is defined by

\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}),a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{l}}\in\mathbb{R},i_{j}\in\langle n\rangle,j=1,\cdots,l,

where
\mathbb{R}
denotes the real field,
\langle n\rangle=\{1,\cdots,n\}
and
n
is a positive integer. We use
\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
to denote the set of all order
l
dimension
n
real tensors. If
l=1
,
\mathbb{R}^{[1,n]}
reduces to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is commonly used to denote the set of all dimension
n
real vectors. More specifically,
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
(
\mathbb{R}_{++}^{n}
)
is indicted as the set of all nonnegative (positive) vectors. Let
{{0}}
denote a null number, a null vector, a null matrix or a null tensor. Let
\mathcal{A},~\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
. The notation
\mathcal{A}\geq\mathcal{B}

(>\mathcal{B})
refers that every element of
\mathcal{A}
is no less than (strictly greater than) the corresponding one of
\mathcal{B}
. Furthermore,
||\cdot||
denotes the 2-norm of a vector or a matrix.

Qi
[
2
]
and Lim
[
25
]
independently gave the definition of tensor eigenvalues and eigenvectors, i.e., a complex number
\lambda
and a nonzero complex vector
{\bf{x}}
are called as an eigenvalue and an eigenvector of
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
, respectively, if the following equation holds:

\mathcal{A}{\bf{x}}^{l-1}=\lambda{\bf{x}}^{[l-1]},

where
{\bf{x}}^{[l-1]}=(x_{1}^{l-1},...,x_{n}^{l-1})^{T}
. Besides,
(\lambda,{\bf{x}})
is called as an
H
-eigenpair
if both
\lambda
and
{\bf{x}}
are real. Furthermore, the spectral radius of
\mathcal{A}
is signified by
\rho(\mathcal{A})=\max\{\left|\lambda\right||\lambda\in\sigma(\mathcal{A})\}
with
\sigma(\mathcal{A})
being the set of all eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
. We call
\mathcal{I}_{l}\in\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
a
unit tensor
if its entries are given by

{\delta_{{i_{1}}\cdots{i_{l}}}}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}1,{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{i_{1}}=\cdots={i_{l}},\\
0,{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}{\kern 1.0pt}\mbox{else.}\\
\end{array}\right.

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
is a
\mathcal{Z}
-tensor
if its off-diagonal entries are non-positive. Besides,
\mathcal{A}
is an
\mathcal{M}
-tensor
(see the works of
[
26
,
27
]
) if there exists a nonnegative tensor
\mathcal{B}
and a positive real number
\eta\geq\rho(\mathcal{B})
such that
\mathcal{A}=\eta\mathcal{I}_{l}-\mathcal{B}.

Furthermore, if
\eta>\rho(\mathcal{B})
,
\mathcal{A}
is a
strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor
. Let
A\in{\mathbb{R}^{[2,n]}}
and
\mathcal{B}\in{\mathbb{R}^{[k,n]}}
. A product
[
28
]

\mathcal{C}=A\mathcal{B}\in\mathbb{R}^{[k,n]}
is introduced by

{c_{j{i_{2}}\cdots{i_{k}}}}=\sum\limits_{{j_{2}}=1}^{{n}}{{a_{j{j_{2}}}}{b_{{j_{2}}{i_{2}}\cdots{i_{k}}}}}.

In
[
10
]
, it is pointed out that if
\mathcal{A}
is a left-nonsingular tensor if and only if
\mathcal{A}=M(\mathcal{A})\mathcal{I}_{l}
and
M(\mathcal{A})
is nonsingular. If
\mathcal{A}
is a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor, then
M(\mathcal{A})
is a nonsingular
\mathcal{M}
-matrix. Besides, the some concepts on tensor splittings are listed as follows.

Definition 2.1
.

[
10
]

Let
\mathcal{A},{\kern 2.0pt}\mathcal{E},{\kern 2.0pt}\mathcal{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{[l,n]}
.

(1)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
is a splitting of

if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is a left-nonsingular.

(2)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
is a regular splitting of

if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is left-nonsingular with

, and
\mathcal{F}\geq 0
.

(3)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
is a weak regular splitting of
\mathcal{A}
if and only if
\mathcal{E}
is left-nonsingular with
M(\mathcal{E})^{-1}\geq 0
and
M(\mathcal{E})^{-1}\mathcal{F}\geq 0
.

(4)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
is a convergent splitting if and only if

.

3
Tensor splitting methods with Anderson acceleration

Let
f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})=g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-{\bf{x}}.
The tensor splitting methods with the relaxed Anderson acceleration is proposed for solving the multilinear systems (
1.1
) as follows.

Algorithm 1
Restarted tensor splitting methods with the relaxed Anderson acceleration(RTSMRAA)

1:

Require: Given a positive vector
{\bf{b}}
, a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor
\mathcal{A}
with its (weak) regular splitting
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
, maximum
k_{{\bf{max}}}
, a positive initial vector
{\bf{z}}_{0}
and
m\geq 1
.

2:

Update
{\bf{z}}_{1}=g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})
,
{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{1}={\bf{z}}_{1}
,
{\bf{y}}_{1}={\bf{z}}_{1}
and
f_{0}={\bf{z}}_{1}-{\bf{z}}_{0}

3:

for
k=1,2,…,
k_{{\bf{max}}}

do

4:

Set
m_{k}=\min\{m,k\}

5:

Update

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{k+1}&=g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{k}),\\
f_{k}&={\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{k+1}-{\bf{z}}_{k}.\end{split}

(3.1)

6:

Set
Q_{k}=(f_{k-m_{k}},...,f_{k})
.

7:

Determine
\boldsymbol{\alpha}^{(k)}=(\alpha_{0}^{(k)},...,\alpha_{m_{k}}^{(k)})^{T}
that solves

\min_{\alpha=(\alpha_{0},...,\alpha_{m_{k}})^{T}}{||Q_{k}\alpha||}~\textit{s.t.}~\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{k}}\alpha_{i}=1.

(3.2)

8:

Update

{\bf{y}}_{k+1}=\sum_{i=0}^{m_{k}}\alpha_{i}^{(k)}g_{\mathcal{E}}(z_{k-m_{k}+i}).

9:

if

{\bf{y}}_{k+1}\geq 0
and
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{k}}|\alpha_{i}^{(k)}|\leq\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}

then

10:

Choose
\theta_{k}\in[0,1]
to update
{\bf{z}}_{k+1}=\theta_{k}{\bf{y}}_{k+1}+(1-\theta_{k}){{\boldsymbol{\mu}}}_{k+1}
.

11:

else

12:

Update
{\bf{z}}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k+1}
.

13:

end

if

14:

Update
k\leftarrow k+1
.

15:

end

for

16:

return

{\bf{z}}_{k}

Remark 3.1
.

The optimization problem (
3.2
) can be solved by the following equivalent the square problem
[
23
]
:
Give
\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(k)}=(\zeta_{0}^{(k)},\zeta_{1}^{(k)},...,\zeta_{m_{k}-1}^{(k)})^{T}
by solving

\min_{\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(k)}=(\zeta_{0}^{(k)},\zeta_{1}^{(k)},...,\zeta_{m_{k}-1}^{(k)})^{T}}{||f_{k}-F_{k}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(k)}||},

(3.3)

where
F_{k}=(\Delta f_{k-m_{k}},...,\Delta f_{k-1})
and
\Delta f_{i}=f_{i+1}-f_{i}
.
Furthermore, Step 8 in the RTSMRAA is written as follows:

{\bf{y}}_{k+1}={\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{k+1}-G_{k}\boldsymbol{\zeta}^{(k)},

(3.4)

where
G_{k}=(\Delta g_{k-m_{k}},...,\Delta g_{k-1})
and
\Delta g_{i}=g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{i+1})-g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{i}).

The solution
\boldsymbol{\zeta}
of the least square problem (
3.3
) and the solution
\alpha
of the optimization problem (
3.2
) are related by
\alpha_{0}=\zeta_{0},\alpha_{i}=\zeta_{i}-\zeta_{i-1}
for
i=1,...,m_{k}-1
and
\alpha_{m_{k}}=1-\zeta_{m_{k}-1}
.

4
Convergence analysis

In this section, we will consider the convergence analysis of Algorithm
3
. Let
\mathbb{U}_{\delta}\equiv\{{\bf{x}}\big|||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||\leq\delta,\delta>0\}
. We write
a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{l}}
as
a_{i_{1}\pi}
, where
\pi=i_{2}\cdots i_{l}
.
Notice that for any
\mathcal{A}=(a_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{l}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{[l,n]}
, it is easy to check that

{\mathcal{A}}{\bf{x}}^{l-1}=\overline{\mathcal{A}}{\bf{x}}^{l-1}

where
\overline{\mathcal{A}}=(\overline{a}_{i_{1}i_{2}\cdots i_{l}})
is given by:

{\overline{a}_{ii_{2}\cdots i_{l}}}=\frac{1}{(l-1)!}\sum\limits_{\pi\in\mathbb{G}}{{a_{i\pi}}},

and
\mathbb{G}
denotes the set of all permutations in
({i_{2}}\cdots{i_{l}})
. Then
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
is symmetric on the last
l-1
indices (
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
is called semi-symmetric
[
29
]
). For the sake of clarity, we always denote the semi-symmetric of a tensor by the notation ‘
\overline{{\cdot}}
’ (e.g.,
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
). Firstly, we give some lemmas as follows.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor,
{\bf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
be a (weak) regular splitting. Then the function
g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
has the following properties:

(1)

g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
for all
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
.

(2)

g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
is monotone increasing in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, i.e., for any
0\leq{\bf{x}}\leq{\bf{y}}
,
g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})\leq g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{y}})
.

Proof
:
Since
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
is a (weak) regular splitting, the second assertion is obvious. Because
{\bf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
, it is obtained that
M(\mathcal{E})^{-1}{\bf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
. Thus, the first affirmation is proved.

Proposition 4.2
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
4.1
, for any initial vector
{\bf{z}}_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
with
{{0}}<\mathcal{A}{{\bf{z}}_{0}^{l-1}}\leq{\bf{b}}
, we get
{\bf{z}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
generated by the RTSMRAA if
\theta_{k}\in[0,1)
in Step 10 of the RTSMRAA for all
k\geq 1
.

Proof
:
Since
\mathcal{A}{\bf{z}}_{0}^{l-1}\leq{\bf{b}}
, it is true that
{\bf{z}}_{0}\leq{\bf{z}}_{1}
under the assumption of Lemma
4.1
.
By Lemma
4.1
, we know
\boldsymbol{\mu}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
for any
k\geq 1
via the induction. Then the proof is completed.

Remark 4.3
.

If
\theta_{k}=1
in Step 10 of the RTSMRAA for some
k
, it is noted that the TSM is just improved by the restarted Anderson acceleration for the
k
-th iteration, i.e.,
{\bf{z}}_{k}={\bf{y}}_{k}
. In this case, we still set
{\bf{y}}_{k+1}\geq 0
rather than
{\bf{y}}_{k+1}>0
in Step 9 of the RTSMRAA. That’s because, by Lemma
4.1
, we can still get a vector
{\bf{z}}_{k+1}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
. If
{\bf{y}}_{k+1}
is not nonnegative for all
k
, the RTSMRAA reduces to the TSM. Therefore, the next step
{\bf{z}}_{k+1}
can be always positive by Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
{\bf{x}}^{*}
be the solution of the multilinear systems (
1.1
) with
\mathcal{A}
being a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor and
{\bf{b}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{++}
. For every (weak) regular splitting
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{E}-\mathcal{F}
, there exists a
\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
such that the mapping
g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
has the following properties for a sufficiently small
\delta
:

(i)

For any
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
,
g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
.

(ii)

For any
{\bf{x}}
,
{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
, there exists a real number
c\in[0,1)
such that

||g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{y}})||\leq c||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}||.

(iii)

For any
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
,

\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})=\big(\mathcal{E}g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})^{[l-2]}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{\overline{F}}{\bf{x}}^{l-2},~\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})=\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-I.

(iv)

\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*})
is Lipschitz continuously, i.e., for any
{\bf{x}}
,
{\bf{y}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
, there exists a real number
\gamma>0
such that

||\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{y}})||\leq\gamma||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}||.

Proof
:
Since
\mathcal{A}
is a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor and
{\bf{b}}
is positive, by Lemma
2.3
,
{\bf{x}^{*}}
is the unique positive solution. By the proof of Theorem 5.7 in
[
10
]
, we have that
\mathcal{E}g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}^{*}})^{[l-2]}=\mathcal{E}({\bf{x}^{*}})^{[l-2]}
is nonsingular and
\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})=\big(\mathcal{E}g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})^{[l-2]}\big)^{-1}\mathcal{\overline{F}}{\bf{x}}^{l-2}
satisfying
\rho(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*}))\leq c<1
. Because
g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
and
\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
are continuous, there exists
\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
such that
\rho\big(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})\big)\leq c<1
and
\mathcal{E}g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})^{[l-2]}
is nonsingular for any
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
. Thus, it is easy to check that the affirmation
(ii)
holds by differential mean value theorem and the term
(iii)
is given. Besides, for any
{\bf{x}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
, we have

\displaystyle||g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-{\bf{x}}^{*}||=||g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*})||\leq c||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||<||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||\leq\delta.

Then the desired result

is obtained.
Furthermore, by the result 3.3.5 of
[

]
and the second G-derivative
\nabla^{2}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
of
\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})
at
{\bf{x}}
is continuous, we have

||\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{y}})||\leq\max\limits_{{\bf{x},{\bf{y}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\big(||\nabla^{2}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}+t({\bf{y}}-{\bf{x}}))||\big)||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{y}}||.

Since
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
is a closed set, the term
(iv)
is shown by
\gamma=\max\limits_{{\bf{x},{\bf{y}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}}\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}\big(||\nabla^{2}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}+t({\bf{y}}-{\bf{x}}))||\big).

Proposition 4.5
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
4.4
, we have
\big(\mathbb{I}\setminus\{{\bf{x}}^{*}\}\big)\cap\big(\mathbb{U}_{\delta}\setminus\{{\bf{x}}^{*}\})\neq\emptyset
, where
\mathbb{I}=\big\{{\bf{x}}\big|{\bf{x}}>0,0<\mathcal{A}{\bf{x}}^{l-1}\leq b\big\}
and
\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
is defined in Lemma
4.4
.

Proof
:
By Lemma
2.4
,
\mathbb{I}\setminus\{{\bf{x}}^{*}\}\neq\emptyset
. Let
p({\bf{x}})=\mathcal{A}{\bf{x}}^{l-1}
which is a continuous function from
\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Thus, there exists a real number
\delta^{{}^{\prime}}>0
such that
0<\mathcal{A}{\widehat{{\bf{x}}}}^{l-1}\leq b
for any
\widehat{{\bf{x}}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\delta^{{}^{\prime}}}\setminus\{{\bf{x}}^{*}\}
. Let
\delta^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=\min\big\{\delta^{{}^{\prime}},\delta\big\}
. It is easy to check that

. Therefore, the proof is completed.

.

Under the assumption of Lemma
4.4
, there exists
\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}\subseteq\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
with sufficiently small
\hat{\delta}
such that for any

,

||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*})({\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*})||\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||^{2}

(4.1)

and

(1-c)||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||\leq||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})||\leq(1+c)||{\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*}||.

(4.2)

Proof
:
The formula (
4.1
) is a direct result by 3.2.12 of
[
30
]
and
(iii)
of Lemma
4.4
. The second formula can be shown by
(ii)
of Lemma
4.4
and

\displaystyle||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})||=||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*})||=||g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}})-g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{x}}^{*})+({\bf{x}}-{\bf{x}}^{*})||.

Let
\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{k}={\bf{z}}_{k}-{\bf{z}}^{*}
,
\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{k}={\bf{y}}_{k}-{\bf{z}}^{*}
and
{\bf{z}}^{*}={\bf{x}}^{*}.

Theorem 4.7
.

Under the assumption of Lemma
4.4
, for any initial vector
{\bf{z}}_{0}\in\mathbb{I}\cap\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
, there exists
\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}\subseteq\mathbb{I}\cap\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
with a small enough
\hat{\delta}
such that
{\bf{y}}_{k},~{\bf{z}}_{k}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
(
k\geq 1
), and

||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{k})||\leq\tau\overline{c}^{k}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||,

(4.3)

where
{\bf{y}}_{k}
and
{\bf{z}}_{k}
are generated by the RTSMRAA,
\forall\overline{c}\in(c,1)
and
\tau=\frac{1+c}{1-c}
.

Proof
:
We can always choose the
{\bf{z}}_{0}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
with a sufficiently small
\hat{\delta}
such that
\hat{\delta}<2(1-c)/\gamma
,

\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\tau(2c+\gamma\hat{\delta})}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\leq\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\tau(2c+\gamma\hat{\delta})(1+c)}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m}||{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{0}||\leq\hat{\delta}

(4.4)

and

\frac{\frac{c}{\overline{c}}+\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m-1}}{1-\frac{\hat{\delta}\gamma}{2(1-c)}}\leq 1.

(4.5)

For any initial vector
{\bf{z}}_{0}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}\subseteq\mathbb{I}\cap\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
with a sufficiently small
\hat{\delta}
such that (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) hold, by Lemma
4.4
and the proof of Proposition
4.5
, we know that there exists
\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}\subseteq\mathbb{I}\cap\mathbb{U}_{\delta}
such that
{\bf{z}}_{1},~{\bf{y}}_{1},~{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_{1},~{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{2}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
and the inequality (
4.3
) holds. Assume that
{\bf{y}}_{k},~{\bf{z}}_{k}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
and
||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{k})||\leq\tau\overline{c}^{k}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||
for any
0\leq k\leq N
. Now we will prove
{\bf{z}}_{N+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
and the inequality
||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})||\leq\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||
by the induction. By Lemma
4.6
, we have

f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{k})=\nabla f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*}){{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{k}}+{\varOmega}_{k},

(4.6)

and
||{\varOmega_{k}}||\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}||{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{k}}||^{2}
.
Therefore, it is easy to check that

g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{k})={\bf{z}}^{*}+\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{k}+\varOmega_{k}.

(4.7)

Firstly, we will prove (
4.3
) and
{\bf{y}}_{N+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
from the following two cases.

Case I
: If
{\bf{y}}_{N+1}\leq 0
or
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{k}}|\alpha_{i}^{(k)}|>\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, by Lemma
4.4
, Lemma
4.6
and the hypothesis, we imply
{\bf{z}}_{N+1}=\boldsymbol{\mu}_{N+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
and

\displaystyle||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})||

\displaystyle\leq(1+c)||{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N+1}||

\displaystyle\leq(1+c)||g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})-g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})||

\displaystyle\leq c(1+c)||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N}||

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c(1+c)}{1-c}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})||

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1+c}{1-c}\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||=\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||.

This gives the inequality (
4.3
).

Case II
: If
{\bf{y}}_{N+1}\geq 0
and
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{k}}|\alpha_{i}^{(k)}|\leq\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
,
note that the fact
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}=1
combing with (
4.7
) gives

{\bf{y}}_{N+1}={\bf{z}}^{*}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}.

(4.8)

4.6
, we can obtain

||\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}|\alpha_{i}^{(N)}|||\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\leq\gamma\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}|\alpha_{i}^{(N)}|||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}||^{2}/2.

(4.9)

Since
m_{N}=\min\{m,N\}
, we get

. Thus, by Lemma
4.6
and the assumption, it is not difficult to get that

\displaystyle||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}||\leq\frac{1}{1-c}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N-m_{N}+i})||\leq\frac{\tau}{1-c}\overline{c}^{N-m_{N}+i}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\leq\frac{\tau}{1-c}\overline{c}^{-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||

(4.10)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}||^{2}&\leq\frac{1}{1-c}||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}||||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N-m_{N}+i})||\\
&\leq\frac{\hat{\delta}\tau}{1-c}\overline{c}^{N-m_{N}+i}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\\
&\leq\frac{\hat{\delta}\tau}{1-c}\overline{c}^{N-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||,\end{split}

(4.11)

together with
\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}|\alpha_{i}^{(N)}|\leq\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}
, the inequalities (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we imply that

||\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\leq\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\gamma\hat{\delta}\tau}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{N-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\leq\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\gamma\hat{\delta}\tau}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||

(4.12)

and

||\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}||\leq\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\tau c}{1-c}\overline{c}^{-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||.

(4.13)

From (
4.8
), we obtain

{\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N+1}}=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N-m_{N}+i}+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i},

combing with (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and the condition (
4.4
) yields

\displaystyle||\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N+1}||\leq\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\tau(2c+\gamma\hat{\delta})}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\leq\hat{\delta},

(4.14)

and hence
{\bf{y}}_{N+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
. Because
{\boldsymbol{\mu}_{N+1}}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}
by Lemma
4.4
, it is found that
{\bf{z}}_{N+1}\in\mathbb{U}_{\hat{\delta}}.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.6
, we can give the Taylor’s formula of
f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}})
at the true solution
{\bf{z}}^{*}
as follows.

f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})=(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I){\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N+1}}+\varOmega_{N+1},

(4.15)

with
||\varOmega_{N+1}||\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}||\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N+1}||^{2}
.
This combing with (
4.8
) and the fact
\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)=(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})=&(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)\big(\theta_{k}\hat{\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N+1}+(1-\theta_{k})(g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})-{\bf{z}}^{*})\big)+\varOmega_{N+1}\\
=&\theta_{k}\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I){\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N-m_{N}+i}+\theta_{k}(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}\\
&+(1-\theta_{k})(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)(g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})-{\bf{z}}^{*})+\varOmega_{N+1}.\end{split}

(4.16)

By (
4.9
)-(
4.12
), (
4.15
) the inductive assumption and the fact
||\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N-m_{N}+i})||\leq||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})||

\displaystyle\begin{split}&||\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I){\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}_{N-m_{N}+i}+(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\\

=&||\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N-m_{N}+i})-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\\
\leq&c||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})||+||\sum\limits_{i=0}^{m_{N}}\alpha_{i}^{(N)}\varOmega_{N-m_{N}+i}||\\
\leq&(\frac{c}{\overline{c}}+\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m-1})\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\end{split}

(4.17)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}||(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)(g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})-{\bf{z}}^{*})||=&||(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})+(\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})-I)\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{N}||\\
\leq&||\nabla g_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}^{*})f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N})||+||\varOmega_{N}||\\
\leq&(\frac{c}{\overline{c}}+\frac{\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m-1})\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||.\end{split}

(4.18)

Note that
||\varOmega_{N+1}||\leq\frac{\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})||
. By (
4.16
)-(
4.18
), the hypothesis (
4.5
) and the fact
\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\geq 1
, it is not difficult to get that

\displaystyle\begin{split}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{N+1})||&\leq\theta_{k}\frac{\frac{c}{\overline{c}}+\frac{\kappa_{\boldsymbol{\alpha}}\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m-1}}{1-\frac{\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}}\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||+(1-\theta_{k})\frac{\frac{c}{\overline{c}}+\frac{\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}\overline{c}^{-m-1}}{1-\frac{\gamma\hat{\delta}}{2(1-c)}}\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||\\
&\leq\tau\overline{c}^{N+1}||f_{\mathcal{E}}({\bf{z}}_{0})||.\end{split}

(4.19)

The proof is completed.

6
Conclusion

The main contribution of this paper is aimed at proposing the restarted nonnegativity preserving tensor splitting methods by the relaxed Anderson acceleration for solving the multilinear systems with a strong
\mathcal{M}
-tensor and the local convergence analysis is also given. From the numerical experiments, it is seen that the proposed methods with a suitable parameter perform better than the tensor splitting methods without acceleration. Besides, the increasing value of
m
does not improve the given algorithm better, and the proposed algorithm is valid just for a small value of
m.
However, how to find an optimal parameter
\theta_{k}
is still an open problem, which is one of our future works.

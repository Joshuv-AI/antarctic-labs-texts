Wave-Medium Interactions in Dynamic Matter and Modulation Systems
Zhiyu Li, Xikui Ma, Zoé-Lise Deck-Léger, Amir Bahrami, Christophe Caloz

Abstract
Space-time modulation systems have recently garnered significant attention due to their resemblance to moving-matter systems, unique properties and promising applications. Unlike conventional moving-matter systems, modulation systems do not involve any net motion of matter, and are therefore easier to implement and capable to attain relativistic and superluminal velocities. However, the fundamental wave-medium interaction aspects in such media, such as scattering and energy-momentum relations, have been essentially unexplored to date. In this paper, we fill this gap, considering three dynamic systems: moving-matter blocs, moving-perturbation interfaces and moving-perturbation truncated periodic structures, and provide corresponding general formulations along with comparisons. Our investigation reveals significant roles played by the “pushing” and “pulling” effects of the moving interface onto the wave in such systems. Moreover, it describes different energy and momentum transfers between moving media and homogenized moving-perturbation structures that result from conventional and reverse Fresnel-Fizeau drag effects.

VI
Conclusion

This work has filled a gap in understanding wave-medium interactions in dynamic systems.
Deriving general solutions and analyzing energy-momentum relations, we have found that the interface velocity and the refractive index contrast of the media are two important factors influencing interactions. Specifically, comparing the different dynamic systems, we discovered that the Fresnel-Fizeau drag accounts for the variations in interactions across the systems.
These may greatly help development practical implementations of
modulation
systems.

Appendix A
Time Averages for Harmonic Dynamic Systems

In
stationary
harmonic systems, time averages can be employed when the observation time scale significantly exceeds the wave’s period. This approach simplifies the analysis of energy and momentum relations by averaging out rapid oscillations
[
62
]
. However, in
dynamic
harmonic systems, where the wave frequency is not conserved, time-averaging becomes more challenging. Nonetheless, we will demonstrate here that it remains feasible under certain specific conditions.

Assume the time-harmonic electric field function

E_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}=A_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}\cos(\omega_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}),

(12)

where
A_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}
,
\omega_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}
and
\phi_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}
are the amplitude, frequency and initial phase of the incident, reflected and transmitted fields, respectively. Next consider, for instance, the subluminal regime in the general formula (
7a
). Substituting Eq. (
12
) into Eq. (
7a
) under the subluminal assumption, and averaging the resulting time integral over the interaction duration,
\Delta t=t_{2}-t_{1}
, yields the exact time-averaged power transfer density

\begin{split}\langle p_{\mathrm{s}}\rangle=&\frac{A_{\mathrm{t}}^{2}}{\eta_{2}}\Bigg(1-\frac{n_{2+}v}{c}\Bigg)\frac{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\cos^{2}(\omega_{\mathrm{t}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{t}})~\mathrm{d}t}{\Delta t}\\
&+\frac{A_{\mathrm{r}}^{2}}{\eta_{1}}\Bigg(1+\frac{n_{1}v}{c}\Bigg)\frac{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\cos^{2}(\omega_{\mathrm{r}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{r}})~\mathrm{d}t}{\Delta t}\\
&-\frac{A_{\mathrm{i}}^{2}}{\eta_{1}}\Bigg(1-\frac{n_{1}v}{c}\Bigg)\frac{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\cos^{2}(\omega_{\mathrm{i}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{i}})~\mathrm{d}t}{\Delta t},\end{split}

(13)

where the relations between the wave amplitudes
A_{\mathrm{i,r,t}}
and the exact expression for the refractive index of the second medium in the
+z
direction,
n_{2+}
, are given by Eqs. (
20
) and (
18
) in Appendix
B
. Note that all the expressions outside of the integrands in Eq. (
13
) are time independent.

Applying the trigonometric identity
\cos^{2}\theta=(1+\cos 2\theta)/2
to the time-averaged integral terms in Eq. (
13
) and solving the resulting integrals with the relation
T_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}=2\pi/\omega_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}
, we find

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}&\cos^{2}(\omega_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}})~\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\cos(2\omega_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}t+2\phi_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}})~\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sin(2\omega_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}t+2\phi_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}})|_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}}{8\pi}\frac{T_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}}{\Delta t}.\end{split}

(14)

If
\Delta t
is much larger than the wave periods
T_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}
, viz.,
\Delta t\gg T_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}
, the second term on the right-hand side of the last equality in Eq. (
14
) is negligible compared to
1/2
.
Equation (
14
) reduces then to

\frac{1}{\Delta t}\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\cos^{2}(\omega_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}t+\phi_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}})~\mathrm{d}t\overset{\Delta t\gg T_{\mathrm{i},\mathrm{r},\mathrm{t}}}{\sim}\frac{1}{2}.

(15)

Substituting Eq. (
15
) into Eq. (
13
) yields Eqs. (
8a
). Thus, time averaging remains valid in dynamic harmonic systems, with changed wave periods, under the condition of large interaction duration versus the largest wave period. The same procedure and conclusion apply to the other velocity regimes.

) in the subluminal regime, where the interfaces are purely spatial and the frequencies are conserved (

\omega^{\pm\prime}=\gamma(\omega^{\pm}\mp v_{\mathrm{f}}k^{\pm})

(17a)

and the superluminal relations

\frac{\omega_{\zeta}}{\omega_{\mathrm{i}}}=\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{1+n_{2-}v/c}\quad\textrm{and}\quad\frac{\omega_{\xi}}{\omega_{\mathrm{i}}}=\frac{1-n_{1}v/c}{1-n_{2+}v/c}.

(17b)

In these relations, the (effective) refractive indices of the second medium in the
\pm z

[
6
]

n_{2\pm}=c(\sqrt{\epsilon_{2\|}\mu_{2\|}}\pm\xi_{2}),

(18)

with the parameters in Eqs. (
16a
) for the M system, in Eqs. (
16b
) for the I system and in Eqs. (
16d
) and (
16e
) for the H system.

[

,
60

\mathbf{E}_{1\|}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{1\|}=\mathbf{E}_{2\|}+\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{B}_{2\|}

(19a)

\mathbf{H}_{1\|}-\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{D}_{1\|}=\mathbf{H}_{2\|}-\mathbf{v}\times\mathbf{D}_{2\|}.

(19b)

Eliminating all the field quantities, we obtain the subluminal reflection coefficient
r=A_{\mathrm{r}}/A_{\mathrm{i}}
and transmission coefficient
\tau=A_{\mathrm{t}}/A_{\mathrm{i}}

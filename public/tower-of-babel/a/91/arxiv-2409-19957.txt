1.
Introduction

A
smooth family
of compact complex manifolds will mean a proper holomorphic submersion. Let

\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta

be such a family over the unit disc, and write
{\cal X}_{t}=\pi^{-1}(t)
. Throughout the paper, the fibers are assumed to have complex dimension
n
. Suppose that
{\cal X}_{t}
is Kähler for every
t\in\Delta^{*}:=\Delta\setminus\{0\}
. A natural question is whether the central fiber
{\cal X}_{0}
must also be Kähler. This problem has been studied since the early development of deformation theory in complex geometry.

In complex dimension two, the answer is affirmative by Kodaira’s classification of compact complex surfaces together with Siu’s theorem
[
25
]
; see also
[
7
,
17
]
. In higher dimensions, however, Kählerness need not be preserved under specialization. Hironaka constructed a smooth deformation
[
16
]
whose general fibers are Kähler whereas the central fiber is not. Nevertheless, Hironaka’s central fiber is bimeromorphic to a Kähler manifold. Recall that a compact complex manifold belongs to
Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
if it is bimeromorphic to a compact Kähler manifold. This motivates the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.1
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. If
{\cal X}_{t}
is Kähler for every
t\in\Delta^{*}
, then the central fiber
{\cal X}_{0}
belongs to Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
.

More generally, one may ask which geometric properties are preserved under holomorphic specialization. For instance, if all fibers over
\Delta^{*}
are projective, Moishezon, Kähler, or in Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
, must the central fiber have the corresponding property? Recall that a compact complex manifold is
Moishezon
if it is bimeromorphic to a projective manifold. These questions are closely related, and we briefly recall several results relevant to the present work.

Popovici
[
21
]
proved that if every fiber
{\cal X}_{t}
,
t\in\Delta^{*}
, is projective and the central fiber
{\cal X}_{0}
satisfies either of the following conditions:

(1)

the Hodge number
h^{0,1}({\cal X}_{t})
is deformation invariant near
0
;

(2)

{\cal X}_{0}
admits a
strongly Gauduchon metric

\omega
, namely a Hermitian metric for which
\partial\omega^{n-1}
is
\bar{\partial}
-exact,

then
{\cal X}_{0}
is Moishezon. Barlet
[
2
]
obtained an analogous result when the punctured fibers are Moishezon. Rao and Tsai
[
23
]
subsequently showed that if a family contains uncountably many Moishezon fibers, then every fiber satisfying either of the above conditions is Moishezon. In forthcoming work of Rao
[
22
]
, he proves that the auxiliary conditions can be removed when the family contains uncountably many Moishezon fibers. For the general fibers are Kähler manifolds, the author and Liu
[
18
]
proved that the central fiber is also Kähler under some mild conditions of metric and cohomology on the central fiber.

Our first main result addresses Conjecture
1.1
by constructing a positive current of positive volume on the central fiber.

Theorem 1.2

(cf. Theorem
4.4
)
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Assume that
{\cal X}_{t}
is Kähler for every
t\in\Delta^{*}
. If
\pi
is Kähler at some point
t_{0}\in\Delta^{*}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
, then there exists a positive
d
-closed
(1,1)
-current
T
on
{\cal X}_{0}
such that

\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{T\})>0.

This result reduces Conjecture
1.1
, under the additional hypothesis that
\pi
is Kähler at one point of the punctured disc, to the following conjecture proposed by Boucksom
[
5
]
, often referred to as the Boucksom–Demailly–Păun conjecture.

Conjecture 1.3
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold carrying a positive
d
-closed
(1,1)
-current
T
such that
\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{T\})>0
. Then
X
belongs to Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
.

Boucksom–Guedj–Lu
[
6
, Theorem C]
established this criterion under the additional assumption that the upper volume
\overline{\text{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{X})
is finite for a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
on
X
. The upper volume, defined in (
2.1
), was introduced and studied by Guedj–Lu
[
15
]
; its finiteness is independent of the choice of Hermitian metric by
[
15
, Theorem A]
. Whether the upper volume is finite on every compact complex manifold remains an important open problem. To the best of our knowledge, no compact complex manifold with infinite upper volume is currently known.

Combining Theorem
1.2
with the criterion of Boucksom–Guedj–Lu yields the following consequence.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. Assume that
{\cal X}_{t}
is Kähler for every
t\in\Delta^{*}
. Suppose moreover that

•

\pi
is Kähler at some point
t_{0}\in\Delta^{*}
in the sense of Definition
3.1
;

•

the central fiber
{\cal X}_{0}
admits a Hermitian metric
\omega_{{\cal X}_{0}}
such that
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{{\cal X}_{0}})<\infty
.

Then
{\cal X}_{0}
belongs to Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
.

Our second main result treats the projective case without any auxiliary hypothesis on the central fiber.

Theorem 1.5

(cf. Theorem
5.3
)
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds. If
{\cal X}_{t}
is projective for every
t\in\Delta^{*}
, then the central fiber
{\cal X}_{0}
is Moishezon.

Thus Conjecture
1.1
holds, in particular, when all punctured fibers are projective.

We briefly describe the strategy of the paper. We first review the volume theory of pseudo-effective Bott–Chern classes and compare the volume of a
d
-closed class in the sense of Boucksom–Guedj–Lu with the volume defined using the absolutely continuous parts of positive currents. We then study relative Kähler forms. Using the deformation theorem for Kähler cones of Demailly–Păun
[
12
, Theorem 0.9]
, we construct a class
\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{R})
and an open dense subset
U\subset\Delta^{*}
such that
\alpha_{t}
is Kähler for
t\in U
and of type
(1,1)
for every
t\neq 0
. Since the class need not remain of type
(1,1)
on the central fiber, we construct
d
-closed
(1,1)
representatives on the punctured fibers with at most a finite-order pole in the parameter. We then normalize the Kähler representatives by the Calabi–Yau theorem. A uniform Gauduchon mass estimate, together with Boucksom’s semicontinuity theorem for the absolutely continuous parts of positive currents
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
, produces a positive
d
-closed
(1,1)
-current
T
on
{\cal X}_{0}
with
\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{T\})>0
. Finally, in the projective case, we combine a persistent integral Hodge class with elliptic compactness to obtain a big line bundle on the central fiber.

2.
Preliminaries on cohomology and volumes

We collect the cohomological and pluripotential-theoretic preliminaries used throughout the paper.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold endowed with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
.

2.1.
Bott–Chern and Aeppli cohomology

For
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, let
{\cal E}^{p,q}(X)
denote the Fréchet space of smooth
(p,q)
-forms on
X
, and set
{\cal E}(X)={\cal E}^{0,0}(X)
. These spaces form the double complex
({\cal E}^{\bullet,\bullet}(X),\partial,\overline{\partial})
, with
d=\partial+\overline{\partial}
.
The
Bott–Chern cohomology
of
X
is the bigraded algebra

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}\partial\cap\mathrm{Ker}\overline{\partial}}{\mathrm{Im}\partial\overline{\partial}}.

Denote by
H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})\subset H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
the subset of classes represented by real
(1,1)
-forms. Associated with a Hermitian metric is the Bott–Chern Laplacian

\Delta_{BC}:=(\partial\overline{\partial})(\partial\overline{\partial})^{*}+(\partial\overline{\partial})^{*}(\partial\overline{\partial})+(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)^{*}+(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)^{*}(\overline{\partial}^{*}\partial)+\overline{\partial}^{*}\overline{\partial}+\partial^{*}\partial,

which is self-adjoint and elliptic. We shall use the following Hodge decomposition.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
1
, Theorem 2.3]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold endowed
with a Hermitian metric. Then there exists an orthogonal decomposition

{\cal E}^{p,q}(X)=\mathrm{Ker}\Delta_{BC}\oplus\mathrm{Im}\partial\overline{\partial}\oplus(\mathrm{Im}\partial^{*}+\mathrm{Im}\overline{\partial}^{*}),

and an isomorphism

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{Ker}\Delta_{BC}.

The
Aeppli cohomology
of
X
is the bigraded
H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})
-module

H^{\bullet,\bullet}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C}):=\frac{\mathrm{Ker}\partial\overline{\partial}}{\mathrm{Im}\partial+\mathrm{Im}\overline{\partial}}.

Bott–Chern and Aeppli cohomology are paired by integration as follows.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
24
, Lemma 2.5]
)
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold. For any
(p,q)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, the wedge product induces a nondegenerate bilinear pairing

\begin{CD}H^{p,q}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{C})\times H^{n-p,n-q}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{\wedge}}>{}>H^{n,n}_{A}(X,\mathbb{C})@>{{}^{\int_{X}}}>{}>\mathbb{C},\end{CD}

\begin{CD}(\alpha,\beta)@>{}>{}>\int_{X}\alpha\wedge\beta.\end{CD}

2.2.
Volumes of pseudo-effective classes

A function
\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is called
quasi-plurisubharmonic
(quasi-psh) if it is locally the sum of a plurisubharmonic function and a smooth function.

Definition 2.3
.

For a smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
, let
PSH_{\theta}(X)
denote the set of
\theta
-psh functions that are not identically
-\infty
; explicitly,

PSH_{\theta}(X):=\{\varphi:X\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}\mid\varphi\text{ is quasi-psh and }dd^{c}\varphi\geq-\theta\text{ locally}\}.

When
\theta=\omega_{X}>0
is a Hermitian form, define

(2.1)

\overline{\text{vol}}_{j}(\omega_{X})=\sup\left\{\int_{X}(\omega_{X}+dd^{c}\varphi)^{j}\wedge\omega_{X}^{n-j}\;\middle|\;\varphi\in PSH_{\omega_{X}}(X)\cap{\cal E}(X)\right\}.

For further details on these quantities, see
[
15
, Section 3]
.

For every
B>0
, multiplication by
B
identifies
PSH_{\omega_{X}}(X)
with
PSH_{B\omega_{X}}(X)
. Consequently,

\overline{\text{vol}}_{n}(B\omega_{X})=B^{n}\overline{\text{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{X}).

A current
T
on
X
is called
quasi-closed
if
dT
is smooth. By
[
6
, Theorem 2.2]
, the non-pluripolar product
\langle T^{n}\rangle
is well defined for every quasi-closed current
T
.

Define the Bott–Chern space by

\BC^{1,1}(X):=\frac{{\cal E}^{1,1}(X)}{\{dd^{c}{\cal E}(X)\}}.

This is a Hausdorff Fréchet space, and
[
6
, Section 1.1]
gives

\BC^{1,1}(X)\cong\frac{\{T\,\text{quasi-closed}\,\mbox{$(1,1)$-{current}}\}}{\{dd^{c}U|U\,\text{distribution}\}}.

For a quasi-closed current
T
, we denote its
dd^{c}
-class by
\{T\}\in\BC^{1,1}(X)
. A real
(1,1)
-current
T
is called
quasi-positive
if
T\geq\gamma
for some smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\gamma
. Thus
T
is both quasi-positive and quasi-closed if and only if
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
for some smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\theta
and some quasi-psh function
\varphi
.

Let
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
and
T^{\prime}=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi^{\prime}
be quasi-positive and quasi-closed currents. We say that
T
is
more singular
than
T^{\prime}
if
\varphi\leq\varphi^{\prime}+O(1)
, and that
T
and
T^{\prime}

have equivalent singularities
if
\varphi=\varphi^{\prime}+O(1)
. The current
T
is said to
have analytic singularities
if the quasi-psh function
\varphi
has analytic singularities.

Definition 2.4

(
[
6
, Definition 1.14]
)
.

Let
\theta
be a smooth real
(1,1)
-form on
X
. A class
\{\theta\}\in\BC^{1,1}(X)
is called

•

pseudo-effective
(abbreviated psef) if it contains a positive
(1,1)
-current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
;

•

big
if it contains a current
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq\epsilon\omega_{X}
for some
\epsilon>0
.

Definition 2.5

(
[
6
, Definition 3.1]
)
.

The
lower volume
and
upper volume
of a quasi-closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
are defined by

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(T):=\inf_{S}\langle S^{n}\rangle,\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(T):=\sup_{S}\langle S^{n}\rangle,

where
S
ranges over all quasi-closed positive
(1,1)
-currents in the same
dd^{c}
-class as
T
and with equivalent singularities.

Definition 2.6

(
[
6
, Definition 3.8]
)
.

For a big
dd^{c}
-class
\{\theta\}\in\BC^{1,1}(X)
, its
lower volume
and
upper volume
are defined by

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}):=\sup_{0\leq T\in\{\theta\}}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(T),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}):=\sup_{0\leq T\in\{\theta\}}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(T),

where
T
ranges over all positive currents in
\{\theta\}
.

These definitions extend to pseudo-effective classes by approximation:

Definition 2.7

(
[
6
, Definition 3.13]
)
.

If
\{\theta\}\in\BC^{1,1}(X)
is pseudo-effective, define

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta+\epsilon\omega_{X}\}),\quad\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}):=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta+\epsilon\omega_{X}\}).

For
d
-closed pseudo-effective classes, Boucksom–Guedj–Lu proved that the lower and upper volumes coincide.

Proposition 2.8

(
[
6
, Proposition 3.16]
)
.

If
\theta
is a smooth
d
-closed
(1,1)
-form and
\{\theta\}
is pseudo-effective, then

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\})=\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}).

Accordingly, for a
d
-closed pseudo-effective class
\{\theta\}
we write simply
\text{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\})
for this common value. Let
{\cal E}\subset H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
denote the pseudo-effective cone. Extending the volume by zero outside
{\cal E}
, one obtains the following continuity theorem.

Theorem 2.9

(
[
6
, Theorem 3.20]
)
.

The volume function
\mathrm{vol}_{n}:\allowbreak H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous. In
particular, it vanishes outside of the big cone.

We shall also use the following criterion for membership in Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
.

Theorem 2.10

(
[
6
, Theorem C]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact complex manifold endowed with a Hermitian metric
\omega_{X}
. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

•

X
belongs to Fujiki’s class
\mathcal{C}
.

•

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{X})<\infty
and there exists a
d
-closed positive
(1,1)
-current
T
with

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{T\})>0
.

For a quasi-positive
(1,1)
-current
T
on
X
, write its Lebesgue decomposition as
T=T_{ac}+T_{sg}
, where
T_{ac}
denotes the absolutely continuous part. If
T\geq\gamma
for a smooth real form
\gamma
, then
T_{ac}\geq\gamma
almost everywhere. The absolutely continuous part
T_{ac}
may be viewed as a
(1,1)
-form with
L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}
coefficients, and
T_{ac}^{m}
is therefore defined pointwise almost everywhere for
0\leq m\leq n
. Following
[
5
, Definition 1.3]
, define

\widetilde{\text{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}):=\sup_{0\leq T\in\{\theta\}}\int_{X}T_{ac}^{n}.

To compare
\text{vol}_{n}
with
\widetilde{\text{vol}}_{n}
for
d
-closed pseudo-effective classes, we use the following regularization theorem of Demailly and Boucksom.

Theorem 2.11

(
[
11
, Proposition 3.7]
,
[
5
, Theorem 2.4]
)
.

Let
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi
be a
d
-closed positive
(1,1)
-current on a compact Hermitian manifold
(X,\omega_{X})
, where
\theta
is a smooth real
(1,1)
-form and
\varphi
is quasi-psh. Assume that
T\geq\gamma
for a smooth real
(1,1)
-form
\gamma
. Then there exists a sequence of functions
\varphi_{k}
with analytic singularities converging to
\varphi
such that, with
T_{k}:=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{k}
, the following hold:

•

T_{k}\to T
weakly;

•

T_{k}\geq\gamma-\epsilon_{k}\omega_{X}
, where
\epsilon_{k}>0
and
\epsilon_{k}\to 0
;

•

T_{k,ac}\to T_{ac}
almost everywhere on
X

The next proposition identifies these two notions of volume in the setting needed below.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
X
be an
n
-dimensional compact complex manifold with a Hermitian metric

\omega_{X}
satisfying
\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{X})<\infty
. Let

\theta
be a
d
-closed smooth real
(1,1)
-form and assume that
\{\theta\}\in H^{1,1}_{BC}(X,\mathbb{R})
is pseudo-effective. Then

\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\})=\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\}).

Proof.

We prove the two inequalities separately. The finiteness assumption ensures that all volumes below are finite; this also follows from the estimates established in the first part of the argument.

Let
T=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi\geq 0
be an arbitrary
d
-closed positive current in

\{\theta\}
. Apply Theorem
2.11
and write

T_{k}=\theta+dd^{c}\varphi_{k},\qquad T_{k}\geq-\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X},\qquad\varepsilon_{k}\downarrow 0,

with
T_{k,ac}\to T_{ac}
almost everywhere. Put

S_{k}=T_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X}\geq 0
. Resolving the analytic singularities of

T_{k}
, we obtain a modification
\rho_{k}:Y_{k}\to X
such that

\rho_{k}^{*}T_{k}=\beta_{k}+[E_{k}],

where
\beta_{k}
is smooth and
E_{k}
is an effective divisor. Hence

(\rho_{k}^{*}S_{k})_{ac}=\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\rho_{k}^{*}\omega_{X}\geq 0

and, since the exceptional and polar sets have Lebesgue measure zero,

(2.2)

\int_{X}(T_{k,ac}+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X})^{n}=\int_{Y_{k}}(\beta_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\rho_{k}^{*}\omega_{X})^{n}.

The invariance of the upper volume under modifications and addition of divisors, together with the definition for smooth positive forms, gives

(2.3)

\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X}\})\geq\int_{X}(T_{k,ac}+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X})^{n};

see
[
6
, Lemma 3.5 and Definition 3.8]
. Since the forms on the right-hand
side are semipositive and converge almost everywhere to
T_{ac}
, Fatou’s lemma
yields

\int_{X}T_{ac}^{n}\leq\liminf_{k\to\infty}\int_{X}(T_{k,ac}+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X})^{n}.

By Definition 3.13 of
[
6
]
and Proposition
2.8
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\overline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta+\varepsilon_{k}\omega_{X}\})=\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\}).

Combining this with (
2.3
), we obtain

\int_{X}T_{ac}^{n}\leq\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\}).

Taking the supremum over all
T\geq 0
in
\{\theta\}
gives

(2.4)

\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\})\leq\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\}).

If the right-hand side vanishes, the desired equality follows immediately.

Assume now that
\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\})>0
. By
Theorem
2.9
, the class
\{\theta\}
is big. Fix
\delta>0
.
Proposition 3.12 of
[
6
]
gives a modification
\rho:Y\to X
and a
decomposition

(2.5)

\{\rho^{*}\theta\}=\{\omega_{Y}\}+\{E\},

where
\omega_{Y}>0
is smooth,
E
is an effective real divisor, and

(2.6)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{Y})>\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\})-\delta.

We claim that
\omega_{Y}
is
d
-closed. Indeed, equality
(
2.5
) means that

\rho^{*}\theta-\omega_{Y}-[E]=dd^{c}u
for a distribution
u
; applying
d
and
using
d\theta=d[E]=0
gives
d\omega_{Y}=0
. Thus
\omega_{Y}
is a Kähler
form. Proposition 3.16 of
[
6
]
therefore gives

(2.7)

\underline{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\omega_{Y})=\int_{Y}\omega_{Y}^{n}.

Set

R:=\rho_{*}(\omega_{Y}+[E]).

Then
R
is a
d
-closed positive
(1,1)
-current on
X
. Pushing forward
(
2.5
) and using
\rho_{*}\rho^{*}\theta=\theta
for a
modification shows that
\{R\}=\{\theta\}
. Since

(\omega_{Y}+[E])_{ac}=\omega_{Y}
, Boucksom’s
push-forward formula for absolutely continuous parts

[
5
, Proposition 2.2]
gives

R_{ac}=\rho_{*}\omega_{Y}\qquad\text{almost everywhere}.

A modification is biholomorphic outside analytic subsets of Lebesgue measure
zero. Consequently

(2.8)

\int_{X}R_{ac}^{n}=\int_{Y}\omega_{Y}^{n}.

Equations (
2.6
)–(
2.8
) imply

\widetilde{\mathrm{vol}}_{n}(\{\theta\})\geq\int_{X}R_{ac}^{n}>\mathrm{vol}_{n}(\{\theta\})-\delta.

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
and combining the resulting inequality with (
2.4
) proves the proposition.
∎

3.
Kähler morphisms

We next discuss relative Kähler structures. Even if every fiber of a smooth proper morphism
f:{\cal X}\to B
is Kähler, the total space need not be Kähler when the base is Kähler; see
[
4
, Example 3.9]
. The appropriate relative notion is the following.

Definition 3.1
.

A smooth proper morphism
f:{\cal X}\to B
between complex manifolds is called a
Kähler morphism
if there exists an open cover
\{U_{i}\}
of
{\cal X}
and smooth real-valued functions
\varphi_{i}
on
U_{i}
such that
\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{j}
is the real part of a holomorphic function on
U_{i}\cap U_{j}
, and
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{i}
is positive on
T_{{\cal X}/B}|_{U_{i}}
.

We say that
f
is
Kähler at a point

s\in B
if its restriction over some open neighborhood of
s
is a Kähler morphism.

For a Kähler morphism, the local forms
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{i}
agree on overlaps and hence glue to a global
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-form
\omega
on
{\cal X}
. Its restriction
\omega|_{{\cal X}_{t}}
is a Kähler form on every fiber. We refer to such an
\omega
as a relative Kähler form.

3.1.
Sheaves of pluriharmonic functions

We recall the sheaf-theoretic description of Bott–Chern classes that will be used below; see

Let

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>{\cal O}_{\cal X}@>{\text{-2Im}}>{}>PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}@>{}>{}>0,\end{CD}

The real and complex versions are related by the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>{\cal O}_{{\cal X}}@>{\text{-2Im}}>{}>PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}@>{}>{}>0\\

3.2.
Relative Kähler forms

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\cal X}:={\cal E}_{\cal X}/PH_{{\cal X}}
and
\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}:={\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}/PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}

\Theta\in\Gamma({\cal X},\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})
may be represented by an open cover

and functions
\varphi_{i}\in{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}(U_{i})

\varphi_{i}-\varphi_{j}\in PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}(U_{i}\cap U_{j})

\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{i}
then glue to a
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-form
\omega
defined by

(3.8)

\omega|_{U_{i}}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{i}.

The corresponding characteristic maps fit into the commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\Gamma({\cal X},\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})@>{{}^{\hat{c}_{1}}}>{}>H^{1}({\cal X},PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
\Gamma({\cal X},\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X}})@>{{}^{c_{1}}}>{}>H^{1}({\cal X},PH_{{\cal X}}).\end{CD}

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
f:{\cal X}\to B
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds, where
B\subset\mathbb{C}
is a pseudoconvex domain. Then
f
is a Kähler morphism if and only if there exists a class
\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{R})
such that

\alpha_{t}:=\iota_{t}^{*}\alpha

is a Kähler class on
{\cal X}_{t}
for every
t\in B
, where
\iota_{t}:{\cal X}_{t}\hookrightarrow{\cal X}
denotes the inclusion.

Proof.

Suppose first that
f
is Kähler. By Definition
3.1
, the local forms
\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\varphi_{i}
glue to a global
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-form
\omega
whose restriction to each fiber is Kähler. Thus
\alpha=[\omega]_{\mathrm{dR}}
has the required property.

Conversely, assume that
\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{R})
restricts to a Kähler class on every fiber. We first show that
\alpha
admits a global
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-representative. The short exact sequence (
3.2
), together with the Leray spectral sequence, yields a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\cal X},PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})@>{}>{}>H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{R})@>{{}^{\phi}}>{}>H^{2}({\cal X},{\cal O}_{\cal X})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{{}^{\cong}}V{}V\\
H^{0}(B,R^{2}f_{*}\mathbb{R})@>{{}^{\widetilde{\phi}}}>{}>H^{0}(B,R^{2}f_{*}{\cal O}_{\cal X}).\end{CD}

Since
\alpha_{t}
is Kähler, its image in
H^{2}({\cal X}_{t},{\cal O}_{{\cal X}_{t}})
vanishes for every
t\in B
. Hence
\widetilde{\phi}_{t}(\alpha_{t})=0
for all
t
, and therefore

\phi(\alpha)=0\in H^{2}({\cal X},{\cal O}_{\cal X}).

It follows from (
3.2
) and the fine resolution (
3.3
) that
\alpha
is represented by a
d
-closed real
(1,1)
-form
\Omega
on
{\cal X}
.

We next lift the class of
\Omega
to a global section of
\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}
. Since
f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\simeq PH_{B,\mathbb{R}}
, the exact sequence

(3.9)

\begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathbb{R}@>{}>{}>{\cal O}_{B}@>{\text{-2Im}}>{}>PH_{B,\mathbb{R}}@>{}>{}>0\end{CD}

and its associated long exact sequence apply. The domain
B
is Stein, so Cartan’s Theorem B gives
H^{k}(B,{\cal O}_{B})=0
for
k\geq 1
. Moreover,
B
has the homotopy type of a CW-complex of real dimension at most one
[
20
, p. 39]
; hence
H^{k}(B,\mathbb{R})=0
for
k\geq 2
. Consequently,

H^{k}(B,PH_{B,\mathbb{R}})=0\qquad(k\geq 1).

The Leray spectral sequence therefore gives

H^{1}({\cal X},PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})\simeq\Gamma(B,R^{1}f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}).

From

0\longrightarrow PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow 0

and the softness of
{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}
, we obtain

(3.10)

0\longrightarrow f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow f_{*}{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\\
\longrightarrow R^{1}f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow 0.

Let
\mathcal{M}
denote the image of
f_{*}{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}
in
f_{*}\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}
. Thus

0\longrightarrow f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow f_{*}{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow 0.

Since
f_{*}{\cal E}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}
is soft and
H^{k}(B,PH_{B,\mathbb{R}})=0
for
k\geq 1
, the associated long exact sequence gives
H^{k}(B,\mathcal{M})=0
for
k\geq 1
. Hence the exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow f_{*}\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow R^{1}f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}\longrightarrow 0

yields

(3.11)

\begin{CD}\Gamma(B,f_{*}\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})@>{}>{}>\Gamma(B,R^{1}f_{*}PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}(B,\mathcal{M})=0\\
@V{{}^{\cong}}V{}V@V{{}^{\cong}}V{}V\\
\Gamma({\cal X},\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})@>{}>{}>H^{1}({\cal X},PH_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}}).\end{CD}

Therefore there exists
\Theta\in\Gamma({\cal X},\mathcal{K}_{{\cal X},\mathbb{R}})
whose associated Bott–Chern class is
[\Omega]
.

It remains to arrange positivity along the fibers. Fix
t_{0}\in B
. Since
\alpha_{t_{0}}
is Kähler, there exists a smooth real-valued function
\psi_{t_{0}}
on
{\cal X}_{t_{0}}
such that

\sqrt{-1}\bigl(\partial\overline{\partial}\Theta|_{{\cal X}_{t_{0}}}+\partial\overline{\partial}\psi_{t_{0}}\bigr)

is a Kähler form on
{\cal X}_{t_{0}}
. Choose a sufficiently small ball
V_{t_{0}}\Subset B
centered at
t_{0}
. By smooth local triviality,
\psi_{t_{0}}
extends to a smooth function
\widetilde{\psi}_{t_{0}}
on
f^{-1}(V_{t_{0}})
. After shrinking
V_{t_{0}}
, positivity persists, so

\sqrt{-1}\bigl(\partial\overline{\partial}\Theta+\partial\overline{\partial}\widetilde{\psi}_{t_{0}}\bigr)|_{{\cal X}_{s}}

is Kähler for every
s\in V_{t_{0}}
.

Choose a countable locally finite refinement
\{U_{i}\}
of the resulting cover of
B
, let
\widetilde{\psi}_{i}
be the corresponding local functions, and let
\{\rho_{i}\}
be a partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{i}\}
. Then

\omega:=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}\left(\Theta+\sum_{i}f^{*}\rho_{i}\,\widetilde{\psi}_{i}\right)

is a global relative Kähler form. Thus
f
is a Kähler morphism.
∎

3.3.

We briefly recall the facts about relative cycle spaces and variations of Hodge structure that enter the deformation argument.

Let

There is a natural projection

Proposition 3.4

(
[
18
, Proposition 3.9]
)
.

Let

be a smooth proper morphism whose fibers are Kähler, and let
A
be a connected component of

\mu|_{A}:A\to S
is proper.

We also recall the numerical characterization of the Kähler cone due to Demailly–Păun.

Theorem 3.5

(
[
12
, Main Theorem 0.1]
,
[
10
, Corollary 1.3]

Let

be a compact Kähler manifold. Then its Kähler cone

is a connected component of the set

of real
(1,1)
-classes

for every irreducible analytic subset

We finally fix notation for the Gauss–Manin local system; see

8

The resulting maps fit into the commutative diagram

Proposition 3.6

Let

4.

4.1.
Auxiliary lemmas

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
a
be a smooth real-valued function on a disc, let
b
be a smooth
complex-valued function, and let
\ell\geq 1
be an integer. If

(4.1)

m(t):=a(t)-2\operatorname{Re}\bigl(t^{-\ell}b(t)\bigr)\geq 0\qquad(0<|t|<\varepsilon),

then
b(t)=O(|t|^{\ell})
. In particular,
m
is bounded near
0
.

Proof.

Expand the smooth function
b
to order
\ell-1
in the real variables, expressed in terms of
t
and
\bar{t}
:

(4.2)

b(t)=\sum_{p+q<\ell}c_{pq}t^{p}\bar{t}^{q}+O(|t|^{\ell}).

Suppose that some coefficient of total degree less than
\ell
is nonzero,
and let
d<\ell
be the smallest such degree. Write
t=re^{i\theta}
.
Multiplying by
r^{\ell-d}
and letting
r\downarrow 0
gives

(4.3)

r^{\ell-d}m(re^{i\theta})\longrightarrow H(\theta):=-2\operatorname{Re}\!\sum_{p+q=d}c_{pq}e^{i(p-q-\ell)\theta}.

The left-hand side is nonnegative, hence
H(\theta)\geq 0
for every
\theta
.
On the other hand, every frequency satisfies

p-q-\ell\leq d-\ell<0.

Thus
H
has zero average on the circle:

\int_{0}^{2\pi}H(\theta)\,d\theta=0.

A continuous nonnegative function with zero average must vanish identically,
so
H\equiv 0
.

The complex trigonometric polynomial in (
4.3
) contains only
strictly negative frequencies, and for fixed
p+q=d
these frequencies are
pairwise distinct. If its real part vanishes identically, all those Fourier
coefficients must vanish. This contradicts the choice of
d
.
Consequently, every coefficient with
p+q<\ell
in (
4.2
) is zero.
Therefore
b(t)=O(|t|^{\ell})
, and (
4.1
) gives
m(t)=O(1)
.
∎

5.
The projective case

We begin with two consequences of the variation of Hodge structure and the exponential sequence.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds and
assume that
X_{t}
is projective for every
t\neq 0
. Then there exist an integral
class
\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{Z})
and a point
t_{*}\in\Delta^{*}
such that

(1)

\alpha_{t}:=\iota_{t}^{*}\alpha
is of type
(1,1)
for every
t\neq 0
;

(2)

\alpha_{t_{*}}
is the first Chern class of an ample line bundle on

X_{t_{*}}
.

Proof.

Using the differentiable trivialization (
3.12
), identify the local system
R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{Z}
with the fixed countable lattice

\Lambda=H^{2}(X_{t_{0}},\mathbb{Z})
. For
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let

H_{\lambda}=\{t\in\Delta^{*}:\lambda_{t}\in H^{1,1}(X_{t})\}.

Since every fiber over
\Delta^{*}
is Kähler, the fiberwise Hodge–de Rham
spectral sequence degenerates at
E_{1}
. Upper semicontinuity of the Hodge numbers, together with constancy of the Betti numbers, implies local constancy of all
h^{p,q}
; Grauert’s theorem then shows that the Hodge filtration is by
holomorphic subbundles. The locus
H_{\lambda}
is therefore the zero locus
of the holomorphic projection of the flat section
\lambda
to

(R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathbb{C}\otimes\mathcal{O})/F^{1}
, hence is a closed analytic subset of

\Delta^{*}
.

Let
\Lambda_{\mathrm{amp}}\subset\Lambda
consist of those
\lambda
whose parallel transport is ample at some point of
\Delta^{*}
. Since every punctured
fiber is projective,

\Delta^{*}=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{amp}}}H_{\lambda}.

Since
\Delta^{*}
is a Baire space and
\Lambda_{\mathrm{amp}}
is countable, at least one analytic set
H_{\lambda}
has nonempty interior. Since

\Delta^{*}
is connected, the identity theorem gives

H_{\lambda}=\Delta^{*}
. By the definition of
\Lambda_{\mathrm{amp}}
, the
same
\lambda
is ample at some
t_{*}\in\Delta^{*}
. Finally, under the trivialization (
3.12
),
\lambda
extends to an integral class

\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{Z})
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be as in Lemma
5.1
. If

\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{Z})
satisfies

\alpha_{t}\in H^{1,1}(X_{t})
for every
t\neq 0
, then there is a holomorphic line
bundle
{\cal L}^{*}
on
{\cal X}^{*}:=\pi^{-1}(\Delta^{*})
such that

c_{1}({\cal L}^{*})=\alpha|_{{\cal X}^{*}}.

Proof.

Consider the exponential sequence on
{\cal X}^{*}
,

H^{1}({\cal X}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}^{*}}^{*})\longrightarrow H^{2}({\cal X}^{*},\mathbb{Z})\longrightarrow H^{2}({\cal X}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}^{*}}).

Since
\Delta^{*}
is Stein, Cartan’s Theorem B and the Leray spectral sequence give

H^{2}({\cal X}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}^{*}})\simeq\Gamma(\Delta^{*},R^{2}\pi_{*}\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}^{*}}).

Since the fibers are Kähler on
\Delta^{*}
, cohomology and base change
identify the value of the image of
\alpha
at
t
with the image of

\alpha_{t}
in
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathcal{O}_{X_{t}})
. This vanishes because

\alpha_{t}
is of type
(1,1)
. Hence the image of
\alpha
in
H^{2}({\cal X}^{*},\mathcal{O}_{{\cal X}^{*}})
vanishes. Exactness of the exponential sequence then yields the desired holomorphic line bundle.
∎

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
\pi:{\cal X}\to\Delta
be a smooth family of compact complex manifolds over
the disc
\Delta
. Assume that the fibers
{\cal X}_{t}
are projective
for all
t\in\Delta^{*}
. Then
{\cal X}_{0}
is Moishezon.

Proof.

For
n=1
, the central fiber is a compact Riemann surface and is projective.
Assume
n\geq 2
and fix the differentiable trivialization (
3.12
).

Step 1: a line bundle on the punctured family.

By Lemma
5.1
, choose

\alpha\in H^{2}({\cal X},\mathbb{Z})
and
t_{*}\in\Delta^{*}
such that
\alpha_{t}
is of type

(1,1)
for all
t\neq 0
and
\alpha_{t_{*}}
is ample. By
Lemma
5.2
, there is a line bundle

(5.1)

{\cal L}^{*}\longrightarrow{\cal X}^{*},\qquad c_{1}({\cal L}^{*})=\alpha|_{{\cal X}^{*}}.

Write

Step 2: polynomial section growth on every punctured fiber.

By Serre vanishing, after increasing

Fix

By Grauert’s direct-image theorem,

is coherent. It is torsion-free: a local
section killed by a nonzero holomorphic function on the base vanishes away
from the discrete zero set of that function and hence vanishes identically.
A torsion-free coherent sheaf on the nonsingular curve

is locally
free. By upper semicontinuity, the higher cohomology groups above continue to
vanish in a neighborhood of

is used.

to the image of
\alpha
in

\ell

On the projective fiber

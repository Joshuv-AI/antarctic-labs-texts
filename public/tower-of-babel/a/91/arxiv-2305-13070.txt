Areas associated to a quadrilateral
Oleg Mushkarov, Nikolai Nikolov

Abstract
. We study the relationship between the areas of the consecutive quadrilaterals cut from a convex quadrilateral in the plane by means of a finite or infinite number of straight lines intersecting two of its opposite sides. Moreover, we obtain a geometric description of all possible areas obtained in this way given the ratios of the lengths of consecutive segments the lines divide these two opposite sides.

51M04

\mathcal{A}_{1}=[AA_{1}D_{1}D],\mathcal{A}_{2}=[A_{1}A_{2}D_{2}D_{1}],\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n}=[A_{n-1}BCD_{n-1}]

Fig. 1

and analytic relations between

\mathcal{A}_{2}

Understanding the article requires a basic knowledge of linear

2.
Area attainable points in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

We will first consider the case when
the sides of the quadrilaterals are divided into
3
segments. Let
their ratios be determined by the triples
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})

(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},p_{3}^{\prime})

Definition.
A point
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\mathbb{R}^{3}

ABCD

\mathcal{A}

\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}

with a common initial point
O=(0,0,0)
.

Proposition 1
.

Set

\Delta=(p_{1}+p_{2}+p_{3})p_{1}^{\prime}p_{3}^{\prime}p_{2}-(p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime}+p_{3}^{\prime})p_{1}p_{3}p_{2}^{\prime}.

a) If
\Delta\neq 0,
then
\mathcal{A}
is the union of two
nonintersecting open trihedral angles with a common vertex
O
and
a common face, and an open ray with origin
O,
lying on this face
and different from its edges.

b) If
\Delta=0,
then
\mathcal{A}
is an open angle with vertex

O
in the plane

(1)

\alpha:\ (p_{2}p_{3}^{\prime}-p_{3}p_{2}^{\prime})x_{1}+(p_{3}p_{1}^{\prime}-p_{1}p_{3}^{\prime})x_{2}+(p_{1}p_{2}^{\prime}-p_{2}p_{1}^{\prime})x_{3}=0

when
p_{1}:p_{1}^{\prime}\neq p_{3}:p_{3}^{\prime}
or in the plane

(2)

\alpha:\ \frac{p_{2}+p_{3}}{p_{1}}x_{1}-\frac{p_{1}+2p_{2}+p_{3}}{p_{2}}x_{2}+\frac{p_{1}+p_{2}}{p_{3}}x_{3}=0

when
p_{1}:p_{1}^{\prime}=p_{3}:p_{3}^{\prime}
.

Note that if
\Delta=0
and two of the ratios
p_{1}:p_{1}^{\prime},

p_{2}:p_{2}^{\prime},

p_{3}:p_{3}^{\prime}
are equal, then the third one is also equal
to them.

\vec{v}_{1}=(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}),

\vec{v}_{2}=(p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}^{\prime},p_{3}^{\prime}),

\vec{v}_{0}=\vec{v}_{1}+\vec{v}_{2},

\vec{v}_{3}=(p_{1}p_{1}^{\prime},p_{2}p_{2}^{\prime}+p_{2}p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime}p_{1},p_{3}p_{3}^{\prime}+p_{3}(p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime})+p_{3}^{\prime}(p_{1}+p_{2})),

and denote by

Note first that if

, then

Let now

and denote by

in the

\frac{\mathcal{A}_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{1}+\mathcal{A}_{2}}{\mathcal{A}_{0}}=\frac{(p_{0}+p_{1}+p_{2})(p_{0}^{\prime}+p_{1}^{\prime}+p_{2}^{\prime})}{p_{0}p_{0}^{\prime}},

Area attainable points in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

In this case
\mathcal{A}
is a subset of the

\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
(p_{1},\dots,p_{n})

(p_{1}^{\prime},\dots,p_{n}^{\prime})

If

for

The analog of Proposition

in higher dimensions is the
following

Proposition 4
.

Let

and set

\Delta_{i}=(p_{i-1}+p_{i}+p_{i+1})p_{i-1}^{\prime}p_{i+1}^{\prime}p_{i}-(p_{i-1}^{\prime}+p_{i}^{\prime}+p_{i+1}^{\prime})p_{i-1}p_{i+1}p_{i}^{\prime},\ 2\leq i\leq n-1.

a) If at least one of the numbers

Sketch of proof.

4.
Area attainable points in
\ell^{1}

Now we will consider the case when the sides of the quadrilaterals
are divided into infinitely many segments.

Let
p=(p_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
p^{\prime}=(p_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be two
infinite sequences of positive integers. We will say that an
infinite sequence
x=(x_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of real numbers is area
attainable if there is a quadrilateral
ABCD
in the plane and
points
A_{0}=A,A_{1},A_{2},\dots
and
D_{0}=D,D_{1},D_{2},\dots

and

A_{0}A_{1}:A_{1}A_{2}:\dots=p_{1}:p_{2}:\dots

and

x_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i}=[A_{i-1}A_{i}D_{i}D_{i-1}]
for all

Denote by
\ell^{1}
the space of all sequences

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}|r_{i}|<\infty.
It
is clear that the set
\mathcal{A}
of area attainable points is

or

be given infinite sequences
of positive real numbers and let
\Delta_{i}
(

) be the
numbers defined in Proposition
4
.

\Delta_{i}
is different from

0,
then
\mathcal{A}
is the union of two nonintersecting open
trihedral angles in the same
3
-dimensional vector subspace of

\ell^{1}
with a common vertex

and a common face,
and an open ray with origin
O
lying on this face and different
from its edges.

b) If all numbers
\Delta_{i}
are equal to

then
\mathcal{A}

is an open (
2

\ell^{1}

The proof of this proposition is the same as that of

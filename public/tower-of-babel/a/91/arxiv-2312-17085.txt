A remark on selection of solutions for the transport equation
Jules Pitcho

Abstract
. We prove that for bounded, divergence-free vector fields in L l ​ o ​ c 1 ​ ( ( 0 , + ∞ ) , B ​ V l ​ o ​ c ​ ( ℝ d , ℝ d ) ) L^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty);BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})) regularisation by convolution of the vector field selects a single solution of the transport equation for any locally integrable initial datum. We recall the vector field constructed by Depauw in [ 13 ] , which lies in the above class of vector fields. We show that the transport equation along this vector field has at least two bounded weak solutions for any bounded initial datum.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Cauchy problem

In this note, we study the initial value problem for the continuity equation posed on
[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(IVP)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}_{x}({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\rho)&=0,\\
\rho(0,x)&=\bar{\rho}(x),\end{split}\right.

where
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)
is a given vector field,
\rho=\rho(t,x)
is an unknown real-valued function, and
\operatorname{div}_{x}
is the divergence operator on vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We are interested in weak solutions of (
IVP
).

Definition 1.1
.

Consider a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We shall say
\rho\in L_{loc}^{\infty}([0,+\infty);L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
is a weak solution to (
IVP
) along
b
, if for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+{{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right)\;dxdt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}(x)\phi(0,x)\;dx.

If, additionally
\rho\in L_{loc}^{\infty}([0,+\infty);L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, we shall say that it is a bounded weak solution to (
IVP
) along
b
.

We are interested in selection of weak solutions of (
IVP
) when they are non-unique. Let us recall the classical theory of existence and uniqueness of weak solutions of (
IVP
).

1.2.
The classical theory

Given a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it is convenient to work with its extension by zero to
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which we denote by
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
and define as

(1.1)

\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)\qquad&{\rm if}\quad(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
0\qquad&{\rm if}\quad(t,x)\notin[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{split}\right.

When
b
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
x
with Lipschitz constants on compact sets, which are time integrable, the Cauchy-Lipschitz theorem provides unique global solutions on
\mathbb{R}
to the ODE understood in the sense of distributions

(ODE)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,s,x)&=\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,s,x)),\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(s,s,x)&=x,\end{split}\right.

for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
These solutions are then bundled into a 2-parameter family of maps
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which we will call the flow along
b
, and satisfies the classical stability estimate

|x_{1}-x_{2}|\exp\Big(-\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(u)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}du\Big|\Big)\leq|{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,s,x_{1})-{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,s,x_{2})|\leq|x_{1}-x_{2}|\exp\Big(\Big|\int_{s}^{t}\|\nabla_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(u)\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}du\Big|\Big),

and the group property for every
r,s,t\in\mathbb{R}

{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,s,{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(s,r,\cdot))={\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,r,\cdot).

In this setting, weak solutions
\rho
of (
IVP
) along
b
are uniquely given by the classical formula

(1.2)

\rho(t,\cdot){\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}(t,0,\cdot)_{\#}\bar{\rho}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}.

If the vector field
b
satisfies suitable growth assumptions, then the global existence and uniqueness of the flow
X
is still valid. For simplicity, we choose to restrict ourselves to bounded vector fields in this work. Bounded vector fields to which the classical theory applies will be refered to as smooth vector fields. On the contrary, bounded vector fields to which this theory does not apply will be called non-smooth vector fields.

1.3.
Basic definitions

Our purpose is to study the selection of weak solutions of (
IVP
) defined by the classical theory under regularisation of a non-smooth
b
. A regularisation of
b
is a sequence
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
C^{\infty}([0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\to{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
in
L^{1}_{loc}
. This leads to the following definition.

Definition 1.2
.

Consider a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We shall say that the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
, if the sequence of unique weak solutions
\rho^{k}
of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
converges uniquely in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
k\to+\infty
.

Remark 1.3
.

We can similarly define well-posedness along a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{h})_{h\in I}
, where
I
is not necessarily countable. For simplicity, in this paper we restrict our attention to regularisations indexed by the natural numbers.

For a smooth
b
, the Cauchy problem is well-posed along any regularisation of
b
by using (
1.2
) and the classical stability of the flow
X
under smooth perturbation of
b
.
If we approximate a non-smooth
b
in a stronger topology than
L^{1}_{loc}
, then we can have stronger convergence than in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the solutions along the approximation. This will be a key fact in the proof our main theorem.

For a non-smooth
b
, weak limits of weak solutions of (
IVP
) along a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are expected to be non-unique, and to depend on the choice of the regularisation. A stronger notion of well-posedness is therefore when the weak limit coincides for several regularisations of
b
.
This leads to the following definition.

Definition 1.4
.

Consider a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Consider a family
\mathcal{R}
consisting of regularisations of
b
. We shall say that the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along
\mathcal{R}
, if there exists a unique
\rho\in L_{loc}^{1}([0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for every
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{R}
, the unique weak solutions
\rho^{k}
of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
converge to
\rho
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
k\to+\infty
.

In the study of non-linear wave equations with rough initial data, a similar notion of well-posedness is used (see the reviews
[
22
,
23
]
). In fact, if the initial datum is random and rough, the Cauchy problem can be well-posed with respect to some regularisation but not with respect to another
[
22
,
21
]
.

1.4.
Review of relevant results

Let us review the known existence and uniqueness results on weak solutions of (
IVP
) when
b
is non-smooth.
The work of Ambrosio
[
3
]
following on the work DiPerna-Lions
[
14
]
reads as follows in the context of divergence-free vector fields.

Theorem 1.5
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L_{loc}^{1}([0,+\infty);BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. Then, there exists a unique bounded weak solution of (
IVP
).

The uniqueness part of the above theorem was proved in
[
3
]
assuming only that for almost every
t\in\mathbb{R}
the divergence at time
t
of
b
is absolutely continuous with respect to
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. The proof is based on a commutator estimate showing that any bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) must be renormalised.
By a fine measure-theoretic analysis of Lagrangian representations of solutions of (
IVP
), the hypothesis on the divergence has been further relaxed to
near incompressibility
in the work of Bianchini and Bonicatto
[
4
]
.

In general, when uniqueness of bounded weak solutions holds for (
IVP
), then well-posedness in the sense of Definition
1.2
along any regularisation of
b
is true, up to some mild assumptions on the regularisations ensuring that the solutions of (
IVP
) along the regularised vector fields remain uniformly bounded. More precisely, the following statement may be deduced from
[
3
]
(see also the Appendix for a proof).

Proposition 1.6
.

Consider an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume the hypothesis of Theorem
1.5
on
b
. Consider a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of
b
such that

(1.3)

{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,0,\cdot)_{\#}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}\leq C{\mathscr{L}}^{d},

where the constant
C
is uniform in
k
and on compact time intervals.
Then, the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along the regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
.

It would be interesting to establish how much (
1.3
) can be relaxed, and whether solutions with similar properties as those obtained by convex integration (see
[
17
,
16
,
18
,
7
,
19
,
20
,
8
]
) may be constructed as limit of smooth approximations of (
IVP
) when (
1.3
) fails by too much. These solutions are however “pathological” in that they are not selected by the vanishing diffusivity scheme, as shown by Bonicatto, Ciampa and Crippa in
[
5
]
.

Let us now come to the non-uniqueness results.
First Depauw constructed a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
in
[
13
]
, which is not in
L^{1}_{loc}([0,+\infty);BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}))
, and for which uniqueness of bounded weak solutions to (
IVP
) fails. Ciampa, Crippa and Spirito then constructed in
[
9
]
an unbounded, divergence-free and autonomous vector field, and a family of initial data, for which the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along two different regularisations of
b
, but the two corresponding solutions do not coincide.
By adapting the construction of Depauw, De Lellis and Giri constructed in
[
12
]
a bounded vector field and an initial datum, for which the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along two different regularisations of
b
(in the sense of Definition
1.2
), but the two corresponding solutions do not coincide.

This was then extended by Colombo, Crippa and Sorella in
[
10
]
, where for every
\alpha\in[0,1)
, they construct divergence-free vector fields
b
in
C^{\alpha}([0,2]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, an initial datum
\bar{\rho}
for which the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is not well-posed (in the sense of Definition
1.2
) along a certain regularisation of
b
, which is given by convolution with a standard mollifier. Although not directly related to the present work, we note that for those same vector fields, they show that the vanishing diffusivity regularisation scheme fails to select a single solution. In a recent contribution, Huysmans and Titi
[
15
]
have moreover constructed a bounded vector field for which the vanishing diffusivity scheme selects a solution, which is not entropy-admissible in the sense of Dafermos
[
11
]
.

1.5.
Statement of the theorems

For vector fields for which uniqueness of bounded weak solutions of (
IVP
) may fail, we are interested in well-posedness of the Cauchy problem along a whole regularisation class.
We consider the regularisation class obtained by mollification of the vector field with an arbitrary standard mollifier.
We recall that a function
\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called a standard mollifier if
\theta\geq 0
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\theta(t,x)dxdt=1.

We write for every
k\in\mathbb{N}

\theta^{k}(t,x):=k^{d+1}\theta(kt,kx).

By a slight abuse of notation, the convolution
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k}
then denotes the restriction to
[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
of the convolution
\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k}
, where we recall that
\tilde{{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}}
defined in (
1.1
) is the extension by zero to negative times, and we recall that

(\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k})(t,x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t-s,x-y)\theta^{k}(s,y)dyds.

We define the convolution regularisation class

\mathcal{R}_{conv}:=\Big\{({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\,;\,\theta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),\;\theta\geq 0,\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d+1}}\theta(t,x)dtdx=1\Big\}.

Remark 1.7
.

For a divergence-free vector field
b
and a constant initial datum, the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is automatically well-posed along
\mathcal{R}_{conv}
. Indeed, in this case, for every
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{R}_{conv}
, the constant function is the unique bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly, this sequence of constant functions then converges uniquely to the same constant function.

We have the following well-posedness theorem for this regularisation class.

Theorem 1.8
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L_{loc}^{1}((0,+\infty);{\rm BV_{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Then, the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) is well-posed along the regularisation class
\mathcal{R}_{conv}
.

Remark 1.9
.

Note that well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) along the regularisation class
\mathcal{R}_{conv}
does not in general imply existence of a weak solution of (
IVP
). When
1<p\leq+\infty
, and when the initial datum is in
L^{p}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, weak limit points of weak solutions of (
IVP
) along a regularisation of
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by convolution are necessarly weak solutions of (
IVP
). However, when the initial datum is in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we cannot directly pass into the limit in the weak formulation of (
IVP
) because
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
need not converge strongly to
b
in
L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Remark 1.10
.

We stress that our result holds for a locally integrable initial datum, whereas in Theorem
2.5
due to Ambrosio, the initial datum needs to be essentially bounded. This is because the notion of well-posedness along the regularisation class
\mathcal{R}_{conv}
is weaker than the notion of existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in the functional class
L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,+\infty);L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. It is in fact a strictly weaker notion as the weak solutions constructed by convex integration
[
17
,
16
,
18
,
7
,
19
]
cannot be constructed by regularisation of the vector field by convolution.

It is interesting to note that for non-linear wave equations with random and rough initial data
[
22
,
23
]
, the class of regularisations by convolution also plays an important role, although for different reasons than in this work.

The bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}:[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
constructed by Depauw in
[
13
]
belongs to
L_{loc}^{1}((0,+\infty);BV_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2}))
. The following variation on Depauw’s non-uniqueness result shows that Theorem
1.8
provides a non-trivial selection for bounded initial data, even amongst solutions which have the same sign as the initial datum.

Theorem 1.11
.

Consider an initial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, there exists at least two bounded weak solutions of the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
. Moreover, if
\bar{\rho}
is non-negative and
\bar{\rho}\neq 0
, then there exists at least two non-negative bounded weak solutions of the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
.

Remark 1.12
.

Given an arbitrary bounded initial datum, a bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
always exists by weak compactness. Adding the non-zero solution with zero initial datum given by Depauw constructs another bounded weak solution with the same initial datum. This was pointed out by one of the anonymous referees. However, even if the initial datum is non-negative, the second solution constructed by this procedure need not be non-negative. Some additional work is required to prove that, when the initial datum is non-negative, there exist two non-negative bounded weak solutions.

Remark 1.13
.

It would be interesting to establish a characterising property intrinsic to the weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
selected by Theorem
1.8
. We moreover expect that the weak solutions constructed in Theorem
1.11
are distinct from the weak solution selected by Theorem
1.8
.

1.6.
Outline of ideas

1.6.1.
Ideas for Theorem
1.8

The convergence of weak solutions of (
IVP
) along a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
by convolution will be controlled by the convergence of solutions to a family backwards problem along
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with final datum given by a test function. The convergence of the solutions to the backwards problem will be established in Lemma
3.1
and shown to be
pointwise in time
. In order to get uniform in
k
control of the Jacobian of the flow along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
, we will crucially use that regularisation by convolution preserves the divergence-free structure. This will conclude well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
IVP
) along the regularisation class
\mathcal{R}_{conv}
.

1.6.2.
Ideas for Theorem
1.11

The classical construction of the vector field of Depauw
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
gives two non-unique bounded weak solutions
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
.
We will observe that any initial datum
\bar{\rho}
can be weakly approximated by a sequence
\bar{\rho}_{1}^{k}
localised on
\zeta_{1}(2^{-k-1},\cdot)
and by a sequence
\bar{\rho}_{2}^{k}
localised on
\zeta_{2}(2^{-k-1},\cdot)
. Correspondingly, we have unique bounded weak solutions
\rho_{1}^{k}
and
\rho_{2}^{k}
of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
truncated up to time
2^{-k-1}
and with initial datum
\bar{\rho}_{1}^{k}
and
\bar{\rho}_{2}^{k}
respectively thanks to Theorem
1.5
. Weak limit points of the sequences
(\rho_{1}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
and
(\rho_{2}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
are then proven to be distinct bounded weak solutions of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
with initial datum
\bar{\rho}
.

2.
The boundary value problem

2.1.
Definitions

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the boundary value problem posed on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

(BVP)

\left\{\begin{split}\partial_{t}\rho+\operatorname{div}_{x}({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\rho)&=0,\\
\rho(s,x)&=\bar{\rho}(x).\end{split}\right.

We will work with bounded weak solutions of (
BVP
).

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a boundary datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and a closed interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
\rho\in L^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a bounded weak solution on
I
to (
BVP
) along
b
, if for every
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

\int_{s}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+{{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right)\;dxdt=-\int_{-\infty}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho\left(\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial t}+{{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi\right)\;dxdt=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}(x)\phi(s,x)\;dx.

For bounded weak solutions of the boundary value problem (
BVP
) on
\mathbb{R}
, we omit to specify the time interval. We record the following existence theorem for (
BVP
).

Theorem 2.2
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and an intial datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then, there exists a bounded weak solution
\rho
of (
BVP
) along
b
satisfying
\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a standard mollifier, and let
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k}
be a regularisation of
b
. Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}
be the unique flow of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
. Then, the unique weak solution
\rho^{k}
of (
BVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
is given by :

\rho^{k}(t,x){\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,s,x)_{\#}\bar{\rho}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}.

Moreover,
\|\rho^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}=\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
because
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,s,\cdot)_{\#}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
. Therefore, by the Banach-Alaoglou Theorem, there is an increasing map
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\rho^{\psi(k)}
converges weak-star in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to some
\rho
as
k\to+\infty
, and
\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{t.x}}\leq\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}
by weak lower semicontinuity of the norm. Since
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}\to{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}
strongly in
L^{1}_{loc}
, it follows that
\rho
is a bounded weak solution of (
2.1
) along
b
.
∎

2.2.
Time continuous representative

It is a standard fact that a bounded weak solution of (
BVP
), although only in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
always has a representative in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, so we can take traces in time. This is recorded in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Consider a bounded vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a boundary datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a bounded weak solution
\rho
of (
BVP
) along
b
, and a compact time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
with non-empty interior. Then,
\rho
admits a unique representative
\tilde{\rho}
in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
for which it holds that :

(i)

\sup_{t\in I}\|\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(I;L^{\infty}_{x})};

(ii)

for every
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a real constant
L_{\phi,I}>0
such that for a.e.
t\in I

(2.1)

\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}(t,x)\phi(x)dx\Big|\leq L_{\phi,I}\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})},

in particular, if
\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=0
, then
\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)=\tilde{\rho}(s,\cdot)
for every
t,s\in I
.

We shall call the unique representative in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of a bounded weak solution
\rho
of (
BVP
),
the time continuous representative
of
\rho
.

Proof.

STEP 1 (A-priori bounds): Choose a representative of
\rho
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. As
\rho
is a bounded weak solution of (
BVP
) along
b
, it holds that in
L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
:

(2.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x){\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(x)dx.

Let
J\subset\mathbb{R}
be a compact time interval.
For every
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a real constant
L_{\phi,J}>0
such that for a.e.
t\in J

(2.3)

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx\Big|&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big|\rho(t,x){\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi(x)\big|dx,\\
&\leq\|\nabla_{x}\phi\|_{L_{x}^{1}}\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(J;L_{x}^{\infty})}\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}=L_{\phi,J}\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}(J\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\\
\end{split}

Therefore, the function

\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx,

admits a Lipschitz continuous representative with Lipschitz constant bounded by
L_{\phi,J}
on
J
by
[
6
, Theorem 8.2]
.

Consider the set
S_{\phi}\subset\mathbb{R}
, on which the above function coincides with its Lipschitz continuous representative. Note that
S_{\phi}
depends on which representative of
\rho
is chosen, and that
S_{\phi}
has full measure by
[
6
, Theorem 8.2]
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be a countable subset of
C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
dense in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which exists by separability. Define

S_{\mathcal{N}}=\bigcap_{\phi\in\mathcal{N}}S_{\phi}.

Notice that this set has full measure in
\mathbb{R}
, and is therefore also dense in
\mathbb{R}
.
For every
\phi\in\mathcal{N}
, define the function
f_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as the unique continuous extension to
\mathbb{R}
of the function defined for
t\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
by

(2.4)

f_{\phi}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx.

We then have for every
\phi\in\mathcal{N}

(2.5)

\sup_{t\in J}|f_{\phi}(t)|\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(J;L_{x}^{\infty})}\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

STEP 2 (Choosing a representative):
We now seek a representative
\tilde{\rho}
in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
of
\rho
.
Equip
C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact time intervals, and equip
\mathcal{N}
with the topology induced from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consider the linear operator

(2.6)

\Lambda:\mathcal{N}\ni\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}).

Note that by (
2.5
), it is a continuous linear operator.
By density of
\mathcal{N}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by (
2.5
),
\Lambda
admits a unique continuous extension
\tilde{\Lambda}
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which satisfies

(2.7)

\sup_{t\in J}\big|[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t)\big|\leq\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}(J;L^{\infty}_{x})}\|\phi\|_{L^{1}_{x}}.

Equipping
(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*}
with the weak-star topology, we can then identify
\tilde{\Lambda}
with a family of linear functionals

\tilde{\Lambda}:\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}\in(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*},

such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni\phi\mapsto[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t)\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, by the duality
(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*}\cong L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have that
\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}
can be uniquely identified with an element
\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let us check that
\tilde{\rho}
is in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. The weak-star topology on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is induced by the family of seminorms defined by
p_{\phi}(f)=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)f(x)dx|
for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and every
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we then have

(2.8)

\begin{split}\Big|p_{\phi}\Big(\tilde{\rho}(t_{1},\cdot)-\tilde{\rho}(t_{2},\cdot)\Big)\Big|&=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\tilde{\rho}(t_{1},x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\tilde{\rho}(t_{2},x)dx\Big|\\
&=\Big|\tilde{\Lambda}_{t_{1}}(\phi)-\tilde{\Lambda}_{t_{2}}(\phi)\Big|\\
&=\Big|[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t_{1})-[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t_{2})\Big|,\end{split}

which can be made arbitrarly small by continuity of
\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)
and by taking
|t_{1}-t_{2}|
sufficiently small.
Therefore,
\tilde{\rho}\in C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and

(2.9)

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}(t,x)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Notice also that by construction,
\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}
is the unique continuous extension of the linear functional

\Lambda_{t}:\mathcal{N}\ni\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}(t)\in\mathbb{R}.

So, for every
t\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
we have

(2.10)

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{N}.

Together with (
2.9
), the above equation implies by density of
\mathcal{N}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
that for every
t\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
it holds that
\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)=\rho(t,\cdot)
as bounded functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Since
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
has full measure in
\mathbb{R}
, we have
\tilde{\rho}(t,x)=\rho(t,x)
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d+1}
-a.e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, whence
\tilde{\rho}
is a bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
b
. Item
(i)
then follows by (
2.7
) for the time interval
I
. Item
(ii)
follows by making a-priori bounds on
\tilde{\rho}
as in (
2.3
).

∎

2.3.
Convergence of time continuous representatives

Solutions of (
BVP
) along a suitable regularisation of a bounded, divergence-free
b
have their time continuous representative converging
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. This is recorded in the following lemma.

Lemma 2.4
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and boundary datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and a regularisation
({\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\operatorname{div}_{x}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}=0
. Assume that the unique bounded weak solution
\rho^{k}
of (
BVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
converges weakly-star in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to some bounded weak solution
\rho
of (
BVP
) along
b
.
Then, the time continuous representative of
\rho^{k}
converges in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the time continuous representative of
\rho
.

Proof.

Let
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}
be the unique flow of
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
. Then, the unique weak solution
\rho^{k}
of (
BVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
is given by :

\rho^{k}(t,x){\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,s,x)_{\#}\bar{\rho}{\mathscr{L}}^{d},

and by Lemma
2.3
, has a unique time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}^{k}
. Similarly,
\rho
has a unique time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}
.

We will use a subsubsequence arguement to show that
(\tilde{\rho}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
has a unique accumulation point. Accordingly, let
\xi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be an increasing map. We will show that there is another increasing map
\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}
converges in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the time continuous representative of
\tilde{\rho}
as
k\to+\infty
.

STEP 1 (A-priori bounds): Since
b
is divergence-free, then
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}
is also divergence-free, and we have
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}(t,s,\cdot)_{\#}{\mathscr{L}}^{d}={\mathscr{L}}^{d}
, whence
\|\rho^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}=\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
. Therefore, by item
(i)
of Lemma
2.3
and taking a covering of
\mathbb{R}
by compact intervals, we get

(2.11)

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\tilde{\rho}^{k}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}=\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

By the a-priori bound (
2.1
), for every
\phi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and every compact time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, there exists a real constant
L_{\phi,I}>0
such that
the functions

f_{\phi}^{k}:\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho^{k}(t,x)\phi(x)dx,

are continuous with Lipschitz constant on
I
bounded by
L_{\phi,I}
.
and satisfy

\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R},k\in\mathbb{N}}|f^{k}_{\phi}(t)|\leq\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}}\|\phi\|_{L_{x}^{1}}.

STEP 2 (Compactness):
Let
\mathcal{N}\subset C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be a countable, dense subset of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
By Ascoli’s Theorem, for every
\phi\in\mathcal{N}
, there is an increasing map
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that
f^{(\psi\circ\xi)(k)}_{\phi}
converges uniformly on compact time intervals as
k\to+\infty
. Therefore, there exists a diagonal increasing map
\delta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
such that

(C)

for every
\phi\in\mathcal{N}
, the function
f^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}_{\phi}
converge uniformly on compact time intervals to a continuous function
f_{\phi}
as
k\to+\infty
.

STEP 3 (Choosing a representative): We reason as in Step 2 of the proof of Lemma
2.3
.
Equip
C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
with the topology of uniform convergence on compact time intervals, and equip
\mathcal{N}
with the topology induced from
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Consider the continuous linear operator

(2.12)

\Lambda:\mathcal{N}\ni\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}\in C(\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R}).

By density of
\mathcal{N}
in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by (
2.11
),
\Lambda
admits a unique continuous extension
\tilde{\Lambda}
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Equipping
(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*}
with the weak-star topology, we can then identify
\tilde{\Lambda}
with a family of linear functionals

\tilde{\Lambda}:\mathbb{R}\ni t\mapsto\Lambda_{t}\in(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*},

such that for every
t\in\mathbb{R}

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}:L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\ni\phi\mapsto[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t)\in\mathbb{R}.

Thus, by the duality
(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{*}\cong L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
we have that
\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}
can be uniquely identified with an element
\zeta(t,\cdot)
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Let us check that
\zeta
is in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
. The weak-star topology on
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is induced by the family of seminorms defined by
p_{\phi}(f)=|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)f(x)dx|
for every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and every
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For every
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and every
t_{1},t_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, we then have

(2.13)

\begin{split}\Big|p_{\phi}\Big(\zeta(t_{1},\cdot)-\zeta(t_{2},\cdot)\Big)\Big|&=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\zeta(t_{1},x)dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\zeta(t_{2},x)dx\Big|,\\
&=\Big|\tilde{\Lambda}_{t_{1}}(\phi)-\tilde{\Lambda}_{t_{2}}(\phi)\Big|,\\
&=\Big|[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t_{1})-[\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)](t_{2})\Big|,\end{split}

which can be made arbitrarly small by continuity of
\tilde{\Lambda}(\phi)
and by taking
|t_{1}-t_{2}|
sufficiently small.
Therefore,
\zeta\in C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and

(2.14)

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Notice also that by construction,
\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}
is the unique continuous extension of the linear functional

\Lambda_{t}:\mathcal{N}\ni\phi\mapsto f_{\phi}(t)\in\mathbb{R}.

So, for every
t\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
we have

(2.15)

\tilde{\Lambda}_{t}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{N}.

STEP 4 (Identification of
\zeta=\tilde{\rho}
):
Locally uniformly in
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
we have

(2.16)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)\phi(x)dx\qquad\forall\phi\in\mathcal{N}.

Therefore, for any
\psi\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R})
and any
\phi\in\mathcal{N}
, we have

(2.17)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\phi(x)\psi(t)dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)\phi(x)\psi(t)dxdt.

Let
\varepsilon>0
.
By density in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the set

\mathcal{D}:=\Big\{\sum_{k=1}^{N}\phi_{k}(x)\psi_{k}(t)\;:\;\phi_{k}\in\mathcal{N},\;\psi_{k}\in C^{1}_{c}(\mathbb{R}),\;N\in\mathbb{N}\Big\},

for any
\eta\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there is
\nu\in\mathcal{D}
such that
\|\eta-\nu\|_{L_{t,x}^{1}}<\varepsilon
.
Write

(2.18)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\eta(t,x)dxdt=&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)(\eta(t,x)-\nu(t,x))dxdt\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\nu(t,x)dxdt.\\
\end{split}

Using (
2.11
) and Hölder inequality, we have

(2.19)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)(\eta(t,x)-\nu(t,x))dxdt\Big|\leq\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\|\eta-\nu\|_{L^{1}_{t,x}}\leq\varepsilon\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}.

By (
2.17
), we have for
k
sufficiently large that

(2.20)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\nu(t,x)dxdt-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)\nu(t,x)dxdt\Big|<\varepsilon,

and as
\|\zeta\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}\leq\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
, we also have that

(2.21)

\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)(\nu(t,x)-\eta(t,x))dxdt\Big|\leq\varepsilon\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}},

therefore we have

(2.22)

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\eta(t,x)dxdt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\zeta(t,x)\eta(t,x)dxdt.

So,
it holds that
\rho^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}
converges weakly-star in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
\zeta
as
k\to+\infty
.
By the hypothesis of the lemma, we have
\zeta=\rho
as functions in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By uniqueness of the time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}
of
\rho
, we have
\zeta=\tilde{\rho}
as functions in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.

STEP 5 (Convergence to
\tilde{\rho}
): We now prove that
\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}
converges to
\tilde{\rho}
in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
as
k\to+\infty
. Let
\phi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\tilde{\phi}\in\mathcal{N}
, and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\|\tilde{\phi}-\phi\|_{L^{1}_{x}}<\varepsilon
. Note that,

(2.23)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\phi(x)dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\Big(\phi(x)-\tilde{\phi}(x)\Big)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)-\tilde{\rho}(t,x)\Big)\tilde{\phi}(x)dx\\
&+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}(t,x)\Big(\tilde{\phi}(x)-\phi(x)\Big)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\rho(t,x)\phi(x)dx.\end{split}

The first term is bounded by
\varepsilon\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
by Hölder inequality and by (
2.11
). The second term is bounded by
\varepsilon
for
k
sufficiently large thanks to (C). The third term is bounded by
\varepsilon\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
by Hölder inequality and by (
2.11
).
Therefore, we see that

\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}(t,x)\phi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\tilde{\rho}(t,x)\phi(x)dx,

whence,
\tilde{\rho}^{(\delta\circ\xi)(k)}
converges to
\tilde{\rho}
in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
as
k\to+\infty
. Since
\xi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
was an arbitrary increasing map, by the subsubsequence lemma, it follows that the whole sequence
\tilde{\rho}^{k}
converges in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the time continuous representative of
\tilde{\rho}
as
k\to+\infty
, which proves the thesis.
∎

2.4.
Uniqueness for
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R};BV_{loc})

We will use the following straightforward consequence of Theorem
1.5
for the boundary value problem (
BVP
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a boundary datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Assume that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R};{\rm BV_{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
Then, there exists a unique bounded weak solution of (
BVP
) along
b
.

Proof.

Recall that
s\in\mathbb{R}
is the time at which the boundary datum is imposed in (
BVP
).
Let
\rho
be a bounded weak solution of (
BVP
) whose existence follows from Theorem
2.2
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\rho
has a unique time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}
.
Define the vector fields
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\leq}(t,x)={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(s-t,x)
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\geq}(t,x)={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t+s,x)
. Then, for
t\in(0,+\infty)

\rho^{\leq}(t,x)=\rho(s-t,x)
and
\rho^{\geq}(t,x)=\rho(t+s,x)
are bounded weak solutions of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\leq}
and
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{\geq}
respectively, and they are thus uniquely determined for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d+1}
-a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
by Theorem
1.5
. Therefore
\rho(t,x)
is uniquely determined for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d+1}
-a.e.
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This proves the thesis.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.8

3.1.
Backward uniqueness for
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L^{1}_{loc}((0,+\infty);BV_{loc})

The following crucial lemma uniquely identifies bounded weak solutions for a boundary datum at a time
s>0
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider a bounded, divergence-free vector field
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
, a boundary datum
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
s>0
. Assume that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\in L_{loc}^{1}((0,+\infty);{\rm BV_{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and that
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,\cdot)\equiv 0
for
t<0
.
Then, there exists a unique bounded weak solution
\rho_{\bar{\rho},s}
to the boundary value problem (
BVP
).

Moreover, for every standard mollifier
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}_{\bar{\rho},s}^{k}
of the unique bounded weak solution of (
BVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k}
converges in
C(\mathbb{R};w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
to the time continuous representative of
\rho_{\bar{\rho},s}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be a bounded weak solution of (
BVP
) along
b
whose existence follows from Theorem
2.2
. By Lemma
2.3
,
\rho
has a unique time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}
.
To prove uniqueness, we will show that
\tilde{\rho}
is uniquely determined by bounded weak solutions of (
BVP
) along a regularised version of
b
. Let us consider for any
\tau>0
the vector fields

{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{\tau}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}(t,x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t\geq\tau,\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\quad t<\tau.\end{split}\right.

STEP 1 (Determining
\tilde{\rho}(t,\cdot)
for
t\in(0,+\infty)
):

3.2.

We can now conclude this section.
Let us give some preliminary tools, which will be useful for establishing weak compactness. Fix a measure space

\lim_{C\to+\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathscr{F}}\int_{\{|f|>C\}}|f|d\mu=0.

The Dunford-Pettis theorem (see
[

) asserts that
\mathscr{F}
is relatively weakly sequentially compact subset of

if and only if
\mathscr{F}
is equi-integrable.

Proof of Theorem

.

Extend

{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}={\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\star\theta^{k}
. Recall that
{\mbox{\boldmath$X$}}^{k}
is the flow along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}

we have that

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}^{k}(t,x)|\leq\|{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}},

therefore the following finite speed of propagation property holds:

(FS)

for every

, every

, and every

we have

STEP 1 (Solutions from the classical theory):
By the classical theory, we have

(i)

the density

(ii)

) along

By (FS) above, and since

is compactly supported, the following property holds:

there exists

sufficiently large that

and
\operatorname{supp}\phi(s,\cdot)\subset B_{R}(0)
for every

.

We will use a subsubsequence argument to characterise the weak limit points of

. Let

be an increasing map.

STEP 2 (Compactness):
Let us check relative weak sequential compactness of the sequence

in

for every

{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for
i=1,2
;

(f)

for every
t\in[0,+\infty),
we have
\zeta_{1}(t,x)=1-\zeta_{2}(t,x)
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(g)

for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
and for
i=1,2
, we have

\fint_{S}\zeta_{i}(2^{-(k+1)},x)dx=1/2\qquad\forall S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{1}.

4.3.
Weak approximation of initial datum

Given a sequence of indicator functions, which is oscillating infinitely fast, and satisfies a constraint of spatial homogeneity, any initial datum
\bar{\rho}
can be weakly approximated by a sequence localised on these indicator functions. This is recorded in the following lemma.

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider a sequence
(f^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
f^{k}(x)\in\{0,1\}
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and such that and every
S\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k}
, we have

(4.2)

\fint_{S}f^{k+1}(y)dy=1/2.

Consider a function
\bar{\rho}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Then, there exists a sequence of functions
(\bar{\rho}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

(4.3)

\bar{\rho}^{k}f^{k+1}=\bar{\rho}^{k},

and

(4.4)

\bar{\rho}^{k}\rightharpoonup\bar{\rho}\quad\text{$w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

Moreover, if
\bar{\rho}
is non-negative, then the sequence
(\bar{\rho}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
can be chosen to consist of non-negative functions.

Proof.

Define

(4.5)

\bar{\rho}^{k}(x):=2f^{k+1}(x)\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k}}\mathbb{1}_{S}(x)\fint_{S}\bar{\rho}(y)dy,

and observe that (
4.3
) is satisfied.
Note that

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|\bar{\rho}^{k}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}\leq 2\|\bar{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}},

and that finite linear combinations of functions in

\Big\{\mathbb{1}_{S}\,:\,S\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{l},\;l\in\mathbb{N}\Big\},

are dense in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Therefore, to prove (
4.4
), it suffices to prove that for every
l\in\mathbb{N}
and every
\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{l}
, we have

(4.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}^{k}(x)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{S}}(x)dx\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}(x)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{S}}(x)dx,

as
k\to+\infty.

Let
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
\tilde{S}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{l}
.
Then, for
k\geq l
, we have

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}^{k}(x)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{S}}(x)dx&=2\int_{\tilde{S}}f^{k+1}(x)\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{1}}\mathbb{1}_{S}(x)\fint_{S}\bar{\rho}(y)dydx,\\
&=2k^{d}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{k}^{1},S\subset\tilde{S}}\int_{S}f^{k+1}(x)dx\int_{S}\bar{\rho}(y)dy,\\
\text{by \eqref{eqn_control_local_average}}&=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}^{1}_{k},S\subset\tilde{S}}\int_{S}\bar{\rho}(y)dy,\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\bar{\rho}(y)\mathbb{1}_{\tilde{S}}(y)dy.\end{split}

Moreover it is clear that if
\bar{\rho}
is non-negative, then so are the functions defined by (
4.5
).
This proves the thesis.
∎

4.4.
Proof of Theorem
1.11

We can now conclude this section.

Proof of Theorem
1.11
.

If
\bar{\rho}=0
, then
\zeta_{i}(t,x)-1/2
is a bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
with initial datum
\bar{\rho}=0
for
i=1,2
.
Suppose that
\bar{\rho}\neq 0
.
Consider the following sequence of bounded, divergence-free vector fields:

{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}^{k}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}(t,x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t\geq 2^{-k},\\
0\qquad&\text{if}\quad t<2^{-k}.\end{split}\right.

By Section
4.2
, for
i=1,2
the densities
\zeta_{i}^{k}
given by:

\zeta_{i}^{k}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}\zeta_{i}(t,x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t\geq 2^{-k},\\
\zeta_{i}(2^{-k},x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t<2^{-k},\end{split}\right.

are both bounded weak solutions of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}^{k}
.

STEP 1 (Apply Lemma
4.1
):
For
i=1,2
, the sequence
(\zeta_{i}(2^{-k},\cdot))_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfies the hypothesis of Lemma
4.1
by item (
g
) of Section
4.2
. So there exists a sequence
(\bar{\rho}_{i}^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
uniformly bounded in
L_{x}^{\infty}
and such that for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\bar{\rho}_{i}^{k}(x)\zeta_{i}(2^{-k-1},x)=\bar{\rho}_{i}^{k}(x),

and also

\bar{\rho}_{i}^{k}\rightharpoonup\bar{\rho}\qquad\text{ in $w^{*}-L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

STEP 2 (Getting weak solutions of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
): For
i=1,2
, we apply Theorem
1.5
. There thus exists a unique bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}^{k}
with initial datum
\bar{\rho}_{i}^{k}
, which we shall denote by
\rho^{k}_{i}
, and satisfies
\|\rho^{k}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{t,x}}=\|\bar{\rho}^{k}_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}
. Therefore, by the Banach-Alaoglou Theorem, along an increasing map
\psi:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, we have
\rho^{\psi(k)}_{i}
converge to
\rho_{i}
weakly-star in
L^{\infty}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
k\to+\infty
. Since
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}^{k}\to{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
in
L^{1}_{loc}
, it follows that
\rho_{i}
is a bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}
with initial datum
\bar{\rho}
.

STEP 3 (Control on
\rho_{i}
):
By Theorem
1.5
, for
M\in\mathbb{R}
there exists a unique bounded weak solution of (
IVP
) along
{\mbox{\boldmath$b$}}_{DP}^{k}
with initial datum
M\zeta_{i}(2^{-k},x)-\bar{\rho}^{k}_{i}(x)
for
i=1,2
, and by linearity it is

\rho_{i}^{k,M}(t,x):=\left\{\begin{split}M\zeta_{i}(t,x)-\rho^{k}_{i}(t,x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t\geq 2^{-k},\\
M\zeta_{i}(2^{-k},x)-\bar{\rho}^{k}_{i}(x)\qquad&\text{if}\quad t<2^{-k}.\end{split}\right.

Now, choose

M>\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N},i=1,2}\|\bar{\rho}^{k}_{i}\|_{L_{x}^{\infty}},

so that
\rho^{k,-M}\leq 0\leq\rho^{k,M}
, and notice that we have that
\rho^{\psi(k),-M}_{i}
and
\rho^{\psi(k),M}_{i}
converge weakly-star in
L^{\infty}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
M\zeta_{i}-\rho_{i}
and
-M\zeta_{i}-\rho_{i}
respectively as
k\to+\infty
, whence

(4.8)

-M\zeta_{i}\leq\rho_{i}\leq M\zeta_{i}\qquad\text{for $i=1,2$}.

STEP 4 (
\rho_{1}\neq\rho_{2}
): By Lemma
2.3
, we have the time continuous representative
\tilde{\rho}_{1}
and
\tilde{\rho}_{2}
, of
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
respectively. We also know that
\rho_{i}(0,\cdot)=\bar{\rho}(\cdot)
, and since

\bar{\rho}\neq 0
, we have for
i=1,2
that

\sup_{t<\delta}\|\tilde{\rho}_{i}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}}>0,

for
\delta>0
sufficiently small. Thus, there exists
a function
\phi\in L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for
i=1,2
, we have

(4.9)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{i}(t,x)|\phi(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\tilde{\rho}_{i}(t,x)|\phi(t,x)dxdt>0.

Now by (
4.8
) and since
\zeta_{i}(t,x)=\mathbb{1}_{\operatorname{supp}\zeta_{i}}(t,x)
, we have

(4.10)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{i}(t,x)|\phi(t,x)\zeta_{i}(t,x)dxdt=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{i}(t,x)|\phi(t,x)dxdt>0.\end{split}

Since
\zeta_{1}(t,x)+\zeta_{2}(t,x)=1
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d+1}
-a.e.
(t,x)\in(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have for
i,j=1,2
with
i\neq j

(4.11)

\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\rho_{i}(t,x)|\phi(t,x)\zeta_{j}(t,x)dxdt=0.

Since
\phi\zeta_{1}
and
\phi\zeta_{2}
are in
L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, by the duality
\Big(L^{1}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\Big)^{*}\cong L^{\infty}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have that
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are distinct as functions in
L^{\infty}((0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

STEP 5 (Non-negativity): Assume now that
\bar{\rho}
is non-negative, i.e. that
\bar{\rho}(x)\geq 0
for
{\mathscr{L}}^{d}
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and that
\bar{\rho}\neq 0
. Then, by Lemma
4.1
, for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the functions
\bar{\rho}_{1}^{k}
and
\bar{\rho}_{2}^{k}
are non-negative. Therefore, the solutions
\rho_{1}^{k}
and
\rho_{2}^{k}
of (
IVP
) given in Step 2 are non-negative. Therefore, the
L^{\infty}
weak-star limit points
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are also non-negative. This concludes the proof.
∎

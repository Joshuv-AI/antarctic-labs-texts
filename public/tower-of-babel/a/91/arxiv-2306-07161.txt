Minimal Terracini loci in projective space Thanks: Partially supported by GNSAGA of INdAM
Edoardo Ballico, Maria Chiara Brambilla

Abstract
. We characterize the number of points for which there exist non-empty Terracini sets of points in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} . Then we study minimally Terracini finite sets of points in ℙ n \mathbb{P}^{n} and we obtain a complete description, in the case of ℙ 3 \mathbb{P}^{3} , when the number of points is less than twice the degree of the linear system.

Primary: 14C20; Secondary:14N07

1.
Introduction

The notion of
Terracini locus
in projective spaces has been recently introduced in

[
3
]
and then
extended to other projective varieties and investigated in

[
2
,
4
,
5
,
10
]
.
This property encodes the fact that a set of double points imposes dependent conditions to a linear system, hence it gives information for interpolation problems over double points in special position.

Moreover it can be interpreted in terms of special loci contained in higher secant varieties to projective varieties
as follows.
Recall that
the
k
-th higher secant variety
\sigma_{k}(X)
of a
projective variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the Zariski closure of the
union of all the linear spaces spanned by
k
independent points
of
X
.
The variety
X
is called
k
-defective if it has dimension less than the expected one, i.e.
\min(N,k\dim(X)+k-1).

By the famous Terracini lemma
[
13
]
a variety is
k
-defective if the tangent spaces to
X
at
k
general points
span a linear space of dimension less than the expected one.
Even when the variety is not
k
-defective, there may be special sets of points such that the span of the tangent spaces drops dimension. We call
Terracini
such special sets of points.
For non-defective varieties, we can see the Terracini sets as the points of the abstract secant variety for which
the
differential of the map to the secant variety is not injective, see e.g.
[
3
]
for more details.

The interest in this subject is also motivated by the connection with the theory of tensors, see e.g.
[
12
,
6
]
for general reference. In particular, since symmetric tensors can be identified with homogeneous polynomials, the development of geometric methods in projective spaces can give contribution to the study of the rank and decompositions of symmetric tensors.

In this paper we focus on the case of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and we say that
a finite set of points
S
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is
Terracini
with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0,\ h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0,\mbox{ and }\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
the sets of all subsets
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
of cardinality
x
which are Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
.

In the language of secant varieties, the first condition means that the secant variety
\sigma_{x}(X)\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
does not fill the ambient space, since
\dim\sigma_{x}(X)=N-h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{Z}(d))
(see e.g.
[
6
, Corollary 1]
.
On the other hand, if

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
, then
the number
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))

equals the so-called
x
-defect
, that is
x(n+1)-\dim\sigma_{x}(X)-1
(see Lemma
2.5
).

Notice that there are no Terracini sets in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, see Lemma
3.3
.
The first result of this paper characterizes the triples
n,d,x
such that the Terracini locus is non-empty, as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix positive integers
n
,
d
and
x
.

(i) If either
n=1
or
d=2
, then
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)=\emptyset
for any
x
.

(ii)
\mathbb{T}(2,3;x)=\emptyset
for any
x
.

(iii)
If
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 3
and
(n,d)\neq(2,3)
,
then
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\neq\emptyset
if and only if
x\geq n+\lceil d/2\rceil
.

In order to make a finer description it is very useful to study
minimally Terracini loci
.
The
minimally Terracini
property has been introduced in
[
2
, Definition 2.2]
for any projective variety.
A Terracini set of points
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is said to be minimally Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))=0\mbox{ for all }A\subsetneq S.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
the set of all
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
which are minimally Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
.

In Theorem
3.1
we see that if
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
is minimally Terracini for some
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
, then such
d
is unique and
it is the maximal integer
t
such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(t))>0
.

Note that, for fixed
n,d
, we know that
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
is not empty for infinitely many
x
, by Theorem
1.1
. On the other hand,
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}\subseteq\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)

is not empty
only for finitely many
x
, as proved in Proposition
3.4
.
In other words the minimality property is a strong condition which
allows us to prove interesting bounds and characterizations of the triples
n,d,x
for which
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
is or is not empty.

In Section
4
we investigate the sets of points on rational normal curves and on their degenerations (reducible rational normal curves). In particular Theorem
4.2
and Proposition
4.7
completely describe the minimal Terracini sets contained in such curves.
Since rational normal curves contain elements of
\mathbb{T}(n,d;1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil)^{\prime}
, we may formulate the following conjecture:

Conjecture 1.2
.

For any

x\leq\left\lfloor\dfrac{nd+1}{2}\right\rfloor
,
we have
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

Here we prove the conjecture for
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, Proposition
5.2
, and for
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, Theorem
1.3
.

After the easy description of the situation in the plane (see Section
5
), we focus on the case of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
, and we obtain the following three results, which are the main results of this paper.

Theorem 1.3
.

Fix integers
d\geq 4
and
x
such that
2x\leq 3d+1.
Then
\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Fix integers
d\geq 7
and
x=1+\lceil{3d}/{2}\rceil
. Then
S\in\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}
if and only if
S
is contained in a rational
normal curve.

Theorem 1.5
.

Fix integers
d\geq 17
and
x
such that
1+\lceil{3}d/2\rceil<x<2d
. Then
\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}=\emptyset
.

The bound in Theorem
1.5
is sharp, as shown in Example
6.2
, where
2d
points lie on an elliptic curve.

Summing up, our results prove that, given
d>0
and
x\leq 2d
, then the minimal Terracini loci
\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}
are empty except for

\bullet
either
x=1+\lceil{3d}/{2}\rceil
, and in this case the points lie on a rational normal curve,

\bullet
or
x=2d
, and in this case the points may lie on an elliptic curve.

We call
(0,1+\lceil{3d}/{2}\rceil),(1+\lceil{3d}/{2}\rceil,2d)
the first two gaps where the minimal Terracini loci are empty.

The situation is completely analogous in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, where the first two gaps are
(0,d+1)
and
(d+1,\lfloor 3d/2\rfloor)
, see Section
5
.

We expect that a similar behaviour happens also in any dimension
n\geq 2
.

The paper is organized as follows:
in Section
2
we present the preliminary results and in particular we introduce the notion of critical scheme, which is a crucial tool in our proofs.
Section
3
contains the first properties of Terracini and minimal Terracini sets and
the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
In Section
4
we characterize the minimally Terracini sets of points on rational normal curves and their degenerations. Section
5
is devoted to the plane, while Section
6
to the case of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and to the proofs of Theorems

1.3
,

1.4
and
1.5
.

We thank the referee for many useful suggestions that improved our presentation.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

We work over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
.
For any
x\in\mathbb{N}
, let
S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
denote the set of all subsets of cardinality
x
of a projective space
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.
For any set
E\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, let
\langle E\rangle
denote the linear span of
E
in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is well-known that the set of configurations of
n+2
points of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in linear general position is an open orbit for the action of
\mathrm{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
.

Definition 2.2
.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}_{1}(n,d;x)
the set of all
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
such that

•

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)\subseteq\mathbb{T}_{1}(n,d;x)
the set of all
S\in\mathbb{T}_{1}(n,d;x)
such that

•

\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

We call
Terracini locus
the set
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
and we say that a finite set
S
is

Terracini with respect to

\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

Obviously
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)=\emptyset
for all
x\leq n
, since every
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
spans
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

We recall from
[
2
, Definition 2.2]
the following important definition; it applies to any projective variety, but we write it now only in the case of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

Definition 2.3
.

A set
S
is said to be
minimally Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
if it is Terracini and moreover

•

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))=0
for all
A\subsetneq S
.

We denote by
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
the set of all
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
which are minimally Terracini with respect to
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
.

In the next remark we recall the exceptional cases of the Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem, which are all the cases when any general set of points is minimally Terracini.

Remark 2.4
.

Assume

(n,d;x)\in\{(2,4;5),(3,4;9),(4,4;14),(4,3;7)\}
.
Then by the Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem
[
1
]
we know that the Veronese variety
\nu_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n})
is
x
-defective.

Fix a general
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
. We have that

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
because the
x
-secant variety does not fill the ambient space,
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
because it is defective.
Moreover, since
x\geq n+1
, we have
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and hence

S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

We prove now that
S
is minimal.
Indeed, since
S
is general, then any subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
of cardinality
y<x
is general in

S(\mathbb{P}^{n},y).

Since the secant variety
\sigma_{y}(\nu_{d}(\mathbb{P}^{n}))
is not defective for any
y\leq x-1
, then
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))=0
.
Then we have proved that
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
.

We collect here some preliminary results we will use in the sequel.

Lemma 2.5
.

For any zero-dimensional scheme
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
and any integer
t\geq 0
,
we have
h^{i}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))=0
for all
i\geq 2
, and

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t)-h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))=\binom{n+t}{n}-\deg(Z).

Proof.

Since
Z
is zero-dimensional, we have
h^{i}(\cal{O}_{Z}(t))=0
for all
i>0
. Obviously
h^{i}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t))=0
for all
i>0
. Then from the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

we obtain the formulas in the statement.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
W\subset Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be zero-dimensional schemes and
t\geq 0
. Then we have

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{W}(t))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{W}(t))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t)),

and

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t+1))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t+1))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(t)).

Proof.

Since
W\subset Z
, then we have the exact sequences

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{W,Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{W}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

and

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{W}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{W,Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

Since
Z
is zero-dimensional, then
h^{i}(\cal{I}_{W,Z}(d))=0
for all
i\geq 1
. Then we get

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{W}(d))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{W}(d))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d)).

From
the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(t+1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{H}(t+1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

where
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
is an hyperplane,
it follows that

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\leq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d+1))\mbox{\ \ and \ }h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d+1))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d)).

∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Given a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
and any finite set
S\subset H
, we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap H,H}(d))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap H,H}(d))+h^{1}(\cal{I}_{S}(d-1)).

Proof.

From
the residual exact sequence with respect to
H

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{S}(d-1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S\cap H,H}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

and by Lemma
2.5

the statement
follows.
∎

We recall from
[
7
]
the following useful lemma.

Lemma 2.8
.

[
7
, Lemma 34]

Let
Z
be a zero dimensional scheme in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0
.
If
\deg(Z)\leq 2d+1
, then there is a line
L
such that
\deg(Z\cap L)\geq d+2
.
In particular it follows that
\deg(Z)\geq d+2
.

We recall the following lemma which we learned from K. Chandler (
[
8
,
9
]
).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
W
be an integral projective variety,
\cal{L}
a line bundle on
W
with
h^{1}(\cal{L})=0
and
S\subset W_{\reg}
a finite collection of points. Then

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{(2S,W)}\otimes\cal{L})>0
if and only if there is a scheme
Z\subset 2S
such that any connected component of
Z
has degree

\leq 2
and such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}\otimes\cal{L})>0
.

The schemes
Z
appearing in Lemma
2.9
are curvilinear subscheme of a collection of double points. More precisely in the following definition we introduce the notion of
critical schemes
, which are the crucial tools in our proofs.

Definition 2.10
.

Given
S
a collection of
x
points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
we say that a zero-dimensional scheme
Z
is
d
-critical for
S
if:

•

Z\subseteq 2S
and any connected component of
Z
has degree
\leq 2
,

•

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0
,

•

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}}(d))=0
for any
Z^{\prime}\subsetneq Z
.

Note that Lemma
2.9

implies that for every
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
there
exists a
d
-critical scheme for
S
.

The next lemmas describe the properties of a critical scheme.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
Z
be a zero-dimensional scheme such that

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}}(d))=0
for any
Z^{\prime}\subsetneq Z
.
Then
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))=1
.

Proof.

Assume
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\geq 2
and take a subscheme
Z^{\prime}\subset Z
such that
\deg(Z^{\prime})=\deg(Z)-1
.
We have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z^{\prime}}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))-\deg(Z)+\deg(Z^{\prime})>0
. Thus
Z
is not critical, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Fix
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
and take
Z
critical for
S
. Then
Z_{\red}=S
.

Proof.

Assume
S^{\prime}:=Z_{\red}\neq S
. Lemma
2.9
gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))>0
.
Thus
S
does not belong to
\mathbb{T}(n,d;,x)^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 2.13
.

Fix integers
n\geq 2
,
d>t\geq 1
and
x>1
. Take
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
and a critical scheme
Z
for
S
. Take
D\in|\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t)|
with
Z\nsubseteq D
. Then
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{D}(Z)}(d-t))>0
.

Proof.

Since
Z\nsubseteq D
and is critical, then Definition
2.10
gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap D}(d))=0
. Thus the residual exact sequence with respect to
D

gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{D}(Z)}(d-t))>0
.
∎

3.
First results on minimally Terracini sets of points

We now prove the fact that if
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
is minimally Terracini for some
\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(d)
, then such
d
is unique and it is the maximal integer

t
such that
h^{1}(I_{2S}(t))>0
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Fix
n\geq 2
and
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
. Then

(i)
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d+1))=0
,

(ii)
S\notin\mathbb{T}(n,t;x)
for any
t\geq d+1
,

(iii)
S\notin\mathbb{T}(n,t;x)^{\prime}
for any
t\leq d-1
.

Proof.

We now prove (i) by contradiction.
Assume
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d+1))>0
.
By Lemma
2.9
, there is a
(d+1)
-critical scheme
Z
for
S
. Recall that, in particular, every component of
Z
has degree
\leq 2
. Moreover, by Lemma
2.12
we have
S\subset Z\subset 2S
, whereas from Lemma
2.11
we know that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d+1))=1
.

Fix
p\in Z_{\red}
and call
Z(p)
the connected component of
Z
supported at
p
.
Set
L:=\langle Z(p)\rangle
. Then
L
is either a line, or a point
L=Z(p)=\{p\}
.

Let
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a general hyperplane containing
L
.
Since
Z
is curvilinear, by generality of
H
we can assume that the scheme
Z\cap H
is equal to the scheme
Z\cap L
. Let us denote
\zeta=Z\cap H=Z\cap L
.
We will consider separately two possibilities:
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta,H}(d+1))>0
and
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta,H}(d+1))=0
.

(a)
Assume first
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta,H}(d+1))>0
.
Then
L
is a line. Since
\zeta\subset L
, then we have
the following diagram, whose rows and columns are exact sequences,
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{I}_{L,H}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{I}_{\zeta,H}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{I}_{\zeta,L}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{I}_{L,H}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{O}_{H}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{O}_{L}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{O}_{\zeta}(d+1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cal{O}_{\zeta}(d+1)}

From the diagram, we get
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta,L}(d+1))>0
,
which implies
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta}(d+1))>0
.

Since
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
n\geq 2
, the set
S^{\prime}=S\cap L
is different from
S
.
Now
by Lemma
2.9
we have that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d+1))>0
, and
Lemma
2.6
implies that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))>0
.
Hence we have
S\notin\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

(b)
Now assume
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\zeta,H}(d+1))=0
.
In this case the residual exact sequence with respect to
H
gives

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(d))>0
.
Since
\Res_{H}(Z)_{\red}\subseteq S\setminus\{p\}
, by Lemma
2.9

we have that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2(S\setminus\{p\})}(d))>0
. This contradicts the minimality of
S
, that is

S\notin\mathbb{T}(n,d,x)^{\prime}
, a contradiction.

Now it is easy to prove (ii). Indeed by using (i) and Lemma
2.6
, we get, for any
t\geq d+1
, that

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(t))\leq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d+1))=0
. Hence
S\notin\mathbb{T}(n,t;x)
.

We prove (iii) by contradiction. Indeed assume
t\leq d-1
and

S\in\mathbb{T}(n,t;x)^{\prime}
. But then by (i) we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(t+1))=0
. Then, since
t+1\leq d
by Lemma
2.6
we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(t+1))=0
, which contradicts the assumption

S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
.
∎

The following result is a kind of
concision
or
autarky
for Terracini loci of Veronese varieties.

Proposition 3.2
.

Take a finite set of points
S\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
M:=\langle S\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then

h^{1}(M,\cal{I}_{2S\cap M,M})(d))>0\ \mbox{ if and only if }\ h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap M}(d))
. Since
M
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay, we get
h^{1}(M,\cal{I}_{2S\cap M,M}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap M}(d))
.
Hence the
only if
part is obvious.

Now assume

. Take a hyperplane

H\supseteq M

It is sufficient to prove that

Take a critical scheme

We start now the classification of Terracini and minimal Terracini sets of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

Obviously

Lemma 3.3

\mathbb{T}(1,d;x)=\mathbb{T}_{1}(1,d;x)=\emptyset

The following proposition shows a key difference between

Proposition 3.4

Let

Lemma 3.5
.

\mathbb{T}(n,2;x)=\emptyset
for all

and all

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that

, we

.

First assume

Since

Now assume

The following result shows that many elements of

Lemma 3.6

∎

Lemma 3.7
.

For any

.

Proof.

The case

3.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We are now in position to give the proof of Theorem
1.1
which classifies Terracini loci. We start with the following lemma.

Lemma 3.8
.

Assume
n\geq 1
and
d\geq 2
. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be a zero-dimensional scheme such that
\deg(Z)\leq d+n+1
,
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0
and
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Then there is a line
L
such that
\deg(L\cap Z)\geq d+2

and
\deg(Z)=d+n+1
.

Proof.

The lemma is trivial for
n=1
.

We prove the statement by induction on
n\geq 2
.
First we assume
n=2
. Since
\deg(Z)\leq 2d+1
, there is a line
L
such that
\deg(Z\cap L)\geq d+2
, by Lemma
2.8
. Clearly, since
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, we get
\deg(Z)=d+3
.

Now assume
n>2
. Take a hyperplane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
such that
w:=\deg(Z\cap H)
is maximal. Since
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we have
n\leq w<z
and
\langle Z\cap H\rangle=H
.

If
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap H,H}(d))>0
, then by induction we have that
there is a line
L
such that
\deg(L\cap(Z\cap H))\geq d+2

and
\deg(Z\cap H)=d+n
. Hence it follows that

\deg(L\cap Z)\geq d+2

and
\deg(Z)\geq d+n+1
, hence
\deg(Z)=d+n+1
.

Now assume
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap H,H}(d))=0
and by the residual exact sequence with respect to
H

(1)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(d-1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z\cap H,H}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(d-1))>0
.
By Lemma
2.8
, since
\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))\leq z-w\leq d+1\leq 2d+1
, we have a line
L
with
\deg(L\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq d+2
. Since
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we must have
\deg(Z)\geq\deg(Z\cap L)+n-1\geq d+n+1
. Hence the assumption

\deg(Z)\leq d+n+1
implies that

\deg(Z)=d+n+1
and

\deg(L\cap Z)\geq d+2
.
∎

The following Proposition
3.10
proves the emptyness of the Terracini locus for small number of points.
We give first a numerical lemma which will be used in the proof of the proposition.

Lemma 3.9
.

Given
x,y,m,n,d\in\mathbb{N}
, such that

d\geq 3
,
n\geq 3
,
m<n
,
x\geq y+n-m
,

x\leq n+\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil-1
,

y(m+1)\geq\binom{m+d}{m}
,
then we have
m=1
.

Proof.

Using the assumptions, in particular
y\leq x-(n-m)\leq x-1
, we have

\binom{m+d}{m}\leq(x-1)(m+1)\leq\left(m+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil-2\right)(m+1)\leq\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(m+1).

We prove now by induction on
m\geq 2
that

(2)

\binom{m+d}{m}>\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(m+1).

It is easy to check that (
2
) is true for
m=2
and any
d\geq 3
.
Now we assume (
2
) for
m
and we have, by using the induction hypothesis:

\binom{m+1+d}{m+1}=\binom{m+d}{m+1}+\binom{m+d}{m}=\binom{m+d}{m}\left(\frac{d}{m+1}+1\right)>

>\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(m+1)\left(\frac{d}{m+1}+1\right)=\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(d+m+1)=

=\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(m+2)+\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(d-1)\geq\left(m+\frac{d}{2}\right)(m+2)

where the last inequality holds because

\left(m-1+\frac{d}{2}\right)(d-1)\geq(m+2)

for any
d\geq 3
and
m\geq 1
.

Hence since we have proved (
2
) for any
m\geq 2
, we conclude that
m=1
.
∎

Proposition 3.10
.

Assume
n,d\geq 2
and fix an integer
x
such that

x\leq n+\left\lceil\frac{d}{2}\right\rceil-1.

Then
\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)=\emptyset
.

Proof.

The case
d=2
is true by Lemma
3.5
, hence we can assume
d\geq 3
.

Assume
n=2
. Assume by contradiction that

S\in\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)
. Let
Z
be a critical scheme for
S
, then we have
\deg(Z)\leq 2x\leq d+3
. Hence by Lemma
3.8
there exists a line
L
such that

\deg(Z\cap L)\geq d+2
and hence

, a contradiction.

Assume
n\geq 3
and use now induction on
n
.
By contradiction assume

S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
. Let
S^{\prime}\subseteq S
be the minimal subset such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))>0
. Set
y:=\#S^{\prime}
,
M:=\langle S^{\prime}\rangle
,
and

. Proposition
3.2
gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}\cap M,M}(d))>0
.
Notice that Lemma
2.9
and Lemma
2.8
imply that
2y\geq d+2
.

(I)
If
m<n
, then we consider two cases.

(a)
If
h^{0}(M,\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}\cap M}(d))>0
, then we have
y\geq m+\lceil d/2\rceil
by the induction assumption. Then
x\geq y+(n-m)=n+\lceil d/2\rceil
, a contradiction.

(b)
If

h^{0}(M,\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}\cap M}(d))=0
, then
y(m+1)\geq\binom{m+d}{m}
.
Since
S
spans
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, then
x\geq y+(n-m)
. Hence
by Lemma
3.9
, we get
m=1
. Then
M
is a line
and in this case we have again a contradiction because, since

, we have

(II)
Thus we may assume
m=n
.
Let
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
be any hyperplane such that
H
is spanned by
S^{\prime}\cap H
. Let
S^{\prime\prime}=S^{\prime}\cap H
. Then
n\leq\#(S^{\prime\prime})<y
.
Since
\Res_{H}(2S^{\prime\prime})=S^{\prime\prime}
, we have the exact sequence:

(3)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{S^{\prime\prime}}(d-1)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime\prime}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime\prime}\cap H,H}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0.

The minimality of
S^{\prime}
and Proposition
3.2
give
h^{1}(H,\cal{I}_{{2S^{\prime\prime}\cap H},H}(d))=0
.

(a) Now,
if
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{S^{\prime\prime}}(d-1))>0
, then

(a.1) either

\#(S^{\prime\prime})\geq n+d
, which gives a contradiction with
x\leq n+\lceil\frac{d}{2}\rceil-1
;

(a.2)
or
\#(S^{\prime\prime})\leq n+d-1
.
In the latter case, Lemma
3.8
applied to
S^{\prime\prime}\subset H
gives
\#(S^{\prime\prime})=n+d-1
, which also contradicts
x\leq n+\lceil{d}/{2}\rceil-1
.

(b)
Hence we may assume
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{S^{\prime\prime}}(d-1))=0
. From the exact sequence (
3
), we get

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime\prime}}(d))=0
.

We consider now the residual exact sequence with respect to the quadric hypersurface
2H
:

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}}(d-2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime\prime},2H}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

where

\Res_{2H}({2}S^{\prime})=S^{\prime}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}
.

Since the quadric hypersurface

in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay, we get
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime\prime},2H}(d))=0
, which implies
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{S^{\prime}\setminus S^{\prime\prime}}(d-2))>0
. Then by Lemma

, we have

.
But since
\deg(S^{\prime}\setminus S^{\prime\prime})=\#(S^{\prime}\setminus S^{\prime\prime})\leq y-n\leq\lceil d/2\rceil-1
, we have a contradiction, since

∎

We now give the proof of the main result of this section.

:

We start now to analyze the set of points lying on a rational normal curve.

Lemma 4.1

Assume by contradiction that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0

-critical scheme

for

.
Since

is scheme-theoretically cut-out by quadrics, there is

such that

Since

We have

is projectively normal.

Note that

\deg(\Res_{Q}(Z))<n(d-2)+2

Then we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{Q}(Z),C}(d-2))=h^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(n(d-2)-\deg(\Res_{Q}(Z))))=0,

and we have
a contradiction with
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{Q}(Z)}(d-2))>0
.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Fix integers
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 3
and assume
(n,d)\neq(2,3)
.
Given a rational normal curve
C\in\cal{C}_{n}
and a collection
S\subset C
of
x
points on the curve.
Then

(i) if
n\geq 3,d\geq 4
and
x\geq 1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil
, then
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
;

(ii) if
n\geq 4,d=3

and
x=1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil
, then
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
;

(iii) if
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 4
and
x=1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil
, then
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
.

Proof.

By the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{C\cup 2S}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{2S\cap C,C}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

since

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S\cap C,C}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(nd-2x))>0

whenever
x\geq 1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil
,
we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.
Since
x\geq n+1
and
C
is a rational normal curve, then
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
.

If
n\geq 3
then
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{C}(2))\geq 2
, hence
C
is contained in a quadric hypersurface.
Thus if
d\geq 4
, we have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
.
Hence
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
and we have proved (i).

Assume now
x=1+\lceil{nd}/{2}\rceil
. Fix a collection
A
of
x
general points on
C
and
note that by generality

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\geq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2A}(d))
.

Hence, assuming
d=3
we have

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(3))\geq\binom{n+3}{3}-(n+1)x>0

where the last inequality is true for any

n\geq 5
.
If
n=4
and
x=7
, we have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(3))\geq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2A}(3))=1
, by the Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem. In consequence
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,3:x)
, which ends the proof of (ii).

Now assume
n=2
and
x=d+1
.
We have
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))\geq\binom{d+2}{2}-3(d+1)>0
, for
d\geq 5
.
If
d=4
and
x=5
, then

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(4))\geq h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2A}(4))=1
, again by the Alexander-Hirschowitz theorem.
Hence
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
for
n=2
and
d\geq 4
.

In order to complete the proof of (iii) we need to prove the minimality of
S
, and this follows by Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

Recall that by Theorem
1.1
we know that
\mathbb{T}(2,3;x)=\emptyset
for all
x>0
.
Moreover in the proof of Lemma
3.7
, we have seen that a set of

x\geq 5
points in linearly general position in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is not Terracini. Hence if
S
is a collection of
x\geq 5
points on a rational normal cubic curve we have
S\not\in\mathbb{T}(3,3;5)
.

4.1.
Degenerations of rational normal curves.

We introduce now the notion of
reducible rational normal curves
.

Definition 4.4
.

A reduced, connected and reducible curve
T\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
, for
n\geq 2
, such that
\deg(T)=n
and
\langle T\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
is called
reducible rational normal curve
.

Of course, in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
a reducible rational normal curve is a reducible conic.

Since
T
is connected, there is an ordering
T_{1},\dots,T_{s}
of the irreducible component such that each
T[i]:=T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq s
, is connected.
We say that each such ordering of the irreducible components of
T
is a
good ordering
.

Set
n_{i}:=\deg(T_{i})
. Note that
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
and
\dim\langle T_{i}\rangle\leq n_{i}
with equality if and only if
T_{i}
is a rational normal curve in its linear span.
For
i=1,\dots,s-1
we have the following Mayer-Vietoris exact sequence

(4)

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{T[i+1]}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{T[i]}(t)\oplus\cal{O}_{T_{i+1}}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{T[i]\cap T_{i+1}}(t)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

in which
T[i]\cap T_{i+1}
is the scheme-theoretic intersection. Since
T[i+1]
is connected,
\deg(T[i]\cap T[i+1])>0
. Thus (
4
) gives
\dim\langle T[i+1]\rangle\leq\dim\langle T[i]\rangle+n_{i}
with equality if and only if

\deg(T[i]\cap T[i+1])=1
,
T_{i+1}
is a rational normal curve in its linear span and
\langle T[i]\rangle\cap\langle T_{i+1}\rangle
is the point
T[i]\cap T_{i+1}
.

Since
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
, by induction on
i
we get
p_{a}(T)=0
and each
T_{i}
is a rational normal curve in its linear span. Using (
4
) and induction on
t
we also get
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{T}(t))=0
and
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{T}(t))=nt+1
for all
t\geq 0
, and that the restriction map
H^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}(t))\xrightarrow{\ \ }H^{0}(\cal{O}_{T}(t))
is surjective, i.e.
T
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay. In the same way we see that each
T[i]
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay in its linear span.

Recall that each
T_{i}
is smooth. For any
p\in T_{i}
let
L_{i}(p)
denote the tangent line of
T_{i}
at
(p)
. Take
p\in\mathrm{Sing}(T)
and let
T_{i_{1}},\dots T_{i_{k}}
,
k\geq 2
, be the irreducible components of
T
passing through
p
. Since
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
and
p_{a}(T)=0
, the
k
lines
L_{i_{1}}(p),\dots,L_{i_{k}}(p)
through
p
span a
k
-dimensional linear space (such a singularity is often called a seminormal or a weakly normal curve singularity).

An irreducible component
T_{i}
of
T

is said to be a
final component
if
\#(T_{i}\cap\mathrm{Sing}(T))=1
. Since
s\geq 2
,
T
has at least
2
final components (e.g.
T_{1}
and
T_{s}
for any good ordering of the irreducible components of
T
), but it may have many final components
(e.g. for some
T
with
s\geq 3
we may have
\#(T_{i}\cap\mathrm{Sing}(T))=1
for all
i\geq 2
and there is one
T
, unique up to a projective transformation, formed by
n
lines through the same point).

Remark 4.5
.

Take a (reducible) rational normal curve
T\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
. Since
h^{1}(\cal{O}_{T})=0
, the exact sequence

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{T}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{n}}\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{O}_{T}\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

gives
h^{2}(\cal{I}_{T})=0
. Since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{T}(1))=0
, the Castelnuovo-Mumford Lemma implies that the homogeneous ideal of
T
is generated by quadrics. Thus
T
is scheme-theoretically cut out by quadrics.

Lemma 4.6
.

Fix
n\geq 2
,
d\geq 4
.
Let
T
be a reducible rational normal curve in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
and
S\in S(\mathbb{P}^{n},x)
such that
S\subset T_{\reg}
and
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
. If
2x\geq dn+2
, then
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.

Proof.

Since
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{T}(2))=\binom{n}{2}
, we have that
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{2S}(d))>0
if
d\geq 4
.

Set
Z:=2S\cap T
. Since
S\cap\mathrm{Sing}(T)=\emptyset
,
\deg(Z)=2x
and
Z
is a Cartier divisor of
T
.
Since
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{T}(d))=nd+1
, then
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z,T}(d))\geq 1
. Hence
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))\geq 1
, since
T
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay, and

S\in\mathbb{T}_{1}(n,d;x)
. Finally, since by assumption
\langle S\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{n}
, we conclude that
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Assume
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 5
and set

x=1+\left\lceil\frac{nd}{2}\right\rceil.

Fix a reducible rational normal curve
T=T_{1}\cup\cdots\cup T_{s}\subset\mathbb{P}^{n}
,
s\geq 2
. Assume the existence of
S\in\mathbb{T}(n,d;x)^{\prime}
such that
S\subset T
. Set
n_{i}:=\deg(T_{i})
and
x_{i}:=\#(S\cap T_{i})
. Then:

(i)

S\subset T_{\reg}
;

(ii)

n
is even and
d
is odd;

(iii)

every final component
T_{i}
of

has
n_{i}
odd and
2x_{i}=n_{i}d+1
.

Proof.

Set
W:=2S\cap T
.
Note that
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{s}\geq x
and that
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{s}=x
if and only if
S\subset T_{\reg}
.
We have
n=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}
,
2x=nd+2
if
nd
is even and
2x=nd+3
if
n
and
d
are odd. Obviously

and hence
s-1<x
.

Step 1.

We prove first of all that, for any
i
:

(5)

2x_{i}\leq n_{i}d+1

Assume, by contradiction, that there exists
i
such that
2x_{i}>n_{i}d+1
and set
S^{\prime}=S\cap T_{i}
.
Note that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime},{T_{i}}}(d))

since
T_{i}
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay.
Then since
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{T_{i}}(d))=n_{i}d+1
and
\deg(2S^{\prime})\geq n_{i}d+2
, we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))>0
and hence

, a contradiction.

Step 2.

We prove now (i) by contradiction.
Set
S_{1}:=S\cap\mathrm{Sing}(T)
and
S_{2}:=S\setminus S_{1}
. Since
T
has at most
s-1
singular points,
S_{2}\neq\emptyset
.
We assume by contradiction that
S_{1}\neq\emptyset
.

For each
o\in\mathrm{Sing}(T)
let
m(o)
denote the number of irreducible components of
T
passing through
o
.
We saw that
T
has Zariski tangent of dimension
m(o)
and hence the connected component
W(o)
of
W
supported at the point

has degree
m(o)+1
.
Thus, denoting
w=\deg(W)
, we have

(6)

w=2\#(S_{2})+\sum_{o\in S_{1}}(m(o)+1)\geq 2x+\#(S_{1}).

If
\deg(W)\geq nd+4
, then fix
u\in S_{2}
and set
S^{\prime}:=S\setminus\{u\}
.
Note that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime},T}(d))

since

is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay.
Then, since
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{T}(d))=nd+1
and
\deg(2S^{\prime})\geq w-2\geq nd+2
, we have
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{2S^{\prime}}(d))>0
and hence

, a contradiction.

Then we can assume

(7)

\deg(W)\leq nd+3.

(a) Assume first
nd
even.
Hence we have
2x=nd+2
. Then it follows that

\#S_{1}=1
, say
S_{1}=\{u\}
, and
T
is nodal at
u
. Since
p_{a}(T)=0
,
T
is connected, the irreducible components of

are smooth
and
T
is nodal at

has
2
connected components. Call

and

the closures in

of the two connected components of

and

or
\deg(W\cap T^{\prime\prime})\geq\dim\langle T^{\prime\prime}\rangle+2
. Thus

and we have a contradiction. We have proved (i) in this case.

(b) Now assume
d
odd and
n
odd.
Then
2x=nd+3
, and by using (
6
) and (
7
) we get
S_{1}=\emptyset
. We have proved (i) in this case.

Step 3.

Since
d\geq 5
, a good ordering of the irreducible components of
T

and
s-1
Mayer-Vietoris exact sequences give

. Let
Z
be a critical scheme for
S
, that is

. Since

and

, the Castelnuovo-Mumford’s lemma gives that

is globally generated.
Since

is globally generated and every connected component of

,

for a general

.
Since

and

. Thus

. Since

(8)

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{W,T}(d))>0.

Step 4.

We prove now (ii). Recall that, since
S\subset T_{\reg}
,
we have
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{s}=x
and
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{s}=n
.

Assume by contradiction that

is even. Recall the inequality (
5
) from Step 1.
If

is even

, is equivalent to

, and this implies

which contradicts the assumption

. We have proved that

is odd.

From now on, we assume
d
odd. Recall (
5
), and in particular:

2x_{i}\leq n_{i}d+1
for all odd

and

for all even

.

Now assume

In particular, since

. Let

Step 5.

We finally prove (iii). We know that

is odd and

Let
T_{i}
any final component of

5.
Minimally Terracini finite sets in the plane

In this section we focus on the case of the plane.
We deduce from
[

]
the following result, which we will need in the sequel.

Remark 5.1
.

Fix positive integers

such that

.
Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a zero-dimensional scheme,
Z\neq\emptyset
. If
\deg(Z)=z
and

is the maximal integer

such that

,
then either there is line

Proposition 5.2
.

Fix integers

and

(a) If

\mathbb{T}(2,d;x)^{\prime}=\emptyset

(b) Let

be a critical scheme for

. We have

We prove now the other implication of (b).

S^{\prime}\subsetneq S
, since
\deg(2S^{\prime}\cap C)\leq 3d-2
.
Thus
S\in\mathbb{T}(2,d;3d/2)^{\prime}
.

Example 5.4
.

Take
d
odd,

, and set
x:=(3d+1)/2
. Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
a smooth plane cubic. Take

such that

. By assumption

is a Cartier divisor of

. Since

and
\deg(\cal{O}_{C}(d-3)))=3d-9>\#S
, then

. Since

6.
Minimally Terracini finite sets in
\mathbb{P}^{3}

Now we consider the case of finite sets of points in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
The following proposition extends Remark
5.1
to the case of schemes of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Fix a positive integer
d
. Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
be a zero-dimensional scheme such that
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}
, its connected components have degree
\leq 2
and
z:=\deg(Z)\leq 3d+1
. We have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0

if and only if one of the following cases occur:

(i) there is a line
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
\deg(L\cap Z)\geq d+2
;

(ii) there is a conic
D
such that
\deg(D\cap Z)\geq 2d+2
;

(iii) there is a plane cubic
T
such that
\deg(T\cap Z)=3d
and
T\cap Z
is the complete intersection of
T
and a degree
d
plane curve.

Proof.

Set
S:=Z_{\red}
.

Since the
if
part is trivial, we only need to prove the
only if
part.

We use induction on
d
. The case
d=1
is obvious,
since conditions
\deg(Z)\leq 4
and
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}
imply that
Z
is linearly independent and hence
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(1))=0
.

Assume
d\geq 2
and that the proposition is true for lower degrees.
If there is a plane
H
such that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap H}(d))>0
, then we may use Remark
5.1
and we conclude.

Now we
assume that

(10)

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap H}(d))=0\text{ for any plane }H\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}.

Take a plane
H\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
such that
w:=\deg(Z\cap H)
is maximal.
Since
\langle Z\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}

then we have
z\geq 4
, and
w\geq 3
,
and hence
\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))=z-w\leq 3(d-1)+1
.
Since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap H}(d))=0
by (
10
), then the residual exact sequence with respect to
H
gives
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(d-1))>0
.
The inductive assumption applied to the scheme
\Res_{H}(Z)
implies that we are in one of the following cases:

case (i
′
):
either there is a line
R
such that

\deg(R\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq d+1
,

case (ii
′
):
or there is a conic
E
such that
\deg(E\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq 2d
,

case (iii
′
):
or there is a plane cubic
C
such that
\deg(C\cap\Res_{H}(Z))=3d-3
and
\Res_{H}(Z)\cap C
is the complete intersection of
C
and a degree
d-1
plane curve.

We analyse separately the three cases in the following three steps (a), (b), (c).

Step (a).

Assume first that we are in case (iii
′
). Since

\deg(\Res_{H}(Z)\cap C)=3d-3
, then

z-w=\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))\geq 3d-3
.
On the other hand, since
\Res_{H}(Z)\cap C
is contained in a plane, we also have

w\geq 3d-3
and hence
z\geq 6d-6
.
Now since
z\leq 3d+1
, we get
d=2
.

Since
d=2
, we have
z\leq 7
. Moreover
since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(1))>0
, then the scheme
\Res_{H}(Z)
is linearly dependent and so we have

w\geq\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))
, by the maximality assumption on

.
So we have
z-w\leq 3=2(d-1)+1
, and
by Lemma
2.8
, it follows that there is a line
J
such that
\deg(J\cap\Res_{H}(Z))=(d-1)+2=3

Take now a plane
M\supset J
such that
w^{\prime}:=\deg(M\cap Z)

is maximal. Since
\dim|\cal{I}_{J}(1)|=1
, we have
w^{\prime}\geq 4
.
We get
w=w^{\prime}=4
and
z=7
. Taking
M
instead of
H
and repeating the argument above, we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{M}(Z)}(1))>0
, and again by Lemma
2.8
, it follows that
there exists a line
K
such that
\deg(K\cap\Res_{M}(Z))=3
, hence
\Res_{M}(Z)\subset K
.

If
\deg(K\cap Z)=4
or
\deg(J\cap Z)=4
, then we are in case (i) and the theorem is proved.

Now we exclude the remaining case which is

(11)

\deg(Z\cap K)=\deg(Z\cap J)=3.

Assume by contradiction (
11
)
and
consider separately the following three possibilities:
either
J\cap K\neq\emptyset
and
J\neq K
, or
K\cap J=\emptyset
, or
J=K
.

(a1) Assume that

and
J\neq K
. Recall that
any connected component
of
Z
has degree
\leq 2
, and clearly we have

\deg(J\cap K)=1
. Hence the plane spanned by

gives

.

(a2) Assume

. Since

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(2))>0
, by Lemma
2.5
we have

\dim|\cal{I}_{Z}(2)|=h^{0}(\cal{I}_{Z}(2))-1\geq 3

) imply that

is not a an irreducible quadric cone or a double points. Moreover, since

is general, thent

is not the union of a plane containing

and a plane containing

and

Note that, by using (

), we have

is
1
, and hence it follows that

Now, taking cohomology

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{\Res_{J\cup K}(Z),Q}(0,2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z,Q}(2,2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{(Z\cap J)\cup(Z\cap K),Q}(2,2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0,

(a3) Assume finally that
J=K
.
Recall that
all the connected components of
Z
have degree
\leq 2
and
S=Z_{\red}
.
From (
11
)

\#(S\cap J)=3
, each connected component of
Z
supported at

has degree
2
and none of them is contained in
J
.
Moreover, since

, we have that

is a simple point
p
. Let

. Set

\leq 2
, we have

and

. Hence we have

.
It follows from the exact sequence that

Fix now

A

. Take a plane

.

2.6
, that there is a plane
U
such that

, and this contradicts our assumption (
10
).

Step (b).

Assume now that we are in case (ii
′
).
Since there is a conic
E
such that

, we get
w\geq 2d
and
z-w\geq 2d
. It follows that

, which contradicts the assumptions

and

.

Step (c).

Assume finally that we are in case (i
′
), i.e. assume that there is a line
R
such that

\deg(R\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq d+1
. If
\deg(R\cap Z)\geq d+2
, then we may take
L=R

and the theorem is proved.

Now we assume that
\deg(R\cap Z)=d+1
and we will prove that either we are again in case (i), or we have a contradiction.

Since
\deg(R\cap Z)=d+1
, then we have
R\cap Z=R\cap\Res_{H}(Z)
.
By the maximality assumption on
H
we also know that
w=\deg(Z\cap H)\geq d+1
.

Take a general plane
M\supset R
and consider the scheme

.
Since

, we have
\deg(X)\geq w+d+1\geq 2d+2
. Hence,
the hypothesis

\deg(\Res_{H\cup M}(Z))\leq d-1
.
Then, by Lemma
2.8
, we get

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H\cup M}(Z)}(d-2))=0
.

The residual exact sequence of
Z
with respect to

0\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{\Res_{H\cup M}(Z)}(d-2)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{Z}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }\cal{I}_{X,H\cup M}(d)\xrightarrow{\ \ }0

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X}(d))=h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X,H\cup M}(d))\geq h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z}(d))>0
.

Since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap M}(d))=0

10

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{X\cap M}(d))=0

2.6
.
The residual exact sequence of

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{M}(X)}(d-1))>0
.

We consider now separately the two following cases: either the line
R
is contained in
H
, or it is not contained.

(c1) Assume
H\supset R
. Recall that
S=Z_{\red}
. Since each connected component of
Z
has degree
\leq 2
, we deduce the following facts:
\#(S\cap R)=d+1
, each connected component of

2
and no connected component of

is contained in
R
.

Take general planes
H_{1},H_{2}\in|\cal{I}_{R}(1)|
. Since

and

are general,

.
Since

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H_{1}\cup H_{2}}(Z)}(d-2))=0
.
Then the residual exact sequence of

Take a connected component
A
of

. Since
\deg(A)=2
and
\deg(A\cap R)=1
, there is a unique plane

containing

.
Since

(c2) We assume now

.
For any

Since

for any

.
Recall that

.
Thus if

Since

Notice that if

Thanks to Proposition

Now we will prove Theorem
1.4
, which characterizes the elements of
\mathbb{T}(3,d;1+\lceil 3d/2\rceil)^{\prime}

Notice that one implication follows from

(iii).
By Proposition

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We only need to prove that any

Given

Given

be a critical scheme for

and

Take a quadric

is maximal.

Step (a).

In this step we want to prove that
Z\subset Q
.
Assume by contradiction that
Z\nsubseteq Q
. Since
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))=10

h^{0}(\cal{I}_{A}(2))>0
for every zero-dimensional scheme

such that

. Thus
w\geq 9
. By the minimality of

, we also have

,
hence
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{Q}(Z)}(d-2))>0

Since
\deg(\Res_{Q}(Z))\leq z-w\leq 3(d-2)-\varepsilon

6.1
implies that we are in one of the following cases:

(i)

there is a line
L
such that
\deg(\Res_{Q}(Z)\cap L)\geq d
,

(ii)

there is a plane conic
D
such that
\deg(\Res_{Q}(Z)\cap D)\geq 2d-2

(iii)

\varepsilon=0
,

,

and
\Res_{Q}(Z)
is the complete intersection of a plane cubic and a plane curve of degree

.

(a1) First we exclude cases (ii) and (iii). Indeed, in both cases (ii) and (iii) there is a plane
U
such that

\deg(U\cap Z)\geq\deg(U\cap\Res_{Q}(Z))\geq 2d-2

(a2) We assume now that we are in case (i), i.e. there is a line
L

\deg(L\cap\Res_{Q}(Z))\geq d.

Note that, since
Z\not\subset Q
,
there is a plane
H
such that

. We have

, by the minimality of

(b) Assume

Thus from now on we assume

. Let

(b1) Assume that

, of

.

(b2) Assume that

\deg((Y\cap(M\setminus T_{s}))\leq 3

\deg(\Res_{M}(Z))\leq 4d-2-2\lceil d/2\rceil-1\leq 3(d-1)

6.1

.

(b2.1) Assume first the existence of the line

. Since

, we get

odd and
\deg(Z\cap L_{1})=d+1
. Since

L_{1}\cap T_{s}=\emptyset
. Let

. There is an integral quadric

. Thus

. Thus it is sufficient to prove that
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z\cap Q,Q}(d))=0
. Since

,

. First assume

.

(b2.2) Assume the existence of the conic

. Since

, we get

and hence

\lceil d/2\rceil

. If

.
Now assume that

We give now the proof of Theorem
1.5
, which states that
\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}
is empty if

1+\lceil{3}d/2\rceil<x<2d
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
:

Assume by contradiction the existence of
S\in\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}
and fix a critical scheme
Z
of
S
. Set
z:=\deg(Z)\leq 4d-2
.

Set
Z_{0}=Z
. For any
i>0
,
let
Q_{i}
be a quadric surface such that
z_{i}:=\deg(Z_{i-1}\cap Q_{i})
is maximal and set
Z_{i}:=\Res_{Q_{i}}(Z_{i-1})
.
The sequence
\{z_{i}\}_{i\geq 1}
is weakly decreasing. Let
e
be the maximal
i
such that
z_{i}\neq 0
. Then
z=z_{1}+\cdots+z_{e}
and
Z_{e}=\emptyset
.
Since
h^{0}(\cal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(2))=10
,
z_{i}\geq 9
for all
i<e
, hence we have
e\leq(4d+6)/9
, for

z\leq 4d-2
.
By Lemma
2.13
, since
Z
is critical and
S\in\mathbb{T}(3,d;x)^{\prime}
, we have

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z_{e-1}}(d-2e+2))>0
.

(I) Assume first
e\geq 2
, i.e.
Z
is not contained in any quadric surface.
Since
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{Z_{e-1}}(d-2e+2))>0
, then Proposition
6.1
implies that either
z_{e}\geq 3(d-2e+2)+1

or there is a line

L
such that

\deg(Z_{e-1}\cap L)\geq d-2e+4
or there is a plane conic
D
such that
\deg(Z_{e-1}\cap D)\geq 2d-4e+6
.

(I.a)
First assume
z_{e}\geq 3(d-2e+2)+1
. Since the sequence
z_{i}
is weakly decreasing, we get
z\geq e(3d-6e+6)
.
It is easy to check that
e(3d-6e+6)>4d-2
for any
d\geq 13
and
2\leq e\leq(4d+6)/9
. This contradicts our hypotesis.

(I.b)
Now assume the existence of a plane conic
D
such that
\deg(Z_{e-1}\cap D)\geq 2d-4e+6
.
Since
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{D}(2))=5
, we get

z_{i}\geq(2d-4e+6)+4
for all
i<e
.
Thus

z\geq e(2d-4e+10)-4
.
It is easy to check that

e(2d-4e+10)-4>4d-2
for any
2\leq e\leq(4d+6)/9
, and this gives again a contradiction.

(I.c)
Finally assume the existence of a line

L
such that

\deg(Z_{e-1}\cap L)\geq d-2e+4
. Since
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{L}(2))=7
, we have
z_{i}\geq(d-2e+4)+6
for all
i<e
.
Hence
z\leq e(d-2e+10)-6
.
It is easy to check that

e(2d-4e+11)-6>4d-2
for any
4\leq e\leq(4d+6)/9
. Hence we get
e\in\{2,3\}
.

Let
H
be a general plane containing
L
. Since each
connected component of
Z
has degree
\leq 2
, we may assume
Z\cap L=Z\cap H
.

(I.c1)
First assume

e=3
. Since

z_{1}\geq z_{2}\geq z_{3}\geq d-2
and
z_{1}+z_{2}\geq\lceil 2z/3\rceil
, we have
\deg(\Res_{Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\cup H}(Z))\leq z-\lceil 2z/3\rceil-(d-2)=\lfloor z/3\rfloor-d+2<d-3=(d-5)+2
, since
d\geq 7
.
Since
S
is minimally Terracini, we get
Z\subset Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}\cup L
. Since
e>2
and
H
is
contained in a quadric surface,
Z\nsubseteq Q_{1}\cup H
.
Since
S
is minimally Terracini,
h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{Q_{1}\cup H}(Z)}(d-3))>0
.
We have:

\deg(\Res_{Q_{1}\cup H}(Z))\leq(z-z_{1})-(d-2)\leq

z-\lceil z/3\rceil-d+2\leq\frac{5d+2}{3}\leq 2(d-3)+1
, for
d\geq 17
.
Hence there is a line
R
such that
\deg(R\cap\Res_{Q_{1}\cup H}(Z))\geq d-1
.
Taking a general plane containing
R
and taking again the residual, we get

.
But since
h^{0}(\cal{I}_{R\cup L}(2))>0
and

, we have a
contradiction.

(I.c2)
Now assume
e=2
and hence
z_{1}\geq\lceil z/2\rceil
.
We have

\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))\leq z-d
and

h^{1}(\cal{I}_{\Res_{H}(Z)}(d-1))>0
.

First assume
\langle\Res_{H}(Z)\rangle=\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Since
z-d\leq 3(d-1)+1
, Proposition

6.1
implies that either there is a plane cubic
T_{3}
with

the complete intersection of
T_{3}
and a
degree

d-1
plane curve or there is a conic
T_{2}
such that
\deg(T_{2}\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq 2d
or there is a line
T_{1}
such that
\deg(T_{1}\cap\Res_{H}(Z))\geq d+1
.

First assume the existence of
T_{3}
. Since
\deg(\Res_{H\cup\langle T_{3}\rangle}(Z))\leq 1
, by minimality of
S
we
get
Z\subset H\cup\langle T_{3}\rangle
, contradicting the assumption

.

Assume the existence of
T_{2}
.
Since
\deg(\Res_{H\cup\langle T_{2}\rangle}(Z))\leq z-3d\leq d-1
, we get

, again a contradiction.

Now assume the existence of
T_{1}
. Take a general quadric
U\in|\cal{I}_{L\cup{T_{1}}}(2)|
. Since
\deg(\Res_{U}(Z))\leq z-2d-1\leq 2(d-2)+1

2.8

such that
\deg(R_{1}\cap\Res_{U}(Z))\geq d
. Take a general

. Since

\deg(\Res_{U^{\prime}}(Z))\leq z-3d-1<d
and

is minimally Terracini,

, contradicting the assumption
e>1
.

Now assume
\dim\langle\Res_{H}(Z)\rangle\leq 2
. The only new case is if
\deg(\Res_{H}(Z))=3d-2
and
\Res_{H}(Z)
is contained
in a plane cubic
C
. Since
\deg(\Res_{\langle C\rangle}(Z))\leq d
,
S
is not minimally Terracini.

(II). Assume now
e=1
, that is
Z
is contained in a quadric
Q
.

If
Q
is reducible
we argue as in step (b) of the proof of Theorem
1.4

So we can assume that

is irreducible and reduced.

Set

. Take

, such that

w_{1}=\deg(W_{0}\cap D_{1})

.

, is weakly decreasing.
Let

be the maximal

such that

, i.e.

and and

.

By Lemma
2.13
, since

is critical for

minimal, we have

. Since
\dim|\cal{O}_{Q}(2)|=8
, if

, then

. Thus

for

, hence

(II.a)
If
c=1
, then we have

where

is an integral quadric. Hence
D_{1}
is a complete intersection of two quadrics.
If

is integral, then by Remark

, a contradiction.
If

(II.b)
Now we assume

. Hence either

, or
d

. Then we have

, which is a contradiction.

Now assume

.
Since

\deg(W_{c-1})\leq 6
.
Thus either there is a line

\deg(W_{c-1})=6

. Since

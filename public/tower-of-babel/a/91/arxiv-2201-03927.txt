Melkersson condition for extension of Serre subcategories
Ismael Akray, Runak H. Mustafa and Reza Sazeedeh

Abstract
. Let R R be a commutative noetherian ring and let 𝔞 \mathfrak{a} be an ideal of R R . In this paper, we study a certain condition, namely C 𝔞 C_{\mathfrak{a}} , introduced by Aghapournahr and Melkersson, on the extension of two subcategories of R R -modules. We extend and generalize some of the main results of Yoshizawa [Y1,Y2]. As an example of extension of subcategories, we study the weakly Laskerian modules and we find some conditions under which the local cohomology modules of a weakly Laskerian module lie in an arbitrary Serre subcategory. Eventually, we investigate the cofiniteness of the local cohomology modules of weakly Laskerian modules.

Lemma 2.1
.

4.
Melkersson subsets of extension of Serre subcategories

In this section we study the Melkersson subset induced by Yoshizawa [Y2] and we generalize and extend some of the main results of [Y2] which has been proved for a prime ideal and for those Serre subcategories which are closed under injective envelopes. Let
\mathcal{S}
be a subcategory of modules. We first recall from [Y2] the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

The
Melkersson subset

\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]
of
\Spec R
consists of all prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
such that
\mathcal{S}
satisfies
C_{\mathfrak{p}}
condition. For any non-negative integer
i
, the subset
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]_{\geq i}
consists of all
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]
such that ht
\mathfrak{p}\geq i
. We also recall that
\mathcal{S}
is a
Melkersson subcategory
provided that
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]=\Spec R
.

We notice that a Melkersson subcategory of
R
-modules is not a Serre subcategory in general. To be more precise,
\mathcal{F}w
is a Melkersson subcatgory of
R
-modules while it is not Serre. Yoshizawa [Y2, Theorem 3.4] proved the following result for those Serre subcategoris of
R
-modules which are closed under injective envelopes. Here we remove this condition and give a more general version.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a Serre subcategory of modules. Then
\Supp[\mathcal{S}]\cap\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]\subseteq\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]
. In particular,

\Supp[\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}\cap\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]=\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

Given
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp[\mathcal{S}]\cap\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]
and an
R
-module
M
with
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)=M
and
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}
, there exists an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow(0:_{M}\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow S
such that
N
is finitely generated and
S\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
\mathcal{S}
is Serre,
R/\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
and
N
is noetherian, there exist prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}_{i}\in\Supp N
,
1\leq i\leq n
and a filtration
0=N_{0}\subseteq N_{1}\subseteq N_{1}\dots\subseteq N_{n}=N
such that
N_{i}/N_{i-1}\cong R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
. The fact that
\Supp N\subseteq V(\mathfrak{p})
forces
N\in\mathcal{S}
; and hence
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]
, we deduce that
M\in\mathcal{S}
so that
M\in\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}
. Conversely, according to [Y2, Proposition 3.2], we have
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}\subseteq\Supp[\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}
and so it suffices to prove that
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}\subseteq\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}]
. Assume that
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]_{\geq 1}
and
M
is an
R
-module such that
M=\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(M)
and
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}
. Then
M\in\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}
so that there is an exact sequence
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow M\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0
such that
N
is finitely generated and
S\in\mathcal{S}
. The fact that
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}]\Supp\mathcal{S}
implies that
R/\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
and Since
\Supp N\subseteq V(\mathfrak{p})
, a similar argument mentioned above, deduce that
N\in\mathcal{S}
so that
M\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

We recall from [Z] that an
R
-module
M
is a
minimax module
if there is a finitely generated submodule
N
of
M
such that
M/N
is artinian. The subcategory of minimax modules is denoted by
\mathcal{M}
, and so clearly
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\mathcal{A}
where
\mathcal{A}
is the subcategory of Artinian modules.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be the subcategory of minimax modules of
\Mod R
. Then
\mathcal{M}
satisfies
C_{\mathfrak{m}}
condition for each maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
of
R
.

Proof.

We observe that
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\mathcal{A}
where
\mathcal{A}
is the subcategory of artinian modules. It is known by a result of Melkersson that
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{A}]=\Spec R
. If
\dim R=0
, then
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{A}
and there is nothing to prove. If
\dim R>0
, the result follows by
section

4
.
∎

The following result establishes a relation between the injective indecomposable modules of
\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}
where
\mathcal{S}
is a Serre subcategory of modules.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a Serre subcategory of modules and
\mathfrak{p}
be an prime ideal of
R
such that
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{N}\mathcal{S}
. Then
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}
if either
{\rm ht}\mathfrak{p}>0
or
{\rm ht}\mathfrak{p}=0
and
R/\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

There exists an exact sequence of modules
0\longrightarrow N\longrightarrow E(R/\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0
such that
N
is finitely generated and
S\in\mathcal{S}
. For the first condition, if
\mathfrak{p}\notin\Supp S
, we have
N_{\mathfrak{p}}\cong E(R/\mathfrak{p})
and hence
E(R/\mathfrak{p})
is a finitely generated
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
-module so that
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
is Cohen-Macaulay and hence
\dim R_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
which is a contradiction. Then
R/\mathfrak{p}\in\mathcal{S}
. Given every
\mathfrak{q}\in V(\mathfrak{p})\setminus\{\mathfrak{p}\}
, since
\mathfrak{p}\in\Supp S
, we have
R/\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{S}
. Thus
R/\mathfrak{q}\in\mathcal{S}
for any
\mathfrak{q}\in\Supp N
as
\Ass N=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. Considering a finite filtration of

0=N_{n}\subset N_{n-1}\subset\dots\subset N_{1}\subset N_{0}=N
of submodules of
N
such that
N_{i}/N_{i+1}\cong R/\mathfrak{p}_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-1
, we deduce that
N\in\mathcal{S}
. Now, the above exact sequence implies that
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}
. If the second condition holds, the previous argument conclude that
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{S}
.
∎

We notice that in the above result, if
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal ideal of
R
such that

, then

may not be in
\mathcal{S}
by the following example.

Examples 4.5
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a
1\leq d
-dimensional Gorenstein local ring and for every non-negative integer
n
, let
\mathcal{D}_{n}
denote the subcategory of all modules of dimension
\leq n
. It is clear that
\mathcal{D}_{n}
is Serre and if
0\longrightarrow R\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{{\rm ht}\mathfrak{p}=0}E(R/\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow 0
is an exact sequence of modules, then
X
is a submodule of
\bigoplus_{{\rm ht}\mathfrak{p}=1}E(R/\mathfrak{p})
. Then
X\in\mathcal{D}_{d-1}
and so
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\in\mathcal{N}\mathcal{D}_{d-1}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\Min R
while
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\notin\mathcal{D}_{d-1}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\Min R
. Specially, if

, then

, but
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\notin\mathcal{A}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\Min R
where
\mathcal{A}
is the subcategory of artinian modules. Furthermore, if

, then

while
E(R/\mathfrak{p})\notin\mathcal{F}
for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\Min R
.

Melkersson [M1, Theorem 1.6 and Corollary 1,7] showed that if
R
is a local ring and
\mathfrak{a}
is an ideal of

, then an artinian module

is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite if and only if
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})
has finite length and the subcategory of artinian
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite modules, denoted by

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

{\rm ht}\mathfrak{a}=\dim R-1

{\rm(ii)}

\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]_{\geq\dim R-1}

Proof.

In order to prove (i), given an

is
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite. To do (ii), by a similar proof of [Y2, Proposition 6.2] we show that

is finitely generated
R/\mathfrak{a}
-module so that
\dim R/\mathfrak{a}=0
. But this implies that ht
\mathfrak{a}=\dim R
which is a contradiction. We now prove that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}
satisfies the condition
C_{\mathfrak{a}}
. Assume that
M
is an
R
-module such that
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=M
and
(0:_{M}\mathfrak{a})\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}

has finite length; and hence [M1, Theorem 1.6] implies that

. We show that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]
; otherwise since
(0:_{E(R/\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{m})
has finite length,
(0:_{E(R/\mathfrak{m})}\mathfrak{m})\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}
; and hence

which is a contradiction. If
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]_{\dim R-1}
, then we have
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
; otherwise it follows from [SR, Corollary 2.10] that
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}
satisfies the condition
C_{\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{q}}
and since
\mathfrak{a}+\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary, using [SR, Proposition 2.4], we have
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]
which is a contradiction. Clearly,
\mathfrak{q}\in\Min\mathfrak{a}
. For the second assertion, if
\mathfrak{p}
and
\mathfrak{q}
are distinct prime ideals in
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]_{\geq\dim R-1}
, by a similar argument
\mathfrak{p}+\mathfrak{q}
is
\mathfrak{m}
-primary so that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{A}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]_{\geq\dim R-1}
which is a contradiction.
∎

For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
, Melkersson [M2, Corollary 4.4] proved that the subcategory of
\mathfrak{a}
-cofinite minimax modules is Serre and we denote this subcategory by

. The following proposition generalizes [Y2, Proposition 6.7] for an arbitrary ideal of

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a local ring such that
\dim R\geq 2
and let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of

such that
{\rm ht\mathfrak{a}}=\dim R-1
. Then
\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{M.\mathfrak{a}-cof}]_{\geq\dim R-1}=\emptyset
. Furthermore,
\mathcal{S}_{M.\mathfrak{a}-cof}
does not satisfy the condition

.

Proof.

As
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}.\mathfrak{a}-cof}
, contains all modules of finite length, a similar argument in the proof of

implies that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathbb{M}[\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M},\mathfrak{a}-cof}]

for every
\mathfrak{p}\in\Min\mathfrak{a}

as

which is a contradiction. Assume that

\mathfrak{m}
-prmiary. Assume that

has finite length,

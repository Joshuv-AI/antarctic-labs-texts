A new result on boundedness of the Riesz potential in central Morrey–Orlicz spaces
Evgeniya Burtseva, Lech Maligranda

Abstract
We improve our results on boundedness of the Riesz potential in the central Morrey–Orlicz spaces and the corresponding weak-type version. We also present two new properties of the central Morrey–Orlicz spaces: nontriviality and inclusion property.

1
Central Morrey–Orlicz spaces

A function
\Phi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
is called a
Young function
, if it is a nondecreasing convex
function with
\lim_{u\to 0^{+}}\Phi(u)=\Phi(0)=0
, and not identically
0
or
\infty
in
(0,\infty)
.
It may have jump up to
\infty
at some point
u>0
, but then it should be left continuous at
u
.

To each Young’s function
\Phi
one can associate another convex function
\Phi^{*}
, i.e., the
complementary function

to
\Phi
, which is defined by

\Phi^{*}(v)=\sup_{u>0}\,[uv-\Phi(u)]~~{\rm for}~~v\geq 0.

Then
\Phi^{*}
is also a Young’s function and
\Phi^{**}=\Phi
. Note that
u\leq\Phi^{-1}(u)\Phi^{*^{-1}}(u)\leq 2u

for all
u>0,
where
\Phi^{-1}
is the right-continuous inverse of
\Phi
defined by

\Phi^{-1}(v)=\inf\{u\geq 0\colon\Phi(u)>v\}~{\rm with}~\inf\emptyset=\infty.

We say that Young’s function
\Phi
satisfies the
\Delta_{2}
-
condition
and we write shortly
\Phi\in\Delta_{2}
, if

0<\Phi(u)<\infty
for
u>0
and there exists a constant
D_{2}>1
such that

\Phi(2u)\leq D_{2}\Phi(u)~~{\rm for~all}~~\ u>0.

For any Young’s function
\Phi
, the number
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and an open ball

B_{r}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon|x|<r\},r>0
we can define
central Morrey–Orlicz spaces
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)

as all
f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
such that

\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}=\sup_{r>0}\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}<\infty,

where

\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}=\inf\big\{\varepsilon>0\colon\dfrac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}}\Phi\big(\dfrac{|f(x)|}{\varepsilon}\big)\,dx\leq 1\big\}.

Similarly, the
weak central Morrey–Orlicz spaces
WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
are defined as

WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\colon\|f\|_{WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}=\sup_{r>0}\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r},\infty}<\infty\right\},

where

\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r},\infty}=\inf\big\{\varepsilon>0\colon\sup_{u>0}\Phi(\dfrac{u}{\varepsilon})\,\dfrac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\,d(f\chi_{B_{r}},u)\leq 1\big\},

and
d(f,u)=|\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\colon|f(x)|>u\}|
.

The properties of these spaces can be found in
[
3
]
. If
\Phi(u)=u^{p},\,1\leq p<\infty
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R},

then
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)=M^{p,\lambda}(0)
and
WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)=WM^{p,\lambda}(0)
are classical central and
weak central Morrey spaces. Moreover, for
\lambda=0
the spaces
M^{\Phi,0}(0)=L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

WM^{\Phi,0}(0)=WL^{\Phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are classical Orlicz and weak Orlicz spaces.

In the following lemma and later,
B(x_{0},r_{0})
will denote an open ball with the center at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
radius
r_{0}>0
, that is,
B(x_{0},r_{0})=\{x\in{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\colon|x-x_{0}|<r_{0}\}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\Phi
be a Young’s function,
\Phi^{*}
its complementary function,
0\leq\lambda\leq 1

and
r>0
. Then

({\rm i})

\int_{B_{r}}|f(x)g(x)|\,dx\leq 2\,|B_{r}|^{\lambda}\,\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}\|g\|_{\Phi^{*},\lambda,B_{r}}
.

({\rm ii})

\|\chi_{B(x_{0},r_{0})}\|_{\Phi^{\ast},\lambda,B_{r}}\leq\frac{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})|}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})|}\right)
, where
B_{r}\cap B(x_{0},r_{0})\neq\emptyset
for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r_{0}>0
.

In particular,
\|\chi_{B_{r}}\|_{\Phi^{*},\lambda,B_{r}}\leq\dfrac{\Phi^{-1}\left(|B_{r}|^{\lambda-1}\right)}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda-1}}.

({\rm iii})

\|\chi_{B_{t}}\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}=1/{\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B_{t}|}\right)}
and

\|\chi_{B_{t}}\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}=\dfrac{1}{\Phi^{-1}(|B_{t}|^{\lambda-1})}
for any
t>0
.

Proof of this lemma can be found in
[
3
, Lemma 1]
.

2
Riesz potential in the central Morrey–Orlicz spaces

We will work with the central Morrey–Orlicz spaces, defined by the Orlicz functions. A function
\Phi\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)

is called an
Orlicz function
, if it is a strictly increasing continuous and convex function with
\Phi(0)=0
.

Let
f\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a Lebesgue measurable function and
\alpha\in(0,n)
.
The Riesz potential is defined as

I_{\alpha}f(x)=\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{f(y)}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}\,dy,\,\,\text{for}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

The linear operator
I_{\alpha}
plays an important role in various branches of analysis, including potential theory,
harmonic analysis, Sobolev spaces, partial differential equations and can be treated as a special singular integral.
That is why it is important to study its boundedness between different spaces. Many authors investigated
boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
in Morrey, Orlicz and Morrey–Orlicz spaces. We present here our main theorem on
the boundedness of the Riesz potential in the central Morrey–Orlicz spaces.

In order to prove our result we will use estimate from
[
11
]
for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator in central
Morrey–Orlicz spaces. The Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
or
centred maximal function

Mf
of a function

f
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is defined at each
x{\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
as

Mf(x)=\sup\limits_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int\limits_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|\,dy.

For any Orlicz function
\Phi
and
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
, maximal operator
M
is bounded on

M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
, provided
{\Phi}^{*}\in{\Delta}_{2}
, and then there exists a constant
C_{0}>1
such that

\|Mf\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\leq C_{0}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)},\quad\text{for all}~f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)

(see
[
11
, Theorem 6(i)]
). Moreover, the maximal operator
M
is bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to

WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
, that is, there exists a constant
c_{0}>1
such that
\|Mf\|_{WM^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\leq c_{0}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}
for all
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
(see
[
11
, Theorem 6(ii)]
).

Furthermore, in the proof of the main result we will use Hedberg’s pointwise estimate from
[
6
, p. 506]
.

Lemma 2

(Hedberg)
.

If
f\colon{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lebesgue measurable function
and
\alpha\in(0,n)
, then for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0

\int_{|y-x|\leq r}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}dy\leq C_{H}\,r^{\alpha}Mf(x),

with
C_{H}=\frac{2^{n}}{2^{\alpha}-1}v_{n}
, where
v_{n}=|B(0,1)|=\pi^{n/2}/\Gamma(n/2+1)
.

Proof.

For the sake of completeness, we include its proof, taking care about the constant
C_{H}
in the estimate.
For any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0

\displaystyle\int_{|y-x|\leq r}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{r2^{-m-1}<|y-x|\leq r2^{-m}}\frac{|f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(x,r2^{-m})\setminus B(x,r2^{-m-1})}\frac{|f(y)|}{(r2^{-m-1})^{n-\alpha}}dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha}r^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m\alpha}(r2^{-m})^{-n}\int_{B(x,r2^{-m})}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha}r^{\alpha}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m\alpha}\frac{v_{n}}{|B(x,r2^{-m})|}\int_{B(x,r2^{-m})}|f(y)|dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha}r^{\alpha}\,v_{n}\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}2^{-m\alpha}Mf(x)=\frac{2^{n}\,v_{n}}{2^{\alpha}-1}\,r^{\alpha}Mf(x).

∎

THEOREM 1
.

Let
0<\alpha<n,

\Phi,\Psi
be Orlicz functions and either
0<\lambda,\mu<1,\lambda\neq\mu
or
\lambda=0

and
0\leq\mu<1.
Assume that there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}\geq 1
such that

\int_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq C_{1}\,\Psi^{-1}(u^{\mu-1})\quad\text{for all}\ u>0

(1)

and

u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{u})+\int_{u}^{r}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq C_{2}\,\Psi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\mu}}{u})\quad\text{for all}\ r>u>0.

(2)

({\rm i})

If
{\Phi}^{*}\in{\Delta}_{2}
, then
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to
M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)
,
that is, there exists a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(n,C_{0},C_{H},C_{1},C_{2})\geq 1
such that
\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}\leq C_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}

for all
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
.

({\rm ii})

The operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to
WM^{\Psi,\mu}(0)
, that is,
there exists a constant
c_{3}=c_{3}(n,c_{0},C_{H},C_{1},C_{2})\geq 1
such that
\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{WM^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}\leq c_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}
for all
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
.

In our earlier paper
[
3
, Theorem 3]
it was proved result under conditions (
1
) and (
3
),
and the latter means that

\int_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}\leq C_{4}\,\Psi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\mu}}{u})\quad\text{for all}\ u>0,\,\,r>0.

(3)

The condition (
3
) is stronger than the assumption (
2
) because

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{u}^{2u}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{t})\,\frac{dt}{t}\geq u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\int_{u}^{2u}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{2u})\,\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{2}\int_{u}^{2u}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{u})\frac{dt}{t}=\frac{\ln{2}}{2}u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\lambda}}{u})

and clearly the integral in (
2
) is smaller than the integral in (
3
). This improvement provides us with larger
classes of Orlicz functions
\Phi
and
\Psi,
defining central Morrey–Orlicz spaces where the operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded.

In the simplest case, when
\Phi(u)=u^{p},\Psi(u)=u^{q}
where
1<p<q<\infty
, then the convergence of the integral
in (
1
) means
p<\frac{n(1-\lambda)}{\alpha}
and the assumption itself gives equality

\frac{\alpha}{n}+\frac{\lambda-1}{p}=\frac{\mu-1}{q}
. Assumptions (
2
) and (
3
) are both
equivalent and give the following equations:
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}
and
\frac{\lambda}{p}=\frac{\mu}{q}
.
Of course, with the above assumptions, the operator
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
M^{p,\lambda}(0)
to
M^{q,\mu}(0)
.

Only later, on the Examples
2
and
3
, we will see that the conditions (
1
) and (
2
) hold but
estimate (
3
) fails, which shows that our Theorem 1 improves Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
.

Let us comment on what we can get when the numbers
\lambda
and
\mu
come from “boundaries”.

Remark 1
.

If
\lambda=\mu=0
we come to the same conclusion as in
[
3
, Remark 4]
, that is, condition (
1
) is sufficient for
the boundedness of
I_{\alpha}
from Orlicz space
L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to weak Orlicz space
WL^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).
If, in addition

\Phi^{\ast}\in\Delta_{2}
, then
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Note that in this case
condition (
2
) follows from (
1
).

Remark 2
.

If
\lambda=0
and
0<\mu<1
, then the condition (
3
) is not satisfied, as we already mentioned in
[
3
, Remark 3]

and therefore the result proved in
[
3
]
does not include boundedness of the Riesz potential in this case.
On the other hand, in this case, assumption (
1
) is stronger than (
2
). Indeed,

\displaystyle u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{1}{u})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\int\limits_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{1}{t}\bigr)\frac{dt}{t}\leq 4\,C_{1}\Psi^{-1}(u^{\mu-1})\leq 4\,C_{1}\Psi^{-1}(\frac{r^{\mu}}{u})\quad\text{for all}\,\,\,r>u>0.

Therefore, if (
1
) holds, then
I_{\alpha}
is bounded from
L^{\Phi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{\Psi,\mu}(0).

In particular, when
\Phi(u)=u^{p},~\Psi(u)=u^{q}
,
0<\frac{\alpha}{n}<\frac{1}{p}
and
\frac{\alpha}{n}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{\mu-1}{q}
,
then (
1
) holds. In fact, for all
u>0

\int_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{1}{t})\frac{dt}{t}=\int\limits_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}-\frac{1}{p}-1}\,dt=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{n}}u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}=\frac{q}{1-\mu}u^{\frac{\mu-1}{q}}=C_{1}\Psi^{-1}(u^{\mu-1}),

and we obtain (
1
) with
C_{1}=\frac{q}{1-\mu}.
Thus, from Theorem
1
we get that
I_{\alpha}

is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
M^{q,\mu}(0)
. This result, in particular, was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.

Remark 3
.

If
0<\lambda<1
and
\mu=0
, the conditions (
2
) and (
3
) are not satisfied. Additionally,
I_{\alpha}

is not bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to
L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by applying the necessary condition for boundedness
of
I_{\alpha}
given in
[
3
, Theorem 2 (ii)]
.
In fact, let
R\geq 1,

x_{R}=(R,0,...,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f_{R}(x)=\chi_{B(x_{R},1)}(x).
Following the same arguments
as in
[
8
, Proposition 1]
and in
[
3
, Theorem 2 (ii)]
we obtain that

\|f_{R}\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\leq\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}(\frac{v_{n}^{\lambda}}{2^{n}v_{n-1}}R^{\lambda n})}~~{\rm and}~~\|I_{\alpha}f_{R}\|_{L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\geq\frac{2^{\alpha-n}v_{n}}{\Psi^{-1}(\frac{1}{v_{n}})}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\|I_{\alpha}f_{R}\|_{L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}}{\|f_{R}\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha-n}v_{n}}{\Psi^{-1}(\frac{1}{v_{n}})}\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{-1}(\frac{v_{n}^{\lambda}}{2^{n}v_{n-1}}R^{\lambda n})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{\alpha-n}v_{n}}{\Psi^{-1}(\frac{1}{v_{n}})}\min(1,\frac{v_{n}^{\lambda}}{2^{n}v_{n-1}})\liminf\limits_{R\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{-1}(R^{\lambda n})=\infty,

and therefore
I_{\alpha}
is not bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to
L^{\Psi}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Remark 4
.

If
0<\lambda=\mu<1
, then the assumption (
2
) does not hold. Moreover, if either

a=\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\Phi^{-1}(t)}{\Psi^{-1}(t)}>0
or
b=\liminf\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Phi^{-1}(t)}{\Psi^{-1}(t)}=\infty
, then by Theorem 2 in
[
3
]
the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}

is not bounded from
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
to
M^{\Psi,\lambda}(0)
. In particular,
I_{\alpha}
is not bounded
from
M^{p,\lambda}(0)
to
M^{q,\lambda}(0)
for any
1\leq p,q<\infty
(see also
[
8
]
).
There remains an unresolved case when
a=0
and
b<\infty
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

(i) For any
x\in B_{r}
and
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
we consider two disjoint subsets

B_{r}^{1}=\{x\in B_{r}\colon\Phi\left(\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{0}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\leq|B_{r}|^{\lambda-1}\},

and

B_{r}^{2}=\{x\in B_{r}\colon\Phi\left(\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{0}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)>|B_{r}|^{\lambda-1}\}.

We estimate the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}f(x)
by a sum of two integrals

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|\leq 2r}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy+\int_{|y|>2r}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{1}f(x)+I_{2}f(x).

For
x\in B_{r}^{1}
and
|y|\leq 2r
we have
|y-x|\leq|y|+|x|\leq 3r
, and so

I_{1}f(x)=\int_{|y|\leq 2r}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy\leq\int_{|y-x|\leq 3r}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy.

By Hedberg’s pointwise estimate, given in Lemma
2
, we obtain

I_{1}f(x)\leq C_{5}\,|B_{r}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}Mf(x),~{\rm where}~C_{5}=C_{H}\,3^{\alpha}\,v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}.

This implies, for
x\in B_{r}^{1}
, that

I_{1}f(x)\leq C_{0}\,C_{5}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}|B_{r}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(|B_{r}|^{\lambda-1}).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\int\limits_{u}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\geq\int\limits_{u}^{2u}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\frac{dt}{t}\geq\ln{2}~u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}((2u)^{\lambda-1})

\displaystyle\geq 2^{\lambda-1}\ln 2~u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(u^{\lambda-1})\geq\frac{1}{4}\,u^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(u^{\lambda-1}),

for any
u>0
. Thus, applying assumption (
1
) we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\,C_{0}\,C_{1}\,C_{5}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(|B_{r}|^{\mu-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4}{2^{n(\mu-1)}}\,C_{0}\,C_{1}\,C_{5}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(|B_{2r}|^{\mu-1})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 4\cdot 2^{n}\cdot C_{0}\,C_{1}\,C_{5}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(|B_{2r}|^{\mu-1}).

To estimate the second integral
I_{2}f(x)
, first note that when
x\in B_{r}^{1}
and
|y|>2r
we have

|x|<r<|y|/2
and
|y-x|\geq|y|-|x|>|y|/2
, and so
|x-y|^{\alpha-n}<2^{n-\alpha}|y|^{\alpha-n}
.
Thus, following Hedberg’s method, as in
[
3
, pp. 18–20]
, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{2}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha}\int_{|y|>2r}|f(y)||y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy=2^{n-\alpha}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\int\limits_{2^{k}\cdot 2r<|y|\leq 2^{k+1}\cdot 2r}|f(y)||y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k+1}r)^{\alpha-n}\int_{|y|\leq 2^{k+2}r}|f(y)|\,dy.

Then, from Lemma
1
, it follows that

\displaystyle I_{2}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha+1}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k+1}r)^{\alpha-n}|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda}\,\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{2^{k+2}r}}\|\chi_{B_{2^{k+2}r}}\|_{\Phi^{\ast},\lambda,B_{2^{k+2}r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n-\alpha+1}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k+1}r)^{\alpha-n}|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda}\,\frac{\Phi^{-1}(|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda-1})}{|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{2n-\alpha+1}v_{n}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k+1}r)^{\alpha}\,\Phi^{-1}(|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{2n-\alpha+1}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n\ln{2}}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}|B_{2^{k+1}r}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(|B_{2^{k+2}r}|^{\lambda-1})\int\limits_{|B_{2^{k+1}r}|}^{|B_{2^{k+2}r}|}\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{2n-\alpha+2}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{|B_{2^{k+1}r}|}^{|B_{2^{k+2}r}|}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\int\limits_{|B_{2r}|}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\frac{dt}{t},~{\rm where}~C_{6}=2^{2n-\alpha+2}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}.

Applying assumption (
1
) we get

I_{2}f(x)\leq C_{1}\,C_{6}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(|B_{2r}|^{\mu-1}).

Thus, for
x\in B_{r}^{1}
, we obtain

|I_{\alpha}f(x)|\leq I_{1}f(x)+I_{2}f(x)\leq 2\,C_{7}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(|B_{2r}|^{\mu-1}),

where
C_{7}=C_{1}\cdot\max\{4\cdot 2^{n}\cdot C_{0}\,C_{5},C_{6}\}
. Since
2^{n(\mu-1)}<1
it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}^{1}}\Psi\left(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{2\,C_{7}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq|B_{r}^{1}||B_{2r}|^{\mu-1}\leq 2^{n(\mu-1)}|B_{r}|^{\mu}<|B_{r}|^{\mu}.

Let now
x\in B_{r}^{2}.
We can write
I_{\alpha}f(x)
as follows

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}f(x)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|\leq\delta}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy+\int_{|x-y|>\delta}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle I_{3}f(x)+I_{4}f(x),

where
\delta
is defined in the following way

\Phi\left(\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{0}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)=\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{\delta}|}.

(4)

Since
x\in B_{r}^{2}
it follows that
|B_{\delta}|<|B_{r}|
.
Hedberg’s pointwise estimate from Lemma 2 to
I_{3}f(x)
gives

I_{3}f(x)\leq C_{H}\,\delta^{\alpha}Mf(x)=C_{H}\,(\delta^{n}v_{n})^{\alpha/n}\,v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}Mf(x)=v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}\,C_{H}\,|B_{\delta}|^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}Mf(x),

and from the assumption (
2

I_{3}f(x)\leq v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}\,C_{2}\,C_{H}\frac{\Psi^{-1}(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|})}{\Phi^{-1}(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{\delta}|})}Mf(x).

Next, since equality (
4
) holds it follows that

I_{3}f(x)\leq v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}\,C_{0}\,C_{2}\,C_{H}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|}).

Applying again Hedberg’s method for
I_{4}f(x)
we obtain

\displaystyle I_{4}f(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|x-y|>\delta}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\,\int\limits_{2^{k}\delta<|x-y|\leq 2^{k+1}\delta}|f(y)||x-y|^{\alpha-n}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k}\delta)^{\alpha-n}\int\limits_{|x-y|\leq 2^{k+1}\delta}|f(y)|\,dy

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k}\delta)^{\alpha-n}\int\limits_{B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}}|f(y)|\chi_{B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)}(y)\,dy,

where
B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}
is the smallest ball with the centre at origin containing
B(x,2^{k+1}\delta).

From Lemma
1
, using the fact that
B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}\cap B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)=B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)
, we get

\displaystyle I_{4}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k}\delta)^{\alpha-n}|B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}|^{\lambda}\|f\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}}\|\chi_{B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)}\|_{\Phi^{\ast},\lambda,B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k}\delta)^{\alpha-n}\,|B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)|\,\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}|^{\lambda}}{|B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)|}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n+1}v_{n}}{n\ln{2}}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}(2^{k}\delta)^{\alpha}\,\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}|^{\lambda}}{|B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)|}\right)\int\limits_{|B(x,2^{k}\delta)|}^{|B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)|}\frac{dt}{t}.

Since
|x|\leq r
and
2^{k}\delta\leq(\frac{t}{v_{n}})^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq 2^{k+1}\delta
it follows that

\displaystyle|B_{|x|+2^{k+1}\delta}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle v_{n}(r+2^{k+1}\delta)^{n}\leq v_{n}\left(r+2\frac{t^{\frac{1}{n}}}{v_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}}\right)^{n}=(v_{n}^{\frac{1}{n}}r+2t^{\frac{1}{n}})^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(|B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{n}}+2t^{\frac{1}{n}})^{n}\leq 2^{n}(|B_{r}|^{\frac{1}{n}}+t^{\frac{1}{n}})^{n}\leq 4^{n}\max\{|B_{r}|,t\}.

So using the concavity of
\Phi^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle I_{4}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n+1}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n\ln{2}}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\infty}\int\limits_{|B(x,2^{k}\delta)|}^{|B(x,2^{k+1}\delta)|}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{4^{\lambda n}(\max\{|B_{r}|,t\})^{\lambda}}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{4^{\lambda n}2^{n+1}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n\ln{2}}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\int\limits_{|B_{\delta}|}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{(\max\{|B_{r}|,t\})^{\lambda}}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{8}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\left[\int\limits_{|B_{\delta}|}^{|B_{r}|}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{t}\right)\frac{dt}{t}+\int\limits_{|B_{r}|}^{\infty}t^{\frac{\alpha}{n}}\Phi^{-1}(t^{\lambda-1})\frac{dt}{t}\right],

where
C_{8}=\frac{4^{\lambda n}2^{n+1}v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n\ln{2}}\leq\frac{4^{n}\cdot 2^{n+2}\cdot v_{n}^{1-\frac{\alpha}{n}}}{n}
.
Based on the assumptions of (
1
), (
2
) and the fact that
|B_{\delta}|<|B_{r}|
we get

\displaystyle I_{4}f(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{8}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\left[C_{2}\,\Psi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|}\right)+C_{1}\,\Psi^{-1}(|B_{r}|^{\mu-1})\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{8}\,(C_{2}+C_{1})\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|}\right).

Thus, for
x\in B_{r}^{2}
we obtain

\displaystyle|I_{\alpha}f(x)|\leq I_{3}f(x)+I_{4}f(x)\leq C_{9}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\Psi^{-1}\left(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|}\right),

with
C_{9}=v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}\,C_{0}\,C_{2}\,C_{H}+C_{8}\,(C_{1}+C_{2})
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}^{2}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{9}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}{|B_{\delta}|}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|B_{r}|^{\mu-\lambda}\int_{B_{r}}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{Mf(x)}{C_{0}\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B_{r}|^{\mu-\lambda}\int_{B_{r}}\Phi\Bigl(\frac{Mf(x)}{\|Mf\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx\leq|B_{r}|^{\mu}.

Finally, since
B_{r}=B_{r}^{1}\cup B_{r}^{2}
and the last two sets are disjoint, and by the convexity of
\Psi
it follows that

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{r}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{B_{r}^{1}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx+\int\limits_{B_{r}^{2}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{B_{r}^{1}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{2\,C_{7}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{B_{r}^{2}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{C_{9}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|B_{r}|^{\mu},

where
C_{3}=2\max\{2\,C_{7},C_{9}\}.
Hence,
\|I_{\alpha}f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}\leq C_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}
.

(ii) Similarly to the previous case, we will present
B_{r}
as a union of two disjoint subsets
B_{r}=B_{r}^{1}\cup B_{r}^{2}
,
where

and
B_{r}^{2}

Then we get

\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{c_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{1}})(x)|}{4\,c_{7}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)+\frac{1}{2}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|I_{\alpha}(f\chi_{B_{r}^{2}})(x)|}{2\,c_{9}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr)=:\frac{1}{2}(I_{5}+I_{6}),

where
c_{3}=2\,\max\{4\,c_{7},2\,c_{9}\},c_{7}=C_{1}\max\{4\cdot 2^{n}\cdot c_{0}\,C_{5},C_{6}\},c_{9}=v_{n}^{-\alpha/n}\,c_{0}\,C_{2}\,C_{H}+C_{8}(C_{1}+C_{2})
.
We follow the same calculations as in the proof of Theorem 3(ii) in

3
]
and we get

d\left(\Psi\left(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{c_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right),u\right)\leq d(I_{5},u)+d(I_{6},u)

and

\sup\limits_{u>0}\frac{\Psi(u)}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\,d\left(\frac{|I_{\alpha}f(x)|}{c_{3}\,\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}},u\right)\leq\sup\limits_{u>0}\frac{u}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\,d(I_{5},u)+\sup\limits_{u>0}\frac{u}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\,d(I_{6},u),

where we used the property

for any

From the first part of the proof of this theorem for any

For

Two properties of central Morrey–Orlicz spaces

Properties of Morrey and central Morrey spaces were considered by several authors (for example V. I. Burenkov,
V. S. Guliyev, E. Nakai, Y. Sawano and others). Here we will present some properties of central Morrey–Orlicz
spaces. It is known that
M^{p,\lambda}(0)\neq\{0\}
if and only if
\lambda\geq 0
(see
[
1
]
). In the next
proposition we describe when the central Morrey–Orlicz space
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
is nontrivial.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\Phi
be an Orlicz function and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.
The space
M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)\neq\{0\}
if
and only if
\lambda\geq 0.

Proof.

Let first
\lambda<0
and
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0),
such that
f\not\equiv 0.
Then

\sup\limits_{r>0}\Bigl\|\frac{f}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}=1

and therefore

\Bigl\|\frac{f}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\Bigr\|_{\Phi,\lambda,B_{r}}\leq 1\,\,\text{for all}\,\,r>0.

Thus,

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int\limits_{B_{r}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq 1\,\,\text{for all}\,\,r>0.

On the other hand, there exists
t_{0}>0,
such that
\int\limits_{B_{t_{0}}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\,dx>0

and for any
r>t_{0}
and
\lambda<0
we have

\int\limits_{B_{t_{0}}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq\int\limits_{B_{r}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq|B_{r}|^{\lambda}\rightarrow 0\,\,\text{as}\,\,r\rightarrow\infty,

which means that
f(x)=0
on
B_{t_{0}}
and we are done.

Let now
\lambda\geq 0.
Then we will show that there exists
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0),
such that
f\not\equiv 0
.
We follow ideas from
[
8
, Proposition 1]
and consider function
f_{R}(x)=\chi_{B(x_{R},1)}(x),
where
R>1

and
x_{R}=(R,0,...,0).
We will show that
f\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)
for any
\lambda\geq 0.
In our previous paper
we have shown that

\|f_{R}\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}=\sup\limits_{r>R-1}\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{R},1)|})},

for details we refer to the proof of Theorem 2 in
[
3
]
. Since
|B_{r}\cap B(x_{R},1)|\leq|B(x_{R},1)|=v_{n}
it follows
that
\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{R},1)|}\geq\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{v_{n}}
and
\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{R},1)|})}\leq\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{v_{n}}\bigr)}.
Thus,

\displaystyle\|f_{R}\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}=\sup\limits_{r>R-1}\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{|B_{r}\cap B(x_{R},1)|}\bigr)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{r>R-1}\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}{v_{n}}\bigr)}=\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}\bigl(\frac{|B_{R-1}|^{\lambda}}{v_{n}}\bigr)},

where the last equality is true since
\lambda\geq 0.
Therefore,
f_{R}\in M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0).

Next we consider inclusion properties of central Morrey–Orlicz spaces. In the case of classical Morrey and classical central
Morrey spaces it is known that if
1\leq p<q<\infty,0\leq\mu<\lambda<1
and

\frac{1-\lambda}{p}=\frac{1-\mu}{q}
, then

M^{q,\mu}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}M^{p,\lambda}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})\quad{\rm and}\quad M^{q,\mu}(0)\overset{1}{\hookrightarrow}M^{p,\lambda}(0).

(5)

Both inclusions are proper (see, for example,
[
5
]
). We also note that the second embedding in (
5
)
is also true for
1<\lambda<\mu
. We have shown in
[
3
]
that the embeddings (
5
) follow
by the Hölder–Rogers inequality with
\frac{q}{p}>1
. In the next theorem we present inclusion properties of central
Morrey–Orlicz spaces.

Proposition 2
.

Let
\Phi
and
\Psi
be Orlicz functions,
0\leq\lambda,\mu<1
. Then
M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)\hookrightarrow M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)

if and only if there are constants
A_{1},A_{2}>0,
such that

(i)

\Phi(\frac{u}{A_{1}})\leq\Psi(u)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\mu-1}}~\text{for all}~u>0

and

(ii)

\Phi(\frac{u}{A_{2}})\leq\Psi(u)r^{\lambda-\mu}~\text{for all}~u,r>0,
satisfying
\Psi^{-1}(r^{\mu-1})<u
.

Proof.

Let first
f\in M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)
,
f\not\equiv 0,

B_{r}
be any open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and functions
\Phi
and
\Psi

satisfy conditions (i) and (ii). Then

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\int_{B_{r}}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq 1

and so
\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)<\infty
a.e. in
B_{r}
. We divide
B_{r}
into two disjoint subsets

B_{r}^{3}:=\Bigl\{x\in B_{r}:|B_{r}|^{\mu-1}<\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)<\infty~\text{a.e.}\Bigr\}

and

B_{r}^{4}:=\Bigl\{x\in B_{r}:|B_{r}|^{\mu-1}\geq\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)~\text{a.e.}\Bigr\}.

Let us denote
t=|B_{r}|
and
u=\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}
. Then, for
x\in B_{r}^{3}
we have
0<t^{\mu-1}<\Psi(u)

and from (ii) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}^{3}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{A_{2}\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}^{3}}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)|B_{r}|^{\lambda-\mu}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\int_{B_{r}^{3}}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\mu}}\int_{B_{r}}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx\leq 1.

For
x\in B_{r}^{4}
we get
0<\Psi(u)\leq t^{\mu-1}
and from (i) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}^{4}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{A_{1}\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}^{4}}\Psi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\mu-1}}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}^{4}}\left(|B_{r}|^{\mu-1}\right)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\mu-1}}\,dx=\frac{|B_{r}^{4}|}{|B_{r}|}\leq 1.

Thus,

\frac{1}{|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\int_{B_{r}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{2\max(A_{1},A_{2})\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx

\leq\frac{1}{2|B_{r}|^{\lambda}}\left[\int_{B_{r}^{3}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{A_{1}\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx+\int_{B_{r}^{4}}\Phi\left(\frac{|f(x)|}{A_{2}\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}}\right)\,dx\right]\leq 1

and so
\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\leq 2\max(A_{1},A_{2})\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}
.

Let now
\|f\|_{M^{\Phi,\lambda}(0)}\leq C\|f\|_{M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)}
for any
f\in M^{\Psi,\mu}(0)
and some
constant
C>0.
First for any
t>0
we consider
f_{t}(x)=\chi_{B_{t}}(x).
Then, using Lemma
1
(iii) we obtain

\frac{1}{\Phi^{-1}(|B_{t}|^{\lambda-1})}\leq\frac{C}{\Psi^{-1}(|B_{t}|^{\mu-1})},

which also means that
\Psi^{-1}(s^{\mu-1})\leq C\Phi^{-1}(s^{\lambda-1})
for all
s>0
or
s^{\mu-1}\leq\Psi(C\Phi^{-1}(s^{\lambda-1}))
.
By change of variables
\Phi^{-1}(s^{\lambda-1})=u
we get

\Phi(u)^{\frac{\mu-1}{\lambda-1}}\leq\Psi(Cu)~{\rm for\,all}~u>0,

so we have condition (i) with
A_{1}=C
.

For the proof of the second part, i.e. to prove the necessity of the condition (ii), we refer to the proof of Lemma 4.12 and Theorem 4.1
in
[
7
]
and to Theorem 4.9 and Lemma 4.10 in
[

]

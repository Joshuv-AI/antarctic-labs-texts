Envelopes of straight line families in the plane
Takashi Nishimura

Abstract
. There is a widespread method to represent the envelope when a given hyperplane family creates an envelope. However, one sometimes encounters cases when the widespread method fails to represent the desired envelope precisely, and is confused. At the same time, one wants to find a correct method to draw the envelope precisely. In this article, focused on straight line families in the plane, an easy to understand explanation is given on the recently discovered correct method to represent the envelope precisely. Moreover, it is explained when and why the widespread method fails to represent the precise shape of envelope as well.

1.
Introduction

We start from an elementary example.

Example 1
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the mapping
defined by
f(t)=\left(t,\sin t\right)
.
The regular curve
f

gives a parametrization of the non-singular curve

\mathcal{C}=\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,Y=\sin X\right\}.

The affine tangent line
L_{t}
to
\mathcal{C}

at a point
\left(t,\sin t\right)
may be defined by

\left(X-t,Y-\sin t\right)\cdot\left(-\cos t,1\right)=0,

where the dot in the center stands for the standard scalar product of
two vectors
\left(X-t,Y-\sin t\right)
and

\left(-\cos t,1\right)
in the vector space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Since the straight line family
\left\{L_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

is the affine tangent line family to
\mathcal{C}
, we believe
that the sine curve
\mathcal{C}
must be
an envelope of
\left\{L_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. Thus, by using the widespread method to represent
the envelope of
\left\{L_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

(
in the case of Example
1
, the widespread method
means just to calculate the concrete form of the discriminant set

\mathcal{D}
defined
as follows. For more details on
the widespread method,
for instance refer to
[
1
]
),
we try to confirm that
\mathcal{C}
is actually an envelope of

\left\{L_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.
Set

F\left(X,Y,t\right)=\left(X-t,Y-\sin t\right)\cdot\left(-\cos t,1\right)=-\cos tX+Y+t\cos t-\sin t.

We have the following.

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>\left|\>\exists t\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ s.t. }F(X,Y,t)=\frac{\partial F}{\partial t}(X,Y,t)=0\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>\left|\>\exists t\in\mathbb{R}\mbox{ s.t. }-\cos tX+Y+t\cos t-\sin t=\sin t(X-t)=0\right.\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>\left|\>Y=X-2k\pi\;(k\in\mathbb{Z})\mbox{ or }Y=-X+(2k+1)\pi\;(k\in\mathbb{Z})\mbox{ or }Y=\sin X\right.\right\}

\displaystyle\supsetneqq

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}.

Faced with the fact that
\mathcal{C}
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{D}
,
we are confused.
In addition, we want to know a correct method
to represent
\mathcal{C}
precisely.
Let us review the correct method given in
[
7
]
in the case of
this example.
There are three steps. The first step is to normalize the defining equation

F=0
. That is to say, replace
the defining equation
F=0
with a new one
G=0

having the form

G(X,Y,t)=X\cos\theta(t)+Y\sin\theta(t)-a(t).

Then, we have (for example)

G(X,Y,t)=X\cos\theta(t)+Y\sin\theta(t)-a(t)=X\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}+Y\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}-\frac{-t\cos t+\sin t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0},}

which implies that

\cos\theta(t)=\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\;\sin\theta(t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\;a(t)=\frac{-t\cos t+\sin t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}.

The second step (the most important step)
is to find a
C^{\infty}
function
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}

satisfying

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=b(t)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t).

Elementary calculations show

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=\frac{\sin t\left(t+\cos t\sin t\right)}{\left(\cos^{2}t+1\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{-\sin t}{\cos^{2}t+1}.

Hence, we have
b(t)=\frac{-\left(t+\cos t\sin t\right)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}
.
The final step is just to substitute

a(t)=\frac{-t\cos t+\sin t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\;b(t)=\frac{-\left(t+\cos t\sin t\right)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\;\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)=\left(\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\right)

into

a(t)\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)+b(t)\left(-\sin\theta(t),\cos\theta(t)\right).

Then, we have the desired parametrization of
\mathcal{C}
as follows.

\displaystyle a(t)\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)+b(t)\left(-\sin\theta(t),\cos\theta(t)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-t\cos t+\sin t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\left(\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\right)+\frac{-\left(t+\cos t\sin t\right)}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\left(\frac{-1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t\cos^{2}t-\sin t\cos t+t+\cos t\sin t}{\cos^{2}t+1},\frac{-t\cos t+\sin t+t\cos t+\cos^{2}t\sin t}{\cos^{2}t+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{t\left(\cos^{2}t+1\right)}{\cos^{2}t+1},\frac{\sin t\left(1+\cos^{2}t\right)}{\cos^{2}t+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(t,\sin t\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t).

2.
Preliminaries

Any straight line
L
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
may be defined
as follows, where
\theta,a
are real numbers.

L=\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;X\cos\theta+Y\sin\theta=a\}.

Any straight line family
\mathcal{L}
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
may be defined
as
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,
where
\theta,a:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are

C^{\infty}
functions and
L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}
is
a straight line as follows.

L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}=\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\;|\;X\cos\theta(t)+Y\sin\theta(t)=a(t)\}.

Definition 1
.

Given a straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,
the mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
defined by

\nu(t)=\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)

is called the
Gauss mapping
of

\mathcal{L}
.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family
in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
A
C^{\infty}
mapping
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called
an
envelope created by

\mathcal{L}

if the following two hold for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

\displaystyle{\rm(a)}

\displaystyle\frac{df}{dt}(t)\cdot\nu(t)=0,

\displaystyle{\rm(b)}

\displaystyle f(t)\in L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}.

Thus, an envelope is a
C^{\infty}
mapping giving a solution of the
first order linear differential equation (a) with one constraint condition
(b).

Example 2
.

Let
L_{t}
and
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be as in Example
1
.
Thus,

L_{t}=\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,-\cos tX+Y+t\cos t-\sin t=0\right\},\quad f(t)=\left(t,\sin t\right).

Then, it is easily seen that conditions (a), (b) in Definition
2

are satisfied. Hence, by definition,

f
must be an envelope of the straight line family

\left\{L_{t}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

Definition 3
.

A mapping
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called
a
frontal curve
if there exists a mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}

such that the following equality holds for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

\frac{df}{dt}(t)\cdot\nu(t)=0.

The mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
given above is called
the
Gauss mapping
of the frontal
f
.

By definition, any envelope created by a straight line family is a frontal curve.
Conversely, again by definition,
any frontal curve
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

with Gauss mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}

is an envelope of the straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,
where
\nu(t)=\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)
and

a(t)=f(t)\cdot\nu(t)
.
Hence, these two notions are essentially the same
although the notion of frontal curve has only
recently been recognized and investigated.
As an excellent survey article on frontal curve,

[
4
]
is recommended to readers.

3.
Answer to the existence problem on envelopes

The following is the key notion in this article.

Definition 4

(
[
7
]
)
.

A straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is said to be
creative
if there exists
a
C^{\infty}
function
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

[formula]

\underline{\frac{da}{dt}(t)=b(t)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)}

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
The function
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
creator
.

Theorem 1

(
[
7
]
)
.

A straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
creates an envelope if and only if it is creative.

Remark 1
.

(1)

In Example
1
,
both
\cos\theta(t)
and
\sin\theta(t)
are
even functions while
b(t)
is an odd function. Hence,

b(t)
cannot be
obtained as the pullbuck
(by
\nu
)
of a
C^{\infty}
section of the cotangent bundle

T^{*}S^{1}\to S^{1}
.

(2)

In Example
1
, we already calculated

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{-\sin t}{\cos^{2}t+1}
.
This implies that
t=k\pi

(k\in\mathbb{Z})
are singular points of
the Gauss mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
in Example

1
.
Thus, as one can find in p.492 of
[
9
]
,
for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
the dimension of the quotient vector space

V_{k}(\nu)/w\nu\left(V_{k}(1)\right)
is greater than or equal to 1,
where
V_{k}(\nu)
is the vector space consisting of cotangent vector
field germs
\xi:(\mathbb{R},k\pi)\to T^{*}S^{1}
along
\nu:(\mathbb{R},k\pi)\to S^{1}

and
w\nu\left(V_{k}(1)\right)
is
the vector space consisting of composition germs

\eta\circ\nu:(\mathbb{R},k\pi)\to T^{*}S^{1}
of the Gauss mapping germ

\nu:(\mathbb{R},k\pi)\to S^{1}
with
C^{\infty}
section germs

\eta:(S^{1},\nu(k\pi))\to T^{*}S^{1}
.
Therefore, the above fact “
b(t)
cannot be obtained as the pullbuck
of a
C^{\infty}
section of the cotangent bundle

T^{*}S^{1}\to S^{1}
by
\nu
”  is not a surprizing fact.

(3)

By the above fact “
b(t)
cannot be obtained as the pullbuck
of a
C^{\infty}
section of the cotangent bundle
T^{*}S^{1}\to S^{1}

by
\nu
” , it seems that Contact Geometry is useless for
the proof of Theorem
1
. Theorem
1
is proved by
the anti-orthotomic technique developed in
[
5
]
.
Moreover, not only Theorem
1
but also Theorem
2
in
Section
4
and Theorem
3
in Section
5

can be obtained by the anti-orthotomic technique at once.

(4)

Just as the special case (
n=1
case) of
[
7
]

Remark 1.1(a), it is reasonable to say that the height function
a:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is

differentiable with respect to
the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
if there exists
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying

(*)
in Definition
4
.
Under this terminology,

the creator
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
may be
called as the
derived function
of the height function
a

with respect to
differentiation by the Gauss mapping
\nu
. Hence,
Theorem
1
may be replaced with the assertion

“A straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
creates an envelope
if and only if the height function

a:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is differentiable
with respect to
the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
”
.

Example 3
.

Set
\theta(t)=a(t)=0
.
In this case,

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{da}{dt}(t)=0
.
Hence, an arbitrary
C^{\infty}
function
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a creator,
that is to say, it
satisfies the equality

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=b(t)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)\quad(\forall t\in\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, the straight line family

\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
creates an envelope.

Example 4
.

Set
\theta(t)=0
,
a(t)=t
.
In this case,

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=0
and

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=1
.
Hence, there does not exist
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=b(t)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)\quad(\forall t\in\mathbb{R}).

Therefore, the straight line family

\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is not creative.

Example 5
.

Set
\theta(t)=t
,
a(t)=0
.
In this case,

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=1
and

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=0
.
Hence,

0=\frac{da}{dt}(t)=\left(\frac{\frac{da}{dt}(t)}{\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)}\right)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{0}{1}\times 1

holds.

Thus, by Theorem
1
,
the straight line family

\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
creates
an envelope.

More generally, for a given straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
,
suppose that the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
of
\mathcal{L}
is non-singular.
Then, we have

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=\left(\frac{\frac{da}{dt}(t)}{\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)}\right)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
. Thus, we have the following.

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
of
\mathcal{L}
is non-singular.
Then, the family
\mathcal{L}
is always creative.
Therefore, by Theorem
1
, it always creates an envelope.

Example 6
.

Set
\theta(t)=t^{2}
,
a(t)=0
.
In this case,

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=2t
and

\frac{da}{dt}(t)\equiv 0
.
Thus,
t=0
is a singular point of the Gauss mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
.
Nevertheless, we have

0=\frac{da}{dt}(t)=0\times\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t).

for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Thus, by Theorem
1
,
the straight line family

\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
creates
an envelope.

Example 7

(Evolute of the graph of
Y=\sin X
)
.

Consider the affine normal line to the graph of

Y=\sin X
at
\left(t,\sin t\right)
.
The defining equation of it may be

F\left(X,Y,t\right)=\left(X-t,Y-\sin t\right)\cdot\left(1,\cos t\right)=X+Y\cos t-t-\cos t\sin t=0.

Thus, the normalized defining equation
G(X,Y,t)=0
may be

G\left(X,Y,t\right)=X\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}}+Y\frac{\cos t}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}}-\frac{t+\cos t\sin t}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}}=0.

Hence, we have

(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t))=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}},\frac{\cos t}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}}\right)
and

a(t)=\frac{t+\cos t\sin t}{\sqrt{1+\cos^{2}t}}
.
By calculations, we have

\frac{da}{dt}(t)=\frac{\cos t\left(3\cos t+\cos^{3}t+t\sin t\right)}{\left(1+\cos^{2}t\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}},\quad\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{-\sin t}{1+\cos^{2}t}.

For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\frac{da}{dt}(k\pi)\neq 0

and
\frac{d\theta}{dt}(k\pi)=0
.
Thus, there are no creator
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
1
,
the straight line family

\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

does not create an envelope, namely,
the evolute of the graph of
Y=\sin X
does not exist.

4.
Answer to the uniqueness problem on envelopes

Theorem 2

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is creative.
Then,
\mathcal{L}
creates a unique envelope if and only if
the set consisting of regular points of the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
of
\mathcal{L}
is dense in
\mathbb{R}
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that the Gauss mapping

\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
of
\mathcal{L}
is non-singular.
Then, the family
\mathcal{L}
creates a unique envelope.

Example 8
.

As in Example
1
, set

\left(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t)\right)=\left(\frac{-\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}\right),\;a(t)=\frac{\sin t-t\cos t}{\sqrt{\cos^{2}t+1}}.

Then, in Example
1
, we confirmed

\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=\frac{-\sin t}{\cos^{2}t+1}
.
Thus, the set of regular points of the Gauss mapping is a dense set
in
\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
2
, the graph of
Y=\sin X
must be the
unique envelope.

Example 9
.

As in Example
3
, set
\theta(t)=a(t)=0
.
In Example
3
, we confirmed that
the straight line family

\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
creates an envelope by
Theorem
1
.
Since
\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)=0
, the set of regular points of
the Gauss mapping is empty. Thus, by Theorem
2
,
the created envelopes are not unique.

Example 10
.

As in Example
6
, set
\theta(t)=t^{2},\;a(t)=0
.
We already confirmed that

6.
When and why the widespread method fails

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Then, the normalized equation

G(X,Y,t)=X\cos\theta(t)+Y\sin\theta(t)-a(t)=0

is a defining equation of
\mathcal{L}
.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is creative.
Then, by Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-X\sin\theta(t)+Y\cos\theta(t)\right)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)-\frac{da}{dt}(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-X\sin\theta(t)+Y\cos\theta(t)-b(t)\right)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t).

Assume moreover that the Gauss mapping
\nu:\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}

is non-singular.
Then,
by definition,
it follows
\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t)\neq 0
for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus, for any fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
the Jacobian determinant of the linear mapping

\mathbb{R}^{2}\ni(X,Y)\mapsto\left(G(X,Y,t),\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t)\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

is non-zero. Hence, the solution of the system of linear equations
G(X,Y,t)=\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t)=0
must be a unique
2
-dimensional vector.
Therefore,
solving the system of equations
G(X,Y,t)=\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t)=0
gives the precise shape of envelope of

\mathcal{L}
.
Namely, the widespread method works well in this case
to represent the envelope precisely.

Hence we have the following.

Proposition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

be a straight line family in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that the Gauss mapping of
\mathcal{L}
is non-singular.
Then, the unique envelope created by the straight line family can be
directly obtained by the widespread method.

Nextly,
assume that the Gauss mapping
\nu
is singular at

t=t_{0}
.

Then, by definition, we have

\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t_{0})=\left(-X\sin\theta(t_{0})+Y\cos\theta(t_{0})-b(t_{0})\right)\frac{d\theta}{dt}(t_{0})=0,

which implies

\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>\left|\>G(X,Y,t_{0})=\frac{\partial G}{\partial t}(X,Y,t_{0})=0\right.\right\}=\left\{(X,Y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\>\left|\>G(X,Y,t_{0})=0\right.\right\}=L_{\left(\theta(t_{0}),a(t_{0})\right)}.

Thus, the widespread method fails to get the precise shape of envelope
in this case.

Therefore, the following proposition holds.

Proposition 2
.

Given a creative straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, suppose that
the Gauss mapping of
\mathcal{L}
is singular.
Then, the widespread method fails to get an envelope precisely
at and only at singular points of Gauss mapping.

These two propositions yield

Corollary 5
.

Given a creative straight line family

\mathcal{L}=\left\{L_{\left(\theta(t),a(t)\right)}\right\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}

in the plane
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
in order to get an envelope created by

\mathcal{L}
precisely,
the widespread method can be used when and only when
the Gauss mapping is non-singular.

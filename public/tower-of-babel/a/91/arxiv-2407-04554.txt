A Ramanujan bound for Drinfeld modular forms
Sjoerd de Vries

Abstract
We prove a Lefschetz trace formula for Böckle–Pink crystals on tame Deligne–Mumford stacks of finite type over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} and apply it to the crystal associated to the universal Drinfeld module. Combined with the Eichler–Shimura theory developed by Böckle, this leads to a trace formula for Hecke operators on Drinfeld modular forms. As an application, we deduce a Ramanujan bound on the traces of Hecke operators.

Introduction

Drinfeld modules of rank 2 are function field analogues of elliptic curves. Since their inception due to Drinfeld
[
Dri74
]
, much of the theory of elliptic curves has been translated to the function field setting. In particular, there is a theory of Drinfeld modular forms
[
Gos80
]
, which are defined as sections of certain line bundles on moduli spaces of Drinfeld modules. In
[
Böc02
]
, Böckle develops an Eichler–Shimura theory for Drinfeld modular forms, showing that one can realise spaces of Drinfeld cusp forms as the compactly supported cohomology of certain crystals.

In the classical setting, Eichler–Shimura theory naturally leads to a proof of the Ramanujan bound, which states the following. Let
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
be a congruence subgroup of level
N
, let
p
be a prime number not dividing
N
, and let
\alpha
be an eigenvalue of the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{p}
acting on the space
\operatorname{S}_{k}(\Gamma)
of cusp forms of weight
k
and level
\Gamma
. Then
|\alpha|\leq 2p^{(k-1)/2}
,
where
|\cdot|
denotes the complex absolute value. Equivalently, for all
n\geq 1
, we have

|\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{p}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k}(\Gamma))|\leq\dim\operatorname{S}_{k}(\Gamma)\cdot 2^{n}p^{n(k-1)/2}.

This bound was conjectured in 1916 by Ramanujan in the case
k=12
and
\Gamma=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Almost six decades later, it became a celebrated result of Deligne
[
Del69
,
Del74
]
. Let us sketch the idea of the proof. By Eichler–Shimura theory, cusp forms arise in the cohomology of certain
\ell
-adic local systems on moduli spaces of elliptic curves, and the Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{p}
acts locally at
p
as the sum of Frobenius and Verschiebung. Deligne’s idea was to prove the Ramanujan bound by combining Eichler–Shimura theory with the purity of cohomology which follows from the Weil conjectures (more specifically, the Riemann hypothesis).

In the function field setting, an analogous Ramanujan bound has previously been observed
[
Böc02
,
LM08
,
NR21
,
BV19
]
, but due to the lack of purity for cohomology of crystals, a proof of this fundamental inequality has remained elusive. In this paper, we explain how the purity argument can be circumvented by using the Lefschetz trace formula. The moduli interpretation of the points on modular curves then allows us to deduce the Ramanujan bound for Drinfeld modular forms from the Riemann hypothesis for Drinfeld modules. The statement of the Ramanujan bound in level 1 is as follows:

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
4.13
)
.

Let
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
denote the space of Drinfeld cusp forms of weight
k\geq 2
and type
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then for any prime
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
with residue field of size
\mathcal{P}
and any

, we have

|\Tr(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.00006pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})|_{\infty}\leq\mathcal{P}^{n\left(\frac{k}{2}+l-k\right)}.

Along the way, we prove several results of independent interest. For good coefficient rings

Let

Let

denote the space of Drinfeld cusp forms of weight

2.4

Descent

A (quasi-)coherent sheaf on a Deligne–Mumford stack is equivalent to a (quasi-)coherent sheaf with a descent datum on an étale cover. In this subsection, we prove that the same is true for crystals. Throughout,

Proposition 2.16

(Descent for

This follows from descent for (quasi-)coherent sheaves and unwinding definitions. In particular, a morphism

\tau

\mathfrak{X}
if and only if it is compatible with the descent data.

Corollary 2.17
.

The pullback functor
\pi^{*}\colon\textsf{Coh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)\to\textsf{Coh}_{\tau}(X,B)
is exact and conservative. Moreover,
\underline{\mathcal{F}}

\pi^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}

Using the equivalence from Proposition

as the functor sending

\tau

\tau

\widehat{\mathcal{F}}:=\varinjlim\sigma_{*}^{\bullet}\tau
for the direct limit. The adjoint of the canonical isomorphism
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}\to\sigma_{*}\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
gives
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
the structure of a
\tau
-sheaf, which we denote by
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}
. We call
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}
the
perfection
of
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
. The assignment
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\mapsto\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}
yields a functor
\text{perf}\colon\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)\to\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)
.

One easily sees that
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}=0
if
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is nilpotent. More generally, if
f
is a nil-isomorphism, then
\text{perf}(f)
is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.19
.

The induced functor
\overline{\text{perf}}\colon\textsf{QCrys}(\mathfrak{X},B)\to\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)
is an exact, fully faithful right adjoint to the localisation functor.

Proof.

Exactness follows from the exactness of filtered direct limits in
\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)
, cf.
[
Stacks
, Tag 0781]
. The rest is formal: the adjunction is proved as in
[
BP09
, Prop. 3.4.8]
, and the counit is an isomorphism by
[
BP09
, Prop. 3.3.13]
; the latter is equivalent to the right adjoint being fully faithful.
∎

Proposition 2.20

(Descent for crystals)
.

Let
\textsf{C}(-)\in\{\textsf{Crys}(-,B),\textsf{QCrys}(-,B)\}
. Then
\pi^{*}
induces an equivalence of categories
\textsf{C}(\mathfrak{X})\xrightarrow{\sim}\textsf{C}(X\to\mathfrak{X})
.

Proof.

We first extend the functor
\overline{\text{perf}}
to the categories of descent data. Note that for
i\in\{1,2\}
, we have a natural isomorphism
\widehat{\text{pr}^{*}_{i}\underline{\mathcal{F}}}\xrightarrow{\sim}\text{pr}^{*}_{i}\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{F}}}
, since
\text{pr}_{i}\colon X\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X\to X
is flat and hence
\text{pr}_{i}^{*}\sigma_{*}\cong\sigma_{*}\text{pr}_{i}^{*}
. It is then clear that a descent datum
\psi
of objects in
C
induces a descent datum
\hat{\psi}
of their perfections, and that a morphism
f
in
\textsf{C}(X\to\mathfrak{X})
induces a morphism
\hat{f}
in
\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(X\to\mathfrak{X},B)
.
Since the perfection functor is fully faithful on
\textsf{C}(X)
and
\textsf{C}(X\times_{\mathfrak{X}}X)
by Lemma
2.19
,
so is the perfection functor on
\textsf{C}(X\to\mathfrak{X})
.

Now consider the diagram

\textsf{C}(\mathfrak{X})
\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(\mathfrak{X},B)
\textsf{C}(X\to\mathfrak{X})
\textsf{QCoh}_{\tau}(X\to\mathfrak{X},B)
\textsf{QCrys}(\mathfrak{X},B)
\textsf{QCrys}(X\to\mathfrak{X},B)
q
q
\overline{\text{perf}}
\overline{\text{perf}}
\pi^{*}
\pi^{*}_{\textsf{Q}}

where
q
denotes localisation. By the above, the perfection functors are fully faithful. Since
\pi_{\textsf{Q}}^{*}
is an equivalence by Proposition
2.16
,
\pi^{*}
is also fully faithful. Moreover, we have a natural isomorphism
q\circ\overline{\text{perf}}\cong\text{id}
by Lemma
2.19
. Hence a simple diagram chase, along with the fact that
\textsf{C}(X\to\mathfrak{X})
is a full subcategory of
\textsf{QCrys}(X\to\mathfrak{X},B)
, shows that

is essentially surjective and hence an equivalence.
∎

We record the following consequence, which is proved in the same way as Corollary
2.17
.

Corollary 2.21
.

The functor
\pi^{*}\colon\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)\to\textsf{Crys}(X,B)
is exact and conservative. ∎

2.5

Derived categories of crystals

An important aspect of the theory of crystals are the functors
f^{*},f_{!}
and
\otimes
. Together, these establish a pre-6-functor formalism in the sense of
[
Man22
, Appendix A.5]
.
Our primary goal is to apply the theory of crystals to the moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}_{r}
. For this reason, we study the bounded derived category
D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))

of crystals on a tame Deligne–Mumford stack
\mathfrak{X}
. One could mimic the constructions from
[
BP09
]
to construct the derived functors on
D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))
directly. Instead we will, where possible, apply the results of
[
Man22
]
to construct the functors abstractly.

Recall that a morphism
f\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
is called
compactifiable
if it can be factored as
f=\overline{f}\circ j
, where
j
is an open immersion and
\overline{f}
is proper.

Theorem 2.22
.

Let
f\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
be a morphism between Deligne–Mumford stacks of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
B\to B^{\prime}
be a ring map. Then we have the following functors:

\displaystyle f^{*}

\displaystyle\colon D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{Y},B));

\displaystyle-\otimes^{L}-

\displaystyle\colon D^{-}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))\times D^{-}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))\longrightarrow D^{-}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B));

\displaystyle-\otimes^{L}_{B}B^{\prime}

\displaystyle\colon D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B^{\prime})).

If
f
is proper, we also have a derived pushforward

Rf_{*}\colon D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{Y},B))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)),

and if
f
is compactifiable, we have a proper pushforward

Rf_{!}\colon D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{Y},B))\longrightarrow D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)).

These functors satisfy all usual compatibilities, including the base change isomorphism and projection formula.

Proof.

By
[
BP09
, Chapter 6]
, the theorem is true when
f
is a morphism of schemes. We use
[
Man22
, A.5]
to extend it to representable morphisms of stacks, as follows. With notation as in loc. cit., we let
(\mathcal{C},E)
be the geometric setup given by
\mathcal{C}=\textsf{Sch}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}
and
E
is the class of compactifiable morphisms. One easily checks that the pair
(I,P)
, where
I
is the class of open immersions and
P
is the class of proper morphisms, forms a suitable decomposition of
E
as in Def. A.5.9 of loc. cit. Let
\mathcal{D}\colon\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\to\textsf{Cat}_{\infty}^{\otimes}
be the functor sending
X\mapsto D^{-}(\textsf{Crys}(X,B))
and
f\mapsto f^{*}
. Then Prop. A.5.10 in loc. cit. yields a pre-6-functor formalism
\mathcal{D}\colon\text{Corr}(\mathcal{C})_{E,all}\to\textsf{Cat}_{\infty}
. One can do the same with
\mathcal{D}(X)
replaced with
D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B))
(cf. Definition
3.1
), by
[
BP09
, Chapter 7]
, or with
D^{-}(\textsf{QCrys}(X,B))
.

Let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be the category of Deligne–Mumford stacks over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let
E^{\prime}
denote the class of compactifiable morphisms which are representable by schemes. Then
[
Man22
, Prop. A.5.16]
yields the desired pre-6-functor formalism for crystals on stacks, which automatically satisfies the claimed compatibilities. The only difference is that the functors thus obtained are defined on the
bounded above
derived category. To see that boundedness is preserved by
f^{*}
and
Rf_{!}
, we apply Corollary
2.21
. It implies that if
\pi\colon X\to\mathfrak{X}
is an étale cover and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{-}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))
satisfies
\pi^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(X,B))
, then
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B))
; combining this with proper base change yields the claim. Note that so far,
Rf_{!}
is only defined for representable
f
.

To define
Rf_{!}
for compactifiable morphisms, it suffices to define
Rf_{*}
for proper
f
. To see this, consider a decomposition
f=\bar{f}\circ j
with
\bar{f}
proper and
j
an open immersion. Then we may define
Rf_{!}:=R\bar{f}_{*}\circ j_{!}
, noting that
Rj_{!}=j_{!}
is exact. By the same arguments as in
[
BP09
, §6.7]
, the assignment
f\mapsto Rf_{!}
is then natural in
f
and does not depend on the chosen compactification; moreover, if
f
is representable by schemes, this definition of
Rf_{!}
coincides with the previous one.

To define
Rf_{*}
for proper
f
, note that this functor already exists on the level of coherent sheaves
[
Ols16
, Thm 11.6.1]
.
It can be extended to
\tau
-sheaves by virtue of the base change morphism
\sigma^{*}f^{*}\to f^{*}\sigma^{*}
. This functor
Rf_{*}
on
\tau
-sheaves preserves nilpotence, so it induces a functor
Rf_{*}
on crystals which behaves as one expects.

The last functor
-\otimes^{L}_{B}B^{\prime}
falls outside of the scope of
[
Man22
]
, but is simple to describe: it is induced by the extension of scalars functor on the underlying sheaves, and is straightforward to construct for crystals on stacks. This completes the proof.
∎

3

Lefschetz trace formula

In this section, we define
l
-series of flat crystals on tame Deligne–Mumford stacks. In the case of schemes, the
l
-series is the logarithmic derivative of the crystalline
L
-function defined in
[
BP09
]
. Our main result is a Lefschetz trace formula which relates the
l
-series of the fibers of a crystal to the
l
-series of its compactly supported cohomology, cf. Theorem
3.9
.

3.1

Flatness

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
In order to begin discussing
l
-series of
B
-crystals, one needs to isolate a class of crystals for which it makes sense to talk about the trace of
\tau
when considered as a
B
-linear map. This leads to the notion of flat crystals.

Definition 3.1
.

A crystal
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)
is
flat
if the functor
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\otimes-\colon\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)\to\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)
is exact. The full subcategory of flat crystals is denoted by
\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathfrak{X},B)
.

Flatness is preserved by all the derived functors from Theorem
2.22
,
and the derived tensor product
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\otimes^{L}\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}
of two bounded complexes
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathfrak{X},B))
lies again in
D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathfrak{X},B))
.

The following result shows that flatness is a pointwise property, and hence the theory of flat crystals over stacks is analogous to the theory for schemes.

Lemma 3.2
.

A crystal
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}(\mathfrak{X},B)
is flat if and only if
x^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is flat for every
x\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.

Proof.

Let
\pi\colon X\to\mathfrak{X}
be an étale cover by a scheme. By Corollary
2.21
and the fact that pullback commutes with tensor product,
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is flat if and only if
\pi^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is flat.
Since moreover every
x\colon\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})\to\mathfrak{X}
lifts to a closed point of
X
after a finite field extension, the result follows from
[
BP09
, Prop. 7.2.6 and Cor. 4.6.3]
.
∎

The following result is
[
BP09
, Prop. 9.3.4]
. It implies that flatness is related to being pointwise locally free, and will allow us to define crystalline
L
-functions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
B
is artinian, and let
k/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be finite. Let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}(\Spec(k),B)
.

1.

The perfection
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}
from Construction
2.18
is naturally isomorphic to the direct summand
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{ss}\subset\underline{\mathcal{F}}
on which
\tau
acts as an isomorphism. In particular,
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}\in\textsf{Coh}_{\tau}(\Spec(k),B)
.

2.

\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is flat if and only if the underlying sheaf
\widehat{\mathcal{F}}
of
\underline{\widehat{\mathcal{F}}}
is locally free.
\Box

3.2

L
-functions and
l
-series of crystals

From now on, we assume that
B
is a
good coefficient ring
as defined in
[
BP09
, Def. 9.7.4]
. For example,
B
can be artinian or a normal integral domain; in particular,
A=\mathcal{O}_{C}(C\setminus\{\infty\})
is a good coefficient ring. If
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{n}
are the minimal primes of
B
, we denote by
Q_{B}:=B_{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus B_{\mathfrak{p}_{n}}
the quotient ring of
B
. Note that the inclusion
B\hookrightarrow Q_{B}
is flat, so the functor
-\otimes_{B}Q_{B}
on crystals is exact.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
be a flat
B
-crystal on a scheme
X
. For a closed point
x\in|X|
of degree
d
, denote by
i_{x}\colon\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})\to X
the corresponding inclusion. We now recall the
L
-function of a crystal, defined in
[
BP09
, Chapter 9]
.

Definition 3.4
.

Suppose
B
is an artinian
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra, and let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B)
. The
crystalline
L
-function
associated to
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is the power series

L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=\prod_{x\in|X|}\det_{B}\left(1-t\tau\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)^{-1}\in 1+tB[\![t]\!].

For general good coefficient rings
B
and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B)
, define

L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}Q_{B},t)\in 1+tB[\![t]\!].

For a derived crystal
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B))
, define

L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)=\prod_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{i},t)^{(-1)^{i}}.

Our aim is to extend the notion of
L
-functions to crystals on tame Deligne–Mumford stacks.
To do this without losing the good functoriality properties
L
-functions satisfy, one must take into account the fact that points can have automorphisms.

More precisely, let
\mathfrak{X}
be a tame Deligne–Mumford stack of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then for every
x\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
, we have a group scheme
\underline{\Aut}_{x}
, given by the following 2-fiber product:

\underline{\Aut}_{x}

\lrcorner

\mathfrak{X}
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
\mathfrak{X}\times\mathfrak{X}
\Delta
(x,x)

The conditions on
\mathfrak{X}
imply that
\underline{\Aut}_{x}\to\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
is finite étale and the cardinality of the group
\Aut(x):=\underline{\Aut}_{x}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})

is coprime to
p
. We denote by
[\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})]
the finite set of isomorphism classes of objects in the groupoid
\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B)
with
B
artinian. Consider the map
\text{d}\log\colon 1+tB[\![t]\!]\to B[\![t]\!]
sending
f\mapsto f^{\prime}/f
. Then we have

t\cdot\text{d}\log L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{x\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})}\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{x^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)t^{n}.

Proof.

Using the definition of the
L
-function, basic properties of
\text{d}\log
, and the fact that

-t\cdot\text{d}\log\det_{R}(1-t\psi\ |\ M)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\Tr_{R}(\psi^{n}\ |\ M)t^{n}

for any endomorphism
\psi
of a projective
R
-module
M
(see e.g.
[
GS97
, Thm 4.7]
), we obtain the formula

t\cdot\text{d}\log L(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=\sum_{x\in|X|}\sum_{n\geq 1}\Tr_{B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)t^{n}.

Next, for a closed point
x\in|X|
with residue field
k_{x}
of degree
d
, we have

\det_{B}\left(1-t\tau\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)=\det_{k_{x}\otimes B}\left(1-t^{d}\tau^{d}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right),

by
[
BP09
, Lemma 8.1.4]
. Using the chain rule, this gives

\Tr_{B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)=\begin{cases}d\Tr_{k_{x}\otimes B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)&\text{ if }d\mid n;\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Finally, if
d\mid n
, there are precisely
d
maps
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\in X(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
with image
x
. For any such map
x_{j}=i_{x}\circ\overline{x}_{j}
, we have

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{x_{j}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \overline{x}_{j}^{*}\widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)=\Tr_{k_{x}\otimes B}\left(\tau^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{i_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right).

Combining the above gives the formula from the lemma.
∎

Definition 3.6
.

Suppose
B
is an artinian
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra, and let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathfrak{X},B)
. The
crystalline
l
-series
associated to
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is the power series

l(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{x\in[\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})]}\frac{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}\left(\mathcal{\tau}^{n}\ \bigg|\ \widehat{x^{*}\mathcal{F}}\right)}{\#\Aut(x)}t^{n}\in tB[\![t]\!].

For general good coefficient rings
B
and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B)
, define

l(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=l(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}\otimes_{B}Q_{B},t)\in tB[\![t]\!].

For a derived crystal
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(X,B))
, define

l(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}(-1)^{i}l(X,\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{i},t).

Remark 3.7
.

It follows from Lemma
3.5
that
l(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)=t\cdot\text{d}\log L(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)
if
\mathfrak{X}
is a scheme.

Lemma 3.8
.

Given a distinguished triangle
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\to\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet}\to\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\bullet}\to\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}[1]
of flat derived crystals on
\mathfrak{X}
, we have

l(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{G}}^{\bullet},t)=l(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)+l(\mathfrak{X},\underline{\mathcal{H}}^{\bullet},t).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the statement for artinian
B
and for a distinguished triangle induced by a short exact sequence of complexes. By the definition of
l
-series, it suffices to prove it in a single degree and at a single point
x\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
. In this setting, the statement follows because the perfection functor is exact and traces are additive in short exact sequences.
∎

3.3

The trace formula

One of the main results of
[
BP09
]
is the trace formula: for a morphism
f\colon Y\to X
of schemes of finite type and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(Y,A))
, we have

L(Y,\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)\sim_{\text{uni}}L(X,Rf_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t),

where
\sim_{\text{uni}}
means that the quotient of these power series is a unipotent polynomial; that is, an element in
P\in 1+B[t]
such that
P-1
is nilpotent.
We now state the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.9

(Lefschetz trace formula)
.

Let
B
be a good coefficient ring. Let
f\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
be a compactifiable morphism of tame Deligne–Mumford stacks of finite type over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then for any
\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\in D^{b}(\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathfrak{Y},B))
, we have

l(\mathfrak{Y},\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)\sim_{\text{nil}}l(\mathfrak{X},Rf_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t),

where
\sim_{\text{nil}}
means that the difference of these power series is
tP
for some nilpotent polynomial
P
. In particular, if
B
is reduced, the
l
-series are equal.

We will prove the trace formula by hand for the classifying stack of a finite étale group scheme. Combining this with the trace formula for schemes will then imply the theorem in the generality stated above.

Lemma 3.10
.

Theorem
3.9
holds when
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{Y}
are schemes.

Proof.

By
[
BP09
, Thm 9.8.2]
, we may replace
B
with
Q_{B}
, so in particular we may assume
B
is artinian. Thus the lemma follows from
[
BP09
, Thm 9.6.5]
, Lemma
3.5
, and the fact that
\text{d}\log P
is nilpotent if
P
is unipotent.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Fix
d\geq 1
. Let
G
be a finite étale group scheme over
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})
of order coprime to
p
. Let
s\colon\mathcal{B}G=[*/G]\to\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})
be the classifying stack of
G
, and let
\underline{\mathcal{F}}\in\textsf{Crys}^{\text{flat}}(\mathcal{B}G,B)
be a flat
B
-crystal on
\mathcal{B}G
. Then we have

l(\mathcal{B}G,\underline{\mathcal{F}},t)=l(\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}}),Rs_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}},t).

Proof.

By replacing
q
with
q^{d}
and
\tau
with
\tau^{d}
, we may assume that
d=1
.
We further assume that
B
is artinian and
\underline{\mathcal{F}}=\widehat{\underline{\mathcal{F}}}
.
The finite étale group
G
is equivalent to an abstract finite group
\bar{G}:=G(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})
with a group automorphism
\sigma\colon\bar{G}\to\bar{G}
induced by the action of
\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q})
. We extend this correspondence to
G
-torsors as in
[
Beh91
, Prop. 2.3.2]
; see also
[
Ols15
,
7.8–7.10]
.

Let
k/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a field extension of degree
n
. Define a group action of
\bar{G}
on itself via
x\star_{n}g=xg\sigma^{n}(x^{-1})
. Then
\mathcal{B}G(k)
is equivalent to the action groupoid for
\star_{n}
. Explicitly, the groupoid
\mathcal{B}G(k)
has as objects the elements of
\bar{G}
and as morphisms

\text{Hom}_{\mathcal{B}G(k)}(g,h)=\{x\in\bar{G}\ |\ xg\sigma^{n}(x^{-1})=h\}.

In particular,
[\mathcal{B}G(k)]=\bar{G}/\star_{n}
, the set of orbits in
\bar{G}
under the action
\star_{n}
. Moreover,
\Aut_{\mathcal{B}G(k)}(g)
is identified with the stabilizer of
g
.

Since
\underline{\mathcal{F}}
is flat, it is represented by a locally free
\tau
-sheaf on
\mathcal{B}G
. Applying Proposition
2.16
to the étale cover
\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q})\to\mathcal{B}G
induced by the trivial torsor, we see that this data is equivalent to a pair
(M,\tau)
, where
M
is a
B
-module equipped with a
G
-action and
\tau\colon\sigma^{*}M\to M
is a
G
-linear map, where
G
acts on
\sigma^{*}M
through
\sigma^{-1}
. Let
\bar{M}:=\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes M
denote the induced
\bar{G}
-representation, and
\bar{\tau}:=\text{id}\otimes\tau
the
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes B
-linear endomorphism satisfying
\bar{\tau}(gm)=\sigma(g)\bar{\tau}(m)
. If
[g]\in\bar{G}/\star_{n}
represents an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-point of
\mathcal{B}G
, then
\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[g]^{*}M
may be identified with
\bar{M}
. The induced endomorphism
\bar{\tau}^{n}_{[g]}
of
\bar{M}
is then conjugate to
\bar{\tau}^{n}\circ g
(note that this does not depend on the chosen representative of
[g]
).

Since
\mathcal{B}G
is proper, we have
Rs_{!}=Rs_{*}
.
The functor
s_{*}
is given by taking
G
-invariants (note that these are respected by
\tau
).
Since
\mathcal{B}G
is tame,
s_{*}
is exact.
We conclude that
Rs_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}\cong s_{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}
, which is represented by
(M^{G},\tau)
.
Now fix
n\geq 1
. We have

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in[\mathcal{B}G(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})]}\frac{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}(\mathcal{\tau}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003ptx^{*}\mathcal{F})}{\#\Aut(x)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[g]\in\bar{G}/\star_{n}}\frac{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}(\tau^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003pt[g]^{*}M)}{\#\text{Stab}_{\bar{G}}(g)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[g]\in\bar{G}/\star_{n}}\frac{\Tr_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes B}(g\circ\bar{\tau}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003pt\bar{M})}{\#\text{Stab}_{\bar{G}}(g)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{g\in\bar{G}}\frac{\Tr_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes B}(g\circ\bar{\tau}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003pt\bar{M})}{\#\bar{G}}

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes B}\left(\frac{1}{\#\bar{G}}\sum_{g\in\bar{G}}g\circ\bar{\tau}^{n}\ \bigg|\ \bar{M}\right)

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}\otimes B}(\bar{\tau}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003pt\bar{M}^{\bar{G}})

\displaystyle=\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes B}(\tau^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 3.00003pt\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\otimes M^{G}),

which verifies the trace formula.
∎

We now deduce the trace formula as stated in Theorem
3.9
. The following proof is inspired by
[
Beh91
, §2]
, as well as the treatment
[
Sun12
]
. It uses noetherian induction on the topological space
|\mathfrak{Y}|:=\mathfrak{Y}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q})/\Gal(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

We apply a sequence of reduction steps.

1. By Lemma
3.8
and the fact that
Rf_{!}
preserves distinguished triangles, it suffices to prove the theorem for a single crystal, as we can apply the truncation sequence

T_{\leq n}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\longrightarrow\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\longrightarrow T_{>n}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}\longrightarrow T_{\leq n}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet}[1]

to reduce the statement to complexes of smaller length.

2. It is enough to show the statement for the structure morphisms
s_{\mathfrak{Y}}\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q})
for all
\mathfrak{Y}
. Indeed, given
f\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to\mathfrak{X}
, we have
Rs_{\mathfrak{Y},!}\cong Rs_{\mathfrak{X},!}Rf_{!}
and hence

l(\mathfrak{Y},\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)\sim_{\text{nil}}l(\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q}),Rs_{\mathfrak{Y},!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t)\sim_{\text{nil}}l(\mathfrak{X},Rf_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}^{\bullet},t).

3. By noetherian induction, it is enough to show that the trace formula holds on a non-empty open substack of
\mathfrak{Y}
, cf.
[
BP09
, Lemma 9.6.8]
. Hence we may assume that
\mathfrak{Y}
is a quotient stack; in particular, it admits a coarse moduli scheme
\pi\colon\mathfrak{Y}\to Y
.

4. It is enough to show the statement for the coarse moduli space
\pi
. Indeed, we already know the statement for the structure map
s_{Y}
since
Y
is a scheme, and we have
Rs_{\mathfrak{Y},!}\cong Rs_{Y,!}R\pi_{!}
.

5. By proper base change and the local definition of
l
-series, we may take a point
y\in|Y|
of degree
d
and replace
\pi
with
\pi_{y}\colon\mathfrak{Y}_{y}\to y
. Then
\pi_{y}
defines an
\underline{\text{Aut}}_{y}
-gerbe, but any gerbe over a finite field is neutral
[
Beh03
, Cor. 6.4.2]
. Hence
\pi_{y}
is of the form
\mathcal{B}G\to\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{q^{d}})
, where
G=\underline{\text{Aut}}_{y}
is a finite étale group scheme of order coprime to
p
. Thus we are done by Proposition
3.11
.
∎

4

Applications to Drinfeld modular forms

Our aim in this section is to apply the Lefschetz trace formula to certain crystals on the moduli stack of Drinfeld modules. This will yield a trace formula for Hecke operators on Drinfeld cusp forms, from which we will deduce the Ramanujan bound.

For the remainder of this paper, Drinfeld modules will be assumed to be of rank
r=2
. We briefly recall the definition of Drinfeld modular forms, as well as the Eichler-Shimura theory developed by Böckle
[
Böc02
]
, before proving our main results.

4.1

Background

Let
A=\mathcal{O}_{C}(C\setminus\{\infty\})
and
K=\Frac(A)
as before. Let
K_{\infty}
be the completion of
K
at the place
\infty
, and let
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
be the completion of an algebraic closure of
K_{\infty}
. Denote by
\Omega:=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus K_{\infty}
the Drinfeld upper half-plane, seen as a rigid-analytic space. It has an action of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(K_{\infty})
by Möbius transformations.

A subgroup
\Gamma\subseteq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
is called a
congruence subgroup
if it contains

\Gamma(\mathfrak{n})=\left\{M\in\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)\ \bigg|\ M\equiv\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\pmod{\mathfrak{n}}\right\}

for some non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{n}\trianglelefteq A
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix integers
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
and a congruence subgroup
\Gamma
. A
Drinfeld modular form of weight
k
, type
l
, and level
\Gamma
is a function
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
satisfying the following properties:

1.

For each
\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma
, we have

f(\gamma z)=\det(\gamma)^{-l}(cz+d)^{k}f(z).

(1)

2.

f
is holomorphic on
\Omega
and at the cusps of
\Gamma
(see
[
Gek86
, V.2.4]
).

If additionally
f
vanishes at all the cusps of
\Gamma
, we call
f
a
cusp form
. We denote the space of Drinfeld cusp forms of weight
k
, type
l
, and level
\Gamma
by
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\Gamma)
.

Remark 4.2
.

By a modular form of level 1, we mean a modular form of level
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A)
. We write
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
for the space of cusp forms of weight
k
, type
l
, and level 1.

Remark 4.3
.

In
[
Böc02
, §5]
, Böckle defines
adelic Drinfeld modular forms
and shows that spaces of adelic Drinfeld modular forms are naturally isomorphic to spaces of Drinfeld modular forms. The advantage of this is that one can define Hecke operators on Drinfeld modular forms for more general rings
A
than just
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. We will use the adelic language where necessary without recalling all details. In particular, we will consider modular forms of level
\mathcal{K}
, where
\hat{A}=\varprojlim A/I
is the completion of
A
and
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
is an admissible subgroup. For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
not dividing the minimal conductor of
\mathcal{K}
, we denote by
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
the corresponding Hecke operator acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
.

Remark 4.4
.

When
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, the action of the Hecke operators on adelic cusp forms is different from the Hecke operators defined in
[
Gos80
,
Gek88
]
. For clarity, we distinguish between the two notions of Hecke operators: we retain the notation
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for the adelic Hecke operators, and write
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}
the Hecke operators from loc. cit. Let us emphasise an important difference between the two.

From Definition
4.1
, it follows immediately that we have canonical isomorphisms
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\cong\operatorname{S}_{k,l^{\prime}}
for any
l\equiv l^{\prime}\pmod{q-1}
. This isomorphism is compatible with the Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}
. However, it is not equivariant with respect to the Hecke operators
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
: the action differs by a character
[
Böc02
, Remark 6.12]
. Given integers
k,l\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=P^{l-k}\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]},

where
P
is the monic generator of
\mathfrak{p}
. This is why we define
l\in\mathbb{Z}
as an integer and not as an element in
\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}
.

4.2

Böckle–Eichler–Shimura theory

Denote by
\Theta\colon\mathfrak{M}_{2}\to\Spec(A)
the structure morphism of the moduli stack of Drinfeld modules. Fix a maximal ideal
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, corresponding to a map
i_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\to\Spec(A)
. Similarly, for the completion
K_{\infty}
of
K=\Frac(A)
at
\infty
, write
i_{K_{\infty}}\colon\Spec(K_{\infty})\to\Spec(A)
.

The
universal Drinfeld module over
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
is the Drinfeld module
\varphi_{\text{univ}}:=\text{id}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{2}\to\mathfrak{M}_{2}
. We denote its associated
\tau
-sheaf by
\underline{\mathcal{F}}:=\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi_{\text{univ}})
, cf. Construction
2.13
.

Definition 4.5
.

Fix integers
k\geq 2
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. With notation as above, we define the
A
-crystals

\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}:=(\det\underline{\mathcal{F}})^{\otimes l-k+1}\otimes\text{Sym}^{k-2}\underline{\mathcal{F}};\qquad\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}:=R^{1}\Theta_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}.

We call
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}
the
crystal of Drinfeld cusp forms of weight
k
and type
l
.

Similarly, given an admissible subgroup
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
, one has a moduli space
\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathcal{K}}
of Drinfeld modules with level
\mathcal{K}
-structure. If
\mathcal{K}
has minimal conductor
\mathfrak{n}\trianglelefteq A
, then the moduli space of Drinfeld modules with level
\mathcal{K}
-structure is representable by a scheme over
\Spec(A[\mathfrak{n}^{-1}])
. Forgetting the level structure defines an étale cover
\pi_{\mathcal{K}}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathcal{K}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{2}
, and we define

\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}):=\pi_{\mathcal{K}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l};\qquad\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}):=R^{1}\Theta_{\mathcal{K},!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}).

We call
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
the
crystal of cusp forms of weight
k
, type
l
, and level
\mathcal{K}
. For any prime
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A[\mathfrak{n}^{-1}]
, one has a Hecke operator
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on the crystals of cusp forms via correspondences
[
Böc02
, Section 13.1]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
l\in\mathbb{Z}
, and let
x\in\mathfrak{M}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
correspond to the Drinfeld module
(E,\varphi)
.

1.

The perfection of
x^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}\otimes_{A}K
is the coherent
\tau
-sheaf

\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,l}(\varphi)_{K}:=((\det\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi))^{\otimes l-k+1}\otimes\Sym^{k-2}\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi))\otimes_{A}K.

2.

The crystals
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}
,
\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
,
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}
, and
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
are flat.

Proof.

By definition of the universal Drinfeld module, we have
x^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}\cong\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)
as
A
-crystals. By Example
2.14
,
\tau
acts as an isomorphism on
\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)\otimes_{A}K
, since
\pi_{\varphi}=\tau^{n}
is an isogeny and hence becomes invertible after tensoring with
K
. Hence
\tau
also acts as an isomorphism on
\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,l}(\varphi)_{K}
. Since
K
is artinian, the perfection functor sends a
\tau
-sheaf to the direct summand on which
\tau
acts as an isomorphism, which yields 1. For 2, note that
\underline{\mathcal{M}}(\varphi)
is flat, as its underlying sheaf is locally free. Hence the result follows from Lemma
3.2
and the fact that flatness is preserved under the functors from Theorem
2.22
.
∎

For any rigid-analytic space
\mathcal{X}
, there is a category of
rigid
A
-crystals
which we denote by
\widetilde{\textsf{Crys}}(\mathcal{X},A)
. For any scheme
X
over
\Spec(K_{\infty})
, one can define a rigidification functor

(-)^{\text{rig}}:\textsf{Crys}(X,A)\longrightarrow\widetilde{\textsf{Crys}}(X^{\text{rig}},A)

where
X^{\text{rig}}
is a certain rigid analytic space associated to
X
.
When applied to the crystal of cusp forms, one can show the following
[
Böc02
, Cor. 10.13]
:

Lemma 4.7
.

The crystal
i_{K_{\infty}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})^{\text{rig}}
is represented by a rigid
\tau
-sheaf of the form

\underline{\tilde{\mathds{1}}}_{K_{\infty}}\otimes_{A}P_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}),

where
P_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
is a projective finitely generated
A
-module and
\underline{\tilde{\mathds{1}}}_{K_{\infty}}
denotes the trivial rigid
\tau
-sheaf on
\text{Spm}(K_{\infty})
. ∎

For a scheme
X
, the trivial
\tau
-sheaf
\underline{\mathds{1}}_{X}
on
X
over
A
is given by the pair
(\mathcal{O}_{X\otimes A},\tau)
, where

\tau\colon(\sigma_{X}\times\text{id})^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X\otimes A}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{X\otimes A}

is given by the adjoint of the map
x\otimes a\mapsto x^{q}\otimes a
. The construction for rigid
\tau
-sheaves is entirely similar. In particular, the functor taking
\tau
-invariants

\displaystyle(-)^{\tau}\colon\widetilde{\textsf{Crys}}(\mathcal{X},A)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow A\textsf{-Mod}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathcal{F}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto H^{0}(\mathcal{X}\times\Spec(A),\mathcal{F})^{\tau},

which is well-defined on crystals, sends
\underline{\tilde{\mathds{1}}}_{\mathcal{X}}\otimes_{A}P\mapsto P
.

We can now state the Eichler–Shimura isomorphism for Drinfeld cusp forms, which is proven in
[
Böc02
, Thm 10.3]
in the case
l=k-1
and which is readily generalised to arbitrary types
l
:

Theorem 4.8

(Eichler–Shimura isomorphism)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
be an admissible subgroup. Then there is a Hecke-equivariant isomorphism

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\otimes_{A}\left(i_{K_{\infty}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})^{\text{rig}}\right)^{\tau}\cong\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})^{\vee},

(2)

called the
Eichler–Shimura isomorphism for Drinfeld cusp forms
.
\Box

There is also an Eichler–Shimura relation. Classically, this says that for a prime
p\nmid N
, the Hecke operator at
p
is the sum of the
p
-Frobenius and the Verschiebung as correspondences on
X_{0}(N)
. For Drinfeld modules, the Hecke operator at
\mathfrak{p}
is simply the
\mathfrak{p}
-Frobenius
[
Böc02
, Theorem 13.10]
:

Theorem 4.9

(Eichler–Shimura relation)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{K}
has minimal conductor
\mathfrak{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{p}
be a prime not dividing
\mathfrak{n}
. Then we have an equality

i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\tau^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})}

(3)

as endomorphisms of
i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
.
\Box

The above results extend to the crystals
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}
of level 1. In particular, we obtain the following:

Corollary 4.10
.

Denote by
\underline{\hat{\mathcal{S}}}^{K,\mathfrak{p}}_{k,l}
the perfection of
i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}\otimes_{A}K
. Then we have

\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes K}\left(\tau^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}\ \bigg|\ \hat{\mathcal{S}}^{K,\mathfrak{p}}_{k,l}\right)=\Tr_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}\left(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 3.00003pt\bigg|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\right).

Proof.

Consider
\mathcal{K}=\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{n})
for some proper non-zero ideal
\mathfrak{n}
not contained in
\mathfrak{p}
. Then
\mathcal{K}(\mathfrak{n})\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
is a normal subgroup with quotient
G\cong\operatorname{GL}_{2}(A/\mathfrak{n}A)
, and as a result
\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathcal{K}}
is a Galois cover of
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
over
\Spec(A[\mathfrak{n}^{-1}])
with Galois group
G
. In other words, we can identify
\mathfrak{M}_{2}
with the stack quotient
[\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathcal{K}}/G]
. Via base change, this statement holds also over both
K_{\infty}
and
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Since a
G
-action on a crystal induces by functoriality a
G
-action on its rigidification, we define
i_{K_{\infty}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}^{\text{rig}}:=(i_{K_{\infty}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})^{\text{rig}})^{G}
. Then the Eichler–Shimura isomorphism (
2
) and Lemma
4.7
imply

i_{K_{\infty}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}^{\text{rig}}\cong(\underline{\mathds{1}}_{K_{\infty}}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})^{\vee})^{G}\cong\underline{\mathds{1}}_{K_{\infty}}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{S}_{k,l}^{\vee}.

Next,
i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}=\tau^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}
follows from the Eichler–Shimura relation (
3
) and Corollary
2.21
, since exact and conservative functors are faithful. Combining the above and noting that
A
is reduced, the Hecke action on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
can be computed on the underlying sheaf of any locally free representative of the crystal
i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}
. This may also be done after tensoring with
K
, which gives the desired expression.
∎

4.3

A trace formula for Hecke operators

Let
\pi\in\bar{K}
be algebraic over
K
of degree
\leq 2
, and denote its Galois conjugate by
\bar{\pi}
. Then we set for
m\geq 0
,

\Tr_{m}(\pi):=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\pi^{i}\bar{\pi}^{m-i}.

Combining Corollary
4.10
with the Lefschetz trace formula, we obtain a trace formula for Hecke operators on Drinfeld cusp forms of level 1.

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal with residue field
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Fix a weight
k\geq 2
and a type
l\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then for every
n\geq 1
, we have

\Tr_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})=\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]/\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}}\Tr_{k-2}(\pi_{\varphi})\cdot(\pi_{\varphi}\bar{\pi}_{\varphi})^{l-k+1},

(4)

where the sum is taken over the set of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
, and
\pi_{\varphi}\in\bar{K}
denotes the Frobenius endomorphism of
(E,\varphi)
.

Proof.

Let
j_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}}\to\mathfrak{M}_{2}
be the fiber of the moduli space of Drinfeld modules at
\mathfrak{p}
. Consider the
l
-series of the crystal
j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}
.
By Lemma
4.6
, we have

l(\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}},j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l},t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]/\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}\frac{\Tr_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}\otimes K}\left(\tau^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}\ \bigg|\ \underline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,l}(\varphi)_{K}\right)}{\#\Aut(\varphi)}t^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}.

By Lemma
2.5
, we have
\#\Aut(\varphi)=-1
for all
\varphi
. Moreover, the endomorphism
\tau^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}
of the
\tau
-sheaf associated to
\varphi
may be identified with the Frobenius endomorphism
\pi_{\varphi}
of
\varphi
.
If the characteristic polynomial of
\pi_{\varphi}
has roots
\pi_{\varphi}
and
\bar{\pi}_{\varphi}
, then by definition of
\underline{\mathcal{M}}_{k,l}(\varphi)_{K}
we obtain

l(\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}},j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l},t)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{[(E,\varphi)]/\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}}-(\pi_{\varphi}\bar{\pi}_{\varphi})^{l-k+1}\Tr_{k-2}(\pi_{\varphi})t^{\deg(\mathfrak{p})n}.

On the other hand, since
\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}}
is compactifiable
[
Ryd11
, Theorem F]
, we may apply the trace formula to the crystal
j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}
under the structure map
s_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}}\to\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. We have
R^{i}\Theta_{!}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}=0
for
i\neq 1
by
[
BP09
, Theorem 8.4.2(a)]
and étale descent. Combined with proper base change, this gives
Rs_{\mathfrak{p},!}j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l}\cong i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}[-1]
. Thus the trace formula states that

l(\mathfrak{M}_{2,\mathfrak{p}},j_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{F}}_{k,l},t)=l(\Spec(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}),i_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}[-1],t),

with equality holding on the nose since
A
is reduced. Applying Corollary
4.10
and comparing coefficients yields the desired equation (
4
).
∎

Remark 4.12
.

Let
\mathcal{K}\subset\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
be an admissible subgroup. Applying the Lefschetz trace formula to the crystal
\underline{\mathcal{S}}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
yields a similar formula for traces of Hecke operators on Drinfeld cusp forms of level
\mathcal{K}
: for any
\mathfrak{p}
not dividing the minimal conductor of
\mathcal{K}
, we have

\Tr_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbf{T}^{n}_{\mathfrak{p}}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K}))=-\sum_{[(E,\varphi,[\psi])]/\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}}\Tr_{k-2}(\pi_{\varphi})\cdot(\pi_{\varphi}\bar{\pi}_{\varphi})^{l-k+1},

where the sum is taken over the set of isomorphism classes of Drinfeld modules over
\mathbb{F}_{{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}}
with level
\mathcal{K}
-structure.

4.4

Ramanujan bounds

As a consequence of the trace formula, we obtain bounds on the traces of Hecke operators. Denote by
|\cdot|_{\infty}
a fixed extension to
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
of the norm on
K
given by
|x|_{\infty}=q^{-\deg(\infty)v_{\infty}(x)}
.

Corollary 4.13

(Ramanujan bound)
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\trianglelefteq A
be a maximal ideal of degree
d
. Then for every
n\geq 1
, we have

|\Tr_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})|_{\infty}\leq(q^{nd})^{\frac{k}{2}+l-k}.

(5)

Proof.

For any Drinfeld module over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}
with Frobenius endomorphism
\pi
, the elements
\pi
and
\bar{\pi}
are Weil numbers of rank
2
over
\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}^{n}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q^{nd}}
. In particular,

|\pi|_{\infty}=|\bar{\pi}|_{\infty}=q^{\frac{nd}{2}}.

By equation (
4
), the definition of
\Tr_{k-2}
, and the triangle inequality, we obtain

q^{-nd(l-k+1)}|\Tr_{\mathbb{C}_{\infty}}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}\hskip 1.99997pt|\hskip 1.00006pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l})|_{\infty}\leq\max_{\pi}\{|\Tr_{k-2}(\pi,\bar{\pi})|_{\infty}\}\leq q^{nd(k-2)/2}.

This yields the desired inequality.
∎

Remark 4.14
.

Using Remark
4.12
, one can use the same argument to obtain a Ramanujan bound for the traces of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
acting on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}(\mathcal{K})
for any
\mathfrak{p}
not dividing the minimal conductor of
\mathcal{K}
.

Remark 4.15
.

The bound (
5
) involves the exponent
k/2+l-k
, which at first sight looks strange. We argue that the main term in the exponent is
k/2
, whereas the term
l-k
is an artefact of the adelic interpretation of Hecke operators. As explained in Remark
4.4
, it is natural to view
l
as an element in
\mathbb{Z}/(q-1)\mathbb{Z}
, but then the adelic Hecke operator is only defined up to a character twist. After a suitable normalisation, the exponent does indeed reduce to
k/2
, as demonstrated by the case
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
(cf. Corollary
4.16
below).

Corollary 4.16

(Ramanujan bound for
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
)
.

Let
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and fix a monic and irreducible polynomial
P\in A
of degree
d
. Let
\mathfrak{p}=(P)
and denote by
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}
the Hecke operator for
\mathfrak{p}
as defined in
[
Gek88
, §7]
. Then for every
n\geq 1
, we have

\deg\Tr\left(\left(\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}\right)^{n}\hskip 1.00006pt\bigg|\hskip 1.99997pt\operatorname{S}_{k,l}\right)\leq\frac{ndk}{2}.

Proof.

This follows because
|f|_{\infty}=q^{\deg(f)}
for
f\in A
, and the fact that
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]}=P^{k-l}\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
∎

We conclude with an observation about the Hecke eigenvalues. In characteristic 0, Newton’s identities imply that for an operator
\mathbf{T}
acting on a
d
-dimensional vector space
V
, knowing
\Tr(\mathbf{T}^{n})
for
n=1,\ldots,d
is equivalent to knowing the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{T}
. Although this fails in characteristic
p
, one can still say the following.

Corollary 4.17
.

Suppose the action of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{S}_{k,l}
does not have
p
repeated eigenvalues. Then any eigenvalue
\alpha
of
\mathbf{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
satisfies

|\alpha|_{\infty}\leq(q^{d})^{\frac{k}{2}+l-k}.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of the Ramanujan bound (
5
) and
[
Vri25
, Prop. 4.2]
.
∎

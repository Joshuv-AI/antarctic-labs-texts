On the Stability of Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann Iterations
Hui Ouyang

Abstract
Firstly, we invoke the weak convergence (resp. strong convergence) of translated basic methods involving nonexpansive operators to establish the weak convergence (resp. strong convergence) of the associated method with both perturbation and approximation. Then we employ the technique obtaining the result above to extend convergence results from the classic Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations to their relaxation variants for finding a common fixed point of associated nonexpansive operators. At last, we show applications on generalized proximal point algorithms for solving monotone inclusion problems.

Convergence of the Perturbed or Approximate Method

In this section, we borrow the terminology used in
[
11
]
. Consider some problems with data
D
.
Let
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

G_{k}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be the iteration mapping that is defined from the data
D
.
The
basic method
is generated by conforming to the iteration scheme:

\displaystyle x_{0}=x\in\mathcal{H}\quad\text{and}\quad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})~x_{k+1}=G_{k}x_{k}.

With such a basic method, we have the following associated methods.

(i)

The
perturbed method
is given by the iteration scheme:

\displaystyle x_{0}=x\in\mathcal{H}\quad\text{and}\quad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})~x_{k+1}=F_{k}x_{k},

where for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the iteration mapping
F_{k}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is closely related to
G_{k}
and depends on the perturbed (or approximate) data of
D
.

(ii)

The
translated basic method
is defined by the iteration scheme:

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\mathbb{N})\quad\xi_{0}(i)=x_{i}\in\mathcal{H}\quad\text{and}\quad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})~\xi_{k+1}(i)=G_{k+i}\xi_{k}(i).

(iii)

The
approximate method
is defined by the iteration scheme

\displaystyle x_{0}=x\in\mathcal{H}\quad\text{and}\quad(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})~x_{k+1}=G_{k}x_{k}+e_{k},

where
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is the sequence of
error terms
.

Note that, as stated in
[
11
, Remark 2.1]
, if
\xi_{0}(i)=x
and
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

G_{k}\equiv G
, that is, the iterations mappings are independent on the iteration numbers
k
, then the translated basic method coincides with the basic one.

Lemma

3.1
is essentially a special case of
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
. As stated in
[
11
]
,
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
is
“substantially proved in
[
3
, Remark 14]
”
.
Notice that
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
is on a general topology weaker than the norm topology in Banach spaces. In
Lemma

3.1
, we simplify the context and add some details on the proof of the weak and norm topologies in Hilbert spaces to make the proof easier to understand, but the idea of the proof is almost the same as that of
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\left(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right)

G_{k}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
be nonexpansive
and let
(e_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
be in
\mathcal{H}
. Define

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad y_{k+1}=G_{k}y_{k}+e_{k}\text{ and }y_{0}\in\mathcal{H};

(3.1a)

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\mathbb{N})(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\xi_{k+1}(i)=G_{k+i}\xi_{k}(i)\text{ and }\xi_{0}(i)=y_{i}.

(3.1b)

Suppose that

and that for every

,

(i)

There exists a point

strongly converges to

5.3
Weak and strong convergence of generalized proximal point algorithms

Recall that

A:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is maximally monotone with
\operatorname{zer}A\neq\varnothing
and that

\displaystyle(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad x_{k+1}=\left(1-\lambda_{k}\right)x_{k}+\lambda_{k}\operatorname{J}_{c_{k}A}x_{k}+\eta_{k}e_{k},

(5.7)

where

and
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

,

In this section, we consider the weak and strong convergence of the sequence generated by conforming to the iteration scheme

5.7
for solving monotone inclusion problems.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}_{++}
. It is clear that

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\frac{c_{k}}{c}-1\right|<\infty\Rightarrow c_{k}\to c\Rightarrow\inf_{k\in\mathbb{N}}c_{k}>0.

actually implies the popular assumption

for the convergence of generalized proximal point algorithms.

Theorem 5.7
.

Suppose that
(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

(

(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})

(i)

Define

\displaystyle(\forall i\in\mathbb{N})\left(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right)\quad

\bar{\xi}

(ii)

Suppose that

converges weakly to a point in
\operatorname{zer}A

Define

\displaystyle\left(\forall k\in\mathbb{N}\right)\quad G_{k}:=(1-\lambda_{k})\operatorname{Id}+\lambda_{k}\operatorname{J}_{cA}\text{ and }F_{k}:=(1-\lambda_{k})\operatorname{Id}+\lambda_{k}\operatorname{J}_{c_{k}A}.

Conclusion and Future Work

In this work, applying a specific instance of
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
which originates from
[
3
, Remark 14]
, we deduce the weak convergence (resp. strong convergence) of the iteration method (involving nonexpansive operators) with both approximation and perturbation from the weak convergence (resp. strong convergence) of associated translated basic methods. Based on this result and some beautiful properties of averaged operators, by employing some convergence results on the classical Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations, we establish the weak and strong convergence of relaxation variants of Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations for finding a fixed point of the associated nonexpansive operators. In addition, bearing the powerful properties of the resolvent of maximally monotone operators in mind, we apply all previous convergence results and proof techniques to generalized proximal point algorithms and obtain some weak and strong convergence results on generalized proximal point algorithms for solving monotone inclusion problems.

Note that relaxation variants of Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations and generalized proximal point algorithms cover generalized versions of multiple popular optimization algorithms.
In the future, by specifying the nonexpansive operators in relaxation variants of Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann iterations or the maximally monotone operators in generalized proximal point algorithms accordingly,
we shall elaborate applications of our convergence results for more particular algorithms such as the Douglas-Rachford splitting algorithm, the three-operator splitting schemes, the alternating direction method of multipliers, and so on.

Last fall degree of semi-local polynomial systems
Ming-Deh A. Huang (USC, mdhuang@usc.edu)

Abstract
. We study the last fall degrees of semi-local polynomial systems, and the computational complexity of solving such systems for closed-point and rational-point solutions, where the systems are defined over a finite field. A semi-local polynomial system specifies an algebraic set which is the image of a global linear transformation of a direct product of local affine algebraic sets. As a special but interesting case, polynomial systems that arise from Weil restriction of algebraic sets in an affine space of low dimension are semi-local. Such systems have received considerable attention due to their application in cryptography. Our main results bound the last fall degree of a semi-local polynomial system in terms of the number of closed point solutions, and yield an efficient algorithm for finding all rational-point solutions when the prime characteristic of the finite field and the number of rational solutions are small. Our results on solving semi-local systems imply an improvement on a previously known polynomial-time attack on the HFE (Hidden Field Equations) cryptosystems. The attacks implied in our results extend to public key encryption functions which are based on semi-local systems where either the number of closed point solutions is small, or the characteristic of the field is small. It remains plausible to construct public key cryptosystems based on semi-local systems over a finite field of large prime characteristic with exponential number of closed point solutions. Such a method is presented in the paper, followed by further cryptanalysis involving the isomorphism of polynomials (IP) problem, as well as a concrete public key encryption scheme which is secure against all the attacks discussed in this paper.

An interesting question is whether more general semi-local polynomial systems, especially those which do not arise from Weil restriction, can still be used as the basis for constructing cryptosystems. The attacks implied in our results extend to public key encryption functions which are based on semi-local systems where either the number of closed point solutions is small, or the characteristic of the field is small. It remains plausible to construct public key cryptosystems based on semi-local systems over a finite field of large prime characteristic with exponential number of closed point solutions. Such a method is presented in §
5
.

Solving the polynomial system

such that
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda

We extend the determinant-of-Jacobian method

,

to solve the square 1-semi-local case of the isomorphism of polynomials (IP) problem. We also discuss how the method may be applied to mount a partial attack for square

We describe a concrete and simple public key encryption scheme based on a non-square 2-semi-local system. The system is secure against all the attacks discussed in this paper. Whether an efficient attack can be found on the system is an interesting open problem that requires further investigation.

Suppose

and
d_{2}=\deg m_{2}
. Then by induction

\deg g_{i}<\deg f

\Delta_{i}=\max(d_{i},2d)
for
i=1,2
. Let
\alpha_{i}=m_{i}-g_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then
\deg\alpha_{i}\leq\max(d_{i},d)
for
i=1,2
.
We have

m-g_{1}g_{2}=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}g_{2}+\alpha_{2}g_{1}.

We have

where

and
\deg g<\deg f\leq d
.
Since

, it follows that

.

Case 1:
\deg m\leq 2d
then

for

. In this case
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{1}g_{2}
, and
\alpha_{2}g_{1}
are all of degree no greater than

.
Since

\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in V_{{\mathcal{F}},2d}

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}+\alpha_{1}g_{2}+\alpha_{2}g_{1}\in V_{{\mathcal{F}},2d}
.
We get

Case 2:
\deg m>2d
. In this case

\deg\alpha_{i}\leq\max(d_{i},d)=d_{i}
for

. So
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{1}g_{2}
, and
\alpha_{2}g_{1}
are all of degree no greater than

. Since

,

\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}
,
\alpha_{1}g_{2}
, and
\alpha_{2}g_{1}

.

.

Lemma 2.5

3.1.

Let

c

4.
Weil descent systems

Throughout this section suppose

is a finite field and

is an extension of degree
n
over

Suppose

is a basis of
K
over

.
Suppose

. Let

for

.
For
\hat{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\bar{k}^{n}
, let

for

The
K
-linear map from

to

The

of

with respect to
{\bf\theta}
consists of an ordered set
\hat{f}
of
n

defined by the following eqution:

Let
\sigma
be the Frobenius automorphism of
K
over
k
. Then

(f(t(\hat{x}_{1}),\ldots,t(\hat{x}_{c})))^{\sigma^{i}}=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\theta_{j})^{\sigma^{i}}.

We have

((\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\theta_{j})^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}f_{j}\theta_{j}^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}=({\langle}{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}},\hat{f}{\rangle})_{i=0}^{n-1}=\mu\circ\hat{f},

where

with
{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}}
as the
i
-th row, if the rows are indexed by

.

On the other hand,

((f(t(\hat{x}_{1}),\ldots,t(\hat{x}_{c})))^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}=(f^{\sigma^{i}}({\langle}{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}},\hat{x}_{1}{\rangle},\ldots,{\langle}{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}},\hat{x}_{c}{\rangle}))_{i=0}^{n-1}=(f^{\sigma^{i}}(\lambda_{i}(\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\hat{x}_{c}))_{i=0}^{n-1}=(f^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}\circ\lambda,

where
\lambda\in Gl_{cn}(K)
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{i})_{i=0}^{n-1}
where
\lambda_{i}:\bar{k}^{cn}\to\bar{k}^{c}
sending
(\hat{x}_{1},\ldots,\bar{x}_{c})\in\bar{k}^{cn}
to
({\langle}{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}},\hat{x}_{1}{\rangle},\ldots,{\langle}{\bf\theta}^{\sigma^{i}},\hat{x}_{c}{\rangle})
.
Note that up to row permutation
\lambda
is the block diagonal matrix with
c
copies of
\mu
on the diagonal.

Therefore,
\mu\circ\hat{f}=(f^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}\circ\lambda
.

Now suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
is an ordered set of
c

f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}\in K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{c}]
. Let
\hat{f}_{i}
be the ordered set consisting of the Weil descent of

with respect to

for

, consists of the polynomials in

,

. We have

\tilde{\mu}\circ\ \hat{{\mathcal{F}}}=(f_{1}^{\sigma^{i}},\ldots,f_{c}^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}\circ\lambda

where

is the block diagonal matrix with

\mu

Therefore for

\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}=\tilde{\mu}^{-1}\circ(f_{1}^{\sigma^{i}},\ldots,f_{c}^{\sigma^{i}})_{i=0}^{n-1}\circ\lambda

and we conclude that

is a

-semi-local polynomial system.

If

5.
Cryptographic applications

5.1.
Improved attack on HFE

In the general HFE (Hidden Field Equations) public key cryptosystem, a multivariate public encryption map is constructed by disguising a univariate polynomial over a large finite field in a
K/k
-Weil descent, where
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
K=\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, and
n
is determined by the security parameter. Let
F\in K[x]
and let
\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}
be the Weil descent of
{\mathcal{F}}=\{F\}
with respect to a publicly known basis of
K
over
k
. Choose random
\lambda,\mu\in Gl_{n}(k)
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}\lambda
. In the HFE system
{\mathcal{G}}
can serve as the public encryption function, with
\lambda,\mu
as the secret decryption key. The degree of
\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}
needs to be
O(1)
so that the size of
{\mathcal{G}}
is polynomially bounded.
A well-known choice of
F
is the
extended Dembowski-Ostrom polynomial

F=\sum_{0\leq i\leq j<r}a_{ij}x^{q^{i}+q^{j}}+\sum_{0\leq i<r}b_{i}x^{q^{i}}+c

with
a_{ij},b_{i},c\in K
.

For cryptanalysis one would like to solve
{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\alpha
given
\alpha\in k^{n}
. The solution set is
Z_{k}({\mathcal{G}}-\alpha)=Z(\{{\mathcal{G}}-\alpha\}\cup{\mathcal{E}})
where
{\mathcal{E}}=\{x_{i}^{q}-x_{i}:i=1,\ldots,n\}
and
k=\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The results in
[
11
]
bounds the last fall degree of
\{{\mathcal{G}}-\alpha\}\cup{\mathcal{E}}
by
O(dq)
, leading to an attack that runs in
n^{O(dq)}
time. The more recent results in
[
8
,
12
]
bounds the last fall degree of
\{{\mathcal{G}}-\alpha\}\cup{\mathcal{E}}
by
O(q\log_{q}d)

(with the bound in
[
8
]
improving on the bound in
[
12
]
by a factor of 1/2), leading to an attack that solves the system in
n^{O(q\log_{q}d)}
time.
When
d=O(1)
and
q=O(1)
, the running time is polynomial in
n
in these attacks.
Our results lead to a further improvement wherever
{\mathcal{G}}
is injective, as elaborated below.

For
\alpha\in k^{n}
,
\mu\circ(\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}-\mu^{-1}(\alpha))\circ\lambda={\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
where
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}={\mathcal{G}}-\alpha
. By Theorem
4.1
,
{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}
is a 1-semi-local with the local components
F^{\sigma^{i}}-u^{\sigma^{i}}_{\alpha}
where
\sigma
is the Frobenius map over
k
, and
u_{\alpha}=\rho(\mu^{-1}(\alpha))
.

Note that for
\alpha\in k^{n}
,
{\mathcal{G}}(x)=\alpha
has a unique solution in
k^{n}
if and only if
\hat{{\mathcal{F}}}(z)=\mu^{-1}(\alpha)
has a unique solution in
k^{n}
, if and only if
F(y)=u_{\alpha}
has a unique solution in
K
. In this case,

F^{\sigma^{i}}(y)=u^{\sigma^{i}}_{\alpha}
has a unique solution in
K
,
so
Z_{K}(\{F^{\sigma_{i}}-u^{\sigma_{i}}_{\alpha}\})=1
.
It follows from Theorem
3.2
that
d_{{\mathcal{G}}_{\alpha}\cup{\mathcal{E}}^{\prime}}=O(dp)
, where
d=\deg F
. Therefore
{\mathcal{G}}(\hat{x})=\alpha
can be solved in
n^{O(dp)}
time.
This improves on the attacks in
[
8
,
11
,
12
]
, when
d=O(1)
.

More generally, one can consider using semi-local systems as the basis for constructing public key cryptosystems. The attacks implied in our results extend to public key encryption functions which are based on semi-local polynomials systems where either the number of closed point solutions is small, or the characteristic of the field is small. It remains plausible to construct public key cryptosystems based on semi-local polynomial systems with exponential number of closed point solutions over a finite field of large prime characteristic. Such a method is discussed below.

5.3.
Cryptanalysis

Note that solving the polynomial system

for

5.3.1.

Let
{\bf g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
with

Let

to

denote the

by

Below we give a self-contained account of the determinant-of-Jacobian attack

The following lemma is an easy consequence of linearity in taking partial derivatives. That is,
\partial_{i}(af+bg)=a\partial_{i}f+b\partial_{i}g

,
f,g\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
{\bf f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{n})
with
f_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]

{\bf g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
with
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
.
Suppose
{\bf g}=\lambda\circ{\bf f}
where
\lambda
is a linear map from
k^{n}
to
k^{n}
. Then

J({\bf g})=A_{\lambda}\cdot J({\bf f})
.

The following lemma follows from the chain rule: if

, and
{\bf g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})
where
g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]
, then for
i=1,\ldots,m
,
\partial_{i}f(g_{1},\ldots,g_{n})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\partial_{j}f)\circ{\bf g}\cdot\partial_{i}g_{j}
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
{\bf f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
with
f_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and
{\bf g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
with
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Suppose
{\bf g}={\bf f}\circ\lambda
where
\lambda
is a linear map from
k^{n}
to
k^{n}
. Then

J({\bf g})=J({\bf f})\circ\lambda\cdot A_{\lambda}
.

From the above two lemmas we have the following (see Lemma 18 of
[
2
]
).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
{\bf f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
with
f_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and
{\bf g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})
with
g_{i}\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Suppose
{\bf g}=\mu\circ{\bf f}\circ\lambda
where
\mu
is a linear map from
k^{m}
to
k^{m}
and
\lambda
is a linear map from
k^{n}
to
k^{n}
. Then

J({\bf g})=A_{\mu}\cdot J({\bf f})\circ\lambda\cdot A_{\lambda}
.

Now consider the case of square semi-local polynomial systems, that is,
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda
in
n
variables, where
{\mathcal{F}}
is
c
-local, divided into
n/c
subsystems, each containing
c
polynomials in
c
local variables.

Suppose we are given
{\mathcal{G}}
and
c
-local
{\mathcal{F}}
and we want to find
\mu,\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
such that
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda
. By Lemma
5.3
,
J({\mathcal{G}})=A_{\mu}\cdot J({\mathcal{F}})\circ\lambda\cdot A_{\lambda}
, hence

\det J({\mathcal{G}})=\det A_{\mu}\cdot\det(J({\mathcal{F}})\circ\lambda)\cdot\det A_{\lambda}
. Let
\det A_{\mu}\det A_{\lambda}=\alpha\in k
. Since
\det(J({\mathcal{F}})\circ\lambda)=(\det{{\mathcal{F}}})\circ\lambda
, we have arrived at a polynomial equivalence problem: given
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
and
\det J({\mathcal{F}})
, to find
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
and a constant
\alpha\in k
such that
\det J({\mathcal{G}})=\alpha(\det J({\mathcal{F}}))\circ\lambda
.

Note that
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
and
\det J({\mathcal{F}})
should not be given in dense form in general since their degree is
O(n)
. Instead, given a polynomial
H
in
n
variables of degree
d
in dense from, an arithmetic circuit of size polynomial in
n
and
d
can be constructed for the evaluation of
\det J({\mathcal{H}})
on input
x\in k^{n}
(see Theorem 17 of
[
2
]
).

This approach has been applied to solve the isomorphism of polynomials problem efficiently when
{\mathcal{F}}
is special. For example, the case
{\mathcal{F}}=(x_{1}^{d},\ldots,x_{n}^{d})
is successfully tackled in
[
2
]
(see also
[
14
]
). We will extend the attack to square 1-semi-local polynomial systems (the case
{\mathcal{F}}
is 1-local).

5.3.2.
Determinant-of-Jacobian method for solving square 1-semi-local case of IP problem

As noted before, solving the polynomial system
{\mathcal{G}}(x)=y
also reduces to solving the isomorphism of polynomials problem with
{\mathcal{G}}
being 1-semi-local: to find
\mu,\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k)
such that
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda
.

Lemma 5.4
.

Suppose
h=f\circ\lambda\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
where
f
is univariate defined over
k
and
\lambda
is a linear form in
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
over
k
. Then every irreducible factor of
h
over
k
is of the form
g\circ\lambda
where
g
is an irreducible factor of
f
.

Proof
Write
f=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(x-\alpha_{i})
with
\alpha_{i}\in\bar{k}
. Then
f\circ\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^{d}(\lambda-\alpha_{i})
. So if
g
is an irreducible factor of
f\circ\lambda
. Then
g
is the product of
\lambda-\tau^{j}(\alpha)
where
\tau
is the Frobenius automorphism of
\bar{k}
over
k
and
\alpha=\alpha_{i}
for some
i
. Then
g=f^{\prime}\circ\lambda
where
f^{\prime}
is the product of the conjugate factors
x-\tau^{j}(\alpha)
, and
f^{\prime}
is an irreducible factor of
f
. The lemma follows.
\Box

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
g\in k[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
. Suppose there exists univariate
f\in k[x]
and a linear map
\lambda:k^{n}\to k
(
\lambda(0)=0
), such that
g=f\circ\lambda
.
Then
\lambda
is the unique linear factor of
g-g(0)
with
\lambda(0)=0
, hence
\lambda
can be obtained by factoring
g-g(0)
.

Proof
We have
f(x)-f(0)=x^{e}\prod_{i=1}^{d-e}(x-\alpha_{i})
where
d=\deg f
,
e\geq 1
,
\alpha_{i}\in\bar{k}
with
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
for all
i
. Since
g(0)=f(\lambda(0))=f(0)
, we have

g-g(0)=f\circ\lambda-f(\lambda(0))=(f-f(0))\circ\lambda=\lambda^{e}\prod_{i=1}^{d-e}\lambda-\alpha_{i},

and the lemma easily follows.
\Box

As mentioned before, given
{\mathcal{G}}
in dense from, a polynomial size arithmetic circuit can be constructed for the evaluation of
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
on input
x\in k^{n}

[
2
]
. Then by applying Kaltofen’s algorithm
[
13
]
with the arithmetic circuit for
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
as the input, we can factor
\det(J({\mathcal{G}}))
over
k
in random polynomial time.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}=(f_{1}(x_{1}),\ldots,f_{n}(x_{n}))
. Then
J({\mathcal{F}})
is diagonal with
\partial_{i}f_{i}
as the
(i,i)
-th entry. Put
h_{i}=\partial_{i}f_{i}
. Then

\det(J({\mathcal{F}})\circ\lambda)=(\det J({\mathcal{F}}))\circ\lambda=\prod_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}(\lambda_{i}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})).

By Lemma
5.3
,
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
is a constant multiple of
(\det J({\mathcal{F}}))\circ\lambda
.
So by Lemma
5.4
, every irreducible factor
g
of
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
over
k
is of the form
h\circ\lambda_{i}
for some
i
with
h
being an irreducible factor of
h_{i}
. By Lemma
5.5
, up to constant multiple,
\lambda_{i}
can be obtained as the unique linear form that appears in the factorization of
g-g(0)
.
It follows that we can obtain
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
up to constant multiples and permutation.

5.3.3.
Square
c
-semi-local case for
c>1

Suppose
n=cm
and the variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
are divided into
m
groups, the first group
x_{1},\ldots,x_{c}
; the second group
x_{c+1},\ldots,x_{2c}
, and so on.
Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
is
c
-local, so that
{\mathcal{F}}
as a set is divided
m
subsets
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
where
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
depends on the
i
-th group of variables. Let
h_{i}=\det J({\mathcal{F}}_{i})
. Then
\det J({\mathcal{G}})=\alpha\prod_{i=1}^{m}h_{i}(\lambda_{ci-c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{ci})
with
\alpha\in k
. By factoring
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
we obtain irreducible factors of these

h_{i}(\lambda_{ci-c+1},\ldots,\lambda_{ci})
. In special cases, we can deduce useful information on the
\lambda_{i}
’s.
For example, if the first polynomial
f_{1}
of
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
is univariate then
\partial_{i}f_{1}=0
for
i\neq 1
, it follows that
\partial_{1}f_{1}
is a factor of
h_{1}=\det J({\mathcal{F}}_{1})
. It then follows that
(\partial_{1}f_{1})\circ\lambda_{1}
is a factor of
\det({\mathcal{G}})
. So
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
has an irreducible factor of the form
h\circ\lambda_{1}
where
h
is univariate. After factoring
\det J({\mathcal{G}})
, we may try every irreducible factor as a candidate and apply the attack for the 1-semi-local case to it to obtain
\lambda_{1}
up to a constant multiple.

Thus, for
c>1
, the determinant-of-Jacobian attack may be mounted to derive partial information about
\lambda
.
It will be interesting to investigate the scope of square
c
-semi-local systems where a full attack can be made. However we will turn our attention on constructive means for preventing the determinant-of-Jacobian attack.

5.3.4.
Non-square semi-local construction that avoids the determinant-of-Jacobian attack

We note that the determinant-of-Jacobian attack, which is based on the equality in Lemma
5.3
, depends critically on the the system being square.
To avoid such attacks we apply a simple trick to modify a square
c
-semi-local construction into a non-square
c
-semi-local construction, for
c>1
.

Suppose we have a local
c
-polynomial map that
{\bf f}:k^{c}\to k^{c}
defined by
c
homogeneous polynomials
f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
of degree
d
in
c
variables
x_{1},\ldots,x_{c}
. Let
h
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
in
x_{1},\ldots x_{c}
. Then the map
{\bf f}^{\prime}:k^{c}\to k^{c+1}
sending
x\in k^{c}
to
(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{c}(x),h(x))
is injective. For security consideration we also require
h
not to be linearly dependent on
f_{1},\ldots f_{c}
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
is a
c
-local system in
n=mc
variables divided into
m
blocks
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, where each
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
consists of
c
homogeneous polynomials of degree
d
in
c
local variables
x_{ci-c+1},\ldots,x_{ci}
. Apply the trick to modify each
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}:k^{c}\to k^{c}
into some
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\prime}:k^{c}\to k^{c+1}
by adding a homogeneous
H_{i}
of degree
d
in the same
c
local variables, we get a non-square
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}:k^{n}\to k^{n+m}
defined by
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
.

Below we describe a concrete construction of 2-semi-local
{\mathcal{G}}:k^{2m}\to k^{3m}
where
n=2m
.

As before,
choose a finite field
k
of characteristic
p
large enough (say linear in the security parameter), and
choose
n
that is large enough, say linear in the security parameter.

Assume
|k^{*}|
is not divisible by 3, so that
x^{3}
defines a bijection on
k
. Let
{\bf f}=(f_{1},f_{2})
where
f_{1}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}^{3}
, and
f_{2}(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{2}^{3}
. Then
{\bf f}
is bijective on
k^{2}
. Let
h(x_{1},x_{2})=x_{1}^{2}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}^{2}
. Then
{\bf f}^{\prime}=(f_{1},f_{2},h)
defines an injective map from
k^{2}
to
k^{3}
.

We construct 2-local polynomial system
{\mathcal{F}}
with
m
blocks
{\mathcal{F}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}_{m}
using
{\bf f}
. Thus
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
consists of
f_{1}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i}),f_{2}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}
defines a bijection on
k^{n}
.

We construct 2-local polynomial system
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
with
m
blocks
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{m}
using
{\bf f}^{\prime}
. Thus
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{i}
consists of
f_{1}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i}),f_{2}(x_{2i-1},x_{2i}),h(x_{2i-1},x_{2i})
. Then
{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}
defines an injective map from
k^{n}
to
k^{n+m}
.

Choose random
\lambda\in Gl_{n}(k),\mu\in Gl_{n+m}(k)
. Let
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\circ\lambda
.
Then announce
{\mathcal{G}}
as the public encryption map from
k^{n}
to
k^{n+m}
.

The secret decryption key consists of
\lambda
and
\mu
.

From the equality
{\mathcal{G}}=\mu\circ{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\circ\lambda
it should be clear why we require
h
to be linearly independent of
f_{1},\ldots,f_{c}
. Otherwise since
\mu^{-1}{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\circ\lambda
, such a linear dependence will induce linear conditions on
\mu^{-1}
.

The system defined by
{\mathcal{G}}
is not square, consisting of
n+m
polynomials in
n
variables. So the determinant-of-Jacobian attack cannot be applied directly.

In trying to adapt the attack to the non-square case, we may consider the submatrix
B
of
A_{\mu}^{-1}
consisting of the
n=2m
rows numbered
3i-2
,
3i-1
for
i=1,\ldots,m
and note that
B{\mathcal{G}}={\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda
. Note that
J(B{\mathcal{G}})=J({\mathcal{F}}\circ\lambda)=J({\mathcal{F}})\circ\lambda
is a diagonal matrix with
3\lambda_{i}^{2}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
on the diagonal.
However
B
is unknown, moreover
B
and
J({\mathcal{G}})
are not square matrices. So not only
BJ({\mathcal{G}})
cannot be computed, but also
\det(BJ({\mathcal{G}})
, being in this case a complicated function in the entries of
B
and
J({\mathcal{G}})
, cannot be split. Therefore useful information about the determinant cannot be extracted simply from
J({\mathcal{G}})
.

The simple construction described above resists all the attacks considered in this paper. It remains an interesting question whether an efficient attack can be found.

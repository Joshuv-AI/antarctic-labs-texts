Abstract
Skyrmions are particle-like vortices of magnetization with non-trivial topology, which are usually stabilized by Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interactions (DMI) in noncentrosymmetric bulk materials. Exceptions are centrosymmetric Gd - and Eu -based skyrmion-lattice (SkL) hosts with zero DMI, where both the SkL stabilization mechanisms and magnetic ground states remain controversial. We address these here by investigating both the static and dynamical spin properties of the centrosymmetric SkL host Gd 2 ​ PdSi 3 \text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}} using muon spectroscopy ( μ \mu SR). We find that spin fluctuations in the non-coplanar SkL phase are highly anisotropic, implying that spin anisotropy plays a prominent role in stabilizing this phase. We also observe strongly-anisotropic spin dynamics in the ground-state (IC-1) incommensurate magnetic phase of the material, indicating that it hosts a meron-like multi- q q structure. In contrast, the higher-field, coplanar IC-2 phase is found to be single- q q with nearly-isotropic spin dynamics.

Fig.

1
(a)].
Near
T\approx T_{N}
the muon relaxation rate exhibits a critical divergence characteristic of a continuous phase transition as we enter the IC-2 phase, and at
T<T_{N}
it crosses over into a low-

\lambda_{\mathrm{L}}\propto T^{p}
with
p=1.2(3)
.
The observed isotropy of spin fluctuations in this phase [
Fig.

Eq.

1
] applicable, with the measured
p\approx 1
indicating that the IC-2 phase is a 1D (single-
q
) magnetic structure (

), as previously suggested
Kurumaji et al. 2019
;
Hirschberger et al. 2020a
.
This stands in contrast to multi-
q
IC-1 and SkL phases found at lower applied fields.

To summarize, our finding of different low-
T
in-plane and out-of-plane spin fluctuations in both
non-coplanar SkL and ground-state IC-1 phases of centrosymmetric
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
contrasts with isotropic fluctuations found in the
coplanar single-
q
IC-2 phase.
This should supply a clue to the stabilization mechanism for centrosymmetric skyrmions.
In contrast to DMI-stabilized skyrmions
Mochizuki 2012
;
Garst et al. 2017
;
Xing et al. 2020
;
Weber et al. 2022
;
Soda et al. 2023
, systematic predictions for SkL spin dynamics for different stabilization-mechanisms are lacking.
Nevertheless, it seems unlikely that a spin model without strong intrinsic anisotropy could explain the observed highly-anisotropic SkL and IC-1 spin dynamics.
This agrees with suggestions that spin anisotropy
Leonov and Mostovoy 2015
;
Ju et al. 2023
;
Hayami 2023
, combined with long-range interactions, is important for stabilizing the SkL phase
Paddison et al. 2022
;
Hayami and Motome 2021
;
Paddison et al. 2024
;
Hirschberger et al. 2021
.
A quantitative determination of the anisotropy of intrinsic magnetic interactions in
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
would thus be crucial
Ju et al. 2023
.
We note that anisotropic magnetic interactions are also found in other representatives of the hexagonal
\text{R\/}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
family (

= rare earth)

;
Frontzek et al. 2006
, in the tetragonal centrosymmetric SkL hosts
\text{GdRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{GdRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

Sarkar et al. 2024
;
Nomoto and Arita 2023
;
Khanh et al. 2020
;
Garnier et al. 1996
;
Garnier et al. 1995
, and even (weakly) in cubic
Gd
-based magnets

magnetic structure (triple-

from RXS and

While double-

(square-lattice) ML-like states have recently been reported in thin-plate DMI-based
\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}\text{Zn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{GdRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{GdRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Si}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

magnetic ML. Furthermore, the IC-1 phase is the ZF ground state in
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{Co}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{8}}}\text{Zn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{9}}}\text{Mn}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}

\text{GdRu}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{Ge}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

The multi-

In conclusion, in our muon-spectroscopy study of centrosymmetric
\text{Gd}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{PdSi}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}
we have found large anisotropy in spin dynamics, with qualitatively different behaviour of dominant out-of-plane and subdominant in-plane spin fluctuations in the SkL phase.
We have also established the meron-like triple-
q
nature of its incommensurate IC-1 ground state with dominant in-plane, and nearly-absent out-of-plane, fluctuations.
The higher-field IC-2 phase was found to be coplanar and single-
q
with isotropic spin fluctuations.
Our results suggest that the enigmatic stabilization mechanism behind SkL phases in centrosymmetric
Gd
- and
Eu
-based materials is likely to be intimately related to spin anisotropy,
and not solely RKKY+dipolar.
Further local-probe studies of these and related centrosymmetric compounds
Li et al. 2023
;
Khatua et al. 2023
;
Kotsanidis et al. 1990
;
Tang et al. 2011
should be informative in exploring this.
Studies of anisotropic spin excitations and their dispersions via inelastic neutron scattering
Paddison et al. 2022
, and the anisotropy of static spin correlations via RXS, especially on single crystals, would also be valuable.
Finally, our
\mu
SR methods could also be extended to study low-
T
spin anisotropy and dynamics of metastable skyrmions in DMI-based SkL hosts
Li et al. 2023
.

Relativistic Khronon Theory in agreement with Modified Newtonian Dynamics and Large-Scale Cosmology
Luc Blanchet, Constantinos Skordis

Abstract
We propose an extension of General Relativity (GR) based on a space-time foliation by three-dimensional space-like hypersurfaces labeled by the Khronon scalar field τ \tau . We show that this theory (i) leads to modified Newtonian dynamics (MOND) at galactic scales for stationary systems; (ii) recovers GR plus a cosmological constant in the strong field regime; (iii) is in agreement with the standard cosmological model and the observed cosmic microwave background anisotropies at linear cosmological scales, where the theory reduces to a subset of the generalized dark matter (GDM) model. We compute the second order action on a Minkowski background and show that it contains the usual tensor modes of GR and a scalar degree of freedom with dispersion relation ω = 0 \omega=0 . We find that the deconstrained Hamiltonian is bounded from below for wavenumbers larger than ∼ 10 − 31 ​ eV \sim 10^{-31}\,\text{eV} and unbounded for smaller wavenumbers.

The main drawback of the BM theory, that concerns us here, is that it does not explain the effects commonly attributed to dark matter, which occur at large scales in cosmological perturbations. We therefore extend the BM theory by adding, in a quite natural way, a kinetic term for the Khronon scalar field. Following

The Khronon behaves like a GDM fluid with time-dependent equation of state in the background, and with zero viscosity and non-zero,

Using the GDM framework we investigate the allowed constraints on cosmology and the CMB (namely the cosmological dark matter which should be close enough to a “pure dust” model) which are consistent with recovering the MOND phenomenology at galactic scales. In particular we find that a kinetic term for the Khronon based on the Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) form of the action reproduces in a natural way all observations.

Finally, we also show that the theory recovers GR plus a cosmological constant in the strong field, high acceleration regime, and in particular has the same parametrized post-Newtonian (PPN) parameters as GR for tests in the Solar System.

where once again we have used the equality of the potentials
\phi
and
\psi
through (

).

3.2.4
Consistency of the PN field equations

In analogy with the matter equations (
16
) and following
[
47
]
, we define the Khronon “mass density”

\rho_{\tau}\equiv-\frac{1}{4\pi G}\left[\vec{\nabla}\cdot\left({\cal J}_{\cal Y}\vec{\nabla}\Xi\right)+\mu^{2}\Xi\right]\,,

(27a)

and the Khronon “velocity” field

v_{\tau}^{i}\equiv-\vec{\nabla}^{i}\sigma\,.

(27b)

Such definitions are motivated by the fact that the components of the Khronon stress-energy tensor (
12
) in leading PN order take the same form as for ordinary matter,
i.e.

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{00}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\tau}c^{2}+{\cal O}(c^{0})\,,

(28a)

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{0i}

\displaystyle=\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{i}c+{\cal O}(c^{-1})\,,

(28b)

\displaystyle{\cal T}^{ij}

\displaystyle={\cal O}(c^{0})\,.

(28c)

The above PN scaling of the Khronon tensor justifies the PN expansion we have adopted for the metric (
15
) and Khronon field (
17
).

With the previous definitions, we may rewrite the Einstein field equations (
25
) and (
26
) in the useful form

\displaystyle\Delta\phi

\displaystyle=4\pi G\bigl({\rho_{m}}+\rho_{\tau}\bigr)+{\cal O}(c^{-2})\,,

(29a)

\displaystyle\Delta\zeta^{i}-\vec{\nabla}^{i}\vec{\nabla}_{j}\zeta^{j}-\vec{\nabla}^{i}\dot{\phi}

\displaystyle=4\pi G\left({\rho_{m}}v_{m}^{i}+\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{i}\right)+{\cal O}(c^{-2})\,,

(29b)

which shows that the Einstein field equations, together with the continuity equation (
16d
) for matter, imply the continuity equation for the Khronon quantities (
27
),
i.e.

\dot{\rho}_{\tau}+\vec{\nabla}_{i}\left(\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{i}\right)={\cal O}(c^{-2})\,.

(30)

The latter equation is nothing but the slow motion approximation of the Khronon field equation
\nabla_{\mu}{\cal S}^{\mu}=0
since from eq. (
14
) we have

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{0}

\displaystyle=-4\pi G\,\rho_{\tau}+{\cal O}(c^{-2})\,,

(31a)

\displaystyle{\cal S}^{i}

\displaystyle=-4\pi G\,\frac{\rho_{\tau}v_{\tau}^{i}}{c}+{\cal O}(c^{-3})\,.

(31b)

and the Kronon equation reduces to
\dot{{\cal S}}^{0}+c\,\partial_{i}{\cal S}^{i}={\cal O}(c^{-2})
. This shows the consistency of the Khronon equation with the Einstein field equations in the slow motion approximation.

3.2.5
Check in adapted coordinates

By performing a coordinate transformation
\{x^{\mu}\}\longrightarrow\{\bar{x}^{\mu}\}
such that
\bar{t}\equiv\bar{x}^{0}/c=t+\sigma/c^{2}

\bar{x}^{i}=x^{i}

\bar{\tau}=\bar{t}+{\cal O}(c^{-4})\,,

(32)

while the ADM form of the metric in these coordinates reads

\displaystyle\bar{N}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\Xi}{c^{2}}+{\cal O}(c^{-4})\,,

(33a)

\displaystyle\bar{N}_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{c}\vec{\nabla}_{i}\sigma+\frac{1}{c^{3}}\left[4\zeta_{i}+2\phi\vec{\nabla}_{i}\sigma-\sigma\vec{\nabla}_{i}\dot{\sigma}\right]+{\cal O}(c^{-5})\,,

(33b)

\displaystyle\bar{q}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\left(1-\frac{2\psi}{c^{2}}\right)-\frac{1}{c^{2}}\vec{\nabla}_{i}\sigma\vec{\nabla}_{j}\sigma+{\cal O}(c^{-4})\,,

(33c)

where we recall that

To summarize, the MOND dynamics is recovered in the unitary gauge as well, as it should be, since physics does not depend on the gauge choice. This was the approach taken in Ref.
[
18

. However, as was realized in Ref.
[
47

\bar{N}_{i}

5
5

3.2.6
Stationary configurations

We now consider the case where the system is, in addition, stationary. This means that
\dot{\phi}=\dot{\sigma}=0
for the fields and
\dot{{\rho_{m}}}=\dot{v}_{m}^{i}=0
for the matter, thus, the continuity equation for matter reduces to the constraint
\vec{\nabla}_{i}\left({\rho_{m}}v_{m}^{i}\right)=0

\vec{\nabla}\cdot(\rho_{\tau}\vec{\nabla}\sigma)=0

\sigma=0

and the acceleration vector (

\displaystyle A_{0}

\displaystyle={\cal O}(c^{-3})\,,

(34a)

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle=\vec{\nabla}_{i}\phi+{\cal O}(c^{-2})\,.

(34b)

Thus the acceleration of the congruence of unit vector field

reduces to the physical Newtonian acceleration, and from (

becomes the modified Poisson (or modified Helmholtz) equation

\vec{\nabla}\cdot\left[\left(1+{\cal J}_{\cal Y}\right)\vec{\nabla}\phi\right]+\mu^{2}\phi=4\pi G{\rho_{m}}+{\cal O}(c^{-2})\,.

(35)

3.2.7
Choice of function
{\cal J}({\cal Y})

The equation (
35

{\cal J}({\cal Y})
. Indeed, one can get a wide variety of solutions through such a choice, many of which may not necessarily lead to either Newtonian nor MONDian dynamics and may not fit observations altogether. Our goal is to specify the conditions that
{\cal J}
needs to satisfy in order that both Newtonian and MONDian regimes emerge under appropriate conditions. The fact that we shall recover the MOND regime only for stationary systems is still satisfying because most tests of MOND (rotation curves of galaxies, Tully-Fisher relation,

) are performed in stationary situations.

From the first term in (
35

{f}(y)
, where
y\equiv|\nabla\phi|/a_{0}
, as
6
6

6

We denote the MOND function as
{f}(y)
instead of the usual

to avoid conflict with the constant scale
\mu

). We may also denote

the “gravitational susceptibility” of the dark matter (in the sense of
[

).

{f}(y)=1+{\cal J}_{\cal Y}({\cal Y})\,,

(36)

and from (

to be
{\cal Y}\simeq a_{0}^{2}y^{2}/c^{4}
in the nonrelativistic limit. Admissible functions

are those for which the MOND function is linear,

. The latter constant can be set to be 1 so that

represents the measured Newton’s gravitational constant.

Integrating (
36

{\cal J}
in order for MOND to emerge in the limit

{\cal J}=\Lambda-{\cal Y}+\frac{2c^{2}}{3a_{0}}\,{\cal Y}^{3/2}+{\cal O}\left(y^{4}/a_{0}^{2}\right)\,,

(37)

where
\Lambda
is the cosmological constant. Note that the low acceleration regime

is also the regime of linear cosmological perturbations, since the acceleration
A_{\mu}
is a first order quantity in cosmological perturbations. Thus,
{\cal Y}
is second order in cosmology and therefore,

the CMB.

With the above condition on the function
{\cal J}
,
7
7

7

Note that
{\cal J}
is always of order
{\cal O}(c^{-4})
, and
{\cal J}_{{\cal Y}}
of order
{\cal O}(c^{0})

.

[

(see also
[

). As such, the

We expect the phenomenology of spherically symmetric solutions to the Khronon theory to be very similar to that in the case of the
AeST
theory, studied in
[
59
]
. The effect of the
\mu^{2}\phi
term in eq. (

\phi

r_{C}\sim\left(\frac{r_{M}}{\mu^{2}}\right)^{1/3}\quad\text{with}\quad r_{M}\sim\sqrt{\frac{GM}{a_{0}}}\,,

Finally, in order to recover the Newtonian law in the high acceleration regime

4
Cosmology

4.1
Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) cosmology

4.1.1
General FLRW background

We consider first a FLRW background cosmology in the synchronous coordinate system. For simplicity, we choose
c=1
from this section onwards. The metric is

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\mathrm{d}t^{2}+a^{2}\gamma^{\kappa}_{ij}\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

(41)

where
t
is the cosmic time and
\gamma^{\kappa}_{ij}
is a spatially homogeneous and isotropic metric of spatial curvature
\kappa
. The symmetries of the FLRW spacetime also require that the Khronon field is a function of time only, that is,
\tau=\overline{\tau}(t)
, so that the background value of
{\cal Q}
is

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal Q}}=\dot{\overline{\tau}}\,,

(42)

with the dot denoting derivatives with respect to cosmic time
t
, and the overbar always denoting the background FLRW value. Furthermore, the acceleration vanishes in the background,
\overline{A}_{\mu}=0
.

Defining the Hubble parameter as
H\equiv\dot{a}/a
, the Einstein equations (
9
) lead to the Friedmann equations

\displaystyle 3H^{2}+\frac{3\kappa}{a^{2}}-\Lambda

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I\not={\cal K}}\overline{\rho}_{I}+\overline{{\cal Q}}\,\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}-\overline{{\cal K}}\,,

(43a)

\displaystyle-\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}+\frac{\kappa}{a^{2}}\right)+\Lambda

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I\not={\cal K}}\overline{P}_{I}+\overline{{\cal K}}\,,

(43b)

where
\overline{{\cal K}}\equiv{\cal K}(\overline{{\cal Q}})
and
\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}\equiv{\cal K}_{{\cal Q}}(\overline{{\cal Q}})
, and as indicated the index
I\not={\cal K}
runs over all types of ordinary matter species,
i.e.
, excluding the contribution of the Khronon field. The cosmological constant
\Lambda
is the one appearing in eq. (
37
). The Khronon contribution makes it possible to defining the Khronon energy density and pressure respectively as

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi G}\left(\overline{{\cal Q}}\,\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}-\overline{{\cal K}}\right)\,,

(44a)

\displaystyle\overline{P}_{{\cal K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\overline{{\cal K}}}{8\pi G}\,.

(44b)

Thus, on a FLRW background, the Khronon can be casted as a perfect fluid with time-dependent equation of state
w(t)
given by

\displaystyle w\equiv\frac{\overline{P}_{{\cal K}}}{\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}}=\frac{\overline{{\cal K}}}{\overline{{\cal Q}}\,\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}-\overline{{\cal K}}}\,.

(45)

As the equation of state
w
is time-dependent, this also prompts the definition of the adiabatic speed of sound
c_{\rm ad}^{2}
defined as

\displaystyle c_{\rm ad}^{2}\equiv\frac{\mathrm{d}\overline{P}_{{\cal K}}}{\mathrm{d}\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}}=\frac{\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}}{\overline{{\cal Q}}\,\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}{\cal Q}}}\,,

(46)

which obeys the well-known equation
\dot{w}=3(w-c_{\rm ad}^{2})(1+w)H
, see
e.g.

[
52
]
.

The above casting of the Khronon field into a perfect fluid, is consistent with the Bianchi identities and energy conservation,
\dot{\overline{\rho}}_{{\cal K}}+3H(1+w)\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}=0
, which can also be derived from the Khronon equation
\nabla_{\mu}{\cal S}^{\mu}=0
, where (
14
) implies
\overline{{\cal S}}^{0}=-c^{2}\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}
and
\overline{{\cal S}}^{i}=0
. Specifically, these equations may be integrated once to give

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal K}}_{{\cal Q}}=\frac{I_{0}}{a^{3}}\,,

(47)

where
I_{0}
is a constant set by initial conditions. Note that the non-integrated version of the Khronon equation with (
14
), leads in the FLRW background to

\displaystyle\dot{\overline{{\cal Q}}}+3Hc_{\rm ad}^{2}\overline{{\cal Q}}=0\,,

(48)

a relation which can be sometimes useful in reducing formulas.

4.1.2
FLRW background in adapted coordinates

Rather than the synchronous coordinate system one can also use the adapted coordinates of appendix
A
where
t=\tau
. In this case, one cannot set the lapse to
1
as in the synchronous case, and the metric must take the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-\overline{N}^{2}\mathrm{d}\tau^{2}+a^{2}\gamma^{\kappa}_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

(49)

where
\overline{N}=\overline{N}(\tau)=1/\overline{{\cal Q}}(\tau)
is at this point unspecified. This leads to the Friedmann equations

\displaystyle 3\frac{\mathcal{H}^{2}}{\overline{N}^{2}}+\frac{3\kappa}{a^{2}}-\Lambda+\overline{{\cal K}}+{\overline{N}}\,\overline{{\cal K}}_{N}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I\not={\cal K}}\overline{\rho}_{I}\,,

(50a)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\overline{N}^{2}}\Bigl[2\mathcal{H}^{\prime}+3\mathcal{H}^{2}\left(1-2c_{\rm ad}^{2}\right)\Bigr]-\frac{\kappa}{a^{2}}+\Lambda-\overline{{\cal K}}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I\not={\cal K}}\overline{P}_{I}\,,

(50b)

where we pose
\mathcal{H}=\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}=\overline{N}H
, and the dependence of
\overline{{\cal K}}
on
\overline{N}
is via the condition
\overline{{\cal Q}}=1/\overline{N}
. The Khronon equation (
14
) in the background is equivalent to
\overline{N}^{\prime}=3\mathcal{H}c_{\rm ad}^{2}\overline{N}
, and yields

\displaystyle\overline{N}^{2}\overline{{\cal K}}_{N}=-\frac{I_{0}}{a^{3}}\,,

(51)

which becomes an algebraic relation determining
\overline{N}(a)
. This in turn, is used in (
50
) turning it into a modified Friedmann equation and removing also the fluid interpretation of the Khronon (recall that
I
runs over all types of matter species but excludes the Khronon).

4.1.3
Approximate dust solutions

Specifying a function
{\cal K}({\cal Q})
, completely determines the form of
\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}
,
\overline{P}_{{\cal K}}
,
w
and

. In addition, eq. (
47
) may then be inverted to find a solution for

, and equivalently (
51

. This then, when inserted into (

. Thus the form of

fully determines the type of cosmological evolution that ensues, and, indeed, depending on this form, one can have cosmologies which are not necessarily compatible with observations in the absence of a dark matter component. Examples are, the stiff-fluid case where
{\cal K}\propto{\cal Q}^{2}
(
w=1
) and the radiation-type case
{\cal K}\propto{\cal Q}^{4}
(
w=1/3
).

Our interest is to determine the conditions on
{\cal K}({\cal Q})
that may lead to approximate dust solutions for the Khronon, in accordance with observations. Let us first say that exact dust solutions,
i.e.

w=0
, are impossible as they would imply that
{\cal K}=0
. A simple case emerges following
[
48

49
]

{\cal K}({\cal Q})
is expandable as a Taylor series around
{\cal Q}=1
. This was the motivation for the specific choice made in eq. (
7
).

We first focus on this specific case, that we refer to as the “quadratic” function, which we recall here:

\displaystyle{\cal K}({\cal Q})=\mu^{2}({\cal Q}-1)^{2}\,.

(52)

Then
{\cal K}_{{\cal Q}}=2\mu^{2}({\cal Q}-1)
and eq. (
47
) can be solved for
\overline{{\cal Q}}
to get

\displaystyle\overline{{\cal Q}}=1+\frac{I_{0}}{2\mu^{2}a^{3}}\,,

(53)

where
I_{0}
is a constant. Hence we find that the energy density of the Khronon is

\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}=\frac{\mu^{2}}{8\pi G}\left(\overline{{\cal Q}}^{2}-1\right)=\frac{I_{0}}{8\pi Ga^{3}}\left(1+\frac{\tilde{w}_{0}}{a^{3}}\right)\,,

(54)

where we posed
\tilde{w}_{0}=\frac{I_{0}}{4\mu^{2}}
. The constant
I_{0}
is directly related to the Khronon energy density today
\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K},0}=\frac{I_{0}}{8\pi G}(1+\tilde{w}_{0})
(
i.e.
when
a=1
); in particular it is positive meaning also that
\overline{{\cal Q}}\geqslant 1
. We can rewrite

\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}=\frac{\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K},0}}{a^{3}}\,\frac{1+\frac{\tilde{w}_{0}}{a^{3}}}{1+\tilde{w}_{0}}=\frac{\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K},0}}{a^{3}}\left(1-w_{0}+\frac{w_{0}}{a^{3}}\right)\,,

where we have also introduced the value of the Khronon equation of state today

, see (

\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K},0}=\frac{3H_{0}^{2}}{8\pi G}\,\Omega_{{\cal K},0}\,,

where

is the Hubble-Lemaître parameter and
\Omega_{{\cal K},0}\sim 0.26
is the measured fraction of dark matter. From (

), the Khronon energy density is approximately that of dust,
i.e.
up to some additional correction scaling as
a^{-6}
, while the equation of state evolves as

w=\frac{\overline{{\cal Q}}-1}{\overline{{\cal Q}}+1}=\frac{\tilde{w}_{0}}{\tilde{w}_{0}+a^{3}}\,.

(57)

In the deep past,
a\rightarrow 0
and

so that the Khronon with the quadratic functional form of

The adiabatic speed of sound is likewise given as

4.2
Linear perturbations on a FLRW background

We now consider linear perturbations on a FLRW background and show that one can recast the Khronon equations into the generalized dark matter (GDM) model

Keeping the synchronous coordinate system for the background FLRW dynamics, and considering only scalar modes, the metric is

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=

\displaystyle-\left(1+2\phi\right)\mathrm{d}t^{2}-2\vec{\nabla}_{i}\zeta\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x^{i}+a^{2}\bigl[\left(1-2\psi\right)\gamma_{ij}^{\kappa}+\hat{D}_{ij}\nu\bigr]\mathrm{d}x^{i}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\,,

where

\displaystyle\tau=\overline{\tau}+\sigma\,,

\overline{{\cal Q}}=\dot{\overline{\tau}}
as in the previous section, and
{\cal Q}=\overline{{\cal Q}}\left(1-\phi\right)+\dot{\sigma}
, while the normal

to the spacetime foliation and acceleration

4.3
Khronon cosmology and the MOND limit

4.3.1
Tension between the cosmology and MOND in the case of the quadratic function

As discussed above, with any choice for the function
{\cal K}({\cal Q})
the Khronon field behaves like GDM on a FLRW background plus linear perturbations. Furthermore, the functional choice
{\cal K}=\mu^{2}({\cal Q}-1)^{2}
, the “quadratic” function, leads to approximate dust solutions in the late universe which can make the Khronon in agreement with cosmological observations, notably the CMB and large scale structure, provided these dust solutions can be extended as far back as the radiation-matter equality. However, the equation of state (
57

in the Early Universe. The turning point may be found from (
57

\tilde{w}_{0}\sim a^{3}
.
10
10

10

From (

The constraints on GDM found in

at the

\tilde{w}_{0}\approx w_{0}<5.3\times 10^{-16}\qquad\mbox{(for cosmology)}\,,

(76)

\tilde{w}_{0}=\frac{2\pi G\,\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K},0}}{\mu^{2}c^{2}}=\frac{3H_{0}^{2}\,\Omega_{{\cal K},0}}{4\mu^{2}c^{2}}\,.

Given that from observations

Such a bound is incompatible with having a MOND limit in galaxies, as even for our own galaxy, we should have MOND behaviour out to tens of

\mu^{-1}\gtrsim 100\,{\rm kpc}\,,

4.3.2
Case of functions with higher (cubic and quartic) powers

From the discussion in

\displaystyle w=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{I_{0}}{2\mu^{2}{\cal K}_{n+1}\,a^{3}}\right)^{1/n}\left[n+1+n\left(\frac{I_{0}}{2\mu^{2}{\cal K}_{n+1}a^{3}}\right)^{1/n}\right]^{-1}\,.

4.3.3
Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) type of functions

We would like to explore a function which has a well-defined Taylor expansion at

{\cal Q}\gg 1

\displaystyle{\cal K}({\cal Q})=\frac{2\mu^{2}}{\lambda_{D}}\left[1-\sqrt{1-\lambda_{D}\left({\cal Q}-1\right)^{2}}\right]\,,

where
\mu

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{0}}{8\pi Ga^{3}}+{\cal O}\left(\frac{1}{a^{6}}\right)\qquad\qquad\qquad~\text{(when $a\to\infty$, late universe)}\,,

(90a)

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}_{{\cal K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{I_{0}}{8\pi Ga^{3}}\Bigl(1+\lambda_{D}^{-1/2}\Bigr)+{\cal O}\left(a^{0}\right)\qquad\text{(when $a\to 0$, early universe)}\,.

\displaystyle c_{\rm ad}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{{q}(1-\lambda{q}^{2})}{1+{q}}\,.

(93b)

\displaystyle=\frac{{\cal Z}}{\left(1+\lambda_{D}{\cal Z}^{2}\right)\left[\sqrt{1+\lambda_{D}{\cal Z}^{2}}+{\cal Z}\right]}\,.

(94b)

5.1
The normal modes

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}=\eta^{\mu\nu}-h^{\mu\nu}\,,

5.2
The Hamiltonian formulation

We start again from the scalar mode action (
99
) before fixing the gauge, and switch to Fourier space to get

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{-3|\dot{\psi}_{\vec{k}}|^{2}+\frac{1}{12}k^{4}|\dot{\nu}_{\vec{k}}|^{2}+k^{2}\left[\left(\frac{1}{6}k^{2}\dot{\nu}_{\vec{k}}-\dot{\psi}_{\vec{k}}\right)\zeta_{\vec{k}}^{*}+c.c.\right]

\displaystyle+k^{2}|\psi_{\vec{k}}-\frac{1}{6}k^{2}\nu_{\vec{k}}|^{2}-k^{2}\left[\left(\psi_{\vec{k}}-\frac{1}{6}k^{2}\nu_{\vec{k}}\right)\phi_{\vec{k}}^{*}+c.c.\right]+\left(\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2}\right)\left|\dot{\sigma}_{\vec{k}}-\phi_{\vec{k}}\right|^{2}\bigg\}\,,

(104)

where
c.c.
means complex conjugate of the term within the square bracket. Notice that (
104
) does not contain any time derivatives of the fields
\zeta_{\vec{k}}
and
\phi_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=-2\left(3\dot{\psi}_{\vec{k}}+k^{2}\zeta_{\vec{k}}\right)\,,

(105)

\displaystyle P^{(\nu)}_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}k^{4}\left(\dot{\nu}_{\vec{k}}+2\zeta_{\vec{k}}\right)\,,

(106)

\displaystyle P^{(\sigma)}_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle=2(\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2})(\dot{\sigma}_{\vec{k}}-\phi_{\vec{k}})\,,

(107)

and, after a Legendre transformation, the Hamiltonian is found to be

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{-\frac{1}{12}\bigl|P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}}\bigr|^{2}+\frac{3}{k^{4}}\bigl|P^{(\nu)}_{\vec{k}}\bigr|^{2}+\frac{1}{4(\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2})}\bigl|P^{(\sigma)}_{\vec{k}}\bigr|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad-k^{2}\bigl|\psi_{\vec{k}}-\frac{1}{6}k^{2}\nu_{\vec{k}}\bigr|^{2}+C_{\zeta}\zeta_{\vec{k}}^{*}+C_{\phi}\phi_{\vec{k}}^{*}+c.c.\bigg\}\,,

(108)

where
C_{\phi}
and
C_{\zeta}
are two constraints

\displaystyle C_{\phi}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{2}P^{(\sigma)}_{\vec{k}}+k^{2}\psi_{\vec{k}}-\frac{k^{4}}{6}\nu_{\vec{k}}\approx 0\,,

(109a)

\displaystyle C_{\zeta}

\displaystyle\equiv-P^{(\nu)}_{\vec{k}}-\frac{k^{2}}{6}P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}}\approx 0\,,

(109b)

imposed by the non-dynamical fields
\phi
and
\zeta

\approx
to denote weakly vanishing constraints (those that vanish only on-shell).

We require that the constraints are preserved by time evolution according to the Hamiltonian
H=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}{\cal H}
, with
{\cal H}
being the Hamiltonian density in Fourier space. We define the Poisson brackets on phase space as

\displaystyle\left\{f,g\right\}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\int\mathrm{d}^{3}k\left[\sum_{A}\left(\frac{\delta f}{\delta X^{A}}\frac{\delta g}{\delta P^{*}_{X^{A}}}-\frac{\delta g}{\delta X^{A}}\frac{\delta f}{\delta P^{*}_{X^{A}}}\right)\right]\,,

(110)

where
X^{A}\equiv\{\psi_{\vec{k}},\nu_{\vec{k}},\sigma_{\vec{k}}\}
and
P_{X^{A}}\equiv\{P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}},P^{(\nu)}_{\vec{k}},P^{(\sigma)}_{\vec{k}}\}
. The Poisson brackets define the time evolution of a variable
f
via
\dot{f}=\left\{f,{\cal H}\right\}
, and applied to the constraints (
109
) give

\displaystyle\dot{C}_{\phi}

\displaystyle=C_{\zeta}\,,

(111a)

\displaystyle\dot{C}_{\zeta}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(111b)

In other words, the constraints are preserved by time evolution on-shell. Therefore as one might expect, the stability of the primary constraints in the absence of gauge fixing does not create new constraints. Having ensured the stability of constraints in the Hamiltonian, we can now simplify the system by employing gauge fixing.

In the Hamiltonian formulation, primary first-class constraints generate gauge transformations. The infinitesimal change of a phase space quantity
f
under this gauge transformation generated by the constraint
C_{a}
(with
a=\zeta,\phi
) is given by:

\displaystyle\Delta f=\left\{f,C_{a}^{*}[\epsilon_{a}]\right\}\,,

(112)

where we have introduced the smearing
C^{*}_{a}[\epsilon_{a}]
of a constraint
C^{*}_{a}
with test function
\epsilon_{a}
:

\displaystyle C^{*}_{a}[\epsilon_{a}]\equiv\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\epsilon_{a,\vec{k}}\,C^{*}_{a,\vec{k}}\,.

(113)

Consider the following gauge transformations generated by the constraints
C_{\zeta}
and
C_{\phi}
,

\displaystyle\Delta\nu

\displaystyle=\left\{\nu,C_{\zeta}^{*}[\epsilon_{\zeta}]\right\}=-\epsilon_{\zeta}\,,

(114a)

\displaystyle\Delta P_{\nu}

\displaystyle=\left\{P_{\nu},C_{\phi}^{*}[\epsilon_{\phi}]\right\}=\frac{1}{6}k^{4}\epsilon_{\phi}\,.

(114b)

Thus, we may set
\nu
and
P_{\nu}
to zero by a gauge transformation by choosing
\epsilon_{\zeta}=\nu
and
\epsilon_{\phi}=-\frac{6}{k^{4}}P_{\nu}
.
We then check what constraints are placed on the Lagrange multipliers
\zeta,\phi
by this gauge fixing. We invoke two new gauge fixing constraints:

\displaystyle G_{\nu}

\displaystyle\equiv\nu\approx 0\,,

(115a)

\displaystyle G_{P_{\nu}}

\displaystyle\equiv P_{\nu}\approx 0\,,

(115b)

and find

\displaystyle\left\{G_{\nu},{\cal H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{6}{k^{4}}G_{P_{\nu}}-2\zeta_{\vec{k}}\,,

(116a)

\displaystyle\left\{G_{P_{\nu}},{\cal H}\right\}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}k^{4}\left(\phi_{\vec{k}}-\psi_{\vec{k}}\right)+\frac{1}{18}k^{6}G_{\nu}\,.

(116b)

Therefore the following gauge restrictions are placed on the Lagrange multipliers:
\zeta=0
and
\phi=\psi
. We recognize these conditions, respectively, as a restriction to the Newtonian gauge and the content of the Einstein field equation here dictating equality between metric potentials in this gauge. We may adopt these conditions alongside the constraints
G_{\nu}

G_{P_{\nu}}
in the Hamiltonian (

\displaystyle P^{(\sigma)}_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle\approx-2k^{2}\psi_{\vec{k}}\,,

(117a)

\displaystyle P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}}

\displaystyle\approx 0\,,

(117b)

so that the deconstrained Hamiltonian density is

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{({\rm dec})}=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{(1-\alpha)k^{2}-\mu^{2}}{\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2}}k^{2}\;|\psi_{\vec{k}}|^{2}\,.

(118)

In the Newtonian case

Discussions

6.1
The Hořava Khronometric theory

The model we have proposed is reminiscent of, and indeed was inspired
[
18
,
19
]
by the Hořava-Lifshitz (HL) gravity
[
43
,
44
,
45
]
. In eq. (
37
) we found that in order to account for the MONDian low acceleration regime when
{\cal Y}\to 0
, the function
{\cal J}({\cal Y})
takes essentially the form
{\cal J}=-{\cal Y}+{\cal O}({\cal Y}^{3/2})
, so that the gravitational part of the Lagrangian in adapted coordinates, see eq. (
130

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{g}=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\sqrt{q}N\Bigl[{\cal R}+K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}+2{\cal Y}+{\cal O}({\cal Y}^{3/2})\Bigr]\,,

(119)

where we recall that
{\cal Y}=A_{i}A^{i}/c^{4}
in adapted coordinates, and we skip other terms like
{\cal K}(1/N)
for this discussion. The theory (
119
) is only invariant under the subgroup of diffeomorphisms leaving invariant the preferred time foliation. In particular the term
2{\cal Y}
implies a breaking of the local Lorentz invariance (LLI).

On the other hand the HL gravity postulates a more general Lagrangian involving three arbitrary constants
\lambda
,
\beta
and
\alpha
labeling LLI-violating terms such that

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\text{HL}}_{g}=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\sqrt{q}N\Bigl[{\cal R}+(1-\beta)K_{ij}K^{ij}-(1+\lambda)K^{2}+2\alpha{\cal Y}+\cdots\Bigr]\,.

(120)

The term
2\alpha{\cal Y}
was added in
[
44
,
45
]
to provide stability of the scalar degree of freedom, within the so-called “non-projectable” version of the theory for which the lapse
N
depends not only on time as in the original “projectable” version
[
43
]
, but also on space coordinates. In particular the constant
\alpha
must satisfy
[
44
]

0<\alpha<1\,,

(121)

while a non-zero
\beta
leads to a speed of the tensor gravitational wave (GW) different from
c
.
In addition to the explicit terms in (
120
), the ellipsis contain terms which are of high order (fourth and sixth) in spatial derivatives but with no time derivatives, and which ensure the power-counting renormalizability of the theory at high energy; these terms are suppressed below some high energy scale. Introducing the Khronon (which is the Stückelberg field associated with the broken diffeomorphism invariance), the covariant version of (
120

\displaystyle{\cal L}^{\text{HL}}_{g}=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\sqrt{-g}\biggl[R-\frac{1}{3}(\beta+3\lambda)\Theta^{2}-\beta\sigma_{\mu\nu}\sigma^{\mu\nu}+2\alpha{\cal Y}+\cdots\biggr]\,,

(122)

where
\Theta=K=\nabla_{\mu}n^{\mu}

\sigma_{\mu\nu}=K_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}\Theta q_{\mu\nu}
is the shear of the spacetime congruence
n_{\mu}
, with
q_{\mu\nu}\equiv g_{\mu\nu}+n_{\mu}n_{\nu}
and the extrinsic curvature
K_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}+c^{-2}n_{\mu}A_{\nu}
(symmetric in
\mu\nu
in the case of a hypersurface-orthogonal congruence). The theory (
122
) is also recognized as a sub-class of Einstein-Aether theories
[
10
,
11
]
for which the Aether

is hypersurface-orthogonal.

Taking the model (
120
)–(
122
) at face, the effective Newton’s constant (as measured in a Cavendish type experiment) in the low energy limit,
i.e.
setting the dots to zero, reads

G_{\text{N}}=\frac{G}{1-\alpha}\,,

(123)

while the PPN parameters of the theory are the same as for GR, with the exception of the preferred-frame parameters
\alpha_{1}
and
\alpha_{2}
given by
[
45
,
66
]
(see
[
46

\displaystyle\alpha_{1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8(\alpha-\beta)}{1-\beta}\,,

(124a)

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\alpha-\beta)\bigl[2\alpha(1+\beta+2\lambda)-\beta(3+\beta+3\lambda)-\lambda\bigr]}{(1-\alpha)(1-\beta)(\beta+\lambda)}\,.

(124b)

Furthermore the cosmological equations (on a FLRW background) are

\displaystyle 3\Bigl(1+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{3\lambda}{2}\Bigr)H^{2}+\frac{3\kappa}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I}\overline{\rho}_{I}\,,

(125a)

\displaystyle-\Bigl(1+\frac{\beta}{2}+\frac{3\lambda}{2}\Bigr)\left(2\dot{H}+3H^{2}\right)-\frac{\kappa}{a^{2}}

\displaystyle=8\pi G\sum_{I}\overline{P}_{I}\,.

(125b)

The term
2\alpha{\cal Y}\propto A_{i}A^{i}
in the action corresponds to a linear perturbation of the FLRW background and has no effect on (
125

The present model (
119
) looks to be a particular case of the HL gravity. However there is the crucial difference that in the HL model the violation of local Lorentz invariance is motivated by the completion of GR at high energy, while in (
119

\alpha=1
which is required in eq. (
119

123
) and the PPN parameters (
124

2{\cal Y}
is required to cancel the Newtonian gravity in the low acceleration regime and to replace it by the MOND gravity, thanks to the term
\sim{\cal Y}^{3/2}
in (

G_{\text{N}}
happens in the high acceleration regime, where
\alpha=0
, following the function
{\cal J}({\cal Y})
given by eq. (
40

\beta=\lambda=0
we recover GR and in particular the same PPN limit as GR.

6.2
Degrees of freedom and further discussion about the dispersion relation

At all instances the theory has three dynamical degrees of freedom when expanded on a Minkowski background to quadratic order. However, only two are propagating which are the usual massless tensor modes as in GR. The third degree of freedom is non-propagating (it has
\omega=0
and so there is no associated wave), but still dynamical as its solution is of the form
A_{\vec{k}}+B_{\vec{k}}t
. Such modes have been previously identified to exist in generic Lorentz-violating massive gravity theories
[
65
]
, and also exist in the case of AeST
[
39
,
67
]
, and for the ghost condensate theory
[
49
]
in the limit when the higher-derivative interactions are ignored. In our case, there are no higher derivative interaction terms in the action, which are also quadratic in the fields (and so contribute to the usual dispersion relation corresponding to a linear wave equation). However, the higher order terms (for instance, the MOND term), will effectively lead to a non-linear propagation equation for the third degree of freedom, that is, the non-propagating
\omega=0
mode will become propagating. We expect the full (non-perturbative) Hamiltonian analysis to reveal the same three dynamical degrees of freedom, similarly to
[
68
]
but less complicated and can be used to study the perturbative Hamiltonian dynamics on more general backgrounds.

Let us discuss further the Hamiltonian and the linear instability found above. This is due to the “condensate” term
\mu^{2}({\cal Q}-1)^{2}
which equals
\mu^{2}\phi^{2}
in the unitary gauge.
In the Newtonian case where
\alpha=0
, we have that the Hamiltonian is positive for
k>\mu
but in the MOND case, where
\alpha=1
it would seem that the Hamiltonian is always negative even as
k\rightarrow\infty
. However, eq. (
118
) is of the same form as the AeST Hamiltonian for the
\omega=0
mode analysed in
[
67
]
. There it was shown that including the MOND (non-linear) term, leads once more to a Hamiltonian bounded from below for
k\geqslant\mu
, and still unbounded from below for
k<\mu
. Thus, the same type of Jeans instability persists also at the MOND limit. Taking
\mu^{-1}\sim 22.3\,{\rm Mpc}
as in Fig.
1
the deconstrained Hamiltonian is bounded from below for wavenumbers larger than
\sim 3\times 10^{-31}\,\text{eV}
.

There can be further terms which can influence the conclusions regarding the Hamiltonian. A specific case is the HL extension discussed above, with parameters
\beta
and
\lambda
(we set
\beta=0
to have GW travelling at the speed of
light as required by observations).
Including the perturbations coming from (
122
), we find that the third mode in this case is propagating and has dispersion relation

\displaystyle\omega^{2}=\frac{\lambda\left[(1-\alpha)k^{2}-\mu^{2}\right]}{(2+3\lambda)\left(\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2}\right)}k^{2}\,,

(126)

which in the limit
\mu\rightarrow 0
is consistent with
[
45
]
. Notice that the instability structure is unchanged from the
\beta=\lambda=0
case of section
5.2
, that is, we have that
\omega^{2}<0
for
\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,k<\mu
.
13
13

13

This seems to depend on the sign of
\lambda
and
2+3\lambda
. However,
2+3\lambda>0
to have a well-defined Friedman equation (
125

\lambda>0
to have
\omega^{2}>0
in the
\sqrt{1-\alpha}\,k>\mu

However, in this case, the instability turns from linear to exponential. The corresponding deconstrained Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle{\cal H}^{({\rm dec})}

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\bigg\{\frac{\lambda}{4(2+3\lambda)}|P^{(\psi)}_{\vec{k}}|^{2}+\frac{(1-\alpha)k^{2}-\mu^{2}}{\alpha k^{2}+\mu^{2}}k^{2}|\psi_{\vec{k}}|^{2}\bigg\}\,,

(127)

which is well-defined in the limit
\lambda\rightarrow 0
and corresponds to (

Assuming that both
\alpha
and
\lambda
are small, the maximum instability rate
\Gamma_{\text{max}}=-\mathrm{i}\omega_{\text{max}}
is
\Gamma_{\text{max}}=\mu\sqrt{\lambda/8}
which we require to be smaller than
H_{0}
. Of particular interest is the subcase
\lambda=2\alpha
for which
\omega^{2}=k^{2}
in the
k\gg\mu

[

), that is, the Khronon perturbations are also luminal at high momenta.
In this case, the PPN parameters (

\alpha_{1}=-8\alpha
while
\alpha_{2}=\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{1-\alpha}\ll\alpha_{1}
which is negligible. The
observational bounds on

, and hence,
\mu\lesssim 1000\,H_{0}
, corresponding to
\mu^{-1}\gtrsim 10\,{\rm Mpc}
.
This bound is consistent with having a MOND limit and can be weaker for smaller

.

7
Conclusion

We have proposed (extending previous works
[
18
,
19
]
) a modified gravity theory to account for the phenomenology of dark matter at the scale of galaxies as summarized by the MOND formula
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
. Although this theory cannot be considered as fundamental (there is an arbitrary function in the action which is not explained by fundamental physics), it owns a number of attractive features:

1.

It is based on only two dynamical fields: the metric and the scalar Khronon field (plus ordinary matter fields);

2.

It recovers MOND at the scale of galaxies (in the weak acceleration regime), with however the restriction to systems being stationary;

3.

In the strong acceleration regime the theory agrees with GR and in particular has the same PPN limit as GR for tests in the Solar System and in binary pulsars;

4.

The theory has no propagating GW with helicity
0
or helicity
1
, so GW are the same as in GR;

5.

Last but not least, it can be arbitrarily close to the
\Lambda
-CDM cosmological model at the level of linear cosmological perturbations,
where it retrieves the full observed spectrum of CMB anisotropies (for a wide range of parameters
\lambda_{D}
and
\mu
).

We note the importance of checking the validity of the MOND limit in galaxies when (
37
) is taken as a low energy effective field theory expansion which comes with a new scale. A naive estimate is that the MOND term in (
37
), that is,
M_{p}^{2}|\vec{\nabla}\phi|^{3}/a_{0}\rightarrow|\vec{\nabla}\phi_{c}|^{3}/\Lambda_{0}^{2}
where
\phi_{c}\sim\phi/M_{p}
is the canonically normalized version of
\phi
, leads to a strong-coupling scale of
\Lambda_{0}\sim\sqrt{a_{0}M_{p}}
. Given that
a_{0}\sim H_{0}/6
then
\Lambda_{0}\sim\text{meV}
corresponding to
\Lambda_{0}^{-1}\sim 10^{-4}\,\text{m}
, which is clearly much smaller than the scale of galaxies. However, given that there are more dynamical degrees of freedom in the theory, this naive estimate is bound to change. We leave a more thorough investigation of this estimate to a future study.

We also hightlight that to next order in cosmological perturbations, the MOND terms will become relevant and the correspondence with
\Lambda
-CDM will break down. In that case, it is necessary to use
N
-body simulations to determine the non-linear cosmological large scale structure, and it would be interesting to check where (and how) quasistatic MONDian sources might emerge in such a setup. This is left for a future investigation.

Formulation in adapted coordinates

It is always possible to adopt a coordinate system for which the coordinate time

. Hence in this coordinate system

where

. Introducing the shift
N_{i}=g_{0i}
and the spatial metric
q_{ij}=g_{ij}
, we have the usual 3+1 form for the metric,

\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-c^{2}N^{2}\mathrm{d}t^{2}+q_{ij}\bigl(\mathrm{d}x^{i}+cN^{i}\mathrm{d}t\bigr)\bigl(\mathrm{d}x^{j}+cN^{j}\mathrm{d}t\bigr)\,.

(128)

The acceleration takes the 3-dimensional expression (with
A^{0}=0
)

\displaystyle A^{i}

\displaystyle=c^{2}D^{i}\ln N\,,

(129a)

\displaystyle{\cal Y}

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{i}A^{i}}{c^{4}}=D_{i}\ln ND^{i}\ln N\,,

(129b)

where
D^{i}=q^{ij}D_{j}
(acting here on a scalar) denotes the covariant derivative compatible with the spatial metric,
i.e.

D_{i}q_{jk}=0
. The covariant action (
6
) becomes, after discarding a total divergence (with
\sqrt{-g}=N\sqrt{q}
)

\displaystyle\!\!\!S=\frac{c^{4}}{16\pi G}\!\int\!\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}^{3}\mathbf{x}\,\sqrt{q}N\Bigl[{\cal R}+K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}-2{\cal J}({\cal Y})+2{\cal K}({\cal Q})\Bigr]+S_{m}\bigl[\Psi,N,N_{i},q_{ij}\bigr]\,,

(130)

where
{\cal R}
is the 3-dimensional scalar curvature of the 3-metric
q_{ij}
and
K_{ij}
is the extrinsic curvature

K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2N}\left(\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}q_{ij}-D_{i}N_{j}-D_{j}N_{i}\right)\,.

(131)

In such adapted coordinates the Khronon field has disappeared and the independent dynamical degrees of freedom are geometrical:
N
,
N_{i}
and
q_{ij}
(and the matter fields
\Psi
). See section
6.1
for a generalization of the action inspired by Hořava gravity.

For the variation of the minimally and universally coupled matter action we adopt a notation similar to that in
[
18
]

\displaystyle\rho\equiv-\frac{1}{c^{2}\sqrt{q}}\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta N}\,,\qquad J^{i}\equiv\frac{1}{c\sqrt{q}}\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta N_{i}}\,,\qquad T^{ij}\equiv\frac{2}{N\!\sqrt{q}}\frac{\delta S_{m}}{\delta q_{ij}}\,,

(132)

where
\rho
,
J^{i}
and
T^{ij}
reduce to the usual notions of mass density, current density and spatial stresses in the case of a perfect fluid. The variation with respect to the lapse
N
gives

{\cal R}+K^{2}-K_{ij}K^{ij}-2{\cal J}+4{\cal Y}\,{\cal J}_{\cal Y}+2{\cal K}-2{\cal Q}\,{\cal K}_{\cal Q}+\frac{4}{c^{2}}D_{i}\bigl({\cal J}_{\cal Y}A^{i}\bigr)=\frac{16\pi G}{c^{2}}\,\rho\,.

(133)

Next, the variation with respect to
N_{i}
yields the momentum constraint equation as in GR,

D_{j}\left(K^{ij}-q^{ij}K\right)=-\frac{8\pi G}{c^{3}}J^{i}\,.

(134)

Varying with respect to the spatial metric
q_{ij}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{cN}D_{t}\left(K^{ij}-q^{ij}K\right)+\frac{2}{N}D_{k}\left[N^{(i}(K^{j)k}-q^{j)k}K)\right]+2K^{ik}K^{j}_{\phantom{j}k}-KK^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}q^{ij}\left(K^{kl}K_{kl}+K^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+{\cal G}^{ij}-\frac{1}{N}\left(D^{i}D^{j}N-q^{ij}D_{k}D^{k}N\right)-\frac{2}{c^{4}}{\cal J}_{\cal Y}\,A^{i}A^{j}+\left({\cal J}-{\cal K}\right)\,q^{ij}=\frac{8\pi G}{c^{4}}T^{ij}\,,

(135)

where
{\cal G}^{ij}={\cal R}^{ij}-\frac{1}{2}q^{ij}{\cal R}
is the 3-dimensional Einstein tensor, and we use the convenient notation
D_{t}\equiv\partial_{t}-cN^{k}D_{k}
. The trace of eq. (
135
), combined with the constraint equation (
134
), gives

-\frac{4}{cN}D_{t}K-3K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}-{\cal R}+\frac{4}{N}D_{i}D^{i}N-4{\cal Y}\,{\cal J}_{\cal Y}+6\left({\cal J}-{\cal K}\right)=\frac{16\pi G}{c^{4}}\Bigl(T^{i}_{i}+\frac{2c}{N}N_{i}J^{i}\Bigr)\,.

(136)

Finally, by eliminating
{\cal R}
between (
133
) and (
136
) we obtain the following Poisson-like equation,

\frac{1}{c^{2}}D_{i}\left[\left(1+{\cal J}_{\cal Y}\right)A^{i}\right]+{\cal J}+{\cal Y}-{\cal K}-\frac{1}{2}{\cal Q}\,{\cal K}_{\cal Q}-\frac{D_{t}K}{cN}-K^{ij}K_{ij}=\frac{4\pi G}{c^{2}}\biggl(\rho+\frac{2}{cN}N_{i}J^{i}+\frac{1}{c^{2}}T^{i}_{i}\biggr)\,,

(137)

which is the generalization of eq. (3.13) in the BM theory.

Compact Hermitian surfaces with pointwise constant Gauduchon holomorphic sectional curvature
Haojie Chen, Xiaolan Nie

Abstract
. Motivated by a recent work of Chen-Zheng [ 8 ] on Strominger space forms, we prove that a compact Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to a Gauduchon connection ∇ t \nabla^{t} is either Kähler, or an isosceles Hopf surface with an admissible metric and t = − 1 t=-1 or t = 3 t=3 . In particular, a compact Hermitian surface with pointwise constant Lichnerowicz holomorphic sectional curvature is Kähler. We further generalize the result to the case for the two-parameter canonical connections introduced by Zhao-Zheng [ 31 ] , which extends a previous result by Apostolov-Davidov-Muškarov [ 2 ] .

1.
Introduction

This note mainly concerns Hermitian surfaces with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to a Gauduchon connection
\nabla^{t}
. Given a Hermitian manifold
(M,J,g)
, there is a one-parameter family of canonical Hermitian connections
\nabla^{t}
introduced by Gauduchon
[
11
]
in 1997, which is the line joining the Chern connection
\nabla^{c}
, the Strominger connection
\nabla^{s}
(also known as Bismut connection) and the Lichnerowicz connection
\nabla^{l}
. The last one is the restriction of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{LC}
on the holomorphic tangent bundle
T^{1,0}M
(also known as the associated connection
[
10
]
or the induced Levi-Civita connection
[
18
]
). For any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the Gauduchon connection is defined by

\nabla^{t}=\frac{1+t}{2}\nabla^{c}+\frac{1-t}{2}\nabla^{s}=t\nabla^{c}+(1-t)\nabla^{l}.

In particular,
\nabla^{1}=\nabla^{c}
,
\nabla^{-1}=\nabla^{s}
and
\nabla^{0}=\nabla^{l}
(see also
[
31
]
). If
g
is Kähler, then all
\nabla^{t}
coincide with the Levi-Civita connection. Otherwise, they are mutually different. While each Gauduchon connection exhibits its own geometry, the properties of the whole connection family may reflect the intrinsic features of the Hermitian manifold.

There has been many recent studies on the curvatures of special connections on Hermitian manifolds, e.g. for the Chern connection (
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
22
]
), the Levi-Civita connection (
[
2
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
21
]
), the Strominger connection (
[
8
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
28
]
,
[
29
]
) and the Lichnerowicz connection (
[
10
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
19
]
). There are also some recent work on the curvatures of general Gauduchon connections (see
[
1
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
31
]
etc). Among various curvature notions, the holomorphic sectional curvature is a natural substitute of sectional curvature on Hermitian manifolds (see e.g.
[
15
]
,
[
32
]
and the references therein). A fundamental question is to understand Hermitian manifolds with constant or pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature. In 1985, Balas-Gauduchon
[
4
]
prove that a compact Hermitian surface with constant nonpositive holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
\nabla^{c}
must be Kähler. This result was beautifully extended by Apostolov-Davidov-Muškarov
[
2
]
in 1996. They prove the following theorem, as an application of their classification of compact self-dual Hermitian surfaces.

Theorem 1.1

(Apostolov-Davidov-Muškarov)
.

Any compact Hermitian surface with point-wise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to the Levi-Civita connection or the Chern connection must be Kähler.

Recently, an important new progress is made by S. Chen and F. Zheng in
[
8
]
, where they study the (weak) Srominger space forms, i.e., compact Hermitian manifolds with (pointwise) constant Strominger holomorphic sectional curvature. In particular, they give a complete classification in dimension two and prove that

Theorem 1.2

(Chen-Zheng)
.

Any compact Hermitian surface
(M,g)
with pointwise constant Strominger holomorphic sectional curvature must be either Kähler or an isosceles Hopf surface with an admissible metric.

Motivated by the above results, we study Hermitian surfaces with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to an arbitrary Gauduchon connection. Using similar techniques as in
[
8
]
, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to a Gauduchon connection
\nabla^{t}
. Then either (i)
g
is Käher, or (ii)
(M,g)
is an isosceles Hopf surface with an admissible metric and in this case
t=-1
or
t=3
.

Note that for a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
, by the Schur’s lemma, if the holomorphic sectional curvature is pointwise constant, it must be constant. A complete Kähler manifold with constant holomorphic sectional curvature is called a complex space form.
It is known that a simply connected complex space form is holomorphically isometric to
\mathbb{CP}^{n},\mathbb{C}^{n}
or
\mathbb{CH}^{n}
(
[
6
]
,
[
12
]
). Therefore, in case (i) of Theorem
1.3
,
(M,g)
is isomorphic to one of the following:
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
, complex torus, hyperelliptic surface and a compact quotient of
\mathbb{CH}^{2}
. Admissible metrics on an isosceles Hopf surface are a special class of Hermitian metrics which are conformal to the standard Hopf metric (see section 5 for more details).

Remark
1.4
.

The case for the Chern connection (
t=1
) is proved by Apostolov-Davidov-Muškarov
[
2
]
and the case for the Strominger connection (
t=-1
) is proved by Chen-Zheng
[
8
]
.

As
\nabla^{0}=\nabla^{l}
, we have a direct corollary.

Corollary 1.5
.

Any compact Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to the Lichnerowicz connection is Kähler.

From the proof of Theorem
1.3
, it follows that an admissible metric on isosceles Hopf surfaces has the same pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvatures with respect to
\nabla^{s}
and
\nabla^{3}
. However, the curvature tensors of
\nabla^{s}
and
\nabla^{3}
are different. In particular, the standard Hopf metric has zero curvature tensor for
\nabla^{s}
and nonzero curvature tensor for
\nabla^{3}
. This gives an example of compact Hermitian surfaces with zero Gauduchon holomorphic sectional curvature and nonvanishing curvature tensor (Corollary
5.4
).

Next, we put the Levi-Civita connection into consideration. As is known, if
g
is not Kähler, the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{LC}
is not a Hermitian connection and does not coincide with any
\nabla^{t}
. As introduced by Zhao-Zheng in
[
31
]
, define the two-parameter canonical connections

D^{t}_{s}=(1-s)\nabla^{t}+s\nabla^{LC}

for any
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. It is the connection plane spanned by the Levi-Civita connection and the one-parameter Gauduchon connections
\nabla^{t}
. Note that
D^{t}_{1}\equiv\nabla^{LC}
and
D^{t}_{0}=\nabla^{t}
. For a non-Kähler metric
g
and
s\neq 0
,
D^{t}_{s}
is not a Hermitian connection either. We obtain the following result which is a generalization of Theorem
1.3
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to a canonical
(t,s)
connection
D^{t}_{s}
. Then either (i)
g
is Käher, or (ii)
(M,g)
is an isosceles Hopf surface with an admissible metric and in this case
(1-t+ts)^{2}+s^{2}=4
.

Remark
1.7
.

When
s=1
,
D_{1}^{t}
is the Levi-Civita connection. In this case, the result is proved in
[
2
]
.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.6
, we prove the following characterizations.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Hermitian surface. If one of the following is satisfied,

(i)

g
has positive or negative pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
D^{t}_{s}
for some
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

(ii)

g
has zero holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
D^{t}_{s}
for
(t,s)
satisfying
(1-t+ts)^{2}+s^{2}\neq 4
,

then
(M,g)
is Kähler and a complex space form.

On the other side, if
g
is non-Kähler and has zero holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to some
D^{t}_{s}
, then it must be a scalar multiple of the standard metric on an isosceles Hopf surface and
(1-t+ts)^{2}+s^{2}=4
.

A key step to prove Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.6
is to show that a Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
\nabla^{t}
or
D^{t}_{s}
must be a self-dual Riemannian 4-manifold. We show that it also holds for general almost Hermitian surfaces (Proposition
3.3
). A natural question is to classify compact almost Hermitian surfaces with constant or pointwise constant Gauduchon holomorphic sectional curvature.

In section 5, we also study Hermitian metrics with pointwise constant Gauduchon holomorphic sectional curvatures on higher dimensional isosceles Hopf manifolds and prove the following (Proposition 5.3).

Proposition 1.9
.

A Hermitian metric
\omega
on an isosceles Hopf manifold which is conformal to the standard Hopf metric has pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
D^{t}_{s}
if and only if
(1-t+ts)^{2}+s^{2}=4
and
\omega
is an admissible metric.

The structure of the paper is as follows. In section 2, we briefly introduce the canonical connections and holomorphic sectional curvature on an almost Hermitian manifold. In section 3, we show that an almost Hermitian surface with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature must be self-dual. In section 4, we compute the transformation of curvature components of the canonical connections
\nabla^{t}
and
D^{t}_{s}
under conformal change of Hermitian metrics on a complex manifold. In section 5, we study Hermitian metrics with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature on an isosceles Hopf manifold. In section 6, we prove Theorem
1.6
and Corollary
1.8
. Throughout the paper, we
assume
M
to be connected.

3.
self-duality

In this section, we relate almost Hermitian surfaces with pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature to Riemannian self-dual 4-manifolds. We refer to
[

]

[
5

and the reference therein for more details on self-dual 4-manifolds.

Given a Riemannian 4-manifold
(M,g)
,
g
induces a metric on the bundle
\Lambda^{2}TM
. The curvature of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{LC}
induces a self-adjoint endomorphism
\mathcal{R}
of
\Lambda^{2}TM
by
g(\mathcal{R}(X\wedge Y),Z\wedge W)=-R(X,Y,Z,W)
, for
X,Y,Z,W\in TM
. The Hodge
*

\Lambda^{2}TM
preserves

and satisfies
*^{2}=id
. The
+1,-1
eigenbundles of
*
are denoted by
\Lambda^{2}_{+},\Lambda^{2}_{-}
. Define the Weyl tensor operator to be

\mathcal{W}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{R}+*\mathcal{R}*)-\dfrac{s_{g}}{12}id,

where
s_{g}
is the Riemannian scaler curvature of
g
. Define

\mathcal{W}_{+}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{W}+*\mathcal{W}),\ \ \ \mathcal{W}_{-}=\dfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{W}-*\mathcal{W}).

Then
\mathcal{W}_{+}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{+}}=\mathcal{W}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{+}},\mathcal{W}_{+}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{-}}=0
and
\mathcal{W}_{-}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{-}}=\mathcal{W}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{-}},\mathcal{W}_{-}|_{\Lambda^{2}_{+}}=0
.
And for
\alpha_{+},\beta_{+}\in\Lambda^{2}_{+}
,
\alpha_{-},\beta_{-}\in\Lambda^{2}_{-}
,

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{W}_{+}(\alpha_{+}),\beta_{+})

\displaystyle=g(\mathcal{R}(\alpha_{+}),\beta_{+})-\dfrac{s_{g}}{12}g(\alpha_{+},\beta_{+}),

(3.1)

\displaystyle g(\mathcal{W}_{-}(\alpha_{-}),\beta_{-})

\displaystyle=g(\mathcal{R}(\alpha_{-}),\beta_{-})-\dfrac{s_{g}}{12}g(\alpha_{-},\beta_{-}).

The operators
\mathcal{R},\mathcal{W},\mathcal{W}_{+},\mathcal{W}_{-}
and the inner product

can be all extended complex linearly to
\Lambda^{2}TM\otimes\mathbb{C}
.

Definition 3.1
.

(M,g)
is called self-dual (anti-self-dual) if
\mathcal{W}_{-}=0
(resp.

[

]
for the Hermitian case (see also Lemma 3 in

8
]
) and in the proof of Theorem 1.3 in

for the almost Hermitian case.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
(M,J,g)
be an almost Hermitian surface. Then
(M,g)
is self dual if and only if the following equations hold:

\displaystyle R_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}=0,\ \ \ R_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}-R_{1\bar{2}1\bar{1}}=0,

\displaystyle 2R_{1\bar{2}2\bar{1}}+2R_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}-R_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-R_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0.

Then we prove

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be an almost Hermitian surface and
(t,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Assume that the holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to
D^{t}_{s}
is pointwise constant. Then
M
is self-dual.

Proof.

We follow the same steps as in
[
8
]
. As
D^{t}_{s}
has pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature
c
, by Lemma
2.5
, for any unitary frame,

\widehat{R}^{D}_{i\bar{j}k\bar{l}}=\dfrac{c}{2}(\delta_{ij}\delta_{kl}+\delta_{il}\delta_{kj}).

In particular,

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}=\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}=\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}=\widehat{R}^{D}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=c,\ \ \ \widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}=\dfrac{c}{2}.

Next, from (
2.14
) we have the following:

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{k\bar{l}i\bar{j}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{k\bar{l}i\bar{j}}-\dfrac{1}{4}(p^{2}-2p-1+s^{2})(T^{j}_{rk}\overline{T^{i}_{rl}}+T^{j}_{ri}\overline{T^{k}_{rl}}+T^{l}_{rk}\overline{T^{i}_{rj}}+T^{l}_{ri}\overline{T^{k}_{rj}})

\displaystyle+(1-s^{2})(T^{j}_{\bar{r}\bar{l}}+T^{l}_{\bar{r}\bar{j}})(\overline{T^{i}_{\bar{k}\bar{r}}}+\overline{T^{k}_{\bar{i}\bar{r}}}).

In particular, letting
b=p^{2}-2p-1+s^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}-\frac{1}{2}bT^{2}_{12}\overline{T^{1}_{12}}+2(1-s^{2})T^{2}_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}\overline{T^{1}_{\bar{2}\bar{1}}},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}-\frac{1}{2}bT^{2}_{21}\overline{T^{1}_{21}}+2(1-s^{2})T^{2}_{\bar{2}\bar{1}}\overline{T^{1}_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-b|T^{1}_{21}|^{2}-4(1-s^{2})|T^{1}_{\bar{2}\bar{1}}|^{2},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}-b|T^{2}_{12}|^{2}-4(1-s^{2})|T^{2}_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}|^{2},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}^{D}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}-\frac{1}{4}b(|T^{2}_{21}|^{2}+|T^{1}_{12}|^{2})-(1-s^{2})(|T^{2}_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}|^{2}+|T^{1}_{\bar{1}\bar{2}}|^{2}).

So from (
3.2
), we get

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}=0,\ \ \ \widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}-\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0

(3.3)

\displaystyle 4\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}-\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-\widehat{R}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0

For curvatures of the Levi-Civita connection, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}

\displaystyle={R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}},\ \ \ \widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}=\dfrac{1}{2}({R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{2}}+{R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{1}})={R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{1}},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}({R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}+{R}_{2\bar{2}1\bar{2}})={R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}},

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{4}({R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}+{R}_{2\bar{1}1\bar{2}}+{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{1}}+{R}_{2\bar{2}1\bar{1}})=\dfrac{1}{2}({R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}+{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{1}}),

\displaystyle\widehat{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}

\displaystyle={R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}},\ \ \ \widehat{R}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}={R}_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}

Therefore, putting them into (
3.3
), we get

\displaystyle{R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{2}}=0,\ \ \ \ {R}_{1\bar{2}1\bar{1}}-{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0

\displaystyle 2({R}_{1\bar{1}2\bar{2}}+{R}_{1\bar{2}2\bar{1}})-{R}_{1\bar{1}1\bar{1}}-R_{2\bar{2}2\bar{2}}=0

These are exactly the equations in Lemma
3.2
. So we finish the proof.
∎

5.
Isosceles Hopf manifolds

In this section, we apply the formulae in section 4 to study Hermitian metrics which are conformal to the standard Hopf metric and have pointwise constant holomorphic sectional curvature with respect to

\sigma:(z_{1},\cdots,z_{n})\longrightarrow(a_{1}z_{1},\cdots,a_{n}z_{n})

0<a=|a_{1}|=\cdots=|a_{n}|<1

\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
\omega_{h}=\dfrac{\sqrt{-1}}{|z|^{2}}(dz_{1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{1}+\cdots+dz_{n}\wedge d\bar{z}_{n})

is viewed as a column vector). As

is invariant under

which is called the

In
[
8
]

\omega_{h}
and have pointwise constant Strominger holomorphic sectional curvature and prove the following result (Proposition 1 there).

(
[
8
]
)

M_{\sigma}
be an isosceles Hopf manifold. A Hermitian metric
\widetilde{\omega}
on

conformal to
\omega_{h}

\widetilde{\omega}=\dfrac{c_{0}}{|z|^{2}+^{t}zAz+\overline{{}^{t}zAz}}\omega_{0}

and symmetric
(n\times n)

and
D_{\sigma}AD_{\sigma}=a^{2}A
, where
D_{\sigma}=diag\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{n}\}
.
\widetilde{\omega}
is then called an

In the following, we will generalize Proposition
5.1

\nabla^{t}

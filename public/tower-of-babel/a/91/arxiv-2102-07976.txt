A General Descent Aggregation Framework for Gradient-based Bi-level Optimization
Risheng Liu, Pan Mu, Xiaoming Yuan, Shangzhi Zeng and Jin Zhang

Abstract
In recent years, a variety of gradient-based methods have been developed to solve Bi-Level Optimization (BLO) problems in machine learning and computer vision areas. However, the theoretical correctness and practical effectiveness of these existing approaches always rely on some restrictive conditions (e.g., Lower-Level Singleton, LLS), which could hardly be satisfied in real-world applications. Moreover, previous literature only proves theoretical results based on their specific iteration strategies, thus lack a general recipe to uniformly analyze the convergence behaviors of different gradient-based BLOs. In this work, we formulate BLOs from an optimistic bi-level viewpoint and establish a new gradient-based algorithmic framework, named Bi-level Descent Aggregation (BDA), to partially address the above issues. Specifically, BDA provides a modularized structure to hierarchically aggregate both the upper- and lower-level subproblems to generate our bi-level iterative dynamics. Theoretically, we establish a general convergence analysis template and derive a new proof recipe to investigate the essential theoretical properties of gradient-based BLO methods. Furthermore, this work systematically explores the convergence behavior of BDA in different optimization scenarios, i.e., considering various solution qualities (i.e., global/local/stationary solution) returned from solving approximation subproblems. Extensive experiments justify our theoretical results and demonstrate the superiority of the proposed algorithm for hyper-parameter optimization and meta-learning tasks. Source code is available at https://github.com/vis-opt-group/BDA .

Note that, for existing methods within the explicit BR category, the explicit BR approximation by optimization iteration dynamics raises an issue regarding approximation quality. In fact, without the LLS assumption, the dynamics procedures of existing methods, in general may not be good approximations. This is because in this case, the optimization dynamics converge to some minimizers of the LL objective, but not necessarily to the one that also minimizes the UL objective. This unpleasant situation was noticed by both the machine learning and the optimization communities; see, e.g.,

Further, these existing methods only concern the convergence towards stationary or global/local minimum, thus lack comprehensive convergence analyses.

In response to these limitations, this work proposes a novel framework termed Bi-level Descent Aggregation (BDA). Specifically, we propose a gradient type method for solving BLOs by aggregating UL and LL objectives. Theoretically, this work provides a general proof recipe as a basic template for the convergence analysis. In particular, in the absence of LLS, the BDA convergence was strictly guaranteed as long as the embedded inner simple bi-level dynamics meet the so-called

II
Gradient-based BLOs: A Brief Review

As for the BLO model in Eq. (
2
), it is worthwhile noting that the LL solution set
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})
may have multiple solutions for every (or some) fixed
\mathbf{x}
. However, it is challenging to solve BLO, especially when the LL solution set is not a singleton. Thus, in learning and vision application scenarios, the most straightforward idea of designing GBMs is to enforce the singleton assumption on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})
. With such LLS condition, the BLO model in Eq. (
2
) actually can be simplified as follows:

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x}):=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})),\ s.t.,\ \mathbf{y}^{\ast}=\ \underset{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{Y}}{\mathrm{argmin}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}).

(3)

Thus the optimization task reduces to solve a single-level problem (i.e.,
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x}))
) with an optimal LL variable
\mathbf{y}^{\ast}
. In this way, the gradient of
\varphi
(w.r.t.,
\mathbf{x}
) can be written as

\underbrace{\nabla\varphi(\mathbf{x})}_{\text{grad. of}\ \mathbf{x}}=\underbrace{\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x}))}_{\text{direct grad of}\ \mathbf{x}}+\underbrace{\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right)^{\top}}_{\text{BR Jacobian}}\underbrace{\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast})}_{\text{direct grad. of}\ \mathbf{y}},

(4)

where “grad.” denotes the abbreviation of gradient and
(\cdot)^{\top}
means the transpose operation. In existing GBMs, they actually first numerically approximate
\mathbf{y}^{*}
by
\mathbf{y}_{K}
and thus define
\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))
. Then the UL variable
\mathbf{x}
can be updated based on the following practical formulation

\nabla\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\!=\!\nabla_{\mathbf{x}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))\!+\!\left(\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right)^{\top}\!\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})).

In particular, a variety of techniques

have been developed to explicitly formulate

as the recurrent parameters of a gradient-based dynamic system, i.e.,

Theoretically, these existing convergence results all require that the LL dynamics

is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})

. We should also point out that the LLS condition actually plays the key role for most of existing GBMs (e.g.,
[

35
]
). These approaches often require restrictive assumptions (e.g., strong convexity) to meet this assumption. Besides, some researches
[

27

36

prove that UL value-function converges to a first-order stationary point, i.e.,
\lim_{T\to\infty}\nabla\varphi(\mathbf{x}_{T})\to 0

IV-A

Two Essential Convergence Properties

Now we are ready to establish the new convergence analysis template, which describes the main steps to achieve the converge guarantees for our bi-level updating scheme (stated in Eqs. (
6
)-(
8

\mathcal{T}_{k}

(1)

UL objective convergence property:
For each
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}

\lim\limits_{K\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\varphi(\mathbf{x}).

(2)

LL objective convergence property:

\{\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})\}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{X}
, and for any
\epsilon>0

such that whenever

\sup_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\{f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))-f^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})\right\}\leq\epsilon.

Indeed, the general recipe provides us a criterion to design different stable algorithms. Under these two essential properties, we thoroughly analyze the bi-level optimization problem and provide comprehensive theoretical results. Based on the developed BDA algorithm scheme, we first provide convergence results towards global and local minimum in Section

\varepsilon_{m}\rightarrow 0
, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon+\varepsilon_{m}=\varphi(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon,\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}.

By taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have
\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{x}),\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
which implies
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
.

We next show that
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
as
K\rightarrow\infty
. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
,
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\leq\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})
, by taking
K\rightarrow\infty
and
with the
UL objective convergence property
, we have

\limsup_{K\rightarrow\infty}\left\{\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\right\}\leq\varphi(\mathbf{x}),\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X},

and thus

\limsup_{K\rightarrow\infty}\left\{\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\right\}\leq\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x}).

So, if
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
does not hold, then there exist
\delta>0
and subsequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{l}\}
of
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}\}
such that

\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{l}(\mathbf{x})<\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})-\delta,\ \forall l.

(11)

Since
\mathcal{X}
is compact, we can assume without loss of generality that
\mathbf{x}_{l}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
by considering a subsequence. Then, as shown in above, we have
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
. And, by the same arguments for deriving Eq. (
10
), we can show that
\forall\epsilon>0
, there exists
k(\epsilon)>0
such that
\forall l>k(\epsilon)
, it holds

\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\varphi_{l}(\mathbf{x}_{l})+\epsilon.

By letting
l\rightarrow\infty
,
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
and the definition of
\mathbf{x}_{l}
, we have

\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})=\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\liminf_{l\rightarrow\infty}\left\{\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{l}(\mathbf{x})\right\},

which implies a contradiction to Eq. (
11
). Thus we have
\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})\rightarrow\inf_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
as
K\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

IV-C

Towards Local Minimum

Theorem 2
.

(Convergence towards Local Minimum)
Suppose both the LL and UL objective convergence properties hold and let
\mathbf{x}_{K}
be a local
\varepsilon_{K}
-minimum of
\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})
with uniform neighborhood modulus
\delta>0
, i.e.,

\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x}_{K})\leq\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon_{K},\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{K})\cap\mathcal{X}.

Then we have that any limit point
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
of the sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}\}
is a local minimum of
\varphi
, i.e., there exists
\tilde{\delta}>0
such that

\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{x}),\quad\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\tilde{\delta}}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\cap\mathcal{X}.

Proof.

For any limit point
\bar{\mathbf{x}}
of the sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}\}
, let
\{\mathbf{x}_{l}\}
be a subsequence of
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}\}
such that
\mathbf{x}_{l}\rightarrow\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\mathcal{X}
and
\mathbf{x}_{l}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
.
As
\{\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})\}
is uniformly bounded on
\mathcal{X}
, we can have a subsequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{m}\}
of
\{\mathbf{x}_{l}\}
satisfying
\mathbf{y}_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\rightarrow\bar{\mathbf{y}}
for some
\bar{\mathbf{y}}
. It follows from the
LL objective convergence property
that for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
M(\epsilon)>0
such that for any
m>M(\epsilon)
, we have

f(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))-f^{\ast}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\leq\epsilon.

By letting
m\rightarrow\infty
, and since
f
is continuous and
f^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})
is USC on
\mathcal{X}
, we have

f(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{y}})-f^{\ast}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\epsilon.

As
\epsilon
is arbitrarily chosen, we have
f(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{y}})-f^{\ast}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq 0
and thus
\bar{\mathbf{y}}\in\mathcal{S}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})
.
Next, as
F
is continuous at
(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{y}})
, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
M(\epsilon)>0
such that for any
m>M(\epsilon)
, it holds

F(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\bar{\mathbf{y}})\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))+\epsilon.

Then, we have, for any
m>M(\epsilon)
and
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
,

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})=\!\inf_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathcal{S}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})}\!F(\bar{\mathbf{x}},\mathbf{y})\leq F(\mathbf{x}_{m},\mathbf{y}_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m}))+\epsilon\!=\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m})+\epsilon.

Next, as
\mathbf{x}_{m}
is a local
\varepsilon_{m}
-minimum of
\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x})
with uniform neighborhood modulus
\delta
, it follows

\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\leq\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon_{m},\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\cap\mathcal{X}.

Since
\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2+\|\mathbf{x}_{m}-\bar{\mathbf{x}}\|}(\mathbf{x}_{m})\subseteq\mathbb{B}_{\delta}(\mathbf{x}_{m})
, we have that for any
\epsilon>0
,
\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\cap\mathcal{X}
, there exists
M(\epsilon)>0
such that whenever
m>M(\epsilon)
,

\displaystyle\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x}_{m})+\epsilon\leq\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon+\varepsilon_{m}.

Taking
m\rightarrow\infty
and by the UL objective convergence property and
\varepsilon_{m}\rightarrow 0
,
\forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\cap\mathcal{X}
we have

\displaystyle\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}\varphi_{m}(\mathbf{x})+\varepsilon_{m}+\epsilon=\varphi(\mathbf{x})+\epsilon.

By taking
\epsilon\rightarrow 0
, we have

\varphi(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\leq\varphi(\mathbf{x}),\ \forall\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\cap\mathcal{X},

which implies
\bar{\mathbf{x}}\in\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{B}_{\delta/2}(\bar{\mathbf{x}})\cap\mathcal{X}}\varphi(\mathbf{x})
, i.e,
\bar{x}
is a local minimum of
\varphi
.
∎

Lemma 1
.

Let

+\frac{\mu s_{u}\alpha_{k}}{s_{l}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u})+\frac{\mu}{2s_{l}}(1-\alpha_{k}s_{u}L_{F})\|\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u}\|^{2}\\
+\frac{1}{2s_{l}}\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{y}_{k+1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2s_{l}}\left\|\left((1-\mu)\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{l}+\mu\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u}\right)-\mathbf{y}_{k+1}\right\|^{2}\\

(12)

Proof.

It follows from the definitions of

(13)

Thus, for any
\mathbf{y}
, we have

\displaystyle 0=\alpha_{k}\langle\nabla\Psi(\mathbf{y}_{k}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u}\rangle+\langle\frac{\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u}-\mathbf{y}_{k}}{s_{u}},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{u}\rangle,\

(14)

\displaystyle 0=\beta_{k}\langle\nabla\psi(\mathbf{y}_{k}),\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{l}\rangle+\langle\frac{\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{l}-\mathbf{y}_{k}}{s_{l}},\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{z}_{k+1}^{l}\rangle.\ \

(15)

As

Lemma 2
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}

Proof.

(28)

+2\mu s_{u}DM_{F}+2(1-\mu)s_{l}c_{\beta}DM_{f},\end{array}

Theorem 5
.

Suppose Assumptions
1

\mathcal{X}

\underline{\beta}>0

|\beta_{k}-\beta_{k-1}|\leq\frac{c_{\beta}}{(k+1)^{2}}
with some
c_{\beta}>0
,
then we have that both the LL and UL objective convergence properties hold.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
are both compact, and
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is continuous on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}
, we have that

\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{Y}

\mathcal{X}

\mathbf{y}
by
M_{0}
for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{Y}

4

C>0

\begin{array}[]{l}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))-f^{*}(\mathbf{x})\leq C\sqrt{\frac{1+\ln K}{K^{\frac{1}{4}}}}.\end{array}

\{\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})\}\subset\mathcal{Y}

Further, in the following, we will show that when

\mathbf{y}
uniformly in

Theorem 6
.

Suppose Assumptions
1

\mathbf{y}
uniformly in
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}

\mathcal{X}
is compact, and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}(\mathbf{x})
is nonempty for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}

\{\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{x})\}

9

\mu\in(0,1)

\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{k+1}

\underline{\beta}>0

Proof.

According to the update scheme of

9

Stationarity Analysis of BDA

This part provides the convergence behavior of the problem that
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})
is solved to (approximate) stationarity.
We consider the special case where
\mathcal{Y}=\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the LL objective function

is
\sigma
-strongly convex for any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
. In this case, the solution set of LL problem
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})
is a singleton, and we denote its unique solution by

. In the following, we are going to show the convergence of BDA with respect to stationary points in this special case. Our analysis is partly inspired by
[
31

. We first make the following assumptions.

Assumption 2
.

F
and
f
are both twice continuously differentiable. For any
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
,

is
\sigma
-strongly convex.

Before providing the convergence results, we begin with a lemma.

Lemma 5
.

Let
\{a_{k}\}
and
\{b_{k}\}
be sequences of non-negative real numbers. Assume that
b_{k}\rightarrow 0
and there exist
\rho\in(0,1)
,
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

a_{k+1}\leq\rho a_{k}+b_{k},\ \forall k\geq n_{0}.

Then
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}a_{k}=0
.

Proof.

As
b_{k}\rightarrow 0
, there exists
B>0
such that
b_{k}\leq B
for all
k
. And we have for any
k\geq n_{0}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{k+1}\leq\rho a_{k}+b_{k}\leq\rho a_{k}+B\leq\rho^{k-n_{0}}a_{n_{0}}+\frac{B}{1-\rho},\end{array}

which implies the boundedness of the sequence
\{a_{k}\}
and thus there exists
A
such that
a_{k}\leq A
for all
k
.

For any
\epsilon>0
, since
b_{k}\rightarrow 0
, there exists
k_{1}>n_{0}
such that
b_{k}\leq\frac{(1-\rho)\epsilon}{2}
for all
k\geq k_{1}
. And for any
k\geq k_{1}
,

\begin{array}[]{l}a_{k+1}\leq\rho a_{k}+b_{k}\leq\rho a_{k}+\frac{(1-\rho)\epsilon}{2}\leq\rho^{k-k_{1}}A+\frac{\epsilon}{2}.\end{array}

Since
\rho\in(0,1)
, there exists
k_{2}\geq k_{1}
such that for any
k\geq k_{2}
,
\rho^{k-k_{1}}A\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
and hence
a_{k+1}\leq\epsilon
. As
\epsilon
is arbitrarily chosen, we obtain that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}a_{k}=0
.
∎

By applying implicit function theorem on the optimality condition of the LL problem, we obtain that
\mathbf{y}^{*}(\mathbf{x})
is differentiable on
\mathcal{X}
and its derivative is given by

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}=-\left(\nabla_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x}))\right)^{-1}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})).\end{array}

(35)

Hence, the function
\varphi(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x}))
is also differentiable and its derivative is given by Eq. (
4

5

Proposition 1
.

Suppose Assumptions
1
and
2
are satisfied,
f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
is level-bounded in
\mathbf{y}

Proof.

According to the update scheme of

\mathbf{y}_{k+1}-\mathbf{y}^{*}\!=\!\mathbf{y}_{k}-s\beta_{k}(1-\mu)\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{k})-\mathbf{y}^{*}-s\alpha_{k}\mu\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{k}).

As

Let

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{y}_{k+1}-\mathbf{y}^{*}\|\leq\rho\|\mathbf{y}_{k}-\mathbf{y}^{*}\|+s\alpha_{k}\mu\|\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{k})\|.

As shown in Theorem

\begin{array}[]{l}\sup\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}_{k+1}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}-\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right\|\leq\rho\sup\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\left\|\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}-\frac{\partial\mathbf{y}^{\ast}(\mathbf{x})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\right\|\\

This section first provides a comparison with the existing LLS scheme by a high dimension counter-example in Section

VII-A

Comparison with Existing LLS Theories

As aforementioned, a number of gradient-based methods have been proposed to solve BLO in Eq. (
1
). However, these existing methods all rely on the uniqueness of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{x})
(i.e., LLS assumption). That is, rather than considering the original BLO in Eq. (
1
), they actually solve the simplification in Eq. (
3
). By considering
\mathbf{y}
as a function of
\mathbf{x}
, the idea behind these approaches is to take a gradient-based first-order scheme (e.g, gradient descent, stochastic gradient descent, or their variations) on the LL subproblem. Therefore, with the initialization point
\mathbf{y}_{0}
, a sequence
\{\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{x})\}_{k=0}^{K}
parameterized by
\mathbf{x}
can be generated, e.g.,

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbf{y}_{k+1}(\mathbf{x})=\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{x})-s_{l}\nabla_{\mathbf{y}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{k}(\mathbf{x})),\ k=0,\cdots,K-1,\end{array}

(39)

where
s_{l}>0
is an appropriately chosen step size. Then by considering
\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})
(i.e., the output of Eq. (
39
) for a given
\mathbf{x}
) as an approximated optimal solution to the LL subproblem, we can incorporate
\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x})
into the UL objective and obtain a single-level approximation model, i.e.,
\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))
. Next, by unrolling the iterative update scheme in Eq. (
39
), we can calculate the derivative of
F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K}(\mathbf{x}))
(w.r.t.
\mathbf{x}
) to optimize Eq. (
3
) by automatic differentiation techniques
[
14
,
43
]
.

\mathbf{y}

The UL objective
F
is indeed a function of both the UL variable
\mathbf{x}
and the LL variable
\mathbf{y}
. Conventional gradient-based bi-level methods (Eq. (
39
)) only use the gradient information of the LL subproblem to update
\mathbf{y}
. Thanks to the LLS assumption, for fixed UL variable
\mathbf{x}
, the LL solution
\mathbf{y}
can be uniquely determined. Thus the sequence
\{\mathbf{y}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
could converge to the true optimal solution, that minimizes both the LL and UL objectives. However, when LLS is absent,
\{\mathbf{y}_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K}
may easily fail to converge to the true solution. Therefore,
\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}
may tend to be incorrect limiting points.

Example 1
.

(Counter-Example)
With
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the following BLO problem:

\begin{array}[]{l}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{z}\|^{4}+\|\mathbf{y}-\mathbf{e}\|^{4},\\
s.t.\ (\mathbf{y},\mathbf{z})\in\arg\min\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}\|^{2}-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{y},\end{array}

(40)

where
\mathcal{X}=[-100,100]\times\cdots[-100,100]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mathbf{e}
denotes the vector whose elements are all equal to
1
. By simple calculation, we know that the unique optimal solution of Eq. (
40
) is
\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{y}^{*}=\mathbf{z}^{*}=\mathbf{e}
. However, if adopting the existing gradient-based scheme in Eq. (
39
) with initialization
(\mathbf{y}_{0},\mathbf{z}_{0})=(0,0)
and varying step size
s_{l}^{k}\in(0,1)
, we have that
\mathbf{y}_{K}=(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))\mathbf{x}
and
\mathbf{z}_{K}=0
. Then the approximated problem of Eq. (
40
) amounts to

\begin{array}[]{l}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K},\mathbf{z}_{K})=\|\mathbf{x}\|^{4}+\|(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{e}\|^{4}.\end{array}

Consider sequence

\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}=\arg\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K},\mathbf{z}_{K}),

it follows from the first-order optimality condition that,

0=4\|\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}\|^{2}\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}+4a_{K}\|a_{K}\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}-\mathbf{e}\|^{2}(a_{K}\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}-\mathbf{e}),

(41)

where
a_{K}=(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))
.
Then, if sequence
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}\}
converge to a limit point
\mathbf{e}
, and since
\{a_{K}\}
is bounded, there exist subsequences
\{\mathbf{x}_{K_{\ell}}^{*}\}\subset\{\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}\}
and
\{a_{K_{\ell}}\}\subset\{a_{K}\}
such that
\{\mathbf{x}_{K_{\ell}}^{*}\}\rightarrow\mathbf{e}
and
\{a_{K_{\ell}}\}\rightarrow\bar{a}
. By considering subsequences
\{\mathbf{x}_{K_{\ell}}^{*}\}
and
\{a_{K_{\ell}}\}
in Eq. (
41
) and taking
K_{\ell}\rightarrow\infty
, we should have

\begin{array}[]{l}0=\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}\mathbf{e}+\bar{a}\|\bar{a}\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{e}\|^{2}(\bar{a}\mathbf{e}-\mathbf{e})\\
\quad=[1+(\bar{a}-1)^{3}\bar{a}]\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}\mathbf{e},\end{array}

and thus
0=1+(\bar{a}-1)^{3}\bar{a}
. However, since
a_{K}=(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))\in[0,1]
, then
\bar{a}\in[0,1]
and

1+(\bar{a}-1)^{3}\bar{a}\geq 1-|(\bar{a}-1)\bar{a}|\geq\frac{3}{4}>0,

which is a contradiction to
0=1+(\bar{a}-1)^{3}\bar{a}
.
Therefore, any subseuqnce of
\{\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}\}
cannot converge to the true solution (i.e.,
\mathbf{x}^{*}=\mathbf{e}
).

Remark 3
.

Actually, even with strongly convex UL objective w.r.t. LL variable
\mathbf{y}
, the existing bi-level based methods still may fail to reach an optimal solution. For example, with
\mathbf{x}\in[-100,100]
and
\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we consider the following BLO problem:

\begin{array}[]{c}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in[-100,100]}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-1\|^{2},\\
s.t.\ \mathbf{y}\in\arg\min\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{1}\|^{2}-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{1}.\end{array}

By simple calculation, we know that the unique optimal solution of Eq. (
3
) is
\mathbf{x}^{*}=1,\mathbf{y}^{*}=(1,1)
. However, if adopting the existing gradient-based scheme in Eq. (
39
) with initialization
\mathbf{y}_{0}=(0,0)
and varying step size
s_{l}^{k}\in(0,1)
, we have that
[\mathbf{y}_{K}]_{1}=(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))\mathbf{x}
and
[\mathbf{y}_{K}]_{2}=0
. By defining
\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})=F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{K})
, we have

\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}=\frac{(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))}{1+(1-\prod_{k=0}^{K-1}(1-s_{l}^{k}))^{2}}.

It is easy to check that

\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{2}
.
So
\mathbf{x}_{K}^{*}
cannot converge to the true solution (i.e.,
\mathbf{x}^{*}=1
).

Remark 4
.

In applications, to achieve the LLS, people sometimes add a strongly convex regularization term to the LL subproblem. We must clarify that this strategy is only heuristic, which usually causes unpredictable large deviation from the true solution.

Indeed, even the strongly convex regularization is set to be vanishing, such an approximation procedure cannot guarantee any convergence to the true solution. We will take the counter-example in Remark
3
again for illustration. Specifically, we introduce a quadratic term
1/2\varepsilon\|\mathbf{y}_{2}\|^{2}
to the LL subproblem

\min\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\|\mathbf{y}_{2}\|^{2}-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{1}.

Apparently, the LL objective becomes strongly convex. But it can be checked that the optimal solution to such bilevel problem with regularized LL

\begin{array}[]{l}\min\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in[-100,100]}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}_{2}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{1}-1\|^{2},\\
s.t.\ \mathbf{y}\in\arg\min\limits_{\mathbf{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{2}\|\mathbf{y}_{1}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon\|\mathbf{y}_{2}\|^{2}-\mathbf{x}^{\top}\mathbf{y}_{1},\end{array}

becomes
\mathbf{x}^{*}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}
,
\mathbf{y}_{1}^{*}(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{2}
,
\mathbf{y}_{2}^{*}(\varepsilon)=0
which is obviously no longer the true solution to the original bilevel model. Moreover, even with
\varepsilon
tending
0
, unfortunately,
\mathbf{x}^{*}(\varepsilon)
,
\mathbf{y}_{1}^{*}(\epsilon)
and
\mathbf{y}_{2}^{*}(\epsilon)
still fail to converge to the true solution
(1,1,1)
.

VII-B

Comparison with the Work in
[
1
]

First of all, this work significantly improves the assumptions required by our convergence analysis. That is, we successfully remove the strong convexity property on the UL objective, the level-bounded in
\mathbf{y}
and locally uniformly in
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{X}
property on the LL objective. Furthermore, we replace the essential condition “LL solution set property” by “LL objective convergence property”, which is much weaker and easily verifiable. In this way, we actually obtain a more general and feasible proof recipe for challenging real-world applications.

We also extend our convergence results to other optimization scenarios, such as local and stationary results. Specifically, we obtain convergence results for the case that there are only local solutions to the UL approximation (i.e., “
\min_{\mathbf{x}}\varphi_{K}(\mathbf{x})
”). Moreover, we provide new methodology to analyze the convergence behaviors of our BDA in the scenario that we can only obtain the stationary points for the UL approximation. Therefore, this work has comprehensively analyzed the convergence behaviors for our BDA in various (i.e., global, local and stationary) optimization scenarios.

Algorithmically, this work establishes a more general framework, in which we introduce the projection-based operations to handle set constrains in BLOs and design more flexible strategy to set the aggregation parameters during iterations. We also established a one-stage fast approximation to BDA for solving large-scale real-world problems.

We design a high-dimensional counter-example (see Eq. (40)) and conduct various experiments to verify our new theoretical findings, i.e., the efficiency of BDA for BLOs without the LLS condition and both the UL and LL objectives are convex but not strongly convex. We further do new experiments to more clearly analyze the components of BDA and report more results on real applications (e.g., with new evaluation metrics, on more challenging benchmarks and compared with more state-of-the-art approaches).

VII-C

One-stage BDA: A Fast Implementation

Multi-step of the LL iteration modules
\mathcal{T}_{k}
will cause a lot of memory consumption that may be an obstacle in modern massive-scale deep learning applications. Thus it would be useful to simplify iteration steps. This part provides an extension scheme leveraging a one-stage simplification to reduce complicated gradient-based calculation steps
[
17
]
. By setting
K=1
in Eq. (
9
), the algorithm reads as

\displaystyle\mathbf{y}_{1}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{T}_{1}\left(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathtt{Proj}_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{y}_{0}-s\partial_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})\right),

(42)

where
\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})=\alpha F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})+\beta f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})
and
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
denote the aggregation parameters. Indeed, if
(\mathbf{y}_{0}-s\partial_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0}))\in\mathcal{Y}
, with this one-stage simplification, we can simplify the back-propagation calculation with the following finite difference approximation

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d\varphi_{1}(\mathbf{x})}{d\mathbf{x}}=\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}+\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})}{\partial\mathbf{y}_{1}}\frac{d\mathbf{y}_{1}}{d\mathbf{x}}\\
\quad\quad\quad\approx\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}-s\frac{\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{+})-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{-})}{2\epsilon},\end{array}

where
\mathbf{h}_{0}^{\pm}=\mathbf{y}_{0}\pm\epsilon\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})/\partial\mathbf{y}_{1}
and
\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{x}}F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{x}}f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})
. Since
\mathcal{Y}
can be a big interval, this case (i.e.,
(\mathbf{y}_{0}-s\partial_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0}))\in\mathcal{Y}
) is often satisfied in general. If
(\mathbf{y}_{0}-s\partial_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0}))\notin\mathcal{Y}
, the above back-propagation can be calculated by the following form

\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d\varphi_{1}(\mathbf{x})}{d\mathbf{x}}\approx\frac{\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})}{\partial\mathbf{x}}\\
\quad\quad\quad-\frac{\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{++})-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{-+})-\left(\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{+-})-\partial_{\mathbf{x}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{h}_{0}^{--})\right)}{4\epsilon^{1+\frac{1}{2}}},\end{array}

where
\mathbf{h}_{0}^{\pm+}=\mathbf{y}_{0}\pm\epsilon\mathtt{Proj}_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{z}_{0}+\epsilon^{1/2}\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})/\partial\mathbf{y}\right)
and
\mathbf{h}_{0}^{\pm-}=\mathbf{y}_{0}\pm\epsilon\mathtt{Proj}_{\mathcal{Y}}\left(\mathbf{z}_{0}-\epsilon^{1/2}\partial F(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{1})/\partial\mathbf{y}\right)
with
\mathbf{z}_{0}=\mathbf{y}_{0}-s\partial_{\mathbf{y}}\phi(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}_{0})
.

f(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y})=\sum_{(\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})\in\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{tr}}}[\sigma(\mathbf{x})]_{i}\ell(\mathbf{y};\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i}),

where

\mathbf{y}

and
f
w.r.t.
\mathbf{y}
is required to be convex. To satisfy this requirement, we design the classifier with a fully connected layer.

Meta-Learning

Meta-learning aims to leverage a large number of similar few-shot tasks to learn an algorithm that should work well on novel tasks in which only a few labeled samples are available. In particular, we consider the few-shot learning problem
[
46
,
47
]
, where each task is to discriminate
N

\mathbf{x}
such that each task can be solved only with
M

N

M
-shot). Following the experimental protocol used in recent works that the network architecture is with four-layer CNNs followed by fully connected layer, we separate the network architecture into two parts: the cross-task intermediate representation layers (parameterized by
\mathbf{x}
) outputs the meta features and the multinomial logistic regression layer (parameterized by
\mathbf{y}^{j}
) as our ground classifier for the
j
-th task. We also collect a meta training data set
\mathcal{D}=\{\mathcal{D}^{j}\}
, where
\mathcal{D}^{j}=\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{tr}}^{j}\cup\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{val}}^{j}
is linked to the
j
-th task. Then for the

\ell(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{j};\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{tr}}^{j})
as the task-specific loss and thus the LL objective can be defined as

\begin{array}[]{c}f(\mathbf{x},\{\mathbf{y}^{j}\})=\sum\limits_{j}\ell(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{j};\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{tr}}^{j}).\end{array}

As for the UL objective, we also utilize cross-entropy function but define it based on
\{\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{val}}^{j}\}
as

\begin{array}[]{c}F(\mathbf{x},\{\mathbf{y}^{j}\})=\sum\limits_{j}\ell(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{y}^{j};\mathcal{D}_{\mathtt{val}}^{j}).\end{array}

IX
Conclusions

This work established a flexible descent aggregation framework with task-tailored iteration dynamics modules to solve bi-level tasks by formulating BLO in Eq. (
2
) from the viewpoint of optimistic bi-level. We provided a new algorithmic framework to handle the LLS issue. Then, this work strictly proved the convergence of the developed framework without the LLS assumption and the strong convexity in the UL objective. Focusing on different solution qualities (namely, global, local, and stationarity), this work elaborated the convergence results respectively. Finally, extensive experiments justified our theoretical results and demonstrated the superiority of the proposed algorithm for hyper-parameter optimization and meta-learning.

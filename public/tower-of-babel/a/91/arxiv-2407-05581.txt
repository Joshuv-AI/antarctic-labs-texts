Symmetric Biderivations on Complex Semisimple Lie Algebras Thanks: This work was supported by the National Natural Science Foundation of China (12071405, 12301040) and Nanhu Scholars Program of XYNU (No. 012111, 2021030)
Shiyuan Liu, Dong Liu, Yueqiang Zhao

Abstract
. This paper studies biderivations on finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebras to their finite-dimensional modules. More precisely, we prove that all such symmetric biderivations are trivial. As applications, we determine all biderivations on Takiff algebras and symplectic oscillator algebras, as well as all supersymmetric biderivations over some Lie superalgebras including all finite-dimensional classical simple Lie superalgebras.



Key words
: Lie (super)algebra, biderivation.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 17B20, 17B40, 17B65.

1.
Introduction

Derivations and generalized derivations are important in the study of structure of various algebras. Bre
\breve{\mathrm{s}}
ar et al. introduced the notion of biderivation of rings in
[
1
]
, and they showed that all biderivations of noncommutative prime rings are inner. In
[
17
]
, the biderivations of Lie algebras was introduced and the authors proved that all skew-symmetric biderivations of finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
0
are inner. In recent years, many scholars gave their attentions to the study of biderivations of many Lie algebras using case by case discussions, see
[
3
,
4
,
14
,
13
,
8
,
15
]
.
Certainly, some complicated calculations were used in many papers.

The skew-symmetric biderivations of Lie algebras and Lie superalgebras have been sufficiently studied. In
[
2
,
12
]
, the authors proved that all (super-)skew-symmetric biderivations on any perfect and centerless Lie (super)algebras are inner (super-)biderivations. With this result, all skew-symmetric biderivations on many Lie (super)algebras can be determined (see
[
2
]
for details).

It is well known that any biderivation can be decomposed into a sum of a skew-symmetric biderivation and a symmetric biderivation, and the later can determined commutative post-Lie algebra structures, which are connected with the homology of partition posets and Koszul operads
[
16
]
.
However, there is no any sufficient tool to determine symmetric biderivations on some Lie algebras and Lie superalgebras. In
[
5
]
, Chen et al. use a conceptual method to determine all symmetric biderivations on finite-dimensional simple Lie algebras. In
[
17
]
, Wang et al. study symmetric biderivations from the simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
to its finite-dimensional irreducible modules by concrete calculations.
In
[
6
]
, Dilxat et al. determine all symmetric biderivations from the Virasoro algebra to its density modules. Based on it, all symmetric biderivations of some Lie superalgeras related to the Virasoro algebra are easily to determined.

From
[
5
,
17
,
6
]
, we seen that it is important to determine all symmetric biderivations from a finite-dimensinoal semisimple complex Lie algebra to its arbitrary finite-dimensional module. Inspired by
[
5
]
,
we proved that all the symmetric biderivations from a finite-dimensinoal semisimple complex Lie algebra to its arbitrary finite-dimensional module are trivial in this paper (Theorem
3.2
below), by using some representation theory of semisimple Lie algebras. By this main result, we can easily determine all the symmetric biderivations of reductive Lie algebras, Takiff algebras, the symplectic oscillator algebras, and the super-symmetric biderivations of certain Lie superalgebras.

Throughout this paper, all vector
spaces and algebras are over the complex field
\mathbb{C}
.

2.
Biderivations

We first recall the notion of derivations.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. A linear map
D:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
is called a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
if

D([x,y])=xD(y)-yD(x),~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Note that a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to its adjoint representation is just an usual derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
For any
v\in V
, the linear map

D_{v}:\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V,~x\longmapsto xv

is a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
, which is called inner. Denote by
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g},V)
the set of derivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
and
\mathrm{IDer}(\mathfrak{g},V)
the set of inner derivations. Then
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a linear space and
\mathrm{IDer}(\mathfrak{g},V)
is a subspace of
\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. The 1st cohomology group is defined as

H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g},V)/\mathrm{IDer}(\mathfrak{g},V).

We have the following Whitehead’s Lemma

Theorem 2.1

(
[
7
, Proposition 6.1]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a semisimple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic
0
,
V
a finite dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Then
H^{1}(\mathfrak{g},V)=0
. In particular, the derivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
are all inner.

We now introduce the notion of biderivations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module. A bilinear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
is called a biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
if

\displaystyle\delta([x,y],z)=x\delta(y,z)-y\delta(x,z),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\delta(x,[y,z])=y\delta(x,z)-z\delta(x,y)

(2.2)

hold for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Note that a biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to its adjoint representation is just a usual biderivation of
L
. It is elementary to check that a bilinear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
is a biderivation if and only if the linear maps

\displaystyle\delta(\cdot,x):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V,~y\longmapsto\delta(y,x),

\displaystyle\delta(x,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V,~y\longmapsto\delta(x,y)

are both derivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Assume that
\gamma:V\longrightarrow W
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism. Then the bilinear map
\gamma\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow W
is a biderivation from
V
to
W
.

A biderivation
\delta
from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
is said to be symmetric (resp. skew-symmetric) if
\delta(x,y)=\delta(y,x)
(resp.
\delta(x,y)=\delta(y,x)
) for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. It is well-know that any biderivation can be decomposed into a sum of a symmetric biderivation and a skew-symmetric biderivation. For skew-symmetric biderivations, we have the following theorem

Theorem 2.2

(
[
2
, Theorem 3.2]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a perfect Lie algebra and
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module such that

Z_{V}(\mathfrak{g}):=\{v\in V\mid xv=0~\text{for all}~x\in\mathfrak{g}\}=\{0\}.

Then every skew-symmetric biderivation
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
is of the form
\delta(x,y)=\gamma([x,y])
for some
\gamma\in\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g},V)
.

In contrast to the skew-symmetric biderivations, there are no sufficient tools to determine the symmetric biderivations. In this paper, we mainly study the symmetric biderivations from a complex semisimple Lie algebra to its finite-dimensional module. The
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
case has been studied in
[
18
]
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
18
]
)
.

Let
V
be an irreducible
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-module. Then all the biderivations from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
to
V
are trival.

Now we show the following corollary which will be used in the next section.

Corollary 2.4
.

All the biderivations from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
to its finite-dimensional module are trival.

Proof.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-module. By Weyl’s theorem,
V
is completely irruducible, i.e., V can be written as a direct sum of its irreducible submodules, say

V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m}.

For
1\leq i\leq m
, let
p_{i}:V\longrightarrow V_{i}
be the canonical projection. Let
\delta
be a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
to
V
. Then for each
1\leq i\leq m
,
p_{i}\delta
is a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
to
V_{i}
. By the above lemma,
p_{i}\delta=0
. It follows that the image of
\delta
is
0
. Hence,
\delta=0
.
∎

3.
Main Result

We first recall some basic facts about finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebras and their finite dimensional modules.

In the following, let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, all the
\mathfrak{g}
-modules are assumed to be finite-dimensional. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\mathfrak{g}
admits a Cartan decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

where
\Phi\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
is the root system. Let
\Phi^{+}
(resp.
\Phi^{-}
) be the set of positive (resp. negative) roots with respect to a certain chosen base. Set

\mathfrak{n}^{+}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},~\mathfrak{n}^{-}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}.

For each
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, choose an nonzero element
e_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
. Then there exist unique

f_{\alpha}\in\Phi^{-}
and
h_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{h}
such that

[h_{\alpha},e_{\alpha}]=2e_{\alpha},~[h_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}]=-2f_{\alpha},~[e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}]=h_{\alpha}.

Hence
S_{\alpha}=\mathbb{C}e_{\alpha}\oplus\mathbb{C}h_{\alpha}\oplus\mathbb{C}f_{\alpha}
is a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
, which is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. It is well known that
\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by the elements
e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha},h_{\alpha}~\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.

Let
V
be a finite-dimensional irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-module. It is well known that
V
is both a highest weight module and a lowest weight module. Hence, if
\dim V>1
, then for each weight vector
v
of
V
, there exist weight vectors (or
0
)
v_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}^{\prime},\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that

w=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(e_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}+f_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}^{\prime}).

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebra and
V,W
be
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with
V
finite-dimensional. Assume that a bilinear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times V\longrightarrow W
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

For each
x\in\mathfrak{g}
,
\delta(x,\cdot):V\longrightarrow W
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism;

(2)

For each
v\in V
,
\delta(\cdot,v):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow W
is a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
W
,

then
\delta=0
.

Proof.

We first assume that
V
is irreducible with highest weight vector
v
of highest weight
\Lambda
and lowest weight vector
v^{\prime}
of lowest weight
\Lambda^{\prime}
. Let
W^{\prime}=\delta(\mathfrak{g},V)
be the image of
\delta
, then

W^{\prime}=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\left(\delta(e_{\alpha},V)+\delta(h_{\alpha},V)+\delta(f_{\alpha},V)\right).

It is obviously that
\dim W^{\prime}<\infty
and

f_{\alpha}W^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}_{\Lambda}=e_{\alpha}W^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\{0\},~\forall\alpha\in\Phi^{+}.

Since
\delta(\cdot,v)
and
\delta(\cdot,v^{\prime})
are both derivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
W^{\prime}
, by Whitehead’s lemma, there exist
w,w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
such that

\delta(x,v)=xw,~\delta(x,v^{\prime})=xw^{\prime},\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

In particular,

\displaystyle\delta(e_{\alpha},v^{\prime})=e_{\alpha}w^{\prime}\in e_{\alpha}W^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}=\{0\},

\displaystyle\delta(f_{\alpha},v)=f_{\alpha}w\in f_{\alpha}W^{\prime}\cap W^{\prime}_{\Lambda}=\{0\},

for any
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
.
It follows that

\delta(e_{\alpha},V)=\delta(f_{\alpha},V)=0,~\forall\alpha\in\Phi^{+},

since
\delta(e_{\alpha},\cdot)
and
\delta(f_{\alpha},\cdot)
are both
\mathfrak{g}
-homomorphisms and
V
is irreducible.
Since
\mathfrak{g}
is generated by the elements
e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha}
’s and
\delta(\cdot,v)
is a derivation for each
v\in V
, we obtain that

\delta(\mathfrak{g},V)=0.

That is
\delta=0
.

Now, consider the general case. Let
V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m}
with each
V_{i}
irreducible.
For each
i
, the bilinear map
\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}\times V_{i}}
satisfies the condition (1) and (2). Hence,
\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}\times V_{i}}=0
, i.e.
\delta(\mathfrak{g},V_{i})=0
since
V_{i}
is irreducible. We obtain

\delta(\mathfrak{g},V)=\delta(\mathfrak{g},V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m})=\delta(\mathfrak{g},V_{1})+\delta(\mathfrak{g},V_{2})+\cdots+\delta(\mathfrak{g},V_{m})=0.

Equivalently,
\delta=0
.
∎

Now we can prove our main result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebra and
V
be a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
Then all the symmetric biderivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
are all trival.

Proof.

Assume that
\delta
is a symmetric biderivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
. Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Note that the bilinear map
\delta_{S_{\alpha}\times S_{\alpha}}
gives a symmetric biderivations from
S_{\alpha}
to
V
. Then, by Corollary
2.4
,
\delta|_{S_{\alpha}\times S_{\alpha}}=0
, i.e.,

\delta(x,y)=0,~\forall x,y\in S_{\alpha}.

(3.1)

Let
x\in S_{\alpha}
. For any
y\in S_{\alpha}
and
z\in\mathfrak{g}
, one has

\delta(x,[y,z])=y\delta(x,z)-z\delta(x,y)=y\delta(x,z).

It follows that

\delta(x,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V,~z\longmapsto\delta(x,z)

is an
S_{\alpha}
-module homomorphism. Hence, the bilinear map
\delta|_{S_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{g}}
satisfies the conditions (1) and (2) in above proposition. It follows that
\delta|_{S_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{g}}=0
. In particular,

\delta(e_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g})=\delta(f_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g})=\delta(h_{\alpha},\mathfrak{g})=0.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is spanned by the elements
e_{\alpha},f_{\alpha},h_{\alpha}~\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, we have
\delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g})=0
, i.e.
\delta=0
.
∎

We can have immediately the following corollary, which was also obtained in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra. Then all the symmetric biderivations
\mathfrak{g}
are trival.

Moreover, we have

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex Lie algebra containing a semisimple subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
V
be a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module.
If
\delta
is a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
, then
\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g})=0
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}_{0}}=0
. Note that
\mathfrak{g}
can be viewed as a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module via the adjoint action. Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. For any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},~z\in\mathfrak{g}
, one has

\delta(x,[y,z])=y\delta(x,z)-z\delta(x,y)=y\delta(x,z),

which implies that
\delta(x,\cdot):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module homomorphism. Hence, the bilinear map

\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V

satisfies the conditions (1) and (2) Proposition
3.1
. It follows that
\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g})=0
.
∎

4.
Applications

In this section, we give some examples to show how to use Theorem
3.2
to determine all the symmetric biderivations of some Lie algebras.

4.1.
Preliminaries

Let’s do some preparations for the further discussion.
The following lemma can be easily checked by a direct computation.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
be a symmetric biderivation. Then we have

\delta(x,[y,z])+\delta(y,[z,x])+\delta(z,[x,y])=0,~\forall x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}.

The following proposition can be deduced from the above lemma.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a complex Lie algbera and
V
be a
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
c
be a central element in
\mathfrak{g}
. Assume that
\delta
is a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
. Then
\delta(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)},c)=0
.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, by Lemma
4.1
, one has

\delta(c,[x,y])=\delta(c,[x,y])+0+0=\delta(c,[x,y])+\delta(x,[y,c])+\delta(y,[c,x])=0.

The proof is completed.
∎

We call a
\mathfrak{g}
-module faithful if
Z_{V}(\mathfrak{g})=0
.
The following proposition is easy but useful.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, and
W,V,U
be
\mathfrak{g}
-modules with
W,V
finite-dimensional and
V
faithful. If a bilinear map
\delta:W\times V\longrightarrow U
satisfies the following conditions

(1)

For each
w\in W
,
\delta(w,\cdot):V\longrightarrow U
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism;

(2)

For each
v\in V
,
\delta(\cdot,v):V\longrightarrow U
is a
\mathfrak{g}
-module homomorphism,

then
\delta=0
.

Proof.

We first assume that
V
is irreducible. Then
\dim V>1
since
V
is a faithful
\mathfrak{g}
-module. Let
w\in W
and
v\in V
be two weight vectors. If
\mathrm{wt}(w)\neq\mathrm{wt}(v)
, then
\delta(w,v)=0
. Assume that
\mathrm{wt}(w)=\mathrm{wt}(v)
. We can find weight vectors (or
0
)
v_{\alpha},v_{\alpha}^{\prime},\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
such that

v=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(e_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}+f_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}^{\prime}).

We have

\delta(w,v)=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(\delta(w,e_{\alpha}v_{\alpha})+\delta(w,f_{\alpha}v_{\alpha}^{\prime}))=\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}(e_{\alpha}\delta(w,v_{\alpha})+f_{\alpha}\delta(w,v_{\alpha}^{\prime}))=0.

It follows that
\delta=0
.

Assume that
V
is reducible. Then by Weyl’s theorem,
V
can be written as a direct sum of irreducible
\mathfrak{g}
-submodules

V=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m}.

Since
V
is faithful,
V_{i}
is faithful for each
1\leq i\leq m
. By the above discussion,

\delta(W,V_{i})=0,~i=1,2,\cdots,m.

Hence,

\delta(W,V)=\delta(W,V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m})=\delta(W,V_{1})+\delta(W,V_{2})+\cdots+\delta(W,V_{m})=0.

That is
\delta=0
.
∎

We can prove the following two theorems.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex Lie algbera containing a semisimple Lie algbera
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
V
a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module. If
\mathfrak{g}
is faithful as a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module,
then all the symmetric biderivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
are trival.

Proof.

By Corollary
3.4
,
\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g})=0
. Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. For any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
z\in\mathfrak{g}
, one has

\displaystyle\delta(x,[y,z])

\displaystyle=y\delta(x,z)-z\delta(x,y)=y\delta(x,z),

\displaystyle\delta([y,z],x)

\displaystyle=y\delta(z,x)-z\delta(y,x)=y\delta(x,z).

Hence,
\delta(\cdot,x)
and
\delta(x,\cdot)
are both
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module homomorphisms from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
. Since,
\mathfrak{g}
is faithful as a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module, we have

\delta=0
by Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\delta
be a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
.
Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex perfect Lie algbera containing a semisimple Lie algbera
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,
V
a finite-dimensional
\mathfrak{g}
-module. If the center of
\mathfrak{g}
equals

Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})=\{x\in\mathfrak{g}\mid[y,x]=0~\text{for all~}y\in\mathfrak{g}\},

then all the symmetric biderivations from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
are trival.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be a symmetric biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}
to
V
.
As a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module,
\mathfrak{g}
can be decomposed as

\mathfrak{g}=W\oplus Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0}),

where
W
is a faithful
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-submodule of
\mathfrak{g}
. By Corollary
3.4
,
\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g})=0
.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}
and
w\in W
. For any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, one has

\displaystyle\delta(x,[y,w])

\displaystyle=y\delta(x,w)-w\delta(x,y)=y\delta(x,z),

\displaystyle\delta([y,x],w)

\displaystyle=y\delta(x,w)-x\delta(y,w)=x\delta(w,v).

Hence,
\delta(x,\cdot):W\longrightarrow V
and
\delta(\cdot,w):\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow V
are both
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module homomorphisms. Since,
W
is a faithful
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module, by Proposition 4.3, we have

\delta(x,w)=0,~\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},w\in W.

(4.1)

Since
\mathfrak{g}
is perfect, by Proposition
4.2
,

\delta(\mathfrak{g},Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0}))=\delta(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)},Z_{\mathfrak{g}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0}))=0.

The proof is completed.
∎

4.2.
Reductive Lie algebras

A reductive Lie algebra is a finite-dimensional central extension of certain semisimple Lie algebras. In the following, we just consider the simplest case.
We remark here that the discussion can be easily applied to determine the symmetric biderivations of a reductive Lie algebra. We leave it to the interested readers.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the
1
-dimensional central extension of a semisimple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, i.e.
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}c
with
[c,\mathfrak{g}]=0
. We will determine the symmetric biderivations of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\delta
be a symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then by Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
4.2
, we have

\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{0})=0,~\delta(\mathfrak{g}^{(1)},c)=\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},c)=0.

Since
\delta(\cdot,c)
is a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
, there exists some
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\delta(c,c)=\lambda c.

Conversely, for each
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
, the formula

\delta(x+\mu_{1}c,y+\mu_{2}c)=\mu_{1}\mu_{2}\lambda c,~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{C}

(4.2)

indeed determines a symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
.
To summarize, we obtain

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathbb{C}c
be a reductive Lie algbera, where
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is semisimple. Then each symmetric biderivation
\delta
of
\mathfrak{g}
is of the form

\delta(x+\mu_{1}c,y+\mu_{1}c)=\lambda\mu_{1}\mu_{2}c,~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\mathbb{C},

for a complex number
\lambda
.

4.3.
The Takiff algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite-dimensional complex semisimple Lie algebra, the Takiff algebra
[
11
]
associated
\mathfrak{g}
is, by definition,

\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathbb{C}[t]/(t^{2})
. Let
V=\mathfrak{g}\otimes\overline{t}
. Then
V
is an ideal of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
. As a
\mathfrak{g}
-module,
V
is isomorphic to the adjoint representation. Note that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes V
with bracket

[x+v,y+w]=[x,y]+xw-yv,~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},v,w\in V.

Here,

x(y\otimes\overline{t})=[xy]\otimes\overline{t},~\forall y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We can obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 4.7
.

All the symmetric biderivations of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
are trival.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be a symmetric biderivation of
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
.
Note that
\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
is isomorphic to
V\oplus V
as a
\mathfrak{g}
-module, which is faithful. Hence, by Theorem
4.4
,
\delta=0
.
∎

4.4.
The symplectic oscillator algebras

Following
[
9
]
, we recall the symplectic oscillator algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
. Recall that the symplectic complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
can be realized the matrix Lie algbera consisting all the
2n\times 2n
complex matrices with block form

\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&-A^{\mathrm{T}}\end{pmatrix},

where
A,B,C
are
n\times n
matrices with
B=B^{\mathrm{T}}
and
C=C^{\mathrm{T}}
.
It is well known that
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}
has the natural representation on
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
by the left matrix multiplication.
Note that
\mathbb{C}^{2n}
is an irreducible
\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
-module via the natural action.

The Heisenberg Lie algbera
H_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{C}z
is the Lie algbera with bracket given by

[e_{i},e_{n+i}]=z,~[z,H_{n}]=0.

The symplectic oscillator algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is the semidirect product Lie algbera

\mathfrak{g}_{n}=\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})\ltimes H_{n}

with bracket

[x,v]=xv,~[x,z]=0

for all
x\in\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C}),~v\in\mathbb{C}^{2n}
. It is easy to see that
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
is perfect and

Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{n}}({\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})})=\mathbb{C}z.

By Theorem
4.5
, we obtain the following theorem immediately

Theorem 4.8
.

All the symmetric biderivations of
\mathfrak{g}_{n}
are trival.

4.5.
The Schr
\ddot{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{o}}}
dinger algebras

Let us recall the definition of the Schr
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
dinger algebra
\mathcal{S}_{n}
in
(n+1)
-dimensional space-time following
[
10
]
. The Schr
\ddot{\mathrm{o}}
dinger algebra
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is a finite-dimensional, non-semisimple and perfect complex Lie algbera, and it is the semidirect product Lie algebra

\mathcal{S}_{n}=(\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C}))\ltimes H_{n},

where
H_{n}=\mathbb{C}^{2n}\oplus\mathbb{C}z
is the Heisenberg Lie algbera. The element
z
is central in
\mathcal{S}_{n}
. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathfrak{sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
. Then
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is a semisimple subalgebra of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
and
Z_{\mathcal{S}_{n}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})=\mathbb{C}z
is the center of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
. It is obviously that
\mathcal{S}_{n}
is perfect. By Theorem
4.5
, we obtain

Theorem 4.9
.

All the symmetric biderivations of
\mathcal{S}_{n}
are trival.

4.6.
Lie superalgebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
be a (complex) Lie superalgebra. A homogeneous bilinear map
\delta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{g}
is said to be a super-biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
if it satisfies

\displaystyle\delta([x,y],z)=(-1)^{|\delta||x|}[x,\delta(y,z)]-(-1)^{|y|(|\delta|+|x|)}[y,\delta(x,z)],

(4.3)

\displaystyle\delta(x,[y,z])=(-1)^{(|\delta|+|x|)|y|}[y,\delta(x,z)]-(-1)^{|z|(|\delta|+|x|+|y|)}[z,\delta(x,y)]

(4.4)

for all homogeneous elements
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}
. See
[
6
,
19
]
for details.
Note that
\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\times\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}}
is a biderivation from
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
to
\mathfrak{g}
(viewed as a
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
-module via the adjoint action).

A super-biderivation
\delta
is said to be super-symmetric, if

\delta(x,y)=(-1)^{|x||y|}\delta(y,x),~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

We have the following theorem.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
be a perfect finite-dimensional complex Lie superalgebra with
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
reductive. Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}]
. Assume that the dimension of

Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})=\{v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}\mid[x,v]=0~\text{for all}~x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\}

is at most one.
Then all the super-symmetric biderivations of
\mathfrak{g}
are trival.

Proof.

Note that
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
is semisimple. Let
\Phi
be the root system of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\Phi^{+}
the set of positive roots. Both
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
can be viewed as
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-modules via the adjoint action.
Assume that
\delta
is a super-symmetric biderivation of
\mathfrak{g}
. By Theorem
3.2
, we have

\delta(x,y)=0,~\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}.

(4.5)

Since
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}]
, by Proposition
4.2
, one has

\delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},c)=\delta([\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}],c)=0

(4.6)

for any central element
c
of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
.

Let
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. For any
y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
, one has

\delta(x,[y,v])=(-1)^{(|\delta|+|x|)|y|}[y,\delta(x,v)]-(-1)^{|v|(|\delta|+|x|+|y|)}[v,\delta(x,y)]=[y,\delta(x,v)].

It implies that
\delta(x,\cdot)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}
is a
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. Let
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
. Then for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, one has

\delta([x,y],v)=(-1)^{|\delta||x|}[x,\delta(y,v)]-(-1)^{|y|(|\delta|+|x|)}[y,\delta(x,v)]=[x,\delta(y,v)]-[y,\delta(x,v)].

It implies that
\delta(\cdot,v)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}}
is a derivation from
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
to
\mathfrak{g}
. By Proposition
4.3
,
\delta|_{\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}=0
, i.e.,

\delta(x,v)=0,~\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}.

(4.7)

Let
v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
. For any
x\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
w\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
, we have

\displaystyle\delta([x,w],v)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|\delta||x|}[x,\delta(w,v)]-(-1)^{|v|(|\delta|+|x|)}[w,\delta(x,v)]=[x,\delta(w,v)],

\displaystyle\delta(v,[x,w])

\displaystyle=(-1)^{|v||[x,w]|}\delta([x,w],v)=-[x,\delta(w,v)]=-[x,(-1)^{|w||v|}\delta(v,w)]

\displaystyle=[x,\delta(v,w)].

Hence,
\delta(\cdot,v)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}
and
\delta(v,\cdot)|_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}
are both
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-module homomorphism from
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
to
\mathfrak{g}
.

By Weyl’s theorem,
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}
can be written as a direct sum of irreducible
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
-submodules, say

\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{m}.

We claim that there exist at most one
i
such that
\dim V_{i}=1
. Otherwise, there exist
i\neq j
such that

\mathfrak{g}_{0}V_{i}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}V_{j}=0
. Then

V_{i}\oplus V_{j}\subseteq Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0}),

which forces
\dim Z_{\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}}(\mathfrak{g}_{0})\geq 2
, a contradiction. By Proposition
4.3
,

\delta(V_{i},V_{j})=0

if
\dim V_{i}>1
or
\dim V_{j}>1
.
Assume that
v,w\in V_{i}
with
\dim V_{i}=1
. Then
v=aw
for some
a\in\mathbb{C}
. Since

\delta(w,w)=(-1)^{|w||w|}\delta(w,w)=-\delta(w,w),

we have
\delta(w,w)=0.

Hence,

\delta(v,w)=a\delta(w,w)=0,

We obtain that

\delta(v,w)=0,~\forall w,v\in\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}.

Let
c
be a central element of
\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{0}}
. Then
c\in[\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}},\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}}]
. Hence, we must have

\delta(c,\mathfrak{g}_{\overline{1}})=0.

(4.8)

It also implies that

\delta(c_{1},c_{2})=0

(4.9)

for any central elements
c_{1},c_{2}
. We complete the proof.
∎

Since any classical Lie superalgebra satisfies the condition of above theorem. We can obtain the following corollary,
which has been obtained by concrete, but a little complicated, computations in
[
19
]
.

Corollary 4.11
.

All the super-symmetric biderivations of a classical simple Lie superalgebra are trival.

Dynamics of elastic wires: Preserving Area Without Nonlocality
Leonie Langer

Abstract
. We derive an H − 1 H^{-1} -gradient flow of the elastic energy which preserves the enclosed area of evolving planar curves. For this new sixth-order evolution equation, we prove a global existence result. Additionally, by penalizing the length, we show convergence to an area constrained critical point of the elastic energy.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification

53E40 (primary), 35K30, 35B40 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

We model a closed planar elastic wire by a sufficiently smooth regular curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
The elastic energy of the wire is then given by

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{1}}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\kappa=\partial_{s}^{2}\gamma
(with
\partial_{s}=|\partial_{x}\gamma|^{-1}\partial_{x}
) is the curvature vector and
\,\mathrm{d}s=|\partial_{x}\gamma|\,\mathrm{d}x
denotes the arclength measure of
\gamma
.
The study of the elastic energy (
1.1
) dates back to the 17th and 18th centuries. At that time, Jacob Bernoulli, his nephew Daniel, and Euler derived it to determine the shape of an elastic beam bent by an attached weight, see for example
[
30
]
. Even today, the elastic energy finds applications across various fields, from engineering and physics to biomechanics and materials science. It appears as the
\Gamma
-limit of an energy functional for lipid bilayer membranes (see
[
43
]
) and of discrete bending energies for atomic chains (see
[
1
]
,
[
18
]
).

If the elastic wire is extensible, the elastic energy can be made arbitrarily small by enlarging the wire.
In many situations, this is unphysical and thus also a penalization of length is taken into account.
We consider

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)=\mathcal{E}(\gamma)+\lambda\mathcal{L}(\gamma)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{1}}}\left(|\kappa|^{2}+2\lambda\right)\,\mathrm{d}s

for some
\lambda\geq 0
. When
\lambda>0
, the term
\lambda\mathcal{L}
measures the energy expended on stretching the extensible wire.

Energetically favoured states of the elastic wire are critical points of (
1.2
).
These critical points are called elasticae and satisfy the Euler–Lagrange equation

(1.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa=0.

Here,
\nabla_{s}=P^{\perp}\partial_{s}
, where
P^{\perp}
is the normal projection along
\gamma
defined by
P^{\perp}\phi=\phi-\langle\phi,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\partial_{s}\gamma
for vector fields
\phi:{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
. For
\lambda>0
, solutions to (
1.3
) have been classified in several works, see for instance
[
29
]
,
[
15
]
, or
[
40
, Lemma 5.4]
. In particular, the only closed elasticae are multifold coverings of the circle and of the figure eight elastica. For
\lambda=0
, no critical points of (
1.2
) exist, see e.g.
[
39
, Section 2.2]
.

The most straightforward dynamic approach to study (
1.2
), is to consider its
L^{2}
-gradient flow given by

(1.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma=-\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa-\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa+\lambda\kappa.

Here,
\partial_{t}^{\perp}=P^{\perp}\partial_{t}
. To account for the parametrization invariance of (
1.2
), it is a common approach to only prescribe the normal component of velocity.

In the last decades, several authors have studied (
1.4
)
in different variants. First Polden (
[
44
]
) and then Dziuk, Kuwert, and Schätzle (
[
16
]
) considered the case of closed curves. Later, the problem was extended to non-closed curves (see for example
[
41
]
,

[
13
]
,
[
31
]
) and to networks (see for example
[
10
]
,

[
22
]
). In
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, curves can be described by an inclination angle function, which yields not a fourth but a second order flow equation. This flow has been first considered by Wen (
[
52
]
) and later by
[
33
]
,
[
32
]
. For a more detailed overview, we refer to the survey article
[
35
]
.

In several works, inextensibility of the wire is assumed, i.e. the length of the evolving curve is kept fixed (
[
27
]
,
[
9
]
,

[
16
]
,
[
46
]
).
In contrast, to the best of our knowledge, there is only one work (
[
42
]
) in which the elastic flow under the constraint of fixed enclosed
area is studied. The area preserving scenario is physically relevant, for instance, to describe an interface between an inner domain and an outer domain filled with two kinds of incompressible viscous fluids. This relevance is further indicated by the existence of several works exploring area preserving variants for other flows of curves, see for example
[
20
]
,
or the recent articles
[
49
]
,
[
26
]
. In
[
35
, Section 6]
, the authors explicitly point out the absence of studies of an area preserving elastic flow.

In the aforementioned article
[
42
]
, Okabe derives a system of equations with a nonlocal Lagrange multiplier that governs
the
L^{2}
-gradient of (
1.1
) under the constraints of inextensiblity and preserved enclosed area.
For this nonlocal system, existence of a unique solution and convergence is proven.
In some models, local evolution equations that do not depend on any action at a distance are preferred (see for example
[
19
]
).
This motivates to search for an evolution equation which decreases (
1.2
) and preserves the enclosed area without the need for nonlocal Lagrange multipliers.
Inspired by the Cahn–Hilliard equation, which can be seen as an
H^{-1}
-gradient flow preserving a fixed total mass without any nonlocal Lagrange multiplier, we consider a
H^{-1}
-gradient approach for (
1.2
). To do so, we first define appropriate Hilbert spaces that depend on the curve
\gamma
and, if the curve evolves, on time. The definition includes a zero-integral condition, which allows for a Poincaré-type inequality, as also found in the derivation of the Cahn-Hilliard equation (see e.g.
[
19
]
).
In these Hilbert spaces, we compute the gradient of (
1.2
) and take its negative as the flow velocity, see
Section

2
. This approach transforms the classical elastic flow (
1.4
) with fourth order velocity into the sixth order flow

(1.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma=\nabla_{s}^{2}\Big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\Big).

The gradient flow structure ensures that under the flow (
1.5
), the energy (
1.2
) decreases (see
Lemma

3.3
). Moreover, the signed enclosed area
\mathcal{A}
is preserved, i.e.

(1.6)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\gamma,\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s,

is constant (see
Lemma

3.5
).
Here,
\nu
denotes the normal vector given by the counter-clockwise rotation of
\partial_{s}\gamma
by
\pi/2
.
Hence, (
1.5
) is a flow for the elastic energy that preserves the enclosed area without nonlocality.
In this article we aim to understand this flow.

We investigate a smooth regular initial datum
\gamma_{0}\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with rotation index
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
and enclosed area
\mathcal{A}(\gamma_{0})=A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
evolving under the area preserving elastic flow (
1.5
). So we study the initial value problem

(1.7)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma=\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{cases}

Throughout the entire article, the enclosed area
A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
of the initial datum is considered arbitrary, including the case where
A_{0}=0
. We also consider arbitrary rotation index
\omega_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
, in particular allowing the case
\omega_{0}=0
.

Even though our approach increases the order of the classical elastic flow by two,
the behavior of solutions does not deteriorate.
We show long time existence of solutions and, if we penalize the length,
convergence. By prescribing only the normal part of the velocity, we achieve uniqueness of the solution up to reparametrization. We refer to this as geometric uniqueness.

Theorem 1.1

(Global existence and convergence)
.

For any
\lambda\geq 0
, there exists a geometrically unique global smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of (
1.7
).
If
\lambda>0
, there exists a family of smooth diffeomorphisms
\Phi(t)\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
,
t\in(0,\infty)
, such that
\gamma(t,\Phi(t,\cdot))
converges smoothly for
t\to\infty
to a stationary solution
\gamma_{\infty}
.

By a stationary solution, we mean a smooth regular curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with rotation index
\omega_{0}
and
\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=A_{0}
such that

\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)=0.

Since
\gamma
describes a closed curve, this is equivalent to

(1.8)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)=0.

This equation is the classical elasticae equation (
1.3
) with a possibly nonzero constant multiple of the normal vector on the right hand side.
The presence of this nonzero term implies that there are stationary solutions which do not correspond to a multiple covering of a circle or of the figure eight elastica.
The existence of non-elastica solutions of (
1.8
) was proven by deriving an explicit parametrization using Jacobi elliptic functions, see for example
[
48
]
.
By showing convergence of the flow, we provide an
alternative proof for the existence of solutions to (
1.8
) that do not satisfy (
1.3
), meaning they are not elasticae.

Theorem

1.1
implies in particular that the solution remains within a compact set for all times if
\lambda>0
. Since no maximum principle is available for higher-order flows like (
1.5
), which would allow us to conclude this property by comparison arguments, this is a remarkable result. The same holds true for the classical elastic flow.

For the convergence result in
Theorem

1.1
, the length penalization is necessary. Indeed, for
\lambda=0
, solutions do not converge in general. If
\lambda=0
and
A_{0}=0
, the length of the evolving curve always becomes unbounded for
t\to\infty
(see
Lemma

6.10
).

To prove the global in time existence in
Theorem

1.1
, we first establish suitable interpolation inequalities, which we then use to show that the solution remains bounded in finite time. For the proof of convergence, we use that stationary solutions are area constrained critical points of (
1.2
) and rely on a suitable version of a constrained Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, see
[
45
]
. The
H^{-1}
-gradient flow structure obstructs the usual procedure to show convergence (see for example
[
36
]
,
[
13
]
or
[
46
]
). Indeed, the time-dependent Hilbert spaces in which the energy decreases do not coincide as sets, unlike for the
L^{2}
-gradient flow.
By considering suitably defined time-independent norms, we overcome this difficulty.

We point out that in
[
2
]
, an evolution equation similar to (
1.5
) is studied. The authors consider the
L^{2}
-gradient flow of the Dirichlet energy of the scalar curvature (without penalization of length), which also yields a sixth order equation. However, this equation does not preserve the enclosed area in general and shows a completely different asymptotic behavior.
In
[
2
]
, the authors classify the stationary solutions as
\omega
-fold covered circles. In particular, this shows that the solution for an initial datum with
\omega=0
cannot converge. Convergence to an
\omega
-fold covered circle is shown under the assumption that the length of the evolving curve remains bounded, which is the case for small initial energy. The length preserving and area preserving variants of the flow discussed in
[
2
]
(with nonlocal Lagrange multipliers), are studied in
[
37
]
and
[
54
]
.

This article is structured as follows. In
Section

2
we define an appropriate Hilbert space setting in which the area preserving elastic flow is derived as a gradient flow.
Section

3
lists some basic properties of the evolution equation; in particular, we see that the flow (
1.5
) indeed preserves the enclosed area.
Section

4
briefly addresses the question of short time existence, before we prove global in time existence and subconvergence (for
\lambda>0
) in
Section

5
.
Section

6
is devoted to prove full convergence using a suitable Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, which we first derive.

2.
Derivation of the flow equation

2.1.
An appropriate Hilbert space setting

For a given smooth regular curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we define the vector space

(2.1)

\displaystyle H_{\gamma}:=\Big\{Y\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}:Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma},\;Y\in L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}),\;\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\in L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}),\;\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=0\Big\}.

Here,

\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}
denotes the arclength measure of
\gamma
,
\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}
is the normal projection of the arclength derivative
\partial_{s_{\gamma}}
along
\gamma
and
\nu_{\gamma}
is the normal vector of
\gamma
.
On
H_{\gamma}
, we consider the inner product

(2.2)

\displaystyle\langle Y_{1},Y_{2}\rangle_{H_{\gamma}}=\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y_{1},\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}

which induces the norm

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}=\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}.

This is justified by the following lemma, which also shows that on
H_{\gamma}
,
\|\cdot\|_{H_{\gamma}}
is equivalent to the Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}}=\|\cdot\|_{W^{1,2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
, depending on

\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
,
\|\kappa_{\gamma}\|_{C^{0}}
,
\|\partial_{x}\gamma\|_{C^{0}}
,
and
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{x}\gamma|
,
such that

(2.4)

\displaystyle c_{1}\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}\leq c_{2}\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}\quad\text{ for }Y\in H_{\gamma}.

In particular,
(H_{\gamma},\|\cdot\|_{H_{\gamma}})
is a Hilbert space.

Proof.

Let
y:=\langle\nu_{\gamma},Y\rangle
. Then
\partial_{s_{\gamma}}y\in L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})
and

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}y\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=0.

Thus, with Poincaré’s inequality, we obtain

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}y^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\leq C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(\partial_{s_{\gamma}}y)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=C\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=C\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}

for a constant
C
depending on
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
.
Moreover,
Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma}
implies that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y=\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y-\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\kappa_{\gamma},\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma.

Here, we use
\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\nu_{\gamma}=-\langle\kappa_{\gamma},\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma
.
Equation (
2.6
) yields together with (
2.5
) that

(2.7)

\displaystyle C_{1}\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}\leq\|Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}+\|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}\leq C_{2}\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}

for constants

C_{1}>0
depending on the inverse of
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}
and
C_{2}>0
depending on
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
and
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}
.
From
\partial_{{s_{\gamma}}}Y=|\partial_{x}\gamma|^{-1}\partial_{x}Y
and
\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=|\partial_{x}\gamma|\,\mathrm{d}x
, we conclude

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|Y\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}}=\|Y\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{x}Y\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|Y\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}+\|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\big)

for some
C
depending on
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{x}\gamma|
and
\max_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{x}\gamma|
. From this, the left inequality of (
2.4
) follows with (
2.5
) and (
2.7
). The right inequality follows similarly leading to the equivalence of the norms.
Moreover,
H_{\gamma}
is a closed subspace of
W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Thus,
(H_{\gamma},\|\cdot\|_{H_{\gamma}})
is a Hilbert space.
∎

The Hilbert space
H_{\gamma}
is geometric in the following sense.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma\circ\varphi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth reparametrization of
\gamma
. If
Y\in H_{\gamma}
, then
Y\circ\varphi\in H_{\tilde{\gamma}}
and
\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}=\|Y\circ\varphi\|_{H_{\tilde{\gamma}}}
. For
Y_{1},Y_{2}\in H_{\gamma}
, we have
\langle Y_{1}\circ\varphi,Y_{2}\circ\varphi\rangle_{H_{\tilde{\gamma}}}=\langle Y_{1},Y_{2}\rangle_{H_{\gamma}}
.

Proof.

Let
Y\in H_{\gamma}
. If the smooth diffeomorphism
\varphi
of the reparametrization is orientation preserving, then
\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\nu_{\gamma}\circ\varphi
. If
\varphi
changes the orientation of
\gamma
, then
\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}=-\nu_{\gamma}\circ\varphi
.
In both cases, we obtain

(2.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{Y}:=Y\circ\varphi=\langle Y\circ\varphi,\nu_{\gamma}\circ\varphi\rangle\,\nu_{\gamma}\circ\varphi=\langle\tilde{Y},\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle\,\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}.

Moreover, substitution and
|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}(x)|=|\varphi^{\prime}(x)\,\partial_{x}\gamma(\varphi(x))|
yields

(2.10)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{Y}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\tilde{\gamma}})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y(\varphi(x))|^{2}|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}<\infty

as well as

(2.11)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{s_{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tilde{Y}\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\tilde{\gamma}})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(\langle\partial_{s_{\tilde{\gamma}}}\tilde{Y},\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}}\rangle)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{1}{|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}(x)|}\big(\langle\partial_{x}(Y(\varphi(x))),\nu_{\gamma}(\varphi(x))\rangle\big)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(2.12)

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\frac{1}{|\partial_{x}\gamma(z)|}(\langle\partial_{x}Y(z),\nu_{\gamma}(z)\rangle)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}z=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}<\infty.

Along the same lines, we obtain

(2.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\tilde{\gamma}},\tilde{Y}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\tilde{\gamma}}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma}(\varphi(x)),Y(\varphi(x))\rangle|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

since
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=0
.
Everything together results in
\tilde{Y}\in H_{\tilde{\gamma}}
. Moreover, with (
2.3
), (
2.11
) yields
\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}=\|\tilde{Y}\|_{H_{\tilde{\gamma}}}
.
The rest of the statement follows similarly.
∎

In the following, we denote by
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
the dual space of
H_{\gamma}
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\phi\in H_{\gamma}^{-1}
. We call
X\in H_{\gamma}
a
weak solution
of

(2.14)

\displaystyle-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X=\phi

if
\phi(Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle_{H_{\gamma}}=\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X,\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}
for all
Y\in H_{\gamma}
.

Note that by the Riesz representation theorem, for all
\phi\in H_{\gamma}^{-1}
, there exists a unique weak solution
of (
2.14
).

We define an inner product on
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
by

(2.15)

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{1},\phi_{2}\rangle_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}:=\langle X_{1},X_{2}\rangle_{H_{\gamma}}=\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X_{1},\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})},

where
X_{i}\in H_{\gamma}
is the unique weak solution of
-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X_{i}=\phi_{i}
,
i=1,2
. Moreover, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\|X\|_{H_{\gamma}}=\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}.

Remark 2.4
.

If the weak solution
X
of (
2.14
) is sufficiently smooth, precisely
\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X\in L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})
, then
\phi\in H_{\gamma}^{-1}
is represented by
\phi=\langle-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}
. We identify the functional
\phi
with the function
-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X
using this
L^{2}
-pairing. With this identification,
H_{\gamma}\subseteq L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})\subseteq H^{-1}_{\gamma}
. Moreover, this identification allows us to express the
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
-norm as

(2.17)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}=\sup_{Y\in H_{\gamma}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H_{\gamma}
. Then
\phi
is interpreted as an element of
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
as
H_{\gamma}\ni Y\mapsto\langle\phi,Y\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}
. In this case, the weak solution
X
of (
2.14
) is smooth (and coincides with the strong solution). Indeed, with (
2.6
), for all
Y\in W^{1,2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)
, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle C(\gamma)\left|\langle\partial_{x}X,\partial_{x}Y\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}\right|\leq\left|\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}X,\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\right|

(2.19)

\displaystyle\quad\leq\left|\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X,\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}Y\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|\langle X,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma,\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\right|

(2.20)

\displaystyle\quad\leq|\phi(Y)|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}(\partial_{s_{\gamma}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|)\langle X,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}X,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\right|

(2.21)

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|\langle X,\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\right|+\left|\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|\langle X,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\langle\kappa_{\gamma},Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\right|

(2.22)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\|Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\leq C\|Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)},

for some
C=C(\gamma,\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})},\|X\|_{H_{\gamma}})
, that changes along the lines. It follows that
X\in W^{2,2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)\subset C^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. With similar arguments we achieve
X\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

On
H_{\gamma}
, we have the following interpolation inequality.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H_{\gamma}
. Then

(2.23)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2}\leq\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

Let
X\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H_{\gamma}
be the solution of
-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X=\phi
. With (
2.16
), we have

(2.24)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X,\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X,\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

(2.25)

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\|\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}=\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}}.\qed

Moreover, on
H_{\gamma}
, the
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
-norm is equivalent to another norm, which we will use in
Section

6
as an auxiliary norm.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H_{\gamma}
. Define

(2.26)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\star}:=\sup_{Y\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x.

Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending on
\|\kappa_{\gamma}\|_{C^{0}}
,
\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
as well as
\min_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{x}\gamma|
and
\|\partial_{x}\gamma\|_{C^{0}}
such that

(2.27)

\displaystyle c_{1}\|\phi\|_{\star}\leq\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\leq c_{2}\|\phi\|_{\star}.

Proof.

For the proof, we consider

(2.28)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\textup{aux}}:=\sup_{Y\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

and show the equivalence of
\|\cdot\|_{\textup{aux}}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}
on
C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap H_{\gamma}
. Estimating the arclength element
|\partial_{x}\gamma|
from below and from above, this equivalence yields the claim.

For
Y\in H_{\gamma}\subset W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
,
Lemma

2.1
yields the existence of a constant
c_{2}
depending on
\gamma
such that

(2.29)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{Y\in H_{\gamma}\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{H_{\gamma}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\leq c_{2}\sup_{Y\in H_{\gamma}\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\leq c_{2}\|\phi\|_{\textup{aux}}.

For the other inequality, we first note that if
\phi\in H_{\gamma}
, then
\phi
is orthogonal to
\partial_{s}\gamma
and thus
\langle\phi,Y\rangle=\langle\phi,Y^{\perp}\rangle
, where
Y^{\perp}:=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma}
for any
Y\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Moreover,
|Y^{\perp}|^{2}\leq|Y|^{2}
and

(2.30)

\displaystyle|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}(Y^{\perp})|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y-\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\partial_{s}\gamma-\langle Y,\kappa_{\gamma}\rangle\partial_{s}\gamma-\langle Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}

(2.31)

\displaystyle=|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y|^{2}-2(\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle)^{2}-2\langle Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\kappa_{\gamma}\rangle+(\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle)^{2}

(2.32)

\displaystyle\quad+(\langle Y,\kappa_{\gamma}\rangle)^{2}+(\langle Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle)^{2}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}

(2.33)

\displaystyle=|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y|^{2}-(\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle)^{2}-2\langle Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\kappa_{\gamma}\rangle+|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}|Y|^{2}

(2.34)

\displaystyle\leq C(|Y|^{2}+|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y|^{2})

for a constant
C
depending on bounds on
\kappa_{\gamma}
. Thus,
\|Y^{\perp}\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}
for a constant
C
depending on bounds on
\kappa_{\gamma}
and the arclength element
|\partial_{x}\gamma|
. As
\langle\phi,Y\rangle=\langle\phi,Y^{\perp}\rangle
, we conclude that

(2.35)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\textup{aux}}=\sup_{Y\in W^{1,2}\setminus\{0\}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\leq C\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in W^{1,2}\setminus\{0\},\\
Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma}\end{subarray}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

with
C
depending on the same quantities as above.
Now we note that
\phi\in H_{\gamma}
also implies that

(2.36)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y-\frac{\nu_{\gamma}}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=:\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y-C_{\gamma}(Y)\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

for
Y\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, for
Y\in W^{1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\{Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma}\}
, we have

(2.37)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y-C_{\gamma}(Y)\nu_{\gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}-\mathcal{L}(\gamma)C_{\gamma}(Y)^{2}\leq\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|Y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

and

(2.38)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}(Y-C_{\gamma}(Y)\nu_{\gamma})|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}+2C_{\gamma}(Y)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\langle\kappa_{\gamma},\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

(2.39)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\gamma}(Y)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}.

Since

(2.40)

\displaystyle C_{\gamma}(Y)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{2}}\|Y\|^{2}_{L^{1}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}\leq\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\|Y\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}^{2},

(
2.37
) and (
2.39
) imply that

\|Y-C_{\gamma}(Y)\nu_{\gamma}\|_{W^{1,2}}\leq C\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}

for a constant
C
depending on bounds on
\kappa_{\gamma}
and
|\partial_{x}\gamma|
and the energy
\mathcal{E}(\gamma)
. We conclude from (
2.35
) that

(2.41)

\displaystyle\|\phi\|_{\textup{aux}}\leq C\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in W^{1,2}\setminus\{0\},\\
Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma}\end{subarray}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\leq C\!\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}Y\in W^{1,2}\setminus\{0\},Y=\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\nu_{\gamma},\\
\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle Y,\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=0\end{subarray}}\;\frac{1}{\|Y\|_{W^{1,2}}}\!\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma},

with
C
depending on the quantities listed above. By the definition of
H_{\gamma}
and
Lemma

2.1
, this is

\|\phi\|_{\textup{aux}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{-1}_{\gamma}}.

∎

2.2.
The area preserving elastic flow as a gradient flow

Flow equation (
1.5
) can be derived as the
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
-gradient flow. For this, we consider a smooth regular curve
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
and a smooth normal variation
\phi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}

such that
\phi\in H_{\gamma}\subseteq H_{\gamma}^{-1}
. For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, the perturbed curve
\gamma_{\varepsilon}:=\gamma+\varepsilon\phi
is also regular.
With
\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)=\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}\kappa_{\gamma}+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}\kappa_{\gamma}-\lambda\kappa_{\gamma}
, we have

(2.42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}^{\prime}(\gamma)(\phi)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}.

In general,
\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\not\in H_{\gamma}
. So we define

(2.43)

\displaystyle W:=\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-C_{\gamma}\nu_{\gamma}\in H_{\gamma}\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\quad\text{with}\quad C_{\gamma}:=\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}.

With
X\in H_{\gamma}\cap C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
as the weak solution of
-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X=\phi
and
w=-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}W
, we obtain

(2.44)

\displaystyle\langle w,\phi\rangle_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}W,\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}X\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle W,-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

(2.45)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\phi\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}.

Here, we used in the third step that

(2.46)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle C_{\gamma}\nu_{\gamma},\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}=0,

since
\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}X=-\phi\in H_{\gamma}
.
Comparing (
2.42
) and (
2.45
), we define the
H_{\gamma}^{-1}
-gradient

(2.47)

\displaystyle\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma):=w=-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}\big(\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-C_{\gamma}\nu_{\gamma}\big)=-\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}\kappa_{\gamma}+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}\kappa_{\gamma}-\lambda\kappa_{\gamma}\big).

To properly adress the geometric nature of the problem, we set only the normal component of velocity equal to the negative gradient, yielding

(2.48)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma=-\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),

which is (
1.5
). Note that, in general, the space with respect to which the gradient is computed changes in time.

3.
Basic properties of the flow

In order to incorporate the invariance of the elastic energy under reparametrizations, we defined (
1.7
) in such a way that a reparametrization of a solution again solves (
1.7
) with reparametrized initial data. More precisely, we have the following.

Remark 3.1

(Invariance under reparametrization)
.

Let
0<T<\infty
and
\gamma\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth solution to (
1.7
). Then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\gamma=-\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)+h\partial_{s}\gamma

for some smooth
h\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\Phi\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}

be a smooth one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms. Then the reparametrization
\tilde{\gamma}(t,x):=\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x))
solves

(3.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x))+\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)\partial_{x}\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x))

(3.3)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x)))+h(t,\Phi(t,x))\partial_{s}\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x))+\partial_{t}\Phi(t,x)\partial_{x}\gamma(t,\Phi(t,x))

(3.4)

\displaystyle=-\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma})+\tilde{h}\partial_{s}\tilde{\gamma}

for some smooth
\tilde{h}\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus,
\partial_{t}^{\perp}\tilde{\gamma}=-\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\tilde{\gamma}(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}(\Phi(0,\cdot))
.

In what follows, we will frequently use the following lemma which gives the time derivative of various geometric quantities related to a curve
\gamma
moving under a general flow.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
T>0
, be a sufficiently smooth solution of the flow
\partial_{t}\gamma=V+h\partial_{s}\gamma
, where
V
is the normal velocity and
h=\langle\partial_{t}\gamma,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle
. Then

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\!\,\mathrm{d}s)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{s}h-\langle\kappa,V\rangle)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(3.6)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{s}-\partial_{s}\partial_{t}

\displaystyle=(\langle\kappa,V\rangle-\partial_{s}h)\partial_{s},

(3.7)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\partial_{s}\gamma

\displaystyle=\nabla_{s}V+h\kappa,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nu

\displaystyle=(-\langle\nabla_{s}V,\nu\rangle-h\langle\kappa,\nu\rangle)\partial_{s}\gamma,

(3.9)

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\kappa

\displaystyle=\nabla_{s}^{2}V+\langle\kappa,V\rangle\kappa+h\nabla_{s}\kappa,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\phi-\nabla_{s}\nabla_{t}\phi

\displaystyle=(\langle\kappa,V\rangle-\partial_{s}h)\nabla_{s}\phi+\langle\kappa,\phi\rangle\nabla_{s}V-\langle\nabla_{s}V,\phi\rangle\kappa,

for a sufficiently smooth normal field
\phi\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

Here,
\nabla_{t}=\partial_{t}^{\perp}=P^{\perp}\partial_{t}
. For a proof of (
3.5
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), see for example
[
16
, Lemma 2.1]
. Formula (
3.8
) follows from (
3.7
).

Clearly, the flow of
Lemma

3.2
does not change the rotation index
\omega
given by

(3.11)

\displaystyle 2\pi\omega=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}k\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\kappa,\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

Next, we note the basic but crucial fact that the flow (
1.5
) decreases the energy (
1.2
). This follows by a direct computation using (
3.5
), (
3.9
) and integration by parts. All the terms containing a tangential velocity cancel.

Lemma 3.3
.

For a sufficently smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
T>0
, to (
1.5
), we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)=-\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{1}}}|\nabla_{s}\left(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\right)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq 0.

Remark 3.4
.

Clearly, we also observe the energy decrease in the abstract setting of
Section

2
, where we have

(3.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\langle\nabla_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\partial_{t}\gamma\rangle_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}=-\big\|\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma\big\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}^{2}=-\big\|\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\big\|_{H_{\gamma}^{-1}}^{2}

(3.14)

\displaystyle=-\big\|\nabla_{s}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\big\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}^{2},

which is (
3.12
).

At the same time, the flow (
1.5
) preserves the signed enclosed area (
1.6
).

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a sufficently smooth solution to (
1.5
). Then

(3.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=0.

Proof.

Using (
3.8
) and integration by parts
yields

(3.16)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\partial_{t}\gamma,\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

So, as it should be, only the normal part of the velocity plays a role.
With integration by parts and since
\partial_{s}\nu=-k\,\partial_{s}\gamma
is tangential,
we immediately obtain

(3.17)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{A}(\gamma)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s}\left(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\right),\partial_{s}\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=0.

A more advanced property to study is the preservation of embeddedness. For a sixth-order equation, embeddedness is generally not preserved, see
[
5
]
. However, by an energy argument developed in
[
38
]
, the energy decay (

) suffices to provide an explicit bound on the initial energy which ensures preservation of embeddedness. Note that according to Hopf’s Umlaufsatz, this only makes sense for
\omega=\pm 1
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\omega=1
,
\lambda>0
and
C^{\ast}\approx 146.628
be as in
[
38
, Theorem 1.1]
. Let
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a solution to (

) with embedded initial datum
\gamma_{0}
such that

(3.18)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}))^{2}\leq 4\lambda C^{\ast}.

Then
\gamma
is embedded for all

.

Proof.

If
\gamma_{0}
is stationary, the claim is trivial. So with (

) we can assume that

for all

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\gamma(t))\mathcal{L}(\gamma(t))\leq\frac{1}{4\lambda}(\mathcal{E}(\gamma(t))+\lambda\mathcal{L}(\gamma(t)))^{2}<\frac{1}{4\lambda}(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}))^{2}\leq C^{\ast}

we conclude that

describes an embedded curve for all

∎

A short computation shows that if

such that a curve describing a circle with enclosed area

4.
Short time existence

In this section we show how to prove short time existence. Addressing this question is additionally motivated by the fact that we will use several of the arguments again in the proof of convergence in
Section

6
. Our method of proof works not only for a smooth initial datum, but also for
\gamma_{0}\in C^{7,\alpha}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
\alpha>0
. This could be improved further, but since we are mainly interested in questions about the long time behavior, we do not focus on this.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\gamma_{0}\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth regular curve. Then there exists some
T>0
and a smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
to (
1.7
). This solution is unique up to reparametrizations.

Proof.

The idea is to use the reparametrization argument in
Section

A.1
and to
find the solution as a graph over the initial datum. This approach leads to a scalar problem, which we can uniquely solve using
Proposition

A.4
.

Step 1: Good neighborhood.

By
Lemma

A.1
, there exists a
W^{5,2}
-neighborhood
V
of the smooth immersion
\gamma_{0}
, such that for all
\zeta\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap V
, there exists a diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
and a reparametrization
\tilde{\zeta}=\zeta\circ\Phi

such that
\tilde{\zeta}=\gamma_{0}+\varphi\nu_{0}
for some

\varphi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
.
By
Remark

A.2
, the smoothness of
\zeta
and the smoothness of
\gamma_{0}
yield
\Phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};{\mathbb{S}^{1}})
and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
.

Step 2: Velocity in terms of
\gamma
.

Let
\bar{\gamma}\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a sufficiently smooth immersed curve. Continuing the explicit calculation from
[
46
, Proposition A.1]
, we obtain

(4.1)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\bar{\gamma})

\displaystyle=P^{\perp_{\bar{\gamma}}}\!\left(\frac{\partial_{x}^{6}\bar{\gamma}}{|\partial_{x}\bar{\gamma}|^{6}}+\tilde{Q}(\partial_{x}\bar{\gamma},\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\bar{\gamma},|\partial_{x}\bar{\gamma}|^{-1})\right)

for
\tilde{Q}
a vector valued polynomial.
If
\bar{\gamma}
is time-dependent and
\bar{\gamma}(t,x)=\gamma_{0}(x)+\bar{\varphi}(t,x)\nu_{0}(x)
, then

P^{\perp_{\bar{\gamma}}}\partial_{x}^{6}\bar{\gamma}
consists of the term
\partial_{x}^{6}\bar{\varphi}P^{\perp_{\bar{\gamma}}}\nu_{0}
and of terms containing derivatives of
\varphi
up to order 5.

As long as
\nu_{0}^{\perp}=P^{\perp_{\bar{\gamma}}}\nu_{0}
does not vanish, we obtain

(4.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\bar{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial_{x}^{6}\bar{\varphi}\,\nu_{0}^{\perp}}{|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{0}+\bar{\varphi}\nu_{0})|^{6}}+Q(\cdot,\bar{\varphi},\partial_{x}\bar{\varphi},\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\bar{\varphi},|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{0}+\bar{\varphi}\,\nu_{0})|^{-1})\nu_{0}^{\perp}

with a scalar valued polynomial
Q
. Here,

\gamma_{0}
and its derivatives are considered as fixed functions and are included in
Q
.

Step 3: Construction of a solution.
The previous steps motivate to consider the system

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\frac{\partial_{x}^{6}\varphi}{|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{0}+\varphi\nu_{0})|^{6}}+Q(\cdot,\varphi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\varphi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{0}+\varphi\nu_{0})|^{-1})\quad&\text{in }(0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\displaystyle\varphi(0,\cdot)=0&\text{on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{cases}

By
Proposition

A.4
, there exists
T>0
and a unique solution
\varphi\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
to (
4.3
). With this solution, we define
\gamma:=\gamma_{0}+\varphi\nu_{0}\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. By possibly making
T>0
smaller, we achieve
\gamma(t,\cdot)\in V
and
P^{\perp_{\gamma}}\nu_{0}=\nu_{0}^{\perp}\neq 0
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Moreover,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma=\partial_{t}\varphi\,\nu_{0}^{\perp}=\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)

by (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). Thus,
\gamma
solves (
1.7
).

Step 4: Geometric uniqueness.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}([0,\tilde{T}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be another solution to (
1.7
) in the sense that

(4.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{\perp}\tilde{\gamma}=\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma})\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{\gamma}(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}\circ\tilde{\Phi}

for a smooth orientation preserving diffeomorphism
\tilde{\Phi}\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
. For the sake of readability, we do not change the notation, but we reparametrize
\tilde{\gamma}
by
\tilde{\Phi}^{-1}
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}
. By
Remark

3.1
, the reparametrization also satisfies
\partial_{t}^{\perp}\tilde{\gamma}=\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma})
. Now, make
\tilde{T}
so small that
\tilde{\gamma}(t,\cdot)\in V
for all
t\in[0,\tilde{T}]
. Then there exists a reparametrization (which we again do not rename) such that
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma_{0}+\tilde{\varphi}\nu_{0}
for some
\tilde{\varphi}\in C^{\infty}([0,\tilde{T})\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
with
\tilde{\varphi}(0)=0
. Since
\partial_{t}^{\perp}\tilde{\gamma}=\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma})
and
\tilde{\varphi}(0)=0
, we see that
\tilde{\varphi}
solves (
4.3
). As the solution to (
4.3
) is unique, it follows that
\tilde{\varphi}=\varphi
and therefore
\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
. We conclude that every solution to (
1.7
) is a reparametrization of the solution
\gamma
constructed in Step 3.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

More generally, the solution can also be found as a graph over any arbitrary reference immersion
\hat{\gamma}\in W^{5,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, provided that it satisfies
\|\hat{\gamma}-\gamma_{0}\|_{W^{4,2}}<r
, where
r
is the constant of
Lemma

A.1
. The reference curve in (
4.2
) and the initial datum in (
4.3
) then change accordingly.

5.
Global existence and subconvergence

5.1.
Geometrical lemmata and interpolation inequalities

Using (
3.5
) and integration by parts directly yields the following lemma.

Lemma 5.1
.

For a sufficiently smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of a flow
\partial_{t}\gamma=V+h\partial_{s}\gamma
and a normal vector field
\phi:[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we have

(5.1)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{3}\phi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\phi,\nabla_{t}\phi-\nabla_{s}^{6}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\phi\,\partial_{s}h\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi|^{2}\langle\kappa,V\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

For the following, we introduce a quite common notation (see for example
[
16
]
or
[
12
]
). Let
\phi
and
\phi_{i}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, be normal vector fields. We denote by
\phi_{1}\ast\phi_{2}\ast\cdots\ast\phi_{k}

a term of the type

(5.2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\langle\phi_{i_{1}},\phi_{i_{2}}\rangle\cdots\langle\phi_{i_{k-1}},\phi_{i_{k}}\rangle,\quad&\text{ if }k\text{ is even,}\\
\langle\phi_{i_{1}},\phi_{i_{2}}\rangle\cdots\langle\phi_{i_{k-2}},\phi_{i_{k-1}}\rangle\phi_{i_{k}},\quad&\text{ if }k\text{ is odd,}\end{cases}

where
i_{1},\dots,i_{k}
is some permutation of
1,\dots,k
. By
P^{a}_{b}(\phi)
, we denote any linear combination of terms of the type

(5.3)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{j_{1}}\phi\ast\cdots\ast\nabla_{s}^{j_{b}}\phi\qquad\text{ with }j_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\text{ for all }k\in\{1,\dots,b\}\text{ and }j_{1}+\cdots+j_{b}=a

with coefficients bounded by some universal constant. Observe that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}P_{b}^{a}(\phi)=P^{a+1}_{b}(\phi).

Lemma 5.2
.

For a sufficiently smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of

\partial_{t}\gamma=\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)+h\partial_{s}\gamma=\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa+P^{2}_{3}(\kappa)-\lambda\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+h\partial_{s}\gamma,

we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa-\nabla_{s}^{m+6}\kappa=P^{m+4}_{3}(\kappa)+P^{m+2}_{5}(\kappa)+P_{3}^{m+2}(\kappa)-\lambda\nabla_{s}^{m+4}\kappa+h\nabla_{s}^{m+1}\kappa.

Proof.

The claim follows by induction on
m
. For
m=0
we use (
3.9
). The induction step follows with (
3.10
) and (
5.4
).
∎

For the convenience of the reader, we repeat in the following lemmata two results of
[
16
]
and
[
12
]
without giving the proof.

Lemma 5.3

(
[
16
, Proposition 2.5]
)
.

Let
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a sufficiently smooth curve. Then for
P^{a}_{b}(\kappa)
with
b\geq 2
containing only derivatives of
\kappa
of order at most
k-1
with
2k+1>a+\frac{1}{2}b
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|P^{a}_{b}(\kappa)|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{k}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{\mu}{2-\mu}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{b-\mu}{2-\mu}}+C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{a+b-1}

for any
\varepsilon>0
,
\mu=(a+\frac{1}{2}b-1)/k
, and
C=C(k,a,b)
.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
12
, Lemma 4.6]
)
.

Let
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a sufficiently smooth curve. Assume that
\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}\leq C_{m}
for some
m\geq 1
and
\|\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}\leq C_{0}
. Then

(5.7)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{l}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}\leq C\quad\text{ for }\,0\leq l\leq m

with a constant
C=C(C_{m},C_{0},m,l)
.

5.2.
Bounds on the length

To prove a longtime existence result we have to control the length of the evolving curve. A uniform bound from below is given by
Fenchel’s theorem which yields

(5.8)

\displaystyle 4\pi^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\|\kappa\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}\leq 2\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}).

Here, for the second estimate, we used
Lemma

3.3
. If
\lambda>0
, we also have a uniform bound on the length from above by

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}).

In order to allow for
\lambda=0
, we show that the length grows at most linearly.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
T>0
and
\gamma\colon[0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a smooth solution to (
1.7
). Then

(5.10)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(\gamma)\leq C(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0})).

Proof.

Using (
3.5
) and integration by parts and applying
Lemma

5.3
with
a=2
,
b=4
,
k=2
yields

(5.11)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{L}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\partial_{t}\gamma,\kappa\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{3}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s

(5.12)

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C(\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{5}

for all
t\in(0,T)
, where
\varepsilon>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small. The claim follows with
Lemma

3.3
.
∎

5.3.
Proof of global existence and subconvergence

With the preliminary work of
Section

5.1
and
5.2
we are now ready to prove global existence for
\lambda\geq 0
. If
\lambda>0
, we additionally obtain subconvergence. This result crucially relies on the fact that, in this case, the length of the evolving curve is uniformly bounded from above.

Theorem 5.6

(Global existence and subconvergence)
.

For any
\lambda\geq 0
,
there exists a geometrically unique global smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of (
1.7
).
If
\lambda>0
, then as
t_{i}\to\infty
the curves
\gamma(t_{i},\cdot)
subconverge smoothly, when reparametrized with constant speed and suitably translated, to some
stationary solution
\gamma_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Theorem

4.1
gives a smooth solution
\gamma
of (
1.7
) in a small time interval. We assume by contradiction that this solution does not exist globally in time and denote by
0<T<\infty
the maximal existence time. Let
h\in C^{\infty}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
be the scalar tangential velocity, i.e.
\partial_{t}\gamma=\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)+h\partial_{s}\gamma.

Step 1: Bound on
\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

With
Lemma

5.1
and
Lemma

5.2
it follows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa&|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa,P^{m+4}_{3}(\kappa)+P^{m+2}_{5}(\kappa)+P^{m+2}_{3}(\kappa)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+2}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}h\langle\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa,\nabla_{s}^{m+1}\kappa\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\partial_{s}h\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\langle\kappa,V\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s,\end{split}

where
V=\nabla_{s}^{2}\left(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\right)
.
The second integral is negative and can be ignored in the following estimates.
The two integrals containing the tangential velocity vanish after integration by parts.
The other terms on the right hand side of (
5.3
) have the structure

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}P^{2m+4}_{4}(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}P^{2m+2}_{6}(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}P^{2m+2}_{4}(\kappa)\,\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

With
Lemma

5.3
(for
a=2m+4
,
b=4
,
k=m+3
and for
a=2m+2
,
b=6
,
k=m+3
), we know that

(5.13)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|P^{2m+4}_{4}(\kappa)|\,\mathrm{d}s,\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|P^{2m+2}_{6}(\kappa)|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C(m,\varepsilon)\left(\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{1}}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2m+7}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Further,

Lemma

5.3
with
a=2m+2
,
b=4
,
k=m+3
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|P^{2m+2}_{4}(\kappa)|\,\mathrm{d}s\leq&\;\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C(m,\varepsilon)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{2}{3}m+3}\\
&+C(m)\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2m+5}\end{split}

for any
\varepsilon>0
. Lastly, we use
Lemma

5.3
with
a=2m
,
b=2
and
k=m+3
to see that

(5.14)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m+3}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{m}{3}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+C\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2m+1}.

Putting (
5.3
), (
5.13
), (
5.3
) and (
5.14
)
together, choosing
\varepsilon
small enough and using
Lemma

3.3
, we get

(5.15)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}),m).

It follows that
\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}(t)\leq\|\nabla_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}(0)+C(\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{0}),m)
.

Step 2: Bound on
\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{C^{0}}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

By Step 1,
Lemma

3.3
and
Lemma

5.4
we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m)

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. As in
[
7
, Theorem 2.2]
one shows that

(5.17)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial_{s}^{m+1}\kappa\|_{L^{1}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}+\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{-1}\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{1}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}

(5.18)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{s}^{m+1}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}+\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}.

The length is bounded in finite time by
Lemma

5.5
and (
5.8
). Thus, we conclude that

(5.19)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{m}\kappa\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m,T).

Note that this also implies that

(5.20)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{s}^{m}V\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m,T).

Step 3: Global existence.

Due to
Remark

3.1
, we can consider the reparametrization

\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
with constant speed, i.e. such that
|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}|=\mathcal{L}(\gamma)
. This ensures that
|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}|
is bounded away from zero and bounded from above in the finite time interval
[0,T]
, see (
5.8
) and
Lemma

5.5
. Moreover,

(5.21)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{m+2}\tilde{\gamma}=\mathcal{L}(\gamma)^{m+2}\partial_{s}^{m}\tilde{\kappa}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, which implies by Step 2 that

\|\partial_{x}^{m+2}\tilde{\gamma}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m,T)\quad\text{ for }m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}.

With the control of the normal velocity, we additionally obtain
\|\tilde{\gamma}\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},T).

These bounds allow for a smooth extension of
\tilde{\gamma}
to
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
. The short time existence result gives a smooth extension for
t>T
which contradicts the maximality of
T
. Thus, the maximal existence time must be infinite.

Step 4: Subconvergence for
\lambda>0
.

In the case of
\lambda>0
, the length is bounded independently of
T
(see (
5.9
)).
Thus, the bounds in (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) do not depend on
T
.
Again, we consider the reparametrization
\tilde{\gamma}
with constant speed such that

|\partial_{x}\tilde{\gamma}|
is controlled up to
T=\infty
(in this case independently of
T
).
Moreover, adding a suitable translation like

(5.22)

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}(t,\cdot)-p(t):=\tilde{\gamma}(t,\cdot)-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\tilde{\gamma})}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\tilde{\gamma}\,\mathrm{d}s_{\tilde{\gamma}},

controls
\|\tilde{\gamma}-p\|_{C^{0}}
up to
T=\infty
.
In total,
\|\partial_{x}^{m}(\tilde{\gamma}-p)\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m)
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t\in[0,\infty)
.
So, given any sequence
(t_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
,
t_{i}\to\infty
, there exists a subsequence
(t_{i_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\tilde{\gamma}(t_{i_{k}},\cdot)-p(t_{i_{k}})
converges smoothly to a limit curve
\gamma_{\infty}
.

Step 5: Limits of convergent subsequences.

We define
\tilde{V}:=\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa
and
u(t):=\|\nabla_{s}\tilde{V}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s)}
. With (
3.5
), we have

(5.23)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}u(t)=2\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\tilde{V},\nabla_{s}\tilde{V}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\nabla_{s}\tilde{V}|^{2}\big(\partial_{s}\tilde{h}-\langle\kappa,V\rangle\big)\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\tilde{h}=\langle\partial_{t}\tilde{\gamma},\partial_{s}\tilde{\gamma}\rangle
is the tangential velocity of
\tilde{\gamma}
. With a computation using (
3.9
), (
3.10
), and
Lemma

5.2
, we see that the term
\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\big(|\kappa|^{2}\kappa\big)
appearing in
\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\tilde{V}
can be written as

(5.24)

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\big(|\kappa|^{2}\kappa\big)=P(\kappa)+\tilde{h}\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(|\kappa|^{2}\kappa\big),

where
P(\kappa)
represents several polynomials of the form
P^{a}_{b}(\kappa)
,
b\in\mathbb{N}
odd and
5\geq a\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, we see with
Lemma

5.2
that

(5.25)

\displaystyle\nabla_{t}\nabla_{s}\tilde{V}=P(\kappa)+\tilde{h}\Big(\nabla_{s}^{4}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(|\kappa|^{2}\kappa\big)-\lambda\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa\Big)=P(\kappa)+\tilde{h}\nabla_{s}^{2}\tilde{V}.

Here,
P(\kappa)
represents now polynomials of the form
P^{a}_{b}(\kappa)
,
b\in\mathbb{N}
odd and
7\geq a\in\mathbb{N}
. With this and integration by parts, we see that all terms containing the tangential velocity
\tilde{h}
in (
5.23
) cancel. Thus, with the previous steps,
|\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}u(t)|
is bounded by a constant depending on
\gamma_{0}
for
t\in[0,\infty)
.
Since
u\in L^{1}((0,\infty))
due to
Lemma

3.3
, this is sufficient to deduce
\lim_{t\to\infty}u(t)=0
.
It follows that
\gamma_{\infty}

solves (
1.8
) and is therefore stationary.
∎

Remark 5.7

(Flow without tangential velocity)
.

Like in some other articles, for example
[
16
]
or
[
12
]
, one could also impose that the tangential velocity is zero and study

(5.26)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\gamma=V=\nabla_{s}^{2}\big(\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa\big)\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\gamma(0,\cdot)=\gamma_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{cases}

In this case, Step 3 in the proof of global existence is different, because one does not want to work with a reparametrization. This difficulty can be overcome with the following considerations. First, one proves bounds on
\|\partial_{x}^{m}\kappa\|_{C^{0}}

for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
:
By induction, it can be shown that for a sufficiently smooth function
h\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
or vector field
h\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
, for example
h=\kappa
or
h=V
, we have

(5.27)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{m}h=|\partial_{x}\gamma|^{m}\partial_{s}^{m}h+\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}F_{m-1}(|\partial_{x}\gamma|,\dots,\partial_{x}^{m-1}|\partial_{x}\gamma|)\partial_{s}^{j}h,

where
F_{m-1}
is a polynomial of degree at most
m-1
.
With Step 2, it therefore suffices to find a bound on
\|\partial_{x}^{m}|\partial_{x}\gamma|\|_{C^{0}}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. For this, we proceed by induction as in
[
12
, p. 649]
. For
m=0
, we note that
\partial_{t}|\partial_{x}\gamma|=-\langle\kappa,V\rangle|\partial_{x}\gamma|
by (
3.5
). Since
\|\langle\kappa,V\rangle\|_{C^{0}}
is bounded by (
5.19
) and (
5.20
) and
|\partial_{x}\gamma_{0}|
is bounded from below and above, we conclude that

(5.28)

\displaystyle C^{-1}<|\partial_{x}\gamma|<C\quad\text{ for some }C=C(\gamma_{0},T).

For the induction step from
m-1
to
m
one uses (
5.27
) for
h=\langle\kappa,V\rangle
to obtain bounds
\|\partial_{x}^{i}\langle\kappa,V\rangle\|_{C^{0}}\leq C(\gamma_{0},m,T)
for
0\leq i\leq m
.
Differentiating
\partial_{t}|\partial_{x}\gamma|=-\langle\kappa,V\rangle|\partial_{x}\gamma|
, we thus obtain

\partial_{t}\partial_{x}^{m}|\partial_{x}\gamma|\leq-\langle\kappa,V\rangle\partial_{x}^{m}|\partial_{x}\gamma|+C(\gamma_{0},m,T)

which yields the bound on
\|\partial_{x}^{m}|\partial_{x}\gamma|\|_{C^{0}}
, that we wanted to find. Note that the bounds on
\|\partial_{x}^{m}\kappa\|_{C^{0}}
also yield bounds on
\|\partial_{x}^{m}V\|_{C^{0}}
for
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Again, the uniform bounds on
\kappa
,
V
,
\gamma
and all their derivatives yield a smooth extension up to the maximal existence time and beyond.

6.
Convergence

6.1.
Stationary solutions as constrained critical points

Working with curves, that enclose a given area
A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, we introduce the constraint functional

(6.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}\colon W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{G}(\gamma)=\mathcal{A}(\gamma)-A_{0}=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}-A_{0}.

Here,
W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
denotes the space of all
W^{2,2}
-immersions.
A short computation shows that the Fréchet derivative is given by

(6.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\gamma)\colon W^{2,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\gamma)(Y)=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma},

which is a surjective mapping. Hence, the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}\colon W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\mathbb{R}
has a critical point subject to the constraint
\mathcal{G}=0
at
\gamma\in W^{2,2}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\{\mathcal{G}=0\}
if and only if there exits
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(6.3)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\gamma)(Y)+c_{1}\mathcal{G}^{\prime}(\gamma)(Y)

(6.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\Big(\langle\nabla_{s_{\gamma}}^{2}Y,\kappa_{\gamma}\rangle-\frac{1}{2}|\kappa_{\gamma}|^{2}\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma\rangle+\lambda\langle\partial_{s_{\gamma}}Y,\partial_{s_{\gamma}}\gamma\rangle\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}-c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nu_{\gamma},Y\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}

for all
Y\in W^{2,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. For
a computation of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\gamma)
, see for example
[
12
, Lemma A.1]
.
If
\gamma
is sufficiently regular, we obtain from (
6.3
) the Euler Lagrange equation

(6.5)

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa-c_{1}\nu=\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-c_{1}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma),

where
\kappa=\kappa_{\gamma}
and
\nu=\nu_{\gamma}
.
Since a stationary solution is a smooth regular curve satisfying (
1.8
), we see that for any stationary solution, there is
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that (
6.5
) holds. Thus, any stationary solution is an (area-)constrained critical point.

6.2.
A constrained Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality

The proof of the convergence result in
Section

6.3
will mainly relay on a constrained version of the Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality.
We will use this inequality for the
L^{2}
-gradients of the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
and the constraint functional
\mathcal{G}
. So, we work in the domain of these gradients.
Moreover, we first work with variations normal to a given regular curve. With this in mind, we define the following Hilbert spaces and functionals.

Definition 6.1
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}\in W^{4,2}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. The space of normal vector fields along
\hat{\gamma}
is denoted by

(6.6)

\displaystyle V^{\perp}:=W^{4,2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{Y\in W^{4,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle Y,\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}\rangle=0\}

(cf. (
A.1
)).
Moreover, we set

(6.7)

\displaystyle L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{Y\in L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle Y,\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}\rangle=0\text{ a.e.}\}.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be so small such that
\hat{\gamma}+Y
is immersed for all
Y\in U_{\varepsilon}:=B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset V^{\perp}
. With this, we define

(6.8)

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad E_{\lambda}(Y):=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma}+Y),

(6.9)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R},\qquad G(Y):=\mathcal{G}(\hat{\gamma}+Y).

As in
[
13
]
or in
[
46
]
, we first prove a Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality in
V^{\perp}
, i.e. for normal directions. Since we work in a constrained setting, the inequality will be based on
[
45
]
. The following two lemmata cover the assumptions in
[
45
, Cor. 5.2]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
E_{\lambda}
be defined as in (
6.8
).
Then the following holds.

1.

The energy
E_{\lambda}\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
is analytic.

2.

The
L^{2,\perp}
-gradient
dE_{\lambda}\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(6.10)

\displaystyle Y\mapsto P^{\perp}\Big(\nabla_{s_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}}^{2}\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}+\frac{1}{2}|\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}|^{2}\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}-\lambda\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}\Big)|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|,

with
P^{\perp}=\langle\cdot,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}
, is analytic.

3.

The derivative of the gradient evaluated at zero
(dE_{\lambda})^{\prime}(0)\colon V^{\perp}\to L^{2,\perp}
is Fredholm of index zero.

For the definition and fundamental properties of analytic maps between Banach spaces, see for example

[
53
]
. A detailed proof of
Lemma

6.2
in the case of open elastic curves can be found in
[
13
, Section 3]
. By slightly changing the function spaces (no boundary conditions but working in
{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
), this proof is transferred to the case of closed curves. A proof of 3. in
Lemma

6.2
is also given in
[
36
]
. We remark that for this Fredholm property of the second variation, it is crucial that we consider only variations in normal direction.

For the constrained version of the Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality, we additionally have to analyze the functional
G
.

Lemma 6.3
.

The constraint functional
G
defined in (
6.9
) satisfies the following.

1.

The functional
G\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
is analytic.

2.

The
L^{2,\perp}
-gradient
dG\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(6.11)

\displaystyle Y\mapsto-P^{\perp}\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|=-\langle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y},\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\,|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|

is analytic.

3.

The derivative of the gradient evaluated at zero
(dG)^{\prime}(0)\colon V^{\perp}\to L^{2,\perp}
is compact.

4.

If
\hat{\gamma}
in
Definition

6.1
is given such that
\mathcal{A}(\hat{\gamma})=A_{0}
, we have
G(0)=0
and
dG(0)\neq 0
.

Proof.

1. and 2.
As in
[
13
, Lem. 3.4]
and
[
46
, Prop. 4.5]
, the functions

(6.12)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}\to C({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}),\;Y\mapsto|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|\quad\text{ and }\quad\ U_{\varepsilon}\to W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;Y\mapsto\partial_{s_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)

are analytic. Since rotation by a fixed angle is a welldefined, continuous, linear operator on
W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset C^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(6.13)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}\to W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}),\quad Y\mapsto\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}

is also analytic. The function
Y\mapsto\hat{\gamma}+Y
is analytic as a map from
U_{\varepsilon}
to
W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Thus, the scalar product

(6.14)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}\to W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}),\quad Y\mapsto\langle\hat{\gamma}+Y,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}\rangle

is analytic.
As
W^{3,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is continuously embedded into
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
Y\mapsto\langle\hat{\gamma}+Y,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}\rangle
is also analytic as a map from
U_{\varepsilon}
to
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Since scalar multiplication
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})\times C({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
is analytic, so is the map

(6.15)

\displaystyle U_{\varepsilon}\to L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}),\quad Y\mapsto\langle\hat{\gamma}+Y,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y}\rangle|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|.

Since integration over
{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
is a welldefined continuous and linear operator on
L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R})
, the analyticity of
G\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to\mathbb{R}
follows. Similar arguments and the continuity and linearity of the projection
P^{\perp}\colon L^{2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
yields the analyticity of the
L^{2,\perp}
-gradient
dG\colon U_{\varepsilon}\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

3.
With the projection
P^{\perp}=\langle\cdot,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}
, we compute

(6.16)

\displaystyle(dG^{\prime})(0)Y

\displaystyle=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\;dG(tY)=-P^{\perp}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\;\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+tY}\;|\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}|-P^{\perp}\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\;|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+tY)|

(6.17)

\displaystyle=\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}|\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}|\langle Y,\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle.

Here, we used that

(6.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\;\partial_{s_{\hat{\gamma}+tY}}(\hat{\gamma}+tY)=\nabla_{s_{\hat{\gamma}}}Y

(see for example
[
13
, (B.5)]
), which implies that the first summand in (
6.17
) vanishes. For the second summand, we used

(6.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\Big|_{t=0}\;|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+tY)|=-|\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}|\langle Y,\kappa_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle

(see for example
[
13
, (B.2)]
). We see that
(dG^{\prime})(0)\colon V^{\perp}\to L^{2,\perp}
is of zeroth order in
Y
.
Hence it is compact.

4.
The assumptions on
\hat{\gamma}
imply that
G(0)=\mathcal{G}(\hat{\gamma})=0
. Moreover,
|\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}|\neq 0
and
\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\neq 0
. Thus,
dG(0)=\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}|\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}|\neq 0
.
∎

With this, we prove a first version of a constrained Łojasiewicz-Simon gradient inequality which reads as follows.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
\hat{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}_{\mathrm{Imm}}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a constrained critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. Then there exist constants
C>0
,
\vartheta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
r>0
, such that

(6.20)

\displaystyle|E_{\lambda}(0)-E_{\lambda}(Y)|^{1-\vartheta}\leq C\|P(Y)dE_{\lambda}(Y)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}

for all
Y\in B_{r}(0)\subset U_{\varepsilon}\subset V^{\perp}
with
G(Y)=0
. Here,
P(Y)\colon L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the orthogonal projection onto
\{X\in L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y},X\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{\gamma}+Y})}=0\}
.

Proof.

We check that the assumptions in
[
45
, Corollary 5.2]
are satisfied for
E_{\lambda}
and
G
defined on
U_{\varepsilon}\subset V^{\perp}
. In
Lemma

6.2
and
Lemma

6.3
we have seen that
E
and
G
are analytic. Assumption (i), i.e.
V^{\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\hookrightarrow L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
densely, is given. Assumptions (ii) and (iii) follow from
Lemma

6.2
and assumptions (iv) to (vi) from
Lemma

6.3
. Thus, by
[
45
, Corollary 5.2]
, the set of area preserving normal variations

(6.21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\{Y\in U_{\varepsilon}:G(Y)=0\}

is locally a submanifold of codimension
1
near
0
. Since
\hat{\gamma}
is a constrained critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
,
0
is a critical point of
E_{\lambda}
on
\mathcal{M}
. Thus,
[
45
, Corollary 5.2]
further yields the existence of constants
C,r>0
and
\vartheta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
such that for all
Y\in\mathcal{M}
with
\|Y\|_{W^{4,2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}\leq r
, it holds that

(6.22)

\displaystyle|E(Y)-E(0)|^{1-\vartheta}\leq C\|P(Y)dE(Y)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)},

where
P(Y)\colon L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is the orthogonal projection onto the closure of the tangent space
\overline{T_{Y}\mathcal{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}}
. With
[
45
, Proposition 3.3]
it follows that

(6.23)

\displaystyle\overline{T_{Y}\mathcal{M}}^{\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}}

\displaystyle=\{X\in L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle dG(Y),X\rangle_{L^{2}(dx)}=0\}

(6.24)

\displaystyle=\{X\in L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}+Y},X\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\hat{\gamma}+Y})}=0\}.\qed

To prove a version of the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality for variations that are not necessarily normal, the reparametrization argument in
Section

A.1
is crucial.

With
Lemma

A.1
,
Theorem

6.4
can be extended to area preserving variations with tangential component.

Theorem 6.5
.

Let the immersion
\hat{\gamma}\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a constrained critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
.
Then there exist constants
C>0
,
\vartheta\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
and
\tilde{r}>0
such that

(6.25)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma})|^{1-\vartheta}\leq C\,\Big\|\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-\frac{\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma)}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}\Big\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}

for all
\gamma\in W^{4,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathcal{G}(\gamma)=0
and
\|\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\|_{W^{4,2}}\leq\tilde{r}
.

Proof.

Let
C>0
,
\vartheta\in(0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
r>0
be given as in
Theorem

6.4
. Moreover, let
\tilde{r}=\tilde{r}(\hat{\gamma},r)
be the constant of
Lemma

A.1
. We take some
\gamma\in W^{4,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathcal{G}(\gamma)=0
and
\|\gamma-\hat{\gamma}\|_{W^{4,2}}\leq\tilde{r}
. By
Lemma

A.1
, there exists a
W^{4,2}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
and
Y\in B_{r}(0)\subset V^{\perp}
such that
\gamma\circ\Phi=\hat{\gamma}+Y
. Since
0=\mathcal{G}(\gamma)=\mathcal{G}(\gamma\circ\Phi)
, we know that
G(Y)=0
. Thus, the invariance of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
under reparametrization and
Theorem

6.4
yields

(6.26)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma})|^{1-\vartheta}

\displaystyle=|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)-\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma})|^{1-\vartheta}=|E_{\lambda}(Y)-E_{\lambda}(0)|^{1-\vartheta}

(6.27)

\displaystyle\leq C\|P(Y)dE_{\lambda}(Y)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

By
[
45
, Remark 5.4]
, the projection operator
P(Y)\colon L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\to L^{2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
is given by

(6.28)

\displaystyle P(Y)X=X-\frac{\langle dG(Y),X\rangle_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}}{\|dG(Y)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}}dG(Y).

In particular, we have
P(Y)(c_{1}dG(Y))=0
for

(6.29)

\displaystyle c_{1}=c_{1}(\gamma)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}(\gamma)}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma),\nu_{\gamma}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma}.

Moreover, comparing
dE_{\lambda}
and
dG
to the
L^{2}
-gradients, we have

(6.30)

\displaystyle dE_{\lambda}(Y)=P^{\perp}\left(\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)\right)|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|,\quad dG(Y)=P^{\perp}\left(\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)\right)|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|,

where
P^{\perp}=\langle\cdot,\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}\rangle\nu_{\hat{\gamma}}
. With these two observations, we obtain

(6.31)

\displaystyle\|P(Y)dE_{\lambda}(Y)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}

\displaystyle=\|P(Y)(dE_{\lambda}(Y)+c_{1}dG(Y))\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(6.32)

\displaystyle\leq\|dE_{\lambda}(Y)+c_{1}dG(Y)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}

(6.33)

\displaystyle=\|P^{\perp}\left(\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)+c_{1}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)\right)|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}x)}.

By Sobolev embedding and possibly making
r
smaller, we can assume that
|\partial_{x}(\hat{\gamma}+Y)|
is uniformly bounded for
\|Y\|_{W^{4,2}}\leq r
. So, putting (
6.26
) and (
6.32
) together yields

(6.34)

\displaystyle|\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)-\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\hat{\gamma})|^{1-\vartheta}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma\circ\Phi)+c_{1}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma\circ\Phi)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma\circ\Phi})}

for a modified constant
C>0
. It remains to notice that the gradients are geometric, i.e.

(6.35)

\displaystyle\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma\circ\Phi)=\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)\circ\Phi,\quad\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma\circ\Phi)=\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma)\circ\Phi.

With this, we obtain

(6.36)

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma\circ\Phi)+c_{1}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma\circ\Phi)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma\circ\Phi})}=\|\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma)+c_{1}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{G}(\gamma)\|_{L^{2}(\,\mathrm{d}s_{\gamma})}

and the claim follows.
∎

6.3.
Proof of full convergence

We are now prepared to enhance the subconvergence result to a full convergence result. This finally proves
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 6.6

(Convergence)
.

Let
\lambda>0
and
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the global smooth solution of (
1.7
). Then there exists a family of smooth diffeomorphisms
\Phi(t)\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
,
t\in(0,\infty)
, such that
\gamma(t,\Phi(t,\cdot))
converges smoothly for
t\to\infty
to a stationary solution
\gamma_{\infty}
.

For the proof of full convergence, we use an argumentation similar to
[
13
, Section 5]
, but due to the
H^{-1}
-structure of the flow, applying the Łojasiewicz inequality is more challenging and time-dependent norms have to be handled. In
[
21
]
, the authors have to face similar difficulties. We note that in
[
46
]
, an elegant way to shorten the proof of convergence in
[
13
, Section 5]
is shown, but it seems that this cannot be transferred to
H^{-1}
-flows.

Proof of
Theorem

6.6
.

We take a smooth solution
\gamma\colon[0,\infty)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
of
Theorem

5.6
and
\tilde{\gamma}(t,\cdot)-p(t)
of (
5.22
), which is the reparametrized with constant speed and translated solution.

Step 1: Convergent sequence.

By the subconvergence result (
Theorem

5.6
), there exists a sequence of times
t_{i}\to\infty
, such that
\tilde{\gamma}(t_{i},\cdot)-p(t_{i})
converges smoothly to a stationary solution
\gamma_{\infty}
(which will be kept fix throughout the proof). As discussed in
Section

6.1
,
\gamma_{\infty}
is an area constrained critical point of
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
. Due to (
3.12
), we know that

(6.37)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma(t))=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\tilde{\gamma}(t)-p(t))\geq\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{\infty})\quad\text{for all }t\in[0,\infty).

We assume that the inequality is strict. If not, there exists
\bar{t}\in[0,\infty)
such that
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma(\bar{t}))=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{\infty})
. But due to
Lemma

3.3
, this means
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma(t))=\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{\infty})
for all
t\geq\bar{t}
, so
\partial_{t}^{\perp}\gamma\equiv 0
on
[\bar{t},\infty)
by (
3.12
).
Thus, only the tangential velocity changes on on
[\bar{t},\infty)

and the claimed convergence follows trivially.

Step 2: Fixing
\delta=\delta(\gamma_{\infty})
.

For the following, we fix
\delta>0
such that for all
\zeta\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
with
\|\zeta-\gamma_{\infty}\|_{C^{1}}<\delta
, it holds that

(6.38)

\displaystyle|\partial_{x}\zeta|\geq M:=\frac{1}{2}\min|\partial_{x}\gamma_{\infty}|=\frac{\mathcal{L}(\gamma_{\infty})}{2}>0,

see (
5.8
), and such that

(6.39)

\displaystyle|\alpha^{\perp}|:=\Big|\alpha-\Big\langle\alpha,\frac{\partial_{x}\zeta}{|\partial_{x}\zeta|}\Big\rangle\frac{\partial_{x}\zeta}{|\partial_{x}\zeta|}\Big|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\alpha|

for all vector fields
\alpha
normal to
\gamma_{\infty}
. The latter inequality can be achieved by

(6.40)

\displaystyle\Big|\Big\langle\alpha,\frac{\partial_{x}\zeta}{|\partial_{x}\zeta|}\Big\rangle\Big|=\Big|\Big\langle\alpha,\frac{\partial_{x}\zeta}{|\partial_{x}\zeta|}-\frac{\partial_{x}\gamma_{\infty}}{|\partial_{x}\gamma_{\infty}|}\Big\rangle\Big|

becoming small for small distance

\|\zeta-\gamma_{\infty}\|_{C^{1}}
.

Step 3: New initial datum and new flow.

Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}\min\{\delta,\tilde{r},1\}
, where
\tilde{r}
is given as in
Theorem

6.5
for
\hat{\gamma}=\gamma_{\infty}
. Then
Lemma

A.1
yields the existence of
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\tilde{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon,\gamma_{\infty})>0
such that for all
X\in B_{\tilde{\varepsilon}}(0)\subset W^{7,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,

(6.41)

\displaystyle(\gamma_{\infty}+X)\circ\Phi=\gamma_{\infty}+Y

for a
W^{7,2}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi
and
Y\in B_{\varepsilon}(0)\subset W^{7,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
normal to
\gamma_{\infty}
.
Due to the smooth subconvergence, there is an index
i\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|\tilde{\gamma}(t_{i})-p(t_{i})-\gamma_{\infty}\|_{W^{7,2}}<\tilde{\varepsilon}
.
Thus, there exists a
W^{7,2}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi_{0}
such that

(6.42)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{i})-p(t_{i}))\circ\Phi_{0}=\gamma_{\infty}+N_{0}

for some
N_{0}\in W^{7,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
normal to
\gamma_{\infty}
with

(6.43)

\displaystyle\|N_{0}\|_{W^{7,2}}<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}\min\{\delta,\tilde{r}\}.

For this index
i
, we set
\bar{\gamma}_{0}:=(\tilde{\gamma}(t_{i})-p(t_{i}))\circ\Phi_{0}=\gamma_{\infty}+N_{0}
.
Possibly choosing
\varepsilon>0
smaller, we can ensure that
\Phi_{0}
does not change the orientation.
So
\bar{\gamma}_{0}
describes a smooth closed curve with
\mathcal{A}(\bar{\gamma}_{0})=A_{0}
.
Moreover, by
Remark

A.2
,
\Phi_{0}
and
N_{0}
are smooth.

The idea now is to take
\bar{\gamma}_{0}

6.4.
Stationary solutions

The convergence result outlined in
Section

6.3
raises interest in gaining a deeper understanding of stationary solutions.

From (
1.8
),
we see that
\gamma
is a stationary solution if and only if there exists a constant
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(6.78)

\displaystyle\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa+\tfrac{1}{2}|\kappa|^{2}\kappa-\lambda\kappa=c_{1}\nu.

If
c_{1}=0
and
\lambda>0
, we obtain the classical elasticae equation (
1.3
), for which the solutions are classified.
So in a certain sense, the constant
c_{1}
measures the deviation from the stationary solution to an elastica. Remember that the only closed elasticae are multifold coverings of the circle and of the figure eight elastica, see
[
40
, Lemma 5.4]
.

For
c_{1}\neq 0
, (
6.78
) is the equation which determines the equilibrium shapes of cylindrical fluid membranes exposed to a pressure difference
c_{1}
and experiencing a tensile stress
\lambda
, see
[
47
]
or
[
48
]
.
In this context, (
6.78
) is the Euler–Lagrange equation corresponding to the problem of minimizing the elastic energy

(6.79)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\gamma}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s+\lambda\mathcal{L}(\gamma)+c_{1}\mathcal{A}(\gamma)

with penalized area (
\lambda>0
given) and penalized length (
c_{1}>0
given).
In
[
47
]
, a complete classification of the critical points corresponding to closed curves is presented. Similarly,
[
48
]
explicitly provides the solutions to (
6.78
) in a format comparable to
[
47
]
. The authors state a condition under which the solution describes a closed curve and investigate when the curve is simple.
By
[
11
, Lemma 5.4]
, minimizing (
6.79
) is equivalent to minimize
\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}
under the constraint of fixed enclosed area. The latter problem is studied in
[
50
]
and
[
51
]
for
\omega=1
.
In particular, we note that from the mentioned articles, it follows that for
c_{1}\neq 0
, there exist solutions of (
6.78
) that do not correspond to a multifold covered circle or a multifold covered figure eight. Concrete examples can be found in
[
47
]
,
[
48
]
or
[
51
]
. We note that the references mentioned are by no means complete. For further references, see also the citations within the mentioned articles.

If
A_{0}\neq 0
, the defect
c_{1}
in (
6.78
) can be measured in terms of the unpenalized elastic energy and the length of the curve.

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\gamma
be a stationary solution and
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\gamma
satisfies (
6.78
). Then

(6.80)

\displaystyle 2c_{1}A_{0}=\mathcal{E}(\gamma)-\lambda\mathcal{L}(\gamma).

Proof.

We assume that
\gamma\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies (
6.78
) on
{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
. This implies

(6.81)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\langle\kappa,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s-\lambda\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\kappa,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=c_{1}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\gamma,\nu\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

The first integral can be written as

(6.82)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s}^{2}\kappa,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\partial_{s}\nabla_{s}\kappa,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\langle\gamma,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\langle\nabla_{s}\kappa,\kappa\rangle\langle\gamma,\partial_{s}\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

(6.83)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\kappa|^{2}\langle\kappa,\gamma\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s.

Putting this into (
6.81
) and with another integration by parts in the third integral of (
6.81

6.80
).
∎

Without penalizing the length, solutions do not converge in general. A simple example for arbitrary rotation index is given by the following situation.

Lemma 6.10
.

and

. Then, proceeding as in the proof of

5.6

, Lemma 4.1 and Remark 4.2]
. In addition to the proof of short time existence (
Theorem

4.1
), it is also used in the proof of the constrained Łojasiewicz–Simon gradient inequality (
Theorem

6.5
).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
k\geq 4
and
\hat{\gamma}\in W^{k+1,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
be a regular curve.
We define

(A.1)

\displaystyle V^{\perp}:=W^{4,2,\perp}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})=\{Y\in W^{4,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2}):\langle Y,\partial_{x}\hat{\gamma}\rangle=0\}.

Then, there exists a constant
r=r(\hat{\gamma})>0
such that
for all
X\in B_{r}(0)\subset W^{k,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
, there exists a
W^{k,2}
-diffeomorphism
\Phi\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
such that

(A.2)

\displaystyle(\hat{\gamma}+X)\circ\Phi=\hat{\gamma}+Y

for some
Y\in V^{\perp}\cap W^{k,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

For
r>0
given, there exists

\tilde{r}=\tilde{r}(\hat{\gamma},r)>0
such that for all
X\in B_{\tilde{r}}(0)\subset W^{k,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
(
A.2
) holds
for some
Y\in B_{r}(0)\subset V^{\perp}\cap W^{k,2}({\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Remark A.2
.

With the notations in the preceding lemma, it can be shown that smoothness of the reference curve
\hat{\gamma}
and smoothness of
X\in B_{r}(0)
yields smoothness of the diffeomorphism
\Phi
and the normal variation
Y
. For details on this, see
[
13
, Proof of Lemma 4.1]
.

A.2.
Parabolic Hölder spaces and the linear problem

For the proof of short time existence, we work with parabolic Hölder spaces of order
6
, cf.
[
17
, Section II.3.1]
or
[
28
]
.

Let
l\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\!\setminus\mathbb{Z}
,

a,b\in\mathbb{R}
with
a<b
,
T>0
and let
Q_{T}:=(0,T)\times(a,b)
. The parabolic Hölder space
H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
is the space of all functions
f\colon\overline{Q_{T}}\to\mathbb{R}
with continuous derivatives
\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f
for
6m+n<l
and finite norm

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)}:=\,

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\lfloor l\rfloor}\sum_{6m+n=j}\sup_{(t,x)\in{Q_{T}}}\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f(t,x)\right|

(A.4)

\displaystyle+\sum_{6m+n=\lfloor l\rfloor}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t,x_{1}),(t,x_{2})\in Q_{T}\\
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f(t,x_{1})-\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f(t,x_{2})\right|}{\left|x_{1}-x_{2}\right|^{l-\lfloor l\rfloor}}

(A.5)

\displaystyle+\sum_{0<l-6m-n<6}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(t_{1},x),(t_{2},x)\in Q_{T}\\
t_{1}\neq t_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{\left|\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f(t_{1},x)-\partial_{t}^{m}\partial_{x}^{n}f(t_{2},x)\right|}{\left|t_{1}-t_{2}\right|^{\frac{l-6m-n}{6}}}.

To define the parabolic Hölder space
H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
, we identify
f\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\to\mathbb{R}
with its periodic counterpart
f\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
. By choosing
a,b
such that the interval
(a,b)
contains two periods of
f
,
H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
consists of the functions with periodic counterpart in
H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left(\overline{Q_{T}}\right)
.

We first consider the linear parabolic problem

(A.6)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}L\varphi:=\partial_{t}\varphi-\sum_{q=0}^{6}a_{q}\partial_{x}^{q}\varphi=f\quad&\text{ in }(0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\varphi(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\end{cases}

for some
f\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
, coefficients
a_{q}\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
and
\varphi_{0}\in C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
L
be parabolic,
l\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{Z}
and assume that
a_{q}\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
,
q=0,\ldots,6
. Then for all
f\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
and
\varphi_{0}\in C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
, there exist a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
to (
A.6
) and a constant
C>0
independent of
\varphi
,
f
and
\varphi_{0}
such that

(A.7)

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}\leq C\Big(\|f\|_{H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}+\|\varphi_{0}\|_{C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})}\Big).

Proof.

First, assume that
a_{6}\equiv 1
and
a_{q}\equiv 0
for
q=0,1,\dots,5
. Like in
[
25
, Section 3]
one proves that in this case, a weak solution to (
A.6
) exists. Localizing the problem and using
[
17
, Theorem VI.21]
one obtains Hölder regularity and (
A.7
). As in
[
3
, Section 3.1.4]
, the general linear problem (
A.6
) is solved with the method of continuity.
∎

A.3.
The nonlinear problem

In this subsection, we proceed as in
[
23
, Section 2.5]
and
[
14
, Section 3.3]
. For a smooth immersed reference curve
\gamma_{\ast}
with normal
\nu_{\ast}
, we define the open set

(A.8)

\displaystyle W:=\big\{\varphi\in C^{1}([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}};\mathbb{R}):|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi(t,\cdot)\nu_{\ast})|\neq 0\big\}

and

F\colon{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{7}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as

(A.9)

\displaystyle F\big(\cdot,\varphi,\partial_{x}\varphi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{6}\varphi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\nu_{\ast})|^{-1}\big):=\langle\nabla_{s}^{2}\nabla_{L^{2}}\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\nu_{\ast}),\nu_{\ast}^{\perp}\rangle

(A.10)

\displaystyle\qquad=\frac{1}{|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\,\nu_{\ast})|^{6}}\,\partial_{x}^{6}\varphi+Q\big(\cdot,\varphi,\partial_{x}\varphi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\varphi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\,\nu_{\ast})|^{-1}\big),

see (
4.2
). Since
Q
is a polynomial with smooth coefficients, the mapping

(A.11)

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}\colon H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W

\displaystyle\to H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right),

(A.12)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto F\big(\cdot,\varphi,\partial_{x}\varphi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{6}\varphi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\nu_{\ast})|^{-1}\big),

l\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\!\setminus\mathbb{Z}
, is welldefined. With this, we write (
4.3
) as

(A.13)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{\varphi}=\mathbf{F}[\varphi]\quad&\text{ in }(0,T)\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\varphi(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{cases}

Proposition A.4
.

Take
\varphi_{0}\in C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
such that
\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi_{0}\nu_{\ast}
is an immersed curve. Then there exists
T>0
and a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
to (
A.13
).

Proof.

First, we assume that
0<l<1
.

Step 1: Linearized problem.

Let us make the following observation. The Fréchet derivative of
\mathbf{F}
at
\xi\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W
can be written as

(A.14)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[\xi]=\sum_{q=0}^{6}a_{q}\partial_{x}^{q}

with
a_{6}=|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\xi\nu_{\ast})|^{-6}
and
a_{q}(t,x)=\tilde{a}_{q}(t,x,\xi,\partial_{x}\xi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\xi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\xi\nu_{\ast})|^{-1})
,
q=0,\dots,5
, for smooth functions
\tilde{a}_{q}\colon[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\times\mathbb{R}^{6}\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
.
In particular,
a_{q}\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
for
q=0,\dots,6
.
Since
\xi\in W
and by compactness,
a_{6}
is uniformly bounded away from zero on
[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
. Hence,
\partial_{t}-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[\xi]
is a parabolic operator and
Proposition

A.3
yields a unique solution
\sigma\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
to

(A.15)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dot{\sigma}-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[\xi]\sigma=f\quad&\text{ in }[0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\sigma(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\end{cases}

for
f\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
.

Step 2: Special solution.
Now, we consider (
A.15
) with
\xi\equiv 0
and
f=\mathbf{F}[\varphi_{0}]-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[0]\varphi_{0}
. Here,
\varphi_{0}\in C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
is interpreted as function
\varphi_{0}\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W
constant in time. Since
f\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
, there exists a unique solution in
H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
to (
A.15
). In the following, we will denote this solution by
\sigma
. Since
\varphi_{0}\in W
, we can make
T>0
small enough such that
\sigma\in W
. Now, we define

(A.16)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}:=\partial_{t}\sigma-\mathbf{F}[\sigma]\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right).

Evaluated at
t=0
, we have

(A.17)

\displaystyle\tilde{f}(0,\cdot)=\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[0]\sigma|_{t=0}+\mathbf{F}[\varphi_{0}]-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[0]\varphi_{0}-\mathbf{F}[\sigma]|_{t=0}=0.

Here, we used (
A.15
) and the smoothness of
\mathbf{F}
.

Step 3: Application of the Inverse Function Theorem.

Let
0<k<l
such that
k\not\in\mathbb{Z}
and consider the nonlinear operator

(A.18)

\displaystyle\Phi\colon H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W

\displaystyle\to H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\times C^{6+k}({\mathbb{S}^{1}}),

(A.19)

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\partial_{t}\xi-\mathbf{F}[\xi],\,\xi(0,\cdot)\big).

By Step 1, the Fréchet derivative

(A.20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}\Phi[\sigma]\colon H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\to H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\times C^{6+k}({\mathbb{S}^{1}}),

(A.21)

\displaystyle\zeta

\displaystyle\mapsto\big(\partial_{t}\zeta-\mathrm{D}\mathbf{F}[\sigma]\zeta,\,\zeta(0,\cdot)\big)

is a linear isomorphism. It follows by the Local Inverse Function Theorem that
\Phi
is a local diffeomorphism at
\sigma
. So there is a neighborhood
U\subset H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W
of
\sigma
and a neighborhood
V\subset H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\times C^{6+k}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
of
\Phi(\sigma)=(\tilde{f},\varphi_{0})
such that
\Phi\colon U\to V
is a diffeomorphism.

Step 4: Cut-off in time.
Choose for
0<\varepsilon<\min\{1,\frac{T}{2}\}
cut-off functions
\eta_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}([0,T])
such that
0\leq\eta_{\varepsilon}\leq 1
,
0\leq\dot{\eta}_{\varepsilon}\leq 2\varepsilon^{-1}
and
\eta_{\varepsilon}\equiv 0
on
[0,\varepsilon]
as well as
\eta_{\varepsilon}\equiv 1
on
[2\varepsilon,T]
. Define

(A.22)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}:=\eta_{\varepsilon}\tilde{f}.

Analogously as in
[
23
, Lemma 2.5.8]
, one shows that
f_{\varepsilon}\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)

with norm bounded independently of
\varepsilon
.
Here, we need the assumption
l<1
. The crucial point is estimating the Hölder seminorm in time. We have

(A.23)

\displaystyle|f_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-f_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|\leq|\tilde{f}(t_{1})-\tilde{f}(t_{2})|+|\tilde{f}(t_{1})||\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|

for
t_{1}<t_{2}\in[0,T]
. Assume
t_{1}<2\varepsilon
, otherwise
|\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|=0
. For
t_{2}\leq 3\varepsilon
, we obtain with (
A.17
) that

(A.24)

\displaystyle|\tilde{f}(t_{1})||\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|

\displaystyle=|\tilde{f}(t_{1})-\tilde{f}(0)||\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\,t_{1}^{\frac{l}{6}}\,|t_{1}-t_{2}|

(A.25)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\,t_{1}^{\frac{l}{6}}\,|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{l}{6}}\,t_{2}^{1-\frac{l}{6}}\leq\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\,t_{2}\,|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{l}{6}}\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{l}{6}}

for a constant
C
not depending on
\varepsilon
.
For
t_{2}>3\varepsilon
, one has

(A.26)

\displaystyle|\tilde{f}(t_{1})||\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{1})-\eta_{\varepsilon}(t_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq Ct_{1}^{\frac{l}{6}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{l}{6}}\leq C|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{l}{6}}.

Again, the constant
C
does not depend on
\varepsilon
, which allows us to show the claim.
Moreover, one proves with standard arguments (see for example
[
24
, Section 6.8]
) and the Arzelà–Ascoli theorem, that for
0<k<l<1
,
H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
is compactly embedded in
H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
.
It follows that there exists a sequence
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
,
n\to\infty
, and
\hat{f}\in H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
such that

(A.27)

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon_{n}}\to\hat{f}\;\text{ in }H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\;\text{ for }n\to\infty.

Due to (
A.17
) and the continuity of
\tilde{f}
, we find that
f_{\varepsilon}\to\tilde{f}
,
\varepsilon\to 0
uniformly in the sup norm. So we conclude that
\hat{f}=\tilde{f}
.

Step 5: Existence of solution.

By (
A.27
), there exists some
\varepsilon_{\ast}>0
small enough such that
(f_{\varepsilon_{\ast}},\varphi_{0})\in V
with
V
given as in Step 3. This implies the existence of a unique
\varphi\in U
such that

(A.28)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi=\mathbf{F}[\varphi]+f_{\varepsilon_{\ast}}\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}.

With (
A.22
) and the definition of
\eta_{\varepsilon}
, we conclude that
\varphi
solves (
A.13
) for
t\in[0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]
.

Step 6: Regularity.
So far, we have
\varphi\in H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\cap W
for
0<k<l<1
. Defining

(A.29)

\displaystyle a_{6}(t,x):=|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\nu_{\ast})|^{-6}\in H^{\frac{5+k}{6},5+k}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\subset H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)

and

(A.30)

\displaystyle p(t,x):=P(\cdot,\varphi,\dots,\partial_{x}^{5}\varphi,|\partial_{x}(\gamma_{\ast}+\varphi\nu_{\ast})|^{-1})\in H^{\frac{1+k}{6},1+k}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)\subset H^{\frac{l}{6},l}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)

with
P
as in (
A.10
),
Proposition

A.3
yields a unique solution
\xi\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
to

(A.31)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}\xi-a_{6}\partial_{x}^{6}\xi=p\quad&\text{ in }[0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}},\\
\xi(0,\cdot)=\varphi_{0}&\text{ on }{\mathbb{S}^{1}}.\end{cases}

Since
\varphi
solves (
A.31
) on
[0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
, we conclude that
\xi=\varphi
for
t\leq\varepsilon_{\ast}
. Hence, we obtain
\varphi\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,\varepsilon_{\ast}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
.

Step 7: Uniqueness.

Assume we have two solutions
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,\varepsilon_{i}]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
,
i=1,2
and
\varepsilon_{1}\leq\varepsilon_{2}\leq\varepsilon_{\ast}
. Define

(A.32)

\displaystyle T^{\ast}=\sup\big\{t\in[0,\varepsilon_{1})\colon\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}\text{ in }C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})\text{ on }[0,t]\big\}.

We consider
\Phi
of Step 3 for variable
T
and show that for
T>0
small enough,

(A.33)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}|_{[0,T]},\varphi_{2}|_{[0,T]}\in U\quad\text{ as well as }\quad(0,\varphi_{0})\in V.

From the proof of the Inverse Function Theorem,
it follows that there exists
r>0
such that

(A.34)

\displaystyle B_{r}(\sigma)\subset U(T)\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{r}(\tilde{f},\varphi_{0})\subset V(T)\quad\text{ for all }0<T\leq\varepsilon_{1}.

Here,
\sigma
and
\tilde{f}
are given as in Step 2. Now, we first note that with (
A.16
) and (
A.17
), for
q=0,\dots,6
,

(A.35)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{q}(\varphi_{i}-\sigma)|_{t=0}=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\partial_{t}(\varphi_{i}-\sigma)|_{t=0}=\big(\mathbf{F}[\varphi_{i}]-\mathbf{F}[\sigma]-\tilde{f}\big)|_{t=0}=0.

With
Lemma

A.5
it follows that

(A.36)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{i}-\sigma\|_{H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }T\to 0.

Similarly, (
A.17
) implies

(A.37)

\displaystyle\|\tilde{f}\|_{H^{\frac{k}{6},k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}\to 0\quad\text{ as }T\to 0.

Due to (
A.34
) we conclude that for
T>0
small enough, (
A.33
) holds and thus

(A.38)

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}=\varphi_{2}\text{ in }{H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}.

The regularity of
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
even gives coincidence in
{H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}
. It follows that
T^{\ast}>0
.
Now, assume
T^{\ast}<\varepsilon_{1}
. Then
\varphi_{1}(T^{\ast})=\varphi_{2}(T^{\ast})=:\tilde{\varphi}_{0}\in C^{6+l}({\mathbb{S}^{1}})
. Taking
\tilde{\varphi}_{0}
as new initial datum and repeating the arguments yields a contradiction to the definition of
T^{\ast}
. Hence, we get
T^{\ast}=\varepsilon_{1}
and uniqueness is proven.

Bootstrapping and using the same arguments as in Step 6 shows the claim for
l>1
.
∎

In the last step of the proof of
Proposition

A.4
, we use the following observation.

Lemma A.5
.

Let
0<k<l<1
and
f\in H^{\frac{6+l}{6},6+l}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)
such that

(A.39)

\displaystyle f(0,\cdot)=\partial_{t}f(0,\cdot)=\partial_{x}f(0,\cdot)=\dots=\partial_{x}^{6}f(0,\cdot)=0.

Then

\|f\|_{H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}\to 0
as
T\to 0.

Proof.

Consider the periodic counterpart
f\colon[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and let the interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
contain at least two periods of
f
. Then

(A.40)

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{\frac{6+k}{6},6+k}\left([0,T]\times{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\right)}=\sup_{[0,T]\times I}|f|+\sup_{[0,T]\times I}|\partial_{t}f|+\sup_{[0,T]\times I}|\partial_{x}f|+\dots+\sup_{[0,T]\times I}|\partial_{x}^{6}f|

(A.41)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,T],x_{1},x_{2}\in I\\
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\partial_{t}f(t,x_{1})-\partial_{t}f(t,x_{2})|}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{k}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t\in[0,T],x_{1},x_{2}\in I\\
x_{1}\neq x_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\partial_{x}^{6}f(t,x_{1})-\partial_{x}^{6}f(t,x_{2})|}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{k}}

(A.42)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T],x\in I\\
t_{1}\neq t_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\partial_{t}f(t_{1},x)-\partial_{t}f(t_{2},x)|}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{k}{6}}}+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T],x\in I\\
t_{1}\neq t_{2}\end{subarray}}\frac{|\partial_{x}f(t_{1},x)-\partial_{x}f(t_{2},x)|}{|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{5+k}{6}}}

(A.43)

\displaystyle\qquad+\dots+\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,T],x\in I\\

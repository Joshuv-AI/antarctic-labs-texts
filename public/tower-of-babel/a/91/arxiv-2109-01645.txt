Dual boundary complexes of Betti moduli spaces over the two-sphere with one irregular singularity
Tao Su

Abstract
. The weak geometric P=W conjecture of L. Katzarkov, A. Noll, P. Pandit, and C. Simpson states that, a smooth Betti moduli space of complex dimension d d over a punctured Riemann surface has the dual boundary complex homotopy equivalent to a sphere of dimension d − 1 d-1 . Via a microlocal geometric perspective, we verify this conjecture for a class of rank n n wild character varieties over the two-sphere with one puncture, associated with any Stokes Legendrian link defined by an n n -strand positive braid.



Introduction

Let
C
be a genus
g
closed Riemann surface with
k
punctures
\sigma=\{p_{1},\text{\tiny$\cdots$},p_{k}\}
,
and
G=GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.
Modulo extra input, the tame nonabelian Hodge correspondence over noncompact curves
[
Sim90
,
Kon93
]

induces a diffeomorphism

\mathrm{NAH}:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\simeq\mathcal{M}_{B}

between two moduli spaces:
the Dolbeault moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}
of stable filtered regular (parabolic)
G
-Higgs bundles
on
(\Sigma,\sigma)
with parabolic degree
0
;
and the Betti moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{B}
of stable filtered
G
-local systems
on
\Sigma\setminus\sigma
with parabolic degree
0
.
There are also various generalizations: wild NAH over curves
[
BB04
]
; Replace

[
HKSZ22

[

,
Sim92
,
Sim95
,
Moc11
,
Moc21
]
.
As a consequence, we obtain an isomorphism on cohomology (with rational coefficients)

\mathrm{NAH}^{*}:H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B})\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}).

The famous (cohomological) P=W conjecture of Cataldo, Hausel, and Migliorini then states:

Conjecture 0.1

(
[
dCHM12
]
, Cohomological P=W)
.

\mathrm{NAH}^{*}(W_{2k}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B})=W_{2k+1}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{B}))=P_{k}H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}})

where
P_{\bullet}
is the Perverse-Leray filtration on
H^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}})
associated to
the Hitchin map
h:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}
.

This has now been given three very different proofs in the major case of twisted
GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-character varieties

[
MS22
,
HMMS22
,
MSY23
]
.
For earlier works or various extensions, see also
[

,
dCMS22
,
dCM20
,
Dav23
,
Mau21
,
FM22
,
MMS22

.

Aiming at a geometric interpretation of the cohomological P=W conjecture,
L. Katzarkov, A. Noll, P. Pandit, and C. Simpson
[
KNPS15
, Conj.1.1]
,
[
Sim16
, Conj.11.1]
formulated a
geometric analogue, called the geometric P=W conjecture.
Via
\mathrm{NAH}
, it relates the ‘asymptotic behavior of the Betti moduli space at infinity’
to the ‘Hitchin map at infinity’ on the Dolbeault side.
More concretely, take a log compactification
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{B}
of the (smooth) Betti moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{B}

with simple normal crossing boundary divisor
\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}:=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{B}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{B}
.
The combinatorics of the intersections of the irreducible components of
\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}

is encoded by a simplicial complex
\mathbb{D}\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}
, called the dual boundary complex.
The homotopy type of the dual boundary complex is an invariant of the algebraic variety
\mathcal{M}_{B}
.

Now, a weak form of the geometric P=W conjecture states that

Conjecture 0.2

(
[
KNPS15
,
Sim16
]
, weak geometric P=W)
.

The dual boundary complex
\mathbb{D}\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}
is homotopy equivalent to the sphere
S^{d-1}
,
where
d=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathcal{M}_{B}
.

More generally, the full geometric P=W conjecture identifies two fibrations up to homotopy:
On the Dolbeault side, the ‘Hitchin fibration at infinity’

\overline{h}:N_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}=\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Dol}}\setminus h^{-1}(B_{R}(0))\xrightarrow{h}\mathbb{A}\setminus B_{R}(0)\rightarrow(\mathbb{A}\setminus B_{R}(0))/\text{\tiny scaling}\penalty\ =S^{d-1},R\gg 0;

On the Betti side, a fibration (up to homotopy) of the form
\alpha:N_{B}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}
,
where
N_{B}^{*}
is the punctured tubular neighborhood of the boundary divisor
\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{B}
.
In other words,

Conjecture 0.3

(
[
KNPS15
,
Sim16
]
, Geometric P=W)
.

There exists a homotopy commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{\mathrm{Dol}}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces N_{B}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S^{d-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{D}\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{h}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

where the bottom arrow corresponds to the weak geometric P=W conjecture.

By
[
MMS22
, Thm.6.2.6]
, the geometric P=W conjecture implies the cohomological P=W conjecture in top weights,
hence the terminology. Otherwise, there’s no implication in either direction. To interpret the latter in all degrees,
it’s expected that a refined version of the geometric P=W conjecture is required.

To the best of the author’s knowledge, the full geometric P=W conjecture has been established only for a few cases

admits a somewhat concrete description: the Painlevé cases ((g,n)=(0,2), wild

)

Sza19
,

]
;

(g,k)=(1,0)
(singular

) or

[

, Thm.B]
.
From now on, the main concern will be the weak geometric P=W conjecture.
Apart from the two lists above, previous work of other authors has also established the case

G=SL_{2}(\mathbb{C})

[
Kom15
,
Sim16
,
FF23
]
. However, all these proofs use some special features of the moduli spaces under
investigation, which rarely apply to the general case.
In this article, the main goal is to establish the weak geometric P=W conjecture for
a class of rank

wild character varieties over
(\mathbb{P}^{1},\infty)
. Moreover, following the same strategy of the proof
(cell decomposition), the author has been able to prove the conjecture for all very generic character varieties

Let’s add a complementary remark. A
folklore conjecture
predicts that, all smooth Betti moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{B}

are log Calabi-Yau:
\exists

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{B}
such that

i.e.

Then, the weak geometric P=W conjecture may be regarded as a special case of the more general conjecture:

Conjecture 0.4

The dual boundary complex of a log Calabi-Yau variety is a sphere.

The only known partial result is due to J. Kollár and C. Xu

If

Results

Throughout the context, we fix a base field
\mathbb{K}
(for example,
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
).
Let
(C,\sigma=\{p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k},\{\Lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k})
be a genus
g
closed Riemann surface with
k
punctures

\sigma=\{p_{1},\text{\tiny$\cdots$},p_{k}\}
, such that
\Lambda_{i}
is a Legendrian link in the co-sphere bundle
T^{\infty}(C\setminus\sigma)
whose front projection encircles
p_{i}
.
Each
\Lambda_{i}
is identified with the cylindrical closure
\beta_{i}^{\circ}

of some positive braid
\beta_{i}\in\mathrm{Br}_{n_{i}}^{+}
.
Let
\vec{r}=(\vec{r}_{i})_{i=1}^{k}:\pi_{0}(\Lambda)=\text{\tiny$\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}$}\pi_{0}(\Lambda_{i})\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a suitable map.
As in
[
STZ17
,
STWZ19
]
, we may consider the moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\vec{r}}(C,\sigma=\{p_{i}\};\{\Lambda_{i}\})
of (microlocal rank
\vec{r}
) constructible sheaves on
C
whose
micro-support at infinity are contained in
\Lambda=\text{\tiny$\sqcup_{i=1}^{k}$}\Lambda_{i}
and whose stalks at
\sigma
are acyclic.
Taking the microlocal stalks gives rise to the microlocal monodromy:

\mu\mathrm{mon}:\mathfrak{M}_{\vec{r}}(C,\{p_{i}\},\{\Lambda_{i}\})\rightarrow\text{\tiny$\prod_{i=1}^{k}$}\mathcal{L}o_{\vec{r}_{i}}(\Lambda_{i}),

where
\mathcal{L}o_{\vec{r}_{i}}
is the moduli stack of rank
\vec{r}_{i}
local systems in degree
0
on
\Lambda_{i}
.
The Betti moduli stack
\mathfrak{M}_{L}(C,\{p_{i}\},\{\Lambda_{i}\})
is the substack of
\mathfrak{M}_{\vec{r}}(C,\{p_{i}\},\{\Lambda_{i}\})
consisting of objects whose microlocal monodromy is quasi-isomorphic
to a fixed local system
L\in\text{\tiny$\prod_{i=1}^{k}$}\mathcal{L}o_{\vec{r}_{i}}(\Lambda_{i})
.
The associated good moduli space
[
Alp13
]

\mathcal{M}_{L}(C,\{p_{i}\},\{\Lambda_{i}\})
is called a
Betti moduli space
in this article.
The terminology is justified by the microlocal reformulation of irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence over curves
(see
[
STWZ19
, Sec.3.3]
or Appendix
B
), according to which
a wild character variety over a complex curve
C
can be identified with
a Betti moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{L}(C,\{p_{i}\},\{\Lambda_{i}\})
where each
\Lambda_{i}
is a ‘Stokes Legendrian link’ encircling
p_{i}
.

Our main theme is the weak geometric P=W conjecture for
\mathcal{M}_{B}=\mathcal{M}_{L}(\mathbb{C}P^{1},\{\infty\},\{(\Delta\beta\Delta)^{\circ}\})
,
where
\beta_{1}=\Delta\beta\Delta\in\mathrm{Br}_{n}^{+}
with
\Delta
a half-twist, and we take
\vec{r}=1
.
Then, our main theorem is the following:

Main Theorem

(Theorem
4.7
)
.

If the twisted conjugacy class defining
[L]
is (twisted) semisimple and generic (Definition
2.19
),
then the Betti moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{B}
(if nonempty) is a smooth affine and connected variety,
and the weak geometric P=W conjecture holds for
\mathcal{M}_{B}
, i.e. we have a homotopy equivalence

\mathbb{D}\partial\mathcal{M}_{B}\sim S^{\dim\mathcal{M}_{B}-1}
.

In the initial version of this article, the main theorem was specifically formulated for connected braid closures
(\Delta\beta\Delta)^{\circ}
. Subsequently, this restriction has been lifted, resulting in a more general formulation. Notably, this expansion encompasses cases such as the (unramified/untwisted) wild character varieties over
(\mathbb{C}P^{1},\infty)
discussed in
[
Boa07
]
, which were excluded in the initial version.
The author expresses gratitude to the anonymous referee for suggesting the consideration of such a more general situation.

The strategy for proving the main result employs a
remove/reduction lemma
due to C. Simpson
[
Sim16
, Lem.2.3]
.
This lemma enables the elimination of an irreducible closed variety in the form of

from a smooth variety

Subsequently, the main theorem follows by induction from the cell decomposition of

Proposition 0.5

(Proposition

. Cell decomposition)
.

\mathcal{M}_{B}
admits a cell decomposition into locally closed subvarieties

\mathcal{M}_{B}(\mathbb{K})=\sqcup_{p\in\mathcal{W}^{*}(\beta_{1})}\mathcal{M}_{B}^{p}(\mathbb{K})

such that

(1)

For each
p\in\mathcal{W}^{*}(\beta_{1})
, we have a natural isomorphism

\mathcal{M}_{B}^{p}(\mathbb{K})\cong(\mathbb{K}^{*})^{a(p)}\times\mathbb{K}^{b(p)},

such that
a(p)+2b(p)=d=\dim\mathcal{M}_{B}
.

(2)

\exists!p_{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{*}(\beta_{1})
such that

is open and dense in

The cell decomposition relies a more concrete description of

Constructible sheaves and Legendrian knots

In this section, we review the microlocal theory of sheaves with a general reference to
[
KS90
]
.
The specific focus of this article is on constructible sheaves defined on a surface

,

1.1.
Micro-support

Let
M
be a real analytic manifold and
\mathbb{K}
be a fixed base field (or more generally, commutative ring).
By a sheaf on
M
, we mean a
complex of sheaves of
\mathbb{K}
-modules
on
M
. Denote by
\mathscr{S}h(M;\mathbb{K})

the triangulated dg category of sheave on
M
. Given a sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
M
, the notion of
micro-support

(or
singular support
)
SS(\mathcal{F})
, roughly speaking, measures the co-directions in
T^{*}M
along which
the local sections of
\mathcal{F}
fail to propagate. More precisely, we have

Definition 1.1

(Micro-support.
[
KS90
, Def.5.1.2]
)
.

Let
p=(x_{0},\xi_{0})\in T^{*}M
be a covector. We say
p\notin SS(\mathcal{F})
if there exists
a neighborhood
U
of
p
, such that for any
x_{1}\in M
and
C^{1}
-function
\varphi
on a neighborhood of
x_{1}

satisfying
(x_{1},d\varphi(x_{1}))\in U
and
\varphi(x_{1})=0
, we have

R\Gamma_{\{\varphi(x)\geq 0\}}(\mathcal{F})_{x_{1}}\simeq 0.

Equivalently, via the exact triangle

R\Gamma_{\{\varphi(x)\geq 0\}}(\mathcal{F})\rightarrow\mathcal{F}\rightarrow R\Gamma_{\{\varphi(x)<0\}}(\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{+1}
,
we have a quasi-isomorphism

\mathcal{F}_{x_{1}}\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma_{\{\varphi(x)<0\}}(\mathcal{F})_{x_{1}}.

The intuitive meaning of the last quasi-isomorphism is exactly that the local sections of
\mathcal{F}

propagate along the co-direction
p
.
The key properties of the micro-support
SS(\mathcal{F})
are as follows:

(1)

SS(\mathcal{F})\cap 0_{M}=Supp(\mathcal{F})
is the support of
\mathcal{F}
, where
0_{M}
the zero section of
T^{*}M
.

(2)

For any sheaf
\mathcal{F}
,
SS(\mathcal{F})
is a conic (i.e. invariant under the action of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
which
scales the cotangent fibers) and closed co-isotropic subset of
T^{*}M
.

(3)

If
\mathcal{F}
is a constructible sheaf with respect to a Whitney stratification
\mathcal{S}
, then
SS(\mathcal{F})
is
a conic Lagragian (i.e. Lagragian wherever it’s smooth) subset of
T^{*}M
with

SS(\mathcal{F})\subset L_{\mathcal{S}}\coloneqq\cup_{i\in\mathcal{S}}T^{*}_{S_{i}}M
.

(4)

(Triangular inequality)
If
\mathcal{F}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}_{3}\xrightarrow{+1}
is an exact triangle in
\mathscr{S}h(M;\mathbb{K})
,
then
SS(\mathcal{F}_{i})\subset SS(\mathcal{F}_{j})\cup SS(\mathcal{F}_{k})
for all distinct

(5)

(Microlocal Morse lemma)
If
f:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

(x,df(x))\notin SS(\mathcal{F})
for all

,

. Then the restriction map is a quasi-isomorphism:

R\Gamma(f^{-1}(-\infty,b);\mathcal{F})\xrightarrow{\sim}R\Gamma(f^{-1}(-\infty,a);\mathcal{F})

,
and let
\Lambda\subset T^{\infty}M

Definition 1.2
.

We

\mathscr{S}h_{\Lambda}(M)

\mathbb{K}
-modules on

To simplify the study of the (fine moduli) stack
\mathfrak{M}
representing a given moduli problem,
one would like to associate an algebraic space which still captures much of the geometry. Classically,
one uses the weaker notion of a
coarse moduli space
, whose points are in bijection with the isomorphism classes
of objects over the given algebraically closed field. For algebraic stacks with finite inertia
(e.g. separated Deligne-Mumford stacks), this works well, the coarse moduli spaces exist by the Keel-Mori theorem.
However, for algebraic stacks with infinite stabilizers as in our case, the coarse moduli space do not exist in general.

In fact, what we need is a generalization of coarse moduli spaces, i.e. the notion of
good moduli spaces
associated
to algebraic stacks (possibly with infinite stabilizers)
[
Alp13
]
, which we recall below.

1.3.1.

4.

(\mathbb{C}P^{1},\infty)

4.1.
Background on dual boundary complexes

We firstly review the basic concepts, properties, and tools concerning the dual boundary complex of a complex algebraic variety.

If
X
is a smooth quasi-projective variety over
\mathbb{C}
, by Hironaka’s resolution of singularities, one can obtain a smooth compactification
\overline{X}
with simple normal crossing divisor
D
at infinity. We may also assume the intersections of the irreducible components of
D
are connected. By encoding the information of the intersections of the irreducible components of
D
, we get a simplicial complex, called
dual boundary complex

\mathbb{D}\partial X
.

More precisely, let
D=\cup_{i=1}^{m}D_{i}
with
D_{i}
’s the irreducible components.

Definition 4.1
.

The dual boundary complex
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is the dual complex of
D
: The 0-simplices are
1,2,\ldots,m
, with
i
corresponds to the irreducible component
D_{i}
.

\mathbb{D}\partial X
contains the simplex
[i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{k}]
if and only if
D_{i_{1}}\cap D_{i_{2}}\cap\ldots\cap D_{i_{k}}
is non-empty.

The definition of
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is justified by the following

Theorem 4.2
.

[
Dan75
]

The homotopy type of
\mathbb{D}\partial X
is an invariant of
X
, and is independent of the choice of the compactification
\overline{X}
.

(Toy examples)
.

(1)

For
X=\mathbb{A}^{1}
, we can take
\overline{X}=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with
D=\{\infty\}
. So,
\mathbb{D}\partial(\mathbb{A}^{1})=*
.

(2)

For

, we can take
\overline{X}=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with

. So,
\mathbb{D}\partial(\mathbb{G}_{m})=S^{0}
.

(3)

For

, we can take

So
\mathbb{D}\partial(\mathbb{A}^{1}\times\mathbb{G}_{m})=[1,2]\cup_{\{1\}}[1,3]

[

, Prop.2.6]

Let

be a smooth irreducible quasi-projective variety over

. Let

be a non-empty open subset, with the complement

Appendix B
Irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence over curves

Here, we give a microlocal reformulation of the irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence over curves, following
[
STWZ19
, §3.3]
.
For related discussions in a different perspective, see also
[
Boa21
]
.

Let
C
be a compact Riemann surface with punctures (or marked points)
\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\}
. The main related case in this article is
C=\mathbb{C}P^{1}
with puncture
\{p_{1}=\infty\}
. We’re interested in integrable meromorphic connections
(H,\nabla)
on
C
with prescribed (possibly irregular) singularities along the punctures.

In the regular case, by taking
monodromy
of connections, the Riemann-Hilbert correspondence states that there’s an equivalence of categories between the (rank
r
) integrable meromorphic connections on
C
with regular singularities along the punctures
\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\}
, and (rank
r
) locally constant sheaves on
C\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\}
, in other words, (rank
r
) representation of the fundamental group of
C\setminus\{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k}\}
. Passing to moduli spaces, we then obtain a
complex analytic
isomorphism between a
de Rham moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{DR}}
of regular connections, and a
Betti moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{B}}
of locally constant sheaves (or fundamental group representations), i.e. character varieties.
The isomorphism is not algebraic, since taking the monodromy of a connection involves an exponential.

The regular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence admits a generalization to the irregular case, by specifying the
formal types of irregular singularities
of the connections at the punctures on the one hand, and encoding the
Stokes data
of the solution sheaves on the other. This is termed as
irregular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence
, about which we review some details here.

Fix a puncture
p_{i}
, let
u
be the local coordinate near
p_{i}
with
u(p_{i})=0
. Let
(H,\nabla)
be a rank
r
holomorphic bundle with meromorphic connection on
C
as above, hence
\nabla
is holomorphic on a punctured disk at
p_{i}
.

Notation B.1
.

\mathbb{C}\{u\}:=\text{the algebra of convergent power series in $u$}
, and
\mathbb{C}\{u\}[u^{-1}]:=\text{the fraction field}
;

\mathbb{C}[[u]]:=\text{the algebra of formal power series in $u$}
, and
\mathbb{C}((u)):=\text{the fraction field}
.

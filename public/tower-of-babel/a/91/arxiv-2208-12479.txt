A mod- p p metaplectic Montréal functor
Robin Witthaus

Abstract
. We extend Colmez’s functor defined for GL 2 ⁡ ( 𝐐 p ) \operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}) to the category of finitely generated smooth admissible mod- p p representations of the two-fold metaplectic cover of GL 2 ⁡ ( 𝐐 p ) \operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}) . We compute the images of the absolutely irreducible genuine objects and obtain a bijection between the genuine supersingular representations and four-dimensional irreducible Galois representations invariant under twist by all characters of order two. Restricted to genuine objects, the extended functor naturally takes values in the category of what we call metaplectic Galois representations – Galois representations with a certain extra structure encoding the aforementioned twist-invariance.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The
p
-adic and mod-
p
local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})

Integral weight modular forms may be viewed as automorphic representations of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
and, after fixing an auxiliary prime
p
, they have a Galois representation of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}/\mathbf{Q})
with coefficients in an algebraic closure of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
attached to them satisfying local-global compatibility at all finite primes; that is to say, for each finite place
\ell
, the smooth complex representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
appearing as the local component of the automorphic representation is determined by the restriction of the Galois representation to the decomposition group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{\ell}/\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
at
\ell
through the local Langlands correspondence after passage to the corresponding Weil-Deligne representation. If
\ell\neq p
, then the local Galois representation at
\ell
can essentially be recovered from the corresponding representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{\ell})
. At
\ell=p
however, passage to the Weil-Deligne representation is not fully faithful and hence the global correspondence does not restrict to a bijective correspondence locally at
p
. As explained in
[
7
]
, the search for a
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence grew out of the desire to fix this issue. It roughly speaking aims to relate irreducible admissible unitary Banach space representation of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
defined over a finite extension of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
to Galois representations of
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}/\mathbf{Q}_{p})
defined over the same finite extension.

The starting point of the
p
-adic and mod-
p
local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
was the classification of the smooth irreducible (admissible)
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-linear representation by Barthel-Livné
[
1
]
(working more generally for finite extensions of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
) and Breuil
[
5
]
. Namely, Barthel and Livné were able to describe all the irreducible representations occuring as a subquotient of a parabolic induction, and, building on their approach via universal spherical Hecke modules, Breuil managed to parametrize all the other –so-called
supersingular
– representations. This parametrization allowed him to write down a bijection between the set of isomorphism classes of supersingular representations and the set of isomorphism classes of two-dimensional continuous irreducible
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-linear representations of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
, which already suggested that there should be a mod-
p
local Langlands correspondence. Motivated by the possible existence of a
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence, Breuil
[
6
]
computed the reduction of (the completion of) locally algebraic unramified principal series depending on small weights, which led him to the formulation of the semi-simple mod-
p
local Langlands correspondence as it can be found at the beginning of
loc. cit.
In
[
13
]
, Colmez observes a close relation between
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules and smooth representations of the mirabolic subgroup of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
(and thus of the standard Borel if a central character is fixed). Inspired by the work
[
11
]
of Colmez, Berger and Breuil
[
4
]
compute the unitary completion of locally algebraic principal series representations and relate it to two-dimensional crystalline Galois representations via
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules; they also prove compatibility with Breuil’s semi-simple mod-
p
correspondence. Colmez
[
12
]
has turned his previously mentioned observation into an exact functor from the category of finite length
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
-representations (admitting a central character) to the category of
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules, which is equivalent to the category of Galois representations of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
via Fontaine’s equivalence. This functor is not only defined for mod-
p
coefficients, in which case it recovers Breuil’s correspondence after semi-simplification, but also for torsion coefficients and thus for Banach space representations yielding a
functorial
approach to a
p
-adic Langlands correspondence. This functor has been studied in more detail ever since, see for example the works
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
of Colmez-Dospinescu-Paškūnas and Paškūnas, respectively.

1.2.
Motivation and goal

Half-integeral weight modular forms, on the other hand, may be viewed as genuine automorphic representations of the so-called
metaplectic cover
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{Q}})
– a central extension by the roots of unity
\mu_{2}
in
\mathbf{Q}
, where “genuine” means that
\mu_{2}
acts non-trivially. It is natural to ask whether these analytic objects also have a Galois representation attached to them in a reasonable way. One possible answer to this question is to make use of Shimura’s correspondence (
[
24
]
,
[
20
]
,
[
18
]
), which assigns an integral weight modular form to one of half-integral weight in a suitable Hecke-equivariant fashion, and then pass to the corresponding global Galois representation. This is unsatisfying as it does not give any new information about Galois representations, so it seems worthwhile to look for a direct way of realizing such a metaplectic correspondence.

We would like to investigate this question from a
p
-adic point of view locally at
p
, i.e. letting
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
denote the pullback of the global covering to the local group at
p
, we are interested in a metaplectic version of the
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence relating irreducible admissible unitary genuine
p
-adic Banach space representations of
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
to certain
p
-adic Galois representations of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
. Both,
p
-adic Banach space representations residually of finite length and
p
-adic Galois representations, can be reduced mod-
p
(and semi-simplified to obtain something well-defined), so any
p
-adic correspondence will give rise to a semi-simple mod-
p
metaplectic correspondence (at least if
p>2
so that there is a non-trivial character
\mu_{2}\to\mathbf{F}_{p}^{\times}
). In fact, it seems easier to first study the mod-
p
situation. A complete classification of the smooth irreducible genuine
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-representations of
\widetilde{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
is given in
[
26
]
and the goal of this paper is to establish a relation to mod-
p
Galois representations by extending Colmez’s functor.

1.3.
Results

We fix an odd prime number
p
and a finite field
k
of characteristic
p
. We let
G=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
. The metaplectic covering

1\to\mu_{2}=\{\pm 1\}\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1

is described by an explicit
2
-cocycle relying on the Hilbert symbol, and it splits over the maximal compact open subgroup. Let
\iota\colon\mu_{2}\hookrightarrow k^{\times}
be the non-trivial group homomorphism. We let
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)
denote the category of smooth
k
-linear representations of
\widetilde{G}
. Of particular interest for us is the full subcategory
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{G},\iota}(k)
of
genuine
representations, i.e. those on which the central subgroup
\mu_{2}
acts via
\iota
.

The category
\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}}
of étale
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules over
k((X))
is equivalent to the category
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fin}}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}}(k)
of finite dimensional smooth
k
-linear representations of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
via Fontaine’s equivalence. Colmez’s functor
\mathbf{D}\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{G}(k)\to\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}}
is defined on the category of finitely generated admissible
k
-representations of
G
and has the property that the image of an object only depends on the restriction of that object to the mirabolic subgroup
P=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}&\mathbf{Q}_{p}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. More formally, using Breuil’s generalization
[
9
]
, one may view Colmez’s functor as defined on the category of smooth
P
-representations at the cost of replacing the target category by its ind-category:
\mathbf{D}\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{P}(k)\to\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}})
. When composed with the restriction functor
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{G}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{P}(k)
, this more general functor takes values in the subcategory of compact objects and recovers Colmez’s functor.

What allows us to extend Colmez’s functor is the observation that the pullback
\widetilde{P}
of the metaplectic covering to
P
is simply equal to the product of groups
P\times\mu_{2}
. We may therefore consider the restriction functor
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{P}(k)
and Corollary
4.14
establishes:

Proposition 1.1
.

The composition
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{P}(k)\xrightarrow{\mathbf{D}}\operatorname{Ind}(\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}})
takes values in the subcategory of compact objects, yielding the exact functor

\mathbf{D}\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)\to\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}},

whose restriction to the full subcategory
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{G}(k)
recovers Colmez’s functor.

Putting
P^{+}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}&\mathbf{Z}_{p}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and letting
Z
denote the center of
G
, the key for establishing this result is to show the following: Given a finite dimensional
\widetilde{K}\widetilde{Z}
-stable subspace
M
of
\pi\in\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)
generating
\pi
as a
G
-representation, the
P^{+}
-subrepresentation of
\pi
generated by
M
is admissible in the sense that its subspace of
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&\mathbf{Z}_{p}\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
-invariants is finite dimensional. This can either be proved by making sure that an appropriate version of Emerton’s formalism of ordinary parts also works for
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G},\iota}(k)
to establish an analogue of
[
17
, Theorem 3.4.7]
, or it can be deduced from known properties of the irreducible objects in
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)
. We choose to take the latter approach.

Denote by
\mathscr{V}\colon\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}}\simeq\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fin}}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}}(k)
Fontaine’s equivalence and put
\mathbf{V}=\mathscr{V}\circ\mathbf{D}
. Evaluating the extended functor at the genuine supersingular representations, we obtain our main result (Theorem
5.23
):

Theorem 1.2
.

The functor
\mathbf{V}\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fin}}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}}(k)
induces a bijection

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Absolutely irreducible}\\
\text{genuine supersingular}\\
\text{$k$-representations of $\widetilde{G}$}\end{array}\right\}\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\text{Absolutely irreducible}\\
\text{smooth $\rho\colon\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}\to\operatorname{GL}_{4}(k)$}\\
\text{s.t.\ $\rho\cong\rho\otimes\chi$}\\
\text{for all characters $\chi$ with $\chi^{2}=\mathbf{1}$}\end{array}\right\},

both sides considered up to isomorphism.

This theorem consists of two parts, namely surjectivity and injectivity. It is not difficult to write down what the Galois representations appearing as the target of the bijection look like. The hard part for showing surjectivity is to compute the image of a genuine supersingular representation explicitly, see Proposition
5.20
for a description. The detailed study of the supersingular objects in
[
26
]
allows us to put ourselves into a setup to which we can apply a general result of Florian Herzig on computing Galois representations attached to certain “nice” smooth
k
-representations of
P^{+}
, see Section
4.2
. Injectivity comes down to the fact, proved in
[
26
]
, that two genuine supersingular representations are isomorphic if and only if their restrictions to
P
are.

There is a conceptual explanation for the twist-invariance under all characters of order two. Namely, consider the pullback
1\to\mu_{2}\to\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}\to\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to 1
of the metaplectic cover to the center
Z\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
of
G
, and for
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}
with image
z\in\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
, define
\chi_{\tilde{z}}=(\mu_{-1}^{v(z)}\omega^{v(z)}\mu_{\omega(z)})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}
, where
v\colon\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to\mathbf{Z}
is the
p
-adic valuation,
\mu_{?}
is the unramified character mapping
p
to
?
and
\omega
is the mod-
p
cyclotomic character. The conjugation action of an element
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{Z}\cong\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}
is given by the
\widetilde{G}\supset\mu_{2}
-valued character
\chi_{\tilde{z}}\circ\det
.
In particular, the action of
\tilde{z}
on a genuine representation
\pi
defines a
\widetilde{G}
-equivariant isomorphism between
\pi
and its twist by
\chi_{\tilde{z}}\circ\det
. Now, the functor
\mathbf{D}
is compatible with twist by a character
\chi
in the sense that
\mathbf{D}(\pi\otimes\chi\circ\det)=\mathbf{D}(\pi)\otimes\chi
, meaning that the underlying vector space remains the same but the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-action is twisted according to
\chi
, see Definition
4.7
. Thus, any Galois representation arising as the image of a genuine representation under
\mathbf{V}
is invariant under twist by the characters
\chi_{\tilde{z}}
, for
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}
, which assemble into a
surjective
homomorphism
\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}\twoheadrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}^{\operatorname{cts}}(\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times},\mu_{2})
. This more conceptual point of view leads to the following notion.

Definition 1.3
.

A
metaplectic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module over
k((X))
is a pair
(D,\theta)
consisting of
D\in\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}}
and
\theta\colon\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}\to\operatorname{Aut}_{k((X))}(D)
a smooth and genuine
k((X))
-linear action such that
\theta(\tilde{z})\colon D\cong D\otimes\chi_{\tilde{z}}
is
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-equivariant. Denote the resulting category by
\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}
.

The extended functor then naturally induces the
metaplectic Montréal functor

\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G},\iota}(k)\to\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}.

Using Fontaine’s equivalence, one may translate the additional structure
\theta
on a metaplectic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
(D,\theta)
into additional structure on the Galois representation
\mathscr{V}(D)
, and call the resulting object a metaplectic Galois representation. We note however that the additional structure on such a metaplectic Galois representation is
not
simply given by a (smooth and genuine) action on the underlying
k
-vector space defining appropriate isomorphisms, see Section
5.1.2
.

If we let
S\subset\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
denote the subgroup of squares, then the characters
\chi_{\tilde{z}}
are trivial for all
\tilde{z}\in\widetilde{S}
, hence we have a restriction functor
\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{S},\iota}(\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}})\simeq\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{S}(\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}})
, where the equivalence follows from the equality of groups
\widetilde{S}=S\times\mu_{2}
and is given by
-\boxtimes\iota
. This functor admits an explicit left adjoint denoted by
\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}}_{\widetilde{S}}(-\boxtimes\iota)
, see Section
5.1.1
, nicely interacting with the functor
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}
as explained in Proposition
5.8
.

We further utilize Colmez’s construction
[
13
]
from
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules to smooth representations of the mirabolic subgroup, to obtain a functor
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\colon\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{B},\iota}(k)
from metaplectic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-modules to smooth genuine representations of the metaplectic Borel. Moreover, there is a natural transformation
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\circ\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}\Rightarrow(-)|_{\widetilde{B}}
of functors
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G},\iota}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{B},\iota}(k)
, see Proposition
5.13
.

Finally, we compute the images of the absolutely irreducible representations in
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\widetilde{G},\iota}(k)
. As we will recall in more detail in the main text below, these are either supersingular or principal series representations. Putting
B_{S}=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}&\mathbf{Q}_{p}\\
0&S\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, we have
\widetilde{B}_{S}=B_{S}\times\mu_{2}
, and any absolutely irreducible genuine principal series is of the form
\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})=\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{G}}_{\widetilde{B}_{S}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\boxtimes\iota)
for smooth characters
\chi_{1}
,
\chi_{2}
of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
and
S
, respectively. The isomorphism class of
\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
is uniquely determined by the pair
(\chi_{1}|_{S},\chi_{2}|_{S})
. Denoting by
\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{\operatorname{bc}}\subset\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{G}}_{\widetilde{B}}\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{T}}_{\widetilde{T}_{S}}(\chi_{1}\otimes\chi_{2}\boxtimes\iota)
the
\widetilde{B}
-invariant subspace of functions whose support is contained in the preimage of the big cell
B\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)B
under the projection
\widetilde{G}\twoheadrightarrow G
, we obtain the following result (Proposition
5.16
).

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\chi_{1},\chi_{2}\colon\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}\to k^{\times}
be smooth characters, and let
\mathscr{D}(\chi_{2})
be the
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module corresponding to
\chi_{2}
viewed as a character of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
via local class field theory.

(i)

The object
\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}}_{\widetilde{S}}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2})|_{S}\boxtimes\mathscr{D}(\chi_{2})\boxtimes\iota)
is irreducible in
\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}
.

(ii)

There is an isomorphism

\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\cong\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}}}_{\widetilde{S}}((\chi_{1}\chi_{2})|_{S}\boxtimes\mathscr{D}(\chi_{2})\boxtimes\iota)

in the category
\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}
.

(iii)

The canonical
\widetilde{B}
-equivariant map
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\to\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})
has image
\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{\operatorname{bc}}
and induces a non-split short exact sequence

0\to\operatorname{Ind}^{\widetilde{T}}_{\widetilde{T}_{S}}(\chi_{2}\omega\otimes(\chi_{1}\omega^{-1})|_{S}\boxtimes\iota)\to\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2}))\to\tilde{\pi}(\chi_{1},\chi_{2})^{\operatorname{bc}}\to 0.

Interestingly, these statements can be essentially reduced to the corresponding known results for principal series of
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
.

Concerning absolutely irreducible genuine supersingular representations, we have already explained above that their image under
\mathbf{V}
, or equivalently
\mathbf{D}
, is explicitly described and absolutely irreducible. Parts (iii) and (iv) of Proposition
5.20
further establish:

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
\pi
be an absolutely irreducible genuine supersingular
k
-representation of
\widetilde{G}
, and let
\zeta\boxtimes\iota\colon S\times\mu_{2}=\widetilde{S}\cong Z(\widetilde{G})\to k^{\times}
be its central character.

(i)

The canonical
\widetilde{B}
-equivariant map
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\pi)\xrightarrow{\cong}\pi
is an isomorphism.

(ii)

The metaplectic
(\varphi,\Gamma)
-module
\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\pi)
is the unique object in
\operatorname{\widetilde{\Phi\Gamma}^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X)),\iota}}
whose restriction to
S
is isomorphic to
\zeta\boxtimes\mathbf{D}(\pi)\in\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{S}(\operatorname{\Phi\Gamma^{\operatorname{\acute{e}t}}_{k((X))}})
such that the
\widetilde{B}
-action on
\widetilde{\mathbf{\Omega}}\widetilde{\mathbf{D}}(\pi)
extends to a smooth
\widetilde{G}
-action.

1.4.
Outlook

We hope that this work serves as the starting point of a possible metaplectic
p
-adic local Langlands correspondence for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
. Building on the computations of mod-
p
reductions of locally algebraic unramified genuine principal series appearing in
[
26
]
, one may write down a speculative semi-simple mod-
p
metaplectic local Langlands correspondence relating the mod-
p
automorphic side to metaplectic Galois representations of dimension
2n^{2}=8
, where
n^{2}=[L:\mathbf{Q}_{p}]
with
L
being the compositum of all degree-two extensions of
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
. However, real evidence for such a correspondence can only come from the
p
-adic picture. This makes a closer investigation of the relation between the completions of the aforementioned locally algebraic representations and certain (crystalline) Galois representations, as in the work of Breuil-Berger
[
4
]
for
\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
, inevitable.

1.5.
Conventions and notations

Throughout this work, we fix an odd prime number
p
and a finite field
k
of characteristic
p
with an algebraic closure
\bar{k}
. We put
G=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Q}_{p})
and use the standard notations
B\supset T\supset Z
to denote the subgroup of upper triangular matrices, the subgroup of diagonal matrices and the center of
G
, respectively, and we let
K=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
be the maximal compact open subgroup. We identify
Z\cong\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
via the diagonal embedding and let
S\subset\mathbf{Q}_{p}^{\times}
denote the subgroup of squares.

Given a topological group
\mathcal{G}
, the category of smooth
k
-linear representation of
\mathcal{G}
will be denoted by
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\mathcal{G}}(k)
. Here, smooth means that the stabilizer of each vector is open.

For a closed subgroup
\mathcal{H}\subset\mathcal{G}
, we let
\operatorname{Ind}^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}}(-)\colon\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\mathcal{H}}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{\mathcal{G}}(k)
be the smooth induction defining a left adjoint of the restriction functor
(-)|_{\mathcal{H}}
. If
\mathcal{H}
is in fact open, we similarly denote by
\operatorname{c-Ind}^{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathcal{H}}(-)
the compact induction defining a right adjoint of the restriction functor.

Finally, if
\mathcal{G}
is abelian, we let
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{sq}}
be its subgroup of squares.

2.
Genuine representation theory

In this section, we remind the reader of the classification of the smooth absolutely irreducible genuine mod-
p
representations of the two-fold metaplectic cover of
G
in terms of universal spherical Hecke modules, and then prove a finiteness result, which will play an important role for extending Colmez’s functor.

2.1.
Recollection

We recollect some facts about the metaplectic extension of
G
, for details we refer the reader to
[
26
, §2]
.
The metaplectic cover of
G
is the topological central extension

1\to\mu_{2}=\{\pm 1\}\to\widetilde{G}\to G\to 1

defined by the
2
-cocycle
\sigma\colon G\times G\to\mu_{2}
,

(1)

\sigma(g_{1},g_{2})=\left(\frac{\mathfrak{c}(g_{1}g_{2})}{\mathfrak{c}(g_{1})},\frac{\mathfrak{c}(g_{1}g_{2})}{\mathfrak{c}(g_{2})}\det(g_{1})\right)\text{ for all $(g_{1},g_{2})\in G\times G$},

4.
An extension of Colmez’s functor

In
[
12
]
, Colmez defines a functor from the category of finitely generated smooth admissible
k
-representations of
G
to the category of Galois representations of
\mathscr{G}_{\mathbf{Q}_{p}}
on
k
-vector spaces. This functor has the property that the image of an object only depends on the restriction of that object to the mirabolic subgroup

(i), we have an equality of groups

, so we may also precompose with the restriction functor
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)\to\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{sm}}_{P}(k)
and the finiteness results of Section

will imply that the composite again takes values in the subcategory of compact objects, thus extending Colmez’s functor to the whole category
\operatorname{Mod}^{\operatorname{fg.adm}}_{\widetilde{G}}(k)
.

.

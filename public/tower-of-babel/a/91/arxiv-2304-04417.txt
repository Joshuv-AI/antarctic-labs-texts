Tip growth in a strongly concentrated aggregation model follows local geodesics Thanks: https://people.bath.ac.uk/fh350/ , fh350@bath.ac.uk
Frankie Higgs

Abstract
We analyse the aggregate Loewner evolution (ALE), introduced in 2018 by Sola, Turner and Viklund to generalise versions of diffusion limited aggregation (DLA) in the plane using complex analysis. They showed convergence of the ALE for certain parameters to a single growing slit. Started from a non-trivial initial configuration of k k needles and the same parameters, we show that the small-particle scaling limit of ALE is the Laplacian path model , introduced by Carleson and Makarov in 2002, in which the tips grow along geodesics towards ∞ \infty . The proof of this result involves analysis of Loewner’s equation near its singular points, and we extend usual martingale methods to this setting. Most conformal growth models introduce an extra regularisation factor to deal with the singularities in Loewner’s equation at the sharp tips and right-angle bases of slit particles. As an intermediate step we prove a limit result for a model with no such regularisation factor, developing methods which should prove useful in analysing other weakly-regularised models with non-trivial limits.

Introduction

We study two models of growth in the plane, identified with
\mathbb{C}
.
The aggregate Loewner evolution (ALE) model
was introduced in
[
14
]

as a version of diffusion limited aggregation (DLA)
which could take advantage of the natural isotropy of
\mathbb{C}

and complex-analytic tools.
Via the Riemann mapping theorem,
we have a one-to-one correspondence between a certain family
of conformal maps and the collection of
compact simply-connected subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
By composing simple conformal maps (corresponding to “particles”)
we construct a map representing more complicated clusters.
The continuity of the one-to-one correspondence between maps
and clusters allows us to prove limit results about the cluster
by analysing the corresponding map,
so a powerful set of tools from complex analysis
becomes available for analysing growth models.

The Laplacian path model (LPM) was introduced in
[
1
]

as a model of tip growth,
in which
k
simple curves attached to the unit disc
grow towards
\infty
in the Riemann sphere.
The growth speeds are controlled by
a certain Laplacian field,
which allows the LPM to model “screening” effects,
in which the growth rate of each curve is affected by how much
its “view of
\infty
” is blocked by other curves.
The strength of the influence of this field is parameterised
by a single
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
.

We will prove that the ALE model of particle aggregation converges,
in the limit as individual particles become small,
to the LPM.
This is an extension of the result of
[
14
]
,
that ALE started from a trivial initial configuration
converges to an LPM with one arm, i.e. to a straight line.
Our methods involve an explicit analysis of the tips of particles,
where usual martingale methods are difficult to apply
due to singularities in
Loewner’s equation

which describes both the ALE and LPM.

The initial cluster is drawn with thin black lines,

\sigma
is large enough that some particles

which does not occur in the limiting regime.
In the three diagrams on the left one slit seems to grow much slower
than the rest,
agreeing with the observation in
[

]
that there is competition
between the arms in the Laplacian path model,
with only a certain number (depending on
\eta
)
likely to survive (i.e. growth to a length proportional to the diameter
of the entire cluster) as
T\to\infty
.
The code used for the simulations is available at

https://github.com/frankiehiggs/ALE-from-slits
.

1.1
Loewner’s equation

Loewner’s equation describes growing sets in the complex plane.
The aggregate Loewner evolution (ALE) model
of particle growth is defined by composing conformal maps,
but we can also view it as a solution to Loewner’s equation
with a certain driving function.
Loewner’s equation also defines the claimed limit:
the Laplacian path model (LPM).

Loewner’s equation encodes families of growing sets
by measures on the cylinder
\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
.
Continuity properties of the encoding
allow us to show the sets are close
if the corresponding measures are close.

Definition 1.1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<1\}

be the open unit disc,
and
\Delta=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\overline{\mathbb{D}}

the open
exterior disc
,
the complement of the closed unit disc in the Riemann sphere.

Given a compact, simply connected
K\subseteq\mathbb{C}

with
0\in K
(and
K\not=\{0\}
),
by the Riemann mapping theorem there is a unique conformal map

f_{K}:\Delta\to\Delta\setminus K

with
f_{K}(\infty)=\infty

and
f_{K}(z)=e^{c}z+O(1)

for some
c=c(K)\in\mathbb{R}
as
z\to\infty
.
We call
c(K)
the
(logarithmic) capacity
of
K
.

If
K
is a strict superset of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, then
c(K)>0
.
1
1

1

This follows from the fact that
c(K)=\int_{0}^{1}\log|f_{K}(e^{2\pi it})|\,\mathrm{d}t
.

Another useful property of logarithmic capacity
is
additivity
:
if
f_{K}
can be written
f_{K_{1}}\circ f_{K_{2}}
,
then
c(K)=c(K_{1})+c(K_{2})
.

We can encode certain families of growing compact sets
(K_{t})_{t\geq 0}

using
Loewner’s equation
.

Definition 1.1.2
.

Let
\xi:[0,T]\to\mathbb{R}
be a càdlàg function.
Then there is a unique solution
\varphi:[0,T]\times\Delta\to\Delta
to Loewner’s equation,

\varphi_{0}(z)=z,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{t}(z)=\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(z)z\frac{z+e^{i\xi_{t}}}{z-e^{i\xi_{t}}},\quad z\in\Delta,

(1.1)

corresponding to a growing cluster

K_{t}:=\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus\varphi_{t}(\Delta)

which is parameterised by capacity,
i.e.
c(K_{t})=t
.
The
driving function

\xi_{t}

encodes where the growth at time
t
is located on the cluster boundary.

We can generalise (
1.1
) to encode clusters growing
at arbitrary parts of their boundary, specified by a measure:

Definition 1.1.3
.

Given a family of probability measures
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
,
subject to measurability conditions on
t\mapsto\mu_{t}

there is a unique solution to Loewner’s equation

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}(z)=z,\quad\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi_{t}(z)=\varphi_{t}^{\prime}(z)\int_{\mathbb{T}}z\frac{z+e^{i\theta}}{z-e^{i\theta}}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(\theta),\quad z\in\Delta.

(1.2)

It is a simple generalisation to start Loewner’s equation from
a non-trivial initial condition
\varphi_{0}
,
provided
\varphi_{0}
is a conformal map of the form
f_{K}
for some
K
as above.

1.2
Particle aggregation models

We construct the aggregate Loewner evolution (ALE) cluster
by composing conformal maps.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{c}>0
,
and define
d=d(\mathbf{c})>0

to be the unique length
such that
\overline{\mathbb{D}}\cup(1,1+d]

has capacity
\mathbf{c}
.
We can determine explicitly that
d
satisfies
(d+2)^{2}/(d+1)=4e^{\mathbf{c}}
,
and asymptotically
d\sim 2\mathbf{c}^{1/2}
as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.

Let
f^{\mathbf{c}}
be the unique conformal map

\Delta\to\Delta\setminus(1,1+d]

with
f^{\mathbf{c}}(z)\sim e^{\mathbf{c}}z
as
z\to\infty
.
Given an angle
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
,
we can define the rotated map

\displaystyle f^{\theta,\mathbf{c}}:\Delta\to\Delta\setminus e^{i\theta}(1,1+d],

\displaystyle f^{\theta,\mathbf{c}}(z)=e^{i\theta}f^{\mathbf{c}}(e^{-i\theta}z).

Given a sequence of angles

(\theta_{n})_{n\geq 1}

and of capacities
(c_{n})_{n\geq 1}
,
let
f_{j}=f^{\theta_{j},c_{j}}

and

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}:=f_{1}\circ f_{2}\circ\dots\circ f_{n}.

Define the
n
th cluster

K_{n}
as the complement of
\Phi_{n}(\Delta)
,
so

\Phi_{n}:\Delta\to\mathbb{C}_{\infty}\setminus K_{n}.

Note that the total capacity is

c(K_{n})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}
.

Any cluster constructed this way can be encoded by Loewner’s equation.
For
t\geq 0
let
n_{t}=\inf\{n\geq 1:\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}>t\}
(if
c_{n}=\mathbf{c}
for all
n
, then
n_{t}=\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor+1
).
Let
\xi_{t}:=\theta_{n_{t}}
,
and let
(\varphi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
be the solution to (
1.1
)
with driving measure
(\xi_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.
Then for all
n
,
\varphi_{c(K_{n})}=\Phi_{n}
.

1.3
Aggregate Loewner evolution

If
(\theta_{n})_{n\geq 1}

and
(c_{n})_{n\geq 1}

are stochastic processes,
then
(K_{n})_{n\geq 1}

as constructed in

Section 1.2

is a stochastic process
in the space of compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}
.
The aggregate Loewner evolution ALE(
\alpha,\eta,\sigma
),
introduced in
[
14
]

is one such process.

Definition 1.3.1
.

We define the ALE inductively:
for
n\geq 0

we choose
\theta_{n+1}
,
conditional on
\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}
,
according to the probability density function

\displaystyle h_{n+1}(\theta)=\frac{1}{Z_{n}}\left|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}\left(e^{\sigma+i\theta}\right)\right|^{-\eta},\,\theta\in(-\pi,\pi],

where
Z_{n}:=\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{\sigma+i\theta})|^{-\eta}\,\mathrm{d}\theta

is a normalising factor.
The particle capacities can also be modified.
For
n\geq 0
let

c_{n+1}=\mathbf{c}|\Phi_{n}^{\prime}(e^{\sigma+i\theta_{n+1}})|^{-\alpha}.

Several simulations of the ALE in the setting of Theorem
1.1

are shown in Figure
1
.

Remark
.

We have introduced three parameters:
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
,
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
,
and
\sigma>0
. In this paper we will work with
\alpha=0
,
which means that all particles have the same capacity.

If
\sigma=0

then the poles and zeroes of
\Phi_{n}^{\prime}

on
\partial\Delta=\mathbb{T}

mean that the measure
h_{n+1}

would not necessarily be well-defined.
We will take
\sigma>0
but with
\sigma\to 0

as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.

The other parameter,
\eta
,
controls the influence of
harmonic measure
.
If
\eta=0

then
(\theta_{n})_{n\geq 1}

is a sequence of independent random variables uniformly distributed on
(-\pi,\pi]
,
corresponding to the Hastings–Levitov model
[
7
]
.

We can view
|(\Phi_{n}^{-1})^{\prime}(z)|

as the “density of harmonic measure”
at
z\in\partial K_{n}
,
and so when
\eta>0
,

h_{n+1}
is concentrated on areas of high harmonic measure,
and when
\eta<0
it is concentrated
on areas of low harmonic measure.

Remark
.

In
[
8
]
,
a phase transition was proved for negative
\eta
: for
\eta<-2

and sufficiently small
\sigma

the ALE cluster converges to a
Schramm–Loewner evolution
.

The authors of
[
14
]
showed for
\eta>1

that the scaling limit of an ALE with initial cluster
\overline{\mathbb{D}}

is a single straight slit.
In fact they showed a stronger degeneracy result:
with probability tending to 1 as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
,
each particle attaches near the tip of its immediate predecessor.
Theorem
1.1
extends this result to the case where
K_{0}

is the union of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
with more than one slit.

\eta
and
\alpha

is available at
this link
.

1.4
Laplacian path model

The Laplacian path model (LPM)
was defined in 2002 by Carleson and Makarov
[
1
]

to generalise several models of needle-like growth in mathematical physics.
We define the Laplacian path model as a growing set
in the complex plane using Loewner’s equation (
1.2
):
the LPM is encoded by an initial configuration of
k
arms

K_{0}
(which uniquely determines
\varphi_{0}
),
and a driving measure
(\mu_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

Definition 1.4.1
.

Let
K_{0}
be the union of rays of the form

e^{i\theta_{j}}(1,1+d_{j}]
,
where the
\theta_{j}
are distinct angles in
(-\pi,\pi]

and
d_{j}>0

for
j=1,\dots,k
.
Let
\Delta_{0}=\Delta\setminus K_{0}
,
and
\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
the unique conformal map

\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{LPM}}:\Delta\to\Delta_{0}

satisfying
\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{LPM}}(z)=e^{c_{0}}z+O(1)

as
z\to\infty
for some positive
c_{0}
.

We define the LPM cluster with parameter
\eta
,
which has
k
growing slits whose
tips at time
t>0
are at
a^{j}_{t}\in\Delta\setminus K_{0}
,
via Loewner’s equation
with initial condition
\varphi_{0}(z)=\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{LPM}}(z)
.
We have the driving measure

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}p^{j}_{t}\delta_{\phi^{j}_{t}},

(1.3)

where
e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}}
is
the preimage of
a^{j}_{t}
under
\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
,
and
p^{j}_{t}=\frac{|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}})|^{-\eta}}{Z_{t}}
,
and

Z_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}})|^{-\eta}
.

Remark
.

•

We can think of the growth of each slit in the geodesic LPM at time
t

as being in the direction of the hyperbolic geodesic
from
a^{j}_{t}
to
\infty
in
\Delta\setminus K_{t}
,
at a speed proportional to

|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}})|^{-(\eta-1)}
.

•

The weight given to the
j
th slit by (
1.3
)
depends on the second derivative of
\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
,
while the probability of attaching near a given point
in the ALE model depends on the first derivative of
\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{ALE}}
.

In
[
1
]
,
Carleson and Makarov
obtained the definition involving the second derivative
by considering growth speeds
proportional to the Laplacian field
\nabla G_{t}
,
where
G_{t}
is the Green’s function of
\Delta\setminus K_{t}
,
given by
G_{t}(z)=\log|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime}(z)|
.

We could also derive
\mu_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}}

by removing the regularisation from the ALE attachment measure.
For
\sigma>0
, let
\mu_{t}^{\sigma}

be the regularised ALE measure on
\mathbb{T}

corresponding to the LPM cluster at time
t
,
i.e. the measure with density
h^{\sigma}(\theta)\propto|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime}(e^{\sigma+i\theta})|^{-\eta}
.
If
\eta>1
then as
\sigma\to 0

the density becomes concentrated around the zeroes of
(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime}

at
\phi_{t}^{j}
for
j=1,\dots,k
,
and
|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime}(e^{\sigma+i\phi^{j}_{t}})|\sim\sigma|(\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}})^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\phi_{t}^{j}})|
.
Hence

\mu_{t}^{\sigma}\to\mu_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
as
\sigma\to 0

for a fixed time
t
.

•

The
\eta
above corresponds to
\eta-1
in the definitions of
[
1
]
;
we have shifted it to match the
\eta

used in the definition of the ALE in
[
14
]
.

•

For any configuration of
k
slits,

K_{0}
is a “gearlike domain” as defined in
[
11
]
,
and so Schwarz–Christoffel methods can be used to compute
an expression for
\Phi_{0}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
.
To compute the expression,
the preimages of all the tips and bases of the slits
must be determined,
which can be done numerically using methods in
[
11
]
.
We used this to compute the maps for arbitrary slit configurations
in our simulation of the ALE shown in Figure
1
.

Almost all known results about the Laplacian path model appear in

[
1
]
and
[
12
]
.
In
[
1
]
, Carleson and Makarov defined both the geodesic LPM above
and the
needle LPM
,
in which the cluster is a collection of straight slits
which grow at speed proportional to
|(\Phi^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t})^{\prime\prime}(e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}})|^{-(\eta-1)}
.

Selander examined in his PhD thesis
[
12
]

a version of the geodesic LPM in which the weights

p^{j}_{t}
are fixed constants,
and the needle LPM with growth speeds
proportional to harmonic measure at the tips,
which corresponds to
\eta=3/2
.
Each of these can be viewed as a simplification of a “non-branching DLA”.

For the needle LPM with
\eta=3/2
,
Selander proved results about the stability of stationary solutions:
slit configurations in which the ratio of lengths remains constant
for all
t\geq 0
.
For his modified geodesic model,
he proved convergence as
t\to\infty

to a stationary configuration determined by the weights,
when started from any initial configuration.

For a simplified “chordal” geodesic LPM,
in which finitely many curves grow from the tip of an infinite half-line,
Carleson and Makarov
proved a stability result:
starting the process from a two-arm configuration,
if
\eta<2
then both arms always survive,
while there are configurations in which only one arm survives
if
\eta>2
.
We will use some of the techniques they developed
for the chordal case
when we analyse the ALE and non-chordal LPM.

For the unsimplified geodesic LPM
(i.e. the model defined by (
1.3
)),
Carleson and Makarov also proved a stability result.
Theorem 5 of
[
1
]
states that if
1<\eta<\eta_{c}

for a critical value
\eta_{c}=\frac{18}{3+4\log 2}\approx 3.11815

then the symmetric three-armed cluster
is a

stationary solution,
i.e. the driving measure for an LPM started from an initial condition

to three symmetric arms in a certain sense

, to a symmetric driving measure

\frac{1}{3}(\delta_{\theta}+\delta_{\theta+2\pi/3}+\delta_{\theta+4\pi/3})
.
Moreover, it is unstable for

They also proved limiting results for the simpler “needle” LPM,
and made a number of conjectures about the geodesic LPM.

Although little progress has been made since 2002
on analysing the geodesic LPM,
various modifications have been studied and applied.
In
[
5

, Gubiec and Szymczak

The LPM and similar models have also been used to model the development of cracks
in materials and formation of systems of rivers and streams.
In particular, the chordal geodesic LPM with two needles at the tip of a half-line
can be used to model the bifurcation of a stream.
Carleson and Makarov proved that the angle between the two resulting streams

(also predicted by other authors using conformal mappings

which agrees with the average angle of

1.5
Main result

Our main theorem concerns convergence of the ALE as
\mathbf{c}\to 0

to the Laplacian path model cluster started from the same initial conditions.

Theorem 1.1
.

For a fixed
T>0
,
let
(\Phi^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the ALE(
\alpha,\eta,\sigma
) map
started from the initial cluster

K_{0}\cup\overline{\mathbb{D}}
, where

K_{0}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{k}e^{i\phi^{j}_{0}}(1,1+d_{j}]

for
d_{j}>0
and distinct
\phi^{j}_{0}\in[0,2\pi)
.
Let
\mu^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{t}
be the driving measure for
\Phi^{\mathrm{ALE}}
,
i.e.
\mu^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{t}=\delta_{\theta_{\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor+1}}
.
Let the parameters be
\alpha=0
,
\eta>1
and
\sigma=\mathbf{c}^{\gamma}

for
\gamma=\max\left(\frac{2(\eta+2)}{\eta-1},8\right)
(see the remark after Theorem
3.1
for an explanation of each term).

Let
(\Phi^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
be the map for the LPM
started from the same initial conditions,
and let
\mu^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t}
be the driving measure

\mu^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t}=\sum_{j}\bar{p}^{j}_{t}\delta_{\bar{\phi}^{j}_{t}}
.

Then
as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
,
\mu^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}

converges in distribution to
\mu^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}

as random elements of the space of measures
S=\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
,
where
m_{[0,T]}
is normalised Lebesgue measure on
[0,T]
.

In particular this means if
K^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{t}
and
K^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{t}

are the respective clusters at time
t
,
we have
K^{\mathrm{ALE}}_{T}\to K^{\mathrm{LPM}}_{T}

weakly as a random element of the space of compact subsets of
\mathbb{C}

(equipped with the Carathéodory topology).

Remark
.

In the slit convergence result of
[
14
]
, a different value of
\gamma
is used,
which is
\Theta((\eta-1)^{-2})

as
\eta\downarrow 1
,
while the
\gamma
given above is
\Theta((\eta-1)^{-1})
.
The slit convergence result of
[
14
]
starts with
a single slit of capacity
\mathbf{c}
,
while ours begin at a macroscopic size,
so it is not surprising that they require a much smaller
\sigma

for their attachment measure to detect the tip of that small initial slit.

To show the convergence of the ALE model to the LPM,
we introduce two intermediate models which we will call the
auxiliary ALE, and the multinomial model.

The auxiliary ALE attaches particles exactly at the tips of its
k
slits,
essentially replacing the ALE attachment measure,
which is supported on
\mathbb{T}
at each step,
with a finite-dimensional process supported on
k
atoms like the LPM.

We will then introduce the multinomial model
to replace the
regularised derivative in the ALE with the second derivative
on
the boundary.
This is quite simple.
The most difficult and involved step is showing that
the multinomial model,
which is a discrete-time random growth model,
converges to the continuous-time deterministic LPM.
We essentially prove a strong law of large numbers
at each tip:
if we have attached a large number of slits by time
t

and the probability of attaching at slit
j

is
p
, then this is similar to a solution to Loewner’s equation at time
t

with a driving function that always gives weight
p
to the tip of slit
j
.
It is complicated by the fact that the probabilities and weights
both change over time, as do the preimages of the tips.
Another important technical detail is that Loewner’s equation is singular at the tips.

Analytic tools

The choice of a slit as our particle shape
is very convenient because the map and its derivative have explicit expressions:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathbf{c}>0
and
f=f^{\mathbf{c}}:\Delta\to\Delta\setminus(1,1+d]

Let
\beta=\beta_{\mathbf{c}}

Then
\beta\sim 2\mathbf{c}^{1/2}

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{e^{\mathbf{c}}}{2w}(w+1)^{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{w^{2}+2(1-2e^{-\mathbf{c}})w+1}{w^{2}+1}}\right)-1

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(w)=\frac{f(w)}{w}\frac{w-1}{(w-e^{i\beta})^{1/2}(w-e^{-i\beta})^{1/2}}.

(2.2)

Proof.

See
[
14

.

Definition 3.1.3

2.5

to compare the two remaining derivatives

\displaystyle\Bigg|\left(R_{\Delta_{1}^{n}}\circ\Phi^{*}_{0}\circ R_{-\Delta_{1}^{n}}\circ\overline{\Psi}^{j}_{0}\circ f^{*}_{\hat{\theta}^{*}_{n_{j}(1)}+\Delta_{n_{j}(1)}^{n}}\circ\dots\circ\overline{\Psi}^{j}_{N_{j}-1}\right)^{\prime}\Big(f^{*}_{n_{j}(N_{j})}(\overline{\Psi}_{N_{j}}(e^{\sigma+i\theta}e^{i\bar{\phi}_{n}^{j}}))\Big)\Bigg|.

Lemma 3.4

Lemma 3.5
.

Let

\displaystyle(f^{-1})^{\prime}(w)=\frac{f^{-1}(w)}{w}\frac{w-1}{\sqrt{(w+1)^{2}-4e^{\mathbf{c}}w}}

4
Multinomial to Laplacian path model

The step from the multinomial model
to the LPM is the most difficult
and technically involved part of the proof
of Theorem
1.1
,
so deserves its own section.
We must track the movement of the preimages of the tips
in the two models,
which are governed by Loewner’s equation
close to the driving function
where it is singular,
and the second derivative of the maps
\Phi_{n}^{\mathrm{multi}}

and
\Phi_{t}^{\mathrm{LPM}}
,
which are governed by the second spatial derivative
of the same singular PDE.

As the Laplacian path model is generated by a driving
measure

rather than a function, we will first specify what we mean by convergence
of driving measures.

Definition 4.0.1
.

Given a metric space
(X,d)
,
and two Borel measures
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
on
X
,
the bounded Wasserstein distance

d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})

is defined

d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}-\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{2}\right|,

where
\mathcal{H}=\{\varphi\in C(X):\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}+\|\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq 1\}
,
for

\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}=\sup_{x\not=y}\frac{|\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)|}{d(x,y)},\quad\|\varphi\|_{\infty}=\sup_{x}|\varphi(x)|.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a separable metric space,
then
d_{\mathrm{BW}}
metrises weak convergence of finite measures on
X
,
i.e. if
\mu
,
\mu_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
are finite measures on
X
,
then
\mu_{n}\Rightarrow\mu
if and only if
d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\mu_{n},\mu)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

Proof.

See Theorem 11.3.3 of
[
4
]
.
∎

Remark
.

Weak convergence
\mu_{n}\Rightarrow\mu

of measures on
X
can also be implied by convergence

\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu

for bounded continuous functions
\varphi
.
We use the smaller space of test functions

\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
in this section because
it is substantially easier to prove convergence
of the integrals for our two measures in this case,
as we make use of the bound on
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}

in the proof of Corollary
4.3
,
and this still suffices to imply weak convergence of the measures.

Remark
.

We can view each driving measure
(\mu_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}

as a single probability measure
\mu
on the cylinder
S=\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]

given by
\mu_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}

where

is normalised Lebesgue measure on

.
Then by Proposition 1 of

]
,
weak convergence of these measures on the cylinder implies convergence
of the corresponding clusters in the Carathéodory topology.

We will use Proposition

to deduce convergence of the cylinder measures corresponding to

Definition 4.0.2
.

Let

x_{j}(t):=e^{i\phi^{j}_{t}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\bar{x}_{j}(t):=e^{i\bar{\phi}^{j}_{t}}

where
\phi^{j}_{t}
and
\bar{\phi}^{j}_{t}

are the preimages defined in the statement

.

For

\displaystyle I^{j}_{t}:=1\{\xi_{t}=x_{j}(t)\}=1\{\theta_{\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor+1}=\phi^{j}_{\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor\mathbf{c}}\}=I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}},

(4.1)

for

,
and note that
\mathbb{E}(I^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n})=p^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}
.

For all

Note that for some constant

for all

and all

.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the same assumptions as Theorem
1.1
,
there exists a universal constant

such that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(T)\leq A\mathbf{c}^{1/2R}\right]\to 1

as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.

Corollary 4.3
.

If
\mu_{t}=\delta_{\theta_{\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor+1}}
for the multinomial model
and
\bar{\mu}_{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\bar{p}^{j}_{t}\delta_{\bar{\phi}^{j}_{t}}

for the Laplacian path model,
then

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\mu_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]})\to 0

in probability as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.
Hence
\mu_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}
converges in distribution,
as a random element of the space of measures on
S=\mathbb{T}\times[0,T]
,
to
\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}
.

Proof of Corollary
4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
. For simplicity assume that
T

is an integer multiple of
\mathbf{c}
.
Then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left(I^{j}_{t}\varphi(x_{j}(t))-\bar{p}^{j}_{t}\varphi(\bar{x}_{j}(t))\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{T}\left((I^{j}_{t}-p^{j}_{t})\varphi(x_{j}(t))+(p^{j}_{t}-\bar{p}^{j}_{t})\varphi(x_{j}(t))+\bar{p}^{j}_{t}(\varphi(x_{j}(t))-\varphi(\bar{x}_{j}(t)))\right)\mathrm{d}t.

The latter two terms are small with high probability
using Theorem
4.2
,
so we only need to bound the ”martingale” terms
\int_{0}^{T}(I^{j}_{t}-p^{j}_{t})\varphi(x_{j}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t
for each
j
.
We can write this as

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\frac{T}{\mathbf{c}}-1}\left[(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}}\varphi(x_{j}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t+\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}}(p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{t})\varphi(x_{j}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t\right].

Since
|p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{t}|\leq A\mathbf{c}

by Proposition
4.4
,
we have
|\sum_{n=0}^{\frac{T}{\mathbf{c}}-1}\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}}(p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{t})\varphi(x_{j}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t|\leq AT\mathbf{c}
.
For the remaining term, we would like to simply compute second moments and
so show the term is small,
but need some way of doing this uniformly in
\varphi
.
Choose some large
N
,
and for simplicity assume it is a factor of
T/\mathbf{c}
.
Then write

\displaystyle\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=\frac{(m-1)T}{\mathbf{c}N}}^{\frac{mT}{\mathbf{c}N}-1}(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}}\varphi(x_{j}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=1}^{N}\mathbf{c}\varphi(x_{j}(mT/N))\sum_{n=\frac{(m-1)T}{\mathbf{c}N}-1}^{\frac{mT}{\mathbf{c}N}}(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})+\delta,

where
|\delta|\leq\frac{AT^{2}}{N}
using
\|\varphi\|_{\mathrm{Lip}}\leq 1
.
Now we will be able to find a bound independent of
\varphi
, as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\mathbf{c}\sum_{n=\frac{(m-1)T}{\mathbf{c}N}}^{\frac{mT}{\mathbf{c}N}-1}(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbf{c}^{2}\frac{T}{\mathbf{c}N}=\frac{4T\mathbf{c}}{N}

by conditional independence of the increments
I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}
.
Define the event

\displaystyle E_{m}=\left\{\left|\mathbf{c}\sum_{n=\frac{(m-1)T}{\mathbf{c}N}-1}^{\frac{mT}{\mathbf{c}N}}(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})\right|\geq\frac{2\sqrt{T}\mathbf{c}^{1/4}}{N}\right\},

then
\mathbb{P}(E_{m})\leq\mathbf{c}^{1/2}N
,
and so

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\sum_{m=1}^{N}\mathbf{c}\varphi(x_{j}(mT/N))\sum_{n=\frac{(m-1)T}{\mathbf{c}N}-1}^{\frac{mT}{\mathbf{c}N}}(I^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{n\mathbf{c}})\right|\geq 2\sqrt{T}\mathbf{c}^{1/4}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left(\bigcup_{m=1}^{N}E_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{c}^{1/2}N^{2}.

We chose
N
arbitrarily,
so if
1\ll N\ll\mathbf{c}^{-1/4}
,
we have

\displaystyle\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{H}}\left|\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\bar{\mu}_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\right|\to 0

in probability, as required.
∎

4.1
Lipschitz bound on the locations and probabilities

Proposition 4.4
.

There exists a constant
A=A(k,K_{0},T,\eta)

such that,
almost surely,
for
0\leq t,s\leq T
,

|x_{j}(t)-x_{j}(s)|\leq A|t-s|
and

|p_{t}^{j}-p_{s}^{j}|\leq A|t-s|
,
for all
j=1,\dots,k

in the multinomial model.
For the Laplacian path model, for all
j
and for
0\leq t,s\leq T
,
we also have

|\bar{x}_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|\leq A|t-s|
and

|\bar{p}_{t}^{j}-\bar{p}_{s}^{j}|\leq A|t-s|
.

We prove this by carefully examining the derivatives of
\Phi_{t}

and
\bar{\Phi}_{t}
near the tips.
The following lemma examines higher derivatives than necessary for this paper
because it is not too much extra work and may be useful in future research.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\Phi_{t}
be the solution to Loewner’s equation
for driving function
\xi_{t}
corresponding to the ALE as above.
Let
q_{t}(z)=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\Phi_{t}(z)=\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(z)z\frac{z+\xi_{t}}{z-\xi_{t}}

for
z\in\Delta
.
Note that
we can write Loewner’s equation in the integral form

\Phi_{t}(z)=\Phi_{0}(z)+\int_{0}^{t}q_{s}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s

and for
m\geq 1
,

\Phi_{t}^{(m)}(z)=\Phi_{0}^{(m)}(z)+\int_{0}^{t}q_{s}^{(m)}(z)\,\mathrm{d}s
.
Then for
m\geq 0
,
at times
t
when

\xi_{t}=x_{j}(t)

for some
j
, we have

\displaystyle q_{t}^{(m)}(x_{j}(t)):=\lim_{z\to x_{j}(t)}q_{t}^{(m)}(z)

\displaystyle=m\Phi_{t}^{(m)}(\xi_{t})+3\xi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{(m+1)}(\xi_{t})+\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{m+1}\Phi_{t}^{(m+2)}(\xi_{t}).

Proof.

Let
h(z)=z\frac{z+\xi_{t}}{z-\xi_{t}}=z+2\xi_{t}+\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}
.
Then
h^{\prime}(z)=1-\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}}
,
and for
p\geq 2
,
h^{(p)}(z)=2\xi_{t}^{2}\frac{(-1)^{p}p!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{p+1}}
.

Using the product rule,

\displaystyle q_{t}^{(m)}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{m}\binom{m}{p}h^{(m-p)}(z)\Phi_{t}^{(p+1)}(z)

(4.2)

\displaystyle=2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m}{p}\frac{(-1)^{m-p}(m-p)!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}\Phi^{(p+1)}(z)+mh^{\prime}(z)\Phi_{t}^{(m)}(z)+h(z)\Phi_{t}^{(m+1)}(z).

Consider the Taylor expansion
of each derivative of
\Phi_{t}
about
\xi_{t}=e^{i\phi_{t}^{j}}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{(p+1)}(z)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{t}^{(p+1)}(\xi_{t})+(z-\xi_{t})\Phi_{t}^{(p+2)}(\xi_{t})+\dots+\frac{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}{(m-p+1)!}\Phi_{t}^{(m+2)}(\xi_{t})

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+O\left((z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+2}\sup_{|w-\xi_{t}|<|z-\xi_{t}|}|\Phi_{t}^{(m+3)}(w)|\right).

Substituting each of these into (
4.2
),
for
1\leq r\leq m-1
, the coefficient of
\Phi_{t}^{(r)}(\xi_{t})
is

\displaystyle 2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}\binom{m}{p}\frac{(-1)^{m-p}(m-p)!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}\frac{(z-\xi_{t})^{r-p-1}}{(r-p-1)!}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-r+2}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}\frac{m!}{p!(r-1-p)!}(-1)^{m-p}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}\,m(m-1)\dots r}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-r+2}}\sum_{p=0}^{r-1}\binom{r-1}{p}(-1)^{m-p}.

If
r>1
this is just a multiple of the binomial expansion
of
(1-1)^{r-1}=0
, so there is no
\Phi_{t}^{(r)}(\xi_{t})

term in
q_{t}^{(m)}(\xi_{t})
.
If
r=1
the coefficient is non-zero, but
\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(\xi_{t})=0
.
Next, the coefficient of
\Phi_{t}^{(m)}(\xi_{t})
is,
again comparing coefficients with the binomial expansion of
(1-1)^{m-1}
,

\displaystyle 2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m}{p}\frac{(-1)^{m-p}(m-p)!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}\frac{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p-1}}{(m-p-1)!}+m(1-\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2m\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m-1}{p}(-1)^{m-p}+m(1-\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}})

\displaystyle=\frac{2m\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}}(-(1-1)^{m-1}+1)+m(1-\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}})

\displaystyle=m

if
m\geq 2
.
Then
writing
\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m}{p}(-1)^{m-p}=(1-1)^{m}-\binom{m}{m-1}(-1)^{1}-\binom{m}{m}(-1)^{0}=m-1
,
we find that the coefficient of
\Phi_{t}^{(m+1)}(\xi_{t})

in the expansion of (
4.2
) is

\displaystyle 2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m}{p}\frac{(-1)^{m-p}(m-p)!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}\frac{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p}}{(m-p)!}+(m-\frac{2m\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}})(z-\xi_{t})+z+2\xi_{t}+\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}(0-\binom{m}{m-1}(-1)^{1}-\binom{m}{m}(-1)^{0})+m(z-\xi_{t})-\frac{2(m-1)\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}+z+2\xi_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}(m-1)+m(z-\xi_{t})-\frac{2(m-1)\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}}+z-\xi_{t}+3\xi_{t}

\displaystyle=3\xi_{t}+(m+1)(z-\xi_{t}).

Finally, as
\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m+1}{p}(-1)^{m+1-p}=-\frac{1}{2}m(m+1)+m
,
the coefficient of
\Phi_{t}^{(m+2)}(\xi_{t})
is

\displaystyle 2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m}{p}\frac{(-1)^{m-p}(m-p)!}{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}\frac{(z-\xi_{t})^{m-p+1}}{(m-p+1)!}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+\frac{m}{2}(1-\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{(z-\xi_{t})^{2}})(z-\xi_{t})^{2}+(z+2\xi_{t}+\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{z-\xi_{t}})(z-\xi_{t})

\displaystyle=2\xi_{t}^{2}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\frac{m!}{p!(m-p+1)!}(-1)^{m-p}-m\xi_{t}^{2}+2\xi_{t}^{2}+O(z-\xi_{t})

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}^{2}\left(\frac{-2}{m+1}\sum_{p=0}^{m-2}\binom{m+1}{p}(-1)^{m+1-p}-m+2\right)+O(z-\xi_{t})

\displaystyle=\xi_{t}^{2}\left(\frac{2}{m+1}(\frac{(m+1)m}{2}-m)-m+2\right)+O(z-\xi_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{m+1}+O(z-\xi_{t}),

and so taking the limit
z\to\xi_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle q_{t}^{(m)}(\xi_{t})

\displaystyle=m\Phi_{t}^{(m)}(\xi_{t})+3\xi_{t}\Phi_{t}^{(m+1)}(\xi_{t})+\frac{2\xi_{t}^{2}}{m+1}\Phi_{t}^{(m+2)}(\xi_{t}),

as claimed.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

When

q_{t}(x_{j}(t))=\Phi_{t}^{\prime}(x_{j}(t))\,x_{j}(t)\frac{x_{j}(t)+\xi_{t}}{x_{j}(t)-\xi_{t}}=0

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Equation (2.7) of

]

gives a useful expression for the evolution of

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}x_{j}(t)

Remark

Proof of Proposition
4.7
.

,

,

Equation (2.6) in

determined by

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\bar{x}_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{l\not=j}\bar{p}^{j}_{t}\bar{x}_{j}(t)\frac{\bar{x}_{j}(t)+\bar{x}_{l}(t)}{\bar{x}_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{l}(t)}

(4.7)

and similarly for the multinomial model,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}x_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\text{if $\xi_{t}=x_{j}(t)$,}\\
-x_{j}(t)\frac{x_{j}(t)+x_{l}(t)}{x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)}&\text{if $\xi_{t}=x_{l}(t)$ for $l\not=j$}\end{matrix}\right..

(4.8)

Write

\displaystyle\lambda^{j,l}_{t}=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\text{if }j=l\\
-x_{j}(t)\frac{x_{j}(t)+x_{l}(t)}{x_{j}(t)-x_{l}(t)}&\text{otherwise}\end{matrix}\right.,

and likewise

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{t}=\left\{\begin{matrix}0&\text{if }j=l\\
-\bar{x}_{j}(t)\frac{\bar{x}_{j}(t)+\bar{x}_{l}(t)}{\bar{x}_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{l}(t)}&\text{otherwise.}\end{matrix}\right.

Therefore for
t\in[0,T]
we can write,
integrating (
4.7
)
and (
4.8
)
with respect to time,

\displaystyle\bar{x}_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=\bar{x}_{j}(0)+\sum_{l\not=j}\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}\bar{p}^{l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle x_{j}(t)

\displaystyle=x_{j}(0)+\sum_{l\not=j}\int_{0}^{t}\lambda^{j,l}_{s}I^{l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
I^{l}_{s}
is as defined in (
4.1
).
To bound the size of
\bar{x}_{j}(t)-x_{j}(t)
,
we can expand the difference of the integrands
into three terms

\displaystyle\lambda^{j,l}_{s}I^{l}_{s}-\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}\bar{p}^{l}_{s}

\displaystyle=(\lambda^{j,l}_{s}-\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s})I^{l}_{s}+\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})+\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(p^{l}_{s}-\bar{p}^{l}_{s}).

As the denominators in the above definitions of
\lambda^{j,l}_{t}

and
\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{t}

are bounded below,
we can linearise

|\lambda^{j,l}_{s}-\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}|\leq A\left(|x_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|+|x_{l}(s)-\bar{x}_{l}(s)|\right)\leq A\delta_{\mathrm{x}}(s)
, and

\sum_{l\not=j}|\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}||p_{s}^{l}-\bar{p}_{s}^{l}|\leq A\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(s)
,
and so by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle|x_{j}(t)-\bar{x}_{j}(t)|

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{l\not=j}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right|.

(4.9)

To finish our proof we need to bound the martingale-type terms

\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
.
Note that for
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
with
n\leq T/\mathbf{c}
,
by Proposition
4.4
,

\displaystyle\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}\wedge t}\bar{\lambda}_{s}^{j,l}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}\wedge t}\bar{\lambda}_{s}^{j,l}(I^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}})\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}\wedge t}\bar{\lambda}_{s}^{j,l}(p^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=(I^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}})\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}\wedge t}\bar{\lambda}_{s}^{j,l}\,\mathrm{d}s+O(\mathbf{c}).

We will bound each term in the sum on the right-hand side of (
4.9
),
so fix some
l\not=k
.
Let
X_{n}:=(I^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{n\mathbf{c}})\int_{n\mathbf{c}}^{(n+1)\mathbf{c}}\bar{\lambda}_{s}^{j,l}\,\mathrm{d}s

and note that
X_{n}
is a bounded martingale increment,

\mathbb{E}[X_{n}|\theta_{1},\dots,\theta_{n}]=0
,
so define the martingale
M_{n}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}X_{n}
.
Note that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\leq T}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s-M_{\left\lfloor t/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}\right|=O(\mathbf{c}),

so if we can bound
\max_{n\leq T/\mathbf{c}}|M_{n}|
then our result follows.

The increments
X_{n}
are bounded by
A\mathbf{c}
for a constant
A

depending only on
T,K_{0}
and
\eta
,
so
\mathbb{E}[|M_{\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}|^{2}]\leq A^{2}\mathbf{c}
.
Therefore, by Doob’s submartingale inequality,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\max_{n\leq T/\mathbf{c}}|M_{n}|\geq\frac{1}{k}\mathbf{c}^{1/4}\right]\leq\frac{A\mathbf{c}}{\mathbf{c}^{1/2}},

for a constant
A
.
So for sufficiently small
\mathbf{c}
,
on an event of probability at least
1-A\mathbf{c}^{1/2}

we have
\sum_{l\not=j}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\bar{\lambda}^{j,l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq 3\mathbf{c}^{1/4}

and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

By the chain rule we have
\Phi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}(t))=\kappa_{j}(0)\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(w_{j}(0))

and so

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}(t))

\displaystyle=\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(w_{j}(0))}{w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0)}\times\kappa(0)(w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0)).

We will repeatedly make use of expansions of the form

ab-\bar{a}\bar{b}=(a-\bar{a})b+\bar{a}(b-\bar{b})

to linearise the difference of complex expressions.
Applying this to the above decomposition of
\Phi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}(t))

and its LPM equivalent, we have

\displaystyle\left|\Phi_{t}^{\prime\prime}(x_{j}(t))-\bar{\Phi}_{t}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x}_{j}(t))\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(w_{j}(0))}{w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0)}-\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{j}(0))}{\bar{w}_{j}(0)-\bar{x}_{j}(0)}\right||\kappa(0)(w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0))|

\displaystyle\mspace{10mu}+\left|\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{j}(0))}{\bar{w}_{j}(0)-\bar{x}_{j}(0)}\right|\left|\kappa_{j}(0)(w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0))-\bar{\kappa}_{j}(0)(\bar{w}_{j}(0)-\bar{x}_{j}(0))\right|.

We claim that both
\left|\frac{\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(\bar{w}_{j}(0))}{\bar{w}_{j}(0)-\bar{x}_{j}(0)}\right|

and

are bounded.
The former is bounded by
\sup_{\zeta\in[x_{j}(0),\bar{w}_{j}(0)]}|\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)|

The Schwarz–Christoffel representation
[

tells us

\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(z)=\frac{\Phi_{0}(z)}{z}S(z)
,
where
S(z):=\frac{\prod_{l=1}^{k}(z-x_{j}(0))}{\sqrt{\prod_{l=1}^{k}(z-b_{+}^{l})(z-b_{-}^{l})}}
,
and
b_{\pm}^{j}
are the two preimages of the base of the
j
th slit.
We can differentiate this to verify that
|\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\zeta)|
is bounded
when
\zeta
is not close to any of the

,
which is the case for
\zeta\in[x_{j}(0),\bar{w}_{j}(0)]
.
Next note that
\sum_{l=1}^{k}|Q_{s}^{j,l}|=O((t-s)^{-1/2})
, which is integrable,
and so we can establish a finite upper bound
and positive lower bound

|\kappa_{j}(0)(w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0))|

using (
4.14
)
(the proof of which does not rely on (
4.13
)).

To simplify the first term

4.19
),

we can use the fundamental theorem of calculus to write

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}^{\prime}(w_{j}(0))=(w_{j}(0)-x_{j}(0))\int_{0}^{1}\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(\alpha w_{j}(0)+(1-\alpha)x_{j}(0))\,\mathrm{d}\alpha,

Then, using the Schwarz–Christoffel representation again to bound

we can bound the first term on the RHS of (

\displaystyle A\int_{0}^{1}\left|\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(pw_{j}(0)+(1-p)x_{j}(0))-\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}(p\bar{w}_{j}(0)+(1-p)\bar{x}_{j}(0))\right|\mathrm{d}p

which is equal to

4.18
) is bounded by a constant multiple of

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|+\int_{0}^{t}|p^{l}_{s}-\bar{p}^{l}_{s}||\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}|Q^{j,l}_{s}-\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s.

(4.23)

As in the proof of Proposition
4.7
,
for
s\in[m\mathbf{c},(m+1)\mathbf{c})

we can write
I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s}=I^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}+O(\mathbf{c})
,
and so the first term in (
4.23
) can be written

\left|\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(I^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}})\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|+O(\mathbf{c}).

Since
\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}
is deterministic
and
(I_{m\mathbf{c}}^{l}-p_{m\mathbf{c}}^{l})_{m\geq 0}

are martingale increments
with respect to
(\theta_{m})_{m\geq 0}
,
this sum resembles a martingale,
so we could try to control it
by bounding its second moment.
However, by conditioning on
H_{j}(n)

we can no longer use adaptedness.
Fortunately,
for all
n\leq\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor
,

\mathbb{P}(H_{j}(n))\geq A^{-1}

and so for each
l\in\{1,\dots,k\}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(I^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}})\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\,\Bigg|\,H_{j}(n)\right]

\displaystyle\leq A\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(I^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}})\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 2A\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\left(\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}|\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\right],

where the final inequality comes from the martingale property
and the fact that
|I^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{l}_{m\mathbf{c}}|\leq 1

(the factor of 2 is because we bound both the real and imaginary
parts of
\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}
by
|\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|
).
Then
|\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\leq A/\sqrt{t-s}
,
and so

\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}(\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}|\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\,\mathrm{d}s)^{2}\leq A\mathbf{c}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}(\sqrt{m+1}-\sqrt{m})^{2}\leq A\mathbf{c}\sum_{m=0}^{n-1}\frac{A}{m+1}\leq A\mathbf{c}\log\frac{t}{\mathbf{c}}
.
Define the event

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{p}}^{t}:=\left\{\sum_{l=1}^{k}\left|\int_{0}^{t}(I_{s}^{l}-p_{s}^{l})\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{2}{R}}\right\},

(4.24)

then by Markov’s inequality,

\mathbb{P}(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{t})\geq 1-A\mathbf{c}^{4/R}\log(T/\mathbf{c})

for sufficiently small
\mathbf{c}
.

For the second term in (
4.23
),
we use the bound
|\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|\leq A/\sqrt{t-s}

to get

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{t}|p_{s}^{l}-\bar{p}_{s}^{l}||\bar{Q}_{s}^{j,l}|\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}.

(4.25)

For the final term with
l\not=j
,
the denominators in (
4.16
) stay away from 0
so we can linearise

\displaystyle|Q^{j,l}_{s}-\bar{Q}^{j,l}_{s}|

\displaystyle\leq A\left(|(w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s))-(\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s))|+|x_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|+|x_{l}(s)-\bar{x}_{l}(s)|\right)

\displaystyle\leq A|(w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s))-(\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s))|+A\delta_{\mathrm{x}}(s).

The
l=j
term is linearised similarly,

\displaystyle|Q^{j,j}_{s}-\bar{Q}^{j,j}_{s}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3|x_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|}{|\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|}+\frac{|(w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s))-(\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s))|}{|w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s)||\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|}

\displaystyle\leq A\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{x}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}+A\frac{|(w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s))-(\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s))|}{t-s},

which absorbs the
l\not=j
terms.
Note that we have used
|w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s)|\geq A^{-1}\sqrt{t-s}

for all
s\leq t
,
which follows from the displayed part of Definition
4.2.2
.
Combined with (
4.25
),
on the event
E_{\mathrm{p}}^{t}\cap H_{j}(n)

this gives our claimed bound.
∎

Proof of Lemma
4.12
.

Since the
s
-derivative of
u_{s}

is singular as
s\uparrow t
,
we will carefully analyse and control the singularity.
To simplify the notation we will write
w_{j}=w_{j}(s)
, etc..
Subtract (
4.22
) from its LPM equivalent to obtain

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{s}=

\displaystyle-((w_{j}-x_{j})-(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}))

(4.26)

\displaystyle+\sum_{l\not=j}\left(-I^{l}_{s}\frac{2x_{l}^{2}}{(x_{j}-x_{l})(w_{j}-x_{l})}(w_{j}-x_{j})+\bar{p}^{l}_{s}\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})\right)

\displaystyle-I^{j}_{s}\left(3x_{j}+\frac{2x_{j}^{2}}{w_{j}-x_{j}}\right)+\bar{p}^{j}_{s}\left(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}\right).

We will linearise all these terms to write (
4.26
)
in the form
\dot{u}_{s}=\gamma(s)u_{s}+\alpha(s)

for suitable functions
\gamma
and
\alpha
.
The first line on the right-hand side is clearly
-u_{s}
.
The
l
th summand on the second line is a difference of the form

\bar{a}\bar{b}\bar{c}-abc
,
so can be linearised as

(\bar{a}-a)\bar{b}\bar{c}+a(\bar{b}-b)\bar{c}+ab(\bar{c}-c)
,
giving

\displaystyle(\bar{p}^{l}_{s}-I^{l}_{s})\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}

\displaystyle+I^{l}_{s}\left(\frac{2x_{l}^{2}}{(x_{j}-x_{l})(w_{j}-x_{l})}-\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}\right)(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})

\displaystyle+I^{l}_{s}\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}((\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})-(w_{j}-x_{j})).

If we write
\bar{p}^{l}_{s}=p^{l}_{s}+(\bar{p}^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})
,
then the first term becomes the sum of

-(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}

and something bounded by
A\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)
.
The final term is also bounded by a multiple of
u_{s}
,
and so for two bounded functions
A^{l}_{1}
and
A^{l}_{2}

we can write the
l
th summand as

\displaystyle-(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}+A_{1}^{l}(s)\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)+A_{2}^{l}(s)u_{s}.

(4.27)

Similarly we can write the last line of (
4.26
)
as

\displaystyle-(I^{j}_{s}-p^{j}_{s})\left(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}\right)+(\bar{p}^{j}_{s}-p^{j}_{s})\left(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle+3I^{j}_{s}\left(\bar{x}_{j}-x_{j}\right)+I^{j}_{s}\left(\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}-\frac{2x_{j}^{2}}{w_{j}-x_{j}}\right).

Linearising the final term on the line above
by writing
\bar{x}_{j}^{2}=\bar{x}_{j}x_{j}+\bar{x}_{j}(\bar{x}_{j}-x_{j})
,
and
x_{j}^{2}=x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}+x_{j}(x_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})
,
we find that it is equal to

\frac{2I^{j}_{s}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}{(w_{j}-x_{j})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}u_{s}+I^{j}_{s}\left(\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}+\frac{2x_{j}}{w_{j}-x_{j}}\right)(\bar{x}_{j}-x_{j}).

On
H_{j}(n)
we have
|w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s)|\geq A^{-1}\sqrt{t-s}
for all
s<t

and
|\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s)|\geq A^{-1}\sqrt{t-s}
,
so we can bound all the terms involving

|\bar{p}^{j}_{s}-p^{j}_{s}|
and
|\bar{x}_{j}-x_{j}|

by a constant multiple
of
\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}
.
Therefore the last line of (
4.26
)
can be rewritten

\displaystyle-(I^{j}_{s}-p^{j}_{s})\left(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}\right)+\frac{2I^{j}_{s}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}{(w_{j}-x_{j})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}u_{s}+A^{j}(s)\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}

(4.28)

for a bounded function
A^{j}
.
Substituting (
4.27
)
and (
4.28
) back into (
4.26
),
and noting that the
A^{l}_{1}
terms can be absorbed
by the
A^{j}
term, we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{s}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2I^{j}_{s}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}{(w_{j}-x_{j})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}+\sum_{l\not=j}A^{l}_{2}(s)-1\right)u_{s}+A^{j}(s)\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}-\sum_{l\not=j}\left((I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s})\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}\right)-(I^{j}_{s}-p^{j}_{s})\left(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}}\right).

Let

\gamma(s):=\frac{2I^{j}_{s}x_{j}\bar{x}_{j}}{(w_{j}-x_{j})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}+\sum_{l\not=j}A^{l}_{2}(s)-1

and let
\alpha(s)
denote the remaining terms in
(
4.29
),

\alpha(s):=A^{j}(s)\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}+\sum_{l=1}^{k}C^{l}_{s}(I^{l}_{s}-p^{l}_{s}),

where
C^{j}_{s}:=-(3\bar{x}_{j}+\frac{2\bar{x}_{j}^{2}}{\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j}})

and
C^{l}_{s}:=-\frac{2\bar{x}_{l}^{2}(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{j})}{(\bar{x}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})(\bar{w}_{j}-\bar{x}_{l})}
for
l\not=j
.

The ODE becomes

\dot{u}_{s}=\gamma(s)u_{s}+\alpha(s)
.
Let

Y(s):=\int_{s}^{t}\alpha(r)\,\mathrm{d}r,

then we can write
u
using an integrating factor as

u_{s}=-Y(s)+\int_{s}^{t}Y(r)\gamma(r)\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{r}\gamma(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\!\mathrm{d}r,

and so by the triangle inequality

\displaystyle|u_{s}|\leq|Y(s)|+\int_{s}^{t}|Y(r)||\gamma(r)|\exp\left(-\int_{s}^{r}\mathrm{Re}\gamma(v)\,\mathrm{d}v\right)\!\mathrm{d}r.

(4.30)

Note that
since
\arg(w_{j}(s)-x_{j}(s))-\arg x_{j}(s)\to 0

and

\arg(\bar{w}_{j}(s)-\bar{x}_{j}(s))-\arg\bar{x}_{j}(s)\to 0

as
s\uparrow t
,
the real part of
\gamma(s)
is bounded below,
and so (
4.30
)
gives us a simpler bound

\displaystyle|u_{s}|\leq|Y(s)|+A\int_{s}^{t}|Y(r)||\gamma(r)|\mathrm{d}r.

Conditional on
H_{j}(n)
we almost surely have
|\gamma(s)|\leq A/(t-s)

for all
s\in[0,t)
,
so
|Y(s)|\leq X(s)
for the
X
defined in
Lemma
4.12
,
and the result follows.
∎

Proof of Corollary
4.13
.

Let
M_{s}^{l}:=\int_{s}^{t}C_{r}^{l}(I^{l}_{r}-p^{l}_{s})\,\mathrm{d}s
.
All
k
terms
|M_{s}^{l}|
can be bounded in exactly the same way,
so write
M_{s}=M^{j}_{s}
and assume that

X(s)=A\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(r)}{\sqrt{t-r}}\,\mathrm{d}r+|M(s)|
.
Let
G(s):=\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}
.
We have the bounds

\displaystyle|u_{0}|

\displaystyle\leq|X(0)|+A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|X(s)|}{t-s}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq A\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}|G(s)|\,\mathrm{d}s}_{(A1)}+\underbrace{|M_{0}|}_{(A2)}+A\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\left(\int_{s}^{t}|G(r)|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{d}s}_{(A3)}+A\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\left|M_{s}\right|}{t-s}\mathrm{d}s}_{(A4)},

(4.31)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|u_{s}|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|X(s)|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s+A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|X(r)|}{t-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|X(s)|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s

(4.32)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}+A\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{t-r}\int_{r}^{t}|G(v)|\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s}_{(B1)}+A\underbrace{\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|M_{r}|}{t-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s}_{(B2)}.

We will first bound the terms involving
G
:
(A1), (A3) and (B1).
Almost by definition,
(A1) is bounded by
A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{\sqrt{t-s}}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{(t-s)^{51/100}}\,\mathrm{d}s
.

By Tonelli’s theorem, (A3) in (
4.31
)
can be written

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|G(r)|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{r}\frac{|G(r)|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}|G(r)|\log\frac{t}{t-r}\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(r)\frac{\log\frac{t}{t-r}}{\sqrt{t-r}}\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(r)}{(t-r)^{51/100}}\mathrm{d}r,

where the last inequality holds,
modifying the constant
A
,
because

\log\frac{t}{t-r}\leq 100\left(\frac{t}{t-r}\right)^{1/100}

for all
r\in(0,t)
.

We can also bound (B1) in (
4.32
)
by repeated applications of Tonelli’s theorem:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{t-r}\int_{r}^{t}|G(v)|\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{s=0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{r=s}^{t}\frac{1}{t-r}\int_{v=r}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(v)}{\sqrt{t-v}}\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=A\int_{v=0}^{t}\int_{r=0}^{v}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(v)}{\sqrt{t-v}}\frac{1}{t-r}\int_{s=0}^{r}\frac{1}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle=A\int_{v=0}^{t}\int_{r=0}^{v}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(v)}{\sqrt{t-v}}\frac{1}{t-r}\log\frac{t}{t-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{v=0}^{t}\int_{r=0}^{v}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(v)}{\sqrt{t-v}}\frac{t^{1/100}}{(t-r)^{1+\frac{1}{100}}}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}v

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(v)}{(t-v)^{51/100}}\,\mathrm{d}v.

Now we will bound the terms
in (
4.31
) and (
4.32
)
involving
M_{s}
:
(A2), (A4) and (B2).
First write
I^{j}_{r}-p^{j}_{r}=I^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{r}=(I^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor})+(p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{r})
.
Let
M_{s}^{\prime}=\int_{s}^{t}(I^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor})C_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r
,
then by Lemma
4.4

we have
|p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{r}|\leq A\mathbf{c}

for all
r\in[0,T]
, so

\displaystyle|M_{s}-M_{s}^{\prime}|=\left|\int_{s}^{t}(p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{r})C_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}|p^{j}_{\mathbf{c}\left\lfloor r/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}-p^{j}_{r}||C_{r}|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{s}^{t}\mathbf{c}(t-r)^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq A\mathbf{c}\sqrt{t-s}.

Therefore in all of (A2), (A4) and (B2)
we can replace
M
with
M^{\prime}

by adding an extra error term bounded by
A\sqrt{t}\,\mathbf{c}
.
Recall that
t=n\mathbf{c}
.
If
t-s<10\mathbf{c}
then we have the deterministic bound

|M^{\prime}_{s}|\leq A\int_{0}^{t-s}r^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\leq A\sqrt{t-s}
.
If
t-s\geq 10\mathbf{c}
,
it is easy to bound the second moment of
|M^{\prime}_{s}|

since
(I^{j}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{m\mathbf{c}})_{m\geq 1}

are martingale increments:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(|M_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}\,|\,H_{j}(n))\leq A\mathbb{E}(|M_{s}^{\prime}|^{2})

\displaystyle=A\sum_{m=\left\lfloor s/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}((I^{j}_{m\mathbf{c}}-p^{j}_{m\mathbf{c}})^{2})\left(\int_{m\mathbf{c}\vee s}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}|C_{r}|\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A\sum_{m=\left\lfloor s/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}^{n-1}\left(\int_{m\mathbf{c}\vee s}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}}(n\mathbf{c}-r)^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=A\sum_{m=0}^{n-\left\lfloor s/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor-1}\left(\int_{m\mathbf{c}}^{(m+1)\mathbf{c}\wedge(t-s)}r^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq A\mathbf{c}\log\left(\frac{t-s}{\mathbf{c}}\right)\leq A\mathbf{c}\log(1/\mathbf{c}).

For
s\in[0,t-10\mathbf{c}]
, define the event

\displaystyle E_{s}:=\{|M_{s}^{\prime}|\leq\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{4}{R}}\},

then by Markov’s inequality

1-\mathbb{P}(E_{s}|H_{j}(n))\leq\mathbb{P}(|M_{s}^{\prime}|^{2}>\mathbf{c}^{1-\frac{8}{R}}|H_{j}(n))\leq\frac{A\mathbf{c}\log(1/\mathbf{c})}{\mathbf{c}^{1-\frac{8}{R}}}\leq A\mathbf{c}^{\frac{8}{R}}\log(1/\mathbf{c}).

We will choose a particular sequence
(s_{m})_{1\leq m\leq N_{t}}

in
[0,t-10\mathbf{c}]
such that
\bigcap_{m=1}^{N_{t}}E_{s_{m}}

holds with high probability,
and on this event
|M_{s}^{\prime}|
is bounded uniformly on
[0,t-10\mathbf{c}]
.
Let
r_{1}=10\mathbf{c}
and
s_{1}=t-r_{1}
.
for
m\geq 1
set
r_{m+1}=r_{m}+\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{R}}\sqrt{r_{m}}
and
s_{m}=\max(t-r_{m},0)
.
Then
r_{m+1}-r_{m}\geq\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{R}}\sqrt{r_{m}-r_{m-1}}
,
and so
r_{m+1}-r_{m}\geq\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2^{m}}+\frac{1}{R}(2-\frac{1}{2^{m}})}\geq\mathbf{c}^{\frac{4}{R}}
for
m
greater than some constant
m_{R}
.
Hence
N_{t}:=\min\{m:r_{m}\geq t\}\leq A\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{4}{R}}
.
Let
s\in(s_{m+1},s_{m})
for some
m\geq 1
,
then

\displaystyle|M^{\prime}_{s}-M^{\prime}_{s_{m}}|\leq\int_{s}^{s_{m}}|C_{r}|\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\leq(s_{m}-s_{m+1})\sup_{r\in[s_{m+1},s_{m}]}|C_{r}|

\displaystyle\leq A(r_{m+1}-r_{m})r_{m}^{-1/2}

\displaystyle=A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}.

Therefore, on
\bigcap_{m=1}^{N_{t}}E_{s_{m}}
,

|M^{\prime}_{s}|\leq A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}
for all
s\in[0,t-10\mathbf{c}]
,
and by the union bound
\mathbb{P}(\bigcap_{m=1}^{N_{t}}E_{s_{m}}|H_{j}(n))\geq 1-N_{t}A\mathbf{c}^{8/R}\log(1/\mathbf{c})\geq 1-A\mathbf{c}^{4/R}\log(1/\mathbf{c})
.

On this event we can bound (A4) of (
4.31
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{|M_{s}^{\prime}|}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t-10\mathbf{c}}\frac{\mathbf{c}^{1/R}}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s+\int_{t-10\mathbf{c}}^{t}\frac{A\sqrt{t-s}}{t-s}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}\log(1/\mathbf{c})+A\mathbf{c}^{1/2},

and similarly (B2) of (
4.32
) has a bound

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{|M_{r}^{\prime}|}{t-r}\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}s\leq A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}(\log(1/\mathbf{c}))^{2}+A\mathbf{c}^{1/2}.

We can then use these bounds in (
4.31
)
and (
4.32
) to complete our proof.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.8
.

The five results from Lemma
4.9

to Corollary
4.13

show that for a given
n\leq\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor
,
the bound
\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{(t-s)^{51/100}}\mathrm{d}s+A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}(\log(1/\mathbf{c}))^{2}

holds with high (conditional) probability if
H_{j}(n)
occurs.
Let
B(t)
be the event that this bound holds at time
t
.
We will derive the claimed global bound
from the fact that
\mathbb{P}(B(n\mathbf{c})|H_{j}(n))
is close to 1
for fixed
n
.

For simplicity assume that
\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}

and
T/\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}}
are integers.
For
i\in\{0,\dots,T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1\}
the
probability that
H_{j}(n)
does not hold for any

n\in\{i{\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}}+1,i{\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}}+2,\dots,(i+1){\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}}\}

is bounded by
(1-p)^{\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}}

for a positive constant
p
,
and so the expected number of such
i
is less than
T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}(1-p)^{\mathbf{c}^{-1+\frac{1}{2R}}}\ll 1
.
Therefore by Markov’s inequality there exists,
with very high probability,
a sequence of times
\tau_{0}<\tau_{1}<\dots<\tau_{T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1}

such that for each
i
,

\tau_{i}\in(i\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}},(i+1)\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}}]
,

\tau_{i}/\mathbf{c}
is an integer,

H_{j}(\tau_{i}/\mathbf{c})
holds,
and
\tau_{i+1}-\tau_{i}\leq 2\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}}
.
Since
\mathbb{P}(B(n\mathbf{c})|H_{j}(n))\geq 1-A\mathbf{c}^{4/R}\log(1/\mathbf{c})

for a fixed
n
,
the conditional probability of

\bigcap_{i=0}^{T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1}B(\tau_{i})

given
(\tau_{i})_{0\leq i\leq T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1}

is at least
1-A\mathbf{c}^{\frac{7}{2R}}\log(1/\mathbf{c})
.
Therefore with high probability we have a sequence of times

(\tau_{i})_{0\leq i\leq T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1}

as above such that
B(\tau_{i})
holds for each
i
.

Let
t\in(\tau_{i},\tau_{i+1})
for some
i\leq T\mathbf{c}^{-\frac{1}{2R}}-1
.
Then by Proposition
4.4
,

\displaystyle\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(t)

\displaystyle=\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(\tau_{i})+(\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(t)-\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(\tau_{i}))

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(\tau_{i})+A(\tau_{i+1}-\tau_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(\tau_{i})+A\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}}

\displaystyle\leq A\int_{0}^{\tau_{i}}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{(\tau_{i}-s)^{51/100}}\mathrm{d}s+A\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}},

where the extra
A\mathbf{c}^{1/R}(\log(1/\mathbf{c}))^{2}
term
is absorbed by the
A\mathbf{c}^{1/2R}
.
Then since
t>\tau_{i}
,
we have
(\tau_{i}-s)^{-51/100}<(t-s)^{-51/100}

for all
s\in(0,\tau_{i})
,
and so

\delta_{\mathrm{p}}(t)\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{(t-s)^{51/100}}\mathrm{d}s+A\mathbf{c}^{\frac{1}{2R}}

for all
t\in[0,T]
, as claimed.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Propositions
4.7

and
4.8

give us
\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(t)\leq A\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(s)}{(t-s)^{51/100}}\,\mathrm{d}s+A\mathbf{c}^{1/2R}

for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Using a singular version of Grönwall’s inequality
(see
[
15
, Corollary 2]
),
this implies

\delta_{\mathrm{total}}(T)\leq A\mathbf{c}^{1/2R}
as required.
∎

5
Proof of main theorem

We did most of the hard work in Section
4
,
and will now bring together the intermediate convergence results
to show convergence of the ALE to the LPM.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\xi_{t}
be the driving function of the ALE,
and
\bar{\mu}_{t}
the driving measure of the LPM.
To show that the two converge in distribution,
we will show that
d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]})\to 0

in probability as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.

Note that another way of writing (
3.1
)
for the auxiliary process is

\displaystyle\Phi_{n}^{*}=R_{\delta_{1}+\dots+\delta_{n}}\circ\left(\Phi_{0}\circ f_{\theta_{1}^{*}-\delta_{1}}\circ f_{\theta^{*}_{2}-(\delta_{1}+\delta_{2})}\circ\dots\circ f_{\theta^{*}_{n}-(\delta_{1}+\dots+\delta_{n})}\right)\circ R_{-(\delta_{1}+\dots+\delta_{n})}

So let
\xi_{t}^{*}
be the driving measure for the angle sequence

(\theta^{*}_{n}-(\delta_{1}+\dots+\delta_{n}))_{n\leq\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor}
.
By Theorem
3.1
we have a coupling between

\xi^{*}
and
\xi
,
such that if we define the event

E_{1}=\{\tau_{D}\wedge\tau_{\mathrm{coupling}}>\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor\}
,
then
\mathbb{P}(E_{1})\geq 1-A\mathbf{c}^{1/2}
.
On
E_{1}
note that

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}|\xi_{t}-\xi^{*}_{t}|\leq\left(\frac{T}{\mathbf{c}}+2\right)D.

Next, to pass from the auxiliary model to the multinomial model,
let
\xi^{\mathrm{multi}}_{t}

be the driving measure of the multinomial model,
define the event

E_{2}=\{\tau_{\not=}>\left\lfloor T/\mathbf{c}\right\rfloor\}
,
and note that on
E_{2}
the models coincide:

\xi^{\mathrm{multi}}_{t}=\xi^{*}_{t}
for all
t\in[0,T]
.
By Corollary
3.8
,

\mathbb{P}(E_{2})\geq 1-AT\mathbf{c}^{-2}D
.

Finally, by Corollary
4.3
,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi^{\mathrm{multi}}_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]})\to 0

in probability as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
.

Then by the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]})

\displaystyle\leq d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\delta_{\xi^{*}_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]})

\displaystyle\phantom{\leq}+d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi^{*}_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}),

and on
E_{1}\cap E_{2}
this is bounded by

\displaystyle T\left(\frac{T}{\mathbf{c}}+2\right)\!D+d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi^{\mathrm{multi}}_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]}).

Since
\mathbf{c}^{-1}D=o(1)
,
this upper bound tends to zero in probability as
\mathbf{c}\to 0
,
and
\mathbb{P}(E_{1}\cap E_{2})\to 1
,
giving us

d_{\mathrm{BW}}(\delta_{\xi_{t}}\otimes m_{[0,T]},\bar{\mu}_{t}\otimes m_{[0,T]})\overset{p}{\to}0
as required.
∎

A note on the Independent domination polynomial of zero divisor graph of rings
Bilal Ahmad Rather

Abstract
In this note we consider the independent domination polynomial problem along with their unimodal and log-concave properties which were earlier studied by Gürsoy, Ülker and Gürsoy (Soft Comp. 2022). We show that the independent domination polynomial of zero divisor graphs of ℤ n \displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n} for n ∈ { p ​ q , p 2 ​ q , p ​ q ​ r , p α } \displaystyle n\in\{pq,p^{2}q,pqr,p^{\alpha}\} where p , q , r \displaystyle p,q,r are primes with 2 < p < q < r \displaystyle 2<p<q<r are not unimodal thereby contradicting the main result of Gürsoy, Ülker and Gürsoy [ 13 ] . Besides the authors show that the zero of the independent domination polynomial of these graphs have only real zero and used concept of Newton’s inequalities to establish the log-concave property for the afore said polynomials. We show that these polynomials have complex zeros and the technique of Newton’s inequalities are not applicable. Finally, by definition of log-concave, we prove that these polynomials are log-concave and fix the flaws in Theorem 10 of Gürsoy, Ülker and Gürsoy [ 13 ] .



Keywords: Zero divisor graphs, commutative ring, independent domination polynomial; unimodal; log-concave; Computing

Next, we will check the unimodal property of the independent domination polynomial of the zero divisor graphs for
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{pq},\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q},\mathbb{Z}_{pqr}
and
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{p^{\alpha}}.

We recall that the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}})
is
\displaystyle(p-1)x,
which is clearly unimodal as sequence of its coefficients form an increasing sequence of non-negative integers. From now onwards, whenever we consider
\displaystyle n=p^{\alpha}

\displaystyle p>2
to avoid
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}})

Next for
\displaystyle D_{i}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2p}),x)=x+x^{p-1}
to be unimodal, we have
\displaystyle a_{0}=0\leq a_{1}=1
which must be less than or equal to coefficient of
\displaystyle x^{p-1}
, which can happen if and only if
\displaystyle p-1=2
as its coefficient is already one. Otherwise if
\displaystyle p>2,
then some coefficients between the linear term and
\displaystyle(p-1)
-th must be zero. Which will imply that
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2p}),x))>2,
thereby contradicting the requirement for unimodal property. Also, by definition the log-concave property is satisfied trivially for
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}})

\displaystyle D_{i}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2p}),x)=x+x^{p-1},
we see that
\displaystyle 1=a_{1}^{2}\geq a_{0}a_{2}=0
and for
\displaystyle i\geq 2
, we get
\displaystyle 0=a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}=0
, which are always true, and it follows that
\displaystyle D_{i}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2p}),x)
is log-concave. We make these observation precise in the following result.

.

Let
\displaystyle p
be a prime. Then the following hold.

(i)

For prime
\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}})

(ii)

For prime
\displaystyle p>2

\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2p})

\displaystyle p=3.

The independent domination polynomial of

\displaystyle p<q

\displaystyle p=2

Proposition 2.4
.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q})
with primes
\displaystyle p>q
is log-concave but not unimodal.

Proof.

From Theorem 5
[
13

\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p^{2}q})
is

D_{i}(G,x)=x^{\phi(q)+\phi(pq)}+\phi(p)x^{\phi(p^{2})+1}+x^{\phi(p^{2})+\phi(pq)}.

For
\displaystyle p=3
and
\displaystyle q=2,
the above polynomial becomes

D_{i}(G,x)=x^{3}+2x^{7}+x^{8},

and it is clear that
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))=2
, since the change of decreasing (increasing) directions of coefficients is two. So, it is not unimodal. Now, for other values of
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q
, if
\displaystyle\phi(q)+\phi(pq)<\phi(p^{2})+1
, then we obtain
\displaystyle p(q-1)<p^{2}-p<p^{2}-p+1
and from it, we get
\displaystyle q<p
, which is true. Also,
\displaystyle\phi(p^{2})+1\leq\phi(p^{2})+\phi(pq)
. Thus, the coefficients of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
increase, then decrease and then again increase, then decrease. Thus, it implies that
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))>2
and
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
is not unimodal. It is easy to see that the coefficients of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
satisfies the conditions
\displaystyle a_{i}^{2}\geq a_{i-1}a_{i+1}
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i\leq\phi(p^{2})+\phi(pq)
and it implies the log-concave property of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x).

Proposition 2.5
.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{pqr})

\displaystyle|\phi(pq)-\phi(r)|\neq 2.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{pqr})

\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{pqr}

\displaystyle p<q<r

(2.3)

If
\displaystyle p\neq 2,
then all the exponents of the polynomial given in (
2.3
) are Euler function, which are even and so is their sum. Thus,
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))=4
as coefficients increase four times and decrease three times. So,
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
is not unimodal. If
\displaystyle p=2
, then exponent
\displaystyle\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(p)
in (
2.3
) is odd and other coefficients are even. In this case
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))
is at least three and it implies that
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
is not unimodal. However depending on
\displaystyle p<q<r
, it is clear that either
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
or
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)
is the largest exponent. Also, if
\displaystyle\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(p)<\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)
with
\displaystyle p=2
, then
\displaystyle\phi(q)>\phi(p)=1
and
\displaystyle\phi(qr)>\phi(2r)=\phi(pr)=\phi(r)
. It implies that there is difference of more two between
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)
and
\displaystyle\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(p).
Thus, in this case we obtain
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))=4
and polynomial is not unimodal. Next, we turn our attention toward the log-concave property of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
. It is clear that the minimum value of
\displaystyle r
is
\displaystyle 5
, so
\displaystyle\phi(r)-\phi(q)\geq 2
and
\displaystyle\phi(pr)\sim\phi(p)(\phi(q)+2)>\phi(pq)
. It implies that the difference between
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)
and
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
is more than two. Likewise, same is true for
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)
and
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq).
However, depending on the values of
\displaystyle p,q,r
, sometimes
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
is larger and sometimes
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)
is larger. Therefore,
\displaystyle x^{\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(p)}+x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)}+x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)}
or
\displaystyle x^{\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(p)}+x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pq)+\phi(q)}+x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)}
satisfies the property of log-concave. We are concerned with coefficients between
\displaystyle x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)}
and
\displaystyle x^{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)}.
If
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
and
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)
differ by two, then

a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)+1}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)}a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)+2}=1,

where
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)=\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)+2
or

a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+1}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)}a_{\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+2}=1,

where
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)+2=\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r).

So, with the above observation, the polynomial given in (
2.3
) is log-concave if and only if the difference between
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
and
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)
is away from two.
∎

As classified by the above result, we mention some of the classes of the independent domination polynomials of zero divisor graphs of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{pqr}
which are not log-concave.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2\cdot 3\cdot 5})
is

\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)=x^{7}+x^{12}+x^{14}+x^{16},

which is not log-concave, since
\displaystyle a_{15}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{14}\cdot a_{16}=1.

2.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2\cdot 5\cdot 7})
is

\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)=x^{11}+x^{32}+x^{34}+x^{36},

which is not log-concave, since
\displaystyle a_{35}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{34}\cdot a_{36}=1.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{2\cdot 3\cdot 7})

which is not log-concave, since
\displaystyle a_{21}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{20}\cdot a_{22}=1.

The independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{3\cdot 5\cdot 7})

\displaystyle a_{43}^{2}=0\ngeq a_{42}\cdot a_{44}=1.

For the above classes of polynomials, we see that
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(r)
is bigger or sometimes
\displaystyle\phi(qr)+\phi(pr)+\phi(pq)

Next, we discuss the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
when
\displaystyle n
is a prime power. We will state the result without proof, since it is similar to Theorem 7 given in

]
. We recall number theoretic facts:
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}p^{i}=p^{\ell}-1
, where
\displaystyle p
is prime and
\displaystyle\ell
is a positive integer and for relatively primes
\displaystyle p
and
\displaystyle q
, we have
\displaystyle\phi(pq)=\phi(p)\phi(q)
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
be a zero divisor graph of
\displaystyle\mathbb{Z}_{n}
. Then the following hold.

(i)

If
\displaystyle n=p^{2m},
where
\displaystyle p
is prime and
\displaystyle m
is a positive integer, then the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi(p^{i})\Big(x^{1+p^{2m-1}-p^{2m-1-(i-1)}}\Big)+x^{p^{2m-1}-p^{m}}.

(ii)

If
\displaystyle n=p^{2m+1},
where
\displaystyle p
is prime and
\displaystyle m
is a positive integer, then the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G
is given by

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\phi(p^{i})\Big(x^{1+p^{2m}-p^{2m-(i-1)}}\Big)+x^{p^{2m}-p^{m}}.

The following results gives the non unimodal and log-concave property of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
when
\displaystyle n
is prime power.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
\displaystyle n
is the prime power, then the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is log-concave but not unimodal.

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle n=p^{2m+1}
, where
\displaystyle p
is prime and
\displaystyle m
is a positive integer. Then the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
is

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\phi(p)x+\phi(p^{2})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m})}+\phi(p^{3})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m})+\phi(p^{2m-1})}+\dots+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\phi(p^{m-1})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m})+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+3})}+\phi(p^{m})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m})+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+2})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+x^{\phi(p^{2m})+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+1})}.

All the exponents of the above polynomial are odd except last exponent which is even. So,
\displaystyle 2\leq\eta(D_{i}(G,x))
and it follows that
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
is not unimodal. In fact
\displaystyle\eta(D_{i}(G,x))=m+1
as all the coefficients of
\displaystyle x^{1+p^{2m}-p^{2m-(i-1)}}
are strictly increasing, for
\displaystyle i=1,2,\dots,m
and
\displaystyle\phi(p^{2m})+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+1})=p^{2m}-p^{m}
is strictly greater than
\displaystyle 1+p^{2m}-p^{2m-(i-1)}
, for
\displaystyle i=1,2,\dots,m.
So the coefficients of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
increase precisely
\displaystyle m+1
times.

For
\displaystyle n=p^{2m},
the independent domination polynomial of
\displaystyle G\cong\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
in standard form is

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\phi(p)x+\phi(p^{2})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m-1})}+\phi(p^{3})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})}+\dots+

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad\phi(p^{m-2})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+3})}+\phi(p^{m-1})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+2})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\quad+x^{\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+1})}+\phi(p^{m})x^{1+\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+1})}.

As in odd case, the exponents of the above expression are odd except
\displaystyle\phi(p^{2m-1})+\phi(p^{2m-2})+\dots+\phi(p^{m+1})=p^{2m-1}-p^{m}
and coefficients increase precisely
\displaystyle m+1
times. From the representation of coefficients and exponents of
\displaystyle D_{i}(G,x)
, it is easy to see that
\displaystyle 1+p^{2m-1}-p^{2m-1-(i-1)}
and
\displaystyle 1+p^{2m}-p^{2m-(i-1)}
have large gaps for respective values of
\displaystyle i.
Thus by definition, the log-concave property holds trivially.
∎

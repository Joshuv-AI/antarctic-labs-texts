Abstract
The bipartite Turán number of a graph H H , denoted by ex ⁡ ( m , n , H ) \mathrm{ex}(m,n;H) , is the maximum number of edges in any bipartite graph G = ( X , Y , E ) G=(X,Y;E) with | X | = m |X|=m and | Y | = n |Y|=n which does not contain H H as a subgraph. In this paper, we determined ex ⁡ ( m , n , F ℓ ) \mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell}) for arbitrary ℓ \ell and appropriately large n n with comparing to m m and ℓ \ell , where F ℓ F_{\ell} is a linear forest which consists of ℓ \ell vertex disjoint paths. Moreover, the extremal graphs have been characterized. Furthermore, these results are used to obtain the maximum spectral radius of bipartite graphs which does not contain F ℓ F_{\ell} as a subgraph and characterize all extremal graphs which attain the maximum spectral radius. AMS Classification: 05C35; 05C50; 05C38 Key words: bipartite Turán number; spectral radius; linear forest; bipartite graph.

For two vertex disjoint graphs

,

.

1.1
The bipartite Turán extremal problem

For two graphs
G
and
H
, we say that a graph
G
is
H
-free
if it does
not contain a subgraph isomorphic to
H
, i.e.,
G
contains no copy of
H
.
The
Turán number
, denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
, of a graph
H
is the maximum number of edges in
H
-free graphs on
n
vertices. It is a fundamental problem of extremal graph theory to determine the value
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
for a graph
H
, and which is called the
Turán problem
. The Erdős-Stone-Simonovits theorem (see
[
7
,
8
]
) stated that

\mathrm{ex}(n,H)=\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi(H)-1}\right)\binom{n}{2}+o(n^{2})
, where
\chi(H)
is the chromatic number of
H
. Hence it is a challenge and notorious problem to determine the order of magnitude of
\mathrm{ex}(n,H)
for a bipartite graph
H
. For a bipartite graph
H
, the bipartite Turán number, denoted by
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;H)
, of a bipartite graph
H
is the maximum number of edges in an
H
-free bipartite graph
G=(X,Y;E)
with
|X|=m
,
|Y|=n
and
m\leq n
. The problem of determine the value
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;H)
is called the
bipartite Turán problem
, or the
Zarakiewicz problem
(may see
[
2
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
), which is closed interconnected to the Turán problem. Füredi proved that
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;K_{s,t})\leq(s-t+1)^{1/t}nm^{1-1/t}+tm^{2-2/t}+tn
for
m\geq s
,
n\geq t
,
s\geq t\geq 1
. Some recent results and progress on
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;H)
for bipartite graphs
H
can be referred to
[
1
,
24
,
28
]
and references therein.

In 1959, Erdős and Gallai in
[
6
]
proved the following pioneering result, which opens a new subject for bipartite graphs.

Theorem 1.1
.

(Erdős and Gallai
[
6
]
).
Let
n\geq k
. Then
\mathrm{ex}(n,P_{k})\leq(k-2)n/2
.

In 1984, Gyárfás, Rousseau and Schelp determined
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{k})
and characterized all bipartite extremal graphs for all values of
m,n
and
k
in
[
14
]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Gyárfás, Rousseau and Schelp
[
14
]
).
For three positive integers
k,m,n
with
n\geq m\geq p^{\prime}+1
and
p^{\prime}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1\geq 0
, the following holds.

(1) If
k
is even, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}p^{\prime}n,&\hbox{for $p^{\prime}+1\leq m\leq 2p^{\prime}$;}\\
p^{\prime}(m+n-2p^{\prime}),&\hbox{for $m\geq 2p^{\prime}+1$.}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the extremal graph is either
K_{p^{\prime},m-p^{\prime}}\cup K_{p^{\prime},n-p^{\prime}}
or
K_{p^{\prime},n}\cup\overline{K}_{m-p^{\prime}}
.

(2) If
k=3
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{3})=m.

Moreover, the extremal graph is
M_{m}\cup\overline{K}_{n-m}
.

(3) If
k=5
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{5})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}n+m,&\hbox{for $m=n$ and $n$ is even;}\\
n+m-1,&\hbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
n=m
is even, then the unique extremal graph consists of copies of
C_{4}
, otherwise the extremal graphs consist of copies of
C_{4}
and a star or a double star.

(4) If
k
is odd with
k\geq 7
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{k})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(p^{\prime}+1)^{2},&\hbox{for $m=n=p^{\prime}+1$;}\\
2(p^{\prime}+1)^{2},&\hbox{for $m=n=2p^{\prime}+2$;}\\
p^{\prime}(m+n-2p^{\prime}),&\hbox{for $m\geq 2p^{\prime}+3$ or $n>m=2p^{\prime}+2$;}\\
p^{\prime}n+m-p^{\prime},&\hbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Moreover, if
n=m=p^{\prime}+1
, then the unique extremal graph is
K_{p^{\prime}+1,p^{\prime}+1}
;
if
m=n=2p^{\prime}+2
, then the unique extremal graph is
2K_{p^{\prime}+1,p^{\prime}+1}
;
if
m\geq 2p^{\prime}+3
or
n>m=2p^{\prime}+2
, then the unique extremal graph is
K_{p^{\prime},n-p^{\prime}}\cup K_{p^{\prime},m-p^{\prime}}
;
otherwise, the unique extremal graph is
Z^{p^{\prime}}_{m,n}
.

Note that

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{k})=mn
for
n\geq m
and
1\leq m\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1
. Combining with Theorem
1.2
, we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
k\geq 2
and
p^{\prime}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1
.
If
n
is sufficiently larger than
m
, then
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;P_{k})\leq\max\{m,p^{\prime}(m+n-1)\}
.

A linear forest is a forest consisting of vertex disjoint paths.
Many researchers focused on determining the Turán numbers for a linear forest. For the rest of this paper, let
F_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{k_{i}}
be a linear forest with
{\ell}\geq 1
and

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{\ell}\geq 2
and
p=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lfloor k_{i}/2\rfloor-1
. In 2011, Gorgol
[
13
]
obtained a lower bound for
\mathrm{ex}(n,kP_{3})
by way of construction. Later,
Bushaw and Kettle
[
3
]
determined the exact value
\mathrm{ex}(n,kP_{\ell})
for sufficient large
n
, which is improved by Lidický, Liu, and Palmer
[
18
]
who determined the Turán numbers for linear forests
F_{\ell}
when the order of a graph is sufficiently large.

Theorem 1.4
.

(Lidický, Liu and Palmer
[
18
]
).
Let
F_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{k_{i}}
be a linear forest with
{\ell}\geq 1
and

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{\ell}\geq 2
and
p=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lfloor k_{i}/2\rfloor-1
. If there exists an
1\leq i\leq\ell
with
k_{i}\neq 3
, then for sufficiently large
n
,

\mathrm{ex}(n,F_{\ell})=\binom{p}{2}+p(n-p)+c,

where
c=1
if all
k_{i}
are odd and
c=0
otherwise.
Moreover, if
c=1
then equality holds if and only if
G=K_{p}\nabla(\overline{K}_{n-p-2}\cup K_{2})
.
Otherwise, the equality holds if and only if
G=K_{p}\nabla\overline{K}_{n-p}
.

Later, Campos and Lopes
[
4
]
, and Yuan and Zhang
[
26
]
, independently, determined the exact Turán number for
kP_{3}
.
Very recently, Yuan and Zhang
[
27
]
determined the exact Turán number for even liner forest which consists of paths on even number of vertices.

In this paper, motivated by the above results, we determine the bipartite Turán number
\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})
for a linear forest
F_{\ell}
for large
n
and characterize the bipartite extremal graphs. The first main result of this paper can be stated as follow.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
F_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{k_{i}}
be a linear forest with
{\ell}\geq 1
and

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{\ell}\geq 2
and
p=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lfloor k_{i}/2\rfloor-1
.
If
p+1\leq m
and
n
is sufficiently larger with compare to
p
and
m
, then the following holds.

(1). If not all
k_{1},k_{2},\ldots,k_{\ell}
are odd, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}pn,&\hbox{for $p+1\leq m\leq 2p$;}\\
p\left(n-\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor+1\right)+\left(m-p\right)\left(\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor-1\right),&\hbox{for $m\geq 2p+1$}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the extremal graphs are
K_{p,n}\cup\overline{K}_{m-p}
for
p\leq m\leq 2p-1
,
K_{p,i}\cup K_{p,n-i}
with
0\leq i\leq\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor-1
for
m=2p
and
K_{p,n-\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor+1}\cup K_{m-p,\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor-1}
for
m\geq 2p+1
.

(2). If all
k_{i}
’s are odd and
k_{\ell}\neq 3,5,7
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}pn+m-p,&\hbox{for $p+1\leq m\leq 2p$;}\\
p\left(n-\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor+1\right)+\left(m-p\right)\left(\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor-1\right),&\hbox{for $m\geq 2p+1$,}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for
\ell\geq 2
, the extremal graphs are
Z_{m,n}^{p}
for
p+1\leq m\leq 2p
and
K_{p,n-\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor+1}\cup K_{m-p,\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor-1}
for
m\geq 2p+1
.

(3). If all
k_{i}
’s are odd and
k_{\ell}=3
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}pn+m-p,&\hbox{for $k_{1}=\cdots=k_{\ell}=3$;}\\
pn+1,&\hbox{otherwise,}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the unique extremal graphs are the graph obtained from
K_{p,n}
by joining
m-p
independent edges connecting new
m-p
isolated vertices to
m-p
vertices with degree
p
in
K_{p,n}
respectively for
k_{1}=\cdots=k_{\ell}=3
, and
Z_{p+1,n}^{p}\cup\overline{K}_{m-p-1}
otherwise.

(4). If all
k_{i}
’s are odd and
k_{\ell}=5
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})=pn+m-p.

Moreover, the unique extremal graph is
Z_{m,n}^{p}
for
\ell\geq 2
.

(5). If all
k_{i}
’s are odd and
k_{\ell}=7
, then

\mathrm{ex}(m,n;F_{\ell})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}pn+m-p,&\hbox{for $p+1\leq m\leq 3p$;}\\
p\left(n-\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor+1\right)+\left(m-p\right)\left(\left\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\right\rfloor-1\right),&\hbox{for $m\geq 3p+1$,}\end{array}\right.

Moreover, for
\ell\geq 2
, the extremal graphs are
Z_{m,n}^{p}
for
p+1\leq m\leq 3p
and
K_{p,n-\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor+1}\cup K_{m-p,\lfloor k_{\ell}/2\rfloor-1}
for
m\geq 3p+1
.

The proof of Theorem
1.5
will be given in Section 3.

1.2
The spectral Turán extremal problem

The
adjacency matrix

A(G)
of
G
is the
n\times n
matrix
(a_{ij})
, where

a_{ij}=1
if
v_{i}
is adjacent to
v_{j}
, and
0
otherwise. The
spectral radius
of
G
, denoted by
\lambda(G)
, is the largest eigenvalue of
A(G)
, while the least eigenvalue of
A(G)
is denoted by
\lambda_{n}(G)
.
The spectral radius of a graph may at times give some valuable information about the structure of graphs.
For example, it is well-known that
\lambda(G)
is located between the average degree and the maximum degree of
G
, and the
chromatic number of a graph
G
is at most
\lambda(G)+1
.

The spectral version of the Turán problem may be stated as follows: How large is the spectral radius
\lambda(G)
of an
H
-free graph
G
of order
n
?
Nikiforov
[
21
]
gave an excellent survey for the spectral Turán problem.

In particular, Nikiforov
[
20
]
in 2010 determined the maximum spectral radius of a
P_{k}
-free graph of order
n
, which is spectral counterpart of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.6
.

(Nikiforov
[
20
]
)
Let
G
be a
P_{k}
-free graph of order
n\geq 2^{4k}
, where
k\geq 1
and
p^{\prime}=\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1
. Then the following holds.

(1) If
k
is even, then
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(K_{p^{\prime}}\nabla\overline{K}_{n-p^{\prime}})
and equality holds if and only if
G=K_{p^{\prime}}\nabla\overline{K}_{n-p^{\prime}}
.

(2) If
k
is odd, then
\lambda(G)\leq\lambda(K_{p^{\prime}}\nabla(\overline{K}_{n-p^{\prime}-2}\cup K_{2}))
and equality holds if and only if
G=K_{p^{\prime}}\nabla(\overline{K}_{n-p^{\prime}-2}\cup K_{2})
.

For linear forest, Chen, Liu, and Zhang
[
5
]
in 2019 determined the maximum spectral radius of an
F_{\ell}
-free graph of order
n
and characterized all the extremal graphs, which is the spectral counterpart of Theorems
1.4
also.

If the host graphs are complete bipartite graphs, then the problem of determining the maximum spectral radius of an
H
-free bipartite graphs is called the bipartite spectral Turán problem or the spectral Zarankiewicz problem (see
[
2
]
). In 2015, Zhai, Lin, and Gong
[
29
]
obtained the maximum spectral radius of a
P_{k}
-free bipartite graph and characterized all the extremal graphs, which is also the spectral counterpart of Theorem
1.2
.

Motivated by the above results, we can employ Theorem
1.5
to determine the maximum spectral radius of
F_{\ell}
-free bipartite graphs and characterize all the extremal graphs. As an application, we determine the minimum least eigenvalue
\lambda_{n}(G)
of an
F_{\ell}
-free bipartite graph and characterize all the extremal graphs. The second main result of this paper can be stated as follows.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
F_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{k_{i}}
be a linear forest with
{\ell}\geq 1
,

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{\ell}\geq 2
and
p=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lfloor k_{i}/2\rfloor-1\geq 1
.
If
G
is an
F_{\ell}
-free bipartite graph of sufficiently large order
n
with comparing to
p
, then

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{p(n-p)}

with equality if and only if
G=K_{p,n-p}
.

Corollary 1.8
.

Let
F_{\ell}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\ell}P_{k_{i}}
be a linear forest with
{\ell}\geq 1
,

k_{1}\geq\cdots\geq k_{\ell}\geq 2
and
p=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\lfloor k_{i}/2\rfloor-1\geq 1
.
If
G
is an
F_{\ell}
-free graph of sufficiently large order
n
with comparing to
p
, then

\lambda_{n}(G)\geq-\sqrt{p(n-p)}

with equality if and only if
G=K_{p,n-p}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
G=(A,B;E)
be a bipartite graph with
|A|=a
and
|B|=n
, where
a
is a constant. If
a\geq b
and
e(G)\geq bn
for sufficiently large
n
with comparing to
a
, then there exists a vertex set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with size
t=\lceil b\rceil
and a constant
c>0
such that the common neighbours of
A^{\prime}
is at least
cn
.

Proof.

Let
X
be the set of vertices of
B
with degree less than
t-1
. Since
e(G)\geq bn
, we have
(t-1)|X|+(n-|X|)a\geq bn
. Then
|X|\leq\frac{a-b}{a-t+1}n
, which implies that there are at least
n-|X|\geq\frac{b-t+1}{a-t+1}n
vertices of
B
with degree at least
t
. Note that there are
{a\choose t}

t
-sets in
A
. By the
pigeonhole principle, there exists a vertex set
A^{\prime}\subseteq A
with size
t
such that the common neighbours of
A
is at least
cn
, where
c=\frac{b-t+1}{(a-t+1){a\choose t}}>0.
The proof is complete.

\square

K_{p,n-p}
is spanning subgraph of
G
.

\square

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.
Let
G
be an
F_{\ell}
-free bipartite graph of order
n
with the maximum spectral radius. Since
K_{p,n-p}
is
F_{\ell}
-free,

\displaystyle\lambda(G)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda(K_{p,n-p})=\sqrt{p(n-p)}.

(14)

If
G
is connected, then by Lemma
4.1
,
K_{p,n-p}
is spanning subgraph of
G
. Since
G
is a bipartite graph, we have
G=K_{p,n-p}
.
Assume that
G
is not connected. Let
G_{1}
be a component of
G
such that
\lambda(G_{1})=\lambda(G)
. Set
n_{1}=|V(G_{1})|
.
Note that
G
is triangle-free. By Wilf theorem
[
25
, Theorem 2]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{n_{1}^{2}}{4}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\lambda^{2}(G_{1})=\lambda^{2}(G)\geq p(n-p),

which implies that
n_{1}\geq 2\sqrt{p(n-p)}
, i.e.,
n_{1}
is also sufficiently large. By Case 1,

\lambda(G)=\lambda(G_{1})\leq\sqrt{p(n_{1}-p)}<\sqrt{p(n-p)},

which is a contradiction.
This completes the proof.

\square

Proof of Corollary
1.8
.
By a result of Favaron, Mahéo and Saclé
[
9
]
, we have
\lambda_{n}(G)\geq\lambda_{n}(H)
for some spanning bipartite subgraph
H
. Moreover, the equality holds if and only if
G=H
, which can be deduced by its original proof.
By Theorem
1.7
,

\lambda(H)\leq\sqrt{p(n-p)}

with equality if and only if
H=K_{p,n-p}
.
Since the spectrum of a bipartite graph is symmetric
[
19
]
,

\lambda_{n}(H)\geq-\sqrt{p(n-p)}

with equality if and only if
H=K_{p,n-p}
. Thus
we have

\lambda_{n}(G)\geq-\sqrt{p(n-p)}

with equality if and only if
G=K_{p,n-p}
.

\square

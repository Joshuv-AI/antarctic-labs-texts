Global regularity of some axisymmetric, single-signed vorticity in any dimension
Deokwoo Lim

Abstract
We consider incompressible Euler equations in any dimension d ≥ 3 d\geq 3 imposing axisymmetric symmetry without swirl. While the global regularity of smooth flows in this setting has been well-known in d = 3 d=3 , the same question in higher dimensions d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 remains unsolved. Recently, global regularity for the case d = 4 d=4 with some extra decay assumption on vorticity is obtained by proving global estimate of the radial velocity. Now we prove that the vorticity with single-sign and a similar decay assumption is globally regular for any d ≥ 4 d\geq 4 . This is due to pointwise decay estimate of radial velocity in sufficiently large radial distance, which depends on time. The result is of confinement type for support growth, which is going back to Marchioro [ 18 ] and Iftimie–Sideris–Gamblin [ 13 ] for ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} . In particular, we follow the approach of Maffei–Marchioro [ 17 ] for d = 3 d=3 so that we generalize the confinement into any dimension.

1
Introduction

1.1
Axisymmetric, swirl-free Euler equations

We consider the incompressible Euler equations in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for dimensions
d\geq 3
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u+\nabla p&=0,\\
\nabla\cdot u&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

Here, our concern is
axisymmetric
and
swirl-free
flows, where the velocity field
u
takes the form

u=u^{r}(r,z)e^{r}+u^{z}(r,z)e^{z},

(1.2)

in the cylindrical coordinate system
(r,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\cdots,\theta_{d-3},\phi,z)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Now we denote the scalar vorticity
\omega
, which is defined as
\omega:=\partial_{z}u^{r}-\partial_{r}u^{z}
. Then the Euler equations (
1.1
) becomes the following vorticity equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\omega+u\cdot\nabla\omega

\displaystyle=\frac{(d-2)u^{r}}{r}\omega,

(1.3)

with

\begin{split}u^{r}&=-\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\partial_{z}\psi,\qquad u^{z}=\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\partial_{r}\psi,\end{split}

(1.5)

where
\psi
is the scalar stream function that is given in the next section.
In addition, the divergence-free condition in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
can be written as

\nabla\cdot u=\partial_{r}u^{r}+\partial_{z}u^{z}+\frac{d-2}{r}u^{r}=0.

Interestingly, if we define the
relative
vorticity as
\xi:=r^{-(d-2)}\omega
, the vorticity equation (
1.3
) and the divergence-free condition above lead
\xi
to satisfy the following simple transport equation

\partial_{t}\xi+u\cdot\nabla\xi=0,

(1.6)

which makes the quantity
\|\xi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
to be conserved in time for any
p\in[1,\infty]
.

1.2
Main result

In
d=3
, the global regularity of axisymmetric and swirl-free smooth solutions of the Euler equations (
1.1
) was established by Ukhovskii–Yudovich
[
21
]
(also see Danchin
[
7
]
).
The following theorem presents that the single-sign and the compact support of
\omega
implies global regularity of
\omega
for any
d\geq 3
. We assume
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
s>1+d/2
to use the local well-posedness (
[
14
,
15
,
19
]
) of the corresponding solution
u
of (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 3
, and assume that
\omega_{0}
is single-signed, compactly supported in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and satisfies
r^{-(d-2)}\omega_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then
the corresponding solution
\omega
of (
1.3
) is global in time.
In particular, for any
t\geq 0
, it satisfies

\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{(d-2)/(d+1)},

(1.7)

for some
C>0
depending only on
d
,
\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
\|r^{-(d-2)}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
, and
S_{0}:=\sup\{r>0:(r,z)\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,\omega_{0}\}
.

Remark 1.2
.

Theorem
1.1
is a direct consequence of the following estimate of the support size in the radial direction

S(t):=\sup_{s\in[0,t]}\sup\{r>0:(r,z)\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,\omega(s)\},

(1.8)

with
S(0)=S_{0}
:

S(t)\leq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\geq 0,

(1.9)

for some
C>0
, which depends only on the above four quantities. Indeed, using the conservation of
\|\xi(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|r^{-(d-2)}\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
in time and (
1.9
), we obtain (
1.7
):

\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\bigg\|\frac{\omega(t)}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot S(t)^{d-2}=\bigg\|\frac{\omega_{0}}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot S(t)^{d-2}\leq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{(d-2)/(d+1)}.

1.3
Literature review and key ideas

BKM criterion in general dimensions and axisymmetric, swirl-free solutions.
The local well-posedness of a solution
u\in C\big([0,T_{\text{max}});H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)\cap C^{1}\big([0,T_{\text{max}});H^{s-1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\big)
of (
1.1
) for
s>1+d/2
is established in Kato
[
14
]
, Kato–Lai
[
15
]
(also see Miller
[
19
]
), where
T_{\text{max}}\in(0,\infty]
is the maximal time of existence of
u
. Naturally, one can ask whether
T_{\text{max}}<\infty
could hold in certain cases or not. The Beale–Kato–Majda criterion provides an answer for that question (
[
1
]
for
d=3
,
[
16
]
for any
d\geq 3
). In 3D, it says that
T_{\text{max}}=\infty
if for any
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists
M\in(0,\infty)
such that the vorticity field
\nabla\times u
satisfies

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla\times u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt\leq M.

In higher dimensions, the integrand is replaced by the
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of the rotation matrix
\Omega:=\frac{1}{2}[(\nabla u)-(\nabla u)^{\text{tr}}]
. On the other hand, when we consider axisymmetric and swirl-free solutions,
the rotation matrix is expressed as

\Omega=\frac{1}{2}\omega(e^{r}\otimes e^{z}-e^{z}\otimes e^{r}),

where
e^{r}\otimes e^{z}
is the tensor product between
e^{r}
and
e^{z}
, so the term
\|\Omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
becomes equivalent with the norm
\|\omega\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
of the scalar vorticity
\omega
. That is, showing that
\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
is bounded by some regular function of
t
is sufficient enough to prove the global regularity of
\omega
.

The big difference between the 3D case and higher dimensional case is that in
d\geq 4
, the relative vorticity
\xi=r^{-(d-2)}\omega
is, in general, not in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
even when
\omega
is smooth. This is one of the reasons why the global regularity of axisymmetric, swirl-free smooth solutions of (
1.1
) in any
d\geq 4
is still unknown.

Support growth of a single-signed vorticity in 2D.
When the vorticity is allowed to be single-signed, the confinement argument of Marchioro
[
18
]
says that one can improve the support growth estimate from a
pointwise
decaying estimate of the radial component
\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot u
of the velocity field. For a vortex patch in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, as in
[
18
]
, one may simply prove that the support is contained in the region
\{|x|\leq C(1+t)^{1/2}\}
. There are two key ideas in showing this estimate. First, the angular impulse

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2}\omega(x)dx,

which is conserved in time, is equal to the norm
\||x|^{2}\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, when we have only one sign. Next, the conservation implies the following time-independent estimate in the local region:

\int_{||x|-R|\leq R/2}\omega(t,x)dx\leq\int_{|x|>R/2}\omega(t,x)dx\lesssim\frac{1}{R^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2}\omega_{0}(x)dx.

This enables us to obtain a decay rate of
\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot u
. The simple estimate with exponent
1/2
is improved in
[
18
]
with exponent
1/3
and by Iftimie–Sideris–Gamblin
[
13
]
with exponent
1/4
, involving a logarithmic term.
Two important ideas were used to obtain these refinements. One is the dynamics of
\omega
, that is,
\omega
is a solution of the 2D Euler equations. This is used to calculate the time-derivative of the term

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\phi_{R}(y)\omega(t,y)dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\phi_{R}(y)\cdot u(t,y)\omega(t,y)dy,

where
\phi_{R}
is a suitable smooth, non-negative, bounded, radial, and increasing function that satisfies
1_{\{|y|>R\}}\leq\phi_{R}
. The other is the symmetrization of the term in the right-hand side, which is a double-integral:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla\phi_{R}(y)\cdot u(t,y)\omega(t,y)dy\simeq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathcal{K}_{R}(y,\overline{y})\omega(t,\overline{y})\omega(t,y)d\overline{y}dy,

for some kernel
\mathcal{K}_{R}
. The function
\phi_{R}
is an approximation of the simple function
1_{\{|x|>R\}}
in some sense (such as
L^{1}
), so roughly speaking, what we have is an estimate of the term

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{|x|>R}\omega(t,x)dx.

Then we make
R
to depend on
t
. We refer the reader to Serfati
[
20
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, and Iftimie
[
12
]
, Iftimie–Lopes Filho–Nussenzveig Lopes
[
10
,
11
]
, and Choi–Denisov
[
4
]
in other 2D domains as well.

Support growth of a single-signed vorticity in higher dimensions.

In the 3D axisymmetric, swirl-free setting with single-signed, compactly supported vorticity in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, Maffei–Marchioro
[
17
]
followed the spirit of the above argument to show that the vorticity is contained in the region
\{r\leq C(1+t)^{1/4}\ln(e+t)\}
. This is because the fluid impulse

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}x\times\omega(x)dx

is conserved in time, and in the axisymmetric, swirl-free setting, this yields the time-preservation of the radial impulse

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}r\omega(x)dx\simeq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r^{2}\omega(r,z)dzdr.

Again, by the sign condition of
\omega
, this quantity is the same as the term
\|r\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
.

We slightly improve the logarithmic power of
[
17
]
for
d=3
and generalize this argument up to any
higher dimensional case
d\geq 3
to get the bound (
1.9
).
Once again, this is thanks to the conservation of the radial impulse:

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}r\omega(x)dx\simeq_{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}r^{d-1}\omega(r,z)dzdr.

While this quantity is conserved in general, it is equivalent with the norm
\|r\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
only when
\omega
is single-signed.

Related works in higher dimensions.
In the previous work of the author in collaboration with Choi and Jeong
[
6
]
, we showed the global regularity in
d=4
of axisymmetric, swirl-free solution
\omega
, which shows decay as
r\to\infty
and goes to
0
sufficiently fast as
r\to 0
. In addition, we proved that if
\omega
is single-signed and compactly supported, then it is globally regular, at least up to
d\leq 7
. Such restriction in dimension was made because we used the Feng–Sverak (
[
8
]
) type global estimate of
u^{r}

\|u^{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim_{d}\|r^{d-2}\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/4}\bigg\|\frac{\omega}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/4}\bigg\|\frac{\omega}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/2},

which gave us for any
t\geq 0
,

S^{\prime}(t)\lesssim_{d}S(t)^{(d-3)/4}\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1/4}

In the next section, we overcome this restriction
d\leq 7
in dimension by using the confinement argument.

Recently, Gustafson–Miller–Tsai
[
9
]
obtained an upper and lower bound of the radial impulse for global time in
d=3,4
, and for local time in any
d\geq 5
. In particular, their lower bound improves the vorticity growth result by Choi–Jeong
[
5
]
in
d=3
. As a note, for the case
d=3
, Childress–Gilbert–Valiant
[
3
]
and Childress–Gilbert
[
2
]
suggest that
\|\omega(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
and its related quantities can blow up in infinite time.

In section
2
, we shall prove a time-independent pointwise estimate of
u^{r}
when its associated relative vorticity is a compactly supported patch as a warm-up calculation. We included this estimate to convince the readers that global regularity is very natural for one-signed vorticity. In section
3
, using the dynamics of the Euler flow, we will show several technical lemmas that are required for proving a refined pointwise estimate of
u^{r}
, which holds for sufficiently large
r=r(t)>0
.
In section
4
, the last section, we finish the proof of this estimate and show Theorem
1.1
.

2
Support growth of a patch for any
d\geq 3

2.1
The explicit form of
u^{r}

We let
d\geq 3
and denote the
(r,z)
-half plane as

\Pi:=\left\{(r,z):r\geq 0,z\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

First, let us figure out the explicit form of
u^{r}
. To do this, we need the exact form of the scalar stream function
\psi
, where its precise derivation can be found in
[
19
, Sec. 3]
.
The stream function
\psi
is given as

\psi(r,z)=\iint_{\Pi}G_{d}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})\omega(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\bar{z}d\bar{r},

with the kernel

\begin{split}G_{d}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})&:=c_{d}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{(r\overline{r})^{d-2}\cos\theta\sin^{d-3}\theta}{[r^{2}+\overline{r}^{2}-2r\overline{r}\cos\theta+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2-1}}d\theta\\
&=c_{d}(r\overline{r})^{d/2-1}F_{d}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg),\end{split}

for some constant
c_{d}>0
and

F_{d}(s):=\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{\cos\theta\sin^{d-3}\theta}{[2(1-\cos\theta)+s]^{d/2-1}}d\theta,\quad s>0.

(2.1)

Then from (
1.5
), we can obtain the explicit form of
u^{r}
:

\begin{split}u^{r}(r,z)&=-\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\iint_{\Pi}\partial_{z}G_{d}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})\omega(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\simeq_{d}\iint_{\Pi}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\omega(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}.\end{split}

(2.2)

The following is a key estimate of
F_{d}^{\prime}
from
[
6
]
. This is a generalization of the estimate in
d=3
from
[
8
]
. Also see the recent paper
[
9
]
as well.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
6
, Lem. 2.2]
)
.

F_{d}^{\prime}
satisfies

|F_{d}^{\prime}(s)|\lesssim_{d}\min\bigg\{\frac{1}{s},\frac{1}{s^{d/2+1}}\bigg\},\quad s>0.

(2.3)

This was obtained by expanding
F_{d}^{\prime}
near
s=0
and
s=\infty
. We use this estimate of
F_{d}^{\prime}
to obtain a
pointwise
estimate of
u^{r}
and show Theorem
1.1
.

2.2
A time-independent pointwise estimate of
u^{r}
for a patch

If we are interested only in global regularity with a rough estimate, then adapting the early work of
[
18
]
for 2D vortex patch, we can generate a time-independent pointwise estimate of
u^{r}
,
which holds for
any

(r,z)\in\Pi
when the relative vorticity
\xi=r^{-(d-2)}\omega
is given as a patch
1_{\mathcal{A}},\ \mathcal{A}\subset\Pi
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
d\geq 3
and let
\xi=1_{\mathcal{A}}
for some compact set
\mathcal{A}\subset\Pi
. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any
(r,z)\in\Pi
, we have

|u^{r}(r,z)|\leq C\bigg[\frac{1}{r^{d}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\bigg],

(2.4)

where
C>0
depends only on
d
,
\|r^{d-2}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}
, and
\|r^{2d-3}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}
.

Remark 2.3
.

For a solution
\xi(t)
of (
1.6
) with initial data
\xi_{0}
, assuming its existence, the quantities

\|r^{d-2}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\simeq_{d}\|\xi\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\|r^{2d-3}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\simeq_{d}\|r\omega\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

are conserved in time.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

We fix
(r,z)\in\Pi
. Let us recall the form (
2.2
) of
u^{r}
, which in our case becomes

\begin{split}u^{r}(r,z)&=\iint_{\Pi}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\omega(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&=\iint_{\Pi}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\overline{r}^{d-2}\xi(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&=\iint_{\mathcal{A}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}.\end{split}

We split the integral domain
\mathcal{A}
into

\begin{split}A_{1}&:=\mathcal{A}\cap\bigg\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:|r-\overline{r}|>\frac{r}{2}\bigg\},\\
A_{2}&:=\mathcal{A}\cap\bigg\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:|r-\overline{r}|\leq\frac{r}{2},\;|z-\overline{z}|>\frac{r}{2}\bigg\},\\
A_{3}&:=\mathcal{A}\cap\bigg\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:|r-\overline{r}|\leq\frac{r}{2},\;|z-\overline{z}|\leq\frac{r}{2}\bigg\}.\end{split}

Then in
A_{1}
, we have

\begin{split}\bigg|\iint_{A_{1}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|&\leq\iint_{A_{1}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}|z-\overline{z}|}{r^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{(r\overline{r})^{d/2+1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2+1}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\iint_{A_{1}}\frac{r\overline{r}^{2d-3}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{(d+1)/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{r^{d}}\cdot\|r^{2d-3}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}.\end{split}

In
A_{2}
, we get

\begin{split}\bigg|\iint_{A_{2}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|&\leq\iint_{A_{2}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}|z-\overline{z}|}{r^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{r\overline{r}}{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\iint_{A_{2}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-3}}{r^{d/2-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\lesssim\iint_{A_{2}}\underbrace{\bigg(\frac{\overline{r}}{r}\bigg)^{d/2-1}}_{\lesssim_{d}1}\cdot\frac{\overline{r}^{d-2}}{r}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{r}\cdot\|r^{d-2}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}.\end{split}

In
A_{3}
, we have

\begin{split}\bigg|\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|&\leq\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-3}}{r^{d/2-1}}\cdot\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{\overline{r}^{d-2}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\lesssim_{d}r^{d-2}\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}.\end{split}

Here, note that we can estimate the integral in the last term as

\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\leq\int_{B_{\alpha}}\frac{1}{|y|}dy\lesssim\alpha,

where
B_{\alpha}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:|y|<\alpha\}
is the disk whose measure in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the same as the measure of
A_{3}
in
\Pi
. In addition, we get

\begin{split}|A_{3}|&=\bigg|\mathcal{A}\cap\bigg\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:|r-\overline{r}|\leq\frac{r}{2},\;|z-\overline{z}|\leq\frac{r}{2}\bigg\}\bigg|=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}|r-\overline{r}|\leq r/2,\\
|z-\overline{z}|\leq r/2\end{subarray}}\xi(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&=\iint_{\begin{subarray}{c}|r-\overline{r}|\leq r/2,\\
|z-\overline{z}|\leq r/2\end{subarray}}\frac{\overline{r}^{2d-3}}{\overline{r}^{2d-3}}\cdot\xi(\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{r^{2d-3}}\cdot\|r^{2d-3}\xi\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}.\end{split}

From this, we obtain

\begin{split}\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}&\lesssim\alpha\lesssim|A_{3}|^{1/2}\lesssim_{d,\xi}\frac{1}{r^{d-3/2}}.\end{split}

This gives us

\bigg|\iint_{A_{3}}\frac{\overline{r}^{(3d)/2-4}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|\lesssim_{d,\xi}r^{d-2}\cdot\frac{1}{r^{d-3/2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}.

Summing these up, we obtain (
2.4
).
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Considering that
\xi_{0}
is a patch, as long as the solution of (
1.6
) exists, we can get the following time-independent pointwise decaying estimate of
u^{r}
from Proposition
2.2
:

|u^{r}(t,r,z)|\lesssim_{d,\xi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r}}\quad\text{for any}\quad r\geq 1.

(2.5)

From this, we have the ordinary differential inequality (see the definition of
S(t)
in (
1.8
))

S^{\prime}(t)\lesssim_{d,\xi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{S(t)}}\quad\text{for any}\quad r=S(t).

Solving this gives us, at least formally,

S(t)\lesssim(1+t)^{2/3}.

3
Support growth of a single-signed vorticity for any
d\geq 3

3.1
Refinement of the pointwise estimate of
u^{r}

Now we prove our main result. We assume the conditions in Theorem
1.1
, including the condition
u_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
s>1+d/2
. We follow the spirit of
[
18
,
13
]
, using the dynamics of
\omega
. Precisely, we take the approach of
[
17
]
, which is implicated in our Lemma
3.3
. Additionally, without loss of generality, we assume that
\omega_{0}
is non-negative. Then for each
t\geq 0
and
r\geq 0
, we use the notation

m_{r}(t):=\iint_{A_{r}}\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r},\quad A_{r}:=\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:\overline{r}\geq r\},

where
\omega
is the solution of (
1.3
) with the initial data
\omega_{0}
.

We introduce Lemma
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
. The proofs of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
1.1
are postponed after showing some technical lemmas that are needed to prove the proposition. From below, the constant
C>0
may change line-by-line, and depends only on
d
,
\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
,
\|r^{-(d-2)}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
S_{0}
, unless mentioned otherwise.

The following lemma shows a
pointwise
estimate of
u^{r}
, which holds for any point in
\Pi
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and
(r,z)\in\Pi
, we have

|u^{r}(t,r,z)|\leq C\bigg[\frac{1}{r^{d}}+(r^{d-2}+1)m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2}\bigg].

(3.1)

Proof.

We recall the integral form (
2.2
) of
u^{r}
. We split the integral range into
E:=\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:|r-\overline{r}|\leq r/2\}
and
\Pi\setminus E
, and use the estimate (
2.3
) in Lemma
2.1
. On
\Pi\setminus E
, we have

\begin{split}\bigg|\iint_{\Pi\setminus E}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)&\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|\leq\iint_{\Pi\setminus E}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}|z-\overline{z}|}{r^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{(r\overline{r})^{d/2+1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2+1}}\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\iint_{\Pi\setminus E}\frac{r\overline{r}^{d-1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{(d+1)/2}}\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\leq\frac{Cr}{r^{d+1}}\cdot\|r^{d-1}\omega(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\\
&\simeq_{d}\frac{C}{r^{d}}\cdot\|r\omega(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\frac{C}{r^{d}}\cdot\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.\end{split}

Here, we used the conservation
\|r\omega(t)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

for a single-signed
\omega
. On the other hand on
E
, we have

\begin{split}\bigg|\iint_{E}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\bigg|&\leq\iint_{E}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}|z-\overline{z}|}{r^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{r\overline{r}}{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\iint_{E}\bigg(\frac{\overline{r}}{r}\bigg)^{d/2-1}\cdot\frac{\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq C\iint_{E}\frac{\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}.\end{split}

Now we split the integral range of the last term above into
E\cap B_{k}
and
E\setminus B_{k}
, where

B_{k}=\{(\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi:[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}<k\},\quad k:=m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2}.

Then we get

\begin{split}\iint_{E\cap B_{k}}\frac{\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}&=\iint_{E\cap B_{k}}\frac{\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})}{\overline{r}^{d-2}}\cdot\frac{\overline{r}^{d-2}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\bigg\|\frac{\omega(t)}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\Pi)}Cr^{d-2}\iint_{B_{k}}\frac{1}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\bigg\|\frac{\omega_{0}}{r^{d-2}}\bigg\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}Cr^{d-2}k=Cr^{d-2}m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2},\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\iint_{E\setminus B_{k}}\frac{\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{1/2}}d\overline{z}d\overline{r}&\leq\frac{1}{k}\iint_{A_{r/2}}\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&=\frac{m_{r/2}(t)}{k}=m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2}.\end{split}

This proves (
3.1
).
∎

The following proposition says that we can shrink
m_{r}(t)
as much as we want it to be when
r
is depending on time.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
q\geq d
be an integer. Then there exists
C>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and
r\geq 0
that satisfies

r\geq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)},

we have

m_{r}(t)\leq\frac{1}{r^{q}}.

(3.2)

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is given in section
4
.

3.2
Some technical lemmas

To prove Proposition
3.2
, we need several lemmas.
The first lemma below shows that
m_{r_{2}}(t)
is expressed in terms of
m_{r_{1}}(s)
for earlier times
s\leq t
and for
r_{1}<r_{2}
. This type of lemma for
d=3
is the heart of
[
17
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and
r_{1},r_{2}>0
satisfying
S_{0}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
, we have

\begin{split}m_{r_{2}}(t)&\leq J_{d}(r_{1},r_{2})\int_{0}^{t}m_{r_{1}}(s)ds,\quad J_{d}(r_{1},r_{2}):=\frac{Cr_{2}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d}}.\end{split}

(3.3)

Proof.

First, we let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
be a smooth even function that satisfies
\phi(0)=1
, monotone on
[0,\infty)
, and supported on
[-1,1]
.
Then for each
r_{1},r_{2}>0
satisfying
S_{0}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
, we define a smooth monotone function
\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}:[0,\infty)\longrightarrow[0,1]
, which is given as

\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}(s):=\begin{cases}1-\phi(\frac{s-r_{1}}{r_{2}-r_{1}})&\text{ if }s>r_{1}\\
0&\text{ if }0\leq s\leq r_{1}\end{cases}.

(3.4)

Note that
\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}
satisfies

\displaystyle\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}|_{r=0}

\displaystyle=0,

(3.5)

\displaystyle|\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\quad\text{for any}\quad r>0,

(3.6)

\displaystyle|\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)-\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(\overline{r})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C|r-\overline{r}|}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\quad\text{for any}\quad r,\overline{r}>0,

(3.7)

for some absolute constant
C>0
. Also for any
t\geq 0
, we define

g(t):=\iint_{\Pi}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}(r)\omega(t,r,z)dzdr.

Since
S_{0}\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
, we have

m_{r_{2}}(t)\leq g(t)\leq g(0)+\int_{0}^{t}|g^{\prime}(s)|ds=\int_{0}^{t}|g^{\prime}(s)|ds.

Our goal is to show

|g^{\prime}(t)|\leq J_{d}(r_{1},r_{2})m_{r_{1}}(t).

(3.8)

Once the above is shown, then the proof of (
3.3
) is complete.

To begin with, we take the time derivative of
g
:

\begin{split}g^{\prime}(t)&=\iint_{\Pi}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}\partial_{t}\omega dzdr\\
&=\iint_{\Pi}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}\bigg[-(u^{r}\partial_{r}+u^{z}\partial_{z})\omega+\frac{d-2}{r}u^{r}\omega\bigg]dzdr\\
&=\iint_{\Pi}\bigg[[u^{r}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}+\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}(\underbrace{\partial_{r}u^{r}+\partial_{z}u^{z}}_{=-\frac{d-2}{r}u^{r}})]+\frac{d-2}{r}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}u^{r}\bigg]\omega dzdr=\iint_{\Pi}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}u^{r}\omega dzdr.\end{split}

We used the fact that
\omega
solves (
1.3
) in the second equality. In the third one, we used integration by parts, where the boundary term vanishes because
\omega
is compactly supported and
\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}|_{r=0}=0
. In the last one, we used the divergence-free condition

\nabla\cdot u=\partial_{r}u^{r}+\partial_{z}u^{z}+\frac{d-2}{r}u^{r}=0.

Now we have

\begin{split}g^{\prime}(t)&=\iint_{\Pi}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)u^{r}(t,r,z)\omega(t,r,z)dzdr\\
&\simeq_{d}\iint_{\Pi}\iint_{\Pi}\frac{\overline{r}^{d/2-2}(z-\overline{z})}{r^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})\omega(t,r,z)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}dzdr.\end{split}

Then symmetrizing the above integral, we have

\begin{split}g^{\prime}(t)&\simeq_{d}\iint_{\Pi}\iint_{\Pi}[\overline{r}^{d-2}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)-r^{d-2}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(\overline{r})]\frac{z-\overline{z}}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg)\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})\omega(t,r,z)d\overline{z}d\overline{r}dzdr.\end{split}

(3.9)

We denote the kernel of the above integral as

\begin{split}L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})&:=[\overline{r}^{d-2}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)-r^{d-2}\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(\overline{r})]\frac{z-\overline{z}}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg),\end{split}

and use the notation as an abbreviation:

\omega=\omega(t,r,z)dzdr,\quad\overline{\omega}=\omega(t,\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}.

Then note that
L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})
is symmetric, and that
L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})=0
if
r\in[r_{1},r_{2}]^{C}
and
\overline{r}\in[r_{1},r_{2}]^{C}
, because of
\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}\equiv 0
on
[r_{1},r_{2}]^{C}
, which means

\begin{split}{\mathrm{supp}}\,L&\subset\{(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi\times\Pi:r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}\}\cup\{(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})\in\Pi\times\Pi:r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2}\}.\end{split}

From this, we have

\begin{split}|g^{\prime}(t)|&\lesssim_{d}\iint_{\Pi}\iint_{\Pi}|L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega\\
&\leq\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}|L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega+\iint_{\Pi}\iint_{r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2}}|L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega\\
&=2\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}|L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega.\end{split}

Now we split
L=L_{1}+L_{2}
, where

\begin{split}L_{1}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})&:=\overline{r}^{d-2}[\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(r)-\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(\overline{r})]\frac{z-\overline{z}}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg),\\
L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})&:=(\overline{r}^{d-2}-r^{d-2})\eta_{r_{1}r_{2}}^{\prime}(\overline{r})\frac{z-\overline{z}}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}F_{d}^{\prime}\bigg(\frac{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}{r\overline{r}}\bigg).\end{split}

From this, we get

\begin{split}|g^{\prime}(t)|&\lesssim_{d}\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}|L(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega\\
&\leq\underbrace{\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}|L_{1}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega}_{=:(A)}+\underbrace{\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}|L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega}_{=:(B)}.\end{split}

To estimate the term
|L_{1}|
, we can consider the following two cases:

Case I.

r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2},\;|r-\overline{r}|>r/2
.

In this case, the term
|L_{1}|
is estimated as

\begin{split}|L_{1}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|&\lesssim_{d}\overline{r}^{d-2}\cdot\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\cdot|r-\overline{r}|\cdot\frac{|z-\overline{z}|}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{(r\overline{r})^{d/2+1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2+1}}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\cdot\frac{r\overline{r}^{d-1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2}}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\cdot\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{r^{d-1}}\leq\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d-1}}.\end{split}

Case II.

r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2},\;|r-\overline{r}|\leq r/2
.

Here,
we can estimate the term
|L_{1}|
as

\begin{split}|L_{1}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|&\lesssim_{d}\overline{r}^{d-2}\cdot\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\cdot|r-\overline{r}|\cdot\frac{|z-\overline{z}|}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{r\overline{r}}{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\overline{r}}{r}\bigg)^{d/2-1}\lesssim_{d}\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d-1}}.\end{split}

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}(A)&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d-1}}\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{\Pi}\overline{r}^{d-1}\overline{\omega}\omega\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d-1}}\cdot\underbrace{\|r^{d-1}\omega\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}}_{=\|r^{d-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}}\cdot m_{r_{1}}(t).\end{split}

(3.10)

Now let us focus our attention to
(B)
. Note that
L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})=0
if
\overline{r}\in[r_{1},r_{2}]^{C}
. From this, we have

\begin{split}(B)&=\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2}}|L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|\overline{\omega}\omega.\end{split}

Similarly, to estimate the term
|L_{2}|
, let us consider these two cases:

Case I’.

r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2},\;r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2},\;|r-\overline{r}|>r/2
.

In this case, the term
|L_{2}|
is estimated as

\begin{split}|L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|&\lesssim_{d}|r-\overline{r}|\cdot\underbrace{(\overline{r}^{d-3}+\overline{r}^{d-4}r+\cdots+\overline{r}r^{d-4}+r^{d-3})}_{(d-2)\text{ -terms}}\cdot\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\cdot\frac{|z-\overline{z}|}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{(r\overline{r})^{d/2+1}}{[(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}]^{d/2+1}}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\cdot\bigg(\frac{\overline{r}^{d-2}}{r^{d-1}}+\frac{\overline{r}^{d-3}}{r^{d-2}}+\cdots+\frac{\overline{r}^{2}}{r^{3}}+\frac{\overline{r}}{r^{2}}\bigg)=\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\cdot\bigg(\frac{1}{r^{d-1}\overline{r}}+\frac{1}{r^{d-2}\overline{r}^{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{r^{3}\overline{r}^{d-3}}+\frac{1}{r^{2}\overline{r}^{d-2}}\bigg)\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})r_{1}^{d}}.\end{split}

Case II’.

r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2},\;r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2},\;|r-\overline{r}|\leq r/2
.

Here, we can estimate the term
|L_{2}|
as

\begin{split}|L_{2}(r,z,\overline{r},\overline{z})|&\lesssim_{d}|r-\overline{r}|\cdot(\overline{r}^{d-3}+\overline{r}^{d-4}r+\cdots+\overline{r}r^{d-4}+r^{d-3})\cdot\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\cdot\frac{|z-\overline{z}|}{(r\overline{r})^{d/2}}\cdot\frac{r\overline{r}}{(r-\overline{r})^{2}+(z-\overline{z})^{2}}\\
&\lesssim_{d}\frac{1}{r_{2}-r_{1}}\cdot\frac{r^{d/2-2}}{\overline{r}^{d/2-1}}\lesssim_{d}\frac{\overline{r}^{d-1}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})r_{1}^{d}}.\end{split}

Hence, we obtain

\begin{split}(B)&\leq\frac{C}{(r_{2}-r_{1})r_{1}^{d}}\iint_{r_{1}\leq r\leq r_{2}}\iint_{r_{1}\leq\overline{r}\leq r_{2}}\overline{r}^{d-1}\overline{\omega}\omega\\
&\leq\frac{C}{(r_{2}-r_{1})r_{1}^{d}}\cdot\|r^{d-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\cdot m_{r_{1}}(t).\end{split}

(3.11)

Finally, summing up (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we have

\begin{split}|g^{\prime}(t)|&\leq C\|r^{d-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\cdot\bigg[\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d-1}}+\frac{1}{(r_{2}-r_{1})r_{1}^{d}}\bigg]\cdot m_{r_{1}}(t)\\
&=C\|r\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\cdot\frac{r_{2}}{(r_{2}-r_{1})^{2}r_{1}^{d}}\cdot m_{r_{1}}(t).\end{split}

∎

The second lemma is an estimate of
m_{r}(t)
in terms of only
r
and
t
, which is a direct application of the previous lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exists
C>0
such that for any integer
p\geq 1
, any
t\geq 0
and any
r\geq 2S_{0}
, we have

m_{r}(t)\leq\frac{C^{p}p^{p}t^{p}}{r^{p(d+1)+d-1}}.

(3.12)

Proof.

We denote
\alpha_{j}:=r(1-\frac{j}{2p})
for
j=0,1,\cdots,p
. Then note that we have
\alpha_{0}=r
,
\alpha_{p}=r/2
, and for each
j=1,\cdots,p
, by recalling the definition of
J_{d}(r_{1},r_{2})
in Lemma
3.3
, we have

\begin{split}J_{d}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j-1})&\leq\frac{Cr(1-\frac{j-1}{2p})}{(\frac{r}{2p})^{2}r^{d}(1-\frac{j}{2p})^{d}}\leq\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}.\end{split}

Using this and the inequality (
3.3
) from Lemma
3.3
, for each
j=1,\cdots,p
, we get

\begin{split}m_{\alpha_{j-1}}(t)&\leq J_{d}(\alpha_{j},\alpha_{j-1})\int_{0}^{t}m_{\alpha_{j}}(s)ds\leq\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}\int_{0}^{t}m_{\alpha_{j}}(s)ds.\end{split}

(3.13)

From this, we get

\begin{split}m_{r}(t)&=m_{\alpha_{0}}(t)\leq\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}\int_{0}^{t}m_{\alpha_{1}}(s_{1})ds_{1}\\
&\leq\bigg(\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}\bigg)^{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}m_{\alpha_{2}}(s_{2})ds_{2}ds_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\bigg(\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}\bigg)^{p-1}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{p-3}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-2}}m_{\alpha_{p}}(s_{p-1})ds_{p-1}ds_{p-2}\cdots ds_{2}ds_{1}\\
&\leq\bigg(\frac{Cp^{2}}{r^{d+1}}\bigg)^{p}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{p-3}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-2}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-1}}m_{r/2}(s_{p})ds_{p}ds_{p-1}ds_{p-2}\cdots ds_{2}ds_{1}.\end{split}

Then using the estimate

\begin{split}m_{r/2}(s_{p})&=\iint_{\overline{r}\geq r/2}\omega(s_{p},\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}\\
&\leq\bigg(\frac{r}{2}\bigg)^{-(d-1)}\iint_{\Pi}\overline{r}^{d-1}\omega(s_{p},\overline{r},\overline{z})d\overline{z}d\overline{r}=\bigg(\frac{r}{2}\bigg)^{-(d-1)}\cdot\|r^{d-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)},\end{split}

we have

\begin{split}m_{r}(t)&\leq\frac{C^{p}p^{2p}}{r^{p(d+1)}}\cdot\bigg(\frac{r}{2}\bigg)^{-(d-1)}\cdot\|r^{d-1}\omega_{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Pi)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{p-3}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-2}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-1}}1ds_{p}ds_{p-1}ds_{p-2}\cdots ds_{2}ds_{1}.\end{split}

Finally, from the computation of the following term

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{p-3}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-2}}\int_{0}^{s_{p-1}}1ds_{p}ds_{p-1}ds_{p-2}\cdots ds_{2}ds_{1}&=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\cdots\int_{0}^{s_{p-3}}\frac{(s_{p-2})^{2}}{2}ds_{p-2}\cdots ds_{2}ds_{1}\\
&=\cdots=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{(s_{1})^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}ds_{1}=\frac{t^{p}}{p!},\end{split}

and the simple inequality
\frac{p^{p}}{p!}\leq e^{p}
, we obtain

\begin{split}m_{r}(t)&\leq\frac{C^{p}p^{2p}}{r^{p(d+1)+d-1}}\cdot\frac{t^{p}}{p!}\leq\frac{C^{p}p^{2p}}{r^{p(d+1)+d-1}}\cdot\frac{e^{p}t^{p}}{p^{p}}\\
&\leq\frac{C^{p}p^{p}t^{p}}{r^{p(d+1)+d-1}}.\end{split}

∎

The final lemma is about some facts from elementary computations. The first computation, where we roughly have
k\approx\tau\ln y
, enables us to get the decay rate of
m_{r}
as fast as we want it to be. The second computation provides the necessary condition that
r
must satisfy in order to obtain the decay of
m_{r}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

(a) For any integer
\tau\geq 1
, there exist
C=C(\tau)>0
and
y_{0}=y_{0}(\tau)>1
such that for any
y\geq y_{0}
, there exists an integer
k=k(\tau,y)\geq 1
that satisfies

y^{\tau/k}k\leq C\ln(e+y).

(3.14)

(b) For any integer
d\geq 3
and any
B>0
, there exists
A=A(d,B)>0
such that for any
x\geq 0
and any
y\geq 0
satisfying

y\geq A[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)},

we have

y\geq B[(1+x)\ln(e+y)]^{1/(d+1)}.

(3.15)

Proof.

(a) We let
\tau\geq 1
be an integer, and for any
y>1
and any
s>0
, we define

f_{y}(s):=y^{\tau/s}s-C\ln(e+y),

where
C>0
will be taken later. Note that
f_{y}
is increasing for all
s>\tau\ln y
:

f_{y}^{\prime}(s)=y^{\tau/s}\bigg(1-\frac{\tau\ln y}{s}\bigg)>0\quad\text{for all}\quad s>\tau\ln y.

Now we take
C:=2e(\tau+1)
. Then we can take
y_{0}=y_{0}(\tau)>1
that satisfies

f_{y}(\tau\ln y+1)\leq 0\quad\text{for any}\quad y\geq y_{0},

(3.16)

because we have

\begin{split}f_{y}(\tau\ln y+1)&=y^{1/(\ln y+1/\tau)}(\tau\ln y+1)-C\ln(e+y)\\
&\leq[\underbrace{y^{1/\ln y}}_{=e}(\tau+1)-C]\ln(e+y)\longrightarrow-\infty\quad\text{as}\quad y\longrightarrow\infty.\end{split}

Thus, from (
3.16
) and the fact that
f_{y}
is increasing on
[\tau\ln y,\infty)
, for any
y\geq y_{0}
, we have

f_{y}(s)\leq 0\quad\text{for any}\quad s\in[\tau\ln y,\tau\ln y+1].

Finally, we choose any positive integer
k=k(\tau,y)\in[\tau\ln y,\tau\ln y+1]
to obtain (
3.14
):

f_{y}(k)=y^{\tau/k}k-C\ln(e+y)\leq 0.

(b) We let
d\geq 3
. We first claim that there exists an absolute constant
C_{0}>0
such that for any
\alpha>0
and any
x\geq 0
, we have

\frac{\ln\big[e+\alpha[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}\big]}{\ln(e+x)}\leq C_{0}(1+\sqrt{\alpha}).

(3.17)

Once this claim is shown, then for any
B>0
, we take any
A=A(d,B)>0
satisfying

B=\frac{A}{C_{0}^{1/(d+1)}(1+\sqrt{A})^{1/(d+1)}},

(3.18)

where the above claim (
3.17
) gives us for any
x\geq 0
,

B^{d+1}=\frac{A^{d+1}}{C_{0}(1+\sqrt{A})}\leq\frac{A^{d+1}\ln(e+x)}{\ln\big[e+A[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}\big]}.

(3.19)

Then for any
y\geq A[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}
, using the fact that
y^{d+1}/\ln(e+y)
is monotonically increasing in
y>0
, we have

\frac{y^{d+1}}{\ln(e+y)}\geq\frac{A^{d+1}(1+x)\ln(e+x)}{\ln\big[e+A[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}\big]}\geq B^{d+1}(1+x),

which proves (
3.15
).

Now let us prove our claim (
3.17
). First, note that there exists an absolute constant
c>1
that satisfies

c\sqrt{1+x}\geq[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}\quad\text{for any}\quad x\geq 0.

Now we let
\alpha>0
and
x\geq 0
. Then we have

\frac{\ln\big[e+\alpha[(1+x)\ln(e+x)]^{1/(d+1)}\big]}{\ln(e+x)}\leq\frac{\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}\big)}{\ln(e+x)}.

Also, note that there exists
x_{0}=x_{0}(\alpha)>0
that satisfies

x_{0}=c\alpha\sqrt{1+x_{0}}.

In particular, we have

x_{0}=\frac{(c\alpha)^{2}+c\alpha\sqrt{(c\alpha)^{2}+4}}{2}.

Then if
x\geq x_{0}
, we have
x\geq c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}
, which gives us

\frac{\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}\big)}{\ln(e+x)}\leq\frac{\ln(e+x)}{\ln(e+x)}=1.

(3.20)

If
x<x_{0}
, then we get

\begin{split}\frac{\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}\big)}{\ln(e+x)}&\leq\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x_{0}}\big)=\ln(e+x_{0})\\
&=\ln\bigg(e+\frac{(c\alpha)^{2}+c\alpha\sqrt{(c\alpha)^{2}+4}}{2}\bigg)\leq\ln\big(e+(c\alpha+2)^{2}\big)\\
&\leq 2\ln(c\alpha+2+\sqrt{e})\leq 2\ln\big(e^{C^{\prime}}(\alpha+1)\big)\\
&\leq 2\big(C^{\prime}+\ln(\alpha+1)\big),\end{split}

where
C^{\prime}=\ln\max\{c,2+\sqrt{e}\}
. Then using
\ln(\alpha+1)\leq\sqrt{\alpha}
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}\big)}{\ln(e+x)}&\leq 2\big(C^{\prime}+\ln(\alpha+1)\big)\\
&\leq 2(C^{\prime}+\sqrt{\alpha})\leq C_{0}(1+\sqrt{\alpha}),\end{split}

(3.21)

where
C_{0}=2\max\{C^{\prime},1\}
. Hence, from (
3.20
) and (
3.21
), we have (
3.17
):

\sup_{x\geq 0}\frac{\ln\big(e+c\alpha\sqrt{1+x}\big)}{\ln(e+x)}\leq C_{0}(1+\sqrt{\alpha}).

∎

4
Proof of the main result

We present the proof of Proposition
3.2
and Theorem
1.1
in this section.

4.1
Proof of Proposition
3.2

Using lemmas from section
3
, let us prove Proposition
3.2
.

Proof of Proposition
3.2
.

We let
d\geq 3
,
q\geq d
be integers, and
\omega_{0}
satisfy the conditions from Theorem
1.1
. Then from Lemma
3.4
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
p\in\mathbb{N}
, any
t\geq 0
, and any
r\geq 2S_{0}
, we have (
3.12
):

m_{r}(t)\leq\frac{(C^{\prime})^{p}p^{p}t^{p}}{r^{p(d+1)+d-1}}.

(4.1)

Now we fix
\tau:=q-d+1
. Then from Lemma
3.5
(a), there exist
C^{\prime\prime}>0
and
y_{0}>1
such that for any
r\geq y_{0}
, there exists an integer
k=k(r)\geq 1
that satisfies (
3.14
) with
y=r
:

r^{\tau/k}k\leq C^{\prime\prime}\ln(e+r).

We can rewrite this as

k^{k}\leq\frac{[C^{\prime\prime}\ln(e+r)]^{k}}{r^{q-d+1}}.

(4.2)

Then we set

B:=(C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime})^{1/(d+1)}.

Then from Lemma
3.5
(b), there exists
A>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and any
r\geq A[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}
, we have (
3.15
) with
x=t
and
y=r
:

r\geq B[(1+t)\ln(e+r)]^{1/(d+1)}.

Rewriting this with respect to
t
, we get

\begin{split}t&\leq 1+t\\
&\leq\frac{1}{B^{d+1}}\cdot\frac{r^{d+1}}{\ln(e+r)}\leq\frac{1}{C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\frac{r^{d+1}}{\ln(e+r)}.\end{split}

(4.3)

Finally, we take
C:=\max\{2S_{0},y_{0},A\}>0
. Then for any
t\geq 0
and any
r\geq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}
, summing up (
4.1
) with the choice
p=k(r)
, (
4.3
), and (
4.2
), we obtain (
3.2
):

\begin{split}m_{r}(t)&\leq\frac{(C^{\prime})^{k}k^{k}t^{k}}{r^{k(d+1)+d-1}}\\
&\leq\frac{(C^{\prime})^{k}}{r^{k(d+1)+d-1}}\cdot\frac{[C^{\prime\prime}\ln(e+r)]^{k}}{r^{q-d+1}}\cdot\frac{1}{(C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime})^{k}}\cdot\frac{r^{k(d+1)}}{[\ln(e+r)]^{k}}=\frac{1}{r^{q}}.\end{split}

∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
1.1

Finally, we finish this paper by showing Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

It suffices to prove the estimate (
1.9
);

S(t)\leq C[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}\quad\text{for any}\quad t\geq 0,

thanks to Remark
1.2
and the BKM criterion. We let
t\geq 0
. First, from Lemma
3.1
, there exists
C^{\prime}>0
such that for any
t\geq 0
and
(r,z)\in\Pi
, we have (
3.1
):

|u^{r}(t,r,z)|\leq C^{\prime}\bigg[\frac{1}{r^{d}}+(r^{d-2}+1)m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2}\bigg].

(4.4)

In addition, choosing
q=4d-4
from Proposition
3.2
, we can take
C^{\prime\prime}>0
such that for any
r\geq C^{\prime\prime}[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}
, we have

m_{r/2}(t)\leq\bigg(\frac{2}{r}\bigg)^{4d-4}.

(4.5)

Thus, combining (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), we can choose
C_{0}:=\max\{C^{\prime\prime},C^{\prime}(1+2\cdot 2^{2d-2})\}>0
such that for any
r\geq C_{0}[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}
, we have

\begin{split}|u^{r}(t,r,z)|&\leq C^{\prime}\bigg[\frac{1}{r^{d}}+(r^{d-2}+1)m_{r/2}(t)^{1/2}\bigg]\\
&\leq C^{\prime}\bigg[\frac{1}{r^{d}}+2r^{d-2}\bigg(\frac{2}{r}\bigg)^{2d-2}\bigg]=\frac{C_{0}}{r^{d}}.\end{split}

Here, we denote
\mathcal{F}(t):=[(1+t)\ln(e+t)]^{1/(d+1)}
. Then note that for any
r\geq C_{0}\mathcal{F}(t)
, we have

\begin{split}|u^{r}(t,r,z)|&\leq\frac{C_{0}}{r^{d}}\leq\frac{1}{C_{0}^{d-1}\mathcal{F}(t)^{d}}\\
&\leq\frac{d+1}{C_{0}^{d-1}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t).\end{split}

Then we take
C:=\max\{C_{0},(d+1)/C_{0}^{d-1},S_{0}\}>0
, which gives us for any
r\geq C\mathcal{F}(t)
,

|u^{r}(t,r,z)|\leq\frac{d+1}{C_{0}^{d-1}}\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(t)\leq[C\mathcal{F}(t)]^{\prime}.

(4.6)

This proves (
1.9
); for any
x\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,\omega_{0}
, we have

\Phi_{t}^{r}(x)\leq C\mathcal{F}(t),

(4.7)

where
\Phi_{t}(\cdot)=\Phi(t,\cdot)
is the flow map that uniquely solves the flow map ODE

\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}(x)=u\big(t,\Phi_{t}(x)\big),\quad\Phi_{0}(x)=x,

(4.8)

since we have
\Phi_{0}^{r}(x)\leq C
and (
4.6
) implies

\bigg|\frac{d}{dt}\Phi_{t}^{r}(x)\bigg|=\big|u^{r}\big(t,\Phi_{t}(x)\big)\big|\leq[C\mathcal{F}(t)]^{\prime}\quad\text{if}\quad\Phi_{t}^{r}(x)=C\mathcal{F}(t)\quad\text{happens for some}\quad t\geq 0.

∎

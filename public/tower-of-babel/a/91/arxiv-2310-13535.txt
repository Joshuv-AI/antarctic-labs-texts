Well-posedness for the Schrödinger-KdV system on the half-line
E. Compaan, W. Shin, N. Tzirakis

Abstract
. In this paper we obtain improved local well–posedness results for the Schrödinger-KdV system on the half-line. We employ the Laplace–Fourier method in conjunction with the restricted norm method of Bourgain appropriately modified in order to accommodate the bounded operators of the half–line problem. Our result extends the previous local results in [ 6 ] , [ 7 ] and [ 21 ] matching the results that Wu, [ 28 ] , obtained for the real line system. We also demonstrate the uniqueness for the full range of locally well–posed solutions. In addition we obtain global well–posedness on the half–line for the energy solutions with zero boundary data, along with polynomial–in–time bounds for higher order Sobolev norms for the Schrödinger part.

35Q53, 35Q55

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the initial-boundary value problem for the Schrödinger–KdV system posed on the positive half line

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\alpha uv+\beta|u|^{2}u,\\
\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v+v\partial_{x}v=\gamma\partial_{x}(|u|^{2}),\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\\
u(0,t)=f(t),\quad v(0,t)=g(t),\end{cases}

\displaystyle(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},

where
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{C}
,
v=v(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
are real constants.

The system on the real line models the nonlinear interaction of a short wave modeled by the Schrödinger part and a long wave modeled by the KdV part. The system arises in several fields of physics and fluid dynamics. The case
\beta\neq 0
discribes non-resonant interactions while the case
\beta=0
describes resonant interactions. For a detailed discussion of the applications we cite the papers
[
28
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
21
]
and the references therein. We should note that although the decoupled equations, namely the cubic nonlinear Schrödinger equation and the KdV equations are completely integrable the coupled system is not. In our work we study the system in the half–line interval imposing boundary conditions to the solution operators. From our point of view the model we study corresponds to the case where a wave generator acting as a fixed wave source, produces a disturbance in such a way that a wave motions are generated and then evolve into a medium in only one direction where the motion is unrestricted.

Our goal is to improve the previous local well–posedness results in
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
21
]
, and match the best possible results that are available on the real line,
[
28
]
, by utilizing recent techniques that have been developed to study nonlinear PDE on semi–infinite domains. Our method is based on the restricted norm method (
X^{s,b}
–method) of Bourgain. Since the dispersive effects of the system on the half–line are weaker than the full real line system, one has to set up a modification of the
X^{s,b}
method to accomodate the presence of the bounded operators. Our method is also inspired by previous developments in IBVP by Bona and his collaborators
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
, Kenig and Colliander in
[
9
]
, and Holmer
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
. Our approach is most closely related to those in
[
1
]
and
[
9
]
. Regarding the Schrödinger–KdV system, the first attempt to obtain a well–posedness theory for the half–line in
[
6
]
used the Colliander–Kenig method. The method is based on writting an implicit solution–formula with certain terms enforcing the boundary conditions. The drawback of the method is that uniqueness cannot be proved with the tools in use. The method was developed further in the Ph.D. thesis of Holmer in
[
22
]
,
[
23
]
. For more applications of this method on certain initial–boundary value problems the reader can consult
[
5
]
.

Another method that has been employed for the resolution of IBVP is the unified transform method of Fokas, introduced in
[
16
]
. See also
[
17
]
and the references therein for more recent examples and especially for applications of the method to nonlinear problems. The method is quite general and works for a variety of linear and nonlinear problems that are posed on complicated domains. This is the method the authors employ in
[
21
]
.

In the present manuscript we improve the well–posedness theory for the semi–infinite interval for
L^{2}
based solutions by employing multilinear harmonic analysis techniques. We use the methods developed in
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
where the authors extended the initial data of certain dispersive PDE posed on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
to the whole real line and then employed the Laplace and Fourier transform method to solve the linear problem in the usual Sobolev spaces. After this an equivalent integral representation (Duhamel’s formula) of the solution is written using the trace of the linear boundary operator and the nonlinearity. Trace theorems dictate the regularity of the boundary functions, while the nonlinearity of each particular PDE dictates the Banach space in which a fixed point of the integral equation is sought. Our method has the advantage of establishing that the nonlinear evolution is smoother than the initial data. These smoothing estimates enable us to conclude that our solutions are unique in the sense that they are independent of a particular extension of the initial data.
We should note that for IBVP, uniqueness of solutions is not straightforward at all, especially in a low regularity regime where there are no conservation laws or
a priori
bounds that are derived from the differential form of the conserved quantities. In this paper by proving a priori bounds for smooth solutions we obtain uniqueness for higher regularities. Persistence of regularity estimates then allows us to obtain uniqueness for rougher solutions. Persistence of regularity for the system (
1
) can be obtained after one has established certain smoothing estimates. We discuss this issue in Section
5.2
.

In the context of our work, well–posedness means existence and uniqueness of strong solutions
[
8
]
along with continuity of the data to solution map, at least locally in time (for a more precise definition see Section
3
). With the notations defined in Section
2
, we now state our main results..

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
(s,k)
is admissible if

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lr}0\leq s<\frac{5}{2},\;s\neq\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }\beta\neq 0,\\
-\frac{3}{16}<s<\frac{5}{2},\;s\neq\frac{1}{2}&\text{if }\beta=0,\end{array}

-\frac{3}{4}<k<\frac{7}{2}
,
k\neq\frac{1}{2}
and
s-2<k<\min(4s,s+1)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For an admissible pair
(s,k)
, the equation (
1
) is locally well-posed in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
in the sense of Definition
3.2
.
Moreover, for any

\displaystyle\begin{array}[]{lr}0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}\min(k-s+2,k+1,4s,5-2s,1),&\text{if }\beta\neq 0,\\
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}\min(k-s+2,k+1,5-2s,1),&\text{if }\beta=0\text{ and }s\geq 0,\\
0\leq a<\frac{1}{2}\min(s+k+\frac{3}{2},k+1,5-2s,1),&\text{if }\beta=0\text{ and }s<0,\end{array}

we have the smoothing for the Schrödinger part

(2)

\displaystyle u-S_{0}^{t}(u_{0},f)\in C_{t}H^{s+a}_{x}([0,T_{max})\times\mathbb{R}^{+}),

where
T_{max}
is the lifetime of
(u,v)
.

For
s<\frac{5}{2}
,
k<\frac{7}{2}
and
s,k\neq\frac{1}{2}
, our well-posedness region matches that of
[
28
]
up to the boundary, which is so far the best known result. We do not expect nonlinear smoothing for the KdV part, as it is known,
[
24
]
, that the nonlinear evolution of KdV equation on
\mathbb{R}
is not smoother than the initial data.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(s,k)
be an admissible pair satisfying
s\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
. Given
(u_{0},f,v_{0},g)\in\mathcal{H}^{s,k}
, the solution
(u,v)
to the system (
1
) has a global-in-time extension provided
\alpha\gamma>0
,
f=0
and
g=0
. Moreover,
\|u\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}
can grow at most polynomially-in-time.

In
[
7
]
, the authors proved global well-posedness for (
1
) with zero boundary condition in the energy space
(s,k)=(1,1)
. We extend this result for all
s\geq 1
and
k\geq 1
using persistence of regularity. We note this and the polynomial growth result are all based on smoothing estimates.

Now we outline the organization of the paper. In Section
2
, we define several function spaces to be used in this paper and briefly discuss their basic properties. In Section
3
, we define the notion of a solution by reformulating the equation (
1
) in terms of Duhamel’s formula involving boundary terms. In Section
4
, we state several linear and nonlinear estimates needed to close the contraction argument. In Section
5
we present the proofs of our main results. First, we show the existence of local solutions via Banach’s fixed point theorem. Then we utilize the smoothing estimate demonstrated in Section
4
to prove the persistence of regularity property of solutions. Using this, we show the uniqueness of low-regularity solutions. Finally, we discuss nonlinear smoothing and the growth of the Sobolev norm of solutions. In Section
6
, we prove the uniqueness of high-regularity solutions by Gronwall’s inequality. Finally, we present the proofs of key multilinear estimates in Section
7
. A standard Lemma that is used throughout the paper is stated separately in the Appendix.

2.
Notation & Function spaces

2.1.
Notation

Define the one-dimensionl Fourier transform as

\displaystyle\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}f(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\xi}f(x)dx.

If
u(x,t)
is a function on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}
we define its space-time Fourier transform
\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)
by

\displaystyle\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)=\mathcal{F}u(\xi,\tau)=\int_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}}e^{-i(x\xi+t\tau)}u(x,t)dxdt.

Let
S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0}
denote the solution to the linear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}
, i.e.
S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0}
solves

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x).\end{cases}

For
\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty)
, let
S^{t}_{0}(u_{0},f)
denote the solution to the corresponding linear initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
:

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad u(0,t)=f(t).\end{cases}

Similarly, let
W_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}v_{0}
and
W^{t}_{0}(v_{0},g)
denote the solutions to the linear Airy equation on
\mathbb{R}
and the linear Airy IBVP on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
,

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\end{cases}\text{and}\quad\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v=0,\quad(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+},\\
v(x,0)=v_{0}(x),\quad v(0,t)=g(t),\end{cases}

respectively.

For a space-time function
f
, we denote

\displaystyle D_{0}f(t)=f(0,t).

We denote by
\chi:=\chi_{(0,\infty)}
the indicator function of
(0,\infty)
. Let
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.

2.2.
Function spaces

Define the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
by the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle\xi\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

and its restriction on
\mathbb{R}^{+}=(0,\infty)
by the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}=\inf\{\|g\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}:g=f\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{+}\}.

Define the Schrödinger Fourier restriction space
X^{s,b}
and the KdV Fourier restriction space
\widetilde{X}^{k,b}
by the following norms:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}=\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\langle\tau+\xi^{2}\rangle^{b}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}},

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}}=\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}\widehat{v}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

To handle the boundary terms, we are forced to choose
b<\frac{1}{2}
. On the other hand, it is known that the key bilinear estimate
\|\partial_{x}(v_{1}v_{2})\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}}\lesssim\|v_{1}\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}}\|v_{2}\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}}
fails for all
b<\frac{1}{2}
. To rectify this, we need the low-frequency modification space
D_{\alpha}
introduced in
[
9
]
defined by the norm

\|v\|_{D_{\alpha}}=\|\chi_{[-1,1]}(\xi)\langle\tau\rangle^{\alpha}\widehat{v}(\xi,\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

We shall write
\widetilde{X}^{k,b}_{\alpha}
for
\widetilde{X}^{k,b}\cap D_{\alpha}
. Note that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\approx\left\|\left(\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}+\chi_{|\xi|<1}\langle\tau\rangle^{\alpha}\right)\widehat{v}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}

\displaystyle\approx\left\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\left(\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}+\chi_{|\xi|<1}\langle\tau\rangle^{\alpha}\right)\widehat{v}(\xi,\tau)\right\|_{L^{2}_{\xi,\tau}}.

For our persistence of regularity argument, we need our resolution space to be embedded in
C_{t}H^{s}_{x}\times C_{t}H^{k}_{x}
. However, since the space
X^{s,b}\times\widetilde{X}^{k,b}_{\alpha}
does not embed in
C_{t}H^{s}_{x}\times C_{t}H^{k}_{x}
for any
b<\frac{1}{2}
, we need the modified spaces
Y^{s,b}
and
\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}
as introduced in
[
4
]
, defined by the following norms:

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}=\|u\|_{X^{s,b}}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{s}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}},

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}=\|u\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}+\|\langle\xi\rangle^{k}\widehat{u}(\xi,\tau)\|_{L^{2}_{\xi}L^{1}_{\tau}}.

For any time interval
I
, define the time-localized space
Y^{s,b}(I)
by the norm

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}=\inf\{\|v\|_{Y^{s,b}}:v=u\text{ on }\mathbb{R}\times I\}.

We analogously define the space
\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I)
. Finally,
\mathcal{H}^{s,k}
will denote the set of all elements
(u_{0},f,v_{0},g)\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{\frac{k+1}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
satisfying the additional compatibility conditions
u_{0}(0)=f(0)
for
s>\frac{1}{2}
and
v_{0}(0)=g(0)
for
k>\frac{1}{2}
.

We collect some basic properties of the above spaces that will be used later.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
4
]
)
.

For any
s,k\in\mathbb{R}
, we have inclusions
Y^{s,b}(I)\hookrightarrow C_{t}H^{s}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R})
and
\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I)\hookrightarrow C_{t}H^{k}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R})
with inequalities

\displaystyle\|u\|_{C_{t}H^{s}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{C_{t}H^{k}_{x}(I\times\mathbb{R})}\leq\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I)}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
27
, Lemma 2.11]
)
.

For any
T>0
and
-\frac{1}{2}<b_{1}<b_{2}<\frac{1}{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{X^{s,b_{1}}}\lesssim T^{b_{2}-b_{1}}\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{X^{s,b_{2}}}

and

\displaystyle\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b_{1}}}\lesssim T^{b_{2}-b_{1}}\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b_{2}}}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
be nonempty open intervals such that
I_{1}\cap I_{2}\neq\varnothing
. Then for any
s,k,b,\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{1}\cup I_{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{1})}+\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{2})}

and

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I_{1}\cup I_{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I_{1})}+\|u\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}(I_{2})}.

Proof.

Let
I
be an open time interval, and
\eta(t)
be a Schwartz function supported on another open interval
J
. We claim that

(3)

\displaystyle\|\eta(t)u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I\cap J)}.

By
[
27
, Theorem 2.11]
and Young’s convolution inequality, we have
\|\eta(t)u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\eta(t)u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}=\inf_{u=\widetilde{u}\text{ on }I\cap J}\|\eta(t)\widetilde{u}\|_{Y^{s,b}(I)}\lesssim\inf_{u=\widetilde{u}\text{ on }I\cap J}\|\widetilde{u}\|_{Y^{s,b}}=\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I\cap J)}.

Finally, by considering smooth cutoff functions
\eta_{1},\,\eta_{2}
such that
\eta_{1}+\eta_{2}=1
on
I_{1}\cup I_{2}
and
\operatorname{supp}(\eta_{1})\cap(I_{2}\setminus I_{1})=\operatorname{supp}(\eta_{2})\cap(I_{1}\setminus I_{2})=\varnothing
, the inequality

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{1}\cup I_{2})}\lesssim\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{1})}+\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}(I_{2})}

readily follows from the triangle inequality and (
3
). We can similarly prove the second inequality.
∎

3.
Notion of a solution

We will construct solutions to (
1
) first by solving some integral equation on the entire real line, and then restricting the corresponding solutions to the half line. For this, we will need the following lemma regarding the extension of Sobolev functions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
:

Lemma 3.1
.

[
12
]

Let
h\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
for some
-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
.

(i) If
-\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{1}{2}
, then
\|\chi_{(0,\infty)}h\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}
.

(ii) If
\frac{1}{2}<s<\frac{3}{2}
and
h(0)=0
, then
\|\chi_{(0,\infty)}h\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|h\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}
.

To construct the solutions to (
1
) we begin by extending
S^{t}_{0}(0,f)
and
W^{t}_{0}(0,g)
to all
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We use the following explicit formulas:

\displaystyle S^{t}_{0}(0,f)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-i\beta^{2}t+i\beta x}\beta\widehat{\chi f}(-\beta^{2})d\beta+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\beta^{2}t-i\beta x}\beta\widehat{\chi f}(-\beta^{2})d\beta

\displaystyle:=S_{1}f+S_{2}f,

\displaystyle W_{0}^{t}(0,g)=\frac{3}{\pi}Re\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\beta^{3}t}e^{-i\beta x\cos(\frac{\pi}{3})}e^{-\beta x\sin(\frac{\pi}{3})}\widehat{\chi g}(\beta^{3})\beta^{2}d\beta,

see
[
10
,
12
]
. Let
\rho\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
be a function supported on
(0,\infty)
such that
\rho
=1 on
[0,\infty)
. We further assume that

(4)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{\infty}e^{-iy\cos(\frac{\pi}{3})}e^{-y\sin(\frac{\pi}{3})}\rho(y)dy=0.

We extend
S^{t}_{0}(0,f)
and
W_{0}^{t}(0,g)
to all
x\in\mathbb{R}
by letting

\displaystyle S_{2}f(x,t)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\beta^{2}t-ix}\rho(\beta x)\beta\widehat{\chi f}(-\beta^{2})d\beta

and

\displaystyle W_{0}^{t}(0,g)=\frac{3}{\pi}Re\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\beta^{3}t}e^{-i\beta x\cos(\frac{\pi}{3})}e^{-\beta x\sin(\frac{\pi}{3})}\rho(\beta x)\widehat{\chi g}(\beta^{3})\beta^{2}d\beta.

For
(u_{0},v_{0})\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\times H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
, let
(u_{0e},v_{0e})
be an extension of

to the full line satisfying

and

. Note that the existence of this extension is assured by Lemma

. Consider the system of integral equations

(5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}u=S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0e}-i\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}F(u,v)dt^{\prime}+S_{0}^{t}(0,f-p-q),\\

,

, and any

\beta(\xi,\tau)=\langle\tau-\xi^{3}\rangle^{b}+\chi_{|\xi|\leq 1}\langle\tau\rangle^{\alpha}
.
However, since

\displaystyle\langle\xi\rangle^{a}=\langle\xi_{1}+\xi_{2}\rangle^{a}\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{a}+\langle\xi_{2}\rangle^{a},

the desired bound directly follows from the

[

, Lemma 5.10 (a)]

5.1.
A contraction argument

In this section, we show the local existence of solutions to (
5
) by a contraction argument. Define the map
\Gamma=(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})
by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(u,v)=\eta(t)S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0e}-i\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}F(u,v)dt^{\prime}+\eta(t)S_{0}^{t}(0,f-p-q),

\displaystyle\Gamma_{2}(u,v)=\eta(t)W_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}v_{0e}+\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}W_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}G(u,v)dt^{\prime}+\eta(t)W_{0}^{t}(0,g-\widetilde{p}-\widetilde{q}),

where

F(u,v)=\eta(t/T)(\alpha uv+\beta|u|^{2}u),\quad p(t)=\eta(t)D_{0}(S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0e}),\\
q(t)=\eta(t)D_{0}\left(\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}F(u,v)dt^{\prime}\right),\quad G(u,v)=\eta(t/T)(\gamma(|u|^{2})_{x}-vv_{x}),\\
\widetilde{p}(t)=\eta(t)D_{0}(W_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}v_{0e}),\quad\widetilde{q}(t)=\eta(t)D_{0}\left(\int_{0}^{t}W_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}G(u,v)dt^{\prime}\right).

Let
(s,k)
be an admissible pair. We start by estimating the
Y^{s,b}
-norm of
\Gamma_{1}(u,v)
. Pick any

such that

, where

is a constant provided in Propositions
4.9
,
4.10
,
4.13
and
4.14
. Combining Lemma
4.6

4.9
and
4.10

\left\|\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}F(u,v)dt^{\prime}\right\|_{Y^{s,b}}\lesssim\|F(u,v)\|_{X^{s,-b}}\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}\|F(u,v)\|_{X^{s,-b^{\prime}}}\\
\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}(\|u\|_{Y^{s,b^{\prime}}}\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b^{\prime}}_{\alpha}}+\|u\|_{Y^{s,b^{\prime}}}^{3})\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}(\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}+\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}^{3}).

Moreover, by Lemmas
4.4
and
3.1
, we have

\|\eta(t)S_{0}^{t}(0,f-p-q)\|_{Y^{s,b}}\lesssim\|(f-p-q)\chi_{(0,\infty)}\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}\\
\lesssim\|f-p\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|q\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}\lesssim\|f\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|p\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}+\|q\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}.

By the Kato smoothing Lemma

\displaystyle\|p\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|\eta(t)S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t}u_{0e}\|_{C_{x}H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}.

By Propositions
4.13
and
4.14
,

(10)

\left\|\int\chi_{R}(\xi,\tau)\langle\tau+\xi^{2}\rangle^{\frac{2s-3}{4}}|\widehat{F(u,v)}(\xi,\tau)|d\xi\right\|_{L^{2}_{\tau}}\\
\lesssim\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}}\|v\|_{\widetilde{X}^{k,b^{\prime}}}+\|\eta(t/T)u\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}}\|u\|_{X^{s,b^{\prime}}}^{2}\\
\lesssim T^{b-b^{\prime}}(\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}+\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}^{3}).

Using Lemma
4.7
and (

\|q\|_{H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim\left\|\eta(t)\int_{0}^{t}S_{\mathbb{R}}^{t-t^{\prime}}F(u,v)dt^{\prime}\right\|_{C_{x}H^{\frac{2s+1}{4}}_{t}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}))}\\

We can similarly show that

\displaystyle\|\Gamma_{2}(u,v)\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}\lesssim\|v_{0}\|_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+\|g\|_{H^{\frac{k+1}{3}}(\mathbb{R}^{+})}+T^{b-b^{\prime}}(\|u\|_{Y^{s,b}}^{2}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k,b}_{\alpha}}^{2}).

5.2.
Persistence of regularity

In this section we establish the following persistence of regularity result. The proof relies on the smoothing estimates in section

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(s_{0},k_{0})
and
(s_{1},k_{1})
be admissible pairs such that

and

. Let

\displaystyle(u,v)\in Y^{s_{1},b}([0,T^{*}])\times\widetilde{Y}^{k_{1},b}_{\alpha}([0,T^{*}]).

Proof.

We first assume that
(s_{1},k_{1})
satisfies
s_{0}\leq s_{1}<s_{0}+a
and
k_{0}\leq k_{1}<k_{0}+a
, where

is a positive smoothing index provided in Propositions
4.13
,
4.14
,
4.15
and
4.16
. Let

. Taking the

), we have for some

\displaystyle T^{\epsilon}(\|u\|_{Y^{s_{0},b}([0,T^{*}])}+\|u\|_{Y^{s_{0},b}([0,T^{*}])}^{2}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k_{0},b}_{\alpha}([0,T^{*}])})\ll 1,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{Y^{s_{1},b}([0,T])}+\|v\|_{\widetilde{Y}^{k_{1},b}_{\alpha}([0,T])}\lesssim\|u_{0e}\|_{H^{s_{1}}}+\|f\|_{H_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}^{\frac{2s_{1}+1}{4}}}+\|v_{0e}\|_{H^{k_{1}}}+\|g\|_{H_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}^{\frac{k_{1}+1}{3}}}.

By a standard blow-up alternative argument, we can pick

only depending on
\|u\|_{Y^{s_{0},b}([0,T^{*}])}
and
\|v\|_{Y^{k_{0},b}_{\alpha}([0,T^{*}])}
such that (

Pick a fixed small number

Remark 5.2
.

For the real-line problem, the persistence of regularity would readily follow from the trivial one-sided smoothing estimate as in Remark

. For the half-line problem it seems that additional correction terms

5.3.
Uniqueness of solutions

Since the solutions we constructed in Section
5.1
were obtained as fixed points of operators that depend on the extensions of the initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
, it is not clear whether or not these solutions depend on the corresponding extensions. That is, to ensure the uniqueness of the solutions to equation (

and

Lemma 5.3

6.
Uniqueness of high regularity solutions

In this section we discuss the uniqueness of solutions to (

(\frac{3}{2}+,\frac{5}{2}+)

The following argument is a variant of the one obtained in
[
19
]
. Assume that
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
are two solutions to (
1
) with the same initial and boundary data. Then the difference
(u,v):=(u_{1}-u_{2},v_{1}-v_{2})
satisfies

(16)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\partial_{x}^{2}u=\alpha(uv_{1}+u_{2}v)+\beta(u|u_{1}|^{2}+(\overline{u}u_{2}+\overline{u_{1}}u)u_{2})

(17)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v+\partial_{x}^{3}v=\partial_{x}\left(\gamma(u\overline{u_{1}}+\overline{u}u_{2})-\frac{1}{2}v(v_{1}+v_{2})\right)

on
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with zero initial and boundary conditions.

We estimate the
H^{1}
-norms of
u
and
v
. In this section, all implicit constants depend only on
\|u_{1}\|_{C_{t}H^{\frac{3}{2}+}_{x}}+\|u_{2}\|_{C_{t}H^{\frac{3}{2}+}_{x}}+\|v_{1}\|_{C_{t}H^{\frac{5}{2}+}_{x}}+\|v_{2}\|_{C_{t}H^{\frac{5}{2}+}_{x}}
. Also, in the following calculations, we assume that
(u_{1},v_{1})
and
(u_{2},v_{2})
are smooth. The argument extends to the general case using a limiting argument, see
[
22
]
for details.

First, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Re}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{u}u_{t}dx

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{u}\left[\alpha(uv_{1}+u_{2}v)+\beta(u|u_{1}|^{2}+(\overline{u}u_{2}+\overline{u_{1}}u)u_{2})-u_{xx}\right]dx.

Integrating by parts and using the zero boundary condition for
u
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\operatorname{Im}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{u}\left[\alpha(uv_{1}+u_{2}v)+\beta(u|u_{1}|^{2}+(\overline{u}u_{2}+\overline{u_{1}}u)u_{2}\right]dx

\displaystyle\leq 2|\alpha|\left(\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}\|v_{1}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}+\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\|u_{2}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+2|\beta|\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}\left(\|u_{1}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}+\|u_{2}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}.

We used the embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Also we have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\|v\|_{L^{2}_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}}^{2}

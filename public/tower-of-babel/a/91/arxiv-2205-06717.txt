The existence of m m -tree-connected ( g , f + f ′ − m ) (g,f+f^{\prime}-m) -factors using ( g , f ) (g,f) -factors and m m -tree-connected ( m , f ′ ) (m,f^{\prime}) -factors
Morteza Hasanvand

Abstract
Let G G be a graph and let g g , f f , and f ′ f^{\prime} be three positive integer-valued functions on V ⁡ ( G ) V(G) with g ≤ f g\leq f . Tokuda, Xu, and Wang (2003) showed that if G G contains a ( g , f ) (g,f) -factor and a spanning f ′ f^{\prime} -tree, then G G also contains a connected ( g , f + f ′ − 1 ) (g,f+f^{\prime}-1) -factor. In this note, we develop their result to a tree-connected version by proving that if G G contains a ( g , f ) (g,f) -factor and an m m -tree-connected ( m , f ′ ) (m,f^{\prime}) -factor, then G G also contains an m m -tree-connected ( g , f + f ′ − m ) (g,f+f^{\prime}-m) -factor, provided that f ≥ m f\geq m . In addition, we show that g g allows to be nonnegative. Keywords : Spanning tree; matching; connected factor; bounded degree; tree-connectivity.

Introduction

In this note, all graphs have no loop, but multiple edges are allowed and a simple graph is a graph without multiple edges.
Let
G
be a graph.
The vertex set, the edge set, and the maximum degree of
G
are denoted by
V(G)
,
E(G)
,
\Delta(G)
, respectively.
The degree
d_{G}(v)
of a vertex

is the number of edges of
G
incident to
v
.
The set of edges of
G
that are incident to
v
is denoted by
E_{G}(v)
.
For a set
A
of integers, an
A
-factor
is a spanning subgraph with vertex degrees in
A
.
Let
g
and

be two nonnegative integer-valued functions on
V(G)
.
A
(g,f)
-factor
refers to spanning subgraph
F
such that for each vertex
v
,
g(v)\leq d_{F}(v)\leq f(v)
.
A graph
G
is called
m
-tree-connected
, if it has
m
edge-disjoint spanning trees.
Throughout this article, all variables

are positive integers.

Li, Xu, Chen, and Liu
[
5
]
showed that every graph containing a Hamiltonian path and a

-factor. More generally, they proved the following stronger assertion. Note that by inserting a Hamiltonian path into a

.
(
[

]
)

Let

-factor and a Hamiltonian path, then it contains a connected
(g,f+1)
-factor,
where
g
and

are positive integer-valued functions on

with
2\leq g\leq f
.

Later, Tokuda, Xu, and Wang
[
6
]

to the following version by replacing the assumption of having a spanning
f^{\prime}
-free.
In Section

, we refine this result by showing that

Theorem 1.2

.
(
[

]
)

Let

be a graph. If

-factor and a spanning

-factor, where

,

, and

-factor using a

-factor and an

-factor. As a consequence, if a graph

Theorem 1.3

.

Let

2
Connected factors with bound degrees

In this section, we devote a stronger version to Theorem
1.2
as the following result
which provides a relationship between connected factors, matchings, and spanning trees.
This result was implicitly proved in
[
6
]
for simple graphs and factors
F
with minimum degree at least one. The proof presented here is a revised version of the proof of Theorem 1 in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.1

.

Let
G
be a graph with a factor
F
.
Let
M
be a matching of
F
having exactly one edge of every non-trivial component of
F
such that the edge is incident to
a non-cut vertex of
F
. If
T
is a spanning tree of
G
, then the factor
F\setminus E(M)
of
G
can be extended to a connected factor
H

such that for each vertex
v
,

d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-1\}.

Proof.

We may assume that
G=T\cup F
.
Set
M=\{x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,x_{t}y_{t}\}
,
X=\{x_{i}:1\leq i\leq t\}
, and
Y=\{y_{i}:1\leq i\leq t\}
.
Assume that each
x_{i}\in X
is not a cut vertex in
F
.
For a graph
G^{\prime}
and a vertex
v
, we denote by
E_{G^{\prime}}(v)
the set of edges of
G^{\prime}
that are incident to
v
.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be the set of all
2
-tuples
(H,T_{0})
such that

H
is a connected factor of
G
containing
E(F)\setminus E(M)
and
T_{0}
is a spanning tree of
H
in which
for each
v\in V(H)
the following condition holds:
if (i)
d_{H}(v)=d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)
or (ii)
v=x_{i}
and
x_{i}y_{i}\notin E(H)
,
then
E_{T_{0}}(v)\cap E_{F}(v)=\emptyset
.
Note that
(G,T)\in\mathcal{A}
.
Define
\mathcal{A}_{0}
to be the set of all
2
-tuples
(H,T_{0})\in\mathcal{A}
with the minimum
|E(H)|
.
Now, we prove the following claim.

Claim A.
If
(H,T_{0})\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
, then
for each vertex
v
,
d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-1\}
.

Proof of Claim A.

Suppose, to the contrary, that

d_{H}(u)=d_{T}(u)+d_{F}(u)
and
d_{F}(u)>0
, for some vertex
u
.
Let
C
be the non-trivial component of
F
containing
u
and let

x_{i}y_{i}
be the single edge in
E(C)\cap E(M)
.
For convenience, let us set
x=x_{i}
and
y=y_{i}
.
First, assume that
d_{H}(v)=d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)
, for all
v\in V(C)
.
Since
d_{H}(x)=d_{G}(x)
, we have
xy\in E(H)
and
also item (i) implies that
E_{T_{0}}(x)\cap E_{F}(x)=\emptyset
and
xy\notin E(T_{0})
.
Define
H^{\prime}=H-xy
and
T^{\prime}_{0}=T_{0}
.
Note that
H^{\prime}
contains
E(F)\setminus E(M)
,

d_{H^{\prime}}(y)<d_{G}(y)\leq d_{T}(y)+d_{F}(y)
, and

E_{T^{\prime}_{0}}(x)\cap E_{F}(x)=\emptyset
.
It is easy check that
(H^{\prime},T^{\prime}_{0})\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Since
|E(H^{\prime})|=|E(H)|-1
, we arrive at a contradiction.

Now, assume that
d_{H}(w)<d_{T}(w)+d_{F}(w)
, for some vertex
w
in
V(C)
.
If
xy\in E(H)
, we take
ab
to be an edge on
a
wu
-path
in the connected graph
C
such that

d_{H}(a)<d_{T}(a)+d_{F}(a)
and
d_{H}(b)=d_{T}(b)+d_{F}(b)
.
If
xy\notin E(H)
, then
d_{H}(y)<d_{G}(y)\leq d_{T}(y)+d_{F}(y)
and also
x\neq u
.
In this case, we can take
ab
to be an edge on
a
yu
-path in the connected graph
C-x
such that

d_{H}(a)<d_{T}(a)+d_{F}(a)
and
d_{H}(b)=d_{T}(b)+d_{F}(b)
.
In both cases, since
d_{H}(b)=d_{G}(b)
, we must have
ab\in E(H)

and also item (i) implies that
E_{T_{0}}(b)\cap E_{F}(b)=\emptyset
and
ab\notin E(T_{0})
.
Thus there is an edge
bc\in E(T_{0})
such that
T_{0}-bc+ab
is connected (we might have
b=c
).
Define
H^{\prime}=H-bc
and
T^{\prime}_{0}=T_{0}-bc+ab
.
Since
E_{T_{0}}(b)\cap E_{F}(b)=\emptyset
, we have
bc\notin E(F)
and so the graph
H^{\prime}
contains
E(F)\setminus E(M)
.
Moreover,
d_{H^{\prime}}(a)\leq d_{H}(a)<d_{T}(a)+d_{F}(a)
,
d_{H^{\prime}}(b)<d_{G}(b)\leq d_{T}(b)+d_{F}(b)
,
and
d_{H^{\prime}}(c)<d_{G}(c)\leq d_{T}(c)+d_{F}(c)
.
According to the construction, if
xy\notin E(H^{\prime})
then we must have
xy\notin E(H)
and
x\notin\{a,b\}
;
moreover, by item (ii),
E_{T_{0}}(x)\cap E_{F}(x)=\emptyset
and so
E_{T^{\prime}_{0}}(x)\cap E_{F}(x)=\emptyset
.
It is easy to check that
(H^{\prime},T^{\prime}_{0})\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Since
|E(H^{\prime})|=|E(H)|-1
, we again arrive at a contradiction and so the claim holds.

Claim B.
If
(H,T_{0})
is a
2
-tuple of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
with the maximum
|E(H)\cap E(M)|
, then
for all vertices
v
,
d_{H}(v)\geq d_{F}(v)
.

Proof of Claim B.

Suppose, to the contrary, that

d_{H}(u)<d_{F}(u)
for some vertex
u
.
Hence
u
must be incident to the edges in
E(M)\setminus E(H)
.
We may assume that
u\in\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
and
x_{i}y_{i}\notin E(H)
, and also
d_{H}(u)=d_{F}(u)-1
.
If
u=x_{i}\in X
, then by item (ii),
E_{T_{0}}(u)\cap E_{F}(u)=\emptyset
and so

d_{H}(u)\geq d_{F\setminus E(M)}(u)+d_{T_{0}}(u)\geq d_{F}(u)
.
Thus
u=y_{i}\in Y
.
For convenience, let us again set
x=x_{i}
and
y=y_{i}
.
Since
xy\not\in E(T_{0})
, there is an edge
xz\in E(T_{0})
such that
T_{0}-xz+xy
is connected.
Note that we must have
y\neq z
.
Define
H^{\prime}=H-xz+xy
and
T^{\prime}_{0}=T_{0}-xz+xy
.
By item (ii),
E_{T_{0}}(x)\cap E_{F}(x)=\emptyset
, and so
xz\notin E(F)
.
This implies that
H^{\prime}
contains
E(F)\setminus E(M)
.
Moreover,
d_{H^{\prime}}(x)<d_{G}(x)\leq d_{T}(x)+d_{F}(x)
,

d_{H^{\prime}}(z)<d_{G}(z)\leq d_{T}(z)+d_{F}(z)
, and
d_{H^{\prime}}(y)=d_{F}(y)<d_{T}(y)+d_{F}(y)
, and also

xy\in E(H^{\prime})
.
It is easy check that
(H^{\prime},T^{\prime}_{0})\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
.
Since
|E(H^{\prime})|=|E(H)|
and
|E(H^{\prime})\cap E(M)|=|E(H)\cap E(M)|+1
, we arrive at a contradiction. Hence the claim holds.

Since
\mathcal{A}_{0}
is not empty, we can consider
(H,T^{\prime})
as a
2
-tuple of
\mathcal{A}_{0}

with the maximum
|E(H)\cap E(M)|
.
By Claims A and B, the graph
H
is the desired connected factor we are looking for.

\Box

[

]
and
can be proved by Theorem
2.1

with a little extra effort.

Corollary 2.2

.
(
[
2
]
)

If every matching of a graph
G
can be extended to a spanning
f^{\prime}
-tree, then
every
(g,f)
-factor can also be extended to a connected
(g,f+f^{\prime}-1)
-factor, where

g
is a nonnegative integer-valued function on
V(G)
, and
f
and
f^{\prime}
are positive integer-valued functions on
V(G)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a
(g,f)
-factor of
G
and consider
M
as an arbitrary matching of
F

satisfying the assumption of Theorem
2.1
.
Note that every non-trivial component of
F
has a non-cut vertex.
By the assumption, the graph
G
has a spanning
f^{\prime}
-tree
T
containing
M
.
Thus Theorem
2.1
implies that
T\cup F
has a connected factor
H_{0}
containing
E(F)\setminus E(M)
such that for each vertex
v
,
d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H_{0}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-1\}
. Define
H=H_{0}\cup M
.
According to this definition,
H
contains
F
and is connected as well.
Let
v\in V(H)
. If
v
is not incident to the edges of
M
,
then we still have
d_{H}(v)=d_{H_{0}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-1\}
. Otherwise, since
E(M)\subseteq E(T)\cap E(F)
, we must automatically have
d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-d_{M}(v)=d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-1
.
Hence
H
is the desired connected
(g,f+f^{\prime}-1)
-factor we are looking for.

[

]
discovered the following result, when

is a positive function.

Corollary 2.3

.

Let
G
be a graph. If
G
contains a
(g,f)
-factor and a spanning
f^{\prime}
-tree, then
G
has a connected
(g,f+f^{\prime}-1)
-factor, where
g
is a nonnegative integer-valued function on
V(G)
, and
f
and
f^{\prime}
are positive integer-valued functions on
V(G)
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a
(g,f)
-factor and let
T
be a spanning
f^{\prime}
-tree of
G
.
Consider
M
as an arbitrary matching of
F
satisfying the assumption of Theorem
2.1
.
Note that every non-trivial component of
F
has a non-cut vertex. For finding such a vertex, it is enough to select a vertex with degree one from a spanning tree of that component.
Now, by Theorem
2.1
, the graph
G
has a connected factor
H
such that for each vertex
v
,
g^{\prime}(v)\leq d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-1\}\leq f(v)+\max\{0,f^{\prime}(v)-1\}=f(v)+f^{\prime}(v)-1
.
Hence the assertion holds.

3
Highly tree-connected factors with bound degrees

In this section, we first devote a similar tree-connected version to Theorem
2.1
in order to prove Theorem
1.3
. For this purpose, we need the following lemma which is a useful tool for finding a pair of edges such that replacing them preserves tree-connectivity of a given tree-connected factor (this is a basic tool in matroid theory
[
1
]
).

Lemma 3.1

.
(see
[
3
]
)

Let
G
be graph with an
m
-tree-connected factor
H
and let
xy\in E(G)\setminus E(H)
. If
Q
is a minimal
m
-tree-connected subgraph of
G
including
x
and
y
, then for every
e\in E(Q)
, the graph
H-e+xy
remains
m
-tree-connected.

The following theorem is essential in this section.

Theorem 3.2

.

Let
G
be a graph with a factor
F
and an
m
-tree-connected factor
T
. If
F\setminus E(T)
is a bipartite graph with one partite set
A=\{v\in V(G):d_{F}(v)\leq m\}
,
then
G
has an
m
-tree-connected factor
H
containing
F\setminus E(M)
such that for each vertex
v
,

d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\},

provided that
M
is a bipartite factor of
F\setminus E(T)
such that for each
v\in V(G)\setminus A
,

d_{M}(v)+d_{F\cap T}(v)\geq m
.

Proof.

Let
B=\{v\in V(G):d_{F}(v)\geq m+1\}=V(G)\setminus A
. Define
G_{0}=T\cup F
and
F^{\prime}=F\setminus E(M)
.
Let
T_{0}
be an
m
-tree-connected factor of
G_{0}\setminus E(M)
such that
(i) for all
v\in A
,
d_{T_{0}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)
, and
(ii) for all
v\in B
,
d_{T_{0}\cup F^{\prime}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-m
,
and (iii) for all
v\in V(G)
,
E_{F\cap T}(v)\subseteq E_{T_{0}}(v)
.
Note that
T
is a natural candidate for
T_{0}
,
since for each
v\in B
, by the assumption, we have

d_{T\cup F^{\prime}}(v)=d_{T}(v)+d_{F^{\prime}}(v)-d_{F^{\prime}\cap T}(v)=d_{T}(v)+(d_{F}(v)-d_{M}(v))-d_{F\cap T}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-m
.
Consider
T_{0}
with the maximum
|E(T_{0})\cap E(F)|
.
Now, are ready to prove the following claim.

Claim.

If
vx\in E(F)\setminus E(M)
and
v\in A
, then
vx\in E(T_{0})
.

Proof of Claim.

Suppose, to the contrary, that there is an edge
vx\in E(F)\setminus(E(M)\cup E(T_{0}))
with
v\in A
.
By item (iii), we must have
x\in B
.
Otherwise,
vx
is an edge of
E(F)\setminus E(T)
with both ends in
A
which is impossible.
Let
Q
be a minimal
m
-tree-connected subgraph of
T_{0}
including
v
and
x
.
Since
Q
is
m
-edge-connected,

d_{Q}(v)\geq m
.
On the other hand,
d_{F}(v)\leq m
.
Thus there exists an edge
vy\in E(Q)\setminus E(F)
so that
vy\in E(T_{0})\setminus E(F)

(we might have
x=y
).
Define
T^{\prime}_{0}=T_{0}-vy+vx
.
By Lemma
3.1
, the graph
T_{0}

is still
m
-tree-connected.
Note that
T^{\prime}_{0}
is a factor of
G_{0}\setminus E(M)
, because of
vx\in E(F)\setminus E(M)
.
In addition,
d_{T^{\prime}_{0}}(u)\leq d_{T_{0}}(u)
when
u\in\{v,y\}
,
and
d_{T^{\prime}_{0}\cup F^{\prime}}(u)\leq d_{T_{0}\cup F^{\prime}}(u)
when
u\in\{x,y\}
.
It is not hard to check that
T^{\prime}_{0}
satisfies items (i), (ii), and (iii).
Since
|E(T_{0}^{\prime})\cap E(F)|>|E(T_{0})\cap E(F)|
, we derive a contradiction to the maximality of
T_{0}
.
Hence the claim holds.

Let
H
be an
m
-tree-connected factor of
G_{0}
containing
E(F^{\prime})
satisfying

d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\}
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Note that
T_{0}\cup F^{\prime}
is a natural candidate for
H
.
In fact, if
v\in A
, then by the above-mentioned claim, we must have
d_{T_{0}\cup F^{\prime}}(v)=d_{T_{0}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)=d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\}
.
Also, if
v\in B
, then by the assumption on
T_{0}
, we must have
d_{T_{0}\cup F^{\prime}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-m
.
Consider
H
with the maximum
|E(H)\cap E(M)|
.
We are going to show that
H
is the desired factor we are looking for.
To prove this, it remains to show that

d_{H}(v)\geq d_{F}(v)
for all
v\in V(G)
.
Suppose, to the contrary, that
d_{H}(x)<d_{F}(x)
for a vertex
x\in V(G)
.
Thus there exists an edge
vx\in E(F)\setminus E(H)
.
We may assume that
x\in B
, because
d_{H}(v)\geq m\geq d_{F}(v)
for all
v\in A
.
Hence
vx\in E(M)\setminus E(H)
.
Let
Q
be the minimal
m
-tree-connected subgraph of
H
including
vx
.
Since
d_{F}(v)\leq m\leq d_{Q}(v)
and
vx\in E(F)\setminus E(Q)
, there must be an edge
vy\in E(Q)\setminus E(F)
(we might have
x=y
).
Define
H^{\prime}=H-vy+vx
.
By Lemma
3.1
, the graph
H^{\prime}
is still
m
-tree-connected.
Note that
d_{H^{\prime}}(u)\leq d_{H}(u)\leq d_{T}(u)+\max\{0,d_{F}(u)-m\}
for all
u\in A
, and

d_{H^{\prime}}(u)\leq d_{H}(u)\leq d_{T}(u)+d_{F}(u)-m
for all
u\in B\setminus\{x\}
.
In addition,
d_{H^{\prime}}(x)\leq d_{F}(x)\leq d_{T}(x)+d_{F}(x)-m
, because of
d_{T}(v)\geq m
.
Since
|E(H^{\prime})\cap E(M)|>|E(H)\cap E(M)|
, we derive a contradiction to the maximality of
H
.
Hence the proof is completed.

Remark 3.3

.

Note that the results of this section can be developed to an
m
-rigid version instead of the current

-tree-connected version using the same process in the proofs. Moreover, these results can be developed to a supermodular version, see

Now, we are ready to prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.4

.

Let
G
be a graph with an
m
-tree-connected factor
T
. If
G
has a factor
F
, then it has an
m
-tree-connected factor
H
such that for each vertex
v
,

d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\}.

Proof.

By induction on
|E(F)|
. For
|E(F)|=0
, the proof is clear.
We may assume that
|E(F)|>0
.
Put
A=\{v\in V(G):d_{F}(v)\leq m\}
and
B=\{v\in V(G):d_{F}(v)\geq m+1\}=V(G)\setminus A
.
If there exists an edge
xy\in E(F)\setminus E(T)
with both ends in
A
,
then by applying the induction hypothesis to the graphs
G^{\prime}=G
and
F^{\prime}=F-xy
,
there is an
m
-tree-connected factor
H
such that for each vertex
v
,

d_{F^{\prime}}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F^{\prime}}(v)-m\}
.
Note that for each
v\in\{x,y\}
,
d_{F}(v)\leq m\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)=d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\}
.
It is easy to see that
H
is the desired factor.
If there exists an edge
xy\in E(F)\setminus E(T)
with both ends in
B
,
then by applying the induction hypothesis to the graphs
G^{\prime}=G-xy
and
F^{\prime}=F-xy
,
there is an
m
-tree-connected factor
H^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that for each vertex
v
,

d_{F^{\prime}}(v)\leq d_{H^{\prime}}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F^{\prime}}(v)-m\}
.
Let
H=H^{\prime}+xy
. If
v\in\{x,y\}
, then we still have
d_{F^{\prime}}(v)\geq m
and so

d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+d_{F}(v)-m
.
Hence it is easy to see that
H
is the desired factor.
We may therefore assume that
F\setminus E(T)
is a bipartite graph with the bipartition
(A,B)
.
Take
M
to be the graph
F\setminus E(T)
. For each
v\in B
,

d_{M}(v)+d_{F\cap T}(v)=d_{F}(v)\geq m
.
Thus by Theorem
3.2
, there is a factor
H
with the desired properties.

\Box

The following corollary provides an interesting and equivalent version for Theorem
3.4
.

Corollary 3.5

.

Let
G
be a graph. If
G
contains a
(g,f)
-factor and an
m
-tree-connected
(m,f^{\prime})
-factor, then it has an
m
-tree-connected
(g,f+f^{\prime}-m)
-factor, where
g
,
f
,
f^{\prime}
are nonnegative integer-valued functions on
V(G)
with
g\leq f
,
m\leq f
, and
m\leq f^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a
(g,f)
-factor and let
T
be an
m
-tree-connected
(m,f^{\prime})
-factor of
G
.
By Theorem
3.4
, the graph
G
has an
m
-tree-connected factor
H
such that for each vertex
v
,
g(v)\leq d_{F}(v)\leq d_{H}(v)\leq d_{T}(v)+\max\{0,d_{F}(v)-m\}\leq f^{\prime}(v)+\max\{0,f(v)-m\}=f^{\prime}(v)+f(v)-m
.
Hence the assertion holds.

\Box

Wavepackets on de Sitter Spacetime
João C.A. BARATA † and Marcos BRUM †‡

Abstract
We construct wavepackets on de Sitter spacetime, with masses consistently defined from the eigenvalues of an irreducible representation of a Casimir element in the universal enveloping algebra of the Lorentz algebra and analyse their asymptotic behaviour. Furthermore, we show that, in the limit as the de Sitter radius tends to infinity, the wavepackets tend to the wavepackets of Minkowski spacetime and the plane waves arising after contraction have support sharply located on the mass shell.



keywords
quantum field theory on de Sitter spacetime; Haag–Ruelle scattering theory; theory of group representations; algebraic quantum field theory

The particle concept on a curved spacetime is ambiguous. This is intrinsically related to the absence of a preferred Hilbert space of states and also presents itself in the absence of an

There is also a Casimir element in the universal enveloping algebra of the Lorentz algebra which can be represented on a similar Hilbert space, and the elements in its spectrum can be related to the mass. However, wavepackets on de Sitter spacetime have not yet been defined in the literature. We are going to define wavepackets on de Sitter spacetime and prove some important properties.

Another desirable feature of any physical theory formulated on a curved spacetime is that it has a sensible flat limit. More precisely, in the limit as the curvature of spacetime tends to zero, one must recover the corresponding physical theory formulated on Minkowski spacetime. homogeneous of certain degree is constructed. Secondly this operator is extended to
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{C})
(with volume measure) by using the Mellin transform. At this step one already obtains a Fourier transform between square integrable functions on the light cone. Thirdly one defines functions in the subregion of
\mathbb{M}_{n+1}
described by
\sqrt{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j})^{2}}>|x_{0}|
using the Fourier expression of functions on the light cone, then proves that the original functions are boundary values of these newly defined ones and their restriction to
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
is surjective on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
.

The region
\sqrt{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(x_{j})^{2}}\eqqcolon|{\bf x}|>|x_{0}|
in
\mathbb{M}_{n+1}
is covered by the hyperboloids

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{R}\colon\ \left\{p\in\mathbb{M}_{n+1}\,|\,-(x_{0}(p))^{2}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}(x_{k}(p))^{2}=R^{2}\right\}.

Let
\Box_{R}=\Box|_{\mathfrak{H}_{R}}
and
f,g\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathfrak{H}_{R},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
be such that
\Box_{R}f=\lambda^{2}f
and
\Box_{R}g=\lambda^{2}g
. Then,

\displaystyle u(R,p)\coloneqq R^{-(n-1)/2+\mathrm{i}\rho}f(p)+R^{-(n-1)/2-\mathrm{i}\rho}g(p)

(4.4)

is a solution of the massless wave equation on
\mathbb{M}_{n+1}
,
\Box u=0
, where

\displaystyle\rho=\sqrt{\lambda^{2}R^{2}-(n-1)^{2}/4}.

Defining on
\mathfrak{C}
the coordinates

,
{\bf x^{\prime}}=\frac{{\bf x}}{s}
,
x^{\prime}_{0}=\frac{x_{0}}{s}=\pm 1

\displaystyle v(s,x^{\prime}_{0},{\bf x^{\prime}})=s^{\sigma}\varpi(x^{\prime}_{0},{\bf x^{\prime}}),

\displaystyle||f||_{\sigma}^{2}\coloneqq\iint|\varpi(x^{\prime}_{0},{\bf x^{\prime}})|^{2}\mathrm{d}x^{\prime}_{0}\mathrm{d}^{n-1}x^{\prime},

\displaystyle s^{-(n-1)/2-\mathrm{i}\rho}\iint|a|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\left[\Theta(a)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)}\Theta(-a)\right]\times\cdot\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime}

and

where

\displaystyle|d(\rho)|=(2\pi)^{-\frac{(n+1)}{2}}\frac{\left|\Upgamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)\right|}{\left|\Upgamma(-\mathrm{i}\rho)\right|}

where

) as a transformation on functions defined on a homogeneous space acted upon by a semi-simple Lie group (as the Lorentz group). Later it received a more geometric interpretation by Helgason (see
[
32
,
58
]
). We will call it here simply “Fourier transform”.

5
Contraction of the Lorentz algebra

After we construct the wavepackets on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
we will compare them with the wavepackets constructed on
\mathbb{M}_{n}

[
34
]
(note that
\mathbb{M}_{n}
has the same number of dimensions as
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
, differently from
\mathbb{M}_{n+1}
, in which
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
is embedded). Although the spacetimes are different, at any point of a curved spacetime (of dimension
n
) the components of the metric tensor can be made equal to the components of the metric tensor of
\mathbb{M}_{n}

[
46
]
, hence a (possibly infinitesimal) neighbourhood of the curved spacetime resembles (i.e., the effects of the curvature can be treated as perturbations) the Minkowski spacetime. Besides, on the level of Lie algebras, the contraction of algebras
[
37
]
allows us to transform the Lorentz algebra
\mathfrak{so}(1,n)
into the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}\coloneqq\mathfrak{so}(1,n-1)\subsetplus\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

As observed in
[
27
]
, almost every element
g\in L
can be written as the product
g=\text{ban}
, with
\text{b}\in B
, where
B
is the normalizer of a subgroup of
L
isomorphic to
\text{SO}_{0}(1,n-1)
.
B
is composed of elements of the form

\displaystyle B\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\text{SO}_{0}(1,n-1)&0\\
0&\pm 1\end{pmatrix}.

Let now
b\in\mathfrak{b}
(the Lie algebra associated to
B
),
a\in\mathfrak{a}
and
n_{i}\in\mathfrak{n}
and define

\displaystyle b^{\prime}\coloneqq b,\qquad a^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{1}{R}a\qquad\text{and}\qquad n^{\prime}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{1}{R}n_{i}.

(5.1)

In the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
, one verifies that the commutation relations satisfied by
b^{\prime}
,
a^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
are those satisfied by the generators of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, where the elements
b^{\prime}
generate the subalgebra
\mathfrak{so}(1,n-1)
(as
b
did),
a^{\prime}
is the generator of time translations and
n^{\prime}
are the generators of spatial translations.
a^{\prime}
and
n^{\prime}
comprise the abelian subalgebra which is normalized by
\mathfrak{so}(1,n-1)
.

The two pictures above are comparable. On the de Sitter spacetime, one may consider a neighbourhood
\mathcal{O}_{p}
of a point
p\in\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
such that
\forall\,x\in\mathcal{O}_{p}
, the components of the metric tensor in the region
\mathcal{O}_{p}
are equal to the components of the metric tensor of the Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{M}_{n}
. Hence, after the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
is taken, the spacetime becomes the Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{M}_{n}
and its group of isometries is the Poincaré group
P_{n}
.

5.1
Representation of the Poincaré algebra

The representation of the Lorentz group defined in (
2.2
) can be induced from a representation of the closed subgroup

\displaystyle Q=MAN\subset L.

Moreover, the induction procedure shows that the functions
f\in\mathcal{L}^{2}(L,\mathrm{d}L)
are completely determined if their values on the quotient space
BAN/MAN=B/M
are known (see the construction of the induced representation in
[
20
, Section 6.1]
), where
\mathrm{d}L
is the unique left invariant Haar measure on
L
. However,
B/M
is diffeomorphic to the hyperboloids
[
27
,
43
]

\displaystyle-x_{0}^{2}+x_{1}^{2}+\dots+x_{n-1}^{2}=-R^{2},

which are the intersections between the hyperplane
x_{n}^{2}=2R^{2}
and the de Sitter spacetime:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{\pm}\coloneqq\big\{p\in\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}\,|\,{-}x_{0}(p)^{2}+x_{1}(p)^{2}+\dots+x_{n-1}(p)^{2}=-R^{2},\,x_{0}(p)\gtrless 0\big\}.

The quotient space
B/M
remains unaltered by the contraction procedure. Therefore, after the contraction, the representation of the Poincaré group
P_{n}
is determined by its representations on the two disjoint hyperboloids
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{+}
and
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{-}
, i.e., it is the direct sum of these subrepresentations. The Hilbert spaces on which these subrepresentations act are
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{+},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
and
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{-},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
, respectively, which are subspaces of
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
. Therefore the identity operator
\mathbbm{1}
on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
is also an identity operator on both
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{+},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
and
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{-},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
.

On the other hand, the irreducible representation on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{+},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
differs from the irreducible representation on
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{-},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
only by the change
\mu^{\prime}\rightarrow-\mu^{\prime}

[
43
]
, where
\mu^{\prime}
is related to the mass of the plane wave of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
by (
4.3
). Furthermore, the mass
\mu
is related to the eigenvalue of the Casimir operator
{\bf C}^{2}
of the Lorentz algebra
\mathfrak{l}
. As we will see now, the eigenvalues of this operator can be easily related to the eigenvalues of a Casimir operator
{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}
of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
.

Let us focus on the irreducible representation of the Lorentz algebra presented in (
3.1
), restricted to, say,
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{+},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
. Both this representation and the one arising after contraction are irreducible. The Casimir operator is (
1\leq i,j\leq n-1
)

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}{\bf m}_{ij}{\bf m}_{ij}+\sum_{i}{\bf m}_{in}{\bf m}_{in}-\sum_{i}{\bf m}_{i0}{\bf m}_{i0}-{\bf a}^{2}=-\mu^{2}R^{2}\mathbbm{1},

where
{\bf a}
is the representation of
a=m_{n0}
. Denoting the representations of
n_{i}
by
{\bf n}_{i}
,

\displaystyle\sum_{i<j}{\bf m}_{ij}{\bf m}_{ij}+\sum_{i}({\bf n}_{i}-{\bf m}_{i0})({\bf n}_{i}-{\bf m}_{i0})-\sum_{i}{\bf m}_{i0}{\bf m}_{i0}-{\bf a}^{2}=-\mu^{2}R^{2}\mathbbm{1}\therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{R^{2}}\sum_{i<j}{\bf m}_{ij}{\bf m}_{ij}+\sum_{i}\left({\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}-{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}\frac{1}{R}{\bf m}_{i0}-\frac{1}{R}{\bf m}_{i0}{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}\right)-({\bf a^{\prime}})^{2}=-\mu^{2}\mathbbm{1}.

(5.2)

Hence, after we take the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
, we find the Casimir operator
{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}
of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, together with its eigenvalue:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}({\bf n^{\prime}}_{i})^{2}-({\bf a^{\prime}})^{2}=-\mu^{2}\mathbbm{1}\eqqcolon{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}.

(5.3)

If we had restricted the irreducible representation of the Lorentz group to the Hilbert space
\mathcal{L}^{2}(\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{b}^{-},\mathrm{d}\Sigma)
, we would only need to make the modification
\mu^{\prime}\mapsto-\mu^{\prime}
, and the above reasoning would apply without further changes. But this modification does not change the value of
\mu^{2}
. Therefore the eigenvalue of the Casimir operator
{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}
of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
is uniquely determined by the eigenvalue of the Casimir operator
{\bf C}^{2}
of the Lorentz algebra
\mathfrak{l}
. This result is the first step in the comparison of the wavepackets on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
, to be constructed in the following section, and the usual wavepackets on
\mathbb{M}_{n}
. We record this result in the following

Lemma 5.1
.

The irreducible representation of the Casimir operator
{\bf C}^{2}
of the Lorentz algebra
\mathfrak{l}
, with eigenvalue
-\mu^{2}R^{2}
, denoted by
{}_{\mu}{\bf C}^{2}
, under the contraction of the Lorentz algebra
\mathfrak{l}
into the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
presented in the former section, is contracted towards the direct sum of two irreducible representations of the Casimir operator
{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}
of the Poincaré algebra
\mathfrak{p}_{n}
, both of them with eigenvalue
-\mu^{2}
, denoted by
{}_{\pm\mu}{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}
,

{}_{\mu}{\bf C}^{2}\underset{R\rightarrow\infty}{\longrightarrow}{{}_{+\mu}{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}}\oplus{{}_{-\mu}{\bf\mathcal{P}}^{2}}.

6.1
Asymptotic behaviour

The estimate of the asymptotic behaviour of the wavepacket is crucial for the construction of a state which, asymptotically, can be interpreted as a free particle. The wavepacket on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
is defined in (
6.1
) above. Since
\xi\in\mathscr{A}
,
\xi\cdot\xi=0
and
\xi_{0}=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\xi_{i})^{2}}
, the wavepacket is a function of fast decrease, in the sense that, if
s\coloneqq|x_{0}|+|{\bf x}|\rightarrow\infty
, where
|{\bf x}|\coloneqq\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i})^{2}}
, the wavepacket decays faster than any inverse power of
s
. This is stated and proved in the following

Theorem 6.2
.

A wavepacket on de Sitter spacetime is a function of fast decrease.

Proof.

First, we prove the theorem using the ambient coordinate system.

Since the derivatives of the wavepacket are the smearing of
\hat{f}
with the derivatives of the plane wave, we will focus only on
\Psi_{\mu}
(see equation (
4.11
)). We will apply the stationary phase method to estimate the asymptotic behaviour of
f

[
51
]
. The oscillatory terms are

\displaystyle\left|\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right|^{-(n-1)/2+\mathrm{i}\mu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{\left(-\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\mu^{\prime}\right)\log\left|\frac{-x_{0}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\xi_{i})^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\xi_{i}}{\mu R}\right|\right\}

\displaystyle=\exp\left\{(|x_{0}|+|{\bf x}|)\left(-\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\mu^{\prime}\right)\Phi_{x}(\xi)\right\},

where

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{|x_{0}|+|{\bf x}|}\log\left|\frac{-x_{0}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(\xi_{i})^{2}}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\xi_{i}}{\mu R}\right|.

(6.3)

It must first be checked whether
\Phi_{x}(\xi)
has any fixed points.

\displaystyle\grad\Phi_{x}(\xi)=\frac{1}{(|x_{0}|+|{\bf x}|)(x\cdot\xi)}\left(x_{1}-x_{0}\frac{\xi_{1}}{\xi_{0}},\ldots,x_{n}-x_{0}\frac{\xi_{n}}{\xi_{0}}\right)

(this expression is valid for the two possible signs of

\forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,
-1\leq\frac{\xi_{i}}{\xi_{0}}\leq 1
. Besides,
(x_{0})^{2}+R^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(x_{i})^{2}
. Therefore, if
\forall\,i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
,

,

\displaystyle(x_{0})^{2}+R^{2}=\left(\frac{x_{0}}{\xi_{0}}\right)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\xi_{i})^{2}=(x_{0})^{2}\therefore R=0,

which is an absurd. Therefore
\grad\Phi_{x}(\xi)\neq 0
, hence the stationary phase method tells that, for

sufficiently large and

\displaystyle f(x)\leqslant c(1+s)^{-m}.

6.2
Flat limit

At last we want to compare the behaviour of the wavepacket (
6.1
) in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
with the usual construction performed on the Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{M}_{n}
. Besides showing consistency of our construction, this comparison will allow us to find an interpretation for
\xi
.

The plane wave (
4.11
) exhibits two terms, one that is different from zero only at the points satisfying
x\cdot\xi>0
, and another term, which is nonzero only for
x\cdot\xi<0
. This second term contains a multiplicative factor that converges to zero exponentially fast in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore in the following we will analyse the behaviour of the wavepacket in the region where
x\cdot\xi>0
.

The function
\hat{f}
is compactly supported on
\mathscr{A}
. We consider its support to be contained in a small neighbourhood of a covector
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathscr{A}
. If we choose a coordinate system in which
\xi^{\prime}
has components
(1,0,\ldots,0,1)
, then

\displaystyle x\cdot\xi^{\prime}>0\ \Rightarrow\ x_{n}>x_{0},

which corresponds to a half of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
which will be designated as
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
. If, however, we pick a different element
\xi^{\prime\prime}
in the support of
\hat{f}
and choose a new coordinate system such that, now,
\xi^{\prime\prime}
has those same components, then

\displaystyle x\cdot\xi^{\prime\prime}>0\ \Rightarrow\ x^{\prime}_{n}>x_{0}.

Since the elements of
\mathscr{A}
can be obtained one from another by a rotation, the half of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
characterized by
x^{\prime}_{n}>x_{0}
is just a rotation of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
. Therefore the region where
x\cdot\xi>0
for every
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}
, the intersection of all the regions just described, is contained in
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
. On the other hand, the region where
x\cdot\xi>0
for some
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}
comprises a neighbourhood containing
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
. But since
\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}
is contained in a small neighbourhood of
\xi^{\prime}
, the regions where
x\cdot\xi>0
for some
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}
and
x_{n}<x_{0}
are contained in small neighbourhoods of
x_{n}=x_{0}
.

We are first going to analyse the asymptotic behaviour of the plane wave in
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
using the chart described in (
2.3
), with
\varepsilon=+1
.

The contraction of the Lorentz algebra presented in Section
5
showed that the generators of the horospheric translations contract into the generators of spatial translations and
a
contracts into the generator of time translations. Moreover, (
2.3
) gives the change of coordinates from
(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})
to
(\tau,{\bf y})
. Actually, this is a restriction to a submanifold, from
\mathbb{M}_{n+1}
to
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
. This fact will play an important role in the following. Analysing (
2.3
) we find

\displaystyle\frac{x_{n}-x_{0}}{R}=\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/R},\qquad\mathrm{therefore}\qquad\begin{cases}\tau=-R\log\left(\dfrac{x_{n}-x_{0}}{R}\right)\quad\textrm{and}\vskip 2.84526pt\\
y_{i}=-\dfrac{Rx_{i}}{x_{n}-x_{0}}.\end{cases}

These equations are necessary in order to write the partial derivatives in the new coordinate system. The generators
a
and
n_{i}
are represented as differential operators as (see (
3.1
) and (
3.2
))

\displaystyle{\bf a}=\mathrm{i}R\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}+\sum_{i}\frac{y_{i}}{R}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}\right)\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad{\bf n}_{i}=\mathrm{i}R\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{i}}.

We remark here that the horospheric translation is represented simply as a partial differentiation. Moreover, all the other terms in the differential operator (
5.2
) have higher powers of
1/R
.

Now, we are going to act with the operators
{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq n-1
) and
{\bf a^{\prime}}^{2}
(defined in (
5.1
)) on the plane wave
\psi_{\mu}
and analyse the behaviour of the result in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
.

\displaystyle{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu R}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\bf n'}_i{\bf n'}_i \left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma} =}{}\times\left\{\frac{\sigma-1}{\mu R}\left[\frac{y_{i}}{R}(\xi_{0}-\xi_{n})-\xi_{i}\right]^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau/R}+\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)\frac{\xi_{0}-\xi_{n}}{R}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/R}\right\},

and

\displaystyle{\bf a^{\prime}}^{2}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=-\frac{\sigma}{\mu R}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}

\displaystyle\hphantom{{\bf a'}^2\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma} =}{}\times\left\{\frac{\sigma-1}{\mu R}\left[-\cosh\left(\frac{\tau}{R}\right)\xi_{0}+\sinh\left(\frac{\tau}{R}\right)\xi_{n}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|y|^{2}}{R^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/R}(\xi_{0}-\xi_{n})\right]^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.\hphantom{{\bf a^{\prime}}^{2}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=}{}+\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)\!\left[-\sinh\left(\frac{\tau}{R}\right)\frac{\xi_{0}}{R}+\cosh\left(\frac{\tau}{R}\right)\frac{\xi_{n}}{R}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|y|^{2}}{R^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\tau/R}\frac{(\xi_{0}-\xi_{n})}{R}\right]\!\right\}.

Yet,

\displaystyle\frac{\sigma}{\mu R}=-\frac{n-1}{2\mu R}+\mathrm{i}\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{n-1}{2\mu R}\right)^{2}}=\mathrm{i}+\mathcal{O}(1/R)

and the same is valid for
\frac{\sigma-1}{\mu R}
. Hence

\displaystyle{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}{\bf n^{\prime}}_{i}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}\left[(\xi_{i})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/R)\right]\text{and}

(6.4)

\displaystyle{\bf a^{\prime}}^{2}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}\left[(\xi_{0})^{2}+\mathcal{O}(1/R)\right].

(6.5)

Besides,
x\cdot\xi=-\tau\xi_{0}+\sum_{i}y_{i}(-\xi_{i})+R\xi_{n}+\mathcal{O}(1/R)
, hence

\displaystyle\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}=\frac{-\tau\xi_{0}+\sum_{i}y_{i}(-\xi_{i})}{\mu R}+\frac{\xi_{n}}{\mu}+\mathcal{O}\big(1/R^{2}\big).

Since the coordinates of a point of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
are parametrized by the pair
(\tau,{\bf y})
, we define
y=(\tau,{\bf y})
,
\overline{\xi}=(\xi_{0},-\xi_{i})
and
y\cdot\overline{\xi}\coloneqq-\tau\xi_{0}+\sum_{i}y_{i}(-\xi_{i})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}=\left(\frac{y\cdot\overline{\xi}}{\mu R}+\frac{\xi_{n}}{\mu}\right)^{\mu R(\mathrm{i}+\mathcal{O}(1/R))}=\left(\frac{y\cdot\overline{\xi}}{\xi_{n}R}+1\right)^{\xi_{n}R\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{\mu}{\xi_{n}}+\mathcal{O}(1/R)\right)}\left(\frac{\xi_{n}}{\mu}\right)^{\mu R(\mathrm{i}+\mathcal{O}(1/R))}.

If
\xi_{n}\neq\mu
, by the principle of stationary phase, for large values of
R
, the term

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\xi_{n}}{\mu}\right)^{\mu R(\mathrm{i}+\mathcal{O}(1/R))}

is a fast decreasing function of
R
. Therefore

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}=0.

However, if
\xi_{n}=\mu
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma-2}=\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left(\frac{y\cdot\overline{\xi}}{\mu R}+1\right)^{\mu R(\mathrm{i}+\mathcal{O}(1/R))}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\overline{\xi}}

(6.6)

and

\displaystyle\overline{\xi}\cdot\overline{\xi}=-\mu^{2}.

(6.7)

Note that, if the exponent was modified from
\sigma-2
to
\sigma
, the above results would remain unchanged.

Therefore, collecting the results (
6.6
), (
6.5
) and (
6.4
) and considering the action of the operator (
5.3
) on the plane wave, one finds

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\left[\sum_{i}({\bf n^{\prime}}_{i})^{2}-({\bf a^{\prime}})^{2}\right]\left(\frac{x\cdot\xi}{\mu R}\right)^{\sigma}=\big[(\xi_{i})^{2}-(\xi_{0})^{2}\big]\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\overline{\xi}}=-\mu^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\overline{\xi}}.

y
represents the coordinates of a point of the resulting Minkowski spacetime
\mathbb{M}_{n}
and
\overline{\xi}
is a timelike vector on the mass shell of
\mathbb{M}_{n}
(
6.7
). Their origin, however, are restrictions of the coordinates
x
of a point on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
and the null covector
\xi
on the absolute, respectively. All other terms of
\Box_{dS}
have higher powers of
1/R
, and therefore their contribution would converge to zero.

We still have to analyse the asymptotic behaviour of
\psi_{\mu}
in the points where
x\cdot\xi>0
for some
\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\hat{f}
, but
x_{n}<x_{0}
. This region can be covered by the chart (
2.3
), with
\varepsilon=-1
. Since those points are contained in a small neighbourhood of
x_{n}=x_{0}
, the previous analysis shows that, after the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
, these points will be at infinity. Thus they are irrelevant, because of the decay of the wavepacket. Therefore we have proved that the plane wave on the whole
\mathbb{M}_{n}
is the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
of the plane wave on half of
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
. Collecting these results in a sentence, on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}_{n}^{+}
,

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\psi_{\mu}(x,\xi)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}y\cdot\overline{\xi}},&\textrm{if $\xi_{n}=\mu$},\\
0,&\textrm{if $\xi_{n}\neq\mu$}.\end{cases}

This function is supported on a set of measure zero in the variable
\xi_{n}
. Hence when we multiply this limit with
\hat{f}
and integrate on the absolute with the measure (
4.10
), the integration over

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2|\overline{\xi}_{0}|}\mathrm{d}\overline{\xi}_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\mathrm{d}\overline{\xi}_{n-1}

is the only one that gives a nonzero result.

Before collecting the results of this section, let us note that the plane wave and wavepacket on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
are of fast decay, but this is not the behaviour of a wavepacket on Minkowski spacetime
[
34
,
35
]
. We can see the change in the asymptotic behaviour as
R
becomes larger. In (
6.6
) one notes that the leading term in the exponent of the plane wave (above (
6.3
)), for large

, is

\displaystyle\frac{1}{s}\mu R\log\left[1+\frac{y\cdot\overline{\xi}}{\mu R}\right]\eqqcolon\Upgamma_{y}(\overline{\xi})

(the term
i
is not important now). Hence if we subtract and add this term to the phase,

\displaystyle\left[\mu R\Phi_{x}(\xi)-\Upgamma_{y}(\overline{\xi})\right]+\Upgamma_{y}(\overline{\xi}),

the term between brackets goes to zero in the limit
R\rightarrow\infty
, but
\grad\Upgamma_{y}(\overline{\xi})
has fixed points. Hence as the de Sitter radius
R
gets larger, the wavepacket decreases more slowly, until it reaches the rate
s^{-3/2}
given by the stationary phase principle
[
51
]
and calculated in
[
34
,
35
]
.

All the results of this section are collected in the following

Theorem 6.3
.

The limit, as
R\rightarrow\infty
, of the wavepacket on the
n
-dimensional de Sitter spacetime is a wavepacket on Minkowski spacetime, analytic in the whole
\mathbb{M}_{n}
, with mass sharply constrained to the mass shell
(
now in the sense of (
6.7
)
)
and determined by the mass of its precedent wavepacket on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
.

7
Conclusions

We have proved that one can consistently construct wavepackets on the de Sitter spacetime whose mass is defined from one of the Casimir elements in the universal enveloping algebra of the Lorentz algebra. The wavepacket is a function of fast decrease, differently from the wavepacket defined on Minkowski spacetime in
[
34
,
35
]
. As we emphasized before, this is a general feature of harmonic analysis on semi-simple Lie groups. The physical interpretation of the wavepacket became clearer after the evaluation of its flat limit, with the wavepackets converging to the usual one defined on Minkowski spacetime with support sharply constrained on the mass shell.

Besides being the first time that wavepackets are constructed on de Sitter spacetime, this is the first time that such a construction is made on any curved spacetime. The extension of this result to other curved spacetimes would require a way to decompose a function into a linear combination (possibly direct integral) of solutions of the wave equation with a specific value of the mass. This construction is possible on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
thanks to the Fourier transform, described in Section
4
.

The first intended application of this result is to formulate a scattering theory on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
, whether à la Haag–Ruelle or Araki–Haag, i.e., either constructing scattering states or calculating collision cross sections
[
4
,
18
,
26
,
49
]
. However, both results on Minkowski spacetime are based on the spectral condition, the fact that the joint energy-momentum spectrum on the one-particle Hilbert space has an isolated point in its spectrum. A similar result does not exist on
\mathrm{d}\mathbb{S}
. Actually, the plane waves written in the hyperbolic coordinate system (equations (
4.12
) and (
4.13
)) are eigenfunctions of the total angular momentum operator and of one of its components (characterized by the quantum number
m
), besides the mass operator. Moreover a one-particle Hilbert space is generated by the Fourier coefficients (
4.14
) for which
\rho\equiv\mu^{\prime}
. However there is no element of the Lorentz algebra of which the plane wave is an eigenfunction and whose eigenvalue is related to the mass. The “time evolution operator”
\partial/\partial\beta
does not represent an element of the Lorentz algebra. Therefore the wavepackets defined in this work are an important result in the pursuit of a scattering theory on a curved spacetime.

. This parameter arises from an intertwiner between homogeneous functions on
\mathfrak{C}
given by the Fourier transform. The space of homogeneous functions of degree
\sigma=-\frac{(n-1)}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho
on
\mathfrak{C}
is generated by functions of the form
h(\zeta,\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})=\zeta^{\sigma}\varpi(\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})
,
\varpi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\big({\pm}1\times S^{n-1}\big)
. The space
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\big({\pm}1\times S^{n-1}\big)
is spanned by spherical harmonics and we will benefit from the fact that the Fourier transform of a spherical harmonic is a spherical harmonic
[
52
]
. Hence we write

\displaystyle\varpi(\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})=(\sign\tau^{\prime})^{k}Y_{j}({\bf\chi^{\prime}}),

k
an
j
being integer numbers, the degree of the spherical harmonics.
\varpi
is even (odd) if
k+j
is even (odd). Therefore

\displaystyle Th(s,t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}})=\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\iint h(\zeta,\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\tau^{\prime}t^{\prime}-{\bf\chi^{\prime}}\cdot{\bf x^{\prime}})s\zeta}\zeta^{n-2}\mathrm{d}\zeta\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime}

(A.1)

\displaystyle\hphantom{Th(s,t',{\bf x'})}{}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\zeta^{\frac{n-3}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho}\left[\int_{S^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}({\bf\chi^{\prime}}\cdot{\bf x^{\prime}})s\zeta}Y_{j}({\bf\chi^{\prime}})\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime}\right]\left[\sum_{\tau^{\prime}=\pm 1}\left(\sign\tau^{\prime}\right)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}t^{\prime}s\zeta}\right]\mathrm{d}\zeta.

Classical Fourier analysis gives

\displaystyle\int_{S^{n-1}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}({\bf\chi^{\prime}}\cdot{\bf x^{\prime}})s\zeta}Y_{j}({\bf\chi^{\prime}})\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{j\pi}{2}}(s\zeta)^{-\frac{(n-2)}{2}}J_{\frac{n+2j-2}{2}}(s\zeta)Y_{j}({\bf x^{\prime}})

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau^{\prime}=\pm 1}\left(\sign\tau^{\prime}\right)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\tau^{\prime}t^{\prime}s\zeta}=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}s\zeta}+(-1)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}t^{\prime}s\zeta}=(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}s\zeta}+\left(-\sign t^{\prime}\right)^{k}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}s\zeta}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\sqrt{\frac{s\zeta\pi}{2}}\big\{\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}+(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)+\mathrm{i}\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}-(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)\big\}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=\begin{cases}2\sqrt{\dfrac{s\zeta\pi}{2}}(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)&\textrm{if $k$ is even},\\
2\mathrm{i}\sqrt{\dfrac{s\zeta\pi}{2}}(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}J_{\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)&\textrm{if $k$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Inserting these results in (
A.1
) one finds

\displaystyle Th(s,t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{2}(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{j\pi}{2}}s^{-\frac{n-3}{2}}Y_{j}({\bf x^{\prime}})\int_{0}^{\infty}\zeta^{\mathrm{i}\rho}J_{\frac{n+2j-2}{2}}(s\zeta)

\displaystyle\hphantom{Th(s,t',{\bf x'}) =}{}\times\big\{\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}+(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)+\mathrm{i}\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}-(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{\frac{1}{2}}(s\zeta)\big\}\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle\hphantom{Th(s,t',{\bf x'}) }{}=\frac{(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{j\pi}{2}}s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}Y_{j}({\bf x^{\prime}})\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\mathrm{i}\rho}J_{\frac{n+2j-2}{2}}(y)

\displaystyle\hphantom{Th(s,t',{\bf x'}) =}{}\times\big\{\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}+(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{-\frac{1}{2}}(y)+\mathrm{i}\big[(\sign t^{\prime})^{k}-(-\sign t^{\prime})^{k}\big]J_{\frac{1}{2}}(y)\big\}\mathrm{d}y.

This integral may be evaluated by analytic continuation. If the integrand were
y^{-\lambda}J_{\eta}(y)J_{\nu}(y)
and
\operatorname{Re}(\eta+\nu)+1>\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)>0
, the integral would converge and the result would be an analytic function of
\lambda
in the interval
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)\in(0,\operatorname{Re}(\eta+\nu)+1)
(see entry 24 in Table 19.2 of
[
19
]
). Thus by analytic continuation the result extends to
\operatorname{Re}(\lambda)=0
:

\displaystyle Th(s,t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}})=(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}2^{\mathrm{i}\rho}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\frac{j\pi}{2}}\times s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\varpi(t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}})

\displaystyle\hphantom{Th(s,t',{\bf x'}) =}{}\times\frac{\Upgamma(-\mathrm{i}\rho)\Upgamma\left(\frac{n+2j\pm 1}{4}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)}{\Upgamma\left(\frac{-(n+2j)\pm 1}{4}+1-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)\Upgamma\left(\frac{n+2j\pm 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)\Upgamma\left(\frac{n+2j\mp 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)}p(k).

(A.2)

In the arguments of the Gamma functions, the upper sign must be chosen if
k
is odd, and the lower sign if
k
is even. Besides,

\displaystyle p(k)=\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}&\textrm{if $k$ is odd},\\
1&\textrm{if $k$ is even}.\end{cases}

We remark that the term
\frac{\Upgamma\left(\frac{n+2j\pm 1}{4}+\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)}{\Upgamma\left(\frac{n+2j\pm 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)}
is just a phase factor and denoting
z\coloneqq\frac{n+2j\mp 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}
, one finds that
\frac{-(n+2j)\pm 1}{4}+1-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}=1-\overline{z}
. Therefore the other two Gamma functions in the denominator in (
A.2
) are

\displaystyle\Upgamma(1-\overline{z})\Upgamma(z)=\overline{\Upgamma(1-z)}\Upgamma(z)=\Upgamma(1-z)\Upgamma(z)\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathrm{i}\Arg\left[\Upgamma(1-z)\right]}=\frac{\pi}{\sin\pi z}\mathrm{e}^{-2\mathrm{i}\Arg\left[\Upgamma(1-z)\right]}.

Collecting all phase factors in a term denoted as
p^{\prime}(n,j,k,\rho)
, and comparing the result of (
A.2
) with (
4.5
), one obtains

\displaystyle\Upgamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)s^{-\frac{(n-1)}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\mathrm{i}\pi}{2}\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad{}\times\iint|a|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\left[\Theta(a)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)}\Theta(-a)\right]\varpi(\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=(2\pi)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{n+2j\mp 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)\right]}{\pi}\Upgamma(-\mathrm{i}\rho)p^{\prime}(n,j,k,\rho)s^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\varpi(t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}}).

Hence

\displaystyle\varpi(t^{\prime},{\bf x^{\prime}})=(2\pi)^{-\frac{(n+1)}{2}}\frac{\Upgamma\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)}{\Upgamma(-\mathrm{i}\rho)}\frac{\pi}{\sin\left[\pi\left(\frac{n+2j\mp 1}{4}-\frac{\mathrm{i}\rho}{2}\right)\right]}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\pi\rho}{2}}p^{\prime\prime}(n,j,k,\rho)

\displaystyle\hphantom{\varpi(t',{\bf x'}) =}{}\times\iint|a|^{-\frac{n-1}{2}-\mathrm{i}\rho}\left[\Theta(a)+\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\pi\left(\frac{n-1}{2}+\mathrm{i}\rho\right)}\Theta(-a)\right]\varpi(\tau^{\prime},{\bf\chi^{\prime}})\mathrm{d}\tau^{\prime}\mathrm{d}^{n-1}\chi^{\prime},

(A.3)

where
p^{\prime\prime}(n,j,k,\rho)
is just another phase factor.

Since the Fourier transform of a spherical harmonic is a spherical harmonic, comparing equations (
4.6
) and (
A.3
) we conclude that the multiplicative parameter in the right-hand side of the latter is
d(\rho)
, whose absolute value is given in (
4.9

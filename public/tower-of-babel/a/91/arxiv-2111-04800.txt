Anomalous changing of geodesics in hairy black holes
Weyner Ccuiro, David Choque, Gustavo Valdivia-Mera

Abstract
We study the motion of test particles and the propagation of light around neutral hairy black holes under the influence of a self-interacting real scalar field minimally coupled to gravity. The goal of the present work is to show that the time-like and null-like geodesics have an anomalous behaviour for a special range of parameters in the dense hair region, defined as Ω ⁡ ( x h ) ≤ r ≤ 2 ​ M ​ G N / c 2 \sqrt{\Omega(x_{h})}\leq r\leq 2MG_{N}/c^{2} .

Hairy Black hole solution

We consider the following modified Einstein-Hilbert theory with an scalar field with non-minimal coupling

I[\textbf{g},\phi]=\frac{1}{2\kappa}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}{R}+\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}-V(\phi)\right]

(1)

c

g

The equations of motion arising from the action principle are

G_{\mu\nu}=\kappa\,T_{\mu\nu},\quad\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\,\partial_{\mu}\left(\sqrt{-g}g^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\phi\right)=\frac{dV}{d\phi}

\begin{split}G_{\mu\nu}&:=R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R\\

(B2)

The Lorentzian signature of the metric imposes the condition
r\geq r_{h}
.
Choosing a new radial coordinate
\rho^{2}=4r_{h}(r-r_{h})
we get the Rindler geometry

B.1 Time-like geodesics

From (
B2
) the equation for time-like geodesics near to black hole is

-c^{2}=-N(r)c^{2}\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{N(r)}+r_{h}^{2}\dot{\varphi}^{2},\qquad N(r)=\frac{(r-r_{h})}{r_{h}}

(B3)

\displaystyle g_{tt}\xi_{(t)}^{t}u^{t}=-\frac{\mathcal{E}}{m}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\frac{dt}{d\tau}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}}{mc^{2}}\right)\frac{1}{N(r)}=\frac{r_{h}\bar{\mathcal{E}}}{r-r_{h}}

(B4)

\displaystyle g_{\varphi\varphi}\xi_{(\varphi)}^{\varphi}u^{\varphi}=\frac{\mathcal{J}}{m}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}=\left(\frac{\mathcal{J}}{mc^{2}}\right)\frac{c^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}=\frac{\bar{\mathcal{J}}c^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}

(B5)

It describes the radial motion of a test body with energy
E=\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{2}-1
in the effective potential

E=\frac{\dot{r}^{2}}{c^{2}}+U_{\text{eff}}(r),\qquad U_{\text{eff}}(r)=\frac{(r-r_{h})}{r_{h}}\left(1+\frac{\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}c^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}\right)-1

(B6)

The polar equation can be constructed
considering the chain-rule
\frac{dr}{d\tau}=\frac{dr}{d\varphi}\frac{d\varphi}{d\tau}
, from that we have

\quantity(\frac{dr}{d\varphi})^{2}=\frac{r_{h}^{4}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}c^{2}}(E-U_{\text{eff}})

(B7)

The typical second order orbital equation
can be constructed taking the the derivative (
B7
) with respect to
\varphi
. The orbital equation and the solution are

\begin{split}\derivative[2]{r}{\varphi}&=-\frac{(\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}c^{2}+r_{h}^{2})r_{h}}{2c^{2}\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}}\\
~\Rightarrow~r(\varphi)&=-\frac{r_{h}}{4}\quantity(1+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}c^{2}})\varphi^{2}+c_{1}\varphi+c_{2}\end{split}

(B8)

The initial condition at the horizon for
(dr/d\varphi)_{r_{h}}
can be obtained from (
B6
), and at horizon we fix
r(\varphi)
as

\quantity(\dv{r}{\varphi})_{r_{h}}=\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}c}\sqrt{E+1}=c_{1},\qquad r(\varphi=0)=r_{h}=c_{2}

(B9)

then, the solution (
B8
) under the above initial conditions is given by

r(\varphi)=-\frac{r_{h}}{4}\quantity(1+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}c^{2}})\varphi^{2}+\quantity(\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}c}\sqrt{E+1})\varphi+r_{h}

(B10)

B.2 Null geodesic

We consider an affine parameter
\xi
and scale it as

\xi\rightarrow\xi/\bar{\mathcal{J}}

\frac{dt}{d\xi}=\frac{r_{h}\bar{\mathcal{E}}}{\bar{\mathcal{J}}(r-r_{h})},\qquad\frac{d\varphi}{d\xi}=\frac{c^{2}}{r_{h}^{2}}

(B11)

in (
B2
) at
ds^{2}=0
we get

\begin{split}\left(\frac{dr}{d\xi}\right)^{2}+&\mathcal{V}_{eff}(r)=\frac{c^{2}}{b^{2}},\\
\mathcal{V}_{eff}(r)&=\frac{(r-r_{h})c^{4}}{r_{h}^{3}},\qquad b^{2}=\frac{\bar{\mathcal{J}}^{2}}{\bar{\mathcal{E}}^{2}}\end{split}

(B12)

considering the chain-rule
\frac{dr}{d\xi}\frac{d\xi}{d\varphi}=\frac{dr}{d\varphi}
we obtain the polar equation

\left(\frac{dr}{d\varphi}\right)^{2}=\frac{r_{h}^{4}}{b^{2}c^{2}}-r_{h}(r-r_{h})

(B13)

Taking the derivative
of the orbital equation and integrating we have

\derivative[2]{r}{\varphi}=-\frac{r_{h}}{2}~\Rightarrow~r(\varphi)=-\frac{r_{h}\varphi^{2}}{4}+c_{1}\varphi+c_{2}

(B14)

The initial conditions
are: from (
B13
) we get
(dr/d\varphi)_{r_{h}}
, and fixing that
r(\varphi=0)=r_{h}

\quantity(\dv{r}{\varphi})_{r_{h}}=\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{bc}=c_{1},\qquad r(\varphi=0)=r_{h}=c_{2}

(B15)

we have

r(\varphi)=-\frac{r_{h}}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{r_{h}^{2}}{bc}\varphi+r_{h}

(B16)

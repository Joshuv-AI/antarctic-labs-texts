The Steinitz Realization Problem
Sameera Vemulapalli

Abstract
. Let K K be a number field and let n ∈ ℤ > 1 n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1} . The Steinitz realization problem asks: does every element of Cl ⁡ ( K ) \Cl(K) occur as the Steinitz class of a degree n n extension of K K ? In this article, we give an affirmative answer to the Steinitz realization problem for all n n and K K .

1.
Introduction

Fix
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>1}
a positive integer. To any degree
n
extension of number fields
L/K
, we may canonically associate an element of the ideal class group of
K
, namely
[\mathfrak{a}]
where
\mathcal{O}_{L}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n-1}\oplus\mathfrak{a}
as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. The class
[\mathfrak{a}]
is called the
Steinitz class
of
L/K
and is denoted
\St(L/K)
. By a theorem of Hecke, the square of the Steinitz class
\St(L/K)
is the class of the relative discriminant
\Disc(L/K)
. The
Steinitz realization problem
asks: does every element of
\Cl(K)
occur as the Steinitz class of a degree
n
extension of
K
?

When
n=2,3,4,5
, there is an affirmative answer to the Steinitz realization problem; in these cases the Steinitz classes of
S_{n}
-extensions are
equidistributed
in
\Cl(K)
when degree
n
number fields are ordered by discriminant. The case
n=2,3
was proven by Kable and Wright
[
2
]
and the case
n=4,5
was proven by Bhargava, Shankar, and Wang
[
1
]
. The proofs rely on the parametrizations of degree
n
number fields; for
n\geq 6
, there are no such parametrizations. The purpose of this article is to give an affirmative answer to the Steinitz realization problem for all
n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Every element of
\Cl(K)
occurs as the Steinitz class of a degree
n
extension of
K
with squarefree discriminant.

We give a sketch of the proof. A
rank
n
ring
over
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is a ring that is locally free of rank
n
as an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module. By work of Wood
[
4
]
, a binary
n
-ic form

f(x,y)=f_{0}x^{n}+\dots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\Sym^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{K})

gives rise to a rank
n
ring
R_{f}
. If
\mathfrak{a}
is an integral ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
f_{n}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
, we construct a rank
n
ring
R_{f}(\mathbf{a})
and an inclusion
R_{f}\xhookrightarrow{}R_{f}(\mathbf{a})
. When
f
is irreducible, both rings are orders in a number field. The Steinitz class of
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
is
[\mathfrak{a}^{-1}]
and
\Disc(R_{f}(\mathbf{a}))=\mathfrak{a}^{-2}\Disc(R_{f})
.

If
\Disc(R_{f}(\mathfrak{a}))
is squarefree as an ideal and
f(x,y)
is irreducible, then
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
is the maximal order in a degree
n
extension of
K
with Steinitz class
[\mathfrak{a}^{-1}]
. Thus,
Theorem

1.1
immediately follows from the existence of an appropriate
f(x,y)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For every nonzero proper prime ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
coprime to
(n!)
and subject to the condition that
\#(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a})>n^{n-2}
, there are infinitely many monic degree
n
polynomials

f(x)\coloneqq x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}\dots+f_{n}

with coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that:

(1)

f
is irreducible over
K
;

(2)

f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
f_{n}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
;

(3)

and
\Disc(R_{f}(\mathfrak{a}))
is squarefree as an ideal in
K
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is a slight generalization of the proof given by Kedlaya when proving that there exist infinitely many irreducible monic polynomials over
\mathbb{Z}
with squarefree discriminant
[
3
]
.

2.
Construction of
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})

By work of Wood
[
4
]
, a binary
n
-ic form
f_{0}x^{n}+\dots+f_{n}y^{n}\in\Sym^{n}(\mathcal{O}_{K}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{K})
gives rise to a rank
n
ring
R_{f}
as follows. As an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module, put
R_{f}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}_{K}^{n}
and label the copies of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
so we write:

R_{f}=(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{n-1}.

Put
u_{1},\dots,u_{n-1}\coloneqq 1
and
u_{n}\coloneqq-f_{n}
. Define
1\in(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{0}
to be the multiplicative identity in
R_{f}
. For
1\leq i\leq j\leq n-1
and
1\leq k\leq n
, define the multiplication maps
(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{j}\rightarrow(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{k}
by:

(1)

x\otimes y\rightarrow-f_{i+j-k}u_{k}xy
if
\max(i+j-n,1)\leq k\leq i
;

(2)

x\otimes y\rightarrow f_{i+j-k}u_{k}xy
if
j<k\leq\min(i+j,n)
;

(3)

and
x\otimes y\rightarrow 0
otherwise.

In the notation above,
(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{n}
refers to
(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{0}
.

For any nonzero integral ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
K
, define
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
to be the locally free
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-module given by:

R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})\coloneqq(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{0}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{-1}(\mathcal{O}_{K})_{n-1}.

There is a natural inclusion of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-modules given by
R_{f}\subseteq R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
has an
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra structure extending that of
R_{f}
if and only if
f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
f_{n}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
. When
f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
f_{n}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
, there is a unique
\mathcal{O}_{K}
-algebra structure on
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
extending that of
R_{f}
, the Steinitz class of
R_{f}(\mathfrak{a})
is
[\mathfrak{a}^{-1}]
and
\Disc(R_{f}(\mathfrak{a}))=\mathfrak{a}^{-2}\Disc(R_{f})
.

Proof.

The first statement follows directly from the multiplication table. The second is immediate from the construction.
∎

3.
Proof of
Theorem

1.2

The proof given in this section is essentially that given by Kedlaya
[
3
]
. The main differences are that we work over a number field and impose a few additional congruence conditions needed for our application. We repeat much of Kedlaya’s construction verbatim, and encourage the reader to refer to Kedlaya’s paper for more detail. Fix a class
[\mathfrak{a}]\in\Cl(K)
, and pick a representative prime ideal
\mathfrak{a}
coprime to
(n!)
. For
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1},b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, put:

Q_{\vec{a}}(x)\coloneqq n(x-a_{1}/n)(x-a_{2})\dots(x-a_{n-1})

P_{\vec{a},b}(x)\coloneqq b+\int_{0}^{x}Q_{\vec{a}}(t)\,dt

The integral in the definition of
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)
should be interpreted formally;
Q_{\vec{a}}(x)
is a polynomial. The principal ideal generated by the discriminant of
P_{\vec{a},b}
is denoted
\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
and is equal to the resultant of
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)
and
Q_{\vec{a}}(x)
. So we obtain the following equality of ideals:

\Delta_{\vec{a},b}=(n^{n}P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n)+n^{n}b)\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}(P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{i})+b).

Suppose
a_{1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
b\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
. If we write
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)=x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}+\dots+f_{n}
and assume that
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)
has coefficients in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then
f_{n}=b\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
and

f_{n-1}=(-1)^{n-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}a_{i}\in\mathfrak{a}.

Furthermore,
\mathfrak{a}^{2}
divides
(n^{n}P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n)+n^{n}b)
and hence divides
\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
. Therefore, we wish to show that there exists a choice of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1},b
with
a_{1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
b\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
such that
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)
has integral coefficients, is irreducible, and the ideal
\mathfrak{a}^{-2}\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
is squarefree. We will do this by fixing an appropriate choice of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}
and then applying a squarefree sieve to choose
b
.

We will first show that there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that:

(1)

a_{1}\in\mathfrak{a}
and
a_{i}\equiv-1\pmod{\mathfrak{a}}
for
i=2,\dots,n-1
;

(2)

P_{\vec{a},0}
has integral coefficients;

(3)

for every prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{a}
, there exists
b
such that
\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
is not divisible by
\mathfrak{p}^{2}
;

(4)

there exists
b_{2}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
such that
\Delta_{\vec{a},b_{2}}
is not divisible by
\mathfrak{a}^{3}
;

(5)

there exists a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
and
b_{1}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that
P_{\vec{a},b_{1}}
is irreducible modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
;

(6)

and
P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n),P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{2}),\dots,P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{n-1})
are all distinct.

To do this, we’ll need the following two lemmas. The following lemma is essentially Lemma 2.3 of
[
3
]
but has been suitably modified for our application.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
be such that
(\gamma)
is a principal prime ideal coprime to
(n(n-1))
. Then there exist infinitely many primes
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
modulo which the polynomial
R(x)=x^{n}-\gamma^{n-1}x+\gamma
is irreducible and its derivative
R^{\prime}(x)=nx^{n-1}-\gamma^{n-1}
splits into linear factors.

Proof.

The polynomial
R^{\prime}(x)
has splitting field
L=K(\zeta_{n-1},n^{1/(n-1)})
, in which
(\gamma)
does not ramify because
(\gamma)
does not divide
(n(n-1))
, where here
\zeta_{n-1}
is an
(n-1)
th root of unity. Thus
R
is an Eisenstein polynomial with respect to any prime above
(\gamma)
in
L
; in particular,
R
is irreducible over
L
.

We first show that the Galois group of
R(x)
over
L
contains an
n
-cycle. The Chebotarev density theorem shows that the factorization types of the polynomial
R(x)
modulo primes of
L
are equidistributed over the Galois group of
R(x)
over
L
. To show that
R(x)
is irreducible modulo infinitely many primes of
L
, it is necessary to show that the Galois group of
R(x)
over
L
contains a full cycle. Let
\nu
be a prime of
L
above
\gamma
. Let
L_{\nu}
be the completion of
L
at
\nu
, and let
M_{\nu}/L_{\nu}
be the extension given by
R(x)
. Since
R
is Eisenstein,
L_{\nu}
is totally ramified, and since
\nu\nmid n
, we have
L_{\nu}=K_{\nu}\left[\sqrt[n]{\pi}\right]
for some uniformizer
\pi
of
L_{\nu}
. The
n
-cycle in question is given by
\zeta_{n}^{i}\sqrt[n]{\pi}\rightarrow\zeta_{n}^{i+1}\sqrt[n]{\pi}
.

Now, the Chebotarev density theorem shows that there are infinitely many prime ideals of
L
of degree
1
over
K
modulo which
R(x)
is irreducible. The norm to
K
of any such prime ideal is a prime ideal of the desired form.
∎

Lemma 3.2

(Kedlaya, Lemma 2.4
[
3
]
)
.

For any field
F
of characteristic zero, there exist
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}\in F
such that

P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n),P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{2}),\dots,P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{n-1})

are distinct.

With these two lemmas in hand, we now proceed with our proof of Theorem
1.2
. Let
\gamma
,
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
, and
R(x)
be as in
Lemma

3.1
and ensure that
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\nmid n!\mathfrak{a}
. Apply
Lemma

3.2
to obtain
a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n-1}
so that

P_{\vec{a}^{\prime},0}(a^{\prime}_{1}/n),P_{\vec{a}^{\prime},0}(a^{\prime}_{2}),\dots,P_{\vec{a}^{\prime},0}(a^{\prime}_{n-1})

have distinct values. Choose a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}_{2}\nmid n!\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{p}_{1}
such the reductions have well-defined and distinct values modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
. Now use the Chinese remainder theorem to choose
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
subject to the following congruence conditions:

•

a_{1}
is coprime to
(n)
and is contained in
\mathfrak{a}
and the ideal generated by
n-1
;

•

the integers
a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1}
are contained in
(n!)
and
a_{i}\equiv-1
modulo
\mathfrak{a}
for
i=2,\dots,n-1
;

•

the quantities
a_{1}/n,\dots,a_{n-1}
are congruent to the roots of
R^{\prime}(x)
modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
;

•

and
a_{i}\equiv a^{\prime}_{i}
modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
;

•

for
i=2,\dots,n-1
, consider the polynomial

F(x_{2},\dots,x_{n-2})=\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}P_{(0,x_{2},\dots,x_{n-1}),0}(x_{i})

as an element of the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Q}[x_{2},\dots,x_{n-1}]
. The degree of this polynomial is
n^{n-2}
. The denominators of the coefficients of
F
are prime to
\mathfrak{a}
, so we may reduce
F
modulo
\mathfrak{a}
and consider it as an element of
\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a}[x_{2},\dots,x_{n-1}]
. It is easy to check that the polynomial
F
is nonzero modulo
\mathfrak{a}
. By assumption,
\#(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a})>n^{n-2}
, so by the Schwartz-Zippel lemma, the vanishing locus of
F
inside the affine space
\AA^{n-2}_{\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{a}}
is not the entirety of the affine space. Choose
a_{2},\dots,a_{n-1}
so that their reductions modulo
\mathfrak{a}
lie outside the vanishing locus of
F
.

We’ll now show that this choice of
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}
satisfies conditions
(1)-(6)
.

(1)

Follows directly from the choice of
a_{i}
.

(2)

Observe that:

Q_{\vec{a}}(x)\equiv(nx-a_{1})x^{n-2}=nx^{n-1}-a_{1}x^{n-2}\pmod{(n!)},

so
P_{\vec{a},0}
has integer coefficients.

(3)

Suppose that
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{a}
is a prime ideal dividing
(n!)
. Then for
b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, we have
P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{i})+b\equiv b\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
for
i=2,\dots,n-1
because
a_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}
. Now suppose
\mathfrak{p}\mid(n)
. Then because
Q_{\vec{a}}(x)\equiv nx^{n-1}-a_{1}x^{n-2}\pmod{(n!)}
, we have
P_{\vec{a},0}=x^{n}-\frac{a_{1}}{n-1}x^{n-1}\pmod{(n!)}
. So:

\displaystyle n^{n}P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n)+n^{n}b

\displaystyle\equiv n^{n}\bigg(\frac{a_{1}^{n}}{n^{n}}-\frac{a_{1}}{n-1}\frac{a_{1}^{n-1}}{n^{n-1}}\bigg)\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\equiv a_{1}^{n}-\frac{n}{n-1}a_{1}^{n}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle\equiv a_{1}^{n}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}.

If
\mathfrak{p}\nmid(n)
, then there exists
b\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}
can be chosen such that

n^{n}P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n)+n^{n}b\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}

Consequently, there exists
b
such that
\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
is not divisible by
\mathfrak{p}
. Now suppose that
\mathfrak{p}
is a prime ideal coprime to
\mathfrak{a}(n!)
, so
\lvert\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\rvert>n
. There are at least
\lvert\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}\rvert-n
choices of
b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}
for which
\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
is indivisible by
p
, because each linear factor rules out exactly one choice of
b
.

(4)

Choose
b\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
so that
P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{1}/n)+b\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^{3}}
. To conclude it suffices to show that
P_{\vec{a},0}(a_{i})\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{a}}
for all
i=2,\dots,n-1
; this follows from the fact that
a_{2},\dots,a_{n-2}
were chosen so that their reductions modulo
\mathfrak{a}
lie outside the vanishing locus of
F
.

(5)

Ensured by the fact that the quantities
a_{1}/n,\dots,a_{n-1}
are congruent to the roots of
R^{\prime}(x)
modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{1}
.

(6)

Ensured by the fact that
a_{i}\equiv a^{\prime}_{i}
modulo
\mathfrak{p}_{2}
.

Given such a choice of the
a_{1},\dots,a_{n-1}
above, let
T
be the set of all
b\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
such that
b\equiv b_{1}\pmod{\mathfrak{p}_{1}}
amd
b\equiv b_{2}\pmod{\mathfrak{a}^{3}}
. For any
b\in T
, the polynomial
P_{\vec{a},b}(x)=x^{n}+f_{1}x^{n-1}+\dots+f_{n}
is irreducible with integral coefficients such that
f_{n}\in\mathfrak{a}^{2}
,
f_{n-1}\in\mathfrak{a}
,
\mathfrak{p}_{1}\nmid\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
, and
\mathfrak{a}^{3}\nmid\Delta_{\vec{a},b}
. For
b\in K
, put
\lvert b\rvert=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{\deg(K)}\lvert\sigma_{i}(b)\rvert}
where
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{\deg(K)}
are the embeddings of
K
into
\mathbb{C}
. The proof of
Theorem

1.2
is concluded by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
T
be a set in
\mathcal{O}_{K}
defined by congruence conditions at a finite set of primes
S
. Let
c_{1},\dots,c_{k},d_{1},\dots,d_{k}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
and put
A(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(c_{i}x+d_{i})
. Suppose that
A(x)
is squarefree away from
S
. For
\mathfrak{p}\neq\mathfrak{a}
, let

a(\mathfrak{p})=\frac{\#\{b\in\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{2}\;\colon\;A(b)\not\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}\}}{\#(\mathcal{O}_{K}/\mathfrak{p}^{2})}

Then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{b\in T\colon\lvert b\rvert\leq N\text{ and }A(b)\text{ is squarefree away from }S\}}{\#\{b\in T\colon\lvert b\rvert\leq N\}}=\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}a(\mathfrak{p}).

If
a(\mathfrak{p})>0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, then
\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}a(\mathfrak{p})>0
.

Proof.

If
a(\mathfrak{p})>0
for all
\mathfrak{p}\notin S
, then there exists a
k
such that:

\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}a(\mathfrak{p})\geq\prod_{\mathfrak{p}\notin S}\bigg(1-\frac{k}{N_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})^{2}}\bigg)>0,

which proves the second claim. To prove the first claim it suffices to prove the following tail estimate:

\#\{b\in T\colon\lvert b\rvert\leq N,\exists\;\mathfrak{p}\text{ s.t. }\Nm(\mathfrak{p})>\sqrt{N}\text{ and }A(b)\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}\}=o(N^{\deg(K)}).

The tail estimate is bounded above by the sum of the following expressions:

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{\Nm(\mathfrak{p})>\sqrt{N}}\#\{b\in T\colon\lvert b\rvert\leq N\text{ and }c_{i}b+d_{i}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}^{2}}\}

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq k}^{k}\sum_{\Nm(\mathfrak{p})>\sqrt{N}}\#\{b\in T\colon\lvert b\rvert\leq N\text{ and }c_{i}b+d_{i}\equiv c_{j}b+d_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{\mathfrak{p}}\}

Both sums are bounded above by
O(N^{\deg(K)-1/2})
, completing the proof.
∎

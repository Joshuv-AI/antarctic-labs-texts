Quantale-Enriched Multicategories Via Actions
Eros Martinelli

Abstract
In this communication, motivated by a classical result that relates cocomplete quantale-enriched categories to modules over a quantale, we prove a similar result for quantale-enriched multicategories.



Keywords:
Quantales, Quantale-Enriched Categories, Quantale-Enriched Multicategories, Strong Monads.

1
Introduction

Lawvere, in his seminal paper
[
24
]
, made the important observation that fundamental mathematical structures do not only constitute the objects of a category but are themselves categories. In fact, it has been known for a long time that ordered sets can be seen as categories enriched in the two element boolean algebra; moreover, monotone maps between them are exactly enriched functors. As a leading example, Lawvere explains how metric spaces fit into his thesis by showing how they are instances of enriched categories and how results from enriched category theory are able to capture important metric constructions.

We must point out that, although it is possible to develop enriched category theory in the more general setting in which the enrichment is taken in a closed symmetric monoidal category, in many cases it is sufficient to take the enrichment in a commutative quantale
V
, that is to say a monoid in the monoidal category of suplattices. This leads to the notion of quantale-enriched categories which can be seen as a generalization of the notion of ordered sets where one substitutes the ordered relation with a more general relation—called enriched structure—with values in the quantale
V
.

Since quantale-enriched categories are a generalization of ordered sets, it is natural to ask which relations there are between the two. The very first observation is that to every quantale-enriched category
X
we can associate an ordered set, called the underlying ordered set of
X
; its order relation relates elements of
X
whose value under the enriched structure of
X
is greater or equal than the unit of
V
. This construction is part of a right adjoint functor between the category of quantale-enriched categories and the category of ordered sets. Due to the form this functor has, any hope to recovery the structure of a quantale-enriched category
(X,a)
from its underlying ordered set is going to be disappointed. In order to mantain such hope, we we must add some structure to the category of ordered sets; a structure that must contain the information which gets lost in the discretization procedure: the values of the enriched relation at elements of
X
.

The solution to this problem is to consider ordered sets equipped with a suitable action of the base quantale subject to conditions that allow us to define a copowered enriched category, where the copower becomes the action itself. This association will give us an equivalence between the category of ordered sets equipped with such an action and the category of copowered categories (see
[
10
]
for the general construction).
The aforementioned equivalence restricts to an equivalence between the category of cocomplete quantale-enriched categories and the category of cocomplete ordered sets equipped with an action of the base quantale, also called the category of modules (see
[
28
]
).
These last two equivalences allow us to reason about enriched categories by using order theoretic arguments. An example where this is not only useful, but it has proven to be essential, is given by the results contained in
[
14
]
, where, in order to obtain the duality between metric compact Hausdorff spaces and (suitably defined) finitely cocomplete categories enriched in the unit interval
[0,1]
, the representation of the latter as ordered sets with an action of
[0,1]
is essential.

In
[
11
]
D. Hofmann and G. Gutierres proved that a similar result holds also for approach spaces. Approach spaces are particular examples of
(T,V)
-categories (see
[
15
]
) where the monad
T
is specialized to the ultrafilter monad
U
and
V
is specialized to the quantale
[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
. For these categories, Clementino, Hofmann and Tholen showed how it is possible to develop many constructions that come from enriched category theory in the more general context of
(T,V)
-categories (see
[
12
,
6
,
13
]
). In particular, in
[
12
]
, Hofmann showed how algebras for a Kock-Zöberlein monad, which generalizes the presheaf monad, characterize cocomplete
(T,V)
-categories. By using the machinery of
(T,V)
-categories, D. Hofmann and G. Gutierres proved that separated (i.e.
T_{0}
) cocomplete approach spaces are equivalent to continuous lattices (cocomplete topological spaces in the
(U,2)
setting) equipped with an action of the quantale
[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
.

The aim of this paper is to prove that a similar result holds also for quantale-enriched multicategories. We also notice that quantale-enriched multicategories, from now on called
(L,V)
-categories, are particular examples of
(T,V)
-categories where the monad
T
is specialized to the list monad
L
.
We prove that the category
\mathtt{CoCts}((L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}})
of cocomplete separated
(L,V)
-categories is equivalent to the category of quantales (ordered cocomplete multicategories) equipped with a suitable action of
V
and denoted
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
.

We must point out that, although approach spaces and quantale-enriched multicategories are both examples of
(T,V)
-categories, the strategy used to prove the main result of this paper bears little relationship to the one used in
[
11
]
; while the latter relies on a careful study of weighted
(U,[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}})
-colimits, the former essentially relies on the fact that we can internalize the notion of monoid in every monoidal category. The deep reason why approach spaces and quantale-enriched multicategories behave differently is an interesting open question the author wants to investigate; the hope is to provide a more general theory of “actions” for
(T,V)
-categories.

The structure of the paper is as follows:

•

In the first section we introduce some background material on
V
-categories. We briefly sketch the equivalence between the category of cocomplete quantale-enriched categories and the category of modules:

\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}.

•

In the second section we introduce
(L,V)
-categories. We show how many constructions that come from enriched category theory can be developed in the more general context of
(L,V)
-categories.

•

The third section contains the first step towards our desired result. We analyze further the equivalence
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
. First we prove that both categories can be equipped with a monoidal structure, then we prove that the aforementioned equivalence extends to the corresponding categories of monoids:

\mathtt{Mon}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}},\boxtimes_{V},V)\simeq\mathtt{Mon}({\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})},\boxtimes_{V},V).

•

In the fourth section we study further the category
\mathtt{Mon}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}},\boxtimes_{V},V)
. We prove that
{\mathtt{Mon}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}},\boxtimes_{V},V)}
is equivalent to a particular subcategory of
V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant}
.

•

In the fifth section we study further the category
\mathtt{Mon}({\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})},\boxtimes_{V},V)
. We prove that it is monadic over
\mathtt{Set}
and that it is equivalent to
\mathtt{CoCts}((L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}})
, the category of cocomplete separated
(L,V)
-categories.

•

In the last section we collect everything together and prove that
\mathtt{CoCts}((L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}})
is equivalent
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
, the category of quantales equipped with a suitable action of
V
.

•

In the appendix we recall some useful materials from
[
17
]
about strong commutative monads we use across the paper.

2
Preliminaries on Quantale-Enriched Categories

In this section we recall/introduce some basic notions of
V
-categories. Our point of view is slightly different from the more "standard" one contained in
[
20
]
, it is more "relational": following
[
3
,
7
]
, we introduce the
quantaloid
of
V
-relations and we define
V
-categories starting from there. This might be seen as an overkill, but it will be clear in the section related to
(L,V)
-categories how this approach allows us to smoothly introduce some concepts also in the
(L,V)
-case.

2.1
V-Categories and V-Functors

Definition 2.1
.

A quantale
(V,\otimes,k)
is a complete lattice endowed with a multiplication
\otimes:V\times V\rightarrow V
that preserves suprema in each variable and for which
k\in V
is the neutral element. If
k\neq\perp,
we call
V
non-trivial.

Remark 2.2
.

When we talk about quantale-enriched categories we always assume our base quantale
V
to be commutative.

Remark 2.3
.

In this paper we assume—unless explicitly stated—our quantales to be non-trivial.

Remark 2.4
.

By the adjoint functor theorem applied to ordered sets, it follows that
-\otimes=
admits a right adjoint (in each variable) denoted by
[-,=]
and called "internal hom".

Definition 2.5
.

Let
(V,\otimes_{V},k_{V})
and
(Q,\otimes_{Q},k_{Q})
be quantales. A
morphism of quantales
is a suprema preserving map
f:V\rightarrow Q
such that, for all
v,w\in V
,

f(v)\otimes_{Q}f(w)=f(v\otimes_{V}w),\ \ k_{Q}=f(k_{V}).

Examples 2.6
.

1.

The two-element boolean algebra
\mathbf{2}=\{0,1\}
with
\wedge
as multiplication and
\Rightarrow
as internal hom is a quantale.

2.

More generally, every frame becomes a quantale with the multiplication given by
\wedge
. In this case we have
k=\top,
where
\top
is the top element of the frame.

3.

[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
(with the opposite of the natural order) with
+
as multiplication is a quantale. The internal hom is given by "truncated minus" defined as
[u,v]=v\ominus u=\mathtt{max}(v-u,0)
.

4.

Consider the set

\Delta=\{\psi:[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,1]\mbox{ $\mid$ }\mbox{for all }\alpha\in[0,\infty]\mbox{ : }\psi(\alpha)=\bigvee_{\beta<\alpha}\psi(\beta)\}

of distribution functions. With the pointwise order it becomes a complete ordered set. For all
{\psi,\phi\in\Delta}
and
\alpha\in[0,\infty]
, define:

\psi\otimes\phi(\alpha)=\bigvee_{\beta+\gamma<\alpha}\psi(\beta)\ast\phi(\gamma),

where
\ast
is the ordinary multiplication on
[0,1]
.
It is shown in
[
9
]
that
(\Delta,\otimes,k)
is a quantale, where
k(0)=0
and, for all
\alpha>0
,
k(\alpha)=1
.

As we stated in the introduction of this section, we are going to present
V
-categories from a more "relational" point of view. The first step is to define the
quantaloid of
V
-relations
which is the enriched generalization of the category
\mathtt{Rel}
of (ordinary) binary relations. For an account on quantaloids we refer to
[
32
]
for a brief overview and to
[
31
]
for a more in depth description.

The quantaloid
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
is the order-enriched category whose objects are sets, and an arrow
r:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y
is given by a function

r:X\times Y\rightarrow V.

The composition of
r:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y
,
s:Y{\mathrel{\to}}Z
is given by "matrix multiplication" and is defined pointwise as

s\circ r(x,z)=\bigvee_{y\in Y}r(x,y)\otimes s(y,z).

The identity arrow
\Id:X{\mathrel{\to}}X
is

\Id(x_{1},x_{2})=\begin{cases}k&\mbox{ if }x_{1}=x_{2},\\
\perp&\mbox{ if }x_{1}\neq x_{2}.\end{cases}

The complete order on
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}(X,Y)
is the one induced (pointwise) by
V
, i.e.

r\leq r^{\prime}\mbox{ in }{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}(X,Y)\mbox{ whenever }r(x,y)\leq r^{\prime}(x,y)\mbox{ in }V\mbox{ for all }x,y\in X,Y.

(1)

Remark 2.7
.

Notice that
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}(X,Y)
is complete because
V
is so. Since the multiplication of
V
preserves suprema in both variables and because suprema commute with suprema, one has

(\bigvee_{i}r_{i})\circ(\bigvee_{j}s_{j})=\bigvee_{i,j}r_{i}\circ s_{j}.

This proves that
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
is a quantaloid.

Remark 2.8
.

Notice that for
V=\mathbf{2}
,
{\mathbf{2}}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
is the quantaloid of relations, and the "matrix multiplication" defined previously becomes the "classical" relational composition.

Remark 2.9
.

Note that (
1
) is equivalent to

k\leq\bigwedge_{x,y\in X,Y}[r(x,y),r^{\prime}(x,y)].

Indeed, consider
x,y\in X,Y
, then we have

r(x,y)\leq r^{\prime}(x,y)\iff k\otimes r(x,y)\leq r^{\prime}(x,y)\iff k\leq[r(x,y),r^{\prime}(x,y)].

Remark 2.10
.

Notice that every function
f:X\rightarrow Y
can be seen as a
V
-relation as follows:

f(x,y)=\begin{cases}k&\mbox{ if }f(x)=y,\\
\perp&\mbox{ if }f(x)\neq y.\end{cases}

The identity in
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}(X,X)
is an example of this construction.

We have also an involution
(-)^{\circ}:{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}^{\mathtt{op}}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
defined as
r^{\circ}(y,x)=r(x,y)
, which satisfies

(1_{X})^{\circ}=1_{X},\ \ (s\circ r)^{\circ}=r^{\circ}\circ s^{\circ},\ \ (r^{\circ})^{\circ}=r.

Definition 2.11
.

A
V
-category is a pair
(X,a)
, where
X
is a set and
a:X{\mathrel{\to}}X
is a
V
-relation that satisfies

•

\Id\leq a;

•

a\circ a\leq a
.

Remark 2.12
.

In this paper, when the
V
-structure is clear from the context, we will denote a
V
-category
(X,a)
simply as
X
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
(X,a)
and
(Y,b)
be
V
-categories. A
V
-
functor

f:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)
is a function between the underlying sets such that

a\leq f^{\circ}\circ b\circ f,

which, in pointwise terms, means that, for all
x,y\in X,

a(x,y)\leq b(f(x),f(y)).

If the equality holds, we call
f

fully faithful
.

Examples 2.14
.

1.

For
V=\mathbf{2}
, a
\mathbf{2}
-category is an ordered set and a
\mathbf{2}
-functor is a monotone map. Notice that the order relation of a
\mathbf{2}
-category
(X,\leq_{X})
does not need to be antisymmetric.

2.

Categories enriched in the quantale
[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
, as first recognized by Lawvere in
[
24
]
, are generalized metric spaces and
[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
-functors between them are non-expansive maps.

3.

Categories enriched in
\Delta
are probabilistic metric spaces, as first recognized in
[
9
]
.

4.

The quantale
V
defines a
V
-category with the
V
-structure given by its internal hom
[-,=]
.

5.

By using the involution
(-)^{\circ}:{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}^{\mathtt{op}}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
, for every
V
-category
(X,a)
, one can define its opposite category
X^{\mathtt{op}}=(X,a^{\circ}).

6.

Let
(X,a)
and
(Y,b)
be
V
-categories. We define the
V
-category formed by all
V
-functors
{f:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)}
, denoted by
([X,Y],[X,Y](-,=))
, with the following
V
-structure:

[X,Y](f,g)=\bigwedge_{x\in X}b(f(x),g(x)).

In particular we have two very important
V
-categories:

\mdmathbb{D}(X)=[X^{\mathtt{op}},V],\mbox{ the category of presheaves},

\mdmathbb{U}(X)=[X,V]^{\mathtt{op}},\mbox{ the category of co-presheaves.}

Notice that they are generalizations (for a general
V
) of the classical down(up)-closed subsets construction that corresponds to the case in which
V=\mathbf{2}
.

7.

Given a
V
-category
(X,a)
, there are two
V
-functors, called the Yoneda embedding and the co-Yoneda embedding:

\mathbf{y}_{X}:X\rightarrow\mdmathbb{D}(X),\ \ x\mapsto a(-,x),

\mathbf{\lambda}_{X}:X\rightarrow\mdmathbb{U}(X),\ \ x\mapsto a(x,=).

Moreover, one can prove that

\mdmathbb{U}(X)[\mathbf{\lambda}_{X}(x),g]=g(x),\ \ \mdmathbb{D}(X)[\mathbf{y}_{X}(x),g]=g(x).

The last two results are known as the co-Yoneda lemma and Yoneda lemma, respectively. Notice that, for a general
X
,
\mathbf{y}_{X}
and
\mathbf{\lambda}_{X}
are not injective functions. They are injective iff
X
is separated (see
[
16
, Proposition 1.5]
).

8.

Let
(X,a)
and
(Y,b)
be
V
-categories. We define their tensor product

X\boxtimes Y=(X\times Y,a\otimes b).

In particular, one has:
X\boxtimes K\simeq X
where
K
denotes the one-point
V
-category
(1,k)
.

For
V=\mathbf{2}
, the ordered structure on
X\boxtimes Y
is the product order. This means that
(x_{1},y_{1})\leq_{X\boxtimes Y}(x_{2},y_{2})
if and only if
x_{1}\leq_{X}x_{2}
and
y_{1}\leq_{Y}y_{2}
.

For
V=[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
, the metric structure on
X\boxtimes Y
is the taxicab metric, which is defined as:

d_{X\boxtimes Y}((x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2}))=d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+d_{Y}(y_{1},y_{2}).

In this way we define
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
as the category whose objects are
V
-categories and whose arrows are
V
-functors. Moreover,
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
becomes an order-enriched category, if we define, for
V
-functors
{f,g:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)}
,

f\leq g\mbox{ whenever }k\leq\bigwedge_{x\in X}b(f(x),g(x)).

With the tensor product previously defined,
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
becomes a closed monoidal category, since one can show that, for
V
-categories
(X,a),(Y,b),(Z,c)
, one has

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}(X\boxtimes Y,Z)\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}(X,[Y,Z])\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}(Y,[X,Z]).

This allows us to define monoids with respect to such product, which we call monoidal
V
-categories.

Definition 2.15
.

A monoidal
V
-category
(X,a,\ast,u_{X})
is a
V
-category
(X,a)
equipped with two
V
-functors:
\ast:X\boxtimes X\rightarrow X
and
u_{X}:K\rightarrow X
, such that
(X,a,\ast,u_{X})
is a monoid (with respect to the monoidal structure
(\boxtimes,K)
).

Remark 2.16
.

Notice that
K=(1,k)
is a separator in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
. This means that, for all pairs of parallel
V
-functors
f,g:X\rightarrow Y
in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
, if
f\cdot z=g\cdot z
for every
V
-functor
z:K\rightarrow X
, then
f=g
.

Remark 2.17
.

Notice that, for
V=\mathbf{2}
, a monoidal ordered set is just an ordered monoid. That is to say it is a monoid endowed with an order relation which is compatible with the monoid structure.

For
V=[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}
, a monoid in
[0,\infty]^{\mathtt{op}}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
is a metric space endowed with a monoid structure on its underlying set which is compatible with the metric. Examples of monoidal metric spaces are the underlying additive groups of normed vector spaces.

2.2
Distributors and the Presheaf Monad

Bénabou introduced distributors in
[
2
]
and since then they played an important role in category theory. They can be seen as generalizations of ideal relations from order theory, that is to say, subsets of the cartesian product of ordered sets
X,Y
which are downward closed in
X
and upward closed in
Y
.

The presheaf construction is one of the cornerstones of category theory. A presheaf is a generalization of a downward closed subset on an ordered set
X
. In the following we are going to show how the presheaf construction is part of a monad defined on
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
.

Definition 2.18
.

Let
(X,a)
and
(Y,b)
be
V
-categories. A
V
-
distributor
(or simply a distributor)
{j:(X,a){\mathrel{\to}}(Y,b)}
is a
V
-relation between the underlying sets such that:

•

j\circ a\leq j;

•

b\circ j\leq j.

Since the composite of distributors is again a distributor, we define the quantaloid
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}
in the same way as we defined
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}.
In
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}(X,X)
the
V
-structure
a
plays the role of the identity, since for every distributor
j:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y
, one has

b\circ j=j\circ a=j.

Given a
V
-functor
f:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)
, we define two arrows in
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
:

•

f_{*}:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y,\ \ f_{*}(x,y)=b(f(x),y);

•

f^{*}:Y{\mathrel{\to}}X,\ \ f^{*}(y,x)=b(y,f(x)).

One has:

Lemma 2.19
.

The
V
-relations
f_{*}
and
f^{*}
are both distributors, moreover,
f_{*}\dashv f^{*}
in
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}.

In this way we have two
2
-functors

(-)_{*}:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}^{\mathtt{co}}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist},\ \ (-)^{*}:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}^{\mathtt{op}}.

By juggling with the definition of distributor, one can show that distributors between
V
-categories
(X,a),(Y,b)
are in bijective correspondence with
V
-functors between
X^{\mathtt{op}}\boxtimes Y
and
V
. It is easy to prove that this correspondence is functorial and that it gives an equivalence of ordered sets

{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}(X,Y)\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}(X^{\mathtt{op}}\boxtimes Y,V)\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}(Y,\mdmathbb{D}(X)),

where we associate to every
V
-distributor
j:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y
its
mate

\ulcorner j\urcorner:Y\rightarrow\mdmathbb{D}(X),\ \ y\mapsto j(-,y).

Proposition 2.20
.

The
2

(-)^{*}:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}^{\mathtt{op}}

2

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Dist}^{\mathtt{op}}}

Notice that, since

A
V

To every

Remark 2.26
.

Notice that the underlying orderd set of the

.

Remark 2.28
.

The arguments we are going to use in this paper rely—mainly—on the monadicity over

Definition 2.29
.

We say that a
V
-category

is copowered if, for all

admits a left adjoint in

denoted by

for all

We say that a

between copowered

In this way we can form the

If we start with a separated copowered category
(X,a)
and we take its underlying ordered set, then
{-\odot x:V\rightarrow X}
becomes a monotone map of the type

-\odot x:(V,\leq)\rightarrow(X,\leq_{a}).

Moreover, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 2.30

Under the same hypothesis as above, the monotone map

enjoys the following properties, for all

,

•

•

•

, for every set
\{u_{i}\mbox{ | }i\in I\}

First observe that

follows from

.

Fix an

. Then, for all

, we have

\displaystyle a(v\odot(u\odot x),y)

\displaystyle=[v,a(u\odot x,y)]

\displaystyle=[v,[u,a(x,y)]]

\displaystyle=[v\otimes u,a(x,y)]

\displaystyle=a((v\otimes u)\odot x,y),

from which

Finally, the last property follows from the adjunction
-\odot x\dashv a(x,=)
.

where the latter is the Eilenberg-Moore category for the enriched powerset monad
\mathtt{P}_{V}
, from which it follows that
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
is monadic over
\mathtt{Set}
.
We have the following commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\leq_{X},\rho)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,a_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X.}

Since we know that
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
is an equivalence, and since the forgetful functor
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
is monadic, by the commutativity of the previous diagram it follows that the forgetful functor
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
is monadic too. We can decompose the forgetful functor
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
as follows.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Sup}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\leq_{X},\rho)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,a_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X,\leq_{X})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Since
\mathtt{Sup}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
is the right adjoint to the powerset functor

\mathtt{P}_{\mathbf{2}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup},\ \ X\mapsto(\mathtt{P}_{\mathbf{2}}(X),\subseteq),

in order to conclude we only need to provide a left adjoint to
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup}
. Then by composing it with the powerset functor we would have our desired monad.

Let’s reveal the identity of the butler in our detective story. For a suplattice
(X,\leq_{X})
, we define an action on
V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X
by:

V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X\xrightarrow{\otimes\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}\Id}V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X,\mbox{ ($\otimes:V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}V\rightarrow V$ is the multiplication of V). }

The fact that it defines an action follows directly from the fact that
\otimes
defines a monoid structure on
V
. Moreover, it is clear that if we have a morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
\Id\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}f
defines an equivariant (with respect to the aforementioned action) arrow.

Proposition 2.43
.

Let
V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}=:\mathtt{Sup}\rightarrow V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
be the functor we described before. Then it is left adjoint to the forgetful functor
U:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup}.

Proof.

The unit at
(X,\leq_{X})
in
\mathtt{Sup}
is given by

\eta_{X}:X\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbf{2}\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X\xrightarrow{}V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X,\quad x\mapsto k\boxtimes_{2}x,

while the counit at
(Y,\leq_{Y},\rho)
in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
is given by

\epsilon_{Y}:V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Y\xrightarrow{\rho}Y.

The unit-counit equations are easily seen to be satisfied.

∎

Remark 2.44
.

We can easily prove that the aforementioned adjunction is monadic. It is straightforward to show that
U:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup}
reflects isomorphisms. Let
X\rightrightarrows Y
be a
U
-split pair in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
. Let
Z
be the coequalizer of this
U
-split pair in
\mathtt{Sup}
, then

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Z}

is a split coequalizer in
\mathtt{Sup}
. By using the split and the universal property of coequalizers, we have a unique arrow
V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Z\rightarrow Z
, as depicted in the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!}

Since
Y\rightarrow Z
is an epimorphism, and since
\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}
preserves epimorphisms, the action
V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}Z\rightarrow Z
makes

an object in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
and thus

is the coequalizer of

in

.

The resulting monad
T=(V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}=,\eta,\mu)
, has as unit

\eta_{X}:X\xrightarrow{\sim}1\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X\rightarrow V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}M,

and as multiplication

\mu_{X}:V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X\xrightarrow{\otimes\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}\Id}V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}X.

If we compose
\mathtt{P}_{\mathbf{2}}
with

, we have the left adjoint to the monadic forgetful functor
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
we were looking for. In this way we obtain a
\mathtt{Set}
monad

that sends a set

to the underlying set of

and a function

Hence we have an equivalence of monads:

\mathtt{P}_{V}\simeq V\boxtimes_{\mathbf{2}}\mathtt{P}_{\mathbf{2}}.

3
Quantale-Enriched Multicategories

(L,V)
-categories are a special case of the more general
(T,V)
-categories, where the list monad
L
is considered. They are also the order-enriched version of
multicategories
(see
[
25
]
and
[
15
]
for an account on them, and
[
23
]
for a historical perspective). The basic idea is that, instead of having arrows with just a single object as the domain, we allow them to have as domain a list of objects.

In this section we introduce
(L,V)
-categories and some of their basic constructions, by mirroring what we have done in the previous section.

3.1
(L,V)-Categories and (L,V)-functors

Recall that the list monad is the monad whose underlying functor is given by

L:\mathtt{Sets}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sets},\ \ f:X\rightarrow Y\mapsto Lf:\amalg_{n\geq 0}X^{n}\rightarrow\amalg_{m\geq 0}Y^{m},\ \ \underline{x}=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\mapsto(f(x_{1}),...,f(x_{n})),

and whose unit and multiplication at a set
X
are defined as:

•

e_{X}:X\rightarrow L(X),\ \ x\mapsto(x);

•

m_{X}:L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L(X),\ \ (\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{n})\mapsto(x_{11},...,x_{1k},...,x_{n1},...,x_{nl}).

Remark 3.1
.

Let
\underline{x}
,
\underline{w}
be lists. In order to avoid possible confusion with the list of lists
\underline{\underline{y}}=(\underline{x},\underline{w})
, we denote the list obtained by concatenating
\underline{x}
and
\underline{w}
as
(\underline{x};\underline{w})
. Moreover, in the case in which one of the two is a single element list, we use the shortcut
(\underline{x};w)
instead of
(\underline{x};(w)).

We can extend (in a functorial way) the list monad
L
to
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
by defining, for
r:X{\mathrel{\to}}Y
:

\tilde{L}r:L(X){\mathrel{\to}}L(Y),\ \ (\underline{x},\underline{y})\mapsto\begin{cases}r(x_{1},y_{1})\otimes...\otimes r(x_{n},y_{n})&\mbox{ if the two lists have the same length,}\\
\perp&\mbox{ otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

One can prove that this particular extension defines a monad on
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
that, moreover, preserves the involution

(-)^{\circ}:{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}^{\mathtt{op}}\rightarrow{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}.

Remark 3.2
.

From now on we will use
L
for both the ordinary list monad and its extension to
{V}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
.

This allows us to define the order-enriched category
{(L,V)}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}
in which a morphism
r:X\mathrel{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\xrightarrow{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}$}]{\xrightharpoonup{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}\hskip-12.05553pt{\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt}}Y
is a
V
-relation of the form

r:L(X){\mathrel{\to}}Y,

and in which composition is given by

s\bullet r=s\circ Lr\circ m_{X}^{\circ},

where
e_{X}^{\circ}:X\mathrel{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\xrightarrow{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}$}]{\xrightharpoonup{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}\hskip-12.05553pt{\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt}}X
is the identity.

Remark 3.3
.

Note that, due to the Kleisli-style composition we defined,
-\bullet r
preserves suprema, but
s\bullet(=)
does not in general.

Definition 3.4
.

An
(L,V)\mbox{-}
category is a pair
(X,a)
, where
X
is a set and
a:X\mathrel{\mathmakebox[\widthof{$\xrightarrow{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}$}]{\xrightharpoonup{\rule{4.37012pt}{0.0pt}}\hskip-12.05553pt{\circ}\hskip 4.30554pt}}X
is an
(L,V)
-relation that satisfies:

•

e_{X}^{\circ}\leq a
;

•

a\bullet a\leq a.

Remark 3.5
.

When
V=\mathbf{2}
, the
(L,\mathbf{2})
-structure of an
(L,\mathbf{2})
-category
(X,a)
is a subset
a\subseteq L(X)\times X
such that:

•

for all
x\in X
,
((x),x)\in a
;

•

given
(\underline{z}_{1},...,\underline{z}_{n})\in L^{2}(X)
,
\underline{x}\in LX
, and
y\in X
, such that

((\underline{z}_{1},...,\underline{z}_{n}),\underline{x})\in La,\mbox{ and }(\underline{x},y)\in a,

then

((\underline{z}_{1};...;\underline{z}_{n}),y)\in a.

Notice that
La
is the subset that corresponds to the relation
La:L^{2}(X)\times LX\rightarrow\mathbf{2}
that one obtains by applying the extension of the list monad to the relation
a:LX\times X\rightarrow\mathbf{2}
.

Definition 3.6
.

Let
(X,a)
and
(Y,b)
be
(L,V)
-categories. An
(L,V)\mbox{-}
functor

f:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)
is a function between the underlying sets such that

a\leq f^{\circ}\circ b\circ Lf,

which, in pointwise terms, means that, for all
\underline{x}\in LX
,
y\in X
,

a(\underline{x},y)\leq b(Lf(\underline{x}),f(y)).

If the equality holds, we call

fully faithful.

Remark 3.7
.

If
V=\mathbf{2}
, then an
(L,\mathbf{2})
-functor
f:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)
satisfies, for all
\underline{x}\in LX
,

,

(\underline{x},y)\in a\mbox{ implies }(Lf(\underline{x}),f(y))\in b.

Notice how this generalizes the classical monotonicity condition.

In this way we define
(L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
as the category whose objects are
(L,V)
-categories and whose arrows are
(L,V)
-functors, moreover,
(L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}
becomes an order-enriched category if we define, for two
(L,V)
-functors
f,g:(X,a)\rightarrow(Y,b)
,

f\leq g\mbox{ whenever }k\leq\bigwedge_{x\in X}b(Lf((x)),g(x)).

Examples 3.8
.

1.

Every set

defines an

-category with

-structure. In particular, we define the one-point

2.

Every set

-category if we consider the free

.

3.

-category where

Let

be

-categories. We can form their tensor product

where

Here

and

are the obvious projections. Unluckily, in general it is not true that

Remark 3.9
.

In general, every monoidal

-category

defines an

{a(\underline{x},y)=a(x_{1}\ast...\ast x_{n},y).}

In this way we can define a

which has a left adjoint

that sends an

Using the aforementioned adjunction, we can extend the monad

Remark 3.10
.

A priori, due to the non-symmetric form of arrows in
{(L,V)}\mbox{-}\mathtt{Rel}

(L,V)
-category that seems to play the role of a dual. Luckily, we can use the adjunction

and the involution in

to define, for an

-category

, its opposite category as

. At first this might be seen as an

.

whose underlying set consists of all

\mdmathbb{D}_{L}(X)[\underline{f},g]=\bigwedge_{(\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{n})\in LX^{2}}[(f_{1}(\underline{x}_{1}),...,f_{n}(\underline{x}_{n})),g(m_{x}((\underline{x}_{1},...,\underline{x}_{n}))))],

Remark 3.11
.

We have a fully faithful functor, called the Yoneda embedding,

\mathbf{y}_{X}:X\rightarrow\mdmathbb{D}_{L}(X),\ \ x\mapsto a(-,x).

Moreover, it can be proved that

\mdmathbb{D}_{L}(X)[L\mathbf{y}_{X}(\underline{x}),g]=g(\underline{x}).

The last result is known as the Yoneda Lemma.

4
Refining
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}

In Section
2.3
we proved that there exists an equivalence

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}.

In this section, first we prove that both categories can be equipped with a monoidal structure, and then we prove that the aforementioned equivalence extends to the corresponding categories of monoids.

In Proposition
D.2
we prove that
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
admits a closed symmetric monoidal structure
(\boxtimes_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}},V)
for which the free functor

\mathtt{P}_{{V}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}

becomes strong monoidal. This monoidal structure comes from the fact that the
V
-powerset monad
(\mathtt{P}_{{V}},u,n)
is a strong commutative monad. Moreover, this monoidal structure has another interesting property: it classifies bimorphisms. This means that there exists a natural isomorphism (for all
(X,\alpha)
,
(Y,\beta)
,
(Z,\theta)\in\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
)

\mathtt{Bim}_{\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}(X\times Y,Z)\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}(X\boxtimes_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}}Y,Z),

where

\mathtt{Bim}_{\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}(X\times Y,=):\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}

is the functor that sends an algebra
(Z,\theta)
, to the set of bimorphisms of the form

f:X\times Y\rightarrow Z
. Here a function
f:X\times Y\rightarrow Z,
is a bimorphism if, for all
x\in X
,
y\in Y
,

f_{x}:Y\rightarrow Z,\mbox{ }y\mapsto f(x,y)\mbox{ and }f_{y}:X\rightarrow Z,\mbox{ }x\mapsto f(x,y)

are morphisms in
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
(see Definition
D.3
and Proposition
D.5
).

Remark 4.1
.

Notice that in the case in which

classifies bimorphisms, where a bimorphism

be a bimorphism between

Proposition 4.4
.

The equivalence

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}

extends to an equivalence beetween the corresponding category of monoids

\mathtt{Mon}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}},\boxtimes_{V},V)\simeq\mathtt{Mon}({\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}},\boxtimes_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}},V).

Proof.

Let us call
[-]:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
the functor which realizes the equivalence
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
which is the composite of

we obtained in Theorem

with
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
we described in Subsection

. Let
f:X\times Y\rightarrow Z
be a bimorphism between
V
-modules. Since equivalences preserve products we have
[X\times Y]\simeq[X]\times[Y]
. Moreover, because for all

,

are morphisms in

, we have
[f_{x}]
and
[f_{y}]
are morphisms in

. From

it follows that
[f_{x}]=[f]_{x}
and
[f_{y}]=[f]_{y}
. This implies that

defines a bimorphism

in
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
. This shows that we have a bijection

\mathtt{Bim}_{V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}}(X\times Y,Z)\simeq\mathtt{Bim}_{\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}([X]\times[Y],[Z])

which can be easily seen to be natual (since

is a functor and all the "change of base" components are given by pre-post composition).
Now, from

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(X\boxtimes_{V}Y,Z)\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}([X\boxtimes_{V}Y],[Z])

and from

(2)

\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}([X]\boxtimes_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}}[Y],[Z])\simeq\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}([X\boxtimes_{V}Y],[Z]),

from which we can deduce that

The compatibility of

and the associators follows from the bijection

Moreover, since

and the unitors are compatible with

Remark 4.5
.

Remember that
V
, as a
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}
-algebra, has the structure given by

n_{1}:\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(1))\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(1),\ \ j\mapsto\bigvee_{w}j(w)\otimes w,

while the
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}
-algebra associated to the cocomplete

-category
(V,[-,=])
is
(V,\alpha)
, where

\alpha(j)=\mathtt{Sup}_{V}(\overline{j})=\bigvee_{w}\overline{j}(w)\otimes w,\mbox{ where }\overline{j}(w)=\bigvee_{v}[w,v]\otimes j(v).

In order to show that
[V]\simeq V
, we need to prove that the two structures are the same, that is to say

\bigvee_{w}j(w)\otimes w=\bigvee_{w}(\bigvee_{v}[w,v]\otimes j(v))\otimes w.

But since

, we have
\mathtt{Sup}_{V}(\mathbf{y}_{V}(v))=\bigvee_{v}[w,v]\otimes v=v
. Thus the result follows.

Remark 4.6
.

Remember that the (left) unitor in the monoidal category
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
, at an object
(X,\alpha)
, is the corresponding morphism to the bimorphism

\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(1)\times X\rightarrow X,\quad(v,x)\mapsto\alpha(v\boxtimes e_{X}(x)).

While the (left) unitor in the monoidal category
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
, at an object
(X,\rho)
, is the corresponding morphism to the bimorphism

The equivalence
[-]
sends
(X,\rho)
to the cocomplete
V
-category
(X,a)
that has
\rho
as copower. The category
(X,a)
is then sent to the
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}
-algebra
(X,\alpha^{\prime})
, where
\alpha^{\prime}=\mathtt{Sup}_{X}(-\circ a)
. In this way, we get that the unitor

becomes the copower of

which is then sent to the map

\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(1)\times X\rightarrow X,\quad(v,x)\mapsto v\odot_{\rho}x.

In order to conclude, we notice that

\displaystyle\mathtt{Sup}_{X}(v\boxtimes e_{X}(x)\circ a)

\displaystyle=\mathtt{Sup}_{X}(v\boxtimes\mathbf{y}_{X}(x)),

\displaystyle=v\odot_{\rho}x.

5
First Interlude: Algebras and Modules

In this section we study further the category

whose objects are morphisms of quantales

.

Second Interlude: Injectives and Monoids

The enriched powerset monad

is a strong commutative monad. This means (see Proposition

) that the free functor

is strong monoidal. Thus

extends to a functor, which we denote again as

Remark 6.1
.

Let

be a monoid. Then

is the free

-algebra whose monoid structure is the following. The unit of

is

The multiplication of

is the unique

-morphism that corresponds to the bimorphism

where

\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(M)\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(M)\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(M\times M),\quad(\psi,\phi)\mapsto\psi\boxtimes\phi:(m,n)\mapsto\psi(m)\otimes\phi(n).

Since
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}
is left adjoint to the forgetful functor

\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set},

by using Lemma

, we can conclude that the diagram
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Mon}({\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}},\boxtimes_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}},V)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Mon}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{Set}}

We are now ready to conclude our tour de force and finally prove our desired result.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
be the category whose objects are quantales
(Q,\ast,k_{Q})
equipped with an action
\rho:V\boxtimes_{2}Q\rightarrow Q
that is a monoid homomorphism and whose arrows are equivariant morphisms of quantales.

Remark 7.2
.

Notice that, to give an arrow
\rho:V\boxtimes_{2}Q\rightarrow Q
in
\mathtt{Sup}
, is equivalent to give an arrow

\rho^{\prime}:V\boxtimes Q\rightarrow Q

in
\mathtt{Ord}
that preserves suprema in each variable. Moreover,
\rho
is an action iff
\rho^{\prime}
is an action. It is also true that
\rho
is a monoid homomorphism iff
\rho^{\prime}
is a monoid homomorphism.

Proposition 7.3
.

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})\simeq(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}.

Proof.

Let
f:V\rightarrow(Q,\ast_{Q},k_{Q})
be an object of
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
. Define the following function:

\rho^{\prime}_{f}:V\boxtimes Q\rightarrow Q,\quad(v,q)\mapsto f(v)\ast_{Q}q.

Because
f
is a morphism of quantales and the multiplication of a quantale preserves suprema, it follows that
\rho^{\prime}_{f}
defines a unique arrow

\rho_{f}:V\boxtimes_{2}Q\rightarrow Q

in
\mathtt{Sup}
. It is straightforward to show that
\rho^{\prime}_{f}
is an action, hence
\rho_{f}
is an action too.

By using the fact that
f:V\rightarrow Q
is an object of
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
, we can also prove that
\rho^{\prime}_{f}
is a monoid homomorphism. Indeed, let
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in Q
, then we have

\displaystyle\rho^{\prime}_{f}((v_{1},q_{1})\ast_{V\boxtimes Q}(v_{2},q_{2}))

\displaystyle=\rho^{\prime}_{f}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2},q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2})

\displaystyle=f(v_{1}\otimes v_{2})\ast_{Q}q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2}

\displaystyle=f(v_{1})\ast_{Q}f(v_{2})\ast_{Q}q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2}

\displaystyle=f(v_{1})\ast_{Q}q_{1}\ast_{Q}f(v_{2})\ast_{Q}q_{2}

\displaystyle=\rho^{\prime}_{f}(v_{1},x_{1})\ast_{V\boxtimes Q}\rho^{\prime}_{f}(v_{2},x_{2}).

Thus
(Q,\rho)
is an object of
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
. Let
h:Q\rightarrow W
, where
f:V\rightarrow Q
and
g:V\rightarrow W
are objects of
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
, be a morphism in
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
. It is straightforward to verify that
h:(Q,\rho_{f})\rightarrow(W,\rho_{g})
is a morphism in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
. Thus we have a functor

F:(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}\rightarrow V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant}).

Let
\rho:V\boxtimes_{2}(Q,\ast,k_{Q})\rightarrow(Q,\ast,k_{Q})
be an object of
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
and let
\rho^{\prime}:V\boxtimes Q\rightarrow Q
be as in Remark
7.2
. Define the following morphism of quantales

f_{\rho^{\prime}}:V\rightarrow Q,\quad v\mapsto\rho^{\prime}(v,k_{Q}).

We have, for
q\in Q
,
v\in V
,

f_{\rho^{\prime}}(v)\ast_{Q}q=\rho^{\prime}(v,k_{Q})\ast_{V\boxtimes Q}\rho^{\prime}(k,q)=\rho^{\prime}(v,q)=\rho^{\prime}(k,q)\ast_{V\boxtimes Q}\rho^{\prime}(v,k_{Q})=q\ast_{Q}f_{\rho^{\prime}}(v).

If
h:(Q,\rho)\rightarrow(W,\theta)
is an arrow in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
, then
h\cdot f_{\rho^{\prime}}=f_{\theta^{\prime}}
. Thus we have a functor

G:V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})\rightarrow(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}.

Easy calculations show that

and

V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
and
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
.

∎

Remark 7.4
.

If
(X,\rho,\leq_{X})
is an object in
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}
, then the map
\rho(v,=):X\rightarrow X
defines a morphism in
\mathtt{Sup}
. Let
\rho:V\boxtimes Q\rightarrow Q
be an object of
V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant})
. We might be tempted to see (or at least, the author was) if something similar holds. Unfortunately,
\rho(v,=):Q\rightarrow Q
does not define a morphism of quantales. Consider

, then we would have

\rho(v,q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2})=\rho(v,q_{1})\ast_{Q}\rho(v,q_{2})

which in general is not true. In the previous proposition we showed that evey

is “essentially” of the form
f(-)\ast_{Q}=
, for an object

of
(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}
. It is easy to see that

\rho(v,q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2}):=f(v)\ast_{Q}q_{1}\ast_{Q}q_{2},

in general is not equal to

\rho(v,q_{1})\ast_{Q}\rho(v,q_{2}):=f(v)\ast_{Q}q_{1}\ast f(v)\ast_{Q}q_{2}.

For example, one can take

,

and

.

The last proposition allows us to conclude:

Theorem 7.5
.

\mathtt{CoCts}((L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}})\simeq V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}(\mathtt{Quant}).

We have the following chain of equivalences

\displaystyle\mathtt{CoCts}((L,{V})\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}})

\displaystyle\simeq\mathtt{Mon}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Mod}},\boxtimes_{V},V)

(by Proposition

\displaystyle\simeq(V\downarrow\mathtt{Quant})_{\spadesuit}

(by Proposition

(by Proposition

)

∎

\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)=2^{X}
, and by

\mathtt{P}_{{2}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set},\mbox{ }X\rightarrow Y\mapsto\mathtt{P}_{{2}}f:\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(Y),

where
\mathtt{P}_{{2}}f(A)=\{f(x)\mbox{ such that }x\in A\}.

It is well known that it is part of a monad
(\mathtt{P}_{{2}},u,n)
, called the powerset monad, where:

•

The unit at
X
is given by
u_{x}:X\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X),\mbox{ }x\rightarrow\{x\};

•

The multiplication at
X
,
n_{X}:\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X))\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)
is defined by

n_{X}(\mathcal{A})=\bigcup\{A\in\mathcal{A}\}.

It is straightforward to see that algebras for this monad are suplattices, where the free functor
{\mathtt{P}_{{2}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup}}
sends a set
X
to
(\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X),\subseteq)
.

Let
X
and
Y
be suplattices. It is possible to form their tensor product
X\boxtimes_{2}Y
, defined as follows

X\boxtimes_{2}Y=\{C\in\mathtt{P}(X\times Y)\mbox{ | }\forall A\in\mathtt{P}(X),\forall B\in\mathtt{P}(Y),A\times B\subseteq C\iff(\bigvee A,\bigvee B)\in C\},

with the order structure induced by the one
P(X\times Y)
has.

Moreover,
-\boxtimes_{2}=
defines a symmetric closed monoidal structure on
\mathtt{Sup}
with unit
\mathbf{2}\simeq\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(1)
. With respect to this monoidal structure, since for
X,Y
,

\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)\boxtimes_{2}\mathtt{P}(Y)\simeq\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X\times Y),

the free functor
\mathtt{P}_{{2}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Sup}
becomes strong monoidal.

The tensor product just defined has another interesting property: it classifies bimorphisms. Let
X
,
Y
and
Z
be in
\mathtt{Sup}
, a bimorphism
f:X\boxtimes Y\rightarrow Z
is a function such that, for all
x,y\in X,Y
,

f_{x}:Y\rightarrow Z,\mbox{ }y\mapsto f(x,y),

f_{y}:X\rightarrow Z,\mbox{ }x\mapsto f(x,y),

are both suprema preserving maps.

This defines, for every
X,Y\in\mathtt{Sup}
, a functor

\mathtt{Bim}(X\boxtimes Y,=):\mathtt{Sup}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set},

where
\mathtt{Bim}(X\boxtimes Y,Z)
denotes the sets of bimorphisms from
X\boxtimes Y
to
Z
. The fact that
\boxtimes_{2}
classifies bimorphisms means that there exists, for all
X,Y,Z\in\mathtt{Sup}
, a natural bijection

\mathtt{Bim}(X\boxtimes Y,Z)\simeq\mathtt{Sup}(X\boxtimes_{2}Y,Z)

which is realized by a universal bimorphism

\pi:X\boxtimes Y\rightarrow X\boxtimes_{2}Y.

That is to say, for every bimorphism
f:X\boxtimes Y\rightarrow Z
, there exists a unique suprema preserving map
{\overline{f}:X\boxtimes_{2}Y\rightarrow Z}
that makes the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\boxtimes Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\boxtimes_{2}Y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\overline{f}}

commute.

Before we conclude this section we must point out that that the tensor product
X\boxtimes_{2}Y
can be also expressed as the coequalizer of the following parallel pair of arrows

\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)\times\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(Y))\xrightarrow{\mathtt{P}_{{2}}t}\mathtt{P}_{{2}}^{2}(X\times Y)\xrightarrow{n}\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X\times Y),

\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)\times\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(Y))\xrightarrow{\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(\alpha\times\beta)}\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X\times Y).

Here we have

t:\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X)\times\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(Y)\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X\times Y),\mbox{ }(A,B)\mapsto A\times B,

and

\alpha\times\beta:\mathtt{P}_{{2}}(X\times Y)\rightarrow X\times Y,\mbox{ }(A\times B)\mapsto(\alpha(A),\beta(B)),

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are the algebras structures on
X
and
Y
respectively.

In the next section we will see how crucial the existence of an arrow like
t
is in order to build the tensor product of algebras.

The main result about strong monads we are interested in is contained in the following theorem.

Theorem C.5
.

[
17
, Lemmas 5.1-5.3]

Let
(T,e,m)
be a strong monad on a symmetric monoidal category
(C,\otimes,1)
such that its associated category of algebras
C^{T}
has coequalizers of reflexive pairs. Then, for each algebras
(X,\alpha)
,
(Y,\beta)
,
\mathtt{Bim}(X\otimes Y,=)
is representable by an algebra
(X\otimes_{T}Y,\alpha\otimes_{T}\beta)
.

If additionally
(T,e,m)
is commutative, then
C^{T}
becomes a symmetric monoidal category with
\otimes_{T}
as tensor product and with the free algebra
(T1,m_{1})
as the unit; moreover, the free functor
F:C\rightarrow C^{T}
becomes strong monoidal. If
C
has equalizers and its monoidal structure is closed, then also
C^{T}
becomes a closed monoidal category.

Remark C.6
.

Let
(X,\alpha)
and
(Y,\beta)
be
T
-algebras. Their tensor product is the coequalizer of the following parallel pair of arrows

T(T(X)\otimes T(Y))\xrightarrow{T\mathtt{dst}_{X,Y}}T^{2}(X\otimes Y)\xrightarrow{m}T(X\otimes Y),

T(T(X)\otimes T(Y))\xrightarrow{T(\alpha\otimes\beta)}T(X\otimes Y).

Note that this is exactly how we defined the tensor product of complete lattices.

Remark C.7
.

Let
(T,m,e)
be a monad with

. Then, if we assume the axiom of choice,
\mathtt{Set}^{T}
is cocomplete (see
[
26
,
1
]
). Thus, Eilenberg-Moore categories for strong monads defined on

always satisfy the hypotesis of Theorem

.

Remark C.8
.

We obtain the associator in
C^{T}
from the one in
C
by using the universal property of bimorphisms. Similarly, we can obtain the unitors in
C^{T}
by using the tensorial strength. As an example, the left unitor at an object

is the arrow associated to the bimorphism

T1\otimes X\xrightarrow{st^{\prime}_{1,X}}T(1\otimes X)\xrightarrow{\simeq}T(X)\xrightarrow{\alpha}X.

Appendix D
Applications

The
V
-powerset monad
(\mathtt{P}_{{V}},u,n)
is the enriched generalization of the classical powerset monad, where we define
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}:\mathtt{Set}\rightarrow\mathtt{Set}
by putting
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)=V^{X}
and, for
f:X\rightarrow Y
and
\phi\in V^{X}

\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(f)(\phi)(y)=\bigvee_{x\in f^{-1}(y)}\phi(x).

Moreover:

•

u_{X}:X\rightarrow V^{X}
is the transpose of the diagonal
\bigtriangleup_{X}:X\times X\rightarrow V
;

•

n_{X}:\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X))\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)
is defined by
n_{X}(\Phi)(x)=\bigvee_{\phi\in V^{X}}\Phi(\phi)\otimes\phi(x)
.

Consider
\mathtt{Set}
as a monoidal category in the usual way, that is to say, with its cartesian structure and consider the following function

\mathtt{st}_{X,Y}:X\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}Y\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X\times Y),\mbox{ }(x,\phi)\mapsto(u_{X}(x)\boxtimes\phi),

where
(u_{X}(x)\boxtimes\phi)(\tilde{x},y)=u_{X}(x)(\tilde{x})\otimes\phi(y).

Long and boring computations that someone must do, show that this makes
(\mathtt{P}_{{V}},u,n)
into a strong monad. Indeed, the commutativity of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X\times Y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{st}_{1,Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

is straightforward. While, from
u_{X\times Y}\simeq u_{X}\boxtimes u_{Y}
, it follows that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X\times Y)\times Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}((X\times Y)\times Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times(Y\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(Y\times Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X\times(Y\times Z))}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{st}_{X\times Y,Z}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{st}_{X,Y\times Z}}

commutes, since

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces((x,y),\psi)}

In the same way it follows that the diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u_{X}\boxtimes u_{Y}.}

To verify the commutativity of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times\mathtt{P}_{{V}}^{2}Y}

Remark D.4
.

Notice that, for
V=\mathbf{2}
, we recover the notion of bimorphism we gave for complete lattices in the first section.

Now we have not only one but two notions of bimorphism! Of course, as one might expect, the two notions coincide.

Proposition D.5
.

Suppose
(X,\alpha),(Y,\beta),(Z,\gamma)
are in
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
. A function
f:X\times Y\rightarrow Z
is a bimorphism according to Definition
C.4
iff it is so according to Definition
D.3
.

Proof.

Let us do the case in which we "fix"
y\in Y
, the other one is similar.

The proof follows by contemplating the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X\times 1)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\clubsuit}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\diamondsuit}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)\times 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)\times Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X\times Y)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(Z)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\spadesuit}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\times Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(1_{X}\times y)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{st}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}X}\times y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\times 1}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha\times\Id_{Y}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{st}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathtt{P}_{{V}}f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\gamma}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\simeq}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1\times y}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}

Here
\clubsuit
commutes since
\mathtt{P}_{{V}}
is a strong monad while
\diamondsuit
commutes because
\mathtt{st}^{\prime}
is a natural transformation.

Suppose
f
is a bimorphism according to
C.4
, then
\spadesuit
commutes, hence the outer diagram too. This implies that
f
is a bimorphism according to
D.3
too.

If
f
is a bimorphism according to
D.3
, then the outer diagram commutes, hence, for all
y\in Y
, we have

\gamma\cdot\mathtt{P}_{{V}}f\cdot\mathtt{st}^{\prime}\cdot 1_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}X}\times y=f\cdot\alpha\times\Id_{Y}\cdot 1_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}X}\times y.

Since
(1_{\mathtt{P}_{{V}}X}\times y:\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)\times 1\rightarrow\mathtt{P}_{{V}}(X)\times Y)_{y\in Y},
is a jointly epic family, we can jointly cancel them in the previous equation. Thus we obtain the commutativity of
\spadesuit
which implies that
f
is a bimorphism according to
C.4
too.

∎

Remark D.6
.

In Theorem
2.24
we proved that the category of algebras for this monads is equivalent to
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})
. Hence the monoidal structure on
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}
transfers to a monoidal structure on
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})
.

In particular, from the previous proposition, and since the equivalence

\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}\simeq\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})

changes only the corresponding structures (and it leaves the underlying sets and arrows unchanged), we have that a
V
-functor
f:(X,a)\boxtimes(Y,b)\rightarrow(Z,c)
is a bimorphism if, for all
x,y\in X,Y
, one has

f_{x}:(Y,b)\rightarrow(Z,c),\mbox{ }y\mapsto f(x,y),

f_{y}:(X,a)\rightarrow(Z,c),\mbox{ }x\mapsto f(x,y),

are cocontinuous
V
-functor.

Since these kind of bimorphisms are classified by a monoidal structure on
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})
, as described at the end of
[
20
]
, by arguments similar to the one we used in Proposition
4.4
, we get that the monoidal structure on
\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})
, induced by the equivalence
\mathtt{Set}^{\mathtt{P}_{V}}\simeq\mathtt{CoCts}({V\mbox{-}\mathtt{Cat}_{\mathtt{sep}}})
, and the one studied in
[
20
]
coincide.

Nonperturbative phase diagram of two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) {\cal N}=(2,2) super-Yang–Mills
Navdeep Singh Dhindsa, Raghav G. Jha, Anosh Joseph, David Schaich

Abstract
We consider two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 2 , 2 ) {\cal N}=(2,2) Yang–Mills theory with gauge group SU( N N ) in Euclidean signature compactified on a torus with thermal fermion boundary conditions imposed on one cycle. We perform non-perturbative lattice analyses of this theory for large 12 ≤ N ≤ 20 12\leq N\leq 20 . Although no holographic dual of this theory is yet known, we conduct numerical investigations to check for features similar to the two-dimensional 𝒩 = ( 8 , 8 ) {\cal N}=(8,8) Yang–Mills theory, which has a well-defined gravity dual. We perform lattice field theory calculations to determine the phase diagram, observing a spatial deconfinement transition similar to the maximally supersymmetric case. However, the transition does not continue to low temperature, implying the absence of a topology-changing transition between black hole geometries in any holographic dual for this four-supercharge theory.



I
Introduction

A version of the holographic duality conjecture relates weakly coupled gravitational theories in
D+1
spacetime dimensions to strongly coupled SU(
N
) supersymmetric Yang–Mills (SYM) theories with maximal supersymmetry in
D
spacetime dimensions in the large-
N
limit
Maldacena 1998
;
Itzhaki et al. 1998
. Studying strongly coupled pure SYM theories using analytical methods can be extremely hard. A lattice formulation of these theories provides an inherently non-perturbative way to investigate them at strong coupling and finite
N
. A naive lattice regularization of a supersymmetric field theory would explicitly break supersymmetry. However, for certain classes of SYM theories, it is possible to preserve a subset of the supersymmetries at non-zero lattice spacing. In particular, these supersymmetric lattice constructions require at least
2^{D}
supercharges in
D
spacetime dimensions. This condition is satisfied for pure SYM theories with sixteen supercharges, with known large-
N
holographic duals, for all
2\leq D\leq 4
. See Ref.
Catterall et al. 2009
for a thorough review of these constructions and Ref.
Schaich 2023
for a review of more recent work.

The two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SU(
N
) SYM theory at finite temperature and compactified on a spatial circle has a well-defined holographic dual at large
N
. The dual gravitational system, at low temperature, contains various types of black hole solutions, with compact spatial circle, arising in Type IIA and IIB supergravity. The phase diagram of the gravitational system is expected to contain a region where homogeneous D1 (black string) solutions that wrap around the spatial circle dominate and another in which D0 (black hole) solutions that are localized on the spatial circle dominate. At low temperature, there exists a first-order Gregory–Laflamme (GL) phase transition between the homogeneous and localized solutions
Gregory and Laflamme 1993
;
Li et al. 1999
;
Martinec and Sahakian 1999
;
Kol 2005
;
Aharony et al. 2004
;
Harmark and Obers 2004
;
Aharony et al. 2006
.
In the dual gauge theory, this corresponds to a ‘spatial deconfinement’ transition with the spatial Wilson line magnitude serving as an order parameter.

In this work, we focus on the gauge theory system with a lower number of supercharges: two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SU(
N
) SYM theory on a Euclidean torus. Although a well-defined holographic dual has not yet been constructed for this theory, we aim to understand how much it resembles its sixteen-supercharge counterpart, which has a well-defined gravity dual. It is also interesting to consider how the reduction of supersymmetry can affect the holographic features of a given theory. We use lattice field theory to investigate the phase structure of
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM and compare this to the corresponding lattice results for
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM
Catterall et al. 2010
;
Catterall et al. 2018a
. Specifically, we map out the line of spatial deconfinement transitions as a function of the shape of the torus quantified by its aspect ratio [Eq. (
10
)].

\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM theory, including Refs.

II
Two-dimensional
{\cal N}=(2,2)
lattice SYM theory

The two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM theory can be obtained by dimensionally reducing four-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=1
SYM. The parent theory, in Euclidean spacetime, has a global symmetry group
\text{SO(}4\text{)}_{E}\times\text{U(1)}
, with
\text{SO(}4\text{)}_{E}
and
U(1)
denoting the Euclidean rotation symmetry and chiral symmetry, respectively. After dimensional reduction, the global symmetry group becomes
\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{E}\times\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{R_{1}}\times\text{U(1)}_{R_{2}}
, with
\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{E}
denoting the Euclidean rotation symmetry,
\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{R_{1}}
the rotation symmetry along the reduced dimensions, and
\text{U(1)}_{R_{2}}
the chiral symmetry.

We turn to a topologically twisted version of the original two-dimensional theory to construct the lattice
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM theory that preserves one of the four supercharges. In flat Euclidean spacetime, twisting amounts to relabelling the fields and supercharges of the original theory. We can take the diagonal sub-group of the rotation groups,
\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{E}
and
\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{R_{1}}
, to define

\text{SO(}2\text{)}^{\prime}\equiv\text{diag}\Big(\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{E}\times\text{SO(}2\text{)}_{R_{1}}\Big),

(1)

which we call the twisted rotation group. After twisting, the fields and supercharges of the untwisted theory rearrange themselves to form representations of the twisted rotation group. The four supercharges of the original theory combine in a way that they become integer-spin supercharges of the twisted theory: a scalar
\mathcal{Q}
, a vector
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
with
a=1
,
2
, and an antisymmetric tensor
\mathcal{Q}_{ab}=-\mathcal{Q}_{ba}
. The fermionic degrees of freedom rearrange themselves into a set of geometric fermions comprising of a scalar fermion
\eta
, a vector fermion
\psi_{a}
and an antisymmetric tensor fermion
\chi_{ab}
. Rearrangements of the fields happen in the bosonic sector as well — the gauge field
A_{a}
and the two scalars
X_{a}
combine to form a complexified gauge field
\mathcal{A}_{a}=A_{a}+iX_{a}
that transforms as a vector under the twisted rotation group.
1
1

1

This complexification promotes SU(
N
) to U(
N
) gauge invariance, as discussed by Ref.
Schaich 2023
and references therein. For notational convenience, we continue to refer to SU(
N
) gauge groups.

After twisting, the action of the original theory can be expressed in a
\mathcal{Q}
-exact form:
S=\mathcal{Q}\Psi
, with

\Psi=\frac{N}{4\lambda}\int d^{2}x~\text{Tr}\left(\chi_{ab}\mathcal{F}_{ab}+\eta\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a},\mathcal{D}_{a}\right]-\frac{1}{2}\eta d\right),

(2)

summing over repeated indices.
Here
\lambda
is the ’t Hooft coupling,
\mathcal{F}_{ab}=\left[\mathcal{D}_{a},\mathcal{D}_{b}\right]
is the complexified field strength, and
\mathcal{D}_{a}=\partial_{a}+\mathcal{A}_{a}
is the complexified covariant derivative. We use an anti-Hermitian basis for the generators of the gauge group with
\text{Tr}(T_{A}T_{B})=-\delta_{AB}
.
The scalar supercharge
\mathcal{Q}
acts on the twisted fields in the following (nilpotent) way:

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{A}_{a}

\displaystyle=\psi_{a},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\psi_{a}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\chi_{ab}

\displaystyle=-\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ab},

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{a}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}\eta

\displaystyle=d,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}d

\displaystyle=0.

Here
\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ab}=\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a},\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{b}\right]
with
\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a}=\partial_{a}+\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{a}
and
\overline{\mathcal{A}}_{a}=A_{a}-iX_{a}
.
We have also introduced a bosonic auxiliary field
d
for the off-shell completion of the supersymmetry algebra.
It obeys the equation of motion

d=\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a},\mathcal{D}_{a}\right].

(3)

After performing the
\mathcal{Q}
variation on
\Psi
and integrating over the auxiliary field
d
, the continuum twisted action takes the form

\begin{split}S=\frac{N}{4\lambda}\int d^{2}x~\text{Tr}\Bigg(&-\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ab}\mathcal{F}_{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a},\mathcal{D}_{a}\right]^{2}\\
&-\chi_{ab}\mathcal{D}_{[a}\psi_{b]}-\eta\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a}\psi_{a}\Bigg).\end{split}

(4)

It is easy to show that this action is invariant under the scalar supercharge:
\mathcal{Q}S=0
.
Using the equations of motion, one can show that it is also invariant under the remaining three twisted supercharges
Catterall 2006
;
Catterall et al. 2009
;
Catterall et al. 2013
.

We can construct a supersymmetric lattice action from the twisted action given in Eq. (
4
).
The lattice action remains exactly invariant under the scalar supercharge
\mathcal{Q}
, thanks to the fact that this is still a nilpotent supercharge,
\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0
, at non-zero lattice spacing.
Note that the other three twisted supercharges, though nilpotent in the continuum, are no longer nilpotent on the lattice, and thus they are broken at non-zero lattice spacing.
However, it can be shown that we recover these broken supersymmetries, along with the Euclidean rotational symmetry, as the continuum limit of the lattice theory is taken
Cohen et al. 2003
;
Catterall 2006
;
Catterall et al. 2009
;
Catterall et al. 2013
.

We discretize the twisted action on a two-dimensional square lattice spanned by two orthogonal unit vectors,
\widehat{\mu}_{a}
, with
a=1
,
2
. We employ the geometrical discretization scheme provided in Ref.
Damgaard and Matsuura 2008
to place the fields and their derivatives on the lattice unit cell in a gauge-invariant way.

On the lattice, the complexified gauge field is mapped to a complexified link,
\mathcal{A}_{a}(x)\rightarrow\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)
, which is an element of the algebra
\mathfrak{gl}(N,\mathbb{C})
.
The field
\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)
lives on an oriented link connecting sites
n
and
n+\widehat{\mu}_{a}
.
Its superpartner, the vector fermion
\psi_{a}(n)
, is also a link variable oriented along the same link.
Similarly,
\eta(n)
lives on the site
n
while
\chi_{ab}(n)
lives on the diagonal of the unit cell. The placements and orientations of the fields ensure a gauge-invariant lattice action.
Finite difference operators replace the covariant derivatives according to the rules given in Ref.
Damgaard and Matsuura 2008
. We have

\begin{split}\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a}^{(-)}f_{a}(n)&=f_{a}(n)\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}(n)-\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}(n-\widehat{\mu}_{a})f_{a}(n-\widehat{\mu}_{a}),\\
\mathcal{D}_{a}^{(+)}f_{b}(n)&=\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)f_{b}(n+\widehat{\mu}_{a})-f_{b}(n)\mathcal{U}_{a}(n+\widehat{\mu}_{b}).\end{split}

(5)

The lattice action, which is local, doubler-free, gauge invariant, and
\mathcal{Q}
supersymmetric, is then

\displaystyle S=\frac{N}{4\lambda_{\text{lat}}}

\displaystyle\sum_{n}\text{Tr}\Bigg[-\overline{\mathcal{F}}_{ab}(n)\mathcal{F}_{ab}(n)+\frac{1}{2}\left(\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a}^{(-)}\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle-\chi_{ab}(n)\mathcal{D}^{(+)}_{~[a}\psi^{\ }_{~b]}(n)-\eta(n)\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{a}^{(-)}\psi_{a}(n)\Bigg].

(6)

In addition to this action, we add a scalar potential with a tunable coefficient
\mu
to lift flat directions in the theory.
While this softly breaks the scalar
\mathcal{Q}
supersymmetry, it is needed in order to carry out numerical calculations. Thus, the complete action is given by

S_{\text{total}}=S+\frac{N\mu^{2}}{4\lambda_{\text{lat}}}\sum_{n,a}\text{Tr}\left[\left(\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}(n)\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)-\mathbb{I}_{N}\right)^{2}\right].

(7)

III
Lattice calculations

III.1
Lattice setup

We denote as
N_{\tau}
and
N_{x}
the number of lattice sites along the temporal and spatial directions.
With
\beta
and
L
the dimensionful temporal and spatial extents, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=aN_{\tau},

\displaystyle L

\displaystyle=aN_{x}.

(8)

We obtain a torus by imposing thermal boundary conditions (BCs), which are periodic for all fields along the spatial direction and periodic (anti-periodic) for bosons (fermions) along the temporal direction.
All fields and variables on the lattice are made dimensionless using the dimensionful ’t Hooft coupling
\lambda
.
We define dimensionless temporal and spatial extents as

\displaystyle r_{\tau}

\displaystyle\equiv\beta\sqrt{\lambda}=N_{\tau}\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{lat}}}=1/t,

(9a)

\displaystyle r_{x}

\displaystyle\equiv L\sqrt{\lambda}=N_{x}\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{lat}}},

(9b)

with
t
denoting the dimensionless temperature and
\lambda_{\text{lat}}\equiv\lambda a^{2}
the dimensionless ’t Hooft coupling.
We also define the aspect ratio
\alpha
mentioned above, which can be expressed in any of the three ways:

\alpha=\frac{L}{\beta}=\frac{r_{x}}{r_{\tau}}=\frac{N_{x}}{N_{\tau}}.

(10)

We carry out numerical calculations with fixed
r_{x}
and
r_{\tau}
, meaning that the
a\to 0
continuum limit corresponds to
N_{x,\tau}\to\infty
while
\lambda_{\text{lat}}\to 0
.

To ensure that the supersymmetry-breaking scalar potential we added to the action in Eq. (
7
) is automatically removed in the
\lambda_{\text{lat}}\to 0
continuum limit, we set

\mu=\zeta\frac{r_{\tau}}{N_{\tau}}=\zeta\sqrt{\lambda}a=\zeta\sqrt{\lambda_{\text{lat}}},

(11)

and carry out numerical computations with fixed (dimensionless)
\zeta
.

This two-dimensional supersymmetric lattice system is implemented in the publicly available parallel software
Bergner et al. 2021
presented by Ref.
Schaich and DeGrand 2015
.
This package provides a rational hybrid Monte Carlo (RHMC) algorithm and measurements of the Wilson lines, scalar eigenvalues, extremal eigenvalues and pfaffian phase of the fermion operator, and other observables of interest.
Using this code, numerical results for the
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM vacuum energy were presented in Ref.
Catterall et al. 2018b
.
There, it was shown that the vacuum energy density vanishes within uncertainties, which is consistent with the absence of dynamical supersymmetry breaking in this theory.
Ref.
Catterall et al. 2018b
also investigated the phase of the pfaffian for
1\leq r_{\tau}\leq 9
, finding that the phase fluctuations are negligible throughout this range and vanish as the continuum limit is taken.

In our numerical calculations, it is necessary to choose values of
\zeta
that are large enough to lift the flat directions without introducing excessive soft
\mathcal{Q}
supersymmetry breaking.
We confirm the successful lifting of flat directions by monitoring the Maldacena loop

M\equiv\frac{1}{NN_{x}}\sum_{x=0}^{N_{x}-1}\text{Tr}\left[\prod_{t=0}^{N_{\tau}-1}\mathcal{U}_{t}(x,t)\right],

(12)

and ensuring that it is stable with an
\mathcal{O}(1)
magnitude.
The largest value is
\left\langle|M|\right\rangle=3.97(9)
, for an ensemble with gauge group SU(12), lattice size
12\!\times\!12
, and a high temperature corresponding to
r_{\tau}=0.6
.
Full numerical results are available in the open data release Ref.
Dhindsa et al. 2023
, along with similar sanity checks of the RHMC acceptance rate and the Creutz equality
\left\langle e^{-\Delta H}\right\rangle=1

Creutz 1988
.

Similarly, we monitor violations of two
\mathcal{Q}
Ward identities to ensure that
\zeta
is not too large.
As discussed in more detail by Ref.
Catterall and Schaich 2015
, each Ward identity can be expressed as the vacuum expectation value of the supersymmetry transformation of a suitable local operator,
\left\langle\mathcal{Q}\mathcal{O}\right\rangle
.
The lattice action in Eq. (
6
) already provides one such local operator.
Because the fermion action is gaussian, this Ward identity fixes the bosonic action per lattice site to be
s_{B}=3N^{2}/2
.
We can therefore use

\frac{\left|\left\langle s_{B}\right\rangle-1.5N^{2}\right|}{1.5N^{2}}

as a normalized measure of its violation.
The largest violation is 0.0234(3) for an ensemble with gauge group SU(12), lattice size
12\!\times\!12
, and a low temperature corresponding to
r_{\tau}=5
.

Similarly, Ref.
Catterall et al. 2014
pointed out that another suitable Ward identity is provided by

\mathcal{Q}\text{Tr}\left[\eta\mathcal{U}_{a}\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right]=\text{Tr}\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{b}^{(-)}\mathcal{U}_{b}\mathcal{U}_{a}\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right]-\text{Tr}\left[\eta\psi_{a}\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right],

(13)

using the equation of motion for the bosonic auxiliary field, Eq. (
3
).
It is convenient to introduce the shorthand notation
B\equiv\text{Tr}\left[\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{b}^{(-)}\mathcal{U}_{b}\mathcal{U}_{a}\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right]
for the purely bosonic term in this difference, and
F\equiv\text{Tr}\left[\eta\psi_{a}\overline{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\right]
for the term involving the
\eta\psi
fermion bilinear that we compute stochastically using random gaussian noise vectors.
Then

\left|\left\langle\frac{B-F}{\sqrt{B^{2}+F^{2}}}\right\rangle\right|

provides a normalized measure of the violation of this Ward identity.
Its largest value is 0.0375(7), again for gauge group SU(12), lattice size
12\!\times\!12
, and
r_{\tau}=5
.
All results for these two Ward identities are also available in the open data release Ref.
Dhindsa et al. 2023
.

III.2
Spatial deconfinement transition

In this work, we focus on the behavior of the Wilson lines wrapping around the torus in either the spatial or temporal direction for various values of
r_{\tau}
and
r_{x}
.
As discussed above, for the maximally supersymmetric
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM theory, the spatial deconfinement phase transition signaled by the spatial Wilson line is related to a topology-changing transition between black-string and black-hole geometries in the holographic dual supergravity solutions.
This transition was observed through lattice field theory calculations in Refs.
Catterall et al. 2010
;
Catterall et al. 2018a
.
That work also monitored the Polyakov loop (that is, the temporal Wilson line) to ensure the system was thermally deconfined, which is needed for holographic duality to hold in principle
Witten 1998
.
We now present our new numerical results for two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM theory, which indicate that the spatial deconfinement transition in this theory is restricted to the high-temperature regime
r_{\tau}\lesssim 1
.

The specific observable we use to analyze the phase transition is the unitarized spatial Wilson line,

W^{u}\equiv\frac{1}{NN_{\tau}}\sum_{t=0}^{N_{\tau}-1}\text{Tr}\left[\prod_{x=0}^{N_{x}-1}U_{x}(x,t)\right].

(14)

Here,
U_{a}(n)
is extracted from the polar decomposition

\mathcal{U}_{a}(n)=e^{X_{a}(n)}U_{a}(n),

(15)

where we can identify
U_{a}(n)
as the unitarized gauge link connecting sites
n
and
n+\widehat{\mu}_{a}
, and
X_{a}(n)
as the scalar field at site
n
.
In the spatially deconfined phase,
W^{u}
sits in one of the degenerate
Z_{N}
vacua with a large magnitude, spontaneously breaking the
Z_{N}
center symmetry.
Note that Eq. (
14

|W^{u}|\leq 1
, with equality for the free theory, so “large” in this context means
0.5\lesssim\left\langle|W^{u}|\right\rangle\leq 1
.
In the spatially confined phase,
\left\langle|W^{u}|\right\rangle\to 0
in the large-
N

We also monitor the unitarized Polyakov loop

P^{u}\equiv\frac{1}{NN_{x}}\sum_{x=0}^{N_{x}-1}\text{Tr}\left[\prod_{t=0}^{N_{\tau}-1}U_{t}(x,t)\right].

(16)

The calculations need to remain in the thermally deconfined phase corresponding to a large Polyakov loop magnitude,
0.5\lesssim\left\langle|P^{u}|\right\rangle\leq 1
, to admit, in principle, a holographic dual interpretation in terms of a black hole geometry.

To analyze the spatial deconfinement transition in more detail, we compute the susceptibility of the spatial Wilson line, defined as

\chi_{{}_{W^{u}}}\equiv N^{2}\left(\left\langle|W^{u}|^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle|W^{u}|\right\rangle^{2}\right).

The location of the susceptibility peak provides the critical inverse temperature

While these

In addition, by analyzing how the height of this peak

By fixing the lattice size so that the number of degrees of freedom scales

(18)

with

In the case of a first-order transition, we expect the peak height to scale proportionally to the number of degrees of freedom,

;

that larger
(N_{x},N_{\tau})
simply reduces the lattice spacing at the transition, with the
a\to 0
continuum limit corresponding to
N_{x}=\alpha N_{\tau}\to\infty
.
We have started to study discretization artifacts for gauge group SU(12) by analyzing larger lattice sizes up to
32\!\times\!32
(Table
1
).
For aspect ratio
\alpha=1
, our results for
\left\langle|W^{u}|\right\rangle
agree within statistical uncertainties across these lattice sizes.
However, the rapid increase in computational costs with the number of colors,
\sim N^{7/2}
, has so far prevented us from repeating our full scaling analysis for these larger systems.

,

\max\{N_{x},N_{\tau}\}=24
(Table
1
).
For each aspect ratio, we use the spatial Wilson line susceptibility to determine the critical
r_{\tau}^{(c)}
that we present in Fig.

.
For all of these results, we use three values of
\zeta

\zeta\to 0
limit where the soft supersymmetry breaking from Eq. (

) vanishes.

For large aspect ratios
\alpha\geq 3/2
where the spatial deconfinement transition occurs at relatively high temperatures corresponding to
r_{\tau}^{(c)}\lesssim 0.5
, we find behavior similar to the two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM case
Catterall et al. 2018a
.
In particular, the transition moves to lower temperatures (larger
r_{\tau}
) as
\alpha
decreases.
For
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM, this behavior persists into the low-temperature holographic regime
r_{\tau}\gg 1
, where the transition is predicted by dual supergravity solutions.
Our new results in Fig.
5
indicate that
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM behaves differently: For smaller
\alpha\leq 1
, the critical inverse temperature becomes roughly constant,
r_{\tau}^{(c)}\approx 1
.

While this is a striking contrast compared to the maximally supersymmetric theory, the absence of a spatial deconfinement transition in the low-temperature region where holography would be valid,
r_{\tau}\gg 1
, does not rule out the existence of a gravity dual for two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM.
Instead, it indicates that any low-temperature dual gravity solution would not undergo a topology-changing transition between a phase dominated by homogeneous D1 (black string) solutions wrapping around the spatial circle and a phase dominated by D0 (black hole) solutions localized on the spatial circle.

IV
Conclusions

In this work, we have used non-perturbative lattice field theory calculations to study the phase diagram of two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM.
We first formulated the finite-temperature lattice theory using a lattice action that preserves one of the four supercharges at non-zero lattice spacing.
From numerical calculations, we observed spatial deconfinement transitions for a range of aspect ratios
1/2\leq\alpha\leq 4
and determined the critical inverse temperatures
r_{\tau}^{(c)}
.
For the case
\alpha=1
, we additionally compared results for gauge groups SU(12), SU(16), and SU(20) to estimate that this may be a continuous second-order phase transition.

Although our high-temperature results corresponding to
\alpha\geq 3/2
are qualitatively similar to those obtained for
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM
Catterall et al. 2018a
, striking differences appear at lower temperatures (aspect ratios
\alpha\leq 1
).
In particular, the transition does not continue moving to lower temperatures for smaller aspect ratios.
Instead, the critical inverse temperature becomes roughly constant around
r_{\tau}^{(c)}\approx 1
.
That is, the transition does not persist in the low-temperature regime where any holographic gravity solution would be valid.
This suggests that the holographic dual of this gauge theory, if it exists, does not undergo a black hole topology-changing transition at low temperatures.

The results presented in this work lay the groundwork for further lattice investigations of
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM at finite temperature.
In particular, we are analyzing the ‘extent of the scalars’,
\text{Tr}\left[X^{2}\right]
, again in order to compare this theory against
\mathcal{N}=(8,8)
SYM, where large-
N
holography relates this observable to bound states of D-branes in the dual gravitational solutions.
We recently presented preliminary results from this analysis in Ref.
Dhindsa et al. 2022
.
Another target of our work is to investigate how the eigenvalues of the scalars spread among themselves, which is interesting because it would be holographically related to the ‘extent of space’.

We can also build on the work reported here by analyzing related lattice field theories.
For example, Ref.
Aharony et al. 2006
provides an incomplete phase diagram of the two-dimensional bosonic theory obtained by removing the fermions from
\mathcal{N}=(2,2)
SYM.
It would be interesting to reproduce and extend this phase diagram using non-perturbative lattice calculations.
Another interesting problem for the future will be to study two-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=(4,4)
SYM.
This would complete the family of two-dimensional pure SYM theories for which lattice calculations can preserve a subset of the supersymmetries at non-zero lattice spacing.
Comparing all three theories with four, eight, and sixteen supercharges would test whether or not the continuation of the spatial deconfinement transition to the low-temperature holographic regime is a unique feature of the maximally supersymmetric case.

Appendix A
Summary of lattice ensembles

Table
1
summarizes the 231 lattice ensembles used in our numerical analyses.
As discussed in Sec.
III
, we consider a range of aspect ratios
1/2\leq\alpha\leq 4
, and for
\alpha=1
additionally investigate several lattice sizes up to
32\times 32
, and three SU(
N
) gauge groups up to
N=20
.
For each ensemble we typically generate 3500 molecular dynamics time units (MDTU), using unit-length trajectories in the RHMC algorithm, and impose a thermalization cut after the first 500 MDTU.
Simple observables including the Maldacena loop, unitarized Wilson line, and the extent of the scalars
\text{Tr}\left[X^{2}\right]
are measured after every trajectory.
More involved measurements including the stochastic computation of the fermion bilinear term that appears in the
\mathcal{Q}
Ward identity Eq. (
13
) are done using configurations saved to disk every 10 MDTU.
Another measurement done using these saved configurations is to compute the extremal eigenvalues of the squared fermion operator
D^{{\dagger}}D
, where
\Psi^{T}D\Psi
corresponds to the terms in the action Eq. (
6
) involving the fermions
\Psi^{T}=(\eta,\psi_{a},\chi_{ab})
.
These eigenvalue computations are performed using a Davidson-type method provided by the PReconditioned Iterative Multi-Method Eigensolver (PRIMME) library
Stathopoulos and McCombs 2010
.

After generating configurations and carrying out these measurements, we use the ‘autocorr’ module in
emcee

Foreman-Mackey et al. 2013
to estimate auto-correlation times
\tau
for four relevant quantities.
These are the magnitude of the Maldacena loop
|M|
, the lowest eigenvalue of
D^{{\dagger}}D
, the Ward identity Eq. (
13
) that involves the
\eta\psi
fermion bilinear, and
\text{Tr}\left[X^{2}\right]
.
The resulting auto-correlation times confirm a posteriori that a minimum of 5
\tau
, and typically well over 10
\tau
, are set aside for thermalization.
We divide our thermalized measurements into blocks for jackknife analyses, with block sizes larger than
\tau
, and at least 30 MDTU.
Our approach ensures that at least 27 such statistically independent blocks are available for each ensemble, more than enough for robust analyses.
This information is summarized in Tables
2
–
8
, which are extracted from the open data release Ref.
Dhindsa et al. 2023
.
That data release also provides a great deal of additional information including extremal eigenvalues of
D^{{\dagger}}D
(which must remain within the spectral range where the rational approximation used in the RHMC algorithm is reliable), and other observables computed in addition to the spatial Wilson line and Polyakov loop discussed above.

Anomalous function of a Lorentz-violating QED effective action and the relation between compact bulk scalar propagator and path integral duality Preprint: APS/123-QED
Huangcheng Yin

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a compact five dimensional spacetime with the structure ℳ 1 , 3 × S 1 \mathcal{M}^{1,3}\times S^{1} . Generally speaking, motion on such a structure will break Lorentz invariance, allowing for causal bulk signals to propagate superluminally. Based on recent articles, we calculate the anomalous function of a gauge invariant but Lorentz-violating term in the 4 ​ D 4D QED effective action by using path integral. Finally, we find that the compact bulk scalar propagator and path integral duality are consistent, this result brings a new perspective: the behavior of breaking Lorentz invariance caused by dimensional compactness can be seen as path integral duality.

Usually, the approach to QFT is based on the canonical formalism,

Functional quantization, also known as the path integral

Quantum anomaly is the phenomenon that some symmetries of

Therefore, it is worth to quantum anomalies

II.2

In a general

This equation nicely illustrate the relation between the compactifiaction of

Switching from a sum over windings to a sum over Kaluza-Klein momentum using the Poisson resummation identity

III
Anomalous function of a 4-dimensional Lorentz-violating QED effective action

III.1
General considerations

Before we calculate the anomaly term of the Lagrangian (
17
), let’s do some preparation. Without loss of generality, consider a set of fermion fields
\psi_{n}(x)
, which we encapsulate into a multiplet denoted
\bm{\psi}(x)
. Consider now the following transformation of the fermion fields:

\bm{\psi}(x)\to U(x)\bm{\psi}(x)

(20)

The Hermitic conjugate of
\bm{\psi}(x)
transforms as
\bm{\psi}^{\dagger}(x)\to\bm{\psi}(x)U^{\dagger}(x)
, so that we have

\overline{\bm{\psi}}(x)=\bm{\psi}^{\dagger}\gamma^{0}\to\bm{\psi}^{\dagger}(x)U^{\dagger}(x)\gamma^{0}=\overline{\bm{\psi}}(x)\gamma^{0}U^{\dagger}(x)\gamma^{0}.

(21)

The measure is transformed with the inverse of the determinant of the transformation,

\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}]\to\frac{1}{det(U)det(\overline{U})}\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}],

(22)

where the matrices
U
and
\overline{U}
carry both indices for the fermion species and space-time indices:

U_{xm,yn}=U_{m,n}(x)\delta(x-y),

(23)

\overline{U}_{xm,yn}=\left(\gamma^{0}U^{\dagger}(x)\gamma^{0}\right)_{m,n}\delta(x-y).

(24)

Split the spinor into right-handed and left-handed projections,

\bm{\psi}_{R}=\left(\frac{1+\gamma^{5}}{2}\right)\bm{\psi},\quad\bm{\psi}_{L}=\left(\frac{1-\gamma^{5}}{2}\right)\bm{\psi},

(25)

and consider a chiral transformation

U(x)=e^{i\alpha(x)\gamma^{5}t},

(26)

where
t
is a Hermitian matrix that does not contain Dirac matrices and
\alpha(x)
is pesudofunction. And

\gamma^{0}U^{\dagger}(x)\gamma^{0}=\gamma^{0}e^{-i\alpha(x)\gamma^{5}t}\gamma^{0}=e^{i\alpha(x)\gamma^{5}t}=U(x).

(27)

Thus,
\overline{U}=U
, and
detU=det\overline{U}
. To calculate anomaly function we need to let
detU\neq 1
thus the measure is not invariant and transforms according to

\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}]\to\frac{1}{(detU)^{2}}\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}].

(28)

And

\frac{1}{(detU)^{2}}=exp\left(-2\text{Tr}lnU\right)=exp\left(i\int d^{4}x\alpha(x)\mathcal{A}(x)\right),

(29)

where
\mathcal{A}(x)
is called the anomaly function and
\mathcal{A}(x)=-2\delta(x-x)tr(\gamma^{5}t)
. In terms of this function, the measure transforms as

\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}]\to e^{i\int d^{4}x\alpha(x)\mathcal{A}(x)}\mathcal{D}[\bm{\psi}]\mathcal{D}[\overline{\bm{\psi}}].

(30)

This measure can be absorbed into a redefinition of Langrangian,

\mathcal{L}(x)\to\mathcal{L}(x)+i\alpha(x)\mathcal{A}(x).

(31)

III.2
Calculation of
\mathcal{A}(x)

Manipulate finite expressions, we must regularize the delta function. This can be done by

\mathcal{A}(x)=-2\lim_{y\to x,M\to+\infty}\text{Tr}\left\{\gamma^{5}t\mathcal{F}\left(-\frac{(c_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\mu}D^{\mu}_{x})^{2}}{M^{2}}\right)\right\}\delta(x-y),

(32)

where

and

\mathcal{F}(0)=1,\quad\mathcal{F}(+\infty)=0,\quad s\mathcal{F}^{\prime}(s)=0\quad at\quad s=0,+\infty.

(33)

Then, we replace the delta function by its Fourier representation,

\delta(x-y)=\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}e^{ik\cdot(x-y)},

(34)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x)

(35)

\lim_{y\to x}\mathcal{F}(\partial_{x})e^{ik(x-y)}=\mathcal{F}(ik+\partial_{x}).

Redefining the integration variable,
k\to Mk

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(x)=-2M^{4}\int\frac{d^{4}k}{(2\pi)^{4}}\lim_{M\to+\infty}\times\text{Tr}\left\{\gamma^{5}t\mathcal{F}\left(B\right)\right\},

(37)

B=c_{\mu\nu}c_{\sigma}^{\mu}k^{\nu}k^{\sigma}-2i\frac{c_{\mu\nu}c_{\sigma}^{\mu}k^{\nu}D_{x}^{\sigma}}{M}-\frac{(c_{\mu\nu}\gamma^{\mu}D_{x}^{\nu})^{2}}{M^{2}}

(38)

where we have used the anticommutation relation

Expand the function

in powers of

term

Define

and

IV
Relation between compact bulk scalar propagator and Path Integral duality

Let’s go back to (
13

\eta^{new}_{MN}=

(-,+,+,+,+)

G^{(d+1)}_{R}=\frac{i}{2\pi R}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\frac{e^{-ik\cdot x}}{k^{2}+(n/R)^{2}+\mu^{2}}.

(44)

Let’s deal this equation by constituting the Schwinger’s proper time version of the propagator,

\displaystyle G^{(d+1)}_{R}=\frac{i}{2\pi R}\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}\int

\displaystyle\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}e^{-ik\cdot x}\int^{\infty}_{0}ds

(45)

\displaystyle\times e^{-s\left(k^{2}+(n/R)^{2}+\mu^{2}\right)}.

The integral of

(46)

For a clearer view, it is advisable to discretize the integral of
s

\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\to\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}}

G^{(d+1)}_{R}=\frac{i}{2\pi R}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z},s\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}exp\left\{-\frac{x^{2}}{s}-s\left[(n/R)^{2}+\mu^{2}\right]\right\}.

(47)

when

n=1

\frac{1}{R}=L_{0}

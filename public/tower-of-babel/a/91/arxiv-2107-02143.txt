E ​ 1 E1 and M1 radiative transitions involving heavy-light axial, pseudoscalar and vector quarkonia in the framework of Bethe-Salpeter equation
Vaishali Guleria, Eshete Gebrehana, Shashank Bhatnagar

Abstract
This work is an extension of our previous work in [ 26 ] to calculate M1 transitions, 0 − ⁣ + → 1 − ⁣ − ​ γ 0^{-+}\rightarrow 1^{--}\gamma , and E1 transitions involving axial vector mesons such as, 1 + ⁣ − → 0 − ⁣ + ​ γ 1^{+-}\rightarrow 0^{-+}\gamma , and 0 − ⁣ + → 1 + ⁣ − ​ γ 0^{-+}\rightarrow 1^{+-}\gamma for which very little data is available as of now. We make use of the general structure of the transition amplitude, M f ​ i M_{fi} derived in our previous work [ 26 ] as a linear superposition of terms involving all possible combinations of + ⁣ + ++ , and − ⁣ − -- components of Salpeter wave functions of final and initial hadrons. In the present work, we make use of leading Dirac structures in the hadronic Bethe-Salpeter wave functions of the involved hadrons, which makes the formulation more rigorous. We evaluate the decay widths for both the above mentioned M ​ 1 M1 and E ​ 1 E1 transitions. We have used algebraic forms of Salpeter wave functions obtained through analytic solutions of mass spectral equations for ground and excited states of 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} , 0 − ⁣ + 0^{-+} and 1 + ⁣ − 1^{+-} heavy-light quarkonia in approximate harmonic oscillator basis to do analytic calculations of their decay widths. We have compared our results with experimental data, where ever available, and other models.

2.
Radiative decay process,
H->H^{\prime}+\gamma
in
4\times 4
BSE under Covariant Instantaneous Ansatz

The Bethe-Salpeter equation that describes the quark-anti quark bound state of momenta
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, relative
momentum
q
, and meson momentum
P
is

S_{F}^{-1}(p_{1})\Psi(P,q)S_{F}^{-1}(-p_{2})=i\int\frac{d^{4}q^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{4}}K(q,q^{\prime\prime})\Psi(P,q^{\prime\prime}),

(1)

where
K(q,q^{\prime\prime})
is the interaction kernel, and
S_{F}^{-1}(\pm p_{1,2})=\pm i{\not}p_{1,2}+m_{1,2}
are the quark and antiquark propagators.
We now make use of the Covariant Instantaneous Ansatz (which is a Lorentz-covariant 3D support), where,

K(q,q^{\prime\prime})=K(\widehat{q},\widehat{q}^{\prime\prime})
on the BS kernel, where the BS kernel depends entirely on the variable,

\widehat{q}_{\mu}=q_{\mu}-\frac{q.P}{P^{2}}P_{\mu}
is the component of
internal momentum of the hadron that is orthogonal to the total
hadron momentum, i.e.
\widehat{q}.P=0
, while
\sigma P_{\mu}=\frac{q.P}{P^{2}}P_{\mu}
is the component of
q
longitudinal
to
P
, where the 4-dimensional volume element is,

d^{4}q=d^{3}\widehat{q}Md\sigma
. Now working on the right side of Eq.(1), and making use of the fact that

\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime\prime})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int Md\sigma^{\prime\prime}\Psi(P,q^{\prime\prime}),

(2)

and the fact that the longitudinal component of
Md\sigma^{\prime\prime}
of
q^{\prime\prime}
does not appear in
K(\hat{q},\hat{q}^{\prime\prime})
, carrying out integration over
Md\sigma
on right side of Eq.(1), we obtain,

S_{F}^{-1}(p_{1})\Psi(P,q)S_{F}^{-1}(-p_{2})=\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}K(\hat{q},\hat{q}^{\prime\prime})\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime\prime})=\Gamma(\hat{q}),

(3)

where,
\Gamma(\hat{q})
is the hadron-quark vertex function, and is directly related to the 4D wave function,
\Psi(P,q)
, and one can express the 4D BS wave function
\Psi(P,q)
in terms of
\Gamma(\hat{q})
as,

\Psi(P,q)=S_{1}(p_{1})\Gamma(\hat{q})S_{2}(-p_{2}),

(4)

Further the 4D hadron-quark vertex, that enters into the definition of the 4D BS wave function in the previous equation, can be identified as,

\Gamma(\hat{q})=\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}^{\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}K(\hat{q},\hat{q}^{\prime\prime})\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime\prime}).

(5)

Following a sequence of steps outlined in
[
30
]
, we get four Salpeter equations which are
effective 3D forms of BSE (Salpeter equations) given below:

\displaystyle(M-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})\psi^{++}(\hat{q})=\Lambda_{1}^{+}(\hat{q})\Gamma(\hat{q})\Lambda_{2}^{+}(\hat{q})

\displaystyle(M+\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\psi^{--}(\hat{q})=-\Lambda_{1}^{-}(\hat{q})\Gamma(\hat{q})\Lambda_{2}^{-}(\hat{q})

\displaystyle\psi^{+-}(\hat{q})=0.

\displaystyle\psi^{-+}(\hat{q})=0

(6)

Thus, in our framework, a crucial role is played by the component,
\hat{q}_{\mu}
, which is always orthogonal to
P_{\mu}
and satisfies the unconstrained relation,
\hat{q}.P=0
, regardless of whether
q.P=0
(i.e.
\sigma=0
), or
q.P\neq 0
(i.e.
\sigma\neq 0
).

The Lorentz-invariant nature of
\hat{q}^{2}
increases the applicability of this framework of Covariant Instantaneous Ansatz all the way from low energy spectra to high energy transition amplitudes. For details, see
[
26
,
33
]
.

The 3D B.S. wave function can be expressed in terms of the projected wave functions as

\psi(\hat{q})=\psi^{++}(\hat{q})+\psi^{+-}(\hat{q})+\psi^{-+}(\hat{q})+\psi^{--}(\hat{q}),

(7)

where

\psi^{\pm\pm}(\hat{q})=\Lambda^{\pm}_{1}(\hat{q})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\psi(\hat{q})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\Lambda^{\pm}_{2}(\hat{q})

(8)

and the projection operators

\Lambda^{\pm}_{j}(\hat{q})=\frac{1}{2\omega_{j}}\bigg[\frac{{\not}P}{M}\omega_{j}\pm J(j)(im_{j}+{\not}\hat{q})\bigg],~~~J(j)=(-1)^{j+1},~~j=1,2

(9)

with the relation

\omega^{2}_{j}=m_{j}^{2}+\hat{q}^{2}

(10)

In radiative transitions involving single photon decays, such as
H\rightarrow H^{\prime}+\gamma
, the

4\times 4
representation of BSE. The single photon decay of

quarkonia is described by the direct and exchange Feynman diagrams as in Figure

P^{\prime}
, and

the corresponding variables of the final hadron. And let

be momentum and polarization vectors of emitted photon, while
\epsilon^{\lambda}
is the polarization vector of final emitted meson. Thus if

, and

are the momenta of the two quarks in initial and final hadron respectively, then, we have, the momentum relations:

\displaystyle P=p_{1}+p_{2};p_{1,2}=\hat{m}_{1,2}P\pm q

\displaystyle P^{\prime}=p^{\prime}_{1}+p^{\prime}_{2};p^{\prime}_{1,2}=\hat{m}_{1,2}P^{\prime}\pm q^{\prime}

(11)

for initial and final hadrons respectively. From the Feynman diagrams we see that conservation of momentum demands that,
P=P^{\prime}+k
. Now, for the first diagram, we have the kinematical relations,
p_{1}=p^{\prime}_{1}+k
, and
-p_{2}=-p^{\prime}_{2}
, where
k=P-P^{\prime}
is the momentum of the emitted photon. And for the second diagram, we have the corresponding relations,
p_{1}=p^{\prime}_{1}
, and
-p^{\prime}_{2}=-p_{2}+k
.

Making use of the above equations, the relationship between the internal momenta,
q
, and
q^{\prime}
of the initial and final hadrons in terms of the photon momentum,
k
can be expressed as,

\displaystyle q^{\prime}=q-\hat{m}_{2}k,

\displaystyle q^{\prime}=q+\hat{m}_{1}k,

(12)

where, the first equation is for Diagram 1, and second equation is for Diagram 2. Here,
\hat{m}_{1,2}=\frac{1}{2}[1\pm\frac{(m^{2}_{1}-m^{2}_{2})}{M^{2}}]
are the Wightman-Garding definitions
[
33
]
of masses of individual quarks, which act like momentum partitioning functions for the two quarks in a hadron.

For initial hadron, its internal momentum has already been decomposed as,
q=(\hat{q},iM\sigma)
, where
\hat{q}
, and
M\sigma
are defined relative to its external momentum,
P
. Similarly for final meson, we again decompose its internal momentum,
q^{\prime}
into two components
q^{\prime}=(\hat{q}^{\prime},iM\sigma^{\prime})
, with
\hat{q}^{\prime}=q^{\prime}-\sigma^{\prime}P
transverse to initial hadron momentum,
P
, and
\sigma^{\prime}=\frac{q^{\prime}.P}{P^{2}}
, longitudinal to
P
. Thus,
P.\hat{q}^{\prime}=0
. The relationship between the transverse components of internal momenta of the two hadrons,
\hat{q}
, and
\hat{q}^{\prime}
is
[
26
]
,

\displaystyle\hat{q}^{\prime}=\hat{q}+\hat{m}_{2}\hat{P}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\hat{q}^{\prime}=\hat{q}-\hat{m}_{1}\hat{P}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\hat{P}^{\prime}=P^{\prime}-\frac{P^{\prime}.P}{P^{2}}P,

(13)

where, the first equation of Eq.(13), holds for Diagram 1, and the second equation holds for Diagram 2, and
\hat{P}^{\prime}
is the component of total momentum
P^{\prime}
of final hadron transverse to initial hadron momentum,
P
. Here,
\hat{q}^{\prime}.P=0
due to
\hat{P^{\prime}}.P=0
. Now, the kinematics gets simplified in the rest frame of the initial meson, where we have
P=(\overrightarrow{0},iM)
, while for emitted meson,
P^{\prime}=(\overrightarrow{P}^{\prime},iE^{\prime})
, where
E^{\prime}=\sqrt{\overrightarrow{P}^{\prime 2}+M^{\prime 2}}
, and since the photon momentum can be decomposed as,
k=(\overrightarrow{k},i|\overrightarrow{k}|)
, where
\overrightarrow{k}=-\overrightarrow{P}^{\prime}
, since final meson and photon would be emitted in opposite directions. Hence we get,
|\overrightarrow{P}^{\prime}|=|\overrightarrow{k}|=\frac{M^{2}-M^{\prime 2}}{2M}
. Thus the energy of the emitted meson can be expressed as,
E^{\prime}=\frac{M^{2}+M^{\prime 2}}{2M}
.

The dot products of momenta of the initial and the emitted meson can be expressed as
[
26
]
,

P^{\prime}.P=-ME^{\prime}=-\frac{M^{2}+M^{\prime 2}}{2}

(14)

Thus, it can be seen that,
-E^{\prime}
acts as the projection of
P^{\prime}
along the direction of initial hadron momentum,
P
. Similarly, the relationship between the longitudinal components of internal momenta of the two hadrons in the two diagrams can be worked out as
[
26
]
,

\displaystyle\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma+\alpha;

\displaystyle\alpha=\hat{m}_{2}\frac{M^{\prime 2}-M^{2}}{2M^{2}}(\Rightarrow Diagram1)

\displaystyle\alpha=-\hat{m}_{1}\frac{M^{\prime 2}-M^{2}}{2M^{2}}(\Rightarrow Diagram2),

(15)

which is again a consequence of the transversality of
\hat{q}^{\prime}
with initial hadron momentum,
P
. Thus, up to Eq.(
2.
), the kinematics is the same for all the three processes (
P\rightarrow V\gamma
,
A^{-}\rightarrow P\gamma
, and

) studied in this work.

M1 Radiative decays,

In this section we study the M1 radiative decay process,

V\rightarrow P\gamma
in our previous work
[
26
]

\gamma_{5}

for V meson) used in our previous work
[
26
]

\Psi_{P}(P,q)=S_{F}(p_{1})\Gamma_{P}(\hat{q})S_{F}(-p_{2})
. Since for transition amplitude calculation, we choose to do calculation in the rest frame of the initial pseudoscalar meson, we write the wave function of the emitted vector meson as,

\Psi_{V}(P^{\prime},q^{\prime})=S_{F}(p^{\prime}_{1})\Gamma_{V}(\hat{q}^{\prime})S_{F}(-p^{\prime}_{2}),

(16)

where as defined earlier,

P

\Gamma_{V}(\hat{q}^{\prime})

\Gamma_{V}(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}^{\prime\prime\prime}}{(2\pi)^{3}}K(\hat{q}^{\prime},\hat{q}^{\prime\prime\prime})\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime\prime\prime}).

(17)

Similarly for final meson, the expression for the 3D BS wave function,
\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})
is expressed in terms of the projection operators as,

\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\psi^{++}(\hat{q}^{\prime})+\psi^{+-}(\hat{q}^{\prime})+\psi^{-+}(\hat{q}^{\prime})+\psi^{--}(\hat{q}^{\prime})

(18)

where

\displaystyle\psi^{\pm\pm}(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\Lambda^{\pm}_{1}(\hat{q}^{\prime})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\psi(\hat{q}^{\prime})\frac{{\not}P}{M}\Lambda^{\pm}_{2}(\hat{q}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Lambda^{\pm}_{j}(\hat{q}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{2\omega^{\prime}_{j}}\bigg[\frac{{\not}P}{M}\omega^{\prime}_{j}\pm J(j)(im_{j}+{\not}\hat{q}^{\prime})\bigg],

(19)

with the relation
\omega^{\prime 2}_{1,2}=m_{1,2}^{2}+\hat{q}^{\prime 2}
.

In Eq.(12), due to relationship between
q^{\prime}
, and

Here, the first term corresponds to the first diagram, where the photon is emitted from the quark, while the second term corresponds to the second diagram where the photon is emitted from the antiquark, and

, and

We can express Eq.(20) as,

\displaystyle M_{fi}=-e_{q}\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{iMd\sigma}{(2\pi)}Tr[\bar{\Gamma}_{V}(\hat{q}^{\prime})S_{F}(p^{\prime}_{1}){\not}\epsilon^{\prime}S_{F}(p_{1})\Gamma_{P}(\hat{q})S_{F}(-p_{2})]

\displaystyle-e_{Q}\int\frac{d^{3}\hat{q}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\int\frac{iMd\sigma}{(2\pi)}Tr[\bar{\Gamma}_{V}(\hat{q}^{\prime})S_{F}(p_{1})\Gamma_{P}(\hat{q})S_{F}(-p_{2}){\not}\epsilon^{\prime}S_{F}(-p^{\prime}_{2})]

(21)

Now, we reduce the above equation to the effective 3D form by integrating over the longitudinal component,
Md\sigma
over the poles of the propagators,

, that are expressed as
[
26
]

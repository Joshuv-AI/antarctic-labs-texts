Abstract
. This paper is devoted to establishing global W 2 , p W^{2,p} estimate for strong solutions to the Dirichlet problem of uniformly elliptic equations in the non-divergence form where the domain is a Lipschitz polyhedra.



2010 Mathematics Subject Classification
35B65, 35D35, 35J25

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with Lipschitz boundary and
f\in L^{p}(\Omega)
with some
1<p<\infty
. Assume that

\displaystyle A\in[C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})]^{3\times 3};\ \text{and}\ \lambda_{0}I_{3}\leq A({\boldsymbol{x}})\leq\lambda_{1}I_{3},\ \forall{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega

(1.1)

for two uniform positive constants
0<\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}<\infty
. Here
I_{3}
denotes the
3\times 3
identity matrix.

In this work, we mainly consider the Dirichlet problem for the elliptic equations with non-divergence structure:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A:D^{2}u=f&\text{in}\ \Omega\\
\\
u=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(1.2)

Here for any
{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\Omega
,
A({\boldsymbol{x}}):D^{2}u({\boldsymbol{x}})
denotes the trace of the matrix
A({\boldsymbol{x}})D^{2}u({\boldsymbol{x}})
.

For global
W^{2,p}
estimate for strong solutions to (
1.2
), it is one of key problems in the regularity theory for elliptic equations and has attracted much attention from mathematicians since 1950s. It is well known that when
\Omega\in C^{1,1}
, then for any
1<p<\infty
, (
1.2
) admits a unique strong solution
u\in W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_{0}
( see chapter 9 of Gilbarg-Trudinger
[
4
]
) with the estimate

\displaystyle\|u\|_{2,p}\leq C\|f\|_{p}

(1.3)

As for the boundary with less regularity, there are few results for global regularity. Recently, Li
[
7
]
obtained
W^{2,p}
estimate for strong solutions with
C^{1,\alpha}
boundary for
p
depending on
\alpha
.

In our work, we get global
W^{2,p}
estimate for strong solutions to (
1.2
) with
p
in possible ranges under weak regularity assumptions for
\partial\Omega
. More precisely, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
A
satisfy
(\ref{assump_1})
. Then the following conclusions hold:

{(1)}
For any
p\in[\frac{6}{5},\frac{4}{3})
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\left(\|A:D^{2}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right),\qquad\forall u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(1.4)

{(2)}
Assume
\Omega
is also convex, then
(\ref{main_estimate1})
holds true for any
p
with
\frac{6}{5}\leq p\leq 2
.

In fact, when
\Omega
be a convex polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, we can obtain a much stronger estimate:

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a convex polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
A
satisfy
(\ref{assump_1})
. Then For any
p\in(\frac{3}{2},2]
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\|A:D^{2}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(1.5)

The following part is organized as follows: Section 2 is devoted to giving some known results; in Section 3, we would like to establish the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
.

2.
preliminary results

In this part we state some known results, which are important to prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
. As for the proofs for these known results, we would like to refer the interested readers to
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
.

The first one is global
W^{2,p}
estimate for Poisson equation given by Dauge
[
2
]
:

Theorem 2.1
.

(Corollary 3.10, 3.12 of
[
2
]
)

Assume
\Omega
is a Lipschitz polyhedral domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i) for any
\frac{6}{5}\leq p<\frac{4}{3}
, there exists a positive constant
C_{p}
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{p}\|\Delta u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall u\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega).

(2.1)

(ii) if
\Omega
is also convex, then (
2.1
) holds for any
\frac{6}{5}<p\leq 2
.

Remark 2.2
.

It is worth noticing that by Dahlberg’s result ( see
[
5
]
), for any
p>1
, there exists some Lipschitz domain
\Omega
and
f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
such that the solution
u
to

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u=f&\text{in}\ \Omega\\
\\
u=0&\text{on}\ \partial\Omega\end{array}\right.

(2.2)

does not belong to
W^{2,p}(\Omega)
. Hence the estimate (
2.1
) usually does not hold for general Lipchitz domain.

Another important tool is the so-called Stein extension Theorem. Before stating the Stein extension Theorem, we would like to give some preliminary knowledge.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Theorem 2 of Chapter VI in
[
8
]
)

Let
F
be any closed set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and
\delta(x)
be the distance of
x
from
F.
Then there exists a function
\Delta(x)
defined in
F^{c}
such that

(a)\ c_{1}\delta(x)\leq\Delta(x)\leq c_{2}\delta(x),\ \ \forall\ x\in F^{c}

(b)

\Delta(x)
is
C^{\infty}
in
F^{c}
and

|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial x^{\alpha}}\Delta(x)|\leq B_{\alpha}(\delta(x))^{1-|\alpha|}

where
B_{\alpha}
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are independent of
F
. Here
\Delta(x)
is called the regularized distance from
F
.

Lemma 2.4
.

(Lemma 2 of Section 3 of Chapter VI in
[
8
]
)
Suppose
D=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y>\phi(x)\}
, where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function satisfying:
|\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq M|x-x^{\prime}|
, for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there exists a constant
c>0
,which depends only on the Lipschitz bound of
D
, so that if
(x,y)\in F^{c}
, then
c\Delta(x,y)\geq\phi(x)-y
.

Let

\delta^{*}=2c\Delta.

Then by Lemma
2.4
, we see that
\delta^{*}(x,y)\geq\phi(x)-y
, if
(x,y)\in F^{c}
. Then we can state the Stein extension theorem as follows:

Theorem 2.5
.

( Theorem
5^{\prime}
in Chapter
VI
in
[
8
]
)

Let
D=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}:y>\phi(x)\}
, where
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function satisfying:
|\phi(x)-\phi(x^{\prime})|\leq M|x-x^{\prime}|
, for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 1
.
Then there exists a bounded linear extension operator
\mathfrak{E}:W^{k,p}(D)\rightarrow W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, and any nonnegative integer
k
. More precisely, for any
f\in W^{k,p}(D)\cap C^{\infty}(D)
,
\mathfrak{E}(f)(x,y)=f(x,y)
, if
(x,y)\in\overline{D}
and

\mathfrak{E}(f)(x,y)=\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)f(x,y+\lambda\delta^{*}(x,y))d\lambda,\ \ \forall\ (x,y)\in{\overline{D}}^{c}.

Here
\psi
is a continuous function on
[1,\infty)
satisfying:

\psi(\lambda)=O(\lambda^{-N})\text{as $\lambda\rightarrow\infty$ for any $N$},

\int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)d\lambda=1\ \text{ and }\ \int_{1}^{\infty}\psi(\lambda)\lambda^{k}d\lambda=0\ \text{ for any $k=1,2,\cdots$.}

Firstly we turn to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which consists of several steps. In the beginning, we choose
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}\in\partial\Omega

Since
\Omega
is a Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
,
there exists an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}
of
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}

in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and new orthogonal coordinates

(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
such that in the new coordinates,

{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}=(0,0,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}

can be represented by

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)+t{\boldsymbol{v}}:(x_{1},x_{2})\in[-R,R]^{2},-H<t<H\},

(3.1)

with the vector
{\boldsymbol{v}}=(0,0,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, and

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\Omega=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)+t{\boldsymbol{v}}:(x_{1},x_{2})\in[-R,R]^{2},t<\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\partial\Omega=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)+t{\boldsymbol{v}}:(x_{1},x_{2})\in[-R,R]^{2},t=\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\overline{\Omega}^{c}=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\{(x_{1},x_{2},0)+t{\boldsymbol{v}}:(x_{1},x_{2})\in[-R,R]^{2},t>\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})\},

for some Lipschitz function
\zeta:[-R,R]^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\zeta(0,0)=0,\text{ and }|\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq 0.05H\quad\forall(x_{1},x_{2})\in[-R,R]^{2}.

(3.2)

Without losing of generality, we assume

. According to

\displaystyle D=\{{\boldsymbol{x}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x_{3}<\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})\}

where
L

.

For any
v\in W^{2,p}({\mathcal{C}}_{0.9R,0.9H}\cap\Omega)

v
can be extended to

C_{1}

k=0,1,2

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{k,p}({\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma R,\sigma H}\cap\overline{\Omega}^{c})}\leq C_{1}\|v\|_{W^{k,p}({\mathcal{C}}_{\sigma R,0.2H}\cap\Omega)}.

(3.5)

Proof.

Obviously,
{\mathcal{C}}_{0.9R,0.9H}\cap\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Thus by Theorem
2.5
,

can be extended to

(see (

and for any

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{k,p}(D)}\leq\tilde{C}_{1}\|v\|_{W^{k,p}({\mathcal{C}}_{0.9R,0.9H}\cap\Omega)}.

Let

\overline{\eta}(x_{3})=1

By (

\displaystyle-0.1H<\zeta(x_{1},x_{2})\text{ and }w({\boldsymbol{x}})=v({\boldsymbol{x}}),\quad\forall-0.1H\leq x_{3}\leq\zeta(x_{1},x_{2}).

(3.6)

In addition, for any

We extend

The following proposition can be viewed a extension of Theorem
2.1
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
be a Lipschitz polyhedra. Then

(1)
for any
6/5\leq p<4/3
, there exits a positive constant
\delta>0
such that if
2R^{2}+H^{2}\leq\delta^{2}\leq 1
, there holds:

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq 2\tilde{C}_{p}\|A:D^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\quad

(3.12)

for any
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
with

\mathrm{supp}v\subset\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\Omega}
.

(2)
if
\Omega
is also convex, then the conclusion of
(1)
still holds for any
6/5\leq p\leq 2
.

Proof.

We denote by
L_{0}
the constant coefficient operator given by

\displaystyle L_{0}u=A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})D^{2}u.

Since
A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})
is symmetric and positive definite, there is an
invertible matrix
B\in\mathbb{R}^{3\times 3}
such that

\displaystyle B^{\top}A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})B=I_{3}.

We define the affine mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
as

\displaystyle{\boldsymbol{x}}=G(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}):=B^{-1}\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}+{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0},\qquad\forall\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

We denote by
\widehat{\Omega}
the preimage of
\Omega
via the above
affine mapping
G
. For any
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)
, we define
\widehat{v}(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}):=v(G(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}))
for any
\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\widehat{\Omega}
.
It’s easy to see that

\displaystyle A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}):D^{2}u(G({\boldsymbol{x}}))=I_{d}:D_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}^{2}\widehat{u}(\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}),\qquad\forall\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}\in\widehat{\Omega}.

According to Theorem
2.1
, for any
p
satisfying: either (1)
\frac{6}{5}\leq p<\frac{4}{3}
, or (2)
\frac{6}{5}\leq p\leq 2
when
\Omega
is also convex,
there is a positive constant
\widehat{C}_{p}
depending only on
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
,
\Omega

and
p
such that

\displaystyle\|D_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}^{2}\widehat{v}\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{\Omega})}+\|D_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}\widehat{v}\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{\Omega})}+\|\widehat{v}\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{\Omega})}\leq\widehat{C}_{p}\|I_{3}:D_{\widehat{{\boldsymbol{x}}}}^{2}\widehat{v}\|_{L^{p}(\widehat{\Omega})}.

Therefore, there is
\tilde{C}_{p}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}_{p}\|L_{0}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)},\qquad\forall v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega)

(3.13)

where the constant
\tilde{C}_{p}
depends only on
{\boldsymbol{x}}_{0}
,
\Omega
and
p
.
Consequently, if
\text{supp}v\subset\overline{{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\Omega}
,
there holds

\displaystyle L_{0}v=(A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})-A):D^{2}v+A:D^{2}v,

and by (
3.13
), it infers

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{C}_{p}\left(\sup_{{\mathcal{C}}_{R,H}\cap\Omega}|A-A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})|\|D^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|A:D^{2}v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\right).

Since
A\in[C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})]^{3\times 3}
, there exists
a positive number
0<\delta\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle|A({\boldsymbol{x}})-A({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})|\leq\dfrac{1}{2(\tilde{C}_{p}+1)},\qquad\forall{\boldsymbol{x}}\in B_{\delta}({\boldsymbol{x}}_{0})\cap\Omega,

and hence

Finally, we would like to conclude the proof of Corollary

Proof of Corollary
1.2
: We argue by contradiction. For simplicity, we write
Lu=A:D^{2}u
. If the conclusion does not hold true, then there must exist a sequence
\{v_{m}\}\subset W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
\|v_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
for any
m
and
\|Lv_{m}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
. From Theorem
1.1
,
\{\|v_{m}\|_{W^{2,p}(\Omega)}\}
is uniformly bounded. Then by compact embedding of
W_{0}^{1,p}\rightarrow L^{p}
, there is a subsequence, still denoted by
\{v_{m}\}
, converging weakly to
v\in W^{2,p}(\Omega)\cap W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
with

\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ Lv=0.

Since
p>3/2
, we see that

by Sobolev embedding theorem. Then by interior
W^{2,p}
estimate (Theorem 9.11 in
[
4
]
), we obtain

. Hence by Alexandrov maximum principle and the fact:

,
v=0
in
\Omega
, which contradicts with the condition:
\|v\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=1
.

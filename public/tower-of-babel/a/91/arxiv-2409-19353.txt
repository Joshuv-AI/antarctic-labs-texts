Uniform estimates of Green functions and Sobolev-type inequalities on real and complex manifolds
Fusheng Deng, Gang Huang, Xiangsen Qin

Abstract
. We prove certain L p L^{p} Sobolev-type and Poincaré-type inequalities for functions on real and complex manifolds for the gradient operator ∇ \nabla , the Laplace operator Δ \Delta , and the operator ∂ ¯ \bar{\partial} . Integral representations for functions are key to get such inequalities. The proofs of the main results involves certain uniform estimates for the Green functions and their gradients on Riemannian manifolds, which are also established in the present work.



1.
Introduction

This article is a continuation and extension of
[
6
]
.
That paper considers the
\bar{\partial}
-Poincaré inequalities
in an open subset of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. In the present work, we consider some
L^{p}
Sobolev-type and Poincaré-type inequalities on manifolds
for the gradient operator
\nabla
, Laplace operator
\Delta
or operator
\bar{\partial}
.
As observed in
[
6
]
, we can use integral representations for functions as the key tool to get such inequalities.
In order to make such an idea work, we need certain uniform estimates for Green functions of the Laplace operator and their gradients on Riemannian manifolds.
This is established in the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\overline{M}=M\cup\partial M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary (of dimension
n\geq 2
), and let
G
be the Green function for the Laplace operator on
M
.
Then
G
has the following uniform estimate:

(i)

If
n\geq 3
, there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},g)>0
such that

|G(x,y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{2-n},

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{1-n},

for all
(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag
.

(ii)

If
n=2
, there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},g)>0
such that

|G(x,y)|\leq C(1+|\ln d(x,y)|),

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq C(1+|\ln d(x,y)|)\frac{1}{d(x,y)},

for all
(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag
.

Please see Subsection
2.1
for various notations and conventions that will be used in this article.
When we say
(M,g)
(resp.
(\overline{M},g)
) is a Riemannian manifold, we mean it is a connected oriented smooth manifold without boundary (resp. with smooth boundary)
and it is equipped with a smooth Riemannian metric (resp. a Riemannian metric which is smooth up to the boundary).
We make such smoothness convention just for convenience, and many results of this paper also hold under weaker regularity assumption,
but we don’t pursue this here. Moreover, we assume all manifolds are of real dimension
n\geq 2
.

Some weaker forms of Theorem
1.1
are already known.
It is well known for bounded domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
, see
[
11
, Theorem 3.3]
for example..
Similar result is stated in
[
2
, Theorem 4.17]
, but with the constant
C
depending on the distance from
x
to the boundary.
It seems that the above uniform estimates cannot be simply obtained through a minor modification of the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 4.17]
.

Following the basic insight in
[
6
]
, we can derive from Theorem
1.1
certain
L^{p}
Sobolev-type inequalities for the gradient operator and the Laplace operator.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(n-p)<np,\ q(n-1)<nr.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\overline{M},g,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(\overline{M}).

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
(\overline{M},g)
is a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary. Let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(n-2p)<np,\ q(n-1)<nr,

and if
n=2
, we moreover require
p>1
if
q>1
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\overline{M},g,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{L^{p}(M)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial M)}

for all
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})\cap C^{2}(M)
such that
\Delta f
has a continuous continuation to the boundary.

Clearly, we can take
1\leq p=q=r<\infty
in Theorem
1.2

and Theorem
1.3
. If
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open subset and
p<n
,
then Theorem
1.2
can be seen as a generalization of
[
20
, Theorem 1.2]
(see also
[
5
, Formula (2.1)]
)
if we overlook the estimates for the constants in the inequalities,
and it is also a generalization of
[
6
, Corollary 1.5]
.
Theorem
1.3
still hold when
p=q=r=\infty
and
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open subset, see Theorem 6.2 of
[
4
, Chapter 2]
.
But we don’t know whether Theorem
1.2
holds or not if
p=q=r=\infty
.

We have the following consequence from Theorem
1.3
for harmonic functions.

Corollary 1.4
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, and let
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(n-1)<np.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\overline{M},g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial M)}

for any harmonic function
f
on
M
with
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
.

When
p=q=2
, Corollary
1.4
follows from
[
24
, Proposition 1.8, Chapter 5]
.

With similar idea, we can get Poincaré-type inequalities for the gradient operator and Laplacian on compact Riemannian manifolds without boundary.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(n-p)\leq np.

There is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(M),

where

f_{\operatorname{ave}}:=\frac{1}{|M|}\int_{M}fdV.

Furthermore, when
p>n
, there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(M).

When
1\leq p<n
, Theorem
1.5
has been proved in
[
12
]
, see Theorem 2.11 there.
For the other cases, one may modify the proof of Theorem
1.2
a little to get them.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary. Assume
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(n-2p)<np,

and if
n=2
, we moreover require
p>1
if
q>1
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{2}(M).

Furthermore, when
p>\frac{n}{2}
, there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\|\Delta f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{2}(M).

When
p=q=2
, Theorem
1.6
is known, one may see
[
22
, Lemma 10.4.9]
.

By the same idea, Theorem
1.1
,
1.3
and
1.6
can be generalized to general uniformly elliptic operators of second order.
We omit the details here.

We observe that Theorem
1.3
and Corollary
1.4
can be generalized to (quasi)-subharmonic functions (not necessarily continuous) on manifolds.
We first recall some notions.

Assume
(M,g)
is a Riemannian manifold without boundary, then an upper semi-continuous function
f\colon M\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
is said to be
subharmonic

if
f\not\equiv-\infty
, and for any open subset
U\subset\subset M
,
any function
h\in C^{0}(\overline{U})
which is harmonic in
U
, we have
f\leq h
in
U
if
f\leq h
on
\partial U
.
The space of all subharmonic functions on
M
is denoted by
\operatorname{SH}(M)
.
For any
f\in\operatorname{SH}(M)
, one can show that
f
is locally integrable and
\Delta f\geq 0
in the sense of distribution.

A function
f\colon M\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
is
quasi-subharmonic
if it can be locally represented by
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f
subharmonic and
f_{2}
in
C^{2}
.
The space of all quasi-subharmonic functions on
M
is denoted by
\operatorname{QSH}(M)
.
For any
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
,
we use
|\Delta f|
to denote the variation measure of
\Delta f
.
We have
|\Delta f|=\Delta f
if
f
is subharmonic on
M
.

Now we can state several integral inequalities about (quasi-)subharmonic functions.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset with smooth boundary.
Assume
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-1},

and if
n=2
, we moreover require
q=1
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,M,g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{\Omega}|\Delta f|+\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},\ \forall f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M).

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset with smooth boundary.
Assume
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-1}.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,M,g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)}

for all nonnegative
f\in\operatorname{SH}(M)
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary.
Assume
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\frac{np}{n-1},

and if
n=2
, we moreover require
q=1
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\int_{M}|\Delta f|,\ \forall f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M).

When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded open subset, Theorem
1.8
is proved by Hörmander,
using a completely different method (
[
15
, Theorem 3.1]
).

Similar to the proof of Theorem
1.3
,
the above three theorems are derived from the following Green-Riesz type formulas (Theorem
1.10
and
1.11
), where for Theorem
1.8
, one needs to note the Green function is always non-positive, so the first term in the right hand side of Equality (
1.1
) can be dropped.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold without boundary,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset
with smooth boundary, and let
G
be the Green function for the Laplace operator on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then for any function
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
, we have

(1.1)

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)+\int_{\partial\Omega}f(y)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}dS(y),\ \forall x\in\Omega.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
G
be the Green function of
M
. Then for any function
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
, we have

f(x)=f_{\operatorname{ave}}+\int_{M}G(x,y)\Delta f(y),\ \forall x\in M.

When
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Omega
is a bounded domain, Theorem
1.10
is well known, see
[
7
, Proposition 4.22, Chapter I]
for example.
However, we cannot find a precise literature for such a general formula on manifolds.
The proofs of Theorem
1.10
and
1.11
are given in Section
7
.

One may also consider the corresponding (quasi-)subharmonic functions
for a general second order uniformly elliptic operator. Similar results of Theorem
1.7
,
1.8

and
1.9
also hold.

Let us turn to some applications of Green functions to complex analysis. Let
(M,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold.
A function
f\colon M\rightarrow[-\infty,\infty)
is
quasi-plurisubharmonic

if it can be locally represented as a sum
f_{1}+f_{2}
with
f_{1}
plurisubharmonic and
f_{2}
is of class
C^{2}
.
Since each plurisubharmonic function on an open subset of
M
is also subharmonic (by using normal coordinates),
so Theorem
1.7
,
1.8

and
1.9
are still valid for quasi-plurisubharmonic functions on
M
.

A Hermitian manifold
(M,h)
is called balanced if we have

\Delta_{d}f=2\Delta_{\partial}f=2\Delta_{\bar{\partial}}f,\ \forall f\in C^{2}(M).

Clearly, every Kähler manifold is
balanced
by the Kähler identities,
see
[
7
, Corollary 6.5, Chapter VI]
, but the converse is not true in general. By a simple observation, we can derive the following integral representation,
which plays a fundamental role in the proof of the corresponding
\bar{\partial}
-version of the above inequalities.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
(M,h)
be a balanced manifold,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open set with smooth boundary, and let
G
be the Green
function for the Laplace operator on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then we have

f(z)=-2\int_{\Omega}\langle\bar{\partial}f(w),\bar{\partial}_{w}G(z,w)\rangle dV(w)+\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial G(z,w)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{w}}f(w)dS(w)

for all
f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\bar{\partial}f
has a continuous continuation to the boundary.

Theorem
1.12
gives an integral representation for
C^{1}
functions on any balanced manifolds (not necessarily Stein or Kähler).
It seems that integral representations for functions are mainly considered only on Stein manifolds in the literatures,
for example, the integral formula discussed in
[
14
, Theorem 4.3.3]
,
whose proof strongly relies on embedding of Stein manifolds into some
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and some fundamental results about coherent analytic sheaves on Stein manifolds.
The approach we adopt here is a direct application of the Green representation formula.

A consequence of Theorem
1.12
is the following
\bar{\partial}
-version of Sobolev-type inequality, which generalizes
[
6
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
(M,h)
be a balanced manifold of complex dimension
n
,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset
with smooth boundary, and let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p,q,r<\infty,\ q(2n-p)<2np,\ q(2n-1)<2nr.

There is a constant
\delta:=\delta(\Omega,M,h,p,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial\Omega)}

for all
f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{1}(\Omega)
such that
\bar{\partial}f
has a continuous continuation to the boundary.

We omit the proof of Theorem
1.13
here,
since, with Theorem
1.12
in hand, the argument of it is parallel that in the proof of Theorem
1.2
and
1.3
.
Let us record the following special case of Theorem
1.13
, which we think has some independent interests.
The last author has used its domain version as an important tool to get the curvature strict positivity of direct image bundles (see the proof of
[
19
, Theorem 1.4]
).

Corollary 1.14
.

Let
(M,h)
be a balanced manifold of complex dimension
n
,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset
with smooth boundary, and let
p,q
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(2n-1)<2np.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,\Omega,h,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(\partial\Omega)},\ \forall\ f\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})\cap\mathcal{O}(\Omega),

where
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
denotes the space of holomorphic functions on
\Omega
.

Since holomorphic functions are always harmonic, then Corollary
1.14
is a special case of Corollary
1.4
.

Similar results of Theorem
1.5
and
1.6
also hold for the
\bar{\partial}
-operator.
In fact, when the considered functions have compact support, we have the following
\bar{\partial}
-version of the classical Poincaré inequality in PDE.

Theorem 1.15
.

Let
(M,h)
be a compact Hermitian manifold of complex dimension
n
, and let
p,q
satisfy

1<p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ q(2n-p)\leq 2np.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,h,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(M).

Furthermore, when
p>2n
, there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,g,p)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(M).

Using partition of unity, Theorem
1.15
is a consequence of Theorem
1.5
and the following lemma, which follows from Proposition 4 of
[
23
, Section 1, Chapter III]
and Fubini’s Theorem.

Lemma 1.16
.

For any
1<p<\infty
, there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(n,p)>0
such that

\delta\|\partial f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})},\ \forall f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Using Lemma
1.16
, it is possible to prove that if
f\in L_{\loc}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
satisfying
|\bar{\partial}f|\in L_{\loc}^{p}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
, then
f
is in the Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{\loc}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
(
1<p<\infty
). When
p=2
, such a fact is known (
[
1
, Lemma 2.26]
). For
p\neq 2
, we cannot find an exact literature for this fact, but the proof
of it is essentially the same with the proof of
[
1
, Lemma 2.26]
since one has Lemma
1.16
.

Note that we do not put any requirement on the metric
h
in Theorem
1.15
, but we require that
p>1
as we don’t know whether Lemma
1.16
still holds or not when
p=1
. The condition
p\neq 1

is very important in the proof of Lemma
1.16
since one only has weak
L^{1}
estimate for Hardy-Littlewoood maximal functions (see Lemma

8.1
). Surprisingly, when
p=1
, the conclusion of Theorem
1.15
also holds if the corresponding manifold is balanced.

Theorem 1.17
.

Let
(M,h)
be a compact balanced manifold of complex dimension
n
, and let

1\leq q\leq\frac{2n}{2n-1}.

Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,h,q)>0
such that

\delta\|f-f_{\operatorname{ave}}\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{1}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{1}(M).

The main idea for the proof of Theorem
1.17
is inspired by the proof of
[
12
, Theorem 2.11]
, but we need to do more efforts, see Section
8
for its proof.

By tracking our proofs, one can see many of the above inequalities can be easily generalized to functions in Sobolev spaces by the density of smooth functions and the continuity of the trace operator.

Finally, using the same method as in
[
6
]
, we derive an improved
L^{2}
-estimate for
\bar{\partial}
on a strictly pseudoconvex open subset in a Kähler manifold.

Theorem 1.18
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset with a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic boundary defining function
\rho
, and let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
such that
A_{E}:=i\Theta^{(E,h)}\wedge\Lambda\geq 0
in bidegree
(n,1)
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,\omega,\Omega,\rho,E,h)>0

such that for any nonzero
\bar{\partial}
-closed
f\in L^{2}_{(n,1)}(\Omega,E),
satisfying

M_{f}:=\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{E}^{-1}f,f\rangle dV<\infty,

there is
u\in L_{(n,0)}^{2}(\Omega,E)
such that
\bar{\partial}u=f
and

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dV\leq\frac{\|f\|}{\sqrt{\|f\|^{2}+\delta M_{f}}}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{E}^{-1}f,f\rangle dV.

Theorem
1.18
can be easily generalized to any
(n,q)
-forms and any
q
-pseudoconvex open subsets.

It is of great interest and importance to consider optimal constants in the estimates presented in all the above theorems and corollaries,
but we will not discuss this topic in depth in the present work, which will be systematically studied in forthcoming works.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations and Conventions

In this subsection, we fix some notations, conventions and collect some knowledge that are needed in our discussions.

Our convention for
\mathbb{N}
is
\mathbb{N}:=\{0,1,2,\cdots\}.
For any subset
A
in a topological space
X
,
we use
\overline{A}
to denote its closure in
X
,
A^{\circ}
to denote the set of its interior point,
and set
\partial A:=\overline{A}\setminus A^{\circ}.
If
A,B
are two subsets of a topological space, then we write
A\subset\subset B
if
\overline{A}
is a compact subset of
B.

In this article, we only consider oriented smooth manifolds with or without boundary, and when we say a manifold, we always mean it is smooth
and oriented. When we consider a smooth manifold
\overline{M}
with boundary, then we use
M
to represent the interior point of
\overline{M}
and use
\partial M
to denote its boundary point, and we always equip
\partial M
with the induced orientation. Clearly, any open subset of an oriented manifold is oriented and any complex manifold is also oriented.

Let
M
be a smooth manifold with or without boundary,
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
, and let
U
be an open subset of
M
, then we use
C^{k}(U)
to denote the space of complex valued functions on
U
which are of class
C^{k}
, and use
C_{c}^{k}(U)
to denote functions in
C^{k}(U)
which have compact support.
More generally, if
A
is an arbitrary subset of
M
, then we use
C^{k}(A)
to denote the space of complex valued
C^{k}
-functions on an open neighborhood of
\overline{A}
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a Riemannian manifold with boundary, then we use
dV
to denote the Riemannian volume form on
M
induced by
g
, and use
dS
to denote the induced volume form on
\partial M
. For any
A\subset M
or
A\subset\partial M
, we also write
|A|
for the volume of
A
. For any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
L^{p}(M)
(resp.
L^{p}(\partial M)
) denote the space of complex valued
L^{p}
-integrable
functions on
M
(resp.
\partial M
), and the corresponding norm is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(M)}
(resp.
\|\cdot\|_{L^{p}(\partial M)}
).
For any
x,y\in M
, let
d(x,y)
be the geodesic distance on
M
induced by
g
. For any
r>0
, set

B(x,r):=\{y\in M|\ d(x,y)<r\}.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold with or without boundary (of dimension
n
), then we use
d
to represent the usual de Rham operator on
M
.
Let
\nabla,\Delta
be the gradient operator and the Laplace operator on
M
, respectively.
In local coordinate, we may write

g=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}g_{ij}dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j},

then we use
\det g:=\det((g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n})
to denote the determinant of
g
, and use
(g^{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
to represent the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}
. Clearly, we have

\nabla f=\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}},\ \Delta f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(\sqrt{\det g}g^{ij}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{j}}\right).

For any

2.2.
Green functions on manifolds

In this subsection, we recall the definitions and some properties of Green functions.
Firstly, we give the definition of Green functions. We use
\Delta^{\operatorname{distr.}}
to denote the distributional Laplacian operator on a Riemannian manifold.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, then a Green function for
M
is
a function
G\colon M\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

G\in C^{\infty}((M\times M)\setminus\diag),
where
\diag:=\{(x,x)|\ x\in M\}.

(ii)

For any
x\in M
,
G(x,\cdot)
is locally integrable near
x
.

(iii)

For any
x\in M
, the distributional Laplacian of
G
satisfies

\Delta_{y}^{\operatorname{distr.}}G(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y)-\frac{1}{|M|},

where
\delta_{x}(\cdot)
denotes the Dirac distribution which is concentrated at the point
x
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary, then a Green function for
\overline{M}
is
a function
G\colon\overline{M}\times\overline{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
which satisfies the following properties:

(i)

G\in C^{\infty}((\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag),
where
\diag:=\{(x,x)|\ x\in\overline{M}\}.

(ii)

For any
x\in M
,
G(x,\cdot)
is locally integrable near
x
.

(iii)

For any
x\in M
, the distributional Laplacian of
G
satisfies

\Delta_{y}^{\operatorname{distr.}}G(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y).

(iv)

For any
x\in M
,
G(x,\cdot)|_{\partial M}=G(\cdot,x)|_{\partial M}=0
.

The above definitions can be easily modified to give the definitions of Green functions for a general elliptic operator on a manifold.

Now we state some properties of Green functions that will be used later.

Lemma 2.3
.

[

,
and let
0<4r<r_{0}.
Fix
x_{0}\in M
, and set
E:=B(x_{0},2r)\setminus\overline{B(x_{0},r)}
. Suppose
u\in C^{2}(\overline{E})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u=0&\text{ in }E,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial B(x_{0},r),\\
u=1&\text{ on }\partial B(x_{0},2r),\end{array}\right.

then there is a constant
C:=C(M,g)>0
such that

\sup_{E}|\nabla u|\leq\frac{C}{r}.

Proof..

The proof is inspired by the proof of
[
11
, Lemma 3.2]
. We may assume
\dim(M)\geq 3
, as the case
\dim(M)=2
is similar
if we note that there is a constant
C:=C(\diam(M))>0
such that

1+|\ln t|\leq\frac{C}{t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\text{ for all }0<t\leq\diam(M).

By the maximum principle, we know
\sup_{x\in E}|u(x)|\leq 1
.
Choose a local coordinate
(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})
on
B(x_{0},4r),
and set

D_{i}:=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\ D_{ij}:=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{i}\partial x_{j}},\ g=\sum_{i,j}g_{ij}dx_{i}\otimes dx_{j}.

If there may be some confusions, we use
D_{x_{i}}
to replace
D_{i}
. Let
N:=\sup_{E}|\nabla u|
, and choose
y_{0}\in E
such that
|\nabla u(y_{0})|\geq\frac{N}{2}.

Choose a cut-off function
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

0\leq\chi\leq 1,\ \chi|_{B(0,\frac{1}{2})}=1,\ \supp(\chi)\subset B(0,1),\ |\chi^{\prime}|\leq 2,\ |\chi^{\prime\prime}|\leq 2.

Set

\eta(x):=\chi\left(\frac{d(y_{0},x)}{rs}\right),\ \forall x\in M,

where
0<s<1
to be determined later. Note that
\overline{B(y_{0},r)}\subset B(x_{0},3r)
. It is clear that

\sup_{B(y_{0},r)}|\nabla d(y_{0},\cdot)|\leq 1.

Using the Hessian comparison theorem (e.g. see the first answer of
https://math.stackexchange.com/questions/1499968/
), there is a constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(M,g)>0
(in the following, when we say a number is a constant,
we mean it only depends on
M,g
) such that

\sum_{i,j}|D_{ij}d(y_{0},x)|\leq C\left(\frac{1}{d(y_{0},x)}+1\right),\ \forall x\in B(y_{0},r),

so there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{M}\sum_{i}|D_{i}\eta|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{rs},\ \sup_{M}\sum_{i,j}|D_{ij}\eta|\leq\frac{C_{2}}{(rs)^{2}}.

We may assume
u\eta=0,\ uD_{i}\eta=0
near
\partial E
for any
i
. If
d(y_{0},x_{0})\leq\frac{3r}{2}
, this is obvious.
If
d(y_{0},x_{0})>\frac{3r}{2}
, we may replace
u
by
1-u
. This is a solution of the equation
\Delta u=0
but with boundary
values
1
on
\partial B(x_{0},r)
and
0
on
\partial B(x_{0},2r)
, and we again have

\sup_{E}|\nabla(1-u)|=N,\ |\nabla(1-u)(y_{0})|\geq\frac{N}{2}.

Let
G
be the Green function in
E
corresponding to the operator

L:=\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}(y_{0})D_{ij},

so we have

u(x)\eta(x)=\int_{E}G(x,y)L(u\eta)(y)dV(y),\ \forall\ x\in E.

Differentiating the above equality, for any
k
and any
x\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\eta(x)D_{k}u(x)+u(x)D_{k}\eta(x)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y_{0})D_{ij}(u\eta)(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}\left(\left(g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y)\right)D_{j}(u\eta)\right)(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}\left(g^{ij}(y)D_{j}(u\eta)\right)(y)dV(y).

By Corollary
3.3
, we may choose a constant
C_{3}>0
such that

|G(x,y)|\leq C_{3}d(x,y)^{2-n},\ \sum_{k}|D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|\leq C_{3}d(x,y)^{1-n},

\sum_{k,l}|D_{y_{l}}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|\leq C_{3}d(x,y)^{-n},\ \forall x,y\in\overline{E},\ x\neq y.

Set

b_{i}:=\sum_{j}g^{ij}D_{j}\sqrt{\det g}.

There also exists a constant
C_{4}>0
such that

\sum_{i,j}|g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(x)|\leq C_{4}d(y_{0},x),\ \forall x\in\overline{E}\setminus\{y_{0}\};

\frac{1}{C_{4}^{2}}\delta^{ij}\leq g^{ij}(x)\leq C_{4}^{2}\delta^{ij},\ \sum_{i}|b_{i}(x)|\leq C,\sum_{i,j}|g^{ij}(x)|\leq C_{4},

\sqrt{\det g(x)}\leq C_{4},\ \sum_{i,j}|D_{i}(g^{ij}(x))|\leq C,\ \sum_{i}D_{i}\sqrt{\det g(x)}\leq C,\ \forall x\in E.

Since

|\nabla u|^{2}=g^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}},

then we have

\sum_{j}\sup_{E}|D_{j}u|\leq C_{4}N.

Set
C:=\max\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4}\}.

Since

\int_{\partial B(y_{0},\epsilon)}d(y_{0},y)^{2-n}dS(y)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\epsilon\rightarrow 0+,

then we know

\int_{\partial B(y_{0},\epsilon)}\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}(g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y))D_{j}(u\eta)(y)dS(y)\rightarrow 0

whenever
\epsilon\rightarrow 0+.
Let
d\lambda
be the usual Lebesgue measure on
B(x_{0},4r)
(which has been identified with its image by the above chosen coordinate).
Thus, using integration by parts and the fact
D_{j}(u\eta)=0
on
\partial E
, for any
k
and any
x\in E
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\left.\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}((g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y))D_{j}(u\eta))(y)dV(y)\right|_{x=y_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\left.\int_{E}D_{y_{i}}(\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y))(g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y))D_{j}(u\eta)(y)d\lambda(y)\right|_{x=y_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{y_{i}}(\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}})(g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y))D_{j}(u\eta)(y)d\lambda(y).

Note that
\supp(\eta)\subset B(y_{0},rs)
, then there is a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i,j}\left.\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}((g^{ij}(y_{0})-g^{ij}(y))D_{j}(u\eta))(y)dV(y)\right|_{x=y_{0}}\right|

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B(y_{0},rs)}C^{2}(d(y_{0},y)^{1-n}+d(y_{0},y)^{-n})d(y_{0},y)\left(CN+\frac{C}{rs}\right)d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{5}(Nrs+1).

Since

0=\Delta u=\sum_{i,j}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det g}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}(g^{ij}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{j}}\sqrt{\det g})\text{ in }E,

we have

\sum_{i,j}D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}u)=-\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}D_{j}\sqrt{\det g}D_{i}u=-\sum_{i}b^{i}D_{i}u\text{ in }E.

We also have

\sum_{i,j}D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}(u\eta))=\sum_{i,j}(D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}u)\eta+D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}\eta)u)+2\sum_{i,j}g^{ij}D_{j}\eta D_{i}u,

so for any
k
and any
x\in E
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}(u\eta))(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)b^{i}(y)D_{i}u(y)\eta(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}\eta)(y)u(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle+2\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y)D_{i}u(y)dV(y).

Since
uD_{j}\eta=0
on
\partial E
and
D_{j}\eta=0
in
B(y_{0},\frac{rs}{2})
, then for any
k
and any
x\in E
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y)D_{i}u(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{E\setminus B(y_{0},\epsilon)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y)D_{i}u(y)\sqrt{\det g(y)}d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{E\setminus B(y_{0},\epsilon)}D_{y_{i}}(\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y))u(y)d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{y_{i}}\left(\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y)\right)u(y)d\lambda(y).

Since
\supp(\eta)\subset B(y_{0},rs)
, for any
k
, we then have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}(u\eta))(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}b^{i}(y)D_{i}u(y)\eta(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}\eta)(y)u(y)dV(y)

\displaystyle-2\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{y_{i}}\left(\sqrt{\det g(y)}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}g^{ij}(y)D_{j}\eta(y)\right)u(y)d\lambda(y).

and then there is a constant
C_{6}>0
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i,j}\int_{E}D_{x_{k}}G(x,y)|_{x=y_{0}}D_{i}(g^{ij}D_{j}(u\eta))(y)dV(y)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C^{2}N\int_{B(y_{0},rs)}d(y_{0},y)^{1-n}dV(y)+\int_{B(y_{0},rs)}C^{3}\frac{d(y_{0},y)^{1-n}}{rs}\left(1+\frac{1}{rs}\right)dV(y)

\displaystyle+2\int_{B(y_{0},rs)\setminus B(y_{0},\frac{rs}{2})}C^{4}\frac{d(y_{0},y)^{-n}}{rs}\left(2d(y_{0},y)+1+d(y_{0},y)\frac{1}{rs}\right)d\lambda(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{6}Nrs+C_{6}\left(1+\frac{1}{rs}\right)+C_{6}\int_{\frac{rs}{2}}^{rs}\frac{1}{rs}\left(2+\frac{1}{t}+\frac{1}{rs}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{6}\left(Nrs+2+\frac{\frac{3}{2}+\ln 2}{rs}\right).

Therefore, combining Formulas (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), there is a constant
C_{7}>0
such that

\sum_{k}|D_{k}u(y_{0})|\leq C_{7}\left(Nrs+1+\frac{1}{rs}\right).

By the definition of gradient, we know there is a constant
C_{8}>0
such that

\frac{N}{2}\leq|\nabla u(y_{0})|\leq C_{8}\left(Nrs+1+\frac{1}{rs}\right).

Set
s:=\min\left\{\frac{1}{C_{8}r_{0}},1\right\}
, we know

N\leq\frac{C_{8}(1+4C_{8})r_{0}}{r},

and then the proof is complete.
∎

5.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Firstly, let us fix some notations. Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary (of dimension
n\geq 2
). It is clear that we may regard
M
as a submanifold of a compact Riemannian manifold
M_{0}
which has no boundary
(for example, we may consider the double of
M
). The corresponding Riemannian metric on
M_{0}
is also denoted by
g
. Now we
start to prove Theorem
1.1
. For convenience, we restate it here.

Theorem 5.1

(= Theorem
1.1
)
.

Let
G
be the Green function of
\overline{M}
, then it has the following uniform estimate:

(i)

If
n\geq 3
, then there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},M_{0},g)>0
such that for all
(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag,

we have

|G(x,y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{2-n},\ |\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq Cd(x,y)^{1-n}.

(ii)

If
n=2
, then there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},M_{0},g)>0
such that for all
(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag,

we have

|G(x,y)|\leq C(1+|\ln d(x,y)|),

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq C(1+|\ln d(x,y)|)\frac{1}{d(x,y)}.

Proof..

We may assume
n\geq 3
. By Lemma
2.3
, we may choose a function
G_{0}\in C^{\infty}((M_{0}\times M_{0})\setminus\diag)
such that
there is a constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(M_{0},g)>0
satisfying (in the following, when we say a number is a constant,
we mean it only depends on
\overline{M},M_{0},g
)

\Delta_{y}^{\operatorname{distr.}}G_{0}(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y)-\frac{1}{|M_{0}|},\ \forall(x,y)\in M_{0}\times M_{0};

-C_{1}d(x,y)^{2-n}\leq G_{0}(x,y)\leq C_{1},\ \forall\ (x,y)\in(M_{0}\times M_{0})\setminus\diag.

Choose two open neighborhoods
U_{1},U_{2}
of
\overline{M}\times\overline{M}
and choose
a function
\rho_{1}\in C^{\infty}(M_{0}\times M_{0})
such that

U_{1}\subset\subset U_{2}\subset\subset M_{0}\times M_{0},\ \rho_{1}\geq 0,\ \rho_{1}|_{(M_{0}\times M_{0})\setminus U_{2}}\equiv 1,\ \rho_{1}|_{U_{1}}\equiv 0.

Set

\rho_{2}(x,y):=\frac{1}{|M_{0}|}-\frac{\rho_{1}(x,y)}{\int_{M_{0}}\rho_{1}(x,z)dV(z)},\ \forall(x,y)\in M_{0}\times M_{0},

then we know

\rho_{2}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(M_{0}),\ \int_{M_{0}}\rho_{2}(x,\cdot)dV=0,\ \forall x\in M_{0}.

By Lemma
2.5
, for any
x\in M_{0}
, we may find a function
\rho_{3}(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(M_{0})
such that

\Delta_{y}\rho_{3}(x,y)=\rho_{2}(x,y),\ \int_{M_{0}}\rho_{3}(x,y)dV(y)=0.

By Lemma
2.6
, we know there is a constant
C_{2}>0
such that

\sup_{y\in M_{0}}|\rho_{3}(x,y)|\leq C_{2}\sup_{y\in M_{0}}|\rho_{2}(x,y)|,\ \forall x\in M_{0}.

Let

C_{3}:=C_{2}\sup_{x,y\in M_{0}}|\rho_{2}(x,y)|,

then we know

\sup_{x,y\in M_{0}}|\rho_{3}(x,y)|\leq C_{3}.

For any
x\in M_{0}
, set

\Gamma(x,y):=G_{0}(x,y)-\rho_{3}(x,y),\ \forall y\in M_{0}\setminus\{x\},

then we get

\Delta_{y}^{\operatorname{distr.}}\Gamma(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y),\ \forall(x,y)\in U_{1},

-C_{1}d(x,y)^{2-n}-C_{3}\leq\Gamma(x,y)\leq C_{1}+C_{3},\ \forall(x,y)\in(M_{0}\times M_{0})\setminus\diag.

For any
x\in M
, using Lemma
2.7
, we can find a function
\Phi(x,\cdot)\in C^{\infty}(\overline{M})
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{y}\Phi(x,y)=0&\text{ in }M,\\
\Phi(x,y)=\Gamma(x,y)&\text{ on }\partial M.\end{array}\right.

By the uniqueness of the Green function, we know

G(x,y)=\Gamma(x,y)-\Phi(x,y),\ \forall(x,y)\in M\times\overline{M}.

Applying the maximum principle, we have

\sup_{x\in M,y\in\overline{M}}\Phi(x,y)\leq C_{1}+C_{3},

then we get

G(x,y)\geq-C_{1}d(x,y)^{2-n}-C_{1}-2C_{3},\ \forall(x,y)\in(M\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag.

Since
M
is bounded and by continuity, there is a constant
C_{4}>0
satisfying

G(x,y)\geq-C_{4}d(x,y)^{2-n},\ \forall(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag,

which completes the proof of the first part of (i).

Now we turn to the proof of the uniform estimate of the gradient of the Green function of Part (i).
Indeed, using Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
4.1
, we can easily get what we wanted by imitating the proof of Theorem

3.1
.
The details is stated as follows.

We may assume
x,y\in M
. For any
x\in M
, set
\delta(x):=\inf_{y\in\partial M}d(x,y).
It is easy to see that
there is
r_{0}>0
such that for any
z\in\partial M
and any
0<r<r_{0}
, there is an open geodesic ball
B^{z}(r)
with radius
r
satisfying

B^{z}(r)\subset M_{0}\setminus\overline{M},\ \overline{B^{z}(r)}\cap\partial M=\{z\}.

Shrinking
r_{0}
if necessary, we may assume
10r_{0}
is less than the injective radius of
M_{0}.

Claim
\clubsuit
:
There is a constant
C_{6}>0
such that

|G(x,y)|\leq C_{6}d(x,y)^{1-n}\delta(x),\ \forall\ (x,y)\in(M\times M)\setminus\diag.

To prove the Claim, we fix
(x,y)\in(M\times M)\setminus\diag
and consider two cases.

Case 1:

\delta(x)<\min\left\{\frac{d(x,y)}{8},r_{0}\right\}
.

Set
r:=\min\left\{\frac{d(x,y)}{8},r_{0}\right\}
. Choose
z_{x}\in\partial M,\ x^{*}\in M_{0}\setminus\overline{M}
such that

B^{z_{x}}(r)=B(x^{*},r),

and set
E:=B(x^{*},2r)\setminus\overline{B(x^{*},r)}.
Using Lemma
2.7
, we may find a function
u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{E})
satisfying

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta u=0&\text{ in }E,\\
u=0&\text{ on }\partial B(x^{*},r),\\
u=1&\text{ on }\partial B(x^{*},2r).\end{array}\right.

By Lemma
4.1
, there is a constant
C_{5}>0
such that

\sup_{E}|\nabla u|\leq\frac{C_{5}}{r}.

As
z_{x}\in\partial B(x^{*},r)\cap\partial M
, then by the mean value theorem, we know

u(x)=|u(x)-u(z_{x})|\leq C_{5}\frac{d(x,z_{x})}{r}=\frac{C_{5}\delta(x)}{r}.

For any
z\in\partial B(x^{*},2r)\cap M
, we have

d(z_{x},z)\leq d(z_{x},x^{*})+d(x^{*},z)=3r,\ d(x,z)\leq d(x,z_{x})+d(z_{x},z)\leq 4r,

d(z,y)\geq d(x,y)-d(x,z)\geq d(x,y)-4r\geq\frac{d(x,y)}{2},

so we know

|G(z,y)|\leq C_{4}d(z,y)^{2-n}\leq C_{4}2^{n}d(x,y)^{2-n}u(z).

Note that for any
z\in\partial M
, we have
G(z,y)=0
. Applying the maximum principle to the set
M\cap E\ni x
, we get

(5.1)

\displaystyle|G(x,y)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}2^{n}d(x,y)^{2-n}u(x)\leq C_{4}C_{5}2^{n}\frac{d(x,y)^{2-n}}{r}\delta(x)

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}C_{5}2^{n}\left(8+\frac{\diam(M)}{r_{0}}\right)d(x,y)^{1-n}\delta(x),

where
\diam(M)
is the diameter of
M
.

Case 2:

\delta(x)\geq\min\left\{r_{0},\frac{d(x,y)}{8}\right\}
.

In this case, we have

\frac{d(x,y)}{\delta(x)}\leq 8+\frac{\diam(M)}{r_{0}},

so we get

(5.2)

|G(x,y)|\leq C_{4}d(x,y)^{2-n}\leq C_{4}\left(8+\frac{\diam(M)}{r_{0}}\right)d(x,y)^{1-n}\delta(x).

Combining
Case 1
and
Case 2
, especially Inequalities (
5.1
) and (
5.2
),
we know
Claim
\clubsuit
holds with a constant

C_{6}:=\max\left\{C_{4}C_{5}2^{n}\left(8+\frac{\diam(M)}{r_{0}}\right),C_{4}\left(8+\frac{\diam(M)}{r_{0}}\right)\right\}.

Now we start to prove the last part of (iv). Fix
(x,y)\in(M\times M)\setminus\diag
, and we also consider two cases.

Case 1:

\delta(y)\leq d(x,y)
.

In this case, we know
G(x,\cdot)
is a harmonic function in
B(y,\delta(y))
. By Lemma
2.8
,
there exists a constant
C_{7}>0
such that

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq\frac{C_{7}}{\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)}\sup_{B\left(y,\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)\right)}|G(x,\cdot)|.

For any
z\in B\left(y,\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)\right)
, we have

d(x,z)\geq d(x,y)-d(y,z)\geq d(x,y)-\frac{1}{2}\delta(y)\geq\frac{d(x,y)}{2},

\delta(z)\leq\delta(y)+d(y,z)\leq 2\delta(y),

then by
Claim
\clubsuit
, we get

|G(x,z)|\leq C_{6}d(x,z)^{1-n}\delta(z)\leq C_{6}2^{n}d(x,y)^{1-n}\delta(y),

and thus we know

(5.3)

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq 2^{n+1}C_{6}C_{7}d(x,y)^{1-n}.

Case 2:

\delta(y)>d(x,y)
.

In this case, we know
G(x,\cdot)
is a harmonic function in
B(y,d(x,y))
. By Lemma
2.8
,
there is a constant
C_{8}>0
such that

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq\frac{C_{8}}{\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)}\sup_{B\left(y,\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)\right)}|G(x,\cdot)|.

For any
z\in B\left(y,\frac{1}{2}d(x,y)\right)
, we have

d(x,z)\geq d(x,y)-d(y,z)\geq\frac{1}{2}d(x,y),

so we have

|G(x,z)|\leq C_{4}d(x,z)^{2-n}\leq C_{4}2^{n-2}d(x,y)^{2-n},

and then we get

(5.4)

|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq 2^{n}C_{4}C_{8}d(x,y)^{1-n}.

By Inequalities (

5.3
), we know the last part of (iv) holds with

C:=\max\left\{2^{n+1}C_{6}C_{7},2^{n}C_{4}C_{8}\right\}.

The proof is complete.
∎

Proof..

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary (of dimension

G
be the Green function.
For any
a>0
such that the exponents of the following integrands are nonnegative, we have

(i)

If
n\geq 3
, then there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},g)>0

such that

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|G(x,y)|^{\frac{-n+a}{2-n}}dV(y)\leq\frac{C}{a},\ \sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{\frac{-n+a}{1-n}}dV(y)\leq\frac{C}{a},

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{\partial M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{\frac{-n+1+a}{1-n}}dS(y)\leq\frac{C}{a}.

(ii)

If
n=2

(iii)

If
n=2
, then there is a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},g)>0
such that for any
1\leq p<\infty
, we have

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|G(x,y)|^{p}dV(y)\leq p^{p}C^{p+1}.

Proof..

(i) By Theorem
5.1
, we know there is a constant
C_{1}:=C_{1}(\overline{M},g)>0
such that
for all
(x,y)\in(\overline{M}\times\overline{M})\setminus\diag,
we have

|G(x,y)|\leq C_{1}d(x,y)^{2-n},\ |\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\leq C_{1}d(x,y)^{1-n}.

By Lemma 3.1 and Lemma 3.4 of
[

(ii) By Theorem

, we know there is a constant

Clearly, there is a constant

(iii) For any

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}d(x,y)^{-pb}dV(y)\leq C_{4}.

Thus, we know

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|G(x,y)|^{p}dV(y)\leq(pC_{2}C_{3})^{p}C_{4}.

Another useful simple lemma is the following

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with a smooth boundary defining function
\rho
, and let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. For any
(k-1)
-form
s_{1}
and
k
-form
s_{2}
which are of class
C^{1}
on
\overline{M}
, we have

\int_{M}\langle ds_{1},s_{2}\rangle dV-\int_{M}\langle s_{1},d^{*}s_{2}\rangle dV=\int_{\partial M}\langle d\rho\wedge s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\frac{dS}{|\nabla\rho|}.

Proof..

We may assume
k\geq 1
.
By Formula (3.5) and Theorem 3.9 of
[
7
, Chapter VI]
, we have

d^{*}s_{2}=(-1)^{nk+n+1}*d*s_{2},\ **d*s_{2}=(-1)^{(n+1)(n-k+1)}d*s_{2},

and then

\displaystyle\langle ds_{1},s_{2}\rangle dV-\langle s_{1},d^{*}s_{2}\rangle dV

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds_{1}\wedge*s_{2}-s_{1}\wedge*d^{*}s_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds_{1}\wedge*s_{2}+(-1)^{nk+n}s_{1}\wedge**d*s_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds_{1}\wedge*s_{2}+(-1)^{nk+n}(-1)^{(n+1)(n-k+1)}s_{1}\wedge d*s_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle ds_{1}\wedge*s_{2}+(-1)^{k-1}s_{1}\wedge d*s_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d(s_{1}\wedge*s_{2}).

By the Stokes theorem, we have

\int_{M}\langle ds_{1},s_{2}\rangle dV-\langle s_{1},d^{*}s_{2}\rangle dV=\int_{\partial M}s_{1}\wedge*s_{2}.

Now it suffices to prove

(s_{1}\wedge*s_{2})|_{\partial M}=\langle d\rho\wedge s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\frac{dS}{|\nabla\rho|}.

This problem is local, we may taking a local orthonormal frame
e_{1},\cdots,e_{n}
of
TM
such that
e_{n}
is the outward unit normal vector of
\partial M
.
Let
e^{1},\cdots,e^{n}
be the dual frame. By linearity, we may assume
s_{2}=e^{j_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{j_{k}}
for some
1\leq j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}\leq n
.
Write

s_{1}=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k-1}\leq n}a_{i_{1}\cdots i_{k-1}}e^{i_{1}}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{i_{k-1}},

then

*s_{2}=\operatorname{sgn}(J,J^{c})e^{J^{c}},\ s_{1}\wedge*s_{2}=\sum_{p=1}^{k}\operatorname{sgn}(J,J^{c})a_{j_{1}\cdots\widehat{j_{p}}\cdots j_{k}}e^{J\setminus\{j_{p}\}}\wedge e^{J^{c}},

where
\operatorname{sgn}
denotes the sign of a permutation,
J^{c}:=\{1,\cdots,n\}\setminus J
. Since the restriction of
e^{n}
on
\partial M
is zero, we know

(s_{1}\wedge*s_{2})|_{\partial M}=(-1)^{n-k}a_{j_{1}\cdots j_{k-1}}e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{n-1}.

Note that
\nabla\rho
is parallel to
e_{n}
, then we have

d\rho=|\nabla\rho|e^{n},

so we get

\langle d\rho\wedge s_{1},s_{2}\rangle=(-1)^{k-1}|\nabla\rho|a_{j_{1}\cdots j_{k-1}}.

Note also that
dS=(-1)^{n-1}e^{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge e^{n-1}
, thus we have

(s_{1}\wedge*s_{2})|_{\partial M}=\langle d\rho\wedge s_{1},s_{2}\rangle\frac{dS}{|\nabla\rho|},

which completes the proof.
∎

Using Green functions, we may give an integral representation, i.e.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
(\overline{M},g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold with boundary. For any
f\in C^{1}(\overline{M}),\ x\in M
, we have

f(x)=-\int_{M}\langle d_{y}G(x,y),df(y)\rangle dV(y)+\int_{\partial M}\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}f(y)dS(y),

Proof..

By density, we may assume
f\in C^{2}(\overline{M})
. By definition, we know
-\Delta=\Delta_{d}=dd^{*}+d^{*}d
.
Fix
x\in M
. By Lemma
6.3

and note that
G(x,\cdot)|_{\partial M}=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)dV(y)+\int_{M}\langle d_{y}G(x,y),df(y)\rangle dV(y)

\displaystyle=-

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{M\setminus B(x,\epsilon)}\langle G(x,y),d^{*}df(y)\rangle dV(y)+\int_{M}\langle d_{y}G(x,y),df(y)\rangle dV(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}(-1)^{n+1}\int_{\partial B(x,\epsilon)}\langle G(x,y)d\rho(y),df(y)\rangle\frac{dS(y)}{|\nabla\rho(y)}.

By Theorem
5.1
, we may find a constant
C:=C(\overline{M},g)>0
such that for all

,
we have

|G(x,y)|\leq C\cdot\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}d(x,y)^{2-n}&\text{ if }n\geq 3,\\
1+|\ln d(x,y)|&\text{ if }n=2,\end{array}\right.

and thus it is clear that

\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0+}\int_{\partial B(x,\epsilon)}\langle G(x,y)d\rho(y),df(y)\rangle\frac{dS(y)}{|\nabla\rho(y)}=0.

By Lemma
2.3
, we get

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)dV(y)+\int_{\partial M}\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}f(y)dS(y),

\displaystyle=-\int_{M}\langle d_{y}G(x,y),df(y)\rangle dV(y)+\int_{\partial M}\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}f(y)dS(y).

∎

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
p,q,r
satisfy

1\leq p<\infty,\ 1\leq q<\infty,\ 1\leq r<\infty,\ q(n-p)<np,\ q(n-1)<nr,

then

(i)

There exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(\overline{M},g,p,q)>0
such that

\delta\|B_{M}f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)},\ \forall f\in C^{0}(\overline{M}),

where

B_{M}f(x):=\int_{M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\cdot|f(y)|dV(y),\ \forall x\in M.

(ii)

There exists a constant
\delta:=\delta(\overline{M},g,q,r)>0
such that

\delta\|B_{\partial M}f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial M)},\ \forall f\in C^{0}(\overline{M}),

where

B_{\partial M}f(x):=\int_{\partial M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|\cdot|f(y)|dS(y),\ \forall x\in M.

Proof..

(i) For any
0<a\leq n,
set

N(a,M):=\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{\frac{-n+a}{1-n}}dV(y).

By Lemma
6.1
, there is a constant
C_{1}:=C(\overline{M},g)>0
such that

N(a,M)\leq\frac{C_{1}}{a}.

Since
M
is bounded, then by Hölder’s Inequality, we may assume
q\geq p
. We moreover assume
p>1

since the case
p=1
can be treated by taking a limit if we note that

\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)}=\lim_{p\rightarrow 1+}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}

and the corresponding constant (in the last line of this part) is uniformly bounded as
p\rightarrow 1+
. Set

b:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{0,\frac{n-p}{p(n-1)}\right\}+\min\left\{\frac{n}{q(n-1)},1\right\}\right),

then by assumption, we have

0<\frac{p(1-n)(1-b)}{p-1}+n\leq n,\ 0<qb(1-n)+n\leq n.

Choose
a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

qa=p,\ q_{0}(1-a)=p,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}=1,

where if
p=q
, then
q_{0}=\infty
.
Fix any
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
, then for any
x\in M,
we have (use Hölder’s Inequality for three functions)

\displaystyle|B_{M}f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|f(y)|^{a}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{b}|f(y)|^{1-a}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{1-b}dV(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{M}|f(y)|^{p}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(y)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{M}|f(z)|^{p}dV(z)\right]^{\frac{q-p}{qp}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{M}|\nabla_{w}G(x,w)|^{\frac{p(1-b)}{p-1}}dV(w)\right]^{\frac{p-1}{p}},

which implies

\displaystyle|B_{M}f(x)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q-p}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{p}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(y)\left[\int_{M}|\nabla_{w}G(x,w)|^{\frac{p(1-b)}{p-1}}dV(w)\right]^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{p(1-n)(1-b)}{p-1}+n,M\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q-p}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{p}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(y),

so (by Fubini’s Theorem)

\displaystyle\|B_{M}f\|_{L^{q}(M)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(qb(1-n)+n,M)N\left(\frac{p(1-n)(1-b)}{p-1}+n,M\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle N(qb(1-n)+n,M)N\left(\frac{p(1-n)(1-b)}{p-1}+n,M\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{qb(1-n)+n}\left(\frac{C_{1}(p-1)}{p-n-pb(1-n)}\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}.

(ii) For any
0<a\leq n-1,
set

N(a,\partial M):=\sup_{x\in M}\int_{\partial M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{\frac{-n+1+a}{1-n}}dS(y),

then by Lemma
6.1
, there is a constant
C_{2}:=C_{2}(\overline{M},g)>0
such that

N(a,\partial M)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{a}.

Similar to (i), we may assume
q\geq r>1
. Set

b:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\min\left\{\frac{n}{q(n-1)},1\right\}+\frac{1}{r}\right),

then we have

0<qb(1-n)+n\leq n,\ 0<\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1}\leq n-1.

Choose
a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

qa=r,\ q_{0}(1-a)=r,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}=1,

where if
q=r
, then
q_{0}=\infty
.
Fix
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
, then for any
x\in M,
we have

\displaystyle|B_{\partial M}f(x)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}|f(y)|^{a}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{b}|f(y)|^{1-a}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{1-b}dS(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(y)|^{qa}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dS(y)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(z)|^{q_{0}(1-a)}dS(z)\right]^{\frac{1}{q_{0}}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{\partial M}|\nabla_{w}G(x,w)|^{\frac{r(1-b)}{r-1}}dS(w)\right]^{\frac{r-1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1},\partial M\right)^{\frac{r-1}{r}}\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(y)|^{r}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dS(y)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(z)|^{r}dS(z)\right]^{\frac{q-r}{qr}},

so we get

\displaystyle|B_{\partial M}f(x)|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1},\partial M\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(y)|^{r}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dS(y)\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{\partial M}|f(z)|^{r}dS(z)\right]^{\frac{q-r}{qr}},

then

\displaystyle\int_{M}|B_{\partial M}f(x)|^{q}dV(x)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1},\partial M\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\int_{(x,y)\in M\times\partial M}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(x)dS(y)

\displaystyle\cdot\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial M)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N(qb(1-n)+n,M)|\partial M|N\left(\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1},\partial M\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}\|f\|_{L^{r}(\partial M)}^{q}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle N(qb(1-n)+n,M)N\left(\frac{(rb-1)(n-1)}{r-1},\partial M\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{1}}{qb(1-n)+n}\left(\frac{(r-1)C_{2}}{(rb-1)(n-1)}\right)^{\frac{q(r-1)}{r}}.

The proof is completed.
∎

q\geq p>1.
Set

b:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\max\left\{0,\frac{n-2p}{p(n-2)}\right\}+\min\left\{\frac{n}{q(n-2)},1\right\}\right),

then by assumption, we have

0<\frac{(n-2)pb+2p-n}{p-1}\leq n,\ 0<n-(n-2)qb\leq n.

Choose
a,q_{0},r_{0}
such that

qa=p,\ q_{0}(1-a)=p,\ \frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q_{0}}+\frac{1}{r_{0}}=1,

where if
p=q
, then
q_{0}=\infty
.
Fix any
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
, then for any
x\in M,
we have

\displaystyle|B_{M}f(x)|^{q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}|f(y)|^{a}|G(x,y)|^{b}|f(y)|^{1-a}|G(x,y)|^{1-b}dV(y)\right]^{q}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{M}|f(y)|^{p}|G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(y)\cdot\left[\int_{M}|f(z)|^{p}dV(z)\right]^{\frac{q-p}{p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\left[\int_{M}|G(x,w)|^{\frac{p(1-b)}{p-1}}dV(w)\right]^{\frac{(p-1)q}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle N\left(\frac{(n-2)pb+2p-n}{p-1},M\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q-p}\int_{M}|f(y)|^{p}|G(x,y)|^{qb}dV(y),

so

\displaystyle\|B_{M}f\|_{L^{q}(M)}^{q}

\displaystyle\leq N(qb(2-n)+n,M)N\left(\frac{(n-2)pb+2p-n}{p-1},M\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{qb(2-n)+n}\left(\frac{C_{1}(p-1)}{(n-2)pb+2p-n}\right)^{\frac{q(p-1)}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}.

Case 2:

n=2
.

If
q>1
, then
p>1
, we set
s:=\frac{p}{p-1}
. By Lemma
6.1
, there is a constant
C_{2}:=C_{2}(\overline{M},g)>1
such that

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|G(x,y)|^{s}dV(y)\leq s^{s}C_{2}^{s+1}.

By Hölder’s Inequality, for any
x\in M
, and any
f\in C^{0}(\overline{M})
, we have

|B_{M}f(x)|^{q}\leq\left[\int_{M}|G(x,y)|^{s}dV(y)\right]^{\frac{q}{s}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}\leq s^{q}C_{2}^{2q}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}^{q}.

Thus, we know

\|B_{M}f\|_{L^{q}(M)}\leq\operatorname{Vol}(M)^{\frac{1}{q}}sC_{2}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{p}(M)}.

If
q=1
, then we may assume
p=1
. By Lemma
6.1
, there is a constant
C_{3}:=C_{3}(\overline{M},g)>1
such that

\sup_{x\in M}\int_{M}|G(x,y)|dV(y)\leq C_{3}.

By Fubini’s Theorem, we have

\|B_{M}f\|_{L^{1}(M)}\leq C_{3}\|f\|_{L^{1}(M)}.

The proof is complete.
∎

The proofs of Theorem
1.10

and

In this section, we give the proofs of Theorem
1.10
and
1.11
. Many simple properties of subharmonic functions on Riemannian manifolds can be easily got from the article

[
3
]
via some minor modifications, so we don’t repeat the arguments.

Theorem 7.1

(= Theorem
1.10
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold without boundary (of dimension
\geq 2
),
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset
with smooth boundary, and let
G
be the Green function for the Laplace operator on
\overline{\Omega}
. Then for any function
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
, we have

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)+\int_{\partial\Omega}f(y)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}dS(y),\ \forall x\in\Omega.

Proof..

Using Lemma
2.7
, we may find a function
h\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
which is strictly subharmonic
in an open neighborhood
U\subset\subset M
of
\overline{\Omega}
, then for any
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
, there exist
f_{1}\in C^{2}(U)
and
f_{2}\in\operatorname{SH}(U)
such that
f|_{U}=f_{1}+f_{2}
(by a partition of unity). Clearly,
\Delta f=\Delta f_{1}+\Delta f_{2},
where
\Delta f_{1}
is the signed measure determined by

f_{1}
. Thus, by Lemma
2.4
, we may assume
f\in\operatorname{SH}(M)
.

Choose an open neighborhood
U\subset\subset M
of
\overline{\Omega}
with smooth boundary. By Lemma
2.4
, for any fixed
x\in U
, we may choose a function
\Gamma(x,\cdot)
such that

\Delta_{y}^{\operatorname{distr.}}\Gamma(x,y)=\delta_{x}(y)\text{ in }U,

and
\Gamma(x,y)=\Gamma(y,x)
for all
x,y\in U,\ x\neq y
.
By Remark
5.2
, we may find a function
\Phi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
such that
for any fixed
x\in U
, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Delta_{y}\Phi(x,y)=0&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
\Phi(x,y)=\Gamma(x,y)&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

By the uniqueness of the Green function, it is easy to see that
G(x,y)=\Gamma(x,y)-\Phi(x,y)
for any
x,y\in\overline{\Omega},\ x\neq y
.
For any fixed
x\in\overline{\Omega}
,
\Phi(\cdot,x)=\Phi(x,\cdot)
is a harmonic function on
\Omega
.
It is obvious that

\Delta_{x}^{\operatorname{distr.}}\left(f(x)-\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(x,y)\Delta f(y)\right)=0\text{ in }U.

By Weyl’s Lemma, there is a harmonic function
h
on
U
such that

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(x,y)\Delta f(y)+h(x)

for almost every
x\in U
. However, for a subharmonic function, its value at every point is uniquely determined, so
we know

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}\Gamma(x,y)\Delta f(y)+h(x),\ \forall x\in U.

Thus, we know

(7.1)

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)+l(x),\ \forall x\in\overline{\Omega},

where

l(x):=\int_{\Omega}\Phi(x,y)\Delta f(y)+h(x),\ \forall x\in\overline{\Omega}.

Since
\Phi\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}\times\overline{\Omega})
and
\Phi(\cdot,y)
is a harmonic function in
\Omega

for any
y\in\overline{\Omega}
, we know
l\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is harmonic in
\Omega
. Similar proofs with Green’s identities and Lemma
2.4
show that

l(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}l(y)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}dS(y),\ \forall x\in\Omega.

Combining Equality (
7.1
) and note that
G|_{\Omega\times\partial\Omega}=0
, we know

l(x)=\int_{\partial\Omega}f(y)\frac{\partial G(x,y)}{\partial\vec{\bm{n}}_{y}}dS(y),\ \forall x\in\Omega,

which completes the proof.
∎

Using the main idea of the proof of Theorem
1.10
, we now give the proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Theorem 7.2

(= Theorem
1.11
)
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold without boundary, and let
G
be the Green function of
M
. Then for any function
f\in\operatorname{QSH}(M)
, we have

f(x)=f_{\operatorname{ave}}+\int_{M}G(x,y)\Delta f(y),\ \forall x\in M.

Proof..

It is clear that

\Delta_{x}^{\operatorname{distr.}}\left(f(x)-\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)\right)=0\text{ in }M.

By Weyl’s Lemma, there is a harmonic function
h
on
M
such that

f(x)=\int_{\Omega}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)+h(x)

for almost every
x\in M
, then

f(x)=\int_{M}G(x,y)\Delta f(y)+h(x),\ \forall x\in M.

Using Lemma
2.3
and note that
\int_{M}G(x,y)dV(y)=0
for all
x\in M
, we then know

h(x)=h_{\operatorname{ave}}=f_{\operatorname{ave}},\ \forall x\in M.

∎

The proof of Theorem

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
1.17
. Firstly, the following weak
L^{1}
-estimate of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal function is well known, see
[
13
, Theorem 3.2.7]
for example.

Lemma 8.1
.

For any
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, and any
t>0
, we have

\left|\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}|\ (Mf)(x)>t\}\right|\leq\frac{3^{n}}{t}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

(Mf)(x):=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}f(y)dV(y).

Please see
[
14
, Theorem 1.9.1]
or
[
1
, Theorem 3.1]
for the classical Bochner-Martinealla formula.
Let
\lip_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
denote Lipschitz functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which have compact supports,
then we know the Bochner-Martinella formula still holds for any
f\in\lip_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by approximation
(note that
|\bar{\partial}f|\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
by
[
8
, Theorem 4, Section 5.7]
),
i.e., we have

Lemma 8.2
.

For any
f\in\lip_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
and any
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that
f(z)\neq 0
, we have

f(z)=-\frac{(n-1)!}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\bar{\partial}f(\xi)\wedge\omega(\xi,z),

where

\omega(\xi,z):=\frac{1}{|\xi-z|^{2n}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j+1}(\bar{\xi}_{j}-\bar{z}_{j})\mathop{\wedge}_{k\neq j}d\bar{\xi}_{k}\wedge\mathop{\wedge}_{l=1}^{n}d\xi_{l}.

Now we give a key lemma which is important for the proof of Theorem
1.15
, which we think has some independent interests.

Lemma 8.3
.

There is a constant
\delta:=\delta(n)>0
such that

\delta\|f\|_{L^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}\leq\|\bar{\partial}f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})},\ \forall f\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n}).

Proof..

The proof is inspired by the proof of
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
.
For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, set

A_{j}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\ 2^{j}<|f(z)|\leq 2^{j+1}\},

and define

f_{j}\colon\mathbb{C}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},\ z\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\text{ if }|f(z)|\leq 2^{j-1},\\
2^{1-j}f(z)-1&\text{ if }2^{j-1}<|f(z)|<2^{j},\\
1&\text{ if }|f(z)|>2^{j}.\end{array}\right.

For any
j\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\bar{\partial}f_{j}=2^{1-j}\bar{\partial}f
for almost every
z
such that
2^{j-1}<|f(z)|<2^{j}
,
and
\bar{\partial}f_{j}=0
for almost any other
z
, then we know
f_{j}\in\lip_{c}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
.
By Lemma
8.2
and note that
\left|\mathop{\wedge}_{k=1}^{n}d\bar{\xi}_{k}\wedge\mathop{\wedge}_{l=1}^{n}d\xi_{l}\right|=2^{n}dV(\xi),

for any
z\in\supp(f)
, we have

|f_{j}(z)|\leq\frac{n!}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}(\xi)|\cdot|\xi-z|^{1-2n}dV(\xi).

We split this integral into two parts as
\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}=\int_{B(z,r)}+\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus B(z,r)}

for some
r
to be determined later. For the first part, we have

\displaystyle\quad\int_{B(z,r)}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}(\xi)|\cdot|\xi-z|^{1-2n}dV(\xi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2^{-k}r)\setminus B(z,2^{-k-1}r)}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}(\xi)|\cdot|\xi-z|^{1-2n}dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2^{-k}r)\setminus B(z,2^{-k-1}r)}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}(\xi)|\cdot(2^{-k-1}r)^{1-2n}dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\int_{B(z,2^{-k}r)}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|\cdot(2^{-k-1}r)^{1-2n}dV

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|B(z,2^{-k}r)|\cdot(2^{-k-1}r)^{1-2n}M(|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|)(z)

\displaystyle=4^{n}|B(0,1)|rM(|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|)(z).

For the second part, we have

\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus B(z,r)}|\bar{\partial}f_{j}(\xi)|\cdot|\xi-z|^{1-2n}d\lambda(\xi)\leq\|\bar{\partial}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}r^{1-2n}.

Choose
r
such that

4^{n}|B(0,1)|rM(|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|)(z)=\|\bar{\partial}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}r^{1-2n},

then we have

|f_{j}(z)|\leq\frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\left(4^{n}|B(0,1)|M(|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|)(z)\right)^{\frac{2n-1}{2n}}\|\bar{\partial}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{2n}},\forall z\in\supp(f).

By Lemma
8.1
, we have

\displaystyle|A_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq|\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\ |f(z)|>2^{j}\}|=|\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}|\ f_{j}(z)=1\}|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\left|\ \frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\right.\left(4^{n}|B(0,1)|M(|\bar{\partial}f_{j}|)(z)\right)^{\frac{2n-1}{2n}}\|\bar{\partial}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}^{\frac{1}{2n}}\geq 1\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}36^{n}|B(0,1)|\|\bar{\partial}f_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{C}^{n})}^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}36^{n}|B(0,1)|\left(\int_{2^{j-1}<|f(z)|<2^{j}}2^{1-j}|\bar{\partial}f|d\lambda\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}36^{n}|B(0,1)|2^{\frac{2n(1-j)}{2n-1}}\left(\int_{A_{j-1}}|\bar{\partial}f|d\lambda\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|f|^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{A_{j}}|f|^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}d\lambda\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{2n(j+1)}{2n-1}}|A_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{2n!}{\pi^{n}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}36^{n}|B(0,1)|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}2^{\frac{4n}{2n-1}}\left(\int_{A_{j-1}}|\bar{\partial}f|d\lambda\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{8n!}{\pi^{n}}\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}}36^{n}|B(0,1)|\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\bar{\partial}f|d\lambda\right)^{\frac{2n}{2n-1}},

and the proof is complete.
∎

.

By a partition of unity, the following lemma can be easily derived from

Lemma 9.1

C_{(0,1)}^{1}(\overline{\Omega},E)\cap\dom(\bar{\partial}^{E,*})
is dense in

Lemma 9.2
.

.

Now we prove Theorem
1.18
.

Theorem 9.3

(= Theorem
1.18
)
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be a Kähler manifold,
\Omega\subset\subset M
be an open subset with a smooth strictly plurisubharmonic boundary defining function
\rho
, and let
(E,h)
be a Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle over
M
such that
A_{E}:=i\Theta^{(E,h)}\wedge\Lambda\geq 0

(n,1)
. Then there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,\omega,\Omega,\rho,E,h)>0

such that for any nonzero
\bar{\partial}
-closed

satisfying

M_{f}:=\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{E}^{-1}f,f\rangle dV<\infty,

\bar{\partial}u=f
and

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dV\leq\frac{\|f\|}{\sqrt{\|f\|^{2}+\delta M_{f}}}\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{E}^{-1}f,f\rangle dV.

Proof..

Set
M_{f}:=\int_{\Omega}\langle A_{E}^{-1}f,f\rangle dV
. Similar to the proof of
[

, Theorem 4.5, Chapter VIII]
, by the Hahn-Banach extension theorem and Riesz representation theorem, it suffices to prove there is a
\delta:=\delta(M,\omega,\Omega,\rho,E,h)>0

(n,1)
-form

|(\alpha,f)|^{2}+\delta\frac{|(\alpha,f)|^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}M_{f}\leq\left(\|\bar{\partial}^{E}\alpha\|^{2}+\|\bar{\partial}^{E,*}\alpha\|^{2}\right)M_{f}.

, we may assume
\alpha\in C^{1}_{(0,1)}(\overline{\Omega})

1.13

\Omega

using a partition of unity, there is a constant
\delta:=\delta(M,\omega,\Omega,\rho,E,h)>0
such that

\delta\|\alpha\|^{2}\leq\|\partial^{E,*}\alpha\|^{2}+\int_{\partial\Omega}L_{\rho}(\alpha,\alpha)dS.

Using Cauchy-Schwarz Inequality and Lemma

, we get

\displaystyle\quad|(\alpha,f)|^{2}+\delta\frac{|(\alpha,f)|^{2}}{\|f\|_{2}^{2}}M_{f}

\displaystyle\leq(A_{E}\alpha,\alpha)\cdot M_{f}+\delta\|\alpha\|^{2}\cdot M_{f}

\displaystyle\leq\left[(A_{E}\alpha,\alpha)+\|\partial^{E,*}\alpha\|^{2}+\int_{\partial M}L_{\rho}(\alpha,\alpha)dS\right]\cdot M_{f}

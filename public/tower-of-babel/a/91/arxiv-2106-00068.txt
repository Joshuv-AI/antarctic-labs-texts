Blowup of Solutions to a Damped Euler Equation with Homogeneous Three-Point Boundary Condition
Ikechukwu Obi-Okoye, Alejandro Sarria

Abstract
. It has been established that solutions to the inviscid Proudman-Johnson equation subject to a homogeneous three-point boundary condition can develop singularities in finite time. In this paper, we consider the possibility of singularity formation in solutions of the generalized, inviscid Proudman-Johnson equation with damping subject to the same homogeneous three-point boundary condition. In particular, we derive conditions the initial data must satisfy in order for solutions to blowup in finite time with either bounded or unbounded smooth damping term.

3.
Finite-time Blowup with Smooth, bounded Damping

In this section, we will derive conditions on the initial data
u_{0}(x)
which show that the presence of a smooth (bounded or unbounded) time-dependent damping term
\alpha(t)
is insufficient to arrest finite-time blowup for any value of the parameter
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Consider the IBVP (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for the generalized inviscid damped Proudman Johnson equation with initial data
u_{0}(x)\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
and smooth bounded damping term
\alpha(t):[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
.

Set

H_{0}=-\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}^{\prime}(x)\,dx=u_{0}(0)

and

(3.2)

M:=\sup_{t\in[0,\infty)}\alpha(t)

for some
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. If

(3.3)

H_{0}<0\quad\text{and, more particularly,}\quad H_{0}<M\left(\frac{1-n}{n}\right)

for fixed
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
n\geq 2
, then

(3.4)

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}}u(0,t)=-\infty

for positive
t^{*}
given by

t^{*}=-\frac{1}{M}\ln\left(1-\frac{M(1-n)}{nH_{0}}\right)

Proof.

Multiplying (
1.1
)i) by
x
, integrating over
x\in[0,1]
and using (
1.2
) gives

(3.5)

H^{\prime}(t)+\alpha(t)H(t)+\frac{n}{n-1}\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{2}=0

for

(3.6)

H(t)=-\int_{0}^{1}u_{x}(x,t)\,dx=u(0,t)

Now, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality implies that

H^{2}\leq\|u_{x}(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{2}

and so equation (
3.5
) yields the inequality

(3.7)

H^{\prime}(t)+\alpha(t)H(t)+\frac{n}{n-1}H^{2}\leq 0

Setting
f=H^{-1}
in (
3.7
) and using an integrating factor argument we obtain

\frac{d}{dt}\left(f(t)e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\,ds}\right)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)\,ds}

which gives, after integrating,

(3.8)

\frac{1}{H(t)}\geq\frac{n(1+H_{0}g(t))}{(n-1)H_{0}g^{\prime}(t)}

for
H_{0}=H(0)
and

(3.9)

g(t)=\frac{n}{n-1}\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\int_{0}^{s}\alpha(z)\,dz}\,ds

Next, from (
3.2
),

\alpha(t)\leq M

for all
t\in[0,\infty)
for some
M\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Consequently,

g^{\prime}(t)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}e^{-Mt}

and, after integrating,

g(t)\geq\frac{n}{n-1}\left(\frac{1-e^{-Mt}}{M}\right)

Using these inequalities on (
3.8
) we obtain

(3.10)

\frac{1}{H(t)}\geq\Lambda(t)

for

(3.11)

\Lambda(t)=\frac{nN(t)}{(n-1)^{2}MH_{0}g^{\prime}(t)}

and

N(t)=M(n-1)+nH_{0}(1-e^{-Mt})

Now, (
3.3
)ii) implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}N(t)=M(n-1)+nH_{0}<0

This, along with

N(0)=M(n-1)>0,\quad N^{\prime}(t)=nMH_{0}e^{-Mt}<0\quad\text{and}\quad N(t)\in C([0,\infty))

implies the existence of a finite time
t^{*}>0
such that

(3.12)

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}}N(t)=0

In turn, the above argument implies that
\Lambda(t)<0
when
t\in[0,t^{*})
and

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}}\Lambda(t)=0

Finally, (
3.7
) gives

H^{\prime}(t)+\alpha(t)H(t)\leq 0

which yields

(3.13)

H(t)\leq H_{0}\,e^{-\int_{0}^{t}\alpha(s)ds}

Since
H_{0}<0
, (
3.13
) implies that
H(t)<0
for as long as it exists. Thus

(3.14)

0\geq\frac{1}{H(t)}\geq\Lambda(t)

and the Theorem follows by letting
t
approach
t^{*}
in (
3.14
).

∎

4.
Finite-time Blowup with Smooth, unbounded Damping

In Theorem
3.1
of the previous section, finite-time blowup was established for smooth and bounded time-dependent damping. In Theorem
4.1
below we show that finite-time blowup is still possible with smooth but unbounded time-dependent damping.

Theorem 4.1
.

Consider the IBVP (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) for the generalized inviscid damped Proudman Johnson equation with initial data
u_{0}(x)\in C^{\infty}([0,1])
. Let
E_{1}(x)
be the exponential integral

(4.2)

E_{1}(x)=\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{e^{-t}}{t}dt,\qquad x>0

If

(4.3)

u_{0}(0)<-\frac{c(n-1)}{ne^{1/c}E_{1}(1/c)}

for
c=\alpha^{\prime}(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
n\geq 2
, then there exists smooth, unbounded damping
\alpha(t):[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
and a finite time
t^{*}>0
such that

(4.4)

\lim_{t\nearrow t^{*}}u(0,t)=-\infty

Proof.

Suppose

H_{0}=-\int_{0}^{1}u_{0}^{\prime}(x)\,dx=u_{0}(0)<0

From (
3.8
) and (
3.13
) we have that

(4.5)

0>\frac{1}{H(t)}\geq\frac{n(1+H_{0}g(t))}{(n-1)H_{0}g^{\prime}(t)}

for
g(t)
as in (
3.9
) and
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
n\geq 2
. Set

\alpha(t)=e^{ct}

for some
c\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Then, after some straightforward computations, we can write (
4.5
) as

(4.6)

0>\frac{1}{H(t)}\geq\frac{1+H_{0}\phi(n)\eta(t)}{e^{\frac{1}{c}}H_{0}e^{-\frac{e^{ct}}{c}}}

for

\phi(n)=\frac{ne^{\frac{1}{c}}}{c(n-1)}

and

\eta(t)=\int_{\frac{1}{c}}^{\frac{e^{ct}}{c}}\frac{e^{-u}}{u}\,du

Note that
\eta(0)=0
, while
\eta^{\prime}(t)>0
and
\eta^{\prime\prime}(t)<0
for all
t\in[0,\infty)
. Moreover, for the exponential integral
E_{1}(x)
as defined in (
4.2
), we have that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\eta(t)=E_{1}(1/c)

The above remarks, along with the fact that the denominator of the right hand-side of (
4.6
) is negative for all
t\in[0,\infty)
, imply that if
H_{0}=u_{0}(0)
satisfies (
4.3
), then there exists a finite time
t^{*}>0
such that (
4.4
) follows.

∎

5.
Conclusions and Open Questions

In this paper, we derived conditions on the initial data which guarantee the formation of singularities in finite time in solutions of the generalized inviscid Proudman-Johnson when smooth and bounded damping is incorporated into the system. Similarly, we have shown that finite-time blowup is also possible if the damping term is unbounded. It is unknown if solutions will persist for all time (instead of blowing up in finite time) if the initial data satisfies the opposite of inequality (
3.3
)ii) in Theorem
3.1
, namely, if

H_{0}\geq M\left(\frac{1-n}{n}\right)

It is also of interest to investigate how solutions to other fluid-related models such as the Boussinesq equations or the MHD equations behave under a homogeneous three-point boundary condition.

A First-Principles Study on Electronic, Thermodynamic, and Dielectric Properties of Monolayer Ca ( OH ) 2 \text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
Mehrdad Rostami Osanloo, Kolade A. Oyekan, William G. Vandenberghe ∗

Abstract
We perform first-principles calculations to explore electronic, thermodynamic, and dielectric properties of two-dimensional (2D) layered, alkaline-earth hydroxides Ca ( OH ) 2 \text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} . We calculate the lattice parameters, exfoliation energies, and phonon spectra of monolayers and also investigate the thermal properties of these monolayers such as Helmholtz free energy, heat capacity at constant volume, and entropy as a function of temperature. We employ Density Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT) to calculate the in-plane and out-of-plane static dielectric constant of the bulk and monolayer samples. We compute the bandgap and electron affinity values using the HSE06 functional and estimate the leakage current density of transistors with monolayer Ca ( OH ) 2 \text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} as dielectrics when combined with HfS 2 \text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and WS 2 \text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} , respectively. Our results show that bilayer Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} (EOT ∼ \sim 0.60 nm) with a lower solubility in water, offers higher out-of-plane dielectric constants and lower leakage currents than bilayer Ca ( OH ) 2 \text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} (EOT ∼ \sim 0.56 nm). Additionally, the out-of-plane dielectric constant, leakage current, and EOT of Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} outperform bilayer h-BN. We verify the applicability of Anderson’s rule, and conclude that bilayers of Ca ( OH ) 2 \text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and Mg ( OH ) 2 \text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} respectively paired with lattice-matched monolayer HfS 2 \text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} and WS 2 \text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}} are effective structural combinations that could lead to the development of innovative multi-functional Field Effect Transistors (FETs).

In this work, we perform accurate first-principles calculations to study the electronic, thermodynamic, and dielectric properties of two novel vdW materials:
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

We also use the HSE06 hybrid functional to calculate the bandgaps, electron affinities, and the effective masses of charge carriers in the monolayer. Computational Methods

We employ DFT as implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package (VASP)
[
69
]
and use the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) as proposed by Pedrow-Burke-Ernzerof (PBE) for the electron exchange and correlation functional
[
70
]
. We set the plane-wave basis energy cut-off to 520 eV for monolayer and bulk
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. The structural relaxations are continued until the force on each atom is less than
10^{-3}
eV/Å. For precise phonon and dielectric calculations, we set a tight energy convergence criteria of
10^{-8}
eV. We employ the same criteria for monolayer TMDs (
\text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
) as we used in our prior work in Ref
[
36
]
. To mesh the Brillouin Zone (BZ), 12
\times 12\times
12 and 12
\times 12\times
1 k-points grids are employed for the bulk and the monolayer structures, respectively. For the heterostructures, we used 15
\times 15\times
1 k-points grids for the structural relaxation and 6
\times 6\times
1 for the HSE06 bandgap calculation. The DFT-D3 approach of Grimme is used to account for interlayer van der Waals interactions
[
71
]
. We establish at least 15 Å vacuum between the monolayers to avoid any non-physical interactions between layers. Moreover, the exfoliation energy is calculated as the ratio of the difference in bulk and monolayer ground state energies to the surface area of the bulks
[
72
,
73
]
. We used the sumo code to plot band structures and Density of States (DOS)
[
74
]
.

To calculate phonon spectrum we use the open-source package; Phonopy
[
75
]
. The first-principles phonon calculations with a finite displacement method (FDM) are generated for a set of displacements
[
4
]
. We use a
4\times 4\times 1
supercell yielding a total of 80 atoms where the atomic displacement distance is
10^{-3}
Å. When the phonon frequencies over the Brillouin zone are computed, the Helmholtz free energy of the phonons under the harmonic approximation can be estimated using the canonical distribution in statistical mechanics, as detailed in Ref
[
75
,
76
]
. Once the phonon frequencies are calculated, we can use thermodynamic relations to determine the thermodynamic properties of the system:

Conclusion

We investigated two novel 2D layered materials,
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

n
-MOS devices. For each material, we calculated the exfoliation energy, band offset, phonon spectrum, thermodynamic properties, EOT, and leakage current. The exfoliation energies confirm that both materials are mechanically exfoliable and can be isolated in layers. The strictly positive phonon spectra clearly show the thermodynamic stability of the monolayers. We studied the thermodynamic properties and observed that the smaller free energy of
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
makes its surface more favorable applications compared to
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
for industrial applications. We also used DFPT to calculate the in-plane and out-of-plane macroscopic dielectric constants. Although the in-plane static dielectric constant of monolayer
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
has relatively a higher dielectric constant (6.40) than
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
(6.33). We calculated the leakage current and the EOT for each material to evaluate its performance as a gate dielectric when combined with TMD channel materials. Our calculations show that bilayer
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

/
\text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
heterostructure. While other layered dielectrics like LaOCl could provide an even better performance,
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/
\text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
heterostructure are in good agreement with the calculated HSE bandgap for
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and its associated TMD channel,
\text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
. The band offset in
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/
\text{HfS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
/
\text{WS}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
heterostructures using Anderson’s rule are 1.20 eV and 1.99 eV, while we calculate a 0.97 eV, and 2.11 eV band offset for monolayer
\text{Ca}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
and
\text{Mg}\text{(}\text{OH}\text{)}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
when combined with

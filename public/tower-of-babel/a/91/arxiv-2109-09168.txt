Inner functions of matrix argument
and conjugacy classes in unitary groups

Abstract




Denote by
\mathrm{B}_{n}
the set of complex square matrices of order
n
, whose Euclidean operator norms
are
<1
. Its Shilov boundary is the set
\operatorname{U}(n)
of all unitary matrices. A holomorphic map
\mathrm{B}_{m}\to\mathrm{B}_{n}

is inner if it sends
\operatorname{U}(m)
to
\operatorname{U}(n)
. On the other hand we consider a group
\operatorname{U}(n+mj)

and its subgroup
\operatorname{U}(j)
embedded to
\operatorname{U}(n+mj)
in a block-diagonal way (
m
blocks
\operatorname{U}(j)
and a unit block of size
n
). For any conjugacy class of
\operatorname{U}(n+mj)

with respect to
\operatorname{U}(j)
we assign a ’characteristic function’, which is a rational inner map
\mathrm{B}_{m}\to\mathrm{B}_{n}
. We show that the class of inner functions, which can be obtained as
’characteristic functions’, is closed with respect to natural operations
as pointwise direct sums, pointwise products, compositions, substitutions to finite-dimensional representations
of general linear groups, etc. We also describe explicitly the corresponding operations on conjugacy classes.

2
Preliminaries

2.1. Linear fractional maps.
We realize the
pseudo-unitary group

\operatorname{U}(n,n)
as the group of all complex block matrices

g=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}
of size
(n+n)
satisfying the condition

g\begin{pmatrix}-1_{n}&0\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}g^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}-1_{n}&0\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}.

For each
g\in\operatorname{U}(n,n)
we consider the following linear fractional transformation
of the space
\operatorname{Mat}(n)
:

\gamma[g;z]=(A+zC)^{-1}(B+zD).

(2.1)

Such transformations send the matrix ball
\mathrm{B}_{n}
to itself
(see, e.g.,
[
21
]
, §6,
[
15
]
, Sect. 2.3). This action of
\operatorname{U}(n,n)
is transitive, the stabilizer of the point
z=0

consists of matrices
\begin{pmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{pmatrix}
, where
a\in\operatorname{U}(n)
,
d\in\operatorname{U}(n)
.
So
\mathrm{B}_{n}
is a homogeneous space

\mathrm{B}_{n}=\operatorname{U}(n,n)/\bigl(\operatorname{U}(n)\times\operatorname{U}(n)\bigr).

2.2. Realization of
\mathrm{B}_{n}
as a domain in a Grassmannian.

Consider the pseudo-Euclidean space

V^{2n}=V_{-}^{n}\oplus V_{+}^{n}:={\mathbb{C}}^{n}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}^{n}

equipped with the Hermitian form
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}_{n}
determined by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}-1_{n}&0\\
0&1_{n}\end{pmatrix}
. Our group
\operatorname{U}(n,n)
preserves this form.

Denote
\operatorname{Gr}(n)
the Grassmanian of all
n
-dimensional subspaces in
V^{2n}
.
We say that a subspace

is
negative
(

\mathcal{M}
is
negative (resp., semi-negative) defined on
L
. A subspace

is

if the form
\mathcal{M}
is zero on
L
. We denote the corresponding subsets in
the Grassmannian by

\operatorname{Gr}^{<0}(n),\qquad\operatorname{Gr}^{\leqslant 0}(n),\qquad\operatorname{Gr}^{0}(n)

respectively.

For any linear map

z:V_{-}^{n}\to V_{+}^{n}
we consider the
n
-dimensional space
L[z]
consisting of vectors of the
form
(v_{-},v_{-}z)
. If
z\in\mathrm{B}_{n}
, then the form
\mathcal{M}
is negative
on
L(z)
. Vice versa, any
n
-dimensional negative subspace in

has the form

L(z)
for some

. Formula (

) corresponds to the natural

2.4. Linear relations.

Let

. For

as the set of all

, for which
there exists
w\in W
satisfying

,
w\oplus u\in Q
.

Let

be a linear subspace. The subspace

consists
of

v\oplus w\in Y
. We can consider

as a product of linear relations.

For a linear relation

we define

as the intersection
Y\cap V
;

—

\operatorname{dom}Y\subset V
is the image of the projection
Y
to
V
along

;

—

\operatorname{im}Y\subset W
is the image of projection of
Y
to
W
;

—

\operatorname{indef}Y

A product of linear relations

2.5. Isotropic category.

Objects of the
isotropic category
, see
[
15
]
, Sect. 2.10, are spaces

where

, 1, 2, ….
A morphism

is a linear relation
Y:V^{2n}\rightrightarrows V^{2m}

satisfying conditions:

1) if

, then
\mathcal{M}_{n}(v,v)=\mathcal{M}_{m}(v^{\prime},v^{\prime})
;

2)
\dim P
is maximal possible, i.e.,
\dim P=m+n
.

A product of morphism is the product of linear relations.
The group of automorphisms of

is
\operatorname{U}(n,n)
.

Emphasize that the product has points of discontinuity.

Equip

with the difference of Hermitian forms in this space,

\mathcal{M}_{m,n}(v\oplus w,v^{\prime}\oplus w^{\prime}):=\mathcal{M}_{n}(v,v^{\prime})-\mathcal{M}_{m}(w,w^{\prime}).

Then the subspace
V_{-}^{n}\oplus V_{+}^{m}\subset V^{2n}\oplus V^{2m}
is negative with respect
to the form

and the subspace
V_{+}^{n}\oplus V_{-}^{m}\subset V^{2n}\oplus V^{2m}

is positive. So we can apply above reasoning and get:

is isotropic if and only if

is a graph
of a unitary operator

.

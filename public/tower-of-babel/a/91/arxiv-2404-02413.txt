1.
Introduction

The Stirling number
{n\brace k}
counts the number of ways to partition a set of
n
objects into
k
nonempty subsets. The Bell polynomials are defined by
\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k}
, with the Bell numbers given by
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1)
. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the
r
-Stirling number
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}
enumerates the number of ways to partition a set of
n+r
objects into
k+r
nonempty subsets such that the first
r
elements are in distinct subsets.
The
r
-Bell polynomials are defined by
\phi_{n,r}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}x^{k}
, with the
r
-Bell numbers given by
\phi_{n,r}=\phi_{n,r}(1)
. Assume that
Y
is a random variable whose moment generating function exists in some neighbourhood of the origin. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1)
, with
S_{0}=0
. Then we define the probabilistic
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}
, as a probabilistic extension of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}
. The probabilistic
r
-Bell polynomials associated with
Y
are defined by
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}x^{k}
, with
the probabilistic
r
-Bell numbers associated with
Y
given by
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}=\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(1)
.

Spivey found the following recurrence relation on the Bell numbers:

\phi_{n+k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}j^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}{n\brace j}\phi_{l}.

The aim of this paper is to generalize the relation for the Bell numbers to that for the probabilistic
r
-Bell polynomials associated with
Y
. Indeed, we obtain

(1)

\phi_{j+n,r}^{Y}(y)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}r^{n-l}\sum_{k=0}^{l}\sum_{m=0}^{j}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\binom{j}{m}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=l}\binom{l}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\Big[S_{k}^{j-m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\Big]\phi_{m,r}^{Y}(y),

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.

By using the simple fact (
16
), we show (
1
), first for
\phi_{n}^{Y}=\phi_{n,0}^{Y}(1)
, second for
\phi_{n}^{Y}(y)=\phi_{n,0}^{Y}(y)
, and finally for
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(y)
. We note here that
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n,0}^{1}(1)
. For the rest of this section, we recall the facts that are needed throughout this paper.

For
n\geq 0
, the Stirling numbers of the second kind are defined by

(2)

x^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [1-26]),

where
(x)_{0}=1,\ (x)_{k}=x(x-1)\cdots(x-k+1),\ (k\geq 1)
.

The Bell numbers are given by

(3)

\phi_{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [15,21]).

The Bell polynomials are defined by

(4)

e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
4
), we note that

(5)

\phi_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [7,8,10,15,21]).

Note that, for
x=1
,
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n}(1)
are the Bell numbers.

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
n\geq 0
, the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind are given by

(6)

(x+r)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}(x)_{k},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [20]).

From (
6
), we note that

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n}{n+r\brace l+r}_{r}(x)_{l}

\displaystyle=(x+r)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{n-k}x^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}r^{n-k}\sum_{l=0}^{k}{k\brace l}(x)_{l}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\sum_{k=l}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{k\brace l}r^{n-k}(x)_{l}.

Thus, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
7
), we get

(8)

{n+r\brace l+r}_{r}=\sum_{k=l}^{n}\binom{n}{k}{k\brace l}r^{n-k},\quad(n\geq l\geq 0).

It is known that the Bell numbers satisfy the recurrence relation

(9)

\phi_{n+1}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\phi_{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,18]).

In 2008, Spivey discovered the following remarkable relation

(10)

\phi_{n+k}=\sum_{l=0}^{k}\sum_{j=0}^{n}j^{k-l}\binom{k}{l}{n\brace j}\phi_{l},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [24]).

The
r
-Bell numbers are defined by

(11)

\phi_{n,r}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-20]).

The
r
-Bell polynomials are given by

(12)

e^{rt}e^{x(e^{t}-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [13-20]).

Thus, by (
12
), we get

(13)

\phi_{n,r}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [6,8,16,17]).

Note that, for
x=1
,
\phi_{n,r}=\phi_{n,r}(1)
are the
r
-Bell numbers.

For any integer
k\geq 0
, the partial Bell polynomials are given by

(14)

\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}x_{m}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\bigg)^{k}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}B_{n,k}\big(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1}\big)\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [10,11,14]),

where

(15)

\displaystyle B_{n,k}(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n-k+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l_{1}+\cdots+l_{n-k+1}\\
l_{1}+2l_{2}+\cdots+(n-k+1)l_{n-k+1}=n\end{subarray}}\frac{n!}{l_{1}!l_{2}!\cdots l_{n-k+1}!}\bigg(\frac{x_{1}}{1!}\bigg)^{l_{1}}\bigg(\frac{x_{2}}{2!}\bigg)^{l_{2}}\cdots\bigg(\frac{x_{n-k+1}}{(n-k+1)!}\bigg)^{l_{n-k+1}}.

We will use the following simple fact

(16)

\displaystyle f(x+t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(x)}{n!}(x+t-x)^{n}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(n)}(x)}{n!}t^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}D_{x}^{n}}{n!}f(x)=e^{tD_{x}}f(x),

where
D_{x}=\frac{d}{dx}
.

Assume that
Y
is a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
,

E\big[e^{tY}\big]=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{n}]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(|t|<r),

exists for some
r>0
(see [3,14,22]). Here
E
denotes the mathematical expectation. Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let
S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\ (k\geq 1)
, with
S_{0}=0
.

Then we recall the probabilistic Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
,
{n\brace k}_{Y}
, which are defined in terms of the
n
-th moments of
S_{l},\ (l=0,1,2,\dots,k)
:

(17)

{n\brace k}_{Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l=0}^{k}\binom{k}{l}(-1)^{k-l}E\big[S_{l}^{n}\big],\quad(0\leq k\leq n),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [3,15]).

The probabilistic Bell polynomials associated with
Y
are defined by

(18)

\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n\brace k}_{Y}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0),\quad(\mathrm{see}\ [14,23]).

From [14], we note that the probabilistic Bell polynomials associated with
Y
satisfy the recurrence relation

(19)

\phi_{n+1}^{Y}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E\big[Y^{k+1}\big]\phi_{n-k}^{Y}(x).

2.
Generalization of Spivey’s recurrence relation

Let
(Y_{j})_{j\geq 1}
be a sequence of mutually independent copies of the random variable
Y
, and let

S_{0}=0,\quad S_{k}=Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k},\quad(k\in\mathbb{N}).

For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the
probabilistic
r
-Stirling numbers of the second kind associated with
Y
by

(20)

\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E[e^{tY}]-1\Big)^{k}e^{rt}=\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!},\quad(k\geq 0).

When
Y=1
,
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}={n+r\brace k+r}_{r},\ (n\geq k\geq 0)
.

From (
20
), we note that

(21)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yt}\big]-1\Big)^{k}e^{rt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[e^{(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{j})t}\big]e^{rt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[e^{(S_{j}+r)t}\big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[(S_{j}+r)^{n}\big]\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
21
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n\geq k\geq 0
, we have

{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{k}\binom{k}{j}(-1)^{k-j}E\big[(S_{j}+r)^{n}\big].

In view of (
13
), we define the
probabilistic
r
-Bell polynomials associated with
Y
by

(22)

\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}x^{k},\quad(n\geq 0).

When
Y=1
,
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)=\phi_{n,r}(x)
. For
x=1
,
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}=\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(1)
are called the probabilistic
r
-Bell numbers associated with
Y
.

From (
22
), we have

(23)

\displaystyle e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}e^{rt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k}}{k!}\Big(E[e^{Yt}]-1\Big)^{k}e^{rt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}x^{k}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}x^{k}{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by (
23
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2
.

The generating function of the probabilistic
r
-Bell polynomials associated with
Y
is given by

(24)

e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}e^{rt}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

From (
24
), we have

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}e^{rt}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}xE[Y^{j}]\frac{t^{j}}{j!}\bigg)^{k}e^{rt}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{l}B_{l,k}\Big(xE[Y],xE[Y^{2}],\dots,xE[Y^{l-k+1}]\Big)\frac{t^{l}}{l!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}r^{m}\frac{t^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\sum_{k=0}^{l}B_{l,k}\Big(xE[Y],xE[Y^{2}],\dots,xE[Y^{l-k+1}]\Big)r^{n-l}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
25
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)=\sum_{l=0}^{n}\binom{n}{l}\sum_{k=0}^{l}B_{l,k}\Big(xE[Y],xE[Y^{2}],\dots,xE[Y^{l-k+1}]\Big)r^{n-l}.

By (
24
), we get

(26)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n+1,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg(e^{x(E[e^{tY}]-1)}e^{rt}\bigg)

\displaystyle=xE[Ye^{Yt}]e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}e^{rt}+re^{x(E[e^{tY}]-1)}e^{rt}

\displaystyle=x\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}E[Y^{k+1}]\frac{t^{k}}{k!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{m}}{m!}+r\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E[Y^{k+1}]\phi_{n-k,r}^{Y}(x)+r\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)\bigg)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Therefore, by comparing the coefficients on both sides of (
26
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

For
n\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{n+1,r}^{Y}(x)=x\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}E[Y^{k+1}]\phi_{n-k,r}^{Y}(x)+r\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x).

From (
18
), we note that

(27)

e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{Y}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}.

By using (
16
), we have

(28)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}

\displaystyle=e^{E[e^{Y(x+t)}]-1}

\displaystyle=e^{E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)+e^{Yx}]-1}

\displaystyle=e^{E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}.

Now, we observe that

(29)

\displaystyle e^{E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)\big]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}E\Big[e^{(Y_{1}+\cdots+Y_{k})x}(e^{Y_{1}t}-1)(e^{Y_{2}t}-1)\cdots(e^{Y_{k}t}-1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\big[e^{S_{k}x}Y_{1}^{l_{1}}Y_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots Y_{k}^{l_{k}}\big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\bigg[e^{S_{k}x}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.
From (
27
), (
28
) and (
29
), we note that

(30)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}=e^{E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E\bigg[S_{k}^{j}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\frac{x^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}^{Y}\frac{x^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\bigg[S_{k}^{l-m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\phi_{m}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\frac{x^{l}}{l!},

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.
On the other hand, by (
27
), we get

(31)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{E[e^{Yx}]-1}=e^{tD_{x}}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l}^{Y}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}D_{x}^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l}^{Y}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l+n}^{Y}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}.

Therefore, by (
30
) and (
31
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
n,l\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{l+n}^{Y}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\bigg[S_{k}^{l-m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\phi_{m}^{Y},

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.

We note here that this reduces to the Spivey’s relation (
10
) if
Y=1
.

\displaystyle\phi_{l+n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}k^{l-m}\phi_{m}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}{n\brace k}k^{l-m}\phi_{m}.

We extend Theorem 2.5 to the probabilistic Bell polynomials associated with
Y
, which are given by (see (
18
), Theorem 2.2)

(32)

e^{x(E[e^{Yt}]-1)}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{Y}(x)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

Observe that

(33)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{y(E[e^{Y(x+t)}]-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{y(E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)+E[e^{Yx}]-1)}

\displaystyle=e^{yE[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}.

Now, we note that

(34)

\displaystyle e^{yE[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\Big(E\big[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)\big]\Big)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}E\Big[e^{(Y_{1}+Y_{2}+\cdots+Y_{k})x}(e^{Y_{1}t}-1)(e^{Y_{2}t}-1)\cdots(e^{Y_{k}t}-1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}E\Big[e^{S_{k}x}(e^{Y_{1}t}-1)(e^{Y_{2}t}-1)\cdots(e^{Y_{k}t}-1)\Big]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\Big[e^{S_{k}x}Y_{1}^{l_{1}}Y_{2}^{l_{2}}\cdots Y_{k}^{l_{k}}\Big]\frac{t^{n}}{n!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigg(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\bigg[e^{S_{k}x}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\frac{t^{n}}{n!},

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.
From (
33
) and (
34
), we have

(35)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}=e^{yE[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)]}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}E\bigg[S_{k}^{j}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\frac{x^{j}}{j!}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\phi_{m}^{Y}(y)\frac{x^{m}}{m!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}\binom{l}{m}E\bigg[S_{k}^{l-m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\phi_{m}^{Y}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\frac{x^{l}}{l!},

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.
On the other hand, by (
32
), we get

(36)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{D_{x}^{n}}{n!}t^{n}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l}^{Y}(y)\frac{x^{l}}{l!}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\phi_{l+n}^{Y}(y)\frac{t^{n}}{n!}\frac{x^{l}}{l!}.

Therefore, by (
35
) and (
36
), we obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 2.6
.

For
n,l\geq 0
, we have

\phi_{l+y}^{Y}(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}\frac{y^{k}}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}E\bigg[S_{k}^{l-m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}Y_{i}^{l_{i}}\bigg]\phi_{m}^{Y}(y),

where
l_{1},l_{2},\dots l_{k}
are positive integers.

When
Y=1
, we note that

(37)

\displaystyle\phi_{l+n}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}y^{k}\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{l_{1}+\cdots+l_{k}=n}\binom{n}{l_{1},\dots,l_{k}}k^{l-m}\phi_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sum_{m=0}^{l}\binom{l}{m}y^{k}{n\brace k}k^{l-m}\phi_{m}(y),

where
l,n
are nonnegative integers. Note that (
37
) reduces to the Spivey’s relation (
10
) by letting
y=1
.

From (
16
) and (
24
), we note that

(38)

\displaystyle e^{tD_{x}}\Big(e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}e^{rx}\Big)=e^{y(E[e^{Y(x+t)}]-1)}e^{r(x+t)}

\displaystyle=e^{y(E[e^{Yx}(e^{Yt}-1)])}e^{rt}e^{y(E[e^{Yx}]-1)}e^{rx}

3.
Conclusion

Let
Y
be a random variable such that the moment generating function of
Y
exists in a neighborhood of the origin. We introduced probabilistic extensions of the
r
-Stirling numbers of the second and the
r
-Bell polynomials, namely the probabilistic
r
-Stirling numbers of the second associated with
Y
,
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}
and the probabilistic
r
-Bell polynomials associated with
Y
,
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(y)
. Here the latter is a natural polynomial extension of the former. Spivey found a recurrence relation for the Bell numbers
\phi_{n}
. In this paper, we generalized the recurrence relation for
\phi_{n}=\phi_{n,0}^{1}(1)
to that for
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(y)
by utilizing the simple fact (
16
).

In more detail, we obtained for
{n+r\brace k+r}_{r,Y}
an explicit expression in terms of the
n
th moments of
S_{j}+r,\,\,(0\leq j\leq k)
in Theorem 2.1. We derived for
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(x)
the generating function, a finite sum expression involving the incomplete Bell polynomials and a recurrence relation, respectively in Theorem 2.2, Theorem 2.3 and Theorem 2.4. Then the relation for the Bell numbers in (
10
) was generalized to first that for
\phi_{n}^{Y}=\phi_{n,0}^{Y}(1)
, second that for
\phi_{n}^{Y}(y)=\phi_{n,0}^{Y}(y)
, and finally that for
\phi_{n,r}^{Y}(y)
, respectively in Theorem 2.5, Theorem 2.6 and Theorem 2.7.

As one of our future projects, we would like to continue to study probabilistic extensions of many special polynomials and numbers and to find their applications to physics, science and engineering as well as to mathematics.

The EKOR stratification on the Siegel modular variety with parahoric level structure
Manuel Hoff

Abstract




Abstract.
We study the arithmetic geometry of the reduction modulo
p
of the Siegel modular variety with parahoric level structure.
We realize the EKOR stratification on this variety as the fibers of a smooth morphism into an algebraic stack parametrizing homogeneously polarized chains of certain truncated displays.

Keywords.
Shimura varieties at parahoric level

1. Introduction

Fix a rational prime
p
, a positive integer
g
, an auxiliary integer
N\geq 3
that is not divisible by
p
and a non-empty subset
J\subseteq\mathbf{Z}
with
J+2g\mathbf{Z}=J
and
-J=J
.
Then we are interested in studying the
Siegel modular variety with parahoric level structure

\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}

over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
.
This is a quasi-projective scheme that parametrizes certain polarized chains of type
J
of
g
-dimensional Abelian varieties with full level
N
structure; its moduli description was first given by De Jong
[
dJo93
]

and then in full generality by Rapoport and Zink
[
RZ96
]
.
The scheme
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
is the prime example of an integral model of a
Shimura variety
at a place of parahoric bad reduction.
While the generic fiber of
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
is smooth, its special fiber typically is singular.
This is related to the fact that the
p
-torsion of an Abelian variety in characteristic
p
is not étale.

Now fix an algebraic closure
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
of
\mathbf{F}_{p}
, let
\breve{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}\coloneqq W(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
be the ring of
p
-typical Witt vectors of
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
, and write
\breve{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\coloneqq\breve{\mathbf{Z}}_{p}[1/p]
for its fraction field.
Denote by
\sigma
the Frobenius on
\breve{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}
.
Also fix a symplectic
\mathbf{Q}_{p}
-vector space
V
of dimension
2g
and a self-dual lattice chain
(\Lambda_{i})_{i\in J}
in
V
.
Then there exists a natural map, called the
central leaves map
,

\Upsilon\colon\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash\GSp(V)(\breve{\mathbf{Q}}_{p});

here
\breve{K}\subseteq\GSp(V)(\breve{\mathbf{Q}}_{p})
is the stabilizer of the lattice chain
(\Lambda_{i})_{i}
, and modding out by
\breve{K}_{\sigma}
is notation for taking the quotient by the
\sigma
-conjugation action
g\colon x\mapsto gx\sigma(g)^{-1}
.
It is roughly given by sending a polarized chain of Abelian varieties to the twisted conjugacy class corresponding to the Frobenius
\Phi
of the associated rational Dieudonné module; see the work of Oort
[
Oor04
]
and also He and Rapoport
[
HR16
]
.
The image of this map is given by
\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash X
, where

X=\bigcupop\displaylimits_{w\in\Adm_{g,J}}\breve{K}w\breve{K}\subseteq\GSp(V)\left(\breve{\mathbf{Q}}_{p}\right)

is a finite union of double cosets that is indexed over the so-called
admissible set

\Adm_{g,J}
.

The fibers of the composition

\lambda\colon\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\right)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Upsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash X\longrightarrow\breve{K}\backslash X/\breve{K}\cong\Adm_{g,J}

define a decomposition of
(\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N})_{\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}}
into smooth locally closed subschemes, the so-called
Kottwitz–Rapoport
(
KR
)
stratification
.
In fact Rapoport and Zink construct the following data:

•

a flat projective scheme
\mathbb{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}
over
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
with an action of the parahoric
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-group scheme
\mathcal{G}
for
\GSp(V)
given by the lattice chain
(\Lambda_{i})_{i}
,

•

a smooth morphism

\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}\longrightarrow\left[\mathcal{G}\backslash\mathbb{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}\right]

that parametrizes the Hodge filtration in the de Rham cohomology of a polarized chain of Abelian varieties.

The scheme
\mathbb{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is called the
local model
; it parametrizes isotropic chains of
g
-dimensional subspaces
C_{i}\subseteq\Lambda_{i}
and satisfies
\mathbb{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})=\breve{K}\backslash X
. One recovers the map
\lambda
by evaluating the smooth morphism above on
\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}
-points; see
[
HR16
, Section 7(ii)]
.

He and Rapoport also consider the composition

\upsilon\colon\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash X\longrightarrow\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash(\breve{K}\backslash X),

where
\breve{K}_{1}\subseteq\breve{K}
is the pro-unipotent radical.
They observe that the codomain of
\upsilon
is a finite set and call its fibers
Ekedahl–Kottwitz–Oort–Rapoport
(
EKOR
)
strata
.

In the hyperspecial case
J=2g\mathbf{Z}
, the EKOR stratification is also just called the
Ekedahl–Oort
(
EO
)
stratification
and was first considered by Oort
[
Oor01
]
.
Two points in
\mathcal{A}_{g,2g\mathbf{Z},N}(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p})
given by two (principally polarized) Abelian varieties lie in the same EO stratum if and only if their
p
-torsion group schemes are isomorphic.
Viehmann and Wedhorn
[
VW13
]
realize the EO stratification as the fibers of a smooth morphism from
(\mathcal{A}_{g,2g\mathbf{Z},N})_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}
into an algebraic stack that parametrizes
F
-zips in the sense of Moonen and Wedhorn
[
MW04
]
with symplectic structure.

Question
.

Is it possible for general
J
to realize the map
\upsilon
, or maybe even
\Upsilon
, as a smooth morphism from
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
into some naturally defined algebraic stack?

The existence of such a smooth morphism would in particular give a new proof of the smoothness of the EKOR strata and the closure relations between them.
More importantly, it also provides a tool that may be applied to further study the geometry of
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
.

Let us give an overview of results that have been obtained so far (and that we are aware of), also considering more general Shimura varieties than the Siegel modular variety:

•

Moonen and Wedhorn
[
MW04
]
introduce the notion of an
F
-zip that is a characteristic
p
analogue of the notion of a Hodge structure.
Given an Abelian variety
A
over some
\mathbf{F}_{p}
-algebra
R
, the de Rham cohomology
H^{1}_{\mathrm{dR}}(A/R)
naturally carries the structure of an
F
-zip.
If
R
is perfect, then the datum of this
F
-zip is equivalent to the datum of the Dieudonné module of the
p
-torsion
A[p]
.
Pink, Wedhorn and Ziegler
[
PWZ15
]
define a group-theoretic version of the notion of an
F
-zip.

Viehmann and Wedhorn
[
VW13
]
define a moduli stack of
F
-zips with polarization and endomorphism structure in a PEL-type situation with hyperspecial level structure.
They construct a morphism from the special fiber of the associated Shimura variety to this stack, parametrizing the EO stratification.
They then show that this morphism is flat and use this to show that the EO strata are non-empty and quasi-affine and to compute their dimension.
The smoothness of the EO strata was already shown by Vasiu
[
Vas08
]
.

Zhang
[
Zha18
]
constructs a morphism from the special fiber of the Kisin integral model, see
[
Kis10
]
, of a Hodge-type Shimura variety to a stack of group-theoretic zips.
They then show that this morphism is smooth and thus gives an EO stratification with the desired properties.
Shen and Zhang
[
SZ22
]
later generalize this to Shimura varieties of Abelian type.

Shen, Yu and Zhang
[
SYZ21
]
further generalize this to Shimura varieties of Abelian type at parahoric level, where the construction of integral models is due to Kisin and Pappas; see
[
KP18
]
.
However, they only construct a morphism from each KR stratum of the special fiber of the Shimura variety into a certain stack of group-theoretic zips, parametrizing the EKOR strata contained in this KR stratum.
Still, they show that this morphism is smooth, thus establishing the smoothness of the EKOR strata.

Hesse
[
Hes20
]
considers an explicit moduli stack of polarized chains of
F
-zips and constructs a morphism from the Siegel modular variety into this stack.
However, it appears that such a morphism is not well behaved
in the general parahoric situation; see
Remark

3.17
.

•

Xiao and Zhu
[
XZ17
]
consider a perfect moduli stack of mixed characteristic local shtukas as well as restricted versions in a hyperspecial situation.
For a Shimura variety of Hodge type, they construct a morphism from the perfection of its special fiber into the stack of local shtukas and show that it realizes the central leaves map.
They state that the induced morphism to the moduli stack of restricted local shtukas is perfectly smooth, see
[
XZ17
, Proposition 7.2.4]
, but the proof appears to be incomplete because the diagram used there is not commutative as required.

Shen, Yu and Zhang
[
SYZ21
]
again generalize this to the parahoric situation.
Similarly to Xiao and Zhu, they construct a morphism from the perfected special fiber of the Shimura variety to their stack of local shtukas and state that the induced morphism to the moduli stack of restricted local shtukas is perfectly smooth, but their proof, see
[
SYZ21
, Theorem 4.4.3]
, employs a similar non-commutative diagram.
This issue is addressed in the errata
[
SYZ24
]
, where Shen, Yu and Zhang state that their proof works with slight modifications; it is not clear to the author why the argument offered in the errata is sufficient to complete the proof.

•

Zink
[
Zin02
]
introduces the notion of a display that is a non-perfect version of the notion of a Dieudonné module.
Bültel and Pappas
[
BP18
]
define a group-theoretic version; this gives a deperfection of the notion of a local shtuka from
[
XZ17
]
in the hyperspecial situation.
In a parahoric Hodge-type situation, Pappas
[
Pap23
]
also gives a definition of group-theoretic displays, however only over
p
-torsion-free
p
-adic rings.
Pappas also constructs a group-theoretic display on the
p
-completion of a Kisin–Pappas integral model.

1.1. Overview

Let us explain the content of the present work.
The letter
R
denotes a
p
-nilpotent ring.

We start by recalling the definition of a (3n-)display in the sense of
[
Zin02
]
.

Definition 1.1

(
cf.
Definition
2.9
)
.

Let
0\leq d\leq h
be integers.
Then a
display of type
(h,d)
over
R
is a tuple
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
that is given as follows:

•

M
is a finite projective
W(R)
-module of rank
h
.

•

M_{1}\subseteq M
is a
W(R)
-submodule containing
I_{R}M
and such that
M_{1}/I_{R}M\subseteq M/I_{R}M
is a direct summand of rank
d
.

•

\Psi\colon\widetilde{M_{1}}\to M
is an isomorphism of
W(R)
-modules that we call the
divided Frobenius
.

Here
W(R)
denotes the ring of Witt vectors of
R
,
I_{R}
denotes the kernel of the projection
W(R)\to R
, and the object
\widetilde{M_{1}}
is a certain finite projective
W(R)
-module attached to
(M,M_{1})
; see
Definition

2.7
for more details.
If
R
is a perfect ring of characteristic
p
, then
\widetilde{M_{1}}
agrees with the Frobenius twist
M_{1}^{\sigma}
.

The category of displays carries a natural duality and an action of the symmetric monoidal category of tuples
(I,\iota)
consisting of an invertible
W(R)
-module
I
and an isomorphism
\iota\colon I^{\sigma}\to I
.

For positive integers
m
and
n
with
m\geq n+1
, we define the notion of an
(m,n)
-truncated display that is inspired by the restricted local shtukas from
[
XZ17
, Definition 5.3.1]
.
The word
“truncated”
refers to the use of truncated Witt vectors; the numbers
m
and
n
roughly measure how truncated the module
M
and the divided Frobenius
\Psi
are.
One should note that this notion of an
(m,n)
-truncated display is different from the notion of an
n
-truncated display as defined by Lau and Zink in
[
LZ18
]
; see
Remark

2.14
for a comparison.
For us it will be crucial to work with the
(m,n)
-truncated objects; see
Remark

3.17
.

The following theorem gives a relation between displays and
p
-divisible groups.

Theorem 1.2

(
cf.

[
Lau13
, Theorem 5.1]
,
Theorem

2.24
)
.

There is a natural functor

\mathbf{D}\colon\left\{\text{$p$-divisible groups of height $h$ and dimension $d$ over $R$}\right\}^{\mathrm{op}}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{displays of type $(h,d)$ over $R$}\right\}.

that restricts to an equivalence between formal
p
-divisible groups and
F
-nilpotent displays.

As we are interested in studying the moduli space
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
of polarized chains of Abelian varieties, it makes sense to make the following definition.

Definition 1.3

(
cf.

Definition

3.9
)
.

A
homogeneously polarized chain of displays of type
(g,J)
over
R
is a tuple

\left((M_{i})_{i},\left(\rho_{i,j}\right)_{i,j},(\theta_{i})_{i},(M_{i,1})_{i},({}_{i})_{i},I,\iota,(\lambda_{i})_{i}\right)

that is given as follows:

•

((M_{i},M_{i,1},{}_{i})_{i},(\rho_{i,j})_{i,j})
is a diagram of shape
J
in the category of displays of type
(2g,J)
such that the homomorphism of
R
-modules

\rho_{i,j}\colon R\otimes_{W(R)}M_{i}\to R\otimes_{W(R)}M_{j}

is of constant rank
2g-(j-i)
for all
i\leq j\leq i+2g
.

•

\theta_{i}\colon(M_{i},M_{i,1},{}_{i})\to(M_{i+2g},M_{i+2g,1},{}_{i+2g})
is an isomorphism such that we have the compatibilities
\theta_{j}\circ\rho_{i,j}=\rho_{i+2g,j+2g}\circ\theta_{i}
and
\rho_{i,i+2g}=p\theta_{i}
.

•

(I,\iota)
is as in
Definition

1.1
.

•

\lambda_{i}\colon(M_{i},M_{i,1},{}_{i})\to(I,\iota)\otimes(M_{-i},M_{-i,1},{}_{-i})^{\vee}
is an antisymmetric isomorphism such that we have
\lambda_{j}\circ\rho_{i,j}=(\mathrm{id}_{(I,\iota)}\otimes\rho_{-j,-i}^{\vee})\circ\lambda_{i}
.

We again also give an
(m,n)
-truncated version of this definition.
If
R
is of characteristic
p
, then we allow
n
to take the additional value
1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt}
that can be thought of as being slightly smaller than
1
;
“
\mathrm{rdt}
”
refers to the term
“reductive quotient”
.
Roughly, the case
n=1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt}
corresponds to only having a divided Frobenius on the graded pieces of the
1
-truncated chain of modules; see
Definition

3.1
.

We then show that the stack
\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}^{(m,n)}
of
(m,n)
-truncated homogeneously polarized chains of displays over
\Spf(\mathbf{Z}_{p})
admits a quotient stack description.

Proposition 1.4

(
cf.

Proposition

3.21
)
.

There exists an equivalence

\mathsf{HPolChDisp}^{(m,n)}_{g,J}\longrightarrow\left[\left(L^{(m)}\mathcal{G}\right)_{\Delta}\backslash\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{loc},(n)}\right],

where we use the following notation:

•

L^{(m)}\mathcal{G}
denotes the
m
-truncated Witt vector positive loop group of
\mathcal{G}
; see
Section

2.1
.

•

\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is the
p
-completion of the local model
\mathbb{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}
, and
\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{loc},(n)}\to\mathrm{M}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a certain
L^{(m)}\mathcal{G}
-equivariant
L^{(n)}\mathcal{G}
-torsor; see
Definition

3.20
.

•

The subscript
\Delta
indicates that we take the quotient by the diagonal action.

Thus our definition of
\mathsf{HPolChDisp}^{(m,n)}_{g,J}
gives (up to a slight difference in normalization) a deperfection of the stack of parahoric restricted local shtukas from
[
SYZ21
, Section 4]
;
see
Remark

3.22
.
In particular, we obtain bijections

\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash X\quad\text{and}\quad\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}^{(m,1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt})}\left(\overline{\mathbf{F}}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow\breve{K}_{\sigma}\backslash\left(\breve{K}_{1}\backslash X\right).

Applying
Theorem

1.2
to the moduli description of
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
, we thus obtain a natural morphism

\Upsilon\colon\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}^{\wedge}\longrightarrow\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}

that realizes the central leaves map; see
Definition

4.3
. Here
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}^{\wedge}
denotes the
p
-completion of
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}
.
For any
m\geq 2
the composition

\left(\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}\right)_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Upsilon}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J,\mathbf{F}_{p}}\longrightarrow\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}^{(m,1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt})}

then realizes the map
\upsilon
parametrizing the EKOR stratification.
Our main result is now the following.

Theorem 1.5

(
cf.

Theorem

4.10
)
.

For every tuple of integers

(m,n)
with
n\neq 1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt}
, the natural morphism
\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}^{\wedge}\to\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}^{(m,n)}
is smooth.
Similarly, for every
m\geq 2
the morphism
(\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N})_{\mathbf{F}_{p}}\to\mathsf{HPolChDisp}_{g,J}^{(m,1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt})}
is smooth as well.

Strategy of proof.

By the Serre–Tate theorem, the smoothness of the morphism at a point of
\lvert\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}^{\wedge}\rvert
corresponding to a polarized chain of Abelian varieties only depends on the associated polarized chain of
p
-divisible groups.
Using
Theorem

1.2
we then show that the morphism is smooth along the formal locus,
i.e.
the locus of chains of Abelian varieties with formal
p
-divisible groups.
To obtain the smoothness in general, we then finally show that there are enough points in
\lvert\mathcal{A}_{g,J,N}^{\wedge}\rvert
that specialize into the formal locus; see
Corollary

4.8
for a precise statement.
∎

As a natural next step, it would be interesting to generalize our results to the case of a more general Shimura variety at parahoric level instead of the Siegel modular variety.
It could be expected that there exists a stack of
(\mathcal{G},\mu)
-displays for every datum
(\mathcal{G},\mu)
consisting of a parahoric
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-group scheme
\mathcal{G}
and a minuscule geometric conjugacy class
\mu
of cocharacters of the generic fiber of
\mathcal{G}
, as well as truncated versions that recover the definition from
[
BP18
]
in the hyperspecial case and give back our definition when the generic fiber of
\mathcal{G}
is either a general linear group or a group of symplectic similitudes.
In a situation where the local group datum
(\mathcal{G},\mu)
comes from a Shimura datum, there then should be a natural smooth morphism from the
p
-completion of the corresponding Shimura variety into the stack of truncated
(\mathcal{G},\mu)
-displays.
As already mentioned above, partial results in this direction have been achieved by Pappas in
[
Pap23
]
.

2. Pairs and displays

In this section we recall some of the theory of (not necessarily nilpotent) displays from
[
Zin02
]
.
We also develop an analogous theory of
(m,n)
-truncated displays that is inspired by the definition of
(m,n)
-restricted local shtukas given in
[
XZ17
]
.

The letter
R
denotes a
p
-nilpotent ring, and
(m,n)
denotes a tuple of positive integers with
m\geq n+1
.

2.1. Witt vectors

We use the following notation concerning Witt vectors:

•

We write
W(R)
for the ring of
(p
-typical
)
Witt vectors of
R
and
I_{R}\subseteq W(R)
for its
augmentation ideal
,
i.e.
the kernel of the projection
W(R)\to R
.

Similarly, we write
W_{n}(R)
for the ring of
n
-truncated Witt vectors of
R
and
I_{n,R}\subseteq W_{n}(R)
for its augmentation ideal.

•

We denote the
Witt vector Frobenius
on
W(R)
by
\sigma\colon W(R)\to W(R)
.
Given a
W(R)
-module
M
, we write
M^{\sigma}\coloneqq W(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W(R)}M
for its Frobenius twist.

Note that
\sigma
induces a ring homomorphism
\sigma\colon W_{m}(R)\to W_{n}(R)
on truncated Witt vectors (here it is crucial that
m\geq n+1
, at least when
R
is not of characteristic
p
).

When
R
is of characteristic
p
, we also write
\sigma\colon R\to R
for the
p
-power Frobenius on
R
.

•

We write

W(R)^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}\coloneqq\left\{x\in W(R)\mid\sigma(x)=x\right\}\subseteq W(R)

for the subring of
\sigma
-invariant elements.
Note that we have a natural isomorphism

\mathbf{Z}_{p}(R)\coloneqq\Cont\left(\lvert\Spec(R)\rvert,\mathbf{Z}_{p}\right)\longrightarrow W(R)^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}};

when
R
is of characteristic
p
, this follows from the chain of identifications

\mathbf{Z}_{p}(R)\cong W\left(\mathbf{F}_{p}(R)\right)\cong W\left(R^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}\right)\cong W(R)^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}},

and the general case follows because the reduction morphism
\mathbf{Z}_{p}(R)\to\mathbf{Z}_{p}(R/pR)
is an isomorphism and the
\sigma
-stable ideal
\ker(W(R)\to W(R/pR))\subseteq W(R)
is killed by a power of
\sigma
, so every
\sigma
-invariant element
x\in W(R/pR)
lifts uniquely to a
\sigma
-invariant element
\widetilde{x}\in W(R)
.

Similarly, we write

W_{m}(R)^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}\coloneqq\left\{x\in W_{m}(R)\mid\sigma(x)=x\in W_{n}(R)\right\}\subseteq W_{m}(R).

Note that this is slightly ambiguous as the chosen
n
does not appear in the notation; however, it should always be clear from the context what
n
is used.

•

Recall that the inverse of the
Verschiebung
is a
\sigma
-linear map
I_{R}\to W(R)
.
We denote its linearization by
\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}\colon I_{R}^{\sigma}\to W(R)
and call it the
divided Frobenius
.
For
x\in I_{R}
we have
p\cdot\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}(1\otimes x)=\sigma(x)
, justifying the name.

We also have a truncated variant of the divided Frobenius
\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}\colon W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}I_{m,R}\to W_{n}(R)
.

•

Let
M
,
N
be finite projective
W(R)
-modules, and let
f\colon M\to I_{R}N\subseteq N
be a
W(R)
-linear map.
We write
f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}
for the composition

f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}\colon M^{\sigma}\xrightarrow{f^{\sigma}}I_{R}^{\sigma}\otimes_{W(R)}N^{\sigma}\xrightarrow{\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}N^{\sigma}.

Then we have
p\cdot f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}=f^{\sigma}
, and given homomorphisms of finite projective
W(R)
-modules
g\colon L\to M
and
h\colon N\to P
, we have
(f\circ g)^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}=f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}\circ g^{\sigma}
and
(h\circ f)^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}=h^{\sigma}\circ f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}
.

Similarly, given finite projective
W_{m}(R)
-modules
M
and
N
and a homomorphism of
W_{m}(R)
-modules
f\colon M\to I_{m,R}N\subseteq N
, we write
f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}
for the composition

\displaystyle f^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}\colon W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}M

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{f^{\sigma}}\left(W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}I_{m,R}\right)\otimes_{W_{n}(R)}\left(W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}N\right)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}\otimes\mathrm{id}}W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}N.

This construction has similar properties to the non-truncated version.

•

Given a smooth affine
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-group scheme

L^{+}G
for the
(
Witt vector
)
positive loop group of

,
i.e.
the flat affine
\mathbf{Z}_{p}
-group scheme given by

(see also
[

, Section 2.2]
).

We also write

We also record the following technical lemma that will be used multiple times throughout the article.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that we are given an admissible linearly topologized ring
A

(
see
[
Sta18
, Tag 07E8]
)
such that
p
is topologically nilpotent in
A
.
Let
M
,
M^{\prime}
be finite projective
A
-modules of rank
h
, and let
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
and
g\colon M^{\prime}\to M
be morphisms of
A
-modules such that
g\circ f=p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M}
and
f\circ g=p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M^{\prime}}
.
Suppose furthermore that there exist integers
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
with
\ell+\ell^{\prime}=h
such that for every continuous ring homomorphism
A\to k
with
k
an algebraically closed field, the induced homomorphisms of
k
-vector spaces

f\colon k\otimes_{A}M\longrightarrow k\otimes_{A}M^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad g\colon k\otimes_{A}M^{\prime}\longrightarrow k\otimes_{A}M

are of ranks
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
.

Then the induced homomorphisms of
A/pA
-modules

f\colon M/pM\longrightarrow M^{\prime}/pM^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad g\colon M^{\prime}/pM^{\prime}\longrightarrow M/pM

are of constant ranks
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
, in the sense that their respective images are direct summands
(
hence finite projective
)
of the indicated rank.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{a}\subseteq A
be an ideal of definition that contains
p
.
Note that the condition that
f
and
g
are of constant ranks
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
, is representable by a finitely presented locally closed subscheme
Z\subseteq\Spec(A/\mathfrak{a})
.
By assumption we have
\lvert Z\rvert=\lvert\Spec(A/\mathfrak{a})\rvert
, so
Z
is the vanishing locus of a nilpotent ideal
\mathfrak{b}\subseteq A/\mathfrak{a}
.
After replacing
\mathfrak{a}
by the preimage of
\mathfrak{b}
under
A\to A/\mathfrak{a}
, we thus may assume that the homomorphisms of
A/\mathfrak{a}
-modules

f\colon M/\mathfrak{a}M\longrightarrow M^{\prime}/\mathfrak{a}M^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad g\colon M^{\prime}/\mathfrak{a}M^{\prime}\longrightarrow M/\mathfrak{a}M

are of constant ranks
\ell
and
\ell^{\prime}
.

Modulo
\mathfrak{a}
the morphisms
f
and
g
now compose to
0
in both directions and are of complementary constant ranks.
Thus there exist direct sum decompositions

M/\mathfrak{a}M=P\oplus P^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\prime}/\mathfrak{a}M^{\prime}=P\oplus P^{\prime}

with respect to which we have
f=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{P}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}
and
g=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
0&\mathrm{id}_{P^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}
.
Lift these decompositions to

M=Q\oplus Q^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\prime}=Q\oplus Q^{\prime}.

After possibly modifying the lifts, we may then assume that

f=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{Q}&0\\
0&\ast\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad g=\begin{pmatrix}\ast&\ast\\
\ast&\mathrm{id}_{Q^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}.

The assumptions
g\circ f=p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M}
and
f\circ g=p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{M^{\prime}}
then imply that we in fact have

f=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{id}_{Q}&0\\
0&p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{Q^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad g=\begin{pmatrix}p\cdot\mathrm{id}_{Q}&0\\
0&\mathrm{id}_{Q^{\prime}}\end{pmatrix},

so the claim follows.
∎

2.2. Pairs and displays

Let
h
and
d
denote integers with
0\leq d\leq h
.

Definition 2.2
.

A
pair
(
of type
(h,d))
over
R
is a tuple
(M,M_{1})
consisting of a finite projective
W(R)
-module
M
(of rank
h
) and a
W(R)
-submodule
M_{1}\subseteq M
with
I_{R}M\subseteq M_{1}
and such that
M_{1}/I_{R}M\subseteq M/I_{R}M
is a direct summand (of rank
d
).

An
m
-truncated pair over
R
is a tuple
(M,M_{1})
consisting of a finite projective
W_{m}(R)
-module
M
and a
W_{m}(R)
-submodule
M_{1}\subseteq M
with
I_{m,R}M\subseteq M_{1}
and such that
M_{1}/I_{m,R}M\subseteq M/I_{m,R}M
is a direct summand.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
(M,M_{1})
and
(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
be two pairs over
R
.
Then a
morphism

f\colon(M,M_{1})\to(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
is a morphism of
W(R)
-modules
f\colon M\to M^{\prime}
such that
f(M_{1})\subseteq M^{\prime}_{1}
.

In the same way, we also define morphisms of
m
-truncated pairs.

Remark 2.4
.

Let
(M,M_{1})
be a pair over
R
, and let
R\to R^{\prime}
be a morphism of
p
-complete rings.
Then we can form the base change
(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})=(M,M_{1})_{R^{\prime}}
that is a pair over
R^{\prime}
.
It is characterized by

M^{\prime}=W(R^{\prime})\otimes_{W(R)}M\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\prime}_{1}/I_{R^{\prime}}M^{\prime}=R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}\left(M_{1}/I_{R}M\right).

Similarly, we can also base change
m
-truncated pairs.

From the descent result
[
Zin02
, Corollary 34]
, it follows that the assignment
R\mapsto\{\text{pairs over $R$}\}
defines a stack of
W(\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathbf{Z}_{p})})
-linear categories for the fpqc topology; here
W(\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathbf{Z}_{p})})
denotes the sheaf of rings that is given by
R\mapsto W(R)
.

Similarly, using
[
Lau13
, Lemma 3.12]
, we see that the assignment
R\mapsto\{\text{$m$-truncated pairs over $R$}\}
defines a stack of
W_{m}(\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathbf{Z}_{p})})
-linear categories.

Remark 2.5
.

There are natural truncation functors

\left\{\text{pairs over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{$m$-truncated pairs over $R$}\right\}

and

\left\{\text{$m^{\prime}$-truncated pairs over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{$m$-truncated pairs over $R$}\right\}

for
m\leq m^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(M,M_{1})
be a pair over
R
.
Then
(M,M_{1})
has a
normal decomposition

(L,T)
, i.e. a direct sum decomposition
M=L\oplus T
such that
M_{1}=L\oplus I_{R}T
.
Given a second pair
(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
with normal decomposition
(L^{\prime},T^{\prime})
, every morphism of pairs
f\colon(M,M_{1})\to(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
can be written in matrix form
f=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
with

a\colon L\longrightarrow L^{\prime},\quad b\colon T\longrightarrow L^{\prime},\quad c\colon L\longrightarrow I_{R}T^{\prime},\quad d\colon T\longrightarrow T^{\prime}.

The same is true for
m
-truncated pairs.

Proof.

Set
L^{\prime}\coloneqq M_{1}/I_{R}M\subseteq M/I_{R}M
.
By definition it is a direct summand, so we can choose a complement
T^{\prime}\subseteq M/I_{R}M
.
As
W(R)
is Henselian along
I_{R}
, we can now lift the decomposition
M/I_{R}M=L^{\prime}\oplus T^{\prime}
to a decomposition
M=L\oplus T
as desired.
The claim about writing morphisms as matrices with respect to chosen normal decompositions is immediate.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

We define a natural
\sigma
-linear functor

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\left\{\text{pairs (of type $(h,d)$) over $R$}\right\}&\longrightarrow&\left\{\begin{gathered}\text{finite projective $W(R)$-modules}\\
\text{(of rank $h$)}\end{gathered}\right\},\\[14.22636pt]
(M,M_{1})&\longmapsto&\widetilde{M_{1}}\end{array}

as follows:

•

Given a pair
(M,M_{1})
over
R
with normal decomposition
(L,T)
, we set
\widetilde{M_{1}}\coloneqq L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}
.

•

Given two pairs
(M,M_{1})
and
(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
with normal decompositions
(L,T)
and
(L^{\prime},T^{\prime})
and a morphism
f=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\colon(M,M_{1})\to(M^{\prime},M^{\prime}_{1})
, we define

\widetilde{f}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}a^{\sigma}&p\cdot b^{\sigma}\\
c^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}&d^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}\colon\widetilde{M_{1}}\longrightarrow\widetilde{M^{\prime}_{1}}.

In the same way we also define a natural functor

\begin{array}[]{r c l}\left\{\text{$m$-truncated pairs over $R$}\right\}&\longrightarrow&\left\{\text{finite projective $W_{n}(R)$-modules}\right\},\\[2.84526pt]
(M,M_{1})&\longmapsto&\widetilde{M_{1}}.\end{array}

Remark 2.8
.

We make the following remarks.

•

Checking that the functor
(M,M_{1})\mapsto\widetilde{M_{1}}
from
Definition

2.7
is well defined amounts to checking that the definition of
\widetilde{f}
is compatible with identities and composition.
This can be easily verified using the properties of
(\text{-})^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}
; see
Section

2.1
.

The definition
\widetilde{f}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}a^{\sigma}&p\cdot b^{\sigma}\\
c^{\sigma,\mathrm{div}}&d^{\sigma}\end{pmatrix}
imitates the expression
\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}^{\sigma}\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}
; the latter
a priori
only makes sense after base changing to
W(R)[1/p]
, but then the two terms agree.

•

Let
(M,M_{1})
be a pair over
R
.
Then we can informally think of
\widetilde{M_{1}}
as the
“correct version”
of the Frobenius twist
M_{1}^{\sigma}
that usually fails to be a finite projective
W(R)
-module.

We have a natural surjective
W(R)
-linear map

M_{1}^{\sigma}\cong L^{\sigma}\oplus\left(I_{R}^{\sigma}\otimes_{W(R)}T^{\sigma}\right)\xrightarrow{(\mathrm{id},\sigma^{\mathrm{div}}\otimes\mathrm{id})}L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}\cong\widetilde{M_{1}},

and this map actually is an isomorphism when
R
is a perfect ring of characteristic
p
.

We also have natural
W(R)
-linear maps

\widetilde{M_{1}}\cong L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}}L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}\cong M^{\sigma}\quad\text{and}\quad M^{\sigma}\cong L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}\xrightarrow{\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}}L^{\sigma}\oplus T^{\sigma}\cong\widetilde{M_{1}}

that we can informally think of as the
“inclusion”
and the
“multiplication-by-
p
map”
, respectively.
Similar maps also exist in the truncated situation.

•

The theory of
higher displays
as developed in
[
Lau21
]
and
[
Dan21
]
gives a way to conceptualize
Definition

2.7
.

Let
W(R)^{\oplus}
be the
\mathbf{Z}
-graded ring underlying the Witt frame (see
[
Dan21
, Definition 2.2]
), and recall that it is equipped with two ring homomorphisms
\tau,\sigma\colon W(R)^{\oplus}\to W(R)
.
Now giving a pair
(M,M_{1})
over
R
is equivalent to giving a finite projective graded
W(R)^{\oplus}
-module whose type is concentrated in
[0,1]
, and the functors
(M,M_{1})\mapsto M
and
(M,M_{1})\mapsto\widetilde{M_{1}}
correspond to base changing along
\tau
and
\sigma
, respectively.

Definition 2.9
.

A
display over
R
is a tuple
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
, where
(M,M_{1})
is a pair over
R
and
\Psi\colon\widetilde{M_{1}}\to M
is an isomorphism of
W(R)
-modules.
We call
\Psi
the
divided Frobenius
of the display.

Similarly, an
(m,n)
-truncated display over
R
is a tuple
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
, where
(M,M_{1})
is an
m
-truncated pair over
R
and
\Psi\colon\widetilde{M_{1}}\to W_{n}(R)\otimes_{W_{m}(R)}M
is an isomorphism of
W_{n}(R)
-modules.

Remark 2.10
.

Similarly to what was explained for pairs in
Remarks

2.4
and
2.5
, one can also base change and truncate displays.

The assignment
R\mapsto\{\text{displays over $R$}\}
defines an fpqc-stack of
\underline{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
-linear categories; here
\underline{\mathbf{Z}_{p}}
denotes the sheaf that is given by
R\mapsto\mathbf{Z}_{p}(R)
.

Similarly, the assignment
R\mapsto\{\text{$(m,n)$-truncated displays over $R$}\}
defines a stack of
W_{m}(\mathcal{O}_{\Spf(\mathbf{Z}_{p})})^{\sigma=\mathrm{id}}
-linear categories.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
be a display over
R
.
We define the
Frobenius of
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
as the composition

F_{M}\colon M^{\sigma}\longrightarrow\widetilde{M_{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}M,

where the first arrow is the
“multiplication-by-
p
map”
introduced in
Remark

2.8
.
Furthermore, we say that
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
is
F
-nilpotent
if there exists some
N\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that the
R/pR
-module homomorphism

R/pR\otimes\left(F_{M}\circ\dotsb\circ F_{M}^{\sigma^{N-1}}\right)\colon R/pR\otimes_{W(R)}M^{\sigma^{N}}\longrightarrow R/pR\otimes_{W(R)}M

vanishes.

Now let
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
be an
(m,n)
-truncated display over
R
.
Then we again define the
Frobenius of
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
as the composition

F_{M}\colon W_{n}(R)\otimes_{\sigma,W_{m}(R)}M\longrightarrow\widetilde{M_{1}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Psi}}{{\longrightarrow}}W_{n}(R)\otimes_{W_{m}(R)}M

and say that
(M,M_{1},\psi)
is

-nilpotent
if there exists some

such that the

-module homomorphism

\left(R/pR\otimes_{W_{n}(R)}F_{M}\right)\circ\dotsb\circ\left(R/pR\otimes_{W_{n}(R)}F_{M}\right)^{(p^{N-1})}\colon\left(R/pR\otimes_{W_{m}(R)}M\right)^{(p^{N})}\longrightarrow R/pR\otimes_{W_{m}(R)}M

Remark 2.12
.

It follows immediately from the definition that an (
(m,n)
-truncated) display over

is
F
-nilpotent if and only if its

-truncated base change to

is
F
-nilpotent.

Remark 2.13

(Relation with Dieudonné modules)
.

Suppose that
R
is perfect of characteristic
p
.
Then there is the classical notion of a
Dieudonné module over
R
.

Such a Dieudonné module over
R
is a tuple
(M,F_{M})
consisting of a finite projective
W(R)
-module
M
and an isomorphism
F_{M}\colon M^{\sigma}[1/p]\to M[1/p]
that satisfies

.

We have a natural equivalence of categories

\left\{\text{displays over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{Dieudonn\'{e} modules over $R$}\right\}

that sends a display
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
to
(M,F_{M})
, where
F_{M}
is the Frobenius from
Definition

2.11
.
The inverse of this equivalence is given by sending
(M,F_{M})
to
(M,M_{1},\Psi)
, where

M_{1}\coloneqq p\cdot F_{M}^{-1}(M)^{\sigma^{-1}}\subseteq M\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi\colon\widetilde{M_{1}}\cong p\cdot F_{M}^{-1}(M)\xrightarrow{p^{-1}\cdot F_{M}}M.

Moreover, the display corresponding to a Dieudonné module
(M,F_{M})
over

is of type
(h,d)
if and only if
M
is a finite projective
W(R)
-module of rank
h
and
M/F_{M}(M^{\sigma})
is a finite projective
R
-module of rank
d
.

Remark 2.14

(Relation with truncated displays in the sense of Lau and Zink)
.

Let us recall the notions of
truncated pairs and displays
from
[

]
; see also
[

]

Consider the ring

where

denotes the

.
We have

There again is a

that sends an

.

2.3. Duals and twists

Definition 2.15
.

Let
(M,M_{1})
be a pair over
R
.
Then we define its
dual

(M,M_{1})^{\vee}\coloneqq\left(M^{\vee},M_{1}^{\ast}\right)

as follows:

•

M^{\vee}=\Hom_{W(R)}(M,W(R))
is the dual of the finite projective
W(R)
-module
M
.

•

M_{1}^{\ast}\subseteq M
is the
W(R)
-submodule of all
\omega\colon M\to W(R)
such that
\omega(M_{1})\subseteq I_{R}
.
Equivalently, it is the preimage under

M^{\vee}\longrightarrow M^{\vee}/I_{R}M^{\vee}\cong\left(M/I_{R}M\right)^{\vee}

of the orthogonal complement
(M_{1}/I_{R}M)^{\perp}\subseteq(M/I_{R}M)^{\vee}
.

This endows the category of pairs over
R
with a
W(R)
-linear duality in the sense of
Definition

A.3
and
Remark

A.6
.

We similarly define duals of
m
-truncated pairs.

Remark 2.16
.

Note that if
(M,M_{1})
is an (
m
-truncated) pair of type
(h,d)
over
R
, then its dual
(M,M_{1})^{\vee}
is of type
(h,h-d)
.

Lemma 2.17
.

The functor

\left\{\,\text{pairs over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\,\text{finite projective $W(R)$-modules}\,\right\},\quad(M,M_{1})\longmapsto\widetilde{M_{1}}

is naturally compatible with dualities.

The same is true for the functor

\left\{\text{$m$-truncated pairs over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\,\text{finite projective $W_{n}(R)$-modules}\,\right\},\quad(M,M_{1})\longmapsto\widetilde{M_{1}}.

Proof.

Given a pair
(M,M_{1})
over
R
, we need to define a natural isomorphism
\widetilde{M_{1}^{\ast}}\to\widetilde{M_{1}}^{\vee}
.
Let
(L,T)
be a normal decomposition of
(M,M_{1})
.
Then
(T^{\vee},L^{\vee})
is a normal decomposition of
(M,M_{1})^{\vee}
, and we can define the desired isomorphism as

\left\{\,\text{pairs over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\,\text{finite projective $W(R)$-modules}\,\right\},\quad(M,M_{1})\longmapsto\widetilde{M_{1}}

Proof.

Remark 2.23

Definition 3.1

•

Remark 3.2
.

There are obvious natural truncation functors

\left\{\text{chains over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{$n$-truncated chains over $R$}\right\}

\left\{\text{$n^{\prime}$-truncated chains over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{$n$-truncated chains over $R$}\right\}

When

\left\{\text{$1$-truncated chains over $R$}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{$(1\text{-}\mathrm{rdt})$-truncated chains over $R$}\right\}

by sending a

-truncated chain

Remark 3.3
.

Let

Then actually already the morphism of

-truncated chains.

for all

.

•

One can now check that both

From the smoothness of

-truncated chain of Lau–Zink-pairs lifts.

•

Consider the

-truncated chain of Lau–Zink pairs

0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha_{1}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
[x^{-1}y]\cdot p^{n}&1\end{pmatrix},

•

Now let

\rho^{\mathrm{lift}}_{0,1}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}p^{n}\cdot a&1+p^{n}\cdot b\\
p+[y]\cdot p^{n}+p^{n+1}\cdot c&p^{n}\cdot d\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{and}\quad\rho^{\mathrm{lift}}_{1,2}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}p^{n}\cdot e&1+p^{n}\cdot f\\
p+[y]\cdot p^{n}+p^{n+1}\cdot g&p^{n}\cdot h\end{pmatrix}

This gives a contradiction as

really should be multiplication by

This proves the claim.

Definition A.3
.

A

is a category

that is equipped with an equivalence of categories

and a natural isomorphism

A functor

between categories with duality is a functor

that is equipped with a natural isomorphism

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x^{\vee})^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F(x)^{\vee\vee}}

is commutative (where the second horizontal arrow denotes the inverse of the dual of the isomorphism

).

between two functors

as above is a natural isomorphism

such that the diagram

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{\vee,-1}}

is commutative.

-category.

.

The
(2,1)
-category of (essentially small) categories with duality has finite

-products, and the natural forgetful

preserves finite

\left\{\text{rigid symmetric monoidal categories}\right\}\longrightarrow\left\{\text{symmetric monoidal categories with duality}\right\}.

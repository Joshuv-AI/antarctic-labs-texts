On the Cohomological Hall Algebra of a Character Variety
Vivek Mistry

Abstract
. A multiplication on the 2D cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of the variety of commuting matrices was described by Schiffman and Vasserot. This construction can be generalised to other varieties that exist as the zero-locus of a function on a smooth ambient variety. On the 2D CoHA of the character variety of the fundamental group of a genus g g Riemann surface, we compare the multiplication induced by the standard presentation and that of a brane tiling presentation.



1.
Introduction

In
[
10
]
Schiffman and Vasserot defined a multiplication on the vector space
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{H}_{\textnormal{BM}}^{G_{n}}(C_{n},\mathbb{C})
of
G_{n}=\textnormal{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant Borel-Moore homology (or equivalently the dual of the the
G_{n}
-equivariant compactly supported cohomology) of the commuting variety
C_{n}=\big\{(A,B)\in\textnormal{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})^{2}\,\,\big|\,\,[A,B]=0\big\}
, giving rise to a so-called 2D cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA). We have an algebra homomorphism
\widehat{\mu}:\mathbb{C}[x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\langle a,b\rangle
given by

x\longmapsto[a,b]=ab-ba

which induces the map of varieties
\mu_{n}:\textnormal{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})^{2}\rightarrow\textnormal{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
that sends

(A,B)\longmapsto[A,B]=AB-BA.

The commuting variety can be written as the zero-locus
C_{n}=\mu_{n}^{-1}(0)
of this function on the smooth ambient variety
\textnormal{Mat}_{n}(\mathbb{C})^{2}
induced from a homomorphism of smooth finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebras. This construction can be generalised to the (equivariant) compactly supported cohomology of any such variety that can be written in a similar way, as we will explain in Section 3.1.

Specifically we are interested in the 2D CoHA of the character variety
\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})])
of the fundamental group of a Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}
of genus
g
. This has the natural or
standard presentation
as the zero-locus of the
\textnormal{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant function
\lambda_{n}:\textnormal{GL}_{n}^{2g}(\mathbb{C})\rightarrow\textnormal{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})
given by

(A_{i},B_{i})_{i=1}^{g}\longmapsto\prod_{i=1}^{g}A_{i}B_{i}A_{i}^{-1}B_{i}^{-1}-\textnormal{Id}_{n}

which is induced from the algebra homomorphism
\widehat{\lambda}:\mathbb{C}[x]\rightarrow\mathbb{C}\langle a_{1}^{\pm 1},b_{1}^{\pm 1},\ldots,a_{g}^{\pm 1},b_{g}^{\pm 1}\rangle
that sends

x\longmapsto\prod_{i=1}^{g}a_{i}b_{i}a_{i}^{-1}b_{i}^{-1}-1.

This gives rise to a particular 2D CoHA structure on the vector space
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{H}_{c}\big(\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})]),\mathbb{Q}\big)^{\vee}
.

However, there is an alternative way to express the character variety as the zero-locus of a function induced from an algebra homomorphism, via brane tilings and Jacobi algebras. As explored in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
, taking a brane tiling
\Delta
of a Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}
we can obtain a quiver and potential
(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta})
such that there exists a cut
E
for
W_{\Delta}
and an isomorphism of algebras

\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E)\cong\textnormal{Mat}_{r}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})])

for the localised 2D Jacobi algebra of
(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E)
, where
r=|Q_{\Delta,0}|
(see [
[
2
]
, Proposition 5.4]). Hence we have isomorphisms

\displaystyle\textnormal{Rep}_{nr}(\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E))

\displaystyle\cong\textnormal{Rep}_{nr}(\textnormal{Mat}_{r}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})]))

\displaystyle\cong\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})]).

From standard results of Jacobi algebras we obtain an algebra homomorphism

{\mathbb{C}\langle x_{e}\,:\,e\in E\rangle\rightarrow\mathbb{C}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta}\setminus E)}
given by

x_{e}\longmapsto\frac{\partial W_{\Delta}}{\partial e}

which induces a
\textnormal{GL}_{n}^{r}(\mathbb{C})
-equivariant function

\textnormal{Rep}_{nr}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta}\setminus E)\rightarrow\textnormal{Mat}_{nr\times nr}(\mathbb{C})^{|E|}

whose zero-locus is
\textnormal{Rep}_{nr}(\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E))
. This induces another 2D CoHA structure on the cohomology
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{H}_{c}\big(\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})]),\mathbb{Q}\big)^{\vee}
and therefore the natural question is to ask if in fact these two 2D CoHA structures are the same?

In this paper we shall prove an affirmative answer to this question

Theorem 1.1
.

The 2D CoHA

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{H}_{c}\big(\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})]),\mathbb{Q}\big)^{\vee}

is isomorphic as an algebra to the 2D CoHA

\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\textnormal{H}_{c}\big(\textnormal{Rep}_{nr}(\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E)),\mathbb{Q}\big)^{\vee}

under the isomorphism induced by
\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q}_{\Delta},W_{\Delta},E)\cong\textnormal{Mat}_{r}(\mathbb{C}[\pi_{1}(\Sigma_{g})])
from [
[
2
]
, Proposition 5.4].

The utility of this result is that it allows one to use powerful theorems already developed for CoHAs of quivers and Jacobi algebras, such as dimensional reduction from
[
3
]
and the PBW isomorphism from
[
4
]
, in the character variety setting allowing for new avenues of study of these cohomological objects.

-matrices

\displaystyle\textnormal{Mat}_{n}:=\textnormal{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C})

\displaystyle\textnormal{Mat}_{m,n}\subset\textnormal{Mat}_{m+n}

\displaystyle\textnormal{GL}_{n}:=\textnormal{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C})

2.
Quivers, Jacobi algebras, and brane tilings

2.1.
2D Jacobi algebras

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1})
be a quiver with potential
W\in\mathbb{C}Q/[\mathbb{C}Q,\mathbb{C}Q]
. For
n=(n_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{|Q_{0}|}
let

M_{n}(Q)=\bigoplus_{a:i\rightarrow j\in Q_{1}}\textnormal{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{C}^{n_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{n_{j}})

denote the space of
n
-dimensional representations of the path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
and let

\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\mathbb{C}Q)\cong M_{n}(Q)/G_{n}

denote the stack of
n
-dimensional representations of
\mathbb{C}Q
, where
G_{n}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}\textnormal{GL}_{n_{i}}
acts on
M_{n}(Q)
via simultaneous conjugation. For
a\in Q_{1}
define
\partial W/\partial a\in\mathbb{C}Q
by taking all the terms in
W
containing the arrow
a
, cyclically permuting
a
to the front of each term and then deleting it. The
Jacobi algebra
of the
(Q,W)
is the quotient algebra

\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W)=\mathbb{C}Q/I_{W}

where
I_{W}
is the ideal
\big(\partial W/\partial a\,\,\,|\,\,a\in Q_{1}\big)
. Analogously we define
M_{n}(\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W))
as the space of
n
-dimensional representations of
\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W)
and
\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W))
as the stack of
n
-dimensional representations of
\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W)
. Define
G_{n}
-equivariant maps of varieties

\textnormal{Tr}(W)_{n}:M_{n}(Q)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}

by

(R_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\longmapsto\textnormal{Tr}(W(R_{a}))

and

(\partial W/\partial a)_{a\in Q_{1}}:M_{n}(Q)\rightarrow M_{n}(Q^{\textnormal{op}})

by

(R_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}\longmapsto\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial b}(R_{a})\right)_{b\in Q_{1}}.

Then we have that

\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W))\cong\textnormal{crit}((\textnormal{Tr}(W)_{n}))/G_{n}

or alternatively

\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W))\cong(\partial W/\partial a)_{a\in Q_{1}}^{-1}(0)/G_{n}

where
0\in M_{n}(Q^{\textnormal{op}})
is the representation that assigns the zero matrix to each arrow
a\in Q^{\textnormal{op}}_{1}
.

A
cut

E\subset Q_{1}
for the potential
W
is a choice of arrows such that
W
is homogeneous of degree 1 with respect to the chosen arrows. Given the triple
(Q,W,E)
let
\widehat{I}_{W,E}
denote the ideal

\big(\partial W/\partial e\,,\,e\,\,\big|\,\,e\in E\big)\lhd\mathbb{C}Q

and let
I_{W,E}
denote the ideal

\big(\partial W/\partial e\,\,\,\big|\,\,e\in E\big)\lhd\mathbb{C}(Q\setminus E)

which makes sense since
\partial W/\partial e
is a sum of paths in
Q\setminus E
for any
e\in E
due to the definition of a cut. We define the
2D Jacobi algebra
as the quotient algebra

\displaystyle\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W,E)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{C}Q/\widehat{I}_{W,E}.

We also write

\textnormal{Jac}(Q\setminus E,W,E)=\mathbb{C}(Q\setminus E)/I_{W,E}

then clearly

\textnormal{Jac}(Q\setminus E,W,E)\cong\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W,E)

as alternative notation we will use later on. Define the
G_{n}
-equivariant map

\partial W/\partial E:M_{n}(Q\setminus E)\rightarrow\prod_{e\in E}\textnormal{Mat}_{n_{s(e)}\times n_{t(e)}}

by

(R_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}\setminus E}\longmapsto\left(\frac{\partial W}{\partial e}(R_{a})\right)_{e\in E}

where
G_{n}
acts on
\prod_{e\in E}\textnormal{Mat}_{n_{s(e)}\times n_{t(e)}}
via simultaneous conjugation. Then

\textnormal{Rep}_{n}(\textnormal{Jac}(Q,W,E))\cong(\partial W/\partial E)^{-1}(0)/G_{n}.

Let
\mathbb{C}\widetilde{Q}
denote the
localised path algebra of
Q
in which we add formal inverses
a^{-1}
to the algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
for each
a\in Q_{1}
with the property that
a^{-1}a=e_{s(a)}
and
aa^{-1}=e_{t(a)}
. We get corresponding notions for the localised Jacobi algebra denoted by
\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q},W)
, and for the localised 2D Jacobi algebra denoted by
\textnormal{Jac}(\widetilde{Q},W,E)
for which we add inverses
a^{-1}
for
a\in Q_{1}\setminus E
.

2.2.
Brane tilings

Let
\Sigma_{g}
be a Riemann surface of genus
g
. A
brane tiling

\Delta
of
\Sigma_{g}
is an embedding
\Gamma\hookrightarrow\Sigma_{g}
of a bipartite graph
\Gamma
such that each connected component of
\Sigma_{g}\setminus\Gamma
is simply connected. We choose a partition of the vertex set of
\Gamma
into two disjoint subsets of black and white vertices such that the edges in
\Gamma
only go between a black vertex and a white vertex.

From a brane tiling we can obtain a quiver with potential
(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta})
. The underlying graph of
Q_{\Delta}
is the dual graph to
\Gamma
in
\Sigma_{g}
, and it is directed so that arrows in
Q_{\Delta}
go clockwise around a white vertex and anticlockwise around a black vertex. For a vertex
v\in\Gamma
let
c_{v}
denote the minimal cycle in
Q_{\Delta}
that encircles
v
, i.e.
c_{v}
is the cycle consisting of all the arrows which are dual to the edges that come out of
v
. Then we take the potential to be

W_{\Delta}=\sum_{v\,\textnormal{white}}c_{v}-\sum_{u\,\textnormal{black}}c_{u}.

A
dimer
for a brane tiling
\Delta
is selection of edges in the tiling such that every vertex is attached to exactly one edge in this selection. Note that dimers need not always exist, for example if the number of black and white vertices is not equal then we will not be able to find a dimer.

The idea behind using brane tilings in the context of fundamental groups of Riemann surfaces is as follows (from
[
2
]
). If the arrow
a\in Q_{\Delta,1}
is dual to the edge between the white vertex
v
and black vertex
u
in
\Gamma
then
a\cdot\partial W_{\Delta}/\partial a=c_{v}-c_{u}.
This tells us that in
\textnormal{Jac}(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta})
we can identify paths that are homotopic via an edge in the brane tiling ie. the edge in
\Delta
“spans” the homotopy in
\Sigma_{g}
. This allows us to link the algebra over the fundamental group of the Riemann surface
\Sigma_{g}
with a Jacobi algebra. However note that not all homotopic paths in
\mathbb{C}Q_{\Delta}
are identified in
\textnormal{Jac}(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta})
, e.g. the minimal cycles
c_{v}
are null-homotopic but are not equal to the constant paths in
\textnormal{Jac}(Q_{\Delta},W_{\Delta})
.

3.
Cohomological Hall algebras

3.1.
The 2D CoHA multiplication

Control of the nonlinear frequency shift for the spin-transfer nanooscillator using a bias magnetic field Preprint: AIP/123-QED
A.A Matveev, A.R. Safin, S.A. Nikitov

Abstract
We investigated the possibilities of controlling the nonlinear frequency shift of the magnetization oscillations in a spin-transfer nanoscillator by varying the magnitude and direction of the bias magnetic field. We considered both isotropic ferromagnetic materials and crystals with uniaxial and cubic crystallographic anisotropies. We have shown that achieving a zero nonlinear frequency shift is possible with a certain orientation of the bias magnetic field vector. The results of the theoretical analysis based on the method of Hamiltonian formalism are in good agreement with the micromagnetic simulations. Our research reveals the way to control the frequency tuning of a spin transfer nanoscillator, which is crucial for spintronic signal generation devices.



The possibility of manipulating magnetization using spin-polarized current enables the development of various spintronic devices
Shao et al. 2021
. Based on the spin-torque effect, it is proposed to design magnetic memory, nanogenerators in gigahertz and terahertz frequency ranges, neuromorphic circuits, magnetic field sensors, amplifiers and other devices
Shao et al. 2021
;
Zvezdin and Ekomasov 2020
;
Locatelli, Cros, and Grollier 2014
;
Zhong et al. 2020
;
Sun 2022
;
Zhu et al. 2023
. The main element in such devices is typically a spin-transfer nanoscillator (STNO)
Sadat Parvini et al. 2023
;
Maciel et al. 2020
;
Pathak, Youm, and Hong 2020
;
Shao et al. 2021
. Such a structure is a stack of free and fixed layers separated by a spacer. The dynamics of magnetization in the free layer determines the properties of the STNO in spintronic devices
Maciel et al. 2020
. The emergence of self-oscillations in a ferromagnetic free layer was theoretically described using the classical Hamiltonian formalism for spin waves
Slavin and Kabos 2005
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. It is known
Nikitov et al. 2020
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Rippard et al. 2004
;
Costa et al. 2017
that there is a certain critical density of the spin polarized current passed through the magnetic film at which the ground state of magnetization turns out to be unstable and supercritical bifurcation occurs. This happens because spin-transfer compensates the intrinsic damping of the magnetic material
Costa et al. 2017
. However, with an increase in the density of the spin polarized current passed through the ferromagnet, the amplitude of the precession of magnetization increases
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. The dependence of the frequency of self-oscillations on the density of such a current turns out to be more complicated. It can be affected by the bias magnetic field
Slavin and Kabos 2005
. With growth of the density of the spin-polarized current, the frequency of the STNO can both increase and decrease
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. In this paper, we investigate the
impact of the bias magnetic field on the frequency shift of the magnetization precession in the STNO’s free layer
. Moreover, when applying the classical Hamiltonian formalism to the description of the dynamics of the structure under study, we take into account the existence of the magnetic crystallographic anisotropy in magnetic thin film. Previously
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Slavin and Kabos 2005
, using such a method, only isotropic magnetics and materials with uniaxial anisotropy were considered.

The investigated system (see Fig.
1
) is the STNO. It consists of a fixed magnetic layer that serves as a polarizer, a nonmagnetic separator (spacer) and a ferromagnetic free layer
Zeng, Finocchio, and Jiang 2013
. The direct current injected into the structure spin-polarizes when passing through a fixed layer
Maciel et al. 2020
. The spin-polarized current creates a spin-transfer torque in the free layer
Sadat Parvini et al. 2023
;
Slonczewski 1996
. This spin-transfer torque creates negative magnetic damping capable of compensating the internal positive damping of the ferromagnet. When spin-polarized current density is large enough, the self-oscillatory precession of magnetization begins. That is, supercritical bifurcation occurs and the magnetization oscillates near the magnetic energy minimum. With a further rise in the density of the spin-polarized current, the amplitude of the magnetization oscillations increases. However, with a sufficiently large current, the equilibrium state of magnetization around which the dynamics occurs can become an energy maximum. Previously
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Slavin and Kabos 2005
, this behavior of magnetization in a free layer was studied by the method of Hamiltonian formalism. Nevertheless, the study of the possibilities of controlling a nonlinear frequency shift using the bias magnetic field magnitude and orientation has not been carried out. With the help of the magnetoresistive effect, the dynamics of magnetization in the free layer is converted into a microwave voltage
Zeng, Finocchio, and Jiang 2013
;
Grimaldi et al. 2014
. Thus an electrical signal is generated. The frequency of this ac signal coincides with the frequency of the magnetization precession. That is why it is crucial to be able to adjust the frequency of magnetization oscillations in order to design spintronic signal generation devices.

The investigated structure (see Fig.
1
) is subjected to the bias magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{0}
. The dynamics of magnetization in this case can be described using the Landau-Lifshitz equation
Atxitia, Hinzke, and Nowak 2016
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008

\dot{\mathbf{m}}=-\gamma\ \mathbf{m}\times\mathbf{H}_{eff}+\alpha\ \mathbf{m}\times\dot{\mathbf{m}}+\beta J\ \mathbf{m}\times\left[\mathbf{p}\times\mathbf{m}\right].

(1)

Here, the first term with effective magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{eff}
describes all the self-interactions and the influence of the bias magnetic field (see below), the second term takes into account the Gilbert damping
Gilbert 2004
with constant
\alpha
, the last term arises due to the flow of a spin-polarized current with a density
J
through the free layer. In equation (
1
)
\gamma
is the module of the gyromagnetic ratio of the electron,
\beta=\gamma\hbar P/\left(2M_{s}eL\right)
is the normalization constant for the current,
\hbar
is the reduced Planck constant, P is the degree of polarization
Haidar and Bailleul 2013
,
M_{s}
is the saturation magnetization,
e
is the elementary electric charge,
L
is the free layer thickness. The effective magnetic field is defined as the variational derivative of the magnetic energy functional
E

Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Slavin and Kabos 2005

\mathbf{H}_{eff}=-\frac{1}{\mu_{0}M_{s}}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\mathbf{m}},

(2)

where
\mu_{0}
is the vacuum magnetic permeability. In the chosen coordinate system (see Fig.
1
) with macrospin approximation, we can consider the magnetic energy in the form

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\biggr(-\mu_{0}M_{s}\mathbf{H}_{0}\cdot\mathbf{m}+\frac{\mu_{0}M_{s}^{2}}{2}\left(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}\right)^{2}

(3)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle K_{u}\left(\mathbf{m}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{u}\right)^{2}-\frac{K_{c}}{2}\left(m_{x}^{4}+m_{y}^{4}+m_{z}^{4}\right)\biggr)\ dV.

Here the first term represents the Zeeman energy associated with the application of a magnetic field
\mathbf{H}_{0}
, the second term is the shape anisotropy (demagnetization energy) of a thin film arising from the dipole-dipole interaction in a ferromagnet, the third term takes into account uniaxial magnetic anisotropy and the last term arises due to the cubic crystallographic anisotropy. The anisotropy constants
K_{u},\ K_{c}
are connected to the frequently used anisotropy fields
H_{u},\ H_{c}
through the relations
H_{u}=2K_{u}/\left(\mu_{0}M_{s}\right),\ H_{c}=2K_{c}/\left(\mu_{0}M_{s}\right)
.

The core of the Hamiltonian formalism
Krivosik and Patton 2010
;
Slavin and Kabos 2005
;
Zakharov, Lvov, and Falkovich 1992
is the transformation of the Landau-Lifshitz equation to the Hamiltonian equations. To achieve this, it is necessary to introduce a new variable describing the dynamics of magnetization. When the current density exceeds a certain critical value in the free layer, an auto-oscillatory precession of magnetization begins. Such magnetization dynamics are often characterized by the amplitude and frequency of precession
Zeng, Finocchio, and Jiang 2013
;
Maciel et al. 2020
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. Therefore, one of the simplest methods of introducing a new variable is a transformation that connect this new variable with the amplitude of precession. Hence, first we look for the direction around which the magnetization is precessing. We consider a case when the current density is not too high and the magnetization oscillates around the minimum energy position of the vector
\mathbf{m}

Slavin and Kabos 2005
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. The ort
\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}
of such direction is found from the equation

-\frac{1}{\mu_{0}M_{s}}\frac{\delta E}{\delta\mathbf{m}}=H\mathbf{e}_{\zeta},

(4)

where
H
is an internal magnetic field. In the Cartesian coordinate system, the vector
\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}
is represented as
\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}=\left(\cos\left(\Theta\right)\cos\left(\varphi\right),\cos\left(\Theta\right)\sin\left(\varphi\right),\sin\left(\Theta\right)\right)
. Then the orientation angles of the vector
\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}
and the internal magnetic field
H
can be found from the system of equations

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\cos\left(\Theta\right)\cos\left(\varphi\right)=H_{0}\cos\left(\Theta_{0}\right)\cos\left(\varphi_{0}\right)

(5)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle H_{u}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{u}\right)\cos\left(\xi\right)+Hc\left(\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{x}\right)^{3},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\cos\left(\Theta\right)\sin\left(\varphi\right)=H_{0}\cos\left(\Theta_{0}\right)\sin\left(\varphi_{0}\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle H_{u}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{u}\right)\sin\left(\xi\right)+Hc\left(\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{y}\right)^{3},

\displaystyle H

\displaystyle\sin\left(\Theta\right)=H_{0}\sin\left(\Theta_{0}\right)+H_{c}\left(\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}\cdot\mathbf{e}_{z}\right)^{3}.

We introduce two orts
\mathbf{e}_{\xi},\ \mathbf{e}_{\eta}
that complement the
\mathbf{e}_{\zeta}
to the right triplet

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\xi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sin\left(\Theta\right)\cos\left(\varphi\right)\mathbf{e}_{x}+\sin\left(\Theta\right)\sin\left(\varphi\right)\mathbf{e}_{x}-\cos\left(\Theta\right)\mathbf{e}_{z},

\displaystyle\mathbf{e}_{\eta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin\left(\varphi\right)\mathbf{e}_{x}+\cos\left(\varphi\right)\mathbf{e}_{x}.

(6)

Hamiltonian equations are written with respect to variables called canonical
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. Such variables can be complex-conjugated and depend on coordinates and time. Hamiltonian equations are convenient for the analysis of nonlinear dynamics
Krivosik and Patton 2010
and, for example, are used in the study of plasma
Kuznetsov 2001
, Bose-Einstein condensate
Zakharov and Nazarenko 2005
and fluid dynamics
Lvov and Tabak 2004
. When applied to the description of the dynamics of magnetization, canonical variables are most often associated with the deviation of the vector
\mathbf{m}
from its equilibrium position
Krivosik and Patton 2010
;
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. For spin waves, the canonical transformation was first presented by Holstein and Primakoff
Holstein and Primakoff 1940
. In our case it can be written as

a=\frac{m_{\xi}-im_{\eta}}{\sqrt{2\left(1+m_{\zeta}\right)}}.

(7)

Here
m_{\xi},\ m_{\eta},\ m_{\zeta}
are the projections of the vector
\mathbf{m}
on the corresponding coordinate axes. The dimensionless variable
a
describes the amplitude of the precession of magnetization in the free layer. The dynamics equation for this variable has the quasi-Hamiltonian form

\frac{\partial a}{\partial t}=-i\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{a}}{\partial a^{*}}+F_{a},

(8)

where
\mathcal{H}_{a}=\gamma E/\left(\mu_{0}M_{s}\right)
is the normalized energy that depends on the complex amplitude
a
,
F_{a}
is the perturbation force. This force takes into account the influence of spin polarized current and Gilbert damping. The derivation of the expression for
F_{a}
can be found in earlier works on the Hamiltonian formalism for spin-transfer oscillators
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
. Since the effect that we show in our work is related to the conservative part of the Hamiltonian equations for complex amplitudes, we do not represent the derivation of expressions for perturbation forces.

\mathcal{H}_{a}
can be decomposed in a series by a dimensionless variable
a
in the vicinity of the minimum magnetic energy. Then one can write

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{a}

\displaystyle=

(9)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle U_{1}|a|^{4}+\left(U_{2}|a|^{2}a^{2}+U_{2}^{*}|a|^{2}a^{*2}\right)+\dots.

The obtained expressions for the coefficients of Hamiltonian cover a wide range of magnetic materials. They are suitable for isotropic ferromagnets for the description of which it is necessary to set

The Hamiltonian obtained (
9
) is quite cumbersome for analysis. Therefore, some transformations should be applied. Let’s start with the Bogolyubov’s diagonalization
Bogoljubov 1958
. This transformation simplify the quadratic part of the Hamiltonian
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Zakharov, Lvov, and Falkovich 1992
. In the investigated case, it can be written as

a=ub-vb^{*},

(15)

where

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{A+\omega_{0}}{\omega_{0}}},

(16)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{A-\omega_{0}}{\omega_{0}}},

\displaystyle\omega_{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{A^{2}-\left|B\right|^{2}}.

With such a substitution, the decomposition for energy has the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{b}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega_{0}|b|^{2}+\left(W_{1}|b|^{2}b+W_{1}^{*}|b|^{2}b^{*}+W_{1}b^{3}+W_{1}^{*}b^{*3}\right)

(17)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle T|b|^{4}+\dots.

Here coefficients are given by

\displaystyle W_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-v^{*2}V^{*}v+2u(Vv-u^{*}V^{*})v^{*}+u^{*}Vu^{2},

(18)

\displaystyle W_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle V^{*}v^{*2}-Vu^{2}v^{*},

(19)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(u^{2}U_{1}-3uU_{2}^{*}v^{*})u^{*2}-3(u^{2}U_{2}-\frac{4}{3}uU_{1}v^{*}+v^{*2}U_{2}^{*})vu^{*}

(20)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle 3v^{*}U_{2}uv^{2}+v^{*2}U_{1}v^{2}.

Note that diagonalization (
15
) is a canonical transformation that is it preserves the Hamiltonian form of the equations. Then the conservative dynamics equation for the complex amplitude
b
can be written as

\frac{\partial b}{\partial t}=-i\frac{\partial\mathcal{H}_{b}}{\partial b^{*}}.

(21)

It is possible to eliminate cubic terms in the (
17
). However, to do this, one have to use a quasi-canonical nonlinear substitution. This means that the Hamiltonian form will be preserved only if the high-order terms are discarded in the decomposition of the Hamiltonian. As it was shown previously
Slavin and Tiberkevich 2008
;
Zakharov, Lvov, and Falkovich 1992
, following nonlinear substitution can be used

b=c+\frac{1}{\omega_{0}}\left(W_{1}c^{2}-2W_{1}^{*}|c|^{2}-W_{2}^{*}c^{*2}\right).

(22)

With such a transformation, the Hamiltonian takes the simple form

\mathcal{H}_{c}=\omega_{0}|c|^{2}+\frac{N}{2}|c|^{4},

(23)

where the non-isochronicity coefficient is given by

N=2\left(T-3\frac{\left|W_{1}\right|^{2}+\left|W_{2}\right|^{2}}{\omega_{0}}\right).

(24)

This coefficient determines the nonlinear frequency shift of the self-oscillatory precession of magnetization in accordance with the expression
Slavin and Kabos 2005

\Omega=\omega_{0}+Np.

(25)

Here
\Omega
is the magnetization oscillations frequency,
p=|c|^{2}
is the power of the precession. If the self-oscillatory precession of magnetization occurs near the minimum energy, then the power always increases with the increase in the density of the spin-polarized current. Then
N
determines how the frequency depends on the density of the spin-polarized current passed through the free layer. Since the coefficient
N
can be adjusted using a bias magnetic field, it is possible to control the nonlinear frequency shift of the STNO.

In conclusion, we theoretically demonstrated that achieving a zero nonlinear shift of a spin-transfer nanocillator frequency is possible using a bias magnetic field with a certain critical polar angle. We have shown that with an increase in the bias magnetic field, this angle moves towards smaller values. We have proved the existence of a minimum critical angle for both isotropic crystals and materials with uniaxial and cubic crystallographic anisotropies. The analysis has shown that the presence of uniaxial and cubic crystallographic anisotropies leads to a change in this minimum critical angle and the value of the critical angle at an arbitrary bias magnetic field compared with an isotropic material. The results of the investigation of the nonlinear frequency shift expand the prospects for the use of spin-transfer nanoscillators in spintronic devices.

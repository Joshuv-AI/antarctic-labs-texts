The phonon mechanism explanation of the superconductivity dichotomy between F ​ e ​ S ​ e FeSe and F ​ e ​ S FeS monolayers on S ​ T ​ O STO and other substrates.
Baruch Rosenstein, B. Ya. Shapiro

Abstract
It was observed recently (K. Shigekawa et al, PNAS 116, 2470 (2019)) that while monolayer iron chalcigenide F ​ e ​ S ​ e FeSe on S ​ r ​ T ​ i ​ O 3 SrTiO_{3} ( S ​ T ​ O STO ) substrate has a very high critical temperature, its chemical and structural ”twin” material F ​ e ​ S / S ​ T ​ O FeS/STO has a very low T c T_{c} if any. To explain this the substrate interfacial phonon model of superconductivity in iron chalcogenides is further developed. The main glue is the oxygen ion Ω s = 60 ​ m ​ e ​ v \Omega_{s}=60mev vibrations longitudinal optical (LO) mode. The mode propagates mainly in the T ​ i ​ O 2 TiO_{2} layer adjacent to the monolayer (and genrally present also in similar highly polarized ionic crystals like B ​ a ​ T ​ i ​ O 3 , BaTiO_{3}, rutile, anatase). It has stronger electron - phonon coupling to electron gas in F ​ e ​ S ​ e FeSe than a well known Ω h = 100 ​ m ​ e ​ v \Omega_{h}=100mev harder LO mode. It is shown that while (taking into account screened Coulomb repulsion effects) the critical temperature of F ​ e ​ S ​ e FeSe on S ​ T ​ O STO and T ​ i ​ O 2 TiO_{2} is above 65 ​ K 65K , it becomes less than 5 ​ K 5K for F ​ e ​ S FeS due to two factors suppressing the electron - phonon coupling. The effective mass in the later is twice smaller and in addition the distance between the electron gas in F ​ e ​ S ​ e FeSe to the vibrating substrate oxygen atoms is 15% smaller than in F ​ e ​ S FeS reducinng the central peak in electron-phonon interaction. The theory is extended to other ionic insulating substrates.

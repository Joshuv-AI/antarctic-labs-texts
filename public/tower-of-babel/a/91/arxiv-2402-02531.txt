Non-existence of classical solutions to a two-phase flow model with vacuum
Hai-Liang Li, Yuexun Wang, Yue Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the well-posedness of classical solutions to a two-phase flow model consisting of the pressureless Euler equations coupled with the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations via a drag forcing term. We consider the case that the fluid densities may contain a vacuum, and the viscosities are density-dependent functions. Under suitable assumptions on the initial data, we show that the finite-energy (i.e., in the inhomogeneous Sobolev space) classical solutions to the Cauchy problem of this coupled system do not exist for any small time.

1.
Introduction

This paper concerns the pressureless Euler-Navier-Stokes (Euler-NS) system for a two-phase flow, which is given by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}\rho+\text{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(\rho u)+\text{div}(\rho u\otimes u)=-\rho(u-w),\\
&\partial_{t}n+\text{div}(nw)=0,\\
&\partial_{t}(nw)+\text{div}(nw\otimes w)+\nabla P=\text{div}\tau+\rho(u-w),\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times[0,\infty)
. Here
\rho=\rho(x,t)
and
u=u(x,t)
represent the scalar density and the velocity field of the pressureless Euler equations, while
n=n(x,t)
,
w=w(x,t)
, and
P=P(n)
represent the scalar density, the velocity field, and the pressure potential of the isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations. The equation of state takes the form

P(n)=An^{\gamma},\quad\gamma>1.

(1.2)

We will set the constant
A
to be the unit for simplicity. The viscous stress tensor
\tau
is defined by

\tau=\nu(n)(\nabla w+(\nabla w)^{\top})+\lambda(n)\text{div}w\mathbb{I}_{d},

(1.3)

where the coefficients
\nu=\nu(n)
and
\lambda=\lambda(n)
are density-dependent functions satisfying

\nu(n)>0,\quad 2\nu(n)+d\cdot\lambda(n)\geq 0.

The initial data is posed by

(\rho,u,n,w)(x,0)=(\rho_{0},u_{0},n_{0},w_{0})(x).

(1.4)

It is an important issue to study the well-posedness of solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes (CNS) system (i.e., equations
\eqref{main0}_{3}
-
\eqref{main0}_{4}

&w\in C\big(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big)\cap C^{1}\big(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\big),\end{aligned}\right.

In the present paper, we are going to investigate whether classical solutions of the Cauchy problem for the pressureless Euler-NS system exist in the inhomogeneous Sobolev space. The analysis of the existence of solutions to a two-phase flow is complicated by the fact that, due to the interaction between the two fluids, the system has quite different structure and properties for different relations between the densities of the two fluids. For some typical cases, we apply the maximum principle for a degenerate parabolic operator as well as for an elliptic type operator from the momentum equation in different cases depending on the sizes of the compact supports of the densities, and we prove that, with suitable assumptions on the initial data near the vacuum, the classical solution does not exist for any short time.

5.1
), so we instead use (
2.7
) and the continuity of the solution to deduce

\displaystyle\rho_{0}\frac{(\eta_{2})_{x}}{(\eta_{1})_{x}}(v_{1}-v_{2})\leq 0

(5.2)

for
(x,t)\in(0,d_{0})\times(0,T]
since
T>0
is assumed to be sufficiently small.
Hence, for any fixed
t\in(0,T]
, it follows from (
5.1
) and (
5.2
) that

L_{t}v_{2}\leq 0\quad\text{in}~(0,d_{0}).

(5.3)

One can also show similarly, for any fixed
t\in(0,T]
, that

L_{t}v_{2}\geq 0\quad\text{in}~(1-d_{0},1).

(5.4)

In the following, we mainly focus on the Hopf’s lemma and the strong maximum principle for the operator
L_{t}
satisfying (
5.3
) since the other scenario (
5.4
) can be handled in a similar fashion. For ease of notation, we assume
D
to be an open and bounded set in
(0,d_{0})
, and denote by
\partial D
the boundary of
D
.

To begin with, we show the weak maximum principle.

Lemma 6
.

Assume that
v\in C_{1}^{2}(D)\cap C(\bar{D})
.
For fixed
t\in(0,T]
, if
v
satisfies

L_{t}v\leq 0\quad\mathrm{in}~D,

(5.5)

and
v
attains its maximum at
x_{0}
, then
x_{0}\in\partial D
.

Proof.

Let us first suppose that
L_{t}v<0
in
D
, and there exists
x_{0}\in D
such that

v(x_{0})=\max_{\bar{D}}v.

Then at this maximum point
x_{0}
, one has

v_{x}(x_{0})=0,~~v_{xx}(x_{0})\leq 0,

which implies
L_{t}v\geq 0
at the point
x_{0}
, and this leads to a contradiction.

Generally, we consider the auxiliary function
v^{\varepsilon}:=v+\varepsilon e^{\beta x}
with
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta>0
. For fixed
t\in(0,T]
, it follows from
T\leq\frac{1}{2M}
and (
3.8
) that

\displaystyle 1/(2B)\leq a(x,t)=\mu(0)\frac{1}{(\eta_{2})_{x}}\leq 2B,

(5.6)

\displaystyle|b(x,t)|\leq\mu(0)\frac{|(\eta_{2})_{xx}|}{(\eta_{2})_{x}^{2}}\leq B\cdot(4MT)\leq 2B.

Therefore it holds from (
5.5
) and (
5.6
) that

\displaystyle L_{t}v^{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=L_{t}v+\varepsilon L_{t}(e^{\beta x})\leq\varepsilon\left(-a\beta^{2}+b\beta\right)e^{\beta x}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left(-\frac{1}{2B}\beta^{2}+2B\beta\right)e^{\beta x}\leq-\varepsilon e^{\beta x}<0\quad\text{in}~D

for sufficiently large
\beta>0
. Thus
v^{\varepsilon}
attains its maximum on
\partial D
. Passing the limit
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
proves the desired result.
∎

Next, we establish the Hopf’s lemma.

Lemma 7
.

Assume that
v\in C^{2}(D)\cap C(\bar{D})
. For fixed
t\in(0,T]
,
if
v
satisfies (
5.5
), and there exists a neighbourhood
D
of
x=0
such that

v(0,t)>v(x,t)\quad\mathrm{for\ all}~x\in D.

(5.7)

Then it holds that

\frac{\partial v(0,t)}{\partial\vec{n}}>0.

where
\vec{n}
is the outer unit normal vector at the point
x=0
.

Proof.

For any
x,\ell\in D
, define

\varphi(\beta,x)=e^{-\beta(x-\ell)^{2}}-e^{-\beta\ell^{2}},

where
\beta>0
will be selected below. By (
5.5
), one may estimate

\displaystyle L_{t}\varphi(\beta,x)

\displaystyle=\left(-4a(x-\ell)^{2}\beta^{2}+2a\beta+2b(x-\ell)\beta\right)e^{-\beta(x-\ell)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(-\frac{\ell^{2}}{2B}\beta^{2}+(4B+4B\ell)\beta\right)e^{-\beta(x-\ell)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq 0\quad\text{for}\ x\in(0,\ell/2),

provided
\beta\geq\beta_{0}:=\beta_{0}(\ell,B)>0
. For such a
\beta_{0}
, it follows that

L_{t}\big(v(x,t)-v(0,t)+\epsilon_{0}\varphi(\beta_{0},x)\big)\leq 0\quad\text{for}\ x\in(0,\ell/2).

(5.8)

In view of (
5.7
), there exists a small constant

\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

v(0,t)\geq v(x,t)+\varepsilon_{0}\varphi(\beta_{0},x)\quad\text{for}\ x=\ell/2.

On the other hand, it holds that

v(x,t)-v(0,t)+\varepsilon_{0}\varphi(\beta_{0},x)=0\quad\text{for}\ x=0.

(5.9)

Applying Lemma
6
to (
5.7
)–(
5.8
), we obtain

v(x,t)-v(0,t)+\varepsilon_{0}\varphi(\beta_{0},x)\leq 0\quad\text{for}\ x\in(0,\ell/2).

This together with (
5.9
) yields

\frac{\partial v(0,t)}{\partial\vec{n}}\geq-\varepsilon_{0}\frac{\partial\varphi(\beta_{0},0)}{\partial\vec{n}}\geq 2\varepsilon_{0}\beta_{0}\ell e^{-\ell^{2}}>0,

and the proof is completed.
∎

The following strong maximum principle is direct consequence of the Hopf’s lemma, whose proof can be found
for instance in
[
14
]
.

Lemma 8
.

Assume that
v\in C^{2}(D)\cap C(\bar{D})
. For fixed
t\in(0,T]
,
if
v
satisfies (
5.5
), and attains its maximum at an interior point
x_{0}\in D
, then
v(x,t)\equiv v(x_{0},t)
for all
x\in D
.

Now, we are ready to show Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

First, for any fixed
t\in(0,T]
, it follows from Lemma
6
that
v_{2}(\cdot,t)
attains its maximum on the set

\{x=0\}\cup\{x=d_{0}\}.

On one hand, by
w_{0}(d_{0})<0
due to (
2.7
), and the continuity on time of the solution, there exists a time
t_{0}\in(0,T]
such that

v_{2}(d_{0},t)<0\quad\mathrm{for}\ t\in(0,t_{0}].

On the other hand, thanks to
\eqref{main-LF-3}_{3}
,
v_{2}(0,t)=0
for
t\in(0,t_{0}]
.

Hence, by Lemma
8
,
v_{2}(\cdot,t)
attains its maximum on the set

\{x=0\}
, namely, for fixed
t\in(0,t_{0}]
,

v_{2}(x,t)<v_{2}(0,t)\quad\text{for}\ x\in(0,d_{0}),

which, together with Lemma
7
, implies

\frac{\partial v_{2}(0,t)}{\partial\vec{n}}>0.

This leads to a contradiction with
(v_{2})_{x}=0
on
\partial\Omega\times(0,T]
in
\eqref{main-LF-3}_{3}
.
∎

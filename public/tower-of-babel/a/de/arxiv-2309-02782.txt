Conductors of Twisted Weil–Deligne Representations
Matthew Bisatt and Ross Paterson

Abstract
. We study the behaviour of conductors of L L -functions associated to certain Weil–Deligne representations under twisting. For each global field K K we prove a sharp upper bound for the conductor of the Rankin–Selberg L L -function L ⁡ ( A ⊠ B , s ) L(A\boxtimes B,s) where A , B / K A,B/K are abelian varieties.

Introduction

L
-functions are a number theoretic orchestra; an ensemble of algebra, geometry, and analysis. One useful tool in this area is the ability to build new (conjecturally automorphic)
L
-functions from old
L
-functions through twisting. In particular, given a global field
K
and two abelian varieties
A,B/K
, one can construct the Rankin–Selberg convolution
L(A\boxtimes B,s)
. There is significant motivation to study these
L
-functions already in the literature: the Rankin–Selberg method, together with the converse theorem, is one of the main lines of attack in the Langlands program
[
3
,
5
,
7
,
9
]
.

Our objective is to study certain local invariants associated to such
L
-functions, namely: the Artin conductor exponent and the Swan conductor exponent
1

1

After all, every good orchestra needs a conductor.
. In order to study these, it will be necessary to understand the associated local Weil–Deligne representations. On this level we must consider the tensor product of representations corresponding to abelian varieties, since this corresponds to
A\boxtimes B
via the local Langlands correspondence. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.1

(
Theorem

3.9
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
of residue characteristic
p
, and write
\rho_{A},\rho_{B}
for the associated
\ell
-adic representations, and
\deg(A),\deg(B)
for the degrees of the associated
L
-polynomials. Define the constant

C_{p}(A,B):=\min\bigg\{\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\},\ \left|{\rho_{A}}\right|-\deg(A),\ \left|{\rho_{B}}\right|-\deg(B)\bigg\}.

Then the Artin conductor exponent of the associated
\ell
-adic representations
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
satisfies

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\dim(\rho_{A})a(\rho_{B})+\dim(\rho_{B})a(\rho_{A})-C_{p}(A,B)\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg).

Moreover, if
a(\rho_{A})\leq 1
then in fact

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\dim(\rho_{A})a(\rho_{B})+\deg(B)a(\rho_{A})-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg).

We obtain this by splitting the conductor exponent into a tame part and a wild part (i.e. the Swan conductor). For this latter piece, we obtain the following bound.

Theorem 1.2

(
Proposition

3.5
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
of residue characteristic
p
. Then

\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\dim(\rho_{A})\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})+\dim(\rho_{B})\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A})-\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\}\min\left\{{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})}\right\}.

Remark 1.3
.

The inequalities of
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
are trivially sharp: if
A
has good reduction, then equality holds as
a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\dim(\rho_{A})a(\rho_{B})
with the remaining terms vanishing (and similarly for
Sw
). A family of nontrivial examples of sharpness, one for each odd prime
p
, is given in
Section

3.4
.

A corollary of our work above is the following global result, which uses a simplified version of the above bound, and answers a question raised to us by Andrew Booker.

Theorem 1.4

(
Corollary

3.12
)
.

Let
A,B
be abelian varieties over a global field
K
of conductors
\mathfrak{N}_{A}

\mathfrak{N}_{B}

\mathfrak{d}(A,B):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\textnormal{ prime}\\
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{N}_{A})v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{N}_{B})>1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p}^{\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(A\boxtimes B)-\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(B)}.

N(A\boxtimes B)\hskip 2.0pt\Bigg|\hskip 2.0pt\frac{\mathfrak{N}_{A}^{2\dim B}\mathfrak{N}_{B}^{2\dim A}}{\mathfrak{d}(A,B)\gcd(\mathfrak{N}_{A},\mathfrak{N}_{B})^{2}},

where
N(A\boxtimes B)
is the conductor of the Rankin–Selberg
L

L(A\boxtimes B,s)

Comparison to current literature

There has been significant interest in understanding conductor exponents of tensor products of Weil–Deligne representations. In the setting of our results, there is the (sharp) bound of Bushnell–Henniart, which we recall below.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
2
]
*Theorem B.2)
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
, with associated Weil–Deligne representations
\rho_{A},\rho_{B}
. Then

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\dim(\rho_{A})a(\rho_{B})+\dim(\rho_{B})a(\rho_{A})-2\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\},

where
a(\cdot)
is the Artin conductor exponent.

Theorem

1.2
is an improvement on this result. Our techniques indicate that the factor of
2
multiplying the minimum arises essentially because the representations are (essentially) symplectic. Meanwhile our factor of
p-1
arises as a consequence of Weil’s theorem on the rationality of the characteristic polynomials of these representations.

Our methods and results are more general than the setting presented above: we prove analogous statements for (local) Weil–Deligne representations satisfying certain technical hypotheses. Throughout, by a (local) Weil–Deligne representation, we implicitly mean a complex Frobenius-semisimple one (equivalently an admissible representation for the Langlands correspondence). In this general setting, Bushnell–Henniart
[
4
]
*Theorem C prove that for two general Weil–Deligne representations
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
one only saves one factor of the minimum:

a(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2})\leq\dim(\rho_{1})a(\rho_{2})+\dim(\rho_{2})a(\rho_{1})-\min\left\{{a(\rho_{1}),a(\rho_{2})}\right\}.

Our approach suggests that if one imposes restrictions on the corresponding Weil–Deligne representations then a better constant, in a similar vein to the results on abelian varieties, in front of the minimum term should be possible.
Indeed, if
\rho_{j}=\oplus_{n\geq 1}\ \sigma_{n}^{(j)}\otimes\textup{sp}(n)
for
j=1,2
, then we expect said constant in the general case to depend on the smallest integer
n
for which some
\sigma_{n}^{(j)}\neq 0
. If, moreover, one knows additional representation theoretic information about the
\sigma_{n}
(e.g. essentially symplectic, or rationality properties), then such bounds should be able to be improved further.

2.
Conductors for group filtrations

Here we abstract the notion of the Artin conductor exponent of a representation to being a number associated to a sequence of subgroups and a representation. We then prove statements about this general object which require no arithmetic input. These results will find arithmetic applications in
Section

3
.

2.1.
Definitions

We begin by introducing the central objects for this section.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a sequence of finite groups such that for all
i\geq 0

•

G_{i+1}\leq G_{i}
;

•

G_{k}=0
for some
k\geq 0
.

We call such
\mathcal{G}
a finite group filtration.

Notation 2.2
.

For a finite group filtration
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
, and finite dimensional complex representations
\tau,\sigma
of
G_{0}
, we define the following notation. For
i\geq 0
:

\displaystyle a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left|{\tau}\right|-\left|{\tau^{G_{i}}}\right|;

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\left|{\left({\tau\otimes\sigma}\right)^{G_{i}}}\right|-\left|{\tau^{G_{i}}\otimes\sigma^{G_{i}}}\right|.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a finite group filtration, and
\tau,\sigma
be representations of
G_{0}
. The conductor exponent
a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau)
and the function
\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau,\sigma)
are defined by:

\displaystyle a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau)}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]};

\displaystyle\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau,\sigma)}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}.

Remark 2.4
.

Note that
a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau)
and
\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau,\sigma)
are decreasing sequences in
i
.

In what follows we will prove results about
a^{\mathcal{G}}
. Our arithmetic motivations are to study two particular cases, which we outline briefly below.

Example 2.5

(Artin conductor)
.

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean local field, and
\tau,\sigma
be Weil representations over
F
. Let
L/F^{\textup{nr}}
be the finite extension through which
\tau
and
\sigma
factor, and
G_{i}\leq\textup{Gal}(L/F^{\textup{nr}})
be the
i
th ramification group in the lower numbering. Then we have an equality

a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau)=a(\tau),

where the right hand side is the usual Artin conductor exponent, with the finite group filtration given by
\mathcal{G}=\left({G_{i}}\right)_{i\geq 0}
. Moreover, we have

\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau,\sigma)\geq\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}_{0}(\tau,\sigma)=\deg(\tau\otimes\sigma)-\deg(\tau)\deg(\sigma)\geq 0,

which measures nontrivial growth in the degree under tensor product.

Example 2.6

(Swan conductor)
.

Let
F
be a non-Archimedean local field, and
\tau
be a Weil representation over
F
. Let
L/F^{\textup{nr}}
be the finite extension through which
\tau
factors, and
G_{i}\leq\textup{Gal}(L/F^{\textup{nr}})
be the
i
th ramification group in the lower numbering. Consider the finite group filtration given by
\mathcal{G}=\left({G_{i+1}}\right)_{i\geq 0}
. Then we have an equality

a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau)=\textup{Sw}(\tau),

where the right hand side is the usual Swan conductor exponent.

2.2.
Conductor bounds for tensor products

We now consider tensor products of representations with some additional structure. In one case, we will consider symplectic representations, in the other we consider representations of
p
-groups with rationality properties. To begin with we will need the following.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a finite group filtration, and let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
be representations of
G_{0}
. Then for all
i\geq 0
,

\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})-a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})=a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})+\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

Proof.

Since
a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{j})=\left|{\tau_{j}}\right|-\left|{\tau_{j}^{G_{i}}}\right|
, clearly the right hand side of the claimed equality is

a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})=\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|-\left|{\tau_{1}^{G_{i}}}\right|\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|-\left|{\tau_{2}^{G_{i}}}\right|\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|+\left|{\left({\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}}\right)^{G_{i}}}\right|.

On the other hand, again unwinding the definition of the
a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
, the left hand side of the claimed equality is also

\displaystyle\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})-a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})

\displaystyle=\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|-\left|{\tau_{1}^{G_{i}}}\right|\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|-\left|{\tau_{2}^{G_{i}}}\right|\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|+\left|{\left({\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2}}\right)^{G_{i}}}\right|.

∎

We will also make use of an elementary lemma to glue together the various steps in the ramification filtration.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
M
be a positive real number, and
(a_{i})_{i=1}^{n},(b_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\subseteq\mathbb{R}_{\geq M}\cup\left\{{0}\right\}
be two decreasing sequences. Let
d_{1},d_{2},...,d_{n}
be positive real numbers. Then

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}b_{i}}{d_{i}}\geq M\min\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{d_{i}},\,\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{b_{i}}{d_{i}}\right).

Proof.

First suppose that
a_{n}b_{n}\neq 0
(so all
a_{i}
and
b_{i}
are at least
M
). Then for all
1\leq i\leq n
,
\frac{a_{i}b_{i}}{d_{i}}\geq M\max\{\frac{a_{i}}{d_{i}},\frac{b_{i}}{d_{i}}\}
and the claim follows.

Now let
k
be minimal such that
a_{k}b_{k}=0
and suppose, without loss of generality, that
a_{k}=0
. This implies that
b_{j}\geq M
for
j<k
so

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}b_{i}}{d_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{a_{i}b_{i}}{d_{i}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle M\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\frac{a_{i}}{d_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}}{d_{i}}.

∎

2.2.1.
Symplectic representations

We now study tensor products of symplectic representations, the main result for which is the following.

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a finite group filtration, and
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
be symplectic representations of
G_{0}
. Then

a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})\leq\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1})-2\min\left\{{a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}),a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})}\right\}-\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

First, we remind the reader of a standard result, for which we give a short proof in the interests of self-containment.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\tau
be a symplectic representation of a finite group
G
. Then
\left|{\tau}\right|-\left|{\tau^{G}}\right|
is even.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be the associated non-degenerate alternating
G
-equivariant bilinear pairing on
\tau
. Since
\omega
is non-degenerate,
\left|{\tau}\right|
is even, and so it is sufficient to check that
\omega
remains non-degenerate when restricted to
\tau^{G}
. Let
a\in\tau^{G}
, and choose
b\in\tau
such that
\omega(a,b)\neq 0
. Then note that, since
\omega
is
G
-equivariant,
\omega\left({a,\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot b}\right)=\left({\#G}\right)\omega\left({a,b}\right)\neq 0
.
In particular, since
\sum_{g\in G}g\cdot b\in\tau^{G}
, the pairing remains non-degenerate on
\tau^{G}
.
∎

We are now ready to prove the bound for symplectic representations.

Proof of
Proposition

2.9
.

Note that

\displaystyle\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1})-a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})-a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}.

Thus, by
Lemma

2.7
, it suffices to prove

\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}\geq 2\min_{j}\left(\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{j})}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}\right).

By
Lemma

2.10
,
a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{j})\in 2\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, hence this follows from
Lemma

2.8
.
∎

2.2.2.
Representations of
p
-groups with rational characteristic polynomial

We now consider tensor products of representations of
p
-groups which have rational characteristic polynomial. The main result here is the following.

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
p
be a prime number, and
\mathcal{G}=(G_{i})_{i\geq 0}
be a finite group filtration such that
G_{0}
is a
p
-group. Let
\tau_{1},\tau_{2}
be representations of
G_{0}
such that for every
g\in G_{0}
and every

the characteristic polynomial of
\tau_{j}(g)
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})\leq\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1})-(p-1)\min\left\{{a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}),a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})}\right\}-\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}).

We first remind the reader of an important standard result, for which we present a short proof.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
p
be a prime number and
G
be a finite
p
-group. Let
\tau
be a representation of
G
, and assume that for all
g\in G
the characteristic polynomial of
\tau(g)
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
. Then

\left|{\tau}\right|-\left|{\tau^{G}}\right|\in\left\{{0}\right\}\cup\mathbb{Z}_{\geq(p-1)}.

Proof.

If
\tau=\tau^{G}
then the result is apparent, so we assume that there is a nontrivial irreducible subrepresentation
\chi
of
\tau
. Since
\left|{\chi}\right|\mid\#G
, and so is a power of
p
, if
\chi
is not
1
-dimensional then the result follows. Hence it remains to consider the case that
\chi
is a
1
-dimensional representation of the
p
-group
G
. Since
\chi
is a character of order a power of
p
, it has at least
p-1
conjugate representations, and since the characteristic polynomials of all elements are rational these conjugates must all be subrepresentations of
\tau
, so again the result follows.
∎

Our proposition now follows.

Proof of
Proposition

2.11
.

By
Lemma

2.7
, we know that for all
i\geq 0

a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}\otimes\tau_{2})=\left|{\tau_{1}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})+\left|{\tau_{2}}\right|a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1})-\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}-\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1},\tau_{2}),

so it is equivalent to prove the claim that
\sum_{i\geq 0}\frac{a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{1})a^{\mathcal{G}}_{i}(\tau_{2})}{[G_{0}:G_{i}]}\geq(p-1)\min\left\{{a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{1}),a^{\mathcal{G}}(\tau_{2})}\right\}
, which follows from
Lemma

2.12
and

2.8
.
∎

3.
Weil–Deligne Representations

We begin this section by reminding the reader of some standard results on Weil–Deligne representations, which we will use throughout. We then go on to prove our main local results from the introduction, and deduce the global conductor results from these at the end.

3.1.
Background

Recall that (Frobenius-semisimple) Weil–Deligne representations are isomorphic to direct sums of representations of the form
\sigma_{n}\otimes\textup{sp}(n)

[

]
*4.1.5, where
\textup{sp}(n)
is the special representation
[
11
]
*4.1.4 and
\sigma_{n}
is a Weil representation. For abelian varieties, it is well-known that the corresponding Weil–Deligne representation is of a specific shape.

Proposition 3.1

(for example,
[
10
]
*Proposition 1.10,
[
6
]
*Corollary 6)
.

Let
A/F
be an abelian variety over a non-Archimedean local field, and
\rho_{A}
the corresponding Weil–Deligne representation. Then there exists a(n essentially) symplectic Weil representation
\tau
and self-dual Artin representation
\sigma
such that

\rho_{A}\cong\tau\oplus(\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(2)).

Moreover, denoting the inertia subgroup by
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
, for every
g\in G_{0}
the characteristic polynomial of
\rho_{A}(g)
has coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.

We use the following result to compute conductor exponents for twists of the special representations.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
8
]
*Proposition p.141)
.

Let
\sigma
be a Weil representation over a non-Archimedean local field. Then

a(\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(n))=na(\sigma)+(n{-}1)\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|,

where
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
is the inertia subgroup.

We combine these results, together with standard results for tensor products of special representations, to note the following useful result.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\sigma
be a Weil representation over a non-Archimedean local field, and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
be positive integers, then

a\left({\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(n)\otimes\textup{sp}(m)}\right)=nm\cdot a(\sigma)+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|\left({nm-\min\left\{{n,m}\right\}}\right),

where
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
is the inertia subgroup.

Proof.

It is a standard result that
\textup{sp}(n)\otimes\textup{sp}(m)\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\psi_{i}\otimes\textup{sp}(k_{i})
, for some unramified Weil characters
\psi_{i}
, and note that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}=nm
, and
r=\min\left\{{n,m}\right\}
(see for example
[
4
]
*Proposition 2.5 and proof. Thus, using
Lemma

3.2

\displaystyle a(\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(n)\otimes\textup{sp}(m))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a\left({\sigma\otimes\sigma_{i}\otimes\textup{sp}(k_{i})}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left({k_{i}a(\sigma)+\left({k_{i}-1}\right)\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=nm\cdot a(\sigma)+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|\left({nm-\min\left\{{n,m}\right\}}\right).

∎

3.2.
Conductor exponents for tensor products

We now consider tensor products of representations associated to abelian varieties. We begin with the case that one of the varieties is semistable, where we write an exact equality for the conductor exponent of the tensor product. Following this we provide a bound for the general case, where our approach will be to split the conductor exponent into the tame part and wild part (i.e. the Swan conductor). We deduce the min inequality for the wild part from
Proposition

2.9
, since the action of wild inertia factors through a finite quotient, and for the tame part we prove a stronger max inequality. There are two families of special cases where the max inequality fails to hold but those are contained in the semistable case.

3.2.1.
The semistable case

We begin by computing the exact conductor exponent in the case that one abelian variety is semistable.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
and suppose that
A
is semistable. Then

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\deg(B)a(\rho_{A})-\left({\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)}\right).

Proof.

Let
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
be the inertia subgroup. By assumption, we can write
\textup{Res}_{G_{0}}\rho_{A}\cong\mathds{1}^{m}\oplus\textup{sp}(2)^{n}
for some
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover, using
Proposition

3.1
we write
\rho_{B}=\tau\oplus(\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(2))
for two Weil representations
\sigma,\tau
.

From this description, via
Lemma

3.2
and
Lemma

3.3
, we compute:

\displaystyle a(\rho_{A})

\displaystyle=n,

\displaystyle a(\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=a(\tau)+2a(\sigma)+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|,

\displaystyle a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=ma(\tau)+2na(\tau)+n\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+2ma(\sigma)+m\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|+n\left({4a(\sigma)+2\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=n\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B}).

On the other hand, by definition

\displaystyle\deg(A)=\left|{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=m+n,

\displaystyle\deg(B)=\left|{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|,

\displaystyle\deg(A\boxtimes B)=\left|{\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}\right)^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=m\left({\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|}\right)+n\left({\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+2\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|}\right)

\displaystyle=n\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|+(m+n)(\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|).

From this it is then clear that

\displaystyle\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})

\displaystyle+\deg(B)a(\rho_{A})-\left({\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)}\right)

\displaystyle=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+n\left({\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|}\right)-n\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}),

as required.
∎

3.2.2.
The general case

We now consider the situation where
A
and
B
are arbitrary abelian varieties.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
of residue characteristic
p
. Then

\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A})-\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\}\min\left\{{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})}\right\}.

Proof.

Let
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
be the inertia subgroup. By
Proposition

3.1
we have

\displaystyle\rho_{1}:=\rho_{A}

\displaystyle=\tau_{1}\oplus\left({\sigma_{1}\otimes\textup{sp}(2)}\right),

\displaystyle\rho_{2}:=\rho_{B}

\displaystyle=\tau_{2}\oplus\left({\sigma_{2}\otimes\textup{sp}(2)}\right),

where for
j\in\left\{{1,2}\right\}
,
\textup{Res}_{G_{0}}\tau_{j}
is a symplectic Artin representation and
\textup{Res}_{G_{0}}\sigma_{j}
is a self-dual Artin representation. Let
\tilde{\rho_{j}}=\tau_{j}\oplus\sigma_{j}^{2}
, note that since
\textup{Res}_{G_{0}}\tilde{\rho_{j}}\cong\textup{Res}_{G_{0}}{\rho_{j}}
, we have an equality
\textup{Sw}(\rho_{j})=\textup{Sw}(\tilde{\rho_{j}})
, and so we work here instead.

We write
G=\textup{Gal}(L/F^{\textup{nr}})
for a finite Galois group through which the representations
\tilde{\rho_{j}}
both factor, and let
G_{i}\leq G
be the
i
th ramification group in the lower numbering. We take the finite group filtration
\mathcal{G}=\left({G_{i+1}}\right)_{i\geq 0}
, for which the quantity
a^{\mathcal{G}}
is the Swan conductor (see also
Example

2.6
). If
p>2
, then as
G_{1}
is a
p
-group and by
Proposition

3.1
the representations
\tilde{\rho_{j}}
satisfy the conditions of
Proposition

2.11
, the result follows. If instead
p=2
, then similarly the representations
\tilde{\rho_{j}}
are symplectic, so we instead apply
Proposition

2.9
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

We ignore the possible improvement to our Swan conductor bound above coming from the
\Delta^{\mathcal{G}}
term in
Propositions

2.9
and
2.11
, since these terms do not clearly have a natural arithmetic interpretation.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
. Assume that both
a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})>1
.
Then

\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}{\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}\right)^{G_{0}}}}\right|=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|-\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg)

Proof.

We rearrange the right hand side of the claimed inequality, to obtain

\displaystyle\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|-\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|-\left|{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}\right|\left|{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|-\bigg(\left|{\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}\right)^{G_{0}}}\right|-\left|{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}\right|\left|{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|\bigg)

\displaystyle=\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}{\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}\right)^{G_{0}}}}\right|.

∎

Remark 3.8
.

If
\rho_{A}
and
\rho_{B}
are both tame representations, which necessarily occurs when
\dim(A),\dim(B)
are strictly less than
(p-1)/2
for the residue characteristic
p
, then this immediately presents the conductor exponent of the tensor product in terms of the same inputs we will bound with later.

We now stitch together the tame and wild parts of the conductor to obtain the following theorem.

Theorem 3.9

(
Theorem

1.1
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
of residue characteristic
p
. Define the constant

C_{p}(A,B):=\min\bigg\{\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\},\ \left|{\rho_{A}}\right|-\deg(A),\ \left|{\rho_{B}}\right|-\deg(B)\bigg\}

Then

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-C_{p}(A,B)\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg).

Moreover, if
a(\rho_{A})\leq 1
then in fact

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\deg(B)a(\rho_{A})-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg).

Proof.

We begin with the case
a(\rho_{A})\leq 1
. Since
a(\rho_{A})\leq 1
, by
Lemma

3.2

A
is semistable, so this is a consequence of
Theorem

3.4
. It remains in this case to show that the claimed equality is within the proposed bound. In other words we must show that
\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-C_{p}(A,B)\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}\geq 0
. Indeed, if
a(\rho_{B})=0
then clearly this is
0
, and else we have

\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-C_{p}(A,B)\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}=\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|-1\geq 0.

Now, by symmetry, we proceed under the assumption that both
a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})>1
. In particular, it follows from
Lemma

3.7
and
Proposition

3.5
that

\displaystyle a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})=

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}{\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B}}\right)^{G_{0}}}}\right|+\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg)

\displaystyle-\bigg(\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|+\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\}\min\left\{{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})}\right\}\bigg).

Now, since

\displaystyle\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|+\max\left\{{2,p-1}\right\}\min\left\{{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq C_{p}(A,B)\bigg(\max\left\{{\left|{\frac{\rho_{A}}{\rho_{A}^{G_{0}}}}\right|,\left|{\frac{\rho_{B}}{\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}}\right|}\right\}+\min\left\{{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{A}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{B})}\right\}\bigg)

\displaystyle\geq C_{p}(A,B)\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),\ a(\rho_{B})}\right\},

the result follows.
∎

This bound requires a reasonable amount of information to compute, and so we provide a weaker, but more uniform, bound as a corollary below.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
A
and
B
be abelian varieties over a non-Archimedean local field
F
of residue characteristic
p
. Then

a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})\leq\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-2\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}-\Delta(A,B),

where

\Delta(A,B):=\begin{cases}\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)&\textnormal{if }a(\rho_{A})a(\rho_{B})>1,\\
0&\textnormal{else.}\end{cases}

Proof.

This is immediate from
Theorem

3.9
so long as
C_{p}(A,B)\geq 2
, and so it remains to show this precisely when (without loss of generality)
\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|-\deg(\rho_{A})\leq 1
. In this case, it is clear from
Lemma

3.2
and
Proposition

3.1
that in fact
a(\rho_{A})\leq 1
. In this case, writing
G_{0}\leq G_{F}
for the inertia subgroup, it follows from
Theorem

3.9
that

\displaystyle a(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{B})

\displaystyle=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\deg(B)a(\rho_{A})-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\left|{\rho_{A}}\right|a(\rho_{B})+\left|{\rho_{B}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|a(\rho_{A})-\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg).

In particular, it suffices to prove the new claim that

\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|a(\rho_{A})+\bigg(\deg(A\boxtimes B)-\deg(A)\deg(B)\bigg)\geq 2\min\left\{{a(\rho_{A}),a(\rho_{B})}\right\}+\Delta(A,B).

If
a(\rho_{A})=0
then this is immediate, so via the symmetric argument for
B
we are left with the case that
a(\rho_{A})=1
and
a(\rho_{B})\geq 1
. If
a(\rho_{B})=1
, then both sides are equal to
2
; else
a(\rho_{B})>1
, in which case
\left|{\rho_{B}/\rho_{B}^{G_{0}}}\right|\geq 2
and so the claim is again clear.
∎

We now remark on a useful bound for the difference in the degrees in the case of a single abelian variety.

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a non-Archimedean local field
F
. Suppose
a(\rho_{A})>0
. Then

\deg\left({\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{A}}\right)-\deg(\rho_{A})^{2}\geq 1.

Proof.

By
Proposition

3.1
, we write
\rho_{A}=\tau\oplus\left({\sigma\otimes\textup{sp}(2)}\right)
, where
\sigma,\tau
are self-dual Weil representations. Write
G:=\textup{Gal}(L/F^{\textup{nr}})
for a finite Galois group through which these representations factor,
G_{0}
for the inertia subgroup, and
\textup{Irr}(G_{0})
for the set of irreducible (complex) characters of
G_{0}
. Firstly, we identify

\displaystyle\deg(\rho_{A})

\displaystyle=\left|{\tau^{G_{0}}}\right|+\left|{\sigma^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\tau,\mathds{1}}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}+\left\langle{\sigma,\mathds{1}}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}.

Now, by
Lemma

3.3
,

\displaystyle\deg(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{A})

\displaystyle=\left|{(\tau\otimes\tau)^{G_{0}}}\right|+2\left|{(\tau\otimes\sigma)^{G_{0}}}\right|+2\left|{(\sigma\otimes\sigma)^{G_{0}}}\right|

\displaystyle=\left\langle{\tau,\tau}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}+2\left\langle{\tau,\sigma}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}+2\left\langle{\sigma,\sigma}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G_{0})}\left\langle{\tau,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}^{2}+2\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}^{2}+2\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}\left\langle{\tau,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G_{0})}\left({\left\langle{\tau,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}+\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}}\right)^{2}+\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}.

In particular,

\deg(\rho_{A}\otimes\rho_{A})-\deg(\rho_{A})^{2}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G_{0})\\
\chi\neq\mathds{1}\end{subarray}}\left({\left\langle{\tau,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}+\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{\chi\in\textup{Irr}(G_{0})}\left\langle{\sigma,\chi}\right\rangle_{G_{0}}.

Since
a(\rho_{A})>0
, by
Lemma

3.2
we note that either
\tau\neq\tau^{G_{0}}
or
\sigma\neq 0
. In particular this difference is at least
1
.
∎

3.3.
Global results

Having shown our local results, we conclude by deducing the global results.

Corollary 3.12

(
Theorem

1.4
)
.

Let
A,B
be abelian varieties over a global field
K
of conductors
\mathfrak{N}_{A}
and
\mathfrak{N}_{B}
. Define

\mathfrak{d}(A,B):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega^{0}_{K}\\
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{N}_{A})v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{N}_{B})>1\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p}^{\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(A\boxtimes B)-\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(A)\deg_{\mathfrak{p}}(B)}.

Then

N(A\boxtimes B)\hskip 2.0pt\Bigg|\hskip 2.0pt\frac{\mathfrak{N}_{A}^{2\dim B}\mathfrak{N}_{B}^{2\dim A}}{\mathfrak{d}(A,B)\gcd(\mathfrak{N}_{A},\mathfrak{N}_{B})^{2}},

where
N(A\boxtimes B)
is the conductor of the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(A\boxtimes B,s)
.

Proof.

Immediate from
Corollary

3.10
.
∎

Corollary 3.13
.

Let
A
be an abelian variety over a global field
K
of conductor
\mathfrak{N}_{A}
. Moreover, write
\mathfrak{N}_{A,2}:=\prod\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{p}\in\Omega^{0}_{K},\\
v_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathfrak{N}_{A})\geq 2\end{subarray}}\mathfrak{p}
. Then

N(A\boxtimes A)\hskip 2.0pt\Bigg|\hskip 2.0pt\frac{\mathfrak{N}_{A}^{4\dim A-2}}{\mathfrak{N}_{A,2}},

where
N(A\boxtimes A)
is the conductor of the Rankin–Selberg
L
-function
L(A\boxtimes A,s)
.

Proof.

It follows from
Corollary

3.12
that
N(A\boxtimes A)\mid\frac{\mathfrak{N}_{A}^{4\dim A-2}}{\mathfrak{d}(A,A)}
, and so it is sufficient to show that
\mathfrak{N}_{A,2}\mid\mathfrak{d}(A,A)
. This is immediate from
Lemma

3.11
.
∎

3.4.
Sharpness: a family of examples

The purpose of this subsection to provide a family of nontrivial examples of sharpness for each
p
for the inequalities of
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
. Our examples will be Jacobians
J_{\alpha}
, indexed by
\alpha\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, of the curves

C_{\alpha}/\mathbb{Q}_{p}:y^{2}=x^{p}-\alpha.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
p
be an odd prime number, and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that
a^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\bmod{p^{2}}
. Then the inequalities in
Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
are equalities when
A=J_{a},B=J_{p}
.

Lemma 3.15
.

Let
p
be an odd prime, and
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\times}
such that
a^{p-1}\not\equiv 1\bmod{p^{2}}
. Then
\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}})=1
and
\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{p}})=p
.

Proof.

We compute the Swan exponent using the formula in
[
1
]
*Theorem 12.3; namely, whenever
\alpha\not\in(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{\times})^{p}
it is given by

\textup{Sw}(J_{\alpha})=v(\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})/\mathbb{Q}_{p}})-[\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha}):\mathbb{Q}_{p}]+f_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})/\mathbb{Q}_{p}},

where
\Delta,f
are the discriminant and residue degree respectively. As
x^{p}-p
and
(x+a+p)^{p}-a
are Eisenstein, for both
\alpha\in\left\{{a,p}\right\}
the extension
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})/\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is totally ramified of degree
p
with ring of integers
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\sqrt[p]{\alpha}]
. In particular,
\Delta_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha})/\mathbb{Q}_{p}}=\pm\alpha^{p-1}p^{p}
, and the claim follows.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

3.14
.

We begin with the Swan conductor. It is immediate from
Lemma

3.15
that

\left|{\rho_{J_{a}}}\right|\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{p}})+\left|{\rho_{J_{p}}}\right|\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}})-\max\{2,p{-}1\}\min\{\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}}),\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{p}})\}=p(p-1),

so we will now compute
\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}}\otimes\rho_{J_{p}})
and show this is the same. As the curves
C_{\alpha}
are hyperelliptic, the image of the wild inertia group under
\rho_{J_{\alpha}}
is isomorphic to the wild inertia group of the Galois group of
x^{p}-\alpha
which is precisely
\mathbb{F}_{p}
for
\alpha\in\left\{{a,p}\right\}
. Since they have rational characteristic polynomial on
G_{0}
and dimension
p-1
, we must have

\displaystyle\textup{Res}_{G_{1}}\rho_{J_{a}}

\displaystyle\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p-1}\chi_{a}^{\otimes i}

\displaystyle\text{and }\textup{Res}_{G_{1}}\rho_{J_{p}}\cong\bigoplus_{i=1}^{p-1}\chi_{p}^{\otimes i},

where
\chi_{\alpha}
is a choice of nontrivial Artin representation over
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\zeta_{p})
with dimension
1
factoring through
\mathbb{Q}_{p}(\sqrt[p]{\alpha},\zeta_{p})
. We then directly compute from the definition that
\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}}\otimes\rho_{J_{p}})=p(p-1)
, as required.

For the Artin conductor, when
G_{1}
has no fixed points nor does
G_{0}
and so it is easy to compute from the above that for
\alpha\in\left\{{a,p}\right\}
, we have
a(\rho_{J_{\alpha}})=(p-1)+\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{\alpha}})
, and that the degree terms are all
0
, so

\displaystyle\left|{\rho_{J_{a}}}\right|a(\rho_{J_{p}})+\left|{\rho_{J_{p}}}\right|a(\rho_{J_{a}})-C_{p}(J_{a},J_{p})\min\{a(\rho_{J_{a}}),a(\rho_{J_{p}})\}-(\deg(J_{a}\boxtimes J_{p})-\deg(J_{a})\deg(J_{p}))

\displaystyle=p(p-1)+(2p-1)(p-1)-(p-1)p-0

\displaystyle=(2p-1)(p-1).

Moreover,
a(\rho_{J_{a}}\otimes\rho_{J_{p}})=(p-1)^{2}+\textup{Sw}(\rho_{J_{a}}\otimes\rho_{J_{p}})=(2p-1)(p-1)
as required.
∎

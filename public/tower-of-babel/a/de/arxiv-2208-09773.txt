Skew Howe duality for Types 𝐁𝐃 \mathbf{BD} via q q -Clifford algebras
Willie Aboumrad

Abstract
. We extend a quantized skew Howe duality result for Type 𝐀 \mathbf{A} algebras to orthogonal types via a seesaw. We develop an operator commutant version of the First Fundamental Theorem of invariant theory for U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{so}_{n}) using a double centralizer property inside a quantized Clifford algebra. We obtain a multiplicity-free decomposition of tensor powers of the U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 2 ​ n ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n}) spin representation by explicitly computing joint highest weights with respect to an action of U q ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 2 ​ n ) ⊗ U q ′ ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔬 m ) U_{q}(\mathfrak{so}_{2n})\otimes U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}_{m}) . Clifford algebras are an essential feature of our work: they provide a unifying framework for classical and quantized skew Howe duality results that can be extended to include orthogonal algebras of types 𝐁𝐃 \mathbf{BD} .

U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}_{m})\supset U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}_{m-1})\supset\cdots\supset U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}_{3})

[

It is a remarkable feature of the quantum case in the orthogonal setting that the commutant of the

[
Abo22

U_{q}^{\prime}(\mathfrak{so}_{m})
as a bona fide subalgebra of

using explicit formulas in terms of the standard

[
Abo22
]
to obtain a quantized duality theorem for Types
\mathbf{BD}

3.
The classical case

In this section we develop orthogonal duality theory in the classical case. In particular, we (re)-prove the classical
O(n)\times SO(m)
-duality
3.6
, this time using a double centralizer property inside a Clifford algebra. This theorem is well-known to experts but our method lays the foundations for our treatment of the more difficult quantum case in
Section

4
.

We prove
3.6
in three steps. First, in
Section

3.1
we show that for any complex vector space
V
there are actions of
\mathfrak{so}(V\oplus V^{*})
and of
\mathfrak{so}(V)
on the exterior algebra
\bigwedge(V)
that factor through the Clifford algebra
Cl(V\oplus V^{*})
. Then we construct commuting embeddings of
\mathfrak{so}_{2n}
and
\mathfrak{so}_{m}
into
Cl\left(\mathbb{C}^{nm}\oplus(\mathbb{C}^{nm})^{*}\right)
in
Section

3.2
. Finally, we compute a multiplicity-free decomposition of
\bigwedge(\mathbb{C}^{nm})
as an
\mathfrak{o}_{2n}\otimes\mathfrak{so}_{m}
-module in
Section

3.3
.

As in
[
Abo22
]
, we work at the Lie algebra level throughout, since we are most interested in the quantum case and it is the enveloping algebra
U(\mathfrak{g})
, rather than the Lie group
G=\exp(\lieg)
, that more closely resembles
U_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
.

S

Remark 3.2
.

The notation

\mathfrak{so}^{(1,1)}
, and

is motivated by the following observations. Each element in

\bigwedge^{p-2}(W)

\mathfrak{so}^{(2,0)}
is a product of two
raising operators
taking
\bigwedge^{p}(W)
into
\bigwedge^{p+2}(W)

\mathfrak{so}^{(1,1)}
are products of a raising and a lowering operator, thus preserving components of homogeneous degree.

The subalgebra
\mathfrak{so}^{(1,1)}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{gl}(V)

\mathfrak{gl}(V)\cong V\otimes V^{*}
and
\{\varepsilon_{v},\iota_{f}\}=f(v)

[

,
]

\displaystyle\varphi(X_{v,f})={1\over 2}[\varepsilon_{v},\iota_{f}]=\varepsilon_{v}\iota_{f}-\frac{1}{2}f(v),

in

acts on

by the rightmost operator in

Note that the

-action defined by
[

]

Aug-PDG: Linear Convergence of Convex Optimization with Inequality Constraints
Min Meng, Xiuxian Li

Abstract
This paper investigates the convex optimization problem with general convex inequality constraints. To cope with this problem, a discrete-time algorithm, called augmented primal-dual gradient algorithm (Aug-PDG), is studied and analyzed. It is shown that Aug-PDG can converge semi-globally to the optimizer at a linear rate under some mild assumptions, such as the quadratic gradient growth condition for the objective function, which is strictly weaker than strong convexity. To our best knowledge, this paper is the first to establish a linear convergence for the studied problem in the discrete-time setting, where an explicit bound is provided for the stepsize. Finally, a numerical example is presented to illustrate the efficacy of the theoretical finding.



I
Introduction

This paper deals with the constrained optimization problem formulated as follows:

\displaystyle\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}~~f(x)

\displaystyle\text{~~s.t.}~~g(x)\leq 0,

(1)

where the objective function
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and
g(x)=(g_{1}(x),g_{2}(x),\ldots,g_{m}(x))^{T}
with
g_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
being convex and continuously differentiable. By resorting to the (or augmented) Lagrangian
L(x,\lambda)
of problem (
1
), the corresponding (or augmented) primal-dual gradient algorithm (PDG) (or Aug-PDG) can be designed as

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k}),

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}

\displaystyle=[\lambda_{k}+\alpha\nabla_{\lambda}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})]_{+},

(2)

where
\alpha
is a positive stepsize and
[\cdot]_{+}
denotes the projection operator onto the nonnegative orthants component-wisely. It is known that (
2
) can find a saddle point of the Lagrangian
L(x,\lambda)
, and thus it has been extensively studied to solve the constrained optimization problem
[
1
]
.

Optimization has wide applications in power systems
[
2
,
3
]
, wireless communication
[
4
]
, game theory
[
5
,
6
]
, to name just a few. To date, there is a large body of literature on theoretical analysis of asymptotic convergence of various algorithms, including primal-dual gradient-based algorithms, for tackling the optimization problem under different settings
[
7
,

,

,

,

,

,

.

In recent decades, researchers have focused on the exponential/linear convergence of primal-dual gradient-based algorithms.
It is well-known that when the objective function is strongly convex and smooth, the gradient decent algorithm for unconstrained convex optimization can achieve global exponential convergence in continuous-time and global linear convergence in discrete-time. In the context of constrained optimization with equality constraints
Ax=b
or affine inequality constraints
Ax\leq b
, PDG is proved to converge globally and exponentially in continuous-time setup
[
16
]
. A proximal gradient
flow was proposed in
[
17
]
, which can be applied to resolve convex optimization problems with affine inequality constraints and has global exponential convergence when
A
has full row rank. Local exponential convergence of the primal-dual gradient dynamics can be established with the help of spectral bounds of saddle matrices
[
18
]
. Recently, the authors in
[
19
]
proved that the Aug-PDGD in continuous-time for optimization with affine equality and inequality constraints achieves global exponential convergence, and the global linear converge of primal-dual gradient optimization (PDGO) in discrete-time was discussed in
[
20
]
by contraction theory.
It should be noted that the aforementioned works focus on unconstrained optimization or constrained optimization with affine equality and/or affine inequality constraints. For the case with nonlinear inequality constraints, the asymptotic/sublinear convergence has been extensively studied such as in
[
21
]
. However, the linear convergence for the optimization with nonlinear inequality constraints is seldom investigated in the literature. One exception is the recent work
[
22
]
, where the authors established a semi-global exponential convergence of Aug-PDGD in the sense that the convergence rate depends on the distance from the initial point to the optimal point.

However,
[
22
]
concentrates on the continuous-time dynamics. As discrete-time algorithms are easily implemented in practical applications, in this paper, the discrete-time algorithm is addressed for the optimization problem with nonlinear inequality constraints. Theoretical analysis based on a quadratic Lyapunov function that has non-zero off-diagonal terms is presented to show that the Aug-PDG achieves semi-global linear convergence, where an explicit bound is established for the stepsize. The numerical results suggest that the Aug-PDG indeed has different linear convergence rates for different initial points.

III

II
Preliminaries

Consider problem (
1
). An augmented Lagrangian associated with problem (
1
) is introduced as
[
23
]

\displaystyle L(x,\lambda):=f(x)+U_{\rho}(x,\lambda),

(3)

where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\rho>0
is the penalty parameter, and

\displaystyle U_{\rho}(x,\lambda):=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{[\rho g_{i}(x)+\lambda_{i}]_{+}^{2}-\lambda_{i}^{2}}{2\rho}.

(4)

It can be verified that
U_{\rho}(x,\lambda)
is convex in
x
and concave in
\lambda
, and
U_{\rho}(x,\lambda)
is continuously differentiable, i.e.,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}U_{\rho}(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}[\rho g_{i}(x)+\lambda_{i}]_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x),

(5)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\lambda}U_{\rho}(x,\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{[\rho g_{i}(x)+\lambda_{i}]_{+}-\lambda_{i}}{\rho}e_{i},

(6)

where
e_{i}
is an
n
-dimensional vector with the
i
th entry being 1 and others 0. Then the augmented primal-dual gradient algorithm (Aug-PDG) can be explicitly written as

\displaystyle x_{k+1}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=x_{k}-\alpha\nabla f(x_{k})-\alpha\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}[\rho g_{i}(x_{k})+\lambda_{i,k}]_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x_{k}),

(7a)

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}+\alpha\nabla_{\lambda}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=\lambda_{k}+\alpha\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{[\rho g_{i}(x_{k})+\lambda_{i,k}]_{+}-\lambda_{i,k}}{\rho}e_{i},

(7b)

where
\alpha\in(0,\rho]
is the stepsize to be specified. Here, the initial conditions are arbitrarily chosen as
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
.

To proceed, the following results are vital for solving the constrained optimization problem.

Lemma 1

For Aug-PDG (
7
), if
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
, then
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
for all
k\geq 0
.

Proof.
This result can be proved by mathematical induction. First note that
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
. Assume now that
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
for some
k\geq 0
, then by (
7b
), one has that

\displaystyle\lambda_{k+1}

\displaystyle=(1-\alpha/\rho)\lambda_{k}+\alpha\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\frac{[\rho g_{i}(x_{k})+\lambda_{i,k}]_{+}}{\rho}e_{i}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\alpha/\rho)\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the first inequality is obtained based on the definition of the notation
[\cdot]_{+}
and the second inequality is derived following
0<\alpha\leq\rho
and inductive assumption
\lambda_{k}\geq 0
. The proof is thus completed.
\blacksquare

Lemma 2

A primal-dual pair
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is an equilibrium point of the Aug-PDG (
7
) if and only if
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is a Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) point of (
1
).

Proof.
If a primal-dual pair
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is an equilibrium point of the Aug-PDG (
7
), that is,

\displaystyle x^{*}

\displaystyle=x^{*}-\alpha\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*}),

\displaystyle\lambda^{*}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{*}+\alpha\nabla_{\lambda}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*}),

then
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
and
\nabla_{\lambda}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
. For
\nabla_{\lambda}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
, an equivalent condition is that for any
i\in[m]
,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}^{*}=[\rho g_{i}(x^{*})+\lambda_{i}^{*}]_{+},

(8)

which implies
\lambda_{i}^{*}\geq 0
,
g_{i}(x^{*})\leq 0
, and
\lambda_{i}^{*}g_{i}(x^{*})=0
. For
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
, one can equivalently obtain that
\nabla f(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}[\rho g_{i}(x^{*})+\lambda_{i}^{*}]_{+}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})=\nabla f(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{*}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})=0
. Thus, it can be claimed that the primal-dual pair
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is a KKT point.

Conversely, if
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is a KKT point of (
1
), then
\nabla f(x^{*})+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}^{*}\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})=0
,
\lambda_{i}^{*}g_{i}(x^{*})=0
,
\lambda^{*}\geq 0
and
g_{i}(x^{*})\leq 0
. Via a simple computation,
\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
and
\nabla_{\lambda}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})=0
, which implies that
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is an equilibrium point of the Aug-PDG (
7
).
\blacksquare

Main Results

III-A

Convergence Results

The following assumptions are essential for deriving the main result.

Assumption 1

The problem (
1
) has a unique feasible solution
x^{*}
, and at
x^{*}
, the linear independence constraint qualification (LICQ) holds at
x^{*}
, i.e.,
\{\nabla g_{i}(x^{*})\mid i\in{\mathcal{I}}\}
is linearly independent, where
{\mathcal{I}}:=\{i\in[m]\mid g_{i}(x^{*})=0\}
is the so-called active set at
x^{*}
.

Under Assumption
1
, the optimal Lagrangian multiplier
\lambda^{*}
is also unique
[
24
]
. Denote by
J
the Jacobian of
g(x)
at
x^{*}
and
J_{\mathcal{I}}
the matrix composed of the rows of
J
with the indices in
{\mathcal{I}}
. LICQ in Assumption
1
also implies that
J_{\mathcal{I}}J_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top}\succ 0

[
22
]
. Define

\displaystyle\kappa:=\lambda_{\min}(J_{\mathcal{I}}J_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top})>0

(9)

to be the smallest eigenvalue of
J_{\mathcal{I}}J_{\mathcal{I}}^{\top}
.

Assumption 2

The objective function
f(x)
has a quadratic gradient growth with parameter
\mu>0
over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle(\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(x^{*}))^{\top}(x-x^{*})\geq\mu\|x-x^{*}\|^{2},~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(10)

The concept of quadratic gradient growth was introduced in
[
25
]
, which is a relaxation of strong convexity condition for guaranteeing linear convergence of gradient-based optimization algorithms. In fact, the class of functions having quadratic gradient growth include the strongly convex functions as a proper subset and some functions with quadratic gradient growth are even not convex.

Assumption 3

The objective function
f
is
l
-smooth over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e.,
\|\nabla f(x)-\nabla f(y)\|\leq l\|x-y\|
for any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For any
i\in[m]
,
g_{i}(x)
is
L_{gi}
-smooth and has bounded gradient, i.e.,
\|\nabla g_{i}(x)-\nabla g_{i}(y)\|\leq L_{gi}\|x-y\|
and
\|\nabla g_{i}(x)\|\leq B_{gi}
for some
L_{gi},B_{gi}>0
and any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Denote
{\mathcal{I}}^{c}:=[m]\backslash{\mathcal{I}}
,
L_{g}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}L_{gi}^{2}}
and
B_{g}:=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}B_{gi}^{2}}
. Under Assumption
3
, one can obtain that

\displaystyle\|J\|

\displaystyle\leq B_{g},

(11)

\displaystyle\|g(x)-g(y)\|

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}(g_{i}(x)-g_{i}(y))^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{m}B_{gi}^{2}\|x-y\|^{2}}

\displaystyle=B_{g}\|x-y\|.

(12)

Denote
d_{0}:=\sqrt{\|x_{0}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{0}-\lambda^{*}\|^{2}}
. Before giving the main result of this paper, it is convenient to list the following concept similar to that in continuous-time setting
[
26
]

Definition 1

Consider the dynamics

\displaystyle\|z(t)-z_{e}\|\leq c\gamma^{t}\|z_{0}-z_{e}\|,~\forall t\geq 0.

Then the main result is presented as follows.

Theorem 1

Under Assumptions

\displaystyle\alpha<\min\left\{1,\rho,\frac{2\mu}{b_{1}+2a_{4}\delta},\frac{\kappa\delta}{2b_{2}+4a_{5}\delta},\frac{1-\pi^{*}}{2\rho(b_{2}+2a_{5}\delta)}\right\},

\displaystyle\delta<\min\left\{\frac{\mu}{2a_{3}},\frac{1-\pi^{*}}{2\rho(\kappa+8B_{g}^{2}+L_{g}^{2}(1-\pi^{*}))},B_{g}^{-1}\right\},

where

Proof. The proof is postponed to the next subsection.

Remark 1

The selection of parameters
\alpha
and
\delta
ensures that
c_{1},c_{2},c_{3}
are positive, and then
\gamma>0
can be guaranteed. From Theorem
1
, one can see that the convergence rate is related to
\pi^{*}=[\rho\max\{g_{i}(x^{*})\}/(\sqrt{C}d_{0})+1]_{+}^{2}
and decrease to 0 as
d_{0}
goes to infinity. The decreasing rate also changes as
(x_{k},\lambda_{k})
approaches the optimal point. Specifically, the decreasing rates are small at the beginning and then become large when
(x_{k},\lambda_{k})
goes to the optimal point. Therefore, Theorem
1
does not guarantee the existence of a global linear convergence rate, and consequently only semi-global linear stability can be ensured.

Remark 2

To our best knowledge, this paper is the first to investigate the linear convergence for problem (
1
) in discrete-time setup. Compared with the most related literature
[
22
]
, where a continuous-time algorithm, called Aug-PDGD, was studied with a semi-global exponential convergence, a discrete-time algorithm Aug-PDG is analyzed here with a semi-global linear convergence. Although discrete-time algorithms may be obtained by discretizing the continuous-time Aug-PDGD using such as explicit Euler method, it is unclear how to select the sampling stepsize to guarantee the convergence especially in the sense of semi-global convergence. In comparison, an explicit bound on the stepsize
\alpha
is established here in Theorem
1
.

III-B

Proof of Theorem 1

To prove Theorem
1
, an intermediate result is needed as follows.

Lemma 3

[
19
]

For any

, there exists
\xi\in[0,1]
such that
[y]_{+}-[y^{*}]_{+}=\xi(y-y^{*})
. Specifically,
\xi
can be chosen as
\xi=\frac{[y]_{+}-[y^{*}]_{+}}{y-y^{*}}

Then the proof of Theorem

Define

\displaystyle{V}_{\delta,k}

\displaystyle=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{x}_{k}-x^{*}\\
\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{*}\end{array}\right]^{\top}Q_{\delta}\left[\begin{array}[]{c}{x}_{k}-x^{*}\\
\lambda_{k}-\lambda^{*}\end{array}\right],

where

\displaystyle Q_{\delta}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}I_{n}&\delta J^{\top}\\
\delta J&I_{m}\end{array}\right].

As
\delta<B_{g}^{-1}
from (

), one has
I_{m}-\delta^{2}JJ^{\top}\succeq(1-\delta^{2}B_{g}^{2})I_{m}\succ 0
, which implies

by Schur complement. Then, in the following, we discuss the bound of

\displaystyle{V}_{\delta,k+1}=

\displaystyle\|{x}_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\|\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\delta({x}_{k+1}-x^{*})^{\top}J^{\top}(\lambda_{k+1}-\lambda^{*}).

Note that
(x^{*},\lambda^{*})
is the KKT point of (
1
), that is,

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})

\displaystyle=0,

(23)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\lambda}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})

\displaystyle=0.

(24)

By iterations in (
7

\displaystyle\|{x}_{k+1}-x^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-\alpha\nabla_{x}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})-x^{*}+\alpha\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|x_{k}-x^{*}\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\nabla_{x}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})-\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*})\|^{2}

\displaystyle~~~-2\alpha(\nabla_{x}L(x_{k},\lambda_{k})-\nabla_{x}L(x^{*},\lambda^{*}))^{\top}(x_{k}-x^{*}).

Note that

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}U(x_{k},\lambda_{k})-\nabla_{x}U(x^{*},\lambda^{*})

\displaystyle[\rho g_{i}(x_{k})+\lambda_{i,k}]_{+}-[\rho g_{i}(x^{*})+\lambda_{i}^{*}]_{+}

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{x}U(x_{k},\lambda_{k})-\nabla_{x}U(x^{*},\lambda^{*})\|

V
Conclusion

In this paper, the linear convergence of an Aug-PDG in discrete-time for convex optimization with nonlinear inequality constraints has been investigated. Under some mild assumptions, the Aug-PDG has been proved to semi-globally converge at a linear rate, which depends on the distance from the initial point to the optimal point. Future research of interest may be to devise a method for solving the optimization problem under nonlinear constraints with a global linear convergence rate.

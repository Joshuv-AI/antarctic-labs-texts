Efficient-Empathy: Towards Efficient and Effective Selection of Empathy Data
Linzhuang Sun †§ , Hao Liang †‡ , Jingxuan Wei § , Linkun Sun ⋄ , Bihui Yu § , Bin Cui ‡∗ , Wentao Zhang ‡∗

Abstract
. In recent years, with the rapid advancements in large language models (LLMs), achieving excellent empathetic response capability has become a crucial prerequisite. Consequently, managing and understanding empathetic datasets has gained increasing importance. However, empathetic data are typically trained without any quality selection, leading to inefficient data usage and wasted computational resources. Additionally, using raw data can result in low performance in empathetic dialogues. In this work, we present Efficient-Empathy, a sensibility and rationality score-based data selection algorithm that automatically selects sensibility and rationality data while discarding low-quality data. With only the sensibility data (59% of the full dataset), our trained sensibility model efficiently achieves state-of-the-art (SoTA) performance. Furthermore, with multiple data selection hyperparameters, the sensibility model demonstrates SoTA performance, showcasing the robustness of our method. By integrating sensibility and rationality data with a MoE structure, we achieve even higher performance, demonstrating the effectiveness of our Efficient-Empathy algorithm.

The core contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

•

New Perspective.

Low data efficiency and low robustness are significant impediments to the practical adoption of empathy models. To the best of our knowledge, this study represents the first attempt to address these challenges from a data selection perspective.

•

New Method.

We propose a new data selection method for empathy, introducing the first sensibility and rationality-based data selection framework. Utilizing our meticulously curated sensibility and rationality scores, we pioneer the integration of sensibility and rationality data with a MoE model. Our method, which combines curated selected data with the MoE model, enables efficient, robust, and effective management of empathy data.

•

SoTA Performance.

(1)
High Efficiency.
From a data-driven perspective, we analyze the distribution of sensibility and rationality cognition in empathetic dialogues. With a carefully curated dataset, our sensibility expert model outperforms the baseline using only 59% of the data.

(2)
High Robustness.
As shown in Table

(3)
Effectiveness in Empathetic Response.

2.
Related Work

2.1.
Empathetic Response Generation

Imbuing models with empathy to enhance emotional experience is a crucial aspect of AGI.
Rashkin et al. (2019)
constructed the ED dataset, the most authoritative dataset for measuring the empathic abilities of models, which has since become a benchmark for empathy research. Building on this dataset, numerous researchers have focused on improving models’ empathy comprehension by integrating emotional labels and external knowledge.

Sabour et al. (2022)
introduces commonsense knowledge inference into this task by enriching historical conversation data with the pre-trained COEMT model
(
Bosselut et al., 2019
)
. Diverged from this approach,
Li et al. (2022b)
incorporate external knowledge through Graph Neural Networks (GNN) for context encoding. Additionally,
Wang et al. (2022)
posit that using detailed sentiment labels can improve the accuracy of capturing user sentiment and puts forward a sentiment loss mechanism with multiple levels of granularity to enhance model training. Moreover,
Kim et al. (2022)
strives to provide empathetic responses by analyzing context at the word level.
Chen and Liang (2022)
employed a emotion detection algorithm based on psychological principles to pinpoint important statements in conversations.
Zhao et al. (2023)
take a different approach by not only recognizing the emotions of others but also assessing saved model emotional state. Furthermore,
Qian et al. (2023a)
divides the empathetic response task into two steps: verifying the semantic content and infusing emotional expression.

In the LLM era, many researchs explore ways to boost the empathic potential of models through meticulous design of prompts
(
Qian et al., 2023b
;
Wang et al., 2023
;
Yang et al., 2024
)
. Differently,
Sun et al. (2023)
delves into the general sensibility and rationality, exploring their respective contributions to empathy. However, the importance of fine-grained sensibility and rationality cognition for empathy still lacks comprehensive investigation.

2.2.
Data Quality and Data Selection

The advent of large language models has brought about a substantial increase in the volume of training data.
(
Touvron et al., 2023a
;
OpenAI, 2023b
)
In this scenario, the quality and quantity of data become paramount. LLMs, trained on vast amounts of data, can capture subtle nuances and complex patterns in language, excelling in various natural language processing tasks. However, the increase in data volume also brings new challenges, particularly in data management, cleaning, and annotation.
(
Bai et al., 2024
)
In this section, we mainly discuss the effectiveness of data quality and data selection.

Data Quality

: High-quality data can significantly enhance the performance of models.
(
meta llama, 2024
)
As the volume of data increases, ensuring high data quality becomes more challenging because it requires more resources for data cleaning, selection and annotation.
(
Bai et al., 2024
)
Poor quality data can lead to models learning incorrect patterns and making inaccurate predictions.

Data Selection

:
LLMs-based methods were commonly used in data selection.
(
Bai et al., 2024
)
For instance,
Du et al. (2023)
leverages DeBERTa
(
He et al., 2020
)
for scoring, retaining high-quality data, and combining it with the k-center greedy algorithm to select diverse data.
Chen et al. (2023b)
score the accuracy of data using ChatGPT to pick out high-quality data.
Xu et al. (2023)
use GPT-4 to rewrite data to increase their complexity and then streamline it by reducing its variety and improving its quality.
Liu et al. (2023)
train two models using ChatGPT’s labeled data to score the quality and complexity of the data.
Lu et al. (2023)
rely on ChatGPT to tag each instance, defining its complexity and diversity based on these tags.
Parkar et al. (2024)
first cluster the data, and then use GPT-4 to select high-quality data for each cluster.

2.3.
Mixture of Expert

The MoE framework is initially introduced by
Jacobs et al. (1991)
with the aim of merging the functionalities of domain models.
Shazeer et al. (2017)
suggestes the use of token-level routing and sparsely-gated mechanisms to enhance the robustness of expert model. Recently, as
Lepikhin et al. (2020)
and
Fedus et al. (2022)
extend MoE to the transformer structure, a growing number of sophisticated routing mechanisms
(
Pan et al., 2024
)
, load balancing techniques
(
Zhong et al., 2024
)
, and distributed training strategies
(
Gale et al., 2023
)
have been proposed.

Recently, the integration of MoE with LLMs has yielded significant advancements in general domains. Mixtral-8x22B
(
Jiang et al., 2024
)
combines eight 22B parameter models, achieving performance comparable to GPT. DeepSeekMOE
(
Dai et al., 2024
)
enrich the expert configuration by implementing shared and fine-grained expert mechanisms. Qwen1.5-MoE-A2.7B
Team (2024)
, utilizing the MoE approach, match the performance of a 7B parameter model while activating only one-third of its parameters. However, the application of MoE in the domain of empathetic responses remains largely unexplored.

3.
Efficient-Empathy Data Selection

Figure 3
.
The overall pipeline of Efficient-Empathy consists of three parts: (a) the Data Selection Module, which classifies the empathetic dataset into sensibility, rationality, and discard datasets; (b) the Domain Expert Training Module, which uses the selected datasets to fine-tune LLMs and acquire sensibility and rationality experts; and (c) the Expert Mixing Module, which integrates the sensibility and rationality experts into the MoE empathy model.

3.1.
ED Datasets

Our study is based on the authoritative EmpatheticDialogues (ED) dataset
(
Rashkin et al., 2019
)
, which consists of 25,000 daily conversations encompassing 32 uniformly distributed emotional labels. This dataset is meticulously curated through crowdsourcing, involving 810 workers from Amazon Mechanical Turk
1
1

1

https://www.mturk.com
. Each conversation is constructed in a one-on-one format, pairing two workers. One worker assumes the role of the speaker, responding according to a given emotional label and situation, while the other worker assumes the role of the listener, providing empathetic responses to the speaker. On average, each conversation consists of 4.31 exchanges, with each exchange containing approximately 15.2 words.

3.2.
ED Data Selection

We use the prompt shown in Figure

S

Before evaluating the processed dataset, we perform data cleaning to ensure the accuracy of the GPT-evaluated scores. This includes correcting common typographical errors, such as fixing "Rationalality" to "Rationality".

After the data cleaning process, we start the selection process by establishing a threshold value

3.3.
Distribution of Rationality and Sensibility

In this section, we provide an analysis of the distribution of rationality and sensibility scores. Each dialogue in the dataset is evaluated on a scale from 0 to 10 for both rationality and sensibility. To visualize the distribution of these scores, we plot a 2D histogram showing the frequency of each score from 0 to 10.

Figure
4
illustrates the 2D histogram for both rationality and sensibility scores. The x-axis represents the rationality scores, while the y-axis represents the sensibility scores. The color intensity indicates the frequency of each combination of scores, with a color bar on the right side providing the exact frequency values.

From the 2D histogram, we can observe several notable trends and patterns:

•

High-Frequency Clusters
: The highest frequency cluster is found at the combination of a rationality score of 2 and a sensibility score of 8, with a frequency of 8603. This indicates that dialogues with low rationality and high sensibility are common in the dataset.

•

General Trends
: There is a tendency for dialogues to score higher on sensibility compared to rationality. This is evidenced by the higher frequencies of scores in the upper part of the y-axis (sensibility scores) compared to the x-axis (rationality scores). This matches the ED dataset, which typically contains dialogues with high sensibility content.

•

Balanced Scores
: Dialogues with balanced rationality and sensibility scores (e.g., both scores around 5) are less frequent. This highlights the rarity of dialogues that equally exhibit both logical reasoning and emotional depth, suggesting a potential area for improving dialogue generation models to better balance these attributes.

•

Outliers
: Some outliers exist where dialogues have extremely high sensibility but very low rationality, and vice versa. These outliers are crucial for understanding edge cases and improving model robustness in handling diverse dialogue scenarios.

This visualization helps in understanding the overall distribution and identifying any trends or patterns in the data, which can be valuable for further analysis and improving the dialogue system.

4.2.
Empathetic MoE Architecture

Building upon the insights of domain experts, we employ the MoE approach to incorporate them into a comprehensive empathetic model, denoted as
M_{e}
. Diverging from the Branch-Train-Merge (BTM)
(
Li et al., 2022a
)
methodology, which exclusively consolidates the final feature representations, our approach introduces a collaborative configuration of Feed-Forward Network (FFN) layers. It further allocates decision-making weights to individual experts via a sophisticated soft routing system, thereby enhancing the model’s selective empathy capabilities.

In a single expert model, each transformer block comprises an attention module and an FFN module. The structure of
M_{e}
is similar, with the exception that the FFN layer is substituted by an MoE layer, which is a combination of multiple single expert FFNs. Particularly, in the
i
-th transformer block, the FFN layers of
M_{s}
and
M_{r}
are denoted as
FF_{i}^{s}(x)
and
FF_{i}^{r}(x)
, respectively. The
i
-th MoE layer
MoE_{i}(x)
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle MoE_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=Router_{i}(x)\cdot FF_{i}^{s}(x)

(6)

\displaystyle\quad+(1-Router_{i}(x))\cdot FF_{i}^{r}(x),

where

(7)

\begin{split}Router_{i}(x)=SoftMax(W_{i}x+b_{i}),\end{split}

Router_{i}
serves as a soft routing mechanism that output values ranging between 0 and 1 to regulate the influence of domain experts.

W_{i}
and
b_{i}
is the linear transformation and bias of the
i
-th MoE layer. For the parameters and weights of other layers in the Efficient-Empathy model, we initialize them using the average weights of the corresponding layers in the expert models:

(8)

M_{e}^{i}(x)=\begin{cases}\text{MoE}_{i}(x)&\text{MoE Layer}\\
\text{Average}(M_{s}^{i}(x),M_{r}^{i}(x))&\text{Other Layer}\end{cases}

After constructing the model, we introduce new random initialized router parameters and then average the weights of domain experts. Consequently, a second stage of training is conducted in the subsequent experiment to activate the overall parameter weights of the empathy model.

5.
Experiments

In this section, we first introduce the experimental setups, including data selection and the training process. We then aim to answer the following questions to verify the effectiveness, efficiency, and robustness of our proposed Efficient-Empathy model:

Q1
: Can our model achieve SoTA performance with the sensibility and rationality dataset selected by Efficient-Empathy?

Q2
: Can we achieve SoTA performance efficiently by only utilizing the sensibility data?

Q3
: Can our model robustly and consistently achieve SoTA performance with varying data selection thresholds?

Q4
: Can our model achieve good human evaluation results, given the importance of human evaluation for empathy?

Q5
: Can we visualize our results with well-analyzed case studies?

Q6
: Is the SoTA performance achieved due to our MoE structure or the Efficient-Empathy data selection method?

5.1.
Experimental Setup

5.1.1.
Datasets

We utilize the ED dataset with three Efficient-Empathy selected subsets: Sensibility, Rationality, and Neutral.

5.1.2.
Models

We employ four commonly used LLMs: Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat, LLaMA2-13B-Instruct, LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, Qwen1.5-72B-Chat. The models are trained using HuggingFace’s Trainer framework. During the experiments, we use the DeepSpeed library with AdamW as the optimizer and set zero_optimization to 3. For LoRA-Tuning, we use a rank of 8, an alpha value of 32, a dropout rate of 0.1, and target specific modules, including q_proj, k_proj, v_proj, o_proj, gate_proj, up_proj, and down_proj.

5.1.3.
MoE Training

To develop a model with comprehensive empathetic capacity, we employ domain-specific experts using the MoE approach. Initially, we create two duplicates of the seed model to train sensibility and rationality experts in parallel. After completing the training of these experts, we integrate them into a MoE model using the method described in Section
4
. Subsequently, all parameters of the empathy model, except the router, are kept frozen while we perform further fine-tuning on the entire training dataset. This training phase aims to enable the router to distinguish between tokens with disparate cognitive attributes and assign corresponding expert weights, while preventing the overfitting of sub-experts.

Automatic Evaluation Metrics
: We use corpus-level BLEU
(B-1 to B-4), sentence-level ROUGE (R-1, R-2), and Distinct (Dist-1, Dist-2) as automatic evaluation metrics. BLEU and ROUGE scores quantify the resemblance between the generated text and the ground-truth text, with higher scores indicating greater likeness. Distinct-N evaluates the diversity of the content, with higher values suggesting a wider range of diverse representations. The perplexity metric is not utilized as it measures confidence in the generated sentences, which is not specific to empathy scenarios. Given the absence of authoritative literature demonstrating that LLMs surpass humans in empathy judgment, we rely on scientific human evaluation to assess the methods’ effectiveness.

Human Evaluation Metrics
: We select Coherence, Empathy, Information, and Continuity as human evaluation metrics.

•

Coherence
: Evaluates the correspondence between the text produced by the model and the desired response.

•

Empathy
: Assesses the model’s ability to understand the speaker’s situation and effectively express concern.

•

Informativeness
: Gauges the amount of information present in the generated responses.

•

Continuity
: Reflects the model’s capability to sustain the conversation.

We conduct an A/B test to compare the effectiveness of our model against several baselines. Specifically, we randomly select 200 examples from the test dataset. For each instance, the context is paired with two responses: one generated by our model and the other by a baseline model. Three evaluators assess each pair of responses and determine a winner, a loser, or a tie based on the four dimensions: Coherence, Empathy, Information, and Continuity.

The discrepancy between BLEU and Distinct metrics can be attributed to their fundamental differences. Distinct-N measures the diversity of the generated text by calculating the ratio of unique n-grams, promoting lexical variety and creativity. In contrast, BLEU-n and ROUGE-n assess the n-gram overlap between the generated text and reference texts, focusing on precision and recall, respectively. High Distinct-N scores indicate a broad range of expressions, which, while enhancing diversity, reduce the likelihood of exact n-gram matches with the reference texts, thereby resulting in lower BLEU-n and ROUGE-n scores. This highlights the inherent trade-off between achieving diversity and maintaining overlap with reference texts in text generation tasks. Given that the Distinct metric of our model significantly surpasses that of the baselines, we have opted not to further analyze the distinctiveness of LLMs.

5.3.
Efficient Sensibility Data

To address
Q2
, in this section, we evaluate the effect of sensibility on empathetic performance. We selected 23,862 (59%) dialogues from a total of 40,250 based on their sensibility scores. Specifically, dialogues with rationality scores below a certain threshold and sensibility scores above it were included in the sensibility dataset. We then used both the selected sensibility data (Sensibility) and the full dataset (Full F.T.) to fine-tune three commonly used Large Language Models (LLMs): LLaMA3-8B-Instruct, Qwen1.5-7B-Chat, and Qwen1.5-1.8B-Chat.

From Table
2
, we observe that the models trained on the sensibility data outperform those trained on the full dataset across all three models. This demonstrates the efficiency and effectiveness of our data selection method, indicating that focusing on dialogues with higher sensibility and lower rationality improves empathetic performance.

Our results verify the hypothesis that robust sensibility is essential for empathetic performance. Training models with a focused dataset of high sensibility and low rationality dialogues (59% of the full data) not only reduces the amount of training data required, thereby reducing training costs, but also surpasses all previous SoTA methods. This is evidenced by the consistently higher BLEU (B-1 to B-4), ROUGE (R-1, R-2), and Distinct (Dist-1, Dist-2) scores across all evaluated models. The improvement in these metrics highlights the importance of sensibility in enhancing the empathetic capabilities of LLMs.

In summary, our data selection process, which emphasizes dialogues with high sensibility and low rationality, proves to be an effective strategy for improving empathetic response performance in LLMs. This approach not only enhances model efficiency by reducing the training data size but also leads to SoTA empathetic interactions, as reflected in the superior evaluation metrics.

Robustness of Efficient-Empathy

Discard Data:

The visualized distribution highlights the importance of both sensibility and rationality in determining the suitability of data for empathetic modeling.

This demonstrates the stability and effectiveness of our data selection method across varying thresholds, ensuring that the models are trained on the most relevant and impactful data.

Our approach aims to address these shortcomings by combining sensibility and rationality to foster a deeper connection. By immediately acknowledging that the listener has had similar experiences in the first sentence of our response, we quickly establishes empathy and demonstrates a clear understanding of the speaker’s feelings. Subsequently, the second sentence asks specific questions about the speaker’s experience, which not only shows a genuine interest in their story but also actively encourages the conversation to continue, making the interaction more personalized and dynamic.

In Table
5

You’re not usually anxious about trains
" assumes the speaker’s typical state of mind regarding trains. By making this assumption, LLaMA risks misrepresenting or misunderstanding the speaker’s feelings or experiences. If the speaker does not identify with this assumption, they may feel misunderstood or unfairly labeled. Similarly, Qwen1.5-72B-Chat’s approach fails to adequately address the speaker’s unique situation. This generality can lead to a lack of engagement and depth in the dialogue.

In analyzing different expert approaches, we distinguish between the Rationality expert and the Sensibility expert. The Rationality expert primarily focuses on identifying and understanding the causes of the horror of the train. While it may offer logical and practical solutions, it often fails to consider the speaker’s emotional state. Conversely, the Sensibility expert emphasizes understanding and empathizing with the user’s sacred feeling. However, it may not effectively guide the conversation towards practical solutions or a deeper understanding of the issue at hand.

Our proposed approach seeks to balance these two expert perspectives, aiming to both understand the user’s state and address the underlying issues without making unwarranted assumptions. By showing genuine concern for whether the user is experiencing difficulties through "
Did you have trouble
", we create a supportive environment that validates the user’s feelings and experiences. Furthermore, instead of making assumptions, we directly ask about the cause of the problem through "
Why?
". This method allows the speaker to provide their own explanation, ensuring that their perspective is accurately represented.

6.
Conclusion

Empathy is a critical component of human social interaction and communication. However, high-quality empathetic data remains scarce, necessitating efficient and effective data selection algorithms. This paper presents Efficient-Empathy, a new approach to empathetic data selection by leveraging LLM-rated sensibility and rationality scores. Remarkably, our model outperforms all previous models using only 59% of the selected sensibility data. Furthermore, by integrating the selected sensibility and rationality data with a MoE model, it achieves even higher SoTA performance without adding extra data.

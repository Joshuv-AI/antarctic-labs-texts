On generic bricks over tame algebras
R. Bautista, E. Pérez, L. Salmerón

Abstract
We prove that if Λ \Lambda is a tame finite-dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field and G G is a generic Λ \Lambda -module, then G G is a generic brick if and only if it determines a one-parameter family of bricks with the same dimension. In particular, we obtain that Λ \Lambda admits a generic brick if and only if Λ \Lambda is brick-continuous.

Introduction

Throughout this work, unless we specify otherwise,
k
denotes a fixed algebraically closed field and
\Lambda
is a tame finite-dimensional
k
-algebra. We will denote by
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
the category of left
\Lambda
-modules and by
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
its full subcategory of finite-dimensional modules.

Recall that given
G\in\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, the
endolength

\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)
of
G
is the length of
G
when considered as an
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)^{op}
-module. The module
G
is called
generic
if it is indecomposable, with infinite length as
\Lambda
-module, but
with finite endolength. Generic modules were introduced by Crawley-Boevey in
[
6
]
, where he proved that a tame algebra
\Lambda
is characterized by the fact that, for each endolength
d\in\mathbb{N}
, there are only finitely many generic
\Lambda
-modules with endolength
d
. This characterization of tame-representation type is important, because it provided a useful definition of tame algebras for more general rings, see
[
7
]
. Crawley-Boevey proved also that
whenever
G
is a generic
\Lambda
-module, we have that
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)/{\rm rad}{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)\cong k(x)
, the field of rational functions in the variable
x
, and
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)
is split over its radical.
The proofs of these facts use bocses techniques, which go back to the work of the Kiev School in representation theory, see also
[
18
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
5
]
. Moreover, Crawley-Boevey
proved in
[
6
]
that, in the tame case, any generic
\Lambda
-module
G
gives rise to infinite parameter families of non-isomorphic indecomposable
\Lambda
-modules as follows.

Recall that a rational algebra
\Gamma
is a
finitely generated localization of
k[x]
, so
\Gamma=k[x]_{h}
, for some polynomial
h\in k[x]
. If
G
is a generic
\Lambda
-module and
\Gamma
is a rational algebra, then a
realization of
G
over
\Gamma
is a finitely generated
\Lambda\hbox{-}\Gamma
-bimodule
M
such that if
K
is the quotient field of
\Gamma
, then
M\otimes_{\Gamma}K\cong G
and
\dim_{K}(M\otimes_{\Gamma}K)=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)
. This notion was proposed by
Crawley-Boevey in
[
6
]
, where he proved the following.

Theorem 1.1

(Crawley-Boevey)
.

If
\Lambda
is a tame
finite-dimensional algebra, then for each generic
\Lambda
-module
G
there is a realization
M
over some rational algebra
\Gamma
with the following properties:

1.

As a right
\Gamma
-module,
M
is
free of rank equal to the endolength of
G
.

2.

The functor
M\otimes_{\Gamma}-:\Gamma\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}

preserves isomorphism classes, indecomposability, and Auslander-Reiten sequences.

In this work, given a realization
M
of
G
over some rational algebra
\Gamma=k[x]_{h}
, we are mainly interested in the one-parameter family of indecomposable
\Lambda
-modules
M(\lambda):=M\otimes_{\Gamma}\Gamma/(x-\lambda)
, for
\lambda\in D(h):=\{\lambda\in k\mid h(\lambda)\not=0\}
.

We recall also that a
\Lambda
-module
N
is called a
brick
if
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(N)
is a division algebra. So, a finite-dimensional
\Lambda
-module
N
is a
brick
if
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(N)\cong k
. For instance, if
\Lambda
is tame hereditary, a finite-dimensional brick corresponds to a preprojective, or a preinjective module, or to a module lying in the mouth of a regular component in the Auslander-Reiten quiver of
\Lambda
, see
[
2
]
and
[
17
]
.

Following Mousavand and Paquette, we say that the algebra
\Lambda
is
brick-infinite
if it admits an infinite family of non-isomorphic finite-dimensional bricks, see
[
14
]
,
[
9
]
, and
[
19
]
. It is called
brick-continuous
if it admits an infinite family of non-isomorphic bricks with the same dimension. A generic
\Lambda
-module
G
is called a
generic brick
if
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)\cong k(x)
. The study of bricks in representation theory of finite-dimensional algebras has a long history, see
[
16
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
14
]
, and
[
15
]
. Recently, it has received a lot of attention because it plays some relevant role in
\tau
-tilting theory of algebras and brick analogues to the Brauer-Thrall conjectures have been formulated, see
[
13
]
and
[
15
]
.

Biserial algebras are an important class of tame algebras, see
[
8
]
.
In
[
14
]
, K. Mousavand and C. Paquette proved that a biserial algebra
\Lambda
is brick-infinite if and only if it admits a generic brick.
They conjectured, see
[
14
, (4.1)]
, for a general tame finite-dimensional algebra
\Lambda
,
that
\Lambda
is brick-continuous if and only if
\Lambda
admits a generic brick.

The aim of this work is to prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a tame finite-dimensional algebra,
assume that
G
be a generic
\Lambda
-module, and let
M
be a realization of
G
over a rational algebra
\Gamma
, then the following are equivalent:

1.

G
is a generic brick;

2.

For infinitely many
\lambda\in k
, we have
that
M(\lambda)
is a brick.

As a consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a tame finite-dimensional algebra. Then,
\Lambda
is brick-continuous if and only if
\Lambda
admits a generic brick.

While we are on the subject, recall that Crawley-Boevey has proved, in
[
7
, (9.5)]
, that, for any finite-dimensional algebra
A
: for some
d\in\mathbb{N}
there are infinitely many non-isomorphic finite-dimensional indecomposable
A
-modules with endolength
d
if and only if
A
has a generic module. Then, the following natural question arises for a finite-dimensional algebra
A
. Is it true that, there is some
d\in\mathbb{N}
and infinitely many non-isomorphic finite-dimensional
A
-bricks with endolength
d
if and only if
A
has a generic brick?

Any two realizations
M
and
N
of the same generic
\Lambda
-module
G
, determine one-parameter families
\{M(\lambda)\}_{\lambda}
and
\{N(\lambda)\}_{\lambda}
, respectively, such that
M(\lambda)\cong N(\lambda)
, for almost all
\lambda\in k
, see
[
6
, (5.2)]
. Thus, in order to prove Theorem (
1.2
),
it will be enough to show one realization
M
over some rational algebra
\Gamma
that satisfies the equivalence stated in the preceding theorem.

In order to prove this,
we may assume, and we will from now on, that
\Lambda
is basic and indecomposable. This is the case because, if
\Lambda_{b}
is the basic algebra of
\Lambda
and
P\otimes_{\Lambda_{b}}-:\Lambda_{b}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
is a Morita equivalence, then, any generic
\Lambda
-module
G
admits a realization of the form
M\cong P\otimes_{\Lambda_{b}}M_{b}
, where
M_{b}
is realization of a generic
\Lambda_{b}
-module
G_{b}
such that
P\otimes_{\Lambda_{b}}G_{b}\cong G
. This follows from the argument in the proof of (
3.2
).

Furthermore, we assume
that
\Lambda=kQ/I
, where
Q
is a finite connected quiver and
I
is an admissible ideal of
kQ
, see
[
1
, II]
.

The idea of the proof is to use the fact that any generic
\Lambda
-module
G
admits a realization
M
such that
G
and
M
are obtained from a minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
through an appropriate composition of functors

{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{F}}{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\ {\rm Coker}\ }}\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod},

where
F
is full and faithful, and
{\rm Coker}
is the usual cokernel functor from the category
{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)
of morphisms between projectives with superfluous image. If
\hat{G}
and
\{X_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda}
are objects in
{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)
such that
{\rm Coker}(\hat{G})\cong G
and
{\rm Coker}(X_{\lambda})\cong M(\lambda)
, what we have to show is that every endomorphism in the radical of
{\rm End}_{{\cal P}(\Lambda)}(\hat{G})
factors through objects in
{\cal P}(\Lambda)
of the form
P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{id}}P
and
P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0
if and only if, for infinitely many
\lambda\in k
,
every endomorphism of the radical of
{\rm End}_{{\cal P}(\Lambda)}(X_{\lambda})
factors through objects in
{\cal P}(\Lambda)
of the form
P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{id}}P
and
P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}0
.

We will assume that the reader has some familiarity with bocses techniques in terms of ditalgebras (an acronym for “differential tensor algebras”), as presented in
[
3
]
. Compositions of reduction functors between module categories of ditalgebras are an important tool in our arguments. In very general terms, we work with layered ditalgebras
{\cal A}=(T,\delta)
such that
T
is a graded tensor algebra
T=T_{R}(W)
, with
R
a commutative algebra and
W=W_{0}\oplus W_{1}
an
R
-
R
-bimodule, such that, in the gradding of
T
, the elements of
R
and
W_{0}
have degree 0 and those of
W_{1}
have degree 1. The category of
{\cal A}
-modules
{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
has as objects the left
A
-modules, where
A=T_{R}(W_{0})
, and the morphisms
f:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}Y
in
{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
are pairs of morphisms
f=(f^{0},f^{1})
, with
f^{0}\in{\rm Hom}_{R}(X,Y)
and
f^{1}\in{\rm Hom}_{A\hbox{-}A}(V,{\rm Hom}_{k}(X,Y))
, where
V=AW_{1}A
is the homogeneous component of
T
of degree 1.
These pairs must satisfy the condition
f^{0}(ax)=af^{0}(x)+f^{1}(\delta(a))(x)
, for
x\in X
and
a\in A
, that is

f^{0}
is almost a morphism of
A
-modules, we have a correction which depends on
\delta(a)
and
f^{1}
. The composition is performed according to a special rule which uses again the differential
\delta
of
{\cal A}
, see
[
3
, §2]
. There is a canonical embedding functor
L_{\cal A}:A\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
which is the identity on objects and maps each morphism of
A
-modules
f:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}Y
onto the morphism
(f,0):X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}Y
of
{\cal A}
-modules.

An important example is the Drozd’s ditalgebra
{\cal D}
associated to the algebra
\Lambda
, which is very useful because there is an equivalence of categories
\Xi_{\Lambda}:{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)
. This was used by Drozd to prove his famous tame-wild dichotomy theorem, see
[
10
]
. His main idea to prove this theorem is to construct a minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
and a composition of reduction functors

H:{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal D}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
,
which, composed with
\Xi_{\Lambda}
and
{\rm Coker}
, permitted some understanding of
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
. This idea was enriched by Crawley-Boevey in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
to show the characterization of tame algebras and his theorem on realizations of generic modules mentioned before. The advantage of minimal ditalgebras
{\cal B}
is that their module category is relatively simple, for instance the radical morphisms between important
{\cal B}
-modules admit manageable descriptions which permit to make explicit computations.

Kernel-generating systems for
{\rm Coker}^{1}

In this section we introduce a family of special morphisms, which will play a crucial role in our arguments. This construction has some general features, so we start by considering an artin algebra
A
over a commutative artin ring. We will later specialize to the case of a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
\Lambda=kQ/I
over an arbitrary field
k
. We denote by
J
the radical of the artin algebra
A
.

Remark 2.1
.

Recall that the category
{\cal P}(A)
consists of the triples
X=(P_{1},P_{2},\phi)
where
P_{1},P_{2}
are projective
A
-modules and
\phi:P_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P_{2}
is a morphism in
A\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
. A morphism
u:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X^{\prime}
in
{\cal P}(\Lambda)
is a pair
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
of morphisms of
A
-modules such that the following diagram commutes

\begin{matrix}P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi}}&P_{2}\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{1}$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{2}$}}$\hss}\\
P^{\prime}_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi^{\prime}}}&P^{\prime}_{2}.\\
\end{matrix}

We denote by
{\cal P}^{1}(A)
the full subcategory of
{\cal P}(A)
whose objects are the triples
(P_{1},P_{2},\phi)
such that
\phi(P_{1})\subseteq JP_{2}
.
For each projective
P\in A\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, we consider the objects
S(P)=(P,0,0)\in{\cal P}^{1}(A)
and
U(P)=(P,P,id_{P})\in{\cal P}(A)
. The cokernel functor
{\rm Coker}:{\cal P}(A)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}A\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
is full and dense, and maps
S(P)
and
U(P)
to
0
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
u:(P_{1},P_{2},\phi)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2},\phi^{\prime})
is any morphism in
{\cal P}(A)
with
{\rm Coker}(u)=0
, then
u=v+w
, for some morphisms
v,w:(P_{1},P_{2},\phi)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}(P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2},\phi^{\prime})
, where
v
factors through
S(P_{1})
and
w
factors through
U(P_{2})
in
{\cal P}(A)
.

Proof.

Set
M:={\rm Coker}(\phi)
and
M^{\prime}:={\rm Coker}(\phi^{\prime})
. Then, we have the commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi}}&P_{2}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\eta}}&M\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{1}$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{2}$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle 0$}}$}\big\downarrow\\
P^{\prime}_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi^{\prime}}}&P^{\prime}_{2}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\eta^{\prime}}}&M^{\prime}\\
\end{matrix}

where
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are the canonical projections. Since
\eta^{\prime}u_{2}=0
, there is a morphism
s:P_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P^{\prime}_{1}
such that
\phi^{\prime}s=u_{2}
. Then, we have the commutative diagrams

\begin{matrix}P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi}}&P_{2}\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle id$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle 0$}}$\hss}\\
P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{0}}&0\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{1}-s\phi$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle 0$}}$\hss}\\
P^{\prime}_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi^{\prime}}}&P^{\prime}_{2}\\
\end{matrix}\hbox{ \ \ \ \ and \ \ \ \ }\begin{matrix}P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi}}&P_{2}\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phi$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle id$}}$\hss}\\
P_{2}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{id}}&P_{2}\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle s$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{2}$}}$\hss}\\
P^{\prime}_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi^{\prime}}}&P^{\prime}_{2}\\
\end{matrix}

Hence,
v:=(u_{1}-s\phi,0)
and
w=(s\phi,u_{2})
are the desired morphisms.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
P
is an indecomposable projective
A
-module, we have:

1.

There is a morphism of
A
-modules
\phi_{P}:L_{P}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P
with

projective, which is not a retraction (that is such that
{\rm Im}\,\phi_{P}\subseteq JP
), such that for any projective

, any morphism
h:P^{\prime}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P

\phi_{P}
.
Moreover, whenever

, we get that

2.

There is a morphism of
A

\psi_{P}:P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}R_{P}
with
R_{P}
projective, which is not a section (that is such that
{\rm Im}\,\psi_{P}\subseteq JR_{P}
), such that, for any projective

, any morphism
g:P\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P^{\prime}

. Moreover, whenever

(1): Consider a projective cover

and the inclusion

. Then, it is easy to show that the composition

(2): Consider the duality

Remark 2.4
.

Given any indecomposable projective
A
-module

, the morphisms
\phi_{P}
and

are uniquely determined up to isomorphism, respectively, by the properties listed in items (1) and (2) of the last lemma.

Lemma 2.5
.

Given
X\in{\cal P}^{1}(A)
and an indecomposable projective
A
-module
P
, we have:

1.

Any morphism
u:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}U(P)
in
{\cal P}(A)
factors through
\alpha_{P}
.

2.

Any morphism
v:U(P)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X
in
{\cal P}(A)
factors through
\beta_{P}
.

Proof.

Assume
X=(P_{1},P_{2},\phi)
,
u=(u_{1},u_{2})
, and

.

In order to prove (1), notice that, since
u:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}U(P)
is a morphism and

, we have

and
\phi(P_{1})\subseteq JP_{2}
. Thus,
u_{1}(P_{1})\subseteq JP
and

is not a retraction. So, there is a morphism
h:P_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}L_{P}
such that

, and we obtain the commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}P_{1}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\phi}}&P_{2}\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle h$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle u_{2}$}}$\hss}\\

\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\phi_{P}$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle id$}}$\hss}\\

\end{matrix}

Then, we get

, with
\zeta:=(h,u_{2})
.

In order to prove (2), notice that, since

is a morphism and

, we get
v_{2}=\phi v_{1}
and

. Thus,

is not a section. So, there is a morphism
g:R_{P}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}P_{2}
such that

and we obtain the commutative diagram

\begin{matrix}P&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{id}}&P\\
\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle id$}}$}\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow\hbox to0.0pt{$\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\psi_{P}$}}$\hss}\\

Proposition 2.6
.

Let

be a morphism in
{\cal P}^{1}(A)
, where
X
and
X^{\prime}
have underlying finite-length modules. Then,
{\rm Coker}(u)=0
if and only if

is a finite sum of morphisms which factor either by some object
S(P)
or by some morphism
\gamma_{P}:L(P)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}R(P)
, with

indecomposable.

Proof.

Suppose that
u=\sum_{i=1}^{r}g_{i}\gamma_{P_{i}}h_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{s}g^{\prime}_{j}h^{\prime}_{j}
, for some morphisms
h_{i}:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}L(P_{i})
,
g_{i}:R(P_{i})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X^{\prime}
,
h^{\prime}_{j}:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}S(P^{\prime}_{j})
, and
g^{\prime}_{j}:S(P^{\prime}_{j})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X^{\prime}
, with
P_{i}
and
P^{\prime}_{j}
indecomposable projectives. Then, applying the Coker functor to this sum, we get

because each

U(P_{i})
. Conversely, assume that

. From (

),
we know that

, where

factors through some

and

, where

and
P^{\prime}
are finite-length projectives. Then,

and

, where

and

, for some morphisms

and
g:\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}U(P_{i})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X^{\prime},

and
w=g^{\prime}h^{\prime}
, for some morphisms

h^{\prime}:X\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}S(P^{\prime}_{j})
and
g^{\prime}:\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}S(P^{\prime}_{j})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}X^{\prime}
.

-injection of

-projection of

on

Lemma 2.7

Remark 3.1

Recall that a minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
is a layered ditalgebra with triangular seminested layer

with

, that is a finite product of indecomposable algebras

The factors

are of two different types as follows:
we have
\mathfrak{P}=\mathfrak{M}\uplus\mathfrak{Z}
with

, for

z\in\mathfrak{M}
, and
R_{z}\cong k
, for
z\in\mathfrak{Z}
.
Moreover, if

is the corresponding decomposition of the unit in
R
as a sum of primitive orthogonal idempotents, we consider the factors

as subspaces of
R
through the isomorphisms
R_{z}\cong k[x]_{h_{z}}\mathfrak{f}_{z}

, and
R_{z}\cong k\mathfrak{f}_{z}
, for

The elements of
\mathfrak{P}
are called
the points of

{\cal B}
and the elements of
\mathfrak{M}
are called the
marked points of

{\cal B}
.

Given

, the indecomposable finite-dimensional

-modules are, up to isomorphism, of the form

and

acts invertibly on the

-module

-module, an
R_{z}
-module, and an

-module. We have an isomorphism of

-modules

So, the indecomposable finite-dimensional

-modules are, up to isomorphism, of the form

h_{z}(\lambda)\not=0

, and

Lemma 4.2
.

Consider the left dual

\,{}^{*}\hat{W}_{1}:={\rm Hom}_{S}(_{S}\hat{W}_{1},S)
and, for
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}
, the usual dual spaces
D(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f})={\rm Hom}_{k}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f},k)
. Then,
for any
\mathfrak{f},\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{F}
, we have the linear isomorphism

D(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f})={\rm Hom}_{k}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f},k)\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\overline{\,\cdot\,}}}\mathfrak{f}\,{\rm Hom}_{S}(\hat{W}_{1},S)\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\mathfrak{f}(\,^{*}\hat{W}_{1})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime},

which maps each linear map
\xi\in D(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f})
onto
\overline{\xi}\in\,^{*}\hat{W}_{1}
defined by
\overline{\xi}(w)=\xi(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}w\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
, for
w\in\hat{W}_{1}
.

Proof.

Given
\xi\in D(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f})
,
w\in\hat{W}_{1}
, and
s\in S
, we have

\overline{\xi}(sw)=\xi(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}sw\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\xi(s\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}w\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=s\xi(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}w\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=s\overline{\xi}(w),

where the third equality holds because
s\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\mu\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
, for a scalar
\mu\in k
. So, indeed we have
\overline{\xi}\in\,^{*}\hat{W}_{1}
. Since
(\mathfrak{f}\overline{\xi}\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})(w)=\overline{\xi}(w\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\xi(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}w\mathfrak{f})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}=\overline{\xi}(w)
, for all
w
, we see that, indeed,
\overline{\xi}\in\mathfrak{f}(\,^{*}\hat{W}_{1})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}
.

The linear map
\overline{\,\cdot\,}
is bijective with inverse given by the composition of
the following chain of isomorphisms

\begin{matrix}\mathfrak{f}(\,^{*}\hat{W}_{1})\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}&=&\mathfrak{f}{\rm Hom}_{S}(\hat{W}_{1},S)\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\cong{\rm Hom}_{S}(\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f},S\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})\hfill\\
&\cong&{\rm Hom}_{S}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f},S\mathfrak{f}^{\prime})\cong{\rm Hom}_{k}(\mathfrak{f}^{\prime}\hat{W}_{1}\mathfrak{f},k).\\
\end{matrix}

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

For any simple modules

and

Proof.

Consider a pair

and take any

. Then we have that
id_{N^{\prime}}-fg=id_{N^{\prime}}-(0,h^{1})
, for some
(0,h^{1})\in{\rm End}_{\cal B}(N^{\prime})
. From
[

is nilpotent in the algebra

. So,

is invertible, and

.

Reciprocally, assume that

with
f^{0}\not=0
. Since

are
one-dimensional,
f^{0}:N\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}N^{\prime}
is an

-linear isomorphism. Let

∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Assume

Proof.

This is similar to the proof of

Lemma 4.9

We have the following vector space decomposition of the radical of the endomorphism algebra

{\rm rad}{\rm End}_{\cal B}(k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0})=\bigoplus_{v\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{f}_{0}}}{\cal H}_{v}

Proof.

Define

(0,\hat{\psi})

For each linear transformation

Lemma 7.1

General Case:

By assumption,

c^{v_{i}}_{w_{2},w_{1}}=c^{v_{i}}_{\tau_{1}}=c_{i,1}=0
. Hence,
\psi^{x}_{w_{2},w_{1},p}=\psi^{x}_{v_{1},q}
and, therefore,
f^{x}_{w_{2},p}f^{x}_{w_{1}}=f^{x}_{v_{1},q}
. That is
f^{x}_{v_{1},q}
factors through
S^{x}_{\mathfrak{f}_{z_{t}}}
.

The other possibility for the form of
\tau_{1}
is
\tau_{1}=w_{2}\otimes u_{t}\otimes w_{1}
, with
(w_{2},t,w_{1})\in\mathbb{I}_{3}
. As before, there is
p\in{\rm End}_{k}(k(x))
such that
q=p_{c^{v_{1}}_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1}}}
. Using (
7.5
)(2), we obtain

f^{x}_{w_{2},p}f^{x}_{u_{t}}f^{x}_{w_{1}}=f^{x}_{v_{1},q}.

Thus, the morphism
f^{x}_{v_{1},q}
factors through
f^{x}_{u_{t}}
.

Now, take
i\in[2,c_{0}]
and assume that
f^{x}_{v_{j},q_{j}}
has the wanted form, for every
j\in[1,i-1]
and
q_{j}\in{\rm End}_{k}(k(x))
. Take
q\in{\rm End}_{k}(k(x))
and let us show that
f^{x}_{v_{i},q}
is a
k
-linear combination of morphisms which factor as wanted.

Again, since
C(x,y)
is normal, we have
c^{v_{i}}_{\tau_{i}}(x,y)=c_{i,i}(x,y)\not=0
and there is some
p\in{\rm End}_{k}(k(x))
with
q=p_{c_{\tau_{i}}^{v_{i}}}
. Now, assume that
\tau_{i}
has the form
\tau_{i}=w_{2}\otimes u_{t}\otimes w_{1}
, for some
(w_{2},t,w_{1})\in\mathbb{I}_{3}
. Thus,
q=p_{c_{w_{2},t,w_{1}}^{v_{i}}}
and, from (
7.5
)(2), we have the composition

f^{x}_{w_{2},p}f^{x}_{u_{t}}f^{x}_{w_{1}}=(0,\psi^{x}_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1},p}).

Here, we have
\psi^{x}_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1},p}(v_{i})=\hat{p}_{c^{v_{i}}_{w_{2},t,w_{1}}}=\hat{q}
.
For
s>i
, we have
\psi^{x}_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1},p}(v_{s})=0
, because, by the normality of
C(x,y)
, we have
c_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1}}^{v_{s}}=c^{v_{s}}_{\tau_{i}}=c_{s,i}=0
. Therefore, we have

\psi^{x}_{w_{2},u_{t},w_{1},p}=\psi^{x}_{v_{i},q}+\psi^{x},

where
\psi^{x}\in{\rm Hom}_{S\hbox{-}S}(\hat{W}_{1},{\rm Hom}_{k}(k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0},k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0}))
is such that
\psi^{x}(v_{s})=0
, for all
s\geq i
. This implies that
\psi^{x}=\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}\psi^{x}_{v_{j},q_{j}}
, where
q_{j}\in{\rm End}_{k}(k(x))
is such that
\psi^{x}(v_{j})=\hat{q}_{j}
. Therefore, we have

f^{x}_{w_{2},p}f^{x}_{u_{t}}f^{x}_{w_{1}}=f^{x}_{v_{i},q}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}f^{x}_{v_{j},q_{j}}.

Then, we can apply the induction hypothesis to each
f^{x}_{v_{j},q_{j}}
, to obtain that
f^{x}_{v_{i},q}
is a
k
-linear combination of morphisms as wanted.

In the remaining case, where
\tau_{i}=w_{2}\otimes w_{1}
, we proceed similarly.
∎

In the following, we will show that the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
fixed at the begining of §4 and satisfying (
3.7
), can be replaced by a minimal ditalgebra of the same type but which is, in addition, admissible.

Remark 8.3

(
Reduction by localization).

Given a seminested ditalgebra
{\cal A}
, we have the seminested ditalgebra
{\cal A}^{l}
obtained from
{\cal A}
by localization. It has an associated full and faithful reduction functor
F^{l}:{\cal A}^{l}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal A}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}
, see
[
3
, (17.7) and (26.4)]
. Here, we concentrate on the particular case of minimal ditalgebras with only one marked point, which is the context of interest in the following.

We adopt the notation of the beginning of §4 for the constituents of our minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
and consider a non-zero polynomial
g\in k[x]
. Then, the localization of
{\cal B}
with respect to the element
g(x)
is the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}^{l}
, with layer
(R^{l},W_{1}^{l})
such that
R^{l}=k[x]_{g(x)h(x)}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\times(\times_{z\in\mathfrak{Z}}k\mathfrak{f}_{z})
and
W_{1}^{l}=R^{l}\otimes_{R}W_{1}\otimes_{R}R^{l}
. The canonical epimorphism of algebras
R\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}R^{l}
and the morphism of
R
-
R
-bimodules
\phi:W_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}W^{l}_{1}
, which maps each
w
onto
1\otimes w\otimes 1
, induce a morphism of ditalgebras
\hat{\phi}:{\cal B}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal B}^{l}
, that is a morphism of graded algebras

\hat{\phi}:T_{R}(W_{1})\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}T_{R^{l}}(W_{1}^{l})
such that
\delta^{l}\hat{\phi}=\hat{\phi}\delta
, where
\delta
and
\delta^{l}
denote the differentials of
{\cal B}
and
{\cal B}^{l}
respectively.

We want to compare the morphisms
\hat{\delta}
and
\hat{\delta}^{l}
, see (
8.1
). Notice that, if we denote with the same symbols the differentials
\delta
and
\delta^{l}
, and their restrictions to
W_{1}
and
W_{1}^{l}
, respectively, we have:

\delta^{l}\phi=(\phi\otimes\phi)\delta

(1)

and

\begin{matrix}(1\otimes\delta^{l})\delta^{l}\phi&=&(1\otimes\delta^{l})(\phi\otimes\phi)\delta=(\phi\otimes\delta^{l}\phi)\delta\hfill\\
&=&(\phi\otimes(\phi\otimes\phi)\delta)\delta=(\phi\otimes\phi\otimes\phi)(1\otimes\delta)\delta.\end{matrix}

(2)

For the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}^{l}
, we have the
R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\hbox{-}R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}
-bimodule

U^{l}=\left[\bigoplus_{t=1}^{n}\mathfrak{f}_{0}W^{l}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{z_{t}}\otimes_{R^{l}}\mathfrak{f}_{z_{t}}W^{l}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\right]\oplus\left[\bigoplus_{t=1}^{n}\mathfrak{f}_{0}W^{l}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{r_{t}}\otimes_{R^{l}}k(1\otimes u_{t}\otimes 1)\otimes_{R^{l}}\mathfrak{f}_{l_{t}}W^{l}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\right]

and the corresponding morphism
\hat{\delta}^{l}:\mathfrak{f}_{0}W^{l}_{1}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}U^{l}
. From (1) and (2), we derive the equality

\hat{\phi}\hat{\delta}=\hat{\delta}^{l}\phi.

For
v\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{f}_{0}}
, we have
\hat{\delta}(v)=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}c_{\tau}^{v}\tau
. Therefore,

\hat{\delta}^{l}(\phi(v))=\sum_{\tau\in\mathbb{T}}c^{v}_{\tau}\hat{\phi}(\tau)
. There is a natural choice of directed basis
\mathbb{B}^{l}:=\phi(\mathbb{B})
for the
R^{l}\hbox{-}R^{l}
-bimodule
W_{1}^{l}
, with this choice, we see that
\mathbb{T}^{l}=\hat{\phi}(\mathbb{T})
is the chosen directed basis for
U^{l}
. Thus, the coefficients defining
\hat{\delta}^{l}
are the same that the coefficients defining
\hat{\delta}
.

Any numberings
v_{1},\ldots,v_{c_{0}}
of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{f}_{0}}
and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{c_{1}}
of
\mathbb{T}
, determines the numberings
\phi(v_{1}),\ldots,\phi(v_{c_{0}})
of
\mathbb{B}^{l}_{\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{f}_{0}}
and
\hat{\phi}(\tau_{1}),\ldots,\hat{\phi}(\tau_{c_{1}})
of
\mathbb{T}^{l}
. With these numberings, we have the equality of coefficient matrices
C(x,y)=C^{l}(x,y)
.

Lemma 8.4
.

If the coefficient matrix
C(x,y)
of our minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}
has rank
c_{0}
, then there is a polynomial
g(x)\in k[x]
such that the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}^{l}
obtained by localization of
{\cal B}
with respect to this polynomial
g(x)
is admissible.

Proof.

We have our fixed directed basis
\mathbb{B}
for
W_{1}
and consider any numberings
v_{1},\ldots,v_{c_{0}}
of
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{f}_{0},\mathfrak{f}_{0}}
and
\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{c_{1}}
of
\mathbb{T}
. So, we have
\delta(v_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{c_{1}}c_{i,j}(x,y)\tau_{j}
and the coefficient matrix
C(x,y)=(c_{i,j}(x,y))\in M_{c_{0}\times c_{1}}(k[x,y])
has rank
c_{0}
by assumption.

We can reorder the elements of
\mathbb{T}
in such a way that
C(x,y)=(C_{0},C_{1})
, with
C_{0}\in M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(k[x,y])
and
C_{1}\in M_{c_{0}\times(c_{1}-c_{0})}(k[x,y])
, where
C_{0}
has non-zero determinant. The matrix
C_{0}
has entries in the polynomial algebra
k(y)[x]
. Using elementary operations on the rows of
C_{0}
, we can transform it into an upper triangular matrix. So, there is a matrix
A\in M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(k(y)[x])
, which is invertible and such that
AC_{0}
is an upper triangular matrix. Consider
g(y)\in k[y]
such that
A\in M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(k[y]_{g(y)}[x])
and the minimal ditalgebra
{\cal B}^{l}
obtained from
{\cal B}
by localization with respect to
g(x)
.

Since the matrix
A=(a_{i,j}(x,y))
is invertible in

M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(k[y]_{g(y)}[x])\subseteq M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(k[x]_{h(x)g(x)}\otimes_{k}k[x]_{h(x)g(x)})=M_{c_{0}\times c_{0}}(R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\otimes_{k}R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}),

the elements
v^{l}_{1},\ldots,v^{l}_{c_{0}}
defined by

v^{l}_{i}:=\sum_{j=1}^{c_{0}}{a_{i,j}}\phi(v_{j})

form a basis for the

R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}\hbox{-}R^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}
-bimodule

\mathfrak{f}_{0}W_{1}^{l}\mathfrak{f}_{0}
, see

[
3
, (26.1)]
. From (
8.1
) and (
8.3
), we know that

\hat{\delta}^{l}(\phi(v_{i}))=\sum_{t=1}^{c_{1}}c_{i,t}\hat{\phi}(\tau_{t}).

Then, we have

\begin{matrix}\hat{\delta}^{l}(v^{l}_{i})&=&\sum_{j=1}^{c_{0}}{a_{i,j}}\hat{\delta}^{l}\phi(v_{j})=\sum_{j,t}a_{i,j}c_{j,t}\hat{\phi}(\tau_{t})\hfill\\
&=&\sum_{t=1}^{c_{0}}c^{l}_{i,t}\hat{\phi}(\tau_{t})+\sum_{t>c_{0}}c^{l}_{i,t}\hat{\phi}(\tau_{t}),\hfill\\
\end{matrix}

where
c^{l}_{i,t}:=\sum_{j=1}^{c_{0}}a_{i,j}c_{j,t}
, for all
i\in[1,c_{0}]
and
t\in[1,c_{1}]
. For
t\in[1,c_{0}]
, the coefficient
c^{l}_{i,t}
is the
(i,t)
-entry of the upper triangular matrix
AC_{0}
, so
c^{l}_{i,t}=0
, for
i>t
. Since
AC_{0}
has rank
c_{0}
, we have
c^{l}_{i,i}\not=0
, for each
i\in[1,c_{0}]
. Finally, as we did before in (
5.2
), we can replace each one of the new basis elements

v^{l}_{i}
by the product
h(x)^{m}v_{i}^{l}g(x)^{m}
for a suitable
m\in\mathbb{N}
to obtain that the corresponding coefficient matrix is normal (with entries in
k[x,y]
).
∎

.

We already have a minimal ditalgebra

Proposition 8.6
.

If
{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(M(\lambda))=k
, for infinitely many
\lambda\in D(h)
, then

{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)\cong k(x).

Proof.

Assume that

{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(M(\lambda))=k
, for infinitely many
\lambda\in D(h)
. From (
5.8
), we know that the matrix
C(x)=(c^{v_{i}}_{\tau_{j}}(x,x))
has rank
c_{0}
. This implies that the coefficient matrix
C(x,y)=(c^{v_{i}}_{\tau_{j}}(x,y))
has rank
c_{0}
. Then, as observed in the previous remark, we may assume that
{\cal B}
is admissible and we can apply (
8.2
) to
{\cal B}
, so every morphism

is a
k
-linear combination of morphisms which factor through some object
S^{x}_{\mathfrak{f}_{z_{t}}}
or through some morphism
f^{x}_{u_{t}}
, for some
t\in[1,n]
.

We may assume that
\hat{G}=F(k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0})
. Given any morphism
g\in{\rm rad}{\rm End}_{\Lambda}(G)
, there is some
\hat{g}\in{\rm rad}{\rm End}_{{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)}(\hat{G})
, with
{\rm Coker}(\hat{g})=g
. Since
F:{\cal B}\hbox{-}{\rm Mod}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits}{\cal P}^{1}(\Lambda)
is full and faithful, we have
F(f)=\hat{g}
, for some

. So, there are finitely many commutative diagrams in

\begin{matrix}k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{f_{i}}}&k(x)\mathfrak{f}_{0}\\

&\hskip-5.0pt\mathop{\searrow\hskip-6.0pt\raise 5.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle h^{\prime}_{j}$}}\hbox{\hskip 11.38092pt}&\hskip-8.0pt\mathop{\hskip-5.0pt\raise 6.0pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle g^{\prime}_{j}$}\hskip-5.0pt\nearrow}\hbox{\hskip 22.76228pt}\\
&\hbox{\hskip 22.76228pt}S^{x}_{\mathfrak{f}_{z_{t_{j}}}}&\\
\end{matrix}

such that

.

and using the diagrams of (

), we obtain finitely many commutative diagrams in

\begin{matrix}\hat{G}&\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\hat{f}_{i}}}&\hat{G}\\

Proof of the Corollary
(
1.3
):
If
G
is a generic brick, then a realization
M
of
G
as in Theorem (
1.1
) is free by the right, with rank equal to the endolength of
G
. Then, by (
1.2
),
M
determines an infinite family
\{M(\lambda)\}_{\lambda}
of finite-dimensional bricks
M(\lambda)=M\otimes_{\Gamma}\Gamma/(x-\lambda)
with
k
-dimension
d=\hbox{\rm endol}\,(G)
.

Conversely, assume that
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is an infinite family of non-isomorphic bricks with dimension
d
. Applying
[
6
, (5.5)]
to
d
, we obtain a generic
\Lambda
-module
G
and a realization
M
of
G
over some rational algebra
\Gamma
, such that for almost every
i\in I
,
M_{i}\cong M\otimes_{\Gamma}\Gamma/(x-\lambda_{i})^{m_{i}}
, for some
\lambda_{i}\in k
and
m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
. We may assume that this realization is as in (
1.1
), so
M\otimes_{\Gamma}-
preserves almost split sequences. This implies that for each
\lambda
, we have
almost split sequences in
\Lambda\hbox{-}{\rm mod}
of the form:

\begin{matrix}\ E_{1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\alpha_{1}}}E_{2}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{\beta_{1}}}E_{1},\\
\,\\
\ E_{m}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{(\alpha_{m},\beta_{m-1})}}E_{m+1}\oplus E_{m-1}\smash{\mathop{\hbox to20.0pt{\rightarrowfill}}\limits^{(\beta_{m},\alpha_{m-1})}}E_{m},\hbox{ for }m\geq 2,\\
\end{matrix}

with

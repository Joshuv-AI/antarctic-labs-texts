Numerical simulations for the Ising model on three dimensional lattices with coordination number equal 5: static and dynamic critical phenomena.
Lourdes Bibiana Merino-Solís, Francisco Sastre

Abstract
In this work we performed numerical simulations for the Ising model on three dimensional lattices with coordination number equal 5. With Monte Carlo simulations in the static case we evaluated the critical temperature and the static critical exponents ν \nu , γ \gamma and β \beta . Once that we have the critical temperature value we investigated the dynamical critical behavior with Glauber dynamics, starting from disordered states. From our simulations we obtained the values for the dynamic exponent z = 2.037 ​ ( 8 ) z=2.037(8) , the exponent for the autocorrelation λ / z = 1.364 ​ ( 5 ) \lambda/z=1.364(5) , the exponent of the critical initial increase θ ′ = 0.109 ​ ( 8 ) \theta^{\prime}=0.109(8) and the asymptotic value of the fluctuation-dissipation ratio X ∞ = 0.433 ​ ( 1 ) X^{\infty}=0.433(1) . All of these results are in good agreement to the previous values reported for the 3D Ising model universality class.

There is not analytic solution in the three dimensional case, even though it has been solved in the one and two dimensional cases.

In this work we analyzed first the static critical phenomena with standard Finite Size Scaling (FSS) theory. The free energy of magnetic systems is described by the scaling ansatz

F(L,T)=L^{(2-\alpha)/\nu}{\cal F}(\varepsilon L^{1/\nu},hL^{(\gamma+\beta)/\nu}),

(1)

here
L
is the linear dimension (
N=L^{d}
),
\varepsilon=(T-T_{c})/T_{c}
,
h
is the external magnetic field and
\alpha,~\beta,~\gamma,~\nu
are the static critical exponents. Our simulations were carried out without external field, then we are setting
h=0
.
From Eq. (
1
) we can derive the scaling forms for the magnetisation, the susceptibility and the Binder cumulant

M=L^{-\beta/\nu}{\cal M}(\varepsilon L^{1/\nu}),

(2a)

\chi=L^{\gamma/\nu}{\cal X}(\varepsilon L^{1/\nu}),

(2b)

U={\cal U}(\varepsilon L^{1/\nu}).

(2c)

Once that we have the critical temperature we analyzed the relaxation process when our system, initially in a high temperature state, is
suddenly quenched at
t=0
to the critical temperature
T_{c}
. For an initial magnetisation
M_{0}
, the dynamic scaling form for the magnetisation is given by
[
22
]

M(t,M_{0})=t^{-\beta/(\nu z)}{\cal F}_{M}(tM_{0}^{1/\kappa}),

(3)

where
z
is the dynamic critical exponent and the exponent
\kappa
is related to
z
with

\kappa=\theta^{\prime}+\beta/(\nu z),

(4)

here
\theta^{\prime}
is the short-time exponent that describes the critical initial increase of the magnetisation, for small values of
M_{0}
the expected behavior is
[
23
]

M(t)\propto t^{\theta^{\prime}}.

(5)

Starting with
M_{0}=0
the autocorrelation function decays with the power law

A(t)=\langle\phi(0,\bm{r})\phi(t,\bm{r})\rangle\propto t^{-\lambda/z},

(6)

where the autocorrelation exponent
\lambda
can be expressed in function of
\theta^{\prime}
with
[
24
]

\lambda=d-z\theta^{\prime}.

(7)

Other important dynamic quantities analized are the two-time autocorrelation
C(t,s)
and the autoresponse functions
R(t,s)
that
satisfy (see
[
26
]
and references therein)

\displaystyle C(t,s){}

\displaystyle=\langle\phi(s,\bm{r})\phi(t,\bm{r})\rangle-\langle\phi(s,\bm{r})\rangle\langle\phi(t,\bm{r})\rangle

(8)

\displaystyle=s^{-b}f_{\text{\tiny$C$}}(t/s),

R(t,s)=\left.\frac{\delta\langle\phi(t,\bm{r})\rangle}{\delta h(s,\bm{r})}\right|_{h=0}=s^{-1-a}f_{\text{\tiny$R$}}(t/s),

(9)

where
a
and
b
are ageing exponents, and
f_{\text{\tiny$C,R$}}(x)
are scaling functions.
In simple magnetic systems with non-conserved dynamics quenched to
T=T_{c}
(our case) we have
[
25
]

a=b=2\frac{\beta}{\nu z}.

(10)

When
x=t/s\gg 1
the scaling functions obey the power law
[
23
]

f_{\text{\tiny$C,R$}}(x)\approx A_{\text{\tiny$C,R$}}x^{-\lambda/z}.

(11)

Since the sudden quench to the critical point moves the system away from its equilibrium state,
the correlation and the response no longer satisfy the equilibrium fluctuation-dissipation theorem (FDT):
TR(t,s)=\partial_{s}C(t,s)
, where
we are setting
k_{B}=1
.
Cugliandolo and Kurchan
[
27
]
proposed that, with the introduction of the fluctuation-dissipation ratio (FDR)

X(t,s):=T\frac{R(t,s)}{\partial_{s}C(t,s)},

(12)

it is possible to characterise when a system is far away from equilibrium.
Godrèche and Luck
[
28
,
29
]
proposed that the asymptotic value of the FDR

X^{\infty}=\lim_{s\to\infty}\left(\lim_{t\to\infty}X(t,s)\right),

(13)

should be a universal quantity in the same way that
the non-equilibrium critical exponents. Combining (
8
)-(
13
) we have

X^{\infty}=\frac{\nu zTA_{\text{\tiny$R$}}}{(\nu\lambda-2\beta)A_{\text{\tiny$C$}}},

(14)

since the partial derivative of the correlation must scale as

\partial_{s}C(t,s)\approx s^{-1-a}\frac{(\nu\lambda-2\beta)A_{\text{\tiny$C$}}}{\nu z}x^{-\lambda/z},~\mbox{if}~x\gg 1.

(15)

II
Model and simulation details

The Hamiltonian for the ferromagnetic Ising model in absence of external magnetic field is given by

{\cal H}=-\sum_{\langle l,m\rangle}\sigma_{l}\sigma_{m},

(16)

where
\sigma_{l}=\pm 1
,
J>0
is the coupling constant and the sum runs over all nearest-neighbor pairs. As mentioned in the last section
we will work in a lattices with
N=L^{3}
sites. Each spin

\sigma_{i,j,k}
interacts with the spins:
\sigma_{i+1,j,k}
,
\sigma_{i-1,j,k}
,
\sigma_{i,j,k+1}
,
\sigma_{i,j,k-1}
and

\sigma_{i+1,j+1,k}
, if
i
is even, or
\sigma_{i-1,j-1,k}
if
i
is odd.
In the static part we will use the dimensionless coupling
K=1/T
, the scaling forms (1) and (2) are still valid with

\varepsilon=(K-K_{c})/K_{c}
.
In our simulations each spin will change its sign with probability

p(\sigma_{l}\to-\sigma_{l})=\frac{1}{2}[1-\sigma_{l}\tanh(KH_{l})],

(17)

here
H_{j}
is the sum of the 5 spins that interact with the spin
\sigma_{l}
.
The instantaneous magnetisation per site is defined as

\mu(t)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k}\sigma_{k}.

(18)

II.1
Static simulations

The thermal averages of the magnetisation moments can be evaluate as time averages with

\langle M^{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\tau-t_{o}}\sum_{t=t_{o}}^{\tau}|\mu(t)|^{j},

(19)

where
t_{o}
is the transient time and
\tau-t_{o}
is the running time, times are measured in Monte Carlo time steps (MCTS).
Then we can obtain from the simulations the susceptibility

\chi=NK\left(\langle M^{2}\rangle-\langle M\rangle^{2}\right),

(20)

and the Binder cumulant

U=1-\frac{\langle M^{4}\rangle}{\langle M^{2}\rangle^{2}}.

(21)

The critical coupling
K_{c}
and the critical Binder cumulant
U^{*}
can be evaluated using the curves
U
vs.
K
for different system sizes, since the scaling form (
2c
)
predicts that, for sufficiently large enough sizes, the curves intersect at the critical point
(K_{c},U^{*})
.
The value of the critical Binder cumulant is an universal quantity that depends on cluster shape and boundary
conditions
[
30
,
31
]
. Once that we have the critical point we can evaluate the exponents

1/\nu
with the scaling form (
2c
). In the same way we can obtain the critical exponents
\beta
and
\gamma
with the scaling
forms (
2a
) and (
2b
) respectively.

II.2
Dynamic simulations

In the critical dynamic we prepared the system flipping spins at random until we reach the desired initial value
M_{0}

and then we let evolve the system
following the rule given by (
17
).
The time dependent magnetisation after the quench to the critical temperature is evaluated using

M(t)=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{l}\sigma_{l}(t)\right\rangle,

(22)

here
\langle\cdot\rangle
denotes sampling average.The autocorrelation can be computed, starting with
M_{0}=0
, using

A(t)=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{l}\sigma_{l}(t)\sigma(0)_{l}\right\rangle.

(23)

The two time correlation function is evaluated using

C(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{l}\sigma_{l}(t)\sigma(s)_{l}\right\rangle-M(s)M(t).

(24)

The time derivative
\partial_{s}C(t,s)
can be approximate with

\displaystyle\partial_{s}C(t,s){}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{l}\sigma_{l}(t)[\sigma_{l}(s+1)-\sigma(s)_{l}]\right\rangle

(25)

\displaystyle-M(t)\Bigl(M(s+1)-M(s)\Bigr).

The response can be evaluated without introducing any magnetic field in the simulation following the approach
proposed by Chatelain
[
32
,
33
]
.
First we let the system evolve during a time
s
and then for the next
N
spin flips attempt (following
a sequential updating scheme) we store the quantities

\Delta_{l}(s)=\text{\scriptsize$\frac{1}{T}$}\Bigl[\sigma_{l}(s+1)-\tanh\left(\text{\scriptsize$\frac{1}{T}$}H_{l}(s)\right)\Bigr],

(26)

here
H_{l}(s)
is the sum of the 5 spins that interact with the spin
\sigma_{l}

at the moment that the spin flip is attempted.
After the
N
spin flip attempts we evaluate the two time response function at the time
t>s
with the relation

R(t,s)=\frac{1}{N}\left\langle\sum_{l}\sigma_{l}(t)\Delta_{l}(s)\right\rangle.

(27)

With this method
C(t,s)
,
R(t,s)
and
\partial_{s}C(t,s)
can be evaluated in the same simulation starting with
M_{0}=0
.

All the simulations, in the static and dynamic cases, were carried out using the parallel platform CUDA
[
34
]
on a
Graphics Processing Unit (GPU). In the static simulations
we divide the lattice into 4 sub-lattices and performed each MCTS in 4 iterations. For the dynamic simulations we divided the
lattice in 16 sub-lattices and performed each MCTS in 16 iterations. The procedure is equivalent to
update the spins sequentially in a standard Computer Processing Unit (CPU) simulation. In FIG.
2
) we show, as examples, the
comparison between the GPU and CPU for the a) static and b) dynamic simulations.

V
Conclusions

We have performed numerical simulations using the parallel platform CUDA to study the static critical phenomena and the critical dynamic in the three dimensional Ising model on an alternate geometry with coordination number equal 5. We present numerical evidence that the static critical exponents
\nu,~\gamma
and
\beta
, and the dynamic critical exponents
\lambda/z,~\theta^{\prime}
and
z

are in good agreement with the expected values of the three dimensional Ising model universality class. Our result for the critical Binder
cumulant seems to corroborate that this quantity depends on the boundary conditions used in the simulation. Additional simulations with alternative
boundary conditions in this system could give additional evidence in this matter.
We were able to succesfully implent the method proposed by Chatelain for the evaluation of the response function using the parallel platform CUDA.
Finally we provided numerical evidence that the limiting value of fluctuation-dissipation ratio in the three dimensional model starting from a dissordered state
by Calabrese and Gambassi with the two-loop
\epsilon
expansion is correct.

Abstract
In this paper, based on Stein-Weiss Lemma, we study the sharp constants for multilinear integral operators with the nonnegative kernels on Heisenberg group Lebesgue space, weighted Lebesgue space and weighted Morrey space. Using the integral, we can easily calculate the sharp constants for multilinear Hilbert operator, the multilinear Hardy-Littlewood-P o ´ ​ l ​ y ​ a \acute{o}lya operator and the multilinear Hardy operator on Heisenberg group Lebesgue space. In addition, we also consider the boundedness for multilinear Calder o ´ \acute{o} n-Zygmund operator on A p A_{p} weighted Morrey space.

Now, we recall some basic knowledge about Heisenberg group.

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is non-commutative nilpotent Lie group, with the underlying manifold
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
and the group law.

Let

x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1}),y=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{2n},y_{2n+1}),

then

x\circ y=\left(x_{1}+y_{1},\ldots,x_{2n}+y_{2n},x_{2n+1}+y_{2n+1}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}(y_{j}x_{n+j}-x_{j}y_{n+j})\right).

Note that Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
is a homogeneous group with dilations

\delta_{r}(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})=(rx_{1},rx_{2},\ldots,rx_{2n},r^{2}x_{2n+1}),\quad r>0.

The Haar measure on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
coincides with the usual Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
. We denote any measurable set
E\subset\mathbb{H}^{n}
by
|E|
, then

|\delta_{r}(E)|=r^{Q}|E|,d(\delta_{r}x)=r_{Q}dx,

where
Q=2n+2
is called the homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{H}^{n}
.

The Heisenberg distance derived from the norm

|x|_{h}=\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{2n}x_{i}^{2}\right)^{2}+x_{2n+1}^{2}\right]^{1/4},

where
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{2n},x_{2n+1})
is given by

d(p,q)=d(q^{-1}p,0)=|q^{-1}p|_{h}.

This distance
d
is left-invariant in the sense that
d(p,q)
remains unchanged when
p
and
q
are both left-translated by some fixed vector on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Besides,
d
satisfies the triangular inequality defined by
[
8
]

d(p,q)\leq d(p,x)+d(x,q),\quad p,x,q\in\mathbb{H}^{n}.

For
r>0
and
x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
, the ball and sphere with center
x
and radius
r
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
are given by

B(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)<r\}

and

S(x,r)=\{y\in\mathbb{H}^{n}:d(x,y)=r\}.

Then we have

|B(x,r)|=|B(0,r)|=\Omega_{Q}r^{Q},

where

\Omega_{Q}=\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}

denote the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)
on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
that is
\omega_{Q}=Q\Omega_{Q}
(see
[
3
]
). For more details about Heisenberg group can be refer to
[
2
]
and
[
16
]
.

Sharp constants for multilinear integral operators on Heisenberg group

In this section, we begin to study multilinear integral operators on Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. Now we give our main result as follows.

Theorem 2.1
.

For operators
H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)
, which maps
L^{p}_{1}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p}_{m}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
. That is,

{\left\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\right\|_{{L^{{p_{1}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\times\cdots\times{L^{{p_{m}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\to{L^{p}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})}}\leq{C_{m}},

(2.1)

where

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{p}

and
1\leq p_{j},p\leq\infty
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, the optimal constant is

C_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p_{i}}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(2.2)

Moreover, if operator
H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)
is bounded, we can obtain the norm of the operator
H(f_{1},\ldots,

f_{m})(x)
. That is,

{\|H(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\|_{{L^{{p_{1}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\times\cdots\times{L^{{p_{m}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\to{L^{p}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})}}={C_{m}}.

(2.3)

Theorem 2.2
.

For operator
H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)
, which maps
L^{p_{1}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{\alpha_{1}p_{1}}{p}}dx)\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{\alpha_{m}p_{m}}{p}}dx)
to
L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)
. That is,

\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p_{1}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{\alpha_{1}p_{1}}{p}}dx)\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{\alpha_{m}p_{m}}{p}}dx)\rightarrow L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}\leq{D_{m}},

(2.4)

where

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{p},\quad\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}=\alpha

and
1\leq p_{j}
,
p\leq\infty
,
\alpha_{j}<pQ(1-1/p_{j})
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
, then the sharp constant is

D_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p_{j}}-\frac{\alpha_{j}}{p}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(2.5)

Moreover, if operator
H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)
is bounded, we can obtain the norm of the operator
H(f_{1},\ldots,

f_{m})(x)
. That is

{\|H(f_{1},\dots,f_{m})\|_{{L^{{p_{1}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\times\cdots\times{L^{{p_{m}}}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\to{L^{p}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})}}={D_{m}}.

(2.6)

In order to prove Theorem
2.1
and Theorem
2.2
, we first need to give the following lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
, if
t\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
and
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, then we have

\|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=|t|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

(2.7)

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}\cdot)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}|t|_{h}^{-Q}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=|t|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle=|t|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p}}\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}.

Lemma
2.1
is thus proved.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
, where
e_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)
, then we have

K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})=K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}).

When
\alpha_{i}=0
, the sharp constant on Heisenberg group Lebesgue space will be easy to get. So we only prove Theorem
2.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.2
.

Set

g_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})\mathrm{d}\xi_{j},\quad x\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

then
g_{j}(j=1,\ldots,m)
are radial functions. By change of variables, we have

\displaystyle H(g_{f_{1}},\ldots,g_{f_{m}})(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K\left(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right)g_{1}\left(y_{1}\right)\ldots g_{m}(y_{m})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K\left(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\int_{|\xi_{j}|=1}f_{j}(\delta_{|y|_{h}}\xi_{j})d\xi_{j}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=|y_{j}|_{h}}f_{j}(z_{j})|y_{j}|_{h}^{-Q}dz_{j}\right)dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{|y_{j}|_{h}=|z_{j}|_{h}}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-Q}dy_{j}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(z_{j})dz_{i}\cdots dz_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(z_{j})dz_{i}\cdots dz_{m}

\displaystyle=H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x).

Using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{j}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}dx)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}f(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})d\xi_{j}\right|^{p_{i}}|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\left\{\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left(\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}\left|f\left(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j}\right)\right|^{p_{i}}d\xi_{j}\right)\left(\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}d\xi_{j}\right)^{{p_{i}}/p^{\prime}_{i}}|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}\mathrm{d}x\right\}^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\omega_{Q}^{-1/{p_{i}}}\left\{\int_{0}^{+\infty}\int_{|x^{\prime}|_{h}=1}\left(\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}|f(\delta_{r}\xi_{j})|^{p_{i}}d\xi_{j}\right)r^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}+Q-1}dx^{\prime}dr\right\}^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f_{j}(y)|^{p_{i}}dy\right)^{1/{p_{i}}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}dx)}.

Thus, we have obtained

\frac{\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}dx)}}\leq\frac{\|H(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\|_{L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{p_{i}\alpha_{i}}{p}}dx)}}.

This implies the operator
H
and its restriction to radial function have same norm in
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
, without loss of generality, we assume that
f_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,m
are radial functions in the rest of the proof.

By Minkowski’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod^{m}_{j=1}f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}y_{j})dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\right|^{p}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\left|\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(\delta_{|y_{j}|_{h}}x)\right|^{p}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{1/p}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\left(\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}|f_{j}(\delta_{|y_{j}|_{h}}x)|^{p_{j}}|x|_{h}^{p_{j}\alpha_{j}/p}dx\right)^{1/p_{j}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f(\delta_{|y|_{h}}\cdot)\|_{L^{p_{j}}(|x|_{h}^{p_{j}\alpha_{j}/p}dx)}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

Using Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{j}-\alpha_{j}/p}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(|x|_{h}^{p_{j}\alpha_{j}/p}dx)}

\displaystyle\leq D_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}}(|x|_{h}^{p_{j}\alpha_{j}/p}dx)}.

Next, take

f_{j}=|x|_{h}^{-\left(\frac{Q}{p^{j}}+\frac{\alpha_{j}}{p}\right)},

through a simple calculation, we can get

H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})=D_{m}|x|_{h}^{-\left(\frac{Q}{p}+\alpha\right)}.

Moreover,

\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx)}=D_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p^{j}}(|x|_{h}^{\frac{p^{j}\alpha^{j}}{p}}dx)}.

The proof of Theorem
2.2
is finished.

In this section, we will give some special cases for the multilinear operators on Heisenberg group. The Hilbert operator is the essential extension of the classical Hilbert’s inequality , the multilinear Hilbert operator is defined by

B_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,y_{m})(x):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{(|x|^{n}+|y_{1}|^{n}+\cdots+|y_{m}|^{n})^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

\int_{0}^{\infty}B_{1}^{*}f(x)g(x)dx\leq\frac{\pi}{\sin(\pi/p)}\|f\|_{L^{p}(0,\infty)}\|g\|_{L^{p^{\prime}(0,\infty)}}.

Now, we give the definition of the multilinear Hilbert operator on Heisenberg group.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose that each

\mathbb{H}^{n}
for

Theorem 3.2
.

If

satisfy

Proof.

The kernel of the operator in (
3.1
) satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
. Using the polar coordinates and making change of variables, we have

\displaystyle\|B_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}\cdots|y_{m}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{m}}}{(1+|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q}+\cdots+|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\omega_{Q}^{m}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r_{1}^{Q-Q/p_{1}-1}\cdots r_{m}^{Q-Q/p_{m}-1}}{(1+r_{1}^{Q}+\cdots+r_{m}^{Q})^{m}}dr_{1}\cdots dr_{m}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-1/p_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{-1/p_{m}}}{(1+t_{1}+t_{2}+\cdots+t_{m})^{m}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m},

Set

I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m}):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{m}^{-\beta_{m}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m})^{\alpha}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m}.

By a simple calculate

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}dt=\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-t)^{-\beta}t^{\alpha+\beta-2}dt.

By the definition of Beta function
B
, we have

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+t)^{\alpha}t^{\beta}}dt=B(1-\beta,\alpha+\beta-1).

Using the variable substitution
t_{m}=(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m}-1)q_{m}
, we have

\displaystyle I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t_{1}^{-\beta_{1}}\cdots t_{m-1}^{-\beta_{m-1}}}{(1+t_{1}+\cdots+t_{m-1})^{\alpha-1+\beta_{m}}}dt_{1}\cdots dt_{m-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(1+q_{m})^{\alpha}q_{m}^{\beta_{m}}}dq_{m}

\displaystyle=B(1-\beta_{m},\alpha+\beta_{m}-1)I_{m-1}(\alpha-1+\beta_{m},\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m-1}).

Using the inductive method together with the properties of Gamma function
\Gamma
, we have

I_{m}(\alpha,\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{m})=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{k}\Gamma(1-\beta_{i})\Gamma(\alpha-k+\prod_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i})}{\Gamma(\alpha)}.

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle\|B_{m}^{h}\|_{\prod_{i=1}^{m}H^{\infty}_{\alpha_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow H^{\infty}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{Q}^{m}}{Q^{m}}\frac{\prod^{m}_{i=1}\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}{\Gamma(m)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{2\pi^{n+\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma(n/2)}{(n+1)\Gamma(n)\Gamma((n+1)/2)}\right)^{m}\frac{\prod^{m}_{i=1}\Gamma(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}})\Gamma(\frac{1}{p})}{\Gamma(m)}.

Thus, we finished the proof.

23
]
, Hardy et al. obtained the norm of Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})(1<q<\infty)
, that is

\|P_{1}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+}))}=\frac{p^{2}}{p-1}.

We give the definition of the multilinear Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator on Heisenberg group.

Definition 3.3
.

Suppose that
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
be nonnegative locally integrable functions on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. The
m
-linear
n
-dimensional Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator is defined by

P^{h}_{m}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})}{[\text{max}(|x|_{h}^{Q},|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},\ldots,|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\backslash\{0\}.

(3.2)

Theorem 3.4
.

If
1<p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\leq\infty
satisfy

0<\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{p}\leq 1,

then
P_{m}^{h}
is bounded from
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
and

\|P_{m}^{h}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}=\frac{m\Omega_{Q}^{m}p}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-1/p_{j})}.

Proof.

The kernel of the operator in (
3.2
) satisfies the conditions of Theorem
2.1
. In order to get the sharp constant of mutilinear Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya operator, we will consider the following two scenarios and combine them to obtain our result.

1.case when
m=2
.

In this case , we have

P^{h}_{2}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}.

By calculation,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}

\displaystyle\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}<1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}-2Q}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|y_{2}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}-2Q}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle:=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}.

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}<1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{2}}{(1-1/p_{1})(1-1/p_{2})}

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}-2Q}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}}{1-1/p_{2}}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p-Q}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{2}p}{(1-1/p_{2})}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}<|y_{2}|_{h}}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}-2Q}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}}{1-1/p_{1}}\int_{|y_{2}|_{h}>1}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p-Q}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{2}p}{(1-1/p_{1})}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{H}^{n}}\frac{|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}}|y_{2}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{2}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},|y_{2}|_{h}^{Q})]^{2}}dy_{1}dy_{2}

\displaystyle=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2p\Omega_{Q}^{2}}{(1-1/p_{1})(1-p_{2})}.

2.case when
m\geq 3
.

Let

\displaystyle E_{0}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{k}|_{h}\leq 1,1\leq k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{1}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{1}|_{h}>1,|y_{k}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h},2\leq k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{i}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{i}|_{h}>1,|y_{j}|_{h}<|y_{k}|_{h},|y_{k}|_{h}\leq|y_{i}|_{h},1\leq j<i<k\leq m\};

\displaystyle E_{m}=\{(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n}:|y_{m}|_{h}>1,|y_{j}|_{h}<|y_{m}|_{h},1<j<m\};

obviously, we have

\bigcup_{j=0}^{m}E_{j}=\mathbb{H}^{n}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{H}^{n},E_{i}\cap E_{j}=\emptyset.

Taking

K_{j}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{i}}}{[\text{max}(1,|y_{1}|_{h}^{Q},\ldots,|y_{m}|_{h}^{Q})]^{m}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m},

then we begin to calculate
K_{j}
with
j=0,1,\ldots,m
.

K_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\int_{|y_{j}|\leq 1}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{j}}dy_{j}=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{m}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-1/p_{j})},

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{|y_{1}|>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{1}-Qm}dy_{1}\prod_{j=2}^{m}\int_{|y_{j}|_{h}\leq|y_{1}|_{h}}|y_{j}|_{h}^{-Q/p_{j}}dy_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{m-1}}{\prod_{j=2}^{m}(1-1/p_{j})}\int_{|y_{1}|_{h}>1}|y_{1}|_{h}^{-Q/p-Q}dy_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{m}p}{\prod_{j=2}^{m}(1-1/p_{j})}.

So we can deduce that

K_{j}=\frac{\Omega_{Q}^{m}p}{\prod_{1\leq i\leq m,i\neq j}(1-1/p_{j})}.

Then we can obtain that

K_{m}=\frac{m\Omega_{Q}^{m}p}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}(1-1/p_{j})}.

Combining the above two cases, we finished the proof.
∎

Next, define the mutilinear Hardy operator on Heisenberg group as follows,

D_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x):=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|B(0,|x|_{h})|}\int_{B(o,|x|_{h})}|f_{i}(x_{i})|dx_{i},

(3.3)

where
x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We conclude from (
3.3
) that

D_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)\prod_{i=1}^{m}Df_{i}(x),

where

D^{h}f_{i}(x):=\frac{1}{|B(0,|x|_{h})|}\int_{B(o,|x|_{h})}|f_{i}(x_{i})|dx_{i}.

(3.4)

The operator
D^{h}
in (
3.4
) is defined by Wu and Fu in
[
19
]
.

Now we define a kernel function
K
as

K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})=\frac{1}{(\Omega_{Q}|x|_{h}^{Q})^{m}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\chi_{|y_{i}|_{h}\leqslant|x|_{h}}(y_{i},x).

(3.5)

Clearly, the operator
D_{m}^{h}
is equivalent to

D_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x)=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(x_{i})dx_{1}\cdots dx_{m}.

Next, we formulate our main theorem.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
f_{i}\in L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
for
1<p_{i}\leqslant\infty
with
i=1,\ldots,m
. If
1<p\leqslant\infty
and

\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{p_{m}}=\frac{1}{p},

then we have

\|D_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leqslant\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

and
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}
is the sharp bound.

Proof.

The kernel in (
3.5
) satisfies the Theorem
2.1
. According to Theorem
2.1
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|D_{m}^{h}\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K\left(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}|y_{i}|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{p_{i}}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{Q}^{-m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int_{|y_{i}|_{h}\leqslant 1}|y_{i}|^{-\frac{Q}{p_{i}}}dy_{i}

\displaystyle=\Omega_{Q}^{-m}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{1}r_{i}^{Q-1}\int_{S^{Q-1}}r_{i}^{-\frac{Q}{p_{i}}}d\sigma\left(y_{i}^{\prime}\right)dr_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\omega_{Q}}{\Omega_{Q}}\right)^{m}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\int_{0}^{1}r_{i}^{Q-1-\frac{Q}{p_{i}}}d\sigma\left(y_{i}^{\prime}\right)dr_{i}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}

This completes the proof.
∎

We remark that we can use the H
\ddot{o}
lder’s inequality to prove the
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\rightarrow L^{p}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
-boundedness of
D_{m}^{h}
, i.e.,

\|D_{m}^{h}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{p}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})\leqslant\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|D^{h}f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i}}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}\leqslant\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{pi}(\mathbb{H}^{n})}

holds. Obviously
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}
is a bound of the operator
D_{m}^{h}
. However, it is not clear that
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{p_{i}}{p_{i}-1}
must be the sharp bound.

4
Sharp constants for multilinear integral operator on Morrey space with two power weight in Heisenberg group

The Morrey space with power weight as a special case of Lebesgue space and
A_{p}
weighted Morrey space have some very interesting properties. Inspired by
[
6
]
, we begin to consider sharp constant for multilinear integral operator on Morrey space with power weight in Heisenberg group.

For any measurable function
\omega^{*}
over a set
E
is given by

\omega^{*}(E)=\int_{E}w^{*}dx.

In what follows,
B(|x|_{h},R)
denotes the ball centered at
|x|_{h}
with radius
R
,
|B(|x|_{h},R)|
denotes the Lebesgue measure of
B(|x|_{h},R)
.

We will use this notation in the following definition of two power weighted Morrey spaces.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\omega^{*}_{1},\omega^{*}_{2}:\mathbb{H}^{n}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
are positive measurable function,
1\leq q<\infty
and
-1/q\leq\lambda<0
. The two power weighted Morrey space
L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\omega^{*}_{1},\omega^{*}_{2})
is defined by

L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\omega^{*}_{1},\omega^{*}_{2})=\{f:\|f\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},\omega^{*}_{1},\omega^{*}_{2})}<\infty\},

where

\|f\|_{L^{q,\lambda}\left(\mathbb{H}^{n},w^{*}_{1},w^{*}_{2}\right)}=\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}w_{1}(B(|a|_{h},R))^{-(\lambda+1/q)}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|f(x)|^{q}w_{2}(x)dx\right)^{1/q}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f_{i}
be radial functions in
L^{q_{j},\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})
,
1\leq q<\infty
,
-\frac{1}{q}\leq\lambda<0
,
1<q_{j}<\infty
,
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{q_{m}}
,
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\cdots+\gamma_{m}
,
-\frac{1}{q_{j}}\leq\lambda_{j}<0
with
j=1,\ldots,m
.
Then we have

\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}\leq A_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda}_{j}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})},

(4.1)

where

A_{m}=\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|y_{j}|^{Q\lambda_{j}-\frac{1}{q_{j}}\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}+\alpha(\lambda_{j}+\frac{1}{q_{j}})}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(4.2)

Moreover, if
\alpha\neq-Q,-\frac{1}{q_{j}}<\lambda_{j}<0
and
q\lambda=q_{j}\lambda_{j}
with
j=1,\ldots,m
, then we have

\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{\prod_{j=1}^{m}L^{q_{j},\lambda_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})\rightarrow L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}=A_{m}.

(4.3)

To facilitate the proof of Theorem
4.2
, we need to give an important result that is similar in form of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
,
-\frac{1}{q}\leq\lambda<0
and
\alpha,\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. If
t\in\mathbb{H}^{n}
,
f\in L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})
, then we have

\|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}\cdot)\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}=|t|_{h}^{Q\lambda-\frac{\gamma}{q}+\alpha(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}.

(4.4)

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}\cdot)\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|f(\delta_{|}t|_{h}x)|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|f(\delta_{|t|_{h}}x)|^{q}||t|_{h}x|^{\gamma}|t|_{h}^{-\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{q}-\frac{\gamma}{q}}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|t\alpha|_{h},|t|_{h}R)}|f(x)|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t|_{h}^{-\frac{Q}{q}-\frac{\gamma}{q}}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}||t|_{h}x|_{h}^{\alpha}|t|_{h}^{-\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|t\alpha|_{h},|t|_{h}R)}|f(x)|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t|_{h}^{Q\lambda-\frac{\gamma}{q}+\alpha(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|ta|_{h},|t|_{h}R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|t\alpha|_{h},|t|_{h}R)}|f(x)|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\gamma}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|t|_{h}^{Q\lambda-\frac{\gamma}{q}+\alpha(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\|f\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}.

This finishs the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

Next, we will give the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Set

g_{j}(x)=\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})d\xi_{j},\quad x\in\mathbb{H}^{n},

we have obtained that

H(g_{f_{1}},\ldots,g_{f_{m}})(x)=H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x).

Using Minkowski’s inequality and Hölder’s inequality, for
j=1,\ldots,m
, we have

\displaystyle\|g_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j},\gamma_{j}}{q}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}\left|\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})d\xi_{j}\right|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\sup_{a\in\mathbb{H}^{n},R>0}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})|^{q}|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}d\xi_{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|x|_{h}^{\alpha}dx\right)^{-(\lambda+\frac{1}{q})}\left(\frac{1}{\omega_{Q}}\int_{|\xi_{j}|_{h}=1}\int_{B(|a|_{h},R)}|f_{j}(\delta_{|x|_{h}}\xi_{j})|^{q}|x|^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}}dxd\xi_{j}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}.

Thus, we have

\frac{\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}}\leq\frac{\|H(g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}}{\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|g_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda_{j}}(\mathbb{H}^{n}|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}}.

Then by Minkowski’s inequality, Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.2
and Lemma
4.1
, we can easily get

\displaystyle\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}K(e_{1},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})\prod_{j=1}^{m}|y_{j}|^{Q\lambda_{j}-\frac{1}{q_{j}}\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}+\alpha(\lambda_{j}+\frac{1}{q_{j}})}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}.

Taking

f_{j}(x)=|x|_{h}^{Q\lambda_{j}-\frac{1}{q_{j}}\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}+\alpha(\lambda_{j}+\frac{1}{q_{j}})},j=1,\ldots,m,

we have

\|H(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\|_{L^{q,\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\gamma})}=A_{m}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{q_{j},\lambda}(\mathbb{H}^{n},|x|_{h}^{\alpha},|x|_{h}^{\frac{q_{j}\gamma_{j}}{q}})}.

The proof of Theorem
4.2
is finished.
∎

5
The Boundedness for multilinear Calder
\acute{o}
n-Zygmund operators on Heisenberg group weighted Morrey spaces

In this section, we give the boundedness for multilinear integral operator on Heisenberg group weighted Morrey spaces.
The classical
A_{p}
weight theory was introduced by Muckenhoupt in the study of weighted
Lebesgue boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal functions, one can see Chapter 7 in
[
4
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

A weight
\omega
is a nonnegative locally integrable function on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
. We denote the ball
of radius
r
centered
x_{0}
by
B=B(x_{0},r)
, we say that
\omega\in A_{p}
for some
1<p<\infty
, if

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)\,dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\,dx\right)^{p-1}\leq C\quad\mbox{for every ball}\;B\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n},

where
C
is a positive constant which is independent of
B
.

We say
\omega\in A_{1}
, if

\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)\,dx\leq C\,\underset{x\in B}{\mbox{ess\,inf}}\,\omega(x)\quad\mbox{for every ball}\;B\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n},

we denote

{A_{\infty}}=\bigcup\limits_{1\leq p<\infty}{{A_{p}}}.

Definition 5.2
.

A weight function
\omega
is said to belong to the reverse Hölder class
RH_{r}
if there exist two constants
r>1
and
C>0
such that the following reverse Hölder inequality holds

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)^{r}\,dx\right)^{1/r}\leq C\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega(x)\,dx\right)\quad\mbox{for every ball}\;B\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}.

It is well known that if
\omega\in A_{p}
with
1<p<\infty
, then
\omega\in A_{r}
for all
r>p
and
\omega\in A_{q}
for any
1<q<p
. If
\omega\in A_{p}
with
1\leq p<\infty
, then there exists
r>1
such that
\omega\in RH_{r}
.

Now let us recall the definitions of multiple weights.

Definition 5.3
.

For
m
exponents
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}
, we denote
\vec{P}
by the vector
\vec{P}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{m})
. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}\in[1,\infty)
and
p\in(0,\infty)
with
1/p=\sum_{k=1}^{m}1/{p_{k}}
. Given
\vec{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{m})
, set
\nu_{\vec{\omega}}=\prod_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}^{p/{p_{i}}}
. We say that
\vec{\omega}
satisfies the
A_{\vec{P}}
condition if it satisfies

\sup_{B}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega_{i}(x)^{1-p^{\prime}_{i}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}<\infty,

(5.1)

where
p_{i}=1,

\left(\frac{1}{|B|}\int_{B}\omega_{i}(x)^{1-p^{\prime}_{i}}\,dx\right)^{1/{p^{\prime}_{i}}}
is understood as
{(\mathop{\inf}\limits_{x\in B}{\omega_{i}}(x))^{-1}}
.

The classical Morrey spaces
L^{p,\lambda}
were first introduced by Morrey
[
12
]
to study the local behavior of solutions to second order elliptic partial differential equations. In 2009, Komori and Shirai
[
7
]
considered the weighted Morrey spaces
L^{p,\kappa}(\omega)
and studied the boundedness of some classical operators such as the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and the Calderón-Zygmund operator on these spaces.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa<1
and
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then the weighted Morrey space is defined by

L^{p,\kappa}(\omega)=\big\{f:\big\|f\big\|_{L^{p,\kappa}(\omega)}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{B\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}}\omega{(B)^{-\frac{\kappa}{p}}}{\|f\|_{{L^{p}}(B,\omega dx)}}<\infty\big\}.

Definition 5.5
.

Let
0<p<\infty
,
0<\kappa<1
and
\omega
be a weight function on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, then weighted weak Morrey space is defined by

WL^{p,\kappa}(\omega)=\left\{f:\big\|f\big\|_{WL^{p,\kappa}(\omega)}=\mathop{\sup}\limits_{B\subseteq\mathbb{H}^{n}}\omega{(B)^{-\frac{\kappa}{p}}}{\left\|f\right\|_{W{L^{p}}(B,\omega dx)}}<\infty\right\}.

(5.2)

We now recall the definitions of multilinear Calderón-Zygmund operators with Dini kernel.

Definition 5.6
.

For any
t\in(0,\infty),
let
{K}(x,{y_{1}},\cdots,{y_{m}})
be a locally integrable function defined away from the diagonal
x={y_{1}}=\cdots={y_{m}}
in
(\mathbb{H}^{n})^{m+1}
. We say
K
is a kernel of type
\theta
if for some constants
A>0,
such that

(1)

\left|{K(x,\vec{y})}\right|\leq\frac{A}{{{{(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|}_{h}}})}^{mn}}}};

(2)

\left|{K(x,\vec{y})-K(x,{y_{1}},\cdots,{y_{i}}^{\prime},\cdots,{y_{m}})}\right|\leq\frac{A}{{{{(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|}_{h}}})}^{mn}}}}\cdot\theta(\frac{{{{\left|{{{({{y^{\prime}}_{i}})}^{-1}}{y_{i}}}\right|}_{h}}}}{{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|}_{h}}}}});

(3)

\left|{K(z,\vec{y})-K(x,\vec{y})}\right|\leq\frac{A}{{{{(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|}_{h}}})}^{mn}}}}\cdot\theta(\frac{{{{\left|{{{(x)}^{-1}}z}\right|}_{h}}}}{{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|}_{h}}}}}),

where
(2)
holds for any
i\in\{1,\cdots,m\}
, whenever
{\left|{{{({y_{i}}^{\prime})}^{-1}}{y_{i}}}\right|_{h}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq m}\{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|_{h}}\}

and
(3)
holds whenever
{\left|{{{(x)}^{-1}}z}\right|_{h}}\leq\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq m}\{{\left|{{{({y_{j}})}^{-1}}x}\right|_{h}}\}
.

When
\theta\left(t\right)={t^{\gamma}}
for some
\gamma>0
, we say
K
is a
m
-linear Calderón-Zygmund kernel.

We say
T:{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\times\cdots\times{\mathscr{S}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\to{\mathscr{S}}^{\prime}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})
is an
m
-linear Calderón-Zygmund operator with kernel
K
if

T(\vec{f})(x)=\int_{{{\mathbb{(}{\mathbb{R}^{n}})^{m}}}}{K(x,\vec{y})\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{f_{j}}({y_{j}})d{\vec{y}}}},

for any
\vec{f}\in{C_{c}^{\infty}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})\times\cdots\times{C_{c}^{\infty}}({{\mathbb{H}^{n}}})
and any
x\notin\bigcap\limits_{j=1}^{m}{{\rm{supp}}{f_{j}}}
, and
T
can be extended to be a bounded operator from
{L^{{q_{1}}}}\times\cdots\times{L^{{q_{m}}}}
to
{L^{q}}
, for some
1\leq{q_{1}}\cdots,{q_{m}}<\infty,\frac{1}{q}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{m}{\frac{1}{{{q_{k}}}}}
.

T
is called a
m
-linear Calderón-Zygmund operator with Dini kernel
K
when
K
is a kernel of type
\theta\in Dini(1)
.

Proof of Theorem

.

For any ball

, let

, where

, for any

and

. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}(y_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}(f_{i}^{0}(y_{i})+f_{i}^{\infty}(y_{i}))

(5.9)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}\in\{0,\infty\}}f_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(y_{m})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}^{0}(y_{i})+\sum_{\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{m}\neq 0}f_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}^{\alpha_{m}}(y_{m}).

Thus, we have

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}{(B)^{-\frac{\kappa}{p}}}{\|{H({f_{1}},\ldots,{f_{m}})}\|_{{L^{p}}(B,{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}dx)}}

(5.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\displaystyle\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}{(B)^{-\frac{\kappa}{p}}}{\|{H(f_{1}^{0},\ldots,f_{m}^{0})}\|_{{L^{p}}(B,{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}dx)}}+\sum\limits_{{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+{\alpha_{m}}\neq 0}{{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}{{(B)}^{-\frac{\kappa}{p}}}{{\|{T(f_{1}^{{\alpha_{1}}},\ldots,f_{m}^{{\alpha_{m}}})}\|}_{{}_{{L^{p}}(B,{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}}dx)}}}}

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle{\displaystyle I^{0}}+\sum\limits_{{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+{\alpha_{m}}\neq 0}{{I^{{\alpha_{1}}\ldots,{\alpha_{m}}}}}.

Next, we need to prove

I^{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{m}}\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(\omega_{i})},

(5.11)

where
{\alpha_{i}}\in(0,\infty)
, for any
i=1,\ldots,m
. Using Lemma
5.2
, we have
\nu_{\vec{\omega}}\in A_{mp}
. Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
5.10
allows us to get

\displaystyle I^{0}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\int_{2B}|f_{i}(x)|^{p_{i}}\omega_{i}(x)dx\right)^{1/p_{i}}

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(\omega_{i})}\cdot\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}(2B)^{\kappa/p_{i}}}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa(\omega_{i})}}\cdot\frac{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(2B)^{\kappa/p}}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(B)^{\kappa/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(\omega_{i})}.

For
H
, we have

\displaystyle|T(\vec{f})(x)|\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}}\frac{|\prod_{j=1}^{m}f_{j}(y_{j})|}{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(y_{i})^{-1}x|_{h}\right)^{mQ}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}.

(5.13)

To obtain these conclusions, we need establish some geometric relationships by trigonometric inequality as follows.

(i) If
x\in B
,

, we have

\left|(y)^{-1}x\right|_{h}\approx\left|(y)^{-1}x_{0}\right|_{h};

(ii) If

,

,

, we have

|{(y)^{-1}}x|_{h}^{Q}\approx\left|{{2^{j+1}}B}\right|.

For other terms, we first consider the case of
\alpha_{1}=\cdots=\alpha_{m}=\infty
. For
x\in B
, we have

\displaystyle\left|H(f_{1}^{\infty},\ldots,f_{m}^{\infty})(x)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{H}^{nm}\backslash(2B)^{m}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\ldots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(y_{i})^{-1}x|_{h})^{mQ}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

(5.14)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\int_{(2^{j+1}B)^{m}\backslash(2^{j}B)^{m}}\frac{|f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})|}{(\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(y_{i})^{-1}x|_{h})^{mQ}}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{m}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\int_{2^{j+1}B}|f_{i}(y_{i})|dy_{i}.

By using Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|T(f_{1}^{\infty},\ldots,f_{m}^{\infty})(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|}\left(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\left|f_{i}(y_{i})\right|^{p_{i}}\omega_{i}(y_{i})dy_{i}\right)^{1/p_{i}}\left(\int_{2^{j+1}B}\omega_{i}(y_{i})^{1-p_{i}^{\prime}}dy_{i}\right)^{1/p_{i}^{\prime}}

(5.15)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{|2^{j+1}B|^{m}}\cdot\frac{\left|2^{j+1}B\right|^{\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)}}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left(\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(\omega_{i})}\omega_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/p_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\|f_{i}\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}(\omega_{i})}\right)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}\omega_{i}(2^{j+1}B)^{\kappa/p_{i}}}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(2^{j+1}B)^{1/p}}\right)

\displaystyle I^{\infty,\ldots,\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}\left(\omega_{i}\right)}\right)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}(B)^{(1-\kappa)/p}}{\nu_{\vec{\omega}}\left(2^{j+1}B\right)^{(1-\kappa)/p}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|f_{i}\right\|_{L^{p_{i},\kappa}\left(\omega_{i}\right)}\right)\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{|B|}{\left|2^{j+1}B\right|}\right)^{\delta(1-\kappa)/p}

(5.17)

The last inequality holds since

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

G4CASCADE: A data-driven implementation of ( n n , γ \gamma ) cascades in Geant4
Leo Weimer, Emma Ellingwood, Otis Fischer, Michela Lai, Shawn Westerdale

Abstract
De-excitation γ \gamma -ray cascades from neutron captures form a dominant background to MeV-scale signals. The Geant4 Monte Carlo simulation toolkit is widely used to model backgrounds in nuclear and particle physics experiments. While its current modules for simulating ( n n , γ \gamma ) signals, G4NDL and G4PhotonEvaporation , are excellent for many applications, they do not reproduce known γ \gamma -ray lines and correlations relevant at 2 – 15 MeV 215\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV} . G4CASCADE is a new data-driven Geant4 module that simulates ( n n , γ \gamma ) de-excitation pathways, with options for how to handle shortcomings in nuclear data. Benchmark comparisons to measured γ \gamma -ray lines and level structures in the ENSDF database and other sources show significant improvements, with decreased residuals and full energy conservation. This manuscript describes the underlying calculations performed by G4CASCADE , its various usage options, and benchmark comparisons. G4CASCADE for Geant4 -10 is available on GitHub at https://github.com/UCRDarkMatter/CASCADE

3
The
G4CASCADE
software

Daughter nuclei energy levels and transitions, including branching ratios and conversion coefficients, are input to
G4CASCADE
from an
ENSDF
-derived library.

G4CASCADE
integrates into
Geant4
’s high precision neutron modules (
G4NeutronHP
), and runs if data exist for a given isotope and if a user-defined environment variable is set to true. Otherwise,
G4NDL
and/or
G4PhotonEvaporation
are used as normal.

After a neutron is captured,
G4CASCADE
begins the de-excitation cascade at the energy level nearest the neutron separation energy. Any excess energy is dissipated by a
\gamma
-ray
or is ignored, depending on user preference.

G4CASCADE
simulates nuclear de-excitation by moving down the energy levels randomly, weighted by the branching ratios for each transition.
For each transition,
\gamma
-rays
are released with energy equal to the difference between the initial and final levels.
Doing so allows for the correlated emission of
\gamma
-rays
with appropriate intensities for many isotopes.
Level structures were generated by converting
ENSDF
thermal neutron capture data for each available isotope.
A binary serialization format is used for this database for faster loading times.

Branching ratios are calculated based on several factors in the
ENSDF
files.
For each transition, the Relative Intensity (
RI
) and the Total Intensity (
\mathtt{TI}
) are multiplied by normalization factors,
\mathtt{NR}
and
\mathtt{NT}
.
If
RI
is defined, the branching ratio BR is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{BR}&=\mathtt{RI}.\end{aligned}

(1)

If
RI
is not defined but
\mathtt{TI}
is, then

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{BR}&=\frac{\mathtt{TI}}{1+\mathtt{CC}}.\end{aligned}

(2)

If the conversion coefficient (
\mathtt{CC}
) is defined, two transitions to the same level are possible, with branching ratios for
\gamma
-ray
and conversion electron emission,
\text{BR}_{\gamma}
and
\text{BR}_{e}
, given by,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{BR}_{\gamma}&=\mathtt{RI}\text{ or }\frac{\mathtt{TI}}{1+\mathtt{CC}},\\
\text{BR}_{e}&=\text{BR}_{\gamma}\times\mathtt{CC}.\end{aligned}

(3)

If an inner shell conversion electron is emitted, the atomic relaxation and Auger cascades are simulated using
G4RDAtomicDeexcitation
.
When both
\mathtt{RI}
and
\mathtt{TI}
are blank, the transition is removed.

ENSDF
lists some
\gamma
-rays
that cannot be placed in the level structure.

G4CASCADE
ignores them by default, but can optionally add them at the top energy level.
Unplaced
\gamma
-ray
and conversion electron transitions’ branching ratios are calculated as in Equations (
2
) and (
3
).
Since these transitions end at an energy level not in the level structure,
G4PhotonEvaporation
is used to complete the de-excitation cascade to the ground state.
This option is referred to as
G4CASCADE
(PE).

After capturing a fast neutron, a nucleus may have energy above the highest known energy level.
Since unbound levels are unknown for most nuclei, approximations must be made.

G4CASCADE
provides users with two options.
First, the energy of the nucleus can be set to the highest available energy level within the level structure, ignoring the excess energy.
This results in a small violation of energy conservation, typically
<200\text{\,}\mathrm{eV}
, although this may be larger if the neutron has more kinetic energy at the time it is captured.
Alternatively, the excess energy may be dissipated as a single
\gamma
-ray
; doing so conserves energy, though introduces
\gamma
-ray
lines that have not been experimentally observed.

5
Discussion

G4CASCADE
’s data-driven approach generally improves upon
G4PhotonEvaporation
and
G4NDL
by agreeing better with data and conserving energy, which
G4NDL
does not do, and it adds atomic de-excitation cascades following conversion electron emissions.
Intensities of
\gamma
-ray
are often more accurately reproduced by
G4CASCADE
than
G4PhotonEvaporation
:
G4CASCADE
decreases the RMS residual by more than
90\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for
\hphantom{{}^{\text{12}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{12}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{12}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{12}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{12}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{C}
,
\hphantom{{}^{\text{28}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{28}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{28}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{28}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{28}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Si}
, and
\hphantom{{}^{\text{48}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{48}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{48}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{48}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{48}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Ti}
, by
80\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for
\hphantom{{}^{\text{40}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{40}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{40}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{40}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{40}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Ar}
, and
56\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
for
\hphantom{{}^{\text{129}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Xe}
.
Isotopes with many unplaced
\gamma
-ray
lines tend to perform less well in
G4CASCADE
.

G4NDL
often includes these lines, with occasional lines that do not appear in
ENSDF
or have different intensity, but it does not properly correlate them or conserve energy.

For
\hphantom{{}^{\text{56}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Fe}
,
CapGam
does not include the relative intensity of a
14\text{\,}\mathrm{k}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}

\gamma
-ray
emitted by the decay of the first excited state of
\hphantom{{}^{\text{57}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Fe}
, likely due to the very high internal conversion coefficient for this transition.
Ref.
[
35
]
reports recent measurement of the
\hphantom{{}^{\text{56}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Fe}
(
n
,
\gamma
)
\hphantom{{}^{\text{57}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{57}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Fe}
reaction, including an updated level structure with the
14\text{\,}\mathrm{keV}
transition and other additions.

G4CASCADE
uses this level structure instead of
ENSDF
for neutron captures on
\hphantom{{}^{\text{56}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{56}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Fe}
.

Missing nuclear structure data can bias
\gamma
-ray
intensities predicted by
G4CASCADE
.
Energy levels for which transitions to or from them are unknown are removed from
G4CASCADE
’s level structure.
If doing so also removes a transition with a large branching ratio, the branching ratio of other transitions from the same level are effectively enhanced, inflating the relative intensities of downstream
\gamma
-rays
.
This effect is particularly pronounced in
\hphantom{{}^{\text{129}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Xe}
and
\hphantom{{}^{\text{155}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{155}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{155}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{155}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{155}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Gd}
in Fig.
3
, where several incomplete energy levels cause significant bias in subsequent transitions.

The
NuDEX
code
[
7
]
provides a similar but alternative model, not currently integrated into
Geant4
, which bases
\gamma
-rays
on
ENSDF
, with missing energy levels completed by the
DICEBOX
statistical nuclear model code and
RIPL-3
nuclear structure data
[
36
]
.
While
NuDEX
and
G4CASCADE
are data-driven and therefore fill similar niches,
G4CASCADE
provides options to integrate unplaced
\gamma
-rays
into the de-excitation cascade using
G4PhotonEvaporation
, comes fully-integrated into
Geant4
, and simulates atomic relaxation
X-rays
and Auger electrons following emission of an inner shell electron.

NuDEX
and
G4CASCADE
reproduce
\gamma
-ray
lines in
CapGam
comparably well, though
NuDEX
’s use of the
DICEBOX
statistical model is likely more accurate than
G4CASCADE
’s use of
G4PhotonEvaporation
.
As a result,
NuDEX
reproduces
CapGam
’s
\gamma
-ray
lines for high-
Z
isotopes more accurately than
G4CASCADE
, since these isotopes typically have more unplaced
\gamma
-ray
lines.

For
\hphantom{{}^{\text{129}}_{\text{}}}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}{}^{\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\displaystyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\textstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}{\hbox to0.0pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle\vphantom{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}\text{129}$}}}\kern 0.0pt\text{Xe}

Likewise, measurements of

6
Conclusions

G4CASCADE
is a data-driven module for simulating
(
n
,
\gamma
)
signals in
Geant4
.
By addressing shortfalls in existing
(
n
,
\gamma
)
in
Geant4
,
G4CASCADE
will help improve background and signal acceptance calculations in MeV-scale rare event searches.
The GitHub repository hosting
G4CASCADE
includes code for generating the input binary files, so that new nuclear structure data or models can be integrated into the model as desired.
As such, additional level structure data for high-
Z
nuclei would further improve
G4CASCADE
’s performance, as would measurements informing the structure of unbound levels, which may be particularly important for fast neutron-capture signals that become important in the
1115\text{\,}\mathrm{MeV}
range.
G4CASCADE
currently does not accurately simulate gamma emission timing, and releases all gammas for one event at the same time following capture. An area of future work is to improve this, likely using data-driven methods.

G4CASCADE
was developed for
Geant4
-10
; an extension to
Geant4
-11
is foreseen.
Additional updates are also planned to allow users to supplement level structures using
DICEBOX
.

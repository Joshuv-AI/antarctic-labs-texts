Concentration Behavior of Ground States for L 2 L^{2} -Critical Schrödinger Equation with a Spatially Decaying Nonlinearity
Yong Luo and Shu Zhang

Abstract
We consider the following time-independent nonlinear L 2 L^{2} -critical Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u ​ ( x ) + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u ​ ( x ) − a ​ | x | − b ​ | u | 1 + 4 − 2 ​ b N = μ ​ u ​ ( x ) ​ in ​ ℝ N , -\Delta u(x)+V(x)u(x)-a|x|^{-b}|u|^{1+\frac{4-2b}{N}}=\mu u(x)\,\ \hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}, where μ ∈ ℝ \mu\in{\mathbb{R}} , a > 0 a>0 , N ≥ 1 N\geq 1 , 0 < b < min ⁡ { 2 , N } 0<b<\min\{2,N\} , and V ⁡ ( x ) V(x) is an external potential. It is shown that ground states of the above equation can be equivalently described by minimizers of the corresponding minimization problem. Introduction

In this paper, we study the following time-independent nonlinear focusing Schrödinger equation:

-\Delta u(x)+V(x)u(x)-au^{1+2\beta^{2}}(x)|x|^{-b}=\mu u(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(1.1)

where
N\geq 1
,
a,\,\beta>0
,
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
,
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the chemical potential, and
V(x)
is an external potential. Equation (
1.1
) arises in various physical context such as nonlinear optics, plasma physics and Bose-Einstein condensates (BECs) (cf.
[
3
,
22
,
24
]
), where the function
a|u|^{2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}
is a potential which can either stand for corrections to the nonlinear power-law response or for some inhomogeneities
in the medium. Due to the singularity of
|x|^{-b}
at
x=0
, much attention have been attracted to existence, asymptotic behavior and uniqueness of solutions for (
1.1
) in the last two decades, see
[
1
,
10
,
12
,
13
,
23
]
and the references therein.

As illustrated in Theorem
A.1
in the Appendix, ground states of (
1.1
) can be equivalently described by minimizers of

e(a)=\inf\limits_{u\in\mathcal{M}}E_{a}(u),

(1.2)

where the energy functional
E_{a}(u)
is defined by

E_{a}(u):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2})dx-\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}}{|x|^{b}}dx,\,\ u\in\mathcal{H},

(1.3)

and

\mathcal{M}:=\big\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}dx=1\big\}.

(1.4)

Here the space
\mathcal{H}
is defined as

\mathcal{H}:=\Big\{u\in H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}):\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx<\infty\Big\}

(1.5)

with the associated norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}}=\Big\{\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+[1+V(x)]|u(x)|^{2}\Big)dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{2}}
. To discuss equivalently ground states of (
1.1
), throughout the whole paper we shall therefore focus on investigating (
1.2
), instead of (
1.1
).

In the sequel, we always assume that
V(x)
satisfies

(
V_{1})
.

0\leq V(x)\in{L^{\infty}_{loc}}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)=\infty
.

When

, (
1.2
) is a homogeneous constraint variational problem, for which there are many existing results of (
1.2
) (cf.
[
5
,
9
,
16
,
17
,
18
,
25
]
). To be more precise, when

, (
1.2
) is the so-called

-supercritical problem. One can take a suitable test function to derive that

, and thus (
1.2
) admits no minimizers for any

under this case. However, if

, (
1.2
) is in the
L^{2}
-subcritical case, and we can prove (
1.2
) always

by using energy estimates and the compactness embedding in Lemma

below, see also
[
20
]
. As for the
L^{2}
-critical case where
\beta^{2}=\frac{2}{N}
, the existence and nonexistence of minimizers for (
1.2
) and various quantitative properties of (

[
15
,
16
,
17
,
18

When

, (
1.2
) is the inhomogeneous constraint variational problem which contains the nonlinear term
K(x)|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}
with
K(x)=|x|^{-b}
. Note that similar inhomogeneous problems were analyzed recently in
[
6
,
7
]
, which however focused mainly on the case where

does not have any singularity. On the other hand,
[
12
]
studied the existence, instability of standing waves of (
1.1
) in the

-critical case
\beta^{2}=\frac{2-b}{N}
and

. Inspired by the above works, in this paper we shall mainly studied minimizers (ground states) of (
1.2
) in the critical case
\beta^{2}=\frac{2-b}{N}
, where
0<b<\min\{2,N\}
. For a general class of

satisfying the assumptions
(V_{1})
, we shall give the existence and nonexistence, blow up behavior, and local uniqueness of minimizers for (

We first introduce the following associated limit equation

-\triangle u+u-|x|^{-b}|u|^{2\beta^{2}}u=0\ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

(1.6)

Note that (
1.6
) admits a unique positive radial solution, see the proof in
[

for

,
[

]

\frac{a^{*}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\leq\|\nabla u(x)\|^{2}_{2}\|u(x)\|^{2\beta^{2}}_{2}\ \mbox{in}\ \ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),

(1.7)

where the constant

(1.8)

and
``="
holds in (

) if and only if

(

Recall also from

that

admits the following exponential decay,

|Q(x)|,\quad|\nabla Q(x)|\leq Ce^{-|x|}\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty.

Moreover, one can derive from (

) and (

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla Q|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|Q|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|Q|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx.

By applying the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

,

2.

If

).

Moreover,

for

, and

for

.

In view of Theorem

, a natural question arises whether there exist minimizers for

when

.
In contrast to the homogeneous
b=0

satisfying

.

. Using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, it is easy to obtain that

. On other hand, taking the same test function as in (

) below
and letting

, one can obtain that

. Therefore, we conclude from above that

and thus our second result can be summarized as follows:

Theorem 1.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
and assume
a=a^{*}
, then we have

1.

If
\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)\leq e(a^{*})<V(0)

.

2.

If
\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)=e(a^{*})=V(0)

\lim_{a\nearrow a^{\ast}}e(a)=e(a^{\ast})=V(0)

Theorem

and

In view of Theorem

. Therefore, we next focus on the limit behavior of minimizers

To state our results, we need some additional assumptions of

, for which we define

The function

Following the above definition and the assumption

Combined the assumption

2
Existence of minimizers

This section is devoted to prove Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 with regard to the existence and nonexistence of minimizers for the problem (
1.2
). Motivated by
[
16
]
, we first introduce the following compactness lemma
[
2
, Theorem 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume
V(x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}V(x)=\infty
, then the embedding
\mathcal{H}\hookrightarrow L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is compact for
2\leq q<2^{*}
, where
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
if
N\geq 3
and
2^{*}=+\infty
if
N=1,2
.

This compactness property, together with the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
), allows us to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

(1). Firstly, we prove that
e(a)
has at least one minimizer for all
0\leq a<a^{\ast}=\|Q\|^{2\beta^{2}}_{2}
.
Indeed, for any fixed
0\leq a<a^{\ast}
and
u\in\mathcal{M}
, we derive from the assumption
(V_{1})
and Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
) that

\begin{split}E_{a}(u)&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(|\nabla u(x)|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2})dx-\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}dx\\
&\geq(1-\frac{a}{a^{*}})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx\\
&\geq(1-\frac{a}{a^{*}})\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx\geq 0.\end{split}

(2.1)

This implies that
e(a)\geq 0
for
0<a<a^{*}
.

Then we can choose a minimizing sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset\mathcal{H}
satisfying
\|u_{n}\|_{2}=1
and
\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{a}(u_{n})=e(a)
. Due to (
2.1
), we obtain that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{2}dx
and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u_{n}|^{2}dx
are bounded uniformly for
n
. Following Lemma 2.1, we may assume passing if necessary to a subsequence,

u_{n}\rightharpoonup u\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ \mathcal{H},\,\ u_{n}\rightarrow u\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ 2\leq q<2^{*}

for some
u\in\mathcal{H}
.
Thus we conclude that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|u(x)|^{2}dx=1
.
For any
s\in(\frac{N}{N-b},\infty)
, note that any

f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f|x|^{-b}dx

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}\big\||x|^{-b}\big\|_{L^{\bar{s}}(B_{R}(0))}+\frac{1}{R^{b}}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}

(2.2)

\displaystyle\leq C(R)\|f\|_{L^{s}(B_{R}(0))}+\frac{1}{R^{b}}\|f\|_{L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})},

where
\bar{s}=\frac{s}{s-1}
satisfies
1<\bar{s}<\frac{N}{b}
. Therefore, taking
f_{n}=|u_{n}(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}-|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}
, we have
f_{n}\to 0
in
\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for
s\in(\frac{N}{N-b},\frac{N^{2}}{(N-2)(N+2-b)})
if

,
s\in(\frac{N}{N-b},\infty)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{n}(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx.

(2.3)

Consequently, by weak lower semicontinuity, we deduce that
e(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}E_{a}(u_{n})\geq E_{a}(u)\geq e(a)
. Therefore,
u
must be a minimizer of
e(a)
, thus Theorem
1.1
(1) is proved and
e(a)=E_{a}(u)>0
.

(2). We next show that there is no minimizer for
e(a)
as
a>a^{\ast}
.
Let
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
be a nonnegative cut-off function satisfying
\varphi(x)=1
if

, and
\varphi(x)=0
if
|x|>2
. Set for all

(2.4)

where

is chosen so that

. By scaling, we deduce from the exponential decay of

in (

\frac{1}{A^{2}_{\tau}}=\frac{1}{\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}Q^{2}(x)\varphi^{2}(\frac{x}{\tau})=1+o\big((\tau)^{-\infty}\big)\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ \tau\rightarrow\infty.

(2.5)

Here we use the notation
f(t)=o(t^{-\infty})
for a function

satisfying
\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow\infty}|f(t)|t^{s}=0
for all

Following (

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{\tau}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}u_{\tau}^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\\

(2.6)

On the other hand, since the function

(2.7)

Hence, we have

\begin{split}e(a)&\leq E_{a}(u_{\tau}(x))\\

Therefore, if

\{u_{n}\}
, still denoted by
\{u_{n}\}
, such that
\|u_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Moreover, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u_{n}(x)|^{2}dx\leq E_{a^{\ast}}(u_{n})\leq e(a^{\ast})+1.

(2.9)

Since
\{u_{n}\}
is unbounded in
\mathcal{H}
, then we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{2}dx\rightarrow\infty.

(2.10)

Let

\varepsilon^{-2}_{n}:=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{n}(x)|^{2}dx,

(2.11)

then by (
2.10
), we have that
\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
. Now we defined the
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})-
normalized function

w_{n}(x):=\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}_{n}u_{n}(\varepsilon_{n}x),

(2.12)

which satisfies

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{n}(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|w_{n}(x)|^{2}dx=1.

(2.13)

Therefore
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for
n
, and Sobolev embedding theorem implies that passing if necessary to a subsequence

w_{n}\rightharpoonup w_{0}\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ w_{n}\rightarrow w_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L_{loc}^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ 2\leq q<2^{*}.

Next, we claim that

\lim_{n\to\infty}w_{n}=\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}\quad\hbox{strongly in $H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})$}.

(2.14)

Actually, by the definition of
w_{n}
, we obtain from Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
) that

\begin{split}C&\geq e(a^{*})=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}E_{a^{*}}(u_{n})\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big(|\nabla u_{n}|^{2}-\frac{a^{*}}{1+\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}u_{n}^{2+2\beta^{2}}+V(x)|u_{n}(x)|^{2}\Big)dx\Big\}\\
&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\Big\{\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}_{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big[|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}-\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}w_{n}^{2+2\beta^{2}}\Big]dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(\varepsilon_{n}x)|w_{n}(x)|^{2}dx\Big\}\\
&\geq 0.\end{split}

(2.15)

Therefore, we derive from (
2.13
), the fact
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and above that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{n}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{n}(x)|^{2}dx=1.

(2.16)

On the other hand, similar to the proof of (
2.3
), we have

\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{0}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{n}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx=1,

(2.17)

which implies
w_{0}\not\equiv 0
. Moreover, by the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.7
) and weak lower semicontinuity, we have

\begin{split}0&=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\varepsilon_{n}^{2}e(a^{*})\\
&\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\Big[|\nabla w_{n}|^{2}-\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}w_{n}^{2+2\beta^{2}}\Big]dx\\
&\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{0}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{0}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx\\
&\geq\Big[1-\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}w^{2}_{0}(x)dx\Big)^{\beta^{2}}\Big]\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{0}(x)|^{2}dx.\end{split}

(2.18)

Since
\|w_{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}\leq 1
, the above inequality further implies that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|w_{0}(x)|^{2}dx=1,\,\ \int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{0}(x)|^{2}dx=\frac{2a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{0}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx=1.

(2.19)

Combing the above facts, we obtain that Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality is achieved by
w_{0}
and hence
w_{0}=\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}
in view of (
2.19
) and (
1.10
). By the norm preservation, we finally conclude that
w_{n}
converges to
w_{0}=\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}
strongly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
and (
2.14
) is thus proved.

Based on (
2.14
), by Fatou’s lemma, we obtain

e(a^{\ast})\geq\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(\varepsilon_{n}x)|w_{n}(x)|^{2}dx\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(0)|w_{0}(x)|^{2}dx=V(0),

(2.20)

which contradicts the assumption
e(a^{\ast})<V(0)
. Hence the minimizing sequence
\{u_{n}\}
of
e(a^{*})
is bounded uniformly in
\mathcal{H}
and Theorem
1.2
(1) is proved.

(2). We next consider the case
a=a^{\ast}
and
V(0)=0
. Note that
e(a^{*})\geq 0
. On the other hand, we can use the same test function as that of (
2.4
) to derive that
e(a^{\ast})\leq V(0)=0
, and thus
e(a^{\ast})=0
. Now suppose that there exists a minimizer
u\in\mathcal{M}
at
a=a^{\ast}
. Then we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u(x)|^{2}dx=0=\inf\limits_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x),

(2.21)

as well as

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u(x)|^{2}dx&=\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx.\end{split}

(2.22)

This is a contradiction, since from the first equality
u
must have compact support, while from the last equality it must equal to
\frac{n^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(nx)}{\|Q\|_{2}}>0
, where
n\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a constant. This gives the nonexistence of minimizers for
e(a^{*})
at
a=a^{*}
. Moreover, taking the same test function as in (
2.4
), we have
V(0)=\inf_{x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)\leq\lim_{a\nearrow a^{\ast}}e(a)\leq V(0)
and hence
\lim_{a\nearrow a^{\ast}}e(a)=V(0)=e(a^{*})
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.∎

3
Mass concentration as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.3
, which focuses on the concentration behavior of positive minimizers of
e(a)
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
. Let
u_{a}
be a positive minimizer of
e(a)
, by variational theory,
u_{a}
satisfies the following Euler-Lagrange equation

-\Delta u_{a}(x)+V(x)u_{a}(x)=\mu_{a}u_{a}(x)+au_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}(x)|x|^{-b}\quad\mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(3.1)

where
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\mu_{a}=e(a)-\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx.

(3.2)

We first establish the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, and let
u_{a}
be a positive minimizer of
e(a)
. Define

\varepsilon_{a}:=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{a}|^{2}dx\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(3.3)

and

w_{a}(x)=\varepsilon^{\frac{N}{2}}_{a}u_{a}(\varepsilon_{a}x),

(3.4)

then we have

1.

\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
.

2.

w_{a}\rightarrow\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}
strongly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
.

3.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
independent of
a\in(a,a^{*})
such that

|w_{a}(x)|,\,\ |\nabla w_{a}(x)|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\beta}{4}|x|}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

(3.5)

Proof.
1. On the contrary, suppose that there exists a sequence
\{a_{k}\}
satisfies
a_{k}\nearrow a^{\ast}
as
k\rightarrow\infty
such that the sequence
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. By Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, we obtain that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u_{n}|^{2}dx\leq e(a)\leq C
uniformly for
n
and any
a\in(0,a^{*})
and hence
\{u_{a_{k}}\}
is also bounded uniformly in
\mathcal{H}
.
Following Lemma
2.1
, there exists a subsequence, still denoted by
\{a_{k}\}
, of
\{a_{k}\}
and
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

{u_{a_{k}}}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ \mathcal{H}\,\ \hbox{and}\,\ {u_{a_{k}}}\rightarrow u_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,(2\leq q<2^{*}).

Combining the above convergence with (
2.2
) gives that

0=e(a^{\ast})\leq E_{a^{\ast}}(u_{0})\leq\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}E_{a_{k}}(u_{a_{k}})=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}e(a_{k})=0,

and thus
u_{0}
is a minimizer of
e(a^{\ast})
, which however contradicts with Theorem
1.2
(2). Therefore we concluded that
\varepsilon_{a}\rightarrow 0
as
a\nearrow a^{\ast}
.

2. Note from Theorem
1.2
(2) that
0=e(a^{*})=\lim_{a\nearrow a^{*}}e(a)
, thus
\{u_{a}\}
is also a minimizing sequence of
e(a^{*})
. Moreover, we obtain from (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla w_{a}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|w_{a}|^{2}dx=1\,\ \hbox{for any $a\nearrow a^{*}$.}

Hence
\{w_{a}\}
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
for
a
, and passing if necessary to a subsequence

w_{a}\rightharpoonup w_{0}\,\ \hbox{weakly in}\,\ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ w_{a}\rightarrow w_{0}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L_{loc}^{q}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}),\,\ 2\leq q<2^{*}.

Note that the proof of the claim
(
2.14
)
do not rely on the assumption
V(0)>0
and
e(a)<V(0)
. Thus, the same argument of proving (
2.14
) yields that

w_{a}\rightarrow\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.6)

Next we prove that

w_{a}(x)\rightarrow\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.7)

For
N=1
, this is trivial since the embedding
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
is continuous.

We next focus on the case
N\geq 2
.
We claim that

w_{a}(x)\rightarrow 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ |x|\rightarrow\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.8)

In fact, we derive from (
3.7
) that for
2\leq q<2^{*}

\int_{|x|\geq\gamma}|w_{a}(x)|^{q}\rightarrow 0\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ \gamma\rightarrow\infty\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \ \mbox{for}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.9)

On the other hand, recall from (
3.1
) and (
3.4
) that
w_{a}
satisfies the following equation

-\Delta w_{a}+\varepsilon_{a}^{2}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)w_{a}=\mu_{a}\varepsilon_{a}^{2}w_{a}+aw_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}\quad\hbox{in}\,\ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},

(3.10)

where
\mu_{a}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier satisfying (
3.2
).
In view of (
2.2
) and (
3.2
), we derive from the convergence in (
3.7
) that

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{a}^{2}\mu_{a}&=\varepsilon_{a}^{2}e(a)-\varepsilon_{a}^{2}\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\\
&=\varepsilon_{a}^{2}e(a)-\frac{a\beta^{2}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|w_{a}|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\to-\beta^{2}\,\ \hbox{as}\,\ a\nearrow a^{*}.\end{split}

(3.11)

Hence for
a\nearrow a^{*}
, we have

-\Delta w_{a}-c(x)w_{a}(x)\leq 0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N},\ \ \mbox{where}\ \ c(x)=w^{2\beta^{2}}_{a}|x|^{-b}.

(3.12)

Note from Lemma
A.2
in the Appendix that
c(x)\in L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
where
m\in\Big(\frac{N}{2},\frac{N^{2}}{2N+2b-4}\Big)
if
N\geq 3
and
m\in(1,\frac{2}{b})
if
N=2
.
Applying De-Giorgi-Nash-Moser theory
[
19
, Theorem 4.1]
to (
3.12
), we conclude that

\max\limits_{B_{1}(\rho)}w_{a}(x)\leq C\Big(\int_{{B_{2}(\rho)}}|w_{a}(x)|^{q}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.

(3.13)

where
\rho\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
is arbitrary, and
C>0
depends only on the bound of
\|c(x)\|_{L^{m}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})}
. Therefore, the claim (
3.8
) is proved in view of (
3.9
) and (
3.13
).

Based on (
3.8
), to finish the proof of (
3.7
), the rest is to show that

w_{a}\to\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}\,\ \hbox{strongly in}\,\ L^{\infty}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

(3.14)

Since
w_{a}(x)
satisfies (
3.10
), we denote

G_{a}(x):=\varepsilon_{a}\mu_{a}w_{a}(x)-\varepsilon_{a}^{2}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)w_{a}(x)+w^{1+2\beta^{2}}_{a}|x|^{-b},

so that

-\Delta w_{a}(x)=G_{a}(x)\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

(3.15)

Because
w_{a}(x)
is bounded uniformly in
H^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, follow (
3.13
), we can obtain that

w_{a}\ \ \mbox{is bounded}\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

(3.16)

Therefore, for any
r\in(1,\frac{N}{b})
,
w_{a}^{1+2\beta^{2}}|x|^{-b}
is bounded uniformly in
L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, which implies that
G_{a}(x)
is bounded uniformly in
L^{r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. For any large
R>0
, it then follows from
[
14
, Theorem 9.11]
that

\|w_{a}(x)\|_{W^{2,r}(B_{R})}\leq C\Big(\|w_{a}(x)\|_{L^{r}(B_{R+1})}+\|G_{a}(x)\|_{L^{r}(B_{R+1})}\Big),

(3.17)

where
C>0
is independent of
a>0
and
R>0
. Therefore,
w_{a}(x)
is also uniformly in
W^{2,r}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Since for
r>\frac{N}{2}
, the embedding
W^{2,r}(B_{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(B_{R})
is compact, see
[
14
, Theorem 7.26]
, we then deduce that there exists a subsequence
\{w_{a_{k}}\}
of
\{w_{a}(x)\}
such that

\lim\limits_{a_{k}\nearrow a^{*}}w_{a_{k}}(x)=\tilde{w}_{0}(x)\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L^{\infty}(B_{R}).

In view of (
3.6
) and the fact that
R>0
is arbitrary, we obtain that

\lim\limits_{a_{k}\nearrow a^{*}}w_{a_{k}}(x)=\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}\ \ \mbox{uniformly}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ L_{loc}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N}).

(3.18)

Since the above convergence is independent of what subsequence we choose, we deduce that
w_{a}(x)\rightarrow\frac{\beta^{\frac{N}{2}}Q(\beta x)}{\|Q\|_{2}}
in
L_{loc}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
as
a\nearrow a^{*}
and (
3.7
) is proved in view of (
3.8
).

(3). Following (
3.8
), (
3.10
) and (
3.11
), we obtain that there exists
R>0
large enough such that as
a\nearrow a^{*}

-\Delta w_{a}(x)+\frac{\beta^{2}}{4}w_{a}(x)\leq 0\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0).

(3.19)

Then by the comparison principle, we then have as
a\nearrow a^{*}
,

w_{a}(x)\leq Ce^{-\frac{\beta|x|}{2}}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}\backslash B_{R}(0).

(3.20)

Combing the above estimate and the fact (
3.16
), we conclude that

w_{a}(x)\leq Ce^{-\frac{\beta|x|}{2}}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

(3.21)

Moreover, applying the local elliptic estimate
[
14
, (3.15)]
to (
3.15
) yields that as
a\nearrow a^{*}
,

|\nabla w_{a}(x)|\leq Ce^{-\frac{\beta|x|}{4}}\ \ \mbox{in}\ \ {\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

(3.22)

Therefore, the exponential decay (
3.5
) is then proved. ∎

Completion the proof of Theorem 1.3.
In view of Lemma
3.1
, to establish Theorem
1.3
, it remains to prove that

\begin{split}\varepsilon_{a}&:=\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{a}|^{2}\Big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\\
&=\frac{(a^{\ast}-a)^{\frac{1}{l+2}}}{\lambda}+o\big((a^{*}-a)^{\frac{1}{l+2}}\big)\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*},\end{split}

(3.23)

where
\lambda>0
is as in Theorem
1.3
.

In order to prove (
3.23
), we take the following test function

u_{\alpha}(x):=\frac{\alpha^{\frac{N}{2}}}{\|Q\|_{2}}Q(\alpha x),

where
\alpha\in(0,\infty)
is to be determined later. Direct calculations give that as
\alpha\to\infty

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{\alpha}(x)|^{2}dx=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla Q|^{2}dx=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}},

\begin{split}&\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|u_{\alpha}(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx\\
=&\frac{a\alpha^{2}}{(1+\beta^{2})\|Q\|_{2}^{2+2\beta^{2}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}|Q(x)|^{2+2\beta^{2}}dx=\frac{a}{a^{*}}\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}},\end{split}

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}_{\alpha}(x)dx=\frac{1}{\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V\Big(\frac{x}{\alpha}\Big)Q^{2}(x)dx=\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)Q^{2}(x)dx+o(\alpha^{-l}).

Thus we have

\begin{split}e(a)\leq E_{a}(u_{\alpha})&=\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{a^{\ast}}+\frac{1}{\alpha^{l}\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)Q^{2}(x)dx+o(\alpha^{-l})\\
&:=g(\alpha)+o(\alpha^{-l}).\end{split}

(3.24)

Therefore, we have

\begin{split}e(a)&\leq\min_{\alpha\in(0,+\infty)}g(\alpha)+o(\alpha^{-l})=g\Big(\Big(\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)Q^{2}(x)dx\beta^{2}la^{*}}{2\|Q\|_{2}^{2}(a^{*}-a)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{l+2}}\Big)+o((a^{*}-a)^{\frac{l}{l+2}})\\
&=\Big(\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)Q^{2}(x)dx}{\|Q\|^{2}_{2}\beta^{l}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{l+2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{l}{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{l+2}}+\Big(\frac{2}{l}\Big)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}\Big]\Big(\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{a^{\ast}}\Big)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}+o((a^{*}-a)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}).\end{split}

(3.25)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}e(a)&=E_{a}(u_{a})\\
&=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla u_{a}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{a}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}u_{a}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(x)|u_{a}(x)|^{2}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}_{a}}\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|\nabla w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx-\frac{a^{\ast}}{1+\beta^{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{a}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx\Big)\\
&+\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{(1+\beta^{2})\varepsilon^{2}_{a}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{a}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)|w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx.\end{split}

(3.26)

Under the assumption
(V_{2})
, we deduce from Lemma
3.1
(2) and (3) that

\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)|w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx\\
&=\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{a}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}(0)}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)|w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}/{B_{\varepsilon_{a}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}(0)}}V(\varepsilon_{a}x)|w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx\\
&=\varepsilon^{l}_{a}\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{a}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}(0)}h(x)|w_{a}(x)|^{2}dx+o(\varepsilon^{l}_{a})\\
&=\frac{[1+o(1)]\varepsilon^{l}_{a}}{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}\beta^{l}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)|Q(x)|^{2}dx+o(\varepsilon^{l}_{a})\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.\end{split}

(3.27)

Similarly, we deduce from (
3.11
) that

\begin{split}&\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{(1+\beta^{2})\varepsilon^{2}_{a}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}|x|^{-b}w_{a}^{2+2\beta^{2}}(x)dx=[1+o(1)]\frac{(a^{*}-a)}{a^{*}\varepsilon^{2}_{a}}\ \ \mbox{as}\ \ a\nearrow a^{*}.\end{split}

(3.28)

Therefore, we obtain that as
a\nearrow a^{*}

\begin{split}e(a)&\geq\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{a^{\ast}\varepsilon^{2}_{a}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{l}_{a}}{\beta^{l}\|Q\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)|Q(x)|^{2}dx+o(\varepsilon^{l}_{a}+\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{\varepsilon^{2}_{a}})\\
&=g(\beta\varepsilon_{a}^{-1})+o(\varepsilon^{l}_{a}+\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{\varepsilon^{2}_{a}})\\
&\geq\min_{t\in(0,+\infty)}g(t)+o((a^{*}-a)^{\frac{l}{l+2}})\\
&=\Big(\frac{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}h(x)Q^{2}(x)dx}{\|Q\|^{2}_{2}\beta^{l}}\Big)^{\frac{2}{l+2}}\Big[\Big(\frac{l}{2}\Big)^{\frac{2}{l+2}}+\Big(\frac{2}{l}\Big)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}\Big]\Big(\frac{a^{\ast}-a}{a^{\ast}}\Big)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}+o((a^{*}-a)^{\frac{l}{l+2}}).\end{split}

(3.29)

Note from the upper bound estimates of energy in (
3.25
) that the above inequalities must be achieved and hence
g(\beta\varepsilon_{a}^{-1})=\min_{t\in(0,+\infty)}g(t)
. This further implies that

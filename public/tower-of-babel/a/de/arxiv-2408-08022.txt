Sharp Quartic Pinching for the Mean Curvature Flow in the Sphere
Artemis A. Vogiatzi

Abstract
We prove a sharp quartic curvature pinching for the mean curvature flow in 𝕊 n + m \mathbb{S}^{n+m} , m ≥ 2 m\geq 2 , which generalises Pu’s work on the convergence of submanifolds in 𝕊 n + m \mathbb{S}^{n+m} to a round point. Using a blow up argument, we prove a codimension and a cylindrical estimate, where in regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately codimension one, quantitatively, and is weakly convex and moves by translation or is a self shrinker. With a decay estimate, the rescaling converges smoothly to a totally geodesic limit in infinite time, without using Stampacchia iteration or integral analysis.



1
Introduction

Let
F_{0}\colon\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m}
,
m\geq 2
be a smooth immersion of a compact manifold
\mathcal{M}^{n}
. The mean curvature flow starting from
F_{0}
is the following family of submanifolds
F\colon\mathcal{M}^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}^{n+m},
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}\partial_{t}F(p,t)&=H(p,t),\ \ \text{for}\ \ p\in\mathcal{M},t\in[0,T)\\
F(p,0)&=F_{0}(p),\end{array}\right.\end{split}

where
H(p,t)
denotes the mean curvature vector of
\mathcal{M}_{t}=F_{t}(p)=F(p,t)
at
p
. This is a system of quasilinear weakly parabolic partial differential equations for
F
. Geometrically, the mean curvature flow is the steepest descent flow for the area functional of a submanifold and hence it is a natural curvature flow.

In the case of codimension one, a crucial step in the study of singularity formation in the mean convex mean curvature flow is the convexity estimate. This states that in regions of large mean curvature, the second fundamental form is almost positive definite.

In
[
8
]
, Huisken proved that closed convex hypersurfaces under the mean curvature flow evolve into spherical singularities, using Stampacchia iteration, the Michael–Simons–Sobolev inequality together with recursion formulae for symmetric polynomials. In
[
9
]
, Huisken generalises this theorem to Riemannian background curvature spaces with strict convexity depending on the background curvature.

In contrast, White
[
28
,
29
]
uses compactness theorems of geometric measure theory together with the rigidity of strong maximum principle for the second fundamental form. Haslhofer-Kleiner
[
7
]
developed an alternative approach to White’s results based on Andrews’s non-collapsing
[
1
]
result for the mean curvature flow.

The analysis of high codimension mean curvature flow is far more challenging, than in the hypersurface case, since convexity does not make sense anymore. An alternative condition to convexity was introduced by Andrews–Baker in
[
2
]
: on a compact submanifold, if
|H|>0
, there exists a
c>0
, such that

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq c|H|^{2},

which is preserved by codimension one mean curvature flow. Also, this condition makes sense for all codimensions. For
c=\min\{\frac{4}{3n},\frac{1}{n-1}\}
, they were able to prove convergence to a round sphere. Note that
|A|^{2}-\frac{1}{n-1}|H|^{2}<0,H>0
implies convexity in codimension one.

In
[
10
]
, Huisken proved a convergence theorem for the mean curvature flow of quadratically pinched hypersurfaces in the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
. In
[
4
]
, Baker and Nguyen generalised that result and proved a sharp convergence theorem for high codimension mean curvature flow as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}=F_{0}\left(\mathcal{M}^{n}\right)
be a closed submanifold smoothly immersed in
\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
, with constant curvature
\bar{K}
. If
\mathcal{M}_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|A|^{2}\leq\frac{4}{3n}|H|^{2}+\frac{n}{2}\bar{K},\quad n=2,3\\
|A|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n-1}|H|^{2}+2\bar{K},\quad n\geq 4,\end{array}\right.

then either the mean curvature flow has a unique, smooth solution on a finite time interval and the submanifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
contracts to a point, as
t\to T
or it exists for all time and the submanifold
\mathcal{M}_{t}
converges to a totally geodesic submanifold of
\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
, as
t\to\infty
.

If the relationship of
|A|^{2}
and
|H|^{2}
is nonlinear, these pinching conditions could be improved. In
[
16
]
, Lei and Xu obtained a convergence theorem for the mean curvature flow of arbitrary codimension in spheres as follows.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
F_{0}:\mathcal{M}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
be an
n
-dimensional, smooth, compact submanifold with constant curvature
\bar{K}
,
n\geq 6
. Assume
\mathcal{M}_{0}
satisfies

\displaystyle|A|^{2}<\gamma(n,|H|,\bar{K}),

then the mean curvature flow with the initial value
F_{0}
either converges to a round point in finite time, or converges to a total geodesic sphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
.
Here
\gamma(n,|H|,\bar{K})
is an explicit positive scalar defined by

\displaystyle\gamma(n,|H|,\bar{K})=\min\left\{\alpha\left(|H|^{2}\right),\beta\left(|H|^{2}\right)\right\},

where

\displaystyle\alpha(x)

\displaystyle=n\bar{K}+\frac{n}{2(n-1)}x-\frac{n-2}{2(n-1)}\sqrt{x^{2}+4(n-1)\bar{K}x},

\displaystyle\beta(x)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left(x_{0}\right)+\alpha^{\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\alpha^{\prime\prime}\left(x_{0}\right)\left(x-x_{0}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle x_{0}

\displaystyle=\frac{2n+2}{n-4}\sqrt{n-1}\left(\sqrt{n-1}-\frac{n-4}{2n+2}\right)^{2}\bar{K},

Lei and Xu’s convergence theorems imply that the Ricci curvatures of the initial submanifolds are positive, without impling the positivity of the sectional curvatures. In
[
26
]
, Pu proved a convergence theorem that improved the theorem of Baker and Nguyen, using a quartic pinching condition and the condition introduced by Lei and Xu in
[
16
]
. By setting

\displaystyle b(x)=(1-\delta)\left(\frac{x}{n-1}+2\bar{K}\right)+\delta\alpha(x),\ x\geq 0,

where
\delta=\frac{\sqrt{12n+9}-7}{2(n-2)},n=4,5,6
, he proved the following theorem.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
F_{0}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
be an n-dimensional, smooth compact submanifold immersed in the sphere,
n\geq 4
,
m\geq 2
. If
\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq\begin{cases}b\left(|H|^{2}\right),&n=4,5,6,\\
\sqrt{\left(\frac{|H|^{2}}{n-1}+2\bar{K}\right)^{2}+(2n-4)\bar{K}^{2}},&n\geq 7,\end{cases}

then the mean curvature flow with the initial value
F_{0}
converges to a round point in finite time or converges to a totally geodesic sphere of
\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
, as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

The above pinching condition for
n\geq 7
is shown to be sharp.
The purpose of this paper is to obtain an extension of Pu’s result, using a suitable quartic pinching condition on the submanifold. More specifically, we prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
F_{0}:\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
be an n-dimensional, smooth compact submanifold,
n\geq 8
,
m\geq 2
, with constant curvature
\bar{K}
. If
\mathcal{M}_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle|A|^{2}\leq\sqrt{\left(\frac{|H|^{2}}{n-2}+4\bar{K}\right)^{2}+(4n-16)\bar{K}^{2}},

(1.1)

for all
0\leq t<T
, where
T
is the maximal time of existence, then one of the following holds. If
T<\infty
, then
\mathcal{M}_{t}
contracts to a codimension one limiting flow as
t\to T
or if
T=\infty
, then
\mathcal{M}_{t}
converges to a smooth totally geodesic submanifold as
t\to T
.

Condition (
1.1
) is called the quartic pinching condition for
\mathcal{M}
. The quartic pinching condition is sharp, since
|A|^{4}-\left(\frac{|H|^{2}}{n-2}+4\right)^{2}-(4n-16)=\frac{4((n-2)^{2}-1)}{(n-2)^{2}}\cdot\frac{s^{4}}{r^{4}}
, by considering the submanifold
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^{2}(r)\times\mathbb{S}^{n-2}(s)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n+1}(1)\subset\mathbb{S}^{n+m}(1)
, with
r^{2}+s^{2}=1
. Also, it satisfies
\sqrt{\left(\frac{|H|^{2}}{n-2}+4\bar{K}\right)^{2}+(4n-16)\bar{K}^{2}}>\frac{|H|^{2}}{n-2}+4\bar{K}
. The latter indicates that the term involving
\bar{K}

becomes much more influential when considered in the quartic pinching condition than in the quadratic pinching condition. This shows that
\bar{K}
has an improved proportional impact in the quartic pinching condition.

For the case of finite time, the statement is proven by a blow up argument using gradient estimates on the second fundamental form and a cylindrical type estimate. For the case of infinite time, the statement is proven directly using the maximum principle, without using Stampacchia iteration or integral analysis that is used in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
16
]
and
[
26
]
.

Nguyen in
[
23
]
developed a surgery construction allowing high codimension mean curvature flow with cylindrical pinching to pass through singularities. This generalised the codimension one result of
[
12
]
(see also
[
7
]
) to high codimension. A key aspect of this surgery procedure is the codimension estimate presented in
[
20
]
, which shows that near regions of high curvature, singularities become approximately codimension one. Another crucial component is the cylindrical estimate, which shows that nears regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately cylindrical of the form
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}
. These estimates are essential for the surgery to work and allow us to control the geometry of the submanifold in regions of high curvature.

This paper is part of a series of papers, aiming to extend Nguyen’s result on high codimension mean curvature flow with surgery in Euclidean space to Riemannian manifolds.

The outline of the paper is as follows. In section 2, we give all the technical tools needed for our work and set up our notation. In section 3, we give the proof for the preservation of the quartic pinching condition along the mean curvature flow. In section 4, we prove the gradient estimates. In section 5, we prove the codimension estimate, the cylindrical estimate and give a full classification for the singularity models of quartically pinched solutions in high codimension mean curvature flow. Finally, in section 6, we prove that the submanifold converges smoothly to a totally geodesic limit in infinite time.

3
Preservation of the quartic pinching condition

In this section, we show the preservation of the quartic pinching under the mean curvature flow. Denote

\displaystyle a(x):=\sqrt{\left(\frac{x}{n-2}+4\bar{K}\right)^{2}+(4n-16)\bar{K}^{2}},\quad\mathring{a}(x)=a(x)-\frac{x}{n},

4
Gradient estimates

This section presents a proof of the gradient estimate for the mean curvature flow. We establish this estimate directly from the quartic curvature bound
|A|^{2}<a-\varepsilon\omega
, without relying on the asymptotic cylindrical estimates. In fact, we demonstrate that the cylindrical estimates follow as a consequence of the gradient estimates we derive here.

The gradient estimates are pointwise estimates that rely solely on the mean curvature (or, equivalently, the second fundamental form) at a point and not on the maximum of curvature, as is the case with more general parabolic-type derivative estimates. The importance of these estimates is that they allow us to control the mean curvature and, hence, the full second fundamental form on a neighbourhood of fixed size.

Theorem 4.1

(cf.
[
12
]
, Section 6)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{t},t\in[0,T)
be a closed
n
-dimensional, quadratically bounded solution to the mean curvature flow in

, that is

\displaystyle|A|^{2}-a+\varepsilon\omega<0.

Then, there exists a constant
\gamma_{1}=\gamma_{1}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
and a constant
\gamma_{2}=\gamma_{2}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
, such that the flow satisfies the uniform estimate

\displaystyle|\nabla A|^{2}\leq\gamma_{1}|A|^{4}+\gamma_{2},

for every

Proof.

We consider here the evolution equation for
\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}},
where
g=a-|A|^{2}-\varepsilon\omega>0
. Since
|A|^{2}-a<0
and
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is compact, there exists an
\eta(\mathcal{M}_{0})>0,C_{\eta}(\mathcal{M}_{0})>0
, so that

\displaystyle a(1-\eta)-|A|^{2}\geq C_{\eta}>0.

Hence, we set
g=a-|A|^{2}\geq\eta a>\eta|A|^{2}>\varepsilon_{1}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2},
where
\varepsilon_{1}<\eta
and
\varepsilon_{2}>0
. From Proposition
3.3
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)g

\displaystyle=2\left(|\nabla A|^{2}-\left(\mathring{a}^{\prime}-\frac{\varepsilon}{n-2}+2|H|^{2}\mathring{a}^{\prime\prime}\right)|\nabla H|^{2}\right)-2R_{1}+\frac{2}{n}R_{2}+2n\bar{K}|A|^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left(\mathring{a}^{\prime}-\frac{\varepsilon}{n-2}\right)\left(R_{2}+n\bar{K}|H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(|\nabla A|^{2}-\left(\mathring{a}^{\prime}-\frac{\varepsilon}{n-2}+2|H|^{2}\mathring{a}^{\prime\prime}\right)|\nabla H|^{2}\right).

Using Lemma
2.3
and Lemma
3.1
(ii), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)g

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(|\nabla A|^{2}-\left(\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{n-2}\right)|\nabla H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(1-\frac{n+2}{3}\left(\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}-\frac{\varepsilon}{n-2}\right)\right)|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}|\nabla A|^{2},

where it remains positive for any
n
.
The evolution equation for
|\nabla A|^{2}
is given by

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|\nabla A|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+d|\nabla A|^{2}.

If
w,z
satisfy the evolution equations
\partial_{t}w=\Delta w+W
and
\partial_{t}z=\Delta z+Z
, then we find

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)\frac{w}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{z}\left\langle\nabla\left(\frac{w}{z}\right),\nabla z\right\rangle+\frac{W}{z}-\frac{w}{z^{2}}Z=2\frac{\langle\nabla w,\nabla z\rangle}{z^{2}}-2\frac{w|\nabla z|^{2}}{z^{3}}+\frac{W}{z}-\frac{w}{z^{2}}Z.

Furthermore, for any function
g
, we have by Kato’s inequality

\displaystyle\langle\nabla g,\nabla|\nabla A|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla g||\nabla^{2}A||\nabla A|\leq\frac{1}{g}|\nabla g|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+g|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}.

We then get

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{g}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right)\right\rangle\leq-\frac{2}{g}|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}-\frac{2}{g^{3}}|\nabla g|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+\frac{2}{g^{2}}\langle\nabla g,\nabla|\nabla A|^{2}\rangle\leq 0.

Then, if we let
w=|\nabla A|^{2}
and
z=g
, with
W\leq-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+d|\nabla A|^{2}
and
Z\geq\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}|\nabla A|^{2}
, we get

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{g}(-2|\nabla^{2}A|^{2}+c|A|^{2}|\nabla A|^{2}+d|\nabla A|^{2})

\displaystyle-\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}.

We repeat the above computation with
w=\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g},z=g,

\displaystyle W\leq c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}

and
Z\geq 0
, to get

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{g}\left\langle\nabla g,\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\right)\right\rangle+\frac{1}{g}\left(c|A|^{2}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}+d\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}-\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}\frac{|\nabla A|^{4}}{g^{2}}\right).

The nonlinearity then is

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\left(c|A|^{2}+d-\frac{2(n+2)}{3n}\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g}\right).

Since
g>\varepsilon_{1}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon_{2}
, there exists a constant

\displaystyle Ng\geq c|A|^{2}+d.

Hence, by the maximum principle, there exists a constant (with

chosen sufficiently small so that N is sufficiently large, this estimate holds at the initial time), such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\leq\frac{3nN}{2(n+2)}.

Therefore, we see there exists a constant
\mathcal{C}=\frac{3nN}{2(n+2)}=\mathcal{C}(n,\mathcal{M}_{0})
, such that

\displaystyle\frac{|\nabla A|^{2}}{g^{2}}\leq\mathcal{C}

and from the definition of

5
Convergence for finite time

In this section, we use a blow up argument to prove a codimension and a cylindrical estimate. In particular, we show that in regions of high curvature, the submanifold becomes approximately codimension one, quantitatively, and is weakly convex and moves by translation or is a self shrinker.

We need the evolution equation of the quantity
\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}
. Firstly, we separate the second fundamental form in the principal direction and the second fundamental form in the orthogonal direction and compute their evolution equations separately. Later, we find estimates for the reaction and gradient terms, as well as for the lower order terms. Since in the limit the background space is Euclidean, the result will follow from the maximum principle.

Lastly, we give a full classification for singularity models of quartically pinched solutions in high codimension mean curvature flow.

5.1
The evolution of
\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}

Here, we compute the evolution equation of the quantity
\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}

In order to prove the codimension estimate, we need good estimates for the reaction terms in this equation. These are proven following Andrews-Baker. From (
2.1
) and (

\displaystyle 2\sum_{p,q,\alpha,\beta}H^{\alpha}A_{ij}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\beta}A_{pq}^{\alpha}

Then

To prove (

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|\mathring{h}_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i,j,p,q}\mathring{h}_{ij}\mathring{h}_{pq}A^{\beta}_{ij}A^{\beta}_{pq}=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\left(\sum_{i,j}\mathring{h}_{ij}A^{\beta}_{ij}\right)^{2}=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{i}A^{\beta}_{ii}\right)^{2}.

By Cauchy-Schwarz,

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|\mathring{h}_{ij}A^{-}_{ij}|^{2}\leq\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\left(\sum_{i}\lambda_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i}(A^{\beta}_{ii})^{2}\right)=|\mathring{h}|^{2}\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i}(A^{\beta}_{ii})^{2}.

Using

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|R^{\bot}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}=\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+\sum_{i,j,k}|\mathring{h}_{ik}A^{-}_{jk}-\mathring{h}_{jk}A^{-}_{ik}|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,p}\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle

and (

) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|R_{ij}^{\perp}(\nu_{1})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i,j,k}\left(\mathring{h}_{ik}A^{\beta}_{jk}-\mathring{h}_{jk}A^{\beta}_{ik}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,p}\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i,j}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}(A^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}+\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,p}\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i\neq j}\left(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}\right)^{2}(A^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}+\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,p}\langle\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1}),\mathring{h}_{ip}A^{-}_{jp}-\mathring{h}_{jp}A^{-}_{ip}\rangle.

Since

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j}|R_{ij}^{\perp}(\nu_{1})|^{2}\leq 2|\mathring{h}|^{2}\sum_{\beta\geq 2}\sum_{i\neq j}(A^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}+\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})|^{2}+4|\bar{R}_{ij}(\nu_{1})||\mathring{h}||A^{-}|.

5.3
). For

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}-\frac{2(1-\delta)}{nc_{n}-1}\leq\frac{3}{2}-6(1-\delta)\leq 0\ \ \text{and}\ \ 2-\frac{nc_{n}(1-\delta)}{nc_{n}-1}\leq 2-4(1-\delta)\leq 0,

which gives (
5.4
).
∎

We will show that the rest of the gradient terms satisfy the following:

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\left\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}+(1-\delta)(n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

For the gradient terms, we denote
\tilde{c}_{n}=\frac{2(n-1)}{n(n+2)}
, where
\frac{1}{n}<\tilde{c}_{n}\leq\frac{3}{n+2}
.

Lemma 5.5

(Lower bound for Bochner term of
\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|A^{-}|^{2}
)
.

If
\frac{1}{n}<\tilde{c}_{n}\leq\frac{3}{n+2}
and
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n-2}
, then

\displaystyle 2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}-2\right)|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}(|A^{-}|^{2}+f-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

We begin by applying Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk}+\mathring{h}_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2\sum_{i,j,k}\langle\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk},\mathring{h}_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle+|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Multiplying both sides of (
5.2
) by
\frac{2(n-1)}{(n+2)(nc_{n}-1)}
, gives

\displaystyle\tilde{c}_{n}|H|^{2}

\displaystyle>\frac{2(n-1)}{(n+2)\left(nc_{n}-1\right)}\left(f+|A^{-}|^{2}+|\mathring{h}|^{2}-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}\right)

\displaystyle>\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}\left(f+|A^{-}|^{2}+|\mathring{h}|^{2}-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}\right).

In view of (
2.17
), our observations give us that

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}\left(f+|A^{-}|^{2}+|\mathring{h}|^{2}-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}\right)|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Subtracting the
|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}
term on the right-hand side gives

\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}(f+|A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}-2\right)|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2},

which gives the estimate of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 5.6

(Lower bound for Bochner term of
\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)f
)
.

If
\frac{1}{n}<\tilde{c}_{n}\leq\frac{3}{n+2}
, then

\displaystyle 2\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n+2}{n-1}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+(n-2)|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-(n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

Using (
2.10
) and (
2.11
), we have

\displaystyle|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla|H\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk}+h_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Note that by (
2.7
), (
2.8
) and (
2.16
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}A^{-}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle+\nabla_{i}h_{jk}|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}-\frac{(n-4)}{n(n+2)}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{n-4}{2(n-1)}\right)\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{2(n-1)}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}.

In view of (
2.15
) and (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j,k}|\hat{\nabla}_{i}^{\bot}A^{-}_{jk}+h_{jk}\nabla_{i}^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{3}{n+2}-\tilde{c}_{n}\right)|H|^{2}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{2}(f-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Thus, by the three previous computations, we have

\displaystyle 2\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n+2}{n-1}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+(n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}(f-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{n+2}{n-1}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+(n-2)|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+(2-n)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2},

which gives the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.7

(Upper bound for gradient term of
\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|A^{-}|^{2}
)
.

If
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n-2}
, then

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\left(2a_{2}+2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{2}}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

Using the definition of
Q_{ijk}
, we get

\displaystyle|Q|\leq|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|H|^{-1}|\mathring{h}||\nabla|H||.

(5.13)

It easily follows from the definition of
f

5.2
) that

\displaystyle f\leq\left(c_{n}-\frac{1}{n}\right)|H|^{2}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}<\frac{2}{n(n-2)}|H|^{2}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}.

For
|H|\to\infty
, we get
f<\frac{2}{n(n-2)}|H|^{2}.
Consequently, using (
2.16
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}|\nabla|H||^{2}

\displaystyle<\frac{n(n+2)}{2(n-1)}\frac{2}{n(n-2)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}.

(5.14)

Then,

\displaystyle|\langle A^{-},\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla^{\bot}_{i}\nu_{1}\rangle^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j,k}\sum_{\beta\geq 2}(A^{\beta}_{ij})^{2}\langle\nabla^{\bot}_{k}\nu_{1},\nu_{\beta}\rangle^{2}

and (

) gives

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 4|Q||\langle A^{-},\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|+|H|^{-1}|\mathring{h}||\nabla|H||\right)|A^{-}||\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|.

Now to each of these three summed terms above we apply Young’s inequality with constants

. Specifically, we have

\displaystyle 4|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle||A^{-}||\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|\leq 2a_{1}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{1}}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2},

\displaystyle 4|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle||A^{-}||\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|=4|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|\frac{|A^{-}|}{\sqrt{f}}f^{\frac{1}{2}}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|\leq 2a_{2}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{2}}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2},

\displaystyle 4|H|^{-1}|\mathring{h}||\nabla|H|||A^{-}||\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|\leq 2a_{3}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}\left|\nabla|H|\right|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\leq 2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Note we used (
5.14
) in the last inequality. Hence,

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2a_{1}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\left(2a_{2}+2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{a_{1}}|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{2}}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Setting
\alpha_{1}=1
and keeping
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
as they are for now, we get the desired result.
∎

Lemma 5.8

(Gradient term estimate)
.

If
\frac{1}{n}<\tilde{c}_{n}\leq\frac{3}{n+2}
,
\frac{1}{n}<c_{n}\leq\frac{1}{n-2}

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\left\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\right\rangle\leq 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}+(1-\delta)(n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Proof.

Expanding
|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}
using (
2.12
) and using the inequality of Lemma
5.5
, gives us

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle=2|\hat{\nabla}^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}\geq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}-2\right)|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}(|A^{-}|^{2}+f-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Multiplying the result in Lemma
5.6

(1-\delta)

\displaystyle 2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)\geq(1-\delta)\frac{n+2}{n-1}\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\sum_{i,j,k}|\langle\nabla_{i}^{\perp}\mathring{A}_{jk},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle 2|\nabla^{\perp}A^{-}|^{2}+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\left(|\nabla^{\perp}A|^{2}-\tilde{c}_{n}|\nabla^{\perp}H|^{2}\right)\geq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}-2\right)|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}(|A^{-}|^{2}+f-2\sqrt{n}\bar{K})|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}

\displaystyle-(1-\delta)(n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

On the other hand, the first result of Lemma
5.7
gives us that

\displaystyle 4\sum_{i,j,k}Q_{ijk}\langle A^{-}_{ij},\nabla_{k}^{\perp}\nu_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2|\langle\nabla^{\perp}A^{-},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}+\left(2a_{2}+2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}|\langle\nabla^{\perp}\mathring{A},\nu_{1}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle+2|A^{-}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{2}}f|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}+\frac{2}{a_{3}}|\mathring{h}|^{2}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}.

Therefore, it only remains to compare the coefficients of like terms in the two inequalities above. For the term

\displaystyle 2\leq\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}+2(1-\delta)(n-2)\Longleftrightarrow\delta\leq\frac{3n^{2}-5n-10}{2(n-2)(n+2)}.

(5.15)

For
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
, we need at least

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha_{3}}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}-2

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\alpha_{3}=\frac{2(n+2)}{(n-1)(n-2)-2(n+2)},

\displaystyle\frac{2}{\alpha_{2}}=\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\alpha_{2}=\frac{2(n+2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}.

Using these values for
\alpha_{2}
and
\alpha_{3}
, for the coefficients of

\displaystyle 2a_{2}+2a_{3}\frac{n+2}{(n-1)(n-2)}\leq(1-\delta)\frac{n+2}{n-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\frac{4(n+2)}{(n-1)(n-2)}+\frac{4(n+2)^{2}}{\left((n-1)(n-2)-2(n+2)\right)(n-1)(n-2)}\leq(1-\delta)\frac{n+2}{n-1}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\frac{4\left((n-1)(n-2)-2(n+2)\right)+4(n+2)}{(n-2)\left((n-1)(n-2)-2(n+2)\right)}\leq(1-\delta)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\delta\leq\frac{n^{3}-11n^{2}+24n+4}{n^{3}-7n^{2}+8n+4}.

Also, from (

\displaystyle\frac{n^{3}-11n^{2}+24n+4}{n^{3}-7n^{2}+8n+4}\leq\frac{3n^{2}-5n-10}{2(n-2)(n+2)}\Longleftrightarrow n\geq 6.

All in all, we need
n\geq 6

Let

be sufficiently small so that each of our above calculations hold. We begin by splitting off the desired nonpositive term in the evolution equation.

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}=\frac{1}{f}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|A^{-}|^{2}-|A^{-}|^{2}\frac{1}{f^{2}}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)f+2\left\langle\nabla\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f},\nabla\log f\right\rangle

Using the previous calculations and (

), we have the following estimate.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{f}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)|A^{-}|^{2}-(1-\delta)|A^{-}|^{2}\frac{1}{f^{2}}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)f

\displaystyle<\frac{2}{f}\left(1+(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}\right)\sum_{\alpha,\beta\geq 2}\left(\sum_{i,j}|\bar{R}_{ij\alpha\beta}|^{2}+4|\bar{R}_{ij\alpha\beta}||A^{-}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+2(1-\delta)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{nc_{n}-1}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}(|A^{-}|^{2}+2f)+\frac{nc_{n}}{nc_{n}-1}|\mathring{h}|^{2}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}\right)

\displaystyle+\frac{2}{f}\left(\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n+2}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\bot}\nu_{1}|^{2}\right)+(1-\delta)\frac{2}{f}\left((n-2)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}|\nabla^{\perp}\nu_{1}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle<C\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}+C^{\prime},

where

depend on the background curvature
\bar{R}
, the dimension of the submanifold
n
,
\bar{K}
and
c_{n}
.
Thus, according to our previous calculations, and using Young’s inequality, we get (
5.2
), which was our initial claim:

\displaystyle\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}

\displaystyle<2\left\langle\nabla\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f},\nabla\log f\right\rangle-\delta\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f^{2}}\left(\partial_{t}-\Delta\right)f+C\frac{|A^{-}|^{2}}{f}+C^{\prime}.

Theorem 5.9

(Codimension estimate, cf.
[
24
]
, Theorem 5.12)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{M}^{n}\times[0,T)\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{n+m}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{K}}}\right)
be a smooth solution to the mean curvature flow, so that
F_{0}(p)=F(p,0)
is compact and quartically pinched, where
n\geq 8,m\geq 2
.
Then,
\forall\varepsilon>0,\exists H_{0}>0
, such that if
f\geq H_{0}
, then

\displaystyle|A^{-}|^{2}\leq\varepsilon f+C_{\varepsilon},

\forall t\in[0,T)
, where
C_{\varepsilon}=C_{\varepsilon}(n,m)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}
is quartically bounded and from (
3.2
), there exist constants
C,D
, such that
|A^{-}|^{2}\leq Cf+D
. Therefore, the above estimate holds for all
\varepsilon\geq\frac{c_{n}}{\delta}
. Indeed, from the (
3.2
), we have
|A|^{2}\leq a<c_{n}|H|^{2}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}
and we can make a little bit more space so that

\displaystyle|A^{-}|^{2}+|A^{+}|^{2}=|A|^{2}\leq(c_{n}-\delta)|H|^{2}-C_{\delta}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}

and therefore,

\displaystyle\delta|H|^{2}\leq c_{n}|H|^{2}-|A|^{2}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}.

But since
|A^{-}|^{2}\leq|A|^{2}<c_{n}|H|^{2}+2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}
, we have
\frac{\delta|A^{-}|^{2}}{c_{n}}\leq\frac{\delta|A|^{2}}{c_{n}}<\delta|H|^{2}+\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}
, so from (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}|A^{-}|^{2}

\displaystyle<c_{n}|H|^{2}+\left(1+\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}\right)2\sqrt{n}\bar{K}-|A|^{2}

\displaystyle<-|A|^{2}+a+\left(\left(1+\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}\right)2\sqrt{n}-4\right)\bar{K}

\displaystyle=f+\left(\left(1+\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}\right)2\sqrt{n}-4\right)\bar{K},

which means that

\displaystyle|A^{-}|^{2}\leq\frac{c_{n}}{\delta}f+\frac{c_{n}}{\delta}\left(\left(1+\frac{\delta}{c_{n}}\right)2\sqrt{n}-4\right)\bar{K}\leq\varepsilon f+C_{\varepsilon}.

Hence, let
\varepsilon_{0}
denote the infimum of such
\varepsilon
for which the estimate is true and suppose
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. We will prove the theorem by contradiction. Hence, let us assume that the conclusions of the theorem are not true. That is, there exists a family of mean curvature flow
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{k}
with points
(p_{k},t_{k})
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|A_{k}^{-}(p_{k},t_{k})|^{2}}{f_{k}(p_{k},t_{k})}=\varepsilon_{0},

(5.17)

with
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and
f_{k}(p_{k},t_{k})\rightarrow\infty
.
We perform a parabolic rescaling of
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{k}
in such a way that
f_{k}
at
(p_{k},t_{k})
becomes
1
. If we consider the exponential map
\exp_{\bar{p}}\colon T_{\bar{p}}\mathbb{S}\cong\mathbb{R}^{n+m}\to\mathbb{S}^{n+m}
and
\gamma
a geodesic, then for a vector
v\in T_{\bar{p}}\mathbb{S}
, then

\displaystyle\exp_{\bar{p}}(v)=\gamma_{\bar{p},\frac{v}{|v|}}(|v|),\ \ \gamma^{\prime}(0)=\frac{v}{|v|}\ \ \text{ and}\ \ \gamma(0)=\bar{p}=F_{k}(p_{k},t_{k}).

That is, if
F_{k}
is the parameterisation of the original flow
\mathcal{M}_{t}^{k}
, we let
\hat{r}_{k}=\frac{1}{f_{k}(p_{k},t_{k})}
, and we denote the rescaled flow by
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{t}^{k}
and we define its parameterisation by

\displaystyle\overline{F}_{k}(p,\tau)=\exp^{-1}_{F_{k}(p_{k},t_{k})}\circ F_{k}(p,\hat{r}^{2}_{k}\tau+t_{k}).

In the Riemannian case, when we change the metric after dilation, we do not need to multiply the immersion by the same constant as we would do in the Euclidean space. When we rescale the background space, we see that

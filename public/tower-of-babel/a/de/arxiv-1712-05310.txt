Quantifying over Boolean announcements
Hans van Ditmarsch \rsuper a, Tim French \rsuper b

Abstract
. Various extensions of public announcement logic have been proposed with quantification over announcements. The best-known extension is called arbitrary public announcement logic, 𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{APAL} . It contains a primitive language construct □ ​ φ \Box\varphi intuitively expressing that “after every public announcement of a formula, formula φ \varphi is true”. The logic 𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{APAL} is undecidable and it has an infinitary axiomatization. Now consider restricting the 𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{APAL} quantification to public announcements of Boolean formulas only, such that □ ​ φ \Box\varphi intuitively expresses that “after every public announcement of a Boolean formula, formula φ \varphi is true”. This logic can therefore be called Boolean arbitrary public announcement logic , 𝐵𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{BAPAL} . The logic 𝐵𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{BAPAL} is the subject of this work. Unlike 𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{APAL} it has a finitary axiomatization. Also, 𝐵𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{BAPAL} is not at least as expressive as 𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{APAL} . A further claim that 𝐵𝐴𝑃𝐴𝐿 \mathit{BAPAL} is decidable is deferred to a companion paper.

1.
Introduction

Public announcement logic (
\mathit{PAL}
)
[
GG97
,
Pla89
]
extends
epistemic logic with operators for reasoning about the effects of specific
public announcements. The formula
[\psi]\varphi
means that “
\varphi

is true after the truthful announcement of
\psi
”. This means that, when interpreted in a Kripke model with designated state, after submodel restriction to the states where
\varphi
is true (this includes the designated state, ‘truthful’ here means true),
\psi
is true in that restriction. Arbitrary public
announcement logic (
\mathit{APAL}
)
[
BBvD+08
]
augments this with
operators for quantifying over public announcements. The formula

\Box\varphi
means that “
\varphi
is true after the truthful announcement of
any formula that does not contain
\Box
”.

Quantifying over the communication of information as in

\mathit{APAL}
has applications to epistemic protocol synthesis, where
we wish to achieve epistemic goals by communicating information to agents, but
where we do not know of a specific protocol that will achieve the goal, and where we may not
even know if such a protocol exists. In principle, synthesis problems can be solved
by specifying them as formulas in the logic, and applying model-checking or
satisfiability procedures. However in the case of
\mathit{APAL}
, while there is
a
PSpace
-complete model-checking procedure
[
ÅBvDS10
]
, the
satisfiability problem is undecidable in the presence of multiple agents
[
FvD08
]
.

The quest for a decidable version of public announcement logic with quantification has been going on for a while. Group announcement logic and coalition announcement logic, that are logics with quantifiers over public information change that are similar to
\mathit{APAL}
, are also undecidable
[
ÅvDF16
]
. Whereas the ‘mental model’ arbitrary public announcement logic of
[
CS15
]
is decidable. Yet other dynamic epistemic logics have more generalized quantification, namely over non-public information change. Of those, arbitrary arrow update logic
[
vDvdHK17
]
is undecidable, whereas refinement modal logic
[
BvDF+14
]
and arbitrary action model logic
[
Hal13
]
are decidable. The above-mentioned
[
CS15
]
, wherein a decidable arbitrary public announcement logic is presented, is an interesting case. Decidability is achieved by restricting epistemic modalities, while retaining arbitrary announcements (of formulas containing such modalities). These special modalities are not labelled with an (abstract set of) agents, but with programs using propositional variables. This severely constrains the relational (‘Kripke’) models (possibly) satisfying the formulas bound by the epistemic modalities, which is how decidability can then be obtained for this logic. Instead, in the logic that we will propose, we do not restrict the epistemic modalities, but restrict the quantification over the announcements, the dynamic modalities.

We propose a multi-agent epistemic logic with public announcements and with quantification over public announcements of Boolean formulas (so-called Boolean announcements). We call this
Boolean arbitrary public announcement logic
(
\mathit{BAPAL}
). It is therefore a version of
\mathit{APAL}
: as said, in
\mathit{APAL}
we allow quantification over any quantifier-free (
\Box
-free) formulas, including formulas with announcement modalities and knowledge modalities. For this logic we obtain the following results.

Unlike
\mathit{APAL}
,
\mathit{BAPAL}
has a (complete)
finitary axiomatization
.

For
\mathit{APAL}
only an infinitary axiomatization is known
[
BvD15
]
, although it has not been proved that a finitary axiomatization cannot exist
[
Kui17
]
. A finitary axiomatization for a logic much like
\mathit{APAL}
, called ‘
\mathit{APAL}
with memory’, was proposed in
[
BÖS18
]
. Its structures are topological and its semantics allow to refer to states prior to an announcement (it is history-based). As non-bisimilar states can become bisimilar after an announcement, this semantics makes it possible to continue to distinguish such states; which is the basis for the soundness of their complete axiomatization. Their
\Box
quantifies over epistemic formulas, which in their setting is different from quantifying over formulas that may also contain announcements (the
\Box
-free formulas mentioned above). The semantics are therefore different from that of
\mathit{APAL}
and also different from those of
\mathit{BAPAL}
, with quantification over Booleans. It is an open question if ‘
\mathit{APAL}
with memory’ and
\mathit{APAL}
have the same validities.

\mathit{BAPAL}
is
not at least as expressive
as
\mathit{APAL}
.

Also,
\mathit{BAPAL}
is more expressive than
\mathit{PAL}
, which can be shown just as for
\mathit{APAL}
. An open question remains whether
\mathit{APAL}
is not at least as expressive as
\mathit{BAPAL}
.

We further claim that:

Unlike
\mathit{APAL}
,
\mathit{BAPAL}
is
decidable
.

As most such logics with quantification over information change are undecidable, this seems remarkable. That result in not reported in this work but in a companion paper in progress.

There seems to be many applications, in particular in planning, wherein it makes sense only to consider quantifications over Booleans.

Consider cards cryptography wherein two communicating agents (the principals) attempt to learn the card deal without other players (eavesdroppers) learning the card deal, or even something stronger, such as not learning the ownership of any single card other than their own
[
FW96
,
vD03
,
CFvDFDST13
]
. When modelling initial uncertainty about a stack of cards being dealt over a finite set of players, single states in such models can be uniquely identified with a card deal. Therefore, public announcements restricting such models correspond to Booleans. For example, let there be three cards
0,1,2
and three players Alice, Bob, and Eve, and suppose that Alice announces (truthfully) that she holds card
0
. This corresponds to the public announcement of (some elementary Boolean representation of) two card deals, namely the one wherein, additionally, Bob holds 1 and Eve holds 2, and the one wherein Bob holds 2 and Eve holds 1.

As another example, consider multi-agent planning for publicly observable sensing actions under uncertainty
[
Lev96
,
vDHL11
,
BA11
,
CHL+16
]
: given multiple agents that are uncertain about a number of system parameters (lights, switches, temperature settings) they may be informed, or they may be informing each other, about their observations of the state of the light. Or they may be planning to make such observations, and contingent on the outcome of such observations take further action.

and in the subsequent Section
3
we give various results for this semantics that will be used in later sections. Section
4
is on the expressivity of
\mathit{BAPAL}
. Section

2.1.
Syntax

We start with defining the logical language and some crucial syntactic notions.

{defi}
[Language]
The language of Boolean arbitrary public announcement logic is defined as follows, where
a\in A
and
p\in P
.

{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}(A,P)\ \ni\ \varphi::=p\ |\ \neg\varphi\ |\ (\varphi\wedge\varphi)\ |\ K_{a}\varphi\ |\ [\varphi]\varphi\ |\ \Box\varphi

Other propositional connectives are defined by abbreviation. For
K_{a}\varphi
read ‘agent
a
knows
\varphi
’. For
\Box\varphi
, read ‘after any Boolean announcement,
\varphi
(is true)’. For
[\varphi]\psi
, read ‘after public announcement of
\varphi
,
\psi
’. The dual modalities are defined by abbreviation:
\hat{K}_{a}\varphi:=\neg K_{a}\neg\varphi
,
\langle\varphi\rangle\psi:=\neg[\varphi]\neg\psi
, and
\Diamond\varphi:=\neg\Box\neg\varphi
. Unless ambiguity results we often omit one or both of the parameters
A
and
P
in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}(A,P)
, and write
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}(P)
or
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
. Unless ambiguity results we often omit parentheses occurring in formulas. Formulas are denoted
\varphi,\psi
, possibly primed as in
\varphi^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime\prime},\dots,\psi^{\prime},\dots

We also distinguish the language
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{el}}
of
epistemic logic
(without the constructs
[\varphi]\varphi
and
\Box\varphi
) and the language
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}
of
propositional logic
(without additionally the construct
K_{a}\varphi
), also known as the
Booleans
. Booleans are denoted
\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}
, etc.

The set of propositional variables that occur in a given formula
\varphi
is denoted
\mathit{var}(\varphi)
(where one that does not occur in
\varphi
is called a
fresh variable
), its
modal depth

d(\varphi)
is the maximum nesting of
K_{a}
modalities, and its
quantifier depth

D(\varphi)
is the maximum nesting of
\Box
modalities. These notions are inductively defined as follows.

•

\mathit{var}(p)=\{p\}
,
\mathit{var}(\neg\varphi)=\mathit{var}(K_{a}\varphi)=\mathit{var}(\Box\varphi)=\mathit{var}(\varphi)
,
\mathit{var}(\varphi\wedge\psi)=\mathit{var}([\varphi]\psi)=\mathit{var}(\varphi)\cup\mathit{var}(\psi)
;

•

D(p)=0
,
D(\neg\varphi)=D(K_{a}\varphi)=D(\varphi)
,
D(\varphi\wedge\psi)=D([\varphi]\psi)=\max\{D(\varphi),D(\psi)\}
,
D(\Box\varphi)=D(\varphi)+1
;

•

d(p)=0
,
d(\neg\varphi)=d(\Box\varphi)=d(\varphi)
,
d(\varphi\wedge\psi)=\max\{d(\varphi),d(\psi)\}
,
d([\varphi]\psi)=d(\varphi)+d(\psi)
,
d(K_{a}\varphi)=d(\varphi)+1
.

Arbitrary announcement normal form
is a syntactic restriction of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
that pairs all public announcements with arbitrary Boolean announcement operators. It plays a role in the decidability proof. We will show that any formula in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
is equivalent to one in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{aanf}}
.

{defi}
[Arbitrary announcement normal form]
The language fragment
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{aanf}}
is defined by the following syntax, where
a\in A
and
p\in P
.

\varphi::=p\ |\ \neg\varphi\ |\ (\varphi\wedge\varphi)\ |\ K_{a}\varphi\ |\ [\varphi]\Box\varphi

We now define necessity forms and possibility forms. Necessity forms are used in derivation rules in the proof system.

{defi}
[Necessity form]
Consider a new symbol
\sharp
. The
necessity forms
are defined inductively as follows, where
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
and
a\in A
.

\psi(\sharp)::=\sharp\mid(\varphi\rightarrow\psi(\sharp))\mid K_{a}\psi(\sharp)\mid[\varphi]\psi(\sharp)

By induction on the necessity form
\psi(\sharp)
the reader may easily verify that each
\psi(\sharp)
contains a unique occurrence of the symbol
\sharp
. If
\psi(\sharp)
is a necessity form and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
, then
\psi(\varphi)
is
\psi(\sharp)[\varphi/\sharp]
(the substitution of
\sharp
in
\psi(\sharp)
by
\varphi
), where we note that
\psi(\varphi)\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
. A
possibility form
is the dual of a necessity form. They are therefore defined as:

\psi\{\sharp\}::=\sharp\mid(\varphi\wedge\psi\{\sharp\})\mid\hat{K}_{a}\psi\{\sharp\}\mid\langle\varphi\rangle\psi\{\sharp\}

Similarly to above, notation
\psi\{\varphi\}
means that
\sharp
is substituted by
\varphi
in
\psi\{\sharp\}
.

Given necessity form
\psi(\sharp)
, let
\overline{\psi}\{\sharp\}=t(\psi(\sharp))
be obtained by defining inductively:
t(\sharp)=\sharp
,
t(\varphi\rightarrow\psi(\sharp))=\varphi\wedge t(\psi(\sharp))
,
t(K_{a}\psi(\sharp))=\hat{K}_{a}t(\psi(\sharp))
and
t([\varphi]\psi(\sharp))=\langle\varphi\rangle t(\psi(\sharp))
. Note that
\overline{\psi}\{\sharp\}
is indeed a possibility form. We will later show that
\neg\psi(\neg\varphi)
is equivalent to
\overline{\psi}\{\varphi\}
.

2.3.
Semantics

We continue with the semantics of our logic.

{defi}
[Semantics]
The interpretation of formulas in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
on epistemic models is defined by induction on formulas.

Assume an epistemic model
M=(S,\sim,V)
, and
s\in S
.

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M_{s}\models p&\mbox{iff}&s\in V(p)\\
M_{s}\models\neg\varphi&\mbox{iff}&M_{s}\not\models\varphi\\
M_{s}\models\varphi\wedge\psi&\mbox{iff}&M_{s}\models\varphi\text{ and }M_{s}\models\psi\\
M_{s}\models K_{a}\varphi&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{for all }t\in S:s\sim_{a}t\text{ implies }M_{t}\models\varphi\\
M_{s}\models[\varphi]\psi&\mbox{iff}&M_{s}\models\varphi\text{ implies }M^{\varphi}_{s}\models\psi\\
M_{s}\models\Box\psi&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{for all }\varphi_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}:M_{s}\models[\varphi_{0}]\psi\end{array}

where
[\![\varphi]\!]_{M}:=\{s\in S\mid M_{s}\models\varphi\}
; and where epistemic model
M^{\varphi}=(S^{\prime},\sim^{\prime},V^{\prime})
is such that:
S^{\prime}=[\![\varphi]\!]_{M}
,
{\sim^{\prime}_{a}}={\sim_{a}}\cap([\![\varphi]\!]_{M}\times[\![\varphi]\!]_{M})
, and
V^{\prime}(p):=V(p)\cap[\![\varphi]\!]_{M}
. For
(M^{\varphi})^{\psi}
we may write
M^{\varphi\psi}
. Formula
\varphi
is
valid on model
M
, notation
M\models\varphi
, if for all
s\in S
,
M_{s}\models\varphi
. Formula
\varphi
is
valid
, notation
\models\varphi
, if for all
M
,
M\models\varphi
.

Given
M_{s}
and
M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
, if for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
,
M_{s}\models\varphi
iff
M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}\models\varphi
, we write
M_{s}\equiv M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
. Similarly, if this holds for all
\varphi
with

, we write
M_{s}\equiv^{n}M^{\prime}_{s^{\prime}}
, and if this holds for all
\varphi
with

.

Note that the languages of
\mathit{APAL}
and
\mathit{BAPAL}
are the same, but that their semantics are different. The only difference is the interpretation of

: in
\mathit{APAL}
, this quantifies over the

-free fragment
[

]
, so that, given the eliminability of public announcements from that fragment

\begin{array}[]{lcl}M_{s}\models\Box\psi&\mbox{iff}&\mbox{for all }\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{el}}:M_{s}\models[\varphi]\psi\quad\quad\quad\quad\text{($\mathit{APAL}$\ semantics of }\Box\varphi)\end{array}

3.
Semantic results

We continue with basic semantic results for the logic. They will be used in various of the later sections. Various well-known results for any dynamic epistemic logic with propositional quantification generalize straightforwardly to

3.1.
Bisimulation invariance

We start with the bisimulation invariance of
\mathit{BAPAL}
. This is shown as for
\mathit{APAL}
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
M_{s},N_{s^{\prime}}
be epistemic models. Then
M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{s^{\prime}}
implies
M_{s}\equiv N_{s^{\prime}}
.

Proof 3.1
.

We prove that: for all
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
, and for all
M_{s},N_{s^{\prime}}
: if
M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{s^{\prime}}
, then
M_{s}\models\varphi
iff
N_{s^{\prime}}\models\varphi
; from which the required follows by restricting the scope of
\varphi
to the consequent of the implication. The proof is by induction on the structure of
\varphi
, where the
\Box
-depth
D(\varphi)
takes lexicographic precedence over formula structure (i.e.,
\psi_{1}
is less complex than
\psi_{2}
, if
D(\psi_{1})<D(\psi_{2})
, or if
D(\psi_{1})=D(\psi_{2})
and
\psi_{1}
is a subformula of
\psi_{2}
). The non-standard inductive cases are
[\varphi]\psi
and
\Box\psi
. In either case we only show one direction of the equivalence in the conclusion; the other direction is similar.

Case
[\varphi]\psi

Let
M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{s^{\prime}}
and
M_{s}\models[\varphi]\psi
. The latter is by definition equal to:
M_{s}\models\varphi
implies
M^{\varphi}_{s}\models\psi
. Let us now assume
M_{s}\models\varphi
.

First, from
M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{s^{\prime}}
and
M_{s}\models\varphi
, it follows by induction that
N_{s^{\prime}}\models\varphi
.

Second, this not only holds for
s
but for any
t
in the domain of
M
and
t^{\prime}
in the domain of
N
: from
M_{t}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{t^{\prime}}
and
M_{t}\models\varphi
, it follows by induction that
N_{t^{\prime}}\models\varphi
.

This allows us to show that
M^{\varphi}_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N^{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}
, namely, given
\mathfrak{R}:M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N_{s^{\prime}}
, by the relation
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}:M^{\varphi}_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}N^{\varphi}_{s^{\prime}}
such that for all
t,t^{\prime}
:
(t,t^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
iff (
(t,t^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{R}
and
M_{t}\models\varphi
). We now show that the relation
\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
is a bisimulation. The clause
atoms
is obvious. For

, assuming some
(s,s^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
, let
s\sim_{a}t
in
M^{\varphi}
. Let
t^{\prime}
be such that

and

. From

, we get with induction that

. Therefore,
(t,t^{\prime})\in\mathfrak{R}^{\prime}
. As

Third, having shown that

Winding up, we now have shown that (

Case
\Box\psi

Let

Corollary 2

Let

such that

Lemma 3
.

Let two models

M_{s}\equiv^{n}N_{s^{\prime}}
.

.

We show the above by proving the following statement:

For all

, for all

with

, for all models

: if
M_{s}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}^{n}N_{s^{\prime}}
, then
M_{s}\models\varphi
iff
N_{s^{\prime}}\models\varphi
.

We prove this by refining the complexity measure used in the previous proposition: we now, additionally, give modal depth

(i.e.,

and

and

is a subformula of

). For clarity we give the —essentially different— case

and also the —essentially the same— cases

and

and do not require the induction over

.

Case

3.2.
Arbitrary announcement normal form and necessity form

We continue with results for the arbitrary announcement normal form and for the necessity form. The former are important to show decidability of
\mathit{BAPAL}
, and the latter to show that the axiomatization is complete.

Lemma 4
.

Every formula of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
is semantically equivalent to a formula in arbitrary announcement normal form.

Proof 3.3
.

We give the proof by defining a truth preserving transformation
\delta
from
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{aanf}}
.
This is defined with the following recursion:

\begin{array}[]{lcllcl}\delta(p)&=&p&\delta(\neg\psi)&=&\neg\delta(\psi)\\
\delta(\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime})&=&\delta(\psi)\wedge\delta(\psi^{\prime})&\delta(K_{a}\psi)&=&K_{a}\delta(\psi)\\
\\
\delta([\varphi]p)&=&\delta(\varphi\rightarrow p)&\delta([\varphi]\neg\psi)&=&\delta(\varphi\rightarrow\neg[\varphi]\psi)\\
\delta([\varphi](\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime}))&=&\delta([\varphi]\psi\wedge[\varphi]\psi^{\prime})&\delta([\varphi]K_{a}\psi)&=&\delta(\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}[\varphi]\psi)\\
\delta([\varphi][\varphi^{\prime}]\psi)&=&\delta([\varphi\wedge[\varphi]\varphi^{\prime}]\psi)&\\
\\
\delta(\Box\psi)&=&[\top]\Box\delta(\psi)&\delta([\varphi]\Box\psi)&=&[\delta(\varphi)]\Box\delta(\psi)\end{array}

We have to show that the translation is truth preserving and that the translation procedure terminates.

The truth preservation is obvious for the clauses in rows 1 and 2 and for
\delta([\varphi]\Box\psi)=[\delta(\varphi)]\Box\delta(\psi)
. Concerning
\delta(\Box\psi)=[\top]\Box\delta(\psi)
we note that
\Box\psi\leftrightarrow[\top]\Box\psi
is a valid equivalence. The translation clauses in rows 3 to 5 employ the valid equivalences
[\varphi]p\leftrightarrow(\varphi\rightarrow p)
,
[\varphi]\neg\psi\leftrightarrow(\varphi\rightarrow\neg[\varphi]\psi)
,
[\varphi](\psi\wedge\psi^{\prime})\leftrightarrow([\varphi]\psi\wedge[\varphi]\psi^{\prime})
,
[\varphi]K_{a}\psi\leftrightarrow(\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}[\varphi]\psi)
, and
[\varphi][\varphi^{\prime}]\psi\leftrightarrow[\varphi\wedge[\varphi]\varphi^{\prime}]\psi
. These are well-known from
\mathit{PAL}

[
Pla89
,
vDvdHK08
]
(and for further reference we note that they are listed as axioms of
\mathit{BAPAL}
in Section
5
).

We proceed by showing termination. For the clauses in rows 1, 2, and 6 we observe that each occurrence of
\delta
on the right-hand side of an equation binds a formula that is less complex than the formula bound by
\delta
on the left-hand side of the equation (and in
\delta(p)=p
,
\delta
has disappeared on the right-hand side). For the clauses in rows 3 to 5 we refer to complexity measures in
\mathit{PAL}

[
vDvdHK08
,
BvD15
]
. Taking (e.g.) the measure
[
vDvdHK08
, Def. 7.21]
,
[
vDvdHK08
, Lemma 7.22]
shows that the complexity of the right-hand side of each equivalence above is either equal to or lower than the complexity of the left-hand side of the equivalence. Therefore,
\delta
will always return a formula in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{aanf}}
.

Lemma 5
.

Given are necessity form
\psi(\sharp)
, possibility form
\overline{\psi}\{\sharp\}
, and
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
. Then
\neg\psi(\varphi)
is equivalent to
\overline{\psi}\{\neg\varphi\}
.

Proof 3.4
.

This is easily shown by induction on the structure of necessity forms and using that
\neg(\chi\rightarrow\psi(\varphi))
iff
\chi\wedge\neg\psi(\varphi)
,
\neg K_{a}\psi(\varphi)
iff
\hat{K}_{a}\neg\psi(\varphi)
, and
\neg[\chi]\psi(\varphi)
iff
\langle\chi\rangle\neg\psi(\varphi)
.

In the following lemma we show that a necessity form of arbitrary shape
\psi(\sharp)
can be transformed into a necessity form of unique shape
\psi_{1}\rightarrow[\psi_{2}]\sharp
. With respect to instantiations
\psi(\theta)
of such necessity forms this is a validity preserving transformation in both directions. Note that it is not a truth preserving transformation. This result will be used in Section
5
to show that two versions of the axiomatization with different derivation rules are both complete (the translation is not only validity preserving but also derivability preserving).

Lemma 6
.

Let
\psi(\sharp)
be a necessity form and
\theta\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
. Then there are
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
such that
\models\psi(\theta)
iff
\models\psi_{1}\rightarrow[\psi_{2}]\theta
.

Proof 3.5
.

Consider the following recursively defined translation
\tau
(also employing subrecursion and subsubrecursion).

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tau(\sharp)&=&\top\rightarrow[\top]\sharp\\
\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow\sharp)&=&\varphi\rightarrow[\top]\sharp\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow\psi_{1}\rightarrow\psi_{2}(\sharp))&=&\tau(\varphi\wedge\psi_{1}\rightarrow\psi_{2}(\sharp))\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}\psi(\sharp))&=&\tau(\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\psi(\sharp))\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow[\psi]\sharp)&=&\varphi\rightarrow[\psi]\sharp\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow[\psi_{1}](\psi_{2}\rightarrow\psi_{3}(\sharp)))&=&\tau(\varphi\wedge[\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}\rightarrow[\psi_{1}]\psi_{3}(\sharp))\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow[\psi_{1}]K_{a}\psi_{2}(\sharp))&=&\tau(\hat{K}_{a}(\varphi\wedge\psi_{1})\rightarrow[\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(\sharp))\\
\tau(\varphi\rightarrow[\psi_{1}][\psi_{2}]\psi_{3}(\sharp))&=&\tau(\varphi\rightarrow[\psi_{1}\wedge[\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}]\psi_{3}(\sharp))\\
\\
\tau(K_{a}\psi(\sharp))&=&\tau(\psi(\sharp))\\
\\
\tau([\varphi]\sharp)&=&\top\rightarrow[\varphi]\sharp\\
\tau([\varphi]K_{a}\psi(\sharp))&=&\tau(\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\rightarrow[\varphi]\psi(\sharp))\\
\tau([\varphi](\psi_{1}\rightarrow\psi_{2}(\sharp))&=&\tau([\varphi]\psi_{1}\rightarrow[\varphi]\psi_{2}(\sharp))\\
\tau([\varphi][\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(\sharp))&=&\tau([\varphi\wedge[\varphi]\psi_{1}]\psi_{2}(\sharp))\end{array}

We can now observe that:

•

The translation
\tau
terminates.

Here we use again that formulas of shape
[\varphi][\psi]\eta
are at least as complex as
[\varphi\wedge[\varphi]\psi]\eta
. We should also observe that in any clause of shape
\tau(x\rightarrow y)=\tau(z\rightarrow w)
,
y
is at least as complex as
w
, and that in any claus of shape
\tau([x]u)=\tau(z\rightarrow w)
,
[x]u
is at least as complex as
w
. The complexity the antecedent
z
of the implication does not matter.

•

\tau(\psi(\theta))
is a neccessity form of shape
\psi_{1}\rightarrow[\psi_{2}]\theta
, as required.

There are four clauses in which
\tau
does not appear on the right-hand side, and in all those cases the right-hand side has the required shape.

•

For all
\theta\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
,
\models\psi(\theta)
iff
\models\tau(\psi(\theta))
, as required.

This holds because all clauses are validity preserving in both directions. Apart from propositional tautologies this can be justified by one or more of the following observations:

In epistemic logic,
\models K_{a}\psi
implies
\models\psi
, and
\models\psi
implies
\models K_{a}\psi
. The validity of these in
\mathit{BAPAL}
is also obvious. This is used in the, maybe surprising, case
\tau(K_{a}\psi(\sharp))
.

The
\mathit{PAL}
validities listed in the above Lemma

, there justifying truth preservation, are also frequently used here; in addition (as this might otherwise be oblique) we use the validity
[\varphi](\psi\rightarrow\eta)\leftrightarrow([\varphi]\psi\rightarrow[\varphi]\eta)
. This is used in various cases including
\tau([\varphi](\psi_{1}\rightarrow\psi_{2}(\sharp))
.

In epistemic logic,
\models\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}\psi
iff
\models\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\psi
. Let us prove this. We note that
\models\varphi\rightarrow K_{a}\psi
implies
\models\hat{K}_{a}\varphi\rightarrow\hat{K}_{a}K_{a}\psi

3.3.
BAPAL validities involving the quantifier

Some
\mathit{BAPAL}
validities are as follows. They play no auxiliary role as tools in later sections, but they serve to compare
\mathit{BAPAL}
to other logics with quantification over information change, where such properties sometimes hold and sometimes not.

Proposition 7
.

•

\models\Box\varphi\rightarrow\varphi
(T)

•

\models\Box\varphi\rightarrow\Box\Box\varphi
(4)

•

\models\Box\Diamond\varphi\rightarrow\Diamond\Box\varphi
(MK)

•

\models\Diamond\Box\varphi\rightarrow\Box\Diamond\varphi
(CR)

Proof 3.6
.

•

Let
M_{s}
be arbitrary. Assume
M_{s}\models\Box\varphi
. Then
M_{s}\models[\top]\varphi
. As
\models[\top]\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi
, therefore
M_{s}\models\varphi
.

•

The dual validity
\Diamond\Diamond\varphi\rightarrow\Diamond\varphi
follows from the observation that for all
\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}
,
\langle\varphi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}\rangle\varphi
is equivalent to
\langle\varphi_{0}\wedge\psi_{0}\rangle\varphi
.

•

The validity of MK is shown as in
\mathit{APAL}
. Assume that
M_{s}\models\Box\Diamond\varphi
. Given the set
\mathit{var}(\varphi)
of propositional variables occurring in
\varphi
, let
{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}
be the characteristic function for the valuation of
\mathit{var}(\varphi)
in state
s
. We then have
M_{s}\models[{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}]\Diamond\varphi
, and
M^{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}_{s}\models\Diamond\varphi
. In the model
M^{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}_{s}
the valuation of the propositional variables in
\mathit{var}(\varphi)
is constant in the domain (for each such
p\in\mathit{var}(\varphi)
, either
V(p)={\mathcal{D}}(M^{\delta_{s}(\varphi)})
or
V(p)=\emptyset
). We now use that for on models where the valuation of variables is constant, every formula that is true in a state of the model is also valid on the model. For
\mathit{APAL}
this result is found in
[
BBvD+08
, Lemma 3.2]
(see also
[
vDvdHI12
, Lemma 1]
). The inductive proof of this result depends on the validity of
\Box\top\leftrightarrow\top
and
\Box\bot\leftrightarrow\bot
. These are also valid for
\mathit{BAPAL}
. From this it is easy to show that on such models,
\models\varphi\rightarrow\Box\varphi

[
BBvD+08
, Lemma 3.3]
. Then, with duality and with the validity
\Box\varphi\rightarrow\varphi
shown in the first item, we obtain that
\Diamond\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi
and
\Box\varphi\leftrightarrow\varphi
are both valid on such models, and therefore also
\Diamond\varphi\rightarrow\Box\varphi
. From that and
M^{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}_{s}\models\Diamond\varphi
then follows that
M^{\delta_{s}(\varphi)}_{s}\models\Box\varphi
. Thus
M_{s}\models\langle\delta_{s}(\varphi)\rangle\Box\varphi
, and
M_{s}\models\Diamond\Box\varphi
.

•

In order to prove the validity of CR we have to show that: for all
\varphi_{0},\psi_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}
and for all
M
with non-empty denotation of
\varphi_{0}
and of
\psi_{0}
there are
\varphi^{\prime}_{0},\psi^{\prime}_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}
such that
M^{\varphi_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{0}}{\raisebox{1.29167pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\medspace\underline{\!\leftrightarrow\!}\medspace$}}M^{\psi_{0}\psi^{\prime}_{0}}
. The obvious choice is
\varphi^{\prime}_{0}=\psi_{0}
and
\psi^{\prime}_{0}=\varphi_{0}
, as

M^{\varphi_{0}\varphi^{\prime}_{0}}=M^{\varphi_{0}\psi_{0}}=M^{\varphi_{0}\wedge\psi_{0}}=M^{\psi_{0}\wedge\varphi_{0}}=M^{\psi_{0}\varphi_{0}}=M^{\psi_{0}\psi^{\prime}_{0}}.

The proof of the validity 4 is more direct than in
[
BBvD+08
]
, where it is used that
\langle\chi\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
is equivalent to
\langle\langle\chi\rangle\psi\rangle\varphi
. Formula
\langle\chi\rangle\langle\psi\rangle\varphi
is not equivalent to
\langle\chi\wedge\psi\rangle\varphi
for all
\chi,\psi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
.

The proof of the validity CR for
\mathit{BAPAL}
is easier than for other quantified epistemic logics such as
\mathit{APAL}
, where the proof needs to ‘close the diamond at the bottom’, which just as in the case of MK needs declaring the values of all atoms, i.e., we must choose
\varphi^{\prime}_{0}=\psi^{\prime}_{0}={\delta_{s}(\varphi)}
. For such a proof see e.g.
[
vDFH21
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Note that not all logics with quantification over announcements satisfy all the properties of the quantifier shown in this subsection. For example, the logic known as
\mathit{APAL+}
(quantifying over announcements of so-called positive formulas, corresponding to the universal fragment in first-order logic) does not satisfy the validity
\Box\varphi\rightarrow\Box\Box\varphi

[
vDFH21
]
.

3.4.
Boolean closure

We will now define the novel notion of
Boolean closure
, and prove some lemmas for it. These are used when proving the soundness of the axiomatization of the logic
\mathit{BAPAL}
, later.

{defi}
[Boolean closure]
Consider the union denoted
\ddot{P}
of the set of atoms
P
and the disjoint set of atoms
\{p_{\varphi_{0}}\mid\varphi_{0}\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}(P)\}
. The
Boolean closure
of a model
M=(S,\sim,V)
for atoms
P
is the model
\ddot{M}=(S,\sim,\ddot{V})
that is as
M
, except that the
Boolean closed valuation

\ddot{V}
is for atoms
\ddot{P}
and such that
\ddot{V}(p)=V(p)
for
p\in P
and
\ddot{V}(p_{\varphi_{0}})=[\![\varphi_{0}]\!]_{M}
for
p_{\varphi_{0}}\in\ddot{P}\setminus P
.

As
P
is countably infinite, and as the Booleans on
P
can be enumerated,
\ddot{P}
is also countably infinite. Given an epistemic model, then for each atom and for each Boolean there are also infinitely many atoms with the same value on the Boolean closure of that model. E.g.,
p
has the same value as
p_{p\wedge p}
(i.e., the atom
q
corresponding to the Boolean
p\wedge p
), and the same value as
p_{p\wedge p\wedge p}
(the atom
q^{\prime}
corresponding to Boolean
p\wedge p\wedge p
), etc. We proceed with some other properties of the Boolean closure, in the form of lemmas.

Lemma 8
.

On a Boolean closed model
\ddot{M}
, for all Booleans, including Booleans of atoms in
\ddot{P}\setminus P
, there is an atom with the same value.

Proof 3.7
.

The proof is by induction on the structure of a Boolean
\varphi\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{pl}}(\ddot{P})
.

If
\varphi
is an atom, it is obvious.

If
\varphi=\neg\psi
, by induction we may assume that there is an atom

4.
Expressivity

Given logical languages
{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}

{\mathcal{L}}

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}

\varphi^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}

M_{s}\models^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}
iff
M_{s}\models\varphi
. If
{\mathcal{L}}
is not at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
and
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
is not at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\mathcal{L}}
is
incomparable
to
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
. If
{\mathcal{L}}
is at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
is at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\mathcal{L}}
is
as expressive as

{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
. If
{\mathcal{L}}
is at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
but
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
is not at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}
, then
{\mathcal{L}}
is (strictly)
more expressive
than
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
. The combination of a language with a semantics given a class of models determines a logic. In this work we only consider model class
\mathcal{S}5
. We abbreviate “given logic
L
determined by language
{\mathcal{L}}
, model class
\mathcal{S}5
and satisfaction relation
\models
, and logic
L^{\prime}
determined by language
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
, model class
\mathcal{S}5
and satisfaction relation
\models^{\prime}
,
{\mathcal{L}}
is at least as expressive as
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}
,” by “

is at least as expressive as
L^{\prime}
,” and similarly for other expressivity terminology. Note that in this work the language
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathit{bapal}}
is the same for

6.
Conclusions and further research

We proposed the logic
\mathit{BAPAL}
. It is an extension of public announcement logic. It contains a modality
\Box\varphi
intuitively corresponding to: “after every public announcement of a Boolean formula,
\varphi
is true”. We have shown that
\mathit{BAPAL}
is more expressive than
\mathit{EL}
and not at least as expressive as
\mathit{APAL}
, and that it has a finitary complete axiomatization.

For further research we wish to report the decidability of the satisfiability problem of
\mathit{BAPAL}
and of yet another logic with quantification over announcements, called
positive arbitrary public announcement logic
,
\mathit{APAL+}
. The logic
\mathit{APAL+}
has a primitive modality “after every public announcement of a positive formula,
\varphi
is true”. The positive formulas correspond to the universal fragment in first-order logic. These are the formulas where negations do not bind modalities. It has been reported in
[
vDFH21
]
.

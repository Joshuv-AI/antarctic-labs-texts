Calculation of the one loop box integral at Finite Temperature and Density
A. S. Khvorostukhin

Abstract
Calculation of hadronization, decay or scattering processes at non-zero temperatures and densities within the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio-like models requires some techniques for computation of Feynmann diagrams. Decomposition of Feynman diagrams at the one loop level leads to the appearance of elementary integrals with one, two, three, and four fermion lines. For example, evaluation of the π ​ π \pi\pi scattering amplitude requires calculating a box diagram with four fermion lines. In this work, the real and imaginary parts of the box integral at the one loop level are provided in the form suitable for numerical evaluation. The obtained expressions are applicable to any value of temperature, particle mass, and chemical potential. We pay special attention to the conditions for the existence of the appearing improper integrals and correct the results Klevansky for the three fermion lines. As a result, we have obtained constraints on possible values of particle momenta. Among the expressions for the box integral, the general formulas for the integral with an arbitrary number of lines are derived for the case with zero or collinear fermion momenta.

Due to the complexity of the QCD Lagrangian and the nonperturbativity of QCD at energies relevant for the deconfinement/chiral phase transition, effective theories of strongly interacting matter at non-zero temperatures/densities are under intensive study, see, e.g.

Extension of QCD Lagrangian-inspired effective models such as the Nambu-Jona-Lasinio (NJL) one

This decomposition technique can be generalized to non-zero temperatures using the Matsubara formalism

The study of meson scattering processes (for example,
\pi\pi

;
JRS2002

Klevansky

We carefully investigate for what values of the parameters all integrals that appear exist. This work brings us to the necessity of correcting the results
Klevansky
for the three-line elementary integral. We also show that though the integrals with three and more fermion lines seem convergent, our analysis leads to the insistence of using the three momentum cutoff regularization scheme with
|\mathbf{p}|<\Lambda

The paper is organized as follows. Section II shows how the original integral can be represented as a sum of elementary integrals. Section III is devoted to the correction of the result
Klevansky
for the three line function. We underline that one should carefully consider the question of the existence of multiple integrals. After that, the results for the four line integral are given depending on configurations of particle momenta. The simplest cases when all three momenta are collinear or equal to zero are discussed in Sec. IV. More complicated
“planar”
configurations are considered in Sec. V. The main original result of the paper is formulated in Sec. VI, where the expression for the most general case of independent momenta is given. We also shortly discuss how to calculate a one-loop integral with any number of fermion lines in Sec. VII. Finally, the results are summarized in Conclusions. Some technical details are presented in two Appendices.

Two new definite integrals

To calculate the box integral, we need to evaluate the integral

Let us start with the consideration of the first one. In the general case, integration is quite difficult. Fortunately, we can consider only a much simpler case when trinomials

\phi

VI.1
). We obtain the constraint

\displaystyle=(C-k)\frac{u(S-Kb)-K(C-k)d}{u^{2}-(C-k)^{2}d},\quad\mathcal{U}=\sqrt{\frac{C-k}{k}}\frac{(S-Kb)(C-k)-Ku}{u^{2}-(C-k)^{2}d}\,.

\displaystyle+i\pi\sum_{l=1}^{L}{\rm sgn}(\lambda_{l})\,\delta(\lambda_{l}(E-E_{0l})+\mathbf{p}\mathbf{k}_{l})\prod_{s=1,s\neq l}^{L}\frac{1}{\lambda_{s}(E-E_{0s})+\mathbf{p}\mathbf{k}_{s}}.

(119)

\displaystyle{\rm Re}\mathcal{Z}^{\pm}_{L}\left(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{L}^{+};\mathcal{T}\right)

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\mathcal{Z}^{\pm}_{L}\left(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{L}^{+};\mathcal{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}{\rm sgn}(\lambda_{l})\int\limits_{m}^{\Lambda_{E}}\!{\mathrm{d}}E\,p\,f(\pm E-\mu)

\displaystyle\times\int\limits_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\phi\,\delta(\lambda_{l}(E-E_{0l})+\mathbf{p}\mathbf{k}_{l})\prod_{s=1,s\neq l}^{L}\frac{1}{\lambda_{s}(E-E_{0s})+\mathbf{p}\mathbf{k}_{s}},

(121)

where we can see only the terms without
\delta
-functions or with a single one.

Equation (
VII
) can be simplified by performing integration in the imaginary part:

\displaystyle{\rm Im}\mathcal{Z}^{\pm}_{L}\left(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}_{L}^{+};\mathcal{T}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{L}}\frac{1}{{\prod_{s=1}^{L}k_{s}}}\sum_{l=1}^{L}{\rm sgn}(\lambda_{l})\int\limits_{m}^{\Lambda_{E}}\!{\mathrm{d}}E\,\frac{f(\pm E-\mu)}{p^{L-1}}\,\Pi_{l}(E)\Theta\left(\Phi_{l}(E)\right),

(122)

with

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}(E)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{0}^{2\pi}{\mathrm{d}}\phi\,\prod_{s=2}^{L}\frac{1}{z_{s}-z_{1}\cos\delta_{s}+\cos(\phi-\phi_{s})\sin\delta_{s}\sqrt{1-z_{1}^{2}}},

(123)

and

\displaystyle\Pi_{l}(E)

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{-1}^{1}{\mathrm{d}}x\frac{\Theta(-\Delta_{l}(x))}{(z_{1}+x)\sqrt{-\Delta_{l}(x)}}\prod_{s=2,s\neq l}^{L}\frac{\sin\delta_{l}}{\Psi_{ls}(x)}

\displaystyle\times\left[\prod_{s=2,s\neq l}^{L}[\beta_{ls}+x\alpha_{ls}-\sin(\phi_{l}-\phi_{s})\sin\delta_{s}\sqrt{-\Delta_{l}(x)}]+\prod_{s=2,s\neq l}^{L}[\beta_{ls}+x\alpha_{ls}+\sin(\phi_{l}-\phi_{s})\sin\delta_{s}\sqrt{-\Delta_{l}(x)}]\right]

(124)

for
l>1
.

The real part can be considered only when
\Phi_{l}(E)\leq 0
, as follows from Sec. IV.B. We can also see that the integrand in the definition of
\Pi_{1}(E)
has no singularities when
\Delta_{1s}>0
. For symmetry reasons, one should conclude that the imaginary part exists only if
\Phi_{ls}(E)>0
.

VIII
Conclusions

In the article, we have given the formulas required for numerical evaluation of the four fermion line integral at finite temperature/density that is often needed for theoretical calculations such as within the NJL model. Both the real and imaginary parts of these functions are presented separately. It was proved that the integrals do not exist for some values of the parameters. We give the corresponding constraints for all considered cases.

Derivation of the necessary expressions obliged us to correct the results obtained in
Klevansky
for the integral with three lines and, moreover, to refine the approach used in the cited paper. It was shown that careful consideration of the conditions for the existence plays a crucial role in obtaining valid results.

We have also found, as a by-product, expressions for integrals with any number of lines when momenta are in simple
’dot’
or ’
line
’ configurations. For a general configuration, the corresponding expressions are presented as triple and double integrals allowing numerical evaluation.

Appendix A
Summation of inverse trigonometric functions

In this Appendix we just cite some beautiful and useful formulas from
Gradshteyn
. The sum and difference of two arccosines are

\displaystyle\arccos X+\arccos Y

\displaystyle=2\pi\Theta\!\left(-X-Y\right)+{\rm sgn}(X+Y)\arccos\left(XY-\sqrt{1-X^{2}}\sqrt{1-Y^{2}}\right),

(125)

\displaystyle\arccos X-\arccos Y

\displaystyle={\rm sgn}(Y-X)\arccos\left(XY+\sqrt{1-X^{2}}\sqrt{1-Y^{2}}\right).

(126)

For arctangents we have

\displaystyle\arctan X+\arctan Y

\displaystyle=\arctan\frac{X+Y}{1-XY}+\pi\,{\rm sgn}(X+Y)\Theta\!\left(XY-1\right),

\displaystyle=\Theta\!\left(|X+Y|\right)\arctan\frac{XY-1}{X+Y}+\frac{\pi}{2}\,{\rm sgn}(X+Y),

(127)

where the second line is obtained with the help of

\displaystyle\arctan X+\arctan\frac{1}{X}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\,{\rm sgn}X.

(128)

Appendix B
Some properties of quadratic trinomials arising during the evaluation of integrals

When we make integration over angles, it is convenient to exclude the variable
E
using
z_{l}
. But after that, in the final expressions, we should make the inverse transition as a result of which the following functions arise:

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(E)

\displaystyle\equiv\Phi(E;k_{l},\lambda_{l},\varepsilon_{l},m)=p^{2}k_{l}^{2}(1-z_{l}^{2}),

(129)

\displaystyle\Phi_{ls}(E)

\displaystyle=p^{2}k_{l}^{2}k_{s}^{2}\Delta_{ls},

(130)

\displaystyle\Phi_{123}(E)

\displaystyle=p^{2}k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}\Delta_{123}.

(131)

Recognizing that these functions are just trinomials, let us introduce the generalized trinomial

\displaystyle\Omega_{L}(E;\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}^{+}_{L},m)

\displaystyle=C_{\Omega}(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}^{+}_{L},m)\,E^{2}-2B_{\Omega}(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}^{+}_{L},m)\,E+A_{\Omega}(\mathcal{X}^{+}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{X}^{+}_{L},m)

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{b}^{(L)}E-\mathbf{a}^{(L)}\right]^{2}-\left[w^{(L)}\right]^{2}p^{2},

(132)

where we demand
\left|C_{\Omega}\right|\neq 0
. The coefficients are

\displaystyle A_{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{a}^{(L)}\right]^{2}+m^{2}\left[w^{(L)}\right]^{2},\quad B_{\Omega}=\mathbf{a}^{(L)}\mathbf{b}^{(L)},\quad C_{\Omega}=\left[\mathbf{b}^{(L)}\right]^{2}-\left[w^{(L)}\right]^{2}.

(133)

As we can easily see, the determinant is

\displaystyle D_{\Omega}=\left[w^{(L)}\right]^{2}\left\{A_{\Omega}-m^{2}\left[\mathbf{b}^{(L)}\right]^{2}-\frac{\left[\mathbf{a}^{(L)}\times\mathbf{b}^{(L)}\right]^{2}}{[w^{(L)}]^{2}}\right\},

(134)

where we used that

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{2}\mathbf{b}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\right)^{2}+\left(\mathbf{a}\mathbf{b}\right)^{2}.

(135)

Using the above properties, one can prove that the magnitude of both roots of
\Omega_{L}(E)
is not less than
m
. As we have seen, the conditons for the existence of the considered improper integrals have the form
\Omega_{L}(E)>0
for any
E\in[m,\Lambda_{E}]
. It means that the sign of
\Omega_{L}(E)
in the integration interval is fixed which is possible only if both roots lie outside. Then we can prove

\displaystyle\Theta\!\left(\Omega_{L}(E)\right)

\displaystyle=\Theta\!\left(C_{\Omega}\right)\Theta\!\left(\Omega_{L}(E)\right)

(136)

and

\displaystyle\Theta\!\left(-\Omega_{L}(E)\right)

\displaystyle=\Theta\!\left(D_{\Omega}\right)\Theta\!\left(-C_{\Omega}\right)\Theta\!\left(-\Omega_{L}(E)\right).

(137)

The vectors
\mathbf{a}^{(L)}
and
\mathbf{b}^{(L)}
can be expressed as linear combinations of a set of vectors
\mathbf{q}_{l}
for any
L
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(L)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\varepsilon_{l}\mathbf{q}_{l},\quad\mathbf{b}^{(L)}=\sum_{l=1}^{L}\lambda_{l}\mathbf{q}_{l},

(138)

and
\mathbf{q}_{l}=\mathbf{q}_{l}\left(\mathbf{k}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{k}_{L}\right)
.

Using the generalized trinomial, one can write

\displaystyle\Phi_{l}(E)

\displaystyle=-\Omega_{1}\left(E;\mathcal{X}_{l}^{+},m\right)

(139)

with

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(1)}_{l}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{l}\frac{\mathbf{k}}{k},\quad\mathbf{b}^{(1)}_{l}=\lambda_{l}\frac{\mathbf{k}}{k},\quad w^{(1)}_{l}=k_{l},

(140)

and
\mathbf{k}
is an arbitrary vector.

For
L=2
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi_{ls}(E)

\displaystyle=\Omega_{2}\left(E;\mathcal{X}_{l}^{+},\mathcal{X}_{s}^{+},m\right),

(141)

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(2)}_{ls}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{s}\mathbf{k}_{l}-\varepsilon_{l}\mathbf{k}_{s},\quad\mathbf{b}^{(2)}_{ls}=\lambda_{s}\mathbf{k}_{l}-\lambda_{l}\mathbf{k}_{s},\quad w^{(2)}_{ls}=\left|\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s}\right|.

(142)

We also need the coefficients for
L=3
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}\mathbf{k}_{2}\times\mathbf{k}_{3}+\varepsilon_{2}\mathbf{k}_{3}\times\mathbf{k}_{1}+\varepsilon_{3}\mathbf{k}_{1}\times\mathbf{k}_{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}\mathbf{k}_{2}\times\mathbf{k}_{3}+\lambda_{2}\mathbf{k}_{3}\times\mathbf{k}_{1}+\lambda_{3}\mathbf{k}_{1}\times\mathbf{k}_{2},

(143)

\displaystyle w^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{k}_{1}(\mathbf{k}_{2}\times\mathbf{k}_{3}).

(144)

One can easily prove

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}_{ls}^{(2)}\times\mathbf{b}_{ls}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\varrho_{ls}(\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s}),

(145)

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(3)}\times\mathbf{b}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=w^{(3)}\left(\varrho_{12}\mathbf{k}_{3}-\varrho_{13}\mathbf{k}_{2}+\varrho_{23}\mathbf{k}_{1}\right),

(146)

where

\displaystyle\varrho_{ls}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{s}\,\varepsilon_{l}-\lambda_{l}\varepsilon_{s}=\lambda_{l}\lambda_{s}(E_{0l}-E_{0s}).

(147)

Then, using

\displaystyle\mathbf{a}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{t}(\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s})-\mathbf{a}^{(2)}_{ls}\times\mathbf{k}_{t},

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{t}(\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s})-\mathbf{b}^{(2)}_{ls}\times\mathbf{k}_{t},

(148)

and Eq. (
135
), we find

\displaystyle\Phi_{123}(E)\left(\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[\mathbf{b}^{(3)}E-\mathbf{a}^{(3)}\right]\left(\mathbf{k}_{l}\times\mathbf{k}_{s}\right)\right\}^{2}+\left[w^{(3)}\right]^{2}\Phi_{ls}(E)

(149)

from which one can see that
\Phi_{123}(E)\geq 0
if
\Phi_{ls}(E)\geq 0
.

Similar expressions are also valid for
\Phi_{ls}(E)

\displaystyle\Phi_{ls}(E)k_{s}^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left[\mathbf{b}^{(2)}E-\mathbf{a}^{(2)}\right]\mathbf{k}_{s}\right\}^{2}-\left[w^{(2)}\right]^{2}\Phi_{l}(E)

(150)

results in non-negativity of
\Phi_{ls}(E)\geq 0
if
\Phi_{l}(E)\leq 0

The generalized trinomial
\Omega_{L}

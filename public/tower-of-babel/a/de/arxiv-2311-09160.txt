Characteristic classes for flat Diff(M)-foliations Thanks: Version date: November 15, 2023
Steven Hurder

Abstract
. In this work we relate the known results about the homotopy type of classifying spaces for smooth foliations, with the homology and cohomology of the discrete group of diffeomorphisms of a smooth compact connected oriented manifold. The Mather-Thurston Theorem forms a bridge between the results on the homotopy types of classifying spaces and the various homology and cohomology groups that are studied. We introduce the algebraic K-theory of a manifold M that is derived from the discrete group of diffeomorphisms of M, and observe that the calculations of homotopy groups in this work are about these K-theory groups. We include a variety of remarks and open problems related to the study of the diffeomorphism groups and their homological invariants using the Mather-Thurston Theorem.

1.
Introduction

Let
M
be a smooth connected orientable manifold of dimension
q
. Let
{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)
denote the subgroup of compactly supported diffeomorphisms of the topological group
{\rm Diff}(M)
, and let
{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)
and
{\rm Diff}^{+}(M)
be the respective subgroups of orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms.

For a topological group
G
, we let
G^{\delta}
denote the group with the discrete topology. The cohomology groups
H^{*}(BG^{\delta};{\mathcal{A}})
are the universal characteristic classes for flat bundles associated with
G^{\delta}
. For the identity map
\iota\colon G^{\delta}\to G
, there is a natural induced map
\iota^{*}\colon H^{*}(BG;{\mathcal{A}})\to H^{*}(BG^{\delta};{\mathcal{A}})
. When
G
is a connected Lie group,
Milnor calculated this map for many cases in
[
99
]
. For example, when the coefficient ring
{\mathcal{A}}
is finite and
G
is solvable, the map is an isomorphism. In contrast, when
{\mathcal{A}}
has characteristic
0
and
G
is a compact or complex semisimple Lie group, Milnor showed that the map is trivial. One can ask for analogous results in the case of the groups
{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)
and
{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)
.

The classifying space
B{\rm Diff}(M)
classifies
M
-bundles over a base space
X
, and the classifying space
B{\rm Diff}(M)^{\delta}
classifies
M
-bundles with discrete structure group, that is, with a foliation on the total space over
X
that is transverse to the fibers. The cohomology groups
H^{*}(B{\rm Diff}(M)^{\delta};{\mathcal{A}})
, for a coefficient group
{\mathcal{A}}
, give invariants for foliated
M
-bundles, which is one motivation for the study of the topological properties of the spaces
B{\rm Diff}(M)^{\delta}
.

The inclusion map
\iota_{\delta}\colon{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta}\to{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)
induces a map of the classifying spaces, which has a homotopy theoretic fiber
B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}
, and there is a fibration

(1)

B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota_{\delta}}}{{\longrightarrow}}B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)\ .

The space
B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}
classifies foliations on a product space
X\times M
which are transverse to the factor
M
, and are a product outside of a compact subset of
X\times M
.
The cohomology of the fiber space
B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}
is viewed as comparing the cohomology of the group
{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)
equipped with two topologies, one continuous and the other discrete (see
[
111
,
128
]
.)
The following is the most basic problem, which remains for the most part unsolved:

PROBLEM 1.1
.

Calculate the groups

H_{*}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)};{\mathcal{A}})
and
H_{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathcal{A}})
, and the map between them, for
M
a connected oriented manifold, and
{\mathcal{A}}
an abelian group.

The direct approach to the study of
H_{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathbb{Q}})
is to construct a group action of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
on
M
, that is, a homomorphism
\Phi\colon\Gamma\to{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)
, and show that the induced map
\Phi_{*}\colon H_{*}(B\Gamma;{\mathbb{Q}})\to H_{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathbb{Q}})
has non-trivial classes in its image. The celebrated work of Thurston in
[
135
]
did this for the case
M={\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. The various constructions for higher dimensional manifolds described in Section
6
also use this approach for
M={\mathbb{S}}^{q}
a higher dimensional sphere, and for a few other cases where
M
is a homogeneous space. There are very few other examples where this approach has been carried through.
Nariman considered in
[
115
]
the case where
M=G
is a connected Lie group, and related
H_{2}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(G)^{\delta};{\mathbb{Q}})
with the algebraic K-theory group
K_{2}({\mathbb{C}})^{+}
.

Another approach to Problem
1.1
is to study the spectral sequence associated to the fibration (
1
), which relates the cohomology groups
H^{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M);{\mathcal{A}})
and
H^{*}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)};{\mathcal{A}})
with the groups
H^{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathcal{A}})
.
Nariman studied this spectral sequence in
[
115
]
, with a focus on the induced map on cohomology
\iota_{\delta}^{*}\colon H^{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M);{\mathbb{Q}})\to H^{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathbb{Q}})
.

The approach in this work uses the Mather-Thurston Theorem
3.2
to reformulate the problem in terms of the homotopy groups of a space associated to
{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta}
.
The works of Mather
[
94
,
95
]
and Thurston
[
136
]
show that there is a map
\tau\colon B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}\to{\mathcal{S}}(M)
which induces isomorphisms in homology, where
{\mathcal{S}}(M)
is the space of compactly supported sections of a bundle with fiber the classifying space
B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
of framed smooth codimension-q foliations.
The following notion is key to our study of Problem
1.1
:

DEFINITION 1.2
.

The algebraic K-theory groups of
M
are defined as

(2)

{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)=\pi_{*}\left(\left\{B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}\right\}^{+}\right)\cong\pi_{*}\left({\mathcal{S}}(M)\right)\ .

Here,
\displaystyle\left\{B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)_{\delta}\right\}^{+}
denotes the space obtained by applying the Quillen +-construction. These are called algebraic K-theory groups, in analogy with the definition of the algebraic K-theory groups of a ring
[
143
]
.
We denote these groups by
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)
to distinguish them from the usual K-theory of
M
denoted by
K^{*}(M)
, and the K-homology denoted by
K_{*}(M)
.

For
M={\mathbb{R}}^{q}
, one has
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})\cong\Omega^{q}B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
is a
q
-fold
based loop
space, and
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})\cong\pi_{q+n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
. These groups are absolutely enormous, as seen for example in Theorem
7.4
. The Mather-Thurston Theorem
3.2
says there is an isomorphism
H_{*}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})};{\mathbb{Z}})\cong H_{*}({\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{q});{\mathbb{Z}})
, and as
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})
is an H-space, its rational homology is a free graded algebra over the generating set
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

For
M={\mathbb{S}}^{q}
, one has
{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{S}}^{q})\cong\Lambda^{q}B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
is a
q
-fold
free loop
space, so
{\mathcal{K}}_{n}({\mathbb{S}}^{q})\cong\pi_{n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})\oplus\pi_{q+n}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
. There is an inclusion of the based loops into the free loops,
i\colon\Omega^{q}B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}\to\Lambda^{q}B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
which induces an inclusion map
i_{*}\colon{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbb{R}}^{q})\to{\mathcal{K}}({\mathbb{S}}^{q})
. The calculation of the homology groups
H_{*}({\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{S}}^{q});{\mathcal{A}})
is no longer straightforward, even with rational coefficients
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathbb{Q}}
, as it requires unknown properties of the homotopy theory of
B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
to calculate the differentials in the minimal model for the space. Haefliger describes the construction of this model in
[
51
]
.

For
M
a connected orientable manifold in general, the approach to studying
{\mathcal{K}}(M)
and
H_{*}({\mathcal{S}}(M);{\mathcal{A}})
using minimal models encounters even more difficulties.

The strategy of our approach to Problem
1.1
is to construct elements of
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)
which pair non-trivially with cohomology classes in
H^{*}({\mathcal{S}}(M);{\mathbb{R}})
that are derived from the secondary characteristic classes in
H^{*}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{R}})
. This is used to show that their images under the Hurewicz homomorphism
h\colon{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)\to H_{*}({\mathcal{S}}(M);{\mathbb{Q}})\cong H_{*}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}(M)};{\mathbb{Q}})
are non-trivial. Additional arguments in special cases are used to show that some of these classes then map non-trivially into
H_{*}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M)^{\delta};{\mathbb{Q}})
.

This method yields uncountably many independent cycles in
H_{*}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)};{\mathbb{Q}})
, each of which is represented by a homomorphism
\rho\colon\Gamma\to{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)
of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
, as discussed in Section
8
. The constructions reveal no information on the group
\Gamma
or how it acts on
M
. In fact, Freedman estimates in
[
32
]
that using the approach to the proof of the Mather-Thurston Theorem via foliated surgery in Meigniez
[
97
]
, the number of generators for
\Gamma
can be “exponentially large”.

The results obtained by the approach using the secondary invariants of foliations depend upon the dimension
q
and the topology of
M
, and are given in detail in later sections of this work. We formulate here some general results, as an overview of the results obtained.

.

be a smooth connected orientable manifold of dimension

which tend to infinity as

, and for

, linear functionals
\phi_{i}\colon{\mathcal{K}}_{q+1}(M)\to{\mathbb{R}}
, such that the vector-valued homomorphism

(3)

\vec{\phi}=(\phi_{1},\ldots,\phi_{v_{q}})\colon{\mathcal{K}}_{q+1}(M)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\ \longrightarrow\ {\mathbb{R}}^{v_{q}}

is a surjection when restricted to the rational subspace
{\mathbb{V}}_{q}\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
.

COROLLARY 1.4
.

Let
M
be a smooth connected orientable manifold of dimension

2.
Foliation classifying spaces

We give an overview of the definitions and basic properties of the classifying spaces for foliations introduced by Haefliger in
[
49
,
50
]
. All foliations and maps are assumed to be smooth.

Let
\Gamma_{q}
denote the pseudogroup generated by local
C^{\infty}
-diffeomorphisms of
{\mathbb{R}}^{q}
. A codimension
q
-foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
on
M
, along with a good open covering of a paracompact manifold
M
, defines a 1-cocycle with values in
\Gamma_{q}
.
Haefliger observed in
[
49
]
that there is a classifying space
B\Gamma_{q}
for these
\Gamma_{q}
-cocycles, so
that the foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
induces a continuous map
\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}}\colon M\to B\Gamma_{q}
whose homotopy class is independent of the choice of the good covering. One says that
\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}}
is the classifying map for
{\mathcal{F}}
.
The Jacobian map of a local diffeomorphism induces a universal map
\nu\colon B\Gamma_{q}\to B{\rm O}(q)
, which classifies the normal bundle on the universal foliated bundle over
B\Gamma_{q}
.

There are alternate models for the classifying space
B\Gamma_{q}
which are all weak homotopy equivalent, by Greenberg
[
46
]
, Jekel
[

]
, McDuff
[

]
, Moerdijk
[

]
, and Segal
[

]
.
Any of these models suffice for “classifying” foliations of codimension-q on paracompact manifolds.

The work by Bott, Shulman and Stasheff
[
14
]
(see also the text by Dupont
[

]
)
constructed a semi-simplicial model for
B\Gamma_{q}
and a semi-simplicial de Rham complex of forms whose cohomology groups are isomorphic to

. The existence of a de Rham complex for

allows one to use de Rham homotopy methods in the study of the topology of the space, as for example in

]
.

There are various alternate subclasses of groupoids that arise for foliations with some additional structure, as discussed by Haefliger in

. We consider here

COROLLARY 3.4
.

The space
{\mathcal{S}}(M)
is simply connected.

Let

Given a smooth connected manifold

We thus have the equality stated in Definition

{\mathcal{K}}_{\ell}(M)\ =\ \pi_{\ell}\left(\left\{B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)}\right\}^{+}\right)\cong\pi_{\ell}({\mathcal{S}}(M))\ ,\ {\rm for\ all}\ \ell\geq 1\ .

We thus can formulate the problem addressed in this work:

PROBLEM 3.5
.

For a smooth connected manifold

of dimension

, construct classes which map to non-zero classes in the image of the Hurewicz map

We will first introduce techniques for constructing non-trivial classes in

are uncountable for an infinite range of

Thus, the refined strategy for constructing non-trivial classes in

to first construct elements of

4.
Calculations of the K-theory groups

We first make some general remarks about the calculation of the groups
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)

A major difficulty with the study of

The case when

We now give some methods for analyzing the space

5.
Secondary characteristic classes

The techniques in the previous section for constructing classes in
{\mathcal{K}}_{*}(M)
assume there is a spherical class
\phi\in H^{n}_{\pi}(B\Gamma_{q}^{+};{\mathbb{R}})
.
The secondary classes for smooth foliations provides the canonical (and essentially only) approach to showing the existence of spherical classes for
B\Gamma_{q}^{+}
for degrees
n>2q
. We give a brief introduction to their construction. Lawson in his survey
[
88
]
gives a more extensive discussion of the secondary invariants and their properties.

The normal bundle
Q
to a smooth-foliation
{\mathcal{F}}
, when restricted to a leaf
L_{x}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
, has a natural flat connection
\nabla^{L_{x}}
defined by the leafwise parallel transport on
Q
restricted to
L_{x}
. An
adapted connection

\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
on
Q\to M
is any connection whose restrictions to leaves equals this natural flat connection. An adapted connection need not be flat over
M
. The connection data provided by
\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
can be thought of as a “linearization” of the normal structure to
{\mathcal{F}}
along the leaves. Thus,
\nabla^{{\mathcal{F}}}
captures aspects of the data provided by the Haefliger groupoid
\Gamma_{{\mathcal{F}}}^{r}
of
{\mathcal{F}}
– it is a “partial linearization” of the highly nonlinear data which defines the homotopy type of
B\Gamma_{{\mathcal{F}}}
. In this section, we discuss the applications of this partial linearization to the study of the space
B\Gamma_{q}
.

The seminal observation was made by Bott around 1970. The cohomology ring
H^{*}(BO(q);{\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{R}}[p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}]
where
2k\leq q
, and
p_{j}
has graded degree
4j
.

(Bott Vanishing
[
9
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a codimension-

,

The key observation is that the Bott Vanishing Theorem holds at the level of the differential forms representing the characteristic classes of the normal bundle, and not just for their cohomology classes.

Denote by

6.
Foliated manifolds with non-trivial secondary classes

We survey the literature on examples of foliated manifolds which are used to show the non-triviality of some of the universal classes in
H^{*}(B\Gamma_{q};{\mathbb{R}})
and
H^{*}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{R}})

First, we recall the case for

where there
H^{3}(WO_{1})\cong{\mathbb{R}}
is generated by the class
y_{1}c_{1}

(Godbillon-Vey

]

Let

is closed, and its cohomology class

GV({\mathcal{F}})\in H^{3}(M;{\mathbb{R}})

depends only on the concordance class of
{\mathcal{F}}
.

The paper
[

]
also included an example by Roussarie to show this class was non-zero. Soon afterwards, Thurston gave a construction in

(Thurston

]

The Godbillon-Vey class is a surjection

GV\colon\pi_{3}(B\Gamma_{1})\to{\mathbb{R}}

In this section, we give a review the known results about the spherical secondary classes for the spaces

and

First, we give the prototypical construction of spherical cohomology classes.

THEOREM 7.1
.

Let
\phi\in H^{2q+1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{R}})
be a cohomology class such that there exists a continuous family of framed of codimension-q foliations
\{({\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda},s_{\lambda})\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}

\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}^{*}(\phi)\in H^{2q+1}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
is non-trivial, and varies continuously with
\lambda

\Lambda

\lambda\in\Lambda
, there exists
[f_{\lambda}]\in\pi_{2q+1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
so that the pairing

\langle\phi,[f_{\lambda}]\rangle
is non-vanishing and varies continuously with
\lambda
. That is,
\phi
induces a map

\phi\colon\pi_{2q+1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})\to{\mathbb{R}}
which is surjective.

Proof.

First note that as the evaluation map
\phi\colon H_{2q+1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a homomorphism, if it is onto an open set, then it is onto all of
{\mathbb{R}}
.

Now let
\{F_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\Lambda\}
be a continuous family of codimension-q foliations on
Y
. Suppose that the normal bundles to the foliations admit trivializations

that depend continuously on
\lambda
. Then there exists a continuous family of classifying maps for these foliations, denoted by

\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}\colon Y\to B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
, where we suppress the dependence on the framing
s_{\lambda}
. Let
\phi\in H^{2q+1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q};{\mathbb{R}})
be a cohomology class such that the pull-back
\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}^{*}(\phi)\in H^{\ell}(Y;{\mathbb{R}})
is non-trivial for
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

Since
Y
has the homotopy type of a CW complex, there is a CW complex
Y_{(q+1)}
with all cells in dimension at least
q+2
, and a map
\xi\colon Y\to Y_{(q+1)}
such that the map
H_{2q+1}(Y;{\mathbb{Z}})\to H_{2q+1}(Y_{(q+1)};{\mathbb{Z}})
is injective. The space
Y_{(q+1)}
is obtained by collapsing the cells of dimension less than
q+2
in a CW decomposition of
Y
. As a consequence of Theorem
3.3
and basic obstruction theory, the classifying maps
\chi_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}
descend to a family of maps

\overline{\chi}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}\colon Y_{(q+1)}\to B\overline{\Gamma}_{q}
such that
\overline{\chi}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}^{*}(\phi)\in H^{2q+1}(Y_{(q+1)};{\mathbb{R}})
is non-trivial for
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

By the Rational Hurewicz Theorem

as applied to the space
Y_{(q+1)}
for

, the map

is an isomorphism. The class
\overline{\chi}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}^{*}(\phi)\in H^{2q+1}(Y_{(q+1)};{\mathbb{R}})
is non-vanishing, so there exists a cycle
[Z]\in H_{2q+1}(Y_{(q+1)},{\mathbb{Z}})
such that the pairing
\langle\overline{\chi}_{{\mathcal{F}}_{\lambda}}^{*}(\phi),[Z]\rangle\neq 0
. Moreover, if
\Lambda
does not consists of isolated points, the evaluation pairing varies continuously with the parameter

, so

Choose a map

By the Bott Vanishing Theorem,

, using a theory of residues for the Pontrjagin classes.

Haefliger observed in the note
[
52
]
the following more general formulation. Suppose there is given a collection of forms
\{\phi_{i}\mid i\in{\mathcal{I}}\}\subset\Omega^{*}_{deR}(B\Gamma_{q}^{+})

which satisfy

for

are rationally independent. Choose a collection of maps
\{f_{i}\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{n_{i}}\to B\Gamma_{q}^{+}\mid i\in{\mathcal{I}},n_{i}=\deg(\phi^{i})\}
for which their classes are independent when restricted to the homology classes they determine. Then for the induced map on their wedge product,

(24)

\widehat{f}\colon X=\bigvee_{i\in{\mathcal{I}}}\ {\mathbb{S}}^{n_{i}}\to B\Gamma_{q}^{+}\ ,

the induced map in rational homotopy theory,

have the structure of a free graded Lie algebra, with multiplication given by the Whitehead product, and this space is infinite dimensional if the index set

is not a singleton.
Note that the rational Hurewicz homomorphism
h_{*}\colon\pi_{*}(B\Gamma_{q}^{+})\to H_{*}(B\Gamma_{q}^{+};{\mathbb{Q}})
maps all of these Whitehead product classes to zero.

Both of the observations, by Schweitzer and Whitman and by Haefliger, are subsumed by the author’s thesis work

,

We briefly recall some aspects of this approach.

Recall that a model for a differential graded algebra

(over
{\mathbb{Q}}
or
{\mathbb{R}}
) is a DGA map
\psi\colon{\mathfrak{M}}\to{\mathcal{A}}

where

is a free DGA and the map

induces an isomorphism on cohomology groups. Then
{\mathfrak{M}}
is a minimal model for
{\mathcal{A}}
if it is the minimal such DGA. Minimal models exist under some basic restrictions. Let
{\mathfrak{M}}({\mathcal{A}})\to{\mathcal{A}}
denote a minimal model for
{\mathcal{A}}
.

For a semi-simplicial space
X
, let
\Omega^{*}_{deR}(X)
denote the DGA of semi-simplicial forms on
X
, and let
{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
denote a minimal model for
\Omega^{*}_{deR}(X)
. Let
\pi^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}(X))
denote the space of indecomposable elements in
{\mathfrak{M}}(X)
.
A basic result of minimal model theory is that there is a natural isomorphism
\pi^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}(X))\cong{\rm Hom}(\pi_{*}(X),{\mathbb{R}})
so that the space
\pi^{*}({\mathfrak{M}}(X))
consists of “dual homotopy” classes.

The map (
17
) defined by the Strong Bott Vanishing Theorem
5.3
is natural, so induces a DGA map

(25)

\Delta\colon I_{q}\longrightarrow\Omega_{deR}^{*}(B\Gamma^{+}_{q})\ ,

where recall that we defined

to be the truncated polynomial algebra. This determines a map on minimal models

which induces a map on dual homotopy, denoted by

\Delta^{\#}\colon\pi^{*}(I_{q})\to{\rm Hom}(\pi_{*}(B\Gamma^{+}_{q}),{\mathbb{R}})
.
The classes in the image of

are called the
dual homotopy invariants
of foliations in
[

]
. For example, corresponding to the indecomposable element of

defined by the Pontrjagin class

In this section we recall these results, and the relation to the techniques developed in previous sections.

Recall that
B\overline{\Gamma}_{1}=B\Gamma_{1}^{+}

[

]

By Thurston
[

the Godbillon-Vey class is spherical, hence one has:

PROPOSITION 9.1
.

There exists a collection of maps
\{f_{i}\colon{\mathbb{S}}^{3}\to B\Gamma_{1}^{+}\mid i\in{\mathcal{I}}\}
where
{\mathcal{I}}
is an uncountable set, and their classes

The Godbillon-Vey class defines a surjection

GV\colon\pi_{3}(B\Gamma_{1}^{+})\cong H^{3}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}({\mathbb{R}})^{\delta};{\mathbb{Z}})\to{\mathbb{R}}

of
{\mathbb{R}}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}

choose a map

\langle GV,[f_{i}]\rangle=a_{i}
.
∎

The next result follows using standard properties of homology and H-spaces.

THEOREM 9.2

The cohomology ring

\pi_{2}(\Omega B\Gamma_{1}^{+})\cong H_{2}(\Omega B\Gamma_{1}^{+};{\mathbb{Z}})

The analysis of the homology and cohomology groups of the spaces

and

The first difference is that the spaces of secondary invariants,

\displaystyle H^{*>4}(WO_{2})

\displaystyle\bigwedge(y_{1}c_{1}^{2},\ y_{1}c_{2})\ ,\ |y_{1}c_{1}^{2}|=|y_{1}c_{2}|=5

\displaystyle H^{*}(W_{2})

\displaystyle\bigwedge(y_{1}c_{1}^{2},\ y_{1}c_{2},\ y_{2}c_{2},\ y_{1}y_{2}c_{2})\ ,\ |y_{1}c_{1}^{2}|=|y_{1}c_{2}|=5,\ |y_{2}c_{2}|=7,\ |y_{1}y_{2}c_{2}|=8\ .

[

\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}\subset H^{3}(B{\rm Diff}_{c}^{+}(M);{\mathbb{R}})

H^{4}(B\overline{{\rm Diff}_{c}(M)};{\mathbb{R}})

The class
\Delta(y_{2}c_{2})\in H^{7}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{2};{\mathbb{R}})

[

\Delta(y_{1}y_{2}c_{1}^{2})\in H^{8}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{2};{\mathbb{R}})

\pi_{8}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{2})\to H_{8}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{2};{\mathbb{Z}})

The second difference is that for

We recall the known facts about the spherical cohomology groups
H_{\pi}^{*}(B\Gamma_{2}^{+};{\mathbb{R}})

Let
\widehat{\nu}\colon B\overline{{\rm Diff}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to B\overline{\Gamma}_{2}

\widehat{\nu}

A.2

PROPOSITION 10.1

(39)

{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

Appendix A
Basic homotopy techniques

We recall some basic results from homotopy theory that are frequently used in our constructions.

A.1.
Hurewicz Theorems

The Hurewicz Theorem relates the homotopy and homology groups.

THEOREM A.1
.

Let
X
be an
r
-connected space for
r\geq 1
. That is,
X
is a simply connected space with
\pi_{k}(X)=0
for
0\leq k\leq r
. Then the Hurewicz homomorphism
h_{*}\colon\pi_{k}(X)\to H_{k}(X;{\mathbb{Z}})
is an isomorphism for
0<k\leq r+1
.

The Rational Hurewicz Theorem relates
\pi_{*}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}
with the groups
H_{*}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})
.
Klaus and Kreck give an elementary proof of this result in
[
84
]
, and provide additional interesting discussions on this fundamental theorem.

THEOREM A.2
.

Let
X
be a simply connected space with
\pi_{k}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}=0
for
0\leq k\leq r
. Then the Hurewicz map induces an isomorphism

(67)

h\colon\pi_{k}(X)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}\longrightarrow H_{k}(X;{\mathbb{Q}})

for
1\leq k\leq 2r
, and a surjection for
k=2r+1
.

A.2.
Integration over the fiber

The operation of “integration over the fiber” is often used in the context of sphere bundles, but it applies much more generally.
It was first introduced in a paper by Chern
[
23
]
, and stated more formally by Borel and Hirzebruch in
[
8
, Section 8]
, where they explain the operation in terms of the differential on the
E_{2}
-term of the spectral sequence associated to the fibration. This definition was related to the definition using differential forms by Auer
[
1
]
. Bott and Tu discuss integration over the fiber for vector bundles in
[
16
, Section 6]
.

THEOREM A.3
.

Let
p\colon E\to B
be a fibration with fiber
X=p^{-1}(b)
, where
B
is connected and
X
is a compact oriented manifold of dimension
q
. Then for
n\geq q
, there exists a natural homomorphism

(68)

\int_{X}\ \colon H^{n}(E;{\mathbb{R}})\to H^{n-q}(B;{\mathbb{R}})\ ,

called
integration over the fiber
.
For
\alpha\in H^{n}(E;{\mathbb{R}})
and
\omega\in H^{\ell}(B;{\mathbb{R}})
,
\omega\cdot\int_{X}(\alpha)=\int_{X}(p^{*}\omega\cdot\alpha)
.

A.3.
Brace products

The
brace product
was introduced by James in
[
76
]
.

PROPOSITION A.4
.

Let

\displaystyle F\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}E\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\longrightarrow}}B

be a fibration, and suppose there exists a section
\sigma\colon B\to E
.
Then there is a well-defined product, called the
brace product
,

(69)

\{\cdot,\cdot,\}_{\sigma}\colon\pi_{i}(E)\times\pi_{j}(F)\to\pi_{i+j-1}(F)\ .

where the subscript
\sigma
indicates that the product depends upon the choice of the section
\sigma
.

Proof.

Given classes
\alpha\in\pi_{i}(E)
and
\beta\in\pi_{j}(F)
the Whitehead product

[\sigma_{\#}(\alpha),\iota_{\#}(\beta)]\in\pi_{i+j-1}(E_{q})
is the image of a unique class denoted
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\sigma}\in\pi_{i+j-1}(B\overline{\Gamma}_{q})
.
∎

The brace product vanishes when the space
E
is a product fibration, so the non-triviality of a pair
\{\alpha,\beta\}_{\sigma}
is a measure of the “homotopy twisting” of the fibration. Kallel and Sjerve
[
78
]
relate the brace product to the structure of free loop space
\Lambda E
.

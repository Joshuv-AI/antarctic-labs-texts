Harmonic vector fields on extended 3-dimensional Riemannian Lie groups
F. Koudjo, E. Loubeau, L. Todjihounde

Abstract
Given two Riemannian manifolds ( B , g B ) (B,g_{B}) and ( F , g F ) (F,g_{F}) , we give harmonicity conditions for vector fields on the Riemannian warped product B × f F B\times_{f}F , with f : B ⟶ ] 0 , + ∞ [ f:B\longrightarrow]0,+\infty[ , using a characteristic variational condition. Then, we apply this to the case B = ℝ B=\mathbb{R} and F F is a three-dimensional connected Riemannian Lie group G G equipped with a left-invariant metric, to determine harmonic vector fields on ℝ × f G \mathbb{R}\times_{f}G . We give examples of harmonic vector fields on G G which are not left-invariant and determine harmonic vector fields on ℝ × f G \mathbb{R}\times_{f}G . We conclude with some examples of vector fields on ℝ × f G \mathbb{R}\times_{f}G which are harmonic maps.



Keywords
: Harmonic vector fields, harmonic maps, tangent sphere bundle, Riemannian Lie groups, warped products.

Preliminaries

2.1
The tangent bundle and the unit tangent sphere
bundle

Let
(M,g)
be an
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\nabla

the associated Levi-Civita connection. Its Riemann curvature tensor

R
is defined by

R(X,Y)Z=\nabla_{X}\nabla_{Y}Z-\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z

for all vector fields
X,Y
and
Z
on
M
. The tangent bundle of
(M,g)
, denoted by
TM
, consists of
pairs
(x,u)
where
x
is a point in
M
and
u
a tangent vector
to
M
at
x
. The mapping
\pi:TM\rightarrow M:(x,u)\mapsto x
is
the natural projection from
TM
onto
M
. The tangent space
T_{(x,u)}TM
at a point
(x,u)
in
TM
is the direct sum of the vertical subspace
\mathcal{V}_{(x,u)}=Ker(d\pi|_{(x,u)})
and the horizontal subspace
\mathcal{H}_{(x,u)}
, with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
of
(M,g)
:

T_{(x,u)}TM=\mathcal{H}_{(x,u)}\oplus\mathcal{V}_{(x,u)}.

For any vector
w\in T_{x}M
, there exists a unique vector
w^{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{(x,u)}
at the point
(x,u)\in TM
, called the

horizontal lift
of
w
to
(x,u)
, such that
d\pi(w^{h})=w

and a unique vector
w^{v}\in\mathcal{V}_{(x,u)}
, the

vertical lift
of
w
to
(x,u)
, such that
w^{v}(df)=w(f)

for all functions
f
on
M
. Hence, every tangent vector

\bar{w}\in T_{(x,u)}TM
can be decomposed as

\bar{w}=w_{1}^{h}+w_{2}^{v}
for uniquely determined vectors

w_{1},w_{2}\in T_{x}M
. The
horizontal
(resp.

vertical
)
lift
of a vector field
X
on
M
to
TM
is
the vector field
X^{h}
(resp.
X^{v}
) on
TM
whose value
at the point
(x,u)
is the horizontal (respectively, vertical)
lift of
X_{x}
to
(x,u)
.

The tangent bundle
TM
of a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
can be
endowed in a natural way with a Riemannian metric
g_{S}
, the

Sasaki metric
, depending only on the Riemannian structure
g

of the base manifold
M
. It is uniquely determined by

\begin{array}[]{ll}g_{S}(X^{h},Y^{h})=g_{S}(X^{v},Y^{v})=g(X,Y)\circ\pi,&g_{S}(X^{h},Y^{v})=0\\
\end{array}

(2.1)

for all vector fields
X
and
Y
on
M
. More intuitively, the
metric
g_{S}
is constructed in such a way that the vertical and
horizontal subbundles are orthogonal and the bundle map
\pi:(TM,g_{S})\mapsto(M,g)
is a Riemannian submersion.
We denote by
\mathfrak{X}(M)
the set of globally defined vector fields on the base manifold
(M,g)
. In the sequel, we concentrate on the map
V:(M,g)\rightarrow(TM,g_{S})
(resp.
V:(M,g)\rightarrow(T_{1}M,g_{S})
). The tension field
\tau_{1}(V)
of
V:(M,g)\rightarrow(TM,g_{S})
is given by
[
7
]

\tau(V)=(-S(V))^{h}+(-\bar{\Delta}V)^{v},

(2.2)

where
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
is a local orthonormal frame field of
(M,g)
,
\displaystyle S(V)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{R(\nabla_{e_{i}}{V},V)e_{i}}
and

\displaystyle\bar{\Delta}V=\nabla^{*}\nabla V=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\{\nabla_{\nabla_{e_{i}}{e_{i}}}V-\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}V\}
. Consequently,
V
defines a harmonic map from
(M,g)
to
(TM,g_{s})
if and only if

\displaystyle\tr[R(\nabla_{.}V,V).]=0\quad\text{ and}\quad\nabla^{*}\nabla V=0.

(2.3)

A smooth vector field
V
is said to be a harmonic section if and only if it is a critical point of
E^{v}
where
E^{v}
is the vertical energy. The corresponding Euler-Lagrange equation is given by

\nabla^{*}\nabla V=0.

2.2
Warped Products

Let
(B^{m},g_{B})
and
(F^{n},g_{F})
be Riemannian manifolds with
f:B\longrightarrow\text{ }]0,+\infty[
a smooth function on B. The warped product
M=B\times_{f}F
is the product
manifold
B\times F
equipped with the metric

g=\pi^{*}(g_{B})\oplus(f\circ\pi)^{2}\sigma^{*}(g_{F})
;
where
\pi:M\longrightarrow B
and
\sigma:M\longrightarrow F
are the usual projections. Then

(B,g_{B})
is called the base,
(F,g_{F})
is the fiber and
f
the warping function of the warped product,
\pi^{-1}(p)=\{p\}\times F
are the fibers and
\sigma^{-1}(q)=B\times\{q\}
the leaves. The vectors tangent to leaves are called horizontal and those tangent to the fibers vertical, hence

\displaystyle T_{(p,q)}(B\times F)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{(p,q)}(\{p\}\times F)\oplus T_{(p,q)}(B\times\{q\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{(p,q)}(\{p\}\times F)\oplus T_{(p,q)}(\{p\}\times F)^{\perp}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{(p,q)}(B\times\{q\})^{\perp}\oplus T_{(p,q)}(B\times\{q\})

for all
(p,q)\in M
.

Vector fields
X
on
B\times F
are horizontal if

d\pi_{(p,q)}(X)=X_{p}\quad\text{and }\quad d\sigma_{(p,q)}(X)=0

that is

X\in T_{(p,q)}(B\times\{q\})^{\perp}\quad\text{and }\quad d\pi_{(p,q)}(X)=X_{p}.

Vector fields
X
on
B\times F
are vertical if

d\pi_{(p,q)}(X)=0\quad\text{and }\quad d\sigma_{(p,q)}(X)=X_{q}

that is

X\in T_{(p,q)}(\{p\}\times F)^{\perp}\quad\text{and }\quad d\sigma_{(p,q)}(X)=X_{p}.

If
X\in T_{p}B
and
q\in F
then the horizontal lift of
X
to
(p,q)
is the unique vector
X^{*}
in
T_{(p,q)}(B\times F)
such that

d\pi_{(p,q)}(X^{*})=X_{p}\quad\text{and }\quad d\sigma_{(p,q)}(X)=0

If
X\in T_{p}(F)
and
q\in F
then the vertical lift of
X
to
(p,q)
is the unique vector
X^{*}
in
T_{(p,q)}(B\times F)
such that

d\pi_{(p,q)}(X)=0\quad\text{and }\quad d\sigma_{(p,q)}(X)=X_{q}

(
[
14
]
)
.

Let
(B^{m},g_{B})
and
(F^{n},g_{F})
be Riemannian manifolds with

be vector fields on

X_{2},Y_{2}

,

and

1.

\grad^{M}(f\circ\pi)=(\grad^{B}(f),0)
;

2.

\nabla_{(X_{1},0)}(Y_{1},0)=(\nabla^{B}_{X_{1}}Y_{1},0)
;

3.

\nabla_{(0,X_{2})}(0,Y_{2})=(0,\nabla^{F}_{X_{2}}Y_{2})-fg^{F}(X_{2},Y_{2})\grad^{B}(f\circ\pi)\\
\\
\text{ }\quad\text{ }\quad\text{ }\quad\qquad=(0,\nabla^{F}_{X_{2}}Y_{2})-\dfrac{1}{f}g((0,X_{2}),(0,Y_{2}))\grad^{B}(f\circ\pi)

\nabla_{(0,X_{2})}(Y_{1},0)=\dfrac{Y_{1}(f)}{f}(0,X_{2})

\nabla_{(X_{1},0)}(0,Y_{2})=\dfrac{X_{1}(f)}{f}(0,Y_{2})

\grad^{M}(h\circ\sigma)=\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}(0,\grad^{F}h)

3
Harmonic vector fields on warped products

In this section, we determine the harmonicity conditions for vector fields on the warped product
M=B\times_{f}F
with
f:B\longrightarrow]0,+\infty[
.

Let
\{e^{\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1_{,\cdots,}m}
be an orthonomal basis of
(B,g_{B})
and
\{e^{\prime\prime}_{i}\}_{i=1_{,\cdots,}n}
an orthonomal basis of
(F,g_{F})
. Then
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1,_{,\cdots,}m+n}
is an orthonomal basis of
(M,g)
with
e_{i}=(e^{\prime}_{i},0)
for
i=1,_{\cdots},m
and

e_{i+m}=\dfrac{1}{f}(0,e^{\prime\prime}_{i})
for
i=1,_{\cdots},n
. Hence, for
i=1,_{\cdots},m\text{ }\text{ }

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}{e_{i}^{\prime}},0)

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)}{(V_{1},0)}+\nabla_{\left(e_{i}^{\prime},0\right)}{(0,V_{2})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\frac{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)(f)}{f}\left(0,V_{2}\right).

Moreover

\displaystyle(e_{i}^{\prime},0)f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g\bigg((\grad^{B}f,0),(e_{i}^{\prime},0)\bigg)=g_{B}(\grad^{B}f,e_{i}^{\prime})=e_{i}^{\prime}(f).

Hence, for
i=1,_{\cdots},m\text{ }\text{ }

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}V=\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}(f)}{f}\left(0,V_{2}\right).

so

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)}(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0)+\nabla_{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)}\left(\dfrac{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)(f)}{f}(0,V_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}(f)}{f}\nabla_{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)}(0,V_{2})+(e_{i}^{\prime},0)\left(\dfrac{(e_{i}^{\prime},0)f}{f}\right)\left(0,V_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}(f)^{2}}{f^{2}}\left(0,V_{2}\right)+\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}(f)}{f}\left(0,V_{2}\right)-\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}(f)^{2}}{f^{2}}\left(0,V_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\dfrac{e_{i}^{\prime}e_{i}^{\prime}(f)}{f}\left(0,V_{2}\right)

and

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nabla_{e_{i}}e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nabla_{\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime},0\right)}(V_{1},0)+\nabla_{\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime},0\right)}(0,V_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\nabla^{B}_{\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime}}V_{1},0\right)+\dfrac{\left(\nabla^{B}_{e_{i}^{\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime}\right)(f)}{f}(0,V_{2}).

For

i=m+1,_{\cdots},m+n
, we have

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}{e_{i}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\dfrac{1}{f}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(\dfrac{1}{f}\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})+(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\big(\dfrac{1}{f}\big)(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}\left(\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\right)-\dfrac{1}{f}\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)

because

(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})(f)=g\left((\grad^{B}f,0),(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)=g_{B}(\grad^{B}f,0)+f^{2}g_{F}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})=0.

For
i=m+1,_{\cdots},m+n
, we compute

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(V_{1},V_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(V_{1},0)+\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(0,V_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-fg^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)+\dfrac{(V_{1},0)(f)}{f}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)+\dfrac{(V_{1},0)(f)}{f^{2}}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)+\dfrac{V_{1}(f)}{f^{2}}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime}).

therefore

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left[\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left(\dfrac{1}{f}(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2})\right)-\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left(g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})(\grad^{B}f,0)\right)\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left(\dfrac{(V_{1},0)f}{f^{2}}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)\right].

We now compute the previous terms and sum on
i=m+1,_{\cdots},m+n
:

i.)

\displaystyle\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left[\dfrac{1}{f}(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)+(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(\dfrac{1}{f}\right)\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left\{\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-fg^{F}(e_{i}^{\prime\prime},\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-g^{F}(e_{i}^{\prime\prime},\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right).

ii.)
\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left[g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})(\grad^{B}f,0)\right]\\

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\dfrac{(\grad^{B}f,0)(f)}{f}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})+(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\dfrac{(\grad^{B}f)(f)}{f}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})+e_{i}^{\prime\prime}\left(g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\grad^{B}f(f)}{f}(0,V_{2})+e_{i}^{\prime\prime}\left(g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right)\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)

iii.)

\displaystyle\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}\left[\dfrac{(V_{1},0)(f)}{f^{2}}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}\big[\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(V_{1},0)(f)\big](0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})+(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})\left(\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}(V_{1},0)(f)\right)(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}(V_{1},0)(f)\nabla_{(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})}(0,e_{i}^{\prime\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}V_{1}(f)\left((0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime\prime})-f(\grad^{B}f,0)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}V_{1}(f)(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime\prime})-\dfrac{n}{f}V_{1}(f)(\grad^{B}f,0)

Hence, gathering all the terms, and summing on
i=m+1,_{\cdots},m+n
, we obtain

\displaystyle\nabla_{e_{i}}\nabla_{e_{i}}V

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{f^{2}}\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2}\right)-\dfrac{1}{f}e_{i}^{\prime\prime}(g^{F}(V_{2},e_{i}^{\prime\prime}))\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)-\dfrac{\grad^{B}f(f)}{f^{2}}(0,V_{2})

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{f^{3}}V_{1}(f)\left(0,\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}e_{i}^{\prime\prime}\right)-n\dfrac{V_{1}(f)}{f^{2}}\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right)-\dfrac{1}{f}g^{F}(e_{i}^{\prime\prime},\nabla^{F}_{e_{i}^{\prime\prime}}V_{2})\left(\grad^{B}f,0\right),

Time-Reversal of Stochastic Maximum Principle
Amirhossein Taghvaei

Abstract
Stochastic maximum principle (SMP) specifies a necessary condition for the solution of a stochastic optimal control problem. The condition involves a coupled system of forward and backward stochastic differential equations (FBSDE) for the state and the adjoint processes. Numerical solution of the FBSDE is challenging because the boundary condition of the adjoint process is specified at the terminal time, while the solution should be adaptable to the forward in time filtration of a Wiener process. In this paper, a “time-reversal” of the FBSDE system is proposed that involves integration with respect to a backward in time Wiener process. The time-reversal is used to propose an iterative Monte-Carlo procedure to solves the FBSDE system and its time-reversal simultaneously. The procedure involves approximating the Föllmer’s drift and solving a regression problem between the state and its adjoint at each time. The procedure is illustrated for the linear quadratic (LQ) optimal control problem with a numerical example.

II-A

Backward stochastic integration

We follow the notation introduced in
[
24
, Sec. 2]
.
We use
W_{t}
to denote a standard
n
-dimensional Wiener process
which introduces the forward and backward filtrations

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}_{t}:=

\displaystyle\sigma(W_{s};0\leq s\leq t),\quad\cev{\mathcal{F}}_{t}:=\sigma(W_{T}-W_{s};t\leq s\leq T),

for
t\in[0,T]
(the backward filtration is increasing as
t\downarrow 0

For any continuous
{\cev{\mathcal{F}}}_{t}
-measurable process
\cev{V}_{t}
,
the backward stochastic integral

\displaystyle\int_{T-t}^{T}\cev{V}_{s}\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{s}:=-\int_{0}^{t}\cev{V}_{T-s}\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{s}

where the right-hand-side is the usual Itô integral with respect to the Wiener process
\tilde{W}_{t}:=W_{T-t}-W_{T}
. Numerically, the backward stochastic integral may be approximated as

\int_{T-t}^{T}\cev{V}_{s}\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{s}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\cev{V}_{T-k\Delta t}(W_{T-k\Delta t}-W_{T-(k+1)\Delta t})

where
\Delta t=\frac{t}{n}
.

We use the notation
\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{t}
to express stochastic differential equations (SDEs) that involve backward stochastic integration.
For example, a SDE of the form

\mathrm{d}\cev{X}_{t}=a(\cev{X}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\cev{X}_{t})\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{t},\quad\cev{X}_{T}=\xi,

(4)

is a short-hand notation for

\displaystyle\cev{X}_{T-t}

\displaystyle=\xi-\int_{T-t}^{T}a(\cev{X}_{s})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{T-t}^{T}\sigma(\cev{X}_{s})\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{s},~\forall t\in[0,T]

(5)

which defines a
{\cev{\mathcal{F}}}_{t}
-adapted solution
\cev{X}_{t}
.

Lemma 1

The SDE (
4
) is equivalent to the SDE

\mathrm{d}\tilde{X}_{t}=-a(\tilde{X}_{t})\mathrm{d}t+\sigma(\tilde{X}_{t})\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{t},\quad\tilde{X}_{0}=\xi,

(6)

where
\tilde{W}_{t}:=W_{T-t}-W_{T}
and
\tilde{X}_{t}:=\cev{X}_{T-t}
is adapted to the forward filtration
\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}:=\sigma\{\tilde{W}_{s};0\leq s\leq t\}
, for
t\in[0,T]
.

Proof:

Using
\tilde{X}_{t}=\cev{X}_{T-t}
in (
5
) yields

\displaystyle\tilde{X}_{t}

\displaystyle=\xi-\int_{T-t}^{T}a(\tilde{X}_{T-s})\mathrm{d}s-\int_{T-t}^{T}\sigma(\cev{X}_{s})\cev{\mathrm{d}}W_{s}

\displaystyle=\xi-\int_{0}^{t}a(\tilde{X}_{s})\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\tilde{X}_{s})\mathrm{d}\tilde{W}_{s},

concluding the forward in time SDE (
6
).
∎

II-B

Time reversal of SDE

Consider the two stochastic processes

IV
Linear quadratic setting

In this section, we consider the special case where the dynamic model is linear, the initial distribution is Gaussian, and the control cost is quadratic, i.e.

\displaystyle a(x,u)=Ax+Bu,\quad\sigma(x)=\sigma,\quad p_{0}=\mathcal{N}(m_{0},\Sigma_{0})

\displaystyle\ell(x,u)=\frac{1}{2}\left[x^{\top}Qx+u^{\top}Ru\right],\quad\ell_{f}(x)=\frac{1}{2}x^{\top}Q_{f}x

where

Q,Q_{f}\succeq
are assumed to be positive semi-definite,

R\succ 0
is assumed to be positive definite, and
\mathcal{N}(m,\Sigma)
denotes a Gaussian distribution with mean
m
and covariance
\Sigma
.
Under the LQ setting, the Hamiltonian (
3a
) becomes

H(x,u,y,z):=\frac{1}{2}\left[x^{\top}Qx+u^{\top}Ru\right]+y^{\top}(Ax+Bu)+\text{Tr}(\sigma^{\top}z),

concluding the FBSDE

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}

\displaystyle=AX_{t}\mathrm{d}t+BU_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\mathrm{d}W_{t},\quad X_{0}\sim p_{0}

(17a)

\displaystyle-\mathrm{d}Y_{t}

\displaystyle=A^{\top}Y_{t}\mathrm{d}t+QX_{t}\mathrm{d}t-Z_{t}\mathrm{d}W_{t},\quad Y_{t_{f}}=Q_{f}X_{t_{f}}.

(17b)

Moreover, the control input that minimizes the Hamiltonian, according to (
3d
), is equal to

U_{t}=-R^{-1}B^{\top}Y_{t}
.

We present the solution of the FBSDE system above for an affine form of the control according to

\displaystyle U_{t}=K_{1}(t)X_{t}+K_{2}(t)Y_{t}+K_{3}(t)Z_{t}+K_{4}(t)

(17c)

for some time-varying parameters
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
,
K_{3}
, and
K_{4}
with appropriate dimensions.

Proposition 2

Consider the FBSDE (
17a
)-(
17b
)
with affine form of control as in (

). Then, the FBSDE is solved with

and
Z_{t}=G_{1}(t)\sigma
where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}

\displaystyle+G_{1}(t)BK_{2}(t)G_{1}(t)+Q=0,\quad G_{1}(T)=Q_{f},

V
Numerical Algorithm

A numerical procedure is proposed to approximate the
solution to the FBSDE (
17a
)-(
17b
), its time-reversal (
19a
)-(
19b
), and the optimal control input. While the procedure is presented for the LQ setting, it is generalizable to the nonlinear case as explained in Remark
3
.

Initialization:

The algorithm begins with generating
N
independent samples
\{X^{i}_{0}\}_{i=1}^{N}
from the initial distribution
p_{0}=\mathcal{N}(m_{0},\Sigma_{0})
. Each sample
X^{i}_{0}
, for
i=1,\ldots,N
, is then paired with a zero initialization of the control inputs
U^{i,0}_{t}=\cev{U}^{i,0}_{t}=0
, and a random realization of the Weiner processes
W^{i}_{t}
and
\tilde{W}^{i}_{t}
, that will be used for the simulation of the FBSDE and its time-reversal, respectively.

Iterations:

The
k
-th iteration, for
k=1,2,3,\ldots,k_{f}
, involves the following steps:

1.

Forward simulations of (
17a
) according to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X^{i,k}_{t}\!=\!AX^{i,k}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+BU^{i,k-1}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\mathrm{d}W^{i}_{t},~X^{i,k}_{0}=X^{i}_{0}

(20a)

for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
t\in[0,T]
.

2.

Approximation of the Fölmer’s drift. For the LQ setting, this amounts to approximating the mean and covariance according to

\displaystyle m^{k}_{t}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}X^{i,k}_{t},~\Sigma^{k}_{t}\approx\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(X^{i,k}_{t}-m^{k}_{t})(X^{i,k}_{t}-m^{k}_{t})^{\top}.

3.

Simulation of time-reversed FBSDE (
19a
)-(
19b
) according to

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\cev{X}^{k,i}_{t}=A\cev{X}^{k,i}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+B\cev{U}^{i,k-1}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+\sigma\cev{\mathrm{d}}{\tilde{W}}^{i}_{t}

(20b)

\displaystyle+D(\Sigma^{k}_{t})^{-1}(\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t}-m^{k}_{t})\mathrm{d}t,~X^{i,k}_{T}\sim\mathcal{N}(m^{k}_{T},\Sigma^{k}_{T}),

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}\cev{Y}^{i,k}_{t}=A^{\top}\cev{Y}^{i,k}_{t}\mathrm{d}t+Q\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t}\mathrm{d}t-G_{1}^{k}(t)\sigma\cev{\mathrm{d}}\tilde{W}^{i}_{t}

(20c)

\displaystyle-G_{1}^{k}(t)D(\Sigma^{k}_{t})^{-1}(\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t}-m^{k}_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\quad Y^{i,k}_{T}=Q_{f}X^{i,k}_{T},

for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
t\in[0,T]
,
where

\displaystyle G_{1}^{k}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathop{\text{\rm arg\,min}}_{G}\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\cev{Y}^{i,k}_{t}-G\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t}\|^{2}.

(20d)

4.

Gradient descent update of the control to minimize the Hamiltonian according to

\displaystyle U^{i,k}_{t}

\displaystyle=U^{i,k-1}_{t}-\eta\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}(X^{i,k}_{t},U^{i,k-1}_{t},Y^{i,k}_{t},Z^{k}_{t}),

\displaystyle\cev{U}^{i,k}_{t}

\displaystyle=\cev{U}^{i,k-1}_{t}-\eta\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}(\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t},\cev{U}^{i,k-1}_{t},\cev{Y}^{i,k}_{t},\cev{Z}^{k}_{t}),

for a small step-size
\eta>0
, where
Z^{k}_{t}=\cev{Z}^{k}_{t}=G_{1}^{k}(t)\sigma
,
Y^{i,k}_{t}=G_{1}^{k}(t)X^{i,k}_{t}
, and
\frac{\partial H}{\partial u}(x,u,y,z)=Ru+B^{\top}y
.

Output:
At the end of the iterations, the samples
(X^{i,k_{f}}_{t},U^{i,k_{f}}_{t},G^{k_{f}}_{1}(t)X^{i,k_{f}}_{t},G^{k_{f}}_{1}(t)\sigma)_{i=1}^{N}

are expected to approximate the probability law of the quadruple
(\hat{X},\hat{U},\hat{Y},\hat{Z})
that satisfy the SMP conditions (
3b
)-(
3c
)-(
3d
) in the LQ setting.
A feedback control law is obtained according to
U_{t}=\mathop{\text{\rm arg\,min}}_{u}H(X_{t},u,Y_{t},Z_{t})=-R^{-1}B^{\top}Y_{t}\approx-R^{-1}B^{\top}G^{k_{f}}_{1}(t)X_{t}
, given a realization of the state process
X_{t}
.

Remark 3

In order to extend the procedure to the nonlinear setting, it is necessary to use a score function approximation algorithm in step 2, and solve a nonlinear regression problem

\min_{\phi}\,\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\cev{Y}^{i,k}_{t}-\phi(\cev{X}^{i,k}_{t})\|^{2}

to find
\phi^{k}(t,x)

for all
t\in[0,T]
in step 3. When
\phi^{k}(t,x)
is available, we can evaluate the Wasserstein derivative according to Lemma
2
and use
\cev{Z}_{t}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x}(t,\cev{X}_{t})\sigma(\cev{X}_{t})
to find
\cev{Z}_{t}
.

VI
Conclusion

This paper presents the time-reversal of the FBSDE that appears in stochastic maximum principle. The time-reversal is used to propose an iterative numerical algorithm to approximately solve the FBSDE and obtain the optimal control input. The algorithm is presented and numerically illustrated for the LQ setting. The extension to the nonlinear setting and subsequent comparison to the related algorithms is the subject of future research.

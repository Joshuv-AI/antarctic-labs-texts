Congruences for modular forms and applications to crank functions


Abstract
In this paper, motivated by the work of Mahlburg, we find congruences for a large class of modular forms. Moreover, we generalize the generating function of the Andrews-Garvan-Dyson crank on partition and establish several new infinite families of congruences. In this framework, we showed that both the birank of an ordered pair of partitions introduced by Hammond and Lewis, and k k -crank of k k -colored partition introduced by Fu and Tang process the same as the partition function and crank. AMS Classifications: 05A17, 11P83 Keywords: Modular form, Congruence, Partition function, Crank

Introduction

The objective of this paper is to present a modular form approach to deriving congruences of the crank-type generating functions.
This work was inspired by the key construction of
Mahlburg
[
12
]
that
the generating functions of crank is deeply related to Klein forms and weakly holomorphic modular forms of half-integral weight on the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{1}(l^{j})
.
This leads to arithmetic properties of the crank of partitions, which confirmed a conjecture of Ono
[
13
]
.

Recall that a partition of a nonnegative integer
n
is any nonincreasing sequence of positive integers whose sum is
n
.
As usual, let
p(n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
.
A breakthrough of applying modular forms to partition functions is due to Ono
[
14
]
.
Along with this direction,
the congruence properties for partition function are deeply investigated in the subsequential work .
For example, Ahlgren and Ono
[
1
]
showed that
for prime
\ell\geq 5
and
m>0
, there are infinitely many non-nested arithmetic progressions
An+B
such that for every integer
n
we have

p(An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{m}}.

See
[
6
,
8
,
16
]
for more results.

To give the combinatorial interpretations of the well-known Ramanujan’s congruences,
Dyson
[
5
]
conjectured the existence of such a statistic named crank,
and its definition was discovered by Andrews and Garvan
[
3
]
.
The crank of a partition was defined as the largest part if the partition contains no ones,
and otherwise as the number of parts larger than the number of ones minus the number
of ones.
For more details, please refer to Andrews and Garvan
[
3
,
9
]
.

In view of partition congruences,
Ono further conjectured that the crank functions
enjoy all of the partition congruences.
The work of Mahlburg
[
12
,
13
]
provided an
elegant answer to these questions.
Let
M(m,n)
denote the number of partitions of
n
with crank is
m
.
Then the generating function of
M(m,n)
can be written as

\displaystyle\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M(m,n)z^{m}q^{n}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}}{(zq,z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}.

(1.1)

Mahlburg
[
12
,
13
]
developed the theory of congruences for crank generating functions, which are shown to possess the same sort of arithmetic properties as partition functions.
For instance,
[
12
,
13
]
stated that if
\tau
is a positive integer,
then there are infinitely many non-nested arithmetic
progressions
An+B
such that

\displaystyle M(m,\ell^{j},An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{\tau}},

simultaneously for every
0\leq m\leq\ell^{j}-1
.

Motivated by the work of Mahlburg,
we define
crank-type generating function

as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{F}_{r,d,t}(z,q)=\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{r,d,t}(m,n)z^{m}q^{n}=\frac{(q^{r};q^{r})_{\infty}^{d}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{t}(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}.

(1.2)

Note that taking
r=t=1
and
d=2
,
(
1.2
) reduces to the ordinary crank generating function (
1.1
).
When
r=d=t=1
, (
1.2
) becomes the generating function of the birank of an ordered pair of partitions
given by Hammond and Lewis
[
10
]
.
Taking
r=t=1
and
d=k-1
in (
1.2
),
we obtain the generating function
introduced by Bringmann and Dousse
[
4
]
,
whose combinatorial interpretation is given by Fu and Tang
[
7
]
as the
k
-crank of
k
-colored partition.

Our main result shows that the coefficients of crank-type generating function enjoy the same arithmetic property as partition functions as well as crank.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
j,v
be two positive integers. We fix a prime number
\ell>dr^{2}
such that
\left(\frac{g^{2}d-tr}{\ell}\right)
are the same for all
g|r
. Then there are infinitely many non-nested arithmetic
progressions
An+B
such that

\displaystyle M_{r,d,t}(m,\ell^{j},An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v}},

simultaneously for every
0\leq m\leq\ell^{j}-1
.

Theorem
1.1
provides the congruence of birank and
k
-crank.
Recall that the birank
b(\pi)
of an ordered pair of partitions
\pi=(\lambda(1),\lambda(2))

[
10
]
is the number of parts in the first partition minus the number of parts in the second partition, that is
b(\pi)=\#(\lambda(1))-\#(\lambda(2))
. The number of ordered pairs of partitions of weight
n
having birank
m
will be written as
R(m,n)
, hence the generating function for
R(m,n)
is

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}R(m,n)z^{m}q^{n}=\frac{1}{(zq,q/z;q)_{\infty}}.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
R(m,N,n)
denote the number of ordered pairs of partitions of weight
n
with birank congruent to
m
modulo
N
.
Suppose that
\ell\geq 5
is prime and that
\tau
and
j
are positive integers.
Then there are infinitely many non-nested arithmetic
progressions
An+b
such that

\displaystyle R(m,\ell^{j},An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{\tau}},

simultaneously for every
0\leq m\leq\ell^{j}-1
.

The
k
-colored partition is a
k
-tuple of partitions
\lambda=(\lambda^{(1)},\lambda^{(2)},\ldots,\lambda^{(k)})
. For
k\geq 2
, Fu and Tang
[
7
]
defined the
k
-crank of
k
-colored partition as follows:

k\text{-crank}(\lambda)=\ell(\lambda^{(1)})-\ell(\lambda^{(2)}),

(1.3)

where
\ell\left(\pi^{(i)}\right)
denotes the number of parts in
\pi^{(i)}
.
Let
M_{k}(m,n)
denote the number of
k
-colored partitions of
n
with
k
-crank

.
The generating function of

can be derived by Bringmann and Dousse
[
4
]
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}M_{k}(m,n)z^{m}q^{n}=\frac{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2-k}}{(zq;q)_{\infty}(z^{-1}q;q)_{\infty}}.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
M_{k}(m,N,n)
denote the number of
k
-colored partitions of
n
with
k
-crank congruent to
m
modulo

. Suppose that

is prime and that
\tau
and

are positive integers.
Then there are infinitely many non-nested arithmetic
progressions
An+b
such that

\displaystyle M_{k}(m,\ell^{j},An+B)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{\tau}},

simultaneously for every
0\leq m\leq\ell^{j}-1
.

Preliminaries

In this section, we present some basic proposition of modular forms of half-integer weight which will be used in the following sections, see
[
15
]
for details.
Let
f=\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n}\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)
be a modular form of weight
k\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
with Nebentype character
\chi
.

Let
q=e^{2\pi iz}
. If

f=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a(n)q^{n}\in M_{k}(\Gamma_{0}(N),\chi)

and
\psi
is a Dirichlet character with modulus
M
, we define the twist of
f
by
\psi
to be

f(\tau)\otimes\psi=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\psi(n)a(n)q^{n}.

It is well-known that
f(\tau)\otimes\psi\in M_{k}(NM^{2},\chi\psi^{2})
.
Fix a constatn
\epsilon
, we define

\widetilde{f_{\epsilon,\psi}}(\tau)=f(\tau)-\epsilon f(\tau)\otimes\psi.

In the following, when
\epsilon
and the character
\psi
is specified, we would write it as
\tilde{f}
.

With the notation above, let
p\nmid N
be a prime number, then the action of the half-integra Hecke operator
T_{p^{2}}
is defined by

f|T_{p^{2}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(a(p^{2}n)+\chi(p)\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}n}{p}\right)p^{k-\frac{3}{2}}a(n)+\chi(p^{2})\left(\frac{(-1)^{k-\frac{1}{2}}}{p^{2}}\right)p^{2k-2}a\left(\frac{n}{p^{2}}\right)\right)q^{n}.

We need the following result from Serre
[
17
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
f_{i}(\tau)\in S_{k_{i}}(\Gamma_{1}(N_{i}))
be half-integer weight cusp forms with algebraic integer coefficients where
k_{i}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}\backslash\mathbb{Z}
for
i=1,2,\cdots,m
. Then for any
M\geq 1
, there exists a positive proportion of primes
p\equiv-1\pmod{N_{1}\cdots N_{m}M}
such that

f_{i}|T_{p^{2}}\equiv 0\pmod{M},

for every
i=1,2,\cdots,m
.

Let
\vec{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})\in\mathbb{Q}^{2}
. We set
\tau^{\prime}=a_{1}\tau+a_{2}
and
q_{\tau^{\prime}}=\textbf{e}(\tau^{\prime})
. The Klein form is given by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}}(\tau)=q_{\tau^{\prime}}^{\frac{a_{1}-1}{2}}\frac{(q_{\tau^{\prime}};q)_{\infty}(q/q_{\tau^{\prime}};q)_{\infty}}{(q;q)_{\infty}^{2}}.

(2.1)

We recall some basic properties of Klein forms, one can find more details in
[
11
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The Klein form
\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}}(\tau)
satisfies the transformation formula, i.e. for any
\gamma\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, we have

\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}}|_{\gamma}(\tau)=\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}\cdot\gamma}(\tau).

If
(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, then we have

\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}+(n_{1},n_{2})}(\tau)=(-1)^{n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{1}n_{2}}\textbf{e}\left(\frac{a_{1}n_{2}-a_{2}n_{1}}{2}\right)\mathfrak{k}_{\vec{a}}(\tau).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number, then we have

\frac{\eta^{\ell}(\ell\tau)}{\eta(\tau)}\in M_{\frac{\ell-1}{2}}\left(\Gamma_{0}(\ell),\left(\frac{\ast}{\ell}\right)\right).

(2.2)

Lemma 2.4
.

For integer
j\geq 2
, let

E_{j}(\tau)=\frac{\eta^{\ell^{j}}(\tau)}{\eta(\ell^{j}\tau)}.

Then we have
E_{j}(\tau)\in M_{\frac{\ell^{j}-1}{2}}(\Gamma_{0}(\ell^{j}),\chi_{j})
where
\chi_{j}=\left(\frac{(-1)^{\frac{\ell^{j}-1}{2}}\ell^{j}}{\ast}\right)
. Moreover,
E_{j}(\tau)
vanishes at every cusp
\frac{a}{c}
with
\ell^{j}
not dividing
c
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

The following lemmas play central roles in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
e_{\ell}=\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)e^{2\pi in/\ell}
be the Gauss sum. Then the twisted modular form has the following expression.

\displaystyle f(z)\otimes\left(\frac{\ast}{\ell}\right)=\frac{e_{\ell}}{\ell}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)f(\tau)\left|\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{n}{\ell}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right..

(3.1)

Proof.

The right hand side of (
3.1
) can be written as

\displaystyle\frac{e_{\ell}}{\ell}\sum_{n=1}^{\ell-1}\sum_{m\geq 1}a(m)\left(\frac{n}{\ell}\right)\textbf{e}\left(2\pi im\left(\tau-\frac{n}{\ell}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{e_{\ell}}{\ell}\sum_{m\geq 1}e_{\ell}a(m)\left(\frac{-m}{\ell}\right)q^{m}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{m\geq 1}a(m)\left(\frac{m}{\ell}\right)q^{m},

which completes the proof.

Consider a positive integer
N
and set
\zeta=e^{2\pi i/N}
in (
1.2
).
For any residue class
m\pmod{N}
, elementary calculations give the generating function for the crank

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}M_{r,d,t}(m,N,n)q^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\mathfrak{F}_{r,d,t}(\zeta^{s},q)\zeta^{-ms}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{s=0}^{N-1}\zeta^{-ms}\left(\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-q^{rn})^{d}}{(1-q^{n})^{t}(1-\zeta^{s}q^{n})(1-\zeta^{-s}q^{n})}\right).

(3.2)

Substituting the definition of the Klein form (
2.1
) into (
3
), we consider the following series

\begin{split}g_{m}(\tau)&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{s=1}^{N-1}\frac{\omega_{s}\zeta^{-ms}}{\mathfrak{k}_{(0,\frac{s}{N})}(\tau)}\frac{\eta^{t\ell}(\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(r\tau)}{\eta^{t}(\tau)}+\frac{\eta^{t\ell}(\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(r\tau)}{\eta^{t}(\tau)}\\
&:=G_{m}(\tau)+P(\tau)\end{split}

where
\omega_{s}=\zeta^{s/2}(1-\zeta^{-s})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
r,d,t,\ell,v
defined as before, then we have

\frac{\widetilde{G_{m}}(24\tau)}{\eta^{t\ell}(24\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(24r\tau)}E_{j+1}(24\tau)^{\ell^{v}}\in S_{\lambda+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(576r\ell^{j+1}),\chi)

(3.3)

for some integer

Proof.

By the Lemma
2.4

\frac{a}{c}

\ord_{\frac{a}{c}}\eta^{t\ell}(\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(r\tau)=\frac{t\ell^{3}+dr^{2}\ell^{v}}{24}<\frac{\ell^{2}-1}{24}\ell^{v}.

Hence it is enough to show that
\frac{\widetilde{G_{m}}(\tau)}{\eta^{k\ell}(\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(r\tau)}
vanishes at each cusp
\frac{a}{c}
with
\ell^{j+1}|c
. Now we choose suitable integers

A=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\

.

Now we compute the order of

at the cusp
\frac{a}{c}
. To do that, let

a_{1}=ra/g
,

\displaystyle\Delta(r\tau)|_{12}A=(c\tau+d)^{12}\Delta\left(\frac{ar\tau+br}{c\tau+d}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(c\tau+d)^{12}\Delta\left(B\left(\frac{g\tau+h}{r/g}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta\left(\frac{g\tau+h}{r/g}\right),

where

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

be an integer large enough, then we have

\frac{\tilde{P}(24\tau)}{\eta^{t\ell}(24\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(24r\tau)}E_{j+1}(24\tau)^{\ell^{v}}\in S_{\lambda^{\prime}+1/2}(\Gamma_{0}(576\ell^{\max\{3,j+1\}}),\chi).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

be a formal power series such that
f(q)\equiv\sum_{n\geq 0}a_{n}q^{n\ell}\pmod{\ell^{v}}
. If the series
\sum b_{n}q^{n}

coincide on the subsequence
\{\ell m+d\,|\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, then the series
\sum b_{n}q^{n}f(q)
and
\sum b_{n}^{\prime}q^{n}f(q)

\{\ell m+d\,|\,m\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\ell^{v}

Proof.

By the assumption,
\ell^{v}
divides the coefficient of

of

. So in the expression of

The following theorem gives the precise congruence relations for
M_{r,d,t}
,which implies Theorem

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
r,d,t,j,v
be positive integers and
\ell>dr^{2}
be a prime number such that
\left(\frac{g^{2}d-tr}{\ell}\right)
are the same for all
g|r

p\equiv-1\pmod{24\ell}
such that

M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{np^{3}+k-dr}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v}}

(3.5)

for all
0\leq m<\ell^{j}
and
n\equiv k-dr-24\beta\pmod{24\ell}
with
p\nmid n
where
\beta
is a certain integer between
0
and
\ell
.

Proof.

We first assume that
v\geq\max\{5,t\}
satisfies
\ell^{v}\equiv-1\pmod{24r}
. We set
\epsilon=\left(\frac{24(dr-t)}{\ell}\right)
,

\left(\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\ell}\right)=0

\widetilde{g_{m}}(\tau)

coincides with
\widetilde{g_{m}}(\tau)
on the subsequent
\{\ell n^{\prime}+\alpha+\beta\}

\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(24r\tau)=q^{dr\ell^{v}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{24rn})^{d\ell^{v}}\equiv q^{dr\ell^{v}}\prod_{n\geq 1}(1-q^{24rd\ell^{v}n})\pmod{\ell^{v}}

Hence by the Lemma
3.4
, we see that the subsequence of
\frac{\widetilde{g_{m}}(24\tau)}{\eta^{t\ell}(24\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(24r\tau)}
with the indices
\{\ell n^{\prime}+\alpha+\beta\}
coincides with
\frac{g_{m}(24\tau)}{\eta^{t\ell}(24\ell\tau)\eta^{d\ell^{v}}(24r\tau)}
on the indices
\{\ell n^{\prime}+\alpha+\beta\}
which is exactly

\sum_{n^{\prime}\equiv\alpha+\beta\pmod{\ell}}\ell^{j}M_{r,d,t}(m,\ell^{j},n^{\prime}-\alpha)q^{24n^{\prime}-k\ell^{2}-dr\ell^{v}}\pmod{\ell^{v}}

By changing the variable, we get

\sum_{n\equiv 24\beta+dr-k\pmod{24\ell}}\ell^{j}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{n+k-dr}{24}\right)q^{n}.

Combining Lemma
3.2
and Lemma

and

\sum_{n\equiv 24\beta+dr-k\pmod{24\ell}}\ell^{j}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{n+k-dr}{24}\right)q^{n}\equiv f_{m}(\tau)+f(\tau)\pmod{\ell^{v}}.

Moreover, by applying Theorem
2.1
, we can find a positive propotion of primes
p\equiv-1\pmod{24\ell}
such that

f_{m}|T_{p^{2}}=f|T_{p^{2}}\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v}}.

This implies that

\begin{split}&\ell^{j}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{p^{2}n+k-dr}{24}\right)+\ell^{j}\chi(p)\left(\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}n}{p}\right)p^{\lambda-1}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{n+k-dr}{24}\right)\\
&+\ell^{j}\chi(p^{2})\left(\frac{(-1)^{\lambda}}{p^{2}}\right)p^{2\lambda-1}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{n/p^{2}+k-dr}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v}}.\end{split}

Finally, replacing
n
by
pn^{\prime}
with
p\nmid n^{\prime}
, we get

\ell^{j}M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{p^{3}n^{\prime}+k-dr}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v}}

where
n^{\prime}\equiv k-dr-24\beta\pmod{24\ell}
. This gives

M_{r,d,t}\left(m,\ell^{j},\frac{p^{3}n^{\prime}+k-dr}{24}\right)\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{v-j}}

But we note that there are infinite many
v
satisfies
\ell^{v}\equiv-1\pmod{24r}
, so the congruence relation
(
3.5
)
holds for all
v\in\mathbb{N}
.

M_{r,d,t}(m,n)
?
Moreover, can we find any other kind of congruence of
M_{r,d,t}(m,n)

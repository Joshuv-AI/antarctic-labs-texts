Abstract
The free scalar field is only conformally invariant when non-minimally coupled to gravity. It amounts to amending, or improving, the energy momentum tensor (EMT) by a 1 12 ​ R 2 ​ ϕ 2 \frac{1}{12}R^{2}\phi^{2} -term in the Lagrangian. For Goldstones, due to a spontaneously broken global internal symmetry, improvement is prohibited which can for instance be seen from low energy theorems. When a massless dilaton due to spontaneous conformal symmetry breaking is added the low energy theorems are altered. Concretely, the dilaton can be improved and automatically improves the Goldstone such that trace of the EMT of the combined dilaton-Goldstone system is zero. The latter improves the convergence of flow theorems ending up in a Goldstone phase. Furthermore, Weyl-gauging of the scalar field is discussed as an alternative to the standard improvement.

The free massless scalar field, minimally coupled to gravity, is not conformal unless the spacetime dimension is
d=2
.
This leads to problematic ultraviolet (UV) properties in

[

,
1

of a broken internal global symmetry
[

In this work it is shown that Goldstones can be improved in the presence of a dilaton,
the (pseudo) Goldstone arising from spontaneous breaking of conformal symmetry (for comments of scale versus conformal symmetry

Whereas improvement is sometimes considered

Before summarising the solution proposed,

1.1
The improved free scalar field reviewed

It is well-known that for a scalar field in curved space
there are two terms

{\cal L}=\frac{1}{2}\left((\partial\varphi)^{2}-\xi R\varphi^{2}\right)\;,

(1.1)

with dimensionless couplings which are quadratic in the field e.g.
[
17
]
.
1
1

1

At the level of a four-derivative kinetic term there exists a unique conformal extension
[
18
]
.
It has been argued that such theories can be ghost free
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and that they have interesting properties for models of cosmology
[
22
,
23
]
. The treatment of this case is outside the scope of this paper.

Besides the standard kinetic term there is another one proportional to the Ricci scalar
R

which may be regarded as a non-minimal coupling to gravity. The specific value of the
\xi
-parameter
is related to the improvement discussed below.
The corresponding classical energy momentum tensor (EMT),
defined by the metric variation, reads

T_{\mu\nu}=2\frac{\delta}{\delta{g^{\mu\nu}}}\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}{\cal L}\Big|_{g_{\mu\nu}=\eta_{\mu\nu}}=\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial_{\nu}\varphi-\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\xi(\partial^{2}\eta_{\mu\nu}-\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu})\varphi^{2}\;,

(1.2)

where the flat space limit has been assumed for simplicity.
Taking the trace and using the equation of motion (EOM)
\partial^{2}\varphi=0
,
one gets

T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=-d_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\xi(d-1)\partial^{2}\varphi^{2}=(d-1)(\xi-\xi_{d})\partial^{2}\varphi^{2}\;,

(1.3)

where
d_{\varphi}\equiv\frac{d-2}{2}
is the free scalar dimension and

\xi_{d}
is

\xi_{d}\equiv\frac{(d-2)}{4(d-1)}\Big|_{d=4}=\frac{1}{6}\;,

(1.4)

or
\xi_{d}=d_{\varphi}/(2(d-1))
in alternative variables.
Evidently, setting
\xi=\xi_{d}
leads to
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=0
and corresponds to the famous improvement.
Further note that
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=0
, on physical states,
is equivalent to conformal symmetry,
whereas
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=\partial_{\alpha}X^{a}
signals scale invariance only
[
24
]
.
Conformality in
d=2
is automatic in that
\xi_{2}=0
and thus
\xi=0
,
where the free scalar field serves as an example of a simple conformal theory
e.g.
[
24
]
.

Historically, the value
\xi_{4}=\frac{1}{6}
was first noted in the context of
conformal symmetry in GR
[
25
]

and later seen as a necessary choice
to obey the weak equivalence principle
[
3
]
(or also
[
26
]
).
In QFT it was shown that
\xi=\xi_{d}
leads to a UV-finite EMT for the free field
which is a
necessity, in a renormalisable theory,
since
T_{\mu\nu}
is an observable
[
1
]
.
The authors, Callan, Coleman and Jackiw (CCJ), referred to the corresponding EMT as
“the improved EMT” which has become standard terminology.
Similarly the value
\xi=\xi_{d}
guarantees UV-finiteness of the integrated Casimir energy
[
2
]
.
Altogether this may be taken as an indication that conformality, rather than scale invariance, plays a more fundamental role in physics.

1.2
The problem with Goldstones

Dolgov and Voloshin concluded that
\xi=0
for Goldstones by applying a soft theorem
to gravitational form factors
[
4
]
reviewed in Sec.
2.4.1
(cf.
[
5
,
7
]
for further discussion). In order to make the presentation more concrete, the standard
chiral spontaneous symmetry breaking (SSB) of QCD-like theories,
SU(N_{F})_{L}\times SU(N_{F})_{R}\to SU(N_{F})_{V}
, is assumed and its Goldstones are
referred to as pions, e.g.
[
27
,
28
]
.
A straightforward way to see that there is a problem is to note that
the
\delta{\cal L}=-\frac{1}{2}\xi R\varphi^{2}
term cannot be built
out of the non-linear coset field
U=\exp(i\pi/F_{\pi})
, as it either breaks

SU(N_{F})_{L}\times SU(N_{F})_{R}
-invariance
\delta{\cal L}\propto R\mathrm{Tr}[U+U^{\dagger}]
or
it is trivial
\delta{\cal L}\propto R\mathrm{Tr}U^{\dagger}U\propto R
. This equally applies to abelian Goldstones
such as the
\eta^{\prime}
or the axion as it is the shift symmetry that forbids writing these terms.

For the Goldstone EFT the renormalisation properties are not important if we assume
a UV-complete theory but the connection to the weak equivalence principle remains valid and the
non-vanishing classical trace is
problematic in the IR in connection with renormalisation group (RG) flow theorems. This aspect is briefly
summarised in App.
B
. Whereas it would be unreasonable to
infer the existence of a dilaton in a QCD like theory on this matter, it seems worthwhile to take note
of the simplifications in that case.

1.3
The dilaton improves Goldstones

Let us turn to adding a dilaton
D
to the system which realises the broken conformal symmetry non linearly

\chi\equiv F_{D}e^{-\hat{D}}\;,\quad\hat{D}\equiv\frac{D}{F_{D}}\;,

(1.5)

where
F_{D}
is the dilaton decay constant, the order parameter of the broken symmetry. It has units of mass
and hatted quantities are, hereafter, understood to be dimensionless
divided by the appropriate power of
F_{D}
.
The following term

{\cal L}^{R}_{d}=\frac{\kappa}{4}\,R\,\chi^{d-2}\;,

(1.6)

is globally Weyl covariant
{\cal L}^{R}_{d}\to e^{d\alpha}{\cal L}^{R}_{d}
(cf. App.
A

\chi
-term transform such that it compensates
for the transformation of the Ricci scalar and vice versa. When the kinetic term is added the Weyl symmetry becomes a
local
symmetry
provided

\kappa=\kappa_{d}\equiv\frac{2}{(d-1)(d-2)}\Big|_{d=4}=\frac{1}{3}\;,

(1.7)

(
\kappa_{d}=1/(d_{\varphi}(d-1))=2/(d_{\varphi}^{2}\xi_{d})
) is chosen and this implies
conformality
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=0
(e.g.
[
29
]
).
The normalisation is chosen such that in
d=4
the quadratic orders in the fields match:
\xi_{4}R\varphi^{2}\leftrightarrow\kappa_{4}RD^{2}
.
And since
\xi_{4}=\frac{1}{6}
and
\kappa_{4}=\frac{1}{3}
this may be interpreted as
a double improvement w.r.t. to non Goldstone fields. This will become clearer when
studying the gravitational form factors.

The aim of this paper is to work out the consequences of a dilaton for the Goldstone system.
In the ideal case such a dilaton is massless when all explicit symmetry breaking is removed, though with a light mass it can still be of interest but not for improving flow theorems. This raises the question whether
a dilaton is a likely scenario? In a conformal field theory (CFT), in the spontaneously broken phase, a massless dilaton is
considered to be present (e.g.
[

]

Flat space in

We proceed in showing

)
by

\displaystyle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\kappa_{4}\partial^{2}\chi^{2}-2{\cal L}_{\text{kin,4}}^{\pi}+4V_{4}-2{\cal L}_{\text{kin,4}}^{D}

\displaystyle\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:Veom}}}{{=}}\;

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\kappa_{4}\partial^{2}\chi^{2}-(\partial\chi)^{2}-\chi\partial^{2}\chi+F_{4}(V_{4})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle(3\kappa_{4}-1)\big\{\chi\partial^{2}\chi+(\partial\chi)^{2}\big\}+F_{4}(V_{4})\;,

(2.6)

where the functional
F_{4}
of the potential

F_{4}(V_{4})=4V_{4}-\partial_{\ln\chi}V_{4}\;.

(2.7)

As indicated the EOM were used above which is permitted since

is
to be verified on physical states only.
One deduces that the improvement

T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=F_{4}(V_{4})\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\kappa_{4}=\frac{1}{3}\;,

(2.8)

Let us pause a moment and reflect on how the improvement works.
The underlying symmetry is conformal symmetry.
The problematic term for the single pion system is the kinetic term which is also the
only one. It is by adding the
\chi^{2}
-prefactor that it becomes conformally invariant and this
couples the dilaton to the pions. Hence it is no surprise that conformality emerges upon using the dilaton EOM.

At last let us turn to the potential.
If scale symmetry is assumed to be unbroken then the only scale invariant term is

\displaystyle V_{\Delta}(\hat{\chi})

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{m_{D}^{2}F_{D}^{2}}{\Delta-d}\left(\frac{1}{\Delta}\hat{\chi}^{\Delta}-\frac{1}{d}\hat{\chi}^{d}\right)

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle c+m_{D}^{2}F_{D}^{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}\hat{D}^{2}-\frac{d+\Delta}{3!}\hat{D}^{3}+\frac{(d+\Delta)^{2}}{4!}\hat{D}^{4}+{\cal O}(D^{5})\big)\;

(2.10)

The potential (
2.1.1

nor that it is different from zero as it is unknown in general and might very well be model-dependent.
Let us mention in passing that when

\displaystyle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}

\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{2}\kappa_{d}\nabla^{2}\chi^{d-2}-(d-2)({\cal L}_{\text{kin,d}}^{\pi}+{\cal L}^{R}_{d}+{\cal L}_{\text{kin,d}}^{D})+dV_{d}

\displaystyle\frac{d-1}{2}\ \kappa_{d}\nabla^{2}\chi^{d-2}-\frac{d-2}{2}\chi^{d-4}(\nabla\chi)^{2}-\chi^{d-3}\nabla^{2}\chi-\frac{d-4}{2}(\nabla\chi)^{2}+F_{d}(V_{d})

\displaystyle(\kappa_{d}\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}-1)\big\{\chi\nabla^{2}\chi+(d-3)(\nabla\chi)^{2}\big\}\chi^{d-4}+F_{d}(V_{d})\;,

2.3
Scalar-dilaton system and Weyl-gauging

It is interesting to return to the (non-Goldstone) scalar
\varphi
as Weyl-gauging with the dilaton
provides an alternative to the CCJ improvement.
The idea is to turn the Weyl symmetry into a local symmetry where the dilaton takes on the role
of the gauge field. This was developed in the early 70’s
[
44
]
(cf. also
[
41
]
).
The key is to introduce the Weyl covariant derivative

{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi=\partial_{\mu}+(s_{\varphi}g_{\mu\nu}-iS_{\mu\nu})(\partial^{\nu}\hat{D})\varphi
,
where
S_{\mu\nu}
is the generator of Lorentz transformations and
s_{\varphi}
is the Weyl-weight.
For a scalar the Weyl-weight is
s_{\varphi}=1
(in this section we work in
d=4
) and
the
S_{\mu\nu}
-term can be dropped
such that

{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi=(\partial_{\mu}+(\partial_{\mu}\hat{D}))\varphi\;,

(2.18)

and thus transforms covariantly with Weyl-weight two (
\varphi\to e^{\alpha}\varphi)

{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi\to e^{2\alpha}{\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi\;.

(2.19)

Why we did not need this covariant derivative for the dilaton and the pion in the previous section?
For the dilaton we observe that
{\cal D}_{\mu}\chi=0
and thus one cannot get around the improvement term as the relation
to spontaneous symmetry breaking suggests. For the pion the Weyl-weight is zero, as previously stated, which
renders the Weyl-covariant derivative superfluous:

{\cal D}_{\mu}U=\partial_{\mu}U
.

2.3.1
Trace of the energy momentum tensor

Let us return to the (non-Goldstone) scalar and consider the
\varphi
-dilaton Lagrangian

{\cal L}_{\varphi\text{-}\chi}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\chi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}({\cal D}\varphi)^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\chi}^{2}m_{\varphi}^{2}\varphi^{2}\;,

(2.20)

where we have introduced the compensator
\hat{\chi}^{2}
for the
\varphi
mass term. The EMT is obtained by
metric-variation and assumes a form

T_{\mu\nu}={\cal D}_{\mu}\varphi{\cal D}_{\nu}\varphi+\partial_{\mu}\chi\partial_{\nu}\chi-\eta_{\mu\nu}{\cal L}_{\varphi\text{-}\chi}+T^{R}_{\mu\nu}\;,

(2.21)

similar to the one for the pion-dilaton system (
2.4
).
To investigates its tracelessness one must take into account the EOM. For
the scalar
\varphi
field and the dilaton they read

{(\partial^{2}+\hat{\chi}^{2}m^{2}+[\partial^{2}\hat{D}-(\partial\hat{D})^{2}])\varphi=0}\;,

(2.22)

and

\chi\partial^{2}\chi=\partial^{\mu}((\partial_{\mu}\varphi)\varphi)+\partial^{\mu}((\partial_{\mu}D)\varphi^{2})-\hat{\chi}^{2}m_{\varphi}^{2}\varphi^{2}\;,

(2.23)

respectively. Using

\frac{1}{2}({\cal D}\varphi)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial\varphi)^{2}+\varphi{D}\cdot\partial\varphi+\frac{1}{2}(\partial{D})^{2}\varphi^{2}\;,

(2.24)

we may combine them into an equation

{\chi\partial^{2}\chi=({\cal D}\varphi)^{2}-2\hat{\chi}^{2}m_{\varphi}^{2}\varphi^{2}}\;.

(2.25)

analogous to the one for the pion-dilaton system (
2.3
).

We may now proceed as for the pion-dilaton system and write

\displaystyle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\kappa_{4}\partial^{2}\chi^{2}-(\partial\chi)^{2}-({\cal D}\varphi)^{2}-2\hat{\chi}^{2}m_{\varphi}^{2}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:chieom}}}{{=}}\;

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}\kappa_{4}\partial^{2}\chi^{2}-(\partial\chi)^{2}-\chi\partial^{2}\chi

\displaystyle\;=\;

\displaystyle(3\kappa_{4}-1)\big\{\chi\partial^{2}\chi+(\partial\chi)^{2}\big\}|_{\kappa_{4}=\frac{1}{3}}=0\;,

(2.26)

which again vanishes the appropriate improvement parameter. This is the expected result as the Weyl-covariant derivative rendered
our Lagrangian locally Weyl invariant. As an additional bonus the computation shows that (at this level) the
scalar field Weyl-weight is indeed unity. In stating covariance in (
2.19
)
s_{\varphi}=1
was assumed but we might have left it as an unknown and claimed that the Weyl-weight of the covariant derivative is of Weyl-weight
s_{\varphi}+1
.

The vanishing of the TEMT for a scalar particle with mass at LO is a realisation of the
scenario of “massive hadrons in a conformal phase”
[
37
]
. The difference
to that paper is that
\langle\varphi(p)|T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}|\varphi(p)\rangle=0
and
m_{\varphi}\neq 0
was established
for zero momentum transfer only. In that case the Weyl-compensator on the mass term proved sufficient since
the Weyl-covariant derivative only affects
{\cal O}(q^{2})
-behaviour. At the technical level it is the
incorporation of the improvement term and the matrix element of the dilaton decay constant (
2.2
)
into the EFT that makes the derivation more straightforward.
In the case where the theory flows into an IRFP we may expect that NLO
terms in the Lagrangian, which probe higher energies, would break this pattern and introduce deviation in higher orders in
q^{2}
. It is curious to have the two alternatives Weyl-gauging and the Ricci-term (sometimes referred to as
Ricci-gauging
[
45
]
). The general conditions of when Ricci-gauging provides an alternative to Weyl-gauging
has been worked out in
[
45
]
. We do stress that the two are generally dynamically inequivalent

and
that the dilaton is a specific interpretation of the Weyl gauge field.

2.4
Improvement and gravitational Goldstone form factors

Here we consider constraints on gravitational form factors in the Goldstone sector

2.4.1
Low energy theorems for form factors

Let us specialise to the case where the scalar particle is the pion,

The crucial ingredient is the standard soft-pion theorem.
Let

in the limit

(2.37)

the remainder term, e.g.
[
27

The latter is usually zero, a potentially delicate point to be discussed case by case.

2.4.2
Massive dilaton

If the dilaton is massive one might consider integrating out the dilaton and the system is then reduced to
standard chiral perturbation theory (
\chi

time ago

46
]

However, we will see that the theorem still applies for a massive dilaton and explain why that is the case.

We know that
\langle\pi^{a}(p)|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|\pi^{a}(p)\rangle=2m_{\pi}^{2}

must hold at zero momentum transfer (or simply that

We may therefore work in the limit of

2.37

\langle\pi^{a}(p^{\prime})|T_{\mu\nu}(0)|\pi^{a}(p)\rangle\to 0\;,\quad\text{for}\quad p\to 0\quad\text{or}\quad p^{\prime}\to 0\;.

(2.38)

From (

\frac{1}{2}g^{\pi}_{1}(0)+g^{\pi}_{2}(0)=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad g^{\pi}_{2}(0)=-\frac{1}{2}\;,

follows for a massive dilaton.
We may now compute the trace, restoring the mass of the pion as of above, to obtain

\langle\pi^{a}(p^{\prime})|T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}|\pi^{a}(p)\rangle=2m_{\pi}^{2}+d_{\varphi}q^{2}\big|_{d=4}=2m_{\pi}^{2}+q^{2}\;,

(2.40)

where higher terms in

and the pion mass are omitted as NLO.
This result was derived in
[

for
m_{\pi}=0
and used in
[
7

for

These low energy theorems are also important in extracting

[

[
48

for related work).
As (

in the soft limit we should expect it to hold without a dilaton and for a dilaton with a (soft) mass. If that would not be the case
then we would be able to rule out one of the two. Let us verify our claim explicitly.

No dilaton (or just pions):

in that case the trace of the EMT assumes

T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=-(\partial\pi)^{2}+2m_{\pi}^{2}\pi^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\pi^{2}+m_{\pi}^{2}\pi^{2}\;,

(2.41)

which follows from (

V_{4}=\frac{1}{2}m_{\pi}^{2}\pi^{2}

Taking the matrix element it is easily verified that (

[

Massive dilaton with improvement:

in this case the

The leading terms of the trace of the EMT, including the dilaton, follow from

T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=m_{\pi}^{2}\pi^{2}+F_{D}^{2}m_{D}^{2}\hat{D}+{\cal O}(\hat{D}^{2},\pi^{3})\;.

In order to obtain a contribution the dilaton must propagate to two pions. Thus we need to consider

Using the kinematics

In fact we may turn the logic around and regard the low energy constraint (

If the dilaton is massless then conformal symmetry can be expected

3.1
Improvement or not - conformal versus scale invariance?

The observation of CCJ
[
1
]

was that a free scalar field can be made conformal by adding the improvement
term. This corresponds to changing the theory and hence the additional label of
“improved EMT”.
For a UV complete theory such as QCD there should be no choice. The theory
is either conformal or not in the IR. If the dilaton is a valid degree of freedom
then
\kappa
in (
1.6
) should be seen as a low energy constant
which is determined by the UV theory (or determined by experiment).

Can one argue in favour or disfavour of IR-scale of -conformality?
It is the literature’s view that scale invariance implies conformal invariance since the former requires a virial current with exact scaling dimension
d-1
. This is
unlikely without symmetry protection, unless the theory is non-interacting. We refer the reader to

[
50
]
for a review
and Refs.
[
51
,
52
,
53
,
54
,
55
]
for an ever closer understanding of this matter.
However, in the deep-IR the pion theory is trivial and thus falls into the exceptional case.
It seems worthwhile to recall that the remarks about flow theorems and the weak equivalence principle,
raised in Sec.
1.2
, point in favour of IR-conformality.

3.2
On the running
\xi(\mu)
and
\kappa(\mu)

Let us discuss RG effects on
\xi(\mu)
and then turn to the particular role
at FPs.
The non-minimal coupling
\xi
in (
1.1

it will generally appear through RG effects, cf.

[
56
,
57
,
58
,
59
]

an expansion in a coupling constant

\xi(\mu)=\xi_{d}+\Delta\xi(\lambda(\mu))
.
At trivial FPs

which is automatic since
the coupling approaches zero. This leads to all the good properties such as
UV-finiteness and compatibility with the weak equivalence principle in the deep-IR.

Concretely, the renormalisation and the RG equation were first studied in

-models
[

57
,
58

been an exceptional circumstance.
The counterterms render the RG equation non-homogeneous. This means that

is not an RGFP,
in the interacting theory.
In the
\lambda\phi^{4}
-model,
\Delta\xi\to 0
at the IRFP is discussed in
[
58
]
.
For the UV, the situation is unclear since
\lambda\phi^{4}
has either the triviality problem
[
60
]
or
an unknown non-perturbative FP.
The situation in this paper concerns Goldstones which are specific fields of an effective theory which is IR-free. One can therefore expect that for
\kappa(\mu)=\kappa_{d}+\Delta\kappa(\mu)
,
\Delta\kappa(\mu)\to 0
for
\mu\to 0
.

In summary,
\xi(\mu)
and
\kappa(\mu)
ought to assume their free field values at trivial FPs.
Outside FPs they are RG-scale dependent and as such
renormalisation scheme-dependent quantities.

4
Conclusions

The leading order Lagrangian (
2.1
) of the combined dilaton-pion system
is locally Weyl invariant. The improvement term (
1.6
) for the dilaton
automatically improves the pions.
In particular
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=0
has been shown to hold by
the use of the equation of motion, cf. Sec.
2.1
.
In Sec.
2.2
it was emphasised that the improvement term realises the matrix element of the order
parameter of spontaneous symmetry in the effective theory.
We demonstrated, in Sec.
2.3
, that Weyl-gauging is an alternative to the standard CCJ-procedure
for the (non-Goldstone) scalar; albeit dynamically inequivalent.
Hence, the dilaton does not only improve Goldstone but also ordinary scalars.
The effect of a dilaton mass mass on the form factors were studied, in Sec.
2.3.2
, in different regimes
making the connection between the dilaton decoupling phase and the dilaton phase more transparent.

We discussed low energy theorems with a massless, a massive and no dilaton in Sec.
2.4
.
A discontinuity was observed between the massless and massive case.
It can be traced back to the remainder (
2.37
)
in the soft theorem which contributes only for a massless dilaton. As a byproduct we find indirect evidence that the operator breaking
the internal symmetry (chiral symmetry for the pions) ought to have scaling dimension
\Delta_{\chi}=d-2
(
2.47
).
This is consistent with the mass anomalous dimension assuming the value
\gamma_{*}=1
,
deduced in many other ways
[
36
,
13
]
, in an infrared-fixed-point interpretation
of QCD.

That the dilaton itself can be improved is known

[
8
,
9
,
10
,
12
]
and also that it improves
the axion in
{\cal N}=1
supersymmetry
[
11
]
. We believe our works adds with regards to
a) the discussion of a non-abelian internal symmetry (going beyond the free theory example
cf. the discussion in App.
C
)
b) the fact that the
d
-dependence of the improvement parameter
\kappa_{d}
differs from the one of the non-Goldstone free field
\xi_{d}
, c) the connection to flow theorems (cf. Sec.
1.2
and App.
B
)
and d) the specific discussion of the out-of-fixed-point behaviour in Sec.
3.2
.
The latter discussion is based on well-known papers which are however often overlooked in this context.

From the physics viewpoint the improvement of Goldstones concerns the infrared sector of the theory.
As such it is linked to the compatibility with the weak equivalence principle and
improved infrared behaviour which benefits flow theorems. In an ultraviolet complete theory like QCD improvement is of non-perturbative nature and thus
hard to assess by analytic methods.
However, the arguments given above and the fact that
conformal is seen as more generic than scale symmetry lends some support to the idea that Goldstone sectors could be accompanied by a dilaton
[

The Minkowski metric reads
\eta_{\mu\nu}=\textrm{diag}(1,-1,-1,-1)
,
a generic spacetime metric is denoted by
g_{\mu\nu}

Weyl transformations are defined by

g_{\mu\nu}\to e^{-2\alpha(x)}g_{\mu\nu}\;,\quad\hat{D}\to\hat{D}-\alpha(x)\;,

where the normalised dilaton field
\hat{D}
is defined in (
1.5
).
Kinetic terms are abbreviated as follows
(\partial\varphi)^{2}\equiv\partial_{\mu}\varphi\partial^{\mu}\varphi
.
The sign conventions for the Ricci scalar are those of
[
61
]
which are rather standard.
From the definition (

\sqrt{-g}\to e^{-d\alpha(x)}\sqrt{-g}\;,\quad\chi^{n}\to e^{n\alpha(x)}\chi^{n}\;,

(A.2)

Appendix B
Flow Theorems and Improvement Terms

Flow theorems state a hierarchy between the UV and the IR limit of the Weyl anomaly coefficients for theories
flowing from a UV to an IR CFT (or a fixed point). Originally formulated in

d=2
, known as the
c
-theorem, by Zamalodchikov
[
62
]
, Cardy
[
63
]
provided an insightful formula

c_{\text{UV}}-c_{\text{IR}}=3\pi\int d^{2}xx^{2}\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(x)T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(0)\rangle>0\;,

(B.1)

where
c
is the central charge in the Weyl anomaly
\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}\rangle=-\frac{1}{24\pi}cR
. One can infer from (
B.1
) that for the integral to converge it is
important that
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}\to 0
fast enough in the UV
and
the IR.

In
d=4
the Weyl anomaly reads
\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}\rangle=-(\beta_{a}^{*}E_{4}+\beta_{b}^{*}R^{2}+\beta_{c}^{*}W^{2})+\frac{4}{3}\bar{b}^{*}\Box R
(closely following the conventions in
[
64
]
) with the asterisk denoting the IR limit e.g.

\beta_{a}^{*}=\beta_{a}|_{\mu=0}
. The well-known
a
-theorem concerns the flow of
the Euler term
E_{4}

[
65
,
66
,
10
,
52
,
67
]
but
the
\Box R
-term is also a candidate
[
64
]
.
11
11

11

With regard to the latter the main point is that
whereas the
\Box R
-term can be changed by adding a local counterterm in a specific theory it would cancel in a difference such as in (
B.1
).
We shall argue that the QCD case with non-improved pions is problematic in the IR.
Convergence in the UV can be shown to hold performing resummation with
RG methods
[
68
]
.

Let us start with the
\Box R
-term for which the flow theorem takes on a very analogous formula to (
B.1
)

\bar{b}_{\text{UV}}-\bar{b}_{\text{IR}}=\frac{1}{3\,2^{9}}\int d^{4}xx^{4}\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(x)T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(0)\rangle>0\;,

(B.2)

in that it is expressible in terms of a two-point function. If the IR theory is standard QCD with free pions in the IR, then
the non-improved pion theory

T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\pi^{2}
leads to
\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(x)T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(0)\rangle\propto{1}/{x^{8}}
which
is logarthimcally divergent in the IR
[
64
]
. In that reference the discussion is phrased in

Let us turn to the
a
-theorem for which a two-point function is not sufficient and our
main point is that the same problem occurs as it still contains the two-point function in (
B.2
).
We will use the language in
[
10
,
52
]
.
A pathway to positivity is given by unitarity and the four-point function scattering amplitude
{\cal A}(s)
of
four external dilaton fields
\tau
which are put “’on-shell”
\partial^{2}\tau=0
. The difference of the Weyl anomaly coefficients

\beta_{a}^{\text{UV}}-\beta_{a}^{\text{IR}}\propto\alpha(\infty)-\alpha(0)

{\cal A}(s)=s^{2}\alpha(s)/f^{4}
(
f

One finds (cf. Eq. 3.7 in
[
52
]
)

f^{4}{\cal A}(s)=\langle T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(p_{1}+p_{2})T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}(p_{3}+p_{4})\rangle+\dots

(B.3)

with
p_{i}

s\equiv(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}
the centre of mass and the dots stand for other contributions, including higher point-functions.
With
T^{\rho}_{\phantom{\rho}\rho}=-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\pi^{2}
this leads to
{\cal A}(s)\supset s^{2}\ln s
and
\textrm{Im}\alpha(s)=c_{0}+\dots
.
Since
\beta_{a}^{\text{UV}}-\beta_{a}^{\text{IR}}\supset c^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}\textrm{Im}\alpha(s^{\prime})/s^{\prime}ds^{\prime}
the same type of logarithmic divergence is found as for the
\Box R
-flow above. Note that for all other correlation functions the on-shell condition
p_{i}^{2}=0

removes the
-\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}\pi^{2}
-term. In essence it is that
p_{i}^{2}=0
does not imply
(p_{1}+p_{2})^{2}=0

which leads to the apparent non-convergence.

In summary if the system can be improved, with a
dynamical
dilaton,
then these problems are not there.

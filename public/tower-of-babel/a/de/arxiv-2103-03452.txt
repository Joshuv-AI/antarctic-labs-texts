FedDR – Randomized Douglas-Rachford Splitting Algorithms for Nonconvex Federated Composite Optimization
Quoc Tran-Dinh and Nhan H. Pham, Dzung T. Phan and Lam M. Nguyen

Abstract
We develop two new algorithms, called, FedDR and asyncFedDR , for solving a fundamental nonconvex composite optimization problem in federated learning. Our algorithms rely on a novel combination between a nonconvex Douglas-Rachford splitting method, randomized block-coordinate strategies, and asynchronous implementation. They can also handle convex regularizers. Unlike recent methods in the literature, e.g., FedSplit and FedPD, our algorithms update only a subset of users at each communication round, and possibly in an asynchronous manner, making them more practical. These new algorithms can handle statistical and system heterogeneity, which are the two main challenges in federated learning, while achieving the best known communication complexity. In fact, our new algorithms match the communication complexity lower bound up to a constant factor under standard assumptions. Our numerical experiments illustrate the advantages of our methods over existing algorithms on synthetic and real datasets.

Introduction

Training machine learning models in a centralized fashion becomes more challenging and marginally inaccessible for a large number of users, especially when the size of datasets and models is growing substantially larger.
Consequently, training algorithms using decentralized and distributed approaches comes in as a natural replacement.
Among several approaches, federated learning (FL) has received tremendous attention in the past few years since it was first introduced in
[
18
,
30
]
.
In this setting, a central server coordinates between many local users (also called agents or devices) to perform their local updates, then the global model will get updated, e.g., by averaging or aggregating local models.

Challenges.

FL provides a promising solution for many machine learning applications such as learning over smartphones or across organizations, and internet of things, where privacy protection is one of the most critical requirements.
However, this training mechanism faces a number of fundamental challenges, see, e.g.,
[
31
]
.
First, when the number of users gets substantially large, it creates
communication bottleneck
during model exchange process between server and users.
Second, the local data stored in each local user may be different in terms of sizes and distribution which poses a challenge:
data or statistical heterogeneity
.
Third, the variety of users with different local storage, computational power, and network connectivity participating into the system also creates a major challenge, known as
system heterogeneity
.
This challenge also causes unstable connection between server and users, where some users may be disconnected from the server or simply dropped out during training.
In practice, we can expect only a subset of users to participate in each round of communication.
Another challenge in FL is
privacy concern
.
Accessing and sharing local raw data is not permitted in FL.
In addition, distributed methods exchange the objective gradient of local users, and private data can be exposed from the shared model such as the objective gradients
[
51
]
.
Therefore, FL methods normally send the global model to each user at the start of each communication round, each user will perform its local update and send back only the necessary update for aggregation.

Our goal and approach.

Our goal in this paper is to further and simultaneously address these fundamental challenges by proposing two new algorithms to train the underlying common optimization model in FL.
Our approach relies on a novel combination between randomized block-coordinate strategy, nonconvex Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting, and asynchronous implementation.
While each individual technique or partial combinations is not new, our combination of three as in this paper appears to be the first in the literature.
To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work developing randomized block-coordinate DR splitting methods for nonconvex composite FL optimization models, and they are fundamentally different from some works in the convex setting, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
]
.

Contribution.

Our contribution can be summarized as follows.

(a)

We develop a new FL algorithm, called
FedDR
(
Fed
erated
D
ouglas-
R
achford), by combining the well-known DR splitting technique and randomized block-coordinate strategy for the common nonconvex composite optimization problem in FL.
Our algorithm can handle nonsmooth convex regularizers and allows inexact evaluation of the underlying proximal operators as in FedProx or FedPD.
It also achieves the best known
\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)
communication complexity for finding a stationary point under standard assumptions (Assumptions
2.1
-
2.2
), where
\varepsilon
is a given accuracy.
More importantly, unlike FedSplit
[
33
]
and FedPD
[
49
]
, which require full user participation to achieve convergence, our analysis does allow partial participation by selecting a subset of users to perform update at each communication round.

(b)

Next, we propose an asynchronous algorithm,
asyncFedDR
, where each user can asynchronously perform local update and periodically send the update to the server for proximal aggregation.
We show that
asyncFedDR
achieves the same communication complexity
\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)
as
FedDR
(up to a constant factor) under the same standard assumptions.
This algorithm is expected to simultaneously address all challenges discussed above.

Let us emphasize some key points of our contribution.
First, the best known
\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)
communication complexity of our methods matches the lower bound complexity up to a constant factor as shown in
[

, even with inexact evaluation of the objective proximal operators.
Second, our methods rely on a DR splitting technique for nonconvex optimization and can handle possibly nonsmooth convex regularizers, which allows us to deal with a larger class of applications and with constraints
[

.
Furthermore, it can also handle both statistical and system heterogeneity as discussed in FedSplit
[

[

However, FedSplit only considers the convex case, and both FedSplit and FedPD require all users to update at each communication round, making them less practical and applicable in FL.
Our methods only require a subset of users or even one user to participate in each communication round as in FedAvg or FedProx.
In addition, our aggregation step on the server is different from most existing works due to a proximal step on the regularizer. It is also different from

Third, as FedProx

Finally, requiring synchronous aggregation at the end of each communication round may lead to slow-down in training due to the heterogeneity in computing power and communication capability of local users.
It is natural to have asynchronous update from local users as in, e.g.,
[

Our asynchronous variant,

Nonconvex Optimization Models in Federated Learning

The underlying optimization model of many FL applications can be written into the following form:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}\Big\{F(x):=f(x)+g(x)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x)+g(x)\Big\},

(1)

where
n
is the number of users, and each
f_{i}
is a local loss of the
i
-th user, which is assumed to be nonconvex and
L
-smooth (see Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
below), and
g
is a proper, closed, and convex regularizer.
Apart from these assumptions, we will not make any additional assumption on (
1
).
We emphasize that the use of regularizers
g
has been motivated in several works, including
[
47
]
.

Let
\mathrm{dom}(F):=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}:F(x)<+\infty\right\}
be the domain of
F
and
\partial{g}
be the subdifferential of
g

[
1
]
.
Since (
1
) is nonconvex, we only expect to find a stationary point, which is characterized by the following optimality condition.

Definition 2.1
.

If
0\in\nabla f(x^{*})+\partial{g}(x^{*})
, then
x^{*}
is called a [first-order] stationary point of (
1
).

The algorithms for solving (
1
) developed in this paper will rely on the following assumptions.

Assumption 2.1

(Boundedness from below)
.

\mathrm{dom}(F)\neq\emptyset
and
F^{\star}:=\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{p}}F(x)>-\infty
.

Assumption 2.2

(
L
-smoothness)
.

All functions
f_{i}(\cdot)
for
i\in[n]:=\{1,\cdots,n\}
are
L
-smooth, i.e.,
f_{i}
is continuously differentiable and there exists
L\in(0,+\infty)
such that

\|\nabla{f}_{i}(x)-\nabla{f}_{i}(y)\|\leq L\|x-y\|,\quad\forall x,y\in\mathrm{dom}(f_{i}).

(2)

Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
are very standard in nonconvex optimization.
Assumption
2.1
guarantees the well-definedness of (
1
) and is independent of algorithms.
Assuming the same Lipschitz constant
L
for all
f_{i}
is not restrictive since if
f_{i}
is
L_{i}
-smooth, then by scaling variables of its constrained formulation (see (
11
) in Supp. Doc.), we can get the same Lipschitz constant
L
of all
f_{i}
.

Proximal operators and evaluation.

Our methods make use of the proximal operators of both
f_{i}
and
g
.
Although
f_{i}
is
L
-smooth and nonconvex, we still define its proximal operator as

\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(x):=\mathrm{arg}\!\displaystyle\min_{y}\big\{f_{i}(y)+\tfrac{1}{2\eta}\|y-x\|^{2}\big\},

(3)

where
\eta>0
.
Even
f_{i}
is nonconvex, under Assumption
2.2
, if we choose
0<\eta<\frac{1}{L}
, then
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
is well-defined and single-valued.
Evaluating
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
requires to solve a strongly convex program.
If
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
can only be computed approximately up to an accuracy
\epsilon\geq 0
to obtain
z
, denoted by
x_{+}:\approx\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(x)
, if
\|x_{+}-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(x)\|\leq\epsilon_{i}
.
Note that instead of absolute error, one can also use a relative error as
\|x_{+}-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(x)\|\leq\epsilon_{i}\|x_{+}-x\|
as in
[
37
]
.
For the convex function
g
, its proximal operator
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}
is defined in the same way as (
3
).
Evaluating
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
can be done by various existing methods, including local SGD and accelerated GD-type algorithms.
However, this is not our focus in this paper, and therefore we do not specify the subsolver for evaluating
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
.

Gradient mapping.

As usual, let us define the following gradient mapping of
F
in (
1
).

\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(x):=\tfrac{1}{\eta}\big(x-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}(x-\eta\nabla{f}(x))\big),\quad\eta>0.

(4)

Then, the optimality condition
0\in\nabla{f}(x^{*})+\partial{g}(x^{*})
of (
1
) is equivalent to
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(x^{*})=0
.
However, in practice, we often wish to find an
\varepsilon
-approximate stationary point to (
1
) defined as follows.

Definition 2.2
.

If
\tilde{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\big[{\left\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\tilde{x})\right\|^{2}}\big]\leq\varepsilon^{2}
, then
\tilde{x}
is called an
\varepsilon
-stationary point of (
1
), where the expectation is taken overall the randomness generated by the underlying algorithm.

Note that, for
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\tilde{x})
to be well-defined, we require
\tilde{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
.
In our algorithms below, this requirement is fulfilled if
\tilde{x}\in\mathrm{dom}(f)
, which is often satisfied in practice as
\mathrm{dom}(f)=\mathbb{R}^{p}
.

FedDR Algorithm and Its Convergence Guarantee

Prior to our work, FedSplit
[
33
]
exploits similar update steps as ours by adopting the Peaceman-Rachford splitting method to solve the convex and non-composite instances of (
1
).
FedSplit can overcome some of the key challenges as discussed earlier.
Following this idea, we take the advantages of the DR splitting method to first derive a new variant to handle the nonconvex composite problem (
1
).
This new algorithm is synchronous and we call it
FedDR
.
The central idea is as follows: First, we reformulate (
1
) into (
12
) by duplicating variables.
Next, we apply a DR splitting scheme to the resulting problem.
Finally, we combine such a scheme with a randomized block-coordinate strategy.

The complete algorithm is presented in Algorithm
1
, where its full derivation is in Supp. Doc.
A.1
.

Algorithm 1

(FL with Randomized DR (
FedDR
))

1:

Initialization:
Take
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
. Choose
\eta>0
and
\alpha>0
, and accuracies
\epsilon_{i,0}\geq 0
(
i\in[n]
).

2:

Initialize the server with
\bar{x}^{0}:=x^{0}
and
\tilde{x}^{0}:=x^{0}
.

3:

Initialize each user
i\in[n]
with
y_{i}^{0}:=x^{0}
,
x_{i}^{0}:\approx\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{0})
, and
\hat{x}^{0}_{i}:=2x_{i}^{0}-y_{i}^{0}
.

4:

For
k:=0,\cdots,K
do

5:

[
Active users
] Generate a proper realization
\mathcal{S}_{k}\subseteq[n]
of
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
(see Assumption
3.1
).

6:

[
Communication
] Each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
receives
\bar{x}^{k}
from the server.

7:

[
Local update
]
For each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
do
: Choose
\epsilon_{i,k+1}\geq 0
and update

y^{k+1}_{i}:=y_{i}^{k}+\alpha(\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}),\quad x_{i}^{k+1}:\approx\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k+1}),\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}:=2x^{k+1}_{i}-y^{k+1}_{i}.\vskip-4.30554pt

8:

[
Communication
] Each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
sends
\Delta{\hat{x}}^{k}_{i}:=\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}-\hat{x}^{k}_{i}
back to the server.

9:

[
Sever aggregation
] The server aggregates
\tilde{x}^{k+1}\!:=\tilde{x}^{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\Delta{\hat{x}}^{k}_{i}
.

10:

[
Sever update
] Then, the sever updates
\bar{x}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\big)
.

11:

End For

Let us make the following remarks.
Firstly,
FedDR
mainly updates of three sequences
\{\bar{x}^{k}\}
,
\{x^{k}_{i}\}
and
\{y^{k}_{i}\}
.
While
\bar{x}^{k}
is an averaged model to approximately minimize the global objective function
F
,
x^{k}_{i}
act as local models trying to optimize a regularized local loss function w.r.t. its local data distribution, and
y^{k}_{i}
keeps track of the residuals from the local models to the global one.
Secondly, we allow
x_{i}^{k}
to be an approximation of
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k})
up to an accuracy
\epsilon_{i,k}\geq 0
as defined in (
3
), i.e.,
\|x_{i}^{k}-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y^{k}_{i})\|\leq\epsilon_{i,k}
for all
i\in[n]
if
k=0
and for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}
if
k>0
.
If
\epsilon_{i,k}=0
, then we get the exact evaluation
x_{i}^{k}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k})
.
Approximately evaluating
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
can be done, e.g., by local SGD as in FedAvg.
Thirdly, Algorithm
1
is different from existing randomized proximal gradient-based methods since we rely on a DR splitting scheme and can handle composite settings.
Here, three iterates
y^{k}_{i}
,
x_{i}^{k}
, and
\hat{x}^{k}_{i}
at Step
7
are updated sequentially, making it challenging to analyze convergence.
Lastly, the subset of active users
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is sampled from a random set-valued mapping
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
.
As specified in Assumption
3.1
, this sampling mechanism covers a wide range of sampling strategies.
Clearly, if
\mathcal{S}_{k}=[n]
and
g=0
, then Algorithm
1
reduces to FedSplit, but for the nonconvex case.
Hence, our convergence guarantee below remains applicable, and the guarantee is sure.
Note that both our model (
1
) and Algorithm
1
are completely different from
[
47
]
.

3.1
Convergence of Algorithm
1

Let us consider a proper sampling scheme
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
of
[n]
, which is a random set-valued mapping with values in
2^{[n]}
, the collection of all subsets of
[n]
.
Let
\mathcal{S}_{k}
be an iid realization of
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\sigma(\mathcal{S}_{0},\cdots,\mathcal{S}_{k})
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{S}_{0},\cdots,\mathcal{S}_{k}
.
We first impose the following assumption about the distribution of our sampling scheme
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
.

Assumption 3.1
.

There exist
\mathbf{p}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{p}_{n}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}\big(i\in\hat{\mathcal{S}}\big)=\mathbf{p}_{i}>0
for all
i\in[n]
.

This assumption covers a large class of sampling schemes as discussed in
[
36
]
, including non-overlapping uniform and doubly uniform.
This assumption guarantees that every user has a non-negligible probability to be updated.
Note that
\mathbf{p}_{i}=\sum_{\mathcal{S}:i\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{S})
due to Assumption
3.1
.
For the sake of notation, we also denote
\hat{\mathbf{p}}:=\min\{\mathbf{p}_{i}:i\in[n]\}>0
.

The following theorem characterizes convergence of Algorithm
1
with inexact evaluation of
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
.
Due to space limit, we refer the reader to Lemma
A.6
in Sup. Doc. for more details about the choice of stepsizes and related constants.
The proof of this theorem is defered to Sup. Doc.

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and
3.1
hold.
Let
\{(x^{k}_{i},y^{k}_{i},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},\bar{x}^{k})\}

where

, and

Remark 3.1
.

[
Choice of accuracies
\epsilon_{i}^{k}
]
To guarantee
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{K+1}\epsilon_{i,k}^{2}\leq M
in Theorem

for a given constant

and for all

, one can choose, e.g.,
\epsilon_{i,k}^{2}:=\frac{M}{2(k+1)^{2}}

(see Supp. Doc.

Remark 3.2

(
Comparison
)
.

Since (

\mathcal{O}\left(\varepsilon^{-2}\right)

[

]
.
However, different from the convergence analysis of FedSplit and FedPD

This can potentially further resolve the communication bottleneck

We note that FedSplit is a variant of the Peaceman-Rachford splitting method, i.e.

4
AsyncFedDR and Its Convergence Guarantee

Motivation.

Although
FedDR
has been shown to converge, it is more practical to account for the system heterogeneity of local users.
Requiring synchronous aggregation at the end of each communication round may lead to slow down in training.
It is natural to have asynchronous update from local users as seen, e.g., in
[
35
,
39
]
.
However, asynchronous implementation remains limited in FL.
Here, we propose
asyncFedDR
, an asynchronous variant of
FedDR
, and analyze its convergence guarantee.
For the sake of our analysis, we only consider
\mathcal{S}_{k}:=\{i_{k}\}
, the exact evaluation of
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
, and bounded delay, but extensions to general
\mathcal{S}_{k}
and inexact
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
are similar to Algorithm
1
.

4.1
Derivation of asyncFedDR

Let us first explain the main idea of
asyncFedDR
.
At each iteration
k
, each user receives a delay copy
\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}
of
\bar{x}^{k}
from the server with a delay
d^{k}_{i_{k}}

The active user
i_{k}
will update its own local model
(y_{i}^{k},x^{k}_{i},\hat{x}^{k}_{i})

Once completing its update, user

just sends an increment

to the server to update the global model, while others may be reading.
Overall, the complete

is presented in Algorithm

Probabilistic model.

Let

We also use

Let

A.1
Derivation of Algorithm
1

Our first step is to recast (
1
) into a constrained reformulation.
Next, we apply the classical Douglas-Rachford (DR) splitting scheme to this reformulation.
Finally, we randomize its updates to obtain a randomized block-coordinate DR variant.

(a) Constrained reformulation.

With a little abuse of notation, we can equivalently write (
1
) into the following constrained minimization problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle\min_{x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}}&\Big\{F(\mathbf{x}):=f(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{x})\equiv\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i})+g(x_{1})\Big\}\\
\text{s.t.}&x_{2}=x_{1},\ x_{3}=x_{1},\ \cdots,x_{n}=x_{1}.\end{array}\right.

(11)

where
\mathbf{x}:=[x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}]
concatenates
n
duplicated variables
x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{n}
of
x
in (
1
) such that it forms a column vector in
\mathbb{R}^{np}
.
Such duplications are characterized by
x_{2}=x_{1},x_{3}=x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}=x_{1}
, which define a linear subspace
\mathcal{L}:=\{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}:x_{2}=x_{1},\ x_{3}=x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}=x_{1}\}
in
\mathbb{R}^{np}
.

(b) Unconstrained reformulation.

Let
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}
be the indicator function of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e.
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})=0
if
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{L}
, and
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})=+\infty
, otherwise.
Then, we can rewrite (
11
) into the following unconstrained setting:

\min_{\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{np}}\Big\{F(\mathbf{x}):=f(\mathbf{x})+g(\mathbf{x})+\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})\equiv\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i})+g(x_{1})+\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})\Big\}.

(12)

Clearly, (
12
) can be viewed as a composite nonconvex minimization problem of
f(\mathbf{x})
and
g(\mathbf{x})+\delta_{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x})
.
The first-order optimality condition of (
12
) can be written as

0\in\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\partial{g}(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\partial{\delta_{\mathcal{L}}}(\mathbf{x}^{\star}),

(13)

where
\partial{\delta_{\mathcal{L}}}
is the subdifferential of
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}
, which is the normal cone of
\mathcal{L}
(or, equivalently,
\partial{\delta_{\mathcal{L}}}(\mathbf{x})=\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
if
\mathbf{x}\in\mathcal{L}
, the orthogonal subspace of
\mathcal{L}
, and
\partial{\delta_{\mathcal{L}}}(\mathbf{x})=\emptyset
, otherwise), and
\partial{g}
is the subdifferential of
g
.
Note that since
f
is nonconvex, (
13
) only provides a necessary condition for
\mathbf{x}^{\star}:=[x^{\star}_{1},\cdots,x^{\star}_{n}]
to be a local minimizer.
Any
\mathbf{x}^{\star}
satisfying (
13
) is called a (first-order) stationary point of (
12
).
In this case, we have
x^{\star}_{i}=x_{1}^{\star}
for all
i\in[n]
.
Hence, using (
13
), we have
0\in\nabla{f}(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\partial{g}(\mathbf{x}^{\star})+\mathcal{L}^{\perp}
.
This condition is equivalent to
0\in\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{\star})+\partial{g}(x_{1}^{\star})
.
However, since
x^{\star}_{i}=x_{1}^{\star}
for all
i\in[n]
, the last inclusion becomes
0\in\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{1}^{\star})+\partial{g}(x_{1}^{\star})
.
Equivalently, we have
x^{\star}:=x^{\star}_{1}
to be a stationary point of (
1
).

(c) Full parallel DR variant.

Let us apply the DR splitting method to (
13
), which can be written explicitly as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbf{y}^{k+1}&:=&\mathbf{x}^{k}+\alpha(\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\mathbf{x}^{k}),\\
\mathbf{x}^{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{n\eta f}(\mathbf{y}^{k+1}),\\
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{n\eta(g+\delta_{\mathcal{L}})}(2\mathbf{x}^{k+1}-\mathbf{y}^{k+1}),\end{array}\right.

(14)

where
\eta>0
is a given such that
n\eta
is a step-size and
\alpha\in(0,2]
is a relaxation parameter
[
40
]
.
If
\alpha=1
, then we recover the classical Douglas-Rachford scheme
[
27
]
and if
\alpha=2
, then we recover the Peaceman-Rachford splitting scheme
[
1
]
.
Note that the classical DR scheme studied in
[
27
]
was developed to solve monotone inclusions, and in our context, convex problems.
Recently, it has been extended to solve nonconvex optimization problems, see, e.g.,
[
20
,
40
]
.

Let us further exploit the structure of
f
,
g
, and
\delta_{\mathcal{L}}
in (
12
) to obtain a special parallel DR variant.

•

First, since
f(\mathbf{x})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i})
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}\Big\{f(\mathbf{x})+\tfrac{1}{2n\eta}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{y}^{k+1}\|^{2}\Big\}&=&\displaystyle\min_{\mathbf{x}}\Big\{\tfrac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x_{i})+\tfrac{1}{2\eta}\|x_{i}-y_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}\Big]\Big\}\\
&=&\frac{1}{n}\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\displaystyle\min_{x_{i}}\Big\{f_{i}(x_{i})+\tfrac{1}{2\eta}\|x_{i}-y_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}\Big\}.\end{array}

Hence, we can decompose the computation of
\mathbf{x}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{n\eta f}(\mathbf{y}^{k+1})
from (
14
) as
x_{i}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y^{k+1}_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
.

•

Next, we denote
\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}:=2\mathbf{x}^{k+1}-\mathbf{y}^{k+1}
, or equivalently, in component-wise
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}:=2x^{k+1}_{i}-y_{i}^{k+1}
for all
i\in[n]
.

•

Finally, the third line of (
14
)
\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{n\eta(g+\delta_{\mathcal{L}})}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})
can be rewritten as

\hskip 0.0pt\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{n\eta(g+\delta_{\mathcal{L}})}(\hat{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&{\mathrm{arg}\!\!\!\!\!\!\displaystyle\min_{[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]}}\big\{g(x_{1})+\tfrac{1}{2n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}\big\}\\
&\text{s.t.}\quad x_{i}=x_{1},\ \ \text{for all}\ i=2,\cdots,n.\end{array}\right.\hskip-17.22217pt

(15)

Let us solve(
15
) explicitly.
First, we define a Lagrange function associated with (
15
) as

\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{x},\mathbf{z})=g(x_{1})+\frac{1}{2n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}z_{i}^{\top}(x_{i+1}-x_{1}),

where
z_{i}
(
i=1,\cdots,n-1)
are the corresponding Lagrange multipliers.
Hence, the KKT condition of (
15
) can be written as

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}&\partial{g}(\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1})+\tfrac{1}{n\eta}(\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}-\hat{x}_{1}^{k+1})-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}z_{i}=0,\\
&\tfrac{1}{n\eta}(\bar{x}^{k+1}_{i+1}-\hat{x}_{i+1}^{k+1})+z_{i}=0,\quad\text{for all}\ i=1,\cdots,n-1,\\
&\bar{x}^{k+1}_{i+1}=\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1},\quad\text{for all}\ i=1,\cdots,n-1.\end{array}\right.

Summing up the second line from
i=1
to
i=n-1
and combining the result with the last line of this KKT condition, we have

n\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}z_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i+1}-\bar{x}_{i+1}^{k+1})=\sum_{i=2}^{n}\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}-(n-1)\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}.

Substituting this expression into the first line of the KKT condition, we get

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}-(n-1)\bar{x}_{1}^{k+1}=\hat{x}_{1}^{k+1}+n\eta\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}z_{i}\in\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}+n\eta\partial{g}(\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}).

(16)

This condition is equivalent to
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}\in n\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}+n\eta\partial{g}(\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1})
.
By introducing a new notation
\bar{x}^{k+1}:=\bar{x}^{k+1}_{1}
, we eventually obtain from the last inclusion that

\begin{array}[]{l}\bar{\mathbf{x}}^{k+1}:=[\bar{x}^{k+1},\cdots,\bar{x}^{k+1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{np},\quad\text{where}\quad\bar{x}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}\right).\end{array}

If we introduce a new variable
\tilde{x}^{k+1}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}
, then
\bar{x}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\big)
.

Putting the above steps together, we obtain the following parallel DR variant for solving (
1
):

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k+1}_{i}&:=&y_{i}^{k}+\alpha(\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}),\quad\forall i\in[n]\\
x_{i}^{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k+1}),\quad\forall i\in[n]\\
\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1}&:=&2x^{k+1}_{i}-y^{k+1}_{i},\quad\forall i\in[n]\\
\tilde{x}^{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}_{i}^{k+1},\\
\bar{x}^{k+1}&:=&\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\big).\end{array}\right.

(17)

This variant can be implemented in parallel.
It is also known as a special variant of Tseng’s splitting method
[
1
]
in the convex case.
This variant also covers
FedSplit
in
[
33
]
for FL as a special case when
g=0
,
f_{i}
is convex for all
i\in[n]
, and
\alpha=2
.
In fact,
FedSplit
is a variant of the Peaceman-Rachford method, and is different from our algorithms due to
\alpha<2
.
If
g=0
(i.e., without regularizer), then the last line of (
17
) reduces to
\bar{x}^{k+1}=\tilde{x}^{k+1}
.

(d) Inexact block-coordinate DR variant.

Instead of performing update for all users
i\in[n]
as in (
17
), we propose a new block-coordinate DR variant, called
FedDR
, where only a subset of users
\mathcal{S}_{k}\subseteq[n]
performs local update then send its local model to server for aggregation.
For user
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
, the local model is unchanged, i.e., for all
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
:
y^{k+1}_{i}=y^{k}_{i}
,
x^{k+1}_{i}=x^{k}_{i}
, and
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=\hat{x}^{k}_{i}
.
Hence, no communication with the server is needed for these users.
Furthermore, we assume that we can only approximate the proximal operator
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
up to a given accuracy for all
i\in[n]
.
In this case, we replace the exact proximal step
x_{i}^{k}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k})
by its approximation
x_{i}^{k}:\approx\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k})
up to a given accuracy
\epsilon_{i,k}\geq 0
such that

\|x_{i}^{k}-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k})\|\leq\epsilon_{i,k}.

(18)

Since
x_{i}^{k}
is approximately computed from
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y^{k}_{i})
as in (
18
), we have

x_{i}^{k}=z^{k}_{i}+e_{i}^{k},\quad\text{where}\quad z_{i}^{k}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y^{k}_{i})\quad\text{and}\quad\|e_{i}^{k}\|\leq\epsilon_{i,k}.

(19)

We will use this representation of
x_{i}^{k}
and
x_{i}^{k+1}
in our analysis in the sequel.

More specifically, the update of our inexact block-coordinate DR variant can be described as follows.

•

Initialization:

Given an initial vector
x^{0}\in\mathrm{dom}(F)
and accuracies
\epsilon_{i,0}\geq 0
.

Initialize the server with
\bar{x}^{0}:=x^{0}
.

Initialize all users
i\in[n]
with
y^{0}_{i}:=x^{0}
,
x_{i}^{0}:\approx\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{0})
, and
\hat{x}_{i}^{0}:=2x_{i}^{0}-y_{i}^{0}
.

•

The
k
-th iteration (
k\geq 0
):
Sample a proper subset
\mathcal{S}_{k}\subseteq[n]
so that
\mathcal{S}_{k}
presents as the subset of
active users
.

•

(
Communication
) Each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
receives
\bar{x}^{k}
from the server.

•

(
Local/user update
) For each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, given
\epsilon_{i,k+1}\geq 0
, it updates

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k+1}_{i}&:=&y_{i}^{k}+\alpha(\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i})\\
x_{i}^{k+1}&:\approx&\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{k+1})\\
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}&:=&2x^{k+1}_{i}-y^{k+1}_{i}.\end{array}\right.\vskip-4.30554pt

Each user
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
does nothing, i.e.:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcl}y^{k+1}_{i}&:=&y_{i}^{k}\\
x_{i}^{k+1}&:=&x^{k}_{i}\\
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}&:=&\hat{x}^{k}_{i}.\end{array}\right.

•

(
Communication
) Each user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
sends only
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}
to the server.

•

(
Global/Server update
) The server aggregates
\tilde{x}^{k+1}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}
, and then compute
\bar{x}^{k+1}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}(\tilde{x}^{k+1})
.

This scheme is exactly Algorithm
1
.
However, the global update on
\tilde{x}^{k+1}
can be simplified as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{x}^{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}^{n}\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\not\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}^{n}\hat{x}^{k}_{i}\\
&=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}^{k}_{i}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}^{n}(\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}^{k})\\
&=&\tilde{x}^{k}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\Delta{\hat{x}}^{k}_{i}.\end{array}

This step is implemented in Algorithm
1
.

To analyze convergence of Algorithm
1
, we conceptually introduce
z_{i}^{0}
and
z_{i}^{k+1}
for
i\in[n]
as follows:

z_{i}^{0}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{0}),\quad z_{i}^{k+1}:=\begin{cases}\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})&\text{if}\quad i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\\
z_{i}^{k}&\text{if}\quad i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k},\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad x_{i}^{k}:=z_{i}^{k}+e_{i}^{k}.

(20)

Here,
e_{i}^{k}
is the vector of errors.
Note that
z_{i}^{0}
and
z_{i}^{k+1}
do not exist in actual implementation of Algorithm
1
, and we only have their approximations
x_{i}^{0}
and
x_{i}^{k+1}
, respectively.
For any
k\geq 0
, since
x_{i}^{k+1}=x_{i}^{k}
and
z_{i}^{k+1}=z_{i}^{k}
for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we have
\|x_{i}^{k+1}-z_{i}^{k+1}\|=\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|=\|x_{i}^{k}-z_{i}^{k}\|=\|e_{i}^{k}\|
for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
.
To guarantee
\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|=\|e_{i}^{k}\|
for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we must choose
\epsilon_{i,k+1}:=\epsilon_{i,k}
for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
.

Note that in Algorithm
1
, we have not specified the choice of
\mathcal{S}_{k}
.
The subset
\mathcal{S}_{k}
is an iid realization of a random set-valued mapping
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
from
[n]
to
2^{[n]}
, the collection of all subsets of
[n]
.
Moreover,
\hat{\mathcal{S}}
is a proper sampling scheme in the sense that
\mathbf{p}_{i}:=\mathbb{P}(i\in\hat{S})>0
for all
i\in[n]
as stated in Assumption
3.1
.
By specifying this probability distribution
\mathbf{p}:=(\mathbf{p}_{1},\cdots,\mathbf{p}_{n})
, we obtain different sampling strategies ranging from uniform to non-uniform as discussed in
[
36
]
.
Our analysis below holds for arbitrary sampling scheme that satisfies Assumption
3.1
.

A.2
Further details of comparison

We have compared our methods, Algorithm
1
and Algorithm
2
, with various existing FL methods in the introduction (Section
1
).
Here, let us further elaborate this comparison in more detail.
Due to the rapid development of FL in the last few years, it is impossible to review a majority of works in this field.
Hence, we only select a few algorithms that we find most related to our work in this paper.

•

FedAvg:
FedAvg
[
29
]
has become a de facto standard federated learning algorithm in practice.
However, it has several limitations as discussed in many papers, including
[
23
]
.
It is also difficult to analyze convergence of FedAvg, especially in the nonconvex case and heterogeneity settings (both statistical and system heterogeneity).
Moreover, FedAvg originally specifies SGD with a fixed number of epochs and a fixed learning rate as its local solver, making it less flexible in practice.
Convergence analysis of FedAvg requires additional assumptions apart from the standard smoothness of
f_{i}
.
Moreover, its extension to the composite setting, e.g., in
[
47
]
only focuses on the convex case, and requires a set of strong assumptions, including bounded heterogeneity.
Since it was proposed, several attempts have been made to analyze convergence of FedAvg in both convex and nonconvex settings, see, e.g.,
[
10
,
11
,
24
,
26
,
43
]
.

•

FedProx:
FedProx proposed in
[
23
]
, on the one hand, can be viewed as an extension of FedAvg, but on the other hand, can be cast into a quadratic penalty-type method for the constrained reformulation (
11
) of (
1
). Indeed, when
g=0
, from (
11
), we can define a quadratic penalty function with a penalty parameter
\mu>0
as follows:

P_{\mu}(\mathbf{x}):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x_{i})+\frac{\mu}{2n}\sum_{i=2}^{n}\|x_{i}-x_{n+1}\|^{2}.

First, we apply an alternating minimization strategy to minimize
P_{\mu}
over
[x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}]
and then over
x_{n+1}
.
Next, instead using the full minimization over all blocks
x_{1},\cdots,x_{n}
, a block coordinate descent strategy is applied by selecting a subset of blocks
\mathcal{S}_{k}\subseteq[n]
at random.
Finally, we replace the exact minimization problem of each block
x_{i}
by its inexact computation.
This method exactly leads to FedProx in
[
23
]
.
While FedProx can potentially handle a major heterogeneity challenge, it relies on a [local] dissimilarity assumption, which could be difficult to check.
In addition, this assumption limits the application of FedProx.

•

Other methods:

FedPD
proposed in
[
49
]
is exactly an augmented Lagrangian method applying to the constrained reformulation (
11
) of (
1
) when
g=0
, combining with an alternating minimization strategy as in FedProx.
However, FedPD requires all users to update their computation and flips a biased coin to decide if a global communication is carried out.
This method essentially violates one crucial requirement of FL, which is known as system heterogeneity.
Another FL method is
FedSplit
in
[
33
]
, which also requires all users to participate into each communication round.
This method also relies on Peaceman-Rachford splitting scheme
[
1
]
and is different from our algorithms.
Its convergence analysis is only shown for convex problems in
[
33
]
.
However, as shown in
[
33
]
, this scheme can overcome the fundamental statistical heterogeneity challenge in FL.

In contrast to the above methods, our methods developed in this paper always converges under standard assumptions (i.e., only the
L
-smoothness and boundedness from below).
The proposed methods can handle the majority of challenges in FL, including system and statistical heterogeneity.
We also allow one to use any local solver to evaluate
\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}
up to a given adaptive accuracy.
Moreover, our methods can handle convex regularizers (in particular, convex constraints), and can be implemented in an asynchronous manner.

A.3
Preparatory lemmas

We first present a useful lemma to characterize the relationship between
x^{k}_{i}
and
y^{k}_{i}
for all iteration
k
.
Then, we prove a sure descent lemma to establish the main results in the main text.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\{(y^{k}_{i},x^{k}_{i},z_{i}^{k})\}
be generated by Algorithm
1
and (
20
) starting from
z^{0}_{i}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y^{0}_{i})
for all
i\in[n]
as in (
20
).
Then, for all
i\in[n]
and
k\geq 0
, we have

y^{k}_{i}=z^{k}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i}),\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{x}_{i}^{k}=2x_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{k}.

(21)

Proof.

We prove (
21
) by induction.
For
k=0
, due to the initialization step, Step
1
of Algorithm
1
and (
20
) with
z_{i}^{0}:=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta f_{i}}(y_{i}^{0})
, we have
y^{0}_{i}=z^{0}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{0}_{i})
and
\hat{x}_{i}^{0}=2x_{i}^{0}-y_{i}^{0}
as in (
21
).

Suppose that (
21
) holds for all
k\geq 0
, i.e.,
y^{k}_{i}=z^{k}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i})
and
\hat{x}_{i}^{k}=2x_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{k}
.
We will show that (
21
) holds for
k+1
, i.e.
y^{k+1}_{i}=z^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})
and
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=2x_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k+1}
for all
i\in[n]
, respectively.
We have two cases:

•

For any user
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, from the optimality condition of (
20
), we have

\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})+\tfrac{1}{\eta}(z^{k+1}_{i}-y^{k+1}_{i})=0\quad\Rightarrow\quad y^{k+1}_{i}=z^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i}).

Moreover,
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=2x_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k+1}
due to Step
7
of Algoritihm
1
.

•

For any user
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
, since
z_{i}^{k+1}:=z_{i}^{k}
due to (
20
),
x^{k+1}_{i}=x^{k}_{i}
, and
y^{k+1}_{i}=y^{k}_{i}
, we can also write
y^{k+1}_{i}
as

y^{k+1}_{i}=y^{k}_{i}\overset{(*)}{=}z^{k}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i})=z^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i}).

Here,
(*)
follows from our induction assumption.
Moreover, for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we maintain
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=\hat{x}_{i}^{k}
in Algoritihm
1
.
By our induction assumption, and
x^{k+1}_{i}=x^{k}_{i}
and
y^{k+1}_{i}=y^{k}_{i}
, we have
\hat{x}^{k+1}_{i}=\hat{x}_{i}^{k}=2x_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{k}=2x_{i}^{k+1}-y_{i}^{k+1}
.

In summary, both cases above imply that
y^{k+1}_{i}=z^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})
and
\hat{x}_{i}^{k}=2x_{i}^{k}-y_{i}^{k}
hold for all
i\in[n]
, which proves (
21
).
∎

Our next lemma is to bound
\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}
in terms of
\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\{(\bar{x}^{k}_{i},z_{i}^{k},x^{k}_{i})\}
be generated by Algorithm
1
and (
20
), and
\alpha>0
.
Then, for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
and any
\gamma_{1}>0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(1+\gamma_{1})\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2(1+\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}}\big(\|e^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}+\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\big)\Big].\end{array}

(22)

In particular, if
e_{i}^{k}=e_{i}^{k+1}=0
, then
\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\leq\frac{2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

From the update of
y^{k+1}_{i}
and Lemma
A.1
, for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, we have

\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(y^{k+1}_{i}-y^{k}_{i})\overset{\eqref{eq:yk_xk_relation}}{=}\frac{1}{\alpha}(z^{k+1}_{i}-z^{k}_{i})+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}(\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i})).

Using this expression and
\|a+b\|^{2}\leq 2\|a\|^{2}+2\|b\|^{2}
, we can bound
\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}&=&\|\frac{1}{\alpha}(z^{k}_{i}-z^{k+1}_{i})+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}(\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\|z^{k}_{i}-z^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|\nabla f_{i}(z^{k}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(z^{k+1}_{i})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\|z^{k+1}_{i}-z^{k}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}L^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|z^{k+1}_{i}-z^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\quad(\text{by the $L$-smoothness of $f_{i}$})\\
&=&\frac{2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}-e^{k+1}_{i}+e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\quad\text{(by \eqref{eq:z_i^k_var})}\\
&\leq&\frac{2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\Big[(1+\gamma_{1})\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2(1+\gamma_{1})}{\gamma_{1}}\big(\|e^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}+\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\big)\Big].\end{array}

Here, we have used Young’s inequality twice in the last inequality.
This proves (
22
).
When
e_{i}^{k}=e_{i}^{k+1}=0
, we can set
\gamma_{1}=0
in the above estimate to obtain the last statement.
∎

We still need to link the norm
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{k}_{i}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}
to the norm of gradient mapping
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|
.

Lemma A.3
.

Let
\{(\bar{x}^{k}_{i},x^{k}_{i},z_{i}^{k})\}
be generated by Algorithm
1
and (
20
), and
\alpha>0
and
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
be defined by (
4
).
Then, for any
\gamma_{2}>0
, we have

\hskip-0.43057pt\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{n\eta^{2}}\bigg\{(1+\eta L)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[(1+\gamma_{2})\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\gamma_{2})}{\gamma_{2}}\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\Big]\bigg\}.\hskip-8.61108pt

(23)

In particular, if
e_{i}^{k}=0
for all
i\in[n]
, then we have
\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1+\eta L)^{2}}{n\eta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}
.

Proof.

From Step
9
of Algorithm
1
and (
21
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\tilde{x}^{k}\overset{\tiny\text{Step~\tiny\ref{step:o5}}}{=}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{x}^{k}_{i}\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:yk_xk_relation}}{=}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2x^{k}_{i}-y^{k}_{i})\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:yk_xk_relation}}{=}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}(2x_{i}^{k}-z_{i}^{k}-\eta\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k})).\end{array}

(24)

From the definition (
4
) of
\mathcal{G}_{\eta}
and the update of
\bar{x}^{k}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\eta\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:grad_mapping}}{=}&\|\bar{x}^{k}-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}(\bar{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla{f}(\bar{x}^{k}))\|\\
&=&\|\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k}\big)-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}(\bar{x}^{k}-\eta\nabla{f}(\bar{x}^{k}))\|\\
&\leq&\|\tilde{x}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}+\eta\nabla{f}(\bar{x}^{k})\|\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:lma5_proof1}}{=}&\frac{1}{n}\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}[(2x_{i}^{k}-z_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k})+\eta(\nabla{f_{i}}(\bar{x}^{k})-\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k})]\|,\end{array}

where we have used the non-expansive property of
\mathrm{prox}_{g}
in the first inequality and
\nabla{f}(\bar{x}^{k})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nabla{f_{i}}(\bar{x}^{k})
in the last line.

Finally, using the
L
-smoothness of
f_{i}
, we can derive from the last inequality that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\eta^{2}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{1}{n^{2}}\Big[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\|2x_{i}^{k}-z_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|+\eta L\|z_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|\big)\Big]^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(\|2x_{i}^{k}-z_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|+\eta L\|z_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|\big)^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[(1+\eta L)\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|+(1+\eta L)\|e_{i}^{k}\|\big]^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{n}(1+\eta L)^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[(1+\gamma_{2})\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\gamma_{2})}{\gamma_{2}}\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\big],\end{array}

which proves (
23
), where
\gamma_{2}>0
.
Here, we have used Young’s inequality in the second and the last inequalities, and
x_{i}^{k}=z_{i}^{k}+e_{i}^{k}
from (
20
) in the third line.
∎

To analyze convergence of Algorithm
1
, we introduce the following Lyapunov function:

V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k}):=g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\Big].

(25)

First, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma A.4
.

Suppose that Assumption
2.1
,
2.2
, and
3.1
hold.
Let
\{(z_{i}^{k},x_{i}^{k},y_{i}^{k},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},\bar{x}^{k})\}

).
Let
V_{\eta}^{k}
be defined by (
25

Then, for any

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\gamma_{3}\eta}E_{k+1}^{2},\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(26)

where

.

E_{k+1}=0
, then we allow
\gamma_{3}=0
.

Proof.

First, from
\bar{x}^{k+1}=\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\big)
at Step
9
of Algorithm
1
, we have
\frac{1}{\eta}(\tilde{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k+1})\in\partial{g}(\bar{x}^{k+1})
.
Using this expression and the convexity of
g
, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}g(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{\eta}\langle\tilde{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(27)

Next, since
y_{i}^{k+1}=z^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla{f_{i}}(z^{k+1}_{i})
due to (
21
) and
x^{k+1}_{i}=z^{k+1}_{i}+e_{i}^{k+1}

\begin{array}[]{lcl}x_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:z_i^k_var}}{=}&z_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k+1})+e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta(\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})-\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k+1}))\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:yk_xk_relation}}{=}&y_{i}^{k+1}+e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1},\end{array}

(28)

where
\xi_{i}^{k+1}:=\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})-\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k+1})
.
Using this relation, we can derive

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\Delta_{k+1}&:=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k+1}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\
&=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\bar{x}^{k}-2x^{k+1}_{i}+(x^{k+1}_{i}+\eta\nabla{f_{i}}(x^{k+1}_{i})),\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:proof_est2_inexact}}{=}&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\bar{x}^{k}-2x^{k+1}_{i}+y_{i}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle\\
&\overset{\tiny\text{Step}~\ref{step:o5}}{=}&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{\eta}\langle\bar{x}^{k}-\tilde{x}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

Summing up this expression and (
27
), and using the definition of
V_{\eta}^{k}
in (
25
), we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k+1}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\big]+g(\bar{x}^{k+1})\\
&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\
&&-{~}\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle.\end{array}

By Young’s inequality and
e_{i}^{k+1}=e_{i}^{k}
for
i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}
due to (
20
), for any
\gamma_{3}>0
, we can estimate

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathcal{T}_{[1]}&:=&\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1},\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{2n\eta}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[\frac{1}{\gamma_{3}}\|e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}+\gamma_{3}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\big]\\

Substituting this inequality into the last estimate, we eventually obtain (
26
).
However, if

, then we can deduce from the above inequality that
\gamma_{3}
can be set to zero.
∎

Lemma A.5

(Sure descent lemma)
.

Suppose that Assumption
2.1
,
2.2
, and
3.1

Let

and (

be defined by (
25

Then, the following estimate holds:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{[2-\alpha(L\eta+1)-2L^{2}\eta^{2}-4\alpha\gamma_{4}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})]}{2\alpha\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{2(1+\eta L)^{2}}{\gamma_{4}\eta\alpha^{2}n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}[\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}],\end{array}

(29)

where
E_{k+1}^{2}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}

\gamma_{3},\gamma_{4}>0

E_{k+1}^{2}=0
, then we allow
\gamma_{3}=0
, and if
e^{k}_{i}=e_{i}^{k+1}=0
for all
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}

\gamma_{4}=0

Proof.

First, using (
26
), we can further derive

\hskip-2.15277pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:V_pro2_inexact}}{\leq}&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\
&&+{~}g(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}}f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta n}\sum_{i\notin\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}g(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2},\end{array}\hskip-25.83325pt

(30)

where in (*) we have used the fact that only users in
\mathcal{S}_{k}
perform update and added/subtracted
x^{k}_{i}
in the term
\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle

On the other hand, from the

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\frac{L}{2n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle\\

&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big[f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\Big]\\

&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle.\end{array}\hskip-8.61108pt

(32)

From the update of
y^{k+1}_{i}
, for
i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}
, and similar to the proof of (
28
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}x^{k}_{i}-\bar{x}^{k}&=&\frac{1}{\alpha}(y^{k}_{i}-y^{k+1}_{i})\\

Consequently, using the last expression and the

-smoothness of
f_{i}
, we can further bound (

) as

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{(1+\eta L)}{2\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{\alpha\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{1}{\alpha n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i},\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle s_{i}^{k},x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{\alpha n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta}{\alpha n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle s_{i}^{k},\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})\rangle\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\
&=&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{\eta}{\alpha n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})\|^{2}+\frac{[\alpha(L\eta+1)-2]}{2\alpha\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\langle s_{i}^{k},(x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i})+\eta(\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}))\rangle\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:L_smooth}}{\leq}&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{\eta L^{2}}{\alpha n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}+\frac{[\alpha(L\eta+1)-2]}{2\alpha\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}\\
&&+\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\big[\frac{1}{\gamma_{4}}\|s_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+2\gamma_{4}\|x_{i}^{k}-x_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}+2\gamma_{4}\eta^{2}\|\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k})-\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})\|^{2}\big]\\
&=&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{[2-\alpha(L\eta+1)-2L^{2}\eta^{2}]}{2\alpha\eta n}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x^{k+1}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\gamma_{4}\eta}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}}\|s_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{2\gamma_{4}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})}{n\eta}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&-{~}\frac{(1-\gamma_{3})}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}+\frac{(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\eta\gamma_{3}}E_{k+1}^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, we bound
\|s_{i}^{k}\|^{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|s_{i}^{k}\|^{2}&=&\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\|e_{i}^{k+1}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k+1}-(e_{i}^{k}+\eta\xi_{i}^{k})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}}\big[\|e_{i}^{k}\|+\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|+\eta\|\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k})-\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k})\|+\eta\|\nabla{f_{i}}(x_{i}^{k+1})-\nabla{f_{i}}(z_{i}^{k+1})\|\big]^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{2(1+\eta L)^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}(\|e_{i}^{k}\|^{2}+\|e_{i}^{k+1}\|^{2}).\end{array}

Substituting this inequality into the last estimate, we obtain (
29
).
The last statement follows from the last statement of Lemmas
A.3
and
A.4
.
∎

(33)

Now, to show the boundedness of

(36)

Next, from (

3.1

, and

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{k}}\|x_{i}^{k+1}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]&\geq&\frac{\alpha^{2}}{2(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})(1+\gamma_{1})}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{p}_{i}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x_{i}^{k}\|^{2}\\

A.5

– The inexact variant

.

(39)

Substituting these estimates into (
23

A.3
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{2(1+\eta L)^{2}(1+\gamma_{2})}{\eta^{2}\beta}\left[V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})\mid\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]\right]\\

\mathcal{F}_{k}

k:=0
to
k:=K
, and multiplying the result by

, we get

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]&\leq&C_{1}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\eta}^{0}(\bar{x}^{0})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[V_{\eta}^{K+1}(\bar{x}^{K+1})\right]\right)\\

5
).

1
.

5

B.1
Asynchronous implementation: Dual-memory approach

Let us provide more details on the implementation of our asynchronous algorithm. When a user finishes its local update, the updated model (or model difference) is sent to the server for a proximal aggregation step.
When the server is performing a proximal aggregation step, other users might need to read from the global model.
To allow concurrent read/write operations, one easy method is to have two models stored on the server, denoted as model 1 and model 2.
At any given time, one model is on “read” state (it is supposed to be read from) and the other will be on “write” state (it will be written on when the server finishes aggregation).
Suppose model 1 is on a “read” state and model 2 is on a “write” state, then all users can read from model 1.
When the server completes the proximal aggregation, model 2 becomes the latest model and it will change to a “read” state while model 1 is on a “write” state.
This implementation detail is also discussed in
[
34
]
, which is termed by a
dual-memory approach
.

B.2

.
Note that
\left\{\mathcal{C}_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 0}
forms a partition of
\Omega
and measurable.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}:=\sigma(\mathcal{C}_{k})
be the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
\mathcal{C}_{k}
and
\mathcal{F}:=\sigma(\cup_{k=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{C}_{k})
.
Clearly,
\left\{\mathcal{F}_{k}\right\}_{k\geq 0}
forms a filtration such that
\mathcal{F}_{k}\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{k+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathcal{F}
for
k\geq 0
that is sufficient to cope with the evolution of Algorithm
2
.

For each
\mathcal{C}_{k}(\xi^{0:k})
we also equip with a probability
\mathbf{p}(\xi^{0:k}):=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{k}(\xi^{0:k}))
.
Then,
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
forms a probability space.
Our conditional probability is defined as
\mathbf{p}((i,d)\mid\xi^{0:k}):=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{k+1}(\xi^{0:k+1}))/\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{k}(\xi^{0:k}))
, where we set
\mathbf{p}((i,d)\mid\xi^{0:k}):=0
if
\mathbf{p}(\xi^{0:k})=0
.
We do not need to know these probabilities in advance.
They are determined based on the particular system such as hardware architecture, software implementation, asynchrony, and our strategy for selecting active user.

Now, if
X
is a random variable defined on
\Omega
, then as shown in
[
5
]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[X\mid\mathcal{F}_{k}]=\sum_{(i,d)\in[n]\times\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{p}((i,d)\mid\xi^{0:k})X(\xi^{0:k},(i,d)).

(50)

Note from Assumption
4.1
that

\mathbf{p}(i\mid\xi^{0:k}):=\sum_{d\in\mathcal{D}}\mathbf{p}((i,d)\mid\xi^{0:k})\geq\hat{\mathbf{p}}.

(51)

Our probability model described above allows us to handle a variety class of asynchronous algorithms derived from the DR splitting scheme.
Here, we do not make independent assumption between the active user
\hat{i}_{k}
and the delay vector
\hat{d}^{k}
.

Lemma B.1

(Sure descent)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and

\{(x^{k}_{i},y^{k}_{i},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},\tilde{x}^{k},\bar{x}^{k})\}

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{ll}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&+{~}\frac{\tau}{n\eta}\sum_{l=k+1-\tau}^{k}(l-k+\tau)\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2}\leq V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}\\

(53)

where

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\rho&:=&\begin{cases}\frac{2(1-\alpha)-(2+\alpha)L^{2}\eta^{2}-L\alpha\eta}{\alpha\eta n}&\text{if}\quad 2\tau^{2}\leq n,\\

Proof.

Let
V_{\eta}^{k}
be defined by (
25
).
For
(x_{i}^{k},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},y_{i}^{k})
updated as in Algorithm
2
, the results of Lemma

still hold true.
Hence, (
26
) still holds for Algorithm
2
with
\gamma_{3}=0
and

, i.e.:

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k+1}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k+1}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\
&&-{~}\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

Using this inequality, the update of
x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}
for
i=i_{k}
, and
x^{k+1}_{i}=x_{i}^{k}
for
i\neq i_{k}
, we can expand

\hskip-0.6458pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}\big[f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{2\eta n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}+x^{k}_{i_{k}}-x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-30.1388pt

(54)

Now, by the

Plugging this inequality into (

) and expanding the third last term of (

), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i\neq i_{k}}\big[f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\langle\nabla{f_{i}}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}})+\frac{L}{2n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{2\eta n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2\eta n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{1}{\eta n}\langle x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}},x^{k}_{i_{k}}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle\\
&&-{~}\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&g(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big[f_{i}(x^{k}_{i})+\langle\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}_{i}),\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\rangle+\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\big]\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{\eta n}\langle x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}},x^{k}_{i_{k}}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}}),\bar{x}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}\rangle+\frac{(1+L\eta)}{2\eta n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{\eta n}\langle x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}},x^{k}_{i_{k}}-\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta n}\langle x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}},\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle\\

(55)

From

at Step

\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}=\frac{1}{\alpha}(y^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-y^{k}_{i_{k}})\overset{\eqref{eq:yk_xk_relation}}{=}\frac{1}{\alpha}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}})+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}(\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}})).

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}}),\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\rangle&=&\frac{1}{\alpha n}\langle\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}}),x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\rangle\\
&&+{~}\frac{\eta}{\alpha n}\|\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}})\|^{2},\end{array}\hskip-4.30554pt

(57)

and

\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{(1+L\eta)}{2\eta n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{\alpha n}\|\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{n}\langle\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}}),\bar{x}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}\rangle+\frac{1}{\eta n}\langle x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}},\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-\bar{x}^{k}\rangle\\

Next, using Young’s inequality twice in the above estimate, we can further expand

\hskip-1.07639pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&\leq&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{\alpha(L\eta+1)+2L^{2}\eta^{2}-2}{2\eta\alpha n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}+\frac{\eta}{2n}\|\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k+1}_{i_{k}})-\nabla f_{i_{k}}(x^{k}_{i_{k}})\|^{2}\\

&&-{~}\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\

&&+{~}\frac{1}{\eta n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&=&V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{[\alpha(L^{2}\eta^{2}+L\eta+2)+2L^{2}\eta^{2}-2]}{2\alpha\eta n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}\frac{1}{n\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-21.52771pt

(59)

Using (
52
), we can bound

as follows:

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\bar{x}^{k-d^{k}_{i_{k}}}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:delay_update}}{=}&\big\|\sum_{l\in J_{i_{k}}^{k}}(\bar{x}^{l}-\bar{x}^{l+1})\big\|^{2}\\

&&+{~}\tau^{2}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-38.74988pt

(60)

Now, we consider two cases as follows.

Case 1:
If
n\geq 2\tau^{2}
, then by plugging (
60
) into (
59
), we finally arrive at

\begin{array}[]{ll}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&+{~}\frac{\tau}{n\eta}\sum_{l=k-\tau+1}^{k}[l-(k-\tau)]\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2}\leq V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})\\

&-{~}\frac{[2(1-\alpha)-(2+\alpha)\eta^{2}L^{2}-\alpha\eta L]}{2\alpha\eta n}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}-\frac{(n-2\tau^{2})}{2n\eta}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.\end{array}

if

, then using (

), we can show that

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\bar{x}^{k+1}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}&=&\big\|\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k+1}\big)-\mathrm{prox}_{\eta g}\big(\tilde{x}^{k}\big)\big\|^{2}\leq\|\tilde{x}^{k+1}-\tilde{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\

\begin{array}[]{ll}V_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})&+{~}\frac{\tau}{n\eta}\sum_{l=k-\tau+1}^{k}[l-(k-\tau)]\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2}\leq V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})\\

).

Lemma B.2

(Sure descent lemma)
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and
4.1
hold.
Let
\left\{(x^{k}_{i},y^{k}_{i},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},\tilde{x}^{k},\bar{x}^{k})\right\}

2
and

be defined as in (

).

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k}):=V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{1}{n\eta}\sum_{l=k-\tau}^{k-1}[l-(k-\tau)+1]\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(62)

Suppose that we choose
0<\alpha<\bar{\alpha}
and
0<\eta<\bar{\eta}
, where

\begin{array}[]{ll}&\bar{\alpha}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if}~2\tau^{2}\leq n,\\

Then, the following statement holds:

\frac{\rho}{2}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2}\leq\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1}),

\rho:=\begin{cases}\frac{2(1-\alpha)-(2+\alpha)L^{2}\eta^{2}-L\alpha\eta}{\alpha\eta n}&\text{if}\quad 2\tau^{2}\leq n,\\

Moreover,
\rho
is positive.

Proof.

If we define
\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k}
as in (

) of Lemma

, i.e.:

\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k}):=V_{\eta}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})+\frac{\tau}{\eta n^{2}}\sum_{l=k-\tau}^{k-1}[l-(k-\tau)+1]\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2},

53
), we have

\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{k+1}(\bar{x}^{k+1})\leq\widetilde{V}^{k}(\bar{x}^{k})-\frac{\rho}{2}\|x^{k+1}_{i_{k}}-x^{k}_{i_{k}}\|^{2},

which is equivalent to (
64
).

Now, we find conditions of
\alpha
and
\eta
such that
\rho
and
\theta
are positive.
We consider two cases as follows.

Case 1:
If
2\tau^{2}\leq n
, then

\rho:=\tfrac{2(1-\alpha)-(2+\alpha)L^{2}\eta^{2}-L\alpha\eta}{\alpha\eta n}.

.

, we require

.

.
These are the choices in (

) when

.

Case 2:
If
2\tau^{2}>n
, then

\rho:=\tfrac{n^{2}[2(1-\alpha)-(2+\alpha)L^{2}\eta^{2}-L\alpha\eta]-\alpha(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})(2\tau^{2}-n)}{\alpha\eta n^{3}}.

Let
c:=\frac{2\tau^{2}-n}{n^{2}}>0
.
In order to guarantee that
\rho>0
, we need to choose
0<\alpha<1
and
\eta>0
such that

\begin{array}[]{ll}&2-2\alpha-\frac{\alpha(2\tau^{2}-n)}{n^{2}}>\big[2+\alpha+\frac{\alpha(2\tau^{2}-n)}{n^{2}}\big]L^{2}\eta^{2}+L\alpha\eta,\\
\text{and}&0<\alpha<\frac{2n^{2}}{2n^{2}+(2\tau^{2}-n)}=\frac{2}{2+c}.\end{array}

Using the definition of
c
, the first condition becomes
2-2\alpha-c\alpha>L\alpha\eta+(2+\alpha+c\alpha)L^{2}\eta^{2}
.
First, we need to impose
2-2\alpha-c\alpha>0
, leading to
0<\alpha<\frac{2}{2+c}
.
Next, we solve the above inequality w.r.t.
\eta>0
to get

0<\eta<\bar{\eta}:=\tfrac{\sqrt{16-8\alpha-(7+4c+4c^{2})\alpha^{2}}-\alpha}{2L[2+(1+c)\alpha]}.

These are the choices in (
63
) when
2\tau^{2}>n
.
To guarantee
\bar{\eta}>0
, we need to choose
\alpha<\frac{4}{1+\sqrt{1+4(2+c+c^{2})}}
.
Combining four conditions of
\alpha
, we get
0<\alpha<\frac{2}{2+c}
.
Finally, we conclude that under the choice of
\alpha
and
\eta
as in (
63
), we have
\rho>0
and
\theta>0
.
∎

Next lemma bounds the term
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\right]
in order to bound
\mathbb{E}\big[\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\big]
.

Lemma B.3
.

Suppose that Assumptions
2.1
,
2.2
, and
4.1
hold.
Let
\left\{(x^{k}_{i},y^{k}_{i},\hat{x}^{k}_{i},\bar{x}^{k})\right\}
be generated by Algorithm
2
.
Then, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\right]\leq D\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\right],

(65)

where
D:=\frac{8\alpha^{2}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2})(\tau^{2}+2Tn\hat{\mathbf{p}})\ +\ 8n^{2}(1+L^{2}\eta^{2}+T\alpha^{2}\hat{\mathbf{p}})}{\hat{\mathbf{p}}\alpha^{2}n^{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
t_{k}(i):=\min\left\{t\in\{0,\cdots,T\}:\mathbf{p}(i\mid\xi^{0:k+t-1})\geq\hat{\mathbf{p}}\right\}
.
In fact,
t_{k}(i)
is the first time in the iteration window
[k,k+T]
, user
i
is active, i.e. gets updated.
For any
\gamma\in(0,1)
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right](\omega)&=&\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{p}(i\mid\xi^{0:t-1})\|\bar{x}^{t}-x^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:assA4_01}}{\geq}&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{(*)}{\geq}&\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|-\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-(\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i})\|\right]^{2}\\
&\geq&-2\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-(\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i})\|\\
&&+{~}\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{(**)}{\geq}&\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\right]\\
&&-{~}4\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}-4\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2},\end{array}

where (*) comes from the reverse triangle inequality
\|a-b\|^{2}\geq(\|a\|-\|b\|)^{2}
and (**) is due to
4\|v\|^{2}+4\|s\|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}\geq 2\|u\|\|v+s\|
.
Note that the conditional expectation above is only taken w.r.t.
\hat{i}_{k}
, which is
\sigma(d^{k},\mathcal{F}_{k-1})
-measurable.
For simplicity of notation, we drop
(\omega)
in the sequel.

Rearranging the last inequality, we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}&\leq&\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]+4\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}\\
&&+{~}4\hat{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(66)

Next, we bound the term
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k+t_{k}(i)}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}&=&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\sum_{t=k}^{k+t_{k}(i)-1}(\bar{x}^{t+1}-\bar{x}^{t})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{k}(i)\sum_{t=k}^{k+t_{k}(i)-1}\|\bar{x}^{t+1}-\bar{x}^{t}\|^{2}\qquad\text{(Young's inequality)}\\
&\leq&T\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{t=k}^{k+t_{k}(i)-1}\|\bar{x}^{t+1}-\bar{x}^{t}\|^{2}\qquad\text{(since $t_{k}(i)\leq T$)}\\
&=&nT\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|\bar{x}^{t+1}-\bar{x}^{t}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:asdr_eq5_2b}}{\leq}&\frac{2T(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{n}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}.\end{array}\hskip-17.22217pt

(67)

We can also bound
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{k+t_{k}(i)}_{i}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}&=&\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\sum_{t=k}^{k+t_{k}(i)-1}(x^{t+1}_{i}-x^{t}_{i})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{k}(i)\sum_{t=k}^{k+t_{k}(i)-1}\|x^{t+1}_{i}-x^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\qquad\text{(Young's inequality)}\\
&\leq&T\sum_{t=k}^{k+T-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x^{t+1}_{i}-x^{t}_{i}\|^{2}\qquad\text{(since $t_{k}(i)\leq T$)}\\
&=&T\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\quad\text{(since only user $i_{t}$ is updated at iteration $t$)}.\end{array}\hskip-25.83325pt

(68)

Let us bound the first term on the right-hand side of (
66
) as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]&\leq&2\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t-d^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\\
&&+{~}2\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t}-\bar{x}^{t-d^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].\end{array}\hskip-12.91663pt

(69)

However, similar to the proof of (
60
) and (
61
), we can show that

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|\bar{x}^{t}-\bar{x}^{t-d^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}}\|^{2}&\overset{\tiny\eqref{eq:asdr_eq5_1}}{\leq}&\tau\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\sum_{l=t-\tau}^{t-1}\|\bar{x}^{l+1}-\bar{x}^{l}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\tau^{2}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\|\bar{x}^{t+1}-\bar{x}^{t}\|^{2}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:asdr_eq5_2b}}{\leq}&\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\frac{2\tau^{2}(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{n^{2}}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(70)

On the other hand, by using (
56
), we have

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\|\bar{x}^{t-d^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}&=&\|y^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-y^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\qquad\text{(by the update of $y_{\hat{i}_{k}}^{k}$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:A2})}\\
&\overset{\eqref{eq:asdr_eq5_10}}{=}&\big\|\frac{1}{\alpha}(x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}})+\frac{\eta}{\alpha}(\nabla f_{\hat{i}_{t}}(x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}})-\nabla f_{\hat{i}_{t}}(x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}))\big\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{2}{\alpha^{2}}\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}+\frac{2\eta^{2}}{\alpha^{2}}\|\nabla f_{\hat{i}_{t}}(x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}})-\nabla f_{\hat{i}_{t}}(x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}})\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}.\end{array}

(71)

Therefore, plugging (
70
) and (
71
) into (
69
), we have

\hskip 0.0pt\begin{array}[]{lcl}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{t}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]&\leq&\frac{4\tau^{2}(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{n^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\\
&&+{~}\frac{4(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{\alpha^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\\
&=&\frac{4(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})[\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}+n^{2}]}{n^{2}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right].\end{array}\hskip-15.0694pt

(72)

Substituting (
67
), (
68
), and (
72
) into (
66
), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{\hat{\mathbf{p}}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}&\leq&\frac{4(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})[\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}+n^{2}]}{n^{2}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\\
&&+\frac{8\hat{\mathbf{p}}T(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})}{n}\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}+4\hat{\mathbf{p}}T\sum_{t=k}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\\
&\leq&\frac{4(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})[\tau^{2}\alpha^{2}+n^{2}]}{n^{2}\alpha^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{\hat{i}_{t}}-x^{t}_{\hat{i}_{t}}\|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t-1}\right]\\
&&+{~}\frac{4\hat{\mathbf{p}}T[2(1+\eta^{2}L^{2})+n]}{n}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}.\end{array}

Finally, taking full expectation both sides of the last inequality w.r.t.
\sigma(d^{k},\mathcal{F}_{k-1})
, and multiplying the result by

, we arrive at

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\bar{x}^{k}-x^{k}_{i}\|^{2}\right]\leq D\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\right],

where

B.4
The proof of Theorem
4.1
: Convergence of Algorithm
2

By Assumption
4.1
, for each
T
iterations, the probability of each user
i
getting updated is at least
\hat{\mathbf{p}}>0
.
Hence, from (
64
) of Lemma
B.2
, we sum up from
t:=k-\tau
to

, and have

\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\leq\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t}(\bar{x}^{t})-\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t+1}(\bar{x}^{t+1})\big],

where
\rho>0
is given in Lemma
B.2
.
Now, take full expectation both sides of this inequality w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{k}
, we obtain

\frac{\rho}{2}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\big[\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\big]\leq\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\Big[\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t}(\bar{x}^{t})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t+1}(\bar{x}^{t+1})\big]\Big].

(73)

Next, using (
23
) from Lemma
A.3
with
\gamma_{2}=0
, we have

\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\leq\frac{(1+\eta L)^{2}}{n\eta^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_{i}^{k}-\bar{x}^{k}\|^{2}.

), we obtain

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]&\leq&\frac{(1+\eta L)^{2}D}{n\eta^{2}}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\mathbb{E}\left[\|x^{t+1}_{i_{t}}-x^{t}_{i_{t}}\|^{2}\right],\end{array}

where

is given in Lemma

.

Combining the last inequality and (

), we arrive at

\begin{array}[]{l}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2(1+\eta L)^{2}D}{n\eta^{2}\rho}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\left(\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t}(\bar{x}^{t})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t+1}(\bar{x}^{t+1})\big]\right).\end{array}

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\frac{1}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\mathcal{G}_{\eta}(\bar{x}^{k})\|^{2}\right]&\leq&\frac{\hat{C}}{K+1}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{t=k-\tau}^{k+T}\left[\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t}(\bar{x}^{t})\big]-\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{V}_{\eta}^{t+1}(\bar{x}^{t+1})\big]\right]\\

10

Using the definition of
\rho
,
\theta
, and

into

.
The remaining conclusion of the theorem is a direct consequence of (

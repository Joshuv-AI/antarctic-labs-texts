Convergence of Proximal Splitting Algorithms in CAT( κ \kappa ) Spaces and Beyond
Florian Lauster, D. Russell Luke

Abstract
In the setting of CAT( κ \kappa ) spaces, common fixed point iterations built from prox mappings (e.g. prox-prox, Krasnoselsky-Mann relaxations, nonlinear projected-gradients) converge locally linearly under the assumption of linear metric subregularity. Linear metric subregularity is in any case necessary for linearly convergent fixed point sequences, so the result is tight. To show this, we develop a theory of fixed point mappings that violate the usual assumptions of nonexpansiveness and firm nonexpansiveness in p p -uniformly convex spaces.

Before continuing with pointwise almost

brief but important observation about fixed points of compositions of quasi strictly nonexpansive

Lemma 7
.

Let

and
T_{2}
be quasi strictly nonexpansive on
(G,d)
with

\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2}\neq\emptyset

\Fix T_{1}\circ T_{2}=\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2}

Proof.

The inclusion
\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2}\subset\Fix\left(T_{1}\circ T_{2}\right)
is clear.
Assume there exists a
y\in\Fix(T_{1}\circ T_{2})\setminus(\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2})
and choose
x\in\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2}
.
Then

d(y,x)\leq d(T_{1}\circ T_{2}y,x)\leq d(T_{2}y,x)\leq d(y,x)

with either

or
d(T_{1}\circ T_{2}y,x)<d(T_{2}y,x)
as

.
This is a contradiction, so
\Fix T_{1}\circ T_{2}=\Fix T_{1}\cap\Fix T_{2}

∎

The next result shows that the convex combination of an almost nonexpansive mapping with the identity mapping

Proposition 12

(Krasnoselsky-Mann relaxations)
.

Let
(G,d)
be a
p
-uniformly convex space and

be pointwise almost nonexpansive at all

with violation

. Then

is pointwise almost
\alpha
-firmly
nonexpansive at all

with constant

\alpha_{\beta}=\frac{\alpha\beta^{p-1}}{\alpha\beta^{p-1}-\alpha\beta+1}

and violation
\epsilon_{\beta}:=\epsilon\beta
.

Proof.

Clearly
\Fix T=\Fix T_{\beta}
and
d(x,T_{\beta}x)^{p}=\beta^{p}d(x,Tx)^{p}
.
Let

then

\displaystyle d(y,T_{\beta}x)^{p}

\displaystyle=d(y,\beta Tx\oplus(1-\beta)x)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\beta d(y,Tx)^{p}+(1-\beta)d(y,x)^{p}-\frac{c}{2}\beta(1-\beta)d(x,Tx)^{p}

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon\beta)d(x,y)^{p}-\frac{c}{2}\beta^{1-p}\left(\tfrac{1-\alpha}{\alpha}+1-\beta\right)d(x,T_{\beta}x)^{p}.

Setting

Definition 13

(17)

, the quantifier “relative to” is dropped.

6
Open Problems

There are two obvious next steps for this work. First and foremost is to determine the
requirements for quantitative convergence of proximal splitting methods for the
case when the individual prox mappings do not have common fixed points – the so-called

inconsistent case
– since it is too limiting to require that the fixed points of the
constituent elements of splitting methods coincide. The second item to explore is qualitative settings in which
metric subregularity comes “for free”. In linear settings, polyhedrality and isolated
fixed points suffice to guarantee metric subregularity
[
7
, Propositions 3I.1 and 3I.2]
and this was
successfully used to prove local linear convergence of the ADMM/Douglas-Rachford algorithms in a convex setting

[
2
, Theorem 2.7]
. In more general settings,
the Kurdyka-Łojasiewicz (KL) property – which is equivalent to metric subregularity
[
6
, Corollary 4 and Remark 5]

– is satisfied by
semi-algebraic
functions
[
5
]
.
Analogues to these properties
for
p
-uniformly convex spaces would be very useful.

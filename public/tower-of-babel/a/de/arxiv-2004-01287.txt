Abstract

We determine the irreducible 2-modular representations of the symplectic group
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
whose restrictions to every abelian subgroup has a trivial constituent. A similar result is obtained for maximal tori of
G
. There is significant information on
the existence of eigenvalue 1 of elements of
G
in a given irreducible representations of
G
.

1.
Introduction

The primary motivation of this work lies in the study of eigenvalues of semisimple elements of groups of Lie type
in their representations over fields of the natural characteristic. This problem is not treatable in full generality,
however, a number of special cases of it were discussed in literature. In
[
23
]
there appeared a number of useful observations, and certain comments are available in surveys
[
24
]
and
[
25
]
. Occurrance of eigenvalue 1 deserves a particular attention, as this carries some geometrical meaning, and is significant in applications. See
[
7
]
, where one can learn the level of difficulty of the eigenvalue 1 problem. In general, relatively little is known.

Other motivations come from the study of finite groups with disconected prime graphs

[
12
,
13
]
, from a characterization of groups by the set of their element orders
[
29
, Lemma 10]
, from the study of decomposition numbers
[
27
]
, from other applications described in
[
7
]
and
[
25
]
,
in each case with a specific aspect of the general problem. The complexity of the problem in general justifies considerations of special cases,
one of these is dealt with in the current paper, where we specify the problem to the symplectic group
Sp_{2n}(2)
,
n>1
. Note that the group
GL_{n}(2)
is dealt with in
[
28
]
.

To state our results,
we need a parametrization of the irreducible representations of
G
. This is available in terms of algebraic groups.
So we first recall some fact of algebraic group representation theory, specified here to the group

\mathbf{G}=Sp_{2n}(F)
, where
F
is algebraically closed field of characteristic 2.

Note that every 2-modular irreducible representation of
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
lifts to a representation of the algebraic group
\mathbf{G}=Sp_{2n}(F)
. The irreducible representations
\phi
of
\mathbf{G}
are parameterized by
so called highest weights, equivalently, by the strings of non-negative integers
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
An irreducible representation of
\mathbf{G}
(and the highest weight of it) is called 2-
restricted
if
0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\leq 1
. If
\phi
is 2-restricted
then the restriction
\phi|_{G}
is irreducible and all irreducible representations of
G
are obtainable in this way. Moreover, the irreducible representations
\rho
of
G
are parameterized by the strings
\omega=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
with
0\leq a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\leq 1
. So we write
\rho_{\omega}
to specify the parameter.
Set
\omega_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
with 1 at the
i
-th position.

For a group
X
let
1_{X}
be the trivial one-dimensional representation of
X
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
, and let
\rho
be an irreducible F-representation of G with highest weight
\omega=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
Then
1_{A}
occurs as a constituent of the restriction
\rho|_{A}
for every abelian subgroup A of G if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

a_{n}=0
and either
\sum a_{i}
is even or
\sum a_{i}>2;

(2)

a_{n}=1
and
\sum a_{i}i\geq 2n
.

Theorem
1.1
yields a sufficient condition for an arbitrary element
g\in G
to have eigenvalue 1
in a given irreducible representation
\rho
. Obtaining a sharp necessary condition for an arbitrary
g
does not seem to be
a realistic task; in Theorem
1.3
we provide a criterion for
\rho(g)
to have eigenvalue 1 for every
g\in G
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
n
be a natural number. Then the Singer height
{\rm Si}(n)
of n is the maximum number l such that there are natural numbers
n_{1},\ldots,n_{l}
such that
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{l}\leq n
and the numbers
2^{n_{1}}+1,\ldots,2^{n_{l}}+1
are coprime to each other.

Obviously,
1\leq{\rm Si}(m)\leq{\rm Si}(n)
for
m<n
, but in general the behavior of
{\rm Si}(n)
is quite irregular.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\rho_{\omega}
be an irreducible representation of
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
with highest weight
\omega=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
.
Then
1
is an eigenvalue of
\rho(g)
for every
g\in G
if and only if one of the following holds:

(1)

a_{n}=0
and
\omega\neq\omega_{i}
for
i
odd;

(2)

a_{n}=1
and
\sum a_{i}i\geq n+{\rm Si}(n)
.

Using terminology of
[
7
, Definition 1.2]
, a representation
\rho
of a group
G
is called
unisingular
if
\rho(g)
has eigenvalue 1 for every
g\in G
. So Theorem
1.3
classifies irreducible unisingular representations of
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
in characteristic 2. Theorem
1.4
refines this result for elements of prime-power order.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
G=Sp_{2n}(2)
and let
\rho
be an irreducible
F
-representation of
G
with highest weight
\omega
. Suppose that
\omega\neq\omega_{i}
for
i
odd or
i=n
.
If
g\in G
is of prime power order then
1
is an eigenvalue of
\rho(g)
.

We have rather precise information on eigenvalues of
\rho_{\omega_{n}}(g)
for an arbitrary semisimple element
g\in G
, see Lemma
3.11
, and also of
\rho_{\omega}(g)
for
a_{n}=1
(Theorem
4.5
). However,
the occurrence of eigenvalue 1 of
\rho_{\omega_{i}}(g)
with
i<n
odd for an arbitrary
g\in G
does not reveal
good regularities, and we have no uniform result even for
p
-elements
g
.
If

then
\rho(g)
has eigenvalue 1 for every
g\in G
whenever so has

for

of order

. Similar ”testing” elements exist also when

Another test is Proposition

As a by-product we obtain the following result on the occurrence of weight 0 in an irreducible representation

of the algebraic group

Theorem 1.5
.

Let

be a

Note that, in view of

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The weight system of type
C_{n}

For a precise definition of weight system of type
C_{n}
see
[
1
]
. We recall
here some notions of the theory.

A
weight lattice

\Omega
is formed by
strings of integers
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, which are called
weights of

\Omega
, and those with non-negative
entries
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
are called
dominant weights
.
The subset of dominant weights is denoted by
\Omega^{+}
.
The weights
\omega_{i}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)

(1\leq i\leq n)
, where
1
occupies the
i
-th position, are
called
fundamental
, and the weight
(0,\ldots,0)
is called the zero weight. We usually simplify this by denoting this by 0. So arbitrary weights can be written as
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\omega_{i}
with
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathop{\mathbb{Z}}\nolimits
. For an integer
m>1
a dominant weight

(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
or
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\omega_{i}
is called
m
-
restricted
if
a_{i}<m
for
i=1,\ldots,n
. (We use this mainly for
m=2.
) We denote by
\Omega^{\prime}
the subset of weights
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n-1},0)
.

The theory of weight system of type
C_{n}
singles out a sublattice
\mathcal{R}
of index
2
in
\Omega
called the
root lattice
. In addition, one singles out some elements
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathcal{R}
called
simple roots
, which form a
\mathop{\mathbb{Z}}\nolimits
-basis of
\mathcal{R}
. Denote by
\mathcal{R}^{+}
the set of non-negative
linear combinations of simple roots. For weights
\omega,\mu
one writes
\mu\preceq\omega

if
\omega-\mu\in\mathcal{R}^{+}
; if additionally
\omega\neq\mu
we write
\mu\prec\omega
. If
\mu\prec\omega
are dominant then we say that
\mu
is a subdominant weight for
\omega
.
The weights
\omega\in\mathcal{R}
are called
radical
. We write
0\prec\mu
to state that the weight 0 is a subdomininant weight of
\mu
.

The theory of weights can be viewed as a part of linear algebra, and it is somehow independent from the theory of algebraic groups, see for instance
[
1
]
, where detailed data on relations between
fundamental weights and simple roots are tabulated in
[
1
, Planche III]
.

In our analysis an essential role is plaid by another basis
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
of
\Omega
; specifically
\varepsilon_{1}=\omega_{1}
and

\varepsilon_{i}=\omega_{i}-\omega_{i-1}
for
i=2,\ldots,n
.

We have
\omega_{i}=\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, and
\omega_{i}
is radical if and only if
i
is even. The simple roots are
\alpha_{i}=\varepsilon_{i}-\varepsilon_{i+1}

for
i<n
, and
\alpha_{n}=2\varepsilon_{n}
. Note that
\alpha_{i}=2\omega_{i}-\omega_{i-1}-\omega_{i+1}
for
i<n
, and
\alpha_{1}=2\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}
,
\alpha_{n}=2\omega_{n}-2\omega_{n-1}
.

The Weyl group
W
of
\mathbf{G}
(or the weight system
\Omega
) acts on
\Omega
as a group of linear transformations.
Specifically,
W
transitively permutes
\pm\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\pm\varepsilon_{n}
. In fact,
W
has an elementary abelian normal subgroup
W_{1}

which acts on
\pm\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\pm\varepsilon_{n}
by changing the signs, and
W
is a semidirect product
W_{1}\cdot S_{n}
, where
S_{n}
is the symmetric group; the latter acts on
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
by permutations.
It follows that the
W
-orbit of
\omega_{i}=\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i}
consists of weights
\pm\varepsilon_{j_{1}}\pm\cdots\pm\varepsilon_{j_{i}}
,
where
\varepsilon_{j_{1}},\ldots,\varepsilon_{j_{i}}\in\{\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}\}
and
j_{1}<\cdots<j_{i}
.

For a weight
\mu=\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}
set
\delta(\mu)=\sum a_{i}i
and
\gamma(\mu)=\sum a_{i}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

(1)

\delta(\alpha_{i})=0
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
, and
\delta(\alpha_{n})=2
.

(2)

\gamma(\alpha_{i})=0
for
i=2,\ldots,n-1
, and
\gamma(\alpha_{1})=1=\gamma(\alpha_{n})
.

(3)
Let
\omega,\nu\in\Omega
. If
\omega>\mu
then
\delta(\omega)\geq\delta(\mu)
and
\delta(\omega)-\delta(\mu)

is even. In particular,
\omega\in{\mathcal{R}}
if and only if
\delta(\omega)
is even.

Proof. (1) and (2) follow straightforwardly from the above expressions of the simple roots in terms of the dominant weights,
and (3) follows from (1) and the definition of the partial order
\omega>\mu
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\omega
be a dominant weight of
C_{n}
.

(1)
If
0\prec\omega
then
\omega_{2}\preceq\omega.

(2)
If
\omega
is not radical then
\omega_{1}\preceq\omega.

(3)

Let

\omega=\sum a_{i}\omega_{i}

and
c=\sum a_{i}
. Then
c\omega_{1}\preceq\omega
or
(c-1)\omega_{1}\prec(c-1)\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\prec\omega.

In addition, if
\omega_{1}\prec\omega
and
c>2
then
3\omega_{1}\preceq\omega
.

(4)

\omega_{i-2}\prec\omega_{i}
for
i=3,\ldots,n
. Moreover,
\omega\prec\omega_{i}
if and only if
i<j
and
j-i
is odd.

(5)
Suppose that
\omega\neq\omega_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. If
\omega_{1}\prec\omega
then
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\preceq\omega
; if
0\prec\omega
then
2\omega_{1}\preceq\omega.

(6)

Let
l=\delta(\omega)
, and
l=j+kn
with
0\leq j<n
. If
j=0
then
k\omega_{n}\preceq\omega
, otherwise
\omega_{i}+k\omega_{n}\preceq\omega
.
In addition, if
k>0
then
\omega_{n}\preceq\omega
if
l-n
is even, and
\omega_{1}+\omega_{n}\preceq\omega
if
l-n
is odd.

Proof. The statements (1),(2) are well known.

(3) Set
c_{1}=\sum_{i~odd}a_{i}
,
c_{2}=\sum_{i~even}a_{i}
.
By (1),(2) we have
\omega_{1}\preceq\omega_{i}
for
i
odd and
\omega_{2}\preceq\omega_{i}
for
i
even. Therefore,

c_{1}\omega_{1}+c_{2}\omega_{2}\preceq\omega
. As
2\omega_{1}\prec 2\omega_{2}
, the result follows if
c_{2}
is even, otherwise
we have
(c-1)\omega_{1}\prec(c-1)\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\prec
. If
\omega_{1}\prec\omega
then
c
is odd in the former case, whence the claim,
and
c
is even in the latter case. Then
c\geq 4
, and the result follows.

(4) The first claim is obvious as
\omega_{i}-\omega_{i-2}=\varepsilon_{i}+\varepsilon_{i-1}
is a positive root
[
1
, Planche III]
. For the second one
follows from Lemma
2.1
(3).

(5) By (3),
c\omega_{1}\preceq\omega
or
(c-1)\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\preceq\omega
. If
\omega_{1}\prec\omega
then
c
is odd in the former case
and even in the latter one. As
(c-2)\omega_{1}\prec c\omega_{1}
for
c>2
, we have
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\prec 3\omega_{1}\preceq\omega
in the former case, and
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\preceq\omega
in the latter case. If
0\prec\omega
then
c
is even in both the cases, whence

2\omega_{1}\preceq\omega
or
2\omega_{1}\prec 2\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}\preceq\omega
, as required.

(6) It suffices to prove this for
\omega
minimal, in the sense that there is no dominant weight
\mu\prec\omega
such that
\delta(\mu)=\delta(\omega)
. As
\delta(\alpha_{i})=0
for
i<n
, it follows that
\omega-\alpha_{i}
is not a dominant weight for every
i=1,\ldots,n-1
. This implies
a_{i}\leq 1
for
i=1,\ldots,n-1
.

Suppose that there are
i<j<n
such that
a_{i}=a_{j}=1
. As
\omega_{i}+\omega_{j}=\omega_{i-1}+\omega_{j+1}+\alpha_{i}+\cdots+\alpha_{j}
, we have
\omega_{i-1}+\omega_{j+1}\prec\omega_{i}+\omega_{j}
and
\delta(\omega_{i}+\omega_{j})=\delta(\omega_{i-1}+\omega_{j+1})
(because
j<n
). This is a contradiction. So there is at most one
i<n
such that
a_{i}>0
, so
\omega=k\omega_{n}
or
\omega_{i}+k\omega_{n}
, as required.

This also implies the additional statement if
l\leq n+1
. Suppose
l>n+1
. Now choose
\omega
to be minimal in the sense that there is no dominant weight
\mu\prec\omega
such that
\delta(\mu)>n+1
. If

a_{n}>1
then
\delta(\omega-\alpha_{n})=l-2
, contradicting the minimality of
\omega
unless

l-2\leq n+1
, that is,
l\leq n+3
. In this case
n+3\geq l=\delta(\omega)\geq\delta(2\omega_{n})=2n
, whence
n\leq 3
.
If
n=3
then
\omega=2\omega_{3}
and
\omega-\alpha_{3}=2\omega_{2}>\omega_{1}+\omega_{3}
as required. If
n=2
then
\omega=\omega_{1}+2\omega_{2}
or
2\omega_{2}
, and
the lemma is true in these cases as
\omega_{2}\prec 2\omega_{1}\prec 2\omega_{2}
.

Let
a_{n}=1
. Then
\omega=\omega_{i}+\omega_{n}
and
0\prec\omega_{i}
if
i
is even, otherwise
\omega_{1}\prec\omega
, whence the result.

2.2.
Abelian subgroups and maximal tori: generalities

Let
V
be the natural
\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits_{2}G
-module and
A
an abelian subgroup of
G
of odd order. By Maschke’s theorem,

V=U_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus U_{m}
, where
U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}
are irreducible
F_{2}A
-modules. Each
U_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,m
, is either non-degenerate or totally isotropic; in the latter case there is a unique
j
such that
U_{i}+U_{j}
is non-degenerate.
Moreover,
U_{j}
is dual to
U_{i}
as
F_{2}A
-modules. We can rearrange the above decomposition and write

V=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}
, where each
V_{i}

In the theory of finite reductive groups an important role belongs to maximal tori, which are abelian groups
of order coprime to the characteristic of the ground field. Every maximal abelian subgroup of odd order is a maximal torus, but not conversely. The definition and general properties of maximal tori is available in

[
4
]
and
[

]
, for specific properties of maximal tori in classical groups used in this paper one can
consult with
[

[

]

[

. The maximal tori in

are in bijective correspondence with the decompositions

defined above, and the conjugacy classes of maximal tori in
G
are in bijective correspondence with the
G
-orbits of the decompositions. These can be
parameterized by the strings
(\pm n_{1},\ldots,\pm n_{k})
up to the ordering, the sign minus is chosen whenever the torus acts
on

irreducibly (

(1,\ldots,1)
corresponds to the torus of one element
\mathop{\rm Id}\nolimits
,
the string
(-1,\ldots,-1)

(so

). A maximal torus of order

is called a

(-n)
. A maximal torus corresponding to

is a cyclic group of order

.
Every maximal torus

Sometimes it is convenient to use

Let

2.3.
Representations and their weights

The above parametrization of the irreducible representations of
G=Sp_{2n}(2)

is based on Steinberg’s theorem
[
10
, §2.7]
saying that the irreducible representations of
Sp_{2n}(2)
are in bijection with the
2
-restricted dominant weights of
\mathbf{G}
.
In fact, the representation theory of
\mathbf{G}
plays a significant role in proving the above result. The representation theory of the algebraic group

Sp_{2n}(F)
is described on the language of the theory of the weight system of type
C_{n}
.

Let
F
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>0
. The group
\mathbf{G}=Sp_{2n}(F)
is defined as the group of
(2n\times 2n)
-matrices over
F
preserving a non-degenerate alternating form
(*,*)
on
V=F^{2n}
. A basis
e_{1},\ldots,e_{2n}
of
V
is called a Witt basis if
(e_{i},e_{2n+1-i})=1
and

(e_{i},e_{j})=0
if
j\neq 2n+1-i
for
i=1,\ldots,n
and
1\leq j\leq 2n
. The subgroup
\mathbf{T}
of diagonal matrices in
\mathbf{G}

under a Witt basis is of shape
\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t_{n}^{-1},\ldots,t_{1}^{-1})
(
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}\in F^{\times}
).
We refer to this group as a reference torus, and define a maximal tori in
\mathbf{G}
as those conjugate to
\mathbf{T}

in
\mathbf{G}
. The weights are defined as rational homomorphisms
\mathbf{T}\rightarrow F^{\times}
, and
\varepsilon_{i}
are defined as those sending

\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t_{n}^{-1},\ldots,t_{1}^{-1})
to
t_{i}
.

The Weyl group
W
of
\mathbf{G}
is defined as
W:=N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})/\mathbf{T}
. The conjugation action of
N_{\mathbf{G}}(\mathbf{T})
on
\mathbf{T}
yields the action of
W
on
\Omega={\rm Hom}(\mathbf{T},F^{\times})
. In this action
W
preserves the set
\{\pm\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\pm\varepsilon_{n}\}
and acts
on this set transitively.
Note that the
W
-orbit of any weight
\omega\in\Omega
contains a unique dominant weight.

For a
\mathbf{G}
-module
V
or a representation
\rho
of
\mathbf{G}
we denote by
\Omega(V)
,
\Omega(\rho)

the set of weights of
V,\rho
, respectively. Then
\Omega(V)
is
W
-invariant, in particular,
\Omega(V)=-\Omega(V)
.

Let

\mu
be a dominant weight and
W
the Weyl group of
\mathbf{G}
.
Denote by
\chi_{\mu}
the sum of distinct weights
w(\mu)
with
w\in W
.
For a semisimple element
g\in G
let

be a conjugate of

in
\mathbf{T}
(it is well known that every semisimple element of
\mathbf{G}
is conjugate to one in
\mathbf{T}
). Then
g^{\prime}
is not unique but the value
\chi_{\mu}(g^{\prime})
does not depend from the choice of
g^{\prime}\in\mathbf{T};
so
\chi_{\mu}
is a well defined function
on the semisimple elements of
G
called an
orbit character
. (This is well known to be a generalized Brauer character of
G
, see
[
10
, §5.7]
.)

If
F=\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits}_{p}
and
\beta_{\omega}
is the Brauer character of
V_{\omega}
then

\beta_{\omega}=\sum m_{\mu}\chi_{\mu}
, where
\mu
runs over the dominant weights of
V_{\omega}
and

is the multiplicity of
\mu
in
V_{\omega}
, see
[

, §2.2 and §5.6]
for details. Therefore,
\beta_{\omega}|_{G}=\sum m_{\mu}\chi_{\mu}|_{G}
; this is used below for computations of eigenvalue 1 of elements

on

.

Lemma 2.5
.

(3)

(3) Clearly, either

[

together with 0 form a subfield
E_{1}
, say, of order
q^{n/2}
. So
h\in E_{1}
. In addition,
n/2
is odd (otherwise
E_{1}
contains
\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits_{q^{2}}
, which is false as
h
is irreducible). Let
d_{1}
be a generator of
E_{1}^{\times}
and

d=\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(d_{1},({}^{t}d_{1}^{-1})^{J}))
and
D=\langle d\rangle
. Then
g\in D
.

Note that
Sp_{n}(q)
contains a cyclic subgroup of order
q^{n/2}-1
.
So the result follows if we show that every cyclic subgroup of
G
of order
q^{n/2}-1
is conjugate to
D
.
For this it suffices to show that
D
contains a Sylow
r
-subgroup
R
of
G
for some prime
r
such that
C_{G}(R)=E^{\times}
. We can assume
n>2
, as
SU_{2}(q)\cong Sp_{2}(q)
,
and
g\in Sp_{2}(q)
is real whenever
(|g|,q)=1
.

By
[
11
, Theorem 5.2.14]
,
q^{n}-1
is divisible by a prime
r

such that
r
does not divide
q^{j}-1

for any
j<n
, unless
q=2,n=6
or
n=2
. By the above,
n>2
; if
(q,n)=(2,6)
then we take
r=7
. Then
|R|=7

and one can easily check that
C_{G}(R)=E^{\times}
.

In the non-exceptional case let
R
be the Sylow
r
-subgroups of
D
. Then
R
is
a Sylow
r
-subgroups of
GL_{n/2}(q^{2})
. Comparing the orders of
GL_{n/2}(q^{2})
and
G
we conclude that

R
remains a Sylow
r
-subgroups of
G
. (Note that
(r,q^{j}+1)=1
for
0<j<n
as otherwise
r
divides
q^{2j}-1

and
(q^{2j}-1,q^{i}-1)=q^{(i,j)}-1
as
i
is odd; this contradicts the choice of
r
as
(i,j)<n
.)
Finally, one easily observe that
C_{G}(R)=E^{\times}
.
\Box

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=SL_{n+1}(F)
,
n
even, and let
\mathbf{H}=SL_{n+1}(F)
. Let
\phi
be an irreducible representation of
\mathbf{L}
with
p
-restricted highest weight
\lambda
. Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
be the fundamental weights of
\mathbf{L}
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}
be those of
\mathbf{H}
.

(1)
Suppose that
n>2
if
p=2
and if
\nu
is the highest weight of a composition factor of
\phi|_{\mathbf{H}}
and
\nu
is
p
-restricted then
\nu\in\{0,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}\}
. Then
\lambda\in\{\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\}
.

(2)
Suppose that
\lambda=\lambda_{i}
with
i\leq n
. Then
\phi_{\lambda}|_{\mathbf{H}}
contains an irreducible constituent of highest weight
\omega_{i-1}
. (If
i=1
then
\omega_{i-1}
is interpreted as the zero weight.)

(3)
Let
\psi
be any representation of
\mathbf{L}
and
{\mathbf{G}}=Sp_{n}(F)
. Then
\psi|_{{\mathbf{G}}}
has weight
0
.

Proof. We can view
\mathbf{H}
as a subgroup of
\mathbf{L}
of shape
\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(\mathbf{H},1)
or
\mathop{\rm diag}\nolimits(1,\mathbf{H})
. Then the reference torus
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{H}}
of
\mathbf{H}
(chosen to be the group of diagonal matrices) is contained in that of
\mathbf{L}
and a Borel subgroup
B
of
\mathbf{H}
containing
\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{H}}
is contained in a Borel subgroup of
\mathbf{L}
that contains the reference torus
\mathbf{T}
of
\mathbf{L}
. Then
\varepsilon_{n}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{H}})=1
in the former case and
\varepsilon_{1}(\mathbf{T}_{\mathbf{H}})=1
in the latter case.

(1) Let
\lambda=\sum a_{i}\lambda_{i}
. Then the restriction
\phi|_{\mathbf{H}}
has composition factors with highest weights

a_{1}\omega_{1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}\omega_{n-1}
and
a_{2}\omega_{1}+\cdots+a_{n}\omega_{n-1}
. These weights are
p
-restricted so must lie in
\{0,\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n-1}\}
. This implies the claim unless possibly
\lambda=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n}
.
Then
\phi_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n}}
is well known to be the unique non-trivial irreducible constituent of the adjoint module (when
\mathbf{L}
acts by conjugation of the
((n+1)\times(n+1))
-matrices with trace 0). For
n>3
one can easily observe that
\phi_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{n-1}}
is a constituent of the adjoint module and hence of
V_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{n}}
, whence the result. If
n=2
then the restriction of

\phi_{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}}
to
\mathbf{H}
has an irreducible constituent of highest weight
2\omega_{1}
, which is
p
-restricted for
p\neq 2
. So this
option is rules out.

(2)
V_{\lambda}
has weight
\mu:=\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i-1}+\varepsilon_{n}
. Let
0\neq v\in V_{\lambda}
be a vector in the
\mu
-weight space. Then
v
is a primitive vector for
\mathbf{H}
, that is,
Bv=\langle v\rangle
.
Therefore,
V^{\prime}:=\langle\mathbf{H}v\rangle
is a
\mathbf{H}
-module (possibly reducible) with highest weight
\varepsilon_{1}+\cdots+\varepsilon_{i-1}

[
18
, Theorem 39]
. Then one can factorize
V^{\prime}
by a maximal submodule to obtain a composition factor with highest weight
\omega_{i-1}
, as claimed.

(3) It suffices to prove this for tensor-indecomposable irreducible representations, and then we can assume that
\psi
has a
p
-restricted highest weight
\mu
, say. Then either
0\prec\mu
or
\lambda_{i}\preceq\mu
for some
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. If
i
is even then
\psi_{\lambda_{i}}|_{\mathbf{G}}
has weight 0 by Lemma
5.5
. If
i
is odd then
\psi_{\lambda_{i}}|_{\mathbf{H}}
has a constituent
\rho
, say, of highest weight
\omega_{i-1}
; as
i-1
is even,
\rho|_{\mathbf{G}}
has weight 0, by Lemma
5.5
.
\Box

Corollary 5.12
.

Let
H=SL_{n+1}(q)
or
SU_{n+1}(q)
, where n is even, p a prime dividing q, and let
\rho
be a p-modular representation of H.
Then
\rho(g)
has eigenvalue
1
for every real element
g\in H
.

Proof. It suffices to prove the theorem for
\rho
irreducible, and
with
p
-restricted highest weight. By Lemma
5.8
, we can assume that
g\in G\cong Sp_{n}(q)
. So the result follows from Lemma
5.11
(3).
\Box

Theorem 5.13
.

Let
\mathbf{H}=SL_{n+1}(\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits}_{2})
, let
H=SL_{n+1}(2)
or
SU_{n+1}(2)
, and let
g\in H
be a semisimple element. Let
\phi
be an irreducible representation of
\mathbf{H}
with highest weight
\lambda
.

(1)
Suppose that
g\in H
is real. Then
\phi(g)
has eigenvalue
1

unless (possibly)
n+1
is even and
\lambda=2^{i}\lambda^{\prime}
, where
\lambda^{\prime}
is some fundamental weight of
\mathbf{H}
.

(2)
Suppose that
H=SL_{n+1}(2)
and
|g|
divides
2^{i}+1
for some i, or
H=SU_{n+1}(2)
and
|g|
divides
2^{i}-1
for some i odd. Then the conclusion of
(1)
holds.

Proof. It suffices to prove (1) as
g
in (2) is real by Lemmas
5.9
and
5.10
. Furthermore, if
n+1
is odd then
the argument in the proof of Corollary
5.12
works and yields the result.
So we assume that
n+1
is even. By Lemma
5.8
, we can assume that
g\in G=Sp_{n+1}(2)
.

Let
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}
be the fundamental weights of
\mathbf{H}
and
\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{(n+1)/2}
those of
\mathbf{G}=Sp_{n+1}({\overline{\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits}_{2}})
. Suppose first that
\lambda
is 2-restricted.
By Lemma
5.7
,
\rho|_{\mathbf{G}}
has either weight 0 (and we are done) or all weights of an
irreducible representation of
\mathbf{G}
with highest weight
\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}
.
In the latter case the result follows by Lemma
3.3
(3).

Suppose that
\lambda
is not 2-restricted. Then
\lambda=\sum 2^{j}\mu_{j}
, where
\mu_{j}
are 2-restricted dominant weights.
Then
\phi=\otimes_{j}\rho_{2^{j}\mu_{j}}
and
\phi|_{H}=\otimes_{j}\rho_{\mu_{j}}|_{H}
. Clearly, we only need to examine the case where
this product has at least two terms. If
\mu_{j}
is not radical, denote by

\mu_{j}^{\prime}
the fundamental weight such that
\mu_{j}^{\prime}\preceq\mu_{j}
, otherwise set
\mu_{j}^{\prime}=\mu_{j}
.
Then
\Omega(\otimes_{j}\rho_{\mu^{\prime}_{j}})\subseteq\Omega(\otimes_{j}\rho_{\mu_{j}})
, so it suffices to prove the theorem for the case with
\mu_{j}=\mu^{\prime}_{j}
. Set
\nu_{j}=\mu^{\prime}_{j}|_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}
, where
\mathbf{T}^{\prime}
is a maximal torus of
\mathbf{G}
as in Lemma
5.4
, which tells us that
\nu_{j}\in\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}
whenever
\mu_{j}^{\prime}
is not radical. For such

j
the weights of the Weyl module
\tilde{V}_{\nu_{j}}
are weights of
\rho_{\mu_{j}}
(Lemma
5.3
). In addition, either

\nu_{j}
is radical or
j
is odd and
\tilde{V}_{\nu_{j}}
has weights
\pm\omega_{1}
(Lemmas
2.2
and
2.5
). So the tensor product of two such modules
has weight 0. It follows that that we can write
\phi|_{\mathbf{G}}=\phi_{1}\otimes\phi_{2}
, where
\phi_{1}|_{\mathbf{G}}
has weight 0
and
\phi_{2}=\rho_{\mu_{j}}
for some
j
, where
\rho_{\mu_{j}}
contains all weights of
\tilde{V}_{\nu_{j}}
for some
\nu_{j}\in\{\omega_{1},\ldots,\omega_{n}\}
with
j
odd. If
\phi_{1}
is trivial then the result follows by Corollary
5.6
(3). Otherwise,
\phi_{1}|_{\mathbf{G}}
has weight
\omega_{2}
. Indeed, the weights of
\sigma\in\Omega(\phi_{1})
have the same residue modulo the radical weights (as so are the weights of every multiple
\rho_{\mu_{j}}
), and
\sigma|_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}=0
for some
\sigma
. It follows that
\sigma|_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}
is radical for every
\sigma\in\Omega(\phi_{1})
. In particular,
this is the case for
\sigma|_{\mathbf{T}^{\prime}}
to be the highest weight of a non-trivial composition factor of
\phi_{1}|_{\mathbf{G}}
. The latter has weight
\omega_{2}
by Lemma
2.5
, and hence the weight
\varepsilon_{2}+\varepsilon_{3}
if
n>2
. So all weights of
V_{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}}
are weights of
\phi|_{\mathbf{G}}
. Then the result follows by Lemma
3.3
.

\Box

Proof of Theorem

1.7
. The result is a special case of Theorem
5.13
.

The following Lemmas
5.14
and
5.15
show that the result of Theorem
5.13
is in a sense best possible.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
h\in H=SL_{2n}(2)
. Suppose that
|h|=p
, where
p=2^{n}+1
is a prime. Let
\phi
be an
i
-th exterior power of the natural
\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits_{2}H
-module, where i is odd. Then
\phi(h)
does not have eigenvalue
1
.

Proof. Note that
h
is conjugate to a Singer cycle in
G=Sp_{n}(2)
. By Lemma
5.5
, the composition factors of
\phi|_{\mathbf{G}}
are of highest weights
\omega_{j}^{\prime}
for
j
odd. So Lemma
3.2
yields the result.
\Box

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
h\in H=SU_{n}(2)
. Suppose that
|h|=p
, where
p=2^{n/2}-1
is a prime. Let
\phi
be an
i
-th exterior power of the natural
\mathop{\mathbb{F}}\nolimits_{4}H
-module, where
i<n-1
is odd. Then
\phi(h)
does not have eigenvalue
1
.

Proof. This follows from Lemma
3.1
. Indeed,
n/2
is odd, so
h
is conjugate to an element of
G=Sp_{n}(2)
(Lemmas
5.10
and
5.8
).
Moreover,
h\in G
is a Singer cycle, so
h
is a generator of a (cyclic) maximal torus
T_{w}
of
G
labeled by an element
w
of the Weyl group which transitively permutes
\varepsilon_{1},\ldots,\varepsilon_{n}
. Let
W_{i}
be as in Lemma
3.1
. Then
w
is conjugate to an element of
W_{i}
if and only if
i=n
. As in Lemma
5.14
one observes that the composition factors of
\phi|_{G}
are of highest weights
\omega_{j}^{\prime}
for
j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
odd. Then the reasoning in the proof of Lemma
3.2
shows
that
h
does not have eigenvalue 1 on
V_{\omega^{\prime}_{i}}
whenever
i<n-1
odd.
\Box

Example of a foliation for Ueda type one
Paulo Sad

Abstract
We give an example of a projective 2-dimensional foliation which is regular along a curve of genus 3 and has Ueda type one with normal bundle of order two. 1 1 1 MSC Number: 32S65



Introduction

In this Introduction we present a short list of results of the so called Ueda Theory, developed in
[
5
]
with focus in local properties. Later on this matter was studied in
[
4
]
from a global point of view, and recently appered again in the work of
[
3
]
.

Let
C
be a smooth, compact, connected holomorphic curve contained in some holomorphic surface
M
satisfying
C.C=0
(
C.C
stands for the self intersection number of
C
inside
M
). We consider a type
\nu
coordinate system
\{\psi_{i}:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow M\}
for a neighborhood of
C
:

z_{k}=\psi_{k}^{-1}\circ\psi_{i}(z_{i},y_{i})

y_{k}={\lambda}_{ik}(z_{i})y_{i}+f_{ik}(z_{i})y_{i}^{\nu+1}+\dots

for some
\nu\geq 1
.

The normal bundle
N_{C}
to
C
is given by

z_{k}=\psi_{k}^{-1}\circ\psi_{i}(z_{i},0)

y_{k}={\lambda}_{ik}(z_{i})y_{i}

Since
C.C=0
, we may assume that
\lambda_{ik}(z_{i})=\lambda_{ik}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

The collection
\{f_{ik}\}
is a cocycle of the sheaf of holomorphic functions of
N_{C}^{-1}
; when it is a coboundary, we may replace the type
\nu
coordinate system by a
\nu+1
coordinate system. The curve
C
has finite type
n\in\mathbb{N}
when there exists a system of coordinates of type
n
whose associated cocycle is not a coboundary; we say that
n
is the
Ueda type
of
C
. The Ueda type of
C
is
infinite
when the cocycle associated to any coordinate system is a coboundary.

The finite type is characterized by the following result:

Theorem 1
If
C
has finite type then it has a fundamental system of pseudo-concave neighborhoods.

In particular, there are no compact curves in a neighborhood of
C
disjoint of
C
.

When the Ueda type is infinite, the extra condition of
N_{C}
being a torsion bundle leads to the following

Theorem 2
If
C
has infinite type and its normal bundle
N_{C}
is a torsion bundle of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
then there exists a holomorphic foliation by compact
leaves in a neighborhood of
C
such that
C
is a leaf (in fact the foiation can be seen as a fibration which has
C
as a multiple fiber of multiplicity
k
).

Both results appeared in
[
5
]
. We state now the results of
[
4
]
and
[
3
]
respectively in the case
M
is a projective surface.

Theorem 3
Assume that
N_{C}
is a torsion bundle of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
be the Ueda type of
C
. If
n>k
then in fact
n=\infty
(and Theorem 2 applies).

Theorem 4
Assume
N_{C}
is a torsion bundle of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be the Ueda type of
C
. If
n=k
then there exists a foliation in
M
which has
C
as a leaf.

We remind that
C
being a leaf means in particular that it contains no singularity of the foliation.

Theorem 4 leads to the following question: suppose
N_{C}
is a torsion bundle of order
k\in\mathbb{N}
and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be the Ueda type of
C
; what can be said about the existence of a foliation having
C
as a leaf if
n<k
? We present here an example in the positive direction when
n=1
and
k=2
(correcting a statement of
[
6
]
).

We have in mind the following picture: we start with the projective plane
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with some foliation which has a smooth algebraic invaariant curve
S
of degree
d
; this curve
S
contains singularities
p_{1}\dots,p_{l}
with tangent multiplicities (of the foliation along
S
)
m_{1},\dots,m_{l}
. According to
[
2
]
, the degree
0
divisor
\sum_{j=1}^{l}m_{j}p_{j}-LD_{\infty}
is a principal divisor (
d.L=\sum_{j=1}^{l}m_{j}
and
D_{\infty}
is the divisor obtained intersecting
S
with some line, which we fix at infinity). Since a generic choice of points in
S
produces no ”resonances” there is no foliation having such points as singularities in the invariant curve
S
. In particular, when we blow-up
\mathbb{P}^{2}
at
d^{2}
points in
S
chosen in a generic way the strict transform
\widehat{S}
of
S
can not be a leaf of a foliation in the transformed surface
\widehat{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
.

In this paper we will work with a particular Neeman’s example. This family of examples (see
[
4
]
) is constructed in the following way: we start with a fixed quartic
S
and blow it up at 16 points which lie at two different bitangent lines (4 points
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
) and some cubic (12 points
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
). The Ueda type of
\widehat{S}
is 1, and the normal bundle
N_{\widehat{S}}
is associated to the divisor
\sum_{j=1}^{16}P_{j}-4D_{\infty}
. We see that
2(\sum_{j=1}^{16}P_{j}-4D_{\infty}
) is a principal divisor, so that
N_{\widehat{S}}
is a torsion bundle of order 2. Such a resonance is realized by a foliation in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree 10; it has 16 singularities of tangent multiplicities equal to 2 along
S
. The group of holonomy of
S\setminus\{P_{1},\dots,P_{16}\}
(which is the same of
\widehat{S}
) is infinite, although the local holonomy maps at the singularities are all igual to the identity map.

Example

Let
F(X,Y)
be a degree four polynomial which defines a smooth projective quartic
S
transverse to the line at infinity; we assume also that
\{F=0\}\cap\{F_{X}=0\}
has 12 different points and
l_{1}(X,Y)=Y+X=0
and
l_{2}(X,Y)=Y+X+b=0
are bitangent lines. We put

G=l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}

h=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}(F_{X}+F_{Y})}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}

k=-F_{X}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}}{2}

h^{\prime}=-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}-l_{1}l_{2}F_{XX}

k^{\prime}=F_{X}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XX}}{2}

A=hF_{X}+kF

B=h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F

The foliation
\cal F
we will study is given by

GdF+F(BdX-AdY)=0

Let
\widehat{\cal F}
be the transformed foliation from
\cal F
and
\widehat{S}
be the transformed curve from
S
after blowing-up at the points of
\{G=0\}\cap S
.

Theorem.

\widehat{S}
is a leaf of
\widehat{\cal F}
.

First of all we need to compute the degree of the foliation
\cal F
.

Lemma.

\cal F
has degree 10.

Proof: it ammounts to showing that the homogeneous polynomial of degree 12 in the development of

X(GF_{X}+FB)+Y(GF_{Y}-FA)=

XGF_{X}+YGF_{Y}+F[X(h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F)-Y(hF_{X}+kF)]

is in fact equal to 0. In what follows, all the equalities refer to homogeneous polynomials of top degree. For example,
XGF_{X}+YGF_{Y}=4GF
.

Let us compute
X(h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F)-Y(hF_{X}+kF)
. We have

X(h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F)-Y(hF_{X}+kF)=

=-X(l_{1}l_{2}F_{X})_{X}F_{X}-Y\left[\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}\right]F_{X}

+X\left[\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{XX}F_{X}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XX}}{2}\right]F-Y\left[-F_{X}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}}{2}\right]F

=-X(l_{1}l_{2}F_{X})_{X}F_{X}-Y\left[\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}\right]F_{X}

+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\left[\dfrac{X(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XX}}{2}+\dfrac{Y(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}}{2}\right]F

=-X(l_{1}l_{2}F_{X})_{X}F_{X}-Y\left[\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}\right]F_{X}

+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\dfrac{3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F}{2}

=-\left[\dfrac{X(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{Y(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{Y}}{2}\right]-\dfrac{X(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}^{2}}{2}

-\left[Xl_{1}l_{2}F_{XX}F_{X}+Yl_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}F_{X}\right]-\dfrac{Y(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}^{2}}{2}

+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\dfrac{3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F}{2}

=-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}FF_{X}-3l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}-\dfrac{\left[X(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}^{2}+Y(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}^{2}\right]}{2}

+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\dfrac{3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F}{2}

-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}FF_{X}-3l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}--l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\dfrac{3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}FF_{X}}{2}

=-l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}-3l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}FF_{X}+(X+Y)FF_{X}+\dfrac{3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}FF_{X}}{2}

=-4l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}
.

It follows finally that

XGF_{X}+YGF_{Y}+F[X(h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F)-Y(hF_{X}+kF)]

=4GF-4l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}^{2}F=0
∎

In particular, the line at infinite is not invariant for
\cal F
. Let us observe that the 16 points of
\{G=0\}\cap S
are singularities of
\cal F
having tangent multiplicity equal to 2. These are
all
the singularities of
\cal F
along
S
. In fact, from
[
1
]
we have that
(d({\cal F})+2).d(S)=tang(\cal F,S)+S.S
(
d
stands for
degree
), so that
tang(\cal F,S)=
32. But this is already achieved at the singularities of
\{G=0\}\cap S
.

In order to prove that
\widehat{S}
is a leaf of
\widehat{\cal F}
we have to check at each of the points
P_{1}\dots,P_{16}
the following conditions (see
[
6
]
):

1.
A=\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial Y}

2.
AF_{X}+BF_{Y}=0

3.
A_{Y}=\dfrac{G_{YY}}{2}+\dfrac{F_{YY}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}

4.
B_{Y}-A_{X}=-\dfrac{F_{XY}G_{Y}}{F_{Y}}-G_{XY}

5.
B_{X}=-\dfrac{G_{XX}}{2}-\dfrac{F_{XX}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}

Let us notice that at the points
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
we have
(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}=(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}
and
F_{X}=F_{Y}
.

Condition 1.
We have
A=0
and
\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial Y}=(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}^{2}+2l_{1}l_{2}F_{X}F_{XX}=0
at
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
.
At
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
, we see that
A=hF_{X}=(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}^{2}=\dfrac{\partial G}{\partial Y}

Condition 2.

A=B=0
at
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
implies
AF_{X}+BF_{Y}=0
at these points. As for
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
:

AF_{X}+BF_{Y}=hF_{X}^{2}-(l_{1}l_{2}F_{X})_{X}F_{X}F_{Y}=(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}F_{X}^{2}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}^{2}F_{Y}=0

Condition 3.
We have at
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
:
\dfrac{G_{YY}}{2}=l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}^{2}
,
\dfrac{F_{YY}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}=0
and
A_{Y}=l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}^{2}
. At
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
:

A_{Y}=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{XY}F_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{YY}F_{X}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{YY}F_{X}^{2}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{X}}{2}+

(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{XY}F_{X}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{Y}F_{XY}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XY}}{2}-F_{X}F_{Y}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{Y}}{2}=

F_{X}^{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{YY}F_{X}}{2}+2(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XY}
and

\dfrac{G_{YY}}{2}+\dfrac{F_{YY}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{YY}F_{X}^{2}}{2}+2(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XY}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{YY}F_{X}^{2}}{2F_{Y}}=

F_{X}^{2}+2(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XY}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{YY}}{2}

Condition 4.
At
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
we have
B_{Y}-A_{X}=-2l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}F_{XX}
and
-\dfrac{F_{XY}G_{Y}}{F_{Y}}-G_{XY}=-G_{XY}=-2l_{1}l_{2}F_{XY}F_{XX}
. As for
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}:

B_{Y}-A_{X}=-(l_{1}l_{2})_{XY}F_{X}^{2}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{X}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XX}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{X}+

\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{XX}F_{Y}F_{X}}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}F_{XX}}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{XX}F_{Y}F_{X}}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{X}}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{XY}F_{X}^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{XX}F_{X}}{2}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XY}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}F_{XX}}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XX}}{2}+F_{X}^{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{XY}F_{X}}{2}=-2F_{X}^{2}-3(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XY}-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XX}
; this is exactly
\dfrac{F_{XY}G_{Y}}{F_{Y}}-G_{XY}

Condition 5.
At
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
we have
B_{X}=h^{\prime}F_{XX}=-l_{1}l_{2}F_{XX}^{2}=-\dfrac{G_{XX}}{2}=-\dfrac{G_{XX}}{2}-\dfrac{F_{XX}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}

As for
P_{1},\dots,P_{4}
:
B_{X}=-2F_{X}^{2}-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}-(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}+F_{X}^{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}}{2}=-F_{X}^{2}-\dfrac{5(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}}{2}
.

On the other hand,
-\dfrac{G_{XX}}{2}-\dfrac{F_{XX}G_{Y}}{2F_{Y}}=-F_{X}^{2}-2(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}F_{X}F_{XX}}{2}=-F_{X}^{2}-\dfrac{5(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}F_{XX}}{2}
.

Other examples

The example above fits into to a family where the points
P_{5},\dots,P_{16}
are the zeros along
\{F=0\}
of a cubic
C
which satisfies
C(P_{j})=F_{X}(P_{j}))
for
j=1,2,3,4
. We put

G=l_{1}l_{2}C^{2}

h=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}C}{2}+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{Y}}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}C_{Y}=\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}(C+F_{Y})}{2}+l_{1}l_{2}C_{Y}

k=-C-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}C_{Y}}{2}

h^{\prime}=-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{Y}C}{2}-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}F_{X}}{2}-l_{1}l_{2}C_{X}=-\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}(C+F_{X})}{2}-l_{1}l_{2}C_{X}

k^{\prime}=C+\dfrac{(l_{1}l_{2})_{X}C_{X}}{2}

A=hF_{X}+kF

B=h^{\prime}F_{X}+k^{\prime}F

We have the same Theorem as before; the computations for the proof are the same. It is clear that if
C(P_{j})=\lambda F(P_{j})
for
j=1,2,3,4
and some
\lambda\neq 0
we may replace
C
by
\dfrac{C}{\lambda}
.

We do not know if there are cubics (whose zeroes along
\{F=0\}
are different from
P_{1}\dots P_{4}
) for which the Theorem is untrue.

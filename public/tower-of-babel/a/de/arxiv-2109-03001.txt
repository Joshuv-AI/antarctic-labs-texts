Unifying Farkas lemma and S-lemma: new theory and applications in nonquadratic nonconvex optimization Journal: JOTA
Meijia Yang 1, Yong Xia 2, Shu Wang 3

Abstract
We unify nonlinear Farkas lemma and S-lemma to a generalized alternative theorem for nonlinear nonconvex system. It provides fruitful applications in globally solving nonconvex non-quadratic optimization problems via revealing the hidden convexity.

1
Introduction

The celebrated Farkas lemma dates back to
F1902
, which characterizes when a linear system has no real solution. Farkas lemma plays a key role in developing strong duality theory of linear programming.
Following the spirit of applying the separation theorem of two convex sets to prove Farkas lemma,
one can extend Farkas lemma to nonlinear but convex system, see Theorem 21.1 in
R70
, Section 6.10 in
SW1970
, the survey papers
DJ2014
;
J2000
and references therein.

The first alternative result for nonconvex system may be due to Finsler
F1937
. There are two homogeneous quadratic forms in a system, with one quadratic form equal to zero and the other one less than or equal to zero. This system has nonzero solution if and only if there is a real constant such that the linear combination of these two matrices is always definite. A strict and more general version of Finsler’s theorem, the so-called S-lemma, was first used by Lur’e and Postnikov
LP44
without any theoretical proof at that time. The first proof of S-lemma came out thirty years later due to Yakubovich
Y1971
. Nowadays more proofs of S-lemma are summarized in
P2007
.

S-lemma has many important applications in control theory, robust optimization and convex geometry as well. In nonconvex optimization, it reveals the hidden convexity of some nonconvex problems including the well-known trust-region subproblem
CGT
and its generalizations, see for example,
J2018
;
XWS2016
.

There are many generalizations of S-lemma. The most famous one is due to Polyak
P1998
, who established the alternative theorem for the system with three quadratic forms under some assumptions. Jeyakumar et al.
J2009
extended the domain of the system from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to any linear manifold. Hu and Huang
HH
generalized S-lemma to even order tenors. Xia et al.
XWS2016
established necessary and sufficient conditions for S-lemma with equality. As an extension, S-lemma with interval bounds is proposed in
WX2015
. Other generalizations can be found in recent surveys
P2007
;
DP2006
.

Notice that S-lemma and most of its variations are restricted to nonconvex but quadratic system. The goal of this paper is to establish a general alternative theorem for nonconvex and non-quadratic system. Following the sprit of the extension from Farkas lemma to extended Farkas lemma, we intend to combine S-lemma with the nonlinear Farkas lemma. It leads to a unified alternative lemma for nonconvex non-quadratic system, denoted by U-lemma. U-lemma not only generalizes the nonlinear Farkas lemma and S-lemma as special cases, but also plays a more powerful role in helping polynomially solve some nonconvex non-quadratic optimization problems by revealing hidden convexity.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we review some main results which can help better understand our thinking. In Section 3, we establish the unified lemma (U-lemma) by combining nonlinear Farkas lemma with S-lemma. In Section 4, we apply the U-lemma to solve two nonconvex nonquadratic optimization problems, the
p
-regularized subproblem and the backward error criterion problem, by revealing its hidden convexity.

4
Applications

Our newly established U-lemma fills a gap in alternative theory for non-quadratic nonconvex system. In this section, we show that it has critical applications in globally solving some non-quadratic nonconvex optimization problems by revealing the hidden convexity.

4.1
Generalized

-regularized subproblem with

The generalized

\displaystyle(p{\rm\text{-}RS)}~~\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left\{h(x)=x^{T}Ax+b^{T}x+\rho\|x\|^{p}\right\},

The special case (

is any solution of
the following linear and quadratic equations (the hard case):

2(A+\lambda^{*}I)x+b=0,~x^{T}x=z^{*}.

4.2
Backward error criterion

In numerical analysis, the method with a small backward error is preferred. There are some backward error criteria proposed in
S2003
. The most difficult one is

\displaystyle({\rm BE})~~\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\frac{\|Ax-b\|}{\|A\|\|x\|+\|b\|},

(21)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
,
\|\cdot\|
is standard
l_{2}
-norm and spectral norm for vectors and matrices, respectively. We assume
\|A\|>0
. If
Ax=b
is solvable, then this solution also solves (BE) and
v
(BE)
=0
. Moreover, if
\|b\|=0
, then
v
(BE)
=\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(A^{T}A)}/\|A\|
. In this subsection, we consider the case where
v
(BE)
>0
and
\|b\|>0
, which means that (BE) is a nontrivial nonconvex fractional programming problem.

With the help of U-lemma, we can show the hidden convexity of (BE) (
21
):

\displaystyle\min_{x}\frac{\|Ax-b\|^{2}}{(\|A\|\|x\|+\|b\|)^{2}}

(22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\{x:\|Ax-b\|^{2}-t(\|A\|\|x\|+\|b\|)^{2}<0\}=\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\{(x,z):\|Ax-b\|^{2}-t\|A\|^{2}z-2t\|A\|\|b\|\sqrt{z}-t\|b\|^{2}<0,\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\|x\|^{2}\geq z,~z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}=\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\exists\lambda\geq 0:\|Ax-b\|^{2}-t\|A\|^{2}z-2t\|A\|\|b\|\sqrt{z}-t\|b\|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\lambda(-\|x\|^{2}+z)\geq 0,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~\forall z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\exists\lambda\geq 0:\|Ax-b\|^{2}-t\|b\|^{2}-\lambda\|x\|^{2}\right.

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~+\min_{z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left\{\lambda z-t\|A\|^{2}z-2t\|A\|\|b\|\sqrt{z}\right\}\geq 0,\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\|Ax-b\|^{2}-\lambda\|x\|^{2}-t\|b\|^{2}-\frac{t^{2}\|A\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}}{\lambda-t\|A\|^{2}}\geq 0,\lambda>t\|A\|^{2},~\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{t>0}\left\{t:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{T}A-\lambda I&-A^{T}b\\
-b^{T}A&\|b\|^{2}-t\|b\|^{2}-\frac{t^{2}\|A\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}}{\lambda-t\|A\|^{2}}\end{array}\right)\succeq 0,~\lambda>t\|A\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{w<\|b\|^{2},\lambda>0}\left\{\frac{(w-\|b\|^{2})\lambda}{w\|A\|^{2}-\|A\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}-\lambda\|b\|^{2}}:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{T}A-\lambda I&-A^{T}b\\
-b^{T}A&w\end{array}\right)\succeq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1\left/\right.\inf_{w<\|b\|^{2},\lambda>0}\left\{\frac{\|A\|^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{\|b\|^{2}}{\|b\|^{2}-w}:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}A^{T}A-\lambda I&-A^{T}b\\
-b^{T}A&w\end{array}\right)\succeq 0\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1\left/\right.\inf\limits_{0<\lambda<\lambda_{\min}(A^{T}A)}\frac{\|A\|^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{\|b\|^{2}}{\|b\|^{2}-b^{T}A(A^{T}A-\lambda I)^{-1}A^{T}b},&{\rm if}~A^{T}b\not\in{\rm R}(A),\\
1\left/\right.\inf\limits_{0<\lambda\leq\lambda_{\min}(A^{T}A)}\frac{\|A\|^{2}}{\lambda}+\frac{\|b\|^{2}}{\|b\|^{2}-b^{T}A(A^{T}A-\lambda I)^{+}A^{T}b},&{\rm if}~A^{T}b\in{\rm R}(A),\end{array}\right.

where (
22
) follows from U-lemma (Theorem
3.2
) with
f(x)=\|Ax-b\|^{2}-t\|b\|^{2}
,
g(x)=-\|x\|^{2}
,
q_{0}(z)=-t\|A\|^{2}z-2t\|A\|\|b\|\sqrt{z}
,
q_{1}(z)=z
; (
4.2
) is due to (
4.1
); (
4.2
) holds by introducing

w=\|b\|^{2}-t\|b\|^{2}-\frac{t^{2}\|A\|^{2}\|b\|^{2}}{\lambda-t\|A\|^{2}},

and then
\lambda>t\|A\|^{2}
if and only if
w<\|b\|^{2}
and
\lambda>0
; (
4.2
) is due to Schur complement theorem and R
(A)={\rm Range}(A^{T}A-\lambda_{\min}(A^{T}A)I)
.

Problem (
4.2
) is a univariate convex optimization problem, which can be solved by Newton’s method. Let
\lambda^{*}
and
t^{*}
be the optimal solution and objective value of (
4.2
), respectively. Then the optimal
z
-solution of the minimization subproblem in (
23
) is given by

z^{*}=\left(t^{*}\|A\|\|b\|/(\lambda^{*}-t^{*}\|A\|^{2})\right)^{2}.

The optimal value of (BE) is:

\min_{x}\frac{\|Ax-b\|^{2}}{(\|A\|\|x\|+\|b\|)^{2}}=t^{*}.

So (BE) (
21
) is equivalent to the following problem:

\displaystyle\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},~z\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}

\displaystyle\|Ax-b\|^{2}-\|A\|^{2}t^{*}z-2t^{*}\|A\|\|b\|\sqrt{z}-t^{*}\|b\|^{2}

\displaystyle{\rm s.t.}

\displaystyle\|x\|^{2}\geq z.

Since the objective function is decreasing with respect to
z
, the inequality holds as an equality at the optimal solution. Therefore,
x
is an optimal solution to (BE) (
21
) if and only if

\|x\|^{2}=z^{*},~\|Ax-b\|^{2}=t^{*}(\|A\|\sqrt{z^{*}}+\|b\|)^{2}.

To solve (BE), we first solve two trust-region subproblems

x_{m}={\rm arg}\min_{\|x\|^{2}=z^{*}}\|Ax-b\|^{2},~x_{M}={\rm arg}\max_{\|x\|^{2}=z^{*}}\|Ax-b\|^{2}.

If either
\|Ax_{m}-b\|^{2}
or
\|Ax_{M}-b\|^{2}
equals to
t^{*}(\|A\|\sqrt{z^{*}}+\|b\|)^{2}
, we are done. Otherwise, define

x(\alpha)=\sqrt{z^{*}}\frac{x_{m}+\alpha(x_{M}-x_{m})}{\|x_{m}+\alpha(x_{M}-x_{m})\|}.

Solve the univariate equation

\|Ax(\alpha)-b\|^{2}=t^{*}(\|A\|\sqrt{z^{*}}+\|b\|)^{2},~\alpha\in(0,1)

and obtain a solution
\alpha^{*}
. Then,
x(\alpha^{*})
is an optimal solution of (BE).

5
Conclusion

We unify the nonlinear Farkas lemma and the classical S-lemma to a general alternative theorem, denoted by U-lemma. Some extensions are discussed.
Based on this powerful tool, we reveal hidden convexity of some nonconvex non-quadratic optimization problems,
including the
p
-regularized subproblem (
p>2
), the backward error criterion problem. It helps to globally solve these nonconvex optimization problems in polynomial time. We expect for more extensions and fruitful applications, or even beyond the area of optimization.

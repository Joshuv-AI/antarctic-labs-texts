Finding minimum bounded and homologous chains in simplicial complexes with bounded-treewidth 1-skeleton
Mitchell Black, Amir Nayyeri

Abstract
We consider two problems on simplicial complexes: the Optimal Bounded Chain Problem and the Optimal Homologous Chain Problem. The Optimal Bounded Chain Problem asks to find the minimum weight d d -chain in a simplicial complex K K bounded by a given ( d − 1 ) (d{-}1) -chain, if such a d d -chain exists. The Optimal Homologous Chain problem asks to find the minimum weight ( d − 1 ) (d{-}1) -chain in K K homologous to a given ( d − 1 ) (d{-}1) -chain. Both of these problems are NP-hard and hard to approximate within any constant factor assuming the Unique Games Conjecture. We prove that these problems are fixed-parameter tractable with respect to the treewidth of the 1-skeleton of K K .



1
Introduction

We consider two problems in this paper: the Optimal Bounded Chain Problem and the Optimal Homologous Chain Problem. Both of these problems are NP-hard
[
16
,
22
]
, so it is natural to ask whether more efficient algorithms exist for any special family of simplicial complexes. In this paper, we study algorithms for these problems for simplicial complexes with bounded-width tree decompositions of their 1-skeletons.

Tree decompositions have been used successfully to design algorithms on graphs; see
[
18
]
. Often, graphs with tree decompositions of bounded-width admit polynomial-time solutions to otherwise hard problems. Recently, tree decompositions have also begun to be used for algorithms on simplicial complexes. Existing algorithms use tree decompositions of a variety of graphs associated with a simplicial complex. The most commonly used graph is the dual graph of combinatorial
d
-manifolds
[
3
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
. Other graphs that have been used are the incidence graph between the
d
and
(d+1)
-simplices (a.k.a. level
d
of the Hasse diagram)
[
9
,
5
,
4
]
, the adjacency graph of the
d
-simplices
[
5
]
, and the 1-skeleton
[
3
]
.

Our algorithms use a tree decomposition of the 1-skeleton. We observe that any simplex of a simplicial complex will necessarily be contained in some bag of a tree decomposition of its 1-skeleton. This is analogous to the requirement that each vertex and edge of a graph be contained in some bag of a tree decomposition. In this way, a tree decomposition of the 1-skeleton of a simplicial complex is analogous to a tree decomposition of a graph.

The problems we consider ask to find a chain in a simplicial complex with certain properties. There can be exponentially many such chains, so we cannot simply check whether each chain or subcomplex has the desired property. Our approach is to incrementally build these chains, checking along the way that they have the desired property. Tree decompositions of the 1-skeleton of a simplicial complex define a recursive structure on a simplicial complex that we use to incrementally build our solutions.

Background

3.1
Simplicial complexes

A
simplicial complex
is a set
K
such that (1) each element
\sigma\in K
is a finite set and (2) for each
\sigma\in K
, if
\tau\subset\sigma
, then
\tau\in K
. An element
\sigma\in K
is a
simplex
. A simplex
\tau
is a
face
of a simplex
\sigma
if
\tau\subset\sigma
. Likewise,
\sigma
is a
coface
of
\tau
. The simplices
\sigma
and
\tau
are
incident
.

A simplex
\sigma
with
|\sigma|=d+1
is a
d-simplex
. The set of all
d
-simplices in
K
is denoted
K_{d}
. The
d-skeleton
of
K
is
K^{d}=\cup_{i=0}^{d}K_{d}
. In particular, the 1-skeleton of
K
is a graph. The
dimension
of a simplicial complex is the largest integer
d
such that
K
contains a
d
-simplex.

The union
V=\cup_{\sigma\in K}\sigma
is the set of
vertices
of a simplicial complex. Each simplex in
K
is a subset of
V
. A subset of vertices
U\subset V
defines a subcomplex of
K
. The
subcomplex induced by U
is
K[U]=\{\sigma\in K\,|\,\sigma\subset U\}
.

3.2
Homology

Let
K
be a simplicial complex. The
d
th
chain group

C_{d}(K)
of

is the free abelian group with coefficients over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
generated by
K_{d}
. An element
\gamma\in C_{d}(K)
is a
d-chain
. A
d
-chain
\gamma
with coefficients over
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
naturally corresponds to a set of
d
-simplices in
K
, where the set contains a
d
-simplex

if and only if the coefficient on
\sigma
is 1. We overload notation and use
\gamma
to also refer to both the chain and the set defined by the chain. The
weight
of a chain
\gamma
is the number of simplices the set
\gamma
contains and is denote
\|\gamma\|
; alternatively,
\|\gamma\|
is the
Hamming norm
of
\gamma
. Addition of two
d
-chains takes the symmetric difference of the sets corresponding to these chains.

Let
\sigma
be a
d
-simplex. The

of

is the
(d-1)
-chain
\partial\sigma=\sum_{v\in\sigma}\sigma\setminus\{v\}.

The boundary map linearly extends the notion of boundary from

-simplices to

-chains. The

is the homomorphism
\partial_{d}:C_{d}(K)\to C_{d{-}1}(K)
such that
\partial_{d}\gamma=\sum_{\sigma\in\gamma}\partial\sigma
. When obvious, we drop the

and simply write
\partial_{d}
as

. A (
d{-}1
)-chain
\beta

bounds

d
-chain

if
\partial\gamma=\beta
, and the chain
\gamma

spans

\beta
.

The composition of boundary maps
\partial_{d{-}1}\circ\partial_{d}=0
, the zero map. As
C_{d}(K)
is abelian, then
\text{im}\,\partial_{d}\subset\ker\partial_{d{-}1}
is a normal subgroup. The
(d-1)st homology group
is the quotient group
H_{d{-}1}(K)=\ker\partial_{d{-}1}/\text{im}\,\partial_{d}
. An element
\beta\in\ker\partial_{d{-}1}
is a
(d-1)-cycle
. A
(d{-}1)
-cycle
\beta
is

if
\beta=\partial\gamma
for some
d
-chain
\gamma
. Two
(d{-}1)
-chains

and
h
are

if

is null-homologous.

3.3
Tree decompositions

Let

be a graph. A

is a tuple

is a tree with nodes

is the

]

A

such that (1)

, (2)

such that for some

introduces

. A

is a node

with exactly one child

such that for some

and

All subsets of

The Generic Algorithm

In this section, we present a generic version of the algorithm for our two problems. Later sections discuss the specific algorithms for each problem.

A tree decomposition
(T,X)
of a simplicial complex
K
defines a recursively nested series of subcomplexes of the complex. We can use this series of subcomplexes to recursively build solutions to our problems.

Specifically, subtrees of the tree
T
define subcomplexes of
K
. For a node
t
in the tree, let
V_{t}
be the union of the bags of each of
t
’s descendants, including
t
itself. If
K
is a
d
-dimensional simplicial complex, we define the
subcomplex rooted at t
to be
K_{t}:=K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d}
. See Figure
2
. If
t^{\prime}
is a descendant of
t
, then
K_{t^{\prime}}\subset K_{t}
. We can therefore recurse onto subcomplexes of
K
by recursing onto subtrees
T
.

Figure 2
:
Right: A subtree rooted at
t
. Left: the corresponding subcomplex
K_{t}
.

Each of our algorithms compute a set of
candidate solutions
at each node
t
. The exact definition of candidate solution varies between problems, but intuitively, a candidate solution at a node
t
is a
d
-chain in
K_{t}
that could be a subset of an optimal solution. In each of our algorithms, we are able to define our candidate solutions recursively: if
\gamma
is a candidate solution at
t
, then for each child
t^{\prime}
of
t
,
\gamma\cap K_{t^{\prime}}
is a candidate solution at
t^{\prime}
. This is the key fact our algorithm uses to find candidate solutions at
t
. Our algorithm attempts to build candidate solutions at
t
by adding
d
-simplices in
K_{t}\setminus K_{t^{\prime}}
to candidate solutions at
t^{\prime}
.

We compute the set of candidate solutions at the nodes in a bottom-up fashion, starting at the leaves and moving towards the root. Our general approach for computing the set of candidate solutions at
t
is as follows. Take one candidate solution from each of
t
’s children. Take the union of these candidate solutions. Add a subset of
d
-simplices in
K_{t}
that are not in
K_{t^{\prime}}
for any of
t
’s children
t^{\prime}
to this union. Check if this union is a candidate solution at
t
. Repeat for all sets of candidate solutions at the children and all subset of
d
-simplices.

The above algorithm for computing the set of candidate solutions at a node is generally correct, but because we use a
nice
tree decomposition, we won’t perform every step of this algorithm at any one node. Instead, certain steps of the algorithm are performed only at specialized nodes. For example, we only take the union of candidate solutions from multiple children at join nodes, as join nodes are the only type of node with multiple children. Also, we only add a subset of
d
-simplices in
K_{t}\setminus K_{t^{\prime}}
to the candidate solutions at forget nodes.

It might be counter-intuitive that forgetting a vertex adds
d
-simplices to
K_{t}
, so let’s see why this is the case. Let
t
be a forget node and
t^{\prime}
its unique child. The set of vertices
V_{t}=V_{t^{\prime}}
, so
K[V_{t}]=K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
. However, as the bags
X_{t^{\prime}}\subsetneq X_{t}
, there may be
d
-simplices in
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
that are not in
K[X_{t}]
. As we exclude the
d
-simplices in
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
from
K_{t^{\prime}}
, the
d
-simplices in
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t}]
will be in
K_{t}
but not
K_{t^{\prime}}
. See Figure
3
. Alternatively, for introduce and join nodes, no new
d
-simplices are added to
K_{t}
that do not appear in some
K_{t^{\prime}}
for a child
t^{\prime}
of
t
. If
t
is an introduce node and
t^{\prime}
is its unique child, we can prove
(K_{t})_{d}=(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
. While introducing a vertex
v
may add
d
-simplices to
K[V_{t}]
, we can prove these simplices are always contained in
K[X_{t}]
; see Lemma
5.3
. Similarly, if

is a join node and
t^{\prime}
and

are its two children, we can prove that

.

Optimal Bounded Chain Problem

Let
K
be a simplicial complex and
b\in C_{d{-}1}(K)
a
(d{-}1)
-chain. The
Optimal Bounded Chain Problem
asks to find the minimum weight
d
-chain
c\in C_{d}(K)
bounded by
b
, if such a chain
c
exists. Let
(T,X)
a nice tree decomposition of
K
with root
r
. We will use a dynamic program on
T
to compute
c
.

At a node
t\in T
, we are interested in the portion of
b
contained in
K_{t}
; we define the
partial boundary at t
as
b_{t}:=b\cap(K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}])
. Our key observation is that any
d
-simplex that is incident to
b_{t}
is contained in
K_{t}
. This means that after the iteration in the algorithm where we process
t
, the portion of a candidate solution’s boundary in
K_{t}
but outside of

cannot be changed by adding more

-simplices. Therefore, a candidate solution
\gamma
at
t
should be a
d
-chain in
K_{t}
that satisfies
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}
. Otherwise, we place no restriction on the rest of
\gamma
’s boundary,
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
. There might be other simplices in
K\setminus K_{t}
incident to
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
, so even if
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]\neq b\cap K[X_{t}]
, this portion of
\partial\gamma
might change later in the algorithm. We therefore let
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
be any
(d{-}1)
-chain of
K[X_{t}]
. We call this chain
\beta=\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
. A
d
-chain
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t})
is said to
span
\boldsymbol{\beta}
at t
if
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}
and
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. See Figure
4
.

\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta

\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]

(d{-}1)

For each
(d{-}1)
-chain

Lemma 5.1

The entry

c
such that
\partial c=b
, if such a chain
c
exists.

Proof.

Recall that the bag
X_{r}
of the root
r
in our tree decomposition is empty, so
K[X_{r}]=\emptyset
. Also, each node in the tree decomposition is a descendant of
r
, so
V_{r}=V
,
K_{r}=K
, and
b_{r}=b
. There is a unique
(d{-}1)
-chain
\emptyset\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{r}])
. The entry
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]
is the minimum weight of a
d
-chain
c\in C_{d}(K_{r})=C_{d}(K)
such that (1)
\partial c\cap X_{r}=\emptyset
and, more importantly, (2)
\partial c\setminus K[X_{r}]=\partial c=b_{r}=b
. If no such chain
c
exists, then
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]=\infty
.
∎

We are almost ready to present our algorithm for OBCP, but first, we need a lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

If
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
, then there is a descendant
t_{\sigma}
of
t
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}}]
. In particular, if
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
, then there is a descendant
t_{\sigma}
of
t
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}}]

Proof.

Recall that
K_{t}=K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d}
, so
K_{t}\subset K[V_{t}]
. We prove the more general statement that if
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
, then there is a descendant
t_{\sigma}
of
t
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}}]
. Let
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
. By Lemma
3.2
there is a node
t_{\sigma}
in
T
such that
\sigma\subset X_{t_{\sigma}}
. If
t_{\sigma}
is in the subtree rooted at
t
, we are done. So assume
t_{\sigma}
is not in the subtree rooted at
t
. For every
v\in\sigma
, there is a node
t_{v}
in the subtree rooted at
t
such that
v\in X_{t_{v}}
. As well,
v\in X_{t_{\sigma}}
as
v\in\sigma\subset X_{t_{\sigma}}
. The nodes containing
v
form a connected subtree of
T
. So
v
is contained in the bag of each node on the unique path connecting
t_{v}
and
t_{\sigma}
. In particular,
v\in X_{t}
. Therefore, the bag
X_{t}
contains every
v\in\sigma
, so
\sigma\in K[X_{t}]
.
∎

We now give our dynamic program to compute
\mathbf{V}
on a nice tree decomposition. We compute the table
\mathbf{V}
starting at the leaves of
T
and moving towards the root. At each node
t
in our tree decomposition, we calculate the entries
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
using the entries of
\mathbf{V}
of
t
’s children. Therefore, to specify our dynamic program, it suffices to specify how to calculate
\mathbf{V}
at each type of node in a nice tree decomposition.

5.1
Leaf Nodes

Let
t
be a leaf node. Recall that
X_{t}=\emptyset
, so
K[X_{t}]=\emptyset
. Moreover,
t
has no children, so
V_{t}=K_{t}=b_{t}=\emptyset
. There is a unique
(d{-}1)
-chain
\beta=\emptyset\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. There is also a unique
d
-chain
\gamma=\emptyset\in C_{d}(K_{t})
. The chain
\gamma
spans
\beta
at
t
, so the unique table entry
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,t]=0
.

5.2
Introduce Nodes

Let
t
be an introduce node and
t^{\prime}
the unique child of
t
. Recall that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\sqcup\{w\}
for some vertex
w\in K
. Our first observation is that any simplex in the subcomplex
K[V_{t}]
that contains
w
is only contained in the subcomplex at the bag
K[X_{t}]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
such that
w\in\sigma
. Then
\sigma\in K[X_{t}]\setminus K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
.

Proof.

As
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
, then by Lemma
5.2
, there is a node
t_{\sigma}
in the subtree rooted at
t
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}}]
. Suppose for the purposes of contradiction that
t\neq t_{\sigma}
. Then
t_{\sigma}
is in the subtree rooted at
t^{\prime}
. As
w\in\sigma
, then
w\in X_{t_{\sigma}}
. The set of nodes whose bags contain
w
form a connected subtree of
T
, so each node on the unique path connecting
t
and
t_{\sigma}
must contain
w
in its bag. The node
t^{\prime}
is on this path, but
w\notin X_{t^{\prime}}
, a contradiction. Thus,
t=t_{\sigma}
. As no node in the subtree rooted at
t^{\prime}
contains
\sigma
, then by Lemma
5.2
,
\sigma\notin K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
.
∎

As each simplex that contains
w
is contained in
K[X_{t}]
, then introducing
w
does not change the complexes
K_{t}
outside of
K[X_{t}]
. We prove this in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.4
.

The complexes
K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]
.

Proof.

Observe that
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]=(K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d})\setminus K[X_{t}]=K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}]
. We will therefore prove that
K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}]=K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
.

Let
\sigma\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. It follows that
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
as
V_{t^{\prime}}\subset V_{t}
. We need to show that
\sigma\notin K[X_{t}]
. Suppose
\sigma\in K[X_{t}]
. As
X_{t}\setminus X_{t^{\prime}}=\{w\}
, then
w\in\sigma
. This cannot be the case, as any simplex containing
w
is not in
K_{t^{\prime}}\subset K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
by Lemma
5.3
. So
\sigma\notin K[X_{t}]
and
\sigma\in K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]

Now let
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}]
. We conclude that
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
as
X_{t^{\prime}}\subset X_{t}
. We need to show that
\sigma\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
. Suppose
\sigma\not\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
. Each descendant of
t
is a descendant of
t^{\prime}
except
t
itself. As
X_{t}\setminus X_{t^{\prime}}=\{w\}
, then
V_{t}\setminus V_{t^{\prime}}
can only contain
w
. We conclude
w\in\sigma
. This is a contradiction, as any simplex containing
w
in
K_{t}
is contained in
K[X_{t}]
by Lemma
5.3
. Thus
\sigma\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
.
∎

As introducing
w
does not change
K_{t}
outside of the bag
X_{t}
, then neither the chain group
C_{d}(K_{t})
nor the partial boundary
b_{t}
are changed by introducing
w
. We prove this in the following two lemmas.

Lemma 5.5
.

The chain groups
C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})=C_{d}(K_{t})
.

Proof.

We prove this by showing that
(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}=(K_{t})_{d}
. This follows from Lemma
5.4
as
(K_{t})_{d}=(K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d})_{d}=(K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}])_{d}
.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

The chain
b_{t}=b_{t^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
5.4
, as

b_{t}=b\cap K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]=b\cap K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}.

∎

Introducing
w
to the bag
X_{t}
does not change
C_{d}(X_{t})
or
b_{t}
, so unsurprisingly, the values in the dynamic programming table don’t change either. The following lemma proves this and gives a formula for computing
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
t
be an introduce node and let
t^{\prime}
be the unique child of
t
. Let
\beta\in C_{d-1}(K[X_{t}])
. Then

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\begin{cases}\mathbf{V}[\beta,t^{\prime}]&\text{ if }\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
. We claim that any chain
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
spanning
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
spans
\beta
at
t
and vice versa. Let
\gamma
be a
d
-chain that spans
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
. As
C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})=C_{d}(K_{t})
, then
\gamma
is a
d
-chain in both
K_{t}
and
K_{t^{\prime}}
; moreover,
\partial\gamma\subset K_{t}\cap K_{t^{\prime}}
, so
\partial\gamma=\partial\gamma\cap K_{t}=\partial\gamma\cap K_{t^{\prime}}
. Using this fact and Lemma
5.4
, we see

\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=(\partial\gamma\cap K_{t^{\prime}})\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=(\partial\gamma\cap K_{t})\setminus K[X_{t}]=\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}].

We use the fact that
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
to prove that
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=b_{t}
. Indeed, as
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}
and
b_{t^{\prime}}=b_{t}
from Lemma
5.6
, then
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}
. Moreover, as
\partial\gamma=\beta\sqcup b_{t}
, then the rest of the
\partial\gamma
is
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. This completes the proof that
\gamma
spans
\beta
at
t
. By the same argument, any chain spanning
\beta
at
t
spans
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
. As the same set of chains span
\beta
at
t
and
t^{\prime}
, then
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\mathbf{V}[\beta,t^{\prime}]

Now let
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])\setminus C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
. As
\beta\notin C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
, there must be a simplex
\sigma\in\beta
such that
w\in\sigma
. As any simplex
\sigma
such that
w\in\sigma
is not contained in
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, there is no chain
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t})
that spans
\beta
at
t
as
C_{d}(K_{t})=C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
. Thus,
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\infty
.
∎

5.3
Forget Nodes

Let
t
be a forget node and
t^{\prime}
the unique child of
t
. Recall that
X_{t}\sqcup\{w\}=X_{t^{\prime}}
. Forget nodes add new chains to the chain group
C_{d}(K_{t})
. In particular, any
d
-simplex
\sigma\in(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}
that contains
w
is not contained in
K_{t^{\prime}}
but will be contained in
K_{t}
. We prove this in our first lemma. Let
(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}=\{\sigma\in(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}\,|\,w\in\sigma\}
and let
C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
be the free abelian group on
(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
.

Lemma 5.8
.

The chain group
C_{d}(K_{t})=C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})\oplus C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
.

Proof.

As
C_{d}(K_{t})
is generated by
(K_{t})_{d}
, we can prove the lemma by showing that
(K_{t})_{d}=(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\sqcup(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
.

We first prove that
(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\subset(K_{t})_{d}
. Let
\sigma\in(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
. By the definition of
K_{t^{\prime}}
,
\sigma\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
and
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. We know that
K[V_{t}]=K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
as each descendant of
t^{\prime}
is a descendant of
t
and
X_{t}\subset X_{t^{\prime}}
, so
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
as well. Furthermore,
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t}]
as
K[X_{t}]\subset K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
and
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. As
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
and
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t}]
, then
\sigma\in(K_{t})_{d}
as
(K_{t})_{d}=K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}]
. This proves that
(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\subset(K_{t})_{d}
.

As
(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\subset(K_{t})_{d}
, the next step to proving
(K_{t})_{d}=(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\sqcup(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
is to show that
(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}=(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
. Let
\sigma\in(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
. As
\sigma\in(K_{t})_{d}
, then
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
and
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t}]
. As
\sigma\notin(K_{t})_{d}
, then either
\sigma\not\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
or
\sigma\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. We know that
\sigma\in K[V_{t^{\prime}}]
, as we proved in the previous paragraph that
K[V_{t^{\prime}}]=K[V_{t}]
, so we conclude that
\sigma\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. It must be the case that
w\in\sigma
as
\sigma\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]\subset K[X_{t}]
and
X_{t^{\prime}}\setminus X_{t}=\{w\}
, so
\sigma\in(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
. This proves that
(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\subset(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}

We now prove that
(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}\subset(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
. Let
\sigma\in(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
. We know that
\sigma\in K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, which implies that
\sigma\in K[V_{t}]
as
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]\subset K[V_{t}]
. As
w\in\sigma
, we also have that
\sigma\not\in K[X_{t}]
, so
\sigma\in(K_{t})_{d}
. Lastly, as
\sigma\in(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}
, then
\sigma\notin(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}=K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. Thus,
\sigma\in(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
and
(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}\subset(K_{t})_{d}\setminus(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}
.
∎

Intuitively, we can build the chain
\gamma
that attains
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
by composing a minimal chain
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{d}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
that attains
V[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
and a chain
\gamma_{w}\in C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
of
d
-simplices that all contain
w
. We need to enforce that
\partial(\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{w})=\beta+b_{t}
. The chain
\gamma^{\prime}
includes
b_{t^{\prime}}
in the portion of its boundary outside
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, so we want to choose
\gamma_{w}
to help cover the rest of the boundary
b_{t}\setminus b_{t^{\prime}}
. We can prove that
b_{t}\setminus b_{t^{\prime}}
is contained in
K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, i.e.
b_{t}\setminus b_{t^{\prime}}=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. So if
(\partial(\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}])\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, then
\partial(\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]
will cover all of
b_{t}
. This is equivalently to requiring that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, as
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta^{\prime}
and
\partial\gamma_{w}\cap K[X_{t}]=\gamma_{w}
. Indeed, this is the requirement we see in the formula for
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
in Lemma
5.10
.

We first give a characterization of
b_{t^{\prime}}
.

Lemma 5.9
.

The chain
b_{t^{\prime}}=b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}].

Proof.

The chains
b_{t}
and
b_{t^{\prime}}
are defined
b_{t^{\prime}}=(b\cap K_{t^{\prime}})\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
and
b_{t}=(b\cap K_{t})\setminus K[X_{t}]
. If we consider the difference
b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, we find that

b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=((b\cap K_{t})\setminus K[X_{t}])\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=(b\cap K_{t})\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

as
K[X_{t}]\subset K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. We will use this fact later.

The sets of vertices in the trees rooted at
t
and
t^{\prime}
are equal, i.e.
V_{t}=V_{t^{\prime}}
, but the vertices in the bags
X_{t}\subset X_{t^{\prime}}
. We use these two facts to show that the complexes
K_{t}
and
K_{t^{\prime}}
only differ in the complexes induced by their bags, namely
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
:

K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}].

We have proved that
b_{t}=b\cap(K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
. We have also proved that
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. We use these facts to prove that
b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}
as

b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b\cap K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b\cap K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}.\qed

Lemma
5.9
implies that
b_{t}\setminus b_{t^{\prime}}=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
, as claimed above. We are now ready to give a formula for
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. Then

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\underset{\beta^{\prime},\gamma_{w}}{\min}\;\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]+\|\gamma_{w}\|

where the minimization ranges over
\beta^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{w}
such that

•

\beta^{\prime}\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])

•

\gamma_{w}\in C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])

•

(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta

•

(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

Proof.

First, we show that each
d
-chain on the right hand side of the equation in the lemma spans
\beta
at
t.

Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
that attains
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
, and let
\gamma_{w}\in C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
such that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
and
(\beta+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

Let
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{w}
; we show that
\gamma
spans
\beta
at
t
. The boundary

\partial\gamma=\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w}=b_{t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w}.

If
\gamma
were to span
\beta
at
t
, then
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
and
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}
. The intersection
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]
is

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=

\displaystyle(b_{t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]

(as
b_{t^{\prime}}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\emptyset
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta

(by assumption)

The difference
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]
is

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=

\displaystyle(b_{t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}+(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]

(as
b_{t^{\prime}}\cap K[X_{t}]=\emptyset
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}+b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(by assumption)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]+b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(by Lemma
5.9
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t}

This proves that
\gamma
indeed spans
\beta
at
t
. Moreover, as
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma_{w}
are disjoint, then
\|\gamma\|=\|\gamma^{\prime}\|+\|\gamma_{w}\|
. As
\gamma^{\prime}
achieves
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
by assumption, then
\|\gamma\|=\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]+\|\gamma_{w}\|
.

Next, we verify that the chain that achieves
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
is included in the right hand side of the equation. By Lemma
5.8
,
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma_{w}
for some
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
and
\gamma_{w}\in C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
. Let
\beta^{\prime}=\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
. We need to verify that
\gamma^{\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
and achieves
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
, that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
, and that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
.

We first prove that
\gamma^{\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
. We already know that
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\beta^{\prime}
, so we only need to prove that
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\partial\gamma_{w}\subset K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\partial\gamma=\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta+b_{t})\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\gamma
bounds
\beta
at
t
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\beta\subset K[X_{t}]
and
K[X_{t}]\subset K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}

(by Lemma

)

This proves that

spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
. We delay proving that
\gamma^{\prime}

\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]

We now prove that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. We see that

\displaystyle\beta=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]

(by assumption)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]

(as
\partial\gamma=\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]

(as
\gamma^{\prime}
bounds
\beta^{\prime}
at

)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\cap K[X_{t}]

(as
b_{t^{\prime}}\cap K[X_{t}]=\emptyset
.)

Finally, we prove that
(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]=b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
.

\displaystyle b_{t}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\gamma
spans
\beta
at
t
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

\partial\gamma=\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(b_{t^{\prime}}+\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]

(as
\gamma^{\prime}
bounds
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{\prime}+\partial\gamma_{w})\setminus K[X_{t}]

We are now ready to prove that

spans
\beta
at

∎

5.4
Join Nodes

Let
t
be a join node, and let
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
be the two children of

. Recall that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}=X_{t^{\prime\prime}}
. We first observe that
K_{t^{\prime}}
and
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}
only overlap in

.

Lemma 5.11
.

The complex
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]=(K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t}^{\prime}])\sqcup(K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]).

Proof.

As
K[X_{t}]=K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]
, we see immediately that both

K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]\subset K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]
and
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]\subset K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]
as both
K_{t^{\prime}}\subset K_{t}
and
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\subset K_{t}
.

We now prove that
K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
and
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]
are disjoint. Suppose there is a simplex
\sigma\in(K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}])\cap(K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}])
. Then by Lemma
5.2
, there are nodes
t_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
t_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}
in the subtrees rooted at
t^{\prime}
and
t^{\prime\prime}
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}^{\prime}}]
and
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}}]
. The set of nodes containing
\sigma
form a connected subtree. This is a contradiction, as
t
lies on the unique path connecting
t_{\sigma}^{\prime}
and
t_{\sigma}^{\prime\prime}
and
\sigma\notin K[X_{t}]
by assumption. Hence
K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]\cap K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]=\emptyset
.

We now prove that
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]\subset(K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}])\sqcup(K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}])
. Let
\sigma\in K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]
. By Lemma
5.2
, there is a node
t_{\sigma}
in the subtree rooted at
t
such that
\sigma\in K[X_{t_{\sigma}}]
. We know
t_{\sigma}\neq t
as
\sigma\notin K[X_{t}]
, so
t_{\sigma}
is either in the subtree rooted at
t^{\prime}
or the subtree rooted at
t^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

Lemma
5.11
implies that any
d
-chain in

is the sum of a
d
-chain in
K_{t^{\prime}}
and a
d
-chain in
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}
. We prove this in the following lemma.

Lemma 5.12
.

The chain group
C_{d}(K_{t})=C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})\oplus C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime\prime}})
.

Proof.

As
C_{d}(K_{t})
is generated by
(K_{t})_{d}
, we can prove this by showing that
(K_{t})_{d}=(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\sqcup(K_{t^{\prime\prime}})_{d}
. We first note that

(K_{t})_{d}=(K[V_{t}]\setminus(K[X_{t}])_{d})_{d}=(K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}])_{d}

so it follows by Lemma
5.11
that

(K_{t})_{d}=(K[V_{t}]\setminus K[X_{t}])_{d}=(K[V_{t^{\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}\sqcup(K[V_{t^{\prime\prime}}]\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}])_{d}=(K_{t^{\prime}})_{d}\sqcup(K_{t^{\prime\prime}})_{d}.\qed

Similarly, the boundary
b_{t}
is composed of a portion in
K_{t^{\prime}}
, namely
b_{t^{\prime}}
, and a portion in
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}
, namely
b_{t^{\prime\prime}}
.

Corollary 5.13
.

The chain
b_{t}=b_{t^{\prime}}\sqcup b_{t^{\prime\prime}}.

Proof.

The boundary
b_{t}=b\cap K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]
. Using Lemma
5.11
,

b_{t}=b\cap K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]=(b\cap K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t}])\sqcup(b\cap K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t}])=b_{t^{\prime}}\sqcup b_{t^{\prime\prime}}.\qed

These two lemmas tell us that a chain
\gamma
that spans
\beta
at
t
is the sum of chains
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
. These chains satisfy three conditions:
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}
,
\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime\prime}}
, and
(\partial\gamma^{\prime}+\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. To find the
minimal
chain that spans
\beta
at
t
, we only need to consider minimal chains
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
with properties. The following formula for

confirms this.

Lemma 5.14
.

Let
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. Then

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\underset{\beta^{\prime},\beta^{\prime\prime}}{\min}\;\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]+\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}]

where the minimization ranges over
\beta^{\prime}
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}
such that

•

\beta^{\prime}\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])

•

\beta^{\prime\prime}\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}])

•

\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta

Proof.

We first show that each chain on the right hand side of the equation spans
\beta
at
t
. Let
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime\prime}})
such that
\gamma^{\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
,
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime\prime}
at
t^{\prime\prime}
, and
\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}=\beta
. We claim that
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime\prime}
spans
\beta
at
t
. This follows as

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t}])+(\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\cap K[X_{t}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}+\beta^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta.

and

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])+(\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}+b_{t^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t}

(by Corollary
5.13
)

Moreover,
\|\gamma\|=\|\gamma^{\prime}\|+\|\gamma^{\prime\prime}\|
as
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
are disjoint. If
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
achieve
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
and
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}]
, then
\|\gamma\|=\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t]+\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}]
.

Now let
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t})
be the minimum weight chain that spans
\beta
at
t
. By Lemma
5.12
,
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}+\gamma^{\prime\prime}
for some
\gamma^{\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime\prime}})
. Let
\beta^{\prime}=\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
and
\beta^{\prime\prime}=\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]
. We will show that
\gamma^{\prime}
and
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
achieve
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime},t^{\prime}]
and
\mathbf{V}[\beta^{\prime\prime},t^{\prime\prime}]
respectively.

We first show that
\gamma^{\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
. We already know that
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\beta^{\prime}
, so we only need to prove that
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=b_{t^{\prime}}
. We consider the intersection
(\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}
. We see that

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}+(\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}

(*)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}]

(as
\partial\gamma^{\prime}\subset K_{t^{\prime}}
.)

where line (*) follows from the fact that
(\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}=\emptyset
. This is the case as
\partial\gamma^{\prime\prime}\setminus K[X_{t}]\subset K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t}]

K_{t}^{\prime}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
and
K_{t^{\prime\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}]
are disjoint by Lemma

We can alternatively express

as

\displaystyle(\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}])\cap K_{t^{\prime}}=

\displaystyle b_{t}\cap K_{t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}\cap K_{t^{\prime}}+b_{t^{\prime\prime}}\cap K_{t^{\prime\prime}}

(by Corollary
5.13
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}\cap K_{t^{\prime}}

(*)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle b_{t^{\prime}}

(as
b_{t^{\prime}}\subset K_{t^{\prime}}
.)

where (*) follows as

and

and

spans
\beta^{\prime}
at
t^{\prime}
. The proof that
\gamma^{\prime\prime}
spans
\beta^{\prime\prime}
at

is symmetrical.

As
\gamma
is the minimum weight chain that spans
\beta
at

, then
\gamma^{\prime}
and

must be the chains that achieve

and

and

that span

and

at

at

. So if (say)

did not achieve

5.5
Analysis

We now analyze the running time of the dynamic program, thereby proving Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We first show that we can compute the dynamic programming table entry for each type of node in
T
in
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
time.

We can compute the entry at the leaf node in constant time as we are entering in a single table entry.

We can compute the value
V[\beta,t]
of each
(d{-}1)
-chain
\beta
at an introduce node in constant time. As there are
O(\binom{k+1}{d})=O\left((k+1)^{d}\right)

(d{-}1)
-simplices in
K[X_{t}]
, then there are
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}

(d{-}1)
-chains in
C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. Processing an introduce node thus takes
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
time in total.

We compute the entry at a forget node by performing nested iterations over
C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
and
C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t}])
. There are
O(\binom{k+1}{d})=O\left((k+1)^{d}\right)

(d{-}1)
-simplices in
K[X_{t}]
, so there are
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}

(d{-}1)
-chains
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. There are likewise
O(\binom{k+1}{d})=O(k^{d})

d
-simplices (we choose
d
vertices in addition to
w
) in
(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])_{d}^{w}
and
2^{O(k^{d})}
chains
\gamma_{w}\in C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
. The size of both of the chain groups
C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
and
C_{d}^{w}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
is at most
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
, so the nested iteration over both of these groups takes
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
time.

We compute the entry at a join node by iterating over the chain groups
C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])
and
C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime\prime}}])
, which takes
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
time.

There are
O(kn)
nodes in a nice tree decomposition, and the dynamic programming table entry at each node can be computed in
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}
time. The algorithm therefore takes
2^{O\left(k^{d}\right)}n
time in total. As
d\leq k
by Corollary
3.4
, the running time of our algorithm is also
2^{O\left(k^{k}\right)}n
.
∎

As a corollary, we can test whether two
d{-}1
chains
b
and
h
are homologous by running our algorithm on
b+h
. The chains
b
and
h
are homologous if and only if
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]<\infty
. Hence, we obtain Corollary
1.2
. This implies we can use our algorithm to test whether or not a (
d{-}1
)-cycle
b
is null-homologous, which is to say, whether
b
is homologous to the empty chain.

Our algorithm uses the Hamming norm to measure the weight of a chain, but our algorithm can be easily adapted to solve OBCP in a simplicial complex with weight
d
-simplices. If
w:K_{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
is a weight function on the
d
-simplices, we define a weight function on
d
-chains
w:C_{d}(K)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
such that
w(\gamma)=\sum_{\sigma\in\gamma}w(\sigma)
is the weight of the chain
\gamma
. If we substitute
w(\gamma)
for
\|\gamma\|
everywhere in our algorithm for OBCP, it is easy to see this adapted algorithm finds the minimum weight chain
c
bounded by
b
.

6
Optimal Homologous Chain Problem

Let
K
be a simplicial complex and let
b\in C_{d{-}1}(K)
. The
Optimal Homologous Chain Problem
asks to find the minimum weight
(d{-}1)
-chain
h\in C_{d{-}1}(K)
such that
b+h=\partial c
for some
c\in C_{d}(K)
. Let
(T,X)
be a nice tree decomposition of
K
. We will find
h
using a dynamic program on
T
.

Any chain
c\in C_{d}(K)
defines a chain homologous to
b
, namely
h=\partial c+b
. Instead of searching for the
(d{-}1)
-chain
h
, we can therefore search for the
d
-chain
c
that minimizes the weight
\|\partial c+b\|
. The chain
c
also defines the minimum chain homologous to
b
in a local sense. If we restrict this chain to a subcomplex
K_{t}
, we find that
c\cap K_{t}
is the chain that minimizes
\|\partial(c\cap K_{t})\setminus K[X_{t}]+b_{t}\|
over all chains with boundary satisfying
\partial(c\cap K_{t})\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. Accordingly, for each
(d{-}1)
-chain
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
, we store the dynamic programming table entry

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\underset{\underset{\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta}{\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t}):}}{\min}\|\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]+b_{t}\|.

We say a
d
-chain
\gamma

spans
\boldsymbol{\beta}
at t
if
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta
. Lemma
6.1
shows that the table
\mathbf{V}
contains the weight of the optimal solution.

Lemma 6.1
.

The entry
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]
is the minimum weight
\|\partial c+b\|
for any chain
c\in C_{d}(K)
.

Proof.

The unique entry at the root
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]
contains the weight of the minimum weight
(d{-}1)
-chain homologous to
b
. The bag at the root
X_{t}
is empty, so
K[X_{r}]=\emptyset
. Moreover,
V_{r}=V
, so
K_{r}=K
and
b_{r}=b
. Thus,
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,r]
contains the minimum weight
\|\partial c\setminus K[X_{r}]+b\|=\|\partial c+b\|
for any chain
c\in C_{d}(K_{r})=C_{d}(K)
.
∎

Our algorithm for OHCP is similar to our algorithm for OBCP as we are searching for a
d
-chain
c
, except we are optimizing a different function. We perform a dynamic program on our tree decomposition, and at each node
t
, we store the weight of the chain
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t})
that spans each
(d{-}1)
-chain
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
and minimizes the objective function. We construct the chain
\gamma
using the orthogonal decompositions of the chain groups
C_{d}(K_{t})
from Section
5
. At each node, the two algorithms will loop over the same chain groups. Accordingly, the running time for our algorithm for OHCP is the same as the running time for our algorithm for OBCP, and we obtain Theorem
1.3
.

We now present our dynamic program for OHCP. We compute the entries of
\mathbf{V}
at a node
t
using the entries of
\mathbf{V}
at the children of
t
. Accordingly, we only need to specify how to compute
t
at each type of node in a nice tree decomposition.

6.1
Leaf Nodes

Let
t
be a leaf node. Recall that
X_{t}=\emptyset
, so
K[X_{t}]=\emptyset
. Moreover,
t
has no children, so
V_{t}=K_{t}=b_{t}=\emptyset
. There is a unique
(d{-}1)
-chain
\beta=\emptyset\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
and a unique

-chain
\gamma=\emptyset\in C_{d}(K_{t})
. The chain
\gamma
spans
\beta
at

, so
\mathbf{V}[\emptyset,t]=0
.

6.2
Introduce Nodes

Let
t
be an introduce node and
t^{\prime}
the unique child of
t
. Recall that
X_{t}=X_{t^{\prime}}\sqcup\{w\}
. Let
\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t}])
. We claim the following formula for
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
t
be an introduce node and
t^{\prime}
be its unique child. Let
\beta\in C_{d-1}(K[X_{t}])
. Then

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\begin{cases}\mathbf{V}[\beta,t^{\prime}]&\text{ if }\beta\in C_{d{-}1}(K[X_{t^{\prime}}])\\
\infty&\text{ otherwise}\end{cases}

Proof.

We will show that any chain that spans
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
spans
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
and vice versa. Let
\gamma\in C_{d}(K_{t^{\prime}})
such that
\gamma
spans
\beta
at
t^{\prime}
; that is,
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\beta
. We need to show that
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\beta=\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
as well. We do this by proving the equivalent statement that
\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}]=\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}].
As
\partial\gamma\in K_{t}\cap K_{t^{\prime}}
, then

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\cap(K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}])

(as
\partial\gamma\subset K_{t^{\prime}}
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\cap(K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}])

(as
K_{t}\setminus K[X_{t}]=K_{t^{\prime}}\setminus K[X_{t^{\prime}}]
by Lemma
5.4
)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial\gamma\setminus K[X_{t}].

(as
\partial\gamma\subset K_{t}
)

As a corollary, we see that
\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]=\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]=\beta
. So if
\gamma
spans
\beta
at

, then
\gamma
spans
\beta
at

and vice versa. Moreover, as

by Lemma

, then
\|\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t}]+b_{t}\|=\|\partial\gamma\cap K[X_{t^{\prime}}]+b_{t^{\prime}}\|
. So
\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]
for each
(d{-}1)

\beta
in

.

As

\mathbf{V}[\beta,t]=\infty
.
∎

Forget Nodes

Let

t^{\prime}

7
Comparison with Other Algorithms

In this section, we compare our algorithms for OHCP and OBCP to the algorithms introduced by Blaser and Vȧgset
[
5
]
and Blaser et al.
[
4
]
. Our algorithm and their algorithms are parameterized by the treewidth of different graphs associated with a simplicial complex, so we compare the treewidth of the 1-skeleton to the treewidth of these graphs. We start by defining these graphs.

The
d-connectivity graph
is the graph
Con_{d}(K)
with the
d
-simplices
K_{d}
as vertices and pairs of
d
-simplices that share a
(d{-}1)
-face as edges.
Level d of the Hasse diagram
is the graph
Hasse_{d}(K)
with the
(d{-}1)
and
d
-simplices
K_{d-1}\cup K_{d}
as vertices and edges between
d
-simplices and their
(d{-}1)
-faces.

Our algorithms for OHCP and OBCP run in
2^{O(k^{d})}n
time, where
k
is the treewidth of the 1-skeleton and
n
is the number of vertices in the complex. Blaser and Vȧgset introduce two algorithms for OHCP: one algorithm uses the
d
-connectivity graph, and the other algorithm uses level
d
of the Hasse diagram of
K
. The algorithms of Blaser and Vȧgset run in
O(2^{l(2d+5)}m)
and
O(2^{2l}m)
time respectively, where
l
is the treewidth and
m
is the number of vertices in the given graph. Blaser et al. introduce an algorithm for OBCP that runs in
O(4^{l}m)
time where
l
is the treewidth and
m
is the number of vertices of the Hasse diagram. Note that for the
d
-connectivity graph and level
d
of the Hasse diagram, the number of vertices
m
are
O(\binom{n}{d+1})
and
O(\binom{n}{d}+\binom{n}{d+1})
respectively, and these bounds can be tight.

The goal of this section is to compare the treewidth of the graphs used by our algorithm and by the algorithms of Blaser and Vȧgset and Blaser et al. The following theorem by Blaser and Vȧgset compare the treewidth of
Con_{d}(K)
and
Hasse_{d}(K)

Theorem 7.1

(Proposition 7.1, Blaser and Vȧgset
[
5
]
)
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex. The treewidth
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))\leq\tw(Con_{d}(K))+1
; however,
\tw(Con_{d}(K))
is not bounded by
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))
.

As the treewidth of
Hasse_{d}(K)
is
O(\tw(Con_{d}(K)))
, we will spend the rest of this section comparing the treewidth of
Hasse_{d}(K)
and the treewidth of the 1-skeleton
K^{1}
. We will show that
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))\in O(\binom{\tw(K^{1})+1}{d})
. We will then show that for a certain class of complexes, this upper bound is nearly tight, i.e.
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))\in\Omega(\binom{\tw(K^{1})+1}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{d+1})
. The first result implies that algorithms parameterized by
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))
having running time
O(2^{\tw(K^{1})^{d}}m)
; in other words, these algorithm have (at worst) the same dependence on
\tw(K^{1})
as our algorithms. The second result implies that in some cases, the dependence on
\tw(K^{1})
is almost the same.

7.1
Upper Bounds

In this section, we provide an upper bound on the treewidth of level
d
of the Hasse diagram in terms of the treewidth of the 1-skeleton. The proof of this theorem was developed with Nello Blaser and Erlend Raa Vȧgset.

Theorem 7.2
.

Let
K
be a simplicial complex. Then
\tw(Hasse_{d}(K))\in O(\binom{\tw(K^{1})+1}{d})
.

Proof.

Let
(T,X)
be any tree decomposition of
K^{1}
of width
k
. We will use
(T,X)
as a starting point for constructing a tree decomposition of
Hasse_{d}(K)
of width
O(\binom{k+1}{d})
. For a node
t\in T
, we add all
(d-1)
-simplices in
K[X_{t}]
to a new bag
Y_{t}
. The size of any bag
Y_{t}
is
O(\binom{k+1}{d})
, as any set of
d
vertices in
X_{t}
may be a
(d-1)
-simplex in
K
. By Corollary
3.3
, each
(d-1)
-simplex
\sigma\in Y_{t}
for some
t\in T
as
\sigma\subset X_{t}
for some
t\in T
, and moreover, the set of nodes
\{t\in T:\sigma\in T\}
are connected.

Next, for each
d
-simplex
\tau\in K
, there is a node
t\in T
such that
\tau\subset X_{t}
by Corollary
3.3
. By construction, we conclude that each
(d-1)
-face
\sigma
of
\tau
must be contained in the new bag
Y_{t}
. We add a new node
t_{\tau}
to the tree with bag
Y_{t_{\tau}}=Y_{t}\cup\{\tau\}
, and we connect
t_{\tau}
to
t
. The size of
Y_{t_{\tau}}
is
O(\binom{k+1}{d})
, as
Y_{t_{\tau}}
contains one more vertex than
Y_{t}
.

We now verify that this new tree decomposition satisfies the conditions to be a tree decomposition. Each
(d-1)
-simplex
\sigma
is contained in the bags of the nodes
\{t\in T:\sigma\subset X_{t}\}\cup\{t_{\tau}:\sigma\subset\tau\}
; the nodes
\{t\in T:\sigma\subset X_{t}\}
form a connected subtree as
(T,X)
is a tree decomposition, and each node
t_{\tau}
is connected to some node in
\{t\in T:\sigma\subset X_{t}\}
. Each
d
-simplex
\tau
is contained in the single bag
Y_{t_{\tau}}
. Finally, each edge in
Hasse_{d}(K)
is a pair
\{\sigma,\tau\}
of a
(d-1)
-simplex
\sigma
and a
d
-simplex
\tau
such that
\sigma\subset\tau
. By construction,
\{\sigma,\tau\}\subset Y_{t_{\tau}}
.

This proves there is a tree decomposition of
Hasse_{d}(K)
of width
O(\binom{k+1}{d})
when
k
is the width of any tree decomposition of
K^{1}
. In particular, this proves that there is a tree decomposition of width
O(\binom{\tw(K^{1})+1}{d})
.
∎

7.2
Lower Bounds

Let
\Delta^{n}
be the simplicial complex that is the set of all subsets of a set of vertices
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
. (Intuitively,
\Delta^{n}
is a
(n-1)
-simplex and all its faces.) Our main theorem of this section is a lower bound of
\Omega(\binom{n}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{d+1})
on
\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
. The treewidth of the 1-skeleton
\tw((\Delta^{n})^{1})=n-1
as
(\Delta^{n})^{1}
is a complete graph, so this lower bound shows that the
\Delta^{n}
is close to being a worst-case for the treewidth of the Hasse diagram. Specifically, the lower bound on
\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
differs from the upper bound on
\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
of Theorem
7.2
by a multiplicative factor of
d+1
.

7.2.1
Background: Vertex and Edge Expansion

We will obtain our lower bounds of
\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
using the related notions of vertex and edge expansion. Let
G
be a graph. The
vertex expansion
of
G
is

VE(G)=\underset{S\subset V(G):1\leq|S|\leq\frac{1}{2}|V(G)|}{\min}\frac{|N(S)|}{|S|}

where
N(S)
is the set of vertices in
V(G)\setminus S
with a neighbor in
S
. The vertex expansion of
G
is closely tied to the treewidth of
G
, as evidenced by the following lemma from Chandran and Subramian
[
15
]
.

Lemma 7.3

(Chandran and Subramian, Lemma 9
[
15
]
)
.

Let
1\leq s\leq|V(G)|
. Define
N_{\min}(G,s)
to be

N_{\min}(G,s)=\underset{S\subset V(G):s/2\leq|S|\leq s}{\min}|N(S)|.

Then
\tw(G)\geq N_{\min}(G,s)-1
.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
\tw(G)\geq\frac{VE(G)\cdot|V(G)|}{4}
.

Proof of Corollary.

We obtain the corollary by setting
s=|V(G)|/2
. Using the definition of vertex expansion, any set of vertices
S
with at least
s/2=|V(G)|/4
vertices satisfies
|N(S)|\geq VE(G)\cdot|S|\geq\frac{VE(G)\cdot|V(G)|}{4}
.
∎

We are able to get a lower bound on the vertex expansion (and by Corollary
7.4
, a lower bound on the treewidth of
G
) using the related notion of edge expansion. The
edge expansion
of a graph
G
is

EE(G)=\underset{S\subset V(G):1\leq|S|\leq|V(G)|/2}{\min}\frac{|\delta(S)|}{|S|}

where
\delta(S)
is the set of edges with one endpoint in
S
and one endpoint in
V(G)\setminus S
. The following lemma relates the notion of edge and vertex expansion.

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
d_{\max}
be the maximum degree of a vertex in
G
. Then
VE(G)\geq\frac{EE(G)}{d_{\max}}
.

Proof.

Let
S\subset V(G)
be a set of vertices such that
1\leq|S|\leq|V(G)|/2
. Then
|N(S)|\geq|\delta(S)|/d_{\max}
as each vertex in
N(S)
is incident to at most
d_{\max}
edges in
\delta(S)
. If
S^{*}=\underset{S\subset V(G):1\leq|S|\leq|V(G)|/2}{\arg\min}\frac{|N(S)|}{|S|}
, then

VE(G)=\frac{|N(S^{*})|}{|S^{*}|}\geq\frac{|\delta(S^{*})|}{d_{\max}\cdot|S^{*}|}\geq\frac{EE(G)}{d_{\max}}

∎

We can now combine Corollary
7.4
and Lemma
7.5
to get a lower bound on the treewidth in terms of edge expansion.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
G
be a graph. Then
\tw(G)\geq\frac{EE(G)\cdot|V(G)|}{4\cdot d_{\max}}
.

7.2.2
Background: Edge Transitivity

We are able to get a lower bound on the edge expansion of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
as
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is
edge-transitive
. Let
G
be a graph. A
graph automorphism
is a bijection
\phi:V(G)\to V(G)
such that
\{u,v\}\in E(G)
if and only if
\{\phi(u),\phi(v)\}\in E(G)
. A graph
G
is
edge-transitive
if for any two edges
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}
and
\{u_{2},v_{2}\}
of
G
, there is an automorphism
\phi:V(G)\to V(G)
such that
\{\phi(u_{1}),\phi(v_{1})\}=\{u_{2},v_{2}\}
.

Lemma 7.7
.

The graph
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is edge-transitive.

Proof.

Recall that
\Delta^{n}
is the set of all subsets of a set
V=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}
, and
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
has vertices that are the set of all subsets of size
d
and
d+1
of
V
. As a stepping stone to the lemma, we claim that any bijection of
f:V\to V
defines a graph automorphism
\phi_{f}:V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))\to V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
. Specifically, if
\sigma=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}\}\in V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
is a
(d-1)
-simplex, we define
\phi_{f}
to map
\phi_{f}(\sigma)=\{f(u_{1}),\ldots,f(u_{d})\}
. Note that
\phi_{f}(\sigma)
is a set with
d
distinct elements as
f
is a bijection; or in other words,
\phi_{f}(\sigma)
is a
(d-1)
-simplex. We define
\phi_{f}
analogously on
d
-simplices in
V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
.

We now must verify that
\phi_{f}
is a graph automorphism. Any edge
\{\sigma,\tau\}
of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is a pair of a
(d-1)
-simplex
\sigma
and a
d
-simplex
\tau
such that
\sigma\subset\tau
. It is straightforward to verify that if
\{\sigma,\tau\}
is an edge of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
, then
\{\phi_{f}(\sigma),\phi_{f}(\tau)\}
is also an edge; indeed,
\phi_{f}(\sigma)
and
\phi_{f}(\tau)
are a
(d-1)
and
d
-simplex respectively, and
\phi_{f}(\sigma)\subset\phi_{f}(\tau)
by definition. It is also straightforward to verify the converse.

We now use this fact to show that
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is edge-transitive. Let
\{\sigma_{1},\tau_{1}\}
and
\{\sigma_{2},\tau_{2}\}
be any two edges of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
such that
\sigma_{1}=\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{d-1}\}
,
\tau_{1}=\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{d}\}
,
\sigma_{2}=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{d-1}\}
, and
\tau_{2}=\{v_{0},\ldots,v_{d}\}
. Let
f:V\to V
be any bijection such that
f(u_{i})=v_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq d
. From the previous argument, the map
f
defines a graph automorphism
\phi_{f}
on
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
. It follows immediately from the definition of
\phi_{f}
that
\phi_{f}(\sigma_{1})=\sigma_{2}
and
\phi_{f}(\tau_{1})=\tau_{2}
.
∎

The following lemma by Babai and Szegedy
[
2
]
provides a lower bound on the edge expansion of edge-transitive graphs. Let
a
and
b
be non-zero real numbers. The
harmonic mean
of
a
and
b
is
2\cdot\left(\frac{1}{a}+\frac{1}{b}\right)^{-1}
.

Lemma 7.8

(Babai and Szegedy, 1992)
.

Let
G
be a simple, edge-transitive graph. Let
D
be the diameter of
G
, and let
r
be the harmonic mean of the minimum and maximum degree of
G
. The edge expansion of
G
is
EE(G)\geq\frac{r}{2D}
.

7.2.3
Putting Everything Together

We now calculate the diameter
D
of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
and the parameter
r
from the statement of Lemma
7.8
.

Lemma 7.9
.

The diameter
D
of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is bounded above by
2d+2
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma
and
\tau
be distinct vertices of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
. We claim there is a
|\sigma\oplus\tau|
-length path between
\sigma
and
\tau
in
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
, where
\sigma\oplus\tau
is the symmetric difference of the sets
\sigma
and
\tau
. To prove this, we will show there is a simplex
\sigma^{\prime}
adjacent to
\sigma
such that
|\sigma^{\prime}\oplus\tau|=|\sigma\oplus\tau|-1
. This will imply the lemma, as the largest symmetric difference between two simplices in
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is between two disjoint
(d+1)
-simplices, where the symmetric difference is
2d+2
.

We can prove the existence of
\sigma^{\prime}
by induction. First consider the case that
|\sigma\oplus\tau|=1
. Then we claim it must be the case that
\sigma\subset\tau
or
\tau\subset\sigma
. Indeed, if
\sigma\not\subset\tau
and
\tau\not\subset\sigma
, then
|\sigma\oplus\tau|\geq 2
. In this case,
\sigma^{\prime}=\tau
.

In the case that
|\sigma\oplus\tau|>1
, there are two possibilities. If
\sigma
is a
d
-simplex, then
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma\setminus\{v\}
for any
v\in\sigma\setminus\tau
. If
\sigma
is a
(d-1)
-simplex, then
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma\cup\{v\}
for any
v\in\tau\setminus\sigma
. In both cases, the simplex
\sigma^{\prime}
is a vertex in
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
by the definition of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
, and
|\sigma^{\prime}\oplus\tau|=|\sigma\oplus\tau|-1
.
∎

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
n\geq 2d+1
. The harmonic mean
r
of the min and max degree of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
of the
n
-simplex is bounded below by
d+1
.

Proof.

The degree of a vertex
\sigma\in V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
is either
d+1
and
n-d
if
\sigma
is a
d
or
(d-1)
-simplex respectively, so
d+1
and
n-d
are the maximum and minimum degree of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
. We lower bound the harmonic mean as

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{1}{d+1}+\frac{1}{n-d}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\geq 2\left(\frac{1}{d+1}+\frac{1}{d+1}\right)^{-1}

(as
n\geq 2d+1
)

\displaystyle=d+1

∎

We now have everything we need to lower bound the edge expansion of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
.

Lemma 7.11
.

Let
n\geq 2d+1
. The edge expansion of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is bounded below by
\frac{1}{4}
.

Proof.

We can lower bound the edge expansion of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
by plugging the values calculated in Lemma
7.9
and Lemma
7.10
into the formula given by Lemma
7.8
, namely
EE(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))\geq\frac{r}{2D}=\frac{d+1}{2\cdot(2d+2)}=\frac{1}{4}.

∎

Theorem 7.12
.

Let
n\geq 2d+1
. The treewidth of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is
\Omega(\binom{n}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{d+1})

Proof.

The number of vertices in
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is
|V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))|=\binom{n}{d}+\binom{n}{d+1}
. As
n\geq 2d+1
, the maximum degree of
Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n})
is
d_{\max}=n-d
. We plug these values and the lower bound on
EE(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
into the equation given by Corollary
7.6
to obtain a lower bound on
\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))
.

\displaystyle\tw(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{EE(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))\cdot|V(Hasse_{d}(\Delta^{n}))|}{4\cdot d_{\max}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\binom{n}{d}+\binom{n}{d+1}}{4\cdot 4\cdot(n-d)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\binom{n}{d+1}}{4\cdot 4\cdot(n-d)}

We can derive the equality
\binom{n}{d+1}\cdot\frac{1}{n-d}=\binom{n}{d}\cdot\frac{1}{d+1}
by expanding
\binom{n}{d+1}
, which gives the theorem statement.
∎

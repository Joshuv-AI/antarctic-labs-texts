Synthesis of Stabilizing Recurrent Equilibrium Network Controllers
Neelay Junnarkar, He Yin, Fangda Gu, Murat Arcak, Peter Seiler

Abstract
We propose a parameterization of a nonlinear dynamic controller based on the recurrent equilibrium network, a generalization of the recurrent neural network. We derive constraints on the parameterization under which the controller guarantees exponential stability of a partially observed dynamical system with sector bounded nonlinearities. Finally, we present a method to synthesize this controller using projected policy gradient methods to maximize a reward function with arbitrary structure. The projection step involves the solution of convex optimization problems. We demonstrate the proposed method with simulated examples of controlling a nonlinear inverted pendulum and a plant modeled with neural networks.



I
Introduction

Neural networks (NNs) in control tasks have seen recent success, particularly
through reinforcement learning, which allows for application to control problems
with complex reward functions or unknown dynamics
[
1
]
.
However, many reinforcement learning methods focus on achieving desirable criteria
such as stability by encoding these goals into a reward function which the NN is
trained to maximize.
This does not enforce that a trained controller will guarantee the desired
properties.
In safety critical systems, it is preferable to apply properties such as stability
as a constraint.
Work in
[
2
]
develops methods for provably stable NN controllers
with reinforcement learning methods;
[
3
]
uses recurrent
neural network (RNN) controllers for guaranteed stabilization of partially
observed linear systems. Reference
[
4
]
extends

[
3
]
to uncertain nonlinear systems, and develops convex inner-approximations to the set of stabilizing controller parameters.

Recent work in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
develop an expressive
class of NNs in which the output of a layer is defined implicitly.
The recurrent equilibrium network (REN), a model which leverages these implicit
properties, is presented in
[
7
]
.
References
[
8
]
and
[
9
]

develop REN-based state- and output-feedback controllers with guarantees of stabilizing
linear systems.

We consider the problem of designing a controller that maximizes a reward function with arbitrary structure while guaranteeing stability of a partially-observed system with sector-bounded nonlinearities.
While stability is guaranteed, the reward function can be used to promote other
desirable properties, e.g., by acting as a soft constraint on the control action
magnitude
[
9
]
.
Partially-observed systems are common in practice due to sensing limitations

[
10
]
and estimates of the full state often require historical
output data
[
11
]
.
Sector bounded nonlinearities can describe numerous kinds
of nonlinear functions, e.g., activation functions

[

,
12
,
13
]
, saturation
functions
[

]
, and model uncertainties
[

]

1
Contributions

In this paper we use a model based on the REN to create a class of nonlinear
dynamic controllers.
We derive a Lyapunov condition on the parameters of the REN-based controller under which
the controller guarantees exponential stability of the origin of a partially observed dynamical
system with sector bounded nonlinearities.
Based on the techniques of loop transformation, and change of variables adapted from

[
16
]
, we derive a convex approximation to the set of
stabilizing parameters.
In the case that the plant is a linear system, this convexification procedure is
lossless, i.e. the convex stability condition is equivalent to the non-convex one.
We demonstrate a method to synthesize this controller using gradient-based
reinforcement learning algorithms combined with a convex projection step.
The effectiveness of this controller parameterization is demonstrated on a
nonlinear inverted pendulum model.
We also include an example of stabilizing a plant where the plant model has been learned as an implicit NN.

Compared with
[
3
,
4
]
, this paper extends the
framework from explicit RNN controllers to RENs, enriching the description of
controllers.
Unlike the convex inner approximation presented in
[
4
]
, the convex stability condition presented here for linear plants is also lossless.
Additionally, in contrast to the Youla parameterization method in

[
8
,
9
]
, this paper does not require a “base” output feedback
linear controller, and allows for nonlinear plants, including NN modeled plants.
An example with plants modeled as NNs is included at
https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.00122
.

II-C

Quadratic Constraints for Activation Functions

The stability condition relies on quadratic constraints (QCs)
[
12
,
17
]
to bound the activation function.
The sector bound on the scalar nonlinearities can be expressed as a QC.

Definition 1

Let
\alpha\leq\beta
be given.
The function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
lies in the sector
[\alpha,\beta]
if:

\displaystyle(\varphi(\nu)-\alpha\nu)\cdot(\beta\nu-\varphi(\nu))\geq 0\,\,\,\forall\nu\in\mathbb{R}.

(6)

The interpretation of the sector
[\alpha,\beta]
is that
\varphi
lies between lines passing through the origin with slope
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Many activations are sector bounded: leaky ReLU is sector bounded in
[a,1]
with its parameter
a\in(0,1)
; ReLU and
\tanh
are sector bounded in
[0,1]
(denoted as
\tanh\in
sector
[0,1]
).
Fig.
2
illustrates the sector bound on
\tanh
.

Fig. 2
:
\tanh\in
sector
[0,1]
.

Sector constraints can also be defined for combined activations
\phi
.
From Assumption
3
, the
i
-th scalar activation function
\varphi_{i}
is sector bounded in
[\alpha_{\phi,i},\beta_{\phi,i}]
.
These sectors can be stacked into vectors

, where
\alpha_{\phi}=[\alpha_{\phi,1},...,\alpha_{\phi,n_{\phi}}]

Lemma 1

Let

, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}-2A_{\phi}B_{\phi}\Lambda&(A_{\phi}+B_{\phi})\Lambda\\
(A_{\phi}+B_{\phi})\Lambda&-2\Lambda\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}v\\
w\end{bmatrix}\geq 0,

(7)

where

, and

V
Conclusion

In this paper we presented a parameterization of dynamic controllers with sector bounded nonlinearities to guarantee exponential stability of the origin for partially-observed plants with sector bounded nonlinearities.
This set of plants includes plants modeled by neural networks.
We demonstrated a controller synthesis method in a reinforcement learning context where an arbitrary gradient-based training algorithm can be used with the addition of a projection step that guarantees stability, both during training and of the trained controller.
The benefits of the expressiveness gained by this model and convexification method are demonstrated on a nonlinear inverted pendulum model.

Homological properties of extensions of algebras
Kostiantyn Iusenko, John William MacQuarrie

Abstract
We consider a class of extensions of associative algebras, which we refer to as “strongly proj-bounded extensions”. We prove that the finiteness of the left global dimension and the support of the Hochschild homology is preserved by strongly proj-bounded extensions, generalizing results of Cibils, Lanzillota, Marcos and Solotar. Moreover, we show that the finiteness of the big left finitistic dimension is preserved by strongly proj-bounded extensions. In order to construct examples, we describe a new class of extensions of algebras of finite relative global dimension, which may be of independent interest. The results apply both for abstract (meaning no topology) and pseudocompact algebras. Keywords: Extensions of algebras, Hochschild homology, relative homology, pseudocompact algebras, Han’s conjecture, finitistic dimension conjecture.

Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field. In the series of recent papers (see
[
7
,
8
,
10
,
11
]
and references therein) Cibils, Lanzilotta, Marcos and Solotar consider what they call “bounded” extensions of associative
k
-algebras: that is, extensions of algebras
B\subseteq A
such that
A/B
has finite projective dimension as a
B
-bimodule, such that
A/B
is projective as either a left or a right
B
-module, and such that some tensor power
(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}
is
0
.
The authors compare the homological properties of the algebras in such an extension: namely, they show that the left global dimension of
B
is finite if, and only if, the left global dimension of
A
is finite, and that the Hochschild homology groups of
B
vanish in high enough degree if, and only if the Hochschild homology groups of
A
vanish in high enough degree. In particular, when
B\subseteq A
is a bounded extension of finite dimensional algebras, then Han’s conjecture (see
[
26
]
) holds for
B
if, and only if, it holds for
A
.

We generalize bounded extensions as follows. Say that an extension of
k
-algebras
B\subseteq A
is
strongly proj-bounded
if:

1.

A/B
has finite projective dimension as a
B
-bimodule;

2.

A/B
is projective as either a left or a right
B
-module;

3.

There is
p\geqslant 1
such that
A/B^{\otimes_{B}n}
is projective as a

B
-bimodule
for any
n\geqslant p
;

4.

The
(A^{e},B^{e})
-projective dimension of
A
is finite.

We have two main goals. Firstly, we prove that such extensions satisfy the same homological preservation properties as cited above; secondly, we prove that
B
has finite finitistic dimension if, and only if,
A
does (Theorem
6.16
and Theorem
6.10
). As example corollary, one obtains
[
23
, Theorem A]
as a consequence of Theorem
6.10
. The extra generality of the extensions we consider over bounded extensions has two immediate advantages: firstly, it allows the extension
B\leqslant A
to be “ large”, in the sense that the tensor algebra
T[B,A/B]
can have arbitrarily long non-zero words
v_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes v_{s}
(
s\in\mathbb{N},v_{i}\in A/B
); secondly, even for finite dimensional algebras, it allows extensions for which
A
has more primitive idempotents than
B
, and hence can assist in reductions of Han’s Conjecture and the Finitistic Dimension Conjecture.

We prove that all the results above are true in the category of pseudocompact algebras. Pseudocompact algebras, a class of topological algebra, are among the most natural generalization of finite dimensional algebras, inheriting almost all the powerful formal properties of finite dimensional algebras. They are dual to coalgebras and hence our results have dual versions for coalgebras. That said, pseudocompact algebras have both informal (eg. multiplication is more tractable than comultiplication) and formal (eg. Pontryagin duality) advantages over coalgebras. In nature, pseudocompact algebras and their modules are the objects one should study to understand the representation theory of profinite groups.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section

2
we recall the basic notions of pseudocompact algebras and their pseudocompact modules. In Section
3
we collect the basic properties of relative projective modules and corresponding resolutions as in
[
27
]
; we also develop a general construction of non-trivial extensions of algebras
B\subseteq A
with finite relative global dimension (Section
3.2
) that will be used in Section
4
to construct examples of finite dimensional strongly proj-bounded extensions. We believe that Corollary
3.3
of Theorem
3.2
is of independent interest.
In Section
3.3
we define Hochschild homology for pseudocompact algebras and develop the notion of relative projective modules. Section
4
is devoted to proj-bounded extensions, giving several examples. A key ingredient of the cited work of Cibils, Lanzilotta, Marcos and Solotar
is the so-called Jacobi-Zariski long exact sequence of a bounded extension, which relates the Hochschild homology of
A
, the Hochschild homology of
B
, and the relative Hochschild homology of the extension (see also
[
30
,
31
]
). In Section
5
we give a
Jacobi-Zariski long exact sequence for extensions satisfying Conditions 1 to 3 of a strongly proj-bounded extension (extensions we call simply “proj-bounded”), in both the abstract and pseudocompact cases. Finally, in Section
6
we prove that the finitude of the left global dimension, left big finitistic dimension, and support of the Hochschild homology, are preserved by strongly proj-bounded extensions. The supporting results in Section
6
are of quite a general nature and we expect them to have further use.

Pseudocompact algebras

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field (thought of as a discrete topological ring). A pseudocompact
k
-algebra is an inverse limit of finite dimensional associative
k
-algebras, taken in the category of topological algebras – see for instance
[
4
,
28
]
for an introduction to pseudocompact objects. Morphisms in the category of pseudocompact algebras are continuous algebra homomorphisms. Pseudocompact algebras arise in several natural contexts: completed group algebras of profinite groups, the objects of study in the representation theory of profinite groups, are pseudocompact (cf.
[
4
]
); the algebras dual to coalgebras are precisely the pseudocompact algebras (see for instance
[
41
, Theorem 3.6]
).

Throughout the text, in order to avoid confusion with topological objects, we use the word “abstract” to refer to objects without a topology: thus “abstract algebra” just means “algebra”. Our main results apply to extensions of abstract algebras, as well as to extensions of pseudocompact algebras. In this article, the main use of pseudocompact algebras will be for the construction of examples: the completed path algebra of a quiver (defined below) is a pseudocompact algebra. Working with the abstract path algebra
kQ
of a quiver (even a finite quiver, if it has cycles) is notoriously tricky. But working with the completed path algebra
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
, many technical difficulties do not arise (
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is unital, projective
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
-modules are what one would hope, the Jacobson radical of
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is what one would hope, etc.). The reader whose interest is in finite dimensional or abstract algebras will miss nothing by skipping mentions to pseudocompact algebras.

Definition 2.1
.

Let

be a finite quiver. The

(cf. for instance
[

k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]:=\prodop\displaylimits_{n=0}^{\infty}kQ_{n},

k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
.

Now let

be an arbitrary quiver. We may consider

as the union (that is, direct limit) of its finite subquivers

. The operation “completed path algebra of a finite quiver”
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{-}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
is a contravariant functor (see also
[

]
) to the category of pseudocompact algebras (if

is an inclusion of finite quivers,
k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{\iota}]\hskip-0.86108pt]
sends a path of the larger quiver to itself if it is contained in the smaller, and to

k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q}]\hskip-0.86108pt]:={\underleftarrow{\,\textnormal{lim}}\,\,_{i}}k[\hskip-0.86108pt[{Q_{i}}]\hskip-0.86108pt].

The category of pseudocompact algebras is closed under taking inverse limits, so

is pseudocompact.

A general reference for the majority of the claims in the following paragraphs is
[
4
]
. Let

be a pseudocompact algebra. A

Given a pseudocompact algebra

A free pseudocompact

The category of pseudocompact

Finally, given a pseudocompact algebra

Relative homological algebra

3.1
Relative projective modules, resolutions and relative global dimension

In order to describe relative homological algebra (cf.
[
27
]
), we first recall the notion of relatively projective modules.
Given an extension
B\subseteq A
of algebras, an
A
-module

is called
relatively

-projective
, or
(A,B)
-

if it satisfies either of the following equivalent conditions:

1.

M
is isomorphic to a direct summand of the induced module
A\otimes_{B}V
, for
V
some left
B
-module;

2.

if ever an
A
-module homomorphism onto

splits as a
B
-module homomorphism, then it splits as an
A
-module homomorphism.

For a proof of the equivalence of these conditions, and several other characterizations of relatively projective modules, see for instance
[
27
, Section 1]
and
[
43
, Section 1]
. In the special case where
B=k
is the unique subalgebra of

of dimension

(A,B)
-projective modules coincides with the notion of projective modules.
At the other end of the spectrum, every
A
-module is
(A,A)
-projective. A projective

-module

is always
(A,B)
-projective, and every
A
-module of the form
A\otimes_{B}N
with

B
-module, is
(A,B)
-projective.
An exact sequence of
A
-module homomorphisms

\cdots\to M_{n+1}\xrightarrow{f_{n+1}}M_{n}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}M_{n-1}\to 0

(some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
) is called
(A,B)
-
exact
if, for each
i\geqslant n
, the kernel of
f_{i}
is a direct
B
-module summand of
M_{i}
(cf.
[
27
, Section 1]
). One may check that a sequence of morphisms

\{f_{i}\,|\,i\geqslant n\}
is

(A,B)
-exact if, and only if,

1.

f_{i}\circ f_{i+1}=0
for all
i\geqslant n
,

2.

there exists a
contracting
B
-homotopy
: that is, a sequence of
B
-module homomorphisms
h_{i}:M_{i}\rightarrow M_{i+1}
(

) such that
f_{i+1}h_{i}+h_{i-1}f_{i}
is the identity map on
M_{i}
.

One may now develop the concepts of relative projective dimension and relative global dimension.
Given an
A
-module

, we define the

to be the minimal number

, denoted by
\textnormal{pd}_{(A,B)}M
, such that there is an
(A,B)
-exact sequence

0\rightarrow P_{n}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{1}\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow M\rightarrow 0.

with the

(A,B)
-projective. If such an exact sequence does not exist, the relative projective dimension of

This definition is equivalent to the corresponding definition from
[

, Section 2]
. The
left relative global dimension

\textnormal{gldim}(A,B)
of the extension

is the supremum of the relative projective dimension of the

-modules, if this number exists, and infinity otherwise (“left” because our modules are left modules).

Having relative projective resolutions, the relative derived functors
\textnormal{Tor}^{(A,B)}_{n}
and
\textnormal{Ext}_{(A,B)}^{n}
can be defined, and we refer to
[
27
,
5
]
for the details. Recall that given an

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A,M)=\mathrm{Tor}_{*}^{A^{e}}(M,A).

In particular, when

These spaces were originally defined in
[

]
– see
[

, Section 2]

We present a useful relative analogue of a well-known result about projective modules:

Let

.

Proof.

It is clear that if the kernel of

Assume now that

Now, by the relative version of Schanuel’s Lemma

It follows that the induction hypothesis applies to the sequence

of length

are (all but the last of) the maps of the

\displaystyle t_{0}:P_{0}\oplus\mathrm{Ker}\left(F_{0}\rightarrow M\right)

\displaystyle\to P_{0}\oplus F_{1}

\displaystyle(x,y)

\displaystyle\mapsto(x,s_{0}(y)),

\displaystyle t_{1}:P_{0}\oplus F_{1}

\displaystyle\to F_{2}

\displaystyle(x,z)

\displaystyle\mapsto s_{1}(z),

and

.
It follows from the induction hypothesis that

3.2
Extensions of finite relative global dimension

When we define strongly proj-bounded extensions

Remark 3.8

The principal obstruction to the construction of deeper examples of proj-bounded extensions seems to arise from the surprisingly small amount of literature on the subject of relative homological algebra for associative algebras. By contrast, an enormous amount of work has been done on relative homological algebra for finite groups – such results are hugely influencial in the modular representation theory and block theory of finite groups (see for instance

3.3
Relative homological algebra over pseudocompact algebras

3.3.1
Hochschild homology for pseudocompact algebras

Hochschild cohomology for coalgebras was introduced by Doi in
[
18
]
. Using the standard duality between coalgebras and pseudocompact algebras
(see, for instance,
[
41
, Theorem 3.6]
) one can thus define Hochschild homology for pseudocompact algebras by dualizing these definitions. We choose to work directly with pseudocompact algebras, as it is not any harder. Given a pseudocompact algebra
A
and pseudocompact bimodule
M
(which recall from Section
2
is the same thing as a pseudocompact
A^{e}=A\widehat{\otimes}A^{\rm op}
-module) we define Hochschild homology with coefficients in
M
as

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A,M)=\mathrm{Tor}_{*}^{A^{e}}(M,A).

The Hochschild homology of
A
is defined as

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A):=\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A,A)=\mathrm{Tor}_{*}^{A^{e}}(A,A).

As with abstract algebras, the Hochschild homology of
A
can be computed using the so-called standard Hochschild resolution: for a pseudocompact algebra
A
consider the positively graded
A
-bimodule
C_{*}^{\prime}(A)
defined for
q\in\{1,2,\ldots\}
by

C_{q}^{\prime}(A)=A\widehat{\otimes}A^{\widehat{\otimes}q}\widehat{\otimes}A.

For
q=0
set
C_{0}^{\prime}(A)=A\widehat{\otimes}A.
The vector space
C_{q}^{\widehat{\otimes}}(A)
is a pseudocompact
A
-bimodule for each
q
. Now define
d:C_{q}^{\prime}(A)\rightarrow C_{q-1}^{\prime}(A)
on pure tensors by

d_{i}\left(a_{0}\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}\widehat{\otimes}a_{q+1}\right)=\sumop\displaylimits_{i=0}^{q}(-1)^{i}a_{0}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{i}a_{i+1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q+1}.

The maps
d:C_{q}^{\prime}(A)\rightarrow C_{q-1}^{\prime}(A)
are continuous morphisms of
A
-bimodules satisfying
d^{2}=0
. Define also
s
:

C_{q}^{\prime}(A)\rightarrow C_{q+1}^{\prime}(A)
by

s\left(a_{0}\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}\widehat{\otimes}a_{q+1}\right)=1\widehat{\otimes}a_{0}\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}\widehat{\otimes}a_{q+1}.

One checks that
ds+sd=\mathrm{id}
, and hence the complex
\left(C_{*}^{\prime}(A),b^{\prime}\right)
is a resolution of
A
by free
A
-bimodules.

By definition of the Tor groups,
H_{*}(A,M)
are the homology groups of the chain complex

\left(M\widehat{\otimes}_{A\widehat{\otimes}A^{\text{op }}}C_{*}^{\prime}(A),\text{ id }\widehat{\otimes}d\right).

One can simplify these complexes using the isomorphism

\varphi:M\widehat{\otimes}_{A\widehat{\otimes}A^{\text{op}}}C_{q}^{\prime}(A)\rightarrow C_{q}(A,M):=M\widehat{\otimes}A^{\widehat{\otimes}q}

defined by
\varphi\left(m\widehat{\otimes}a_{0}\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}\widehat{\otimes}a_{q+1}\right)=a_{q+1}ma_{0}\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}
. Passing the
differential
d
through these isomorphisms, we obtain the differential
b:C_{q}(A,M)\rightarrow C_{q-1}(A,M)
given by

\displaystyle b\left(m\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}\right)=

\displaystyle ma_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}

\displaystyle+\sumop\displaylimits_{i=1}^{q-1}(-1)^{i}m\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{i}a_{i+1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots\widehat{\otimes}a_{q}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{q}a_{q}m\widehat{\otimes}a_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\cdots a_{q-1}.

The following proposition shows that presenting any pseudocompact algebra
A
as an inverse limit of finite dimensional algebras
A=\underleftarrow{\,\textnormal{lim}}\,\,A_{i}
, its Hochschild homology can be calculated via the usual Hochschild homology of the algebras
A_{i}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Suppose that
A
is a pseudocompact algebra. The graded vector space
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A)
can be calculated as

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A)=\underleftarrow{\,\textnormal{lim}}\,\,_{I}\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A/I),

(3.1)

where
I
runs through the open ideals in
A
.

Proof.

This result follows from a sequence of checks so we just give a sketch. First observe that a morphism of algebras
\rho:A_{j}\to A_{i}
induces a morphism of complexes
C_{*}^{\prime}(A_{j})\to C_{*}^{\prime}(A_{i})
, and hence a map on their homologies. It follows that presenting
A
as the inverse limit of the inverse system
\{A/I,\rho_{IJ}:A/J\to A/I\}
, we obtain an inverse system of graded vector spaces
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A/I)
.

The argument used to prove
[
40
, Proposition 6.5.7]
shows that the inverse limit of this inverse system is
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A)
.
∎

It is important to observe that the maps of the above inverse system of
\textnormal{HH}_{*}(A/I)
are not necessarily surjective. Indeed,
\mathrm{HH}_{n}(A)
can be zero even if each
\mathrm{HH}_{n}(A_{i})
is non-zero. For instance, taking
A=k[[x]]
we have that
k[[x]]=\underleftarrow{\,\textnormal{lim}}\,\,k[x]/x^{i}
. For each
i
the homology of
A_{i}=k[x]/x^{i}
is non-zero in every positive degree, while
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(k[[x]])
is concentrated in degrees
0
and
1
, as the following example shows.

Example 3.10
.

Consider
A=k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]]
. One can mimic the arguments from
[
44
, Exercise 9.1.3]
to calculate
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A)
.
We obtain

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]])\cong k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]]\,\widehat{\otimes}\,\Lambda^{*}(k^{n}),

where
\Lambda^{*}(k^{n})
is the exterior algebra of the vector space
k^{n}
. In particular,
\mathrm{HH}_{*}(k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]])
is non-zero in degrees
0,\ldots,n
and zero otherwise.

For finite dimensional algebras, a result of Keller
[
32
, Theorem 2.2]
says that if the global dimension of a finite dimensional algebra
A
is finite, then
\textnormal{HH}_{*}(A)
is supported in degree
0
. These examples show that this is not the case for pseudocompact algebras, as the power series ring
k[[x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}]]
has global dimension

by
[
1
, Theorem 1.12]
.

We modify the content of Section

Lemma 3.11

Let

be a pseudocompact

-module. The following are equivalent:

1.

Proof.

That 2 implies 1 is easy: the multiplication map

clearly splits as a

To prove that 1 implies 2, our situation is as follows

\textstyle{A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}V\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{U\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{M\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\iota}
\scriptstyle{\gamma}

where solid arrows are continuous

-module homomorphisms, and

. We are looking for a continuous

-module homomorphism

such that

. With abstract modules, the calculation can be done with elements, but with the completed tensor product it is easier to work formally. Denote by
\varepsilon,\eta

\delta=\varepsilon_{U}(1\widehat{\otimes}\gamma\pi\eta_{V})
is an

and we claim that

is the required splitting of

\displaystyle f\gamma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=f\varepsilon_{U}(1\widehat{\otimes}\gamma\pi\eta_{V})\iota

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{M}(1\widehat{\otimes}\pi\eta_{V})\iota

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{M}(1\widehat{\otimes}\pi)(1\widehat{\otimes}\eta_{V})\iota

\displaystyle=\pi\varepsilon_{A\widehat{\otimes}_{B}V}(1\widehat{\otimes}\eta_{V})\iota

\displaystyle=\pi\iota

\displaystyle=\textnormal{id}_{M}.

∎

We say that

Now just as with abstract modules one can construct

-projective resolutions of pseudocompact modules and define relative

B

is

\mathrm{HH}_{*}(A\mid B,M)=\mathrm{Tor}_{*}^{(A^{e},B^{e})}(M,A).

As in abstract case, the above definition is equivalent to the one using
(A^{e},B\widehat{\otimes}A^{op})
-projective resolutions.

4
Proj-bounded extensions

4.1
Definitions and first examples

We define the extensions of interest to us and provide several examples.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that the extension of
k
-algebras
B\subseteq A
is
proj-bounded
if it satisfies the following three conditions:

1.

A/B
is of finite projective dimension as a
B^{e}
-module

2.

A/B
is projective as either a left or a right
B
-module.

3.

There exists a natural number

(called the
index of projectivity
) such that the tensor power
A/B^{\otimes_{B}n}
is projective as a
B^{e}
-module, for any
n\geqslant p
.

We say that the extension is
strongly proj-bounded
if it satisfies the additional condition that

4.

A
has finite
(A^{e},B^{e})
-projective dimension.

The definitions of proj-bounded and strongly proj-bounded extensions of pseudocompact algebras are the same: one must only replace abstract tensor products
\otimes_{k},\otimes_{B}
with completed tensor products
\widehat{\otimes}_{k},\widehat{\otimes}_{B}
throughout.

Example 4.2
.

A bounded extension of algebras is clearly proj-bounded, since if
A/B^{\otimes_{B}p}=0
, then
A/B^{\otimes_{B}n}=0
is projective for any

. A bounded extension is in fact strongly proj-bounded: this follows from the
(A^{e},B^{e})
-projective resolution

[
8
, Theorem 2.3]
of

, whose length is at most

when

.

Example 4.3
.

The motivating example for the development of bounded extensions comes from

]

(

-bimodule, while Condition 4 of Definition

is satisfied because the finite sequence (2.1) from

remains a relative projective resolution for

-module.

Example 4.4

The smallest example of an extension that is proj-bounded but not strongly proj bounded is

Assume throughout this section that

to a

-module. We will prove the case of abstract extensions of algebras, but throughout, identical results (with almost identical proofs) apply for extensions of pseudocompact algebras. See also
[

]
for recent and related homological results for a different generalization of bounded extensions of algebras.

6.1

Certain arguments in this section are similar to arguments from

Lemma 6.1
.

If

Proof.

By hypothesis

Let

is projective as a left

are projective as

-modules. Hence the sequence obtained is a projective resolution for

of length

If

If

Recall that by hypothesis

B
-bimodule:

0\to P_{n}\to\cdots\to P_{1}\to P_{0}\to A/B\to 0.

Since
A/B
is projective as a right
B
-module, the sequence

0\to P_{n}\otimes_{B}X\to\cdots\to P_{1}\otimes_{B}X\to P_{0}\otimes_{B}X\to(A/B)\otimes_{B}X\to 0

is exact.
But the modules
P_{i}\otimes_{B}X
are projective as left
B
-modules by Lemma
6.3
, and hence the projective dimension of
(A/B)\otimes_{B}X
is at most
n
.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

If the
A
-module
X
has finite projective dimension, then the module
{}_{B}X
has projective dimension not more than
\textnormal{pd}_{A}X+\textnormal{pd}_{B}A
.

Proof.

Let
P_{*}\to X\to 0
be a projective resolution of
X
of length
\textnormal{pd}_{A}X
. Restrict this sequence to
B
, hence treating it as an exact sequence of
B
-modules. By Lemma
6.1
, each module
{}_{B}(P_{i})
has projective dimension bounded above by
\textnormal{pd}_{B}A
. It follows that the projective dimension of
{}_{B}X
is bounded above by
\textnormal{pd}_{A}X+\textnormal{pd}_{B}A
.
∎

The next two propositions do not require the full strength of strongly proj-bounded extensions, see Remark
6.14
.

Proposition 6.6
.

If
A
has finite left finitistic dimension, then so does
B
.

Proof.

Denote by
d_{A}
the finitistic dimension of
A
. Let
Y
be a left
B
-module with finite projective dimension. Since
A/B
is projective as a right
B
-module, the sequence

0\to B\to A\to A/B\to 0

of right
B
-module homomorphisms is split, and hence

0\to B\otimes_{B}Y\to A\otimes_{B}Y\to(A/B)\otimes_{B}Y\to 0

is exact. We will check that the projective dimension of the two terms on the right is limited above by a number independent of
Y
, and hence so is
Y\cong B\otimes_{B}Y
.

Since
Y
has finite projective dimension, so does
A\otimes_{B}Y
by Lemma
6.2
. Hence
A\otimes_{B}Y
has a projective resolution
P_{*}\to A\otimes_{B}Y\to 0
of length not more than
d_{A}
. By Lemma
6.5
,
{}_{B}(A\otimes_{B}Y)
has projective dimension not more that
\textnormal{pd}_{B}A+d_{A}
. The final term
(A/B)\otimes_{B}Y
has projective dimension not more than the projective dimension of
A/B
as a
B
-bimodule by Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

If
A
has finite left global dimension, then so does
B
.

Proof.

The proof is essentially identical to that of Proposition
6.6
: denote by
d_{A}
the left global dimension of
A
and let
Y
be a
B
-module. Considering the same short exact sequence,
{}_{B}(A\otimes_{B}Y)
has projective dimension not more than
\textnormal{pd}_{B}(A)+d_{A}
by Lemma
6.5
, and
(A/B)\otimes_{B}Y
has projective dimension not more than the projective dimension of
A/B
as a
B
-bimodule by Lemma
6.4
.
∎

Proposition 6.8
.

If
B
has finite left finitistic dimension, then so does
A
.

Proof.

Let
X
be an
A
-module with finite projective dimension. Suppose that
(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}
is projective as a
B
-bimodule and that
A
has relative projective dimension less than
p
. The kernel
K
of the map

A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}{p}}\otimes_{B}A\to A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}{p-1}}\otimes_{B}A

in the relative projective resolution of
A
given in
[
11
, Proposition 2.1]
is a direct summand of
A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}{p}}\otimes_{B}A
as an
A
-bimodule by Lemma
3.1
.

We have an exact sequence

0\to K\to A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}\otimes_{B}A\to\ldots\to A\otimes_{B}A\to A\to 0

which remains exact when we apply
-\otimes_{A}X
because the contracting homotopies are right
A
-module maps. So we get an exact sequence

0\to K\otimes_{A}X\to A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}\otimes_{B}X\to\ldots\to A\otimes_{B}X\to X\to 0.

If
X
has finite projective dimension as an
A
-module then it has finite projective dimension as a
B
-module by Lemma
6.5
, and hence
{}_{B}X
has projective dimension limited above by the finitistic dimension of
B
.

Note that each module in this sequence except
X
and
K\otimes_{A}X
is of the form
A\otimes_{B}Y
for some
B
-module
Y
of finite projective dimension, by Lemma
6.4
(for the module
A\otimes_{B}X
we use that
{}_{B}X
is of finite projective dimension). Note also that the projective dimension of each
Y
is limited above by
\textnormal{Findim}(B)
. From Lemma
6.2
it follows that every module in the sequence except perhaps
X
and
K\otimes_{A}X
has projective dimension limited above by
\textnormal{Findim}(B)
. It remains to check that the same is true for
K\otimes_{B}X
. But
K
is a direct summand of
A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}\otimes_{B}A
and hence
K\otimes_{A}X
is a direct summand of
(A\otimes_{B}(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}\otimes_{B}A)\otimes_{A}X
. So
K\otimes_{A}X
also has projective dimension at most
\textnormal{Findim}(B)
by the same argument.

Putting all this together,
X
has a resolution of length limited above by the value such that
(A/B)^{\otimes_{B}p}
is projective as a
B
-bimodule, and the relative projective dimension of
A
. Each module in the resolution has projective dimension limited above by the finitistic dimension of
B
. In particular, the projective dimension of
X
is limited independent of
X
, as required.

∎

Proposition 6.9
.

If
B
has finite left global dimension, then so does
A
.

Proof.

This can be proved exactly as Proposition
6.8
, by swapping the left finitistic dimension of
B
with the left global dimension of
B
throughout. The only difference is that several claims proved in Proposition
6.8
are trivial when
B
has finite left global dimension.
∎

Theorem 6.10
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a strongly proj-bounded extension of algebras.

1.

B
has finite left global dimension if, and only if,
A
does.

2.

\textnormal{Findim}(B)
is finite if, and only if,
\textnormal{Findim}(A)
is finite.

Proof.

Part 1 is Propositions
6.7
,
6.9
and Part 2 is Propositions
6.6
,
6.8
.
∎

Theorem 6.11
.

If
A
is finite dimensional, then
\textnormal{findim}(B)
is finite if, and only if,
\textnormal{findim}(A)
is finite.

Proof.

If
A
is finite dimensional, then the restriction of a finitely generated module to
B
is finitely generated. Thus all the modules appearing in the proofs of Propositions
6.6
,
6.8
may be assumed to be finitely generated.
∎

Remark 6.12
.

In
[
48
]
and in

[
25
]
(see also
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
) the authors study the finitistic dimension conjecture for extensions of Artin algebras. In particular, a new formulation of the finitistic dimension conjecture in terms
of relative homological dimension is given.

Remark 6.13
.

Reduction techniques to attack the finitistic dimension conjecture already appear in the literature. For instance, in
[
23
]
, viewing a finite dimensional algebra as a quotient of a path algebra, the authors present two operations on the quiver of the algebra, namely arrow removal and vertex removal, and show that these operations preserve the finitude of the finitistic dimension. That arrow removal preserves
findim

[
23
, Theorem A]
follows from Theorem
6.11
, as one may observe that the corresponding extension of algebras is strongly proj-bounded.

Remark 6.14
.

The full strength of strongly proj-bounded extensions was used to show that when
B
has finite finitistic dimension, then so does
A
. Analysing the proof of Proposition
6.6
one observes that less is required to show the other implication, and indeed it was recently shown that less still is required, at least for finite dimensional algebras
[
35
]
.

If
A
is a pseudocompact algebra, the (big) left finitistic dimension of
A
is defined in the obvious way: as the supremum of the projective dimensions of those pseudocompact left
A
-modules having finite projective dimension.

Theorem 6.15
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a strongly proj-bounded extension of pseudocompact algebras.

1.

B
has finite global dimension if, and only if,
A
does.

2.

B
has finite left finitistic dimension if, and only if,
A
does.

Proof.

The arguments of this section go through making only superficial changes (tensors are completed tensors, free modules are products rather than sums, etc.). The left and right global dimensions of a pseudocompact algebra coincide by an observation of Brumer
[
4
, p. 449]
.
∎

6.2
Preservation of vanishing of
HH
and Han’s Conjecture

Theorem 6.16
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a strongly proj-bounded extension. Then
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(A)
vanishes for large enough
m
if, and only if,
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B)
vanishes for large enough
m
.

Proof.

We have
\mathrm{HH}_{r}(A|B,A)=0
for any
r
bigger than
\mathrm{pd}_{(A^{e},B^{e})}A
, which is finite by hypothesis, and so Theorem
5.1
implies that
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B,A)\cong\mathrm{HH}_{m}(A)
for sufficiently large
m
. On the other hand, applying the functor
-\otimes_{B^{e}}B
to the sequence

0\to B\to A\to A/B\to 0,

one obtains the long exact sequence

\cdots\to\mathrm{HH}_{m+1}(B,A/B)\to\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B)\to\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B,A)\to\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B,A/B)\to\cdots,

and hence
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B)\cong\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B,A)
for any
m
greater than
\mathrm{pd}_{B^{e}}A/B
. Putting this together,

\mathrm{HH}_{m}(A)\cong\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B)

for large enough
m
and in particular, one is
0
if, and only if, the other is.
∎

Theorem 6.17
.

Let
B\subseteq A
be a strongly proj-bounded extension of pseudocompact algebras. Then
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(A)
vanishes for large enough
m
if, and only if,
\mathrm{HH}_{m}(B)
vanishes for large enough
m
.

Proof.

The proof is just as for Theorem
6.16
, using Theorem
5.2
instead of Theorem
5.1
.
∎

Recall that Han’s conjecture
[
26
, Conjecture 1]
for finite dimensional algebras asserts that if
A
is a finite dimensional algebra, then
\textnormal{HH}_{m}(A)
vanishes for sufficiently large
m
if, and only if,
A
has finite global dimension. The following result generalizes
[
8
, Theorem 4.6]
.

Corollary 6.18
.

If
B\subseteq A
is a strongly proj-bounded extension of finite dimensional algebras, then Han’s conjecture holds for
A
if, and only if, it holds for
B
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Theorems
6.10
and
6.16
.
∎

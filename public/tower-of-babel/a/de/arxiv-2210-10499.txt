Extensions of two-field mimetic gravity
Yunlong Zheng, and Haomin Rao

Abstract
Two-field mimetic gravity was recently realized by looking at the singular limit of the conformal transformation between the auxiliary metric and the physical metric with two scalar fields involved. In this paper, we reanalyze the singular conformal limit and find a more general solution for the conformal factor A A , which greatly broadens the form of two-field mimetic constraint and thus extends the two-field mimetic gravity. We find the general setup still mimics the role of dark matter at the cosmological background level. Moreover, we extend the action by introducing extra possible term for phenomenological interests. Surprisingly, some special cases are found to be equivalent to general relativity, k-essence theory and Galileon theory. Finally, we further extend the theory by allowing the expression of mimetic constraint to be arbitrary without imposed condition, and show that the dark matter-like behavior is unaffected even in this extension.

Introduction

Theories of modified gravity

have attracted considerable attention in the last few years as possible solutions for the dark energy, dark mater, the singularity problem
Novello:2008ra
;
Lehners:2008vx
;
Cai:2014bea
;
Cai:2012va
and so on. Mimetic scenario was first proposed by Chamseddine and Mukhanov
Chamseddine:2013kea
as a modification of Einstein’s general relativity where the scalar field play the role of dark matter. The idea is to express the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
in the Einstein-Hilbert action in terms of an auxiliary metric
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
and a scalar field
\phi
, as follows

g_{\mu\nu}=\left(\tilde{g}^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{\alpha}\phi_{\beta}\right)\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}~,

(1)

in which
\phi_{\alpha}=\nabla_{\alpha}\phi
is the covariant derivative of the scalar field with respect to spacetime, so that the physical metric is invariant under Weyl rescalings of
\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}
. The above relation then leads to a constraint equation

g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}=1~.

(2)

The gravitational equations by varying the Einstein-Hilbert action, which is constructed from the physical metric
g_{\mu\nu}
, contains an extra scalar mode which can mimic the cold dark matter.
Alternatively, one can impose the above constraint by employing a Lagrange multiplier
Golovnev:2013jxa
in the action. The mimetic gravity written in the Lagrange multiplier formalism takes the following form

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}-1\right)+\mathcal{L}_{m}\right]~,

(3)

where we have used the unit reduced Planck mass
M_{p}^{2}=1/(8\pi G)=1
and the most negative signature for the metric.

For phenomenological applications, the mimetic model was generalized in Ref.
Chamseddine:2014vna
;
Lim:2010yk

Although the original mimetic gravity is free of pathologies, it is shown that the scalar perturbation is non-propagating due to the mimetic constraint even being offered a potential, thus can’t be quantized in a usual way. Besides, this may also lead to caustic singularities. To remedy these issues, higher derivative terms of the mimetic field
(\Box\phi)^{2}
are introduced
Chamseddine:2014vna
to promote the scalar mode to be propagating. The equation of motion for the scalar perturbation indeed has the wave-like form by choosing appropriate coefficient. However, the detailed analysis in the action formalism indicates that the mimetic model with higher derivatives always suffer from ghost or gradient instability at the level of linear perturbations

;

;

Another interesting aspect of mimetic gravity is its close relation with non-invertible disformal transformation. It is pointed out in Refs.
Deruelle:2014zza
;
Arroja:2015wpa
;
Domenech:2015tca
that mimetic gravity can be realized through a non-invertible disformal transformation

where the number of degrees of freedom (DOFs) is no longer preserved between the two frames

and the additional DOF plays the role of dark matter. In this regard, the two-field extension of the mimetic scenario
Firouzjahi:2018xob
;
Shen:2019nyp

was recently proposed by looking at the singular limit in the special case of conformal transformation

g_{\mu\nu}=A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}~,

(4)

where
\tilde{X}\equiv\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu},~\tilde{Y}\equiv\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu},~\tilde{Z}\equiv\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}

\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
, and
\tilde{Z}
.

Firouzjahi:2018xob

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}A_{,\tilde{X}}+\tilde{Y}A_{,\tilde{Y}}+\tilde{Z}A_{,\tilde{Z}}~,

(5)

where a comma denotes a partial derivative with respect to the argument. Then the authors of
Firouzjahi:2018xob
claimed that the nontrivial solution for

A=\alpha\tilde{X}+\beta\tilde{Y}+2\gamma\tilde{Z}~,

where
\alpha
and

\phi
and
\psi

Mansoori:2021fjd
with a curved field space manifold.

In this paper, we will reanalyze the singular limit of conformal transformation with two scalar fields, and then extend the two-field mimetic theory. This paper is organized as follows. In section

A

5

6

2
Singular conformal limit and two-field mimetic constraint

As pointed out by Refs.
Deruelle:2014zza
;
Arroja:2015wpa
, one can find the original single field mimetic gravity by performing a singular conformal transformation to the pure Einstein-Hilbert action. Similarly, two-field extension of mimetic gravity can be found by looking at the singular limit of the conformal transformation (
4
) which involves two scalar fields
Firouzjahi:2018xob
. Requiring the eigenvalue for the Jacobian of the transformation
\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial\tilde{g}_{\alpha\beta}}
to be zero, the condition on
A
in the singular limit (
4
) is obtained as

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}A_{,\tilde{X}}+\tilde{Y}A_{,\tilde{Y}}+\tilde{Z}A_{,\tilde{Z}}~.

In this section, we will reanalyze the condition on the conformal factor A, and then we derive the corresponding two-field mimetic constraint and show several forms of
A
to illustrate new possibilities.

2.1
Singular conformal limit

One can write the condition (
5
) on
A
in a simpler way

A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})=c~A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})~,

(7)

which holds for any number or scalar field
c
. We will prove the equivalence between eq. (
5
) and eq. (
7
) in the appendix
A
. This means
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
is a homogeneous function of degree one with respect
to
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{Z}
.
Writing the kinetic term
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z}
in term of the inverse metric and the covariant derivative of the scalar fields, i.e.,
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=A(\phi,\psi,\phi_{\mu},\psi_{\mu},c~\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu})
, one immediately has

A(\phi,\psi,\phi_{\mu},\psi_{\mu},c~\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu})=c~A(\phi,\psi,\phi_{\mu},\psi_{\mu},\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu})~,

(8)

which means
A(\phi,\psi,\phi_{\mu},\psi_{\mu},\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu})
is a linear function with respect to the inverse metric
\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}
.

The authors of
Firouzjahi:2018xob
claimed that the nontrivial solution for
A
is

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\alpha(\phi,\psi)\tilde{X}+\beta(\phi,\psi)\tilde{Y}+2\gamma(\phi,\psi)\tilde{Z}~.

(9)

In the following subsection, several specific forms will be given to demonstrate that the solution of eq. (
7
) or eq. (
8
) is actually far more general than eq. (
9
).

2.2
Two-field mimetic constraint

We now move on to derive the general two-field mimetic constraint for the singular conformal transformation. According to the conformal relation between the physical metric and auxiliary metric (
1
), one has

g^{\mu\nu}=\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}/A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})~,

(10)

and

X=\tilde{X}/A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z}),~Y=\tilde{Y}/A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z}),~Z=\tilde{Z}/A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})~.

(11)

where
X\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu},~Y\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}
and
Z\equiv g^{\mu\nu}\phi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}
. Taking the value of
c
in equation (
7
) to be
g^{\mu\nu}/\tilde{g}^{\mu\nu}
, i.e.,
c=1/A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
, one obtains

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})=c~A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=1~.

(12)

Therefore, the general two-field mimetic constraint equation is found to be

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=1~,

(13)

which is the main result we get in this section.

2.3
Specific forms

In this subsection, we will present several specific forms of the conformal factor
A
which satisfies the condition imposed by the singular conformal limit.

•

Form I:
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\alpha(\phi,\psi)\tilde{X}+\beta(\phi,\psi)\tilde{Y}+\gamma(\phi,\psi)\tilde{Z}
, where
\alpha
and
\beta
are arbitrary functions of the scalar fields
\phi
and
\psi
. This means
A
is a linear function of
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{Y}
at the same time. The associated mimetic constraint is

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=\alpha(\phi,\psi)X+\beta(\phi,\psi)Y+\gamma(\phi,\psi)Z=1~.

(14)

This form has been obtained and discussed in
Firouzjahi:2018xob
.

–

Subform Ia:
\alpha=1,~\beta=\gamma=0
. This case corresponds to the original single field mimetic scenario
Chamseddine:2013kea
, where the conformal factor is taken to be
A(\phi,\tilde{X})=\tilde{X}
and the associated mimetic constraint is

A(\phi,X)=X=1~.

(15)

–

Subform Ib:
\gamma=0
. For this case, the cross term is vanishing. Actually, one can always remove the cross term in (
14
) through the linear transformation of the field space. Then the conformal factor takes the form

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\alpha(\phi,\psi)\tilde{X}+\beta(\phi,\psi)\tilde{Y}
,
and the associated mimetic constraint is

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=\alpha(\phi,\psi)X+\beta(\phi,\psi)Y=1~.

(16)

If one further impose the shift symmetries on both scalar fields, the associated mimetic constraint becomes

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=\alpha X+\beta Y=1~,

(17)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are constants. Cosmological implication of such case is discussed in detail in Refs.
Firouzjahi:2018xob
;
Shen:2019nyp

•

Form II:
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\alpha(\phi,\psi)\tilde{X}^{p}\tilde{Y}^{q}\tilde{Z}^{{1-p-q}}
, where
p
and
q
are contants. Note the conformal factor
A
is not a linear function of
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{Y}
any more. The associated mimetic constraint is

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=\alpha(\phi,\psi)X^{p}Y^{q}Z^{1-p-q}=1~.

(18)

This form hasn’t been discussed yet in the literature, and we hope to come back to study its cosmology soon.

–

Subform IIa:
p+q=1
and
\alpha(\phi,\psi)=1
. The conformal factor takes the form
A(\phi,\tilde{X})=\tilde{X}^{p}\tilde{Y}^{1-p}
and the associated mimetic constraint is
A(\phi,X)=X^{p}Y^{1-p}=1
. One interesting possibility is to choose
p=1/(m+1)
and
q=m/(m+1)
where
m
is an positive integer. The mimetic constraint then reads
X^{1/(m+1)}Y^{m/(1+m)}=1
, which yields

XY^{m}=1~.

(19)

For instance, choosing
m=1
leads to the mimetic constraint
XY=1
.
Another interesting choose is to take
p=n+1
and
q=-n
(
n
is also an positive integer), which gives the conformal factor
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}^{n+1}/\tilde{Y}^{n}
and the mimetic constraint

X^{n+1}/Y^{n}=1~.

(20)

Fox instance, choosing
n=1
gives the mimetic constraint equation
X^{2}/Y=1
.

–

Subform IIb:
p+q\neq 1
. For instance, choosing
p=q=1
the mimetic constraint equation becomes

XY/Z=1~.

(21)

•

Form III:
A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\Sigma_{i}~\alpha_{i}(\phi,\psi)A_{i}(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})
, where
\alpha_{i}
are arbitrary functions of
\phi
and
\psi
, and
A_{i}
satisfy the condition imposed by singular conformal limit. This means that any linear combination of the conformal factors satisfying the singular limit condition (
5
) still safisfies (
5
), which is a natural result of the fact that equation (
5
) is a linear equation of
A
. To show new possibilities of this form, here we give a concrete example where the constraint equation is taken to be

A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=X+Y+\alpha X^{2}/Y=1~.

(22)

Actually, one can prove all the solution of
A
in the singular conformal limit can be written in the general form:

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}\theta(\phi,\psi,\tilde{Y}/\tilde{X},\tilde{Z}/\tilde{X})~,

(23)

where
\theta
is an arbitrary function of
\phi,\psi,Y/X
, and
Z/X
.
For this general form of
A
, the mimetic constraint equation reads

X\theta(\phi,\psi,Y/X,Z/X)=1~.

(24)

For example, one can rewrite form I and form II as

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}\left(\alpha(\phi,\psi)+\beta(\phi,\psi)\frac{\tilde{Y}}{\tilde{X}}+\gamma(\phi,\psi)\frac{\tilde{Z}}{\tilde{X}}\right)~,

(25)

and

A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})=\tilde{X}\left[\alpha(\phi,\psi)\left(\frac{\tilde{Y}}{\tilde{X}}\right)^{q}\left(\frac{\tilde{Z}}{\tilde{X}}\right)^{1-p-q}\right]~,

(26)

which obviously satisfy the form (
23

Xe^{-Y/X}=1~,~\frac{X}{\sin{(Y/X)}}=1~,~\frac{X}{\sin{(Y/X)}e^{-\phi}}=1~.

(27)

3
General two-field mimetic gravity

Similar to the original mimetic scenario, the action for two-field mimetic gravity can be written as

S[\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\phi,\psi]=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\phi,\psi)}\frac{R(g_{\mu\nu}(\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu},\phi,\psi))}{2}~,

(28)

where the relation between the physical metric and the auxiliary metric is (
4
) and the physical metric is invariant under the Weyl rescalings of the auxiliary metric. The conformal factor
A
in the singular limit satisfies the condition (
5
), or equivalently, (
7
), and the associated mimetic constraint is given by (
13
). It is convenient to rewrite the action in the Lagrange multiplier formalism and we have

S[g,\phi,\psi,\lambda]=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg(A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)-1\bigg)\right]~,

(29)

where
\lambda
is a Lagrange multiplier which enforces the mimetic constraint. Recall that function
A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)
satisfies the condition

A=XA_{,X}+YA_{,Y}+ZA_{,Z}~,

(30)

or

A(\phi,\psi,cX,cY,cZ)=c~A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)~,

(31)

in which we have replaced the auxiliary metric in (
5
) and (
7
) by physical metric. Several allowed forms of
A
have been shown in the previous section.

3.1
Equations of motion

This subsection is devoted to the equations of motion for general two-field mimetic gravity (
29
). Varying the action with respect to the metric gives the gravitational equations

G_{\mu\nu}=-T_{\mu\nu}~,

(32)

where the effective energy-momentum tensor for the double mimetic fields is

T_{\mu\nu}=\lambda\left(A_{,X}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}+A_{,Y}\psi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}+\frac{A_{,Z}}{2}(\phi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}+\phi_{\nu}\psi_{\mu})\right)~.

(33)

The divergence of the gravitational equations leads to

\nabla_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}=0~,

(34)

which represents the conservation of energy and momentum. Varying the action with respect to the scalar fields yields

\nabla_{\mu}\left[\lambda(A_{,X}\phi^{\mu}+\frac{A_{,Z}}{2}\psi^{\mu})\right]-\frac{\lambda}{2}A_{,\phi}=0~,\nabla_{\mu}\left[\lambda(A_{,Y}\psi^{\mu}+\frac{A_{,Z}}{2}\phi^{\mu})\right]-\frac{\lambda}{2}A_{,\psi}=0~.

(35)

In addition, the mimetic constraint equation
A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=1
is imposed by the Lagrangian multiplier.

3.2
Cosmological implications

In this subsection, we consider the cosmological implications of general two-field mimetic gravity (
29
) at the background level. For a spatially flat Friedmann-Robertson-Walker (FRW) background, the metric is given by

ds^{2}=dt^{2}-a(t)^{2}\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}~,

(36)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor and t is the cosmic time. The energy density is

\rho=\lambda(A_{,X}\dot{\phi}^{2}+A_{,Y}\dot{\psi}^{2}+A_{,Z}\dot{\phi}\dot{\psi})~,

(37)

and pressure is

\quad p=0~,

(38)

by computing the diagonal components of the energy-momentum tensor (
33
). Using the condition on
A

A=XA_{,X}+YA_{,Y}+ZA_{,Z}=(A_{,X}\dot{\phi}^{2}+A_{,Y}\dot{\psi}^{2}+A_{,Z}\dot{\phi}\dot{\psi})~,

(39)

together with the mimetic constraint equation

A=1~,

(40)

we find
\rho=\lambda
and
p=0
. The conservation of energy yields

\dot{\rho}+3H(\rho+p)=0~,

(41)

in which
H=\dot{a(t)}/a(t)
is the Hubble expansion rate. As the pressure is zero, one get

\rho=\lambda=c/a^{3}~,

(42)

where
c
is constant of integration. This result can also be derived from the scalar equations (
35
) at the cosmological background.

In Ref.
Firouzjahi:2018xob
, the authors found that the two-field mimetic setup after imposing the shift symmetries on both
\phi
and
\psi
mimics the dark matter at the cosmological background level. Here we have greatly generalized their results. Although the functional form of
A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)
in our more general setup (
29
) could be very diverse as shown in the subsection
2.3
, the model still describes a dark matter-like fluid at the cosmological background. This means the dark matter nature is independent of the explicit form of
A
, and it is not necessary to assume shift symmetry to obtain the dark matter-like behavior. Note that there is an exceptional case:
\lambda=0
, where the mimetic scalars field don’t contribute at all and the setup reduces to general relativity. We will encounter and study such case in the next section.
The two-field mimetic action (
29
) at the perturbation level will be studied in detail in future work.

4
Extension with introduced extra term and its relations with other modified theories of gravity

For the original mimetic scenario, the most general term (without higher derivative) one can introduce to the scalar field is a potential term
V(\phi)
as the kinetic term satisfies the mimetic constraint
X=1
and thus can be eliminated. For the two-field mimetic gravity model (
29
), one can also introduce extra possible term for phenomenology so that the extended action takes the following form

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg(A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)-1\bigg)+Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)\right]~,

(43)

where
Q
is an arbitrary function of
\phi,\psi,X,Y
and
Z
.
We should emphasize that the above extension, similar to the case of original mimetic scenario with extra potential term, in general don’t mimic dark matter any more. This is because the pressure
p=Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)
no longer vanishes at the cosmological background level.

Based on whether one of the scalar fields can be eliminated by the mimetic constraint, we can divide the above action into two categories. For our interest in this paper, we focus more on the cases where one of the scalar fields can be removed and show the possible relations with other modified theories of gravity.

4.1
Case where
\psi
can be eliminated

Without loss of generality, we consider the case
\psi
can be eliminated. If we choose
A
in the action (
43
) to be
X/J(\phi,\psi)
,which obviously satisfy the condition (
30
), the mimetic constraint becomes

A=X/J(\phi,\psi)=1~,

(44)

or equivalently,

\psi=f(\phi,X),

(45)

where
f
is the inverse function of
J
. Note that we have assumed
J(\phi,\psi)
explicitly depending on
\psi
, i.e.,
J_{\psi}
is not vanishing, to ensure the existence of the inverse function. Thus the action becomes

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\bigg(\psi-f(\phi,X)\bigg)+Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)\right]~.

(46)

It should be mentioned that the Lagrange multiplier are redefined as the mimetic constraint equation has been rewritten. Variation of the above action with respect to
\tilde{\lambda}
yields the mimetic constraint (
45
). Then substituting the constraint into the action, one can eliminate the scalar field
\psi
.
Therefore, this setup reduces to the scalar-tensor theory with only one scalar filed and no mimetic constraint at all. However, higher derivative of the remaining scalar field may comes out. We will consider some particular cases below according to the explicit form of the term
Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)
.

4.1.1
Subcase without introducing the term
Q

Considering the subcase without introducing the extra term
Q
, i.e., one has

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\left(\frac{X}{J(\phi,\psi)}-1\right)\right]~,

(47)

or equivalently

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\bigg(\psi-f(\phi,X)\bigg)\right]~.

(48)

The mimetic constraint
\psi-f(\phi,X)=0
is enforced by the Lagrange multiplier. Then substituting the constraint equation into the action, the action reduces to the Einstein-Hilbert action

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}~\frac{R}{2}~,

(49)

and one recovers Einstein’s theory of general relativity. Therefore, we find the Weyl scaling invariant two-field mimetic theory (
28
) with

g_{\mu\nu}=\tilde{X}/J(\phi,\psi)\tilde{g}_{\mu\nu}

(50)

is equivalent to general relativity and the number of degrees of freedom in this setup is
2
.

As we have concluded in section
3
, the general two-field mimetic model (
29
) are expected to mimic the dark matter. The special case (
47
) obviously belongs to the action (
29
), but no dark matter appears. This is because the setup (
47
) is actually the exceptional case
\lambda=0
we have mentioned earlier. One can verify this by varying the action with respect to
\psi
.

4.1.2
Subcase with
Q=Q(\phi,\psi,X)

Here we consider the subcase with
Q=Q(\phi,\psi,X)
where
Q
is explicitly dependent on
\phi,\psi
and
X
. One has

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\bigg(\psi-f(\phi,X)\bigg)+Q(\phi,\psi,X)\right].

(51)

By using the two-field mimetic constraint one can reduce the action to

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+K(\phi,X)\right]~,

(52)

where
K(\phi,X)\equiv Q(\phi,f(\phi,X),X)
. Thus this subcase of two-field mimetic gravity is equivalent to K-essence theory
Armendariz-Picon:2000ulo
, which exists 3 degrees of freedom and has various phenomenological implications in astrophysics and cosmology.

4.1.3
subcase with
Q=G_{2}(\phi,X)-Z

We consider the case with
Q=G_{2}(\phi,X)-Z
where
G_{2}
is an arbitrary function of
\phi
and
X
. The action becomes

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\bigg(\psi-f(\phi,X)\bigg)+G_{2}(\phi,X)-\phi^{\mu}\psi_{\mu}\right].

(53)

By using the two-field mimetic constraint, the action reduces to

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+G_{2}(\phi,X)+G_{3}(\phi,X)\Box\phi\right]~,

(54)

where
G_{3}(\phi,X)=f(\phi,X)
. Surprisingly, this subcase corresponds to a type of the Galileon theory
Deffayet:2011gz
, which also exist 3 degrees of freedom and has numerous phenomenological implications.

4.1.4
subcase with
Q=Q(Y)

We consider the case when the function
Q
only depends on
Y=\psi^{\mu}\psi_{\mu}
. After employing the
mimetic constraint, one has
Y=\nabla_{\mu}f(\phi,X)\nabla^{\mu}f(\phi,X)
. Thus, the action

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\tilde{\lambda}}{2}\bigg(\psi-f(\phi,X)\bigg)+Q(Y)\right].

(55)

reduces to

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+Q(\nabla_{\mu}f\nabla^{\mu}f)\right]~,

(56)

which includes higher derivative of the scalar field
\phi
. This case has 4 degrees of freedom and one scalar degree of freedom suffers from Ostrogradski instability, and thus not viable for phenomenology.

4.2
Case where neither of the fields can be eliminated

Usually one can not eliminate any scalar field by the mimetic constraint (
13
), so the form of the action (
43
) can be very diverse and has many possibilities. Here we discuss one special case which assumes the shift symmetry for both scalar field
\phi
abd
\psi
, and the action takes the form

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\bigg(A(X,Y,Z)-1\bigg)+Q(X,Y,Z)\right]~,

(57)

where only kinetic terms are relevant. If requiring
A
to be a linear function of
X
,
Y
and
Z
, the action reduces to

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\big(\alpha X+\beta Y-1\big)+Q(X,Y,Z)\right]~.

(58)

The case with
Q=0
has been investigated in
Firouzjahi:2018xob
;
Shen:2019nyp
and it was found the adiabatic mode is frozen in the perturbation level. Then some interesting and important question can be asked. For instance, whether the adiabatic mode is still frozen or become propagating after adding the extra term
Q
? If the adiabatic mode become propagating, does it suffer from instabilities or not? Can inflation be realized in such case? We leave these questions for future work.

4.3
Degrees of freedom and Ostrogradski ghost

It is important to study the degrees of freedom for the extended two-field mimetic action (
43
). Our main purpose of this subsection is to illustrate that the number of degrees of freedom depends on the form of both
A
and
Q
. The detailed Hamiltonian analysis is beyond the scope of this paper and is left for future investigation.

We first consider the case with
Q=0
. If choose
A=X
, the original one-field mimetic gravity is recovered and the number of degrees of freedom is 3. If one choose
A=X/J(\phi,\psi)
where one of the scalar fields can be eliminated by the mimetic constraint equation, the number of degrees of freedom will be 2. Or if we take
A=\alpha X+\beta Y

Firouzjahi:2018xob
;
Shen:2019nyp

, the number of degrees of freedom will become 4. This implies that the degrees of freedom for the extended two-field mimetic action (
43
) depends on the form of
A
.
The above discussion in subsection
4.1
on the particular case (
46
), where one of the scalar fields can be eliminated by the mimetic constraint equation, shows that the theory possess 2, 3 and 4 degrees of freedom for the subcases (
47
), (
51
) and (
55
) respectively. Therefore, the number of degrees of freedom also depends on the form of
Q
.

Now we discuss the possibility of Ostrogradski ghost. If the theory has 4 degrees of freedom, of which two are scalar modes, then we should be careful as one of the scalar modes may suffer from the Ostrogradski ghost. For instance, there is one Ostrogradski ghost for the case (
55
) and no Ostrogradski ghost for the case where
A=\alpha X+\beta Y
and
Q=0
, although these two cases both have 4 degrees of freedom.

5
Further extension

In the previous sections, we have greatly extend two-field mimetic gravity by obtaining a more general solution for the conformal factor
A
in the singular limit. Moreover, possible extra term are proposed for phenomenological interest in section
4
. This section is devoted to explore further extension.

Note that as the two-field mimetic action in the Lagrange multiplier formalism (
29
) originates from the Weyl invariant gravity (
28
), the allowed expression of mimetic constraint is restricted by the singular limit condition. But if we directly start with the setup (
29
) instead of the Weyl invariant gravity, there will be no imposed condition on the explicit form of the mimetic constraint.Thus, the two-field mimetic model (
29
) can be further generalized as

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}~M(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)\right]~,

(59)

where
M
is an arbitrary function of
\phi,\psi,X,Y
and
Z
. The corresponding mimetic constraint is

M(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=0~,

(60)

and the Einstein equations of motion is
G_{\mu\nu}=-T_{\mu\nu}
in which the effective energy-momentum tensor is given by

T_{\mu\nu}=\lambda\left(M_{,X}\phi_{\mu}\phi_{\nu}+M_{,Y}\psi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}+\frac{M_{,Z}}{2}(\phi_{\mu}\psi_{\nu}+\phi_{\nu}\psi_{\mu})\right)~.

(61)

For the cosmological background, the energy density is

\rho=\lambda\left(XM_{,X}+YM_{,Y}+ZM_{,Z}\right)~,

(62)

and
the pressure is
p=0
. Then the energy conservation law in an expanding universe implies
\rho\propto a^{-3}
, which means the model (
59
) with generalized mimetic constraint still mimics the role of dark matter at the cosmological background level.

6
Conclusion and discussions

The original mimetic scenario can be achieved by considering the conformal transformation in the singular limit. Similarly, two-field mimetic gravity has been proposed in
Firouzjahi:2018xob
by looking at the singular limit of the conformal transformation between the auxiliary and the physical metrics with two scalar fields.

In this paper, we reanalyze the singular conformal limit and obtain a more general solution for the conformal factor
A
. The associated constraint equation is derived to be
A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)=1
, which extends the two-field mimetic constraint and generalize the two-field mimetic gravity in the literature. To illustrate new possibilities, several allowed forms of
A
are given and discussed. Then we consider the cosmological implications and find our extended setup still behaves as dark matter at the background level, which means the dark matter nature is independent of the explicit form of
A
. Moreover, extra possible term
Q
are added to the action for phenomenological applications. Surprisingly, general relativity, k-essence theory and Galileon theory are recovered in some particular cases where one of the scalar fields can be eliminated by the mimetic constraint equation. Finally, we show that the dark matter nature is still, again, unchanged even in the further extension of the two-field mimetic gravity which imposes no condition on the expression of mimetic constraint.
We leave the detailed investigation of the linear perturbation theory for future work.

We have shown two different ways to extend two-field mimetic gravity (
29
), namely (
43
) and (
59
). It is interesting to search for more possible extensions. For instance, one may combine two extensions together, then the action becomes

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}~M(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)+Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)\right]~.

(63)

We may also introduce higher derivative terms of the scalar fields to the action, such as

S=\int\textrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{R}{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}~\bigg(A(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z)-1\bigg)+Q(\phi,\psi,X,Y,Z,\Box\phi,\Box\psi)\right]~.

(64)

The theoretic properties and cosmological applications of these extended models deserve detailed investigations in the future.

Appendix A
The Condition on A in the singular limit

In this appendix, our purpose is to prove that eq. (
5
) and eq. (
7
) is the equivalent description for the condition on A in the singular limit. First, one can define

W(c)=A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})~.

(65)

Take

W(1)=A(\phi,\psi,\tilde{X},\tilde{Y},\tilde{Z})~.

(66)

The derivative of the function

W^{\prime}(c)=\tilde{X}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{X})}+\tilde{Y}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{Y})}+\tilde{Z}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{Z})}~.

(67)

Then we will deduce equation (
7
) from equation (
5
). Replacing
\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}
and
\tilde{Z}
by
c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y}
and
c\tilde{Z}
respectively, equation (
5
) becomes

\displaystyle A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})=

\displaystyle c~\tilde{X}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{X})}+c~\tilde{Y}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{Y})}

\displaystyle+c~\tilde{Z}\frac{\partial A(\phi,\psi,c\tilde{X},c\tilde{Y},c\tilde{Z})}{\partial(c\tilde{Z})}~.

(68)

Employing the expression of the function
W
(
66
) and the its derivative (
67
), the above equation yields

W(c)=c~W^{\prime}(c)~,

(69)

which means
W(c)
is a linear function of

(70)

Using the definition of the function

Now we show how equation (

Using equation (

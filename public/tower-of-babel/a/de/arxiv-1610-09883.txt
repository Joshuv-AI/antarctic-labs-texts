. 1.
Introduction.

In this paper we are concerned with finite time blowup for the semilinear parabolic system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|v|^{p-1}v,&\partial_{t}v=\mu\Delta v+|u|^{q-1}u,\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},&v(\cdot,0)=v_{0},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, where

p,q>1,\quad\mu>0.

The local Cauchy problem for (
1.1
) can be solved in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. We denote by
T=T(u_{0},v_{0})\in(0,+\infty]
the maximal existence time of the classical solution
(u,v)
of problem (
1.1
). If
T<+\infty
, then the solution blows up in finite time
T
in the sense that

\lim_{t\to T}(\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})})=+\infty.

In that case,
T
is called the blowup time of the solution. A point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is said to be a blowup point of
(u,v)
if
(u,v)
is not locally bounded near
(a,T)
in the sense that
|u(x_{n},t_{n})|+|v(x_{n},t_{n})|\to+\infty
for some sequence
(x_{n},t_{n})\to(a,T)
as
n\to+\infty
. We say that the blowup is
simultaneous
if

\limsup_{t\to T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\limsup_{t\to T}\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=+\infty,

(1.2)

and that it is
non-simultaneous
if (
1.2
) does not hold, i.e. if one of the two components remains bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
. For the system (
1.1
), it is easy to see that the blowup is always
simultaneous
. Indeed, if
u
is uniformly bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
, then the second equation would yield a uniform bound on
v
. More specifically, we say that
u
and
v
blow up simultaneously at the same point
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
if
a
is a blowup point both for
u
and
v
.

In the case of a single equation, namely when system (
1.1
) is reduced to the scalar equation

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u,\quad u(\cdot,0)=u_{0},\quad p>1,

(1.3)

the blowup question for equation (
1.3
) has been studied intensively by many authors and no list can be exhaustive. Let us sketch the main results for the case of the equation (
1.3
). Considering
u
a blowup solution to (
1.3
) and
T
its blowup time, we know from Giga and Kohn
[
GK87
]
that

\forall(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T),\quad|u(x,t)|\leq C(T-t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}},

for some positive constant
C
, provided that
1<p\leq\frac{3N+8}{3N-4}
or
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
with
u_{0}\geq 0
. This result was extended by Giga, Matsui and Sasayama
[
GMS04
]
for all
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
without assuming the non-negativity of initial data.

The study of the blow-up behavior of solution (
1.3
) is done through the introduction of similarity variables:

W_{T,a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(x,t),\quad y=\frac{x-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t),

where
a
may or not be a blow-up point of
u
. From (
1.3
), we see that
W_{T,a}
solves the new equation in
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\log T,+\infty)
:

\partial_{s}W_{T,a}=\Delta W_{T,a}-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla W_{T,a}-\frac{W_{T,a}}{p-1}+|W_{T,a}|^{p-1}W_{T,a}.

(1.4)

According to Giga and Kohn in
[
GK89
]
(see also
[
GK85
,
GK87
]
), we know that: If
a
is a blow-up point of
u
, then

\lim_{t\to T}(T-t)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}u(a+y\sqrt{T-t},t)=\lim_{s\to+\infty}W_{T,a}(y,s)=\pm\kappa,

(1.5)

uniformly on compact sets
|y|\leq R
, where
\kappa=(p-1)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}
.

This estimate has been refined until the higher order by Filippas, Kohn and Liu
[
FK92
]
,
[
FL93
]
, Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV92a
]
,
[
HV93
]
,
[
Vel92
]
,
[
Vel93b
]
,
[
Vel93a
]
. More precisely, they classified the behavior of
W_{T,a}(y,s)
for
|y|
bounded, and showed that one of the following cases occurs (up to replacing
u
by
-u
if necessary),

\bullet
either there exists
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
,

\sup_{|y|\leq K\sqrt{s}}\left|W_{T,a}(y,s)-\kappa\left(1+\frac{p-1}{4ps}\sum_{i=1}^{k}y_{i}^{2}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right|=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{\log s}{s}\right).

(1.6)

\bullet
or there exists an even integer
m\geq 4
and constant
c_{\alpha}
not all zero such that

\sup_{|y|\leq Ke^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{m}\right)s}}\left|W_{T,a}(y,s)-\kappa\left(1+e^{-\left(1-\frac{m}{2}\right)s}\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}\right)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\right|=o(1),

where the homogeneous multilinear form
\sum_{|\alpha|=m}c_{\alpha}y^{\alpha}
is non-negative.

From Bricmont and Kupiainen
[
BK94
]
, Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV93
]
, we have examples of initial data leading to each of the above mentioned scenarios. Moreover, Herrero and Velázquez
[
HV92b
]
proved that the asymptotic behavior (
1.6
) is generic in the one dimensional case, and they announced the same for the higher dimensional case, but they never published it. Note also that the asymptotic profile described in (
1.6
) with
k=N
has been proved to be stable with respect to perturbations in the initial data or the nonlinearity by Merle and Zaag in
[
MZ97
]
(see also Fermanian, Merle and Zaag
[
FMZ00
]
,
[
FZ00
]
, Nguyen and Zaag
[
NZ16b
]
for other proofs of the stability).

As for system (
1.1
), much less result is known, in particular in the study of the asymptotic behavior of the solution near singularities. As far as we know, the only available results concerning the blowup behavior are due to Andreucci, Herrero and Velázquez
[
AHV97
]
and Zaag
[
Zaa01
]
where the system (
1.1
) is considered with
\mu=1
.

When
\mu=1
, according to Escobedo and Herrero
[
EH91a
]
(see also
[
EH91b
]
), we know that any nontrivial positive solution of (
1.1
) which is defined for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
must necessarily blow up in finite time if

pq>1,\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\max\{p,q\}+1}{pq-1}\geq\frac{N}{2},

and both functions
u(x,t)
and
v(x,t)
must blow up simultaneously. See also
[
EH93
]
for the case of boundary value problems.

In
[
AHV97
]
, the authors proved that if

pq>1,\quad\text{and}\quad q(p(N-2))<N+2\quad\text{or}\quad p(q(N-2))<N+2,

(1.7)

then every positive solution
(u,v)
of (
1.1
) exhibits the
Type I
blowup, namely that there exists some constant
C>0
such that

\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{u}(t),\quad\|v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C\bar{v}(t),

(1.8)

where
(\bar{u},\bar{v})
solves the following ODE system

\bar{u}^{\prime}=\bar{v}^{p},\quad\bar{v}^{\prime}=\bar{u}^{q},\quad\bar{u}(T)=\bar{v}(T)=+\infty,

whose solution is explicitly given by

\bar{u}(t)=\Gamma(T-t)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\quad\bar{v}(t)=\gamma(T-t)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}

where
(\Gamma,\gamma)
defined by

\gamma^{p}=\Gamma\left(\frac{p+1}{pq-1}\right),\quad\Gamma^{q}=\gamma\left(\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\right).

(1.9)

The estimate (
1.8
) has also been proved by Caristi and Mitidieri
[
CM94
]
in a ball under assumptions on
p
and
q
different from (
1.7
). See also Deng
[
Den96
]
, Fila and Souplet
[
FS01
]
for other results relative to estimate (
1.8
).

The study of blowup solutions for system (
1.1
) is done through the introduction of the following similarity variables for all
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
a
may or may not be a blowup point):

\begin{array}[]{c}\Phi_{T,a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}u(x,t),\quad\Psi_{T,a}(y,s)=(T-t)^{\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}v(x,t),\\
\\
\text{where}\quad y=\dfrac{x-a}{\sqrt{T-t}},\quad s=-\log(T-t).\end{array}

(1.10)

From (
1.1
),
(\Phi_{T,a},\Psi_{T,a})
(or
(\Phi,\Psi)
for simplicity) satisfy the following system: for all
(y,s)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[-\log T,+\infty)
,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\partial_{s}\Phi&=\Delta\Phi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Phi-\left(\dfrac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\Phi+|\Psi|^{p-1}\Psi,\\
&\\
\partial_{s}\Psi&=\mu\Delta\Psi-\dfrac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\Psi-\left(\dfrac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\Psi+|\Phi|^{p-1}\Phi.\end{array}

(1.11)

Assuming (
1.8
) holds, namely that

\forall a\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad\|\Phi_{T,a}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|\Psi_{T,a}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq C,\quad\forall s\geq-\log T,

and considering
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
a blowup point of
(u,v)
, we know from
[
AHV97
]
that (remind that we are considering the case when
\mu=1
)

\bullet
either
(\Phi_{T,a},\Psi_{T,a})
goes to
(\Gamma,\gamma)
exponentially fast,

\bullet
or there exists
k\in\{1,\cdots,N\}
such that after an orthogonal change of space coordinates and up to replacing
(u,v)
by
(-u,-v)
if necessary,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi_{T,a}(y,s)&=\Gamma-\dfrac{c_{1}}{s}(p+1)\Gamma\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+o\left(\dfrac{1}{s}\right),\\
&\\
\Psi_{T,a}(y,s)&=\gamma-\dfrac{c_{1}}{s}(q+1)\gamma\sum\limits_{i=1}^{k}(y_{i}^{2}-2)+o\left(\dfrac{1}{s}\right),\end{array}

(1.12)

where
(\Gamma,\gamma)
is given by (
1.9
) and

c_{1}=c_{1}(p,q)=\frac{2pq+p+q}{8pq(p+1)(q+1)},

(1.13)

and the convergence takes place in
\mathcal{C}^{\ell}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
\ell\geq 0
.

In the first case, we have other profiles, some of them are different from those occurring in the scalar case of (
1.3
), see Theorem 3 and 4 in
[
AHV97
]
for more details. Note that the value of
c_{1}
given in (
1.13
) was not precised in
[
AHV97
]
, but we can justify it by explicit computations as in
[
AHV97
]
.

Beside the results already cited, let us mention to the work by Zaag
[
Zaa01
]
where the author obtained a Liouville theorem for system (
1.1
) that improves the results of
[
AHV97
]
. Based on this theorem, he was able to derive sharp estimates of asymptotic behaviors as well as a localization property for blowup solutions of (
1.1
). For other aspects of system (
1.1
), especially concerning the blowup set, see Friedman and Giga
[
FG87
]
, Mahmoudi, Souplet and Tayachi
[
MST15
]
, Souplet
[
Sou09
]
.

In this paper, we want to study the profile of the solution of (
1.1
) near blowup, and the stability of such behavior with respect to perturbations in initial data. More precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Existence of a blow-up solution for system (
1.1
) with the description of its profile)
.

Consider
a\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. There exists
T>0
such that system (
1.1
) has a solution
(u,v)
defined on
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
such that:

(i)

u
and
v
blow up in finite time
T
simultaneously at one blowup point
a
and only there.

(ii)
There holds that

\begin{array}[]{l}\left\|(T-t)^{\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}u(x,t)-\Phi^{*}\left(\dfrac{x-a}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{|\log(T-t)|}},\\
\\
\left\|(T-t)^{\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}v(x,t)-\Psi^{*}\left(\dfrac{x-a}{\sqrt{(T-t)|\log(T-t)|}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\dfrac{C}{\sqrt{|\log(T-t)|}},\end{array}

(1.14)

where

\Phi^{*}(z)=\Gamma(1+b|z|^{2})^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi^{*}(z)=\gamma(1+b|z|^{2})^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

(1.15)

with
(\Gamma,\gamma)
given by (
1.9
) and

b=b(p,q,\mu)=\frac{(pq-1)(2pq+p+q)}{4pq(p+1)(q+1)(1+\mu)}>0.

(1.16)

(iii)\;
for all
x\neq a
,
(u(x,t),v(x,t))\to(u^{*}(x),v^{*}(x))\in\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})\times\mathcal{C}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash\{0\})
with

u^{*}(x)\sim\Gamma\left(\frac{b|x-a|^{2}}{2|\log|x-a||}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad v^{*}(x)\sim\gamma\left(\frac{b|x-a|^{2}}{2|\log|x-a||}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

as
|x-a|\to 0
.

Remark 1.2
.

The derivation of the blowup profile (
1.15
) can be understood through a formal analysis in Section
2
below. However, we would like to emphasize on the fact that the particular value of
b=b(p,q,\mu)>0
given in (
1.16

Remark 1.6
.

The result of Theorem
1.1
holds for more general nonlinearities than (
1.1
), namely that the nonlinear terms in (
1.1
) are replaced by

F(u,v)=|u|^{p-1}u+f(u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)\quad\text{and}\quad G(u,v)=|v|^{q-1}v+g(u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v),

where

|f(u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)|\leq C(1+|u|^{p_{1}}+|v|^{q_{1}}+|\nabla u|^{r_{1}}+|\nabla v|^{s_{1}}),

and

|g(u,v,\nabla u,\nabla v)|\leq C(1+|u|^{p_{2}}+|v|^{q_{2}}+|\nabla u|^{r_{2}}+|\nabla v|^{s_{2}}),

where

0\leq p_{1}<\frac{p(q+1)}{p+1},\;0\leq q_{1}<p,\quad 0\leq r_{1}<\frac{p(q+1)}{p+\frac{1}{2}pq+\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq s_{1}<\frac{p(q+1)}{q+\frac{1}{2}pq+\frac{1}{2}},

and

0\leq p_{2}<q,\;0\leq q_{2}<\frac{q(p+1)}{q+1},\quad 0\leq r_{2}<\frac{q(p+1)}{p+\frac{1}{2}pq+\frac{1}{2}},\quad 0\leq s_{2}<\frac{q(p+1)}{q+\frac{1}{2}pq+\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that in the setting (
1.10
), the terms

and

turn to be exponentially small. Therefore, a perturbation of our method works although we need in addition some parabolic regularity results in order to handle the nonlinear gradient terms (see
[
EZ11
]
and
[
TZ16
]
for such parabolic regularity techniques). For simplicity, we only give the proof when the nonlinear terms are exactly given by
F(u,v)=|v|^{p-1}v
and
G(u,v)=|u|^{q-1}u
.

As a consequence of our techniques, we show the stability of the constructed solution with respect to perturbations in initial data. More precisely, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.8

(Stability of the blowup profile (
1.15
))
.

Let
(\hat{u}_{0},\hat{v}_{0})
be the initial data of system (
1.1
) such that the corresponding solution
(\hat{u},\hat{v})
blows up in finite time
\hat{T}
at only one blowup point
\hat{a}
and
(\hat{u}(x,t),\hat{v}(x,t))
satisfies (
1.14
) with
T=\hat{T}
and
a=\hat{a}
. Then, there exists a neighborhood
\mathscr{W}_{0}
of
(\hat{u}_{0},\hat{v}_{0})
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that for any
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\mathscr{W}_{0}
, system (
1.1
) has a unique solution
(u,v)
with initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})

. Moreover, parts

Remark 1.9
.

]

for the nonlinear heat equation with a critical power nonlinear gradient term,

\partial_{t}u=\Delta u+|u|^{p-1}u+\mu|\nabla u|^{\frac{2p}{p+1}}\quad\text{with}\;\;p>3,\;\mu>0.

When

which is studied in
[

]
. There are also the cases for the construction of multi-solitons for the semilinear wave equation in one space dimension by Côte and Zaag

One may think that the method used in

]

) treated in
[

]
and
[

. More precisely, our proof relies on the understanding of the dynamics of the selfsimilar version (

) around the profile (

). In the setting (

\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}(y,s)=\binom{\Phi}{\Psi}(y,s)-\binom{\Phi^{*}}{\Psi^{*}}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\to\binom{0}{0}\quad\text{as}\;s\to+\infty.

Satisfying such a property is guaranteed by a condition that
\binom{\Lambda(s)}{\Upsilon(s)}
belongs to some set
\mathscr{V}_{A}(s)\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
which shrinks to

as

(see Definition

below for an example). Since the linearization of system (

) around the profile

gives

), we can use the method of
[

]
and
[
BK94
]
which relies on two arguments:

- The use of the bounding effect of the heat kernel (see Proposition

) to reduce the problem of the control of
\binom{\Lambda(s)}{\Upsilon(s)}
in
\mathscr{V}_{A}(s)
to the control of its positive modes. Note that the linearized operator around the profile, that is
\mathscr{H}+Mc
defined in (

- The control of the positive modes thanks to a topological argument based on the index theory.

In addition to the difficulties concerning the linearized operator mentioned above, we also deal with the number of parameters in the problem (

, and

2.
A formal analysis.

In this section, we give a formal analysis leading to the asymptotic behaviors described in (
1.14
) by means of matching asymptotic. For simplicity, we shall look for
(u,v)
, a positive solution of (
1.1
) in one dimensional case. By the translation invariant in space, we assume that
(u,v)
blows up in finite time
T>0
at the origin, and write
(\Phi,\Psi)
instead of
(\Phi_{T,a},\Psi_{T,a})
for short. From the transformation (
1.10
), the behavior (
1.14
) is equivalent to showing that

\Phi(y,s)\sim\Gamma\left(1+\frac{b|y|^{2}}{s}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\Psi(y,s)\sim\gamma\left(1+\frac{b|y|^{2}}{s}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

(2.1)

as
s\to+\infty
, where
\Gamma
,
\gamma
are defined in (
1.9
) and
b
is given in (
1.16
).

We use here the method of
[
MZ08
]
treated for the complex Ginzburg-Landau equation, which was slightly adapted from the method of Berger and Kohn
[
BK88
]
for equation (
1.3
). Following the approach of
[
MZ08
]
, we try to search formally for system (
1.11
) a regular solution
(\Phi,\Psi)
of the form

\begin{array}[]{ll}\Phi(y,s)&=\Phi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{1}{s}\Phi_{1}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\cdots,\\
\Psi(y,s)&=\Psi_{0}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\frac{1}{s}\Psi_{1}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\cdots\end{array}

(2.2)

Injecting (
2.2
) into (
1.11
) and comparing elements of order
\frac{1}{s^{j}}
with
j=0,1,\cdots
, we obtain for
j=0
,

\begin{array}[]{c}-\dfrac{z}{2}\Phi_{0}^{\prime}-\dfrac{p+1}{pq-1}\Phi_{0}+\Psi_{0}^{p}=0,\\
\\
-\dfrac{z}{2}\Psi_{0}^{\prime}-\dfrac{q+1}{pq-1}\Psi_{0}+\Phi_{0}^{q}=0,\\
\end{array}\quad\text{where}\quad z=\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}},

(2.3)

and for
j=1
,

\begin{array}[]{l}F(z):=\dfrac{z}{2}\Phi_{1}^{\prime}+\left(\dfrac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\Phi_{1}-p\Psi_{0}^{p-1}\Psi_{1}-\dfrac{z}{2}\Phi_{0}^{\prime}-\Phi_{0}^{\prime\prime}=0,\\
\\
G(z):=\dfrac{z}{2}\Psi_{1}^{\prime}+\left(\dfrac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\Psi_{1}-q\Phi_{0}^{q-1}\Phi_{1}-\dfrac{z}{2}\Psi_{0}^{\prime}-\mu\Psi_{0}^{\prime\prime}=0.\\
\end{array}

(2.4)

Solving system (
2.3
) equipped with data at zero

\Phi_{0}(0)=\Gamma\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{0}(0)=\gamma,

we derive

\Phi_{0}(z)=\Gamma(1+bz^{2})^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{0}(z)=\gamma(1+bz^{2})^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

(2.5)

for some integration constant
b
, and
(\Gamma,\gamma)
is given by (
1.9
). Since we want
(\Phi,\Psi)
to be regular, we impose the condition

b>0.

Let us now determine the value of
b
in (
2.5
). To do so, we first evaluate
F
and
G
at
z=0
by using (
2.5
) to find

\begin{array}[]{c}\left(\dfrac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\Phi_{1}(0)-p\gamma^{p-1}\Psi_{1}(0)+2b\left(\dfrac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\Gamma=0,\\
\\
\left(\dfrac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\Psi_{1}(0)-q\Gamma^{q-1}\Phi_{1}(0)+2\mu b\left(\dfrac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\gamma=0.\\
\end{array}

Using the definition of
(\Gamma,\gamma)
given in (
1.9
), one can simplify this system and obtain

\Phi_{1}(0)=\frac{2b\Gamma(p\mu+1)}{pq-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi_{1}(0)=\frac{2b\gamma(q+\mu)}{pq-1}.

(2.6)

Let us now expand
(\Phi_{1},\Psi_{1})
in power of
z
, namely

\begin{array}[]{l}\Phi_{1}(z)=\Phi_{1}(0)+d_{1}z+d_{2}z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}),\\
\Psi_{1}(z)=\Psi_{1}(0)+e_{1}z+e_{2}z^{2}+\mathcal{O}(z^{3}).\end{array}

(2.7)

Injecting these forms into (
2.4
) and expanding
F
and
G
in powers of
z
, we obtain at the order
z
,

\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{\gamma^{p}}{\Gamma}\right)d_{1}-p\gamma^{p-1}e_{1}=0,\\
\\
-q\Gamma^{q-1}d_{1}+\left(\dfrac{1}{2}+\dfrac{\Gamma^{q}}{\gamma}\right)e_{1}=0,\\
\end{array}

which yields

0=\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma^{p+1}+\frac{1}{2}\Gamma^{q+1}+\frac{1}{4}\Gamma\gamma-(pq-1)\Gamma^{q}\gamma^{p}\right)e_{1}:=Ae_{1}.

A straightforward computation gives
A<0
, hence,

d_{1}=e_{1}=0.

For the terms of order
z^{2}
in the expansion of
F
and
G
, we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\left(\dfrac{1}{\gamma^{p}}+\dfrac{1}{\Gamma}\right)d_{2}-\dfrac{p}{\gamma}e_{2}+\dfrac{2b^{2}p(q+1)(p-1)(q+\mu)}{(pq-1)^{2}}-\dfrac{6b^{2}p(q+1)}{pq-1}+b=0,\\
\\
\left(\dfrac{1}{\Gamma^{q}}+\dfrac{1}{\gamma}\right)e_{2}-\dfrac{q}{\Gamma}d_{2}+\dfrac{2b^{2}q(p+1)(q-1)(p\mu+1)}{(pq-1)^{2}}-\dfrac{6\mu b^{2}q(p+1)}{pq-1}+b=0.\end{array}

Multiplying the second equation by
\frac{p(q+1)}{q(p+1)}
, then combining with the first equation, we find that the coefficients of
d_{2}
and
e_{2}
disappear leading to

b=\frac{(pq-1)(2pq+p+q)}{4pq(p+1)(q+1)(1+\mu)},

which is the desired result. Note that our computation fits with the result of the case
\mu=1
by combining (
2.5
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
).

In conclusion, we obtain the following profile for
(\Phi(y,s),\Psi(y,s))
:

(\Phi(y,s),\Psi(y,s))\sim(\varphi(y,s),\psi(y,s)),

where

\varphi(y,s)=\Phi_{0}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})+\frac{1}{s}\Phi_{1}(0)=\Gamma\left(1+\frac{b|y|^{2}}{s}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}+\frac{2b\Gamma(p\mu+1)}{(pq-1)s},

(2.8)

\psi(y,s)=\Psi_{0}(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}})+\frac{1}{s}\Psi_{1}(0)=\gamma\left(1+\frac{b|y|^{2}}{s}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}+\frac{2b\gamma(q+\mu)}{(pq-1)s},

(2.9)

with
b
given in (
1.16
).

3.
Formulation of the problem.

In this section, we give a formulation for the proof of Theorem
1.1
. We will only give the proof in one dimensional case (
N=1
) for simplicity, but the proof remains the same for higher dimensions
N\geq 2
. We want to prove the existence of suitable initial data
(u_{0},v_{0})
so that the corresponding solution
(u,v)
of system (
1.1
) blows up in finite time
T
only at one point
a\in\mathbb{R}
and verifies (
1.14
). From translation invariance of equation (
1.1
), we may assume that
a=0
. Through the transformation (
1.10
), we want to find
s_{0}>0
and
(\Phi(y,s_{0}),\Psi(y,s_{0}))
such that the solution
(\Phi,\Psi)
of system (
1.11
) with initial data
(\Phi(y,s_{0}),\Psi(y,s_{0}))
satisfies

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left\|\Phi(y,s)-\Phi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\left\|\Psi(y,s)-\Psi^{*}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0,

(3.1)

where
\Phi^{*}
and
\Psi^{*}
are given by (
1.15
).

According to the formal analysis in the previous section, let us introduce
\Lambda(y,s)
and
\Upsilon(y,s)
such that

\Phi(y,s)=\Lambda(y,s)+\varphi(y,s),\quad\Psi(y,s)=\Upsilon(y,s)+\psi(y,s),

(3.2)

where
\varphi
and
\psi
are given in (
2.8
) and (
2.9
).

With the introduction of
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
in (
3.2
), the problem is then reduced to constructing functions
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
such that

\lim_{s\to+\infty}\|\Lambda(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}\|\Upsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=0.

and
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
satisfies the following system:

\partial_{s}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}+\binom{F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)}{F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)}+\binom{R_{1}(y,s)}{R_{2}(y,s)},

(3.3)

where

\mathscr{H}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathscr{L}_{1}&0\\
0&\mathscr{L}_{\mu}\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{where }\;\;\mathscr{L}_{\eta}=\eta\Delta-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla,\quad\eta=\{1,\mu\},

(3.4)

Mc=\begin{pmatrix}-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}&\;p\gamma^{p-1}\\
q\Gamma^{q-1}&\;-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\end{pmatrix},

(3.5)

V(y,s)=\begin{pmatrix}0&p\big(\psi^{p-1}-\gamma^{p-1}\big)\\
q\big(\varphi^{q-1}-\Gamma^{q-1}\big)&0\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}0&V_{1}\\
V_{2}&0\end{pmatrix},

(3.6)

\binom{F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)}{F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)}=\binom{|\Upsilon+\psi|^{p-1}(\Upsilon+\psi)-\psi^{p}-p\psi^{p-1}\Upsilon}{|\Lambda+\varphi|^{q-1}(\Lambda+\varphi)-\varphi^{q}-q\varphi^{q-1}\Lambda},

(3.7)

and

\binom{R_{1}(y,s)}{R_{2}(y,s)}=\binom{-\partial_{s}\varphi+\Delta\varphi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\varphi-\left(\frac{p+1}{pq-1}\right)\varphi+\psi^{p}}{-\partial_{s}\psi+\mu\Delta\psi-\frac{1}{2}y\cdot\nabla\psi-\left(\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\right)\psi+\varphi^{q}}.

(3.8)

Note that the term
\binom{F_{1}}{F_{2}}
is built to be quadratic in the inner region
|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
. Indeed, we have for all
K>1
and
s\geq 1
,

\sup_{|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}}\left|F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)\right|\leq C(K)|\Upsilon|^{2},\quad\sup_{|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}}\left|F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)\right|\leq C(K)|\Lambda|^{2}.

Note also that the term
\binom{R_{1}}{R_{2}}
measures the defect preventing
(\varphi,\psi)
from being an exact solution of (
1.11
). Since
(\varphi,\psi)
is an approximate solution of (
1.11
), one easily checks that

\|R_{1}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\|R_{2}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\frac{C}{s}.

(3.9)

Therefore, since we would like to make
(\Lambda,\Upsilon)
go to zero as
s\to+\infty
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the dynamics of (
3.3
) are influenced by the asymptotic limit of its linear term,

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc+V(y,s)\Big)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}\quad\text{as}\quad s\to+\infty.

From the definition (
3.6
), we see that the potential
V(y,s)
has two fundamental properties that will influence strongly our analysis:

(i)\;
We have
(V_{1}(\cdot,s),V_{2}(\cdot,s))\to(0,0)
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
s\to+\infty
, where
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is the weighted
L^{2}
space associated with the weight
\rho_{\eta}
defined by

\rho_{\eta}(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\eta)^{N/2}}e^{-\frac{|y|^{2}}{4\eta}}.

(3.10)

In particular, the effect on
V
inside the
blowup region
or in the
inner region

|y|\leq K\sqrt{s}
will be a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
.

(ii)\;
Outside the
blowup region
or in the
outer region

|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
, we have the following property: for all
\epsilon>0
, there exist
K_{\epsilon}>0
and
s_{\epsilon}>0
such that

\sup_{s\geq s_{\epsilon},|y|\geq K_{\epsilon}\sqrt{s}}\left|V_{1}(y,s)-(-p\gamma^{p-1})\right|+\left|V_{2}(y,s)-(-q\Gamma^{q-1})\right|\leq\epsilon.

In other words, outside the
blowup region
, the linear operator
\mathscr{H}+Mc+V
behaves as

\mathscr{H}+\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}&\pm\epsilon_{1}\\
\pm\epsilon_{2}&-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\end{array}\right).

Given that the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}
is negative (see (
3.19
) below) and that the matrix has negative eigenvalues for
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
small, we see that
\mathscr{H}+Mc+V
behaves as one with fully negative spectrum, which greatly simplifies the analysis in that region.

Since the behavior of the potential
V
inside and outside the
blowup region
is different, we will consider the dynamics for
|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
and
|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
separately for some
K
to be fixed large.

Let us consider a non-increasing cut-off function
\chi_{0}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{0}([0,+\infty))
, with
\text{supp}(\chi_{0})\subset[0,2]
and
\chi_{0}\equiv 1
on
[0,1]
, and introduce

\chi(y,s)=\chi_{0}\left(\frac{|y|}{K\sqrt{s}}\right),

(3.11)

where
K
is chosen large enough so that various technical estimates hold. We define

\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}=(1-\chi)\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.12)

\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}
is the part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
for
|y|\geq K\sqrt{s}
. As announced a few lines above and as we will see in Section
5.2.4
, the spectrum of the linear operator of the equation
satisfied by
\binom{\Lambda_{e}}{\Upsilon_{e}}
is negative, which makes the control of
\|\Lambda_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
and
\|\Upsilon_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}
easily.

While the control of the outer part is easy, it is not the case for the part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
for
|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
. In fact, inside the
blowup region

|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
, the potential
V
can be seen as a perturbation of the effect of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
whose spectrum has two positive eigenvalues, a zero eigenvalue in addition to infinitely negatives ones (see Lemma
3.2
below). Therefore, we have to expand
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
inside the
blowup region
with respect to these eigenvalues in order to control
\|\Lambda(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s})}
and
\|\Upsilon(s)\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s})}
. To do so, we need to find a basis where
\mathscr{H}+Mc
is diagonal or at least in Jordan blocks’ form. Since the operator
\mathscr{H}
is contributed from
\mathscr{L}_{1}
and
\mathscr{L}_{\mu}
, let us first recall well-known spectral properties of the operator
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
, where
\eta\in\{1,\mu\}
.

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
:
Given
\eta>0
, let us consider the Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
which is the set of all
f\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
such that

\|f\|^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}=\big<f,f\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}<+\infty,

where

\big<f,g\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}f(y)g(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy,

(3.13)

and
\rho_{\eta}
is defined by (
3.10
). Note that we can write
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
in the divergence form

\mathscr{L}_{\eta}v=\frac{\eta}{\rho_{\eta}}\;\text{div}\,\Big(\rho_{\eta}\nabla v\Big),

and that
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
is self-adjoint with respect to the weight
\rho_{\eta}
. Indeed, for any
v
and
w
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, it holds that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}v\mathscr{L}_{\eta}w\rho_{\eta}dy=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}w\mathscr{L}_{\eta}v\rho_{\eta}dy.

(3.14)

Let us introduce for each
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
the polynomial

\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)=c_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{2\sqrt{\eta}}\right),

where
H_{n}
is the standard one dimensional Hermite polynomial, i.e

H_{n}(x)=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}(e^{-x^{2}}),

(3.15)

and
c_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}
is chosen so that the term of highest degree in
\tilde{h}_{\alpha}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{N}y_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
. In one-dimensional case, we have

\tilde{h}_{n}(y)=\eta^{\frac{n}{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\frac{n!}{(n-2j)!j!}(-1)^{j}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\eta}}\right)^{n-2j}.

(3.16)

For example,

\tilde{h}_{0}=1,\quad\tilde{h}_{1}=y,\quad\tilde{h}_{2}=y^{2}-2\eta,

\tilde{h}_{3}=y^{3}-6\eta y,\quad\tilde{h}_{4}=y^{4}-12\eta y^{2}+12\eta^{2}.

The family of eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{L}_{\eta}
constitutes an orthogonal basic in
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
in the sense that for any different
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

\mathscr{L}_{\eta}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\tilde{h}_{\alpha},\quad|\alpha|=\alpha_{1}+\cdots+\alpha_{N},

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)\tilde{h}_{\beta}(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy=0,

(3.17)

and that for any
f
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{\eta}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, one can express

f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\big<f,\tilde{h}_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{\eta}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}f_{\alpha}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}.

Remark 3.1
.

We remark that for any polynomial
P_{n}(y)
of degree
n
, we have by (
3.17
),

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{h}_{\alpha}(y)P_{n}(y)\rho_{\eta}(y)dy=0\quad\text{for all}\;\;|\alpha|>n.

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{H}
: Let us consider the functional space
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, which is the set of all
\binom{f}{g}\in L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
such that

\left<\binom{f}{g},\binom{f}{g}\right><+\infty,

where

\left<\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}},\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}\right>:=\big<f_{1},f_{2}\big>_{\rho_{1}}+\big<g_{1},g_{2}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}.

If we introduce for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}
,

h_{\alpha}(y)=a_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\hat{h}_{\alpha}(y)=\hat{a}_{\alpha}\prod_{i=1}^{N}H_{\alpha_{i}}\left(\frac{y_{i}}{2\sqrt{\mu}}\right),

(3.18)

where
H_{n}
is defined by (
3.15
), and
a_{\alpha}
and
\hat{a}_{\alpha}
are constants chosen so that the terms of highest degree in
h_{\alpha}
and
\hat{h}_{\alpha}
is
\prod_{i=1}^{N}y^{\alpha_{i}}
, then

\mathscr{H}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{H}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}=-\frac{|\alpha|}{2}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}.

(3.19)

Moreover, for each
\binom{f}{g}
in
L^{2}_{\rho_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})\times L^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}(\mathbb{R}^{N},\mathbb{R})
, we can write it in the form

\displaystyle\binom{f}{g}=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{N}}\big<f,h_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{1}}\binom{h_{\alpha}}{0}+\big<g,\hat{h}_{\alpha}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{\alpha}}.

\bullet

Spectral properties of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
: As announced in the beginning of Section
3
, we switch back to the case
N=1
for simplicity. Of course, our proof remains valid in the case
N\geq 2
, though with some complications in the notation. We want to find a basis where
\mathscr{H}+Mc
is diagonal or at least in Jordan blocks’ form. More precisely, we have the following:

Lemma 3.2

(Diagonalization of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
in the one dimensional case)
.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exist polynomials
f_{n},g_{n},\tilde{f}_{n}
and
\tilde{g}_{n}
of degree
n
such that

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}=\left(1-\frac{n}{2}\right)\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},

(3.20)

and

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}=-\left(\frac{n}{2}+\frac{(p+1)(q+1)}{pq-1}\right)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}},

(3.21)

where

\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}d_{n,n-2j}\binom{h_{n-2j}}{0}+e_{n,n-2j}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n-2j}},

(3.22)

\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]}\tilde{d}_{n,n-2j}\binom{h_{n-2j}}{0}+\tilde{e}_{n,n-2j}\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n-2j}},

(3.23)

and the coefficients
d_{n,n-2j},e_{n,n-2j}
,
\tilde{d}_{n,n-2j}
,
\tilde{e}_{n,n-2j}
depend on the parameters
p,q
and
\mu
. In particular, we have

\begin{array}[]{ll}d_{n,n}=(p+1)\Gamma,&d_{n,n-2}=n(n-1)p\Gamma(1-\mu),\\
\\
e_{n,n}=(q+1)\gamma,&e_{n,n-2}=n(n-1)q\gamma(\mu-1),\end{array}

(3.24)

and

\begin{array}[]{ll}\tilde{d}_{n,n}=p\Gamma,&\tilde{d}_{n,n-2}=n(n-1)\dfrac{pq\Gamma(p+1)(1-\mu)}{3pq+p+q-1},\\
\\
\tilde{e}_{n,n}=-q\gamma,&\tilde{e}_{n,n-2}=n(n-1)\dfrac{pq\gamma(q+1)(1-\mu)}{3pq+p+q-1}.\end{array}

(3.25)

Remark 3.3
.

The spectrum of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
has two positive eigenvalues
\lambda_{0}=1
and
\lambda_{1}=\frac{1}{2}
corresponding to eigenvectors
\binom{f_{0}}{g_{0}}
and
\binom{f_{1}}{g_{1}}
; a zero eigenvalue
\lambda_{2}=0
corresponding to eigenvector
\binom{f_{2}}{g_{2}}
. Note that in the case when
N\geq 2
, we have

\binom{f_{0}(y)}{g_{0}(y)}=\binom{(p+1)\Gamma}{(q+1)\gamma},\quad\binom{f_{1}(y)}{g_{1}(y)}=\binom{f_{1,i}(y)}{g_{1,i}(y)}_{1\leq i\leq N},

and

\binom{f_{2}(y)}{g_{2}(y)}=\binom{f_{2,ij}(y)}{g_{2,ij}(y)}_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

where

\binom{f_{1,i}(y)}{g_{1,i}(y)}=\binom{(p+1)\Gamma y_{i}}{(q+1)\gamma y_{i}}\quad\text{for}\;1\leq i\leq N,

and

\binom{f_{2,ij}(y)}{g_{2,ij}(y)}=\binom{f_{2,ji}(y)}{g_{2,ji}(y)}=\binom{(p+1)\Gamma y_{i}y_{j}}{(q+1)\gamma y_{i}y_{j}}\quad\text{for}\;1\leq i\neq j\leq N,

and

\binom{f_{2,ii}(y)}{g_{2,ii}(y)}=\binom{(p+1)\Gamma y_{i}^{2}}{(q+1)\gamma y_{i}^{2}}+\binom{2p\Gamma(1-\mu)}{2q\gamma(\mu-1)}\quad\text{for}\;1\leq i\leq N.

(3.26)

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we want to find
\binom{F_{n}}{G_{n}}
in the form of polynomials of degree
n
such that

\Big(\mathscr{H}+Mc\Big)\binom{F_{n}}{G_{n}}=\lambda\binom{F_{n}}{G_{n}}\quad\text{for some }\;\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.27)

Let us assume that

\binom{F_{n}}{G_{n}}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\binom{a_{n,n-i}}{b_{n,n-i}}y^{n-i},\quad a_{n,n}\neq 0,\;b_{n,n}\neq 0.

Plugging this form into (
3.27
) and comparing elements of the order
y^{n-i}
with
i\in\{0,1,\cdots,n\}
, we have for
i=0
,

\Big(Mc-(\lambda+\frac{n}{2})\,\textbf{Id}\Big)\binom{a_{n,n}}{b_{n,n}}=0,

(3.28)

and for
i=1
,

\Big(Mc-(\lambda+\frac{n-1}{2})\,\textbf{Id}\Big)\binom{a_{n,n-1}}{b_{n,n-1}}=0,

(3.29)

and for
i=2,3,\cdots,n
,

\Big(Mc-(\lambda+\frac{n-i}{2})\,\textbf{Id}\Big)\binom{a_{n,n-i}}{b_{n,n-i}}+(n-i+2)(n-i+1)\binom{a_{n,n-i+2}}{\mu\,b_{n,n-i+2}}=0.

(3.30)

Since
(a_{n,n},b_{n,n})\neq(0,0)
, we deduce from (
3.28
) that
\det\Big(Mc-(\lambda+\frac{n}{2})\,\textbf{Id}\Big)=0
, which means that
\lambda
satisfy

\left(\lambda+\frac{n}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(\lambda+\frac{n}{2}\right)\left(\frac{p+q+2}{pq-1}\right)-\frac{(p+1)(q+1)}{pq-1}=0.

Hence, either

\lambda=\lambda_{+}=1-\frac{n}{2}\quad\text{or}\quad\lambda=\lambda_{-}=-\frac{(p+1)(q+1)}{pq-1}-\frac{n}{2}.

(3.31)

Substituting these values of
\lambda
into (
3.28
) yields

\binom{a_{n,n}}{b_{n,n}}=\binom{(p+1)\Gamma}{(q+1)\gamma}\;\;\text{if}\;\lambda=\lambda_{+},\quad\text{and}\quad\binom{a_{n,n}}{b_{n,n}}=\binom{p\Gamma}{-q\gamma}\;\;\text{if}\;\lambda=\lambda_{-}.

Note that for these values of
\lambda
given in (
3.31
), we have by a direct computation for
i=1,2,\cdots,n
,

\text{det}\,\Big(Mc-(\lambda+\frac{n-i}{2})\,\textbf{Id}\Big)=\frac{i^{2}}{4}-\frac{i}{2}\left(\frac{p+q+2}{pq-1}+2\lambda+n\right)\neq 0,

whence, we obtain from equation (
3.29
),

\binom{a_{n,n-1}}{b_{n,n-1}}=\binom{0}{0}.

and from equation (
3.30
) by induction,

\binom{a_{n,n-3}}{b_{n,n-3}}=\binom{a_{n,n-5}}{b_{n,n-5}}=\cdots=\binom{0}{0}.

The couples
\binom{a_{n,n-2}}{b_{n,n-2}}
,
\binom{a_{n,n-4}}{b_{n,n-4}}
,
\cdots
are respectively determined from
\binom{a_{n,n}}{b_{n,n}}
,
\binom{a_{n,n-2}}{b_{n,n-2}}
,
\cdots
through equation (
3.30
).

Since the terms of highest degree of
h_{m}
and
\hat{h}_{m}
are
y^{m}
, and
h_{m}
and
\hat{h}_{m}
are even (or odd respectively) if
m
is even integer (or odd), we can rewrite the expression of
\binom{F_{n}}{G_{n}}
in terms of
\binom{h_{j}}{0}
and
\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{j}}
for
j=0,1,\cdots,n
as stated in (
3.22
) and (
3.23
).

In order to precise the values of
\binom{d_{n,n-2}}{e_{n,n-2}}
and
\binom{\tilde{d}_{n,n-2}}{\tilde{e}_{n,n-2}}
, let us compute
\binom{a_{n,n-2}}{b_{n,n-2}}
.

- For
\lambda=\lambda_{+}
, we use (
3.30
) with
i=2
to get

\binom{a_{n,n-2}}{b_{n,n-2}}=-n(n-1)Mc^{-1}\binom{(p+1)\Gamma}{\mu(q+1)\gamma}=-n(n-1)\binom{\Gamma(p\mu+1)}{\gamma(q+\mu)}.

Recalling from the definition (
3.18
) that
h_{n}(y)=y^{n}-n(n-1)y^{n-2}+\cdots,
and
\hat{h}_{n}(y)=y^{n}-n(n-1)\mu y^{n-2}+\cdots
, we deduce from (
3.22
) that

\binom{d_{n,n-2}}{e_{n,n-2}}=n(n-1)\binom{d_{n,n}}{\mu\,e_{n,n}}+\binom{a_{n,n-2}}{b_{n,n-2}}=n(n-1)\binom{p\Gamma(1-\mu)}{q\gamma(\mu-1)},

which is (
3.24
).

- For
\lambda=\lambda_{-}
, we similarly have

\displaystyle\binom{\tilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tilde{b}_{n,n-2}}

\displaystyle=-n(n-1)\left[Mc+\left(\frac{2pq+p+q}{pq-1}\right)\textbf{Id}\right]^{-1}\binom{p\Gamma}{-\mu q\gamma}

\displaystyle=-\frac{n(n-1)}{3pq+p+q-1}\binom{p\Gamma\big[pq(1+\mu)+(p+\mu q)+(pq-1)\big]}{-q\gamma\big[pq(1+\mu)+(p+\mu q)+\mu(pq-1)\big]}.

Noticing from (
3.23
) that

\binom{\tilde{d}_{n,n-2}}{\tilde{e}_{n,n-2}}=n(n-1)\binom{\tilde{d}_{n,n}}{\mu\,\tilde{e}_{n,n}}+\binom{\tilde{a}_{n,n-2}}{\tilde{b}_{n,n-2}},

hence, (
3.25
) follows after a straightforward calculation. This concludes the proof of Lemma
3.2
.
∎

For the sake of controlling
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
in the region
|y|\leq 2K\sqrt{s}
, we will expand
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
with respect to the family
\left\{\binom{h_{n}}{0},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
and then with respect to the family
\left\{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right\}_{n\geq 0}
. We start by writing

\displaystyle\binom{\Lambda(y,s)}{\Upsilon(y,s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq M}Q_{n}(s)\binom{h_{n}(y)}{0}+\hat{Q}_{n}(s)\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}(y)}+\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)},

(3.32)

(note that this identity is precisely the definition of
\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}
) where
M
is a fixed even integer satisfying

M\geq 4\left[1+\|Mc\|_{\infty}+2\max_{y\in\mathbb{R},s\geq 1,i=1,2}|V_{i}(y,s)|\big)\right],

(3.33)

with
\|Mc\|_{\infty}=\max\left\{q\Gamma^{q-1}+\frac{p+1}{pq-1},p\gamma^{p-1}+\frac{q+1}{pq-1}\right\}
(in view of the definition (
3.5
) of
Mc
, this is indeed a suitable norm for
(2\times 2)
matrices). As we will show in Section
5.2.3
, the choice of
M
is crucial and allows us to successfully use a Gronwall’s inequality in the control of the infinite-dimensional part
\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}
, and

\bullet\;

Q_{n}(s)
and
\hat{Q}_{n}(s)
are the projections of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
on
\binom{h_{n}}{0}
and
\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}
respectively, defined by

Q_{n}(s)=\frac{\left<\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>}{\left<\binom{h_{n}}{0},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>}=\frac{\big<\Lambda,h_{n}\big>_{\rho_{1}}}{\big<h_{n},h_{n}\big>^{2}_{\rho_{1}}}\equiv\Pi_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.34)

\hat{Q}_{n}(s)=\frac{\left<\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>}{\left<\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>}=\frac{\big<\Upsilon,\hat{h}_{n}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}}{\big<\hat{h}_{n},\hat{h}_{n}\big>^{2}_{\rho_{\mu}}}\equiv\hat{\Pi}_{n}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.35)

\bullet\;

\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}=\Pi_{-,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
is called the infinite-dimensional part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
, where
\Pi_{-,M}
is the projector on the subspace of
L_{\rho_{1}}\times L_{\rho_{\mu}}
where the spectrum of
\mathscr{H}
is lower than
\frac{1-M}{2}
. Note that for all
n\leq M
,

\left<\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}},\binom{h_{n}}{0}\right>=\big<\Lambda_{-},h_{n}\big>_{\rho_{1}}=0\;\;\text{and}\;\;\left<\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right>=\big<\Upsilon_{-},\hat{h}_{n}\big>_{\rho_{\mu}}=0.

(3.36)

\bullet\;
We also introduce
\Pi_{+,M}=\textbf{Id}-\Pi_{-,M}
, and the complementary part

\binom{\Lambda_{+}}{\Upsilon_{+}}=\Pi_{+,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}=\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}-\binom{\Lambda_{-}}{\Upsilon_{-}}

which is called the finite-dimensional part of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
, and which satisfies for all
s
,

\left<\binom{\Lambda_{+}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{+}(y,s)},\binom{\Lambda_{-}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{-}(y,s)}\right>=0.

(3.37)

We will expand it with respect to the basis of eigenfunctions of
\mathscr{H}+Mc
computed in Lemma
3.2
, namely the family
\left\{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right\}_{n\leq M}
, as follows:

\displaystyle\binom{\Lambda_{+}(y,s)}{\Upsilon_{+}(y,s)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq M}Q_{n}(s)\binom{h_{n}(y)}{0}+\hat{Q}_{n}(s)\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}(y)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq M}\theta_{n}(s)\binom{f_{n}(y)}{g_{n}(y)}+\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}(y)}{\tilde{g}_{n}(y)},

(3.38)

where
\theta_{n}(s)=P_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)=\tilde{P}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
are projections of
\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}
on
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
respectively. This is possible, since from Lemma
3.2
, we can express
\theta_{n}(s)
and
\tilde{\theta}_{n}(s)
in terms of
Q_{n}(s)
and
\hat{Q}_{n}(s)
as follows:

Lemma 3.4

(Definition of the projection on the modes
\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}
and
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
)
.

We have

\theta_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{M-n}{2}\right]}A_{n+2j,n}\,Q_{n+2j}+B_{n+2j,n}\,\hat{Q}_{n+2j}\equiv P_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon},

(3.39)

and

\tilde{\theta}_{n}=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{M-n}{2}\right]}\tilde{A}_{n+2j,n}\,Q_{n+2j}+\tilde{B}_{n+2j,n}\,\hat{Q}_{n+2j}\equiv\tilde{P}_{n,M}\binom{\Lambda}{\Upsilon}

(3.40)

where the coefficients
A_{n+2j,n}
,
B_{n+2j,n}
,
\tilde{A}_{n+2j,n}
and
\tilde{B}_{n+2j,n}
for

depend on

and
\mu
. In particular, we have

A_{n,n}=\frac{q}{\Gamma(2pq+p+q)},\quad B_{n,n}=\frac{p}{\gamma(2pq+p+q)},

(3.41)

and

A_{n+2,n}=-\frac{\tilde{e}_{n+2,n}}{\Gamma\gamma(2pq+p+q)}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{n+2,n}=\left(\frac{p+1}{q+1}\right)\frac{\tilde{e}_{n+2,n}}{\gamma^{2}(2pq+p+q)}.

(3.42)

Remark 3.5
.

From Lemma
3.4
, we obviously see that when a function is of the form
\sum_{n=0}^{M}\omega_{n}\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}+\tilde{\omega}_{n}\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}
, its projections on

Remark 3.6
.

The precise values given in (
3.41
) and (
3.42
) are crucial in deriving a refined ODE satisfied by the null mode, that is the ODE given in part
(iii)
of Proposition
5.3
(see Lemma
5.7
also).

Proof.

We first note that the matrix of
\left\{\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}},\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
in the basis
\left\{\binom{h_{n}}{0},\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is ”lower triangular” in the sense that we can express the matrix in terms of
(2\times 2)
blocks (see (
3.22
) and (
3.23
)) as follows:

\begin{pmatrix}X_{0}\\
X_{1}\\
X_{2}\\
X_{3}\\
X_{4}\\
\vdots\\
X_{M}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{F}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{F}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathcal{D}_{2,0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{F}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{D}_{3,1}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{F}&\mathbf{0}&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\mathcal{D}_{4,0}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{D}_{4,2}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{F}&\cdots&\mathbf{0}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\cdots&\cdots&\mathcal{D}_{M,M-4}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{D}_{M,M-2}&\mathbf{0}&\mathcal{F}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}Y_{0}\\
Y_{1}\\
Y_{2}\\
Y_{3}\\
Y_{4}\\
\vdots\\
Y_{M}\end{pmatrix},

(3.43)

where
\mathbf{0}
is the
(2\times 2)
zero matrix,

X_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\binom{f_{n}}{g_{n}}\\
\\
\binom{\tilde{f}_{n}}{\tilde{g}_{n}}\end{pmatrix},\quad Y_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\binom{h_{n}}{0}\\
\\
\binom{0}{\hat{h}_{n}}\end{pmatrix},

and

\sup_{|\xi|\leq 2|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|^{1/4},0\leq\tau<1}\left|h(x_{0},\xi,0)-\hat{h}_{K_{0}}(\tau)\right|\leq\epsilon(x_{0})\to 0,

as

, where

\hat{g}_{K_{0}}(\tau)=\Gamma(1-\tau+bK_{0}^{2})^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\quad\hat{h}_{K_{0}}(\tau)=\gamma(1-\tau+bK_{0}^{2})^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

is the solution of system (

) with constant initial data

.

Making
\tau\to 1
and using (

\displaystyle u^{*}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to T}u(x,t)=(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\lim_{\tau\to 1}g(x_{0},0,\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sim(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}}\hat{g}_{K_{0}}(1),

\displaystyle v^{*}(x_{0})

\displaystyle=\lim_{t\to T}v(x,t)=(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}\lim_{\tau\to 1}h(x_{0},0,\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\sim(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}}\hat{h}_{K_{0}}(1),

as
x_{0}\to 0
. From (

|\log(T-t_{0}(x_{0}))|\sim 2\log|x_{0}|,\quad T-t_{0}(x_{0})\sim\frac{|x_{0}|^{2}}{2K_{0}^{2}|\log|x_{0}||}\quad\text{as}\quad x_{0}\to 0,

hence,

u^{*}(x_{0})\sim\Gamma\left(\frac{b|x_{0}|^{2}}{2|\log|x_{0}||}\right)^{-\frac{p+1}{pq-1}},\quad v^{*}(x_{0})\sim\gamma\left(\frac{b|x_{0}|^{2}}{2|\log|x_{0}||}\right)^{-\frac{q+1}{pq-1}},

as
x_{0}\to 0
, which concludes the proof of part

of Theorem

For all

Using Lemma

\displaystyle P_{2,M}\binom{W_{1}g_{2}}{W_{2}f_{2}}

\displaystyle=A_{2,2}\alpha_{2}+B_{2,2}\beta_{2}+A_{4,2}\alpha_{4}+B_{4,2}\beta_{4},

where the values of

,

are explicitly given by (

follows. Part

\bullet\;
Fourth term:
\binom{F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)}{F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)}

We first claim the following:

Lemma 5.8

(Decompositions of
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
)
.

The functions
F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)
and
F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)
given in (

) can be decomposed for all
|\Lambda|\leq 1
,
|\Upsilon|\leq 1
as follows: for all

and
|y|<\sqrt{s}

\displaystyle\left|F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)-\sum_{k=2}^{M+1}\Upsilon^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{l}}\left[F_{1,k}^{l}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\tilde{F}_{1,k}^{l}(y,s)\right]\right|\leq C|\Upsilon|^{M+2}+\frac{C}{s^{M+1}},

and

\displaystyle\left|F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)-\sum_{k=2}^{M+1}\Lambda^{k}\sum_{l=0}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{l}}\left[F_{2,k}^{l}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right)+\tilde{F}_{2,k}^{l}(y,s)\right]\right|\leq C|\Lambda|^{M+2}+\frac{C}{s^{M+1}},

where
F_{i,k}^{l}
is an even polynomials of degree less or equal to
M
and
\tilde{F}_{i,k}^{l}(y,s)
satisfies

|\tilde{F}^{l}_{i,k}(y,s)|\leq\frac{C(1+|y|^{M+1})}{s^{\frac{M+1}{2}}}.

On the other hand, we have for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\geq 1
,

|F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)|\leq C|\Upsilon|^{\bar{p}},\quad|F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)|\leq C|\Lambda|^{\bar{q}},

(5.18)

where
\bar{p}=\min\{2,p\}
and
\bar{q}=\min\{q,2\}
.

Proof.

We only deal with
F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)
since the same proof holds for
F_{2}(\Lambda,y,s)
. We first note that in the region
\{|y|<\sqrt{s}\}
and for
s\geq s_{0}
for some
s_{0}\geq 1
,
\psi(y,s)
is bounded from above and from below. Thus, we Taylor expand
F_{1}
in term of
\Upsilon
and write

\left|F_{1}(\Upsilon,y,s)-\sum_{k=2}^{M+1}E_{1,k}(\psi)\Upsilon^{k}\right|\leq C|\Upsilon|^{M+2}.

Now, we expand
E_{1,k}(\psi)
in terms of the variable
\frac{1}{s}
, and write

\left|E_{1,k}(\psi)-\sum_{l=0}^{M}\frac{1}{s^{l}}E^{l}_{1,k}(\Psi^{*})\right|\leq\frac{C}{s^{M+1}}.

Then, we expand

in terms of

as follows:

\left|E^{l}_{1,k}(\Psi^{*})-\sum_{i=0}^{M/2}e_{1,k}^{l,i}|z|^{2i}\right|\leq C|z|^{M+2}.

Finally, we set

F_{1,k}^{l}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{M/2}e_{1,k}^{l,i}|z|^{2i}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{F}_{1,k}^{l}(y,s)=E^{l}_{1,k}(\Psi^{*})-F_{1,k}^{l}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{s}}\right),

(5.19)

which yields the desired result. This concludes the proof of Lemma
5.8

\tilde{P}_{n,M}

5.8

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}F_{1}h_{m}\rho_{1}dy

\displaystyle+\int_{|y|>\sqrt{s}}F_{1}h_{m}\rho_{1}dy

|\Psi^{*}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{K\sqrt{s^{\prime}}}\left(\|\Lambda(s^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq K\sqrt{s^{\prime}})}+\|\Upsilon(s^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}(|y|\leq K\sqrt{s^{\prime}})}\right)\leq\frac{CA^{M+1}}{s^{\prime}}.

Recalling from (
3.9
) the bound for
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, we have

\left\|(1-\chi(2y,s^{\prime}))R_{i}(s^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{C}{s^{\prime}},\quad i=1,2.

We also have

\displaystyle\left\|(1-\chi(2y,s^{\prime}))\tilde{F}_{1}(s^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|\psi(s^{\prime})\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(|y|\geq K\sqrt{s^{\prime}})}+\|\Upsilon(s^{\prime})\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(|y|\geq K\sqrt{s^{\prime}})}\right)\|\tilde{\Upsilon}_{e}(s^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{r+1}{2(pq-1)}\|\tilde{\Upsilon}_{e}(s^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}},

for
K
large enough, where

r=\min\{p,q\}.

Similarly,

\left\|(1-\chi(2y,s^{\prime}))\tilde{F}_{2}(s^{\prime})\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\frac{r+1}{2(pq-1)}\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{e}(s^{\prime})\|_{L^{\infty}}.

If we set
\lambda(s)=\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\tilde{\Upsilon}_{e}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}
, then we end up with

\displaystyle\lambda(s)

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\frac{r+1}{pq-1}(s-\tau)}\lambda(\tau)

\displaystyle+\int_{\tau}^{s}e^{-\frac{r+1}{pq-1}(s-s^{\prime})}\left(\frac{r+1}{2(pq-1)}\lambda(s^{\prime})+\frac{CA^{M+1}}{\sqrt{s^{\prime}}}+\frac{CA^{M+1}}{s^{\prime}\sqrt{1-e^{-(s-s^{\prime})}}}\right)ds^{\prime}.

Applying Lemma
A.1
, we finally obtain

\lambda(s)\leq e^{-\frac{r+1}{2(pq-1)}(s-\tau)}\lambda(\tau)+\frac{CA^{M+1}}{\sqrt{s}}(s-\tau+\sqrt{s-\tau}).

Since
\text{supp}(1-\chi(y,s))\subset\text{supp}(1-\chi(2y,s))
, we have
\|\Lambda_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\tilde{\Lambda}_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}
and
\|\Upsilon_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\tilde{\Upsilon}_{e}\|_{L^{\infty}}
. This concludes the proof of part
(v)
of Proposition
5.3
.

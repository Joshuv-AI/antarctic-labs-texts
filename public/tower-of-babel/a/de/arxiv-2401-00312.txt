Sequences of operators, monotone in the sense of contractive domination Thanks: The second author is grateful to the University of Vaasa for its hospitality when a final version of the present paper was being prepared.
S. Hassi, H.S.V. de Snoo

Abstract
. A sequence of operators T n T_{n} from a Hilbert space ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} to Hilbert spaces 𝔎 n {\mathfrak{K}}_{n} which is nondecreasing in the sense of contractive domination is shown to have a limit which is still a linear operator T T from ℌ {\mathfrak{H}} to a Hilbert space 𝔎 {\mathfrak{K}} . Moreover, the closability or closedness of T n T_{n} is preserved in the limit. The closures converge likewise and the connection between the limits is investigated. There is no similar way of dealing directly with linear relations. However, the sequence of closures is still nondecreasing and then the convergence is governed by the monotonicity principle. There are some related results for nonincreasing sequences.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 47A30, 47A63, 47B02; Secondary 47B25, 47B65

1.
Introduction

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, be a sequence of linear operators
from a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
, which satisfy

(1.1)

{\rm dom\,}T_{n+1}\subset{\rm dom\,}T_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n}f\|\leq\|T_{n+1}f\|,\,\,f\in{\rm dom\,}T_{n+1}.

Here and elsewhere the notation
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
indicates the class
of all linear relations between the Hilbert spaces
{\mathfrak{H}}
and
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
It will be shown that there exists a limit of this sequence, namely a linear operator

T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, whose domain is given by

{\rm dom\,}T=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|<\infty\right\},

while, furthermore,

\|T_{n}f\|\nearrow\|Tf\|\quad\mbox{for all}\quad f\in{\rm dom\,}T.

The limit is uniquely determined up to partial isometries.
Moreover, it will be shown that closability and closedness of the operators
T_{n}

are preserved in the limit.
The main idea about the existence of the limit is the notion of a representing
map that was described by Szymański
[
14
]
.
In the present paper the emphasis is on how to construct the limit
of the sequence of operators and to discuss analogous sequences of linear relations.
There is a close connection with similar convergence results
in the context of nonnegative forms by Simon
[
13
]
(see also
[
12
]
),
but the details will be left for a treatment in
[
9
]
in terms of Lebesgue
decompositions and Lebesgue type decompositions of semibounded forms.

The monotonicity in (
1.1
) can also be discussed for the case of linear
relations
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
by requiring that
T_{n+1}

contractively dominates
T_{n}
, i.e., there are contractions

C_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{n+1},{\mathfrak{H}}_{n})
which satisfy

(1.2)

C_{n}T_{n+1}\subset T_{n}.

Likewise, this kind of monotonicity is preserved under closures
T_{n}^{**}

and under taking regular parts
T_{n,{\rm reg}}

of the relations
T_{n}
(see below). In general there is no convergence result as for operators.
However, the regular parts
T_{n,{\rm reg}}
form a nondecreasing sequence of closable operators
(as in (
1.1
)) and one may apply the above mentioned results for operators.
Thanks to the condition (
1.2
) the sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint relations

T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}
is nondecreasing in the usual sense and the monotonicity principle
may be applied. This connects the various forms of convergence.

As mentioned above, in the present paper regular parts of operators or relations
play an important role.
The regular part
T_{\rm reg}
of a linear relation

T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
shows up in its Lebesgue decomposition, as follows

T=T_{\rm reg}+T_{\rm sing}\quad\mbox{with}\quad T_{\rm reg}=(I-P)T,\,\,T_{\rm sing}=PT,

where
P
stands for the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}
;
see
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
.
Hence
T_{\rm reg}
is a closable operator, while
T_{\rm sing}
is singular in the sense that
its closure in the graph sense is the product of closed linear subspaces;
note in particular that
{\rm ran\,}T_{\rm reg}\perp{\rm mul\,}T^{**}
.
The regular part
T_{\rm reg}
is the largest closable operator that is dominated by
T

in the sense of contractive domination.
There is an interplay with the closure
T^{**}
of
T
, given by the formula

(1.3)

(T^{**})_{\rm reg}=(T_{\rm reg})^{**},

see Section
11
.
If the relation
T
is closed, then
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}={\rm mul\,}T
and
T_{\rm reg}
is the usual
closed orthogonal operator part of
T
, often denoted by
T_{\rm op}
. In this
case, clearly,
T_{\rm reg}\subset T
and
T
has the decomposition

T=T_{\rm reg}\hplus(\{0\}\times{\rm mul\,}T),

where the sum is componentwise. Note that
the left-hand side of the identity (
1.3
) stands
for the orthogonal operator part of
T^{**}
.
In the general case the following identity

T^{*}T^{**}=(T_{\rm reg})^{*}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}

expresses the nonnegative selfadjoint relation on the left-hand side in terms
of a similar product of closable operators.

The case of a sequence of nonincreasing linear operators will also be discussed
with the same methods. Now closability is not preserved so that the main result
is about a nonincreasing sequence of closed linear operators.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
there is brief

. The representing map

T^{*}T
and

which are used throughout this paper.

In the present paper the interest is in monotone sequences of linear operators

2.
Contractive domination for linear relations

The notion of domination for linear relations was introduced in
[
6
]
.
The definition and some basic properties are given here.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}_{A}
,
{\mathfrak{H}}_{B}
, and
{\mathfrak{H}}
be Hilbert spaces,
let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
and let

B\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{B})
. Then
B
is said to contractively dominate
A
,
denoted by
A\prec_{c}B
,
if there exists a contraction
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})

such that

(2.1)

CB\subset A.

It follows from
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})

that
CB=\{\{f,Cf^{\prime}\}:\,\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in B\}
.
Therefore, (
2.1
) implies

(2.2)

\begin{cases}{}\,\,{\rm dom\,}B\subset{\rm dom\,}A,&{{\rm ker\,}\,}B\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}A,\\
\,\,C({\rm ran\,}B)\subset{\rm ran\,}A,&C({\rm mul\,}B)\subset{\rm mul\,}A.\end{cases}

Observe that Definition
2.1
implies that
the contraction
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
is only fixed
as a mapping form
{\rm ran\,}B
to
{\rm ran\,}A
. In fact,
the boundedness of
C
implies that
C
takes

{\rm\overline{ran}\,}B
into
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}A
. Hence, it may and will be assumed
that

C(({\rm ran\,}B)^{\perp})=\{0\}.

Note that if
A
and
B
are linear relations
which satisfy
B\subset A
, then

B
contractively dominates
A
with
C=I_{{\rm ran\,}B}
.
In particular,
A
contractively dominates
A^{**}
.
Finally, the notion of contractive domination is transitive:

\begin{array}[]{l}A\prec_{c}B\quad\textrm{and}\quad B\prec_{c}C\quad\Rightarrow\quad A\prec_{c}C.\end{array}

If
A\prec_{c}B
with a contraction
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
, then
it follows from (
2.1
) and
[
2
, Proposition 1.3.9]
that

(2.3)

A^{*}\subset B^{*}C^{*}\quad\mbox{and}\quad CB^{**}\subset A^{**}.

In other words, the second inclusion in (
2.3
)
shows that the contractive domination in (
2.1
) is preserved
with the same operator
C
. In particular, if
A\prec_{c}B
,
then the following inclusions are valid:
{\rm ran\,}A^{*}\subset{\rm ran\,}B^{*}

and
{\rm dom\,}B^{**}\subset{\rm dom\,}A^{**}
.
Recall that in the particular case when
A
and
B
in Definition
2.1

are linear operators it is possible to give an equivalent
characterization of contractive domination:
A\prec_{c}B

if and only if

{\rm dom\,}B\subset{\rm dom\,}A\quad\text{and}\quad\|Af\|\leq\|Bf\|,\quad f\in{\rm dom\,}B.

The following result shows that contractive domination is preserved by the regular parts.
This observation goes back to
[
13
]
for the case of nonnegative forms and to
[
4
]
.
Furthermore, it is shown that there is a converse statement in the case of closed linear relations.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
and
B\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{B})
be linear relations.
Then

A\prec_{c}B\quad\Rightarrow\quad A_{\rm reg}\prec_{c}B_{\rm reg}.

Moreover, if the linear relations
A
and
B
are closed, then

A\prec_{c}B\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad A_{\rm reg}\prec_{c}B_{\rm reg}.

Proof.

Assume that
CB\subset A
with a contraction
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
.
By (
2.2
) the operator
C
maps
{\rm mul\,}B^{**}
into
{\rm mul\,}A^{**}
.
Let
P_{B}
be the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm mul\,}B^{**}
and let
P_{A}

be the orthogonal projection onto
{\rm mul\,}A^{**}
. Let
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in B

and write
\{f,f^{\prime}\}=\{f,(I-P_{B})f^{\prime}+P_{B}f^{\prime}\}
(i.e., the Lebesgue decomposition of
B
).
Here
P_{B}f^{\prime}\in{\rm mul\,}B^{**}
and
one concludes that

\{f,Cf^{\prime}\}=\{f,C(I-P_{B})f^{\prime}+CP_{B}f^{\prime}\}\in A,

where
CP_{B}f^{\prime}\in{\rm mul\,}A^{**}
. Now observe that

\{f,(I-P_{B})f^{\prime}\}\in B_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{f,(I-P_{A})C(I-P_{B})f^{\prime}\}\in A_{\rm reg}.

Equivalently,
this leads to
[(I-P_{A})C]B_{\rm reg}\subset A_{\rm reg}
,
and since (
I-P_{A})C
is a contraction
this implies
A_{\rm reg}\prec_{c}B_{\rm reg}
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
and
B\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{B})
be closed linear relations.
Then
A_{\rm reg}
and
B_{\rm reg}
, belonging to
{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{A},{\mathfrak{H}}_{B})
,
are the closed linear operator parts.
Assume the inequality
A_{\rm reg}\prec B_{\rm reg}
. Then
there exists a contraction
C\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
such that
CB_{\rm reg}\subset A_{\rm reg}
.
Without loss of generality one may take
C{\upharpoonright\,}({\rm ran\,}B_{\rm reg})^{\perp}=0
.
Then, in particular,
C{\upharpoonright\,}{\rm ran\,}P_{B}=\{0\}
and it follows from
the Lebesgue decomposition
B=B_{\rm reg}+B_{\rm sing}
that

CB=CB_{\rm reg}\subset A_{\rm reg}.

Since
A
is closed, one sees that
A_{\rm reg}\subset A
.
Therefore,
CB\subset A
and
A\prec_{c}B
.
∎

The equivalence in the above theorem is restricted to closed linear relations.
By modifying the notion of domination the condition that the relations are closed
can be relaxed by introducing a weaker form of the Lebesgue decomposition; cf.

[
4
]
,
[
10
]
.

Contractive domination of closed linear relations can be characterized
in terms of the corresponding nonnegative selfadjoint relations;
see
[
6
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Recall from
[
2
, Definition 5.2.8]
that two nonnegative relations

H_{1}
and
H_{2}
in
{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
satisfy
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
when

(2.4)

{\rm dom\,}H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset{\rm dom\,}H_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(H_{1,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|\leq\|(H_{2,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\|,\,\,f\in{\rm dom\,}H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

With this definition the following theorem is clear.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{A})
and
B\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}_{B})
be closed linear relations.
Then the following statements are equivalent

(i)

A^{*}A\leq B^{*}B
;

(ii)

A\prec_{c}B
or, equivalently,
A_{\rm reg}\prec_{c}B_{\rm reg}
.

Proof.

Let
H_{1}=A^{*}A
and
H_{2}=B^{*}B
.
By Lemma
11.2
it follows that there exist partial isometries

U_{1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{A},{\mathfrak{H}})
and
U_{2}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}}_{B},{\mathfrak{H}})
, such that.

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm dom\,}H_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}A,\quad(H_{1,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=U_{1}A_{\rm reg},\\
{\rm dom\,}H_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}B,\quad(H_{2,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=U_{2}B_{\rm reg}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore by means of (
2.4
) this shows that
A^{*}A\leq B^{*}B
, i.e.,
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
,
is equivalent to the assertions

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm dom\,}B\subset{\rm dom\,}A,\\
\|A_{\rm reg}h\|\leq\|B_{\rm reg}h\|,\quad h\in{\rm dom\,}B.\end{array}\right.

In other words, the inequality
A^{*}A\leq B^{*}B
in (i)
is equivalent to the inequality
A_{\rm reg}\prec_{c}B_{\rm reg}
in (ii).
∎

This characterization makes it possible to apply the monotonicity principle in the next section.

3.
The monotonicity principle

A linear relation
H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is called the
strong graph limit

of a sequence of linear relations
H_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
if for each
\{h,h^{\prime}\}\in H
there exists a sequence
\{h_{n},h_{n}^{\prime}\}\in H_{n}

such that
\{h_{n},h_{n}^{\prime}\}\to\{h,h^{\prime}\}
; see
[
2
, Definition 1.9.1]
.
The strong graph limit is automatically closed, see
[
2
, p. 80]
.
Clearly, if all
H_{n}
are symmetric, then
H
is symmetric.
In particular, if all
H_{n}
are nonnegative, then
H
is nonnegative.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
H_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint relations
and let its strong graph limit
H_{\infty}
be nonnegative and selfadjoint.
Then for every
f\in{\rm dom\,}H_{\infty}
there exists a
sequence
f_{n}\in{\rm dom\,}H_{n}
such that

f_{n}\to f\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(H_{n,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{n}\|\to\|(H_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|.

Proof.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be any nonnegative selfadjoint relation with square root
A^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
Recall that
{\rm mul\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm mul\,}A
, so that
(A^{\frac{1}{2}})_{\rm reg}=(A_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
If
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in A
, then there exists an element
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}
such that
\{f,h\}\in A^{\frac{1}{2}}

and
\{h,f^{\prime}\}\in A^{\frac{1}{2}}
, which gives

(3.1)

(f^{\prime},f)=\|h\|^{2}.

Since
h\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}\subset({\rm mul\,}A)^{\perp}
, one sees that
h=(A_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}f
.
Therefore, it is clear that (
3.1
) may be written as

(3.2)

(f^{\prime},f)=(A_{\rm reg}f,f)=\|(A_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|^{2}.

Now let
f\in{\rm dom\,}H_{\infty}
, then
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in H_{\infty}
for some
f^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{H}}
.
By the strong graph convergence
there exists a sequence

\{f_{n},f_{n}^{\prime}\}\in H_{n}
such that
f_{n}\to f
and
f_{n}^{\prime}\to f^{\prime}
.
Therefore, by definition,
there exist elements
h_{n}\in{\mathfrak{H}}
such that

\{f_{n},h_{n}\}\in(H_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{h_{n},f_{n}^{\prime}\}\in(H_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}},

and, likewise, there exists an element
h\in{\mathfrak{H}}
such that

\{f,h\}\in(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\{h,f^{\prime}\}\in(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then clearly

\|h_{n}\|^{2}=(f_{n}^{\prime},f_{n})\to(f^{\prime},f)=\|h\|^{2},

or, equivalently, using (
3.2
),

\|(H_{n,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{n}\|\to\|(H_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}f\|.\qed

In the case of a nondecreasing sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint
relations
H_{n}
there is a much stronger result. First observe that

H_{m}\leq H_{n}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(H_{m})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}(H_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}},

due to Theorem
2.3
, so that if
H_{n}
is nondecreasing, one also has

(H_{m,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}(H_{n,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following monotonicity principle will be recalled from
[
3
, Theorem 3.5]
,

[
2
, Theorem 5.2.11]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
H_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint relations
and assume they satisfy

H_{m}\leq H_{n},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation
H_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
with

H_{n}\leq H_{\infty},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In fact,
H_{n}\to H_{\infty}
in the strong resolvent sense
or, equivalently, in the strong graph sense.
Moreover, the square root of
H_{\infty}
satisfies

(3.3)

{\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}(H_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|(H_{n,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and, furthermore,

(3.4)

\|(H_{n,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|\nearrow\|(H_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Note that the multivalued parts of the relations
H_{n}
in Theorem
3.2

form a nondecreasing sequence. Of course, if all relations
H_{n}
in Theorem
10.1

are operators, then the limit
H_{\infty}
may still be a linear relation with a nontrivial
multivalued part; see the example below.

Example 3.3
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a nonnegative selfadjoint operator or relation.
Then it is clear that the sequence
H_{n}=nA
of nonnegative selfadjoint operators or relations
is nondecreasing. Hence there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation
H_{\infty}

such that
H_{n}\to H_{\infty}
is the strong graph sense.
To determine
H_{\infty}
, let
\{f,g\}\in H_{\infty}
,
then there exists a sequence
\{f_{n},g_{n}\}\in H_{n}

such that
f_{n}\to f
and
g_{n}\to g
.
Here
g_{n}=nh_{n}
with
\{f_{n},h_{n}\}\in A
and,
clearly,
h_{n}\to 0
. Since
A
is closed, this implies
\{f,0\}\in A
.
Furthermore, note that
h_{n}\in{\rm ran\,}A\subset({{\rm ker\,}\,}A)^{\perp}
.
Hence
g_{n}\in({{\rm ker\,}\,}A)^{\perp}
which implies
g\in({{\rm ker\,}\,}A)^{\perp}
.
Therefore, it follows that

H_{\infty}={{\rm ker\,}\,}A\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}A)^{\perp},

since both relations are selfadjoint. Furthermore one has
{\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}={{\rm ker\,}\,}A

and
(H_{\infty})_{\rm op}={{\rm ker\,}\,}A\times\{0\}
(as in (
3.3
) and (
3.4
)).

For sequences of closed relations which are nondecreasing in the
sense of domination there are close connections with Theorem
3.2

via Theorem
2.3
.

4.
Semi-inner products and representing maps

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
and
let
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
be a linear subspace which is provided with
a semi-inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{+}
. In the following lemma
it will be shown that such a subspace is generated by
a so-called representing map. The assertion is inspired
by
[
14
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
{\mathfrak{H}}
be a Hilbert space with inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)
.
Let
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
be a linear subspace which is provided with a semi-inner product

(\cdot,\cdot)_{+}
. Then there exists a representing map

T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, such that

(\varphi,\psi)_{+}=(T\varphi,T\psi)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}}={\rm dom\,}T.

If
T^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
is a Hilbert space,
is another representing map with
{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}={\mathfrak{D}}
,
then there exists a partial isometry
V\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})

with initial space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T
and final space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T^{\prime}
,
such that
T^{\prime}=VT
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{N}}
be the set of neutral elements in
{\mathfrak{D}}
:

{\mathfrak{N}}=\big\{\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}:\,(\varphi,\varphi)_{+}=0\,\big\}.

Due to the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality the space
{\mathfrak{N}}
is linear.
Hence, one may introduce an inner product on the quotient space

{\mathfrak{D}}/{\mathfrak{N}}
by

[\varphi+{\mathfrak{N}},\psi+{\mathfrak{N}}]=(\varphi,\psi)_{+},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}}.

The completion of this quotient space is indicated by
{\mathfrak{K}}
,
so that
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space.
Denote the inner product on
{\mathfrak{K}}
by
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\mathfrak{K}}
,
so that
(\varphi+{\mathfrak{N}},\psi+{\mathfrak{N}})_{\mathfrak{K}}=[\varphi+{\mathfrak{N}},\psi+{\mathfrak{N}}]

for
\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Next define the operator
T
from
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
to
{\mathfrak{K}}
by

T\varphi=\varphi+{\mathfrak{N}},\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}.

Then it follows that

(T\varphi,T\psi)_{\mathfrak{K}}=[\varphi+{\mathfrak{N}},\psi+{\mathfrak{N}}]=(\varphi,\psi)_{+},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}},

which is the assertion of the lemma.

If
T^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
is a Hilbert space,
is another representing map with
{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}={\mathfrak{D}}
,
then

(T^{\prime}\varphi,T^{\prime}\psi)=(\varphi,\psi)_{+},\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\mathfrak{D}}={\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}.

Then the linear relation
V
from
{\mathfrak{K}}
to
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
, defined by

\big\{\{T\varphi,T^{\prime}\varphi\}:\,\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}\big\},

is an isometric operator from
{\rm ran\,}T
onto
{\rm ran\,}T^{\prime}
,
which can be extended as an isometric operator from
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T
onto
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T^{\prime}
,
such that
T^{\prime}f=VTf
holds for all
f\in{\mathfrak{D}}
.
To get the desired partial isometry
V
it remains to
continue the isometric map to
({\rm ran\,}T)^{\perp}
as a zero mapping.
This gives the desired result.
∎

Let
{\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
be a linear subspace as in Lemma
4.1
.
A sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathfrak{D}}
is said to converge to
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}

in the sense of
{\mathfrak{D}}
, in notation
\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{D}}\varphi
, if

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m}\|_{+}\to 0.

Then
{\mathfrak{D}}
is called
closable
if for any sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathfrak{D}}
one has

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{D}}0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\|\varphi_{n}\|_{+}\to 0,

and, likewise,
{\mathfrak{D}}
is called
closed
if for any sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathfrak{D}}
one has

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{D}}\varphi\quad\Rightarrow\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|\varphi_{n}-\varphi\|_{+}\to 0.

These definitions take a more familiar form in terms of
the representing map
T
in Lemma
4.1

One sees immediately for a sequence
\varphi_{n}\in{\mathfrak{D}}
that

\varphi_{n}\to_{\mathfrak{D}}\varphi\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\quad\mbox{in}\quad{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\|\to 0.

Therefore,
{\mathfrak{D}}
is
closable
if and only if
T
is closable,
and, likewise,
{\mathfrak{D}}
is closed if and only if
T
is closed.

An example of a representing map appears in the following construction that
was used in
[
8
]
.
Let
A\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}})
be a nonnegative contraction in a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
.
The range space
{\mathfrak{A}}={\rm ran\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}
, as a subspace of
{\mathfrak{K}}
,
is provided with the semi-inner product

(4.1)

(A^{\frac{1}{2}}h,A^{\frac{1}{2}}k)_{\mathfrak{A}}=(\pi h,\pi k)_{\mathfrak{K}},\quad h,k\in{\mathfrak{K}},

where
\pi
is the orthogonal projection in
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto

{\rm\overline{ran}\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}=({{\rm ker\,}\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\perp}
. Then
it is clear that the operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{H}})

defined by

A^{\frac{1}{2}}h\mapsto\pi h,\quad h\in{\mathfrak{H}},

with
{\rm dom\,}T={\mathfrak{A}}
, is actually a representing map as follows from (
4.1
).

5.
Nondecreasing sequences of linear operators

It will be shown that a sequence of linear operators,
that is nondecreasing in the sense of contractive domination,
as in Definition
2.1
, has a linear operator as limit.
The limit will be constructed by means of representing maps.
Moreover, it will be shown that closability and closedness of the
operators are preserved in the limit.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of linear operators which satisfy

(5.1)

T_{m}\prec_{c}T_{n},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a linear operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, such that

(5.2)

{\rm dom\,}T=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(5.3)

T_{n}\prec_{c}T\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Moreover, the following statements hold:

(a)

if
T_{n}
is closable for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
T
is closable;

(b)

if
T_{n}
is closed for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, then
T
is closed.

Proof.

Let
T_{n}
be a sequence of operators that satisfies (
5.1
).
Then it is seen by Cauchy’s inequality that the right-hand side
{\mathfrak{D}}

in (
5.2
) is a linear space.
Next the existence of the operator
T
will be shown.
For each
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
define

\|\varphi\|_{+}=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|.

Then
\|\cdot\|_{+}
is clearly a well defined seminorm on
{\mathfrak{D}}

and let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{+}
be the corresponding semi-inner product.
By Lemma
4.1
there exists a linear operator
T
defined on

{\rm dom\,}T={\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
such that

(\varphi,\psi)_{+}=(T\varphi,T\psi),\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}.

This shows the assertion in (
5.3
).

(a) Assume that
T_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is closable. To show that
T
is closable,
it suffices to show that
T=T_{\rm reg}
.
By (
5.3
) one has

T_{n}\prec_{c}T.

Hence there exist contractions
C_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
,
such that
C_{n}T\subset T_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
This implies that

C_{n}T^{**}\subset T_{n}^{**};

see (
2.3
).
In particular, if
\{0,\varphi\}\in T^{**}
, then
\{0,C_{n}\varphi\}\in T_{n}^{**}
,
so that
C_{n}\varphi=0
.
Thus one concludes that
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}C_{n}
.
Let
P
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}
, then
C_{n}P=0
. By means of the Lebesgue decomposition

T=(I-P)T+PT
, this leads to

C_{n}T_{\rm reg}=C_{n}(I-P)T=C_{n}[(I-P)T+PT]=C_{n}T\subset T_{n}.

Hence,
C_{n}T_{\rm reg}\subset T_{n}
for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and thus

(5.4)

\|T_{n}\varphi\|=\|C_{n}T_{\rm reg}\varphi\|\leq\|T_{\rm{reg}}\varphi\|\leq\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Taking the supremum over
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
in (
5.4
)
and combining with (
5.3
) gives

\|T\varphi\|=\|T_{\rm{reg}}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

This implies that
T_{\rm{sing}}=0
and hence
T
is closable.

(b) Assume that
T_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is closed.
To show that
T
is closed, let
\varphi_{n}

be a sequence in
{\rm dom\,}T
such that

(5.5)

\varphi_{n}\to\varphi\,\mbox{ in }\,{\mathfrak{H}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\to 0\,\mbox{ in }\,{\mathfrak{K}}.

Due to (
5.3
) one sees that
T_{k}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\to 0
.
Since for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the operator
T_{k}
is closed
one obtains that
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T_{k}

and
T_{k}(\varphi_{n}-\varphi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
In particular,
\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}
.
In order to verify that
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T
,
first observe that the inequality

\left|\|T\varphi_{n}\|-\|T\varphi_{m}\|\right|\leq\|T(\varphi_{n}-\varphi_{m})\|,

implies, via (
5.5
), that
\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T\varphi_{m}\|<\infty
.
Now it follows from
T_{n}\varphi_{m}\to T_{n}\varphi
and (
5.3
) that

\|T_{n}\varphi\|=\lim_{m\to\infty}\|T_{n}\varphi_{m}\|\leq\lim_{m\to\infty}\|T\varphi_{m}\|\leq\sup_{m\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T\varphi_{m}\|<\infty.

Since this holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one concludes that

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|<\infty
.
Therefore,
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T
. Since by (a) the operator
T
is closable,
it now follows from (
5.5
) that
T
is closed.
∎

The existence of the limit in Theorem
6.1
has been established; however
it is clear that there is no uniqueness. In fact, this question has been already
addressed in Lemma
4.1
. The corollary below is easily verified directly.

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume the conditions from Theorem
5.1

and let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be the limit. If
T^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
is a Hilbert space,
is another limit with
{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}={\rm dom\,}T
,
then there exists a partial isometry
V\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})

with initial space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T
and final space
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T^{\prime}
,
such that
T^{\prime}=VT
.

The following simple result is that an operator that
dominates the sequence also dominates the limit.
This fact will have important consequences.

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume the conditions from Theorem
5.1

and let
T^{\prime}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}^{\prime}
is a Hilbert space,
be a linear operator. Then

T_{n}\prec_{c}T^{\prime},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad T\prec_{c}T^{\prime}.

Proof.

The inequality
T_{n}\prec_{c}T^{\prime}
implies that
{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}\subset{\rm dom\,}T_{n}
and

\|T_{n}\varphi\|\leq\|T^{\prime}\varphi\|
for
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}
.
Since this holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one sees that

{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime}\subset{\rm dom\,}T\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T\varphi\|=\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|\leq\|T^{\prime}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{\prime},

in other words
T\prec T^{\prime}
.
∎

6.
Nondecreasing sequences of closable operators

It is a consequence of Theorem
5.1
that
a sequence of closable linear operators which satisfy (
5.1
)
has a closable limit. The description of the limit
of the closures is of interest.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of linear operators
for which (
5.1
) holds
and assume that
T_{n}
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is closable.
Let
T
be the closable limit of
T_{n}
in (
5.2
) and (
5.3
).
Then the closures
T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
of
T_{n}
satisfy

(6.1)

T_{m}^{**}\prec_{c}T_{n}^{**},\quad m\leq n,\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{n}^{**}\prec_{c}T^{**}.

Consequently, there exists a closed linear operator

S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c}
is a Hilbert space,
such that

(6.2)

{\rm dom\,}S=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}^{**}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}^{**}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(6.3)

T_{n}^{**}\prec_{c}S\prec_{c}T^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n}^{**}\varphi\|\nearrow\|S\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S.

In fact,
{\rm dom\,}T^{**}\subset{\rm dom\,}S
, while

\|S\varphi\|=\|T^{**}\varphi\|
for all
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**}
.

Proof.

The sequence
T_{n}
is assumed to satisfy (
5.1
),
thus it follows that

T_{m}^{**}\prec_{c}T_{n}^{**}
for
m\leq n
, by (
2.3
).
Moreover, by Theorem
5.1
one has
T_{n}\prec_{c}T
,
so that also
T_{n}^{**}\prec_{c}T^{**}
by (
2.3
).
Hence (
6.1
) holds and, in particular, Theorem
5.1
may be applied
to the sequence of closed operators
T_{n}^{**}
.

Recall from Theorem
5.1
that the right-hand side
in (
6.2
) is a linear space.
Moreover, by the same theorem there exists a closed linear operator
S

defined on
{\rm dom\,}S
in (
6.2
) for which (
6.3
) holds;
observe that
S\prec_{c}T^{**}
by Corollary
5.3
.

Now it follows from (
5.3
) and (
6.3
)
that
\|T\varphi\|=\|S\varphi\|
for all
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T
.
Here the operator
S
is closed and
T
is closable, and

S\prec_{c}T^{**}
means that
CT^{**}\subset S
for some
contraction
C\in\mathbf{B}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c})
.
One concludes that
\|S\varphi\|=\|CT^{**}\varphi\|=\|T^{**}\varphi\|

holds in fact for all
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**}
.
∎

A special case of Theorem
5.1
, where all
T_{n}
are bounded everywhere
defined operators, is worth mentioning separately.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
such that

\|T_{m}\varphi\|\leq\|T_{n}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a closed linear operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c}
a Hilbert space, such that

(6.4)

{\rm dom\,}T=\left\{\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(6.5)

T_{n}\prec_{c}T\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Proof.

This is just an application of Theorem
5.1
,
as
\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}={\mathfrak{H}}
.
Hence there exists a linear operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

for which (
6.4
) and (
6.5
) hold.
Since
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
one observes that
T_{n}
,

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is closed, which implies that
T
is closed.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

If in Corollary
6.2
one has
\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\|<\infty
,
then
{\rm dom\,}T={\mathfrak{H}}
and
T\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

by the closed graph theorem.
However, if
\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\|=\infty
,
then by the uniform boundedness principle
there is an element
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}
for which

\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|=\infty

and
{\rm dom\,}T
is a proper subset of
{\mathfrak{H}}
.
Note that
{\rm dom\,}T
is closed
if and only if
T
is a bounded operator.

7.
Nondecreasing sequences of linear relations

In this section the emphasis will be on nondecreasing sequences of
linear relations in the general case, i.e., the relations are not necessarily
operators or not necessarily closed.
However, also the regular parts and the closures form
nondecreasing sequences. In particular, one may apply
Theorem
2.3
, which
leads to a connection with the monotonicity principle in Theorem
3.2
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of linear relations which satisfy

(7.1)

T_{m}\prec_{c}T_{n}\quad m\leq n.

Observe that the regular parts
T_{n,{\rm reg}}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})

of the relations
T_{n}
are closable operators which satisfy

(7.2)

T_{m,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}T_{n,{\rm reg}},\quad m\leq n,

see Lemma
2.2
.
Hence, by Theorem
5.1
, there exists a closable linear operator

T_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm r})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm r}
is a Hilbert space,
such that

(7.3)

{\rm dom\,}T_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n,{\rm reg}}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(7.4)

T_{n,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}T_{\rm r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n,{\rm reg}}\varphi\|\nearrow\|T_{\rm r}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T_{\rm r}.

Moreover, the closures
(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})

are closed linear operators which satisfy

(7.5)

(T_{m,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**},\quad m\leq n,\quad\mbox{and}\quad(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm r})^{**},

see Proposition
6.1
. By the same proposition,
there exists a closed linear operator

S_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c}
is a Hilbert space,
such that

(7.6)

{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}^{**}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and which satisfies

(7.7)

(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}S_{\rm r}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm r})^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|\nearrow\|S_{\rm r}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}.

In fact,
{\rm dom\,}(T_{\rm r})^{**}\subset{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}
, while

\|S_{\rm r}\varphi\|=\|(T_{\rm r})^{**}\varphi\|
for
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}(T_{\rm r})^{**}
.

It follows from (
7.1
) that the closures
T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})

of
T_{n}
are closed relations which satisfy

(7.8)

T_{m}^{**}\prec_{c}T_{n}^{**}\quad m\leq n,

see (
2.3
).
Of course, by Lemma
2.2
also the regular parts of
T_{n}
satisfy such an inequality;
but this gives again (
7.5
), due to the identity

((T_{n})^{**})_{\rm reg}=(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**},

see (
1.3
).
Since the relation
T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
is closed, it follows that the product

H_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

is a nonnegative selfadjoint relation and
by Theorem
2.3
one sees that (
7.8
) implies

H_{m}\leq H_{n},\quad m\leq n.

Thus according to Theorem
3.2

there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation

H_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
which is the limit of the
relations
H_{n}
in the strong resolvent sense or, equivalently,
in the strong graph sense.

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of linear relations which satisfy (
7.1
).
Let
H_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the nonnegative selfadjoint relation, which is the limit
of the nondecreasing sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint
relations
T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
. Then
H_{\infty}
satisfies

(7.9)

{\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}^{**}:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and, furthermore,

(7.10)

(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|\nearrow\|(H_{\infty,{\rm op}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, the limit
S_{\rm r}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c})

of the sequence
(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})

in (
7.6
) and (
7.7
) satisfies

(7.11)

\|(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\varphi\|\nearrow\|S_{\rm r}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}={\rm dom\,}(H_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Consequently, there exists a partial isometry
U\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{K}}_{\rm c},{\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(7.12)

(H_{\infty,{\rm op}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=US_{\rm r}\quad\mbox{and}\quad H_{\infty,{\rm op}}=(S_{\rm r})^{*}S_{\rm r}.

Proof.

It is clear that the product
H_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

is a nonnegative selfadjoint relation. Furthermore,
the closures
T_{n}^{**}
of
T_{n}
satisfy the inequalities (
7.8
).
Therefore, the nonnegative selfadjoint relations

H_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
form a nondecreasing sequence
thanks to Theorem
2.3
.
Thus by Theorem
3.2
there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation

H_{\infty}
such that (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) hold. Remember that

H_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}^{**}=(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{*}(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**},

so that there exists a partial isometry
U_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{K}}_{n},{\mathfrak{H}})
, such that

(H_{n,{\rm op}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=U_{n}\,(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}.

In other words, (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) lead to (
7.9
) and (
7.10
).
Similarly, a comparison of (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) with
(
7.9
) and (
7.10
) shows that (
7.11
) holds.
Therefore, there exists a partial isometry
U\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{L}},{\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(H_{\infty,{\rm op}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=US_{\rm r}
, which is the first assertion in (
7.12
).
This identity shows that also the second assertion in (
7.12
) holds.
∎

Assume that the sequence
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
in Theorem
7.1

has an upper bound, i.e.,
there exists a linear relation
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, such that

(7.13)

T_{n}\prec_{c}T,\quad m\leq n.

For instance, if the sequence
T_{n}
consists of operators then
T
may be taken
as the limit of
T_{n}
by Theorem
5.1
. It follows from (
7.13
) that

T_{n,{\rm reg}}\prec_{c}T_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}.

With these upper bounds it follows for the closable limit
T_{\rm r}
of

that

T_{\rm r}\prec_{c}T_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and hence}\quad(T_{\rm r})^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}.

Consequently, for the closed limit
S_{\rm r}
of
(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}
one has via (

)

S_{\rm r}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm r})^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}.

8.
An example of a nondecreasing sequence

In order to illustrate the various possibilities of convergence a simple example
of a nondecreasing sequence will be presented.
Let
R\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a linear operator and define
the sequence of linear operators

T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, by

(8.1)

T_{n}=\sqrt{n}\,R.

Then it is clear from (

) that

\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm dom\,}T_{n}={\rm dom\,}R\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{n}\prec_{c}T_{n+1},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}},

so that (

) is satisfied.
Hence one can apply Theorem

to
determine the limit
T
of the sequence

.
It follows from (

) and (

) that

(8.2)

{\rm dom\,}T={{\rm ker\,}\,}R\quad\mbox{and}\quad T=O_{\,{{\rm ker\,}\,}R}.

In fact, it is clear that
T
is closable and singular, simultaneously, and that

(8.3)

T^{**}=O_{\,\overline{{{\rm ker\,}\,}}\,R}.

Moreover, observe that it follows from (
8.2
) and (

) that

T^{*}T={{\rm ker\,}\,}R\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}R)^{\perp}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T^{*}T^{**}={\rm\overline{ker}\,}R\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}R)^{\perp}.

Note that in the special case where

this illustrates
[

, Corollary 5.2.13]
.
If, in addition, the operator

is closable,
then all
T_{n}
in (

The closures
T_{n}^{**}
of

are given by

T_{n}^{**}=\sqrt{n}\,R^{**},

and it is clear that (
6.1
) is satisfied.
Hence one can apply Proposition
6.1
to obtain the closed limit
S

of the sequence
T_{n}^{**}
.
It follows from (
6.2
) and (
6.3
) that

(8.4)

{\rm dom\,}S={{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad S=O_{\,{{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**}}.

One sees directly from (

) that
T^{**}\subset S
,
which illustrates the situation in Proposition
6.1
.
The inclusion
T^{**}\subset S
is strict precisely
when
\overline{{{\rm ker\,}\,}}R\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**}
is strict.
As an example where the inclusion is strict, let
R
be an operator such that

R^{-1}
is an operator that is not closable, in which case

{{\rm ker\,}\,}R=\{0\}
and
{{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**}\neq\{0\}
.
Note that the nonnegative selfadjoint relation
S^{*}S
is given by

S^{*}S={{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**}\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}R^{**})^{\perp}.

as follows from (
8.4
).

Next consider the Lebesgue decomposition of

R=R_{\rm reg}+R_{\rm sing},\quad R_{\rm reg}=(I-P)R,\quad R_{\rm sing}=PR,

where
P
be the orthogonal projection form
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}R^{**}
.
Then the regular parts
T_{n,{\rm reg}}
of
T_{n}
in (
8.1
) are given by

T_{n,{\rm reg}}=\sqrt{n}\,R_{\rm reg},

and it is clear that (
7.2
) is satisfied.
For the closable limit
T_{\rm r}
of the sequence
T_{n,{\rm reg}}

it follows from (
7.3
) and (
7.4
) that

{\rm dom\,}T_{\rm r}={{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg}\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{\rm r}=O_{\,{{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg}}.

Since

one sees directly that

,
which is the general situation.
The inequality is strict precisely when
{{\rm ker\,}\,}R\subset{{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg}

Observe that

(T_{\rm r})^{*}T_{\rm r}={{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg}\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg})^{\perp}\quad\mbox{and}\quad(T_{\rm r})^{*}(T_{\rm r})^{**}={\rm\overline{ker}\,}R_{\rm reg}\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}R_{\rm reg})^{\perp}.

The closures of
T_{n,{\rm reg}}
are given by

(T_{n,\rm reg})^{**}=\sqrt{n}(R_{\rm reg})^{**},

) is satisfied.
For the closed limit
S_{\rm r}
of the sequence
(T_{n,{\rm reg}})^{**}

it follows from (

) and (
7.7
) that

{\rm dom\,}S_{\rm r}={{\rm ker\,}\,}(R_{\rm reg})^{**}\quad\mbox{and}\quad S_{\rm r}=O_{\,{{\rm ker\,}\,}(R_{\rm reg})^{**}}.

Therefore, one sees that

(8.5)

(S_{\rm r})^{*}S_{\rm r}={{\rm ker\,}\,}(R_{\rm reg})^{**}\times({{\rm ker\,}\,}(R_{\rm reg})^{**})^{\perp}.

Finally consider
T_{n}
as in (

)
with a general operator

.
Then the product relation

9.
A description of closed linear operators

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
be a sequence of operators
that satisfy (
5.1
). According to Corollary
6.2

there is a closed limit
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
which
satisfies (
6.4
) and (
6.5
).
This section contains some variations on this theme.

First it is shown that any nonnegative selfadjoint operator is rougly speaking
the limit of a certain class of nonnegative bounded linear operators.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a nonnegative selfadjoint operator.
Then there exists a sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint
operators
A_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(9.1)

(A_{m}\varphi,\varphi)\leq(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi),\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}},\quad m\leq n,

and

(9.2)

(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi)\nearrow\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Consider the spectral representation of the nonnegative selfadjoint operator
A

the Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{H}}
:

A=\int_{0}^{\infty}\lambda\,dE_{\lambda}.

By means of this representation
let the nonnegative selfadjoint operators
A_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be defined by

A_{n}=\int_{0}^{n}\lambda\,dE_{\lambda},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Then is is clear that
(A_{m}\varphi,\varphi)\leq(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi)
,
m\leq n
,
for all
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{H}}
. This gives (
9.1
).
By the construction of the sequence
A_{n}
one obtains

(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi)\nearrow\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}},

which gives (
9.2
).
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
9.1

there is some kind converse of Corollary
6.2
.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a closed linear operator.
Then there exists a sequence of linear operators

T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\rm\overline{dom}\,}T,{\mathfrak{H}})
, such that

(9.3)

\|T_{m}\varphi\|\leq\|T_{n}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T,\quad m\leq n,

and

(9.4)

\|T_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Proof.

The product relation
H=T^{*}T
is nonnegative and selfadjoint in
{\mathfrak{H}}

with
{\rm mul\,}H={\rm mul\,}T^{*}=({\rm dom\,}T)^{\perp}
. Then
H=A\hoplus(\{0\}\times({\rm dom\,}T)^{\perp}
,
where
A=H_{\rm reg}
is a nonnegative selfadjoint operator in
{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T
.
Then there exists a sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint
operators
A_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\rm\overline{dom}\,}T)
such that

(A_{m}\varphi,\varphi)\leq(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi),\quad\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T,\quad m\leq n,

and

(A_{n}\varphi,\varphi)\to\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|^{2},\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}T\subset{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T.

Due to
H=T^{*}T
and
A=H_{\rm reg}
one sees that

\|A^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|=\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi={\rm dom\,}A^{\frac{1}{2}}={\rm dom\,}T,

see Lemma
11.2
.
Finally define
T_{n}=A_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}
, so that (
9.3
) and (
9.4
) are satisfied.
∎

The last result in this section is a direct consequence of Proposition
9.2
;
it describes the closability of an operator in terms of a sequence of bounded linear operators;
see for the original statement
[
4
, Theorem 8.8, Theorem 8.9]
.

Corollary 9.3
.

Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a linear operator.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

S
is closable;

(ii)

there exists a sequence of linear operators
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\rm\overline{dom}\,}S,{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces, such that

(9.5)

\|T_{m}\varphi\|\leq\|T_{n}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}S,\quad m\leq n,

and

(9.6)

\|T_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|S\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S.

Proof.

(i)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
Let
S\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a closable operator and denote its closure by
T
.
Then
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
is a closed operator which extends
S
,
such that
{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T={\rm\overline{dom}\,}S
.
Now apply Proposition
9.2
.

(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i) Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{B}}({\rm\overline{dom}\,}S,{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
be a sequence
for which (
9.5
) holds. Then by Corollary
6.2

there exists a closed linear operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\rm\overline{dom}\,}S,{\mathfrak{K}})
,
such that

{\rm dom\,}T=\left\{\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}S:\,\sup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|<\infty\right\},

and which satisfies

\|T_{n}\varphi\|\nearrow\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm\overline{dom}\,}S.

Thanks to (
9.6
) one has
\|S\varphi\|=\|T\varphi\|
for all
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}S
.
Since
T
is closed if follows that

is closable.
∎

An application of these results can be found in
[
7
, Theorem 6.4]
,
where pairs of bounded linear operators are classified in terms of almost domination.

10.
Nonincreasing sequences of linear operators

In this section there is a brief review for the situation of nonincreasing sequences
of linear operators in the sense of contractive domination.
First recall the analog of the monotonicity principle in Theorem
3.2

for nonincreasing sequences; see
[
3
, Theorem 3.7]
.

Theorem 10.1
.

Let
K_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint relations
and assume they satisfy

K_{n}\leq K_{m},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation
K_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
with

(10.1)

K_{\infty}\leq K_{n},\quad n\in{\mathbb{N}}.

In fact,
K_{n}\to K_{\infty}
in the strong resolvent sense
or, equivalently, in the strong graph sense.
Moreover, the square root of
K_{\infty}
satisfies

(10.2)

{\rm ran\,}(K_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}=\left\{\varphi\in\bigcap_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm ran\,}(K_{n})^{\frac{1}{2}}:\,\lim_{n\to\infty}\|((K_{n})^{-{\frac{1}{2}}})_{\rm reg}\varphi\|<\infty\right\}

and, furthermore,

(10.3)

\|((K_{n})^{-{\frac{1}{2}}})_{\rm reg}\varphi\|\nearrow\|((K_{\infty})^{-{\frac{1}{2}}})_{\rm reg}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm ran\,}(K_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

A short proof is included for completeness.
By antitonicity, the sequence
(K_{n})^{-1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nondecreasing;
cf.
[
2
, Corollary 5.2.8]
.
Hence, by Theorem
3.2
, there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation,
say,
(K_{\infty})^{-1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, such that
(K_{\infty})^{-1}

is the limit of the sequence
(K_{n})^{-1}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
in the strong resolvent sense
or, equivalently, in the strong graph sense,
and
(K_{n})^{-1}\leq(K_{\infty})^{-1}
. Then, again by antitonicity,
K_{\infty}\leq K_{n}

and, moreover,
K_{\infty}
is the limit of the sequence
K_{n}

in the strong graph sense.
The rest of the statements is a direct translation of similar statements in Theorem
3.2
.
∎

Note that the multivalued parts of the relations
K_{n}
in Theorem
3.2

form a nonincreasing sequence.
If one of the relations
K_{n}
in Theorem
10.1
is an operator,
then all of its successors are operators and, ultimately,
the limit
K_{\infty}
is an operator.

Example 10.2
.

Let
A\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be a nonnegative selfadjoint operator or relation.
Then it is clear that the sequence
K_{n}=\frac{1}{n}A
of nonnegative selfadjoint operators or relations
is nonincreasing. Hence there exists a nonnegative selfadjoint relation
K_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

such that
K_{n}\to K_{\infty}
is the strong graph sense.
By means of Example
3.3
one sees immediately that

K_{\infty}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}A\times{\rm mul\,}A.

The following result is the analog of Theorem
5.1
for nonincreasing sequences
of linear operators. Due to the sequence being nonincreasing
there are no further convergence
restrictions for the limit as in Theorem
5.1
.

Theorem 10.3
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of linear operators which satisfy

(10.4)

T_{n}\prec_{c}T_{m},\quad m\leq n.

Then there exists a linear operator
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
,
where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space, such that

(10.5)

{\rm dom\,}T=\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}{\rm dom\,}T_{n},

and which satisfies

(10.6)

T\prec_{c}T_{n}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|T_{n}\varphi\|\searrow\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Proof.

Denote the right-hand side of (
10.5
) by
{\mathfrak{D}}
. Now let
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
,
then clearly
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T_{N}
for some
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
.
For all
n\geq N
one has
T_{n}\prec_{c}T_{N}
, which implies that
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T_{n}

for all
n\geq N
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{n}\varphi\|
exists by (
10.4
).
Hence for each
\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}
one may define

\|\varphi\|_{+}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|T_{n}\varphi\|.

Then
\|\cdot\|_{+}
generates a well-defined seminorm on the linear subspace
{\mathfrak{D}}
.
Let
(\cdot,\cdot)_{+}
be the corresponding semi-inner product.
By Lemma
4.1
there exists a linear operator
T
defined on

{\rm dom\,}T={\mathfrak{D}}\subset{\mathfrak{H}}
to a Hilbert space
{\mathfrak{K}}
such that

(\varphi,\psi)_{+}=(T\varphi,T\psi),\quad\varphi\in{\mathfrak{D}}.

This shows the assertion in (
10.6
).
∎

Now Theorem
10.3
will be applied under the assumption that
the linear operators
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
are closed.
Then the corresponding relations
K_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})

are nonnegative and selfadjoint.

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}}_{n})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}_{n}
are Hilbert spaces,
be a sequence of closed linear operators which satisfy (
10.4
)
and let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, where
{\mathfrak{K}}
is a Hilbert space,
be the limit operator satisfying (
10.5
) and (
10.6
).
Let
K_{\infty}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the nonnegative selfadjoint relation,
which is the limit of the nonincreasing sequence of nonnegative selfadjoint
relations
K_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
, so that
K_{\infty}
satisfies
(
10.2
) and (
10.3
).
Then
K_{\infty}
and
T
are connected via

(10.7)

K_{\infty}=T^{*}T^{**}.

Consequently, there exists a partial isometry
U\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(10.8)

(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}=U(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\quad\mbox{or}\quad(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Moreover, for the limit
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
one has

(a)

T
is closable if and only if
T\subset U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
;

(b)

T
is closed if and only if
T=U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
;

(c)

T
is singular if and only if
K_{\infty}
is singular.

Proof.

Let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be the limit operator in (
10.5
) and (
10.6
).
Recall from (
10.6
) that
T\prec_{c}T_{n}
. This leads to

T^{**}\prec_{c}(T_{n})^{**}=T_{n}
, which gives
T^{*}T^{**}\leq T_{n}^{*}T_{n}=K_{n}

by Theorem
2.3
.
Since this holds for all
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
one obtains

(10.9)

T^{*}T^{**}\leq K_{\infty}.

Moreover, recall from (
10.1
) that
K_{\infty}\leq K_{n}
, so that

(K_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}T_{n}
by Theorem
2.3
.
In particular, it follows that
(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}T_{n}
.
Hence one has

{\rm dom\,}T_{n}\subset{\rm dom\,}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|\leq\|T_{n}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T_{n}.

Clearly, with (
10.5
) this now leads to

{\rm dom\,}T\subset{\rm dom\,}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|\leq\inf_{n\in{\mathbb{N}}}\|T_{n}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T.

Thanks to (
10.6
) this reads

{\rm dom\,}T\subset{\rm dom\,}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\|(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|\leq\|T\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T,

or equivalently,
(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}T
.
Since closures and regular parts are preserved under the inequality,
this gives
(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}(T^{**})_{\rm reg}

or
(K_{\infty})^{\frac{1}{2}}\prec_{c}T^{**}
by Lemma
2.2
.
Therefore, one obtains

(10.10)

K_{\infty}\leq T^{*}T^{**}.

Combining the inequalities (
10.9
) and (
10.10
) leads to the inequalities

T^{*}T^{**}\leq K_{\infty}\leq T^{*}T^{**},

or, equivalently,

(T^{*}T^{**}-\lambda)^{-1}\leq(K_{\infty}-\lambda)^{-1}\leq(T^{*}T^{**}-\lambda)^{-1},\quad\lambda<0.

This shows that (
10.7
) holds.
Next (
10.8
) follows thanks to Lemma
11.2
.

Finally, the last assertions concerning the relationship between
T
and
K_{\infty}

will be discussed.

(a) If
T\subset U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then
T
is closable.
Conversely, if
T
is closable, then

T=T_{\rm reg}\subset(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

(b) If
T=U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
, then
T
is closed.
Conversely, if
T
is closed, then

T=(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=U^{*}(K_{\infty,{\rm reg}})^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

(c) If
T
is singular, then
T^{*}={\mathfrak{A}}\times{\mathfrak{B}}

where
{\mathfrak{A}}
and
{\mathfrak{B}}
are closed linear subspaces of
{\mathfrak{K}}
and
{\mathfrak{H}}
, respectively.
Hence
T^{**}={\mathfrak{B}}^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{A}}^{\perp}
, so that
T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{B}}^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{B}}

and
K_{\infty}
is singular.
Conversely, let
K_{\infty}=T^{*}T^{**}
be singular. Then
T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{B}}^{\perp}\times{\mathfrak{B}}

with a closed linear subspace
{\mathfrak{B}}
in
{\mathfrak{H}}
. Hence it follows that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}{\rm mul\,}T^{*}={\rm mul\,}T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{B}},\\
{{\rm ker\,}\,}T^{**}={{\rm ker\,}\,}T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{B}}^{\perp}.\end{array}\right.

Therefore
{\rm\overline{ran}\,}T^{*}=({{\rm ker\,}\,}T^{**})^{\perp}={\rm mul\,}T^{*}
, i.e.
T^{*}
and, hence, also
T
is singular.
∎

In the present circumstances there is in general no preservation of closedness
in Theorem
10.3
.
This will be shown in the following example; it is a simple adaptation of

[
3
, Example 4.5]
or
[
12
, p. 374]
.

Example 10.5
.

Let
T_{n}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{H}}\oplus{\mathbb{C}})
with
{\mathfrak{H}}=L^{2}(0,1)

be given as a column operator by
T_{n}={\rm col\,}(T_{n}^{1},T_{n}^{2})

(see
[
8
]
) with
the operators
T_{n}^{1}
and
T_{n}^{2}
given by

T_{n}^{1}f=\frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\,iDf,\quad f(1)=0,\quad\mbox{and}\quad T_{n}^{2}f=f(0).

Here
D
stands for the maximal differentiation operator in
L^{2}(0,1)
.
Then
T_{n}^{1}
is closed,
T_{n}^{2}
is singular, while the column
T_{n}
is closed.
It is clear that
T_{n}\prec_{c}T_{m}
,
m\leq n
, and the limit
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is given by
Tf=f(0)e
,
where the function
e\in{\mathfrak{H}}=L^{2}(0,1)
is defined by
e(x)=1
.
Moreover, the corresponding nonnegative selfadjoint relation
K_{n}=T_{n}^{*}T_{n}
is the operator
in
{\mathfrak{H}}=L^{2}(0,1)
given by

K_{n}f=-\frac{1}{n}D^{2}f,\quad f^{\prime}(0)=nf(0),\,\,f(1)=0.

The relations
K_{n}
form a sequence that is nonincreasing with the nonnegative selfadjoint limit
K_{\infty}

and, by Theorem
10.4
, one has

K_{\infty}=T^{*}T^{**}.

Now observe that
T^{*}=({\rm span\,}\{e\})^{\perp}\times\{0\}
and
T^{**}={\mathfrak{H}}\times{\rm span\,}\{e\}
,
so that
T
is a singular operator and, in fact
T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{H}}\times\{0\}
.
Hence it follows that

K_{\infty}=T^{*}T^{**}={\mathfrak{H}}\times\{0\}.

11.
Appendix: On the products
T^{*}T
and
T^{*}T^{**}

This appendix contains a number of properties of the
relations
T^{*}T
and
T^{*}T^{**}
when
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})

is a linear relation. The main emphasis is on the interplay with the regular parts
of these relations. For the convenience of the reader, the arguments are included.

Let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
, so that
T^{*}\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{H}})
is a closed linear relation.
The product
T^{*}T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is defined as

(11.1)

T^{*}T=\big\{\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in{\mathfrak{H}}\times{\mathfrak{H}}:\,\{f,h\}\in T,\,\{h,f^{\prime}\}\in T^{*}\,\,\mbox{for some}\,\,h\in{\mathfrak{K}}\big\}.

Hence, for the elements in the right-hand side of (
11.1
) it is clear that

(11.2)

(f^{\prime},f)=\|h\|^{2}.

It follows immediately from (
11.1
) and (
11.2
) that the relation
T^{*}T

is nonnegative. Moreover, it also follows from (
11.1
) and (
11.2
) that

(11.3)

{\rm mul\,}T^{*}T={\rm mul\,}T^{*}.

It is clear from
T\subset T^{**}
that the nonnegative relation

T^{*}T
has a nonnegative extension

T^{*}T^{**}
. Since
T^{**}
is closed the product
T^{*}T^{**}

is selfadjoint; cf.
[
2
, Lemma 1.5.8]
).
Moreover one sees that

(11.4)

T^{*}T\subset T^{*}T^{**}\subset(T^{*}T)^{*}.

In particular, it follows from (
11.4
) that the closure of
T^{*}T
satisfies

(11.5)

(T^{*}T)^{**}\subset T^{*}T^{**}.

However, in general, even when
T
is closable,
there is no equality in (
11.5
).

Recall the definition of the regular part
T_{\rm reg}
:

T_{\rm reg}=(I-P)T
where
P
is the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}
,
so that also
(T^{**})_{\rm reg}=(I-P)T^{**}
. This gives

(T_{\rm reg})^{*}=((T^{**})_{\rm reg})^{*}
, which by taking adjoints leads to the formal identity

(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=((T^{**})_{\rm reg})^{**}
. Note that
(T^{**})_{\rm reg}

is closed, so that
(T^{**})_{\rm reg}=(T_{\rm reg})^{**}
in (
1.3
) is clear.

There is an interesting interplay between linear relations and their regular parts
when forming quadratic combinations. Let
\{f,f^{\prime}\}\in T^{**}
and
\{g,g^{\prime}\}\in T^{*}
,
then by definition there is the identity

(11.6)

(g^{\prime},f)=(g,f^{\prime}).

Recall that the orthogonal projection
P
maps
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}T^{*}
,
and let
Q
be the orthogonal projection from
{\mathfrak{H}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{*}={\rm\overline{dom}\,}T^{**}
.
Therefore the identity (
11.6
) reads

(11.7)

(g^{\prime},(I-Q)f)=((I-P)g,f^{\prime}),

which can be rewritten in terms of the regular parts

(11.8)

((T^{*})_{\rm reg}g,f)=(g,(T_{\rm reg})^{**}f),\,\,\,f\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**},\,\,g\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*},

where the equality (
1.3
) has been used.
Likewise, there is the identity

(11.9)

((T^{*})_{\rm reg}g,f)=(g,T_{\rm reg}f),\,\,\,f\in{\rm dom\,}T,\,\,g\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*},

which also follows from (
11.6
) and (
11.7
).
The following lemma shows the various interrelationships.

Lemma 11.1
.

Let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a linear relation.
Then

(11.10)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\big\{\{\varphi,h\}\in T:\,h\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}\big\}\subset T_{\rm reg},\\
\big\{\{\varphi,h\}\in T^{**}:\,h\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}\big\}\subset(T^{**})_{\rm reg}=(T_{\rm reg})^{**},\end{array}\right.

and

(11.11)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}T^{*}T\subset T^{*}T_{\rm reg}=(T_{\rm reg})^{*}T_{\rm reg},\\
T^{*}T^{**}=T^{*}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=(T_{\rm reg})^{*}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, the multivalued parts in (
11.11
) satisfy

(11.12)

{\rm mul\,}T^{*}={\rm mul\,}(T_{\rm reg})^{*},

and, consequently,

(11.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(T^{*}T)_{\rm reg}\subset(T^{*})_{\rm reg}T_{\rm reg}=\big((T_{\rm reg})^{*}T_{\rm reg}\big)_{\rm reg},\\
(T^{*}T^{**})_{\rm reg}=(T^{*})_{\rm reg}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}=\big((T_{\rm reg})^{*}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\big)_{\rm reg}.\end{array}\right.

In particular,

(11.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\big(\big((T_{\rm reg})^{*}T_{\rm reg}\big)_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi\big)=(T_{\rm reg}\varphi,T_{\rm reg}\psi),\\
\hskip 136.5733pt\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}T_{\rm reg},\,\,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}T,\\
\big(\big((T_{\rm reg})^{*}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\big)_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi\big)=((T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi,(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\psi),\\
\hskip 136.5733pt\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}T^{**},\,\,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**}.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Due to
{\rm dom\,}T^{*}\subset{\rm\overline{dom}\,}T^{*}=({\rm mul\,}T^{**})^{\perp}
and (
1.3
)
one sees that (
11.10
) holds.
Hence it is clear that
T^{*}T\subset T^{*}T_{\rm reg}
.
With the orthogonal projection
P
from
{\mathfrak{K}}
onto
{\rm mul\,}T^{**}
, one sees that

T^{*}(I-P)T=T^{*}(I-P)^{2}T=((I-P)T)^{*}(I-P)T,

which completes the proof of the first part of (
11.11
).
Furthermore, replacing
T
by
T^{**}
in the first part of (
11.11
)
leads with (
1.3
) to the second part;
the original inclusion is now an identity since
T^{*}T^{**}
is selfadjoint.
The identity (
11.12
) is a consequence of (
11.11
) due to (
11.3
).
The consequence in (
11.13
) is obtained from (
11.12
) together with (
11.3
).

It follows from (
11.9
) with

f=\psi
and
g=T_{\rm reg}\varphi
that

\begin{split}((T^{*})_{\rm reg}T_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)=(T_{\rm reg}\varphi,T_{\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}T,\quad T_{\rm reg}\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}.\end{split}

Note that the conditions

\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T
and
T_{\rm reg}\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}

are equivalent to the condition
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}T_{\rm reg}
.
Thus, with (
11.13
), the first assertion in (
11.14
) has been shown.
Likewise, it follows from (
11.8
) with

f=\psi
and
g=(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi
that

\begin{split}&((T^{*})_{\rm reg}(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi,\psi)=((T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi,(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\psi),\\
&\hskip 170.71652pt\varphi,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**},\quad(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}.\end{split}

Note that the conditions

\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{**}
and
(T_{\rm reg})^{**}\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}

are equivalent to the condition
\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}T
, thanks to
(
11.11
). Thus, with (
11.13
),
the second assertion in (
11.14
) has been shown.
∎

There is a special, useful, case of Lemma
11.1
that deserves attention.
It is about the orthogonal operator part of
H=T^{*}T
when
T
is closed.

Lemma 11.2
.

Let
T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}},{\mathfrak{K}})
be a closed linear relation and let

H\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
be the nonnegative selfadjoint relation defined by
H=T^{*}T
.
Then

(11.15)

(H_{\rm reg}\varphi,\psi)=(T_{\rm reg}\varphi,T_{\rm reg}\psi),\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}T^{*}T,\,\,\psi\in{\rm dom\,}T,

and there exists a partial isometry
U\in{\mathbf{B}}({\mathfrak{K}},{\mathfrak{H}})
such that

(H_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}=UT_{\rm reg}.

Proof.

Recall that
H=T^{*}T\in{\mathbf{L}}({\mathfrak{H}})
is nonnegative and selfadjoint and that

{\rm mul\,}H={\rm mul\,}T^{*}
. It follows from Lemma
11.1
that the identity
(
11.15
) is satisfied. Therefore

\|(H_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}\varphi\|=\|T_{\rm reg}\varphi\|,\quad\varphi\in{\rm dom\,}H_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}H={\rm dom\,}T^{*}T={\rm dom\,}(T_{\rm reg})^{*}T_{\rm reg}.

It is clear that
{\rm dom\,}H_{\rm reg}={\rm dom\,}(T_{\rm reg})^{*}T_{\rm reg}
is a core for
(H_{\rm reg})^{\frac{1}{2}}

and for
T_{\rm reg}
; cf.
[
2
, Lemma 1.5.10]
. Hence the assertion follows.
∎

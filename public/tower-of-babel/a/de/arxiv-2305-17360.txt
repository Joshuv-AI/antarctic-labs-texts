Introduction

A fundamental topic in graph theory is that of finding conditions under which a graph is Hamiltonian.
The classical result of Dirac
[
4
]
states that any graph on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree at least
\frac{n}{2}
contains a Hamilton cycle.
The
r
-th power of a graph

is the graph obtained from
H
by joining every pair of vertices with distance at most
r
in
H

As a natural generalization of Dirac’s theorem, the Pósa–Seymour Conjecture received much attention, which predicts that an
n
-vertex graph
G
satisfying
\delta(G)\geq\frac{r}{r+1}n
contains an
r
-th power of a Hamilton cycle.
There had been many excellent results (see e.g.
[
7

,
[
8
]
,
[

]

[
14

in 1997.

Note that the minimum degree condition is sharp as seen by near-balanced complete
(r+1)
-partite graphs.
As for many other problems in the area, this extremal example has the characteristic that it contains a large independence set.
There has thus been significant interest in seeking variants of classical results in extremal graph theory, where one now forbids the host graph from containing a large independent set.
Indeed, nearly 50 years ago, Erdős, Hajnal, Sós and Szemerédi
[
5
]
initiated the study of the Turán problem under the additional assumption of small independence number.
Formally, given a graph

and natural numbers

Erdős–Sós
[

and Erdős–Hajnal–Sós–Szemerédi

\varrho(K_{r})=\begin{cases}1-\frac{2}{r-1}\hfill\text{ if $r$ is odd,}\\
1-\frac{6}{3r-4}\hfill\text{ otherwise.}\end{cases}

More recently, there has been interest in similar questions but where now one seeks for a spanning subgraph in an

1.1
Main results and Lower bound constructions

Our main result is the following, which in particular resolves Conjecture
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Given
\mu>0
and
r\in\mathbb{N}
with
r\geq 2
, there exists
\alpha>0
such that the following holds for sufficiently large

.
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\delta(G)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{r}+\mu\right)n
and
\alpha(G)\leq\alpha n

Then

contains an

-th power of a Hamilton cycle.

The minimum degree condition in Conjecture

is asymptotically best possible, e.g. by considering a union of two disjoint cliques of almost equal size.
Moreover, we cannot expect a result where

Proposition 1.3
.

Given
\alpha>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an
n
-vertex graph

with
\delta(G)\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2})n-1
and
\alpha(G)\leq\alpha n
such that

has no square of a Hamilton cycle.

For general lower bound on the minimum degree condition, we prove the following proposition by the

Proposition 1.4
.

Given
\mu,\alpha>0
and

with
r\geq 3
, the following holds for sufficiently large

. There exists an

-vertex graph

with

\delta(G)\geq\frac{2-\varrho(K_{r})}{3-2\varrho(K_{r})}n-\mu n
and
\alpha(G)\leq\alpha n
such that

has no

-th power of a Hamilton cycle.

The proofs of Proposition
1.3
and Proposition
1.4
are given in Subsection

Note that

complements Theorem

For general

By the aforementioned result of Knierim and Su

We suspect that the “connecting barrier” in Proposition

indeed discloses the best possible minimum degree condition forcing an

-th power of a Hamilton cycle for every

Given

such that the following holds for sufficiently large

The smallest open case is

1.2
Connecting barrier

To obtain an

-th power of a Hamilton cycle, an essential condition is that every two vertices can be connected via an

Motivated by this, we give two constructions for the proofs of Proposition

In the proofs, we apply a degree form of Ramsey–Turán number which was recently studied in

1.3
Proof strategy

Our proof makes use of the absorption method and builds on the techniques developed in
[
16
]
.
The absorption method was introduced by Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi about a decade ago in
[
18
]
.
Since then, it has turned out to be an important tool for studying the existence of spanning structures in graphs, digraphs and hypergraphs.
Under this framework, we find the desired
r
-th power of a Hamilton cycle by four lemmas: the Absorber Lemma (Lemma
2.2
), the Reservoir Lemma (Lemma
2.4
), the Almost Path-cover Lemma (Lemma
2.5
) and the Connecting Lemma (Lemma
2.6
).

The main difficulties lie in proving a connecting lemma.
For every two copies of
K_{r}
, say
X
and
Y
,
Komlós, Sáközy and Szemerédi
[
14
]
also proved such a connecting lemma: they first use the regularity method and transfer the problem to the
reduced graph
, and then use the
cascade
technique to do the connection via an
r
-th power of a walk.
Then the regularity method can find an embedding of an
r
-path that connects
X
and
Y
.
Under our weaker minimum degree condition, using their method we can make such a connection in the reduced graph via an
(r-1)
-power
of a path.
Then some dedicate embedding using the regularity and the assumption of sublinear independence number

can help us to achieve the connection by an
r
-path of constant length.
To reduce the technicality, we first present an easy way to connect (in the reduced graph) via a short sequence of copies of
K_{r+1}
, where consecutive pairs of them share
r-1
vertices; secondly, to ease the embedding process, we introduce the notion of

(see Definition

) and use it to model the way how an
r

For the absorbing path, we first choose a random set
A
of vertices and find an
r
-path
P
where one can free any subset of vertices of
A
.
Then the vertices of
A
can be used to help on connecting
r
-paths in later steps.

Here is a sketch the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
We apply the Reservoir Lemma to obtain a random set
A
.
Then we apply the Absorber Lemma, and find a collection of vertex-disjoint absorbers in
G-A
, one for each vertex of
A
.
By applying the Connecting Lemma, we connect all these
|A|
absorbers one by one by using the vertices in
V(G)\backslash A
, and obtain an
r
-path, say
P_{1}
, which contains all these
|A|
absorbers and every vertex in
A
.
Note that the
r
-path
P_{1}
is an absorbing path in the sense that we can remove any subset of vertices of
A
while keeping the ends of the
r
-path unchanged.
In
G-V(P_{1})
, we apply the Almost Path-cover Lemma, and obtain a family of vertex-disjoint
r
-paths, say
\{P_{2},P_{3},\dots,P_{\ell}\}
for some
\ell=o(n)
, and let
R:=V(G)\setminus\bigcup^{\ell}_{i=1}V(P_{i})
.
By the choice of

, every vertex in
G
has many neighbors in

and thus many absorbers in
A
.
We then choose a collection of vertex-disjoint short

-paths that contain the vertices of
R

A
– this can be achieved by just choosing vertex-disjoint absorbers for vertices of
R
.
By applying the Connecting Lemma, we connect all these
|R|
(short)
r
-paths and all these

(long)
r
-paths one by one to an

-th power of a cycle by using the vertices in

Since all the vertices used are from

), we obtain an
r
-th power of a Hamilton cycle in

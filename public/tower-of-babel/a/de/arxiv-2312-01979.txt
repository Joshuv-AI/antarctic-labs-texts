Radio emission in the Jovian magnetosphere driven by the quasi-linear diffusion
A. Chkhaidze, E. Kalichava

Abstract




Abstract

Electrons traveling along magnetic field lines from Jupiter to Io, driven by quasi-linear diffusion (QLD), emit synchrotron radiation.
By using the small angle approximation, the kinematic equation for the particle distribution gives us the mean pitch angle value.
We described how these ultrarelativistic electrons emit radiation in the GHz range when subjected to external forces.
Despite varying Lorentz factors, we showed that the total QLD-related radiation power is in multiples of
10^{17}ergs/s
.
However, when this radiation reaches the Earth’s surface, its intensity falls within the range of around
10^{-7}erg/(s\cdot cm^{2})
.

Introduction

The paper is organized as follows: in Sec. 2 we summarize the general theory of resonant acceleration. In Sec. 3, we apply the mechanism to Jupiter-Io system, derive and discuss the principal results. In Sec. 4, we put our results in perspective.

\gamma mc^{2}n\leq\dfrac{B^{2}}{8\pi}

Condition (4) will restrict electron beam concentration. According to

during the frozen-in condition plasma may be subjected to the anomalous Doppler effect. This induces unstable resonance cyclotron modes.

\omega-k_{||}c-k_{x}u_{x}-\dfrac{\omega_{B}}{\gamma_{b}}=0

(5)

Where

is beam Lorentz factor.

\omega_{B}

\begin{array}[]{l}u_{x}\approx\dfrac{c^{2}\gamma_{b}}{\rho\,\omega_{B}}\\
\\
\omega_{B}=\dfrac{eB}{mc}\end{array}

(6)

Due to the resonance we may have QLD, which will preserve non-zero pitch angle

]

[
Osmanov(2014)

\dfrac{\partial f}{\partial t}+\dfrac{1}{p_{\bot}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{\bot}}(p_{\bot}[F_{\bot}+G_{\bot}]f^{0})=

(7)

=\dfrac{1}{p_{\bot}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{\bot}}\bigg(p_{\bot}D_{\bot,\bot}\dfrac{\partial f^{0}}{\partial p_{\bot}}\bigg)

\begin{array}[]{l}D_{\bot,\bot}=D\delta E_{k}^{2}\\

\newline

\newline
\\
\delta=\dfrac{\omega_{P}^{2}}{4\omega_{B}^{2}\gamma_{p}^{3}}\\
\newline
\\
\omega_{p}=\sqrt{\dfrac{4\pi n_{p}^{2}}{m}}\end{array}

(8)

Where
D_{\bot,\bot}
denotes the diffusion coefficient and
D=\dfrac{e^{2}}{8c}
. Here we also used
E_{k}^{2}
, which is the wave energy density per unit wavelength. Assuming that the half of the beam’s energy is converted to that of the waves, we get the formula mentioned above.
\omega_{p}
is the Langmuir frequency of the electron-positron plasma component and

is the corresponding number density.
For small angle approximation force due to the synchrotron radiation may be written as:
[

]

\begin{array}[]{l}F_{\bot}=-\alpha_{s}\dfrac{p_{\bot}}{p_{\parallel}}\left(1+\dfrac{p_{\bot}^{2}}{m^{2}c^{2}}\right)\\
\newline
\\
F_{\parallel}=-\dfrac{\alpha_{s}}{m^{2}c^{2}}p_{\bot}^{2}\\
\newline
\\
\alpha_{s}=\dfrac{2e^{2}\omega_{B}^{2}}{3c^{2}}\end{array}

(9)

Where

and
p_{||}
are transversal and longitudinal components of momentum of electron. It should be noted that adiabatic invariant
I=\dfrac{3cp_{\bot}^{2}}{2eB}

[
Lominadze(1983)
]
is conserved because magnetic field changes insignificantly during the cyclotron period. As a result of this, particles also undergo the force responsible for the conservation of the adiabatic invariant.

\begin{array}[]{l}G_{\bot}=-\dfrac{cp_{\bot}}{\rho}\\
\newline
\\
G_{\parallel}=\dfrac{cp_{\bot}^{2}}{\rho p_{\parallel}}\end{array}

(10)

Where
\rho
is curvature radius of the trajectory. Also we assume that different currents of electrons have the same energy
[
Gogaberishvili(2021)
]

n_{p}\gamma_{p}=n_{b}\gamma_{b}

We take into account that
\dfrac{{F_{\bot}}}{{G_{\bot}}}

\dfrac{F_{\bot}}{G_{\bot}}\approx 1.3311\cdot 10^{-8}\bigg(\dfrac{\rho}{2.1297\cdot 10^{10}cm}\bigg)\cdot

\gamma={40,60,80,100}

\dfrac{F_{\bot}}{G_{\bot}}<<1

F_{\bot}

\approx 10^{0}

\approx 10^{5}

\dfrac{1}{p_{\bot}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{\bot}}\left(p_{\bot}\left[G_{\bot}\right]f^{0}\right)=\dfrac{1}{p_{\bot}}\dfrac{\partial}{\partial p_{\bot}}\left(p_{\bot}D_{\bot,\bot}\dfrac{\partial f^{0}}{\partial p_{\bot}}\right)

Solving (13) for the distribution function we get the following result:

f^{0}\left(p_{\bot}\right)=C\exp\left(-\left(\dfrac{p_{\bot}}{\sqrt{\dfrac{2\rho D_{{\bot,\bot}}}{c}}}\right)^{2}\right)

Using (14) we can calculate the mean pitch angle at the starting point of trajectory.

\overline{\psi_{0}}=\dfrac{\overline{p_{\bot_{0}}}}{p_{\parallel}}=\dfrac{\int_{0}^{\infty}{p_{\bot}f^{0}\left(p_{\bot}\right)p_{\bot}}}{p_{\parallel}\int_{0}^{\infty}{f^{0}\left(p_{\bot}\right)p_{\bot}}}=\dfrac{\sqrt{\dfrac{2\rho D_{\bot,\bot}}{c}}}{p_{\parallel}\sqrt{\pi}}=\dfrac{p_{\bot_{0}}}{p_{\parallel}}

(15)

Using the fact that adiabatic invariant is conserved, we can calculate how the pitch angle changes along the whole trajectory.

p_{\bot}(s)=\sqrt{\dfrac{B(s)}{B_{0}}}p_{\bot 0}

(16)

\psi(s)=\sqrt{\dfrac{B(s)}{B_{0}}}\overline{\psi_{0}}

(17)

4
Conclusion

To conclude, we wanted to calculate frequency and the intensity range of QLD related synchrotron radiation from ultralrelativistic electrons in Jupiter-Io system.

We started by modeling Jupiter’s magnetic field and the concentration of the corotating electrons. As these particles move along the trajectory, they undergo the effect of forces. We numerically calculated the results of these effects and showed how the equation for distribution function simplified. We also showed would would be range of the frequency of emitted photons.

Using the data for FAST telescope we determined that the radiation spectrum matched the detectable frequency range. Also the power generated by the radiation would be sufficient to surpass the power threshold, meaning that the FAST telescope will be able to detect the radiation.

To summarise, we determined what would be the frequency range of the emission generated by ultrarelativistic electrons moving from Jupiter to Io torus if their nonzero pitch angles were sustained by QLD. Our results came in agreement with the empirical data. Finally, we numerically calculated the total power and the intensity on earth’s surface.

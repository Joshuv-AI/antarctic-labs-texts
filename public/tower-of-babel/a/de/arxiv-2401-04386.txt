Endomorphisms of a variety of Ueno type and Kawaguchi-Silverman Conjecture Thanks: The author is partially supported by JSPS Grant-in-Aid (A) 15H05738, JSPS Grant-in-Aid (B) 15H03611 and NCTS scholar program.

Abstract
. We first show that the monoid of separable surjective self morphisms of a variety of Ueno type coincides with the group of automorphisms. We also give an explicit description of the automorphism group. As applications, we confirm Kawaguchi Silverman Conjecture for automorphisms of a variety of Ueno type and some Calabi-Yau threefolds, defined over ℚ ¯ \overline{{\mathbb{Q}}} .

1.2.
Kawaguchi-Silverman Conjecture (KSC) for a variety of Ueno type

Let
V
be a normal projective variety defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
. Unless stated otherwise,
x\in V
means that
x
is a closed point of
V
or equivalently,
x\in V(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
. Kawaguchi-Silverman Conjecture (KSC) for
f
asks a relation between the dynamical degree
d_{1}(f)
, which is one of the most basic complex-dynamical or algebro-dynamical invariants that measures algebro-geometric complexity of
f
, and the arithmetic degree function
a_{f}(x)
(
x\in V
), which is an arithmetic dynamical invariant of
f
that measures arithmetic complexity of
f
defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. See
[
KS16b
, Conjecture 6]
for the original statement and excellent surveys
[
Ma23
]
,
[
MZ23
]
and references therein for current status on KSC (See also Subsection
3.1
for a brief account relevant to us). It is known that

1\leq a_{f}(x)\leq d_{1}(f)

for all
x\in V
when
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
(
[
KS16a
, Theorem 3]
and
[
Ma20
]
). For
x\in V
, we denote by

O_{f}(x):=\{f^{n}(x)\,|\,n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}\}

the forward orbit of
x\in V
under
f
. KSC for
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
is the following
[
KS16b
, Conjecture 6]
:

Conjecture 1.6
.

Let
V
be a normal projective variety defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
. Let
x\in V
. Then, if
O_{f}(x)
is Zariski dense in
V
, then
a_{f}(x)=d_{1}(f)
.

Conjecture
1.6
obviously holds if
d_{1}(f)=1
or if there is no
x\in V
with Zariski dense
O_{f}(x)
. As in
[
CLO22
]
, we say that KSC holds
non-trivially and non-vacuously
for
{\rm End}\,(V)
if KSC holds for all elements of
{\rm End}\,(V)
and there are
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
and
x\in V
such that
d_{1}(f)>1
and
O_{f}(x)
is Zariski dense in
V
.

As the first application of Theorem
1.4
and its proof, we show the following:

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
X_{k}^{n}
be a variety of Ueno type over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with
n\geq 3
. Then KSC holds for
{\rm End}\,(X_{k}^{n})={\rm Aut}\,(X_{k}^{n})
non-trivially and non-vacuously.

We give a uniform proof of Theorem
1.7
for
n\geq 3
in Subsection
3.2
by reducing to KSC for an abelian variety due to Kawaguchi-Silverman and Silverman (
[
KS16a
, Theorem 4]
,
[
Si17
, Theorem 1.2]
), via Theorem
1.4
and its proof. It is also worth noticing that Meng and Zhang show that KSC holds for endomorphisms of a
non-resolved

{\mathbb{Q}}
-abelian variety which is by definition a normal projective variety which has an étale in codimension one covering by an abelian variety
[
MZ22
, Theorem 2.8]
.

The most interesting case in Theorem
1.7
is the case where
X_{k}^{n}
is rationally connected. KSC for
{\rm End}\,(V)
is known to be true for any smooth projective rationally connected variety
V
with inter-amplified endomorphism
f_{0}
by
[
MY22
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
MZ22
]
and
[
Yo21
]
). As necessarily
f_{0}\not\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(V)
, our

has no inter-amplified endomorphism by Theorem
1.4
.

1.3.
KSC for a Calabi-Yau threefold with
c_{2}
-contraction

By a complex Calabi-Yau threefold, we mean a smooth projective threefold defined over
{\mathbb{C}}
with
{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(K_{X})\simeq{\mathcal{O}}_{X}
and the underlying analytic space
X^{{\rm an}}
is simply-connected, i.e.,
\pi_{1}(X^{\rm an})=1
. We call

defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
a Calabi-Yau threefold, if
X\times_{{\rm Spec}\,\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}{\rm Spec}\,{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex Calabi-Yau threefold.

Note that
{\rm End}\,(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
for a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
(cf. Remark
2.2
(1)). Let
K=\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. KSC for
{\rm End}\,(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
of a Calabi-Yau threefold

is only interested in the case where
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
is an infinite group, as otherwise
d_{1}(f)=1
for all
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\,(X)
. In such a case, we have a non-zero nef real class

with
(c_{2}(X).x)_{X}=0
(
[
Wi97
]
, see also
[
Mi87
]
for pseudo-effectivity of

).

We call a surjective morphism

from a Calabi-Yau threefold to a normal projective variety
W
with connected fibers a (non-trivial)
c_{2}
-contraction if
(c_{2}(X).f^{*}H)_{X}=0
for some ample Cartier divisor
H
on
W
and
\dim W>0
, possibly
f
is birational. (
[
OS01
]
, see also
[
Og24
]
for updated description). So, KSC for
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
of a Calabi-Yau threefolds
X
over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with

-contraction is of interest.

Actually
[
LS21
, Theorem 1.8]
considered KSC for Calabi-Yau threefolds

with
c_{2}
-contraction and asserted the first statement of the following:

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with a

-contraction. Then KSC holds for
{\rm Aut}\,(X)
. Moreover, KSC holds non-trivially and non-vacuously if

has a birational

-contraction.

Unfortunately, proof of
[
LS21
, Theorem 1.8]
is based on a
claim that
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
is finite when

has a birational

-contraction
, which clearly contradicts to the second statement of Theorem

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

The goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.4
.

For a normal projective variety
V
, we denote by
N^{1}(V)
the free
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module consisting of the numerical equivalence classes of Cartier divisors on
V
. We denote
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}:=N^{1}(V)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
,
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{R}}}:=N^{1}(V)\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{R}}
and so on. Unless stated otherwise, curves and subvarieties of
V
are proper ones.

The following general lemma should be well-known. Our proof here is essentially the same as in
[
Fu02
, Lemma 2.3]
:

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
V
be a normal projective variety and
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
, i.e.,
f:V\to V
is a separable surjective self morphism of
V
. Then

(1)

The natural homorphism
f^{*}:N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}\to N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is
an isomorphism.

(2)

f
is a finite morphism.

(3)

If in addition that the canonical divisor
K_{V}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
0
, then
f
is étale in codimension one, that is, there is a closed algebraic set
S\subset V
of codimension
\geq 2
such that
f|_{V\setminus f^{-1}(S)}:V\setminus f^{-1}(S)\to V\setminus S
is étale.

Proof.

Since
V
is projective, for each irreducible projective curve
C\subset V
, there are a curve
C^{\prime}
and a positive integer
m
such that
f_{*}(C^{\prime})=mC
as one cycles. Thus
f^{*}
is injective by the projection formula. Indeed, if
f^{*}D=0
in
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
, then

0=(f^{*}D.C^{\prime})_{V}=(D.f_{*}C^{\prime})_{V}=m(D.C)_{V}\,\,{{\rm hence}}\,\,(D.C)_{V}=0

for all irreducible curves
C\subset V
. Thus
D=0
in
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
by definition. Since
N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-vector space of finite dimension, the linear map
f^{*}
is then an isomorphism. This shows (1).

Since
V
is projective, if
f
is not finite, then there is an irreducible curve
C\subset V
such that
f(C)
is a point. Then
(f^{*}D.C)_{V}=0
for all
D\in N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
by the projection formula. On the other hand, since
V
is projective, there is a hyperplane class
H\in N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and we have
(H.C)_{V}>0
, a contradiction to the surjectivity of
f^{*}
in (1). This proves (2).

Let us show (3). Let

V^{0}:=V\setminus(f({\rm Sing}\,V)\cup{\rm Sing}\,V)),

V^{00}:=V\setminus f^{-1}(f({\rm Sing}\,V)\cup{\rm Sing}\,V)).

Since
V
is normal and
f
is finite and surjective,
V\setminus V^{0}
and
V\setminus V^{00}
are closed subsets of codimension
\geq 2
in
V
and the morphism
f^{00}:V^{00}\to V^{0}
induced from
f
is a finite surjective morphism. Here
V^{00}
and
V^{0}
are also smooth. As
K_{V}
is
{\mathbb{Q}}
-linearly equivalent to
0
, it follows that there is a regular nowhere vanishing pluricanonical form
\omega
on
V^{0}
. Then
(f^{00})^{*}\omega
is a regular pluricanonical form and it is not identically
0
on
V^{00}
as
f
is separable. Then
(f^{00})^{*}\omega
is also nowhere vanishing on
V^{00}
. In particular, the Jacobian divisor of
f^{00}
has to be zero. Since
V^{00}
and
V^{0}
are smooth, it follows that
f^{00}
is étale. This proves (3).
∎

Remark 2.2
.

(1)

If
X

is

(2)

We use the notation in Definition
1.1
. The multiplication morphism by
2
on
A_{2}^{n}
(
n\geq 2
) induces a finite surjective morphism
[2]:\overline{X}_{2}^{n}\to\overline{X}_{2}^{n}
. The morphism
[2]
is not étale even though
2K_{\overline{X}_{2}^{n}}
is linearly equivalent to
0
. One can also check directly that
[2]
does not lifts to
{\rm End}\,(X_{2}^{n})
, which is also a consequence of Theorem
1.4
when
n\geq 3
.

From now, let
n\geq 3
and
X:=X_{k}^{n}
be a variety of Ueno type. For ease of notation, we also set

E:=E_{k}\,\,,\,\,\sigma:=\sigma_{k}^{n}\,\,,\,\,A:=A_{k}^{n}\,\,,\,\,\overline{X}:=\overline{X}_{k}^{n}\,\,,\,\,\pi:=\pi_{k}^{n}\,\,,\,\,\nu:=\nu_{k}^{n}.

Let
{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}
(
1\leq i\leq m
) be the irreducible components of the exceptional set
{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
of
\nu
. Then

{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)=\cup_{i=1}^{m}{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\,\,,\,\,{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}

and
P_{i}:=\nu({{\mathcal{E}}}_{i})
is a point of
\overline{X}
and

{\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}.

The following elementary observation is crucial in our proof of Theorem
1.4
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\varphi:{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\to X
be a non-constant morphism. Then
\varphi({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})={{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}
for some
i
.

Proof.

Set
D:=\varphi({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})
with reduced structure. Since the Picard number of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
is
1
,
D
is projective and
\varphi
is not constant, it follows that
\dim D=n-1
and therefore
D
is
a Cartier divisor on
X
.

Assume that
D\cap{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}=\emptyset
for all
i
. Then
\nu(D)\subset\overline{X}\setminus{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})
. Since

\pi^{0}:A^{0}:=A\setminus\pi^{-1}({\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X}))\to\overline{X}^{0}:=\overline{X}\setminus{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})

induced from
\pi
is étale, whereas
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
has no non-trivial finite étale cover, the fiber product
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\times_{\overline{X}^{0}}A^{0}
, under
\pi^{0}
and
\nu\circ\varphi
, is a disjoint union of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
. From each factor
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\times_{\overline{X}^{0}}A^{0}
, we have a non-constant morphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\to A^{0}\subset A
via the natural morphism of the fiber product. However, this is impossible as any morphism from
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
to
A
is constant by the universality of the Albanese morphism and the fact that the Albanese variety of
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
is a point by
H^{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{n-1},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}})=0
.

Hence there is
i
, say
1
, such that
D\cap{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\not=\emptyset
. Then,
D|_{{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}}
is either a non-zero effective Cartier divisor on
{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
or
D={{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
. Thus

\dim\,D\cap{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\geq n-2\geq 1

by
n\geq 3
. In particular, there is an irreducible curve
C\subset D\cap{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
.

Let
\overline{X}_{1}
be the blow up of
\overline{X}
at all
P_{j}
with
j\geq 2
except
P_{1}
. Then
\overline{X}_{1}
is projective and we have a birational morphism

\nu_{1}:X\to\overline{X}_{1}

such that
X\setminus{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}\simeq\overline{X}_{1}\setminus\nu_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}_{1})
and
\nu_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}_{1})
is a point, say again
P_{1}
, by slight abuse of language. Let
H
be a very ample line bundle on
\overline{X}_{1}
. Then, as
C\subset{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
and
\nu_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}_{1})
is a point, we have

(C.\nu_{1}^{*}H)_{X}=((\nu_{1})_{*}C.H)_{\overline{X}_{1}}=0.

Let
Q_{0}\in C\subset D
and
Q\in D
and choose a connected curve
C_{Q}\subset D
such that
Q_{0},Q\in C_{Q}
. Since we have a surjective morphism
{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}\to D
, it follows that any two curves on
D
are proportional. Then
C_{Q}=aC
in
{\rm N}_{1}\,(D)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}={\rm N}^{1}(D)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}^{*}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. As then

((\nu_{1})_{*}(C_{Q}).H)_{\overline{X}_{1}}=(C_{Q}.\nu_{1}^{*}H)_{X}=(C_{Q}.\nu_{1}^{*}H|_{D})_{D}=a(C.\nu_{1}^{*}H|_{D})_{D}=a(C.\nu_{1}^{*}H)_{X}=0,

it follows that
\nu_{1}(C_{Q})
is a point. Hence

\nu_{1}(Q)=\nu_{1}(C_{Q})=\nu_{1}(Q_{0})=\nu_{1}(C)=\nu_{1}({{\mathcal{E}}}_{1})=P_{1},

and thefore
D\subset{{\mathcal{E}}}_{1}
. Hence
D={\mathcal{E}}_{1}
as both are irreducible of the same dimension. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Not only the proof but also the statement of Lemma
2.3
is not valid when
n=2
. Indeed, the image of the diagonal
\Delta\subset A_{k}^{2}
under the natural map

is
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
which does not coincide any
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. In fact,

has infinitely many rational curves, as

is either a rational surface or a Kummer K3 surface of product type.

Let
f\in{\rm End}\,(X)
. Our first goal is to show that
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

f({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))\subset{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
.

Proof.

f({\mathcal{E}}_{i})
is not a point by Lemma
2.1
(2). Since
{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\simeq{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1}
, we may apply Lemma
2.3
for
f|_{{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}}:{{\mathcal{E}}}_{i}\to X
to get the result.
∎

Let
\overline{f}:=\nu\circ f\circ\nu^{-1}
. Then
\overline{f}
is a dominant rational map from
\overline{X}
to
\overline{X}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

\overline{f}\in{\rm End}\,(\overline{X})
.

Proof.

By definition,
\overline{f}
is a morphism on
\overline{X}\setminus{\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}
. By Lemma
2.5
, for each
P_{i}\in{\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}
, there is
j
such that
f(\nu^{-1}(P_{i}))={\mathcal{E}}_{j}
. Then

\overline{f}(P_{i})=\nu\circ f\circ\nu^{-1}(P_{i})=\nu({\mathcal{E}}_{j})=P_{j}.

Hence the rational map
\overline{f}
is actually a morphism, as
\overline{X}
is normal. ∎

Lemma 2.7
.

f^{-1}({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))\subset{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
.

Proof.

Otherwise, there is an irreducible component, say
D
, of
f^{-1}({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))
such that
D\not\subset{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
. Here
D
is of dimension
n-1
as
f^{-1}({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))
is the support of the Cartier divisor
f^{*}({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))
. Thus
\nu(D)
is of dimension
n-1\geq 2
by
D\not\subset{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
. However, then we would have

\overline{f}(\nu(D))=\nu\circ f(D)\subset\nu({\rm Exc}\,(\nu))=\cup_{i=1}^{m}P_{i}.

This contradicts to Lemma
2.1
(2). Hence the results follows.
∎

Since we do
not
yet know
\overline{f}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\overline{X}
, the following lemma is not trivial:

Lemma 2.8
.

\overline{f}({\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X}))={\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})
and

\overline{f}^{-1}({\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X}))={\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})
.

Proof.

Let
S:=\{{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be the set of irreducible components of
{\rm Exc}\,(\nu)
. By Lemmas
2.5
and
2.7
, the correspondences
f
and
f^{-1}
from
X
to
X
induce correspondences
f|_{S}
and
f^{-1}|_{S}
from
S
to
S
. By Lemma
2.7
,
f|_{S}
is a surjective morphism. Thus both
f|_{S}
and
f^{-1}|_{S}
are bijective morphisms, as
S
is a finite set. Since
\overline{f}=\nu\circ f\circ\nu^{-1}
, it follows that
\overline{f}|_{{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})}
and
\overline{f}^{-1}|_{{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})}
are bijective morphisms from
{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})
to
{\rm Sing}\,(\overline{X})
, which are iniverse to each other. ∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
f\in{\rm End}\,(X)
. Let
\overline{f}\in{\rm End}\,(\overline{X})
be as in Lemma
2.6
. Then

(1)

There is
f_{A}\in{\rm Aut}\,(A)
such that
\pi\circ f_{A}=\overline{f}\circ\pi
.

(2)

\overline{f}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
and
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
. Moreover,
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
under the identification
{\rm Bir}\,(X)={\rm Bir}\,(\overline{X})
by
\nu
.

Proof.

Since
\pi:A\to\overline{X}
is Galois and the Galois group
\langle\sigma\rangle
of
\pi
does not contain non-trivial translations of
A
, the abelian variety
A
is the Albanese closure of
\overline{X}
(
[
CMZ20
, Lemma 8.1 and its proof]
). Therefore
\overline{f}\in{\rm End}\,(\overline{X})
lifts to some
f_{A}\in{\rm End}\,(A)
, that is, there is an endomorphism
f_{A}
such that
\pi\circ f_{A}=f\circ\pi
, by
[
CMZ20
, Lemma 8.1]
. Here we note that
f_{A}
is separable as so are
\overline{f}
and
\nu
. Indeed, for a finite field extension
L_{1}\subset L_{2}\subset L_{3}
, the extension
L_{1}\subset L_{3}
is separable if and only if so are
L_{1}\subset L_{2}
and
L_{2}\subset L_{3}
.

Set
N:=\pi^{-1}({\rm Sing}\,\overline{X})
. Then
N
is a finite set and
\pi(N)={\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}
as
\pi
is finite and surjective.
Since
\overline{f}^{\pm 1}({\rm Sing}\,\overline{X})={\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}
by Lemma
2.8
, it follows that
f_{A}^{\pm 1}(N)=N
. Since
f_{A}
is separable and therefore necessarily finite and étale by Remark
2.2

(1), it follows that
f_{A}
is also of degree
1
by
f_{A}^{\pm 1}(N)=N
. Therefore
f_{A}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A)
by the Zariski main theorem. Thus, the finite separable surjective morphism
\overline{f}
is also of degree one. Hence
\overline{f}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
again by the Zariski main theorem, as
\overline{X}
is normal. By Lemma
2.6
, we have
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
by
\nu
. On the other hand, since
\nu:X\to\overline{X}
is the blow up at the maximal ideals of
{\rm Sing}\,\overline{X}
, we have
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
by
\nu
. Thus
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
as claimed. Therefore
f\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
. This completes the proof of Lemma
2.9
. ∎

Completion of the proof of Theorem
1.4
.
Now we are ready to complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
. By Lemma
2.9
(2), we have that

{\rm End}\,(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)\simeq{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})

and that
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
is isomorphic to
N(\langle\sigma\rangle)/\langle\sigma\rangle
, where
N(\langle\sigma\rangle)\subset{\rm Aut}\,(A)
is the normalizer of
\langle\sigma\rangle
in
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A)
. Thus we obtain the second statement of Theorem
1.4
readily from the fact that

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A)=A\rtimes{{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}}_{{\rm group}}\,(A)

for an abelian variety
A

[
Mm70
, Page 43, Corollary 1]
and
\langle\sigma\rangle
is in the center of
{{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}}_{{\rm group}}\,(A)
(by the shape of
\sigma
). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

3.1.
Brief review on Kawaguchi-Silverman Conjecture (KSC)

We briefly recall some notion, basic tools and known facts on Kawaguchi-Silverman Conjecture (KSC), relevant to us. We restrict ourselves for
{\rm End}\,(V)
of a normal projective variety
V
.

Let
h_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}:{\mathbb{P}}^{N}={\mathbb{P}}^{N}(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}
be the logarithmic Weil height (
[
HS00
, B1-B4, Part B]
or
[
Mm70
, Appendix II]
for the definition and basic properties).
h_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}
depend on the choice of the projective linear coordinate of
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
but only up to bounded functions. If
H
is an ample line bundle on
V
, then one has then an embedding

V\subset|mH|^{*}={\mathbb{P}}^{N}\,\,{\rm with}\,\,N=\dim|mH|

for large
m
.
By adding suitable constant on
h_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}
, we may and will assume that
h_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(x)\geq m
and define
h_{H}
by

h_{H}:V=V(\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})\to{\mathbb{R}}_{\geq 0}\,\,;\,\,x\mapsto\frac{h_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(x)}{m}\,\,(\geq 1).

h_{H}
is defined up to bounded functions and we always choose
h_{H}
so that
h_{H}(x)\geq 1
for all
x\in V
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
V
be a normal projective variety of dimension
d
over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
H
an ample line bundle on
V
. Let
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
.

(1)

The first dynamical degree
d_{1}(f)
is defined by

d_{1}(f):=\lim_{n\to\infty}((f^{n})^{*}H.H^{d-1})_{V}^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq 1.

(2)

The arithmeic degree
a_{f}(x)
for
x\in X
is defined by

a_{f}(x):=\lim_{n\to\infty}h_{H}(f^{n}(x))^{\frac{1}{n}}\geq 1.

We have the following:

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
V
be a normal projective variety and
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
. Then:

(1)

d_{1}(f)
is well-defined and satisfies
d_{1}(f)\geq 1
. Moreover,
d_{1}(f)
coincides with the spectral radius of
f^{*}|_{N^{1}(V)_{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
.

(2)

a_{f}(x)
(
x\in X
) is well-defined and
a_{f}(x)\geq 1
. Moreover,
a_{f}(x)
does not depends on the choice of
H
,
m

and bounded functions added to
h_{H}
.

(3)

1\leq a_{f}(x)\leq d_{1}(f)
for all
x\in X
.

The assertions (1) is well-known. The proof of
[
Tr15
, Theorem 1.1]
is valid without any change for a normal projective variety
V
and an ample class
H
if
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
. The assertions (2) and (3) are due to Kawaguchi-Silverman
[
KS16a
, Theorem 3]
and
[
Ma20
]
(See also a survey paper
[
Ma23
]
).

The following known results are relevant for us:

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
V
be a normal projective variety and
f\in{\rm End}\,(V)
. Then:

(1)

KSC holds for
{\rm End}\,(V)
when
V
is an abelian variety (
[
KS16a
, Theorem 4]
,
[
Si17
, Theorem 1.2]
).

(2)

KSC holds for
{\rm End}\,(V)
when
\dim V\leq 2
(
[
KS16b
, Theorem 5]
when
\dim\,V=1
, and
[
MZ22
, Theorem 1.3]
, see also
[
Ka08
]
and

[
MSS18
]
, when
\dim V=2
).

(3)

KSC holds for
{\rm End}\,(V)
when
V
is a smooth rationally connected variety with at least one inter amplified endomorphism (
[
MY22
, theorem 1.1]
, see also
[
MZ22
]
,
[
Yo21
]
).

The following proposition or its variants are also known (
[
Si17
]
,
[
MSS18
]

[
LS21

Proposition 3.4

Let

Now we are ready to prove Theorem

Example 4.1

\mu_{3}=\langle\sigma_{3}^{3}\rangle

\pi_{3}:A_{3}\to A_{3}/\mu_{3}=\overline{X}_{3}.

(2)

Let
\zeta_{7}:={\rm exp}(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/7)
and
A_{7}:={\rm Alb}\,(C)
the Albanese variety of the Klein quartic curve

C:=\{[x:y:z]\,|\,xy^{3}+yz^{3}+zx^{3}=0\}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{2}.

A_{7}
is an abelian threefold. Let
g_{7}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
be the automorphism induced from the automorphism

given by

g([x:y:z])=[\zeta_{7}x:\zeta_{7}^{2}y:\zeta_{7}^{4}z].

Then
g_{7}
is of order
7
and the action of
g_{7}
on
H^{0}(\Omega_{A_{7}}^{1})
is
represented by

\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\zeta_{7}&0&0\\
0&\zeta_{7}^{2}&0\\
0&0&\zeta_{7}^{4}\end{array}\right)

under suitable basis of
H^{0}(\Omega_{A_{7}}^{1})
. Let
\mu_{7}=\langle g_{7}\rangle
. Let

\pi_{7}:A_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}:=A_{7}/\mu_{7}

be the quotient morphism and

\nu_{7}:X_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}:=A_{7}/\mu_{7}

be a crepant projective resolution of
\overline{X}_{7}
, which is unique (
[
Og96
]
). Then
X_{7}
is a Calabi-Yau threefold and
\nu_{7}
is a
c_{2}
-contraction of a Calabi-Yau threefold
X_{7}
. Note that the automorphism of order
3
on
C
defined by
[x:y:z]\mapsto[z:x:y]
induces an automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
of order
3
such that
\sigma^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ\sigma=g_{7}^{2}
.

By the explicit descriptions in Example
4.1
, both
X_{3}
and
X_{7}
are defined not only over
{\mathbb{C}}
but also over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. We cite the following from
[
Og96
]
(see also
[
OS01
, Therem 3.4]
):

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
f:X\to W
be a birational
c_{2}
-contraction of a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
. Then
f:X\to W
is isomorphic either
\nu_{3}:X_{3}\to\overline{X}_{3}
or
\nu_{7}:X_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}
. Moreover,
f
is a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction of
X
in each case.

In order to prove Theorem
1.8
, we also need the following structure theorem of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{i})
(
i=3
and
7
) in Theorem
4.2
and Example
4.1
, which may have its own interest:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
X_{3}
and
X_{7}
be as in Example
4.1
. Then, under the notation of Example
4.1
, the following (1) and (2) hold:

(1)

For
X_{3}
, we have

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{3})=A_{3}^{\mu_{3}}\rtimes{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{3})/\mu_{3}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/3)^{\oplus 3}\rtimes{\rm GL}\,(3,{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{3}])/\mu_{3},

and there is
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\,(X_{3})
such that
d_{1}(g)>1
and
O_{g}(x)
for some
x\in X_{3}
is Zariski dense in
X_{3}
both over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

For
X_{7}
, we have

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{7})\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/7)\rtimes({\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3)/\mu_{7}\simeq({\mathbb{Z}}/7)\rtimes(({\mathbb{Z}}/2\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus 2})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3).

In particular
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{7})
is a virtually abelian group, that is,
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{7})
has a finite index abelian subgroup. There is also
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\,(X_{7})
such that
d_{1}(g)>1
and
O_{g}(x)
for some
x\in X_{7}
is Zariski dense in
X_{7}
both over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

The assertion (1) follows from Theorem
1.4
and Proposition
3.5
.

Let us show the assertion (2). Since
\nu_{7}:X_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}
is a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction and
\nu_{7}
is also a unique projective crepant resolution of
\overline{X}_{7}
, we have
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{7})\simeq{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X}_{7})
under
\nu_{7}
. Since
\mu_{7}
contains no non-trivial translation, it follows that
\pi_{7}:A_{7}\to\overline{X}_{7}
is the Albanese reduction
[
CMZ20
, Lemma 8.1]
. In particular, any element of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X}_{7})
lifts to
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A_{7})
equivariantly with respect to
\pi_{7}
by
[
CMZ20
, Lemma 8.1]
. Hence
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X}_{7})
is isomorphic to
N(\mu_{7})/\mu_{7}
, where
N(\mu_{7})
is the normalizer of
\mu_{7}
in
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A_{7})
. Let
C(\mu_{7})
is the centralizer of
\mu_{7}
in
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A_{7})
. We also denote by
N_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
(resp.
C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
) the normalizer (resp. centralizer) of
\mu_{7}
in
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
.

Lemma 4.4
.

N(\mu_{7})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/7\rtimes N_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})={\mathbb{Z}}/7\rtimes(C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3)
.

Proof.

Let
h\in N(\mu_{7})
and decompose
h
as
h=t_{a}\circ h_{0}
, where
h_{0}\in{\rm Aut}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
and
t_{a}
is the translation by
a\in A_{7}
. Then for
x\in A_{7}
we have

h^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h(x)=h_{0}^{-1}(g_{7}(h_{0}(x))+g_{7}(a)-a)=h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}(x)+h_{0}^{-1}(g_{7}(a)-a),

and therefore

h^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h=t_{h_{0}^{-1}(g_{7}(a)-a)}\circ(h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}).

As the left hand side is in
\mu_{7}
, it follows that
h_{0}^{-1}(g_{7}(a)-a)=0
and
h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}\in\mu_{7}
. From
h_{0}^{-1}(g_{7}(a)-a)=0
, we have
g_{7}(a)=a
. This means that
a\in A_{7}^{\mu_{7}}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/7
and we obtain
N(\mu_{7})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/7\rtimes N_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
.

It remains to show that
N_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\simeq C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3
.

Note that
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/6
. Combining
h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}\in\mu_{7}
with the fact that
h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}
and
g_{7}
has the same eigenvalues on
H^{0}(\Omega_{A_{7}}^{1})
, it follows that
h_{0}^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ h_{0}
is either one of
g_{7}
,
g_{7}^{2}
,
g_{7}^{4}
. Hence
h_{0}^{3}\circ g_{7}=g_{7}\circ h_{0}^{3}
. Since the action on
H^{0}(\Omega_{A_{7}}^{1})
of
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
is faithful, it follows that
h_{0}^{3}\in C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
. Moreover, since we have
\sigma^{-1}\circ g_{7}\circ\sigma=g_{7}^{2}
and
\sigma
is of order
3
, where
\sigma
is the automorphism in Example
4.1
(2), it follows that

N_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})=C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\rtimes\langle\sigma\rangle\simeq C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}/3,

as claimed.
∎

Next we compute
C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under
\mu_{14}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/14
, we have

C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/14\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus 2}.

Proof.

We regard
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
and denote by
(A_{7})_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}
and
(A_{7})_{{\mathbb{C}}}
) if
A_{7}
is considered as a
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-variety and a
{\mathbb{C}}
-variety respectively. Since
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}((A_{7})_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}/\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})
is locally of finite type and of dimension
0
as a scheme and
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
is algebraically closed, by the Hilbert zero point theorem, the group of
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued points is the same as the group of
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
-valued points. Therefore

{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}((A_{7})_{\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}}/\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}})={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}((A_{7})_{{\mathbb{C}}}/{\mathbb{C}}),

as abstract groups. So, it suffices to prove Lemma
4.5

for
(A_{7})_{{\mathbb{C}}}
.

In what follows,
A_{7}=(A_{7})_{{\mathbb{C}}}
and also consider
A_{7}
as a complex torus. Then, under the identification
\langle g_{7}\rangle=\langle\zeta_{7}\rangle
given by
g_{7}\leftrightarrow\zeta_{7}
, we have

{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}\subset{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})\,\,,\,\,{\rm hence}\,\,,\,\,{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}\subset C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7}).

On the other hand, by
[
Og96
]
, we have also
H^{1}(A_{7},{\mathbb{Z}})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]
as
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]
-modules with
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]
-action on the left hand side is given by the identification
g_{7}=\zeta_{7}
above. Since
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
acts faithfully on
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
H^{1}(A_{7},{\mathbb{Z}})
, it follows that
C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
also acts faithfully on
H^{1}(A_{7},{\mathbb{Z}})
. The action is not only as
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module but also as
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]
-module by the definition of
C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
. Thus

C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})\subset{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}

as well. Hence
C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})={\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}
as claimed.

Let us compute the abelian group
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}
. If
x\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}
is of finite order, then
x
is a root of
1
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]
. Thus the torsion subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}
is
\mu_{14}
. Since
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{7})
has exactly
6
different embeddings to
{\mathbb{C}}
and no embedding to
{\mathbb{R}}
, by the Dirichlet unit theorem, the rank of
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}
is
6/3-1=2
. Hence
{\mathbb{Z}}[\zeta_{7}]^{\times}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/14\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}^{\oplus 2}
as claimed. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.5
.
∎

By Lemmas
4.4
and
4.5
, we obtain the first two assertions of (2).

It remains to show the last assertion of (2). Setting
{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt}_{A_{7}}={\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt}
,
we have

f:=g_{7}+{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt}\in{\rm End}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7}).

Then
f\in C_{{\rm group}}(\mu_{7})
, as

(g_{7}+{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt})(g_{7}^{5}+g_{7}^{3}+g_{7})=g_{7}^{6}+g_{7}^{5}+g_{7}^{4}+g_{7}^{3}+g_{7}^{2}+g_{7}=-{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt}\,\,,\,\,(g_{7}+{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt})\circ g_{7}=g_{7}\circ(g_{7}+{\rm id\hskip 0.43057pt})

in
{\rm End}_{{\rm group}}(A_{7})
.

Thus
f
descends to
g\in{\rm Aut}\,(X_{7})
.

The next lemma completes the proof of the last statement of (2), as the natural map
A_{7}\dasharrow X_{7}
is dominant and generically finite and
d_{1}(g)=d_{1}(f)
by
[
DN11
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Lemma 4.6
.

d_{1}(f)>1
and
f
has a Zariski dense orbit on
A_{7}
.

Proof.

Since the eigenvalues of
f
on
H^{0}(\Omega_{A_{7}}^{1})
are

\zeta_{7}+1\,\,,\,\,\zeta_{7}^{2}+1\,\,,\,\,\zeta_{7}^{4}+1,

it follows that the eigenvalues of the action of
f
on
H^{1,1}(A_{7})
are

(1+\zeta_{7}^{a})\overline{(1+\zeta_{7}^{b})}\,\,{\rm with}\,\,a,b\in\{1,2,4\}

Therefore,

d_{1}(f)=|\zeta_{7}+1|^{2}>1.

In particular,
f
is of infinite order. Since
1
is not an eigenvalue of
f^{*}
on
H^{1,1}(A_{7})
and on
H^{2,2}(A_{7})
by the description above, it follows that
f
admits no invariant divisors and curves. Thus the Zariski closure of
O_{f}(x)
(
x\in A_{7}
) has to be either a finite set of points or the whole
A_{7}
. Since
f
is of infinite order, it follows then that the Zariski closure of
O_{f}(x)
for some
x\in A_{7}
is the whole
A_{7}
, as
O_{f}(x)
is an infinite set for general
x\in A_{7}
even over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
by Amerik
[
Am11
]
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

This completes the proof of Theorem
4.3
.
∎

4.2.
Completion of the proof of Theorem
1.8

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.8
. As remarked at the beginning of Subsection
4.1
, we know that
{\rm End}\,(X)={\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}\,(X)
for a Calabi-Yau threefold
X
. Let
X
be a Calabi-Yau threefold defined over
\overline{{\mathbb{Q}}}
with a non-trivial
c_{2}
-contraction and
f:X\to W
a maximal
c_{2}
-contraction of
X
.
Then
\dim\,W=1
,
2
or
3
. Our proof of the cases
\dim\,W=1
,
2
below is the same as in
[
LS21
, Theorem 1.8]
. These two cases also follow from
[
Li24
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Let
g\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X)
and
g_{W}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(W)
induced from
g
via the maximality of
f
.

Consider first the case where
\dim\,W=1
. Then
W={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
and by
[
VZ01
]
, the critical set
N\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of
f
satisfies
|N|\geq 3
. Then
g_{W}^{|N|!}
is identity on
N
and therefore
g_{W}^{|N|!}
is identity on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
by
|N|\geq 3
. Hence
g
has no Zariski dense orbit and KSC holds vacuously in this case.

Consider next the case where
\dim\,W=2
. Then
(W,g_{W})
satisfies KSC as
\dim W=2
by Theorem
3.3
(2). Thus
(X,g)
satisfies KSC as well by Proposition
3.4
(1).

Consider the case where
\dim W=3
. Then we have an identification
X=X_{i}
(
i=3
,
7
) and morphisms

X_{i}\xrightarrow{\nu_{i}}\overline{X}_{i}=A_{i}/\mu_{i}\xleftarrow{\pi_{i}}A_{i}

as in Example
4.1
. Let
h\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{i})
. Then
h
descends to
\overline{h}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(\overline{X})
by the maximality of
\nu_{i}
and lifts to
\tilde{h}\in{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(A_{i})
, as
A_{i}
is the Albanese closure of
\overline{X}_{i}
(
[
CMZ20
, Lemma 8.1]
). Since KSC holds for
(A_{i},\tilde{h})
by Theorem
3.3
(1), it follows that KSC holds for
(\overline{X}_{i},\overline{h})
by Proposition
3.4
(2). Thus, KSC also holds for
(X_{i},h)
by Proposition
3.4
(1). KSC also holds non-trivially and non-vacuously for
{\rm Aut\hskip 0.43057pt}(X_{i})
by Theorem
4.3
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.8
.

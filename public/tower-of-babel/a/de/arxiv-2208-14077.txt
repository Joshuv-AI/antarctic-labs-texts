DTAC-ADMM : Delay-Tolerant Augmented Consensus ADMM-based Algorithm for Distributed Resource Allocation
Mohammadreza Doostmohammadian, Wei Jiang, Themistoklis Charalambous

Abstract
Latency is inherent in almost all real-world networked applications. In this paper, we propose a distributed allocation strategy over multi-agent networks with delayed communications. The state of each agent (or node) represents its share of assigned resources out of a fixed amount (equal to overall demand). Every node locally updates its state toward optimizing a global allocation cost function via received information of its neighbouring nodes even when the data exchange over the network is heterogeneously delayed at different links. The update is based on the alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM) formulation subject to both sum-preserving coupling-constraint and local box-constraints. The solution is derivative-free and holds for general (not necessarily differentiable) convex cost models. We use the notion of augmented consensus over undirected networks to model delayed information exchange and for convergence analysis. We simulate our delay-tolerant algorithm for optimal energy reservation-production scheduling.



I
Introduction

Resource allocation refers to the optimal assignment of the (fixed) amount of resources minimizing certain resource-related costs.
Distributed algorithms to solve resource allocation problems over a network have recently emerged in literature, primarily motivated by advances in distributed data processing and cloud computing (instead of centralized single-node solutions) to handle more complex optimization problems and large-scale data sets. Applications include distributed energy resource provisioning for ancillary services
[
1
]
, automatic generation control over the power grid
[

]

[

]
.

Existing distributed solutions span from primary linear Laplacian-gradient

,
5
,
6
]
strategies with all-time feasibility to more recent primal-dual solutions
[

,
8
,

reaching feasibility asymptotically over time. The Laplacian-gradient solutions are known to preserve anytime-feasibility even in the presence of model nonlinearities (over uniformly-connected networks)
[

[

and finite/fixed-time convergence
[

coupling-constraint

[
9
]
. For example, in the generator coordination and economic dispatch problem (EDP), the generated power (assigned resources) needs to meet the grid’s power demand. Otherwise, violating the solution feasibility may lead to disruption in the service and even system breakdown
[
14
]
. In this sense, with feasible initialization, the outcome of these algorithms is feasible at any termination time.
On the other hand, primal-dual solutions need to reach feasibility (sufficiently fast) with no need for feasible initialization.
In this scenario,

In this work, reformulating the primal distributed resource allocation (DRA) problem to its dual form and assuming standard strong-duality, we use the ADMM-based method inspired by

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{x}^{k}=\underline{\mathbf{x}}^{k}-\overline{\mathbf{x}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k},~\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}=\underline{\mathbf{d}}^{k}-\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k},~\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k}=\underline{\mathbf{y}}^{k}-\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k},

\displaystyle\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{0}^{k+1}=\Xi^{n}_{1,\overline{\tau}}\overline{PW}\underline{\mathbf{d}}^{k}+(\Xi^{n}_{1,\overline{\tau}}-\Xi^{n}_{2,\overline{\tau}})\underline{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1},

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\underline{\mathbf{d}}^{k+1}-\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k+1}

(33)

\displaystyle=\overline{PW}\underline{\mathbf{d}}^{k}+\mathbf{u}^{\overline{\tau}+1}_{1}\otimes(\Xi^{n}_{1,\overline{\tau}}-\Xi^{n}_{2,\overline{\tau}})\underline{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}-\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k+1}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(a)}}{{=}}\overline{PW}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}+\mathbf{u}^{\overline{\tau}+1}_{1}\otimes(\Xi^{n}_{1,\overline{\tau}}-\Xi^{n}_{2,\overline{\tau}})\underline{\mathbf{y}}^{k+1}-\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k+1}+\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k},

where, adding
\pm\overline{\mathbf{d}}_{\overline{\tau}}^{k}
in step (a), we used the row-stochasticity of
\overline{PW}
from Remark

For the sake of stability proof analysis, define the
modified
augmented matrix

\displaystyle\widetilde{PW}=\overline{PW}-\overline{P1},

(34)

with

as the augmented version of the stochastic matrix

via the same delay matrix

.
For the PSD
\widetilde{PW}

\rho(\widetilde{PW})\leq\|\widetilde{PW}\|<1
since
\|\overline{PW}-\overline{P1}\|<1
. Define

Then, Eq. (

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k+1}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{PW}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}+\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k},

since

Similarly define the average value

\displaystyle\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{x}^{k+1}=\widetilde{PW}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{x}^{k}+c\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k+1}=\widetilde{PW}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}+c\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}+c(\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k}).

Rearranging the above terms in its compact form we get

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{x}^{k+1}\\
\underline{c\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k+1}\end{array}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\widetilde{PW}&\widetilde{PW}\\
\mathbf{0}&\widetilde{PW}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{x}^{k}\\
c\underline{\mathbf{e}}_{d}^{k}\end{array}\right)+c\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\\
\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k+1}-\underline{\mathbf{z}}^{k}\end{array}\right).

.

V
Application: Scheduling over Energy Grid

Consider the EDP for a group of
n
generators tasked to produce power according to a fixed demand over a specific time interval. The demand
b
may change over certain time intervals, and, thus, one key point is the so-called solution feasibility, referring to the global constraint
\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}=b
to be held at the termination point, implying that the demand
b
need to meet the produced power
\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}

The solution can be either (i) all-time feasible or (ii) gain feasibility sufficiently fast before the termination time. Then, the optimal solution is assigned to the generators, optimizing the following power generation cost model

\displaystyle\min_{y}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i}y_{i}^{2}+\beta_{i}y_{i}+\alpha_{i},

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}=b,~~m_{i}\leq y_{i}\leq M_{i}

(46)

follow the type of the generator

(i.e., oil-fired, coal-fired, etc).

As mentioned in remark
2
,
DTAC-ADMM
algorithm is initialization-free and for any non-feasible initial power generation in
[m_{i}~M_{i}]
it can reach the demand value

. This is better illustrated in Fig.

[

]

0.5
and other link-weights equal to
0.25
. We run the algorithm over
10^{4}
iterations (stopping criteria). Note that in some literature, e.g.
[
15

, the local box-constraints

are not addressed, while our solution perfectly handles these local constraints as shown in Fig.

(primal states). The case of

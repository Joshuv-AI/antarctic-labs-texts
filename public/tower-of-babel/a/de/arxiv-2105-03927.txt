First, we recall some tools we need. The gamma and digamma functions denoted by

\Gamma(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}u^{x-1}e^{-u}du\quad\mbox{and}\quad\psi(x)=\frac{\Gamma^{\prime}(x)}{\Gamma(x)},\quad(x>0).

\Gamma(n+1/2)=\frac{(2n)!\sqrt{\pi}}{4^{n}n!},\quad n=0,1,2,3,....

The functions

\psi^{(0)}(x)=\psi(x)

The polygamma functions satisfy the following fundamental functional equation:

(1.2)

\psi^{(n)}(x+1)-\psi^{(n)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}n!}{x^{n+1}},\quad n=0,1,2,...,

and have the integral representation

(1.3)

\psi^{(n)}(s+1)=(-1)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t^{n}e^{-(s+1)t}}{1-e^{-t}}dt,

It is well known that binomial coefficients play an important role in various subjects such as combinatorics, number theory, probability, and graph theory. In the literature there exist many results dealing with sums involving reciprocals of the binomial coefficients. Particularly, sums involving both reciprocals of the binomial coefficients and harmonic numbers have been receiving much attention. For example, in

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k+1}H_{k}^{2}}{k^{p}\binom{n+k}{k}^{2}},\quad p=0,1.

In

In

Motivated by these results mentioned above in this paper we consider the following family of sums:

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{t^{k}}{k^{p}\binom{n+k}{k}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k-1}t^{k}}{k^{p}\binom{n+k}{k}},\quad p=0,1,2,

for
|t|<1
. While there are many results for sums of the types given above there are almost no results for the power series like these. It is a well-known fact that evaluating the sum of this kind of series explicitly is not easy, and usually it is done by using the gamma-beta functions identity or a combinatorial formula for the reciprocal of the pochhammer symbol. This formula has been used extensively by A. Sofo in his works. But we believe that using proper integrals are more useful for studying these sums and therefore we employ some logarithmic integrals and by this way we provide elegant explicit formulas for these sums.

Lemma 1.1
.

Let
(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
be any two sequences of real or complex numbers. Then we have

(1.12)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{n-1}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n-p}b_{k}a_{p+k}.

If we let

here, we get

(1.13)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{k}b_{j}=\sum\limits_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}b_{k}a_{p+k},

provided that all involved series are convergent.

Lemma 1.2
.

For

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-a)^{k}}{k}\sum_{J=1}^{k}\frac{(-1/a)^{j}}{j}=H_{n}^{(2)}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(H_{k}+H_{n-k}-H_{n})(-a)^{k}}{k}.

The proof follows from (
1.12
) with

and

Lemma 1.4
.

Let

. Then, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{(-a)^{k}}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{p=1}^{j}\frac{(-1/a)^{p}}{p}

(1.14)

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(-2,0)
, and
s
be a positive real number. Then the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{s}}{1-x}\log\left(1-\frac{1-x}{1+a}\right)dx=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(a+1)^{k}\binom{s+k}{k}}-Li_{2}\left(\frac{1}{a+1}\right).

Proof.

Expanding the logarithmic function to its power series, we get

(1.20)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{s}}{1-x}\log\left(1-\frac{1-x}{1+a}\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(1-x^{s})\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-x)^{k-1}}{k(a+1)^{k}}dx

(1.21)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}(1-(1-x)x^{s})\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k-1}}{k(a+1)^{k}}dx.

Let
x\in[0,1]
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash[-2,0]
. Then we, clearly, have

\left|\frac{(1-(1-x)^{s})x^{k-1}}{k(a+1)^{k}}\right|\leq\frac{1}{k|a+1|^{k}},

and
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k[a+1]^{k}}<\infty
, so the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{(1-(1-x)^{s})x^{k-1}}{k(a+1)^{k}}
is uniformly convergent on [0,1] by Weierstrass
M
-test. Therefore, we can reverse the order of integration and summation in (
1.20
) (see
[
35
, pp.192–193]
), and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{s}}{1-x}\log\left(1-\frac{1-x}{1+a}\right)dx

\displaystyle=-\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k(a+1)^{k}}\left[\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{k-1}dx-\int_{0}^{1}x^{s}(1-x)^{k-1}dx\right].

Since we have by (
1.5
) and (
1.6
)

(1.22)

\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{k-1}dx=\frac{1}{k}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}x^{s}(1-x)^{k-1}dx=\frac{1}{k\binom{s+k}{k}},

we find for
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash[-2,0]

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{s}}{1-x}\log\left(1-\frac{1-x}{1+a}\right)dx=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(a+1)^{k}\binom{s+k}{k}}-Li_{2}\left(\frac{1}{a+1}\right).

But this series is also convergent at
a=-2
and
a=0
, so, Lemma 1.6 is valid for
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(-2,0)
by Abel’s limit test. This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 1.7
.

For k=0,1,2,…, and
a\in\mathbb{R}
we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}\log(x+a)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(a+1)}{k+1}-\frac{(-a)^{k+1}}{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\frac{(-1/a)^{j}}{j}

\displaystyle-\frac{(-a)^{k+1}}{k+1}\log(1+1/a).

Proof.

Integrating by parts yields

(1.23)

\int_{0}^{1}x^{k}\log(x+a)dx=\frac{\log(a+1)}{k+1}-\frac{1}{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{k+1}}{x+a}dx.

On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{k+1}}{x+a}dx=(-1)^{k}a^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-(-x/a)^{k+1}-1}{1-(-x/a)}dx

\displaystyle=(-a)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-(-x/a)^{k+1}}{1-(-x/a)}dx+(-a)^{k+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{dx}{x+a}

\displaystyle=(-a)^{k}\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{j=0}^{k}(-x/a)^{j}dx+(-a)^{k+1}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{a}\right).

Inverting the order of summation and integration, we obtain, after a short computation,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{k+1}}{x+a}dx=(-a)^{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k+1}\frac{(-a)^{j}}{j}+(-a)^{k+1}\log\left(1+\frac{1}{a}\right).

Substituting this into (
1.23
), we complete the proof.
∎

Lemma 1.8
.

For
|x|\leq 1
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{(j+1)(j+k+1)}=\frac{1}{2xk}\left(1-\frac{1}{x^{k}}\right)\log^{2}(1-x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\log(1-x)}{kx^{k+1}}\left(H_{k}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{x^{j}}{j}\right)+\frac{1}{kx^{k+1}}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j}\sum_{p=1}^{j}\frac{x^{p}}{p}.

Proof.

By partial fraction decomposition we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{(j+1)(j+k+1)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}H_{j}\left(\frac{1}{j+1}-\frac{1}{j+k+1}\right)x^{j}

(1.24)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j+1}-\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j+k+1}.

Employing (
1.13
) with
b_{j}=1/j
and
a_{j}=\frac{x^{j}}{j+k+1}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j+k+1}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{j(p+j+k+1)}.

From
[
11
]
we know that

(1.25)

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j}=\frac{1}{2}\log^{2}(1-x)+Li_{2}(x).

By partial fraction decomposition, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j+k+1}=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{p+k+1}\left(\frac{1}{j}-\frac{1}{p+j+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{p+k+1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{p+j+k+1}\right)

\displaystyle=-\log(1-x)\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{p+k+1}-\sum_{p=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{p+k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{p+j+k+1}.

Setting

and then deleting the prime one gets

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{j}x^{j}}{j+k+1}=-\frac{\log(1-x)}{x^{k+1}}\sum_{p=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{p}-\frac{1}{x^{k+1}}\sum_{p=k+1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p}}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{j}}{p+j}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\log(1-x)}{x^{k+1}}\left(-\log(1-x)-\sum_{p=1}^{k}\frac{x^{p}}{p}\right)

(1.26)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x^{k+1}}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(p+j)}+\frac{1}{x^{k+1}}\sum_{p=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(p+j)}.

Since
|x|<1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(p+j)}\right|<\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\left|\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(j+1)}\right|=\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x|^{p}}{p}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{|x|^{j}}{j+1}<\infty.

We therefore can reverse the order of sums in the first sum in the last line of (
1

\displaystyle\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(p+j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j}}{p(p+j)}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\sum_{p=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{p+j+1}}{p(p+j+1)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k+1}}{k+1}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{j}=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{x^{k+1}H_{k}}{k+1}.

Applying

(1.27)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{H_{k}x^{k}}{k+1}=\frac{1}{2x}\log^{2}(1-x),

which can easily be derived from (

On the other hand, we have

Theorem 2.1
.

For all
a\in\mathbb{R}\backslash(-2,0]
,and
s
be a positive real number, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-x^{s}}{1-x}\log(x+a)dx

\displaystyle=(\gamma+\psi(s+1))\log(a+1)-Li_{2}\left(\frac{1}{a+1}\right)

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k^{2}(a+1)^{k}\binom{s+k}{k}}.

Theorem 2.20
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
, which is not a negative integer. Then

(2.37)

\psi^{(n)}(t+1)=(-1)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|}{kk!\binom{t+k}{k}},

where
s(n,k)
are Stirling numbers of the first kind.

Proof.

We assume that
t>0
. Making the change of variable
x=e^{-t}
in (
1.3
) yields for
t>-1

(2.38)

\psi^{(n)}(t+1)=(-1)^{n+1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{x^{t}\left(\log(1-(1-x))\right)^{n}}{1-x}dx,

Using (see
[
13
, pg. 351]
)

\left(\log(1-x)\right)^{n}=(-1)^{n}n!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|x^{k}}{k!},\quad|x|<1,

we conclude from (
2.38
) for
t>0
and
|x|<1

\displaystyle\psi^{(n)}(t+1)=(-1)^{n+1}n!\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|x^{t}(1-x)^{k-1}}{k!}dx

(2.39)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n+1}n!\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|(1-x)^{t}x^{k-1}}{k!}dx

Let
u\in(0,1)
. Then for all
x\in[0,u]
, we have

|(1-x)^{t}x^{k-1}|\leq|x|^{k-1}<|u|^{k-1}.

But since this is also valid for
x=0
and
x=1
, we have, for all
x\in[0,1]
,

|(1-x)^{t}x^{k-1}|\leq|x|^{k-1}<|u|^{k-1}.

We therefore get for all
x\in[0,1]

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg|\frac{s(k,n)(1-x)^{t}x^{k-1}}{k!}\bigg|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|u^{k-1}}{k!}<\infty,

since
|u|<1
. So, by Weierstrass
M
-test the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|x^{t}(1-x)^{k-1}}{k!}
is uniformly convergent on
[0,1]
. Hence, we can reverse the order of integration and summation in (
2
) (see
[
35
, Theorem 7.2.4, p.192]
), and

(2.40)

\displaystyle\psi^{(n)}(t+1)=(-1)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|}{k!}\int_{0}^{1}x^{t}(1-x)^{k-1}dx.

Thus, by the help of (
1.5
), we get

\displaystyle\psi^{(n)}(t+1)

\displaystyle=(-)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|}{k!}B(t+1,k)

\displaystyle=(-)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|}{k!}\frac{\Gamma(t+1)\Gamma(k)}{\Gamma(t+k+1)}

\displaystyle=(-)^{n+1}n!\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{|s(k,n)|}{kk!\binom{t+k}{k}},

which is the desired result for
t>0
. If
t\leq 0
, but not an integer, making the change of variable
y=e^{t}
in (
2
), we complete the proof by proceeding exactly as in the case
t>0
.
∎

Formula (

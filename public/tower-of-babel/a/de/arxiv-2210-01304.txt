Derived Character Maps of Group Representations
Yuri Berest, Ajay C. Ramadoss

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct and study derived character maps of finite-dimensional representations of ∞ \infty -groups. As models for ∞ \infty -groups we take homotopy simplicial groups, i.e. homotopy simplicial 𝔊 op {\mathfrak{G}}^{\rm op} -algebras over the algebraic theory of groups (in the sense of [ 4 ] ). We define cyclic, symmetric and representation homology for ‘group algebras’ k ⁡ [ Γ ] k[\Gamma] over such groups and construct canonical trace maps relating these homology theories. In the case of one-dimensional representations, we show that our trace maps are of topological origin: they are induced by natural maps of (iterated) loop spaces that are well studied in homotopy theory. Using this topological interpretation, we deduce some algebraic results about representation homology: in particular, we prove that the symmetric homology of group algebras and one-dimensional representation homology are naturally isomorphic, provided the base ring k k is a field of characteristic zero. We also study the behavior of the derived character maps of n n -dimensional representations in the stable limit as n → ∞ n\to\infty , in which case we show that they ‘converge’ to become isomorphisms.

The properties of homotopy colimits listed in Theorem

are essentially formal. The next result — called the Cofinality Theorem — gives a deeper property of homotopy-theoretic nature that is very useful in computations. To state this result we recall that a functor

is

if its comma-category

under each object

is (weakly) contractible, i.e.
\,B(b\downarrow f)\simeq{\rm pt}\,
. As an example, we point out that every right adjoint functor is right homotopy cofinal:
indeed, if

admits a left adjoint, say

has an initial object (namely,

, where

is
the unit of the adjunction evaluated at

), hence

is contractible for any

(Cofinality Theorem)

If

{\rm hocolim}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f^{\ast}X)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}{\rm hocolim}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)

Proof.

Since

simplicial groups (in the sense of Badzioch
[
4
]
), which is a more general and flexible setting than that of the usual (strict) simplicial groups used in
[
13
,
14
]
.
In Section
3.1
, we define the classifying spaces for such groups, and in Section
3.3
,
the cyclic bar construction and cyclic homology, both of which may be of independent interest.
We begin by reviewing the main results of
[
4
]
specializing to the algebraic theory of (discrete) groups.

3.1.
Homotopy simplicial groups

Let
\mathfrak{G}
denote the small category whose objects
\langle n\rangle
are the finitely generated free groups
\mathbb{F}_{n}={\mathbb{F}}\langle x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle
, one for each
n\geq 0
(with convention that
\langle 0\rangle
is the trivial group), and the morphisms are arbitrary group homomorphisms. Every discrete group
\Gamma
defines a contravariant functor
\underline{\Gamma}:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to{\rm Set}
,
\langle n\rangle\mapsto\Gamma^{n}
, which is simply the restriction of the Yoneda functor
{\rm Hom}(\mbox{--},\Gamma):\mathrm{Gr}^{\rm op}\to{\rm Set}
to
\mathfrak{G}\subset\mathrm{Gr}
. More generally, every simplicial group
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}
(i.e. a simplicial object in
\mathrm{Gr}
) defines a functor

(3.1)

\underline{\Gamma}:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{sSet}\,,\qquad\langle n\rangle\mapsto\Gamma^{n}\,,

where
\Gamma^{n}
denotes the product of
n
copies of the underlying simplicial set of
\Gamma
in the category
\mathrm{sSet}
. The functors (
3.1
) can be characterized by the property of being product-preserving. To make it precise, observe that the category
\mathfrak{G}
carries a (strict) monoidal structure
\amalg:\mathfrak{G}\times\mathfrak{G}\to\mathfrak{G}
given by the coproduct (free product) of free groups:
\langle n\rangle\amalg\langle m\rangle=\langle n+m\rangle
. The opposite category
\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}
is thus equipped with the dual monoidal structure which we simply denote by
\Pi:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\times\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}
. Every object
\langle n\rangle^{\rm o}\in\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}
comes equipped with
n
natural projections:

(3.2)

p_{n,k}:\ \langle n\rangle^{\rm o}\to\langle 1\rangle^{\rm o}\,,\qquad 1\leqslant k\leqslant n\,,

that correspond to the canonical inclusions

i_{n,k}:\,\langle 1\rangle\hookrightarrow\langle n\rangle
,
\,x_{1}\mapsto x_{k}
, in
\mathfrak{G}
. We say that a functor
\mathcal{F}:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{sSet}
is
product-preserving
if the maps induced by (
3.2
)

(3.3)

\mathcal{F}(p_{n}):=\prod_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}(p_{n,k}):\ \mathcal{F}\langle n\rangle\,\to\,(\mathcal{F}\langle 1\rangle)^{n}

are isomorphisms in
\mathrm{sSet}
for all
n\geqslant 0
. It is easy to show that assigning to a simplicial group
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}
the functor (
3.1
) defines an equivalence of categories

(3.4)

\mathrm{sGr}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}_{\otimes}

where
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}_{\otimes}
denotes the full subcategory of product-preserving functors in the diagram category
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
. We will use (
3.4
) to identify
\mathrm{sGr}=\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}_{\otimes}
, thus regarding the simplicial groups as functors of the form (
3.1
). Now, the homotopy simplicial groups are obtained by replacing the assumption that the maps (
3.3
) are isomorphisms in
\mathrm{sSet}
with that of being weak equivalences, which is a more natural condition from the point of view of homotopy theory. Precisely,

Definition 3.1

(Badzioch
[
4
]
)
.

A
homotopy simplicial group
is a functor
\mathcal{F}:\,\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{sSet}
that is weakly product-preserving in the sense that the maps (
3.3
) are weak equivalences in
\mathrm{sSet}
for all
n\geqslant 0
(with convention that
\mathcal{F}\langle 0\rangle\simeq{\rm pt}
).

The category of homotopy simplicial groups (i.e. the full subcategory of all weakly product-preserving functors in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
) does not carry any model structure as it is not closed under colimits. Instead, as suggested in
[
4
]
, one can put a new model structure on the diagram category
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
in which the homotopy simplicial groups are
exhibited as fibrant objects (
cf.

[
4
, Proposition 5.5]
). We call this model structure the
Badzioch model structure
and denote it by
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
. To be precise,
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
is defined by localizing (i.e. taking the left Boufield localization of) the standard projective model structure on
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
with respect to the collection of maps
S=\{i_{n}:\amalg_{k=1}^{n}{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mbox{--},\langle 1\rangle)\to{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\mbox{--},\langle n\rangle)\}_{n\geq 0}
induced by the inclusions
i_{n,k}:\,\langle 1\rangle\to\langle n\rangle
:

\mathrm{sGr}^{h}\,:=\,\mathcal{L}_{S}(\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}})\ .

By definition, the underlying category of
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
is that of
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
but the class of
weak equivalences in
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
— called
S
-local weak equivalences
— is larger: it includes the set
S
in addition to all (objectwise) weak equivalences of diagrams in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}\!\!
.
There is a canonical localization functor
\mathcal{L}_{S}:\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}\to\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
that takes a diagram
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
to its functorial fibrant replacement in the model structure
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
. In this way, one can make any diagram in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
a homotopy simplicial group. On the other hand, the model category of (strict) simplicial groups
\mathrm{sGr}
is related to
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
by a Quillen adjunction:

(3.5)

K:\,\mathrm{sGr}^{h}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{sGr}\,:J

which is obtained by localizing (at
S
) the Quillen adjunction
\,K:\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{sGr}:J\,
between
\mathrm{sGr}
and the model category of all diagrams
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
. In particular, the right adjoint functor in (
3.5
) is given by the inclusion
\,J(\Gamma)=\underline{\Gamma}\,
(see (
3.1
)), while the left adjoint — called the Badzioch rigidification functor —
is described explicitly in Lemma
3.1
below.
Now, the main result of
[
4
]
reads:

Theorem 3.1

(Badzioch)
.

The adjunction (
3.5
) is a Quillen equivalence.

Remark 3.1
.

Theorem
3.1
was proved in
[
4
]
(see
loc. cit.
, Theorem 6.4) for an arbitrary one-sorted algebraic theory (not only the theory of groups). It was extended to all multi-sorted theories in
[
17
]
, and further to limit theories and to diagrams in model categories other than
\mathrm{sSet}
in
[
61
]
.

Next, recall that there is a classical adjunction — called the
Kan loop group construction

[
39
]
— that relates the model category
\mathrm{sGr}
of (strict) simplicial groups to that of (reduced) simplicial sets:

(3.6)

\mathbb{G}\,:\,\mathrm{sSet}_{0}\rightleftarrows\mathrm{sGr}\,:\,\bar{W}

The left adjoint
\mathbb{G}
is called the
Kan loop group functor
, and the right adjoint
\bar{W}
is the
classifying complex functor
on simplicial groups. The properties of these functors are well known and discussed in detail, for example, in
[
29
, Chapter V]
(see also
[
14
, Section 2.2]
). Here, we mention only two important facts: first, the pair (
3.6
) is a Quillen equivalence, both
\mathbb{G}
and
\bar{W}
being homotopy invariant functors (see
[
29
, V.6.4]
). Second, for any reduced simplicial set
X
, there is a weak homotopy equivalence (see
[
29
, V.5.11]
)

(3.7)

|\mathbb{G}(X)|\simeq\Omega|X|

where
\Omega|X|
is the (Moore) based loop space of the geometric realization of
X
. The equivalence (
3.7
) clarifies the topological meaning of the Kan loop group functor
\mathbb{G}

(and justifies its name). Combining now Badzioch’s Theorem
3.1
with Kan’s construction, we get natural equivalences of homotopy categories

(3.8)

{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{sGr}^{h})\,\xrightarrow{\bm{L}K}\,{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{sGr})\,\xrightarrow{\bar{W}}\,{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{sSet}_{0})\,\xrightarrow{|\,\mbox{--}\,|}\,{\mathtt{Ho}}({\rm Top}_{0,\ast})

induced by the above indicated functors. This leads us to the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

For a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
, we define its
classifying space

B\Gamma
by

(3.9)

B\Gamma\,:=\,|\bar{W}\bm{L}K(\Gamma)|

where
\bm{L}K:{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{sGr}^{h})\to{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{sGr})
is the derived rigidification functor (see (
3.11
)).

Note that if
\Gamma
is a (strict) simplicial group, i.e.
\Gamma=J(\Gamma)\,
,
then
B\Gamma\cong|\bar{W}\Gamma|
, since
\bm{L}K\circ J\cong{\rm Id}
. Thus the above definition is a natural extension of Kan’s definition of classifying spaces for simplicial groups (which is, in turn, an extension of the classical definition of
B\Gamma
for ordinary discrete groups).

We conclude this section by giving a simple formula for the Badzioch rigidification functor that did not seem to appear explicitly in
[
4
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

The functor
K:\mathrm{sGr}^{h}\to\mathrm{sGr}
in (
3.5
) is given by the coend

(3.10)

K(\Gamma)=\int^{\langle n\rangle\in\mathfrak{G}}\Gamma\langle n\rangle\otimes\mathbb{F}\langle n\rangle\,,

where
\mathbb{F}:\mathfrak{G}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{Gr}\hookrightarrow\mathrm{sGr},\,\langle n\rangle\mapsto\mathbb{F}_{n}
, is the canonical inclusion functor, and
\,\otimes:\mathrm{sSet}\times\mathrm{sGr}\to\mathrm{sGr}\,
is the standard simplicial tensor action on the category of simplicial groups.

It follows from Lemma
3.1
that the derived functor
\bm{L}K
can be written as the homotopy coend

(3.11)

\bm{L}K(\Gamma)=\int^{\langle n\rangle\in\mathfrak{G}}_{\bm{L}}\Gamma\langle n\rangle\otimes\mathbb{F}\langle n\rangle\ .

For the proof of Lemma
3.1
and formula (
3.11
) (in the general setting of
[
4
]
) we refer to our forthcoming paper
[
11
]
.

3.2.
Representation homology

Let
k
be a commutative ring.
Recall that, for a small category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})
and
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})
the categories of all covariant and contravariant functors from
\mathcal{C}
to
{\rm Mod}_{k}
, respectively. It is well known that these are abelian categories with sufficiently many projective and injective objects. Recall also (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Appendix C.10]
) that there is a natural bi-additive functor

\mbox{--}\otimes_{\mathcal{C},k}\mbox{--}\,:\,{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})\times{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C})\to{\rm Mod}_{k}

called the
functor tensor product
.
Explicitly, for
\mathcal{M}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}
and
\mathcal{N}:\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}
, it is defined by

(3.12)

\mathcal{N}\otimes_{\mathcal{C},k}\mathcal{M}\,:=\,\big[\Moplus_{c\in\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{N}(c)\otimes_{k}\mathcal{M}(c)\big]/R

where
R
is the
k
-submodule spanned by elements of the form
\mathcal{N}(\varphi)x\otimes y-x\otimes\mathcal{M}(\varphi)y
for all
x\in\mathcal{N}(c^{\prime})
,
y\in\mathcal{M}(c)
and
\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(c,c^{\prime})
. The functor (
3.12
) is right exact (with respect to each argument), preserves sums, and is left balanced. Its classical (left) derived functors with respect to each argument are canonically isomorphic and their common value is denoted by
{\rm Tor}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
. More generally, we can extend the bifunctor (
3.12
) to chain complexes of
\mathcal{C}
-modules, i.e. the categories
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))
and
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))
, and define

(3.13)

{\rm Tor}^{\mathcal{C}}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\,:=\,{\rm H}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{C},k}\mathcal{M})

for any
\mathcal{N}\in{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))
and
\mathcal{M}\in{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}))
. Note that
\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{C},k}\mathcal{M}
is an object in the (unbounded) derived category
\mathcal{D}(k)=\mathcal{D}({\rm Mod}_{k})
of
k
-modules, and (
3.13
) is just the usual hyper-Tor functor on chain complexes. Next, observe that there is a natural functor

(3.14)

\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}}\,\xrightarrow{\,k[\,\mbox{--}\,]\,}\,{\rm sMod}_{k}({\mathcal{C}}^{\rm op})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle N}}{{\,\hookrightarrow\,}}\,{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))

transforming the
\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}
-diagrams in
\mathrm{sSet}
(simplicial presheaves on
\mathcal{C}
) to
chain complexes over
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})
. Here
N
stands for
the classical Dold-Kan normalization functor that identifies simplicial objects in
{\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op})
with non-negatively graded chain complexes in
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathcal{C}^{\rm op}))
. Abusing notation, we will write the functor (
3.14
) simply as
\,k[\,\mbox{--}\,]\,
.

We are now in a position to define representation homology of homotopy simplicial groups with coefficients in commutative Hopf algebras. We recall the well-known fact (see, e.g.,
[
60
, Proposition 14.1.6]
) that every such algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
defines a covariant functor (a left
\mathfrak{G}
-module) by the rule

(3.15)

\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}:\mathfrak{G}\to{\rm Mod}_{k}\ ,\quad\langle n\rangle\mapsto{\mathcal{H}}^{\otimes n}\ .

In particular if
G
is an affine algebraic group (e.g.,
G={\rm GL}_{n}(k)
) with coordinate ring
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{O}(G)
, then (
3.15
) can be written in the form
\langle n\rangle\mapsto\mathcal{O}[{\rm Rep}_{G}(\langle n\rangle)]
which makes the functoriality clear.

Definition 3.3
.

The
representation homology
of a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
with coefficients in
{\mathcal{H}}
is defined by

{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})\,:=\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}}_{\ast}(k[\Gamma],\underline{{\mathcal{H}}})\ .

In the special case when
G
is an affine algebraic group over
k
and
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{O}(G)
, we simply write

{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,G)
instead of
{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,\mathcal{O}(G))\,
.

The next lemma shows that the above definition agrees with the Badzioch model structure on
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If two homotopy simplicial groups
\Gamma
and
\Gamma^{\prime}
are weakly equivalent in
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
, then

(3.16)

\mathrm{HR}_{*}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})\,\cong\,\mathrm{HR}_{*}(\Gamma^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}})

for any commutative Hopf algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Proof.

By
[
4
, Proposition 5.6]
, if two homotopy simplicial groups
\Gamma
and
\,\Gamma^{\prime}
are
S
-locally weakly equivalent, then their underlying diagrams are, in fact, weakly equivalent in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
. It therefore suffices to show that (
3.16
) holds for any objectwise weak equivalent diagrams
\Gamma,\Gamma^{\prime}:\,\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{sSet}
. To this end, observe that the linearization functor

(3.17)

k[\,\mbox{--}\,]\,:\,\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}\to{\rm sMod}_{k}(\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op})

is left Quillen with respect to the projective model structures (its right adjoint is the forgetful functor). Since the weak equivalences in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
are defined objectwise and the model structure on
\mathrm{sSet}
is cofibrant, being left Quillen, the functor (
3.17
) is actually homotopy invariant: i.e., it maps weakly equivalent objects in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
to weakly equivalent objects in
{\rm sMod}_{k}(\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op})
, which, in turn, are transformed by the normalization functor
N
to quasi-isomorphic complexes in
{\rm Ch}({\rm Mod}_{k}(\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}))
. Thus if
\Gamma\simeq\Gamma^{\prime}
in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
, then
\,k[\Gamma]\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathfrak{G},k}\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}\,\simeq\,k[\Gamma^{\prime}]\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathfrak{G},k}\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}\,
in
\mathcal{D}(k)
, which implies (
3.16
).
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The result of Lemma
3.2
does
not
extend to arbitrary objects in
\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
, since the functor
(
3.17
) does not preserve
S
-local weak equivalences in general. The latter can be seen easily by
looking at (
3.17
) evaluated at representable simplicial presheaves in
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}
.

An important consequence of Lemma
3.2
is that the representation homology of a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma
depends only on the homotopy type of its classifying space
B\Gamma
(Definition
3.2
). In fact, we have

(3.18)

{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})\,\cong\,{\rm HR}_{\ast}(B\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})

where the ‘
{\rm HR}
’ in the right-hand side stands for representation homology of topological spaces
as defined in
[
14
]
, using a (non-abelian) derived representation functor (see
loc. cit.
,
Definition 3.1). Indeed, by Badzioch’s results (
cf.

[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
), every homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma
is weakly equivalent to a strict one, say
\Gamma^{\prime}
; hence

(3.19)

B\Gamma\,\simeq\,B\Gamma^{\prime}\,\simeq\,\bar{W}\Gamma^{\prime}\ .

On the other hand, by
[
14
, Theorem 4.2]
,
\,{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}})\cong{\rm HR}_{\ast}(\bar{W}\Gamma^{\prime},{\mathcal{H}})\,
, which together with (
3.19
) and the isomorphism (
3.16
) of Lemma
3.2
implies (
3.18
).

We conclude this section by briefly explaining how our approach (Definition
3.3
) relates to derived algebraic geometry (DAG). For a model of DAG, we will take the simplicial presheaf model developed in
[
66
]
. Given a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
and an affine algebraic group (scheme)
G
over
k
with coordinate algebra
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{O}(G)
, we introduce the

derived representation scheme of
\Gamma
in
G
:

(3.20)

{\rm{DRep}}_{G}(\Gamma):=\bm{\mathrm{R}}{\rm{Spec}}\,(k[\Gamma]\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathfrak{G}}\mathcal{O}(G))\ .

Here ‘
\,\bm{\mathrm{R}}{\rm{Spec}}\,
’ denotes the Toën-Vezzosi derived Yoneda functor that assigns to a (homotopy) simplicial commutative algebra
A
— a derived ring in terminology of
[
66
]
— the simplicial presheaf (prestack)

\bm{\mathrm{R}}{\rm{Spec}}(A):\,\mathrm{dAff}_{k}^{\rm op}:=\mathrm{sComm}_{k}\ \to\ \mathrm{sSet}\ ,\quad B\,\mapsto\,{\mathrm{Map}}(QA,\,B)\ ,

where
QA
is a cofibrant replacement of
A
and ‘
\mathrm{Map}
’ is the simplicial mapping space (function complex) in
\mathrm{sComm}_{k}
. The prestack
\bm{\mathrm{R}}{\rm{Spec}}(A)
satisfies the descent condition for étale hypercoverings and hence defines a derived stack (which is a derived affine scheme in the sense of
[
66
]
). On the other hand, for any pointed space (simplicial set)
X
, we can define the
pointed
mapping stack
\bm{\mathrm{Map}}_{*}(X,BG)
to be the homotopy fibre of the canonical map in the (homotopy) category of derived stacks:

(3.21)

\bm{\mathrm{Map}}_{*}(X,BG):={\rm hofib}\,[\bm{\mathrm{Map}}(X,BG)\,\to\,BG]\ ,

where
\bm{\mathrm{Map}}(X,BG)
stands for the (unpointed) derived mapping stack defined in
[
66
, 2.2.6.2]
. This last mapping stack is a basic object of derived algebraic geometry that plays an important role in applications (see, e.g.,
[
51
]
). Now, its relation to representation homology is clarified by the following

Proposition 3.1

(see
[
12
]
)
.

There is a
(
weak
)
equivalence of derived stacks

{\rm{DRep}}_{G}(\Gamma)\,\simeq\,\bm{\mathrm{Map}}_{*}(B\Gamma,BG)

For a detailed proof of Proposition
3.1
and more explanations we refer to
[
12
, Appendix A.1]
.

3.3.
Cyclic homology

We now define cyclic homology for homotopy simplicial groups. To this end, we will associate to each
\Gamma\in\mathrm{sGr}^{h}
a cyclic module
k[B^{\rm cyc}\Gamma]
that generalizes the classical cyclic bar construction
C_{\ast}(k[\Gamma])
when
\Gamma
is an ordinary discrete group.

Let
\Delta
denote the standard (co)simplicial category whose objects are finite ordered sets
[n]=\{0<1<\cdots<n\}
and morphisms are (nonstrictly) order preserving maps. The category
\Delta
is generated by two families of maps
d^{i}_{n}:[n-1]\to[n]
(
0\leqslant i\leqslant n
,
n\geqslant 1
) and
s^{j}_{n}:[n+1]\to[n]
(
0\leqslant j\leqslant n
,
n\geqslant 0
), called the (co)face and (co)degeneracy maps respectively. These maps satisfy the standard (co)simplicial relations listed, for example, in
[
44
, Appendix B.3]
. Connes’
cyclic category

\Delta C
is a natural extension of
\Delta
that has the same objects and is generated by the morphisms of
\Delta
and the cyclic maps
\tau_{n}:[n]\to[n]
,
n\geqslant 0
, satisfying
\tau_{n}^{n+1}={\rm Id}
(see
[
44
, 6.11]
). In fact,
\Delta C
can be characterized by the two properties:

(Cyc1)

For each
n\geqslant 0
,
{\rm Aut}_{\Delta C}([n])\cong C_{n+1}\,
, where
C_{n+1}=\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)\mathbb{Z}\,
,

(Cyc2)

Any morphism
f:[n]\to[m]
in
\Delta C
can be factored uniquely as
f=g\circ\varphi
, where
g\in{\rm Hom}_{\Delta}([n],[m])
and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}_{\Delta C}([n])\,
,

These properties show that
\Delta C
is a
crossed simplicial category

associated to the family of cyclic groups
\{C_{n+1}\}_{n\geqslant 0}
(see
[
44
, 6.3.0]
).

Now, if
\Gamma
is an ordinary discrete group, there is a natural functor

(3.22)

B^{\rm cyc}_{\ast}\Gamma\,:\,\Delta C^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{Set}

called the cyclic bar construction of
\Gamma
that has the property that
k[B^{\rm cyc}_{\ast}\Gamma]\cong C_{\ast}(k[\Gamma])
, where
C_{\ast}(k[\Gamma])
is the standard cyclic module associated to
k[\Gamma]
as an associative
k
-algebra. Explicitly, the functor (
3.22
) is defined by (see
[
44
, 7.3.10]
)

\displaystyle d_{i}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{i-1},g_{i}g_{i+1},\ldots,g_{n})&0\leqslant i<n\\
(g_{n}g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1})&i=n\end{cases}

\displaystyle s_{j}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g_{0},\ldots,g_{j},1,g_{j+1},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle t_{n}(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(g_{n},g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{n-1})

where
(g_{0},\ldots,g_{n})\in\Gamma^{n+1}
. Clearly,
\Gamma\mapsto B^{\rm cyc}_{\ast}\Gamma
gives a functor
B^{\rm cyc}_{\ast}:\mathrm{Gr}\to\mathrm{Set}^{\Delta C^{\rm op}}
. If we identify
\mathrm{Gr}=\mathrm{Set}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}_{\otimes}
as in (
3.1
), then it turns out that
B^{\rm cyc}_{\ast}
coincides with the pull-back functor for a certain natural map
\Psi_{\rm cyc}:\Delta C\to\mathfrak{G}
in
{\rm Cat}
. Specifically,

\left.\begin{aligned} \Psi_{\rm cyc}(d^{i}_{n})&:\langle n\rangle\xrightarrow{}\langle n+1\rangle\,,\qquad(x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-1})\mapsto\begin{cases}(x_{0},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i}x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n})\ ,&0\leq i<n\\

Lemma 3.3

For any discrete group

Proof.

3.4.
Derived character maps

Next, we will construct a family of natural transformations relating the cyclic homology to representation homology of a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma
. In the special case when
{\mathcal{H}}=\mathcal{O}({\rm{GL}}_{n})
, this family contains a distinguished element determined by the usual trace
{\rm{Tr}}_{n}
that gives the derived character map (
1.6
) announced in the Introduction. With our current definitions of representation and cyclic homology the construction is actually very simple.
It is based on two lemmas. The first one is a standard result of homological algebra that simply exhibits the naturality of derived tensor products (
3.13
).

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
f:{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor between small categories. For any complexes
\mathcal{N}\in\mathrm{Ch}(\mathrm{Mod}_{k}\,\mathcal{B}^{\rm op})
and
\mathcal{M}\in\mathrm{Ch}(\mathrm{Mod}_{k}\,\mathcal{B})
, there is a natural map
\,f^{*}\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{{\mathcal{A}},k}f^{*}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}\otimes^{\bm{L}}_{\mathcal{B},k}\mathcal{M}\,
in the derived category
\mathcal{D}(k)
of
k
-modules that induces

f^{*}:\ {\rm{Tor}}^{{\mathcal{A}}}_{\ast}(f^{*}\mathcal{N},\,f^{*}\mathcal{M})\,\to\,{\rm{Tor}}^{\mathcal{B}}_{\ast}(\mathcal{N},\,\mathcal{M})

To apply this lemma in our situation we recall that
every commutative Hopf
k
-algebra
{\mathcal{H}}
defines the covariant functor
\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}:\mathfrak{G}\to\mathrm{Mod}_{k}
by formula (
3.15
). Restricting this functor via
the morphism (
3.23
) gives rise to a cocyclic
k
-module that we denote

B_{\rm cyc}{\mathcal{H}}:=\Psi_{\rm cyc}^{\ast}(\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}):\ \Delta C\to{\rm Mod}_{k}

On the other hand, by Definition
3.4
,
\,\Psi_{\rm cyc}^{*}(k[\Gamma])=k[B^{\rm cyc}(\Gamma)]\,
for any homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma
. Thus, by Lemma
3.4
, the functor
\Psi_{\rm cyc}
induces a canonical map

(3.27)

\Psi^{*}_{\rm cyc}:\ {\rm{Tor}}^{\Delta C}_{\ast}(k[B^{\rm cyc}\,\Gamma],\,B_{\rm cyc}{\mathcal{H}})\ \to\ {\rm{Tor}}^{\mathfrak{G}}_{*}(k[\Gamma],\,\underline{{\mathcal{H}}})

The target of this map is precisely

\mathrm{HR}_{*}(\Gamma,{\mathcal{H}})
(see Definition
3.3
), while the domain differs
from
\mathrm{HC}_{*}(k[\Gamma])
in the second argument
of ‘Tor’ (
cf.
Definition
3.4
).
To connect the two Tor’s we will use the following lemma which we state in the language of affine algebraic groups.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
G
be an affine algebraic group defined over
k
, and let
\mathcal{O}(G)
be its coordinate algebra. There is a natural isomorphism

(3.28)

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\Delta C)}(k,\ B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)])\,\cong\,\mathcal{O}(G)^{G}

where
\mathcal{O}(G)^{G}
denotes the invariant subalgebra of
\mathcal{O}(G)

under the adjoint
G
-action.

Proof.

For
m\geq 0
, denote by
\pi_{m}:[0]\to[m]
the composition of maps
\,d^{0}_{m}\,d^{0}_{m-1}\,\ldots\,d^{0}_{1}\,
in
\Delta C
. It follows from (
3.24
) that
\Psi_{\rm cyc}(\pi_{m}):\langle 1\rangle\to\langle m+1\rangle
is the homomorphism of groups taking the generator
x
of
{\mathbb{F}}\langle x\rangle
to the product of generators
x_{0}x_{1}\ldots x_{m}
in
{\mathbb{F}}\langle x_{0},\ldots,x_{m}\rangle
. The corresponding map
[B_{\rm cyc}\mathcal{O}(G)](\pi_{m}):\mathcal{O}(G)\to\mathcal{O}(G)^{\otimes(m+1)}
can thus be identified with the the
m
-fold coproduct in
\mathcal{O}(G)
:

(3.29)

\Delta_{G}^{(m)}:\ \mathcal{O}(G)\to\mathcal{O}(G^{m+1})\,,\quad P\mapsto\big[(g_{0},g_{1},\ldots,g_{m})\mapsto P(g_{0}\,g_{1}\,\ldots\,g_{m})\big]\ .

Now, it is easy to check that, for a fixed
P\in\mathcal{O}(G)^{G}
, the maps
\Delta_{G}^{(m)}(P):\,k\to\mathcal{O}(G^{m+1})
taking
1\in k
to
\Delta_{G}^{(m)}(P)
assemble to
a morphism of cocylic modules
\,\Delta_{G}(P):\ k\,\to\,B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)]\,
, the commutativity with cyclic operators
\tau_{m}
being a consequence of the
G
-invariance of
P
.
We claim that the assignment
\,P\mapsto\Delta_{G}(P)\,
defines a
k
-linear isomorphism

(3.30)

\Delta_{G}:\ \mathcal{O}(G)^{G}\,\xrightarrow{\sim}\,{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\Delta C)}(k,\ B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)])\ .

The inverse of (
3.30
) can be constructed as follows. Let
\varphi\in{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\Delta C)}(k,\ B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)])
. Note that, for all
[m]\in\Delta C
, its components
\varphi_{[m]}:\,k\to\mathcal{O}(G)^{\otimes(m+1)}\cong\mathcal{O}(G^{m+1})
are
k
-linear maps. Define

, where
1\in k
. Since
\varphi
is a natural transformation,

\varphi_{[m]}(1)=\{[B_{\rm cyc}\mathcal{O}(G)](\pi_{m})\}(\varphi_{[0]}(1))=\{[B_{\rm cyc}\mathcal{O}(G)](\pi_{m})\}(T\varphi)=\Delta^{m}(T\varphi)\ ,

where
\Delta^{m}
is defined in (
3.29
).
Similarly, applying
B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)]
to the cyclic operators
\tau_{m}
in
\Delta C
, we have

\varphi_{[m]}(1)=\{[B_{\rm cyc}\mathcal{O}(G)](\tau_{m})\}(\varphi_{[m]}(1))\,,

from which it follows that
T\varphi(g_{0}\ldots g_{m})=T\varphi(g_{m}g_{0}\ldots g_{m-1})
for all
g_{0},\ldots,g_{m}\in G
. This is equivalent to the assertion that

Thus

defines a

{\rm{Hom}}_{\mathrm{Mod}_{k}(\Delta C)}(k,\ B_{\rm cyc}[\mathcal{O}(G)])\to\mathcal{O}(G)^{G}\,,\qquad\varphi\mapsto T\varphi\ .

It is clear from its construction that the above map is the inverse of (
3.30
).
∎

4.
Topological Realization of Derived Character Maps

In this and next sections, we will prove our main results (Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
) stated in the Introduction. Here we will construct
the required spaces and maps simplicially: in terms of homotopy colimits of small diagrams of simplicial sets
and associated natural maps. Then, in the next section, we will reproduce these maps in topological terms, using Goodwillie homotopy calculus and topological operads. The connection between the two approaches seems
instructive and deserves a further investigation.

4.1.
The space
X_{\Gamma}

Recall that
\mathfrak{G}
denotes the skeleton of the category of finitely generated free groups. There is a natural
abelianization functor

(4.1)

\underline{\mathbb{Z}}\,:\,\mathfrak{G}\to\mathrm{Set}\,,\qquad\langle n\rangle\mapsto\mathbb{Z}^{n}\ ,

that takes the free group
\langle n\rangle={\mathbb{F}}_{n}
to (the underlying set of) its abelianization
\langle n\rangle_{\rm ab}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. As in Section
2
, we can form the category of elements of (
4.1
), using the Grothendieck construction:

(4.2)

\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}\,:=\,\mathfrak{G}\!\smallint\underline{\mathbb{Z}}

The objects of
\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}
are given explicitly by

{\rm Ob}(\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}})\,=\,\{(\langle n\rangle;k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})):\langle n\rangle\in\mathfrak{G}\,,\,(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\}

and the morphism sets are

{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}}((\langle n\rangle;k_{1},\ldots,k_{n}),(\langle m\rangle;l_{1},\ldots,l_{m}))\,=\,\{\varphi\in{\rm Hom}_{\mathfrak{G}}(\langle n\rangle,\langle m\rangle):\ \varphi_{\rm ab}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})=(l_{1},\ldots,l_{m})\}

Note that the abelianized map
\,\varphi_{\rm ab}:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}^{m}\,
above is represented by an integral
(m\times n)
-matrix,
\varphi_{\rm ab}\in{\mathbb{M}}_{m\times n}(\mathbb{Z})
, and its action on
n
-tuples of integers is simply given by matrix multiplication. The category (
4.2
) comes together with the canonical (forgetful) functor

(4.3)

p:\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathfrak{G}\,,\qquad(\langle n\rangle;k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\mapsto\langle n\rangle\ .

Given a homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma:\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}\to\mathrm{sSet}

(4.4)

X_{\Gamma}\,:=\,|\,\mathrm{hocolim}_{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm op}}(p^{\ast}\Gamma)\,|\,,

where
p^{\ast}
is the pullback functor
\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}^{\rm op}}\to\mathrm{sSet}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm op}}
associated to (
4.3
). The relation of the space (
4.4
) to representation homology becomes clear from the following observation.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any
\Gamma
and any commutative ring
k
, there is a natural isomorphism

(4.5)

{\rm H}_{\ast}(X_{\Gamma},k)\,\cong\,\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,\mathbb{G}_{m}(k))

Proof.

We have the sequence of natural isomorphisms

H_{\ast}(X_{\Gamma},k)\,\cong\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\rm op}}_{\ast}(k,\,k[p^{\ast}\Gamma])\,\cong\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\ast}(k[p^{\ast}\Gamma],\,k)\,=\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}_{\mathbb{Z}}}_{\ast}(p^{\ast}k[\Gamma],\,k)\,\cong\,{\rm Tor}^{\mathfrak{G}}_{\ast}(k[\Gamma],\,k[\mathbb{Z}])\ ,

where the first two are standard (see, e.g.,
[
44
, Appendix C.10]
) and the last one follows from the classical Shapiro Lemma (see Lemma
2.4
). To complete the proof it remains to note that
k[\mathbb{Z}]
can be identified with
\mathcal{O}[{\mathbb{G}}_{m}(k)]
as a commutative Hopf algebra.
∎

As in the Introduction, we shorten notation for one-dimensional representation homology, writing

(4.6)

\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}(k[\Gamma])\,:=\,\mathrm{HR}_{\ast}(\Gamma,\mathbb{G}_{m}(k))\ .

Our next goal is to identify the homotopy type of the space
X_{\Gamma}
in terms of the classifying space of
\Gamma
. The following theorem is one of the main results of the present paper.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any homotopy simplicial group
\Gamma
, there is a weak equivalence in
{\rm Top}_{\ast}\!:

(4.7)

X_{\Gamma}\,\simeq\,\Omega\,{\rm SP}^{\infty}(B\Gamma)

where
B\Gamma
is the classifying space of
\,\Gamma

(
see Definition
3.2
)
.

Before proving this theorem, we recall a few basic facts about the Dold-Thom space and related constructions
(see, e.g.,
[
35
, Chap. 4.K]
). For any pointed connected CW complex
X
, the
Dold-Thom space

{\rm SP}^{\infty}(X)
is defined as the infinite symmetric product: namely,

(4.8)

{\rm SP}^{\infty}(X)\,=\,\varinjlim_{n}\,{\rm SP}^{n}(X)

where
{\rm SP}^{n}(X):=X^{n}\!/S_{n}
with

acting on

{\rm SP}^{n}(X)\to{\rm SP}^{n+1}(X)

where ‘

\pi_{i}[{\rm SP}^{\infty}(X)]\,\cong\,H_{i}(X,\mathbb{Z})\,,

that are natural in pointed connected CW complexes

There is an alternative way to obtain this fibration sequence, using Kan’s simplicial group model

of the space
5

5.
Topological character maps via Goodwillie calculus and operads

In this section, we will describe the maps
{\tt{CS}}
and
{\tt{SR}}
explicitly in topological terms, using
Goodwillie calculus and classical operads. The latter approach is based on ideas of Fiedorowicz
[
27
]
that were developed by Ault in
[
2
]
. The former is inspired by results of Biedermann and Dwyer that appeared in
[
18
]
. The interpretation in terms of Goodwillie derivatives leads to a natural nonlinear (polynomial) generalization of topological character maps that deserves a further study (see Section
5.4
).

5.1.
Goodwillie homotopy calculus

Goodwillie calculus provides a universal approximation (“Taylor decomposition”) of basic
homotopy functors that arise in topology in terms of polynomial functors. This method — introduced by T. Goodwillie in the series of papers
[
31
,
32
,
33
]
— has been studied extensively in recent years and has found many interesting applications (see, e.g., the survey papers
[
1
]
and
[
42
]
).

Recall that by a homotopy functor we mean a functor on topological spaces that preserves
weak homotopy equivalences. A homotopy functor
F:\mathrm{Top}_{*}\to\mathrm{Top}_{*}

is called
n
-excisive
(or
polynomial of degree

\leq n
) if it takes any strongly coCartesian
(n+1)
-dimensional cubical diagram in
\mathrm{Top}_{*}

to a Cartesian diagram (
cf.

[
1
, Definition 1.1.2]
).
For
n=0
, this simply means that
F
is homotopically constant: i.e.
F(X)\simeq F(\ast)
for any
X\in{\rm Top}_{\ast}
. For
n=1
, this is the usual Mayer-Vietoris
property: a functor
F
is
1
-excisive if and only if it
maps homotopy pushout squares to homotopy pullback squares in
{\rm Top}_{\ast}

(see
[
1
, Example 1.1.4]
). For
n>1
,
F
enjoys a higher dimensional version of the Mayer-Vietoris property that reduces to the usual one inductively in
n
.

The main construction of Goodwillie calculus can be described as follows (
cf.

[
33
, Theorem 1.8]
).

Theorem 5.1

(Goodwillie)
.

For any homotopy functor
\,F:{\rm Top}_{\ast}\to{\rm Top}_{\ast}\,
on pointed spaces, there exists a natural tower of functors
(
fibrations
)
under
F
:

(5.1)

\begin{diagram}

satisfying the following properties: for all
n\geq 0
,

(1)

\,P_{n}F:\,{\rm Top}_{\ast}\to{\rm Top}_{\ast}
is an
n
-excisive functor,

(2)

\delta_{n}:\,F\to P_{n}F
is the universal weak natural transformation to an
n
-excisive functor.

The last property needs an explanation. By a
weak
natural transformation
\,\delta:F\to P
one means a pair (‘zig-zag’) of natural transformations

\,F\xrightarrow{\delta^{\prime}}G\xleftarrow{\delta^{\prime\prime}}P\,
, where
\delta^{\prime\prime}
is a natural
weak equivalence, i.e.
\,\delta_{X}^{\prime\prime}:G(X)\xleftarrow{\sim}P(X)\,
is a weak homotopy equivalence for all spaces
X\in\mathrm{Top}_{*}
. Note that if
F
and
P
are homotopy functors, a weak natural transformation
\,\delta:F\to P
induces a well-defined natural tranformation between the corresponding functors on the homotopy category
{\mathtt{Ho}}(\mathrm{Top}_{*})
.
Property (2) of Theorem
5.1
then says that the weak natural transformation
\,\delta_{n}:\,F\to P_{n}F
is homotopically initial among all natural transformations from

to
n
-excisive functors.

Given a homotopy functor

to be the homotopy fibre

D_{n}F(X)\,:=\,{\rm hofib}\{P_{n}F(X)\xrightarrow{p_{n}}P_{n-1}F(X)\}

where

is the canonical projection at the
n

). A remarkable fact discovered in
[

(see

equipped with a (naïve) action of the symmetric group

where

are the suspension spectrum and the infinite loop space functors, respectively. The spectrum

6.
Stable character maps and derived Poisson brackets

In this section, we study the behavior of the derived character maps (
1.6
) in the limit as
\,n\to\infty
.
We show that, on simply connected spaces, these maps stabilize, inducing an isomorphism between the graded
symmetric algebra generated by the
S^{1}
-equivariant homology of the free loop space of
X=B\Gamma

and the invariant part of the representation homology in the projective limit
\varprojlim\mathrm{HR}_{*}(\Gamma,{\rm{GL}}_{n})^{{\rm{GL}}_{n}}
. This result is a topological counterpart of a stabilization theorem proved for representation homology of algebras in
[
10
]
. In case when
X
represents a closed manifold, so that its
S^{1}
-equivariant homology carries the Chas-Sullivan bracket, we show that the
stable character map is an isomorphism of Lie algebras, where the Lie bracket on representation homology
is induced by a natural derived Poisson structure on the Quillen model of
X
.

Jack Jeffries, David Lieberman

Abstract
. Bernstein’s inequality is a central result in the theory of D D -modules on smooth varieties. While Bernstein’s inequality fails for rings of differential operators on general singularities, recent work of Àlvarez Montaner, Hernández, Jeffries, Núñez-Betancourt, Teixeira, and Witt establishes Bernstein’s inequality for invariants of finite groups in characteristic zero and certain other mild singularities in positive characteristic. Motivated by extending this result to new classes of singular rings, we introduce a “two-sided” analogue of the Bernstein-Sato polynomial which we call the sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial. We apply this notion to give an effective criterion to verify Bernstein’s inequality, and apply this to show that Bernstein’s inequality holds for the coordinate ring of ℙ a × ℙ b \mathbb{P}^{a}\times\mathbb{P}^{b} via the Segre embedding. We also establish a number of examples and basic results on sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomials.

Introduction

Recently, a number of key results from the theory of
D
-modules on smooth varieties have been extended to
D
-modules over sufficiently mild singularities. Throughout this introduction, let
R
be an algebra over a field
K
of characteristic zero, and
D_{R}
the ring of
K
-linear differential operators in the sense of Grothendieck; when
R
is a polynomial ring, this is the familiar
Weyl algebra
generated over
R
by the partial derivatives with respect to the variables.

A
Bernstein-Sato functional equation
for an element
f\in R
is an equation of the form

\delta(s)\cdot f^{s+1}=b(s)f^{s}

for some
\delta(s)\in D_{R}[s]
and
b(s)\in K[s]
. The existence of a nonzero Bernstein-Sato functional equation for any nonzero element when
R
is smooth is one of the fundamental results in the theory of
D
-modules, and the study of the minimal monic
b(s)
arising in an equation as above for a fixed
f
(i.e., the
Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
f
) has played an important role in singularity theory and algebraic geometry
[
Ber72
,
Mal74
,
Mal83
,
ELSV04
,
MTW05
]
. Work of Àlvarez Montaner, Huneke, and Núñez-Betancourt
[
ÀHN17
]
extended this existence result to
K
-algebras that are direct summands of polynomial rings, a class of rings that includes invariant rings of linearly reductive groups.

Bernstein’s inequality
in the theory of
D
-modules asserts that every nonzero
D_{R}
-module over a smooth
K
-algebra
R
has dimension at least
\dim(R)
, for a suitable sense of dimension akin to the Krull dimension of a module over a commutative ring. This result has many strong consequences; in particular, it easily implies the existence of Bernstein-Sato polynomials, and that local cohomology modules of
R
have finite length as
D_{R}
-modules. Recent work of Àlvarez Montaner, Hernández, Jeffries, Núñez-Betancourt, Teixeira, and Witt extended this result to rings of invariants of finite groups, as well as a wider class of algebras in positive characteristic
[
ÀHJ+23
]
.

The work in this paper is motivated by extending Bernstein’s inequality to new classes of singular rings and studying the relationship between Bernstein’s inequality and Bernstein-Sato theory. These considerations motivate the following definition: a
sandwich Bernstein-Sato functional equation
for an element
f\in R
is an equation of the form

\sum\alpha_{i}(s)f^{s+1}\beta_{i}(s)=b(s)f^{s}

for some
\alpha_{i}(s),\beta_{i}(s)\in D_{R}[s]
and
b(s)\in K[s]
. Here we consider
f
as a differential operator of order zero and regard this as an equality of
differential operators
rather than of ring elements under the module action.

Our main result applies this notion of sandwich Bernstein-Sato equation to give an effective criterion for
R
to satisfy Bernstein’s inequality.

Theorem 1.1

(Corollary
6.2
, cf. Remark
2.40
)
.

Let

be a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}
-graded algebra over a field

-module

We apply our criterion to show that coordinate rings of Segre embeddings of the product of projective spaces satisfy Bernstein’s inequality.

2.1.
Rings of differential operators

Definition 2.1
.

Let
R
be an
A
-algebra, where
R
and
A
are commutative rings. The
ring of differential operators
on
R
is a subring of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R,R)
defined inductively (by order) as follows:

D^{0}_{R|A}=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(R,R),\quad\text{and}

D^{n+1}_{R|A}=\{\delta\in\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(R,R)\,|\,[\delta,\overline{r}]\in D^{n}_{R|A}\forall r\in D^{0}_{R|A}\},

where
[-,-]
denotes the commutator. The union

D_{R|A}=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}D^{n}_{R|A}

is a ring with composition as the multiplication operation.

For an element
\delta\in D_{R|A}
, the
order
of
\delta
, denoted
\operatorname{ord}(\delta)
, is the smallest
n
such that
\delta\in D^{n}_{R|A}
.

It will often be useful to repeatedly apply the commutator, and thus we adopt the following iterative notation:

Notation 2.2
.

For any positive integer
n
and operators
\delta
and
\beta
, we denote

•

[\delta]^{0}_{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\delta

•

[\delta]^{1}_{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\delta,\beta]

•

[\delta]^{n}_{\beta}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\big[[\delta]^{n-1}_{\beta},\beta\big]

•

[\delta]^{\underline{i}}_{{\underline{\beta}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}[\cdots[[\delta]^{i_{1}}_{\beta_{1}}]^{i_{2}}_{\beta_{2}}\cdots]^{i_{n}}_{\beta_{n}}
for any sequence of (not necessarily distinct) operators
\underline{\beta}=\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{n}
and any sequence of natural numbers
\underline{i}=i_{1},\ldots,i_{n}.

Example 2.3
.

Let
K
be a field of characteristic zero, and consider the polynomial ring

. The ring of differential operators

is the

Definition 2.4
.

Let
D
be the ring of differential operators on
R
where

is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded

-algebra. A differential operator has

(1)

.

Let

L

, Proposition 2.17]

f^{a_{1}}\delta_{1}f^{a_{2}}\delta_{2}\cdots f^{a_{t}}\delta_{t}=\sum_{i_{t}=0}^{\operatorname{ord}{\delta_{t}}}\cdots\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{\operatorname{ord}{\delta_{1}}}(-1)^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{t}}A_{t}\cdots A_{1}[\delta_{1}]_{f}^{i_{1}}\cdots[\delta_{t}]_{f}^{i_{t}}f^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{t}-i_{1}-\cdots-i_{t}}.

Proof.

We apply Lemma
2.11
to prove the first equality by induction on

an immediate consequence of the aforementioned equality.
Applying Lemma
2.11
to the last pair of products, we obtain

\delta_{1}f^{a_{1}}\delta_{2}f^{a_{2}}\cdots\delta_{t}f^{a_{t}}=\sum_{i_{t}=0}^{\operatorname{ord}{\delta_{t}}}\binom{a_{t}}{i_{t}}\delta_{1}f^{a_{1}}\delta_{2}f^{a_{2}}\cdots\delta_{t-1}f^{a_{t-1}+a_{t}-i_{t}}[\delta_{t}]_{f}^{i_{t}}.

By the induction hypothesis, we can rewrite

\delta_{1}f^{a_{1}}\delta_{2}f^{a_{2}}\cdots\delta_{t-1}f^{a_{t-1}+a_{t}-i_{t}}=\sum_{i_{t-1}=0}^{\operatorname{ord}{\delta_{t-1}}}\cdots\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{\operatorname{ord}{\delta_{1}}}A_{t-1}\cdots A_{1}f^{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{t}-i_{1}-\cdots-i_{t}}[\delta_{1}]_{f}^{i_{1}}\cdots[\delta_{t-1}]_{f}^{i_{t-1}},

where the constants

agree with those in the statement. Substituting in, the desired equality is seen to hold.

For the second equality, we proceed similarly.
∎

2.2.
Filtered rings and modules

Our notion of dimension for modules over rings of differential operators will be based on filtrations.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
A
be a ring (not necessarily commutative) and let

be a sequence of

-modules. We say that

or

•

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}

,

•

When

is a filtration on

-vector spaces with

Note that, with this convention, the order filtration is a filtration on

but not a

-filtration on

Definition 2.14

Let

,

•

\mathcal{G}^{\bullet}

,

(or

As in the definitions above, we will often use caligraphic font the name of a filtration and the corresponding roman letters for the elements in the sequence.

Remark 2.15

Let

of

4.
Bernstein inequality for bimodules and existence of sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomials

In this section, we will establish a version of Bernstein’s inequality for bimodules and apply this to obtain sufficient conditions for existence of sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomials. We will require a strengthening of the linear simplicity condition for a filtered algebra. In particular, it turns out that linear simplicity of the tensor product

We start by investigating this property.

Lemma 4.1

Let

be a field, and

(1-x\partial_{x})\partial_{y}(R)\subseteq(1-x\partial_{x})\partial_{y}\Big(\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
(i,j)\neq(1,0)\end{subarray}}K\cdot x^{i}y^{j}\Big)\subseteq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
i\neq 1\end{subarray}}K\cdot x^{i}y^{j}\subseteq R,

so
(1-x\partial_{x})\partial_{y}
is a valid differential operator on
R
.

We observe that there is a Bernstein-Sato functional equation

(1-x\partial_{x})\partial_{y}\cdot y^{s+1}=(s+1)y^{s},

so
\mathrm{BS}^{R}_{f}(s)=s+1
.
However,
s
divides
\mathrm{SBS}^{R}_{y}(s)
. Indeed, there is a two-sided ideal
\mathcal{J}
of
D_{R|\mathbb{C}}
that contains
y
but not
1
, since
H^{1}_{(x^{2},x^{3},y,xy)}(R)\cong S/R\cong\mathbb{C}
, which has annihilator
(x^{2},x^{3},y,xy)
as an
R
-module; the annihilator of
H^{1}_{(x^{2},x^{3},y,xy)}(R)
as a
D_{R|\mathbb{C}}
is a two-sided ideal.
Thus, given an SBS-functional equation

\sum_{i}\alpha_{i}(s)y^{s+1}\beta_{i}(s)=b(s)y^{s},

evaluating at
s=0
, the left-hand side is in
\mathcal{J}
, and since
y^{0}=1
, we must have
b(0)=0
.

Thus
\mathrm{BS}^{R}_{f}(s)\neq\mathrm{SBS}^{R}_{f}(s)
in this example.

To generate more examples in which the two notions differ, we now establish a method for detecting roots of sandwich-Bernstein-Sato polynomials via reduction modulo
p
. The following is a two-sided analogue of the result of Mustaţă, Takagi, and Watanabe
[
MTW05
]
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
T
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra and
R=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}T
. Let
f\in T
, and
b(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]
be a polynomial of a sandwich Bernstein-Sato functional equation for the image of
f
in
R
.
Then there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any prime
p>N
, and any two sided ideal
\mathcal{J}\subset\operatorname{End}_{(T/pT)^{p}}(T/pT)
, we have that

f_{p}^{v}\notin\mathcal{J}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{p}^{v+1}\in\mathcal{J}\quad\text{implies}\quad b(v)\equiv 0\text{ modulo }p,

where
f_{p}
denotes map of multiplication by the image of
f
modulo
p
.

Proof.

Let
R
,
T
, and
f
be as above. Fix a two-sided functional equation for
f
over
D_{R|\mathbb{Q}}
. Since differential operators for finitely generated algebras localize, we have
D_{R|\mathbb{Q}}\cong\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}D_{T|\mathbb{Z}}
, so the functional equation can be written as

\sum_{i}\frac{1}{m_{1}}\alpha_{i}(s)f^{s+1}\beta_{i}(s)=\frac{1}{m_{2}}a(s)f^{s}

with
\alpha_{i},\beta_{i}\in D^{n}_{T|\mathbb{Z}}[s]
,
a(s)=m_{2}b(s)\in\mathbb{Z}[s]
, and
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. We claim that the number
{N=\max\{m_{1},m_{2},n\}}
satisfies the statement.

Given
v\in\mathbb{N}
, since
m_{1},m_{2}
are units in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, by Proposition
2.10
(1) we can evaluate the equation at
s=v
and reduce modulo
p
to obtain an equation in
D_{T/pT|\mathbb{F}_{p}}
:

(6.0.1)

\sum_{i}\frac{1}{m_{1}}\alpha_{i}(v)f_{p}^{v+1}\beta_{i}(v)=\frac{1}{m_{2}}a(v)f_{p}^{v},

where
f_{p}
denotes the image of
f
in
T/pT=D^{0}_{T/pT}
.
Note that, by Proposition
2.10
(2)

\alpha_{i}(v),\beta_{i}(v)\in D^{n}_{T/pT|\mathbb{F}_{p}}\subseteq D^{p-1}_{T/pT|\mathbb{F}_{p}}\subseteq\operatorname{End}_{(T/pT)^{p}}(T/pT).

Thus, if
f_{p}^{v+1}\in\mathcal{J}
, the left-hand side of (
6.0.1
) is in
\mathcal{J}
; since
f_{p}^{v}\notin\mathcal{J}
, we must have
a(v)=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, which implies
b(v)=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
∎

Proposition 6.7
.

Let
T
be a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}
-algebra and
R=\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}T
. Let
f\in T
, and
b(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]
be a polynomial of a sandwich Bernstein-Sato functional equation for the image of
f
in
R
.

Suppose that there exists a polynomial
r(s)\in\mathbb{Q}[s]
and an infinite set of prime integers
P
such that for every
p\in P
, there is a two-sided ideal
\mathcal{J}_{p}
in
\operatorname{End}_{(T/pT)^{p}}(T/pT)
such that

r(p)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ f_{p}^{r(p)}\notin\mathcal{J}_{p},\ \text{and}\ f_{p}^{r(p)+1}\in\mathcal{J}_{p},

where the elements
f_{p}^{r(p)},f_{p}^{r(p)+1}\in\operatorname{End}_{(T/pT)^{p}}(T/pT)
denote the multiplication maps by the respective ring elements.
Then
r(0)
is a root of
b(s)
.

Proof.

Fix a number
N
as in the conclusion of the previous proposition; without loss of generality, suppose that every denominator of each coefficient of
r(s)
is less than
N
.

For any prime
p\in P
larger than
N
, by the proposition, we have
b(r(p))\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Since
b(r(p))\equiv b(r(0))\pmod{p}
, we obtain that
b(r(0))\equiv 0\pmod{p}
for infinitely many prime numbers
p
. This implies that that the numerator of
b(r(0))
is divisible by infinitely many primes, which implies that
b(r(0))=0
.
∎

Example 6.8
.

Let

and consider

in

.
We claim that for

and
\mathrm{SBS}^{R}_{f}(s)=(s+1)^{2}(s+2)

To see that

\displaystyle+y\partial_{x}f^{s+1}\partial_{y}^{2}

On the other hand, by Proposition

, we have

To this end, let

Note first that

7.
Bernstein’s inequality for the Segre product of projective spaces

We will apply the theory of sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomials to show that this ring
R
also satisfies Bernstein’s inequality.

Proposition 7.1
.

For a field
K
of characteristic zero and

R:=K[x_{i}y_{j}\,|\,1\leq i\leq a,1\leq j\leq b]\subseteq S:=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{a},y_{1},\ldots,y_{b}],

the element
x_{1}y_{1}
admits a nonzero sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial with roots
-1,-a,-b
.

Proof.

Let
K
be a field of characterstic zero.
First we exhibit a nonzero sandwich Bernstein-Sato equation. We compute for
i,j>1
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\big[x_{i}\partial_{x_{1}},[\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{y_{1}},(x_{1}y_{1})^{s+1}]\big]=(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}\big((s+1)^{2}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}-(s+1)x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}\big),

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j}

\displaystyle:=\big[y_{j}\partial_{y_{1}},[\partial_{y_{j}}\partial_{x_{1}},(x_{1}y_{1})^{s+1}]\big]=(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}\big((s+1)^{2}y_{j}\partial_{y_{j}}-(s+1)y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}\big).

We then have

\sum_{i=2}^{a}\mathcal{A}_{i}-\sum_{j=2}^{b}\mathcal{B}_{j}=(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}\big((s+1)(s+a)x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}-(s+1)(s+b)y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}\big)

in
D_{R}
, using that
\sum_{i=1}^{a}x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}-\sum_{j=1}^{b}y_{j}\partial_{y_{j}}=0
.

We compute further

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}:=[\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}},(x_{1}y_{1})^{s+1}]

\displaystyle=(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}\big((s+1)x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}+(s+1)y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}+(s+1)^{2}\big)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}:=[\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{y_{1}},x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}(x_{1}y_{1})^{s+1}]

\displaystyle=(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}\big((s+1)(x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}})^{2}+(s+2)(x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}})(y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}})

\displaystyle\hbox{}\qquad\qquad\qquad+2(s+1)^{2}(x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}})+(s+1)^{2}(y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}})+(s+1)^{3}\big).

Observe that the operators
E:=x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}
and
F:=y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}
commute in
D_{R}
.

One verifies easily that in the polynomial ring
\mathbb{C}[s,A,B,E,F]
, the polynomial

{(s+1)^{3}(s+A)(s+B)}
is an element of the ideal generated by

\displaystyle\{(s+1)(s+A)

\displaystyle E-(s+1)(s+B)F,(s+1)E+(s+1)F+(s+1)^{2},

\displaystyle(s+1)E^{2}+(s+1)EF+2(s+1)^{2}E+(s+1)^{2}F+(s+1)^{3}\};

in particular, the polynomial
(s+1)^{3}(s+A)(s+B)
is a right
\mathbb{C}[s,A,B,E,F]
-linear combination of the generators above. Since
x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}
and
y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}
commute in the ring
D_{R}
, there is a ring homomorphism from
\mathbb{C}[s,A,B,E,F]
to
D_{R}
mapping
A\mapsto a
,
B\mapsto b
,
E\mapsto x_{1}\partial_{x_{1}}
, and
F\mapsto y_{1}\partial_{y_{1}}
. Applying this homomorphism, we obtain that
(x_{1}y_{1})^{s}(s+1)^{3}(s+a)(s+b)
is a right
D_{R}
-linear combination of
\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D},\mathcal{A}_{i}\,(2\leq i\leq a),
and
\mathcal{B}_{j}\,(2\leq j\leq b)
. In particular, there is a sandwich Bernstein-Sato functional equation with polynomial
(s+1)^{3}(s+a)(s+b)
.

Now we claim that
-1,-a,-b
are all roots of the sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
x_{1}y_{1}
. That
-1
is a root is clear, e.g., since the Bernstein-Sato polynomial divides the sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial. To show that
-a
and
-b
are roots, we will apply Proposition
6.7
. Henceforth, we let
K
be a perfect field of characteristic
p>0
.

Set
I_{i}
to be the
R
-submodule of
S
generated by the monomials in the
y
variables of degree
i
and
J_{j}
to be the
R
-submodule of
S
generated by the monomials in the
x
variables of degree
j
. Note that
I_{i}
is isomorphic to the ideal
(x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{1}y_{b})^{i}R
, and
J_{j}
is isomorphic to the ideal
(x_{1}y_{1},\dots,x_{a}y_{1})^{j}R
.

We proceed to describe an
R^{p}
-module decomposition of
R
. We will use tuple notation for monomials:
\alpha
denotes an
a
-tuple of nonnegative integers,
|\alpha|
the sum of its entries,
||\alpha||
its largest entry (and similarly
\beta
denotes a
b
-tuple, etc.). As
K
-vectorspaces, we have

R=\bigoplus_{|\alpha|=|\beta|}K\cdot x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\qquad I_{i}=\bigoplus_{|\alpha|+i=|\beta|}K\cdot x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\quad\text{and}\quad J_{j}=\bigoplus_{|\alpha|=|\beta|+i}K\cdot x^{\alpha}y^{\beta}.

We have an
R^{p}
-module decomposition

R=F\oplus\bigoplus_{0<i<a}A_{i}\oplus\bigoplus_{0<j<b}B_{j},

where

F=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|=|\beta|\\
||\alpha||,||\beta||<p\end{subarray}}R^{p}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\qquad A_{i}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|=|\beta|+ip\\
||\alpha||,||\beta||<p\end{subarray}}I_{i}^{p}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},\quad\text{and}\quad B_{j}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}|\alpha|=|\beta|-jp\\
||\alpha||,||\beta||<p\end{subarray}}J_{j}^{p}x^{\alpha}y^{\beta},

and
I_{i}^{p}
and
J_{j}^{p}
denote the
R^{p}
submodules of
S^{p}
given by the set of
p
th powers of
I_{i}
and

, respectively.

We claim that
\phi(I_{i})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}M
for each
\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(I_{i},M)
, where
M=R
,
M=J_{j}
, or

with

. Since each module is fine graded (i.e., graded in the
\mathbb{N}^{a+b}
grading where each monomial is weighted by its exponent vector), it suffices to show that there is no fine graded map that sends monomial minimal generators to monomial minimal generators. Since any proper quotient of
I_{i}
has rank zero and each choice of

is torsionfree, any nonzero map

must be injective. If

or

with

, then

has fewer minimal generators than

, so no such injective map exists. If

, note the relation

between generators of

does not hold for any pair of monomial generators of

. This establishes the claim.

It follows that

\mathcal{J}:=\{\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{p}}(R,R)\ |\ \phi(A_{a-1})\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R\}

is a two-sided ideal of
\operatorname{Hom}_{R^{p}}(R,R)
, since for

and

we have

\psi\phi(A_{a-1})\subseteq\psi(\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R\quad\text{and}\quad\phi\psi(A_{a-1})\subseteq\phi(A_{a-1}+\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R)\subseteq\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R.

Assume that

.
Then
(x_{1}y_{1})^{p-a}\notin\mathcal{J}
, since
x_{1}^{a-1}x_{2}^{p-1}\cdots x_{a}^{p-1}y_{1}^{(a-1)p}\in A_{a-1}
. On the other hand
|\alpha|=|\beta|+(a-1)p
and

implies that each coordinate of
\alpha
, in particular the first coordinate, is at least

. Every monomial of

has a

-variable with an exponent at least

. Consequently,
(x_{1}y_{1})^{p-a+1}
times any monomial in

lies in
\mathfrak{m}^{[p]}R
, so
(x_{1}y_{1})^{p-a+1}\in\mathcal{J}
.

Thus, by Proposition
6.7
,
-a
is a root of the sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial of

over

. Similarly,
-b
is a root as well.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

In the example above, the Bernstein-Sato polynomial of
x_{1}y_{1}
in
R
equals that in

, since

is a differentially extensible direct summand of

in the sense of
[

; see

We conclude by applying the theory developed to establish various finiteness properties of for the ring of differential operators of the Segre product of polynomial rings.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
a,b>1
and
R=\mathbb{C}[x_{i}y_{j}\ |\ 1\leq i\leq a,1\leq j\leq b]
the the coordinate ring of the Segre embedding of

(1)

The ring

\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}

(2)

If

-module, and
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is a filtration on

compatible with
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}
, then
\operatorname{Dim}(M,\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})\geq\dim(R)
.

(3)

Every nonzero element of

has a nonzero sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial.

Proof.

For the first statement, notice first that
R_{x_{1}y_{1}}
is regular. Since
x_{1}y_{1}
has a sandwich Bernstein-Sato polynomial with no nonnegative integer roots, by Theorem
5.6
, any Bernstein filtration
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}
on

is linearly simple.

Since

is a direct summand of a polynomial ring (e.g., as the zeroth graded piece of

under the grading that gives each

.

The final statement follows from the first statement by Corollary
4.8
.
∎

Ab Initio Construction of Poincaré and AdS Particles
TaeHwan OH

Abstract
We study the construction of a manifestly covariant worldline action from a coadjoint orbit. A coadjoint orbit is a submanifold in the dual vector space of the Lie algebra and generated by coadjoint actions. Since a coadjoint orbit is a symplectic space, we derive the worldline particle action from the symplectic two-form. One subtlety in formulating worldline particle actions from coadjoint orbits is choosing the coordinate system that sufficiently illustrates physical properties of the particles. We introduce Hamiltonian constraints by the defining conditions of the isometry. This allows us to write a manifestly covariant worldline action. We demonstrate our method to the both massive and massless particles in Minkowski and AdS spacetime.

2.1
Review on the Coadjoint Orbit

For a Lie group
G
, there is a left-invariant vector space called Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
and there exists a Lie algebra dual space
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}

which form a pairing
\langle\cdot\,,\cdot\rangle
with
{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\langle{\cal T}^{a}\,,T_{b}\rangle=\delta^{a}_{b}\,,

(2.1)

where
T_{a}
and
{\cal T}^{a}
are generators of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, respectively.
Note that when
G
is a simple group, then
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\simeq{\mathfrak{g}}

since there exists a non-degenerated bilinear form.
A group action on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
, denoted by
{\rm Ad}^{*}_{g}
,
is called a coadjoint action, and given as a dual of the adjoint action:

\langle{\rm Ad}_{g}^{*}\phi\,,X\rangle=\langle\phi\,,{\rm Ad}_{g^{-}1}X\rangle\,.

(2.2)

where
\phi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\,,\ X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\,,
and
g\in G
.
A coadjoint orbit
{\cal O}^{\phi}
is the submanifold generated by coadjoint actions on
\phi
, such that

{\cal O}^{\phi}=\{\varphi\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\,|\,{\rm Ad}^{*}_{g}\phi=\varphi\,,\ g\in G\}\,,

(2.3)

and the stabiliser
G^{\phi}
is the isotropic subgroup for the coadjoint action:

G^{\phi}=\{g\in G\,|\,{\rm Ad}^{*}_{g}\phi=\phi\}\,.

(2.4)

Since different
\varphi
’s within
{\cal O}^{\phi}
belong to the same equivalence class
with equivalent relation
\phi\sim\varphi={\rm Ad}_{g}^{*}\phi
,
a specific vector
\phi
can serve as a representative vector of
{\cal O}^{\phi}
.

One can immediately find that the coadjoint action
{\rm ad}^{*}_{X}
by the Lie algebra vector
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}

is given as

\langle{\rm ad}_{X}^{*}\phi\,,Y\rangle=-\langle\phi\,,{\rm ad}_{X}Y\rangle=-\langle\phi\,,[X\,,Y]\rangle\,.

(2.5)

The tangent space of the coadjoint orbit is given as

T_{\varphi}{\cal O}^{\phi}=\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}\ |\,{\rm ad}^{*}_{X}\phi=\varphi\,,\ \varphi\in{\cal O}^{\phi}\}\,,

(2.6)

and the stabiliser subalgebra is given as

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\phi}=\{X\in G\,|\,{\rm ad}^{*}_{X}\phi=0\}\,.

(2.7)

From (
2.5
), a stabiliser subalgebra element
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\phi}
should satisfy

\langle\phi\,,[X,Y]\rangle=0\,,

(2.8)

for all
Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Note that
G
can be regarded as a principle
G^{\phi}
–bundle over
{\cal O}^{\phi}
with projection
\pi_{\phi}
:

\displaystyle\pi_{\phi}\,:\ G

\displaystyle\rightarrow{\cal O}^{\phi}\,,

(2.9)

\displaystyle g

\displaystyle\mapsto\pi_{\phi}(g)={\rm Ad}^{*}_{g}\phi\,,

then
{\cal O}^{\phi}
is a homogeneous space, given as

{\cal O}^{\phi}\simeq G/G^{\phi}\,.

(2.10)

One important feature of coadjoint orbits are their nature as a symplectic space,
that is,
{\cal O}^{\phi}
is always even-dimensional manifold,
and a symplectic form
\omega
,
known as a Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau(KKS) two-form, exist.
KKS two-form on
\varphi\in{\cal O}^{\phi}
, denoted by
\omega_{\varphi}
, is expressed as

\omega_{\varphi}\left({\rm ad}^{*}_{X}\varphi\,,{\rm ad}^{*}_{Y}\varphi\right)=\langle\varphi\,,[X,Y]\rangle\,.

(2.11)

Using the projection operation in (
2.9
),
the pull-back of the KKS two-form
\omega
onto group
G
is written as

\Omega\equiv\pi^{*}_{\phi}(\omega)=\langle\phi\,,[\Theta(X),\Theta(Y)]\rangle=-{\rm d}\langle\phi\,,\Theta\rangle\,,

(2.12)

where
\Theta
is a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued left-invariant one-form on
G
, called Maurer-Cartan one-form,
which is expressed as
\Theta=g^{-1}{\rm d}g
.
We used the Maurer-Cartan equation in the last equality in (
2.12
).

Since the two-form
\Omega
is an exact form on
G
, we can find a symplectic potential
\theta
as

\theta=\langle\phi\,,\Theta\rangle\,,

(2.13)

so a worldline action is written in terms of the symplectic potential by

S=\int_{\gamma}\theta=\int_{\gamma}\langle\phi\,,\Theta\rangle=\int_{\gamma}\langle\phi\,,g^{-1}{\rm d}g\rangle\,,

(2.14)

where
\gamma\in G
is a path lying in the group.

2.2
Manifestly Covariant Worldline Action

So far, we discussed how to derive a worldline action from a coadjoint orbit.
However, a subtlety arises when finding an appropriate coordinate system of the orbit.
We have chosen covariance as our criterion for the appropriateness of the coordinate system.
Manifestly covariant worldline actions are formulated
by incorporating Hamiltonian constraints into the action.
As we will show later, we will use Hamiltonian constraints as the defining conditions
of the group.
To see more detailed discussions about the choice of the coordinates,
see Section 2.5 of
[
7
]
.

Manifestly covariant worldline actions for
various groups, classical Lie groups and semi-direct groups,
are formulated systematically
in our previous paper (see
[
7

).
In the rest of the section, we will focus on
SO(2,d-1)
and

which are isometry groups of the

5
Conclusion

We have discussed the construction of manifestly covariant worldline actions
using orbit method by introducing Hamiltonian constraints
that make possible to formulate
ab initio
worldlline action.
To describe Poincaré particles, we choose the inhomogeneous representative vector
for the mass type of the particle
then we find the homogeneous representative vectors from the scalar particle stabiliser subalgebra.
From the representative vector, one can easily find the stabiliser algebra
and identify the geometry of the coadjoint orbit.

For AdS particles, we chose the representatives which can be regarded as analogues
of the Poincaré massive particles.
Remarkably, when the mass parameter becomes equal to the spin one, we find a smaller coadjoint orbit
which can be interpreted as the phase space of the AdS massless particle.

Starting with these representatives, we can formulate worldline actions.
One systematic and convenient approach to construct worldline actions with more appropriate coordinate
is by imposing the defining condition of the isometry as the Hamiltonian constraint.
In this manner, we can construct a manifestly covariant worldline action.
When we compare the stabiliser subalgebra with the first-class constraint,
not only the coadjoint orbits and the physical phase spaces of particle have the same degrees of the freedom,
but also the stabiliser algebras are equivalent to the first-class constraints
if we ignore
d
-dependent parts of the stabiliser algebras like
{\mathfrak{so}}(d-n)
or
{\mathfrak{iso}}(d-n)
.
This equivalence between two objects gives a hint of the dual pair correspondence,
which implies that there exist one-to-one correspondence between
coadjoint orbit of the isometry and the constraint.

The method discussed in this paper can be adapted to different contexts,
such as twistorial particles or particles obeying different isometries like dS or Galilean symmetry.
Furthermore, we hope that this work shed new light on the various physics topics
making use of worldline action.

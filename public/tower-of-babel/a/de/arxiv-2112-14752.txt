Interaction-induced velocity renormalization in magic angle twisted trilayer graphene
Laura Classen, J. H. Pixley, Elio J. König

Abstract
Twistronics heterostructures provide a novel route to control the electronic single particle velocity and thereby to engineer strong effective interactions. Here we show that the reverse may also hold, i.e. that these interactions strongly renormalize the band structure. We demonstrate this mechanism for mirror-symmetric magic angle twisted trilayer graphene at charge neutrality and in the vicinity of a phase transition which can be described by an Ising Gross-Neveu critical point corresponding, e.g., to the onset of valley Hall or Hall order. While the non-interacting model displays massless Dirac excitations with strongly different velocities, we show that interaction corrections make them equal in the infrared. However, the RG flow of the velocities and of the coupling to the critical bosonic mode is strongly non-monotonic and dominated by the vicinity of a repulsive fixed point. We predict experimental consequences of this theory for tunneling and transport experiments and discuss the expected behavior at other quantum critical points, including those corresponding to intervalley coherent ordering.

Introduction
:
Manipulating the nature of low-energy electronic excitations in solid-state compounds has remained a long standing goal in condensed matter physics. Thanks to the recent advances in the isolation and manipulation of two-dimensional materials, it is now possible to dramatically modify the low energy band structure by twisting (i.e. rotating) and stacking.
As first demonstrated in twisted bilayer graphene (TBG), the moiré pattern formed from the interference between the twisted and stacked layers greatly renormalizes the excitation velocity

;

Beyond stacking and twisting two sheets of atoms, recent experiments have shown it is possible to twist various arrangements of a number of graphene layers

;

;

In particular, symmetrically twisted multi-layer graphene systems, where the layers are rotated by an alternating angle
\pm\theta

Khalaf et al. 2019

Thus, they offer an alternative setting where strongly renormalized narrow bands appear at a magic angle (where the Dirac velocity vanishes) as in TBG. For an odd number of layers, these TBG-like bands are supplemented by fast Dirac dispersing bands due to the symmetric nature of the system.

Experiments on TTG have discovered a readily tunable superconducting ground state,

When Dirac electrons reside close to a phase transition and interact with critical bosonic modes,

, diverge

While in TTG devices, the vicinity to external gates implies effective short-range interactions, we highlight that

In contrast to the single-particle magic-angle phenomenon, many-body effects are crucial to the physics in the examples above.
This raises the question how interactions affect the velocity renormalization in TTG. Do the velocities of fast and slow Dirac modes approach each other and does this work for or against the correlation effects in the TBG-like bands which are rooted in the vanishing Dirac velocity?

In this paper we address the velocity renormalization combining the single-particle effects
in a moiré system with the many-body physics of electron-electron interactions.
To this end, we study an Ising Gross-Neveu (GN) theory with a generic ratio of fast and slow Dirac fermions employing a renormalization group (RG) procedure in
D=2+1
dimensions.
This describes the low energy properties of TTG near charge neutrality in the vicinity of a quantum critical point, which separates a Dirac semimetal phase from a gapped (valley) quantum Hall state.
The critical interaction strength can be smoothly varied by changing the twist, which allows us to connect the untwisted critical point to the ground state at the magic-angle
Brillaux et al. 2020
, Fig.
1
b).
We demonstrate that slow and fast Dirac excitations cannot be considered separately as often done in TTG. As a result of their strong influence on each other the slow excitations become faster and the fast ones slower ensuring emergent Lorentz symmetry at the asymptotic critical point.
Overall, the system becomes more correlated in the sense that the mean velocity decreases. More generally, the asymptotic
mean velocity
is determined by the ratio of the number of fast and slow modes.
We estimate the velocity renormalization to be a measureable effect on length scales accessible in experiment and predict the simultaneous suppression of the quasiparticle weight.

Model.

As an effective model for TTG near charge neutrality, we consider the generalized Gross-Neveu theory
(
\hbar=1
throughout and
\sigma_{x,y,z}
are Pauli matrices in sublattice space)

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\pm}}\bar{\psi}_{\alpha,\pm}[\partial_{\tau}+v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+g_{\pm}\phi\sigma_{z}]\psi_{\alpha,\pm}+\frac{N}{2}\phi^{2},

(1)

which describes fast (+) and slow (-) Dirac fermions with
2N=(N_{+}+N_{-})

v_{\pm}
. To leading order, they are related to the twist angle via

\frac{v_{-}}{v_{+}}=\frac{1-3\alpha^{2}}{1+6\alpha^{2}},

(2)

;
Khalaf et al. 2019

The magic angle defined by vanishing velocity occurs at

Interactions are mediated by the Ising order parameter field
\phi

By symmetry,

Sup

The case

(spin + valley) and

(spin + valley + mini-valley) applies to
TTG in the vicinity of the magic angle.
More generally, the model captures the dynamical mass generation in Dirac materials with an arbitrary number of slow and fast flavors near the onset of spontaneous order that breaks an Ising symmetry.

Analysis of RG flow.
The renormalization group equations of the ratio of velocities and coupling constant are closed, i.e. the right hand side of Eqs. (
5a
),(
5b
) only depends on the two running coupling constants
\rho_{g},\rho_{v}
themselves.
We solve these equations numerically and present the solution in Fig.
3
a).
We find an attractive fixed point
\rho_{v}=\rho_{g}=1
, where Lorentz symmetry is emergent and two lines of repulsive fixed points where only one of the two fermion species couples to the critical boson,
\rho_{g}\in\{0,\infty\}
. In particular, the two fixed points at
\rho_{v}=0=\rho_{g}
and
\rho_{v}=\infty=\rho_{g}
are connected to the attractive fixed point by a separatrix. Generally, during the evolution the two coupling constants,
\rho_{v,g}
approach this separatrix in a first, quick RG stage, and then slowly approach isotropy.
This shows that, independent on the difference between bare velocities and coupling constants, the fast and slow Dirac fermions cannot be considered separately once correlation effects are taken into account.

To describe the RG flow for TTG, we choose
N_{-}=2N_{+}
and starting values
\rho_{v}\gg 1

\rho_{g}\sim 1

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln Z_{\psi}^{-}}{d\ln b}

\displaystyle\approx-\frac{1}{N\pi^{2}},\quad\frac{d\ln Z_{\phi}}{d\ln b}\approx-\frac{4}{N\pi^{2}},

Observables and consequences for experiments.
Observables which allow to experimentally access the velocity renormalization include quantum oscillations, as exploited to uncover the velocity renormalization in suspended graphene
Elias et al. 2011
. Another probe is the tunneling density of states that within our RG calculation is given by

\nu(E)=\sum_{\pm}N_{\pm}\frac{Z_{\psi}^{\pm}(b_{E})}{2\pi v_{\pm}^{2}(b_{E})}|E|.

(9)

It not only measures the velocity renormalization, but also the suppression of the quasi-particle weight at the corresponding energy scale
b_{E}=\bar{v}\Lambda/E
, see Fig.
3
c.

Based on our estimate for the extent of the first stage of the RG flow, we expect that experiments mainly explore this first regime.
For example, for transport experiments, the maximum RG time can be estimated as
\ln(b_{\rm final})-\ln(b_{\rm initial})\sim\ln(500K/10mK)\sim 10
, where
50meV\sim 500K
is the mini bandwidth of the fast bands and
10mK
the base temperature of dilution refrigerators.

At the same time,
the bare coupling constants are a priori unknown and variations in twist angle affect the bare
\rho_{v}
in TTG. In addition, other Dirac materials also have different starting values. In these situations
other segments of the RG flow can be probed. This is the rationale behind the above complete theoretical analysis of the RG flow.
Assuming starting values as in Fig.
3

\ln(b)\in(0,10)
,
\rho_{g}
doubles and
\rho_{v}
increases by 20 % - both are certainly measurable effects.

Another implication of our analysis regards the quantum-critical regime as the system is tuned across the quantum phase transition, where experiments can probe quantum critical fluctuations akin to what has been observed in strongly correlated materials. To this end, due to the parametrically slow RG flow, we define ’local’ scaling exponents for the correlation length and anomalous dimensions via
1/\tilde{\nu}=d\ln m/d\ln b
,
\tilde{\eta}_{\psi\pm}=-d\ln Z_{\psi}^{\pm}/2d\ln b
and
\tilde{\eta}_{\phi}=1-d\ln Z_{\phi}/d\ln b
using Eqs. (

)-(

). These are not the standard universal critical exponents but scale-dependent quantities, unless the RG flow reaches one of the fixed points.
However, they establish an analytical relationship between the velocity renormalization and experimentally observable exponents.
At the attractive Lorentz-symmetric fixed point, they reduce to the universal scaling exponents of the Ising GN model

;
Khveshchenko and Paaske 2001
:

\nu=1+8/(3\pi^{2}N)
,
\eta_{\psi+}=\eta_{\psi-}=1/(3\pi^{2}N)
,
\eta_{\phi}=1-16/(3\pi^{2}N)
.

Effect of displacement field and Dirac node offset.
A major experimental tuning knob in twisted trilayer graphene is the displacement field
D_{0}
. The displacement field generates a hybridization of order
D_{0}
of fast and slow modes located at the mini-
K
point. This hybridization vertically splits the two Dirac points to energies
\pm D_{0}
and generates a Fermi surface of size
p_{F}=D_{0}/\sqrt{v_{+}v_{-}}
at charge neutrality.
The renormalization group flow calculated above describes the system at energies larger than
D_{0}
. The evaluation of running coupling constants at the scale
D_{0}
serves as bare values for the physics at lower energies, which is not of Gross-Neveu type (instead, e.g., logarithmic Fermi surface instabilities may occur in Cooper and density wave channels).

We also comment on a possible offset in energy between the Dirac node of fast and slow fermions. Such an offset would also provide an infrared cut-off for the integral of RG equations, beyond which the impact of the Fermi surface in one of the two carrier types becomes relevant.
Ab initio
Park et al. 2021b
calculations for TTG predict an offset of the order of meV which is thus small as compared to the mini-band width.

Conclusions:

Motivated by the recent discoveries in TTG,
we have presented the leading renormalization group equations for an Ising Gross-Neveu theory in
D=2+1
dimensions where the order parameter field couples to
N_{+}
fast and
N_{-}
slow fermion fields. We found a two-stage RG flow, with an ultimate Lorentz-symmetric fixed point at which all fermion velocities are equal, but the mean velocity is reduced, thereby enhancing correlation physics.
We predict that this velocity renormalization leads to an increase of the slow and decrease of the fast Dirac velocity in TTG.
We remark that a U(1) generalization of our Gross-Neveu theory would correspond to intervalley coherent order in TTG
Christos et al. 2021
, which is expected to display qualitatively similar behavior to the velocity renormalization and critical exponents we have found based on studies of the XY-Gross-Neveu model
Rosenstein et al. 1993
;
Roy et al. 2016
;
Zerf et al. 2017
;
Zerf et al. 2016
;
Li et al. 2017
;
Classen et al. 2017
;
Iliesiu et al. 2018
. The generalization of our theory to experimentally relevant
Park et al. 2021a
;
Zhang et al. 2021
twisted pentalayar graphene and other van-der-Waals materials with more than two Dirac velocities is left for future work.
Beyond our application to TTG at charge neutrality, where
N_{+}=4
and
N_{-}=8
, we highlight that our theory may also apply to TTG in the vicinity of correlated insulating states at integer filling per moiré unit cell. Following the paradigm of a cascade
Zondiner et al. 2020
;
Wong et al. 2020
of resetting Dirac node energies, the effective theory may again be described by Eq. (
1
), yet with a reduced number of slow modes
N_{-}<8
.

Here, we have introduced the Hubbard-Stratonovich field
\vec{\phi}=(\phi_{+},\phi_{-})^{T}
, which is composed of the order parameter field in fast and slow sectors and we suppress the flavor index
\alpha
.
Importantly, the order parameter fields in fast and slow sectors have the same symmetry properties, such that the microscopic interaction generally couples them, i.e.
g_{+-}\neq 0
.

In the large N limit, the instability occurs when the inverse RPA propagator

D^{-1}(\omega=0,\vec{q}=0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{++}^{2}&g_{+-}^{2}\\
g_{+-}^{2}&g_{--}^{2}\end{array}\right)^{-1}+\frac{1}{N}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Pi_{+}&0\\
0&\Pi_{-}\end{array}\right)

(S4)

acquires a zero mode (here
\Pi_{\pm}
is the fermionic susceptibility at zero external frequency and momentum). One may expand
\vec{\phi}=\phi\hat{e}_{0}+\phi_{\perp}\hat{e}_{\perp}
, where
D^{-1}(\omega=0,\vec{q}=0)\hat{e}_{0}=0
at criticality, while
D^{-1}(\omega=0,\vec{q}=0)\hat{e}_{\perp}=C\hat{e}_{\perp}
,
C>0
. Thus
\hat{e}_{0}\hat{e}_{0}^{T}
is the projector on the interaction channel which first acquires a zero mode, as determined by the condition
\det[D^{-1}(\omega=0,\vec{q}=0)]=0
or

1+(g_{++}^{2}\Pi_{+}+g_{--}^{2}\Pi_{-})/N=(g_{+-}^{4}-g_{++}^{2}g_{--}^{2})\Pi_{+}\Pi_{-}/N^{2}.

(S5)

Clearly, this condition reduces the number of independent coupling constants
g_{++},g_{--},g_{+-}
from three to two.

In the main text, we tacitly project the interaction onto the critical channel
\hat{e}_{0}
and drop the massive modes
\hat{e}_{\perp}
altogether (the impact of virtual fluctuations can be assumed to be weak without changing qualitative features of the theory). Technically, the projection amounts to keeping only fluctuations
\vec{\phi}=\phi\hat{e}_{0}
in Eq. (
S3
), i.e.

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm int}

\displaystyle=\frac{N\phi^{2}}{2}\hat{e}_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{++}^{2}&g_{+-}^{2}\\
g_{+-}^{2}&g_{--}^{2}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\hat{e}_{0}

\displaystyle+\phi\left(\bar{\psi}_{+}\sigma_{z}\psi_{+},\bar{\psi}_{-}\sigma_{z}\psi_{-}\right)\hat{e}_{0}.

(S8)

We define
\bar{g}^{-2}=\hat{e}_{0}^{T}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}g_{++}^{2}&g_{+-}^{2}\\
g_{+-}^{2}&g_{--}^{2}\end{array}\right)^{-1}\hat{e}_{0}
and absorb
\phi\rightarrow\bar{g}\phi
. Then, by comparison with Eq. (
1
) of the main text,
g_{+}=\bar{g}(1,0)^{T}\hat{e}_{0}
,
g_{-}=\bar{g}(0,1)^{T}\hat{e}_{0}
.

We conclude this section with an estimate of
g_{+}
,
g_{-}
in terms of
g_{++},g_{+-},g_{--}
in the important limit
|\Pi_{++}|\ll|\Pi_{--}|\sim g_{++}\sim g_{+-}\sim g_{--}
. Criticality is driven by slow modes, leading to
\hat{e}_{0}=(g_{-+}^{2},g_{--}^{2})/\sqrt{g_{-+}^{4}+g_{--}^{4}}
,
\hat{e}_{\perp}=(g_{--}^{2},-g_{-+}^{2})/\sqrt{g_{-+}^{4}+g_{--}^{4}}
, with the approximate mean field transition at
1+g_{--}\Pi_{-}/N=0
. By consequence, we can estimate the parameters of Eq. (
1
) of the main text as
g_{+}=g_{-+}^{2}/g_{--},g_{-}=g_{--}
.

S2
Model and mean field solution

As outlined in the main text and in the previous section of this supplement, we consider the model

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\pm}}\bar{\psi}_{\alpha,\pm}[\partial_{\tau}+v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+g_{\pm}\phi\sigma_{z}]\psi_{\alpha,\pm}+\frac{N}{2}\phi^{2}.

(S9)

We assume
N_{+}+N_{-}=2N\gg 1
. It is convenient to parametrize

g_{\pm}=\bar{g}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1/2},\quad v_{\pm}=\bar{v}\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2},

(S10)

where
\bar{g}=\sqrt{g_{+}g_{-}}
and
\rho_{g}=g_{+}/g_{-}
and analogously for velocity. We absorb
\bar{g}
into
\phi
, then

\mathcal{L}=\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\pm}}\bar{\psi}_{\alpha,\pm}[\partial_{\tau}+v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+\rho_{g}^{\pm 1/2}\phi\sigma_{z}]\psi_{\alpha,\pm}+\frac{N}{2\bar{g}^{2}}\phi^{2}.

(S11)

In the case,
N_{+}=N_{-}=N
and
v_{+}=v_{-}=\rho_{g}=1
, this model is the same as the Gross-Neveu(-Yukawa) model, see Ref.
Rosenstein et al. 1991
of the main text.
Note that the energy dimension of
\phi
and
\psi
is one, and of
\bar{g}^{2}
is -1.

S2.1
Fermionic Green’s function

When the
\phi
field condenses, the inverse fermionic Green’s functions are

G_{\pm}^{M_{\pm}}(\textbf{p})^{-1}=(i\epsilon-v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}-M_{\pm}\sigma_{z}),

(S12)

where
M_{\pm}=\rho_{g}^{\pm 1/2}\langle\phi\rangle\equiv\rho_{g}^{\pm 1/2}\bar{M}
. We use the notation of a bold symbol
\mathbf{p}=(\epsilon,\vec{p})
for energy-momentum vectors. Inverting the matrix yields

G_{\pm}^{M_{\pm}}(\textbf{p})=-\frac{i\epsilon+v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+M_{\pm}\sigma_{z}}{\epsilon^{2}+v_{\pm}^{2}\vec{p}^{2}+M_{\pm}^{2}}.

(S13)

We use non-zero
M_{\pm}
to simplify some calculations, but generally we are interested in the symmetric phase and consider
M_{\pm}\rightarrow 0
.

S2.2
Polarization operator and effective boson propagator

The polarization operator (or susceptibility), Fig.
2
a) of the main text, of each fermionic species is

\displaystyle\Pi_{\pm}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle N_{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\int_{\textbf{p}}\text{tr}^{\sigma}[G_{\pm}^{M_{\pm}}(\textbf{p}+\textbf{q}/2)G_{\pm}^{M_{\pm}}(\textbf{p}-\textbf{q}/2)]

(S14)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\pm}}{\pi}\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{v_{\pm}^{2}}\Big[-({v_{\pm}\Lambda/2}-M_{\pm}/2)

\displaystyle+\frac{4M_{\pm}^{2}+q_{\pm}^{2}}{4q_{\pm}}\arctan\left(\frac{q_{\pm}}{2M_{\pm}}\right)\Big].

We introduced the notation
q_{\pm}=\sqrt{\omega^{2}+v_{\pm}^{2}\vec{q}^{2}}
, the ultraviolet cut-off
\Lambda
(
\vec{p}^{2}<\Lambda^{2}
) and
\textbf{q}=(\omega,\vec{q})
,
\textbf{p}=(\epsilon,\vec{p})
.
Thus, to leading order in
N\gg 1
we get the effective bosonic theory

\mathcal{L}_{\rm eff}[\phi]=\frac{N}{2}\int_{\textbf{q}}\phi(-\textbf{q})\underbrace{[\frac{1}{\bar{g}^{2}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\pm}\Pi_{\pm}(\textbf{q})]}_{\equiv D^{-1}(\textbf{q})/N}\phi(\textbf{q}).

(S15)

We see that, in the symmetric phase where
\bar{M}=0
, the effective mass of bosons is given by

\displaystyle m

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{g}^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\sum_{\pm}\alpha_{\pm}\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2},

(S16)

where
\alpha_{\pm}=\frac{N_{\pm}}{N}\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}}
. We stated this in Eq. (
3
) of the main text.

m=0
defines the mean field transition. We remark that in Ref.
Rosenstein et al. 1991
of the main text, the UV cutoff is defined as
{\frac{\bar{v}\pi\Lambda}{2}}={\Lambda_{\rm Rosenstein}}
. From Eq. (
S15
), we obtain the effective bosonic propagator

D(\textbf{q})=\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{m+\sum_{\pm}\alpha_{\pm}q_{\pm}/(8\bar{v}^{2})}.

(S17)

The dimension of the propagator is 1/energy.
At the isotropic point we can set
v_{+}=v_{-}
and
\alpha_{\pm}=1
.

For later reference, we also remark the following relations here. In
the calculation of the fermionic self-energy we need

D(\textbf{q}+\textbf{p})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textbf{p}\ll\textbf{q}}}{{\simeq}}D(\textbf{q})\left(1-D(\textbf{q})\frac{N}{4\bar{v}^{2}}\sum_{\pm}\frac{\alpha_{\pm}}{2}\frac{\langle\textbf{p},\textbf{q}\rangle_{\pm}}{q_{\pm}}\right),

(S18)

with the scalar product

\langle\textbf{p},\textbf{q}\rangle_{\pm}=\epsilon\omega+v_{\pm}^{2}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{q}
.
For the calculation of the two-loop correction to the bosonic mass, Fig.
2
d),e) of the main text, we calculate the second derivative of the polarization operator with respect to the mass
\bar{M}=\langle\phi\rangle
, see Fig.
S1
. We find

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\bar{M}^{2}}\Pi_{\pm}(\textbf{q})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial M_{\pm}^{2}}\Pi_{\pm}(\textbf{q})

(S19)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{N_{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 2}}{\pi v_{\pm}^{2}q_{\pm}}\left(\arctan\left(\frac{q_{\pm}}{2M_{\pm}}\right)-\frac{2M_{\pm}q_{\pm}}{q_{\pm}^{2}+4M_{\pm}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle N\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{\bar{v}^{2}}\frac{\alpha_{\pm}}{2q_{\pm}}.

Figure S1:
Diagrammatic representation of Eq. (
S19
). The differentiation with respect to
\bar{M}
introduces two external legs at zero momentum. In a subsequent step, to calculate the diagrams of Fig.
2
d), e) of the main text, the legs at finite
q
are contracted. This leads to the loop correction of the zero momentum susceptibility.

S3
Loop Corrections and renormalization group

We here present calculations of the loop corrections to the fermionic, and bosonic self energy, as well as details on the RG procedure.

S3.1
Fermionic self energy and vertex correction

The fermionic self energy and the vertex correction, Fig.
2
b) and c) of the main text, are most efficiently calculated by studying the self energy in the symmetry-broken phase

\displaystyle\Sigma_{\pm}(\textbf{p})

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}}D(\textbf{q}-\textbf{p})\sigma_{z}G_{\pm}(\textbf{q})\sigma_{z}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\textbf{q}}\frac{D(\textbf{q})}{q_{\pm}^{2}+M_{\pm}^{2}}M_{\pm}\sigma_{z}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}

\displaystyle-\int_{\textbf{q}}\frac{D(\textbf{q})^{2}(i\omega-v_{\pm}\vec{q}\cdot\vec{\sigma})}{q_{\pm}^{2}+M_{\pm}^{2}}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\sum_{\xi=\pm 1}{N\alpha_{\xi}/8\bar{v}^{2}}\frac{\langle\textbf{p},\textbf{q}\rangle_{\xi}}{q_{\xi}}

\displaystyle=-I_{1}^{\pm}M_{\pm}\sigma_{z}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}-I_{2}^{\pm}i\epsilon\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}+I_{3}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma},

(S20)

where we repeat from the main text that

\alpha_{\pm}=N_{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}/(N\rho_{v}^{\pm 1})
.
The leading logarithmic contributions are encoded in the following dimensionless integrals

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{D(\textbf{q})}{q_{\pm}^{2}},

(S21)

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{8\bar{v}^{2}}\int^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{D(\textbf{q})^{2}}{q_{\pm}^{2}}\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\frac{\omega^{2}}{q_{\xi}},

(S22)

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{8\bar{v}^{2}}\int^{\Lambda}\frac{d^{2}q}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\frac{D(\textbf{q})^{2}}{q_{\pm}^{2}}\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\frac{1}{2}\frac{v_{\xi}^{2}\vec{q}^{2}}{q_{\xi}}.

(S23)

To evaluate these integrals, we change the variables
\omega=\bar{v}x|\vec{q}|
, so that we obtain

\displaystyle I_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{N\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m\bar{v}}\right)g_{1}^{\pm}(v_{+},v_{-},g_{+},g_{-},N_{+},N_{-}),

(S24)

\displaystyle I_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3N\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m\bar{v}}\right)g_{2}^{\pm}(v_{+},v_{-},g_{+},g_{-},N_{+},N_{-}),

(S25)

\displaystyle I_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3N\pi^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m\bar{v}}\right)g_{3}^{\pm}(v_{+},v_{-},g_{+},g_{-},N_{+},N_{-}),

(S26)

where all of the following terms are unity in the isotropic limit

\displaystyle g_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{1}{({x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}})[\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}]},

(S27)

\displaystyle g_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=3\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\sum_{\xi=\pm}\alpha_{\xi}\frac{x^{2}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}}}{({x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}})[\sum_{\xi=\pm}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}]^{2}},

(S28)

\displaystyle g_{3}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=3\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{\sum_{\xi=\pm}\alpha_{\xi}\frac{\rho_{v}^{\xi}/2}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}}}{({x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}})[\sum_{\xi=\pm}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}]^{2}}.

(S29)

Note that none of
g_{1,2,3}^{\pm}
depends explicitly on
\bar{g}
or
\bar{v}
and that
g_{2}^{\pm}=3g_{1}^{\pm}-2g_{3}^{\pm}
. In the isotropic case
\rho_{v}=\rho_{g}=1
, we get
g_{1}^{\pm}=g_{2}^{\pm}=g_{3}^{\pm}=1
. We also remark that we dropped terms with logarithmically slow dependence on
\rho_{v},\rho_{g}
.

S3.2
Two-loop correction to bosonic self-energy

We calculate the leading correction to the polarization operator at zero external momentum, Fig.
2
d) and e) of the main text, by
exploiting Eq. (
S19
) and obtain

\displaystyle\delta\Pi_{\pm}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{\textbf{q}}D(\textbf{q})N\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{\bar{v}^{2}}\frac{\alpha_{\pm}}{2q_{\pm}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}\bar{v}^{2}}\int dx\frac{\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\alpha_{\pm}}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}}}

\displaystyle\times\int^{\Lambda}dq\frac{q}{m+\frac{q}{8\bar{v}}\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\alpha_{\pm}g_{4}^{\pm}-m\frac{4\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\alpha_{\pm}}{\pi^{2}}g_{5}^{\pm}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{m\bar{v}}\right).

(S30)

Again, we dropped slow, logarithmic dependence on
\rho_{v},\rho_{g}
and introduced two more functions which are unity in the isotropic limit

\displaystyle g_{4}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{2/\pi}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}}\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}}},

(S31)

\displaystyle g_{5}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx\frac{2}{\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\pm 1}}(\sum_{\xi}\alpha_{\xi}\sqrt{x^{2}+\rho_{v}^{\xi}})^{2}}.

(S32)

This concludes the calculation of divergent leading order diagrams.

S3.3
Counter-terms and derivation of RG equations

We next present details on the derivation of the RG equations. We introduce renormalized quantities to obtain the renormalized action

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pm,\alpha}\bar{\psi}_{\alpha,\pm}^{R}[Z_{1}^{\pm}\partial_{\tau}+Z_{1}^{\pm}Z_{v}^{\pm}v_{\pm}^{R}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}+Z_{2}^{\pm}(\rho_{g}^{R})^{\pm 1/2}\phi^{R}\sigma_{z}]\psi_{\alpha,\pm}^{R}

\displaystyle+\frac{Z_{3}Z_{2}^{+}Z_{2}^{-}}{Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{-}}\frac{N}{2(\bar{g}^{R})^{2}}(\phi^{R})^{2},

(S33)

where the renormalization factors
Z_{i}
contain counter-terms such that the effective generating functional in terms of renormalized coupling constants and source fields conjugate to renormalized fields (indicated by a superscript
R
) is finite. The counterterms are introduced to render the loop diagrams calculated above finite in the limit
\Lambda\rightarrow\infty
, but come at the expense of
endowing physical quantities with a non-trivial scaling with respect to a running scale
\mu
.
We can
read off the renormalization factors curing the one-loop diagrams from Eq. (
S20
)

\displaystyle Z_{1}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=1-\ln(\Lambda/\mu)\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}g_{2}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1},

(S34a)

\displaystyle Z_{v}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=1-\ln(\Lambda/\mu)\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}[g_{3}^{\pm}-g_{2}^{\pm}]\rho_{g}^{\pm 1},

(S34b)

\displaystyle Z_{2}^{\pm}

\displaystyle=1+\ln(\Lambda/\mu)\frac{2}{\pi^{2}N}g_{1}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}.

(S34c)

Next, we determine
Z_{3}
. To this end we consider the bosonic mass

\displaystyle\frac{D^{-1}(0)}{N}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{3}Z_{2}^{+}Z_{2}^{-}}{Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{-}[\bar{g}^{R}]^{2}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\pm}\left[\frac{Z_{2}^{\pm}}{Z_{1}^{\pm}}\right]^{2}\Pi_{\pm}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{3}}{[\bar{g}^{R}]^{2}}-\sum_{\pm}\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\alpha_{\pm}(\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2}-\frac{1}{N}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}g_{4}^{\pm})

\displaystyle-m\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)\frac{8\tilde{f}_{3}}{3\pi^{2}N}-\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)\left(\sum_{\pm}\pm(6g_{1}^{\pm}-2g_{2}^{\pm})\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\right)\left(\sum_{\pm}\pm\alpha_{\pm}\rho^{\pm 1/2}\right)+\mathcal{O}(\ln(\mu/m)).

(S35)

While it will be of no importance for the final RG equation, we remark that all velocities entering this equation are bare velocities
v^{\rm bare}=Z_{v}^{\pm}v^{R}_{\pm}
containing infinities in the form of counter terms of
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
.
We introduced the function

\tilde{f}_{3}=\sum_{\pm}\frac{3\alpha_{\pm}g_{5}^{\pm}-(3g_{1}^{\pm}-g_{3}^{\pm})}{2}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}.

(S36)

For the calculation of the RG equation, it is more convenient to express Eq. (
S35
) using

\displaystyle m_{\rm bare}

\displaystyle=\frac{Z_{3}}{[\bar{g}^{R}]^{2}}-\sum_{\pm}\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\alpha_{\pm}(\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2}-\frac{1}{N}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}g_{4}^{\pm})

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right)\left(\sum_{\pm}\pm(6g_{1}^{\pm}-2g_{2}^{\pm})\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{\pm}\pm\alpha_{\pm}\rho^{\pm 1/2}\right).

(S37)

Then, Eq. (
S35
) becomes

\displaystyle m_{\rm physical}

\displaystyle=m_{\rm bare}-m_{\rm bare}\frac{8\tilde{f}_{3}}{3\pi^{2}N}\ln\left(\frac{\Lambda}{\mu}\right).

(S38)

S3.4
Renormalization group equations

Next, we express the renormalizations of
v_{\pm},g_{\pm}
by renormalization of relative and mean couplings, i.e.

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln(\bar{v})}{d\ln(\mu)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}N}[g_{3}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}-g_{2}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}+g_{3}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}-g_{2}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}],

(S39)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{v}}{d\ln(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}[-g_{3}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}+g_{3}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}+g_{2}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}-g_{2}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}]\rho_{v},

(S40)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{g}}{d\ln(\mu)}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{3\pi^{2}N}[3g_{1}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}-3g_{1}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}+g_{2}^{+}\rho_{g}^{+1}-g_{2}^{-}\rho_{g}^{-1}]\rho_{g},

(S41)

\displaystyle\frac{dm}{d\ln(\mu)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8\tilde{f}_{3}}{3\pi^{3}N}m.

(S42)

In the main text, we use the notation
g_{1}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}=f_{1}^{\pm}
,
g_{3}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}=f_{2}^{\pm}
,
\tilde{f}_{3}=3f_{3}-\sum_{\pm}(3f_{1}^{\pm}-f_{2}^{\pm})/2
in the presentation of these RG equations, where we also use
g_{2}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}=(3g_{1}^{\pm}-2g_{3}^{\pm})\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}=3f_{1}^{\pm}-2f_{2}^{\pm}
.

S3.5
Wilsonian RG

The leading order
1/N
corrections to
\rho_{v},\rho_{g},\bar{v}
as well as the scaling dimensions of the fields all stem from one-loop diagrams. Therefore, the corresponding RG equations can be obtained using a Wilsonian RG protocol including and iterative integration of fast modes with momenta
q\in(\mu=\Lambda/b,\Lambda)
. However, the renormalization of the bosonic mass stems from two-loop diagrams, Fig.
2
c) of the main text. Conventional wisdom states that the Wilsonian RG may not be applicable to such higher-order corrections, because of potential miscounting of fast degrees of freedom in the phase space of the momentum shell approach. Here we explicitly rederive the RG equations in the Wilsonian formalism and find full agreement with the field theoretical counter-term approach. At the same time, as we believe that the Wilsonian approach is more transparent to the condensed matter readership, we keep it in this supplement despite the redundancy.

The integration of fast modes in the momentum shell readily lead to the effective action

\displaystyle S_{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\int^{<}d^{2}x\int d\tau\Big[\frac{Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{-}}{Z_{3}^{W}Z_{2}^{+}Z_{2}^{-}}\frac{N}{2\bar{g}^{2}}(\phi^{<})^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\pm,\alpha}\bar{\psi}_{\alpha,\pm}^{<}[(Z_{1}^{\pm})^{-1}\partial_{\tau}+(Z_{1}^{\pm}Z_{v}^{\pm})^{-1}v_{\pm}\vec{p}\cdot\vec{\sigma}

\displaystyle+(Z_{2}^{\pm})^{-1}(\rho_{g})^{\pm 1/2}\phi^{<}\sigma_{z}]\psi_{\alpha,\pm}^{<}\Big].

(S43)

Here, the notation
\int^{<}d^{2}x
implicitly defines the real space correspendent to momentum integrals
\int^{<}d^{2}p\dots=\int d^{2}p\Theta(\Lambda/b-|\vec{p}|)\dots
. Similarly, fields with superscript
<
are slow fields. The renormalization of coupling constants
Z_{1}^{\pm}
,
Z_{v}^{\pm}
and
Z_{2}^{\pm}
directly follow from diagrams of the form Fig.
2
b),c) of the main text and are given by Eqs. (
S34
), keeping in mind that
\mu=\Lambda/b
. The bosonic term has a prefactor

\displaystyle\frac{Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{-}}{Z_{3}^{W}Z_{2}^{+}Z_{2}^{-}\bar{g}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\bar{g}^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda/b}{2\pi\bar{v}}\sum_{\pm}\alpha_{\pm}(\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2}-g_{4}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}/N)(b-1)

\displaystyle-\frac{4m}{\pi^{2}N}\ln(b)\sum_{\pm}\alpha_{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}g_{5}^{\pm}.

(S44)

To obtain this quantum correction, we consider diagrams Fig.
2
d) and e) of the main text at a fast bosonic energy momentum with a RPA resummation keeping only bubbles of fast fermions. However, since the momentum is fast, inclusion of fast and slow fermions yields the same result, so that effectively we can use the polarization operator introduced above.

Next, we rescale fields and space time in a manner to reproduce the appearance of the original action

x

\displaystyle=b\textbf{x}_{R},

\displaystyle\psi_{\pm}^{R}(\textbf{x}_{R})

\displaystyle=b(Z_{1}^{\pm})^{-1/2}\psi_{\pm}^{<}(\textbf{x})

(S45)

\displaystyle\phi^{R}(\textbf{x}_{R})

\displaystyle=b\sqrt{\frac{Z_{1}^{+}Z_{1}^{-}}{Z_{2}^{+}Z_{2}^{-}}}\phi^{<}(\textbf{x}),

(S46)

to get

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int d^{2}x_{R}d\tau_{R}\Big[\frac{N}{2\bar{g}^{2}_{R}}[\phi^{R}(\textbf{x}_{R})]^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\pm}\sum_{\alpha=1}^{N_{\pm}}\bar{\psi}^{R}_{\alpha,\pm}(\textbf{x}_{R})\{\partial_{\tau_{R}}-iv_{\pm}^{R}\vec{\nabla}_{x_{R}}\cdot\vec{\sigma}

\displaystyle+[\rho_{g}^{R}]^{\pm 1/2}\phi^{R}(\textbf{x}_{R})\sigma_{z}\}\psi^{R}_{\alpha,\pm}(\textbf{x}_{R})\Big].

(S47)

Now the momentum integral associated to
\int d^{2}x_{R}
runs up to the original cut-off
\Lambda.

The renormalized coupling constants are

\displaystyle v_{\pm}^{R}

\displaystyle=\left[1+\ln(b)\frac{2[g_{3}^{\pm}-g_{2}^{\pm}]\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{3\pi^{2}N}\right]v_{\pm},

(S48)

\displaystyle\rho_{g}^{R}

\displaystyle=\left[1-\ln(b)\sum_{\pm}(\pm 1)\frac{2[g_{2}^{\pm}+3g_{1}^{\pm}]\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{3\pi^{2}N}\right]\rho_{g},

(S49)

\displaystyle m^{R}

\displaystyle=bm\left(1-\ln(b)\frac{8\tilde{f}_{3}}{3\pi^{2}N}\right),

(S50)

where the relationship between coupling constant and bosonic mass is

m^{R}=\frac{1}{[\bar{g}^{R}]^{2}}-\frac{\Lambda}{2\pi\bar{v}}\sum_{\pm}\alpha_{\pm}(\rho_{v}^{\pm 1/2}-\frac{g_{4}^{\pm}\rho_{g}^{\pm 1}}{N}).

(S51)

The renormalization group equations presented in the main text follow directly from these equations by means of differentiation with respect to
\ln(b)
. To get the renormalization of fields, we define
Z_{\psi}^{\pm}=Z_{1}^{\pm}
and
Z_{\phi}=\prod_{\pm}Z_{2}^{\pm}/Z_{1}^{\pm}
. Note that the renormalization group equations reproduce the results obtained in the previous section on the basis of the field theoretical counter-term method.

S4.2
RG flow near
\rho_{v}=1

The RG equations can be calculated exactly to leading order in
\rho_{v}=1+\delta\rho_{v}
, in which case

\displaystyle\frac{d\delta\rho_{v}}{d\ln(b)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{32\rho_{g}^{2}}{15N\pi^{2}\left[n\left(\rho_{g}^{2}-1\right)+\rho_{g}^{2}+1\right]^{2}}\delta\rho_{v},

(S52)

\displaystyle\frac{d\rho_{g}}{d\ln(b)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{16\rho_{g}\left(\rho_{g}^{2}-1\right)}{3N\pi^{2}\left[n\left(\rho_{g}^{2}-1\right)+\rho_{g}^{2}+1\right]}

\displaystyle+\frac{64\rho_{g}^{3}}{5N\pi^{2}\left[n\left(\rho_{g}^{2}-1\right)+\rho_{g}^{2}+1\right]^{2}}\delta\rho_{v},

(S53)

\displaystyle\frac{d\ln(\bar{v})}{d\ln(b)}

\displaystyle=\frac{16n\rho_{g}^{2}}{15N\pi^{2}\left[n\left(\rho_{g}^{2}-1\right)+\rho_{g}^{2}+1\right]^{2}}\delta\rho_{v},

(S54)

where
n=(N_{+}-N_{-})/(N_{+}+N_{-})
. Near the isotropic point, we can expand these RG equations for
\delta\vec{\rho}=(\delta\rho_{v},\delta\rho_{g})
, both
\delta\rho_{v,g}=\rho_{v,g}-1
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\delta\vec{\rho}}{d\ln(b)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{8}{15\pi^{2}N}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
-6&10\end{array}\right)\delta\vec{\rho}.

This leads to scaling dimensions
-\frac{8}{15\pi^{2}N}
[
-\frac{80}{15\pi^{2}N}
] with eigenvectors
(3,2)/\sqrt{13}
[(0,1)], independently on
n
.

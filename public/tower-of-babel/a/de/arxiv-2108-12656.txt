Abstract

1.
Introduction

In
[
Ser72
]
, Serre proved the adelic openness for elliptic curves

over
\mathbb{Q}
without complex multiplication. In other words, the image of the Galois representation

\rho_{E}:\mathrm{Gal}(\bar{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow\varprojlim_{n}{\rm{Aut}}E[n]\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})

He then studied the maximal Galois image for elliptic curves over

-division field has nontrivial intersection with the

-division field.

For the function field analogue of the maximal Galois image problem, Pink and Rütsche
[

]
proved the open image theorem for Drinfeld modules of arbitrary rank with generic characteristic. We let

and

. It is natural then to ask whether there is a Drinfeld

[

]
proved the adelic surjectivity for a Drinfeld

-module over

of rank

under the assumption that

[

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite field with
q=p^{e}
, and
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
(we don’t restrict
q
until we are into a specific section). An
A
-field
K is a field equipped with a homomorphism
\gamma:A\rightarrow K
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebras. The kernel
\text{ker}(\gamma)
is called the
A-characteristic
of
K
, and we say
K
has
generic characteristic
if
\text{ker}(\gamma)=0
. Let
K\{\tau\}
be the ring of skew polynomials along with the commutative rule
\tau\cdot c=c^{q}\cdot\tau
. A
Drinfeld
A
-module over
K
of rank
r\geqslant 1
is a ring homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi

\displaystyle:A\longrightarrow K\{\tau\}

\displaystyle\ \ \ a\longmapsto\phi_{a}=\gamma(a)+\sum^{r\cdot{\rm{deg}}(a)}_{i=1}g_{i}(a)\tau^{i}.

It is uniquely determined by
\phi_{T}=\gamma(T)+\sum^{r}_{i=1}g_{i}(T)\tau^{i}
, where
g_{r}(T)\neq 0
. Two Drinfeld modules
\phi
and
\psi
are isomorphic over
K
if there is an element
c\in K^{*}
such that
c\cdot\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}\cdot c
for all elements
a\in A
. On the other hand, let
K\langle x\rangle=\{\sum^{n}_{i=0}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}\mid a_{i}\in K\}
denote the polynomial ring of
q
-polynomials where the multiplication is defined to be composition of polynomials. It’s easy to see that
K\{\tau\}
is isomorphic to
K\langle x\rangle
under the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}K\{\tau\}&\longrightarrow&K\langle x\rangle\\
\sum^{n}_{i=0}a_{i}\tau^{i}&\mapsto&\sum^{n}_{i=0}a_{i}x^{q^{i}}\end{array}.

Thus we may also view
\phi_{T}
as a
q
-polynomial
\phi_{T}(x)=\gamma(T)x+\sum^{r}_{i=1}g_{i}(T)x^{q^{i}}
.
The Drinfeld module
\phi
over
K
gives
K
an
A
-module structure, where
a\in A
acts on
K
via
\phi_{a}
. We use the notation
{}^{\phi}K
to emphasize the action of
A
on
K
.
The
a
-torsion
is

\phi[a]=\left\{\alpha\in K^{\mathrm{alg}}\mid\phi_{a}(\alpha)=\gamma(a)\alpha+\sum^{r\cdot{\rm{deg}}(a)}_{i=1}g_{i}(a)\alpha^{q^{i}}=0\right\}.

Note that
\phi[a]
is an
A
-module, where
b\in A
acts on
\alpha\in\phi[a]
by

b\cdot\alpha=\phi_{b}(\alpha)
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld module over
K
of rank
r
and
0\neq a\in A
. If the
A
-characteristic of
K
does not divide
a
, then
there is an isomorphism of
A
-modules
\phi[a]\simeq(A/aA)^{r}
.

If the characteristic of
K
does not divide
a
, then
\phi_{a}(x)=\gamma(a)x+\sum^{r\cdot{\rm{deg}}(a)}_{i=1}g_{i}(a)x^{q^{i}}
is a separable polynomial. Therefore
G_{K}=\mathrm{Gal}(K^{\mathrm{sep}}/K)
acts on
\phi[a]
and this action commutes with the action of
A
. From this action we get a
representation
G_{K}\to\mathrm{Aut}_{A}(\phi[a])\cong\mathrm{GL}_{r}(A/aA)
. When
a=\mathfrak{l}
is irreducible, we denoted this representation

\bar{\rho}_{\phi,\mathfrak{l}}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{r}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{l}}).

Obviously
\bar{\rho}_{\phi,\mathfrak{l}}
factor through
\mathrm{Gal}(F(\phi[\mathfrak{l}])/F)
, where the field
F(\phi[\mathfrak{l}])
is defined to be the splitting field of
\phi_{\mathfrak{l}}(x)
over
F
.

Taking inverse limit with respect to
\mathfrak{l}^{i}
, we get the
\mathfrak{l}
-adic Galois representation

{\rho}_{\phi,\mathfrak{l}}:G_{K}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{i}{\mathrm{Aut}}(\phi[\mathfrak{l}^{i}])\cong\mathrm{GL}_{r}(A_{\mathfrak{l}}),

where
A_{\mathfrak{l}}
denotes the completion of
A
at
\mathfrak{l}
.
Combining all representations together, we get the
adelic Galois representation

{\rho}_{\phi}:G_{K}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{a}}{\rm{Aut}}(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])\cong{\rm{GL_{r}}}(\widehat{A}),

where
\hat{A}
is defined as
\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{a}}{A/\mathfrak{a}}
.

Throughout this paper, we set
K
to be
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
equipped with the natural injection
\gamma:A\rightarrow F
. We will focus on Dinfeld
A
-modules
\phi
over
F
of rank
2
, which are usually defined by
\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+g_{2}\tau^{2}
with
g_{1}\in F
and
g_{2}\in F^{*}
.

3.
Abelian extensions over rational function field

In the class field theory, Kronecker-Weber theorem says an abelian extension over
\mathbb{Q}
must be contained in some cyclotomic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. In his work on function field analogue of class field theory, Hayes
[
Hay74
]
gave a complete description on the maximal abelian extension of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
. This section is to summarize Hayes’ results.

(1)

Maximal constant extension
E
:

The maximal constant extension
E
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
is the union of all constant extensions of
\\
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
.

(2)

Carlitz extension
K_{T}
:

Let
C_{T}=T+\tau
be the rank
1
Drinfeld
A
-module over
F
, which is the so-called Carlitz module. Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
A
, we denote by
F(C[\mathfrak{a}])
the splitting field of
C_{\mathfrak{a}}(x)
over
F
. The Carlitz extension
K_{T}
is defined to be the union of
F(C[\mathfrak{a}])
among all ideals in
A
.

(3)

Wildly ramified at infinity
L_{\infty}
:

Let
A^{\prime}
be the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[\frac{1}{T}]
, consider the Drinfeld
A^{\prime}
-module
C^{\prime}_{\frac{1}{T}}=\frac{1}{T}+\tau
over
F
. Set
F_{\nu}=F(C^{\prime}[(\frac{1}{T^{-\nu-1}})])
, we have
[F_{\nu}:F]=q^{\nu}(q-1)
. The action of
b\in A^{\prime}
on
F_{\nu}
is defined via
C^{\prime}_{b}
. In particular, we can identify
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
with automorphisms on
F_{\nu}
that maps
\lambda\in F_{\nu}
to
\beta\lambda
. Let
L_{\nu}
be the fixed field of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
in
F_{\nu}
, we have
[L_{\nu}:F]=q^{\nu}
and
[F_{\nu}:L_{\nu}]=q-1
. Therefore, the place
(\frac{1}{T})
is totally wildly ramified in
L_{\nu}/F
. We know from the definition of
L_{\nu}
that
L_{\nu}\subset L_{\nu+1}
and
L_{\infty}
is defined by the union
L_{\infty}=\cup_{\nu=1}^{\infty}L_{\nu}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The three extensions
E/F
,
K_{T}/F
, and
L_{\infty}/F
are linearly disjoint with each other, and their composite
E\cdot K_{T}\cdot L_{\infty}
is the maximal abelian extension of
F
.

Proof.

See
[
Hay74
]
Proposition 5.2 and Theorem 7.1.
∎

4.
The case
q=2

In this section, we set
A=\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
and
F=\mathbb{F}_{2}(T)
. Let
\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+g_{2}\tau^{2}
be a Drinfeld
A
-module over
F
of rank
2
where
g_{1}\in F
and
g_{2}\in F^{*}
. Suppose further that the mod
(T+i)
Galois representations are surjective for

. As
\bar{\rho}_{\phi,(T+i)}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
factor through
\mathrm{Gal}(F(\phi[T+i])/F)
, we have
\mathrm{Gal}(F(\phi[T+i])/F)\cong\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})\cong S_{3}
, the symmetric group of order
6
. As the theory for mod
(T+1)
representations is similar to mod
(T)
representations, we focus on the mod
(T)
representation tempararily. Since
S_{3}
has a normal subgroup of index
2
, we have a degree
2
subextension
M
of
F(\phi[T])
over
F
. The extension
M/F
is abelian, so Theorem
3.1
implies
M
must lie in either
E
,
K_{T}
, or
L_{\infty}
.

Case(1):

M\subset E
, i.e. the extension
M/F
is a constant extension.

We are able to characterize which type of
\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+g_{2}\tau^{2}
will give a degree
2
constant subextension
M
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given a rank
2
Drinfeld module
\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+g_{2}\tau^{2}
over
\mathbb{F}_{2}(T)
and under the assumption that the mod
(T+i)
representation is surjective,
i\in\{0,1\}
, the degree
2
subextension
M
of
F(\phi[T+i])
over
F
is a constant extension if and only if
g_{1}=0
.

Proof.

It’s obvious that when
g_{1}=0
, the intermediate field
M
is the constant extension
F(\zeta_{3})
over
F
obtained by joining the
3
rd root of unity.

On the other hand, from the assumption that
F(\phi[T])/F\cong S_{3}
and
\phi_{T}(x)=Tx+g_{1}x^{2}+g_{2}x^{4}=x(T+g_{1}x+g_{2}x^{3})
, we can deduce
T+g_{1}x+g_{2}x^{3}
is irreducible over the intermediate field
M
. In other words, there is an element
y\in F(\phi[T])
whose minimal polynomial over
M
is
T+g_{1}x+g_{2}x^{3}
and
F(\phi[T])=M(y)
. As the minimal polynomial of
y
over
M
has all coefficients lie in
F
, we may consider the cubic extension
F(y)/F
. By
[
MW17
]
Corollary 1.4(a), there is a primitive element
z
for
F(y)/F
with characteristic polynomial of the form
x^{3}+x+b
, where
b=\frac{T^{4}g_{2}^{2}}{g_{1}^{6}}\in F^{*}
. Furthermore, in the
[
MW17
]
page 8, they characterized the unique degree
2
subextension
M
of
F(\phi[T])
. We thus have
M/F
is the Artin-Schrier extension with polynomial
x^{2}+x=\frac{1+b^{2}}{b^{2}}
. Because
b\neq 0
, the Artin-schrier extension is not a constant extension. Thus we have proved that
g_{1}\neq 0
would imply
M/F
is not a constant extension.

∎

Since this case happens only when the given Drinfeld module is of the form

\phi_{T}=T+g_{2}\tau^{2},

we can just compare the two extensions
F(\phi[T])/F
and
F(\phi[T+1]/F)
. They both have the constant extension
F(\zeta_{3})
as their degree
2
subextension over
F
. Hence the mod
(T)
and mod
(T+1)
Galois representation have entanglement. i.e.
F(\phi[T])\cap F(\phi[T+1])\supsetneq F
. Thus we can never achieve adelic surjectivity in this case.

Case(2):

M\subset K_{T}
, i.e.
M
is contained in

for some ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of

.

From the given information, we can have the following lattice of field extensions:

F(\phi[T])
F(C[\mathfrak{a}])

Since

can not be of degree

is not equal to
(T)
. Now we consider the extension
K=F(\sqrt[3]{g_{2}})
of

and its composite with

, we have the following lattice:

K(\phi[T])
K(C[\mathfrak{a}])
MK
K

Now we can consider the rank
2
Drinfeld module over

defined by

\psi_{T}=T+g_{1}^{\prime}+\tau^{2},\ \ \text{$g_{1}^{\prime}=\frac{g_{1}}{\sqrt[3]{g_{2}}}$}.

From the theory of Weil pairing for Drinfeld modules(
[
van03

, Proposition 4.7.1), we know
\det\circ\bar{\rho}_{\psi,\mathfrak{a}}=\bar{\rho}_{C,\mathfrak{a}}:\mathrm{Gal}(K^{{\rm{sep}}}/K)\rightarrow(A/\mathfrak{a})^{*}
. Hence we get an extension

from

. Moreover,

The last containment is proper because

is of degree

.
With this containment relationship in hand, we want to prove the following:

Lemma 4.2
.

F(\phi[T])\cap F(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])\supset M\supsetneq F
.

Proof.

It’s enough to prove
F(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])\supset M
. If the degree of the extension

is equal to

, then it’s clear that
F(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])\supset M
. Hence we assume

is a degree

K(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])=KF(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])
MK
F(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])
K
MK\cap F(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])

Firstly, we have

If the index is equal to

This implies

∎

Case(3):

M\subset L_{\infty}
, i.e.

is wildly ramified at infinity.

If the subextension

of

, which is impossible. Thus in our case the degree

of

. The adelic Galois representation

{\rho}_{\phi}:G_{F}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{a}}{\rm{Aut}}(\phi[\mathfrak{a}])\cong{\rm{GL_{2}}}(\widehat{A})

is not surjective.

over

\begin{array}[]{ccccclc}0\longrightarrow&H^{1}/H^{2}&\rightarrow&H/H^{2}&\rightarrow&\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})&\rightarrow 0\\
&\downarrow{\det}&&\downarrow{\det}&&\downarrow{\det}&\\

\varphi_{T}=T+\tau-T^{q-1}\tau^{2}
can still achieve adelic surjectivity.

In
[
Zyw11
]
, Zywina proved the adelic Galois representation
\rho_{\varphi}:G_{F}\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{A})
attached to the Drinfeld
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
-module over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
defined by

\phi_{T}=T+\tau-T^{q-1}\tau^{2}

is surjective under the assumption that
q\geqslant 5
is an odd prime power. However, he used the condition“
q\geqslant 5
” only in proving Proposition 5.1 of
[
Zyw11
]
, and in Lemma 6.1 where he needed to apply Proposition 4.1 of
[
PR09
]
. In the proof of Proposition 5.1 of
[
Zyw11
]
, it’s enough to have
|\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}|\geqslant 3
because we only need to make sure there are at least
3
elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
of degree
1
. In the statement of Proposition 4.1 of
[
PR09
]
, it only requires
|\mathbb{F}_{q}|\geqslant 4
. Hence we can still prove Proposition 5.1 and Lemma 6.1 in
[
Zyw11
]
through the same procedure.
On the other hand, Zywina used the condition “
q
is odd” in Lemma A.3 and in Lemma A.7 of
[
Zyw11
]
. In the proof of Lemma A.7, he applied Theorem 1 in
[
Die80
]
where the theorem is for fields of odd characteristic. But this Theorem 1 is also true for finite field of even characteristic (see argument after Theorem 3 in
[
Die80
]
), hence Lemma A.7 is still true in our case.
Therefore, it remains to prove that Lemma A.3 of
[
Zyw11
]
is true in our case.

Lemma 6.1
.

For each prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
of
A
, the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
is equal to its commutator subgroup. The only normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
with simple quotient is the group
N=\{B\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
, where
B\equiv I_{2}\mod\mathfrak{p}\}

Proof.

This proof is slightly modified from the proof of Lemma A.3 in
[
Zyw11
]
so that we can avoid using the condition “
q
is odd”.

First of all, we prove that
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
is equal to its own commutator subgroup. Let
H
be the commutator subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
. It is a closed normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Now we define the followings

\begin{array}[]{cll}S^{0}&:=&\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})\\
S^{i}&:=&\{s\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})\text{ with }s\equiv 1\mod\mathfrak{p}^{i}\}\ \text{for $i\geqslant 1$}\end{array}

For
i\geqslant 0
, we define
H^{i}=H\cap S^{i}
. We also define
S^{[i]}:=S^{i}/S^{i+1}
and
H^{[i]}:=H^{i}/H^{i+1}
. There is a natural inclusion map from
H^{[i]}
to
S^{[i]}
.

Claim
.

H^{[i]}=S^{[i]}
for all
i\geqslant 0
.

Proof of claim.

Reduction modulo
\mathfrak{p}
gives us that
S^{[0]}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Since
F_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a finite field of even characteristic with order
\geqslant 4
, the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(F_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong{\rm{PSL}}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
is a simple group. As
H^{[0]}
is the commutator subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
,
H^{[0]}
is a nontrivial normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Hence
H^{[0]}=S^{[0]}
.

For
i\geqslant 1
, let
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
be the additive subgroup of
M_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
consists of all matrices of trace
0
. We have an isomorphism

S^{[i]}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}),\ [1+\mathfrak{p}^{i}y]\mapsto[y].

Now we consider
H^{[i]}\hookrightarrow S^{[i]}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. As
H
is a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
,
H^{[i]}
is invariant under conjugation by
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Thus Proposition 2.1 of
[
PR09
]
implies either
H^{[i]}
is contained in the group of scalar matrices or
H^{[i]}
contains
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. Thus it’s enough to prove
H^{[i]}
contains a non-scalar matrix.

Consider the commutator map
S^{0}\times S^{i}\rightarrow S^{i}
defined by
(g,h)\mapsto ghg^{-1}h^{-1}
. This induces a map
S^{[0]}\times S^{[i]}\rightarrow S^{[i]}
that takes values in
H^{[i]}
. Since
S^{[0]}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
S^{[i]}\cong\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
, the induced map becomes

\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\times\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}),\ \ (s,X)\mapsto sXs^{-1}-X.

As the above map takes values in
H^{[i]}
, we can consider

s=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha^{-1}\end{array}\right),\ X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
1&0\end{array}\right),\ \text{where $\alpha$ is a generator of $\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}}^{*}$}.

Then we compute

sXs^{-1}-X=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\alpha^{2}-1\\
\alpha^{-2}-1&0\end{array}\right).

As
\alpha^{2}
is not equal to
1
, the obtained matrix is not a scalar matrix. Hence
H^{[i]}
contains a non-scalar matrix, so
H^{[i]}\supset\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
. This proves
H^{[i]}=S^{[i]}
for all
i\geqslant 1
.
∎

Since we have proved
H^{[i]}=S^{[i]}
for all
i
, we can conclude that
H=\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
.

Now let
N^{\prime}
be a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
with simple quotient. As the Jordan-Hölder factors of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
are
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})
and
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, we have
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})/N^{\prime}\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{\mathfrak{p}})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})/N
because
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
has no abelian quotients. This implies
N^{\prime}=N
because otherwise
NN^{\prime}
will be a proper normal subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})
that contains
N
and
N^{\prime}
, a contradiction.
∎

Now we are able to prove the adelic surjectivity for the Drinfeld
A
-module
\varphi
by going through the same procedure as in Zywina’s work. We conclude this section by stating the theorem:

Theorem 6.2
.

Let
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and
F=\mathbb{F}_{q}(T)
with
q=2^{e}\geqslant 4
. Let
\varphi
be a rank
2
Drinfeld
A
-module over
F
defined by

\varphi_{T}=T+\tau-T^{q-1}\tau^{2}.

Then the adelic Galois representation

{\rho}_{\varphi}:G_{F}\longrightarrow\varprojlim_{\mathfrak{a}}{\rm{Aut}}(\varphi[\mathfrak{a}])\cong{\rm{GL_{2}}}(\widehat{A})

is surjective.

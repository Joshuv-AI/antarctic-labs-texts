Abstract
Hysteresis in the pressure-saturation relation in unsaturated porous media, which is due to surface tension on the liquid-gas interface, exhibits strong degeneracy in the resulting mass balance equation. As an extension of previous existence and uniqueness results, we prove that under physically admissible initial conditions and without mass exchange with the exterior, the unique global solution of the fluid diffusion problem exists and asymptotically converges as time tends to infinity to a possibly non-homogeneous mass distribution and an a priori unknown constant pressure. Keywords: hysteresis, porous media, long time behavior 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 47J40, 35B40, 76S05



Introduction

This paper deals with the problem of existence, uniqueness, and long time stabilization of the solution to the degenerate PDE with hysteresis in a bounded space domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
N\in\mathbb{N}
of class
C^{1,1}

s_{t}-\Delta u=0\quad\mathrm{\ for\ }(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)

(0.1)

where
t>0
is the time variable,
u=u(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}
represents the pressure,
s=s(x,t)\in(0,1)
is the relative saturation of the fluid in the pores, and
\Delta
is the Laplacian in
x
. Hysteresis in the pressure-saturation relation is represented by a Preisach operator
G
in the form

s(x,t)=G[u](x,t).

(0.2)

A detailed justification why it is meaningful to consider Preisach hysteresis in porous media modeling can be found in the introduction of
[
5
]
.

System (
0.1
)–(
0.2
) is considered with boundary condition

-\nabla u(x,t)\cdot n=0

(0.3)

on
\partial\Omega
, and with a given initial condition which includes not only the initial pressure

u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

(0.4)

but also an initial Preisach memory distribution specified below in a rigorous formulation of the Preisach operator in Definition
1.1
. Let us only mention at this point that the time evolution described by equation (
0.1
) is doubly degenerate: in typical situations, the function
s(x,t)=G[u](x,t)
is bounded independently of the evolution of
u
, so that no a priori lower bound for
u_{t}
is immediately available. Furthermore, at every point
x\in\Omega
and every time
t_{0}
where
u_{t}
changes sign (which the engineers call a
turning point
), we have

u_{t}(x,t_{0}-\delta)\cdot u_{t}(x,t_{0}+\delta)<0\ \ \forall\delta\in(0,\delta_{0}(x))\ \Longrightarrow\ \liminf_{\delta\to 0+}\frac{G[u]_{t}(x,t_{0}+\delta)}{u_{t}(x,t_{0}+\delta)}=0,

(0.5)

so that the knowledge of
G[u]_{t}
alone does not give a complete information about
u_{t}
. In particular, if
\Delta u_{0}(x)\neq 0
and the initial memory of
G
corresponds to a turning point, then even a local solution cannot be expected to exist. Hypothesis
1.4
below is shown to avoid this pathological situation. A more detailed discussion about this issue can be found in
[
5
]
, where the existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
0.1
) with Robin boundary conditions on an arbitrary time interval
t\in(0,T)
under suitable hypotheses on the data has been recently proved in the case that the operator
G
is the so-called convexifiable Preisach operator, and its initial memory state is compatible with the initial condition
u_{0}
in (
0.4
), see Section
1
below. The present autonomous case with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions makes it possible to prove a stronger existence and uniqueness statement under the same hypotheses on the data, as well as asymptotic convergence to an a priori unknown equilibrium as time tends to infinity.

The structure of the paper is the following. In Section
1
we list the definitions of the main concepts including convexifiable Preisach operators, and state the main Theorem

. In Section
2
we propose a time discretization scheme with time step

7

1  Statement of the problem

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\lambda\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))
be a given function which we call initial memory distribution and which has the following properties:

\displaystyle|\lambda(x,r_{1})-\lambda(x,r_{2})|\leq|r_{1}-r_{2}|\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \forall r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,\infty),

(1.1)

\displaystyle\exists\Lambda>0:\ \lambda(x,r)=0\ \mathrm{\ for\ }r\geq\Lambda\ \mbox{ and a.\,e.\ }x\in\Omega.

(1.2)

For a given
r>0
, we call the play operator with threshold
r
and initial memory
\lambda
the mapping which, with a given function
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
for
p\geq 1
, associates the solution
\xi^{r}\in L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
of the variational inequality

|u(x,t)-\xi^{r}(x,t)|\leq r,\quad\xi^{r}_{t}(x,t)(u(x,t)-\xi^{r}(x,t)-z)\geq 0\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \forall z\in[-r,r],

(1.3)

with a given initial memory distribution

\xi^{r}(x,0)=\lambda(x,r),

(1.4)

and we denote

\xi^{r}(x,t)=\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u](x,t).

(1.5)

Given a measurable function
\rho:\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
and a constant
\bar{G}\in\mathbb{R}
, the Preisach operator
G
is defined as a mapping
G:L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))\to L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
by the formula

G[u](x,t)=\bar{G}+\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\xi^{r}(x,t)}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r.

(1.6)

The Preisach operator is said to be regular if the density function
\rho
of
G
in (
1.6
) belongs to
L^{1}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R})
, and there exist a constant
\rho_{1}
and a decreasing function
\rho_{0}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
U>0
we have

\rho_{1}>\rho(x,r,v)>\rho_{0}(U)>0\ \mathrm{\ for\ }\mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ (x,r,v)\in\Omega\times(0,U)\times(-U,U).

(1.7)

Let us mention the following classical result (see
[
9
, Proposition II.3.11]
).

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a regular Preisach operator in the sense of Definition
1.1
. Then it can be extended to a Lipschitz continuous mapping
G:L^{p}(\Omega;C[0,T])\to L^{p}(\Omega;C[0,T])
for every
p\in[1,\infty)
.

The Preisach operator is rate-independent. Hence, for input functions
u(x,t)
which are monotone in a time interval
t\in(a,b)
, a regular Preisach operator
G
can be represented by a superposition operator
G[u](x,t)=B(x,u(x,t))
with an increasing function
u\mapsto B(x,u)
called a
Preisach branch
. Indeed, the branches may be different at different points
x
and different intervals
(a,b)
. The branches corresponding to increasing inputs are said to be
ascending
(the so-called wetting curves in the context of porous media), the branches corresponding to decreasing inputs are said to be
descending
(drying curves).

Definition 1.3
.

Let
U>0
be given. A Preisach operator is said to be uniformly counterclockwise convex on
[-U,U]
if for all inputs
u
such that
|u(x,t)|\leq U
a. e., all ascending branches are uniformly convex and all descending branches are uniformly concave.

A regular Preisach operator is called convexifiable if for every
U>0
there exist a uniformly counterclockwise convex Preisach operator
P
on
[-U,U]
, positive constants
g_{*}(U),g^{*}(U),\bar{g}(U)
, and a twice continuously differentiable mapping
g:[-U,U]\to[-U,U]
such that

g(0)=0,\quad 0<g_{*}(U)\leq g^{\prime}(u)\leq g^{*}(U),\quad|g^{\prime\prime}(u)|\leq\bar{g}(U)\ \ \forall u\in[-U,U],

and
G=P\circ g
.

A typical example of a uniformly counterclockwise convex operator is the so-called
Prandtl-Ishlinskii operator
characterized by positive density functions
\rho(x,r)
independent of
v
, see
[
9
, Section 4.2]
. Operators of the form
P\circ g
with a Prandtl-Ishlinskii operator
P
and an increasing function
g
are often used in control engineering because of their explicit inversion formulas, see
[
1
,
2
,
10
]
. They are called the
generalized Prandtl-Ishlinskii operators
(GPI) and represent an important subclass of Preisach operators. Note also that for every Preisach operator
P
and every Lipschitz continuous increasing function, the superposition operator
G=P\circ g
is also a Preisach operator, and there exists an explicit formula for its density, see
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
. Another class of convexifiable Preisach operators is shown in
[
5
, Proposition 1.3]
.

As it has been mentioned in the Introduction, even a local solution to Problem (
0.1
)–(
0.4
) may fail to exist if for example
\lambda(x,r)\equiv 0
and
\Delta u_{0}(x)\neq 0
. Then

is a turning point for all

and there is no way to satisfy (
0.1
) in any sense. We therefore need an initial memory compatibility condition which we state in the following way.

Hypothesis 1.4
.

The initial pressure
u_{0}
belongs to
W^{2,2}(\Omega)
,
\Delta u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and there exist constants
L>0
,
\Lambda>0
and a function
r_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{\,sup\,ess}}_{x\in\Omega}|u_{0}(x)|\leq\Lambda
, condition (
1.2
) is satisfied, and for a. e.
x\in\Omega
the following initial compatibility conditions hold:

\displaystyle\lambda(x,0)

\displaystyle=u_{0}(x)\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.},

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{L}\sqrt{|\Delta u_{0}(x)|}

\displaystyle\leq r_{0}(x)\leq\Lambda\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.},

(1.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\lambda(x,r)

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{\,sign}(\Delta u_{0}(x))\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \mathrm{\ for\ }r\in(0,r_{0}(x)),

(1.10)

\displaystyle-\nabla u_{0}(x)\cdot n

\displaystyle=0\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \mbox{on }\,\partial\Omega.

(1.11)

It was shown in
[
3
, Proposition 2.2.16]
that the solution to (
1.3
) is such that for every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;W^{1,1}(0,T))
the property

|\xi^{r_{1}}(x,t)-\xi^{r_{2}}(x,t)|\leq|r_{1}-r_{2}|\ \mathrm{\ a.\,e.}\ \ \forall r_{1},r_{2}\in(0,\infty)

(1.12)

is preserved during the evolution. From (
1.1
)–(
1.4
) it follows that for a. e.
(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty)
there exists a so-called
active memory level

r^{*}(x,t)\geq 0
such that
|\xi^{r}(x,t)-u(x,t)|=r
and
\xi^{r}_{t}(x,t)=u_{t}(x,t)
for
r<r^{*}(x,t)
,
|\xi^{r}(x,t)-u(x,t)|<r
and
\xi^{r}_{t}(x,t)=0
for
r>r^{*}(x,t)
. In this sense, the value
r_{0}
in (
1.9
)–(
1.10
) represents the active memory level at time
t=0
. The meaning of conditions (
1.8
)–(
1.10
) is that for large values of
\Delta u_{0}(x)
, the initial memory
\lambda
has to go deeper in the memory direction. We refer to
[
5
]
for explanation how Hypothesis
1.4
guarantees the existence of some previous admissible history of the process prior to the time
t=0
and ensures the existence of a continuation for
t>0
.

Problem (
0.1
)–(
0.3
) is to be understood in variational form

\int_{\Omega}G[u]_{t}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(1.13)

for every test function
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
. Existence and uniqueness of a solution to (
0.1
)–(
0.2
) with the Robin boundary condition and admissible initial conditions has been proved in
[
5
]
. We now state the main result of this paper.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let Hypothesis
1.4
be satisfied, and let
G
be a convexifiable Preisach operator in the sense of Definition
1.3
. Then Problem (
1.13
) with initial condition
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)
,
\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u](x,0)=\lambda(x,r)
for all
r>0
admits a unique global solution
u
on
\Omega\times(0,\infty)
such that
u\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))
,
|u(x,t)|\leq\Lambda
a. e.,
\nabla u\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{2}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,\infty);\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
u_{t}
belongs to the Orlicz space
L^{Q}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))
generated by a function
Q(z)
which behaves like
z^{3}
for
z\in(0,1)
and
z^{p}
with
p=1+(2/N)
for
z>1
,
\Delta u,G[u]_{t}\in L^{2}(\Omega\times(0,\infty))
. Moreover, there exist a constant
\bar{u}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|u(x,t)-\bar{u}|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
for all
q\geq 1
, and a function
\bar{\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega\times[0,\infty))
such that
\bar{\lambda}(x,0)=\bar{u}
,
|\bar{\lambda}(x,r)-\bar{\lambda}(x,s)|\leq r-s
for a. e.
x\in\Omega
and all
r>s\geq 0
, and such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u](x,t)-\bar{\lambda}(x,r)|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}x=0
for all
q\geq 1
and
r\geq 0
.

Note that the regularity of
u_{t}
in
[
5
]
is only
L^{p-\varepsilon}
for each
\varepsilon>0
and on bounded time intervals. We propose here a refined estimation technique which allows to derive a global Orlicz bound for
u_{t}
(for the theory of Orlicz spaces see, e. g.,
[
7
]
) and full
L^{p}
bound on bounded time intervals.

Putting
\varphi=1
in (
1.13
) we formally get the identity

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}s(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x=0,

which means that the total liquid mass is preserved during the evolution. The meaning of Theorem
1.5
is that the pressure
u
is asymptotically uniformly distributed in
\Omega
, but because of hysteresis, we cannot expect that the spatial distribution of the liquid mass will also be asymptotically uniform and that the constant limit pressure value
\bar{u}
can be computed explicitly.

2  Time discretization

We proceed as in
[
5
]
, choose a sufficiently small time step
\tau>0
, and replace (
1.13
) with its time discrete system for the unknowns
\{u_{i}:i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}\}\subset W^{1,2}(\Omega)
of the form

\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})\varphi+\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla\varphi\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(2.1)

for
i\in\mathbb{N}
and for every test function
\varphi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
, where
u_{0}
is the initial condition in (
0.4
). Here, the time-discrete Preisach operator
G[u]_{i}
is defined by a formula of the form (
1.6
), namely,

G[u]_{i}(x)=\bar{G}+\ \int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\xi^{r}_{i}(x)}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v\,\mathrm{d}r,

(2.2)

where
\xi^{r}_{i}
denotes the output of the time-discrete play operator

\xi^{r}_{i}(x)=\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u]_{i}(x)

(2.3)

defined as the solution operator of the variational inequality

|u_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i}(x)|\leq r,\quad(\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i-1}(x))(u_{i}(x)-\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-z)\geq 0\quad\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\ \ \forall z\in[-r,r],

(2.4)

with a given initial memory curve

\xi^{r}_{0}(x)=\lambda(x,r),\quad\lambda(x,0)=u_{0}(x),

(2.5)

similarly as in (
1.3
)–(
1.4
). Note that the discrete variational inequality (
2.4
) can be interpreted as weak formulation of (
1.3
) for piecewise constant inputs in terms of the Kurzweil integral, and details can be found in
[
4
, Section 2]
.

Arguing as in
[
5
, Eq. (35)]
, we obtain the two-sided estimate

\frac{1}{C}|G[u]_{i}(x)-G[u]_{i-1}(x)|^{2}\leq(u_{i}(x)-u_{i-1}(x))(G[u]_{i}(x)-G[u]_{i-1}(x))\leq C|u_{i}(x)-u_{i-1}(x)|^{2},

(2.6)

with a constant
C>0
depending only on
\rho_{1}
.

For each
i\in\mathbb{N}
, there is no hysteresis in the passage from
u_{i-1}
to
u_{i}
, so that (
2.1
) is a standard
monotone semilinear elliptic equation which admits a unique solution
u_{i}\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
.

3  Uniform upper bound

Following
[
6
]
, the idea is to test (
2.1
) by
\varphi=H_{\varepsilon}(u_{i}-\Lambda)
, with
H_{\varepsilon}
being a Lipschitz regularization of the Heaviside function
H(s)=1
for
s>0
,
H(s)=0
for
s\leq 0
, and
\Lambda
from (
1.2
). We then let
\varepsilon
tend to
0
. The elliptic term gives a non-negative contribution, and we get for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
that

\int_{\Omega}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})H(u_{i}-\Lambda)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

(3.1)

We define the functions

\psi(x,r,\xi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\xi}\rho(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v,\quad\Psi(x,r,\xi)\coloneqq\int_{0}^{\xi}\psi(x,r,v)\,\mathrm{d}v.

(3.2)

In terms of the sequence
\xi^{r}_{i}(x)=\mathfrak{p}_{r}[\lambda,u]_{i}
we have

G[u]_{i}(x)=\bar{G}+\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i}(x))\,\mathrm{d}r.

(3.3)

Choosing
z=r
in (
2.4
) and using the fact that
\psi
is an increasing function of
\xi
, we get for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
, all
r>0
, and a. e.
x\in\Omega
that

\big(\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i}(x))-\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i-1}(x))\big)\big((u_{i}(x)-\Lambda)-(\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-(\Lambda-r))\big)\geq 0.

The Heaviside function
H
is nondecreasing, hence,

\big(\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i}(x))-\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i-1}(x))\big)\big(H(u_{i}(x)-\Lambda)-H(\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-(\Lambda-r))\big)\geq 0.

From (
3.1
) it follows that

\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i}(x))-\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i-1}(x))\big)H(\xi^{r}_{i}(x)-(\Lambda-r))\,\mathrm{d}r\,\mathrm{d}x\leq 0.

(3.4)

We have by (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) that
\xi^{r}_{0}(x)=\lambda(x,r)\leq\Lambda-r
a. e. By induction we check using (
3.4
) that
\xi^{r}_{i}(x)\leq\Lambda-r
a. e. for all
r\geq 0
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
. We similarly get
\xi^{r}_{i}(x)\geq-\Lambda+r
and, in particular,

|u_{i}(x)|\leq\Lambda,\quad|\xi^{r}_{i}(x)|\leq(\Lambda-r)^{+}

(3.5)

for a. e.
x\in\Omega
and all
r\geq 0
and
i\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, this implies that in (
3.3
) we can actually write

G[u]_{i}(x)=\bar{G}+\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\psi(x,r,\xi^{r}_{i}(x))\,\mathrm{d}r.

(3.6)

4  Estimates of the pressure

We first test (
2.1
) by
\varphi=u_{i}-u_{i-1}
and get

\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\,\mathrm{d}x=0

(4.1)

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Using the elementary inequality
\nabla u_{i}\cdot\nabla(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}-|\nabla u_{i-1}|^{2}\right)
and putting
V_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x
we obtain

\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{\Omega}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\,\mathrm{d}x+V_{i}-V_{i-1}\leq 0.

(4.2)

By comparison in (
2.1
) and by employing estimate (
2.6
), we get for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
that

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\frac{1}{\tau^{2}}\int_{\Omega}|G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\frac{c}{\tau^{2}}\int_{\Omega}(G[u]_{i}-G[u]_{i-1})(u_{i}-u_{i-1})\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.3)

with a constant
c>0
independent of
i
. Coming back to (
4.2
), for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
we thus have

\int_{\Omega}|\Delta u_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{c}{\tau}(V_{i}-V_{i-1})\leq 0.

(4.4)

Consider the complete orthonormal basis
\{e_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
of eigenfunctions of the operator

-\Delta e_{k}=\mu_{k}e_{k}\ \ \mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\quad-\nabla e_{k}\cdot n=0\ \mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega

(4.5)

with eigenvalues
0=\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\dots
. The Fourier expansion of
u_{i}
in terms of the basis
\{e_{k}\}
is of the form

u_{i}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}u_{k}^{i}e_{k}(x)

(4.6)

for all
i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Using the orthonormality of the system
\{e_{k}\}
, we rewrite (
4.4
) in the form

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}^{2}\left(u_{k}^{i}\right)^{2}+\frac{c}{\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\left((u_{k}^{i})^{2}-(u_{k}^{i-1})^{2}\right)\leq 0.

(4.7)

The sequence

V_{i}=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{i}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mu_{k}\left(u_{k}^{i}\right)^{2}

thus satisfies the inequality

\mu_{1}V_{i}+\frac{c}{\tau}(V_{i}-V_{i-1})\leq 0,

and we conclude that

V_{i}\leq V_{0}\left(1+\frac{\mu_{1}\tau}{c}\right)^{-i},

(4.8)

that is,
V_{i}
decays exponentially as
i\to\infty
.

5  Convexity estimate

By (
3.5
), the functions
u_{i}
do not leave the interval
[-\Lambda,\Lambda]
. Since
G
is convexifiable in the sense of Definition
1.3
, there exist positive numbers
g_{*},g^{*},\bar{g}
and a twice continuously differentiable mapping
g:[-\Lambda,\Lambda]\to[-\Lambda,\Lambda]
such that
g(0)=0
,
0<g_{*}\leq g^{\prime}(u)\leq g^{*}<\infty
,
|g^{\prime\prime}(u)|\leq\bar{g}
for all
u\in[-\Lambda,\Lambda]
, and
G
is of the form

G=P\circ g,

(5.1)

where

i=1,2,...
we have

u(x,t_{2i})\leq a,\quad u(x,t_{2i+1})\geq b.

(7.14)

From the elementary inequalities

\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i})\leq u(x,t_{2i})+r,\quad\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i+1})\geq u(x,t_{2i+1})-r

and from (
7.14
) we infer that

\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i+1})-\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i})\geq b-a-2r>0

(7.15)

for
r\in(0,(b-a)/2)
and
x\in\Omega\setminus\Omega^{(n)}
. Hence, for
r<(b-a)/2
we get

\int_{\Omega}|\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i+1}){-}\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i})|\,\mathrm{d}x\geq\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{(n)}}\!|\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i+1}){-}\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i})|\,\mathrm{d}x\geq(|\Omega|{-}|\Omega^{(n)}|)(b{-}a{-}2r).

We thus have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}r|\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i+1})-\xi^{r}(x,t_{2i})|\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq(|\Omega|-|\Omega^{(n)}|)\int_{0}^{\min\{\Lambda,(b-a)/2\}}r(b-a-2r)\,\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\geq(|\Omega|-|\Omega^{(n)}|)\left(\min\{\Lambda,(b-a)/2\}\right)^{2}\,\frac{b-a}{6}>0

for all
i\geq n
, which contradicts (
7.4
). We thus conclude that (
7.10
) holds, which completes the proof of Theorem
1.5
.

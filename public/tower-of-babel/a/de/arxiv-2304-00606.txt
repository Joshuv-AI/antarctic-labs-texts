On Counting Flat Connections over G 2 G_{2} -Orbifolds
Langte Ma

Abstract
. We study the moduli space of G 2 G_{2} -instantons on (projectively) flat bundles over torsion-free G 2 G_{2} -orbifolds. We prove that the moduli space is compact and smooth at the irreducible locus after adding small and generic holonomy perturbations. Consequently, we define an integer-valued invariant that is invariant under C 0 C^{0} -deformation of torsion-free G 2 G_{2} -structures. We compute this invariant for some orbifolds that arise in Joyce’s construction of compact G 2 G_{2} -manifold.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

The study of gauge theory over manifolds with special holonomy was initiated by Donaldons–Thomas
[
DT98
]
as an extension of the gauge theory techniques in dimension
3
and
4
. In dimension
7
, the holonomy group over Riemannian manifolds that enters the picture is
G_{2}
, i.e. the automorphism group of octonions as a subgroup of
SO(7)
. For such manifolds, Donaldson–Segal
[
DS11
]
proposed a program of constructing invariants by relating the moduli space of instantons and calibrated submanifolds, which was further elaborated by Doan–Walpuski
[
DW19
]
. Ideally, one expects to have a thorough understanding of the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons, then extracts an enumerative invariant combining counting irreducible points and tracing through the potential failure of compactness. Invariants of such kind should be useful in the study of deformation properties of
G_{2}
-structures.

Although a general understanding of the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons is still elusive, progress has been made through the study of explicit examples. The class of examples that motivate this paper comes from Joyce’s construction
[
Joy96
]
. Roughly speaking, these compact
G_{2}
-manifolds are obtained from flat
G_{2}
-orbifolds by resolving the singularties using families of hyperkähler ALE spaces. In
[
Wal13
]
, Walpuski constructed examples of non-trivial
G_{2}
-instantons on Joyce’s
G_{2}
-manifolds by developing the gluing technique that grafts families of finite energy ASD instantons on ALE spaces to flat connections on
G_{2}
-orbifolds. Therefore analyzing instantons over the building blocks would lead to a complete picture of the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons over
G_{2}
-manifolds from Joyce’s list.

In this paper, we carry out the first step that studies the moduli space of flat connections over
G_{2}
-orbifolds. Since the deformation theory of flat connections in dimension
7
is not Fredholm, we choose to consider
G_{2}
-instantons over flat bundles. Heuristically, one can think of the
G_{2}
-structure as a deformation to the flatness equation of connections. To get a regular moduli space, one needs to further perturb the
G_{2}
-instanton equation by holonomy perturbations. After establishing the compactness of the moduli space, one can then extract an enumerative invariant by an appropriate counting procedure. The second step that studies
G_{2}
-instantons over a family of ALE spaces will be carried out in a future paper by the author in collaboration with Galt
[
GM23
]
. This approach for analyzing moduli spaces of instantons also appeared in Kronheimer’s work
[
Kro91
]
of counting instantons over
K3
surfaces. Our work is partially inspired by his method.

1.2.
Main Results

The notion ‘orbifold’ in this paper primarily means effective orbifold in the classical sense used by Satake
[
Sat56
]
and Thurston
[
Thu97
]
. When a general perspective of ‘orbifold’ shows up, we will address it explicitly. We also recall basics of effective orbifold in
Section 2
.

We write
(X,\phi)
for a typical compact smooth
G_{2}
-orbifold which means the holonomy group of the Levi–Civita connection on the Riemannian orbifold
X
is contained in
G_{2}\subset SO(7)
. As explained below in
Section 2
, the information of such a structure is encoded in a
3
-form
\phi\in\Omega^{3}(X;\mathbb{R})
satisfying

(1.1)

d\phi=0\qquad d\star_{\phi}\phi=0,

where
\star_{\phi}
means the Hodge star defined with respect to the metric induced from
\phi
. One further requires that at each point
x\in X
there is an orbifold chart
\tilde{U}_{x}\rightarrow U_{x}
centered at
x\in U_{x}\subset X
so that
\phi|_{x}
, when lifted to
\tilde{U}_{x}
, is identified with the standard
3
-form
\phi_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
:

(1.2)

\phi_{0}:=dx_{123}+dx_{145}+dx_{167}+dx_{246}-dx_{257}-dx_{347}-dx_{356},

where
dx_{ijk}:=dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}\wedge dx_{k}
. The condition (
1.1
) is usually referred to as the torsion-free property of the
G_{2}
-structure.

We write
P^{\dagger}
for a principal
U(r)
-orbibundle over
(X,\phi)
and
P
the adjoint bundle of
P^{\dagger}
with structure group
PU(r)
. Ignoring the regularity issue, we write
{A}
for the space of
C^{\infty}
-connections on the adjoint bundle
P
and
{G}
the gauge group consisting of
C^{\infty}
-automorphisms of
P^{\dagger}
that fixes the determinant line
\det P^{\dagger}
. Alternatively, one can think of
{A}
as the space of unitary connections on
P^{\dagger}
that induce a fixed connection on
\det P^{\dagger}
, and
{G}
consists of automorphisms that preserve this space of connections. For a unitary connection
A^{\dagger}
on
P^{\dagger}
, we write
A
for the induced connection on the adjoint bundle
P
.

Definition 1.1
.

A unitary connection
A^{\dagger}\in{A}
is called a projective
G_{2}
-instanton with respect to
\phi
if

F_{A}\wedge\star_{\phi}\phi=0,

where
A
is the induced connection of
A^{\dagger}
on the adjoint bundle
P
.

In
Section 4
, we shall introduce a Banach space
{P}
of holonomy perturbations. Each element
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
gives rise to a
{G}
-invariant functional
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}:{A}\rightarrow\Omega^{6}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})
that can be used to perturb the
G_{2}
-instanton equation. Here
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
denotes the Lie algebra bundle associated to
P
by the adjoint action. We write

\mathcal{M}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\{A\in{A}:F_{A}\wedge\star_{\phi}\phi+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0\}/{G}

for the moduli space of
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed projective
G_{2}
-instantons on
P^{\dagger}
. Let
Z(r)\subset U(r)
be the center of
U(r)
, which is a cylic group of order
r
. We say a connection
A\in{A}
is irreducible if its isotropy group
\Stab(A)\subset{G}
is
Z(r)
. We prove the following result concerning the structure of the moduli space.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be a principal
U(r)
-orbibudle over a torsion-free
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
whose Chern classes satisfy

(1.3)

(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(P^{\dagger})-2rc_{2}(P^{\dagger})=0\in H^{4}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z}).

Suppose
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
is a generic small perturbation. Then the following hold.

(1)

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
is compact.

(2)

The irreducible locus
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
is a smooth
0
-dimensional manifold, i.e. it’s transversely cut out by the perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton equation.

We explain why the torsion-free assumption (
1.1
) plays an important role in
Theorem 1.2
. The closedness condition
d\phi=0
is used to provide an a priori bound on the
L^{2}
-norm of curvature for
G_{2}
-instantons. When the instanton equation is not perturbed, the requirement (
1.3
) combined with the closedness of
\phi
implies that all
G_{2}
-instantons are flat connections. The coclosed condition for
\phi
is used essentially to get an elliptic deformation theory for
G_{2}
-instantons.

We only sort out the orientability issue for the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
in limited cases of orbifolds. Concretely, we require the singular set of
X
be either locally modeled on the fixed point set of a
\phi
-preserving involution or an intersection of the former models in certain proper sense. Locally, it forces the singular set to be either ‘associative’ (calibrated submanifold) or a curve arising as the intersection of two associative submanifolds. Nevertheless, such orbifolds have covered most cases appeared in Joyce’s construction. We call such orbifolds simple
G_{2}
-orbifolds.

Besides (
1.3
), we also make another topological assumption on
P^{\dagger}
for the purpose of avoiding reducible instantons. We call such bundles admissible in
subsection 3.3
. With these preparations, we can define an enumerative invariant.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be an admissible
U(r)
-bundle over a torsion-free, compact, smooth
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Then counting irreducible projective
G_{2}
-instantons on
P^{\dagger}
defines an invariant

n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\#\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z}/2.

If we further require
(X,\phi)
be a simple
G_{2}
-orbifold, and
c_{1}(P^{\dagger})
even or
r=2
, then counting with signs gives us

n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\#\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z}

after fixing extra orientation data. Moreover, the invariant
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is invariant under
C^{0}
-deformation of torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures on
X
.

Although we haven’t found examples of torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures on a fixed smooth orbifold
X
resulting in different values of
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
, the torsion-free condition has been used in an essential way to deduce the deformation invariance. Because we need the closedness to guarantee compactness in
Theorem 1.2
. Note that the
h
-principle has been worked out for coclosed
G_{2}
-structures by Crowley–Nordström
[
CN15
]
. So connecting torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures by a path of coclosed
G_{2}
-structures is not hard to achieve. It would be interesting to understand how the moduli space of admissible bundles can change at a jumping point where the
G_{2}
-structure starts to fail being closed.

Since we work on (projectively) flat bundles, there is chance that the invariant we defined is merely topological. This is indeed the case if the moduli space of projectively flat connections is already non-degenerate in the following sense.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A^{\dagger}
be an irreducible projectively flat connection on an admissible principal
U(r)
-bundle
P^{\dagger}
. We say
A
is non-degenerate if

H^{1}(X,\rho_{A})=0,

where
\rho_{A}:\pi^{\orb}_{1}(X)\rightarrow SO(r^{2}-1)
is the representation associated to the induced flat connection on the bundle
\mathfrak{su}(P^{\dagger})
consisting of traceless skew-Hermitian endomorphisms of
P^{\dagger}
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be an admissible
U(r)
-bundle over a torsion-free, compact, smooth
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Suppose every irreducible projectively flat connection on
P^{\dagger}
is non-degenerate. Then the invariant
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is independent of the choices of the torsion-free
G_{2}
-structure
\phi
.

We ask for the torsion-free condition in
subsection 1.2
to ensure that the invariant
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is defined. In the non-degenerate case, the moduli spaces together with their deformation structures are identified canonically with respect to different torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures. When
(X,\phi)
is a flat orbifold, a Weitzenböck formula argument shows that every irreducible projectively flat connection is non-degenerate automatically. In particular, all orbifolds arising from Joyce’s construction satisfy the non-degeneracy condition automatically. We compute the invariant for one such orbifold by exhausting all irreducible representations. Such computation for many other flat orbifolds in Joyce’s construction can be worked out in a similar way.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be the flat simple orbifold constructed in Example 3 of
[
Joy96
]
. Then there are admissible
U(2)
-bundles
P^{\dagger}
on
(X,\phi)
whose corresponding invariant is
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})=2^{6}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

To conclude, we mention that a homotopy invariant
\nu(X,\phi)\in\mathbb{Z}/48
for
G_{2}
-structures (not necessarily torsion-free) over
7
-manifolds was introduced by Crowley–Nordström
[
CN15
]
as an ‘
\hat{A}
-defect’. If we consider non-simply-connected
G_{2}
-manifolds
(X,\phi)
with
H^{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})=0
, an invariant
n_{\phi}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}
can be defined by summing up the counting of all irreducible
G_{2}
-instantons over flat
SU(2)
-bundles. We suspect there might be a relation between
n_{\phi}(X)
and
\nu(X,\phi)
, which is analogous to the case of the Casson invariant and Rohlin invariant on homology
3
-spheres. Computations of the
\nu
-invariant over Joyce’s manifolds were carried out in the work of Scaduto
[
Sca20
]
.

1.3.
Outline

In
Section 2
, we review necessary materials concerning torsion-free
G_{2}
-orbifolds. In
Section 3
, we set up the frame work for the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons on
G_{2}
-orbifolds, including deformation, reducibility, and orientation. In
Section 4
, we introduce holonomy perturbations following the work of Donaldson, and prove transversality for irreducible moduli spaces. In
Section 5
, we prove the compactness of moduli spaces under small perturbations. In

, we define the invariant
n_{\phi}(X)
and verify its deformation invariance. Calculations for examples are also included in the end.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the basic materials centering around gauge theory over
G_{2}
-manifolds, and explain how to extend corresponding notions to the orbifold setting. For a more detailed exposition on the geometry of
G_{2}
-manifolds, one may consult Joyce’s book
[
Joy00
]
. As for the gauge theoretical perspective on
G_{2}
-manifolds, the paper by Donaldson–Segal
[
DS11
]
is an excellent reference.

2.1.
Linear Algebra Related to
G_{2}

We start with a brief review on the linear algebra behind the Lie group
G_{2}
. For a more comprehensive treatment, one may consult the notes by Salamon–Walpuski
[
SW17
]
. Let
V=(\mathbb{R}^{7},g_{0})
be the
7
-dimensional real vector space
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the standard Euclidean inner product
g_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{7}dx_{i}^{2}
, where
(x_{1},...,x_{7})
are the standard coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
. An orientation over
V
is also fixed so that the corresponding volume form is
\vol_{0}=dx_{1}\wedge...\wedge dx_{7}
. Over
V
, we have a
3
-form

(2.1)

\phi_{0}:=dx_{123}+dx_{145}+dx_{167}+dx_{246}-dx_{257}-dx_{347}-dx_{356},

where
dx_{ijk}:=dx_{i}\wedge dx_{j}\wedge dx_{k}
. We define
G_{2}
to be the subgroup of
GL_{\mathbb{R}}(7)
consisting of elements that preserve the
3
-form
\phi_{0}
. It is known that
G_{2}
is a compact, connected, simply-connected, semisimple Lie group of real dimension
14
(c.f.
[
Joy00
,
SW17
]
). The
3
-form
\phi_{0}
defines a cross-product on
V
via the relation

(2.2)

\phi_{0}(u,v,w)=g_{0}(u\times v,w),\quad\forall\;u,v,w\in V.

Here a cross-product means a skew-symmetric bilinear form
\times:V\times V\rightarrow V
satisfying

g_{0}(u\times v,u)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad|u\times v|^{2}=|u|^{2}|v|^{2}-g_{0}(u,v)^{2},\quad\forall\;u,v\in V.

If we identify
\mathbb{R}^{7}
with the imaginary part of the octonions
\mathbb{O}
, the cross product given by
\phi_{0}
is the octonionic multiplication followed by taking imaginary part. The volume form
\vol_{0}
of
V
is related to the
3
-form
\phi_{0}
by

(2.3)

\iota_{u}\phi_{0}\wedge\iota_{v}\phi_{0}\wedge\phi_{0}=6g_{0}(u_{0},v_{0})\vol_{0},\quad\forall\;u,v\in V,

where
\iota_{u}
is the contraction map. (
2.3
) implies that the
3
-form
\phi_{0}
induces the inner product
g_{0}
once an orientation of
V
is chosen.

The induced
G_{2}
-action on the space of alternating
2
-tensors
\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
splits the space into the sum of irreducible
G_{2}
-representations:

(2.4)

\Lambda^{2}V^{*}:=\Lambda^{2}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14},

where
\Lambda^{2}_{7}
is isomorphic to the fundamental representation of
G_{2}
on
\mathbb{R}^{7}
, and
\Lambda^{2}_{14}
is isomorphic to the adjoint
G_{2}
-representation on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{2}
. The irreducible representations can also be written down explicitly as (c.f.
[
SW17
, Theorem 8.5]
):

(2.5)

\Lambda^{2}_{7}=\left\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}:\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega)=2\omega)\right\}\text{ and }\Lambda^{2}_{14}=\left\{\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}:\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega)=-\omega\right\}.

In other words,
\Lambda^{2}_{7}
and
\Lambda^{2}_{14}
are the
2
- and
-1
-eigenspaces of the operator
\star(\phi_{0}\wedge-)
acting on
\Lambda^{2}V^{*}
respectively. Using the relation

\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega))=2\omega+\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega),\quad\forall\;\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*},

one can write down the projection map as

(2.6)

\pi_{7}(\omega)=\frac{1}{3}(\omega+\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega))\text{ and }\pi_{14}(\omega)=\frac{1}{3}(2\omega-\star(\phi_{0}\wedge\omega)).

The equation (
2.6
) immediately gives us the following generalization of (
2.3
):

(2.7)

\omega\wedge\omega\wedge\phi_{0}=\left(2|\pi_{7}(\omega)|^{2}-|\pi_{14}(\omega)|^{2}\right)\vol_{0},\quad\forall\;\omega\in\Lambda^{2}V^{*}.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}^{3}(V)
the orbit of
\phi_{0}
under the
GL^{+}_{\mathbb{R}}(7)
-action, where
GL^{+}_{\mathbb{R}}(7)
is the group consisting of orientation-preserving automorphisms of
V
. We refer to an element
\phi\in\mathcal{P}^{3}(V)
as a positive
3
-form. One sees immediately that
\mathcal{P}^{3}(V)
is an open subset of
\Lambda^{3}V^{*}
which can be identified with
GL^{+}_{\mathbb{R}}(7)/G_{2}
. The discussion above tells us that every positive
3
-form
\phi
determines an inner product
g_{\phi}
and volume form
\vol_{\phi}
on an oriented
\mathbb{R}^{7}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\phi\in\mathcal{P}^{3}(V)
be a positive
3
-form. A oriented
3
-plane
W\subset V
is said to be
\phi
-associative if
\vol_{W}=\phi|_{W}
, where
\vol_{W}
is the volume form on
W
induced from
\mathfrak{g}_{\phi}
.

Due to (
2.2
), every
\phi
-associative subspace
W
arises as the span of
u,v
and
u\times_{\phi}v
for some linearly independent vectors
u,v\in V
. Associative subspaces are closely related to invariant subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{7}
under the action of finite subgroups of
G_{2}
. For instance, any element
\sigma\in G_{2}
of order
2
fixes a associative subspace in
V
(c.f.
[
Joy00
, Proposition 10.8.1]
). In general, the space of invariant vectors in
V
under the action of a finite subgroup in
G_{2}
can be described as follows.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
Joy00
, Proposition 11.1.3]
)
.

Let
\Gamma\subset G_{2}
be a finite subgroup, and

V^{\Gamma}:=\{v\in V:\gamma v=v\;\;\forall\gamma\in\Gamma\}

be the subspace of
\Gamma
-invariant vectors. Then
V^{\Gamma}
takes one of the following forms.

(a)

V^{\Gamma}=V\text{ or }\{0\}
.

(b)

V^{\Gamma}
is an associative subspace, in which case
\Gamma
is conjugate to a finite subgroup of
SU(2)
so that
V/\Gamma\simeq\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}/\Gamma
.

(c)

V^{\Gamma}\simeq\mathbb{R}
, in which case
\Gamma
is conjugate to a finite subgroup of
SU(3)
so that
V/G\simeq\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{3}/\Gamma
.

2.2.
G_{2}
-Orbifolds

Following the treatment in
[
ALR07
]
, we first review the notion ‘effective orbifold’ in the classical sense
[
Sat56
]
. Let
X
be a paracompact Hausdorff space. An
n
-dimensional orbifold chart on
X
is a triple
(\tilde{U},G,\phi)
where

(1)

\tilde{U}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open neighborhood of the origin;

(2)

G
is a finite subgroup of
O(n)
;

(3)

\phi:\tilde{U}\rightarrow X
is a
G
-invariant map that induces a homeomorphism
\bar{\phi}:\tilde{U}/G\rightarrow\phi(\tilde{U})
.

An
n
-dimensional orbifold atlas
{U}=\{(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}
consists of a family of
n
-dimensional orbifold charts that cover
X
and satisfy the following compatibility assumption

•

Given two charts
(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})
and
(\tilde{U}_{\beta},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\beta})
, for any
x\in\varphi_{\alpha}(\tilde{U}_{\alpha})\cap\varphi_{\beta}(\tilde{U}_{\beta})
one can find a third chart
(\tilde{U}_{\gamma},G_{\gamma},\varphi_{\gamma})
covering
x
and smooth embeddings
\lambda_{\alpha\gamma}:\tilde{U}_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
,
\lambda_{\beta\gamma}:\tilde{U}_{\gamma}\hookrightarrow\tilde{U}_{\beta}
such that

\varphi_{\gamma}=\varphi_{\alpha}\circ\lambda_{\alpha\gamma}=\varphi_{\beta}\circ\lambda_{\beta\gamma}

Given smooth embedding
\lambda_{\beta\alpha}:\tilde{U}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\tilde{U}_{\beta}
satisfying
\varphi_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\beta}\circ\lambda_{\beta\alpha}
, it can be shown
[
Moe02
]
that for each
g_{\alpha}\in G_{\alpha}
there exists a unique element in
G_{\beta}
, denoted by
\lambda_{\beta\alpha}(g_{\alpha})
, such that

\lambda_{\beta\alpha}\circ g_{\alpha}=\lambda_{\beta\alpha}(g_{\alpha})\circ\lambda_{\beta\alpha}.

The assignment
\lambda_{\beta\alpha}:G_{\alpha}\rightarrow G_{\beta}
is injective and makes the embedding
G_{\alpha}
-equivariant.

Definition 2.3
.

An
n
-dimensional orbifold
X
is a paracompact Hausdorff space equipped with a maximal
n
-dimensional orbifold atlas.

Given a point
x
in an orbifold
X
, we can choose an orbifold chart
(\tilde{U},G,\varphi)
that covers
x
. Let
\tilde{x}\in\varphi^{-1}(x)\subset\tilde{U}
be a preimage of
x
. We denote by
G_{\tilde{x}}
the subgroup of
G
consisting of stabilizers of
\tilde{x}
. Different choices of
\tilde{x}
in
\varphi^{-1}(x)
and orbifold charts give rise to canonically isomorphic stabilizers
G_{\tilde{x}}
. Thus we denote by
G_{x}
as an abstract group isomorphism to
G_{\tilde{x}}
for some
\tilde{x}\in\varphi^{-1}(x)
, and refer to
G_{x}
as the isotropy group of
x
. Locally near
x
, the orbifold
X
is modeled on
\tilde{U}_{x}/G_{x}
where
\tilde{U}_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open set so that
x
is identified with the origin under the quotient. We denote by

(2.8)

S_{X}:=\{x\in X:G_{x}\neq 1\}

the set of points with non-trivial isotropy group. Each point in
S_{X}
is called a singular point of the orbifold
X
.

Given an orbifold chart
(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})
, one can consider the tangent bundle
T\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
which is naturally a
G_{\alpha}
-equivariant bundle with the
G_{\alpha}
-action induced from differentiating the
G_{\alpha}
-action on the base
\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
. The orbifold charts
(T\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha})
can be patched together to get

TX:=\bigsqcup_{\alpha}(T\tilde{U}_{\alpha}/G_{\alpha})/\sim,

where the patching map is given by the differential of the embeddings
\lambda_{\beta\alpha}
. The total space
TX
is a
2n
-dimensional orbifold, which we refer to as the tangent bundle of
X
. There is a natural projection
\pi:TX\rightarrow X
whose fiber at
x\in X
is

\pi^{-1}(x)=T_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{U}/G_{\tilde{x}},

where
\tilde{x}\in\phi^{-1}(x)
is a point in the preimage of
x
.

Such a construction can be carried over to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}X
, the symmetric tensor bundle
\Sym^{*}T^{*}X
, and the alternating tensor bundle
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}X
. Locally over a chart
(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha})
these bundles are
G_{\alpha}
-equivariant bundles. A global section of these bundles consists of locally
G_{\alpha}
-equivariant sections that are compatible under the patching maps. In particular, a Riemannian metric
g
over an orbifold

is a positive-definite section of
\Sym^{2}T^{*}X
. Analogous to the manifold scenario, an orbifold Riemannian metric
g
gives rise to a Levi–Civita connection on all tensor bundles of
X
, which we denote by
\nabla_{g}
.

We say
X
is orientable if
\Lambda^{n}T^{*}X
admits a nowhere vanishing section. Such a section specifies an orientation of
X
. Combining with the Riemannian metric
g
, we get a volume form
\vol
over
X
. The integration over
X
is defined as follows. Locally over
U_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}(\tilde{U}_{\alpha})\subset X
, a compactly supported
n
-form
\omega_{\alpha}
on
U_{\alpha}
arises as the
G_{\alpha}
-quotient of an equivariant
n
-form
\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha}
. Then we define

\int_{U_{\alpha}}\omega_{\alpha}:=\frac{1}{|G_{\alpha}|}\int_{\tilde{U}_{\alpha}}\tilde{\omega}_{\alpha},

where the integral on the RHS is defined with the help of the volume form. To integrate a general
n
-form over
X
, we take a partition of unity subordinate to
\{U_{\alpha}\}
to truncate the
n
-form, and add up integrals on the charts.

Let
(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha})
be an oriented orbifold chart. For each
\tilde{x}\in\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
, we denote by
\mathcal{P}^{3}(T_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{U}_{\alpha})
the set of positive
3
-forms on
T_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}^{3}(T\tilde{U}_{\alpha})=\bigcup_{\tilde{x}}\mathcal{P}^{3}(T_{\tilde{x}}\tilde{U}_{\alpha})
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let

be an oriented
7
-dimensional orbifold.

•

-structure over

-form

-quotient of an equivariant positive

-form

•

Denote by

the induced Riemannian metric from an almost

-structure

. We say

-structure if it is

The condition

is equivalent to that the Levi-Civita connection

is torsion-free. The following result provides us with a useful criterion to justify whether an almost

-structure is parallel or not.

Lemma 2.5

(
[

, Proposition 10.1.3]
)
.

Let

be an almost
G_{2}
-structure over an orbifold

. Then
\phi
is

-parallel if and only if

d\phi=0\quad\text{ and }\quad d\star_{\phi}\phi=0,

where
\star_{\phi}
is the Hodge star given by the induced metric

.

Let
(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})
be an orbifold chart of
X
, and
U_{\alpha}=\varphi_{\alpha}(\tilde{U}_{\alpha})\subset X
. In the definition, the restriction of a
G_{2}
-structure

is given by the quotient of a

-equivariant positive
3
-form

. Since

-form

. Thus, up to

can be identified with a discrete subgroup of

. The conjugacy classes of finite subgroups of

have been classified in
[

. All other finite subgroups of

lie in either

or

Typical examples of compact

Now we briefly discuss the homotopy and (co)homology groups of orbifolds following the treatment in
[
ALR07
]
. To each orbifold
X
, one can associate a classifying space
c:\mathcal{B}X\rightarrow X
which has the homotopy type of a CW-complex
[
Seg74
]
. This classifying space is unique up to weak homotopy equivalent. Then we define the homotopy groups and (co)homology groups of the orbifold
X
respectively as

(2.11)

\pi^{\orb}_{n}(X,x):=\pi_{n}(\mathcal{B}X,x_{c})\quad H^{\orb}_{n}(X;R):=H_{n}(\mathcal{B}X;R)\quad H^{n}_{\orb}(X;R):=H^{n}(\mathcal{B}X;R),

where
x_{c}\in\mathcal{B}X
satisfies
c(x_{c})=x
, and
R
is any commutative ring. It is proved
[
ALR07
, Corollary 1.24]
that every (classical) compact
n
-orbifold
X
is the global quotient of a smooth manifold
M
under a smooth, effective, and almost free action by a compact Lie group
G
. Indeed, one can take
M
to be the total space of the frame bundle of
X
and
G=O(n)
. In this case the classifying space
\mathcal{B}X
arises as the Borel construction
EG\times_{G}M
, where
EG
is the classifying bundle of the compact Lie group
G
. So the orbifold (co)homology of
X=M/G
is the equivariant (co)homology of
(M,G)
.

One can also consider the deRham cohomology over an orbifold whose chain complex consists of orbifold
n
-forms
\Omega^{n}(X)
, and whose differential is the exterior derivative on forms. We denote the deRham cohomology by
H^{*}_{\dR}(X)
which can be identified with the (non-orbifold) singular cohomology
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{R})
of
X
with
\mathbb{R}
-coefficient (c.f.
[
Sat56
]
). Moreover, the orbifold cohomology
H^{*}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{R})
is also isomorphic to usual singular cohomology
H^{*}(X;\mathbb{R})
.

When
G
is a finite group, the fibration
M\rightarrow EG\times_{G}M\rightarrow BG
gives rise to a short exact sequence:

(2.12)

1\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\pi^{\orb}_{1}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow G\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 1.

By passing to the universal cover
\tilde{M}
of

, the orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\orb}(X)
can be identified with the group of covering transformations on
\tilde{M}

[
ALR07
, Theorem 2.18]
. The orbifold fundamental group has a more elementary description which corresponds to the definition of the fundamental group of manifolds. We recall this description following the exposition in
[
Hae90
]
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
X
be an orbifold equipped with an orbifold atlas
\{(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}_{\alpha\in I}
. Let
x,y\in X
be two fixed points.

•

An orbipath
\gamma
from
x
to
y
consists of the following data.

(1)

A partition
0=t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq...\leq t_{n}=1
of the unit interval
[0,1]
.

(2)

An index function
\tau:\{1,...,n\}\rightarrow I
and a family of continuous paths
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}:[t_{i-1},t_{i}]\rightarrow\tilde{U}_{\tau(i)}
such that

\varphi_{\tau(1)}(\tilde{\gamma}_{1}(0))=x,\quad\varphi_{\tau(i)}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i}(t_{i-1}))=\varphi_{\tau(i-1)}(\tilde{\gamma}_{i-1}(t_{i-1})),\quad\varphi_{\tau(n)}(\tilde{\gamma}_{n}(1))=y.

•

Two orbipaths
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
from
x
to
y
are equivalent if, after passing to subdivisions of
[0,1]
and refinements to a common index function
\tau
, the local lifts are related by
\tilde{\gamma}_{i}=g_{i}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}^{\prime}_{i}
for some
g_{i}\in G_{\tau(i)}
.

•

Two orbipaths
\gamma
and
\gamma^{\prime}
are homotopic relative to ends if one can find a continuous family of orbipaths
(\gamma^{s})_{s\in[0,1]}
so that for all
s\in[0,1]
one has

\gamma^{s}(0)=x,\quad\gamma^{0}=\gamma,\quad,\gamma^{1}=\gamma^{\prime},\quad\gamma^{s}(1)=y.

•

The orbifold fundamental group
\pi^{\orb}_{1}(X,x)
is defined to the set of orbipaths based at
x
, up to orbipath equivalence and homotopy relative to ends, which is equipped with the obvious group structure.

One can heuristically think of an orbipath as a continuous path on
X
with chosen local lifts, and the orbifold fundamental group as homotopy classes of such objects. The notion orbipath will be useful when we consider holonomy of connections over orbibundles.

2.3.
Orbibundles and Connections

Definition 2.8
.

Let
X
be an
n
-orbifold equipped with an orbifold atlas
\{(\tilde{U}_{\alpha},G_{\alpha},\varphi_{\alpha})\}
.

•

A rank-

\{\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\}

\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\tilde{U}_{\alpha}

\tilde{E}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\tilde{E}_{\beta}
that are compatible with the embeddings
\tilde{U}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\tilde{U}_{\beta}
.

•

Let
G
be a compact Lie group. A principal

-orbibundle

over

is a collection
\{\tilde{P}_{\alpha}\}
of
G_{\alpha}
-equivariant principal

-bundles
\pi_{\alpha}:\tilde{P}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\tilde{U}_{\alpha}
(the left

-action commutes with the right

\tilde{P}_{\alpha}
) equipped with fiberwise isomorphic embeddings
\tilde{P}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\tilde{P}_{\beta}
that are compatible with the embeddings
\tilde{U}_{\alpha}\hookrightarrow\tilde{U}_{\beta}
.

Let
P
be a principal
G
-orbibundle over
X
. Same as the case of manifolds
[
KN63
]

over

is defined as a compatible collection of

-equivariant bundle sequence:

(2.13)

0\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\tilde{P}_{\alpha}\times\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\psi_{\alpha}}T\tilde{P}_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\alpha,*}}\pi_{\alpha}^{*}T\tilde{U}_{\alpha}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow 0,

where

is the Lie algebra of

, and
\psi_{\alpha}
is the infinitesimal Lie algebra action that induces the isomorphism between

and the vertical fiber of

. A splitting of (

which gives us a

, or as a right inverse of

To a representation

(2.14)

(

)
.

The holonomy map induces a one-to-one correspondence:

\mathfrak{F}(X,G)/\sim\quad\longleftrightarrow\quad\Hom(\pi^{\orb}_{1}(X),G)/\Ad.

3.

G_{2}
-Orbifolds

In this section, we set up the framework of gauge theory over

-orbifolds following Donaldson–Segal
[
DS11
]
. For a more comprehensive treatment, one may consult the thesis of Sá Earp
[
SE09
]
.

To start, we pin-down the convention of orbifolds and bundles. Let
(X,\phi)
be a compact orbifold equipped with a torsion-free
G_{2}
-structure, and
\pi:P^{\dagger}\rightarrow X
a principal
U(r)
-bundle. From
P^{\dagger}
, we obtain a line bundle
\det P^{\dagger}
via the determinant homomorphism
\det:U(r)\rightarrow U(1)
and a
PU(r)
-bundle
P
via the projective homomorphism
\pr:U(r)\rightarrow PU(r)
. A general connection of

is denoted by
A^{\dagger}
, which induces a connection
\theta_{A}
on
\det P^{\dagger}
and a connection
A
on
P
.

3.1.
G_{2}
-Instantons

We fix a smooth connection
A^{\dagger}_{0}
on
P^{\dagger}
as a reference connection. We also fix an integer

to ensure the Soboleve embedding
L^{2}_{k}\hookrightarrow C^{0}
. Then we get an induced connection
A
on the
PU(r)
-bundle
P
. Denote by
{A}_{k}
the space of all
PU(r)
-connections over
P
in class
L^{2}_{k}
. Explicitly
A\in{A}_{k}
if and only if

\|A-A_{0}\|_{L^{2}_{k,A_{0}}(X)}:=\sum_{i=0}^{k}\left(\int_{X}|\nabla^{i}_{A_{0}}(A-A_{0})|^{2}d\vol\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty,

where
\nabla^{i}_{A_{0}}
means to take the
i
-th derivative with respect to
A_{0}
. Then
{A}_{k}
is an affine space modeled on
L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})
, where
\Lambda^{*}
denotes
\Lambda^{*}T^{*}X
.

Remark 3.1
.

We always identify
\mathfrak{pu}(r)
with the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{su}(r)
via the isomorphism
\ad:\mathfrak{su}(r)\rightarrow\mathfrak{pu}(r)
given by differentiating the adjoint map. The inner product on
\mathfrak{su}(r)
is defined via the Killing form:

\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle:=-\tr(\ad(\alpha)\circ\ad(\beta))=-2r\tr(\alpha\beta),\quad\forall\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{su}(r).

The gauge group
{G}_{k+1}
is defined as follows. Note that the adjoint action of
U(r)
on
SU(r)
factors through
PU(r)
. So we can consider bundles

G^{\prime}_{P}:=P\times_{\Ad}SU(r)\qquad G_{P}:=P\times_{\Ad}PU(r).

Sections of the second bundle
G_{P}
are automorphisms of
P
. The projective homomorphism
\pr:SU(r)\rightarrow PU(r)
induces a map
\pr_{P}:G^{\prime}_{P}\rightarrow G_{P}
. We define the gauge group to be

{G}_{k+1}:=L^{2}_{k+1}(G^{\prime}_{P})

which acts on the bundle
P
by pre-composing with
\pr_{P}
. Elements in
{G}_{k+1}
are commonly referred to as ‘determinant-1’ of
SU(r)
gauge transformations. Sections of
G^{\prime}_{P}
act on the space of connections via pull-backs as usual:

(3.1)

u\cdot A:=A-(d_{A}u)u^{-1},\quad u\in{G}_{k+1},\;A\in{A}_{k}.

Over
7
-orbifolds, the Sobolev multiplication theorem
L^{2}_{k+1}\times L^{2}_{k}\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{k}
holds when
k\geq 3
. Thus gauge transformations in
{G}_{k+1}
preserves
{A}_{k}
. The Lie algebra of
{G}_{k+1}
is identified with
L^{2}_{k+1}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})
.

Alternatively, one can work with
U(r)
-connections over
P^{\dagger}
with an extra constraint. Denote by
\theta_{0}=\det A^{\dagger}_{0}
the induced connection on the determinant line
\det P^{\dagger}
. Then the space
{A}_{k}
of connections can be identified with the space of
L^{2}_{k}

U(r)
-connections
A^{\dagger}
on
P^{\dagger}
such that
\det A^{\dagger}=\theta_{0}
. Gauge transformations on
P^{\dagger}
that preserve
\theta_{0}
have trivial determinants, thus form the aforementioned group
{G}_{k+1}
.

Denote by
\psi:=\star_{\phi}\phi
the
4
-form associated to the positive
3
-form
\phi
. The
G_{2}
-variant of the Chern-Simons functional is defined as:

(3.2)

\cs_{\phi}(A_{0}+a):=-2r\int_{X}\left(\frac{1}{2}\tr(a\wedge F_{A_{0}+a})+\frac{1}{3}\tr(a\wedge a\wedge a)\right)\wedge\psi.

It’s clear that the Chern–Simons functional defines an analytic functional over the space of connections
\cs_{\phi}:{A}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

The gradient of the Chern–Simons functional is given by

\grad\cs_{\phi}(A)=\star_{\phi}(F_{A}\wedge\psi).

Proof.

We identify the tangent space
T_{A}{A}_{k}
at
A=A_{0}+a
by
L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})
. Then the differential can be computed as

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2r}D\cs_{\phi}|_{A}(\alpha)=&-\int_{X}\frac{1}{2}\left(\tr(a\wedge d_{A_{0}}\alpha)+\tr(\alpha\wedge d_{A_{0}}a)\right)\wedge\psi\\
&-\int_{X}\frac{1}{3}\left(\tr(\alpha\wedge a\wedge a)+\tr(a\wedge\alpha\wedge a)+\tr(a\wedge a\wedge\alpha)\right)\wedge\psi.\end{split}

Integration by parts gives us

\int_{X}\tr(a\wedge d_{A_{0}}\alpha)\wedge\psi=\int_{X}\tr(\alpha\wedge d_{A_{0}}a)\wedge\psi.

The cyclic-permutation invariance of the trace operator tells us that

\begin{split}D\cs_{\phi}|_{A}(\alpha)&=-2r\int_{X}\tr(\alpha\wedge(d_{A_{0}}a+a\wedge a))\wedge\psi\\
&=-2r\int_{X}\tr(\alpha\wedge F_{A}\wedge\psi)\\
&=\langle\alpha,\star_{\phi}(F_{A}\wedge\psi)\rangle_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 3.3
.

Let
P^{\dagger}\rightarrow X
be a principal
U(r)
-orbibundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. A unitary connection
A^{\dagger}
over
P^{\dagger}
is a projective
G_{2}
-instanton if

F_{A}\wedge\psi=0,

where
A
is the induced connection of
A^{\dagger}
on the
PU(r)
-bundle
P
.

As we work with connections on the
PU(r)
-bundle
P
in most times, we shall simply refer to
A
as a
G_{2}
-instanton if
F_{A}\wedge\psi=0
.
subsection 3.1
tells us that a
G_{2}
-instanton is a critical point of the Chern–Simons functional
\cs_{\phi}
. This perspective of
G_{2}
-instantons arises as a reflection on the scenario in dimension 3 where flat connections are critical points of the Chern–Simons functional. On the other hand,
G_{2}
-instantons also bear similarities with ASD instantons in dimension
4
. Recall in (
2.4
) that the
G_{2}
-action on
\Lambda^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{7})^{*}
decomposes into two
G_{2}
-representations
\Lambda^{2}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14}
. Since the
3
-form
\phi
is parallel, such a decomposition carries through to the bundle of
2
-forms over the
G_{2}
-orbifold
X
immediately:

\Lambda^{2}T^{*}X=\Lambda^{2}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14}.

Denote by
\pi_{7}:\Lambda^{2}\rightarrow\Lambda^{2}_{7}
the orthogonal projection. For simplicity, we shall write
\star
for
\star_{\phi}
when the context is clear.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
P\rightarrow X
be a principal
U(r)
-orbibundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

A
is a
G_{2}
-instanton over
P
.

(b)

\pi_{7}(F_{A})=0
.

(c)

\star_{\phi}(F_{A}\wedge\phi)=-F_{A}
.

Proof.

The equivalence between (b) and (c) follows from (
2.5
). For the equivalence between (a) and (c), we note the following identity (c.f.
[
SW17
, Equation (4.60)]
)

\star(\psi\wedge\star(\psi\wedge F_{A}))=F_{A}+\star(F_{A}\wedge\phi).

Since the adjoint of
\psi\wedge-
is
\star(\psi\wedge(\star-))
, we conclude that
F_{A}+\star(F_{A}\wedge\phi)=0
if and only if
\psi\wedge F_{A}=0
. This completes the proof.
∎

The second criterion (b) in
subsection 3.1
takes
G_{2}
-instantons to the analogous setting of ASD instantons in dimension four. Recall
SO(4)
admits two irreducible
3
-dimensional representations, which decomposes the bundle of
2
-forms over a
4
-manifold into two parts
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{+}\oplus\Lambda^{-}
consisting of the self-dual and anti-self-dual
2
-forms respectively. Then a connection

is ASD if its self-dual part vanishes, i.e.
\pi_{+}(F_{A})=0
. The analogy with ASD instantons can be brought further by considering the Yang–Mills functional

(3.3)

\begin{split}\mathcal{YM}:{A}_{k}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
A&\longmapsto\int_{X}|F_{A}|^{2}d\vol.\end{split}

When
A
is a
G_{2}
-instanton, (

) and (

(3.4)

\mathcal{YM}(A)=\|\pi_{14}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=-\int_{X}2r\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\phi=8\pi^{2}\langle p_{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{P})\smile[\phi],[X]\rangle,

where

is the real Pontryagin class of the orbibundle
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
. Since the RHS of (

,
G_{2}
-instantons are the absolute minima of the Yang–Mills functional. This property also characterizes ASD instantons in dimension four.

Definition 3.5
.

The moduli space of projective
G_{2}
-instantons over
P^{\dagger}
is defined to be

\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\left\{A\in{A}_{k}:F_{A}\wedge\psi=0\right\}/{G}_{k+1}.

Although we work with connections in
L^{2}_{k}
class, each equivalence class of
G_{2}
-instantons admit smooth representatives. So we choose to omit regularity in the notation of moduli spaces.

3.2.
Local Deformation

The local structure of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is best understood via the study of the ‘deformation complex’. Infinitesimally, the action of the gauge group
{G}_{k+1}
on
{A}_{k}
is given by

\left.{d\over dt}\right|_{t=0}e^{t\xi}\cdot A=-d_{A}\xi,\quad\xi\in L^{2}_{k+1}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})

Moreover, the linearization of the
G_{2}
-instanton operator
F_{A}\wedge\psi
is given by

\left.{d\over dt}\right|_{t=0}F_{A+t\alpha}\wedge\psi=d_{A}\alpha\wedge\psi,\quad\alpha\in L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P}).

This leads us to consider the following sequence
E_{A}(X)
of differential operators:

L^{2}_{k+1}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})\xrightarrow{-d_{A}}L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})\xrightarrow{d_{A}\wedge\psi}L^{2}_{k-1}(X,\Lambda^{6}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})\xrightarrow{d_{A}}L^{2}_{k-2}(X,\Lambda^{7}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P}).

We note that the sequence
E_{A}(X)
becomes a chain complex if and only if
A
is a
G_{2}
-instanton, in which case we refer to
E_{A}(X)
as the deformation complex of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
at
[A]
. This complex
E_{A}(X)
is an elliptic complex in the sense that the associated sequence given by the principal symbols of the differential operators are exact (c.f.
[
DS11
]
). So the cohomology of
E_{A}(X)
is finite dimensional.

The cohomology of
E_{A}(X)
has concrete geometric meanings similar to the deformation complex of
ASD
instantons in dimension four.
H^{0}(E_{A}(X))
is the Lie algebra of the isotropy group
\Stab(A)
of
A
. To better understand
\Stab(A)
, we consider the corresponding connection
A^{\dagger}
on
P^{\dagger}
. Let’s fix a non-singular basepoint
x_{0}\in X
, and identify the fiber of
G^{\prime}_{P}|_{x_{0}}
with
SU(r)
.
u\in\Stab(A)
means that
u
is
A^{\dagger}
-parallel, thus is determined by its restriction to
x_{0}
. Thus
\Stab(A)
is identified with a subgroup of
SU(r)
(actually it’s a Lie subgroup although not necessarily closed). Denote by
\Hol(A^{\dagger},x_{0})\subset U(r)
the holonomy group generated by automorphisms of
P^{\dagger}|_{x_{0}}
obtained via
A^{\dagger}
-parallel transports along orbi-loops based at
x_{0}
. Then
\Stab(A)
is identified with the centralizer of
\Hol(A^{\dagger},x_{0})
in
SU(r)
. We denote by
Z(r)
the center of
SU(r)
which consists of multiples of the identity matrix by
r
-th roots of unity.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
A^{\dagger}
be a unitary connection on
P^{\dagger}
that induces the connection
A
on
P
. Then the following are equivalent.

(a)

\Stab(A)=Z(r)
.

(b)

\dim H^{0}(E_{A}(X))=0
.

(c)

\Hol(A^{\dagger})
acts irreducibly on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
.

Proof.

(a)\Longrightarrow(b)
. This follows directly from the fact that
H^{0}(E_{A}(X))
is the Lie algebra of
\Stab(A)
.

(b)\Longrightarrow(c)
. Suppose the action of
\Hol(A^{\dagger})
splits into
\mathbb{C}^{r}
two invariant subspaces
\mathbb{C}^{r_{1}}
and
\mathbb{C}^{r_{2}}
. For each element
\zeta\in U(1)
, we define a map on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
by multiplying the first
r_{1}
factors by
\zeta^{r_{2}}
and the remaining
r_{2}
factors by
\zeta^{-r_{1}}
. Then each map defines an element in
SU(r)
that intertwines with the action of
\Hol(A^{\dagger})
. Such an assignment embeds a neighborhood of
1\in U(1)
in
SU(r)
. This implies that
\dim H^{0}(E_{A}(X))\geq 1
.

(c)\Longrightarrow(a)
. Since
\Hol(A^{\dagger})
acts irreducibly on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
, Schur’s Lemma tells us that each element in
\Stab(A)
must act on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
via a scalar multiplication. Since
\Stab(A)
lies in
SU(r)
and contains
Z(r)
, we conclude that
\Stab(A)=Z(r)
.
∎

Definition 3.7
.

A connection
A\in{A}_{k}
is said to be irreducible if its isotropy group
\Stab(A)=Z(r)
, and reducible otherwise. We denote the space of irreducible connections of class
L^{2}_{k}
by
{A}^{*}_{k}
.

Let
A
be a
G_{2}
-instanton. Since each element in
\Stab(A)
is
A
-parallel, one readily checks that
E_{A}(X)
is
\Stab(A)
-equivariant with respect to the adjoint action. The first cohomology
H^{1}(E_{A}(X))
represents the Zariski tangent space of
[A]
in
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
. The argument in
[
DK90
, Chapter 4]
can be applied to obtain a Kuranishi obstruction map
\mathfrak{o}_{A}:H^{1}(E_{A}(X))\rightarrow H^{2}(E_{A}(X))
so that a neighborhood of
[A]
in
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
can be identified with a neighborhood in
\mathfrak{o}_{A}^{-1}(0)/\Stab(A)
. Note that when
\Stab(A)=Z(r)
is the center, the action of
\Stab(A)
on
E_{A}(X)
is trivial. In particular, when
H^{0}(E_{A}(X))=H^{2}(E_{A}(X))=0
, a neighborhood of
[A]
is identified with a neighborhood of the origin in
H^{1}(E_{A}(X))
. This interpretation motivates the following definition.

Definition 3.8
.

A
G_{2}
-instanton
[A]\in\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is said to be regular if
H^{2}(E_{A}(X))=0
.

By taking adjoints on the even part of
E_{A}(X)
, one can wrap-up the information of
E_{A}(X)
into a single elliptic operator:

(3.5)

L_{A}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-d^{*}_{A}\\
-d_{A}&\star(d_{A}\wedge\psi)\end{pmatrix}:L^{2}_{k}(X,(\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L^{2}_{k-1}(X,(\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P}).

Note that
L_{A}
is self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product. So we conclude

0=\ind L_{A}=h^{1}(E_{A}(X))+h^{3}(E_{A}(X))-h^{0}(E_{A}(X))-h^{2}(E_{A}(X)),

where
h^{i}(E_{A}(X))=\dim H^{i}(E_{A}(X))
. From the discussion above, we conclude that a regular irreducible
G_{2}
-instanton is isolated in the moduli space.

3.3.
Reducible
G_{2}
-Instantons

We start with a line bundle
L
over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Denote by
\omega_{\phi}
the harmonic
2
-form representing
c_{1}(L)
with respect to the
G_{2}
-structure
\phi
. The
G_{2}
-representation
\Lambda^{2}=\Lambda^{2}_{7}\oplus\Lambda^{2}_{14}
decomposes the space of harmonic
2
-forms into
\mathcal{H}^{2}(X)=\mathcal{H}^{2}_{7}(X)\oplus\mathcal{H}^{2}_{14}(X)
since
\phi
is parallel with respect to
g_{\phi}
.
G_{2}
-instantons over
L
can be characterized by the following observation.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
L\rightarrow X
be a line bundle. Then a unitary connection
A
over
L
is a
G_{2}
-instanton if and only if
F_{A}=-2\pi i\omega_{\phi}
with
\omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{14}(X)
. Moreover, gauge equivalence classes of
G_{2}
-instatnons are parametrized by the Jacobian torus

J(X):=H^{1}_{\orb}(X;i\mathbb{R})/H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

Let
A
be a
G_{2}
-instanton over
L
. Then (
2.5
) tells us that

\star(F_{A}\wedge\phi)=-F_{A}\Longrightarrow\star F_{A}=-F_{A}\wedge\phi.

The Bianchi identity then implies that
F_{A}
is harmonic. Since
i/2\pi F_{A}
represents
c_{1}(L)
, we conclude that
F_{A}=-2\pi i\omega_{\phi}
. Since
\pi_{7}(F_{A})=0
, we see that
\omega_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{14}(X)
. The converse follows from the definition of
G_{2}
-instantons.

Given two
G_{2}
-instantons
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
over
L
, their difference
a=A_{1}-A_{0}\in\Omega^{1}(X;i\mathbb{R})
is closed since their curvature forms coincide with
-2\pi i\omega_{\phi}
. Any unitary gauge transformation in the identity component of the gauge group takes the form
u=e^{\xi}
with
\xi\in\Omega^{0}(X;i\mathbb{R})
. Since
e^{\xi}\cdot A=A-d\xi
, we see that, up to gauge transformations in the identity component,
G_{2}
-instantons are identified with
H^{1}(X;i\mathbb{R})
. Passing to the classifying space of the orbifold
X
, we see that the components of the unitary gauge group are identified with
H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})
. The result then follows by identifying the action of elements from different components of the gauge group with the action of
H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})
on
H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{R})
as in the case of manifolds.
∎

With the knowledge of reducible
G_{2}
-instantons over line bundles, we can obtain a necessary condition for the existence of reducible projective
G_{2}
-instantons over unitary bundles of higher rank, which is analogous to that of the ASD instanton case
[
Kro05
, Proposition 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be a principal
U(r)
-bundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Denote by
\omega^{\dagger}_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(X)
the harmonic representative of
c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger})
. Suppose
P^{\dagger}
admits a reducible projective
G_{2}
-instanton. Then one can find an integer
r^{\prime}\in(0,r)
and an integral harmonic class
\omega\in\mathcal{H}^{2}(X)
such that

\omega-\frac{r^{\prime}}{r}\omega^{\dagger}_{\phi}\in\mathcal{H}^{2}_{14}(X).

Proof.

The argument is completely analogous to that of
[
Kro05
, Proposition 2.3]
. Let
A^{\dagger}
be a reducible projective
G_{2}
-instanton over
P^{\dagger}
. Then
A^{\dagger}
reduces the structure group of
P^{\dagger}
to
U(r_{1})\times U(r_{2})
for some integers
r_{1}
,
r_{2}
satisfying
r_{1}+r_{2}=r
, i.e. one can find a sub-bundle
Q^{\dagger}\subset P^{\dagger}
of the form
Q^{\dagger}=P^{\dagger}_{1}\times_{X}P^{\dagger}_{2}
, where
P^{\dagger}_{i}
is a principal
U(r_{i})
-bundle over
X
. The connection
A^{\dagger}
decomposes accordingly as
A^{\dagger}_{1}\oplus A^{\dagger}_{2}
. Denote by
\theta_{i}=\det A^{\dagger}_{i}
the induced connection on the determinant line. Then the corresponding curvature is given by the trace
F_{\theta_{i}}=\tr F_{A^{\dagger}_{i}}.
We also denote
\theta=\det A^{\dagger}
. Since
A^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}_{1}\oplus A^{\dagger}_{2}
is a projective
G_{2}
-instanton, we conclude that

\left(F_{A^{\dagger}_{i}}-\frac{1}{r_{i}}\tr F_{A^{\dagger}_{i}}\otimes\id\right)\wedge\psi=0.

The same formula also holds for
A^{\dagger}
. From this formula, we conclude that

\pi_{7}\left(\tr(F_{A^{\dagger}})/r\right)=\pi_{7}\left(\tr(F_{A^{\dagger}_{1}})/r_{1}\right)=\pi_{7}\left(\tr(F_{A^{\dagger}_{2}})/r_{2}\right).

In particular, we get

\left(F_{\theta_{1}}-\frac{r_{1}}{r}F_{\theta}\right)\wedge\psi=0.

Now the result follows from the Chern–Weil homomorphism by taking
\omega
to be the harmonic representative of
c_{1}(P^{\dagger}_{1})
and
r^{\prime}=r_{1}
.
∎

Usually
subsection 3.3
is not very helpful with avoiding reducible
G_{2}
-instantons in contrast with the ASD instanton case. Because any torsion-free
G_{2}
-manifold
(M,\phi)
whose holonomy group coincides with
G_{2}
has
b_{1}(M)=0
. In this case, the necessary condition imposed by
subsection 3.3
automatically holds. However, when the holonomy group is a proper subgroup of
G_{2}
, one can indeed use
subsection 3.3
to avoid reducibles. Since we primarily work with flat connections, the reducible ones can be avoided by purely imposing topological assumptions on
P^{\dagger}
.

Definition 3.11
.

A principal
U(r)
-bundle
P^{\dagger}
over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
is called admissible if

(1)

(r-1)c_{1}^{2}(P^{\dagger})-2rc_{2}(P^{\dagger})=0\in H^{4}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})
.

(2)

There exists
\lambda\in H^{\orb}_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
such that
\langle c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger}),\lambda\rangle
is coprime to
r
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be an admissible principal
U(r)
-bundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Then
P^{\dagger}
does not admit any reducible projective
G_{2}
-instantons.

Proof.

Let
A^{\dagger}
be a projective
G_{2}
-instanton over
P^{\dagger}
, and
A
the corresponding connection over the adjoint
PU(r)
-bundle
P
. (
3.4
) tells us that
\|F_{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=4\pi^{2}/r\langle p_{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{P})\smile[\phi],[X]\rangle
. Since
p_{1}(\mathfrak{g}_{P})=(r-1)c_{1}^{2}(P^{\dagger})-2rc_{2}(P^{\dagger})
, we conclude that
F_{A}=0
. Thus
A^{\dagger}
is projectively flat.

Suppose
A^{\dagger}
decomposes as
A^{\dagger}=A^{\dagger}_{1}\oplus A^{\dagger}_{2}
. The argument of
subsection 3.3
implies that
F_{\theta_{1}}-r_{1}/rF_{\theta}=0
. In particular, the Chern–Weil homomorphism implies that
r_{1}/rc_{1}(\det P^{\dagger})
is an integral class. This contradicts to the fact that
\langle c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger}),\lambda\rangle
is coprime to
r
for any
\lambda\in H^{\orb}_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
since we can choose
\lambda\in H^{\orb}_{2}(X;\mathbb{Z})
so that the pairing between
\lambda
and
r_{1}/r\cdot c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger})
to be a non-zero integer.
∎

3.4.
Orientation in Simple Cases

The orientability issue of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
has been addressed by Donaldson–Segal
[
DS11
]
and Walpuski
[
Wal13
]
in the manifold case. Recently Joyce–Upmeier
[
JU21
]
established a canonical assignment for orientations of the moduli space with an extra input called ‘flag structures’. Rather than pursuing a full generalization to
G_{2}
-orbifolds, we will work out the orientability for simple
G_{2}
-orbifolds.

We write
\Ind L_{A}:=\ker L_{A}\otimes(\cok L_{A})^{*}
, and
\Ind\mathbb{L}\rightarrow{A}_{k}
for the vector bundle whose fiber at
A
is
\Ind L_{A}
. The gauge group
{G}_{k+1}
acts on
\Ind L_{A}
by extending the action
u\omega u^{-1}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
-valued forms
\omega
.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be a simple
G_{2}
-orbifold, and
P^{\dagger}\rightarrow X
a
U(r)
-bundle with either

or

even. Then
{G}_{k+1}
acts trivially on
\det\Ind\mathbb{L}
.

Note that
subsection 3.4
implies that
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is orientable. Because the tangent space
T_{[A]}\mathcal{M}
of a regular irreducible connection is identified with
H^{1}(E_{A}(X))
whose determinant is identified with
\det E_{A}(X)
. One can further identify

canonically using the metric on

-valued forms. Since

Corollary 3.14
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be a simple
G_{2}
-orbifold, and
P^{\dagger}\rightarrow X
a
U(r)
-bundle with either
r=2
or
c_{1}(P^{\dagger})
even. Suppose the irreducible moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is regular. Then
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is orientable.

The proof of
subsection 3.4
is a standard application of the Atiyah–Singer’s index theory. To set it up, we write
P_{u}
for the bundle over
S^{1}\times X
obtained by identifying the boundary of
[0,1]\times P
via the gauge transformation
u\in{G}_{k+1}
. Given
A\in{A}_{k}
, we choose a smooth path
(A_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
with
A_{0}=A
and
A_{1}=u\cdot A
. Then we get an elliptic operator on
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}
-valued forms of
S^{1}\times X
:

S_{u}(A):=\frac{d}{dt}+L_{A_{t}}:L^{2}_{k}(S^{1}\times X,(\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L^{2}_{k-1}(S^{1}\times X,(\Lambda^{0}\oplus\Lambda^{1})\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}).

The index can be related to the spectral flow
\ind S_{u}(A)=\Sf(L_{A_{t}})
, which is independent of the choice of the paths
(A_{t})
from the standard theory of Atiyah–Patodi–Singer
[
APS76
]
. Moreover, the weight of the
u
-action on
\det\Ind\mathbb{L}
is given by
(-1)^{\Sf(L_{A_{t}})}
. Thus the proof of
subsection 3.4
is reduced to the following statement.

Lemma 3.15
.

Under the assumption of
subsection 3.4
, we know
\ind S_{u}(A)
is even for all
A\in{A}_{k}
and
u\in{G}_{k+1}
.

Proof.

The
G_{2}
-structure
\phi
determines a spin structure
\mathfrak{s}
on
S^{1}\times X
. As explained in
[
Wal13
]
, the principal symbol of
S_{u}(A)
is dual to that of the Dirac operator
D_{\mathbb{A}}
on
\mathfrak{s}
twisted by
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}
, where
\mathbb{A}=d/dt+A_{t}
is the induced connection on
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}
. To compute the index of
D_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})
, we make use of the orbifold generalization of Atiyah–Singer’s index theorem in
[
Kaw81
]
.

Let’s write
Y=S^{1}\times X
temporarily. For each
y\in Y
, the isotropy group
G_{y}=\mathbb{Z}/2
or
V_{4}
, where
V_{4}
is the Klein four-group. Explicitly we write
\mathbb{Z}/2=\{1,-1\}
and
V_{4}=\{1,a,b,ab\}
with
a=(-1,1)
and
b=(1,-1)
. We consider the modified singular sets:

\Sigma_{I}:=\{(y,-1)\in Y\times\mathbb{Z}/2:G_{y}=\mathbb{Z}/2\},\quad\Sigma_{V}:=\{(y,g)\in Y\times V_{4}:G_{y}=V_{4},\;\;g\neq 1\}.

These singular sets patch together to an ineffective orbifold
\Sigma_{Y}:=\Sigma_{I}\sqcup_{\sim}\Sigma_{V}
, where
(y,-1)\sim(y,g)
if and only if there is an inclusion of orbifold charts
(\tilde{U}_{y},G_{y})\hookrightarrow(\tilde{U}_{y^{\prime}},G_{y^{\prime}})
that sends
-1\in G_{y}
to
g\in G_{y^{\prime}}
. To see the orbifold structure on
\Sigma_{Y}
, we write
\tilde{U}^{g}_{y}
for the
g
-invariant subset of
\tilde{U}_{y}
. Then the orbifold atlas over
\Sigma_{Y}
is given by
\{(\tilde{U}^{g}_{y},G_{y}):y\in Y,g\neq 1\in G_{y}\}
. Note that
\tilde{U}^{g}_{y}
is an open ball of dimension either
2
or
4
when
g
is non-trivial. Thus we can write
\Sigma_{Y}=\Sigma_{Y}^{(2)}\cup\Sigma_{Y}^{(4)}
into the disjoint of
2
- and
4
-dimensional components. We also need to consider the normal bundle
N(\Sigma_{Y})
of
\Sigma_{Y}
locally given by
(\tilde{U}_{y}/\tilde{U}^{g}_{y},G_{y})\rightarrow(\tilde{U}^{g}_{y},G_{y})
. In particular,
g
acts on the fiber
N(\Sigma_{Y})|_{(y,g)}
by
-1
-multiplication.

With the above set-up, the orbifold version of Atiyah–Singer’s index theorem
[
Kaw81
]
tells us that

\begin{split}\ind D_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})&=\int_{Y}\ch(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})\hat{A}(Y)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y}}\ch(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})\hat{A}_{\pi}(N(\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y}))\hat{A}(\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y})\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma^{(2)}_{Y}}\ch(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})\hat{A}_{\pi}(N(\Sigma^{(2)}_{Y}))\hat{A}(\Sigma^{(2)}_{Y})\\
&=:I_{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})+I_{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})+I_{3}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}}),\end{split}

where
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}}=\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}\otimes\mathbb{C}
, and
\hat{A}_{\pi}
is the twisted
\hat{A}
-class in
[
LM89
, Page 267]
.

The Chern character of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}}
can be computed as follows. The differential of the adjoint homomorphism gives us an isomorphism
\ad:\mathfrak{su}(r)\rightarrow\mathfrak{pu}(r)
. Thus we can identify
\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}}=\mathfrak{su}(P^{\dagger}_{u})
. Furthermore,
\End(P^{\dagger}_{u})=\mathfrak{su}(P^{\dagger}_{u})\otimes\mathbb{C}\oplus\underline{\mathbb{C}}
. After throwing away the terms of degree greater than
8
, we can compute

\begin{split}\ch(P^{\dagger}_{u})&=r+s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}s_{2}+\frac{1}{6}s_{3}+\frac{1}{24}s_{4}\\
&=r+c_{1}+\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}^{2}-2c_{2})+\frac{1}{6}(c_{1}^{3}-3c_{2}c_{1}+3c_{3})\\
&+\frac{1}{24}(c_{1}^{4}-4c_{2}c_{1}^{2}+4c_{3}c_{1}+2c_{2}^{2}-4c_{4}),\end{split}

where
s_{i}
is the polynomial that expresses the ith power sum into elementary symmetric polynomials whose inputs are the Chern classes
c_{i}
of
P^{\dagger}_{u}
. Thus the Chern character of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}}
is given by

\begin{split}\ch(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})&=\ch(\End(P^{\dagger}_{u}))-1=\ch(P^{\dagger}_{u})\ch(P^{\dagger,*}_{u})-1\\
&=\left(r+s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}s_{2}+\frac{1}{6}s_{3}+\frac{1}{24}s_{4}\right)\left(r-s_{1}+\frac{1}{2}s_{2}-\frac{1}{6}s_{3}+\frac{1}{24}s_{4}\right)-1\\
&=r^{2}-1+rs_{2}-s^{2}_{1}+\frac{rs_{4}}{12}-\frac{s_{1}s_{3}}{3}+\frac{s^{2}_{2}}{4}\\
&=r^{2}-1+(r-1)c_{1}^{2}-2rc_{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{12}\left((r-1)c_{1}^{4}-4rc_{2}c_{1}^{2}+(4r-12)c_{3}c_{1}+(2r+12)c^{2}_{2}-4rc_{4}\right).\end{split}

Note that
Y=S^{1}\times X
is a product with circle. So up to the eighth degree, the
\hat{A}
-class is given by
\hat{A}=1-p_{1}/24
, where
p_{1}:=p_{1}(Y)
. Note that the normal bundle
N(\Sigma_{Y})
is pulled back from the normal bundle of the correspondingly constructed singular sets
\Sigma_{X}
whose components have dimension either
1
or
3
. Up to the third degree,
\hat{A}_{\pi}=\pm 1
, where the sign can be worked out following
[
AB68
]
.

Since
\det u=\id
, we know that

c_{1}(P^{\dagger}_{u})=c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger}_{u})=\pi^{*}c_{1}(\det P^{\dagger})=\pi^{*}c_{1}(P^{\dagger}),

where
\pi:S^{1}\times X\rightarrow X
is the projection. Thus by dimension counting, we know

\int_{Y}c_{1}^{4}=0\qquad\int_{Y}c_{1}^{2}\cdot p_{1}=0\qquad\int_{\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y}}c_{1}^{2}=0\qquad\int_{\Sigma^{(2)}_{Y}}c_{1}=0.

Now we can compute

\begin{split}&I_{1}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})=\frac{1}{12}\int_{Y}rc_{2}\cdot p_{1}+\frac{1}{6}\int_{Y}-2rc_{2}c_{1}^{2}+(2r-6)c_{3}c_{1}+(r+6)c^{2}_{2}-2rc_{4}\\
&I_{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})+I_{3}(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}_{P_{u}})=\pm\int_{\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y}}rc_{2}.\end{split}

We can twist the spin bundle of
Y
by
P^{\dagger}
, and get corresponding terms:

\begin{split}&I_{1}(P^{\dagger})=\frac{1}{24}\int_{Y}c_{2}\cdot p_{1}+\frac{1}{12}\int_{Y}-2c_{2}c_{1}^{2}+2c_{3}c_{1}+c^{2}_{2}-2c_{4}\\
&I_{2}(P^{\dagger})+I_{3}(P^{\dagger})=\pm\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma^{(4)}_{Y}}c_{2}.\end{split}

The above computation tells us that

\ind D_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})-(12+2r)\ind D_{\mathbb{A}}(P^{\dagger})\equiv-\frac{1}{2}\int_{Y}c_{2}\cdot p_{1}-3\int_{Y}c_{3}\cdot c_{1}\;\mod 2.

Since
Y
is spin, we know
p_{1}(Y)
is divisible by four. Thus we conclude that

\ind D_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})\equiv\int_{Y}c_{3}(P^{\dagger}_{u})\cdot\pi^{*}c_{1}(P^{\dagger})\;\mod 2

So when
\rk(P^{\dagger})=2
or
c_{1}(P^{\dagger})
is even, we know that
\ind D_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathfrak{g}_{P_{u}})
is even.
∎

Remark 3.16
.

The last product term
c_{3}c_{1}
also appears in the manifold case if one considers
U(r)
-bundles. Joyce–Upmeier
[
JU21
, Theorem 1.2]
proved the orientability for
U(r)
-bundles with the help of the embedding
U(r)\hookrightarrow SU(r+1)
. Thus
c_{3}c_{1}
has to be even at least in the manifold case, although we don’t know a good reason for this.

4.
Perturbations and Transversality

Holonomy perturbations have been constructed for achieving transversality of ASD connections in dimension 4
[
Don87
]
and flat connections in dimension 3
[
Flo88
]
. In this section, we argue that the transversality of the irreducible stratum of
G_{2}
-instantons can also be achieved via holonomy perturbations.

4.1.
Holonomy Perturbations

Let
P^{\dagger}
be a
U(r)
-bundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Suppose we have an
l
-tuple
\boldsymbol{\rho}=(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{l})
of smooth immersions
\rho_{i}:S^{1}\times D^{6}\rightarrow X
satisfying

(4.1)

\rho_{i}(s,x)=\rho_{j}(s,x),\quad\text{ for all }s\in[-\epsilon,\epsilon],\;x\in D^{6}

where
s
is the parameter of
S^{1}
representing
e^{i2\pi s}
, and
\epsilon>0
is a small constant. Denote by
\rho_{x}:S^{1}\rightarrow X
the orbi-loop given by
\rho(-,x)
based at
\rho(0,x)
. Let
\eta:U(r)^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function that is invariant under the diagonally
SU(2)
-adjoint action, and
\nu\in\Omega^{2}(D^{6})
be a non-negative
2
-form supported near the center of
D^{6}
so that
\int_{D^{6}}\nu=1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Given
(\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta,\nu)
as above, we define

\begin{split}\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}:{A}_{k}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\\
A&\longmapsto\int_{D^{6}}\eta\left(\Hol_{\rho_{1,x}}(A^{\dagger}),...,\Hol_{\rho_{l,x}}(A^{\dagger})\right)\nu\end{split}

to be the associated cylinder function over
{A}_{k}
.

Now we derive the formal gradient of the function
\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}
. Denote the
i
-the partial derivative of the function
\eta
by
\partial_{i}\eta:U(r)^{l}\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}(r)^{*}
, which arises as the restriction of
d\eta
to the
i
-th factor
T^{*}U(r)\simeq\mathfrak{u}^{*}(r)
of
T^{*}U(r)^{l}
. Due to the requirement that
\rho_{i}(0,x)=\rho_{j}(0,x)
, there is no ambiguity in writing
\Hol_{\boldsymbol{\rho},x}(A^{\dagger}):=\left(\Hol_{\rho_{1,x}}(A^{\dagger}),...,\Hol_{\rho_{l,x}}(A^{\dagger})\right)
. Then the differential of the cylinder function
\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}
takes the form

(4.2)

d\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}|_{A}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\int_{D^{6}}\partial_{i}\eta|_{\Hol_{\boldsymbol{\rho},x}(A^{\dagger})}\left(-\int_{0}^{1}(\rho^{*}_{i}a)(s)ds\cdot\Hol_{\rho_{i},x}(A^{\dagger})\right)\nu,\quad a\in\Omega^{1}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P}).

Note that
\mathfrak{u}(r)=\mathfrak{su}(r)\oplus i\mathbb{R}
. We can compose
\partial_{i}\eta
with the restriction of
\mathfrak{u}(r)^{*}
to
\mathfrak{su}(r)^{*}
and identify
\mathfrak{su}(r)^{*}
with
\mathfrak{su}(r)
via the Killing form. The resulting operator is denoted by
(\partial_{i}\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}:U(r)^{l}\rightarrow\mathfrak{su}(r)
. The conjugation invariance of
\eta
implies that
(\partial_{i}\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}(\Hol_{\boldsymbol{\rho}}(A^{\dagger}))
is a section of
\rho_{i}^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{P}
over
\{0\}\times D^{6}
. This section can be extended to a section over
S^{1}\times D^{6}
via parallel transport of
A^{\dagger}
along the
S^{1}
-factor, which might gain discontinuity over
\{0\}\times D^{6}
with jumps given by
\Hol_{\rho_{i},x}(A^{\dagger})
-conjugation. Let’s temporarily denote by
H_{i}
this extended section of
\rho_{i}^{*}\mathfrak{g}_{P}
over
S^{1}\times D^{6}
(following the notation of
[
KM11
, Page 876]
). Then it follows from (
4.2
) that the gradient of
\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}
is

(4.3)

\grad\tau_{\boldsymbol{\rho},\eta}=\star_{\phi}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l}(\rho_{i})_{*}(H_{i}\otimes\nu)\right).

Although each single term
(\rho_{i})_{*}(H_{i}\otimes\nu)
might be discontinuous at
\{0\}\times D^{6}
, the diagonal conjugation invariance of
\eta
implies that the sum on the RHS of (
4.3
) is at least continuous. We can actually get stronger estimates on the gradient of cylinder functions.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\tau
be a cylinder function as above, and
a=A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0}\in L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2))
be the connection form of a general connection
A^{\dagger}\in{A}_{k}
. Then we have the following estimates.

(1)

There exists a constant
K_{0}
so that

\|\grad\tau|_{A^{\dagger}}\|_{C^{0}}\leq K_{0}.

(2)

For each
1\leq j\leq k
, there exists a constant
K_{j}
so that

\|\grad\tau|_{A^{\dagger}}\|_{L^{2}_{j}}\leq K_{j}\left(1+\|a\|^{j}_{L^{2}_{j}}\right).

(3)

For each positive integer
n\geq 1
and
0\leq j\leq k
, there exists a constant
K_{n,j}
so that

\|D^{n}\grad\tau|_{A^{\dagger}}(a_{1},...,a_{n})\|_{L^{2}_{j}}\leq K_{n,j}\left(1+\|a\|^{j}_{L^{2}_{j}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\|a_{i}\|_{L^{2}_{j}}.

All constants appeared above are independent of the connection
A^{\dagger}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider the case when there is a single smooth immersion
\rho:S^{1}\times D^{6}\rightarrow X
. The argument for general cases is almost identical except for extra complexity in notations. Now we assume the cylinder function takes the form
\tau(A)=\int_{D^{6}}\eta(\Hol_{\rho,x}(A^{\dagger}))\nu
so that

\grad\tau=\star_{\phi}\rho_{*}(H\otimes\nu).

Let’s write
A^{c}=\rho^{*}A^{\dagger}
and
A^{c}_{0}=\rho^{*}A^{\dagger}_{0}
for the pulled-back connections over
S^{1}\times D^{6}
, and
a^{c}=A^{c}-A^{c}_{0}
the corresponding connection form. We can further decompose
a^{c}=b(s)+\beta(s)ds
, where
b(s)
is an
S^{1}
-family of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued
1
-forms over
D^{6}
, and
\beta(s)
is an
S^{1}
-family of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued
0
-forms over
D^{6}
.

(1) Since the Lie group
U(r)
is compact, we know
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}(\Hol_{\rho}(A^{\dagger}))
is bounded in
C^{0}
by a constant that is independent of
A^{\dagger}
. Note that the section
H
is obtained from
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}(\Hol_{\rho}(A^{\dagger}))
by parallel transporting along the
S^{1}
-direction of
S^{1}\times D^{6}
. Since the space of
U(r)
-connections over
S^{1}
is compact modulo gauge transformations, the gauge-invariant section
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}(\Hol_{\rho}(A^{\dagger}))
gives rise a uniform
C^{0}
-bound on
H
.

(2) The constants in the statement will all depend on the reference connection
A^{\dagger}_{0}
. So we may assume
\rho^{*}A^{\dagger}_{0}
is the trivial connection over
S^{1}\times D^{6}
to simplify notations. Then we know
\Hol_{\rho}(A^{\dagger})=\exp(-\int^{1}_{0}\beta(s)ds)
. From the construction of
H
and gauge-invariance of
\eta
, we know

(4.4)

\begin{split}H|_{(s,x)}&=\exp(-\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt)\cdot(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}(\Hol_{\rho,x}(A^{\dagger}))\cdot\exp(\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt)\\
&=(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}\left(\exp(-\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt)\cdot\exp(-\int^{1}_{0}\beta(s)ds)\cdot\exp(\int^{1}_{0}\beta(s)ds)\right).\end{split}

So the derivatives of
H
along the
S^{1}
-direction involves the composition of derivatives of
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}
with products among the terms

(4.5)

\partial^{i}_{s}\beta(s),\quad\exp(-\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt),\quad\exp(\int^{1}_{0}\beta(s)ds),

where
\partial^{i}_{s}
means to take the
i
-th derivative along the
S^{1}
-direction.
The derivatives of
H
along the
D^{6}
-direction involves the composition of derivatives of
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}
with products among the terms

(4.6)

\int_{0}^{s}\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)dt,\quad\int_{0}^{1}\nabla^{k}_{x}\beta(t)dt,\quad\exp(-\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt),\quad\exp(\int^{1}_{0}\beta(s)ds),

where
\nabla^{i}_{x}
means to take the
i
-th derivative along the
D^{6}
-direction. Recall that
a^{c}=\rho^{*}A^{\dagger}-\rho^{*}A^{\dagger}_{0}
is the connection form on
S^{1}\times D^{6}
. It’s clear that

\|\partial^{i}_{s}\beta(s)\|_{L^{2}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}\leq const.\|a^{c}\|_{L^{2}_{i}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}

As for the
L^{2}
-norm of
\int^{s}_{0}\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)dt
, we first apply integration by parts to get the following estimates:

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{1}\left|\int^{s}_{0}\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)dt\right|^{2}ds&\leq\int_{0}^{1}s\int^{s}_{0}|\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)|^{2}dtds\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(\int^{1}_{0}|\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)|^{2}dt-\int^{1}_{0}s|\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(s)|^{2}ds\right)\\
&\leq\int^{1}_{0}|\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)|^{2}dt.\end{split}

Thus we conclude that

(4.8)

\left\|\int^{s}_{0}\nabla^{i}_{x}\beta(t)dt\right\|_{L^{2}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}\leq\|\nabla^{u}_{x}\beta(s)\|_{L^{2}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}\leq const.\|a^{c}\|_{L^{2}_{i}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}.

Since the
j
-th derivative of
H
is given by the composition of derivatives of
(d\eta)^{0}_{\dagger}
with products of terms in (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) so that in each product the total order of derivatives add up to
j
, we conclude that

\|H\|_{L^{2}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}\leq const.\left(1+\|a^{c}\|^{j}_{L^{2}_{j}(S^{1}\times D^{6})}\right),

which further implies the claimed estimate

\|\star_{\phi}\rho_{*}(H\otimes\nu)\|_{L^{2}_{j}}\leq const.\left(1+\|a\|^{j}_{L^{2}_{j}}\right).

(3) Once we know the expression of
H
in (
4.4
), the proof is almost the same as that of
[
Ma20
, Proposition 3.2]
. The only difference comes from the adjoint action of
\exp(\int^{s}_{0}\beta(t)dt)
, whereas in
[
Ma20
, Proposition 3.2]
one does not need to apply further parallel transport. This extra complication can dealt with as above using the estimate (
4.7
).
∎

tells us that the gradient of a cylinder function defines a map

\grad\tau:{A}_{k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L^{2}_{k}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P}).

Furthermore, this map is smooth due to (3) of
subsection 4.1

Fix a countable family of cylinder functions

(1)

For each integer

that are dense in the space of all

-topology.

(2)

For each integer

-invariant functions
\eta_{\alpha}:U(r)^{l}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
associated to
\tau_{\alpha}
that are dense in the space of all smooth

with respect to
C^{\infty}
-topology.

Then we fix
C_{\alpha}:=\sup\{K_{i,\alpha}:0\leq i\leq\alpha\}
, wehre

is the constant appeared in

\tau_{\alpha}
. We define

{P}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\pi}=\{\pi_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}}:\pi_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R},\;\;\sum_{\alpha}C_{\alpha}|\pi_{\alpha}|<\infty\right\}

to be the space of sequences
\boldsymbol{\pi}=\{\pi_{\alpha}\}
of finite norm
\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|:=\sum_{\alpha}C_{\alpha}|\pi_{\alpha}|
. We refer to
{P}
as the space of holonomy perturbations.

Due to the construction of
{P}
, the sum
\tau_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}:=\sum_{\alpha}\pi_{\alpha}\tau_{\alpha}
defines a smooth function on
{A}_{k}
, which we use to perturb the Chern–Simons functional
\cs_{\phi}
:

\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}:=\cs_{\phi}+\tau_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}:{A}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.

Then we define the perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton equation to be the vanishing equation of the gradient of the perturbed functional:

(4.9)

\grad\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0\Longleftrightarrow F_{A}\wedge\psi+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0,

where
\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=\sum_{\alpha}\pi_{\alpha}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{l_{\alpha}}(\rho_{\alpha,i})_{*}(H_{\alpha,i}\otimes\nu)\right)
with
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\alpha}=(\rho_{\alpha,1},...,\rho_{\alpha,l_{\alpha}})
.

4.2.
Transversality

In this subsection, we establish the transversality of the moduli space of perturbed irreducible
G_{2}
-instantons. Following the notations in
[
KM07
]
, we write
\mathcal{T}_{j}
for the tangent bundle of
{A}_{k}
completed with respect to the
L^{2}_{j}
-norm for
j\leq k
, which is identified with
{A}_{k}\times L^{2}_{j}(X,\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})
. The infinitesimal action of the gauge group
{G}_{k+1}
decomposes the tangent bundle into two pieces:

(4.10)

\mathcal{T}_{j}:=\mathcal{J}_{j}\oplus\mathcal{K}_{j},

where
\mathcal{J}_{j}|_{A}=\im d_{A}
is the
L^{2}_{j}
-tangent space of the gauge orbit at
A
, and
\mathcal{K}_{j}|_{A}=\ker d^{*}_{A}
is the
L^{2}
-orthogonal complement. The local gauge invariant of
\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
implies that its gradient
\grad\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is a section of
\mathcal{K}_{k-1}
. One can also argue directly by writing
\grad\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=\star_{\phi}(F_{A}\wedge\psi+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A))
and compute that
d_{A}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0
.

We consider the smooth map

(4.11)

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}:{P}\times{A}^{*}_{k}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{k-1}\\
(\boldsymbol{\pi},A)&\longmapsto\grad\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)\end{split}

The essence of the transversality problem is to show that the map
\mathcal{F}
is transverse to the zero section of
\mathcal{K}_{k-1}
. To do so, we introduce the Hessian

(4.12)

\begin{split}\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}:\mathcal{K}_{k}&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{k-1}\\
a&\longmapsto\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}(D\grad\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}|_{A}a),\end{split}

where
\Pi_{\mathcal{K}}:\mathcal{T}_{k-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{k-1}
is the fiberwise
L^{2}
-orthogonal projection. When
A
is a critical point of
\cs_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
, one sees that
\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is a self-adjoint Fredholm map from the argument in
[
KM07
, Proposition 12.3.1]
.

Lemma 4.4
.

The map
\mathcal{F}
defined in (
4.11
) is transverse to the zero section of
\mathcal{K}_{k-1}
.

Proof.

Let
(\boldsymbol{\pi},A)\in\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0)
be an element in the zero set. The derivative of
\mathcal{F}
at
(\boldsymbol{\pi},A)
is

(4.13)

D\mathcal{F}|_{(\boldsymbol{\pi},A)}(\boldsymbol{o},a)=\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}a+\star_{\phi}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A),

where
(\boldsymbol{o},a)\in{P}\times\mathcal{K}_{k}
. Since
\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
is self-adjoint with respect to the
L^{2}
-inner product, we can identify the cokernel of
\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
with
\ker\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
. Then it suffices to show that for each non-zero
a\in\ker\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
, one can find
\boldsymbol{o}\in{P}
such that
\langle a,\star_{\phi}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A)\rangle_{L^{2}}\neq 0
.

Note that
\star_{\phi}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A)=\grad\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}
. We see that
\langle a,\star_{\phi}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A)\rangle_{L^{2}}=D\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}|_{A}(a)
. Suppose, on the contrary, there is an element
a\in\ker\Hess_{A,\boldsymbol{\pi}}
such that
D\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}|_{A}(a)=0
for all
\boldsymbol{o}\in{P}
. Then for
A_{\epsilon}=A+\epsilon a
with
\epsilon>0
sufficiently small, we have

\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A)=\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A_{\epsilon})\text{ and }[A]\neq[A_{\epsilon}]\in{B}^{*}_{k}={A}^{*}_{k}/{G}_{k+1}.

However, this is impossible for the following reason. Recall each term in
\tau_{\boldsymbol{o}}(A)
is of the form
\int_{D^{6}}\eta_{\alpha}(\Hol_{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{\alpha}}(A^{\dagger}))\nu
. Since the functions
\eta_{\alpha}
’s are chosen to be dense in the space of all
SU(2)
-invariant functions as in (2) of
subsection 4.1
, we know for all words
W
of a fixed length sufficiently large, say
L
, one has

\tr\left(W\left(\prod_{i=1}^{L}\Hol_{\rho_{i}}(A^{\dagger})\right)\right)=\tr\left(W\left(\prod_{i=1}^{L}\Hol_{\rho_{i}}(A_{\epsilon}^{\dagger})\right)\right)

for all
L
-tuple immersions
\boldsymbol{\rho}=(\rho_{1},...,\rho_{L})
. Then
[
Don02
, Lemma 5.13]
provides us with
g\in U(r)
such that
g\Hol_{\rho_{i}}(A^{\dagger})g^{-1}=\Hol_{\rho_{i}}(A_{\epsilon}^{\dagger})
. Since
A^{\dagger}
and
A^{\dagger}_{\epsilon}
has the same determinant, we know
g\in SU(r)
. Recall (1) in
subsection 4.1
that the immersions
\rho_{i}
are dense in the space of smooth immersions. Since both
A
and
A_{\epsilon}
are irreducible, we conclude that
A
and
A_{\epsilon}
are gauge equivalent, which is a contradiction.
∎

subsection 4.2
has the following immediate consequence using the Sard–Smale theorem and the standard transversality argument in differential topology (c.f.
[
KM07
, Lemma 12.5.1]
).

Corollary 4.5
.

With respect to a generic perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
, every irreducible
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton is regular in the sense of
subsection 3.2
.

5.
Compactness of Moduli Spaces

Due to the work of Uhlenbeck
[
Uhl82
]
, the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons is known to be non-compact for general bundles. In contrast to ASD connections in dimension
4
, a compactification of such moduli spaces is still far from commencement due to the wild behavior of the bubbling off subsets. Nevertheless, we are able to obtain the compactness of the moduli space of
G_{2}
-instantons over projectively flat bundles.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be a projectively flat principal
U(n)
-bundle over a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. There exists a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
of perturbed
G_{2}
-instantons is compact with respect to all
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
satisfying
\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|<\epsilon_{0}
.

Since later in the proof we will use letter ‘
r
’ for the radius of small balls, we choose to write
U(n)
-bundles rather than
U(r)
-bundles in this section to avoid confusion. The proof of
Theorem 5.1
is more or less standard in higher dimensional gauge theory (c.f.
[
Nak88
]
). The argument adopted here was communicated to the author by Simon Donaldson in the case of Hermitian–Yang–Mills connections. Some parts of the proof are also sketched in
[
Don22
]
. We start by recalling the gauge fixing result in
[
Uhl82
]
.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
Uhl82
, Theorem 1.3]
)
.

Let
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
be the unit ball and
p>1
an exponent. There exist constants
\epsilon_{1}>0
,
\mathfrak{c}_{1}>0
such that each unitary connection
A
on the trivial
SU(n)
-bundle satisfying
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\epsilon_{1}
is gauge equivalent to a connection
A^{\prime}=d+a^{\prime}
satisfying

(1)\;d^{*}a^{\prime}=0\qquad(2)\;\star a^{\prime}|_{\partial\mathbb{B}}=0\qquad(3)\;\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{1}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{1}\|F_{A}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{B})}

Recall
P
is the induced
PU(r)
-bundle on which we have fixed a reference connection
A_{0}
. Let
A
be a perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton which satisfies

F_{A}\wedge\psi+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0

for some
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
. We thus get

\pi_{7}(F_{A})=-\star(\psi\wedge\star\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)).

From (
2.7
) and the admissibility of
P
, we know that
\|F_{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=3\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.
With the
C^{0}
bound on the perturbation, we get a priori bounds on
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}}
and
\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{C^{0}}
. In order to apply Uhlenbeck’s result
Theorem 5.2
, we need to improve these bounds to the
L^{7/2}
norm. To do this, we work locally over balls in
X
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{R}^{7}
be the unit ball equipped with the standard
G_{2}
-structure
\phi_{0}
,
B^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{B}
an interior ball, and
q\in(0,7)
. There are constants
\epsilon_{2}>0
,
\mathfrak{c}_{2}>0
such that for each connection
A=d+a
on the trivial
SU(r)
-bundle satisfying

(1)\;d^{*}a=0\qquad(2)\;\star a|_{\partial\mathbb{B}}=0\qquad(3)\;\|a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\epsilon_{2}

one has

\|a\|_{L^{q}_{1}(B^{\prime})}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{2}\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B})}\right).

Proof.

Let’s consider the operator
\delta:=d^{*}\oplus\pi_{7}\circ d:\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{B},\mathfrak{su}(r))\rightarrow\Omega^{0}(\mathbb{B},\mathfrak{su}(r))\oplus\Omega^{2}(\mathbb{B},\mathfrak{su}(r))
. The symbol of this operator is given by
a\mapstochar\rightarrow(\iota_{\xi}a,\pi_{7}(\xi\wedge a))
when evaluating over a non-zero covector
\xi\in T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{7}
. Using the equivalence between
\pi_{7}(a\wedge\xi)=0
and
\xi\wedge a\wedge\star\phi_{0}=0
, one readily checks that the symbol evaluating on
\xi
is injective when
\xi
is non-zero. The elliptic regularity estimate tells us

\|a\|_{L^{p}_{1}}\leq const.\left(\|\delta a\|_{L^{p}}+\|a\|_{L^{1}}\right)

for any
a\in\Omega^{1}(\mathbb{B},\mathfrak{su}(r))
compactly supported in
\mathbb{B}
. Since the
3
-form
\phi_{0}
is exact on
\mathbb{B}
, Stokes’ theorem tells us

2\|\pi_{7}(da)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\|\pi_{14}(da)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-2n\int_{\mathbb{B}}\tr(da\wedge da)\wedge\phi_{0}=0,

from which we conclude that
\|da\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=3\|\pi_{7}(da)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
. In particular, we see that every compactly supported
1
-form in
\ker\delta
has to vanish identically. So the
L^{1}
-term in the RHS of the elliptic estimate above can be dropped.

Now let
A=d+a
be a connection satisfying the assumption, and
\beta:\mathbb{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a cut-off function with
\beta|_{B^{\prime}}\equiv 1
. Then

(5.1)

|\delta(\beta a)|\leq|d\beta||a|+|\beta a\wedge\beta a|+|\pi_{7}(F_{A})|.

Let’s first assume
q\in(0,14/5]
. Then we have the Sobolev embedding
L^{2}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{q}
. Combining with Uhlenbeck’s result
Theorem 5.2
, we get

\|a\|_{L^{q}}\leq const.\|a\|_{L^{2}_{1}}\leq const.\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}}

when
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}}
is sufficiently small. With the elliptic estimate of
\delta
, we get

(5.2)

\|\beta a\|_{L^{q}_{1}}\leq const.\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{q}}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{q}}\right).

The Hölder’s inequality tells us
\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{q}}\leq\|\beta a\|_{L^{mq}}\|\beta a\|_{L^{nq}}
for each positive pair
(m,n)
satisfying
1/m+1/n=1
. Let
m=7/q
and
n=7/(7-q)
. We have the Sobolev embeddings
L^{7/2}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{mq}
and
L^{q}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{nq}
, from which it follows that

\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{q}}\leq const.\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}}\|\beta a\|_{L^{q}_{1}}.

When
\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}}
is sufficiently small, we can rearrange the quadratic term to the LHS of (
5.2
) to conclude that

\|a\|_{L^{q}_{1}(B^{\prime})}\leq const.\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{B})}\right).

Now for
q\in(14/5,7)
, we have the Sobolev embedding
L^{14/5}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{q}
. So (
5.2
) continues to hold with the help of the estimate for
\|\beta a\|_{L^{14/5}_{1}}
. The same argument then applies to complete the proof.
∎

Note that the energy
\mathcal{YM}(A)
is not conformal invariant. As a compensation, we can consider the normalized energy over balls

(5.3)

\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(x)):=r^{-3}\int_{B_{r}(x)}|F_{A}|^{2},

where
B_{r}(x)
is the ball of radius
r
centered at
x\in X
. It is clear the normalized energy is conformally invariant. The normalized energy enjoys the following monotonicity property, which gives a control on the normalized energy when restricted on smaller balls.

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\phi_{0})
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the standard
G_{2}
-structure. There exists a constant
\mathfrak{c}_{3}>0
such that each connection
A
on the trivial
SU(n)
-bundle satisfies

\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(0))\leq\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,\mathbb{B})+\mathfrak{c}_{3}\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}.

Proof.

Let’s write
B_{r}
for the ball centered at the origin of radius
r
. The derivative of the normalized energy along the radial direction can be computes as

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(0))=-3r^{-4}\int_{B_{r}}|F_{A}|^{2}+r^{-3}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|F_{A}|^{2}.

Appealing to the relation
-\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\phi_{0}=2|\pi_{7}(F_{A})|^{2}-|\pi_{14}(F_{A})|^{2}
, we find that

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(0))=-9r^{-4}\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r})}+3r^{-4}\int_{B_{r}}-2n\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\phi_{0}+r^{-3}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|F_{A}|^{2}.

Let
\nu=1/3r\partial_{r}
be the radial vector field on
\mathbb{B}
. Since
G_{2}
acts on the spheres
\partial B_{r}
transitively and preserves
\phi_{0}
, one can verify the relation
d\iota_{\nu}\phi_{0}=\phi_{0}
directly over points along one axis. Writing
\iota_{\nu}\phi_{0}=\omega_{0}
, we have

\int_{B_{r}}\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\phi_{0}=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\omega_{0}.

Given any
2
-form
\alpha
, there is a relation

(5.4)

3\alpha\wedge\alpha\wedge\omega_{0}\leq|\alpha|^{2}r\vol_{\partial B_{r}}

on the
r
-sphere
\partial B_{r}
. To verify this relation, we use the transitivity of the
G_{2}
-action on the
r
-sphere again, which means it suffices to work at the point
\boldsymbol{x}_{0}=(r,0,...,0)
on the
x_{1}
-axis where
\vol_{\partial B_{r}}=dx_{234567}
and
\omega_{0}|_{\boldsymbol{x}_{0}}=1/3\cdot x_{1}(dx_{23}-dx_{45}-dx_{67})
. Then one can write out
\alpha
in local coordinates to complete the computation. From (
5.4
), it follows that

3r^{-4}\int_{\partial B_{r}}-2n\tr(F_{A}\wedge F_{A})\wedge\omega_{0}+r^{-3}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|F_{A}|^{2}\geq 0.

In this way, we get a lower bound on the derivative

\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(0))\geq-9r^{-4}\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r})}\geq const.r\cdot\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}.

The result now follows from integration from
r
to
1
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\phi_{0})
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the standard
G_{2}
-structure. There exists constants
\epsilon_{4}>0,\mathfrak{c}_{4}>0
such that for each connection
A
on the trivial
SU(n)
-bundle satisfying

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r}(x))}\leq\epsilon_{4},

where
B_{r}(x)\Subset B_{1/2}(0)
is some ball contained in the ball centered at the origin of radius
1/2
, one has

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{3r/4}(x))}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{4}\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\right).

Proof.

Let’s rescale and translate the ball
B_{r}(x)
to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}
, and denote the corresponding connection by
A^{\prime}=d+a^{\prime}
. Since the
L^{7/2}
-norm of the curvature and the normalizer energy is conformally invariant, we have

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r}(x))}=\|F_{A^{\prime}}\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\quad\text{ and }\quad\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A,B_{r}(x))=\hat{\mathcal{E}}(A^{\prime},\mathbb{B})=\|F_{A^{\prime}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}.

Then
Theorem 5.2
and
Section 5
tell us that

\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(B_{3/4}(0))}\leq const.\left(\|F_{A^{\prime}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A^{\prime}})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\right)<1

once
\|F_{A^{\prime}}\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}
is sufficiently small. Using the Sobolev multiplication
L^{7/2}_{1}\times L^{7/2}_{1}\hookrightarrow L^{7/2}
, we have

\|F_{A^{\prime}}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{3/4}(0))}\leq const.\left(\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(B_{3/4}(0))}+\|a^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{7/2}_{1}(B_{3/4}(0))}\right)\leq const.\|a^{\prime}\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(B_{3/4}(0))}.

Rescale back to
B_{r}(x)
, we get

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{3r/4}(x))}\leq const.\left(r^{-3/2}\||F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r}(x))}\right).

Note that
B_{r+1/2}(x)\Subset\mathbb{B}
.
Section 5
gives us

\begin{split}r^{-3}\||F_{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}&\leq(r+1/2)^{-3}\|F_{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(B_{r+1/2}(x))}+const.\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{7/2}(B_{r+1/2}(x))}\\
&\leq const.\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\right)^{2},\end{split}

which can be substituted back to the inequality above to complete the proof.
∎

Now we are prepared to bound the
L^{7/2}
-norm of the curvature on unit balls.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\phi_{0})
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the standard
G_{2}
-structure and
B^{\prime}\Subset B_{1/2}(0)
an interior ball. There are constants
\epsilon_{5}>0
,
\mathfrak{c}_{5}>0
such that for each connection
A
on the trivial
SU(n)
-bundle satisfying

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\epsilon_{5}\quad\text{ and }\quad\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\epsilon_{5},

one has the following bound

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B^{\prime})}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{5}\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\right).

Proof.

Given a point
x\in\mathring{B}_{1/2}(0)
, we denote by
D(x)=1/2-|x|
the distance between
x
and the boundary
\partial B_{1/2}(0)
. Consider

r(x)=\sup\{r<D(x):\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r}(x))}\leq\epsilon_{4}\},

where
\epsilon_{4}
is the constant in
Section 5
. We claim that when
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}
and
\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}
are sufficiently small, one can find
M>0
such that
D(x)\leq Mr(x)
holds for all
x\in B_{1/2}(0)
.

Let’s assume the claim is true for a moment. Then given any interior ball
B^{\prime}\Subset B_{1/2}(0)
, one can cover
B^{\prime}
with finitely many balls of the form
B_{3r/4}(x)
so that
B_{r}(x)\Subset B_{1/2}(0)
and
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r}(x))}\leq\epsilon_{4}
. Then we get the desired estimate on
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B^{\prime})}
by applying
Section 5
to each of the balls.

Now we prove the claim. Suppose one can find a sequence
x_{i}\in\mathring{B}_{1/2}(0)
such that
D(x_{i})/r(x_{i})\rightarrow\infty
. After taking a subsequence, we may assume that
x_{i}\rightarrow x_{o}
. Note that
x_{o}\in\partial B_{1/2}(0)
, for otherwise one gets
r(x_{o})=0
which is impossible. Now we fix
r_{o}>0
so that
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r_{o}}(x_{o}))}<\epsilon_{4}
, and choose
x_{k}
so that
B_{r(x_{k})}(x_{k})\subset\mathring{B}_{3r_{o}/4}(x_{o})
.
Section 5
tells us that

(5.5)

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{3r_{o}/4}(x_{o}))}\leq\mathfrak{c}_{4}\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}\right).

By choosing
\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}
and
\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\mathbb{B})}
sufficiently small, we can arrange that the RHS of (
5.5
) is less than
\epsilon_{4}
. In particular, for some
\epsilon>0
, we get

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{r(x_{k})+\epsilon}(x(k)))}\leq\|F_{A}\|_{L^{7/2}(B_{3r_{o}/4}(x_{o}))}<\epsilon_{4}.

This contradicts to the choice of
r(x_{k})
.
∎

Now we move on to control the higher derivatives of connection
1
-forms.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
(\mathbb{B},\phi_{0})
be the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{7}
equipped with the standard
G_{2}
-structure and
B^{\prime}\Subset\mathbb{B}
an interior ball. There is a constant
\epsilon_{6}>0
such that for each connection
A=d+a
on the trivial
SU(n)
-bundle satisfying

d^{*}a=0\quad\text{ and }\quad\|a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(\mathbb{B})}\leq\epsilon_{6}

one has

\|a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l}(B^{\prime})}\leq p_{l}\left(\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{B})}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}(\mathbb{B})}\right)\quad\forall\;l\geq 2,

where
p_{l}:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a polynomial with positive coefficients and no zero-th order term.

Proof.

Let
\delta:=d^{*}\oplus\pi_{7}
be the operator considered in the proof of
Section 5
, and
\beta:\mathbb{B}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
a cut-off function with
\beta|_{B^{\prime}}\equiv 1
. The elliptic estimates of
\delta
gives us

\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l}}\leq const.\left(\|a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}+\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}\right).

In general, the Leibinz rule gives us the Sobolev inequality:

\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}\leq const.\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{l}\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{k}}.

So when
\|a\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}}
is sufficiently small, one can rearrange the term containing
\|\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l}}
to get the desired estimate. To see why the constant
\epsilon_{6}
can be chosen to be independent of
l
, we note that when
l\geq 4
the Sobolev multiplication inequality becomes:

\|\beta a\wedge\beta a\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}\leq\|\beta a\|^{2}_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}}.

So there is no need to do the rearrangement when
l\geq 4
.
∎

Proof of
Theorem 5.1
.

Let
A
be a
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton satisfying the equation
F_{A}\wedge\psi+\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)=0
. We fix a positive number
r_{0}>0
so such for each
x\in X
one can find
r_{x}\geq r_{0}
such that the geodesic ball
B_{r_{x}}(x)
is contained in an orbifold chart
(\tilde{U}_{x},G_{x},\varphi_{x})
. Let
\tilde{B}_{r_{x}}(x)\subset\tilde{U}_{x}
be a ball containing a connected component of
\varphi_{x}^{-1}(B_{r_{x}}(x))
and
\tilde{A}_{x}
the corresponding equivariant connection on
\tilde{U}_{x}
.

Since
\|F_{A}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=3\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
, we know when
\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|
is sufficiently small, one can guarantee that

\hat{\mathcal{E}}(\tilde{A}_{x},\tilde{B}_{r_{x}}(x))<\epsilon_{5}\text{ and }\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(\tilde{B}_{r_{x}}(x))}<\epsilon_{5},

where
\epsilon_{5}
is the constant in
Section 5
. Then
Section 5
gives us a bound on
\|F_{\tilde{A}_{x}}\|_{L^{7/2}(\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{x})}
for some interior ball
\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{x}\Subset\tilde{B}_{r_{x}}(x)
. We can choose
\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|
to be small so that the assumption of
Theorem 5.2
applies. We conclude that after possible gauge transformations over
\tilde{U}_{x}
, the connection
\tilde{A}_{x}=d+\tilde{a}_{x}
satisfies
d^{*}\tilde{a}_{x}=0
and

\|\tilde{a}_{x}\|_{L^{7/2}_{1}(\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{x})}\leq const.\left(r_{0}^{-3}\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}(X)}\right).

Now
Section 5
tells us that

(5.6)

\|\tilde{a}_{x}\|_{L^{7/2}_{l}(\tilde{B}^{\prime}_{x})}\leq p_{l}\left(r_{0}^{-3}\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(X)}+\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}(X)}\right).

In order to control
\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}(X)}
, we recall that
\pi_{7}(F_{A})=-\star(\psi\wedge\star\sigma_{\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A))
. Then
subsection 4.1
tells us

\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{7/2}_{l-1}(X)}\leq const.\|\pi_{7}(F_{A})\|_{L^{2}_{l-1}(X)}\leq const.\left(1+\|A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0}\|^{l-1}_{L^{2}_{l-1}(X)}\right)\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|,

where the constant depends on
\psi
and the orbifold chart
(\tilde{U}_{x},G_{x},\varphi_{x})
. A priori, we only have
A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0}\in L^{7/2}_{1}
. The recursive inequality (
5.6
) tells us that after a possible gauge transformation
(A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0})|_{U_{x}}\in L^{7/2}_{2}(U_{x})
. The gauge patching argument in
[
Weh05
, Chapter 7]
enables us to find a global gauge transformation
g
so that
g\cdot A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0}\in L^{7/2}_{2}(X)
. Iterating this process, we see that
A^{\dagger}-A^{\dagger}_{0}\in L^{7/2}_{l}
for any
l
after possible gauge transformations.

Now we have proved that the restriction of each
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton to a small orbifold chart admits a connection
1
-form that is bounded in
L^{7/2}_{l}
for any
l
, under the assumption that
\|\boldsymbol{\pi}\|
is sufficiently small. So given any sequence of
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed
G_{2}
-instantons, we can find a subsequence, using a diagonalization procedure, so that the restriction of this subsequence to a given small orbifold chart converges in
C^{\infty}
-topology to a
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed
G_{2}
-instanton, after possible gauge transformations. We then cover
X
with finitely many small orbifold charts, and run the argument over each of them. Finally, we pathch the gauge transformations applied to each charts as in
[
Weh05
, Chapter 7]
to get the desired convergence on
X
. This proves the compactness of
\mathcal{M}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
.
∎

6.
Deformation Invariants

Now we come to the point of defining an enumerative invariant for
G_{2}
-orbifolds by incorporating the techniques developed above. We first pin-down the precise definitions, then calculate the invariants for some orbifolds appeared in Joyce’s construction
[
Joy96
]
.

6.1.
Definition of Invariants

Definition 6.1
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be a compact
G_{2}
-orbifold, and
P^{\dagger}\rightarrow X
an admissible principal
U(r)
-orbibundle.

(a)

When
(X,\phi)
is simple, and either
r=2
or
c_{1}(P^{\dagger})
divisible by
2
, we define

n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\#\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z}

as the signed counting of irreducible
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed projective
G_{2}
-instantons with respect to a small generic perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
and orientation data. Moreover, for fixed
c\in H^{2}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, we write

n^{c}_{\phi}(X):=\sum_{P^{\dagger}}n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z},

where the sum ranges over all rank-
2
admissible bundles
P^{\dagger}
satisfying
c_{1}(P^{\dagger})=c
.

(b)

When
(X,\phi)
is not simple, we define

n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\#\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z}/2

as the counting of irreducible
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed projective
G_{2}
-instantons with respect to a small generic perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
.

(c)

When
(X,\phi)
is simple and satisfies
H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})=0
, and
P^{\dagger}
is an
SU(2)
-bundle with
c_{2}(P^{\dagger})=0\in H^{4}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})
, we define

n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\#\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z}

as the signed counting of irreducible
\boldsymbol{\pi}
-perturbed (non-projective)
G_{2}
-instantons with respect to a small generic perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
and orientation data. Moreover, we write

n_{\phi}(X):=\sum_{P^{\dagger}}n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})\in\mathbb{Z},

where the sum ranges over all
SU(2)
-bundles over
X
with vanishing
c_{2}
.

The items in
subsection 6.1
require some explanation. In (a), we define
n^{c}_{\phi}(X)
as the sum of admissible bundles with
c_{1}=c
. The requirement
c^{2}_{1}-4c_{2}=0
implies that there are only finitely many choices of
c_{2}
. Note that the higher homotopy groups
\pi_{n}(U(2))
with
4\leq n\leq 7
are all finite. Thus obstruction theory tells us there are only a finite number of
U(2)
-bundles over
X
once
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
are determined. So the sum defining
n^{c}_{\phi}(X)
is finite. In
(c)
, the requirement
H^{1}_{\orb}(X;\mathbb{Z})=0
implies that there are no non-trivial flat reducible
SU(2)
-connections, and the unique reducible flat
SU(2)
-connection is isolated from the irreducible ones. These properties continues to hold if the perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
is chosen to be sufficiently small. Thus the counting that defines
n_{\phi}(X)
makes sense.

As for the orientation data mentioned in
subsection 6.1
, we refers to Joyce–Upmeier
[
JU21
]
for the manifold case where they considered ‘flag structures’ as additional inputs. We believe such a work carries through the orbifold case after unraveling the corresponding homotopy theory. We choose not to illustrate the orientation data here for the reason of lacking practical applications. If the moduli space is non-empty, one can alway fix an orientation at one point, and use it to orient other points via the spectral flow of the deformation operator along a path.

Theorem 6.2
.

All invariants in
subsection 6.1
are independent of the choices of the perturbation
\boldsymbol{\pi}
, and invariant under
C^{0}
-deformation of torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures.

Proof.

The independence of perturbations follows from a standard argument. Given two small generic perturbations
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{1}
so that the moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}_{i}}(X,P^{\dagger})
are both regular. We can choose a path of small perturbations
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow{P}
, connecting
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{0}
and
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{1}
, transverse to the image
\pr_{1}(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(0))
, where
\mathcal{F}
is the map defined in (
4.11
), and
\pr_{1}:{P}\times{A}_{k}\rightarrow{P}
is the projection onto the first factor. Then transversality tells us that

\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\gamma}:=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\gamma(t)}(X,P^{\dagger})

gives an (oriented) cobordism from
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}_{0}}(X,P^{\dagger})
to
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}_{1}}(X,P^{\dagger})
. Thus the counting with respect to
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{0}
-instantons and
\boldsymbol{\pi}_{1}
-instantons coincides.

Suppose now we have a continuous path
\Phi:=(\phi_{t})_{t\in[0,1]}
of torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures on
X
. Then we consider

\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}(X,P^{\dagger}):=\bigcup_{t\in[0,1]}\mathcal{M}_{\phi_{t}}(X,P^{\dagger})\subset[0,1]\times{A}_{k}/{G}_{k+1}.

We can consider the map analogous to (
4.11
):

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\Phi}:{P}\times{A}^{*}_{k}\times[0,1]&\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{k-1}\\
(\boldsymbol{\pi},A,t)&\longmapsto\grad\cs_{\phi_{t},\boldsymbol{\pi}}(A)\end{split}

Due to our assumption,
\mathcal{F}_{\Phi}
is smooth on the first two components and only continuous on the third. However,
subsection 4.2
tells us that the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{F}_{\Phi}
on the first two components are already transverse to the zero section of
\mathcal{K}_{k-1}
. Thus the implicit function theorem tells us for a generic
\boldsymbol{\pi}\in{P}
, the perturbed moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
is a
C^{0}

1
-manifold. Since each
\phi_{t}
is torsion-free,
Theorem 5.1
tells us that
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi,\boldsymbol{\pi}}(X,P^{\dagger})
is compact when
\boldsymbol{\pi}
is sufficiently small. Thus we get an (oriented) cobordism from
\mathcal{M}_{\phi_{0}}(X,P^{\dagger})
to
\mathcal{M}_{\phi_{1}}(X,P^{\dagger})
, which implies the deformation invariance. Here the oriented corbordism means on a neighborhood of
\mathcal{M}_{\Phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
in
[0,1]\times{A}_{k}/{G}_{k+1}
, the determinant line of the index bundle of the deformation complex is trivialized so that the orientation is transported inside this neighborhood rather than ‘along the
C^{0}
curve’.
∎

Recall in the introduction section, an irreducible projectively flat connection
A^{\dagger}
of
P^{\dagger}
is said to be non-degenerate if
H^{1}(X,\rho_{A})=0
, where
\rho_{A}
is the representation
\rho_{A}:\pi^{\orb}_{1}(X)\rightarrow SO(r^{2}-1)
given by the induced flat connection on the bundle
\mathfrak{su}(P^{\dagger})
. Equivalently, if we write
\ad_{A}
for the local system arising as
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
twisted by
A
, then
H^{1}(X,\ad_{A})=H^{1}(X,\rho_{A})
.
subsection 1.2
follows from the following observation.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
[A^{\dagger}]
be a gauge class of irreducible projectively flat connection on an admissible bundle
P^{\dagger}
of a
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Then
[A^{\dagger}]
is regular as a point in the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
if and only if
[A^{\dagger}]
is a non-degenerate projectively flat connection.

Proof.

Recall for the deformation complex
E_{A}(X)
, irreducibility of
A
implies that

H^{0}(E_{A}(X))=H^{3}(E_{A}(X))=0\qquad H^{1}(E_{A}(X))\simeq H^{2}(E_{A}(X)).

To prove the lemma, it suffices to identify
H^{1}(E_{A}(X))
with
H^{1}(X,\ad_{A})
. We claim that
\forall a\in\Omega^{1}(X,\mathfrak{g}_{P})

d_{A}a\wedge\psi=0\Longleftrightarrow d_{A}a=0.

To prove the claim, suppose
d_{A}a\wedge\psi=0
, equivalently
\pi_{7}(d_{A}a)=0
by
subsection 3.1
. Then (
2.7
) tells us that

|\pi_{14}(d_{A}a)|^{2}\vol=2r\tr(d_{A}a\wedge d_{A}a)\wedge\phi)=2rd\left(\tr(a\wedge d_{A}a)\wedge\phi)\right),

where the last equality uses the fact that
A
is flat. Integrating over
X
gives us that
\pi_{14}(d_{A}a)=0
. Thus
d_{A}a=0
. This proves the claim. The identification between
H^{1}(E_{A}(X))
and
H^{1}(X,\ad_{A})
follows from the claim directly.
∎

Proof of
subsection 1.2
.

subsection 6.1
tells us that
\mathcal{M}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is regular for any torsion-free
G_{2}
-structure
\phi
under the given assumptions. Thus we can choose
\boldsymbol{\pi}=0
for defining
n_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
. In this case, the cohomology of the deformation complexes are identified for all torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures canonically by
subsection 6.1
. Thus the counting with signs agrees for different torsion-free
G_{2}
-structures.
∎

When the
G_{2}
-orbifold is flat, Walpuski
[
Wal13
]
gave a simpler criterion to justify non-degeneracy by appealing to the Weitzenböck formula of Dirac operators. Suppose
A^{\dagger}
is a projectively flat connection. Then the flat Levi–Civita connection on
X
coupled with the flat connection
\ad_{A}
on
\mathfrak{g}_{E}
gives a representation
\rho^{\LC}_{A}:\pi_{1}^{\orb}(X)\rightarrow\Aut((\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})|_{x_{o}})
for a reference non-singular point

.

Lemma 6.4

(
[
Wal13
, Proposition 9.2]
)
.

Let
(X,\phi)
be a flat
G_{2}
-orbifold, and
A^{\dagger}
a projectively flat connection on a
U(r)
-bundle
P^{\dagger}
of
X
. Then
A^{\dagger}
is non-degenerate if and only if the representation
\rho^{\LC}_{A}
fixes no non-trivial vectors in
(\Lambda^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{g}_{P})|_{x_{o}}
.

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
P^{\dagger}
be an admissible bundle over a flat torsion-free
G_{2}
-orbifold
(X,\phi)
. Then the moduli space of irreducible projective instantons
\mathcal{M}^{*}_{\phi}(X,P^{\dagger})
is regular.

Proof.

Since every irreducible projective
G_{2}
-instanton
A^{\dagger}
induces a flat connection
A
on
\mathfrak{g}_{P}
, we know that, after coupling with the flat Levi–Civita connection,
\rho^{LC}_{A}
fixes no non-trivial vectors.
∎

6.2.
Calculation of Invariants

In this subsection, we calculate the invariant

for some examples.

Example 6.1
.

The first example we consider is the manifold
X=T^{3}\times K3
whose
G_{2}
-structure is induced from the metric given by the product of a flat metric on
T^{3}
with a hyperkähler metric on
K3
. We may identify

\alpha,\beta,\gamma
for embedded curves in

representing the three factors respectively. Let

with

and

\mathcal{R}(P^{\dagger})=\{\rho:\pi_{1}(T^{3}\backslash\gamma)\rightarrow SU(2):\rho([\alpha,\beta])=-1\}/\Ad.

After identifying

\rho(\alpha)=i

respectively. To see why both

is pulled-back from

on

Then invoking

.
[
Kro05
, Lemma 6.3]
tells us that one can find a path of connections on

that joins

whose spectral flow of the deformation operator for the flatness equation on

is

. Over

splits as the sum of the deformation complexes of the flat connections on

and

for pull-back connections. Since

is complex, if we pull-back the path above, the resulting spectral flow of the deformation operator of

-instantons joining

is still even. After fixing an orientation on either of

In general, following the construction of Kronheimer

, Section 6.2]

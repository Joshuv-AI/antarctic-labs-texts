Optimal control of quantum system in fermion fields: Pontryagin-type maximum principle (I)
Penghui Wang, Shan Wang

Abstract




Abstract

In this paper, the Pontryagin-type maximum principle for optimal control of quantum stochastic systems in fermion fields is obtained. These systems have gained significant prominence in numerous quantum applications ranging from physical chemistry to multi-dimensional nuclear magnetic resonance experiments.
Furthermore, we establish the existence and uniqueness of solutions to backward quantum stochastic differential equations driven by fermion Brownian motion.
The application of noncommutative martingale inequalities and the martingale representation theorem enables this achievement.

47C15, 49K27, 81S25, 81Q93, 81V74.

Infinite-dimensional quantum control system; Pontryagin-type maximum principle; Backward quantum stochastic differential equations; Noncommutative martingale representation theorem.

To obtain the Pontryagin-type maximum principle, the following main ideas play key roles.

•

For
p\in[1,\infty)
, the noncommutative space

can be decomposed into

L^{p}(\mathscr{C})=L^{p}(\mathscr{C}_{e})\oplus L^{p}(\mathscr{C}_{o}),

and the details are listed in
[

. The fermion Brownian motion
\{W(t);t\geq 0\}
commutes with the elements of
L^{p}(\mathscr{C}_{e})
, and anti-commutes with the elements of
L^{p}(\mathscr{C}_{o})
. This allows us to overcome the difficulty of the noncommutativity of QSDEs in fermion fields.

•

To address quantum stochastic calculus with respect to the fermion Brownian motion
\{W(t);t\geq 0\}
, the canonical anti-commutation relation (
1.2
) is critical, which implies that

W(t)^{*}W(t)=W(t)^{2}=t\textrm{I},\quad t\in[0,\infty).

•

The Burkholder-Gundy inequality with respect to noncommutative martingales, given by Pisier and Xu
[
35
]
, plays a significant role in solving the QSDEs in fermion fields and obtaining the corresponding estimates. Based on this, we prove that the solution to the corresponding backward quantum stochastic differential equation (BQSDE for short) is a necessary condition of
Problem (QOC)
with an infinite-dimensional control domain
U

•

Finally, we investigate the solution to BQSDEs by using the noncommutative martingales representation theorem and noncommutative martingale inequalities.

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\frac{1}{2}\Big\{\langle\Phi(t)_{e}-\Phi(t)_{o},(F^{e}_{xx}(t)-F^{o}_{xx}(t)+G_{xx}(t))(y^{\varepsilon}(t),y^{\varepsilon}(t))\rangle

\displaystyle+\langle\phi(t),D_{xx}(t)(y^{\varepsilon}(t),y^{\varepsilon}(t))\rangle\Big\}+\left\langle L_{x}(t),y^{\varepsilon}(t)+z^{\varepsilon}(t)\right\rangle dt+\textit{{o}}(\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}).

By
[
32
, Chapter 12.4]
, from (
3.9
) and (
3.56
), we have the following dual product

\displaystyle\langle P(T)y^{\varepsilon}(T),y^{\varepsilon}(T)\rangle=-\left\langle h_{xx}(\bar{x}(T))y^{\varepsilon}(T),y^{\varepsilon}(T)\right\rangle

(3.62)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)_{e}-G(t)_{o}),\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G_{x}(t))\rangle\chi_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)dt

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{T}\langle Q^{e}(t)y^{\varepsilon}(t)_{o}+Q^{o}(t)y^{\varepsilon}(t)_{e},(F_{x}^{e}(t)-F_{x}^{o}(t)+G_{x}(t))y^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle dt

\displaystyle-2\int_{0}^{T}\langle P^{o}(t)(F_{x}(t)+G_{x}^{e}(t)-G^{o}_{x}(t))y^{\varepsilon}(t),(F_{x}^{e}(t)-F_{x}^{o}(t)+G_{x}(t))y^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle dt

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{T}\langle\mathbb{H}_{xx}(t,\bar{x}(t),\bar{u}(t),\phi(t),\Phi(t))y^{\varepsilon}(t),\ y^{\varepsilon}(t)\rangle dt+\textit{{o}}(\varepsilon^{\frac{3}{2}}).

Substituting (
3.62
) into (
3.16
), and combining with (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle 0\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(u^{\varepsilon}(\cdot))-\mathcal{J}(\bar{u}(\cdot))

(3.63)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\textrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\chi_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)\Big\{-\delta L(t)\langle\phi(t),\delta D(t)\rangle+\langle\Phi(t)_{e}-\Phi(t)_{o},\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G(t))\rangle

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)_{e}-G(t)_{o}),\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G(t))\rangle\Big\}dt+\textit{{o}}(\varepsilon).

Therefore, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\textrm{Re}\int_{0}^{T}\Bigg\{\frac{1}{2}\left\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)_{e}-G(t)_{o}),\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G(t))\right\rangle+\delta\mathbb{H}(t)\Bigg\}\chi_{E_{\varepsilon}}(t)dt

\displaystyle\leq\textit{{o}}(\varepsilon),

where

\delta\mathbb{H}(t):=\mathbb{H}(t,\bar{x}(t),u(t),\phi(t),\Phi(t))-\mathbb{H}(t,\bar{x}(t),\bar{u}(t),\phi(t),\Phi(t)).

Let
\varepsilon\to 0
, for a.e.
t\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\textrm{Re}\mathbb{H}(t,\bar{x}(t),u(t),\phi(t),\Phi(t))-\textrm{Re}\mathbb{H}(t,\bar{x}(t),\bar{u}(t),\phi(t),\Phi(t))

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\textrm{Re}\left\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)_{e}-G(t)_{o}),\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G(t))\right\rangle\leq 0.

Thus (
3.58
) is proved.
∎

Remark 3.1
.

In (
3.58
),

\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)_{e}-G(t)_{o}),\delta(F(t)_{e}-F(t)_{o}+G(t))\rangle

(3.64)

indicates that the operators do not commute with each other.
If the maps
F(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
,
G(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):[0,T]\times L^{p}(\mathscr{C})\times U\to L^{p}(\mathscr{C}_{e})
are adapted, then
F(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot),G(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)
can commute with
dW(t)
, and (
3.64
) can be transformed into

\langle(P^{e}(t)-P^{o}(t))\delta(F(t)+G(t)),\delta(F(t)+G(t))\rangle.

Then, Theorem
3.3
is similar to the classical results
[
13
, Theorem 2.6]
and
[
32
, Theorem 12.17]
.

4
The Solutions to Backward Quantum Stochastic Differential Equations

In section
3
, we present a necessary condition for quantum optimal control of (
1.3
). That is,
the solutions to BQSDEs is necessary for the Pontryagin-type Maximum Principle. In this section, we investigate the solution to (
3.55
) in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})
for
p^{\prime}\in(1,2]
.
Let us introduce the following semilinear BQSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} dy(t)&=f(t,y(t),Y(t))dt+Y(t)dW(t),\ {\rm in}\ [0,T),\\
y(T)&=y_{T},\end{aligned}\right.

(4.1)

where
y_{T}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}_{T})
is given, the map
f(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot):[0,T]\times L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})\times L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})\to L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})
is adapted,
Y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
.
To obtain the solution to (
4.1
), we assume the following condition:

Assumption 4.1
.

The map
f(\cdot,y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))
is adapted for each
y(t)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}_{t})
and
Y(t)\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}_{t})
,
f(\cdot,0,0)\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathscr{C};L^{1}(0,T))
, and there exist nonnegative functions
g_{1}(\cdot)\in L^{1}(0,T)
and
g_{2}(\cdot)\in L^{2}(0,T)
such that

\displaystyle\|f(t,y_{1},Y_{1})-f(t,y_{2},Y_{2})\|_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq g_{1}(t)\|y_{1}-y_{2}\|_{p^{\prime}}+g_{2}(t)\|Y_{1}-Y_{2}\|_{p^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\ \forall y_{1},\ y_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}_{t}),

\displaystyle\ Y_{1},\ Y_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}_{t}).

Definition 4.1
.

A pair
(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
is called a solution to (
4.1
) if
y(\cdot)
is an
L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})
-valued adapted, continuous process,
Y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
,
f(\cdot,y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathbb{A}}(\mathscr{C};L^{1}(0,T))
a.s., and for any
t\in[0,T]
,

y(t)=y_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}f(\tau,y(\tau),Y(\tau))d\tau-\int_{t}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau).

(4.2)

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that Assumption
4.1
holds. Then, there exists a unique pair
(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
which satisfies (
4.2
). Furthermore,

\|(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\|_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)}\leq\mathcal{C}\left(\|y_{T}\|_{p^{\prime}}+\int_{0}^{T}\|f(\tau,0,0)\|_{p^{\prime}}d\tau\right).

(4.3)

Proof.

We divide the proof into two steps.

Step 1.
First, we claim that for any
f(\cdot)\in L_{\mathbb{A}}^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C};L^{1}(0,T))
and
y_{T}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C})
, there is a pair
(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
such that

y(t)=y_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau-\int_{t}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau),\quad t\in[0,T).

Let

M(t):=\mathbb{E}\left(y_{T}-\int_{0}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau\bigg|\mathscr{C}_{t}\right),\quad y(t):=\mathbb{E}\left(y_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau\bigg|\mathscr{C}_{t}\right).

(4.4)

Thus,
M(0)=y(0)=\mathbb{E}(M(T))
. By the noncommutative martingales representation theorem
[
35
, Theorem 4.6]
, there exists
Y(\cdot)\in\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
such that

M(t)=M(0)+\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)dW(\tau),\quad t\in(0,T].

(4.5)

Hence,

\left\|\int_{0}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau)\right\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\leq\|M(T)\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\leq 2\left(\|y_{T}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\left\|\int_{0}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right).

(4.6)

From (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle y_{T}-\int_{0}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=M(0)+\int_{0}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau)

\displaystyle=y(0)+\int_{0}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau),

and

\displaystyle y(t)

\displaystyle=M(t)+\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=y(0)+\int_{0}^{t}Y(\tau)dW(\tau)+\int_{0}^{t}f(\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=y_{T}-\int_{t}^{T}f(\tau)d\tau-\int_{t}^{T}Y(\tau)dW(\tau).

Then,
y(\cdot)=y(0)+\int_{0}^{\cdot}f(\tau)d\tau+\int_{0}^{\cdot}Y(\tau)dW(\tau).

It is clear that
y(\cdot)\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))
.
From (
4.4
), we obtain that

\|y(\cdot)\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))}\leq\mathcal{C}\left(\|y_{T}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\left\|\int_{0}^{T}f(t)dt\right\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right).

(4.7)

On the other hand, from (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we have

\|(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot)\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)}\leq\mathcal{C}\left(\|y_{T}\|^{2}_{p^{\prime}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{T}f(t)dt\right\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}\right).

(4.8)

Step 2.
We prove that the pair
(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(0,T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(0,T)
is the unique solution to (
4.1
).
For fixed
T_{1}\in[0,T)
and any
(z(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))

\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)
. We consider the following BQSDE

\left\{\begin{aligned} dy(t)&=f(t,z(t),Z(t))dt+Y(t)dW(t),\ {\rm in}\ [T_{1},T),\\
y(T)&=y_{T}.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.9)

By the result in
Step 1
, the pair
(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)
is the unique solution to (
4.9
).
Then, we define a map

\Gamma:C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)\to C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)

by

\Gamma(z(\cdot),Z(\cdot))=(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot)).

Next, we claim that, for
T_{1}
being sufficiently close to
T
,

\displaystyle\|\Gamma(z,Z)-\Gamma(\bar{z},\bar{Z})\|_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\|(z(\cdot)-\bar{z}(\cdot),Z(\cdot)-\bar{Z}(\cdot))\|_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)},

\displaystyle\forall(z,Z),(\bar{z},\bar{Z})\in C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T).

To show (
4.10
), let

(\hat{y}(\cdot),\hat{Y}(\cdot)):=\Gamma(z,Z)-\Gamma(\bar{z},\bar{Z}),\quad\hat{f}(\cdot):=f(\cdot,z(\cdot),Z(\cdot))-f(\cdot,\bar{z}(\cdot),\bar{Z}(\cdot)).

Then,
(\hat{y}(\cdot),\hat{Y}(\cdot))
is solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} d\hat{y}(t)&=\hat{f}(t)dt+\hat{Y}(t)dW(t),\ {\rm in}\ [T_{1},T),\\
\hat{y}(T)&=0.\end{aligned}\right.

(4.11)

By Assumption
4.1
, the Hölder inequality and (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\|(\hat{y}(\cdot),\hat{Y}(\cdot))\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}\leq\mathcal{C}\left\|\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\hat{f}(t)dt\right\|^{2}_{p^{\prime}}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\left\|f(t,z(t),Z(t))-f(t,\bar{z}(t),\bar{Z}(t))\right\|_{p^{\prime}}dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\|z(\cdot)-\bar{z}(\cdot)\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))}\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{1}(t)|dt\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{C}\|Z(\cdot)-\bar{Z}(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}^{2}\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{2}(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\left\{\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{1}(t)|dt\right)^{2}+\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{2}(t)|^{2}dt\right\}

\displaystyle\cdot\|(z(\cdot)-\bar{z}(\cdot),Z(\cdot)-\bar{Z}(\cdot))\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)},

and

\displaystyle\|(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}\leq\mathcal{C}\left\{\|y_{T}\|^{2}_{p^{\prime}}+\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\|f(t,z(t),Z(t))\|_{p^{\prime}}dt\right)^{2}\right\}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}\left\{\|y_{T}\|_{p^{\prime}}^{2}+\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\|f(t,0,0)\|_{p^{\prime}}+g_{1}(t)\|z(t)\|_{p^{\prime}}+g_{2}(t)\|Z(t)\|_{p^{\prime}}dt\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle+\mathcal{C}\left\{\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{1}(t)|dt\right)^{2}+\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{2}(t)|^{2}dt\right\}\left\|(z(\cdot),Z(\cdot))\right\|^{2}_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}.

Let us choose
T_{1}\in[0,T)
such that

\mathcal{C}\left\{\left(\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{1}(t)|dt\right)^{2}+\int_{T_{1}}^{T}|g_{2}(t)|^{2}dt\right\}\leq\frac{1}{4}.

(4.14)

Then, by (
4.12
), we obtain (
4.10
). This shows that the map
\Gamma
is contractive. Hence, there exists a unique fixed point, which is a solution to (
4.1
) on
[T_{1},T]
.
Moreover, from (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we obtain that

\|(y(\cdot),Y(\cdot))\|_{C_{\mathbb{A}}(T_{1},T;L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathscr{C}))\times\mathcal{H}^{p^{\prime}}(T_{1},T)}\leq\mathcal{C}\left(\|y_{T}\|_{p^{\prime}}+\int_{T_{1}}^{T}\|f(t,0,0)\|_{p^{\prime}}dt\right).

(4.15)

Repeating the above argument, we obtain the existence of solution to
(
4.1
).
Finally, the uniqueness and the estimate (
4.3
) follow from (
4.15
).
∎

By Assumption
3.1
and
[

, there exists constant
\mathcal{C}
such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\left\{D_{x}(t)^{*}\phi+\left(F^{e}_{x}(t)-F^{o}_{x}(t)+G_{x}(t)\right)^{*}\left(\Phi_{e}-\Phi_{o}\right)-L_{x}(t)\right\}

Failure of strong unique continuation for harmonic functions on RCD Spaces
Qin Deng, Xinrui Zhao

Abstract
. Unique continuation of harmonic functions on RCD \RCD space is a long-standing open problem, with little known even in the setting of Alexandrov spaces. In this paper, we establish the weak unique continuation theorem for harmonic functions on RCD ⁡ ( K , 2 ) \RCD(K,2) spaces and give a counterexample for strong unique continuation in the setting of RCD ⁡ ( K , N ) \RCD(K,N) space for any N ≥ 4 N\geq 4 and any K ∈ ℝ K\in\mathbb{R} .



0.
introduction

Unique continuation of solutions to second order elliptic equations on Euclidean domains is a well-studied problem in PDE theory. Carleman’s pioneering work
[
Car39
]
established strong unique continuation in dimension 2 with
C^{2}
coefficients. Later, Bers and Nirenberg
[
BN54
]
showed the same for equations in non-divergence form with measurable coefficients using tools from complex analysis. For equations in divergence form, some results were obtained by
[
AM94
,
Ale98
,
Sch98
]
.

When
n\geq 3
, unique continuation was demonstrated by Aronszajn
[
Aro57
]
for equations with
C^{2,1}
coefficients and later improved to equations with
C^{0,1}
Coefficients by Aronszajn, Krzywicki and Szarski
[
AKS62
]
using a Carleman-type inequality. Garofalo and Lin
[
GL86
,
GL87
]
used a simpler argument involving frequency estimates to show a similar result. The regularity is sharp by Plis
[
Pli63
]
and Miller
[
Mil74
]
. For more discussions on the frequency function and their applications, see for example
[
CM97a
,
CM97b
,
Alm00
,
CM11
,
CM21d
]
.

In this note we will prove two results – the failure of strong unique continuation in
\RCD(K,N)
spaces for
N\geq 2
and weak unique continuation in the case of
\RCD(K,2)
spaces.

Consider the equation

(0.1)

\displaystyle Lu=-D_{\alpha}(ag^{\alpha\beta}D_{\beta}u+b^{\alpha}u)+c^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}u+eu=0

with

(0.2)

\displaystyle a\in C^{0,1},\,\,a\geq\gamma>0,\,\,b^{\alpha},c^{\alpha},e\in L^{\infty},\,\,g^{\alpha\beta}\,\,\text{is the metric tensor}.

Our first result is unique continuation for solutions to such equations in the two-dimensional case.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Given function
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
which is a solution of (
0.1
) on a connected open subset
\Omega
of a
\RCD(K,2)
space
(X,d,m)
, if we have that
\phi|_{B_{r}(x)}=0
for some
x\in\Omega
and
r>0
, then
\phi\equiv 0
.

We note that by a result of Lytchak and Stadler
[
LS18
]
, it suffices to consider Alexandrov space in dimension 2 of curvature at least K and collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
spaces, which is classified in
[
KL16
]
.

It is known by a result of Nikolaev
[
Nik83
]
(cf. Berestovskij and Nikolaev
[
BN93
]
) that Alexandrov spaces with sectional curvature bounded both from above and below have local harmonic coordinates around every point such that the metric tensor is
C^{1,\alpha}
. Combine with results of Aronszajn, Krzywicki and Szarski
[
AKS62
]
and Garofalo and Lin
[
GL86
]
, one obtains strong unique continuation.

More precisely, we say that u vanishes to infinite order at
x_{0}
if there exists
R>0
such that for each integer
N>0

(0.3)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}|u|^{2}dx\leq c_{N}r^{N},\quad\text{ for }r<R.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Given function
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
which is a solution of (
0.1
) on a connected open subset
\Omega
of an Alexandrov space
(X,d)
with curvature bounded both from above and below, if we have that
\phi
vanishes of infinite order to
x_{0}
(see (
0.3
)) for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
, then
\phi\equiv 0
.

We also discuss spaces with two-sided bounded Ricci curvature or with Ricci curvature bounded from below and sectional curvature bounded from above in later sections. Finally we show that if we only assume that
(X,d,m)
is
\RCD(K,N)
, there are counterexamples for strong unique continuation.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

For each
N\geq 4
and
K
, there exists some
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,d,m)
and some
u
which is harmonic on some connected
\Omega\subseteq X
and vanishing to infinite order at some
x_{0}\in\Omega
, but that is not identically zero. Moreover, if
K\leq 0
,
u
may be chosen to be a global harmonic function.

Remark
\the\fnum
.

Actually we can prove that there exists a point
x_{0}
in an
\RCD(K,N)
space
(X,d,m)
such that every harmonic function which vanishes at
x_{0}
, vanishes at infinite order.

We note that we were only able to construct counterexamples when the point
x_{0}
in the above proposition is singular, and it remains an open question whether this is possible at some regular point.

1.
Local Charts in the 2-dimensional case

For an
\RCD(K,2)
space
(X,d,m)
, we have, from Lytchak and Stadler
[
LS18
]
, that

Proposition
\the\fnum
.

(
[
LS18
]
)
If
m=\mathcal{H}^{2}
(i.e. the space is non-collapsed), then
(X,d)
is an Alexandrov space with curvature at least
K
.

Therefore, it suffices to consider
2
-dimensional Alexandrov spaces of curvature at least
K
and collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
spaces. For an Alexandrov space, we have that

Proposition
\the\fnum
.

(
[
OS94
]
)
Let
(X,d)
be an
n
-dimensional Alexandrov space and denote
S_{X}
as the set of singular points. Then there exists a
C^{0}
-Riemannian structure on
X\setminus S_{X}\subset X
satisfying the following :

(1)

There exists an
X_{0}\subset X\setminus S_{X}
such that
X\setminus X_{0}
is of
n
-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero and that the Riemannian structure is
C^{\frac{1}{2}}
-continuous on
X_{0}\subset X
;

(2)

The metric structure on
X\setminus S_{X}
induced from the Riemannian structure coincides with the original metric of
X
.

See
[
OS94
, Section 1]
for a definition of
C^{r}
-Riemannian structure. If an Alexandrov space has sectional curvature not only bounded from below, but also from above, we have the stronger

Proposition
\the\fnum
.

(
[
Nik83
]
, cf.
[
BN93
,
KKK19
]
)
Let
(X,d)
be an Alexandrov space with bounded curvature. Then in a neighbourhood of each interior point there exists a harmonic coordinate system so that the components of the metric tensor in any harmonic coordinate system in
X
are continuous functions of class
W^{2,p}
, where for
p
we can take any number not less than one. Harmonic coordinate systems specify on
X
an atlas of
class
C^{3,\alpha}
for any
0<\alpha<1
.

From the Sobolev embedding theorem for Alexandrov space (cf.
[

]
), this implies that the metric tensor is
C^{1,\alpha}
in the corresponding harmonic coordinate systems.

Note that the major difference between Proposition
1
and Proposition
1
is that once we have bounded curvature, a good chart can be found around any point in the space, while if curvature is bounded merely from below, the chart can only be found around regular points and the metric tensor might even be discontinuous on the chart, if we do not remove an n-dimensional Hausdorff measure zero set, see for example
[
AB18
]
. We will handle this difficulty in the following sections.

Recall that a solution (sub-solution, super-solution) of (
0.1
) on an Alexandrov space is defined as (cf.
[
OS94
,
Shi01
,
KMS01
,
Hua11
,
AGS14
,
GT18
]
)

Definition
\the\fnum
.

A function
u:X\to\mathbb{R}
is a solution (resp. sub-solution, super-solution) of (
0.1
) if
u\in W^{1,2}_{loc}(X)
such that for all
\phi\in Lip_{0}(X)
and
\phi\geq 0
, we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\int_{X}-a\nabla u\cdot\nabla\phi-ub\cdot\nabla\phi+\phi c\cdot\nabla u+e(z)u\phi\,\,dm=0\,(\text{resp. }\leq 0,\geq 0).

where the inner product is induced by the Riemannian structure around regular points.

Using local charts, we may consider harmonic functions locally around regular points as a solution of an elliptic equation in divergence form with measurable coefficients

(1.2)

\displaystyle\int_{X}-g_{ij}u_{i}\phi_{j}-ub_{i}\phi_{i}+\phi c_{i}u_{i}+e(z)u\phi\,\,dm=0\,(\text{resp. }\leq 0,\geq 0).

2.
unique continuation in Alexandrov space with bounded curvature

In general, consider on a Euclidean domain the equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle Lu=-D_{\alpha}(a^{\alpha\beta}(z)D_{\beta}u+b^{\alpha}(z)u)+c^{\alpha}(z)D_{\alpha}u+e(z)u=0

with

(2.2)

\displaystyle a^{\alpha\beta}\in C^{0,1},\,\,\lambda\,I\leq(a^{\alpha\beta})\leq\Lambda\,I,\,\,b,c,e\in L^{\infty}.

It is known that

Proposition
\the\fnum
.

(
[
AKS62
,
GL87
]
, cf.
[
KT01
]
)
If u is a solution of (
2.1
) with coefficients satisfying (
2.2
) and vanish of infinite order at an interior point
x_{0}
(see (
0.3
)), then we know that
u=0
in the connected component of the domain containing
x_{0}
.

For weaker assumptions on coefficients, see for example Koch and Tataru
[
KT01
]
. Using the previous proposition, we have

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

If
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
is a solution of (
0.1
) on a connected open subset
\Omega
of an Alexandrov space
(X,d)
with curvature bounded both from above and below, and if there exists
x_{0}\in\mathring{\Omega}
and
R>0
such that for each integer
N>0
we have (
0.3
), then
\phi\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let us consider a harmonic coordinate system around
x_{0}
. From Proposition
1
we know that the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
are
C^{1,\alpha}
. Then we can rewrite the equation (
1.1
) as

(2.3)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(a\,g^{\alpha\beta}D_{\alpha}uD_{\beta}\phi+u\,b^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}\phi+\phi\,c^{\alpha}D_{\alpha}u+e(z)\,u\,\phi)\,\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}dx=0,\text{ for all }\phi\in Lip_{0}(U).

As we have
a\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}g^{\alpha\beta}\in C^{0,1}
,
\lambda\,I\leq(a\sqrt{det(g_{ij})}g^{\alpha\beta})\leq\Lambda\,I
and the rest coefficients are all
L^{\infty}
. So from Proposition
2
we have that
u
is zero around
x_{0}
. This shows that the points at which
u
vanishes of infinite order form an open set
V
.

Now it suffices to show that the set V of points at which u vanishes of infinite order is also a closed set. Given any
x\in\overline{V}\setminus V
, there is a sequence of points
x_{i}\in V
with
x_{i}\to x
. From Proposition
1
we know that around any point in the space there is a harmonic coordinate chart. So consider a harmonic chart C around x. There exists
N
such that for
i\geq N
, we have
x_{i}\in C\cap V
. From Proposition
2
we know that
\phi=0
on C. This shows that
x
is also a point at which u vanishes of infinite order. So
\overline{V}=V
and
V
is open. As
\Omega
is connected and V is non-empty, we know that
V=\Omega
, that is,
\phi=0
on
\Omega
.

∎

3.
Unique continuation in non-collapsed
\RCD(K,N)
space

It is natural to ask if it would be possible to weaken the assumption of bounded sectional curvature. A natural generalization would be to weaken the assumption of sectional curvature bounded from below to the
\RCD(K,N)
condition. Indeed from Kapovitch and Ketterer
[
KK20
]
we know that a
\CD(K,N)
and CAT(k) space
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{N})
is a space with bounded sectional curvature. More precisely,

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

(
[
KK20
]
) Let
N\geq 2
be a natural number and let
(X,d,\mathcal{H}^{N})
be a complete metric measure space which is
CAT(k)
(has curvature bounded above by
k
in the sense of Alexandrov) and satisfies
\CD(K,N)
. Then
k(N-1)\geq K
, and
(X,d)
is an Alexandrov space of curvature
bounded below by
K-k(N-2)
. In particular, X is infinitesimally Hilbertian.

Combining with Theorem
2
we have that solution of (
0.1
) on such a space have strong unique continuation property. Note that the background measure here must be the proportional to the Hausdorff measure and that the situation is much more complicated otherwise.

Indeed, from Kapovitch, Kell and Ketterer
[
KKK19
]
, if
(X,d,m)
is a complete metric measure space which is
CAT(k)
and satisfies
\RCD(K,N)
, the current optimal regularity for the metric tensor is in
BV\cap C^{0}
. Thus the previous argument using Proposition
2
does not work. What is worse, from Plis
[
Pli63
]
and Miller
[
Mil74
]
, even if the metric tensor is
C^{0,\alpha}
, there are counterexamples to weak unique continuation.

If we only assume that the space has two sided Ricci curvature bound with a non-collapsed Ricci limit space structure, then let us recall a classical theorem in Cheeger-Colding theory(
[
CC97
,
CC00a
,
CC00b
]
) considering the limit space of a non-collapsing sequence of manifolds with two sided bounds on Ricci curvature.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

(
[
CC97
]
)
Let
(Y^{n},y)
be the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of a sequence
\{(M_{i}^{n},p_{i})\}
for which there exists
v>0
such that
Vol(B_{1}(p_{i}))\geq v>0
for all
i
. If moreover we have that

\displaystyle|\Ric_{M^{n}_{i}}|\leq n-1,

then we know that the regular points of Y form a
C^{1,\alpha}
-Riemannian manifold.

For a general higher dimensional manifold, we know that if the metric tensor is
C^{0,1}
, we have unique continuation theorem for harmonic functions.

Note that as the metric tensor around regular points in Y is
C^{1,\alpha}
, combining a similar argument in the proof Theorem
5
and
[
CN12
]
we can show that,

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

If
(Y^{n},y)
is the Gromov-Hausdorff limit of a sequence,
\{(M_{i}^{n},p_{i})\}
and there exists
v>0
such that
Vol(B_{1}(p_{i}))\geq v>0
and

\displaystyle|\Ric_{M^{n}_{i}}|\leq n-1.

Then we have strong unique continuation theorem for harmonic functions on
Y
at regular points.

Remark
\the\fnum
.

The Theorem
3
also implies the weak unique continuation on the whole space.

4.
Classification of collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
spaces

In this section, we show that a collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
space
(X,d,m)
(i.e. where
m
is not proportional to
\mathcal{H}^{2}
) is necessarily a point, a line, a ray, an interval or a circle. The results of this section were shown in
[
KL16
]
. Since the proof simplifies somewhat using the recently established non-branching property of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces in
[
Den20
]
, we include a self-contained proof. For the same result on Ricci limit space, cf.
[
Hon13
]
.

We now recall some relevant definitions of
\RCD
theory, beginning with the definition of tangent cones.

Definition
\the\fnum
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space and
\bar{x}\in X
. A pointed metric measure space
(Y,d_{Y},m_{Y},\bar{y})
is called a tangent cone of
(X,d,m)
at
\bar{x}
if there exists a sequence of radii
r_{i}\to 0^{+}
so that

(X,r_{i}^{-1}d,\frac{1}{m(B_{r_{i}}(\bar{x}))}m,\bar{x})\to(Y,d_{Y},m_{Y},\bar{y})

in the pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff topology. The collection of all tangent cones at
\bar{x}
is denoted
Tan(X,d,m,\bar{x})
.

For the definition of pointed measured Gromov-Hausdorff convergence and relevant compactness results giving the existence of tangent cones at any point, see, for instance,
[
GMS15
, Section 3]
. Note that in general, tangent cones may depend on the sequence
r_{i}\to 0^{+}
and need not be unique at any given point. We now define regular points as follows.

Definition
\the\fnum
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space. For any integer
k\geq 1
and
x\in X
, we say that
x
is a
k
-dimensional regular point if

Tan(X,d,m,x)=\{(\mathbb{R}^{k},d_{\mathbb{R}^{k}},\frac{1}{\omega_{k}}\mathscr{H}^{k},0_{k})\}.

We denote by
R_{k}
the set of all
k
-dimensional regular points.

The following theorem about the structure of regular sets follows from the works of
[
MN19
,
BS20
]
.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

[
BS20
, Theorem 0.1]

Let
(X,d,m)
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space. There exists a unique integer
n\in[1,N]
, called the essential dimension of
X
, so that

m(X\backslash R_{n})=0.

By combining the results of
[
BGHZ21
]
and
[
DG18
]
, it is known that an
\RCD(K,2)
space is collapsed iff its essential dimension is
1
or
0
. The second case is trivial so we will only consider the case that the essential dimension is
1
. We will prove something slightly more general for any
\RCD(K,N)
space with essential dimension
1
which will imply the desired result.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space and suppose
x\in R_{1}
, then
x
lies in the interior of some geodesic.

Proof.

Suppose this is not the case, then there exists
r_{i},\epsilon_{i}\to 0
and
p_{i},q_{i}\in X
so that all of the following holds:

•

d(p_{i},x)
=
d(q_{i},x)=r_{i}

•

d(p_{i},x)+d(x,q_{i})-d(p_{i},q_{i})=\epsilon_{i}r_{i}

•

x
does not lie on a geodesic
\overline{p_{i}q_{i}}
.

For each
i
, fix
z_{i}
on
\overline{p_{i}q_{i}}
so that
d(z_{i},x)=d(\overline{p_{i}q_{i}},x)
. There are two cases:

Case 1:

\liminf\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(z_{i},x)}{r_{i}}=0

Up to choosing a subsequence, we may assume
s_{i}:=d(z_{i},x)
is so that
\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{s_{i}}{r_{i}}=0
and
(X,\frac{1}{s_{i}}d,x)
converges to
(\mathbb{R}^{1},d_{E},O)
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense. Let
z_{i}\to z_{\infty}
. There is a line through
z_{\infty}
from the limit of
\overline{p_{i}q_{i}}
. However, it is clearly that
O
is not on this line since the distance between
O
and the line must be
1
. This is a contradiction.

Case 2:

\liminf\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(z_{i},x)}{r_{i}}>0

Up to choosing a subsequence, we may assume
(X,\frac{1}{r_{i}}d,x)
converges to
(\mathbb{R}^{1},d_{E},O)
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense with
p_{i}\to p_{\infty}
,
q_{i}\to q_{\infty}
and
\overline{p_{i}q_{i}}
converging to some limit geodesic
\overline{p_{\infty}q_{\infty}}
. By our assumptions, we have

•

d(\overline{p_{\infty}q_{\infty}},O)>0

•

d(p_{\infty},O)+d(O,q_{\infty})=d(p_{\infty},q_{\infty})=2

It is impossible to find three points on
\mathbb{R}
which satisfy these properties. This gives a contradiction.
∎

We now prove a classification result for
\RCD(K,N)
spaces with essential dimension
1
.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Let
(X,d,m)
be an
\RCD(K,N)
space with essential dimension
1
.
X
is a line, ray, interval or circle. Moreover,
m=h\mathcal{H}^{1}\ll\mathcal{H}^{1}
where
h
is a
\MCP(K,N)
density.

Proof.

Fix any
x\in R_{1}
. By the previous lemma, we may assume that
x
lies in the interior of some geodesic
\overline{pq}
. We claim that for any
y\in X
, any geodesic
\overline{yx}
extends either
\overline{px}
or
\overline{qx}
. Combining this with the non-branching property of
\RCD(K,N)
spaces is enough to give the desired classification result of the theorem.

Fix any geodesic
\overline{yx}
. If
\overline{yx}\cap\overline{px}\neq\{x\}
, then
\overline{yx}
necessarily extends
\overline{px}
by the non-branching property of
X
and we are done. The same is true if we replace
\overline{px}
with
\overline{qx}
in the previous sentence. Therefore, we may assume
\overline{yx}\cap\overline{px}=\{x\}=\overline{yx}\cap\overline{qx}
.

Fix
r_{i}\to 0
so that
(X,\frac{1}{r_{i}}d,x)
converges to
(\mathbb{R},d_{E},O)
in the pointed Gromov-Hausdorff sense. For each
i
choose
y_{i}
on
\overline{yx}
so that
d(y_{i},x)=r_{i}
. Similarly, choose
p_{i}
and
q_{i}
on
\overline{px}
and
\overline{qx}
respective so that
d(p_{i},x)=d(q_{i},x)=r_{i}
. Since the blow-up converges to
\mathbb{R}
, we have either

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(y_{i},p_{i})}{r_{i}}=0\text{ and }\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(y_{i},q_{i})}{r_{i}}=2

or

\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(y_{i},p_{i})}{r_{i}}=2\text{ and }\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}\frac{d(y_{i},q_{i})}{r_{i}}=0.

Without loss of generality we assume the first case. We show
\overline{yx}
extends
\overline{px}
. Suppose not, then by non-branching any geodesic
\overline{y_{i}q_{i}}
can only intersect
\overline{p_{i}q_{i}}
at
q_{i}
. In particular,
x
is not in the interior of
\overline{y_{i}q_{i}}
. Repeating the same argument as in the previous lemma gives a contradiction.

The claim about
m
follows from, for example,
[
CM20b
]
.
∎

Combined with the discussion in the beginning of the section immediately gives the following corollary.

Corollary
\the\fnum
.

Any collapsed
\RCD(K,2)
space which is not a point is isometric to a line, a ray, an interval or a circle.

5.
Proof of Theorem
0

Let’s first we recall the corresponding unique continuation theorem in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
for elliptic equations of divergence form with merely measurable coefficients.

Theorem
\the\fnum

(
[
AM94
,
Ale98
,
Sch98
]
)
.

Given a solution u of equation

(5.1)

\displaystyle Lu=-D_{\alpha}(a^{\alpha\beta}(z)D_{\beta}u+b^{\alpha}(z)u)+c^{\alpha}(z)D_{\alpha}u+e(z)u=0

on a simply connected domain
\Omega
in the plane where
A=(a^{\alpha\beta})
is a
2\times 2
symmetric matrix of
L^{\infty}(\Omega)
coefficients satisfying for some constant
0<\lambda\leq 1
the uniform elliptic condition

(5.2)

\displaystyle\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{\alpha,\beta=1}^{2}a^{\alpha\beta}(z)\xi_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\leq\lambda^{-1}|\xi|^{2},\text{ for all }z\in\Omega,\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

If there exists a point
x\in\Omega
such that

(5.3)

\displaystyle u(z)=o(|d(x,z)|^{n})\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then we have that
u\equiv 0
.

The key of the proof is to reduce the problem to an elliptic system which is handled by Bers and Nirenberg
[
BN54
]
. For more details, see, for example, Schulz
[
Sch98
]
.

We now prove Theorem
0
using the results from the previous sections.

Theorem
\the\fnum
.

Given a harmonic function

Proof.

We first consider the non-collapsed case. Using Proposition

, we may assume we are working with an Alexandrov surface. By Proposition
1
, we know that there exists coordinate chart around any regular point so that the metric tensor is

outside a measure zero set, that is, there exists some

so that

\displaystyle|g_{ij}(x)-g_{ij}(y)|=O(|xy|^{\frac{1}{2}}),\,\,for\,\,x\in U\setminus S,\,\,y\in U.

Therefore, given any solution of (
0.1
) on an Alexandrov surface, the function in the local chart around a regular point satisfies the assumptions of Theorem
5
.

Given any function
\phi\in W^{1,2}(\Omega)
which is a solution of (
0.1
) with

for some

and

. We show that

Assume otherwise, then

is nonempty in

. Given a sequence of points

converging to

. Then for large enough i,

is in the chart. So we can use Theorem

to conclude that

is actually in

. This argument shows that any point in

Now consider any geodesic from a regular point in

to a regular point in

. Every point on the geodesic must be regular by
[

. However, the geodesic cross

If

Theorem

\mathcal{E}_{N}(\mu):=\int\rho^{1-\frac{1}{N}}dm.

Since the space has a weighted manifold structure with the corresponding
4
-Ricci curvature lower bound outside the tip, it is clear that for any
x\in X\setminus\{V\}
, the
\CD_{loc}
condition is satisfied.

Thus it suffices to check the
\CD_{loc}
condition around the vertex
V
.

Lemma
\the\fnum
.

On
X=(\mathbb{R}^{3},g_{\epsilon,3})
with

(6.31)

\displaystyle g_{\epsilon,3}=dr^{2}+(\frac{1}{2}r^{1+\epsilon})^{2}g_{S^{2}},

the vertex
V
cannot be in the interior of any geodesic between any
x,y\in B_{\eta}(V)
, where
\eta>0
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for small enough
\eta>0
, any
x,y\in B_{\eta}(V)
, any curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to X
with
\gamma(0)=x,\,\gamma(1)=y
and
\gamma(s)=V
for some
s\in[0,1]
, the length
|\gamma|>d(x,y)
.

Since we know that
|\gamma|\geq d(V,x)+d(V,y)=r(x)+r(y)
and as

(6.32)

\displaystyle d(x,y)=d((r(x),\theta(x)),(r(y),\theta(y)))\leq|r(x)-r(y)|+\frac{\pi}{2}\min\{r(x),r(y)\}^{1+\epsilon}\leq r.

Given
\eta
small enough, for
x,y\in B_{\eta}(V)
we have that

d(x,y)\leq|r(x)-r(y)|+\frac{\pi}{2}\min(\{r(x),r(y)\})^{1+\epsilon}<r(x)+r(y)\leq|\gamma|.

This shows that
\gamma
cannot be a geodesic between
x
and
y
.

∎

Now we show the
\CD_{loc}
condition around the vertex
V
. Given any
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in P_{2}(X,d,m)
supported in
B_{\eta}(V)
, where
\eta
is given in (
6
). From
[
Lis07
]
we know that there exists a
\pi\in P(Geo(X))
such that
(e_{t})_{\#}(\pi)=\mu_{t}
. As we know that from Lemma
6
all geodesics do not have vertex
V
as an interior point and also essentially do not have
V
as the boundary point as both
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are absolutely continuous with respect to
m=r^{(1-\eta)}dvol_{g_{0}}
. Thus as
X\setminus\{V\}
has
4
-Ricci curvature lower bound, (
6.30
) is satisfied in this case.

By
[
KK20
, Proposition 6.7]
, it is known that a
\CD(K,N)
space
(X,d,m)
is infinitesimally Hilbertian iff
m
-a.e. point
x\in X
has a Euclidean space as one of its tangent cones. This clearly applies for the constructed example since it is a weighted Riemannian manifold away from

and so the
\RCD(K,4)
condition is verified.

Step 3. Existence of non-trivial harmonic functions.

Next we show that there exists at least one non-trivial harmonic function locally for the spaces constructed above. In the case of
K\leq 0
, we show the existence of a global harmonic function.

First we show that we can construct harmonic function in
B_{s}(0)
with any given continuous boundary value using Perron’s method. For more discussions on Perron’s method on metric spaces see for example
[
BB11
]
.

Given any continuous function
g:\partial B_{s}(0)\to\mathbb{R}
, consider

(6.33)

\displaystyle S_{g}=\{h:B_{s}(0)\to\mathbb{R}\,|\,\Delta_{X}h\geq 0,\,\,h|_{\partial B_{s}(0)}\leq g\}.

Define

(6.34)

\displaystyle f(x)=\sup_{v\in S_{g}}v(x).

From Perron’s method and as all points in
\partial B_{s}(0)
are regular, we have that f is continuous in
B_{s}(0)
, harmonic in
0<r<s
and
f|_{\partial B_{s}(0)}=g
. We show that it is harmonic in
B_{s}(0)
. Given any compactly supported function
u:B_{s}(0)\to\mathbb{R}
, we have, from Cheng-Yau estimate,

(6.35)

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\nabla f|\leq\frac{C\sup_{B_{2r}(0)}(|f-f(0)|)}{r}\leq\frac{Ce^{-\frac{\epsilon}{4}(log\,r)^{2}}}{r},

where the last inequality comes from (
6.48
). So we have that

(6.36)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{s}(0)}\langle\nabla u,\,\nabla f\rangle\,d\nu=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{B_{s}(0)\setminus B_{t}(0)}\langle\nabla u,\,\nabla f\rangle\,d\nu=\lim_{t\to 0}\int_{\partial B_{t}(0)}u\langle\nabla f,\,\partial_{r}\rangle d\nu=0.

This shows that
f
is harmonic in
B_{s}(0)
with
f|_{\partial B_{s}(0)}=g
. In particular, if we choose
g\not\equiv 0
, we have that
f\not\equiv 0
.

Next we show that the existence of a global harmonic function in the example for
K\leq 0
. Note that the space we are considering is a cone at infinity. We can solve the Dirichlet problem on
\partial B_{i}
with almost the same boundary data to get a harmonic function
u_{i}:B_{i}\to\mathbb{R}
. Now it suffices to show that
u_{i}
converges to a non-trivial global harmonic function
u_{\infty}:X\to\mathbb{R}
. The key is a three circle theorem to control the behavior of
u_{i}
. The observation dates back to
[
Zha99
,
Din04
]
, cf.
[
Xu16
]
.

Denote
M_{u}(r)=\frac{1}{\mu(B_{r})}\int_{B_{r}}u^{2}d\mu
. For the space constructed above, from a similar argument as Theorem 3.2 in
[
Xu16
]
, we have that given
s
such that

(6.37)

\displaystyle s(s+(1-\eta))\notin\{k(k+1),k\in\mathbb{N}\}

there exists integer
k_{0}>1
such that for any
r\geq k_{0}
and
u(x)
harmonic over
B_{r}
, if

(6.38)

\displaystyle M_{u}(r)\leq 2^{2s}M_{u}(\frac{r}{2})

then

(6.39)

\displaystyle M_{u}(\frac{r}{2})\leq 2^{2s}M_{u}(\frac{r}{4}).

Then using Lemma 10.7 in
[
Che99
]
(see the argument of Theorem 2.1 of
[
Din04
]
) we have that given any harmonic function
u_{\infty}
on the tangent cone at infinity of X, we can find a sequence of harmonic functions
u_{i}
on
B_{R_{i}}
with
R_{i}\to\infty
such that

(6.40)

\displaystyle\mathop{lim}\limits_{i\to\infty}|u_{i}\circ\Psi_{\infty,i}-u_{\infty}|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\infty}(1))}=0,

where
\Psi_{\infty,i}:B_{\infty}(1)\to B_{R_{i}}
is an
\epsilon_{i}
-approximation with
\mathop{lim}\limits_{i\to\infty}\epsilon_{i}=0
.

Finally we take
\alpha
such that
\alpha(\alpha+(1-\eta))=2
and
\phi_{1}
is a eigenfunction on
S^{2}
with respect to the eigenvalue
2
. Set
u_{\infty}=r^{\alpha}\phi_{1}(x)
and construct
u_{i}
as above. From a blow up argument (cf. Lemma 4.4 in
[
Xu16
]
) we know that for any
d>\alpha
with
\alpha(\alpha+(1-\eta))\notin\{k(k+1),k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
r_{0}\in(0,1)
there exists
i_{0}=i_{0}(d-\alpha_{1},r_{0})>0
such that if
i\geq i_{0}
, for any
r\in[r_{0}R_{i},R_{i}]
,

(6.41)

\displaystyle M_{u_{i}}(r)\leq 2^{2d}M_{u_{i}}(\frac{r}{2}).

Combining with (
6.38
), (
6.39
) and Cheng-Yau gradient estimate we have that there exists
k_{0}(d)
such that

(6.42)

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{i}=\frac{u_{i}}{\sqrt{M_{u_{i}}(\frac{k_{0}}{2})}}

converge to a global non-trivial harmonic function on X. This is similar to the proof in the smooth case, cf.
[
Din04
,
Xu16
]
.

Step 4. Failure of strong unique continuation.

Now given any harmonic function
f
defined on an open subset of
X
containing the vertex, without loss of generality we assume that
f(0)=0
. From Cheng-Yau gradient estimate (
[
CY75
]
, cf.
[
Jia14
]
) we have that on
B_{r}(0)
,

(6.43)

\displaystyle\sup_{B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|\leq\frac{C}{r}\sup_{B_{2r(0)}}|u|.

From the maximum principle and the fact that
\partial B_{r}(0)
is connected, we have that on
\partial B_{r}(0)
there exists
x_{r}\in\partial B_{r}(0)
such that
u(x_{r})=0
. As
diam(\partial B_{r}(0))=\frac{\pi}{2}r^{1+\epsilon}
, we have that

(6.44)

\displaystyle\sup_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|u|\leq\frac{\pi}{2}r^{1+\epsilon}\sup_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|\nabla u|\leq Cr^{\epsilon}\sup_{\partial B_{2r}(0)}|u|.

Denote

(6.45)

\displaystyle q(r)=\sup_{\partial B_{r}(0)}|u|

and

(6.46)

\displaystyle D=\sup_{\frac{r_{0}}{2}\leq r\leq r_{0}}q(r).

Thus we have that

(6.47)

\displaystyle q(r)\leq Cr^{\epsilon}q(2r).

Using (
6.47
), we have that on
\frac{r_{0}}{2^{k+1}}\leq r\leq\frac{r_{0}}{2^{k}}
,
q(r)\leq 2^{\frac{\epsilon k(k+1)}{2}}(Cr^{\epsilon})^{k}D\leq(Cr^{\frac{\epsilon}{3}})^{\log_{2}r_{0}-\log_{2}r}D
. Thus we have

(6.48)

\displaystyle q(r)\leq Cr^{k}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{3}(\log r)^{2}}.

Since

(6.49)

\displaystyle\lim_{r\to 0}\frac{e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{3}(\log r)^{2}}}{r^{m}}=\lim_{r\to 0}e^{-\frac{\epsilon}{3}(\log r)^{2}-m\,\log r}=0,

for any
m\geq 0
, we have that

(6.50)

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{r}(0)}f\leq Cq(r)\leq Ce^{-\frac{\epsilon}{3}(\log r)^{2}+k\log r}=o(r^{m}),\,\,\text{ for all }m\geq 0.

This offers a counterexample for strong unique continuation property if we can find a harmonic function
f\not\equiv 0
, whose existence is guaranteed by the step 3.

In all we show that for the spaces constructed above, the strong unique continuation property of harmonic functions fails.

∎

Remark
\the\fnum
.

Although there is a Hopf fibration
S^{1}\to S^{3}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}S^{2}
, which can be regarded as a Riemannian submersion with totally geodesic fibres and they carry the metrics
g^{S^{1}},g^{S^{3}},\frac{1}{4}g^{S^{2}}
, it is unclear to us whether one can construct a Ricci limit space using warped products as in
[
CC97
]
. More precisely, denote
k_{2}=\pi^{*}(\frac{1}{4}g^{S^{2}})
and
g^{S^{3}}=k_{1}+k_{2}
and we consider metric on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\{0\}
as

(6.51)

\displaystyle dr^{2}+(\chi r^{1-\eta})^{2}k_{1}+(\frac{1}{2}r^{1+\epsilon})^{2}k_{2}.

We have, for
X
tangent to
S^{1}
and
Y
tangent to
S^{2}
,

(6.52)

\displaystyle Ric(\partial_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta(1-\eta)+2\epsilon(1+\epsilon)}{r^{2}}\partial_{r},

(6.53)

\displaystyle Ric(X)

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta(1-\eta)-2(1-\eta)(1+\epsilon)}{r^{2}}X,

(6.54)

\displaystyle Ric(Y)

\displaystyle=(\frac{2\epsilon(1+\epsilon)}{r^{2}}+\frac{4-(1+\epsilon)^{2}r^{2\epsilon}}{r^{2+2\epsilon}}-\frac{(1-\eta)(1+\epsilon)}{r^{2}})Y.

Such spaces do not have Ricci curvature lower bound in the
S^{1}
direction as
\eta<2(1+\epsilon)
.

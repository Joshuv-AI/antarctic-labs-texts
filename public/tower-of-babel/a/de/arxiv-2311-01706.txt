Abstract
. In our recent work, we introduced a generalization of the prime ideal factorization in Dedekind domains for submodules of finitely generated modules over Noetherian rings. In this article, we find conditions for the intersection of two submodules to have the same factorization as the submodules. We also find the relation between the factorizations of a submodule N N in an R R -module M M and the ideal Ann ⁡ ( M / N ) \mathrm{Ann}(M/N) in the ring R R and give a condition for their equality.



1.
Introduction

Throughout this article,
R
will be a commutative Noetherian ring with identity, and
M
will be a finitely generated unitary
R
-module. The reference for standard terminology and notations will be
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
.

Let
N
be a proper submodule of an
R
-module
M
. Then the ideal
(N:M)
in
R
is prime if for any
a\in R
and
x\in M
,
ax\in N
implies
a\in(N:M)
or
x\in N
. We say
N
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-prime submodule of
M
when
(N:M)=\mathfrak{p}
, and in this case,
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}

[
5
, Theorem 1]
.

We say a submodule
K
of
M
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N
in
M
and denote it as
N\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K
if
N
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-prime submodule of
K
. A
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension
K
of
N
is said to be maximal in
M
if there is no
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension
L
of
N
in
M
such that
L\supset K
. Since
M
is Noetherian, maximal
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extensions exist. It is proved that if
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal element in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
, then
(N:\mathfrak{p})
is the unique maximal
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N
in
M

[
2
, Theorem 11]
and it is called a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N
in
M
.

A filtration of submodules
\mathcal{F}:N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M
is called a regular prime extension (RPE) filtration of
M
over
N
if each
M_{i}
is a regular
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
-prime extension of
M_{i-1}
in
M
,
1\leq i\leq n
. RPE filtrations are defined and studied in
[
2
]
. Also, RPE filtrations are weak prime decompositions defined in
[
1
]
.

The following result shows that
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
is precisely the set of prime ideals occurring in any RPE filtration of
M
over
N
.

Lemma 1.1
.

[
2
, Proposition 14]

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
. If
N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M
is an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
, then
\mathrm{Ass}(M/M_{i-1})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. In particular,
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}\}
.

The following lemma characterizes the submodules occurring in an RPE filtration.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
3
, Lemma 3.1]

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
. If
N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}M_{i+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M
is an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
, then
M_{i}=\{x\in M\mid{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}x\subseteq N\}
for
1\leq i\leq n
. In other words,
M_{i}=(N:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}})
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

The occurrences of two prime ideals in an RPE filtration can be interchanged provided they satisfy the following condition.

Lemma 1.3
.

[
2
, Lemma 20]

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
and
N=M_{0}\subset\cdots\subset M_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}M_{i+1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M
be an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
. If
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
, then there exists a submodule
K_{i}
of
M
such that
N=M_{0}\subset\cdots\subset M_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}K_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i+1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n}=M
is an RPE filtration of
M
over
N
.

Remark 1.4
.

So for every reordering
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}^{\prime},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}^{\prime}
of
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}},\dots,{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
with
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}^{\prime}\not\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}^{\prime}
for
i<j
, we can get an RPE filtration

N\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}^{\prime}}{\subset}M_{1}^{\prime}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}^{\prime}}{\subset}M_{2}^{\prime}\subset\cdots\subset M_{n-1}^{\prime}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}^{\prime}}{\subset}M_{n}^{\prime}=M.

In particular, if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
is minimal among
\{{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}},\dots,{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}\}
, then we can have an RPE filtration

N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\subset}M_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}K_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+2}}{\subset}K_{i+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}K_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M

[
3
, Remark 2.5]
. In general, if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
occurs
r
times in an RPE filtration, then there exists an RPE filtration

N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\subset}M_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i+1}\subset\cdots\\
\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M_{i+r-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}M_{i+r}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+2}}{\subset}\cdots\subset M_{n}=M

where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for
j<i
.

It is proved that in any RPE filtration of
M
over
N
, the number of occurrences of each prime ideal is unique
[
2
, Theorem 22]
. Hence, if
N=M_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}M_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}M_{n}=M
is an RPE filtration, then the product
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
is uniquely defined for
N
in
M
and it is called the generalized prime ideal factorization of
N
in
M
. We write
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
and in short, we call it the prime factorization of
N
in
M
. Generalized prime ideal factorization of submodules is defined and studied in
[
7
]
.

In
[
7
]
it was observed that in a finitely generated module over a Noetherian ring, distinct submodules may have the same prime factorization. For example, in
k[x,y]
, the ideals
(x^{2},y)
and
(x,y^{2})
have the same prime factorization
(x,y)^{2}

[
7
, Example 2.5]
. We see that their intersection
(x^{2},xy,y^{2})
also has the prime factorization
(x,y)^{2}
. In this article we show that this need not always be true. For submodules
N
and
K
of an
R
-module
M
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)
, we find conditions for
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
. We also compare the prime factorizations of a submodule
N
in
M
and the ideal
\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
in
R
. We show that the product
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
is a multiple of the product
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathrm{Ann}(M/N))
and give a sufficient condition for
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathrm{Ann}(M/N))
.

We use the following lemmas.

Lemma 1.5
.

[
3
, Lemma 2.8]

If
N\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K
is a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension in
M
, then for any submodule
L
of
M
,
N\cap L\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K\cap L
is a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension in
L
when
N\cap L\neq K\cap L
.

Hence intersecting a regular prime extension with a submodule gives a regular prime extension whenever equality does not occur.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
N_{1}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}N_{2}
and
K_{1}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}K_{2}
be regular prime extensions in
M
. If
N_{1}\cap K_{1}\neq N_{2}\cap K_{2}
, then
N_{1}\cap K_{1}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}N_{2}\cap K_{2}
is a regular prime extension in
M
.

Proof.

We have
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq(N_{1}\cap K_{1}:N_{2}\cap K_{2})
since
\mathfrak{p}N_{2}\subseteq N_{1}
and
\mathfrak{p}K_{2}\subseteq K_{1}
. Now let
a\in(N_{1}\cap K_{1}:N_{2}\cap K_{2})
and
x\in N_{2}\cap K_{2}\setminus N_{1}\cap K_{1}
. Then
ax\in N_{1}\cap K_{1}
. Without loss of generality, we assume
x\notin N_{1}
. Then since
N_{1}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}N_{2}
is a prime extension,
ax\in N_{1}
implies
a\in(N_{1}:N_{2})=\mathfrak{p}
. Hence
(N_{1}\cap K_{1}:N_{2}\cap K_{2})=\mathfrak{p}
.

Let
ax\in N_{1}\cap K_{1}
for some
x\in N_{2}\cap K_{2}
,
a\in R
such that
x\notin N_{1}\cap K_{1}
. Without loss of generality, assume
x\notin N_{1}
. Then
ax\in N_{1}\cap K_{1}\subseteq N_{1}
implies
a\in(N_{1}:N_{2})=\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
N_{2}\cap K_{2}
is a
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N_{1}\cap K_{1}
, and
\{\mathfrak{p}\}=\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{N_{2}\cap K_{2}}{N_{1}\cap K_{1}})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{M}{N_{1}\cap K_{1}})
.

Let
L
be any
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N_{1}\cap K_{1}
in
M
and let
x\in L
. Then
\mathfrak{p}x\subseteq N_{1}\cap K_{1}
, i.e.,
x\in(N_{1}:\mathfrak{p})\cap(K_{1}:\mathfrak{p})=N_{2}\cap K_{2}
[Lemma
1.2
], and hence
L\subseteq N_{2}\cap K_{2}
. So
N_{2}\cap K_{2}
is a maximal
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N_{1}\cap K_{1}
in
M
.

Suppose
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Ass}(M/{N_{1}\cap K_{1}})
and
\mathfrak{q}\supseteq\mathfrak{p}
. Then
\mathfrak{q}=(N_{1}\cap K_{1}:x)
for some
x\in M
. Since
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
,
\mathfrak{p}x\subseteq N_{1}\cap K_{1}
. That is,
x\in(N_{1}:\mathfrak{p})\cap(K_{1}:\mathfrak{p})=N_{2}\cap K_{2}
. This implies,
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{N_{2}\cap K_{2}}{N_{1}\cap K_{1}})=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. That is,
\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{p}
is a maximal element in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/{N_{1}\cap K_{1}})
, and hence
N_{2}\cap K_{2}
is a regular
\mathfrak{p}
-prime extension of
N_{1}\cap K_{1}
in
M
.
∎

The next lemma gives a condition satisfied by the prime factorization of a submodule.

Lemma 1.7
.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
having
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
. Then
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\not\subseteq N
whenever
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\supset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for every
1\leq j\leq n
.

Proof.

There exists an RPE filtration

N=N_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{1}\subset\cdots\subset N_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}N_{i}\subset\cdots\subset N_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}N_{n}=M.

If for some
i
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\supset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,n
, by Remark
1.4
we can have an RPE filtration

N=N_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\subset}N_{i-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}K_{i}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+2}}{\subset}K_{i+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}K_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}M.

Then by Lemma
1.2
,
K_{n-1}=\{x\in M\mid{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}x\subseteq N\}
. So
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\subseteq N
would imply
M\subseteq K_{n-1}
, which is a contradiction. Hence
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\not\subseteq N
.
∎

Lemma
1.7
does not hold if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for some
j
. Let
R=k[x,y,z]/(xy-z^{2})
. Then
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}=(\overline{x},\overline{y},\overline{z})
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}=(\overline{x},\overline{z})
are prime ideals in
R
and
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{2}
has the RPE filtration

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{2}=(\overline{x}^{2},\overline{x}\overline{y},\overline{x}\overline{z})\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}(\overline{x})\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}(\overline{x},\overline{z})\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}R.

If
M=R
and
N={{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{2}
, then we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{2}
. But
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{2}M=N
.

Remark 1.8
.

For a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
R
, we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}

[
7
, Example 2.2]
. In fact, the only ideal in
R
having
\mathfrak{p}
as its generalized prime ideal factorization is
\mathfrak{p}
itself. For suppose
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{p}
for an ideal
\mathfrak{a}
in
R
. Then
\mathfrak{a}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}R
is an RPE filtration, which implies
(\mathfrak{a}:R)=\mathfrak{p}
. So
\mathfrak{p}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}
. Also,
\mathrm{Ass}(R/\mathfrak{a})=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
and therefore,
\mathfrak{a}
is
\mathfrak{p}
-primary, which gives
\sqrt{\mathfrak{a}}=\mathfrak{p}
. Hence we get
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{p}
.

2.
Main Results

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}
be ideals in
R
. If
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b})
and it is a product of at most two prime ideals, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Proof.

If
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{p}
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
R
, then by Remark
1.8
,
\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{p}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}=\mathfrak{p}\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}R
is an RPE filtration, and hence
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})=\mathfrak{p}={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.

If
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b})={\mathfrak{p}}_{1}{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
for some prime ideals
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1},{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
in
R
, we have RPE filtrations

\mathfrak{a}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}R

\mathfrak{b}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{b}}_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}R.

By Remark
1.8
,
{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}={\mathfrak{b}}_{1}={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
. Since
\mathfrak{a}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}
and
\mathfrak{b}\subset{\mathfrak{b}}_{1}
,
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}={\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\cap{\mathfrak{b}}_{1}
. So, using Lemma
1.6
, we have the RPE filtration
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}R
. Therefore
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.
∎

If
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b})
and it is a product of more than two prime ideals, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})
need not be equal to
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
. For example, in
R=k[x,y,z]
, let
\mathfrak{a}=(x^{2},y^{2},xy,xz)
and
\mathfrak{b}=(x^{2},y^{2},xy,yz)
. Then we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{b})=(x,y,z)(x,y)(x,y)
since there are RPE filtrations

(x^{2},y^{2},xy,xz)\overset{(x,y,z)}{\subset}(x,y^{2})\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}(x,y)\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}R

and

(x^{2},y^{2},xy,yz)\overset{(x,y,z)}{\subset}(x^{2},y)\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}(x,y)\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}R
.

But
{\mathcal{P}}_{R}(\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b})=(x,y)(x,y)
since

\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}=(x^{2},y^{2},xy)\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}(x,y)\overset{(x,y)}{\subset}R

is the RPE filtration of
R
over
\mathfrak{a}\cap\mathfrak{b}
.

For submodules
N
and
K
of
M
having the same prime factorization
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
,
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)
need not be equal to
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
even for
n=2
. For example, in the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
, we have the RPE filtrations
2\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0\overset{2\mathbb{Z}}{\subset}\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0\overset{0}{\subset}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
and

0\oplus 2\mathbb{Z}\overset{2\mathbb{Z}}{\subset}0\oplus\mathbb{Z}\overset{0}{\subset}\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
. So the submodules
2\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0
and
0\oplus 2\mathbb{Z}
have the same prime factorization. But
(2\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0)\cap(0\oplus 2\mathbb{Z})=0\oplus 0
and
{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}}(0\oplus 0)=0\neq{\mathcal{P}}_{\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}}(2\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0)
.

Now we find conditions for
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
for submodules
N
and
K
of
M
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
N
and
K
be submodules of
M
with
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
. Then
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
if
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\supset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for every
i\neq j
.

Proof.

We prove by induction on
n
. If
n=1
, then we have
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)=\mathfrak{p}
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
R
. So we have RPE filtrations
N\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}M
and
K\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}M
. By Lemma
1.6
,
M
is a regular
{\mathfrak{p}}
-prime extension of
N\cap K
, and hence
N\cap K\overset{\mathfrak{p}}{\subset}M
is an RPE filtration. Therefore
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)=\mathfrak{p}={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
.

Now let
n>1
, and assume the result is true for
n-1
. Suppose
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(K)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\supset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for every
1\leq i,j\leq n
,
i\neq j
. Then we have RPE filtrations

N={N}_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{N}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n-1}}{\subset}{N}_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}{N}_{n}=M

K={K}_{0}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{K}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n-1}}{\subset}{K}_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}{K}_{n}=M.

Since
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({N}_{1})={\mathcal{P}}_{M}({K}_{1})={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
, by induction hypothesis we get
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}({N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1})={\mathcal{P}}_{M}({N}_{1})={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
. So we have an RPE filtration

{N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}L_{2}\subset\cdots\subset{L}_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}L_{n}=M.

If
{N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1}=N\cap K
, then
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)={\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}
, which implies
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\subseteq N\cap K
. But since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\not\supset{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for every
1\leq j\leq n
, by Lemma
1.7
, we have
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\not\subseteq N
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}\cdots{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}M\not\subseteq K
, which is a contradiction. So
N\cap K\subset{N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1}
. Then by Lemma
1.6
,
N\cap K\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1}
is a regular
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
-prime extension in
M
. Therefore,

N\cap K\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{N}_{1}\cap{K}_{1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}L_{2}\subset\cdots\subset{L}_{n-1}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}{\subset}L_{n}=M

is an RPE filtration of
M
over
N\cap K
. Hence
{\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N\cap K)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{n}}={\mathcal{P}}_{M}(N)
.
∎

For a proper submodule
N
of
M
, let
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
. Next, we compare the prime factorizations
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
and
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
. Then for any submodule
K
of
M
,
\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{R}{(N:K)})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{R}{(N:K)})
. Then
\mathfrak{p}=((N:K):a)=\mathrm{Ann}(\frac{aK+N}{N})
for some
a\in R
. So
\mathfrak{p}
is a minimal element in
\mathrm{Supp}(\frac{aK+N}{N})
, and therefore
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{aK+N}{N})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
N
be a submodule of
M
with
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)={\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
. Then for any submodule
K
of
M
,
\mathcal{P}_{R}((N:K))={\mathfrak{p}}^{r}
, where
r\leq n
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.3
,
\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{R}{(N:K)})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{\mathfrak{p}\}
. So
\mathcal{P}_{R}((N:K))={\mathfrak{p}}^{r}
for some
r
. Suppose
r>n
. Then
{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
. Also, by Lemma
1.7
,
{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}\not\subseteq(N:K)
. So there exists
a\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{r-1}
such that
aK\not\subseteq N
. Since
a\in{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}^{n}M\subseteq N
, we get
aK\subseteq N
, a contradiction. Therefore
r\leq n
.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
. Then
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
. Also,
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})=\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
if every prime ideal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
is isolated.

Proof.

Taking
K=M
in Lemma
2.3
, we get
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
. Suppose every prime ideal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
is isolated. Since
\mathrm{Ann}(R/\mathfrak{a})=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
,
\mathrm{Supp}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})=\mathrm{Supp}(M/N)
, and they have the same set of minimal elements. Therefore, we have

\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\min{\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)}=\min{\mathrm{Supp}(M/N)}=\\
\min{\mathrm{Supp}(R/\mathfrak{a})}=\min{\mathrm{Ass}(R/\mathfrak{a})}\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(R/\mathfrak{a}).

Hence
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})=\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
.
∎

In the above lemma, the condition that all the prime ideals in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
must be isolated cannot be omitted. For, if
M
is the
\mathbb{Z}
-module
\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}
and
N=2\mathbb{Z}\oplus 0
, then
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)=\{2\mathbb{Z},0\}
. But since
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)=0
,
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}})=\{0\}
.

So
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
need not be equal to
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
. Then
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
is a multiple of
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
as a product of prime ideals.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)={\mathfrak{p}}^{n}
for some prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
R
, then taking
K=M
in Lemma
2.4
we get
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={\mathfrak{p}}^{r}
, where
r\leq n
. Hence
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
is a multiple of
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.

Now let
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}^{n_{k}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
’s are distinct primes. Then we have an RPE filtration

N\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}}\\
\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}}=M

(2.1)

such that
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq k
. Let
\mathfrak{a}\overset{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{q}}_{m}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{m}=R
be an RPE filtration of
R
over
\mathfrak{a}
. Then
\{{{\mathfrak{q}}_{1}},\dots,{{\mathfrak{q}}_{m}}\}=\mathrm{Ass}(R/\mathfrak{a})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
[Lemma
2.5
]. So
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}^{r_{k}}
, where
r_{i}\geq 0
. Then by Remark
1.4
, we can have an RPE filtration

{\mathfrak{a}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}}=R.

(2.2)

Suppose
r_{i}>n_{i}
for some
i
and let
i
be the least such integer. Let
N_{i}^{\prime}=N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}}
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i-1}+n_{i}}
. Then
N_{i}^{\prime}=(N:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}})
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}=(\mathfrak{a}:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{r_{i-1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}})
by Lemma
1.2
. Let
a\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}
. Then

a{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}}\subseteq a{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{r_{i-1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}}\subseteq\mathfrak{a}=(N:M).

That is,
aM\subseteq(N:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}})=N_{i}^{\prime}
. Therefore,
a\in(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)
, and this implies
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}\subseteq(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)
.

We have
{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}={\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i-1}+n_{i}}\subset{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i}}
from the filtration (
2.2
) since
n_{i}<r_{i}
. So we have
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\in\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime})
, and therefore for some
b\in R
,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}=({\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}:b)
. Clearly
({\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}:b)\subseteq((N_{i}^{\prime}:M):b)
. If
b\in(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)
, this implies
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}}bM\subseteq N
. That is,
{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}}b\subseteq\mathfrak{a}
. Then

({{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}-r_{1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{n_{2}-r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{(n_{i-1})-(r_{i-1})})\,b\subseteq(\mathfrak{a}:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{r_{i-1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}^{n_{i}})={\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}.

By assumption,
n_{j}\geq r_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,i-1
. If
n_{j}=r_{j}
for
j=1,\dots,i-1
, then this implies
b\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}
, i.e.,
({\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}:b)=R
, a contradiction. If
n_{j}>r_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,i-1\}
, then

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}-r_{1}}{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}^{n_{2}-r_{2}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i-1}}^{(n_{i-1})-(r_{i-1})}\;\subseteq({\mathfrak{a}}_{i}^{\prime}:b)={\mathfrak{p}}_{i}

implies
{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
for some
j<i
, a contradiction. So
b\notin(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)
. Then
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\subseteq((N_{i}^{\prime}:M):b)\subseteq\mathfrak{q}
for some
\mathfrak{q}\in\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{R}{(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)})
. From Lemma
2.3
and (
2.1
) we get
\mathrm{Ass}(\frac{R}{(N_{i}^{\prime}:M)})\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(M/N_{i}^{\prime})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}
. This implies
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{l}
for some
l\in\{i+1,\dots,k\}
, which is not true. Therefore
r_{i}\leq n_{i}
for all
i
. Hence
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
is a multiple of
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})
.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
N
be a proper submodule of
M
and
\mathfrak{a}=\mathrm{Ann}(M/N)
. If every prime ideal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N)
is isolated, then
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{n_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}^{n_{k}}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}
’s are distinct primes. Then by Lemma
2.5
and Theorem
2.6
we have
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})={{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}\cdots{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}^{r_{k}}
, where
1\leq r_{i}\leq n_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. So by Remark
1.4
we have RPE filtrations

N\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{i}}\\
\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}}=M;

{\mathfrak{a}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{i}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{i+1}}{\subset}\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}+\cdots+r_{k}}=R.

Note that
\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}
[Lemma
1.1
].

Suppose
r_{i}<n_{i}
for some
i
. Since
{\mathfrak{p}}_{i}\not\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}_{j}
whenever
i\neq j
, without loss of generality, we assume that
i=1
by applying Remark
1.4
.

By Lemma
1.2
,
N_{r_{1}}=(N:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}})
and
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}}=(\mathfrak{a}:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}})
. So for
a\in R
, we have

a\in{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}}

\Leftrightarrow

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}a\subseteq\mathfrak{a}=(N:M)

\Leftrightarrow

{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}}aM\subseteq N

\Leftrightarrow

aM\subseteq(N:{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}^{r_{1}})=N_{r_{1}}

\Leftrightarrow

a\in(N_{r_{1}}:M)
.

Therefore
{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}}=(N_{r_{1}}:M)
. Since every prime ideal in
\mathrm{Ass}(M/N_{r_{1}})
is isolated, by Lemma
2.5
,

\mathrm{Ass}(M/N_{r_{1}})=\mathrm{Ass}(R/{(N_{r_{1}}:M)})=\mathrm{Ass}(R/{\mathfrak{a}}_{r_{1}})=\{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2},\dots,{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}\}.

Since

N_{r_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{r_{1}+1}\subset\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}}\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}}{\subset}\cdots\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}_{k}}{\subset}N_{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{k}}=M

is an RPE filtration,
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\in\mathrm{Ass}(M/N_{r_{1}})
, a contradiction. Therefore
r_{i}=n_{i}
for all
i
. Hence
\mathcal{P}_{R}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{P}_{M}(N)
.
∎

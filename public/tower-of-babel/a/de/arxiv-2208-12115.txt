Non-existence of perturbed solutions under a second-order sufficient condition
Gerd Wachsmuth

Abstract
We present an optimization problem in infinite dimensions which satisfies the usual second-order sufficient condition but for which perturbed problems fail to possess solutions.

1
Introduction

For nonlinear optimization problems in finite dimensions,
it is well known
that uniqueness of the Lagrange multiplier
together with the standard second-order sufficient condition (SSC)
leads to the stability of the solution
under perturbations of the problem
and this is a key result
for the analysis of SQP methods,
see

[
2
, Proposition 6.3]

and

[
5
, Theorem 4.20]
.
For infinite-dimensional problems,
such a result is not known
and one typically resorts
to a strong second-order sufficient condition
for the analysis of SQP-type methods,
see, e.g.,
[
4
, (2.17)]
and

[
1
, (2.3)]
.
In this note,
we present an example which satisfies
the usual second-order sufficient conditions
but for which perturbed problems
fail to possess solutions.

2
Second-order sufficient conditions and perturbations

For convenience,
we state the standard SSC
for a problem of type

\text{Minimize}\quad f(x)\quad\text{s.t.\ }x\in C,

where

X
is a Banach space,

f\colon X\to\R

is twice continuously Fréchet differentiable on
X

and

C\subset X
is closed and convex.
Due to the simple structure of the constraint,
Robinson’s constraint qualification
is trivially satisfied by the above problem
and we have the following
result,
see
[
3
, Theorem 3.63(i), Remark 3.68]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\bar{x}\in C
,
\beta,\eta>0
be given,
such that
the conditions

\displaystyle-f^{\prime}(\bar{x})

\displaystyle\in\NN_{C}(\bar{x})

(1a)

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(\bar{x})h^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta\norm{h}_{X}^{2}\qquad\forall h\in\hat{C}_{\eta}(\bar{x})

(1b)

hold.
Then, a quadratic growth condition is valid at
\bar{x}
,
i.e., there exist

\delta,\varepsilon>0

such that

f(x)\geq f(\bar{x})+\frac{\delta}{2}\norm{x-\bar{x}}_{X}^{2}\qquad\forall x\in C,\norm{x-\bar{x}}_{X}\leq\varepsilon.

(2)

Here,
\TT_{C}(\bar{x})
and
\NN_{C}(\bar{x})
are
the tangent cone
and the normal cone in the sense of convex analysis,
respectively,
and

\hat{C}_{\eta}(\bar{x}):=\set{h\in\TT_{C}(\bar{x})\given f^{\prime}(\bar{x})h\leq\eta\norm{h}_{X}}

is the so-called approximate critical cone.
The role of the Lagrange multiplier
is played by
-f^{\prime}(\bar{x})

and this element is uniquely determined (for fixed
\bar{x}
).

In finite dimensions,
the assertion of
\cref
thm:SSC
also gives stability w.r.t. perturbations.
As an example, we give a simple result with linear perturbations.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
X
is finite dimensional
and that

\bar{x}\in C
,
\delta,\varepsilon>0

satisfy (
2
).
Then, for any
x\dualspace\in X\dualspace

with

\norm{x\dualspace}_{X\dualspace}<\delta\varepsilon/2

the perturbed problem

\text{Minimize}\quad f(x)+\dual{x\dualspace}{x}_{X}\quad\text{s.t.\ }x\in C

has a local solution
z
satisfying

\norm{z-\bar{x}}_{X}\leq 2\delta^{-1}\norm{x\dualspace}_{X\dualspace}
.

Proof 2.3
.

We consider the localized problem

\text{Minimize}\quad f(x)+\dual{x\dualspace}{x}_{X}\quad\text{s.t.\ }x\in C\cap B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x}).

Since the objective is continuous
and since the feasible set is compact,
there exists a local solution
z
.
The optimality of
z
together with the growth condition (
2
)
implies

\displaystyle\frac{\delta}{2}\norm{z-\bar{x}}_{X}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq f(z)-f(\bar{x})=\parens*{f(z)+\dual{x\dualspace}{z}_{X}}-\parens*{f(\bar{x})+\dual{x\dualspace}{\bar{x}}_{X}}+\dual{x\dualspace}{\bar{x}-z}_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{x\dualspace}_{X\dualspace}\norm{z-\bar{x}}_{X}.

Thus,

\norm{z-\bar{x}}_{X}\leq 2\delta^{-1}\norm{x\dualspace}_{X\dualspace}<\varepsilon
.
This shows the desired inequality
and since the constraint
z\in B_{\varepsilon}(\bar{x})
is not binding,

z
is a local solution of the perturbed problem.

3
Counterexample

We present a counterexample which shows that

\cref
prop:perturbation
fails in infinite dimensions.
We define the operator
S\colon L^{2}(0,1)\to L^{2}(0,1)

via

(Su)(x)=\int_{0}^{x}u(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
.
We further define

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle:=\R\times L^{2}(0,1),\qquad C:=\set[\big]{(t,u)\in X\given\abs{u}\leq t\text{ a.e. on }(0,1)},

\displaystyle f_{h}(t,u)

\displaystyle:=t^{2}+\norm{Su}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\norm{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-ht\qquad\forall(t,u)\in X,

where
h\geq 0
is a linear perturbation parameter
and
h=0
corresponds to the unperturbed problem.
It is clear that

X
is a Banach space,
C\subset X
is closed and convex
and
f_{h}
is twice Fréchet differentiable with

\dual{f_{h}^{\prime}(\bar{t},\bar{u})}{(t,u)}_{X}=2\bar{t}t+2\dual{S\bar{u}}{Su}_{L^{2}(0,1)}-\dual{\bar{u}}{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}-ht

and

f_{h}^{\prime\prime}(\bar{t},\bar{u})(t,u)^{2}=2t^{2}+2\norm{Su}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\norm{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}.

We start by checking that
the point

(\bar{t},\bar{u})=(0,0)

satisfies the SSC from
\cref
thm:SSC for the unperturbed objective
f_{0}
.
It is clear that
(0,0)\in C

and
f_{0}^{\prime}(0,0)=0\in\NN_{C}(0,0)
.
Since
C
is a closed, convex cone, we have

\TT_{C}(0,0)=C
,
thus,
the approximate critical cone is given by

\hat{C}_{\eta}(0,0)=C
.
It remains to verify (
1b
).
Let
(t,u)\in C
be arbitrary.
Then,
\abs{u}\leq t
implies
\abs{(Su)(x)}\leq tx
.
Thus,

\displaystyle f_{0}^{\prime\prime}(0,0)(t,u)^{2}

\displaystyle=2t^{2}+2\norm{Su}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\norm{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq 2t^{2}-2\int_{0}^{1}(tx)^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x-\int_{0}^{1}t^{2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}x=\parens*{2-2/3-1}t^{2}=\frac{1}{3}t^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{6}\parens*{t^{2}+\norm{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}}=\frac{1}{6}\norm{(t,u)}_{X}^{2}

and this verifies (
1b
) with
\beta=1/6
.
Thus,
(\bar{t},\bar{u})=(0,0)

is a local minimizer of
f_{0}
on
C

and one can show that it is even a global minimizer on
C
.

It remains to compute the minimizers of
f_{h}
on
C
.
Thus,
let
h\geq 0
be given and let
(\tilde{t},\tilde{u})\in C

be a local minimizer of
f_{h}
on
C
.

First, we consider the case
\tilde{t}>0
.
Then,
\tilde{u}
is a minimizer of

\text{Minimize}\quad\norm{Su}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\norm{u}_{L^{2}(0,1)}^{2}\quad\text{s.t.\ }-\tilde{t}\leq u\leq\tilde{t}\text{ a.e.\ on $(0,1)$}.

Now, Pontryagin’s maximum principle implies that

\tilde{u}(x)
solves

\text{Minimize}\quad 2(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})(x)u-\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\quad\text{s.t.\ }u\in[-\tilde{t},\tilde{t}]

for almost every
x\in(0,1)
.
Here,
S\adjoint S\tilde{u}
is given by

(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})(x)=\int_{x}^{1}(S\tilde{u})(\xi)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}\xi
.
In particular, we get the implications

\displaystyle(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})(x)

\displaystyle>0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\tilde{u}(x)=-\tilde{t}

\displaystyle(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})(x)

\displaystyle<0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\tilde{u}(x)=\tilde{t}.

We argue that
S\adjoint S\tilde{u}=0
.
Let
x\in[0,1)
be a maximizer of the continuous function
S\adjoint S\tilde{u}
.
Towards a contradiction, suppose that

(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})(x)>0
.
Then, we get
\tilde{u}=-\tilde{t}
in a neighborhood of
x

and this implies

(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})^{\prime}(\xi)=-(S\tilde{u})(\xi)\quad\text{and}\quad(S\adjoint S\tilde{u})^{\prime\prime}(\xi)=-\tilde{u}(\xi)=\tilde{t}>0.

for all
\xi
in a neighborhood of
x
.
Together with
(S\tilde{u})(0)=0
,
this contradicts the assumption that
x
is a maximizer of
S\adjoint S\tilde{u}
.
Hence,

S\adjoint S\tilde{u}\leq 0
.
Similarly, we can show

S\adjoint S\tilde{u}\geq 0
.
Thus,

S\adjoint S\tilde{u}=0

and this gives

\tilde{u}=0

which is, obviously, not a minimizer.
Hence, every local minimizer
(\tilde{t},\tilde{u})
satisfies
\tilde{t}=0

and, consequently,
\tilde{u}=0
.
This shows that
\tilde{t}=0
is a local minimizer of

\text{Minimize}\quad t^{2}-ht\quad\text{s.t.\ }t\geq 0.

This implies
h=0
.

To summarize,

(0,0)
is the only (local) minimizer of
f_{0}
on
C

and satisfies the second-order sufficient conditions.
However, the linearly perturbed functionals

f_{h}
,
h>0
, do not admit local minimizers on
C
.

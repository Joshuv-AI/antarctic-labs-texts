Attractor invariants, brane tilings and crystals
Sergey Mozgovoy, Boris Pioline

Abstract
. Supersymmetric D-brane bound states on a Calabi-Yau threefold 𝒳 \mathcal{X} are counted by generalized Donaldsdon-Thomas invariants Ω 𝖹 ​ ( γ ) \Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma) , depending on a Chern character (or electromagnetic charge) γ ∈ H ∗ ​ ( 𝒳 ) \gamma\in H^{*}(\mathcal{X}) and a stability condition (or central charge) 𝖹 \mathsf{Z} . Attractor invariants Ω ∗ ​ ( γ ) \Omega_{*}(\gamma) are special instances of DT invariants, where 𝖹 \mathsf{Z} is the attractor stability condition 𝖹 γ \mathsf{Z}_{\gamma} (a generic perturbation of self-stability), from which DT invariants for any other stability condition can be deduced. While difficult to compute in general, these invariants become tractable when 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is a crepant resolution of a singular toric Calabi-Yau threefold associated to a brane tiling, and hence to a quiver with potential. We survey some known results and conjectures about framed and unframed refined DT invariants in this context, and compute attractor invariants explicitly for a variety of toric Calabi-Yau threefolds, in particular when 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is the total space of the canonical bundle of a smooth projective surface, or when 𝒳 \mathcal{X} is a crepant resolution of ℂ 3 / G \mathbb{C}^{3}/G . We check that in all these cases, Ω ∗ ​ ( γ ) = 0 \Omega_{*}(\gamma)=0 unless γ \gamma is the dimension vector of a simple representation or belongs to the kernel of the skew-symmetrized Euler form. Based on computations in small dimensions, we predict the values of all attractor invariants, thus potentially solving the problem of counting DT invariants of these threefolds in all stability chambers. We also compute the non-commutative refined DT invariants and verify that they agree with the counting of molten crystals in the unrefined limit.



1.
Introduction and summary

Elucidating the microscopic origin of the entropy of black holes is a key objective of any putative theory of quantum gravity. String theory reached this milestone about 25 years ago, with the first quantitative description of the micro-states of BPS black holes (those black holes preserving a fraction of the supersymmetry of the vacuum), as supersymmetric bound states of D-branes wrapped around
various cycles of the internal Calabi-Yau (CY) three-fold
\mathcal{X}

[
98
]
. The indices counting (with sign) BPS bound states of arbitrary D-brane charge
\gamma\in H^{*}(\mathcal{X})
were soon determined exactly for
\mathcal{X}=T^{6}
,
\mathcal{X}=K3\times T^{2}
or orbifolds thereof preserving at least 16 supercharges (see e.g.
[
96
]
for a review). Despite some early successes, determining the exact BPS indices for a genuine CY3-fold with
\operatorname{SU}(3)
holonomy, hence preserving 8 supercharges, is still an open problem, except for very special charges
\gamma
. Our goal in this article is to review recent
progress in the case of non-compact threefolds, and provide some support for recent conjectures on the BPS indices for local Fano surfaces in the so-called ‘attractor’ or ‘self-stability’ chamber
[
Beaujard:2020sgs
]
.

Mathematically, this physics problem amounts to computing the generalized Donaldson-Thomas (DT) invariants
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma)
for arbitrary
\gamma\in H^{*}(\mathcal{X})

and central charge

\mathsf{Z}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X})
in the space of Bridgeland stability conditions
(or rather, its poorly understood subspace
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})\subset\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X})
spanned by string theory moduli)

[
35
,
65
,
MR2373143
,
8
]
.
Informally,
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma)
is the weighted Euler characteristic of the moduli space of semistable objects with Chern character

\gamma
in the bounded derived category of coherent sheaves
D^{b}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
. It is notoriously hard to calculate in general, except when
\gamma
describes a rank-one coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{X}
(a D6-brane
in physics parlance) and
\mathsf{Z}
is the large volume central charge, in which case
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma)
reduces to the usual Donaldson-Thomas invariant
[
100
]
, computable
from the Gromov-Witten invariants of
\mathcal{X}

[
80
]
. Furthermore, physicists are often
not content knowing
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma)
, but would also like to know its refined (or motivic) version
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma,y)
, which counts BPS states weighted by their angular momentum in 3 dimensions
[
34
,
46
]
. The latter is known for the Hilbert scheme of
n
points (corresponding to bound states of a single D6-brane with
n
D0-branes) on an arbitrary
\mathcal{X}

[
Behrend:2009dc
]
, but hard to
define for general compact CY3-folds and D-brane charges
[
66
]
.

For a non-compact CY3-fold,
the derived category
D^{b}_{c}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
of (compactly supported) coherent sheaves
becomes more tractable,
being equivalent to the bounded derived category of quiver representations
D^{b}(Q,W)

for a certain quiver with potential
(Q,W)
, the vertices correspond to a tilting sequence
of line bundles on
\mathcal{X}

(or in physics parlance, a set of ‘fractional branes’).
When
\mathcal{X}
is toric (i.e. has a
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{3}
-action),
(Q,W)
can be more directly
obtained from a brane tiling
[
45
,
52
,
88
]
.
It is convenient to associate to
\mathsf{Z}
a vector of stability parameters
\theta
, known
in physics as Fayet-Iliopoulos (FI) parameters.
The DT invariants
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma)=\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma)
,
along with their refined version
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma,y)=\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma,y)
,
can in principle be computed using
representation theory techniques,
but the presence of a potential
W
(called superpotential
by physicists) greatly complicates the matter.
The framing induced by the presence of a non-compact D-brane (e.g. a D6-brane wrapped on
\mathcal{X}
)
can be used to overcome these difficulties

[
Szendroi:2007nu
]
.

In general, the generalized DT invariant
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma)
or its refined version depend sensitively on the stability parameter
\theta
,
their jumps across walls of marginal stability in
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{X})

being governed by a universal wall-crossing formula
[
66
]
. A notable exception is the case of small crepant resolutions (sometimes known as ‘local curves’),
where the (unframed) quiver is symmetric
and the skew-symmetrized Euler form
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle

(defined in (
3.2
) below, and also known as the Dirac-Schwinger-Zwanziger pairing)
vanishes, so that DT invariants
are independent of
\theta
(indeed, they count D2-D0 brane bound states,
which are generally free from wall-crossing and equal to Gopakumar-Vafa invariants). The framed indices counting D6-D2-D0 branes do depend on the stability parameters
(\theta,\theta_{\infty})
,
where
\theta_{\infty}
is the FI parameter for the framing vertex, but they vanish in a particular chamber and can be deduced elsewhere by the semi-primitive wall-crossing formula
[
32
,
34
,
Mozgovoy:2011zh
]
, a special case of
[
66
]
. In the non-commutative (NC) chamber, obtained by crossing through all the walls associated
to D2-D0 bound states (see e.g. Figure
7
for the resolved conifold), they have a simple combinatorial interpretation as counting ‘molten crystals’
in a certain ‘classical crystal’ associated to the brane tiling
[
91
,
58
,
Szendroi:2007nu
,
88
,
92
,
2
,
40
]
, or equivalently, dimer configurations on the brane tiling itself
[
88
]
. In the large volume chamber,
obtained by crossing only those rays with negative D0-brane charge, they instead reproduce the usual DT invariants
computed by the (refined) topological vertex
[
1
,
57
]
.
Motivic DT invariants for small crepant resolutions have been computed for arbitrary charge and stability condition in a series of mathematical works
[
18
,
Morrison:2011rz
,
Mozgovoy:2011ps
,
Morrison:2011bc
,
89
]
, as we shall review below.

In contrast, for non-compact CY3-folds admitting compact divisors, such as the
total space of the canonical bundle
K_{S}
of a complex projective surface
S
(sometimes called ‘local surface’),
the unframed quiver is not symmetric, and the unframed indices
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma)
counting D4-D2-D0 bound states have a complicated dependence on the stability parameter
\theta
.
One mathematically natural stability condition is the ‘trivial stability’, where
any quiver representation is considered to be semistable, but the corresponding moduli space is badly singular and its physical interpretation obscure. Another natural stability condition for the
framed quiver is the NC chamber, where unrefined invariants can be computed by counting
molten crystal or dimer configurations, just as for small crepant resolutions. Unfortunately,
computing the refined invariants in this approach appears to be difficult.

Physics instead suggests a stability
condition
\mathsf{Z}_{\gamma}
known as the ‘attractor point’, which corresponds to the string moduli
reached at the horizon of a spherically symmetric black hole with charge
\gamma

[
43
]
. Its key feature is that, in a neighborhood of
\mathsf{Z}_{\gamma}
in
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{X})
, only single-centered black holes (and potentially, special multi-centered configurations
known as ‘scaling solutions’
[
19
]
) contribute to the index.
Mathematically,
\mathsf{Z}_{\gamma}
corresponds to
a generic perturbation
\theta_{\gamma}
of the self-stability parameter
\left\langle-,\gamma\right\rangle
for quiver representations
[
24
,
5
,
Beaujard:2020sgs
]
.
Following
[
Beaujard:2020sgs
]
, we define the attractor invariants (or attractor indices) as

\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)=\Omega_{\theta_{\gamma}}(\gamma,y)
.
These attractor invariants
are closely related to the notion of initial data in the theory of
wall-crossing structures and scattering diagrams
[
67
,
51
,
24
]
,
which we use to show that
\Omega_{\theta_{\gamma}}(\gamma,y)
is independent of the choice of perturbation.
Provided the attractor indices
\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)
can be determined for all dimension vectors
\gamma
, then the DT invariants
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma,y)
, for any stability parameter
\theta
, can be computed
by applying the wall-crossing formula repeatedly, or more efficiently by using the attractor flow
tree formula
[
31
,
72
,
5
]
.

In
[
Beaujard:2020sgs
]
, based on the study of D4-D2-D0 indices on
K_{S}
and their relation to
Vafa-Witten invariants of the surface
S
, it was conjectured that the attractor indices
\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)

for quivers
(Q,W)
associated to local Fano surfaces have a very simple structure:

Conjecture 1.1
.

\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)
vanishes unless
\gamma
is associated to a simple representation of
Q
,
or
\gamma
lies in the kernel of the skew-symmetrized Euler form, that is
\left\langle\gamma,-\right\rangle=0
;

Conjecture 1.2
.

If
\gamma
is not associated to a simple representation of
Q
, then
\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)
vanishes
unless
\gamma
is a multiple of the dimension vector
\delta=(1,1,\dots,1)

associated to a single D0-brane.

Note that Conjecture
1.2
is stronger than Conjecture
1.1
, since
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle

has rank
2
for local surfaces
and therefore has a large kernel, including the special vector
\delta
. Both conjectures were supported by
an analysis of the expected dimension of the moduli space of quiver representations in the attractor chamber, and by the fact that the indices
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma,y)
in suitable chambers agree with known results for Vafa-Witten invariants on Fano surfaces. If correct, these conjectures open a path to compute refined invariants in any chamber, in particular framed DT invariants in the NC chamber.

In this work, after reviewing the results quoted above in more detail, we shall provide additional support for Conjecture
1.1
, and refine it as follows:

i)

For
\mathcal{X}=K_{S}
with
S
one of the toric Fano surfaces
\mathbb{P}^{2},\mathbb{F}_{0},\mathrm{dP}_{1},\mathrm{dP}_{2}
,
we compute the attractor indices

\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)
rigorously, for low values of
\gamma
. Our method is to first
evaluate DT invariants for trivial stability, by extending the ‘double dimensional reduction’
method of
[
Morrison:2011rz
]
, and then to apply the Joyce-Reineke formula to reach
the attractor point; we find that both Conjectures
1.1
and
1.2
are corroborated. The same
holds for some cases with more than one compact divisor, such as
Y^{3,2}
and
a crepant resolution of
\mathbb{C}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{5}
.

ii)

More generally, for almost Fano surfaces such as
\mathbb{F}_{2}
or
\mathrm{PdP}_{2}
, or for
crepant resolutions of the orbifold
\mathbb{C}^{3}/\mathbb{Z}_{r}
, where
\mathbb{Z}_{r}
acts by

1\mapsto\operatorname{diag}(\omega,\omega^{k},\omega^{-k-1}),\qquad\omega=e^{2\pi\mathbf{i}/r},

for some
0\leq k<r/2
, we find that Conjecture
1.1
is corroborated, while Conjecture
1.2
fails when the quiver has cycles which do not pass through all the vertices.

iii)

In all considered cases, we find that the invariant
\Omega_{\theta}(n\delta,y)

corresponding to
n
D0-branes is independent of a stability parameter

\theta
(provided the latter is generic) and of the integer
n\geq 1
, and is given by

(1.1)

\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)=\Omega_{\theta}(n\delta,y)=-(y^{3}+(i+b-3)y+iy^{-1}),

where
i
and
b
are the numbers of internal and boundary lattice points in the toric diagram
of
\mathcal{X}
. Comparing with the virtual Poincaré polynomial
P(\mathcal{X};y)
, defined for any algebraic variety in
§
2.5

and computed for any toric CY3-fold in Lemma

, we see that (
1.1
)
agrees with the motivic invariant
(-y)^{-3}P(\mathcal{X};y)
.
This statement can be deduced from the results of
[
Behrend:2009dc
]
on the Hilbert scheme of
n
points on
\mathcal{X}
(see Remark

For other dimension vectors
\gamma
such that

, corresponding to D2-D0 brane bound states wrapped on an exceptional curve, the index turns out to be either
0
or
-y
.

iv)

We verify these results by computing the framed DT invariants in the NC chamber using the
attractor tree formula (or the Joyce-Reineke wall-crossing formula), and comparing against
the unrefined DT invariants obtained by counting molten crystals

While we are not able to prove these conjectures yet, we note that for
\mathcal{X}=K_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}
, the vanishing of attractor invariants
for dimension vectors outside the kernel of
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle
is closely related to the
scattering diagram construction in
[
22
]
. We also note that
some of the invariants above have been computed independently using exponential network
techniques
[

,
15

14
]
, and agree

From the physics viewpoint, we find it remarkable that the full BPS spectrum of D4-D2-D0 bound states
along with their framed analogues can be reconstructed from such simple data. In particular, it shows that all
such BPS states behave similarly to multi-centered black hole bound states, even though gravity is decoupled and none of the constituents carries enough entropy to form a black hole. An interesting
question is to further separate the attractor index
\Omega_{*}(\gamma)
into a single-centered invariant

\Omega_{\rm S}(\gamma)
(also known as pure Higgs index) from additional contributions from ‘multi-centered scaling solutions’
[
19
,
69
,

]
. A preliminary analysis using the Coulomb branch formula (see
[

and references therein) suggests that for D0-branes on
\mathcal{X}
,

(1.2)

\Omega_{\rm S}(n\delta,y)=\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)+i(y+1/y)=-y^{3}-(b-3)y

which depends only on the number

of boundary points in the toric diagram. Note that

for small crepant resolutions, where
\Omega_{\rm S}(\gamma,y)
and
\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)

necessarily coincide for any
\gamma
due to the vanishing of
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle
.

Finally, we note that for most dimension vectors, the motivic DT invariants fail to be invariant under
y\mapsto 1/y
. This failure can be traced to the D0-brane invariants

in (

) (and D2-D0 branes on exceptional curves when applicable), and is a consequence
of the fact that Poincaré duality does not hold for cohomology with compact support on a non-compact
algebraic variety.
Physically, the relevant index should count

-normalizable bound states, and therefore
the

-cohomology of
\mathcal{X}
and related quiver moduli spaces
[
103
,

]
.
It is natural to speculate that this count can be obtained by retaining the common terms in
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma,y)

and
\Omega_{\theta}(\gamma,1/y)
as argued in
[

,
39
]
. If so, it would follow from
(
1.2
) that the
L^{2}
-analogue of
\Omega_{\rm S}(n\delta,y)
vanishes, and that the

L^{2}
-analogue of
\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)
equals
-i(y+1/y)
, where
i
is the rank of the gauge group in M-theory compactified on
\mathcal{X}
,
as suggested in
[

, §5.1]

is of course the basic property which allows to view D0-branes as Kaluza-Klein gravitons in M-theory
[

]
.

The remainder of this article is organized as follows. In §
2
we recall basic definitions and properties of DT invariants for quivers with relations, and the relation between framed and unframed invariants for symmetric quivers. In §
3
we extend these relations to non-symmetric quivers using wall-crossing formulae, introduce the notion of attractor invariants, and present one version of the attractor tree formula. In §
4
we review the relation between brane tilings and
quivers with potential for singular CY 3-folds, and the relation between molten crystals and
non-commutative DT invariants. In §
5
we survey some known results about motivic DT invariants for small crepant resolutions, i.e. toric CY 3-folds without compact divisors. Finally, in §
6
we present a method for computing motivic DT invariants for local toric CY 3-folds with compact divisors, and apply it to compute attractor indices in a variety of examples, collecting evidence for the conjectures mentioned earlier.

2.
Invariants of quivers with potentials

In this section we review basic facts about representations of quivers with potential, and the
relations between framed and unframed, numerical and motivic Donaldson-Thomas invariants.

2.1.
Jacobian algebras of quivers with potentials

Let
Q=(Q_{0},Q_{1},s,t)
be a quiver with the set of vertices
Q_{0}
, the set of arrows
Q_{1}
, and with
s\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
and
t\colon Q_{1}\to Q_{0}
being the source and target of the arrows.
The paths in
Q
form a basis of the
path algebra

\mathbb{C}Q
, with the composition defined by concatenation of paths.
We define the source and target of
a path
p=a_{n}\dots a_{1}
to be
s(a_{1})
and
t(a_{n})
, respectively. The path

p
is called a
cycle
if
t(p)=s(p)
.
We define the (cyclic)
derivative
of
p
with respect to
a\in Q_{1}
to be

(2.1)

\frac{\partial p}{\partial a}=\sum_{a_{i}=a}a_{i+1}\dots a_{n}a_{1}\dots a_{i-1}.

A
quiver with potential
is a pair
(Q,W)
, where
W
is a linear combination of cycles in
Q
. We denote by
Q_{2}

the set of cycles contributing to
W
.
We define the derivative
\frac{\partial W}{\partial a}
of
W
with respect to
a\in Q_{1}
by linearity.
Define the
Jacobian algebra

J=J(Q,W)=\mathbb{C}Q/\left(\partial W/\partial a\mathchar 58a\in Q_{1}\right).

Example 2.1
.

Let
(Q,W)
be the quiver with one vertex,
3
loops
x,y,z
, and potential
W=xyz-xzy
:
1
x
y
z

Then

(2.2)

\frac{\partial W}{\partial z}=xy-yx=[x,y]

and similarly for other derivatives.
The path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
is the free algebra
\mathbb{C}\left\langle x,y,z\right\rangle
having non-commuting generators
x,y,z
,
while the Jacobian algebra
J(Q,W)
is the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
.
Note that this algebra is a coordinate ring of a CY3-fold
\mathbb{C}^{3}
.
Usually our Jacobian algebras will be non-commutative, although they will still correspond to some CY3-fold.

Define a
cut
of
(Q,W)
to be a subset
I\subset Q_{1}
such that every nonzero term of
W
contains exactly one arrow from
I
.
Given a cut, we define a ‘partial’ Jacobian algebra

(2.3)

J_{I}=J_{I}(Q,W)=\mathbb{C}Q^{\prime}/(\partial W/\partial a\mathchar 58a\in I),\qquad Q^{\prime}=(Q_{0},Q_{1}\backslash I).

There is a natural monomorphism and a natural epimorphism of algebras

(2.4)

i\colon J_{I}\hookrightarrow J,\qquad p\colon J\twoheadrightarrow J_{I}

such that
p\,i=1
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(Q,W)
be as in Example
2.1
and let
I=\left\{z\right\}
.
Then
J=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
and
J_{I}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]
.
The monomorphism
i\colon J_{I}=\mathbb{C}[x,y]\hookrightarrow J=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
corresponds to the projection
\mathbb{C}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{2}
,
(x,y,z)\mapsto(x,y)
and the epimorphism
p\colon J\to J_{I}
corresponds to the inclusion
\mathbb{C}^{2}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
,
(x,y)\mapsto(x,y,0)
.

2.2.
Stability conditions

A
Q
-representation is a tuple
((M_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}},(M_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}})
such that
M_{i}
is a finite-dimensional vector space for
i\in Q_{0}
and
M_{a}\colon M_{i}\to M_{j}
is a linear map for all arrows
a\colon i\to j
in
Q
.
Given a representation
M
of
Q
, we define its
dimension vector

(2.5)

\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M=(\dim M_{i})_{i\in Q_{0}}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}},\qquad\mathbb{N}=\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Define a

stability function
(or a central charge)
to be a linear map

(2.6)

\mathsf{Z}\colon\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\to\mathbb{C}

such that, for all canonical basis vectors
e_{i}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
,

(2.7)

\mathsf{Z}(e_{i})\in\mathcal{H}=\left\{\left.re^{\pi\mathbf{i}\phi}\ \right|r>0,\phi\in(0,1]\right\}\qquad\forall i\in Q_{0}.

We will often write
\mathsf{Z}(M)=\mathsf{Z}(\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M)
for a representation
M
.
For any
0\neq z\in\mathbb{C}
, let
\operatorname{Arg}z
be the unique
\phi\in(-\pi,\pi]
such that
z\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}e^{\mathbf{i}\phi}
.
For any nonzero representation
M
, define its
phase
to be

(2.8)

\phi(M)=\frac{1}{\pi}\operatorname{Arg}\mathsf{Z}(M)\in(0,1].

A representation
M
is called
\mathsf{Z}
-semistable
if, for any subrepresentation
0\neq N\subset M
, we have

(2.9)

\phi(N)\leq\phi(M).

Remark 2.3
.

Let us relate the above definition of a stability function to the notion of a stability condition on a triangulated category
[
23
]
.
A
Bridgeland stability condition
on a triangulated category
\mathcal{D}
is a pair
z=(\mathsf{Z},\mathcal{A})
, where

\mathcal{A}
is the heart of a bounded
t
-structure on
\mathcal{D}
(the corresponding
t
-structure
\mathcal{D}^{\leq 0}\subset\mathcal{D}
is uniquely determined by
\mathcal{A}
as the extension-closed subcategory generated by
\mathcal{A}[n]
for
n\geq 0
) and
\mathsf{Z}\colon K(\mathcal{A})\to\mathbb{C}
is a linear map such that
\mathsf{Z}(F)\in\mathcal{H}
for all
0\neq F\in\mathcal{A}
and
\mathsf{Z}
satisfies the Harder-Narasimhan (HN) property

[
23
]
.
In our earlier discussion the required abelian category is the category of representations
\mathcal{A}=\operatorname{Rep}Q

(and
\mathcal{D}=D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
).
The required linear map is the composition
K(\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}}\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\xrightarrow{\mathsf{Z}}\mathbb{C}
.

We can formulate the above semistability condition using slopes as follows.
Let us decompose

(2.10)

\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\rho,\qquad\theta,\rho\colon\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}\to\mathbb{R}.

where
\theta,\rho
are real-valued linear forms.
As before, we will write
\theta(M)=\theta(\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M)
,
\rho(M)=\rho(\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M)
.
For any non-zero representation
M
, define its
slope

(2.11)

\mu(M)=\frac{\theta(M)}{\rho(M)}=-\frac{\operatorname{Re}\mathsf{Z}(M)}{\operatorname{Im}\mathsf{Z}(M)}\in(-\infty,+\infty].

Then

(2.12)

\phi(N)\leq\phi(M)\iff\mu(N)\leq\mu(M)

and we can use the latter condition to test semistability.
We can change the stability function without changing the stability condition by considering

(2.13)

\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}=\mathsf{Z}-c\rho=-(\theta+c\rho)+\mathbf{i}\rho,\qquad c\in\mathbb{R}.

In particular, assume that we have a representation
M

with
\rho(M)\neq 0
.
Then

\theta^{\prime}=\theta-\frac{\theta(M)}{\rho(M)}\rho

satisfies
\theta^{\prime}(M)=0
.
Therefore
M
is semistable if and only if, for any subrepresentation
N\subset M
, we have
\theta^{\prime}(N)\leq 0
.
The elements
\theta\in\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}},\mathbb{R})\simeq\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
are sometimes called
Fayet-Iliopoulos parameters
or

weights
(note that
\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
can be interpreted as the root lattice of the root system associated with
Q
).
A representation
M
is called
\theta
-semistable
if
\theta(M)=0
and, for any subrepresentation
N\subset M
, we have

(2.14)

\theta(N)\leq 0.

We similarly define
\theta
-semistable representations of the Jacobian algebra.
Note that the condition of
\theta
-semistability becomes trivial for
\theta=0
.

2.3.
Moduli spaces of representations

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, define the
space of representations

(2.15)

R(Q,d)=\bigoplus_{a\colon i\to j}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{d_{j}}).

It is equipped with an action of the group
G_{d}=\prod_{i}\operatorname{GL}_{d_{i}}(\mathbb{C})
(in physics, the complexified gauge group) so that the orbits correspond to isomorphism classes of representations having dimension vector
d
.

Let
R(J,d)\subset R(Q,d)
be the closed subvariety of representations of the Jacobian algebra
J=J(Q,W)
, i.e. of representations satisfying
\partial W/\partial a=0
for all
a\in Q_{1}
.
This subvariety is also equipped with an action of the group
G_{d}
.
We can interpret
R(J,d)
as a critical locus of a map on
R(Q,d)
as follows.
For any representation
M\in R(Q,d)
and any cycle
p=a_{n}\dots a_{1}
, define

(2.16)

\operatorname{tr}(p|M)=\operatorname{tr}(M_{a_{n}}\dots M_{a_{1}})

and define
\operatorname{tr}(W|M)
by linearity.

Lemma 2.4

(See e.g.
[
95
]
)
.

Consider the map

(2.17)

\omega_{d}=\operatorname{tr}(W|-)\colon R(Q,d)\to\mathbb{C},\qquad M\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(W|M).

Then
R(J,d)=\operatorname{crit}\omega_{d}
.

For any weight
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
such that
\theta\cdot d=0
, let

(2.18)

R^{\theta}(Q,d)\subset R(Q,d),\qquad R^{\theta}(J,d)\subset R(J,d)

be open subsets of
\theta
-semistable representations.
We consider moduli spaces

(2.19)

M^{\theta}(Q,d)=R^{\theta}(Q,d)/\!\!/G_{d},\qquad M^{\theta}(J,d)=R^{\theta}(J,d)/\!\!/G_{d},

where
/\!\!/
denotes the good quotient
[
56
, §4.2]
,
and moduli stacks

(2.20)

\mathcal{M}^{\theta}(Q,d)=[R^{\theta}(Q,d)/G_{d}],\qquad\mathcal{M}^{\theta}(J,d)=[R^{\theta}(J,d)/G_{d}].

For
\theta=0
(trivial stability), we have
R(J,d)=R^{0}(J,d)
and we define

(2.21)

M(J,d)=M^{0}(J,d),\qquad\mathcal{M}(J,d)=\mathcal{M}^{0}(J,d).

Similarly, we define
R^{\mathsf{Z}}(J,d)
,
M^{\mathsf{Z}}(J,d)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathsf{Z}}(J,d)
, for any stability function
\mathsf{Z}

using
\theta
-semistability for
\theta
given by the decomposition (
2.10
).

2.4.
NCDT and other numerical framed invariants

Given a vector
\mathsf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
(which we will call a
framing vector
), define a
framed representation
of
Q
to be a representation
M
of
Q
equipped with an element

(2.22)

s\in\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{f}_{i}},M_{i})\simeq\operatorname{Hom}\left(P,M\right),\qquad P=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}P_{i}^{\oplus\mathsf{f}_{i}},

where
P_{i}
is the indecomposable projective representation corresponding to a vertex
i\in Q_{0}
.
We can interpret a framed representation as a representation of a different quiver as follows.
Define a quiver
Q^{\mathsf{f}}
, called a
framed quiver
, to be a quiver obtained from
Q

by adding one vertex labelled by
\infty
and
\mathsf{f}_{i}
arrows
\infty\to i
, for all
i\in Q_{0}
.
Then a framed representation of
Q
can be identified with a representation
M^{\mathsf{f}}=(M,M_{\infty},s)
of
Q^{\mathsf{f}}
such that
\dim M_{\infty}=1
. Physically, the framing vertex corresponds to an infinitely heavy source, also known as defect, as we discuss in §
4.2
. This motivates the label

\infty
for the framing vertex.

For any dimension vector
d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, let
d^{\mathsf{f}}=(d,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{Q^{\mathsf{f}}_{0}}
.
Define the
space of framed representations

(2.23)

R^{\mathsf{f}}(Q,d)=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{f}_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}})\oplus R(Q,d)=R(Q^{\mathsf{f}},d^{\mathsf{f}}).

Similarly, define
J^{\mathsf{f}}=J(Q^{\mathsf{f}},W)
and

(2.24)

R^{\mathsf{f}}(J,d)=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{f}_{i}},\mathbb{C}^{d_{i}})\oplus R(J,d)=R(J^{\mathsf{f}},d^{\mathsf{f}}).

A framed representation
M^{\mathsf{f}}
having dimension vector
d^{\mathsf{f}}=(d,1)
is called
\operatorname{NC}
-stable
(where
\operatorname{NC}
stands for ‘non-commutative’) if it is generated by
M_{\infty}
.
This means that for any subrepresentation
N\subset M^{\mathsf{f}}
with
N_{\infty}\neq 0
, we have
N=M^{\mathsf{f}}
.
This stability corresponds to the weights

(2.25)

\theta^{\mathsf{f}}=(\theta,-\theta\cdot d),\qquad\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}_{<0},

of the framed quiver, satisfying
\theta^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot(d,1)=0
.
For convenience, let us choose a specific vector in this chamber

(2.26)

\theta^{\mathsf{f}}_{\operatorname{NC}}=\left(-\delta,\delta\cdot d\right),\qquad\delta=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}.

Let

(2.27)

R^{\mathsf{f},{\operatorname{NC}}}(J,d)=R^{\theta^{\mathsf{f}}_{\operatorname{NC}}}(J^{\mathsf{f}},d^{\mathsf{f}})\subset R^{\mathsf{f}}(J,d)

denote the subspace of
\operatorname{NC}
-stable framed representations
and

(2.28)

M^{\mathsf{f},{\operatorname{NC}}}(J,d)=R^{\mathsf{f},{\operatorname{NC}}}(J,d)/G_{d}

be the corresponding moduli space (a geometric quotient).
Then we define the partition function of
numerical NCDT invariants
,
or
unrefined NCDT partition function
, as the formal sum

(2.29)

Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}e(M^{\mathsf{f},{\operatorname{NC}}}(J,d),\nu)x^{d},

where
x^{d}=\prod_{i\in Q_{0}}x_{i}^{d_{i}}
, and
e(X,\nu)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}k\,e(\nu^{-1}(k))
is the
weighted Euler number of
X
, with
\nu\colon X\to\mathbb{Z}
the Behrend
function
[
17
]
.
We will discuss later more explicit formulas for this expression.
Note that if
X
is smooth, then
e(X,\nu)=(-1)^{\dim X}e(X)
.
Usually we will consider the basic framings
\mathsf{f}=e_{i}
for
i\in Q_{0}
, and then denote
Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}
by
Z_{i,\operatorname{NC}}
.

More generally, for any weight
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
,
consider the weight

(2.30)

\theta^{\mathsf{f}}=(\theta,-\theta\cdot d)

of the framed quiver.
It satisfies
\theta^{\mathsf{f}}\cdot(d,1)=0
.
A framed representation
M^{\mathsf{f}}
having dimension vector
(d,1)
is
\theta^{\mathsf{f}}
-semistable if and only if for any unframed subobject
N\subset M^{\mathsf{f}}
we have
\theta(N)\leq 0
and for any unframed quotient
M^{\mathsf{f}}\twoheadrightarrow N
, we have
\theta(N)\geq 0
.

Let us assume that
\theta\cdot d^{\prime}\neq 0
for all
0<d^{\prime}\leq d
, so that the inequalities above are strict.
Under this assumption the action of
G_{d}
on
R^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)=R^{\theta^{\mathsf{f}}}(J^{\mathsf{f}},d^{\mathsf{f}})
is free and we define the moduli space

(2.31)

M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)=R^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)/G_{d}

and the partition function of
framed numerical
DT invariants
(assuming that
\theta\cdot d\neq 0
for all
d\neq 0
)

(2.32)

Z_{\mathsf{f},\theta}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}e(M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d),\nu)x^{d}.

As before, we denote
Z_{e_{i},\theta}
for the basic framings by
Z_{i,\theta}
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
Q
be a quiver with one vertex and no loops.
The potential is necessarily trivial.
Consider a framing vector
\mathsf{f}\in\mathbb{N}
.
For any
d\in\mathbb{N}
, the space of framed stable representations

R^{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(J,d)=R^{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(Q,d)
is given by the set of epimorphisms
s\colon\mathbb{C}^{\mathsf{f}}\to\mathbb{C}^{d}
,
which is empty unless
d\leq\mathsf{f}
.
Taking the quotient by
G_{d}=\operatorname{GL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
, we obtain for
d\leq\mathsf{f}

the Grassmannian

(2.33)

M^{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(J,d)=\operatorname{Gr}(\mathsf{f},d).

Its dimension is equal to
d(\mathsf{f}-d)

and its Euler number is the binomial coefficient
\binom{\mathsf{f}}{d}
.
Therefore

(2.34)

Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}}(-1)^{\mathsf{f}d+d}\binom{\mathsf{f}}{d}x^{d}=(1-(-1)^{\mathsf{f}}x)^{\mathsf{f}}.

2.5.
Refined unframed invariants

In this section we will define refined invariants of the stacks
\mathcal{M}(J,d)
of representations of the Jacobian algebra
J=J(Q,W)
.
As we will see in the next section, these invariants can be used to compute framed numerical invariants defined in (
2.29
) and (
2.32
).

For any algebraic variety
X
,
let
P(X;y)
denote its virtual Poincaré polynomial (sometimes we will denote it by
[X]
, the motivic class of
X
). It is additive with respect to complements and, for a smooth projective variety
X
, equals

(2.35)

P(X;y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim X}\dim H^{i}(X,\mathbb{Q})\,(-y)^{i}.

In that case the Laurent polynomial
(-y)^{-\dim X}P(X;y)
is symmetric under
y\to 1/y
, and can be interpreted as a character of the
\operatorname{SU}(2)
-Lefschetz action on
H^{*}(X,\mathbb{C})
; if
X
is the moduli
space of some D-brane bound state localized in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then the Lefschetz action realizes the rotations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
If
X
is non-projective (but has pure Hodge structure), then
P(X;y)
is defined using cohomology with compact support,

(2.36)

P(X;y)=\sum_{i=0}^{\dim X}\dim H^{i}_{c}(X,\mathbb{Q})\,(-y)^{i},

and the Laurent polynomial
(-y)^{-\dim X}P(X;y)
need no longer be symmetric under
y\to 1/y
.
In either case, the specialization of
P(X;y)
at
y=1
gives the Euler number.
Note that for the affine plane
P(\mathbb{A}^{1};y)=P(\mathbb{P}^{1};y)-1=y^{2}

can be identified with the number
q
of points of
\mathbb{A}^{1}
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
We shall often omit the second argument and use
q
and
y^{2}
interchangeably.

For any global stack
[X/G]
(with an appropriate group
G
)
we define
P([X/G])=P(X)/P(G)
.
Note that this class usually no longer specializes to Euler numbers but rather
has a pole at
y=\pm 1
, due to the vanishing of

(2.37)

P(\operatorname{GL}_{n})=q^{n^{2}}(q^{-1})_{n},\qquad(q)_{n}=(q;q)_{n}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1-q^{i}),

at
q=y^{2}=1
.

Let us now assume that the weight
\theta
is such that
\theta\cdot d\neq 0
for all
d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
.
We have seen that in this case the action of
G_{d}
on
R^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)
is free.
Therefore we can replace the corresponding stack by the moduli space
M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)
and define the
partition function of
refined framed DT invariants
informally as

(2.38)

Z_{\mathsf{f},\theta}^{\mathrm{ref}}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(-y)^{\chi_{Q}(d,d)-\mathsf{f}\cdot d}P(M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d))\,x^{d}.

where
\chi_{Q}
is the Euler form defined in (
3.1
).
The partition function of numerical framed DT invariants
(
2.32
) is obtained by specialization at
y=1
.
In particular, we define
Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}^{\mathrm{ref}}=Z_{\mathsf{f},-\delta}^{\mathrm{ref}}
,
cf. (
2.26
).

Remark 2.6
.

The definition (
2.38
) is not precise. More rigorously, one needs to consider the exponential motivic class of the map
\omega_{d}\colon M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(Q,d)\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
(note that
M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(J,d)
is its critical locus) instead of the invariants used above. This technicality can be circumvented when the quiver has a cut
I\subset Q_{1}
(see §
2.1
), which is the case in all cases that we consider.
Note also that

(2.39)

\mathsf{f}\cdot d-\chi_{Q}(d,d)=\dim R^{\mathsf{f}}(Q,d)-\dim G_{d}=\dim M^{\mathsf{f},\theta}(Q,d).

Next we define unframed DT invariants.
Due to the fact that
G_{d}
on
R(J,d)
(or
R^{\theta}(J,d)
) generally acts non-freely,
we can only consider refined invariants for the corresponding stacks, and these invariants may not have a specialization at
y=1
. For trivial stability condition,
we define the generating function of
unframed refined invariants
(often called ‘stacky’ invariants) informally as

(2.40)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}\mathcal{A}_{0}(d,y)x^{d}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(-y)^{\chi_{Q}(d,d)}\frac{P(R(J,d))}{P(G_{d})}x^{d}.

Remark
2.6
again applies.
In the presence of a cut
I\subset Q_{1}
, we have a rigorous definition
[
82
]

(2.41)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(-y)^{\chi_{Q}(d,d)+2\gamma_{I}(d)}\frac{P(R(J_{I},d))}{P(G_{d})}x^{d},\qquad\gamma_{I}(d)=\sum_{(a\colon i\to j)\in I}d_{i}d_{j}

which will be useful for practical computations.

Example 2.7

(Quantum dilogarithm)
.

Let us consider a quiver
Q
with one vertex and no loops.
Its category of representations is equivalent to the category of vector spaces.
The corresponding partition function

(2.42)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{d^{2}}}{P(\operatorname{GL}_{d})}x^{d}=\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{(-q^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-d^{2}}}{(q^{-1})_{d}}x^{d}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{-q^{\frac{1}{2}}x}{q-1}\right)

is called the
quantum dilogarithm
, denoted by
\mathbb{E}(x)
. The plethystic exponential
\operatorname{Exp}
used here is defined in §
2.6
.

For any stability function
\mathsf{Z}
and any ray
\ell\subset\mathbb{C}
, we similarly define

(2.43)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)=\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)x^{d}=\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}(-y)^{\chi_{Q}(d,d)}\frac{P(R^{\mathsf{Z}}(J,d))}{P(G_{d})}x^{d}.

For any weight
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
, we define

(2.44)

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)=\sum_{\theta\cdot d=0}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(d,y)x^{d}=\sum_{\theta\cdot d=0}(-y)^{\chi_{Q}(d,d)}\frac{P(R^{\theta}(J,d))}{P(G_{d})}x^{d}.

Remark 2.8
.

The series
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)
and
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)
are related as follows.
Assume that
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\rho
and
\ell=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}z
for some
z=x+\mathbf{i}y\in\mathbb{C}
.
Then
\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell
if and only if
\operatorname{Im}(\mathsf{Z}(d)\overline{z})=0
.
This means that
\theta^{\prime}(d)=0
for
\theta^{\prime}=x\rho-y\theta
and we obtain

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)=\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)
.
Conversely, given a weight
\theta
, consider the stability function
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+i\delta
and the ray
\ell=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathbf{i}
.
Then
\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell
if and only if
\theta(d)=0
and
we obtain
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)=\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)
.

2.6.
Relation between framed and unframed invariants

In this section we will assume that the quiver
Q
is symmetric, meaning that the number of arrows
i\to j
is equal to the number of arrows
j\to i
for all
i,j\in Q_{0}
.
The general case will be considered later in §
3.4
after discussing
wall-crossing formulae.

First, let us introduce the
plethystic exponential

\operatorname{Exp}\colon\hat{}\mathbb{A}^{+}\to 1+\hat{}\mathbb{A}^{+}
, where
\hat{}\mathbb{A}^{+}
is the maximal ideal in

(2.45)

\hat{}\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{Q}(\;\!\!\!(y)\!\!\!\;)[\![x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]\!]

(here
\mathbb{Q}(\;\!\!\!(y)\!\!\!\;)
is the field of Laurent series).
It is a continuous map satisfying
\operatorname{Exp}(f+g)=\operatorname{Exp}(f)\operatorname{Exp}(g)

and defined on monomials by

(2.46)

\operatorname{Exp}(y^{k}x^{d})=\sum_{m\geq 0}y^{mk}x^{md}.

Generally, one has

(2.47)

\operatorname{Exp}(f)=\exp\left(\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{1}{m}f(y^{m},x_{1}^{m},\dots,x_{n}^{m})\right).

Its inverse is the
plethystic logarithm

(2.48)

\operatorname{Log}(f)=\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{\mu(m)}{m}\log\left(f(y^{m},x_{1}^{m},\dots,x_{n}^{m})\right).

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

Assuming that
Q
is symmetric, we define invariants
\Omega(d,y)
, called
integer DT invariants
, by factorizing the generating function of stacky invariants (
2.40
) as

(2.49)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}}\frac{\Omega(d,y)\,x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

Here
\Omega(d,y)
is a priori a rational function in
y
, but in all our examples it is a Laurent polynomial in
y
with integer coefficients. Note that the denominator
inside the plethystic exponential is sometimes chosen to be
y^{2}-1
or
y-1/y
in the literature,
leading to different conventions for the integer DT invariants.

For any vector
w\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, define the algebra homomorphisms

(2.50)

S_{w}\colon\hat{}\mathbb{A}\to\hat{}\mathbb{A},\qquad x^{d}\mapsto(-y)^{w\cdot d}x^{d}.

(2.51)

\bar{S}_{w}\colon\hat{}\mathbb{A}\to\hat{}\mathbb{A},\qquad x^{d}\mapsto(-1)^{w\cdot d}x^{d}.

Theorem 2.9

(See
[
82
,
86
,
81
]
)
.

Let
Q
be a symmetric quiver. Then

(2.52)

Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=S_{-\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}\frac{(y^{2\mathsf{f}\cdot d}-1)\Omega(d,y)\,x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

In particular, numerical NCDT invariants satisfy

(2.53)

Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=\bar{S}_{\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(-\sum_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}(\mathsf{f}\cdot d)\,\Omega(d,1)\,x^{d}\right).

Remark 2.10
.

As we explain in §
3.4
, these formulae follow by applying the semi-primitive wall-crossing formula,
since
Z_{\mathsf{f},\theta}^{\mathrm{ref}}
is equal to
1
for a stability parameter
\theta
with
\theta_{i}>0
for
i\in Q_{0}
.

Theorem 2.11

(See
[
82
,
86
]
)
.

Let
Q
be a symmetric quiver and let weight
\theta
be such that
\theta\cdot d\neq 0
for all

.
Then the partition function of refined framed DT invariants is given by

(2.54)

Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathsf{f},\theta}(x)=S_{-\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\\

.

The above formula implies that for

Therefore it is enough to determine the partition functions

Z_{i,\theta}(x)=Z_{e_{i},\theta}(x)
for

.

Example 2.13
.

Let
Q
be a quiver with one vertex and no loops.

,

\mathcal{A}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{x}{y^{-1}-y}\right)
.

Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=\bar{S}_{\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}(-\mathsf{f}\,x)=\bar{S}_{\mathsf{f}}(1-x)^{\mathsf{f}}=(1-(-1)^{\mathsf{f}}x)^{\mathsf{f}}.

3.
Wall-crossing formulas and attractor indices

For a symmetric quiver
(Q,W)
, the generating functions of framed invariants defined
in (
2.32
) (or their refined counterpart in (
2.38
)) can be deduced,
for any stability parameter
\theta
, from universal unframed (refined) invariants

\Omega(d,y)
obtained by factorizing the generating function of stacky invariants
as in (
2.49
).
Physically, this can be understood as the appearance or disappearance of BPS states bound to an infinitely heavy defect (also known as framed BPS states), as the phase of the central charge of the defect is varied
[
46
,
7
]
. In that case, the unframed invariants
\Omega(d,y)
count
BPS states with charge
d
far away from the defect, and do not experience wall-crossing by themselves. In contrast, for non-symmetric quivers, the unframed (refined) invariants do
depend on the stability parameter
\theta
, and their contributions to framed partition functions
no longer commute. In this section we review
the wall-crossing formulae which govern this dependence, and we explain how
framed and unframed invariants in any chamber can all be deduced from invariants in the
attractor chamber.

3.1.
Quantum affine space

Let
Q
be a quiver and
\chi_{Q}
be its Euler form,
defined on the lattice
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
by

(3.1)

\chi_{Q}(d,d^{\prime})=\sum_{i}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{i}-\sum_{a\colon i\to j}d_{i}d^{\prime}_{j}.

Consider the skew-symmetric form on
\Gamma

(3.2)

\left\langle d,d^{\prime}\right\rangle=\chi_{Q}(d,d^{\prime})-\chi_{Q}(d^{\prime},d).

Define the corresponding
quantum affine space
(or more precisely, its coordinate ring) to be

(3.3)

\mathbb{A}=\bigoplus_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}\mathbb{Q}(y)x^{d}

equipped with the multiplication

(3.4)

x^{d}\circ x^{d^{\prime}}=(-y)^{\left\langle d,d^{\prime}\right\rangle}x^{d+d^{\prime}}.

This algebra has a decreasing filtration according to the ‘height’
\delta\cdot d
,

(3.5)

F_{i}\mathbb{A}=\bigoplus_{\delta\cdot d\geq i}\mathbb{Q}(y)x^{d},\qquad\delta=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}},

and we consider the corresponding completion

(3.6)

\hat{}\mathbb{A}=\varprojlim_{i}\mathbb{A}/F_{i}\mathbb{A}\simeq\prod_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}}\mathbb{Q}(y)x^{d}

which we will also refer to as the quantum affine space.
All partition functions defined in the previous sections will be considered as elements of this algebra.

3.2.
Basic wall-crossing formulas

In section §
2.5
we constructed, for any stability function
\mathsf{Z}\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
and any ray
\ell\subset\mathbb{C}
, the generating function of stacky invariants

(3.7)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)=\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)x^{d}\in\hat{}\mathbb{A}.

depending on the stability function.
In particular, for any weight
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
, we consider

(3.8)

\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\delta,\qquad\delta=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}},

and
\ell=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathbf{i}
.
Then
\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell
if and only if
\theta\cdot d=0
and we denote
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)
by
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(x)
.
For
\theta=0
we obtain the trivial stability condition as all objects are automatically semistable.
We denote the series
\mathcal{A}_{0}(x)
by
\mathcal{A}(x)
.

Theorem 3.1

(Wall-crossing formula)
.

For any stability function
\mathsf{Z}
, we have

(3.9)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\ell}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x),

where the product runs over rays
\ell
in the upper half-plane ordered clockwise.
In particular, the right hand side is independent of the stability function
\mathsf{Z}
.

We can write the above formula more explicitly as follows.
For any
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
, we have

(3.10)

\mathcal{A}(\gamma,y)=\sum_{{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}\\
\alpha_{1}>_{Z}\dots>_{Z}\alpha_{n}\end{subarray}}}(-y)^{\sum_{i<j}\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle}\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(\alpha_{i},y).

where we write
\alpha<_{Z}\beta
if
\mu_{Z}(\alpha)<\mu_{Z}(\beta)
.
This formula implies that we can express recursively invariants
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma,y)
in terms of invariants
\mathcal{A}(\gamma,y)
.
Therefore, for two stability functions
\mathsf{Z},\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}
, we can express
invariants
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}}(\gamma,y)
in terms of invariants
\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma,y)
.
More precisely, we have

Theorem 3.2

(Joyce-Reineke formula)
.

Let
\mathsf{Z}
and
\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}
be two stability functions.
Given a tuple
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})
of vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and
1\leq k<n
, define
\alpha_{\leq k}=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{k}
,
\alpha_{>k}=\alpha_{k+1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}
and

s_{k}(\alpha)=\begin{cases}-1&\alpha_{k}\leq_{\mathsf{Z}}\alpha_{k+1}\text{ and }\alpha_{\leq k}>_{\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}}\alpha_{>k},\\
1&\alpha_{k}>_{\mathsf{Z}}\alpha_{k+1}\text{ and }\alpha_{\leq k}\leq_{\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}}\alpha_{>k},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

(3.11)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}^{\prime}}(\gamma,y)=\sum_{\gamma=\alpha_{1}+\dots+\alpha_{n}}\prod_{k=1}^{n-1}s_{k}(\alpha)\cdot(-y)^{\sum_{i<j}\left\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}\right\rangle}\cdot\prod_{i}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(\alpha_{i},y).

The above formula was proved for the trivial stability
\mathsf{Z}
in
[
94
]
and
in full generality in
[
60
]
.

3.3.
Stacky, Rational, Integer DT

We say that a stability function
\mathsf{Z}\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}
is
generic
if
\operatorname{Arg}\mathsf{Z}(d)=\operatorname{Arg}\mathsf{Z}(d^{\prime})
implies
d\parallel d^{\prime}
(hence
\left\langle d,d^{\prime}\right\rangle=0
).
Similarly, a weight
\theta\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is called
generic
if

\operatorname{Ker}\theta
has rank
1
(hence
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle
vanishes on
\operatorname{Ker}\theta\subset\Gamma
).

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\rho
be such that
\theta^{\perp}\cap\Gamma=0
, where
\theta^{\perp}=\left\{\left.\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}\ \right|\theta(\gamma)=0\right\}
(this means that
\theta(e_{i})\in\mathbb{R}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Z}
) and
\rho(e_{i})\in\mathbb{Q}_{>0}
for all
i\in Q_{0}
.
If
d,d^{\prime}
have equal phases, then
\theta(d)/\rho(d)=\theta(d^{\prime})/\rho(d^{\prime})
, hence
\theta(md)=\theta(nd^{\prime})
for
m=\rho(d^{\prime})
and
n=\rho(d)
.
This implies that
md=nd^{\prime}
, hence
d\parallel d^{\prime}
.
Therefore
\mathsf{Z}
is generic.
On the other hand, for any indivisible vector
d\in\Gamma
, consider the free abelian group
\Gamma/\mathbb{Z}d
and an injective homomorphism
\theta\colon\Gamma/\mathbb{Z}d\to\mathbb{R}
.
It induces a homomorphism
\theta\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\theta^{\perp}\cap\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}d
.
Therefore
\theta\colon\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
is generic.

Given a generic weight
\theta
, we define invariants
\Omega_{\theta}(d,y)
for
d\in\operatorname{Ker}\theta
,
called
integer DT invariants
, by the formula,
cf. (
2.49
)

(3.12)

\sum_{\theta(d)=0}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(d,y)x^{d}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{\theta(d)=0}\Omega_{\theta}(d,y)x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

Similarly, we define invariants
\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}(d,y)
for
d\in\operatorname{Ker}\theta
,
called
rational DT invariants
, by the formula

(3.13)

\sum_{\theta(d)=0}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(d,y)x^{d}=\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{\theta(d)=0}\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}(d,y)x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right)

or more explicitly

(3.14)

\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}(d,y)=(y^{-1}-y)\sum_{k\mid d}\frac{(-1)^{k-1}}{k}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(d/k,y).

These families of invariants are related by the formula

(3.15)

\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}(d,y)=\sum_{k\mid d}\frac{1}{k}\frac{y^{-1}-y}{y^{-k}-y^{k}}\Omega_{\theta}(d/k,y^{k}).

In
[
75
]
, this relation was interpreted physically as the effect of replacing Bose-Fermi statistics by Boltzmann statistics.
Assuming that specializations at
y=1
exist, we obtain, in the unrefined limit,

(3.16)

\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}(d,1)=\sum_{k\mid d}\frac{1}{k^{2}}\Omega_{\theta}(d/k,1).

Similarly, for a generic stability function
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\rho
and any dimension vector
d
, we define
integer DT invariants
and
rational DT invariants
by

(3.17)

\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)=\Omega_{\theta^{\prime}}(d,y),\qquad\overline{\Omega}_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)=\overline{\Omega}_{\theta^{\prime}}(d,y)

where the stability parameters
\theta^{\prime}
are chosen such that
\theta^{\prime}(d^{\prime})=\operatorname{Im}(\mathsf{Z}(d^{\prime})\bar{}\mathsf{Z}(d))
.
Equivalently, for any ray
\ell\subset\mathbb{C}
, we have

(3.18)

\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)x^{d}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right)=\exp\left(\frac{\sum_{\mathsf{Z}(d)\in\ell}\overline{\Omega}_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

3.4.
Expressing framed invariants in terms of unframed (general case)

In §
2.6
we described how generating functions of framed invariants (refined and numerical) can be obtained from the generating functions of unframed refined invariants in the case of symmetric quivers.
A similar result, albeit more complicated, can be formulated for arbitrary quivers.

Theorem 3.4

(See
[
82
,
86
,
81
]
)
.

For any framing vector
\mathsf{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, we have

(3.19)

Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)=S_{\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{A}(x)\circ S_{-\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{A}(x)^{-1},

where the inverse
(S_{-\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{A})^{-1}=S_{-\mathsf{f}}(\mathcal{A}^{-1})

is taken in the quantum affine plane
\hat{}\mathbb{A}
.

A more general result can be proved for any (generic) stability parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
.
One can uniquely decompose the series
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(x)\in\hat{}\mathbb{A}
in the form (cf. (
3.25
))

(3.20)

\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}_{\theta,+}\circ\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\circ\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-},

where
\mathcal{A}_{\theta,+},\mathcal{A}_{\theta},\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-}
are supported at
\gamma
satisfying respectively
\theta(\gamma)>0
,
\theta(\gamma)=0
,
\theta(\gamma)<0
.
More precisely, consider any stability function
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\rho
.
Then
\operatorname{Arg}\mathsf{Z}(\gamma)=\frac{\pi}{2}
if and only if
\theta(\gamma)=0
and
\operatorname{Arg}\mathsf{Z}(\gamma)<\frac{\pi}{2}
if and only if
\theta(\gamma)<0
.
We have

(3.21)

\mathcal{A}_{\theta,+}=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\operatorname{Arg}\ell>\frac{\pi}{2}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\theta}=\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell},\quad\ell=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathbf{i},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-}=\prod^{\curvearrowright}_{\operatorname{Arg}\ell<\frac{\pi}{2}}\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}.

The series
\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-}
counts objects with HN filtrations having slopes
<0
.
Equivalently, these are objects
M

such that
\theta(N)<0
for all subobjects
N\subset M
.

Theorem 3.5

(See
[
82
,
86
]
)
.

Let
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{Q_{0}}
be such that
\theta\cdot\gamma\neq 0
for all
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
.
Then for any framing vector
\mathsf{f}
we have

(3.22)

Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathsf{f},\theta}(x)=S_{\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-}(x)\circ\left(S_{-\mathsf{f}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta,-}(x)\right)^{-1}.

Remark 3.6
.

The relation (
3.22
) can be understood physically as follows: the spectrum of framed BPS states with stability
\theta
and charge
\gamma
is the union of all bound states between the infinitely
heavy defect defining the framing, and the unframed BPS states with charge
\gamma^{\prime}
such that

\theta(\gamma^{\prime})<\theta(\gamma)
. The formula (
3.22
) comes by applying the
semi-primitive wall-crossing formula to all such bound states
[
7
]
.

3.5.
Relation to wall-crossing structures

In this subsection we explain the relation to the wall-crossing structures studied in

[
67
,
51
]
.
Consider the complete quantum affine space
\hat{}\mathbb{A}
defined in (
3.6
).
Let
\mathfrak{g}

be the maximal ideal

\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}}\mathbb{Q}(y)x^{d}\subset\hat{}\mathbb{A}

and let
G=1+\mathfrak{g}\subset\hat{}\mathbb{A}
.
Then
\mathfrak{g}
is a pro-nilpotent Lie algebra and
G
is the associated pro-unipotent algebraic group such that
\exp\colon\mathfrak{g}\to G
is a bijection.
The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is graded by
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
, hence
\mathfrak{g}=\widehat{\bigoplus}_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}

with
\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}=\mathbb{Q}(y)x^{\gamma}
for
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}\backslash\left\{0\right\}
and zero otherwise, such that
[\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma^{\prime}}]\subset\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma+\gamma^{\prime}}
.
Importantly, we have

(3.23)

\left\langle\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\right\rangle=0\implies[\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma^{\prime}}]=0

The generating series
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}(x)
of stacky invariants corresponding to the trivial stability condition is an element
\mathcal{A}\in G
. Since a
wall-crossing structure
can be defined as an element of the pro-unipotent group
G

[
67
,
51
]
, we interpret the generating series
\mathcal{A}
as such a wall-crossing structure.

Similarly, the generating series
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
, for a weight
\theta\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}
, defines an element in
G
,
and the series
\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}
(up to the factor
y^{-1}-y
) can be interpreted as an element of the Lie algebra
\overline{\Omega}_{\theta}\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\exp(\overline{\Omega}_{\theta})=\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
.
We have seen that
\mathcal{A}_{\theta}
can be obtained from
\mathcal{A}
by a wall-crossing formula.
An alternative way to formulate this property is as follows.
We have a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{+}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{-},

(3.24)

\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{+}=\bigoplus_{\theta(\gamma)>0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{0}=\bigoplus_{\theta(\gamma)=0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{-}=\bigoplus_{\theta(\gamma)<0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}.

These are Lie subalgebras of
\mathfrak{g}
, which in turn define Lie subgroups
G_{\star}^{\theta}\subset G
for
\star=\left\{+,0,-\right\}
.
The map induced by multiplication

(3.25)

G^{\theta}_{+}\times G^{\theta}_{0}\times G^{\theta}_{-}\to G

is a bijection.
Therefore we have a projection
p^{\theta}\colon G\to G^{\theta}_{0}
.
The wall-crossing formula implies

(3.26)

\mathcal{A}_{\theta}=p^{\theta}(\mathcal{A}).

3.6.
Attractor DT invariants and the attractor tree formula

As reviewed above, the wall-crossing formulae allow to compute DT invariants
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(d,y)

for any stability function
\mathsf{Z}
once they are known for a particular stability function
\mathsf{Z}_{0}
(for all dimension vectors).
However, no choice of
\mathsf{Z}_{0}
appears to lead to simple answers (with the exception of the trivial stability for quivers without relations
[
1043.17010
]
).
As explained in the introduction, physics suggests that simplicity can be found by instead
adapting the stability condition to the dimension vector
d
, namely choosing the self-stability

\left\langle-,d\right\rangle

[
73
,
24
,
5
]
. More precisely,
we define the
attractor DT invariants
as

(3.27)

\Omega_{*}(d,y)=\Omega_{\theta_{d}}(d,y)

where
\theta_{d}
, called an
attractor stability
,
is a generic perturbation of the weight
\left\langle-,d\right\rangle
inside
d^{\perp}=\left\{\left.\theta\in\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}^{\vee}\ \right|\theta(d)=0\right\}
.
We define similarly the
rational attractor DT invariants

\overline{\Omega}_{*}(d,y)
and invariants
\mathcal{A}_{*}(d,y)
.
The collection of invariants
(\mathcal{A}_{*}(d,y))_{d}
is called the
initial data
of the wall-crossing structure
\mathcal{A}

[
67
,
51
]
.
It encodes the same information as the collection of attractor DT invariants
(\Omega_{*}(d,y))_{d}
.
More precisely, for any indivisible dimension vector
d
, we have

(3.28)

\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{A}_{*}(nd,y)x^{n}=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\overline{\Omega}_{*}(nd,y)x^{n}}{y^{-1}-y}\right)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\Omega_{*}(nd,y)x^{n}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

Theorem 3.7

(see
[
51
, Theorem 1.21]
)
.

Invariants

\Omega_{*}(d,y),\overline{\Omega}_{*}(d,y),\mathcal{A}_{*}(d,y)
are independent of the choice of a generic perturbation of
\left\langle-,d\right\rangle
.

Proof.

Let
d_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}d\cap\Gamma
be the indivisible vector and let
\theta=\left\langle-,d\right\rangle
.
Then the decomposition (
3.24
) has the form

(3.29)

\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{+}=\bigoplus_{\left\langle\gamma,d\right\rangle>0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{0}=\bigoplus_{\left\langle\gamma,d\right\rangle=0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma},\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{-}=\bigoplus_{\left\langle\gamma,d\right\rangle<0}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}.

We can further decompose

(3.30)

\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{0}=\mathfrak{g}^{\parallel}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\perp},

where
\mathfrak{g}^{\parallel}_{0}=\bigoplus_{\gamma\in\mathbb{N}d_{0}}\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}_{0}
involves the rest of the summands
\mathfrak{g}_{\gamma}
in
\mathfrak{g}^{\theta}_{0}
.
One can show that
\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}_{0}
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}^{\theta}
.
Therefore we have a group homomorphism
\Psi\colon G^{\theta}_{0}\to G^{\parallel}_{0}=\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\parallel}_{0})

with kernel
G^{\perp}_{0}=\exp(\mathfrak{g}^{\perp}_{0})
.
We claim that if we apply
\Psi
to
p^{\theta}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}_{\theta}\in G^{\theta}_{0}
, the corresponding component
\Psi(\mathcal{A}_{\theta})_{d}
in degree
d
is equal to
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}(d,y)
, where
\theta^{\prime}
is a generic perturbation of
\theta
inside
d^{\perp}
. Since
\theta^{\prime}
is a generic perturbation of
\theta
,
we can assume that
\theta(d^{\prime})>0
implies
\theta^{\prime}(d^{\prime})>0
for
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
.
Applying the decomposition (
3.25
) we can write

g=\mathcal{A}=g^{\theta}_{+}\cdot g^{\theta}_{0}\cdot g^{\theta}_{-}

and then
h=g^{\theta}_{0}=h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{+}\cdot h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}\cdot h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{-}
.
The support of
h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\pm}
is contained in
\theta^{\perp}
(in degrees
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
).
Indeed, if
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
is in the support of
h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{+}
, then
\theta^{\prime}(d^{\prime})>0
, hence
\theta(d^{\prime})\geq 0
.
Similarly for the support of
h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{-}
. Now by the uniqueness of the decomposition of
h
with respect to
\theta
, we conclude that
\theta(d^{\prime})=0
in both cases.
The support of
h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\pm}
also doesn’t intersect
(\theta^{\prime})^{\perp}\cap\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}d_{0}
.
Therefore
h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{\pm}\in G^{\perp}_{0}
and we obtain

\Psi(\mathcal{A}_{\theta})=\Psi(g^{\theta}_{0})=h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}
.
On the other hand
g=(g^{\theta}_{+}h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{+})h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}(h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{-}g^{\theta}_{-})
.
By our assumption on
\theta^{\prime}
, we have
g^{\theta}_{+}\in G^{\theta^{\prime}}_{+}
and
g^{\theta}_{-}\in G^{\theta^{\prime}}_{-}
(in degrees
0\leq d^{\prime}\leq d
), therefore
p_{\theta^{\prime}}(g)=h^{\theta^{\prime}}_{0}=\Psi(\mathcal{A}_{\theta})
(in degree
d
).
We conclude that

\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}(d,y)=p_{\theta^{\prime}}(g)_{d}=\Psi(\mathcal{A}_{\theta})_{d}
.
This proves that
\mathcal{A}_{\theta^{\prime}}(d,y)
is independent of the choice of a perturbation.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
Q
is an acyclic quiver, then the only self-stable objects are simple.
Moreover,

(3.31)

\Omega_{*}(d,y)=\begin{cases}1&d=e_{i}\text{ for some }i\in Q_{0},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Choose an order on
Q_{0}
such that
i\leq j
for any arrow
a\colon i\to j
.
Let
M
be a self-stable representation
and
j\in Q_{0}
be a maximal vertex for this ordering such that
M_{j}\neq 0
.
Then
M
has a simple subrepresentation
S_{j}
.
Assume that
d=\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}M
is supported not only at
j
.
Then there exists an arrow
a\colon i\to j
such that
d_{i}\neq 0
(otherwise
M
would be decomposable).
Therefore
\left\langle e_{j},d\right\rangle=\sum_{a\colon i\to j}d_{i}>0

and this contradicts to the assumption that
M
is self-stable.
Therefore
M\simeq S_{j}^{\oplus n}
for some
n\geq 1
.
By stability of
M
, we actually have
M\simeq S_{j}
hence
d=e_{j}
.

Now, consider a dimension vector
d

and a stability function
\mathsf{Z}=-\theta+\mathbf{i}\delta
, where
\theta
is a generic perturbation of
\left\langle-,d\right\rangle
and
\delta=(1,\dots,1)
.
If
M
is a
\mathsf{Z}
-semistable representation such that
\mu_{Z}(M)=\mu_{Z}(d)
, then
M
is supported at only one vertex
j\in Q_{0}
(and therefore
M\simeq S_{j}^{\oplus n}
for some
n\geq 1
) by the same argument as above.
This implies, for
\ell=\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\mathsf{Z}(d)
(cf. Example
2.7
)

(3.32)

\mathcal{A}_{\mathsf{Z},\ell}(x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{(-y)^{n^{2}}}{P(\operatorname{GL}_{n})}x_{j}^{n}=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{x_{j}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

Therefore
\Omega_{*}(d,y)=1
for
d=e_{j}
and zero otherwise.
∎

The above result can be generalized as follows.
Let us say that a quiver
Q
is
strongly connected
if, for any two vertices
i,j\in Q_{0}
, there exists a path from
i
to
j
.
For any
d\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
, let
\operatorname{supp}(d)=\left\{\left.i\in Q_{0}\ \right|d_{i}\neq 0\right\}
and let
Q|_{\operatorname{supp}(d)}
be the corresponding subquiver of
Q
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(Q,W)
be a quiver with potential,

d\in\mathbb{N}^{Q_{0}}
, and
\Omega_{*}(d,y)
be the corresponding attractor invariant.
If
Q|_{\operatorname{supp}(d)}
is not strongly connected,
then
\Omega_{*}(d,y)=0
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\mathcal{A}_{*}(d,y)=0
.
Let
M
be a representation having dimension vector
d
.
According to our assumption, there exists a decomposition
\operatorname{supp}(d)=A\sqcup B
such that
A,B\neq\varnothing
and there are no arrows from
A
to
B

Remark 3.13
.

For a compact CY 3-fold, the attractor index
\Omega_{*}(\gamma,y)
is expected to include contributions both from single-centered BPS black holes with charge
\gamma
, as well as
from ‘scaling configurations’ of BPS black holes with charge
\alpha_{i}
. These
scaling configurations correspond to boundaries of the phase space of multicentered
black hole solutions where the centers come arbitrarily close
[
19
]
and become
indistinguishable from single-centered black holes. In contrast, the r.h.s. of (
3.33
) incorporates compact components of the phase space which are free from such boundaries.
The Coulomb branch formula developed in
[
74
,
76
]
gives a prescription for disentangling these
scaling contributions from genuine single-centered black holes, counted by the ‘single-centered’ invariant
\Omega_{\rm S}(\gamma,y)
(also known as pure Higgs
[
19
]
or quiver invariant
[
68
]
). For symmetric quivers, the invariants
\Omega_{\rm S}(\gamma,y)
coincide with the
DT invariants
\Omega_{\mathsf{Z}}(\gamma,y)
, which are independent of the stability condition
\mathsf{Z}
.
We refer to
[
78
]
for a concise review of the
Coulomb branch formula, which plays a marginal role in the present work.

4.
Brane tilings and crystals

For a toric CY3-fold
\mathcal{X}
, the derived category of coherent sheaves
D^{b}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
is isomorphic
to the derived category of representations of a quiver
(Q,W)
. In physical parlance, the dynamics of D-branes wrapped on
\mathcal{X}
is described by a gauge theory with fields encoded in the quiver
Q
and superpotential
W
. For a certain class of toric CY3-folds,
(Q,W)
can be read off from a brane tiling,
and framed BPS states in the non-commutative chamber can be represented as molten configurations
of a crystal constructed from the brane tiling. In this section we review aspects of these well-known
facts.

4.1.
Basics of toric geometry

A
d
-dimensional toric variety
\mathcal{X}
is a complex manifold with an action of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{d}
having a dense open orbit. It may be decomposed into a set of complex tori
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{d-k}
(the orbits)
with
0\leq k\leq d
, associated to
k
-dimensional cones in a fan
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Let
(v_{1},\dots,v_{r})
be the set of indivisible vectors
in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
generating one-dimensional cones (corresponding to toric divisors in
\mathcal{X}
). For each of the
r-d
linear relations
q_{1}v_{1}+\dots+q_{r}v_{r}=0

among the
v_{i}

\mathbb{C}^{\times}
action on
\mathbb{C}^{r}

(4.1)

\lambda\cdot(z_{1},\dots z_{r})=(\lambda^{q_{1}}z_{1},\dots,\lambda^{q_{r}}z_{r})

Remark 4.1
.

Consider an exact sequence (we assume that
v
is surjective)

(4.2)

0\to L\to\mathbb{Z}^{r}\xrightarrow{v}N=\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to 0,\qquad v(e_{i})=v_{i}.

where
L
is the kernel of the matrix with columns
v_{i}
.
It induces a homomorphism of tori

L\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{C}^{\times}\simeq(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r-d}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r}
.
Therefore we have an action of
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r-d}
on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
.

The variety
\mathcal{X}

is then isomorphic to
(\mathbb{C}^{r}\backslash F_{\Sigma})/(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{r-d}
, where
F_{\Sigma}
is
the intersection of unions
\bigcup_{i\in I}\left\{z_{i}=0\right\}
for all subsets
I\subset\left\{1,\dots,r\right\}
such that
\left\{\left.v_{i}\ \right|i\notin I\right\}
spans a cone in
\Sigma
. More explicitly,

is the zero locus

(4.3)

F_{\Sigma}=Z(z^{\hat{}\sigma}\mathchar 58\sigma\in\Sigma),\qquad z^{\hat{}\sigma}=\prod_{i\mathchar 58v_{i}\notin\sigma}z_{i}.

The toric divisors
D_{i}=\{z_{i}=0\}
in
\mathcal{X}

satisfy the linear equivalence relations
\sum_{i}a_{i}D_{i}=0
whenever
a\cdot q\mathchar 58=\sum_{i}a_{i}q_{i}=0
for
q\in L
.
The canonical line bundle is equal to
K_{\mathcal{X}}=\mathcal{O}(-\sum_{i}D_{i})
.
If
\mathcal{X}
is Calabi-Yau, then the canonical divisor
-\sum_{i}D_{i}
is trivial, hence
\sum_{i}q_{i}=0
for all

.
This means that the vector
\rho=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
satisfies

, hence induces a linear map
\rho\colon N\to\mathbb{Z}
such that

for all

.
Hence all vectors
v_{i}
lie in the same hyperplane.
We project this hyperplane to
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
(for example, taking the first
d-1
coordinates)
and consider the corresponding vectors
\hat{v}_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
.
The toric diagram (defined up to
\operatorname{GL}(d-1,\mathbb{Z})
-transformations) is the convex hull
P
of the vectors
\left\{\left.\hat{v}_{i}\ \right|i=1\dots r\right\}
, triangulated by the restriction of the fan to
\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}
. Internal lattice points correspond to compact divisors in
\mathcal{X}
, while lattice points on the boundary correspond to non-compact divisors (subject to linear equivalences).
The virtual Poincaré polynomial of
\mathcal{X}
is obtained by summing up the Poincaré polynomials of the toric orbits,

(4.4)

P(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{k=0}^{d}n_{k}(q-1)^{d-k}

where

is the number of
k

\Sigma
(hence
n_{0}=1,n_{1}=r
).

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a smooth toric CY3-fold given by a convex toric diagram having
i
internal vertices and
b
boundary vertices. Then

(4.5)

P(\mathcal{X})=q\,(q^{2}+(i+b-3)q+i).

In particular, the virtual Poincaré polynomial is independent of the triangulation.

Proof.

By Pick’s theorem, the number of triangles in the diagram (all having area
{\frac{1}{2}}
) is equal to
2i+b-2
.
By Euler’s formula, the number of edges in the diagram is equal to
(i+b)+(2i+b-2)-1=3i+2b-3
.
Therefore

(4.6)

P(\mathcal{X})=(q-1)^{3}+(i+b)(q-1)^{2}+(3i+2b-3)(q-1)+(2i+b-2)

which simplifies to (
4.5
).
∎

, chiral fields

\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha}
in bifundamental representations
(N_{i},\overline{N_{j}})
, a gauge-invariant
holomorphic superpotential
W(\{\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha}\})
, and real Fayet-Iliopoulos parameters
\theta_{i}
for each
U(N_{i})
factor in
G
. The ranks
N_{i}
are the coefficients of the D-brane charge

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{r}N_{i}\gamma_{i}
on a basis of charges
\gamma_{i}\in H^{*}(\mathcal{X})

associated to a set of ‘elementary D-branes’,
and the net number of chiral fields
|\{\Phi_{ij}^{\alpha}\}|-|\{\Phi_{ji}^{\alpha}\}|
going from
i
to
j
is given by (minus) the skew-symmetrized Euler form

-\langle\gamma_{i},\gamma_{j}\rangle
.
The full BPS spectrum, for given stability parameters

\theta_{i}
, is then obtained as supersymmetric bound states of these elementary constituents, represented by BPS ground states of the quiver quantum mechanics.
In the presence of an infinitely heavy defect of charge
\gamma_{\mathsf{f}}
, such as a D6-brane wrapping
\mathcal{X}
or D4-branes wrapping non-compact divisors in
\mathcal{X}
, the quiver quantum mechanics obtains an additional gauge group
U(N_{\infty})
and arrows
\Phi_{\infty,i}^{\alpha},\Phi_{i,\infty}^{\alpha}
, and computes the
number of framed BPS states.

Mathematically, BPS grounds states are cohomology classes on the moduli space of
\theta
-semistable representations of the quiver with potential
(Q,W)
. The ‘elementary D-branes’,
or ‘fractional branes’ in the context of orbifolds, correspond to a
tilting sequence

T=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}T_{i}
in the derived category of coherent sheaves
D^{b}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
, such that
T_{i}
generate

D^{b}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
and
\operatorname{Ext}^{k}(T,T)=0
for
k\neq 0
.
When
\mathcal{X}
is the total space of the canonical bundle on a complex surface
S
,
a tilting sequence
T
can be constructed by lifting a strong exceptional collection of line bundles
on
S

[
55
,
11
]
. Note however that the lifted sequence need not be exceptional, in particular
\operatorname{End}(T_{i})=\Gamma(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}})
may have dimension
>1
.
The triangulated category
D^{b}(\operatorname{coh}\mathcal{X})
is then equivalent to the category of representations of the Jacobian algebra
J(Q,W)
for a quiver with potential
(Q,W)
associated to
T

[
10
,
9
]
.

For a wide class of toric CY threefolds, the construction of the tilting sequence

T
can be by-passed and the
quiver
(Q,W)
can be read off from a
brane tiling

[
53
,
45
]
. The latter is a bipartite graph
G
embedded in a
2
-dimensional (real) torus
\mathcal{T}
, or equivalently
a periodic bipartite graph
\tilde{G}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Each vertex carries a color, black or white,
such that edges connect only vertices with different colors. The quiver
Q
is then the dual graph of
G
: the vertices
i\in Q_{0}
correspond to faces of
G
(i.e. the connected components of
\mathcal{T}\backslash G
) and the arrows
a\colon i\to j\in Q_{1}
to edges common to
faces
i
and
j
. The arrows are oriented so that they go clockwise around white vertices of
G
and go anti-clockwise around black vertices of
G
.

Let
Q_{2}
be the set of connected components of
\mathcal{T}\backslash Q
, or equivalently the set of vertices of
G
.
Let
Q_{2}^{+}
and
Q_{2}^{-}
correspond to the sets of white and black vertices of
G
.
For any face
F\in Q_{2}
, let
w_{F}
be the cycle obtained by going along the arrows of
F
(defined up to a cyclic shift). The potential
W
is then

(4.11)

W=\sum_{F\in Q_{2}^{+}}w_{F}-\sum_{F\in Q_{2}^{-}}w_{F}.

Note that
|Q_{0}|-|Q_{1}|+|Q_{2}|=0
, where
|Q_{i}|
denotes the cardinality of the set
Q_{i}
,
since the Euler number of the two-dimensional torus vanishes.

Conversely, starting with a quiver
(Q,W)
with
|Q_{0}|-|Q_{1}|+|Q_{2}|=0
, it is straightforward to reconstruct the brane tiling: for every term
w
in
W
, we construct a polygon with edges corresponding to the arrows of
w
and orientation depending on the sign of
w
.
Then we glue these polygons along equal edges and obtain a torus with an embedded quiver.
Considering the dual graph, we obtain the required bipartite graph embedded in a torus.
However, not all quivers corresponding to toric CY3 singularities satisfy the
torus condition
|Q_{0}|-|Q_{1}|+|Q_{2}|=0
, see e.g.
[

]
for some
counter-examples.

Example 4.5
.

Let
\Gamma\subset\operatorname{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
be an abelian group.
The corresponding
3
-dimensional representation of
\Gamma
can be decomposed as
\mathbb{C}^{3}=\rho_{1}\oplus\rho_{2}\oplus\rho_{3}
, where
\rho_{i}
are

\Gamma

The set of
\Gamma

Then

\rho_{1}\rho_{2}\rho_{3}=1

The corresponding Mckay quiver has the set of vertices

and arrows

for all

and

.
For any

and a permutation

(4.12)

\rho=\rho\rho_{\pi(1)}\rho_{\pi(2)}\rho_{\pi(3)}\to\rho\rho_{\pi(1)}\rho_{\pi(2)}\to\rho\rho_{\pi(1)}\to\rho

The potential

is the sum over all such cycles (up to a cyclic shift), weighted with

\operatorname{sgn}(\pi)

This implies that

Conversely, starting from the brane tiling, one may recover both the toric diagram of
\mathcal{X}
and
the tilting sequence
[

52
,

,
20

. In particular,
periodic perfect matchings are in correspondence with integer points on the toric diagram

correspondence is many-to-one except for corner points of

, which correspond to a single perfect
matching
[

]
. More directly, one reconstruct the full toric CY3-fold

from the moduli space of D0-branes, as we discuss next.

4.3.
\Lambda
-grading and torus action

In order to analyze Jacobian algebras associated to brane tilings,
consider the chain complex
\mathcal{C}

of abelian groups
[
88
]

(4.13)

\dots\to 0\to\mathbb{Z}Q_{2}\xrightarrow{d_{2}}\mathbb{Z}Q_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}\mathbb{Z}Q_{0}\to 0\to\dots,

where
d_{2}(F)=\sum_{a\in F}a
for
F\in Q_{2}
and
d_{1}(a)=t(a)-s(a)
for
a\in Q_{1}
.
Its homology
H_{*}(\mathcal{C})
is isomorphic to
H_{*}(\mathcal{T},\mathbb{Z})
.
The quotient

(4.14)

\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}Q_{1}/(d_{2}(F)-d_{2}(F^{\prime})\mathchar 58F,F^{\prime}\in Q_{2})

is a free abelian group of rank
|Q_{0}|+2
.
The projection

(4.15)

\operatorname{wt}\colon\mathbb{Z}Q_{1}\to\Lambda,

which we will call the
weight function
,
induces a
\Lambda
-grading on the path algebra
\mathbb{C}Q
.
For any arrow
a\in Q_{1}
, we have
\frac{\partial W}{\partial a}=\frac{\partial w_{F}}{\partial a}-\frac{\partial w_{F^{\prime}}}{\partial a}
, where
F,F^{\prime}
are two faces that contain
a
.
Note that these derivatives have the same weight in
\Lambda
.
Therefore the ideal generated by
\frac{\partial W}{\partial a}
, for
a\in Q_{1}
, is homogeneous with respect to the
\Lambda
-grading and the Jacobian algebra
J=J(Q,W)
inherits the
\Lambda
-grading.
This implies that the space of representations
R(J,d)
is equipped with an action of the complex torus
T_{\Lambda}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{\times})

(4.16)

t\cdot(M_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}}=(t^{a}M_{a})_{a\in Q_{1}},\qquad t^{a}=t(\operatorname{wt}(a)).

Starting from a brane tiling and the corresponding Jacobian algebra
J=J(Q,W)
,
one may obtain the singular toric CY3
\mathcal{X}
as the moduli space
M(J,\delta)=R(J,\delta)/\!\!/G_{\delta}
,
for the dimension vector
\delta=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}

(physically, this is the moduli space for a single D0-brane).
This is an affine variety with the coordinate ring
\mathbb{C}[M(J,\delta)]=\mathbb{C}[R(J,\delta)]^{G_{\delta}}
isomorphic to the center of
J
.
Note that
G_{\delta}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{Q_{0}}
and the moduli space
M(J,\delta)
is equipped with an action of the torus
T_{\Lambda}/G_{\delta}
.
This torus is isomorphic to
T_{M}=\operatorname{Hom}(M,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
,
where
M=\operatorname{Ker}(d_{1}\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{Z}Q_{1})
is a rank
3
lattice.

To see that
M(J,\delta)
is a 3-dimensional toric variety
with an action of the torus
T_{M}
, we need to describe an open toric orbit in
M(J,\delta)
.
Consider the open subset
T^{\prime}=R(J,\delta)\cap(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{Q_{1}}\subset R(J,\delta)
(which is a torus).
Its points can be identified with maps
x\colon Q_{1}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
\prod_{a\in F}x(a)
is independent of
F\in Q_{2}
.
We obtain a group homomorphism
x\colon\mathbb{Z}Q_{1}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
such that
x(d_{2}(F))=x(d_{2}(F^{\prime}))
for all
F,F^{\prime}\in Q_{2}
.
Hence we can identify
x
with a homomorphism
x\colon\Lambda\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and therefore we obtain
T^{\prime}=T_{\Lambda}
.
Taking the quotient by
G_{\delta}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{Q_{0}}
we obtain the 3-dimensional torus

T_{\Lambda}/G_{\delta}\simeq T_{M}
inside
M(J,\delta)
.

Note that
M(J,\delta)
is generally a singular
3
-dimensional toric CY variety. By considering moduli spaces

(4.17)

M^{\theta}(J,\delta)=R^{\theta}(J,\delta)/G_{\delta}

for generic stability parameters
\theta
, one obtains crepant resolutions of the variety
M(J,\delta)
,
which are still toric since the torus
T_{M}
is also contained in these moduli spaces.
One can construct the tilting bundle
T=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}T_{i}
on
X=M^{\theta}(J,\delta)
as follows
(see e.g.
[
20
]
).
The diagonal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\subset G_{\delta}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{Q_{0}}
acts trivially on
R^{\theta}=R^{\theta}(J,\delta)

and we have a free action of
G_{\delta}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}
on
R^{\theta}
.
Let us fix a vertex
i_{0}\in Q_{0}
.
For any vertex
i\in Q_{0}
, we equip the trivial line bundle
L_{i}=R^{\theta}\times\mathbb{C}
with an action of
G_{\delta}
given by

(4.18)

t\cdot(M,c)=(t\cdot M,t_{i}t_{i_{0}}^{-1}c),

where
t\cdot M
denotes the standard action of
G_{\delta}
on
R^{\theta}(J,\delta)
.
Then the diagonal
\mathbb{C}^{\times}\subset G_{\delta}
acts trivially on
L_{i}
and we have a free action of
G_{\delta}/\mathbb{C}^{\times}
on
L_{i}
.
Taking the quotients, we obtain a line bundle

(4.19)

T_{i}=L_{i}/G_{\delta}\to R^{\theta}/G_{\delta}=M^{\theta}(J,\delta)

over
M^{\theta}(J,\delta)
.
Then
T=\bigoplus_{i\in Q_{0}}T_{i}
is the required tilting bundle (it is the universal family over
M^{\theta}(J,\delta)

[
64
]
)
and induces an equivalence of derived categories
[
101
]

(4.20)

D^{b}_{c}(\operatorname{coh}X)\simeq D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}J),\qquad F\mapsto\operatorname{RHom}(T,F).

For this reason the Jacobian algebra
J
is
called a non-commutative crepant resolution of
M(J,\delta)
.

4.4.
Crystals

In the previous section we introduced the weight function

\operatorname{wt}\colon\mathbb{Z}Q_{1}\to\Lambda
(
4.15
) which equips the Jacobian algebra
J=J(Q,W)
with a
\Lambda
-grading.
Under certain consistency condition on the brane tiling (satisfied in all our examples), this grading can be used to equip framed moduli spaces of
J
-representations with a torus action and parametrize torus-fixed points (and therefore, as we recall below, framed BPS states in the non-commutative chamber) as molten crystals or pyramids
[
88
,

]
.

Under the above-mentioned consistency conditions,
it was proved in
[
88
]
that two paths

in

induce equal elements in

if and only if
\operatorname{wt}(u)=\operatorname{wt}(v)
(we will say in this case that
u,v
are equivalent).
Moreover, any nontrivial path has a nonzero weight.
For any path

, the source of

Note that if

Let

be the set of equivalence classes of paths

It is a basis of the projective

We define a partial order on

by the condition that

if there exists a path

such that

A subset

and

imply

.
In physics literature the poset

is called a

is called an

and the target

is called the

The complement
\Delta_{i}\backslash\mathcal{I}
of a (finite) ideal

is called a

.

Note that we have an embedding

(4.21)

\operatorname{wt}\colon\Delta_{i}\hookrightarrow\Lambda.

Therefore we can parametrize atoms by their weights in
\Lambda
and interpret the crystal

as a subset of the lattice
\Lambda\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{\left\lvert Q_{0}\right\rvert+2}

4.5.
NCDT and molten crystals

In (
2.29
) we defined the partition function
Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(x)
of NCDT invariants as a generating function of invariants of the moduli spaces
M^{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(J,d)
of
\operatorname{NC}
-stable framed representations of the Jacobian algebra.
The moduli space
M^{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}(J,d)
is equipped with an action of the torus
T_{\Lambda}=\operatorname{Hom}(\Lambda,\mathbb{C}^{\times})
.
For the framing vector
\mathsf{f}=e_{i}
, the torus fixed points
are parametrized by finite ideals
\mathcal{I}\subset\Delta_{i}
and the partition function has a simple form
[
88
]

(4.27)

Z_{i,\operatorname{NC}}(x)=\sum_{\mathcal{I}\subset\Delta_{i}}(-1)^{d_{i}+\chi(d,d)}x^{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\mathcal{I}},

where
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\mathcal{I}=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{I}}e_{t(u)}\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
is the dimension vector of the representation corresponding to the ideal
\mathcal{I}
.
In this way we obtain an interpretation of the partition function of NCDT invariants as counting molten crystals (with signs).
By Theorem
2.9
we have (for symmetric quivers)

(4.28)

Z_{i,\mathrm{NC}}(x)=\bar{S}_{e_{i}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(-\sum_{d}d_{i}\,\Omega(d,1)\ x^{d}\right).

Let us define the operator

(4.29)

\bar{T}\colon x^{d}\mapsto(-1)^{\chi(d,d)}x^{d}.

Then the generating function counting melting crystals in
\Delta_{i}
can be written as

(4.30)

Z_{\Delta_{i}}(x)=\sum_{\mathcal{I}\subset\Delta_{i}}x^{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\mathcal{I}}=\bar{S}_{e_{i}}\bar{T}Z_{i,\mathrm{NC}}(x)=\bar{T}\operatorname{Exp}\left(-\sum_{d}d_{i}\,\Omega(d,1)\ x^{d}\right).

Remark 4.8

(Algorithms)
.

The above partition function can be computed using the following algorithm,
which works for any subposet (or subcrystal)
P\subset\Delta_{i}
.
For simplicity we assume that all cycles in
W
have the same length.
We start with the set
P_{0}=\left\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\right\}
of minimal elements in
P
.
Then we apply all admissible arrows to all elements in
P_{0}
and obtain the set
P_{1}
of elements having height
\leq 1
.
Assuming that we constructed the set
P_{k}
of elements having height
\leq k
, we apply all admissible arrows to the elements of
P_{k}\backslash P_{k-1}
and obtain the set
P_{k+1}
of all elements having height
\leq k+1
in
P
.
In order to find the terms of the partition function up to degree
n
, we need only ideals
\mathcal{I}\subset P
having
\leq n
elements.
Any element of such semi-ideal has height
\leq n-1
, hence it is contained in
P_{n}
.
This means that we just need to find all ideals (of size
\leq n
) in the finite poset
P_{n}\subset P
.
This is done using standard algorithms for finding all ideals in finite posets.
See
https://github.com/smzg/msinvar
for an implementation.
An alternative algorithm based on the Quiver Yangian of
[
71
]
has been
developed by the second-named author
[
93
]
and is included in the Mathematica package
CoulombHiggs.m
, see Remark

3.14
.

The above partition function of (numerical) NCDT invariants is itself an important object associated with the Jacobian algebra.
Later we will study partition functions of unframed refined invariants of Jacobian algebra.
Having these refined partition functions, we can determine
Z_{\mathsf{f},\operatorname{NC}}
using the results of §
2.6
and §
3.4
.
Then we can compare the result with the direct computation obtained by applying the algorithms in Remark
4.8
.

5.
BPS indices for small crepant resolutions

In this section we will consider small crepant resolutions of affine singular CY3 varieties, meaning that the exceptional locus of a resolution has dimension
\leq 1
.
This implies that compactly supported sheaves on a resolution have support of dimension
\leq 1
.
In all our examples, we will actually study Jacobian algebras
J(Q,W)
that are non-commutative crepant resolutions of their centers. Moreover, an important feature of all small crepant resolutions
is that the quiver
Q
is symmetric.
Therefore the wall-crossing formula translates to the fact that integer DT invariants
\Omega(d,y)
are independent of the stability parameter and satisfy
(see cf. (
2.49
))

(5.1)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{d}\Omega(d,y)x^{d}}{y^{-1}-y}\right),

where
\mathcal{A}(x)
is the generating function of stacky unframed invariants corresponding to the trivial stability. Our goal will be to compute this generating function explicitly (or review existing results
in the literature)
and determine
the DT invariants
\Omega(d,y)=\Omega_{*}(d,y)=\Omega_{\rm S}(d,y)
.
In this way we also shall get access
to the partition functions of framed invariants (refined and numerical) as explained in §
2.6
.

5.1.
Invariants of
\mathbb{C}^{3}

We consider the quiver
Q
with one vertex, three loops
x,y,z
and potential
W=xyz-xzy
.
The corresponding Jacobian algebra is
J(Q,W)=\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
.

The following result for unframed stacky invariants of
\mathbb{C}^{3}
was obtained in
[
18
]
.
The authors actually study the refined NCDT invariants, but the refined unframed invariants written below can be also obtained from their proof.
For another proof of the formula see
[
85
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

The generating function of (unframed) stacky invariants for
\mathbb{C}^{3}
is

(5.2)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{q^{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}}{q-1}\right)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{-y^{3}\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}}{y^{-1}-y}\right).

This implies that the corresponding DT invariants are

(5.3)

\Omega(n,y)=-y^{3}=(-y)^{-3}\cdot P(\mathbb{C}^{3};y),\qquad n\geq 1.

This is the virtual motive of
\mathbb{C}^{3}

[
18
]
(not to be confused with the virtual Poincaré polynomial

P(\mathbb{C}^{3};y)=y^{6}
).
Physically,
\Omega(n,y)
counts bound states of
n
D0-branes on
\mathbb{C}^{3}
. The fact that it does not depend on
n
is the basic property which allows to view D0-branes as Kaluza-Klein gravitons in M-theory
[
102
]
. We note that (
5.3
) agrees in the unrefined limit with an independent computation
[
13
]
based on exponential networks, which provide a dual representation of BPS states as D3-branes wrapped on special Lagrangian cycles in the mirror CY 3-fold
[
41
]
. It would be interesting to refine
the computations of
[
13
]
so as to include spin dependence, along the lines of
[
47
]
.

Remark 5.2
.

One has a similar formula for counting (unframed) D0 invariants of a smooth CY3-fold
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}

[
18
]

(5.4)

\mathcal{A}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{X}},0}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{q^{-1}[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}]\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}}{q-1}\right)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{(-y)^{-3}[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}]\sum_{n\geq 1}x^{n}}{y^{-1}-y}\right),

where
[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}]=P(\widetilde{\mathcal{X}};y)
is the virtual Poincaré polynomial defined in (

In particular,
let
\mathcal{X}
be a singular toric CY3 variety arising from a brane tiling or the corresponding quiver with potential
(Q,W)
.
Let
\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}
be a crepant resolution of
\mathcal{X}
and
J=J(Q,W)
be the Jacobian algebra.
Then we have an equivalence of derived categories

so that the class of
n
D0-branes on

is mapped to the dimension vector

, where
\delta=(1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
.
This dimension vector is contained in the kernel of the skew-symmetric form, hence the DT invariants

are independent of a (generic) stability parameter
\theta
and, in particular, coincide with the attractor invariant
\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)
.
We obtain from the above formula that

(5.5)

\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)=(-y)^{-3}[\widetilde{\mathcal{X}}],

Applying Lemma
4.2
, we obtain

(5.6)

\Omega_{*}(n\delta,y)=-y^{-1}(y^{4}+(i+b-3)y^{2}+i),

where
i
and

are respectively the numbers of internal and boundary lattice points in the toric diagram of
\mathcal{X}
.

According to §

,
for any framing vector
\mathsf{f}\in\mathbb{N}
,
we have

(5.7)

Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathsf{f},\mathrm{NC}}(x)=S_{-\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{(y^{2\mathsf{f}n}-1)\Omega(n,y)\,x^{n}}{y^{-1}-y}\right)=S_{-\mathsf{f}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{y^{4}(y^{2\mathsf{f}n}-1)x^{n}}{y^{2}-1}\right).

In particular, for
\mathsf{f}=1
, we have (cf.
[
18
]
)

(5.8)

\displaystyle Z^{\mathrm{ref}}_{\mathrm{NC}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{-1}\left(\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{q^{2}(q^{n}-1)x^{n}}{q-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{-1}\left(\prod_{n\geq 1}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k+2}x^{n})^{-1}\right)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\prod_{k=0}^{n-1}(1-q^{k+2-n/2}(-x)^{n})^{-1}.

The partition function of numerical NCDT invariants is given by specialization at
q=1

(5.9)

Z_{\mathrm{NC}}(-x)=\prod_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{(1-x^{n})^{n}}

which is the MacMahon function.
According to (

), it can be also computed by counting molten crystals

Z_{\mathrm{NC}}(-x)=\sum_{\mathcal{I}\subset\Delta_{1}}x^{\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\mathcal{I}},

where the crystal is

(see Example

),
ideals

are plane partitions and
\operatorname{\mathbf{dim}}\mathcal{I}=\left\lvert\mathcal{I}\right\rvert
.
It is a theorem of MacMahon that the generating function of plane partitions is given by

postulates a different deformation of the generating series (

5.2.
Invariants of
\mathbb{C}^{2}/G\times\mathbb{C}

Let
G\subset\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be a finite subgroup and
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{C}^{2}/G\times\mathbb{C}
.
The corresponding McKay quiver is isomorphic to the double quiver (meaning that we add opposite arrows) of a quiver
Q
with an underlying diagram being an extended Dynkin diagram of type
\tilde{A}_{n}
for

,
\tilde{D}_{n}

or
\tilde{E}_{n}
for

Let

be the

for arrows

for

.
The potential on
\hat{Q}
is given by

(5.11)

W=\sum_{(a\colon i\to j)\in Q_{1}}(\ell_{j}aa^{*}-\ell_{i}a^{*}a).

Then the Jacobian algebra

is Morita equivalent to the skew group algebra

G
acts on the coordinates

Therefore the derived category
D^{b}(\operatorname{mod}J)
is equivalent to the derived category of
G

.

In oder to write down the formula for unframed stacky invariants, we need to recall the root systems of affine type.
Let
Q
be of type
\tilde{X}_{N-1}=\tilde{A}_{N-1}
,
\tilde{D}_{N-1}
or
\tilde{E}_{N-1}
.
Let
\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}_{+}
be the set of positive finite roots of type

and

\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}=\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}_{+}\cup(-\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}_{+})
be the set of all finite roots.
Let
\delta
be the indivisible imaginary root.
Then the positive real roots of type
\tilde{X}_{N-1}
are

(5.12)

\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}=\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}_{+}\cup\left\{\left.\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}+n\delta\ \right|n\geq 1\right\}

while the positive imaginary roots are
\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}=\left\{\left.n\delta\ \right|n\geq 1\right\}
.
We define
\Delta_{+}=\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{re}}\cup\Delta_{+}^{\mathrm{im}}
.

Remark 5.3
.

In type
\tilde{A}_{N-1}
we can also describe real roots as follows.
Let
\alpha_{0},\dots,\alpha_{N-1}
be simple roots (so that
\delta=\alpha_{0}+\dots+\alpha_{N-1}
).
For any

.
Then
\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}=\left\{\left.\alpha_{ij}\ \right|j\neq i-1\right\}+\mathbb{N}\delta
.

We can identify the root lattice of type
\tilde{X}_{N-1}
and the lattice
\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{Q_{0}}
.
Under this identification positive roots correspond to dimension vectors of indecomposable
Q
-representations.
The following result was proved in
[
85
]
(see also
[
25
]
for the

case).

Theorem 5.4
.

The generating function of unframed stacky invariants for
J(\hat{Q},W)
is

(5.13)

\mathcal{A}(x)=\operatorname{Exp}\left(\frac{\sum_{d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}}qx^{d}+q(q+N-1)\sum_{d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}}x^{d}}{q-1}\right).

Note that for the trivial group
G
(hence

) we obtain Theorem
5.1
.
The above theorem implies that the corresponding DT invariants are

(5.14)

\Omega(d,y)=\begin{cases}-y&d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}\\
-y(y^{2}+N-1)&d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

The imaginary roots correspond to bound states of
n
D0-branes, with
\Omega(n\delta)=(-y)^{-3}P(\widetilde{}\mathcal{X})
. Real roots instead correspond to D2-D0 bound states, with DT invariants
\Omega(d,y)

interpreted as refined Gopakumar-Vafa invariants
[
50
,
27
]
.
For
N=2
, the result (
5.14
) agrees in the unrefined limit with independent computation based on exponential networks
[
15
]
.

Applying the results of §
2.6
, we can determine partition functions of framed invariants for any framed vector
\mathsf{f}
.
In particular, for
\mathsf{f}=e_{0}
(corresponding to the extended vertex as well as the trivial representation of

),
we obtain numerical NCDT invariants (
2.53
)

(5.15)

Z_{0,\mathrm{NC}}(x)=\bar{S}_{e_{0}}\operatorname{Exp}\left(\sum_{d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{re}}_{+}}d_{0}x^{d}+N\sum_{d\in\Delta^{\mathrm{im}}_{+}}d_{0}x^{d}\right).

This implies

(5.16)

Z_{0,\mathrm{NC}}(-x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})=\prod_{n\geq 1}\left((1-x^{n\delta})^{N}\prod_{d\in\Delta^{\mathsf{f}}}(1-x^{d+n\delta})\right)^{-n}.

According to §
4.5

Equation (

6.
Attractor indices for local surfaces

In this section we study attractor invariants of some local surfaces (line bundles over smooth projective surfaces). As explained in §
4
, they arise as crepant resolutions of affine toric CY3 varieties associated to brane tilings.
In contrast to the previous section, these crepant resolutions are not small, so there are exceptional divisors (corresponding to internal points of the toric diagram), hence two-dimensional compact subvarieties.
On the algebraic side the problem becomes significantly more difficult since the quiver is no
longer symmetric and therefore the quantum affine plane is not commutative.
Instead of the DT invariants
\Omega(d,y)
considered in the symmetric case (independent of a stability parameter), in the non-symmetric case we will study the attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{*}(d,y)
.
It was conjectured in
[
16
]
that they have a particularly nice behavior, namely they vanish unless
d=e_{i}
or
d
is contained in the kernel of the skew-symmetric form
\left\langle-,-\right\rangle
.
In this section we will compute attractor DT invariants
\Omega_{*}(d,y)
explicitly for small
d
.
They turn out to have a particularly simple form, which suggests
a natural conjecture for the value of
\Omega_{*}(d,y)
for arbitrary dimension vectors.
As explained in §
3
, having a general formula for attractor DT invariants, we can compute
DT invariants (both framed and unframed) for any stability parameter. In particular, we shall
compute the framed DT invariants in the non-commutative chamber, and find agreement with
the counting of molten crystals in the unrefined limit.

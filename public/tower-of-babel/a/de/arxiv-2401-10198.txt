Polar multiplicities and integral dependence
Yairon Cid-Ruiz

Abstract
. We provide new criteria for the integrality and birationality of an extension of graded algebras in terms of the general notion of polar multiplicities of Kleiman and Thorup. As an application, we obtain a new criterion for when a module is a reduction of another in terms of certain mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities. Furthermore, we prove several technical results regarding polar multiplicities.

1.
Introduction

The main goal of this paper is to provide multiplicity-based criteria for when an inclusion of graded algebras is integral.
Our criteria are expressed in terms of the general notion of
polar multiplicities
of Kleiman and Thorup
[
14
,
15
]
.

Ever since the seminal work of Rees
[
20
]
, the search for numerical criteria to characterize
integral dependence
has been an important research topic in algebraic geometry, commutative algebra and singularity theory.
Rees showed that, in an equidimensional and universally catenary Noetherian local ring
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
, two
{\mathfrak{m}}
-primary ideals
I\subseteq J
have the same integral closure if and only if they have the same Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity.
Considerable effort has been made to extend this classical result to the case of arbitrary ideals, modules, and, more generally, algebras (see
[
14
,
15
,
25
,
24
,
3
,
10
,
13
,
12
,
19
]
).
Notably, a complete extension of Rees’ theorem for arbitrary ideals was recently obtained by Polini, Trung, Ulrich and Validashti
[
19
]
in terms of the multiplicity sequence of Achilles and Manaresi
[
1
]
.
When
R=\mathcal{O}_{X,0}
and
(X,0)\subset(\mathbb{C}^{N},0)
is a reduced closed analytic subspace of pure dimension, this extension to arbitrary ideals was previously settled by Gaffney and Gassler
[
12
]
in terms of Segre numbers.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}})
be a Noetherian local ring and
A\subseteq B
be a homogeneous inclusion of standard graded algebras over
R=A_{0}=B_{0}
.
Let
X=\text{Proj}(B)
,
Y=\text{Proj}(A)
and
r=\dim(X)
, and consider the associated morphism

f\;:\;U\subseteq X\rightarrow Y\quad\text{ where }\quad U=X\setminus V_{+}(A_{+}B).

Following Kleiman and Thorup
[
14
,
15
]
, the polar multiplicities of
B
are defined by the following intersection numbers

m_{r}^{i}(B)\;=\;\int c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1)\right)^{i}c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\right)^{r-i}\,\big[D\big]_{r}\quad\text{ for all }\quad 0\leq i\leq r;

here
D
denotes the projective completion of the normal cone
C_{Z}X
to
Z
in
X
, where
Z\subseteq X
is the preimage of the closed point of
\text{Spec}(R)
.
Motivated by the work of Simis, Ulrich and Vasconcelos
[
21
]
, we utilize polar multiplicities of the intermediate algebra
G=\gr_{A_{+}B}(B)
to study the integrality and birationality of the extension
A\hookrightarrow B
.
We see
G
as a standard graded
R
-algebra (see
Section 4
), and we consider the following polar multiplicities

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(G)\quad\text{ for all }\quad 0\leq i\leq r.

Our main result is the following theorem which provides criteria for integrality and birationality in terms of the polar multiplicities of the algebras
A
,
B
and
G
.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 4.4
)
.

Under the notations and assumptions above, the following statements hold:

(i)

(Integrality)
Consider the following conditions:

(a)

A finite morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
is obtained.

(b)

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{t}G)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
t\gg 0
.

(c)

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

In general, the implications
(a)

\Rightarrow

(b)
and
(a)

\Rightarrow

(c)
hold.
Moreover, if
B
is equidimensional and catenary, then the reverse implication
(b)

\Rightarrow

(a)
also holds.

(ii)

(Integrality
+
Birationality)
If we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(A)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

Notice that the above theorem yields the equalities
m_{r}^{i}(A)=m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
if we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
.
Therefore we should ask the following question:

Question 1.1
.

If we have
m_{r}^{i}(A)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
, under which conditions do we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
?

This question has already a positive answer in the case
R
is zero-dimensional.
If
R
is Artinian, the only non-vanishing polar multiplicity is
m_{r}^{0}
and it coincides with the usual multiplicity of a graded algebra (see
Section 2
(i) and
Section 4
).
Therefore,
[
21
, Proposition 6.1]
yields a positive answer under the conditions that
\dim(R)=0
and
B
is equidimensional.

Given a finitely generated
R
-module
E
, we can study a notion of
mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities
of
E
by considering the polar multiplicities of the Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}(E)
of
E
.
In
Theorem 5.1
, we provide a criterion for integral dependence of modules in terms of these mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities.

Finally, we also provide a number of technical results on polar multiplicities that might be interesting in their own right:

(i)

Theorem 2.5
gives a precise description of the additive behavior of polar multiplicities.

(ii)

Theorem 2.8
describes the behavior of polar multiplicities under hypersurface sections.

(iii)

Section 2
shows that, under mild conditions, polar multiplicities do not increase under localizations.

(iv)

In
Section 3
, we relate polar multiplicities to certain Stückrad-Vogel cycles and by using a deformation to the normal cone we obtain a length formula.

Outline.

The basic outline of this paper is as follows.
In
Section 2
, we prove a number of basic results regarding polar multiplicities.
By using a technique of deformation to the normal cone, we give a length formula for polar multiplicities in
Section 3
.
The proof of
Theorem A
is given in
Section 4
.
Finally, in
Section 5
, we apply
Theorem A
to obtain a criterion for integral dependence of modules in terms of certain mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities.

2.
Basic results on polar multiplicities

In this section, we recall the notion of polar multiplicities as defined by Kleiman and Thorup
[
14
,
15
]
, and we prove some basic results for these invariants.
Our main goal is to extend some of the results in the elegant treatment of Flenner–O’Carroll–Vogel
[
9
, §1.2]
(of Hilbert-Samuel multiplicities) to the case of polar multiplicities.
The setup below is used throughout this section.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},\kappa)
be a Noetherian local ring with maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
and residue field
\kappa
.
Let
B
be a standard graded algebra over
R=B_{0}
.
Set
X:=\text{Proj}(B)
.

Following the work of Kleiman and Thorup
[
14
,
15
]
, we define
polar multiplicities
as follows.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
B
-module and
\mathscr{M}:=\widetilde{M}
be the corresponding coherent sheaf on
X
.
Let
p:X\rightarrow\text{Spec}(R)
be the natural projection.
Take
Z:=p^{-1}(\{{\mathfrak{m}}\})\subset X
to be preimage of the closed point of
\text{Spec}(R)
and
\mathscr{I}_{Z}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}
be its ideal sheaf.
Consider the corresponding normal cone
C:=C_{Z}X=\fSpec_{X}\left(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}\right)
and its projective completion

D\;:=\;{\mathbb{P}}\left(C\oplus 1\right)\;=\;\fProj_{X}\Bigg(\Big(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}\Big)[z]\Bigg);

here
z
is a new variable of degree one, see, e.g.,
[
11
, Appedinx B.5]
.
Denote by
C_{\mathscr{M}}
the coherent sheaf on
C
determined by the module
\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}\mathscr{M}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}\mathscr{M}
.
Denote by
D_{\mathscr{M}}
the coherent sheaf on
D
determined by the module
\big(\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n}\mathscr{M}/\mathscr{I}_{Z}^{n+1}\mathscr{M}\big)[z]
.

Definition 2.2
.

For all
r\geq\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
and
0\leq i\leq r
, we say that the corresponding
polar multiplicity
of
M
is given by the intersection number

m_{r}^{i}(M)\;:=\;\int c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{D}(1)\right)^{i}c_{1}\left(\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)\right)^{r-i}\,\big[D_{\mathscr{M}}\big]_{r}.

We also set
m^{i}(M):=m_{r}^{i}(M)
with
r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
.

We now express the above invariants as normalized leading coefficients of a certain bivariate Hilbert polynomial.
Consider the following associated graded ring and associated graded module

\mathscr{G}:=\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}B}(B)\;=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\,{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}B\big/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}B\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{G}_{M}:=\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}B}(M)\;=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\,{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\big/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M

with respect to the ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}B
.
Notice that
\mathscr{G}=\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}B}(B)
is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{2}
-graded algebra over the residue field
\kappa
and that
\mathscr{G}_{M}
is a finitely generated bigraded
\mathscr{G}
-module.
For any
v\in\mathbb{Z}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the corresponding
(v,n)
-graded part is given by
\big[\mathscr{G}_{M}\big]_{(v,n)}={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M_{v}\big/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M_{v}
.
We also study the following completed objects

\mathscr{G}^{\star}:=\gr_{({\mathfrak{m}},t)}(B[t])\;=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\mathfrak{m}},t)^{n}B[t]}{({\mathfrak{m}},t)^{n+1}B[t]}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}:=\gr_{({\mathfrak{m}},t)}(M[t])\;=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{({\mathfrak{m}},t)^{n}M[t]}{({\mathfrak{m}},t)^{n+1}M[t]}

where
S:=R[t]
with
t
a new variable,
B[t]:=B\otimes_{R}S
and
M[t]:=M\otimes_{R}S
.
Notice that we obtain an isomorphism of bigraded
\mathscr{G}^{\star}
-modules

(1)

\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}\;=\;\gr_{({\mathfrak{m}},t)}(M[t])\;\cong\;\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}B}(M)[t^{\star}]

where
t^{\star}
is a new variable of degree
(0,1)
.
Hence, we obtain the equality

{\rm length}_{R}\left(\left[\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}\right]_{(v,n)}\right)\;=\;{\rm length}_{R}\left(M_{v}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M_{v}\right).

Let
r:=\dim(\text{Supp}(\mathscr{M}))
.
The bivariate Hilbert polynomials of
\mathscr{G}_{M}
and
\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}
are denoted by
P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}(v,n)
and
P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}}(v,n)
, respectively.
We start with the following degree computations.

Lemma 2.3
.

We have
\deg(P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}})\leq r-1
and
\deg(P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}})=r
.

Proof.

We may substitute
M
by
\overline{M}=M/\text{H}_{B_{+}}^{0}(M)
and
B
by
\overline{B}=B/\text{Ann}_{B}(M)
.
Thus may assume that
\dim(B)=\dim(M)=r+1
and that
\dim(R)=\dim(B/B_{+})<r+1
.
It then follows that
\deg(P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}})\leq\dim(\mathscr{G}_{M})-2=r-1
and
\deg(P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}})\leq\dim(\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star})-2=r
.
To prove the equality
\deg(P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}})=r
, it suffices to check that no minimal prime of
\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}
of dimension
r+2
can contain
[\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(1,0)}\cap[\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(0,1)}
.
Notice that no such minimal prime can contain
[\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(0,1)}
due to
(1)
, and that no such minimal prime can contain
[\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(1,0)}
because
\dim(\mathscr{G}^{\star}/([\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(1,0)}))=\dim(\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R)[t^{\star}])=\dim(R)+1<r+2
.
So the result follows.
∎

Then we write

P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}(v,n)\;=\;\sum_{i+j=r-1}\frac{e\left(i,j;\,\mathscr{G}_{M}\right)}{i!\,j!}\,v^{i}n^{j}\;+\;\text{(lower degree terms)}

and

P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}}(v,n)\;=\;\sum_{i+j=r}\frac{e(i,j;\,\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star})}{i!\,j!}\,v^{i}n^{j}\;+\;\text{(lower degree terms)}.

We have the following connections between the polar multiplicities of
M
and the bivariate polynomials
P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}
and
P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{*}}

Lemma 2.4
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

m_{r}^{i}(M)=e\left(r-i,i;\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}\right)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(ii)

e(i,j+1;\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star})=e(i,j;\mathscr{G}_{M})
for all
i+j=r-1
.

Proof.

(i) This is a direct consequence of
[
14
, Lemma 4.3]
.

(ii) We have the equality
{\text{Hilb}}_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}}(t_{1},t_{2})=\frac{1}{1-t_{2}}{\text{Hilb}}_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}(t_{1},t_{2})
of Hilbert series (see
(1)
).
Then the result follows from
[
6
, Theorem A]
.
∎

\mathscr{R}({\mathfrak{m}};B)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}BT^{n}\subset B[T]\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathscr{R}({\mathfrak{m}};M)\>:=\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}MT^{n}\subset M[T].

\mathscr{R}^{+}({\mathfrak{m}};M)\;:=\;\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}MT^{n}\;\subset\;M[T,T^{-1}].

\mathscr{G}_{M}\;=\;\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}B}(M)\;\cong\;\mathscr{R}^{+}({\mathfrak{m}};M)\big/T^{-1}\mathscr{R}^{+}({\mathfrak{m}};M).

[

Let

Theorem 2.8
.

Assume

Let
M

Set

m_{r-1}^{i}(M/bM)\;\geq\;\alpha m_{r}^{i}(M)+\beta m_{r}^{i+1}(M)+m_{r-1}^{i}(N)\quad\text{ for all \hskip 10.22217pt$0\leq i\leq r-1$},

and equality holds for all

if and only if

is a
\mathscr{G}
-parameter on

Proof.

Notice that we also have

By applying

Q_{2}(v,n)=P_{\mathscr{G}_{bM}^{\star}}(v,n)+P_{\mathscr{G}_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\beta}M/bM}^{\star}}(v,n)-P_{\mathscr{G}_{{\mathfrak{m}}^{\beta}M}^{\star}}(v,n)

(6)

\mathscr{G}

Remark 2.9

Lemma 2.13
.

Suppose that
R
is equidimensional and catenary.
Let
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Spec}(R)
be a prime ideal and assume that

is analytically unramified.
Let

be a finitely generated graded
B
-module.
Set

and

Then

m_{r}^{i}(M)\;\geq\;m_{r^{\prime}}^{i-r+r^{\prime}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}).

Proof.

By making a purely transcendental residue field extension as in the proof of
Section 2
(ii), we may assume the
\kappa

Then
[
19
, Lemma 2.6]
yields a sequence of elements

which can be used to apply

to both

So, we obtain the equalities

m_{r}^{i}(M)\;=\;e_{i}\left(\text{H}^{0}\left(X,\,(M/(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-i})M)^{{\scalebox{1.2}[1]{$\sim$}}}\right)\right)

and

m_{r^{\prime}}^{i-r+r^{\prime}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})\;=\;e_{i-r+r^{\prime}}\left(\text{H}^{0}\left(X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\text{Spec}(R_{\mathfrak{p}}),\,(M_{\mathfrak{p}}/(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-i})M_{\mathfrak{p}})^{{\scalebox{1.2}[1]{$\sim$}}}\right)\right).

Let
N:=\text{H}^{0}\left(X,\,(M/(y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-i})M)^{{\scalebox{1.2}[1]{$\sim$}}}\right)

By flat base change, we get
m_{r^{\prime}}^{i-r+r^{\prime}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=e_{i-r+r^{\prime}}(N_{\mathfrak{p}})

Let
\delta:=\dim(R)
.
From the associativity formula and the fact that

are equidimensional and catenary, we obtain the equalities

e_{i}(N)\;=\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathfrak{q}\in{\rm Min}(N)\\

(also, see

]

∎

2.1.
Polar multiplicities with respect to an ideal of linear forms

In this subsection, we define and study a notion of polar multiplicities with respect to an ideal of linear forms in
B
.
Our results in this subsection profited from
[
24
]
.
Let
I\subset B
be an ideal generated by linear forms in
B
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
B
-module, and set
r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{M}))
.
We consider
\mathscr{R}^{+}(I)=B[IT,T^{-1}]
with an “internal grading”.
This means that
\deg(T)=0
and that
\mathscr{R}^{+}(I)
is a standard graded algebra over
R[T^{-1}]
since
I\subset B
is generated by linear forms.
We then have that
G:=\gr_{I}(B)\cong\mathscr{R}^{+}(I)/T^{-1}\mathscr{R}^{+}(I)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}I^{k}/I^{k+1}
becomes a standard graded
R
-algebra.
Furthermore,
\gr_{I}(M)
is a finitely generated graded module over
G
and the corresponding
v
-graded part is given by

\big[\gr_{I}(M)\big]_{v}\;=\;\bigoplus_{k=0}^{\infty}\left[I^{k}M\big/I^{k+1}M\right]_{v}.

Here, our main interest is in the following invariants.

Definition 2.14
.

We set
m_{r}^{i}(I,M):=m_{r}^{i}\left(\gr_{I}(M)\right)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

The next proposition shows that these polar multiplicities are also additive.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
I\subset B
be an ideal generated by linear forms.
Let
0\rightarrow M^{\prime}\rightarrow M\rightarrow M^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of finitely generated graded
B
-modules.
Set
r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{M}))
.
Then we have the equality

m_{r}^{i}(I,M)\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(I,M^{\prime})+m_{r}^{i}(I,M^{\prime\prime})

for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

We follow a similar approach to
Theorem 2.5
.
Let
N:=\text{Ker}\left(\mathscr{R}^{+}(I;M)\rightarrow\mathscr{R}^{+}(I;M^{\prime\prime})\right)
and
L:=N/\mathscr{R}^{+}(I;M^{\prime})
.
Following the steps that yielded
(2)
,
(3)
and
(4)
, we now have the short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow N/T^{-1}N\longrightarrow\gr_{I}(M)\longrightarrow\gr_{I}(M^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow L\xrightarrow{\hskip 8.19447ptT^{-1}\hskip 8.19447pt}L\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow 0,

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow U\longrightarrow\gr_{I}(M^{\prime})\longrightarrow N/T^{-1}N\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow 0.

We have that
\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{L}))\leq r
.
Then applying
Theorem 2.5
gives the equality

m_{r}^{i}\left(\gr_{I}(M)\right)=m_{r}^{i}\left(\gr_{I}(M^{\prime})\right)+m_{r}^{i}\left(\gr_{I}(M^{\prime\prime})\right).

This concludes the proof.
∎

The following theorem shows that the polar multiplicities considered in this subsection do not change when passing to a reduction.

Theorem 2.16
.

Assume
Section 2
.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
B
-module and
I\subseteq J
be
B
-ideals generated by linear forms.
Set
r=\dim(\text{Supp}(\widetilde{M}))
.
If
I
is a reduction of
J
on
M
, then
m_{r}^{i}(I,M)=m_{r}^{i}(J,M)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

By assumption, we have
I\cdot J^{k}M=J^{k+1}M=J\cdot J^{k}M
for some positive integer
k\geq 1
.
Thus we get
\gr_{I}(J^{k}M)=\gr_{J}(J^{k}M)
.
For each
j\geq 0
, notice that
\gr_{I}\left(J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\right)\cong J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\cong\gr_{J}\left(J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\right)
.
We have the short exact sequences

0\rightarrow J^{k}M\rightarrow M\rightarrow M/J^{k}M\rightarrow 0

and

0\rightarrow J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\rightarrow M/J^{j+1}M\rightarrow M/J^{j}M\rightarrow 0

for all
j\geq 0
.
Hence by applying
§2.1
, we obtain the equalities

\displaystyle m_{r}^{i}(I,M)

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{i}\left(I,J^{k}M\right)+m_{r}^{i}\left(I,M/J^{k}M\right)

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{i}\left(I,J^{k}M\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}m_{r}^{i}\left(I,J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\right)

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{i}\left(J,J^{k}M\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}m_{r}^{i}\left(J,J^{j}M/J^{j+1}M\right)

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{i}\left(J,J^{k}M\right)+m_{r}^{i}\left(J,M/J^{k}M\right)

\displaystyle=m_{r}^{i}(J,M)

that settle the claim of the theorem.
∎

.
We have the cycle

\nu_{i}(\mathbf{f};\mathscr{G}_{M})\;:=\;\sum_{\mathfrak{P}}{\rm length}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\left({(\mathscr{G}_{M})}_{\mathfrak{P}}\big/\left(L_{i-1}+f_{i}\mathscr{G}_{M}\right)_{\mathfrak{P}}\right)\big[\text{Proj}(\mathscr{G}/{\mathfrak{P}})\big]\;\in\;Z_{r-i}\big(X\times_{\text{Spec}(R)}\text{Spec}(\kappa)\big),

where the sum runs over the minimal primes of
\mathscr{G}_{M}\big/\left(L_{i-1}+f_{i}\mathscr{G}_{M}\right)
of dimension

that contain
\mathscr{G}_{+}
.

Remark 3.4
.

By prime avoidance, we can inductively assume that

does not belong to any associated prime

such that

So, for any associated prime

of

Lemma 3.5
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

There are a natural surjective homomorphism

\mathscr{G}_{M}\big/\left(f_{1},\ldots,f_{i}\right)\mathscr{G}_{M}\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(M\big/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})M\big)\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}\big(M\big/(x_{1},\ldots,x_{i})M:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\big)

which are isomorphisms for the graded parts
(v,n)
with
n\gg 0
.

(ii)

We have the equalities

L_{i}\;=\;{\text{in}}\left(\left(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}\right)M\right):_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}\mathscr{G}_{+}^{\infty}\;=\;{\text{in}}\left(N_{i}\right):_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}\mathscr{G}_{+}^{\infty}

of submodules of
\mathscr{G}_{M}
.

Proof.

(i) By induction, it suffices to consider
i=1
, hence set
x=x_{1}
and
f=\text{in}(x)
.
The explicit description of the claimed surjections is as follows

\displaystyle\mathscr{G}_{M}/f\mathscr{G}_{M}\;\cong\;

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M+x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}M}

\displaystyle\;\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\;\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/xM)\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\cap xM}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\twoheadrightarrow\;\;\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(M/(xM:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}))\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M}{{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\cap(xM:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty})}.

Since
(xM:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty})
is obtained by removing primary components of
xM\subset M
that contain
{\mathfrak{m}}^{k}M
for some
k>0
(see, e.g.,
[
8
, §3.6]
), it follows that
{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\cap(xM:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty})={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\cap xM
for
n\gg 0
. Hence to conclude the proof it suffices to show the equality
x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}M={\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\cap xM
for
n\gg 0
.

For the rest of the proof, let
n\gg 0
.
We may assume
f
is filter-regular on
\mathscr{G}
with respect to
\mathscr{G}_{+}
, and consequently we obtain that
\left[\left(0:_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}f\right)\right]_{(*,n)}=0
(see, e.g.,
[
6
, Lemmas 3.6, 3.7]
).
This translates into

{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\,\cap\,({\mathfrak{m}}^{n+2}M:_{M}x)\;=\;{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M,

and then we get

{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+2}M\,\cap\,x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\;=\;x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}M.

Applying this latter equality yields

(7)

{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{1}}M\,\cap\,x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n_{2}}M\;=\;x{\mathfrak{m}}^{\max\{n_{1}-1,n_{2}\}}M

for
n_{1}\gg 0
and
n_{2}\gg 0
.
The Artin-Rees lemma gives a positive integers
n_{0}>0
such that

(8)

{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\,\cap\,xM\;\subseteq\;x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-n_{0}}M.

By combining
(7)
and
(8)
, we obtain the claimed equality

{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\,\cap\,xM\;=\;{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M\,\cap\,x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-n_{0}}M\;=\;x{\mathfrak{m}}^{n-1}M.

Therefore the proof of part (i) is complete.

(ii) It is a direct consequence of part (i).
∎

We are now ready for the main result of this section.
The proof closely follows the arguments of Achilles and Manaresi
[
1
, Theorem 3.3]
.

Theorem 3.6

(Deformation to the normal cone)
.

Assume
Section 3
. We have

\nu_{i}({\mathbf{x}};M)=\nu_{i}(\mathbf{f};\mathscr{G}_{M})

for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathfrak{P}}\in\text{Spec}(\mathscr{G})
be a prime ideal containing
\mathscr{G}_{+}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{P}}\cap B\in\text{Spec}(B)
be its contraction.
Recall that
\mathscr{G}/{\mathfrak{P}}\cong B/{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}\supseteq{\mathfrak{m}}B
.
From
[
4
, Proposition 1.5.15]
and
Section 3
, we obtain the equivalences

\left(\mathscr{G}_{M}/L_{i-1}\right)_{\mathfrak{P}}=0\;\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\;\mathscr{G}_{M}/L_{i-1}\otimes_{B}B_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\;\;\;\Leftrightarrow\;\;\;(\mathscr{G}_{M})_{\mathfrak{p}}/\left({\text{in}}(N_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}}:_{(\mathscr{G}_{M})_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}})_{+}^{\infty}\right)=0.

It is then clear that
{\mathfrak{p}}\not\in\text{Supp}_{B}(M/N_{i-1})
implies
{\mathfrak{P}}\not\in\text{Supp}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathscr{G}_{M}/L_{i-1})
.
On the other hand, if we have
{\mathfrak{P}}\not\in\text{Supp}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathscr{G}_{M}/L_{i-1})
, then
\big[\gr_{{\mathfrak{m}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}}/(N_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}})\big]_{n}=0
for
n\gg 0
, however this can only happen if
(N_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}}\supset{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M_{\mathfrak{p}}
for
n\gg 0
.
Since
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(M/N_{i-1})=0
, the condition
(N_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}}\supset{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}M_{\mathfrak{p}}
yields the vanishing
(M/N_{i-1})_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
.
Therefore, it follows that
{\mathfrak{P}}\in\text{Supp}_{\mathscr{G}}(\mathscr{G}_{M}/(L_{i-1}+f_{i}\mathscr{G}))
if and only if
{\mathfrak{p}}\in\text{Supp}_{B}(M/(N_{i-1}+x_{i}M))
.
To conclude the proof, it suffices to show that the respective lengths appearing as coefficients in the cycles
\nu_{i}({\mathbf{x}};M)
and
\nu_{i}(\mathbf{f};\mathscr{G}_{M})
are equal.

Now, suppose that
{\mathfrak{P}}
appears in
\nu_{i}(\mathbf{f};\mathscr{G}_{M})
and that, equivalently,
{\mathfrak{p}}
appears in
\nu_{i}({\mathbf{x}};M)
(in particular,
\dim(\mathscr{G}/{\mathfrak{P}})=\dim(B/{\mathfrak{p}})=r-i+1
).
Let
\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}}=\mathscr{G}_{M}/L_{i-1}
and
\overline{M}=M/N_{i-1}
.
Then the modules
(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{P}}
and
\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are one-dimensional (see
Section 3
), and since by prime avoidance
f_{i}
and
x_{i}
are nonzerodivisor on
(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{P}}
and on
\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, respectively, we obtain the equalities

{\rm length}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\left(\frac{(\mathscr{G}_{M})_{\mathfrak{P}}}{\left(L_{i-1}+f_{i}\mathscr{G}_{M}\right)_{\mathfrak{P}}}\right)=e\left(f_{i},(\overline{\mathscr{G}}_{M})_{\mathfrak{P}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad{\rm length}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\frac{M_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\left(N_{i-1}+x_{i}M\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)=e\left(x_{i},\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)

expressing the lengths as multiplicities (see, e.g.,
[
9
, Corollary 1.2.14]
).
Again, by
[
4
, Proposition 1.5.15]
,
e(f_{i},(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{P}})=e(f_{i},(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{p}})
.
We have that
\big[(\overline{\mathscr{G}}_{M})_{\mathfrak{p}}\big]_{n+1}=f_{i}\big[(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\big]_{n}
for
n\gg 0
.
In other words,
f_{i}\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a reduction of
[\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{+}
on
(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and then it follows that
x_{i}B_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a reduction of
{\mathfrak{m}}B_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Consequently, we get the equalities

e\left(f_{i},(\overline{\mathscr{G}}_{M})_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=e\left([\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{+},(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)\quad\text{ and }\quad e(x_{i},\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}})=e\left({\mathfrak{m}}B_{\mathfrak{p}},\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right);

see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 14.3]
.
Due to
Section 3
, we get
e\left([\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{+},(\overline{\mathscr{G}_{M}})_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)=e\left([\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}}]_{+},\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}})\right)
.
Finally, by definition, we have
e\left([\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{p}})]_{+},\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}})\right)=e\left({\mathfrak{m}}B_{\mathfrak{p}},\overline{M}_{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
.
This establishes the required equality

{\rm length}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\left(\frac{(\mathscr{G}_{M})_{\mathfrak{P}}}{\left(L_{i-1}+f_{i}\mathscr{G}_{M}\right)_{\mathfrak{P}}}\right)\;=\;{\rm length}_{B_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\frac{M_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\left(N_{i-1}+x_{i}M\right)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)

and finishes the proof.
∎

Next we show that the polar multiplicities of

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
0\leq i\leq r
, we have
m_{r}^{i}(M)=\deg(\nu_{i}(\mathbf{f};\mathscr{G}_{M}))
.

Proof.

We proceed inductively on

.
Set

.
We may fix
n\gg 0
big enough so that we have the equalities

m_{r}^{0}(M)=\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\frac{P_{\mathscr{G}_{M}^{\star}}(v,n)}{v^{r}/r!}=\lim_{v\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm length}_{R}\left(\big[\mathscr{G}_{M}/\mathscr{G}_{+}^{n+1}\mathscr{G}_{M}\big]_{(v,*)}\right)}{v^{r}/r!}=e_{r+1}\big(\mathscr{G}_{M}/\mathscr{G}_{+}^{n+1}\mathscr{G}_{M}\big),

here we see
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}_{+}^{n+1}=\bigoplus_{v\geq 0}[\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}_{+}^{n+1}]_{(v,*)}

e_{r+1}\big(\mathscr{G}_{M}/\mathscr{G}_{+}^{n+1}\mathscr{G}_{M}\big)\;=\;\sum_{\mathfrak{P}}{\rm length}_{\mathscr{G}_{\mathfrak{P}}}\big((\mathscr{G}_{M})_{\mathfrak{P}}\big)\,e_{r+1}(\mathscr{G}/{\mathfrak{P}})

\mathscr{G}_{M}

\mathscr{G}_{+}

.

For

,
Section 2

m_{r}^{i}(M)=e\left(r-i,i-1;\mathscr{G}_{M}/(0:_{\mathscr{G}_{M}}\mathscr{G}_{+}^{\infty})\right)
.

Theorem 3.8

(Length formula for polar multiplicities)
.

Assume

.
For all

m_{r}^{i}(M)\;=\;\sum_{{\mathfrak{p}}}{\rm length}_{B_{\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\frac{M_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\big(\left((x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1})M:_{M}{\mathfrak{m}}^{\infty}\right)+x_{i}M\big)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\right)\cdot e(B/{\mathfrak{p}})

First proof.

4.
Polar multiplicities: integrality and birationality

In this section, we give criteria for the integrality and birationality of an inclusion of graded algebras in terms of polar multiplicities.
Here the following setup is fixed.

Setup 4.1
.

Let
A\subseteq B
be a homogeneous inclusion of standard graded algebra over a Noetherian local ring
R=A_{0}=B_{0}
.
Consider the associated morphism
f:U\subseteq X=\text{Proj}(B)\rightarrow Y=\text{Proj}(A)
where
U=X\setminus V_{+}(A_{+}B)
.
Set
r=\dim(X)
.

A very important observation of Simis, Ulrich and Vasconcelos
[
21
]
is that the integrality and birationality of the inclusion
A\hookrightarrow B
can be studied via the intermediate algebra
\gr_{A_{+}B}(B)
.
As in
[
21
, §3]
, we consider the Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}(A_{+}B)=B[A_{+}BT]\subset B[T]
and the extended Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}^{+}(A_{+}B)=B[A_{+}BT,T^{-1}]\subset B[T,T^{-1}]
with standard
\mathbb{N}
-gradings (we can simply set
\deg(T)=0
and utilize the induced grading).
Thus, we see
\mathscr{R}(A_{+}B)
as a standard graded
R
-algebra and
\mathscr{R}^{+}(A_{+}B)
as a standard graded
R[T^{-1}]
-algebra.
As a consequence, we also see the associated graded ring
G:=\gr_{A_{+}B}(B)\cong\mathscr{R}^{+}(A_{+}B)/T^{-1}\mathscr{R}^{+}(A_{+}B)
as a standard graded
R
-algebra.
Let
t\geq 1
be a positive integer.
From
[
21
, Proposition 3.2]
, the
v
-th graded part of the
G
-module
B_{t}G
is given by

\left[B_{t}G\right]_{v}\;\cong\;\bigoplus_{j=1}^{v-t+1}A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}\big/A_{j}B_{v-j}.

By applying the functor
-\otimes_{R}R/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}
to the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}\big/A_{j}B_{v-j}\longrightarrow B_{v}\big/A_{j}B_{v-j}\longrightarrow B_{v}\big/A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}\longrightarrow 0,

we get the exact sequence

\frac{A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}}{A_{j}B_{v-j}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}}\longrightarrow\frac{B_{v}}{A_{j}B_{v-j}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}B_{v}}\longrightarrow\frac{B_{v}}{A_{j-1}B_{v-j+1}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}B_{v}}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus summing up gives us the following

(9)

P_{\mathscr{G}_{B_{t}G}^{\star}}(v,n)={\rm length}_{R}\big([B_{t}G]_{v}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}[B_{t}G]_{v}\big)\;\geq\;{\rm length}_{R}\big(B_{v}/(A_{v-t+1}B_{t-1}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n+1}B_{v})\big)

for all
v\gg 0
and
n\gg 0
.
As it can be helpful, we provide vanishing criteria for polar multiplicities.

Lemma 4.2
.

We have
m_{r}^{i}(B)=0
if
i>\dim(R)
or
i<r-\dim(\text{Proj}(B/{\mathfrak{m}}B))
.

Proof.

By
Section 2
, we have
m_{r}^{i}(B)=e(r-i,i;\mathscr{G}^{\star})
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.
From
[
5
, Propostion 3.1]
, we obtain the vanishing
e(i,j;\mathscr{G}^{\star})=0
if
i>\dim\big(\text{Proj}(\mathscr{G}^{\star}/([\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(0,1)}))\big)=\dim(\text{Proj}(B/{\mathfrak{m}}B))
or
j>\dim\big(\text{Proj}(\mathscr{G}^{\star}/([\mathscr{G}^{\star}]_{(1,0)})))=\dim(\gr_{\mathfrak{m}}(R))=\dim(R)
.
∎

We consider the following polar multiplicities that will play an important role in the criteria that we provide.

Definition 4.3
.

We set
m_{r}^{i}(A,B):=m_{r}^{i}(A_{+}B,B)=m_{r}^{i}(G)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

The main result of this section is the theorem below.

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume
Section 4
.
The following statements hold:

(i)

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)\geq m_{r}^{i}(A)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(ii)

If
R
is equidimensional and catenary, then
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)\geq m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

(iii)

(Integrality)
Consider the following conditions:

(a)

A finite morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
is obtained.

(b)

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{t}G)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
t\gg 0
.

(c)

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

In general, the implications
(a)

\Rightarrow

(b)
and
(a)

\Rightarrow

(c)
hold.
Moreover, if
B
is equidimensional and catenary, then the reverse implication
(b)

\Rightarrow

(a)
also holds.

(iv)

(Integrality
+
Birationality)
If we obtain a finite birational morphism
f:X\rightarrow Y
, then
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(A)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.
The converse holds if
B
is equidimensional and catenary.

Proof.

(i) From the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow B_{1}G\rightarrow G\rightarrow A\rightarrow 0
and
Theorem 2.5
, we obtain the inequality
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(G)\geq m_{r}^{i}(A)
.

(ii)
Consider the local ring
S:=R[T^{-1}]_{({\mathfrak{m}},T^{-1})}
and the standard graded
S
-algebra
C:=\mathscr{R}^{+}(A_{+}B)\otimes_{R[T^{-1}]}S
.
Then
Theorem 2.8
and
Section 2
yield the inequalities

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(G)\;\geq\;m_{r+1}^{i+1}(C)\;\geq\;m_{r}^{i}(C\otimes_{S}S_{{\mathfrak{m}}S})\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(B),

where the last equality follows from the isomorphism
C\otimes_{S}S_{{\mathfrak{m}}S}\cong B[T]\otimes_{R[T]}R[T]_{{\mathfrak{m}}R[T]}
.
So the result of this part follows.

(iii) Suppose that
A\hookrightarrow B
is an integral extension (i.e.,
\sqrt{A_{+}B}\supseteq B_{+}
).
Then, for
t\gg 0
, we have
B_{t}G=0
and so the short exact sequence
0\rightarrow B_{t}G\rightarrow G\rightarrow G/B_{t}G\rightarrow 0
and
Theorem 2.5
give the equality
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(G)=m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{t}G)
.
Since
A_{+}B
is a reduction of
B_{+}
,
Theorem 2.16
implies that

m_{r}^{i}(A,B)\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(G)\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(\gr_{B_{+}}(B))\;=\;m_{r}^{i}(B),

where the last equality holds since
\gr_{B_{+}}(B)\cong B
.
So, we have the implications (a)
\Rightarrow
(b) and (a)
\Rightarrow
(c).

Suppose that
A\hookrightarrow B
is not an integral extension (i.e.,
\sqrt{A_{+}B}\not\supseteq B_{+}
) and that
B
is equidimensional and catenary.
Let
x\in X
be a closed point with associated prime
P\subset B
such that
P\supseteq A_{+}B
.
By
[
7
, Lemmas 6.5, 6.6]
, we obtain
P\supseteq{\mathfrak{m}}B
,
d_{x}=\dim(\mathcal{O}_{x})=r
and the positivity of the following limit

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm length}_{R}\left(B_{t+nd}/\left[P^{n}\right]_{t+nd}\right)}{n^{r}}\;>\;0

for large enough integers
t\gg 0
and
d\gg 0
.
Hence, from the inclusion
P\supseteq{\mathfrak{m}}B+A_{+}B
and equation
(9)
, we get the positivity of the following limits

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{P_{\mathscr{G}_{B_{t+1}G}^{\star}}(t+nd,n-1)}{n^{r}}\;\geq\;\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{{\rm length}_{R}\left(B_{t+nd}/\left(A_{nd}B_{t}+{\mathfrak{m}}^{n}B_{t+nd}\right)\right)}{n^{r}}\;>\;0.

Consequently,
Section 2
yields

\sum_{i=0}^{r}\,\frac{d^{r-i}}{i!(r-i)!}\,m_{r}^{i}(B_{t+1}G)\;>\;0,

and so we should have
m_{r}^{i}(B_{t+1}G)>0
for some
0\leq i\leq r
.
Finally, since we have the equation
m_{r}^{i}(G)=m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{t+1}G)+m_{r}^{i}(B_{t+1}G)
by
Theorem 2.5
, we obtain the implication (b)
\Rightarrow
(a) (under our current assumption that
B
is equidimensional and catenary).
So the proof of part (iii) is complete.

(iv) Since
m_{r}^{i}(A)=m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{1}G)\leq m_{r}^{i}(G/B_{t}G)
for all
t\geq 1
and we already settled part (iii), in this part we may assume that
A\hookrightarrow B
is an integral extension.
Moreover, by part (iii), we may assume
m_{r}^{i}(A,B)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
.
Then
Section 2
gives the equalities

m_{r}^{i}(A)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}{\rm length}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(A_{\mathfrak{p}})m_{r}^{i}(A/{\mathfrak{p}})\quad\text{ and }\quad m_{r}^{i}(B)=\sum_{\mathfrak{p}}{\rm length}_{A_{\mathfrak{p}}}(B_{\mathfrak{p}})m_{r}^{i}(A/{\mathfrak{p}})

where both sums run through the minimal primes of

of dimension
r+1
.
By
Section 2
, for any relevant minimal prime
{\mathfrak{p}}
of

of dimension

, there is some

such that
m_{r}^{i}(A/{\mathfrak{p}})>0
.
Therefore, under our current assumptions, we obtain that
f:X\rightarrow Y
is birational if and only if
m_{r}^{i}(A)=m_{r}^{i}(B)
for all

.
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

5.
Mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities and reductions of modules

In this short section, by applying the result of
Theorem 4.4
, we obtain a criterion for integral dependence of modules in terms of certain mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities.

Let
R
be a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
.
Let
E
be a finitely generated
R
-module having rank
e\geq 1
.
Following
[
22
]
, we say that the
Rees algebra

\mathscr{R}(E)
is given as the symmetric algebra
\text{Sym}(E)
modulo its
R
-torsion.
Also, we have the equality
\dim(\mathscr{R}(E))=d+e
(see
[
22
, Proposition 2.2]
).
Set
r:=d+e-1
.
We consider the following
mixed Buchsbaum-Rim multiplicities
(see
[
14
,
15
]
):

{\rm br}_{i}(E)\;:=\;m_{r}^{i}\left(\mathscr{R}(E)\right)\quad\text{ for all }\quad 0\leq i\leq r,

that we define as polar multiplicities of the Rees algebra
\mathscr{R}(E)
.
Given an
R
-submodule
U\subseteq E
, we say that
U
is a
reduction
of
E
if
E^{v+1}=UE^{v}
for some
v\geq 0
(equivalently, if
\mathscr{R}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{R}(E)
is an integral extension).
Given an
R
-submodule
U\subseteq E
also having rank
e
, we consider the following invariant

{\rm br}_{i}(U,E)\;:=\;m_{r}^{i}\left(\mathscr{R}(U),\mathscr{R}(E)\right)\quad\text{ for all }\quad 0\leq i\leq r.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
R
be an equidimensional and universally catenary Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
.
Let
U\subseteq E
be an inclusion of finitely generated
R
-modules having rank
e\geq 1
.
Set
r=d+e-1
.
Then the following two conditions are equivalent:

(a)

U
is a reduction of
E
.

(b)

{\rm br}_{i}(U,E)={\rm br}_{i}(E)
for all
0\leq i\leq r
.

Proof.

By applying
Theorem 4.4
to the inclusion of standard graded
R
-algebras
\mathscr{R}(U)\hookrightarrow\mathscr{R}(E)
, the result of the theorem follows.
∎

Question 5.2
.

Under which conditions do we have the equivalence that
U
is a reduction of
E
if and only if
{\rm br}_{i}(U)={\rm br}_{i}(E)
for all
i
?

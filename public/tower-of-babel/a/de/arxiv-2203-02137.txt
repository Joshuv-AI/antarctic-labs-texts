Product structure and regularity theorem for totally nonnegative flag varieties
Huanchen Bao, Xuhua He

Abstract
. The totally nonnegative flag variety was introduced by Lusztig. It has enriched combinatorial, geometric, and Lie-theoretic structures. In this paper, we introduce a (new) J J -total positivity on the full flag variety of an arbitrary Kac-Moody group, generalizing the (ordinary) total positivity. We show that the J J -totally nonnegative flag variety has a cellular decomposition into totally positive J J -Richardson varieties. Moreover, each totally positive J J -Richardson variety admits a favorable decomposition, called a product structure. Combined with the generalized Poincare conjecture, we prove that the closure of each totally positive J J -Richardson variety is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball. Moreover, the J J -total positivity on the full flag provides a model for the (ordinary) totally nonnegative partial flag variety. As a consequence, we prove that the closure of each (ordinary) totally positive Richardson variety is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball, confirming conjectures of Galashin, Karp and Lam in [ 12 ] .

Introduction

1.1.
Totally nonnegative flag varieties of reductive groups

The theory of total positivity on the reductive groups
G
and their partial flag varieties
\mathcal{P}_{K}
was introduced by Lusztig in the seminal work
[
25
]
. The totally nonnegative partial flag variety
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
is a “remarkable polyhedral subspace” (cf.
[
25
]
). It has many nice combinatorial, geometric, and Lie-theoretic properties. And it has been used in many other areas, such as cluster algebras
[
10
]
, the Grassmann polytopes
[
16
]
, the physics of scattering amplitudes
[
1
]
.

We give a quick review of the definition and some nice properties of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
. Let
G
be a connected reductive group, split over
\mathbb{R}
and
B^{\pm}=TU^{\pm}
be the Borel and opposite Borel subgroups of
G
. The full flag variety
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
admits the decompositions into Schubert cells and opposite Schubert cells, both indexed by the Weyl group
W
of
G
. The intersection of a Schubert cell
B^{+}wB^{+}/B^{+}
with an opposite Schubert cell
B^{-}vB^{+}/B^{+}
is called an (open) Richardson variety, and is denoted by
\mathcal{B}_{v,w}
. The variety
\mathcal{B}_{v,w}
is nonempty if and only if
v\leqslant w
, where
\leqslant
is the Bruhat order on
W
.

Let

be the set of simple roots in
G
. Let
P^{+}_{K}\supset B^{+}
be the standard parabolic subgroup associated to a subset
K
of
I
. For the partial flag
\mathcal{P}_{K}=G/P^{+}_{K}
, we have the decomposition into the projected Richardson varieties
\mathcal{P}_{K}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in Q_{K}}\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha}
. The definition and the closure relation of the projected Richardson varieties are more complicated and we skip the details in the introduction.

Let
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}
be the totally nonnegative part of
U^{-}
.
The totally nonnegative part
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
of the partial flag variety
\mathcal{P}_{K}
is by definition, the closure of
U^{-}_{\geqslant 0}P^{+}_{K}/P^{+}_{K}
in
\mathcal{P}_{K}
. In the case where
\mathcal{P}_{K}
is the Grassmannian,
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
is the totally nonnegative Grassmannian
[
35
]
.

The totally positive projected Richardson variety
\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}
is, by definition, the intersection of the totally nonnegative partial flag
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
with the projected Richardson variety
\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha}
. We then have the stratification

\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in Q_{K}}\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}.

We have many remarkable properties on the totally positive projected Richardson varieties.

(1)

\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
admits a natural monoid action of
G_{\geqslant 0}
and a natural duality (see
[
26
]
);

(2)

\mathcal{P}_{\geqslant 0}
admits an representation-theoretic interpretation via canonical basis (see
[
26
]
and
[
27
]
);

(3)

\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}
is a cell and is a connected component of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
(see
[
37
]
);

(4)

The closure of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}
is a union of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha^{\prime},>0}
for some
\alpha^{\prime}
(see
[
38
]
);

(5)

The cell decomposition

is a regular CW complex.

The last property is called the regularity theorem of
\mathcal{P}_{K,\geqslant 0}
. In particular, the closure
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}}
is homeomorphic to a closed ball. It was conjectured by Postnikov for totally nonnegative Grassmannian and by Williams
[
43
]
for totally nonnegative partial flag varieties of split real reductive groups. Important progress has been made in
[

]
,
[
39
]
,
[

]
,
[
13
]
,
[

]
. It was finally established by Galashin, Karp and Lam
[

1.2.
Totally nonnegative Kac-Moody flag varieties

The theory of total positivity on the reductive groups and their flag varieties have been generalized to arbitrary Kac-Moody groups by Lusztig in a series of papers
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
31
]
,
[
32
]
and
[
33
]
, and by us in
[
5
]
. For the full flag variety of an arbitrary Kac-Moody group, we proved in
[
5
]
that the totally nonnegative flag variety
\mathcal{B}_{\geqslant 0}
has a representation-theoretic interpretation, is a union of totally positive Richardson varieties, and each totally positive Richardson variety is a cell.

However, the closure relations among the cells and the geometric structure of these closures were not established. For reductive groups, there is a natural duality coming from
B^{+}\leftrightarrow B^{-}
, which plays a significant role in establishing geometric properties of the flag varieties. Such duality does not exist for Kac-Moody groups, which leads to extra difficulty in the study of totally nonnegative flag varieties for the general Kac-Moody groups than the reductive groups. We shall overcome the obstacles and establish results in the general setting of
J
-total positivity using the “product structure”.

1.3.
J
-total positivity

Unless otherwise stated, in the rest of this paper we assume that
G
is a Kac-Moody group, split over
\mathbb{R}
. We fix a subset
J
of
I
. Let
{}^{J}\!B^{+}\subset P^{+}_{J}
be the Borel subgroup opposite to
B^{+}
and
{}^{J}\!B^{-}\subset P^{-}_{J}
be the Borel subgroup opposite to
B^{-}
. The
{}^{J}\!B^{+}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
are called the
J
-Schubert cells and
{}^{J}\!B^{-}
-orbits on
\mathcal{B}=G/B^{+}
are called the opposite
J
-Schubert cells, respectively.
For

, the open
J
-Richardson variety is defined to be

{}^{J}\!\mathcal{B}_{v,w}={}^{J}\!B^{+}wB^{+}/B^{+}\bigcap{}^{J}\!B^{-}vB^{+}/B^{+}.

It is known that
{}^{J}\!\mathcal{B}_{v,w}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, where
{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}
is the
J
-twisted Bruhat order.

Our motivation to study the
J
-Richardson varieties comes from the partial flag varieties. The projected Richardson varieties in a partial flag variety
\mathcal{P}_{K}
and their geometric structures come from the projection map
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{K}
. Roughly speaking, the projection map
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}_{K}
folds the Richardson varieties in a rather complicated way, which makes the projected Richardson varieties rather complicated to study. In
[
6
]
we introduced an “atlas model”
\mathcal{P}_{K}\dashrightarrow\tilde{}\mathcal{B}
of the partial flag variety and regarded the projected Richardson varieties in
\mathcal{P}_{K}
as certain
J
-Richardson varieties in the full flag variety
\tilde{}\mathcal{B}
of another (larger) Kac-Moody group.

However, the “atlas model”
\mathcal{P}_{K}\dashrightarrow\tilde{}\mathcal{B}
is not compatible with the total positivity on
\mathcal{P}_{K}
and the ordinary total positivity
\tilde{}\mathcal{B}
. This should not be a surprise, as the ordinary total positivity on
\tilde{}\mathcal{B}
is suitable for the decomposition into the (ordinary) Richardson varieties, not the
J
-Richardson varieties. To provide a “model” for the total positivity on
\mathcal{P}_{K}
, we introduce the
J
-total positivity. The
J
-total positivity on the flag variety is “compatible” with the stratification by
J
-Richardson varieties.

It is worth mentioning that when
J=\emptyset
, the
J
-Schubert (resp. opposite
J
-Schubert) varieties are just the Schubert (resp. opposite Schubert) varieties; the
J
-total positivity coincides with the ordinary total positivity. Therefore, our main results apply to the setting of the ordinary total positivity. If the Weyl group
W_{J}
is finite, then the
J
-total positivity can be obtained from the ordinary total positivity by multiplying
\dot{w}_{J}
on the left, where
w_{J}
is the longest element of
W_{J}
. In general, the
J
-total positivity is quite different from the ordinary total positivity.

1.5.
Regularity Theorem

We establish the regularity theorem for the links of the identity in the totally positive cells in
U^{-}
, the (ordinary) totally positive cells in the partial flag varieties, and the totally positive
J
-Richardson varieties in the full flag varieties.

Theorem D

(Theorem
5.3
, Theorem
5.7
, Theorem
5.10
, Theorem
5.11
)
.

All the following three spaces are regular CW complexes homeomorphic to closed balls:

(1)

the link of the identity in
\overline{U^{-}_{w,>0}}
, for any
w\in W
;

(2)

the totally nonnegative projected Richardson variety
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{K,\alpha,>0}}
;

(3)

the totally nonnegative
J
-Richardson variety
\overline{{}^{J}\!\mathcal{B}_{w_{1},w_{2},>0}}
.

For reductive groups, the regularity of the link was first established by Hersh in
[
15
]
; the regularity of

was established by Galashin, Karp and Lam in
[
12
]
. The generalization of regularity theorems in
[
15
,
12
]
for Kac-Moody groups was conjectured by Galashin, Karp and Lam in
[
12
, conjecture 10.2]
. Theorem D (1) & (2) proves the conjectures, and part (3) is a new regularity result.

To prove regularity theorems, we follow

]
for the use of the generalized Poincaré conjecture
[

]
,
[

]
and

as well as some general results on the poset topology. One then needs to show that each space

Instead, we use the product structure to study (inductively) the open neighborhood of a point in each space

X=\bigsqcup_{\alpha\in Q}X_{\alpha}
a
regular CW complex
if it satisfies the following two properties.

(1)

For each
\alpha\in Q
, there exists a homeomorphism from a closed ball to
\overline{X}_{\alpha}
mapping the interior of the ball to
X_{\alpha}
.

(2)

For each
\alpha
, there exists
Q^{\prime}\subset Q
, such that
\overline{X}_{\alpha}=\bigsqcup_{\beta\in Q^{\prime}}X_{\beta}
.

The face poset of
X
is the poset
(Q,\leqslant)
, where
\beta\leqslant\alpha
if and only if
X_{\beta}\subset\overline{X}_{\alpha}
.

We refer to
[
6
, §4]
and the references therein for the definitions of graded, thin, and shellable posets . We have the following result (see
[
3
]
).

Theorem 2.1
.

Suppose that
X
is a regular CW complex with face poset
Q
. If
Q\bigsqcup\{\hat{0},\hat{1}\}
(adjoining a minimum
\hat{0}
and a maximum
\hat{1}
) is graded, thin, and shellable, then
X
is homeomorphic to a sphere of dimension rank
(Q)-1
.

2.5.
The Poincare conjecture

Recall that an
n
-dimension topological manifold with boundary is a Hausdorff space
X
such that every point
x\in X
has an open neighborhood homeomorphic to either
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, or
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
mapping
x
to a point in
\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
. In the latter case, we say that
x
is on
\partial X
, the boundary of
X
.

The following theorem can be derived from the generalized poincare conjecture and Brown’s collar theorem. We refer to
[
12
, §3.2]
for details and history.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
X
be a compact
n
-dimensional topological manifold with boundary, such that
\partial X
is homeomorphic to an
(n-1)
-dimensional sphere and
X-\partial X
is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional open ball. Then
X
is homeomorphic to an
n
-dimensional closed ball
D^{n}
.

Following

For

Proposition 3.1

(1)

The poset
(W,{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,})
graded, thin and shellable. In particular, any of the convex interval of

(2)

Proof.

It follows from

It is easy to see that

We follow

{{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}}_{w}={}^{J}\!B^{+}\dot{w}B^{+}/B^{+},\quad{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}={}^{J}\!B^{-}\dot{v}B^{+}/B^{+},\quad{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}={{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}}_{w}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}^{v}.

{{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}}_{v,w}\neq\emptyset

By

{{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}}_{v,w}

{{}^{J}\!\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}

For any

\displaystyle{}^{J}\!\sigma_{r,+}:\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\rightarrow\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\bigcap{}^{J}U^{+},\qquad h_{1}h_{2}h_{2}.

We have the following isomorphism as a special case of

(3.1)

{}^{J}\!\sigma_{r}=({}^{J}\!\sigma_{r,+},{}^{J}\!\sigma_{r,-}):\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\xrightarrow{\sim}(\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\bigcap{}^{J}U^{+})\times(\dot{r}U^{-}\dot{r}^{-1}\bigcap{}^{J}U^{-}).

3.4.
Some general results

Note that the map
{}^{J}\!c_{r}
in (

) is defined over
\mathbb{R}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
. Let
Y\subset{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
with
\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{Y}
. Then

(1)

for any
v\leqslant v^{\prime}\leqslant u
, we have
\overline{Y}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},u}=\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}))}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},u}
;

(2)

for any
u\leqslant w^{\prime}\leqslant w
, we have
\overline{Y}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{u,w^{\prime}}=\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,-}(Y\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}))}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{u,w^{\prime}}
.

Remark 3.3
.

Similar results hold if the Hausdorff closure is replaced by the Zariski closure.

Proof.

Set
Y^{\prime}=Y\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})
. Then
\overline{Y}\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})=\overline{Y^{\prime}}\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})
. We have the following isomorphism via restriction

\overline{Y^{\prime}}\bigcap\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}\cong\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y^{\prime})}\times\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,-}(Y^{\prime})}.

Since
\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{Y}\bigcap(\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+})\subset\overline{Y^{\prime}}
, we must have
\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y^{\prime})}
and
\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,-}(Y^{\prime})}
. Since the isomorphism is stratified, we have

\overline{Y^{\prime}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},u}\cong(\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y^{\prime})}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},u})\times\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\cong\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y^{\prime})}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},u}.

The composition is actually the identity map. Now part (1) follows. Part (2) is proved in the same way.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
and
Y
be a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. If
Y\subset\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}
, then

(1)

\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{Y}
;

(2)

\overline{Y}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}={}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)
is a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}(\mathbb{R})
;

(3)

\overline{Y}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{u,w}={}^{J}\!c_{u,-}(Y)
is a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{u,w}(\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Let
\mu
be a dominant regular coweight. Then for any
g\in U^{-}
, we have
\lim_{t0}\mu(t)g\mu(t)^{-1}=1
. Set
\mu^{\prime}=u(\mu)
. Thus
\lim_{t0}\mu^{\prime}(t)\dot{u}gB^{+}/B^{+}=\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}
for any

. Since
Y
is a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
, it is stable under the action of
\mu^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}_{>0})
. This shows that
\dot{u}B^{+}/B^{+}\in\overline{Y}
. Part (1) is proved.

Thanks to (
3.3
), we see that
{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)
is a connected component in
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}(\mathbb{R})
. Therefore
{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)
is closed in
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}
and thus equals to
\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}
. Hence by Lemma

, we have
{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)=\overline{Y}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}=\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y)}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}
. We proved part (2). Part (3) can be proved similarly.
∎

Now we prove the main result of this section.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
Y_{v,w}
be a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. We define

Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v,w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}},\text{ for any $v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w$}.

Assume that for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have

.
Then the map
{}^{J}\!c_{u}
restricts to an isomorphism

{}^{J}\!c_{u}:Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\cong Y_{v^{\prime},u}\times Y_{u,w^{\prime}}.

Remark 3.6
.

We refer to the isomorphism above as a

.

Proof.

The proof consists of the following steps

(i)

for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have
{}^{J}\!c_{u}:Y_{v,w}\cong Y_{v,u}\times Y_{u,w}
;

(ii)

for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have
{}^{J}\!c_{u}:Y_{v^{\prime},w}\cong Y_{v^{\prime},u}\times Y_{u,w}
;

(iii)

for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have
{}^{J}\!c_{u}:Y_{v,w^{\prime}}\cong Y_{v,u}\times Y_{u,w^{\prime}}
;

(iv)

for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have
{}^{J}\!c_{u}:Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\cong Y_{v^{\prime},u}\times Y_{u,w^{\prime}}
.

Thanks to Lemma
3.4
(at
u
), we see

Y_{u,w}={}^{J}\!c_{u,-}(Y_{v,w})\quad\text{ and }\quad Y_{v,u}={}^{J}\!c_{u,+}(Y_{v,u}),\text{ for any }v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w.

Part (i) follows. We also see that
Y_{u,w}
and
Y_{v,u}
are connected components of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{u,w}(\mathbb{R})
and
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,u}(\mathbb{R})
, respectively.

Let

in part (i). Applying Lemma

(at

) to

(at

\displaystyle Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\overline{Y_{v,w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{v^{\prime},-}(Y_{v,w})}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v^{\prime},w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={}^{J}\!c_{w^{\prime},+}(Y_{v^{\prime},w})={}^{J}\!c_{w^{\prime},+}({}^{J}\!c_{v^{\prime},-}(Y_{v,w})).

,

and

\displaystyle\overline{Y_{v^{\prime},w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=\overline{{}^{J}\!c_{v^{\prime\prime},-}(Y_{v^{\prime},w})}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v^{\prime\prime},w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime\prime},w^{\prime\prime}}

4.
A regular CW complex

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

We fix
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
. Let
Y_{v,w}
be a connected component of
{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v,w}(\mathbb{R})
. We define

Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}=\overline{Y_{v,w}}\bigcap{}^{J}\!\mathring{\mathcal{B}}_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})\text{ for any $v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w$}.

Assume that for any
v{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}v^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}u{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w^{\prime}{\,{}^{J}\!\!\leqslant\,}w
, we have
Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}\subset\dot{u}B^{-}B^{+}/B^{+}
.
Then
\overline{Y_{v,w}}=\bigsqcup_{(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})}Y_{v^{\prime},w^{\prime}}
is a regular CW complex homeomorphic to a closed ball of dimension
{}^{J}\ell(w)-{}^{J}\ell(v)
.

4.1.
Links

Links can be defined for arbitrary Whitney stratified spaces; see
[

, Definition 4.25]
. We shall not consider this abstract definition here, but follow
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
and

7.1.
Motivation

The totally nonnegative partial flag variety

has a cellular decomposition and each cell admits an explicit parametrization. However,

does not have an obvious product structure. On the other hand, for the

In this section, we will introduce the basic

(\{(u,t)\in Q_{K};(r,r)\preceq(u,t)\preceq(v,w)\},\preceq)\longleftrightarrow\{\tilde{w}\in\tilde{W};\tilde{\nu}(r,r)\,{}^{I^{\flat}}\!\!\!\!\leqslant\tilde{w}\,{}^{I^{\flat}}\!\!\!\!\leqslant\tilde{\nu}(v,w),{}^{I^{\flat}}\!\!\!\!\leqslant).

Hence
Y_{r}
is a topological manifold with boundary

\partial Y_{r}=\bigsqcup_{(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in Q_{K};(r,r)\preceq(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\precneq(v,w)}\mathcal{P}_{K,(v^{\prime},w^{\prime}),>0}.

By Proposition
5.5
(4),
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{K,(v,w),>0}}=\bigcup_{r\in W^{K};(r,r)\preceq(v,w)}Y_{r}
is an open covering. In particular,
\overline{\mathcal{P}_{K,(v,w),>0}}
is a topological manifold with boundary

\bigcup_{r\in W^{K};(r,r)\preceq(v,w)}Y_{r}=\bigsqcup_{(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in Q_{K};(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\preceq(v,w)\text{ and }(r,r)\preceq(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\text{ for some }r\in W^{K}}\mathcal{P}_{K,(v^{\prime},w^{\prime}),>0}.

By definition,
(w^{\prime},w^{\prime})\preceq(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
for any
(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})\in Q_{K}
. In other words, there always exists
r\in W^{K}
with
(r,r)\preceq(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})
. This finishes the proof.

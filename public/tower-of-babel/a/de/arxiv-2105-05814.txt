D5-brane in AdS black holes with nonzero gauge flux
Koichi Nagasaki

Abstract
We find the probe D5-brane solution on the black home spacetime which is asymptomatically AdS 5 × S 5 \mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5} . These black holes have spherical, hyperbolic and toroidal structures. Depending on the gauge flux on the D5-brane, the D5-brane behaves differently. By adding the fundamental string, the potential energy of the interface solution and the Wilson loop is given in the case of non zero gauge flux.

Black hole solution

The solutions of the Einstein equation which are the asymptotically AdS is given as follows:

ds_{k,d+1}^{2}=-f_{k}(r)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{f_{k}(r)}+r^{2}d\Sigma_{k,d-1}^{2},

(1)

where
k
distinguishes three different topologies:
k=\pm 1,0
.
For
k=1
case this solution has the structure of sphere, for
k=0
this solution has the structure of torus and for
k=-1
this solution has the structure of hyperbolic space.
In the above metric the angular part is

d\Sigma_{k,d-1}^{2}=\begin{cases}d\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta d\Omega_{d-2}^{2},&(k=+1)\\
d\theta^{2}+d\Omega_{d-2}^{2},&(k=0)\\
d\theta^{2}+\sinh^{2}\theta d\Omega_{d-2}^{2},&(k=-1)\end{cases}.

(2)

The function
f_{k}(r)
is

f_{k}(r)=k+r^{2}-\frac{r_{\mathrm{m}}^{d-2}}{r^{d-2}},\;\;r_{\mathrm{m}}=\frac{16\pi G_{d+1}M}{(d-1)\Sigma_{k,d-1}},

(3)

where
\Sigma_{k,d-1}
is the dimensionless volume for each geometry
[
33
]
.
Especially, in the case of sphere, it is the volume of
(d-1)
-dimensional sphere:

\Omega_{k-1}=2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)
.
For toroidal and hyperbolic cases, these are defined by appropriately regularizing.
We define a function
s_{k}(\theta)
such that

s_{k}(\theta)=\begin{cases}\sin^{2}\theta&(k=+1)\\
1&(k=0)\\
\sinh^{2}\theta&(k=-1)\end{cases}.

(4)

By this notation, we write the angular part as

d\Sigma_{k,d-1}^{2}=d\theta^{2}+s_{k}(\theta)d\Omega_{d-2}^{2}
in the following equations.

We consider the black hole solution in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}\times S^{5}
where the black hole is locate in the center of the AdS spacetime.
We set the coordinates

(t,r,\theta,\phi,\psi)
on
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}
and
(\varphi,\chi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\phi_{3})
on
S^{5}
.
It is convenient to redefine the radial coordinate
y=1/r
where
y=0
corresponds to the AdS boundary.
In these coordinates, the metric is

ds_{k,5}^{2}=\frac{1}{y^{2}}\Big(-F_{k}(y)dt^{2}+\frac{dy^{2}}{F_{k}(y)}+d\Sigma_{k,3}^{2}\Big),

(5)

where a new metric function is

F_{k}(y):=y^{2}f_{k}(1/y)=1+ky^{2}-r_{\mathrm{m}}^{d-2}y^{d}.

(6)

It goes to unity when
y\rightarrow 0
.
Then it recovers the flat AdS spacetime sufficiently far from black holes.

We introduce a probe D5-brane which extends to the directions

t
,
\phi
,
\psi
in
\mathrm{AdS}_{5}
and
\varphi
and
\chi
in
S^{5}
planarly and occupies one-dimensional subspace in
(r,\theta)
plane:

y=y(\sigma),\;\;\theta=\theta(\sigma).

(7)

There exists a gauge flux on the D5-brane.
We assume it extends on the
S^{2}
subspace on
S^{5}
.
Then our assumption is

\mathcal{F}=-\kappa\;d\text{vol}[S^{2}]=-\kappa\sin\varphi d\varphi\wedge d\chi.

(8)

D5-brane action and EOM

The D5-brane action is

S=S_{\mathrm{DBI}}+S_{\mathrm{WZ}}=-T_{5}\int\sqrt{-\det(g_{k}+\mathcal{F})_{\mathrm{ind}}}+T_{5}\int\mathcal{F}\wedge C_{4}.

(9)

C_{4}
is the Ramond-Ramond 4-form which satisfies

dC_{4}=4d\mathrm{vol[AdS]}+4d\mathrm{vol}[S^{5}]
.
The volume form of the AdS spacetime is

\displaystyle d\text{vol[AdS]}=-\frac{1}{y^{5}}s_{k}(\theta)\sin\phi dtdyd\theta d\phi d\psi.

By definition we find the
C_{4}
as

C_{4}=-\frac{1}{y^{4}}s_{k}(\theta)\sin\phi dtd\theta d\phi d\psi+4\alpha_{4},

(10)

where
\alpha_{4}
is the 4-form on the
S^{5}
which satisfies
d\alpha_{4}=d\text{vol}[S^{5}]
.
Taking the product
\mathcal{F}\wedge C_{4}
,
\alpha_{4}
part vanishes and gives

\mathcal{F}\wedge C_{4}=\frac{\kappa}{y^{4}}s_{k}(\theta)\sin\phi\sin\varphi dtd\theta d\phi d\psi d\varphi d\chi.

(11)

To find the DBI action we calculate the determinant

\sqrt{-\det(g_{k}+\mathcal{F})_{\text{ind}}}=\frac{1}{y^{4}}\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}}\;s_{k}\sin\phi\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}}\sin\varphi.

(12)

The D5-brane action is obtained as the sum of the above terms:

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=-T_{5}\int_{\text{AdS}}dtd\sigma d\phi d\psi\int_{S^{2}}d\varphi d\chi\frac{s_{k}\sin\phi\sin\varphi}{y^{4}}\Big(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}}\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}}-\kappa\theta^{\prime}\Big)

\displaystyle=-(4\pi)^{2}T_{5}\Delta t\;\int d\sigma\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}\Big(\sqrt{1+\kappa^{2}}\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}}-\kappa\theta^{\prime}\Big).

(13)

To obtain the last form we performed the integral in the angular directions
(
\phi,\psi,\varphi,\chi
), where
the metrics on two spheres,
S^{2}\subset\mathrm{AdS}
and
S^{2}\subset S^{5}
, are

ds_{S^{2}\subset\mathrm{AdS}}^{2}=d\phi^{2}+\sin^{2}\phi d\psi^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad ds_{S^{2}\subset S^{5}}^{2}=d\varphi^{2}+\sin^{2}\varphi d\chi^{2}.

(14)

These coordinates take the value
\phi,\varphi\in[0,\pi),\;\psi,\chi\in[0,2\pi]
.

The Lagrangian is, eliminating the whole constant factor,

\mathcal{L}(\sigma)=\frac{s_{k}(\theta)}{y^{4}}\big(\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}}-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime}\big),

(15)

where we defined
\tilde{\kappa}:=\kappa(1+\kappa^{2})^{-1/2}
which takes the value
|\tilde{\kappa}|\leq 1
.
The equations of motion for
y
and
\theta
are

\frac{d}{d\sigma}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y^{\prime}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial y}=0,\;\;\frac{d}{d\sigma}\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\theta^{\prime}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\theta}=0.

Let us fix the gauge freedom as

\sqrt{y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}}=1
.
It simplifies the equations of motion as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}\Big(y^{\prime}\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}\Big)+4(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\frac{s_{k}}{y^{5}}-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}\partial F_{k}\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}

\displaystyle=0,

(16)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}\Big((\theta^{\prime}F_{k}-\tilde{\kappa})\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}\Big)-(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\frac{\partial s_{k}}{y^{4}}

\displaystyle=0.

(17)

By dividing by the common factor,
s_{k}/y^{4}
, these are

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}+\frac{4}{y}(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}\partial F_{k}

\displaystyle=0,

(18)

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime\prime}F_{k}+\theta^{\prime}y^{\prime}\partial F_{k}+(\theta^{\prime}F_{k}-\tilde{\kappa})\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}-(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\partial\log s_{k}

\displaystyle=0.

(19)

We fixed the gauge
y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}=1
.
Let us confirm it is preserved along the integral curve.
By differentiating by
\sigma
,

\frac{d}{d\sigma}(y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k})=2y^{\prime}y^{\prime\prime}+2\theta^{\prime}\theta^{\prime\prime}F_{k}+\theta^{\prime 2}y^{\prime}\partial F_{k}.

(20)

Substituting the equation of motion,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}(y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k})

\displaystyle=2y^{\prime}\Big(-y^{\prime}\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}-\frac{4}{y}(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})+\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}\partial F_{k}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\theta^{\prime}\Big(-\theta^{\prime}y^{\prime}\partial F_{k}-(\theta^{\prime}F_{k}-\tilde{\kappa})\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}+(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\partial\log s_{k}\Big)+\theta^{\prime 2}y^{\prime}\partial F_{k}

\displaystyle=-2(y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}}+2(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log(y^{-4})+2(1-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log s_{k}

\displaystyle=-2(y^{\prime 2}+\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}-1)\frac{d}{d\sigma}\log\frac{s_{k}}{y^{4}},

where the last expression is zero if we use the gauge condition.

Summarizing the above, we find the equations of motion:

\displaystyle y^{\prime\prime}+y^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}\partial\log s_{k}+\frac{4}{y}(1-y^{\prime 2}-\tilde{\kappa}\theta^{\prime})-\frac{1}{2}\theta^{\prime 2}\partial F_{k}

\displaystyle=0,

(21)

\displaystyle\theta^{\prime\prime}F_{k}+\theta^{\prime}y^{\prime}\partial F_{k}+(\theta^{\prime 2}F_{k}-1)\partial\log s_{k}-\frac{4}{y}(y^{\prime}\theta^{\prime}F_{k}-\tilde{\kappa}y^{\prime})

\displaystyle=0.

(22)

Here the functions are given as

\partial\log s_{k}=\begin{cases}2\cot\theta&(k=1)\\
0&(k=0)\\
2\coth\theta&(k=-1)\end{cases},\quad F_{k}=\begin{cases}1+y^{2}-r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}y^{4}&(k=1)\\
1-r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}y^{4}&(k=0)\\
1-y^{2}-r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}y^{4}&(k=-1)\end{cases}.

(23)

Wilson loop operator

We add a fundamental string which ends on the previous D5-brane.
We impose the Dirichlet boundary condition on the D5-brane and the AdS boundary.
We know that for spherical and toroidal cases the probe brane can extend into the black hole horizon.
We focus on these cases.

We look for a static solution.
The string is parametrized as

X^{0}(\tau,\lambda)=t(\tau),\;\;X^{1}(\tau,\lambda)=y(\lambda),\;\;X^{2}(\tau,\lambda)=\theta(\lambda),\;\;\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{1}).

(28)

We impose the Dirichlet boundary condition at the both ends of the string,
\lambda=0,\lambda_{1}
.

Boundary condition

Suppose that the brane solution is given by the embedding
(Y(\sigma),\Theta(\sigma))
and the string solution is given by
(y(\lambda),\theta(\lambda))
.
The boundary conditions at both ends of the string are

\displaystyle\lambda=0:

\displaystyle\quad\theta(0)=\theta_{0},\;\;\theta^{\prime}(\lambda)|_{\lambda=0}=0,

(29a)

\displaystyle\lambda=\lambda_{1}:

\displaystyle\quad\theta(\lambda_{1})=\Theta(\sigma_{1}),\;\;y(\lambda_{1})=Y(\sigma_{1}),\;\;\frac{dY(\sigma)}{d\Theta(\sigma)}\cdot\frac{dy(\lambda)}{d\theta(\lambda)}\Big|_{y=y_{1}}=-F_{k}(y_{1}).

(29b)

We choose the conformal gauge
h_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(1,1)
and move to the Euclidean metric.
The Polyakov action of this fundamental string is

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\lambda\sqrt{h}h^{ab}\partial_{a}X^{\mu}\partial_{b}X^{\nu}g_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int d\tau d\lambda y^{-2}\Big(F_{k}(y)\dot{t}^{2}+\frac{y^{\prime 2}}{F_{k}(y)}+\theta^{\prime 2}\Big),

(31)

where we used the metric

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{y^{2}}\Big(F_{k}(y)dt^{2}+\frac{dy^{2}}{F_{k}(y)}+d\Sigma_{k,3}^{2}\Big),

(32a)

\displaystyle F_{k}(y)

\displaystyle=1+ky^{2}-r_{\mathrm{m}}^{2}y^{4}.

(32b)

The equations of motion for
t
and
\theta
are

\ddot{t}=0,\;\;\frac{d}{d\lambda}\big(y^{-2}\theta^{\prime}\big)=0.

(33)

The first equation means
t
is a linear function of
\tau
.
The second can be solved by an integration constant
L

\theta^{\prime}=y^{2}L

The equation of motion for

\displaystyle 2y^{\prime\prime}\frac{y^{-2}}{F_{k}(y)}+y^{\prime 2}\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big(\frac{y^{-2}}{F_{k}(y)}\Big)-\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big(y^{-2}F_{k}(y)\Big)+2yL^{2}=0.

By multiplying
y^{\prime}

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\sigma}(y^{\prime 2}y^{-2}F_{k}(y)^{-1}-y^{-2}F_{k}(y)+y^{2}L^{2})=0.

Therefore we find

\displaystyle y^{\prime 2}y^{-2}F_{k}(y)^{-1}-y^{-2}F_{k}(y)+y^{2}L^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{const},

\displaystyle y^{\prime 2}-F_{k}(y)^{2}+y^{4}F_{k}(y)L^{2}

\displaystyle=(\mathrm{const})\times y^{2}F_{k}(y).

(34)

The Virasoro constraint says the constant on the righthand side is zero.

Virasoro constraint

The Virasoro constraint:
T_{00}=-T_{11}=0
.

T_{00}=\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{0}X\cdot\partial_{0}X-\partial_{1}X\cdot\partial_{1}X).

(35)

T_{00}=0
gives

y^{\prime}=\sqrt{F_{k}(y)(F_{k}(y)-y^{4}L^{2})}.

(36)

By the boundary condition (

y^{\prime}(\lambda_{1})=0
, this
L

L^{2}=F_{k}(y_{1})y_{1}^{-4}.

(37)

Then the integration constant

is determined by the string position on the D5-brane (see Figure
).
By substituting it,

is

y^{\prime}=\sqrt{F_{k}(y)\Big(F_{k}(y)-\frac{y^{4}}{y_{1}^{4}}F_{k}(y_{1})\Big)}.

(38)

By this constraint the Polyakov action is simplified as

\displaystyle\frac{dS_{\mathrm{P}}}{dt}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{y_{1}}d\sigma y^{-2}\Big[F_{k}(y)+\frac{y^{\prime 2}}{F_{k}(y)}+\theta^{\prime 2}\Big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{y_{1}}d\sigma y^{-2}F_{k}(y).

(39)

The integral for this action is diverge.
Hence we consider the two strings: one ends on the D5-brane and the other is on the black hole horizon.
We find the effect of the probe D5-brane by taking the difference of these two.
When the fundamental string ends on the D5-brane

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{\sigma_{1}}d\sigma y^{-2}F_{k}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{y_{1}}\frac{dy}{y^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{y^{-4}F_{k}(y)}{y^{-4}F_{k}(y)-y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1})}}

(40)

On the other hand if the string go through the horizon, since in this case
L=0
,

S_{\mathrm{P0}}=\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{y_{\mathrm{h}}}dyy^{-2}=\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\int_{0}^{y_{1}}\frac{dy}{y^{2}}+\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\Big[-y^{-1}\Big]_{y_{1}}^{y_{\mathrm{h}}},

(41)

where the horizon is determined by
F_{k}(y_{\mathrm{h}})=0

Then their difference
\Delta S_{\mathrm{P}}:=S_{\mathrm{P}}-S_{\mathrm{P0}}
is

\Big(\frac{T}{2\pi\alpha^{\prime}}\Big)^{-1}\Delta S_{\text{P}}=-\frac{1}{y_{1}}+\frac{1}{y_{\mathrm{h}}}+\int_{0}^{y_{1}}\frac{dy}{y^{2}}\Bigg(-1+\sqrt{\frac{y^{-4}F_{k}(y)}{y^{-4}F_{k}(y)-y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1})}}\Bigg).

(42)

The variation of
\theta
with respect to
y
is

\frac{d\theta}{dy}=\frac{\theta^{\prime}}{y^{\prime}}=\sqrt{\frac{y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1})}{F_{k}(y)(y^{-4}F_{k}(y)-y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1}))}}.

(43)

The initial value of
\theta
denotes as
\theta=\theta_{1}
.

\frac{\pi}{2}=\theta_{1}+\int_{0}^{y_{1}}dy\sqrt{\frac{y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1})}{F_{k}(y)(y^{-4}F_{k}(y)-y_{1}^{-4}F_{k}(y_{1}))}}.

(44)

5
Discussion

The interface is a nonlocal object which determines the boundary condition between two gauge theories.
This operator corresponds to a probe D5-brane in the bulk AdS spacetime.
In this paper we studied the behavior of this solution in the three different black hole spacetimes: sphere, toros and hyperbolic space.
Unlike the flat AdS spacetime
[
19
]
this D5-brane has a nontrivial configuration.
We found that for black hole spacetimes which has spherical and toroidal structures the probe D5-brane can exist inside the horizon only when the gauge flux on D5-brane is zero and bend so that it avoids the horizon if the brane has a nonzero gauge flux.
For hyperbolic space there is no such a solution even if the gauge flux is zero.

From the perspective of gauge theory two different gauge groups
\mathrm{SU}(N)
and
\mathrm{SU}(N-k)
can exist on the both sides of the interface, where the difference of the gauge group
k
is related to the gauge flux on the D5-brane
[
19
]
as

k=-\frac{T_{5}}{T_{3}}\int\mathcal{F}=\frac{\kappa}{\pi\alpha^{\prime}}.

(56)

Here
T_{5}
and
T_{3}
are tension of the D5-brane and the D5-brane.
We introduced the gauge flux
\kappa
on the D5-brane.

By adding a fundamental string we calculate the potential energy between the interface and a Wilson loop.
For a trivial D5-brane solution (
\kappa=0
) we find the behavior of potential energy between the interface and the test particle (Figure
7
for sphere and Figure
7
for torus).

We were interested in a more general case, where the probe brane has non zero gauge flux.
In this case where gauge theories with different gauge groups exist on the both sides of the interface.
For a special case (Figure
8
) where the string has the trivial configuration, we found the potential energy by the Nambu-Goto action (Figure
14
, Figure
14
and Figure
14
).
For non trivial gauge flux, we found this potential energy has the similar behavior although the behavior of zero flux solution is very different for the hyperbolic spacetime case: this case has no solution which approaches the black hole horizon (Figure
4
).
For all topologies (sphere, torus and hyperbolic space) the potential energy between the interface and the Wilson loop becomes an increasing function of
\kappa
and also increasing with respect to the mass of black holes.

An important direction is to generalize the correspondence to a time-dependent process by considering a drag force
[
34
]
, which was described by a string with constant velocity, or considering the rotating black holes.

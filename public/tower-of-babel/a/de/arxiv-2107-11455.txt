Introduction

In the present paper we will study curvature properties of invariant almost Hermitian structures on homogeneous spaces. Special attention will be given to providing examples of
Kähler like scalar curvature
(
Klsc
)
metric
, that is, almost Hermitian structures
(g,J)
satisfying
s=2s_{\rm C}
, where
s
is the Riemannian scalar curvature and
s_{\rm C}
is the Chern scalar curvature on generalized flag manifolds.

Recall the geometric meaning of the Riemannian scalar curvature
s
: given a Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
, the volume of the geodesic ball of radius
r
with center at
p\in M
has the asymptotic expansion as follows:

\displaystyle\operatorname{vol}(B(p,r))=\omega_{n}r^{n}\bigg(1-\dfrac{s(p)}{6(n+2)}r^{2}+\mathcal{O}\big(r^{4}\big)\bigg),

(1.1)

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unity ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see
[
6
,
9
]
. Therefore the Riemannian scalar curvature
s(p)
is positive or negative at a point
p
, if the volume of a small geodesic ball at
p
is respectively smaller or larger than the corresponding Euclidean ball of the same radius. If the metric is not Kähler, there is not an immediate geometric interpretation for the Chern scalar curvature
s_{\rm C}
. However if the Hermitian structure is non-Kähler but satisfies
s=2s_{\rm C}
, a curvature
s_{\rm C}
has an interpretation like
s
in the expression (
1.1
).

Dabkowski and Lock in
[
9
]
call the Hermitian metrics satisfying the equation

\displaystyle s=2s_{\rm C}

by
Kähler like scalar curvature
(
Klsc
)
metric
, and the authors exhibit examples of non-compact Hermitian manifolds satisfying
s=2s_{\rm C}
.

According to
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
nearly-Kähler manifolds and almost-Kähler manifolds satisfying
2s_{\rm C}-s=0
are Kähler. Fu and Zhou show in
[
12
]
that if the pair
(g,J)
belongs to the Gray–Hervella class
\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{3}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{4}
with
2s_{\rm C}=s
then
(g,J)
is Kähler. Recently Lejmi–Upmeier propose the following question
[
18
, Remark 3.3]
:

Question 1.1
.

Do higher-dimensional closed almost Hermitian non-Kähler manifolds with
2s_{\rm C}=s
exist?

In this paper we will investigate the Question
1.1
in a class of high dimensional homogeneous spaces called
generalized flag manifolds
, equipped with an invariant almost Hermitian structure. It is well know that this class of homogeneous spaces have a very rich almost Hermitian geometry, see for instance
[
20
,
21
]
. We will proceed by computing explicitly the Hermitian scalar curvatures, using the tools of Lie theory. The approach to compute explicitly the Hermitian scalar curvatures on flag manifolds is by analyzing the decomposition of the covariant derivative of the Kähler form with respect to the Levi-Civita connection, the codifferential of the Lee form, and the scalar Riemannian curvature. See Section
3
for details.

Let us denote by
\nabla^{t}
be the 1-parameter family of connection introduced by Gauduchon in
[
14
]
:

\displaystyle g\big(\nabla^{t}_{X}Z,Y\big)={}

\displaystyle g(D_{X}Z,Y)-\frac{1}{2}g(J(D_{X}J)Z,Y)

\displaystyle+\frac{t}{4}g((D_{JY}J+JD_{Y}J)X,Z)-\frac{t}{4}g((D_{JZ}J+JD_{Z}J)X,Y),

where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection.
It is well know that
\nabla^{1}
coincides with the Chern connection.
According to
[
12
]
, let us define the Hermitian scalar curvatures
s_{1}(t)=R^{t}(u_{\overline{i}},u_{i},u_{j},u_{\overline{j}})

and
s_{2}(t)=R^{t}(u_{\overline{i}},u_{j},u_{i},u_{\overline{j}})
,
where
R^{t}
is the curvature tensor associated to
\nabla^{t}
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1,2,\dots,n}
is a unitary frame. We will call
s_{1}(t)
and
s_{2}(t)
the first and second Hermitian scalar curvature. One of the main features of the generalized flag manifolds is that the first scalar curvature
s_{1}(t)
is constant in
t
and we will denote this curvature just by
s_{1}
and it coincides with the Chern scalar curvature
s_{\rm C}
. Section
3
for details about the comments above.

With the discussion above in mind, let us rephrase the Question
1.1
in our context:

Question 1.2
.

Do higher-dimensional generalized flag manifolds
G/K
equipped with
G
-invariant almost Hermitian structure with
2s_{1}=s
exist?

Preliminaries: generalized flag manifolds

and invariant almost Hermitian structures

In this section we recall some well know results about the geometry of generalized flag manifolds, from a Lie theoretical point of view. References about Riemannian and Hermitian geometry of flag manifolds are
[
1
,
3
,
6
,
20
,
21
]
.

2.1
Generalized flag manifolds

Let
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
be a complex semi-simple Lie algebra. Given a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}
of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
, denotes by
\Pi
the set of roots with respect to the pair
\big(\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}},\mathfrak{h}\big)
. We have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\Pi}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},

where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}\colon\forall H\in\mathfrak{h},[H,X]=\alpha(H)X\}
denote the corresponding
1
-dimensional (complex) root space.

The Cartan–Killing form is defined by

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle=\tr(\ad(X)\ad(Y))

and its restriction to
\mathfrak{h}
is non-degenerate. Given a root
\alpha\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
we define
H_{\alpha}
by
\alpha(\cdot)=\langle H_{\alpha},\cdot\rangle
. Moreover we denote
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{H_{\alpha}\colon\alpha\in\Pi\}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}}^{*}
being the real subspace of
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
spanned by the roots.

Let us fix a Weyl basis of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
given by

\displaystyle X_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha},\qquad\text{such that}\quad\langle X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha}\rangle=1\quad\text{and}\quad[X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}]=m_{\alpha,\beta}X_{\alpha+\beta},

with
m_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathbb{R}
,
m_{-\alpha,-\beta}=-m_{\alpha,\beta}
and
m_{\alpha,\beta}=0
if
\alpha+\beta
is not a root.

Let
\Pi^{+}\subset\Pi
be a choice of positive roots,
\Sigma
be the corresponding system of simple roots and
\Theta
be a subset of
\Sigma
. Let us fix the following notation:
\langle\Theta\rangle
is the set of roots spanned by
\Theta
,
\Pi_{M}=\Pi\setminus\langle{\Theta}\rangle
be the set of complementary roots and
\Pi_{M}^{+}
be the set of complementary positive roots.

Let

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\Theta}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\langle\Theta\rangle^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\langle\Theta\rangle^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha}\oplus\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{M}^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}

be a parabolic sub-algebra of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
determined by
\Theta
.

The generalized flag manifold
\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}
is the homogeneous space

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}=G^{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\Theta},

where
G^{\mathbb{C}}
is a complex connected Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
P_{\Theta}
is the normalizer of
\mathfrak{p}_{\Theta}
in
G^{\mathbb{C}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the compact real form of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\Span_{\mathbb{R}}\{i\mathfrak{h}_{\mathbb{R}},A_{\alpha},iS_{\alpha};\alpha\in\Pi\},

where
A_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}-X_{-\alpha}
and
S_{\alpha}=X_{\alpha}+X_{-\alpha}
. We remark that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is semi-simple.

Denote by
G
the compact real form of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
with
\Lie(G)=\mathfrak{g}
and let
\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}
the Lie algebra of
K_{\Theta}:=P_{\Theta}\cap G
. It is well known that the Lie group
K_{\Theta}\subset G
is a centralizer of a torus.

We have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\langle\Theta\rangle^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}\oplus\sum_{\alpha\in\langle\Theta\rangle^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\alpha},

where
\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}^{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the complexification of the real Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}=\mathfrak{g}\cap\mathfrak{p}_{\Theta}
.

The Lie group
G
also acts transitively on
\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}
and we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}=G^{\mathbb{C}}/P_{\Theta}=G/(P_{\Theta}\cap G)=G/K_{\Theta}.

When
\Theta=\varnothing
we have the following decomposition

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\beta\in\Pi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}\oplus\sum_{\beta\in\Pi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta},

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{\Theta}=\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\sum_{\beta\in\Pi^{+}}\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}

is a minimal parabolic sub-algebra (Borel sub-algebra) of
\mathfrak{g}^{\mathbb{C}}
and

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}=G^{\mathbb{C}}/P=G/T

is called
full flag manifold
, where
T=P\cap G
is a maximal torus of
G
.

Recall that the flag manifold
\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}=G/K_{\Theta}
is a reductive homogeneous space, that is, there exists a subspace
\mathfrak{m}
of
\mathfrak{g}
such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}\oplus\mathfrak{m}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Ad(k)\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{m}\qquad\forall k\in K_{\Theta},

and one can identify the tangent space
T_{x_{0}}\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}
with
\mathfrak{m}
, where
x_{0}=eK_{\Theta}
is the origin of the
\mathbb{F}_{\Theta}
(trivial coset).

Let us give a description of the tangent space
\mathfrak{m}
in terms of the Lie algebra structure of
\mathfrak{g}
as follows:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{\Theta}\oplus\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{M}}{\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}},

with
\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}=\mathfrak{g}\cap(\mathfrak{g}_{\beta}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{-\beta})
and for each root
\beta\in\Pi_{M}
,
\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}
has real dimension two and it is spanned by
A_{\beta}
and
\sqrt{-1}S_{\beta}
, and we have the following identification

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}=\sum_{\beta\in\Pi_{M}}\mathfrak{u}_{\beta}.

An important ingredient to study invariant tensors on homogeneous space is the
isotropy representation
. We will restrict ourselves to the situation of flag manifolds. In this case, since the flag manifolds are
reductive
homogeneous spaces it is well know that the isotropy representation is equivalent to the following representation

\displaystyle\Ad(k)\mid_{\mathfrak{m}}\colon\ \mathfrak{m}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}.

The isotropy representation decomposes
\mathfrak{m}
into irreducible components, that is,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{n},

where each component
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
satisfies
\Ad(K_{\Theta})(\mathfrak{m}_{i})\subset\mathfrak{m}_{i}
.
We have also that each component
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
is irreducible, that is, the only invariant sub-spaces of
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
by
\Ad(K_{\Theta})|_{\mathfrak{m}_{i}}
are the trivial sub-spaces. We will call the sub-spaces
\mathfrak{m}_{i}
by
isotropic summands
of the isotropy representation.

Remark 2.1
.

In the sequel we will omit the symbol
\Theta
whenever there is no risk of confusion. We will denote a flag manifold just by
\mathbb{F}=G/K
.

2.2
Invariant metrics

Let us denote by
(\cdot,\cdot)
the Cartan–Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
. For each
\Ad(K)-
invariant inner product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Lambda}
on
\mathfrak{m}
, there exists a unique
(\cdot,\cdot)-
self-adjoint, positive operator
\Lambda\colon\mathfrak{m}\longrightarrow\mathfrak{m}
commuting with
\Ad(k)\left|{}_{\mathfrak{m}}\right.
for all
k\in K
such that

\displaystyle(X,Y)_{\Lambda}=(\Lambda X,Y),\qquad X,Y\in\mathfrak{m}.

Therefore an invariant Riemannian metric
g
on
\mathbb{F}
is completely determined by the invariant inner product
(X,Y)_{\Lambda}
, and the inner product is determined by
\Lambda
.

The vectors
A_{\alpha}
,
\sqrt{-1}S_{\alpha}
,
\alpha\in\Pi
, are eigenvectors of
\Lambda
associated to the same eigenvalue
\lambda_{\alpha}
.

The invariant inner product
(X,Y)_{\Lambda}
admits a natural extension to a symmetric bilinear form on
\mathfrak{m}^{\mathbb{C}}
. On the complexified tangent space we have
\Lambda(X_{\alpha})=\lambda_{\alpha}X_{\alpha}
with
\lambda_{\alpha}>0
and
\lambda_{-\alpha}=\lambda_{\alpha}
.

Notation
.

In the sequence of this work, we will abuse the notation and will denote the invariant metric
g
just by the operator
\Lambda
associated to the invariant inner product. We also denote the invariant metric
g
just by a
n
-tuple of positive number
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
representing the eigenvalues of the operator
\Lambda
and parametrized by the number of irreducible components.

2.3
Invariant almost complex structures

Definition 2.2
.

An almost complex structure on the flag manifold
\mathbb{F}
is a tensor
J
such that for every point
x\in\mathbb{F}
, there is an endomorphism
J\colon T_{x}\mathbb{F}\longrightarrow T_{x}\mathbb{F}
such that
J^{2}=-\id
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
G
-invariant almost complex structure
J
on
\mathbb{F}=G/K
is an almost complex structure that satisfies

\displaystyle J_{ux}={\rm d}E_{u}J_{x}{\rm d}E_{u^{-1}},\qquad\text{for all}\quad u\in G,

where
{\rm d}E_{u}\colon T(G/K)\to T(G/K)
denotes the differential of the left translation by
u
, that is, for all
X\in T_{x}(G/K)
we have

\displaystyle{\rm d}E_{u}J_{x}X=J_{ux}{\rm d}E_{u}X.

The following result allows us to describe invariant almost complex structures on flag manifolds in terms of complex structure in a simple vector space, namely the tangent space at the origin (trivial coset) of the homogeneous space.

Proposition 2.4
.

There exist an
1-1
correspondence between a
G
-invariant almost complex structure
J
and a linear endomorphism
J_{x_{0}}\colon T_{x_{0}}\mathbb{F}\to T_{x_{0}}\mathbb{F}
satisfying
J_{x_{0}}^{2}=-\id
and commute with the isotropy representation, that is,

\displaystyle\Ad^{G/K}(k)J_{x_{0}}=J_{x_{0}}\Ad^{G/K}(k)\qquad\text{for all}\quad k\in K.

An interesting consequence of the Proposition
2.4
is that
J(\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha})=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
, where
\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}
is the root space associated to the root

. The eigenvalue of

\pm\sqrt{-1}
and the eigenvectors on

As a consequence of the discussion above we conclude that an invariant almost complex structure on

Proposition 2.5

(

3
Scalar curvatures of invariant almost Hermitian structures

3.1
Review: general results about curvatures of almost Hermitian structures

Let
(M,J,g)
be an almost Hermitian manifold with real dimension
2n
, with
J
being an almost complex structure orthogonal with respect to the Riemannian metric
g
. A linear connection
\nabla
on
M
is Hermitian if it preserves the metric
g
and the almost complex structure
J
, that is,
\nabla g=0
and
\nabla J=0
(we are not assuming that
J
is integrable).

Lets us recall the
1
-parameter family of Hermitian connection defined by Gauduchon in
[
14
]
as follow:

\displaystyle g\big(\nabla^{t}_{X}Z,Y\big)={}

\displaystyle g(D_{X}Z,Y)-\frac{1}{2}g(J(D_{X}J)Z,Y)

\displaystyle+\frac{t}{4}g((D_{JY}J+JD_{Y}J)X,Z)-\frac{t}{4}g((D_{JZ}J+JD_{Z}J)X,Y),

where
D
is the Levi-Civita connection.

There are three special cases:

(i)

t=0
,
\nabla^{0}
is the first canonical Hermitian connection, also know as Lichnerowicz connection or minimal connection.

(ii)

t=1
,
\nabla^{1}
is the second canonical Hermitian connection, also know as Chern connection (this connection was used by Chern in the integrable case, see
[
8
]
).

(iii)

t=-1
,
\nabla^{-1}
is the Bismut connection. In the integrable case,
\nabla^{-1}
is characterized by its anti-symmetric torsion, see for instance
[
10
,
17
]
.

According to
[
12
]
, let us define the Hermitian scalar curvatures
s_{1}(t)
and
s_{2}(t)
by

\displaystyle s_{1}(t)=R^{t}(u_{\overline{i}},u_{i},u_{j},u_{\overline{j}}),\qquad s_{2}(t)=R^{t}(u_{\overline{i}},u_{j},u_{i},u_{\overline{j}}),

where
R^{t}
is the curvature tensor associated to
\nabla^{t}
and
\{u_{i}\}_{i=1,2,\dots,n}
is a unitary frame. We call
s_{1}(t)
and
s_{2}(t)
the
first
and
second
Hermitian scalar curvature, respectively.

For an Hermitian manifold equipped with the Chern connection
\nabla^{1}
or the Bismut connection
\nabla^{-1}
, the relations between the Hermitian scalar curvatures and Riemannian scalar curvatures were widely study, see for instance
[
13
,
19
]
.

Let
\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{2n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame of
(M,g,J)
. Recall the
J
-twisted version of the Ricci tensor, called
J
-Ricci tensor and denoted by
\Ric_{J}
(also called the
*
-Ricci tensor
[
12
,
16
,
22
]
) defined by

\displaystyle\Ric_{J}(X,Y)=R(e_{A},X,Je_{A},JY).

The corresponding
J
-scalar curvature, denoted by
s_{J}
is given by
s_{J}=\Ric_{J}(e_{A},e_{A})
.

The Nijenhuis tensor
N
is given by

\displaystyle N(X,Y)=-[JX,JY]+J[JX,Y]+J[X,JY]+[X,Y],

where
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)
.

Let us consider the fundamental (or Kähler) 2-form

\displaystyle F(X,Y)=g(JX,Y).

The Lee form
\alpha_{F}
of
(M,J,g)
is defined by

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}=J\delta F,

where
\delta=-{*{\rm d}*}
is the codifferential with respect to
g
. The Lee form is also defined by

\displaystyle{\rm d}F=({\rm d}F)_{0}+\dfrac{1}{n-1}\alpha_{F}\wedge F,

where
({\rm d}F)_{0}
is the primitive part of
{\rm d}F
.

The covariant derivative of
F
with respect to the Levi-Civita connection
D
is

\displaystyle(DF)(X,Y,Z)=\dfrac{1}{2}\left[{\rm d}F(X,Y,Z)-{\rm d}F(X,JY,JZ)-N(JX,Y,Z)\right].

Moreover,

\displaystyle(DF)(X,Y,Z)=-(DF)(X,Z,Y)=-(DF)(X,JY,JZ).

The following expression of
DF
will be very useful for our purposes (see
[
12
,
14
]
)

\displaystyle(DF)(X,Y,Z)={}

\displaystyle({\rm d}F)^{-}(X,Y,Z)-\dfrac{1}{2}N(JX,Y,Z)

\displaystyle+\dfrac{1}{2}[({\rm d}F)^{+}(X,Y,Z)-({\rm d}F)^{+}(X,JY,JZ)],

where

({\rm d}F)^{+}
is the
(1,2)+(2,1)
-part of
{\rm d}F
and
({\rm d}F)^{-}
is the
(0,3)+(3,0)
-part of
{\rm d}F
.

Consider
N^{0}=N-\mathfrak{b}N
, where
\mathfrak{b}
is the Bianchi projector,
\mathfrak{b}N^{0}=0
and
\mathfrak{b}N
is the anti-symmetric part of
N
defined by

\displaystyle\mathfrak{b}N(X,Y,Z)=\dfrac{1}{3}[N(X,Y,Z)+N(Y,Z,X)+N(Z,X,Y)].

According to
[
14
]
, we have

\displaystyle 3\mathfrak{b}N(X,Y,Z)=({\rm d}^{c}F)^{-}(X,Y,Z)=({\rm d}F)^{-}(JX,JY,JZ).

The four components described above
({\rm d}F)^{-}
,
N^{0}
,
({\rm d}F)^{+}_{0}
and
\alpha_{F}
provide us several geometric information about an almost Hermitian manifold. The
16
classes of almost Hermitian structures described by Gray–Hervella in
[
16
]
correspond to the vanishing of the some subset of

\displaystyle\big\{({\rm d}F)^{-},N^{0},({\rm d}F)^{+}_{0},\alpha_{F}\big\}.

Let us describe some remarkable classes of almost Hermitian structures:

•

\{0\}=

Kähler class
: all components vanish,
({\rm d}F)^{-}=N^{0}=({\rm d}F)^{+}_{0}=\alpha_{F}=0
.

•

\mathcal{W}_{1}=

nearly-Kähler class
:

.

•

\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}=

(1,2)
-symplectic manifolds
(or

i-Kähler):
({\rm d}F)^{+}_{0}=\alpha_{F}=0
.

•

\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{3}=

:
\alpha_{F}=0
.

The Hermitian metric
g
induces a natural inner product on
\wedge^{k}M
, the bundle of real
k
-forms, and also on
TM\otimes\wedge^{k}M
the bundle of
TM
-valuated
k
-forms. The norm of the covariant derivative of the Kähler form is given by

\displaystyle\|DF\|^{2}=\|{\rm d}F\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\big\|N^{0}\big\|^{2}-\dfrac{2}{3}\|({\rm d}F)^{-}\|^{2}=\|({\rm d}F)^{+}\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{4}\big\|N^{0}\big\|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{3}\|({\rm d}F)^{-}\|^{2}.

In particular, if

is integrable, then

.

3.2
Computations on generalized flag manifolds

Let us consider a flag manifold
G/K
. We will consider on
G/K

g
and an invariant almost complex structure
J
. Recall the notation introduced in Section
2
: we will represent an invariant metric
g
by an
n

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
, where

is the number of irreducible components of the isotropy representation of

\mathfrak{m}=\mathfrak{m}_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathfrak{m}_{n}

\{X_{\alpha}\}
is a basis of

induced by the Weyl basis then

has length

is parametrized by a set of sign
\{\varepsilon_{\alpha}\}=\pm 1
, with
\varepsilon_{-\alpha}=-\varepsilon_{\alpha}
, where

is in the set of roots
\Pi
. Each vector

is an eigenvector of

with eigenvalue
\varepsilon_{\alpha}\sqrt{-1}
and every vector in an irreducible component

is associated to the same
\varepsilon_{i}
.

We will write the Nijenhuis tensor in a similar way as
[
21
]
. We have
N=0
, except in the following situation:

\displaystyle g(N(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}),X_{\gamma}))=-\lambda_{\gamma}m_{\alpha,\beta}(\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta}+\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\gamma}+\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\gamma}+1),

where
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
.

We also have

\displaystyle g((N(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}),JX_{\gamma})_{=}-\sqrt{-1}\lambda_{\gamma}m_{\alpha,\beta}(\epsilon_{\alpha}\epsilon_{\beta}\epsilon_{\gamma}+\epsilon_{\alpha}+\epsilon_{\beta}+\epsilon_{\gamma}),

where
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
.

The next result was initially proved by San Martin-Negreiros
[
21
]
in the case of full flag manifolds and by R. de Jesus in her Ph.D. Thesis for the case of generalized flag manifold. We include the proof here for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 3.3
.

Every invariant almost Hermitian structure
(g,J)
on a generalized flag manifolds is cosymplectic.

Proof.

An almost Hermitian structure is cosymplectic if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(X)=\dfrac{1}{2n-1}\sum_{i}{\rm d}F(X,X_{i},Y_{i})=0,

where
\{X_{i}\}
is a basis of the tangent space,
\{Y_{i}\}
is a basis of the dual space with relation a nondegenerate form
F
. We take
\{X_{i}\}=\big\{A_{\alpha},\sqrt{-1}S_{\alpha};\alpha\in\Pi^{+}_{M}\big\}
and
\{Y_{i}\}=\big\{\sqrt{-1}S_{\alpha},A_{\alpha};\allowbreak\alpha\in\Pi^{+}_{M}\big\}
. So,

\displaystyle{\rm d}F(X,X_{\alpha}-X_{-\alpha},{\rm i}X_{\alpha}+{\rm i}X_{-\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad{}={\rm i}\,{\rm d}F(X,X_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})+{\rm i}\,{\rm d}F(X,X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha})-{\rm i}\,{\rm d}F(X,X_{-\alpha},X_{\alpha})-{\rm i}\,{\rm d}F(X,X_{-\alpha},X_{-\alpha})

\displaystyle\qquad{}=2{\rm i}({\rm d}F(X,X_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})+{\rm d}F(X,X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha})).

We know that
{\rm d}F(X_{\alpha},X_{\beta},X_{\gamma})=0
unless
\alpha+\beta+\gamma=0
.
Thus, we take
X=X_{\beta}
and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\rm d}F(X_{\beta},X_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})=0,&\text{because if}\quad\alpha+\beta+\alpha=0\quad\text{then}\quad\beta=-2\alpha,\\
{\rm d}F(X_{\beta},X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha})=0,&\text{because if}\quad\alpha+\beta-\alpha=0\quad\text{then}\quad\beta=0,\end{cases}

and it is a contradiction.
Therefore, for every root
\gamma
,

\displaystyle\alpha_{F}(X)=\dfrac{1}{2n-1}\sum_{\alpha>0}{\rm d}F(X_{\gamma},X_{\alpha}-X_{-\alpha},{\rm i}X_{\alpha}+{\rm i}X_{-\alpha})=0.

∎

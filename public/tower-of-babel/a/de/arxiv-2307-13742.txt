Dirac cohomology and Θ \Theta -correspondence for complex dual pairs
S. Afentoulidis-Almpanis, G. Liu, S. Mehdi

Abstract
. We study the behavior of Dirac cohomology under Howe’s Θ \Theta -correspondence in the case of complex reductive dual pairs. More precisely, if ( G 1 , G 2 ) (G_{1},G_{2}) is a complex reductive dual pair with G 1 G_{1} and G 2 G_{2} viewed as real groups, we describe those Harish-Chandra modules π 1 \pi_{1} of G 1 G_{1} with nonzero Dirac cohomology whose Θ \Theta -liftings Θ ⁡ ( π 1 ) \Theta(\pi_{1}) still have nonzero Dirac cohomology. In this case, we compute explicitly the Dirac cohomology of Θ ⁡ ( π 1 ) \Theta(\pi_{1}) .



1.
Introduction

In the late 1970’s, Howe introduced the
\Theta
-correspondence in a series of papers
[
How79
,
How89a
,
How89b
]
to relate irreducible representations of reductive dual pairs
(G_{1},G_{2})
, i.e. of reductive groups
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
such that
G_{1}
is the centralizer of
G_{2}
, and vice versa, when they are viewed as subgroups of a larger group. The
\Theta
-correspondence, or
\Theta
-lifting, and its applications have been broadly studied. In particular, the
\Theta
-correspondence has been established in many cases, including the case of Archimedean local fields
[
How89b
]
, p-adic local fields with
p
odd
[
Wal90
]
, finite fields
[
AMR96
]
, dual pairs of general linear groups for arbitrary residue characteristic
[
Mín08
]
, for orthogonal-symplectic or unitary dual pairs
[
GT16
]
or for quaternionic dual pairs
[
GS17
]
. Our results involve the explicit description by Adams and Barbasch of the
\Theta
-correspondence in the case of complex Lie groups viewed as real groups
[
AB95
]
.

On the other hand, in the early 1970’s, geometric Dirac operators were used by Parthasarathy to realize explicitly discrete series representations of a real semisimple Lie group
G
in terms of
L^{2}
-sections of twisted vector bundles over the Riemannian symmetric space
G/K

[
Par72
]
. (Here
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
.) Later, as a byproduct, he deduced a necessary condition for unitarity of highest weight modules in terms of an inequality on the infinitesimal characters, known as the Parthasarathy - Dirac inequality
[
Par80
]
. Though this inequality provides an efficient tool to detect non-unitary modules, it does not single out their infinitesimal characters. To solve this issue, in the late 1990’s, Vogan introduced an algebraic analogue of Parthasarthy’s Dirac operator and defined the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(X)
for an irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X
. (Here
{\mathfrak{g}}
stands for the complexified Lie algebra of
G
.) Then he conjectured that nonzero Dirac cohomology reveals the infinitesimal character of a unitary irreducible
X

[
Vog97
]
. Vogan’s conjecture was proved by Huang and Pandžić in 2001
[
HP02
]
and was extended by Kostant to the case of reductive homogeneous spaces
G/H

[
Kos03
]
(known as Kostant cubic Dirac cohomology), paving the way for tremendous research activities. For finite-dimensional module Dirac cohomology coincides with the kernel of the Dirac operator and is well-understood
[
Kos99
,
HKP09
,
MZ14b
]
. We mention that the
kernel of non-cubic
Dirac operators for
finite-dimensional
modules was studied
by the first author in
[
Afe21
]
. However, for infinite-dimensional modules, except for unitary modules, Dirac cohomology need not be the kernel of the Dirac operator and may be hard to compute. Nevertheless, just to mention a few cases, Kostant constructed quotients of Verma modules with non-zero (cubic) Dirac cohomology
[
Kos03
]
, while Barbasch and Pandžić computed Dirac cohomology for unipotent representations of complex groups
[
BP11
]
.

It turns out that Dirac cohomology provides an interesting invariant which can be used to classify
({\mathfrak{g}},K)
-modules. Indeed, modules having nonzero Dirac cohomology include most of the
A_{\mathfrak{q}}(\lambda)
-modules, in particular the discrete series representations, finite-dimensional modules, unitary highest weight modules, and many unipotent representations. Dirac cohomology can also be related to coherent families and characters. In
[
MPV17
]
, Mehdi, Pandžić and Vogan defined a notion of algebraic Dirac index and showed that this behaves well with respect to translation functors defined for coherent families of
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules. Later, Mehdi, Pandžić, Vogan and Zierau defined a Dirac index polynomial over the Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
and connected this polynomial with nilpotent orbits and associated cycles of Harish-Chandra modules
[
MPVZ20
]
. Dirac cohomology has also become a useful tool in
C^{*}
-algebras and non-commutative geometry (see for instance
[
CHST22
,
CHS22
]
).

In this paper, we study the behavior of the Dirac cohomology under the
\Theta
-correspondence. More precisely, let

(G_{1},G_{2})
be a reductive dual pair (see Sections
3
and
4
for definitions). Let

\pi_{1}
be a Harish-Chandra module of
G_{1}
and
\Theta(\pi_{1})
the
\Theta
-lifting of
\pi_{1}
. By convention, if

\pi_{1}
is not in the
\Theta
-correspondence, we write
\Theta(\pi_{1})=0
. Write
H_{D}(\pi_{1})
for the Dirac cohomology of
\pi_{1}
. The Dirac series of
G_{1}
(resp.
G_{2}
) is the set of Harish-Chandra modules
\pi_{1}
(resp.
\pi_{2}
) with nonzero Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(\pi_{1})
(resp.
H_{D}(\pi_{2})
).

Problem 1.1
.

Find those Harish-Chandra modules
\pi_{1}
in the Dirac series of
G_{1}
for which the
\Theta
-lifting

\Theta(\pi_{1})
belongs to the Dirac series of
G_{2}
. Moreover, determine explicitly

H_{D}(\Theta(\pi_{1}))
whenever it is nonzero.

In this paper, we will address this problem in the case when
G
is a classical complex Lie group viewed as a real group. For complex Lie groups, to our knowledge, the study of Dirac series was initiated by Barbasch and Pandžić in
[
BP11
]
. The authors provide sufficient conditions for certain families of unitary representations of
G
to have nonzero Dirac cohomology (see Section
4
). They also conjectured necessary conditions for a representation of
G
to be in the Dirac series. The first results supporting this conjecture were obtained in
[
Don15
,
DD20
]
, while the full conjecture was proved recently by Barbasch, Dong and Wong in
[
BDW22
]
. Our study involves some of these results.

Our paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we present an overview of the classification of irreducible representations of complex Lie groups formulated by Zhelobenko in
[
Zhe74
]
. The main results of this section are gathered in Theorem
2.2
. In Section
3
, we recall basics facts about the
\Theta
- correspondence and collect the results of Barbasch and Adams
[
AB95
]
we will need. Next, in Section
4
, we discuss some features of the Dirac cohomology, in particular several useful results of Dong and Wong
[
BDW22
]
, as well as a list of the unipotent representations with nonzero Dirac cohomology from
[
BP11
]
. Finally, Sections
5
and
6
contain our main results, i.e. an explicit study of the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}(\Theta(\pi_{1}))
of the
\Theta
-lifting
\Theta(\pi_{1})
of
\pi_{1}
, when
\pi_{1}
is in the Dirac series of
G_{1}
for reductive dual pairs of type II and I (Theorem
5.1
, Theorem
6.1
respectively).

2.
Irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules of complex Lie groups

In this section, we recall the classification of irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules given by Zhelobenko in
[
Zhe74
]
when
G
is a complex semisimple Lie group viewed as a real group. Let
G
be such a Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be its Lie algebra. We fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
in
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and a positive system
\Delta^{+}
for the set
\Delta:=\Delta(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{0})
of
{\mathfrak{t}}_{0}
-roots in
{\mathfrak{g}}_{0}
, with Weyl group
W(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{0})
. For every positive root
\alpha
, we choose a standard
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-triplet
\{H_{\alpha},X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha}\}
such that there exist real numbers
N_{\alpha,\beta}
with

\displaystyle[X_{\alpha},X_{\beta}]

\displaystyle=N_{\alpha,\beta}X_{\alpha+\beta}

\displaystyle\text{and }\hskip 5.69054ptN_{-\alpha,-\beta}

\displaystyle=N_{\alpha,\beta}\;\;\forall\beta\in\Delta.

Denote by
\overline{\cdot}
the conjugation of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
with respect to the real form of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
defined by
\sum\limits_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}\mathbb{R}\{H_{\alpha},X_{\alpha},X_{-\alpha}\}
. Define also the antiautomorphism

(\cdot)^{t}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}_{0}

which fixes the elements of
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
while, for every
\beta\in\Delta
, it maps the root vector
X_{\beta}
to
X_{-\beta}
. Consider the following (real) Lie subalgebras:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\mid X+\bar{X}^{t}=0\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\Delta}i\mathbb{R}\{H_{\beta}\}\oplus\mathbb{R}\{X_{\beta}-X_{-\beta}\}\oplus i\mathbb{R}\{X_{\beta}+X_{-\beta}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\{X\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\mid X=\bar{X}^{t}\}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\Delta}\mathbb{R}\{H_{\beta}\}\oplus i\mathbb{R}\{X_{\alpha}-X_{-\beta}\}\oplus\mathbb{R}\{X_{\beta}+X_{-\beta}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\Delta}i\mathbb{R}\{H_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{0}:

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{p}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{0}=\sum\limits_{\beta\in\Delta}\mathbb{R}\{H_{\beta}\}

Then, as real Lie algebras,
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{h}_{0}
can be decomposed into

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{p}_{0}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{a}_{0}.

Let us now consider the complexification
\mathfrak{g}:=(\mathfrak{g}_{0})_{\mathbb{C}}
of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. Let
i
and
j
be the imaginary units coming from the complex structure of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and the complexification of
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, respectively. As a complex Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{g}
is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}_{0}
, the isomorphism being given by:

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus j\mathfrak{g}_{0}\ni X_{1}+jX_{2}\mapsto(X_{1}+iX_{2},\bar{X}_{1}+i\bar{X}_{2})\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}\times\mathfrak{g}_{0}.

Using the above isomorphism, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{k}_{0})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\{(X,-X^{t})\mid X\in\mathfrak{k}_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{k}_{0}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{h}_{0})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\{(H,\bar{H})\mid H\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\}\oplus i\{(H,\bar{H})\mid H\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{t}_{0})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\{(H,-H)\mid H\in\mathfrak{t}_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{t}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}

\displaystyle:=(\mathfrak{a})_{\mathbb{C}}\cong\{(H,H)\mid H\in\mathfrak{a}_{0}\oplus i\mathfrak{a}_{0}=\mathfrak{h}\}.

Therefore, the vector dual
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
of
\mathfrak{h}
can be described in two different ways. Either as the direct sum
\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{k}^{*}\oplus\mathfrak{a}^{*}
or as the direct product
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{*}\times\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{*}
. In other words, an element
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
can be described as a pair
(\mu,\nu)
where
\mu\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
,
\nu\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and
\lambda=\mu\oplus\nu
, or as a pair
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
where
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{*}
and

\lambda(H_{1}+jH_{2})=\lambda_{1}(H_{1}+iH_{2})+\lambda_{2}(\bar{H}_{1}+i\bar{H}_{2})
for every
H_{1},H_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}
. In particular, one has:

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu+\nu}{2}

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{-\mu+\nu}{2}

Now fix
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{*}\times\mathfrak{h}_{0}^{*}
, with the corresponding
\mu
and
\nu
as above, and assume that
\mu=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
is integral. Consider the character
\mathbb{C}_{\mu}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\nu}
of the Cartan subgroup
H:=TA
of
G
, where
T
and
A
are the analytic subgroups of
G
with Lie algebras
\mathfrak{t}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
respectively. Then form the induced representation

\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\mathbb{C}_{\mu}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\nu}\otimes 1)

where
B
is the Borel subgroup of
G
containing
H
, associated with the positive system
\Delta^{+}
, and consider its space of
K
-finite vectors

X(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}):=\mathrm{Ind}_{B}^{G}(\mathbb{C}_{\mu}\otimes\mathbb{C}_{\nu}\otimes 1)_{K\mathrm{-finite}}.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
Zhe74
]
)
.

The
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
contains the
K
-type of extremal weight
\mu=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
with multiplicity

.

Let
L(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
be the unique irreducible subquotient of
X(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
containing the

-type of extremal weight
\mu
. The classification of the irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-modules is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
Zhe74
]
)
.

Let
X(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
and

be as above.

1.

Every irreducible admissible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module is of the form

3.
\Theta
-lifting for classical complex Lie groups

In this section, we discuss the
\Theta
-correspondence, still in the case when
G
is complex Lie groups viewed as a real group. Let
(W,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be a finite-dimensional complex symplectic vector space and consider the symplectic group
Sp(W)
of
W
. Let
G_{1},G_{2}
be two closed subgroups of
Sp(W)
. We say that the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
forms a reductive dual pair if

(i)

the centralizer of
G_{1}
(resp.
G_{2}
) in
G
is
G_{2}
(resp.
G_{1}
), i.e.
Z_{G}(G_{1})=G_{2}
(resp
Z_{G}(G_{2})=G_{1}
),

(ii)

G_{1}
and
G_{2}
act reductively on
W
, i.e.
W
decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible
G_{1}G_{2}
-representations.

If
W
does not contain proper nontrivial
G_{1}G_{2}
-invariant subspaces, we say that the pair
(G_{1},G_{2})
is irreducible. It turns out that for the complex case there are two types of irreducible reductive dual pairs, pairs of type I and pairs of type II
[
How79
]
:

Type I:

G_{1}:=O_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and
G_{2}:=Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C})
,

Type II:

G_{1}:=GL_{m}(\mathbb{C})
and
G_{2}:=GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
.

Consider, now, the Heisenberg group
H:=H(W)
of the symplectic space
W
, i.e. the group
H(W):=W\times\mathbb{C}
equipped with the group law:

(w_{1},t_{1})\cdot(w_{2},t_{2}):=(w_{1}+w_{2},t_{1}+t_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\langle w_{1},w_{2}\rangle).

The group
Sp(W)
acts on
H
by the action:

g\cdot(w,t):=(gw,t),\text{ for }g\in Sp(W),(w,t)\in H

and fixes the elements of the center

Z:=\{(0,t)|t\in\mathbb{C}\}\cong\mathbb{C}

of
H
. As the following theorem indicates, the center
Z
is of great importance for the representation theory of
H
.

Theorem 3.1

(Stone-von Neumann Theorem
[
Mac49
,
vNe31
,
Sto30
]
)
.

Let
\psi
be a central character of
H
. Up to isomorphism, there is a unique irreducible unitary representation of
H
with central character
\psi

Fix a nontrivial central character
\psi
of
H
and
(\rho,\mathcal{H})
a unitary irreducible representation of
H
with central character
\psi
. Any other central character is of the form
\psi_{t}(x):=\psi(tx)
,
x\in\mathbb{C}
, for some
t\in\mathbb{C}
. Let
\rho_{t}
be the corresponding irreducible representation given by Theorem
3.1
. Let
g\in Sp(W)
and consider the representation
\rho_{t}(g\cdot)
of
H
. Since the group
Sp(W)
acts on
H
and leaves the center
Z
invariant,
\rho_{t}(g\cdot)
is equivalent with
\rho_{t}(\cdot)
. In other words, there exists an intertwining operator
\omega(g)
between
\rho_{t}(g\cdot)
and
\rho_{t}(\cdot)
. The map
\omega
defines a representation of
Sp(W)
on
\mathcal{H}
known as the metaplectic representation of
Sp(W)
. Let
\mathrm{Irr}(\cdot)
denotes for the irreducible representations of the corresponding group and set

\displaystyle\Omega_{1}

\displaystyle:=\{\pi_{1}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{1})|\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{1}}(\omega,\pi_{1})\neq 0\}

\displaystyle\Omega_{2}

\displaystyle:=\{\pi_{2}\in\mathrm{Irr}(G_{2})|\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{2}}(\omega,\pi_{2})\neq 0\}.

Then, the conditon

\{(\pi_{1},\pi_{2})\in\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}|\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{1}G_{2}}(\omega,\pi_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\pi_{2})\neq 0\}

\pi_{1}\in\Omega_{1}\longleftrightarrow\pi_{2}\in\Omega_{2}

known as the

Moreover, one has

\dim\mathrm{Hom}_{G_{1}G_{2}}(\omega,\pi_{1}\widehat{\otimes}\pi_{2})\leq 1

for any

]
. We say that
\pi_{1}
and

\displaystyle=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k},\overbrace{c_{k+1},\ldots,c_{m-l}}^{m-l-k},\overbrace{n-m-1,\ldots,-n+m+1}^{n-m},c_{m-l+1},\ldots,c_{m})

4.
Dirac cohomology for complex Lie groups

Let
G
be a real noncompact semisimple connected Lie group with complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,
\mathfrak{t}
a Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the Killing form of
\mathfrak{g}
,
K
a maximal compact subgroup of
G
of complexified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{k}
associated with a Cartan involution
\theta
of
G
, and

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}

the Cartan decomposition with respect to
\theta
. Let
\mathbf{C}(\mathfrak{p})
be the Clifford algebra of
\mathfrak{p}
, i.e. the quotient of the tensor algebra
T(\mathfrak{p})
of
\mathfrak{p}
by its ideal generated by the elements
X\otimes Y+Y\otimes X-\langle X,Y\rangle
,
X,Y\in\mathfrak{p}
, and
S
a spinor space of
\mathbf{C}(\mathfrak{p})
, i.e. a simple
\mathbf{C}(\mathfrak{p})
-module. The space
S
, equipped with the
\mathfrak{k}
-action defined via a composition map

(1)

\mathfrak{k}\overset{\mathrm{ad}}{\longrightarrow}\mathfrak{so}(\mathfrak{p})\overset{\varphi}{\longrightarrow}\mathbf{C}(\mathfrak{p})\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}(S),

becomes a
\mathfrak{k}
-module, known as the spin module of
\mathfrak{k}
.

In the late 90’s, Vogan introduced an algebraic Dirac operator as follows
[
Vog97
]
. For every
\mathfrak{g}
-module
(\pi,X)
, he defined the algebraic Dirac operator associated with
X
to be the linear operator

(2)

D_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X):X\otimes S\rightarrow X\otimes S

given by

D_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)=\sum_{i}\pi(Z_{i})\otimes\gamma(Z_{i})

where
\{Z_{i}\}
is an orthonormal basis for
{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then,
D_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}}(X)
is
\mathfrak{k}
-equivariant and
\ker D_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}}(X)
is a
\mathfrak{k}
-representation. Vogan defined the Dirac cohomology of
X
to be the
\widetilde{K}
-module

(3)

H_{D}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)=\frac{\ker D_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)}{\text{Im}D_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)\cap\ker D_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)},

where
\widetilde{K}
denotes the spin double cover of
K
. It turns out that the algebraic Dirac operator
D_{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{k}}(X)
and the Dirac cohomology
H_{D}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)
are powerful tools to study representations of
\mathfrak{g}
. For instance, assuming that
X
is irreducible, if
H_{D}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{k}}}(X)
contains a
\widetilde{K}
-module with highest weight
\beta
then
X
has infinitesimal character
\beta+\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
. In other words, the infinitesimal character of a module can be recovered from its Dirac cohomology
[
HP02
]
.

Dirac cohomology has been studied for various families of groups and modules. We are interested in the case where the group
G
is a complex Lie group viewed as real. The set of irreducible unitary representations of
G
with nonzero Dirac cohomology is called the Dirac series of
G
and will be denoted by
\widehat{G}^{d}
.
In
[
BP11
]
, Barbasch and Pandžić provided a sufficient condition for a unitary representation

\pi:=L(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})
of
G
to be in the Dirac series.
Namely, they proved the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

(
[
BP11
]
)
.

Let
P=MN
be a parabolic subgroup of
G
and let
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\mathfrak{m}}^{+}\cup\Delta(\mathfrak{n})
be the corresponding system of positive roots. Let
\pi_{\mathfrak{m}}
be an irreducible unitary representation of
M
with nonzero Dirac cohomology, and let
\xi
be a unitary character of
M
which is dominant with respect to
\Delta^{+}
. Suppose that twice the infinitesimal character of
\pi=\mathrm{Ind}^{G}_{P}[(\pi_{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes\xi)\otimes 1]
is regular and integral. Then
\pi
has nonzero Dirac cohomology.

Moreover, they conjectured a sufficient condition for a representation of
G
to have nonzero Dirac cohomology. The conjecture was proved by Barbasch, Dong and Wong:

Theorem 4.2

(
[
BDW22
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected complex simple Lie group and
\pi\in\widehat{G}
with regular and half-integral infinitesimal character. Then
\pi\in\widehat{G}^{d}
if and only if
\pi
is parabolically induced from a unipotent representation with nonzero Dirac cohomology, tensored with a unitary character of
M
.

Then notion of unipotent representation was introduced by Barbasch and Vogan in the 1980’s
[
BV85
]
and plays an important role in Representation Theory of reductive Lie groups. The general theory of unipotent representations requires tools beyond the scope of this work. However, in this paper, we only need unipotent representations with nonzero Dirac cohomology for classical complex Lie groups. It turns out that these representations are fairly simple and are explicitly described in
[
BP11
]
. For the sake of completeness, we give a brief review of these representations. For more details, the reader is refered to
[
BP11
]
.

4.1.
Type A

The regular half-integral unipotent representations of
GL_{m}(\mathbb{C})
with nonzero Dirac cohomology are all spherical, i.e. of the form
L(0,\nu)
with

2\nu=(a-1,a-3,\ldots,b,b-1,\ldots,-b+1,-b,\ldots,-a+3,-a+1),

where
\{a,b\}
is a partition of
m
with
a\equiv b+1\mod 2
(hence
m
should be odd).

4.2.
Type B

The regular half-integral unipotent representations of
B_{n}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology are all spherical with infinitesimal character of the form

2\lambda=2\lambda^{\prime}=(-2b+1,-2b+3,\ldots,-1;-2a,-2a+2,\ldots,-2),

where
0<a\leq b
are integers with
a+b=n
.

4.3.
Type C

There are
2
half-integral unipotent representations of
C_{n}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology; the oscillator representations
\pi_{\text{even}}
and
\pi_{\text{odd}}
. Their infinitesimal characters are

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(2n-1,2n-3,\ldots,1)

in the case of
\pi_{\text{even}}
, and

\displaystyle\lambda=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2n-1,2n-3,\ldots,1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2n-1,2n-3,\ldots,-1)

in the case of
\pi_{\text{odd}}
. Their corresponding parameters are

\displaystyle\mu=

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\nu=

\displaystyle 2\lambda

and

\displaystyle\mu=

\displaystyle(0,\ldots,0,1)

\displaystyle\nu=

\displaystyle 2\lambda,

respectively.

4.4.
Type D

The regular half-integral representations of
D_{n}
are of
2
types. For
0<a\leq b
integers with
n=a+b
, there are
2
representations, one spherical
\pi_{\text{even}}
with

\lambda=\lambda^{\prime}=\frac{1}{2}(2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,1;2b-2,2b-4,\ldots,0)

and another one nonspherical
\pi_{\text{odd}}
with

\displaystyle\lambda=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,1;2b-2,2b-4,\ldots,0)

\displaystyle\lambda^{\prime}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,-1;2b-2,2b-4,\ldots,0)

Their parameters are

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=2\lambda

and

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle=(0,\ldots,0,1)

\displaystyle\nu

\displaystyle=2\lambda,

respectively.

5.
Dirac cohomology and
\Theta
-lifting for dual pairs of type II

In this section, the main result is the following theorem. We keep the previous notation.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
(G_{1},G_{2}):=(GL_{m}(\mathbb{C}),GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
,
m\leq n
, be a complex reductive dual pair of type II. Let
\pi_{1}
be an irreducible unitary representation in the Dirac series of
G_{1}
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}
has parameters
\mu_{1}=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
and
\nu_{1}=\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}
. Then the
\Theta
-lifting
\pi_{2}:=\Theta(\pi_{1})
belongs to the Dirac series of
G_{2}
, with

H_{D}(\pi_{2})=2^{[\frac{n}{2}]}V(2\lambda_{2}-\rho_{2}),

where
\rho_{2}
is the half-sum of positive roots of
G_{2}
, and

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(\mu_{2}+\nu_{2})

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\cdot(\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{n-m})

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=\nu_{1}\cdot(\overbrace{n-m-1,\ldots,-n+m+1}^{n-m}).

(As before the symbol
\cdot
stands for concatenation.)

The rest of this section is devoted to the proof of this theorem.
Let
(G_{1},G_{2}):=(GL_{m}(\mathbb{C}),GL_{n}(\mathbb{C}))
and
\pi_{1}\in\widehat{G}_{1}^{d}
. Then, according to Theorem
4.2
, there is a parabolic subgroup
P_{1}=M_{1}N_{1}
of
G_{1}
, a unipotent representation
\pi_{M_{1}}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology, and a unitary character
\xi_{1}
of
M_{1}
such that

\pi_{1}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{1}}^{G_{1}}((\pi_{M_{1}}\otimes\xi_{1})\otimes 1).

The Levi factor
M_{1}
of
P_{1}
consists of a direct product

M_{1}=M_{1,1}\times\ldots\times M_{1,k},

where
M_{1,s}:=GL_{m_{1,s}}(\mathbb{C})
,
s=1,\ldots,k
.
Then
\pi_{M_{1}}
is the tensor product

\pi_{M_{1}}=\pi_{M_{1,1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\pi_{M_{1,k}},

where
\pi_{M_{1,s}}
is a unipotent representation of
M_{1,s}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology. According to Subsection
4.1
, they are all spherical, i.e. of the form
\pi_{M_{1,s}}=L_{M_{1,s}}(0,\nu_{1,s})
with

\displaystyle 2\nu_{1,s}=(

\displaystyle a_{1,s}-1,a_{1,s}-3,\ldots,b_{1,s},b_{1,s}-1,\ldots,-b_{1,s}+1,-b_{1,s},

\displaystyle\ldots,-a_{1,s}+3,-a_{1,s}+1),

where
\{a_{1,s},b_{1,s}\}
is a partition of
m_{1,s}
with
a_{1,s}\equiv b_{1,s}+1\mod 2
(hence
m_{1,s}
should be odd).
On the other hand,
\xi_{1}
consists of a direct product

\xi_{1}=\xi_{1,1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\xi_{1,k}

where
\xi_{1,s}
is a unitary character of
M_{1,s}
. The unitary characters of
M_{1,s}=GL_{m_{1,s}}(\mathbb{C})
are central and of the form

\displaystyle\xi_{1,s}:Z(GL_{m_{1,s}}(\mathbb{C}))\cong(\mathbb{C},\times)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow(\mathbb{S}_{1},\times)

\displaystyle z=e^{x+iy}

\displaystyle\mapsto e^{ik_{1,s}y},

where
k_{1,s}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
In other words, the corresponding linear form
\xi_{1,s}
is trivial on
\mathfrak{a}
while on
\mathfrak{t}
is given by

\xi_{1,s}=\frac{k_{1,s}}{m_{1,s}}(1,\ldots,1).

Therefore,
\pi_{1}=L(\mu_{1},\nu_{1})
with
\mu_{1}=(\xi_{1,1}|\ldots|\xi_{1,k})
and
\nu_{1}=(\nu_{1,1}|\ldots|\nu_{1,k})
. If necessary, act by an element
w_{1}
of
W(\mathfrak{gl}(m,\mathbb{C}))
and write
\mu_{1}
and
\nu_{1}
in the form

\mu_{1}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{m-k-l},b_{1},\ldots,b_{l}),

with
a_{1}\geq\ldots\geq a_{k}>0>b_{1}\geq\ldots\geq b_{l}
,
and
\nu_{1}=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
, as in Theorem
3.4
. Then
\pi_{2}
, the
\Theta
-lifting of
\pi_{1}
, is
\pi_{2}=L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})^{*}
where

\displaystyle\mu_{2}

\displaystyle=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{k},\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{m-l-k},\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{n-m},b_{1},\ldots,b_{l})

\displaystyle\nu_{2}

\displaystyle=(c_{1},\ldots,c_{k},\overbrace{c_{k+1},\ldots,c_{m-l}}^{m-l-k},\overbrace{n-m-1,\ldots,-n+m+1}^{n-m},c_{m-l+1},\ldots,c_{m}).

Acting again with an element
w_{2}
of
\Delta(\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C}))
, we can write

\mu_{2}=(\xi_{1,1}|\ldots|\xi_{1,k}|\overbrace{0,\ldots,0}^{n-m})

and

(4)

\nu_{2}=(\nu_{1,1}|\ldots|\nu_{1,k}|\overbrace{n-m-1,\ldots,-n+m+1}^{n-m}).

Set
\mu^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
to be the weights of
\mathfrak{gl}(n-m,\mathbb{C})
consisting of the last
(n-m)
-coordinates of
\mu_{2}
and
\nu_{2}
respectively. In other words,

\displaystyle\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\overbrace{(0,\ldots,0)}^{n-m}

\displaystyle\nu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=(n-m-1,\ldots,-n+m+1)=2\rho^{\prime},

where
\rho^{\prime}
stands for half the sum of the positive roots of
\mathfrak{gl}(n-m,\mathbb{C})
viewed as the lower diagonal block of
\mathfrak{gl}(n,\mathbb{C})
. The parameters
\mu^{\prime}
and
\nu^{\prime}
correspond to the trivial representation of
GL_{n-m}(\mathbb{C})
. Indeed, the only
K
-type of the trivial representation is the trivial one, corresponding to
\mu^{\prime}=0
, and the infinitesimal character of the trivial representation is exactly
\rho^{\prime}
.
Let
M_{2}:=M_{1}\times M^{\prime}
and
P_{2}:=M_{2}\times N_{2}=(M_{1}\times M^{\prime})\times N_{2}
. Then
L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})
is the unique irreducible subquotient of the representation

\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{2}}^{G_{2}}((\pi_{M_{1}}\otimes 1)\otimes 1)

containing the
K
-type with extremal weight
\mu_{2}
. Nevertheless, according to
[
Tad09
,
Bar96
]
, this representation is irreducible so that

L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{2}}^{G_{2}}((\pi_{M_{1}}\otimes 1)\otimes 1).

Since
\pi_{M_{1}}
has nonzero Dirac cohomology and

is parabolically induced by

[

]
,
L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})
has nonzero Dirac cohomology. Since
L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})
is unitary, by
[

, 10]
, we deduce that
\pi_{2}=L(\mu_{2},\nu_{2})^{*}
has nonzero Dirac cohomology.

From
[
BDW22
, Theorem 1.6, Conjecture 2.5]
, the Dirac cohomology

of
\pi_{1}
is

H_{D}(\pi_{1})=2^{[\frac{l_{1}}{2}]}V(2\lambda_{1}-\rho_{1}).

On the other hand, since
H_{D}(\pi_{2})\neq 0
, from
[
BDW22
, Theorem 1.6, Conjecture 2.5]
again, we deduce that

H_{D}(\pi_{2})=2^{[\frac{l_{2}}{2}]}V(2\lambda_{2}-\rho_{2}).

6.
Dirac cohomology and
\Theta
-lifting for dual pairs of type I

The main result of this section is the following theorem.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(G_{1},G_{2})
be a complex reductive dual pair of type I. Let
\pi_{1}
be an irreducible unitary representation in the Dirac series of
G_{1}
. Suppose
\pi_{1}
has parameters
\mu_{1}
and
\nu_{1}
. Then the
\Theta
-lifting
\pi_{2}:=\Theta(\pi_{1})
of
\pi_{1}
has a trivial Dirac cohomology, except when
(G_{1},G_{2})=(O_{2m}(\mathbb{C}),Sp_{2n}(\mathbb{C}))
and the parameter
\nu_{1}
is of the form

\nu_{1}=(\nu_{1,1}\mid\ldots\mid\nu_{1,k}\mid 2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,1;2b-2,2b-4,\ldots,0),

where each
\nu_{1,s}
corresponds to a unipotent representation of some
GL
-factor and
b=n-m+1
. In this case, the parameter
\nu_{2}
of
\pi_{2}=L(\nu_{2},\mu_{2})
is of the form

\displaystyle\nu_{2}=(

\displaystyle\nu_{1,1}\mid\ldots\mid\nu_{1,k}\mid 2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,1;

\displaystyle 2n-2m,2n-2m-2,\ldots,-2b+2)

In the rest of this section, we prove Theorem
6.1
.
Let
(G_{1},G_{2})
be a complex reductive dual pair of type I. Define

\Theta(\widehat{G}_{1}^{d}):=\{\Theta(\pi_{1})|\pi_{1}\in\widehat{G}_{1}^{d}\}

Suppose that
\pi_{1}\in\widehat{G}_{1}^{d}
. Then, according to Theorem
4.2
, there is a parabolic subgroup
P_{1}=M_{1}N_{1}
of
G_{1}
and a unipotent representation
\pi_{M_{1}}
of
M_{1}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology such that

\pi_{1}=\mathrm{Ind}_{P_{1}}^{G_{1}}(\pi_{M_{1}}\otimes 1).

The Levi factor
M_{1}
of
P_{1}
consists of a direct product

M_{1}=M_{1,1}\times\ldots\times M_{1,k}\times G(t),

where
M_{1,s}:=GL_{m_{1,s}}(\mathbb{C})
,
s=1,\ldots,k
, and
G(t)
is a group of the same type as
G_{1}
of rank

.
Then
\pi_{M_{1}}
is a tensor product

\pi_{M_{1}}=\pi_{M_{1,1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\pi_{M_{1,k}}\otimes\pi_{G(t)},

where
\pi_{M_{1,s}}
is a unipotent representation of
M_{1,s}
with nonzero Dirac cohomology, and
\pi_{G(t)}
is a unipotent representation of
G(t)
with nonzero Dirac cohomology. For the sake of simplicity, set

and
\pi^{1}_{1}:=\pi_{M_{1,1}}\otimes\ldots\otimes\pi_{M_{1,k}}

and
\pi_{M_{1}}=\pi^{1}_{1}\otimes\pi_{G(t)}

In what follows, we assume that
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{1}^{\prime}
(or
\mu_{1}
and
\nu_{1}
) correspond to
\pi_{1}
. Then using Theorems
3.2
and
3.4
, we find the corresponding parameters
(\lambda_{2},\lambda_{2}^{\prime})
or
\mu_{2},\nu_{2}
of
\pi_{2}
and we comment on the necessary conditions for the Dirac cohomology of

We remark that there is an infinite number of regular half-integral unipotent representations for

or

. As a consequence, the non-triviality of Dirac cohomology is not in general preserved for the reductive dual pairs of Type I. In the rest of this section we will give a more detailed analysis of this fact.

6.1.
\mathbf{G_{1}:=B_{m},G_{2}:=C_{n},m\leq n}

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{1}=2\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=(

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{1,1};\ldots;2\lambda_{1,k}\mid-2b+1,-2b+3,\ldots,-1;

\displaystyle-2a,-2a+2,\ldots,-2),\quad 0<a\leq b.

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{2}=2\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=(

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{1,1};\ldots;2\lambda_{1,k}\mid-2b+1,-2b+3,\ldots,-1;

\displaystyle-2a,-2a+2,\ldots,-2;2n-2m-1,2n-2m-3,\ldots,1)

The even coordinates

correspond to a part of the Levi factor

. Nevertheless, they do not correspond to a
GL
-factor. More precisely, they do not correspond to a unipotent representation while they can not interact with the coordinates
\lambda_{1,1};\ldots;\lambda_{1,s}
to give a unipotent representation of some
GL
-factor. On the other hand, no unipotent representation of

involves even coordinates.

Therefore,
\Theta(\widehat{G}_{1}^{d})\cap\widehat{G}_{2}^{d}=\emptyset
.

6.2.
\mathbf{G_{1}:=C_{m},G_{2}:=B_{n},m\leq n}
,
\mathbf{\pi}_{\text{even}}

2\lambda_{1}=2\lambda_{1}^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1,1};\ldots;\lambda_{1,k}\mid 2m-1,2m-3,\ldots,1).

\displaystyle 2\lambda_{2}=2\lambda_{2}^{\prime}=(

\displaystyle\lambda_{1,1};\ldots;\lambda_{1,k}

\displaystyle\mid 2m-1,2m-3,\ldots,1;2n-2m-1,2n-2m-3,\ldots,1)

.

6.3.
\mathbf{G_{1}:=C_{m},G_{2}:=B_{n},m\leq n}
,
\mathbf{\pi}_{\text{odd}}

By using a similar argument as in the previous case, we can deduce that

.

6.4.
\mathbf{G_{1}:=D_{m},G_{2}:=C_{n}}
,
\mathbf{\pi}_{\text{even}}

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle=(\nu_{1,1}\mid\ldots\mid\nu_{1,k}\mid 2a-1,2a-3,\ldots,3,1;2b-2,2b-4,\ldots,0),

6.5.
\mathbf{G_{1}:=D_{m},G_{2}:=C_{n}}

\mathbf{\pi}_{\text{odd}}

By using a similar argument as in the previous case, we can deduce that in the vast majority of cases, the Dirac cohomology is not preserved. The only exception is when

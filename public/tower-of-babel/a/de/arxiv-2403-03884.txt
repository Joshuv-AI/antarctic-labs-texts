Towards a Schauder theory for fractional viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations
Espen R. Jakobsen, Artur Rutkowski

Abstract
. We survey some results on Lipschitz and Schauder regularity estimates for viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations with subcritical Lévy diffusions. The Schauder estimates, along with existence of smooth solutions, are obtained with the help of a Duhamel formula and L 1 L^{1} bounds on the spatial derivatives of the heat kernel. Our results cover very general nonlocal and mixed local-nonlocal diffusions, including strongly anisotropic, nonsymmetric, mixed order, and spectrally one-sided models.

1.
Introduction

The goal of this note is an elementary exposition of some existing results on classical well-posedness and Schauder regularity theory for viscous Hamilton–Jacobi (HJ) equations. We are interested in the following problem:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{L}u+H(t,x,u,Du)=f(t,x)\quad&{\rm in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0)=u_{0}\quad&{\rm in}\ \mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

Here,
\mathcal{L}
is a translation invariant diffusion operator of the form:

(1.2)

\mathcal{L}u=B\cdot Du+\Div(ADu)+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(\cdot+z)-u(\cdot)-Du(\cdot)\cdot z\textbf{1}_{B(0,1)}(z))\,\nu(dz),

with Lévy triplet
(B,A,\nu)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\times\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
where

A\geq 0

and the Lévy measure
\nu
is a nonnegative Borel measure satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(1\wedge|z|^{2})\nu(dz)<\infty
. The function
H\in C((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is called the Hamiltonian. Throughout the paper we assume that the operator
\mathcal{L}
is subcritical, i.e. a negative operator of order
\alpha\in(1,2]
. The order is
2
when
A>0
, otherwise it depends on the behavior of
\nu
near
z=0
.
The order condition is implicitly formulated through the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
, see Section
2
below. Because the linear diffusion term is the principal term, we call (
1.1
) a
viscous
HJ equation.

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
u
is a classical solution to (
1.1
) if
u\in C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\partial_{t}u,Du,\mathcal{L}u\in C((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and the equation and the initial condition are satisfied pointwise.

For linear (possibly nonlocal) PDEs in non-divergence form, Schauder regularity theory loosely speaking states that if data and coefficients are
\beta
-Hölder continuous, then solutions are
\alpha+\beta
Hölder continuous where
\alpha
is the order of the equation
[
24
,
36
,
37
,
39
]
. In this paper we will give two results of this type for the nonlinear equation (
1.1
). As opposed to many other papers on regularity theory, our method of proof also produces well-posedness of solutions with this regularity.
In Section
4
we give uniform in time Schauder estimates for smooth initial conditions and Lipschitz
f
, and in Section
5
we have Schauder estimates for initial data and
f
with low Hölder regularity where gradients will blow-up for small times. For the first type of results, to handle locally Lipschitz Hamiltonians in the long time existence proof, we need uniform Lipschitz estimates on the solutions. Such estimates along with a comparison principle are proved in Section
3
.

The viscous HJ equations arise in the optimal stochastic control problems or differential games. The unknown function
u
then represents the (upper or lower) value function for the optimally controlled process. Equation (
1.1
) corresponds to the case of controlled drift but not diffusion. When
H
is convex in
Du
, it corresponds to the control problem

\displaystyle u(T-t,x)=\inf_{\alpha_{s}\in\mathcal{A}}E\Big(\int_{t}^{T}\big[L(X^{t,x}_{s},\alpha_{s},s)+f(s,X^{t,x}_{s})\big]\,ds+u_{0}(X_{T}^{t,x})\Big),

where
L
and
f
are running costs,
u_{0}
a terminal cost,
\mathcal{A}
is the set of admissible controls, and
X_{s}=X_{s}^{t,x}
is the solution of an SDE driven by a Lévy process
N_{s}
with generator
\mathcal{L}
,

dX_{s}=b(\alpha_{s},X_{s})\,ds+dN_{s},\quad s>t;\qquad X_{t}=x.

We refer e.g. to Fleming and Soner
[
23
, Chapter IV.3]
for more details in the case of Gaussian noise. For non-Gaussian (Lévy) noise see Øksendal and Sulem
[
40
]
, and for differential games see Carmona
[
11
]
.

Early contributions to viscous HJ equations were given by Kružkov
[
35
]
and Crandall and Lions
[
18
]
.
Nowadays they have an extensive literature and are still an active area of research. With hundreds of papers, we sample a few to give an idea of some of activity which encompasses well-posedness, regularity, boundary value problems, long time/ergodic/asymptotic problems, singular solutions, approximation and numerics, homogenization, control and large deviations, and stochastic perturbations
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
13
,
17
,
19
,
28
,
38
,
41
]
.
The list is very far from being complete or even representative. Our own main motivation comes from the theory of mean field games with individual noises. There the Nash equilibria are characterized by a forward-backward system consisting of a viscous HJ equation and a Fokker–Planck equation, see
[
1
,
10
,
12
]
for the background and
[
15
,
22
]
for mean field games with nonlocal diffusions.

In equation
(
1.1
) the diffusion term dominates and induces a regularizing effect, which allows to solve the equation with the help of the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
and a Duhamel formula, yielding the so-called mild solutions. In the nonlocal case, this approach to the viscous HJ equation was used by Imbert
[
29
]
and many other authors
[
3
,
21
,
22
,
30
,
32
,
33
]
, whereas the local case goes back at least to Ben-Artzi
[
7
]
. We note that for regular data the Duhamel formula for the viscous HJ equation works somewhat similar to the linear case, at least for the short-time results. In this connection we mention the work of Mikulevičius and Pragarauskas
[
39
]
who study regularization properties for the heat equation with quite general Lévy-type diffusions. More recently, Schauder estimates (without existence of classical solutions) were developed by Dong, Jin and Zhang
[
20
]
for fully nonlinear equations involving nonlocal operators with the Lévy measure comparable to the one of the fractional Laplacian. The results given below cover a large class of translation invariant diffusions which were not present in
[
20
]
, including strongly anisotropic and nonsymmetric stable operators.

The long-time existence under
local
regularity assumptions for
H
is more delicate, as it requires global Lipschitz bounds for the solutions, which do not follow from the Duhamel formula. In general, these Lipschitz bounds can be obtained using the comparison principle and viscosity solution techniques, see Imbert
[
29
]
and Jakobsen and Karlsen
[
31
]
. If the solution is known to be more regular pointwise in time, one can also apply the Bernstein method, which is quite straightforward when one assumes that
f(t)\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
uniformly in
t
. A more sophisticated Bernstein-type argument can be applied in order to get bounds for
u
in terms of weaker norms of
f
, see e.g. Goffi
[
26
]
, but this is beyond the scope of this note.

In case that
u_{0},f(t)\in C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
t\in(0,T)
with
\beta\in(0,1)
we can still obtain classical solutions, but
Du(t)
and
\mathcal{L}u(t)
can, in general, blow up for small
t
. There are many works concerning the viscous HJ equations with irregular initial data, see e.g., Gilding, Guedda, and Kersner
[
25
]
, Benachour and Laurençot
[
8
]
for the local diffusions and
[
22
,
29
,
32
,
43
]
for the nonlocal case. However, we are not aware of papers which explicitly study blow up rates for
u_{0}\in C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
data in the vein of Theorem
5.1
below, obtained by the authors together with Amund Bergset, cf. his master thesis
[
9
]
. Admittedly, we only allow Hamiltonians with global Lipschitz regularity there so far; an extension to locally Lipschitz Hamiltonians seems non-trivial and interesting.
In a future work we aim to study mean field game systems with Hölder payoff functions. The blowup for the viscous HJ equation means that the drift term in the corresponding Fokker–Planck equation may become large close to the terminal time, which poses a difficulty for compactness arguments.

2.
Assumptions

We adopt rather standard assumptions on
H
and
f
, which we will use in Sections
3
and
4
. We note that by assuming regularity for higher order derivatives in
(H1):
and
(F0):
below, one could get higher regularity of solutions to the viscous HJ equation. Here, for the sake of clarity, we work with quite weak assumptions, but strong enough to allow for the classical solution setting.

(H0):

H\colon[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous and for every
R
there exists
C_{R}
such that for every
t,|u|\leq R
,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
p,q\in B_{R}
,

\displaystyle|H(t,x,u,p)-H(t,y,u,q)|\leq C_{R}(|x-y|+|p-q|).

(H1):

H
is smooth and for all
l\in\mathbb{N}^{2d+1}
with
|l|\leq 2
,
\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|D^{l}_{x,u,p}H(\cdot,x,\cdot,\cdot)|
is locally bounded.

(H2):

For every
R>0
there exists
C_{R}>0
such that for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
t,|u|\leq R
, and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|H(t,x,u,p)-H(t,y,u,p)|\leq C_{R}(1+|p|)|x-y|.

(H3):

For every
T>0
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(T)\in\mathbb{R}
such that for all
t\in[0,T]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
u\leq v
, and
p\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

H(t,x,v,p)-H(t,x,u,p)\geq\gamma(v-u).

(F0):

f\in C_{b}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and for every
T>0
there exists
C
such that for
t\in[0,T]
and
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\displaystyle|f(t,x)-f(t,y)|\leq C|x-y|.

Note that
(H1):
is strictly stronger than
(H0):
.

In the Schauder estimates we use the fact that the order of the operator is greater than 1. We express that through appropriate estimates for the heat kernel. If we let
\psi
be the Fourier multiplier corresponding to
\mathcal{L}
(see e.g.
[
34
]
), then the heat kernel of
\mathcal{L}
is defined as

\displaystyle K_{0}=\delta_{0},\quad\mathcal{F}(K_{t})(\xi)=e^{-t\psi(\xi)},\quad t>0.

(K):

There is
\mathcal{K}>0
and
\alpha\in(1,2]
, such that the heat kernels
K
and
K^{*}
of
\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}^{*}
respectively are smooth densities of probability measures, and for
\tilde{K}=K,K^{*}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
we have

\|D^{\beta}\tilde{K}(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq\mathcal{K}t^{-\frac{\beta}{\alpha}}.

Under
(K):
we have
K_{t}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for every
t>0
and
K_{t}
satisfies
(\partial_{t}-\mathcal{L})K_{t}=0
pointwise on
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Here are some examples of operators satisfying
(K):
(taken mostly from
[
22
, Section 4]
):

(i)

There is
c>0
such
\langle Ax,x\rangle\geq c|x|^{2}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, e.g.
\mathcal{L}=\Delta
. Then
(K):
is satisfied with
\alpha=2
.

(ii)

The Lévy measure
\nu
has an absolutely continuous part with density
\nu_{ac}(z)\approx|z|^{-d-\alpha}
for all
|z|\leq 1
and
\alpha\in(1,2)
, see
[
22
, Theorem 4.3]
, based on the pointwise estimates in
[
27
]
.

(iii)

\mathcal{L}=-(-\partial^{2}_{x_{1}})^{\alpha_{1}/2}-(-\partial^{2}_{x_{2}})^{\alpha_{2}/2}-\ldots-(-\partial^{2}_{x_{d}})^{\alpha_{d}/2}
, where
\alpha_{i}\in(1,2]
. Then
\alpha=\min_{i}\alpha_{i}
.

(iv)

The family of Riesz–Feller operators on
\mathbb{R}
, including the spectrally one-sided model with
A=0
and
\nu(z)=|z|^{-1-\alpha}\textbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(z)
, see
[
2
, Lemma 2.1 (G7) and Proposition 2.3]
.

(v)

The generator of the CGMY model in finance, see
[
22
, Example 4.4]
.

(vi)

If
\mathcal{L}
satisfies
(K):
and
L
is any other Lévy operator, then
\mathcal{L}+L
satisfies
(K):
.

3.
Comparison principle and Lipschitz estimates

In the next sections we will construct short-time solutions via fixed point arguments and we will get rather strong regularity estimates, but only on short time intervals. For the sake of obtaining long-time solutions we will establish a global in time Lipschitz bound using different methods in Theorem
3.3
below.

We first give a comparison principle in order to obtain supremum bounds. A similar result was given by Imbert
[
29
, Theorem 2]
for purely nonlocal
\mathcal{L}
in the (more general) viscosity solution setting; it extends rather easily to general Lévy operators.

Theorem 3.1
.

Assume that
(H0):
,
(H2):
,
(H3):
, and
(F0):
hold. Let
u_{0}
be uniformly continuous and bounded and suppose that
u
and
v
are respectively classical sub- and supersolution to (
1.1
), i.e.,
u(0,x)\leq u_{0}(x)\leq v(0,x)
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathcal{L}u+H(t,x,u,Du)\leq f(t,x)\quad{\rm in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{L}v+H(t,x,v,Dv)\geq f(t,x)\quad{\rm in}\ (0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Then
u\leq v
on
[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Note that under present assumptions
f
can be absorbed into
H
, so we can assume that
f=0
. Since the diffusion operator
\mathcal{L}
is linear and translation invariant, it suffices to follow the proof of Imbert. First of all, since the Lévy measure
\nu
is finite outside neighborhoods of 0, there exists a function
\varphi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\displaystyle\|D\varphi\|_{\infty}+\|D^{2}\varphi\|_{\infty}\leq C,\quad\lim\limits_{|x|\to\infty}\varphi(x)=\infty,\quad\mathcal{L}\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Since the sub/supersolutions are classical, we do not need to introduce the sub- and superdifferentials after
[
29
, (14)]
, because the operator can be evaluated pointwise, and we have (in the language of
[
29
]
)
DU(\overline{t},\overline{x})-DV(\overline{s},\overline{y})=\alpha D\varphi(\overline{x})
. Then it is easy to obtain the following counterpart of inequality
[
29
, (16)]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}V(\overline{s},\overline{y})-\mathcal{L}U(\overline{t},\overline{x})\leq-\alpha\mathcal{L}\varphi(\overline{x}).

The rest of the proof does not depend on the form of the diffusion operator.
∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Assume that
(H0):
,
(H2):
,
(H3):
, and
(F0):
hold. If
u_{0}
is uniformly continuous and bounded and
u
is a classical solution to (
1.1
), then

(3.1)

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\infty}\leq e^{-\gamma t}\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}+(1-\tfrac{1}{\gamma}e^{-\gamma t})(\|f\|_{\infty}+\|H(\cdot,\cdot,0,0)\|_{\infty}),\quad t\in[0,T).

Proof.

Let
w(t)
denote the right hand side of (
3.1
). Then the proof follows by comparison since by
(H3):

w
and
-w
are super- and subsolutions of (
1.1
).
∎

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that
(H0):
,
(H2):
,
(H3):
, and
(F0):
hold,
u_{0}\in C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and let
u
be a classical solution to (
1.1
). Then

\displaystyle\|Du\|_{\infty}\leq C(T,H,f)(1+\|Du_{0}\|_{\infty}),\quad t\in[0,T).

Remark 3.4
.

We only give the proof in the case of regular data and solutions to showcase the elegant and more accessible Bernstein method. For less regular data one can apply the viscosity solution proof of Imbert
[
29
]
. In order to adapt the proof of Imbert to mixed local-nonlocal operators an appropriate viscosity solution formulation is needed, see e.g., Jakobsen and Karlsen
[
31
]
in this regard.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
for regular data and solutions.

If
(H1):
is satisfied,
f\in C([0,T],C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and
u\in C([0,T],C^{3}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, then we can differentiate the equation in
x_{i}
and get

(3.2)

\begin{split}\partial_{t}u_{x_{i}}-\mathcal{L}u_{x_{i}}+H_{x_{i}}(t,x,u,Du)&+u_{x_{i}}H_{u}(t,x,u,Du)\\
&+Du_{x_{i}}\cdot H_{p}(t,x,u,Du)=f_{x_{i}}.\end{split}

Now, let
w=\frac{1}{2}|Du|^{2}
. If we multiply (
3.2
) by
u_{x_{i}}
and sum over
i
we get

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-\mathcal{L}w+\sum\limits_{i}\Gamma(u_{x_{i}})

\displaystyle+Du\cdot H_{x}(t,x,u,Du)+2wH_{u}(t,x,u,Du)

\displaystyle+Dw\cdot H_{p}(t,x,u,Du)=Df\cdot Du,

where (recall that
A\geq 0
)

\displaystyle\Gamma(u)=\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}u^{2}-2u\mathcal{L}u)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(u(x+y)-u(x))^{2}\,\nu(dy)+Du\cdot ADu\geq 0.

Furthermore, we have
w\geq 0
and
H_{u}\geq\gamma
by
(H3):
. Therefore, by
(H2):
and Corollary
3.2
,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w-\mathcal{L}w+Dw\cdot H_{p}(t,x,u,Du)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,-Du\cdot H_{x}(t,x,u,Du)-2\gamma w+\tfrac{1}{2}\|Df\|_{\infty}^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}w

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,C(T,H,f)(1+w).

Let
C=C(T,H,f)
and note that
v=e^{Ct}(1+\|w(0)\|_{\infty})-1
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}v-\mathcal{L}v+Dv\cdot H_{p}(t,x,u,Du)=C(1+v),\quad v(0)=\|w(0)\|_{\infty}\geq w(0).

Therefore by the comparison principle for the linear parabolic problems we get that
w\leq v
, which ends the proof in this case.
∎

4.
Uniform Schauder estimates with regular initial data

In this section we give uniform in time Schauder estimates under the assumption that the initial data is regular. The result and proof were essentially given in
[
32
]
. We repeat the arguments here in order to demonstrate the technique where the regularity of the heat kernel is used to gain
\alpha-\varepsilon
derivatives over the forcing term
f
. Similar results were given earlier by, e.g., Imbert
[
29
]
and Ersland and Jakobsen
[
22
]
, but with only one derivative gain. It seems that in order to gain full
\alpha
regularity one needs a stronger assumption than
(K):
, see e.g.
[
20
]
or
[
14
]
.

In the proof we first show the existence of a short-time mild solution using the Duhamel formula and the Banach fixed point theorem. Then we show that the mild solution is classical, which enables us to use the results of the previous section to obtain the long time existence. Finally, we get the uniqueness from the comparison principle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\alpha-1)
and assume that
(H1):
,
(H2):
,
(H3):
,
(F0):
, and
(K):
are satisfied and let
u_{0}\in C^{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then problem (
1.1
) has a unique classical solution
u
satisfying

(4.1)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{t}u\|_{\infty}+\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\,c(T,\mathcal{K})\Big(c(H)+\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|u_{0}\|_{C^{1+\alpha-\varepsilon}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big).

Proof.

We denote
\sigma=\alpha-1-\varepsilon
, so that
1+\alpha-\varepsilon=2+\sigma
.

1) Short-time mild solutions by Banach’s fixed point theorem.

Let

\displaystyle R=R(u)=\sup_{t}\|u(t)\|_{C^{1}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\quad\!R_{1}=2(\|u_{0}\|_{C^{2+\sigma}_{b}}+\sup_{t}\|f(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})+1,

and define the space

\displaystyle X=\{u\in C_{b}((0,T],C^{2+\sigma-\varepsilon}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})):\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{2+\sigma}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq R_{1}\}.

Note that for all
u\in X
we have
R\leq R_{1}
.
For
u\in X
,
t\in[0,T)
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the fixed-point map:

\displaystyle S(u)(t,x):=K_{t}\ast u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}K_{t-s}\ast[H(s,\cdot,u,Du)+f(s)](x)\,ds.

We will now estimate the
C^{2+\sigma}_{b}
norms of
S(u)
in order to see that it belongs to
X
. The time continuity of the
C^{2+\sigma-\varepsilon}_{b}
norms follows from the
C^{2+\sigma}_{b}
estimates, see e.g.
[
32
, Lemma B.2]
.
First note that for
k=1,2
we have

(4.2)

\begin{split}&D_{x}^{k}S(u)(t,x)\\
=\,&K_{t}\ast D_{x}^{k}u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}D_{x}K_{t-s}\ast D_{x}^{k-1}[H(s,\cdot,u,Du)+f(s)](x)\,ds.\end{split}

By
(K):
and interpolation for Hölder functions (e.g.
[
32
, Remark 2.22 d)]
),

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|D_{x}K_{t-s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\,ds\leq\mathcal{K}\int_{0}^{T}(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\,ds=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}\mathcal{K}T^{\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha}}:=c_{T,\alpha},

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\|D_{x}K_{t-s}(\cdot+h)-D_{x}K_{t-s}(\cdot)\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{|h|^{\sigma}}\,ds\leq\mathcal{K}\!\int_{0}^{T}(t-s)^{-\frac{1+\sigma}{\alpha}}\,ds=:c_{T,\alpha,\sigma},

for all
h\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. Therefore by Young’s inequality for convolutions and

(H1):
we have for
k=0,1,2
(for
k=0
we use
\|K_{t-s}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
):

\displaystyle\|D^{k}Su(t)\|_{\infty}\leq

\displaystyle\,\|D^{k}u_{0}\|_{\infty}+(T\vee c_{T,\alpha})\Big(\sup\limits_{t}\|f(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+C(H,R)(1+\sup_{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})\Big),

\displaystyle[D_{x}^{2}Su(t)]_{C^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq

\displaystyle\,[D_{x}^{2}u_{0}]_{C^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+c_{T,\alpha,\sigma}\Big(\sup_{t}\|f(t)\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad+C(H,R)(1+\sup_{t}\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{2}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})\Big).

Since
c_{T,\alpha}
and
c_{T,\alpha,\sigma}
converge to 0 as
T\to 0^{+}
, by taking
T
small enough we get
\sup_{t}\|Su(t)\|_{C^{2+\sigma}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq R_{1}
.
Thus
S
maps
X
to
X
.

Then it remains to show that
S
is a contraction. The computations are similar to the above, we only inspect the highest order term to see that the bounds in
(H1):
are sufficient: by (

5.
Schauder estimates with irregular initial data

In an ongoing project with Amund Bergset we study viscous HJ equations with initial data
u_{0}\in C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
\beta\in(0,1)
.
In this case
\|Du(t)\|_{\infty}
blows up as
t\to 0^{+}
, and therefore we need more explicit and global control of growth/regularity of the Hamiltonian in
p
. For simplicity we consider the following global Lipschitz condition:

(
HLip
)

\displaystyle H=H(p)\ \textnormal{ and }\ H,D_{p}H\ \textnormal{\ are globally Lipschitz in}\ p.

It should be possible to consider e.g. power-type Hamiltonians, but the analysis would be more delicate in this case. For local diffusions with subquadratic Hamiltonians and measure initial data see
[
8
]
. Superquadratic Hamiltonians were studied e.g. in
[
16
]
.

Our result shows that the
\beta
-Hölder continuity of the data is preserved by the solution, while higher order Hölder norms blow up with a specific rate as
t\to 0
. There are two cases depending on whether
\alpha+\beta
is greater or smaller than
2
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that
T>0
, (
HLip
) holds,
f\in L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, and
u_{0}\in C^{\beta}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then (
1.1
) has a unique classical solution
u
. Moreover for every small
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C
such that for all

:

\displaystyle(i)\ \quad\|u(t)\|_{\infty}+[u(t)]_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+t^{\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}}\|Du(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+t^{\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon}{\alpha}}[Du(t)]_{\alpha+\beta-\varepsilon}\leq C,

if
\alpha+\beta\leq 2
,

\displaystyle(ii)\quad\|u(t)\|_{\infty}+[u(t)]_{C^{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+t^{\frac{1-\beta}{\alpha}}\|Du(t)\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+t^{\frac{2-\beta}{\alpha}}\|D^{2}u(t)\|_{\infty}+t^{\frac{\alpha-\varepsilon}{\alpha}}[D^{2}u(t)]_{\alpha+\beta-\varepsilon}\leq C,

The proof uses a similar strategy as the proof of Theorem

Compared to earlier results, this result cover a larger class of nonlocal operators, gives the behaviour of solution smoothness near

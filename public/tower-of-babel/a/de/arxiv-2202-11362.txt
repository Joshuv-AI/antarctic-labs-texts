Local well-posedness and global existence for the Popowicz system
Wei Tan and, Zhaoyang Yin

Abstract
Popowicz system, as the interacting system of Camassa-Holm and Degasperis-Procesi equations, has attracted some attention in recent years. In this paper, we first study the local well-posedness for the cauchy problem of Popowicz system in nonhomogeneous Besov spaces B p , r s × B p , r s B^{s}_{p,r}\times B^{s}_{p,r} with s > max ⁡ { 2 , 1 p + 3 2 } s>\max\{2,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{2}\} or ( s = 2 , 2 ≤ p ≤ ∞ , 1 ≤ r ≤ 2 ) (s=2,2\leq p\leq\infty,1\leq r\leq 2) . Moreover, a new blow-up criterion and global existence with different initial values are obtained. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 35Q30, 76B03, 76D05, 76D99. Keywords : Popowicz system, Local well-posedness, Global existence, Bseov spaces, Bony decomposition.

Introduction

The aim of this paper is to study the local well-posedness in Besov spaces and global existence with different initial values for the Popowicz system:

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}m_{t}+(2u+v)m_{x}+3(2u_{x}+v_{x})m=0,\\
n_{t}+(2u+v)n_{x}+2(2u_{x}+v_{x})n=0,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),v(0,x)=v_{0}(x),\end{array}\right.

where
m=u-u_{xx}
and
n=v-v_{xx}
. The system (
1.1
) was first derived recently by Z. Popowicz in
[
29
]
and
[
28
]
,
based on the observation that the second Hamiltonian operator of the Degasperis-Procesi equation could be considered as the Dirac reduced Poisson
tensor of the second Hamiltonian operator of the Boussinesq equation.
It can be regarded as a system coupled by the camassa-Holm (C-H) and Degasperis-Procesi (D-P) equations,
so it is also called interacting system of the C-H and D-P equations by some scholars
[
41
,
33
,
39
]
.

Actually, when
m=u=0
, Popowicz system (
1.1
) is simplified to the famous C-H equation, which is completely integrable.
As a typical shallow water wave equation
[
5
,
12
]
, many different properties of C-H equation are studied by using different methods and techniques.
Constantin studied its bi-Hamiltonian structure
[
8
]
and obtained its exact peaked solitons of the form
ce^{-|x-ct|}
with
c>0

[
14
]
.
The local well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of the C-H equation in Sobolev spaces and Besov spaces was discussed in
[

,

,

,

]
.
It was shown that there exist global strong solutions to the C-H equation and finite time blow-up strong solutions to the C-H equation
[

,

]
.
The existence and uniqueness of global weak solutions and global conservative and dissipative solutions were also inverstigated in
[
13
,
34
,
4
,
3
]
.
When
n=v=0
, the system (
1.1
) becomes the famous D-P equation by rescaling the time variable.
As another typical shallow water wave model, D-P equation has also attracted extensive attention. Its traveling wave solution and soliton solution are reported respectively
[
22
,

]
. The local well-posedness, blow-up phenomena, global strong solutions and global weak solutions were studied in

In fact, the D-P equation has not only peakon solutions

However, to the best of our knowledge, there are few studies on the related properties of system (

).
Barnes and Hone studied its conservative peakons and explicit dynamics of two peakons
[

.
The local well-posedness of strong solutions and blow-up phenomena in Sobolev spaces are discussed in

Recently, Zhou studied the local well-posedness of solutions for this system (

) in nonhomogeneous Besov spaces

In this paper, we aim to improve the local well-posedness for the Cauchy problem of (

The structure of the paper is as follows: in Section 2, we introduce some preliminaries which will be used in sequel.

Preliminaries

Proposition 2.1
.

[
1
]

Let
\mathcal{C}\overset{def}{=}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
be an annulus and
\mathcal{B}\overset{def}{=}\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
be a ball.
There exists a couple of smooth functions (
\chi,\varphi
) valued in [0, 1], such that
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{B})
and
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{C})
. Moreover,

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ \chi(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,

\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash\{0\},\ \sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,

and

\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\cap\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j^{\prime}}\cdot)=\emptyset\,\ \text{if}\,\ |j-j^{\prime}|\geq 2,

\mathrm{Supp}\ \chi(\cdot)\cap\mathrm{Supp}\ \varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)=\emptyset\,\ \text{if}\,\ j\geq 1.

Denote
\mathcal{F}
by the Fourier transform and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
by its inverse.
Let
u
be a tempered distribution in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we can define the nonhomogeneous dyadic blocks
\Delta_{j}
and nonhomogeneous low frequency cut-off
operator
S_{j}
as follows:

\Delta_{j}u=0\,\ \text{if}\,\ j\leq-2,\quad\Delta_{-1}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\chi\mathcal{F}u),

\Delta_{j}u=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\varphi(2^{-j}\cdot)\mathcal{F}u)\,\ \text{if}\,\ j\geq 0,

S_{j}u=\sum_{j^{\prime}=-\infty}^{j-1}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u.

Then the Littlewood-Paley decomposition is given as follows:

u=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\Delta_{j}u\quad\text{in}\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Proposition 2.3
.

[
1

Suppose that

We have

(1) Topological properties:

is a Banach space which is continuously embedded in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(2) Density: If

is dense in

(3) Dual properties: If

(4) Embedding: If
1\leq p_{1}\leq p_{2}\leq\infty
and
1\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq\infty
, then
B^{s}_{p_{1},r_{1}}\hookrightarrow B^{s-d(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{p_{2}})}_{p_{2},r_{2}}.

If
s_{1}<s_{2}

(5) Algebraic properties:

\Leftrightarrow

B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}\Leftrightarrow s>\frac{d}{p}\ \text{or}\ s=\frac{d}{p},\ r=1.\quad

(6) Fatou property: if

, then an element

and a subsequence
(u_{n_{k}})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
exist such that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}u_{n_{k}}=u\ \text{in}\ \mathcal{S}^{\prime}\quad\text{and}\quad\|u\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}\leq C\liminf_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|u_{n_{k}}\|_{B^{s}_{p,r}}.

(7) Let
m\in\mathbb{R}
and
f
be a
S^{m}
-mutiplier, (that is
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a smooth function and satisfies that
\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}

\exists C=C(\alpha)
, such that

Then the operator

Secondly, some properties of nonhomogeneous Bony decomposition that need to be used in this paper are introduced.

Let

The nonhomogeneous paraproduct of

T_{u}v=\sum_{j}S_{j-1}u\Delta_{j}v.

The nonhomogeneous remainder of

R(u,v)=\sum_{|j^{\prime}-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{j^{\prime}}u\Delta_{j}v.

At least formally, the operators

uv=T_{u}v+T_{v}u+R(u,v).

3
Local well-posedness

In this section, we will establish local well-posedness of (
1.1
) in Besov spaces.
We firstly provide the framework in which we shall reformulate system (
1.1
). Note that if
p(x):=\frac{1}{2}e^{|x|},x\in\mathbb{R}
,
then
(1-\partial_{x}^{2})^{-1}f=p*f
for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
(p*m,p*n)=(u,v)
. Then, system (
1.1
) can be rewritten as follows:

(3.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u_{t}+(2u+v)u_{x}=p*\big(-3(2u_{x}+v_{x})u+v_{x}u_{xx}-v_{xx}u_{x}\big)\overset{\Delta}{=}F,\\
v_{t}+(2u+v)v_{x}=p*\big(-2(2u_{x}+v_{x})v-2v_{x}u_{xx}-v_{xx}v_{x}\big)\overset{\Delta}{=}H,\\
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),v(0,x)=v_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

In order to prove the local well-posedness of the strong solution of system
3.1
, we give the definition of space
E^{s}_{p,r}(T)
and a lemma which plays a key role in the proof.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
T>0,\ s\in\mathbb{R},
and
1\leq p,r\leq\infty.
Set

E^{s}_{p,r}(T)\triangleq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}C([0,T];B^{s}_{p,r})\cap C^{1}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,r}),&\text{if}\ r<\infty,\\
C_{w}([0,T];B^{s}_{p,\infty})\cap C^{0,1}([0,T];B^{s-1}_{p,\infty}),&\text{if}\ r=\infty.\end{array}\right.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
s>max\{2,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{2}\}
(or

\|uv\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}.

Proof: The proof of this Lemma is divided into two parts according to the value of
s
. By virtue of bony’s decomposition, we have

uv=T_{u}v+T_{v}u+R(u,v).

Firstly, we prove the case of
s>max\{2,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{2}\}

According to Proposition 2.3 and 2.6, since
B_{p,r}^{s}\hookrightarrow B_{p,r_{1}}^{s}(r\leq r_{1})
, we have

(3.2)

\|T_{u}v\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{-\frac{1}{p}}_{\infty,\infty}}\|v\|_{B^{s-3+\frac{1}{p}}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{0}_{p,\infty}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}},

and

(3.3)

\|T_{v}u\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\leq C\|v\|_{B^{-1}_{\infty,\infty}}\|u\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\leq C\|v\|_{B^{\frac{1}{p}-1}_{p,\infty}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}.

When
2\leq p\leq\infty
, according to Propositions 2.3 and 2.7, we get

(3.4)

\|R(u,v)\|_{B^{s-3}_{p,r}}\leq C\|R(u,v)\|_{B^{s-3+\frac{1}{p}}_{\frac{p}{2},r}}\leq C\|R(u,v)\|_{B^{2s-4}_{\frac{p}{2},r}}\leq C\|u\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}\|v\|_{B^{s-2}_{p,r}}.

When
1\leq p\leq 2

&\leq C(\|(2u_{x}+v_{x})u\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{x}u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}}+\|v_{xx}u_{x}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\leq C(\|2u_{x}+v_{x}\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{2}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{2}})\\
&\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}})(\|u\|_{H^{2}}+\|v\|_{H^{2}}).\end{split}

An inequality similar to (
4.9
) can be obtained by proofs similar to the firstly.

If
T^{*}
is finite, and
\int_{0}^{T^{*}}(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}})dt^{{}^{\prime}}<\infty
,
then
(u,v)\in L^{\infty}([0,T^{*});B^{s}_{p,r}\times B^{s}_{p,r})
, which
contradicts the assumption that
T^{*}
is the maximal existence time.

On the other hand, by Theorem (
3.3
) and the fact that
B^{s}_{p,r}\hookrightarrow C^{0,1}
,
if
\int_{0}^{T^{*}}(\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}})dt^{{}^{\prime}}=\infty
, then
(u,v)
must blow up in finite time.

Remark 2.
The blow-up criterion Theorem
4.1
obtained in this paper is an improvement of
[
33
]
and
[
39
]
.
The conditions required for their results are that there exists
M>0
such that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{xx}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{xx}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M

for
t\in[0,T)
.

4.2.  Global existence

First we prove a conserved quantity for (
1.1
).

Theorem 4.2
.

If
m_{0},n_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}),s>\frac{1}{2},
then as long as the solution
m(t,x),n(t,x)
given by (1.1) exists, we have

(4.10)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}m+ndx^{{}^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{0}+n_{0}dx^{{}^{\prime}}.

Proof:
Arguing by density, it suffices to consider the case where
u,v\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.
Using integration by parts, we deduce that

(4.11)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}m+ndx^{{}^{\prime}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}m_{t}+n_{t}dx^{{}^{\prime}}&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-(2u+v)(m_{x}+n_{x})-(2u_{x}+v_{x})(3m+2n)dx^{{}^{\prime}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-(2u_{x}+v_{x})(2m+n)dx^{{}^{\prime}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-(2u_{x}+v_{x})(2u-2u_{xx}+v-v_{xx})dx^{{}^{\prime}}\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-2(uv)_{x}+2(u_{x}v_{x})_{x}dx^{{}^{\prime}}=0.\end{split}

Let us consider the ordinary differential equation:

(4.12)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}q_{t}(t,x)=(2u+v)(t,q(t,x)),\quad t\in[0,T),\\
q(0,x)=x,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{array}\right.

If
(m,n)\in B^{s}_{p,r}\times B^{s}_{p,r}
with
(s,p,r)
being as in Theorem
3.3
, then
2u+v\in C([0,T);C^{0,1})
. According to the classical results in the theory of ordinary differential equations, we can easily infer that (
4.12
) have a unique solution
q\in C^{1}([0,T)\times\mathbb{R};\mathbb{R})
such that the map
q(t,\cdot)
is an increasing diffeomorphism of
\mathbb{R}
with

q_{x}(t,x)=\exp\Big(\int_{0}^{t}(2u_{x}+v_{x})(t^{\prime},q(t^{\prime},x)\Big)dt^{\prime}>0,\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T)\times\mathbb{R}.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
m_{0},n_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}\bigcap L^{1}
with
(s,p,r)
satisfies Theorem
4.1
, and
m_{0}\geq 0,n_{0}\geq 0
.
Then the corresponding solution
m,n
of (
1.1
) exists globally in time.

Proof:

From (
1.1
), we know

(4.13)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d}{dt}m(t,q(t,x))=-3\big((2u_{x}+v_{x})m\big)(t,q(t,x)),\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{d}{dt}n(t,q(t,x))=-2\big((2u_{x}+v_{x})m\big)(t,q(t,x)),\\[4.30554pt]
\end{array}\right.

hence

(4.14)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}m(t,q(t,x))=m_{0}(x)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\big(-3(2u_{x}+v_{x})(t^{{}^{\prime}},q(t^{{}^{\prime}},x))\big)dt^{{}^{\prime}}},\\
n(t,q(t,x))=n_{0}(x)e^{\int_{0}^{t}\big(-2(2u_{x}+v_{x})(t^{{}^{\prime}},q(t^{{}^{\prime}},x))\big)dt^{{}^{\prime}}},\\
\end{array}\right.

which implies that
(m,n)
doesn’t change sign. That is, when
m_{0}\geq 0,n_{0}\geq 0
, we have
m\geq 0,n\geq 0
.
Moreover, we get

(4.15)

\begin{split}|u_{x}|=|p_{x}*m(t,x)|\leq|p_{x}|*m(t,x)=p*m(t,x)=u(t,x)\Rightarrow|u_{x}|_{L^{\infty}}\leq|u|_{L^{\infty}},\\
\|u_{x}+v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u+v\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|p*(m+n)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|m+n\|_{L^{1}}=C\|m_{0}+n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\overset{\Delta}{=}C_{0}.\end{split}

Using integration by parts, we deduce that

(4.16)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}mdx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-\Big((2u+v)m_{x}+3(2u_{x}+v_{x})m\Big)dx\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-2(2u_{x}+v_{x})mdx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}-4(u_{x}+v_{x})mdx\\
&\leq 4\|u_{x}+v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|m\|_{L^{1}}\leq 4C_{0}\|m\|_{L^{1}}.\end{split}

Applying Gronwall’s inequality yields that, for all
t\in[0,T)
,

(4.17)

\|m\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{4C_{0}T},

similarly,

(4.18)

\|n\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{2C_{0}T}.

Combining (4.15), (4.17) and (4.18), we get

\begin{split}\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|p_{x}*m\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|m\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{4C_{0}T},\\
\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|p_{x}*n\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|n\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{2C_{0}T}.\end{split}

Moreover, we have

\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{2C_{0}T}+2\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}e^{4C_{0}T}.

Combining Theorem 4.1 then allows us to complete the proof of Theorem 4.3.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(m,n)
be the corresponding local solution of (
1.1
), if the initial data
m_{0},n_{0}\in B^{s}_{p,r}\bigcap L^{1}
with
(s,p,r)
satisfies Theorem
4.1
,
m_{0}(x)
and
n_{0}(x)
are odd functions such that
m_{0}\leq 0
,
n_{0}\leq 0
when
x\leq 0
, and
m_{0}\geq 0
,
n_{0}\geq 0
when
x\geq 0
, then the solution
m(t,x),n(t,x)
exists globally.

Proof.

Set
T
be the maximal time of
m(t,x)
and
n(t,x)
.
One can easily deduce that
m(t,x)
and
n(t,x)
are odd functions if
m_{0}(x)
and
n_{0}(x)
are odd functions.
According to (
4.14
), we can deduce that if
m_{0}\leq 0
and
n_{0}\leq 0
when
x\leq 0
,
m_{0}\geq 0
and
n_{0}\geq 0
when
x\geq 0
, then we obtain

(4.19)

\displaystyle m(t,x)\leq 0,n(t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\leq q(t,0);~~m(t,x)\geq 0,n(t,x)\geq 0~~when~~x\geq q(t,0),~~\forall t\in[0,T),

where
q(t,\xi)=\xi+\int_{0}^{t}(2u+v)(\tau,q(\tau,\xi))d\tau
is the characteristic curves.

Next we want to prove that

(4.20)

\displaystyle m(t,x)\leq 0,n(t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\leq 0;~~m(t,x)\geq 0,n(t,x)\geq 0~~when~~x\geq 0,~~\forall t\in[0,T).

If
q(t,0)=0
for any
t\in[0,T)
, then by (
4.19
) we immediately get (
4.20
). Other if
q(t,0)>0
(or
q(t,0)<0
) for some
t\in[0,T)
, by (
4.19
) we deduce that

m(t,x)\leq 0,n(t,x)\leq 0~~when~~x\in[-q(t,0),0];~~m(t,x)\geq 0,n(t,x)\geq 0~~when~~x\in[0,q(t,0)].

Since
m(t,x),n(t,x)
are odd functions such that
m(t,x)=-m(t,-x),n(t,x)=-n(t,-x),~x\in[-q(t,0),q(t,0)]
, we obtain

m(t,x)=0,n(t,x)=0~~when~~x\in[-q(t,0),q(t,0)].

By (
4.19
) again we still get (
4.20
). Note that the function
u(t,0)=m(t,0)=0
,
v(t,0)=n(t,0)=0
.
Using the characteristic method and calculations similar to Theorem 4.2, we have

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(m+n)dx^{{}^{\prime}}=0,

then

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|m|+|n|dx^{{}^{\prime}}=\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|m+n|dx^{{}^{\prime}}=2\frac{d}{dt}\int_{0}^{+\infty}(m+n)dx^{{}^{\prime}}=0,

thus,

\|m\|_{L^{1}}+\|n\|_{L^{1}}=\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

Similar to the proof of Theorem 4.3, we get

(4.21)

\begin{split}&\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|p*m\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|p_{x}*m\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|m\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}),\\
&\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|p*n\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|p_{x}*n\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|n\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}).\end{split}

According to (4.21), we have

\|u_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v_{x}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C(\|m_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\|n_{0}\|_{L^{1}}).

By the blow-up criteria we obtain the global existence.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the local well-posedness of Popowicz system in Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}\times B^{s}_{p,r}
with
s>\max\{2,\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{2}\}

or
(s=2,2\leq p\leq\infty,1\leq r\leq 2)
,
which is an extension of Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
1\leq p,r\leq+\infty
and
s>\max\{\frac{5}{2},2+\frac{1}{p}\}
in
[
41
]
.
We also obtained a new blow up criterion, which is an extension of
[
33
]
and
[
39
]
.
Besides, we studied the global existence with different initial values of Popowicz systems for the first time.
As the interacting system of C-H and D-P equations, Popowicz system has many properties worthy of further study, such as blow-up of solutions
[
10
]
, existence of weak solutions
[
13
]
, global conservation weak solutions
[
3
]
, dynamic behavior of solitons
[
31
]
and ill-posedness
[
20
]
, which will be studied in our future work.

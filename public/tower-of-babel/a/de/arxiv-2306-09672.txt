A Generalized vanishing theorem for Blow-ups of Quasi-smooth Stacks
Yu Zhao

Abstract
. We prove a generalized vanishing theorem for certain quasi-coherent sheaves along the derived blow-ups of quasi-smooth derived Artin stacks. We give four applications of the generalized vanishing theorem: we prove a K K -theoretic version of the generalized vanishing theorem which verified a conjecture of the author [ 31 ] and give a new proof of the K K -theoretic localization theorem for quasi-smooth derived schemes through the intrinsic blow-up theory of Kiem-Li-Savvas [ 17 ] ; we prove a desingularization theorem for quasi-smooth derived schemes and give an approximation formula for the virtual fundamental classes; we give a resolution of the diagonal along the projection map of blow-ups of smooth varieties, which strengthens the semi-orthogonal decomposition theorem of Orlov [ 24 ] ; we illustrate the relation between the generalized vanishing theorem and weak categorifications of quantum loop and toroidal algebra actions on the derived category of Nakajima quiver varieties. We also propose several conjectures related to birational geometry and the L ∞ L_{\infty} -algebroid of Calaque-Căldăraru-Tu [ 5 ] .

We recall the fact that the quantum loop algebra has generators

, where

over

(strictly speaking, we need to twist a line bundle which depends on the quiver

and
Theorem

We will explain the details of the above discussions in

and construct a weak categorification of quantum loop and toroidal algebra actions on the Grothendieck group of quiver varieties.

Appendix B

In this section, we study the desingularization of quasi-smooth derived schemes. The main theorem of this section is

Theorem B.1

(Desingulization Theorem)
.

Let
X
be a non-empty quasi-smooth derived scheme. We assume that there is a closed embedding
p:X\to S
where

is a smooth variety. Then there exists a collection of quasi-smooth derived schemes

for

, where

(1)

X_{0}\cong X
and
X_{n}\cong\emptyset
;

(2)

X_{i}\cong\mathbb{B}l_{Z_{i-1}/X_{i-1}}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

(3)

for any
0\leq i<n
,
X_{i}
admits a closed embedding
p_{i}:X_{i}\to S_{i}
where
S_{i}
is a smooth variety.

Together with
Eq.

1.1
, we have the following theorem for the virtual fundamental class of quasi-smooth derived schemes:

Theorem B.2

(Approximation Theorem)
.

Let

be a quasi-smooth derived scheme with a closed embedding into a smooth ambient variety. Let

. Then there exists a collection

(2)

is a smooth scheme such that

(3)

is a perfect complex over

with tor-amplitude

such that

(B.1)

[\mathcal{O}_{X}]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{r_{i}}p_{i*}(det(\mathcal{F}_{i})^{-1}\sum_{j=0}^{-r_{i}}S^{j}(\mathcal{F}_{i}^{\vee})).

Appendix C
Intrinsic Blow-ups and Virtual Localization Formula

Let
U
be a locally of finite presentation and quasi-compact derived scheme over
\mathbb{C}
, with an action by a connected reductive
G
such that the (derived) fixed locus
\pi_{0}(U)^{G}
is non-empty. In Theorem 5.3 of
[
11
]
, Hekking-Rydh-Savvas proved that the derived blow-up
\mathbb{B}l_{U^{G}}U
is the derived enhancement of the intrinsic blow-up of
\pi_{0}(U^{G})
in
\pi_{0}(U)
in the sense of
[
17
]
. In this section, we will prove that the intrinsic blow-up along the fixed locus will induce a virtual localization formula for the perfect obstruction theory coming from a quasi-smooth derived scheme.

C.1.
The
G
-theory of a derived scheme

Let
U
be a quasi-smooth derived scheme over
\mathbb{C}
. The homotopy category of the
\infty
-category
\mathrm{Coh}(U)
has a natural
t
-structure such that the heart is
Coh(\pi_{0}(U))
. Hence we have that the
G
-theory

G(X):=K(\mathrm{Coh}(X))\cong G(\pi_{0}(U)).

The above formula also holds for the equivariant
G
-theory. Now we assume that there is a torus
T
acts on
X
.

Lemma C.1

(Proposition 3.24 or Proposition 7.4)
.

The fixed locus
U^{T}
is also quasi-smooth over
\mathbb{C}
.

Let
g:Y\to U
be a
T
-equivariant closed embedding such that
(U-Y)^{T}=\emptyset
. Then by Thomason
[
25
]

\displaystyle G^{T}(U-Y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))

\displaystyle\cong G^{T}(\pi_{0}(U)-\pi_{0}(Y))\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))

\displaystyle\cong 0

Hence we have the concentration lemma by the localization theorem

Lemma C.2

(Concentration lemma)
.

The embedding
g
induces an isomorphism

g_{*}:G^{T}(Y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))\cong G^{T}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

Specifically, we denote
i_{T}:U^{T}\to U
as the closed embedding of fixed locus. Then we have the isomorphism

i_{T*}:G^{T}(U^{T})\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))\cong G^{T}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep}(T)}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

C.2.
The infinite wedge sum
[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}\mathcal{F}}]

Let
Y
be a quasi-smooth derived scheme over
\mathbb{C}
, with a trivial torus
T
action. Let
\mathcal{F}
be
T
-equivariant quasi-coherent sheaf of perfect-amplitude contained in
[0,1]
. Moreover, we assume that for every closed point

, the
T
-action on

has no trivial sub-representations. The purpose of this subsection is to define

[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}\mathcal{F}}]\in G_{0}^{T}(Y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

The torus
T

induces an action of
T
on

. By

, we have

\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(1))-\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})\cong\mathbb{V}^{*}_{X}(\mathcal{F})

and
(\mathbb{V}_{Y}^{*}(\mathcal{F}))^{T}\cong\emptyset
. Thus by the concentration lemma

C.2
, there exists a unique class, which we denote as

[\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}}(1)}]\in G_{0}^{T}(\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F}))\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))

such that

i_{0*}[\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}}(1)}]\cong[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{V}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(1))}].

It is the inverse of
1-[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}}(1)]
in
G_{0}^{T}(\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F}))\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))
.

Definition C.3
.

We define the class

[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}\mathcal{F}}]\in G_{0}^{T}(Y)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))

as

pr_{\mathcal{F}*}(\frac{1}{1-[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}}(1)]})+(-1)^{r}det(\mathcal{F})^{-1}\sum_{l=0}^{-r}[S^{l}(\mathcal{F}^{\vee})]

where

The

Lemma C.4
.

Let
g:S\to Y
be a map of derived schemes, such that
g^{*}\mathcal{F}\cong\{V\to W\}
, where

has no components with trivial representations, and

[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}(g^{*}\mathcal{F})}]\cong[\wedge^{\bullet}V][\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}W}].

Proof.

We can assume that

, as the projectivization is compatible with pull-backs. We denote

and

as the ranks of

and

Over
\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})

[\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(1)}]\cdot pr^{*}_{\mathcal{F}}[\wedge^{\bullet}W]=\sum_{i=0}^{w}(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}[\wedge^{i-j}pr_{\mathcal{*}}W][\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(j)].

We first prove the case that

\displaystyle pr_{\mathcal{F}*}[\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{Y}(\mathcal{F})}(1)}]\cdot[\wedge^{\bullet}W]

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{w}(-1)^{i}\sum_{j=0}^{i}(-1)^{j}[\wedge^{i-j}W][S^{j}\mathcal{F}]

\displaystyle=[\wedge^{\bullet}V].

Next we prove the case
r\leq 0
be the induction on

C.3.
The virtual localization theorem

Now we consider the virtual localization theorem. Let
U
be a quasi-smooth derived scheme with a constant virtual dimension over
\mathbb{C}
with a torus
T
action. Let
V_{1},\cdots,V_{l}
be the connected components of
U^{T}
, which are all quasi-smooth due to
Lemma

C.1
. As the rank of
C_{U^{T}/U}
is a locally constant, all
C_{V_{j}/U}
has constant ranks which we denote as
r_{j}
.

Theorem C.5

(Virtual localization theorem)
.

We have

[\mathcal{O}_{U}]=\sum_{j=1}^{l}i_{V_{j}*}[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}C_{V_{j}/U}}]\in G_{0}^{T}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

Proof.

We consider the intrinsic blow-up
\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}}U
. Then by
Eq.

1.1
, we have

[\mathcal{O}_{U}]=[pr_{U^{T}/U*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}/U}}]+\sum_{j=1}^{l}i_{V_{j}*}(-1)^{r_{j}}det(C_{V_{j}/U})^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{-r_{i}}[S^{k}(C_{V_{j}/U}^{\vee})].

Moreover, we consider the exceptional divisors
\eta_{j}:E_{j}\to\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}}U
, where
E_{j}\cong\mathbb{P}_{V_{j}}(C_{V_{j}/U})
. Then
(\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}}U-\cup_{j=1}^{l}E_{j})^{T}=\emptyset
and thus we have the isomorphism:

\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}\eta_{j*}:\bigoplus_{j=1}^{l}G_{0}(E_{j})\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))\to G_{0}(\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}}U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

By restricting to
E_{j}
, we have

[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}/U}}]\cong\sum_{j=1}^{l}\eta_{j*}[\frac{1}{1-\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{V_{j}(C_{V_{j}/U})}}(1)}].

in
G_{0}(\mathbb{B}l_{U^{T}}U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T))
.
Thus we have
[\mathcal{O}_{U}]
is the sum of all

i_{V_{j}*}(pr_{C_{V_{j}/U}*}\frac{1}{1-[\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{V_{j}}(C_{V_{j}/U})}(1)]}+(-1)^{r_{j}}det(C_{V_{j}/U})^{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{-r_{j}}[S^{k}(C_{V_{j}/U}^{\vee})])

for all
1\leq j\leq l
, which is

\sum_{j=1}^{l}i_{V_{j}*}[\frac{1}{\wedge^{\bullet}C_{V_{j}/U}}]\in G_{0}^{T}(U)\otimes_{\mathrm{Rep(T)}}Frac(\mathrm{Rep}(T)).

∎

Fourier transform decay of distributions in Hardy-Morrey spaces Thanks: Work supported in part by CNPq (409306/2016-9) and FAPESP (2013/17636-5 and 2018/15484-7).
Marcelo F. de Almeida, Tiago Picon

Abstract
. In this paper we establish decay estimates for Fourier transform on Hardy-Morrey spaces and its localizable version. Our work include some aspects to these spaces linked up with pointwise Fourier estimates, in particular a natural approach on cancellation moment conditions. As application, we discuss the optimality for continuity of Fourier multipliers and pseudodifferential operators in Hardy-Morrey spaces.

1.
Introduction

The theory of Morrey spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
have been extensively developed during the last decades in the several settings
(see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
27
]
).
These spaces describe local regularity of functions in
L^{q}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
by special averages on balls as a refinement of Lebesgue spaces. Among many applications of Morrey spaces we highlight the classical gain of Sobolev embedding into Hölder function space
C^{0,\delta}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
archived by Morrey (see
[
4
]
for details). In the other hand, it is well known that Hardy space
H^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(
[
11
]
) are nice substitutes of Lesbegue spaces and that a rich functional distributional space shines for
0<q\leq 1
with several applications in Harmonic Analysis, Functional Analysis and PDEs (see e.g.
[
10
,
23
]
).

In
[
18
,
20
]
the authors considered an hybrid space
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
, called the Hardy-Morrey space, presenting a natural characterization via smooth maximal function and atomic decomposition theorem (see
[
20
, p.100]
and
[
18
, Theorems 1.3 and 1.4]
). As expected, these spaces recover the Morrey spaces for
1<q<\lambda<\infty
and Hardy spaces for
0<q=\lambda<1
(see
[
11
]
). Variations of Hardy-Morrey spaces have been studied in
[
2
,
16
,
17
]
and explored in several contexts as Calderón-Zygmund theory, singular integral operators
[
19
]
and characterizations of trace-law to Riesz potentials
[
21
]
. These studies are motivated by applications on regularity theory of PDEs, geometric harmonic/potential analysis and fluid dynamics as well. We point out that comparable to Morrey space
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
some properties for
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
breaks down when
0<q<\lambda<1
. For instance, the Hardy-Morrey spaces are not closed by multiplication on test functions and for this reason some linear operators (as pseudodifferential operators) in general are not bounded on
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. It follows by intrinsic relation with Hardy spaces empowered with suitable nature of Morrey norm.

In this paper we carry further to study of decay estimates for Fourier transform of distributions in Hardy-Morrey spaces (see Definition
2.1
) and its localizable version
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see Definition
2.2
).
Our main result is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda\leq 1
.
If
f\in\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then there exists
C=C(n,q,\lambda)>0
such that
\widehat{f}(\xi)
is a continuous function and satisfies the pointwise Fourier transform decay

(1.1)

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq C|\xi|^{n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},{\quad\forall\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}.

If
f\in h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
then its Fourier transform satisfies

(1.2)

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\lesssim\langle\xi\rangle^{n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right)}\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}},

where
\langle\xi\rangle:=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}
.

The proof of the previous theorem is presented in Theorem
3.3
for distributions in Hardy-Morrey spaces and Theorem
4.1
for localizable version. As we can see some aspects of these spaces linked up to a suitable analysis on the necessity of moment conditions inspired by works due to Goldberg
[
13
]
and Hounie & Kapp
[
15
]
. Also, as we can see by Remark
3.4
, the decay estimates for Fourier transform are not expected in Morrey spaces as
1\leq q<\lambda<\infty
. Our approach follows by using the classical maximal function definition of the Hardy-Morrey spaces contributing to simplify several proofs found in the literature. In addition, closely related to Bownik & Wang
[
5
]
we present an analysis on moment conditions on Hardy-Morrey spaces and cancellation properties on its localizable version.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In the Section

in
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as

2.
Hardy-Morrey space
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}

In this section we recall some properties and aspects of Hardy-Morrey spaces and its nonhomogeneous version linked up to a suitable analysis on the necessity of moment conditions that can not be found in the literature. A measurable function
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to Morrey space
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if it satisfies

\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}:=\sup_{Q}|Q|^{{1}/{\lambda}-{1}/{q}}\left(\int_{Q}|f(y)|^{q}dy\right)^{1/q}<\infty,

for
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
, where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
with sidelength
\ell_{Q}>0
and Lebesgue measure
|Q|
. The Morrey spaces endowed by functional
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}
are complete metric spaces and include strictly (see
[
30
]
) the Lebesgue spaces
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. However these spaces differ in several points, for instance, the functions in
\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can not be approximated by smooth functions as long as
1\leq q<\lambda<\infty
.
We refer
[
3
,
4
,
12
,
27
]
for an introduction on these spaces for
1\leq q<\lambda<\infty
and
[
28
, Chapter 3]
for
0<q<\lambda<\infty
.

Jia and Wang had introduced the following class of tempered distributions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
. We say that a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, if there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx\neq 0}
such that the smooth maximal function

M_{\varphi}f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where

(2.1)

M_{\varphi}f(x)=\sup_{0<t<\infty}\left|\varphi_{t}\ast f(x)\right|,\quad\quad\varphi_{t}(x)=t^{-n}\varphi(x/t).

The functional
\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}:=\|M_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}
defines a quasi-norm as
0<q<1
and is a norm for
q\geq 1
(we always refer as a “norm” for simplicity). It follows that
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
endowed by distance

d(f,g):=\|f-g\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}
is a complete metric space
[
20
, Lemma 2.5]
. We point out that
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with
\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
1<q\leq\lambda<\infty
, i.e. there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only the parameters
n,q,\lambda
such that

(2.2)

c_{1}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\leq c_{2}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}.

Moreover, the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{HM}_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
holds for
1<\lambda<\infty

(see
[
18
,
21
]
). Obviously some properties are inherited from Morrey spaces, for instance the convexity in Morrey spaces are naturally extended by Hardy-Morrey spaces i.e.

\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda_{2}}_{q_{2}}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda_{1}}_{q_{1}}}^{\theta}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda_{3}}_{q_{3}}}^{1-\theta},

where
0<q_{1}<q_{2}<q_{3}\leq\infty
and
0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\lambda_{3}\leq\infty
satisfy

\frac{1}{q_{2}}=\frac{\theta}{q_{1}}+\frac{1-\theta}{q_{3}}
and
{\frac{1}{\lambda_{2}}=\frac{\theta}{\lambda_{1}}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\lambda_{3}}
for
0<\theta<1
. Now, consider
\delta_{R}f(x):=f(Rx)\,
for
R>0
. Since
\varphi_{t}\ast\delta_{R}f={\delta_{R}(\varphi_{tR}\ast f)}
, it follows from scaling property of Morrey space
\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
that

(2.3)

\|\delta_{R}f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}=\|M_{\varphi}(\delta_{R}f)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}=\|\delta_{R}(M_{\varphi}f)\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}=R^{-n/\lambda}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},

which is the expected scaling for distributions in Hardy-Morrey space
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Analogous to Hardy spaces another maximal characterizations are obtained for Hardy-Morrey spaces, that will be describe in sequel. For each
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\left\{0\right\}
let
\mathcal{F}:=\{\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,\beta}\,:\,\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}\,\text{ such that }\,|\alpha|\leq N,\,|\beta|\leq N\,\}
be a finite collection of semi-norms defined on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and consider

\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{F}}:=\left\{\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,:\,\|\varphi\|_{\alpha,\beta}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left|x^{\alpha}\partial_{x}^{\beta}\varphi(x)\right|\leq 1,\,\text{ for all }\,\|\cdot\|_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{F}\right\}.

We define the
grand maximal function
of
f
by

\displaystyle{M_{\mathcal{F}}f(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{S_{F}}}M_{\varphi}f(x)}

and the
non-tangential
version of
M_{\varphi}
is defined by

(2.4)

M_{\varphi}^{\ast}f(x):=\sup_{|x-y|<t}\left|\varphi_{t}\ast f(y)\right|.

Clearly
M_{\varphi}f(x)\leq M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f(x)
and
M_{\varphi}f(x)\lesssim M_{\mathcal{F}}f(x)

The notation

means that there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f(x)\leq Cg(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Lastly, we say that
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a bounded tempered distribution if
f\ast\varphi\in L^{\infty}
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The following characterization can be stated for Hardy-Morrey spaces.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
and
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. The following statements are equivalents:

(i)

There is
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx=1
such that
M_{\varphi}f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(ii)

There exist a collection
\mathcal{F}
so that
M_{\mathcal{F}}f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

(iii)

f is a bounded tempered distribution and
M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Moreover,

(2.5)

\|M_{\mathcal{F}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim\|M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim\|M_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim\|M_{\mathcal{F}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}.

Hence,

\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
if one (thus all) of the properties above are satisfied and from Theorem
2.1

\|M_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}
is independent of the choice of the function
\varphi
. As a consequence of Definition

, the Hardy-Morrey spaces cover the Hardy spaces i.e
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=H^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<q<\infty

and naturally these spaces are nice substitutes of Morrey spaces and describe a local control of Hardy spaces when
0<q\leq 1
and
q<\lambda
. By reasons of previous comments, we emphasize our study on
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

and

.

Analogous to previous maximal functions, we may define the truncated version of
tangential, non-tangential
and
grand maximal function
for
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
respectively as follows

\displaystyle m_{\varphi}f(x)=\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\left|(\varphi_{t}\ast f)(x)\right|,\,\,m_{\varphi}^{\ast}f(x)=\sup_{|x-y|<t}\left|(\varphi_{t}\ast f)(y)\right|\;\text{and}\;m_{\mathcal{F}}f(x):=\sup_{\varphi\in\mathcal{S_{F}}}m_{\varphi}f(x),

and the following nonhomogeneous version of
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
. We say that a tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
belongs to
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, called nonhomogeneous (or localizable) Hardy-Morrey spaces, if there exists
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx\neq 0
such that

m_{\varphi}f\in\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The functional
\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}:=\|m_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}
defines a quasi-norm as
0<q<1
and the space is a complete metric space. Clearly the nonhomogeneous version covers the localizable Hardy spaces due to Goldberg
[
13
]
i.e.
h\mathcal{M}_{p}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=h^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
holds and moreover
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
coincides with Morrey spaces as
1<q<\lambda<\infty
.
An analogous characterization for
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, as Theorem
2.1
, can be stated with suitable adaptation for truncated maximal functions

The next assertion provides a way of transferring properties from
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to the corresponding localizable space
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This lemma was first announced in
[

, and here we present only some steps of the proof by convenience.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
24
]
)
.

Let
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\psi(x)dx=1
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}\psi(x)dx=0
for all multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
such that
|\alpha|\neq 0
. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\|f-\psi\ast f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\leq C\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}},

for all
f\in h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
0<q\leq 1
and
q\leq\lambda<\infty
.

Proof.

Given
f\in h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we can estimate

\displaystyle\|f-\psi\ast f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\left|\varphi_{t}\ast f\right|\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}+\;\;\left\|\sup_{0<t\leq 1}\left|(\varphi_{t}\ast\psi)\ast f\right|\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}+\;\,\left\|\sup_{t>1}\left|(\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}\ast\psi)\ast f\right|\right\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle:=\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}+I_{1}(f)+I_{2}(f).

Since
\left\{\varphi_{t}\ast\psi\right\}_{t\leq 1}
and
\left\{\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}\ast\psi\right\}_{t>1}
are bounded on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
(see
[
13
, Lemma 4]
), then

|(\varphi_{t}\ast\psi)\ast f|\lesssim m_{\mathcal{F}}f\;\text{ for }\;t\leq 1\hskip 9.24994pt\text{ and }\hskip 9.24994pt|(\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t}\ast\psi)\ast f|\lesssim m_{\mathcal{F}}f\;\text{ for }\;t>1,

which yields
I_{j}(f)\lesssim\|m_{\mathcal{F}}f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}
for
j=1,2
as we wished to show.
∎

2.1.
Some properties of Hardy-Morrey spaces

The next two lemmas are well known and we given a proof just for read convenience.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda<\infty
and
0<p\leq\gamma<\infty
be such that
p/\gamma\,=\,q/\lambda
. If
f\in\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\lambda\leq\gamma
then there exists
C>0
independent of

such that

(2.6)

\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{p}^{\gamma}}\leq C\,t^{n\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},\quad\quad\;t>0,

provided
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx\neq 0
.

Proof.

By inequality (
2.5
) we can infer

(2.7)

\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}\leq\|M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}},\quad\quad t>0.

Now let
Q_{t}
be a cube with sidelength
\ell_{Q_{t}}=t
, then

(2.8)

\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{\infty}\lesssim\left(\frac{1}{|Q_{t}|}\int_{Q_{t}}|M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f(y)|^{q}dy\right)^{1/q}\lesssim|Q_{t}|^{-\frac{1}{\lambda}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim t^{-\frac{n}{\lambda}}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}},

where in the first inequality we used the pointwise control
M_{\varphi}f(x)\leq M^{\ast}_{\varphi}f(x)
for all
|x-y|<t
. Now, from convexity of Morrey spaces using (
2.7
) and (

) we obtain the desired estimate provided
\lambda\leq\gamma
satisfy
p\lambda=q\gamma

∎

Taking

at (

(2.9)

\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{\mathcal{M}_{1}^{\gamma}}\leq C\,t^{-\frac{n}{\lambda}(1-q)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},\quad\quad\;t>0.

Analogous to the Hardy spaces, moment conditions play a fundamental role in the theory of Hardy-Morrey spaces. The Fourier transform decaying (
3.1
) implies that restriction

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx=0
is necessary for a bounded, compactly supported function
f
belong to Hardy-Morrey space
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
0<q\,\leq\lambda<1
. Bootstrapping the previous argument we may obtain

(2.10)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0\;\text{ for }\;|\alpha|\leq\left\lfloor n\left({1}/{\lambda}-1\right)\right\rfloor

where
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots x_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}
for
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}_{0}
and
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
denotes the floor function. This property shows us that Hardy-Morrey spaces for
0<q\leq\lambda<1
are not closed by multiplication on test functions. Indeed, let
f
be as in Proposition
3.2

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)dx=0
. Hence, for all
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

we conclude that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\varphi(x)dx=0
which is a contradiction, since
f\not\equiv 0
. The necessity of moment conditions for bounded and compactly supported functions on
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
0<q\leq 1
and
q\leq\lambda<\infty

(2.11)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}x^{\alpha}f(x)dx=0\;\text{ for }\;|\alpha|\leq L\,\,\,\,\text{with}\;\;L\geq N_{q}:=\left\lfloor n\left({1}/{q}-1\right)\right\rfloor,

belongs to
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and moreover
\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}|Q|^{1/\lambda}
for all cube
Q\supseteq\text{supp}(f)
.

Proof.

Let
f
be a bounded function compactly supported in a cube
Q=Q(x_{Q},\ell_{Q})
and let
Q^{\ast}=2Q
be the double cube of
Q
with sidelength
\ell_{Q^{\ast}}=2\ell_{Q}
and center

J

\displaystyle M_{\varphi}f(x)\lesssim\mathds{1}_{J\cap Q^{\ast}}(x)M_{\varphi}f(x)+\mathds{1}_{J\backslash Q^{\ast}}(x)\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|x-x_{Q}|/\ell_{Q}}\right)^{n+L+1},

where

denotes the indicator function of a subset

\displaystyle\leq|J|^{q/\lambda-1}\left(\int_{J\cap Q^{\ast}}|M_{\varphi}f(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{{{q}}/{r}}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|^{1-{q}/{r}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{r}dx\right)^{{q}/{r}}|J|^{q/\lambda-1}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|^{1-{q}/{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}|Q|^{q/r}|J|^{q/\lambda-1}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|^{1-{q}/{r}}

|J|\leq|Q|
then

\displaystyle|Q|^{q/r}|J|^{q/\lambda-1}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|^{1-{q}/{r}}=\left(\frac{|J|}{|Q|}\right)^{q/\lambda-q/r}\left(\frac{|J\cap Q^{\ast}|}{|J|}\right)^{1-q/r}|Q|^{q/\lambda}\leq|Q|^{q/\lambda},

where we choose
r>\lambda
.

Since
x\in J\backslash Q^{\ast}
satisfies
|x-x_{Q}|\geq 2\ell_{Q}
, from (
2.12
) and assuming
|J|>|Q|
we have,

\displaystyle|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\int_{J\backslash Q^{\ast}}|M_{\varphi}(f)(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{|x-x_{Q}|\geq 2\ell_{Q}}\left(1+\frac{|x-x_{Q}|}{\ell_{Q}}\right)^{-(n+L+1)q}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}\ell_{Q}^{(n+L+1)q}\int_{2\ell_{Q}}^{\infty}r^{-q(n+L+1)+n-1}dr

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}|Q|^{{q}/{\lambda}},

in view of
L\geq\left\lfloor n\left({1}/{q}-1\right)\right\rfloor
. The case
|J|\leq|Q|
we easily have the estimate

\displaystyle|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\int_{J\backslash Q^{\ast}}|M_{\varphi}(f)(x)|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}\int_{J\backslash Q^{\ast}}\left(1+\frac{|x-x_{Q}|}{\ell_{Q}}\right)^{-(n+L+1)q}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}|J\backslash Q^{\ast}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}|Q|^{q/\lambda}.

Combining the previous controls, we obtain

\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}|Q|^{1/\lambda}
. In particular,
f\in\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
∎

2.2.
Atomic decomposition in Hardy-Morrey spaces

Definition 2.3

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
0<q\leq 1
and
q\leq\lambda<\infty
. A function
a
mensurable is called a bounded
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})-

for multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

To fix the dependence on support of

for every
(q,\lambda,\infty)-

We denote the atomic space
\textbf{at}\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

(

Definition 2.4

Let

Lemma 2.6
.

If

Proof.

Let
a_{Q}
be an

is a rough
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}-

Consider

as

. Since

\displaystyle|J|^{q/\lambda-1}\int_{J}|m_{\varphi}a_{Q}(x)|^{q}dx

∎

We denote the atomic space
\textbf{at}h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

where
\{a_{Q}\}_{Q}
are bounded

\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}:=\sup_{J}\left\{\left({|J|^{q/\lambda-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subseteq J\end{subarray}}\left(|Q|^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{\lambda}}\,|s_{Q}|\right)^{q}\right)^{{1}/{q}}\right\}<\infty.

The functional

\|f\|_{\textbf{at}h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}:=\inf\left\{\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}:f=\sum_{Q}s_{Q}a_{Q}\right\}

defines a quasi-norm for the space usually called quasi-atomic norm. Now we ready to announce an atomic decomposition for localizable Hardy-Morrey space

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
0<q\leq 1
,
q<\lambda<\infty
and
f\in h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\{a_{Q}:Q\;\text{dyadic}\}

\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}

(2.17)

f=\sum_{Q\,:\,dyadic}s_{Q}a_{Q}\;\text{ in }\;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;\text{ and }\;\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}.

Conversely, for every sequence
\{a_{Q}\}_{Q}
of bounded
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}-
atoms and scalars

\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}
satisfying
\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{\lambda,q}<\infty
, then
{f=\sum_{Q}s_{Q}a_{Q}\in h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and

\left\|f\right\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}

Proof.

Let us start with the second part. Given sequence
\{a_{Q}\}_{Q}
of bounded
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}-

satisfying (

where
\{a_{1,Q}\}_{Q}
are
\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}-

\{a_{2,Q}\}_{Q}
are rough

\|\{s_{1,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}+\|\{s_{2,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}\simeq\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}.

From atomic decomposition Theorem
2.5
, we have

and

\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\leq\left\|f_{1}\right\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|\{s_{1,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}.

Now fix a dyadic cube
J
and assume that
f_{2}=\sum_{Q}s_{2,Q}a_{2,Q}
that converges as distribution in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
then

\displaystyle\Big\|m_{\varphi}{\Big(\sum_{Q}s_{2,Q}a_{2,Q}\Big)}\Big\|^{q}_{L^{q}(J)}\leq\sum_{Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}\int_{J\cap Q^{\ast}}|m_{\varphi}(a_{2,Q})|^{q}dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}\|a_{2,Q}\|^{q}_{L^{\infty}}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}|Q|^{-q/\lambda}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|,

where

, since
\ell_{Q}\geq 1
. Fixed a cube

\sum_{Q\subseteq J}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}|Q|^{-q/\lambda}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|\lesssim\sum_{Q\subseteq J}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}|Q|^{1-q/\lambda}\lesssim|J|^{1-q/\lambda}\|\{s_{2,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{{\lambda,q}}^{q}

and the second sum is bounded by

\sum_{J\subset Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}|Q|^{-q/\lambda}|J\cap Q^{\ast}|\leq|J|^{1-q/\lambda}\sum_{J\subset Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}(|J|/|Q|)^{q/\lambda}.

Fixed
J
dyadic there exists a subset

such that each cube

associated to rough atom in the decomposition given of

is uniquely determined by

for

such that

. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{J\subset Q}|s_{2,Q}|^{q}(|J|/|Q|)^{q/\lambda}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in\tilde{N}}|s_{2,Q_{k,J}}|^{q}2^{-knq/\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\in\tilde{N}}\left\{|s_{2,Q_{k,J}}|^{q}\right\}\sum_{k}2^{-knq/\lambda}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{k\in\tilde{N}}\left\{|Q_{k,J}|^{q/\lambda-1}|Q_{k,J}|^{1-q/\lambda}|s_{2,Q_{k,J}}|^{q}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{k\in\tilde{N}}\left\{|Q_{k,J}|^{q/\lambda-1}\sum_{\widetilde{Q}\,\subseteq\,Q_{k,J}}\left(|\widetilde{Q}|^{1/q-1/\lambda}|s_{2,\widetilde{Q}}|\right)^{q}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\{s_{2,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{\lambda,q}^{q}

uniformly on
J
, where

in the third inequality is taken on the family of dyadic cubes generators of

. Combining the previous controls we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{2}\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sup_{J}\left\{\left(|J|^{{q}/{\lambda}-1}\Big\|m_{\varphi}{\Big(\sum_{Q}s_{2,Q}a_{2,Q}\Big)}\Big\|^{q}_{L^{q}(J)}\right)^{1/q}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\{s_{2,Q}\}_{Q}\|_{\lambda,q}.

The conclusion follows from (
2.18
).

Let us moving on to the first part. Suppose

2.2

(2.19)

\displaystyle f-\psi\ast f=\sum_{Q\,:\,\text{dyadic}}s_{Q}a_{Q}\;\text{ in }\;\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
\{a_{Q}\}_{Q}
are
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}
-atoms and the scalars
\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}
satisfies
\left\|\{s_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\lambda,q}\lesssim\|f-\psi\ast f\|_{\mathcal{HM}^{\lambda}_{q}}\lesssim\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}
.
Now we will decompose
\psi\ast f
into rough atoms. Indeed, let us write

(2.20)

\psi\ast f=\sum_{Q}(\psi\ast f)\mathds{1}_{Q}\,:=\sum_{Q}b_{Q},

(2.21)

\psi\ast f=\sum_{Q}\tilde{s}_{Q}\tilde{b}_{Q},

where
\tilde{s}_{Q}=|Q|^{1/\lambda-1/q}\|m^{\ast}_{\psi}f\|_{L^{q}(Q)}
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\{\tilde{s}_{Q}\}_{Q}\right\|_{\lambda,q}=\sup_{J}\left\{\left({|J|^{\frac{q}{\lambda}-1}}\sum_{Q\subseteq J}\left(|Q|^{{1}/{q}-{1}/{\lambda}}\,|\tilde{s}_{Q}|\right)^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup_{J}\left\{\left(|J|^{\frac{q}{\lambda}-1}\sum_{Q\subseteq J}\int_{Q}|m_{\psi}^{\ast}f|^{q}dy\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|f\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}.

It follows from (

(2.22)

f=\sum_{Q\,:\,\text{dyadic}}s_{Q}a_{Q}+\sum_{Q\,:\,\text{dyadic}}\tilde{s}_{Q}\tilde{b}_{Q}\;\text{ in }\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

∎

Remark 2.8

According to

3.
Pointwise Fourier transform decay in
\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}

We start presenting a special function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to be considered at the next proposition.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\text{supp}(\varphi)\subset B(0,1)
and nonnegative Fourier transform such that
\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)\geq 1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
, where the Fourier transform is defined as

\widehat{\varphi}(\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\varphi(x)dx.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be a nonnegative real valued function with
\text{supp}(\eta)\subseteq B(0,1/2)
. Then
\psi:=\eta\ast\eta
is supported on
B(0,1)
and has nonnegative Fourier transform. Since
\widehat{\psi}(0)=(\int\eta(x)dx)^{2}>0
, by continuity we can choose
c_{1}>1
and
c_{2}>0
sufficiently large such that
\displaystyle\min_{|\xi|\leq 1}\{c_{2}\widehat{\psi}(\xi/c_{1})\}\geq 1
. Hence
\varphi(x):=c_{1}^{n}c_{2}\psi(c_{1}x)
has the desired properties.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
0<q\leq\lambda\leq 1
and
a_{Q}
an
(q,\lambda,\infty)
-atom, then
\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)
is continuous and satisfy

(3.1)

|\widehat{a_{Q}}(\xi)|\lesssim|\xi|^{n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right)}\|a_{Q}\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},{\quad\quad\quad\forall\,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}

with implicit constant independent of
a_{Q}
.

Proof.

Let
f=a_{Q}
be a function compactly supported on a cube

centered at
x_{0}
with sidelength

. Let

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{L^{1}}\leq|Q(x_{0},2t)|^{1-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\|\varphi_{t}\ast f\|_{\mathcal{M}^{\gamma}_{1}}\lesssim t^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)}t^{-n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}=t^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},

). Since
\widehat{\varphi}(\zeta)\geq 1
on sphere

|\widehat{f}(\xi)|\leq|\widehat{\varphi}(\xi/|\xi|)\,\widehat{f}(\xi)|=|(\varphi_{|\xi|^{-1}}\ast f)^{\wedge}(\xi)|\leq\|\varphi_{|\xi|^{-1}}\ast f\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim|\xi|^{n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},

, it follows that

is supported at

\displaystyle|\hat{f}(\xi)|\leq\|\varphi_{|\xi|^{-1}}\ast f\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim|Q|\,\|\varphi_{|\xi|^{-1}}\ast f\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ell_{Q}^{n\left(1-\frac{1}{\lambda}\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim|\xi|^{n\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}-1\right)}\|f\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}},

if provided

\displaystyle|J|^{\frac{q}{\lambda}-1}\sum_{Q\,:\,\text{dyadic}}|s_{Q}|^{q}\|M_{\varphi}a_{Q}\|^{q}_{L^{q}(J)},\leq C\|\left\{s_{Q}\right\}_{Q}\|_{\lambda,q}^{q}

cube.

(3.6)

for

where the last inequality we use (

), which yields

is continuous and

3.4
). Fixed a cube

for

. Indeed, we start writing

and
q<\lambda<\infty
. Then the operator
b(x,D)\in OpS^{0}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
maps continuously
h\mathcal{M}^{\lambda}_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into itself.

As an application of Theorem
4.1
, we may conclude that pseudodifferential operators in the Hörmander class
OpS^{m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can not be bounded in
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<q<\lambda\leq 1
and
m>0
. Indeed, consider the pseudodifferential operator
J_{m}(D)
associated to the symbol
\langle\xi\rangle^{m}\in S_{1,0}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
m>0
.
According to the proof of Proposition
3.5
, there exists
a_{\varepsilon}(x):=\varepsilon^{-n/\lambda}a_{Q}(x/\varepsilon)
an
h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}-
atom with
\|a_{\epsilon}\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\simeq\|a_{\epsilon}\|_{\mathcal{HM}_{q}^{\lambda}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim 1
for
0<\epsilon\leq 1
.
Let
g_{\epsilon}(x):=J_{m}(D)a_{\epsilon}(x)
that satisfies
\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})=\langle\xi\rangle^{m}\widehat{a}_{\varepsilon}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{n})
, then
|\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}(0,1/{4k\varepsilon})|\simeq\varepsilon^{n(1-{1}/{\lambda})-m}
. Hence, from Theorem
4.1
one has

\|J_{m}a_{\varepsilon}\|_{h\mathcal{M}_{q}^{\lambda}}\gtrsim\frac{|\widehat{g}_{\varepsilon}(0,1/{4k\varepsilon})|}{\langle(0,1/4k\varepsilon)\rangle^{n(1/\lambda-1)}}\simeq\varepsilon^{-m}\rightarrow\infty\;,

as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, thus the Theorem
4.3
can not be extended for operators in the class
OpS^{m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
0<q<\lambda\leq 1
with
m>0
. Moreover, in the same spirit of Proposition
3.7
, the previous argument can be extended to show that if
0<q_{1},q_{2}\leq 1
,
q_{1}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq 1
satisfies
\frac{q_{2}}{\lambda_{2}}=\frac{q_{1}}{\lambda_{1}}
and

0<m<n({1}/{\lambda_{1}}-{1}/{\lambda_{2}})
then there exists
\sigma(x,D)\in OpS^{-m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
which is not continuous from
h\mathcal{M}_{q_{1}}^{\lambda_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\;
to
\;h\mathcal{M}_{q_{2}}^{\lambda_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, the condition
m\geq n({1}/{\lambda_{1}}-{1}/{\lambda_{2}})
is natural. The case
m=0
was settled by
[
29
]
.

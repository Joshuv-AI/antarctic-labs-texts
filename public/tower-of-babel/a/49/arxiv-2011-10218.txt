Optimizing Approximate Leave-one-out Cross-validation to Tune Hyperparameters
Ryan Burn ryan.burn@gmail.com

Abstract
For a large class of regularized models, leave-one-out cross-validation can be efficiently estimated with an approximate leave-one-out formula (ALO). We consider the problem of adjusting hyperparameters so as to optimize ALO. We derive efficient formulas to compute the gradient and hessian of ALO and show how to apply a second-order optimizer to find hyperparameters. We demonstrate the usefulness of the proposed approach by finding hyperparameters for regularized logistic regression and ridge regression on various real-world data sets.

1
Introduction

Let
{\cal D}=\{\left(y_{1},\boldsymbol{x}_{1}\right),\cdots,\left(y_{n},\boldsymbol{x}_{n}\right)\}
denote
a data set, where
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{p}
are features and
y_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
are
responses. In many applications, we model the observations as independent and identically distributed
draws from an unknown joint distribution of the form

q\left(y_{i},\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\right)=q_{1}\left(y_{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}_{*}\right)q_{2}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}_{*}\right)
,
and we estimate
\boldsymbol{\beta}_{*}
using the optimization problem

\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}\triangleq\operatorname*{argmin}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell\left(y_{i}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)+R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)\right\},

(1)

where
\ell
is a loss function,
R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}r_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})
is a regularizer,
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
represents hyperparameters.

R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
controls the complexity of the model by penalizing larger values of
\boldsymbol{\beta}
and can
prevent overfitting. We aim to select
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
so as to minimize the out-of-sample prediction
error

\displaystyle\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}\triangleq\mathbb{E}\left[\ell\left(y_{o}\mid\boldsymbol{x}_{o}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}\right)\mid{\cal D}\right],

where
\left(y_{o},\boldsymbol{x}_{o}\right)
is an unseen sample from the distribution
q\left(y,\boldsymbol{x}\right)
.
Because
q
is unknown, we estimate
\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}
with a function

f\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mid{\cal D}\right)
and apply a hyperparameter optimization algorithm to find

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{\lambda}}\triangleq\operatorname*{argmin}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}f\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\mid{\cal D}\right).

Empirical evidence has shown leave-one-out cross-validation (LO) to be an accurate method for
estimating
\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}

(
Rad et al. 2020
)
. In general, LO can be expensive to compute,
requiring a model to be fit
n
times; however, under certain conditions, it can be efficiently
estimated using a closed-form approximate leave-one-out formula (ALO), and the approximation has
been shown to be accurate in high-dimensional settings
(
Rad and Maleki 2020
)
. In addition, for the special
case of ridge regression, ALO is exact and equivalent to Allen’s PRESS
(
Allen 1974
)
.

In this paper, we derive efficient formulas to compute the gradient and hessian of ALO, given a
smooth loss function and a smooth regularizer, and we show how to apply a trust-region algorithm
to find hyperparameters for several different classes of regularized models.

1.1
Relevant Work

Grid search is a commonly used approach for hyperparameter optimization.
Although it can work well for models with only a single hyperparameter, it
requires a search space to be specified and quickly becomes inefficient when
multiple hyperparameters are used
(
Bergstra and Bengio 2012
)
. Other scalable
gradient-based approaches have focused on using holdout sets to approximate

\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}

(
Do et al. 2008
;
Bengio 2000
)
.
Using ALO, we expect our objective function to be a more accurate
proxy for
\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}
. We further improve on previous
approaches by computing the hessian of our objective function and applying
a trust-region algorithm, allowing us to make global convergence guarantees.

1.2
Notation

We denote vectors by lowercase bold letters and matrices by uppercase bold letters. We use the notation

\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{a_{i}\right\}_{i}\right]
to denote the column vector

\left(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots\right)^{\top}
. For a function

f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote its 1
st
, 2
nd
, 3
rd
, and 4
th

derivatives by
\dot{f}
,
\ddot{f}
,
\dddot{f}
, and
\ddddot{f}
, respectively.
Finally, given a function
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote the gradient by

\nabla f
and the hessian by
\nabla^{2}f
.

2
Preliminary: Trust-region Methods

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{p}\to\mathbb{R}
denote a twice-differentiable objective function.
Trust-region methods are iterative, second-order optimization algorithms that produce a sequence

\left\{\boldsymbol{x}_{k}\right\}
, where the
k
th
iteration is generated by updating the previous iteration
with a solution to the subproblem
(
Sorensen 1982
)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{x}_{k}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}+\boldsymbol{\hat{s}}_{k}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\hat{s}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{argmin}_{\boldsymbol{s}}\left\{f\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}\right)+\nabla f\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}\right)^{\top}\boldsymbol{s}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{s}^{\top}\nabla^{2}f\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}\right)\boldsymbol{s}\right\}

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\quad\mathrm{s.t.}\quad\|\boldsymbol{s}\|\leq\delta_{k}.

The subproblem minimizes the second-order approximation of
f
at
\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1}
within the
neighborhood
\|\boldsymbol{s}\|\leq\delta_{k}
, called the trust region. Using the trust region,
we can restrict the second-order approximation to areas where it models
f
well. Efficient algorithms
exist to solve the subproblem regardless of whether
\nabla^{2}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{k-1})
is positive-definite,
making trust-region methods well-suited for non-convex optimization problems
(
Moré and Sorensen 1983
)
.
With proper rules for updating
\delta_{k}
and standard assumptions, such as Lipschitz continuity of

\nabla f
, trust-region methods are globally convergent. Moreover, if
\nabla^{2}f
is
Lipschitz continuous for all
\boldsymbol{x}
sufficiently close to a nondegenerate second-order stationary
point
\boldsymbol{x}_{*}
, where
\nabla^{2}f(\boldsymbol{x}_{*})
is positive-definite, then trust-region methods
have quadratic local convergence
(
Nocedal and Wright 1999
)
.

3
Preliminary: ALO

Put
\ell_{i}(u)\triangleq\ell(y_{i}\mid u)
. The LO estimate for
\mathrm{Err}_{\mathrm{out}}
is
defined as

\displaystyle\mathrm{LO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}_{/i}\right),

where

\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}_{/i}\triangleq\operatorname*{argmin}_{\boldsymbol{\beta}}\left\{\sum_{j\neq i}\ell_{j}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{j}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)+R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\left(\boldsymbol{\beta}\right)\right\}.

(2)

ALO works by finding
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
and then using a single step of Newton’s method to approximate

\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}_{/i}
from the gradient and hessian of Equation
2
at
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{g}_{/i}
and
\boldsymbol{H}_{/i}
denote the gradient and hessian, respectively, of
Equation
2
at
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
. The ALO approximation to

\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}_{/i}
is

\boldsymbol{\tilde{\beta}}_{/i}\triangleq\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}-\boldsymbol{H}_{/i}^{-1}\boldsymbol{g}_{/i}
,
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{ALO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}\left(\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\tilde{\beta}}_{/i}\right).

Computed naively, this formula would require solving a linear system of order
p

n
times, but
we can achieve a more efficient form by applying the matrix inversion lemma. Let
\boldsymbol{X}
represent
the feature matrix whose i
th
row is
\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
; let
\boldsymbol{H}
denote the hessian of
Equation
1
at
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
. Define

\boldsymbol{W}\triangleq\nabla^{2}R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})
and
u_{i}=\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
.
Then,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{W},

where
\boldsymbol{A}
is a diagonal matrix with
\boldsymbol{A}_{ii}=\ddot{\ell_{i}}\left(u_{i}\right)
, and

\displaystyle\mathrm{ALO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}\left(u_{i}+\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}\right),

where
h_{i}\triangleq\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}
. See
Rad and Maleki 2020
for a derivation.

4
Optimizing ALO

Put

\displaystyle\tilde{u}_{/i}\triangleq u_{i}+\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f\left(\boldsymbol{\lambda}\right)\triangleq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\ell_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i}).

We present formulas
for computing
\nabla f
and
\nabla^{2}f
. See Appendix A and Appendix B for
derivations. In general,
\nabla^{2}f
will not be positive-definite; however, we can use a
trust-region algorithm to find local minimums.

Theorem 1
.

The gradient of
\mathrm{ALO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\dot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}+\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}),

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}.

The diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{W}
have derivatives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right)_{ii}

\displaystyle=\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right)_{jj}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\ddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})+\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}},

and
\tilde{u}_{/i}
has derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}+\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}^{2}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}.

Theorem 2
.

The hessian of
\mathrm{ALO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\ddot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}+\dot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}^{2}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\\
+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}\partial h_{i}}\times\left[\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}+\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right]\\
+\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}},\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\cdot\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right\}_{i}\right]\\
-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla^{2}R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla^{2}R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\\
-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\cdot\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right\}_{j}\right],\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=2\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}.

The diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{A}
and
\boldsymbol{W}
have second derivatives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\right)_{ii}

\displaystyle=\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}+\ddddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\right)_{jj}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\frac{\partial^{2}\ddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})+\frac{\partial\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}+\frac{\partial\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\\
+\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}+\ddddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{t}},\end{multlined}

and
\tilde{u}_{/i}
has second derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{(\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)+\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\ddddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right))h_{i}^{2}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)^{2}h_{i}^{3}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}\partial h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}}.

4.1
Computational Complexity

Let
q
denote the number of hyperparameters.
How best to proceed with the computations will depend on which is greater
n
or
p
. We assume first
that
n>p
.
\boldsymbol{H}
and its Cholesky factorization
\boldsymbol{L}
can be computed in

\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}n\right)
operations. Let
\boldsymbol{h}
denote the vector of
h_{i}
values.
The complexity of computing the ALO value, gradient, and
hessian is dominated by the cost of evaluating
\boldsymbol{h}
and its derivatives. Using the formula

\displaystyle h_{i}=\|\boldsymbol{L}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\|^{2},

\boldsymbol{h}
can be computed with
\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}n\right)
operations. For the derivatives of

\boldsymbol{h}
, we first compute the matrices
\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}
, which can be done
in
\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}qn\right)
operations. The derivatives can then, also, be computed with
\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}qn\right)

operations using the formulas

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{t}_{i}={\boldsymbol{L}^{-1}}^{\top}\boldsymbol{L}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}=\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{t}_{i}.

For the second derivatives of
\boldsymbol{h}
, we, similarly, first compute the matrices

\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}
, which can be done in
\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}q^{2}n\right)

operations, and then compute

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{r}_{si}=\boldsymbol{L}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}=\boldsymbol{r}_{si}^{\top}\boldsymbol{r}_{ti}-\boldsymbol{t}_{i}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{t}_{i}

with
\mathcal{O}\left(p^{2}q^{2}n\right)
operations.

When
p>n
and
\boldsymbol{W}
is nonsingular, we can achieve better complexity by evaluating the equations in a different order.
We apply the matrix inversion lemma to

\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}=\left[\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X}+\boldsymbol{W}\right]^{-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}=\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\left[\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\right]^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}.

Computing the matrix
\boldsymbol{A}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{X}\boldsymbol{W}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}
and its Cholesky factorization
can be done in
\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2}p\right)
operations, and a product
\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}b
can be done in
\mathcal{O}\left(np\right)

operations. Applying the same approach, where we avoid explicitly computing the
p
-by-
p
matrices,
we can compute the gradient and hessian in
\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2}qp\right)
and
\mathcal{O}\left(n^{2}q^{2}p\right)
operations,
respectively. If
\boldsymbol{W}
is singular but only has
k
zero diagonal entries, where
k\ll p
,
we can combine this approach with block matrix inversion and still achieve more efficient formulas.

5
Examples

By customizing
\ell_{i}
and
R_{\lambda}
, we can adopt Theorem
1
and
Theorem
2
to a broad range of models. Putting
\ell_{i}(u)\triangleq(y_{i}-u)^{2}
gives us
regularized least squares; putting
\ell_{i}(u)\triangleq\log\left[1+\exp(-y_{i}u)\right]
gives
us regularized logistic regression. If we define

\displaystyle R_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\triangleq\lambda^{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\beta}\right\|^{2},

we have standard ridge regularization. Defining

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\triangleq\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lambda_{g_{j}}^{2}\lvert\beta_{j}\rvert^{2}

allows us to use different regularization strengths for different groups of variables, and putting

\displaystyle R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\beta})\triangleq\lambda_{1}^{2}\sum_{j=1}^{p}\lvert\beta_{j}\rvert^{1+\lambda_{2}^{2}}

gives us bridge regularization
(
Fu 1998
)
. We present versions of Theorem
1
and
Theorem
2
for the specific cases of ridge regression and logistic regression with
ridge regularization.

5.1
Ridge Regression

Because Equation
1
is a quadratic for ridge regression, the Newton approximation step in
ALO is exact,
\boldsymbol{\tilde{\beta}}_{/i}=\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}_{/i}
, and ALO and LO are equivalent. Before
stating theorems for the LO gradient and hessian, we first introduce more familiar notation. Put

\displaystyle\hat{y}_{i}\triangleq u_{i},\quad\hat{y}_{/i}\triangleq\tilde{u}_{/i},\quad\varepsilon_{i}\triangleq y_{i}-\hat{y}_{i},\quad\mbox{and }\varepsilon_{/i}\triangleq y_{i}-\hat{y}_{/i}.

\hat{y}_{i}
and
\hat{y}_{/i}
denote the
i
th
in-sample and out-of-sample prediction,
respectively; and
\varepsilon_{i}
and
\varepsilon_{/i}
denote the
i
th
in-sample and
out-of-sample prediction error, respectively. Using this notation,

\displaystyle\mathrm{LO}_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\varepsilon_{/i}^{2},

where

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{/i}=\frac{\varepsilon_{i}}{1-2h_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad h_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}.

Corollary 3
.

For ridge regression, the gradient of
\mathrm{LO}_{\lambda}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(-2\varepsilon_{/i}\times\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}\times\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}{\partial\lambda}+\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=-2\lambda\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\lambda\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},

and
\hat{y}_{/i}
has derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}=\frac{1}{1-2h_{i}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}=\frac{-2\varepsilon_{i}}{(1-2h_{i})^{2}}.

Corollary 4
.

For ridge regression, the hessian of
\mathrm{LO}_{\lambda}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[2\left(\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2}-2\varepsilon_{/i}\times\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\right],

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}+2\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\hat{y}_{i}\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\\
+\frac{\partial\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}\times\left(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2},\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=8\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-2}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}-2\boldsymbol{x}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=4\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-3}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{X}+\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I}\right)^{-2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},

and
\hat{y}_{/i}
has second derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial\hat{y}_{i}\partial h_{i}}=\frac{2}{(1-2h_{i})^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\partial^{2}\hat{y}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}=\frac{-8\varepsilon_{i}}{(1-2h_{i})^{3}}.

5.2
Ridge Regularized Logistic Regression

For ridge regularized logistic regression, we have

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{H}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\boldsymbol{A}\boldsymbol{X}+2\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{I},

\displaystyle\ell_{i}(u)

\displaystyle\triangleq\log\left[1+\exp(-y_{i}u)\right],

\displaystyle\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{-y_{i}}{1+\exp(y_{i}u)},

\displaystyle\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=qp,

\displaystyle\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=qp\left(q-p\right),\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\ddddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u\right)

\displaystyle=qp\left(q^{2}+p^{2}\right)-4q^{2}p^{2},

with
p=\left(1+\exp(-u)\right)^{-1}
and
q=1-p
.

Corollary 5
.

For ridge regularized logistic regression, the gradient of
\mathrm{ALO}_{\lambda}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\dot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}\right),

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}+\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=-4\lambda\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\mbox{ and }

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda}\boldsymbol{X}+4\lambda\boldsymbol{I}.

The diagonal matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
has derivative

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda}\right)_{ii}

\displaystyle=\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda},

and
\tilde{u}_{/i}
has derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}+\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}^{2}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}.

Corollary 6
.

For ridge regularized logistic regression, the hessian of
\mathrm{ALO}_{\lambda}
can be computed as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[\ddot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\left(\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2}+\dot{\ell}_{i}(\tilde{u}_{/i})\times\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\right],

where

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}^{2}}\times\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2}+2\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\times\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\\
+\frac{\partial\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}}\times\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}\times\left(\frac{\partial h_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2},\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\cdot\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2}\right\}_{i}\right]+32\lambda^{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-2}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}-4\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=2\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\boldsymbol{X}+4\boldsymbol{I}.

The diagonal matrix
\boldsymbol{A}
has second derivative

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}\right)_{ii}

\displaystyle=\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial^{2}u_{i}}{\partial\lambda^{2}}+\ddddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda}\right)^{2},

and
\tilde{u}_{/i}
has second derivatives

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{(\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)+\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\ddddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right))h_{i}^{2}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)^{2}h_{i}^{3}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial u_{i}\partial h_{i}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{2}}+\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i}}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}},\mbox{ and}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\tilde{u}_{/i}}{\partial h_{i}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)}{(1-\ddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)h_{i})^{3}}.

7
Conclusion

In this paper, we demonstrated how to select hyperparameters by computing the
gradient and hessian of ALO and applying a second-order optimizer to find a
local minimum. The approach is applicable to a large class of commonly used
models, including regularized logistic regression and ridge regression. We
applied ALO optimization to fit regularized models to various real-world data
sets. We found that when using a single-parameter regularizer, we were able to
find hyperparameters with better LO values than standard grid search approaches
and frequently were able to do so in less time. ALO optimization, furthermore,
scales to handle multiple hyperparameters, and we demonstrated how it could be
used to fit hyperparameters for bridge regularization.

Appendix A. Proof of Theorem
1

To derive the derivative of
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
, we observe that at the optimum of Equation
1

the gradient is zero:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{x}_{i}+\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})=0.

Differentiating both sides of the equation gives us

\displaystyle\phantom{\Leftrightarrow}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\boldsymbol{x}_{i}+\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\right)=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\boldsymbol{H}\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right)+\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})=0

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}=-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}).

For the derivative of
h_{i}
, we apply this formula for differentiating an inverse matrix:

\displaystyle\frac{d\boldsymbol{E}^{-1}}{dt}=-\boldsymbol{E}^{-1}\frac{d\boldsymbol{E}}{dt}\boldsymbol{E}^{-1}.

The other derivatives are derived as straightforward differentiations of their associated value
formulas.

Appendix B. Proof of Theorem
2

For the second derivative of
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left[-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\right]

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\right)

\displaystyle=-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}-\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\right).

Now,

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right)\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\left[\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right]\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\cdot\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{\dddot{\ell}_{i}\left(u_{i}\right)\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right\}_{i}\right]+\frac{\partial\nabla^{2}R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\\
+\mathrm{vec}\left[\left\{\dddot{r}_{j,\boldsymbol{\lambda}}(\hat{\beta}_{j})\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\times\frac{\partial\hat{\beta}_{j}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\right\}_{j}\right]\quad\mbox{and}\end{multlined}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left(\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial\nabla^{2}R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}})\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}+\frac{\partial\nabla R_{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}(\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}).

Combining the equations, the second derivative of
\boldsymbol{\hat{\beta}}
becomes

For the second derivative of
h_{i}
, we derive

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{2}h_{i}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left(-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=\!\begin{multlined}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}+\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}\\

\displaystyle=2\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i}-\boldsymbol{x}_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\boldsymbol{H}}{\partial\lambda_{s}\partial\lambda_{t}}\boldsymbol{H}^{-1}\boldsymbol{x}_{i},

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda_{t}}\left(\boldsymbol{X}^{\top}\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{A}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\boldsymbol{X}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{W}}{\partial\lambda_{s}}\right)

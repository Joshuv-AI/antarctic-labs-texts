Fano schemes of symmetric matrices of bounded rank
Ahmad Mokhtar

Abstract
. We study the geometry of the Fano schemes F k ​ ( SD n r ) \mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}) of the projective variety SD n r \mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r} defined by the r × r r\times r minors of a symmetric n × n n\times n matrix filled with indeterminates. These schemes are fine moduli spaces parameterizing ( k + 1 ) (k+1) -dimensional linear spaces of symmetric matrices of rank less than r r . We prove that the schemes F k ​ ( SD n r ) \mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}) can have generically non-reduced components, characterize their irreducibility and connectedness, and give results on their smoothness. Our approach to connectedness also applies to Fano schemes of rectangular matrices as well as alternating matrices and answers a question of Ilten and Chan. Furthermore, we give a complete description of F 1 ​ ( SD n r ) \mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}) and show that when r = n r=n , the Fano schemes of lines have the expected dimension. As an application, we provide geometric arguments for several previous results concerning spaces of symmetric matrices of bounded rank.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic not equal to 2.
For an integer
n\geq 3
, consider the
\mathbb{K}
-vector space
S_{n}
of symmetric
n\times n
matrices with entries in
\mathbb{K}
.
Fixing an integer
3\leq r\leq n
, we are interested in studying those subspaces
Q\subset S_{n}
such that every matrix in
Q
has rank less than
r
.

Denote by
\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}
the closed subscheme of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\binom{n+1}{2}-1}
cut out by the
r\times r
minors of the generic symmetric matrix

(1.1)

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1,1}&x_{1,2}&\cdots&x_{1,n}\\
x_{1,2}&x_{2,2}&&x_{2,n}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{1,n}&x_{2,n}&\cdots&x_{n,n}\end{pmatrix},

filled with
\binom{n+1}{2}
independent forms
x_{i,j}
.
The variety
\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}
is irreducible and has dimension
n(r-1)-\binom{r-1}{2}-1
(see
[
Kut74
, Theorem 1, Proposition 6.1]
).
In this article we study the
Fano scheme

\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
that parameterizes those
k
-planes in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{\binom{n+1}{2}-1}
that lie on
\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}
.
This is a subscheme of the Grassmannian
\Gr\left(k+1,\binom{n+1}{2}\right)
whose closed points correspond to
(k+1)
-dimensional linear subspaces
Q\subset S_{n}
containing matrices of rank less than
r
.
See
[
EH16
, Chapter 6]
for details on Fano schemes.

Our original motivation for studying the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
was to generalize the work of Ilten and Chan
[
CI15
]
on Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
of rectangular matrices of bounded rank to the symmetric setting.
Their systematic use of the dimensions of tangent spaces to
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
at select points characterizes the irreducibility and smoothness of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
, and gives results on the components and connectedness of these schemes.
Their techniques will prove helpful in our study of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
, although we will encounter new challenges in the symmetric case.
The difficulty is due to the existence of generically non-reduced components which prevents us from leveraging the full power of tangent space dimensions.

The next reason to study
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is to provide geometric proofs for several previous results concerning subspaces of
S_{n}
of bounded rank.
One motivation for these results is a classical theorem due to Kronecker and Weierstrass that gives a canonical form for a pencil of singular matrices (see
[
Gan59
, Chapter XII]
).
Even though the classification of subspaces of
S_{n}
of bounded rank in linear dimension at least 3 is open, there are partial results.
One is Meshulam’s theorem
[
Mes89
, Theorem 1]
giving the maximum dimension of a subspace of symmetric (and alternating) matrices with rank less than
r
.
In Proposition
2.20
we give a short proof of this theorem using
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Actually, our method treats subspaces of symmetric, alternating and rectangular matrices of bounded rank simultaneously and gives a unified geometric proof of Meshulam’s theorem for symmetric and alternating matrices and of Flanders’ theorem
[
Fla62
, Theorem 1]
for rectangular matrices.

Another result we will reprove (in Corollary
4.5
) is Loewy and Radwan’s theorem
[
LR94
, Theorem 6.1]
that classifies subspaces of
S_{n}
of bounded rank having the maximum dimension in Meshulam’s result.
Loewy and Radwan’s proof, although lengthy, uses elementary methods in linear algebra and graph theory and has the advantage that it imposes few restrictions on the ground field
\mathbb{K}
.
We will give a shorter geometric proof for an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{K}
by studying the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.

Our third motivation concerns the classification of trilinear forms, i.e. elements
\omega\in\mathbb{K}^{k+1}\otimes\mathbb{K}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{K}^{n}
up to the action of
\mathrm{GL}(k+1)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)\times\mathrm{GL}(n)
.
Classifying the orbits in general is an open problem but particular cases have been studied (see e.g.
[
Par01
]
and
[
Ng95
]
).
A closely related problem is the classification of elements in
\mathbb{K}^{k+1}\otimes\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
, or equivalently
\Hom\left((\mathbb{K}^{k+1})^{*},\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})\right)
, up to
\mathrm{GL}(k+1)\times\mathrm{GL}(n)
.
With respect to the standard bases, an element
\omega:(\mathbb{K}^{k+1})^{*}\to\Sym^{2}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
is an
n\times n
matrix filled with linear forms in the coordinates of
(\mathbb{K}^{k+1})^{*}
.
We may assume, after possibly quotienting by the kernel, that
\omega
is injective.
Up to
\mathrm{GL}(k+1)
,
\omega
then corresponds to the pair
(k+1,Q)
, where
Q
is the image of
\omega
, a
(k+1)
-dimensional subspace of
n\times n
symmetric matrices.
The Fano scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is the moduli space of those
Q
having elements with rank less than
r
.

The study of linear spaces of singular symmetric matrices is also related to the study of quadrics.
A subspace
Q\subset S_{n}
gives a linear family of quadrics.
The linear family
Q
is called singular if every quadric in
Q
is given by a singular matrix, otherwise
Q
is non-singular.
Pencils of quadrics have been extensively studied.
A theorem due to Weierstrass and Segre classifies the
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-orbits of non-singular pencils of quadrics via a combinatorial object called the Segre symbol (see
[
FMS21
, Theorem 1.1]
).
In
[
BM22
]
the author studies singular pencils of quadrics of constant rank.
Orbits of nets of conics have been classified in
[
AEI23
]
.
Our Fano schemes parameterize linear spaces (not necessarily pencils) of quadrics of bounded rank.
We hope that this study leads to a better understanding of these linear spaces.

Finally, this study provides a family of examples for general Hilbert schemes.
For a projective scheme
Y\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
, the general Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{f}(Y)
is the fine moduli space parameterizing those closed subschemes of
Y
that have a fixed Hilbert polynomial
f
.
When
Y=\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{n}
, we get the classical Hilbert scheme
\mathrm{Hilb}_{f}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{n})
which is known to be connected
[
Har66
]
.
But for general Hilbert schemes, connectedness is not guaranteed.
The Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(Y)
are particular examples of general Hilbert schemes.
In
[
CI15
]
, the authors show that the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
that parameterize
k
-planes of the space of
m\times n
matrices of rank less than
r
is not always connected.
We will see that the Fano scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is also not always connected (see Corollary
1.5
) and we will even encounter generically non-reduced components (see Proposition
1.9
).
Our investigation will provide a family of examples of general Hilbert schemes that arise naturally but exhibit such geometric pathologies.

1.2.
Main results

The general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(n)
over the field
\mathbb{K}
acts on the space of symmetric matrices
S_{n}
through conjugation
(B,M)\mapsto B\cdot M=BMB^{t}
, for every
B\in\mathrm{GL}(n)
and
M\in S_{n}
.
Here
B^{t}
denotes the transpose of
B
.
This action naturally induces an action of
\mathrm{GL}(n)
on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.

An example of a subspace of
S_{n}
whose every matrix has rank less than
r
is a
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-translate of a subspace of matrices with
1+n-r
zero rows.
This is a particular example of what is called a compression space, first introduced in
[
EH88
]
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
V,W
be
\mathbb{K}
-vector spaces of dimensions
n
and
m
respectively and fix
r\leq m,n
.
For an integer
0\leq s\leq r-1
, a linear subspace
Q\subset\Hom(V,W)
is an
s
-compression space if every element of
Q
maps a fixed
(s+1+n-r)
-dimensional subspace of
V
into a fixed
s
-dimensional subspace of
W
.

For an
s
-compression space
Q
, every element in
Q
has rank less than
r
since every
r
-dimensional subspace of
V
intersects the fixed
(s+1+n-r)
-dimensional subspace in dimension at least
s+1
and thus every
r
-dimensional subspace of
V
meets the kernel of every element in
Q
non-trivially.
After a suitable choice of bases for
V
and
W
, an
s
-compression space, considered as a subspace of all
m\times n
matrices, is of the block form

\begin{pNiceArray}.

A
symmetric
(or
alternating
) compression space is a compression space consisting of symmetric (or alternating) matrices.
We denote the maximum dimension of an
s
-compression space by
\kappa(s)
(see Definition

).

Our first result is a complete characterization of the connectedness of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Our approach also treats the connectedness of Fano schemes of rectangular matrices of bounded rank
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
, and Fano schemes of alternating matrices of bounded rank
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
(see §
2.1
for the exact definitions of
D_{m,n}^{r}
and
\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r}
).

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
X
be one of the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
where
r\leq m\leq n
and
k
are integers such that
X
is non-empty.
The compression spaces form a closed subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}\subset X
and the natural map
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{\textbf{C}})\rightarrow\pi_{0}\left(X\right)
on the sets of connected components of
C
and
X
is bijective.
In particular,
X
is connected if and only if
C
is.

We will prove this in §
3
and there we will combinatorially describe the connectedness of the compression subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}\subset X
and obtain the following characterization of the connectedness of
X
.

Corollary 1.5
.

Let
X
be as in Theorem
1.4
.
Define

\mathbf{V}=\left\{s\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,0\leq s\leq s_{\max},\,\mathrm{and}\,k\leq\kappa(s)\right\},

where

s_{\max}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor&\text{if }X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\,\text{ or }\,\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r}),\\
r-1&\text{if }X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r}).\end{cases}

Assume
s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{\ell}
are the distinct elements of
\mathbf{V}
in ascending order.
Then
X
is disconnected if and only if

(1)

\ell\geq 2
, and

(2)

At least two terms of the sequence

g(\{s_{1},s_{2}\}),g(\{s_{2},s_{3}\}),\ldots,g(\{s_{\ell-1},s_{\ell}\}),g(\{s_{1},s_{\ell}\})

are less than
k
, where
g(\{s,s^{\prime}\})=\kappa(s^{\prime})-(s+1+n-r)(s^{\prime}-s)
for
s<s^{\prime}
.

Corollary
1.5
completes the partial characterization of the connectedness of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
by Ilten and Chan
[
CI15
, Theorem 5.3]
.
In loc. sit. the authors ask whether there exist
m,n,r
such that
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
is connected but its compression subscheme is disconnected
[
CI15
, Question 8.2]
.
Theorem
1.4
answers this question in the negative.

The next result (proved in §
5
) characterizes the irreducibility of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
We note that by Meshulam’s theorem
[
Mes89
, Theorem 1]
, the scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is non-empty if and only if

k\leq\max\left\{\kappa(0),\kappa(s_{\max})\right\},

where
s_{\max}=\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor
.
We will give a short proof of this fact in §
2.3
.

Theorem 1.6
.

The Fano scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is irreducible if and only if

\max\left\{\kappa(1),\kappa(s_{\max})\right\}<k\leq\kappa(0),

or

\max\left\{\kappa(0),\kappa(s_{\max}-1)\right\}<k\leq\kappa(s_{\max}).

In the particular case of the Fano scheme of lines
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
we can describe all the irreducible components.

We will prove the following result in §

.

Theorem 1.8
.

The subschemes
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
for

are distinct, and are exactly the irreducible components of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Each component

and their intersection is non-empty.
In particular when

, the components are equidimensional and of the expected dimension.

It is known that if the Fano scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(Y)
of a general hypersurface

of degree

is non-empty, then it has the
expected dimension

(k+1)(N-k)-\binom{k+d}{k}
.
When the Fano scheme of a hypersurface, e.g.
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{n})
, has the expected dimension, it is given by the vanishing of a section of a certain vector bundle on the Grassmannian.
In particular, its class in the Chow ring of the Grassmannian can be determined using tools from intersection theory (see
[

, Proposition 6.4]
).

For higher values of

, a full characterization of the irreducible components of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is difficult since non-compression components appear.
Although we suspect that
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
forms an irreducible component for every

, we are able to prove this only when

,

, or when

(see Corollary

and Proposition

).
One obstacle in proving
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)

Proposition 1.9
.

When the closed subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is an irreducible component (e.g. when
k=1
or
k=\kappa(s)
or
s=\frac{r-1}{2}
), it is generically reduced if and only if
s=\frac{r-1}{2}
.

We will prove this in Section
6
.
In particular, all components of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
(except one component when
r
is odd) are generically non-reduced.
Obviously the scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is not smooth when it has a generically non-reduced component.
We will show in Proposition

that when
r

, the scheme has smooth points.
Based on the above corollary and the characterization of connectedness, we formulate a conjecture on the smoothness of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
(see Conjecture
7.2
).

1.3.
Related work and organization

The study of linear spaces of matrices with bounded rank has attracted attention in the last sixty years.
Dieudonné
[
Die49
, Theorem 1]
showed that a subspace of singular
n\times n
matrices has dimension at most
n(n-1)
and those with this maximum dimension must be
0
- or
(n-1)
-compression spaces.
Flanders generalized this result to rectangular matrices of bounded rank and Atkinson, Lloyd, Beasley and de Seguins Pazzis
[
AL80
,
Bea87
,
Seg16
]
improved Flanders’ classification to dimensions near the maximal one.

For symmetric matrices, Meshulam
[
Mes89
, Theorem 1]
proved the equivalent of Flanders’ result and determined the maximum dimension of a subspace of
S_{n}
of bounded rank.
Loewy and Radwan
[
LR94
, Theorem 6.1]
showed subspaces having the maximum dimension are compression spaces and de Seguins Pazzis
[
Seg16a
, Theorem 1.3]
generalized this classification to subspaces near the maximal dimension.
For a study of subspaces of
S_{n}
of
fixed
rank, see e.g.
[
IL99
]
.

Fano schemes have been studied in numerous contexts.
Fano
[
Fan04
]
studied the family of lines on a cubic hypersurface having a finite number of singularities.
Altman and Kleiman
[
AK77
]
gave a modern treatment of these moduli spaces.
There have also been extensive studies on Fano schemes of hypersurfaces
[
BV79
,
Lan97
]
and complete intersections
[
DM98
,
IK22
]
.

In
[
CI15
]
, the authors study the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
and
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(P_{m,n}^{r})
where
D_{m,n}^{r}
and
P_{m,n}^{r}
are subschemes of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{K}}^{mn-1}
given by the vanishing of the
r\times r
determinants, respectively permanents, of the generic
m\times n
matrix.
They give results on irreducibility, smoothness, connectedness and components of these schemes.

This paper is organized as follows.
In §
2
we review preliminary results on compression spaces, ending it with a short proof of Flanders’ and Meshulam’s theorems.
In §
3
we characterize the connectedness of the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
, and
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
and prove Theorem
1.4
and Corollary
1.5
.
In §
4
we will prove results on irreducible components of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
and give a geometric proof for Loewy and Radwan’s theorem.
In §
5
we will prove Theorem
1.8
on the Fano scheme of lines and characterize the irreducibility of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
by proving Theorem
1.6
.
In §
6
we study the smoothness of the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
via tangent space dimensions, discuss the existence of generically non-reduced components, and prove Proposition
1.9
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
The varieties
\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}
,
\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r}
and
D_{m,n}^{r}

The main focus of this paper is the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
of symmetric matrices of bounded rank.
However, in §
3
, we will treat the connectedness of these schemes together with that of Fano schemes of alternating matrices
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and rectangular matrices
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
of bounded rank.
In this section we define
\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r},D_{m,n}^{r}
and related terminology.
When considering alternating or rectangular matrices,
\mathbb{K}
can be of any characteristic (even 2).

An
alternating
matrix is a skew-symmetric matrix with zeros on the diagonal.
Denote by
A_{n}
the vector space of
n\times n
alternating matrices over
\mathbb{K}
.
For integers
2<r\leq n
, where
r
is even, we define
\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r}
to be the subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{\binom{n}{2}-1}
defined by the vanishing of the
r\times r
Pfaffians of the generic alternating matrix

(2.1)

\begin{pmatrix}0&x_{1,2}&\cdots&x_{1,n}\\
-x_{1,2}&0&\cdots&x_{2,n}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
-x_{1,n}&-x_{2,n}&\cdots&0\end{pmatrix}.

The group
\mathrm{GL}(n)
acts on
A_{n}
by
BMB^{t}
for every
B\in\mathrm{GL}(n)
and
M\in A_{n}
.
The matrix
BMB^{t}
is obtained by applying to
M
a sequence of
row-column
operations.
A row-column operation is an elementary row operation followed by the same operation on the columns of the matrix.
This action induces an action on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
.

Remark 2.2
.

For an even integer
r
, the
r\times r
Pfaffians of (

) generate a prime ideal (see
[
KL80
, Theorem 12]
) that cuts out the locus of alternating matrices of rank at most
r-2
(see
[
BE77
, Corollary 2.6]
).
We choose Pfaffians over minors because the ideal generated by minors of size
r
(or
r-1
) is not prime.

Next, fix integers
2\leq r\leq m\leq n
.
Let
M_{m,n}
be the vector space of all
m\times n
matrices over
\mathbb{K}
and define

to be the subscheme of
\mathbb{P}^{mn-1}
given by the vanishing of the
r\times r
minors of the generic
m\times n

(2.3)

\begin{pmatrix}x_{1,1}&x_{1,2}&\cdots&x_{1,n}\\
x_{2,1}&x_{2,2}&\cdots&x_{2,n}\\
\vdots&&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{m,1}&x_{m,2}&\cdots&x_{m,n}\end{pmatrix}.

The group
\mathrm{GL}(m)\times\mathrm{GL}(n)
acts on
M_{m,n}
, and hence on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
, via
B_{1}MB_{2}^{t}
for every
(B_{1},B_{2})\in\mathrm{GL}(m)\times\mathrm{GL}(n)
and

.

A linear space of matrices

can be represented non-uniquely as a matrix with linear forms as entries by picking a basis in

(see Example

).
We will freely switch between the subspace and the matrix perspective.
For example the expressions

and

are both valid, the latter expressing that we are acting on a chosen matrix that represents

.
The expression

always means

.

Next fix
2\leq r\leq m\leq n
and let

.
The

-compression space is the subspace of

of the block form (

Define

to be the projective dimension of the standard

(2.5)

\kappa(s,r)=\begin{cases}s(s+1+n-r)+\binom{r-s}{2}-1&\text{for symmetric matrices},\\

Remark 2.6
.

For
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})

u^{t}u
for a row vector
u\in\mathbb{K}^{n}

2.2.
Bilinear forms

An
n\times n
matrix
A
over
\mathbb{K}
defines a bilinear form
\beta_{A}:\mathbb{K}^{n}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}
via
\beta_{A}(u,w)=uAw^{t}
for row vectors
u,w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
A change of basis on
\mathbb{K}^{n}
corresponds to changing the matrix
A
to
BAB^{t}
for some
B\in\mathrm{GL}(n)
.
We say
u,w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
are orthogonal with respect to
A
and write
u\perp_{A}w
if
\beta_{A}(u,w)=0
.
For a set
Q
of matrices,
u\perp_{Q}w
means
u\perp_{A}w
for every
A\in Q
.
Similarly, for
U,W\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
, by
U\perp_{Q}W
we mean
u\perp_{Q}w
for every
u\in U,w\in W
.
The next proposition characterizes compression spaces in terms of bilinear forms.

Proposition 2.7
.

A linear space of
n\times n
matrices
Q
over
\mathbb{K}
is an
s
-compression space if and only if there exist subspaces
U,W\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
of dimensions
s+1+n-r
and
n-s
respectively, such that
U\perp_{Q}W
.

Proof.

A matrix
A\in Q
defines a map
L_{A}:\mathbb{K}^{n}\to(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{*}
by
v\mapsto(x\mapsto\beta_{A}(x,v))
for every
v\in\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
The matrix of
L_{A}
with respect to the standard basis on
\mathbb{K}^{n}
and the dual basis on
(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{*}
is
A
.
We also have
u\perp_{A}w
if and only if
L_{A}(u)
vanishes on
w
.

Suppose first that
Q
is an
s
-compression space.
By definition, there exist subspaces
U\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
and
U^{\prime}\subset(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{*}
of dimensions
s+1+n-r
and
s
, respectively such that
L_{A}(U)\subset U^{\prime}
for every
A\in Q
.
Let
W=\mathrm{Ann}\,(U^{\prime})=\{w\in\mathbb{K}^{n}:f(w)=0,\,\text{for every}\,f\in U^{\prime}\}
.
Then
\dim W=n-s
and
U\perp_{Q}W
.
Conversely, suppose
U
and
W
have dimensions
s+1+n-r
and
n-s
and
U\perp_{Q}W
.
Take
U^{\prime}=\mathrm{Ann}\,(W)
, the subspace of
(\mathbb{K}^{n})^{*}
vanishing on
W
.
Then
L_{A}(U)\subset U^{\prime}
for every
A\in Q
.
Because
\dim U^{\prime}=s
, we deduce that
Q
is an
s
-compression space.
∎

An immediate corollary is that when we consider
s
-compression spaces of symmetric or alternating matrices, we may assume
0\leq s\leq\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor
.

Corollary 2.8
.

Every
s
-compression space
Q\subset S_{n}
or
A_{n}
is also an
(r-s-1)
-compression space.

Proof.

This follows from Proposition
2.7
by changing the roles of
U
and
W
.
∎

We now describe compression spaces of

and

Corollary 2.9
.

Let

Then every symmetric or alternating

-compression space is a subspace of a

-translate of the standard

Proof.

Let

or

By Proposition

Define

Note that

Since

Choose a basis

Then extend

With respect to a suitable ordering of the elements in

has zero blocks similar to the standard

).
∎

We distinguish the case where the orthogonal subspaces in Proposition

Definition 2.10

Let

or

be a linear subspace and let

and

Example 2.11
.

Nested

-compression spaces are exactly subspaces of

-translates of the standard

-compression space.
On the other hand, by Corollary

, an

but

The subspace of

-compression space.
Because if it were a nested

Remark 2.12
.

Being a (nested)

-compression space is a closed property.

2.3.
Fixed points

We will use the
Borel subgroup

G_{B}(n)\subset\mathrm{GL}(n)
consisting of all invertible upper triangular matrices and the unipotent subgroup
G_{U}(n)
consisting of those matrices in
G_{B}(n)
with ones on the diagonal.
We say a point of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
is a
Borel fixed point
, respectively a
unipotent fixed point
, if it is fixed under the action of
G_{B}(n)
, respectively
G_{U}(n)
.
For the action of
\mathrm{GL}(n)\times\mathrm{GL}(m)
on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
, we define the Borel subgroup and the unipotent subgroup to be
G_{B}(n)\times G_{B}(m)
and
G_{U}(n)\times G_{U}(m)
, respectively.
This section gives several properties of the unipotent fixed points of the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}),\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
and fully characterizes the Borel fixed points.

The utility of fixed points of
G_{B}(n)
is due to Borel’s fixed point theorem
[
Bor91
, Theorem 10.4]
which states that the action of
G_{B}(n)
on a non-empty complete variety (e.g. every
G_{B}(n)
-stable closed subscheme of our Fano schemes) has a fixed point.
On the other hand, Horrocks’s theorem
[
Hor69
, Theorem 6.2]
states in our setting that the set of connected components of the unipotent fixed locus of the scheme is in bijection with the set of connected components of the scheme.

1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq n\end{subarray}}a_{i,\ell}q_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}a^{\prime}_{j,\ell^{\prime}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\leq\ell\leq m\\
j\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq n\end{subarray}}a_{i,\ell}q_{\ell,\ell^{\prime}}a^{\prime}_{j,\ell^{\prime}}=0,

where the first equality is due to
A,A^{\prime}
being upper triangular and the second equality is due to condition
2
.

Since non-zero entries of
Q
vary freely,
Q
has
k+1
non-zero entries.
This means the number of non-zero entries of
AQ(A^{\prime})^{t}
is at most
k+1
.
But
AQ(A^{\prime})^{t}
is also a subspace of
\mathcal{V}
of dimension
k+1
.
Therefore, the number of non-zero entries of
AQ(A^{\prime})^{t}
is exactly
k+1
and they are linearly independent and are positioned exactly the same as non-zero entries of
Q
.
Thus
Q
and
AQ(A^{\prime})^{t}
are equal as subspaces of
\mathcal{V}
.
The proof for
\mathcal{V}=S_{n}
or
A_{n}
is similar except that we take
A^{\prime}=A
and consider only non-zero entries on and above the diagonals of
Q
and
AQA^{t}
.

Conversely, assume
Q
is fixed under the action of the Borel subgroup.
Since every Borel fixed point is a unipotent fixed point, by Lemma
2.15
part
(i)
, the matrix
Q
satisfies condition
2
.
To show
Q
satisfies
1
, it is enough to prove if
q_{i,j}\neq 0
for some
i,j
, then the subspace
Q
contains
e_{\{i,j\}}
,
e_{\langle i,j\rangle}
or
e_{(i,j)}
depending on whether
\mathcal{V}=S_{n}
,
A_{n}
or
M_{m,n}
respectively.

Assume
\mathcal{V}=S_{n}
or
A_{n}
.
First we consider the case
i\neq j
.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
and
1\leq\ell\leq n
, define
E_{n,\ell,\alpha}
to be the diagonal
n\times n
matrix with entry
(\ell,\ell)
equal to
\alpha
and ones elsewhere on the diagonal.
When
\mathcal{V}=S_{n}
, for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{K}^{*}
we act on the matrix
Q
to get the matrix

(\beta-1)(\alpha-1)q_{i,j}e_{\{i,j\}}=E_{n,j,\beta}\cdot\left(E_{n,i,\alpha}\cdot Q-Q\right)-\left(E_{n,i,\alpha}\cdot Q-Q\right),

representing a subspace of
Q
, which gives
e_{\{i,j\}}\in Q
(in the above, the dot refers to the
\mathrm{GL}(n)
action on the chosen matrix of
Q
).
When
\mathcal{V}=A_{n}
, a similar equation with
e_{\{i,j\}}
replaced by
e_{\langle i,j\rangle}
holds showing
e_{\langle i,j\rangle}\in Q
.

If
i=j
, then necessarily
\mathcal{V}=S_{n}
and the matrix
E_{n,i,\alpha}\cdot Q-Q
represents a subspace of
Q
and has nonzero entries only in the
i
-th row (and column) and in particular the
(i,i)
entry equals
(\alpha^{2}-1)q_{i,i}
.
Using the proof of the claim for entries not on the diagonal, we can zero out all the entries in the
i
-th row (and column) except the entry on the diagonal.
This shows
e_{\{i,i\}}\in Q
.

Finally, for
\mathcal{V}=M_{m,n}
we use a similar computation on the matrix
Q
to get

(\beta-1)(\alpha-1)q_{i,j}e_{(i,j)}=(I_{m},E_{n,j,\beta})\cdot\left((E_{m,i,\alpha},I_{n})\cdot Q-Q\right)-\left((E_{m,i,\alpha},I_{n})\cdot Q-Q\right),

representing a subspace of
Q
, hence
e_{(i,j)}\in Q
.
∎

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
X
be one of the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
.
A Borel fixed point
[Q]
of the Grassmannian lies on
X
if and only if for some
s
,
Q
is a subspace of the form (
1.3
) when

and of the form (
1.7
) when
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
.
In particular, Borel fixed points of

Proof.

If for some

,

is a subspace of the standard

-compression space, then clearly
[Q]\in X
.
Conversely, if

is a Borel fixed point of

∎

Proposition 2.20
.

Let
X
be one of the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
.

k\leq\max\left\{\kappa(0),\kappa\left(s_{\max}\right)\right\},

where

is as in (

s_{\max}=\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor
when

\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and

when

k

[

and since

will satisfy the inequality of the statement.
∎

3.
Connectedness

In this section we characterize when the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
are connected.
First we prove Theorem
1.4
by using the unipotent fixed points.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

We saw in Remark
2.12
that
C
is closed.
Let
Y
be the closed subscheme of
X
consisting of unipotent fixed points.
By Proposition
2.16
, we have the inclusions
Y\rightarrow\mathrm{\textbf{C}}\rightarrow X
, inducing the maps

(3.1)

\pi_{0}(Y)\rightarrow\pi_{0}(\mathrm{\textbf{C}})\rightarrow\pi_{0}\left(X\right).

The closed subscheme
C
is Borel-stable.
By Borel’s fixed point theorem
[
Bor91
, Theorem 10.4]
, every connected component of
C
contains a Borel fixed point which is unipotent fixed.
The first map in (
3.1
) is thus surjective.
By Horrocks’ theorem
[
Hor69
, Theorem 6.2]
, the set of connected components of the unipotent fixed points is in bijection with the set of connected components of the scheme, i.e. the composition in (
3.1
) is bijective and therefore the second map is bijective.
∎

Our goal is to obtain a combinatorial description of connectedness for the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
and prove Corollary
1.5
.
We will associate each of these schemes with a graph whose set of connected components is in bijection with the set of connected components of the scheme.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
X
be one of the schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
where
r\leq m\leq n
and
k
are integers and set

s_{\max}=\begin{cases}\left\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\right\rfloor&\text{if }X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\,\text{ or }\,\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r}),\\
r-1&\text{if }X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r}).\end{cases}

Define
\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{X}=(\mathbf{V},\mathbf{E})
to be the complete simple graph on the vertex set
\mathbf{V}=\{s\in\mathbb{Z}:0\leq s\leq s_{\max}\}
equipped with a function
g:\mathbf{V}\cup\mathbf{E}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
that labels the vertices and edges of
\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{X}
as follows.
For a vertex
s\in\mathbf{V}
we define
g(s)=\kappa(s)
and for the edge
\{s,s^{\prime}\}\in\mathbf{E}
with
s<s^{\prime}
we set

(3.3)

g(\{s,s^{\prime}\})=\kappa(s^{\prime})-(s+1+n-r)(s^{\prime}-s).

We define the graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
associated to
X
to be the subgraph of
\mathcal{\tilde{G}}_{X}
obtained by deleting those vertices and edges whose labels are less than
k
.

Remark 3.5
.

When
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
, in Definition

, the label of the vertex

X

s

Similarly, by the next lemma, the label for the edge
\{s,s^{\prime}\}
denotes the highest integer
k
such that
X
contains a Borel fixed point that is both a nested
s
- and a nested
s^{\prime}
-compression space or equivalently
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
intersects
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
.
A similar remark holds when

substituted by

.

Lemma 3.6
.

If
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
and
[Q]\in X
is a Borel fixed point that is a nested
s
-compression space, then
Q
is a subspace of the standard
s
-compression space.
The statement also holds for
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
when
[Q]
is a Borel fixed
s
-compression space.

Proof.

Let
X
be
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
.
By assumption there are subspaces
U\subset W\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
of dimensions
s+1+n-r
and
n-s
with
U\perp_{Q}W
.
Moreover, by Proposition
2.17
, we can write
Q
as a matrix in linear forms such that the
(i,j)
entry is either zero, or a free variable, say
z_{i,j}
.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{U}
be a
(s+1+n-r)\times n
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{K}
whose rows form a basis for
U
with respect to the standard basis of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
Define a similar
(n-s)\times n
matrix
\mathcal{B}_{W}
for
W
.
We may assume that
\mathcal{B}_{U}
and
\mathcal{B}_{W}
are in row-echelon form.
Let
I_{U}\subset\{1,\ldots,n\}
be the indices of columns containing leading 1’s in
\mathcal{B}_{U}
and similarly define
I_{W}
for
\mathcal{B}_{W}
.
The sets
I_{U}
and
I_{W}
have sizes
s+1+n-r
and
n-s
respectively.
Assume the elements of
I_{U}
and
I_{W}
in ascending order are

I_{U}=\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{s+1+n-r}\},\qquad I_{W}=\{i^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,i^{\prime}_{n-s}\}.

We claim the
(i,i^{\prime})
entry in
Q
is zero for every
i\in I_{U}
and
i^{\prime}\in I_{W}
.
By construction, there are elements
u=e_{i}+\sum_{j>i}\alpha_{j}e_{j}\in U
and
w=e_{i^{\prime}}+\sum_{j>i^{\prime}}\beta_{j}e_{j}\in W
for suitable scalars
\alpha_{j},\beta_{j}
.
If the
(i,i^{\prime})
entry in
Q
is not zero, the term
z_{i,i^{\prime}}
appears in
uQw^{t}
, contrary to
u\perp_{Q}w
.

Next, we show that for every
i\geq r-s
and
i^{\prime}\geq s+1
, the
(i,i^{\prime})
entry in
Q
is zero which puts
Q
in the form (
1.7
).
When
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
, apply Proposition
2.17
part
2
to the zero entry
(i_{1},i^{\prime}_{1})
in
Q
.
Because the dimensions of
U
and
W
imply
i_{1}\leq r-s
and
i^{\prime}_{1}\leq s+1
, we have the required zero blocks in
Q
.

When
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
, if
i_{1}\neq i^{\prime}_{1}
, then the same argument as in the symmetric case applies.
Suppose now that
i_{1}=i^{\prime}_{1}\leq s+1
.
If
i^{\prime}_{2}\leq s+1
, we can apply Proposition
2.17
to the zero entry
(i_{1},i^{\prime}_{2})
because
i_{1}\neq i^{\prime}_{2}
and we are done.
If
i^{\prime}_{2}>s+1
, then
\dim W=n-s
gives
i^{\prime}_{2}=s+2
.
On the other hand,
r
is even in the alternating case and
\dim U\leq\dim W
yields
i^{\prime}_{2}=s+2\leq r-s
.
Another application of Proposition
2.17
to the zero entry
(i_{1},i^{\prime}_{2})
in
Q
gives that for every
i\geq s+1
and
i^{\prime}\geq r-s
, the
(i,i^{\prime})
entry in
Q
is zero, putting this matrix in the desired form.
The proof is the same for
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
by viewing an
m\times n
matrix
A\in Q
as a bilinear map
\mathbb{K}^{m}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}\to\mathbb{K}
via
(u,w)\mapsto uAw^{t}
.
∎

Lemma 3.7
.

The set of connected components of a scheme
X=\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
,
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{Pf}_{n}^{r})
or
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(D_{m,n}^{r})
is in bijection with the set of connected components of its associated graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
.
In particular,
X
is connected if and only if
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is a connected graph.

Proof.

Let
C
be the subscheme of
X
consisting of all compression spaces, i.e.
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}=\bigcup_{s}\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
By definition
\mathcal{G}_{X}
has a node for each
s
such that
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)\neq\emptyset
and by Remark
3.5
, two nodes corresponding to
s,s^{\prime}
are adjacent if and only if
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
intersects
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
.
This means the connected components of the graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
are in bijection with the connected components of
C
.
Now use Theorem
1.4
.
∎

Next, we prove Corollary
1.5
that characterizes when the graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
, and as a result
X
, is connected.

Proof of Corollary
1.5
.

By Lemma
3.7
, the scheme
X
is disconnected if and only if the graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is.
The two conditions of the statement are equivalent to the fact that the graph
\mathcal{G}_{X}
has at least two vertices and the cycle
s_{1}-s_{2}-\cdots-s_{\ell}-s_{1}
is missing two edges.
We need to show
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is disconnected if and only if these two conditions on
\mathcal{G}_{X}
hold.

One direction is clear.
Assume that
\mathcal{G}_{X}
has at least two vertices and the cycle is missing two edges.
If
\ell=2
,
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is obviously disconnected, so assume
\ell\geq 3
.
Consider the graph
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
in Definition
3.2
.
Build the subgraph
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
of
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
by removing those edges whose labels are less than
k
.
This is similar to building
\mathcal{G}_{X}
from
\tilde{\mathcal{G}}_{X}
except that we are not deleting any vertices.
This makes
\mathcal{G}_{X}
a subgraph of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
.

We claim the following statements hold for
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
(we use
\sim
for the relation of two vertices being adjacent):

(1)

If
i\not\sim i+1
then
s\not\sim i+1
for
0\leq s\leq i
,

(2)

If
i\not\sim i+1
then
i\not\sim s
for
i+1\leq s\leq s_{\max}
.

The following are immediate consequences of the above two statements:

(a)

If
i\not\sim i+1\not\sim i+2
, then
i+1
is an isolated vertex (i.e. not adjacent to any other vertex),

(b)

If
i\not\sim i+1
and
j\not\sim j+1
for
0\leq i<j<s_{\max}
, then every vertex
s
with
i+1\leq s\leq j
is an isolated
vertex: the function
g(\{s,s+1\})
is convex in
s
and by assumption
g(\{i,i+1\}),g(\{j,j+1\})<k
.
This shows
g(\{s,s+1\})<k
or equivalently
s\not\sim s+1
for
i\leq s\leq j
.
Now use statement
(a)
.

(c)

If
i\not\sim i+1
for some
0\leq i<s_{\max}
and
s_{\max}\not\sim 0
, then there are no edges between
\mathbf{V}_{1}=\{0,\ldots,i\}
and
\mathbf{V}_{2}=\{i+1,\ldots,s_{\max}\}
: by statements
1
and
2
we have
0\not\sim i+1
and
i\not\sim s_{\max}
, i.e.
k
is larger than all of

(3.8)

g(\{0,i+1\}),g(\{i,i+1\}),g(\{i,s_{\max}\}),g(\{0,s_{\max}\}).

We claim
k>g(\{s,s^{\prime}\})
for all
0\leq s\leq i
and
i+1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s_{\max}
, which is equivalent to no edges between
\mathbf{V}_{1}
and
\mathbf{V}_{2}
.
Momentarily think of the polynomial defining
g(\{s,s^{\prime}\})
as a function that accepts a pair of real numbers
(s,s^{\prime})
with domain
s<s^{\prime}
.
This function is convex since its Hessian is positive semidefinite.
In particular when four points in the domain are the vertices of a quadrilateral (such as those in (
3.8
)), then the value of the function at every convex combination of the vertices, e.g.
(s,s^{\prime})
with
s,s^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,
0\leq s\leq i
, and
i+1\leq s^{\prime}\leq s_{\max}
, is at most the maximum of the values at the vertices.

Assuming these claims hold, we prove that
\mathcal{G}_{X}
is disconnected.
By assumption, at least two edges are missing from
\{s_{1},s_{2}\},\{s_{2},s_{3}\},\ldots,\{s_{\ell-1},s_{\ell}\},\{s_{\ell},s_{1}\}
.
There are two cases: if
\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\}
and
\{s_{j},s_{j+1}\}
are missing in
\mathcal{G}_{X}
for some
1\leq i<j<\ell
, then
\{s_{i},s_{i}+1\}
and
\{s_{j},s_{j}+1\}
are also missing in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
.
This is because either
s_{i+1}=s_{i}+1
or
s_{i+1}>s_{i}+1
.
If the latter holds, then the vertex
s_{i}+1
is not present in
\mathcal{G}_{X}
, meaning that
k>g(\{s_{i}+1,s\})
for all
s\neq s_{i}+1
, i.e.
s_{i}+1
is isolated in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
.
A similar argument works for
\{s_{j},s_{j}+1\}
.
By statement
(b)
,
s_{j}
is isolated in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
and hence in
\mathcal{G}_{X}
.

The second case is when
\{s_{i},s_{i+1}\}
and
\{s_{\ell},s_{1}\}
are missing in
\mathcal{G}_{X}
for some
1\leq i<\ell
.
Then
\{s_{i},s_{i}+1\}
and
\{s_{\max},0\}
are missing in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
and by statement
(c)
, there are no edges between
\{0,\ldots,s_{i}\}
and
\{s_{i}+1,\ldots,s_{\max}\}
in
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}_{X}
and therefore there are no edges between
\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{i}\}
and
\{s_{i+1},\ldots,s_{\ell}\}
in
\mathcal{G}_{X}
, completing the proof.

Proof of statement
1
: by assumption
k>g(\{i,i+1\})
.
Because

g(\{i,i+1\})-g(\{0,i+1\})=(n-r)i\geq 0

and
g(\{s,i+1\})
is convex in
s
when
s<i+1
, we deduce
k>g(\{s,i+1\})
for
0\leq s\leq i
, i.e.
s\not\sim i+1
.

Proof of statement
2
: if
i+1=s_{\max}
, there is nothing to prove, so let
i+2\leq s_{\max}
.
In the symmetric case
s_{\max}\leq\frac{r-1}{2}
, and we have
i+2\leq\frac{r-1}{2}
.
Therefore,

\Delta=g(\{i,i+1\})-g(\{i,\frac{r-1}{2}\})=\frac{1}{8}\left(r-2i-5\right)\left(r-2i-3\right)\geq 0,

which together with

and convexity of
g(\{s,s^{\prime}\})
yields

s\geq i+1

i\not\sim s
.
In the alternating and rectangular cases,

and
s_{\max}=r-1
respectively, and a similar argument using
\Delta=g(\{i,i+1\})-g(\{i,s_{\max}\})
goes through.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For
0\leq s\leq\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\rfloor
, the closed subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is isomorphic in its reduced structure to the flag variety
\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-1,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
and is an irreducible component of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{\kappa(s)}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
In particular,
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is the
\mathrm{GL}(n)
orbit of the standard
s
-compression space and does not intersect other components (if any) of the scheme.

Proof.

The nested
s
-compression spaces form an irreducible closed subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{\kappa(s)}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
as it is the image of the morphism in (
4.1
).
Denote by
\phi
the composition of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{\kappa(s)}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\to\Gr(\kappa(s),S_{n})
with the map in (
4.1
).
Then
\phi
is
\mathrm{GL}(n)
-equivariant and bijective onto its image.

A standard argument using affine charts of the flag variety and the Grassmannian shows the differential
d\phi
is injective at the flag that maps to the standard
s
-compression space
[Q]
.
Because
\mathrm{GL}(n)
acts transitively on
\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
,
d\phi
is injective everywhere, hence
\phi
is a closed embedding.
This implies
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is isomorphic in its reduced structure to
\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
.
In particular it is the orbit of any single point, say
[Q]
, and does not intersect other components.

The point
[Q]
is the unique Borel fixed point in
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
.
If
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is contained properly in an irreducible component, then any Zariski open neighborhood of
[Q]
must contain a point that is not a nested
s
-compression space which contradicts Proposition
4.2
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is an irreducible component.
∎

Corollary 4.5
.

Proof.

Let

.
By assumption

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
r=2s+1
and assume
[Q]\in\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is a nested
t
-compression space only for
t=s
.

(i)

There exists a unique flag
U\subset W\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
with
\dim U=s+1+n-r
,
\dim W=n-s
, and
U\perp_{Q}W
.

(ii)

If a flag
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfies
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}\geq 2n-r+1
and
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
, then
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}=2n-r+1
and
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}
is the unique flag in part
(i)
.

Proof.

Part
(i)
: the existence of the flag
U\subset W
follows from the definition of
[Q]
.
To show uniqueness, assume
U_{1}\subset W_{1}
is another flag satisfying the conditions.
We have
\dim U\cap U_{1}\geq\dim U+\dim U_{1}-n=1+n-r
.
Let
p\geq 0
be such that
\dim U\cap U_{1}=p+1+n-r
.
It follows
\dim(U+U_{1})=n-p
.
Since
U\cap U_{1}\perp_{Q}U+U_{1}
,
[Q]
is a nested
p
-compression space, forcing
p=s
and therefore
U_{1}=U
.

Next, we show
W_{1}=W
.
If
s=0
, then
W=W_{1}=\mathbb{K}^{n}
.
If
s>0
and
W\neq W_{1}
, then
\dim W+W_{1}>n-s
.
Choose subspaces
U_{2}\subset U
and
W_{2}\subset W+W_{1}
with
U_{2}\subset W_{2}
,
\dim U_{2}=(s-1)+1+n-r
and
\dim W_{2}=n-(s-1)
.
Then
U_{2}\perp_{Q}W_{2}
turns
[Q]
into a nested
(s-1)
-compression space, a contradiction.

Part
(ii)
: the inequality implies
\dim U^{\prime}\geq 1+n-r
.
Let
p\geq 0
be such that
\dim U^{\prime}=p+1+n-r
.
Then
\dim W^{\prime}\geq n-p
, and
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
makes
[Q]
a nested
p
-compression space forcing
p=s
.
If
\dim W^{\prime}>n-s
, then similar to the proof of part
(i)
,
[Q]
becomes a nested
(s-1)
-compression space.
We conclude
\dim W^{\prime}=n-s
.
Now use part
(i)
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
0<n^{\prime}<n
and assume
[Q]\in\Gr(k+1,S_{n})
is given by an
n\times n
matrix of the form

Q=\begin{pmatrix}P&0\\
0&P^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}

where
P
and
P^{\prime}
are square blocks of size
n^{\prime}
and
n-n^{\prime}
, respectively, with linear forms as entries.
Let
U=\spn\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n^{\prime}}\}
and
W=\spn\{e_{n^{\prime}+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
and define
\pi_{U}
and
\pi_{W}
to be projections of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
onto
U
and
W
.
For a flag
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfying
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
,

(i)

U^{\prime}\cap U\perp_{P}\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
and
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\perp_{P^{\prime}}W^{\prime}\cap W
,

(ii)

if
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}\geq n+\ell+1
for some
0\leq\ell<n
, then
\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})\geq n^{\prime}+\ell+1
or
\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W\geq n-n^{\prime}
,

(iii)

if
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}=n+\ell+1
for some
0\leq\ell<n
, we have
\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})=n^{\prime}+\ell+1
if and only if
\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W=n-n^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Part
(i)
: let
u\in U^{\prime}\cap U
and
v\in\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
.
Then
v=w^{\prime}-w^{\prime\prime}
for some
w^{\prime}\in W^{\prime}
and
w^{\prime\prime}\in\pi_{W}(W^{\prime})
.
Since
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
and
U\perp_{Q}W
we have
u\perp_{Q}w^{\prime}
and
u\perp_{Q}w^{\prime\prime}
, hence
u\perp_{Q}v
.
This shows
U^{\prime}\cap U\perp_{Q}\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
and since
U^{\prime}\cap U,\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})\subset U
, they are also orthogonal with respect to
P
.
The proof of
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\perp_{P^{\prime}}W^{\prime}\cap W
is similar.

Part
(ii)
: By the exact sequence
0\to U^{\prime}\cap U\to U^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi_{W}}W
we have
\dim U^{\prime}=\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})
and similarly
\dim W^{\prime}=\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W
.
Assume to the contrary that
\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})<n^{\prime}+\ell+1
and
\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W<n-n^{\prime}
.
Adding these inequalities yields

\left(\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\right)+\left(\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W\right)<n+\ell+1.

But the left-hand side is
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}
, a contradiction to
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}\geq n+\ell+1
.

To prove
(iii)
, for each equality in the if and only if statement, subtract it from
\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W^{\prime}=n+\ell+1
.
∎

Theorem 5.4
.

The following holds for the Fano scheme of lines
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.

(i)

The subschemes
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
for
s=0,1,\ldots,\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor
are distinct, and are exactly the irreducible components of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Moreover,
\bigcap_{s}\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)\neq\emptyset
.

(ii)

Let
\,\mathcal{U}(s)
be the restriction of the universal bundle on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{\kappa(s)}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
to
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
.
The fiber of the natural map in (
4.6
) (with
k=1
) over a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
is finite.

Proof.

Part
(i)
: by the Kronecker canonical form of a pencil of singular matrices
[
Gan59
, XII.4]
, every line on
\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r}
is a compression space, hence a nested one by Corollary
2.9
.
Therefore, the irreducible closed subschemes
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
cover
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
For every
0\leq s\leq\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor
, we show
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
has a point not contained in
\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\neq s}\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s^{\prime})
which proves that
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
is an irreducible component.

Set
r^{\prime}=2s+1
and
n^{\prime}=r^{\prime}+n-r
and assume we have a pencil
[P]\in\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}^{r^{\prime}}_{n^{\prime}})
with
P
an
n^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
matrix over
\mathbb{K}[z_{0},z_{1}]_{1}
that is a nested
t
-compression space only for
t=s
.
Also, take
P^{\prime}
to be a generic symmetric matrix of size
n-n^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{K}[z_{0},z_{1}]_{1}
and define the
n\times n
matrix

Q=\begin{pmatrix}P&0\\
0&P^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}.

Define
U=\spn\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n^{\prime}}\}
and
W=\spn\{e_{n^{\prime}+1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
, so that
\mathbb{K}^{n}=U\oplus W
and the matrices
P
and
P^{\prime}
are pencils of bilinear forms on
U
and
W
respectively.
We claim
[Q]\in\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
and is a nested
t
-compression space if and only if
t=s
.

To show
Q
is a nested
s
-compression space, let
U_{1}\subset W_{1}
be a flag in
U
with
U_{1}\perp_{P}W_{1}
making
P
into a nested
s
-compression space.
By definition
\dim U_{1}=s+1+n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
and
\dim W_{1}=n^{\prime}-s
.
Then the flag
U_{1}\subset W_{1}+W
in
\mathbb{K}^{n}
satisfies
U_{1}\perp_{Q}W_{1}+W
and makes
Q
into a nested
s
-compression space because
\dim U_{1}=s+1+n-r
and
\dim W_{1}+W=n-s
.
Here we used
n-r=n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
.

Conversely, assume
Q
is a nested
t
-compression space and
U^{\prime}\subset W^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{K}^{n}
is a flag satisfying
\dim U^{\prime}=t+1+n-r
,
\dim W^{\prime}=n-t
and
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
.
Our goal is to show
t=s
.
By Lemma
5.3
part
(i)
, with
\ell=n-r=n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
, we have
U^{\prime}\cap U\perp_{P}\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
and
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\perp_{P^{\prime}}W^{\prime}\cap W
.
Since
P^{\prime}
is not singular,
\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W\leq\dim W=n-n^{\prime}
.
By Lemma
5.3
parts
(ii)
and
(iii)
, the orthogonal flag
U^{\prime}\cap U\subset\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
in
U
satisfies
\dim U^{\prime}\cap U+\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})\geq 2n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}+1
.
Applying Lemma
5.2
part
(ii)
, we have that this last inequality is an equality and that
U^{\prime}\cap U\subset\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
is the unique flag making
P
a nested
s
-compression space.
In particular
\dim U^{\prime}\cap U=s+1+n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
and
\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})=n^{\prime}-s
.
Lemma
5.3
part
(iii)
now gives
\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})+\dim W^{\prime}\cap W=n-n^{\prime}
.

We compare some of the above dimensions and use
n-r=n^{\prime}-r^{\prime}
and
r^{\prime}=2s+1
.
Comparing the dimensions of
U^{\prime}\cap U
and
U^{\prime}
gives
s\leq t
.
Next, note that
U^{\prime}\cap U
is contained in
\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})
and they have the same dimension which means
\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})=U^{\prime}\cap U
.
We conclude that
\pi_{U}(U^{\prime})=U^{\prime}\cap U
implying
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})=U^{\prime}\cap W
.
Therefore
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\subset W^{\prime}\cap W
is a flag in
W
and by Lemma
5.3
part
(i)
,
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\perp_{P^{\prime}}W^{\prime}\cap W
.

The exact sequences
0\to U^{\prime}\cap U\to U^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi_{W}}W
and
0\to W^{\prime}\cap W\to W^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\pi_{U}}U
give the dimensions of the flag
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\subset W^{\prime}\cap W
as

(5.5)

\begin{split}\dim\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})&=\dim U^{\prime}-\dim U^{\prime}\cap U=t-s,\\
\dim W^{\prime}\cap W&=\dim W^{\prime}-\dim\pi_{U}(W^{\prime})=(n-n^{\prime})-(t-s).\end{split}

Since
P^{\prime}
is general and
\pi_{W}(U^{\prime})\perp_{P^{\prime}}W^{\prime}\cap W
, by Lemma
5.1
we must have
t-s=0
.

To complete the proof of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)\backslash\bigcup_{s^{\prime}\neq s}\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, we need to produce an
n^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
matrix
P
such that
[P]
, as a point on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n^{\prime}}^{r^{\prime}})
is a nested
t
-compression space only for
t=s
.
Take the nested
s
-compression space

P=\begin{pmatrix}0&B\\
B^{t}&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\text{with}\quad B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc|c}z_{0}&z_{1}&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&&\text{\large 0}\\
&&z_{0}&z_{1}&\end{array}\right)_{s\times(s+1+n^{\prime}-r^{\prime})},

where the entries not shown in
B
are all zero.
In
[
CI15
, Proof of Proposition 4.5]
, the authors prove this point is not a
t
-compression space for
t<s
and by Corollary
2.8
, it is also not a
t
-compression space for
t>s
since
s
is the middle integer in
0,\ldots,r^{\prime}-1
.
Therefore,
[P]
is a nested
t
-compression space only for
t=s
.
The last statement in
(i)
holds because
\bigcap_{s}\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
contains the point

\begin{pmatrix}z_{0}&z_{1}&&\\
z_{1}&&&\\
&&\text{\Large 0}&\\
&&&\end{pmatrix}.

Part
(ii)
: the map in (
4.6
) is proper with image
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)
.
For a proper map, the dimension of fibers is upper semi-continuous on the target, therefore it is enough to show the fiber over
[Q]
is a single point.
Since
\Gr(k+1,\mathcal{U}(s))
is a bundle over
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
and by Corollary
4.4
,\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is isomorphic in its reduced structure to
\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
, it is enough show a flag
[U^{\prime},W^{\prime}]\in\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
satisfying
U^{\prime}\perp_{Q}W^{\prime}
is uniquely determined by
[Q]
.

Using (
5.5
) and
t-s=0
, we have that
U^{\prime}=U^{\prime}\cap U
and we saw in the proof of part
(i)
that
U^{\prime}\cap U
is uniquely determined by
[P]
(the uniqueness is due to Lemma
5.2
and does not depend on the representative matrix
P
).
We claim
W^{\prime}=U^{\prime}\oplus W
, which shows that
W^{\prime}
is also uniquely determined by
[P]
.
By (
5.5
),
\dim W^{\prime}\cap W=n-n^{\prime}=\dim W
, i.e.
W\subset W^{\prime}
.
We have subspaces
U^{\prime},W\subset W^{\prime}
with
U^{\prime}\cap W=0
(since
U^{\prime}\subset U
and
U\cap W=0
) and

\dim U^{\prime}+\dim W=(s+1+n^{\prime}-r^{\prime})+(n-n^{\prime})=n-s=\dim W^{\prime}.

Therefore,
W=U^{\prime}\oplus W
.
∎

Corollary 5.6
.

The closed subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
has dimension

\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)=(s+1+n-r)(r-2s-1)+s(n-s)+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1).

In particular for
k=1
, each irreducible component
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
has dimension
nr+(s-1)(n-r)-5
and when
r=n
, the components are equidimensional and of the expected dimension.

Proof.

The second statement follows from the first.
By Theorem
5.4
part
(ii)
, there exists a line
[L]\in\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
for which there are only finitely many flags
U\subset W
in
\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})
such that
U\perp_{L}W
.
After acting by
\mathrm{GL}(n)
, we may assume the standard
s
-compression space
[Q]\in\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
contains this line.
Take a
k
-plane
[P]\in\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
with
L\subset P\subset Q
.
Then there are only finitely many flags
U\subset W
with
U\perp_{P}W
.
The map in (
4.6
) is proper with image
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
and the fiber over
[P]
is finite.
By upper semi-continuity of fiber dimensions, the fiber over a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is finite.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)

\displaystyle=\dim\Gr\left(k+1,\mathcal{U}(s)\right)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1)

\displaystyle=\dim\mathrm{Fl}_{s+1+n-r,n-s}(\mathbb{K}^{n})+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1)

\displaystyle=(s+1+n-r)(r-2s-1)+s(n-s)+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1).

∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let

with

.
Assume that

(or equivalently the closed subschemes
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
and
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
are non-empty).
Then
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
and
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
are distinct and one does not contain the other.

Proof.

Let
Q
be the standard
s
-compression space.
By Theorem
5.4
part
(i)
, there is a line
L
inside
Q
such that
L
is not a nested
s^{\prime}
-compression space.
Any
k
-plane inside

containing

is also not a nested
s^{\prime}
-compression space, i.e.
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is not contained in
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
.
The statement is now proved since the argument is symmetric in

.

Next, we prove Theorem
1.6
characterizing the irreducibility of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

If
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is irreducible, then there is only one
0\leq s\leq\left\lfloor\frac{n-1}{2}\right\rfloor
such that
k\leq\kappa(s)
.
This is because if
s\neq s^{\prime}
and
k\leq\min\{\kappa(s),\kappa(s^{\prime})\}
, then
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s),\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s^{\prime})
would lie on one irreducible component, namely
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Let
\mathcal{U}
be the universal bundle on
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
The natural map from the Grassmann bundle
\Gr(2,\mathcal{U})
to
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{1}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
then has an irreducible image containing both
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s),\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{1}(s^{\prime})
which contradicts Theorem

Using the convexity of

in

for only one integer

Conversely, if the conditions on

, Theorem 1.3]
, every point of

is a compression space, i.e.

∎

6.
Smoothness

6.1.

Deformation theory provides a tool to compute the dimensions of Zariski tangent spaces at points of

We describe the general setup.
Let

, in the expression

\tilde{p}_{i,j}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\frac{s-i+1}{2}}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\frac{s-j+1}{2}}(-1)^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{s-j-\ell^{\prime}+1}{\ell^{\prime}}z_{2}^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}},

we make the change of variable
L=\ell+\ell^{\prime}
to get

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{i,j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{s-\frac{i+j}{2}+1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell\leq L\\
0\leq L-\ell\leq s-j+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{L}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{s-j-(L-\ell)+1}{L-\ell}z_{2}^{L}

(6.16)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{s-j+2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}0\leq\ell\leq L\\
0\leq L-\ell\leq s-j+1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{L}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{s-j-(L-\ell)+1}{L-\ell}z_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle+\,\text{(terms with degree higher than $s-j+2$)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{s-j+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(-1)^{L}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{s-j-(L-\ell)+1}{L-\ell}z_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{s-j+2}(-1)^{s-j+2}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{\ell-1}{s-j+2-\ell}z_{2}^{s-j+2}

\displaystyle+\,\text{(terms with degree higher than $s-j+2$)}

(6.17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{L=0}^{s-j+2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(-1)^{L}\binom{s-i-\ell+1}{\ell}\binom{s-j-(L-\ell)+1}{L-\ell}z_{2}^{L}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{s-j+2}\binom{s-i+1}{0}\binom{-1}{s-j+2}z_{2}^{s-j+2}

\displaystyle+\,\text{(terms with degree higher than $s-j+2$)}.

Similar to (
6.16
),

(6.18)

\begin{split}\tilde{p}_{i+1,j-1}=&\sum_{L=0}^{s-j+2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}(-1)^{L}\binom{s-i-\ell}{\ell}\binom{s-j-(L-\ell)+2}{L-\ell}z_{2}^{L}\\
&+\text{(terms with degree higher than $s-j+2$)}.\end{split}

By Jensen’s formula (Lemma
6.5
), the double summations in (
6.17
) and (
6.18
) are equal, hence

\tilde{p}_{i,j}-\tilde{p}_{i+1,j-1}=-z_{2}^{s-j+2}+\,\text{(terms with higher degree)}.

This completes the proof of part
(ii)
.
The last statement of the lemma follows from parts
(i)
and
(ii)
.
∎

Using Fano schemes, we can prove the following result on symmetric matrices.

Corollary 6.19
.

Let
k,s\geq 1
and assume
D
is a general
s\times(s+1)
matrix with entries in
\mathbb{K}[z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}]_{1}
.
Then a symmetric matrix
A
of size
s+1
over
\mathbb{K}[z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}]_{1}
satisfies

(6.20)

\det\begin{pmatrix}0&D\\
D^{t}&A\end{pmatrix}=0,

if and only if
A=\tilde{D}+\tilde{D}^{t}
, where
\tilde{D}
is an
(s+1)\times(s+1)
matrix whose rows are in the row span of
D
.

Proof.

One direction is clear.
For the other, let
n=r=2s+1
and consider the Fano scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Without loss of generality we may assume
k\leq\kappa(s)
.
Up to
\mathrm{GL}(n)
action, a general point
[Q]\in\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is of the form (
6.10
) where
B
and
D
are matrices filled with general linear forms.

The dimension of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
, by Corollary
5.6
is
s(s+1)+\left(\kappa(s)-k\right)(k+1)
and by Proposition
6.9
, the Fano scheme contains a point in
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
with tangent space dimension equal to
\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
This means the dimension of the tangent space to
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{n})
at
[Q]
equals
\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
This dimension together with Lemma
6.1
give that for a general
D
, the dimension of all matrices
A
satisfying (
6.20
) is
a_{\det}=s(s+1)=s(n-s)
.
Since
D
is general, the space of all matrices
A
satisfying (
6.20
) contains the subspace of those matrices of the form
\tilde{D}+\tilde{D}^{t}
where
\tilde{D}
is an
(s+1)\times(s+1)
matrix in the row span of
D
and this subspace has dimension
s(n-s)
too.
Therefore every matrix
A
satisfying (
6.20
) is of the required form.
∎

Next, we compute the dimension of the tangent space at a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.

Proposition 6.21
.

Let
[Q]
be a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)\subset\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Then

\dim T_{[Q]}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\right)=s(s+1+n-r)+(s+1+n-r)(r-2s-1)(k+1)+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1).

Proof.

Up to
\mathrm{GL}(n)
action,
Q
is the matrix in (
6.2
) in which the entries in blocks
B,\ldots,E
are general linear forms in
z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}
.
Since
E
is general,
\det E\neq 0
and by Lemma
6.1
,

\dim T_{[Q]}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\right)=a_{\det}+(s+1+n-r)(r-2s-1)(k+1)+(\kappa(s)-k)(k+1).

Our task is to show
a_{\det}=s(s+1+n-r)
.
Call a matrix
A
of size
s+1+n-r
over
\mathbb{K}[z_{0},\ldots,z_{k}]_{1}
permissible if all
s
-anchored
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
minors of (
6.3
) vanish.
The space of all permissible
A
contains a subspace of dimension
s(s+1+n-r)
, namely the set of all matrices
\tilde{D}+\tilde{D}^{t}
where
\tilde{D}
is a square matrix of size
s+1+n-r
whose rows are in the row span of the general matrix
D
.

Conversely, we show if
A
is a permissible matrix, then it is of the form
\tilde{D}+\tilde{D}^{t}
.
Consider the principal
s
-anchored
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
minors of (
6.3
).
By assumption these minors vanish and are of the form

\det\begin{pNiceArray},

for submatrices
D^{\prime},A^{\prime}
of
D
and
A
respectively.
By Corollary
6.19
, the vanishing of the above determinant implies
A^{\prime}
is the sum of a unique matrix in the row span of
D^{\prime}
and its transpose.
Since this holds for
A^{\prime}
in every principal
s
-anchored
(2s+1)\times(2s+1)
minor in (
6.3
), it also holds for
A
, i.e.
A=\tilde{D}+\tilde{D}^{t}
where rows of
\tilde{D}
are in the row span of
D
.
This completes the proof.
∎

6.2.
Smoothness

In this section we use the dimensions of tangent spaces of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
to detect components and give results on the smoothness of our Fano schemes.

Proposition 6.22
.

Let
r
be odd,
s=\frac{r-1}{2}
and
k\leq\kappa(s)
.
The subscheme
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is an irreducible component that contains a smooth point of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
Moreover, when
k=\kappa(s)
, every point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is a smooth point of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.21
and Corollary
5.6
, when
r-2s-1=0
,
a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
has tangent space dimension equal to
\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
Therefore
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is a component containing a smooth point of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
When
k=\kappa(s)
, by Corollary
4.4
,
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{\kappa(s)}(s)
is a single
\mathrm{GL}(n)
orbit, proving the last statement.
∎

Next, we prove Proposition
1.9
which states that our Fano schemes can have generically non-reduced components.

Proof of Proposition
1.9
.

Let
[Q]
be a general point of
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
The irreducible component
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is generically reduced if and only if
\dim T_{[Q]}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\right)=\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
.
By Proposition
6.21
and Corollary
5.6

\dim T_{[Q]}\left(\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})\right)-\dim\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)=(r-2s-1)\left((s+1+n-r)k-s\right).

We now have the desired result since
\left((s+1+n-r)k-s\right)>0
.
∎

7.
Further questions

In our investigation of the Fano schemes
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
, we were able to prove that the closed subschemes
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
consisting of nested
s
-compression spaces are irreducible components when
k=1
or
\kappa(s)
or when
r=2s+1
.
When
r=2s+1
, the proof used the dimensions of tangent spaces since in this case
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
is generically reduced.
By Proposition
1.9
when
r\neq 2s+1
, such a proof will not work.
The proof of the case
k=1
(Theorem
5.4
) relied on the Kronecker canonical form for a pencil of matrices.
Moreover, we resolved the case
k=\kappa(s)
(Corollary
4.4
) by looking at affine charts corresponding to Borel fixed points but when
k<\kappa(s)
, the affine chart of a Borel fixed nested
s
-compression space could contain a point that is not a nested
s
-compression space.

Question 7.1
.

Is
\mathrm{\textbf{C}}_{k}(s)
an irreducible component of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
for arbitrary
n,r,k
and
s
?

In Section
6
, we gave partial results on the smoothness of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
.
We believe by using the results of this section together with those in Section
3
, one can fully characterize the smoothness of these schemes and prove the following.

Conjecture 7.2
.

The scheme
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
is smooth if and only if
r
is odd and

\max\left\{\kappa(0),\kappa\left(\frac{r-3}{2}\right)\right\}<k\leq\kappa\left(\frac{r-1}{2}\right).

Our next question involves de Seguins Pazzis’ theorem
[
Seg16a
, Theorem 1.3]
which states that when
k>\max\{\kappa(1),\kappa(s_{\max}-1)\}
, the Fano scheme consists only of compression spaces.
Here
s_{\max}=\lfloor\frac{r-1}{2}\rfloor
.
It is interesting to know if this bound can be improved.
One reason this is important is that when we know the closed points of
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
are all compression spaces, then we can classify the irreducible components of the scheme.

Question 7.3
.

Given
n
and
r
, what is the largest value
k
for which
\mathrm{\textbf{F}}_{k}(\mathrm{SD}_{n}^{r})
has a non-compression point?

Looking at the above question in a different way, it would be interesting if there is an invariant for a subspace of symmetric matrices of rank less than
r
that can detect being a compression space.
This invariant could lead to a characterization of when we have only compression spaces.

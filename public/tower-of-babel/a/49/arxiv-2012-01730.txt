On initial-boundary value problem of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in the half space
Tongkeun Chang and Minsuk Yang

Abstract
. We study the initial-boundary value problem of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in the half space. We prove the existence of weak solutions in the standard Besov space valued random processes when the initial data belong to the critical Besov space.



1.
Introduction

In this paper we study the following stochastic Navier–Stokes equations

du(t,x)=\big(\Delta u(t,x)-\nabla p(t,x)+\divg(u\otimes u)(t,x)\big)dt+g(t,x)dB_{t}

(1.1)

for
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
,
n\geq 2
, with the boundary condition
u(t,x)=0
for
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times{\mdmathbb R}^{n-1}
, the initial condition
u(0,x)=u_{0}
for
x\in{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
, and the random noise
g(t,x)dB_{t}
.
Here
{\left\{B_{t}(\omega):t\geq 0,\omega\in\Omega\right\}}
denotes an
n
-dimensional Wiener process defined on a probability space
\Omega
.
The Navier–Stokes system is considered as a reliable model both in science and engineering.
In spite of huge effort done by countless mathematicians, many mathematical issues related to the Navier–Stokes equations including uniqueness and regularity remain as open questions.
Moreover, one of the important issues is to understand turbulence in fluid motions.
Although turbulent phenomena can be frequently observed in a lot of real world situations, it is extremely hard to define or characterize them mathematically.
Because chaotic behavior in fluid motions may be interpreted as a presence of randomness, many interests on the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations have been increased.

The study of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations started around early 70s’, for example, by Bensoussan and Temam
[
2
]
and Foias
[
14
]
.
Although many mathematicians made important contributions, we list here only a few of them.
Capiński and Gatarek
[
6
]
obtained an existence theorem for the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in a Hilbert space setting.
Mikulevicius and Rozovskii
[
20
]
proved the existence of a global weak (martingale) solution of the stochastic Navier–Stokes equation.
Kim
[
16
]
established the existence of local strong solutions to the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in
{\mdmathbb R}^{3}
when the initial data are sufficiently small and slightly more regular.
Taniguchi
[
22
]
studied the energy solutions to the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in two dimensional unbounded domains.
For a recent overview, we refer the reader to the lecture notes or monographs by Flandoli
[
13
]
, Kuksin and Shirikyan
[
19
]
, and Breit, Feireisl, Hofmanová
[
4
]
and the references cited therein.

For the deterministic Navier–Stokes equations, Fujita and Kato
[
15
]
proved existence of mild solutions.
Many papers continued to study such solutions with wider class of initial data, which should be in some special classes due to the scaling structure of the Navier–Stokes equations.
Adapting harmonic analysis tools, for example, Littlewood–Paley theory, Fujita–Kato’s result extended to the refined function space setting like Besov spaces.
For this, we refer the reader the monograph by Cannone
[
5
]
.
After that, Koch and Tataru
[
17
]
extends the class of initial data to
BMO^{-1}
.
This line of development is well illustrated in the monograph by Bahouri, Chemin, and Danchin
[
1
]
.

Recently, Du and Zhang
[
12
]
proved existence of solutions to the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in the whole space.
Due to a technical reason they found solutions in Chemin–Lerner type spaces with the norm defined by a weighted sum of space-time integration of Littlewood–Paley projections.
Our aim of this paper is to establish existence of solutions to the stochastic Navier–Stokes equations in the half space.
We found our solutions in the standard Besov spaces.

To state precise concept of solutions we need to recall some standard definitions.
Let
(\Omega,{\mathcal{G}},\{{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\},{\mdmathbb P})
be a probability space, where
\{{\mathcal{G}}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
is a filtration of
\sigma
-fields
{\mathcal{G}}_{t}\subset{\mathcal{G}}
with
\mathcal{G}_{0}
containing all
{\mdmathbb P}
-null subsets of
\Omega
.
Assume that
w_{\cdot}
is a one-dimensional
\{\mathcal{G}_{t}\}
-adapted Wiener processes defined on
(\Omega,{\mathcal{G}},{\mdmathbb P})
.
We denote by
{\mdmathbb E}X
the expectation of a random variable
X
.
We suppress the argument
\omega\in\Omega
of random variable
X(\omega)
when it brings no confusion.

We note that the solution
u
and data
(u_{0},g)
in (
1.1
) are random variables.
We construct suitable spaces for them using the Besov spaces.
We shall use two different types of spaces.
The first type emphasizes the regularity in
x
whereas the second type does the joint regularity in
(t,x)
.
We can consider
u
and
g
as Banach space-valued stochastic processes.
Hence
(\Omega\times(0,\infty),\mathcal{P},{\mdmathbb P}\bigotimes\ell(0,\infty))
is a suitable choice for their common domain, where
{\mathcal{P}}
is the predictable
\sigma
-field generated by
\{{\mathcal{G}}_{t}:t\geq 0\}
(see, for instance, pp. 84–85 of
[
18

(0,\infty)

For a Banach space
X
and
\alpha\in{\mdmathbb R}
we define the stochastic Banach spaces
{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,T),{\mathcal{P}};X)
to be the space of
X

{\left\|f\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{r}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,T),{\mathcal{P}};X)}=\Bigg({\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{T}s^{\alpha r}{\left\|f(s,\cdot)\right\|}_{X}^{r}ds\Bigg)^{1/r}.

Here is the definition of solutions.

Definition 1.1

(Local solution)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
2\leq p,q\leq\infty
.
Assume that
u_{0}
is a
\dot{B}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
-valued
{\mathcal{F}}_{0}
-measurable random variable.
We say that
(u,\tau)
is a local solution for the stochastic Navier-Stokes equations (
1.1
) if
u\in{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))

is a progressively measurable process and for any
0\leq t<\infty
and

\tau(\omega)=\inf{\left\{0\leq T\leq\infty:{\left\|u(\omega)\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\geq R\right\}}

where
R
is a positive number, and for almost surely,
u\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
satisfies

\displaystyle\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}u(x,t\wedge\tau)\Phi(x)dx-{\left\langle u_{0},\Phi(\cdot,0)\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int^{t\wedge\tau}_{0}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\left(u\cdot\Delta\Phi+u\cdot(\Phi_{t}+(u\otimes u):\nabla\Phi)\right)dxdt+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau}{\left\langle g,\Phi\right\rangle}dB_{t}

for every
\Phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\overline{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}_{+}\times[0,\tau(\omega))
with
\mbox{div}_{x}\,\Phi=0
,
\Phi\big|_{x_{n}=0}=0
,
where
{\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle}
denotes the duality pairing.

The exact definitions and notations will be given in the next section.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
{\mathcal{S}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
denote the Schwartz space on
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
and
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
the space of tempered distributions, which is the dual space of
{\mathcal{S}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
.
The Fourier transform of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
is initially defined by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\xi}f(x)dx,

and extended to the dual space
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
.

Definition 2.1

(Besov spaces on
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
)
.

Let
\Psi\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
be a radial Schwartz function whose Fourier transform is
nonnegative, supported in the ball
|\xi|\leq 2
, equal to
1
in the ball

|\xi|\leq 1
.
Let

\widehat{\Phi}(\xi)=\widehat{\Psi}(\xi)-\widehat{\Psi}(2\xi).

We denote the Littlewood-Paley projection operator by

\Delta_{j}f(x)=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\Phi}(2^{-j}\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)\;d\xi\quad\text{ for }j\in{\mdmathbb Z}.

Notice that

\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}\widehat{\Phi}(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\qquad\text{ for }\xi\neq 0.

We define for
s\in{\mdmathbb R}
and
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, the homogeneous Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
, which is the set of
f\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
with the norm

{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}=\begin{cases}\Bigg(\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}(2^{sj}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})})^{q}\Bigg)^{1/q}<\infty&\quad\text{ for }q<\infty,\\
\sup_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{sj}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}<\infty&\quad\text{ for }q=\infty.\end{cases}

Definition 2.2

(Besov spaces on
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
)
.

For
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
s\in{\mdmathbb R}
, the space

\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})=\{F|_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}:F\in\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})\}

is defined as the set of restrictions of
F\in\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
with the norm

{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}=\inf{\left\{{\left\|F\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}:F\in\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}),~F|_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}=f\right\}}.

For
1\leq p,q<\infty
and
s\in{\mdmathbb R}
, the space
{\dot{B}^{s}_{pq0}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
in
\dot{B}^{s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
.

We denote by
{\mdmathbb B}_{pq0}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
the stochastic Besov space with the norm

{\left\|f\right\|}_{{\mdmathbb B}_{pq0}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}={\mdmathbb E}{\left\|f\right\|}_{B_{pq0}^{-1+\frac{n}{p}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}

Remark 2.3
.

Let
0\leq s<\infty
,
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
, and let
p^{\prime},q^{\prime}
denote the Hölder conjugates of
p
,
q
, respectively.
Then

(\dot{B}^{s}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))^{\prime}=\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})\quad\text{ and }\quad(\dot{B}^{s}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))^{\prime}=\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}).

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a Banach space and
I
an interval in
{\mdmathbb R}
.
For
1\leq r\leq\infty
and
0\leq\alpha<\infty
, we denote by
L^{r}_{\alpha}(I;X)
the weighted Bochner space with the norm

{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{r}_{\alpha}(I;X)}=\Bigg(\int_{I}s^{\alpha r}{\left\|f(s)\right\|}_{X}^{r}ds\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{r}}.

In particular, if
\alpha=0
, then it is the same as the usual Bochner space, i.e.,
L^{r}_{0}(I;X)=L^{r}(I;X)
.

Definition 2.5

(Newtonian potential)
.

The fundamental solution of the Laplace equation in
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
is defined by

N(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\vskip 5.69054pt\frac{1}{\sigma_{n}(2-n)|x|^{n-2}}&\mbox{if }n\geq 3,\\
\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln|x|&\mbox{if }n=2.\end{array}\right.

Here
\sigma_{n}
denotes the surface area of the unit sphere in
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 3.3 in
[
8
]
)
.

If
1<p<\infty
and
0\leq s<\infty
, then

{\left\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(\cdot-y)f(y)dy\right\|}_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})},

where
\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
is the space of all restriction of
f\in\dot{H}^{s}_{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})
on
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
, which is the standard homogenous factional Sobolev space defined by the Fourier inversion.
(In particular,
\dot{H}^{0}_{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})=L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
.)

Lemma 2.7
.

If
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
0<s<1/p^{\prime}
, then

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(\cdot-y)f(y)dy\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})},

\displaystyle{\left\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(\cdot-y^{*})f(y)dy\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

where
p^{\prime}
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
, i.e.,
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
.

Proof.

Applying the real interpolation (see for example Section 2 in
[
9
]
) to Lemma
2.6
we get

{\left\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(\cdot-y)f(y)dy\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{s}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

Since the operator

Tf(x):=\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(x-y)f(y)dy

is symmetric, we use the duality argument to obtain

{\left\|\nabla_{x}^{2}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}N(\cdot-y)f(y)dy\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

We note that if
-1/p<s<1/p^{\prime}
, then

\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})=\dot{B}^{-s}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}).

This proves the first estimate and the proof of the second estimate is almost the same.
∎

Definition 2.8

(Heat kernel)
.

The fundamental solution of the heat equation in
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}
is defined by

\Gamma_{t}(x)=\Gamma(x,t)=\begin{cases}(2\pi t)^{-\frac{n}{2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{4t}}&\text{ if }t>0\\
0&\text{ if }t\leq 0.\end{cases}

We define

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}*f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}\Gamma(x-y,t)f(y)dy,

\displaystyle\Gamma_{t}^{*}*f(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}\Gamma(x-y^{*},t)f(y)dy.

The following lemma is a consequence of a pointwise estimate of the localized heat kernel,
which is obtained by the standard integration by parts argument.

From now on we simply write
{\left\|f\right\|}_{p}
instead of
{\left\|f\right\|}_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}
.

Lemma 2.9
.

There exists a positive constant
c
such that for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
t>0
, and
j\in{\mdmathbb Z}

{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}\lesssim\exp(-ct2^{2j}){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}.

Proof.

Fix
t>0
.
For
\lambda>0
we define

K_{\lambda}(x,t)=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\Psi}(\xi/\lambda)e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}d\xi.

By a change of variables we have

K_{\lambda}(x,t)=\lambda^{n}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}e^{2\pi i\lambda x\cdot\xi}\widehat{\Psi}(\xi)e^{-t\lambda^{2}|\xi|^{2}}d\xi.

Using the identity

(I-\Delta_{\xi})e^{2\pi i\lambda x\cdot\xi}=(1+4\pi^{2}|\lambda x|^{2})e^{2\pi i\lambda x\cdot\xi}

we can carry out repeated integration by parts to get the pointwise estimate of the localized heat kernel

K_{\lambda}(x,t)\lesssim\frac{\lambda^{n}\exp(-c_{1}t\lambda^{2})}{(1+|\lambda x|^{2})^{(n+1)/2}}

for some positive constant
c_{1}
.
Using a change of variable yields

\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}K_{\lambda}(x,t)dx\lesssim\exp(-c_{1}t\lambda^{2}).

(2.1)

The implied constant does not depend on
\lambda
.
Since
\Delta_{j}=(\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1})\Delta_{j}
and the convolutions commute, we have

\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)=(\Delta_{j-1}+\Delta_{j}+\Delta_{j+1})\Gamma_{t}*(\Delta_{j}f).

Applying Young’s inequality and (
2.1
) we obtain that

\displaystyle{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left({\left\|K_{2^{j-1}}\right\|}_{1}+{\left\|K_{2^{j}}\right\|}_{1}+{\left\|K_{2^{j+1}}\right\|}_{1}\right){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\exp(-c_{1}t2^{2j-2})+\exp(-c_{1}t2^{2j})+\exp(-c_{1}t2^{2j+2})\right){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle\leq 3\exp(-ct2^{2j}){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}

where
c=c_{1}/4
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

If
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
\beta\in{\mdmathbb R}
, then

{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}+{\left\|\Gamma_{t}^{*}*f\right\|}_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{\beta-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}.

Proof.

Fix
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
If
1\leq q<\infty
, then by Lemma
2.9

\displaystyle{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}^{q}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{q\beta j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}^{q}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{q\beta j}\exp(-ct2^{2j}q){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}^{q}dt

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{q\beta j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}^{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\exp(-ct2^{2j}q)dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{cq}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{(q\beta-2)j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}^{q}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{cq}{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{\beta-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}^{q}.

Note that the operator norm does not depend on
q
since
\lim_{q\to\infty}(cq)^{1/q}=1
.
If
q=\infty
, then by Lemma
2.9

\displaystyle{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{\beta}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}

\displaystyle=\sup_{t>0}\sup_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{\beta j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}\sup_{t>0}2^{\beta j}\exp(-ct2^{2j}){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle=\sup_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{\beta j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}

\displaystyle={\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{\beta}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}.

The estimates for
\Gamma_{t}^{*}*f
can be done by exactly the same way.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
0<\alpha<\infty
.
Then

{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}+{\left\|\Gamma_{t}^{*}*f\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha-2/q}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}.

Proof.

First we consider the case
q=\infty
.
Using the identity
f=\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}\Delta_{j}f
and the triangle inequality and applying Lemma
2.9
, we get

t^{\alpha}{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{p}\leq t^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}\lesssim t^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}\exp(-ct2^{2j}){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}.

Since
2^{-2\alpha j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}\leq{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}
for all
j
, we have

t^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}\exp(-ct2^{2j}){\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}(t2^{2j})^{\alpha}\exp(-ct2^{2j})\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}.

Thus, we have for all
t
,

t^{\alpha}{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{p}\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{p\infty}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}.

This proves the lemma for the case
q=\infty
.

Now we consider the case
q=1
.
By the same way

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}{\left\|\Gamma_{t}*f\right\|}_{p}dt

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}{\left\|\Delta_{j}(\Gamma_{t}*f)\right\|}_{p}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha}\exp(-ct2^{2j})dt

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j\in{\mdmathbb Z}}2^{-2\alpha j-2j}{\left\|\Delta_{j}f\right\|}_{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}(t2^{2j})^{\alpha}\exp(-ct2^{2j})2^{2j}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|f\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha-2}_{p1}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}

since the integral
\int_{0}^{\infty}(t2^{2j})^{\alpha}\exp(-ct2^{2j})2^{2j}dt
does not depend on
j
by a change of variable.
This proves the lemma for the case
q=1
.
Using complex interpolation (5.6.3. Theorem in
[
3
]
)

[L_{\alpha}^{\infty}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})),L_{\alpha}^{1}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))]_{\theta}=L_{\alpha}^{q}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}))

and (6.4.5. Theorem in
[

]

[\dot{B}_{p\infty}^{-2\alpha}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}),\dot{B}_{p1}^{-2\alpha-2}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})]_{\theta}=\dot{B}_{pq}^{-2\alpha-2/q}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})

with

, we get the result.
The estimates for
\Gamma_{t}^{*}*f
can be done by exactly the same way.
∎

Definition 2.12

(Helmholtz projection
{\mdmathbb P}
in
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
)
.

Let
\ {\mathcal{F}}=(F_{kl})_{k,l=1}^{n}
with

F_{kl}=F_{lk}\quad\text{ and }\quad F_{mk}|_{x_{n}=0}=0.

If
f=\mbox{div}{\mathcal{F}}
, then the Helmholtz projection operator
{\mdmathbb P}f
in
{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
is defined by

{\mdmathbb P}f=\mbox{div}\,{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime},

where
{\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}=(F_{km}^{\prime})_{k,m=1}^{n}
is given by

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{nm}

\displaystyle=F_{nm}-\delta_{nm}F_{nn},\ m=1,\cdots,n,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle=F_{\beta\gamma}-\delta_{\beta\gamma}F_{nn}+\sum_{q=1}^{n}D_{x}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{y_{q}}N^{+}(x,y)F_{\beta q}(y)dy

\displaystyle\quad+D_{x}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\Big(D_{y_{n}}N^{+}(x,y)F_{n\beta}(y)-D_{y_{\beta}}N^{+}(x,y)F_{nn}(y)\Big)dy,\quad\beta,\gamma\neq n,

\displaystyle F^{\prime}_{\beta n}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n-1}D_{x}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{x_{n}}N^{+}(x,y)F_{\beta\gamma}(y)dy+D_{x_{\beta}}\int_{R_{+}}D_{x_{n}}N^{+}(x,y)F_{nn}(y)dy

\displaystyle\quad-2F_{\beta n}(x)-2\sum_{\gamma=1}^{n-1}D_{x}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{x}N^{-}(x,y)F_{\beta n}(y)dy\quad\beta\neq n.

Here we used shorthand notations

\displaystyle N^{+}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=N(x-y)+N(x-y^{*}),

\displaystyle N^{-}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=N(x-y)-N(x-y^{*}).

Remark 2.13
.

By Lemma
2.6
for
1<p<\infty

\displaystyle\|{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

(2.2)

Lemma 2.14

(Hardy-Littilewood-Sobolev inequality
[
23
]
)
.

Let
0<\lambda<1
and define for

I_{\lambda}f(t)=\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\lambda}f(s)ds.

Then the operator

is bounded if

3.
Linearized Navier-Stokes equations

In this section, we consider the initial-boundary value problem of the stochastic linearized Navier–Stokes equations in half space

du(t,x)=\big(\Delta u(t,x)-\nabla p(t,x)+{\rm div}\,{\mathcal{F}}(t,x)\big)dt+g(t,x)dB_{t}

(3.1)

in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
with the boundary condition
u|_{x_{n}=0}=0
and the initial condition
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}
.
The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
2\leq p<\infty
,
2<q<\infty
, and
0\leq 2\alpha<1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}
.
Assume
u_{0}\in\dot{\mdmathbb B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
with
\divg u_{0}=0
,
{\mathcal{F}}\in{\mathcal{L}}^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
for some
p_{1}
,
q_{1}
, and
\alpha_{1}
satisfying

q_{1}\leq q,\quad\alpha\leq\alpha_{1}<1-\frac{1}{q_{1}},\quad\bigg(\frac{n}{p_{1}}-\frac{n}{p}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{2}{q_{1}}-\frac{2}{q}\bigg)+2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha)=1,

(3.2)

and
g\in{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}^{q}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
for some
p_{2}
and
\alpha_{2}
satisfying

p_{2}\leq p,\quad\alpha\leq\alpha_{2},\quad\bigg(\frac{n}{p_{2}}-\frac{n}{p}\bigg)+2(\alpha_{2}-\alpha)=1.

Then there is at least one weak solution
u\in{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
to the Stokes equations (
3.1
) satisfying

{\left\|u\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq C_{1}\Big(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{\mdmathbb B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}+{\left\|g\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}^{q}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}+{\left\|{\mathcal{F}}\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big).

(3.3)

Moreover, the solution
u
is unique in
{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
when
0\leq\alpha<1-\frac{1}{q}
.

Before proving the proposition we recall the following solvability lemma for the initial-boundary value problem of the deterministic linearized Navier–Stokes equations in half space.
For the proof we refer the reader to Theorem 1.2 in
[
7
]
for the case
q<\infty
and to Theorem 1.3 in
[
9
]
for the case
q=\infty
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
2<q\leq\infty
, and
0\leq 2\alpha<1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{2}{q}
.
Assume
u_{0}\in\dot{B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
with
\divg u_{0}=0
,
{\mathcal{F}}\in L^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
for some
p_{1}
,
q_{1}
, and
\alpha_{1}
satisfying (
3.2
).
Then there is at least one weak solution
u_{1}\in L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
to the Stokes equations

\partial_{t}u_{1}=\Delta u_{1}-\nabla p_{1}-\mbox{div}{\mathcal{F}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg u_{1}=0

(3.4)

in
(0,\infty)\times{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
with the boundary condition
u_{1}|_{x_{n}=0}=0
and the initial condition
u_{1}|_{t=0}=u_{0}
.
Moreover, the solution satisfies the estimate

{\left\|u_{1}\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{B^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}+\|{\mathcal{F}}\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(3.5)

Moreover, the solution
u_{1}
is unique in
L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
when
0\leq\alpha<1-\frac{1}{q}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

For each fixed
\omega\in\Omega
, we solve the Stokes equation (
3.4
) with the initial data
u_{0}(\omega)
to obtain the unique solution
u_{1}
satisfying the estimate (
3.5
).
If we solve the following stochastic Stokes problem

du_{2}(t,x)=\big(\Delta u_{2}(t,x)-\nabla p_{2}(t,x)\big)dt+g(t,x)dB_{t}

(3.6)

in
\Omega\times(0,\infty)\times{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}
with the boundary condition
u_{2}|_{x_{n}=0}=0
and the initial condition
u_{2}|_{t=0}=0
, then we can take
u=u_{1}+u_{2}
.
We note that the solution
u_{2}
can be represented by

u_{2}(x,t)=\int_{0}^{t}T_{K}g(x,t,s)dB_{s},

where

T_{K}g(x,t,s)=\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}K(x,y,t-s)g(y,s)dy

and the kernel
K=(K_{ij})
is given by

\begin{split}K_{ij}(x,y,t)&=\delta_{ij}\Big(\Gamma(x-y,t)-\Gamma(x-y^{*},t)\Big)\\
&\quad+4(1-\delta_{jn})D_{x_{j}}\int^{x_{n}}_{0}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n-1}}D_{x_{i}}N(x-z)\Gamma(z-y^{*},t)dz\end{split}

(3.7)

for
i,j=1,\cdots,n
.
Here
y^{*}=(y^{\prime},-y_{n})
.
(See
[
21
]
for details.)
We have

\displaystyle{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}{\left\|u_{2}\right\|}_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}^{q}dt

\displaystyle={\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{0}^{t}T_{K}g(x,t,s)dB_{s}\right|}^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dt

\displaystyle\lesssim{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{0}^{t}{\left|T_{K}g(x,t,s)\right|}^{2}ds\right|}^{\frac{p}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{p}}dt

\displaystyle\leq{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|T_{K}g(x,t,s)\right|}^{p}dx\right|}^{\frac{2}{p}}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dt

by the Burkholder–Davis–Gundy inequality (Section 2.7 in
[
18
]
) and Minkowski’s integral inequality.
We now recall the following estimate

\displaystyle\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|T_{K}g(x,t,s)\right|}^{p}dx

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y)f(y,s)dy\right|}^{p}dx

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y^{*})f(y,s)dy\right|}^{p}dx

in Section 3.1 in
[
10
]
.
Then we obtain that, by Young’s inequality and Lemma
2.14
,

\displaystyle{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}{\left\|u_{2}\right\|}_{L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}^{q}dt

\displaystyle\leq{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y)g(y,s)dy\right|}^{p}dx\right|}^{\frac{2}{p}}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\quad+{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}{\left|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\Gamma_{t-s}(x-y^{*})g(y,s)dy\right|}^{p}dx\right|}^{\frac{2}{p}}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha q}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{p_{2}}+\frac{n}{p}}{\left\|g(s)\right\|}_{L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}^{2}ds\right)^{\frac{q}{2}}dt

\displaystyle\leq{\mdmathbb E}\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{\alpha_{2}q}{\left\|g\right\|}_{L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}^{q}dt,

where
\bigg(\frac{n}{p_{2}}-\frac{n}{p}\bigg)+2(\alpha_{2}-\alpha)=1
.
Combining this together with Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that

{\left\|u\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\lesssim{\left\|g\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}^{q}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

Summing this and (
3.5
) we get (
3.3
).
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

We divide the proof into a few steps.

Step 1)

Define the Banach space
{\mathcal{X}}
and its closed subset
{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
as

{\mathcal{X}}:={\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathcal{C}}_{R}:={\left\{u\in{\mathcal{X}}:{\left\|u\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq R\right\}},

where the number
0<R\leq 1
will be determined in later.
Fix
0\leq\alpha<\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}
and an initial data
u_{0}\in\dot{\mdmathbb B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})
with
\divg u_{0}=0
.
Then for each
v\in{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
there exists a unique solution
w(\omega)
, for every
\omega\in\Omega
, of the following problem

dw=\big(\Delta w+\nabla P_{v}-{\rm div}\,(\chi_{v}v\otimes v)\big)dt+gdB_{t}\quad\text{ and }\quad\divg w=0

(4.1)

with the boundary condition
w|_{x_{n}=0}=0
and the initial condition
w|_{t=0}=u_{0}
by Proposition
3.1
.
Thus, we can define the solution map by

S(v)=w.

We introduced the function
\chi_{v(\omega)}:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
defined by

\chi_{v(\omega)}(t):=\theta({\left\|v(\omega)\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}),

where
\theta:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]
is a continuous decreasing function defined by

\theta(s)={\left\{0\vee(2-R^{-1}s)\right\}}\wedge 1.

Step 2)

Choose
q_{1}=\frac{q}{2}
,
p_{1}=\frac{p}{2}
,
\alpha_{1}=2\alpha
so that
\bigg(\frac{n}{p_{1}}-\frac{n}{p}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{2}{q_{1}}-\frac{2}{q}\bigg)+2(\alpha_{1}-\alpha)=1
.
Hence
-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}=-1+\frac{n}{p}
.
For every
\omega\in\Omega
, we get by Hölder’s inequality

{\left\|\chi_{v}v\otimes v\right\|}_{L^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq{\left\|\sqrt{\chi_{v}}v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}^{2}\leq 4R^{2}.

If
v\in{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
, then by using Proposition
3.1
and assumption of Theorem
1.2

\displaystyle{\left\|S(v)\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}

\displaystyle={\left\|w\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq C_{1}(\|u_{0}\|_{\dot{\mdmathbb B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}+{\left\|g\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}_{\alpha_{2}}^{q}(\Omega\times(0,\infty),{\mathcal{P}};L^{p_{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}+4R^{2})

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\delta+4R^{2}).

Now, we can take
\delta=R^{2}
so that

{\left\|S(v)\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq 5C_{1}R^{2}.

(4.2)

Step 3)

We claim that

|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(4.3)

Note first that
\chi_{v(\omega)}(t)=1
if
{\left\|v(\omega)\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq R
and that
\chi_{v(\omega)}(t)=0
if
{\left\|v(\omega)\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\geq 2R
.
To prove the claim, we may consider the following six cases.

(a)

If
{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq R
, then
|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|=|1-1|=0
.

(b)

If
{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq R\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq 2R
, then

\displaystyle|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|

\displaystyle=|1-(2-R^{-1}{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))})|

\displaystyle=R^{-1}|{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}-R|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}|{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(c)

If
{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq R\leq 2R\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}
, then

\displaystyle|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|

\displaystyle=|1-0|\leq\frac{{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}}{2R-R}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(d)

If
R\leq{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq 2R
, then

\displaystyle|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|

\displaystyle=|(2-R^{-1}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))})-(2-R^{-1}{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))})|

\displaystyle=R^{-1}|{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(e)

If
R\leq{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq 2R\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}
, then

\displaystyle|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|

\displaystyle=|(2-R^{-1}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))})-0|

\displaystyle=R^{-1}|2R-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}|{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}-{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}|

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(f)

If
2R\leq{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}
, then
|\chi_{u}-\chi_{v}|=|0-0|=0
.

Thus, (
4.3
) always holds true.

Step 4)

We claim that there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

{\left\|S(u)-S(v)\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}\leq C_{2}R{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{{\mathcal{X}}}.

(4.4)

Note first that

|\chi_{u}u\otimes u-\chi_{v}v\otimes v|\leq|(\chi_{u}-\chi_{v})(u\otimes u)|+|\chi_{v}(u-v)\otimes u|+|\chi_{v}v\otimes(u-v)|.

(a)

\chi_{u}(t)>0
and
\chi_{v}(t)>0

\displaystyle{\left\|(\chi_{u}-\chi_{v})(u\otimes u)\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,t;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4R{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

and

\displaystyle{\left\|\chi_{v}(u-v)\otimes u\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}+{\left\|\chi_{v}v\otimes(u-v)\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big({\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}+{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq 4R{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(b)

If

and
\chi_{v}(t)=0
, then by the similar way

\displaystyle{\left\|(\chi_{u}-\chi_{v})(u\otimes u)\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,t;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\leq R^{-1}{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4R{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,t;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

and
{\left\|\chi_{v}(u-v)\otimes u\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}={\left\|\chi_{v}v\otimes(u-v)\right\|}_{L^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}=0
.

Thus, we use Theorem
3.1
to obtain that

\displaystyle{\left\|S(u)-S(v)\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|\chi_{u}u\otimes u-\chi_{v}v\otimes v\right\|}_{{\mathcal{L}}^{\frac{q}{2}}_{2\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{\frac{p}{2}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim 4R{\left\|u-v\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

This proves the claim (
4.4
).

Step 5)

From (
4.2
)
S({\mathcal{C}}_{R})\subset{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
and from (
4.4
) the map
S
is contractive in the complete space
{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
whenever
R\leq\frac{1}{5C_{1}+C_{2}}
.
Therefore we obtain a unique solution
u
to (
4.1
) in
{\mathcal{C}}_{R}
by the Banach fixed point theorem.
Now, we define the stopping time

\tau(\omega)=\inf{\left\{0\leq T\leq\infty\,:{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\geq R\right\}}.

If
\tau(\omega)<\infty
, then we set
u(t)=0
for
t\geq\tau(\omega)
so that
(u,\tau)
is the unique solution in the sense of Definition
1.1
.

Notice from definition of
\tau
that for all
h\in(0,\infty)
,

\{\omega\,|\,\tau(\omega)\leq h\}\subset\{\omega\,|\,\|u\|_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,h\wedge\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\geq R\}.

Using the Chebyshev inequality we obtain that for all
h\in(0,\infty)
,

{\mdmathbb P}(\tau=0)\leq{\mdmathbb P}(\{\tau\leq h\})\leq\frac{1}{R}{\mdmathbb E}\|u\|_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,h\wedge\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

Since
\|u\|_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,h\wedge\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\leq\|u\|_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,h;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\rightarrow 0
as
h\to 0
almost surely, we have

{\mdmathbb P}(\tau=0)=0\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mdmathbb P}(\tau>0)=1.

By the definition of the stopping time
\tau
we have

{\mdmathbb P}(\tau<\infty)={\mdmathbb E}1_{\left\{\tau<\infty\right\}}\leq{\mdmathbb E}\Bigg(1_{\left\{\tau<\infty\right\}}\frac{{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,T;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}}{R}\Bigg)\leq\frac{1}{R}{\mdmathbb E}\Big(\lim_{T\to\infty}{\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,T\wedge\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big)

By Fatou’s Lemma and (
4.2
) it is bounded by

\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{R}{\mdmathbb E}\Big({\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,T\wedge\tau;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{R}{\mdmathbb E}\Big({\left\|u\right\|}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\infty;L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\Big)\leq\frac{5C_{1}R^{2}}{R}=5C_{1}R.

If
R<\frac{1}{5C_{1}+C_{2}}\wedge\frac{\epsilon}{5C_{1}}
, then

{\mdmathbb P}(\tau=\infty)=1-{\mdmathbb P}(\tau<\infty)\geq 1-\epsilon.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Using the estimates (

\displaystyle{\left\|v\right\|}_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim{\left\|\tilde{u}_{0}\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}\lesssim{\left\|u_{0}\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha-\frac{2}{q}}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

Step 3)

For
V
we have the following estimate.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
\alpha>0
,

\|V\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\lesssim\|u\|^{2}_{L^{q}_{\alpha}(0,\tau(\omega);L^{p}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

Proof.

We can decompose
V
as the sum

V(x,t)=V_{1}(x,t)+V_{2}(x,t)+V_{3}(x,t),

where
V_{1}
,
V_{2}
and
V_{3}
are defined by

\displaystyle V_{1i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=D_{x^{\prime}}\big({\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}(x,t)-{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}(x,t)\big)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{y^{\prime}}(\Gamma(x-y,t-s)-\Gamma(x-y^{*},t-s))F^{\prime}_{i}(y,s)dyds,

\displaystyle V_{2i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=4\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}(N(x-y)-N(x-y^{*}))D_{y^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}_{j}(y,t)dy

\displaystyle\quad-2\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{n}}(N(x-y)-N(x-y^{*}))\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}R_{j}^{\prime}D_{y^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}_{j}(y,t)dy,

\displaystyle V_{3i}(x,t)

\displaystyle=-4\delta_{in}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}R^{\prime}_{j}D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}_{j}(x,t),

with

.
Notice that

\begin{split}{\left\|V_{k}(t)\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}&\leq{\left\|V_{1}(t)\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}+{\left\|V_{2}(t)\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}+{\left\|V_{3}(t)\right\|}_{\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}\\

\|D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}+\|D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}\|_{L^{q}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{-2\alpha}_{pq}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}\lesssim\|F^{\prime}\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{\alpha_{1}}(0,\infty;L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))}.

(5.5)

Step 4)

Now we prove the technical estimate (
5.5
).
Since the estimate of
D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}^{*}F^{\prime}
can be derived by exactly the same way as that of
D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}
, we focus on estimating the term
D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}
, which is done by duality argument.
If
\psi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(0,\infty;\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}0}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}))
, then by Fubini’s theorem

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}(x,t)\psi(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}(x,t)\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}\Bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}F^{\prime}(y,s)D_{y^{\prime}}\Gamma(x-y,t-s)dyds\Bigg)\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}F^{\prime}(y,s)\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}D_{y^{\prime}}\Gamma(x-y,t-s)\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)dxdydsdt.

Integrating by parts and using Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}F^{\prime}(y,s)\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}D_{y^{\prime}}\Gamma(x-y,t-s)\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)dxdy\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}F^{\prime}(y,s)\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}}\Gamma(y-x,t-s)D_{x^{\prime}}\widetilde{\psi}(x,t)dxdy\Bigg|

\displaystyle=\Bigg|\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}F^{\prime}(y,s)\Gamma_{t-s}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(y,t)dy\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|F^{\prime}(s)\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}{\left\|\Gamma_{t-s}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

Using the semigroup property of the heat kernel and Young’s convolution inequality, we get

\displaystyle{\left\|\Gamma_{t-s}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\Gamma_{t-s}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle={\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p_{1}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}\right\|}_{L^{r}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}{\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(t-s)^{-\frac{n}{2}(1-\frac{1}{r})}{\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})}{\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p^{\prime}},

where
1+1/p_{1}^{\prime}=1/r+1/p^{\prime}
.
Since Lemma
2.11
yields

{\left\|\Gamma_{(t-s)/2}*D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\right\|}_{p^{\prime}}\lesssim(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\|D^{\prime}\widetilde{\psi}(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{-1+2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})}\lesssim(t-s)^{-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\|\widetilde{\psi}(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n})},

we obtain that

{\left\|\Gamma_{t-s}*D^{\prime}\psi(t)\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}\lesssim(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\|\psi(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}.

Combining the estimate above and then using Hölder’s equality, we get

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{{\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+}}D_{x^{\prime}}{\mathcal{U}}F^{\prime}(x,t)\psi(x,t)dxdt\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{t}{\left\|F^{\prime}(s)\right\|}_{L^{p_{1}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}(t-s)^{\frac{n}{2}(\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}})-\frac{1}{2}+\alpha}\|\psi(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}dsdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\lambda}f(t)\|\psi(t)\|_{\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})}dt

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|I_{\lambda}f\right\|}_{L^{q}(0,\infty)}{\left\|\psi\right\|}_{L^{q^{\prime}}(0,\infty;{\dot{B}^{2\alpha}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}({\mdmathbb R}^{n}_{+})})},

If

,

Step 6)

Fianlly, we prove the estimate (

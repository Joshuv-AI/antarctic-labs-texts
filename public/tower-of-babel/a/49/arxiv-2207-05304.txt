Embedding procedure and wormhole solutions in f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity
Zinnat Hassan, G. Mustafa, Joao R.L. Santos, P. K. Sahoo

Abstract
An intriguing solution that appears in General Relativity (GR) but has not been observed so far is the wormhole. This exotic solution describes a topological bridge connecting two distinct universes or two different points in the same universe. It is known that the traversable wormhole solutions violate all the energy conditions in GR, resulting in their instability. In this work, we are going to unveil new wormhole solutions for f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) gravity where Q Q is the non-metricity scalar, which is responsible for the gravitational interaction. The energy conditions to constraint these wormhole solutions were derived using the embedding procedure. This procedure consists of rewriting the density and the pressures of the solutions as those presented by General Relativity. Then, the nontrivial contributions coming from new theories of gravity are embedded into the effective equations for density and pressures. Along with our approach, we carefully analyze two families of f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) models and we used two different shape functions to build the wormholes solutions for each of these f ⁡ ( Q ) f(Q) models. We are going to present new scenarios with the possibility of traversable wormholes satisfying SEC or DEC energy conditions in the presence of exotic matter.

2
The
f(Q)
gravity

In this section, we are going to briefly present some generalities about the
f(Q)
gravity. The so-called symmetric teleparallel gravity or
f(Q)
gravity was introduced by Jimenez et al.
[
14
]
. The action for this modified gravity is given by

\mathcal{S}=\int\frac{1}{2}\,f(Q)\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x+\int\mathcal{L}_{m}\,\sqrt{-g}\,d^{4}x\,,

(1)

where
g
is the determinant of the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
, and
\mathcal{L}_{m}
is Lagrangian density of matter.

The nonmetricity tensor is given by

Q_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\bigtriangledown_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\lambda\mu}g_{\beta\nu}-\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\lambda\nu}g_{\mu\beta},

(2)

where
\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
is the affine connection and it can be decomposed into the following components,

\Gamma^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=\{^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\}+K^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+L^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu},

(3)

where
\{^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\}
is the Levi-Civita connection defined by

\{^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}\}=\frac{1}{2}g^{\beta\sigma}\left(\partial_{\mu}g_{\sigma\nu}+\partial_{\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}-\partial_{\sigma}g_{\mu\nu}\right),

(4)

the disformation
L^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
is defined by

L^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}Q^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}-Q_{(\mu\,\,\,\,\,\,\nu)}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\beta},

(5)

and
K^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
is the contortion written as

K^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=\frac{1}{2}T^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}+T_{(\mu\,\,\,\,\,\,\nu)}^{\,\,\,\,\,\,\beta},

(6)

with the torsion tensor
T^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
defined as the anti-symmetric part of the affine connection,
T^{\beta}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=2\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,[\mu\nu]}
.

One can also establish a superpotential related to the nonmetricity tensor as

\displaystyle P^{\alpha}\;_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\left[-Q^{\alpha}\;_{\mu\nu}+2Q_{(\mu}\;^{\alpha}\;{}_{\nu)}+Q^{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}\right.

(7)

\displaystyle\left.-\tilde{Q}^{\alpha}g_{\mu\nu}-\delta^{\alpha}_{(\mu}Q_{\nu)}\right],

where

Q_{\alpha}=Q_{\alpha}\;^{\mu}\;{}_{\mu},\;\tilde{Q}_{\alpha}=Q^{\mu}\;_{\alpha\mu}.

(8)

are two independence traces.

which enable us to define the nonmetricity scalar as

Q=-Q_{\alpha\mu\nu}\,P^{\alpha\mu\nu}.

(9)

In order to find the field equations for this theory of gravity, we can set that the action (
1
) is constant in respect to variations over the metric tensor
g_{\mu\nu}
, resulting in

\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\bigtriangledown_{\gamma}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}\,P^{\gamma}\;_{\mu\nu}\right)+\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}f\\
+f_{Q}\left(P_{\mu\gamma i}\,Q_{\nu}\;^{\gamma i}-2\,Q_{\gamma i\mu}\,P^{\gamma i}\;_{\nu}\right)=-T_{\mu\nu},

(10)

where
f_{Q}=\frac{df}{dQ}
, and
T_{\mu\,\nu}
is the standard energy-momentum tensor, whose form is

T_{\mu\nu}=-\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{m}\right)}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}.

(11)

Moreover, by taking action (
1
) constant in respect to variations over the connection, we are able to derive the extra constraint

\bigtriangledown_{\mu}\bigtriangledown_{\nu}\left(\sqrt{-g}\,f_{Q}\,P^{\gamma}\;_{\mu\nu}\right)=0.

(12)

The torsionless and curvatureless make the affine connection
\Gamma
have the following form
[
14
]

\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=\left(\frac{\partial x^{\lambda}}{\partial\xi^{\beta}}\right)\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\nu}\xi^{\beta}.

(13)

where
\xi
is the frame of transformation. This transformation of
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}
is the core of the covariant formulation of
f(Q)
gravity, which preserves the Lorentz covariance under the nonmetricity framework. Two excellent reviews about this calculation were nicely presented by Xu et al. in
[
21
]
, and by Jimenez et al.
[
22
]

In this study, we have considered a special coordinate choice, the so-called coincident gauge, so that
\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\,\,\,\mu\nu}=0
.

Then, the nonmetricity (
2
) reduces to

Q_{\lambda\mu\nu}=\partial_{\lambda}g_{\mu\nu},

(14)

and thereby vastly simplifies the calculation since only the metric is the fundamental variable. However, in this case, the action no longer remains diffeomorphism invariant, except for standard General Relativity
[
22
]
. One can use the covariant formulation of
f(Q)
gravity to avoid such an issue. Since the affine connection in Eq. (
13
) is purely inertial, one could use the covariant formulation by first determining affine connection in the absence of gravity
[
23
]
.

Using Eq. (

(19)

3.1
The Embedding Procedure

In order to successfully embed the non-trivial contributions from
f(Q)
gravity into the energy density and pressures related to the traversable wormhole solution, let us rewrite Eqs. (
18
) - (
20
) in the following forms

\rho=\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left[2f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}+f_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{b^{{}^{\prime}}}{r-b}\right)+\right.\\
\left.f\,\frac{r^{2}}{2(r-b)}\right],

(21)

p_{r}=\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left[f_{Q}\left(\frac{2\,b-r}{r(r-b)}\right)-f\,\frac{r^{2}}{2(r-b)}\right],

(22)

p_{t}=\frac{1}{2\,r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left[-2\,f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}+f_{Q}\,\left(\frac{r\,b^{{}^{\prime}}+b-2\,r}{r(r-b)}\right)-\right.\\
\left.f\,\frac{r^{2}}{2(r-b)}\right].

(23)

Comparing Eqs. (
18
) - (
20
) with the Morris-Throne traversable wormhole equations for General Relativity
[
26
]
, we are able to find the following effective density and pressures

\tilde{\rho}=\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2\,f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right)-\frac{1}{f_{Q}}\left(\rho-\frac{f}{2}\right)\,,

(24)

\tilde{P_{r}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1}{r^{2}}+\frac{1}{f_{Q}}\left(P_{r}+\frac{f}{2}\right)\right)\,,

(25)

\tilde{P_{t}}=-\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right)-\frac{1}{f_{Q}}\left(P_{t}+\frac{f}{2}\right)\,.

(26)

These previous relations unveil that the nonmetricity contributions are embedded into
\tilde{\rho}
,
\tilde{P_{r}}
, and
\tilde{P_{t}}
, and are crucial to properly determine the different set of energy conditions for the wormholes.

3.2
Energy Conditions

The energy conditions are fundamental tools for modified theories of gravity, since they enable us to carefully analyze the casual and geodesic structure of space-time. One path to derive such conditions is through the Raychaudhuri equations, which describe the action of congruence and attractiveness of the gravity for timelike, spacelike, or lightlike curves.
Since we are working with an anisotropic fluid, the energy conditions for standard GR are such that

\bullet
Strong energy conditions (SEC) if
\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{j}}\geq 0
,
\tilde{\rho}+\sum_{j}\tilde{P_{j}}\geq 0
,
\forall j
.

\bullet
Dominant energy conditions (DEC) if
\tilde{\rho}\geq 0
,
\tilde{\rho}\pm\tilde{P_{j}}\geq 0
,
\forall j
.

\bullet
Weak energy conditions (WEC) if
\tilde{\rho}\geq 0
,
\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{j}}\geq 0
,
\forall j
.

\bullet
Null energy condition (NEC) if
\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{j}}\geq 0
,
\forall j
.

where
j=r,t
and
P_{j}
and
\rho
denotes the pressures and energy density, respectively.

So, taking the effective density and pressures from Eqs. (
24
)-(
26
), we yield to

\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{r}}=-\frac{1}{2\,f_{Q}}\left(\rho+P_{r}\right)+\frac{1}{2r}\left(-\frac{2}{r}+\frac{r\,b^{{}^{\prime}}+b}{r^{2}}\right)\\
+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right);

(27)

\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{t}}=-\frac{1}{f_{Q}}\left(\rho+P_{t}\right)+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}};

(28)

\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{P_{r}}=-\frac{1}{2\,f_{Q}}\left(\rho-P_{r}\right)+\frac{f}{2\,f_{Q}}+\frac{r\,b^{{}^{\prime}}+b}{2\,r^{3}}\\
+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right);

(29)

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{P_{t}}=-\frac{1}{2\,f_{Q}}

(30)

\displaystyle\times\left(\rho-P_{t}\right)+\frac{f}{f_{Q}}+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{2}{r}+\frac{3\,f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right);

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{r}}+2\tilde{P_{t}}=-\frac{1}{2\,f_{Q}}

\displaystyle\times\,\left(\rho+P_{r}+2\,P_{t}\right)+\frac{b}{r^{3}}-\frac{f}{2\,f_{Q}}.

(31)

Therefore, the previous equations enable us to compute the following set of energy conditions for wormholes at
f(Q)
gravity:

WEC:
\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{\rho}\geq 0\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho\geq 0\,\,\,
with
\,\,f_{Q}\leq 0
,
\,\,\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{2\,f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right)+\frac{f}{2\,f_{Q}}\geq 0.

NEC:
\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{r}}\geq 0\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho+P_{r}\geq 0\,\,\,
with
f_{Q}\leq 0
,
\,\,\,\,\,\frac{1}{2r}\left(-\frac{2}{r}+\frac{r\,b^{{}^{\prime}}+b}{r^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right)\geq 0\,\,\,\,
and
\,\,\,\tilde{\rho}+\tilde{P_{t}}\geq 0\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\ \rho+P_{t}\geq 0\,\,\,
with
\,\,\,f_{Q}\leq 0
,
\,\,\,\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\geq 0

\,\,\,\,\,\tilde{\rho}-\tilde{P_{r}}\geq 0\,\,\Rightarrow\,\,\,\,\,\,\rho-P_{r}\geq 0\,\,\,
with
\,\,\,f_{Q}\leq 0

\,\,\,\,\frac{r\,b^{{}^{\prime}}+b}{2\,r^{3}}+\frac{1}{r}\left(1-\frac{b}{r}\right)\left(\frac{1}{r}+\frac{f_{QQ}Q^{{}^{\prime}}}{f_{Q}}\right)\geq 0\,\,\,\,

5
Results and discussions

This letter carefully investigated the viability of wormholes solutions in
f(Q)
gravity, considering specific sets of shape functions, and an energy-momentum tensor for an anisotropic fluid.

In order to find the energy conditions, we made use of the constraints imposed by the Raychaudhuri Eq. using the embedding procedure introduced by Mandal et al.
[
18
]
. The constraints derived together with the restrictions over the shape functions allowed us to fix our free parameters, yielding to wormholes solutions that satisfy at least one energy condition for positive energy density. The present methodology using the embedding procedure also complements the discussions recent carried by some of us in
[
19
]
.

Firstly we derive the energy conditions for model I, considering both shape functions (SF-1 and SF-2). For SF-1, we saw that NEC is violated for the radial pressure for any value of
\beta
with
m<1
. For
m>1
, NEC will be satisfied. Also, DEC is satisfied in the entire spacetime and SEC is violated for bigger values of
r
. For SF-2, we have found that all the energy conditions are violated close to the throat for
r<1
. Such features are the same presented by wormholes solutions in GR non-minimally coupled with scalar fields. These behaviors indicate the possibility of stable traversable wormhole solutions for
f(Q)
gravity, with the exotic matter at their throats.

For model-II, we consider logarithmic function of
Q
and studied the energy conditions for two different shape functions. For SF-1, we observed that NEC is violated for both pressures close to the throat, for
\alpha\geq-3.5
and
m\geq 2
. Besides, we find that DEC is violated in the neighborhood of the throat and SEC is violated for
\alpha\leq-3.5
. Again for SF-2, we found that NEC is violated for the tangential pressure close to the throat if we work with a small range of positive values of
\alpha
and for negative values of
\beta
. DEC is also violated in the vicinity of the WH throat whereas SEC is satisfied for
\alpha\approx 1
or for
r
close to the throat. Wormholes that do not obey DEC were found as solutions of the so-called
f(R,T)
gravity
[
30
]
. Although, the wormholes derived in
[
30
]
also satisfy NEC, indicating that they do not have exotic matter at their throats. Consequently, our work presents an interesting scenario with the possibility of stable exotic traversable wormhole solutions which satisfy SEC in the presence of exotic matter. The standard General Relativity approach can be recovered if we take
\alpha=\beta=0
in both
f(Q)
families
[
14
]
. In such cases, all energy conditions for traversable wormholes would be violated as pointed by Morris and Thorne
[
10
]
.

The methodology here presented can be easily applied to new families of
f(Q)
as well as for several other theories of gravity, opening a new path in deriving wormhole solutions, and to constraint them through energy conditions. Moreover, we could also work with a different equation of state using the Chaplygin gas
[
30
]
or the Van der Waals fluid. It would also be interesting to constraint our free parameters by combining the energy conditions together with another noncommutative geometry, as recently approached by Hassan et al.
[
31
]
.

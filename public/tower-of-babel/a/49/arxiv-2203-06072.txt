1.
Introduction

In the representation theory of finite groups there are important conjectures which predict a strong relationship between the representations of a finite group and those of its
\ell
-local subgroups, for a prime
\ell
. The Alperin–McKay conjecture is one of these conjectures which refines the McKay conjecture using the notion of
\ell
-blocks introduced by Brauer.

In
[
11
,
19
]
the McKay and the Alperin–McKay conjecture have been reduced to the verification of a so-called
inductive condition
for all finite simple groups and primes
\ell
.
Moreover, to prove the Brauer height zero conjecture it remains to validate the inductive Alperin–McKay (iAM) condition for all simple groups
[
15
]
.
Using the inductive McKay condition, Malle and Späth established the McKay conjecture for
\ell=2

[
13
]
, while for odd primes due to the work of multiple authors the inductive McKay condition only remains open for simple groups of type
{\mathrm{D}}
.

For the iAM condition
[
19
]
dealt with simple groups of Lie type, when
\ell
is the defining characteristic, and for alternating groups, when the prime
\ell
is odd.
Additionally
[
12
]
dealt with simple groups in certain low rank cases, while
[
7
]
dealt with the blocks of maximal defect in type
\mathrm{A}
.
In
[
3
]
Späth and the author provided a criterion to verify the iAM condition which is tailored towards groups of Lie type and was applied in type
{\mathrm{A}}
.
In
[
6
]
, this criterion was successfully applied to the groups
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
for primes
\ell\mid q-1
.
Furthermore in
[
17
]
Ruhstorfer used the results in type
{\mathrm{A}}
with a reduction to quasi-isolated blocks to prove the iAM condition for all blocks in type
\mathrm{A}
whenever
\ell\geq 5
.
Recently Ruhstorfer and the author have established the Alperin–McKay conjecture for all
2
-blocks of maximal defect
[
2
]
.
There has also been a large amount of success in verifying the inductive (blockwise) Alperin weight condition via an analogous criterion given in
[
4
]
.

The labelling for blocks in groups of Lie type by
d
-cuspidal pairs,
({{\mathbf{L}}},\lambda)
as in
[
5
]
, provides strong evidence that the normaliser of
{{\mathbf{L}}}
should be a suitable local subgroup for the iAM condition (this was proven for type
\mathrm{A}
in
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
).
The main result of this paper validates condition
[
3
, Theorem 2.4 (iv)]
for finite groups of Lie type in type
\mathrm{C}
without the assumption that
\ell\mid q-1
:

Theorem 1.1
.

{thmA}

Let
p
be a prime,
{{\mathbf{G}}}:=\operatorname{Sp}_{2n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
,
\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}:=\operatorname{CSp}_{2n}(\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p})
, and

F_{p}:{\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}}\rightarrow{\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}}
a Frobenius endomorphism defining an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-structure.
For some positive integer
m
let
F=F_{p}^{m}
and
D:=\langle\widehat{F_{p}}\rangle
where
\widehat{F}_{p}
is the induced automorphism of
F_{p}
on
{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}
.
Assume that, for some positive integer
d
, the subgroup
{{\mathbf{L}}}
is a
d
-split Levi subgroup of
({{{\mathbf{G}}}},F)
and set

N:={\rm N}_{{{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}}}({{\bf L}})
,
\widetilde{N}:={\rm N}_{{\widetilde{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}}}({{{\mathbf{L}}}})
, and
\widehat{N}:={\rm N}_{{{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}}\rtimes D}({{\bf L}})
.
If
\psi\in\operatorname{Irr}(N)
lies above a
d
-cuspidal character of
{\bf L}^{F}
, then there exists a
\widetilde{N}
-conjugate
\psi_{0}
of
\psi
such that

(i)

{thm11a}

(\widetilde{N}\widehat{N})_{\psi_{0}}=\widetilde{N}_{\psi_{0}}\widehat{N}_{\psi_{0}}
, and

(ii)

\psi_{0}
extends to
\widehat{N}_{\psi_{0}}
.

To prove Theorem
1.1
, the criterion proven in
[
3
, Theorem 4.1]
is considered.
Given
{{\mathbf{L}}}
a
d
-split Levi subgroup, the main step is to paramterise the characters of its normaliser via
{{\mathbf{L}}}^{F}
and the corresponding relative Weyl group.
Then with this parametrisation investigate the action of automorphisms.
In particular, the following is proven:

Theorem 1.2
.

{StarConddSpLevi}

Assume the setup of Theorem
1.1
and set
W=N/{\bf L}^{F}
and
\widehat{W}:=\widehat{N}/{\bf L}^{F}
.

(a)

{thm12a}

There exists a
\widehat{W}
-equivariant extension map with respect to
{\bf L}^{F}\lhd N
.

(b)

{thm12b}

Let
\lambda
be a
d
-cuspidal character of
{\bf L}^{F}
and
\widetilde{\lambda}\in\operatorname{Irr}(({\bf L}Z({\bf\widetilde{G}}))^{F}\mid\lambda)
and
\xi_{0}\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{\widetilde{\lambda}})
.
Then each character
\xi\in\operatorname{Irr}(W_{\lambda}\mid\xi_{0})
is
\operatorname{N}_{\widehat{W}}(W_{\widetilde{\lambda}},W_{\lambda})_{\xi_{0}}
-invariant.

In addition to the main theorem contributing to the validation of the iAM condition for
{\rm PSp}_{2n}(q)
, there are other consequences.
For groups of Lie type with abelian Sylow
\ell
-subgroups, suitable bijections for the iAM condition and the blockwise Alperin weight condition were constructed by Malle
[
12
, Theorem 2.9]
assuming
1.2(a)
for analogous local subgroups.
As a consequence of the proof of Theorem
1.1
, the Alperin–McKay conjecture holds via
[
12
, Theorem 2.9]
for
{\rm Sp}_{2n}(q)
at primes
\ell\geq 5
where the Sylow
\ell
-subgroups are abelian.
Beyond the scope of the Alperin–McKay conjecture, recently Rossi
[
16
, Theorem 8.3]
showed for a
d
-cuspidal pair
({{\mathbf{L}}},\lambda)
that condition
1.2(a)
yields an equivariant bijection between the
d
-Harish-Chandra series
\mathcal{E}({{\mathbf{G}}}^{F},({{\mathbf{L}}},\lambda))
and
\operatorname{Irr}(\operatorname{N}_{{{\mathbf{G}}}^{F}}({{\mathbf{L}}})\mid\lambda)
.
Moreover, condition
1.1(i)
forms an ingredient for validating the inductive Dade condition for
{\rm PSp}_{2n}(q)

[
16
, Theorem 9.2]
.

This paper is organised in the following way: Section
2
outlines some notation required for sets and signed permutations, and includes some results that will be required in the later sections.
Section
3
is used to parametrise
d
-split Levi subgroups in any classical type via sets.
In Section
4
the restriction to type
\mathrm{C}
is made and an explicit description of
d
-split Levi subgroups and their normalisers is provided.
Moreover, some important properties of the irreducible characters of these subgroups are discussed.
Finally in Section
5
the Clifford theory arising in the relative Weyl group is studied in order to complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

2.
Preliminary results

{Prelim}

2.A.
Sets and permutations

The structure of
d
-split Levi subgroups and their normalisers will be described explicitly via certain permutations and signed permutations.
In this section the required notation will be set up and some properties will be stated.

2.A.1.
Signed permutations

Let
\Omega
be a finite set, then the symmetric group on
\Omega
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{S}(\Omega)
.
In particular, if
|\Omega|=n
, then
\mathfrak{S}(\Omega)\cong\mathfrak{S}_{n}
, the symmetric group on
n
objects.
For a finite subset
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathbb{N}_{>0}
denote by
\pm\mathcal{J}:=\{\pm i\mid i\in\mathcal{J}\}
and the group of signed permutations on
\mathcal{J}
is defined as

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})&:=&\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}(\pm\mathcal{J})\mid\sigma(-i)=-\sigma(i)\text{ for all }i\in\mathcal{J}\}.\\
\end{array}

As with symmetric groups, for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, denote by
\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}
the signed permutations on
\underline{n}:=\{1,\dots,n\}
.
If
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
|\mathcal{J}|=n
, then
\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})\cong\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}
.

Given a finite subset
\mathcal{J}\subset\mathbb{N}_{>0}

and a cycle
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})\in\mathfrak{S}(\pm\mathcal{J})
, define the cycle
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})^{\prime}:=(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{r})
.
Then for
m
disjoint cycles
\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{m}\in\mathfrak{S}(\pm\mathcal{J})
define
(\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{m})^{\prime}:=\sigma_{1}^{\prime}\cdots\sigma_{m}^{\prime}
.
This yields a natural embedding of
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
into
\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})
by identifying an element
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
with the element
\tau\tau^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})
.
It follows from the definition that
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})
if and only if it is a product of elements of the form
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r},-a_{1},\dots,-a_{r})
or
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{r})
for some
a_{1},\dots,a_{r}\in\pm\mathcal{J}
.
Therefore the map which sends both
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r})(-a_{1},\dots,-a_{r})
and
(a_{1},\dots,a_{r},-a_{1},\dots,-a_{r})
to
(|a_{1}|,\dots,|a_{r}|)\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
extends to a homomorphism
\overline{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}a}:\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})\rightarrow\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
with
{\rm ker}(\overline{\color[rgb]{1,1,1}a})=\langle(i,-i)\mid i\in\mathcal{J}\rangle\cong C_{2}^{|\mathcal{J}|}
.
For any element
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})
, denote by
\overline{\sigma}
its image in
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
.
For
\tau\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
it follows that
\tau=\overline{\tau\tau^{\prime}}
and thus

\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J})=\langle(i,-i)\mid i\in\mathcal{J}\rangle\rtimes\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})\cong C_{2}\wr\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J}).

Lemma 2.1
.

{CentsgnSymm}

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}
.
Then

\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}(\sigma)\cong\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}C_{2i}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{m_{1,i}}\right)\times\left(\prod_{i=1,\text{ odd}}^{n}C_{2i}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{m_{2,i}}\right)\times\left(\prod_{i=1,\text{ even}}^{n}(C_{2}\times C_{i})\wr\mathfrak{S}_{m_{2,i}}\right),

where
m_{1,i}
equals the number of disjoint cycles
\alpha
of
\sigma
with length
2i
and
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}
while
2m_{2,i}
is the number of disjoint cycles
\beta
of
\sigma
with length
i
and
\beta\neq\beta^{\prime}
.

Proof.

After conjugation any element
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}
can be written as

\sigma=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i,1}\cdots\alpha_{i,m_{1,i}}\right)\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}(\beta_{i,1}\beta_{i,1}^{\prime})\cdots(\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}^{\prime})\right)

where each
\beta_{i,j}=\overline{\beta_{i,j}}
and
\alpha_{i,j}=(a_{j,1},\dots,a_{j,i},-a_{j,1},\dots,-a_{j,i})
with
\overline{\alpha_{i,j}}=(a_{j,1},\dots,a_{j,i})
.
Set
\mathcal{I}_{\alpha_{i,j}}:=\{k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}\mid\alpha_{i,j}(k)\neq k\}\subset\{1,\dots,n\}
and
\delta_{\alpha_{i,j}}:=\prod_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{\alpha_{i,j}}}(k,-k)
and similarly define
\mathcal{I}_{\beta_{i,j}}
and
\delta_{\beta_{i,j}}
.
The kernel of the projection map from
\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}(\sigma)
to
\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(\overline{\sigma})
is the subgroup generated by the involutions
\delta_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
\delta_{\beta_{i,j}}
.
Furthermore, for
\mathcal{I}_{1,i}:=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m_{1,i}}\mathcal{I}_{\alpha_{i,j}}
and
\mathcal{I}_{1,i}:=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{m_{2,i}}\mathcal{I}_{\beta_{i,j}}
it follows that

\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}(\sigma)=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{1,i})}(\alpha_{i,1}\cdots\alpha_{i,m_{1,i}})\right)\times\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{2,i})}((\beta_{i,1}\beta_{i,1}^{\prime})\cdots(\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}^{\prime}))\right).

For
1\leq i\leq n
and
1\leq j\leq m_{1,i}
set
\tau_{1,i,j}:=(a_{j,1},a_{j+1,1})\cdots(a_{j,i},a_{j+1,i})\in\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{I}_{1,i})}(\overline{\alpha}_{i,1}\cdots\overline{\alpha}_{i,m_{1,i}})
which satisfies
\overline{\alpha}_{i,j}^{\tau_{1,i,j}}=\overline{\alpha}_{i,j+1}
and
[\overline{\alpha}_{i,k},\tau_{1,i,j}]=1
whenever
k\neq j,j+1
.
Direct calculation shows that

\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{1,i})}(\alpha_{i,1}\cdots\alpha_{i,m_{1,i}})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{1,i}}\langle\alpha_{i,j}\rangle\right)\rtimes\langle\tau_{1,i,j}\tau_{1,i,j}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq j<m_{1,i}\rangle

as this group surjects onto
\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{I}_{1,i})}(\overline{\alpha}_{i,1}\cdots\overline{\alpha}_{i,m_{1,i}})
with kernel generated by the
\alpha_{i,j}^{i}=\delta_{\alpha_{i,j}}
.

Similarly define involutions
\tau_{2,i,j}\in\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{I}_{2,i})}(\beta_{i,1}\cdots\beta_{i,m_{2,i}})
such that
\beta_{i,j}^{\tau_{2,i,j}}=\beta_{i,j+1}
and
[\beta_{i,k},\tau_{1,i,j}]=1
whenever
k\neq j,j+1
.
As before direct calculation shows that

\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{I}_{2,i})}(\beta_{i,1}\beta_{i,1}^{\prime}\cdots\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}\beta_{i,m_{2,i}}^{\prime})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{2,i}}\langle\beta_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}^{\prime},\delta_{\beta_{i,j}}\rangle\right)\rtimes\langle\tau_{2,i,j}\tau_{2,i,j}^{\prime}\mid 1\leq j<m_{2,i}\rangle.

The result now follows by observing

\langle\beta_{i,j}\beta_{i,j}^{\prime},\delta_{\beta_{i,j}}\rangle\cong\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}C_{2i}&i\text{ odd}\\
C_{i}\times C_{2}&i\text{ even}\\
\end{array}\right.\qed

2.A.2.
Set partitions and permutations

{PermPart}

Given a subset
\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, with
|\mathcal{J}|=k
, write
\mathcal{J}=\{\mathcal{J}(1),\dots,\mathcal{J}(k)\}
with
\mathcal{J}(i)<\mathcal{J}(i+1)
and set

w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}:=\big(\mathcal{J}(1),\mathcal{J}(2),\dots,\mathcal{J}(k),-\mathcal{J}(1),-\mathcal{J}(2),\dots,-\mathcal{J}(k)\big)\in\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\mathcal{J}).

If
\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}\subset\mathbb{N}_{>0}
with
|\mathcal{J}|=|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|=k
and
\mathcal{J}\cap\mathcal{J}^{\prime}=\emptyset
set

\tau_{\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}:=\prod_{i=1}^{k}(\mathcal{J}(i),\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(i))(-\mathcal{J}(i),-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(i))=\prod_{r=0}^{k-1}\big((\mathcal{J}(1),\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(1))(-\mathcal{J}(1),-\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(1))\big)^{(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}})^{r}}.

For a collection of sets
\mathcal{I}:=\{\mathcal{J}\mid\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0}\}
and a positive integer
i\in\mathbb{N}{>0}
set
\mathcal{I}_{i}:=\{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}\mid|\mathcal{J}|=i\}
and
\underline{\mathcal{I}}:=\bigcup_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}\mathcal{J}
.
A collection of sets
\mathcal{I}=\{\mathcal{J}\mid\mathcal{J}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0}\}
is called a partition of
n
, denoted by
\mathcal{I}\vdash\underline{n}
, if
\underline{\mathcal{I}}=\underline{n}
and the sets
\mathcal{J}
are disjoint.
For a partition
\mathcal{I}\vdash\underline{n}
set
w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}}:=\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}
and
w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}_{s}}:=\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}_{s}}w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}}
.
By the proof of Lemma
2.1
, it follows that
\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\underline{\mathcal{I}}_{i})}(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}_{s}})
with

\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}(\underline{\mathcal{I}}_{i})}(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{I}_{s}})=\left(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{i}}\langle w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}_{i,j}}\rangle\right)\rtimes\langle\tau_{\mathcal{J}_{i,j}}\mid 1\leq j\leq m_{i}-1\rangle\cong\prod_{i=1}^{n}C_{2i}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{m_{i}},

where
\mathcal{I}_{i}=\{\mathcal{J}_{i,1},\dots,\mathcal{J}_{i,m_{i}}\}
and
\tau_{\mathcal{J}_{i,j}}:=\tau_{\mathcal{J}_{i,j},\mathcal{J}_{i,j+1}}
for
1\leq j<m_{i}
.
Note for
1\leq j<j^{\prime}\leq m_{i}
direct calculation shows that
\tau_{\mathcal{I}_{i,j},\mathcal{I}_{i,j^{\prime}}}=(\tau_{\mathcal{I}_{i,j}})^{\tau_{\mathcal{I}_{i,j+1}}\dots\tau_{\mathcal{I}_{i,j^{\prime}-1}}}
.

Finally a specific partition is provided which will help describe
d
-split Levi subgroups and their normalisers.
For any two positive integers
k,m\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
1\leq i\leq m
set

\mathcal{J}_{i}^{k,m}:=\{i+jm\mid 0\leq j\leq k-1\}\subset\underline{km},

and
\mathcal{J}^{k,m}:=\{\mathcal{J}_{i}^{k,m}\mid 1\leq i\leq m\}\vdash\underline{km}
.
Note that
\mathcal{J}_{i}^{k,m}(j)=i+(j-1)m
.
Moreover

C_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{km}}(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}^{k,m}})=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{m}\langle w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}^{k,m}_{i}}\rangle\right)\rtimes\langle\tau_{\mathcal{J}^{k,m}_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq m-1\rangle\cong C_{2k}\wr\mathfrak{S}_{m},

and whenever
k
is odd
\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}((w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}^{k,m}})^{2})=\operatorname{C}_{\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}}(w^{\prime}_{\mathcal{J}^{k,m}})
.

2.A.3.
Normalisers for certain subgroups in
\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}

{NormsgnSymm}

The next result considers normalisers in a wreath product of any abelian group by a symmetric group, which will be useful later. However it can also be immediately applied to
\mathfrak{S}^{\pm}_{n}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

{NormInWrOfWr}

Let
G=A\wr\mathfrak{S}_{n}
with
A
an abelian group and
\mathcal{I}\vdash\underline{n}
.
To each
\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}
associate a subgroup
A_{\mathcal{J}}\leq A
and let
H_{\mathcal{I}}=\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}A_{\mathcal{J}}\wr\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})\leq G
.
Set
Z_{\mathcal{I}}:=\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}\Delta_{\mathcal{J}}A
, where

\Delta_{\mathcal{J}}A=\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in A^{n}\mid a_{i}=1\text{ if }i\not\in\mathcal{J}\text{ and }a_{i}=a_{j}\text{ if }i,j\in\mathcal{J}\},

and

S_{H_{\mathcal{I}}}=\langle\tau_{\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\mid|\mathcal{J}|=|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\text{ and }A_{\mathcal{J}}=A_{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\rangle.

Then

\operatorname{N}_{G}(H_{\mathcal{I}})=\left(Z_{\mathcal{I}}H_{\mathcal{I}}\right)\rtimes S_{H_{\mathcal{I}}}.

Proof.

Under the projection homomorphism from
A^{n}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{n}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the group
\operatorname{N}_{G}(H_{\mathcal{I}})
gets mapped to a subgroup of
\operatorname{N}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{n})
.
For the subgroup
S_{\mathcal{I}}:=\langle\overline{\tau}_{\mathcal{J},\mathcal{J}^{\prime}}\mid|\mathcal{J}|=|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}|\rangle
it follows that
\operatorname{N}_{\mathfrak{S}_{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{n})=\left(\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})\right)\rtimes S_{\mathcal{I}}
.
The group
S_{\mathcal{I}}
permutes the factors of
A^{n}
and thus
\operatorname{N}_{S_{\mathcal{I}}}(H_{\mathcal{I}}\cap A^{n})=S_{H_{\mathcal{I}}}
.
Moreover, by definition
S_{H_{\mathcal{I}}}\leq\operatorname{N}_{G}(H_{\mathcal{I}})
and hence

\operatorname{N}_{G}(H_{\mathcal{I}})=\operatorname{N}_{A^{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}})H_{\mathcal{I}}\rtimes S_{H_{\mathcal{I}}}.

Let
\sigma\in\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
and take
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in\operatorname{N}_{A^{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}})
so that
[g,\sigma]=g^{-1}g^{\sigma^{-1}}\in H_{\mathcal{I}}\cap A^{n}
.
Hence for each
i\in\mathcal{J}
it follows that
g_{\sigma(i)}\in g_{i}A_{\mathcal{J}}
.
In particular, for
g_{\mathcal{J}}:=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})
with
a_{i}=g_{i}
whenever
i\in\mathcal{J}
and
a_{i}=1
otherwise, there is an
h_{\mathcal{J}}=(h_{1},\dots,h_{n})
with
h_{i}\in A_{\mathcal{J}}
whenever
i\in\mathcal{J}
and
h_{i}=1
otherwise, such that
g_{\mathcal{J}}h_{\mathcal{J}}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{J}}A
.
Thus for
h=\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}h_{\mathcal{J}}
then
gh\in Z_{\mathcal{I}}
and so
\operatorname{N}_{A^{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}})\leq Z_{\mathcal{I}}H_{\mathcal{I}}
.
Moreover
[Z_{\mathcal{I}},\sigma]=1
for any
\sigma\in\prod_{\mathcal{J}\in\mathcal{I}}\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{J})
, as for any
g=(g_{1},\dots,g_{n})\in Z_{\mathcal{I}}
and
i,j\in\mathcal{J}
then
g_{i}=g_{j}
.
Hence
Z_{\mathcal{I}}\leq\operatorname{N}_{A^{n}}(H_{\mathcal{I}})
.

Remark 2.3
.

{NormProdsgnSymmSym}

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
and
\mathcal{I}\vdash n
.
Assume that
\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}_{1}\sqcup\mathcal{I}_{2}
and set

Lemma 2.4

{ExtensionwithWreath}

The character

3.A.

Let

3.A.1.

{ChevRel}

Every

For each

As an immediate consequence, it follows that

3.B.2.
Extended Weyl group

{ExtWeylGp}

Recall from Section
3.A.1
that the extended Weyl group was defined to be
V_{\Phi}:=\langle{\bf n}_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\rangle
and contained the normal subgroup
H_{\Phi}:=\langle h_{\alpha}\mid\alpha\in\Phi\rangle
of order
(2,q-1)^{|\Pi|}
such that
V_{\Phi}/H_{\Phi}\cong W_{\Phi}
.
If
\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}
are orthogonal root subsystems of
\Phi
and
\Pi_{i}
is a set of simple roots in
\Phi_{i}
, then
\Pi_{1}\sqcup\Pi_{2}
forms a set of simple roots in
\Phi_{1}\sqcup\Phi_{2}
.
Moreover,
W_{\Phi_{1}\sqcup\Phi_{2}}\cong W_{\Phi_{1}}\times W_{\Phi_{2}}
and by comparing group orders
H_{\Phi_{1}\sqcup\Phi_{2}}=H_{\Phi_{1}}\times H_{\Phi_{2}}
.
Hence
V_{\Phi_{1}}\cap V_{\Phi_{2}}\leq H_{\Phi}\cap V_{\Phi_{i}}=H_{\Phi_{i}}
for
i=1,2
.
However
H_{\Phi_{1}}\cap H_{\Phi_{2}}
is trivial and thus
V_{\Phi_{1}}\cap V_{\Phi_{2}}=1
.

Corollary 3.3
.

{IDisjVDirProd}

Let
\Phi
be a root system of type
A_{n-1},C_{n}
or
D_{n}
and
\mathcal{J}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{J}_{k}
disjoint subsets of
\underline{n}
.
Then
\langle V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{i}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq k\rangle=V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{1}}}\times\dots\times V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}}
.
If furthermore there is some
\mathcal{J}\subset\underline{n}
and elements
g_{1},\dots,g_{k}\in V_{\Phi}
such that
(V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}}})^{g_{i}}\leq V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{i}}}
, then the map

\begin{array}[]{ccc}V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}}}&\rightarrow&V_{\Phi}\\
x&\mapsto&\prod_{i=1}^{k}x^{g_{i}}\\
\end{array}

is an injective group homomorphism.

Proof.

As
\Phi
is a root system of type
A_{n-1}
,
C_{n}
or
D_{n}
, the Chevalley relations imply
[x_{\alpha}(t),x_{\beta}(u)]=1
for any pair of orthogonal roots
\alpha
and
\beta
in
\Phi
.
Thus for any
l\leq k-1
, it follows that
[V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{J}_{l}}},V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{l+1}}}]=1
.
Moreover, as the root subsystems
\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{J}_{l}}
and
\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{l+1}}
are orthogonal, the intersection
V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{J}_{l}}}\cap V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{l+1}}}=1
.
Hence the first statement follows by an inductive argument after observing that
\langle V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{i}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq l\rangle\leq V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{1}\cup\dots\cup\mathcal{J}_{l}}}
.

The defined map is a homomorphism of groups as the commutators
[V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{i}}},V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{j}}}]=1
whenever
i\neq j
.
Moreover,
\langle V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{i}}}\mid 1\leq i\leq k-1\rangle\cap V_{\Phi_{\mathcal{J}_{k}}}=1
, implies that the map must be injective.
∎

{ParaSubSys}

CAT(0) spaces of higher rank II
Stephan Stadler

Abstract
. This belongs to a series of papers motivated by Ballmann’s Higher Rank Rigidity Conjecture. We prove the following. Let X X be a CAT(0) space with a geometric group action Γ ↷ X \Gamma\curvearrowright X . Suppose that every geodesic in X X lies in an n n -flat, n ≥ 2 n\geq 2 . If X X contains a periodic n n -flat which does not bound a flat ( n + 1 ) (n+1) -half-space, then X X is a Riemannian symmetric space, a Euclidean building or non-trivially splits as a metric product. This generalizes the Higher Rank Rigidity Theorem for Hadamard manifolds with geometric group actions.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Main results

A central role in the geometry of nonpositively curved spaces is played by
flats
– isometric embeddings of Euclidean spaces.
One expects that in a CAT(0) space with enough extremal curvature zero, flats are uniformly distributed throughout the space and special geometry appears –
at least if the isometry group is sufficiently large. This is made precise by Ballmann’s conjecture which is the main motivation for the present paper.

Conjecture

(Higher Rank Rigidity)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
.
If every geodesic in
X
lies in an
n
-flat,
n\geq 2
, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean
building or non-trivially splits as a metric product.

Recall that an isometric action is called
geometric
if it is properly discontinuous and cocompact.
For Hadamard manifolds Higher Rank Rigidity is a celebrated theorem by Ballmann
[
Bal85
]
and independently Burns-Spatzier
[
BS87
]
, see also
Eberlein-Heber
[
EH90
]
.

In
[
Sta22b
]
we have confirmed Higher Rank Rigidity as well as the Closing Lemma for spaces without 3-flats.
More precisely, we obtained

Theorem 1.1

(
[
Sta22b
, Theorems A and B]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space without 3-flats.
Suppose
X
admits a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
. If
X
contains a complete geodesic which does not bound a flat half-plane, then it also contains a

\Gamma
-periodic geodesic which does not bound a flat half-plane. On the other hand, if every complete geodesic in
X
bounds a flat half-plane,
then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space, a Euclidean building or non-trivially splits as a metric product.

Moreover, we have proved the following general result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
Sta22a
, Theorem A]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space whose Tits boundary has dimension
n-1\geq 1
.
Suppose that every geodesic in
X
lies in an
n
-flat.
If
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean building or
X
non-trivially splits as a metric product.

A flat is periodic, if its stabilizer in the isometry group contains a subgroup acting geometrically on the flat.
Recall that if
X
is a rank
n
symmetric space or Euclidean building with a geometric group action, then periodic
n
-flats are dense in the space of
n
-flats in
X

[
BB95
, Theorem 8.9]
,
[
Mos73
, Lemma 8.3]
,
[
PR72
, Theorem 2.8]
. Note that Theorem
1.2
does not make any symmetry assumptions besides the periodic flat.

We say that a CAT(0) space has
rank at least
n
,
\operatorname{rank}(X)\geq n
, if every geodesic in
X
lies in an
n
-flat
1
1

1

See
[
OR22
]
for a definition of rank for CAT(0) spaces whose isometry groups satisfy the duality condition.
.
In the present paper we consider CAT(0) spaces
X
of
higher rank
, i.e. rank at least
2
. We
will work under the assumption of a geometric group action but remove any condition on the dimension of the Tits boundary.
Note that the conditions
\operatorname{rank}(X)=n\geq 2
and
\dim(\partial_{T}X)=n-1

in combination ensure that the Tits boundary is geodesically complete (
[
BL06
, Lemma 2.1]
).
This allowed us in
[
Sta22a
]
to rely on a deep result of Lytchak:

Theorem 1.3

(
[
Lyt05
, Main Theorem]
)
.

Let
Z
be a finite-dimensional geodesically complete
CAT(1) space. Suppose
Z
has a proper closed subset
A
which is symmetric, i.e. contains with each
point all of its antipodes. Then
Z
is
a spherical join or a spherical building.

If the Tits boundary of a locally compact geodesically complete CAT(0) space is a spherical building, then rigidity follows from Leeb’s theorem
[
Lee00
, Main Theorem]
.
However, Tits boundaries of CAT(0) spaces tend to be not geodesically complete.
One can find examples even among
4
-dimensional real-analytic Hadamard manifolds which admit geometric group actions
[
HS98
]
.
If
V
is such a manifold, then
V
has necessarily rank
1
.
However, the product with any other CAT(0) space
X
has higher rank, yet
V\times X
cannot be subject to Theorem
1.2

as it has to contain maximal flats of different dimensions. Vice versa, if Higher Rank Rigidity holds, then a higher rank space can only contain
maximal flats of different dimensions if it splits non-trivially.

Recall that a flat is called
Morse
, if it does not bound a flat half-space.

Main Theorem.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space of rank at least
n\geq 2
with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
.
If
X
contains a periodic Morse
n
-flat, then
X
is a Riemannian symmetric space or a Euclidean building or
X

non-trivially splits as a metric product.

Corollary A
.

Let
M
be a Hadamard manifold with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright M
.
If
M
has higher rank, then
M
is a Riemannian symmetric space or non-trivially splits as a metric product.

1.2.
Strategy

The proof of our
Main Theorem
relies on Leeb’s rigidity theorem.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
Lee00
, Main Theorem]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact, geodesically complete CAT(0) space. If
\partial_{T}X
is a connected thick irreducible spherical building,
then
X
is either a symmetric space or a Euclidean building.

As we have explained above, in our setting the Tits boundary might not be geodesically complete.
Thus we cannot use Lytchak’s rigidity theorem to show that the Tits boundary is a spherical join or a spherical building.
Our starting point is the existence of regular points at infinity:

Theorem 1.5

(
[
Sta22a
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space. Suppose that
X
contains a periodic
n
-flat
F
. Then either
F
bounds a flat
(n+1)
-half-space, or

\partial_{T}F\subset\partial_{T}X
contains a dense subset which is open in
\partial_{T}X
.

Using dynamical ideas based on work of Papasoglu-Swenson
[
PS09
]
and Guralnik-Swenson
[
GS13
]
,
we then show the existence of a non-empty proper closed symmetric subset in
\partial_{\infty}X
.
Note that this is not the same setup than in Theorem
1.3
.
It is essential, that we find such sets which are closed in the cone topology and not only in the Tits topology.
The proof then relies on the basic observation that
minimal closed symmetric subsets constitute an equidistant decomposition of the Tits boundary, and therefore,
a well-defined metric quotient – a submetry
\delta:\partial_{T}X\to\Delta
.
We then study this submetry carefully.
First control on
\delta
comes from the fact that
each logarithmic map from the Tits boundary to the space of directions at some point defines
a submetry between the corresponding quotients.
Using logarithmic maps for varying sets of points, we can
show that the Tits boundary is geodesically complete in horizontal directions.
A key role is then played by the periodic Morse flat
\hat{F}
.
It turns out that
\delta
restricts to a submetry
\hat{\delta}:\partial_{T}\hat{F}\to\Delta
.
We show that if
\partial_{T}X
is irreducible, then
\hat{\delta}

is discrete and
\Delta
is the quotient of
\partial_{T}\hat{F}
by a finite group action. We conclude the proof by showing that
\partial_{T}\hat{F}

has to be top-dimensional if
\partial_{T}X
is irreducible. In this case Theorem
1.2
from
[
Sta22a
]
applies.
The last step relies on the fact that submetries of round spheres to spaces of constant curvature 1 are very well understood, in fact classified.

1.3.
Organization

In Section
2
we recall necessary basics from metric geometry and agree on notation.
Apart from several standard facts, we also review the structure of submetries from round spheres and prove several results needed later on.
We also recall the notion of a Morse flat and its basic properties.
In Section
3
we introduce symmetric and visually symmetric subsets, preparing for the structure of a submetry.
We also proof a first basic distance estimate induced by proper closed symmetric subsets (Lemma
3.7
) which is ultimately responsible
for rigidity.
Further, we discuss almost near points. Segments between almost near points always extend to geodesics of length
\pi
.
This will be used to show geodesic completeness in horizontal directions of submetries. We also show that almost near points lead to many spherical triangles.
In Section
4
we show that closed minimal symmetric sets provide a submetry of the Tits boundary (Proposition
4.4
).
We then discuss the branching behavior of horizontal geodesics and prove splitting results based on particular splittings of the Tits boundary of a
periodic Morse flat.
We begin Section
5
by showing that a periodic Morse flat in a higher rank space leads to a non-trivial proper symmetric subset at infinity
and therefore to a submetry of the Tits boundary. We then study the Tits boundary of a periodic Morse flat within the Tits boundary of our space and show
that it carries a structure reminiscent of a Coxeter complex. We then consider induced submetries and show in the irreducible case that the dimension of
the base of our submetry is equal to the dimension of the Tits boundary of the periodic Morse flat.
We prove the
Main Theorem
by showing that the dimension of the base is equal to the dimension of the Tits boundary unless the space is reducible.
At last, we conclude Corollary
A
.

2.1.
Metric spaces

Euclidean
n
-space with its flat metric will be denoted by
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and the unit sphere by
S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We denote the distance between two points
x
and
y
in a metric space
X
by
|x,y|
.
If
A\subset X
denotes a subset, then
|x,A|
refers to the greatest lower bound for distances from points in
A
to
x
.
Similarly, for subsets
A,A^{\prime}\subset X
we denote by
|A,A^{\prime}|
the greatest lower bound for distances from points in
A
to points in
A^{\prime}
.
For
x\in X
and
r>0
, we denote by
B_{r}(x)
and
\bar{B}_{r}(x)
the open and closed
r
-ball around
x
, respectively.
A
geodesic

is an isometric embedding of an interval. It is called a
geodesic segment
, if it is compact.
The
endpoints
of a geodesic segment
c
are denoted by
\partial c
.
A geodesic segment
c

branches
at an endpoint
y\in\partial c=\{x,y\}
, if there are geodesics
c^{\pm}
starting in
x
which strictly contain
c

and such that
c^{-}\cap c^{+}=c
.

A
triangle
is a union of three geodesics connecting three points.
If
x,y,z
are point with unique geodesics between them, then we denote the corresponding triangle by
\triangle(x,y,z)
.

X
is a
geodesic metric space
if
any pair of points of
X

is connected by a geodesic.
It is
geodesically complete
if every geodesic segment is contained in a complete local geodesic.

For a sequence of metric spaces
(X_{k})
with uniformly bounded diameter, we denote by
X_{\omega}:=\mathop{\hbox{$\omega$-lim}}X_{k}
its ultralimit
with respect to a non-principal ultrafilter
\omega
on the natural numbers.
Recall that if a sequence of surjective 1-Lipschitz maps
f_{k}:X_{k}\to Y_{k}
between metric spaces
X_{k},Y_{k}

with uniformly bounded diameters induce a surjective 1-Lipschitz maps
f_{\omega}:X_{\omega}\to Y_{\omega}
.

2.2.
Submetries

In this section we recall the notion of a submetry. Originally the concept was introduced by Berestovskii in
[
Ber87
]

as a metric analog of a Riemannian submersion. If
B
is a spherical building, then the natural
1
-Lipschitz map
\delta:B\to\Delta_{mod}

which folds the building onto is model chamber is a submetry. Vice versa, CAT(1) spaces
which admit certain kinds of submetries are spherical buildings
[
Lyt05
]
.
This explains the relevance of submetries for us.

Two subsets
X^{\pm}
in a metric space
X
are called
equidistant
, if for every point
x^{\pm}\in X^{\pm}

there exists a point
y^{\mp}\in X^{\mp}
such that
|x^{\pm},y^{\mp}|=|X^{\pm},X^{\mp}|
holds.

A decomposition of a metric space
X
into closed pairwise equidistant subsets
\{X_{y}\subset X|\ y\in Y\}
induces a natural metric on the quotient space
Y
:

|y,y^{\prime}|:=|X_{y},X_{y^{\prime}}|.

The natural projection
\delta:X\to Y
is a submetry in the sense of
[
Ber87
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
submetry

\delta:X\to Y
between metric spaces is a map which sends for every point
x
in
X
and each radius
r\geq 0

the closed
r
-ball around
x
surjectively onto the closed
r
-ball around
\delta(x)
.

Submetries on a metric space
X
are in one-to-one correspondence to closed equidistant decompositions of
X
.
Each submetry is a 1-Lipschitz map. The composition of two submetries is again a submetry.
Vice versa, if two submetries
f:X\to Y
and
h:X\to Z
have the property that every
f
-fiber is contained in an
h
-fiber,
then the natural map
g:Y\to Z
with
g\circ f=h
is a submetry.

If
\delta:X\to Y
is a submetry, then
two points
x,x^{\prime}
in
X
are called
\delta
-near, if
|x,x^{\prime}|=|\delta(x),\delta(x^{\prime})|
holds.
In this case,
\delta
maps any geodesic between
x
and
x^{\prime}
isometrically onto a geodesic between
\delta(x)

and
\delta(x^{\prime})
. Such geodesics in
X
are called
horizontal
. More generally, a geodesic in
X
is called
piecewise horizontal
,
if it is the union of finitely many horizontal geodesics.

We collect a couple of results on submetries of round spheres which will be used later on.
Most of these results hold in greater generality but we restrict to the cases of interest for us.

Let
\delta:S^{n}\to\Delta
be a submetry. Then
\Delta
is an Alexandrov space dimension
k\leq n
and curvature at least 1
[
KL22
, Proposition 3.1]
,

[
BGP92
]
.
For any rectifiable curve
\gamma
in
\Delta
starting in a point
\delta(x)
we find a rectifiable curve
\tilde{\gamma}
to
S^{n}
starting in
x
such that

\delta\circ\tilde{\gamma}=\gamma
and
\delta
preserves the length of
\tilde{\gamma}

[
KL22
, Lemma 2.8]
. Such a curve is called a

horizontal lift
of
\gamma
.
The submetry
\delta
is differentiable at every point
x\in S^{n}
in the following sense.
There exists a map
d\delta(x):T_{x}S^{n}\to T_{\delta(x)}\Delta
, such that for every
sequence
t_{i}\to 0
, the submetry
\delta
seen as a map between rescaled spaces

\delta:(\frac{1}{t_{i}}S^{n},x)\to(\frac{1}{t_{i}}\Delta,\delta(x))
converge to the map
d\delta(x)
.
The differential
d\delta(x):T_{x}S^{n}\to T_{\delta(x)}\Delta

is an
infinitesimal submetry
, a submetry which commutes with the natural dilations on the Euclidean cones

T_{x}S^{n}
and
T_{\delta(x)}\Delta

[
KL22
, Proposition 3.3]
.

The base
\Delta
allows for a natural disjoint decomposition into strata
\Delta^{l}\subset\Delta
,
0\leq l\leq k
.
The
l
-dimensional stratum
\Delta^{l}
is
the set of points
y\in\Delta
whose tangent space
T_{y}\Delta
splits off
\mathbb{R}^{l}
as a direct
factor but not
\mathbb{R}^{l+1}
.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
KL22
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

Let
\delta:S^{n}\to\Delta
be a submetry with
\dim(\Delta)=k
. For any
0\leq l\leq k
, the stratum
\Delta^{l}
is
an
l
-dimensional topological manifold which is locally closed and locally
convex in
\Delta
. The maximal stratum
\Delta^{k}
is open and globally convex.

If a geodesic
\gamma
in
\Delta
starts in a point of
\Delta^{l}_{+}:=\bigcup_{i=l}^{k}\Delta^{i}
, then it stays in this set
[
KL22

.
Thus the codimension 1 stratum
\Delta^{k-1}
consists precisely of the points

where the tangent space
T_{y}\Delta

is isometric to a
k
-dimensional Euclidean half-space. The top-dimensional stratum is sometimes referred to as the set of
regular points
,

\Delta_{reg}:=\Delta^{k}
.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
KL22
, Theorem 10.5]
)
.

Let
\delta:S^{n}\to\Delta
be a submetry. Then
\delta:\delta^{-1}(\Delta_{reg})\to\Delta_{reg}

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
Riemannian submersion.

We call a submetry

transnormal
, if any extension of a
\delta
-horizontal geodesic in

is a piecewise horizontal

This is equivalent to saying that an antipode of a direction tangent to a
\delta

\delta
-horizontal geodesic.
Since the horizontal space
H_{x}\subset\Sigma_{x}S^{n}
is convex for any submetry
[

, Proposition 12.5]
, it follows that for transnormal submetries

Proposition 2.4

(
[
KL22
, Proposition 12.5]
)
.

Let
\delta:S^{n}\to\Delta
be a transnormal submetry. Then every fiber of

is a
\mathcal{C}^{1,1}
submanifold in
S^{n}
.

.

Let
\delta:S^{n}\to\Delta
be a submetry such that every geodesic in

By

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\delta:S^{m}\to S^{n}_{+}
be a submetry where

is a hemisphere.
Then
\delta
splits as
(\delta_{1},\delta_{2}):S^{n-1}\circ S^{m-n}\to S^{n-1}\circ\{v\}

Write

.

Let
\delta:S^{m}\to\Delta
be a transnormal submetry.
Let

be a point with
\delta(x)=y\in\Delta^{n-1}

Let
\delta:S^{m}\to\Delta

Proposition 2.9
.

Let
\hat{\delta}:\hat{\sigma}\to\Delta
be a transnormal submetry of a round
n

\hat{\sigma}

(n+1)

reflections

\sigma_{i}\subset S^{n}
with
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n+1}\sigma_{i}=\emptyset

\hat{\delta}

is invariant under all
f_{i}
,

. Then either every geodesic in
\hat{\sigma}
is piecewise
\hat{\delta}
-horizontal,
or there exists a disjoint decomposition
I^{-}\dot{\cup}I^{+}=\{1,\ldots,n+1\}
such that
\hat{\sigma}=\sigma^{-}\circ\sigma^{+}

where
\sigma^{\pm}=\bigcap_{i\in I^{\pm}}\sigma_{i}
and every geodesic
\xi^{-}\xi^{+}

\xi^{\pm}\in\sigma^{\pm}

\hat{\delta}
-horizontal.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n+1}\in\hat{\sigma}
points such that
\xi_{i}\in\bigcap_{j\neq i}\sigma_{j}
.
If the links
\Sigma_{\xi_{i}}\hat{\sigma}
are horizontal for

, then all links
\Sigma_{\xi}\hat{\sigma}
are horizontal and every geodesic
is piecewise
\hat{\delta}
-horizontal since
\hat{\delta}
is transnormal and the directions
\log_{\xi}(\xi_{i})

span the tangent space at
\xi
.
So let us assume that
\Sigma_{\xi_{1}}\hat{\sigma}
is not horizontal and therefore splits into horizontal and vertical directions,

\Sigma_{\xi_{1}}\hat{\sigma}=H_{\xi_{1}}\circ V_{\xi_{1}}
. By assumption, this splitting is invariant under all reflections

f_{i}
,

. Set
v_{i}:=\log_{\xi_{1}}(\xi_{i})
. It follows that there is a disjoint decomposition

such that the directions

with

are vertical and the directions

with

are horizontal.
By assumption

is not empty.
Before we proceed, we need to make sure that vertical directions stay vertical.

Sublemma 2.10
.

Let
\Pi
be a fiber of

2.5.
CAT(1) spaces

CAT(1) spaces play a particular role in our investigations as they appear as Tits boundaries and links of CAT(0) spaces.
Recall that the spherical join
Z_{1}\circ Z_{2}
of two CAT(1) spaces
Z_{1}
and

is a CAT(1) space of diameter

A subset

\pi

If

A subset in a CAT(1) space is called

2.6.

The

If

is locally compact, then

The

– the intrinsic metric associated to the

.
The Tits boundary of a CAT(0) space is a CAT(1) space.
For any point

It satisfies the following rigidity. If

to

is isometric to a flat sector

If

and

are CAT(0) spaces, then their metric product

is again a CAT(0) space.
We have

\Sigma_{(x_{1},x_{2})}(X_{1}\times X_{2})=\Sigma_{x_{1}}X_{1}\circ\Sigma_{x_{2}}X_{2}

If

is a geodesically complete CAT(0) space, then any join decomposition of

is induced by a metric product decomposition of

, Proposition 2.3.7]
.

A

-flat

in a CAT(0) space

is a closed convex subset isometric to

.
Its Tits boundary

-sphere.
On the other hand, if

is locally compact and

2.7.
Basics on ideal boundaries

We record two facts which relate the cone topology and the Tits metric.

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space.
Let
A\subset\partial_{\infty}X
be a closed subset and
\eta
a point in
\partial_{\infty}X\setminus A

represented by a ray
\rho
. Then
\lim\limits_{t\to\infty}\angle_{\rho(t)}(\eta,A)=|\eta,A|

holds.

Proof.

Since
\log_{x}:\partial_{T}X\to\Sigma_{x}X
is 1-Lipschitz for every point
x\in X
, we have
\limsup\limits_{t\to\infty}\angle_{\rho(t)}(\eta,A)\leq|\eta,A|
.
So if the statement fails, then we find
\epsilon>0
,
t_{i}\to\infty
and
\xi_{i}\in A
with
\xi_{i}\to\xi\in A
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
holds

\angle_{\rho(t_{i})}(\eta,\xi_{i})\leq|\eta,A|-\epsilon.

But for any
\delta>0
we find
i_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
|\eta,\xi|\leq\angle_{\rho(t_{i})}(\eta,\xi)+\delta
for

i\geq i_{0}
. Choose
j_{0}\geq i_{0}
such that
\angle_{\rho(t_{i_{0}})}(\eta,\xi)\leq\angle_{\rho(t_{i_{0}})}(\eta,\xi_{j})+\delta
for
j\geq j_{0}
.
Together, we obtain

|\eta,\xi|\leq\angle_{\rho(t_{i_{0}})}(\eta,\xi_{j})+2\delta\leq\angle_{\rho(t_{j})}(\eta,\xi_{j})+2\delta\leq|\eta,A|-\epsilon+2\delta.

This leads to a contradiction if we choose
\delta<\frac{\epsilon}{2}
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space.
Let
A^{\pm}
be closed subsets of
\partial_{\infty}X
. Then there exist
points
\xi^{\pm}\in A^{\pm}
with

|\xi^{-},\xi^{+}|=|A^{-},A^{+}|.

Proof.

If there exists
\eta\in A^{-}\cap A^{+}
, we can take
\xi^{-}=\xi^{+}=\eta
. Otherwise,
there exist sequences of points
\xi^{\pm}_{i}\in A^{\pm},
such that
|\xi_{i}^{-},\xi_{i}^{+}|\to|A^{-},A^{+}|
.
Since
\partial_{\infty}X
is compact, we may assume that
\xi^{\pm}_{i}\to\xi^{\pm}
with respect to the cone topology.
By lower semicontinuity of the Tits metric, we conclude
|\xi^{-},\xi^{+}|\leq|A^{-},A^{+}|
.
By assumption,
A^{\pm}
is closed and therefore
\xi^{\pm}\in A^{\pm}
.
∎

2.8.
Dynamics at infinity

Recall the following construction from
[
GS13
]
.
Let
G
be a discrete group acting on a compact Hausdorff space

.
Identify the Stone-Čech compactification
\beta G
with the set of ultrafilters on

T^{\omega}:Z\to Z;\ z\mapsto\mathop{\hbox{$\omega$-lim}}_{g}gz.

Then for fixed

which sends
\omega

is continuous. The family of operators
\{T^{\omega}\}_{\omega\in\beta G}

is closed under composition and the inverse map

extends to a continuous involution

.

Now let

be a locally compact CAT(0) space and
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
a geometric action.
Then
\bar{X}=X\cup\partial_{\infty}X
is a compact Hausdorff space and the above construction
applies. By the semi-continuity of the Tits metric, every operator
T^{\omega}
is a 1-Lipschitz map
\partial_{T}X\to\partial_{T}X

[
GS13
]
.

For
\omega\in\beta\Gamma\setminus\Gamma
and

we denote by
\omega^{+}:=T^{\omega}(x)

and
\omega^{-}:=T^{S\omega}(x)
the
attracting
and
repelling
point, respectively. The definition does not depend on the choice of
x\in X
.

The key tool for studying the dynamics of
\Gamma
on
\partial_{\infty}X

is the following theorem due to Papasoglu and Swenson.

Theorem 2.13

(
\pi
-convergence
[
PS09
, Lemma 19]
)
.

Let
\Gamma
be a group acting properly discontinuously on a locally compact CAT(0)
space
X
, and let
\omega\in\beta\Gamma\setminus\Gamma
, then

|\omega^{-},\xi|+|T^{\omega}(\xi),\omega^{+}|\leq\pi

holds for every
\xi\in\partial_{T}X
.

Proof.

It is enough to show the following. Suppose that
(\gamma_{k})
is a sequence in
\Gamma

with
\gamma_{k}^{\pm 1}x\to\omega^{\pm}
for some (and then any) point
x\in X
and such that
\gamma_{k}\xi\to\eta
in
\partial_{\infty}X
.
Then for any point
y\in X
we have

\angle_{y}(\gamma_{k}^{-1}x,\xi)=\angle_{\gamma_{k}y}(x,\gamma_{k}\xi)\leq\pi-\angle_{x}(\gamma_{k}y,\gamma_{k}\xi).

Now choose
y_{k}\in y\xi\setminus\{y\}
with
y_{k}\to y
and
x_{k}\in x\gamma_{k}\xi\setminus\{x\}
with
x_{k}\to x
.
Passing to the limit

and using
[
KL97
, Lemma 2.1.5]
, we conclude

\angle_{y}(\omega^{-},\xi)\leq\pi-\angle_{x}(\omega^{+},\eta).

Taking the supremum over
y\in X
and the infimum over

yields the result.
∎

An element
\omega\in\beta\Gamma
is said to

from a point
\xi\in\partial_{\infty}X
, if
the following holds. Let
x\in X
and
\rho:[0,\infty)\to X
be a geodesic ray asymptotic to
\xi
.
Then there exists a constant

such that the set of all
\gamma\in\Gamma
with
\gamma x\in N_{r}(\rho^{\prime})
lies in
S\omega

for every subray
\rho^{\prime}\subset\rho
. In particular, the repelling point is
\omega^{-}=\xi
.
Here is the basic example. Suppose
(\gamma_{k})
is a sequence in
\Gamma
such that
(\gamma_{k}\rho(k))

is bounded.
If we push forward a non-principal ultrafilter on
\mathbb{N}
to
\Gamma

via
(\gamma_{k})
, then we obtain an element
\omega
which pulls from
\xi
.
In particular, if
\Gamma
acts geometrically then we can pull from every point in
\partial_{\infty}X
.
We will make use of the following.

Lemma 2.14

(
[
GS13
, Lemma 3.19]
)
.

Let
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
be a round
n
-sphere which bounds a flat

If

pulls from a point

is a round

-sphere which also bounds a flat.

A subset

2.9.
Morse flats

Morse quasiflats in metric spaces were introduced and studied in
[
HKS22a
,
HKS22b
]
. They simultaneously generalize Morse quasi-geodesics as well as top-dimensional quasiflats.
Their significance lies in the fact that they are quasi-isometry invariant and display nice asymptotic behavior, namely they
have unique tangent cones at infinity. This makes them a strong tool in asymptotic geometry, for instance in the study of quasi-isometric rigidity of infinite groups.
Here we are only concerned with the very particular case of periodic Morse flats. Recall that a flat
F
in a metric space
X
is

X
contains a subgroup which acts geometrically on
F
.
A periodic

-flat

is called

, if it does not bound a flat
(n+1)
-half-space.
Periodic Morse

-flats are precisely Ballmann’s rank 1 axes
[
Bal95
, Chapter III.3]
. If

\gamma
is a periodic geodesic in a CAT(0) space

, then
\gamma
is Morse if and only if (one and then) both ideal endpoints of

\gamma
are isolated in the Tits boundary. In higher dimensions, we have the following, which follows immediately from

[
HKS22a
, Corollary 11.5]
and
[
HKS22b
, Proposition 6.14]
.

Proposition 2.15
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space and
F\subset X
a periodic

-flat with
\sigma:=\partial_{T}F
.
Then

is Morse if and only if for every point

the induced map

\tilde{H}_{n}(\sigma,\sigma\setminus\{\xi\},\mathbb{Z})\to\tilde{H}_{n}(\partial_{T}X,\partial_{T}X\setminus\{\xi\},\mathbb{Z})

is injective.

An important consequence is that any ideal boundary point has an antipode in the Tits
boundary of a periodic Morse flat.

Corollary 2.16

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space and

\sigma:=\partial_{T}F

Then any point in
\partial_{T}X
has an antipode in

Let
\gamma
be a geodesic with endpoint

If
\Sigma_{\xi}\sigma\subset B_{\pi}(v)

\Sigma_{\xi}\sigma
is contractible inside

since every ball of radius less than

is contractible in a CAT(1) space. By
[

, the logarithm map provides a homotopy equivalence between a small enough punctured ball around

\tilde{H}_{n}(\sigma,\sigma\setminus\{\xi\},\mathbb{Z})\to\tilde{H}_{n}(\partial_{T}X,\partial_{T}X\setminus\{\xi\},\mathbb{Z})

The following is similar to

For any subset

(ii)

(i)

, since for every point

Direct consequence of (i) since

.

A visually symmetric subset is also called

Corollary 3.5

Let

A closed subset

\operatorname{Ant}(A)\subset\overline{\operatorname{Ant}(A)}=\overline{\operatorname{Ant}_{vis}(A)}\subset A.

Definition 3.11

of

Corollary 3.13

Lemma 3.14

Let

∎

Proposition 3.15

Let
\xi,\eta
be points in

\eta

Proof.

By Reshetnyak majorization, we find a 1-Lipschitz map

\tilde{\triangle}
denotes the comparison triangle and
\varphi
maps every side isometrically.
Let

be a geodesic ray representing

. For every

and an ultralimit with respect to a non-principal ultrafilter

f_{\omega}=\mathop{\hbox{$\omega$-lim}}f_{k}:\tilde{\triangle}\to\Sigma_{\omega}

where
\Sigma_{\omega}=\mathop{\hbox{$\omega$-lim}}\Sigma_{\rho(k)}X
.
For a point in
\partial_{T}X
we use a subscript

to denote its image under

. For every

is geodesically complete

By Lemma

maps

isometrically, hence so does

Moreover, since

provides an isometry

∎

4.

4.1.
Minimal closed symmetric sets

The goal of this section is to show that the existence of a non-trivial, proper, closed, symmetric subset

leads to a non-trivial submetry

(Proposition

Definition 4.1

Let

For a subset

Lemma 4.2

Let

|\cdot,A|
is constant on

.
By Lemma
2.12

for some points

By Corollary

We choose a base point

Using Lemma

and

large enough, the following estimates hold

\displaystyle|B_{\eta},A|

\displaystyle=|\eta_{\infty},\xi|=\angle_{o}(\eta_{\infty},\xi)\geq\angle_{o}(\eta_{k},\xi)-\epsilon

In the next to last step we have used Corollary

Since

was arbitrary, the claim follows.
∎

Let

be a locally compact and geodesically complete CAT(0) space.

It follows from Corollary

4.3.
Spherical splittings

In this section we collect some splitting results which can be read off the branching behavior of
\delta
-horizontal
geodesics. First, let us recall the following general result.

Theorem 4.17

(
[
BMS16
, Theorem 2.9]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space with finite-dimensional Tits boundary and where
\partial_{\infty}X
contains a dense family of
top-dimensional round spheres. Suppose

\partial_{\infty}X
contains a non-empty proper closed convex subset
Z
which is symmetric.
Then
\partial_{T}X
splits as
\partial_{T}X\cong Z\circ Z^{\perp}
.

Note, by Corollary
2.17
, this applies to all locally compact CAT(0) spaces with geometric group actions.
Thus in order to find spherical splittings of such spaces, one is on the lookout for closed convex symmetric sets at infinity.

Proposition 4.18
.

Let
\hat{\sigma}=\sigma_{1}\circ\sigma_{2}
be a splitting into round spheres.
Suppose that every pair of points
\xi_{i}\in\sigma_{i}
,
i=1,2
, the geodesic
\xi_{1}\xi_{2}
is piecewise horizontal and does not branch
in
\partial_{T}X
. Then
\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{i})
, the set of antipodes of
\sigma_{i}
, is a closed convex symmetric proper subset of
\partial_{\infty}X
.

Proof.

Note first that if a point
\zeta\in\partial_{T}X
has constant distance
\frac{\pi}{2}
from every point in
\sigma_{1}
, then it has to lie in
\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
.
Indeed, by Corollary
2.16
,
\zeta
has an antipode
\hat{\zeta}
in
\hat{\sigma}
. By assumption,
\hat{\zeta}
lies on a non-branching piecewise horizontal geodesic
\xi_{2}\xi_{1}

with
\xi_{i}\in\sigma_{i}
. Then the segment
\hat{\zeta}\xi_{1}
is initially horizontal and since
\xi_{2}\xi_{1}
does not branch, we can extend

\hat{\zeta}\xi_{1}
to
\zeta
. Thus
\hat{\zeta}=\xi_{2}\in\sigma_{2}
.

Now we show that
\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
is closed. Let
\hat{\zeta}_{k}\to\hat{\zeta}
be a convergent sequence in
\partial_{\infty}X
with
\hat{\zeta}_{k}\in\operatorname{Ant}(\zeta_{k})
,
\zeta_{k}\in\sigma_{2}
.
Let
\xi_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
be an arbitrary point and denote by
\hat{\xi}_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
its antipode.
Arguing as before, we find geodesics
\gamma_{k}
and
\hat{\gamma}_{k}
from
\zeta_{k}
to
\hat{\zeta}_{k}
through the point
\xi_{1}
and
\hat{\xi}_{1}
, respectively.
Passing to limits, we obtain geodesics
\gamma
and
\hat{\gamma}
from a point
\zeta\in\sigma_{2}
to
\hat{\zeta}
through the point
\xi_{1}
and
\hat{\xi}_{1}
, respectively.
By semicontinuity of the Tits metric, we conclude
|\hat{\zeta},\xi_{1}|=|\hat{\zeta},\hat{\xi}_{1}|=\frac{\pi}{2}
. Therefore,
\hat{\zeta}\in\operatorname{Ant}(\zeta)\subset\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
.

Before we show symmetry, observe that if
\hat{\zeta}
is antipodal to a point
\zeta\in\sigma_{2}
,
and
\xi_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
is arbitrary, then the geodesic
\hat{\zeta}\xi_{1}
is piecewise horizontal and does not branch.
Indeed, it is contained in a round
1
-sphere which also contains the points
\zeta
and
\hat{\xi}_{1}
.
Since
\zeta\hat{\xi}_{1}
does not branch, neither does
\hat{\zeta}\xi_{1}
.
Thus, if
\hat{\zeta}^{\prime}
is another point in
\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
which is antipodal to
\hat{\zeta}
, then there is a geodesic
from
\hat{\zeta}
to
\hat{\zeta}^{\prime}
through the point
\xi_{1}
. In particular,
|\hat{\zeta}^{\prime},\xi_{1}|=\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Since
\xi_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
was arbitrary, we conclude
\hat{\zeta}^{\prime}\in\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
as required.

At last, let us show convexity. Let
\hat{\zeta}_{2}
and
\hat{\zeta}_{2}^{\prime}
be points at distance less than
\pi
and antipodal to points
\zeta_{2}
and
\zeta_{2}^{\prime}
in
\sigma_{2}
,
respectively. For every point
\xi_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
the Lune Lemma provides a spherical lune spanned by
\xi_{1},\hat{\xi}_{1}

and
\hat{\zeta}_{2},\hat{\zeta}_{2}^{\prime}
. In particular, the segment
\hat{\zeta}_{2}\hat{\zeta}_{2}^{\prime}
has constant distance
\frac{\pi}{2}
from
\xi_{1}
.
Again, since
\xi_{1}\in\sigma_{1}
was arbitrary, we conclude
\hat{\zeta}_{2}\hat{\zeta}_{2}^{\prime}\subset\operatorname{Ant}(\sigma_{2})
as required.
∎

The following auxiliary result, which guarantees a particular branching pattern along horizontal geodesics, will be used later in order to find
join decompositions as required by Proposition
4.18
.

Lemma 4.19
.

Suppose that there exists a point
\xi\in\partial_{T}X
and a direction
v\in H_{\xi}
such that
\operatorname{Ant}(v)
contains a round 1-sphere
\sigma
of genuine directions.
Then any geodesic in direction
v
can only contain branch points at times which are multiples of
\frac{\pi}{2}
.

Proof.

Choose an antipode
\hat{\xi}
of
\xi
. By Proposition
4.12
, the circle
\sigma\subset\Sigma_{\xi}\partial_{T}X
consists of
\delta
-horizontal directions.
By the Lune Lemma, the union of geodesics from
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}
in the directions of
\sigma

form a round 2-sphere
\bar{\sigma}\subset\partial_{T}X
. By Lemma
4.13
, there exist
times
0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{k}=\pi
such that any geodesic
\gamma
connecting
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}
in a direction of
\sigma
has the property that

\gamma(t_{i})
is
\delta
-near to
\gamma(t_{i+1})
. Let
\gamma
be such a geodesic.
The claim follows if we can show that

\gamma
does not branch for
t\neq\frac{\pi}{2}
.
More precisely, we need to rule out that
\gamma
or its inverse parametrization branch at a time
t_{i_{0}}\neq\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Since the setting is symmetric, we can assume that
\gamma
itself branches. Moreover, we may assume
t_{i_{0}}>\frac{\pi}{2}
.
Indeed, if
\hat{\gamma}
denotes the unique geodesic in
\hat{\sigma}
, starting in
\xi
and such that
\gamma\cup\hat{\gamma}
form a closed geodesic,
then
\hat{\gamma}
branches at
\hat{t}=\pi-t_{i_{0}}
. So if
t_{i_{0}}<\frac{\pi}{2}
, then we consider
\hat{\gamma}
instead.
Now let
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
denote the antipode of
\xi
obtained by prolonging the segment
\xi\gamma(t_{i_{0}})
along the branch.
Denote by
w\in\Sigma_{\xi}\hat{\sigma}
the starting direction of
\gamma
. Let
w^{\prime}\in\Sigma_{\xi}\hat{\sigma}
be a direction close to
w
.
We show that the piecewise
\delta
-horizontal geodesic
\gamma^{\prime}
from
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
in the direction
w^{\prime}
has to branch in
t_{i_{0}}
.

By the Lune Lemma, we find two spherical lunes
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
spanned by the directions
w
and
w^{\prime}
and corresponding to the points
\hat{\xi}
and
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
. If
\gamma^{\prime}
branches at time
t_{j}
, then the segment
\gamma(t_{i_{0}})\gamma^{\prime}(t_{j})
is contained in
\lambda\cap\lambda^{\prime}
. Thus the directions between
w
and
w^{\prime}

realize every branch time between
t_{i_{0}}
and
t_{j}
. Since branching is only possible at the finitely many times
t_{i}
, we conclude
t_{j}=t_{i_{0}}
.
By continuity, we infer that every geodesic from
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
in a direction of
\sigma
has to branch at
t_{i_{0}}
.
Let
\hat{\gamma}
denote the particular geodesic which starts in direction
\hat{w}\in\Sigma_{\xi}\hat{\sigma}
antipodal to
w
.
Then the points
\gamma(t_{i_{0}})
and
\hat{\gamma}(t_{i_{0}})
are joined by two geodesics, one through
\hat{\xi}
and one through
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
.
This is a contradiction since
|\gamma(t_{i_{0}}),\hat{\gamma}(t_{i_{0}})|=2(\pi-t_{i_{0}})<\pi
.
∎

5.
Morse flats and higher rank

5.1.
Tits boundary of a periodic Morse flat

Throughout this section, we assume that
X
is a locally compact CAT(0) space which contains a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat
\hat{F}
with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
.
Recall from
[
Sta22a
]
that
a round sphere
\sigma\subset\hat{\sigma}
is called
singular
, if there exists a round hemisphere
\tau^{+}\subset\partial_{T}X

with
\partial\tau^{+}=\sigma
and
\tau^{+}\cap\hat{\sigma}=\sigma
such that the union
\tau^{+}\cup\tau^{-}
is a round sphere for every
round hemisphere
\tau^{-}\subset\hat{\sigma}
with
\partial\tau^{-}=\sigma
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\tau\subset\partial_{T}X
be a round
n
-hemisphere with
\tau\cap\hat{\sigma}=\partial\tau
. Then

\tau\cup\hat{\sigma}

is a convex subset in
\partial_{T}X
which splits off
\partial\tau
. Thus
\partial\tau

is a singular hypersphere in
\hat{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
\zeta
be the center of
\tau
.
Denote by
\zeta^{\pm}\in\hat{\sigma}
the two points perpendicular to
\partial\tau
.
Since
\hat{\sigma}
is the Tits boundary of a Morse flat,
\zeta
has to be antipodal to both points
\zeta^{\pm}
(Proposition
2.15
).
Thus, the claim follows from the Lune Lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.2

(
[
Sta22a
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space. Suppose that
X
contains a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat
\hat{F}
with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
. Then
the complement in
\partial_{T}F
of the set of regular points
\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
can be covered by a finite set of singular spheres of positive codimension.

In the following we denote by
\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{s}\subset\hat{\sigma}
the finitely many singular hyperspheres provided by
Theorem
5.2
.
For a point
\xi\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
we define

C_{\xi}:=\bigcap_{\xi\in\tilde{\sigma}}\tilde{\sigma}\subset\hat{\sigma}

as the intersection of all round
n
-spheres
\tilde{\sigma}
in
\partial_{T}X
containing
\xi
.
By Theorem
5.2
,
C_{\xi}
contains a neighborhood of
\xi
and therefore has dimension
n
.
Our next aim is to show that
C_{\xi}
agrees with the closure of one of the components of
\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}
, in
particular it’s an
n
-dimensional spherical polytope.
We need some preparation.

Recall that by Rademacher’s theorem, almost every point
p
in the boundary
\partial C
of an
n
-dimensional closed convex subset
C
in
S^{n}

has a Euclidean tangent space,
T_{p}\partial C\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space and
\hat{F}\subset X
a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
.
Let
\hat{F}^{\prime}\subset X
be a second
(n+1)
-flat with
\hat{\sigma}^{\prime}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
\alpha=\hat{\sigma}\cap\hat{\sigma}^{\prime}
is non-empty and of dimension
n
.
Then for every point
\xi\in\partial\alpha
with
T_{\xi}\partial\alpha\cong\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, there exists a singular hypersphere
\sigma\subset\hat{\sigma}

which
is tangent to
\alpha
at
\xi
.
More precisely,
\xi\in\sigma
and
\alpha\cap\sigma\subset\partial\alpha
.

Proof.

Since
X
is locally compact and
\hat{F}
is Morse, there exists
R>0
, such that
\hat{F}

does not bound a flat strip of width
R
. Set
C_{R}:=\{x\in\hat{F}:\ |x,\hat{F}^{\prime}|\leq R\}

and pick a base point
o\in\partial C_{R}
. Note that
C_{R}
is a closed convex subset of
\hat{F}
.
If
C_{o}(\alpha)
denotes the cone from
o
over
\alpha
, then
C_{o}(\alpha)\subset C_{R}
and
C_{R}

has sublinear distance from
C_{o}(\alpha)
in the sense

\frac{|C_{o}(\alpha)\cap B_{r}(o),C_{R}\cap B_{r}(o)|}{r}\to 0.

Thus, for
\xi\in\partial\alpha
, we can find a sequence
x_{k}\in\partial C_{R}
such that
x_{k}\to\xi
. Denote by
x_{k}^{\prime}\in\hat{F}^{\prime}

the closest point to
x_{k}
. Thus
|x_{k},x_{k}^{\prime}|=R
. Now we use elements
\gamma_{k}
in the stabilizer of
\hat{F}
to move
x_{k}

to a fixed compact subset of
\hat{F}
. We may assume that each
\gamma_{k}
acts as a translation on
\hat{F}
and therefore fixes
\hat{\sigma}

pointwise. Passing to a subsequence, we obtain limits
\gamma_{k}(x_{k})\to x_{\infty}
and
\gamma_{k}(\hat{F}^{\prime},x_{k}^{\prime})\to(\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime},x_{\infty}^{\prime})
.
By construction,
|x_{\infty},\hat{F}^{\prime}_{\infty}|=|x_{\infty},x^{\prime}_{\infty}|=R
. In particular,
\hat{F}^{\prime}_{\infty}
cannot be parallel to
\hat{F}
.
We claim that
\hat{F}^{\prime}_{\infty}
is asymptotic to
\hat{F}
along an
(n+1)
-dimensional flat half-space.
To see this, note first that
\angle_{o}(x_{k},\xi)\to 0
and therefore
\angle_{x_{k}}(o,\xi)=\pi-\angle_{o}(x_{k},\xi)\to\pi
.
Thus we have the pointed convergences
\gamma_{k}(x_{k}\xi,x_{k})\to(x_{\infty}\xi,x_{\infty})

and
\gamma_{k}(x_{k}o,x_{k})\to(x_{\infty}\hat{\xi},x_{\infty})
where
\hat{\xi}
is the antipode of
\xi
in
\hat{\sigma}
.
We denote by
c
the complete geodesic through
x_{\infty}
which is asymptotic to the points
\xi,\hat{\xi}
.
Since the ray
x_{k}\xi
and the segment
x_{k}o
are contained in
C_{R}
, we conclude
c\subset\bar{N}_{R}(\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime})
.
(Thus
c
is parallel to
\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}
and since
|x_{\infty},\hat{F}^{\prime}_{\infty}|=R
, it has distance
R
from
\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}
.)
In particular,
\xi,\hat{\xi}\in\partial_{T}\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}
. Since
\alpha
is fixed by
\gamma_{k}
and contained in
\hat{\sigma}^{\prime}
, we also have
\alpha\subset\partial_{T}\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}
.
Therefore,
\partial_{T}\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}
has to contain the convex hull of
\hat{\xi}
and
\alpha
in
\hat{\sigma}
.
By assumption, the tangent space to
\partial\alpha
at
\xi
is
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
(and therefore the tangent space to
\alpha

at
\xi
is an
n
-dimensional Euclidean half-space). It follows that this convex hull has to contain the round
n
-hemisphere

\hat{\tau}\subset\hat{\sigma}
determined by
\hat{\xi}
and
\alpha
. Since
\hat{F}^{\prime}_{\infty}
is not parallel to
\hat{F}
, we conclude

\partial_{T}\hat{F}_{\infty}^{\prime}\cap\hat{\sigma}=\hat{\tau}
. Thus
\sigma=\partial\hat{\tau}
is a singular hypersphere as claimed.
∎

Corollary 5.4
.

Let
X
be a locally compact CAT(0) space and
\hat{F}\subset X
a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
.
Let
\hat{F}^{\prime}\subset X
be a second
(n+1)
-flat with
\hat{\sigma}^{\prime}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
\alpha=\hat{\sigma}\cap\hat{\sigma}^{\prime}
is non-empty and of dimension

. Then
there exists finitely many singular hyperspheres
\{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{k}\}
in
\hat{\sigma}
such that

is the closure of a component of

\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\sigma^{i}
. In particular,

\alpha
is an
n
-dimensional spherical polytope.

Proof.

By Proposition
5.3
, almost every point in
\partial\alpha
is tangent to one of the singular hyperspheres
\sigma^{i}
.
Let
\{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{k}\}
denote those which are tangent to some point in
\partial\alpha
.
It follows that
\alpha
has to agree with the closure of a component of
\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\sigma^{i}
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

There exists finitely many round singular hyperspheres
\{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{s}\}
in
\hat{\sigma}
such that
for every point

\xi\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
, the set

C_{\xi}
is the closure of one of the components of

\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}
. In particular,
there are only finitely many distinct

and
each

is an
n
-dimensional spherical polytope.

Proof.

Choose a point

\xi\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
.
Recall that
C_{\xi}
is the intersection of all round
n
-spheres in
\partial_{T}X
which contain the point
\xi
.
By Corollary
5.4
, every finite such intersection has the claimed structure.
Since by Theorem
5.2
there are only finitely many singular hyperspheres,
the set

can be obtained as a finite intersection of round
n
-spheres.
At last, observe that if

is bounded by the hyperspheres
\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{j}
, then none of the other singular hyperspheres
\sigma^{i}

can cut through

, since, by Lemma

spans a round
n

\hat{\sigma}\setminus\sigma^{i}
.
The claim follows.
∎

5.2.
Periodic Morse flats and symmetric sets

In this section we consider a locally compact CAT(0) space
X
with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X

and such that every geodesic in
X
lies in an
(n+1)
-flat,
n\geq 1
. We
will show that a

\Gamma
-periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat
\hat{F}\subset X
leads to a non-empty proper closed symmetric
\Gamma
-invariant subset in
\partial_{\infty}X
and therefore, by Proposition
4.4
, to a non-trivial submetry of the
Tits boundary. The proof is dynamical and we will freely use the notation and results from Section
2.8
.

As before, we will set
\hat{\sigma}=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
and denote the finitely many singular hyperspheres in
\hat{\sigma}
by
\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{s}
.
Moreover, we define a
chamber (in
\hat{\sigma}
)
to be the closure of one of the components of
\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}
.
By Corollary
5.5
, for each regular point
\xi\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
the intersection of all round
n
-spheres in
\partial_{T}X
which contain
\xi
is a chamber.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
C\subset\hat{\sigma}
be a chamber and let
\xi
be a point in the interior of
C
. If
\hat{\xi}\in\partial_{T}X

is antipodal to
\xi
,
then there exists an
(n+1)
-flat
F\subset X
such

and
\hat{\xi}
are contained in
\partial_{T}F
.

Proof.

If
\hat{\xi}
is visually antipodal to
\xi
, then the claim follows since every geodesic in
X

lies in an
(n+1)
-flat and
C
is the intersection of all round
n
-spheres in
\partial_{T}X

which contain a regular point from

(Corollary
5.5
).
By Lemma
3.2
, there exists a sequence
(\hat{\xi}_{k})
of visual antipodes of
\xi
with
\hat{\xi}_{k}\to\hat{\xi}
.
Denote by
F_{k}
a
(n+1)
-flat such that
\sigma_{k}:=\partial_{T}F_{k}
contains
C
and
\hat{\xi}_{k}
.
Let
f_{k}:S^{n}\to\partial_{T}X
be an isometric embedding with image
\sigma_{k}
.
Passing to a subsequence, we can assume that

converges pointwise with respect to the cone topology to a 1-Lipschitz map

. Since the image of

contains
C
and
\hat{\xi}

n
-sphere

Because

cannot bound a round hemisphere in

.
Thus
[
Lee00
, Proposition 2.1]
implies the claim.
∎

In order to show that chambers are incompressible we will make use of the following.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
C\subset\hat{\sigma}
be a chamber and
\gamma\in\Gamma
. Suppose that
\xi\in\gamma C

is a point such that
\gamma^{-1}\xi
is regular in
C
. Let
\hat{\xi}\in\hat{\sigma}
be an antipode of
\xi
.
Then
\hat{\xi}
is regular as well. More precisely, if
s>0
is such that
B_{s}(\xi)\subset\gamma C
,
then there exists a chamber
\hat{C}\subset\hat{\sigma}
such that
B_{s}(\hat{\xi})\subset\hat{C}
.

Proof.

Choose
s>0
as in the assumption and
let
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}\in B_{s}(\hat{\xi})
. Extend the geodesic
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}\hat{\xi}
inside
\hat{\sigma}
to a point
\hat{\xi}^{\prime\prime}\in\hat{\sigma}

such that
\hat{\xi}\hat{\xi}^{\prime\prime}
lies in a single chamber
\hat{C}\subset\hat{\sigma}
. Choose a regular point
\hat{\eta}\in\hat{C}\cap B_{s}(\hat{\xi})
.
Extend the geodesic
\hat{\eta}\xi
to antipode
\eta
. Since
|\eta,\xi|>\pi-s
and
B_{s}(\xi)\subset\gamma C
, we have
\eta\in\gamma C
.
By Lemma
5.6
, we find a round
n
-sphere
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
which contains the chamber
\hat{C}
and the point
\eta
, and therefore
also the translated chamber
\gamma C
. In particular, we see that
\xi
is the only antipode of
\hat{\xi}
in
\gamma C
and
\sigma
is given by the union of all geodesics
from
\xi
to
\hat{\xi}
. Now we extend the geodesic
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}\hat{\xi}^{\prime\prime}
inside
\sigma
to a geodesic
c^{\prime}
of length
\pi
ending in an antipode
\xi^{\prime}
of
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
.
Since
|\hat{\xi}^{\prime},\hat{\xi}|<s
we see that
\xi^{\prime}\in B_{s}(\xi)\subset\gamma C
. By Lemma
5.6
, we find a round
n
-sphere
\sigma^{\prime}

in
\partial_{T}X
which contains the translated chamber
\gamma C
and the point
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
. Again,
\xi^{\prime}
is the only antipode of
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
in
\gamma C

and therefore
\sigma^{\prime}
is the union of all geodesics from
\xi^{\prime}
to
\hat{\xi}^{\prime}
. In particular,
\sigma^{\prime}
contains the geodesic

and therefore also the point
\hat{\xi}
.
But now both round
n
-spheres
\sigma
and
\sigma^{\prime}
contain the point
\hat{\xi}
and the translated chamber
\gamma C
. Since
\xi
lies in the interior of
\gamma C

and
\partial_{T}X
is CAT(1), this implies
\sigma=\sigma^{\prime}
. Consequently,
B_{s}(\hat{\xi})\subset\sigma
. Since

is a round
n
-sphere, we must have

B_{s}(\hat{\xi})\subset\hat{\sigma}
as claimed.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
C\subset\hat{\sigma}
be a chamber, then
C
is incompressible.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a regular point in
C
and assume
(\gamma_{k})
is a sequence in
\Gamma

with
\eta_{k}\to\xi
with
\eta_{k}=\gamma_{k}\eta
. We need to show that a limit of
\gamma_{k}C
with respect to the cone topology is isometric to

.

Let
\hat{\xi}
be an antipode of
\xi
in
\hat{\sigma}
(Corollary
2.16
).
Since the diameter of
\partial_{T}X
is equal to
\pi

|\hat{\xi},\eta_{k}|\to\pi
.
Extend the geodesic
\eta_{k}\hat{\xi}
inside
\hat{\sigma}
to an antipode
\hat{\xi}_{k}\in\hat{\sigma}
. Then
\hat{\xi}_{k}\to\hat{\xi}
.
By Lemma
5.7
, if
s_{0}>0

B_{s_{0}}(\eta)\subset C

B_{s_{0}}(\hat{\xi}_{k})\subset\hat{\sigma}
.
Therefore
\hat{\xi}
is regular and if

denotes the chamber containing
\hat{\xi}
, then by Lemma
5.6
,
we find a round
n

\hat{C}
and
\gamma_{k}C

with image

∎

Lemma 5.9
.

The chambers of
\hat{\sigma}
are precisely the maximal incompressible sets in
\hat{\sigma}
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma^{1}
be a singular hypersphere in
\hat{\sigma}
and let

be a round
n
-hemisphere
with
\tau\cap\hat{\sigma}=\sigma^{1}

\tau^{\pm}\subset\hat{\sigma}
the two round
n
-hemispheres
determined by

is the boundary of a Morse flat, the two round

. Thus by
[

, Proposition 2.1]
, there are
(n+1)
-flats

Following

Lemma 5.13

Every chamber in

.

If

Proof.

Denote by
T^{\nu}
the folding map

Let
\mu

Let

Let
\xi

\hat{\xi}
of

By Corollary

5.3.
Induced submetries and the dimension of the base

For this section we assume that
X
is a non-flat locally compact CAT(0) space
with a geometric group action
\Gamma\curvearrowright X
and where every geodesic lies in an
(n+1)
-flat.
In particular,
X
is geodesically complete. Moreover, we assume that
X
contains a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat
\hat{F}
with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
.
By Proposition
5.14
, this means that the ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}X

contains a non-trivial proper closed symmetric
\Gamma
-invariant subset. Thus, by Proposition
4.4
, we have a non-trivial submetry

\delta:\partial_{T}X\to\Delta.

Our main goal is:

Proposition 5.15
.

Let
X
be a non-flat locally compact CAT(0) space where every geodesic lies in an
(n+1)
-flat.
Suppose that
X
contains a periodic Morse
(n+1)
-flat
\hat{F}
with
\hat{\sigma}:=\partial_{T}\hat{F}
.
Moreover, assume
\dim(\partial_{T}X)=m
and that there exists a family of round
m
-spheres in
\partial_{T}X
which are dense
with respect to the cone topology.
If
\partial_{T}X
is irreducible,
then
\Delta
is an
n
-dimensional spherical orbifold.

Our starting point is the observation that
\delta
restricts to a submetry on certain round spheres in
\partial_{T}X
, including top-dimensional ones and
Tits boundaries of Morse flats.

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
\sigma\subset\partial_{T}X
be a round sphere such that every direction at a point
\xi\in\sigma
has an antipode in

\Sigma_{\xi}\sigma
. Then the restriction
\delta_{\sigma}:=\delta|_{\sigma}
is a submetry
\delta_{\sigma}:\sigma\to\Delta
and every
\delta_{\sigma}
-horizontal geodesic is
also
\delta
-horizontal. In particular, this applies if
\dim(\sigma)=\dim(\partial_{T}X)
or if
\sigma
is the Tits boundary of a periodic Morse flat.

Proof.

By assumption, every point in
\partial_{T}X
has an antipode in
\sigma
. Thus
\delta_{\sigma}
is surjective.
Intersecting the fibers of
\delta
with
\sigma
clearly gives closed symmetric subsets in
\sigma
.
It only remains to show that they are equidistant. Let
\xi
be a point in
\sigma
and
\xi\eta

a
\delta
-horizontal geodesic in
\partial_{T}X
. Then we can extend
\eta\xi
up to an antipode
\hat{\eta}\in\sigma
.
Further, denote by
\eta^{\prime}
the antipode of
\hat{\eta}
in
\sigma
. Then
\xi\eta^{\prime}
is
\delta
-horizontal
and realizes the distance from
\xi
to the
\delta
-fiber through
\eta
. This shows the claim.
The supplement follows from
[
BL06
, Lemma 2.1]
and Corollary
2.16
.
∎

Let
\hat{\delta}:\hat{\sigma}\to\Delta
be the induced submetry obtained from restricting
\delta
.
To get control on
\delta
, our strategy is to study
\hat{\delta}
first.

We denote
by
\{\sigma^{1},\ldots,\sigma^{s}\}
a set of singular hyperspheres in
\hat{\sigma}
provided by Corollary
5.5
.
For every regular point

\xi\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
holds that
C_{\xi}
agrees with the closure of one of the components of

\hat{\sigma}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}
. Note that
s
will denote the number of singular hyperspheres.
The upshot is that the hyperspheres
\sigma^{i}\subset\hat{\sigma}
act as “mirrors” for horizontal geodesics.
This allows us to understand
\hat{\delta}
.

Lemma 5.17
.

Denote by
f_{i}:\hat{\sigma}\to\hat{\sigma}
the reflection at
\sigma^{i}\subset\hat{\sigma}
.
Then for all
1\leq i\leq s
, the submetry
\hat{\delta}
is invariant under
f_{i}
, namely
\hat{\delta}=\hat{\delta}\circ f_{i}
.

Proof.

Denote by
\tau^{\pm}\subset\hat{\sigma}
the two round hemispheres determined by
\sigma^{i}
.
Let
\tau_{i}\subset\partial_{T}X
be a round
n
-hemisphere with
\tau_{i}\cap\hat{\sigma}=\sigma^{i}
.
By Lemma
5.1
, the union
\hat{\sigma}\cup\tau_{i}
is a convex subset of
\partial_{T}X
which splits off
\sigma^{i}
.
Thus
\xi
and
f_{i}(\xi)
have a common antipode in
\tau_{i}
.
∎

This motivates the following terminology.

Definition 5.18
.

The finitely many singular hyperspheres
\sigma^{i}\subset\hat{\sigma}
,
1\leq i\leq s
,
will be called
reflecting hyperspheres
.

Next we show that horizontal geodesics in
\hat{\sigma}
can only branch off at a point on a reflecting hypersphere.
This requires the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 5.19
.

Let
\tilde{\xi}\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
be a regular point. Further, let
\xi\in C_{\tilde{\xi}}

and
\hat{\xi}\in\operatorname{Ant}_{vis}(\xi)
. Then there exists an
(n+1)
-flat
F\subset X

which contains the set
C_{\tilde{\xi}}
and the point
\hat{\xi}
in its ideal boundary
\partial_{\infty}F
.

Proof.

Let
c\subset X
be a complete geodesic with
\partial_{\infty}c=\{\xi,\hat{\xi}\}
.
Choose a sequence
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
of points in

with
x_{k}\to\hat{\xi}
.
Let
F_{k}\subset X
be an
(n+1)
-flat which contains the geodesic ray
x_{k}\tilde{\xi}
.
By definition,
C_{\tilde{\xi}}\subset\partial_{\infty}F_{k}
. In particular, the geodesic ray
x_{k}\xi

lies in
F_{k}
. By local compactness, we can extract a limit flat

with the desired properties.
∎

Lemma 5.20
.

Suppose that
\xi\eta\subset\hat{\sigma}
is a piecewise horizontal geodesic which branches at the point
\eta
.
Then there exists a reflecting hypersphere
\sigma^{i_{0}}
which contains
\eta
and such that
\xi\eta
is transversal to
\sigma^{i_{0}}
,
i.e. not contained in
\sigma^{i_{0}}
.

Proof.

After possibly changing
\xi
to an interior point of
\xi\eta
, we may assume that
\xi\eta
is contained in
C_{\tilde{\xi}}
for some
\tilde{\xi}\in\hat{\sigma}_{reg}
.
If the segment
\xi\eta
branches at
\eta
, then we can extend it to a piecewise horizontal geodesic

joining
\xi
to an antipode
\hat{\xi}

such that
\gamma
intersects
\hat{\sigma}
precisely in
\xi\eta
. Now suppose for contradiction that the claim fails.
Since
C_{\tilde{\xi}}
is a spherical polytope bounded by the hyperspheres

, this can only happen if we can extend
\xi\eta

inside
C_{\tilde{\xi}}
to a point
\eta^{\prime}
. By Lemma
3.2
, we find a sequence
\hat{\xi}_{k}
of visual antipodes of
\xi

which converge to
\hat{\xi}
in the cone topology. From Lemma
5.19

(n+1)
-flats

with

and
\hat{\xi}_{k}\in\partial_{\infty}F_{k}

\gamma_{k}\subset\partial_{T}F_{k}
from
\xi

to
\hat{\xi}
through the point
\eta
also contains the point
\eta^{\prime}
. Passing to a limit with respect to the cone topology, we obtain a geodesic
\gamma_{\infty}\subset\partial_{T}X

from
\xi

\hat{\xi}
which still contains the segment
\xi\eta^{\prime}
.
Since
\gamma

\xi\eta

\gamma=\gamma_{\infty}
. Contradiction.
∎

An immediate consequence is the following.

Corollary 5.21
.

Suppose that

lies in a single reflecting hypersphere, say
\xi\in\sigma^{1}\setminus\bigcup_{i\neq 1}\sigma^{i}

If a horizontal direction

has an antipode in

Corollary 5.22
.

Suppose that
\sigma:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}\neq\emptyset
. Then
\partial_{T}X
splits non-trivially as a spherical join.

Proof.

Pick a point
\xi\in\sigma
. By Lemma
5.16
,
\delta
restricts to a submetry
\hat{\delta}:\hat{\sigma}\to\Delta

such that
\hat{\delta}
-horizontal directions are
\delta
-horizontal.
In particular, there exists a
\delta
-horizontal direction

.
By Lemma
5.20
, the piecewise
\delta
-horizontal geodesic
\gamma
in direction
v

cannot branch since no
\sigma^{i}
is transversal to
\gamma
. The claim follows from Lemma
4.7
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
5.15
.

Since
\partial_{T}X
is irreducible, Corollary
5.22

implies
\bigcap_{i=1}^{s}\sigma^{i}=\emptyset
. Thus after renumbering, we may assume

By Lemma
5.17
and Proposition

, either every geodesic in
\hat{\sigma}

\hat{\delta}

5.4.
Dimension of the Tits boundary and rigidity

The aim of this section is to prove the
Main Theorem
.
We begin with some auxiliary observations.

Corollary 5.23
.

Suppose that there exists a geodesic
\gamma\subset\hat{\sigma}
of length
\pi
whose interior intersects only one of the reflecting hyperspheres.
Moreover, assume that the intersection is perpendicular at the midpoint.
Then
\partial_{\infty}X
contains a proper subset which is closed convex and symmetric.

Proof.

Let’s assume that
\gamma
intersects
\sigma^{1}
at its midpoint.
By assumption

\partial\gamma\subset\bigcap_{i=2}^{s}\sigma^{i}
. Thus
\hat{\sigma}=\sigma^{+}\circ\sigma^{-}
with
\sigma^{+}=\partial\gamma
and
\sigma^{-}=\sigma^{1}
.
Thus the claim follows from Proposition
4.18
.
∎

The following is an immediate consequence of the chain rule and the fact that the fibers of

By Theorem

\bar{\delta}
-horizontal directions and
\hat{\delta}

From Proposition

we know that either

Now let us assume that

Then, by Lemma

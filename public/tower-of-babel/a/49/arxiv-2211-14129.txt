II
Mass and current coupling of the tetraquark
X

In this section, we consider the scalar diquark-antidiquark state
X=[cs][\overline{c}\overline{s}]
and extract its spectroscopic parameters from the
two-point sum rule analysis
Shifman:1978bx
;
Shifman:1978by
. It is
known that the sum rule method operates with correlation functions and
interpolating currents of particles under investigations. There are
different ways to construct a scalar tetraquark and corresponding current
using a diquark and an antidiquark with different spin-parities
Chen:2010ze
. Thus, one may construct such state using the pseudoscalar
c^{T}Cs
or vector
c^{T}C\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}s
diquarks and
corresponding antidiquarks, where
C
is the charge-conjugation operator.
But, we assume that
X
is built of a scalar diquark
c^{T}C\gamma_{5}s

and antidiquark
\overline{c}\gamma_{5}C\overline{s}^{T}
: The reason is
that the scalar diquark (antidiquark) configuration is the most attractive
and stable two-quark system
Jaffe:2004ph
.

The structures
\epsilon c^{T}C\gamma_{5}s
and
\widetilde{\epsilon}\overline{c}\gamma_{5}C\overline{s}^{T}
are the color antitriplet and
triplet states of the color
SU_{c}(3)
group, respectively. Then the
interpolating current for the tetraquark
X
has the form

J(x)=\epsilon\widetilde{\epsilon}\left[c_{b}^{T}(x)C\gamma_{5}s_{c}(x)\right]\left[\overline{c}_{m}(x)\gamma_{5}C\overline{s}_{n}^{T}(x)\right],

(2)

where
\epsilon\widetilde{\epsilon}=\epsilon^{abc}\epsilon^{amn}
and
a,b,c,m
and
n
are color indices. This current belongs to
[\overline{\mathbf{3}}_{c}]_{cs}\otimes[\mathbf{3}_{c}]_{\overline{c}\overline{s}}
representation of the color group and corresponds to the scalar state
with quantum numbers
J^{\mathrm{PC}}=0^{++}
. The current
J(x)
describes
a ground-state scalar particle with lowest mass and required spin-parities.

The mass
m
and coupling
f
of the tetraquark
X
can be determined from
analysis of the correlation function
\Pi(p)

\Pi(p)=i\int d^{4}xe^{ipx}\langle 0|\mathcal{T}\{J(x)J^{{\dagger}}(0)\}|0\rangle.

(3)

To derive the required sum rules, one has to express
\Pi(p)
using the
spectroscopic parameters of the tetraquark
X
. For these purposes, we
insert into the correlation function
\Pi(p)
a complete set of states with
quantum numbers
0^{++}
and perform integration over
x
in Eq. (
3
). As a result, we get

\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p)=\frac{\langle 0|J|X(p\rangle\langle X(p)|J^{\dagger}|0\rangle}{m^{2}-p^{2}}+\cdots.

(4)

The obtained expression forms a hadronic representation of
\Pi(p)
and is the
phenomenological (physical) side of sum rule. Here, the contribution coming
from the ground-state particle
X
is written down explicitly, whereas
contributions of higher resonances and continuum states are denoted by
the ellipses.

The function
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p)
can be further simplified by
employing the matrix element

\langle 0|J|X(p)\rangle=fm.

(5)

It is easy to find that, in terms of the parameters
m
and
f
, the
function
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p)
takes the following form

\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p)=\frac{m^{2}f^{2}}{m^{2}-p^{2}}+\cdots.

(6)

The
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p)
has simple Lorentz structure proportional to
\mathrm{I}
, and relevant invariant amplitude
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p^{2})

is given by r.h.s. of Eq. (
6
).

To determine the QCD side of the sum rules
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p)
, we use
the interpolating current
J(x)
in Eq. (
3
), and contract the heavy
and light quark fields. After simple manipulations, we obtain

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p)=i\int d^{4}xe^{ipx}\epsilon\widetilde{\epsilon}\epsilon^{\prime}\widetilde{\epsilon}^{\prime}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{c}^{bb^{\prime}}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\gamma_{5}S_{s}^{cc^{\prime}}(x)\right]\mathrm{Tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{s}^{n^{\prime}n}(-x)\gamma_{5}S_{c}^{m^{\prime}m}(-x)\right],

(7)

where
S_{c}(x)
and
S_{s}(x)
are the
c
and
s
-quark propagators,
respectively. Explicit expressions of these propagators are presented in
the Appendix (see, also Ref.
Agaev:2020zad
). In Eq. (
7

we have also used the notation

\widetilde{S}_{c(s)}(x)=CS_{c(s)}^{T}(x)C.

(8)

The correlation function
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p)
should be computed in the
operator product expansion (
\mathrm{OPE}
) with some accuracy. The
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p)
has also a trivial structure
\sim\mathrm{I}
and is

\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2})

the invariant amplitudes
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p^{2})
and
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2})

After these operations, the sum rule equality starts to depend on the Borel

The Borel transformation of

\Pi(M^{2},s_{0})=\int_{4\mathcal{M}^{2}}^{s_{0}}ds\rho^{\mathrm{OPE}}(s)e^{-s/M^{2}}+\Pi(M^{2}),

(9)

where
\mathcal{M}=m_{c}+m_{s}

, but include into analysis terms proportional to

two-point spectral density
\rho^{\mathrm{OPE}}(s)
is calculated as an

\Pi(M^{2})

includes nonperturbative contributions extracted directly from
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p)

\Pi(M^{2},s_{0})
is computed by taking
into account nonperturbative terms up to dimension
10

of

is written down in the Appendix.

(10)

(11)

where

Decay

The spectroscopic parameters of the tetraquark
X
form a basis to determine
its kinematically allowed decay channels. Because
X(3960)
was observed in
the
D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
invariant mass distribution, we treat the decay
X\rightarrow D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
as a dominant mode of
X
. The two-meson
threshold for this process
\approx 3937~\mathrm{MeV}
is below the mass of
X
. Other decay channels that should be considered in this paper are
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
and
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta
.
The kinematical limits for realization of these processes do not exceed
\approx 3941~\mathrm{MeV}
which is less than
m
as well. It is easy to see
also that decays of the scalar tetraquark with spin-parities
J^{\mathrm{PC}}=0^{++}
to two pseudoscalar mesons with
J^{\mathrm{PC}}=0^{-+}
preserves
the spin and quantum numbers
\mathrm{P}
and
\mathrm{C}
of the initial
state
X
.

The partial width of the decay
X\rightarrow D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
is
determined by a coupling
G
that describes the strong interaction at the vertex

XD_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
. Apart from
G
, it depends also on the masses and decay
constants of the initial and final particles. The mass and coupling of
X

have been calculated in the present article, whereas physical parameters of
the mesons
D_{s}^{+}
and
D_{s}^{-}
are known from other sources.
Therefore, the only physical quantity to be found here is the strong coupling
G
.

To evaluate
G
, we use the QCD three-point sum rule method, and start our
analysis from the correlation function

\displaystyle\Pi(p,p^{\prime})=i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}ye^{i(p^{\prime}y-px)}\langle 0|\mathcal{T}\{J^{D_{s}^{+}}(y)

\displaystyle\times J^{D_{s}^{-}}(0)J^{\dagger}(x)\}|0\rangle,

(18)

where
J(x)
,
\ J^{D_{s}^{+}}(y)
and
J^{D_{s}^{-}}(0)
are the
interpolating currents for the tetraquark
X
, and the pseudoscalar mesons
D_{s}^{+}
and
D_{s}^{-}
, respectively. The four-momenta of
X
and
D_{s}^{+}
are denoted by
p
and
p^{\prime}
, whereas the momentum of the
meson
D_{s}^{-}
is equal to
q=p-p^{\prime}
. The current
J(x)
is given
by Eq. (
2
), whereas for the mesons, we use the following currents:

\displaystyle\ J^{D_{s}^{+}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{s}_{j}(x)i\gamma_{5}c_{j}(x),

\displaystyle J^{D_{s}^{-}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{c}_{i}(x)i\gamma_{5}s_{i}(x),

(19)

with
i
and
j
being the color indices.

To continue our study of the strong coupling
G
, we follow usual recipes of
the sum rule method and compute the correlation function
\Pi(p,p^{\prime})
. To this end, we employ the physical parameters of the tetraquark and
mesons participating in this process. The correlator
\Pi(p,p^{\prime})

found by this way constitutes the phenomenological side
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
of the sum rule. It is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{\langle 0|J^{D_{s}^{+}}|D_{s}^{+}(p^{\prime})\rangle\langle 0|J^{D_{s}^{-}}|D_{s}^{-}(q)\rangle}{(p^{2}-m^{2})(p^{\prime 2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\langle D_{s}^{-}(q)D_{s}^{+}(p^{\prime})|X(p)\rangle\langle X(p)|J^{\dagger}|0\rangle}{(q^{2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2})}+\cdots,

(20)

where
m_{D_{s}}
is the mass of the mesons
D_{s}^{\pm}
. To derive Eq. (
20
), we isolate the contribution of the ground-state particles
from ones due to higher resonances and continuum states. In Eq. (
20
) the ground-state term is presented explicitly, whereas the dots
stand for the other contributions.

The function
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
can be modified by
employing the matrix elements of the mesons
D_{s}^{\pm}

\langle 0|J^{D_{s}^{\pm}}|D_{s}^{\pm}\rangle=\frac{m_{D_{s}}^{2}f_{D_{s}}}{m_{c}+m_{s}},\

(21)

with
f_{D_{s}}
being their decay constants. The vertex
XD_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
is modeled as

\langle D_{s}^{-}(q)D_{s}^{+}(p^{\prime})|X(p)\rangle=G(q^{2})p\cdot p^{\prime}.

(22)

Using these matrix elements, one can easily find a new expression for
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
:

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})=G(q^{2})\frac{m_{D_{s}}^{4}f_{D_{s}}^{2}fm}{(m_{c}+m_{s})^{2}(p^{2}-m^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p^{\prime 2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2})(q^{2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2})}\frac{m^{2}+m_{D_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}}{2}+\cdots.

(23)

The double Borel transformation of the correlation function
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
over variables
p^{2}
and
p^{\prime 2}
is given by
the formula

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})=G(q^{2})\frac{m_{D_{s}}^{4}f_{D_{s}}^{2}fm}{(m_{c}+m_{s})^{2}(q^{2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2})}e^{-m^{2}/M_{1}^{2}}

\displaystyle\times e^{-m_{D_{s}}^{2}/M_{2}^{2}}\frac{m^{2}+m_{D_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}}{2}+\cdots.

(24)

The correlator
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
and its Borel
transformation have a simple Lorentz structure which is proportional to
\mathrm{I}
. As a result, the relevant invariant amplitude
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
is determined by the whole expression
written down in Eq. (
23
).

To derive the QCD side of the three-point sum rule, we express
\Pi(p,p^{\prime})
in terms of the quark propagators, and get

\displaystyle\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p,p^{\prime})=\int d^{4}xd^{4}ye^{i(p^{\prime}y-px)}\epsilon\widetilde{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{c}^{ib}(y-x)\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{s}^{ni}(x-y)\gamma_{5}S_{c}^{mj}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\gamma_{5}S_{s}^{jc}(-x)\right],

(25)

The correlator
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p,p^{\prime})
is computed by taking
into account the nonperturbative contributions up to dimension
6
. This
function contains the same trivial Lorentz structure as
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
. Having denoted by
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
the corresponding invariant amplitude, equated the double Borel
transformations
\mathcal{B}\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})

and
\mathcal{B}\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
, and
performed continuum subtraction, we find the sum rule for the strong
coupling
G(q^{2})
.

The amplitude
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
after the
Borel transformation and continuum subtraction procedures can be expressed
using the spectral density
\rho(s,s^{\prime},q^{2})
which is
proportional to a relevant imaginary part of
\Pi^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p,p^{\prime})

\displaystyle\Pi(\mathbf{M}^{2},\mathbf{s}_{0},q^{2})=\int_{4\mathcal{M}^{2}}^{s_{0}}ds\int_{\mathcal{M}^{2}}^{s_{0}^{\prime}}ds^{\prime}\rho(s,s^{\prime},q^{2})

\displaystyle\times e^{-s/M_{1}^{2}}e^{-s^{\prime}/M_{2}^{2}}.

(26)

The Borel and continuum threshold parameters are denoted in Eq. (
26
) by
\mathbf{M}^{2}=(M_{1}^{2},\ M_{2}^{2})
and
\mathbf{s}_{0}=(s_{0},\ s_{0}^{\prime})
, respectively. Then, the sum rule for
G(q^{2})
reads

\displaystyle G(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2(m_{c}+m_{s})^{2}}{m_{D_{s}}^{4}f_{D_{s}}^{2}fm}\frac{q^{2}-m_{D_{s}}^{2}}{m^{2}+m_{D_{s}}^{2}-q^{2}}

(27)

\displaystyle\times e^{m^{2}/M_{1}^{2}}e^{m_{D_{s}}^{2}/M_{2}^{2}}\Pi(\mathbf{M}^{2},\mathbf{s}_{0},q^{2}).

The coupling
G(q^{2})
is also a function of the Borel and continuum
threshold parameters, which, for the sake of simplicity, are not shown in
Eq. (
27
). In what follows, we introduce a variable
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
and label the obtained function
G(Q^{2})
.

Equation (
27
) contains the spectroscopic parameters of the
tetraquark
X
, and the masses and decay constants of the mesons
D_{s}^{\pm}
. These parameters are input information for our numerical computations:
Their values are collected in Table
1
, which contains also
parameters of the mesons
\eta_{c}
,
\eta^{\prime}
and
\eta

appearing at final stages of the other processes. For the masses of the
mesons and decay constant
f_{D_{s}}
, we use information from Ref.
PDG:2022
. As the decay constant of the meson
\eta_{c}
, we employ sum
rule’s prediction from Ref.
Colangelo:1992cx
.

For numerical calculations of
G(Q^{2})
one has to fix working windows for
the Borel and continuum subtraction parameters
\mathbf{M}^{2}
and
\mathbf{s}_{0}
. The constraints imposed on
\mathbf{M}^{2}
and
\mathbf{s}_{0}

are usual for sum rule calculations: They have been discussed and explained
in the section

. The regions for

and

). The
parameters

(28)

) are well correlated with the
D_{s}^{+}

\sqrt{s_{0}^{\prime}}\approx(m_{D_{s}}+0.35)\ \mathrm{GeV}

M_{2}

.

Thus, we calculate

at fixed

(29)

(33)

\displaystyle\left.-2\left(a^{2}b^{2}+a^{2}c^{2}+b^{2}c^{2}\right)\right]^{1/2}.

), it is easy to find

IV
Processes
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}

and
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta

The processes
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
and
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta
, in general, can be studied by a manner described above. But, it
is well known that due to
U(1)
anomaly there is a mixing in the system of
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mesons
Feldmann:1998vh
. This phenomenon leads
to some subtleties in the choice of the interpolating currents for these
particles. The
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
mixing can be described in the
framework of different approaches: The physical particles
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
can be expressed using either the octet-singlet or quark-flavor
bases of the flavor
SU_{f}(3)
group. It turns out that mixing of the
physical states, decay constants and higher twist distribution amplitudes in
the
\eta-\eta^{\prime}
system take simple forms in the quark-flavor basis
|\eta_{q}\rangle=(\overline{u}u+\overline{d}d)/\sqrt{2}
and
|\eta_{s}\rangle=\overline{s}s

Feldmann:1998vh
;
Agaev:2014wna
;
Agaev:2015faa
. Therefore, for our purposes
it is convenient to describe the mesons
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
in the
quark-flavor basis.

Then, the physical mesons
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are expressed using
the basic states
|\eta_{q}\rangle
and
|\eta_{s}\rangle

\begin{pmatrix}\eta\\
\eta^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}=U(\varphi)\begin{pmatrix}|\eta_{q}\rangle\\
|\eta_{s}\rangle\end{pmatrix},

(35)

where

U(\varphi)=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\varphi&-\sin\varphi\\
\sin\varphi&\cos\varphi\end{pmatrix},

(36)

is the mixing matrix in
|\eta_{q}\rangle-|\eta_{s}\rangle
basis with
\varphi
being a mixing angle. This assumption on the state mixing implies
that the same pattern applies to relevant currents, decay constants and wave
functions as well.

In this context the interpolating currents for the mesons
\eta
and
\eta^{\prime}
are given by the expressions

\displaystyle J^{\eta}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sin\varphi\overline{s}_{j}(x)i\gamma_{5}s_{j}(x),

\displaystyle J^{\eta^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\varphi\overline{s}_{j}(x)i\gamma_{5}s_{j}(x),

(37)

where
j
is the color index. Let us emphasize that in Eq. (
37
), we write down only
\overline{s}s
component of the currents, which contribute to the
decays under analysis.

We begin our calculations from the decay
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
. In this case, one should explore the correlation function

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}(p,p^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i^{2}\int d^{4}xd^{4}ye^{i(p^{\prime}y-px)}\langle 0|\mathcal{T}\{J^{\eta_{c}}(y)

(38)

\displaystyle\times J^{\eta^{\prime}}(0)J^{\dagger}(x)\}|0\rangle,

with
\ J^{\eta_{c}}(y)
being the interpolating current of the meson
\eta_{c}

\ J^{\eta_{c}}(x)=\overline{c}_{i}(x)i\gamma_{5}c_{i}(x).

(39)

The ground-state contribution to the correlation function
\widetilde{\Pi}(p,p^{\prime})
in terms of the involved particles’ matrix elements has the
form

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})=\frac{\langle 0|J^{\eta_{c}}|\eta_{c}(p^{\prime})\rangle\langle 0|J^{\eta^{\prime}}|\eta^{\prime}(q)\rangle}{(p^{2}-m^{2})(p^{\prime 2}-m_{\eta_{c}}^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{\langle\eta^{\prime}(q)\eta_{c}(p^{\prime})|X(p)\rangle\langle X(p)|J^{\dagger}|0\rangle}{(q^{2}-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2})}+\cdots,

(40)

where the dots indicate effects of higher resonances and continuum states.
The function
\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})
can be
simplified by invoking the matrix elements of the mesons
\eta_{c}
and
\eta^{\prime}

\displaystyle\langle 0|J^{\eta_{c}}|\eta_{c}\rangle=\frac{m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}\ f_{\eta_{c}}}{2m_{c}},\

\displaystyle 2m_{s}\langle\eta^{\prime}|\overline{s}i\gamma_{5}s|0\rangle=h_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s},

(41)

where
m_{\eta_{c}}
and
\ f_{\eta_{c}}
are the mass and decay constant
of the
\eta_{c}
meson. The twist-3 matrix element of the local operator
\overline{s}i\gamma_{5}s
sandwiched between the meson
\eta^{\prime}

and vacuum states is denoted by
h_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s}

Agaev:2014wna
. The parameter
h_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s}
complies with
mixing effect, and we get

h_{\eta^{\prime}}^{s}=h_{s}\cos\varphi.

(42)

The parameter
h_{s}
in Eq. (
42
) can be defined theoretically

Agaev:2014wna
, but for our analysis it is enough to use phenomenological values of
h_{s}
and
\varphi

\displaystyle h_{s}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(0.087\pm 0.006)~\mathrm{GeV}^{3},

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 39.3^{\circ}\pm 1.0^{\circ}.

(43)

The vertex
X\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
is chosen in the following form

\langle\eta^{\prime}(q)\eta_{c}(p^{\prime})|X(p)\rangle=g_{1}(q^{2})p\cdot p^{\prime},

(44)

where
g_{1}
is the strong coupling corresponding to the vertex
X\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
. Using these matrix elements, one can obtain a new
expression for
\Pi^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p,p^{\prime})=g_{1}(q^{2})\frac{fmm_{\eta_{c}}^{2}\ f_{\eta_{c}}h_{s}\cos^{2}\varphi}{4m_{c}m_{s}{}(p^{2}-m^{2})}

\displaystyle\times\frac{1}{(p^{\prime 2}-m_{\eta_{c}}^{2})(q^{2}-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2})}\frac{m^{2}+m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}-q^{2}}{2}+\cdots.

The QCD side of the sum rule for
g_{1}(q^{2})
is given by the formula

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p,p^{\prime})=-\cos\varphi\int d^{4}xd^{4}ye^{i(p^{\prime}y-px)}\epsilon\widetilde{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\times\mathrm{Tr}\left[\gamma_{5}S_{c}^{ib}(y-x)\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{s}^{jc}(-x)\gamma_{5}\widetilde{S}_{s}^{nj}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\left.\times\gamma_{5}S_{c}^{mi}(x-y)\right],

(46)

The sum rule for the coupling
g_{1}(q^{2})
is derived using Borel
transformations of invariant amplitudes
\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{Phys}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
and
\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
and reads

\displaystyle g_{1}(q^{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{8m_{c}m_{s}}{fmm_{\eta_{c}}^{2}\ f_{\eta_{c}}h_{s}\cos\varphi}\frac{q^{2}-m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}}{m^{2}+m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}-q^{2}}

(47)

\displaystyle\times e^{m^{2}/M_{1}^{2}}e^{m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}/M_{2}^{2}}\widetilde{\Pi}(\mathbf{M}^{2},\mathbf{s}_{0},q^{2}).

Here,
\widetilde{\Pi}(\mathbf{M}^{2},\mathbf{s}_{0},q^{2})
is the Borel
transformed and subtracted amplitude
\widetilde{\Pi}^{\mathrm{OPE}}(p^{2},p^{\prime 2},q^{2})
.

The coupling
g_{1}(q^{2})
is calculated using the following Borel and
continuum threshold parameters in the
\eta_{c}
channel

M_{2}^{2}\in[3,4]~\mathrm{GeV}^{2},\ s_{0}^{\prime}\in[9.5,10.5]~\mathrm{GeV}^{2},

(48)

whereas as
M_{1}^{2}
and
s_{0}
for the
X
channel, we employ Eq. (
15
). The strong coupling
g_{1}
is defined at the mass shell of
the
\eta^{\prime}
meson. The fit function
\mathcal{G}_{1}(Q^{2})
given
by Eq. (
30
) has the parameters
\mathcal{G}_{1}^{0}=0.26~\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
,
c_{1}^{1}=4.72
, and
c_{1}^{2}=-3.52
. Relevant computations
yield

g_{1}\equiv\mathcal{G}_{1}(-m_{\eta^{\prime}}^{2})=(1.9\pm 0.3)\times 10^{-1}\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1}.

(49)

The partial width of this decay can be found by means of the formula Eq. (
32
), in which one should make substitutions
G\rightarrow g_{1}
,
m_{D_{s}}^{2}\rightarrow m_{\eta_{c}}^{2}
and
\lambda\left(m,m_{D_{s}},m_{D_{s}}\right)\rightarrow\widetilde{\lambda}\left(m,m_{\eta_{c}},m_{\eta^{\prime}}\right)
. Then, for the process
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}
, we get

\Gamma\left[X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}\right]=(3.0\pm 0.7)~\mathrm{MeV}.

(50)

Analysis of the decay
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta
can be performed in a
similar way. Omitting further details, let us write down predictions
obtained for key quantities. Thus, the strong coupling
g_{2}
at the vertex

X\eta_{c}\eta
is determined by the equality

g_{2}\equiv|\mathcal{G}_{2}(-m_{\eta}^{2})|=(1.4\pm 0.2)\times 10^{-1}\ \mathrm{GeV}^{-1},

(51)

where parameters of the fit function are
\mathcal{G}_{2}^{0}=-0.15~\mathrm{GeV}^{-1}
,
c_{2}^{1}=5.76
, and
c_{2}^{2}=-4.44
. The partial width of
the decay
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta
is

\Gamma\left[X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta\right]=(5.2\pm 1.1)~\mathrm{MeV}.

(52)

With this information at hands, it is not difficult to find the full width
of the scalar tetraquark
X

\Gamma_{\mathrm{X}}=(42.2\pm 12.0)~\mathrm{MeV}.

(53)

This estimate is in excellent agreement with the LHCb data.

V
Concluding notes

In this article, we have calculated spectral parameters of the scalar
tetraquark
X
in the framework of the QCD two-point sum rule method. We
evaluated also full width of
X
, by taking into account its decay modes
X\rightarrow D_{s}^{+}D_{s}^{-}
,
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta^{\prime}

and
X\rightarrow\eta_{c}\eta
. Our result for the mass
m=(3976\pm 85)~\mathrm{MeV}
of the tetraquark
X
overshoots the corresponding LHCb datum,
but is compatible with
m_{\exp}
provided one takes into account
the corresponding theoretical and experimental errors. Our prediction for the
full width
\Gamma_{\mathrm{X}}=(42.2\pm 12.0)~\mathrm{MeV}
of
X
is in
excellent agreement with
\Gamma_{\mathrm{exp}}
from Eq. (
1
).

In Ref.
LHCb:2022vsv
the LHCb Collaboration assumed that the
resonance
X(3960)
is composed of four
c\overline{c}s\overline{s}
quarks.
This assumption is relied on theoretical predictions of Ref.
Chen:2017dpy

A sequent calculus for a semi-associative law
Noam Zeilberger

Abstract
. We introduce a sequent calculus with a simple restriction of Lambek’s product rules that precisely captures the classical Tamari order, i.e., the partial order on fully-bracketed words (equivalently, binary trees) induced by a semi-associative law (equivalently, right rotation). We establish a focusing property for this sequent calculus (a strengthening of cut-elimination), which yields the following coherence theorem: every valid entailment in the Tamari order has exactly one focused derivation. We then describe two main applications of the coherence theorem, including: 1. A new proof of the lattice property for the Tamari order, and 2. A new proof of the Tutte–Chapoton formula for the number of intervals in the Tamari lattice 𝖸 n \mathsf{Y}_{n} .

1.
A sequent calculus for the Tamari order

1.1.
Definitions and terminology

For reference, we recall here the definition of the sequent calculus introduced in Section
0.2
, and clarify some notational conventions.
The four rules of the sequent calculus are:

A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B,\Delta\longrightarrow CA,B,\Delta\longrightarrow C\qquad\Gamma,\Delta\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma\longrightarrow A\Delta\longrightarrow B

\qquad A\longrightarrow A\qquad\Gamma,\Theta,\Delta\longrightarrow B\lx@proof@logical@and\Theta\longrightarrow A\Gamma,A,\Delta\longrightarrow B

Uppercase Latin letters (
A,B,\dots
) range over
formulas
, corresponding to fully-bracketed words:
we say that every formula is either
atomic
(ranged over by lowercase Latin letters
p,q,\dots
) or else
non-atomic
if it is a product (
A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B
).
Uppercase Greek letters
\Gamma,\Delta,\dots
range over
contexts
, corresponding to lists of formulas, with concatenation of contexts (which is of course a strictly associative operation) indicated by a comma.
We will often restrict to non-empty contexts (that is, to lists of length
\geq 1
), although not always (the empty context is denoted
\Delta=\cdot
).
Let us emphasize that as in Lambek’s system
[
16
]
but in contrast to Gentzen’s original sequent calculus
[
10
]
, there are no rules of
weakening
,

, or
exchange
, so the order and the number of occurrences of a formula within a context matters.

A

is a pair of a context
\Gamma
and a formula
A
.
Abstractly, a

is a tree (technically, a
rooted planar tree with boundary
)
whose internal nodes are labelled by the names of rules and whose edges are labelled by sequents satisfying the constraints indicated by the given rule.
The
conclusion
of a derivation is the sequent labelling its outgoing root edge, while its
premises
are the sequents labelling any incoming leaf edges.
A derivation with no premises is said to be
closed
.
In addition to writing “
\Gamma\longrightarrow A
” as a notation for sequents, sometimes we also use it as a shorthand to indicate that the given sequent is
derivable
using the above rules, in other words that there exists a closed derivation whose conclusion is that sequent (it will always be clear which of these two senses we mean).
Sometimes we will need to give an explicit name to a derivation with a given conclusion, in which case we write the name over the sequent arrow.

As in the introduction,
when constructing derivations we sometimes write
L
and
R
as shorthand for
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }L
and
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }R
, and usually don’t bother labelling the instances of

since they are always clear from context.

Finally, we introduce a few more specialized notions.
We define the
frontier

\partial A
of a formula

to be the ordered list of atoms occurring in
A

\partial(A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B)=(\partial A,\partial B)

), and the frontier of a context

as the concatenation of the frontiers of its formulas (
\partial\Gamma=\partial A_{1},\dots,\partial A_{n}
).
If
\sigma
is an arbitrary function sending atoms to atoms (not necessarily injectively), we write

and

The following properties are immediate by examination of the four sequent calculus rules.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose that

(1)

(Frontier preservation:)
\partial\Gamma=\partial A
.

(2)

\sigma\Gamma\longrightarrow\sigma A

1.2.
Completeness

Let
A\leq B
denote the Tamari order on formulas, that is, the least preorder such that
(A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C\leq A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C)
for all
A
,
B
, and
C
, and such that
A_{1}\leq A_{2}
and
B_{1}\leq B_{2}
implies
A_{1}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B_{1}\leq A_{2}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B_{2}
.

We begin by establishing completeness of the sequent calculus relative to the Tamari order, which is the easier direction.

Theorem 2

(Completeness)
.

If
A\leq B
then
A\longrightarrow B
.

Proof 1.1
.

We must show that the relation
A\longrightarrow B
is reflexive and transitive, and that the multiplication operation satisfies a semi-associative law and is monotonic in each argument.
All of these properties are straightforward:

(1)

Reflexivity: immediate by
id
.

(2)

Transitivity: immediate by
cut
(with
\Gamma=\Delta=\cdot
and
\Theta
a singleton).

(3)

Semi-associativity:

(A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C)A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B,C\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C)A,B,C\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C)\lx@proof@logical@and A\longrightarrow AB,C\longrightarrow B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C\lx@proof@logical@and B\longrightarrow BC\longrightarrow C

(4)

Monotonicity:

A_{1}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B_{1}\longrightarrow A_{2}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B_{2}A_{1},B_{1}\longrightarrow A_{2}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B_{2}\lx@proof@logical@and A_{1}\longrightarrow A_{2}B_{1}\longrightarrow B_{2}

1.3.
Soundness

To prove soundness relative to the Tamari order, first we have to explain the interpretation of general sequents.
The basic idea is that we can interpret a
non-empty context
as a
left-associated product
.

is interpreted as an entailment of the form

(\cdots(A_{0}\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A_{1})\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\cdots)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A_{n}\leq B

Let
\phi[-]
be the operation taking any non-empty context
\Gamma
to the formula
\phi[\Gamma]
given by its left-associated product.
Recursively,
\phi[-]
is defined by the following equations:

\phi[A]=A\qquad\phi[\Gamma,A]=\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A

Critical to soundness of the sequent calculus will be the following property of
\phi[-]
(expressing that it is an “oplax” homomorphism, in the terminology of monoidal functors):

Proposition 3

(Oplaxity)
.

\phi[\Gamma,\Delta]\leq\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\phi[\Delta]
for all non-empty contexts
\Gamma
and
\Delta
.

Proof 1.2
.

By induction on
\Delta
.
The case of a singleton context
\Delta=A
is immediate.
Otherwise, if
\Delta=(\Delta^{\prime},A)
, we reason as follows:

\displaystyle\phi[\Gamma,\Delta^{\prime},A]

\displaystyle=\phi[\Gamma,\Delta^{\prime}]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A

(by definition)

\displaystyle\leq(\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\phi[\Delta^{\prime}])\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A

(by the i.h. and monotonicity)

\displaystyle\leq\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(\phi[\Delta^{\prime}]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }A)

(semi-associativity)

\displaystyle=\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\phi[\Delta^{\prime},A]

(by definition)

The operation
\phi[-]
can also be equivalently described in terms of a
right action

{A}\circledast\Delta
of an arbitrary context on a formula, defined by the following equations:

{A}\circledast\cdot=A\qquad{A}\circledast(\Delta,B)=({A}\circledast\Delta)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B

(Right action)
.

\phi[\Gamma,\Delta]={\phi[\Gamma]}\circledast\Delta
for all non-empty
\Gamma
and arbitrary
\Delta
.

Proposition 5

(Monotonicity)
.

If
A\leq A^{\prime}
then
{A}\circledast\Delta\leq{A^{\prime}}\circledast\Delta
.

Proof 1.3
.

Both properties are immediate by induction on
\Delta
.
(In the case of Prop.

-\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B
.)

We are now ready to prove soundness.

Theorem 6

(Soundness)
.

If
\Gamma\longrightarrow A
then
\phi[\Gamma]\leq A
.

Proof 1.4
.

By induction on the (closed) derivation of
\Gamma\longrightarrow A
.
There are four cases, corresponding to the four rules of the sequent calculus:

•

(Case
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }L
): The derivation ends in

A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B,\Delta\longrightarrow CA,B,\Delta\longrightarrow C

Since
\phi[A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B]=A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B=\phi[A,B]
, we have that
\phi[A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B,\Delta]={\phi[A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B]}\circledast\Delta={\phi[A,B]}\circledast\Delta=\phi[A,B,\Delta]\leq C
by the induction hypothesis.

•

(Case
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }R

\Gamma,\Delta\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma\longrightarrow A\Delta\longrightarrow B

\phi[\Gamma]\leq A
and
\phi[\Delta]\leq B
.
But then
\phi[\Gamma,\Delta]\leq\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\phi[\Delta]\leq A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B

(Case

•

(Case

\displaystyle\phi[\Gamma,\Theta,\Delta]

\displaystyle={\phi[\Gamma,\Theta]}\circledast\Delta

\displaystyle\leq{(\phi[\Gamma]\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }\phi[\Theta])}\circledast\Delta

\displaystyle\leq B

(i.h.)

1.4.
Focusing completeness

Cut-elimination theorems are a staple of proof theory, and often provide a rich source of information about a given logic.
In this section we will prove a
focusing
property for the sequent calculus, which is an even stronger form of cut-elimination originally formulated by Andreoli in the setting of linear logic
[
1
]
.

{defi}

A context
\Gamma
is said to be

Definition 7
.

reducible if its leftmost formula is non-atomic, and

Definition 8
.

irreducible otherwise.
A sequent
\Gamma\longrightarrow A
is said to be:

•

Definition 9
.

left-inverting if
\Gamma
is reducible;

•

Definition 10
.

right-focusing if
\Gamma
is irreducible and
A
is non-atomic;

•

Definition 11
.

atomic if
\Gamma
is irreducible and
A
is atomic.

Proposition 12
.

Any sequent is either left-inverting, right-focusing, or atomic.

{defi}

A closed derivation
\mathcal{D}
is said to be

Definition 13
.

focused if left-inverting sequents only appear as the conclusions of
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }L
, right-focusing sequents only as the conclusions of
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }R
, and atomic sequents only as the conclusions of
id
.

We write “
\Gamma^{\mathrm{irr}}
” to indicate that a context
\Gamma
is irreducible.

Proposition 14
.

A closed derivation is focused if and only if it is constructed using only
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }L
and the following restricted forms of
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }R
and
id
(and no instances of
cut
):

\Gamma^{\mathrm{irr}},\Delta\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma^{\mathrm{irr}}\longrightarrow A\Delta\longrightarrow B\qquad p\longrightarrow p

{exa}

One way to derive

((p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }q)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\longrightarrow p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }((q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s)

is by cutting together the two derivations

((p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }q)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\longrightarrow(p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r))\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s(p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }q)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r,s\longrightarrow(p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r))\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\lx@proof@logical@and(p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }q)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r\longrightarrow p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r)\mathcal{SA}_{p,q,r}s\longrightarrow s

and

(p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r))\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s\longrightarrow p\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }((q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }s)\mathcal{SA}_{p,q\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }r,s}

where
\mathcal{SA}_{A,B,C}
is the derivation of the semi-associative law
(A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B)\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }(B\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }C)
from the proof of Theorem
2
.
Clearly this is
not
a focused derivation (besides the cut rule, it also uses instances of
\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle*{3.0}\end{picture}\ }R
and

1.6.
Notes and related work

The coherence theorem says in a sense that focused derivations provide a canonical representation for intervals of the Tamari order.
Although the representations are quite different, in this respect it is very roughly comparable to the “unicity of normal chains” that was established by Tamari and Friedman as part of their original proof of the lattice property of
\mathsf{Y}_{n}

[
9
]
.
A natural question is whether the sequent calculus can be used to better understand and further simplify the proof of the lattice property: we turn to this question in Section
2
below.

An easy observation is that one obtains the dual Tamari order (cf. Footnote
1

\Gamma,A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B\longrightarrow C\Gamma,A,B\longrightarrow C\qquad\Gamma,\Delta\longrightarrow A\mathbin{\,\begin{picture}(-1.0,-3.0)\circle{3.0}\end{picture}\ }B\lx@proof@logical@and\Gamma\longrightarrow A\Delta\longrightarrow B

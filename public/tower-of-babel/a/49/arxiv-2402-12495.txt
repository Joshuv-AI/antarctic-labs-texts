On virtual resolutions of points in a product of projective spaces
Isidora Bailly-Hall, Christine Berkesch, Karina Dovgodko, Sean Guan, Saisudharshan Sivakumar, Jishi Sun

Abstract
. For finite sets of points in ℙ n × ℙ m \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m} , we produce short virtual resolutions, as introduced by Berkesch, Erman, and Smith [ 3 ] . We first intersect with a sufficiently high power of one set of variables for points in ℙ n × ℙ m \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m} to produce a virtual resolution of length n + m n+m . Then, we describe an explicit virtual resolution of length 3 3 for a set of points in sufficiently general position in ℙ 1 × ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2} , via a subcomplex of a free resolution. This first result generalizes to ℙ n × ℙ m \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m} work of Harada, Nowroozi, and Van Tuyl [ 17 ] and the second partially generalizes work of [ 17 ] and Booms-Peot [ 6 ] , which were both for ℙ 1 × ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1} . Along the way, we also note an explicit relationship between Betti numbers and higher difference matrices of bigraded Hilbert functions for ℙ n × ℙ m \mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m} .

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
, as we will consider finite sets of points
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}

Let
S=\Bbbk[x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]
denote the
Cox ring
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}

\Bbbk

\deg(x_{i})=(1,0)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

\deg(y_{j})=(0,1)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

Let
\mathbf{x}=x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
and
\mathbf{y}=y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}

The

of

B:=\langle x_{0},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\rangle\cap\langle y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle\cap\langle\mathbf{y}\rangle.

Let

\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
, and let

denote the homogeneous vanishing ideal of

A graded free complex of

of a finitely generated

(1)

For each

B^{t}H_{i}(F_{\bullet})=0
, and

(2)

H_{0}(F_{\bullet})/\Gamma_{B}(H_{0}(F_{\bullet}))\cong M/\Gamma_{B}(M)
,

where
\Gamma_{B}(M)\coloneqq\{m\in M\mid B^{t}m=0\text{ for some }t\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Equivalently,
F_{\bullet}
is a virtual resolution of

if
\widetilde{F_{\bullet}}
is a locally free resolution of
\widetilde{M}
over
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
.

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}

Theorem 1.1

For all

A second method for finding virtual resolutions, called

Definition 1.2
.

For a finite set of points
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
,
the
Hilbert matrix

H_{X}
has entries

(H_{X})_{i,j}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j)\quad\text{for all }(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}

If

H_{X}(i,j)=\min\{|X|,T_{i,n}T_{j,m}\}\quad\text{for all}\quad i,j\geq 0,

with

T_{a,b}:=\binom{a+b}{b},

then we say that

.

4.1

\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
.
We will further say that

sufficiently general position

Theorem 1.3

(See

It is well-known that Hilbert functions can be computed as alternating sums of graded Betti numbers.
For

Definition 1.4
.

Define a partial order on
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by letting
(i,j)\geq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
if
i\geq i^{\prime}
and
j\geq j^{\prime}
. For any infinite matrix
H=(H_{i,j})
indexed by
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,
the
column difference operator

\Delta^{C}
and
row difference operator

\Delta^{R}
are

\displaystyle\Delta^{C}(H)_{i,j}:=H_{i,j}-H_{i-1,j}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Delta^{R}(H)_{i,j}:=H_{i,j}-H_{i,j-1}.

Note that the operators
\Delta^{C}
and
\Delta^{R}
commute.

Proposition 1.5
.

If
M
is a finitely generated bigraded
S
-module with minimal free resolution

F_{\bullet}:\quad\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0}S(-i,-j)^{\beta_{0,(i,j)}}\leftarrow\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0}S(-i,-j)^{\beta_{1,(i,j)}}\leftarrow\cdots\leftarrow\bigoplus_{i,j\geq 0}S(-i,-j)^{\beta_{k,(i,j)}}\leftarrow\cdots,

then the Hilbert matrix
H_{M}
satisfies

\big((\Delta^{C})^{n+1}(\Delta^{R})^{m+1}H_{M}\big)_{i,j}=B_{i,j},

where

B_{p,q}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\beta_{k,(p,q)}

for all
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
.

2.
A short virtual resolution via intersection in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}

By the end of this section, we prove
1.1
.
We begin with several preliminary results. First, we note that after a change of coordinates, we may assume that
y_{0}
is a nonzero divisor on
S/I_{X}
; see
2.1
.

For a point

\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}=[1:a_{1}:a_{2}:\cdots:a_{n}]\times[1:b_{1}:b_{2}:\cdots:b_{m}]\in\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m},

and for
i,j>0
, let
L_{a_{i}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=a_{i}x_{0}-x_{i}
and
L_{b_{j}}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=b_{j}y_{0}-y_{j}
. Let

I_{\mathbf{a}}=\langle L_{a_{1}},L_{a_{2}},\ldots,L_{a_{n}}\rangle\text{ and }I_{\mathbf{b}}=\langle L_{b_{1}},L_{b_{2}},\ldots,L_{b_{m}}\rangle,

so that
I_{\mathbf{a}}+I_{\mathbf{b}}
is the bihomogeneous ideal for the point
\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}
.

Assumption 2.1
.

Throughout this paper, we will consider a finite set of points
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
.
After a linear change of coordinates, we may and will assume that every point in
X
is of the form
[1:a_{1}:\cdots:a_{n}]\times[1:b_{1}:\cdots:b_{m}]
.

Notation 2.2
.

Let
\pi_{1}(X)=\{P_{1},P_{2},\ldots,P_{\ell}\}
denote the set of distinct first coordinates in
X
, so
\ell=|\pi_{1}(X)|
.
Let
X_{k}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\pi_{1}^{-1}(P_{k})\cap X
denote the pre-image in
X
of
P_{k}
, and write

X_{k}=\{P_{k}\times Q_{k,1},P_{k}\times Q_{k,2},\ldots,P_{k}\times Q_{k,r_{k}}\}\subseteq X,

where
r_{k}=|\pi_{1}^{-1}(P_{k})|
.
Thus, for each
k
with
1\leq k\leq\ell
,

(2.1)

\displaystyle I_{X_{k}}=I_{P_{k}}+\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}}.

Note that the ideal
I_{X}
is necessarily
B
-saturated.
For each
j\in\{1,2,\dots,\ell\}
,
let

J_{j}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{j}I_{X_{k}}.

Note that
J_{j}=I_{Y_{j}}
, where
Y_{j}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{j}X_{k}
for
1\leq j\leq\ell
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a set of at least two points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
with
\ell=|\pi_{1}(X)|
and
J_{\ell-1}
as in
2.2
.
If
t\geq\ell-1
, then

\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}\subseteq I_{X_{\ell}}+J_{\ell-1}
.

Proof.

We prove this statement by induction on
\ell=|\pi_{1}(X)|
.
The base case
\ell=1
is trivial.
If
\ell=2
, then

I_{X_{1}}=\langle L_{a_{1}},\ldots,L_{a_{n}},G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k}\rangle
,
where
L_{a_{i}}
is a linear form in
x_{0}
and
x_{i}
, and each
G_{i}
is a polynomial in the
\mathbf{y}
-variables;
the ideal
I_{X_{2}}=\langle L_{a_{1}}^{\prime},\ldots,L_{a_{n}}^{\prime},G_{1}^{\prime},G_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,G_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\rangle
is of the same form.
Then together,

I_{X_{1}}+I_{X_{2}}=\langle x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n},G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{k},G_{1}^{\prime},G_{2}^{\prime},\ldots,G_{k^{\prime}}^{\prime}\rangle,

which contains
\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}
for all
t\geq 1
, as desired.

Now for any
\ell>2
, write
X=Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2}\sqcup X_{\ell}
,
where

Y_{1}=\bigsqcup_{k=1}^{\ell-2}{X_{k}}

and
Y_{2}={X_{\ell-1}}
,
so
|\pi_{1}(Y_{1})|=\ell-2
and
|\pi_{1}(Y_{2})|=1
.
Then by the inductive hypothesis,

\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{\ell-2}\subseteq I_{X_{\ell}}+I_{Y_{1}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle\subseteq I_{X_{\ell}}+I_{Y_{2}}.

Putting these together yields the desired result:

\displaystyle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{\ell-2}\cdot\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle\subseteq(I_{X_{\ell}}+I_{Y_{1}})\cdot(I_{X_{\ell}}+I_{Y_{2}})\subseteq I_{X_{\ell}}+\left(I_{Y_{1}}\cap I_{Y_{2}}\right)=I_{X_{\ell}}+\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell-1}I_{X_{k}}=I_{X_{\ell}}+J_{\ell-1}.\qquad\quad\,\qed

We now begin working towards
2.7
, which provides a primary decomposition of
\left\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle
, the final ingredient needed to prove
1.1
.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
I
is an ideal in
S
such that
S/I
has a nonzero divisor
L
of degree
(0,1)
, then for all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

H_{S/(I+\langle L\rangle)}(i,j)=H_{S/I}(i,j)-H_{S/I}(i,j-1).

Proof.

There is a graded short exact sequence

0\to\frac{S}{I:\langle L\rangle}(0,-1)\xrightarrow{\ \ \cdot\overline{L}\ \ }\frac{S}{I}\longrightarrow\frac{S}{I+\langle L\rangle}\to 0,

and, since
L
is a nonzero divisor on
S/I
,

I:\langle L\rangle=I
, so the desired equality of Hilbert functions follows.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
X
be a finite set of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
as in
2.1
. If
i\geq|\pi_{1}(X)|-1
, then there is an equality of bigraded pieces for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}
:

(2.2)

\left[\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}=\left[\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}.

Proof.

Because the vector space on the right-hand side of (
2.2
) is contained in the left-hand side, it suffices to show that these vector spaces have the same dimension, which we use induction on
\ell\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|\pi_{1}(X)|
.
The base case
\ell=1
is a tautology for all
i
.

By way of induction, suppose that
\ell>1
and (
2.2
) holds for all sets of points with strictly fewer distinct first coordinates than
X
.
To examine the right-hand side of (
2.2
), note that
2.1
and
2.4
together imply that

(2.3)

H_{S/\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j)=H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j)-H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j-1).

Since
I_{X}=\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell}I_{X_{k}}
, there is a graded short exact sequence

0\to S/I_{X}\to S/J_{\ell-1}\oplus S/I_{X_{\ell}}\to S/(J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}})\to 0,

where
J_{\ell-1}
is as in
2.2
.
By
2.3
, the ideal
J_{\ell-1}+\langle I_{X_{\ell}}\rangle
contains
\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{\ell-1}
.
Hence

H_{S/(J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}})}(i,j)=0
for all
i\geq\ell-1
,
so by the additivity of Hilbert functions on graded short exact sequences, for all
j
,

(2.4)

\displaystyle H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j)

\displaystyle=H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j)+H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j)

(2.5)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j-1)

\displaystyle=H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j-1)+H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j-1).

Combining (
2.3
), (
2.4
), and (
2.5
) yields the following expression for the Hilbert function of the right-hand side of (
2.2
):

(2.6)

H_{S/\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j)=H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j)+H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j)-H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j-1)-H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j-1).

Now to examine the left-hand side of (
2.2
),
it follows from the inductive hypothesis that

\left[\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell-1}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}=\left[\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell-1}I_{X_{k}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}.

Since there is a graded short exact sequence

0\to\frac{S}{\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle}\to\frac{S}{J_{\ell-1}+\langle y_{0}\rangle}\oplus\frac{S}{I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle}\to\frac{S}{J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle}\to 0,

the following equality of Hilbert functions holds for all
j
:

H_{S/\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j)=H_{S/(J_{\ell-1}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j)+H_{S/(I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j)-H_{S/(J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j).

Next, by
2.3
, since
i\geq|\pi_{1}(X)|-1
, there is a containment

\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{i}\subseteq J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}}
, so

S_{i,j}=\left[\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell-1}I_{X_{k}}+I_{X_{\ell}}\right]_{(i,j)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad H_{S/(J_{\ell-1}+I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j)=0.

Since
y_{0}
is a nonzero divisor on
S/J_{\ell-1}
and
S/I_{X_{\ell}}
, by
2.4
, for all
j
,

\displaystyle H_{S/(J_{\ell-1}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j)

\displaystyle=H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j)-H_{S/J_{\ell-1}}(i,j-1)

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad H_{S/(I_{X_{\ell}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle)}(i,j)

\displaystyle=H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j)-H_{S/I_{X_{\ell}}}(i,j-1).

Comparing with (
2.6
), it follows that for
i\geq\ell-1
,

H_{S/\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j)=H_{S/\bigcap_{k=1}^{\ell}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j),

so (
2.2
) is an equality, as desired.
∎

When a set of points
X
has a generic Hilbert function, equality can be obtained sooner.

Corollary 2.6
.

If
X
is a finite set of points with generic Hilbert function in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
as in
2.1
and
d=\min\{r\mid T_{r,n}\geq|X|\}
, then (
2.2
) holds for all
i\geq d
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

First consider the case that
j=0
. Here,

\left[\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,0)}=[I_{X}]_{(i,0)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left[\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,0)}=[I_{X}]_{(i,0)},

so (
2.2
) holds.
When
j\geq 1
, just as in
2.5
, because the vector space on the right-hand side (RHS) of (
2.2
) is contained in the left-hand side (LHS), it suffices to show that for all
i\geq d
, the dimension of the RHS is is greater than or equal to the dimension of the LHS. This is equivalent to showing that

\dim_{\Bbbk}\left[\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}\leq\dim_{\Bbbk}\left[\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}.

In turn, this is equivalent to showing

H_{RHS}(i,j)\leq H_{LHS}(i,j)
.
By
2.1
and
2.4
, it follows that

H_{RHS}(i,j)=H_{S/\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle}(i,j)=H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j)-H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j-1).

Since the points are in sufficiently general position and
i\geq d
,

H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j)=H_{S/I_{X}}(i,j-1)=|X|
, so
H_{RHS}(i,j)=0
.
∎

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a finite set of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
as in
2.1
and

.
Then for any integer

, there is a primary decomposition of the form

\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle=\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\cap\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle.

Remark 2.8
.

Note that when
m=1
, then the statement of
2.7
can be made more explicit, since for each point
\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}\in X
,
I_{\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}}=\langle L_{a_{1}},\ldots L_{a_{n}},L_{b_{1}}\rangle
.
Then by (

), for any

,

I_{X_{k}}=\langle L_{P_{k,1}},L_{P_{k,2}},\ldots,L_{P_{k,n}},L_{Q_{k,1}}L_{Q_{k,2}}\cdots L_{Q_{k,r_{k}}}\rangle.

Since each
L_{Q_{k,j}}
is a linear form in

or

, their product will be a polynomial of degree

in

and

. By

is of the form

, with

. Therefore, the product of the

\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle=\langle L_{P_{k,1}},L_{P_{k,2}},\ldots,L_{P_{k,n}},y_{0},y_{1}^{r_{k}}\rangle.

Proof of
2.7
.

For any
S
-ideals
I_{1},I_{2},I_{3}
,
(I_{1}\cap I_{2})+I_{3}\subseteq(I_{1}+I_{3})\cap(I_{2}+I_{3})
; in particular, for all
t\geq 0
,

(2.7)

\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle\subseteq\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\cap\left\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle.

We will now show that when
t\geq|\pi_{1}(X)|-1
, the left-hand side of (
2.7
) contains the right-hand side, and it suffices to do so on each bigraded piece, i.e., show that for all
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and
t\geq|\pi_{1}(X)|-1
,

(2.8)

\left[\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\cap\left\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}\subseteq\left[\left\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}.

We consider two cases, based on a comparison between
i
and
t
.
First,
0\leq i<t

and suppose that
f
is in the left-hand side of (
2.8
); in particular,

f\in\left[\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}
.
Since
i<t
by assumption,
f
is of low degree, so it must be that
f\in\langle y_{0}\rangle
.
This clearly also places
f
in the right-hand side of (
2.8
), so the equality holds in this case.

Second, if
i\geq t
, then
[\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}]_{i,j}=S_{(i,j)}
, so

\left[\left\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}=S_{(i,j)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\left[\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}=\left[\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}.

Thus it suffices to show that for
i\geq t
,

\left[\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)}\subseteq\left[\langle I_{X},y_{0}\rangle\right]_{(i,j)},

which was shown in
2.5
, finishing this case.
Having established both cases, we have now shown that (
2.7
) is an equality for
t\geq|\pi_{1}(x)|-1
.

It now remains to show that the ideals on the right-hand side of (
2.7
) are primary.
By
[
24
]
*Proposition 6.1.7,
\left\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\right\rangle
is primary.
To see that
\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle
is primary for each
1\leq k\leq|\pi_{1}(X)|
, recall from
2.2
that for each
k
,

X_{k}=\{P_{k}\times Q_{k,1},\ldots,P_{k}\times Q_{k,r_{k}}\}

and

\displaystyle\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\ =\ I_{P_{k}}+\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle\ =\ \left\langle L_{P_{k,1}},L_{P_{k,2}},\ldots,L_{P_{k,n}},G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{s},y_{0}\right\rangle,

where
L_{P_{k,i}}=P_{k,i}x_{0}-x_{i}

(with
P_{k,i}
allowed to be
0
)
are the linear forms generating each
I_{P_{k}}

and
G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s}
are forms in the
\mathbf{y}
such that

\langle G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s}\rangle=\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}}
.
Putting this together,

S/\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle=S/\left\langle L_{P_{k,1}},L_{P_{k,2}},\ldots,L_{P_{k,n}},G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s},y_{0}\right\rangle\\
\cong\Bbbk[x_{0},y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}]/\left\langle G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s},y_{0}\right\rangle,

so it suffices to show that the ideal

J\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\langle G_{1},G_{2},\dots,G_{s},y_{0}\rangle
is primary in
\Bbbk[x_{0},y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
.
To this end, let
J^{\prime}
denote the ideal generated by the same elements, but viewed as an ideal of
\Bbbk[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
.
Since
J=J^{\prime}\Bbbk[x_{0},y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
, it suffices to show that
J^{\prime}
is primary in
\Bbbk[y_{0},\dots,y_{m}]
.
Now by
2.1
,

Q_{k,j}=[1:Q_{k,{j_{1}}}:\ldots:Q_{k,{j_{m}}}]
,
so

I_{Q_{k,j}}=\langle Q_{k,{j_{1}}}y_{0}-y_{1},\ldots,Q_{k,{j_{m}}}y_{0}-y_{m}\rangle.

Combining this with

\left\langle\prod_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}},y_{0}\right\rangle\subseteq\left\langle\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}},y_{0}\right\rangle=J^{\prime},

it follows that

\langle y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle^{r_{k}}\subseteq J^{\prime}\subseteq\langle y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}\rangle
,
so the radical of
J^{\prime}
is
\langle\mathbf{y}\rangle
, which is maximal in
\Bbbk[y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}]
.
Thus
J^{\prime}
is primary in
\Bbbk[y_{0},\ldots,y_{m}]
, as desired.
∎

We are now prepared to prove the main result of this section.

Proof of
1.1
.

Since the ideal
I_{X}
is
B
-saturated,
I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}:B^{\infty}=I_{X}
.
Thus a minimal free resolution of

is indeed a virtual resolution of
S/I_{X}
.

Next, note that by
[
3
]
*Proposition 2.5.a, the minimal free resolution of

has length at least

, the codimension of
I_{X}
.
It is thus left to show that the minimal free resolution of
S/(I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t})
is of length at most (and thus equal to)

.
To begin, by the Auslander–Buchsbaum formula,

\pdim(S/(I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t})=n+m+2-\depth(S/(I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}),

thus it suffices to show that the depth of
S/(I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t})
is at least
2
.
To do this, we produce a regular sequence on
S/(I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t})
of length two.

Without loss of generality by

,
y_{0}
is a nonzero divisor on
S/I_{X}
.
Further, we claim that
y_{0}
is a nonzero divisor on

Indeed, if

with

then

and
y_{0}f\in\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t}
.
This will only happen if

We are now left to produce a nonzero divisor on

.

To find such a nonzero divisor, note that since

,

provides the primary decomposition

\langle I_{X}\cap\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle=\bigcap_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle\cap\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle.

Now from (

),

\displaystyle\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=I_{P_{k}}+\bigcap_{j=1}^{r_{k}}I_{Q_{k,j}}+\langle y_{0}\rangle,

so the zero divisors of

are the elements belonging to the associated primes of

, namely

(2.9)

\displaystyle\bigcup_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\sqrt{\langle I_{X_{k}},y_{0}\rangle}\cup\sqrt{\langle\langle\mathbf{x}\rangle^{t},y_{0}\rangle}=\bigcup_{P_{k}\in\pi_{1}(X)}\langle I_{P_{k}},G_{1},G_{2},\ldots,G_{s},y_{0}\rangle\cup\langle\mathbf{x},y_{0}\rangle,

where each

is a polynomial in only the

-variables.
Thus, let

be any element of degree

in

In this way,

does not belong to the set in (

Therefore,

∎

3.
Difference Hilbert matrices and Betti numbers

In this section, we prove
1.5
, which relates a certain difference matrix of a Hilbert matrix to the Betti numbers of the corresponding free resolution.

Recall that
S
is the Cox ring of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
and
M
a finitely generated bigraded
S
-module with minimal bigraded free resolution
F_{\bullet}
with Betti numbers
\beta_{k,(i,j)}
and for all
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

B_{p,q}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\beta_{k,(p,q)}
.
Recall
\Delta^{C}
and
\Delta^{R}
from
1.4
, and let

T_{i,n}:=\binom{i+n}{n}

denote the number of monomials of degree
i
in
\Bbbk[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
,
so
T_{i,n}T_{j,m}=H_{S}(i,j)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

There is an equality

H_{M}(i,j)=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}
.

Proof.

For all
k\geq 0
,
H_{S}(i,j)=T_{i,n}T_{j,m}
and

F_{k}=\bigoplus_{p,q\geq 0}S(-p,-q)^{\beta_{k,(p,q)}}
, so

\displaystyle H_{F_{k}}(i,j)=\dim_{\Bbbk}(F_{k})_{i,j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}\dim_{\Bbbk}(S(-p,-q)_{i,j})^{\beta_{k,(p,q)}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}\dim_{\Bbbk}(S_{i-p,j-q})^{\beta_{k,(p,q)}}

(3.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n}T_{j-q,m}\beta_{k,(p,q)}.

By definition of
B_{p,q}
, for any
(p,q)\leq(i,j)
and
k
sufficiently large,
\beta_{k,(p,q)}=0
and thus
H_{F_{k}}(i,j)=0
.
Since
0\leftarrow M\leftarrow F_{\bullet}
is an exact sequence in the category of graded
S
-modules,

H_{M}(i,j)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}H_{F_{k}}(i,j).

Substituting (
3
) into the right hand side now yields the desired result.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Two identities hold for
\Delta^{C}
and
\Delta^{R}
applied to the Hilbert matrix of
M
:

\displaystyle(\Delta^{C}H_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n-1}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad(\Delta^{R}H_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n}T_{j-q,m-1}B_{p,q}.

Proof.

We prove the identity for
\Delta^{C}
; the proof for
\Delta^{R}
is analogous.
By
3.1
,

\displaystyle H_{M}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.

Now separate the sum into two parts,
noting that when
p=i
,
T_{i-p,n}=T_{0,n}=1
, so that

\displaystyle H_{M}(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\leq j}T_{j-q,m}B_{i,q}+\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i-1,j)}T_{i-p,n}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.

By
3.1
applied to
(i-1,j)
,

H_{M}(i-1,j)=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i-1,j)}T_{i-1-p,n}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.

Now by definition of
\Delta^{C}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\Delta^{C}H_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=H_{M}(i,j)-H_{M}(i-1,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\leq j}T_{j-q,m}B_{i,q}+\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i-1,j)}(T_{i-p,n}-T_{i-1-p,n})T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.

Using the binomial identities

T_{i-p,n}-T_{i-1-p,n}=T_{i-p,n-1}
,
(
3.2
) simplifies to

\displaystyle(\Delta^{C}H_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\leq j}T_{j-q,m}B_{i,q}+\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i-1,j)}T_{i-p,n-1}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.

Recombining the last two sums yields

(\Delta^{C}H_{M})(i,j)=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,n-1}T_{j-q,m}B_{p,q}.\qed

1.5
now follows by applying each of
\Delta^{C}
and
\Delta^{R}
repeatedly.

Proof of
1.5
.

Combining Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
,

\displaystyle G_{M}(i,j)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\big((\Delta^{C})^{n}(\Delta^{R})^{m}H_{M}\big)(i,j)

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}T_{i-p,0}T_{j-q,0}B_{p,q}

\displaystyle\;=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}B_{p,q}.

Now applying
\Delta^{C}
,

\displaystyle(\Delta^{C}G_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=G_{M}(i,j)-G_{M}(i-1,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i,j)}B_{p,q}-\sum_{(p,q)\leq(i-1,j)}B_{p,q}\,\,=\,\,\sum_{q\leq j}B_{i,q},

so applying
\Delta^{R}
yields the desired result:

\displaystyle(\Delta^{R}\Delta^{C}G_{M})(i,j)

\displaystyle=\sum_{q\leq j}B_{i,q}-\sum_{q\leq j-1}B_{i,q}\,=\,B_{i,j}.\qed

4.
Sufficiently general points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

We now discuss the notion of sufficiently general points, as per the setting of
1.3
.
We begin with the notion of a generic Hilbert function for
S/I_{X}
, sketched for us by Gregory G. Smith.
While this result holds over any smooth toric variety, for continuity of notation, we state the result only for
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

For every
N\geq 1
and all
n,m\geq 1
, there exists a dense open subset
U\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m})^{N}
, such that for any
X=\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{N}\}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
, if
(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{N})\in U
, then

H_{X}(i,j)=\min\{|X|,T_{i,n}T_{j,m}\}\quad\text{for all}\quad i,j\geq 0,

where
T_{i,n}\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\binom{i+n}{n}

is equal to the number of monomials of degree
i
in
\Bbbk[x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.

Proof.

A natural parameter space for
N
distinct points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
is simply the open subset of
(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m})^{N}
, the
N
-fold product of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
, obtained by removing the large diagonals, i.e., subsets where two points are equal.
Note that the Hilbert function of the Cox ring
S
of
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
at
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
is
H_{S}(i,j)=T_{i,n}T_{j,m}
.

If the Hilbert function of a set
Y
of
N
distinct points disagrees with
H_{S}(i,j)
for some
(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
, then it must be strictly smaller.
It follows that matrix with
N
rows and
H_{S}(i,j)
columns, corresponding to evaluating the
N
points in
Y
at the monomial basis for the Cox ring
S
in degree
(i,j)
has less than maximal rank.
Thus the corresponding maximal minors of the matrix, interpreted as polynomial equations in the Cox ring of the parameter space
(\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m})^{N}
, determine a closed subset that contains
Y
, as desired.
∎

Example 4.2
.

If
X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
is a finite set of sufficiently general points with
|X|=N\geq 12
,

N=6q+r=3q^{\prime}+r^{\prime}\quad\text{with}\quad 0\leq r\leq 5\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq r^{\prime}\leq 2,

\small H_{X}=\bordermatrix{~&0&1&2&\cdots&j&\cdots&d&d+1&\cdots\cr 0&1&3&6&\cdots&T_{j,2}&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr 1&2&6&12&\cdots&2T_{j,2}&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\cr c_{j}-1&c_{j}&3c_{j}&6c_{j}&\cdots&c_{j}T_{j,2}&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr c_{j}&c_{j}+1&3(c_{j}+1)&6(c_{j}+1)&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\cr q-1&q&3q&6q&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr q&q+1&3q+3&N&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\cr q^{\prime}-1&q^{\prime}&3q^{\prime}&N&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr q^{\prime}&q^{\prime}+1&N&N&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\cr N-1&N&N&N&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr N&N&N&N&\cdots&N&\cdots&N&N&\cdots\cr\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots&&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\cr}.\normalsize\qed

Moving towards
1.3
,
we now turn our attention to the specific setting of
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
, for which we need an additional assumption on set of points that restricts their first Betti numbers.

Notation 4.3
.

Given a matrix
H
with indices
(i,j)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, set

(4.1)

DH\mathrel{\vbox{\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\Delta^{C})^{2}(\Delta^{R})^{3}H.

For a finite set of points
X
in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
,

1.5
implies that the Betti numbers of
S/I_{X}
satisfy

(4.2)

DH_{X}(i,j)=-\beta_{1,(i,j)}+\beta_{2,(i,j)}-\beta_{3,(i,j)}+\beta_{4,(i,j)}.

for all
(i,j)>(0,0)
.
Ideally, we would like to say that under certain assumptions (for example, when
X
has generic Hilbert matrix), the matrix
DH_{X}
completely determines all Betti numbers of
S/I_{X}
. This amounts to saying that a certain version of the Minimal Resolution Conjecture (MRC) holds for sets of points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Originally formulated by Lorenzini in
[
20
]
for points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
, the MRC states that for a set of points in sufficiently general position, Betti numbers cannot overlap, meaning that
\beta_{i,j}\beta_{i+1,j}=0
for all
0\leq i\leq n
and
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Later, Mustaţǎ  generalized the MRC to sets of points in arbitrary projective varieties
[
22
]
.
A more detailed discussion of the history and current status of the MRC can be found in the introduction of
[
6
]
.

In
[
14
]
, it is shown that the MRC holds for all sufficiently general sets of points lying on a smooth quadric in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.
The techniques of
[
14
]
are highly specific to the geometry of
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and therefore difficult to generalize to higher dimensions or multiprojective spaces.
The recent studies
[
17
,
6
]
of virtual resolutions for points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
show a weakened partial version of the MRC, which provides enough control to obtain their results.

For the purpose of our study, we now state a weakened version of the MRC for points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
that parallels
[
14
]
.
Here we only require that the Betti numbers
\beta_{1,(i,j)}
are entirely predicted by
DH_{X}
.
This assumption provides enough control over Betti numbers to prove
1.3
.

Conjecture 4.4

(Weakened Minimal Resolution Conjecture)
.

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer.
There exists an dense open subset
U\subseteq(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2})^{N}
such that for every
(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{N})\in U
, the set of points
X=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{N}\}
satisfies:

(1)

X
has a generic Hilbert matrix as in
1.2
, and

(2)

For every fixed
(i,j)>(0,0)
,
the bigraded Betti numbers of
S/I_{X}
are such that
\beta_{1,(i,j)}>0
if and only if

\displaystyle DH_{X}(i,j)<0\textrm{ and }DH_{X}(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq 0\textrm{ for all }(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\leq(i,j)\textrm{ with }(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})\neq(0,0),

with

as in
4.3
, and whenever the above condition holds,
\beta_{1,(i,j)}=-DH_{X}(i,j)
.

Example 4.5
.

For each

, we verified in Macaulay2 that

holds for
50
random sets of

points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
with generic Hilbert function
[
15
]
.
∎

5.
A short virtual of a pair resolution in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}

In this section, we prove

.
To begin,
The virtual of a pair construction is one of the methods given by Berkesch, Erman, and Smith to construct virtual resolutions, and it relies on Maclagan and Smith’s notion of multigraded regularity.
We do not define this invariant in general, but instead provide a simplified characterization for our specific setting.

Proposition 5.1

(See
[
21
]
*Proposition 6.7)
.

Let
X
be a finite set of points in
\mathbb{P}^{n}\times\mathbb{P}^{m}
. Then the
multigraded regularity
of
S/I_{X}
is

\reg(S/I_{X})=\left\{\mathbf{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\mid H_{X}(\mathbf{d})=|X|\right\}.

Example 5.2
.

Recall that for points in sufficiently general position in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
, the Hilbert matrix is given by

H_{X}(i,j)=\min\{|X|,T_{i,1}T_{j,2}\}\quad\text{for all}\quad i,j\geq 0,

Therefore if

so

for all finite sets of

.
∎

]
*Theorem 1.3)

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
X
be a set of
N\geq 12
points in sufficiently general position in
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and let
F_{\bullet}
be the minimal free resolution of
S/I_{X}
with Betti numbers
\beta_{k,(i,j)}
.
If
DH_{X}(i,j)
is the first positive entry in the
i
th row of
DH_{X}
(excluding the
0
th and
1
st row),
then
\beta_{2,(i,j)}=DH_{X}(i,j)
, i.e., that positive entry corresponds exactly to the number of first syzygies of
S/I_{X}
of degree
(i,j)
.
Furthermore,
\beta_{2,(i,j^{\prime})}=0
for all
j^{\prime}<j
,
i.e., there are no first syzygies of smaller degree coming from that row.

Proof.

This argument follows the approach of
[
6
]
*Lemma 3.2.
Let
F_{\bullet}
be the minimal free resolution of
S/I_{X}
. For
i\geq 0
, let
C^{\leq i}
denote the subcomplex of
F_{\bullet}
with summands generated in degree
\mathbf{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})
such that
a_{1}\leq i
.
These complexes provide a filtration of
F_{\bullet}
, so for
i\geq 1
, let
C^{i}
denote the cokernel of the natural inclusion
C^{\leq i-1}\hookrightarrow C^{\leq i}
.

When
K\gg N
, we claim that
C^{\leq i}
is a virtual resolution of a pair for
\left(S/I_{X},(i-1,K-2)\right)
.
To see this, note that
(0,N-2)\in\reg(S/I_{X})
by
5.1
.
Thus
(i-1,K-2)\in\reg(S/I_{X})
for all
i\geq 1,K\gg N
.
By choosing
K
to be the largest degree in the second coordinate of a generator of any summand in
C^{\leq i}
,

C^{\leq i}
is exactly the virtual resolution of the pair
(S/I_{X},(i-1,K-2))
.
Now
0\to C^{\leq i-1}\to C^{\leq i}\to C^{i}\to 0
is a short exact sequence of complexes, and
C^{\leq i-1}
and
C^{\leq i}
are both virtual resolutions of
S/I_{X}
by
5.3
, so
C^{i}
must have irrelevant homology.
Further, since the first coordinates of the degrees of all generators in
C^{i}
are fixed at
i
, it is actually a complex over
\Bbbk[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}]
with homology modules annihilated by some power of
\langle y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}\rangle
. The Acyclicity Lemma
[
10
]
*Lemma 20.11 thus ensures that
H_{k}(C^{i})=0
for all
k>1
.

Now if
DH_{X}(i,j)
is the first positive entry with
i\geq 2
in the
i
th row of
DH_{X}
, then

C^{i}\colon\quad 0\leftarrow C^{i}_{1}\leftarrow C^{i}_{2}\leftarrow C^{i}_{3}\leftarrow C^{i}_{4}\leftarrow 0

is a minimal free resolution over
\Bbbk[y_{0},y_{1},y_{2}]
, so there are no units in its maps and the minimal generating degrees of summands must increase with homological degree.

By
4.4
, it follows that the Betti numbers
\beta_{1,(i,j^{\prime})}
come precisely from the first negative entries of the
i
th row of
DH_{X}
, where
j^{\prime}<j
.
By inspection from
5.6
,

0>DH_{X}(i,j-1)=\beta_{1,(i,j-1)}
and thus
\beta_{1,(i,j)}=0
.
Further, since the minimal degree of generators must increase in
C^{i}
,

\beta_{3,(i,j)}=\beta_{4,(i,j)}=0
and

DH_{X}(i,j)=\beta_{2,(i,j)}
, as desired.
∎

We are now prepared to prove the main theorem of this section.

Proof of
5.4
.

Since
X
has the generic Hilbert matrix in
1.2
,
H_{X}(N-1,0)=|X|=N
, so
(N-1,0)\in\reg(S/I_{X})
by
[
21
]
*Proposition 6.7.
Thus
5.3
produces a virtual resolution of
S/I_{X}
that is the subcomplex of the minimal free resolution of
S/I_{X}
consisting of all summands generated in degrees at most
(N,2)
.

We now examine the minimal free resolution of
S/I_{X}
.
By
4.4
,
the first Betti numbers can be read from the first negative entries in the rows of
DH_{X}
,
and these occurs at positions
(q,2),(q+1,2),(q^{\prime},1),(q^{\prime}+1,1)
, and
(N,0)
by
5.6
.
Since graded Betti numbers in a minimal free resolution must increase in degree due to minimality and by
4.4
, it also follows that
\beta_{k,(i,j)}=0
for
k>1
for the
(i,j)
for which
\beta_{1,(i,j)}\neq 0
.

Next, by
5.7
,
the second Betti numbers can be read from the first positive entries occurring after a negative entry along a row of
DH_{X}
.
By
5.6
, these occur in positions
(q^{\prime},2),(q^{\prime}+1,2)
, and
(N,1)
, with values which match the statement of the theorem.
Since graded Betti numbers in a minimal free resolution must increase in degree due to minimality, it also follows that
\beta_{k,(i,j)}=0
for
k>2
for the
(i,j)
for which
\beta_{2,(i,j)}\neq 0
.

Third Betti numbers are now only possible in degrees
(q^{\prime}+2,2),(q^{\prime}+3,2),\dots,(N,2)
. However, minimality of the original complex disallows both
\beta_{3,(i,j)}
and
\beta_{4,(i,j)}
to be nonzero.
Thus, only
\beta_{3,(N,2)}
can be nonzero and
\beta_{4,(N,2)}=0
, so by
1.5
,
\beta_{3,(N,2)}=3
.
Having exhausted the possible degrees for nonzero Betti numbers, the proof is now complete.
∎

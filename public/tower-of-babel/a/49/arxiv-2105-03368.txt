Least-energy nodal solutions of nonlinear equations with fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces
Anouar Bahrouni, Hlel Missaoui, Hichem Ounaies

Abstract
In this paper, we study the existence of least-energy nodal (sign-changing) weak solutions for a class of fractional Orlicz equations given by ( − △ g ) α ​ u + g ⁡ ( u ) = K ⁡ ( x ) ​ f ​ ( u ) , in ​ ℝ N , (-\triangle_{g})^{\alpha}u+g(u)=K(x)f(u),\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N}, where N ≥ 3 , ( − △ g ) α N\geq 3,\ (-\triangle_{g})^{\alpha} is the fractional Orlicz g g -Laplace operator, while f f ∈ C 1 ​ ( ℝ ) \in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}) and K K is a positive and continuous function. Under a suitable conditions on f f and K K , we prove a compact embeddings result for weighted fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces. Next, by a minimization argument on Nehari manifold and a quantitative deformation lemma, we show the existence of at least one nodal (sign-changing) weak solution.



Keywords:
Nodal solutions, Fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces, Nehari manifold method, least energy.

Introduction

In this paper, we are concerned with the existence of leat-energy
nodal (sign-changing) weak solutions
for the following problem

(-\triangle_{g})^{\alpha}u+g(u)=K(x)f(u),\ \ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{N},

(P)

The novelty of our work (see Theorem
2.9

features of this paper are the following:

(1)

the existence of nodal solutions.

(2)

the presence of the new fractional

Laplacian.

(3)

the lack of compactness due to the free action of the translation group on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first paper proving the
existence of sign-changing solutions with the combined effects
generated by the above features.

Recently, non-local problems and operators have been widely studied in the literature
and have attracted the attention of lot of mathematicians coming
from different research areas. This type of non-local

The real-valued function

The conjugate

\tilde{G}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\tilde{g}(s)\ ds,

\tilde{g}(t)=\sup\{s:\ g(s)\leq t\}

We have

st\leq G(s)+\tilde{G}(t),

(2.1)

We say that

(2.2)

If

(B\prec\prec A

(2.3)

In what follows, we assume that the

The Orlicz space

\|u\|_{(G)}=\inf\left\{\lambda>0\ :\ \rho(\frac{u}{\lambda})\leq 1\right\}.

Next, we introduce the Orlicz-Sobolev spaces and the fractional Orlicz-Sobolev spaces.

We denote by

W^{1,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
the Orlicz-Sobolev space defined by

W^{1,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\bigg\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\ \frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ i=1,...,N\bigg\}.

W^{1,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\|u\|_{1,G}=\|u\|_{(G)}+\||\nabla u|\|_{(G)}.

We denote by
W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{u\in L^{G}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):\ \overline{\rho}(u)<\infty\bigg\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\overline{\rho}(u)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G\left(\frac{u(x)-u(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{dxdy}{|x-y|^{N}}}.

We provide

\|u\|_{\alpha,G}=\|u\|_{(G)}+[u]_{(\alpha,G)},

Compactly embedding

In this section, we are going to prove a compactness embedding

\displaystyle{L^{M}_{K}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\bigg\{u:\ \mathbb{R}^{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\ u\ \text{mesurable and}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)M(|u|)\ dx<\infty\bigg\}}.

.

Suppose that the assumptions
(g_{1})-(g_{3})
and
(K_{1})-(K_{2})

-function such that

\lim_{|t|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{M(t)}{G(\lambda t)}=0,\ \ \text{for all}\ \ \lambda>0

\begin{array}[]{rlll}\displaystyle{\lim_{|t|\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{M(t)}{G_{*}(\lambda t)}=0}&and&\displaystyle{\lim_{|t|\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{M(t)}{G(\lambda t)}=0,}&\text{for all}\ \ \lambda>0\end{array}

\displaystyle=\displaystyle{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)f(w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}dx}.

By Fatou Lemma, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g\left(\frac{w(x)-w(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}\right)\frac{w^{\pm}(x)-w^{\pm}(y)}{|x-y|^{\alpha+N}}dxdy

(4.60)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}g(w^{\pm})w^{\pm}dx

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle{\liminf_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)f(w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}dx}.

In light of Lemma
3.2
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)f(w_{n}^{\pm})w_{n}^{\pm}dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}K(x)f(w^{\pm})w^{\pm}dx.

(4.61)

Combining
(\ref{4.5})
and
(\ref{4.7})
, we deduce that

\langle J^{\prime}(w),w^{\pm}\rangle\leq 0.

(4.62)

Putting together
(\ref{4.2})
and
(\ref{4.8})
and arguing as in the proof of Lemma
4.8
-(a), we deduce that
s,t\leq 1
.

Next we show that
J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})=c_{0}
and
t=s=1.

Exploiting the fact that
tw^{+}+sw^{-}\in\mathcal{M},\ w_{n}\in\mathcal{M},\ t,s\leq 1
and by applying Lemma
4.4
, Fatou Lemma and Lemma
3.2
, we conclude that

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})

\displaystyle=J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(tw^{+}+sw^{-}),tw^{+}+sw^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq J(w)-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w),w\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}[J(w_{n})-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}J(w_{n})=c_{0}.

Thus,
tw^{+}+sw^{-}\in\mathcal{M}
and
J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})=c_{0}.

Suppose that
0<t<1
or
0<s<1
, then

\displaystyle c_{0}\leq J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})

\displaystyle=J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(tw^{+}+sw^{-}),tw^{+}+sw^{-}\rangle

\displaystyle<J(w)-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w),w\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}[J(w_{n})-\frac{1}{2m}\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w_{n}),w_{n}\rangle]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{n\rightarrow+\infty}J(w_{n})=c_{0},

it yields a contradiction, thus
t=s=1
, so
w=w^{+}+w^{-}\in\mathcal{M}
and
J(w^{+}+w^{-})=c_{0}.

It remains to show that
w
is a critical point of
J,
that is

J^{{}^{\prime}}(w)=0.
The proof is similar to the argument used in

[
12
]
. For the reader’s convenience we will give the details. We
argue by contradiction, suppose that
w
is a regular point of
J
,
thus
J^{{}^{\prime}}(w)\neq 0
. Then, there exist
\beta>0
and
v_{0}\in W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\|v_{0}\|=1,
such that

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(w),v_{0}\rangle=2\beta>0
. By the continuity of

J^{{}^{\prime}},
we choose a
radius
R
so that
\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(v),v_{0}\rangle=\beta>0
for every
v\in B_{R}(w)\subset\mathbb{X}
with
v^{\pm}\neq 0.

Let us fix
D=(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})\times(\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2})\ \subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, such that

(i)

(1,1)\in D
and
\Phi^{w}(t,s)=(0,0)
in
\bar{D}
if and only if
(t,s)=(1,1);

(ii)

c_{0}\notin h^{w}(\partial D);

(iii)

\{tw^{+}+sw^{-}:(t,s)\in\bar{D}\}\subset B_{R}(w),

where
h^{w}
and
\Phi^{w}
are defined in Lemma
4.8
.

Let
0<r<R
, such that

\mathcal{B}=\overline{B_{r}(w)}\subset B_{R}(w)\ \text{and}\ \mathcal{B}\cap\{tw^{+}+sw^{-}\ :(t,s)\in\partial D\}=\emptyset.

(4.63)

Now, let us define a continuous mapping
\rho:W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow[0,+\infty[
such that

\rho(u):=\text{dist}(u,\mathcal{B}^{c})\ \text{for all}\ u\in W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and a bounded Lipschitz vector field
V:W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\rightarrow W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
given by
V(u)=-\rho(u)v_{0}
. According to the general theory of differential equations,
the following Cauchy problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{r c l}\eta^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau,u)&=&V(\eta(\tau,u)),\ \text{for all}\ \tau>0,\\
&&\\
\eta(0,u)&=&u.\end{array}\right.

(E)

admits a unique continuous solution
\eta(.,u)
. Moreover, there exists
\tau_{0}>0
such that for all
\tau\in[0,\tau_{0}]
the following properties hold:

(a)

\eta(\tau,u)=u
for all
u\notin\mathcal{B};

(b)

\tau\rightarrow J(\eta(\tau,u))
is decreasing for all
\eta(\tau,u)\in\mathcal{B};

(c)

J(\eta(\tau,w))\leq J(w)-\frac{r\beta}{2}\tau_{0}.

Indeed,
(a)
follows by the definition of
\rho.
Regarding
(b),
we observe that

\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\eta(\tau,u)),v_{0}\rangle=\beta>0
for
\eta(\tau,u)\in\mathcal{B}\subset B_{R}(w),
by the definition of
\rho,
we infer that
\rho(\eta(\tau,u))>0
, then

\frac{d}{d\tau}(J(\eta(\tau)))=\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\eta(\tau)),\eta^{{}^{\prime}}(\tau)\rangle=-\rho(\eta(\tau))\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\eta(\tau)),v_{0}\rangle=-\rho(\eta(\tau))\beta<0,\ \text{for all}\ \eta(\tau)\in\mathcal{B},

that is
J(\eta(\tau,u))
is decreasing with respect to
\tau.

(c)
Since
\eta(\tau,u)\in\mathcal{B}
for every
\tau\in[0,\tau_{0}],
we assume that

\|\eta(\tau,w)-w\|\leq\frac{r}{2}\ \text{for any}\ \tau\in[0,\tau_{0}].

It follow that
\rho(\eta(\tau,w))=\text{dist}(\eta(\tau,w),\mathcal{B}^{c})\geq\frac{r}{2},
then

\frac{d}{d\tau}J(\eta(\tau,w))=-\rho(\eta(\tau,w))\beta\leq-\frac{r\beta}{2}.

Integrating on
[0,\tau_{0}],
we get

J(\eta(\tau,w))-J(w)\leq-\frac{r\beta}{2}\tau_{0}.

We consider a suitable deformed path
\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}:\overline{D}\rightarrow W^{\alpha,G}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
defined by

\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s):=\eta(\tau_{0},tw^{+}+sw^{-}),\ \text{for all}\ (t,s)\in\overline{D}.

We see that

\max_{(t,s)\in\overline{D}}J(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s))<c_{0}.

Indeed, by
(b)
and the fact that
\eta(0,u)=u,
we get

\displaystyle J(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s))

\displaystyle=J(\eta(\tau_{0},tw^{+}+sw^{-}))\leq J(\eta(0,tw^{+}+sw^{-}))

\displaystyle=J(tw^{+}+sw^{-})=h^{w}(t,s)<c_{0}\ \text{for all}\ (t,s)\in\overline{D}\setminus\{(1,1)\}.

For
(t,s)=(1,1),
in virtue of
(c)
, we obtain

\displaystyle J(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(1,1))

\displaystyle=J(\eta(\tau_{0},w^{+}+w^{-}))=J(\eta(0,w))

\displaystyle\leq J(w)-\frac{r\beta}{2}\tau_{0}<J(w)=h^{w}(1,1)=c_{0},

thus,
\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(\overline{D})\cap\mathcal{M}=\emptyset,
that is,

\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s)\notin\mathcal{M}\ \text{for all}\ (t,s)\in\overline{D}.

(4.64)

On the other side, let
\Psi_{\tau_{0}}:\overline{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

\Psi_{\tau_{0}}:=\left(\frac{\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s)),(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s))^{+}\rangle}{t},\frac{\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s)),(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(t,s))^{-}\rangle}{s}\right),

By
(\ref{4.35})
and
(a)
, for all
(t,s)\in\partial D
and
\tau=\tau_{0},
we observe that

\Psi_{\tau_{0}}(t,s)=\left(\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(tw^{+}+sw^{-}),w^{+}\rangle,\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(tw^{+}+sw^{-}),w^{-}\rangle\right)=\Phi^{w}(t,s).

Using Brouwer’s topological degree, we obtain

\text{deg}(\Psi_{\tau_{0}},D,(0,0))=\text{deg}(\Phi^{w},D,(0,0))=\text{sgn}(\det(\Phi^{w})^{{}^{\prime}}(1,1))=1,

\Psi_{\tau_{0}}
has a zero
(\bar{t},\bar{s})\in D
, namely

\Psi_{\tau_{0}}(\bar{t},\bar{s})=(0,0)\Longleftrightarrow\langle J^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(\bar{t},\bar{s})),(\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(\bar{t},\bar{s}))^{\pm}\rangle=0.

Consequently there exists
(\bar{t},\bar{s})\in D
such that
\bar{\eta}_{\tau_{0}}(\bar{t},\bar{s})\in\mathcal{M}
, thus the contradiction with
(\ref{eq5.9}).

We conclude that
w
is a critical point of
J.

1.
Introduction

In a recent paper (see
[
43
]
) I. Snopce and P.A. Zalesskiĭ showed that for pro-
\ell
completions of right-angled Artin groups,
the following four properties are equivalent: the Bloch-Kato property, 1-cyclotomicity, the elementary type property, and the realizability as a maximal pro-
\ell
Galois group .
This theorem can be seen as a pro-
\ell
analogue of a result of C. Droms (see
[
11
]
) for discrete right-angled Artin groups, and provides evidence to the conjecture that a finitely generated cyclotomically oriented
Bloch-Kato pro-
\ell
group
(G,\theta)
is necessarily of elementary type (see
[
31
, § 7.5]
), and therefore to the
Elementary Type Conjecture
formulated by I. Efrat in
[
13
]
.

In this paper we generalize the concept of right-angled Artin pro-
\ell
group to non-necessarily trivially oriented examples.

By an
oriented graph

\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
we will understand a non-empty
set of vertices

\mathscr{V}
together with a
set of edges

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}\smallsetminus\Delta(\mathscr{V}),\qquad\text{where }\Delta(\mathscr{V})=\{\>(v,v)\>\mid\>v\in\mathscr{V}\>\}.

Thus, for an edge
\mathbf{e}=(v,w)\in\mathscr{E}
, its inverse
\bar{\mathbf{e}}=(w,v)
might be contained in
\mathscr{E}
(in which case
\mathbf{e}
will be said to be an
ordinary edge
), or not (in which case
\mathbf{e}
will be said to be
special
). Hence
\mathscr{E}
is the disjoint union
\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}}\sqcup\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}
of the sets of ordinary edges and special edges.

In a similar fashion,
\mathscr{V}
is the disjoint union
\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{o}}\sqcup\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{s}}
of the sets of
ordinary vertices
and
special vertices
, satisfying the following condition: the origin (i.e., the first coordinate) of every edge is an ordinary vertex, i.e., a vertex can be special only if either it is isolated or it is the end of a special edge (see §
2.2
below).

For an oriented graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
and a continuous homomorphism
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}

we call the pro-
\ell
group
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
given by the presentation

(1.1)

G_{\Gamma,\lambda}=\left\langle\>v\in\mathscr{V}\>\mid\>\forall\>\mathbf{e}=(v,w)\in\mathscr{E},\>wvw^{-1}=\begin{cases}v&\text{if }\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}},\\
v^{\lambda(1)}&\text{if }\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\end{cases}\>\right\rangle_{\mathrm{pro-}{\ell}}

the
oriented right-angled Artin pro-
\ell
group
(oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG for short) associated to
\Gamma
and
\lambda
.
By definition, every oriented right-angled Artin pro-
\ell
group
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
carries the orientation

\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}\colon G_{\Gamma,\lambda}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
given by
\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}(v)=1
if
v\in\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{o}}
, and
\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}(v)=\lambda(1)
if
v\in\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{s}}
.
Thus, if
\Gamma
is an oriented graph without special edges, then
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is just a right-angled Artin pro-
\ell
group, and
\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}=\mathbf{1}
is the constant
1
-function.
In spite of the rather elementary presentation (
1.1
), oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs yield surprising variety and flexibility — for example, among oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs one finds free pro-
\ell
groups,
\ell
-adic analytic groups, and even finite
\ell
-groups.

Our main goal is to prove the following “oriented analogue” of
[
43
, Thm. 1.2, Thm. 1.5]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Gamma
be an oriented graph, let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a continuous homomorphism
(
satisfying
\image(\lambda)\subseteq 1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\ell=2
)
, and let
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
be the oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG associated to
\Gamma
and
\lambda
. The following are equivalent.

(0)

The oriented graph
\Gamma
is of elementary type
(see Definition
2.9
).

(i)

The pro-
\ell
group
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is isomorphic to the maximal pro-
\ell
Galois group of a field containing a primitive
\ell
-th root of 1
(
and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
\ell=2
)
.

(ii)

The pro-
\ell
group
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is a Bloch-Kato pro-
\ell
group.

(iii)

The oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
is a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
\ell
group.

(iv)

The oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
is an oriented pro-
\ell
group of elementary type.

(v)

Every finitely generated closed subgroup of
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is again isomorphic to an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG.

Note that condition (0) in Theorem
1.1
gives a combinatorial (and rather immediate) criterion to check whether an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG may occur as a maximal pro-
\ell
Galois group or not. On one handside, Theorem
1.1
provides a plethora of brand new examples of pro-
\ell
groups which do not occur as maximal pro-
\ell
Galois groups.
It is worth mentioning that the relations involved in the presentation of an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG are elementary — just an elementary commutator times, possibly, the
\lambda(1)
-th power of a generator —, especially in comparison with other examples of pro-
\ell
groups which are known not to occur as maximal pro-
\ell
Galois groups (see, e.g.,
[
2
,
37
]
,
[
6
, § 9]
,
[
26
, § 7]
), whose presentations require higher commutators.

On the other hand, considering the richness of the family of oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs, Theorem
1.1
corroborates the Elementary Type Conjecture, and the
Smoothness Conjecture
, formulated by Ch. De Clercq and M. Florence (see
[
7
]
).

In fact, there are several other conjectures concerning the structure of the maximal pro-
\ell
Galois group
G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell)
of a field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
\ell
-th root of 1, and its
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-cohomology, e.g., L. Positselski’s formulation of
Bogomolov Conjecture
, which predicts that
G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell)
is a free-by-locally uniform pro-
\ell
group (see
[
29
]
and
[
32
]
); J. Minač and N.D. Tân’s
Massey Vanishing Conjecture
, which predicts that
G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell)
satisfies the
n
-Massey product vanishing property for every
n>2
(see
[
25
]
); and the
Universal Koszulity Conjecture
, formulated by J. Minač et al., which predicts that
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell),\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is a universally Koszul algebra (see
[
23
]
).
These three properties are satisfied by oriented pro-
\ell
groups of elementary type (see respectively
[
32
, Thm. 1.2]
,
[
36
, Thm. 1.3]
, and
[
23
, § 1]
).
Therefore, from Theorem
1.1
one concludes the following
providing evidence for the three aforementioned conjectures.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
\Gamma
be an oriented graph and let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a continuous homomorphism
(
satisfying
\image(\lambda)\subseteq 1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\ell=2
)
.
If
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}\simeq G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell)
for some field
\mathbb{K}
containing a primitive
\ell
-th root of 1, then

(i)

G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
has the Bogomolov-Positselski property, i.e., it is the Frattini pro-
\ell
cover of a locally uniform pro-
\ell
group with free pro-
\ell
kernel;

(ii)

for every
n>2
,
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
satisfies the
n
-Massey product vanishing property, i.e., every non-empty
n
-fold Massey product associated to an
n
-tuple of elements of
\mathrm{H}^{1}(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
contains 0;

(iii)

the algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is universally Koszul.

Subsequently, we will focus on oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs associated to
chordal
oriented graphs.

Recall that an oriented graph
\Gamma
is said to be chordal if it does not contain cycles other than triangles as induced subgraphs.

Moreover, an oriented graph will be said to be
specially oriented
if the terminus of a special edge is always a special vertex (see Definition
2.5
). Note that an oriented graph of elementary type is always specially oriented and chordal, but not vice-versa.

For oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs associated to chordal specially oriented graphs we prove the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Gamma
be a chordal specially oriented graph, let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a continuous homomorphism
(
satisfying
\image(\lambda)\subseteq 1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\ell=2
)
, and let
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
be the oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG associated to
\Gamma
and
\lambda
.
Then:

(i)

G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
has the Bogomolov-Positselski property;

(ii)

every finitely generated closed subgroup of
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
. In particular,
G
is coherent;

(iii)

the
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-cohomology algebra
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is quadratic — in particular, it is isomorphic to the exterior Stanley-Reisner
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-algebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\ddot{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{op}})
associated to
\ddot{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Note that Theorem
1.3
–(i) can be seen as an oriented pro-
\ell
analogue of a theorem of H. Servatius, C. Droms and B. Servatius (see
[
42
]
). One may speculate whether for a
generalized pro-
\ell
RAAG’s
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
based on a specially oriented graph
\Gamma

the Bogomolov-Positselski property of
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
is equivalent to the
chordality of
\Gamma
.
Theorem
1.3
–(ii) can be seen as an oriented pro-
\ell
analogue of another theorem of C. Droms (see
[
10
]
). As before one is tempted to believe that the coherence property of
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}

for a specially oriented graph
\Gamma
is equivalent to the
chordality of
\Gamma
.
Theorem
1.3
–(iii) can be seen as an oriented analogue of a result of M. Salvetti on discrete RAAG’s
(cf.
[
39
]
) which holds independently of the chordality of the underlying graph. Therefore, one may conjecture
that Theorem
1.3
–(iii) holds for all
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
based on specially oriented graphs
\Gamma
(see
1.4
below).

One reason why it is interesting to investigate right-angled Artin pro-
\ell
groups
G_{\Gamma}
(with
\Gamma
a graph without special vertices and edges) in a Galois-theoretic context
is the fact that
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is isomorphic to the exterior Stanley-Reisner
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-algebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\Gamma^{\mathrm{op}})
associated to
\Gamma
, and thus it is quadratic and Koszul (see, e.g.,
[
28
, § 1.2]
).
Moreover, one has that the
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-group algebra

\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma})

associated to the augmentation filtration
is isomorphic to the Cartier-Foita
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-algebra
\mathbf{R}_{\Gamma}
associated to
\Gamma
, and thus it is quadratic and Koszul as well.
In particular, it is the
quadratic dual
of
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
(see
[
1
, Thm. 1.2]
).

The same phenomenon occurs for oriented pro-
\ell
groups
(G,\theta)
of elementary type: if
G
is such a pro-
\ell
group, then both algebras
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
and
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G)
are Koszul and quadratic dual to each other (see
[
24
, Thm. A–B]
).
Moreover, the same phenomenon is conjectured to hold for finitely generated maximal pro-
\ell
Galois groups of fields containing a primitive
\ell
-th root of 1, see
[
24
, Conj. 1.3 and Ques. 1.5]
.
We suspect that the same holds also for oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs associated to specially oriented graphs. Altogether we ask the following.

Question 1.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be a specially oriented graph, let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a continuous homomorphism
(
satisfying
\image(\lambda)\subseteq 1+4\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\ell=2
)
, and let
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
be the oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG associated to
\Gamma
and
\lambda
.

(1)

Is it true that the
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-graded
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-group algebra
\mathrm{gr}_{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma,\lambda})
is quadratic — and thus isomorphic to the Cartier-Foita
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-algebra associated to
\Gamma
?

(2)

Is it true that the cohomology ring
\mathbf{H}^{\bullet}(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is isomorphic to the exterior Stanley-Reisner
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
-algebra
\mathbf{\Lambda}_{\bullet}(\ddot{\Gamma}^{\mathrm{op}})
?

2.
Graphs

Although there is no standard notion of a
graph
, all notions known to the authors
have one common feature: a graph consists of a pair of sets
(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
,
a set of vertices

\mathscr{V}
,
and
a set of edges

\mathscr{E}
.
In this paper we make use of two different notions —
naïve
graphs and
oriented
graphs —, which are now discussed in more detail.

2.1.
Naïve graphs

A
naïve graph

\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
consists of a non-empty set of
vertices

\mathscr{V}
and a set of
edges

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathscr{V})
, where
\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathscr{V})
denotes the set of subsets of
\mathscr{V}
of cardinality
2
.
The naïve graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
satisfying
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathscr{V})
is said to be a
complete graph
.
Subgraphs are defined in the obvious way, and a subgraph
\Lambda=(\mathscr{V}^{\prime},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})
of the naïve graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
is said to be induced, if

(2.1)

\mathscr{E}^{\prime}=\mathscr{E}\cap\mathscr{P}_{2}(\mathscr{V}^{\prime}).

Moreover, the subgraph is said to be proper if
\mathscr{V}^{\prime}\subsetneq\mathscr{V}.

Henceforth, we will always consider finite naïve graphs, i.e., naïve graphs with a finite number of vertices.

Recall that a
tree
is a connected naïve graph
\mathrm{T}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
,
\mathscr{V}\neq\varnothing
, without circuits as subgraphs (cf.
[
41
, § 2.2]
).
Every connected naiv̈e graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
contains a
maximal subtree
, i.e., a tree
\mathrm{T}=(\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{T}},\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{T}})
which is a subgraph of
\Gamma
such that
\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{T}}=\mathscr{V}
(cf.
[
41
, Prop. 2.11]
).

.

2.2.
Oriented graphs

An
oriented graph

\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
consists of a non-empty set of
vertices

\mathscr{V}
, partitioned as a disjoint union
\mathscr{V}=\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{s}}\sqcup\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{o}}
; and a set of
edges

\mathscr{E}\subseteq\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}\smallsetminus\Delta(\mathscr{V}),

where
\Delta(\mathscr{V})=\{\,(v,v)\mid v\in\mathscr{V}\,\}
denotes the diagonal in
\mathscr{V}\times\mathscr{V}
.
By definition, every oriented graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
comes equipped with two maps, the
origin

o\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{V}
given by the projection on the first coordinate, and the
terminus

t\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{V}
, given by the
projection on the second coordinate.
One has a partition
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\sqcup\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}}
, where

\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}=\{\>\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}\>\mid\>(t(\mathbf{e}),o(\mathbf{e}))\notin\mathscr{E}\>\}

is the set of
special edges
, while
\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}}
is the set of
ordinary edges
.
Analogously, the elements of
\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{s}}
and
\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{o}}
are called special vertices and ordinary vertices respectively, and they must satisfy the following condition: if
\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}
, then
o(\mathbf{e})\in\mathscr{V}_{\mathrm{o}}
.

Every oriented graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
defines a naïve graph

\ddot{\Gamma}=(\mathscr{V},\ddot{\mathscr{E}})
, where

(2.3)

\ddot{\mathscr{E}}=\{\,\{o(\mathbf{e}),t(\mathbf{e})\}\mid\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}\,\}.

The notions of subgraphs, induced subgraphs, cliques and patching of oriented graphs are defined in the obvious way.
Henceforth, we will consider only finite oriented graphs.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be an oriented graph.
We realize a special edge
(v,w)\in\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}
as an arrow originating at
v
and pointing at
w
.
If
(v,w),(w,v)\in\mathscr{E}
, then we realize them as a unique “unoriented” edge joining
v
and
w
.
For example, the two pictures

3.1.
Preliminaries on pro-
\ell

We work in the world of pro-
\ell
groups.
Henceforth, every subgroup of a pro-
\ell

In particular, for a pro-
\ell
group

and a positive integer

will denote the closed subgroup of
G
generated by the
n

G
.
Moreover, for two elements

{}^{g}h=ghg^{-1},\qquad\text{and}\qquad[g,h]={}^{g}h\cdot h^{-1}.

Given two subgroups

of
G

[H_{1},H_{2}]
will denote the closed subgroup of
G
generated by all commutators
[h,g]

with

and
g\in H_{2}
.
In particular:

(a)

will denote the closure of the commutator subgroup
[G,G]
of
G
;

(b)

\Phi(G)=G^{\ell}\cdot G^{\prime}
will denote the Frattini subgroup of
G
;

(c)

G_{(3)}
will denote the subgroup of

defined by

G_{(3)}=\begin{cases}G^{\ell}\cdot[G^{\prime},G]&\text{if }{\ell}\neq 2\\
G^{4}\cdot(G^{\prime})^{2}\cdot[G^{\prime},G]&\text{if }{\ell}=2.\end{cases}

For the properties of Galois cohomology of pro-
\ell
groups we refer to
[
40
, Ch. I]

3.2.

Let

1+{\ell}\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}=\{1+{\ell}\lambda,\>\lambda\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\}.

\ell
group
(G,\theta)
comes endowed with a distinguished normal subgroup of
G
,

K_{\theta}(G)=\left\langle\>{}^{g}h\cdot h^{-\theta(g)}\>\mid\>g\in G,\>h\in\kernel(\theta)\>\right\rangle.

(cf.
[
12
]
).
One has
K_{\theta}(G)\subseteq\Phi(G)
, and moreover
K_{\theta}(G)\supseteq\kernel(\theta)^{\prime}
, so that the quotient
\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
is an abelian pro-
\ell
group.
Observe that if
\theta
is trivial (i.e., constantly equal to 1), then
K_{\theta}(G)=G^{\prime}
.

One has the following (cf., e.g.,
[
32
, Prop. 2.6 and § 3.2]
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be an oriented pro-
\ell
group with torsion-free orientation.
The following are equivalent:

(i)

The map

(3.1)

\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)/{\ell}^{n})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{\ell}),

induced by the epimorphism of continuous
G
-modules
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}(1)/{\ell}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, is surjective for every
n\geq 1
.

(ii)

The quotient
\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
is a free abelian pro-
\ell
group.

(iii)

There exists an epimorphism of pro-
\ell
groups
\varphi\colon G\to\bar{G}
where
\bar{G}
is locally uniform and
\kernel(\varphi)\subseteq\Phi(G)
, inducing an epimorphism of oriented pro-
\ell
groups
(G,\theta)\to(\bar{G},\eth_{\bar{G}})
.

If these conditions hold, then
\kernel(\varphi)=K_{\theta}(G)
and

(\bar{G},\eth_{\bar{G}})\simeq\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)\rtimes(G/\kernel(\theta),\theta_{/\kernel(\theta)}).

An oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G,\theta)
with torsion-free orientation
\theta
satisfying the above equivalent conditions is said to be
Kummerian
(cf.
[
12
, Def. 3.4]
).
If moreover the oriented pro-
\ell
group
(H,\theta|_{H})
is Kummerian for every subgroup
H
of
G
, then the oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G,\theta)
is said to be
1-cyclotomic
(cf.
[
31
]
).
(Observe that in
[
7
,
35
,
34
]
, a 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
\ell
group is said to be
1-smooth
.)

Example 3.5
.

(a)

If
(G,\theta)
is a
\theta
-abelian oriented pro-
\ell
group with torsion-free orientation, then, by Proposition
3.4
–(iii), it is 1-cyclotomic, as
(H,\theta|_{H})
is
\theta|_{H}
-abelian for every subgroup
H
.

(b)

If
G
is a free pro-
\ell
group, then the oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G,\theta)
is 1-cyclotomic for any orientation
\theta\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
(cf.
[
31
, § 2.2]
).

(c)

If
(G,\theta)
is an oriented pro-
\ell
group with trivial orientation
\theta\equiv\mathbf{1}
,
then
(G,\theta)
is Kummerian if, and only if, the abelianization
G/G^{\prime}
is a free abelian pro-
\ell
group
(cf.
[
12
, Example 3.5–(1)]
).
Consequently,
(G,\theta)
is 1-cyclotomic if, and only if,
H/H^{\prime}
is a free abelian pro-
\ell
group for every subgroup
H
of
G
(pro-
\ell
groups satisfying this property are also called
absolutely torsion-free pro-
\ell
groups
, cf.
[
46
]
).

1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
\ell
groups arise naturally in Galois theory (cf.
[
12
, Thm. 4.2]
and
[
31
, § 2.3]
).

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a field containing a primitive
\ell
-th root of 1 (and also
\sqrt{-1}
if
\ell=2
).
Then
(G_{\mathbb{K}}(\ell),\hat{\theta}_{\mathbb{K},\ell})
is a torsion-free 1-cyclotomic oriented pro-
\ell
group.

The
pro-
\ell
version
of De Clercq-Florence’s Smoothness Conjecture (cf.
[
7
, Conj. 14.25]
, see also
[
35
, Conj. 2.10]
) states the following.

Conjecture 3.7
.

If a pro-
\ell
group
G
may be endowed with a 1-cyclotomic orientation
\theta\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
, then
G
is
weakly
Bloch-Kato.

The definition of weakly Bloch-Kato pro-
\ell
group may be found in
[
7
, Def. 14.23]
— for our purposes it is enough to remark that a Bloch-Kato pro-
\ell
group is (unsurprisingly) also weakly Bloch-Kato (see also
[
35
, § 2.3]
).

Remark 3.8
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be a Kummerian oriented pro-
\ell
group, with
\theta
a torsion-free orientation. Then by Proposition
3.4
one has

(G/K_{\theta}(G),\theta_{/K_{\theta}(G)})\simeq\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)\rtimes(G/\kernel(\theta),\theta_{/\kernel(\theta)})

with
\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
a free abelian pro-
\ell
group.
Now let
N
be a normal subgroup of
G
contained in
\kernel(\theta)
such that the map

\mathrm{res}_{G,N}^{1}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}(G,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})\longrightarrow\mathrm{H}^{1}(N,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})^{G}

is injective — namely, by duality the map
N/N^{\ell}[N,G]\to G/\Phi(G)
induced by the inclusion
N\hookrightarrow G
is injective.
Then the group
\bar{N}=NK_{\theta}(G)/K_{\theta}(G)
is an isolated subgroup of the free pro-
\ell
group
\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)
.
Therefore,

\frac{\kernel(\theta)}{K_{\theta}(G)}\simeq\bar{N}\times A\qquad\text{and}\qquad\frac{\kernel(\theta_{/N})}{K_{\theta_{/N}}(G/N)}\simeq\frac{\kernel(\theta)/K_{\theta}(G)}{\bar{N}}\simeq A,

where

is a free pro-
\ell
group.
Thus, also the oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G/N,\theta_{/N})
is Kummerian by Proposition
3.4
.

3.5.
Oriented pro-
\ell
groups of elementary type

The following definition is due to I. Efrat (cf.

, § 3]
, see also
[

, § 7.5]

Definition 3.9
.

The family of oriented pro-
\ell

is the smallest class of oriented pro-
\ell
groups such that

(a)

the oriented pro-
\ell
group

\ell
group
(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell},\lambda)
for any linear orientation
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}

(b)

any oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G,\theta)
with

a
Demushkin group
and
\theta\colon G\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
its canonical orientation (cf.
[
19
, Thm. 4]
and
[
31
, § 5.3]
);

(c)

if
(G_{1},\theta_{1})
and
(G_{2},\theta_{2})
are two oriented pro-
\ell
groups of elementary type, then also the free product

(G_{1},\theta_{1})\amalg^{\hat{\ell}}(G_{2},\theta_{2})

is an oriented pro-
\ell
group of elementary type;

(d)

if
(G,\theta)
is an oriented pro-
\ell
group of elementary type and
A
is a free abelian pro-
\ell
group, then also the oriented pro-
\ell
group

By
[

, Thm. 1.3]
, if

is an oriented pro-
\ell

is Bloch-Kato and

is 1-cyclotomic.
Moreover, one has the following fact (cf.
[
31
, § 3.3–3.4]
).

Fact 3.10
.

Let
(G,\theta)
be an oriented pro-
\ell
group and let

be a subgroup.

(i)

If
(G,\theta)=(G_{1},\theta_{1})\amalg^{\hat{\ell}}(G_{2},\theta_{2})
, then

(H,\theta|_{H})\simeq{\coprod_{i\in I}}^{\hat{\ell}}(H_{i},\tau_{i}),

where

is a subgroup of either

or

— and

denotes the restriction of
\theta_{1}
or
\theta_{2}
respectively —, or

is a free pro-
\ell
group.

(ii)

If

for some oriented pro-
\ell

(G_{\circ},\theta_{\circ})
and

for some subgroup

In particular, if

\ell

\ell

,

, Conj. 1.2]

[

, § 10]

.

Let

(

4.
Oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs

4.1.
Oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs

From now on a non-trivial torsion-free orientation
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
will be called a
linear
orientation.
Recall from the Introduction the definition of an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
associated to an oriented graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
and a linear orientation
\lambda
.
The following fact is a direct consequence of the definition of an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG.

Fact 4.1
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be an oriented graph, and let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a linear orientation.

(i)

If
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}\sqcup\Gamma_{2}
, then the associated oriented pro-
\ell
group
(\mathcal{G}_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
decomposes as free pro-
\ell
product of oriented pro-
\ell
groups

(G_{\Gamma_{1},\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma_{1},\lambda})\amalg^{\hat{\ell}}(G_{\Gamma_{2},\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma_{2},\lambda}).

(ii)

More generally, if
\Gamma
is the patching of two induced subgraphs
\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2}
along a common induced subgraph
\Lambda
,
then then the associated oriented pro-
\ell
group
(\mathcal{G}_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
decomposes as
amalgamated free pro-
\ell
product
of oriented pro-
\ell
groups

(G_{\Gamma_{1},\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma_{1},\lambda})\amalg_{G_{\Lambda,\lambda}}^{\hat{\ell}}(G_{\Gamma_{2},\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma_{2},\lambda}),

with amalgam
G_{\Lambda,\lambda}
(for the definition of amalgamated free pro-
\ell
product of oriented pro-
\ell
groups see
[
32
, Def. 5.4]
).

(iii)

If
\Gamma=\nabla(\Gamma^{\prime})
for some induced subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
, with tip
v\in\mathscr{V}
, then one has

(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})\simeq\langle\>v\>\rangle\rtimes(G_{\Gamma^{\prime},\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma^{\prime},\lambda}).

4.2.
Oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs and generalized pro-
\ell
RAAGs

Following
[
30
, § 5.1]
, we say that an
\ell
-labelled graph
is an oriented graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
such that
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}
, endowed with a map
f=(f_{o},f_{t})\colon\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\to\ell\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\times\ell\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, which we call the
\ell
-labelling
of
\Gamma
.

The
generalized pro-
\ell
RAAG
associated to a
\ell
-labelled graph
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
with labelling
f
is the pro-
\ell
group with presentation

\left\langle\>v\in\mathscr{V}\>\mid\>[v,w]v^{f_{o}(\mathbf{e})}w^{f_{t}(\mathbf{e})}=1\text{ for }\mathbf{e}=(v,w)\in\mathscr{E}\>\right\rangle.

Now let
\lambda
be a linear orientation, and let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be any oriented graph, with
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\sqcup\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}}
.
Then the pro-
\ell
group
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is the generalized pro-
\ell
RAAG associated to a
\ell
-labelled graph
\Gamma^{\prime}=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E}^{\prime})
such that
\ddot{\mathscr{E}}^{\prime}=\ddot{\mathscr{E}}
, with
\ell
-labelling

f(\mathbf{e})=\begin{cases}(\lambda(1)-1,0)&\text{if }\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{s}}\\
(0,0)&\text{if }\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}_{\mathrm{o}}.\end{cases}

4.4.
Specially oriented graphs and Kummerianity

The goal of this subsection is to prove that Kummerianity characterises oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs associated to specially oriented graphs.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be an oriented graph, let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a linear orientation, and let
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda})
be the associated oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG.
First, we need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\theta\colon G_{\Gamma,\lambda}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}^{\times}
be a torsion-free orientation such that the oriented pro-
\ell
group
(G_{\Gamma,\lambda},\theta)
is Kummerian, and let
\varphi\colon G_{\Gamma,\lambda}\to\bar{G}
be an epimorphism satisfying condition–(iii) of Proposition
3.4
.
Moreover, for every edge
\mathbf{e}\in\mathscr{E}
, let
H_{\mathbf{e}}
be the subgroup of
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
generared by
o(\mathbf{e}),t(\mathbf{e})
.
Then the restriction

\varphi|_{H_{\mathbf{e}}}\colon H_{\mathbf{e}}\longrightarrow\bar{G}

is a monomorphism of locally uniform pro-
\ell
groups.

Proof.

Set
G=G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
.
By Proposition
3.4
,
\kernel{\varphi}=K_{\theta}(G)
, and thus
\eth_{\bar{G}}=\theta_{/\kernel(\theta)}
.
For every edge
e=(v,w)\in\mathscr{E}
,
\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}})
is a subgroup of the locally uniform pro-
\ell
group
\bar{G}
, and thus it is locally uniform as well, with associated canonical orientation
\eth_{\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}})}=\eth_{\bar{G}}|_{\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}})}
.
Moreover,
K_{\theta}(G)\subseteq\Phi(G)
by definition, so that
\varphi(v)
and
\varphi(w)
are linearly independent modulo
\Phi(\bar{G})
, and
H_{\mathbf{e}}
is 2-generated.

On the other hand, consider the epimorphism of pro-
\ell
groups

\varphi|_{H_{\mathbf{e}}}\colon H_{\mathbf{e}}\longrightarrow\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}}).

By Example
4.3
–(a),
H_{\mathbf{e}}
is a quotient of the 2-generated locally uniform pro-
\ell
group

H=\langle\>v,w\>\mid\>R_{\lambda}(\mathbf{e})=1\>\rangle,

with associated canonical orientation
\eth_{H}
.
Altogether, one has a chain of epimorphisms of pro-
\ell
groups

H\to H_{\mathbf{e}}\to\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}})
, where both
H
and
\varphi(H_{\mathbf{e}})
are 2-generated locally uniform pro-
\ell
groups.
Therefore, both homomorphism are isomorphisms of 2-generated locally uniform pro-
\ell
groups, and one has a chain of isomorphism of oriented pro-

Set

.
Suppose that

7.5.
Coherent oriented pro-
\ell
RAAGs

A finitely generated pro-
\ell
group
G
is said to be
coherent
if every finitely generated subgroup
H\subseteq G
is also finitely presented, i.e., if
|\mathrm{H}^{1}(H,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})|<\infty
implies
|\mathrm{H}^{2}(H,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})|<\infty
.
Moreover,
G
is said to be
of type
FP_{\infty}
if
\mathrm{H}^{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
is finite for every
n\geq 1
.
We prove that every finitely generated subgroup of an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG associated to a chordal specially oriented graph — even if it may not occur as an oriented pro-
\ell
RAAG — is of type
FP_{\infty}
.

Theorem 7.8
.

Let
\Gamma=(\mathscr{V},\mathscr{E})
be a chordal specially oriented graph, and let
\lambda
be a linear orientation.
Then every finitely generated subgroup of
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is of type
FP_{\infty}
.
In particular,
G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is coherent.

Proof.

Set
G=G_{\Gamma,\lambda}
, and let
H
be a finitely generated subgroup of
G
.
Moreover, set

K=K_{\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}}(G)\cap H\qquad\text{and}\qquad Q=\frac{HK_{\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}}(G)}{K_{\theta_{\Gamma,\lambda}}(G)}.

Then one has a short exact sequence of pro-
\ell
groups

\textstyle{\{1\}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{K\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{H\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{Q\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\{1\}}

where
K
is a free pro-
\ell
group by Theorem
7.7
, and
Q
is locally powerful as
\Gamma
is specially oriented (cf. Theorem
4.9
).
By
[
18
, Thm. 2, § 3]
,
H
is of type
FP_{\infty}
if, and only if,
H_{n}(K,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}[\![Q]\!]
-module for each

Since

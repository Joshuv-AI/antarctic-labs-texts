1.
Certain properties of the Fox derivations

With any group
G
there is associated its group ring
{\bf Z}[G]
. An element of
{\bf Z}[G]
is a sum

\sum a_{g}g,\,g
ranging over the elements of
G
, where the integer
a_{g}
is equal to zero for all but a finite number of
g
.
Addition and multiplication in
{\bf Z}[G]
are defined by
\sum a_{g}g+\sum b_{g}g=\sum(a_{g}+b_{g})g

and
(\sum a_{g}g)\cdot(\sum b_{g}g)=\sum(\sum_{h}a_{gh^{-1}}b_{h})g
. The element
a
of
{\bf Z}
is identified with the element
a\cdot 1
of
{\bf Z}[G]
and the element
g
of
G
is identified with the element
1\cdot g
of
{\bf Z}[G]
, so that
{\bf Z}
and
G
are to be regarded as subsets of
{\bf Z}[G]
.
By a derivation in a group ring
{\bf Z}[G]
will be meant any mapping
\partial
of
{\bf Z}[G]
into itself which satisfies
\partial(u+v)=\partial(u)+\partial(v)
,
\partial(uv)=\partial(u)v+\varepsilon(u)\partial(v)
for any
u,\,v\in{\bf Z}[G]
, where
\varepsilon:\,{\bf Z}[G]\to{\bf Z}
is the natural augmentation.

Let
F
be a free group on free generators
\{g_{j}|j\in J\}
,
N
— a normal subgroup of
F
. We denote by
D_{j}

(j\in J)
the Fox derivations of the group ring
{\bf Z}[F]
. They are uniquely defined by the conditions
D_{j}(g_{j})=1,\,D_{k}(g_{j})=0\,\mbox{for}\,k\neq j
. It is easy to deduce the following relations:

(3)

\displaystyle D_{j}(f^{-1}nf)\equiv D_{j}(n)f\mod{{\bf Z}[F]\cdot(N-1)};

\displaystyle D_{j}(f^{-1})=-D_{j}(f)f^{-1};

\displaystyle u-\varepsilon(u)=\sum_{j\in J}(g_{j}-1)D_{j}(u);

where
u\in{\bf Z}(F),\,f\in F,\,n\in N
.

Let
G
be a group
x,\,y\in G
;
X,\,Y
are subgroups in
G
. Then
[x,y]=x^{-1}y^{-1}xy
,
x^{y}=y^{-1}xy
;

[X,Y]
denotes the subgroup generated by all commutators of the type
[x,y]
and
XY
denotes the subgroup generated by

\{xy\mid x\in X,\,y\in Y\}
.
If
A
is subgroup in
G
, then
A^{G}
denotes the normal subgroup generated by
A
in
G
.
By
\gamma_{k}(G)
we denote the
k
-th term of the lower central series of a group

, i.e.

\gamma_{1}(G)=G
,
\gamma_{i+1}(G)=[\gamma_{i}(G),G]
.

2.
A theorem on freedom for relatively free groups with a single relation

Let

— a subgroup of
F
generated by
\{g_{j}\mid j\in K\}

.
Define also that

,
\Delta_{0}^{\prime}={\bf Z}[H_{1}]
.

A coset of

-coset if an elements of
H

We call the

\beta
-coset the length of the shortest word in it and
shall define Schreier representatives inductively, using the

of a coset.

Choose the empty word as a representative of
N
. If
L
is a
\alpha
-coset of
length
one, choose any word of length one
in

as it representative. Assuming we have chosen representatives for all
\alpha
-cosets of
length
less then
r
, i.e. we have a Schreier coset function

for all
\alpha
-cosets of
length
less then
r
.
If
L
is a
\alpha
-coset of
length

r

(z_{m}\in\{g_{j}^{\pm 1}\mid j\in K\})
is a word in
L
,
we choose
\overline{z_{1}\ldots z_{r-1}}z_{r}
as a representative of
L
. Denote by

the system of representatives of
\alpha
-cosets.

If
L
is a
\beta
-coset of
length

L
as it representative. Assuming we have chosen representatives for all
\alpha
-cosets and all
\beta
-cosets of

r

L

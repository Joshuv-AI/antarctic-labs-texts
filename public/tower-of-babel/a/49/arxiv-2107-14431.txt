Mean Lipschitz-Killing curvatures for homogeneous random fractals Thanks: Steffen Winter was supported by DFG grant WI 3264/5-1 and Martina Zähle by DFG grant ZA 242/8-1.
Jan Rataj, Steffen Winter, Martina Zähle

Abstract
. Homogeneous random fractals form a probabilistic extension of self-similar sets with more dependencies than in random recursive constructions. For such random fractals we consider mean values of the Lipschitz-Killing curvatures of their parallel sets for small parallel radii. Under the Uniform Strong Open Set Condition and some further geometric assumptions we show that rescaled limits of these mean values exist as the parallel radius tends to 0 0 . Moreover, integral representations are derived for these limits which extend those known in the deterministic case.

1.
Introduction

Fractal versions of the
k
-th order Lipschitz-Killing curvatures
C_{k}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
known from convex geometry, differential geometry and geometric measure theory have been considered in
[
Wi08
,
WZ13
]
and subsequent papers for deterministic self-similar sets and in
[
Za11
]
for random recursive constructions. Compared to the latter class of models, homogeneous random fractals, as first considered in
[
Ha92
]
(for a special case), possess more dependencies in their structure. This leads, in particular, to the phenomenon that their a.s. Minkowski dimension, which agrees again with the a.s. Hausdorff dimension, is determined by a different equation than in the recursive case (see
[
RU11
]
together with
[
Tr17
]
for the general case). Moreover, in
[
Tr21a
]
it is shown that in the non-deterministic case there exists no gauge function for an exact Hausdorff measure. It was conjectured in
[
Za20
]
that the almost sure Minkowski content does not exist either. Meanwhile this has been proved in
[
Tr21b
]
. In contrast to this striking difference in the almost sure behaviour of both models, it turned out that for expectations the results are the same as in the case of stochastically self-similar sets, i.e., random recursive constructions (see
[
Za20
]
).

The Minkowski content may be viewed as the marginal case
k=d
of the fractal Lipschitz-Killing curvatures. It is the aim of the present paper to extend the above mentioned results regarding Minkowski contents of homogeneous random fractals
F
to all Lipschitz-Killing curvatures.

For a deterministic self-similar set
F
satisfying the open set condition and some additional assumptions, Lipschitz-Killing curvatures can be introduced by approximation with parallel sets. If
r>0
is a regular value of the distance function
d(\cdot,F)
of
F
, then the
r
-parallel set
F(r):=\{x:\,d(x,F)\leq r\}
has ‘nice’ geometric properties. (More precisely, its boundary is a Lipschitz manifold and the closure of its complement has positive reach.) In particular,
F(r)
admits Lipschitz-Killing curvature measures
C_{k}(F(r),\cdot)
,
k=0,\dots,d-1
. We use the notation
C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),\cdot)
for the variation measures, and
C_{k}(F(r)):=C_{k}(F(r),\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the total mass.
Provided that almost all
r>0
are regular values (which is always the case if
d\leq 3
, see
[
Fu85
]
),
the (total)
fractal Lipschitz-Killing curvatures
of
F
can be introduced as rescaled (essential or averaged) limits

\operatorname{ess\,lim}_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limits\varepsilon^{D-k}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))\qquad\text{ or }\qquad\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{|\ln\delta|}\int_{\delta}^{1}\varepsilon^{D-k}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))~\varepsilon^{-1}d\varepsilon,

where
D
is the Minkowski dimension of
F
.
It turned out that the first one (essential limit) exists in the case of non-lattice self-similar sets, while the second one (averaged limit) exists in general, see
[
Wi08
]
and
[
Za11
]
for details.

In order to give some idea of the results obtained in the paper, we give an informal description of the considered model, a detailed definition can be found in Section
3
. Consider a random iterated function system (random IFS)
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{N})
consisting of a random number
N\geq 2
of contracting similarities
f_{1},\dots,f_{N}
with (random) contraction ratios
r_{1},\dots,r_{N}
fulfilling
0<r_{\min}\leq r_{i}\leq r_{\max}<1
for some deterministic values
r_{\min},r_{\max}
. We assume the
Uniform Open Set Condition
(UOSC): there exists a (deterministic) nonempty open set
O
such that almost surely,
f_{i}(O)\subset O
and
f_{i}(O)\cap f_{j}(O)=\emptyset
,
i\neq j\leq N
(cf. (
3.3
)). The
homogeneous random fractal

F
is defined by means of an i.i.d. random sequence
(f^{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, where each random IFS
f^{n}=(f^{n}_{1},\dots,f^{n}_{N^{n}})
has the same distribution as
f
. Then
F
is obtained by applying at each construction step
n\in\mathbb{N}
the same chosen IFS
f^{n}
to all components of that step. More precisely, denoting
\Sigma_{n}:=\{\sigma_{1}\sigma_{2}\dots\sigma_{n}:\,1\leq\sigma_{i}\leq N^{i},1\leq i\leq n\}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
f_{\sigma}:=f^{1}_{\sigma_{1}}\circ\dots\circ f^{n}_{\sigma_{n}}
,
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
, the homogeneous random fractal associated to the random IFS
f
is the random set
F
defined by

F:=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}f_{\sigma}(\overline{O}).

If
\mathbb{E}N<\infty
(which we assume here throughout), then the equation
\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}=1
has a unique solution
D\in[0,d]
, called the
mean Minkowski dimension
of
F
. (Note that it is given by the same formula as in the random recursive case, cf.
[
Za20
]
and (
3.9
) below.) We consider the measure
\mu:=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}\{|\ln r_{i}|\in\cdot\}r_{i}^{D}
and its mean value
\eta:=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\ln r_{i}|\,r_{i}^{D}.

Further, we let
\Sigma_{*}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}
, and for any
r>0
, we denote by
\Sigma(r)
the set of all finite words
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\dots\sigma_{n}\in\Sigma_{*}
such that
r^{1}_{\sigma_{1}}\cdots r^{n}_{\sigma_{n}}\leq\frac{r}{2|O|}<r^{1}_{\sigma_{1}}\cdots r^{n-1}_{\sigma_{n-1}}
. Here
|O|
denotes the diameter of
O
. For
\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}
let
O_{\sigma}:=f_{\sigma}(O)
. Observe that
O_{\sigma}
is open, while its parallel sets
O_{\sigma}(r)
,
r\geq 0
, are closed.

Our main result Theorem
3.2
establishes the existence of fractal Lipschitz-Killing curvatures for a large class of homogeneous random fractals. The following statement is a special case, as can easily be seen from Proposition
5.2
(d). It provides this existence under a stronger but easier to state hypothesis.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a homogeneous random fractal with
\mathbb{E}N<\infty
satisfying UOSC with some open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
F\cap O\neq\emptyset
almost surely. Let
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
and assume further that

(i)

with probability one, almost all
r>0
are regular values of
d(\cdot,F)
;

(ii)

there exists a constant
c>0
such that with probability one,

C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\right)\leq cr^{k},\text{ for a.a. }r>0\text{ and all }\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)\text{ with }\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}.

Define
F_{i}:=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n},\sigma_{1}=i}f_{\sigma}(\overline{O})
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
and

R_{k,1}(r):=\mathbb{E}C_{k}(F(r))-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(0,r_{i}]}(r)\,C_{k}\big(F_{i}(r)\big),\quad r>0.

Then,

\overline{C}_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}:=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{|\ln\delta|}\int_{\delta}^{1}\varepsilon^{D-k}\mathbb{E}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))~\varepsilon^{-1}d\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{1}r^{D-k-1}R_{k,1}(r)\,dr.

Moreover, if the measure
\mu
is non-lattice, then

C_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}:=\operatorname{ess\,lim}_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limits\varepsilon^{D-k}\mathbb{E}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))=\overline{C}_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}.

As in the deterministic case, the values
\overline{C}_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}
(and
C_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}
, in case they exist) can be interpreted as the
mean (averaged) fractal Lipschitz-Killing curvatures
of
F
of order
k
. (Note that in contrast to common differential geometric terminology in our paper the term ‘mean’ curvatures is used in the probabilistic sense of expectations.) For the cases
k=d-1
and
k=d
, conditions (i) and (ii) are in fact not needed.

Our result covers the case of deterministic self-similar sets considered in
[
Wi08
]
and
[
Za11
]
. As a nontrivial example, we discuss below a family of random homogeneous fractals, for which the associated random IFS choose between two deterministic IFS generating Sierpiński type gaskets, see Section
5
. In this case, the values of
C_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}
are determined explicitly.

2.
Curvature measures of parallel sets

2.1.
Definitions and relevant properties

For certain classes of compact sets
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(including many classical singular sets) it turns out that for Lebesgue-almost all distances
r>0
the parallel set

(2.1)

K(r):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:d(x,K)=\min_{y\in K}|x-y|\leq r\}

possesses the property that the closure of its complement

(2.2)

\widetilde{K(r)}:=\overline{K(r)^{c}}

is a set with positive reach with Lipschitz boundary. (Recall that
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a set with positive reach, if for some
\delta>0
every point
x\in X(\delta)
has a unique point
\Pi_{X}(x)\in X
nearest to
x
.)
A sufficient condition is that
r
is a regular value of the Euclidean distance function to
K
(see Fu
[
Fu85
, Theorem 4.1]
together with
[
RZ03
, Proposition 3]
). In this case
both sets
\widetilde{K(r)}
and
K(r)
are
Lipschitz
d
-manifolds of bounded curvature
in the sense of
[
RZ05
]
, implying that their
k-th Lipschitz-Killing curvature measures
,
k=0,1,\ldots,d-1
, are determined in this general context and agree with the classical versions in the special cases. (See also Chapter 9 in
[
RZ19
]
for some background and extensions.)
Moreover, they satisfy

(2.3)

C_{k}(K(r),\cdot)=(-1)^{d-1-k}C_{k}\big(\widetilde{K(r)},\cdot\big)\,,

which implies that the
C_{k}(K(r),\cdot)
are signed measures with finite
variation measures

C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(K(r),\cdot)
and, moreover, that they have explicit integral representations which reduce to the ones in
[
Za86b
]
(cf.
[
RZ05
, Theorem 3]
for the general case).
The corresponding normal cycle representation will briefly be mentioned below. We list some of the main properties of curvature measures of parallel sets which will be used repeatedly:

2C_{d-1}(K(r),\cdot)
agrees with
(d-1)
-
dimensional Hausdorff measure

\mathcal{H}^{d-1}
on the boundary
\partial K(r)
. The latter is a finite measure for all
r>0
and all compact sets
K
. Therefore we use the notation

C_{d-1}(K(r),\cdot):=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial K(r)\cap(\cdot))

in the general case. For completeness we also define

C_{d}(K(r),\cdot)
to be the
Lebesgue measure restricted to

K(r)
. The
total curvatures
of
K(r)
are denoted by

(2.4)

C_{k}(K(r)):=C_{k}(K(r),\mathbb{R}^{d})\,,~~k=0,\ldots,d\,.

By an associated Gauss-Bonnet theorem (see
[
RZ03
, Theorems 2,3]
) the
Gauss curvature

C_{0}(K(r))
coincides with the
Euler-Poincaré characteristic

\chi(K(r))
, whenever the curvature measure
C_{0}(K(r),\cdot)
is defined as described above. For smooth boundaries
\partial K(r)
in the differential geometric setting,
C_{k}(K(r))
can be interpreted as the
total
k
-th order mean curvature
of
K(r)
, which is extrinsic if
d-1-k
is odd. For
k=d-2
it is also known as extrinsic
total mean curvature
of
K(r)
, and for
k=d-3
it coincides with the intrinsic
total scalar curvature
of
\partial K(r)
, up to certain constants.

The curvature measures are
motion invariant
, i.e.,

(2.5)

C_{k}(g(K(r)),g(\cdot))=C_{k}(K(r),\cdot)~~\mbox{for any Euclidean motion}~g\,,

they are
homogeneous of degree

k
, i.e.,

(2.6)

C_{k}\big((\lambda K)(\lambda r),\lambda(\cdot)\big)=C_{k}\big(\lambda K(r),\lambda(\cdot)\big)=\lambda^{k}\,C_{k}(K(r),\cdot)\,,~~\lambda>0\,,

and
locally determined
, i.e.,

(2.7)

C_{k}(K(r),(\cdot)\cap G)=C_{k}(K^{\prime}(r^{\prime}),(\cdot)\cap G)

for any open set
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
K(r)\cap G=K^{\prime}(r^{\prime})\cap G
, where
K(r)
and
K^{\prime}(r^{\prime})
are
both parallel sets such that the closures of their complements have positive reach.

2.2.
Unit normal cycles, measurability and continuity

We now summarize some facts about sets with positive reach needed in the sequel:

Recall that
\operatorname{reach}X
of a set
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is defined as the supremum over all
r\geq 0
such that for every point
x
in the
r
-parallel set of
X
there is a unique point
\Pi_{X}x\in X
nearest to
x
. The mapping
\Pi_{X}
(on its domain) is called the
metric projection
onto
X
. For a set
X
of positive reach the
unit normal bundle
is defined as

\operatorname{nor}X:=\{(x,n)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times S^{d-1}:x\in X,\,n\in\operatorname{Nor}(X,x)\}

where
\operatorname{Nor}(X,x)
is the dual cone to the (convex) tangent cone of
X
at
x
.

If additionally
\operatorname{nor}X\cap\rho(\operatorname{nor}X)=\emptyset
for the
normal reflection

\rho\>(x,n)\mapsto(x,-n)
, then
X
is a
d
-dimensional Lipschitz manifold with boundary (see
[
RZ03
, Proposition 3]
).

For general
X
with
\operatorname{reach}X>0
there is an associated rectifiable current called the
unit normal cycle
of
X
which is given by

N_{X}(\varphi):=\int_{\operatorname{nor}X}\langle a_{X}(x,n),\varphi(x,n)\rangle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(d(x,n))

for an appropriate unit simple
(d-1)
-vector field
a_{X}=a_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge a_{d-1}
associated a.e. with the tangent spaces of
\operatorname{nor}X
and for integrable differential
(d-1)
-forms
\varphi
. In these terms for
k\leq d-1
the curvature measure may be represented by

C_{k}(X,B)=N_{X}\llcorner\mathbf{1}_{B\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\varphi_{k})=\int_{\operatorname{nor}X\cap(B\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\langle a_{X}(x,n),\varphi_{k}(n)\rangle\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(d(x,n))

for any bounded Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where the
k
-th Lipschitz-Killing curvature form
\varphi_{k}
does not depend on the points
x
and is defined by its action on a simple
(d-1)
-vector
\eta=\eta_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\eta_{d-1}
as follows: Let
\pi_{0}(y,z):=y
and
\pi_{1}(y,z):=z
be the coordinate projections in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
e^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,e^{\prime}_{d}
be the dual basis of the standard basis in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathcal{O}_{k}
the surface area of the
k
-dimensional unit sphere. Then we have

\langle\eta,\varphi_{k}(n)\rangle:=\mathcal{O}_{d-k}^{-1}\sum_{\varepsilon_{i}\in\{0,1\},\,\sum\varepsilon_{i}=d-1-k}\langle\pi_{\varepsilon_{1}}\eta_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge\pi_{\varepsilon_{d-1}}\eta_{d-1}\wedge n,e^{\prime}_{1}\wedge\ldots\wedge e^{\prime}_{d}\rangle\,.

Below we will use the following nice behavior of parallel sets with sufficiently large distances. The diameter of a compact set
K
is denoted by
|K|
.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
Za11
, Theorem 4.1]

For any
R>\sqrt{2}
and
k=0,1,\ldots,d
there exists a constant
c_{k}(R)
such that for any compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and any
r\geq R|K|
,

\operatorname{reach}(\widetilde{K(r)})\geq|K|\sqrt{R^{2}-1}\,,

\partial K(r)
is a
(d-1)
-dimensional Lipschitz submanifold, and

\sup_{r\geq R|K|}\frac{C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(K(r),\mathbb{R}^{d})}{r^{k}}\leq c_{k}(R)\,.

(It is well-known that for compact convex sets
K
the last two properties hold for all
r>0
, the last one is the sharper version of an isodiametric inequality with an optimal constant.)

In the general case we define the set of
regular pairs
of compact sets and distances by

(2.8)

\operatorname{Reg}:=\bigg\{(r,K)\in[0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K}:\widetilde{K(r)}\in\mathcal{PR},\,\operatorname{nor}\widetilde{K(r)}\cap\rho(\operatorname{nor}\widetilde{K(r)})=\emptyset\bigg\}\,,

where
\mathcal{K}
denotes the family of all nonempty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\mathcal{PR}
stands for the
space of subsets of

\mathbb{R}^{d}

with positive reach
and
\rho
is the
normal reflection
given by
(x,n)\mapsto(x,-n)
.

Remark 2.2
.

An equivalent representation of regular pairs at positive distances is

(2.9)

\operatorname{Reg}\cap((0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K})=\left\{(r,K)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{K}:\,r\text{ is a regular value of }d_{K}\right\},

where
d_{K}:x\mapsto d(x,K)
is the distance function of
K
, see Appendix.

In Fu
[
Fu85
, Theorem 4.1]
it is shown that in space dimensions
d\leq 3
for any compact set
K
there exists a bounded exceptional set
E
of Lebesgue measure
0
such that for any
r\notin E
the set
\widetilde{K(r)}
has positive reach and
\operatorname{nor}\widetilde{K(r)}\cap\rho(\operatorname{nor}\widetilde{K(r)})=\emptyset
. Moreover, if
r>\sqrt{d/(2d+2)}|K|
, these two properties hold for
any
space dimension
d
.
It follows that in space dimensions
d\leq 3
for any compact
K
the pair
(r,K)
is regular for Lebesgue-a.a.
r
. In higher dimensions we will formulate this as a regularity condition on the random fractal sets.

For probabilistic purposes we need the following measurability properties.
Let
\mathcal{F}
denote the space of all closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
provided with the Vietoris topology (generated by the sets
\{A\in\mathfrak{F}:A\cap O\neq\emptyset\}
and
\{A\in\mathfrak{F}:A\cap C=\emptyset\}
for open
O
and closed

\sigma

\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{F})
.
On its subspace
\mathcal{K}
(of

d_{H}(K,L):=\max\left\{\max_{x\in K}d(x,L),\max_{y\in L}d(y,K)\right\},\quad K,L\in\mathcal{K},

and
\mathfrak{B}(\mathcal{K})
denotes the Borel
\sigma

\mathcal{K}

Lemma 2.3
.

The following assertions hold.

(i)

\mathcal{PR}\in\mathfrak{B}(\mathfrak{F})
.

(ii)

The mapping

from

(iii)

For any bounded Borel set

and

-mea- surable.

(iv)

Proof.

(i) See Proposition 1.1.1 in
[

.

(ii) It is easy to see that for the centered balls

(iii) See Theorem 2.1.2 (i) and Theorem 6.2.2 in

(iv) Due to (iii), the mappings

for any probability measure

.

(v) This follows from (i), (ii), and the fact that the mapping

from

into the space of closed subsets of

is measurable.

In order to apply the classical Renewal theorem to the curvatures of random fractals we additionally need the following continuity property.
Recall that the set of regular values of the distance function
d_{K}
is open. (This can e.g. be seen from (
6.1
) in the Appendix.) Therefore, there exists for each regular value
r_{0}
an
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
r\in(r_{0}-\varepsilon,r_{0}+\varepsilon)
,
r
is a regular value of
d_{K}
, too.

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
(r_{0},K)\in\operatorname{Reg}
with
r_{0}>0
and
any
k\in\{0,\ldots,d\}
, the measures
C_{k}(K(r),\cdot)
converge weakly to
C_{k}(K(r_{0}),\cdot)
as
r\to r_{0}
.

A detailed proof will be given in the Appendix, see Theorem
6.1
.

Remark 2.5
.

If
k=d
or
k=d-1
, then the weak convergence in Lemma
2.4
remains valid for general compact sets
K
and almost all
r_{0}>0
, i.e., we need not restrict
K
to the class
\mathcal{PR}
or
r_{0}
to regular values of
K
. To see this, note that
r\mapsto V_{d}(K(r))=\mathcal{L}^{d}(K(r))
is a
Kneser function
(see
[
St76
, Lemma 5]
) and that
V_{d-1}(K(r))=\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial K(r))=\frac{d}{dr}\mathcal{L}^{d}(K(r))
for all
r>0
up to a countable set (see
[
RW10
, Corollary 2.5]
).
The continuity of
V_{d}(r)
at all
r>0
and of
V_{d-1}(K(r))
at all
r>0
up to a countable set follows then from the properties of Kneser functions (see
[
St76
, Lemma 2]
).

Furthermore, since both mappings
K\mapsto\mathcal{L}^{d}(K)
and
K\mapsto\mathcal{H}^{d-1}(\partial K)
are measurable on
\mathcal{K}
(cf.
[
Za82
]
), Lemma
2.3
(iii) and (iv) are valid for
k=d
and
k=d-1
with
\mathcal{PR}
replaced by the larger space
\mathcal{K}
.

3.
Construction of homogeneous random fractals and statement of the results

For fixed
0<r_{\min}<r_{\max}<1
let
\operatorname{Sim}
be the set of all contractive similarities
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
with contraction ratios
r
such that
r_{\min}\leq r\leq r_{\max}
. We equip
\operatorname{Sim}
with the topology given by uniform convergence on compact sets.
\mathcal{B}
denotes the associated Borel
\sigma
-algebra. The space
\Omega_{0}:=\bigcup_{k=2}^{\infty}\operatorname{Sim}^{k}
together with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\big\{A\subset\Omega_{0}:A\cap\operatorname{Sim}^{k}\in\otimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{B}~\mbox{for all}~k\geq 2\big\}
, and with a distribution
\mathbb{P}_{0}
on it provide the primary probability space
[\Omega_{0},\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P}_{0}]
. This space is used to generate a random
iterated function system
(IFS)
(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})
, with a random number
N
of mappings
f_{1},\ldots,f_{N}
chosen randomly from
\operatorname{Sim}
. Note that, by construction,
N\geq 2
.

For the definition of the homogeneous model, we need a sequence of independent and equally distributed random IFS. Therefore, the basic probability space for the model is the product space

(3.1)

[\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P}]:=\bigotimes_{n=1}^{\infty}~[\Omega_{0},\mathcal{F}_{0},\mathbb{P}_{0}]

and the expectation symbol
\mathbb{E}
will be used for integration with respect to
\mathbb{P}
.

The elements of
\Omega
are denoted by

\omega=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\ldots):=((f_{1}^{1},\ldots,f_{N^{1}}^{1}),(f_{1}^{2},\ldots,f_{N^{2}}^{2}),\ldots)\,,

and
r_{i}^{n}
are the contraction ratios of the similarities
f_{i}^{n}
. For
f_{i}^{1}
,
r_{i}^{1}
and
N^{1}
we will often write
f_{i}
,
r_{i}
and
N
, resp. Below we will use the measurable mapping

\theta:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega,\quad\theta(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\ldots):=(\omega_{2},\omega_{3},\ldots)\,,

and for a random element
X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
we define the shifted random element
X^{(n)}
by

(3.2)

\displaystyle X^{(n)}(\omega):=X(\theta^{n}\omega),\quad\omega\in\Omega\,,

where
\theta^{n}
is the
n
-fold application of the shift
\theta
.
(If it is clear from the context, the argument
\omega
will be omitted.)

Note that, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
(f_{1}^{n},\ldots,f_{N^{n}}^{n})
is a random IFS of random length
N^{n}
with distribution
\mathbb{P}_{0}
representing the
n
-th construction step. For different
n
they are independent of each other.

In the sequel we assume that the
Uniform Open Set Condition
(UOSC) is satisfied: there exists a nonempty bounded open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that
\mathbb{P}_{0}
-a.s.

(3.3)

\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(O)\subset O\quad\mbox{and}\quad f_{i}(O)\cap f_{j}(O)=\emptyset~,i\neq j\,.

Then with
\mathbb{P}
-probability 1 all IFS in the product space fulfill this UOSC.

The corresponding random fractal set is introduced by means of a random
coding tree
:

\Sigma_{n}=\Sigma_{n}(\omega):=\{\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n}:1\leq\sigma_{i}\leq N^{i},\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
is the set of all
nodes
at level
n
and
\Sigma_{*}:=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}
is the set of all nodes of the tree, where
\Sigma_{0}
denotes the empty code at level
0
.

Recall from (
3.2
) that
\Sigma_{l}^{(k)}
is defined by
\Sigma_{l}^{(k)}(\omega)=\Sigma_{l}(\theta^{k}\omega)
,
\omega\in\Omega
. For
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{k}\in\Sigma_{k}
and
\tau=\tau_{1}\ldots\tau_{l}\in\Sigma_{l}^{(k)}
we write
\sigma\tau:=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{k}\tau_{1}\ldots\tau_{l}\in\Sigma_{k+l}
for the
concatenation
of these codes.
If
\sigma=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{n}\in\Sigma_{n}
and
0\leq k\leq n
, then
\sigma|k:=\sigma_{1}\ldots\sigma_{k}
denotes the
restriction
to the first
k
entries of
\sigma
, and
|\sigma|:=n
is the
length
of
\sigma
. For any fixed
n=1,2,\ldots
, we associate to each
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
the same random IFS
\left(f_{1}^{n+1},\ldots,f_{N^{n+1}}^{n+1}\right)
. This leads to the homogeneous structure. (In the
V
-variable case these random IFS are chosen by means of
V
different types. Here we have
V=1
, and in the case of random recursive constructions, where
V=\infty
, for different
\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
the IFS are i.i.d.) Furthermore, we define the random mappings

f_{\sigma}:=f_{\sigma_{1}}^{1}\circ f_{\sigma_{2}}^{2}\circ\dots\circ f_{\sigma_{n}}^{n}

with contraction ratios
r_{\sigma}:=r_{\sigma_{1}}^{1}r_{\sigma_{2}}^{2}\ldots r_{\sigma_{n}}^{n}
.
Then the random compact set

(3.4)

F=F(\omega):=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}f_{\sigma}(\overline{O})

is
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. determined and measurable with respect to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{K})
, the Borel
\sigma
-algebra determined by the Hausdorff distance
d_{H}
on the space
\mathcal{K}
of nonempty compact subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It is called the associated
homogeneous random fractal
.
F
is
stochastically self-similar
in the following sense (recall from (
3.2
) that
F^{(n)}(\omega)=F(\theta^{n}\omega)
):

F=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(F^{(1)})~~,~~\mathbb{P}\,\text{- a.s.}~.

More generally, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

(3.5)

F=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}f_{\sigma}(F^{(n)})~~,~~\mathbb{P}\,\text{- a.s.}~,

where the random compact set
F^{(n)}
is independent of the random mappings

\{f_{\sigma},\,\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}
} and has the same distribution as
F
. We will also use the abbreviation

F_{\sigma}:=f_{\sigma}(F^{(|\sigma|)})~~{\rm for}~~~~\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}\,.

For a boundedness condition in the application of the Renewal theorem we will further use a formula similar to (
3.5
) with respect to some Markov stop: Fix an arbitrary constant
R>\sqrt{2}|O|
and define for all
0<r<R
a random subset of codes by

(3.6)

\Sigma(r):=\{\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}:R\,r_{\sigma}\leq r<R\,r_{\sigma||\sigma|-1}\}\,,

where, by convention,
r_{\sigma||\sigma|-1}=1
, if
|\sigma|=1
. It is convenient to set
\Sigma(r):=\Sigma_{0}
for
r\geq R
. Then we have

(3.7)

F=\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma(r)}F_{\sigma}~~,~~\mathbb{P}\,\text{- a.s.}~.

In order to formulate the main results we also need the following random sets of
boundary codes
, i.e., codes
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
for which the parallel set
F_{\sigma}(r)
has distance less than
r
to the
boundary
of the first iterate
f(O):=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N}f_{i}(O)
of the basic open set
O
under the random similarities:

(3.8)

\Sigma_{b}(r):=\big\{\sigma\in\Sigma(r):F_{\sigma}(r)\cap(f(O)^{c})(r)\neq\emptyset\big\}\,,

Our considerations below do not depend on the choice of the constant
R
which is related to Lemma
2.1
, (where the above
R
corresponds to
R|O|
).

In the sequel many relationships between random elements are fulfilled only with probability
1
. We will not mention this, if it can be seen from the context.
Furthermore, the different meanings of
F=F(\omega)
as random set and
F(r)=F(\omega,r)
as parallel set of the random set
F
will also be clear from the context.

The measurability properties of the random elements used in the sequel follow from their definitions together with Lemma
2.3
and Remark
2.5
.

Recall now that
2\leq N
and assume that
\mathbb{E}N<\infty
. Let
D
be the number determined by

(3.9)

\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}=1\,.

Note that UOSC implies
D\leq d
.

(3.10)

\mu(\cdot):=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(\cdot)}(|\ln r_{i}|)\,r_{i}^{D}

is an associated probability distribution for the logarithmic contraction ratios
r_{i}
of the primary random IFS. The corresponding mean value is denoted by

(3.11)

\eta:=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}|\ln r_{i}|\,r_{i}^{D}.

For our main result we need a slightly stronger condition than UOSC (
3.3
), namely the

Uniform Strong Open Set Condition
(USOSC). It is satisfied, if

(3.12)

\mbox{UOSC holds for some $O$ such that}~~\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)>0\,.

Remark 3.1
.

In the literature instead of
\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)>0
the condition

\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)=1

has been used. By the following arguments one can see that these conditions are equivalent:
If
\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)>0
, there must be some
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that the set

S_{0}:=\{\omega:\exists~\sigma\in\Sigma_{n_{0}}~~\mbox{with}~~f_{\sigma}(\overline{O})\subset O\}

has positive probability. (Otherwise
F
would concentrate on the boundary of
O
.) Then the sets
S_{k}:=\theta^{kn_{0}}(S_{0})
,
k=0,1,2,\ldots
, are independent and have all the same probability. Hence the Borel-Cantelli lemma implies that
\mathbb{P}\big(\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\bigcup_{k\geq n}S_{k}\big)=1
, in particular,
\mathbb{P}(\bigcup_{k=0}^{\infty}S_{k})=1
. This and UOSC lead to
\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)=1
.

By Lemma
2.1
the boundary
\partial F(r)
of the parallel set
F(r)=F(\omega,r)
is a
(d-1)
-Lipschitz manifold and
\widetilde{F(r)}
has positive reach for all
r\geq R
, where
R
is some constant such that
R>\sqrt{2}|O|\geq\sqrt{2}|F|
. For
r<R
, we will use the following regularity condition. (Recall from (
2.8
) the definition of the set
\operatorname{Reg}
of regular pairs.)
The random set
F
is called
regular
if the measure of irregular pairs vanishes, i.e., if

(3.13)

\mathbb{P}\times\mathcal{L}\big(\{(\omega,r)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty):(r,F)\notin\operatorname{Reg}\}\big)=0\,.

In this case we also consider the set

\operatorname{Reg}_{*}:=\{(\omega,r):(r,F_{\sigma})\in\operatorname{Reg}~~\mbox{for all}~\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}\}\,

(Recall that
\Sigma_{*}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Sigma_{n}
.)
and note that

(3.14)

\int\int\mathbf{1}_{(\operatorname{Reg}_{*})^{c}}(\omega,r)\mathcal{L}(dr)\mathbb{P}(d\omega)=0\,.

(
To see this, let
\mathcal{N}(\omega):=\{r>0:(r,F)\notin\operatorname{Reg}\}

and observe that

\displaystyle\int\int\mathbf{1}_{(\operatorname{Reg}_{*})^{c}}(\omega,r)\mathcal{L}(dr)\mathbb{P}(d\omega)=\int\mathcal{L}\big(\bigcup_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}}r_{\sigma}(\omega)\mathcal{N}(\theta^{|\sigma|}(\omega))\big)\mathbb{P}(d\omega)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}\mathcal{L}\big(r_{\sigma}(\omega)\mathcal{N}(\theta^{n}(\omega)\big)\mathbb{P}(d\omega)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}r_{\sigma}(\omega)\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}(\theta^{n}(\omega))\mathbb{P}(d\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{n}}r_{\sigma}(\omega)\int\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{N}(\omega^{\prime}))\mathbb{P}(d\omega^{\prime})\mathbb{P}(d\omega)=0\,,

since under the regularity condition (
3.13
) the inner integral vanishes. Here we have used that the random sets
\mathcal{N}(\theta^{n}(\omega))
are independent of the events up to the step
n
and have the same distribution as
\mathcal{N}(\omega)
.
)

Now we can formulate our main result. In the sequel, the occurring essential limits and suprema are always meant with respect to
Lebesgue-a.a. arguments.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
and let
F
be a homogeneous random fractal satisfying the Uniform Strong Open Set Condition (
3.12
) with basic set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\mathbb{E}N<\infty
. Let
R>\sqrt{2}|O|
. For
k\leq d-2
we additionally suppose the following:

(i)

if
d\geq 4
, then
F
is regular in the sense of (
3.13
),

(ii)

for all
r_{0}\in(0,R)
,

\mathbb{E}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r_{0}\leq r\leq R}\limits\,\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{b}(r)}C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(F(r),\partial(F_{\sigma}(r))\cap\partial\big(\bigcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r),~\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma}F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\big)\bigg)<\infty\,,

(iii)

there is a constant
C>0
such that with probability 1,

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{b}(r)}\,r^{-k}\,C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(F(r),\partial(F_{\sigma}(r))\cap\partial\big(\bigcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r),~\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma}F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\big)\bigg)\bigg|\sharp(\Sigma_{b}(r))\bigg]\leq C

for Lebesgue almost all
r\in(0,R]
.

Let
L>0
and set for almost all
r>0
,

R_{k,L}(r):=\mathbb{E}C_{k}(F(r))-\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(0,Lr_{i}]}(r)\,C_{k}\big(F_{i}(r)\big)\,.

Then the following assertions hold:

(I)

If the measure
\mu
is non-lattice, then

C_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}:=\operatorname{ess\,lim}_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limits\varepsilon^{D-k}\mathbb{E}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{L}r^{D-k-1}R_{k,L}(r)\,dr\,.

(II)

If the measure
\mu
is lattice with constant
c
, then for almost all
s\in[0,c)

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}e^{(k-D)(s+nc)}\mathbb{E}\,C_{k}\big(F(e^{-(s+nc)})\big)=\frac{1}{\eta}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}e^{(k-D)(s+mc)}R_{k,L}\big(e^{-(s+mc)}\big).

(III)

In general,

\overline{C}_{k,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}:=\lim_{\delta\rightarrow 0}\frac{1}{|\ln\delta|}\int_{\delta}^{1}\varepsilon^{D-k}\mathbb{E}\,C_{k}(F(\varepsilon))~\varepsilon^{-1}d\varepsilon=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{L}r^{D-k-1}R_{k,L}(r)\,dr.

Remark 3.3
.

In conditions (ii) and (iii) the boundary signs
\partial
can be omitted, since
\operatorname{int}F_{\sigma}(r)\subset\operatorname{int}F(r)
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}
and the curvature measures are concentrated on the boundary of the set
F(r)
. Similarly as in the deterministic case (see
[
Wi11
, Example 4.10]
) one can construct an example of a homogeneous random fractal where these conditions are not satisfied.

Remark 3.4
.

It is a consequence of the statement that the limit expressions in (I), (II) and (III) do not depend on the choice of the constant
L
, which gives some flexibility in applications, see Section
5
. This independence can also be seen directly using the self-similarity of
F
, cf. Lemma
4.5
. The proof of Theorem
3.2
will be given for the choice
L=R
, where
R
is the constant appearing in the conditions (ii) and (iii). It is easily seen that, if these two conditions are satisfied with some
R>\sqrt{2}|O|
, then they are also satisfied with any other constant
\tilde{R}>\sqrt{2}|O|
instead of
R
. (Indeed, this is obvious for
\tilde{R}<R
, since in this case the suprema in (ii) are taken over a smaller range of values and also the expectation in (iii) needs to be bounded for a smaller range of values
r
only. For
\tilde{R}>R
, this follows from Lemma
2.1
, which implies that, for almost all
R<r\leq\tilde{R}
, almost surely

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(F(r),\partial(F_{\sigma}(r))\cap\partial\big(\bigcup_{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r),~\sigma^{\prime}\neq\sigma}F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\big)\bigg)\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\big(F(r)\big)\leq c_{k}(R)\tilde{R}^{k}.

Thus, if conditions (ii) and (iii) hold with
R
, they also hold with
\tilde{R}>R
, the latter possibly with a larger constant
C
.)
This shows that a proof of Theorem
3.2
for some
L>\sqrt{2}|O|
(e.g. for
L=R
) implies indeed the validity of the stated formulas for any
L>\sqrt{2}|O|
.
The argument in Lemma
4.5
below shows that the latter restriction can be relaxed to
L>0
.

Remark 3.5
.

The special case of deterministic self-similar sets satisfying OSC is included in Theorem
3.2
. In this case, statement and formulas reduce to the ones obtained in
[
Wi08
,
Za11
]
(and in
[
Ga00
]
for the case
k=d
). The formulas in
[
Wi08
, Thm. 2.3.6]
are stated with
L=1
and the ones in
[
Za11
, Thm. 2.3.8]
correspond to the choice
L=R
, but Remark
3.4
applies similarly in these situations.

Note also that the formulas for the mean fractal curvatures are structurally equal to the ones obtained for the fractal curvatures (almost surely and in the mean) for self-similar random sets in
[
Ga00
,
Za11
]
. Indeed, if
F
is a homogeneous random fractal as in Theorem
3.2
generated by some random IFS, and
K
denotes the random self-similar set generated by the same random IFS, then the formulas for their mean fractal curvatures coincide, except that in the integrand
R_{k,L}
the set
F
has to be replaced by
K
. It turns out that in certain situations, both functions coincide, see Example
5.3
.
In general, this is probably not true.

4.
Proofs

The proof of Theorem
3.2
is split into several steps. We start with the main part in which the problem is reduced to an application of the classical Renewal theorem. In order to verify the assumptions of this theorem, we will show that the function arising in the renewal equation is Lebesgue-a.e. continuous and bounded by a directly Riemann integrable function. For the first property condition (ii) is used and for the second one condition (iii). The required estimates are shown in a sequence of lemmas. Part of the estimates can be reduced to Lemma
2.1
(see Lemma
4.2
below). The others follow from Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
, where the latter is essential. Finally, in Lemma
4.5
it is shown that the limit formula in the assertion of the theorem is the same for all
L>0
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
for
L=R
and up to the estimates (
4.1
) and (
4.4
).

For

L>0
the function
\xi_{k}^{L}
is
(\mathbb{P}\times\mathcal{L})
-almost everywhere defined by

\xi_{k}^{L}(r)=\xi_{k}^{L}(\omega,r):=\mathbf{1}_{(0,L]}(r)C_{k}(F(r))-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(0,Lr_{i}]}(r)C_{k}(F_{i}(r)),\quad(\omega,r)\in\operatorname{Reg}_{*}.

Below we will see that the expectations of the absolute values of the two summands on the right hand side are finite. Then in view of (
3.14
) the function

R_{k,L}=\mathbb{E}\xi_{k}^{L}\,

in Theorem
3.2
is determined at a.a. arguments. If
L=R
, where
R
is as in Theorem
3.2
, we will omit the subscript
R
and write
\xi_{k}(r):=\xi_{k}^{R}(r)
and

R_{k}(r)=\mathbb{E}\xi_{k}(r)\,.

By the motion invariance and scaling property of
C_{k}
, we get for
(\omega,r)
as above,

\xi_{k}(r)=\mathbf{1}_{(0,R]}(r)C_{k}(F(r))-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(0,R]}(r/r_{i})r_{i}^{k}C_{k}(F^{(1)}(r/r_{i}))\,.

In order to translate the problem into the language of the Renewal theorem, we substitute
r=Re^{-t}
and define

\displaystyle Z_{k}(t)=Z_{k}(\omega,t)

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)e^{(k-D)t}C_{k}(F(Re^{-t})),

\displaystyle z_{k}(t)=z_{k}(\omega,t)

\displaystyle:=e^{(k-D)t}\xi_{k}(Re^{-t}),

whenever
(\omega,Re^{-t})\in\operatorname{Reg}_{*}
.
Note that
z_{k}(\omega,t)=0
for
t<0
. We infer from the above relations that for such
(\omega,t)
,

Z_{k}(\omega,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}Z_{k}(\theta(\omega),t-|\ln r_{i}|)+z_{k}(\omega,t),

where
(\theta(\omega),t-|\ln r_{i}(\omega)|)\in\operatorname{Reg}_{*}
for
i=1,\ldots,N(\omega)
.

Denote

T:=\left\{t>0:\left(\omega,Re^{-t}\right)\in\operatorname{Reg}_{*}~{\rm for}~\mathbb{P}\text{- a.a.}~\omega\right\}.

Let
\mu^{\ast n}
be the
n
th convolution power of the distribution
\mu=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(\cdot)}(|\ln r_{i}|)r_{i}^{D}
, if
n\geq 1
,
\mu^{\ast 0}
the Dirac measure at
0
, and

U(t):=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\mu^{\ast n}((0,t]),~t>0.

Note that the summands on the right vanish for
n>t/|\ln r_{\max}|
, so that the summation is finite for each
t>0
. (In the sequel
U
-a.a. means a.a. with respect to the corresponding measure.) Below we will show the following.

Lemma 4.1
.

t\in T
implies
t-s\in T
for
\mu
-a.a.
s\leq t
and for
U
-a.a.
s\leq t
.

Then we infer for
t\in T
from the above equality for
Z_{k}(\omega,t)
that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Z_{k}(t)|\leq

\displaystyle\int\sum_{i=1}^{N(\omega)}r_{i}(\omega)^{D}\left|Z_{k}\left(\theta(\omega),t-|\ln r_{i}(\omega)|\right)\right|\mathbb{P}(d\omega)+\mathbb{E}|z_{k}(t)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathbb{E}|Z_{k}(t-s)|\mu(ds)+\mathbb{E}|z_{k}(t)|\,.

In view of Lemma
4.1

we obtain from iterated application of this inequality that

\mathbb{E}|Z_{k}(t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|z_{k}(t-s)|dU(s),\quad t\in T.

Below we will show that there exist some constants
c_{k}>0
and
\delta>0
such that for all
u\in T
we have

(4.1)

\mathbb{E}|z_{k}(u)|\leq c_{k}\mathbf{1}_{[0,\infty)}(u)e^{-\delta u}.

(In the proof for
k\leq d-2
we will use condition (iii) of Theorem
3.2
.) Furthermore, since in our case
U(t)\leq t/|\ln r_{\max}|
for all
t>0
, we infer from (
4.1
) and the definition of
Z_{k}
that for some constants
d_{k}
and
d^{\prime}_{k}
,

\mathbb{E}|Z_{k}(t)|\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|z_{k}(t-s)|dU(s)<d_{k}\,,~t\in T,

and consequently,

(4.2)

\mathbb{E}|C_{k}(F(r))|\leq d^{\prime}_{k}\,r^{k-D}|\ln(r/R)|~,~~\mbox{for a.a.}~0<r\leq R.

This shows in particular the finiteness of the expectations mentioned at the beginning of the proof.

Moreover, we can repeat to above arguments omitting the absolute value signs and replacing the corresponding inequalities by equalities in order to obtain the renewal equation in the sense of Feller
[
Fe71
]
. We get for all
t\in T

\mathbb{E}Z_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}Z_{k}(t-s)\mu(ds)+\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t)

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}Z_{k}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t-s)\,dU(s).

Below we will also show that for all
0<r_{0}\leq R
,

(4.4)

\mathbb{E}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r>r_{0}}\limits|\xi_{k}(r)|<\infty,

where
\operatorname{ess\,sup}
means the supremum over all arguments where the function is determined.
(In the proof of (
4.4
) for
k\leq d-2
we will use condition (ii) of the theorem.)

We now define two auxiliary functions on
(0,\infty)
by

\displaystyle\overline{z}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t),&{\rm if}~t\in T,\\
\limsup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}t^{\prime}\to t\\
t^{\prime}\in T\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t^{\prime}),&{\rm if}~t\in(0,\infty)\setminus T,\end{cases}

\displaystyle\overline{Z}_{k}(t)

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\overline{z}_{k}(t-s)dU(s),~t>0.

Then in view of Lemma
4.1
and (
4.3
),

(4.5)

\displaystyle\overline{Z}_{k}(t)=\mathbb{E}Z_{k}(t)\quad\text{ for }t\in T.

Assumption (i) and Lemma
2.4
imply that the random function
\xi_{k}(\omega,\cdot)
is continuous in the second argument at all arguments
r
, where
(\omega,r)\in\operatorname{Reg}_{*}
. (For
k=d,d-1
we do not need (i) for this conclusion.)
The dominated convergence theorem (justified by (
4.4
)) yields that at each
t\in T
the function
\overline{z}_{k}
, which agrees with
e^{(k-D)t}\mathbb{E}\xi_{k}(Re^{-t})
at such
t
, is continuous, i.e.,
\overline{z}_{k}
is continuous Lebesgue-a.e.. Moreover, in view of (
4.1
),
\overline{z}_{k}
is bounded by a directly Riemann integrable function. Thus, according to Asmussen
[
As87
, Prop. 4.1, p. 118]
,
\overline{z}_{k}
is directly Riemann integrable, too. Therefore the classical Renewal theorem in Feller
[
Fe71
, p. 363]
can be applied, which yields that

\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\overline{Z}_{k}(t)=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\overline{z}_{k}(t)\,dt.

The right hand side agrees with

\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t)\,dt=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{(k-D)t}R_{k}(Re^{-t})\,dt,

since
\overline{z}_{k}(t)=\mathbb{E}z_{k}(t)
for Lebesgue-a.a.
t
.
Multiplying
R^{D-k}
in this equation and substituting
r=Re^{-t}
under the integral, assertion (I) follows in view of
(
4.5
).

Since
\mathbb{E}Z_{k}
is bounded on finite intervals, in the non-lattice case the corresponding average limit in (III) is a consequence.

In the lattice case, the Renewal theorem provides the limit along arithmetic progressions with respect to the lattice constant, here only for those sequences along which the function is determined. This shows assertion (II). This also implies the average convergence (III). For more details we refer to the arguments of Gatzouras at the end of the proof of
[
Ga00
, Theorem 2.3]
in the classical case.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Lemma 4.3

There exist constants

\mathbb{E}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r_{0}<r\leq R}\limits\xi_{k1}(r)<\infty,\quad 0<r_{0}<R.

Proof.

For
k\in\{d-1,d\}
the estimate (
4.8
) is a simple consequence of the facts that for any compact
K
and

C_{d}(K(r))\leq\operatorname{const}(2r+|K|)^{d}
and

. The latter follows from the Kneser property of the volume function, i.e.,
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\mathcal{L}^{d}(K(r))\leq\frac{d}{r}\mathcal{L}^{d}(K(r))

(see e.g.
[

[

For

A_{r}:=\bigcup_{j\neq k}f_{j}(\overline{O})(r)\cap f_{k}(\overline{O})(r)\,.

Similarly,

The local definiteness of the curvature measure

,

Substituting this in the above expression for

\xi_{k1}(r)=\big|C_{k}(F(r),A_{r})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}C_{k}(F_{i}(r),A_{r})\big|

,

\displaystyle\xi_{k1}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg|C_{k}(F(r),A_{r})-\sum_{i=1}^{N}r^{k}_{i}\,C_{k}\big(F^{(1)}\big(\frac{r}{r_{i}}\big),f_{i}^{-1}(A_{r})\big)\bigg|

\displaystyle\big|\xi_{k3}(r)-\xi_{k4}(r)-\xi_{k5}(r)\big|\leq\left|\xi_{k3}(r)\right|+\left|\xi_{k4}(r)\right|+\left|\xi_{k5}(r)\right|

\displaystyle\xi_{k3}(r)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C_{k}(F(r),A_{r}),

\displaystyle\xi_{k4}(r)

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{k}\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,R]}\big(\frac{r}{r_{i}}\big)\,C_{k}\bigg(F^{(1)}\big(\frac{r}{r_{i}}\big),f_{i}^{-1}(A_{r})\bigg),

) for

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r))

\displaystyle r^{D-\delta-k}\mathbb{E}C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\big(F(r),f(O)^{c}(r)\big)

C
. Therefore, it remains to show that for some
0<\delta<D
,

(4.15)

\sup_{0<r\leq R}\limits\mathbb{E}\big[r^{D-\delta}\sharp(\Sigma_{b}(r))\big]<\infty\,.

This was already proved in
[
Za20
]
. For convenience of the reader we replicate the arguments here.
To this aim we will use USOSC, i.e., UOSC with
O
such that
\mathbb{P}(F\cap O\neq\emptyset)>0
, which implies that there exist some constants
\alpha>0
and
0<\rho<1
such that

(4.16)

\mathbb{P}(\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)\neq\emptyset)>0~~{\rm for}~~\Sigma(\rho,\alpha):=\{\tau\in\Sigma(\rho):d(x,\partial O)>\alpha\,,\,x\in f_{\tau}(\overline{O})\}\,.

Since
\Sigma(\rho)
is a Markov stop, one infers from
\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}=1
that

\mathbb{E}\sum_{\sigma\in\Sigma(\rho)}r_{\sigma}^{D}=1

(see e.g.
[
Za20
, Proposition 1]
).
Then let
\delta
be determined by

(4.17)

\mathbb{E}\bigg(\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}\bigg)=1\,.

We next choose for all
r>0
,

(4.18)

r^{*}:=2R(\alpha r_{\min})^{-1}r\,.

Then we get for
i=1,\ldots,N
and
i\sigma\in\Sigma(r^{*})
with
\sigma=\tau\sigma^{\prime}
for some
\tau\in\Sigma^{(1)}(\rho,\alpha)
that

f_{i\sigma}(F^{(|i\sigma|)})(r)\cap f(O)^{c}(r)=\emptyset\,.

(To see this note that for any
x\in f_{i\sigma}(F^{|i\sigma|})(r)
there exists a
y\in f_{i\sigma}(F^{(|i\sigma|)})
such that
|x-y|\leq r
. Furthermore,
y\in f_{i\sigma}(F^{(|i\sigma|)})\subset f_{i}(F^{(1)})\subset f_{i}(\overline{O})\subset f(\overline{O})
, and
d(y,\partial f(O))\geq d(y,\partial f_{i\sigma}(O))>r_{i\sigma}\alpha>R^{-1}r^{*}r_{\min}\alpha=2r
. Consequently,
d(x,\partial f(O)^{c})\geq d(y,\partial f(O)^{c})-|x-y|>2r-r=r
, i.e.,
x\notin(f(O)^{c})(r)
.)

From this we obtain

\sharp(\Sigma_{b}(r))\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sharp(\{w\in\Sigma(r^{*}):w=i\sigma,\,\sigma\in\Xi^{(1)}(r^{*}/r_{i})\})\,,

where the random sets
\Xi(r)
,
r>0
, are defined as

\Xi(r):=\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\in\Sigma(r):\sigma=\tau\sigma^{\prime}~~\mbox{for some}~~\tau\in\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)\}\,.

In these notations we get

\displaystyle r^{D-\delta}\mathbb{E}\sharp(\Sigma_{b}(r))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle r^{D-\delta}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sharp(\Xi^{(1)}(r^{*}/r_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha r_{\min}/2)^{D-d}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D-\delta}(r^{*}/r_{i})^{D-\delta}\sharp(\Xi^{(1)}(r^{*}/r_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha r_{\min}/2)^{D-d}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D-\delta}(r^{*}/r_{i})^{D-\delta}\mathbb{E}\sharp(\Xi^{(1)}(r^{*}/r_{i}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\operatorname{const}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D-\delta}\psi(r^{*}/r_{i})\,,

where we have used that
\Xi^{(1)}
is independent of the events in the first step and has the same distribution as
\Xi
and then the notation
\psi(r):=r^{D-\delta}\mathbb{E}\sharp(\Xi(r))
. Now it suffices to show that the function
\psi
is bounded.

Similarly as above, using (
4.16
) and the definition of
\Xi(r)
we infer for sufficiently large
M
and
r<\rho
,

\displaystyle\psi(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}(r/r_{\tau})^{D-\delta}\sharp(\Xi^{(|\tau|)}(r/r_{\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}}{{|\tau|=n}}}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}(r/r_{\tau})^{D-\delta}\sharp(\Xi^{(n)}(r/r_{\tau})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{M}\mathbb{E}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}}{{|\tau|=n}}}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}\psi(r/r_{\tau})=\mathbb{E}\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}\psi(r/r_{\tau})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(\rho)\setminus\Sigma(\rho,\alpha)}r_{\tau}^{D-\delta}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r^{\prime}\geq r/\rho}\limits\psi(r^{\prime})=\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r^{\prime}\geq r/\rho}\limits\psi(r^{\prime})\,,

where we have used that the random sets
\Xi^{(n)}(r)
are independent of the behavior of the system up to the step
n
via conditional expectation, that they have the same distribution as
\Xi(r)
, and then (
4.17
). Hence,
\psi(r)\leq\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r^{\prime}\geq r/\rho}\limits\psi(r^{\prime})
for any
r<\rho
which implies

\sup_{r\geq\rho^{k+1}}\psi(r)\leq\sup_{r\geq\rho^{k}}\psi(r)~~\mbox{for all}~k\,.

Since the function
\psi
is bounded on any finite interval away from zero it is bounded on
(0,R)
. This completes the proof of (
4.15
).
∎

We have proved Theorem
3.2
for the special case
L=R
(and it was argued in Remark
3.4
that this implies, it holds for any
L>\sqrt{2}|O|
.) In order to complete the proof, we need to show that the assertions hold also for
L\in(0,\sqrt{2}|O|]
. Recall that
R_{k}=R_{k,R}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
3.2
for any
0<L<R
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}r^{D-k-1}R_{k,L}(r)dr

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{R}r^{D-k-1}R_{k}(r)dr.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}r^{D-k-1}R_{k,L}(r)dr=\int_{0}^{R}r^{D-k-1}R_{k}(r)dr-\int_{L}^{R}r^{D-k-1}\mathbb{E}C_{k}(F(r))dr

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{D-k-1}\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(Lr_{i},Rr_{i}]}(r)C_{k}(F_{i}(r))dr=:I_{1}-I_{2}+I_{3}\,,

provided that all three integrals on the right side converge absolutely. For the first integral
I_{1}
this is included in the proof of Theorem
3.2
for
L=R
, and for
I_{2}
it follows from the estimate (
4.2
). Replacing in
I_{3}
the curvature measure
C_{k}
by
|C_{k}|
and
interchanging expectation and integration we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{D-k-1}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbf{1}_{(Lr_{i},Rr_{i}]}(r)|C_{k}(F_{i}(r))|dr

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{D-k-1}\mathbf{1}_{(Lr_{i},Rr_{i}]}(r)|C_{k}(F_{i}(r))|dr

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{D-k-1}\mathbf{1}_{(L,R]}(r/r_{i})r_{i}^{k}|C_{k}(F(r/r_{i}))|dr

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}\int_{L}^{R}\bar{r}^{D-k-1}|C_{k}(F^{(1)}(\bar{r}))|d\bar{r}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}\mathbb{E}\int_{L}^{R}\bar{r}^{D-k-1}|C_{k}(F^{(1)}(\bar{r}))|d\bar{r}

\displaystyle=\int_{L}^{R}\bar{r}^{D-k-1}\mathbb{E}|C_{k}(F(\bar{r}))|d\bar{r},

where we have substituted
\bar{r}=r/r_{i}
in the second step and used that
F_{i}=f_{i}(F^{(1)})
. For the third step notice that
F^{(1)}
is independent of the first step of the construction. Hence the expectation can be written as a product of two expectations, where the first one
\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{N}r_{i}^{D}
equals 1, by the definition of
D
. In the second one we interchanged again expectation and integration. The last integral is finite because of (
4.2
). Hence, we have shown the existence of the integral
I_{3}
. Now we can repeat the last transformations omitting the absolute value signs in order to get
I_{3}=I_{2}
. Together with the above decomposition this proves the assertion.
∎

5.
Sufficient conditions and examples

We discuss some examples to illustrate our main result and compare it with the known results in the random recursive case.
In order to simplify the verification of the conditions (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
3.2
, we discuss first some simpler sufficient conditions, which may equally be used in the random recursive case. Recall that
O_{\sigma}=f_{\sigma}(O)
for any word
\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}
. As a first step, the following observation clarifies that not too many of the parallel sets
O_{\sigma}(r)
,
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
intersect. Recall also that we assume here a uniform lower bound
r_{\min}
for the contraction ratios, cf. the first lines of Section
3
. For a similar estimate in the deterministic case see e.g.
[
Wi08
, Lemma 5.3.1]
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
F
be a homogeneous random fractal or a random self-similar set satisfying UOSC (
3.3
) for some open set
O
. Then there is a constant
\Gamma>0
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for all
r>0
and all
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
,

\displaystyle\sharp\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r):O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\neq\emptyset\}\leq\Gamma\,.

Proof.

We provide a proof here for the homogeneous model. The one for the random recursive model is literally the same, when
\mathbb{P}
is replaced by the corresponding measure, see, e.g.,
[
Za11
, eq. (9)]
. Let
O
be the open set in UOSC. The condition implies that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for any
r>0
the family
\{O_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\Sigma(r)\}
consists of pairwise disjoint sets.
The definitions of
R>\sqrt{2}|O|
and
\Sigma(r)
imply that for any
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)

(5.1)

\displaystyle|O_{\sigma}|=r_{\sigma}|O|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}Rr_{\sigma}\leq\frac{r}{\sqrt{2}}.

Now fix
r>0
and let
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)
such that
O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\neq\emptyset
. Then, by (
5.1
),

\displaystyle O_{\sigma^{\prime}}\subset O_{\sigma}(2r+|O_{\sigma^{\prime}}|)\subset B\left(f_{\sigma}(x),(2+\sqrt{2})r\right)\subset B\left(f_{\sigma}(x),4r\right),

where
x
is an arbitrary point in
O
, and thus
f_{\sigma}(x)\in O_{\sigma}
. Here
B(y,s)
denotes the closed ball with center
y
and radius
s
.
Recalling that the volume of a ball of radius
4r
is
\kappa_{d}(4r)^{d}
(where
\kappa_{d}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
) and that the volume of each of the (pairwise disjoint) sets
O_{\sigma^{\prime}}
,
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)
is bounded from below by

\displaystyle C_{d}(O_{\sigma^{\prime}})=r_{\sigma^{\prime}}^{d}C_{d}(O)>C_{d}(O)R^{-d}r_{\min}^{d}r^{d},

we conclude that not too many of the sets
O_{\sigma^{\prime}}
can be contained in the ball
B\left(f_{\sigma}(x),4r\right)
. Hence we obtain that
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely

\displaystyle\sharp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r):O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\neq\emptyset\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sharp\left\{\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r):O_{\sigma^{\prime}}\subseteq B(f_{\sigma}(x),4r)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa_{d}4^{d}R^{d}}{r_{\min}^{d}C_{d}(O)}=:\Gamma,

where the constant
\Gamma
is independent of
r>0
and
\sigma
.
∎

Observe that, by UOSC, a.s.
F\subset\overline{O}
, which implies

(5.2)

\displaystyle F(r)\subset O(r)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.s. for any }r>0

and hence,
F_{\sigma}(r)\subset O_{\sigma}(r)
for any
\sigma\in\Sigma_{*}
. Therefore, the assertion of Lemma
5.1
does equally hold with the sets
O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)
replaced by
F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)
.

Now we formulate the announced conditions that imply (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
3.2
. They are almost sure bounds in contrast to the bounds on expectations in (ii) and (iii). In the deterministic case, a condition very similar to the one in (b) is known to be equivalent to the curvature bounds corresponding to (ii) and (iii), cf.
[
Wi11
, Thm. 4.7]
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,d\}
and let
F
be a homogeneous random fractal or a random self-similar set satisfying USOSC and the regularity condition
(i)
in Theorem
3.2
.
Suppose that one of the following equivalent conditions
(a)–(d)
holds:

(a)
there is a constant
c>0
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for almost all
r>0
and all
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\}}F_{\tau}(r)\bigg)\leq cr^{k};

(b)
there is
c^{\prime}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for a.a.
r>0
and all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)
with
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r))\leq c^{\prime}r^{k};

(c)

there is
c^{\prime\prime}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for a.a.
r>0
and all
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r))\leq c^{\prime\prime}r^{k};

(d)
there is
c^{\prime\prime\prime}>0
such that
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for a.a.
r>0
and all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)
with
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r))\leq c^{\prime\prime\prime}r^{k}.

Then the conditions
(ii)
and
(iii)
in Theorem
3.2
are satisfied.

Proof.

Again we provide a proof for the homogeneous model, the one for the recursive case being similar. First we show the equivalence of the four conditions (a)–(d). The implications (a)
\Rightarrow
(b), (c)
\Rightarrow
(b) and (d)
\Rightarrow
(b) are obvious from corresponding set inclusions.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a): Suppose (b) holds. Then
\mathbb{P}
-a.s. for a.a.
r>0
and any
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(\,

\displaystyle F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\}}F_{\tau}(r)\bigg)=C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(F(r),\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\}}F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\tau}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\}}C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\big(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\tau}(r)\big)\leq\Gamma\cdot c^{\prime}r^{k},

since, by Lemma
5.1
, the number of nonzero summands in the last sum is bounded by some constant
\Gamma
independent of
r
or
\sigma
. Moreover, by (b), each of these summands is bounded by
c^{\prime}r^{k}
.
Hence, (b) implies (a) (with constant
c=\Gamma c^{\prime}
), and therefore both conditions are equivalent.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(c): We have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for a.a.
r>0
and
\sigma\in\Sigma(r)
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}

\displaystyle\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\right)\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap U_{r,\sigma}\right)+C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\setminus U_{r,\sigma}\right),

where
U_{r,\sigma}:=\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\}}F_{\tau}(r)
. By (a), the first summand on the right is bounded by
cr^{k}
. For the second summand we infer that

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\setminus U_{r,\sigma}\right)\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{r,\sigma}\right)=C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F_{\sigma}(r),\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus U_{r,\sigma}\right),

by the local definiteness, and therefore, by Lemma
2.1
,

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\setminus U_{r,\sigma}\right)\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F_{\sigma}(r)\right)\leq c_{k}(R)r^{k},

since, by definition of
\Sigma(r)
,
r\geq Rr_{\sigma}>\sqrt{2}|O|r_{\sigma}\geq\sqrt{2}|F_{\sigma}|
. Hence (c) holds (with
c^{\prime\prime}=c+c_{k}(R)
), showing the equivalence of (a) and (c).

(c)
\Rightarrow
(d): We have
\mathbb{P}
-a.s., for a.a.
r>0
and all
\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma(r)
with
\sigma\neq\sigma^{\prime}
, (with
U_{r,\sigma}
defined as above and noting that
F(r)=F_{\sigma}(r)\cup U_{r,\sigma}
)

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}

\displaystyle\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\right)=C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\cap\left(F_{\sigma}(r)\cup U_{r,\sigma}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\cap F_{\sigma}(r)\right)+C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap O_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\cap U_{r,\sigma}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\right)+C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap U_{r,\sigma}\right)

Due to (c), the first summand is bounded by
c^{\prime\prime}r^{k}
, while for the second summand we get

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),O_{\sigma}(r)\cap U_{\sigma,r}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\},O_{\tau}(r)\cap O_{\sigma}(r)\neq\emptyset}F_{\tau}(r)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\tau\in\Sigma(r)\setminus\{\sigma\},O_{\tau}(r)\cap O_{\sigma}(r)\neq\emptyset}C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\left(F(r),F_{\tau}(r)\right).

Now, again by (c), each summand is bounded by
c^{\prime\prime}r^{k}
and, by Lemma
5.1
, the number of summands is bounded by some constant
\Gamma
. Hence (d) holds (with
c^{\prime\prime\prime}=(1+\Gamma)c^{\prime\prime}
).

It suffices now to show that condition (a) implies (ii) and (iii) in Theorem
3.2
.
Applying condition (a) to the expectation in condition (ii), we conclude that
for any
r_{0}\in(0,R)
, this expectation is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\operatorname{ess\,sup}_{r_{0}\leq r\leq R}\limits\,\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{b}(r)}cr^{k}\leq cR^{k}<\infty.

Similarly, the conditional expectation in condition (iii) is bounded from above by

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\bigg[\max_{\sigma\in\Sigma_{b}(r)}\,r^{-k}\,cr^{k}\bigg|\sharp\Sigma_{b}(r)\bigg]\leq c\Gamma

for a.a.
r\in(0,R)
. Hence conditions (ii) and (iii) are satisfied as claimed.
∎

.
Then, for any

\mathbb{P}_{0}
on the space
\Omega_{0}
of the primary random IFS with

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{0}(\{G\})=p,\qquad\mathbb{P}_{0}(\{H\})=1-p\,.

\mathbb{P}_{0}

, although we suppress this dependence in the notation. The two deterministic self-similar sets generated by

and

First note that
F

.

3<N\leq 6
with probability 1.

\mathbb{P}
-a.s. all parallel sets of

. Hence

is convex for these

by convex sets. By Lemma

, there is a constant

and, since curvature measures are locally defined, we get

\displaystyle C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}(F(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r))

\displaystyle=C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\sigma}(r)}F_{\tau}(r),F_{\sigma}(r)\cap F_{\sigma^{\prime}}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C_{k}^{\operatorname{var}}\bigg(\bigcup_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\sigma}(r)}F_{\tau}(r)\bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\Gamma}\max_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\sigma}(r)}C_{k}(F_{\tau}(r))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\Gamma}\max_{\tau\in\Sigma_{\sigma}(r)}r_{\tau}^{k}C_{k}(F^{(|\tau|)}(r^{-1}_{\tau}r))

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\Gamma}R^{-k}r^{k}C_{k}(B(0,1+6R))=:cr^{k},

where in the third step we used
[
Wi08
, Lemma 3.1.4]
and in the last line the monotonicity of total curvatures for convex sets together with the fact that
|F(r_{\tau}^{-1}r)|\leq|F|+2r_{\tau}^{-1}\leq 1+2Rr_{\min}^{-1}\leq 1+6R
.
Hence, by Proposition
5.2
, conditions (ii) and (iii) of Theorem
3.2
are satisfied and we can apply this theorem.

Observe that the scaling exponent

is given by the equation

(5.4)

\displaystyle 3p\cdot 2^{-D}+6(1-p)\cdot 3^{-D}=1,

and that the distribution of the logarithmic contraction ratios is

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{3p}{2^{D}}\mathbf{1}_{(\cdot)}(\ln(2))+\frac{6(1-p)}{3^{D}}\mathbf{1}_{(\cdot)}(\ln(3)).

Hence
\mu
is non-lattice for

(and lattice for
p\in\{0,1\}
). Its mean value is given by

(5.5)

\displaystyle\eta=\frac{3p}{2^{D}}\ln(2)+\frac{6(1-p)}{3^{D}}\ln(3).

In order to compute the limits of the expected rescaled total curvatures, as provided by Theorem

, it remains, to determine

for

.

For the computations it is convenient to set
L:=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{6}
(which is the inradius of an equilateral triangle with sidelength 1). We split the interval
(0,L)
into three pieces. For

, all indicators in
R_{k,L}
are zero almost surely, since
r_{i}\leq\frac{1}{2}
a.s. Hence
R_{k,L}(r)=\mathbb{E}C_{k}(F(r))=C_{k}(T(r))
in this case, since

is large enough such that

has no holes. For
r\in[\frac{L}{3},\frac{L}{2}]
, there are two possible situations. If we condition on the event that in the first step of the construction the IFS
G
is chosen, then there will be a hole in

. Moreover, the indicators in
R_{k,L}
are 1,
N=3
,
r_{i}=\frac{1}{2}
and the sets
F_{i}(r)=(g_{i}T)(r)
are convex.
Since

in this case, the inclusion-exclusion principle implies

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{k}^{L}(r)\big|(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})=G\big]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[C_{k}(F(r))-\sum_{i=1}^{3}C_{k}(F_{i}(r))\bigg|(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})=G\bigg]

\displaystyle=-3C_{k}((g_{1}T)(r)\cap(g_{2}T)(r)).

Otherwise, i.e. if
H
is chosen, we have
\mathbb{E}\big[\xi_{k}^{L}(r)\big|(f_{1},\ldots,f_{N})=H\big]=C_{k}(T(r))
.
This yields

\displaystyle R_{k,L}(r)=(1-p)C_{k}(T(r))-p\cdot 3C_{k}((g_{1}T)(r)\cap(g_{2}T)(r)),\qquad r\in[\frac{L}{3},\frac{L}{2}].

Finally, for

\displaystyle R_{k,L}(r)=\mathbb{E}\sum_{I\subset[N],\sharp I\geq 2}(-1)^{\sharp I-1}C_{k}\bigg(\bigcap_{i\in I}F_{i}(r)\bigg),\qquad r\in(0,\frac{L}{3}),

or

\displaystyle-R_{k,L}(r)=p

\displaystyle\cdot 3\ C_{k}((g_{1}T)(r)\cap(g_{2}T)(r)))+(1-p)\cdot

\displaystyle\left(9C_{k}((h_{1}T)(r)\cap(h_{2}T)(r))-C_{k}((h_{2}T)(r)\cap(h_{4}T)(r)\cap(h_{5}T)(r))\right).

for any

Note that all the sets occurring above in the expressions for

are nonempty and convex.
For

\displaystyle R_{0,L}(r)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}5p-8,&r\in(0,L/3),\\
1-4p,&r\in[L/3,L/2),\\
1,&r\in[L/2,L).\end{cases}

Hence, for

\displaystyle C_{0,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{D}\mathbb{E}C_{0}(F(\varepsilon))=\frac{L^{D}}{D\cdot\eta}\left(1-(1-p)3^{2-D}-p2^{2-D}\right),

For

\displaystyle C_{1}((g_{1}T)(r)\cap(g_{2}T)(r))=C_{1}((h_{1}T)(r)\cap(h_{2}T)(r))=(\frac{2}{3}\pi+\sqrt{3})r

\displaystyle R_{1,L}(r)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\left(3p(\pi+2\sqrt{3})-(9\sqrt{3}+5\pi)\right)\cdot r=:c_{p}\cdot r,&r\in(0,L/3),\\
\frac{3}{2}(1-p)+\underbrace{(\pi-3p(\pi+\sqrt{3})}_{=:\tilde{c}_{p}}\cdot r,&r\in[L/3,L/2),\\

Plugging this into the formula (I) in Theorem

\displaystyle C_{1,F}^{\operatorname{frac}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\searrow 0}\varepsilon^{D-1}\mathbb{E}C_{1}(F(\varepsilon))=\frac{1}{\eta}\int_{0}^{L}r^{D-2}R_{1,L}(r)dr

\displaystyle=\frac{L^{D}}{D\eta}\left(3^{-D}(c_{p}-\tilde{c}_{p})+2^{-D}(\tilde{c}_{p}-\pi)+\pi\right)

Example 5.4

Denote by

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tan}(\widetilde{K(r)},x)

The above observations imply the characterization (

Theorem 6.1

Let

Given

A_{<r}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,d_{A}(y)<r\}.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
r>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a compact set
\Sigma\subset\partial B(x,r)
be given. Then

\operatorname{int}B(s,|s-x|)\subset\Sigma_{<r},\quad s\in\operatorname{conv}\Sigma.

Take any points

On the other hand,

separates

, hence,

, which contradicts the assumption

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

If

is nonempty and compact and

then

\operatorname{reach}\widetilde{K(r)}\geq\inf\{J_{K}(x):\,x\in\partial K(r)\},

Proof.

Denote
\eta:=\inf\{J_{K}(x):\,x\in\partial K(r)\}

\eta>0
.
We will show that

(6.4)

d_{\operatorname{Tan}(\widetilde{K(r)},x)}(y-x)\leq\frac{|y-x|^{2}}{2\eta},\quad x,y\in\widetilde{K(r)}.

This will imply the assertion, see
[
RZ19

If

Denote

Denote further

Lemma 6.4

The function

\liminf_{y\to x}J_{K}(y)\geq J_{K}(x).

First we show that the set-valued function

\displaystyle\leq|x_{n}-x|+|b-x|<\varepsilon+|a-x|-3\varepsilon

\displaystyle<|a-x|-|a-a_{n}|-\varepsilon\leq|a_{n}-x|-\varepsilon

Lemma 6.5

\Phi:(\omega,K)\mapsto\mu^{\omega}(K),\quad(\omega,K)\in\Omega\times\mathcal{K}

\omega\mapsto\int f\,d\mu^{\omega}

f\mapsto\int f\,d\mu^{\omega}

\Psi

Further, given
K\in\mathcal{K}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, consider the function

f_{K,n}:x\mapsto(1-nd_{K}(x))^{+},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Since for any
K,K^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}
we have
|f_{K,n}(x)-f_{K^{\prime},n}(x)|\leq nd_{H}(K,K^{\prime})
, the mapping
\varphi_{n}:K\mapsto f_{K,n}
is continuous from
\mathcal{K}
to
\mathcal{C}_{c}
. Thus, the mapping
\tilde{\varphi}_{n}:(\omega,K)\mapsto(\omega,f_{K,n})
, as well as the composition
\Phi_{n}:=\Psi\circ\tilde{\varphi}_{n}
, are measurable on the product space
\Omega\times\mathcal{K}
. Since
\Phi_{n}(\omega,K)=\int f_{K,n}\,d\mu^{\omega}\to\mu^{\omega}(K)=\Phi(\omega,K)
,
n\to\infty
, for any
(\omega,K)
, the limit function
\Phi
is jointly measurable as well.

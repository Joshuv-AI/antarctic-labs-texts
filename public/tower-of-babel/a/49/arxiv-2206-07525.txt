Homotopy and the Homomorphism Threshold of Odd Cycles
Maya Sankar

Abstract
Consider a family ℱ \mathcal{F} of C 2 ​ r + 1 C_{2r+1} -free graphs, where r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 . Suppose that each graph in ℱ \mathcal{F} has minimum degree linear in its number of vertices. Thomassen showed that such a family has bounded chromatic number, or, equivalently, that all graphs in ℱ \mathcal{F} are homomorphic to a complete graph of bounded size. Considering instead homomorphic images which are themselves C 2 ​ r + 1 C_{2r+1} -free, we construct a family of dense C 2 ​ r + 1 C_{2r+1} -free graphs with no C 2 ​ r + 1 C_{2r+1} -free homomorphic image of bounded size. This provides the first nontrivial lower bound on the homomorphism threshold of odd cycles of length at least 5 and answers a question of Ebsen and Schacht. Our proof introduces a new technique to describe the topological structure of a graph. We establish a graph-theoretic analogue of homotopy equivalence, which allows us to analyze the relative placement of odd closed walks in a graph. This notion has unexpected connections to the neighborhood complex, leading to multiple interesting questions.



1
Introduction

A central question in graph theory is to understand those graphs containing no copy of some fixed subgraph
H
. In one of the first applications of the probabilistic method, Erdős
[
8
]
showed that there are
H
-free graphs of arbitrarily large chromatic number (so long as
H
is not a forest). However, Erdős’s construction is inherently sparse.

A natural follow-up is to restrict one’s focus to
H
-free graphs that are dense in some way, such as having large minimum degree. This interplay of chromatic number and minimum degree was first investigated in the case that
H
is a clique, almost fifty years ago. Given a graph
G
, let
|G|
denote its number of vertices. Andrásfai, Erdős, and Sós
[
3
]
showed that any
K_{r}
-free graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)>\frac{3r-7}{3r-4}|G|
has chromatic number
\chi(G)<r
. Almost simultaneously, Erdős and Simonovits
[
10
]
constructed a family of triangle-free graphs
G_{n}
with unbounded chromatic number that satisfy
\delta(G_{n})/|G_{n}|=\frac{1}{3}-o(1)
.

The results of
[
10
,
3
]
inaugurated the study of the chromatic threshold and, later, the homomorphism threshold. Both investigate the dependency of structural properties, such as the chromatic number, on the minimum degree of a dense
H
-free graph.

1.1
Thresholds

The chromatic and homomorphism thresholds provide different formalizations of the following question. Let
G
be an
H
-free graph on
n
vertices, with minimum degree at least
\alpha n
. For which fixed values of
\alpha
can we provide guarantees about the structure of
G
?

The chromatic threshold asks for the infimum of those
\alpha
for which
\chi(G)
is bounded as a function of
\alpha
. More formally, the
chromatic threshold
of a graph
H
is the quantity

\displaystyle\delta_{\chi}(H)=\inf\{\alpha\colon\exists\

\displaystyle K=K(H,\alpha)\text{ such that}

\displaystyle\text{any $H$-free graph $G$ with $\delta(G)\geq\alpha|G|$ satisfies $\chi(G)\leq K$}\}.

We remark that the chromatic threshold of any bipartite graph is 0, by the Kővári–Sós–Turán theorem
[
16
]
, and that by the Erdős–Stone theorem
[
11
]
, any
H
with
\chi(H)=r\geq 3
has chromatic threshold at most the Turán density
\frac{r-2}{r-1}
of
H
.

Aside from bipartite graphs, the first chromatic thresholds computed were those of cliques and odd cycles. Thomassen
[
27
]
showed that
\delta_{\chi}(K_{3})=\frac{1}{3}
, matching the lower bound from
[
10
]
. This was later extended by Goddard and Lyle
[
12
]
and Nikiforov
[
24
]
, who computed
\delta_{\chi}(K_{r})
for all
r\geq 3
. Thomassen
[
28
]
additionally gave a very short proof that
\delta_{\chi}(C_{2r+1})=0
for all
r\geq 2
. Then, in 2010, Łuczak and Thomassé
[
22
]
and Lyle
[
23
]
proved bounds on the chromatic thresholds of larger families of graphs. Building on these results, Allen, Böttcher, Griffiths, Kohayakawa, and Morris
[
2
]
completely determined the chromatic threshold of any graph
H
.

The triangle-free graphs with
\delta(G)>\frac{1}{3}|G|
may be described more precisely in terms of graph homomorphisms. If
G
and
\Gamma
are graphs, a
homomorphism

\phi:G\to\Gamma
is a map
V(G)\to V(\Gamma)
such that for any edge
xy\in E(G)
, we have
\phi(xy):=\phi(x)\phi(y)\in E(\Gamma)
. If such a
\phi
exists, we say
\Gamma
is a
homomorphic image
of
G
; similarly, if
\mathcal{G}
is a family of graphs and
\Gamma
is a homomorphic image of each
G\in\mathcal{G}
, we say
\Gamma
is a
homomorphic image
of
\mathcal{G}
.

In 2006, Łuczak
[
21
]
showed that for every
\epsilon>0
, there is a finite triangle-free graph
\Gamma_{\epsilon}
such that every triangle-free graph
G
with minimum degree at least
(\frac{1}{3}+\epsilon)|G|
has a homomorphism to
\Gamma_{\epsilon}
. Later, Brandt and Thomassé
[
6
]
fully described the minimal such graphs
\Gamma_{\epsilon}
, showing that they are four-colorable and have
O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)
vertices. This motivated the introduction of the
homomorphism threshold
of a graph
H
, defined as

\displaystyle\delta_{\hom}(H)=\inf\{\alpha\colon\exists\

an
H
-free graph
\Gamma=\Gamma(H,\alpha)
such that

\displaystyle\text{any $H$-free graph $G$ with $\delta(G)\geq\alpha|G|$ has a homomorphism to $\Gamma$}\}.

We remark that
\delta_{\hom}(H)\geq\delta_{\chi}(H)
for any graph
H
, because if there is a homomorphism
G\to\Gamma
then
\chi(G)\leq\chi(\Gamma)
. Łuczak’s work showed that this inequality is tight for
K_{3}
, i.e. that
\delta_{\hom}(K_{3})=\frac{1}{3}=\delta_{\chi}(K_{3})
. Goddard and Lyle
[
12
]
and Oberkampf and Schacht
[
25
]
extended this result to all cliques, showing that
\delta_{\hom}(K_{r})=\delta_{\chi}(K_{r})
for any
r\geq 4
. There has also recently been some work on the homomorphism thresholds of odd cycles. Let
\mathcal{C}_{2r+1}=\{C_{3},C_{5},\ldots,C_{2r+1}\}
; the homomorphism threshold of a family
\mathcal{H}
of forbidden subgraphs is defined analogously to that of a single graph
H
. It was shown that
\delta_{\hom}(\mathcal{C}_{2r+1})=\frac{1}{2r+1}
and
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})\leq\frac{1}{2r+1}
by Letzter and Snyder
[
17
]
(for
r=2
) and Ebsen and Schacht
[
7
]
(for
r\geq 2
).

Computing the homomorphism thresholds of graphs other than cliques and bipartite graphs remains a challenging problem. An inherently related question is whether the inequality
\delta_{\hom}(H)\geq\delta_{\chi}(H)
is ever strict. The results of
[
17
,
7
]
partially resolved this, showing that the families
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{C}_{2r+1}
satisfy
\delta_{\hom}(\mathcal{H})>\delta_{\chi}(\mathcal{H})
for
r\geq 2
. However, it remained unknown whether there were single graphs
H
for which
\delta_{\hom}(H)>\delta_{\chi}(H)
, and Ebsen and Schacht
[
7
]
explicitly asked whether this holds for
H=C_{5}
.

Our work answers their question, presenting the first nontrivial lower bound on
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
for
r\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
r\geq 2
, we have
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})>0
.

Recall that Thomassen
[
28
]
showed
\delta_{\chi}(C_{2r+1})=0
for
r\geq 2
. Thus, these odd cycles are the first graphs
H
known to satisfy
\delta_{\hom}(H)>\delta_{\chi}(H)
. An explicit lower bound on
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
is stated in terms of the volume of a spherical cap in
Corollary

5.8
.

We remark that the results of
[
21
,
12
,
25
,
17
,
7
]
focus on providing upper bounds for
\delta_{\hom}
. The lower bound
\delta_{\hom}(\mathcal{C}_{2r+1})\geq\frac{1}{2r+1}
is achieved without difficulty via a sequence
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots
of increasingly good approximations to a Borsuk graph (defined in
Section

1.2
below), using arguments akin to those in
Section

5
. One could alternatively choose the approximations
G_{i}
a little more carefully, as in
[
7
]
, so that any two vertices lie on a common
C_{2r+3}
— immediately implying that any proper homomorphic image of
G_{i}
contains a shorter odd cycle.

Our work marks the first more elaborate technique for lower-bounding homomorphism thresholds. We introduce a new tool to describe the topological structure of a graph. Then, letting
G
be a graph with appropriate topological properties, we reduce the study of
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic images of
G
to the more tractable analysis of certain small
C_{2r+1}
-free graphs. For some families
\mathcal{G}
of dense
C_{2r+1}
-free graphs, these techniques show that all
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic images of any
G\in\mathcal{G}
have size at least some increasing function of
|G|
, thereby proving
1.1
.

1.2
The Borsuk Graph and Topological Methods

Our topological methods are inspired by Borsuk graphs, infinite graphs constructed on the
n
-dimensional unit sphere
S^{n}
. These were first introduced by Erdős and Hajnal
[
9
]
, and their properties have since been studied in many different contexts
[
15
,
20
,
26
]
. We seek to introduce graph properties that, when applied to Borsuk graphs, capture the underlying topological structure of
S^{n}
.

Given
n\geq 1
and
\epsilon>0
, the Borsuk graph
\BG(n,\epsilon)
is an infinite graph with vertex set
S^{n}
. We place an edge between vertices
x,y\in S^{n}
if the distance between them on the sphere is greater than
\pi-\epsilon
. Equivalently, the neighborhood
N(x)
of any vertex
x\in S^{n}
is a ball of radius
\epsilon
around its antipode
-x
.

The Borsuk graph appears frequently in lower-bound constructions for chromatic and homomorphism thresholds and other extremal quantities
[
2
,
5
,
7
,
17
,
22
]
. In addition to being dense, its useful properties include having large chromatic number and lacking short odd cycles. More specifically,
\BG(n,\epsilon)
has no odd cycles of length at most
1/\epsilon
and has chromatic number at least
n+2
; it is well-known (see
[
9
,
20
]
) that the latter attribute is equivalent to the Borsuk–Ulam theorem.

The Borsuk graph also inspired Lovász’s groundbreaking proof of the Kneser conjecture
[
19
]
, one of the first applications of topological methods to combinatorics. Lovász introduced the
neighborhood complex

{\mathcal{N}}(G)
of a graph
G
, a simplicial complex which, when
G=\BG(n,\epsilon)
, captures some of the topological structure of
S^{n}
. He then bounded
\chi(G)
in terms of topological properties of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
, as follows. We say a topological space
X
is
k
-connected
if for each
\ell=0,1,\ldots,k
, any continuous map
S^{\ell}\to X
extends continuously to a map from the entire ball
B^{\ell+1}
.

Theorem 1.2

(Lovász
[
19
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph whose neighborhood complex
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is
k
-connected. Then
\chi(G)\geq k+3
.

Lovász’s proof of the Kneser conjecture concluded by showing that the neighborhood complex of a Kneser graph had the desired connectivity.

Our work introduces a new tool to describe the topological structure of a graph. We introduce an equivalence relation on the set of walks in a connected graph
G
, which we call
(discrete) homotopy equivalence
in analogy to the algebraic topology setting. This detects topological properties similar to those of the neighborhood complex
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
. One may define a
discrete fundamental group

\pi_{1}(G)
, consisting of homotopy classes of those closed walks based at some fixed
v\in V(G)
. When
G
is nonbipartite — a condition ensuring that
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is connected — we prove (see
6.3
) that the fundamental group of the topological space
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is isomorphic to the index-2 subgroup
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)\subseteq\pi_{1}(G)
comprising closed walks of even length.

Although discrete homotopy and the neighborhood complex are interrelated, the former is distinctly better suited for our application. Because
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{N}}(G))\simeq\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
, studying
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
inherently limits our perspective to even closed walks, whereas analyzing interactions between odd cycles is our goal. Additionally, due to its purely combinatorial definition, discrete homotopy can be simpler to reason about.

At the heart of our argument is the following theorem, from which we deduce
1.1
. We say a connected graph
G
is
simply connected
(see
2.7
) if walks in
G
under discrete homotopy behave like continuous paths in a simply connected topological space under homotopy. An equivalent formulation, discussed in
Section

6
, is that
G
is simply connected if and only if
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
is trivial.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a simply connected graph and fix
r\geq 2
. Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism such that
H
is
C_{2r+1}
-free, and suppose further that some odd cycle
C
of
G
satisfies
|\phi(V(C))|\leq 2r+2
. Then
\chi(G)<8r^{2}
.

Consider the
C_{2r+1}
-free Borsuk graph
\BG\left(8r^{2}-2,\frac{\pi}{2r+1}\right)
, which has chromatic number at least
8r^{2}
. Applying
Theorem

1.3
, we show that any family of arbitrarily good finite approximations to this graph has no
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic image of bounded size. This allows us to lower-bound
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
, proving
1.1
.

Organization.

In
Section

2
, we introduce discrete homotopy and other related preliminaries. In
Section

3
, we reduce the proof of
Theorem

1.3
to studying a well-connected subgraph of the
C_{2r+1}
-free graph
H
; we then complete the proof of
Theorem

1.3
in
Section

4
. We deduce
1.1
from
Theorem

1.3
in
Section

5
by analyzing approximations to Borsuk graphs.
Section

6
builds out our topological framework, defining the discrete fundamental group and proving that
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)\simeq\pi_{1}({\mathcal{N}}(G))
when
G
is non-bipartite. Lastly, we conclude by discussing potential extensions of
Theorem

1.3
and some topological open problems in
Section

7
.

Notation.

If
G
is a graph, we write
|G|
for its number of vertices and, for any
v\in V(G)
, denote its neighborhood by
N(v)
.
In
Sections

2
and
3
, we frequently make use of multisets. We use double braces
\left\{\!\left\{\cdot\right\}\!\right\}
to denote a multiset, and, to further aid the reader, we establish the convention that multisets are always denoted by variables with tildes. We write

for the cardinality of a multiset (taken with multiplicity) and write

\widetilde{A}

with multiplicity equal to its multiplicity in
\widetilde{A}
.

2
Homotopy and Invariants

In this section, we define an equivalence relation, homotopy equivalence, on walks in a graph
G
. We also introduce a family of functions — called intersection invariants — on walks in
G
, and prove they are homotopy-invariant.

Let
G
be a graph. A
walk
of length
k\geq 0
in
G
is a sequence
P=v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
of
k+1
vertices such that
v_{i}v_{i+1}
is an edge of
G
for each
0\leq i<k
. We say
P
is a
closed walk
if its endpoints
v_{0}
and
v_{k}
are equal. We describe
P
as
odd
or
even
depending on the parity of its length
k
, and abbreviate this as the
parity
of the walk
P
. We write
\widetilde{E}(P)
for the multiset of edges of
P
counted with multiplicity; that is,

\widetilde{E}(P)=\left\{\!\left\{v_{i}v_{i+1}:0\leq i<k\right\}\!\right\}.

There are two key differences between walks and paths. Unlike paths, walks may repeat vertices. Moreover, walks are
oriented
— in general,
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and
v_{k}\cdots v_{0}
are different walks. The same distinctions hold between closed walks and cycles.

In a topological space, two continuous paths are homotopic if one may be continuously deformed into the other. We introduce a discrete analogue of this concept, in which two walks of a graph are homotopic if one may be transformed into the other via a sequence of small perturbations.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph. We say two walks
P
and
P^{\prime}
of
G
are
homotopic
or
homotopy equivalent
if we may transform
P
into
P^{\prime}
via a finite sequence of substitution, insertion, and deletion steps, defined as follows.

(Sub)

Given a walk
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0<i<k
, replace
v_{i}
with
v^{\prime}_{i}
for some
v^{\prime}_{i}\in N(v_{i-1})\cap N(v_{i+1})
.

(Ins)

Given a walk
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0\leq i\leq k
, replace
v_{i}
with
v_{i}wv_{i}
for some
w\in N(v_{i})
.

(Del)

Given a walk
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0<i<k
satisfying
v_{i-1}=v_{i+1}
, replace
v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}
with
v_{i-1}
.

Notice that these operations are invertible: we can undo a substitution step via another substitution step, and similarly insertions and deletions are each other’s inverses. Thus, homotopy does give an equivalence relation on walks in
G
.

Example 2.2
.

Let
G
be the 7-vertex graph shown below. Then the two closed walks
v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}v_{0}
and
v_{0}v_{5}v_{6}v_{0}
are homotopic, via the sequence of operations given.

v_{0}
v_{1}
v_{2}
v_{3}
v_{4}
v_{5}
v_{6}
\begin{array}[]{l}\ v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}v_{0}\xrightarrow{\text{\ ins\ }}v_{0}v_{1}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{2}v_{4}v_{2}}v_{0}\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ v_{0}v_{1}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{3}}v_{4}v_{2}v_{0}\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ v_{0}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{5}}v_{3}v_{4}v_{2}v_{0}\\[5.0pt]
\hphantom{v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}v_{0}}\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ v_{0}v_{5}v_{3}v_{4}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{6}}v_{0}\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ v_{0}v_{5}v_{3}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{5}}v_{6}v_{0}\xrightarrow{\text{\ del\ }}v_{0}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}v_{5}}v_{6}v_{0}\end{array}

We state two homotopy invariants which are immediately clear from the definition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
G
be a graph and suppose that the walks
P=v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and
P^{\prime}=w_{0}\cdots w_{\ell}
are homotopic in
G
. Then,

(i)

P
and
P^{\prime}
have the same endpoints, i.e.
v_{0}=w_{0}
and
v_{k}=w_{\ell}
.

(ii)

We have that
k\equiv\ell
(mod 2).

To design more informative homotopy invariants, we need some additional notation. Revisiting
2.1
, notice that concatenating the length-2 paths removed and added in (Sub) forms a closed walk
v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i-1}
of length 4. This motivates studying how the edges of a graph connect via
C_{4}
’s.

Let
H
be a graph. Let
\overset{\diamond}{\sim}
be the equivalence relation on
E(H)
generated by the relations

\{e\overset{\diamond}{\sim}e^{\prime}\colon e,e^{\prime}\in E(H)\text{ contained in the same $C_{4}$}\}.

For any edge
e\in E(H)
, we define its
C_{4}
-closure
, written
[e]^{\diamond}_{H}
, to be the equivalence class of
e
under
\overset{\diamond}{\sim}
.

Now, fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
. Given
e\in E(G)
and its image
f=\phi(e)\in E(H)
, let
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by the edge set
\phi^{-1}\left([f]^{\diamond}_{H}\right)
. Define
[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
to be the edge set of the connected component of
G^{\prime}
containing
e
. We remark that the sets
\left\{[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}:e\in E(G)\right\}
partition
E(G)
.

Say a set
A\subseteq E(G)
is
\phi
-stable
if it may be expressed as a union of sets of the form
[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
. Equivalently,
A
is
\phi
-stable if for any edge
e\in A
, we have
[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}\subseteq A
. Given a
\phi
-stable set
A
and a vertex
u\in V(H)
, we also define

A_{u}:=\left\{e\in A:e\text{ has an endpoint in }\phi^{-1}(u)\right\}.

Using this notation, we define our homotopy invariants.

Definition 2.4
.

Fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
. The
intersection invariants
associated to
\phi
are families of functions
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
and
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
for each
\phi
-stable subset
A\subseteq E(G)
and each vertex
u\in V(H)
. Both
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
and
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
map multisets of edges of
E(G)
to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
, and are defined as

{\mathscr{I}}_{A}(\widetilde{F}):=|\widetilde{F}\cap A|\pmod{2}\quad\text{ and }\quad{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(\widetilde{F}):=|\widetilde{F}\cap A_{u}|\pmod{2},

recalling that the cardinalities of the multisets
\widetilde{F}\cap A
and
\widetilde{F}\cap A_{u}
are taken with multiplicity.
When we want to specify the
\phi
-stable set
A
, we refer to the
A
-intersection invariants

{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
and
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
.

Given an intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
associated to
\phi:G\to H
and a walk
P
of
G
, we define
{\mathscr{I}}(P):={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{E}(P))
. We will now show that intersection invariants are in fact homotopy invariants — if
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic walks in
G
, then
{\mathscr{I}}(P)={\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime})
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
. Let
{\mathscr{I}}
be an intersection invariant associated to
\phi
of either form
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
or
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
.

(i)

Suppose
\widetilde{E}_{1}
and
\widetilde{E}_{2}
are multisets of edges of
E(G)
. Then
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{E}_{1}+\widetilde{E}_{2})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{E}_{1})+{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{E}_{2})
.

(ii)

Let
P=v_{0}v_{1}v_{0}
be a closed walk of length 2 in
G
. Then
{\mathscr{I}}(P)=0
.

(iii)

Let
P=v_{0}v_{1}v_{2}v_{3}v_{0}
be a closed walk of length 4 in
G
. Then
{\mathscr{I}}(P)=0
.

Proof.

Recall from
2.4
that
{\mathscr{I}}
is defined as
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F})\equiv|\widetilde{F}\cap A^{\prime}|\pmod{2}
for some
A^{\prime}\subseteq E(G)
. Then (i) immediately follows from the identity

|(\widetilde{E}_{1}+\widetilde{E}_{2})\cap A^{\prime}|=|\widetilde{E}_{1}\cap A^{\prime}|+|\widetilde{E}_{2}\cap A^{\prime}|.

For (ii), observe
\widetilde{E}(P)=\left\{\!\left\{v_{0}v_{1},v_{1}v_{0}\right\}\!\right\}
repeats a single edge twice. Thus
|\widetilde{E}(P)\cap A^{\prime}|
is either 0 or 2, depending on whether
v_{0}v_{1}\in A^{\prime}
, yielding
{\mathscr{I}}(P)=0
.

It remains to show (iii). Fix a
\phi
-stable set
A\subseteq E(G)
and suppose that
{\mathscr{I}}
is an
A
-intersection invariant. For
i=0,\ldots,3
, set
u_{i}=\phi(v_{i})
. We will use casework on whether or not the four vertices
u_{0},\ldots,u_{3}
are distinct.

First, suppose that
u_{0},\ldots,u_{3}
are distinct vertices of
H
, so that
u_{0}u_{1}u_{2}u_{3}
is a
C_{4}
. Then,
u_{0}u_{1}\overset{\diamond}{\sim}u_{1}u_{2}\overset{\diamond}{\sim}u_{2}u_{3}\overset{\diamond}{\sim}u_{3}u_{0}
in
H
, so the four edges of
\widetilde{E}(P)
are in the same connected component of
\phi^{-1}\left([u_{0}u_{1}]^{\diamond}_{H}\right)
. It follows that
\widetilde{E}(P)\cap A
is either
\widetilde{E}(P)
or empty, and thus that
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}(P)=0
. Now fix a vertex
u\in V(H)
; we will show
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(P)=0
. We observe that
\widetilde{E}(P)\cap A_{u}
is only nonempty if
u\in\{u_{0},\ldots,u_{3}\}
, in which case exactly two edges of
\widetilde{E}(P)
will have an endpoint in
\phi^{-1}(u)
. It follows that
|E(P)\cap A_{u}|
is either 0 or 2, so
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(P)=0
. Thus,
{\mathscr{I}}(P)=0
if
u_{0},\ldots,u_{3}
are distinct, whether
{\mathscr{I}}
has the form
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
or
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
.

Lastly, we show (iii) in the case that the vertices
u_{0},\ldots,u_{3}
are not distinct. Without loss of generality, suppose
u_{0}=u_{2}
. Consider the edges
e_{0}=v_{0}v_{1}
and
e_{1}=v_{1}v_{2}
. We observe that
e_{1}\in[e_{0}]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
because
\phi(e_{0})=\phi(e_{1})
and the two edges
e_{0}
and
e_{1}
are adjacent. Thus,
e_{0}\in A
if and only if
e_{1}\in A
. Now, let
u
be any vertex of
H
. Because
\phi(e_{0})=\phi(e_{1})
, the edge
e_{0}
has an endpoint in
\phi^{-1}(u)
if and only if
e_{1}
does. It follows that
e_{0}\in A_{u}
if and only if
e_{1}\in A_{u}
. Letting
e_{2}=v_{2}v_{3}
and
e_{3}=v_{3}v_{0}
, an analogous argument shows that
e_{2}\in A
if and only if
e_{3}\in A
, and that
e_{2}\in A_{u}
if and only if
e_{3}\in A_{u}
. Thus, the sets

\widetilde{E}(P)\cap A=\left\{\!\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\right\}\!\right\}\cap A\quad\text{ and }\quad\widetilde{E}(P)\cap A_{u}=\left\{\!\left\{e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\right\}\!\right\}\cap A_{u}

both have even cardinality. It follows that
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}(P)={\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(P)=0
as desired.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

Fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
. Let
{\mathscr{I}}
be an intersection invariant associated to
\phi
of either form
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}
or
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
. If
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic walks in
G
then
{\mathscr{I}}(P)={\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime})
.

Proof.

It suffices to consider the case that
P^{\prime}
is formed from
P
by a single insertion, deletion, or substitution operation from
2.1
.

Insertion
. Suppose
P=v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and
P^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v_{i}wv_{i}\cdots v_{k}
. Then
\widetilde{E}(P^{\prime})=\widetilde{E}(P)+\widetilde{E}(v_{i}wv_{i})
, so

{\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{E}(P)+\widetilde{E}(v_{i}wv_{i}))={\mathscr{I}}(P)+{\mathscr{I}}(v_{i}wv_{i})={\mathscr{I}}(P)

by
2.5
(i) and (ii).

Deletion.
If
P^{\prime}
is formed from
P
by deletion, then
P
is formed from
P^{\prime}
by insertion. Thus, the previous argument shows
{\mathscr{I}}(P)={\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime})
.

Substitution.
Suppose
P=v_{0}\cdots v_{i}\cdots v_{k}
and
P^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v^{\prime}_{i}\cdots v_{k}
. Then,

\widetilde{E}(P)+\widetilde{E}(v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i-1})=\widetilde{E}(P^{\prime})+\widetilde{E}(v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}v_{i}v_{i-1}),

so
2.5
(i) and (iii) imply that

{\mathscr{I}}(P)={\mathscr{I}}(P)+{\mathscr{I}}(v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i-1})={\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime})+{\mathscr{I}}(v_{i-1}v_{i}v_{i+1}v_{i}v_{i-1})={\mathscr{I}}(P^{\prime}).\qed

We end this section with a result concerning graphs having few distinct homotopy equivalence classes of walks. We call such graphs
simply connected
, in analogy to the topological setting — a topological space is simply connected if any two continuous paths between the same endpoints are homotopic. (This nomenclature is also suitable because, as shown in
Section

6
, a non-bipartite graph
G
is simply connected if and only if its neighborhood complex
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is a simply connected topological space.)

Definition 2.7
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. We say
G
is
simply connected
if it satisfies the converse of
2.3
— that is, if any two walks of
G
with the same first and last endpoints and same parity are always homotopy equivalent.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism with
G
simply connected. Let
A\subseteq E(G)
be
\phi
-stable and let
{\mathscr{I}}
be an
A
-intersection invariant.

(i)

If
C
and
C^{\prime}
are two odd closed walks of
G
then
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime})
.

(ii)

Suppose
{\mathscr{I}}(C)=1
for some odd cycle
C
of
G
. Then the graph
G\setminus A
is bipartite.

Proof.

(i) Write
C=v_{0}\cdots v_{k-1}v_{0}
and
C^{\prime}=w_{0}\cdots w_{\ell-1}w_{0}
with
k
and
\ell
odd. Because
G
is connected, there is a walk
P=v_{0}x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}w_{0}
from
v_{0}
to
w_{0}
. Let
C^{\prime\prime}
be the concatenation

C^{\prime\prime}=PC^{\prime}P^{-1}=v_{0}x_{1}\cdots x_{m-1}w_{0}w_{1}\cdots w_{\ell-1}w_{0}x_{m-1}\cdots x_{1}v_{0}.

Then
C
and
C^{\prime\prime}
are both walks from
v_{0}
to
v_{0}
of odd lengths
k
and
2m+\ell
, respectively. By
Corollary

2.6
, we have
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime\prime})
. Moreover, we may write the multiset
\widetilde{E}(C^{\prime\prime})
as
\widetilde{E}(C^{\prime\prime})=\widetilde{E}(P)+\widetilde{E}(P)+\widetilde{E}(C^{\prime})
, so

{\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(P)+{\mathscr{I}}(P)+{\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime})\mod 2.

It follows that
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime})
as desired.

(ii) It suffices to show that every odd cycle
C^{\prime}
of
G
contains an edge of
A
. By (i), we have
{\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(C)=1
. If
{\mathscr{I}}={\mathscr{I}}_{A}
then this implies that
|E(C^{\prime})\cap A|
is odd and therefore nonzero. If
{\mathscr{I}}={\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}
for some
u\in V(H)
then we similarly have that
|E(C^{\prime})\cap A_{u}|
is nonzero. Thus, in either case we are able to find an edge of
C^{\prime}
that is in
A
.
∎

3
Bounding Chromatic Number with a
C_{4}
Closure

In this section, we present
Lemma

3.1
, which is a key ingredient in the proof of
Theorem

1.3
. This constitutes the main application of our homotopy theory.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism with
G
simply connected and non-bipartite. Set

s=\min\left\{|\phi(V(C))|:\text{$C$ an odd cycle of $G$}\right\}.

Then there is an edge
f\in E(H)
contained in an odd cycle of length at most
s
for which we have

\chi(G)\leq\max\left(4,\chi\left([f]^{\diamond}_{H}\right)\right).

Lemma

3.1
reduces
Theorem

1.3
to an analysis of
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
in a
C_{2r+1}
-free graph
H
. The condition that
f
is in a short odd cycle of
H
crucially restricts
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
, by forbidding any configuration of
C_{4}
’s which lengthens the short odd cycle to one of length
2r+1
.

We separate the proof of
Lemma

3.1
into two steps. In
Lemma

3.2
, we find an edge
e
of
G
and an
[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
-invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
which satisfy the hypotheses of
2.8(ii)
, showing that
G\setminus[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
is bipartite. In
Lemma

3.3
, we show that if
A=[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
does not induce a bipartite subgraph of
G
, then
\chi(G)\leq\chi(\phi(A))
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
. Suppose
\widetilde{F}
is any multiset of edges of
E(G)
satisfying
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G)}(\widetilde{F})=1
and
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G),u}(\widetilde{F})=0
for all
u\in V(H)
. Then there is an edge
e\in\widetilde{F}
such that
\phi(e)
is in an odd cycle all of whose edges are in
\phi(\widetilde{F})
and moreover
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F})=1
for some
[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
-intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by strong induction on
|\widetilde{F}|
. The statement is vacuously true in the base case
|\widetilde{F}|=0
, because
|\widetilde{F}|\equiv{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G)}(\widetilde{F})=1\pmod{2}
.

When
|\widetilde{F}|>0
, consider the multigraph
X
on
V(H)
with edge multiset
\phi(\widetilde{F})
. The condition
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G),u}(\widetilde{F})=0
implies that every vertex
u
has even multidegree in
X
. Thus, every connected component of
X
has an Eulerian tour, so
\phi(\widetilde{F})
may be written as a sum of edge multisets of closed walks of
H
. The condition
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G)}(\widetilde{F})=1
is equivalent to
|\widetilde{F}|
being odd, so one of these closed walks must have odd length. Call this closed walk
P
, and choose
f\in\widetilde{E}(P)
such that
f
is on an odd cycle in
H
with all edges in
\widetilde{E}(P)\subseteq\phi(\widetilde{F})
. Choose
e\in\widetilde{F}
such that
\phi(e)=f
.

Set
A=[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
. If
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}(\widetilde{F})=1
or
{\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(\widetilde{F})=1
for any
u\in V(H)
then
e
satisfies the conditions of the claim. Suppose that
{\mathscr{I}}_{A}(\widetilde{F})={\mathscr{I}}_{A,u}(\widetilde{F})=0
for all
u\in V(H)
.

Write
\widetilde{F}=\widetilde{F}_{1}+\widetilde{F}_{2}
, where
\widetilde{F}_{1}=\widetilde{F}\cap A
and
\widetilde{F}_{2}=\widetilde{F}\setminus A
. We claim that
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=0
for any intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
associated to
\phi
. First, we handle
B
-intersection invariants
{\mathscr{I}}
such that
B=[e^{\prime}]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
for some
e^{\prime}\in E(G)
, using the fact that
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}^{\prime}\cap B)
for any multiset
\widetilde{F}^{\prime}
. Observe that the equivalence class
B=[e^{\prime}]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
is either equal to or disjoint from
A=[e]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
. If
B=A
then
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}\cap B)={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F})=0
, and if
B\neq A
then
\widetilde{F}_{1}\subseteq A
is disjoint from
B
, so
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1}\cap B)={\mathscr{I}}(\emptyset)=0
. Now, suppose
{\mathscr{I}}
is a
B
-intersection invariant with
B
an arbitrary
\phi
-stable set. We may partition
B
as
\mathcal{P}=\left\{[e^{\prime}]^{\diamond}_{\phi}:e^{\prime}\in B\right\}
, allowing us to write

{\mathscr{I}}_{B}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}}{\mathscr{I}}_{B^{\prime}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=\sum_{B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{P}}{\mathscr{I}}_{B^{\prime},u}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=0.

Hence,
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=0
for any intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
associated to
\phi
.

Now, consider the multiset
\widetilde{F}_{2}
. For any intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
associated to
\phi
, we have
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})=0
, so
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{2})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{1})+{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{2})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F})
. In particular, we have
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G)}(\widetilde{F}_{2})={\mathscr{I}}_{E(G)}(\widetilde{F})=1
and
{\mathscr{I}}_{E(G),u}(\widetilde{F}_{2})={\mathscr{I}}_{E(G),u}(\widetilde{F})=0
for all
u\in V(H)
. Applying the inductive hypothesis to
\widetilde{F}_{2}
yields an edge
e^{\prime}\in\widetilde{F}_{2}
such that
\phi(e^{\prime})
is on an odd cycle all of whose edges are in
\phi(\widetilde{F}_{2})
and furthermore
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{2})=1
for some
[e^{\prime}]^{\diamond}_{\phi}
-intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
. Because
\phi(\widetilde{F})\supseteq\phi(\widetilde{F}_{2})
and
{\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F})={\mathscr{I}}(\widetilde{F}_{2})
, it follows that the edge
e^{\prime}
satisfies the desired conditions.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Fix a graph homomorphism
\phi:G\to H
with
G
simply connected. Suppose
E(G)=A\sqcup B
is a partition of
E(G)
into two
\phi
-stable sets and let
G_{A}
and
G_{B}
be the corresponding edge-induced subgraphs of
G
. Suppose that
G_{A}
is connected and non-bipartite. Then
\chi(G)\leq\chi(\phi(G_{A}))
.

Proof.

Our proof will show the following two statements.

(i)

Every walk
P
in
G
with all edges in
B
and with endpoints
v,v^{\prime}\in V(G_{A})
has even length and its endpoints satisfy
\phi(v)=\phi(v^{\prime})
.

(ii)

Let
\gamma_{0}:V(G_{A})\to[r]
be any proper
r
-coloring of
G_{A}
such that
\gamma_{0}(v)=\gamma_{0}(v^{\prime})
whenever
\phi(v)=\phi(v^{\prime})
. Then
\gamma_{0}
can be extended to a proper
r
-coloring
\gamma
of
G
.

Our desired result follows immediately from (ii) by taking
r=\chi(\phi(G_{A}))
and letting
\gamma_{0}
be the pullback of any
r
-coloring of
\phi(G_{A})
.

We begin by showing (i). Because
G_{A}
is non-bipartite, there is an odd cycle
C\subseteq G_{A}
. Because
E(C)\subseteq A
is disjoint from
B
, we have
{\mathscr{I}}_{B}(C)={\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(C)=0
for any
u\in V(H)
. By
2.8(i)
, we have
{\mathscr{I}}_{B}(C^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(C^{\prime})=0
for any odd closed walk
C^{\prime}
in
G
.

Let
P=w_{0}\cdots w_{k}
be any walk in
G
with all edges in
B
and with
w_{0},w_{k}\in V(G_{A})
. Because
G_{A}
is connected and contains an odd cycle, there are both odd and even walks between any two vertices of
V(G_{A})
. Pick a walk
P^{\prime}=w_{k}w_{k+1}\cdots w_{\ell-1}w_{0}
such that the concatenation
C^{\prime}=PP^{\prime}=w_{0}\cdots w_{\ell-1}w_{0}
has odd length.

We have
0={\mathscr{I}}_{B}(C^{\prime})\equiv|E(C^{\prime})\cap B|=k\pmod{2}
, so
P
has even length. Moreover, for any
u\in V(H)
, each internal vertex
w_{i}
(
0<i<k
) contributes either 0 or 2 to
|\widetilde{E}(P)\cap B_{u}|
, so

{\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(C^{\prime})={\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(P)\equiv\left|\left\{\!\left\{w_{0},w_{k}\right\}\!\right\}\cap\phi^{-1}(u)\right|\mod 2.

Thus, the condition that
{\mathscr{I}}_{B,u}(C^{\prime})=0
for all
u\in V(H)
implies that
\phi(w_{0})=\phi(w_{k})
. This completes the proof of (i).

We now use (i) to show (ii). Extend
\gamma_{0}
to a coloring
\gamma:V(G)\to[r]
as follows. For each vertex
v\in V(G)
, choose any walk
P=vv_{1}\cdots v_{k}
with all edges in
B
and with
v_{k}\in V(G_{A})
. Set

\gamma(v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\gamma_{0}(v_{k})&\text{if $k$ is even,}\\
\text{anything in }[r]\setminus\gamma_{0}(v_{k})&\text{if $k$ is odd.}\end{array}\right.

We claim that the choice of
\gamma(v)
is independent of the choice of
P
. Let
P^{\prime}=vw_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}
be another walk from
v
with all edges in
B
and with
w_{\ell}\in V(G_{A})
. Then the concatenation

P^{-1}P^{\prime}=v_{k}\cdots v_{1}vw_{1}\cdots w_{\ell}

satisfies the hypotheses of (i). In particular, (i) implies that
\phi(v_{k})=\phi(w_{\ell})
and that the length
k+\ell
of
P^{-1}P^{\prime}
is even, so
\gamma_{0}(v_{k})=\gamma_{0}(w_{\ell})
and
k\equiv\ell\pmod{2}
. It follows that
\gamma(v)
does not depend on the path
P
chosen, and thus the coloring
\gamma
is well-defined.

We now show that
\gamma
is a proper coloring of
G
. For vertices
v\in V(G_{A})
, we have that
\gamma(v)=\gamma_{0}(v)
, by choosing
P
to be the length-0 walk
v
. Because
\gamma_{0}
is a proper coloring of
G_{A}
, it follows that
\gamma(v)\neq\gamma(v^{\prime})
for any edge
vv^{\prime}\in A
. Now, consider an edge
vv^{\prime}\in B
. Pick a walk
P=vv_{1}\cdots v_{k}
with each edge in
B
and with
v_{k}\in V(G_{A})
, and let
P^{\prime}=(v^{\prime}v)P=v^{\prime}vv_{1}\cdots v_{k}
. Because the walks
P
and
P^{\prime}
have lengths of opposite parities, one of
\gamma(v)
and
\gamma(v^{\prime})
is equal to
\gamma_{0}(v_{k})
and the other is in
[r]\setminus\gamma_{0}(v_{k})
, implying that
\gamma(v)\neq\gamma(v^{\prime})
. Thus,
\gamma
is a proper
r
-coloring of
G
that extends
\gamma_{0}
, completing the proof of (ii).
∎

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

Let
C
be an odd cycle of
G
with
|\phi(V(C))|=s
. By
Lemma

3.2
applied to the edge (multi)set

, there is an edge
e
of
C
with

on an odd cycle in
\phi(C)
, which odd cycle has at most

vertices. Moreover, setting

, the lemma yields an

-intersection invariant
{\mathscr{I}}
with
{\mathscr{I}}(C)=1
. We take

.

Let

. By

,

. If

If the subgraph of

.
∎

4
Proof of Main Theorem

We may now complete the proof of
Theorem

1.3
. As mentioned at the beginning of
Section

3
, the proof can be reduced to an analysis of
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
, where
f
is an edge on a short odd cycle in the
C_{2r+1}
-free graph
H
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
relies on
Lemmas

4.1
and
4.2
, two intermediate results concerning the structure of
C_{2r+1}
-free graphs. We prove
Theorem

1.3
using these lemmas, and subsequently prove
Lemma

4.1
in
Section

4.1
and
Lemma

4.2
in
Section

4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Fix
r\geq 2
. Suppose
H
is a
C_{2r+1}
-free graph. Let
C
be a shortest odd cycle of
H
and let
f
be an edge of
C
. If
|C|<2r+1
then every edge in
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
is on an odd cycle of length less than
2r+1
containing at least one vertex of
C
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Fix
r\geq 1
. Let
H
be a
C_{2r+1}
-free graph and
v
a vertex of
H
such that every vertex of
H
is within distance
r
of
v
. Then
\chi(H)\leq 4r
.

We now prove
Theorem

1.3
, which is restated here for the reader’s convenience.

Theorem
1.3
.

Let
G
be a simply connected graph and fix
r\geq 2
. Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism such that
H
is
C_{2r+1}
-free, and suppose further that some odd cycle
C
of
G
satisfies
|\phi(V(C))|\leq 2r+2
. Then
\chi(G)<8r^{2}
.

Proof.

By hypothesis,
G
contains an odd cycle and is therefore not bipartite. By
Lemma

3.1
, there is an edge
f\in E(H)
contained in an odd cycle of length at most
2r+2
such that
\chi(G)\leq\max\left(4,\chi\left([f]^{\diamond}_{H}\right)\right)
. Because
H
is
C_{2r+1}
-free, this odd cycle has length at most
2r-1
.

Let
C
be a shortest cycle among those odd cycles of
H
which contain an edge of
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
. By the above reasoning, we have
|C|\leq 2r-1
. Let
f^{\prime}
be an edge in
E(C)\cap[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
. Let
H_{1}
be the subgraph of
H
induced by the edge set
E(C)\cup[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
.

Observe that
H_{1}\setminus[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
is acyclic, as its edge set is a subset of
E(C)\setminus\{f^{\prime}\}
. Hence, every cycle of
H_{1}
contains an edge of
[f]^{\diamond}_{H}
. This implies that
C
is a shortest odd cycle of
H_{1}
. Applying
Lemma

4.1
in
H_{1}
, we see that every edge of
[f^{\prime}]^{\diamond}_{H_{1}}
is on an odd cycle of length at most
2r-1
containing at least one vertex of
C

For each vertex

We begin the proof of

Proposition 4.6
.

Let

be odd cycles in

We find it convenient to prove

if every vertex has even degree; we note that an Eulerian graph need not be connected. Similarly, say that a set of edges

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
H
be a graph and let

be a shortest odd cycle in
H
. Let
X,Y\subseteq E(H)
be Eulerian edge sets with
|X|\equiv|Y|\pmod{2}
. Partition
X=X_{1}\sqcup X_{2}
, where
X_{1}=X\setminus E(C)
and
X_{2}=X\cap E(C)
, and partition
Y=Y_{1}\sqcup Y_{2}
analogously. Then

|Y|\leq|X|+2|Y_{1}\setminus X_{1}|.

Proof.

We first observe that for any sets
S
and
T
, we have

|S|-|T|+|S\sd T|=|S|-|T|+|S\cup T|-|S\cap T|=\left(|S|-|S\cap T|\right)+\left(|S\cup T|-|T|\right)=2|S\setminus T|.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle|Y|-|X|

\displaystyle=|Y_{1}|+|Y_{2}|-|X_{1}|-|X_{2}|

\displaystyle=\left(|Y_{1}|-|X_{1}|\right)-\left(|X_{2}|-|Y_{2}|\right)

\displaystyle=\left(|Y_{1}|-|X_{1}|+|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|\right)-\left(|X_{2}|-|Y_{2}|+|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}|\right)-\left(|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|-|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}|\right)

\displaystyle=2|Y_{1}\setminus X_{1}|-2|X_{2}\setminus Y_{2}|-\left(|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|-|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}|\right).

We now consider the last term. We have

|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|-|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}|=|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|+|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}\sd E(C)|-|C|=|X\sd Y\sd E(C)|-|C|.

Let

. The symmetric difference of Eulerian sets is Eulerian, so

is Eulerian. Additionally,

is odd because

. It follows that

, because every odd-cardinality Eulerian set contains the edge set of an odd cycle, and

is a shortest odd cycle in

. Thus,
|X_{1}\sd Y_{1}|-|X_{2}\sd Y_{2}|=|Z|-|C|\geq 0
. It follows that

|Y|-|X|\leq 2|Y_{1}\setminus X_{1}|-2|Y_{2}\setminus X_{2}|
,
as desired.
∎

4.2
Proof of

4.2

Given a graph

and a vertex

of

denote the set of vertices of

at distance

from

. We show that if

is a

-free graph and

Our strategy is motivated by Thomassen

-free graph

— by separately considering the chromatic numbers of

and

. We remark that, if

-free graph with minimum degree

To bound the chromatic number of each neighborhood, we use the following stronger property.

Definition 4.10

A graph

has a vertex of degree at most

.

It is well-known that any

-degenerate.

Lemma 4.11

. Let

-free graph and

We proceed by contradiction. Suppose

Because
\alpha^{s}
is constant on
W
, it follows by induction that
\alpha^{s-1},\alpha^{s-2},\ldots,\alpha^{0}
are all constant on
W
. However, the identity function
\alpha^{0}:N_{s}(v)\to N_{s}(v)
is not constant on
W
, because the minimum degree condition implies that
W
contains at least
2r+1>1
distinct vertices. This is the desired contradiction.
∎

Proof of
Lemma

4.2
.

Lemma

4.11
implies that for each
s\leq r
, the induced subgraph
H[N_{s}(v)]
is
(2r-1)
-degenerate and thus
2r
-colorable. Additionally, observe that if
|s-t|\geq 2
then the sets
N_{s}(v)
and
N_{t}(v)
are disconnected. Thus, we may
(4r)
-color
H
by using
2r
colors to color the set

N_{0}(v)\cup N_{2}(v)\cup\cdots\cup N_{2\lfloor r/2\rfloor}

of vertices at even distance from
v
, and using a disjoint set of
2r
colors for the set of vertices at odd distance from
v
.
∎

5
The Borsuk Graph

Having proven
Theorem

1.3
, we turn our attention to the homomorphism threshold of odd cycles. To prove
1.1
, we consider a sequence of approximations to a Borsuk graph, and use
Theorem

1.3
to show that this family has no finite
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic image.

Let
S^{n}
denote the
n
-dimensional unit sphere. We equip
S^{n}
with the metric
d
measuring distance along the sphere; for example, any point
x\in S^{n}
and its antipode
-x
have distance
d(x,-x)=\pi
. We write
B_{\epsilon}(x)\subseteq S^{n}
for the open ball of radius
\epsilon
around any point
x\in S^{n}
. Denote the normalized surface measure of this ball by
\mu(n,\epsilon)
; that is,
\mu(n,\epsilon)
is the fraction of the surface of
S^{n}
covered by a spherical cap of radius
\epsilon
.

As defined in
Section

1.2
, the Borsuk graph
\BG(n,\epsilon)
is an infinite graph on
S^{n}
, with an edge between two points
x,y
if
d(x,-y)<\epsilon
. It is well known that
\BG(n,\epsilon)
has chromatic number at least
n+2
, with equality if
\epsilon
is sufficiently small, and that this fact is equivalent to the Borsuk–Ulam theorem (see
[
9
,
20
]
).
We further note that, if
\epsilon\leq\pi/(2r+1)
for some integer
r
, then
\BG(n,\epsilon)
is
C_{2r+1}
-free. Indeed, the
(2r+1)^{\text{th}}
neighborhood of any vertex
x
is
B_{(2r+1)\epsilon}(-x)
, which only contains
x
when
(2r+1)\epsilon>\pi
.

Consider a family of increasingly good approximations to the
C_{2r+1}
-free graph
\BG\left(n,\frac{\pi}{2r+1}\right)
. The goal of this section is to apply
Theorem

1.3
to show that this family has no finite
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic image. Approximations to Borsuk graphs are formally defined as follows.

Definition 5.1
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and
0<\delta<\epsilon
. Let
V\subseteq S^{n}
be a finite set which is antipodally closed, i.e. for each
v\in V
, its antipode
-v
is also in
V
. Let
G
be the subgraph of
\BG(n,\epsilon)
induced by
V
. If for each
x\in S^{n}
, there is some
v\in V
with
d(v,x)<\delta
, then we say
G
is a
\delta
-approximation
to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
.

Applying
Theorem

1.3
requires three intermediate results. We show that if
\delta
is sufficiently small, then any
\delta
-approximation
G
to
\BG\left(n,\frac{\pi}{2r+1}\right)
has large chromatic number (
5.2
) and is simply connected (
5.6
). Additionally, if
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism with
|H|
sufficiently small in terms of
\delta
, we show that
\phi
acts noninjectively on some
C_{2r+3}
of
G
(
5.5
).

Proposition 5.2
.

Fix
n\geq 1
and
0<\delta<\epsilon/2
. If
G
is a
\delta
-approximation to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
then
\chi(G)\geq n+2
.

Proof.

We will construct a homomorphism
\phi:\BG(n,\epsilon-2\delta)\to G
. Because
\BG(n,\epsilon-2\delta)
has chromatic number at least
n+2
, it will follow that
\chi(G)\geq n+2
.

For each vertex
x\in S^{n}
, let
\phi(x)
be the vertex of
G
nearest to
x
. Note that
d(x,\phi(x))<\delta
for each
x\in S^{n}
, because
G
is a
\delta
-approximation. We check that
\phi
maps edges of
\BG(n,\epsilon-2\delta)
to edges of
G
. Indeed, if
x,y\in S^{n}
satisfy
d(x,-y)<\epsilon-2\delta
then

d(\phi(x),-\phi(y))\leq d(\phi(x),x)+d(x,-y)+d(-y,-\phi(y))<\epsilon,

so
\phi(x)
and
\phi(y)
are adjacent in
G
. It follows that
\phi
is a well-defined homomorphism, and consequently that
\chi(G)\geq\chi(\BG(n,\epsilon-2\delta))\geq n+2
.
∎

Our remaining two intermediate results require us to study walks in Borsuk graphs. Because these walks bounce between almost-antipodes, we introduce the following notation, which will allow us to instead reason about sequences of vertices approximating continuous paths in
S^{n}
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}
be vertices of a Borsuk graph
\BG(n,\epsilon)
satisfying
d(v_{i},v_{i+1})<\epsilon
for all
0\leq i<k
. We write
[v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}]
to denote the walk
v_{0}(-v_{1})v_{2}(-v_{3})\cdots(\pm v_{k})
in
\BG(n,\epsilon)
.

Remark 5.4
.

5.3
partially motivates the requirement that
\delta
-approximations have antipodally closed vertex sets.
Indeed, suppose
G
is a
\delta
-approximation to a Borsuk graph
\BG(n,\epsilon)
, and let
v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}\in V(G)
satisfy
d(v_{i},v_{i+1})<\epsilon
for all
i
. Because
V(G)
is antipodally closed, the walk
[v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}]
of
\BG(n,\epsilon)
is also a walk in
G
.

We now prove our remaining two intermediate results.

Proposition 5.5
.

Fix integers
r,n\geq 1
and let
\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{2r+1}
. For any integer
N
there exists some
\delta=\delta(r,n,N)>0
such that if
G
is a
\delta
-approximation to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
and
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism with
|H|\leq N
then
\phi
is noninjective on some
C_{2r+3}
of
G
.

Proof.

Let
\delta
be a small constant, to be determined, and set
\delta_{1}=(4r+2)\delta
and
\delta_{2}=\epsilon-2\delta
. We first show that if
\delta
is sufficiently small then there are vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in V(G)
with
\phi(v)=\phi(v^{\prime})
and
\delta_{1}\leq d(v,v^{\prime})\leq 2\delta_{2}
. Using this, we then construct a
C_{2r+3}
in
G
containing both
v
and
v^{\prime}
.

Choose some
x_{0}\in S^{n}
and let
W
be a maximal subset of
V(G)\cap B_{\delta_{2}}(x_{0})
for which
d(x,x^{\prime})\geq\delta_{1}
for all
x,x^{\prime}\in W
. We claim that the family
\left\{B_{\delta_{1}+\delta}(x):x\in W\right\}
covers
B_{\delta_{2}-\delta}(x_{0})
. Suppose for the sake of contradiction that some
y\in B_{\delta_{2}-\delta}(x_{0})
had distance at least
\delta_{1}+\delta
from each point of
W
. Then there would be a point
x\in V(G)\setminus W
within distance
\delta
of
y
, so
x
would be in
B_{\delta_{2}}(x_{0})
and have distance at least
\delta_{1}
from each point of
W
. However, this contradicts the maximality of
W
.

We now have that
\left\{B_{\delta_{1}+\delta}(x):x\in W\right\}
covers
B_{\delta_{2}-\delta}(x_{0})
. Summing the measures of each set in the cover, we see that
\mu(n,\delta_{1}+\delta)\times|W|\geq\mu(n,\delta_{2}-\delta)
. As
\delta\to 0
, causing
\delta_{1}\to 0
and
\delta_{2}\to\epsilon
, the ratio
\mu(n,\delta_{2}-\delta)/\mu(n,\delta_{1}+\delta)
goes to infinity. Thus, choosing
\delta
small enough yields

N<\frac{\mu(n,\delta_{2}-\delta)}{\mu(n,\delta_{1}+\delta)}\leq|W|.

By the pigeonhole principle, two vertices
v,v^{\prime}\in W
must satisfy
\phi(v)=\phi(v^{\prime})
, as
H
has only
N
vertices. Moreover, we have
\delta_{1}\leq d(v,v^{\prime})\leq 2\delta_{2}
by definition of
W
.

We now construct a
C_{2r+3}
in
G
containing both
v
and
v^{\prime}
. Let
\theta=d(v,v^{\prime})
. Let
\phi:S^{1}\to S^{n}
be an isometric embedding of the unit circle in
S^{n}
such that
\phi(0)=v
and
\phi(-\theta)=v^{\prime}
, where 0 and
-\theta
refer to the points in
S^{1}
with those radial angles. Define a sequence
x_{0},\ldots,x_{2r+3}
of points in
S^{n}
with

\begin{array}[]{c}x_{0}=\phi(0),\ \ldots,\ x_{i}=\phi\left(\frac{i(\pi-\theta)}{2r+1}\right),\ \ldots,\ x_{2r+1}=\phi(\pi-\theta),\\[5.0pt]
x_{2r+2}=\phi\left(\pi-\frac{\theta}{2}\right),\ x_{2r+3}=\phi(\pi).\end{array}

Observe that for
0\leq i<2r+1
, we have

d(x_{i},x_{i+1})=\frac{\pi-\theta}{2r+1}\leq\frac{\pi-\delta_{1}}{2r+1}=\epsilon-2\delta.

For
i=2r+1
and
i=2r+2
, we also have

d(x_{i},x_{i+1})=\frac{\theta}{2}\leq\delta_{2}=\epsilon-2\delta.

Let
v_{i}
be the vertex of
G
closest to
x_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq 2r+3
. Because
G
is a
\delta
-approximation, it follows that
d(v_{i},v_{i+1})<2\delta+d(x_{i},x_{i+1})\leq\epsilon
for all
i
. Therefore,
P=[v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{2r+3}]
is a walk in
G
.

We observe that
x_{0}=-x_{2r+3}=v
and that
x_{2r+1}=-v^{\prime}
. It follows that
v_{i}=x_{i}
at these three indices, so
P
has the form

[v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{2r+3}]=v_{0}(-v_{1})\cdots(-v_{2r+1})v_{2r+2}(-v_{2r+3})=v(-v_{1})\cdots v^{\prime}v_{2r+2}v.

In particular,
P
is a closed walk of length
2r+3
containing both
v
and
v^{\prime}
. Because
\BG\left(n,\frac{\pi}{2r+1}\right)
contains no odd cycles shorter than
C_{2r+3}
,
P
cannot repeat any vertices. Thus, this is a
C_{2r+3}
on which
\phi
acts noninjectively.
∎

Proposition 5.6
.

Fix any
n\geq 2
and
0<\delta<\epsilon/3
. If
G
is a
\delta
-approximation to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
then
G
is simply connected.

Proof.

Let
P=[v_{0},\ldots,v_{k}]
and
P^{\prime}=[v^{\prime}_{0},\ldots,v^{\prime}_{\ell}]
be walks in
G
with
k\equiv\ell\pmod{2}
and additionally satisfying
v_{0}=v^{\prime}_{0}
and
v_{k}=v^{\prime}_{\ell}
. To show that
G
is simply connected, we must show that
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic in
G
. Our proof extends the discrete walks
P
and
P^{\prime}
to continuous paths
f
and
f^{\prime}
in
S^{n}
, takes a continuous homotopy
F
between
f
and
f^{\prime}
, and lastly discretizes
F
to prove a homotopy equivalence between
P
and
P^{\prime}
.

Let
f:[0,1]\to S^{n}
be a continuous function with
f(\frac{i}{k})=v_{i}
for each
0\leq i\leq k
, such that
f
restricts to a geodesic on each interval
\left[\frac{i}{k},\frac{i+1}{k}\right]
. Analogously, let
f^{\prime}:[0,1]\to S^{n}
be a continuous extension of
P^{\prime}
. Because
S^{n}
is simply connected when
n\geq 2
, there is a continuous homotopy
F:[0,1]^{2}\to S^{n}
between
f
and
f^{\prime}
. That is,
F
is a continuous function with boundary conditions
F(0,-)=f
and
F(1,-)=f^{\prime}
, which also satisfies
F(x,0)=v_{0}
and
F(x,1)=v_{k}
for all
x\in[0,1]
.

Because
F
is a continuous function on a compact domain,
F
is uniformly continuous. Thus, there is
\gamma>0
such that if
x,x^{\prime},y,y^{\prime}\in[0,1]
satisfy
|x-x^{\prime}|<\gamma
and
|y-y^{\prime}|<\gamma
then
d(F(x,y),F(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}))<\delta
. Choose sequences
0=x_{0}\leq x_{1}\leq\dots\leq x_{s}=1
and
0=y_{0}\leq y_{1}\leq\dots\leq y_{t}=1
such that
x_{i+1}-x_{i}<\gamma
for all
0\leq i<s
and
y_{j+1}-y_{j}<\gamma
for all
0\leq j<t
. We additionally require that each of the fractions
\frac{1}{k},\ldots,\frac{k-1}{k}
and
\frac{1}{\ell},\ldots,\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}
occurs at least twice in the sequence
y_{0},\ldots,y_{t}
and that
t\equiv k\equiv\ell\pmod{2}
; these conditions may be achieved by adding more elements to the sequence
y_{0},\ldots,y_{t}
.

We now present a homotopy equivalence between
P
and
P^{\prime}
. We construct walks
Q_{0},\ldots,Q_{s}
of
G
such that
Q_{i}
is a discrete approximation of the continuous path
F(x_{i},-)
. We then show that
Q_{i}
is homotopic to
Q_{i+1}
for all
0\leq i<s
, and that
Q_{0}
is homotopic to
P
and
Q_{s}
is homotopic to
P^{\prime}
.

For all
0\leq i\leq s
and
0\leq j\leq t
, define
w_{i,j}
to be the vertex of
G
closest to
F(x_{i},y_{j})
. Observe that
w_{i,0}=v_{0}
and
w_{i,t}=v_{k}
for all
i
. Moreover, if
|i_{1}-i_{2}|,|j_{1}-j_{2}|\leq 1
then
|x_{i_{1}}-x_{i_{2}}|,|y_{j_{1}}-y_{j_{2}}|<\gamma
, so

d\left(w_{i_{1},j_{1}},w_{i_{2},j_{2}}\right)<d\left(w_{i_{1},j_{1}},F(x_{i_{1}},y_{j_{1}})\right)+d\left(F(x_{i_{1}},y_{j_{1}}),F(x_{i_{2}},y_{j_{2}})\right)+d\left(F(x_{i_{2}},y_{j_{2}},w_{i_{2},j_{2}})\right)<3\delta<\epsilon.

For
0\leq i\leq s
, let
Q_{i}=[w_{i,0},w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,t}]
. The prior inequality implies that
Q_{i}
is in fact a walk of
G
.

We show that
Q_{i}
is homotopic to
Q_{i+1}
via the following sequence of substitution steps.

\displaystyle Q_{i}=

\displaystyle\ [w_{i,0},w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,t}]=[w_{i+1,0},w_{i,1},\ldots,w_{i,t}]\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [w_{i+1,0},w_{i+1,1},w_{i,2},\ldots,w_{i,t}]

\displaystyle\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ \cdots\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [w_{i+1,0},\ldots,w_{i+1,j},w_{i,j+1},\ldots,w_{i,t}]\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ \cdots

\displaystyle\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [w_{i+1,0},\ldots,w_{i+1,t-1},w_{i,t}]=[w_{i+1,0},\ldots,w_{i+1,t}]=Q_{i+1}.

At the
j^{\text{th}}
step, we replace
w_{i,j}
with
w_{i+1,j}
. This is a valid substitution because we have shown that both
w_{i,j}
and
w_{i+1,j}
are within
\epsilon
of the vertices
w_{i+1,j-1}
and
w_{i,j+1}
on either side of the substitution.

Thus, it only remains to show that
Q_{0}
is homotopic to
P
— which, by symmetry, also shows that
Q_{s}
is homotopic to
P^{\prime}
.
Write
w_{j}
for
w_{0,j}
. We claim there are indices
0=j_{0}<j_{1}<\cdots<j_{k}=t
such that, for all
0\leq i\leq k
, we have
j_{i}\equiv i\pmod{2}
and
w_{j_{i}}=v_{i}
. For
0<i<k
, such a
j_{i}
exists because of the constraint that
y_{j}=y_{j+1}=\frac{i}{k}
for some index
j
, allowing us to choose
j_{i}\in\{j,j+1\}
of the appropriate parity. Additionally,
j_{k}=t
has the correct parity because our construction stipulates that
t\equiv k\pmod{2}
.

We claim that, for each
i
, the walks
[v_{i},v_{i+1}]
and
[w_{j_{i}},w_{j_{i}+1},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]
are homotopy equivalent.
For any index
j_{i}\leq h\leq j_{i+1}
, notice that

\displaystyle d(v_{i},w_{h})

\displaystyle\leq d(v_{i},f(y_{h}))+d(f(y_{h}),w_{h})\leq d(v_{i},f(y_{h}))+d(f(y_{h}),v_{i+1})=d(v_{i},v_{i+1})<\epsilon.

The second inequality follows because
w_{h}
is the vertex of
G
nearest to
f(y_{h})
, and the subsequent equality holds because
f(y_{h})
is on a geodesic between
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
. Hence, we may perform the following sequence of substitutions on
[w_{j_{i}},w_{j_{i}+1},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]
.

\displaystyle[w_{j_{i}},w_{j_{i}+1},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]=[v_{i},w_{j_{i}+1},w_{j_{i}+2},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [v_{i},v_{i},w_{j_{i}+2},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]

\displaystyle\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [v_{i},v_{i},v_{i},w_{j_{i}+3},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ \cdots\ \xrightarrow{\text{\ sub\ }}\ [v_{i},\ldots,v_{i},w_{j_{i+1}}]=[v_{i},\ldots,v_{i},v_{i+1}]

Repeated deletions then transform this walk (which has odd length
j_{i+1}-j_{i}
) into the single edge
[v_{i},v_{i+1}]
, thus showing that
[w_{j_{i}},w_{j_{i}+1},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]
and
[v_{i},v_{i+1}]
are homotopic walks. One may show that their antipodal images
[-v_{i},-v_{i+1}]
and
[-w_{j_{i}},-w_{j_{i}+1},\ldots,-w_{j_{i+1}}]
are homotopic by performing an analogous sequence of substitution and deletion operations.

Observe that
P
may be written as a concatenation of edges
(-1)^{i}[v_{i},v_{i+1}]=[(-1)^{i}v_{i},(-1)^{i}v_{i+1}]
and
Q_{0}
as a concatenation of subwalks
(-1)^{i}[w_{j_{i}},\ldots,w_{j_{i+1}}]
. The
k
terms in each concatenation are pairwise homotopic, yielding a homotopy equivalence between
P
and
Q_{0}
. An analogous argument shows
Q_{s}
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic, completing the proof that
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic.
∎

Combining
5.2
,
5.5
and
5.6
with
Theorem

1.3
lets us lower-bound
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Fix
r\geq 2
, and set
n=8r^{2}-2
and
\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{2r+1}
. Let
\epsilon/3>\delta_{1}>\delta_{2}>\ldots
be a sequence monotonically decreasing to 0 and let
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of graphs such that
G_{i}
is a
\delta_{i}
-approximation to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
. Then the family
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots
has no finite
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic image.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary there were a finite
C_{2r+1}
-free graph
H
together with graph homomorphisms
\phi_{k}:G_{k}\to H
for each
k
. Applying
5.5
with
N=|H|
, there is
k
sufficiently large that
\phi_{k}
is noninjective on some
C_{2r+3}
of
G_{k}
. Moreover,
G_{k}
is simply connected by
5.6
.

Applying
Theorem

1.3
to
\phi_{k}:G_{k}\to H
, we see that
\chi(G_{k})<8r^{2}
. However,
\chi(G_{k})\geq n+2=8r^{2}
by

, yielding the desired contradiction.
∎

Let
r
,
n
, and
\epsilon
be as in
Theorem

5.7
. To prove
1.1
, we apply
Theorem

5.7
to a sequence of random graphs approximating
\BG(n,\epsilon)
. This lower-bounds
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
by
\mu(\epsilon,n)
, the fraction of
S^{n}
covered by a ball of radius
\epsilon
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Fix
r\geq 2
, and set
n=8r^{2}-2
and
\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{2r+1}
. Then
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})\geq\mu(n,\epsilon)
.

Proof.

Fix a sequence
\epsilon/4>\delta_{1}>\delta_{2}>\cdots
monotonically decreasing to zero. We construct
\delta_{k}
-approximations
G_{k}
to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
such that
\delta(G_{k})/|G_{k}|
approaches
\mu(n,\epsilon)
.

Let
N
be a sufficiently large integer. Choose
N
points
x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}\in S^{n}
uniformly at random, and let
G_{k}
be the subgraph of
\BG(n,\epsilon)
induced by the vertex set
V(G_{k})=\{x_{1},-x_{1},\ldots,x_{N},-x_{N}\}
. With high probability as
N\to\infty
, the graph
G_{k}
will be a
\delta_{k}
-approximation to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
. Furthermore, any vertex
v\in V(G)
has expected degree
1+\mu(n,\epsilon)(2N-2)
. A Chernoff bound implies that, with high probability as
N\to\infty
, every vertex
v\in V(G_{k})
satisfies

\deg(v)=(2+o(1))\mu(n,\epsilon)\cdot N=(1+o(1))\mu(n,\epsilon)\cdot|G_{k}|.

Thus, taking
N
large enough, this construction yields a
\delta_{k}
-approximation
G_{k}
to
\BG(n,\epsilon)
with minimum degree

\delta(G_{k})\geq(1-\delta_{k})\mu(n,\epsilon)\cdot|G_{k}|.

By
Theorem

5.7
, the family
G_{1},G_{2},\ldots
has no finite
C_{2r+1}
-free homomorphic image. Thus,

\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})\geq\limsup_{k\to\infty}\frac{\delta(G_{k})}{|G_{k}|}\geq\mu(n,\epsilon).\qed

Remark 5.9
.

The lower bound
\mu\left(8r^{2}-2,\frac{\pi}{2r+1}\right)
obtained in
Corollary

5.8
is
r^{-(8+o(1))r^{2}}
as
r\to\infty
.

6
The Discrete Fundamental Group

Returning to our homotopy theory, we introduce a graph-theoretic analogue of the fundamental group. Using the discrete fundamental group, we develop an unexpected relationship between our homotopy theory and Lovász’s neighborhood complex.

We briefly recall the definition of the fundamental group in algebraic topology. Let
X
be a path-connected topological space. A
loop
based at a point
x_{0}\in X
is a continuous map
L:[0,1]\to X
with
L(0)=L(1)=x_{0}
. Two such loops are
homotopic
if one loop may be continuously deformed into the other while keeping the basepoint
x_{0}
fixed. Let
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
be the set of homotopy classes of loops in
X
based at
x_{0}
. It is well known (see
[
13
, §1.1]
) that
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})
forms a group under concatenation, and that
\pi_{1}(X,x_{0})\simeq\pi_{1}(X,x_{1})
for any two points
x_{0},x_{1}\in X
. Due to this isomorphism, the fundamental group is often written simply as
\pi_{1}(X)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Given a vertex
v_{0}\in V(G)
, the
discrete fundamental group

\pi_{1}(G,v_{0})
consists of those closed walks in
G
with both endpoints at
v_{0}
, up to homotopy equivalence. This forms a group under concatenation, where the inverse of a walk
v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{k-1}v_{0}
is given by
v_{0}v_{k-1}\cdots v_{1}v_{0}
. Let
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{0})
be the subgroup of
\pi_{1}(G,v_{0})
comprising closed walks of even length.

We state without proof two results which are proved analogously to elementary statements in algebraic algebraic topology.

Proposition 6.1

(See Proposition 1.5 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph and let
v_{0}
and
v_{1}
be vertices of
G
. There is an isomorphism from
\pi_{1}(G,v_{0})
to
\pi_{1}(G,v_{1})
. Moreover, this isomorphism maps
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{0})
to
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{1})
.

We remark that the proof of
6.1
is also very similar to that of
2.8(i)
.
This proposition allows us to simply notate the discrete fundamental group as
\pi_{1}(G)
, and its subgroup of even walks as
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
.

Proposition 6.2

(See Proposition 1.6 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
G
be a connected graph.
G
is simply connected if and only if
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
is trivial.

We conclude this section by demonstrating a connection between the discrete fundamental group and Lovász’s neighborhood complex.
Given a graph
G
, its
neighborhood complex

{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is a simplicial complex on
V(G)
, with faces corresponding to those subsets
W\subseteq V(G)
which have a common neighbor. For example, the neighborhood complex of the complete graph
K_{n}
has a face corresponding to each proper subset of
[n]
, so
{\mathcal{N}}(K_{n})
is the boundary of an
(n-1)
-dimensional simplex.

Notice that
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is a connected topological space if and only if
G
is connected and non-bipartite. In this regime, the fundamental group of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is in fact isomorphic to
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
. As a corollary,
G
is a simply connected graph if and only if
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
is a simply connected topological space, i.e. if and only if
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{N}}(G))
is trivial.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G
be a connected non-bipartite graph. Then
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{N}}(G))\simeq\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G)
.

The proof of
6.3
uses the
edge-path group
, an alternate representation of the fundamental group of a simplicial complex (see e.g.
[
1
, §6.6]
). Let
X
be a simplicial complex on vertex set
V
. An
edge path
of
X
is a sequence
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
of vertices such that
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are contained in the same simplex of
X
for all
i<k
. We say two edge paths are
equivalent
if we may transform one into the other via a finite sequence of steps of the following form:

(Ins1)

Given an edge path
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0\leq i\leq k
, replace
v_{i}
with
v_{i}v_{i}
.

(Del1)

Given an edge path
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0\leq i<k
satisfying
v_{i}=v_{i+1}
, replace
v_{i}v_{i+1}
with
v_{i}
.

(Ins2)

Given an edge path
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
, an index
0\leq i<k
, and a vertex
v^{\prime}
such that
v_{i},v^{\prime},v_{i+1}
are contained in the same simplex of
X
, replace
v_{i}v_{i+1}
with
v_{i}v^{\prime}v_{i+1}
.

(Del2)

Given an edge path
v_{0}\cdots v_{k}
and an index
0<i<k
such that
v_{i},v_{i+1},v_{i+2}
are contained in the same simplex of
X
, replace
v_{i}v_{i+1}v_{i+2}
with
v_{i}v_{i+2}
.

Given a vertex
v_{0}
of
X
, the
edge-path group

{\mathcal{E}}(X,v_{0})
consists of equivalence classes of edge paths starting and ending at
v_{0}
, with concatenation as the group operation. It is well known that
{\mathcal{E}}(X,v_{0})
is isomorphic to
\pi_{1}(X)
. We prove
6.3
by constructing an isomorphism between
{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{N}}(G),v_{0})
and
\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{0})
for some vertex
v_{0}\in V(G)
.

Proof of
6.3
.

We define a map
\phi
from {even closed walks of
G
based at
v_{0}
} to {edge paths in
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
based at
v_{0}
}. We show that
\phi
projects to a well-defined group homomorphism
\overline{\phi}:\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{0})\to{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{N}}(G),v_{0})
, and that
\overline{\phi}
is an isomorphism.

Given an even closed walk
P=v_{0}\cdots v_{2k-1}v_{0}
, let
\phi(P)
be the edge path
v_{0}v_{2}v_{4}\cdots v_{2k-2}v_{0}
. We show that
\phi
maps homotopic closed walks to equivalent edge paths, by checking that the equivalence class of
\phi(P)
is preserved by the three homotopy operations described in
2.1
.

(Ins)

Let
P^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v_{i}wv_{i}\cdots v_{2k-1}v_{0}
be formed from
P
by a single insertion. If
i
is even, then
\phi(P^{\prime})
is obtained by replacing
v_{i}
by
v_{i}v_{i}
in
\phi(P)
, as in (Ins1). If
i
is odd then
\phi(P^{\prime})
is obtained by replacing
v_{i-1}v_{i+1}
by
v_{i-1}wv_{i+1}
in
\phi(P)
. The vertices
v_{i-1}
,
w
, and
v_{i+1}
have a common neighbor
v_{i}
, so they form a simplex of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
. Thus,
\phi(P^{\prime})
is equivalent to
\phi(P)
by (Ins2).

(Del)

If
P^{\prime}
is formed from
P
by a single deletion step, then
P
is formed from
P^{\prime}
by a single insertion step. By the above argument,
\phi(P)
is equivalent to
\phi(P^{\prime})
.

(Sub)

Let
P^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v_{i-1}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i+1}\cdots v_{2k-1}v_{0}
be formed from
P
by a single substitution. If
i
is odd then
\phi(P^{\prime})=\phi(P)
. If
i
is even then
\phi(P^{\prime})
is formed by replacing
v_{i-2}v_{i}v_{i+2}
by
v_{i-2}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i+2}
in
\phi(P)
. Then
\phi(P)
and
\phi(P^{\prime})
are equivalent by the two steps

\displaystyle\phi(P)=v_{0}\cdots v_{i-2}v_{i}v_{i+2}\cdots v_{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{\ Ins2\ }}v_{0}\cdots v_{i-2}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}v_{i+2}\cdots v_{0}

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{\text{\ Del2\ }}v_{0}\cdots v_{i-2}v^{\prime}_{i}v_{i+2}\cdots v_{0}=\phi(P^{\prime}),

which are permissible because
\{v_{i-2},v^{\prime}_{i},v_{i}\}
and
\{v^{\prime}_{i},v_{i},v_{i+2}\}
are simplices of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
with common neighbors
v_{i-1}
and
v_{i+1}
, respectively.

It follows that
\phi
maps homotopic closed walks to equivalent edge paths, yielding a well-defined group homomorphism
\overline{\phi}:\pi_{1}^{\textsc{even}}(G,v_{0})\to{\mathcal{E}}({\mathcal{N}}(G),v_{0})
.

We claim
\overline{\phi}
is surjective. Let
Q=v_{0}v_{2}v_{4}\cdots v_{2k-2}v_{0}
be an edge path of
G
. Because
v_{2i}
and
v_{2i+2}
are contained in the same simplex of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
, they share a common neighbor
v_{2i+1}
. Thus, there is a closed walk
P=v_{0}v_{1}\cdots v_{2k-1}v_{0}
with
\phi(P)=Q
.

Lastly, we prove that
\overline{\phi}
is injective. We show that if
P
and
P^{\prime}
are closed walks with
\phi(P)=Q
equivalent to
\phi(P^{\prime})=Q^{\prime}
, then
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic. It suffices to consider the cases that
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are related by (Ins1) or (Ins2), as (Del1) and (Del2) are the respective inverse transformations. Write
Q=v_{0}\cdots v_{k-1}v_{0}
and
P=v_{0}w_{0}\cdots v_{k-1}w_{k-1}v_{0}
.

(Ins1)

Suppose
Q^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v_{i}v_{i}\cdots v_{k-1}v_{0}
is formed from
Q
by (Ins1). Then
P^{\prime}
takes the form

P^{\prime}=v_{0}w^{\prime}_{0}v_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots v_{i}w^{\prime}_{i}v_{i}w^{\prime\prime}_{i}v_{i+1}w^{\prime}_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}w^{\prime}_{k-1}v_{0}.

Observe that
P
is homotopic to the closed walk

P^{\prime\prime}=v_{0}w^{\prime}_{0}v_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots v_{i}w^{\prime}_{i}v_{i+1}w^{\prime}_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}w^{\prime}_{k-1}v_{0}

via a sequence of substitutions and that
P^{\prime}
may be obtained from
P^{\prime\prime}
by a single insertion. Thus,
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic closed walks.

(Ins2)

Suppose
Q^{\prime}=v_{0}\cdots v_{i}v^{\prime}v_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}v_{0}
is formed from
Q
by (Ins2). Then
P^{\prime}
takes the form

P^{\prime}=v_{0}w^{\prime}_{0}v_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots v_{i}w^{\prime}_{i}v^{\prime}w^{\prime\prime}_{i}v_{i+1}w^{\prime}_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}w^{\prime}_{k-1}v_{0}.

Because
v_{i},v^{\prime},v_{i+1}
are in the same simplex of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
, and thus share a common neighbor
x
. By sequences of substitutions,
P
is homotopic to the closed walk

P^{\prime\prime}=v_{0}w^{\prime}_{0}v_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots v_{i}xv_{i+1}w^{\prime}_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}w^{\prime}_{k-1}v_{0}.

and
P^{\prime}
is homotopic to

P^{\prime\prime\prime}=v_{0}w^{\prime}_{0}v_{1}w^{\prime}_{1}\cdots v_{i}xv^{\prime}xv_{i+1}w^{\prime}_{i+1}\cdots v_{k-1}w^{\prime}_{k-1}v_{0}.

Because
P^{\prime\prime\prime}
is obtained from
P^{\prime\prime}
via a single deletion, the closed walks
P
and
P^{\prime}
are homotopic.

It follows that any closed walks
P
and
P^{\prime}
with
\overline{\phi}(P)=\overline{\phi}(P^{\prime})
are homotopic. Hence,
\phi
is injective, completing the proof that
\overline{\phi}
is a group isomorphism.
∎

Combining
6.2
and
6.3
yields another characterization of simply connected graphs which is equivalent to
2.7
for non-bipartite graphs.

Corollary 6.4
.

Suppose
G
is a connected non-bipartite graph. Then,
G
is simply connected if and only if
\pi_{1}({\mathcal{N}}(G))
is trivial.

7
Concluding Remarks

We discuss possible extensions of
Theorem

1.3
, as well as its limitations when used to lower-bound
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
. Lastly, we conclude with some open questions of a more topological flavor.

7.1
Bounding
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})

As mentioned in
4.3
, our proof of
Theorem

1.3
did not attempt to optimize the constant coefficient in the bound on
\chi(G)
. We conjecture that under the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.3
,
\chi(G)
is in fact at most
2r
.

Conjecture 7.1
.

Let
G
be a simply connected graph and fix
r\geq 2
. Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism such that
H
is
C_{2r+1}
-free, and suppose further that some odd cycle
C
of
G
satisfies
|\phi(V(C))|\leq 2r+2
. Then
\chi(G)\leq 2r
.

We cannot guarantee
\chi(G)<2r
, as shown by the following construction. Let
G
be a large simply-connected
C_{2r+1}
-free graph with
\chi(G)=2r
. Such a graph may be obtained by taking a sufficiently good approximation to a Borsuk graph
\BG\left(2r-2,\epsilon\right)
, with
\epsilon\leq\frac{\pi}{2r+1}
chosen small enough that the graph
G
has chromatic number exactly
2r
. The graph
G
, together with any homomorphism
\phi:G\to K_{2r}
, satisfies the hypotheses of
Theorem

1.3
.

It would be interesting to understand how
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
grows as a function of
r
. Even if we could show
7.1
, this would imply a lower bound of
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})\geq r^{-(2+o(1))r}
, which remains far from the upper bound
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})\leq\frac{1}{2r+1}
shown by Ebsen and Schacht
[
7
]
. One specific question in this direction is to determine whether the best possible lower bound on
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
is given by a sequence of approximations to a Borsuk graph, i.e. whether the exact value of
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})
takes a form similar to the lower bound of
Corollary

5.8
.

Question 7.2
.

Fix
r\geq 2
and set
\epsilon=\frac{\pi}{2r+1}
. Is
\delta_{\hom}(C_{2r+1})=\mu(n,\epsilon)
for some
n
? If so, is
n=2r-1
?

7.2
Topological Open Problems

We remark that
Theorem

1.3
may be generalized from simply connected graphs to any graph
G
with cyclic discrete fundamental group.

Theorem 7.3
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\pi_{1}(G)
cyclic and fix
r\geq 2
. Suppose
\phi:G\to H
is a graph homomorphism such that
H
is
C_{2r+1}
-free, and suppose further that some odd cycle
C
of
G
satisfies
|\phi(V(C))|\leq 2r+2
. Then
\chi(G)<8r^{2}
.

Proof.

We follow the proof of
Theorem

1.3
, with one exception. The condition that
G
is simply connected is used exactly once, to show that
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime\prime})
within the proof of
2.8(i)
. We claim that this step still holds under the alternate hypothesis that
\pi_{1}(G)
is cyclic.

Assume the proof setup of
2.8(i)
. Let
P
be a closed walk generating
\pi_{1}(G,v_{0})
. Because
C
is an odd closed walk based at
v_{0}
, there is
a\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
C
is homotopic to
P^{a}
, the closed walk formed by concatenating
a
copies of
P
. Furthermore, because
C
has odd length and walk parities are preserved by homotopy equivalence, it follows that
a
must be odd. Hence,
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}\left(P^{a}\right)=a\times{\mathscr{I}}(P)={\mathscr{I}}(P)\pmod{2}
. Similarly,
{\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime\prime})={\mathscr{I}}(P)
, yielding that
{\mathscr{I}}(C)={\mathscr{I}}(C^{\prime\prime})
. It follows that
2.8(i)
holds in the more general case that
\pi_{1}(G)
is cyclic. Thus, the proof of
Theorem

1.3
generalizes to all graphs
G
with
\pi_{1}(G)
cyclic. ∎

Because of the interplay between the discrete fundamental group and chromatic number in
Theorem

7.3
, it is natural to ask about thresholds with a more topological nature. Let
G
be an
H
-free graph on
n
vertices, with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq\alpha n
for some fixed
\alpha
. It would be informative to understand the relationship between
G
and various topological properties of
G
, such as the structure of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
or
\pi(G)
. Similar topological properties have been studied for random models of graphs and hypergraphs
[
4
,
14
,
18
]
, but it is not clear how these properties would interact with the
H
-free condition. We ask about one such “topological threshold” explicitly, but any results of this type would be quite interesting.

Question 7.4
.

Fix a graph
H
. Let
\delta_{\cyc}(H)
be the infimum of those
\alpha
such that if
G
is an
H
-free graph with
\delta(G)>\alpha|G|
, then
\pi_{1}(G)
is cyclic. What is the value of
\delta_{\cyc}(H)
?

At first glance, it seems more intuitive to ask for the least minimum bound on
\delta(G)
which implies that
G
is simply connected. However, being simply connected can be too restrictive a property. For example, it is well-known that triangle-free graphs
G
with
\delta(G)>\frac{2}{5}|G|
are bipartite. Conversely, if
G
is a balanced blowup of
C_{5}
, then
\delta(G)=\frac{2}{5}|G|
and
\pi_{1}(G)\simeq\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, determining the threshold
\alpha
above which triangle-free graphs
G
with
\delta(G)>\alpha|G|
are simply connected is fundamentally a problem about bipartite — rather than triangle-free — graphs. Moreover, the generalization of
Theorem

1.3
to
Theorem

7.3
suggests that having cyclic discrete fundamental group is the more germane property.

Looking beyond the fundamental group, it would be valuable to formulate discrete analogues of higher homotopy groups or homology groups. Such constructions would be interesting in their own right, especially if they coincided on a subgroup with the corresponding homology or homotopy group of
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
. It would also be illuminating to understand which properties of a graph
G
— such as its chromatic number — could be determined by analyzing these groups.

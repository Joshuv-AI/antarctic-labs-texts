Well-posedness of martingale problem for SBM with interacting branching
Lina Ji, Jie Xiong, Xu Yang

Abstract
In this paper a martingale problem for super-Brownian motion with interactive branching is derived. The uniqueness of the solution to the martingale problem is obtained by using the pathwise uniqueness of the solution to a corresponding system of SPDEs with proper boundary conditions. The existence of the solution to the martingale problem and the Hölder continuity of the density process are also studied.



Keywords.
super-Brownian motion; interacting branching; function-valued process; stochastic partial differential equation.

AMS Mathematics subject classification (2010):
60H15; 60J68.

1
Introduction and main results

Let
M(\mathbb{R})
be the collection of all finite Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}.
Let
C_{b}^{n}(\mathbb{R})
be the collection of all bounded continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
with bounded derivatives up to
n
th order.
We consider a
M(\mathbb{R})
-valued process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfying the following martingale problem (MP): for
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),
the process

\displaystyle M_{t}(\phi)\equiv\left<X_{t},\phi\right>-\left<X_{0},\phi\right>-\int^{t}_{0}\left<X_{s},\frac{1}{2}\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>ds

(1.1)

is a continuous martingale with

\displaystyle\left<M(\phi)\right>_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\left<X_{s},{\gamma}(\mu_{s},\cdot)\phi^{2}\right>ds,

(1.2)

where
\gamma
is the interacting branching rate depending on the density process. The notation
\left<\nu,\phi\right>
denotes the integral of the function
\phi
with respect to the measure
\nu.
In this paper we assume
X_{0}(dx)=\mu_{0}(x)dx
with
\mu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is the space of functions
f
such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)^{2}dx<\infty.
When
\gamma
is a constant, the process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a super-Brownian motion. In this case the well-posedness of the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) was established by the nonlinear partial differential equation satisfied by its log-Laplace transform. Moreover, a new approach for the well-posedness of the MP was suggested by Xiong
[
21
]
, in which a relationship between the super-Brownian motion and a stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE) satisfied by its corresponding distribution-function-valued process was established. The weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) was also obtained by the strong uniqueness of the solution to the corresponding SPDE in
[
21
]
. See He et. al
[
9
]
for the case of super-Lévy process.

While the superprocesses with interaction are more natural since the branching and spatial motion for many species depend on the processes themselves. When the spatial motion is interactive, the well-posedness of the martingale problem was studied by Donnelley and Kurtz
[
4
]
, see also Perkins
[
15
, Theorem V.5.1]
and Li et. al
[
12
]
. Uniqueness for the historical superprocesses with certain interaction was investigated by Perkins
[
15
]
. Furthermore, the superprocesses with interactive immigration were studied by
[
2
,
7
,
17
]
, see also Li
[
11
, Section 10]
. The well-posedness of the martingale problem for the interactive immigration process was solved by Mytnik and Xiong
[
14
]
. See also
[
22
]
for the well-posedness of the martingale problem for a superprocess with location-dependent branching, interactive immigration mechanism and spatial motion.

However, the hard case for the superprocess with interactive branching was rarely investigated.
We are interested in the case of
\gamma(\mu_{s},x)=\gamma(\mu_{s}(x)),
i.e., the interactive branching mechanism depending on the density process
(\mu_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}.
However, in this case the well-posedness of the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is still an
open problem
. The weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is very difficult to prove. As a first step, throughout this paper we assume
\gamma
satisfies the following condition:

Condition 1.1
.

Fixed integer
n\geq 0.
For
-\infty=a_{0}<a_{1}<\cdots<a_{n}<a_{n+1}=\infty
, let

\displaystyle{\gamma}(\mu_{s},x)=\begin{cases}g_{i}^{2}(\mu_{s}(a_{i+1})),&a_{i}\leq x<a_{i+1},\ i=0,\cdots,n-1,\\
g_{n}^{2}\left(\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}\mu_{s}(y)dy\right),&a_{n}\leq x<\infty,\end{cases}

where
g_{i},\ i=0,\cdots,n
are positive continuous bounded functions from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.

The existence and Hölder continuity of the density process
(\mu_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
are investigated in this paper, which satisfies the following SPDE:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\mu_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta\mu_{t}(x)+\sqrt{\mu_{t}(x)\gamma(\mu_{t},x)}\dot{W}(t,x),

(1.3)

where
\Delta
denotes the one-dimensional Laplacian operator and
\{W(t,x):t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is a time-space Gaussian white noise based on the Lebesgue measure and the dot denotes the derivative in distribution sense. The existence of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) is given by showing the existence of the solution to (
1.3
). Moreover, we show the weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) in Theorem
1.4
. The main idea is to relate the MP with a system of SPDEs, which is satisfied by a sequence of corresponding function-valued processes on intervals. The weak uniqueness of solution to the MP follows from the pathwise uniqueness of the solution to the system of SPDEs, see Section 3. Throughout this paper we always assume that all random variables defined on the same filtered probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathscr{F}_{t},\mathbb{P}).
Let
\mathbb{E}
denote the corresponding expectation.

The main results of this paper without special explanation are stated as below.

(Existence)

(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}

,
1.2

Theorem 1.3
.

(Joint Hölder continuity)
Suppose that

) and
\mu_{0}

(Uniqueness)
Assume

\displaystyle|g_{n}(x)-g_{n}(y)|\leq K|x-y|^{\beta},\qquad x,y\geq 0

for some constant

Then the weak uniqueness of the solution to the MP (
1.1
,
1.2
) holds.

Taking

\displaystyle\langle M(\phi)\rangle_{t}=\int_{0}^{t}\gamma(\langle X_{s},1\rangle)\langle X_{s},\phi^{2}\rangle ds.

The well-posedness of the MP (

Lemma 2.1
.

The martingale defined in (

,

\displaystyle M_{t}(\phi)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi(x)M(ds,dx),\qquad t\geq 0,\ \phi\in C_{b}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big[\gamma(X_{s},z)\delta_{z}(dx)\delta_{z}(dy)\big]X_{s}(dz)

Proof.

Notice that
\gamma
is bounded by Condition
1.1

1.1
,
1.2
), one can see that
\mathbb{E}[\left<X_{t},1\right>]=\left<X_{0},1\right><\infty.
For each
n\geq 1

by

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}\Big[\int_{0}^{n}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\gamma(\mu_{s},x)\phi(x)X_{s}(dx)\Big]

with
\phi\in B(\mathbb{R})^{+}.
Then
\Gamma_{n}(\phi)\leq K\int_{0}^{n}\mathbb{E}[\left<X_{s},1\right>]ds
is bounded for each
n\geq 1.
The rest proof follows by changing

and
H(z,{\mbox{\rm d}}\nu)=0
in the proof of Li
[
11

. We omit it here.
∎

Let
T_{t}(x,dy)

which is absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
(\mu_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}}
is a solution to (
1.3
) with
\mu_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Then for every

we have
\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle]<\infty.

Proof.

By (
1.3

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle=\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}W(ds,dx).

Notice that
\sqrt{x}\leq x+1
for any
x\geq 0.
By Hölder’s inequality one can check that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\langle\mu_{t},1\rangle]\!\!

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle+\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\mu_{s}(x)\gamma(\mu_{s},x)}W(ds,dx)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle\langle\mu_{0},1\rangle+K\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}(x)dsdx\right)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\!\!

\displaystyle K+K\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\left[\langle\mu_{s},1\rangle\right]ds.

The result follows by Gronwall’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
\mu_{0}
satisfies
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{0}(x)^{2p}J(x)dx<\infty

. Then for every

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T,m\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{t}^{m}(x)^{2p}J(x)dx\right]<\infty.

Proof.

Using the convolution form, the solution
\mu_{t}^{m}(x)

2.2

\displaystyle\mu_{t}^{m}(x)\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<\mu_{0},p_{t}(x-\cdot)\right>

(2.6)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{t_{k-1}\wedge t}^{t_{k}\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\gamma(\mu_{t_{k-1}}^{m},z)}G_{m}(\mu_{s}^{m}(z))p_{t-s}(x-z)W(ds,dz).

By Hölder’s inequality we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2}p_{t-s}(x-z)^{2}dz\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2}p_{t-s}(x-z)dz\right|^{p}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2}p_{t-s}(x-z)dz\right|^{p}\cdot\left|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\right|^{p/q}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2p}p_{t-s}(x-z)dz

By the above inequality, (

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)dx\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m}\int_{t_{k-1}\wedge t}^{t_{k}\wedge t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\sqrt{\gamma(\mu_{t_{k-1}}^{m},z)}G_{m}(\mu_{s}^{m}(z))p_{t-s}(x-z)W(ds,dz)\right|^{2p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)dx\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{m}(\mu_{s}^{m}(z))^{2}p_{t-s}(x-z)^{2}dsdz\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\leq K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)dx\left|\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2}+1)p_{t-s}(x-z)^{2}dz\right|^{p}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad{\leq K+K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(x)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2p}p_{t-s}(x-z)dz\right]}

\displaystyle\qquad{\leq K+K\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi(t-s)}}ds\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2p}dz\int_{\mathbb{R}}J(u+z)p_{t-s}(u)du\right]}

\displaystyle\qquad{\leq K+K\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mu_{s}^{m}(z)^{2p}J(z)dz\right]ds.}

By (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{u}p_{t-s}(u)du\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{T|u|}p_{1}(u)du=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{T|B_{1}|}\right]\leq K

for

\displaystyle\int_{a_{i}}^{a_{i+1}}\phi_{i}^{\prime}(x)dx\int^{t}_{0}\int^{u^{i}_{s}(x)}_{0}g_{i}(\nabla u^{i}_{s}(a_{i+1}))W_{i}(ds,dz)

for any
\phi_{i}\in C_{c}^{3}(a_{i},a_{i+1}),i=0,\cdots,n-1,
and

\displaystyle-M_{t}(\phi_{n})\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}\phi_{n}^{\prime}(x)dx\int^{t}_{0}\int^{u^{n}_{s}(x)}_{0}g_{n}(u^{n}_{s}(\infty))W_{n}(ds,dz)

for any
\phi_{n}\in C_{c}^{3}(a_{n},\infty).
Then (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) follow from the approximation method and Lemma
4.2
in Appendix.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
(u_{t}^{n})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies (
3.3
).
Then
(u_{t}^{n}(\infty))_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

\displaystyle u_{t}^{n}(\infty)=u_{0}^{n}(\infty)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u_{s}^{n}(\infty)}g_{n}(u_{s}^{n}(\infty))W_{n}(ds,dz).

(3.10)

Proof.

Recall that
p_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}t}{\mbox{\rm e}}^{-x^{2}/(2t)}
and let

\displaystyle q_{t}^{x}(y):=p_{t}(x+a_{n}-y)-p_{t}(x-a_{n}+y)

for
t>0
and
x,y\geq a_{n}
.
Then (
3.3
) can be written into the following mild form:

\displaystyle u_{t}^{n}(x)\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0}^{n},q_{t}^{x}\right>

(3.11)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Big[\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{n}(y)\}}q_{t-s}^{x}(y){\mbox{\rm d}}y\Big]g_{n}(u_{s}^{n}(\infty))W_{n}(ds,dz)

for any
x\geq a_{n}.
By a change of variable, we have

\displaystyle\left<u_{0}^{n},q_{t}^{x}\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}u_{0}^{n}(y)[p_{t}(x+a_{n}-y)-p_{t}(x-a_{n}+y)]dy

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{x}u_{0}^{n}(x+a_{n}-z)p_{t}(z)dz-\int_{x}^{\infty}u_{0}^{n}(z-x+a_{n})p_{t}(z)dz

\displaystyle\to\!\!

\displaystyle u_{0}^{n}(\infty)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}p_{t}(z)dz=u_{0}^{n}(\infty)

and

\displaystyle\int_{a_{n}}^{\infty}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{n}(y)\}}q_{t-s}^{x}(y)dy\to 1_{\{z\leq u_{s}^{n}(\infty)\}}

In this section we give some results about the process
(u_{t}(x))_{t\geq 0,x\in[0,1]}
satisfying the following SPDE:

\displaystyle u_{t}(x)=u_{0}(x)+\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{2}\Delta u_{s}(x)ds+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{u_{s}(x)}g(\nabla u_{s}(1))W(ds,dz),

where
g
is a positive continuous bounded function from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, and
W(ds,dz)
is a time-space Gaussian white noise with density
dsdz
. Let
\Phi\in C_{c}^{2}(0,1)
satisfies
0\leq\Phi\leq 2
and
\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(x){\mbox{\rm d}}x=1
. For
k\geq 1
and
x\in[0,1]
let

\displaystyle h_{k}(x):=\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z){\mbox{\rm d}}z\cdot\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z){\mbox{\rm d}}z.

(4.1)

Then
h_{k}\in C_{c}^{2}(0,1)
for all
k\geq 1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
f\in C[0,1]
with
f^{\prime}(1)
and
f^{\prime}(0)
exist.
Then

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left<f,h_{k}^{\prime}\right>=f(0)-f(1),\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\left<f,h_{k}^{\prime\prime}\right>=f^{\prime}(1)-f^{\prime}(0).

Proof.

Observe that for each
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle h_{k}^{\prime}(x)=k\Phi(kx)\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz-kx^{k-1}\Phi(x^{k})\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz,\qquad x\in[0,1].

Then by change of variables and dominated convergence,
as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\left<f,h_{k}^{\prime}\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(x)k\Phi(kx)\Big[\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dx-\int_{0}^{1}f(x)kx^{k-1}\Phi(x^{k})\Big[\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dx

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}f(y/k)\Phi(y)\Big[\int_{(y/k)^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dy-\int_{0}^{1}f(y^{1/k})\Phi(y)\Big[\int_{0}^{ky^{1/k}}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dy

converges to
f(0)-f(1)
,
which gives the first assertion.

In the following we prove the second assertion.
Observe that

\displaystyle h_{k}^{\prime\prime}(x)\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle k^{2}\Phi^{\prime}(kx)\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z){\mbox{\rm d}}z-2k^{2}x^{k-1}\Phi(kx)\Phi(x^{k})

(4.2)

\displaystyle-\big[k(k-1)x^{k-2}\Phi(x^{k})+k^{2}x^{2k-2}\Phi^{\prime}(x^{k})\big]\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz

\displaystyle=:\!\!

\displaystyle M_{1,k}(x)-2M_{2,k}(x)-M_{3,k}(x).

By change of variables and dominated convergence again,
as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[f(x)-f(0)]M_{1,k}(x)dx\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}k[f(y/k)-f(0)]\Phi^{\prime}(y)\Big[\int_{y^{k}k^{-k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dy

(4.3)

\displaystyle\to\!\!

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(0)\int_{0}^{1}y\Phi^{\prime}(y)dy=-f^{\prime}(0)

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[f(x)-f(1)]M_{2,k}(x)dx\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(y^{1/k})-f(1)}{y^{1/k}-1}k(y^{1/k}-1)\Phi(y^{1/k})\Phi(y)dy

(4.4)

\displaystyle\to\!\!

\displaystyle 0.

Similarly, as
k\to\infty
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}[f(x)-f(1)]M_{3,k}(x)dx

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{f(y^{1/k})-f(1)}{y^{1/k}-1}k(y^{1/k}-1)\big[k^{-1}(k-1)y^{-1/k}\Phi(y)

\displaystyle\quad\qquad+y^{(k-1)/k}\Phi^{\prime}(y)\big]\cdot\Big[\int_{0}^{ky^{1/k}}\Phi(z)dz\Big]dy

\displaystyle\quad\to f^{\prime}(1)\int_{0}^{1}\ln y[\Phi(y)+y\Phi^{\prime}(y)]dy=-f^{\prime}(1).

(4.5)

Applying integration by parts and the fact
0\leq\Phi\leq 2
and
\mbox{supp}(\Phi)\subset(0,1)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}M_{1,k}(x)dx\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz\Big)^{\prime\prime}\cdot\Big(\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz\Big)dx=\int_{0}^{1}M_{2,k}(x)dx

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle k\int_{0}^{1}\Phi(ky^{1/k})\Phi(y)dy=k\int_{0}^{k^{-k}}\Phi(ky^{1/k})\Phi(y)dy\leq 4k^{1-k}

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}M_{3,k}(x)dx\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\Big(\int_{x^{k}}^{1}\Phi(z)dz\Big)^{\prime\prime}\cdot\Big(\int_{0}^{kx}\Phi(z)dz\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}M_{2,k}(x)dx\leq 4k^{1-k}.

Then combining (
4.2
) with (
4.3
)-(
4
)
one completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that for each
\phi\in C_{c}^{2}(0,1)
,
(u_{t})_{t\geq 0}
satisfies

\displaystyle\left<u_{t},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>ds

(4.6)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\nabla u_{s}(1))\Big[\int_{0}^{1}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)dx\Big]W(ds,dz).

Then for each
\phi\in C_{b}^{2}[0,1]

\displaystyle\left<u_{t},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\big[\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+F_{s}(\phi)]ds

(4.7)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\nabla u_{s}(1))\Big[\int_{0}^{1}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x){\mbox{\rm d}}x\Big]W(ds,dz),

where

\displaystyle F_{s}(\phi):=[\phi(1)\nabla u_{s}(1)-\phi(0)\nabla u_{s}(0)]-[u_{s}(1)\phi^{\prime}(1)-u_{s}(0)\phi^{\prime}(0)].

(4.8)

Proof.

Recall
h_{k}
in (
4.1

For
m\geq 1

\tau_{m}
by

\displaystyle\tau_{m}:=\inf\Big\{t\geq 0:\sup_{x\in[0,1]}|u_{t}(x)|\geq m\Big\}

\inf\emptyset=\infty
.
Then
\lim_{m\to\infty}\tau_{m}=\infty
almost surely.
It follows from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle\left<u_{t\wedge\tau_{m}},\phi h_{k}\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\nabla u_{s}(1))\Big[\int_{0}^{1}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)h_{k}(x)dx\Big]W(ds,dz)

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\left<u_{0},\phi h_{k}\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\left<u_{s},(\phi h_{k})^{\prime\prime}\right>ds.

Notice that

\displaystyle\left<u_{s},(\phi h_{k})^{\prime\prime}\right>=\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}h_{k}\right>+2\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime}h^{\prime}_{k}\right>+\left<u_{s},\phi h^{\prime\prime}_{k}\right>.

It follows from Lemma

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\left<u_{s},(\phi h_{k})^{\prime\prime}\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+[u_{s}(0)\phi^{\prime}(0)-u_{s}(1)\phi^{\prime}(1)]-[\phi(0)\nabla u_{s}(0)-\phi(1)\nabla u_{s}(1)]

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+F_{s}(\phi).

Thus letting
k\to\infty

\displaystyle\left<u_{t\wedge\tau_{m}},\phi\right>\!\!

\displaystyle=\!\!

\displaystyle\left<u_{0},\phi\right>+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\big[\left<u_{s},\phi^{\prime\prime}\right>+F_{s}(\phi)\big]ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t\wedge\tau_{m}}\int_{0}^{\infty}g(\nabla u_{s}(1))\Big[\int_{0}^{1}1_{\{z\leq u_{s}(x)\}}\phi(x)dx\Big]W(ds,dz).

Letting
m\to\infty
the result holds.
∎

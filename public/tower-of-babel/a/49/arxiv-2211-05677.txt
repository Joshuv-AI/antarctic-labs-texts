Multivariate compactly supported C ∞ C^{\infty} functions by subdivision
Maria Charina, Costanza Conti, Nira Dyn

Abstract
This paper discusses the generation of multivariate C ∞ C^{\infty} functions with compact small supports by subdivision schemes. Following the construction of such a univariate function, called Up-function , by a non-stationary scheme based on masks of spline subdivision schemes of growing degrees, we term the multivariate functions we generate Up-like functions. We generate them by non-stationary schemes based on masks of box-splines of growing supports. To analyze the convergence and smoothness of these non-stationary schemes, we develop new tools which apply to a wider class of schemes than the class we study. With our method for achieving small compact supports, we obtain, in the univariate case, Up-like functions with supports [ 0 , 1 + ϵ ] [0,1+\epsilon] in comparison to the support [ 0 , 2 ] [0,2] of the Up-function. Examples of univariate and bivariate Up-like functions are given. As in the univariate case, the construction of Up-like functions can motivate the generation of C ∞ C^{\infty} compactly supported wavelets of small support in any dimension.

1
Introduction

In this paper we design multivariate non-stationary subdivision schemes which generate
C^{\infty}
compactly supported basic limit functions with small supports. We term these functions
Up-like functions
, following the construction of the univariate Up-function studied in
[
8
]
. While stationary subdivision schemes use the same refinement rules in each refinement level, non-stationary subdivision schemes apply level dependent refinement rules. The variety of refinement rules is
what makes this schemes capable of generating limits which cannot be generated by stationary subdivision schemes, such as
exponential splines or conic sections. In these non-stationary examples, the generating schemes have level dependent
refinement rules but the supports of all the different rules is the same, a fact which is taken into account when analyzing their convergence and smoothness (see e.g.
[
12
]
or the survey paper
[
6
]
). Differently, the non-stationary subdivision schemes we study in this paper, are based on refinement rules of growing support size. While the smoothness of the functions generated by stationary schemes is bounded by the support size of their basic limit functions, here we show how non-stationary schemes can be used to generate multivariate
C^{\infty}
basic limit functions of compact and small supports.

In
[
8
]
(see also
[
13
, Example 4.18]
), it is shown that the basic limit function of a univariate non-stationary scheme which applies at refinement level
r
the refinement rules of the stationary scheme generating polynomial splines of degree
r
for
r\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, is a
C^{\infty}
function supported on
[0,2]
. Using a similar approach, with multivariate non-stationary schemes based on refinement rules of three-directional box-splines, we show how to generate multivariate compactly supported
C^{\infty}
basic limit functions. In fact, our method applies to other types of box-splines schemes as well as to other types of schemes with positive masks.
We also present a method for obtaining
C^{\infty}
functions of small supports. With this method, in the univariate case, we design a
C^{\infty}
functions supported in
[0,1+\epsilon]
with positive
\epsilon
arbitrarily small.
We term all the proposed variants of the Up-function,
Up-like functions
and prove the convergence and smoothness of their generating non-stationary schemes. We also analyze the supports of the Up-like functions, and give univariate and bivariate examples.

For deriving the above results, we develop new analysis tools for non-stationary schemes. In particular, we prove a general convergence theorem for non-stationary schemes whose refinement rules are in a certain class. For the smoothness analysis of our non-stationary schemes we use multivariate smoothing factors. The convergence and smoothness theorems we provide apply to a wider class of non-stationary schemes than the class of schemes generating our Up-like functions.

Note that the idea of generating
C^{\infty}
univariate functions of compact support is used to generate univariate
C^{\infty}
wavelets of compact supports (see e.g.
[
5
,
14
]
).
The construction of these wavelets is by a non-stationary subdivision scheme which applies in refinement level
k
refinement rules
of the
k
-th order filter of the Daubechies wavelets
[
7
]
.
The Up-like functions we construct in this paper can also motivate the generation of multivariate
C^{\infty}
compactly supported wavelets.
We also note that, perhaps, the construction and analysis of
C^{\infty}
functions with
compact small supports proposed here, could be performed using Fourier techniques as is done in the univariate case in
[
14
, Secion 2]
. Even if it might be less general, we believe that our time domain approach is elegant and easy to follow.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. After two subsections dealing with background, notation and summary of our proposed new approach, Section
2
presents convergence results of non-stationary subdivision schemes with masks in a special class. Then, Section
3
is devoted to the smoothness analysis of subdivision schemes with a certain number of multivariate smoothing factors in their symbols. In Section
4
, after stating an assumption on the supports of the masks of the non-stationary schemes which ensures the compact
support of their basic limit functions, we compute the precise size of these supports. The closing Section
5
discusses several examples of Up-like functions generated by special
non-stationary schemes based on B-splines’ masks and on three directional box-splines’ masks.

1.1
Background and notation

A
subdivision operator

S_{a}:\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
is defined by

S_{a}f=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,a(\cdot-2\beta)\,f(\beta),\qquad f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),

(1)

with the real-valued sequence
a=\left(a(\alpha)\,:\,\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\right)
called the subdivision
mask
.
The
support
of the mask is the set
\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,|\ a_{\alpha}\neq 0\}
, here assumed to be finite. In our analysis
we also need the notion of
extended support
of a mask
a
,

{\emph{E}supp\,}(a)
, defined as

Definition 1.1
.

{\emph{E}supp\,}(a)=\overline{Convexhull\left(\{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\,|\ a_{\alpha}\neq 0\}\right)}.

Note that, by definition,
{\emph{E}supp\,}(a)
is a compact and convex set.

A mask
a
consists of
2^{d}
sub-masks
\{a(\epsilon+2\alpha),\ \alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}\},\ \epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}
. Each sub-mask corresponding to one

\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}

generates in (
1
)
the refinement rule

(S_{a}f)(\epsilon+2\alpha)=\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,a(\epsilon+2\beta)\,f(\alpha-\beta),\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},\qquad f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}).

A
stationary subdivision scheme
is based on the repeated application of the subdivision operator
S_{a}
,
and generates the sequence of refined values

f^{[r]}=S^{r}_{a}f^{[0]},\quad f^{[0]}\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\quad r\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2)

In contrast, a
non-stationary subdivision scheme
is based on the repeated application of
different
subdivision operators at different refinement levels,
(S_{a_{k}}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, defined by

f^{[r]}=S_{a_{r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}f^{[0]},\quad f^{[0]}\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\quad r\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(3)

The definitions of
convergence
of stationary and non-stationary schemes
vary insignificantly only by the choice of the
subdivision operators in the subdivision recursion. Thus, we define the concept of
convergence only in the more general, non-stationary case. It uses the notion of
infinity norm
of a sequence
f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, defined as

\|\,f\,\|_{\infty}=sup_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}|f(\alpha)|
. In this paper we use only the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
, therefore we omit the subscript
\infty
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. A subdivision scheme
(S_{a_{k}}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})

converges to
C^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
limit functions
, if
for any initial sequence
f^{[0]}\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})
, there exists a

function

g\in C^{s}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, nonzero for at least one nonzero sequence
f^{[0]}
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\,g(2^{-(k+1)}\cdot)-S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}f^{[0]}\,\|=0.

(4)

We write (
4
) as

g=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{r}}S_{a_{r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}f^{[0]},\qquad f^{[0]}\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}).

The convergence of a stationary scheme based on
S_{a}
implies that (see e.g.
[
3
]
)

\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}a(\alpha+\epsilon)=1,\quad\hbox{for}\quad\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}.

(5)

We continue with some
factorization
properties for which we make use of the masks’
symbol

a(z)=\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,a(\alpha)\,z^{\alpha},\qquad z^{\alpha}=z_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot\ldots\cdot z_{d}^{\alpha_{d}},\qquad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d},

and of the notion of
norm of
S_{a}
defined by (see e.g.
[
3
]
)

\|S_{a}\|=\max_{\epsilon\in\{0,1\}^{d}}\sum_{\alpha\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}}\,|\,a(\alpha+\epsilon)\,|\,.

(6)

For a vector
v\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
,
v\not=0
, we define the
directional difference operator

\nabla_{v}:\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\qquad\nabla_{v}\,f=f(\cdot+v)-f,\quad f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),

and, for a basis
V=\{v^{[1]},\ldots v^{[d]}\}
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
with
v^{[1]},\ldots v^{[d]}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
, we define the difference operator as the vector

\nabla_{V}:\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}^{d}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\qquad\nabla_{V}\,f=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\nabla_{v^{[1]}}\,f\\
\vdots\\
\nabla_{v^{[d]}}\,f\end{array}\right)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\quad f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}).

In the stationary case, corresponding to the scheme
(S_{a}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
which is simply denoted by
S_{a}
, it is well known
[
3
,
11
]
that the convergence of
S_{a}
implies the existence of the decomposition

\nabla_{V}\,S_{a}=S_{b}\,\nabla_{V},

(7)

with
V
the canonical basis in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and with a matrix-valued difference subdivision scheme

S_{b}:\ell_{\infty}^{d}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}^{d}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),

which is
contractive
, namely there exists
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\|S_{b}^{\ell}\|<1
.

.

For the sake of simplicity, when

\nabla

\phi_{a}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}S^{r}_{a}\delta,\quad\hbox{with}\quad\delta_{\alpha}\neq 0\ \hbox{only for}\ \alpha=0,\ \hbox{and}\ \delta_{0}=1.

1.2
The proposed new approach

In this paper, we analyze the convergence to the basic limit function

\phi_{0}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta,

(9)

generated by the non-stationary subdivision scheme
(S_{a_{k}}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
with all masks
a_{k}
in a special class, using a novel approach. The novel approach links the convergence
to
\phi_{0}
with
the convergence of the sequence of limits

\varphi_{k}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}^{r}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

(10)

of the corresponding stationary schemes.

The smoothness analysis of
\phi_{0}
is based on special classes of masks whose symbols contain a certain number of multivariate smoothing factors.

Concerning the support of
\phi_{0}
, the novelty is that instead of considering operators based on a sequence of masks with the same support as done in the majority of works on non-stationary schemes, we involve masks of slowly growing supports. This allow us to achieve
C^{\infty}
basic limit functions with compact, small, supports.

2
Convergence analysis

In this section, we show convergence of non-stationary subdivision schemes with masks in a special class, denoted by
{\mathscr{A}}(L,\rho)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
L\in{\mathbb{N}},\ L\geq 1
and
\rho\in{\mathbb{R}},\ \rho<1
. We call
{\mathscr{A}}(L,\rho)
the class of masks
a
with the following three properties:

1)

The mask entries are non-negative;

2)

The stationary subdivision scheme
(S_{a}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
is convergent;

3)

For each mask
a\in{\mathscr{A}}(L,\rho)
, the subdivision operator
S_{a}
is
(L(a),\rho(a))
-level contractive with
L(a)\leq L
and

\rho(a)\leq\rho
.

For this class of masks we prove two preliminary results used in the proof of the main convergence result of this section,
Theorem
2.4
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume
a_{k}\in{\mathscr{A}}(1,\rho),\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, with
\rho<1
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{k,r\rightarrow\infty}\|S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|=0\,.

Proof.

For

we use the relation

S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}(S_{a_{k}}-S_{a_{k+j}})S^{j}_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}

with the convention that the product of the operators
S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}
is the identity for
j+1>r
. This equality is easy to see, since the sum on the right hand-side is a telescoping sum. Therefore,

\|S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\|S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}(S_{a_{k}}-S_{a_{k+j}})S^{j}_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|.

(11)

The convergence of
S_{a_{k}}
and
S_{a_{k+j}}
implies, by
[
4
, Lemma 3]
, the existence of the
operator

S_{D_{k,j}}:\ell_{\infty}^{d}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})\rightarrow\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d}),\qquad S_{a_{k+j}}-S_{a_{k}}=S_{D_{k,j}}\nabla.

Obviously,
\|S_{D_{k,j}}\nabla\|\leq 2
since for any
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
,
\|S_{a_{k}}\|=1
, due to (
5
) and the non-negativity of the masks’ entries.
Using (
7
) repeatedly we get from (
11
),

\begin{array}[]{lll}\|S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|&\leq&\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\|S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}\|}\cdot\|(S_{D_{k,j}}\,\nabla)S^{j}_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|\\
&\leq&\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\|S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}\|}\cdot\|S_{D_{k,j}}\,|_{\nabla}\|\cdot\|S^{j}_{b_{k}}\,|_{\nabla}\|\cdot\|S_{b_{k-1}}\ldots S_{b_{0}}\nabla\delta\|\\
&\leq&2^{d+1}\|S_{b_{k-1}}\ldots S_{b_{0}}\nabla\delta\|\cdot\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{r}\|S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+j+1}}\|\cdot\|S^{j}_{b_{k}}\,|_{\nabla}\|},\end{array}

where we used the fact that
\|\nabla\delta\|\leq 2^{d}
and that
\|S_{D_{k,j}}\,|_{\nabla}\|=\|S_{D_{k,j}}\nabla\|\leq 2
. Next, due to
a_{k}\in{\mathscr{A}}(1,\rho),\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, and we conclude in view of (
5
), (
6
), (
7
) that
\|S_{a_{k}}\|=1
,
\|S_{b_{k}}\|\leq\rho
and therefore

\|S_{b_{k}}|_{\nabla}\|\leq\|S_{b_{k}}\|\leq\rho<1,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|S_{a_{k+r}}\ldots S_{a_{k+i+1}}\|\leq 1.

Thus,

\displaystyle\|S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{d+1}\,\rho^{k}\,\sum_{j=1}^{r}\,\rho^{j}\,

Finally, letting
r
and
k
go to infinity, we obtain

\lim_{k,r\rightarrow\infty}\|S_{a_{k}}^{r}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|\leq\frac{2^{d+1}}{1-\rho}\cdot\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\,\rho^{k}=0\,,

which completes the proof of the Lemma.∎

The next Proposition
2.3
studies the properties of the
function-sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}
, where the function
\varphi_{k}
is the limit of the stationary scheme
S_{a_{k}}
applied
to the initial sequence

S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}}^{d})

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume

a_{k}\in{\mathscr{A}}(1,\rho)
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, with
\rho<1
. Then, the sequence of continuous functions

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}},\quad\hbox{with}\quad\varphi_{k}=\lim_{r\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}^{r}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta,\quad k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0},

(12)

is a Cauchy sequence, with a nonzero, continuous limit.

Proof.

Due to the convergence of each stationary subdivision scheme
S_{a_{k}}
,
k\in N_{0}
, the limits
\varphi_{k}
in (
12
) exist and are at least continuous
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. For

, we write

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{m+n}-\varphi_{m}\|\leq\|\varphi_{m+n}-S_{a_{m+n}}^{r}S_{a_{m+n-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{m}-S_{a_{m}}^{r+n}S_{a_{m-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|S_{a_{m+n}}^{r}S_{a_{m+n-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{m}}^{r+n}S_{a_{m-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi_{m+n}-S_{a_{m+n}}^{r}S_{a_{m+n-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{m}-S_{a_{m}}^{r+n}S_{a_{m-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle\|S_{a_{m+n}}^{r}S_{a_{m+n-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{m+n+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle\|S_{a_{m+n+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{m}}^{r+n}S_{a_{m-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

Thus, due to the convergence of each

.

Now we are ready to prove our main convergence result.

Theorem 2.4
.

A non-stationary scheme
(S_{a_{k}}\,:\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
with

, and

By Proposition
2.3
, there exists a nonzero limit
of the sequence in (

). We show that
\varphi
is the basic limit

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\varphi(2^{-(k+1)}\cdot)-S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|=0.

(13)

The convergence in (

) is ensured, if for any

and for large enough

\displaystyle\ \|\varphi(2^{-{(k+r+1)}}\cdot)-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\varphi(2^{-{(k+r+1)}}\cdot)-\varphi_{k}(2^{-{(k+r+1)}}\cdot)\|+\|\varphi_{k}(2^{-{(k+r+1)}}\cdot)-S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|S_{a_{k}}^{r+1}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta-S_{a_{k+r}}S_{a_{k+r-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta\|<\epsilon\,.

Due to the convergence of each stationary scheme

3
Smoothness analysis

This section is devoted to the smoothness analysis of subdivision schemes with masks in special classes characterized by symbols containing different numbers of multivariate smoothing factors. In particular, Corollary
3.11
allows us to conclude the smoothess of our multivariate Up-like functions, which is one of the main goals of our paper.

3.1
Smoothness of stationary schemes via smoothing factors

We start with the definition of a multivariate smoothing factor and of its symbol.

Definition 3.1
.

A
symbol of a directional smoothing factor
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
in direction
v\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is

\frac{1+z^{v}}{2},\quad\hbox{where}\quad z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{d}),\ \ v=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}),\ \ z^{v}=(z_{1}^{v_{1}},\ldots,z_{d}^{v_{d}}),\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

The
symbol of a (full) smoothing factor
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
,
s_{V}(z)
, is a product of the symbols of
d
directional smoothing factors in
d
linearly independent directions in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}

s_{V}(z)={\displaystyle\Pi_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1+z^{v^{[j]}}}{2}},\quad V=\{v^{[1]},\ldots,v^{[d]}\},\,\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

(14)

We continue by presenting four results, Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.6
and
3.7
, on stationary schemes which are relevant to the convergence and smoothness analysis of non-stationary schemes with masks of growing supports.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
S_{a}
be a converging stationary subdivision scheme with
a(z)
a symbol with positive coefficients. Let
V=\{v^{[1]},\ldots,v^{[d]}\}
be
d
linear independent directions of
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
, and let

D\subset{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
be a collection of directions with possible repeated directions that are considered as different elements of
D
. The symbol

c(z)=a(z)\left(\Pi_{v\in D}\frac{1+z^{v}}{2}\right)\left(\Pi_{j=1}^{d}\frac{1+z^{v^{[j]}}}{2}\right),\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d},

(15)

determines a scheme
S_{c}
which is a converging scheme, satisfying

\nabla_{V}S_{c}=S_{b}\nabla_{V}\ ,

(16)

with
b(z)
a diagonal matrix with diagonal elements

b_{i,i}(z)=\frac{a(z)}{2}\left(\Pi_{v\in D}\frac{1+z^{v}}{2}\right)\left(\Pi_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{d}\frac{1+z^{v^{[j]}}}{2}\right),\ \ i=1,\ldots,d,\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

(17)

Moreover,
S_{c}\in{\mathscr{A}}(1,\frac{1}{2})
.

Proof.

First we derive (
16
). We denote

\tilde{V}^{[i]}:=V\setminus\{v^{[i]}\}
the collection of all the directions in
V
except for
v^{[i]}
, and by
s_{D}(z)
,
s_{\tilde{V}^{[i]}}(z)

the symbols

s_{D}(z)=\Pi_{v\in D}\frac{1+z^{v}}{2}\ ,\quad s_{\tilde{V}^{[i]}}(z)=\Pi_{j=1,\,j\neq i}^{d}\frac{1+z^{v^{[j]}}}{2},\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

Applying the difference in direction
v^{[i]}
to
S_{c}
, we get in terms of symbols

(z^{-v^{[i]}}-1)c(z)=(z^{-v^{[i]}}-1)\frac{1+z^{v^{[i]}}}{2}a(z)s_{D}(z)s_{\tilde{V}^{[i]}}(z)=(z^{-2v^{[i]}}-1)\frac{a(z)}{2}s_{D}(z)s_{\tilde{V}^{[i]}}(z),\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

Writing the above equation in terms of operators, we get

\nabla_{v^{[i]}}S_{c}=S_{b_{i,i}}\nabla_{v^{[i]}}
with
b_{i,i}(z)
as in (
17
).
Since the above holds for all
i=1,\ldots,d
, (
16
) follows with
S_{b}
a diagonal matrix with diagonal elements as in (
17
).
The convergence of
S_{c}
follows from (
16
), (
17
) and the observations

1.

\|S_{b}\|=\max_{i=1,\ldots,d}\|S_{b_{i,i}}\|
,

2.

For any positive mask
e
such that
S_{e}
is a converging scheme,

\|S_{e}\|=1\ ,

3.

The symbol
e_{u}(z)=e(z)\frac{1+z^{u}}{2}
with
u\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
and
e
a positive mask, has positive coefficients and
\|S_{e_{u}}\|\leq\|S_{e}\|
.

Combining these observations with
(\ref{diag})
, we get

\|S_{b_{i,i}}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}
, and therefore
\|S_{b}\|\leq\frac{1}{2}
. This, together with
(\ref{firstdiff})
, implies that
S_{c}\in{\mathscr{A}}(1,\frac{1}{2})
. ∎

Proposition 3.3
.

In the notation of Proposition
3.2
and its proof, let
c(z)=a(z)s_{D}(z)s_{V}(z)
. Then, the first divided differences in direction
v^{[i]}
of the data generated by
S_{c}
,
are mapped from one refinement level to its next refinement level by
S_{c^{*}_{i}}
with
c^{*}_{i}(z)=a(z)s_{D}(z)s_{{\tilde{V}}^{[i]}}(z)
.

Proof.

Applying the difference in direction
v^{[i]}
to
S_{c}
, we get in terms of symbols

(z^{-v^{[i]}}-1)c(z)=(z^{-v^{[i]}}-1)\frac{1+z^{v^{[i]}}}{2}a(z)s_{D}(z)s_{{\tilde{V}}^{[i]}}(z)=(z^{-2v^{[i]}}-1)\frac{c^{*}_{i}(z)}{2},\quad z\in\left({\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}\right)^{d}.

The above equation, written in terms of operators, is

\nabla_{v^{[i]}}S_{c}=S_{\frac{1}{2}c^{*}_{i}}\nabla_{v^{[i]}}.

Multiplying the last equation by
\frac{2^{k+1}}{\|v^{[i]}\|_{2}}
, we finally obtain

\frac{\nabla_{v^{[i]}}}{2^{-(k+1)}\|v^{[i]}\|_{2}}S_{c}=S_{c^{*}_{i}}\frac{\nabla_{v^{[i]}}}{2^{-k}\|v^{[i]}\|_{2}}\ ,

(18)

which completes the proof.
∎

In order to state and prove our next result, we introduce the following sets of masks.

Definition 3.4
.

{\mathscr{C}_{0}}
consists of masks with symbols which are products of a positive symbol of a converging stationary scheme, times
s_{D}(z)
with
D
any collection of directions in

{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
, with possible repetitions of directions.

The next sets of masks depend on a sequence of bases in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
, denoted by
{\mathcal{V}}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of bases in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
. The sets of masks
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{{\mathcal{V}}},\ j\geq 1
is recursively defined from the starting set
{\mathscr{C}}_{0}^{{\mathcal{V}}}:={\mathscr{C}_{0}}
, as

{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{{\mathcal{V}}},\hbox{consists of masks with symbols that are products of a symbol of a mask in}\ {\mathscr{C}}_{j-1}^{\mathcal{V}}\ \hbox{multiplied by}\ s_{V_{j}}(z).

For example, the mask
c
with symbol in (
15
) is in
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}^{\mathcal{V}}
for any
{\mathcal{V}}
with
V_{1}=V
.

Proposition 3.6
.

The sets of masks
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}},\ j\geq 1
satisfy

{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}}\subset{\mathscr{C}}_{j-1}^{\mathcal{V}}\ ,\ \ j\geq 1\ .

(19)

Moreover, all the masks in
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}},\ j\geq 1
are in
{\mathscr{A}}(1,\frac{1}{2})
.

Proof.

It is easy to conclude from the inductive definition of the sets
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}}
that any
c\in{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}}
has a symbol of the form

c(z)=a(z)s_{D_{c}}(z)\Pi_{i=1}^{j}s_{V_{i}}(z)
where
V_{1},\ldots,V_{j}
are the first
j
elements in the sequence
{\mathcal{V}}
,
a(z)
is a symbol with positive coefficient of a converging scheme and
D_{c}

is a collection of directions in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
with possible repetitions of directions. Repeated directions are considered as different elements of
D_{c}
.
Including the directions of
V_{j}
in
D_{c}
, we conclude that
c\in{\mathscr{C}}_{j-1}^{\mathcal{V}}
. Thus, the masks in any
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{\mathcal{V}}
for
j\geq 1
are also in
{\mathscr{C}}_{1}^{\mathcal{V}}
, and therefore by Proposition
3.2
these masks are in
{\mathscr{A}}(1,\frac{1}{2})
.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove the next proposition
dealing with the smoothness analysis of subdivision schemes with masks in
{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{m}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of basis in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
and
c\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{m}
with
m\geq 1
. Then
S_{c}
generates
C^{m-1}
limits.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
m\geq 1
. For
m=1
the claim follows from Proposition
3.2

combined with Theorem
2.4
.
Assume that the claim holds for some
m\geq 1
. We show that then the claim holds for
m+1
.
To prove the claim for
m+1
, we take
c\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{m+1}
. Then, there exists
{\tilde{c}}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{m}
, such that
c(z)={\tilde{c}}(z)s_{V_{m+1}}(z)
.

Introducing the symbols
c^{\ast}_{i}(z)={\tilde{c}}(z)s_{{{\tilde{V}}}^{[i]}_{m+1}}(z),\ \ i=1,\ldots,d
with
{{\tilde{V}}}^{[i]}_{m+1}
the collection of directions in
V_{m+1}
except
v^{[i]}_{m+1}
, we conclude, in view of Proposition
3.3
, that
for
i=1,\ldots,d
, the scheme
S_{c^{*}_{i}}
maps the first divided differences in the direction
v^{[i]}_{m+1}
of the data generated by
S_{c}
,
from one refinement level to its next refinement level. Since
c^{*}_{i}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{m}
, it follows from the induction hypothesis that the
first directional derivatives of the limits of
S_{c}
in the directions of
V_{m+1}

are in
C^{m-1}
. Since
V_{m+1}
is a basis, all these directions are linearly indeperndent, and therefore the limits of
S_{c}
are in
C^{m}
.
This completes the proof of the induction hypothesis for
m+1
.
∎

3.2
Smoothness of non-stationary schemes via smoothing factors

Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
,
3.6
and
3.7
, on stationary subdivision schemes enable us to prove results on non-stationary subdivision schemes with masks in the sets
{\mathscr{C}}_{j}^{{\mathcal{V}}},\ j\in{\mathbb{N}}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of basis in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
. Let the non-stationary scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, with
c_{k}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{j_{k}},\ j_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then the scheme is converging, and its limits are
C^{j^{*}-1}
, where
j^{*}
is the maximal integer satisfying
j^{*}\leq j_{k}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.

Proof: The convergence of the non-stationary scheme follows from the convergence result, Theorem
2.4
, in view of (
19
) and Proposition
3.2
.
The proof of the smoothness of the limits of
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
is similar to the proof of Proposition
3.7
,
it is based on Proposition
3.3
and it uses
induction on
j^{*}
. For
j^{*}=1
the claim about smoothness follows from the convergence claim.
Next, we assume that the claim holds for
j^{*}-1\geq 1
,
and prove it for
j^{*}
.
Since
j_{k}\geq j^{*}
for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
, it follows from (
19
) that for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
we have
c_{k}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{j^{*}}
.
Thus,
c_{k}(z)={\tilde{c}}_{k}(z)s_{V_{j^{*}}}(z)
, with
{\tilde{c}}_{k}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{j^{*}-1}
, for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Introducing the symbols
c^{*}_{i,k}(z)={\tilde{c}}_{k}(z)s_{{\tilde{V}}^{[i]}_{j^{*}}}(z)
, , for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and
i=1,\ldots,d
with
{{\tilde{V}}}^{[i]}_{j^{*}}
the collection of directions in
V_{j^{*}}
except
v^{[i]}_{j^{*}}
, we conclude that the corresponding masks are in

{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{j^{*}-1}
. Moreover, by Proposition
3.3
, the non-stationary scheme
(S_{c^{*}_{i,k}},\ k\in{\mathbb{N}})
,
maps the first divided differences in direction
v_{j^{*}}^{[i]}
of the data generated by
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
from one refinement level to its next refinement level. This observation is valid for
v_{j^{*}}^{[1]},\ldots,v_{j^{*}}^{[d]}
the vectors of
V_{j^{*}}
. The convergence claim of the theorem implies that the
d
non-stationary schemes
(S_{c^{*}_{i,k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}),\ i=1,\ldots,d

are converging. By the induction hypothesis these schemes
generate limits which are in
C_{j^{*}-2}

and therefore the first directional derivatives of the limits of the scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
in the directions of
V_{j^{*}}

are
C^{j^{*}-2}
. Since the
d
directions in
V_{j^{*}}
are linearly independent, the limits of the scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
are
C^{j^{*}-1}
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
{\mathcal{V}}=\{V_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of basis in
{\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
.
Let the non-stationary scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, with
c_{k}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{j_{k}},\ j_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. If for some
j^{*},m\in{\mathbb{N}},\

j_{k}\geq j^{*}
for all
k\geq m
, then the non-staionary scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
generates

C^{j^{*}-1}
limits.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.8
, the non-stationary scheme
(S_{c_{m+k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
converges to
C^{j^{*}-1}
limits. Regarding

S_{c_{m-1}}S_{c_{m-2}}\ldots S_{c_{1}}S_{c_{0}}f^{0}
as
initial data for the scheme
S_{c_{m}}
, with
f^{0}
an initial data tor the scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, we conclude the claim of the theorem.
∎

Two direct consequences of Theorem
3.9
are the following corollaries.
Note that Corollary
3.11
is the result needed to conclude the smoothness of our Up-like functions.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let the non-stationary scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
be as in Theorem
3.9
. If the sequence
\{j_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}

is monotone non-decreasing and unbounded, then the scheme
(S_{c_{k}}:k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
is converging, and its limits are
C^{\infty}
.

Corollary 3.11
.

Let
D_{0}
be a collection of directions in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{d}
, containing a basis
V
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and define
a(z)=2^{d}s_{D_{0}}(z)
. If
S_{a}
is converging, then the symbols

c_{kr+j}(z)=2^{-dk}a(z)^{k+1},\ j=0,\ldots,r-1
for
k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
and some
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, define a converging non-stationary scheme
(S_{c_{\ell}}:\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
, generating
C^{\infty}
limits.

Proof.

Since
S_{a}
is a converging scheme, and since
a(z)
is a symbol with positive coefficients, then

a\in{\mathscr{C}}_{0}
. Let
D_{\ell}
denote the collection of directions corresponding to
c_{\ell}(z)
. By definition,
D_{\ell}
contains at least
[\ell/r]
repetitions of
V
, and the same number of repetitions
of the directions in
D_{0}\setminus\{V\}
. Therefore, for
{\cal V}
the constant sequence of the basis
V
,
we have that

c_{\ell}\in{\mathscr{C}}^{\cal V}_{[\ell/r]}
for
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
.
Thus, by Corollary
3.10
, the scheme
(S_{c_{\ell}}:\ell\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0})
is converging to
C^{\infty}
limits.
∎

{\emph{s}upp\,}(\phi_{0})\subseteq{\mathscr{S}}=\displaystyle{{\,\mathchoice{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\makebox[11.22223pt]{\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(0.0,0.0){\oval(0.0,0.0)}}\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(-3.61111,0.0){$\displaystyle\Sigma$}}}}}{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\makebox[11.22223pt]{\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(0.0,0.0){\oval(0.0,0.0)}}\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(-3.61111,0.0){$\textstyle\Sigma$}}}}}{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\makebox[9.70836pt]{\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(0.0,0.0){\oval(0.0,0.0)}}\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(-2.85417,0.0){$\scriptstyle\Sigma$}}}}}{\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\makebox[8.79173pt]{\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(0.0,0.0){\oval(0.0,0.0)}}\pic@makebox@{\makebox}{}(0.0,0.0){\put(-2.39586,0.0){$\scriptscriptstyle\Sigma$}}}}}\,}_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-(k+1)}{\emph{E}supp\,}(a_{k})}={\emph{E}supp\,}(a_{0})\displaystyle{\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}2^{-(k+1)}\lambda_{k}}\,.

5
Examples

In this section examples of univariate and bivariate Up-like functions are given.

5.1
Univariate case

This subsection shows that in the univariate case there exist non-stationary subdivision schemes with
C^{\infty}

basic limit functions supported on
[0,1+\epsilon]
, with positive
\epsilon
, arbitrarily small.
We know that, to get

C^{\infty}
limits we need a growing number of the factor
\frac{1+z}{2}
in the symbols
a_{0}(z),a_{1}(z),a_{2}(z),\ldots
, but to decrease the support size of the basic limit function, we have to slowly increase the number of
this factor.

Proposition
5.3
considers
the case where
r
repetitions of the same number of smoothing factors are taken before increasing their number. For this case, we explicitly compute the support size of the corresponding basic limit function
.
Before, for completeness, we state and prove two auxiliary lemmas.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
f\in\ell_{\infty}({\mathbb{Z}})
be a sequence of positive numbers with

supp(f)=\{0,1,2,\ldots,u\}\ ,u\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
a(z)=\frac{(1+z)^{m+1}}{2^{m}}
, with
m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
. Then

supp(S_{a}f)=\{0,1,2,\ldots,(2u+m+1)\}\ .

Proof.

First we observe that

supp(S_{a}\delta)=\{0,1,2,\ldots,(m+1)\}\subset\mathbb{Z}\ .

(21)

By the linearity of
S_{a}
we obtain
S_{a}f=S_{a}\sum_{i=0}^{u}f(i)\delta(i-\cdot)=\sum_{i=0}^{u}f(i)(S_{a}\delta)(i-\cdot)
.
In view of (
21
) we get that
supp(S_{a}\delta(i-\cdot)=\{2i,2i+1,\ldots,2i+m+1\}
, and thus

supp((S_{a}f))=\displaystyle\cup_{i=0}^{u}\{0,1,2,\ldots,2i+m+1\}=\{0,1,2,\ldots,(2u+m+1)\},

which proves the claim of the lemma.
∎

The second Lemma is more algebraic.

Lemma 5.2
.

For
q\in(0,1)
we have
\displaystyle{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}jq^{j}=\frac{q}{(1-q)^{2}}\ ,\quad\hbox{and}\quad\ \ \sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j}=\frac{1}{(1-q)^{2}}}\ .

Proof.

The first equality above follows easily from the second one. So we only prove the second equality. Since
0<q<1
,
we have

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)q^{j}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{d}{dq}q^{j+1}=\frac{d}{dq}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}q^{j}=\frac{d}{dq}\left(\frac{q}{1-q}\right)=\frac{1}{(1-q)^{2}}\ .

∎

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
r\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and let

a_{mr}(z)=a_{mr+1}(z)=a_{mr+2}(z)=\ldots=a_{mr+r-1}(z)=\frac{(1+z)^{m+1}}{2^{m}},\qquad m\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}.

(22)

Then, for the limit function

\phi_{0,r}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta,

(23)

we have that

\phi_{0,r}\ \hbox{is}\ C^{\infty},\quad\hbox{and}\quad{\emph{s}upp\,}(\phi_{0,r})=\left[0,1+\frac{1}{2^{r}-1}\right]\,.

Proof.

Note that, for support consideration, any subdivision sequence
f^{[k]},\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
which is defined on
{\mathbb{Z}}
,
should be considered as defined on
2^{-k}{\mathbb{Z}}
requiring a the reparametrization
2^{-k}f^{[k]}\subset 2^{-k}\mathbb{Z}
.
By Lemma
5.1
and in view of (
22
)

supp(\phi_{0,r})=[0,u_{r}]
with

u_{r}=1\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2^{r}}\right)+2\left(\frac{1}{2^{r+1}}+\frac{1}{2^{r+2}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2^{2r}}\right)+3\left(\frac{1}{2^{2r+1}}+\frac{1}{2^{2r+2}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2^{3r}}\right)+\ldots\ .

Thus

u_{r}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)2^{-rj}\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{4}+\ldots+\frac{1}{2^{r}}\right)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(j+1)2^{-rj}\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}2^{-\ell}.

In view of Lemma
5.2
we finally get

u_{r}=\frac{2^{r}-1}{2^{r}}\frac{1}{(1-2^{-r})^{2}}=\frac{2^{r}}{2^{r}-1}=1+\frac{1}{2^{r}-1}\ .

Since the masks
a_{m}\in{\mathscr{C}}_{[m/r]}^{\cal V}
with
{\cal V}=\{1,1,1,...\}
, we conclude based on Corollary
3.11

that
\phi_{0,r}
, defined in (
23
), is
C^{\infty}
.
This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Based on the previous results, we can construct the
C^{\infty}
compactly supported Up-function supported in
[0,2]
,
as well as other
C^{\infty}
functions with a smaller support than the support of the Up-function that are a new type of Up-like functions.

Example 5.4

(Up-function)
.

We consider the case
r=1
that is the symbols

a_{m}(z)=\frac{(1+z)^{m+1}}{2^{m}},\ m\geq 0.

Based on Proposition
5.3
, the basic limit function
\phi_{0,1}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta
is
C^{\infty}
and has support

\left[0,2\right]
.

Example 5.5

(The Up-like function
\phi_{0,2}
)
.

We start by considering the case
r=2
where

a_{2m}(z)=a_{2m+1}(z)=\frac{(1+z)^{m+1}}{2^{m}},\ m\geq 0.

Based on Proposition
5.3
, the basic limit function
\phi_{0,2}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}S_{a_{k}}S_{a_{k-1}}\ldots S_{a_{0}}\delta
is

with support

\left[0,\frac{4}{3}\right]

5.2

For a given

r\in\mathbb{N}
we consider the non-stationary subdivision scheme with masks of the form

a_{mr}=a_{mr+1}=\ldots=a_{(m+1)r-1}=s_{m+1}(e^{[1]},e^{[2]},e^{[3]}),\ \ m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\ ,

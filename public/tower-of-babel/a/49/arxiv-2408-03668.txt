Optimal sums of three cubes in 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
Tim Browning, Jakob Glas, Victor Y. Wang

Abstract
. We use the circle method to prove that a density 1 1 of elements in 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] are representable as a sum of three cubes of essentially minimal degree from 𝔽 q ​ [ t ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[t] , assuming the Ratios Conjecture and that char ⁡ ( 𝔽 q ) > 3 \Char(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3 . Roughly speaking, to do so, we upgrade an order of magnitude result to a full asymptotic formula that was conjectured by Hooley in the number field setting.

2.
Upper density of
S_{A}

Suppose
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq 3
.
In this section we prove
Theorem
1.2
.
We recall the notation
\widehat{B}=q^{B}
, for any
B\in\mathbb{R}
, that was adopted in our previous investigation
[
2
]
.
Define

\mathbb{E}_{r}(T;S)=\frac{|r|}{\widehat{T}}\#\left\{k\in S:|k|<\widehat{T},\;r\mid k\right\},

for any subset
S\subset\mathcal{O}
,
any
T>0

and any
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
, where we recall that
\mathcal{O}^{+}
denotes the set of monic elements in
\mathcal{O}
.
For a given choice of
A>0
we need to prove that

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{1}(T;S_{A})<1.

To begin with, given

r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
such that
\deg(r)\leqslant\frac{T}{3}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{r}(T;S_{A})\leqslant\frac{|r|}{\widehat{T}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{O}\\
|k|<\widehat{T}\\
r\mid k\end{subarray}}\tilde{r}_{A}(k),

where

\tilde{r}_{A}(k)=\#\left\{(x,y,z)\in\mathcal{O}^{3}:x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k,~\deg{x},\deg{y},\deg{z}\leqslant\tfrac{\deg{k}}{3}+A\right\}.

Breaking into residue classes modulo
r
, we obtain

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{O}\\
|k|<\widehat{T}\\
r\mid k\end{subarray}}\tilde{r}_{A}(k)\leqslant\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(u,v,w)\in(\mathcal{O}/r\mathcal{O})^{3}\\
u^{3}+v^{3}+w^{3}\equiv 0\bmod{r}\end{subarray}}S(u)S(v)S(w),

where
S(n)
is the number

x\in\mathcal{O}
such that
\deg x\leqslant\tfrac{1}{3}T+A
and
x\equiv n\bmod{r}
, for any
n\in\mathcal{O}
.
Clearly
S(n)\ll_{A}\widehat{T}^{1/3}/|r|
, where the implied constant is allowed to depend on
A
. But then it follows that there exists a constant
C_{A}>0
depending only on
A
such that

\mathbb{E}_{r}(T;S_{A})\leqslant C_{A}\frac{\rho(r)}{|r|^{2}},

where

\rho(r)=\#\left\{(u,v,w)\in(\mathcal{O}/r\mathcal{O})^{3}:u^{3}+v^{3}+w^{3}\equiv 0\bmod{r}\right\}.

It now follows that

\mathbb{E}_{1}(T;\mathcal{O}\setminus S_{A})\geqslant|r|^{-1}\mathbb{E}_{r}(T;\mathcal{O}\setminus S_{A})\geqslant|r|^{-1}\left(1-C_{A}\frac{\rho(r)}{|r|^{2}}\right),

whence

\mathbb{E}_{1}(T;S_{A})\leqslant 1-|r|^{-1}\left(1-C_{A}\frac{\rho(r)}{|r|^{2}}\right).

The proof of Theorem
1.2
therefore reduces to finding
an element
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
, depending only on
A
, such that

\frac{\rho(r)}{|r|^{2}}<C_{A}^{-1}.

For any finite field
\mathbb{F}_{m}
of characteristic
p
,
we introduce the
cubic Gauss sum

g(\chi)=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{F}_{m}}\chi(u)e_{p}(T_{\mathbb{F}_{m}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}(u)),

where
\chi
is any non-trivial multiplicative cubic character of
\mathbb{F}_{m}

and where
T_{\mathbb{F}_{m}/\mathbb{F}_{p}}:\mathbb{F}_{m}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}
is the trace map.
This has absolute value
\sqrt{m}
and we define the normalised cubic Gauss sum

\widetilde{g}(\chi)=g(\chi)/\sqrt{m}
, over
\mathbb{F}_{m}
.

Let
\varpi\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
be a prime such that

|\varpi|\equiv 1\bmod{3}

and let
\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}
be the finite field that is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O}
, with cardinality

|\varpi|
.
It follows from Theorem 2 in
[
7
]
*§ 10.3 that

\frac{\rho(\varpi)-1}{|\varpi|-1}=|\varpi|+1+2|\varpi|^{-1}\real g(\chi_{\varpi})^{3},

where
\chi_{\varpi}
is any non-trivial cubic character of
\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}
.
But then

(2.1)

\frac{\rho(\varpi)}{|\varpi|^{2}}=1+\frac{c_{\varpi}}{|\varpi|}-\frac{c_{\varpi}}{|\varpi|^{2}},

where
c_{\varpi}=2|\varpi|^{-1}\real g(\chi_{\varpi})^{3}\in\mathbb{Z}
.

The
normalised cubic Gauss sum

\widetilde{g}(\chi_{\varpi})=g(\chi_{\varpi})/|\varpi|^{1/2}
is a complex number with absolute value
1
and we may write

c_{\varpi}=2|\varpi|^{-1}\real g(\chi_{\varpi})^{3}=2|\varpi|^{1/2}\real\widetilde{g}(\chi_{\varpi})^{3},

so that
|c_{\varpi}|\leqslant 2|\varpi|^{1/2}
.
We would like to prove that there are infinitely many primes

\varpi\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
with

|\varpi|\equiv 1\bmod{3}
for which
c_{\varpi}<0
.
This would follow from a function field version of the result by Heath-Brown and Patterson
[
6
, Theorem 2]
, but such a result has yet to be worked out in the literature. Fortunately, since we work with characters over the constant field, an elementary approach is available to us.

Pick an integer
d\geqslant 1
.
The prime number theorem for function fields implies that there are

(2d)^{-1}q^{2d}+O(q^{d})
primes

\varpi\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
such that
\deg(\varpi)=2d
.
Moreover, any such prime will satisfy

|\varpi|\equiv 1\bmod{3}
, since
2d
is even.
For any such prime

\varpi
, we may assume that our
cubic character
\chi_{\varpi}
on
\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
takes the shape
\chi\circ N_{\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}
, where
\chi
is a fixed cubic character on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\subset\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and

N_{\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}/\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}}:\mathbb{F}_{\varpi}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
is the norm map.
It then follows from the Hasse–Davenport relation that

\widetilde{g}(\chi_{\varpi})=-(-\widetilde{g}(\chi))^{d},

where
\widetilde{g}(\chi)
is the normalised Gauss sum on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
,
whence

(2.2)

c_{\varpi}=-2|\varpi|^{1/2}\real\left(-\widetilde{g}(\chi)^{3}\right)^{d}.

We proceed to prove the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist infinitely many integers
d\geqslant 0
such that

\tfrac{9}{10}\leqslant\real\left(-\widetilde{g}(\chi)^{3}\right)^{d}\leqslant 1.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
be such that
-\widetilde{g}(\chi)^{3}=e(\alpha)
.
Suppose first that
\alpha=a/b\in\mathbb{Q}
.
Then any integer
d\geqslant 0
that is divisible by
b

satisfies
e(d\alpha)=1
and thus suffices for the statement of the lemma.

Suppose next that
\alpha\not\in\mathbb{Q}
.
The upper bound is trivial for any
d\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and so we focus on the lower bound.
It follows from Kronecker’s approximation theorem that
there exist infinitely many integers
M\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\|M\alpha\|\leqslant\frac{1}{99}
, where
\|\eta\|
denotes the distance to the nearest integer from any
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any such
M
, taking
d=M
gives
\cos(2\pi\cdot d\alpha)=\cos(2\pi\gamma)
, for some
\gamma\in[-\frac{1}{99},\frac{1}{99}]
. But then it follows that
\cos(2\pi\gamma)\geqslant\frac{9}{10}
, meaning
that the statement of the lemma holds with
d=M
.
∎

Choose
d
in Lemma
2.1

such that
d
is large enough in terms of
q
.
Then we can find
m\geqslant q^{2d}/(4d)
primes

\varpi_{1},\dots,\varpi_{m}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
satisfying
|\varpi_{i}|\equiv 1\bmod{3}
and

\deg(\varpi_{i})=2d
,
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant m
,
and for which

\displaystyle c_{\varpi_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{|\varpi_{i}|}-\frac{1}{|\varpi_{i}|^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{9}{5}\left(\frac{1}{|\varpi_{i}|^{1/2}}-\frac{1}{|\varpi_{i}|^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant-\frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{q^{d}},

in the light of (
2.2
).
Put
r_{d}=\varpi_{1}\cdots\varpi_{m}\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
.
But then it follows from (
2.1
) and
the Chinese remainder theorem that

\frac{\rho(r_{d})}{|r_{d}|^{2}}=\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\frac{\rho(\varpi_{i})}{|\varpi_{i}|^{2}}\leqslant\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\left(1-\frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{q^{d}}\right)\leqslant\prod_{1\leqslant i\leqslant m}\exp\left(-\frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{1}{q^{d}}\right),

since
1-\xi\leqslant\exp(-\xi)
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
On recalling that

m\geqslant q^{2d}/(4d)
, we deduce that

\frac{\rho(r_{d})}{|r_{d}|^{2}}\leqslant\exp\left(-\frac{9}{10}\cdot\frac{q^{d}}{4d}\right).

This is strictly less than
C_{A}^{-1}
on taking
d
to be sufficiently large in terms of
A
, which thereby completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Suppose
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq 3
.
In §

Definition 3.1

\begin{split}\nu_{A,\alpha}(x,y,z)&\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\bm{1}_{\lvert x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}\rvert=q^{\alpha}}\bm{1}_{1\leqslant\lvert x\rvert=\lvert y\rvert\leqslant q^{\alpha/3+A}}\bm{1}_{|y|q^{-1}\leqslant|z|\leqslant|y|},\\

We will typically write

Remark 3.2
.

A few comments are needed to explain our choice of weight function. Firstly, it is not strictly necessary to state

, as this follows from

and

already. Secondly, it is crucial for our argument that

are of roughly the same size. This guarantees that there are not too many points lying on linear subspaces on the Fermat hypersurface
\sum_{i=1}^{6}x_{i}^{3}=0
, as seen in Lemma

. Thirdly, we cannot simply take

, but need to allow for one variable to be smaller than the others. Indeed, if

cancels. When
|x|=|y|=|z|
, then this is only possible if there exists a solution
(x_{0},y_{0},z_{0})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times})^{3}
such that

. But such solutions do not exist for

,
as was implicitly observed by Serre and Vaserstein
[

]
*p. 350.

If

, as above, with

, then we may write

where we recall that

.
It is now time to introduce the relevant local densities.

The analogue of the real density is given by

Next, let

be a parameter to be chosen in due course and define

The relevant local densities at the finite places are conveniently bundled together in the expression

Then, in §

Given

Lemma 3.3

Given

such that
|k|=|P|^{3}q^{\alpha}
with

, let

\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)=\frac{1}{q^{14\tilde{A}+2}}\#\left\{\bm{x}\in\left(\frac{\mathbb{F}_{q}[s]}{(s^{7\tilde{A}+2})}\right)^{3}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}F_{0}(\bm{x})\equiv m\bmod s^{7\tilde{A}+2},\\

Proof.

Interchanging the order of integration and executing the integral over

\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)=q^{4\tilde{A}+1}\vol\{(x,y,z)\in\supp\nu\colon|F_{0}(x,y,z)-kP^{-3}|<q^{-4\tilde{A}-1}\}.

\bm{x}\equiv\bm{a}\bmod s
and
x_{3}\not\equiv 0\bmod s^{2}
. Using Hensel lifting again shows that any such
\bm{x}
lifts to
q^{14\tilde{A}}
solutions modulo
s^{7\tilde{A}+2}
, so that

(3.9)

n_{0}(7\tilde{A}+2)\geqslant q^{14\tilde{A}}.

Now let us assume that
b\geqslant 1
. Then any
\bm{x}
counted by
n_{b}(7\tilde{A}+2)
can be written as
\bm{x}=s^{b}\bm{x}^{\prime}
, where
\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[s]/(s^{7\tilde{A}+2-b})
is such that
v_{s}(x_{1}^{\prime})=v_{s}(x_{2}^{\prime})=0
and
v_{s}(x_{3}^{\prime})=1
. Let
m_{b}=ms^{-3b}
and note that
v_{s}(m_{b})=3\tilde{A}-3b\geqslant 3
. We thus see that
\bm{x}
is counted by
n_{b}(7\tilde{A}+2)
if and only if
F_{0}(\bm{x}^{\prime})\equiv m_{b}\bmod s^{7\tilde{A}+2-3b}
. This only depends on
\bm{x}^{\prime}
modulo
s^{7\tilde{A}+2-3b}
and hence

n_{b}(7\tilde{A}+2)\geqslant q^{6b}n_{0}^{\prime}(7\tilde{A}+2-3b),

where
n_{0}^{\prime}
is defined as
n_{0}
with
m
replaced by
m_{b}
. As
v_{s}(m_{b})\geqslant 1
, the argument leading to (
3.9
) also applies to
n_{0}^{\prime}
and yields

(3.10)

n_{b}(7\tilde{A}+2)\geqslant q^{6b}n_{0}^{\prime}(7\tilde{A}+2-3b)\geqslant q^{14\tilde{A}}.

Inserting (
3.10
) into (
3.8
) gives

\displaystyle\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)

\displaystyle\geqslant q^{-14\tilde{A}-2}\sum_{b=0}^{\tilde{A}-1}q^{14\tilde{A}}=\frac{\tilde{A}}{q^{2}}.

Since
\tilde{A}>A-1\gg A
, and
q
is fixed, the proposition follows.
∎

Finally, for the local densities
(
3.3
)
at the finite places we require the following result,
showing that on average they are not too small.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
N\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
. Then

|N|^{-1}\sum_{|k|<|N|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(N,k)}\ll 1.

Proof.

The quantity
\tilde{\rho}(N,k)
is a multiplicative function of
N
by the Chinese remainder theorem, so that

|N|^{-1}\sum_{|k|<|N|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(N,k)}=|N|^{-1}\sum_{|k|<|N|}\prod_{\varpi^{e}\|N}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{e},k)}.

Since the value of

\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{e},k)
only depends on
k
modulo
\varpi^{e}
, we may further write

(3.11)

|N|^{-1}\sum_{|k|<|N|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(N,k)}=\prod_{\varpi^{e}\|N}A(\varpi^{e}),

where

A(\varpi^{e})=\frac{1}{|\varpi^{e}|}\sum_{|k|<|\varpi^{e}|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{e},k)}.

We need to get a sufficiently sharp upper bound for
A(\varpi^{e})
.

Let
\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi^{e},k)
be defined as for
\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{e},k)
, but with the extra constraint that

(x,y,z)
should be coprime to
\varpi
. Then clearly

\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{e},k)\geqslant\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi^{e},k)=\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k),

by Hensel’s lemma and the fact that the characteristic is not
3
.
Thus it follows that

(3.12)

\begin{split}A(\varpi^{e})&\leqslant\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\sum_{|k|<|\varpi|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k)}\\
&=\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,0)}+\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\sum_{0<|k|<|\varpi|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k)},\end{split}

According to (
2.1
), we have

\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,0)=1+\frac{c_{\varpi}}{|\varpi|}-\frac{c_{\varpi}+1}{|\varpi|^{2}},

where
c_{\varpi}\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfies
|c_{\varpi}|\leqslant 2\sqrt{|\varpi|}
.
Hence there exists an absolute constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,0)}\leqslant 1+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{|\varpi|}}.

We proceed to an analysis of

\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k)
when
\varpi\nmid k
. Identifying
k
with the image of its reduction modulo
\varpi
, it is clear that

\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k)=\tilde{\rho}(\varpi,k)=\frac{1}{r^{2}}\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{3}:x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k\},

where
r=|\varpi|
and
\mathbb{F}_{r}\cong\mathcal{O}/\varpi\mathcal{O}
.
Let us write

\nu(r)=\#\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{F}_{r}^{3}:x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}=k\}
.
We may now appeal to the formulae in
[
7
, Chap. 8]
to calculate this quantity.
If
r\equiv 2\bmod{3}
then
\nu(r)=r^{2}
.
If
r\equiv 1\bmod{3}
, we let
\chi:\mathbb{F}_{r}^{*}\to\mathbb{C}
be a non-trivial character of order
3
. Then one may check that

\nu(r)=r^{2}+3\left(\chi(k)+\bar{\chi}(k)\right)r-2\real J(\chi,\chi),

where
J(\chi,\chi)
is a Jacobi sum and satisfies
|J(\chi,\chi)|=\sqrt{r}
.
We deduce that

\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}^{*}(\varpi,k)}\leqslant 1-\frac{3(\chi(k)+\bar{\chi}(k))}{|\varpi|}+\frac{C_{2}}{|\varpi|^{3/2}},

for a suitable absolute constant
C_{2}>0
.

Bringing these estimates together in (
3.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle A(\varpi^{e})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\left(1+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{|\varpi|}}\right)+\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\sum_{0<|k|<|\varpi|}1

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+\frac{C_{1}}{|\varpi|^{3/2}},

if
|\varpi|\equiv 2\bmod{3}
. On the other hand, if

|\varpi|\equiv 1\bmod{3}
, then

\displaystyle A(\varpi^{e})

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\left(1+\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{|\varpi|}}\right)+\frac{1}{|\varpi|}\sum_{0<|k|<|\varpi|}\left(1-\frac{3(\chi(k)+\bar{\chi}(k))}{|\varpi|}+\frac{C_{2}}{|\varpi|^{3/2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leqslant 1-\frac{3}{|\varpi|^{2}}\sum_{0<|k|<|\varpi|}(\chi(k)+\bar{\chi}(k))+\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}}{|\varpi|^{3/2}}.

But the sum over
k
vanishes, since
\chi,\bar{\chi}
are non-trivial characters modulo
\varpi
and the image of
k
runs over all of
\mathbb{F}_{r}^{*}
on reduction modulo
\varpi
.

Returning to (
3.11
), we conclude that

|N|^{-1}\sum_{|k|<|N|}\frac{1}{\tilde{\rho}(N,k)}\leqslant\prod_{\varpi\mid N}\left(1+\frac{C_{1}+C_{2}}{|\varpi|^{3/2}}\right)\ll 1,

as required.
∎

4.
Variance analysis for density 1

For the rest of the paper, assume
\cha(\mathbb{F}_{q})>3
.
Having carried out our study of local densities, in this section we turn to a variance analysis of
r_{A}(k)
and provide a proof of Theorem
1.1
, assuming Theorem
1.3
, whose proof will be completed in §
7
.

Recall the definition
(
3.4
)
of
l_{A}(k;M)
.
We are interested in the arithmetic variance

\operatorname{Var}_{A}(B;M)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in\mathcal{O}\\
\lvert k\rvert=\widehat{B}\end{subarray}}(r_{A}(k)-l_{A}(k;M))^{2},

ranging over all
k\in\mathcal{O}
with
\deg{k}=B
.
We will analyse this roughly as in
[
9
]
*Chapter 2, which is in turn based on arguments of
[
4
]
. A crucial role in our analysis is played by the counting function

(4.1)

N_{w}(P)\coloneqq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{6}\\
F(\bm{x})=0\end{subarray}}w(\bm{x}/P),

where
w
is the weight function constructed in
Definition
3.1
.

We then define

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}\end{subarray}}|r|^{-6}S_{r}(\bm{0}),

to be the
singular series
associated to our counting function
N_{w}(P)
,
where

(4.3)

S_{r}(\bm{0})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|a|<|r|\\
\gcd(a,r)=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{|\bm{x}|<|r|}\psi\left(\frac{aF(\bm{x})}{r}\right).

Moreover, the
singular integral
is defined to be

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sigma_{\infty}

\displaystyle=\int_{|\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{6}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\theta F(\bm{x}))\differential\bm{x}\differential\theta.

We have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Var}_{A}(B;M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{|k|=\widehat{B}}r_{A}(k)^{2}-2\sum_{|k|=\widehat{B}}r_{A}(k)l_{A}(k;M)+\sum_{|k|=\widehat{B}}l_{A}(k;M)^{2}

\displaystyle=\Sigma_{1}-2\Sigma_{2}+\Sigma_{3},

say. We will analyse each term
\Sigma_{i}
individually, for
i=1,2,3
, with the main bulk of our work being concerned with producing an asymptotic for
\Sigma_{1}
. Before commencing our analysis, we will need two auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
N\in\mathcal{O}
,
B=3d+\alpha\geqslant 0
and suppose that
|N|<|P|^{3}q^{-4\tilde{A}}
. Then for any
a\in\mathcal{O}
with
|a|<|N|
, we have

\mathcal{Q}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k|=\widehat{B}\\
k\equiv a\bmod N\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)^{2}=\sigma_{\infty}\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|},

where
\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)
is given by (
3.1
).

Proof.

After writing
k=Nb+a
, with
|b|=\widehat{B}/|N|
and
|a|<|N|
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=

\displaystyle\int_{|\theta_{1}|,|\theta_{2}|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{6}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\theta_{1}F_{0}(\bm{x}_{1})+\theta_{2}F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2})-a(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})P^{-3})

\displaystyle\times\sum_{|b|=\widehat{B}/|N|}\psi(-(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})bNP^{-3})\differential\bm{x}_{1}\differential\bm{x}_{2}\differential\theta_{1}\differential\theta_{2}.

The assumption
|N|<|P|^{3}q^{-4\tilde{A}}
implies

|a(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})P^{-3}|<|N|q^{4\tilde{A}}|P|^{-3}<1,

so that
\psi(a(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})P^{-3})=1
. Moreover, it follows from orthogonality of additive characters that

\sum_{|b|=\widehat{B}/|N|}\psi(-(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})bNP^{-3})=\frac{\widehat{B}}{|N|}\left(q\mathbf{1}_{|\lvert(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})NP^{-3}\rvert|<\frac{|N|}{q\widehat{B}}}-\mathbf{1}_{|\lvert(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})NP^{-3}\rvert|<\frac{|N|}{\widehat{B}}}\right).

We again have
|(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})NP^{-3}|<1
, so that
||(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})NP^{-3}||=|(\theta_{1}+\theta_{2})NP^{-3}|
. After making the change of variables
\theta_{2}=-\theta_{1}+\gamma
and
\theta=\theta_{1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\frac{\widehat{B}}{|N|}\int_{K_{\infty}^{6}}\int_{|\theta|,|\gamma-\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\theta F(\bm{x})-\gamma F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2}))\left(q\mathbf{1}_{|\gamma|<q^{-\alpha-1}}-\mathbf{1}_{|\gamma|<q^{-\alpha}}\right)\differential\gamma\differential\theta\differential\bm{x},

where we applied the change of variables
\bm{x}=(\bm{x}_{1},-\bm{x}_{2})
. If
w(\bm{x})\neq 0
, then
|F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2})|=q^{\alpha}
. Moreover, if
|\gamma|<q^{-\alpha}
and
|\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}
, then
|\gamma-\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}
holds automatically. This implies

\displaystyle\int_{|\gamma|<q^{-\alpha}}\psi(-\gamma F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2}))\differential\gamma=q^{-\alpha}\mathbf{1}_{|F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2})|<q^{\alpha}}=0.

In addition, if
|\gamma|<q^{-1-\alpha}
, then
\psi(-\gamma F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2}))=1
since
|F_{0}(\bm{x}_{2})|=q^{\alpha}
for
\bm{x}_{2}\in\supp\nu
. We therefore obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{q\widehat{B}}{|N|}\int_{K_{\infty}^{6}}\int_{|\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}w(\bm{x})\psi(\theta F(\bm{x}))\differential\theta\differential\bm{x}\vol(\{|\gamma|<q^{-1-\alpha}\})

\displaystyle=\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|}\sigma_{\infty}

since
\widehat{B}=|P|^{3}q^{\alpha}
.
∎

Let
\bm{y}\in K_{\infty}^{3}
be such that
|\bm{y}|\leqslant q^{\tilde{A}}
and
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{T}^{3}
. If we set
\bm{x}=\bm{y}+t^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\bm{z}
,
then a straightforward computation shows that
|F(\bm{x})|=q^{\alpha}
if and only if
|F(\bm{y})|=q^{\alpha}
. Similarly
\nu(\bm{x})\neq 0
holds if and only if
\nu(\bm{y})\neq 0
. In particular,
\nu
is invariant under translation by
t^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\mathbb{T}^{3}
. If we define

(4.5)

R_{A,\alpha}=\left\{\bm{y}\in(t^{\tilde{A}+1}\mathbb{T}/t^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\mathbb{T})^{3}\colon\begin{array}[]{l}|F_{0}(\bm{y})|=q^{\alpha},\\
1\leqslant|y_{1}|=|y_{2}|\leqslant q^{\tilde{A}},\\
|y_{2}|q^{-1}\leqslant|y_{3}|\leqslant|y_{2}|\end{array}\right\},

we thus have the well-defined identity

(4.6)

\nu(\bm{x})=\sum_{\bm{y}\in R_{A,\alpha}}\mathbf{1}_{|\bm{x}-\bm{y}|<q^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
N\in\mathcal{O}
,
B=3d+\alpha\geqslant 0
and suppose that
|N|<|P|q^{-6\tilde{A}}
. If
\bm{b}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
is such that
|\bm{b}|<|N|
, then

\sum_{\bm{y}\equiv\bm{b}\bmod N}\nu(\bm{y}/P)\sigma_{\infty,A}(F_{0}(\bm{y}))=\sigma_{\infty}\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|^{3}}.

Proof.

An application of the Poisson summation formula yields

\displaystyle\sum_{\bm{y}\equiv\bm{b}\bmod N}

\displaystyle\nu(\bm{y}/P)\sigma_{\infty,A}(F_{0}(\bm{y}))

\displaystyle=\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|^{3}}\int_{|\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\nu(\bm{x})\psi(\theta F_{0}(\bm{x}))\sum_{\bm{v}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}}\psi\left(-\frac{\bm{b}\cdot\bm{v}}{N}\right)I(\theta,\bm{v})\differential\bm{x}\differential\theta,

where we have temporarily written

I(\theta,\bm{v})=\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\nu(\bm{y})\psi\left(\theta F_{0}(\bm{y})+\frac{P\bm{y}\cdot\bm{v}}{N}\right)\differential\bm{y}.

The contribution from
\bm{v}=\bm{0}
is clearly

\displaystyle\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|^{3}}\int_{|\theta|\leqslant q^{4\tilde{A}}}\int_{K_{\infty}^{3}}\nu(\bm{x})\psi(\theta F_{0}(\bm{x}))I(\theta,\bm{0})\differential\bm{x}\differential\theta

\displaystyle=\frac{|P|^{3}}{|N|^{3}}\sigma_{\infty}.

Using (
4.6
) to decompose the weight function
\nu
into smaller boxes and applying a change of variables, we obtain

I(\theta,\bm{v})=q^{3(\alpha-2\tilde{A})}\sum_{\bm{y}\in R_{A,\alpha}}\psi\left(\frac{P\bm{y}\cdot\bm{v}}{N}\right)\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\psi\left(\theta F_{0}(\bm{y}+t^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\bm{z})+\frac{Pt^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\bm{z}\cdot\bm{v}}{N}\right)\differential\bm{z}.

We can now use
[
2
, Lemma 5.1]
to deduce that the inner integral vanishes for any
\bm{y}\in R_{A,\alpha}
, and hence
I(\theta,\bm{v})=0
unless
|P|q^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}|N|^{-1}|\bm{v}|\leqslant\max\{1,|\theta|H_{G}\}
, where

G(\bm{z})=F_{0}(\bm{y}+t^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\bm{z}).

Since
|F_{0}(\bm{y})|=q^{\alpha}
, one readily checks that
H_{G}=q^{\alpha}
. Consequently, if
\bm{v}\neq\bm{0}
then
I(\theta,\bm{v})
can only be non-zero if

|P|q^{\alpha-2\tilde{A}}\leqslant|N|\max\{1,q^{\alpha}|\theta|\}\leqslant|N|q^{\alpha+4\tilde{A}},

which is impossible by our assumption on
|N|
.
∎

For any
r\in\mathcal{O}
, define

\tilde{\rho}(r)=|r|^{-5}\#\{\bm{x}\in(\mathcal{O}/r\mathcal{O})^{6}\colon F(\bm{x})\equiv 0\bmod r\}.

Recalling the definition (
3.4
) of

l_{A}(k;M)
,
it is now easy to call upon the previous two lemmas to evaluate
\Sigma_{2}
and
\Sigma_{3}
.
Firstly, by the definition of
\nu
we have

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}\end{subarray}}\nu(\bm{x}/P)l_{A}(F_{0}(\bm{x});M)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{b}\bmod N}\tilde{\rho}(N,F_{0}(\bm{b}))\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{x}\equiv\bm{b}\bmod N\end{subarray}}\nu(\bm{x}/P)\sigma_{\infty,A}(F_{0}(\bm{x})).

On assuming that
|N|<|P|q^{-6\tilde{A}}
and appealing to
Lemma
4.2
, it now follows that

\Sigma_{2}=\sigma_{\infty}|P|^{3}\sum_{\bm{b}\bmod N}|N|^{-5}\rho(N,F_{0}(\bm{b})),

where

\rho(N,k)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}|N|^{2}\tilde{\rho}(N,k)=\#\{(x,y,z)\in(\mathcal{O}/N\mathcal{O})^{3}\colon x^{3}+y^{3}+z^{3}\equiv k\bmod{N}\}
.
It readily follows
that

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}=\sigma_{\infty}\tilde{\rho}(N)|P|^{3}.

Similarly, Lemma
4.1
gives

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{3}&=\sum_{b\bmod N}\tilde{\rho}(N,b)^{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|k|=\widehat{B}\\
k\equiv b\bmod N\end{subarray}}\sigma_{\infty,A}(k)^{2}\\
&=\sigma_{\infty}|P|^{3}\sum_{b\bmod N}|N|^{-5}\rho(N,b)^{2}\\
&=\sigma_{\infty}\tilde{\rho}(N)|P|^{3},\end{split}

providing only that
|N|<|P|^{3}q^{-4\tilde{A}}
.

Finally, assuming (R2) and Conjecture
6.2
it follows from Theorem
1.3
that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma_{1}&=\sum_{|k|=\widehat{B}}r_{A}(k)^{2}\\
&=N_{w}(P)\\
&=\sigma_{\infty}\mathfrak{S}|P|^{3}+\sum_{L\in\Upsilon}\sum_{\bm{x}\in L\cap\mathcal{O}^{6}}w(\bm{x}/P)+o_{w}(|P|^{3}),\end{split}

as
|P|\to\infty
.
Before completing the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need two more auxiliary results.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any
L\in\Upsilon
, we have

\sum_{\bm{x}\in L\cap\mathcal{O}^{6}}w(\bm{x}/P)\ll A|P|^{3}.

Proof.

By applying a suitable
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear change of variables we may reduce to the case where
L
is given by

(1)

x_{1}+x_{4}=x_{2}+x_{5}=x_{3}+x_{6}=0
, or

(2)

x_{1}+x_{2}=x_{3}+x_{4}=x_{5}+x_{6}=0
.

We begin with (1). Since
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}
determine
x_{4},x_{5},x_{6}
uniquely, it suffices to count
\bm{x}\in\mathcal{O}^{3}
for which
\nu(\bm{x}/P)\neq 0
. Let
1\leqslant b\leqslant\tilde{A}
and suppose that
|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|=q^{b}|P|
. Writing
N=x_{1}^{3}+x_{2}^{3}
, we must then have
|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|\leqslant|x_{3}|q
and

(4.10)

|N+x_{3}^{3}|=q^{\alpha}|P|^{3},

if
\nu(\bm{x}/P)\neq 0
. If
N=0
, then there are
O(q^{b}|P|)
choices for
(x_{1},x_{2})
, while
|x_{3}^{3}|=q^{\alpha}|P|^{3}
implies
|x_{3}|=q^{\alpha/3}|P|
, which in turn implies that

|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|\leqslant q^{\alpha/3+1}|P|
, whence
b\leqslant\alpha/3+1
. Thus the total contribution from this case is
O(|P|^{2})
.

Now let us assume that
N\neq 0
and fix pairwise distinct solutions
\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\beta_{3}\in K^{\text{sep}}
of the equation
x^{3}+N=0
. The absolute value
|\cdot|
extends uniquely to
K^{\text{sep}}
, so that (
4.10
) gives

|(x_{3}-\beta_{1})(x_{3}-\beta_{2})(x_{3}-\beta_{3})|=q^{\alpha}|P|^{3}.

As
|\beta_{i}-\beta_{j}|\gg 1
for
i\neq j
, we have
|x_{3}-\beta_{i}|\gg|x_{3}|\geqslant q^{b-1}|P|
for at least two indices
i\in\{1,2,3\}
. Without loss of generality, assume that it holds for
i=2,3
. We then get

|x_{3}-\beta_{1}|\ll q^{\alpha+2-2b}|P|,

for which there are
O(q^{\alpha+2-2b}|P|)
possible
x_{3}\in\mathcal{O}
. As there are
O(q^{2b}|P|^{2})
choices for
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathcal{O}
, this yields after summing over
b
an overall contribution of
O(A|P|^{3})
, which completes case (1).

For (2), observe that if
\nu(\bm{x}/P)\neq 0
and
x_{1}+x_{2}=0
, then we must have
|x_{3}|=q^{\alpha/3}|P|
and
|x_{1}|=|x_{2}|\leqslant q^{\alpha/3+1}|P|
. In particular, once
x_{3}
is fixed there are
O(q^{\alpha/3}|P|)
choices for
x_{1},x_{2}
. In addition,
x_{3}
determines
x_{4}
uniquely and by the symmetry at hand there are
O(q^{\alpha/3}|P|)
available
x_{4},x_{5}\in\mathcal{O}
. Therefore, the total contribution is
O(q^{\alpha}|P|^{3})
, which is more than sufficient.
∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
N
be as in (
3.2
). Then

\tilde{\rho}(N)=\mathfrak{S}+O(\widehat{M}^{-2/3+\varepsilon})
,
where
\mathfrak{S}
is given by (
4.2
).

Proof.

Using orthogonality of characters and collecting terms according to their greatest common divisor, for any prime power
\varpi^{k}
we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\rho}(\varpi^{k})

\displaystyle=|\varpi|^{-6k}\sum_{a\bmod\varpi^{k}}\sum_{\bm{x}\bmod\varpi^{k}}\psi\left(\frac{aF(\bm{x})}{\varpi^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=0}^{k}|\varpi|^{-6(k-l)}S_{\varpi^{k-l}}(\bm{0}),

in the notation of (
4.3
).
Thus the Chinese remainder theorem yields

\tilde{\rho}(N)=\sum_{r\mid N}|r|^{-6}S_{r}(\bm{0}).

It follows from the definition of
N
that any
r\in\mathcal{O}^{+}
with
\deg r\leqslant M
divides
N
. Therefore

\displaystyle|\tilde{\rho}(N)-\mathfrak{S}|

Given

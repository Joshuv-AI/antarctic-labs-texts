Abstract
. Jet spaces on ℝ n \mathbb{R}^{n} have been shown to have a canonical structure of stratified Lie groups (also known as Carnot groups). We construct jet spaces over stratified Lie groups adapted to horizontal differentiation and show that these jet spaces are themselves stratified Lie groups. Furthermore, we show that these jet spaces support a prolongation theory for contact maps, and in particular, a Bäcklund type theorem holds. A byproduct of these results is an embedding theorem that shows that every stratified Lie group of step s + 1 s+1 can be embedded in a jet space over a stratified Lie group of step s s .

58A20; 22E25; 35R03; 32C09.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

Classical jet spaces are vector bundles
\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{\ell})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with a horizontal bundle
\mathscr{H}^{m}\subset T\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
,
such that jets of functions
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
are exactly those sections that are tangent to
\mathscr{H}^{m}
.
See
[
6
]
for further reference.

It is well known that these jet spaces
\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
carry a structure of stratified Lie group for which
\mathscr{H}^{m}
is left-invariant,
see
[
7
]
.

Stratified Lie groups
are also called
Carnot groups
and we use the two terms as synonyms.

We observed that stratified Lie groups of step 2 can be embedded into a jet space
\mathtt{J}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
for a suitable choice of
n
and
\ell
.
By “embedding” we mean that there is a strata-preserving injective homomorphism of Lie groups.
At the same time, we realized that stratified Lie groups of step larger than 2 cannot be embedded in such jet spaces, see Remark
7.1
.
A heuristic analysis led us to believe that we needed to substitute the
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};\mathbb{R}^{\ell})
with a stratified Lie group.

In this paper, we consider jets adapted to horizontal differentiation of smooth functions
f
from a stratified group
G
into a vector space
W
.
The stratification of
G
induces an “intrinsic” notion of degree both for polynomials and for left-invariant differential operators.
Following
[
2
]
,
it is natural to reorganize the derivatives of
f
using this intrinsic degree.
Equivalently, one can reorganize the homogeneous polynomials of the Taylor expansion of
f
.
See also
[
4
]
.

Such a reorganization allows us to construct vector bundles
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)\to G

endowed with a horizontal bundle
\mathscr{H}^{m}\subset T\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
that characterizes sections defined as “horizontal” jets of functions
G\to W
.
We call such spaces
jet spaces over
G
and we prove two fundamental facts of jet spaces, that is, a prolongation and a de-prolongation theorem.

Finally, we show that every stratified Lie group of step
s+1
can be embedded into some
\mathtt{J}^{s}(G^{\prime};W)
for a stratified Lie group
G^{\prime}
of step
s
.

1.2.
Detailed description of results

For a vector space
W
and a simply connected Lie group
G
with stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s}V_{i}
, bracket generated by
V_{1}
, our starting point is to consider the space
\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
of
k
-multi-linear maps
A
on
V_{1}
given by
k
-th order horizontal differentiation of smooth functions
f:G\to W
at the neutral element
e\in G
.
More precisely, if
v_{1},\dots,v_{k}\in V_{1}
and
\tilde{v}_{1},\dots,\tilde{v}_{k}
denote the corresponding left invariant vector fields on
G
, then

A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=\tilde{v}_{k}\dots\tilde{v}_{1}f(e).

We then define
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m}\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
,
where we set
\mathtt{HD}^{0}(\mathfrak{g};W)=W
.

If
G
is abelian, i.e.,
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
for some
n
, then
\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)
is the space of symmetric
k
-multilinear maps from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
W
.
If
G
is not abelian, we don’t have such a clear characterization of
\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, but we do have an algorithm to construct a basis of it, see Remark
4.8
.

The jet space
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
will be the simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra

\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)

where the semi-direct product is given by the representation of
\mathfrak{g}
over
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
that is induced by
right-contractions
.
If
v\in V_{1}
and
A\in\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, we define the right contraction of
A
by
v
as the
(k-1)
-multi-linear map
v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A\in\mathtt{HD}^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
,

v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1},v).

In Proposition
3.3
, we show that this operation
v\mapsto v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}
extends to a Lie algebra anti-morphism
\mathfrak{g}\to\mathtt{End}(\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W))
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
turns out to be again stratified of step
\max\{s,m+1\}
, with layers of the form

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)_{1}&=&V_{1}\oplus\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)\\
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)_{2}&=&V_{2}\oplus\mathtt{HD}^{m-1}(\mathfrak{g};W)\\
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)_{3}&=&V_{3}\oplus\mathtt{HD}^{m-3}(\mathfrak{g};W)\\
\vdots&&\vdots\end{array}

The contact structure
\mathscr{H}^{m}
of the jet space
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is the left invariant distribution defined by the first layer
\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)_{1}
.
A smooth function
f:G\to W
defines a section
\mathtt{J}^{m}f:G\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
in a natural way, and we call
\mathtt{J}^{m}f
the
(horizontal) jet of
f
.
We show in Proposition
3.8
that a section
\gamma:G\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is the jet of a function if and only if
d\gamma
maps
\mathscr{H}_{G}
to
\mathscr{H}^{m}
, where
\mathscr{H}_{G}
is the left-invariant distribution on
G
defined by
V_{1}
.

Once the definition and the structure of
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
are set, we prove three main results.
First of all, we demonstrate that contact maps
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
are subject to a prolongation theory similar to the classical case and that a Bäcklund type theorem holds.

Theorem A

(Prolongation Theorem)
.

Suppose
m\geq 0
,
\Omega\subset\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is open, and that
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is a contact map. If
\pi_{m}:\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
denotes the projection along
\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, then
there is an open set
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)

and a unique contact map
\hat{F}:\hat{\Omega}\to\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
such that

\pi_{m}\circ\hat{F}=F\circ\pi_{m}.

A more precise statement is Theorem
5.2
, where we give a precise definition of the open set
\hat{\Omega}
.
The size of the set
\hat{\Omega}
remains unclear to us in general.
For sure, if
F
is close enough to the identity map, then
\hat{\Omega}
is not empty.
Moreover, if
F
is itself the prolongation of a contact map, then
\hat{\Omega}=\pi_{m}^{-1}(\Omega)
, see Remark
5.4
.
As a consequence of the following Theorem
B
, this is always the case when
m\geq 2
.
We don’t know if it can be proven that
\pi_{m}(\hat{\Omega})=\Omega
also when
m=1
.

Theorem B

(de-Prolongation Theorem)
.

If
m\geq 2
, then every contact diffeomorphism
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is the prolongation of a contact diffeomorphism
\mathtt{J}^{1}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{1}(G;W)
.

Moreover, suppose that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(A)

\dim(W)>1
, or

(B)

for every
v\in V_{1}\setminus\{0\}
there is
v^{\prime}\in V_{1}
with
[v,v^{\prime}]\neq 0
.

Then every contact diffeomorphism
\mathtt{J}^{1}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{1}(G;W)
is the prolongation of a contact diffeomorphism
\mathtt{J}^{0}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{0}(G;W)
.

We can also state Theorem
B
for contact diffeomorphisms that are defined on domains in
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
, in which case the de-prolongation will only be local a priori.
A precise result is stated in Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
.
We show in Remark
6.3
that the second part of Theorem
B
is sharp.

Finally, we prove that stratified groups embed into jet spaces.
It has already been proved by Montgomery in
[
3
, §6.5.1]
that every manifold of dimension
n
endowed with a distribution of rank
k
is
locally smoothly embeddable
into
\mathtt{J}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{k};\mathbb{R}^{n-k})
.
Our result differ from Montgomery’s as we seek a
global embedding as stratified Lie groups
, that is, we want to reconstruct a stratified Lie group
G
as a stratified subgroup of some jet space.
This is simply not possible with standard jet spaces, as we explain in Remark
7.1
.

Notice that, of course,
G
embeds into
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
, by construction.
However, we prove that we can reconstruct
G
inside the jet space of a stratified group with lower step.

Theorem C

(Embedding into jet spaces)
.

Let
G
be a stratified group of step
s+1
.
Then there exists a stratified group
G^{\prime}
of step
s
and a vector space
W
such that
G
embeds as a stratified group in
\mathtt{J}^{s}(G^{\prime};W)
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce stratified Lie groups and we
fix basic notation, conventions and a few known results.

2.1.
Notation choices

If
V
and
W
are vector spaces, we denote by
\mathtt{Lin}^{k}(V;W)
the vector space of all
k
-multilinear maps from
V
to
W
.
In particular,
\mathtt{End}(V)=\mathtt{Lin}(V;V)
is the Lie algebra of all linear maps
V\to V
with Lie brackets

(1)

[A,B]=AB-BA.

If
E\to G
is a vector bundle over a manifold
G
, we denote by
\Gamma(E)
the space of smooth sections of
E
.
If
X\in\Gamma(E)
and
p\in M
, we write
X(p)
or
X_{p}
for the evaluation of
X
at
p
.
If
G,M
are manifolds, we denote by
C^{\infty}(G;M)
the space of smooth maps
G\to M
.
If
G
is a Lie group, we denote its identity element by
e_{G}
or
e
,
and we identify its Lie algebra with the tangent space at the identity element;
if
p\in G
, then
L_{p}:G\to G
is the left translation
L_{p}(x)=px
.
If
\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra, we denote by
\mathtt{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
the space of derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
and by
\mathtt{Aut}(\mathfrak{g})
the group of Lie algebra automorphisms of
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
G
is a Lie group, we denote by
\mathtt{Aut}(G)
the group of Lie group automorphisms of
G
.

If
V
is a vector space, let
\mathscr{T}(V)=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{\infty}V^{\otimes j}
be the tensor algebra of
V
.
Define the tensor spaces
\mathscr{T}^{m}(V)=V^{\otimes m}
and
\mathscr{T}^{\leq m}(V)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m}\mathscr{T}^{k}(V)
.
It is clear that
\mathscr{T}^{m}(V)^{*}=\mathscr{T}^{m}(V^{*})
.

2.2.
Vector fields

Let
G
be a smooth manifold and
W
a finite dimensional real vector space.
We consider the differential of a smooth map
f:G\to W
as a map
\,\mathrm{d}f:TG\to W
.
More precisely, if
v\in T_{p}G
, then
\,\mathrm{d}f(v)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}|_{t=0}f(\gamma(t))\in W
for any
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to G
smooth with
\gamma(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
.

Vector fields
\tilde{v}\in\Gamma(TG)

are differential operators
C^{\infty}(G;W)\to C^{\infty}(G;W)
:
if
f:G\to W
is smooth, then
\tilde{v}f:G\to W
is the map
\tilde{v}f(p)=\,\mathrm{d}f(\tilde{v}(p))
.
The Lie bracket of vector fields
\tilde{v},\tilde{w}\in\Gamma(TG)
is defined as
the commutator in the algebra of differential operators, i.e.,

(2)

[\tilde{v},\tilde{w}]=\tilde{v}\tilde{w}-\tilde{w}\tilde{v}.

2.3.
Vector fields on a Lie group

Let
G
be a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
If
X\in C^{\infty}(G;\mathfrak{g})
, i.e.,
X
is a smooth function
G\to\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
\tilde{X}
the corresponding vector field on
G
defined by
\tilde{X}(p)=\,\mathrm{d}L_{p}[X(p)]
.
The vector field
\tilde{X}
is characterized by means of the Mauer–Cartan form
\omega_{G}
as the only vector field
\tilde{X}\in\Gamma(TG)
such that
\omega_{G}(\tilde{X}(p))=X(p)
for all
p\in G
.
We define Lie brackets on
C^{\infty}(G;\mathfrak{g})
as follows:
If
X,Y:G\to\mathfrak{g}
, then we define
[X,Y](p)=\,\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}](p)\in\mathfrak{g}
.
Notice that left-invariant vector fields
\tilde{X}:G\to\Gamma(TG)
correspond to constant functions
G\to\mathfrak{g}
, and that if
X,Y:G\to\mathfrak{g}
are constant then
[X,Y](p)=[X(p),Y(p)]
for all
p
.

If
f,g\in C^{\infty}(G)
and
X,Y:G\to\mathfrak{g}
are constant, then

(3)

[fX,gY]=f\cdot(\tilde{X}g)\cdot Y-g\cdot(\tilde{Y}f)\cdot X+fg[X,Y].

Sometimes we will use right-invariant vector fields.
For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we denote by
x^{\dagger}
the right-invariant vector field on
G
with
x^{\dagger}(e_{G})=x
.

2.4.
Derivatives along vectors and linear vector fields

Let
V
be a vector space.
As vector spaces are instances of abelian Lie groups, we are consistent with the notation introduced in Section
2.3
by setting

\tilde{v}f(p)=\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}|_{t=0}f(p+tv)

for any vector
v\in V
.

We will need the following elementary observation.
Notice that a linear map
X\in\mathtt{End}(V)
defines a vector field
\tilde{X}(p)=Xp
.
So, if
X,Y\in\mathtt{End}(V)
, then we have two ways of taking Lie brackets:
as linear maps in (
1
),
[X,Y]_{\mathtt{End}(V)}=XY-YX\in\mathtt{End}(V)
;
as vector fields in (
2
),
[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]_{\Gamma(TV)}=\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}-\tilde{Y}\tilde{X}\in\Gamma(TV)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X,Y\in\mathtt{End}(V)
, then
[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]_{\Gamma(TV)}=-[X,Y]_{\mathtt{End}(V)}^{\widetilde{\penalty\ }}
.

Proof.

Let
f:V\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function and
p\in V
.
Notice that

\displaystyle\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}f(p)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}(\tilde{Y}f)(p+sXp)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}f((p+sXp)+tY(p+sXp))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}f(p+sXp+tYp+stYXp)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}(f(p+sXp+tYp)+f(p+tYp+stYXp))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}(f(p+sXp+tYp)+f(p+tYp)+f(p+stYXp))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}(f(p+sXp+tYp)+f(p+tYp)+sf(p+tYXp)),

where we used the formula
\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{r=0}g(r,r)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}(g(r,0)+g(0,r))
.
Similarly,

\tilde{Y}\tilde{X}f(p)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s}\right|_{s=0}(f(p+sXp+tYp)+f(p+tYp)+sf(p+tXYp)).

Hence,

\displaystyle[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]f(p)

\displaystyle=\tilde{X}\tilde{Y}f(p)-\tilde{Y}\tilde{X}f(p)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}(f(p+tYXp)-f(p+tXYp)))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f(p+t(YXp-XYp))

\displaystyle=(-[X,Y]p)^{\widetilde{\penalty\ }}f(p).

∎

2.5.
Vector valued differential forms

Let
G
be a smooth manifold and
W
a vector space.
We define
W
-valued 1-forms
as

\Omega^{1}(G;W):=\Gamma(\mathtt{Lin}(TG;W)),

where
\mathtt{Lin}(TG;W)
is the vector bundle over
G
whose fiber at
p\in G
is the space
\mathtt{Lin}(T_{p}G;W)
of linear maps from
T_{p}G
to
W
.

Suppose that
G
is a Lie group with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
Via the Mauer–Cartan form, we identify
TG
with
G\times\mathfrak{g}
and thus
\mathtt{Lin}(TG;W)
with
G\times\mathtt{Lin}(\mathfrak{g};W)
.
In particular,

\Omega^{1}(G;W)\simeq C^{\infty}(G;\mathtt{Lin}(\mathfrak{g};W)).

In other words, if
\omega\in C^{\infty}(G;\mathtt{Lin}(\mathfrak{g};W))
, then we define

\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{1}(G;W)
as
\tilde{\omega}(p)(v)=\omega(p)(\,\mathrm{d}L_{p}^{-1}v)
for
v\in T_{p}G
.

A
left-invariant
W
-valued 1-form
is an element
\tilde{\omega}
of
\Omega^{1}(G;W)
such that, for all
p,g\in G
,
\tilde{\omega}(gp)=\tilde{\omega}(p)\circ\,\mathrm{d}L_{g}^{-1}
.
In particular, left-invariant
W
-valued 1-forms correspond to constants in
C^{\infty}(G;\mathtt{Lin}(\mathfrak{g};W))
.

Any
W
-valued 1-form
\tilde{\omega}\in\Omega^{1}(G;W)
defines a subset
\mathrm{Ker}(\tilde{\omega})\subseteq TG
given by all vectors
v\in TG
such that
\tilde{\omega}(v)=0
. In particular,
\mathrm{Ker}(\tilde{\omega})
is a subbundle of
TG
if
\tilde{\omega}
is left-invariant.

2.6.
Anti-semi-direct product

Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be Lie algebras, and
\psi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathtt{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
an anti-morphism of Lie algebras, that is, a linear map such that

\psi([x,y])=-[\psi(x),\psi(y)]
. For a given
\psi
, we obtain a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathfrak{h}
consisting of the vector space
\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{h}
equipped with the bracket

[(x,X),(y,Y)]=([x,y],-[X,Y]+\psi(y)X-\psi(x)Y).

Let
G
and
H
be the corresponding connected simply connected Lie groups of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
. Then there is a anti-morphism of Lie groups
\phi:G\to\mathtt{Aut}(H)
(that is,
\phi(ab)=\phi(b)\phi(a)
) such that

\phi(\exp(x))_{*}=e^{\psi(x)}\in\mathtt{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
, for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. We obtain Lie Group
\mathtt{J}=G\ltimes_{\phi}H
on
G\times H
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{j}=\mathfrak{g}\ltimes_{\psi}\mathfrak{h}
by setting

(a,A)(b,B)=(ab,B\phi(b)A)
.

Assume that
\mathfrak{h}
is abelian, that is, a finite-dimensional vector space.
Then

\displaystyle[(x,X),(y,Y)]

\displaystyle=([x,y],\psi(y)X-\psi(x)Y),

\displaystyle(a,A)(b,B)

\displaystyle=(ab,B+\phi(b)A),

\displaystyle(\exp(x),A)(\exp(y),B)

\displaystyle=(\exp(x)\exp(y),B+e^{\psi(y)}A).

If
(a,A)\in\mathtt{J}
and
(x,X)\in\mathfrak{j}
, then the differential of the left translation
L_{(a,A)}
at
(e_{G},0)
applied to
(x,X)
is

dL_{(a,A)}|_{(e_{G},0)}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
X\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}dL_{a}&0\\
\psi(\cdot)A&\mathrm{Id}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
X\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\tilde{x}(a)\\
\psi(x)A+X\end{pmatrix}.

Hence
if
(x,X)\in\mathfrak{j}
, the corresponding left-invariant vector field

(x,X)^{\widetilde{\penalty\ }}
computed at
(a,A)\in\mathtt{J}
is

(x,X)^{\widetilde{\penalty\ }}(a,A)=(\tilde{x}(a),\psi(x)A+X).

In order to describe the exponential map
\exp_{\mathtt{J}}:\mathfrak{j}\to\mathtt{J}
,
we need to find a curve
t\mapsto(a(t),A(t))\in\mathtt{J}
such that

a(0)=e_{G}
,
A(0)=0
,
a^{\prime}(t)=\tilde{x}(a(t))
and
A^{\prime}(t)=\psi(x)A(t)+X
.
By standard considerations, we get

(4)

\exp_{\mathtt{J}}(x,X)=\left(\exp(x),\int_{0}^{1}e^{(1-s)\psi(x)}X\,\mathrm{d}s\right).

2.7.
Stratified Lie groups

A
stratification
for a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is a direct sum decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{s}
where
[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{1+j}
for
1\leq j\leq s
, and
[V_{i},V_{j}]=0
for
i+j>s
.
The projection
\mathfrak{g}\to V_{i}
will be denoted by
\Pi_{i}
.
A
stratified Lie group
is a connected, simply connected nilpotent Lie group whose Lie algebra is stratified.
Stratified Lie groups are also known as
Carnot groups
and we use the two terms as synonyms.

The subbundle of
TG
given pointwise by
\{\tilde{v}(p)\in T_{p}G:v\in V_{1},\ p\in G\}
is called the
horizontal bundle
and denoted
\mathscr{H}_{G}
.
Furthermore, for every
\lambda>0
, the
dilation

\delta_{\lambda}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, is the Lie algebra isomorphism defined by
\Pi_{i}\circ\delta_{\lambda}=\lambda^{i}\Pi_{i}
.
By conjugating the Lie algebra dilation with the exponential map, we get a corresponding dilation
G\to G
, also denoted
\delta_{\lambda}
, which by definition, is a group isomorphism.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
F:G\to G
be a smooth map with
dF(\mathscr{H}_{G})\subset\mathscr{H}_{G}
and let
p_{0}\in G
be such that
dF|_{\mathscr{H}_{G}(p_{0})}
is injective.
Then
dF(p_{0})
is a linear isomorphism.

Proof.

We say that two vector fields
\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{X}_{2}
are
F
-related if

dF(p)\tilde{X}_{1}(p)=\tilde{X}_{2}(F(p))

for all
p
.
If
\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{Y}_{1}
are
F
-related to
\tilde{X}_{2},\tilde{Y}_{2}
, respectively, then
[\tilde{X}_{1},\tilde{Y}_{1}]
is
F
-related to
[\tilde{X}_{2},\tilde{Y}_{2}]
, see
[
8
, Proposition 1.55]
.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
p_{0}
where
dF|_{\mathscr{H}_{G}(p)}
is injective for all
p\in U
.
Define
d_{G}F(p):\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
by setting

d_{G}F(p)v=dL_{F(p)}^{-1}(dF(p)(dL_{p}v)).

Since
dF(\mathscr{H}_{G})\subset\mathscr{H}_{G}
and
dF|_{\mathscr{H}_{G}(p)}
is injective for all
p\in U
, then we have a smooth function

d_{G}F|_{V_{1}}:U\to\mathtt{GL}(V_{1})
, where
\mathtt{GL}(V_{1})
is the group of invertible linear maps
V_{1}\to V_{1}
.
Given
X_{2}:U\to V_{1}
, define
X^{F}_{1}:U\to V_{1}
as

X^{F}_{2}(p)=d_{G}F(p)|_{V_{1}}^{-1}X_{2}(F(p)).

It is clear that the corresponding vector fields
\tilde{X}^{F}_{1}
and
\tilde{X}_{2}
are
F
-related.

Now, since there is a basis of
T_{F(p_{0})}G
whose elements are evaluations at
p_{0}
of iterated Lie brackets of some horizontal vector fields, then each element of such a basis is in the image of
dF(p_{0})
.
Therefore,
dF(p_{0})
is surjective, and thus a linear isomorphism.
∎

3.
The Jet Space Over a Carnot Group

3.1.
Horizontal derivatives

For
f:G\to W
smooth,
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
p\in G
, we define

as the
k
-multi-linear map from

to

defined by

A_{f,p}^{k}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})=\tilde{v}_{k}\dots\tilde{v}_{1}f(p).

Notice that, if we define

, then
the left invariance of the vector fields
\tilde{v}_{i}

implies that

For

, we denote by
\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
the vector subspace of
\mathtt{Lin}^{k}(V_{1};W)
of all such
k
-multi-linear maps.
For
k=0
, we set
\mathtt{HD}^{0}(\mathfrak{g};W)=W
coherently with the definition of

. We then define

\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{m}\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W).

Elements of
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
are a priori only sums of derivatives of functions
G\to W
.
Corollary
4.6
will show that every element in
\mathtt{HD}^{\leq m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
is the derivative of a function.

3.2.
Right Contractions

If
k\geq 2
and

, then for

, the

of

by

, denoted

\mathtt{Lin}^{k-1}(V_{1};W)
given by

(5)

v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})=\,A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1},v).

Moreover, if

then

Lemma 3.1
.

If
A\in\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
and

, then
v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A\in\mathtt{HD}^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
.
Furthermore, if

and

v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}A_{f,p\exp(tv)}^{k-1}.

Proof.

The first assertion of the Lemma is a consequence of (

is a smooth curve in the vector space
\mathtt{HD}^{k-1}(\mathfrak{g};W)

Turning to the proof of (

then

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{2}\tilde{v}_{1}f_{p}(y)

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}_{2}(\tilde{v}_{1}f_{p})(y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}}\right|_{s_{2}=0}(\tilde{v}_{1}f_{p})(y\exp(s_{2}v_{2}))

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s_{2}}\right|_{s_{2}=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}}\right|_{s_{1}=0}f(py\exp(s_{2}v_{2})\exp(s_{1}v_{1})).

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}A_{f,p\exp(tv)}^{k-1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s_{1}}\right|_{s_{1}=0}\cdots\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}s_{k-1}}\right|_{s_{k-1}=0}f(p\exp(tv)\exp(s_{k-1}v_{k-1})\cdots\exp(s_{1}v_{1}))

\displaystyle=\tilde{v}\tilde{v}_{k-1}\cdots\tilde{v}_{1}f(p)=A_{f,p}^{k}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1},v)=v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

The linear span of the image of the bilinear map

Let

4.3.
Bases

Let
\mathscr{B}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
adapted to the stratification
\bigoplus_{j}V_{j}
, i.e.,
there is a non-decreasing sequence of integers
\{\mathbf{w}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
such that
b_{i}\in V_{\mathbf{w}_{i}}
for all
i
.
If
I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is a multi-index, define
\mathbf{w}(I)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mathbf{w}_{j}I_{j}
.
We denote by
x_{j}
the exponential coordinates given by
\mathscr{B}
, i.e., smooth functions
G\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\exp(\sum_{j}x_{j}(p)b_{j})=p
for all
p\in G
.
The
homogeneous degree
of a monomial
x^{I}=\prod_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{I_{j}}
is

\deg(x^{I})=\mathbf{w}(I)
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Using the above notation,
\{x^{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
is a basis of
\mathscr{P}^{m}_{e}(G;\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\{x^{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
is a linearly independent subset of
\mathscr{P}^{m}_{e}(G;\mathbb{R})
.
To prove the claim, we set
|I|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}I_{j}
, so that
|I|\leq\mathbf{w}(I)\leq s|I|
because
1\leq w_{i}\leq s
for all
i
.
Given
f\in\mathscr{P}^{m}_{e}(G;\mathbb{R})
, we write the Taylor expansion of
f
at 0 in exponential coordinates up to order
m
, that is,

f(x)=\sum_{|I|\leq m}f_{I}x^{I}+\rho(x)
,
where
\rho\in C^{\infty}(G)
is such that
|\rho(x)|\leq C(|x|^{m+1})
for
|x|\leq 1
and some
C\geq 0
.
Here
|\cdot|
denotes the Euclidean norm.
We rearrange this sum as

f(x)=\sum_{|I|\leq m}f_{I}x^{I}+\rho(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=k}f_{I}x^{I}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{sm}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w}(I)=k\\
|I|\leq m\end{subarray}}f_{I}x^{I}+\rho(x).

Therefore, for
x
fixed and
\lambda>0
, we have

\frac{f(\delta_{\lambda}x)}{\lambda^{m}}-\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}f_{I}x^{I}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{k-m}\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=k}f_{I}x^{I}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{sm}\lambda^{k-m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w}(I)=k\\
|I|\leq m\end{subarray}}f_{I}x^{I}+\frac{\rho(\delta_{\lambda}x)}{\lambda^{m}}.

Notice that the left-hand side of the latter identity is constant in
\lambda
, while on the right-hand side we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{k-m}\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=k}f_{I}x^{I}|

\displaystyle=\infty,

if
\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=k}f_{I}x^{I}\neq 0
for some
k
,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\sum_{k=m+1}^{sm}\lambda^{k-m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{w}(I)=k\\
|I|\leq m\end{subarray}}f_{I}x^{I}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\to 0^{+}}\frac{\rho(\delta_{\lambda}x)}{\lambda^{m}}

\displaystyle=0,

where the last limit is follows from the fact
|\delta_{\lambda}x|\leq\lambda|x|
.
We thus obtain that
f(x)=\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}f_{I}x^{I}
.
∎

For a multi-index
I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, define

\tilde{b}^{I}=\tilde{b}_{1}^{I_{1}}\cdots\tilde{b}_{n}^{I_{n}}\in\mathscr{U}(G)
.
Notice that we obtain
(\delta_{\lambda})_{*}\tilde{b}^{I}=\lambda^{-\mathbf{w}(I)}\tilde{b}^{I}
.
By the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt Theorem
[
1
, I.2.7]
,

\{\tilde{b}^{I}\}_{I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}}
is a basis of
\mathscr{U}(G)
.
Since
\tilde{b}^{I}\in\mathscr{U}^{m}(G)
if and only if
\mathbf{w}(I)=m
,
then
\{\tilde{b}^{I}:\mathbf{w}(I)=m\}
is a basis of
\mathscr{U}^{m}(G)
.

We have two ways to build a basis of
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
:
we can take
\{\sigma_{e}(x^{I})\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
,
or the basis dual to
\{\tilde{b}^{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
.
These are not the same, because it is
not
true that
\langle\tilde{b}^{I}|x^{J}\rangle=0
if and only if
I=J
.

We choose to describe the second basis, that is, the basis dual to
\{\tilde{b}^{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
, for both
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
and
\mathscr{P}_{e}^{m}(G;\mathbb{R})
.
In other words, we will get
\{A_{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}\subset\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
such that, for every
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
and
p\in G
,

(21)

A^{m}_{f,p}=\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}(\tilde{b}^{I}f)(p)\cdot A_{I}.

And, for each
p\in G
, we will get
\{P_{p,I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}\subset\mathscr{P}_{p}^{m}(G;\mathbb{R})
such that

(22)

P^{m}_{f,p}=\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}(\tilde{b}^{I}f)(p)\cdot P_{p,I}.

We can obtain the polynomial
P_{p,I}
by imposing
\tilde{b}^{I}P^{J}(p)=\delta^{I}_{J}
and then compute
A_{I}=A^{m}_{P_{e,I},e}
.
The identity (
22
) gives us the homogeneous taylor expansion of
f\in C^{\infty}(G)
at
p
as

f\sim\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}(\tilde{b}^{I}f)(p)\cdot P_{p,I}.

However, we can compute
A_{I}
without taking derivatives and exploiting the algebra structure of
\mathscr{U}(G)
.
Indeed, compute the functions
\tau_{I}:\mathscr{T}^{m}(V_{1})\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(23)

\tau(\xi)=\sum_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}\tau_{I}(\xi)\tilde{b}^{I},

for
\xi\in\mathscr{T}^{m}(V_{1})
.
If
\{P_{p,I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
is the basis of
\mathscr{P}^{m}_{p}(G)
dual to
\{\tilde{b}^{I}\}_{\mathbf{w}(I)=m}
,
then

\langle\tau^{*}P_{p,I}|\xi\rangle=\langle P_{p,I}|\tau(\xi)\rangle=\tau_{I}(\xi).

Therefore,
A_{I}
is given by

(24)

\langle A_{I}|\xi\rangle=\tau_{I}(\xi)

for all
\xi\in\mathscr{T}^{m}(V_{1})
.
Notice that
A_{I}
does not depend on
p
, while
P_{p,I}
does.
Moreover, we can compute
A_{I}
without computing
P_{p,I}
.
Clearly, the isomorphism
\sigma_{p}
is given by
\sigma_{p}(P_{p,I})=A_{I}
.

Remark 4.8
.

We now summarize an algorithm to compute
a basis
for
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
:

(1)

Choose a basis
\mathscr{B}=(b_{1},\dots,b_{n})
of
\mathfrak{g}
adapted to the stratification
\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}
;
We denote by
\Xi^{m}
the basis of
\mathscr{T}^{m}(V_{1})
induced by
\mathscr{B}
.

(2)

Compute the list of multi-indices
\mathscr{I}^{m}=\{I\in\mathbb{N}^{n}:\mathbf{w}(I)=m\}
.

(3)

For each
\xi\in\Xi^{m}
write
\tau(\xi)
in the basis
\{\tilde{b}^{I}\}_{I\in\mathscr{I}^{m}}
as in (
23
),
so that we obtain
\{\tau_{I}(\xi)\}_{I\in\mathscr{I}^{m},\xi\in\Xi^{m}}
.

(4)

For each
I\in\mathscr{I}^{m}
, compute
A_{I}=\sum_{\xi\in\Xi^{m}}\tau_{I}(\xi)\xi^{*}
,
where
\xi^{*}
is the element of
\mathscr{T}^{m}(V_{1}^{*})
dual to
\xi
with respect to the basis
\Xi^{m}
.

(5)

The resulting
\{A_{I}\}_{I\in\mathscr{I}^{m}}
is a basis of
\mathtt{HD}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})
.
Since
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
is the tensor product
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};\mathbb{R})\otimes W
, a basis for
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
is
\{A_{I}\otimes c\}_{I\in\mathscr{I}^{m},c\in\mathscr{C}}
, for a basis
\mathscr{C}
of
W
.

See section
8
for an application.

5.1.
Projection to lower order jet bundles

In this section, we will work on
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
and
\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
simultaneously, and so we will accent notation with
\hat{\,}
to indicate objects on
\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
.
For example, we write
p
for a point in
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
and
\hat{p}
for a point in
\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
; we use
\omega^{\ell}
for a contact form on
\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
and
\hat{\omega}^{\ell}
for a contact form on
\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
, etc.

We denote by
\pi_{m}:\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
the projection along
\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
.
This map is not a morphism of Lie groups, because
\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
is not an ideal of
\mathfrak{j}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, but it maps the horizontal distribution onto the horizontal distribution, although not point by point.
In other words, if
\hat{p}\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)
then
d\pi_{m}|_{\hat{p}}(\mathscr{H}^{m+1}_{\hat{p}})\neq\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}
(as one can see from (
26
) below), however
\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}
is the closure of the union of
d\pi_{m}|_{\hat{p}}(\mathscr{H}^{m+1}_{\hat{p}})
for all
\hat{p}\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)
.
In fact, we don’t need to consider the whole fiber
\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)
,
but any open subset already spans the horizontal distribution in the image,
as the next lemma shows.

Lemma 5.1
.

If
p\in\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
and
U\subset\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
is open and satisfies
\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)\cap U\neq\emptyset
, then

(25)

\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}=\mathrm{span}\left(\bigcup\left\{d\pi_{m}|_{\hat{p}}(\mathscr{H}^{m+1}_{\hat{p}}):\hat{p}\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)\cap U\right\}\right).

Proof.

\supset
Let
\hat{p}=(a,A^{\leq m+1})\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)\cap U
. Since elements of
\mathscr{H}^{m+1}_{\hat{p}}
have the form

(\tilde{v}(a),v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{\leq m+1}+B^{m+1})
where
v\in V_{1}
and
B^{m+1}\in\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, it follows that

(26)

d\pi_{m}|_{\hat{p}}\big((\tilde{v}(a),v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{\leq m+1}+B^{m+1})\big)=(\tilde{v}(a),v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{\leq m+1})\in\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}.

Since
\hat{p}\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)\cap U
is arbitrary we can conclude that the right-hand side of
(
25
)
is a subset of
\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}
.

\subset
By Lemma
3.2
, if
\{v_{1},\dots,v_{r}\}\subset V_{1}
is a basis for
V_{1}
and
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{N}\}\subset\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
is basis for
\mathtt{HD}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
, then
\{v_{i}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}B_{j}:i=1,\dots,r,j=1,\dots,N\}
contains a basis for
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)
.
Given
\hat{p}=(a,A^{\leq m+1})\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)\cap U
, define
\hat{q}_{j}\in\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(\mathfrak{g};W)
by
\hat{q}_{j}=(a,A^{\leq m+1}+B_{j})
.
It follows that
\hat{q}_{j}\in\pi_{m}^{-1}(p)
and by scaling if necessary, we can assume that each
B_{j}
is close enough to
0
so that
\hat{q}_{j}\in U
.

The left invariant field generated by
(v_{i},0)
evaluated at
\hat{q}_{j}
is

\big(\tilde{v}_{i}(a),v_{i}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}(A^{\leq m+1}+B_{j})\big)

and by definition belongs to
\mathscr{H}_{\hat{q}_{i}}^{m+1}
. Furthermore,

d\pi_{m}|_{\hat{q}_{j}}\big((\tilde{v}_{j}(a),v_{j}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}(A^{\leq m+1}+B_{j}))\big)=(\tilde{v}_{j}(a),v_{j}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{\leq m+1}+v_{j}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}B_{j})\in H,

where
H
denotes the right hand side of (
25
). Since
(\tilde{v}_{j}(a),v_{j}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}A^{\leq m+1})\in H
for all
j
, it follows that
(0,v_{j}\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}B_{j})\in H
for all
j
and we conclude that
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{g};W)\subset H
.
∎

5.2.
Contact maps

Given a smooth map
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
from an open set
\Omega\subset\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
,
we write it component wise as

F(a,A)=\left(F_{G}(a,A),F^{0}(a,A),\dots,F^{m}(a,A)\right)

where
F_{G}:\Omega\to G
and
F^{k}:\Omega\to\mathtt{HD}^{k}(\mathfrak{g};W)
are smooth maps.
Furthermore,
F
is said to be a
contact map
if
dF(\mathscr{H}^{m})\subset\mathscr{H}^{m}
which in the case
m\geq 1
, is characterised by the following conditions:

(27)

\begin{array}[]{rll}\omega^{\ell}(F(p))\big(dF(p){\mathbb{X}}_{j}(p)\big)=0,&&0\leq\ell\leq m-1,\ \forall j,\\
\omega^{\ell}(F(p))\big(dF(p){\mathbb{Y}}_{j}(p)\big)=0,&&0\leq\ell\leq m-1,\ \forall j,\\
\theta^{k}(F(p))\big(dF(p){\mathbb{X}}_{j}(p)\big)=0,&&2\leq k\leq s,\ \forall j,\\
\theta^{k}(F(p))\big(dF(p){\mathbb{Y}}_{j}(p)\big)=0,&&2\leq k\leq s,\ \forall j,\\
\end{array}

for all
p\in\Omega
.
In the case
m=0
, the conditions reduce to those given by the forms
\theta^{k}
and simply mean that
F
is contact map of some open set
\Omega\subseteq G\times W
, where

\mathscr{H}^{0}_{(e_{G},0)}=\mathfrak{j}^{0}(\mathfrak{g};W)=V_{1}\times W
.
Note that in the case
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the definition is consistent with the usual contact system as defined in
[
5
, chapter 4]
.

5.3.
Prolongation

Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is open, and that
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is a contact map.
If
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
is open and satisfies
\pi_{m}(\hat{\Omega})\subseteq\Omega
, then a smooth map
\hat{F}:\hat{\Omega}\to\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
satisfying

(28)

\pi_{m}\circ\hat{F}=F\circ\pi_{m}

is called a
prolongation of
F
if
\hat{F}
is a contact map.
Since
\hat{\omega}^{\ell}=\pi_{m}^{*}\omega^{\ell}
,
\ell=1,\dots,m-1
, and
\hat{\theta}^{k}=\pi_{m}^{*}\theta^{k}
,
k=2,\dots,s
, any map
\hat{F}
that satisfies condition (
28
), immediatley satisfies all the contact conditions (
27
) on
\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
except those corresponding to
\hat{\omega}^{m}
.
In particular
\hat{F}_{G}=F_{G}\circ\pi_{m}
,
\hat{F}^{i}=F^{i}\circ\pi_{m}
for
i=0,\dots,m
and
\hat{F}^{m+1}
is determined by the contact conditions corresponding to
\hat{\omega}^{m}
. More precisely, the conditions

(29)

\hat{\omega}^{m}(\hat{F}(\hat{p}))(d\hat{F}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{j}(\hat{p}))=0

are trivial since from (
28
) we have

d\pi_{m}(\hat{F}(\hat{p}))d\hat{F}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{j}(\hat{p})=dF(p)d\pi_{m}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{j}(\hat{p})=dF(\pi_{m}(\hat{p}))0=0

which implies that

d\hat{F}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{j}(\hat{p})\in\text{ker}d\pi_{m}(\hat{F}(\hat{p}))

or equivalently

(30)

d\hat{F}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{j}(\hat{p})\in\mathrm{span}\{{\mathbb{\hat{Y}}}_{k}(\hat{p}):k=1,\dots\}

giving (
29
).

It follows that the determining conditions for
\hat{F}^{m+1}
are the equations

(31)

\hat{\omega}^{m}(\hat{F}(\hat{p}))(d\hat{F}(\hat{p}){\mathbb{\hat{X}}}_{j}(\hat{p}))=0\quad\forall j.

The existence of prolongations is governed by the following result.

Theorem 5.2

(Prolongation Theorem)
.

Supppose
m\geq 0
,
\Omega\subset\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is open, and that
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is a contact map.
Let
v_{1},\dots,v_{r}
be a basis of
V_{1}
and, for
j\in\{1,\dots,r\}
, define

\tilde{N}_{j}:\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)\to TG
as

\tilde{N}_{j}(\hat{p})=dF_{G}({\pi_{m}(\hat{p})})d\pi_{m}(\hat{p})\hat{\mathbb{X}}_{j}(\hat{p}).

Define
\hat{\Omega}\subset\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
as the open set where
\tilde{N}_{1},\dots,\tilde{N}_{r}
are pointwise linearly independent.
Then there is a unique contact map
\hat{F}:\hat{\Omega}\to\mathtt{J}^{m+1}(G;W)
such that

\pi_{m}\circ\hat{F}=F\circ\pi_{m}.

Moreover, if
F
is a diffeomorphism, then
\hat{F}
is also a diffeomorphism and
\hat{F}^{-1}
is the prolongation of
F^{-1}
.

Remark 5.3
.

Uniqueness of the prolongation
\hat{F}
is proved only on
\hat{\Omega}
(cfr. (
40
)).
For instance, a constant map
F
is contact and admits infinite prolongations, but
\hat{\Omega}=\emptyset
.

Remark 5.4
.

We don’t know the size of
\hat{\Omega}
in general.

If
F
is itself a prolongation of a contact map on
\mathtt{J}^{m-1}(G;W)
,
then
\hat{\Omega}=\pi_{m}^{-1}(\Omega)
.

Notice that, by the results in the following Section
6
,
F
is a prolongation as soon as
m\geq 2
.

Let’s prove the above statement about
\hat{\Omega}
.
First, notice that, if
\hat{p}=(a,A^{\leq m+1})
and

6.2.

De-prolongation results are based on the existence of a particular type of characteristic vector field.

For an open set
\Omega\subset\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
, we let
T\mathtt{J}^{m}_{\Omega}=\cup_{p\in\Omega}T_{p}\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
and
\mathscr{H}^{m}_{\Omega}=\cup_{p\in\Omega}\mathscr{H}^{m}_{p}
. Starting with
L^{1}_{\Omega}=\Gamma(\mathscr{H}^{m}_{\Omega})
, we define a filtration of
\Gamma(T\mathtt{J}^{m}_{\Omega})
inductively buy setting
L^{i+1}_{\Omega}=L^{i}_{\Omega}+[L^{1}_{\Omega},L^{i}_{\Omega}]
for
i=1,\dots,s-1
. If
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is a smooth contact diffeomorphism, then
F_{*}L^{i}_{\Omega}=L^{i}_{f(\Omega)}
since
F_{*}L^{1}_{\Omega}=L^{1}_{F(\Omega)}
. A Cauchy characteristic of order
i
over
\Omega
, is a vector field
X\in\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})
such that
[X,\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})]\subset\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})
. The set
\mathcal{C}^{i}_{\Omega}
of all Cauchy characteristics of order
i
over
\Omega
is a Lie algebra thanks to the Jacobi identity. If
F:\Omega\to\mathtt{J}^{m}(G;W)
is a smooth contact diffeomorphism then
F_{*}\mathcal{C}^{i}(\Omega)\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{i}(F(\Omega))
since

\displaystyle F_{*}[X,\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})]

\displaystyle=[F_{*}X,F_{*}\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})]

\displaystyle=[F_{*}X,\Gamma(L^{i}_{F(\Omega)})]\subset F_{*}\Gamma(L^{i}_{\Omega})=\Gamma(L^{i}_{F(\Omega)}).

The particular notion of characteristic we use here, is a vector field
\tilde{X}\in\Gamma(L^{1}_{\Omega})
such
[\tilde{X},[\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}]]\subset\Gamma(L^{2}_{\Omega})
for all
\tilde{Y}\in\Gamma(L^{1}_{\Omega})
.
The characteristic property of these vector fields is preserved by contact transformation.
Indeed, if

In this section we prove Theorem
C
and we compute its application to groups of step 2 and 3.

7.1.
Proof of Theorem
C

For this proof, we will extensively use the identification of the group with its Lie algebra via the exponential map.
More explicitly, if
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of (a nilpotent, simply connected Lie group)
G
, then we define for
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}

xy=\log(\exp(x)\exp(y)),

which, via the BCH formula, has an explicit polynomial expression.
If
f
is a smooth function and
v\in\mathfrak{g}
, then the corresponding left and right invariant vector fields take the form

\tilde{v}f(p)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f(p(tv))\quad\text{ and }\quad v^{\dagger}f(p)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}f((tv)p).

Let
G
be the stratified group from Theorem
C
with stratified Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s+1}V_{j}
.
For
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, we write
x_{j}=\Pi_{j}(x)\in V_{j}
and
x^{\prime}=\sum_{j\leq s}x_{j}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.
The BCH formula gives a map
\eta:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to V_{s+1}
, which is a polynomial, such that

\Pi_{s+1}(xy)=x_{s+1}+y_{s+1}+\eta(x,y).

The function
\eta
has the following properties:

(1)

\eta(x,y)
depends only on
x^{\prime}
and
y^{\prime}
;

(2)

\eta(\delta_{\lambda}x,\delta_{\lambda}y)=\lambda^{s+1}\eta(x,y)
;

(3)

since

\Pi_{s+1}((xy)z)=x_{s+1}+y_{s+1}+z_{s+1}+\eta(x,y)+\eta(xy,z)

and

\Pi_{s+1}(x(yz))=x_{s+1}+y_{s+1}+z_{s+1}+\eta(y,z)+\eta(x,yz),

we have

(46)

\eta(xy,z)+\eta(x,y)=\eta(x,yz)+\eta(y,z).

Define
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}=\oplus_{j=1}^{s}V_{j}
with Lie brackets
[x,y]^{\prime}=[x,y]-\Pi_{s+1}([x,y])
, i.e.,
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}\simeq\mathfrak{g}/V_{s+1}
.
We identify again the group
G^{\prime}
with
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
endowed with the group operation given by the BCH formula.
One can easily check that

(xy)^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})^{\prime}=(x^{\prime}y^{\prime})_{\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}},

where the last term is the group operation given by the BCH formula on
\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}
.

By Theorem
4.5
, the polynomial jet space
\mathtt{J}_{\mathscr{P}}^{s}(G^{\prime};W)
is equivalent to the jet space
\mathtt{J}^{s}(G^{\prime};W)
defined in Section
3.4
.
We will define an injective morphism of stratified Lie algebras
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{j}_{\mathscr{P}}^{s}(G^{\prime};V_{s+1})
.
We consider linear maps
\phi
of the following form:
For every
k\in\{1,\dots,s+1\}
there is a linear map
\phi_{k}:V_{k}\to\mathscr{P}_{e_{G^{\prime}}}^{s+1-k}(G^{\prime};V_{s+1})
such that for all
v\in V_{k}

(47)

\phi(v)=v+\phi_{k}(v)\in V_{k}\oplus\mathscr{P}_{e_{G^{\prime}}}^{s+1-k}(G^{\prime};V_{s+1})=\mathfrak{j}_{\mathscr{P}}^{s}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime};V_{s+1})_{k}.

Notice that
\phi_{s+1}:V_{s+1}\to V_{s+1}
.
Any such map
\phi
is a morphism of stratified Lie algebras
if and only if it is a Lie algebra morphism, because it already preserves the stratification.
Furthermore,
\phi
is a Lie algebra morphism
if and only if for every
v\in V_{i}
and
w\in V_{j}
, we have
[\phi(v),\phi(w)]=\phi([v,w])
, that is, by (
19
),

(1)

if
i+j<s+1
,

(48)

w\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{i}(v)-v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{j}(w)=\phi_{i+j}([v,w]).

(2)

if
i+j=s+1
,

(49)

w\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{i}(v)-v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{j}(w)=[v,w]+\phi_{s+1}([v,w]).

For
v\in V_{k}
,
1\leq k\leq s+1
, we define

(50)

\phi_{k}(v)=(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y),

by which we mean that
\phi_{k}(v)
is the polynomial in
y
resulting from deriving
\eta
in
x
along the right invariant vector field
v^{\dagger}
and evaluating at
x=e
.
Similarly, we will also write
v^{\dagger}_{y}
to denote the derivation in
y
.
Notice that taking the right-invariant vector field
v^{\dagger}
is pretentious, because

(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta(tv,y)=(\tilde{v}_{x}\eta)(e,y).

Notice also that, since
\eta
depends only on
y^{\prime}
,
\phi_{k}(v)
is actually a function on
G^{\prime}
.

We claim that
(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)\in\mathscr{P}^{s+1-k}_{e_{G^{\prime}}}(G^{\prime};V_{s+1})
when
v\in V_{k}
.
Indeed,

\displaystyle(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,\delta_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta((tv),\delta_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta(\delta_{\lambda}(\lambda^{-k}tv),\delta_{\lambda}y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{s+1}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta((\lambda^{-k}tv),y)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{s+1-k}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta((tv),y)=\lambda^{s+1-k}(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y).

Therefore,
(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)\in\mathscr{P}^{s+1-k}_{e_{G^{\prime}}}(G^{\prime};V_{s+1})
, and
\phi
is therefore well defined.

Next, we show that
\phi
does in fact satisfy (
48
) and (
49
).
Let
v\in V_{i}
and
w\in V_{j}
for
1\leq i,j\leq s+1
.
Then, using (
46
),

\displaystyle w\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{i}(v)(y)

\displaystyle=w^{\dagger}_{y}(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,(tw)y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}\eta(rv,(tw)y)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}\left(\eta((rv)(tw),y)+(rv,tw)-\eta(tw,y)\right)

\displaystyle=(w^{\dagger}_{x}v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)+\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}\eta(rv,tw).

Notice that

\eta(rv,tw)-\eta(tw,rv)=\Pi_{s+1}\left(\log(\exp(rv)\exp(tw))-\log(\exp(tw)\exp(rv))\right)

and that the BCH formula implies that

\log(\exp(rv)\exp(tw))=rv+tw+\frac{rt}{2}[v,w]+P(rv,tw)

where
\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}P(rv,tw)=0
.
Therefore

\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{r=0}\left(\eta(rv,tw)-\eta(tw,rv)\right)=\Pi_{s+1}([v,w]).

We conclude that

\displaystyle(w\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{i}(v)-v\raisebox{0.0pt}{\scalebox{1}[-1]{$\lrcorner$}}\phi_{j}(w))(y)

\displaystyle=(w^{\dagger}_{x}v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)-(v^{\dagger}_{x}w^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)+\Pi_{s+1}([v,w])

\displaystyle=(-[v^{\dagger},w^{\dagger}]_{x}\eta)(e,y)+\Pi_{s+1}([v,w])

\displaystyle=([v,w]^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)+\Pi_{s+1}([v,w]),

where we used the standard relation

-[v^{\dagger},w^{\dagger}]=[v,w]^{\dagger}

between right and left invariant vector fields.

We have thus shown that the map
\phi
defined as in (
47
) with (
50
) is a morphism of stratified Lie algebras
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{j}^{s}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime};V_{s+1})
.
The only missing property we need to conclude is that
\phi
is injective.
Since
\phi|_{V_{k}}
is injective for every
k<s+1
and since
\phi
preserves the stratification, we only need to check that
\phi|_{V_{s+1}}
is injective.
But
\phi|_{V_{s+1}}
is the identity, because if
v\in V_{s+1}
, then
(v^{\dagger}_{x}\eta)(e,y)=0
, as
\eta
does not depend on
x_{s+1}
.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(v_{1})(w)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta(tv_{1},w)=\frac{1}{2}[v_{1},w_{2}]+\frac{1}{12}[w_{1},[w_{1},v_{1}],

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(v_{2})(w)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta(tv_{2},w)=\frac{1}{2}[v_{2},w_{1}],

\displaystyle\phi_{3}(v_{3})(w)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\right|_{t=0}\eta(tv_{3},w)=0\text{ (as expected).}

We obtain

\phi(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=(v_{1},v_{2};v_{3};\frac{1}{2}[v_{2},\Pi_{1}(\cdot)];\frac{1}{2}[v_{1},\Pi_{2}(\cdot)]+\frac{1}{12}[\Pi_{1}(\cdot),[\Pi_{1}(\cdot),v_{1}]),

as a map into
\mathtt{J}_{\mathscr{P}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime};V_{3})
.

If we want to write the components of
\phi
as multilinear maps (instead of polynomials),
we can use Theorem
4.5
.
So, if
P:G^{\prime}\to V_{3}
is the map
P(x)=[\Pi_{1}(x),[\Pi_{1}(x),v_{1}]]
, then we need to compute

A^{2}_{P,e}(x_{1},y_{1})=\tilde{y}_{1}\tilde{x}_{1}P(e)
, which we can compute using (
7
).
We obtain

A^{2}_{P,e}(x_{1},y_{1})=[x_{1},[y_{1},v_{1}]]+[y_{1},[x_{1},v_{1}]]

Similarly, for
Q(x)=[v_{1},\Pi_{2}(x)]
, we get

A^{2}_{Q,e}(x_{1},y_{1})=\frac{1}{2}[v_{1},[y_{1},x_{1}]]
.

Therefore, as a map into
\mathtt{J}^{2}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime};V_{3})
, we have

\phi(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})=(v_{1},v_{2};v_{3};\frac{1}{2}[v_{2},\cdot];\frac{1}{2}[v_{1},[y,x]]+\frac{1}{12}[x,[y,v_{1}]]+[y,[x,v_{1}]]),

where
x
and
y
are the place holders to indicate the ordered entries of the bilinear map.

Remark 7.1
.

Notice that if
G=\mathbb{R}^{n}
is abelian, then
\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)
has the property

(51)

[\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)_{i},\mathfrak{j}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)_{j}]=0\qquad\text{ for all $i,j>1$.}

Every stratified subgroup of
\mathtt{J}^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{n};W)

must also satisfy (
51
).
Therefore, not all stratified Lie group of step larger than 2 can be embedded in a standard jet space.

8.
Example: the first Heisenberg Group

8.1.
The first Heisenberg group

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
of the
first Heisenberg group

\mathbb{H}

is the stratified three-dimensional Lie algebra with basis
X,Y,Z

and with the only non-trivial bracket relation
Z=[X,Y]
.
The stratification
\mathfrak{h}=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}
is given by
V_{1}=\mathrm{span}\{X,Y\}
and
V_{2}=\mathbb{R}Z
.
In exponential coordinates
(x,y,z)
, we have

\tilde{X}(x,y,z)=\partial_{x}-\frac{y}{2}\partial_{z},\qquad\tilde{Y}(x,y,z)=\partial_{y}+\frac{x}{2}\partial_{z},\qquad\tilde{Z}(x,y,z)=\partial_{z}.

We will apply the algorithm described in Remark
4.8
to obtain a basis for
\mathtt{HD}^{m}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{R})
for
m\in\{1,2,3\}
.
Moreover, we compute the corresponding basis of
\mathscr{P}_{e}^{m}(G;\mathbb{R})
.
The corresponding basis for
\mathscr{P}_{p}^{m}(G;\mathbb{R})
can be obtained via (
17
).

8.2.
A basis for
\mathtt{HD}^{1}(\mathfrak{h};\mathbb{R})

We have already chosen the basis
\mathscr{B}=\{X,Y\}
of
V_{1}
,
which has a dual basis
\{X^{*},Y^{*}\}
for
V_{1}^{*}
.
The set of multi-indices is
\mathscr{I}^{1}=\{(1,0,0),(0,1,0)\}
.
A basis of

is
\{\tilde{X},\tilde{Y}\}
.
The map

\tau(X)=\tilde{X}
and

24
),

A_{(1,0,0)}=X^{*}\quad{\rm and}\quad A_{(0,1,0)}=Y^{*}

The basis of

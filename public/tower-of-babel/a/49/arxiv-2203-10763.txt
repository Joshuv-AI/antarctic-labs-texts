Performance-Robustness Tradeoffs in Adversarially Robust Linear-Quadratic Control
Bruce D. Lee, Thomas T.C.K. Zhang, Hamed Hassani, Nikolai Matni

Abstract
While ℋ ∞ \mathcal{H}_{\infty} methods can introduce robustness against worst-case perturbations, their nominal performance under conventional stochastic disturbances is often drastically reduced. Though this fundamental tradeoff between nominal performance and robustness is known to exist, it is not well-characterized in quantitative terms. Toward addressing this issue, we borrow from the increasingly ubiquitous notion of adversarial training from machine learning to construct a class of controllers which are optimized for disturbances consisting of mixed stochastic and worst-case components. We find that this problem admits a stationary optimal controller that has a simple analytic form closely related to suboptimal ℋ ∞ \mathcal{H}_{\infty} solutions. We then provide a quantitative performance-robustness tradeoff analysis, in which system-theoretic properties such as controllability and stability explicitly manifest in an interpretable manner. This provides practitioners with general guidance for determining how much robustness to incorporate based on a priori system knowledge. We empirically validate our results by comparing the performance of our controller against standard baselines, and plotting tradeoff curves.



1
Introduction

Modern dynamical systems, from mobile robotics to power plants, require controllers that are simultaneously fast, efficient, and robust. Many control schemes attempt to simultaneously achieve these desiderata by combining them into a single objective function and optimizing it. By combining these distinct goals into a single objective, a natural tradeoff arises. A controller optimized for speed and efficiency may perform poorly in the face of unmodeled phenomena.
For instance, Linear-Quadratic Gaussian (LQG) controllers (a special case of
\mathcal{H}_{2}
controllers) explicitly prioritize nominal performance by penalizing the expectation of a quadratic function of the state and input. It has been shown, however, that such controllers can be extraordinarily fragile to small perturbations of the dynamics
(
Doyle 1978
)
. Replacing the LQG objective with one that considers the response of the system to worst-case perturbations of the dynamics and external disturbances results in robust control methods, such as
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
methods. Such controllers are provably robust, however they tend to be overly conservative.

Toward achieving a balance between the performance of nominal and robust controllers, various approaches have been introduced, most notably mixed
\mathcal{H}_{2}/\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
methods. However, the resulting controllers are often complicated to express and compute
(
Scherer 1995
)
, and lack
a priori
quantitative guarantees on how much nominal performance must be given up so as to attain a desired robustness level. Toward addressing these issues, we take inspiration from the notion of adversarial robustness from machine learning
(
Madry et al. 2017
;
Carlini and Wagner 2017
)
in order to formulate a controller synthesis that balances performance and robustness. The goal of adversarial robustness is to minimize the expected error under the presence of worst-case norm-bounded perturbations to the data, where the perturbations can depend on the underlying stochasticity of the problem, such as the data distribution and additive noise. We consider an analogous adversarially robust state feedback control problem where we aim to minimize the expected LQ cost where the process noise is composed of two components: a zero-mean stochastic noise component and a norm-bounded adversarial perturbation. We show that the solution to this problem admits a closed-form expression in terms of discrete algebraic Riccati equation (DARE) solutions, which in turn allows for novel quantitative performance-robustness tradeoff bounds in which system parameters manifest in a natural and interpretable way.

1.1
Contributions

•

Toward analyzing the adversarially robust control problem we propose, we first show that when viewed through the lens of dynamic games
(
Basar 1991
)
, adversarially robust LQ control relates to a control problem introduced in
Doyle et al. 1989
. We show that the optimal solution to the state feedback version of this problem is given by a central static suboptimal
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
controller, with suboptimality level
\gamma
depending on both the stochastic noise statistics and the budget given to the adversary. Furthermore, both the worst-case adversary and the corresponding optimal controller can be computed from the solution of a DARE.

•

We quantify the performance-robustness tradeoffs of adversarially robust LQ control, both analytically and empirically, and show a clear and interpretable dependence on underlying system theoretic properties such as controllability and stability. In particular, we show that the cost gap incurred by the adversarially robust controller in the nominal setting over the nominal controller is upper bounded by
O(\sigma_{w}^{2}\gamma^{-4}\nu^{-1})
, where
\sigma_{w}^{2}
is the covariance of the additive noise distribution,
\gamma
is the suboptimality level of the suboptimal
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
controller, and
\nu
is the smallest singular value of the controllability gramian. On the other hand, the cost gap is lower bounded by
\Omega\mathopen{}\left(\sigma_{w}^{2}\gamma^{-4}\eta^{2}\right)\mathclose{}
, where
\eta=\left\|W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right\|
is the largest singular value of the controllability gramian for closed loop system under the nominal LQ controller with disturbances as the input. These results show that systems with uniformly good controllability have small performance-robustness tradeoffs, while those that have a highly controllable mode in the nominal closed-loop system (when viewing disturbances as inputs) lead to large performance-robustness tradeoffs.

Related Work

The mixed stochastic/worst-case problem that we consider is not the only way to strike a balance between the performance of stochastic and robust control methods. Most closely related is
Doyle et al. 1989
, which considers disturbances composed of both a bounded power component, and a bounded power spectrum component, and approaches the problem through a deterministic viewpoint.

Paganini 1993
considers a set description of disturbances that also interpolates between
\mathcal{H}_{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
approaches.

Glover and Doyle 1988
provide the class of all stabilizing controllers subject to a
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
norm constraint, while
Bernstein and Haddad 1988
and
Rotea and Khargonekar 1991
consider minimizing
\mathcal{H}_{2}
objective subject to a
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
constraint. These methods often lack a simple closed-form stationary solution. Other recent work attempts to reduce the conservatism of robust control by taking a regret-minimization approach
(
Goel and Hassibi 2020
;
Goel and Hassibi 2021
;
Hazan et al. 2020
)
. None of the aforementioned methods provide a characterization of the cost incurred by enforcing robustness.

Analogous recent work in the machine learning community has analyzed performance-robustness tradeoffs in adversarially robust learning, including precise characterizations of the generalization errors of standard versus adversarially trained models under the random features model
Javanmard et al. 2020
;
Hassani and Javanmard 2022
, and “no free lunch” theorems for obtaining adversarially robust models
Tsipras et al. 2018
;
Dohmatob 2019
;
Yin et al. 2019
. The successful characterization of such performance-robustness tradeoffs in machine learning motivates the control objective we consider. However, the theoretical results from this area are largely intended for the supervised learning setting and do not immediately apply to our setting.
Al Makdah et al. 2020
proves the existence of performance-robustness tradeoffs in control but does not characterize them quantitatively. The extension of adversarial robustness results in machine learning to various control problems is an active area of research
(
Lee et al. 2021
;
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Havens et al. 2022
)
.

Adversarially Robust Linear-Quadratic Control

Consider a fully observed discrete-time linear-time-invariant (LTI) system with state disturbances composed of both stochastic and adversarial components: let
x_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be the system state,
u_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
the input,
w_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\delta_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the stochastic and adversarial components of the process disturbance, respectively. The initial condition
x_{0}
and stochastic component of the process noise
w_{t}
are assumed to be i.i.d. zero-mean with covariance matrices
\Sigma_{0},\;\Sigma_{w}
, respectively, and
\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\left[x_{0}w_{t}^{\top}\right]\mathclose{}=0
for all
t\geq 0
. The LTI system is then defined by the following equation:

\displaystyle x_{t+1}

\displaystyle=Ax_{t}+Bu_{t}+w_{t}+\delta_{t}.

(1)

We assume that the adversarial perturbation sequence
\bm{\delta}
is causal, i.e., it can depend only on the states, inputs, and stochastic noise up to the current timestep; in particular, it must be a measureable function of the randomness
x_{0},w_{0:t}
.
We let
Q\succeq 0
be a weight matrix for the state cost, and
R\succ 0
be a weight matrix for the input cost. We consider the infinite horizon objective

\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w},x_{0}}\mathopen{}\left[x_{T}^{\top}Qx_{T}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}^{\top}Qx_{t}+u_{t}^{\top}Ru_{t}\right]\mathclose{}

subject to the dynamics (
1
).
If the adversarial perturbation is set to zero,
\bm{\delta}=0
, then the above objective is the nominal LQR problem. If the stochasticity is set to zero,
\bm{w},x_{0}=0
, and
\bm{\delta}
are worst-case perturbations with average power bounded by
\varepsilon
, the above objective is the
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
problem. When both stochastic noise and worst-case perturbations are present, we define the resulting control task as the adversarially robust LQR problem.
We denote the three corresponding objectives by
\operatorname{NC}
,
\operatorname{RC}
, and
\operatorname{AC}
respectively:

\displaystyle\operatorname{NC}(K)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w},x_{0}}\mathopen{}\left[x_{T}^{\top}Qx_{T}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}\mathopen{}\left(Q+K^{\top}RK\right)\mathclose{}x_{t}\right]\mathclose{},\;\;x_{t+1}=(A+BK)x_{t}+w_{t}

(2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{RC}(K)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\delta}\mbox{ causal}\\
\left\|\bm{\delta}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\leq T\varepsilon\end{subarray}}x_{T}^{\top}Qx_{T}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}^{\top}(Q+K^{\top}RK)x_{t},\;\;x_{t+1}=(A+BK)x_{t}+\delta_{t},\;x_{0}=0

(3)

\displaystyle\operatorname{AC}(K)

\displaystyle=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}_{\mathbf{w},x_{0}}\mathopen{}\left[\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{\delta}\mbox{ causal}\\
\left\|\bm{\delta}\right\|_{\ell_{2}}^{2}\leq T\varepsilon\end{subarray}}x_{T}^{\top}Qx_{T}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}^{\top}(Q+K^{\top}RK)x_{t}\right]\mathclose{},\;\;x_{t+1}=(A+BK)x_{t}+w_{t}+\delta_{t}

(4)

The adversarial budget
\left\|\bm{\delta}\right\|_{\ell^{2}}^{2}\leq T\varepsilon
is chosen such that the instance-wise adversarial budget satisfies
\left\|\delta_{t}\right\|^{2}\leq\varepsilon
on average.
We note that our attention is restricted to static state feedback control policies
u_{t}=Kx_{t}
, as they are known to be optimal for
\operatorname{NC}
and
\operatorname{RC}

(
Zhou et al. 1996
)
. We will show, as a consequence of Lemma
2.1
and Theorem

\operatorname{AC}
.
In order to ensure that there exists a stabilizing controller, and that minimizing either
\operatorname{NC}
or
\operatorname{RC}
provides a stabilizing controller, we make the standard assumption that
(A,B,Q^{1/2})
is stabilizable and detectable

Under this assumption, there exists a stabilizing state feedback control law

minimizing
\operatorname{NC}

\displaystyle K=-(R+B^{\top}PB)^{-1}B^{\top}PA

(5)

and

is the solution to the Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation (DARE)

\displaystyle P=Q+A^{\top}PA-A^{\top}PB(R+B^{\top}PB)^{-1}B^{\top}PA.

(6)

A solution minimizing
\operatorname{RC}
may be found using Theorem 13.3.3 of
Hassibi et al. 1999

The remainder of this section is devoted to finding a controller minimizing

Inspired by minimax dynamic games
(

\displaystyle\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\mathopen{}\left[\max_{\bm{\delta}\mbox{ causal}}x_{T}^{\top}Qx_{T}+\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}x_{t}Qx_{t}+u_{t}^{\top}Ru_{t}-\gamma^{2}\delta_{t}^{\top}\delta_{t}\right]\mathclose{}.

1.

The controller

with

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=-(R+B^{\top}MB)^{-1}B^{\top}MA,

(9)

\displaystyle M

\displaystyle=P+P(\gamma^{2}I-P)^{-1}P.

The solution approach for the above problem follows that in

The finite horizon version of the problem is solved by defining a saddle point cost-to-go, then recursing backwards in time, solving both an unconstrained concave maximization problem and convex minimization problem at each time step to determine the optimal adversarial perturbation and control input. It should be noted that in contrast to most adversarially robust machine learning problems, adversarially robust LQR provides a closed form expression for the adversarial perturbation.

Performance-Robustness Tradeoff Bounds

This section presents the main results of the paper: we study the tradeoffs that arise in adversarial control by investigating the interplay between the two objectives (
2
) and (
4
). This section will focus on bounding the tradeoffs analytically. The next section will validate these analytical bounds by empirically examining tradeoff curves for particular systems.

We begin by considering the gap between the nominal controller and the
\gamma
-adversarially robust controller when applied in the nominal setting, i.e., we seek to bound the gap
\operatorname{NC}(K_{\gamma})-\operatorname{NC}(K_{\star})
, where
K_{\gamma}
is the
\gamma
-adversarially robust controller given by Lemma
2.1
, and
K_{\star}
is the nominal LQR controller given by equation (
5
).
Let
P_{\star}
be the nominal DARE solution given by the solution to equation (
6
), and let
P_{\gamma}
be the solution to equation (
8
). Also define

\displaystyle M_{\gamma}:=P_{\gamma}+P_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}.

(12)

Given an arbitrary stabilizing linear feedback controller
K
,
results from
Mania et al. 2019
and
Fazel et al. 2018
allow us to characterize the gap in the cost between
K
and the optimal LQR controller
K_{\star}
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{NC}(K)-\operatorname{NC}(K_{\star})=\trace(\Sigma(K)(K-K_{\star})^{\top}(R+B^{\top}P_{\star}B)(K-K_{\star}))

(13)

where
\Sigma(K)=\textnormal{{dlyap}}(A+BK,\Sigma_{w})
is the steady state covariance of the closed loop system under controller
K
. The following bounds on the gap
\operatorname{NC}(K_{\gamma})-\operatorname{NC}(K_{\star})
then follow immediately:

\displaystyle\operatorname{NC}(K_{\gamma})-\operatorname{NC}(K_{\star})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\min}(\Sigma_{w})\sigma_{\min}(R+B^{\top}P_{\star}B)\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|_{F}^{2}

(14)

\displaystyle\operatorname{NC}(K_{\gamma})-\operatorname{NC}(K_{\star})

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Sigma(K_{\gamma})\right\|\left\|R+B^{\top}P_{\star}B\right\|\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|_{F}^{2}.

(15)

We have therefore reduced the task of upper and lower bounding the cost gap between the
\gamma
-adversarially robust controller and the nominal LQR controller in the nominal setting to directly bounding the gap between the two controllers. Recalling that
\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|^{2}_{F}\leq\min\mathopen{}\left\{m,n\right\}\mathclose{}\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|^{2}

Lemma 3.1

(Adapted from Lemma 2 of

Suppose that

\frac{\left\|B^{\top}(M-P)(A+BK_{2})\right\|}{\left\|R+B^{\top}MB)\right\|}\leq\left\|K_{1}-K_{2}\right\|\leq\frac{\left\|B^{\top}(M-P)(A+BK_{2})\right\|}{\sigma_{\min}(R+B^{\top}PB)}.

3.1
Upper Bound

Applying Lemma
3.1
with
P=P_{\star}
and
M=M_{\gamma}
reduces our goal to bounding the spectrum of
M_{\gamma}-P_{\star}
. From the definition of
M_{\gamma}
(
12

\left\|P_{\star}-M_{\gamma}\right\|\leq\left\|P_{\star}-P_{\gamma}\right\|+\frac{\left\|P_{\gamma}\right\|^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\left\|P_{\gamma}\right\|}.

For
\gamma>\gamma_{\infty}
we have
P_{\gamma}\prec P_{\gamma_{\infty}}\prec\gamma_{\infty}^{2}I
(Lemma
C.3
), and thus
\left\|P_{\star}-M_{\gamma}\right\|\leq\left\|P_{\star}-P_{\gamma}\right\|+\frac{\gamma_{\infty}^{4}}{\gamma^{2}-\gamma_{\infty}^{2}},

reducing our task to bounding
\left\|P_{\star}-P_{\gamma}\right\|
, the gap between solutions to the
\gamma
-adversarial and nominal DAREs.

Toward bounding the norm difference of solutions to DAREs, we show that the closed loop dynamics under the adversary
\delta_{t}
can be expressed as perturbations of the nominal system matrices. In particular, recall that for the a noiseless adversarial LQR instance at level
\gamma>0
, the adversary can be represented as
\delta_{t}=\mathopen{}\left(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}^{-1}P_{\gamma}\mathopen{}\left(Ax_{t}+Bu_{t}\right)\mathclose{},

such that we may write
x_{t+1}=Ax_{t}+Bu_{t}+\delta_{t}=\tilde{A}x_{t}+\tilde{B}u_{t}
, for
\tilde{A}:=\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}A
and
\tilde{B}:=\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}B
. This allows us to bound the gaps
||\tilde{A}-A||,\;||\tilde{B}-B||
in terms of
\gamma

C.4
in Appendix
C.2

By bounding the gap between system matrices in the adversarial setting and in the nominal setting, we derive perturbation bounds on the gap
\left\|P_{\gamma}-P_{\star}\right\|

5
Conclusion

We proposed an adversarially robust LQ control problem, and demonstrated that the optimal solution to this problem is given by a central static suboptimal
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
controller. An interesting aspect of this solution is that unlike pure
\mathcal{H}_{2}
controllers, the adversarially robust controller depends upon the noise statistics.
Experiments show that the adversarially robust controller performs similar to mixed
\mathcal{H}_{2}/\mathcal{H}_{\infty}
controllers on a simple system.

We used the adversarially robust control problem as a means to study performance-robustness tradeoffs in control. In particular, we derived quantitative upper and lower bounds on the performance gap between the nominal controller the adversarially robust controller. The bounds show that systems with uniformly good controllability will have small performance-robustness tradeoffs, while those with a highly controllable mode in the closed-loop system (viewing disturbances as inputs) will have a large performance-robustness tradeoff. These trends are corroborated by experiments with a simple linear system by tracing out tradeoff curves. Directions for future work include the extension of the problem setting considered here to the measurement feedback setting, and considering how other adversarial training techniques can be translated to robust controller synthesis.

Lemma C.1

(Lemma 1 in

Let

We also consider an analogous lemma which provides a lower bound.

Lemma C.2

Suppose

and
f_{2}
are
L
-Lipschitz function, and let
x_{1}
minimize
f_{1}
and
x_{2}
minimize

\left\|x_{1}-x_{2}\right\|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{L}

Proof:

By a Taylor expansion of

\displaystyle\nabla f_{1}(x_{2})=\nabla f_{1}(x_{1})+\nabla^{2}f_{1}(\tilde{x})(x_{2}-x_{1})

minimizes
f_{1}

\nabla f_{1}(x_{1})=0
. Futhermore, by the fact that

\displaystyle\left\|\nabla f_{1}(x_{2})\right\|=\left\|\nabla f_{1}(x_{1})+\nabla^{2}f_{1}(\tilde{x})(x_{2}-x_{1})\right\|=\left\|\nabla^{2}f_{1}(\tilde{x})(x_{2}-x_{1})\right\|\leq L\left\|x_{2}-x_{1}\right\|

Dividing both sides of the above inequality by

and leveraging the assumption that
\left\|\nabla f_{1}(x_{2})\right\|\leq\varepsilon
provides the desired result.
\blacksquare

together yields the final upper bound on
\operatorname{NC}(K_{\gamma})-\operatorname{NC}(K)
.

C.3
Proof of Theorem
3.2

We start by stating and proving the spectral upper and lower bounds mentioned in Section
3.2
.

Lemma C.5

Under the assumption
(A,B,Q^{1/2})
is stabilizable and detectable, and for
\gamma\geq\tilde{\gamma}_{\infty}
, we have the following bounds:

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\preceq\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|^{2}\left\|R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right\|W_{\infty}(\tilde{A}+\tilde{B}K_{\star},I)

\displaystyle P_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}+\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\star}

\displaystyle\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\star}\right)\mathclose{}}\mathopen{}\left(W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right)\mathclose{}

Proof:
We start with the upper bound. Begin by recalling that for any
x
,
x^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}x
is the cost of applying controller
K_{\star}
in the noiseless adversarial setting at level
\gamma
from initial state
x
, while
P_{\gamma}
is the cost of applying controller
K_{\gamma}
in the noiseless adversarial setting at level
\gamma
from initial state
x
. If we denote the optimal map from the state to the adversary perturbation in the cases where controller
K_{\star}
and
K_{\gamma}
are deployed as
\tilde{N}_{\gamma}
and
N_{\gamma}
respectively, then we may consider this as the cost gap of applying the controller

\begin{bmatrix}u_{t}\\
\delta_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}K_{\star}\\
\tilde{N}_{\gamma}\end{bmatrix}x_{t}

to the system

x_{t+1}=Ax_{t}+\begin{bmatrix}B&I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u_{t}\\
\delta_{t}\end{bmatrix}

with cost
x_{t}^{\top}Qx_{t}+\begin{bmatrix}u_{t}\\
\delta_{t}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\
0&-\gamma^{2}I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}u_{t}\\
\delta_{t}\end{bmatrix}
. Similarly, we can consider applying the controller

\begin{bmatrix}u_{t}\\
\delta_{t}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}K_{\gamma}\\
N_{\gamma}\end{bmatrix},

which is optimal in this setting.

Then by Lemma 10 in
Fazel et al. 2018
, we have that

\displaystyle x^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}x-x^{\top}P_{\gamma}x=\sum_{t=0}\mathopen{}\left(x_{t}^{\prime}\right)\mathclose{}^{\top}\begin{bmatrix}K_{\star}-K_{\gamma}\\
\tilde{N}_{\gamma}-N_{\gamma}\end{bmatrix}^{\top}\mathopen{}\left(\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\
0&-\gamma^{2}I\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}B&I\end{bmatrix}^{\top}P_{\gamma}\begin{bmatrix}B&I\end{bmatrix}\right)\mathclose{}\begin{bmatrix}K_{\star}-K_{\gamma}\\
\tilde{N}_{\gamma}-N_{\gamma}\end{bmatrix}x_{t}^{\prime}

where
x_{0}^{\prime}=x
, and
x_{t+1}^{\prime}=(A+BK_{\star}+\tilde{N}_{\gamma})x_{t}^{\prime}
. Next, observe that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}R&0\\
0&-\gamma^{2}I\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}B&I\end{bmatrix}^{\top}P_{\gamma}\begin{bmatrix}B&I\end{bmatrix}\preceq\begin{bmatrix}R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B&0\\
0&0\end{bmatrix}

To see that this is the case, we will show that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}B^{\top}(P_{\gamma}-M_{\gamma})B&B^{\top}P_{\gamma}\\
P_{\gamma}B&P_{\gamma}-\gamma^{2}I\end{bmatrix}\preceq 0

Expanding
M_{\gamma}=P_{\gamma}+P_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}
, and multiplying the expression by
-1
, we can see that this is equivalent to showing that

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}B^{\top}P_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-P)^{-1}P_{\gamma}B&B^{\top}P_{\gamma}\\
P_{\gamma}B&\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma}\end{bmatrix}\succeq 0.

As
P_{\gamma}\preceq\gamma^{2}I
, this follows from a Scur complement check for positive definiteness by the fact that
\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma}\succ 0
. Then we have that

\displaystyle x^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}x-x^{\top}P_{\gamma}x\leq\sum_{t=0}\mathopen{}\left(x_{t}^{\prime}\right)\mathclose{}^{\top}(K_{\star}-K_{\gamma})^{\top}\mathopen{}\left(R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right)\mathclose{}(K_{\star}-K_{\gamma})x_{t}^{\prime}

or, by the fact thtat
x_{t+1}^{\prime}=A+BK_{\star}+\tilde{N}_{\gamma}x_{t}^{\prime}=\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star})x_{t}^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\gamma}

\displaystyle\preceq\sum_{t=0}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star})\right)\mathclose{}^{t}\right)\mathclose{}^{\top}(K_{\star}-K_{\gamma})^{\top}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\mathopen{}\left(R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right)\mathclose{}(K_{\star}-K_{\gamma})\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star})\right)\mathclose{}^{t}

as we wanted to show.

Moving on to the lower bound, note that we may express

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{{dlyap}}\mathopen{}\left((I+E)(A+BK_{\star}),Q+K_{\star}^{\top}RK_{\star}-\gamma^{2}(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}E^{\top}E(A+BK_{\star})\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle P_{\star}

\displaystyle=\textnormal{{dlyap}}\mathopen{}\left(A+BK_{\star},Q+K_{\star}^{\top}RK_{\star}\right)\mathclose{}

\displaystyle E

\displaystyle=(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}

Then

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\star}

\displaystyle=(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}(I+E)^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(I+E)(A+BK_{\star})-\gamma^{2}(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}E^{\top}E(A+BK_{\star})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}P_{\star}(A+BK_{\star})

\displaystyle=(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}(\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\star})(A+BK_{\star})+(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}\mathopen{}\left(E^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}+\tilde{P}_{\gamma}E+E^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}E-\gamma^{2}E^{\top}E\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star})

However, we have that

\displaystyle E^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}=\tilde{P}_{\gamma}E=\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})\tilde{P}_{\gamma}

and

\displaystyle E^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}E-\gamma^{2}E^{\top}E=\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-\gamma^{2}I\right)\mathclose{}E=\tilde{P}_{\gamma}^{\top}(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})\tilde{P}_{\gamma}.

Then

\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\star}=\textnormal{{dlyap}}(A+BK_{\star},(A+BK_{\star})^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(A+BK_{\star})).

We can expand the Lyapunov solution as an infinite sum:

\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P_{\star}=\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\left((A+BK_{\star})^{t}\right)\mathclose{}^{\top}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}(A+BK_{\star})^{t}

Next, note that

\tilde{P}_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}}I\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\star}\right)\mathclose{}}I

and thus

\tilde{P}_{\gamma}-P\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\star}\right)\mathclose{}}\sum_{t=1}^{\infty}\mathopen{}\left((A+BK_{\star})^{t}\right)\mathclose{}^{\top}(A+BK_{\star})^{t}

Adding in the term

P_{\gamma}(\gamma^{2}I-P_{\gamma})^{-1}P_{\gamma}\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\gamma})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}}I\succeq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\star}\right)\mathclose{}}I

allows us to start the sum from
0
, providing the desired result.
\blacksquare

Lemma C.6

As long as
\gamma
satisfies

\displaystyle\gamma^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})+\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})^{2}\sigma_{\min}(A+BK_{\star})^{2}\frac{1}{2\left\|R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right\|}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star}),I\right)\mathclose{}\right\|\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right\|,

we have the following bound

\displaystyle\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|\geq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})^{2}\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right\|\sigma_{\min}(A+BK_{\star})}{2\left\|R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right\|(\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}\mathopen{}\left(P_{\star}\right)\mathclose{})}

Proof:

Beginning with the lower bound in (
17
) and applying Lemma
C.5
, we find

\displaystyle\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\sigma_{\min}(A+BK_{\star})}{\left\|R+BM_{\gamma}B\right\|}\bigg(\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})}\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right\|

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star}),I\right)\mathclose{}\right\|\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|^{2}\left\|R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right\|\bigg)

In particular, by defining

\displaystyle a

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\min}(A+BK_{\star})\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}\mathopen{}\left(\mathopen{}\left(I+(\gamma^{2}I-\tilde{P}_{\gamma})^{-1}\tilde{P}_{\gamma}\right)\mathclose{}(A+BK_{\star}),I\right)\mathclose{}\right\|

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\frac{\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})^{2}}{\gamma^{2}-\sigma_{\min}(P_{\star})}\frac{\left\|B^{\top}W_{\infty}(A+BK_{\star},I)\right\|}{\left\|R+B^{\top}M_{\gamma}B\right\|}\sigma_{\min}(A+BK_{\star}),

the above bound can be written

\displaystyle a\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|^{2}+\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|-c\geq 0

which, considering that
\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|\geq 0
, implies that

\displaystyle a\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}+ac}-\frac{1}{2}.

Under the assumption that
ac\leq 2
, this may be simplified to
\left\|K_{\gamma}-K_{\star}\right\|\geq\frac{1}{2}c
.
\blacksquare

Theorem
3.2
then follows from the general lower bound in equation (
14
) and the bound from Lemma
C.6
.

Brauer relations, isogenies and parities of ranks
Alexandros Konstantinou

Abstract
. The present paper illustrates the utility of Brauer relations, Galois covers of curves and the theory of regulator constants in the context of studying isogenies between Jacobians and their relevance to the parity conjecture. This framework presents a unified approach, enabling the reconstruction of a diverse array of classical isogenies and the derivation of local expressions for Selmer rank parities, drawing from an extensive body of existing literature. These include the local expressions found in the works of Mazur–Rubin (dihedral extensions), Coates–Fukaya–Kato–Sujatha ( p g p^{g} isogenies), Kramer–Tunnell (quadratic twists of elliptic curves), Dokchitser–Maistret (Richelot isogenies), and Docking (prym construction).

1.1.
Uniform results for isogenies and rank parity expressions

The main motivation for this paper stems from
[
10

in which Brauer relations are used to establish the parity conjecture for a certain class of twists of elliptic curves. Building upon this groundwork,
[
13

introduces an expanded framework, enabling a broader scope by allowing
K
-automorphism subgroups on curves and pseudo Brauer relations. A Brauer relation is defined as a
\mathbb{Z}
-linear combination of subgroups of a finite group

\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}

G

\bigoplus_{i}\textup{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}\mathbf{1}\cong\bigoplus_{j}\textup{Ind}_{H_{j}^{\prime}}^{G}\mathbf{1}\ \ (H_{i},H_{j}^{\prime}\leq G).

The notion of a pseudo Brauer relation provides an extension of these and is recalled in Definition
2.6

[

asserts that, for a finite group
G
acting on a curve

induces an isogeny between Jacobians

\prod_{j}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}}\to\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}.

Let us call such an isogeny

1.2.
Brauer relations and isogenies

The work of Kani and Rosen
[
19
]
concerns the study of idempotent relations in the algebra of rational endomorphisms of arbitrary abelian varieties. Their central result in the setting of automorphisms on Jacobians proves that Brauer relations induce isogenies between Jacobians as in (
1.1
). The usefulness of this theorem rests ultimately on one’s ability to deduce the existence of an isogeny by presenting an explicit Brauer relation. As a result, applications of this work are widespread across the existing literature, see for example
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
28
]
and
[
32
]
.

One noteworthy application includes Edixhoven’s work
[
16
]
which presents an explicit Brauer relation for
\textup{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
in order to
verify
the existence of an isogeny between Jacobians of certain modular curves. The existence of this isogeny was originally deduced in Chen’s work by utilising the Selberg trace formula, see
[
4
, Theorem 1]
. This provides the main stimulus for the contents of Theorem
1.2
mentioned above which asserts that a range of classical isogenies are pseudo Brauer verifiable in the following sense.

Definition 1.6
.

Let
Y_{1},\dots,Y_{m}
and
Y_{1}^{\prime},\dots,Y_{r}^{\prime}
be curves defined over a number field
K
. In addition, suppose that there exists a
K
-rational isogeny
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{i}}\to\prod_{j=1}^{r}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{j}^{\prime}}
. We say that a pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
for
G
and a curve
X

verifies an isogeny
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{i}}\to\prod_{j=1}^{r}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{j}^{\prime}}
if we can find subgroups
H_{1},\dots,H_{m}
and
H_{1}^{\prime},\dots,H_{r}^{\prime}
of
G
such that

(1)

the quotient curves satisfy
X/H_{i}\cong Y_{i}
and
X/H_{j}^{\prime}\cong Y_{j}^{\prime}
for all
i
and
j
,

(2)

and
\Theta=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}H_{j}^{\prime}
is the underlying pseudo Brauer relation.

In addition, we say that an isogeny
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{i}}\to\prod_{j=1}^{r}\textup{Jac}_{Y_{j}^{\prime}}
is
pseudo Brauer verifiable
if we can find a group
G
acting on a curve
X
and a pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta
for these such that conditions (1)-(2) are satisfied.

Remark 1.7
.

We note that the concept of pseudo Brauer verifiability is primarily focused on verifying the existence of
some
isogeny between
\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}
and
\prod_{j}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}}
. We deliberately refrain from making any specific references to an explicit morphism
\prod_{i}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{i}}\to\prod_{j}\textup{Jac}_{X/H_{j}^{\prime}}
in this definition.

It would be interesting to know whether
any
isogeny between Jacobians is pseudo Brauer verifiable. A slightly more ambitious problem would be deriving similar results in the case of general abelian varieties. A result in this direction can be found in the work of Yu
[
32
, Corollary 10]
, where the author deduces an isogeny between
\textup{Res}_{F/K}A
for
F/K
an abelian extension and
\prod_{\chi\in\textup{Gal}(F/K)}A^{\chi}
, a product of its twists. This is done by using twisted idempotent relations, his generalisation of the Kani-Rosen relations. The underlying isogeny in this case is classically well-known and can be found in
[
20
]
.

1.3.
Local expressions, regulator constants and the parity conjecture

In addition, this paper addresses the systematic derivation of local rank parity expressions for Jacobians. The existence of such expressions is expected in view of the parity conjecture. This conjecture is based on expectations made by the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture combined with the theoretical framework of
L
-functions.

Conjecture 1.12

(Parity conjecture)
.

For an abelian variety
A/K
defined over a number field,

\textup{rk}(A/K)\equiv\sum_{v\ \textup{place of}\ K}\mu(A/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2,

where
(-1)^{\mu(A/K_{v})}=w(A/K_{v})
, and
w(A/K_{v})
denotes the local root number of
A
at a place
v
.

Such global-to-local expressions have established themselves as highly valuable. Their usefulness stems from the relative ease with which local invariants can be computed, a stark contrast to the formidable challenge posed by global invariants such as the rank of an abelian variety. As a result, they prove to be a crucial step in many parity-related proofs, see for example
[
6
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
22
]
.

A notable instance of a global-to-local formula appears in the work of Mazur–Rubin
[
25
]
in the context of base changing elliptic curves to
D_{2p}
-extensions. The primary impetus behind the development of Theorem
1.5
can be traced back to a result in
[
11
]
. Within its pages, the authors employ their theory of regulator constants to give a local expression for the same rank parity as the formula appearing in Mazur–Rubin’s work. This theory was subsequently extended in
[
13
]
, where the new framework allows for a broader scope by permitting the use of subgroups of
K
-automorphisms on curves and pseudo Brauer relations. We now recall the main result of this advancement.

Notation 1.13
.

We write
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for the standard inner product of complex characters of
G
, and we fix an arbitrary embedding
\mathbb{Q}_{p}\subseteq\mathbb{C}
for the purpose of comparing characters. We write
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
for the dual
p^{\infty}
-Selmer group of
\textup{Jac}_{X}
and
\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X})
for its
p^{\infty}
-Selmer rank.

Theorem 1.14

(
[
13
]

.

Let
\Theta
be a pseudo Brauer relation for

, and in addition suppose that

is self-dual as a

\sum_{\tau\in S_{\Theta,p}}\langle\tau,\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X})\rangle\equiv\sum_{v\ \textup{place of}\ K}\textup{ord}_{p}\ \Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})\quad\textup{mod }2,

where
\Lambda_{\Theta}(X/K_{v})
are local invariants as in Definition
2.10
and
S_{\Theta,p}
as in Definition
2.7
.

The self-duality assumption on

1.4.
Outline

This paper is organised as follows. In
\lx@sectionsign 2

2.1.

Notation 2.1

We write

to denote any field of characteristic

,

Definition 2.2

Let

\rho_{1}\oplus\bigoplus_{i}\textup{Ind}_{H_{i}}^{G}\mathbf{1}\cong\rho_{2}\oplus\bigoplus_{j}\textup{Ind}_{H_{j}^{\prime}}^{G}\mathbf{1}.

Notation 2.3
.

Let
\Theta=\sum_{i}H_{i}-\sum_{j}H_{j}^{\prime}
be a pseudo Brauer relation relative to

\left\langle,\right\rangle_{2}
on

Let

,
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}

The present paper will only utilise pseudo Brauer relations relative to the

2.3.
Galois closures, monodromy and Riemann-Hurwitz

We now record some well-known results concerning monodromy. These tools will be of great importance when constructing Galois covers using a down-to-top approach by taking the Galois closure of a branched cover of curves.

Warning
.

For the remainder of this subsection
only
, our primary assumption will be that all curves are geometrically connected and, unless otherwise stated, defined over
\mathbb{C}
. We note that this deviates from the ongoing conventions in §
1.6
.

We adopt a similar notation to the one used in
[
24
]
to record the ramification in the fibre of a branch point and the cycle type of a permutation.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
f:Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a morphism of complex curves, and let
\mathcal{B}_{f}=\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}(\mathbb{C})
be its branch locus. Suppose that the fibre

contains

points each with ramification index

, and suppose that

takes values in
\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then, we write
R_{b_{i}}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(n_{j})^{e_{j}}
to denote the
ramification structure of
f
at the point
b_{i}
. The
ramification structure of
f
is the multi-set given by
\{R_{b_{i}}\}_{i=1}^{r}
.

Definition 2.13
.

Let
\sigma
be a permutation in
S_{n}
expressed as a product of disjoint cycles. Suppose that
\sigma
contains
m_{j}
cycles each of length
t_{j}>1
where
j
takes values in
\{1,\dots,k\}
. Then, we write
r_{\sigma}=\prod_{j=1}^{k}(t_{j})^{m_{j}}
to denote the

\sigma
.

Example 2.14
.

Let
\sigma\in S_{7}
be the element
(1,2)(3,4)(5,6,7)
. Then,
r_{\sigma}=(2)^{2}(3)^{1}
.

Theorem 2.15

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
f:Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a degree
n
cover of complex curves branched at
r
points
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
, and write
F:X_{Z}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
to denote its Galois closure. Then, there exists an
r
-tuple
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
of elements in

such that the ramification structure at

matches the cycle type of
\sigma_{i}
. In addition, the Galois group of

is a transitive subgroup of

generated by the

.

The tuple
(\sigma_{1},\dots,\sigma_{r})
afforded by Theorem

is usually called the

.

Remark 2.16
.

As detailed in
[
24
, Proposition 3.18 & Remark 3.19]
, the cyclic group

afforded by Theorem

coincides (up to conjugacy) with the decomposition group of any point in the fibre

.

The following theorem gives a description for the
G
-module structure of the
\ell
-adic Tate module of the Jacobian variety of a complex curve
X
in terms of the of monodromy of the branched cover
X\to Y
. Given an element
\sigma\in G
, we write
\mathbf{1}_{\sigma}^{*}
to denote the permutation representation
\textup{Ind}_{\langle\sigma\rangle}^{G}\mathbf{1}
.

Theorem 2.17

(
[
17
]
Proposition 1.1)
.

Let
F:X\to Y
be a Galois cover of complex curves which is branched at
\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
, and write
G
to denote its Galois group. In addition, suppose that
g_{i}
is a generator for the decomposition group of any point in

. Then, for any prime
\ell
, the following

-representations

V_{\ell}(\textup{Jac}_{X})\ \ \textup{and}\ \ \mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\oplus(\mathbf{1}_{e}^{*})^{\oplus(2g(Y)-2)}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}(\mathbf{1}_{e}^{*}\ominus\mathbf{1}_{g_{i}}^{*}),

become isomorphic after extending scalars to
\mathbb{C}
, where

denotes the genus of the curve
Y
.

For the remainder of this section, we write
K
to denote a number field or a local field of characteristic
0
.

Corollary 2.18
.

Let
X/K
be a geometrically connected curve. Let
G
be a subgroup of

-automorphisms of

. Then, for any prime
\ell
, Theorem
2.17
holds for

.

Proof.

Base-changing along a field embedding

, it suffices to prove the result for
K
replaced with
\mathbb{C}
and

a complex curve. This is precisely Theorem

.
∎

Corollary 2.19
.

Let
f:Z\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a
K
-rational morphism of geometrically connected curves. We write
\mathcal{B}_{f}=\{b_{i}\}_{i=1}^{r}
to denote its branch locus. Let

be the Galois closure of

, and in addition suppose that

is geometrically connected. Then, for any prime

, the

-representations

V_{\ell}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Z}})\ \ \textup{and}\ \ \mathbf{1}^{\oplus 2}\ominus(\mathbf{1}_{e}^{*})^{\oplus 2}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}(\mathbf{1}_{e}^{*}\ominus\mathbf{1}_{\sigma_{i}}^{*}),

, where the

Proof.

By
[

, the branch locus of

coincides with the branch locus of its Galois closure

we deduce that the groups generated by the

are (up to conjugacy) equal to the decomposition at any point in the fibre of

.

∎

3.

In the following section, we provide a local expression for the

-Selmer rank of the Jacobian of a hyperelliptic curve

base changed to a quadratic field extension

. We first prove pseudo Brauer verifiability for the isogeny

\textup{Res}_{L/K}\textup{Jac}_{Y/L}\to\textup{Jac}_{Y}\times\textup{Jac}_{Y^{d}},

where
Y^{d}
denotes the quadratic twist of
Y
by
d
. We write
Y_{L}
to denote

the base change to

, and as in §

, we write

to denote the curve obtained from

by forgetting the

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
Y/K
be a hyperelliptic curve with affine model

, where

is a square-free polynomial. Then, the function field inclusions
K(x)\subseteq K(x,y)\subseteq L(x,y)
fit into a Galois diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y=X/C_{2}^{a}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{d}=X/C_{2}^{b}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X/C_{2}^{c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{P}_{x}^{1}=X/(C_{2}\times C_{2})}

where
C_{2}^{a},C_{2}^{b},C_{2}^{c}
denote all proper subgroups of
C_{2}\times C_{2}
.

Proof.

The function field

coincides with the biquadratic extension of

obtained by adjoining

and
\sqrt{d}
. By
[
13
, Remark A.31]
, this induces a

-action on

via
K
-automorphisms obtained by separately negating

and

. The quotient curves agree with what is asserted by the claim in view of
[

, Remark A.30]
. Indeed, two of the intermediate field extensions are

which coincide with the function fields of

and

respectively. ∎

Theorem 3.2
.

The pseudo Brauer relation
\Theta=\{e\}+2(C_{2}\times C_{2})-C_{2}^{a}-C_{2}^{b}-C_{2}^{c}
for

and

verifies an isogeny
\textup{Res}_{L/K}\textup{Jac}_{Y/L}\to\textup{Jac}_{Y/K}\times\textup{Jac}_{Y^{d}/K}

The function fields obtained by taking

and

-invariance in Theorem

are

and

respectively. Therefore, the quotients of

by

and

are both curves of genus

\Theta=\{e\}+2(C_{2}\times C_{2})-C_{2}^{a}-C_{2}^{b}-C_{2}^{c}
for

induces an isogeny

[

, it follows

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

be a hyperelliptic curve and

the twist of

by

. Then,

In this section, we use Brauer relations and regulator constants to deduce a local expression for the rank parity of the Jacobian of a genus
2
curve
\textup{Jac}_{X}
admitting a Richelot isogeny under some mild assumption on the Galois group of its
2
-torsion field (see Definition

[

]
and

for details.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

be genus
2

, where

is a square-free polynomial of degree
6

into 3 coprime quadratics subject to the condition that the set
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
is stable under the action of

\phi:\textup{Jac}_{Z}\to A
, where

is a principally polarised abelian variety such that
\phi^{\vee}\circ\phi=[2]_{\textup{Jac}_{Z}}.

Proof.

See
[
30
, Proposition 8.2.3]

∎

We say that
\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
is a
quadratic splitting
for
Z
. In view of
[
2
, Lemma 4.1]
, a Jacobian of a genus
2
curve admits a

-isogeny only when

admits a quadratic splitting. For the remainder of this section, we will assume that
\textup{Gal}(K(\textup{Jax}_{Z}[2])/K)
is a
2
-group. Since
\textup{Gal}(K(\textup{Jax}_{Z}[2])/K)=\textup{Gal}(F)
, this assumption is equivalent to
\textup{Gal}(F)\leq D_{8}\times C_{2}
, where
D_{8}
denotes the dihedral group of order

. Following the terminology introduced in
[

]
, we refer to these curves as

(albeit acknowledging the discrepancy in notation regarding dihedral groups.).

Definition 4.2
.

Let

be a genus

\{F_{1},F_{2},F_{3}\}
. We say that

is a

\textup{Gal}(F_{1}F_{2}F_{3})\subseteq D_{8}\times C_{2}
as a permutation group on

and

act separately on

and

Convention
.

We note that the individual quadratic factors

need not be defined over

. In particular, under the assumption that

is a

curve, then two of the quadratic factors could be Galois conjugate over a quadratic field extension

. When this happens, we adhere to the convention that

and
F_{2}
are Galois conjugate over
K(\sqrt{d})
, while
F_{3}
is necessarily defined over
K
.

Notation 4.3
.

For the remainder of this section, for any

curve

we fix the following constant

. If
Z
admits a quadratic splitting in which
2
quadratic factors are Galois conjugate over a quadratic extension

, then we fix

to be any generator for

. If all quadratic factors are defined over

, then we fix

.

If we assume that the quadratic splitting is non-singular in the sense of

[

, Definition 8.2.4]
, then
[

, Proposition 4.3]
asserts that the target of this isogeny is the Jacobian of a

curve with an affine model

. The polynomials

are defined as follows.

Definition 4.4
.

Let

be a non-singular quadratic splitting for a genus
2

. Then, for
(i,j,k)=(1,2,3),(2,3,1),(3,1,2)

5.

We let
Z/K
be a geometrically connected curve of genus
g
, and in addition we suppose
\textup{Jac}_{Z}[2](k)
has a non-trivial torsion point, or equivalently there exists an unramified double cover
\pi:\tilde{Z}\to Z

denoted

of

containing the identity. It is classically well-known that there exists a

It is worthy of note that

[
15
, §2.4]
establishes that for trigonal curves

[
3

where the authors prove that the Prym is necessarily a Jacobian upon taking a quadratic twist of the cover

Definition 5.1
.

to lie in

in which case

is the trivial twist. We write

Lemma 6.1

\langle V_{\ell},\mathbf{1}_{H}^{*}\rangle=2\cdot\textup{genus}(X_{Z}/H).
The genera of the quotients of
X_{Z}
by
A_{1},A_{2}
and
A_{3}
are known in view of Proposition
7.3
allowing one to deduce that
\langle V_{\ell},\mathbf{1}\rangle=\langle V_{\ell},\sigma\rangle=0,
while
\langle V_{\ell},\epsilon\rangle=\langle V_{\ell},\sigma\otimes\epsilon\rangle=2.

In addition, for
p\geq 3
,

\#\langle\tau\rangle\backslash(S_{p}\times C_{2})/H^{\prime}=\begin{cases}p^{2}-3p+3,&\tau=\tau_{1},\\
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}-3),&\tau=\tilde{\tau}_{\frac{p-3}{2}},\\
\frac{1}{2}(p^{2}-1),&\tau=\tilde{\tau}_{\frac{p-1}{2}}.\end{cases}

Therefore, by invoking Theorem
7.6
, we deduce that
\langle V_{\ell},\mathbf{1}_{H^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=2
, and therefore
\textup{genus}(X_{Y}/H^{\prime})=1.
Moreover,

\langle\sigma\otimes\epsilon,\mathbf{1}_{H^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}^{*}+\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}_{H^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=3-2-1+1=1.

Finally, by writing
\mathbf{1}_{H^{\prime}}^{*}=(\sigma\otimes\epsilon)\oplus T
, we deduce that
\langle V_{\ell},T\rangle=0
as we have shown that
\langle V_{\ell},\sigma\otimes\epsilon\rangle=\langle V_{\ell},\mathbf{1}_{H^{\prime}}^{*}\rangle=2
. From this we deduce that
\Theta=(S_{p-1}\times\{e\})-(S_{p}\times\{e\})-H^{\prime}
is a pseudo Brauer relation for
S_{p}\times C_{2}
and
X_{Y}
. In view of the quotient curves in Proposition
7.3
,
\Theta
verifies an isogeny
\textup{Jac}_{Y}\to E\times\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}/H^{\prime}}.
∎

We now use the pseudo Brauer relation from Theorem
7.8
in order to provide a local expression for
\textup{rk}_{p}\ \textup{Jac}_{Y}
. We first prove some auxiliary lemmata.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
p
be a rational prime. Then, the multiplicities of
\epsilon
and
\sigma\otimes\epsilon
in
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}})
satisfy

\langle\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}}),\epsilon\rangle=\textup{rk}_{p}(E)\ \ \ \textup{and}\ \ \ \langle\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}}),\sigma\otimes\epsilon\rangle=\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}/H^{\prime}}),

Proof.

We start by writing
\epsilon=\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}
. Then,
\langle\mathcal{X}_{p},\epsilon\rangle
satisfies

\langle\mathcal{X}_{p},\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}\rangle\underset{\mathrm{Frob.rec.}}{=}\textup{dim}\ \mathcal{X}_{p}^{A_{1}}-\textup{dim}\ \mathcal{X}_{p}^{S_{p}\times C_{2}}\underset{\textup{Prop.}\ref{Galois_closure}}{=}\textup{dim}\ \mathcal{X}_{p}(E).

The second claim follows in the same way after expressing
\sigma\otimes\epsilon=\mathbf{1}_{A_{3}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{2}}^{*}-\mathbf{1}_{A_{1}}^{*}+\mathbf{1}
, and therefore
\langle\mathcal{X}_{p},\sigma\otimes\epsilon\rangle=\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{Y})-0-\textup{rk}_{p}(E)+0=\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}/H^{\prime}})
as required. ∎

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
\Theta=(S_{p-1}\times\{e\})-(S_{p}\times\{e\})-H^{\prime}
be the pseudo Brauer relation for
X_{Y}/K
afforded by Theorem
7.8
. Then,

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\epsilon)\equiv\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\sigma\otimes\epsilon)\equiv p\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.

Proof.

The calculation for
\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\epsilon)
is straightforward (one can choose the trivial pairing on
\epsilon
). We fix a basis
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{p}\}
for
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. Then,
\sigma\otimes\epsilon
corresponds to the subspace spanned by
v_{i}=e_{i+1}-e_{1}
for
i=1,\dots,p-1
with
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\in S_{p}\times C_{2}
acting via
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})\cdot v_{i}=\textup{sgn}(\rho_{2})(e_{\rho_{1}(i+1)}-e_{\rho_{1}(1)})
. A non-degenerate,
G
-invariant pairing on
\sigma\otimes\epsilon
is given by setting the diagonal entries

\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle v_{i},v_{i}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle=2
while
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle=1
for
i\neq j
. Suppose that the
S_{p-1}
factor in
S_{p-1}\times\{e\}
stabilises
1
. Then for
H\in\{S_{p-1}\times\{e\},S_{p}\times\{e\},H^{\prime}\}
, the space of
H
-invariant vectors is
1
-dimensional spanned by
u_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}v_{i}
,
0
-dimensional and
1
-dimensional spanned by
u_{2}=v_{p-2}-v_{p-1}
respectively. Therefore,
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle u_{1},u_{1}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle=p(p-1)
, while
\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle u_{2},u_{2}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle=2
. Putting everything together,

\mathcal{C}_{\Theta}(\sigma\otimes\epsilon)\equiv\frac{\frac{1}{(p-1)!}\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle u_{1},u_{1}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle}{\frac{1}{2(p-2)!}\langle\mskip-3.0mu\langle u_{2},u_{2}\rangle\mskip-3.0mu\rangle}\equiv p\quad\textup{mod }\mathbb{Q}^{\times 2}.\qed

Theorem 7.11
.

Let
Y/K
be a genus
2
curve which admits a
K
-rational morphism
\phi:Y\to E
of prime degree
p
. In addition, if
p
is an odd prime, suppose that
\phi
is generic. Then,

\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{Y})\equiv\sum_{v\ \textup{place of}\ K}\textup{ord}_{p}\ \Lambda_{\Theta}(X_{Y}/K_{v})\ \quad\textup{mod }2,

where
\Theta=(S_{p-1}\times\{e\})-(S_{p}\times\{e\})-H^{\prime}
is the pseudo Brauer relation for
S_{p}\times C_{2}
and
X_{Y}
afforded by Theorem
7.8
.

Proof.

The
p=2
case is dealt with in Proposition
7.1
. We write
\mathcal{X}_{p}
to denote
\mathcal{X}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{X_{Y}})
. Then, we claim that
S_{\Theta,p}=\{\epsilon,\sigma\otimes\epsilon\}.
Indeed, by combining the proof of Theorem
7.8
with Lemma
2.8
(1), we deduce that
S_{\Theta,p}\subseteq\{\epsilon,\sigma\otimes\epsilon\}
. By Lemma
7.10
, it follows that
S_{\Theta,p}=\{\epsilon,\sigma\otimes\epsilon\}
. Since any
S_{p}\times C_{2}
-representation is self-dual, then Theorem
1.14
is applicable. In view of Lemma
7.9
, the result follows upon applying Theorem
1.14
to
\Theta
afforded by Theorem
7.8
.∎

Remark 7.12
.

We note that elliptic subcovers occur in pairs
(\phi,\psi)
. In particular, given a genus
2
curve admitting an elliptic subcover
\phi:Y\to E
, then there exists a complimentary elliptic subcover
\psi:Y\to E^{\prime}
of the same degree, see
[
23
]
. Therefore, upon interchanging the roles of
\phi
and
\psi
and
E
with
E^{\prime}
if needed, we note that the conditions for the validity of Proposition
7.3
, Theorem
7.8
and Theorem
7.11
may be relaxed to apply when
\phi
or its complementary cover
\psi
conforms to the condition of being generic.

Remark 7.13
.

Drawing from customary techniques, we can derive an analogous local formula in this case is as follows. We let
\phi:Y\to E
be a degree
p
elliptic subcover, and write
\psi:Y\to E^{\prime}
for the complimentary elliptic subcover. Then,
F=\phi^{*}+\psi^{*}
is a
(p,p)
isogeny satisfying
F\circ F^{\vee}=[p]_{\textup{Jac}_{Y}}
. By invoking
[
32
, Theorem 5]
, we deduce

\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{Y})\equiv\sum_{v}\textup{ord}_{p}\ \frac{C_{v}\ (\textup{Jac}_{Y},\phi^{*}\omega_{v}^{0}(E)\wedge\psi^{*}\omega_{v}^{0}(E^{\prime}))}{c_{v}(E)c_{v}({E^{\prime}})}\cdot\theta_{v}\quad\textup{mod }2,

where
\omega_{v}^{0}(E)
and
\omega_{v}^{0}(E^{\prime})
denote a choice of minimal differentials on
E/K_{v}
and
{E^{\prime}}/K_{v}
respectively, while
\theta_{v}=\frac{\mu_{v}(Y)}{\mu_{v}(E)\cdot\mu_{v}(E^{\prime})}
if
p=2
, and otherwise
\theta_{v}=1
. We note that this method for obtaining this local expression for
\textup{rk}_{p}(\textup{Jac}_{Y})
makes no assumption on
\phi
or
\psi
being generic.

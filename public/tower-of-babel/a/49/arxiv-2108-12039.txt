Abstract
We investigate ϕ \phi meson photoproduction on the nucleon and the He 4 \mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}} targets within a dynamical model approach based on a Hamiltonian which describes the production mechanisms by the Pomeron-exchange, meson-exchanges, ϕ \phi radiations, and nucleon resonance excitations mechanisms. The final ϕ ​ N \phi N interactions are included being described by the gluon-exchange, direct ϕ ​ N \phi N couplings, and the box-diagrams arising from the couplings with π ​ N \pi N , ρ ​ N \rho N , K ​ Λ K\Lambda , and K ​ Σ K\Sigma channels. The parameters of the Hamiltonian are determined by the experimental data of γ ​ p → ϕ ​ p \gamma p\to\phi p from the CLAS Collaboration. The resulting Hamiltonian is then used to predict the coherent ϕ \phi -meson production on the He 4 \mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}} targets by using the distorted-wave impulse approximation. For the proton target, the final ϕ ​ N \phi N rescattering effects, as required by the unitarity condition, are found to be very weak, which supports the earlier calculations in the literature. For the He 4 \mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}} targets, the predicted differential cross sections are in good agreement with the data obtained by the LEPS Collaboration. The role of each mechanism in this reaction is discussed and predictions for a wide range of scattering angles are presented, which can be tested in future experiments.

Most experiments performed through photon-nucleus scatterings have been

to estimate the value of the

;

Recently, exclusive
\phi

The measurements for coherent and incoherent

;

;

;
Hiraiwa18
.
In the present work, we focus on the reaction of

\displaystyle\gamma+\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}\to\phi+\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}

Theoretical studies on coherent
\phi
photoproduction from nuclei are rather scarce.
Most studies are for the reactions with deuteron targets

;
RSS05
;
TK07a
;

;

The purpose of the present work is to investigate

Argonne National Laboratory and Osaka University (ANL-Osaka) Collaboration

;

In this approach we construct a model Hamiltonian with the parameters determined by the data of
\phi
photoproduction on the nucleon
targets.
Earlier studies of vector meson photoproduction were mainly in the very high energy region where the Regge phenomenology
is applicable, which led to a fairly successful Pomeron exchange model

.
In the near threshold energy region, however, the mechanisms arising from meson-exchanges and the excitation of nucleon resonances (
N^{*}
)

TOY97
;
TOYM98
;
TLTS99
;
KN19
;
KN20
.
In the present work, we follow the model of Refs.
KN19
;
KN20
for the mechanisms of
\phi
photoproduction.

The unitarity condition requires that the
\gamma N\to\phi N
amplitude must include the
\phi N\to\phi N
final state interaction (FSI) as well.
As shown in the literature
KLNS19
;
HDHH12
;
ABKN11
;
said

N^{*}
)

In addition, the
\phi N\to\phi N
reaction is essential in exploring the possible
\phi
-nucleus bound states, as predicted by lattice quantum
chromodynamics (LQCD) calculations

.
This also accounts for the FSI in the reaction of
\phi
meson photoproduction on
nuclei

In the present work, we elaborate on the model for this reaction as well.
For this end, we construct a model for
\phi N
interactions.
As possible sources for
\phi N
interactions one may consider the gluon-exchange mechanism within quantum chromodynamics (QCD)
as well as the diagrams arising from non-vanishing
\phi NN
coupling.
Another possibility is due to the decay processes of
\phi\to K\bar{K}
and

\phi N\to KY,\pi N,\rho N

In the leading order these interactions can generate the box-diagram mechanisms on the

With a model Hamiltonian constructed by fitting the data of
\phi
photoproduction on the nucleon,
we will investigate its production on nuclei within the multiple scattering formulation

By using the well-established factorization approximation, the photoproduction amplitude on a nucleus can be expressed
in terms of the
\gamma N\rightarrow\phi N
amplitude and a nuclear form factor.
The final
\phi
-nucleus interactions can be calculated from an optical potential which is calculated, in the leading order, from the

\phi N\rightarrow\phi N
amplitude and nuclear form factor.
We will apply this approach to understand the data from the
\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}

\displaystyle\,\mbox{}\times[\bar{u}_{N}(p,m_{s})u_{N}(p^{\prime},m^{\prime}_{s})][\epsilon^{*}_{\mu}(k,\lambda_{\phi})\epsilon^{\mu}(k^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}_{\phi})],

IV.3

To calculate the box-diagrams depicted in Figs.

The

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\phi K{K}}

\displaystyle=ig_{\phi KK}\left(K^{-}\partial_{\mu}{K^{+}}-\partial_{\mu}K^{-}K^{+}\right)\phi^{\mu},

(99)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{KNY}

\displaystyle=-\frac{f_{KNY}}{M_{K}}\bar{Y}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\,N\partial^{\mu}K+\mbox{h.c.},

(100)

where the coupling constant
g_{\phi KK}=4.48

\Gamma_{\phi\to K^{+}K^{-}}=2.09

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\phi\to K^{+}K^{-}}=\frac{g_{\phi KK}^{2}p_{K}^{3}}{6\pi M_{\phi}^{2}},

(101)

For the
\phi N\rightarrow\pi N

\phi N\to\rho N

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\phi\rho\pi}=

\displaystyle-\frac{g_{\phi\rho\pi}}{M_{\phi}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}(\partial_{\mu}\bm{\rho}_{\nu})\cdot(\partial_{\alpha}\bm{\pi})\phi_{\beta},

(102)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\rho NN}=

\displaystyle g_{\rho NN}\bar{N}\left(\bm{\rho}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu}-\frac{\kappa_{\rho NN}}{2M_{N}}\sigma^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\bm{\rho}_{\mu}\right)\cdot\bm{\tau}N,

(103)

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{\pi NN}=

\displaystyle-\frac{f_{\pi NN}}{M_{\pi}}\bar{N}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma_{5}\bm{\tau}\cdot(\partial^{\mu}\bm{\pi})N,

(104)

where
g_{\phi\rho\pi}=1.22
is determined by the decay width of
\phi\rightarrow\rho\pi
.
The
\rho NN
coupling constant is determined by using the Nijmegen potential
SR99
;
RSY99

\displaystyle g_{\rho NN}=2.97,\quad\kappa_{\rho NN}=4.22.

(105)

The couplings of pseudoscalar mesons and baryons are determined by the SU(3) flavor symmetry relations,

\displaystyle\frac{f_{KN\Lambda}}{M_{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-3+2\alpha}{\sqrt{3}}\frac{f_{\pi NN}}{M_{\pi}},

(106)

\displaystyle\frac{f_{KN\Sigma}}{M_{K}}

With

For the reaction of

\displaystyle I_{KY,\phi N}^{\mu}=i\frac{g_{\phi KK}f_{KNY}}{M_{K}}\frac{1}{q^{2}-M^{2}_{K}}\not{q}\gamma_{5}(k-q)^{\mu},

\displaystyle I_{\pi N,\phi N}^{\mu}=-\frac{g_{\phi\rho\pi}g_{\rho NN}}{M_{\phi}}\frac{1}{q^{2}-M^{2}_{\rho}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}k_{\nu}q_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\hskip 43.00009pt\mbox{}\times\left[\gamma_{\beta}-\frac{\kappa_{\rho NN}}{4M_{N}}\left(\gamma_{\beta}\not{q}-\not{q}\gamma_{\beta}\right)\right],

(112)

\displaystyle I_{\rho N,\phi N}^{\mu}=\sum_{\lambda_{\rho}}-i\frac{g_{\phi\rho\pi}}{M_{\phi}}\frac{f_{\pi NN}}{M_{\pi}}\frac{1}{q^{2}-M^{2}_{\pi}}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}k_{\nu}q_{\alpha}

Here,
q=p^{\prime}-p=\textbf{(}E_{N}(p^{\prime})-E_{B}(p),\,\mathbf{p}^{\prime}-\mathbf{p}\textbf{)}

B=Y
or

the polarization vector

of the

meson with momentum

We consider the following form factor for the

\displaystyle F_{\rm Box}(\mathbf{k}_{MB})=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{MB}^{2}}{\Lambda_{MB}^{2}+\mathbf{k}_{MB}^{2}}\right)^{2},

(115)

and

\displaystyle F_{\rm Box}^{\prime}(\mathbf{k}_{MB}^{\prime})=\left(\frac{\Lambda_{MB}^{\prime 2}}{\Lambda_{MB}^{\prime 2}+\mathbf{k}_{MB}^{\prime 2}}\right)^{2},

\displaystyle\mathbf{k}_{MB}^{{}^{\prime}2}

(118)

We choose the cutoff parameters to be

V
Formulation for
A(\gamma,\phi)A
reaction

The differential cross section of coherent photoproduction of a vector meson (
V
) on a nuclear
target (
A
) with
A
nucleons,
\gamma(q)+A(P_{i})\rightarrow V(k)+A(P_{f})
, are obtained as

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{dt}=\frac{\pi}{\lvert\mathbf{q}\rvert\lvert\mathbf{k}\rvert}\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega_{\rm Lab}},

(125)

where the differential cross section in the laboratory (Lab) frame (
\mathbf{P}_{i}=0
) is

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma}{d\Omega_{\rm Lab}}=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}\left|\textbf{k}\right|^{2}E_{V}(\textbf{k})E_{A}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{k})}{\left|E_{A}(\textbf{q}-\textbf{k})|\textbf{k}|+E_{V}(\mathbf{k})(|\mathbf{k}|-|\bm{q}|\cos\theta_{\rm Lab})\right|}

(126)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\frac{1}{2(2J+1)}

(127)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\left\lvert\braket{k\lambda_{V},\Phi_{P_{f}M_{J_{f}}}\lvert T(E)\rvert q\lambda_{\gamma},P_{i}\Phi_{P_{i},M_{J_{i}}}}\right\rvert^{2},

(128)

where
\cos\theta_{\rm Lab}=\hat{\mathbf{q}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{k}}
.
Within the distorted-wave impulse approximation of multiple scattering theory
Feshbach92
,
the scattering
T
-matrix defined by the Hamiltonian of Eq. (
3
) can be written as

\displaystyle T(E)=T^{\rm IMP}(E)+T^{\rm FSI}(E),

(129)

where

\displaystyle T^{\rm IMP}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1,A}\left[B_{\phi N_{i},\gamma N_{i}}+T^{N^{*}}_{\phi N_{i},\gamma N_{i}}\right],

\displaystyle T^{\rm FSI}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)\frac{1}{E-H_{0}}T^{\rm IMP}.

(130)

The impulse term
T^{\rm IMP}
is the term that the
\phi
meson is produced from a single nucleon in
the nucleus, and
T^{\rm FSI}
is the effect due to the scattering of the outgoing
\phi
with the recoiled nucleus.
The
\phi A\rightarrow\phi A
scattering
T
-matrix is defined by

\displaystyle T_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle U_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)

(132)

\displaystyle\mbox{}+U_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)\frac{1}{E-H_{0}+i\epsilon}T_{\phi A,\phi A}(E),

where
U_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)
is the
\phi A
potential.

Within the multiple scattering theory
Feshbach92
, one can define the
\phi A
potential
in terms of the
\phi N
scattering amplitude.
In the first order, we have

\displaystyle U_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)=\sum_{i=1,A}t_{\phi N_{i},\phi N_{i}}(\omega),

(133)

where
t_{\phi N_{i},\phi N_{i}}(\omega)
was defined in Eq. (
8
).
We take the widely used factorization approximation
Feshbach92
to evaluate the
matrix element of
U_{\phi A,\phi A}(E)
.
In the c.m. frame, for the reaction of
\phi(\bm{\kappa})+A(-\bm{\kappa})\to\phi(\bm{\kappa}^{\prime})+A(-\bm{\kappa}^{\prime})
,
we have

\displaystyle U_{\phi A,\phi A}(\bm{\kappa},\bm{\kappa}^{\prime},E)

\displaystyle=\braket{\bm{\kappa}^{\prime}|U_{\phi A,\phi A}|\bm{\kappa}}

(134)

\displaystyle\hskip-50.00008pt=A\braket{\bm{\kappa}^{\prime},(\mathbf{p}_{0}+\mathbf{q})|t_{\phi N,\phi N}(\omega_{0})|\bm{\kappa},\mathbf{p}_{0}}F(q),

(135)

where
\mathbf{q}=\bm{\kappa}-\bm{\kappa}^{\prime}
,
\mathbf{p}_{0}=-\bm{\kappa}/A
,

\omega_{0}=E_{\phi}(\kappa_{0})+E_{N}(\kappa_{0}/A)
with
\kappa_{0}
defined by

E=E_{\phi}(\kappa_{0})+E_{A}(\kappa_{0})
, and

\displaystyle F(q)=\int d\mathbf{r}\,e^{i\mathbf{q}\cdot\mathbf{r}}\rho(\mathbf{r}).

(136)

Here
\rho(\mathbf{r})
is the nuclear density normalized as

\int d\mathbf{r}\,\rho(\mathbf{r})=1
.

V.1
Cross sections from the impulse term

By using the factorization approximation within the multiple scattering
formulation
Feshbach92
, the contribution from the impulse term
T^{\rm IMP}
for spin

J=0

\displaystyle\frac{d\sigma^{\rm IMP}}{d\Omega_{\rm Lab}}=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\pi)^{4}|\mathbf{k}|^{2}E_{V}(\mathbf{k})E_{A}(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k})}{\left|E_{A}(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k})|\mathbf{k}|+E_{V}(\mathbf{k})(|\mathbf{k}|-|\mathbf{q}|\cos\theta_{\rm Lab})\right|}

(137)

\displaystyle\mbox{}\times\left|AF_{T}(t)\bar{t}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})\right|^{2},

(138)

where
t=(q-k)^{2}
and
\bar{t}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})
is the spin-averaged
\gamma N\to VN

amplitude defined by

\displaystyle\left|\bar{t}(\mathbf{k},\mathbf{q})\right|^{2}=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{m_{s},\lambda_{\gamma}}\sum_{m_{s}^{\prime},\lambda_{V}}\left|\braket{k\lambda_{V};p_{f}m_{s}^{\prime}|T_{VN,\gamma N}|q\lambda_{\gamma};p_{i}m_{s}}\right|^{2}.

(139)

Here,
\braket{k\lambda_{V};p_{f}m_{s}^{\prime}|T_{VN,\gamma N}|q\lambda_{\gamma};p_{i}m_{s}}

is the matrix element of the
\gamma(q)+N(p_{i})\rightarrow V(k)+N(p_{f})
process as given in
the previous section.
The initial nucleon momentum
p_{i}
in the initial target state is usually chosen as
p_{i}=(M_{N},\bm{0})

by the frozen nucleon approximation and the final nucleon momentum
p_{f}=(p^{0}_{f},\mathbf{p}_{f})
is
set as

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{f}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{p}_{i}+(\mathbf{q}-\mathbf{k}),

(140)

\displaystyle p^{0}_{f}

\displaystyle=E_{N}(\mathbf{p}_{f})=\sqrt{\mathbf{p}_{f}^{2}+M^{2}_{N}}.

(141)

We follow the standard Hamiltonian formulation within which the
\gamma+N\to V+N

process in nuclei can be off-energy shell, i.e.,

q^{0}+E_{N}(\mathbf{p}_{i})\neq E_{V}(\mathbf{k})+E_{N}(\mathbf{p}_{f})
.

The factor
F_{T}(t)
in Eq. (
138

Within the Pomeron-exchange model of Donnachie and Lanshoff

;

\displaystyle F_{T}(t)=\braket{\Psi_{T}|\sum_{i=1,A}e^{i\bm{\kappa}\cdot\bm{r}_{i}}|\Psi_{T}},

(142)

where
\ket{\Psi_{T}}
, normalized as
\braket{\Psi_{T}|\Psi_{T}}=1
, is the nuclear
ground state in the nuclear c.m. frame, and
\bm{\kappa}
is related to

by

\displaystyle-t=\bm{\kappa}^{2}-\omega^{2},

(143)

with

\displaystyle\omega=\sqrt{\bm{\kappa}^{\,\,2}+M^{2}_{T}}-M_{T}.

(144)

Here

is the mass of the target nucleus
T
.
Clearly,
F_{T}(t)
is related to the nuclear charge form factor
F_{c}(q^{2})

(with no exchange current contribution) by

\displaystyle F_{c}(q^{2})=F_{N}(q^{2})F_{T}(q^{2}=t),

(145)

where
F_{N}(q^{2})
is the nucleon charge form factor.

VII
Summary and Conclusion

In this work, we have investigated
\phi
photoproduction on the nucleon targets and the
\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}
targets
based on the approach developed by the ANL-Osaka collaboration group
KLNS19
.
The starting point is to construct a Hamiltonian which describes the mechanisms of
\phi
photoproduction
by the Pomeron-exchange, meson-exchanges,
\phi
radiations, and
N^{*}
excitation processes.
The final
\phi N
interactions are described by the gluon-exchange and the direct
\phi N
coupling
as well as the box-diagram arising from the
\pi N
,
\rho N
,
K\Lambda
, and
K\Sigma
channels.
The parameters of the Hamiltonian are determined by fitting the data of
\gamma p\rightarrow\phi p

CLAS14
.
The resulting Hamiltonian is then used to predict the coherent
\phi
production on the
\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}
targets by using
the distorted-wave impulse approximation.

For the proton target, we find that the Pomeron-exchange contributions are dominant in the very forward angles
and the meson-exchange mechanisms are crucial in obtaining a good fit to the experimental data in the
large scattering angles, where the
N^{*}
excitations and
\phi
radiation mechanisms are also required to
describe the data at the considered energy region.
The final
\phi N
rescattering effects, as required by the unitarity condition, are found to be very weak and the model
without the FSI effects is enough to reasonably describe the data.

For the
\mathchoice{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-12.40839pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 9.84727pt}}_{{\kern-9.95839pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 9.84727pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-8.10278pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 6.24167pt}}_{{\kern-6.35278pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 6.24167pt}}}{\hphantom{{}^{{{\mathrm{4}}}}_{{{\mathrm{}}}}}\mkern 1.0mu{\mathit{\mathrm{He}}{}}^{{\kern-6.43056pt{\mathrm{4}}\kern 4.56944pt}}_{{\kern-4.68056pt{\mathrm{}}\kern 4.56944pt}}}
target, the calculated differential cross sections are in a very good agreement with the LEPS data
LEPS17

at low energies and for forward scattering angles.
The FSI effects are also found to be negligible because of the further suppression originated from the nuclear form factor.
The bump structures predicted by the present work could be tested by future experiments at a wider range of scattering angles.
However, the structure observed by the LEPS collaboration on
d\sigma/dt
for far forward region
at
E_{\gamma}\sim 2.2\mbox{--}2.4
GeV could not be explained by the present exploratory model and deserves further studies both in experiment and in
theory.

On the other hand, in the considered energy region, it would be interesting to investigate the role of other higher energy meson-baryon channels,
such as
\pi\Delta
,
\rho\Delta
, and
K\Lambda^{*}
, by extending the present work.
In addition, a full scale coupled-channels calculation as what was done by the ANL-Osaka Collaboration could be carried out
to go beyond the box-diagram approximations.
Our efforts in these directions will be reported elsewhere.

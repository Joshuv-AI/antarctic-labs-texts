On the Helgason-Johnson bound
Chao-Ping Dong

Abstract
. Let G G be a simple non-compact linear Lie group. Let π \pi be any irreducible unitary representation of G G with infinitesimal character Λ \Lambda whose continuous part is ν \nu . The beautiful Helgason-Jonson bound in 1969 says that the norm of ν \nu is upper bounded by the norm of ρ ⁡ ( G ) \rho(G) , which stands for the half sum of the positive roots of G G . The current paper aims to give a framework to sharpen the Helgason-Johnson bound when π \pi is infinite-dimensional. We have explicit results for exceptional Lie groups. Ingredients of the proof include Parathasarathy’s Dirac operator inequality, Vogan pencil, and the unitarily small convex hull introduced by Salamanca-Riba and Vogan.

(1)

\Lambda=(\lambda_{a}(\mu),\nu)\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}=\mathfrak{t}^{*}+\mathfrak{a}^{*}.

Now let us state the following beautiful result.

The Helgason-Johnson bound.
Let
\pi
be any irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module whose infinitesimal character is given by (

\|\nu\|\leq\|\rho(G)\|
.

Helgason and Johnson obtained the above result for

However, since a typical irreducible unitary representation of

Can we improve the Helgason-Johnson bound for infinite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations?

After giving a general framework in Section

-module

. Let

Let us also introduce

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. We always fix a Vogan diagram for
\mathfrak{g}_{0}
as
[
16
, Appendix C]
. By doing this we have actually fixed a positive root system
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{f})
. Restricting it to
\mathfrak{t}_{f}^{*}
, we get
(\Delta^{+})^{(0)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
which contains a
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. We fix this
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
once for all. Let
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{l}
be the simple roots of
(\Delta^{+})^{(0)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
, and let
\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{l}
be the corresponding fundamental weights.
There are
s
ways of choosing positive root systems for
\Delta(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
containing
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
, where
s=|W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})|/|W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})|
.
We enumerate them as

(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})=\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})\cup(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}_{f}),\quad 0\leq j\leq s-1.

Let us denote the half sum of roots of
(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
by
\rho^{(j)}
, and put
\rho_{n}^{(j)}:=\rho^{(j)}-\rho_{c}
. Then
\rho_{n}^{(j)}
is the half sum of roots in
(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Let
w^{(j)}
be the unique element in
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}_{f})
such that
w^{(j)}\rho^{(0)}=\rho^{(j)}
, and collect them as the set
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})^{1}
. Then
w^{(0)}=e
, and by a result of Kostant
[
17
]
, the multiplication map induces a bijection from
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})^{1}\times W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
to
W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
.
For any
0\leq j\leq s-1
,
w^{(j)}\alpha_{1},\dots,w^{(j)}\alpha_{l}
are the simple roots of
(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
, and
w^{(j)}\zeta_{1},\dots,w^{(j)}\zeta_{l}
are the corresponding fundamental weights.

Choose an irreducible module
{\rm Spin}_{G}
of the Clifford algebra
C(\mathfrak{p})
. Then as
\mathfrak{k}
-modules, we have the following decomposition

(4)

{\rm Spin}_{G}=2^{[\frac{l_{0}}{2}]}\bigoplus_{j=0}^{s-1}E_{\rho_{n}^{(j)}},

where
l_{0}=\mathop{\hbox{dim}}\nolimits_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{a}_{f}
. See Lemma 9.3.2 of
[
27
]
.

Let us denote the dominant Weyl chamber for
(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
(resp.,
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
) by
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
(resp.,
\mathcal{C}
). Then

(5)

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup_{j=0}^{s-1}\mathcal{C}^{(j)}.

Note that
w^{(j)}\mathcal{C}^{(0)}=\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
for
0\leq j\leq s-1
. The convex hull formed by the
W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
orbits of the points
\rho_{n}^{(j)}
,
0\leq j\leq s-1
, is called the
unitarily small convex hull
, and a
K
-type is said to be
unitariy small
(
u-small
for short henceforth) if its highest weight lies in this polyhedron. See Salamanca-Riba and Vogan
[
21
]
. Otherwise, we will call this
K
-type
u-large
.

2.1.
Lambda norm and spin norm

The following geometric way of describing the lambda norm in
[
23
]
is due to Carmona
[
5
]
. Consider an arbitrary
K
-type
E_{\mu}
. Choose
0\leq j\leq s-1
such that
\mu+2\rho_{c}
is dominant for
(\Delta^{+})^{(j)}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
.
Put

(6)

\lambda_{a}(\mu):=P(\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)}).

Here for any vector
\eta\in i\mathfrak{t}_{f,0}^{*}
,
P(\eta)
stands for the projection of
\eta
to the cone
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
. Namely,
P(\eta)
is the unique point in
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
which is closest to
\eta
. By Proposition 1.1(c) of
[
21
]
, the two vectors
\eta-P(\eta)
and
P(\eta)
are orthogonal to each other. Therefore,

(7)

\|\eta\|^{2}-\|P(\eta)\|^{2}=\|\eta-P(\eta)\|^{2}.

The lambda norm of
\mu
is defined as

(8)

\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}:=\|\lambda_{a}(\mu)\|.

It turns out that
\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}
is independent of the choice of an allowable
j
and is thus well-defined.

On the other hand, the spin norm introduced in
[
8
]
is

(9)

\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}:=\min_{0\leq j\leq s-1}\|\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}+\rho_{c}\|.

Here
\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}
is the unique dominant weight in the
W(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
orbit of
\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}
. We emphasize that
\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}
,
0\leq j\leq s-1
, are precisely all the PRV components
[
20
]
of the tensor product
E_{\mu}\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
as
\mathfrak{k}
-modules. It has been shown in
[
8
]
that

(10)

\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}\geq\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}.

2.2.
Pathasarathy’s Dirac operator inequality and a reformulation

Let
Z_{1},\dots,Z_{m}
be an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{p}_{0}
under the Killing form. Then

D:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}Z_{i}\otimes Z_{i}\in U(\mathfrak{g})\otimes C(\mathfrak{p})

is the Dirac operator introduced by Parthasarathy
[
18
]
. For any irreducible
(\mathfrak{g},K)
-module
X
with infinitesimal character (
1
), the Dirac operator
D
acts on
X\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
. When
X
is unitary, then
D^{2}\geq 0
on
X\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
. Writing out
D^{2}
carefully, one will get Parthasarathy’s Dirac operator inequality
[
19
]

(11)

\|\gamma+\rho_{c}\|\geq\|\Lambda\|,

where
\gamma
is the highest weight of any
\widetilde{K}
-type occurring in
X\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
. Here
\widetilde{K}
is the spin covering group of
K
. This inequality is very effective in non-unitarity test: once a
\widetilde{K}
-type
\gamma
in
X\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
has been detected such that (
11
) fails, then
\pi
must be non-unitary. Finding such a
\gamma
maybe skillful, yet the paper
[
20
]
guarantees that it suffices to consider the PRV components of
X\otimes{\rm Spin}_{G}
. Keeping this in mind, one sees that the inequality (
11
) is equivalent to

(12)

\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}\geq\|\Lambda\|,

where
\mu
is the highest weight of any
K
-type of
\pi
.

2.3.
Non-decreasable
K
-types should be u-small

For any
0\leq j\leq s-1
, collect all the
K
-types
\mu
such that

\mu+2\rho_{c}\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
as
\Omega(j)
. We call a
K
-type
\mu\in\Omega(j)

decreasable
if
\mu-w^{(j)}\zeta_{p}\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
for certain
1\leq p\leq l
. Otherwise,
\mu
is called
non-decreasable
.

Conjecture 2.1
.

Assume that the
K
-type
\mu\in\Omega(j)
is non-decreasable. Then
\mu
should be u-small.

We will give explicit examples of non-decreasable
K
-types in Sections
5
and
6
.
The above conjecture has been verified for all exceptional groups that we shall address.

3.
Sharpening the Helgason-Johnson bound

Learning from Vogan’s lectures
[
25
,
26
]
, this section aims to give a way to improve the Helgason-Johnson bound for infinite-dimensional unitary representations.
Indeed, let
\pi
be such a representation with a lowest
K
-type
E_{\mu}
, and let its infinitesimal character be given as in (
1
). Now by (
12
),

\|\Lambda\|^{2}=\|\lambda_{a}(\mu)\|^{2}+\|\nu\|^{2}\leq\|\mu+m\beta\|^{2}_{\rm spin},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Therefore,

\|\nu\|^{2}\leq\|\mu+m\beta\|^{2}_{\rm spin}-\|\lambda_{a}(\mu)\|^{2},\quad\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

In other words,

(13)

\|\nu\|^{2}\leq{\rm MP}(\mu)-\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}^{2}.

To get a uniform bound of
\|\nu\|^{2}
for all the infinite-dimensional irreducible unitary representations, it suffices to obtain the
maximum
of the RHS of (
13
) over all the
K
-types
\mu
.

Now let us explain the idea of handling the RHS of (
13
). Theorem 1.1 of
[
9
]
and Theorem C of
[
10
]
suggest that when
\mu
is u-large, we should have
{\rm MP(\mu)}=\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}
. Thus we should firstly understand the behavior of
\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}-\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}^{2}
.

Take any
\mu\in\Omega(j)
. We have that

\displaystyle\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}-\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}-\|P(\mu+2\rho_{c}-\rho^{(j)})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}+\rho_{c}\|^{2}-\|P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\|

\displaystyle\leq{\rm I}(\mu)+{\rm II}(\mu),

where

(14)

{\rm I}(\mu)=\|\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}+\rho_{c}\|^{2}-\|\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}+\rho_{c}\|^{2}=2\langle\rho_{c},\{\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\}-(\mu-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\rangle

and

(15)

{\rm II}(\mu)=\|\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}\|^{2}-\|P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\|^{2}.

Here recall that
P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})
is the projection of
\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}
to the cone
\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
. By (
7
),

(16)

{\rm II}(\mu)=\|\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}-P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\|^{2}.

It follows that both
{\rm I}(\mu)
and
{\rm II}(\mu)
are non-negative.

Take any vector
\delta\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
, Lemma
3.3
will guarantee that
{\rm I}(\mu+\delta)\leq{\rm I}(\mu)
since
\delta
must be dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. On the other hand,

\displaystyle{\rm II}(\mu+\delta)

\displaystyle=\|\mu+\delta+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}-P(\mu+\delta+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\mu+\delta+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}-(\delta+P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)}-P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\|^{2}

\displaystyle={\rm II}(\mu),

where the second step holds since
\delta+P(\mu+\rho_{c}-\rho_{n}^{(j)})\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
.

Now we can state the following way of sharpening the Helgason-Johnson bound:

(a) For any
0\leq j\leq s-1
, select finitely many u-small
K
-types
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m(j)}
in
\Omega(j)
so that for any u-large
\mu\in\Omega(j)
, there exists
1\leq i\leq m(j)
such that
\mu-\mu_{i}\in\mathcal{C}^{(j)}
.
Then as deduced above,

{\rm I}(\mu)+{\rm II}(\mu)\leq{\rm I}(\mu_{i})+{\rm II}(\mu_{i}).

Let

(17)

A_{j}:=\max\{{\rm I}(\mu_{i})+{\rm II}(\mu_{i})\mid 1\leq i\leq m(j)\}.

Therefore, for any u-large
\mu\in\Omega(j)
, we have that
\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}-\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}^{2}\leq A_{j}
.

(b) We directly compute

(18)

B=\max_{\mu}\left\{\min_{m}\|\mu+m\beta\|^{2}_{\rm spin}-\|\mu\|^{2}_{\rm lambda}\mid m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\mbox{ such that }\mu+m\beta\mbox{ is u-small}\right\},

where
\mu
runs over all the u-small
K
-types in
\Omega(j)
other than those
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{m(j)}
in step (a).

To sum up,
\|\nu\|^{2}
is upper bounded by
\max\{A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{s-1},B\}
.

3.1.
Technical lemmas for
{\rm I}(\mu)

This subsection aims to prepare some technical lemmas for analyzing the term
{\rm I}(\mu)
defined in (
14
). Let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{l}
be the simple roots of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
, and let
\varpi_{1},\dots,\varpi_{l}
be the corresponding fundamental weights.

Let
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\eta_{i}\varpi_{i}
be
an arbitrary weight. We utilize the following
negative index
algorithm
to conjugate
\eta
to
\mathcal{C}
: if the weight is not yet
dominant, select an index
i
such that
\eta_{i}<0
, then apply the simple reflection
s_{\gamma_{i}}
. We record the corresponding sequence of
simple reflections, and collect all of them as
S(\lambda)
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(Theorem 4.3.1(iv) of Björner and Brenti
[
2
]
, Proposition 4.1 of Eriksson
[
13
]
)

Let
\eta
be any weight. Let
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}},\dots

be any sequence in
S(\eta)
, then the word
s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}
is reduced. Here each
\beta_{i}
is a simple root of
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. In particular, each sequence in
S(\eta)
is of finite length.

Lemma 3.2
.

(Theorem 4.5 of Eriksson
[
13
]
)

Assume that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two weights such that
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Then every sequence
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}}
in
S(\eta_{1})
can be extended to a sequence
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}},\dots,s_{\beta_{N}}
in
S(\eta_{2})
. Here
N\geq n
.

Proof.

Note that for any
1\leq k\leq n
, we have

\langle\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},s_{\beta_{1}}s_{\beta_{2}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k-1}}(\beta_{k})\rangle\geq 0

since
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
and
s_{\beta_{1}}s_{\beta_{2}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k-1}}(\beta_{k})\in\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Therefore,

\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{2}}s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}),\beta_{k}\rangle\geq 0.

Thus for any
1\leq k\leq n
,

\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{2}}s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2}),\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\leq\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{2}}s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{1}),\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle<0.

Now the negative index algorithm finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are two weights such that
\eta_{1}-\eta_{2}
is dominant for
\Delta^{+}(\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{t}_{f})
. Then
\langle\rho_{c},\{\eta_{1}\}-\eta_{1}\rangle\leq\langle\rho_{c},\{\eta_{2}\}-\eta_{2}\rangle
.

Proof.

Take a sequence
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}}
in
S(\eta_{1})
. By Lemma
3.2
, it extends to a sequence
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}},\dots,s_{\beta_{N}}
in
S(\eta_{2})
. Note that
s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}
and
s_{\beta_{N}}\cdots s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}
are reduced expressions due to Lemma
3.1
.

By Lemma 5.5 of
[
11
]
,

\displaystyle\{\eta_{1}\}-\eta_{1}

\displaystyle=s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{1})-\eta_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k}}(\eta_{1})-s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\eta_{1})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\eta_{1}-s_{\beta_{k}}(\eta_{1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle-\eta_{1},\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\beta_{k}).

Therefore,

(19)

\langle\rho_{c},\{\eta_{1}\}-\eta_{1}\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle-\eta_{1},\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\langle\rho_{c},s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\beta_{k})\rangle.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\{\eta_{2}\}-\eta_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k}}(\eta_{2})-s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\eta_{2})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=n+1}^{N}\left(s_{\beta_{k}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2})-s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2})\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle-\eta_{2},\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\beta_{k})-\sum_{k=n+1}^{N}\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2}),\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\beta_{k}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle\rho_{c},(\{\eta_{2}\}-\eta_{2})-(\{\eta_{1}\}-\eta_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\langle\eta_{1}-\eta_{2},\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\langle\rho_{c},s_{\beta_{n}}\cdots s_{\beta_{k+1}}(\beta_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=n+1}^{N}\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2}),\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle\langle\rho_{c},\beta_{k}\rangle.

Since the sequence
s_{\beta_{1}},\dots,s_{\beta_{n}},\dots,s_{\beta_{N}}
belongs to
S(\eta_{2})
, we have that

\langle s_{\beta_{k-1}}\cdots s_{\beta_{1}}(\eta_{2}),\beta_{k}^{\vee}\rangle<0,\quad\forall n+1\leq k\leq N

Now the desired conclusion follows.
∎

6.
Hermitian symmetric case

In this section, we consider the case that
(G,K)
is a Hermitian symmetric pair. Then the
K
-types of an infinite-dimensional
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module
\pi
may
not
be a union of Vogan pencils.
Therefore, if
\pi
is a
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module with a lowest
K
-type
E_{\mu}
and with infinitesimal character (
1
), we can only conclude that

(22)

\|\nu\|^{2}\leq\|\mu\|_{\rm spin}^{2}-\|\mu\|_{\rm lambda}^{2}.

Thus our job is to get the maximum of the RHS of (
22
) over all the
K
-types. Our main result of this section is summarized as follows.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
G
be the Lie group
E6_h
or
E7_h
in
atlas

[
1
,
29
]
. Let
\pi
be any irreducible unitary
(\mathfrak{g},K)
module whose infinitesimal character is given by (
1
). Then we have the following bounds for
\|\nu\|
:

\bullet

E6_h
:

\|\nu\|\leq\sqrt{73}
, while
\|\rho(G)\|=\sqrt{78}
.

\bullet

E7_h
:

\|\nu\|\leq\sqrt{\frac{371}{2}}
, while
\|\rho(G)\|=\sqrt{\frac{399}{2}}
.

In each case, the bound is attained at the trivial representation.

Let
\mathfrak{t}_{f}
be the fundamental Cartan subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
. We may and we will identify
\mathfrak{t}_{f}
with
\mathfrak{t}_{f}^{*}
via the Killing form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
. Let
\mathbb{R}\zeta
be the one-dimensional center of
\mathfrak{k}_{f}
, and let
\mathfrak{t}_{f}^{-}
be the orthogonal complement of
\mathbb{R}\zeta
in
\mathfrak{t}_{f}
under
B(\cdot,\cdot)
.

Let
|W(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{t}_{f})^{1}|=s
, and enumerate its elements as
w^{(j)}
for
0\leq j\leq s-1
. We adopt the notation
\Omega(j)
,
\Omega_{\rm us}(j)
,
\partial\Omega_{\rm us}(j)
,
A_{j}
etc as in Section
3
, with the discrepancy that instead of
\mathfrak{k}
-types, now we use
K
-types. Put

(23)

B=\max_{\mu}\left\{\|\mu\|^{2}_{\rm spin}-\|\mu\|^{2}_{\rm lambda}\right\},

where
\mu
runs over all the
K
-types in
\Omega_{\rm us}(j)\setminus\partial\Omega_{\rm us}(j)
. Then
\|\nu\|^{2}
is upper bounded by
\max\{A_{0},A_{1},\dots,A_{s-1},B\}
.

On well-posedness of Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic incompressible liquid crystal model Thanks: August 7, 2026
Ning Jiang, Yi-Long Luo

Abstract
. We study the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic incompressible liquid crystal model. Under some constraints on the Leslie coefficients which ensure the basic energy law is dissipative, we prove the local-in-time existence and uniqueness of the classical solution to the system with finite initial energy. Furthermore, with an additional assumption on the coefficients which provides a damping effect, and the smallness of the initial energy, the unique global classical solution can be established.



1.
Introduction

The hydrodynamic theory of incompressible liquid crystals was established by Ericksen
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
and Leslie
[
13
,
14
]
in the 1960’s (see also Section 5.1 of
[
20
]
). The so-called Ericksen-Leslie system consists of the following equations of
(\rho(x,t),\mathrm{u}(x,t),\mathrm{d}(x,t)
, where
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
n\geq 2
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\rho\dot{\mathrm{u}}=\rho\mathrm{F}+\mathrm{div}\hat{\sigma}\,,\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\dot{\omega}=\rho_{1}\mathrm{G}+\hat{\mathrm{g}}+\mathrm{div}\pi\,.\end{array}\right.

The system (
1
) represents the conservations laws of linear momentum and angular momentum respectively. Here,
\rho
is the constant fluid density,
\rho_{1}\geq 0
is the inertial constant,
\mathrm{u}=(\mathrm{u}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{u}_{n})^{\top}
is the flow velocity,
\mathrm{d}=(\mathrm{d}_{1},\cdots,\mathrm{d}_{n})^{\top}
is the direction field of the liquid molecules with the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. Furthermore,
\hat{\mathrm{g}}
is the intrinsic force associated with
\mathrm{d}
,
\pi
is the director stress,
\mathrm{F}
and
\mathrm{G}
are external body force and external director body force, respectively. The superposed dot denotes the material derivative
\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla
, and

\omega=\dot{\mathrm{d}}=\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}

represents the material derivative of
\mathrm{d}
.

The constitutive relations for
\hat{\sigma}
,
\pi
and
\hat{\mathrm{g}}
are given by:

(1.3)

\displaystyle\hat{\sigma}_{ij}

\displaystyle=-p\delta_{ij}+\sigma_{ij}-\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{k,i}}\mathrm{d}_{k,j}\,,

\displaystyle\pi_{ij}

\displaystyle=\beta_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{j,i}}\,,

\displaystyle\hat{\mathrm{g}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\gamma\mathrm{d}_{i}-\beta_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i,j}-\rho\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{i}}+\mathrm{g}_{i}\,.

Here
p
is the pressure, the vector
\beta=(\beta_{1}\,,\cdots\,,\beta_{n})^{\top}
and the scalar function
\gamma
are Lagrangian multipliers for the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, and
W
is the Oseen-Frank energy functional for the equilibrium configuration of a unit director field:

(1.4)

\displaystyle 2W=

\displaystyle k_{1}(\mathrm{div}\mathrm{d})^{2}+k_{2}|\mathrm{d}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+k_{3}|\mathrm{d}\times(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}

\displaystyle+(k_{2}+k_{4})\left[\mathrm{tr}(\nabla\mathrm{d})^{2}-(\mathrm{div}\mathrm{d})^{2}\right]\,,

where the coefficients
k_{1},\ k_{2},\ k_{3}
, and
k_{4}
are the measure of viscosity, depending on the material and the temperature.

The kinematic transport
\mathrm{g}
is given by:

(1.5)

\mathrm{g}_{i}=\lambda_{1}\mathrm{N}_{i}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{A}_{ij}

which represents the effect of the macroscopic flow field on the microscopic structure. The following notations

\displaystyle\mathrm{A}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla^{\top}\mathrm{u})\,,\quad\mathrm{B}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}-\nabla^{\top}\mathrm{u})\,,\quad\mathrm{N}=\omega+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}\,,

represent the rate of strain tensor, skew-symmetric part of the strain rate and the rigid rotation part of director changing rate by fluid vorticity, respectively. Here
\mathrm{A}_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}+\partial_{i}\mathrm{u}_{j})
,
\mathrm{B}_{ij}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}-\partial_{i}\mathrm{u}_{j})
,
(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})_{i}=\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}
. The material coefficients
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
reflects the molecular shape and the slippery part between the fluid and the particles. The first term of (
1.5
) represents the rigid rotation of the molecule, while the second term stands for the stretching of the molecule by the flow.

The stress tensor
\sigma
has the following form:

(1.6)

\displaystyle\sigma_{ji}=\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{N}_{i}+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{N}_{j}+\mu_{4}\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mu_{5}\mathrm{A}_{ik}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{A}_{jk}\mathrm{d}_{k}\,.

These coefficients
\mu_{i}(1\leq i\leq 6)
which may depend on material and temperature, are usually called Leslie coefficients, and are related to certain local correlations in the fluid. Usually, the following relations are frequently introduced in the literature.

(1.7)

\lambda_{1}=\mu_{2}-\mu_{3}\,,\quad\lambda_{2}=\mu_{5}-\mu_{6}\,,\quad\mu_{2}+\mu_{3}=\mu_{6}-\mu_{5}\,.

The first two relations are necessary conditions in order to satisfy the equation of motion identically, while the third relation is called
Parodi’s relation
, which is derived from Onsager reciprocal relations expressing the equality of certain relations between flows and forces in thermodynamic systems out of equilibrium. Under Parodi’s relation, we see that the dynamics of an incompressible nematic liquid crystal flow involve five independent Leslie coefficients in (
1.6
).

For simplicity, in this paper, we assume the external forces vanish, that is,
\mathrm{F}=0
,
\mathrm{G}=0
, and the fluid density
\rho=1
. Moreover, we take
k_{1}=k_{2}=k_{3}=1
,
k_{4}=0
in (
1.4
), then

2W=|\mathrm{d}\cdot(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+|\mathrm{d}\times(\nabla\times\mathrm{d})|^{2}+\mathrm{tr}(\nabla\mathrm{d})^{2}\,.

Since
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, this can be further simplified as
2W=|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}
, which implies

(1.8)

\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial\mathrm{d}_{i}}=0,\ \tfrac{\partial W}{\partial(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})}=\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i}\,.

Taking
\beta_{i}=0
, then
\pi_{ij}
reduces to

(1.9)

\partial_{j}\pi_{ji}=\partial_{j}\left(\tfrac{\partial W}{\partial(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})}\right)=\partial_{j}(\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{i})=\Delta\mathrm{d}_{i}\,.

Thus the second evolution equation of (
1
) is

(1.10)

\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+\gamma\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Since
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, it is derived from multiplying
\mathrm{d}
in (
1.10
) that

(1.11)

\gamma\equiv\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}})=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

The detailed derivation of (
1.11
) will be given later with deeper comments on its meanings and implications.

Combining the first equality of (
1.3
) and (
1.8
), one can obtain that

\mathrm{div}\hat{\sigma}=-\nabla p-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\sigma\,,

where
(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})_{ij}=\sum_{k}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\,.
On the other hand, it can yield that by (
1.6
)

\mathrm{div}\sigma=\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,

where

\displaystyle\tilde{\sigma}_{ji}\equiv\big(\tilde{\sigma}(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}})\big)_{ji}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}+\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k})

\displaystyle+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{j}+\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k})+\mu_{5}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj}\,.

Hence, Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal model reduces to the following form:

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+\gamma\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,,\end{array}\right.

on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}
with the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, where the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
is given by (
1.11
).

In this paper, our main concern is the Cauchy problem of (
1.12
) with the initial data:

(1.13)

\mathrm{u}|_{t=0}=\mathrm{u}^{in}(x),\ \dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{t=0}={\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}(x),\ \mathrm{d}|_{t=0}=\mathrm{d}^{in}(x)\,,

where
\mathrm{d}^{in}
and
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}
satisfy the constraint and compatibility condition:

(1.14)

|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1\,,\quad{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0\,.

Let us remark that a particularly important special case of Ericksen-Leslie’s model (
1.12
) is that the term
\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}
vanishes. Namely, the coefficients
\mu_{i}^{\prime}s
,
(1\leq i\leq 6,i\neq 4)
of
\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}
are chosen as 0, which immediately implies
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=0
. Consequently, the system (
1.12
) reduces to a model which is Navier-Stokes equations coupled with a wave map from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+(-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2})\mathrm{d}\,.\end{array}\right.

1.1.
\rho_{1}=0,\lambda_{1}=-1
, parabolic model

When the coefficients
\rho_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=-1
in the third equation of (
1.12
), the system reduces to the parabolic type equations, which are also called Ericksen-Leslie’s system in the literatures. The static analogue of the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system is the so-called Oseen-Frank model, whose mathematical study was initialed from Hardt-Kinderlehrer-Lin
[
8
]
. Since then there have been many works in this direction. In particular, the existence and regularity or partial regularity of the approximation (usually Ginzburg-Landau approximation as in
[
17
]
) dynamical Ericksen-Leslie’s system was started by the work of Lin and Liu in
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
and
[
19
]
.

For the simplest system preserving the basic energy law

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=\Delta\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}=\Delta\mathrm{d}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}\mathrm{d}\,,\quad|\mathrm{d}|=1\,,\end{array}\right.

which can be obtained by neglecting the Leslie stress and specifying some elastic constants. In 2-D case, global weak solutions with at most a finite number of singular times was proved by Lin-Lin-Wang
[
16
]
. The uniqueness of weak solutions was later on justified by Lin-Wang
[
21
]
and Xu-Zhang
[
30
]
. Recently, Lin and Wang proved global existence of weak solution for 3-D case in
[
22
]
.

For the more general parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, local well-posedness is proved by Wang-Zhang-Zhang in
[
28
]
, and in
[
10
]
regularity and existence of global solutions in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was established by Huang-Lin-Wang. The existence and uniqueness of weak solutions, also in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
was proved by Hong-Xin and Li-Titi-Xin in
[
9
]

[
15
]
respectively. Similar result was also obtained by Wang-Wang in
[
26
]
. For more complete review of the works for the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system, please see the reference listed above.

\rho_{1}>0
, hyperbolic model

If
\rho_{1}>0
, (
1.12

\mathrm{u}=0
, and the spacial dimension is
1
. For this case, the system (
1
) can be reduced to a so-called nonlinear variational wave equation. Zhang and Zheng (later on with Bressan and others) studied systematically the dissipative and energy conservative solutions in series work starting from late 90’s
[

For the multidimensional case, to our best acknowledgement, there was no mathematical work on the original hyperbolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system (
1.12
). Very recently, De Anna and Zarnescu
[
3
]
considered the inertial Qian-Sheng model of liquid crystals which couples a hyperbolic type equation involving a second order derivative with a forced incompressible Navier-Stokes equations. It is a system describing the hydrodynamics of nematic liquid crystals in the Q-tensor framework. They proved global well-posedness and twist-wave solutions. Furthermore, for the inviscid version of the Qian-Sheng model, in
[
7
]
, Feireisl-Rocca-Schimperna-Zarnescu proved a global existence of the
dissipative solution
which is inspired from that of incompressible Euler equation defined by P-L. Lions
[

.

It is well-known that the geometric constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
brings difficulties (particularly in higher order nonlinearities) on the Ericksen-Leslie’s system (
1.12
), even in the parabolic case
\rho_{1}=0
. In
[
28
]
, Wang-Zhang-Zhang treated the parabolic case to remove this constraint by introducing a new formulation of parabolic version of (
1.12
). The key feature of their formulation is projecting the equation of
\mathrm{d}
(the third equation of (
1.12
) with
\rho_{1}=0
) the space orthogonal to the direction
\mathrm{d}
. However, for the genuine hyperbolic system (
1.12
) with
\rho_{1}>0
, this technique seems not work. Indeed, projecting the third equation of the system (
1.12
) into the
orthogonal
direction of
\mathrm{d}
, we have:

(1.21)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}-\frac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\Delta\mathrm{u}+\nabla p=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d})+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d})+(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot(\Delta\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d})\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathrm{I}
denotes the
n\times n
identical matrix. If taking
\rho_{1}=0
and
\lambda_{1}=-1
, (
1.21
) is exactly what was employed in
[
28
]
. But for the case
\rho_{1}>0
, the third equation in (
1.21
) include only
(\mathrm{I}-\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d})\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}
, while the
parallel
part of the second derivative term, i.e.
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}
is not included so it will have trouble on the energy estimate. In
[
28
]
, this is not a problem since
\rho_{1}=0
, the leading order term
\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}
is automatically orthogonal to
\mathrm{d}
.

Our approach is projecting the third equation of (
1.12
) to the direction parallel to
\mathrm{d}
:

\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot\ddot{\mathrm{d}}=\gamma\mathrm{d}+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}\cdot(\Delta\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d})\,,

from which we can determine the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}
. Now, the key point is: with
\gamma
given in this form, if the initial data
\mathrm{d}^{in},\ {\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}
satisfy
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
and the compatibility condition
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
, then for the
solution
to (
1.12
), the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
will be
forced
to hold. Hence, these constraints need only be given on the initial data, while in the system (
1.12
), we do not need the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
explicitly any more. These facts will be stated and proved in Section 2.

We remark that spiritually this is similar to
[
24
]
in which they used an unconstrained formulation of the Navier-Stokes equations, namely, the incompressibility is not required in the equations, but only imposed on the initial data.

1.3.
Main results.

The fundamental role played is the
L^{2}
-estimate for (
1.12
), which we call the basic energy law. To ensure the basic energy law is dissipative, the Leslie coefficients should satisfy some constraints, see Section 2. Under these coefficients constraints, we prove the local well-posedness for the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
). With an additional condition
\lambda_{1}<0
which will provide extra damping, the global existence with small initial data can be established. More precisely, the main results are stated in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the integer
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, and let the the initial data satisfy
\mathrm{u}^{in},\ {\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ \nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
,
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
. The initial energy is defined as
E^{in}\equiv|\mathrm{u}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}_{H^{s}}
. If the Leslie coefficients satisfy the relations that either

(1.22)

\mu_{1}\geq 0\,,\mu_{4}>0\,,\lambda_{1}<0\,,\mu_{5}+\mu_{6}+\tfrac{\lambda_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\geq 0\,,

or

(1.23)

\mu_{1}\geq 0\,,\mu_{4}>0\,,\lambda_{1}=0\,,(1-\delta)\mu_{4}(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})\geq 2|\lambda_{2}|^{2}

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
, then the following statements hold:

(I). If the initial energy
E^{in}<\infty
, then there exists
T>0
, depending only on
E^{in}
and Leslie coefficients, such that the system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) admits a unique solution
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Moreover, the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
satisfies

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\int_{0}^{T}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\leq C_{0}\,,

where the positive constant
C_{0}
depends only on
E^{in}
, Leslie coefficients and
T
.

(II). If in addition,
\lambda_{1}<0
, henceforth the coefficients constraints (
1.22
) hold, then there is a constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on Leslie coefficients, such that if the initial data satisfy
E^{in}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, then the system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) has a unique global solution
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,\infty;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,\infty;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
. Moreover, the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{t\geq 0}\left(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\right)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{1}E^{in}\,,

where the constant
C_{1}>0
depends upon the Leslie coefficients and inertia constant
\rho_{1}
.

Remark 1.1
.

The simplified case
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=\mu_{5}=\mu_{6}=0
, which corresponds to the Hyperbolic Ericksen-Leslie model (
1
), satisfies the Leslie’s coefficients relations (
1.23
). So, the local well-posedness of the simplified system (
1
) holds.

We now sketch the main ideas of the proof of the above theorem. One of the key difficulties is that the geometric constraint
\mathrm{d}=1
is hard to be kept in the construction of the approximate system. In stead, this constraint should be put on the initial data, then it will be forced to be satisfied during the evolution if the solutions are smooth. This is the similar treatment in Ericksen-Leslie’s parabolic liquid crystal system. However, for the parabolic case, this property is kept simply by the maximal principle. For example, see
[
16
]
. Inspired by idea from the standard wave map, we consider
h(x,t)=|\mathrm{d}(x,t)|^{2}-1
which satisfies a nonlinear wave equation with a given velocity field
\mathrm{u}
. Then we can use a Gronwall type inequality to show if initially
h(0,x)=0
, then it will vanish provided that
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
are smooth and
\mathrm{d}
is bounded.

Our next step is that for a given smooth velocity field
\mathrm{u}(x,t)
, the equation of
\mathrm{d}(x,t)
can be solved locally in time. We use the mollifier method to construct the approximate solutions
without
assuming the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
. The key point is that we can derive the uniform energy estimate also without this constraint. Then as mentioned above, the condition
|\mathrm{d}|=1
will be automatically obeyed in the time interval that the solution is smooth.

Now we carefully design an iteration scheme to construct approximate solutions
(\mathrm{u}^{k},\mathrm{d}^{k})
: solve the Stokes type equation of
\mathrm{u}^{k+1}
in terms of
\mathrm{u}^{k}
and
\mathrm{d}^{k}
, and solve the wave map type equation of
\mathrm{d}^{k+1}
in terms of
\mathrm{u}^{k}
. Note that the key is that in this construction, the geometric constraint of
\mathrm{d}^{k}
will not be a trouble. So we can derive the uniform energy estimate by assuming
|\mathrm{d}|=1
which will significantly simply the process. We emphasize that in this step the basic energy law play a fundamental role: the main term of the higher order energy are kept in the form of the basic
L^{2}
-estimate. Thus the existence of the local-in-time smooth solutions can be proved.

To prove the global-in-time smooth solutions, the dissipation in the a priori estimate in the last step is not enough. We assume furthermore the coefficient
\lambda_{1}
is strictly negative which will give us an additional dissipation, based on a new a priori estimate by carefully designing the energy and energy dissipation. Then we can show the existence of the global smooth solutions with the small initial data.

The organization of this paper is as follows: in the next section, we prove the basic energy law, and give the conditions on the Leslie coefficients so that the energy is dissipative. In Section
3
, we provide an a priori estimate of the system (
1.12
). In Section
4
, we justify the relation between the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, which guarantees that the condition of unit length of
\mathrm{d}
needs only be put on the initial dat. In Section
5
, we show the local existence of
\mathrm{d}
for a given velocity field
\mathrm{u}
, which will be employed in constructing the iterating approximate system of (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) in Section
6
. In Section
6
, we construct the approximate equation of (
1.12
) by iteration. In Section
7
, we prove the local well-posedness of (
1.12
) with large initial data by obtaining uniform energy bounds of the iterating approximate system (
6.1
). In Section
8
, we globally extend the solution of (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) constructed in Section
7
under the small initial energy condition when the
additional
coefficients constraint
\lambda_{1}<0
is imposed.

2.
Basic energy law

In this section, we formally derive the basic energy law. Throughout this section, the smoothness of the solution
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
to (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) is assumed.

Proposition 2.1
.

If
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
is a smooth solution to the system (
1.12
) with the initial conditions (
1.13
), then the flow satisfies the following relation:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\mu_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-2\lambda_{2}\left\langle\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d},\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle+(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})|\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}=0\,.

Moreover, the basic energy law (
2.1
) is dissipated if the Leslie coefficients satisfy that either the constraints (
1.22
), i.e.

\mu_{1}\geq 0\,,\mu_{4}>0\,,\lambda_{1}<0\,,\mu_{5}+\mu_{6}+\tfrac{\lambda_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}}\geq 0\,,

or the constraints (
1.23
), i.e.

\mu_{1}\geq 0\,,\mu_{4}>0\,,\lambda_{1}=0\,,(1-\delta)\mu_{4}(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})\geq 2|\lambda_{2}|^{2}

for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Taking
L^{2}
-inner product with
\mathrm{u}
in the first equation of (
1.12
), we have

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\left\langle-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{u}\right\rangle+\left\langle\partial_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{ji},\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle\,.

Taking
L^{2}
-inner product with
\dot{\mathrm{d}}
in the third equation of (
1.12
), we obtain

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)-\lambda_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\left\langle\Delta\mathrm{d},\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k},\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{A}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k},\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}\right\rangle\,.

Here we make use of the fact
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}=0
. Noticing that
\left\langle-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d}),\mathrm{u}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta\mathrm{d},\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}\right\rangle=0
, we know

(2.2)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\lambda_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\left\langle\partial_{j}\tilde{\sigma}_{ji},\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d},\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\,.

Now we compute the terms in the righthand side of the above equality (
2.2
).

First, we notice that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{j}(\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j}),\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle=-\mu_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j},\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\mu_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{d}_{p}\mathrm{A}_{kp}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{j},\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle=-\mu_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,.

Here we make use of the relation
\mathrm{B}_{ij}=-\mathrm{B}_{ji}
.

Second, by using the coefficients relation (
1.7
), the skew symmetry of the tensor
\mathrm{B}
and the symmetry of the tensor
\mathrm{A}
, we derive

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{j}(\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}+¡¡\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k}),\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}+¡¡\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k},\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}+\mu_{3}(\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k}),\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k},\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle-\mu_{3}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{B}_{kj}\mathrm{d}_{k},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}|\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-(\lambda_{1}+2\mu_{3})\left\langle B_{ki}\mathrm{d}_{k},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\mathrm{d}_{j}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}|\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d},\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle\,.

Third, by similar calculation in the relation (
2.4
), we imply

(2.5)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{j}(\mu_{5}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj}),\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\mu_{5}\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj},\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle(\mu_{5}-\mu_{6})\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mu_{6}(\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj}),\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki},\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle-\mu_{6}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{kj},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}|\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{k}\mathrm{A}_{ki},\mathrm{d}_{j}\mathrm{B}_{ji}\right\rangle-2\mu_{6}|\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})|\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d},\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle\,.

Finally, we again make use of the same arguments in (
2.4
) or (
2.5
) and then we yield

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left\langle\partial_{j}(\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}),\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle=-\left\langle\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i},\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle(\mu_{2}-\mu_{3})\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mu_{3}(\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{j}),\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i},\mathrm{A}_{ij}+\mathrm{B}_{ij}\right\rangle-\mu_{3}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i}+\mathrm{d}_{i}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{j},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i},\mathrm{B}_{ji}\right\rangle-2\mu_{3}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{j}\dot{\mathrm{d}}_{i},\mathrm{A}_{ij}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}\left\langle\dot{\mathrm{d}},\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\dot{\mathrm{d}},\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle\,.

Therefore, by plugging the equalities (
2.3
), (
2.4
), (
2.5
), (
2.6
) into (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\mu_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-2\lambda_{2}\left\langle\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d},\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\right\rangle+(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6})|\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}=0\,,

and, consequently, we complete the proof of Proposition
2.1
.
∎

3.
A priori estimate

In this section, we derive
a priori
estimate. We assume
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
is a smooth solution to the system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
), and introduce the following energy functionals:

\displaystyle E(t)=

\displaystyle|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,,

and for the case of
\lambda_{1}<0
, i.e. (
1.22
)

\displaystyle D(t)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\mu_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{s}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\lambda_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{s}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}+(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{B})\mathrm{d}+\tfrac{\lambda_{2}}{\lambda_{1}}(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6}+\tfrac{\lambda_{2}^{2}}{\lambda_{1}})\sum_{k=0}^{s}|(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,.

If the case
\lambda_{1}=0
, i.e. (
1.23
) holds, the energy functional
D(t)
is defined as

\displaystyle D(t)=

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\delta\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\mu_{1}\sum_{k=0}^{s}|\mathrm{d}^{\top}(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+(\mu_{5}+\mu_{6}-\tfrac{2\lambda_{2}^{2}}{(1-\delta)\mu_{4}})\sum_{k=0}^{s}|(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{2}(1-\delta)\mu_{4}\sum_{k=0}^{s}\Big(|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}-\tfrac{2|\lambda_{2}|}{(1-\delta)\mu_{4}}|(\nabla^{k}\mathrm{A})\mathrm{d}|_{L^{2}}\Big)^{2}\,.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
s>\tfrac{n}{2}+1
. Assume
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
is a smooth solution to the system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
). Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on Leslie coefficients and inertia density constant
\rho_{1}
, such that

\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E(t)+D(t)\leq CE^{2}(t)+C\sum_{p=1}^{4}E^{\frac{p+1}{2}}(t)D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)\,.

Proof.

For all
0\leq k\leq s
, we act
\nabla^{k}
on the first equation of (
1.12
) and take
L^{2}
-inner product with
\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}
and then we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}),\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma},\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle\,.

Again via acting
\nabla^{k}
on the third equation of (
1.12
) and taking
L^{2}
-inner product with
\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}
, we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\big(\rho_{1}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\rho_{1}\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}\Delta\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d})\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\gamma\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\,.

Therefore, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}),\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\rho_{1}\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad I_{1}

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}\Delta\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d})\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\ \ I_{2}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\gamma\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ I_{3}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma},\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}+\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\ \qquad\ I_{4}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}\,.

Here we divide the righthand side terms of the above equality (
3.1
) into four layers. If
k=0
, the equality (
3.1
) implies the basic energy law (
2.1
) (in this case,
I_{1}=I_{2}=I_{3}=0
). If
1\leq k\leq s
, the layers
I_{1}
,
I_{2}
and
I_{3}
can be controlled by the free energy terms in left hand side of (
3.1
). For the layer
I_{4}
, the intermediate derivatives terms can be controlled by the free energy terms in left hand side of (
3.1
), and the endpoint derivatives terms reduce to the dissipated terms with the same form in basic energy law (
2.1
).

Now we estimate (
3.1
) term by term for
1\leq k\leq s
.

We take advantage of Hölder inequality, Sobolev embedding and the fact
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0
, then we have

(3.2)

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{u},\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}\right\rangle-\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}\nabla^{b+1}\dot{\mathrm{d}},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 2\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b+1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{\dot{H}^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,.

For the second layer
I_{2}
, the endpoint derivative terms will vanish with same reason of
I_{2}=0
in the case
k=0
. So we estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}\Delta\mathrm{d}_{p},\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
1\leq a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}\nabla^{b}\Delta\mathrm{d}_{p},\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}_{i}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
1\leq b\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{k}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{p},\nabla^{a}\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\nabla^{b}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}+\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}_{i}\nabla^{b}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{p}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla^{k}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{p},\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}\nabla^{k}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}\right\rangle

(3.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}|_{L^{2}}|\Delta\mathrm{d}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}+|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{2}}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
1\leq a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{b}\Delta\mathrm{d}_{p}|_{L^{2}}|\nabla^{a}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{u}_{i}|_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
1\leq b\leq k-1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{k}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{p}|_{L^{2}}\big(|\nabla^{a}\partial_{j}\mathrm{u}_{i}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b}\partial_{i}\mathrm{d}_{p}|_{L^{4}}+|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}_{i}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{b}\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\mathrm{d}_{p}|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Here we make use of Hölder inequality, Sobolev embedding and the fact
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}=0
.

For the term
I_{3}
, we have

I_{3}=\left\langle\nabla^{k}\gamma,\mathrm{d}\cdot\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\gamma\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d},\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\,.

Recalling the structure of Lagrangian (
1.11
), for convenience of estimation, the
\gamma
can be divided into three parts:

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}(-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2})\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle=-\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
c\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{1}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{d}|_{L^{4}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
1\leq c\leq k-1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{3}_{H^{s}}\,,

and similarly

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}(|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2})\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\left\langle\mathrm{div}(|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}\nabla^{k}\mathrm{d}),\nabla^{k-1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
1\leq c\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a+1}\mathrm{d}\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{d}\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}\,,

and by the same arguments in the above estimation

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}(-\lambda_{2}(\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d})\mathrm{d})\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\lesssim|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c+e=k\\
a,c,e\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}||\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{u}||\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}||\nabla^{e}\mathrm{d}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle+3|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
a,c\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}||\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{u}||\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|\right\rangle+6|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}||\nabla^{b+1}\mathrm{u}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim|\lambda_{2}|(|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{3}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{4}_{H^{s}})|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Thus, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\leq k-1\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\gamma\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\lesssim\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{3}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|_{\dot{H}^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}+|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{p=1}^{3}|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{p}_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Notice that
\left\langle\nabla^{k}\gamma,\mathrm{d}\cdot\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\right\rangle\lesssim|\nabla^{k}\gamma|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}\cdot\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{2}}
. Since
\mathrm{d}\cdot\dot{\mathrm{d}}=0
, we have

\mathrm{d}\cdot\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}=-\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=k\\
q\geq 1\end{subarray}}\nabla^{p}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\nabla^{q}\mathrm{d}\,.

Then

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}\cdot\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p+q=k\\

4.
Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1

In this section, we prove the following Lemma on the relation between the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
and the geometric constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d})
is a classical solution to the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic system (
1.12
)-(
1.13
) satisfying
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
, and
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
|\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
for some
T\in(0,\infty)
, where
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
.

If the constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
is required, then the Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma
is

(4.1)

\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Conversely, if we give the form of
\gamma
as (
4.1
) and
\mathrm{d}
satisfies the initial data conditions
{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0
,
|\mathrm{d}^{in}|=1
, then
|\mathrm{d}|=1\,.

Remark 4.1
.

In fact, if we assume that the liquid crystal flow with a given bulk velocity
\mathrm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T;W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
( i.e. the form of the third equation of (
1.12
) ) with the last two initial conditions of (
1.13
) has a solution
\mathrm{d}
satisfying
\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
|\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
for some
T\in(0,\infty)
, then the conclusions of Lemma
4.1
also hold.

Proof.

We multiply
\mathrm{d}
in the third equation of the system (
1.12
) and then we get

\displaystyle\gamma|\mathrm{d}|^{2}=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\ddot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\Delta\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}+\lambda_{1}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{div}\nabla\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot\mathrm{d})-\rho_{1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}\cdot(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)\mathrm{d}-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}\cdot\mathrm{d})+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)^{2}(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})-\Delta(\tfrac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}|^{2})

\displaystyle-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

If
|\mathrm{d}|=1
, the above equation reduces to

\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}\,.

Conversely, if we give
\gamma=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}
, from the above calculation and the initial conditions we have

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)^{2}(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)-\lambda_{1}(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)-\Delta(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)=2\gamma(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\,,\\
(\partial_{t}+\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla)(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\big|_{t=0}=2{\tilde{\mathrm{d}}}^{in}\cdot\mathrm{d}^{in}=0\,,\\
(|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1)\big|_{t=0}=|\mathrm{d}^{in}|^{2}-1=0\,.\end{array}\right.

Let
h=|\mathrm{d}|^{2}-1
. Then
h
solves the following Cauchy problem for a given smooth vector field
\mathrm{u}
:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}\ddot{h}-\lambda_{1}\dot{h}-\Delta h=2\gamma h\,,\\
\dot{h}|_{t=0}=0\,,\\
h|_{t=0}=0\,.\end{array}\right.

Our goal is to verify
h(t,x)=0
for all times
t
. Noticing that
|\mathrm{d}|_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty
and
\mathrm{u}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}\,,\nabla\mathrm{d}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
, we deduce that by Sobolve embedding

|\mathrm{u}|_{L^{1}(0,T,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}+|\gamma|_{L^{1}(0,T,L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}<\infty\,.

We denote by
Z(t,x)\equiv\lambda_{1}\dot{h}(t,x)+2\gamma(t,x)h(t,x)
. Multiplying by
\dot{h}
in the equation (
4.3
) and integrating by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}(\rho_{1}|\dot{h}|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}_{L^{2}})=

\displaystyle\left\langle Z,\dot{h}\right\rangle-\left\langle\nabla h,\nabla\mathrm{u}\nabla h\right\rangle\leq|Z|_{L^{2}}|\dot{h}|_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla h|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}|Z|_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla h|_{L^{2}}\Big)\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\dot{h}|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\,,

which implies that

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\dot{h}|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\leq\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}|Z|_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla h|_{L^{2}}\,.

Then by integrating on
[0,t]
we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\dot{h}(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h(t,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}\leq\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\dot{h}(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h(0,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}

\displaystyle+\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}\int_{0}^{t}|Z(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau+\int_{0}^{t}|\nabla\mathrm{u}(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla h(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

Let
G(t)\equiv\sqrt{\rho_{1}|\dot{h}(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}^{2}+|\nabla h(t,\cdot)|^{2}_{L^{2}}}+|h(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}
. One notices that

\displaystyle|Z(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+2|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)\Big(\sqrt{\rho_{1}}|\dot{h}(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+|h(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\Big)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\tfrac{|\lambda_{1}|}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+2|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)G(t)\,,

and by the relation
\partial_{t}h=\dot{h}-\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla h

\displaystyle|h(t,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle|h(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}|\partial_{t}h(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|h(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}(|\dot{h}(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+|\mathrm{u}(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\nabla h(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{2}})\mathrm{d}\tau

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|h(0,\cdot)|_{L^{2}}+\int_{0}^{t}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}+|\mathrm{u}(\tau,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\big)G(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\,.

According to the inequalities (
4.4
), (
4
) and (
4
), we observe that for all
0\leq t\leq T

G(t)\leq G(0)+\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)G(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\,,

where
R(t)=\frac{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}+|\lambda_{1}|}{\rho_{1}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt{\rho_{1}}}|\gamma(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}+|\mathrm{u}(t,\cdot)|_{L^{\infty}}\in L^{1}([0,T])
. Then it is derived from Gronwall inequality and the fact

that

0\leq G(t)\leq G(0)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}R(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\right)=0

holds for all
t\in[0,T]
. Consequently,
h(t,x)=0
holds for all times
t
and then the proof of Lemma
4.1
is finished.

∎

More precisely, the results of well-posedness of the system (

For
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
and
T_{0}>0
, let vector fields
(\mathrm{d}^{in},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in},\mathrm{u})\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}\in H^{s}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}\in H^{s}
and
\mathrm{u}\in L^{1}(0,T_{0};H^{s+1})
. Then there exists a number
0<T\leq T_{0}
, depending only on
\mathrm{d}^{in}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}
and
\mathrm{u}
, such that the system (
5.1
)-(
5.2
) has a unique classical solution
\mathrm{d}
satisfying
\nabla\mathrm{d}\,,\ \dot{\mathrm{d}}\in C(0,T;H^{s})
. Moreover, there is a positive constant
C_{2}
, depending only on
\mathrm{d}^{in}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}
and
\mathrm{u}
, such that the solution
\mathrm{d}
satisfies the following bound

\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(0,T;H^{s})}\leq C_{2}\,.

Proof.

We justify this proposition by dividing into three steps. We first construct an approximate system of the system (
5.1
)-(
5.2
) by standard mollifier methods. Second, we derive a uniform energy bounds. In the end, we take limit in the constructed approximate system by using the results of compactness.

Step 1. Construct the approximate system.
We first define the mollifier operator
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
as

\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathbf{1}_{|\xi|\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}}\mathcal{F}(f)\big)\,,

where the operator
\mathcal{F}
is the standard Fourier transform and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
is the inverse Fourier transform. It is easy to see that the mollifier operator
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
has the property
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}^{2}=\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
. We construct the following approximate system of (
5.1
)-(
5.2
)

(5.3)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}\partial_{t}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}=-\rho_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})+\Delta\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\big(\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\big)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lambda_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\lambda_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{B}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,\\
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}=\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,\\
(\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})|_{t=0}=(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{0})\,.\end{array}\right.

By ODE theory, we can prove that there is a maximal time interval
T_{\epsilon}>0
, depending only on
\mathrm{d}_{0}
,
\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{0}
,
\mathrm{u}
and
T_{0}
, such that the approximate system (
5.3
) admits a unique solution
\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\in C([0,T_{\epsilon});H^{s+1})
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\in C([0,T_{\epsilon});H^{s})
. We figure out that
T_{\epsilon}\leq T_{0}
for all
\epsilon>0
, which is determined by the regularity of
\mathrm{u}
. From the fact
\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}^{2}=\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}
, we observe that
(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})
is also a solution to the approximate system (
5.3
). Thus the uniqueness immediately implies the relation

(5.4)

(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})=(\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\,.

Therefore, from the relation (
5.4
), the solution
(\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})
also solves the following system

(5.5)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{1}\partial_{t}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}=-\rho_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\nabla\cdot\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})+\Delta\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}+\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\big(\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\big)\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad+\lambda_{1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\lambda_{1}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,\\
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}=\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\,,\\
(\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})|_{t=0}=(\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0},\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{0})\,.\end{array}\right.

Step 2. Uniform energy estimate.
We employ the standard energy estimate arguments to derive the uniform energy bounds, where the fact (
5.4
), the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding theory are frequently used.

First, we calculate the
L^{2}
-estimate of the approximate system (
5.5
). Multiplying by
\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}
in the first equation of (
5.5
) and integrating by parts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(5.6)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)=-\left\langle\rho_{1}\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\lambda_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle\,.

It is derived from the Hölder inequality and integration by parts on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
that

\displaystyle-\left\langle\rho_{1}\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle\tfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u},\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}-|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\nabla\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)\,,

and

\displaystyle\left\langle\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle=

\displaystyle\left\langle(-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}{}\top\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+|\lambda_{2}||\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{3}_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}\,,

and

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle\lesssim(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}\,.

Then, by plugging the above three inequalities into the equality (
5.6
) we have the
L^{2}
-estimate

(5.7)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)\lesssim(|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\lambda_{1}|)\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+|\lambda_{2}||\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{3}_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}\,.

Second, we estimate the higher order energy bounds of the approximate system (
5.5
). For all
1\leq k\leq s
, we act the
k
-order derivative operator
\nabla^{k}
on the first equation of the system (
5.5
) and take
L^{2}
-inner product by multiplying
\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}
, then by integrating by parts we obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}

\displaystyle\Big(\rho_{1}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)=\lambda_{1}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}-\rho_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\left\langle\Delta\nabla^{k}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle\,.

Now we estimate the right hand side of the equality (
5.8
) term by term. It is implied by integrating by parts, the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding theory that

(5.9)

\displaystyle-\rho_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle=-\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 2\end{subarray}}\left\langle\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}\nabla^{b+1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle-\rho_{1}\left\langle\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle-\rho_{1}\left\langle\nabla\mathrm{u}\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a\geq 2\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b+1}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\rho_{1}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,,

where the first inequality is derived from the fact
\langle\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\rangle=\langle\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u},|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\rangle
, and similar calculation in (
5.9
) reduces to

(5.10)

\displaystyle\left\langle\Delta\nabla^{k}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\right\rangle=-\left\langle\nabla^{k+1}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle\lesssim|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,,

and

(5.11)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{B}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle+\lambda_{2}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)\Big(|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
b\geq 1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{a+1}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}\Big)|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\,.

It remains to estimate the term
\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle
, which can be divided into three parts. We can directly calculate the following term

\displaystyle-\rho_{1}\left\langle\nabla^{k}(|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{a+b=k}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle+\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
c\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle

(5.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle 2\rho_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b=k\\
a,b\geq 1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}+\rho_{1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c=k\\
1\leq c\leq k-1\end{subarray}}|\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{a}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{b}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{4}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{3}_{H^{s}}\,,

where the last two inequalities are implied by the Sobolev embedding theory and Hölder inequality. According to the same calculation in (
5
), we can estimate

(5.13)

\left\langle\nabla^{k}(|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle\lesssim\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Now we estimate the last term of
\left\langle\nabla^{k}(\gamma(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle
by the Hölder inequality and Sobolev embedding. More precisely, we calculate

(5.14)

\displaystyle-\lambda_{2}\left\langle\nabla^{k}((\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}{}^{\top}\mathrm{A}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}),\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{a+b+c+e=k}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{e+1}\mathrm{u}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}||\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{3}_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{u}|_{L^{2}}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}+3|\lambda_{2}||\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+e=k\\
a\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{e+1}\mathrm{u}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle+3|\lambda_{2}||\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+e=k\\
a,b\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{e+1}\mathrm{u}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle+|\lambda_{2}|\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}a+b+c+e=k\\
a,b,c\geq 1\end{subarray}}\left\langle|\nabla^{a}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{b}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{c}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|\,|\nabla^{e+1}\mathrm{u}|,|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\lambda_{2}||\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)^{3}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Therefore, by substituting the inequalities (
5.9
), (
5.10
), (
5.11
), (
5
), (
5.13
) and (
5.14
) into the equality (
5.8
), we know that the inequality

(5.15)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\rho_{1}|\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla^{k+1}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)\lesssim|\lambda_{1}||\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)

\displaystyle+(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}

\displaystyle+\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}+|\lambda_{2}||\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)^{3}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}

holds for all
1\leq k\leq s
. Combining the
L^{2}
-estimate (
5.7
) and higher order derivative estimate (
5.15
), we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\Big)\lesssim|\lambda_{1}||\nabla^{k}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)

\displaystyle+(|\lambda_{1}|+|\lambda_{2}|)|\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}+|\lambda_{2}||\nabla\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s}}\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)^{3}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\

\displaystyle+\big(|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\big)\big(\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{s}}\,.

Third, we notice that the norm
|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}
in the above
H^{s}
-energy estimate (
5.16
) is uncontrolled by now, so that we should try to control it. We observe that

(5.17)

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{L^{\infty}}+|\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{H^{2}}+1

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{L^{2}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{H^{1}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{H^{1}}+1\,,

where the second inequality is derived from the Sobolev embedding theory. Thanks to the relation
\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}=\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})
and the Sobolev embedding theory, we can calculate

(5.18)

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}}=

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}(\mathrm{u}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{L^{2}}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}+|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{2}}|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|_{L^{2}}|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|_{L^{2}}\,.

If we define the energy functional
E_{\epsilon}(t)
as

E_{\epsilon}(t)=\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\,,

then the inequalities (
5.16
), (
5.17
) and (
5.18
) imply that there is a positive constant
C_{1}
, depending only on
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
,
\rho_{1}
and
\mathrm{d}_{0}
, such that for all
\epsilon>0
the inequality

(5.19)

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq C_{1}(1+|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s+1}})[1+E_{\epsilon}(t)]^{2}

holds for all
t\in[0,T_{\epsilon})
.

In the end, we will evaluate the uniform bounds of the energy functional
E_{\epsilon}(t)
by using Gronwall arguments. Noticing that

E_{\epsilon}(0)=\rho_{1}|\tilde{\mathrm{d}}_{0}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}_{0}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\equiv E^{in}<\infty\,,

we define
T_{\epsilon}^{1}
as

T_{\epsilon}^{1}=\Big\{\tau\in[0,T_{\epsilon});\sup_{t\in[0,\tau]}E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq 2E^{in}\Big\}\geq 0\,.

It is immediately derived from the continuity of the energy functional
E_{\epsilon}(t)
that
T_{\epsilon}^{1}>0
. Then the inequality (
5.19
) implies that for all
t\in[0,T_{\epsilon}^{1}]

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq\Lambda(t)[1+E_{\epsilon}(t)]\,,

where the non-negative function
\Lambda(t)=C_{1}(1+2E^{in})(1+|\mathrm{u}|_{H^{s+1}})\in L^{1}(0,T_{0})
. So Gronwall inequality reduces to

E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq G(t)\equiv\bigg(E^{in}+\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau\bigg)\exp{\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda(\tau)\mathrm{d}\tau}

holds for all
\epsilon>0
, where
G(0)=E^{in}>0
. Since the function
G(t)
is continuous in
t
and is independent of
\epsilon>0
, there is a
T>0
independent of
\epsilon>0
such that
G(t)\leq 2E^{in}
for all
t\in[0,T]
. Hence
T_{\epsilon}^{1}\geq T>0
for all
\epsilon>0
. Therefore, for all
\epsilon>0
and
t\in[0,T]
, we have
E_{\epsilon}(t)\leq 2E^{in}
. Namely, we obtain the following uniform energy bound

(5.20)

\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\mathrm{d}^{\epsilon}-\mathcal{J}_{\epsilon}\mathrm{d}_{0}|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 2E^{in}

for all

Step 3. Pass to the limits.
By the bounds (

), we know that there is a

6.
The iterating approximate system

In this section, we construct the approximate system by iteration. More precisely, the iterating approximate system is constructed as follows: for all integer
k\geq 0

(6.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\partial_{t}\mathrm{u}^{k+1}+\mathrm{u}^{k}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{u}^{k}-\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta\mathrm{u}^{k+1}+\nabla p^{k+1}=-\mathrm{div}(\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k}\odot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k})+\mathrm{div}\tilde{\sigma}(\mathrm{u}^{k+1},\mathrm{d}^{k},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k})\,,\\
\mathrm{div}\mathrm{u}^{k+1}=0\,,\\
\rho_{1}\partial_{t}\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}+\rho_{1}\mathrm{u}^{k}\cdot\nabla\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}=\Delta\mathrm{d}^{k+1}+\gamma(\mathrm{u}^{k},\mathrm{d}^{k+1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1})\mathrm{d}^{k+1}\\
\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad+\lambda_{1}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}+\mathrm{B}^{k}\mathrm{d}^{k+1})+\lambda_{2}\mathrm{A}^{k}\mathrm{d}^{k+1}\,,\\
(\mathrm{u}^{k+1},\mathrm{d}^{k+1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1})\big|_{t=0}=(\mathrm{u}^{in}(x),\mathrm{d}^{in}(x),\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}(x))\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}=\partial_{t}\mathrm{d}^{k+1}+\mathrm{u}^{k}\cdot\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k+1}
is the iterating approximate material derivatives, and

\mathrm{A}^{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}^{k}+\nabla\mathrm{u}^{k}{}^{\top})\,,\mathrm{B}^{k}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\mathrm{u}^{k}-\nabla\mathrm{u}^{k}{}^{\top})\,,

the iterating approximate Lagrangian multiplier
\gamma(\mathrm{u}^{k},\mathrm{d}^{k+1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1})
is

\gamma(\mathrm{u}^{k},\mathrm{d}^{k+1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1})=-\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}|^{2}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k+1}|^{2}-\lambda_{2}\mathrm{d}^{k+1}{}^{\top}\mathrm{A}^{k}\mathrm{d}^{k+1}\,,

and

\displaystyle\big(\tilde{\sigma}(\mathrm{u}^{k+1},\mathrm{d}^{k},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k})\big)_{ji}=

\displaystyle\mu_{1}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{q}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{p}\mathrm{A}^{k+1}_{qp}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{i}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{j}+\mu_{2}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{j}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k}_{i}+\mathrm{B}^{k+1}_{qi}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{q})

\displaystyle+\mu_{3}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{i}(\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k}_{j}+\mathrm{B}^{k+1}_{qj}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{q})+\mu_{5}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{j}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{q}\mathrm{A}^{k+1}_{qi}+\mu_{6}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{i}\mathrm{d}^{k}_{q}\mathrm{A}^{k+1}_{qj}

is the
(j,i)
-entry of the iterating approximate extra stress tensor
\tilde{\sigma}(\mathrm{u}^{k+1},\mathrm{d}^{k},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k})
. The iteration starts from
k=0
, i.e.

(\mathrm{u}^{0}(t,x),\mathrm{d}^{0}(t,x),\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{0}(t,x))=(\mathrm{u}^{in}(x),\mathrm{d}^{in}(x),\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}(x))\,.

Now we state the existence conclusion of the iterating approximate system (
6.1
) as follows:

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose that
s>\frac{n}{2}+1
and the initial data
(\mathrm{u}^{in},\mathrm{d}^{in},\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
\mathrm{u}^{in}\,,\nabla\mathrm{d}^{in}\,,\tilde{\mathrm{d}}^{in}\in H^{s}
. Then there is a maximal number
T^{*}_{k+1}>0
such that the system (
6.1
) admits a unique solution
(\mathrm{u}^{k+1},\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1},\mathrm{d}^{k+1})
satisfying
\mathrm{u}^{k+1}\in C(0,T_{k+1}^{*};H^{s})\cap L^{2}(0,T_{k+1}^{*};H^{s+1})
, and
\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k+1}\,,\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}\in C(0,T_{k+1}^{*};H^{s})
.

Proof.

For the case
k+1
, the vectors
\mathrm{u}^{k}
,
\mathrm{d}^{k}
and
\dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k}
are the known. Namely, the velocity equation of
\mathrm{u}^{k+1}
is a linear Stokes type system, which admits the unique solution
\mathrm{u}^{k+1}\in C(0,\hat{T}_{k+1};H^{s})\cap L^{2}(0,\hat{T}_{k+1};H^{s+1})
on the maximal time interval
[0,\hat{T}_{k+1})
; and the orientation equation of
\mathrm{d}^{k+1}
is a Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal flow with a given bulk velocity
\mathrm{u}^{k}
, which, by Proposition
5.1
, has the unique solution
\mathrm{d}^{k+1}
satisfying
\nabla\mathrm{d}^{k+1}\,,\ \dot{\mathrm{d}}^{k+1}\in C(0,\tilde{T}_{k+1};H^{s})
on the maximal time interval

. We denote
T^{*}_{k+1}=\min\{\hat{T}_{k+1},\tilde{T}_{k+1}\}>0

6.1
is finished.

∎

We remark that
T^{*}_{k+1}\leq T^{*}_{k}

8.
Global existence with small initial data

Proof.

For all
1\leq k\leq s

Proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1
: Global existence with small initial data.
We observe that

C_{\#}\big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t)\leq C^{\#}\big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\big)\,,

and

\mathcal{D}_{\eta}(t)\geq\tfrac{1}{4}\mu_{4}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\,,

where the constants
C^{\#}=4+2\eta_{0}-\lambda_{1}\eta_{0}+2\rho_{1}\eta_{0}>0
and
C_{\#}=\min\{1,1-\eta_{0},1-\eta_{0}\rho_{1}\}>0
, since
\eta_{0}>0
is sufficiently small. As a consequence,

C_{\#}E^{in}\leq\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(0)\leq C^{\#}E^{in}\,.

We define the following number

T^{*}=\sup\bigg\{\tau>0;\sup\limits_{t\in[0,\tau]}C\sum\limits_{p=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{\frac{p}{2}}(t)\leq\tfrac{1}{2}\bigg\}\geq 0\,,

where the constant
C>0
is mentioned as in Lemma
8.1
. We choose the positive number
\epsilon_{0}\equiv\frac{1}{C^{\#}}\min\Big\{1,\frac{1}{256C^{2}}\Big\}>0
. If the initial energy
E^{in}\leq\epsilon_{0}
, we can deduce that

C\sum\limits_{p=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{\frac{p}{2}}(0)\leq\tfrac{1}{4}<\tfrac{1}{2}\,,

which derives that
T^{*}>0
from the continuity of the energy functional
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t)
. Thus for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]

\tfrac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t)+\bigg[1-C\sum\limits_{p=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{\frac{p}{2}}(t)\bigg]\mathcal{D}_{\eta}(t)\leq 0\,,

which immediately means that
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t)\leq\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(0)\leq C^{\#}E^{in}
holds for all
t\in[0,T^{*}]
, and consequently

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}]}\bigg\{C\sum\limits_{p=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{\frac{p}{2}}(t)\bigg\}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}\,.

So we claim that
T^{*}=+\infty
. Otherwise, if
T^{*}<+\infty
, the continuity of the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\eta}(t)
implies that there is a small positive
\epsilon>0
such that

\sup_{t\in[0,T^{*}+\epsilon]}\bigg\{C\sum\limits_{p=1}^{4}\mathcal{E}_{\eta}^{\frac{p}{2}}(t)\bigg\}\leq\tfrac{3}{8}<\tfrac{1}{2}\,,

which contradicts to the definition of
T^{*}
. Therefore we gain

\sup_{t\geq 0}\Big(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+\rho_{1}|\dot{\mathrm{d}}|^{2}_{H^{s}}+|\nabla\mathrm{d}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\Big)(t)+\tfrac{1}{2}\mu_{4}\int_{0}^{\infty}|\nabla\mathrm{u}|^{2}_{H^{s}}\mathrm{d}t\leq C_{3}E^{in}\,,

where
C_{3}>0
depends only on the Leslie coefficients and inertia density constant
\rho_{1}
, and as a consequence, the proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1
is finished.

9.
Conclusions

In this paper, we prove local well-posedness of the small solutions to the Ericksen-Leslie’s hyperbolic liquid crystal model (
1.12
) under the assumptions on the Leslie coefficients which ensure the dissipation of the basic energy law. Then with an additional assumption
\lambda_{1}<0
, we prove the global existence of the smooth solutions. In fact, this paper is only the starting point of the research of the general system (
1.12
) in high dimension. Many problems are left to be investigated in future. For examples:

(1)

The assumption
\lambda_{1}<0
excludes the most important special case (
1
), which is the coupled incompressible Navier-Stokes equations with the wave map to unit sphere. For that case,
\lambda_{1}=0
. We expect that the global smooth solutions with small initial data should exist.

(2)

Physically, the inertial constant
\rho_{1}
is small. Formally, letting
\rho_{1}=0
will gives the parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system which has been studied intensively in the past 30 years. Justification of the limit
\rho_{1}\rightarrow 0
from hyperbolic to parabolic Ericksen-Leslie’s system will be an interesting question. In a paper under preparation joint with S-J. Tang and A. Zarnescu
[
12
]
, we can justify this limit for
\mathrm{u}(x,t)\equiv 0
, i.e. from wave map type to the heat flow type equation in the context of smooth solutions local in time. The initial layer will arise in this limit.

(3)

The existence of the global weak solution of (
1.12
) is another interesting question. The geometric constraint
|\mathrm{d}|=1
will bring serious analytical difficulty.

Statement of main results

Let
(M,g)
be a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold. Its Grauert tube
M_{\tau}
is a Kähler manifold with boundary that is diffeomorphic to the co-ball bundle consisting of co-vectors of length at most
\tau
. The
Szegő projector

\Pi_{\tau}
associated to the boundary of a Grauert tube, that is, the orthogonal projection

\Pi_{\tau}\colon L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})

(1)

onto the space of CR holomorphic functions on the Grauert tube boundary that are square integrable.

Fix a positive, even Schwartz function
\chi
whose Fourier transform is compactly supported with
\hat{\chi}(0)=1
. We study the smoothed out spectral localizations

\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}:=\Pi_{\tau}\chi(\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}-\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}(t)e^{-it\lambda}\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\,dt

(2)

of the Toeplitz operator

\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}\colon H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau}),

(3)

where
D_{\sqrt{\rho}}=\frac{1}{i}\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
is a constant multiple of the Hamilton vector field of the Grauert tube function
\sqrt{\rho}
acting as a differential operator; see
subsection 2.1
for background on Grauert tubes. As explained in
subsection 1.1
, the operator (
2
) is an analogue of the Fourier components (
33
) of the Szegő kernel in the line bundle setting.

The scaling asymptotics is expressed in
Heisenberg coordinates
on
\partial M_{\tau}
in the sense of
[
13
, Section 14, 18]
. We give the precise definition of the coordinates here; additional geometric background is recalled in
section 3
.

Definition 1.1
.

By
Heisenberg coordinates
(relative to the orthonormal frame
Z_{1},\dotsc,Z_{m-1}
) at
T^{1,0}_{p}\partial M_{\tau}
, we mean holomorphic coordinates
z_{0},\dotsc,z_{m-1}
in a neighborhood of
p\in M_{\tau}
with the following properties.

(i)

If we set
\theta=\Rep z_{0}
and
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
, then
x_{1},\dotsc x_{m-1},y_{1},\dotsc,y_{m-1},\theta
form a coordinate system in a neighborhood of
p
in
\partial M_{\tau}
.

(ii)

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle Z_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+i\overline{z}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}O^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}+O^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},

(4)

\displaystyle T

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}O^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{k}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}O^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{k}}+O^{1}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}.

(5)

Here, the Heisenberg-type order
O^{k}
is defined inductively by

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=O^{1}\text{ if $f(\eta)=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\lvert x_{k}(\eta)\rvert+\lvert y_{k}(\eta)\rvert)+\lvert\theta(\eta)\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)$ as $\eta\to p$},

(6)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=O^{k}\text{ if $f=O(O^{1}\cdot O^{k-1})$}.

(7)

Theorem 1.2

(Scaling asymptotics for
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
)
.

Let
\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}
be the spectral projection (
2
) associated to the Grauert tube boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
of a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
m
. Fix
p\in\partial M_{\tau}
. Then, in Heisenberg coordinates centered at
p=0
, the distribution kernel has the following scaling asymptotics:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\theta}{\lambda},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\lambda}};\frac{\phi}{\lambda},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{m,M}}{\tau}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}e^{\frac{1}{\tau}\left(\frac{i}{2}\left(\theta-\phi\right)-\frac{|u|^{2}}{2}-\frac{|v|^{2}}{2}+v\cdot\overline{u}\right)}

(9)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{-\frac{j}{2}}P_{j}(p,u,v,\theta,\phi)+\lambda^{{}^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}}R_{N}\left(p,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda\right)\right),

(10)

where
\lVert R_{N}\left(p,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda\right)\rVert_{C^{j}\left(\{\lvert\theta\rvert+\lvert\phi\rvert+|u|+|v|\leq\rho\}\right)}\leq C_{K,j,\rho}
for
\rho>0,\ j=1,2,3,\dotsc
and
P_{j}
is a polynomial in
u,v,\theta,\phi
.

To give some context to our scaling asymptotics, recall the reduced Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}_{\mathrm{red}}=\mathbf{H}^{m-1}/\{(0,2\pi k):k\in\mathbb{Z}\}=S^{1}\times\mathbb{C}^{m-1}
of degree
m-1
, which is obtained as a discrete quotient of the Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}=\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{m-1}
. The group law on
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}_{\mathrm{red}}
is given by

\displaystyle(e^{it},\zeta)\cdot(e^{is},\eta)=\Big(e^{i(t+s+\Imp(\zeta\cdot\overline{\eta}))},\zeta+\eta\Big).

(11)

The level one Szegő projector
\Pi^{\mathbf{H}}_{1}\colon L^{2}(\mathbf{H}^{m-1}_{\mathrm{red}})\to H^{2}_{1}
is the orthogonal projection onto the space
H^{2}_{1}
of CR holomorphic functions square integrable with respect to
e^{-|z|^{2}}
. Its Schwartz kernel has an exact formula:

\displaystyle\Pi_{1}^{\mathbf{H}}(\theta,z,\varphi,w)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{m-1}}e^{i\left(\theta-\varphi\right)}e^{z\cdot\overline{w}-\frac{1}{2}|z|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|w|^{2}}.

(12)

We refer to
[
2
]
for more details. It is known (cf (
39
)) that, in correctly chosen coordinates, the Szegő kernel in the line bundle case is to leading order some multiple of (
12
); see for example
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
or
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
.
Theorem 1.2
shows that the same phenomenon holds in the Grauert tube setting:

\displaystyle\Pi_{\chi,\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\theta}{\lambda},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\lambda}};\frac{\phi}{\lambda},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)=\frac{C_{m,M}}{\tau}\left(\frac{\lambda}{\tau}\right)^{m-1}\Pi^{\mathbf{H}}_{1}\left(\frac{\theta}{2\tau},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\tau}},\frac{\phi}{2\tau},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)

(13)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{-\frac{j}{2}}P_{j}(p,u,v,\theta,\phi)+\lambda^{{}^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}}R_{N}\left(p,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda\right)\right)

(14)

Another natural operator to study is the spectral projection kernel defined using analytic continuation of Laplace eigenfunctions. Let

\Delta=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\bigg(\sqrt{\lvert g\rvert}g^{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{k}}\bigg)

(15)

be the (positive) metric Laplacian. It is well-known that
\Delta
has a discrete spectrum:

\displaystyle 0=\lambda_{0}^{2}<\lambda_{1}^{2}\leq\dots\lambda_{j}^{2}\leq\dots\to\infty

(16)

with associated
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunctions

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}=\lambda_{j}^{2}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}},\quad\|\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}\|_{L^{2}(M)}=1.

(17)

Our next result concerns the complex analogue of the partial spectral projection kernel

\displaystyle E_{\lambda}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(x)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(y)}.

(18)

The Poisson wave operator is used to analytically continue eigenfunctions. One starts with the half-wave operator
U(t)=e^{it\sqrt{\Delta}}\colon L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)
whose Schwartz kernel can be expressed as a sum of eigenfunctions as well as an oscillatory integral (for instance the Lax–Hörmander parametrix):

U(t,x,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{it\lambda_{j}}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(x)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(y)}=\int_{T^{*}_{y}M}A(t,x,y,\xi)e^{it\lvert\xi\rvert_{y}}e^{i\langle\xi,\exp_{y}^{-1}(x)\rangle}\,d\xi.

(19)

Analytically continuing the oscillatory integral representation in the time variable
t\mapsto t+i\tau\in\mathbb{C}
and the spatial variable
x\to z\in\partial M_{\tau}
yields the Poisson wave kernel (see
Theorem 2.4
):

U(i\tau)\colon L^{2}(M)\to\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau}).

(20)

Here,
\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau})=\ker(\bar{\partial}_{b})
denotes the space of CR holomorphic functions on the Grauert tube boundary. Comparing (
19
) with (
20
), we find

U(i\tau,z,y)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}e^{-\tau\lambda_{j}}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}(y)}.

(21)

Hence, the formula

\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}=e^{\tau\lambda_{j}}U(i\tau)\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}\in\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau})

(22)

defines the analytic extension of Laplace eigenfunctions (
17
).

We define tempered spectral projections
P_{\lambda}
by the eigenfunction partial sums

P_{\lambda}\colon\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau})\to\mathcal{O}(\partial M_{\tau}),\quad P_{\lambda}(z,w)=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}e^{-2\tau\lambda_{j}}\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}(w)}.

(23)

The prefactor
e^{-2\tau\lambda_{j}}
is introduced because of the exponential growth estimate (
[
35
, Corollary 3]
) on complexified eigenfunctions

\lambda_{j}^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}e^{\tau\lambda_{j}}\lesssim\sup_{\zeta\in M_{\tau}}\lvert\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\rvert\lesssim\lambda_{j}^{\frac{m-1}{2}}e^{\tau\lambda_{j}}.

(24)

As before, fix a positive, even Schwartz function
\chi
whose Fourier transform is compactly supported with
\hat{\chi}(0)=1
. The techniques for proving
Theorem 1.2
are easily adapted to prove scaling asymptotics for the smoothed sum

\displaystyle P_{\chi,\lambda}(z,w)=\chi\ast d_{\lambda}P_{\lambda}(z,w)=\sum_{j:\lambda_{j}\leq\lambda}\chi(\lambda-\lambda_{j})e^{-2\tau\lambda_{j}}\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(z)\overline{\varphi^{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda_{j}}(w)}.

(25)

Theorem 1.3

(Scaling asymptotics for
P^{\tau}_{\lambda}
)
.

Let
P_{\chi,\lambda}
be the spectral projection (
25
) associated to the Grauert tube boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
of a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold
M
of dimension
m
. Fix
p\in\partial M_{\tau}
. Then, in Heisenberg coordinates centered at
p=0
, the distribution kernel has the following scaling asymptotics:

\displaystyle P_{\chi,\lambda}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\theta}{\lambda},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\lambda}};\frac{\phi}{\lambda},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{m,M}}{\tau^{m}}\lambda^{\frac{m-1}{2}}\Pi^{\mathbf{H}}_{1}\left(\frac{\theta}{2\tau},\frac{u}{\sqrt{\tau}},\frac{\phi}{2\tau},\frac{v}{\sqrt{\tau}}\right)

(27)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1+\sum_{j=1}^{N}\lambda^{-\frac{j}{2}}P_{j}(p,u,v,\theta,\phi)+\lambda^{{}^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}}R_{N}\left(p,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda\right)\right)

(28)

where
\lVert R_{N}\left(p,\theta,u,\phi,v,\lambda\right)\rVert_{C^{j}\left(\{\lvert\theta\rvert+\lvert\phi\rvert+|u|+|v|\leq\rho\}\right)}\leq C_{K,j,\rho}
for
\rho>0,\ j=1,2,3,\dots
and
P_{j}
is a polynomial in
u,v,\theta,\phi
.

Remark 1.4
.

The leading order asymptotics of
Theorem 1.2
and
Theorem 1.3
differ by a factor of
\lambda^{-\frac{m-1}{2}}
because the analytic extensions
\varphi_{\lambda_{j}}^{\mathbb{C}}
are not
L^{2}
-normalized on
\partial M_{\tau}
.

Theorem 1.3
is the complex analogue of
[
6
, Proposition 10]
, where it was shown that the rescaled real spectral projection kernel
E_{\lambda}(x+\frac{u}{\lambda},y+\frac{v}{\lambda})
from (
18
) exhibits Bessel-type scaling asymptotics as
\lambda\to\infty
. The Heisenberg-scaling of the present paper is performed with parameter
\sqrt{\lambda}
rather than
\lambda
. The complex geometry of the Grauert tube win over the real Riemannian geometry since as
\tau\to 0
our scaling asymptotics do not resemble Bessel asymptotics. It should also be mentioned that, unlike the authors of
[
6
]
, we do not study the sharp (i.e., without the smoothing
\chi
) spectral projection in this paper.

1.1.
Comparison to the line bundle setting

Let
(L,h)\to(M,\omega)
be a positive Hermitian line bundle over a closed Kähler manifold. The
Bergman projections
are orthogonal projections

\Pi_{h^{k}}\colon L^{2}(M,L^{k})\to H^{0}(M,L^{k})

(29)

from the space of
L^{2}
sections of the
k
th tensor power of the line bundle onto the space of square integrable holomorphic sections.

The Bergman projections are related to the Szegő projection

\Pi_{h}\colon L^{2}(\partial D)\to H^{2}(\partial D),

(30)

where
\partial D=\{\ell\in L^{*}:\lVert\ell\rVert_{h^{*}}=1\}
is the unit co-circle bundle. Indeed, let
r_{\theta}
denote the circle action on
\partial D
, then the map

\displaystyle H^{0}(M,L^{k})

\displaystyle\to\{f\in H^{2}(\partial D):f(r_{\theta}x)=e^{ik\theta}f(x)\}

(31)

\displaystyle s_{k}(z)

\displaystyle\mapsto f(x)=(\ell^{\otimes k},s_{k}(z))\quad\text{where $x=(\ell,z)\in\pi^{-1}(z)\times M$}

(32)

defines a unitary equivalence between the space of holomorphic sections of
L^{k}
and the subspace of equivariant functions in
H^{2}(\partial D)
. Since the circle action on
\partial D
commutes with
\overline{\partial}_{b}
, we conclude that the kernel of (
29
) are Fourier coefficients of the kernel of (
30
), that is,

\Pi_{h^{k}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}\Pi_{h}(r_{\theta}x,y)\,d\theta=\left(\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{-ik\theta}r_{\theta}^{*}\Pi_{h}\,d\theta\right)(x,y).

(33)

Note that the Fourier decomposition of
\Pi_{h}
coincides with the spectral decomposition of
D_{\theta}=\frac{1}{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
on
\partial D
.

In the Grauert tube setting,
\partial M_{\tau}
plays the role of the strongly pseudoconvex CR hypersurface
\partial D\subseteq L^{*}
and the Hamilton vector field
\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
of the Grauert tube function on
\partial M_{\tau}
plays the role of the Reeb vector field
\partial/\partial\theta
on
\partial D
. With this analogy in mind, the direct analogue of (
33
) in the Grauert tube setting is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\Pi_{\tau}(G^{t}_{\tau}(z),w)\,dt=\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\,dt\bigg)(z,w),

(34)

in which

•

integration against (the Fourier transform of) a suitably chosen Schwartz function
\hat{\chi}
replaces integration on the circle in the line bundle setting;

•

pullback by the Hamilton flow (
51
) on the Grauert tube boundary replaces that of the circle action in the line bundle setting;

•

the orthogonal projection (
58
) onto the Grauert tube boundary replaces the Szegő projection in the line bundle setting.

It might appear that (
34
) is the correct object to study, but this is not quite right. Unlike the circle action on
\partial D
generated by
\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}
, the geodesic flow on a Grauert tube is never holomorphic, nor does its orbits form a fiber bundle over a quotient space. In particular,
G^{t}_{\tau}
need not commute with
\Pi_{\tau}
, so (
34
) fails to be CR holomorphic in the
z
variable. This issue of holomorphy can be fixed: It is shown in
[
32
, Proposition 5.3]
(see
subsection 2.5
for the statement) that there exists a polyhomogeneous pseudodifferential operator
\hat{\sigma}
on
\partial M_{\tau}
so that

\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}=\Pi_{\tau}\hat{\sigma}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\quad\text{modulo smoothing Toeplitz operators}.

(35)

Evidently, the kernel of (
35
) is CR holomorphic in both variables. Thus, we are led to the CR holomorphic analogue of (
33
) that is

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\Pi_{\tau}\hat{\sigma}(G^{t}_{\tau})^{*}\Pi_{\tau}\,dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\hat{\chi}(t)e^{-i\lambda t}\Pi_{\tau}e^{it\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}}\,dt\quad\text{to leading order}.

(36)

Remark 1.5
.

We note that (
36
) is essentially the spectral decomposition of the elliptic Toeplitz operator
\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}
introduced in (
3
). An interesting problem is to compare the spectrum of the Toeplitz operator with that of
\sqrt{\Delta}
, as

\displaystyle U({i\tau})\sqrt{\Delta}^{\frac{m+1}{2}}U({i\tau})^{*}=\Pi_{\tau}D_{\sqrt{\rho}}\Pi_{\tau}\quad\text{modulo zeroth order Toeplitz operator}

(37)

where
U(i\tau)
is the Poisson-wave operator discussed in
subsection 2.4
. We include further discussions in this direction in a subsequent paper
[
7
, Section 5]
.

1.2.
Related results in the line bundle setting

Scaling asymptotics in the line bundle setting have been proved in varying degrees of generality; see
[
2
,
31
,
26
,
27
,
25
,
17
]
. The simplest setup is a closed Kähler manifold
M
polarized by an ample line bundle
L
. Endow
L
with a Hermitian metric
h
so that the curvature two-form
\Omega
is positive, and let
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\Omega
be the Kähler form on
M
.

Choose coordinates
z=(z_{1},\dotsc,z_{m})
in a neighborhood
U
centered at
p\in M
so that the Kähler potential is locally of the form
\varphi(z)=\lvert z\rvert^{2}+O(\lvert z\rvert^{3})
. We identify the Bergman kernel with the Fourier components of the Szegő kernel using (
30
) and write

\Pi_{h^{k}}(x,y)=\Pi_{h^{k}}(z,\theta,w,\varphi),

(38)

where
x=(z,\theta)
are linear coordinates on
T_{z}M\times S^{1}
. Then the Bergman kernel scaling asymptotics is of the form

\displaystyle k^{-m}\Pi_{h^{k}}\bigg(\frac{z}{\sqrt{k}},\frac{\theta}{k},\frac{w}{\sqrt{k}},\frac{\varphi}{k}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi^{m}}e^{i(\theta-\varphi)+i\Im(z\cdot\bar{w})-\frac{1}{2}\lvert z-w\rvert^{2}}

(39)

\displaystyle\times\bigg[1+\sum_{n=1}^{N}k^{-\frac{n}{2}}b_{k}(z,w)+k^{-\frac{N+1}{2}}R_{N}(z,w)\bigg]

with

\lVert R_{N}\rVert_{C^{j}(\{\lvert z\rvert+\lvert w\rvert\leq\delta\})}\leq C(N,j,\delta).

(40)

The remainder estimate implies Gaussian decay of the Bergman kernel in a neighborhood of the diagonal:

\Big\lvert\Pi_{h^{k}}(z,\theta,w,\varphi)\Big\rvert\leq\frac{k^{m}}{\pi^{m}}e^{\frac{-kD(z,w)}{2}}\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\bigg)\bigg)\quad\text{whenever $d(z,w)\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log k}{k}}$}.

(41)

Here,
D(z,w)
is the Calabi diastasis (
66
), which is bounded above and below by the square
d^{2}(z,w)
of the Riemannian distance function.

A complete asymptotic expansion far away from the diagonal is available thanks to the recent papers
[
30
,
10
,
19
,
11
]
on
analytic Bergman kernel
. For instance,
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
shows (under analyticity assumption of the Kähler potential) that

\Big\lvert\Pi_{h^{k}}(z,\theta,w,\varphi)\Big\rvert=\frac{k^{m}}{\pi^{m}}e^{\frac{-kD(z,w)}{2}}\bigg(1+O\bigg(\frac{1}{k}\bigg)\bigg)\quad\text{whenever $d(z,w)\lesssim\frac{1}{k}$}.

(42)

In more general settings (including when the Kähler potential is no longer assumed to be analytic), global Agmon-type estimates

\Big\lvert\Pi_{h^{k}}(z,\theta,w,\varphi)\Big\rvert\leq Ce^{-c\sqrt{k}d(z,w)}\quad\text{for all $z,w\in M$}

(43)

have been established in
[
8
,
12
,
24
,
1
,
28
,
37
,
9
]
.

In the presence of a real Hamiltonian
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, there is a Hamiltonian flow
\Phi^{M}_{\tau}
and its lift
\Phi^{L}_{\tau}
to
L
in addition to the circle action. In
[
29
]
, the phase space scaling asymptotics is computed near a fixed point
m
and time
\tau
in the graph of
\Phi_{\tau}^{M}
for the dynamical Toeplitz operators

\displaystyle U_{\tau}=R_{\tau}\circ\Pi\circ(\Phi^{L}_{-\tau}\circ\Pi),

(44)

where
R_{\tau}
is a
0
th order Toeplitz operator. These asymptotics have an additional dependence on the derivative of
\Phi^{M}_{-\tau}
at
m
. The nature of the computation in our proof is similar to the one in
[
29
]
.

2.
Geometry of and analysis on Grauert tubes

We assume throughout that
(M,g)
is a closed, real analytic Riemannian manifold of dimension
m
. Grauert tubes were first studied by
[
15
,
16
,
22
,
23
]
in relation to the complex Monge–Ampère equation and complexified geodesics. The Poisson wave operator and the tempered spectral projection (
76
), as well as other related operators, have been studied in
[
34
,
35
,
32
]
.

2.1.
Grauert tube and the cotangent bundle

Bruhat–Whitney proved that a real analytic manifold
M
admits a complexification
M_{\mathbb{C}}
such that
M\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
is a totally real submanifold, that is,
T_{p}M\cap JT_{p}M=\{0\}
for all
p\in M
.

In a neighborhood of
M
in
M_{\mathbb{C}}
, there exists a unique strictly plurisubharmonic function
\rho\colon U\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}\to[0,\infty)
whose square root is the solution of the Monge–Ampère equation

\det\bigg(\frac{\partial^{2}\sqrt{\rho}}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}\bigg)=0\quad\text{on $U\setminus M$},

(45)

with the initial condition that the metric induced by the Kähler form
i\partial\overline{\partial}\rho
restricts to the Riemannian metric
g
on
M
. In fact,
\sqrt{\rho}
is given by

\sqrt{\rho}\colon U\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}\to\mathbb{R},\quad\sqrt{\rho}(z)=\frac{1}{2i}\sqrt{r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{z})},

(46)

where
r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{w})
is the analytic extension of the square of the Riemannian distance function
r\colon M\times M\to\mathbb{R}
to a neighborhood of the diagonal in
M_{\mathbb{C}}\times\overline{M}_{\mathbb{C}}
. We call (
46
) the
Grauert tube function
. For each
0<\tau<\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the sublevel set

M_{\tau}=\{z\in M_{\mathbb{C}}:\sqrt{\rho}(z)<\tau\}

(47)

is called the
Grauert tube of radius
\tau
.

The complexified exponential map can be used to identify (
47
) with the co-ball bundle of radius
\tau
:

B^{*}_{\tau}M=\{(x,\xi):\lvert\xi\rvert<\tau\}.

(48)

To explain this, we introduce notation for geodesic flows.

•

The (geometer’s) geodesic flow
g^{t}\colon T^{*}M\to T^{*}M
is the Hamilton flow of the metric norm squared function
H(x,\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^{2}_{x}
.

•

The homogeneous geodesic flow
G^{t}\colon T^{*}M-0\to T^{*}M-0
is the Hamilton flow of the metric norm function
\sqrt{H}(x,\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert_{x}
. Note that
G^{t}(x,\varepsilon\xi)=\varepsilon G^{t}(x,\xi)
.

Let
\pi\colon T^{*}M\to M
be the natural projection. The exponential map
\exp\colon T^{*}M\to M
is defined by
\exp_{x}\xi=\pi g^{1}(x,\xi)
. Analyticity of the metric allows us to complexify
t\mapsto t+i\tau
in the time variable. For
0<\tau<\tau_{\mathrm{max}}
, the imaginary time geodesic flow
E
is a diffeomorphism (
[
36
, Lemma 1.1]
)

E\colon B^{*}_{\tau}M\to M_{\tau},\quad E(x,\xi)=\exp_{x}^{\mathbb{C}}i\xi.

(49)

With
\sqrt{H}(x,\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert
and
\sqrt{\rho}
the Grauert tube function, we have

H=\rho\circ E\quad\text{and}\quad\sqrt{H}=\sqrt{\rho}\circ E.

(50)

It follows that
E^{-1}
conjugates the geodesic flow
G^{t}
(resp.
g^{t}
) on the cotangent bundle to the Hamilton flow of Hamiltonian vector field
\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
(resp.
\Xi_{\rho}
) on the Grauert tube. In particular, the transfer

G^{t}_{\tau}\colon\partial M_{\tau}\to\partial M_{\tau},\quad G^{t}_{\tau}=E\circ G^{t}\circ E^{-1}|_{\partial M_{\tau}}

(51)

of the geodesic flow from the energy surface
\partial B_{\tau}^{*}M
to the Grauert tube boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
coincides with the restriction of the Hamilton flow of
\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
to
\partial M_{\tau}
.

Finally, if we write
\alpha_{T^{*}M}=\xi\,dx
and
\omega_{T^{*}M}=d\xi\wedge dx
for the canonical 1-form and the symplectic form on the cotangent bundle, then we also have

(E^{-1})^{*}\alpha_{T^{*}M}=\Imp\partial\rho=d^{c}\sqrt{\rho}\quad\text{and}\quad(E^{-1})^{*}\omega_{T^{*}M}=-i\partial\bar{\partial}\rho.

(52)

Note that the right-hand sides of the two equalities are the canonical 1-form and the Kähler form on the Grauert tube
M_{\tau}
.

Remark 2.1
.

It is useful to think of the Grauert tube as the co-ball bundle endowed with an
adapted complex structure

J=J_{g}
induced from the Riemannian metric on
M
. This complex structure is characterized as follows. Let
\gamma\colon\mathbb{R}\to M
be a geodesic (i.e., a
g^{t}
orbit) and let
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}\colon\{t+i\tau\in\mathbb{C}:\tau<\tau_{\mathrm{max}}\}\to M_{\mathbb{C}}
be its analytic continuation to a strip. Then
J_{g}
is the complex structure on
M_{\tau}=B^{*}_{\tau}M
so that
\gamma_{\mathbb{C}}
is a holomorphic map; see
[
22
]
.

2.2.
Contact and CR structure on the boundary of a Grauert tube

The boundary of a Grauert tube, being a level set of the strictly plurisubharmonic function
\rho
, is strongly pseudoconvex and real analytic. We endow
\partial M_{\tau}
with the volume form
d\mu_{\tau}
obtained by pulling back the standard Liouville form
\alpha\wedge\omega_{T^{*}M}^{m-1}
on
\partial B_{\tau}^{*}M_{\tau}
under the symplectic diffeomorphism (
49
):

d\mu_{\tau}=(E^{-1})^{*}(\alpha\wedge\omega_{T^{*}M}^{m-1})\Big|_{\partial M_{\tau}}.

(53)

Let
J=J_{g}
denote the adapted complex structure on a Grauert tube (see
subsection 2.1
). The boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
, being a real hypersurface of the complex manifold
M_{\tau}
, carries a CR structure. The tangent space admits the decomposition

T\partial M_{\tau}=H\oplus\mathbb{R}T,

(54)

where
H=\ker\alpha=JT\partial M_{\tau}\cap T\partial M_{\tau}
is a real,
J
-invariant hyperplane bundle and
T=\Xi_{\sqrt{\rho}}
, called the
Reeb vector field
, is the Hamilton vector field of the Grauert tube function. Equivalently,
JT=\nabla\sqrt{\rho}
.

Complexifying the tangent space yields

\displaystyle T^{\mathbb{C}}\partial M_{\tau}=H^{(1,0)}\partial M_{\tau}\oplus H^{(0,1)}\partial M_{\tau}\oplus\mathbb{C}T

(55)

where
H^{(1,0)}
and
H^{(0,1)}
are the
J
-holomorphic and
J
-antiholomorphic subspaces. The boundary Cauchy–Riemann operators are defined by

\displaystyle\partial_{b}f=df|_{H^{(1,0)}}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\partial}_{b}f=df|_{H^{(0,1)}}.

(56)

2.3.
The Szegő projector and the Boutet de Movel–Sjöstrand parametrix

The Szegő projector is a complex Fourier integral operator (FIO) with a positive complex canonical relation. We recall the Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand parametrix construction for the Szegő kernel in the context of Grauert tubes, but the same construction holds with
\partial M_{\tau}
replaced by any bounded, strongly pseudoconvex domain.

Definition 2.2
.

The Szegő projector
\Pi_{\tau}
is the orthogonal projection

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}\colon L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau},d\mu_{\tau})\to H^{2}(\partial M_{\tau},d\mu_{\tau})

(57)

onto the Hardy space consisting of boundary values of holomorphic functions in
M_{\tau}
that are square integrable with respect to the volume form (
53
). Its distributional kernel
\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)
is defined by the relation

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}f(z)=\int_{\partial M_{\tau}}\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)f(w)\,d\mu_{\tau}(w)\quad\text{for all $f\in L^{2}(\partial M_{\tau})$}.

(58)

The Szegő projector is a Fourier integral operator with a positive complex canonical relation. The real points of this canonical relation is the graph of the identity map on the symplectic cone
\Sigma_{\tau}
spanned by the contact form
\alpha:=d^{c}\sqrt{\rho}|_{T*\partial M_{\tau}}
, i.e.,

\Sigma_{\tau}=\{(\zeta,r\alpha_{\zeta}):r\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\}\subseteq T^{*}(\partial M_{\tau}).

(59)

Using the imaginary time geodesic flow
E
in (
49
), we can construct the symplectic equivalence
\iota_{\tau}
between the cotangent bundle and the symplectic cone:

\iota_{\tau}\colon T^{*}M-0\to\Sigma_{\tau},\quad\iota_{\tau}(x,\xi)=\bigg(E\Big(x,\tau\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert}\Big),\lvert\xi\rvert\alpha_{E(x,\tau\frac{\xi}{\lvert\xi\rvert})}\bigg).

(60)

We now briefly describe the symbol of the Szegő projector. Details can be found in
[
3
, Theorem 11.2]
or
[
33
, Section 3]
. Let
\Sigma_{\tau}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C}
be the complexified normal bundle of (
59
). The symbol
\sigma(\Pi_{\tau})
of
\Pi_{\tau}
is a rank one projection onto a ground state
e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}
, which is annihilated by a Lagrangian system of Cauchy–Riemann equations corresponding to a Lagrangian subspace
\Lambda_{\tau}\subseteq\Sigma_{\tau}^{\perp}\otimes\mathbb{C}
. The time evolution
\Pi_{\tau}\mapsto G^{-t}_{\tau}\Pi_{\tau}G^{t}_{\tau}
under the Hamilton flow (
51
) yields another rank one projection onto some time-dependent ground state
e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}}
, where
\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}
is the pushforward of
\Lambda_{\tau}
under the flow. The quantity

\sigma_{t,\tau,0}=\langle e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}},e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\rangle^{-1}

(61)

appears in
subsection 2.4
and
subsection 2.5
. It was shown in
[
33
]
that
\langle e_{\Lambda_{\tau}^{t}},e_{\Lambda_{\tau}}\rangle
is the
L^{2}
inner product of two Gaussians, hence nowhere vanishing.

Finally, we discuss an oscillatory integral representation of the Szegő kernel. Recall from
subsection 2.1
that
\rho
is the real analytic Kähler potential on the Grauert tube. We introduce the defining function for the boundary
\partial M_{\tau}
of a Grauert tube:

\varphi_{\tau}\colon M_{\tau_{\mathrm{max}}}\to[0,\infty),\quad\varphi_{\tau}(z):=\rho(z)-\tau^{2}

(62)

Let
\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})
be the analytic extension of
\varphi_{\tau}(z)=\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{z})
to
M_{\tau}\times\overline{M}_{\tau}
obtained by polarization. Put (c.f. the formula (
46
) for the Grauert tube function)

\displaystyle\psi_{\tau}(z,w)=\frac{1}{i}\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})=\frac{1}{i}\left(-\frac{1}{4}r^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{w})-\tau^{2}\right).

(63)

By construction,
\psi
is homolomorphic in
z
, antiholomorphic in
w
, and satisfies
\psi(z,w)=-\overline{\psi(z,w)}
.

Theorem 2.3

(The Boutet de Monvel–Sjöstrand parametrix,
[
5
, Theorem 1.5]
)
.

With
\psi
as in (
63
), there exists a classical symbol

s\in S^{m-1}(\partial M_{\tau}\times\partial M_{\tau}\times\mathbb{R}^{+})\quad\text{with}\quad s(z,w,\sigma)\sim\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sigma^{m-1-k}s_{k}(z,w)

(64)

so that the Szegő kernel (
58
) has the oscillatory integral representation

\displaystyle\Pi_{\tau}(z,w)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\sigma\psi_{\tau}(z,w)}s(z,w,\sigma)\,d\sigma\quad\text{modulo a smoothing kernel}.

(65)

We record a key estimate for
\varphi_{\tau}
(or equivalently for the phase function
\psi
). Define the
Calabi diastatis function
by

D(z,w)=\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{z})+\varphi_{\tau}(w,\overline{w})-\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})-\varphi_{\tau}(w,\overline{z}).

(66)

On
\partial M_{\tau}
, the diastatis simplifies to

\displaystyle D(z,w)=-\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})-\varphi_{\tau}(w,\overline{z})=-2\Rep\varphi_{\tau}(z,\overline{w})\quad\text{for $z,w\in\partial M_{\tau}$}.

(67)

In the closure of the Grauert tube,
[
5
, Corollary 1.3]
gives the lower bound

\displaystyle D(z,w)\geq C\big(d(z,\partial M_{\tau})+d(w,\partial M_{\tau})+d(z,w)^{2}\big)\quad\text{for $z,w\in\overline{M_{\tau}}$}.

(68)

Recall the eigenequation (

3.
Heisenberg coordinates on the boundary of a Grauert tube

This section reviews the construction and properties of Heisenberg coordinates following
[
13
, Section 18]
. Roughly speaking, in these coordinates, the strongly pseudoconvex boundary
\partial M_{\tau}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{C}}
is, to a first approximation, the Heisenberg group viewed as a hypersurface in complex Euclidean space. We briefly recall the CR structure on the Heisenberg group.

The Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
of degree
m-1
is the Lie group
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{m-1}
with group law

(\theta,z)\cdot(\phi,w)=(\theta+\phi+2\Imp(z\cdot w),z+w).

(82)

Remark 3.1
.

It is more common to use
t
for the
\mathbb{R}
-coordinate but, since
t
is already reserved for the time parameter of the geodesic or Hamiltonian flow, we use
\theta
instead.

For
1\leq j\leq m-1
, the vector fields

Z_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}+i\overline{z}_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\qquad\overline{Z}_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z}_{j}}+iz_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta},\qquad T=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}

(83)

satisfy the commutation relations

[Z_{j},\overline{Z}_{k}]=-2i\delta_{jk}T\quad\text{and}\quad[Z_{j},Z_{k}]=[\overline{Z}_{j},\overline{Z}_{k}]=[Z_{j},T]=[\overline{Z}_{j},T]=0.

(84)

Furthermore, the
1
-form

\alpha=d\theta-i\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{z}_{j}\,dz_{j}-z_{j}\,d\overline{z}_{j})

(85)

annihilates
Z_{j}
and
\overline{Z}_{j}
for all
1\leq j\leq m-1
. Hence, the complexified tangent space of the Heisenberg group admits the decomposition

T^{\mathbb{C}}\mathbf{H}^{m-1}=H^{(1,0)}\oplus H^{(0,1)}\oplus\mathbb{C}T,

(86)

in which
H^{(1,0)}
and
H^{(0,1)}
are spanned by the holomorphic vector fields
Z_{j}
and the anti-holomorphic vector fields
\overline{Z}_{j}
, respectively. Direct verification shows that the subspace
H^{(1,0)}
defines a CR structure on
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
.

The Levi form
\langle\,,\,\rangle_{L}
is the Hermitian form on
H^{(1.0)}
defined by

\langle Z,W\rangle_{L}=-i\langle d\alpha,Z\wedge\overline{W}\rangle\quad\text{for all $Z,W\in H^{(1,0)}$}.

(87)

Direct computation shows that the Levi form with respect to the basis
Z_{j}
is the identity. Thus,
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
is strongly pseudoconvex.

Set

\displaystyle D_{m}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\zeta=(\zeta_{0},\dotsc,\zeta_{m-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\lvert\zeta_{j}\rvert^{2}<\Imp\zeta_{0}\bigg\}.

(88)

\displaystyle\partial D_{m}

\displaystyle=\bigg\{\zeta=(\zeta_{0},\dotsc,\zeta_{m-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}:\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\lvert\zeta_{j}\rvert^{2}=\Imp\zeta_{0}\bigg\}.

(89)

The Heisenberg group
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
acts on
\mathbb{C}^{m}
by holomorphic affine transformations; given
(\theta,z)=(\theta,z_{1},\dotsc,z_{m-1})\in\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
and
\zeta=(\zeta_{0},\dotsc,\zeta_{m-1})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
, we have
(\theta,z)\cdot\zeta=(\zeta_{0}^{\prime},\dotsc,\zeta_{m-1}^{\prime})
, where

\displaystyle\zeta_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{0}+\theta+i\lvert z\rvert^{2}+2i\sum_{j=1}^{m-1}\zeta_{j}\overline{z}_{j},

(90)

\displaystyle\zeta_{j}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\zeta_{j}+z_{j}\quad\text{for $1\leq j\leq m-1$}.

Note that the group action preserves
D_{m}
(which is biholomorphic to the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{m}
) and
\partial D_{m}
. Indeed,
\mathbf{H}^{m-1}
acts simply and transitively on
\partial D_{m}
. Thus, comparing (
82
) and (
90
), we find that the Heisenberg group may be identified to
\partial D_{m}
via the correspondence

H_{m-1}\ni(\theta,z)\leftrightarrow(\theta,z)\cdot 0=(\theta+i\lvert z\rvert^{2},z_{1},\dotsc,z_{m-1})\in\partial D_{m}.

(91)

On any nondegenerate CR manifold (in particular the boundary of a Grauert tube), there exists a Heisenberg-like coordinate system
(\theta,z_{1},\dotsc,z_{m-1})
so that
Z_{j}=(\partial/\partial z_{j})+i\overline{z}_{j}(\partial/\partial\theta)
and
T=(\partial/\partial\theta)
up to lower order terms. The precise statement,
section 1
, is already given in the introduction, so we do not reproduce it here. An explicit construction of such “normal coordinates” was presented in
[
13
]
. We call the same coordinates “Heisenberg coordinates” in this paper to emphasize the osculating Heisenberg structure (see
subsection 3.1
).

Remark 3.2
.

If
f
is smooth, then

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=O^{1}\text{ if and only if $f(\eta)=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\lvert x_{k}(\eta)\rvert+\lvert y_{k}(\eta)\rvert)+\lvert\theta(\eta)\rvert\right)$},

(92)

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=O^{2}\text{ if and only if $f(\eta)=O\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\lvert x_{k}(\eta)\rvert^{2}+\lvert y_{k}(\eta)\rvert^{2})+\lvert\theta(\eta)\rvert\right)$}.

(93)

Proof.

Direct computation (see

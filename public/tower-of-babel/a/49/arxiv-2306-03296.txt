Abstract
. The proalgebraic fundamental group of a connected topological space X X , recently introduced by the first author, is an affine group scheme whose representations classify local systems of finite-dimensional vector spaces on X X . In this article, we further develop the theory of the proalgebraic fundamental group, in particular, we establish homotopy invariance and a Seifert-van Kampen theorem. To facilitate the latter, we study amalgamated free product of affine group schemes. We also compute the proalgebraic fundamental group of the arithmetically relevant Kucharcyzk-Scholze spaces and compare it to the motivic Galois group.

For the Seifert-van Kampen theorem, we construct and study amalgamated free products of
k

groups.
It is known
[

]

k

k

k

It has the advantage that it leads to a proof of the Seifert-van Kampen theorem for the proalgebraic fundamental group rather directly. The second construction is based on an explicit, albeit quite involved construction with Hopf algebras. It has the advantage that it allows us to deduce properties of the amalgamated free product from properties of the factors. For example, we show that the amalgamated free product of two geometrically reduced

1.2.
Tannakian categories

Our next goal is to show that the fibre product of Tannakian categories is naturally a Tannakian category. To this end, we first recall the basic facts concerning Tannakiann categories. For more background see
[
DM82
]
and
[
Del90
]
.

A
tensor category
is a tuple
(\mathcal{A},\otimes,\phi,\psi)
, where
\mathcal{A}
is a category,
\otimes\colon\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A},\ (X,Y)\rightsquigarrow X\otimes Y
is a functor and
\phi
,
\psi
are isomophisms of functors expressing the associativity and commutativity of
\otimes
. More precisely, the
associativity constraint

\phi
has components
\phi_{X,Y,Z}\colon X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)\to(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z
and the
commutativity constraint

\psi
has components
\psi_{X,Y}\colon X\otimes Y\to Y\otimes X
. The functorial isomorphisms
\phi
and
\psi
are required to satisfy three
coherence conditions
(see
[
DM82
, Section 1]
for details). It is also required that there exists an
identity object

(\mathds{1},u)
. This means that
\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A},\ X\rightsquigarrow\mathds{1}\otimes X
is an equivalence of categories and
u\colon\mathds{1}\to\mathds{1}\otimes\mathds{1}
is an isomorphism.

Then there exists a unique isomorphism of functors
l
with component
l_{X}\colon X\to\mathds{1}\otimes X
such that certain properties are satisfied (
[
DM82
, Prop. 1.3 (a)]
). Similarly, there is functorial isomorphism
r
with components
r_{X}\colon X\to X\otimes\mathds{1}
.

We will usually omit
\phi
and
\psi
from the notation and refer to
(\mathcal{A},\otimes)
, or even
\mathcal{A}
, as a tensor category. A
tensor functor

(\mathcal{A},\otimes)\to(\mathcal{A}^{\prime},\otimes^{\prime})
is a pair
(F,\alpha)
, where
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
is a functor and
\alpha
is an isomorphism of functors with components
\alpha_{X,Y}\colon F(X)\otimes F(Y)\to F(X\otimes Y)
such that three natural properties involving respectively the associativity constraints, the commutativity constraints and the identity objects, are satisfied (
[
DM82
, Def. 1.8]
). We will often omit the functorial isomorphism
\alpha
from the notation and simply refer to
F
as a tensor functor.

A tensor category
(\mathcal{A},\otimes)
is
rigid
, if for every object
X
of
\mathcal{A}
the exists an object
X^{\vee}
(a
dual
of
X
) of
\mathcal{A}
together with morphisms
\operatorname{ev}\colon X\otimes X^{\vee}\to\mathds{1}
and
\delta\colon\mathds{1}\to X^{\vee}\otimes X
such that

X\xrightarrow{r}X\otimes\mathds{1}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\delta}X\otimes(X^{\vee}\otimes X)\xrightarrow{\phi}(X\otimes X^{\vee})\otimes X\xrightarrow{\operatorname{ev}\otimes\operatorname{id}}\mathds{1}\otimes X\xrightarrow{l^{-1}}X

and

X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{l}\mathds{1}\otimes X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\delta\otimes\operatorname{id}}(X^{\vee}\otimes X)\otimes X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\phi^{-1}}X^{\vee}\otimes(X\otimes X^{\vee})\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{ev}}X^{\vee}\otimes\mathds{1}\xrightarrow{r^{-1}}X^{\vee}

are the identity. The above definition of rigid is equivalent to the one used in
[
DM82
]
by
[
Del90
, Section 2]
.
Given
X,X^{\vee}
and
\operatorname{ev}
such that there exists a
\delta
verifying the above conditions, then
\delta
is uniquely determined. Moreover, the pair
(X^{\vee},\operatorname{ev})
is unique up to a unique isomorphism. For a morphism
f\colon X\to Y
the
transpose

{}^{t}f\colon Y^{\vee}\to X^{\vee}
of
f
is defined as the composition

{{}^{t}}f\colon Y^{\vee}\simeq\mathds{1}\otimes Y^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\delta\otimes\operatorname{id}}X^{\vee}\otimes X\otimes Y^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes f\otimes\operatorname{id}}X^{\vee}\otimes Y\otimes Y^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}\otimes\operatorname{ev}}X^{\vee}\otimes\mathds{1}\simeq X^{\vee}.

For
f
invertible one sets
f^{\vee}=({{}^{t}f})^{-1}\colon X^{\vee}\to Y^{\vee}
.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{C}
and

be tensor functors between tensor categories. Then
\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}
naturally acquires the structure of a tensor category such that the projections
\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}

\mathcal{A}
,

and

Cf. the discussion on page 360 of

We define the tensor product
\otimes\colon(\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B})\times(\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B})\to\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}
on objects by

(A,B,c)\otimes(A^{\prime},B^{\prime},c^{\prime})=(A\otimes A^{\prime},B\otimes B^{\prime},c\otimes c^{\prime})

and on morphisms by
(a,b)\otimes(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})=(a\otimes a^{\prime},b\otimes b^{\prime})
. The associativity and commutativity constraints on
\mathcal{A}
and

induce associativity and commutativity constraints on
\mathcal{A}\times_{\mathcal{C}}\mathcal{B}
. The identity object of

the unique isomorphism derived from the fact that

.

If

∎

A rigid tensor category

is

is abelian. In this case

A basic example of a tensor category is the category

The most basic example of a rigid abelian tensor category is the category

1.3.
Amalgamated free products

In this section we recall the definition of amalgamated free products and discuss their relation with fibre products of neutralized Tannakian categories.

2.
The Hopf algebra of the amalgamated free product

In this section we offer an explicit description of the Hopf algebra
k[G_{1}*_{H}G_{2}]
of the amalgamated free product of two
morphisms
H\to G_{1}
and
H\to G_{2}
of
k
-groups in terms of the Hopf algebras
k[G_{1}]
,
k[G_{2}]
and
k[H]
. In other words, we explicitly describe the fibre product in the category of Hopf algebras.
(Recall that all Hopf algebras are assumed to be commutative.) We also discuss properties of amalgamated free products, such as being connected or geometrically reduced.

2.1.
Fibre products of Hopf algebras

To motivate our explicit construction of the fibre product of Hopf algebras, let us revisit the construction of the amalgamated free product in the category of groups from a more categorical/conceptual perspective. For now, assume that
\phi_{1}\colon H\to G_{1}
and
\phi_{2}\colon H\to G_{2}
are morphisms of abstract groups. The amalgamated free product
G_{1}*_{H}G_{2}
(in the category of groups) can be constructed as follows: First form the disjoint union
G_{1}\uplus G_{2}
and then build the free monoid
M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})
on the set
G_{1}\uplus G_{2}
. Finally, the amalgamated free product
G_{1}*_{H}G_{2}
is obtained from
M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})
, by quotienting out by the relations

g_{1}\cdot g_{1}^{\prime}=g_{1}g_{1}^{\prime}\quad\forall\ g_{1},g_{1}^{\prime}\in G_{1},

g_{2}\cdot g_{2}^{\prime}=g_{2}g_{2}^{\prime}\quad\forall\ g_{2},g_{2}^{\prime}\in G_{2},

1_{G_{1}}=1_{M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})},

1_{G_{2}}=1_{M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})},

\phi_{1}(h)=\phi_{2}(h)\quad\forall\ h\in H.

Here the
\cdot
is used to denote multiplication in
M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})
.

This construction can be dualized. Let
k[G_{1}]\to k[H]
and
k[G_{2}]\to k[H]
be morphisms of Hopf algebras. The disjoint union
G_{1}\uplus G_{2}
corresponds to the product algebra
k[G_{1}]\oplus k[G_{2}]=k[G_{1}]\times k[G_{2}]
. The free monoid is the left adjoint of the forgetful functor from the category of monoids to the category of sets. In the Hopf algebra world, we should thus consider the right adjoint
A\rightsquigarrow C(A)
of the forgetful functor from the category of bialgebras to the category of algebras. Finally, taking the quotient of
M(G_{1}\uplus G_{2})
, corresponds to choosing an appropriate subbialgebra
k[G_{1}*_{H}G_{2}]
of
C(k[G_{1}]\times k[G_{2}])
(that turns out to be a Hopf algebra).

To carry out the above idea, we need an explicit description of the right adjoint
A\rightsquigarrow C(A)
of the forgetful functor from the category of bialgebras to the category of algebras. To this end, let us recall the construction of the
cofree coalgebra

on a
k
-vector space

. See
[
Swe69
, Section 6.4]
or
[
Haz03
]
. It comes with a
k
-linear map

characterized by the following universal property: If
D
is a coalgebra and
\varphi\colon D\to V
is a
k
-linear map, there exists a unique morphism
\tilde{\varphi}\colon D\to C(V)
of coalgebras making

\textstyle{C(V)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\pi}
\textstyle{V}
\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\tilde{\varphi}}

commutative. In other words, the functor

is right adjoint to the forgetful functor from coalgebras to vector spaces. We consider the tensor algebra

.
For every

Throughout Section

if the

-power map
(\;)^{p}
is bijective on

. In this section we give Tannakian criteria with applications for when the Tannaka dual group of a neutralized Tannakian category

over

is reduced resp. perfect. The main tool is the functor
\operatorname{Fr}_{+}
from
[

]
and our criteria are implicitely contained in
[
Cou20
]
. For convenience we give short self-contained proofs of the required properties of

. In fact, there are two versions of

and

For a morphism
\varphi

\mathcal{T}

becomes a functor.

Let

as a functor from

\Gamma(G_{/k},\mathcal{O})
of
G
consists of the continuous maps
G\to k
where
k
carries the discrete topology. Note that for the
k
-rational points, we have

\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)^{\operatorname{et}}(k)=\pi^{\operatorname{et}}_{1}(X_{F},x)=G_{F}\;.

Next we show that the connected
k
-group
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is abelian. The inclusion
\mu_{F}\hookrightarrow\overline{F}^{\times}
induces a short exact sequence of compact abelian topological group

1\longrightarrow(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F})^{\vee}\longrightarrow(\overline{F}^{\times})^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\pi}\mu^{\vee}_{F}\longrightarrow 1\;.

Here
\pi
is the map which restricts a character
\chi:\overline{F}^{\times}\to S^{1}
to
\mu_{F}
. All fibres of
\pi
are homeomorphic and in particular
X_{\overline{F}}
, the fibre of our fixed embedding
\iota:\mu_{F}\hookrightarrow S^{1}
is homeomorphic to
V^{\vee}
, the Pontrjagin dual of the
\mathbb{Q}
-vectorspace
V=\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}
. Write
V=\varinjlim_{\Gamma}\Gamma
as a filtered union of finite free
\mathbb{Z}
-submodules. Then
V^{\vee}=\varprojlim_{T}T
where
T=\Gamma^{\vee}\cong(S^{1})^{\operatorname{rk}\Gamma}
is a torus. Let
y\in V^{\vee}
be the image of
\overline{x}
under a homeomorphism
X_{\overline{F}}\xrightarrow{\sim}V^{\vee}
and let
y_{T}
be the image of
y
in
T
. Using
[
Den
, Theorems 4.1 and 4.3]
we obtain isomorphisms of
k
-groups

\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\cong\pi_{k}(V^{\vee},y)=\varprojlim_{T}\pi_{k}(T,y_{T})=\varprojlim_{T}\pi_{1}(T,y_{T})^{\operatorname{alg}}\;.

Since
\pi_{1}(T,y_{T})
is commutative, it follows that
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is commutative as well. If
k
is perfect, then it follows from
[
Mil17
, Theorem 16.13]
that there is a canonical isomorphism of
k
groups

(22)

\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\times\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\;.

Here
\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is the pro-unipotent radical of
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
and
\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
, the maximal pro-reductive quotient of
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
can be identified with the greatest
k
-subgroup of
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
of multiplicative type. Both
\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
and
\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
are commutative and connected. The cohomologies of
X_{\overline{F}}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}/n
and
\mathbb{Q}
have been calculated in
[
KS18
]
. We recall the argument for
H^{1}
and coefficients in any abelian group
A
.

Recall the pro-torus
V^{\vee}=\varprojlim_{T}T
above where
V=\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}
. We have

H^{1}(V^{\vee},\underline{A})=\varinjlim_{T}H^{1}(T,\underline{A})\;.

It follows that translation by an element of
V^{\vee}
induces the identity on
H^{1}(V^{\vee},\underline{A})
since this is well known for the finite dimensional tori
T
. Hence the homeomorphisms
V^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\sim}X_{\overline{F}},\alpha\mapsto\alpha\chi
for
\chi\in X_{\overline{F}}
all induce the same isomorphism, which we will view as an identification

(23)

H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{A})=H^{1}(V^{\vee},\underline{A})\;.

We have

\displaystyle H^{1}(T,\underline{A})

\displaystyle=H^{1}(\Gamma^{\vee},\underline{A})=\operatorname{Hom}(\pi_{1}(\Gamma^{\vee}),A)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}(\operatorname{Hom}(\Gamma,\mathbb{Z}),A)=\Gamma\otimes A

and hence

(24)

H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{A})=\varinjlim_{T}H^{1}(T,\underline{A})=\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes A\;.

The category of commutative unipotent groups
U
over
k\supset\mathbb{Q}
is anti-equivalent to the category of
k
-vector spaces by sending
U
to
Y(U)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(U,\mathbb{G}_{a})
. Given
Y(U)
we recover
U
as

U=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(Y(U),\mathbb{G}_{a/k})\;,

where by definition for all
k
-schemes
S
we have

\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(Y(U),\mathbb{G}_{a/k})(S):=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(Y(U),\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}))\;.

Here, on the right
\operatorname{Hom}_{k}
means
k
-linear maps. Again using
[
Den
, Proposition 4.4]
, we obtain

(25)

Y(\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}),\mathbb{G}_{a/k})=H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{k})\;,

and then

(26)

\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{k}),\mathbb{G}_{a/k})\;.

We now turn to the multiplicative part. Since we want to apply
[
Den
, Proposition 4.4]
which gives a formula for
\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}),\mathbb{G}_{m})
, but not for
\operatorname{Hom}_{k^{\operatorname{sep}}}(\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\otimes_{k}k^{\operatorname{sep}},\mathbb{G}_{m})
, we restrict our discussion to the case where
k=\overline{k}
. Then the category of multiplicative groups over
k
is equivalent to the category of abelian groups by sending
H
to
X^{*}(H)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(H,\mathbb{G}_{m})
. The group
H
is recovered from
X^{*}(H)
as

H=\operatorname{Hom}(X^{*}(H),\mathbb{G}_{m/k})\;.

Here the right hand side is defined by its
S
-valued points, which are the groups

\operatorname{Hom}(X^{*}(H),\mathbb{G}_{m/k})(S):=\operatorname{Hom}(X^{*}(H),\Gamma(S,\mathcal{O}^{\times}))\;.

Using
[
Den
, Proposition 4.4]
, we find

X^{*}(\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}),\mathbb{G}_{m/k})=H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{k}^{\times})\;

and therefore

(27)

\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})=\operatorname{Hom}(H^{1}(X_{\overline{F}},\underline{k}^{\times}),\mathbb{G}_{m/k})\;.

Combining (
25
), (
26
) with (
27
) we get the following

Corollary 5.8
.

In the situation of Corollary
5.7
we have canonical isomorphisms of commutative
k
-groups:

\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\xrightarrow{\sim}\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\times\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\;,\quad\text{if $k$ is perfect}

with

\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k,\mathbb{G}_{a/k})\;,\quad\text{if}\;k\supset\mathbb{Q}

and

\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})=\operatorname{Hom}(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k^{\times}/\mu_{k},\mathbb{G}_{m/k})\;,\quad\text{if}\;k=\overline{k}\supset\mathbb{Q}\;.

Remark 5.9
.

Since the character group
\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}k^{\times}=\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k^{\times}/\mu_{F}
of
\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is a
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space, it has no torsion and hence
\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is connected. The unipotent part
\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
being connected, it follows that
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is connected. We know this of course, since
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})=\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)^{0}
by Corollary
5.7
.

Remark 5.10
.

Let
G\to G^{\prime\prime}
be a surjective i.e. faithfully flat map of affine
k
-groups. Equivalently, the corresponding map of Hopf-algebras
A^{\prime\prime}\to A
is injective. For algebraically closed fields
k
the induced map
G(k)\to G^{\prime\prime}(k)
is surjective, c.f.
[
DG70
, III, §3, Collaire 7.6]
.
In particular, the map
G(k)\to\pi_{0}(G)(k)
is surjective.

It follows from Corollary
5.7
that for
k=\overline{k}
we have a short exact sequence of pro-discrete groups

(28)

1\longrightarrow\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)\times\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)\longrightarrow\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)(k)\longrightarrow G_{F}\longrightarrow 1\;.

If additionally
\operatorname{char}k=0
, then using Corollary
5.8
, we get

\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k,k)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}}(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F},k)

and

\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)=\operatorname{Hom}(\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}k^{\times}/\mu_{F},k^{\times})\;.

Going through the constructions, one sees that the
G_{F}
-action on these groups via (
28
) is the same as the one induced by the natural
G_{F}
-action on
\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}
. The actions are continuous for the pro-discrete topologies on
\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)
and
\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})(k)
but not for the discrete topologies (the
G_{F}
-orbits do not have to be finite). Thus, although these groups are
\mathbb{Q}
-vector spaces, it is not clear whether the extension (
28
) is trivial i.e. a semi-direct product or not. The corresponding question is open for the extension of affine groups in Corollary
5.7
as well.

The étale fundamental group of
X_{F}
is isomorphic to the Galois group of
F
. We now discuss the question, whether the proalgebraic fundamental group
\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)
is related to the motivic Galois group
G_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}
if
k\supset\mathbb{Q}
. We assume that our embedding
\iota:\mu_{F}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the restriction of an embedding
\iota:\overline{F}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, which then also provides a point
\overline{x}
of
X_{\overline{F}}
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{F}(k),\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)
be the Tannakian categories of mixed motives over
F
with coefficients in
k
neutralized by the Betti-realization via
\iota
. Thus e.g. for
M=H^{w}(X)
, with
X
a variety over
\overline{F}
the fibre functor sends
M
to
H^{w}_{B}(X\otimes_{\overline{F},\iota}\mathbb{C},\underline{k})
. We can think of motives in terms of realizations as in
[
Jan90
]
. By definition,
G_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}
resp.
G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}
are the Tannakian duals of
\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)
resp.
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)
with respect to this fibre functor. There is a canonical short exact sequence of
k
-groups

1\longrightarrow G^{0}_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}\longrightarrow G_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}\longrightarrow G_{F/k}\longrightarrow 1\;,

where
G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}=G^{0}_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}
canonically. As explained in the introduction of
[
KS18
]
there should be a “better” space
\tilde{X}_{F}
mapping to
X_{F}
and we would expect
\pi_{k}(\tilde{X}_{F},\tilde{x})
to be closely related to
G_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}
. This suggests a map
G_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}\longrightarrow\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)
of
k
-groups and hence a map

G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}=G^{0}_{\mathcal{M}_{F}(k)}\longrightarrow\pi_{k}(X_{F},x)^{0}=\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\;.

It would map unipotent radicals to unipotent radicals and induce map between the maximal pro-reductive quotients. Since
\pi_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})
is commutative, we would therefore get maps

(29)

(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)})^{\operatorname{ab}}\longrightarrow\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})

and

(30)

(G^{\operatorname{red}}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)})^{\operatorname{ab}}\longrightarrow\pi^{\operatorname{red}}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x})\;.

We will now construct a canonical non-trivial map (
29
) for any field
k\supset\mathbb{Q}
. To do so is equivalent to giving a map of
k
-vector spaces

(31)

Y(\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))\longrightarrow Y((G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)})^{\operatorname{ab}})=\operatorname{Hom}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})\;.

First note that by (
25
) and (
24
) we have

(32)

Y(\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))=\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes k\;.

Next, any
a\in\overline{F}^{\times}
defines a motive
E_{a}=H^{1}(\mathbb{G}_{m},\{1,a\})(1)
over
\overline{F}
. It can also be described as
E_{a}=H^{1}(X_{a})(1)
, where
X_{a}
is the curve obtained from
\mathbb{G}_{m,\overline{F}}
by identifying
1
and
a
. In Deligne’s theory of
1
-motives, we have
E_{a}=[\mathbb{Z}\xrightarrow{\phi}\mathbb{G}_{m}]
, where
\phi(1)=a
, c.f.
[
Del74
, § 10]
. The motive
E_{a}
fits into a canonical extension

0\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(0)\longrightarrow E_{a}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Q}(1)\longrightarrow 0\quad\text{in}\;\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(\mathbb{Q})\;.

Extending coefficients to
k
we get an injective homomorphism

(33)

\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu\otimes k\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}(k(0),k(1))\;,\;(a\;\operatorname{mod}\;\mu)\otimes 1\mapsto E_{a}\otimes k\;.

The motives
k(0)
, resp.
k(1)
correspond to the trivial representation of
G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}}(k)
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
over
k
resp. to the representation denoted
\mathbb{G}_{a}(1)
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}
by the character
\chi:G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}}(k)\to\mathbb{G}_{m/k}
, dual to the full embedding of
\langle k(1)\rangle^{\otimes}
into
\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)
. Then we have

(34)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}(k(0),k(1))=\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}}(k)}(\mathbb{G}_{a},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))=H^{1}(G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))\;.

In the middle, extensions are taken within the category of finite dimensional representations of
G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}
on
k
-vector spaces. For the Hochschild cohomology on the right compare
[
Mil17
, Ch. 15]
. Consider the canonical extension of
k
-groups

1\longrightarrow G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}\longrightarrow G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}\longrightarrow Q\longrightarrow 1\;.

Here
Q=G^{\operatorname{red}}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}
can be interpreted as the Tannakian dual of the category of (direct sums of) pure motives over
\overline{F}
with coefficients in
k
. We now follow the argument in
[
Jan90
, Appendix C4]
. Since Hochschild cohomology of reductive groups over fields of characteristic zero vanishes in positive degrees, the Hochschild-Serre spectral sequence

H^{i}(Q,H^{j}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))\Rightarrow H^{i+j}(G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))

degenerates into isomorphisms

H^{j}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))^{Q}=H^{j}(G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))\;.

In particular, using (
34
) we may identify:

(35)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}(k(0),k(1))=H^{1}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))^{Q}\;.

The character
\chi
above factors over
Q
, the maximal pro-reductive quotient of
G_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}
, and hence the restriction of
\chi
to
G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}
is trivial. Hence we have

(36)

H^{1}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a}(1))^{Q}=H^{1}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})(1)^{Q}=\operatorname{Hom}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})(1)^{Q}\;.

The group on the right is the
\chi^{-1}
eigenspace of the
Q
-action on
\operatorname{Hom}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})
. In any case, we get the following inclusion by combining (
35
) and (
36
)

(37)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}(k(0),k(1))\subset\operatorname{Hom}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})\;.

The promised map (
31

Y(\pi^{u}_{k}(X_{\overline{F}},\overline{x}))\overset{\eqref{eq:5-21}}{=}\overline{F}^{\times}/\mu_{F}\otimes k\overset{\eqref{eq:5-22}}{\hookrightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)}(k(0),k(1))\overset{\eqref{eq:5-26}}{\hookrightarrow}\operatorname{Hom}(G^{u}_{\mathcal{M}_{\overline{F}}(k)},\mathbb{G}_{a})\;.

(The map (
33
) is conjectured to be an isomorphism.)

Contrary to (
29

). The group

is the motivic Galois group for pure motives over

, and

with coefficients in

to the group (

, the

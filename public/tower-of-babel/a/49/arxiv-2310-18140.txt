Study on the behaviors of rupture solutions for a class of elliptic MEMS equations in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2}
Qing Li, Yanyan Zhang

Abstract
This study examines nonnegative solutions to the problem { Δ ​ u = λ ​ | x | α u p in ​ ℝ 2 ∖ { 0 } , u ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 ​ and ​ u > 0 in ​ ℝ 2 ∖ { 0 } , \left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta u=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}{u^{p}}&\hbox{ in}\,\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt] u(0)=0\ \text{and}\ u>0&\hbox{ in}\,\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\\ \end{array}\right. where λ > 0 , \lambda>0, α > − 2 \alpha>-2 , and p > 0 p>0 are constants. The possible asymptotic behaviors of u ⁡ ( x ) u(x) at | x | = 0 |x|=0 and | x | = ∞ |x|=\infty are classified according to ( α , p ) (\alpha,p) . In particular, the results show that for some ( α , p ) (\alpha,p) , u ⁡ ( x ) u(x) exhibits only “isotropic” behavior at | x | = 0 |x|=0 and | x | = ∞ |x|=\infty . However, in other cases, u ⁡ ( x ) u(x) may exhibit the “anisotropic” behavior at | x | = 0 |x|=0 or | x | = ∞ |x|=\infty . Furthermore, the relation between the limit at | x | = 0 |x|=0 and the limit at | x | = ∞ |x|=\infty for a global solution is investigated.



Keywords: MEMS; rupture solution; anisotropic; global solution; convergence

Introduction

\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\Delta u=\displaystyle\frac{\lambda|x|^{\alpha}}{u^{p}}&\hbox{ in}\,\ \mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\{0\},\\[5.69054pt]

Proof:

by (

Therefore, it is reduced to prove

In fact,

\begin{split}H(\tau)=&\int_{0}^{1}~\left(\frac{2(1-\tau^{-2})}{2(\tau^{2}-1)}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\frac{(\xi(\tau-1)+1)^{-2}(\tau-1)}{\sqrt{1+\tau^{2}-(\xi(\tau-1)+1)^{2}-\frac{\tau^{2}}{(\xi(\tau-1)+1)^{2}}}}~d\xi\\

{\mathrel{\to}^{x=(\xi(\tau-1)+1)^{2}}}&\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{1}^{\tau^{2}}\frac{dx}{x\sqrt{(\frac{\tau^{2}-1}{2})^{2}-(x-\frac{\tau^{2}+1}{2})^{2}}}\\
{\mathrel{\to}^{x-\frac{\tau^{2}+1}{2}=\frac{\tau^{2}-1}{2}\sin X}}&\frac{\tau}{2}\int_{-\frac{\pi}{2}}^{\frac{\pi}{2}}\frac{dX}{\frac{\tau^{2}+1}{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}-1}{2}\sin X}\\

=&[\arctan{\tau}-\arctan(-\frac{1}{\tau})]\\
\equiv&\pi/2.\end{split}

(4.9)

So the proof is complete.

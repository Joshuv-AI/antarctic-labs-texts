Introduction

Pseudo-differential operators have been interesting operators in mathematics for a long time.
They include not only classical differential operators with a natural number order but also fractional differential operators.
Moreover, many interesting non-local operators are contained in the class of pseudo-differential operators and it naturally connects them to generators of Markov processes (cf.

L_{p}

They have played important roles in theories of partial differential equations (PDEs) to show the well-posedness of solutions to PDEs in the Sobolev space.
Especially, there commonly appear singularities to obtain optimal regularity estimates of solutions to PDEs and they are overcome by collaboration of many deep theories in analysis and PDEs. Classically,
L_{p}

As mentioned above, there have been tons of researches studying the properties of pseudo-differential operators and the well-posedness of PDEs with them.
However, these theories are built up in an elliptic setting mostly. In other words, there are not many results handling PDEs with pseudo-differential operators in a parabolic setting even though generalizations from elliptic theories to parabolic theories are considered difficult and important in theories of mathematics (especially in PDEs).
In particular, if an additional (time) variable does not satisfy any regularity condition, then this parabolic generalization becomes complicated and non-trivial even though there are many well-constructed theories in an elliptic setting.

Next, we explain a connection between our results and classical important theories in Fourier analysis.
Recall classical Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem (cf.
[
10
, Section 6.2]
).
Let
\psi(\xi)
be a complex-valued function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and assume that there exists a positive constant
M
such that

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(\xi)|\leq M|\xi|^{-|\alpha|},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~\text{for any multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor$}+1,

(1.1)

where
\lfloor d/2\rfloor
denotes the integer which is less than or equal to
d/2
.
Then the operator

\mathcal{T}_{\psi}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi\mathcal{F}[f]\right](x)

becomes
L_{p}
-bounded,
i.e.
, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, there exists a positive constant
N(d,p,M)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{T}_{\psi}f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

However, if a weight
w
is given, then the smoothness on
\psi
in (
1.1
) are not sufficient to guarantee

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{T}_{\psi}f(x)|^{p}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{p}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\qquad\forall f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.2)

In other words, we expect an extra condition on a symbol
\psi
if a weight
w
is additionally given in
L_{p}
-estimates.
Particularly, it is well-known that (
1.2
) holds if
\psi
satisfies stronger smooth conditions than (
1.1
) depending on the weight
w
. For instance, let
w
be a weight in Muckenhoupt’s class
A_{pl/d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(see Definition
2.1
) with
s\in(1,2]
,
d/s<l\leq d
, and

d/l<p
. Then, if

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(\xi)|\leq N|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}~\text{for any multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\leq l$},

we have (
1.2
) (cf.
[
30
, Theorem 1]
).
Based on these theories in Fourier analysis, we find appropriate conditions on
\psi(t,\xi)
to enable equation (
0.1
) to be well-posed in weighted
L_{p}
-spaces. Formally, the solution
u
to (
0.1
) is given by

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{s}^{t}\mathrm{e}^{r\psi(r,-i\nabla)}(x-\cdot)f(s,\cdot)\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}s:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s,

where

\displaystyle p(t,s,x):=1_{0<s<t}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Moreover, if
\psi(r,\xi)\simeq|\xi|^{\gamma}
, then

\displaystyle f\mapsto(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u

becomes a (parabolic) singular integral operator (see Section
5
), where

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left((-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}p\right)(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(1.3)

and

\displaystyle\left((-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}p\right)(t,s,x):=1_{0<s<t}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\xi|^{\gamma}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Similarly to elliptic cases, if

\displaystyle|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq N|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})~\text{for any multi-index $\alpha$ with $|\alpha|\leq\lfloor d/2\rfloor$+1},

(1.4)

then

and

norms of

can be controlled by

[
19
,
22
]
).
However, if a weight
w
is given, the upper bound condition of
\psi(t,\xi)
in (
1.4
) should be enhanced.
That is, it needs to hold for any multi-index with
|\alpha|
which is greater than
\lfloor d/2\rfloor
depending on the weight
w
.
We characterize this as requiring smoothness based on a constant related to each weight. We call this constant
a regularity constant
and denote it by
R_{p,d}^{w}
(see (
2.2
)). In particular, one can easily check that

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor\leq d+1.

In our main theorems (Theorem
2.14
and Theorem
2.15
), we characterize numbers of smoothness of symbols
\psi(t,\xi)
satisfying (
1.4
) depending on

-weights

,
w_{1}
, and

with a help of regularity constants for us to find a positive constant

which is independent of

such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u(t,x)|^{p}w(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}w(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}u(t,x)|^{p}w_{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}w_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t\leq N\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}w_{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}w_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Estimates (
0.3
) and (

We need to mention that if a weight is given by a constant or depends only on time, then our regularity constants are not optimal (Remark

Due to (

), the most important part of our theory is to show

-boundedness of parabolic singular integral operators.
For future applications, we consider general parabolic singular integral operators in the form of

is a kernel satisfying

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\leq N_{1}|t-s|^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}}

2.
Main results

Throughout the paper, we fix
T\in(0,\infty)
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a suitable complex-valued locally integrable function
\psi(t,\xi)
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and almost every
t\in[0,T]
,
we can consider a pseudo-differential operator
\psi(t,i\nabla)
given by

\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[u]\right](x)\qquad u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

More generally, for a complex-valued nice function
u(t,x)
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}

\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[u](t,\cdot)\right](x).

This function
\psi(t,\xi)
is usually called
the symbol
of the pseudo-differential operator.
The operator
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
is called
a time-measurable pseudo-differential operator
if there is no regularity condition on the symbol
\psi
with respect to
t
.
By considering natural constant extensions at
t=0
and
t=T
, we may assume the symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
is defined on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
In this paper, we study the following Cauchy problem with a time-measurable pseudo-differential operator

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\end{cases}

(2.1)

and find appropriate conditions on the symbol for us to establish a well-posedness theory to (
2.1
) in weighted
L_{p}
-spaces.
First, we introduce related functions spaces handling solutions, and inhomogeneous data.

Definition 2.1

(Muckenhoupt weight)
.

For
p\in(1,\infty)
, let
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the class of all nonnegative and locally integrable functions

satisfying

[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}:=\sup_{x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d},r>0}\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{r}(x_{0})}w(x)^{-1/(p-1)}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}<\infty.

We need to relate each
A_{p}
-weight to a nonnegative integer to mention the appropriate smoothness of symbols guaranteeing our

-theory.

Remark 2.2

The class

is increasing as

More precisely, by

c_{w}=c_{w}(d,p,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})>0
and
C_{w}=C_{w}(d,p,[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})>0
such that

1<q:=\frac{p+c_{w}}{1+c_{w}}<p,\quad\text{and}\quad[w]_{A_{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{w}[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Note that the constants
C_{w}
and
c_{w}^{-1}
increase according to
[w]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.

Definition 2.3

(Regularity constant of a weight in
A_{p}
)
.

Let
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and define

\displaystyle R_{p,d}^{w}:=\sup\{p_{0}\in(1,2]:w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

(2.2)

We say that
R_{p,d}^{w}
is
the regularity constant of the weight
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
since this constant plays an important role to characterize the differentiability of a symbol to make
L_{p}
-theories possible.

Remark 2.4
.

In this remark, we assume
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and we denote

p_{w}:=\frac{p(1+c_{w})}{p+c_{w}}\wedge 2\in(1,2].

As pointed out in Remark
2.2
, we have
w\in A_{p/p_{w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Therefore, the set
I_{w,p}:=\{r\in[p/2,\infty):w\in A_{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
is either
[p/2,\infty)
or
(q,\infty)
with
p/2\leq q<p
. This implies that the constant
R_{p,d}^{w}
is well-defined (finite).

We do not know if
w\in A_{p/R_{p,d}^{w}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for each
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
in general since there is no guarantee that
p/R_{p,d}^{w}\in I_{w,p}
.
However, there exists a constant
p_{0}\in(1,2]
such that
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
p_{0}\leq R_{p,d}^{w}\wedge p
, and

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{p_{0}}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d}^{w}}\right\rfloor.

To prove this claim, note that
\left\lfloor d/p\right\rfloor
is left-continuous and piecewise-constant with respect to
p
.
Thus, there exists a
p_{0}\in\left(1,R_{p,d}^{w}\right)
such that
\left\lfloor d/p_{0}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor d/R_{p,d}^{w}\right\rfloor
and
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
It remains to show that
p_{0}\leq R_{p,d}^{w}\wedge p
.

If
p\geq 2
, then it is obvious that
p_{0}<2
, so we only need to consider the case
p\in(1,2)
.
However, it should be noted that
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is only defined for
p>1
, and
A_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-class is not introduced in this paper (see Definition (
2.1
)).
Therefore, for any
p_{0}\in(1,2)
such that
w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, it is clear that
p_{0}
is less than
p
.

Example 2.5
.

The easiest examples of a function in
A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are polynomials,
w_{\alpha}(x):=|x|^{\alpha}
. It is well-known that
w_{\alpha}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if and only if
-d<\alpha<d(p-1)
(cf.
[
10
, Example 7.1.7]
). This implies that for
p_{0}>1
,

p_{0}<\frac{p}{\frac{\alpha}{d}+1}=\frac{pd}{\alpha+d}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad w_{\alpha}\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

Therefore, for
w_{\alpha}(x):=|x|^{\alpha}
with
-d<\alpha<d(p-1)
, one can easily check that the exact value of
R_{p,d}^{w_{\alpha}}
is given by
\frac{pd}{\alpha+d}
.

Next, we introduce two types of weighted
L_{p}
-spaces.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
p,q\in(1,\infty)
.

(i)

For
w_{1}\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we write
f\in L_{q}(\mathbb{R},w_{1};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}))
if

\|f\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{R},w_{1};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}))}:=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}w_{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{q/p}w_{1}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/q}<\infty.

(ii)

For
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, we write
f\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},w)
if

\|f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},w)}:=\left(\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(t,x)|^{p}w(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Note that even if
p=q\in(1,\infty)
, there is no inclusion between
L_{q}(\mathbb{R},w_{1};L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}))
and
L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},w)
.

Next, we introduce Sobolev spaces (Bessel potentials) which fit our weighted spaces.
Recall that
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the Schwartz space and the space of tempered distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, respectively.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(i)

For
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
H_{p}^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
denotes the set of all
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfying

\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}<\infty,

where

(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[(1+|\cdot|^{2})^{\nu/2}\mathcal{F}[f]\right](x).

If
w\equiv 1
, then we omit
w
.

(ii)

For
\nu\in[0,\infty)
and
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
denotes the set of all
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued measurable functions
f
on
(0,T)
satisfying

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+\|(-\Delta)^{\nu}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}<\infty,

where

(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f(t,x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[|\cdot|^{\nu}\mathcal{F}[f(t,\cdot)]\right](x).

If
\nu<0
, then
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
denotes the set of all
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued measurable functions
f
on
(0,T)
satisfying

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}:=\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}<\infty.

Remark 2.8
.

Generally, there is no guarantee that two norms

\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+\|(-\Delta)^{\nu}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\quad\text{and}\quad\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

are equivalent due to the effect of weights.
However, if our weight
w
additionally satisfies

\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}[w(t,\cdot)]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=:N_{0}<\infty,

(2.3)

then two norms are equivalent, which will be shown in Appendix
A
.
In particular, we show that the polynomial
w(t,x):=(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}
with
-d<\alpha<d(p-1)
satisfies (
2.3
) (see Lemma
A.8
).

Definition 2.9
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(i)

C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the set of all
\mathcal{B}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
-measurable functions
f
on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that for any multi-index
\alpha
with respect to the space variable, their derivatives

D^{\alpha}_{x}f\in L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})).

(ii)

C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
denotes the set of all
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that for any multi-index
\alpha
, their derivatives

D^{\alpha}_{x}f\in C([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}),

where
\partial_{t}f(t)
denotes the right derivative and the left derivative at
t=0
and
t=T
, respectively.

Remark 2.10
.

(
i
) In our previous work
[
3
]
, we introduced similar smooth function spaces.
The operators discussed in
[
3
]
have integral representations and are
L_{1}
-bounded.
However, in the present article, we address general pseudo-differential operators that do not possess
L_{1}
-integrability and lack integral representations.
Therefore, it becomes necessary to consider a larger class of functions than those presented in
[
3
]
, in order to approximate solutions.

(
ii
) Let
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Note that for any (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
and almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

D^{\alpha}_{x}f(t,\cdot)\in C(\mathbb{R}^{d})

due to the Sobolev Embedding theorem (
e.g.

[
28
, Theorem 13.1.8]
).

Definition 2.11

(Solution)
.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,
w_{1}\in A_{q}(\mathbb{R})
, and
w_{2}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

we say that
u\in\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu+\gamma}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
(resp.

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\

if there exists a sequence of functions
u_{n}\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\begin{gathered}\partial_{t}u_{n}-\psi(t,-i\nabla)u_{n}\to f\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w),\quad(resp.\,\,L_{q}((0,T),w_{1};H_{p}^{\nu}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}))\,),\end{gathered}

and

u_{n}\to u\quad\text{in}\quad\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu+\gamma}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w),\quad(resp.\,\,L_{q}((0,T),w_{1};H_{p}^{\nu+\gamma}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w_{2}))\,)

as

.

Definition 2.12

(Ellipticity condition of a symbol)
.

We say that a symbol
\psi
satisfies the ellipticity condition (with (
\gamma
,
\kappa
)) if there exists a
\gamma\in(0,\infty)
and
\kappa\in(0,1]
such that

\Re[-\psi(t,\xi)]\geq\kappa|\xi|^{\gamma},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\Re[z]
denotes the real part of the complex number
z
.

Definition 2.13

(regular upper bounds of a symbol)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that a symbol
\psi
has
a
n
-times regular upper bound
(with (
\gamma
,
M
)) if there exist positive constants
\gamma
and
M

such that

|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|\leq M|\xi|^{\gamma-|\alpha|},\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),

for any (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
with
|\alpha|\leq n
.

Recall the regularity constant for a weigh
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
i.e.
,

R_{p,d}^{w}:=\sup\{p_{0}\in(1,2]:w\in A_{p/p_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}.

Here are the main results of this article and we prove them in Section
7
.

Remark 2.16
.

If our weight is a constant function (
i.e.
, without weight), the differentiability
(\lfloor d/R_{p,d+1}^{w}\rfloor+2)
for the symbol

\psi(t,\xi)
in Theorem
2.14
is not optimal.
Indeed, if
w
is a constant, then

\left\lfloor\frac{d}{R_{p,d+1}^{w}}\right\rfloor+2=\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+2.

However, if there is no weight term in the estimate,
\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1
is enough differentiability for the symbol
\psi(t,\xi)
to obtain (
2.4
) as shown in
[
19
]
(or see Theorem
6.9
).
Moreover, if one considers a weight depending only on the time variable

can be relaxed to

\lfloor d/R_{w,p}\rfloor+1
. We will show this result in our next paper based on

-theory for operator-valued Calderón-Zygmund theorem
(cf. see
[

For a general

depending on both the time variable

for the symbol is optimal to guarantee (

3.
The Cauchy problems with smooth functions

Recall the target equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(3.1)

where

\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(t,\cdot)\mathcal{F}[u](t,\cdot)\right](x).

Then the fundamental solution to (
3.1
) is given by

\displaystyle p(t,s,x):=1_{0<s<t}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi,

that is, the integral operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}f(t,x):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

(3.2)

becomes a solution to (
3.1
) if
f
is a sufficiently smooth function.
In this section, we prove that the function
p(t,s,x)
becomes the fundamental solution to (
3.1
) indeed.
We prove some preliminaries first.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
.

(i)

For any
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\mathrm{d}s\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(ii)

For all
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
,
(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}
is a bijective linear operator on
C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(iii)

Suppose that
\psi(t,\xi)
has a
\left(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.
Then
\psi(t,-i\nabla)
is a bounded linear operator on
C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

(
i
)
Put

g(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\mathrm{d}s.

By the dominated convergence theorem and Minkowski’s inequality,

\|D^{\alpha}_{x}g(t_{2},\cdot)-D^{\alpha}_{x}g(t_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq|t_{2}-t_{1}|\|D^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

(3.3)

for all positive numbers
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
.
This implies
g\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and by Remark
2.10
(
ii
) and (
3.3
),
g
is continuous on
[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The fundamental theorem of calculus yields for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

\partial_{t}g(t,x)=f(t,x),\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Therefore,
g\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(
ii
) Let
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
.
It is well-known that
(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}
is a bijective mapping on the Schwartz class (cf.
[
28
, Chapter 13]
).
Thus it suffices to show

(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})

since
\nu
is an arbitrary real number,
Let
\alpha
be a (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
(with respect to space variable).
By a well-known property of the operator
(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}
in
L_{p}
-spaces,

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}_{x}(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle=\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}D^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|+\lfloor|\nu|\rfloor+1}\|D^{\beta}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
N
is independent of
f
. This certainly implies
(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

(
iii
) Let
m(t,\xi):=|\xi|^{-\gamma}\psi(t,\xi)
.
Since
\psi
has a
\left(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1\right)
-times regular upper bound with
\gamma
and
M
, we have

|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}m(t,\xi)|\leq N(\alpha,\gamma,M)|\xi|^{-|\alpha|},\quad\forall|\alpha|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1,\quad\forall(t,\xi)\in[0,T]\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})

by a simple multiplicative law of derivatives.
Thus, using Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem (
e.g.

[
10
, Theorem 6.2.7]
), for all
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
, and
s\in[0,T]
, we have

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}\psi(t,-i\nabla)f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq N\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}D^{\alpha}f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
N=N(d,p,\gamma,M)
.
Moreover, since
p\in(1,\infty)
, by a well-known embedding theorem which can be easily obtained from Mikhlin’s multiplier theorem, we have

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}D^{\alpha}f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|+\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1}\|D^{\beta}f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\beta|\leq|\alpha|+\lfloor\gamma\rfloor+1}\|D^{\beta}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
N
is independent of
f
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
and
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Assume that
\psi(t,\xi)
satisfies the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and has a
\left(\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor+1\right)
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.
Then, there exists a unique solution
u\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
to the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x)+f(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{cases}

(3.4)

Moreover, the solution
u
has the following representation :

u(t,x)=\mathcal{K}f(t,x),\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\mathcal{K}f
is defined in (
3.2
).

Proof.

(Uniqueness)
Suppose that
u\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a solution of the Cauchy problem

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u(t,x)=\psi(t,-i\nabla)u(t,x),\quad&(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,x)=0,\quad&x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.\end{cases}

(3.5)

Taking the Fourier transform to (
3.5
) and using the fundamental theorem of calculus, we have

|\mathcal{F}[u(t,\cdot)](\xi)|\leq M|\xi|^{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}|\mathcal{F}[u(s,\cdot)](\xi)|\mathrm{d}s.

By Gronwall’s inequality, we conclude that
u=0
. From the linearity of equation (
3.4
), we obtain the uniqueness.

(Existence)
Define

u(t,x):=\mathcal{K}f(t,x).

First, we claim that
u\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
In virtue of the dominated convergence theorem, for a (
d
-dimensional) multi-index
\alpha
and
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

D^{\alpha}_{x}u(t,x)=D^{\alpha}_{x}\mathcal{K}f(t,x)=\mathcal{K}(D^{\alpha}_{x}f)(t,x).

Note that by
[
23
, Corollary 3.6]
,

\sup_{s<t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|p(t,s,x)|\mathrm{d}x\leq N,

(3.6)

where
N=N(d,\gamma,\kappa,M)
. Using Minkowski’s inequality and (
3.6
), for all positive numbers
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{K}(D^{\alpha}_{x}f)(t_{2},\cdot)-\mathcal{K}(D^{\alpha}_{x}f)(t_{1},\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\left|\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|p(t,s,\cdot)\|_{L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|D^{\alpha}f(s,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq N|t_{2}-t_{1}|\|D^{\alpha}f\|_{L_{\infty}([0,T];L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
N=N(d,\gamma,\kappa,M)
. Thus, since
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we conclude that
u\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Next, define

v(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\left(\psi(s,-i\nabla)u(s,x)+f(s,x)\right)\mathrm{d}s.

By Lemma
3.1
(
i
),
v\in C_{p}^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
We claim that

u(t,x)=v(t,x),\quad\forall(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

which obviously completes the proof. For
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, applying Fubini’s theorem, we have

\displaystyle v(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\psi(s,\cdot)\int_{0}^{s}\mathcal{F}[f(l,\cdot)]\mathrm{e}^{\int_{l}^{s}\psi(r,\cdot)\mathrm{d}r}\mathrm{d}l\right](x)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left[\mathcal{F}[f(l,\cdot)](\mathrm{e}^{\int_{l}^{t}\psi(r,\cdot)\mathrm{d}r}-1)\right](x)\mathrm{d}l=u(t,x)-\int_{0}^{t}f(s,x)\mathrm{d}s.

The theorem is proved.
∎

4.
An
L_{2}
-boundedness and estimates of the fundamental solution

In this section, we obtain
L_{2}
-estimates of the Cauchy problem and prove some properties of the fundamental solution.
Recall

\displaystyle p(t,s,x):=1_{0<s<t}\cdot\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathrm{e}^{ix\cdot\xi}\mathrm{d}\xi.

Let
f\in C_{p}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then by Theorem
3.2
,

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\mathcal{K}f(t,x):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

becomes the solution to (
3.4
). Moreover, one can easily check that for any
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}\mathcal{K}f(t,x)

(4.1)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}p(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Thus to show the boundedness of
(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}u
in an
L_{p}
-space, it is required to estimate the kernel
P_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and assume that
\psi(t,\xi)
satisfies the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and has a
n
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
N=N(n,M)
such that for all
t>s
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left|D^{n}_{\xi}\left(\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|\leq N|\xi|^{-n}\exp(-\kappa(t-s)|\xi|^{\gamma})\sum_{k=1}^{n}|t-s|^{k}|\xi|^{k\gamma},

(4.2)

where
D^{n}_{\xi}
denotes a (
d
-dimensional) partial derivative with respect to the variable
\xi
whose order is
n
.

Proof.

We use mathematical induction.
The case
n=1
is clear since
|D_{\xi}\psi(t,\xi)|
has a upper bound
N|\xi|^{\gamma-1}
and
|\psi(t,\xi)|
has both lower and upper bounds which are given by
|\xi|^{\gamma}
multiplied by positive constants.
Next, suppose that (
4.2
) holds for all
1,2,\cdots,k
. Then, by Leibniz’s product rule and the hypothesis of the induction,

\displaystyle\left|D^{k+1}_{\xi}\left(\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|=\left|D^{k}_{\xi}\left(\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\int_{s}^{t}D_{\xi}^{1}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left|D^{j}_{\xi}\left(\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\int_{s}^{t}D_{\xi}^{k-j+1}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right|

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{j=0}^{k}\left(|\xi|^{-j}\exp(-\kappa(t-s)|\xi|^{\gamma})\sum_{l=1}^{j}|t-s|^{l}|\xi|^{l\gamma}|t-s||\xi|^{\gamma-k+j-1}\right)

\displaystyle=N|\xi|^{-k-1}\exp(-\kappa(t-s)|\xi|^{\gamma})\sum_{j=0}^{k}\sum_{l=1}^{j}|t-s|^{l+1}|\xi|^{(l+1)\gamma}

\displaystyle\leq N|\xi|^{-k-1}\exp(-\kappa(t-s)|\xi|^{\gamma})\sum_{l=1}^{k+1}|t-s|^{l}|\xi|^{l\gamma},

where
N=N(k,M)
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha
be a (
d
-dimensional) multi-index, and
m\in\{0,1\}
.
Assume that
\psi(t,\xi)
satisfies the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
and has a
n
-times regular upper bound with
(\gamma,M)
.
Then there exists a positive constant
N=N(n,\kappa,\gamma,M,\varepsilon,m,|\alpha|)
such that for all
s<t
,

\begin{gathered}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{n}\left(|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\right)\leq N|t-s|^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}},\\
\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\leq N|t-s|^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}+\frac{d}{2\gamma}},\end{gathered}

Proof.

It is well-known that

|x|\simeq|x^{1}|+\cdots+|x^{d}|~\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In particular, we have,

|x|^{n}\leq N(n)(|x^{1}|^{n}+\cdots+|x^{d}|^{n}),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

(4.3)

By (
4.3
) and elementary properties of the Fourier transform,

\displaystyle|x|^{n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D_{x}^{\alpha}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x^{i}|^{n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\beta|=n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|D_{\xi}^{\beta}\left(\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\partial_{t}^{m}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi.

By Leibniz’s product rule,

\displaystyle D_{\xi}^{\beta}\left(\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\partial_{t}^{m}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)

\displaystyle=D_{\xi}^{\beta}\left(\psi(t,\xi)^{m}\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\beta=\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}}c_{\beta_{0},\beta_{1}}D^{\beta_{0}}_{\xi}(\psi(t,\xi)^{m}\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma})D^{\beta_{1}}_{\xi}\left(\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right).

By Definition
2.12
and Lemma
4.1
,

\displaystyle\left|D_{\xi}^{\beta}\left(\psi(t,\xi)^{m}\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|

(4.4)

\displaystyle\leq N|\xi|^{(m+\varepsilon)\gamma+|\alpha|-n}\exp(-\kappa(t-s)|\xi|^{\gamma})\sum_{k=1}^{n}(t-s)^{k}|\xi|^{k\gamma}.

Therefore,

\sum_{|\beta|=n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|D_{\xi}^{\beta}\left(\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\partial_{t}^{m}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|\mathrm{d}\xi\leq N(t-s)^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}},

where
N=N(n,\kappa,\gamma,\varepsilon,m,|\alpha|)
. Similarly, by (
4.3
), (
4.4
), and Plancherel’s theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D_{x}^{\alpha}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq N\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{i}|^{2n}|\psi(t,-i\nabla)^{m}D_{x}^{\alpha}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{|\beta|=n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|D^{\beta}_{\xi}\left(\psi(t,\xi)^{m}\xi^{\alpha}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\right)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq N|t-s|^{-2(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+2|\alpha|-2n)}{\gamma}},

where
N=N(n,\kappa,\gamma,\varepsilon,m,|\alpha|)
.
The theorem is proved.
∎

Recalling (
4.1
) and handling estimates of various regularities of the solution simultaneously, we introduce the following family of iterated integral operators

\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f(t,x):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}1_{|t-s|<T}+1_{\varepsilon=1}\right)P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s,

where
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. It is remarkable that
P_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is integrable on
(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for all
t>0
and
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
. Thus the integral operator
\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f
is well-defined in a point-wise sense.
However,
P_{1}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
is not integrable on
(0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for any
t>0
.
In this sense, we say that the operator
\mathcal{K}_{1,T}f
is (parabolic) singular. However, one can understand the function
\mathcal{K}_{1,T}f(t,x)
in a point-wise sense if one regards the integral as an iterated integral. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1,T}f(t,x)

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{1}(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)\left((-\Delta)_{y}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f\right)(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)\left((-\Delta)_{y}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}f\right)(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Moreover, since the mapping
f\mapsto\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f
is a pseudo-differential operator as well, the
L_{2}
-boundedness can be easily obtained based on Plancherel’s theorem without any regularity condition on the symbol.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that the symbol
\psi
satisfies the ellipticity condition with
(\gamma,\kappa)
.
Then there exists a constant
N=N(\kappa)
such that

\|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\leq NT^{1-\varepsilon}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}\quad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

Proof.

By Plancherel’s theorem, Fubini’s theorem, and Minkowski’s inequality,

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(\int_{s}^{t}\psi(r,\xi)\mathrm{d}r\right)\mathcal{F}[f(s,\cdot)](\xi)h(\varepsilon,t-s,T)\mathrm{d}s\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa(t-s)\right|\xi|^{\gamma})|\mathcal{F}[f(s,\cdot)](\xi)|h(\varepsilon,t-s,T)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(-\kappa s\right|\xi|^{\gamma})|\mathcal{F}[f(t-s.\cdot)](\xi)|h(\varepsilon,s,T)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(-2\kappa s\right|\xi|^{\gamma})h(\varepsilon,s,T)\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}|\mathcal{F}[f(t-s,\cdot)](\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right)^{1/2}\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\mathcal{F}[f(t,\cdot)](\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}t\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(-2\kappa s\right|\xi|^{\gamma})h(\varepsilon,s,T)\mathrm{d}s\right)^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi,

where

h(\varepsilon,s,T):=1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}1_{0<s<T}+1_{\varepsilon=1}.

By changing variables,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\exp\left(-2\kappa s\right|\xi|^{\gamma})h(\varepsilon,s,T)\mathrm{d}s=N|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s}h(\varepsilon,s,T|\xi|^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}s,

where
N=N(\kappa)
. If
\varepsilon=1
, then

|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s}h(\varepsilon,s,T|\xi|^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{d}s=1.

If
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
, then we split the estimate into two cases,
T|\xi|^{\gamma}\geq 1
and
0<T|\xi|^{\gamma}<1
.
If
T|\xi|^{\gamma}\geq 1
, then

|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s}h(\varepsilon,s,T|\xi|^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}s=|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{T|\xi|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{d}s\leq|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}.

If
0<T|\xi|^{\gamma}<1
, then

|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-s}h(\varepsilon,s,T|\xi|^{\gamma})\mathrm{d}s=|\xi|^{(\varepsilon-1)\gamma}\int_{0}^{T|\xi|^{\gamma}}\mathrm{e}^{-s}\mathrm{d}s\leq T|\xi|^{\varepsilon\gamma}\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}.

Therefore, by Plancherel’s theorem,

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{K}_{\varepsilon,T}f(t,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq NT^{2(1-\varepsilon)}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}[f(t,\cdot)](\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\mathrm{d}t=NT^{2(1-\varepsilon)}\|f\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})}^{2},

where
N=N(\kappa)
. The theorem is proved.
∎

5.
Weighted boundedness for a family of parabolic singular integral operators

Let
u
be a solution to (
3.4
) with a smooth
f
.
Due to the solution representation given in Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle u(t,x)=\mathcal{K}f(t,x)=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}p(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s

and

\displaystyle(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}u(t,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}P_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle:=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\frac{\varepsilon\gamma}{2}}p(t,s,x-y)1_{0<s<T}f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, to obtain a priori estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), we need to show the boundedness of the above integral operators in weighted
L_{p}
-spaces. For future applications and specifying kernel estimates used in the proofs, we consider kernels in a general setting.
More precisely, for
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
, we consider a family of complex-valued measurable functions
K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)
defined on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then we can consider corresponding (iterated) integral operators given by

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}1_{0<t-s<T}+1_{\varepsilon=1}\right)K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,y)f(s,x-y)\mathrm{d}y\right]\mathrm{d}s\qquad\forall f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

(5.1)

We say that the integral operator is (parabolic) singular if
\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s=\infty
for some
t>0
.
It is remarkable that (
5.1
) is well-defined even though the kernel
K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,y)
is singular.
A typical example of these kernels are classical heat kernels,
i.e.
,

K_{\varepsilon}(t,x,y)=\Delta^{\varepsilon}\left((2\pi t)^{d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-|x|^{2}/(2t)}\right).

In particular, the kernel
K_{1}(t,x,y)=\Delta\left((2\pi t)^{d/2}\mathrm{e}^{-|x|^{2}/(2t)}\right)
is singular.

Note that if the kernel
K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)
is not singular and the given weight is a constant, then the boundedness of the operator (
5.1
) is easily obtained on the basis of the generalized Minkowski inequality.
However, if a general weight is given, then the boundedness becomes non-trivial even though the kernel is not singular.
Therefore, we have to show the boundedness of the operators for all
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
based on weighted estimates, and the cut-off function
1_{|t-s|<T}
is necessary to overcome the lack of the integrability of the kernels at large
t
when they are not singular.
For a simplification of the notation, we denote

h(\varepsilon,s,T):=1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}1_{0<s<T}+1_{\varepsilon=1}

and

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}f(t,x):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\varepsilon,t-s,T)K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Since we can obtain a more exact upper bound of the integral operator related to
T
with the help of the cut-off function
h(\varepsilon,t-s,T)
, we separated it from the kernel
K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)
.

Definition 5.1

(Regularity condition on
K_{\varepsilon}
)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we say that a kernel

(or the operator
\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}
) satisfies the

-times regular condition with
(\gamma,N_{1},N_{2})
if there exist positive constants

,
N_{1}
, and

such that for all

,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|\leq N_{1}|t-s|^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}}

and

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x|^{2n}|\partial_{t}^{m}D^{\alpha}_{x}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}\leq N_{2}|t-s|^{-(m+\varepsilon)-\frac{(d+|\alpha|-n)}{\gamma}+\frac{d}{2\gamma}}.

Recall that for any Muckenhoupt’s weight

denotes the regularity constant of the weight

(Definition

Theorem 5.2

(A weighted extrapolation theorem)
.

Assume that there exists a constant

(i)

Let

and

Definition 6.2

Let

Definition 6.3
.

Let

\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{+}
. We say that

\inf_{A\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}|A|\to\infty\quad\text{as}\quad n\to-\infty

where

is the elements of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
containing
(t,x)
.

(ii)

For each

and

An example of filtration of partitions that will be used in the proof of Theorem

Lemma 6.6

By Fubini’s theorem and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|f\ast g(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\int_{R_{2}}^{r}|\nabla g(rw)\cdot w||f(y-\rho w)|\rho^{d-1}\mathrm{d}\rho\sigma(dw)\mathrm{d}r\leq\int_{R_{2}}^{\infty}I_{1}(r)I_{2}(y,r)\mathrm{d}r

Obviously,

\displaystyle=\int_{|z|\leq r}|f(y-z)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}z=\int_{|y-z|\leq r}|f(z)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}z\leq\int_{|x-z|\leq R_{1}+r}|f(z)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}z=\int_{B_{R_{1}+r}(x)}|f(z)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}z.

The lemma is proved.
∎

6.3.

estimates without weights for
p\in(1,2)

Recall

h(\varepsilon,t-s,T):=1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}1_{|t-s|<T}+1_{\varepsilon=1}

and

\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}f(t,x):=\int_{-\infty}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}h(\varepsilon,t-s,T)K_{\varepsilon}(t,s,x-y)f(s,y)\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

In this subsection, we prove the

without weights.
More precisely, we show the following theorem:

Theorem 6.9
.

Suppose that
K_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the
\left(\left\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\right\rfloor+1\right)

(\gamma,N_{1},N_{2})
(Definition
5.1

We first show that our kernel satisfies the important condition so-called Hörmander’s condition to guarantee the

6.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\varepsilon}(t,s_{0},x-y_{0})-K_{\varepsilon}(t,s_{0},x-y_{1})|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|y_{1}-y_{0}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}|\nabla_{x}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s_{0},x-y_{\theta})|\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-n}\sqrt{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla_{x}K_{\varepsilon}(t,s_{0},x)|\mathrm{d}x\leq N2^{-n}|t-s_{0}|^{-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{\gamma}},

where
N=N(d,N_{1},N_{2})
and
y_{\theta}:=\theta y_{0}+(1-\theta)y_{1}
. Therefore, since
s_{0}\in[i_{0}2^{-n\gamma},(i_{0}+1)2^{-n\gamma})
,

\displaystyle I_{3,1}(s_{0},y_{0},y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq N2^{-n}\int_{i_{0}2^{-n\gamma}+2^{-n\gamma+1}}^{\infty}|t-s_{0}|^{-\varepsilon-\frac{1}{\gamma}}(1_{0<t-s_{0}<T}1_{\varepsilon\in[0,1)}+1_{\varepsilon=1})\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq NT^{1-\varepsilon}2^{-n}\int_{i_{0}2^{-n\gamma}+2^{-n\gamma+1}}^{\infty}|t-s_{0}|^{-1-\frac{1}{\gamma}}\mathrm{d}t=NT^{1-\varepsilon},

where
N=N(d,\gamma,\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2})
.
Finally we claim that
I_{2,2}+I_{3,2}\leq NT^{1-\varepsilon}
.
Due to similarity, we only estimate
I_{3,2}
. For estimating
I_{3,2}
, we consider the two cases:
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
and
\varepsilon=1
.

Case 1.

\varepsilon\in[0,1)
.

By Fubini’s theorem and Theorem
6.1
,

\displaystyle I_{3,2}(s_{0},s_{1},y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{i_{0}2^{-n\gamma}+2^{-n\gamma+1}}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{\varepsilon}(t,s_{i},x)|\mathrm{d}x\right)h(\varepsilon,t-s_{i},T)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{i_{0}2^{-n\gamma}+2^{-n\gamma+1}}^{\infty}|t-s_{i}|^{-\varepsilon}1_{0<t-s_{i}<T}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq N\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{s_{i}}^{s_{i}+T}|t-s_{i}|^{-\varepsilon}\mathrm{d}t=NT^{1-\varepsilon},

where
N=N(d,\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2})
.

Case 2.

\varepsilon=1
.

By the fundamental theorem of calculus, Fubini’s theorem, and Theorem
6.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|K_{1}(t,s_{0},x-y_{1})-K_{1}(t,s_{1},x-y_{1})|\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq|s_{1}-s_{0}|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{t}K_{1}(t,s_{\theta},x-y_{1})|\mathrm{d}\theta\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-n\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\partial_{t}K_{1}(t,s_{\theta},x)|\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}\theta

\displaystyle\leq N2^{-n\gamma}\int_{0}^{1}|t-s_{\theta}|^{-2}\mathrm{d}\theta

where
N=N(d,N_{1},N_{2})
and
s_{\theta}:=(1-\theta)s_{0}+\theta s_{1}
. One can observe that for
s\in[i_{0}2^{-n\gamma},(i_{0}+1)2^{-n\gamma})
,

\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{|t-s|}{|t-s_{\theta}|}\leq 2,

Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{3,2}(s_{0},s_{1},y_{1})\leq N2^{-n\gamma}\int_{i_{0}2^{-n\gamma}+2^{-n\gamma+1}}^{\infty}|t-s|^{-2}\mathrm{d}t\leq N,

where
N=N(d,\gamma,N_{1},N_{2})
. The theorem is proved.
∎

Lemma 6.12

\zeta(y)=0

.

.

or
|y|<2b
without loss of generality.
Hence if
(s,y)\in Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)
and
|z|<b

|y-z|\leq 2b

f(r,y-z)=0
. By Lemma
6.6

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{\varepsilon}(s,r,y-z)f(r,z)\mathrm{d}z\right|

6.7
(i)
and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq T^{1-\varepsilon}\int_{t_{0}-3b^{\gamma}}^{s}\left(\int_{b}^{\infty}\lambda^{-p_{0}d(p_{0})-1}\int_{B_{2b+\lambda}(x)}|f(r,z)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}\lambda\right)^{1/p_{0}}|s-r|^{-1-\frac{(d-d(p_{0}))}{\gamma}+\frac{d}{p_{0}^{\prime}\gamma}}\mathrm{d}r

Theorem 6.14
.

There exists a constant
N=N(d,p_{0},\gamma,\varepsilon,N_{1},N_{2},N_{3})
such that for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

(\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}f)^{\sharp}(t,x)\leq NT^{1-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{M}|f|^{p_{0}}(t,x))^{1/p_{0}}.

Proof.

Let
b>0
,
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, and
(t,x)\in Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)
.
Choose a
\eta\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying

•

\eta(t)\in[0,1]
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

•

\eta(t)=1
for all

•

\eta(t)=0
for all

.

. Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}f(s_{1},y_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}f(s_{0},y_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta)(s_{1},y_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta)(s_{0},y_{0})|

\displaystyle\quad+|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta^{*})(s_{1},y_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta^{*})(s_{0},y_{0})|.

By Lemma
6.13
,

\displaystyle-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)}-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)}|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta)(s_{1},y_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta)(s_{0},y_{0})|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}s_{0}\leq NT^{p_{0}(1-\varepsilon)}\mathbb{M}|f|^{p_{0}}(t,x)

and by Lemma
6.12
,

\displaystyle-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)}-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)}|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta^{*})(s_{1},y_{1})-\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta^{*})(s_{0},y_{0})|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}y_{1}\mathrm{d}s_{1}\mathrm{d}y_{0}\mathrm{d}s_{0}

\displaystyle\leq 2-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{Q_{b}^{\gamma}(t_{0},0)}|\mathcal{T}_{\varepsilon,T}(f\eta^{*})(s,y)|^{p_{0}}\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}y\leq NT^{p_{0}(1-\varepsilon)}\mathbb{M}|f|^{p_{0}}(t,x).

Proof.

(i)

Note that
\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}
is a norm due to some properties of
L_{p}
-space.
Thus it suffices to prove the completeness. Suppose that
\{f_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subseteq\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
is a Cauchy sequence.

Case 1.

\nu\geq 0
.

By the completeness of
L_{p}
-spaces, there exist
f
and
g
in
L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}(\|f_{n}-f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+\|(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f_{n}-g\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)})=0.

For
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}((-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f(t,\cdot),\phi(t,\cdot))\mathrm{d}t=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(t,x)1_{0<t<T}(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}\phi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{n}(t,x)1_{0<t<T}(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}\phi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f_{n}(t,x)1_{0<t<T}\phi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}g(t,x)\phi(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

By Theorem
A.1

(v)
,
(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f1_{(0,T)}
is a bounded linear functional on
L_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},\bar{w})
. By virtue of Theorem
A.1

(vi)
,
(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f1_{(0,T)}\in L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1},w)
.
Therefore,

(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f1_{(0,T)}=g,

which certainly implies that
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
.

Case 2.

\nu<0
.

There exists
g\in L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f_{n}-g\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=0.

Denote

f:=(1-\Delta)^{-\nu/2}g\in\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w),

then
f_{n}\to f
in
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
.

(ii)
Let
f
be a bounded linear functional on
L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
. Then, it is easy to check that
fw^{-1/p}
is a bounded linear functional on
L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
, thus,
fw^{-1/p}\in L_{p^{\prime}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. This certainly implies that
f\in L_{p^{\prime}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w})
.

∎

Assumption A.3
.

For almost all
t\in[0,T]
,
w(t,\cdot)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\esssup_{t\in[0,T]}[w(t,\cdot)]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=:N_{0}<\infty.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, and
f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
.
Suppose that Assumption
A.3
holds.
Additionally, assume that

\sup_{R>0}\left(R^{2|\alpha|-d}\int_{R<|\xi|<2R}|D^{\alpha}_{\xi}m(\xi)|^{2}\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2}\leq N^{*},\quad\forall|\alpha|\leq d+1,

(A.2)

where
m(\xi)
is a function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then, there exists a constant
N=N(d,p,N^{*},N_{0})
such that

\|T_{m}f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w(t,\cdot))}\leq N\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},(w(t,\cdot))}\quad(t-a.e.),

Consider the operator

g\mapsto T_{m}g(x):=\mathcal{F}^{-1}[m\mathcal{F}[g(\cdot)]](x).

Then, by Theorem
[
10
, Theorem 6.2.7]
, the operator
T_{m}:L_{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is bounded.
Moreover, recall that
w(t,\cdot)\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

(t-a.e.)
.
Thus, applying
[
8
, Corollaries 6.10, 6.11, and Remark 6.14]
, we have, for almost all
t\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|T_{m}f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w(t,\cdot))}

\displaystyle\leq N[w(t,\cdot)]_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1\vee(p-1)^{-1}}[w(t,\cdot)^{-1/(p-1)}]_{A_{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{1\vee(p^{\prime}-1)^{-1}}\|f(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d},w(t,\cdot))}

where

. The lemma is proved.
∎

Theorem A.5
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)
,
w\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{R}
. Suppose that Assumption
A.3
holds.

(i)

For
\nu_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
(1-\Delta)^{\nu_{0}/2}
is a bijective mapping from
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
to
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu+\nu_{0}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
.

(ii)

If
\nu_{1}\geq\nu_{0}
, then
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{1}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
is continuously embedded into
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{0}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
. More precisely, there exists a positive constant
N
such that

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{0}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{1}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)},\quad\forall f\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

(iii)

If
\nu
is a nonnegative integer, then

\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\simeq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq\nu}\|D^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(iv)

The topological dual space of
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
is
\mathbb{H}_{p^{\prime}}^{-\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\bar{w})
, where

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1,\quad\bar{w}:=w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\in A_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1}).

Proof.

(i)
Recall the definition of
\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)
(Definition
2.7
(ii)).
Then it suffices to show that for all
\nu\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+\|(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\simeq\|f\|_{H_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(A.3)

We prove this equivalence of the two norms above by using Lemma
A.4
.
Let
\nu\geq 0
and put

m_{1}(\xi):=\frac{1}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu/2}},\quad m_{2}(\xi):=\frac{|\xi|^{\nu}}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu/2}},\quad m_{3}(\xi):=\frac{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu/2}}{1+|\xi|^{\nu}}

It is easy to check that
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
and
m_{3}
satisfy (
A.2
). Thus, by Lemma
A.4
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\|T_{m_{1}}(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)},

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\|T_{m_{2}}(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)},

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\|T_{m_{3}}(1+(-\Delta)^{\nu/2})f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(ii)
The result is an easy consequence of
(i)

Indeed, define
m_{4}(\xi):=(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu_{0}/2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\nu_{1}/2}
.
Then
m_{4}
satisfies (
A.2
).
Thus by
(ii)
and Lemma
A.4
,

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{0}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

\displaystyle\simeq\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu_{0}/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\|T_{m_{4}}(1-\Delta)^{\nu_{1}/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|(1-\Delta)^{\nu_{1}/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\simeq\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu_{1}}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.

(iii)
For a multi-index
\alpha
satisfying
|\alpha|\leq\nu
, we denote

m_{5}(\xi):=\frac{(i\xi)^{\alpha}}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\nu/2}}.

It is easy to show that (
A.2
) holds with
m=m_{5}
.
Thus by
(ii)
and Lemma
A.4
,

\begin{gathered}\|D^{\alpha}_{x}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}=\|T_{m_{5}}(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|f\|_{\mathbb{H}_{p}^{\nu}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}.\end{gathered}

To prove the other direction, it is sufficient to show

\|(1-\Delta)^{1/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+N\sum_{j=1}^{d}\|D_{x^{j}}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

(A.4)

due to the mathematical induction. By (
A.3
) and
(ii)
,

\displaystyle\|(1-\Delta)^{1/2}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|g\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+N\|\Delta g\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}

\displaystyle\leq N\|f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)}+N\sum_{i=1}^{d}\|T_{m^{i}}D_{x^{i}}f\|_{L_{p}((0,T)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},w)},

where
g:=(1-\Delta)^{-1/2}f
and

m^{i}(\xi):=\frac{i\xi^{i}}{(1+|\xi|^{2})^{1/2}},\quad i=1,2,\cdots,d.

One can check that
m^{i}
is a form of
m_{5}
. Thus (
A.2
) holds with

.
Finally, applying Lemma
A.4
again, we obtain (
A.4
).

(iv)
Due to (ii),
(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}(1-\Delta)^{-\nu/2}=(1-\Delta)^{-\nu/2}(1-\Delta)^{\nu/2}

(vi).
The theorem is proved.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
-d<\alpha<d(p-1)
and denote
w_{\alpha}(t,x):=(t^{2}+|x|^{2})^{\alpha/2}
.
Then there exists a positive constant

such that for any balls

and almost all

\displaystyle\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{\alpha}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{\alpha}(t,x)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{\alpha}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\left(-\hskip-11.38092pt\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}w_{-\alpha/(p-1)}(t,x)\mathrm{d}x\right)^{p-1}\leq N.

•

for

and
\alpha
. Therefore, (
A.6
) holds.

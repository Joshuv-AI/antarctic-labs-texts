Taming Genus 0 (or 1) components on variables-separated equations
Michael D. Fried

Abstract
. To describe curves of form 𝒞 f , g = def { ( x , y ) | f ⁡ ( x ) − g ⁡ ( y ) = 0 } {{\mathcal{C}}}_{f,g}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\rm def}}}{{=}}\{(x,y)|f(x)-g(y)=0\} and their number theory properties, you must address 𝒞 f , g {{\mathcal{C}}}_{f,g} whose projective normalization has a genus 0 (or 1) component . For f f and g g polynomials and f f indecomposable, [ Fr73a ] distinguished 𝒞 f , g {{\mathcal{C}}}_{f,g} with u = 1 u=1 versus u > 1 u>1 components (Schinzel’s problem). For u = 1 u=1 , [ Fr73b , (1.6) of Prop. 1] gave a direct genus formula. To complete u > 1 u>1 required an adhoc genus computation. [ Fr12 ] revisited later work. Pakovich [ Pak18b ] , an example, dropped the indecomposable and polynomial restrictions, but added 𝒞 f , g {{\mathcal{C}}}_{f,g} is irreducible ( u = 1 u=1 ). He showed – for fixed f f – unless the Galois closure of the cover for f f has genus 0 (or 1), the genus grows linearly in deg ⁡ ( g ) \deg(g) . Cor. 2.20 and Cor. 2.21 extend [ Fr73b , Prop. 1] and use Nielsen classes to generalize Pakovich’s formulation for u > 1 u>1 . Using the solution to the Davenport and Schinzel problems, Hurwitz families track the significance of these components, an approach motivated by Riemann’s relating θ \theta functions and half-canonical classes.

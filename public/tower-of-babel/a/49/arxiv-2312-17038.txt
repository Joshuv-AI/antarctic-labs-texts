Irreducible representations of the Poincaré group with reflections and two-fold Wigner degeneracy
Dharam Vir Ahluwalia, G. B. de Gracia, Julio M. Hoff da Silva, Cheng-Yang Lee, B. M. Pimentel

Abstract
Not all complete set of spinors can be used as expansion coefficients of a quantum field. In fact, Steven Weinberg established the uniqueness of Dirac spinors for this purpose provided: (a) one paid due attention to the multiplicative phases for each of the spinors, and (b) one paired these to creation and annihilation operators in a specific manner. This is implicit in his implementation of the rotational symmetry for the spin half quantum field. Among the numerous complete set of spinors that are available to a physicist, Elko occupies a unique status that allows it to enter as expansion coefficients of a quantum field without violating Weinberg’s no go theorem. How this paradigm changing claim arises is the primary subject of this communication. Weinberg’s no go theorem is evaded by exploiting a uniquely special feature of Elko that allows us to introduce a doubling of the particle-antiparticle degrees of freedom from four to eight. Weinberg had dismissed this degeneracy on the ground that, “no examples are known of particles that furnish unconventional representations of inversions.” Here we will find that this degeneracy, once envisioned by Eugene Wigner, in fact gives rise to a quantum field that has all the theoretical properties required of dark matter. We are deeply saddened to have lost our beloved friend and long-time collaborator Dharam Vir Ahluwalia who has initiated and contributed so much to the theory of mass dimension one fields. His spirit will forever be with us.

3.1
Charge-conjugation

Let
\mathbf{C}
be the charge-conjugation operator. By definition, its action is to map the particle states to the anti-particle states and vice versa. Since charge-conjugation is an internal symmetry, its action on the states
\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau,a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
and
\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau,b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
cannot alter their eigenvalues
\tau
with respect to
\widehat{J}_{z}
. But due to the two-fold degeneracy as elucidated in (
93
-
94
), it may act non-trivially by taking a state, say
\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,1,a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
to a linear combination of
\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,1,b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
and
\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,3,b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}
. Therefore, the most general charge-conjugation transformation for the states at rest are

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau,a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau^{\prime},b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}},

(101)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau,b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathbb{\overline{D}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}k,\tau^{\prime},a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}.

(102)

Boosting to arbitrary momentum, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}p,\tau,a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathbb{D}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}p,\tau^{\prime},b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}},

(103)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}p,\tau,b\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\mathbb{\overline{D}}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})\mathopen{\hbox{${|}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-2.49998pt\hbox{${|}$}}p,\tau^{\prime},a\mathclose{\hbox{${\rangle}$}\mkern 2.0mu\kern-3.49998pt\hbox{${\rangle}$}}.

(104)

As charge-conjugation is an internal symmetry, the matrices
\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{C})
and
\overline{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{C})
must commute with the block-diagonal rotation generator (
90
). Solving the commutators, we obtain

\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{C})=\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}\mathbb{I}&c_{2}\mathbb{I}\\
c_{3}\mathbb{I}&c_{4}\mathbb{I}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\overline{\mathbb{D}}(\mathbf{C})=\left(\begin{matrix}\bar{c}_{1}\mathbb{I}&\bar{c}_{2}\mathbb{I}\\
\bar{c}_{3}\mathbb{I}&\bar{c}_{4}\mathbb{I}\end{matrix}\right).

(105)

Transform back to the original states,

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}|p,\tau,a\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})|p,\tau^{\prime},b\rangle,

(106)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}|p,\tau,b\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\overline{D}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})|p,\tau^{\prime},a\rangle,

(107)

where

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{C})

\displaystyle=S\mathbb{D}(\mathbf{C})S^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{matrix}(c_{1}+c_{4})&-i(c_{2}+c_{3})&(c_{2}-c_{3})&i(c_{1}-c_{4})\\
i(c_{2}+c_{3})&(c_{1}+c_{4})&-i(c_{1}-c_{4})&(c_{2}-c_{3})\\
-(c_{2}-c_{3})&i(c_{1}-c_{4})&(c_{1}+c_{4})&i(c_{2}+c_{3})\\
-i(c_{1}-c_{4})&-(c_{2}-c_{3})&-i(c_{2}+c_{3})&(c_{1}+c_{4})\end{matrix}\right],

(108)

\displaystyle\overline{D}(\mathbf{C})

\displaystyle=D(\mathbf{C})|_{c_{i}\rightarrow\bar{c}_{i}}.

(109)

We may now derive the charge-conjugation transformation for the mass dimension one field.
Using (
106
-
107
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}a(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\mathbf{C}^{-1}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D^{*}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})b(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau^{\prime}),

(110)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}b^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\mathbf{C}^{-1}=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}\overline{D}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})a^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau^{\prime}),

(111)

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}\lambda_{\ell}(t,\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{x}$})\mathbf{C}^{-1}=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2mE_{p}}}\sum_{\tau,\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\big[D^{*}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})b(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau^{\prime})\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)e^{-ip\cdot x}

\displaystyle+\overline{D}_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})a^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau^{\prime})\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)e^{ip\cdot x}\big].

(112)

Charge-conjugation is a symmetry because
\mathbf{C}\lambda(x)\mathbf{C}^{-1}
can be shown to be equal to
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}(x)
up to a constant matrix. To prove this, we define
\mathscr{C}=\gamma^{0}\gamma^{2}
and use the following identities

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1)=+\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4),

(113)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2)=-\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3),

(114)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3)=+\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2),

(115)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4)=-\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1),

(116)

and

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\zeta}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1)=-s\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4),

(117)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\zeta}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2)=+s\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3),

(118)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\zeta}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3)=-s\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2),

(119)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\zeta}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4)=+s\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1).

(120)

Rewrite them in compact forms

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=f_{c}(\tau)\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\nu_{c}(\tau)),

(121)

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\zeta}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=\overline{f}_{c}(\tau)\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\overline{\nu}_{c}(\tau)),

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(x)=\Omega_{C}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2mE_{p}}}\sum_{\tau}

\displaystyle\big[a^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)f_{c}(\tau)\zeta_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\nu_{c}(\tau))e^{ip\cdot x}

\displaystyle+b(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\overline{f}_{c}(\tau)\xi_{\ell}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\overline{\nu}_{c}(\tau))e^{-ip\cdot x}\big].

(123)

Multiply (
123

and equate it to
\mathbf{C}\lambda(x)\mathbf{C}^{-1}

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle=-c_{4}=is\Omega^{*}_{C},

\displaystyle\quad c_{2,3}=0,

(124)

\displaystyle\bar{c}_{1}

\displaystyle=-\bar{c}_{4}=-i\Omega_{C},

\displaystyle\quad\bar{c}_{2,3}=0,

(125)

and hence

D(\mathbf{C})=-is\Omega^{*}_{C}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{O}&\sigma_{y}\\
\sigma_{y}&\mathbb{O}\end{matrix}\right),\quad\overline{D}(\mathbf{C})=i\Omega_{C}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{O}&\sigma_{y}\\
\sigma_{y}&\mathbb{O}\end{matrix}\right).

(126)

Therefore, the charge-conjugation transformation of the field reads

\mathbf{C}\lambda_{\ell}(x)\mathbf{C}^{-1}=\Omega_{C}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}\mathscr{C}_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(x).

(127)

\mathbf{T}^{2}=-1
.

Additional information on the intrinsic parity phase can be obtained by considering the product of charge-conjugation and parity. Since
\mathbf{CP}
is unitary, it is related to
\mathbf{PC}
by

\mathbf{CP}=\epsilon\mathbf{PC},

(206)

where
\epsilon
is a phase to be determined. Multiply (
206
) from the right by
\mathbf{P}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{CP}^{2}

\displaystyle=\epsilon\mathbf{PCP}

\displaystyle=\epsilon^{2}\mathbf{P^{2}C}.

(207)

Since
\mathbf{P}^{2}
is proportional to the identity, it commutes with
\mathbf{C}
thus giving us
\mathbf{CP}^{2}=\epsilon^{2}\mathbf{CP}^{2}
. Therefore, we must have
\epsilon=\pm 1
so
\mathbf{C}
and
\mathbf{P}
must either commute or anti-commute. Explicit computations of
\mathbf{CP}
and
\mathbf{PC}
for mass dimension one bosons and fermions reveals that the intrinsic parity
\Omega_{P}
must be either real or imaginary so
\mathbf{P}^{2}=\pm\mathbb{I}
. We find, for both bosons and fermions

\displaystyle\{\mathbf{C},\mathbf{P}\}=\mathbb{O},

\displaystyle\quad\Omega_{P}=+\Omega^{*}_{P},

(208)

\displaystyle[\mathbf{C},\mathbf{P}]=\mathbb{O},

\displaystyle\quad\Omega_{P}=-\Omega^{*}_{P}.

(209)

Acting
(\mathbf{CPT})^{2}
on the states, we find

\displaystyle(\mathbf{CPT})^{2}|p,\tau,a\rangle=s(\Omega_{C}\Omega_{P}\Omega_{T})^{2}|p,\tau,a\rangle,

(210)

\displaystyle(\mathbf{CPT})^{2}|p,\tau,b\rangle=s(\Omega^{*}_{C}\Omega^{*}_{P}\Omega^{*}_{P})^{2}|p,\tau,b\rangle.

(211)

4
Irreducibility and Wigner degeneracy

The continuous and discrete symmetry transformations for the particle states are

U(R)|p,\tau,a\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(R)|Rp,\tau^{\prime},a\rangle,

(212)

where
D(R)=\exp(i\bm{J\cdot\theta})
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}|p,\tau,a\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{C})|p,\tau^{\prime},b\rangle,

(213)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}|p,\tau,a\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{P})|Pp,\tau^{\prime},a\rangle,

(214)

\displaystyle\mathbf{T}|p,\tau,a\rangle=\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}D_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}(\mathbf{T})|Pp,\tau^{\prime},a\rangle,

(215)

where
D
are
4\times 4
matrices. The question arises as to whether these matrices are irreducible. That is, does there exists a unitary basis transformation which renders all the
D
matrices simultaneously block-diagonal? If such a transformation exist, it would mean that our construct is reducible and the states do not describe elementary particles. We now show explicitly that such a transformation does not exist.

4.1
Irreducibility

Let us take transformation on the state
|p,\tau,a\rangle
to be

|p,\tau,a\rangle\rightarrow\sum_{\tau^{\prime}}Q_{\tau^{\prime}\tau}|p,\tau^{\prime},a\rangle,

(216)

where
Q
is a unitary matrix. Because
\mathbf{C}
,
\mathbf{P}
are linear and
\mathbf{T}
is anti-linear, their basis transformations associated with (
216
) are

\bm{J}\rightarrow Q^{-1}\bm{J}Q,

(217)

and

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{C})\rightarrow Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{C})Q,

(218)

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{P})\rightarrow Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{P})Q,

(219)

\displaystyle D(\mathbf{T})\rightarrow Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{T})Q^{*}.

(220)

To this end, we are able to make
\bm{J}
,
D(\mathbf{P})
and
D(\mathbf{T})
block-diagonal
by choosing

Q^{-1}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{I}+\sigma_{y}&\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{y}\\
\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{y}&-\mathbb{I}-\sigma_{y}\end{matrix}\right).

(221)

The results are

Q^{-1}\bm{J}Q=\frac{1}{2}\left(\begin{matrix}\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}$}&\mathbb{O}\\
\mathbb{O}&\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{\sigma}$}\end{matrix}\right),

(222)

and

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(\mathbf{C})=Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{C})Q=is\Omega^{*}_{C}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{O}&\mathbb{I}\\
\mathbb{I}&\mathbb{O}\end{matrix}\right),

(223)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(\mathbf{P})=Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{P})Q=\Omega^{*}_{P}\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{I}&\mathbb{O}\\
\mathbb{O}&-\mathbb{I}\end{matrix}\right),

(224)

\displaystyle D^{\prime}(\mathbf{T})=Q^{-1}D(\mathbf{T})Q^{*}=\Omega^{*}_{T}\left(\begin{matrix}\sigma_{y}&\mathbb{O}\\
\mathbb{O}&\sigma_{y}\end{matrix}\right),

(225)

so
D^{\prime}(\mathbf{C})
is still block off-diagonal. For the representations to be irreducible, it suffices for us to show that
D^{\prime}(\mathbf{C})
and
D^{\prime}(\mathbf{P})
cannot be simultaneously made block-diagonal by a unitary transformation. Let

R^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}W&X\\
Y&Z\end{matrix}\right),

(226)

where
W,X,Y,Z
are
2\times 2
matrices. Unitarity
RR^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{I}
demands

\displaystyle WW^{{\dagger}}+XX^{{\dagger}}=YY^{{\dagger}}+ZZ^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{I},

(227)

\displaystyle WY^{{\dagger}}+XZ^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{O}.

(228)

From

\displaystyle R^{-1}D^{\prime}(\mathbf{C})R=\left[\begin{matrix}(XW^{{\dagger}}+WX^{{\dagger}})&(XY^{{\dagger}}+WZ^{{\dagger}})\\
(ZW^{{\dagger}}+YX^{{\dagger}})&(ZY^{{\dagger}}+YZ^{{\dagger}})\end{matrix}\right],

(229)

\displaystyle R^{-1}D^{\prime}(\mathbf{P})R=\left[\begin{matrix}(WW^{{\dagger}}-XX^{{\dagger}})&(WY^{{\dagger}}-XZ^{{\dagger}})\\
(YW^{{\dagger}}-ZX^{{\dagger}})&(YY^{{\dagger}}-ZZ^{{\dagger}})\end{matrix}\right],

(230)

and requiring them to be block-diagonal yields

\displaystyle XY^{{\dagger}}+WZ^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{O},

(231)

\displaystyle WY^{{\dagger}}-XZ^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{O}.

(232)

Comparing (
228
) with (
232
), it follows that

WY^{{\dagger}}=XZ^{{\dagger}}=\mathbb{O}.

(233)

In order for
Q
to be invertible, we require

W\neq\mathbb{O},\quad Z\neq\mathbb{O},\quad X=Y=\mathbb{O},

(234)

or

X\neq\mathbb{O},\quad Y\neq\mathbb{O},\quad W=Z=\mathbb{O}.

(235)

Imposing either constraints on
R^{-1}D^{\prime}(\mathbf{C})R
, we find that in both cases it cannot be made block diagonal. Therefore, the particle states furnish an irreducible representation of the extended Poincaré group.

5
Pseudo Hermitian quantum field theory

We have thus far established that the mass dimension one fields respect Lorentz symmetry, are local and irreducible with two-fold Wigner degeneracy. With all these desirable features, there remains one important issue that we have to address. Because
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}
is not equal to
\lambda^{{\dagger}}\gamma^{0}
, the product
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda
while being a Lorentz scalar, is
non-Hermitian
. This is not an issue for the free field theory as the free Hamiltonian is Hermitian and positive-definite.
6
6

6

Similarly, the three-momentum operators are also Hermitian and positive-definite. Since the free Lagrangian density is a Lorentz scalar, we expect the free Lorentz generators to also be Hermitian.
However, the interacting densities constructed from the free fields
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda
in the interacting picture would be non-Hermitian. Therefore, the interacting theory may not be unitary.

To solve this problem, we now take, what is in our opinion, the first step towards developing a consistent
S
-matrix theory for mass dimension one fields.

7

7

A formalism for computing scattering amplitudes and physical observables utilizing the Elko dual along with the generalized unitarity relation was proposed in
Ahluwalia:2022ttu
. However, the proposed formalism is incomplete as it has not taken the Wigner degeneracy and pseudo Hermiticity into account.
Here and in app.
B

Mostafazadeh:2008pw

S

\tau

\displaystyle\eta|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau,a\rangle

\displaystyle=-\vartheta(\tau)|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\rho(\tau),a\rangle,

(275)

\displaystyle\eta|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau,b\rangle

\displaystyle=+\vartheta(\tau)|\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\rho(\tau),b\rangle,

(276)

and

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau,a|\eta

\displaystyle=+\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\rho(\tau),a|\vartheta(\tau),

(277)

\displaystyle\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau,b|\eta

\displaystyle=-\langle\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\rho(\tau),b|\vartheta(\tau).

(280)

6
Conclusions

After more than almost three decades studying Majorana spinors followed by Elko and mass dimension one fields, we have now established their foundations. The mass dimension one fields and their particle states furnish the unitary irreducible representations of the extended Poincaré group.

Comparing to the earlier formulations of the theory, there are important similarities as well as differences. The orthonormal norms and spin-sums of Elko are identical to the ones given under
\tau
-deformation
Ahluwalia:2022ttu
. The difference, which is one of the main results of this paper, is the doubling of degrees of freedom from two to four for the particles as well as the anti-particles. From the discrete symmetry analysis, this is precisely the degeneracy induced by the inclusion of non-trivial discrete symmetries studied by Wigner
wigner1964unitary
and later expanded by Weinberg
(
Weinberg:1995mt
, Sec. 2 C)
. Upon identifying the correct degrees of freedom, it is a straightforward to verify Lorentz covariance for the mass dimension one fields.

There remains much to be done. In the spin-half
\mathcal{R}\oplus\mathcal{L}
representation, the mass dimension one fields constructed from Elko may only be one of the many non-trivial solutions. If more solutions exists, we believe that they can be systematically classified by their discrete symmetries. More generally, one should study quantum field theory with Wigner degeneracy for higher-spin representations of the extended Poincaré group. In the case of Elko and the associated mass dimension one fields, the extension to higher-spin should be straightforward following
Lee:2012td
.

Phenomenologically, we believe that the mass dimension one bosons and fermions are natural dark matter candidates. Due to the mismatch in mass dimensions with the SM fermions (for mass dimension one bosons, there is also a mismatch in statistics), they cannot form doublets or multiplets with the SM fermions. As singlets, mass dimension one particles can only interact with the SM fermions via gravity, Higgs or dark gauge fields thus making them natural candidates as self-interacting dark matter. On the other hand, if there are doublets or multiplets comprised of mass dimension one fields, they may interact with the SM sector through the SM gauge fields. We leave these important phenomenological studies for future investigations.

There are two interactions that distinguish the spin-half mass dimension one fields from their Dirac counterparts. Due to their mass dimensions, the mass dimension one fields are naturally endowed with a renormalizable quartic self-interaction
g(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda)^{2}
. There is also quadratic couplings to neutral and charged scalar fields of the form
g^{\prime}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda)\phi^{2}
and
g^{\prime\prime}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda)\Phi^{{\dagger}}\Phi
. Interactions with the scalar fields provide a natural portal for mass dimension one particles to interact with the SM sector through the Higgs boson. We note, as the Dirac field
\psi
has mass dimension three-half, its quartic self-interaction would be of dimension six and is thus non-renormalizable in four space-time dimensions. The natural interaction between the Dirac and a neutral scalar field is the Yukawa interaction of the form
y\overline{\psi}\psi\phi
. Interactions of the form
y^{\prime}\overline{\psi}\psi\phi^{2}
and
y^{\prime\prime}\overline{\psi}\psi\Phi^{{\dagger}}\Phi
are of mass dimension five so they are non-renormalizable perturbatively.

The interactions that we have written down in the preceding paragraph involving the mass dimension one fields all share a common feature - They are all pseudo Hermitian. As a result, the Hermitian inner-product would not be invariant under time translation generated by these pseudo Hermitian Hamiltonians. This problem is not new and has been studied by Bender, Mostafazadeh, and collaborators
Bender:1998gh
;
Bender:1998ke
;
Bender:2002vv
;
Bender:2007nj
;
Mostafazadeh:2001jk
;
Mostafazadeh:2008pw
. There, the resolution is to introduce new inner-product that is invariant under time translation. For mass dimension one fields, defining the appropriate inner-product that preserves unitarity (in a generalized sense) is essential towards establishing the formalism to compute the
S
-matrix and physical observables. For now, this task remains incomplete and requires further investigations.

To the best of our knowledge, what we have presented here, for the first time, is a physical quantum field theory which realizes Wigner degeneracy with possibly important ramifications for physics beyond the SM.
While we have only studied the kinematics of the mass dimension one fields, resolving the problem of rotational covariance is an important milestone towards the ultimate goals of establishing consistent interacting theories and phenomenological models. It is our hope that one day, in the foreseeable future, we can make the definitive statement -
Dark matter are described by the unitary irreducible representations of the extended Poincaré group with Wigner degeneracy.
This we believe, would be the best tributes we can give to Professor Eugene Wigner and Professor Steven Weinberg, who have contributed so much to our understanding of nature.

Appendix A
Reducible Hermitian formulation

With the introduction of Wigner degeneracy, the Elko spin sums, as computed with the Elko duals (
32a
-
32b
), are Lorentz-invariant. Here, we explore what happens to Elko and the quantum fields, when we instead formulate the theory using the Dirac dual. As we will show, while the resulting quantum field theory is local and respects Lorentz symmetry, it furnishes a reducible representation of the inhomogeneous Lorentz group. Specifically, it is a direct sum of two irreducible representations, where each irreducible sector is described by the mass dimension one fields with Klein-Gordon kinematics.

Under the Dirac dual, we have

\overline{\xi}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=\xi^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\gamma^{0},\quad\overline{\zeta}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=s\zeta^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\gamma^{0}.

(281)

The spin sums are given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau}\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\overline{\xi}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu},

(282)

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau}\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\overline{\zeta}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)

\displaystyle=s\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}.

(283)

For now, we shall ignore the fact that the norms vanish with respect to the Dirac dual and proceed to construct a quantum field theory with
\lambda
and
\overline{\lambda}=\lambda^{{\dagger}}\gamma^{0}
. From (
36a
-
36d
), we know that Elko do not satisfy the Dirac equation. We rewrite these identities as

\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=m\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau),\quad\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu}\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)=-m\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau).

(284)

where

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1)=+i\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2),\quad\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1)=+i\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2),

(285)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2)=-i\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1),\quad\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},2)=-i\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},1),

(286)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3)=-i\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4),\quad\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3)=-i\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4),

(287)

\displaystyle\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4)=+i\xi(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3),\quad\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},4)=+i\zeta(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},3).

(288)

Next, we define a new quantum field

\lambda^{\prime}(x)=(2\pi)^{-3/2}\int\frac{d^{3}p}{\sqrt{2E}}\sum_{\tau}\left[e^{-ip\cdot x}\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)a(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)+e^{ip\cdot x}\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)b^{{\dagger}}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\right],

(289)

so that the action of the Dirac operator on
\lambda
yields

i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda(x)=m\lambda^{\prime}(x).

(290)

Repeat the above calculations with
\xi^{\prime}
and
\zeta^{\prime}
, we find

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau}\xi^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\overline{\xi}^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)

\displaystyle=\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu},

(291)

\displaystyle\sum_{\tau}\zeta^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)\overline{\zeta}^{\prime}(\mbox{\boldmath$\displaystyle\bm{p}$},\tau)

\displaystyle=s\gamma^{\mu}p_{\mu},

(292)

and

i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}(x)=m\lambda(x).

(293)

The fields
\lambda
and
\lambda^{\prime}
do not satisfy the Dirac equation. But by combining them, we can introduce a set of eight-component fields, namely

\Lambda(x)=\left[\begin{matrix}\lambda(x)\\
\lambda^{\prime}(x)\end{matrix}\right],\quad\overline{\Lambda}(x)=\left[\begin{matrix}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(x)&\overline{\lambda}(x)\end{matrix}\right],

(294)

such that
\Lambda
satisfies the Dirac equation

\left(i\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-M\right)\Lambda(x)=\mathbb{O},

(295)

where

\Gamma^{\mu}=\left(\begin{matrix}\mathbb{O}&\gamma^{\mu}\\
\gamma^{\mu}&\mathbb{O}\end{matrix}\right),\quad M=\left(\begin{matrix}m\mathbb{I}&\mathbb{O}\\
\mathbb{O}&m\mathbb{I}\end{matrix}\right).

(296)

Therefore, we obtain the following Lagrangian density

\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}=\overline{\Lambda}\left(i\Gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-M\right)\Lambda.

(297)

Using (
297
), we can readily show that the fields and their conjugate momenta satisfy the canonical commutation/anti-commutation relations and that the free Hamiltonian is positive-definite.

Decomposing the Lagrangian density

The fields
\Lambda
and
\overline{\Lambda}
seem to present a new quantum field theory that are physically distinct to
\lambda
and
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}
owing to the difference in kinematics and mass dimensions. However, these differences turn out to be superficial. Here, we show that the Lagrangian density
\Lambda
and
\overline{\Lambda}
decomposes into a sum of two physically equivalent Lagrangian densities of mass dimension one fields. Therefore, the field theory as described by
\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}
is reducible.

To prove our statement, let us write the free Lagrangian density of
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}
and
\lambda
as

\mathscr{L}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}},\lambda)=\left(\partial^{\mu}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\partial_{\mu}\lambda-m^{2}\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda\right).

(298)

Because of the
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}\lambda
product, the Lagrangian density is pseudo Hermitian. Despite the pseudo-Hermiticity, as we have shown in
2.2
, the theory is local, respects Lorentz symmetry, and the free Hamiltonian is Hermitian and positive-definite. These features, along with the results given in sec.
3
tell us that
298
is the correct irreducible Lagrangian density for mass dimension one fields. By irreducible, we mean that
\mathscr{L}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}},\lambda)
cannot be decomposed into a sum of Lagrangian densities consisting of other independent fields.

We will now demonstrate that
\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}
is reducible. For this purpose, we first use the identity
\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}}=\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}
to rewrite
\mathscr{L}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}},\lambda)
as

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}(\overset{\>{}^{{}^{{{\neg}}}}}{\smash[t]{\lambda}},\lambda)=\mathscr{L}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime},\lambda)=\partial^{\mu}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\partial_{\mu}\lambda-m^{2}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda

(299)

Next, we add a Hermitian conjugate to
\mathscr{L}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime},\lambda)
to obtain a Hermitian Lagrangian density

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime},\lambda)+\mathscr{L}^{{\dagger}}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime},\lambda)

\displaystyle=\left(\partial^{\mu}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\partial_{\mu}\lambda-m^{2}\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda\right)+\left(\partial^{\mu}\overline{\lambda}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}-m^{2}\overline{\lambda}\lambda^{\prime}\right).

(300)

As the first and second term in (
300
) are physically equivalent,
\mathscr{L}_{h}
is reducible. That is, for the purpose of studying mass dimension one fields,
\mathscr{L}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime},\lambda)
suffices. The reason for constructing a reducible
\mathscr{L}_{h}
is to show that it is equal to
m\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}
up to total derivative terms. Integrating
\mathscr{L}_{h}
by parts and ignore the total derivative terms, we obtain

\mathscr{L}_{h}=-\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\lambda-\overline{\lambda}(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\lambda^{\prime}.

(301)

Using the identity

(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\mathbb{I}=(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m\mathbb{I})(-i\gamma^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}+m\mathbb{I}),

(302)

the first term in (
301
) can be written as

\displaystyle-\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\lambda

\displaystyle=-\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)(-i\gamma^{\nu}\partial_{\nu}+m)\lambda

\displaystyle=-\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)(-m\lambda^{\prime}+m\lambda)

\displaystyle=m\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)\lambda^{\prime}-m\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)\lambda

\displaystyle=m\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m)\lambda^{\prime}-m^{2}(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime}+\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda)

\displaystyle=m\left(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}-m\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda\right).

(303)

Repeating the same calculations for the second term of
\mathscr{L}_{h}
, we find

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}_{h}

\displaystyle=m\left[\left(\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\prime}-m\overline{\lambda}^{\prime}\lambda\right)+\left(\overline{\lambda}i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda-m\overline{\lambda}\lambda^{\prime}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=m\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}.

(304)

Since
\mathscr{L}_{h}
is reducible, it follows that
\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda}
is also reducible.

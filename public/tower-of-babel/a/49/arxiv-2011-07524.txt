1.
Introduction

Given a vector field
\mathbf{b}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the following Dirichlet problems for elliptic equations of second-order:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Div(A\nabla u)+\Div(u\mathbf{b})&=f&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.1)

and

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Div(A^{T}\nabla v)-\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v&=g&&\quad\text{in }\Omega,\\
v&=0&&\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

(1.2)

on a bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. Here
A=(a^{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n^{2}}
is a given matrix valued function. We assume that
A
is bounded and uniformly elliptic, that is, there exist
0<\delta<1
and
K>1
such that

\delta|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\quad\text{and}\quad|A(x)|\leq K\quad\text{for all }\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\text{a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(1.3)

Suppose that
g\in W^{-1,q}(\Omega)
for some
1<q<\infty
. For
1<p<\infty
, we say that
v\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of the problem (
1.2
) if

\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v\in L^{1}(\Omega)^{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\phi\,dx=\left<g,\phi\right>

(1.4)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Here
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
denotes the set of all smooth functions with compact support in
\Omega
. Weak solution for (
1.1
) can be similarly defined.

When
\mathbf{b}=\mathbf{0}
, Meyers
[
22
]
proved that if
\Omega
is a bounded smooth domain in

, then there exists

such that for

, then there exists a unique weak solution

of (
1.1
). Moreover, we have

\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}

for some constant

. Here

is the Hölder conjugate to
p_{0}
defined by
p_{0}^{\prime}=p_{0}/(p_{0}-1)
. For several decades, many authors have studied
W^{1,p}
-estimate for problems (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) under various assumptions on

and
\Omega

[
2
,
3

4

6
]
e.g.

One naturally asks the solvability of the problems (

) and (
1.2
) when

is unbounded. To explain the results, we fix a number

(i) When

(ii) Suppose that

2.1.
Lipschitz domain

This subsection is based on the classical paper Verchota
[
29
]
(see also
[
26
,
27
,
28
,
23
]
). A bounded domain
\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
Lipschitz
if for each
x\in\partial\Omega
, there exist a rectangular coordinate system,
(x^{\prime},x_{n})
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, a neighborhood
U=U(x)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
containing
x
and a Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi_{x}=\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\Omega\cap U=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>\varphi(x^{\prime})\}\cap U
.

By a
cylinder

Z=Z_{r}(x)
, we mean an open, right circular, doubly truncated cylinder centered at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius equal to
r
. We say that
Z=Z_{r}(x)
,
x\in\partial\Omega
is a
coordinate cylinder
for
\Omega
if

(i)

the bases of
Z
are some positive distance from
\partial\Omega
;

(ii)

there exists a rectangular coordinate system for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(x^{\prime},x_{n})
,
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
,
x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
, with
x_{n}
-axis containing the axis of
Z
;

(iii)

there exists a Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi=\varphi_{Z}:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
Z\cap\Omega=Z\cap\{(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:y_{n}>\varphi(x^{\prime})\}
and
x=(0,\varphi(0))
.

The pair
(Z,\varphi)
is said to be a
coordinate pair
. Under this coordinate system, we may take our coordinate cylinder
Z_{r}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|y^{\prime}-x^{\prime}|<r,\,|y_{n}-x_{n}|<(1+M)r\}
, where
M
is the Lipschitz constant of
\varphi
.

Since
\partial\Omega
is compact, we fix a covering of the boundary
\partial\Omega
by coordinate cylinders
\{Z_{r_{i}}(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
so that for
1\leq i\leq N
,
(Z_{i},\varphi_{i})
and
(Z_{i}^{*},\varphi_{i}
) are coordinate pair, where
Z^{*}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})=Z_{100r_{i}\sqrt{1+M^{2}}}(x_{i})
and
M=\max_{1\leq i\leq N}\|{\nabla\varphi_{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}
. Define
R_{0}=\min\{r_{i}:i=1,\dots,N\}
. The totality of parameters

Z_{k},\varphi_{k},\|{\nabla\varphi_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})},r_{k},1\leq k\leq N

are said to determine the
Lipschitz character
of
\Omega
.

It is easy to see that for each
x\in\partial\Omega
, we have
B_{R_{0}}(x)\subset Z^{*}_{r_{i}}(x_{i})
for some
i
. From this, we can show that there exists a constant
c>0
depending on the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that

|B_{R}(x)\setminus\Omega|\geq c|B_{R}(x)|

(2.1)

for all
x\in\partial\Omega
and
0<R\leq R_{0}
.

The following approximation of bounded Lipschitz domain by smooth domains can be found in Verchota
[
29
, Theorem 1.12]
, see Appendix of Verchota
[
28
]
for the proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Omega

\mathbb{R}^{n}

Z
and sequence of

2.2.
Lorentz spaces and basic estimates for drifts terms

Let
\Omega
be any domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
. For a measurable function
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, the
distribution function
of
f
is defined by

\mu_{f}(t)=|\{x\in\Omega:|f(x)|>t\}|\quad(t\geq 0).

The decreasing rearrangement of
f
, denoted by
f^{*}(t)
, is defined by

f^{*}(t)=\inf\{\alpha\geq 0:\mu_{f}(\alpha)\leq t\}.

For
1<p<\infty
and
1\leq q\leq\infty
, we define

\|{f}\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}=\begin{cases}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)\right)^{q}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}&\quad\text{if }q<\infty,\\
\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}f^{*}(t)&\quad\text{if }q=\infty.\end{cases}

The set of all
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
\|{f}\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}<\infty
is denoted by
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
and is called the
Lorentz space
with indices
p
and
q
.

The following Hölder inequality and refined Sobolev inequality in Lorentz spaces can be found in standard literatures (see e.g.
[
1
,
16
]
and references therein).

Proposition 2.2
.

(i)

Let
f\in L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)
and
g\in L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)
, where
1<p_{1},p_{2}<\infty
satisfy
1/p=1/p_{1}+1/p_{2}\leq 1
. Assume further that
1/q_{1}+1/q_{2}\geq 1
if
q=1
. Then

fg\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\|{fg}\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}\leq C(p)\|{f}\|_{L^{p_{1},q_{1}}(\Omega)}\|{g}\|_{L^{p_{2},q_{2}}(\Omega)}

for any
q\geq 1
with
1/q_{1}+1/q_{2}\geq 1/q
.

(ii)

Let
1\leq p<n
. Then for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we have

u\in L^{p^{*},p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\|{u}\|_{L^{p^{*},p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C(n,p)\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Using Hölder’s inequality and refined Sobolev’s inequality in Lorentz spaces, we can prove the following basic estimate.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
and let
\mathbf{b}\in L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
.

(i)

Suppose that the pair
(p,q)
satisfies either

(a)

1<p<n
and
q=p^{\prime}
or

(b)

p=n
and
q>n
.

Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,\Omega)>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}|(u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla v|\,dx\leq C\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{W^{1,p}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}

(2.2)

for every
u\in W^{1,p}(\Omega)
and
v\in W^{1,q}(\Omega)
.

(ii)

Suppose in addition that
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. If
p>n
, then

-\int_{\Omega}u\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla u\,dx\geq 0

(2.3)

holds for all
u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. If
n\geq 3
, then (
2.3
) holds for all
u\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

(i) The case (a) can be found in
[
20
, Lemma 3.5]
. Suppose that
(p,q)
satisfies (b). Choose
(s,r)
satisfying
1<s<n^{\prime}
and
nq/(q-n)<r<\infty
so that

1=\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{r}.

Since
\Omega
is bounded and
n^{\prime}\leq n
, the space
L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
L^{s}(\Omega)
. Also, by the Sobolev embedding theorem,
W^{1,n}(\Omega)
is continuously embedded into
L^{r}(\Omega)
. Hence by the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{s}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{L^{r}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{W^{1,n}(\Omega)}\|{v}\|_{W^{1,q}(\Omega)}.

This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) Suppose that
p>n
. Since
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
, it follows that

-\int_{\Omega}(u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{1}{2}u^{2}\right)\geq 0

for all
u\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Since
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
is dense in
{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, it follows that for each
u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a sequence of functions
\{u_{k}\}
in
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow u
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Hence by (i), we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}(u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle=-\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}(u_{k}\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u_{k}\,dx\geq 0.

This completes the proof when
p>n
. If
n\geq 3
and
u\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
, then (
2.3
) follows from
[
20
, Lemma 3.6]
. This completes the proof of Proposition
2.3
.
∎

The following result can be proved by using the Riesz representation theorem on
L^{p}(\Omega)
and the classical Calderón-Zygmund estimate, see
[
20
, Lemma 3.9]
for the proof.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, and let
1<p<\infty
. If
f\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
, then there exists
\mathbf{F}\in L^{p}(\Omega)^{n}
such that

\Div\mathbf{F}=f\quad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{and}\quad\|{\mathbf{F}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,p}(\Omega)}

for some constant

.

2.3.
Existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in
{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
when
n\geq 3

When
n\geq 3
, existence and uniqueness of weak solutions in
{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
for (
1.1
) and (
1.2
) are easily obtained by applying the Lax-Milgram theorem.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 3
. Suppose that
\mathbf{b}\in L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
satisfies
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
.

(i)

For every
f\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
u\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.1
). Moreover, we have

\|{u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C=C(n,\delta,\Omega)>0
.

(ii)

For every
g\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique weak solution
v\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
). Moreover, we have

\|{v}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{g}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C=C(n,\delta,\Omega)>0
.

Proof.

Define
\mathcal{B}:{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\times{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
by

\mathcal{B}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(A\nabla u-u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla v\,dx.

Since
a^{ij}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\mathbf{b}\in L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
, the bilinear form
\mathcal{B}
is bounded by Proposition
2.3
. Moreover, it follows from Proposition
2.3
, uniform ellipticity of
A
, and the Poincaré inequality that there exists a constant
C=C(n,\Omega)>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla u-u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla u\,dx\geq\delta\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq C\delta\|{u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}

for all
u\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence by the Lax-Milgram theorem, for each
f\in W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
u\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\mathcal{B}(u,v)=\left<f,v\right>\quad\text{for all }v\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega).

Note that by the Poincaré inequality, we have

\left|\left<f,u\right>\right|\leq\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\|{u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C=C(n,\Omega)>0
. Hence it follows from Young’s inequality that

\delta\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{\delta}{2}\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,2}(\Omega)}.

for some constant
C=C(n,\Omega,\delta)
.
This proves (i). The proof of (ii) is similar so omitted. This completes the proof of Proposition
2.5
.
∎

3.
Reverse Hölder estimates

In this section, we prove reverse Hölder estimates for the gradient
\nabla v
of a weak solution
v
of the problem (
1.2
). We use the following Poincaré-Sobolev’s inequalities.

Proposition 3.1

(Poincaré-Sobolev inequality)
.

Let
R>0
and let
1\leq p<n
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p)>0
such that

\|{u-(u)_{B_{R}(x_{0})}}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\leq C\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\quad\text{for all }u\in W^{1,p}(B_{R}(x_{0})).

(3.1)

Fix
\lambda>0
. Then there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,\lambda)>0
such that

\|{u}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}\leq C\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))}

(3.2)

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{R}(x_{0}))
satisfying

|\{x\in B_{R}(x_{0}):u(x)=0\}|\geq\lambda|B_{R}(x_{0})|.

Proof.

Inequality (
3.1
) can be found in standard literatures e.g.
[
13
,
9
]
. Inequality (
3.2
) is more or less standard result, but we give a proof for the sake of the completeness. By translation and scaling, it suffices to show the assertion when
R=1
and
x_{0}
is the origin. We first show that there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,\lambda)>0
such that

\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\leq C\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}

(3.3)

for all
u\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
with
|\{x\in B_{1}:u(x)=0\}|\geq\lambda|B_{1}|
. Suppose on the contrary that inequality (
3.2
) is false. Then there exists a sequence
\{u_{k}\}
such that
|\{x\in B_{1}:u_{k}(x)=0\}|\geq\lambda|B_{1}|
but
\|{u_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=1
and
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\|{\nabla u_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=0
. Then by Rellich-Kondrachov’s theorem, we may assume that there exists
u_{0}\in W^{1,p}(B_{1})
such that
u_{k}\rightarrow u_{0}
weakly in
W^{1,p}(B_{1})
and strongly in
L^{p}(B_{1})
. Since
\|{\nabla u_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=0
and
\|{u_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}=0
, it follows that
u_{0}
is a nonzero constant a.e. on
B_{1}
. Hence

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{B_{1}}|u_{k}-u_{0}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\{u_{k}=0\}}|u_{k}-u_{0}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\geq|u_{0}|^{p}\inf|\{u_{k}=0\}|>0.

This contradiction leads us to prove (
3.3
). Since
W^{1,p}(B_{1})
is continuously embedded into
L^{p^{*}}(B_{1})
, it follows that

\|{u}\|_{L^{p^{*}}(B_{1})}\leq C(n,p)\left(\|{u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}+\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}\right)\leq C(n,p,\lambda)\|{\nabla u}\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}.

This completes the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
∎

From now on, we assume that
p_{0}=2
if
n\geq 3
and
p_{0}>2
if
n=2
in this section. We first derive an interior reverse Hölder estimate for the gradient of weak solution.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\mathbf{G}\in L^{2}(\Omega)^{n}
. Assume that
A
satisfies (
1.3
),
\mathbf{b}\in L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
, and
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
v\in{W}^{1,p_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) with
g=\Div\mathbf{G}
. If
\frac{2n}{n+1}<r<2
, then there exists a constant
C
depending only on
\delta
,
K
,
n
and
r
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n-2n/r}(1+\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)})\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v|^{r}\,dx\right)^{2/r}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+CR^{n/2-n/r}\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v|^{r}\,dx\right)^{1/r}\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{G}|^{2}\,dx+\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{G}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}

for all
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.3
, a standard density argument enables us to show that (
1.4
) holds for all
\phi\in{W}^{1,p_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
and choose a cut-off function
\zeta\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))
so that
0\leq\zeta\leq 1
,
\zeta=1
on
B_{R}(x_{0})
, and
\|{\nabla\zeta}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C/R
for some constant
C=C(n)>0
. Denote
\overline{v}=v-(v)_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}
and take
\phi=\zeta^{2}\overline{v}
in (
1.4
). Since
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\overline{v})\zeta^{2}\overline{v}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\zeta)\zeta\overline{v}^{2}\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}[\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla(\zeta\overline{v})]\zeta\overline{v}\,dx\geq 0.

Then we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}(A^{T}\nabla{v})\cdot\nabla v\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}^{2}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\zeta)\,dx+2\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla v\,dx-2\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx

By Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}^{2}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\zeta)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}\|{|\overline{v}|^{2}}\|_{L^{n^{\prime},1}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle=\frac{C}{R}\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\overline{v}}\|_{L^{2n^{\prime},2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}

Hence it follows from Hölder’s inequality in Lorentz spaces and Poincaré-Sobolev’s inequality that there exists a constant
C=C(n,r)>0
such that

\|{\overline{v}}\|_{L^{2n^{\prime},2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\leq CR^{1+n/2n^{\prime}-n/r}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{r}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}

Hence we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}^{2}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\zeta)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq CR^{n-2n/r}\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{r}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}

for some constant
C=C(n,r)>0
. Similarly, it follows from the boundedness of
A
, Hölder’s inequality, and Poincaré-Sobolev’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\overline{v}||\nabla v|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{R}\|{\overline{v}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}

\displaystyle=CR^{n/2-n/r}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{r}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}

for some constant
C=C(n,r,K)>0
. Finally, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\zeta^{2}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla v\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}.

Also, it follows from Young’s inequality and Poincaré-Sobolev’s inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\zeta\overline{v}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla\zeta\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{G}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\overline{v}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{B_{2R}(x_{0})}|\mathbf{G}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{C}{R^{2}}\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{r}(B_{2R}(x_{0}))}^{2}R^{n-2n/r+2}

for some constant
C=C(n,r)>0
. Collecting all of these estimates and using uniform ellipticity of
A
, we get the desired estimate. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.2
.
∎

This section is devoted to proving Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
which are main theorems of this paper. We use the following lemma which can be found in
[
7
, Proposition 3.7]
. See
[
11
, Chapter V]
for the proof.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
1<q_{0}<q_{1}
,
\Phi\geq 0
in a
n
-dimensional cube
Q
, and
\Psi\in L^{q_{1}}(Q)
. Suppose that

\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Phi^{q_{0}}\,dx\leq C_{0}\left(\fint_{B_{8r}(x_{0})}\Phi\,dx\right)^{q_{0}}+C_{0}\fint_{B_{8r}(x_{0})}\Psi^{q_{0}}\,dx+\theta\fint_{B_{8r}(x_{0})}\Phi^{q_{0}}\,dx

for every
x_{0}\in Q
and
0<r\leq R_{2}
such that
B_{8r}(x_{0})\subset Q
, where
R_{2}
and
\theta
are constants with
R_{2}>0
and
\theta\in[0,1)
. Then
\Phi\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(Q)
for
p\in[q_{0},q_{0}+\varepsilon)
and

\left(\fint_{B_{r}(x_{0})}\Phi^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{8r}(x_{0})}\Phi^{q_{0}}\,dx\right)^{1/q_{0}}+C\left(\fint_{B_{8r}(x_{0})}\Psi^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

for all
B_{8r}(x_{0})\subset Q
,
0<r<R_{2}
, where
C
and
\varepsilon
depends only on
n
,
q_{0}
,
q_{1}
,
\theta
, and
C_{0}
, and
\varepsilon
satisfies
0<\varepsilon<q_{1}-q_{0}
.

Using interior and boundary reverse Hölder estimate for the gradient of weak solution, we obtain the following a priori estimate.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

q>2
, and
\mathbf{G}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{n}
. Assume that
A
satisfies (
1.3
),
\mathbf{b}\in L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
, and
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Suppose that
v\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
if
n\geq 3
and
v\in{W}^{1,p_{0}}_{0}(\Omega)
if
n=2
for some
p_{0}>2
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

(4.1)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Then there exists
\varepsilon>0
depending on
n
,
\delta
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that
\nabla v\in L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)^{n}
. Moreover, we have

\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})

for some constant
C
depending on
n
,
q
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
.

Proof.

Extend
v
and
\mathbf{G}
to be zero outside
\Omega
. We write
\tilde{v}
and
\tilde{\mathbf{G}}
to be the extension of
v
and
\mathbf{G}
, respectively. If we set
r=\frac{2n+2}{n+2}
, then it follows from Propositions
3.2
,
3.3
, and Young’s inequality that for any
0<\theta<1
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
n
,
\theta
,
K
,
\delta
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\theta\int_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2}\,dx

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+CR^{n}(1+\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)})\left(R^{-n}\int_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{r}\,dx\right)^{2/r}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+C\int_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}|^{2}\,dx

for any
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
0<R\leq R_{0}/6
. Indeed, if
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
, then the desired inequality follows by Proposition
3.2
. If
B_{2R}(x_{0})\cap\partial\Omega\neq\varnothing
, then there exists
y_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that

|x_{0}-y_{0}|=\mathrm{dist}(x_{0},\partial\Omega)\leq 2R.

This implies that

B_{R}(x_{0})\subset B_{3R}(y_{0})\subset B_{6R}(y_{0})\subset B_{8R}(x_{0}).

It follows from Proposition
3.3
and
3R<R_{0}/2
that the estimate (
4.2
) holds with
B_{3R}(y_{0})
and
B_{6R}(y_{0})
instead of
B_{R}(x_{0})
and
B_{8R}(x_{0})
, respectively. This implies the desired estimate (
4.2
). The case
B_{2R}(x_{0})\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is clear. This proves the estimate (
4.2
).

Set

\Phi=|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{r}\quad\text{and}\quad\Psi=|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}|^{r}.

Dividing
R^{n}
in (
4.2
), we have

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\Phi^{2/r}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\theta\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}\Phi^{2/r}\,dx\right)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+C(1+\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)})\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}\Phi\,dx\right)^{2/r}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}+C\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}\Psi^{2/r}\,dx.

Since
2/r>1
, it follows from Lemma
4.1
that there exists
p>2/r
depending on
n
,
K
,
\delta
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that

\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\Phi^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}\Phi^{2/r}\,dx\right)^{r/2}+C\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}\Psi^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}

for some constant
C
depending on
n
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
,
K
,
\delta
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
. Since
pr>2
, it follows that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
pr=2+\varepsilon
. This implies that

\left(\fint_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2+\varepsilon}\,dx\right)^{1/(2+\varepsilon)}\leq C\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}|\nabla\tilde{v}|^{2}\,dx\right)^{1/2}+C\left(\fint_{B_{8R}(x_{0})}|\tilde{\mathbf{G}}|^{q}\,dx\right)^{1/q}

Therefore by a standard covering argument, we have

\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})

for some constant
C=C(n,q,\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)},\delta,K,\Omega)>0
. This completes the proof of Theorem
4.2
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
when
n\geq 3
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
when
n\geq 3
.

Let
g\in W^{-1,q}(\Omega)
. Then by Proposition
2.4
, there exists
\mathbf{G}\in L^{q}(\Omega)^{n}
such that
\Div\mathbf{G}=g
in
\Omega
. Since
q>2
and
\Omega
is bounded, it follows from Proposition
2.5
that there exists a unique weak solution
v\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
of (
1.2
) satisfying (
4.1
). By Theorem
4.2
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
depending on
n
,
q
,
\delta
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
, and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that
\nabla v\in L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)^{n}
. Moreover, we have

\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C(\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)})

for some constant
C
depending on
n
,
q
,
\Omega
,
\delta
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
. Therefore, it follows from Proposition
2.5
and the Poincaré inequality that

\|{v}\|_{W^{1,2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{g}\|_{W^{-1,q}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C
depending on
\Omega
,
n
,
q
,
\delta
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
. This proves the existence part of Theorem
1.1
. The proof of uniqueness follows by Proposition
2.5
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

In contrast to the case of
n\geq 3
, further scrutiny is necessary when we are working on two-dimensional case since it seems hard to show an existence of weak solution of (
1.2
) by using Lax-Milgram theorem as before. The main reason is that
\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v
does not belongs to
W^{-1,2}(\Omega)
for general
\mathbf{b}\in L^{2,\infty}(\Omega)^{2}
and
v\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. To overcome this difficulty, we use some approximation argument by regularizing
A
and
\mathbf{b}
, and domain approximation of
\Omega
.

The following proposition will be used to construct a solution of an approximate problem to (
1.2
).

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded Lipschitz domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
and let
1<p<\infty
. Suppose that
\mathbf{b}\in L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
satisfies
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
. Then there exist a sequence of domains
\{\Omega_{k}\}
and a sequence of vector fields
\{\mathbf{b}_{k}\}
such that

(i)

there is a covering of
\partial\Omega
by coordinate cylinders
Z
, so that given a coordinate pair
(Z,\varphi)
, then
Z^{*}\cap\partial\Omega_{j}
is given as the graph of a
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi_{j}
for all
j
such that
\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\varphi
uniformly,
\|{\nabla\varphi_{j}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}\leq\|{\nabla\varphi}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n-1})}
, and
\nabla\varphi_{j}\rightarrow\nabla\varphi
pointwise a.e.;

(ii)

\mathbf{b}_{k}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
and
\mathbf{b}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{b}
a.e. on
\Omega
as
k\rightarrow\infty
. Also,
\|{\mathbf{b}_{k}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)}
, where
C
depends only on
n
and
p
. Moreover,

\|{\mathbf{b}_{k}-\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\rightarrow 0

as
k\rightarrow\infty
for any
1<q<p
;

(iii)

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
\Div\mathbf{b}_{k}\geq 0
in
\Omega_{k}
.

Proof.

Choose a sequence of bounded smooth domains
\{\Omega_{k}\}
given in Theorem
2.1
. Extend
\mathbf{b}
by zero outside
\Omega
and denote the extension by
\overline{\mathbf{b}}
. Let
\rho
be a non-negative radial function such that
\supp\rho\subset B_{1}
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\rho\,dx=1
. For
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\rho_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-n}\rho(x/\varepsilon)
and
\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}=(\rho_{\varepsilon}*\overline{\mathbf{b}})
. Then
\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)^{n}
. Also,
\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbf{b}
a.e. on
\Omega
and
\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow\mathbf{b}
in
L^{q}(\Omega)^{n}
for
1<q<p
. By Young’s convolution inequality in weak space (see Theorem 1.4.25 in
[
14
]
e.g.), we have

\|{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\|{\rho_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq c\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{p,\infty}(\Omega)},

where
c=c(n,p)
.
Choose
0<\varepsilon_{k}<\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\mathrm{dist}(\overline{\Omega}_{k},\Omega^{c}),\frac{1}{k}\right\}
. Since
\overline{\Omega}_{k}\subset\Omega
, it is easy to see that
\Div\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\geq 0
in
\Omega_{k}
. Hence the sequence
\{\mathbf{b}_{\varepsilon_{k}}\}
satisfies the desired properties (ii) and (iii). This completes the proof of Proposition
4.3
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
when
n=2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
when
n=2
.

Choose a sequence of domains
\{\Omega_{k}\}
and a sequence of vector fields
\{\mathbf{b}_{k}\}
which are given in Proposition
4.3
. Let
A_{\varepsilon}=(A*\rho_{\varepsilon})
, where
\{\rho_{\varepsilon}\}
is a standard mollifier given in a proof of Proposition
4.3
. Then by Young’s convolution inequality, we have
\|{A_{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq\|{\rho}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}
. Note also that for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\xi^{T}A_{\varepsilon}(x)\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}[\xi^{T}A(x-y)\xi]\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy\geq\delta|\xi|^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\rho_{\varepsilon}(y)\,dy=\delta|\xi|^{2}.

By the Lax-Milgram theorem, there exists a unique
v_{k}\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
satisfying

\int_{\Omega_{k}}(A^{T}_{1/k}\nabla v_{k})\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{k})\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega_{k}}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

(4.3)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega_{k})
. Moreover, we have

\|{\nabla v_{k}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega_{k})}\leq\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)},

where
C
depends only on
q
, and
|\Omega|
.

On the other hand, since coefficients are smooth, it follows from the classical
L^{q}
-theory (see
[
13
]
e.g.) that there exists a unique
u_{k}\in{W}^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
satisfying (
4.3
). Set
w_{k}=u_{k}-v_{k}
. Note that
w_{k}\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
and
w_{k}
satisfies (
4.3
) with
\mathbf{G}=\mathbf{0}
. Hence
\nabla w_{k}=\mathbf{0}
. By the Poincaré inequality, this implies that
w_{k}
is identically zero in
\Omega_{k}
and hence
v_{k}\in{W}^{1,q}_{0}(\Omega_{k})
.

Since
q>2
,
\|{\mathbf{b}_{k}}\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}
for all
k
, and
\Omega_{k}
satisfies the property in Proposition
4.3
(i), it follows from Theorem
4.2
that there exists
p>2
depending on
n
,
\delta
,
K
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
and the Lipschitz character of
\Omega
such that

\|{\nabla v_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{k})}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C
depending on
n
,
\delta
,
q
,
K
, and
\Omega
, which is uniform in
k
. Extend
v_{k}
to be zero outside
\Omega_{k}
and still denote it by
v_{k}
. Then
v_{k}\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and
v_{k}
satisfies

\|{\nabla v_{k}}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C
independent of
k
. By Poincaré inequality, the sequence
\{v_{k}\}
is uniformly bounded in
{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence by the weak compactness result in
{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
, there exists a subsequence (which we still denote it by
\{v_{k}\}
) and
v\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that
v_{k}\rightarrow v
weakly in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
. Since
p>2
, we have
\mathbf{b}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbf{b}
in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Hence by Proposition
2.3
, we have

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}_{1/k}\nabla v_{k})\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}_{k}\cdot\nabla v_{k})\phi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\phi\,dx

for any
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This implies that
v
is a weak solution of (
1.2
) with
g=\Div\mathbf{G}
. Moreover,
v
satisfies

\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C
depending on
q
,
\delta
,
K
, and
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{2,\infty}(\Omega)}
, and
\Omega
. To show the uniqueness part, suppose that
v\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is a weak solution satisfying

\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\phi\,dx=0

(4.4)

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. By Proposition
2.3
, (
4.4
) holds for all
\phi\in{W}^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
\Div\mathbf{b}\geq 0
in
\Omega
, it follows from Proposition
2.3
that

-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)v\,dx\geq 0\quad\text{for all }v\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega).

Since
p>2
, letting
\phi=v
in (
4.4
), we get

\delta\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx=0.

Therefore, it follows from the Poincaré inequality that
v
is identically zero in
\Omega
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
when
n=2
.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

(i) Let
r=(2+\varepsilon)^{\prime}
, where
\varepsilon>0
appears in Theorem
1.1
corresponds to
q=3
. Suppose that
u\in{W}^{1,r}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfies (
1.5
). To show that
u=0
, let
\mathbf{G}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)^{n}
be given. Then by Theorem
1.1
, there exists a unique
\psi\in{W}^{1,r^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla\psi)\cdot\nabla\phi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\psi)\phi\,dx=-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\|{\nabla v}\|_{L^{2+\varepsilon}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{\mathbf{G}}\|_{L^{3}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C=C(n,\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)},\delta,K,\Omega)>0
. Choose a sequence
\psi_{k}\in C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
with
\psi=0
on
\partial\Omega
satisfying
\psi_{k}\rightarrow\psi
in
W^{1,r^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Then by Proposition
2.3
, we can take
\phi=u
, and so we get

\displaystyle-\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{G}\cdot\nabla u\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla\psi)\cdot\nabla u-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\psi)u\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla\psi_{k})\cdot\nabla u-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\psi_{k})u\,dx=0.

Since
\mathbf{G}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)^{n}
, this implies that
\nabla u=\mathbf{0}
a.e. on
\Omega
. Hence by the Poincaré inequality, we conclude that
u
is identically zero in
\Omega
. This completes the proof of (i).

(ii) Let
1<p<2
and let
f\in W^{-1,2-}(\Omega)
be given. By Theorem
1.1
, there exists
2<s^{\prime}<p^{\prime}
such that for every
g\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
, there exists a unique
v=Lg\in{W}^{1,s^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying

\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla v)\cdot\nabla\psi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla v)\psi\,dx=\left<g,\psi\right>

for all
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Moreover, we have

\|{Lg}\|_{W^{1,s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{g}\|_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C>0
depending on
n
,
p
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\delta
,
K
,
\Omega
.

Since
p<s<2
, it follows that
f\in W^{-1,s}(\Omega)
. Define
\ell
on
({W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime}
by
\ell(g)=\left<f,Lg\right>
. Then
\ell
is linear and

\left|\left<f,Lg\right>\right|\leq\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}\|{Lg}\|_{W^{1,s^{\prime}}(\Omega)}\leq C\|{f}\|_{W^{-1,s}(\Omega)}\|{g}\|_{W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)}

for some constant
C>0
depending on
n
,
p
,
\|{\mathbf{b}}\|_{L^{n,\infty}(\Omega)}
,
\delta
,
K
,
\Omega
. This implies that
\ell\in({W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega))^{\prime\prime}
. Since
{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
is reflexive, it follows that there exists
u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

\ell(g)=\left<g,u\right>

for all
g\in W^{-1,p}(\Omega)
. Now given
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}({\Omega})
, define a linear functional
g
by

\left<g,\psi\right>=\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla\phi)\cdot\nabla\psi-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\phi)\psi\,dx\quad\text{for }\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega).

(4.5)

By Proposition
2.3
, a standard density argument shows that (
4.5
) holds for all
\psi\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
. So
g\in W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
. Then by definitions of
L
and
\ell
, we have
Lg=\phi
and so

\displaystyle\mathrel{\phantom{=}}\int_{\Omega}(A\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\phi-(u\mathbf{b})\cdot\nabla\phi\,dx

(4.6)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(A^{T}\nabla\phi)\cdot\nabla u-(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla\phi)u\,dx

\displaystyle=\left<g,u\right>=\ell(g)=\left<f,Lg\right>=\left<f,\phi\right>

for all
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
. This proves that given
f\in W^{-1,2-}(\Omega)
and
1<p<2
, there exists a function
u\in{W}^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying (
4.6
). Since
p<2
is arbitrary chosen, it follows that
u\in{W}^{1,2-}_{0}(\Omega)
. This proves the existence of weak solution
u\in{W}^{1,2-}_{0}(\Omega)
satisfying (
1.1
). The uniqueness part of (ii) follows by (i). This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

The fiber-full scheme
Yairon Cid-Ruiz, Ritvik Ramkumar

Abstract
. We introduce the fiber-full scheme which can be seen as the parameter space that generalizes the Hilbert and Quot schemes by controlling the entire cohomological data. The fiber-full scheme Fib ℱ / X / S 𝐡 {\text{Fib}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{\mathbf{h}} is a fine moduli space parametrizing all quotients 𝒢 \mathcal{G} of a fixed coherent sheaf ℱ \mathcal{F} on a projective morphism f : X ⊂ ℙ S r → S f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S such that R i ​ f ∗ ​ ( 𝒢 ⁡ ( ν ) ) R^{i}{{f}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{G}(\nu)\right) is a locally free 𝒪 S \mathcal{O}_{S} -module of rank equal to h i ​ ( ν ) h_{i}(\nu) , where 𝐡 = ( h 0 , … , h r ) : ℤ r + 1 → ℕ r + 1 \mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1} is a fixed tuple of functions. In other words, the fiber-full scheme controls the dimension of all cohomologies of all possible twistings, instead of just the Hilbert polynomial. We show that the fiber-full scheme is a quasi-projective S S -scheme and a locally closed subscheme of its corresponding Quot scheme. In the context of applications, we demonstrate that the fiber-full scheme provides the natural parameter space for arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay and arithmetically Gorenstein schemes with fixed cohomological data, and for square-free Gröbner degenerations.

1.
Introduction

The Hilbert and Quot schemes were introduced by Grothendieck
[
17
]
and are probably among the most important constructions in algebraic geometry.
The Quot scheme
{\text{Quot}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{P}
is a fine moduli space parametrizing all quotients of a fixed coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on a projective morphism
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
that are flat over
S
and have a fixed Hilbert polynomial
P(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
along all fibers; the Hilbert scheme is a special case with
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
These schemes can be used to construct other important moduli spaces such as the moduli of curves and the Picard scheme
[
34
,
26
]
. They are used to study deformations of curves which is an essential part of birational geometry
[
33
,
31
]
. They also feature prominently in other areas of mathematics. The Hilbert schemes of points on a singular plane curve and a surface are related to knot invariants
[
37
,
15
]
. The Hilbert scheme of points on a surface appears in representation theory
[
36
]
, in combinatorics
[
18
]
and, if the surface is a K3-surface, this Hilbert scheme provides an important class of examples of hyperkähler varieties
[
4
]
.
The main goal of this paper is to introduce a far-reaching generalization of the Hilbert and Quot schemes that controls all the cohomological data of the quotients of the coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
instead of just the Hilbert polynomial.

We start with the classical example of the Hilbert scheme compactification of the space of twisted cubics that was studied by Piene and Schlessinger
[
39
]
.
The motivating example below shows how this well-studied Hilbert scheme decomposes into locally closed subschemes that have constant cohomological data.

Example 1.1

(
Section 6
)
.

In
[
39
]
, it was shown that
\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=H\cup H^{\prime}
is a union of two smooth irreducible components such that the general member of
H
parametrizes a twisted cubic, and the general member of
H^{\prime}
parametrizes a plane cubic union an isolated point. It is also known that
H-H\cap H^{\prime}
is the locus of arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay curves of degree
3
and genus
0
.
We then have a decomposition

\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=(H-H\cap H^{\prime})\sqcup H^{\prime}.

Furthermore, one can show that the functions

h_{X}^{i}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\quad\nu\mapsto\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))\right)

are the same for any element
[X]\in H-H\cap H^{\prime}
and the same for any element
[X]\in H^{\prime}
(for an explicit computation, see
Section 6
).
It then follows that
\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}
can be decomposed into two locally closed subschemes where the cohomological functions
h_{X}^{i}

are constant.
It should also be noted that one might be quite interested in studying
H-H\cap H^{\prime}
as it gives all the closed subschemes of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}
with the same cohomological data as that of a twisted cubic.

As presented below,
the scheme we introduce allows us to provide a unified and systematic treatment of the decomposition seen in
Section 1
.
Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme,
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
be a projective morphism and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.
We follow Grothendieck’s general idea of considering a contravariant functor whose representing scheme (if it exists) is the parameter space one is interested in.

We define the
fiber-full functor
which for an
S
-scheme
T
parametrizes all coherent quotients
\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
such that all higher direct images of
\mathcal{G}
and its twistings are locally free over
T
.
More precisely, for any (locally Noetherian)
S
-scheme
T
we define

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;=\;\left\{\text{coherent quotient $\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}$}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}R^{i}{{f_{(T)}}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{G}(\nu)\right)\text{ is locally free over $T$}\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\end{array}\right\},

where
\mathcal{F}_{T}
is the pull-back sheaf on
X_{T}=X\times_{S}T
and
f_{(T)}:X_{T}\subset\mathbb{P}_{T}^{r}\rightarrow T
is the base change morphism
f_{(T)}=f\times_{S}T
.
We have that

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\;:\;(\text{Sch}/S)^{\text{opp}}\rightarrow(\text{Sets})

is a contravariant functor from the category of (locally Noetherian)
S
-schemes to the category of sets (see
Section 5
).
We stratify this functor in terms of “Hilbert functions” for all the cohomologies.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions.
Then, we define the following functor depending on
\mathbf{h}
:

\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;=\;\left\{\mathcal{G}\in\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\text{$\dim_{\kappa(t)}\left(\text{H}^{i}\left(X_{t},\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu)\right)\right)=h_{i}(\nu)$}\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}},t\in T$}\end{array}\right\},

where
\kappa(t)
denotes the residue field of the point
t\in T
,
X_{t}=X_{T}\times_{T}{\text{Spec}}(\kappa(t))
is the fiber over
t\in T
, and
\mathcal{G}_{t}
is the pull-back sheaf on
X_{t}
.
The idea of this functor is to measure the dimension of
all cohomologies of all possible twistings
.
We easily obtain the stratification

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;=\;\bigsqcup_{\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}}\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)

when
T
is connected, and so it follows that
\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
is a representable functor if all the functors
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
are representable.
When
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we simplify the notation by writing
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}
instead of
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{O}_{X}/X/S}
.

For any numerical polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
, we have Grothendieck’s definition of the Quot functor

\fQuot_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{P}:(\text{Sch}/S)^{\text{opp}}\rightarrow(\text{Sets})

which for an
S
-scheme
T
parametrizes all coherent quotients
\mathcal{F}_{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}
that are flat over
T
and have Hilbert polynomial equal to
P
along all fibers.
The Hilbert functor
\fHilb_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{P}
is the special case of
\fQuot_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{P}
with
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
Then, the fiber-full functor can be thought of as a refinement of the Hilbert and Quot functors due to the following inclusions.
From the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
, we define the function
P_{\mathbf{h}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}h_{i}
.
When
P_{\mathbf{h}}(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial, since the Hilbert polynomial of a sheaf coincides with its Euler characteristic, we automatically get the inclusions

\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\;\subset\;\fHilb^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{X/S}(T)\quad\text{ and }\quad\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;\subset\;\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)

for any (locally Noetherian)
S
-scheme
T
.
If
P_{\mathbf{h}}
is not a numerical polynomial, then
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)=\emptyset
for any
S
-scheme
T
.

The following is the main theorem of this article.
Here, we show that the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is represented by a quasi-projective
S
-scheme that we call the
fiber-full scheme
and we write as
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}
.
From the definition of
\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}
, it follows that the fiber-full scheme
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}
is the finest possible generalization of the Quot scheme
{\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
if one is interested in controlling all the cohomological data.

Theorem A

(
Theorem 5.4
)
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme,
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
be a projective morphism and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions and suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, there is a quasi-projective
S
-scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
that represents the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and that is a locally closed subscheme of the Quot scheme
{\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.

Our main tool for constructing the fiber-full scheme is given in
Theorem 3.3
where we provide a flattening stratification theorem that deals with all the direct images of a sheaf and its possible twistings.
To prove this technical theorem we utilize some techniques previously developed in the papers
[
6
,
8
]
.
In a related direction, we also introduce the notion of fiber-full sheaves and we give three equivalent possible definitions in
Theorem 4.2
.
Under the above notation, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
fiber-full sheaf over
S
if
R^{i}{{f}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{F}(\nu)\right)
is locally free over
S
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Fiber-full sheaves serve as a sheaf-theoretic extension of the notions of
algebras having liftable local cohomology

[
30
]
and
cohomologically full rings

[
10
]
.

It turns out there has been previous interest in stratifying the Hilbert scheme in terms of the whole cohomological data.

In the work of Martin-Deschamps and Perrin
[
32
]
, they were able to control the cohomologies of a sheaf, but not all the possible twistings, as their method would yield the intersection of infinitely many (not necessarily closed) subschemes (see
[
32
, Chapitre VI, Proposition 1.9 and Corollaire 1.10]
); their approach is based on classical techniques related to the Grothendieck complex which are covered, e.g., in
[
22
, §III.12]
.

In the thesis of Fumasoli
[
14
,
13
]
, he stratified the Hilbert scheme by bounding below the cohomological functions of the points of the Hilbert scheme, which is a consequence of the classical upper semicontinuity theorem (see
[
22
, Theorem III.12.8]
).

Our main result
Theorem A
vastly generalizes the two aforementioned approaches and shows that one can indeed stratify the Hilbert and Quot schemes by taking into account all the cohomological data.
In this regard, one important part of our work is to develop the necessary tools that allow us to prove the general stratification result of
Theorem 3.3
.

Next, we describe some applications that follow from the existence of the fiber-full scheme.

There is a large literature on the study of the loci of
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay
(ACM for short) schemes and the loci of
arithmetically Gorenstein
scheme within the Hilbert scheme (see
[
29
,
28
,
27
,
20
,
32
,
12
]
and the references therein).
As a result of considering the fiber-full scheme, we can provide a finer description of these loci and parametrize ACM and AG schemes with a fixed cohomological data.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
h_{0},h_{d}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be two functions, and consider the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
given by
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},0,\ldots,0,h_{d},0,\ldots,0)
where
0:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
denotes the zero function.
To study ACM and AG schemes, since all the intermediate cohomologies vanish in these cases, it becomes natural to consider the following two functors.
For any (locally Noetherian)
S
-scheme
T
, we have

\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)\;=\;\left\{\text{closed subscheme }Z\subset X_{T}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\text{ and $Z_{t}$ is ACM for all $t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}

and

\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)\;=\;\left\{\text{closed subscheme }Z\subset X_{T}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\text{ and $Z_{t}$ is AG for all $t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}.

The following theorem shows the two functors above are representable, and so it provides the natural parameter spaces for ACM and AG schemes with fixed cohomological data.

Theorem B

(
Theorem 5.8
)
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme and
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
be a projective morphism.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
h_{0},h_{d}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be two functions, and consider the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},0,\ldots,0,h_{d},0,\ldots,0):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
.
Suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, there exist open
S
-subschemes
{\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
{\text{AG}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
of
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}
that represent the functors
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
, respectively.

We study several examples of Hilbert schemes that we stratify in terms of fiber-full schemes.
The list includes the Hilbert scheme of points and classical examples such as the Hilbert scheme of twisted cubics and the Hilbert scheme of skew lines (see
Section 6
).
Furthermore, by using the recent classification of Skjelnes and Smith
[
46
]
, we show in
Section 7
that smooth Hilbert schemes coincide with a fiber-full scheme (i.e., cohomological data is constant for points in a smooth Hilbert scheme).

On top of being a generalization of the Hilbert scheme, the fiber-full scheme is also the natural parameter space for
square-free
Gröbner degenerations, an important class of degenerations in algebraic geometry and commutative algebra.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a polynomial ring and
>
be a monomial order on
S
.
Given a closed subscheme
V(I)\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}_{\mathbb{k}}
, a Gröbner degeneration is a flat family over
{\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}[t])
with general fiber isomorphic to
\text{Proj}(S/I)
and special fiber isomorphic to
\text{Proj}(S/\text{in}_{>}(I))
. In particular, the natural parameter space to study Gröbner degenerations is the Hilbert scheme of projective space, and this point of view has been very fruitful in the study of these Hilbert schemes. For example, one proves the connectedness of Hilbert schemes, and more generally Quot schemes, by proving that any two points are connected by a chain of rational curves, each of which is a Gröbner degeneration
[
21
,
38
]
.
Square-free Gröbner degenerations, those degenerations where
\text{in}_{>}(I)
is square-free, have always been an important subclass of Gröbner degenerations, especially since they are amenable to combinatorial techniques (Stanley-Reisner theory).
By the recent work of Conca and Varbaro
[
9
]
the cohomological data is constant along the fibers of a square-free Gröbner degeneration.
Thus, the fiber-full scheme is the natural parameter space to study square-free Gröbner degenerations, as evidenced by
Section 8
.
To phrase this in another way, when the special fiber of a Gröbner degeneration is square-free, the whole degeneration lies inside the same fiber-full scheme.

Our construction of the fiber-full scheme generates many interesting questions on the structure and geometry of these schemes.
One that is particularly interesting, and which we plan to address in a forthcoming paper, is to construct a
reasonable
modular compactification of the fiber-full scheme.
Unlike the Hilbert scheme, fixing the cohomological data, in many cases, ensures that the fiber-full scheme does not contain extraneous components.
Ideally, one would desire it to be the closure of the fiber-full scheme inside the Hilbert scheme.
Not only would this have quite a few enumerative applications, it would also provide a modular description for various interesting loci such as arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay schemes (cf.
[
23
,
24
]
), arithmetically Gorenstein schemes, twisted quartic curves and so on.

Organization.

In

and

, we provide flattening stratifications for various modules and complexes; the most important being the local cohomology modules (

) and the higher direct image sheaves (

In

In

Finally, in

2.
Some flattening stratification theorems in a graded category of modules

In this section, we provide several flattening stratification theorems in a graded category modules; the list includes: the case of modules, cohomology of complexes of modules, Ext modules and local cohomology modules.
For organizational purposes, we divide the section into four different subsections.

2.1.
Flattening stratification of modules

In this subsection, we concentrate on an extension for modules of the flattening stratification theorem given in
[
2
]
(also, see
[
35
, §8]
).
Throughout this subsection, we shall use the following setup.

Setup 2.1
.

Let
A
be ring (always assumed to be commutative and unitary) and
R
be a finitely generated graded
A
-algebra.
For any
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
\kappa(\mathfrak{p}):=A_{\mathfrak{p}}/\mathfrak{p}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the residue field of
\mathfrak{p}
.

For a graded
R
-module
M
, we say that
M
has a
Hilbert function over
A
if for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
the graded part
[M]_{\nu}
is a finitely generated locally free
A
-module of constant rank on
{\text{Spec}}(A)
; and in this case, the Hilbert function is
h_{M}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\;h_{M}(\nu)=\text{rank}_{A}\left([M]_{\nu}\right)
.
If a graded
R
-module
M
has a Hilbert function over
A
, then
M\otimes_{A}B
has the same Hilbert function over any
A
-algebra
B
.

Remark 2.2
.

Let
0\rightarrow L\rightarrow M\rightarrow N\rightarrow 0
be a short exact sequence of graded
R
-modules.

(i)

If
L
and
N
have Hilbert functions over
A
, then
M
has a Hilbert function over
A
given by
h_{M}(\nu)=h_{L}(\nu)+h_{N}(\nu)
.

(ii)

If
M
and
N
have Hilbert functions over
A
, then
L
has a Hilbert function over
A
given by
h_{L}(\nu)=h_{M}(\nu)-h_{N}(\nu)
.

For completeness, we recall the following flatness result.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
A
is Noetherian.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
A
-module.
Then, the following locus

U_{M}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ is a flat $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$-module}\big\}

is an open subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

Proof.

For a proof, see
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
or
[
6
, Lemma 2.5]
.
∎

For a given graded
R
-module
M
and a function
h:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
, we consider the following functor for any ring
B
,

\fF_{M}^{h}(B)\;:=\;\left\{\text{morphism }{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\left[M\otimes_{A}B\right]_{\nu}\text{ is a locally free $B$-module}\\
\text{of rank $h(\nu)$ for all $\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\end{array}\right\}.

We now describe our first flattening stratification theorem.

Theorem 2.4
.

Assume
§2.1
with
A
Noetherian.
Let
M
be a finitely generated graded
R
-module and
h:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a function.
Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

The functor
\fF_{M}^{h}
is represented by a locally closed subscheme
F_{M}^{h}\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
In other words, for any morphism
g:{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
,
M\otimes_{A}B
has a Hilbert function over
B
equal to
h
if and only if
g
can be factored as

{\text{Spec}}(B)\;\rightarrow\;F_{M}^{h}\;\rightarrow\;{\text{Spec}}(A).

(ii)

There is only a finite number of different functions
h_{1},\ldots,h_{m}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
F_{M}^{h_{i}}\neq\emptyset
, and so
{\text{Spec}}(A)
is set-theoretically equal to the disjoint union of the locally closed subschemes
F_{M}^{h_{i}}
.

Proof.

(i) For any morphism
{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
, one has that
\left[M\otimes_{A}B\right]_{\nu}
is locally free of rank
h(\nu)
if and only if
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)-1}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=0
and
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=B
, and that
\text{Fitt}_{j}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=\left(\text{Fitt}_{j}([M]_{\nu})\right)B
(for more details on Fitting ideals, see
[
11
, §20.2]
).

Let
Z_{M}^{h}\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
be the closed subscheme given by
Z_{M}^{h}:={\text{Spec}}\left(A/\left(\sum_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)-1}([M]_{\nu})\right)\right)
.
We have that
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)-1}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=0
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if
{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
factors through
Z_{M}^{h}
.
Therefore, we can substitute
{\text{Spec}}(A)
by
Z_{M}^{h}
, and we do so.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in U_{M}
and suppose that
M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}
has a Hilbert function
h_{M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}=h
over
A_{\mathfrak{p}}
.
By

, there is an affine open neighborhood
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})\subset U_{M}
of

for some

.
Thus
[
47
,
Tag 00NX
]
implies that for all

the function
{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})\rightarrow\mathbb{N},\;\mathfrak{q}\mapsto\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{q})}([M\otimes_{A_{a}}\kappa(\mathfrak{q})]_{\nu})

Consequently, there is an open connected neighborhood
\mathfrak{p}\in V\subset{\text{Spec}}(A_{a})
of

such that

for all

.
It then follows that the following locus

U_{M}^{h}:=\big\{\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)\mid M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}\text{ has a Hilbert function $h_{M\otimes_{A}A_{\mathfrak{p}}}=h$ over $A_{\mathfrak{p}}$}\big\}

is an open subset of
{\text{Spec}}(A)
.

Note that
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=B
if and only if
\mathfrak{P}\cap A\not\supset\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)}([M]_{\nu})
for all
\mathfrak{P}\in{\text{Spec}}(B)
.
Hence, under the condition
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)-1}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=0
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, it follows that
\text{Fitt}_{h(\nu)}([M\otimes_{A}B]_{\nu})=B
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
if and only if

factors through

.
So, after having changed

by

, we have that
\fF_{M}^{h}
is represented by the open subscheme
U_{M}^{h}\subset{\text{Spec}}(A)
.
This completes the proof of this part.

(ii) For each
\mathfrak{p}\in{\text{Spec}}(A)
, let
h_{\mathfrak{p}}
be the function
h_{\mathfrak{p}}(\nu):=\dim_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}([M\otimes_{A}\kappa(\mathfrak{p})]_{\nu})
.
As we have a natural morphism

, it clearly follows that

.
Therefore, by the Noetherian hypothesis, we can show that there is a finite number of distinct functions

such that set-theoretically we have the equality
{\text{Spec}}(A)=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{m}F_{M}^{h_{i}}
.
∎

2.2.
Flattening stratification of the cohomologies of a complex

Here we study how the process of taking tensor product with another ring affects the cohomology of a bounded complex.
In this subsection, we continue using
§2.1
.
The notation below will be used throughout the paper.

Notation 2.5
.

For a (co-)complex of
A
-modules

K^{\bullet}:\,\cdots\rightarrow K^{i-1}\xrightarrow{\phi^{i-1}}K^{i}\xrightarrow{\phi^{i}}K^{i+1}\rightarrow\cdots,

one defines

\text{Z}^{i}\left(K^{\bullet}\right):=\text{Ker}(\phi^{i})
,
\text{B}^{i}\left(K^{\bullet}\right):=\text{Im}(\phi^{i-1})
,

\text{H}^{i}\left(K^{\bullet}\right):=\text{Z}^{i}(K^{\bullet})/\text{B}^{i}(K^{\bullet})
, and
\text{C}^{i}\left(K^{\bullet}\right):=K^{i}/\text{B}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\,\supset\,\text{H}^{i}\left(K^{\bullet}\right)

for all
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
We use analogous notation with lower indices for a complex
K_{\bullet}
.

Remark 2.6
.

A basic result that we shall use several times is the following: for a complex of
A
-modules
K^{\bullet}
and an
A
-module

, we have a four-term exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}\big(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}N\big)\rightarrow\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow K^{i+1}\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow\text{C}^{i+1}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}N\rightarrow 0

of
A
-modules.

The following lemma transfers the burden of studying the cohomologies of a bounded complex to considering the cokernels of the maps.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K^{\bullet}:0\rightarrow K^{0}\rightarrow K^{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow K^{p}\rightarrow 0
be a bounded complex of graded
R
-modules.
Suppose that each
K^{i}
has a Hilbert function over
A
.
Let
{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)
be a morphism.
Then, the following two conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)
has a Hilbert function over
B
for all
0\leq i\leq p
.

(2)

\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
has a Hilbert function over
B
for all
0\leq i\leq p
.

Moreover, if any of the above conditions are satisfied, we have

h_{\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)}\;=\;h_{\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B}+h_{\text{C}^{i+1}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B}-h_{K^{i+1}\otimes_{A}B}

and

h_{\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B}\;=\;\sum_{j=i}^{p}(-1)^{j-i}\left(h_{\text{H}^{j}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)}+h_{K^{j+1}\otimes_{A}B}\right).

Proof.

We have the four-term exact sequence
0\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}\big(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B\big)\rightarrow\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow K^{i+1}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\text{C}^{i+1}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow 0
, which can be broken into short exact sequences

0\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}\big(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B\big)\rightarrow\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow L^{i}\rightarrow 0\quad\text{ and }\quad 0\rightarrow L^{i}\rightarrow K^{i+1}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\text{C}^{i+1}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow 0

where
L^{i}
is some graded
R
-module.

By
§2.1
, if all
\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
have a Hilbert function over
B
then all

have a Hilbert function over
B
and, by the same token, it follows that all
\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)
have a Hilbert function over
B
.
This establishes the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

Suppose that all
\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)
have a Hilbert function over
B
.
As a consequence of
§2.1
, if
\text{C}^{i+1}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
has a Hilbert function over
B
, we obtain that
L^{i}
and, subsequently,
\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
have Hilbert functions over
B
.
Since
\text{C}^{p}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B=\text{H}^{p}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)
, by descending induction on
i
, we get that all
\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
have a Hilbert function over
B
.
So, the other implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) also holds.

The additional equations relating the Hilbert functions of
\text{C}^{i}(K^{\bullet})\otimes_{A}B
and
\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)
are straightforwardly checked.
∎

For a given bounded complex of graded
R
-modules
K^{\bullet}:0\rightarrow K^{0}\rightarrow K^{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow K^{p}\rightarrow 0
such that each

has a Hilbert function over
A

\fF_{K^{\bullet}}^{\mathbf{h}}(B)\;:=\;\left\{\text{morphism }{\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow{\text{Spec}}(A)\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\left[\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)\right]_{\nu}\text{ is a locally free $B$-module}\\
\text{of rank $h_{i}(\nu)$ for all $0\leq i\leq p,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\end{array}\right\}.

For completeness, we include a lemma which shows that, in our setting, flatness is equivalent to being locally free.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
K^{\bullet}:0\rightarrow K^{0}\rightarrow K^{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow K^{p}\rightarrow 0
be a bounded complex of graded
R
-modules.
Suppose that
[K^{i}]_{\nu}
is a finitely generated locally free

-module for all

Let

(1)

[\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)]_{\nu}

-module for all

(2)

[\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)]_{\nu}

-module for all

.

Proof.

The implication (2)

So, we assume that each

As in the proof of

Note that each

Similarly to

[\text{H}^{i}(K^{\bullet}\otimes_{A}B)]_{\nu}
and

.
∎

The following theorem deals with the stratification of the cohomologies of bounded complexes.

Theorem 2.9

Assume

with
A
Noetherian.

Proof.

.

4.
Fiber-full sheaves

In this short section, we introduce the notion of fiber-full sheaf that extends the concept of fiber-full modules from
[
8
]

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme,
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
be a projective morphism, and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.

Definition 4.1
.

We say that
\mathcal{F}
is a
fiber-full sheaf over

if
R^{i}{{f}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{F}(\nu)\right)
is locally free over
S
for all
0\leq i\leq r
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

For every

and

, let
g_{s,q}
be the natural map

{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}
denotes the maximal ideal of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
,
X_{s,q}
be the scheme
X_{s,q}:=X\times_{S}{\text{Spec}}(\mathcal{O}_{S,s}/{\mathfrak{m}}_{s}^{q})
, and
\mathcal{F}_{s,q}:=(1\times_{S}g_{s,q})^{*}\mathcal{F}
be the sheaf on
X_{s,q}
obtained by the pull-back induced by
g_{s,q}
.
For the case

(i.e., when we take the fiber at a point

), we simply write
g_{s}=g_{s,1}
,
X_{s}=X_{s,1}
and
\mathcal{F}_{s}=F_{s,1}
.
The following theorem gives two further equivalent definitions for the notion of a fiber-full sheaf.
The name “fiber-full” is inspired by condition (3) below.

Theorem 4.2
.

Under the above notations, the following three conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is a fiber-full sheaf over
S
.

(2)

\mathcal{F}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module and
\text{H}^{i}(X_{s,q},\mathcal{F}_{s,q}(\nu))
is a free

-module for all
s\in S
,
0\leq i\leq r
,

and

.

(3)

\mathcal{F}
is a locally free
\mathcal{O}_{S}
-module and the natural map
\text{H}^{i}(X_{s,q},\mathcal{F}_{s,q}(\nu))\rightarrow\text{H}^{i}(X_{s},\mathcal{F}_{s}(\nu))
is surjective for all
s\in S
,
0\leq i\leq r
,
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
q\geq 1
.

Proof.

Since the three conditions are local on
S
, we can choose a point
s\in S
and assume that
(B,\mathfrak{b})=(\mathcal{O}_{S,s},{\mathfrak{m}}_{s})
is a Noetherian local ring and
S={\text{Spec}}(B)
.
Moreover, in each of the three above conditions one is assuming that
\mathcal{F}
is flat over
S
.
Let
R:=B[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
with
\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
.
Then, we can choose an integer
m\in{\mathbb{Z}}
such that the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

M:=\bigoplus_{\nu\geq m}\text{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r},\mathcal{F}(\nu))
is a finitely generated graded

-module that is flat over

,

(ii)

M^{\thicksim}\cong\mathcal{F}
and
\left(M\otimes_{B}B/\mathfrak{b}^{q}\right)^{\thicksim}\cong\mathcal{F}_{s,q}

5.
Construction of the fiber-full scheme

In this section, we construct the
fiber-full scheme
which can be seen as a parameter space that generalizes the Hilbert and Quot schemes and that controls all the cohomological data instead of just the corresponding Hilbert polynomial.
We also construct open subschemes of the fiber-full scheme that parametrize arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay and arithmetically Gorenstein schemes.
We use the following setup throughout this section.

Setup 5.1
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme,
f:X\subset\mathbb{P}_{S}^{r}\rightarrow S
be a projective morphism, and
\mathcal{F}
be a coherent sheaf on
X
.

We define the
fiber-full functor
which for an
S
-scheme
T
parametrizes all coherent quotients
\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
such that all higher direct images of
\mathcal{G}
and its twistings are locally over
T
.
That is, we define the following map for any (locally Noetherian)
S
-scheme
T
:

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;:=\;\left\{\text{coherent quotient $\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}$}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}R^{i}{{f_{(T)}}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{G}(\nu)\right)\text{ is locally free over $T$}\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}$}\end{array}\right\}.

One important basic thing about this map is the next lemma, which tells us that

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\;:\;(\text{Sch}/S)^{\text{opp}}\rightarrow(\text{Sets})

is a contravariant functor from the category of (locally Noetherian)
S
-schemes to the category of sets.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
g:T^{\prime}\rightarrow T
be morphism of (locally Noetherian)
S
-schemes.
Then, we have a natural map

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(g)\;:\;\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\rightarrow\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T^{\prime}),\qquad\mathcal{G}\mapsto(1\times_{T}g)^{*}\mathcal{G},

where
(1\times_{T}g)^{*}\mathcal{G}
is the sheaf on
X_{T^{\prime}}
obtained by the pull-back induced by
g
.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of
Section 3
.
∎

We now stratify this functor in terms of “Hilbert functions” for all the cohomologies.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions.
Then, we define the following functor depending on
\mathbf{h}
:

\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;:=\;\left\{\mathcal{G}\in\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;\;\begin{array}[]{|l}\text{$\dim_{\kappa(t)}\left(\text{H}^{i}\left(X_{t},\mathcal{G}_{t}(\nu)\right)\right)=h_{i}(\nu)$}\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}},t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}.

The idea of this functor is to measure the dimension of
all cohomologies of all possible twistings
.
Of course, we obtain the following stratification

\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;=\;\bigsqcup_{\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}}\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)

when
T
is connected.
Therefore,
\fFib_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
is a representable functor if all the functors
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
are representable.
When
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, we simplify the notation by writing
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}
, and we obtain the following alternative description of significant interest

\fFib_{X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}(T)\;:=\;\left\{\text{closed subscheme }Z\subset X_{T}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}R^{i}{{f_{(T)}}_{*}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\nu)\right)\text{ is locally free over $T$ and}\\
\text{$\dim_{\kappa(t)}\left(\text{H}^{i}\left(Z_{t},\mathcal{O}_{Z_{t}}(\nu)\right)\right)=h_{i}(\nu)$}\\
\text{for all $0\leq i\leq r,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}},t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}.

These functors should be thought of as a refinement of the Hilbert and Quot functors in the following sense.

Remark 5.3
.

Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions and suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}:=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}h_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, we automatically obtain the following inclusions

\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\;\subset\;\fHilb^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{X/S}(T)\quad\text{ and }\quad\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)\;\subset\;\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T).

We say that
P_{\mathbf{h}}
is the Hilbert polynomial corresponding with the prescribed “Hilbert functions”
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
of cohomologies.
Note that if the function
P_{\mathbf{h}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}(-1)^{i}h_{i}
does not coincide with a numerical polynomial then
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)=\emptyset
for all
S
-schemes
T
.

Our main result is the following theorem which says that the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is represented by a quasi-projective
S
-scheme.

Theorem 5.4
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be a tuple of functions and suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, there is a quasi-projective
S
-scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
that represents the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and that is a locally closed subscheme of the Quot scheme
{\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.

Proof.

By
Section 5
, there is an injective morphism of functors

\Phi\,:\,\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\rightarrow\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}.

We shall show that
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is a locally closed subfunctor of
\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.
By the existence of the Quot scheme
[
17
,
1
]
, the functor
\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is represented by a projective
S
-scheme
{\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and a universal quotient
\mathcal{F}_{{\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{W}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
in
\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\big({\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\big)
.
Let
Q:={\text{Quot}}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and
\mathcal{W}:=\mathcal{W}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.
Thus, for each
S
-scheme
T
and for each quotient
\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
in
\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
, there is a unique classifying
S
-morphism
g_{T,\mathcal{G}}:T\rightarrow Q
such that

\mathcal{G}=(1\times_{S}g_{T,G})^{*}\mathcal{W}.

By using
Theorem 3.3
, let
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}:={\text{FDir}}_{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathbf{h}}\subset Q
be the locally closed subscheme of
Q
that represents the functor
\fFDir_{\mathcal{W}}^{\mathbf{h}}
.
So, it follows that a quotient in
\mathcal{F}_{T}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{G}
in
\fQuot^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
belongs to
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
if and only if
g_{T,\mathcal{G}}
factors through
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.
Finally, this shows that the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is represented by the
S
-scheme
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and by the universal quotient
\mathcal{F}_{{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}}\twoheadrightarrow(1\times_{S}\iota)^{*}\mathcal{W}
in
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\big({\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\big)
, where
\iota:{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}\hookrightarrow Q
denotes the natural locally closed immersion.
Since
Q
is a projective
S
-scheme, we obtain that
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is a quasi-projective
S
-scheme.
∎

Remark 5.5
.

When the base scheme
S
is well understood, we may simply write the fiber-full schemes as
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X}
and
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{X}
instead of
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
and
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}
, respectively.

Remark 5.6
.

Since the dimensions of the cohomology groups can jump in flat families, the fiber-full scheme is usually not projective
[
22
, Example III.12.9.2]
.

We now recall the following notions.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field and
Y\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
be a closed subscheme.
Let
R_{Y}
be the homogeneous coordinate ring of
Y
.
We say that
Y
is
arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay
(ACM for short) if
R_{Y}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring.
If
R_{Y}
is a Gorenstein ring then
Y
is said to be
arithmetically Gorenstein
(AG for short).

Next, we show the existence of open subschemes of the fiber-full scheme that parametrize ACM and AG schemes.
Recall that a closed subscheme
Y\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
is ACM if and only if the following two conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\text{H}^{i}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\nu))=0
for all
1\leq i\leq\dim(Y)-1
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and

(2)

the natural map
R_{Y}\rightarrow\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{H}^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\nu))
is bijective if
\dim(Y)>0
, or injective if
\dim(Y)=0
.

Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
h_{0},h_{d}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be two functions, and consider the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
given by
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},0,\ldots,0,h_{d},0,\ldots,0)
where
0:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
denotes the zero function.
To study ACM and AG schemes, it then becomes natural to consider the following two functors.
For any (locally Noetherian)
S
-scheme
T
, we have

\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)\;:=\;\left\{\text{closed subscheme }Z\subset X_{T}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\text{ and $Z_{t}$ is ACM for all $t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}

and

\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)\;:=\;\left\{\text{closed subscheme }Z\subset X_{T}\;\begin{array}[]{|l}Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}(T)\text{ and $Z_{t}$ is AG for all $t\in T$}\end{array}\right\}.

Note that, by using the base change results of
§2.3
and
§2.4
, we can immediately deduce that
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
are indeed contravariant functors from the category of (locally Noetherian)
S
-schemes into the category of sets.
The following theorem gives the representability of these two functors.

Theorem 5.8
.

Assume
Section 5
.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
and
h_{0},h_{d}:{\mathbb{Z}}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be two functions, and consider the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},0,\ldots,0,h_{d},0,\ldots,0):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
.
Suppose that
P_{\mathbf{h}}(m)\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
is a numerical polynomial.
Then, there exist open
S
-subschemes
{\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
{\text{AG}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
of
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{X/S}
that represent the functors
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
, respectively.

Proof.

By
Theorem 5.4
, there is a pair
({\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S},\mathcal{I})
representing the functor
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
, where
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
is fiber-full scheme and
\mathcal{I}
is the universal ideal sheaf on
\mathbb{P}_{{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}}^{r}
.
Let
F:={\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}
.
This means that, for each
S
-scheme
T
and for each
Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
, there is a unique classifying
S
-morphism
g_{T,Z}:T\rightarrow F
such that
\mathcal{I}_{Z}=(1\times_{S}g_{T,Z})^{*}\mathcal{I}
is the ideal sheaf on
\mathbb{P}_{T}^{r}
that corresponds with the closed subscheme
Z\subset\mathbb{P}_{T}^{r}
.

Fix
Z\in\fFib^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}(T)
,
g_{T,Z}:T\rightarrow F
and
\mathcal{I}_{Z}=(1\times_{S}g_{T,Z})^{*}\mathcal{I}
.
Since the conditions defining the functors
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
and
\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
are local on
T
, we can restrict the morphism
g_{T,Z}
to affine open subschemes
{\text{Spec}}(B)\subset T
and
{\text{Spec}}(A)\subset F
with
A
being Noetherian.
So, we assume that
T={\text{Spec}}(B)
and
F={\text{Spec}}(A)
.
Let
R:=A[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
with
\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
and
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset R
.
Let
I\subset R
be the saturated ideal
I:=\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{A}^{r},\mathcal{I}(\nu))
.
The saturated ideal and homogeneous coordinate ring of
Z
are given by
I_{Z}:=\bigoplus_{\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\text{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{B}^{r},\mathcal{I}_{Z}(\nu))\cong I\otimes_{A}B
and
R_{Z}:=B[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]/I_{Z}\cong R/I\otimes_{A}B
, respectively.
For all
t\in T
, let
R_{t}:=B[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]\otimes_{B}\kappa(t)\cong R\otimes_{A}\kappa(t)
and
R_{Z,t}:=R_{Z}\otimes_{B}\kappa(t)\cong R/I\otimes_{A}\kappa(t)
.

First, we show that
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
is representable.
By construction,
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{0}(R_{Z,t})=0
for all
t\in T
, and so
Z_{t}
is ACM for all
t\in T
when
d=0
.
If
d>0
, we have that
Z_{t}
is ACM for all
t\in T
if and only if
\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(R_{Z,t})=0
for all
t\in T
.
We have that the locus
V:=\{f\in F\mid\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(R/I\otimes_{A}\kappa(f))=0\}
is an open subscheme of
F
.
When
d>0
,
g_{T,Z}:T={\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow F={\text{Spec}}(A)
factors through
V
if and only if
Z\in\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)
.
Therefore, it follows that, in both cases
d=0
or
d>0
,
\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
is represented by an open subscheme of
{\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}\subset F
.

We now concentrate on the representability of
\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
.
Since a Gorenstein ring is Cohen-Macaulay, we assume that
Z\in\fACM_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)
and so
g_{T,Z}
factors through
{\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}\subset F
.
Therefore, as
R_{Z,t}
is a Cohen-Macaulay ring of dimension
d+1
, it is Gorenstein if and only if its
(d+1)
-th Bass number

\mu_{d+1}(R_{Z,t})\;:=\;\dim_{\kappa(t)}\big(\text{Ext}_{R_{Z,t}}^{d+1}(R_{Z,t}/{\mathfrak{m}}R_{Z,t},R_{Z,t})\big)

is equal to one (see
[
5
, Theorem 3.2.10]
).
By upper semicontinuity, the locus

W:=\big\{f\in F\mid\mu_{d+1}(R/I\otimes_{A}\kappa(f))\leq 1\big\}

is an open subscheme of
F
.
On the other hand, if
f\in{\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}
, then
\mu_{d+1}(R/I\otimes_{A}\kappa(f))\geq 1
.
Finally, it follows that
g_{T,Z}:T={\text{Spec}}(B)\rightarrow F={\text{Spec}}(A)
factors through
{\text{AG}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}:={\text{ACM}}_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}\cap W
if and only if
Z\in\fAG_{X/S}^{h_{0},h_{d}}(T)
.
So, the proof of the theorem is complete.
∎

6.
Examples

In this section, we study some examples of fiber-full schemes as subschemes of the Hilbert scheme. These include the Hilbert scheme of points and classical examples such as the Hilbert scheme of twisted cubics and the Hilbert scheme of skew lines.

Example 6.1

(Points)
.

Let
S
be a locally Noetherian scheme and
f:X\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}_{S}\to S
be a projective morphism.
Let
\mathbf{h}:{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\to\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be the tuple of constant functions defined by
\mathbf{h}:=(c,0,\dots,0)
and let
P_{\mathbf{h}}=c
be the associated Hilbert polynomial.
For any
S
-scheme
T
and
Z\in\fHilb_{X/S}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}(T)
, we have

\dim_{\kappa(t)}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Z_{t},\mathcal{O}_{Z_{t}}(\nu))\right)=\begin{cases}c&\text{ if }i=0\\
0&\text{ if }i>0\end{cases}

for all
t\in T
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.
It follows that

\fFib_{X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}(T)=\fHilb_{X/S}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}(T)

for all
T
and thus

{\text{Fib}}_{X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}=\text{Hilb}_{X/S}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}.

In particular,
{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}}
satisfies Murphy’s law up to retraction for
r\geq 16

[
25
, Theorem 1.3]
. More generally, for any coherent sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
X
, we have
{\text{Fib}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{\mathbf{h}}={\text{Quot}}_{\mathcal{F}/X/S}^{P_{\mathbf{h}}}
.

For the next two examples, let
\mathbb{k}
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

Example 6.2

(Twisted cubics)
.

By the work of
[
39
]
it is known that
\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=H\cup H^{\prime}
is a union of two smooth irreducible components such that the general member of
H
parametrizes a twisted cubic, and the general member of
H^{\prime}
parametrizes a plane cubic union an isolated point. It is also known that
H-H\cap H^{\prime}
is the locus of arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay curves of degree
3
and genus
0
.
Then we have a decomposition

\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=\text{Fib}^{(\mathbf{h},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}\sqcup\text{Fib}^{(\mathbf{h}^{\prime},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=(H-H\cap H^{\prime})\sqcup H^{\prime}

where
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},h_{1}),\mathbf{h}^{\prime}=(h_{0}^{\prime},h_{1}^{\prime}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{2}
are the tuples of functions given by

h_{0}(\nu)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\nu\leq-1\\
3v+1&\text{ if }\nu\geq 0,\end{cases}\quad h_{0}^{\prime}(\nu)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\nu\leq-1\\
2&\text{ if }\nu=0\\
3v+1&\text{ if }\nu\geq 1\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{array}[]{l}h_{1}(\nu)=h_{0}(\nu)-(3\nu+1)\\
h_{1}^{\prime}(\nu)=h_{0}^{\prime}(\nu)-(3\nu+1).\end{array}

To verify this decomposition we appeal to the classification of ideals in
[
39
, §4]
. Since

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=\chi(\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))+h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=3\nu+1+h^{1}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))

for any
[X]\in\text{Hilb}^{3m+1}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}
, it suffices to compute
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))
.
Any subscheme
[X]\in H-H\cap H^{\prime}
is arithmetically Cohen-Macaulay with the ideal sheaf having a resolution

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(-3)^{2}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{3}}(-2)^{3}\to\mathcal{I}_{X}\to 0.

It follows that
h^{0}(\mathcal{I}_{X}(\nu))=3\binom{\nu+1}{3}-2\binom{\nu}{3}
. Using the ideal sheaf exact sequence and the fact that
h^{1}(\mathcal{I}_{X}(\nu))=0
we deduce that
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=\binom{\nu+3}{3}-3\binom{\nu+1}{3}+2\binom{\nu}{3}=3\nu+1
for
\nu\geq 0
and
0
otherwise, as required.

If
[X]\in H^{\prime}
then
\mathcal{I}_{X}=\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{J}
where
X^{\prime}
is a plane cubic and
\mathcal{J}
defines a, possibly embedded,
0
-dimensional subscheme. Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}/\mathcal{I}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}\to 0

of sheaves on
X
. Since
\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}/\mathcal{I}_{X}
is
0
-dimensional, we have

h^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}/\mathcal{I}_{X})=\text{length}(\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}/\mathcal{I}_{X})=(3m+1)-3m=1.

It is straightforward to show that the cohomology of a plane curve
Y
of degree
d
is given by
h^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(\nu))=\binom{\nu+2}{2}-\binom{\nu+2-d}{2}
.
Thus, we deduce that
h^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=h^{0}(X^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(\nu))+1=\binom{\nu+2}{2}-\binom{\nu-1}{2}+1
, as required.

Example 6.3

(Skew Lines)
.

The Hilbert scheme
\text{Hilb}^{2m+2}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}
has been studied in
[
7
]
and
[
42
]
. In parallel with the Hilbert scheme of the twisted cubic, it is known that
\text{Hilb}^{2m+2}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=H\cup H^{\prime}
is a union of smooth components such that the general member of
H
parametrizes two skew lines, and the general member of
H^{\prime}
parametrizes a plane conic union an isolated point. Furthermore
H
is stratified by
\{H_{1},\dots,H_{4}\}
where

(1)

H_{1}=\text{GL}(4)\cdot V((x_{0},x_{1})\cap(x_{2},x_{3}))
is the locus of skew lines,

(2)

H_{2}=\text{GL}(4)\cdot V((x_{0},x_{1})\cap(x_{0},x_{2})\cap(x_{0}^{2},x_{1},x_{2}))
is the locus of lines meeting along an embedded point,

(3)

H_{3}=\text{GL}(4)\cdot V(x_{0}^{2},x_{0}x_{1},x_{1}^{2},x_{0}x_{2}-x_{1}x_{3})
is the locus of pure double structures on a line,

(4)

H_{4}=\text{GL}(4)\cdot V((x_{0},x_{1}^{2})\cap(x_{0}^{2},x_{1},x_{2}))
is the locus of double structures with an embedded point.

We also have
H\cap H^{\prime}=H_{2}\cup H_{4}=\overline{H_{2}}
(see
[
7
, Theorem 1.1(2)]
,
[
42
, Example 2.25]
). Then, we have a decomposition

\text{Hilb}^{2m+2}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=\text{Fib}^{(\mathbf{h},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}\sqcup\text{Fib}^{(\mathbf{h}^{\prime},0,0)}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}_{\mathbb{k}}}=(H-H\cap H^{\prime})\sqcup H^{\prime}

where
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},h_{1}),\mathbf{h^{\prime}}=(h^{\prime}_{0},h^{\prime}_{1}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{2}
are the tuples of functions

h_{0}(\nu)=\begin{cases}0&\text{ if }\nu\leq-1\\
2\nu+2&\text{ if }\nu\geq 0,\end{cases}\quad h_{0}^{\prime}(\nu)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\nu\leq-1\\
2v+2&\text{ if }\nu\geq 0\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad\begin{array}[]{l}h_{1}(\nu)=h_{0}(\nu)-(2\nu+2)\\
h_{1}^{\prime}(\nu)=h_{0}^{\prime}(\nu)-(2\nu+2).\end{array}

To prove this we proceed as in
Section 6
.
If
[X]\in H^{\prime}
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{X}=\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{J}
where
X^{\prime}
is a plane conic and
\mathcal{J}
defines a
0
-dimensional subscheme. Arguing as in
Section 6
, we deduce that
h^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=h^{\prime}_{i}(\nu)
for all
\nu,i
.
Note that
H-\overline{H_{2}}=H_{1}\cup H_{3}
.
If
[X]\in H_{1}
is a pair of skew lines, we have
h^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=2h^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}}(\nu))=h_{i}(\nu)
for all
\nu,i
.
If
[X]\in H_{3}
, then
X
defines a ribbon on
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}
of genus
-1

[
3
, §2]
and there is an exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}}\to\mathcal{O}_{X}\to\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}}\to 0

of sheaves on
X
. It follows that the
h^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(\nu))=h^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{1}}(\nu)^{\oplus 2})=h_{i}(\nu)
for all
\nu,i
, as required.

7.
Smooth Hilbert schemes

In this section, we study the fiber-full scheme as a subscheme of smooth Hilbert schemes, the latter were recently classified in
[
46
]
.
Our main result states that if the Hilbert scheme is smooth, then it is equal to a fiber-full scheme.
For the convenience of the reader, we review some of the discussions from
[
46
, §3]
.
For the rest of this section, we use the following setup.

Setup 7.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
R=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\dots,x_{r}]
be a polynomial ring and
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}=\text{Proj}(R)
.

A numerical polynomial
P\in\mathbb{Q}[m]
of degree at most
r
is the Hilbert polynomial of some subscheme in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
if and only if there exists an
integer partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})
of positive integers such that
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
and

P=P_{\lambda}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{m+\lambda_{i}-i}{\lambda_{i}-1}\,\in\,\mathbb{Q}[m].

The dimension of any subscheme with Hilbert polynomial
P_{\lambda}
is
\lambda_{1}-1
.

For an integer partition
\lambda
, there is a unique saturated lexicographic ideal, denoted by
L(\lambda)
, with Hilbert polynomial
P_{\lambda}
. To describe this, let
a_{j}
be the number of parts in
\lambda
equal to
j
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. If
r\geq\lambda_{1}
we have

L(\lambda)=L(a_{1},\dots,a_{r}):=(x_{0}^{a_{r}+1},x_{0}^{a_{r}}x_{1}^{a_{r-1}+1},\dots,x_{0}^{a_{r}}x_{1}^{a_{r-1}}\cdots x_{r-3}^{a_{3}}x_{r-2}^{a_{2}+1},x_{0}^{a_{r}}x_{1}^{a_{r-1}}\cdots a_{r-2}^{a_{2}}x_{r-1}^{a_{1}}).

The only case not accounted by the condition
r\geq\lambda_{1}
is
\lambda=(r+1)
; in this case the corresponding lexicographic ideals is
(0)
.
For proofs of these facts see
[
46
, Lemma 3.3, Proposition 3.5]
.

Definition 7.2
.

For an integer partition
\lambda
, define the tuple of functions
\mathbf{h}_{\lambda}=(h_{0},\dots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\to\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
given by
h_{i}(\nu):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(L(\lambda))}(\nu))\right)
for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We begin by describing
\mathbf{h}_{\lambda}
explicitly.

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})\neq(r+1)
be an integer partition and
L(\lambda)=L(a_{1}\dots,a_{r})
be the associated lexicographic ideal.
Then, for all
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
we have

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(L(\lambda))}(\nu))\right)=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{\nu+\lambda_{i}-i}{\nu-i+1}+\binom{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{1}-\binom{a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{1}&\text{ if }i=0\\
\binom{a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{i+1}-\binom{a_{i+2}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{i+1}&\text{ if }i>0.\end{cases}

Proof.

Fix
L=L(\lambda)
.
By
[
43
, Lemma 3.2]
, we obtain

\text{Ext}_{R}^{i}(R/L,R)\cong\left(R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{i-2},x_{i-1}^{a_{r-i+1}})\right)(a_{r-i+1}+\cdots+a_{r}+i-1),\quad 1\leq i\leq r.

Note that
a_{l}
in the notation of
[
43
]
corresponds to
a_{l+1}
in our convention. Using the exact sequence

0\to(R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1}))(-p)\to R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1})\to R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1},x_{q}^{p})\to 0,

we deduce that

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{q-1},x_{q}^{p})\right]_{\nu}\right)=\binom{\nu+r-q}{r-q}-\binom{\nu-p+r-q}{r-q}.

Using the above formulas and the local duality theorem (see, e.g.,
[
5
, Theorem 3.6.19]
), we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(L)}(\nu))\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i+1}(R/L)]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\text{Ext}_{R}^{r-i}(R/L,R)]_{-\nu-r-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{r-i-2},x_{r-i-1}^{a_{i+1}})(a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{r}+r-i-1)\right]_{-\nu-r-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{a_{i+1}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{i+1}-\binom{a_{i+2}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{i+1}.

for all
i>0
.
Similarly, since
L
is saturated, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{0}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(L)}(\nu))\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([R/L]_{\nu}\right)+\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{1}(R/L)]_{\nu}\right)

\displaystyle=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([R/L]_{\nu}\right)+\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([\text{Ext}_{R}^{r}(R/L,R)]_{-\nu-r-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{\nu+\lambda_{i}-i}{\nu-i+1}+\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\left[R/(x_{0},\dots,x_{r-2},x_{r-1}^{a_{1}})(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}+r-1)\right]_{-\nu-r-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\binom{\nu+\lambda_{i}-i}{\nu-i+1}+\binom{a_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{1}-\binom{a_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}-\nu-1}{1}.

The formula for
\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left([R/L]_{\nu}\right)
can be found in
[
46
, Lemma 3.3]
.
∎

Before we can prove the main result of this section, we need a simple lemma that relates the cohomologies of
V(fI)
to those of
V(I)
for any subscheme
V(I)\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
of codimension at least two.

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
\lambda=(\underbrace{r,\dots,r}_{a_{r}\emph{-times}},\lambda^{\prime})
be an integer partition with
a_{r}>0
and
[I]\in\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
.
Then, we have
I=fI^{\prime}
with
[I^{\prime}]\in\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda^{\prime}}}_{\mathbb{P}^{r}_{\mathbb{k}}}
,
\deg(f)=a_{r}
and

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(I)}(\nu))\right)=\begin{cases}\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r},\mathcal{O}_{V(I^{\prime})}(\nu-a_{r}))\right)&\text{ if }i\neq r-1\\
\binom{a_{r}-\nu-1}{r}-\binom{-\nu-1}{r}&\text{ if }i=r-1.\end{cases}

Proof.

The first statement can be found in
[
41
, Lemma 2.1]
. The second statement follows from the local duality theorem and
[
43
, Fact 1]
, similar to
Section 7
.
∎

The next proposition provides an equality between the fiber-full scheme and the Hilbert scheme when the latter is smooth.

Proposition 7.5
.

Assume
Section 7
.
Let
\lambda
denote an integer partition for which
\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
is smooth.
Then, we have the equality

\mathrm{Fib}^{\mathbf{h}_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}\,=\,\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}.

Proof.

Since the Hilbert scheme
\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
is smooth, it suffices to just check that
\fFib^{\mathbf{h}_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}({\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}))=\fHilb^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}({\text{Spec}}(\mathbb{k}))
.
By
[
46
, Theorem A]
there are seven different families of
\lambda
for which the Hilbert scheme is smooth. We can reduce to considering partitions that satisfy
a_{r}=0
, i.e., Hilbert schemes parametrizing subschemes of codimension at least two. Indeed, if
a_{r}>0
,
Section 7
implies that

{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}=\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
if and only if

{\text{Fib}}^{\mathbf{h}_{\lambda^{\prime}}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}=\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda^{\prime}}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
where

\lambda=(\underbrace{r,\dots,r}_{a_{r}\text{-times }},\lambda^{\prime})
.
Thus, for the rest of the proof we will only study those partitions in
[
46
, Theorem A]
for which
a_{r}=0
.

The conclusion is immediate for Case (7) as the Hilbert scheme consists of a single point. Case (1) corresponds to the Hilbert scheme of points in
\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{2}
.
In this case
\lambda=(1,\dots,1)
, equivalently
P_{\lambda}
is constant, and this is covered by
Section 6
. Similarly, Case (6) reduces to
\lambda=(1,1,1)
which is also covered by
Section 6
.

To deal with Case (2) and Case (3) we use the fact that they have a unique Borel-fixed point. Let
\lambda
be as in Case (2) or Case (3) and let
[I]\in\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
with
I
saturated. Since
\mathrm{gin}(I)
is Borel-fixed
[
11
, Theorem 15.20]
, we have
\mathrm{gin}(I)=L(\lambda)
. This implies
I
and
L(\lambda)
have the same Hilbert function and thus,
L(\lambda)
is the lexicographic ideal associated to
I
. The result now follows from
[
44
, Theorem 0.1]
. The characteristic assumption of
[
44
]
does not pose any issue because, in our case, the generic initial ideal is strongly stable
[
44
, proof of Theorem 0.1, page 274]
.

Case (4) and Case (5) correspond to Hilbert schemes with two Borel-fixed points. By
[
41
, Theorem A]
we have two cases

•

\lambda=((d+1)^{q},1)
with
d\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
: The general member of
\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
parametrizes
C\cup\{P\}
where
C\subseteq\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{d+1}
is a hypersurface of degree
q
and
P
is a point.

•

\lambda=(2^{q},1)
with
q\geq 4
: The general member of
\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
parametrizes
C\cup P
where
C
is a plane curve of degree
q
and
P
is a point.

In either case, for any subscheme
[X]\in\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
, we have
\mathcal{I}_{X}=\mathcal{I}_{X^{\prime}}\cap\mathcal{J}
with
[X^{\prime}]\in\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}-1}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
and
\mathcal{J}
defining a, possibly embedded,
0
-dimensional subscheme.
Arguing as in
Section 6
, we see that
\text{Fib}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}=\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
if and only if
\text{Fib}^{P_{\lambda}-1}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}=\text{Hilb}^{P_{\lambda}-1}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}}
.
But the latter equality has already been established since
\lambda=((d+1)^{q})
and
\lambda=(2^{q})
, for the aforementioned
d,q
, have a unique Borel-fixed point.
∎

8.
Square-free Gröbner degenerations and the fiber-full scheme

Our results in this short section hint that the fiber-full scheme is the appropriate parameter space to study degenerations into a square-free monomial ideal – a line of research that we plan to address in a subsequent paper.
We now use the following setup.

Setup 8.1
.

Let
\mathbb{k}
be a field,
S=\mathbb{k}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{r}]
be a polynomial ring,
{\mathfrak{m}}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{r})\subset S
be the maximal irrelevant ideal and
>
be a monomial order on
S
.
Let
A=\mathbb{k}[t]
be a polynomial ring and
let
R=A\otimes_{\mathbb{k}}S\cong S[t]
be a polynomial ring over
S
.

We use the notations and conventions of
[
11
, Chapter 15]
for monomial orders and Gröbner bases.
Any element
f\in S
can be uniquely written as

f\;=\;\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\,\mathbf{x}^{\alpha_{j}}

where
\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{k}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha_{j}}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{j,0}}\cdots x_{r}^{\alpha_{j,r}}\in S
is a monomial of
S
.
We uniquely identify the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}=x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots x_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\in S
with the integral vector

\mu(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\;:=\;\left(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}.

Let
\omega=(\omega_{0},\ldots,\omega_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r+1}
be a weight vector.
The corresponding
\omega
-degree of the monomial
\mathbf{x}^{\alpha}=x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots x_{r}^{\alpha_{r}}\in S
is given by

\deg_{\omega}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\;:=\;\mu(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha})\cdot\omega\;=\;\alpha_{0}\omega_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{r}\omega_{r}.

For an element
f\in S
,
\deg_{\omega}(f)
is the maximum
\omega
-degree of the terms of
f
and
\text{in}_{\omega}(f)
is the sum of all the terms of
f
of maximal
\omega
-degree.
For an ideal
I\subset S
, we define
\text{in}_{\omega}(I)\subset S
as the ideal generated by
\text{in}_{\omega}(f)
for all
f\in I
.
For an element
f=\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\,\mathbf{x}^{\alpha_{j}}\in S
, the corresponding
\omega
-homogenization is given by

\hom_{\omega}(f)\;:=\;\sum_{j}\lambda_{j}\,\mathbf{x}^{\alpha_{j}}\,t^{{\deg_{\omega}(f)}-{\deg_{\omega}(\mathbf{x}^{\alpha_{j}})}}\;\in R.

For an ideal
I\subset S
, we define
\hom_{\omega}(I)\subset R
as the ideal generated by
\hom_{\omega}(f)
for all
f\in I
.
For completeness, we recall the following well-known result.

Proposition 8.2

(see
[
11
, Theorem 15.17]
)
.

Let
I\subset S
be an ideal.
Then, there exists a weight vector
\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}^{r+1}
such that the following statements hold:

(i)

\text{in}_{\omega}(I)=\text{in}_{>}(I)
.

(ii)

R/\hom_{\omega}(I)
is a free
A
-module.

(iii)

R/\hom_{\omega}(I)\otimes_{A}A/(t)\cong S/\text{in}_{>}(I)
and
R/\hom_{\omega}(I)\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}]\cong S/I\otimes_{A}\mathbb{k}[t,t^{-1}]
.

We now state the main theorem pertaining to square-free Gröbner degenerations.

Theorem 8.3

(Conca-Varbaro)
.

Assume
Section 8
.
Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal.
If
\text{in}_{>}(I)
is square-free, then we have the equality

\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\big[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/I)\big]_{\nu}\right)\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\big[\text{H}_{{\mathfrak{m}}}^{i}(S/\text{in}_{>}(I))\big]_{\nu}\right)

for all
i\geq 0
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

This is
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Alternative subsequent proofs can be found in
[
40
]
and
[
8
]
.
∎

Finally, we immediately obtain the following corollary.
We write below a direct consequence of
Section 8
and
Theorem 8.3
in terms of the fiber-full scheme.

Corollary 8.4
.

Assume
Section 8
.
Let
I\subset S
be a homogeneous ideal and assume that
\text{in}_{>}(I)
is square-free.
Let
\hom_{\omega}(I)\subset R
be a homogenization with special fiber equal to
\text{in}_{>}(I)
.
Let
Z\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
be the closed subscheme given by
Z=\text{Proj}(S/I)
.
Let
\mathbf{h}=(h_{0},\ldots,h_{r}):{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+1}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
be the tuple of functions given by
h_{i}(\nu):=\dim_{\mathbb{k}}\left(\text{H}^{i}(Z,\mathcal{O}_{Z}(\nu))\right)
.
For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
, let
Z_{\alpha}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}
be the closed subscheme given by
Z_{\alpha}=\text{Proj}\left(R/\hom_{\omega}(I)\otimes_{\mathbb{k}[t]}\mathbb{k}[t]/(t-\alpha)\right)
.
Then, we have that

Z_{\alpha}\;\,\text{ corresponds with a point in }\;\,{\text{Fib}}_{\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{k}}^{r}/\mathbb{k}}^{\mathbf{h}}

for all
\alpha\in\mathbb{k}
.

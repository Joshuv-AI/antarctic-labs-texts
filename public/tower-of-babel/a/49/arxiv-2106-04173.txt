On the stability of shear flows of Prandtl type for the steady Navier-Stokes equations
Qi Chen, Di Wu, Zhifei Zhang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the stability of shear flows of Prandtl type as ( U ⁡ ( y / ν ) , 0 ) \big(U(y/\sqrt{\nu}),0\big) for the steady Navier-Stokes equations under a natural spectral assumption on the linearized NS operator. We develop a direct energy method combined with compact method to solve the Orr-Sommerfeld equation.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
The problem and main result

In this paper, we study the steady Navier-Stokes equations with small viscosity
\nu
on the half-plane
\Omega_{\theta}=\mathbb{T}_{\theta}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
, where
\mathbb{T}_{\theta}
is a torus with the period
2\pi\theta
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &v^{\nu}\cdot\nabla v^{\nu}-\nu\Delta v^{\nu}+\nabla q^{\nu}=g^{\nu},\quad(x,y)\in\Omega_{\theta},\\
&\nabla\cdot v^{\nu}=0,\quad(x,y)\in\Omega_{\theta},\\
&v^{\nu}|_{y=0}=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}_{\theta},\end{aligned}\right.

where
v^{\nu}=(v_{1}^{\nu},v_{2}^{\nu})
and
q^{\nu}
are the unknown velocity and pressure of the fluid respectively, and
g^{\nu}
is a given external force.

We are concerned with the stability of shear flows of Prandtl type as
U^{\nu}(x,y)=(U(y/\sqrt{\nu}),0)
, which is
a solution of (
1.1
) with
g^{\nu}=-\nu\partial_{y}^{2}U^{\nu}
and
q^{\nu}=0
. We make the following structure assumptions on
U
:

(1.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&U(0)=0,\quad U(Y)>0\ \text{in}\ Y>0,\quad\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}U(Y)=1,\\
&\partial_{Y}U(0)>0,\quad\sum_{k=1,2}\sup_{Y\geq 0}(1+Y)^{3}|\partial_{Y}^{k}U(Y)|<\infty.\end{split}

The assumption (
1.2
) implies that

(1.3)

\displaystyle U(Y)\geq C^{-1}\min(1,Y)\sim Y/(1+Y).

In this paper, we take the external force
g^{\nu}=-\nu\partial_{y}^{2}U^{\nu}+f^{\nu}
so that
u^{\nu}=v^{\nu}-U^{\nu}
satisfies

(1.4)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}})\partial_{x}u^{\nu}+\Big(u_{2}^{\nu}\partial_{y}U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}),0\Big)-\nu\Delta u^{\nu}+\nabla p^{\nu}=-u^{\nu}\cdot\nabla u^{\nu}+f^{\nu},\\
&\nabla\cdot u^{\nu}=0,\\
&u^{\nu}|_{y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{n},n\in\mathbb{Z}
the projection on the
n-
th Fourier mode in
x
variable:

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{n}u)(x,y)=u_{n}(y)e^{\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}x},\quad\tilde{n}=\frac{n}{\theta},\quad u_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{2\pi\theta}\int_{0}^{2\pi\theta}e^{-\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}x}u(x,y)dx.

We denote

\mathcal{Q}_{0}=I-\mathcal{P}_{0},

where
I
is the identity operator. It is easy to see that
P_{0}u^{\nu}=\big(u_{0,1}^{\nu},0\big)
due to
\nabla\cdot u^{\nu}=0
.

In a recent work
[
9
]
, D. Gerard-Varet and Y. Maekawa proved the following stability result:

there exist small constants
\nu_{0},\theta_{0}
so that if
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
and
0<\theta\leq\theta_{0}
and
f^{\nu}=\mathcal{Q}_{0}f^{\nu}
with
\|f^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\nu^{\frac{3}{4}}|\log\nu|^{-1}
,
then it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u^{\nu}_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+&\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{y}u^{\nu}_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\|u^{\nu}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
+&\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{0}u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}|\log\nu|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

The smallness assumption
\theta\leq\theta_{0}
plays the key role in the proof of the stability.

The aim of this paper is twofold. The first aim of this paper is to replace the smallness assumption
\theta\leq\theta_{0}
by the following more natural condition:

\theta\in\Sigma(U,\nu),

where the set
\Sigma(U,\nu)
consists of the positive number
\theta
so that the following linearized Navier-Stokes equations have a unique solution
u^{\nu}\in H^{2}(\Omega_{\theta})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{\theta})
for
f^{\nu}\in L^{2}(\Omega_{\theta})
:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}})\partial_{x}u^{\nu}+\Big(u_{2}^{\nu}\partial_{y}U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}),0\Big)-\nu\Delta u^{\nu}+\nabla p^{\nu}=f^{\nu},\\
&\nabla\cdot u^{\nu}=0,\\
&u^{\nu}|_{y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

The set
\Sigma(U,\nu)
is not empty, since small
\theta
belongs to this set, which has been proved in
[
9
]
.
In fact, our assumption is equivalent to the spectral condition:
0
is not an eigenvalue of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\nu}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\nu}u=\mathrm{P}^{\nu}\Big(U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}})\partial_{x}u+\Big(u_{2}\partial_{y}U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}),0\Big)\Big)-\nu\mathrm{P}^{\nu}\Delta u,

where
\mathrm{P}^{\nu}
is the Helmoholtz-Leray projection.
It is very interesting to determine appropriate structure assumptions on
U
such that
0\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})
.

The second aim of this paper is to solve the Orr-Sommerfeld equation by developing a direct method in the sprit of our paper
[
6
]
,
which should be independent of interest. The proof in
[
9
]
used the Rayleigh-Airy iteration method introduced in
[
13
,
10
]
to solve the Orr-Sommerfeld equation.

Now we state our main result.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist positive constants
\nu_{0}
and
\eta
such that the following result holds for
0<\nu\leq\nu_{0}
: if
\theta\in\Sigma(U,\nu)
and
f^{\nu}=\mathcal{Q}_{0}f^{\nu}
with
\|f^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\nu^{\frac{3}{4}}|\log\nu|^{-1}
, then the unique solution
u^{\nu}
to (
1.4
) satisfies

(1.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|u^{\nu}_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+&\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{y}u^{\nu}_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\|u^{\nu}_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
+&\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{0}u^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}|\log\nu|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where
C
is independent of
\theta
and
\nu_{0}
.

Remark 1.2
.

When
\theta
is small, the spectral condition holds and our result is the same as one in
[
9
]
. For
\theta
not small but
\theta\in\Sigma(U,\nu)
, our result is new.

Remark 1.3
.

The works
[
10
,
6
]
consider the stability of shear flows of Prandtl type for the unsteady Navier-Stokes equations for the perturbations in the Gevrey class. In such case, it is enough to establish the resolvent estimates for the linearized operator when the spectral parameter lies in an unstable part of the resolvent set. While, in the steady case, one needs to establish the resolvent estimates when the spectral parameter equals to zero. However, it remains unknown whether
0
lies in the resolvent set of
\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})
for general period
2\pi\theta
in
x
. This is the essential reason why we impose the spectral condition
0\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})
.

1.2.
Some known results

Most of earlier mathematical works are devoted to the study of the inviscid limit of the unsteady Navier-Stokes equations.
In the absence of the boundary, the inviscid limit from the Navier-Stokes equations to the Euler equations has been justified in various functional settings
[
23
,
34
,
2
,
30
,
4
,
1
,
29
]
. In the presence of the boundary, under the Navier-slip boundary condition, the inviscid limit has been also established in
[
3
,
17
,
31
,
18
,
40
,
39
]
.

For non-slip boundary condition, due to the mismatch of the boundary condition between the Navier-Stokes equations and the Euler equations, Prandtl introduced the following boundary layer expansion:

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}u^{\nu}_{1}(t,x,y)=u^{e}_{1}(t,x,y)+u^{BL}\big(t,x,\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big)+O(\sqrt{\nu}),\\
u^{\nu}_{2}(t,x,y)=u^{e}_{2}(t,x,y)+\sqrt{\nu}v^{BL}\big(t,x,\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big)+O(\sqrt{\nu}),\end{array}\right.

where
(u_{1}^{\nu},u_{2}^{\nu})
is the solution of the 2-D Navier-Stokes equations on the half space with non-slip boundary condition,
(u_{1}^{e},u_{2}^{e})
is the solution of the 2-D Euler equations, and
(u^{p},v^{p})=\big(u^{e}_{1}(t,x,0)+u^{BL}(t,x,Y),\partial_{y}u^{e}_{2}(t,x,0)Y+v^{BL}(t,x,Y)\big)
satisfies the Prandtl equation. To our knowledge, the Prandtl expansion (
1.2
) was justified only in some special cases:
the analytic data
[
33
,
37
]
, the initial vorticity vanishing near the boundary
[
28
,
7
]
, as well as the domain and the data with a circular symmetry
[
27
,
32
]
. Initiated by Kato
[
24
]
, there were many works devoted to the conditional convergence to the Euler equations
[
35
,
36
,
25
]
. These conditions can be confirmed for the data which is analytic near the boundary
[
26
,
38
]
.

Grenier
[
12
]
studied the Prandtl expansion of shear type flows as

(1.9)

\displaystyle u^{\nu}_{s}=\big(U^{e}(t,y),0\big)+\Big(U^{BL}\big(t,\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big),0\Big).

When the shear flow
U^{BL}(0,Y)
is linearly unstable for the Euler equations, he proved the
H^{1}
instability of this expansion, and also
L^{\infty}
instability in
[
14
]
. In fact, even for the shear flows which are linearly stable for the Euler equations,
Guo, Grenier and Nguyen
[
13
]
proved that it could be linearly unstable for the Navier-Stokes equations when
\nu
is very small.
When
U^{BL}(t,Y)
is a monotone and concave function, Gerard-Varet, Masmoudi and Maekawa
[
10
]
(see also
[
6
]
) proved the stability of the Prandtl expansion (
1.9
) for the perturbations in the Gevrey class(see
[
11
]
for general cases).

Guo and Nguyen
[
16
]
considered the Prandtl expansion of the steady Navier-Stokes equations over a moving plate.
See
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
for more relevant works. For the steady Navier-Stokes equations with non-slip boundary condition, Gerard-Varet and Maekawa
[
9
]
proved the stability of shear flows
\big(U\big(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big),0\big)
in the Sobolev space. Guo and Iyer
[
15
]
proved the local in time stability of the Blasius flow, and Iyer and Masmoudi
[
22
]
recently proved the global stability.
See also
[
8
]
for a different method.

1.3.
Sketch of the proof

The road map of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is as follows.

The key step is to study the following linearized NS system for each Fourier mode:

(1.10)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}})u_{n}+\Big(u_{n,2}\partial_{y}U(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}),0\Big)-\nu(\partial_{y}^{2}-\tilde{n}^{2})u_{n}+(\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}p_{n},\partial_{y}p_{n})=f_{n},\quad y>0,\\
&\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}u_{n,1}+\partial_{y}u_{n,2}=0,\quad y>0,\\
&u_{n}|_{y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where
u_{n}=u_{n}(y)
is the
n-
th Fourier mode of the velocity
u
. The divergence-free condition and homogeneous Dirichlet condition imply
u_{0}=(u_{0,1},0)
. The main ingredient of this paper is to establish the following estimates(see Proposition
5.2
):

(1)

if
0<|\tilde{n}|\leq\delta_{0}\nu^{-\frac{3}{4}}
and
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
, then

\displaystyle|\tilde{n}|^{\frac{2}{3}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+|\tilde{n}|^{\frac{1}{3}}\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{y}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

(2)

if
|\tilde{n}|\geq\delta_{0}\nu^{-\frac{3}{4}}
and
\theta\in(0,\theta_{0}]
, then

\displaystyle|\tilde{n}|^{2}\nu\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+|\tilde{n}|\nu\|\partial_{y}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3)

if
\theta>\theta_{0}
and
\theta\in\Sigma(U,\nu)
, then

\displaystyle|\tilde{n}|\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|\tilde{n}|\|\partial_{y}u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}.

Compared with
[
9
]
, the result in the case of
\theta>\theta_{0}
and
\theta\in\Sigma(U,\nu)
is completely new.

With the help of the above estimates for the linearized system, we can prove nonlinear stability result by using a fixed point argument in the following functional space:

\displaystyle X_{\nu,\varepsilon}=\big\{u:\|u\|_{X_{\nu}}\leq\varepsilon\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}|\log\nu|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big\},

where

\displaystyle\|u\|_{X_{\nu}}=\|u_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\partial_{y}u_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}+\sum_{n\neq 0}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\nu^{-\frac{1}{4}}\|\mathcal{Q}_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}+\nu^{\frac{1}{4}}\|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{0}u\|_{L^{2}}.

See section 5.3 for the details.

Now we introduce main ideas of the proof for the estimates (1)-(3).

For the case of
\tilde{n}\geq C\nu^{-\frac{3}{4}}
, the diffusion part in (
1.10
) can control the other terms.
Thus, the desired estimate can be proved via standard energy method to (
1.10
).

For the case of
0<\tilde{n}\leq C\nu^{-\frac{3}{4}}
, the situation is much more complicated.
As in
[
9
]
, we need to reformulate the system in terms of the stream function
\phi_{n}
and make a change of variable.
Recall that

\displaystyle u_{n,1}=\partial_{y}\phi_{n},\quad u_{n,2}=-\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}\phi_{n}.

Hence, the stream function
\phi_{n}
satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}U\big(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big)(\partial_{y}^{2}-\tilde{n}^{2})\phi_{n}+\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}\partial_{y}^{2}U\big(\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}\big)\phi_{n}+\nu(\partial_{y}^{2}-\tilde{n}^{2})^{2}\phi_{n}=\mathrm{i}\tilde{n}f_{n,2}-\partial_{y}f_{n,1},\quad y>0,\\
&\phi_{n}|_{y=0}=\partial_{y}\phi_{n}|_{y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Now we rescale the variable
y
to
Y=\frac{y}{\sqrt{\nu}}
and introduce

\displaystyle\phi(Y)={\nu^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\phi_{n}(y),\quad f(Y)=\nu^{\frac{1}{2}}f_{n}(y).

Then
\phi(Y)
satisfies the Orr-Sommerfeld equation

(1.11)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi-U^{\prime\prime}\phi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi=-f_{2}-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}f_{1},\quad Y>0,\\
&\phi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\phi|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

where

\displaystyle\alpha=\tilde{n}\sqrt{\nu},\quad\varepsilon=\tilde{n}^{-1}=\frac{\theta}{n}.

To solve (
1.11
), we first make the following important decomposition to the solution
\phi=\phi_{0}+\phi_{1}
with

\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
solving

(1.12)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{Y}(U\partial_{Y}\phi_{0})-\alpha^{2}U\phi_{0}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi_{0}=-f_{2}-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}f_{1},\\
&\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(1.13)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{1}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi_{1}-U^{\prime\prime}\phi_{1}=\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0}),\\
&\phi_{1}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\phi_{1}|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

For the system (
1.12
), we can obtain our estimate by a direct energy method due to good divergence structure of main part
(see Lemma
4.3
). While, for the system (
1.13
), the source term
\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0})
has a good decay in
Y
and takes the divergence structure. To solve (
1.13
), we consider the following two cases:

In the case when
\varepsilon
is small, we first solve the following system with artificial boundary conditions:

(1.14)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}w|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

In this case, the diffusion term
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi
could be viewed as a perturbation
in some sense. More precisely, we can close our estimates by solving the Airy equation

\displaystyle Uw+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w=U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+f,\quad\partial_{Y}w|_{Y=0}=0,

and the Rayleigh equation

\displaystyle U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=f-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=0.

Thanks to good boundary condition on
w
and our structure assumption on
U
, we can obtain our desired estimates by a direct energy method and using Rayleigh’s trick. See section 2 for the details. In order to match the boundary condition, we need to construct the boundary layer corrector via solving the system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})W_{b}+UW_{b}-U^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{b}=0,\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Phi_{b}=W_{b},\\
&\Phi_{b}|_{Y=0}=0,\ \partial_{Y}\Phi_{b}|_{Y=0}=1.\end{aligned}\right.

For
1\leq|\alpha|<\infty
, the boundary corrector
W_{b}
could be chosen as a perturbation of the Airy function. For
0<|\alpha|\leq 1
, we need to use the slow mode and fast mode to construct the boundary layer corrector as in
[
9
]
.

In the case when
\varepsilon
is not small, the diffusion term can not be viewed as a perturbation. So, the above argument or Rayleigh-Airy iteration
does not work. In this case, we develop the compactness method to solve the system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\varphi|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

More precisely, when
\varepsilon\geq c
and
|\alpha|\leq C
, we can use the compactness method to establish the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}.

To obtain strong convergence of the sequence, we also need to establish the weighted estimates such as

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\big(\varepsilon\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\big),

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Yw\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\big).

To arrive a contradiction, we need to assume that the homogeneous system

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w+Uw-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=0,\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi=w,\quad\partial_{Y}\varphi|_{Y=0}=\varphi|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

has only trivial solution
\varphi=0
. This assumption exactly corresponds to our spectral condition
0\notin\Sigma(U,\nu)
.
See section 4.2 for the details.

2.
Orr-sommerfeld equation with artificial boundary condition

In this section, we study the Orr-Sommerfeld equation with artificial boundary condition

(2.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\quad f(0)=0,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}w|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Compared with the non-slip condition, this kind of boundary condition allows us to pick enough information from the Airy type structure in the Orr-Sommerfeld equation. Our main idea is as follows.

(1)

We first treat nonlocal term as a perturbation. That is, we write (
2.1
) as the following Airy type equation:

\displaystyle Airy[w]:=Uw+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w=\tilde{f}:=U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+f,\quad\partial_{Y}w|_{Y=0}=0.

Then we establish the estimates for general
\tilde{f}
with
(1+Y)^{2}\tilde{f}\in L^{2}
.

(2)

To close the estimates, we need to control
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}
. For this purpose, we treat
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi
as a perturbation. That is, we write (
2.1
) as the following Rayleigh type equation:

\displaystyle Ray[\varphi]:=U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=\bar{f}:=f-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=0,

which brings us an useful estimate of
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}
.

Now we state our main result of this section.

Proposition 2.1
.

Assume that
U
satisfies the structure assumption (
1.2
). Then there exists a small positive number
c_{1}>0
such that if
0<\varepsilon\leq c_{1}
and
\alpha\neq 0
, then for any
f\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
(1+Y)^{2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, there exists a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{4}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to (
2.1
) satisfying

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(1+Y)w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).

2.1.
Estimates for the Airy equation

We consider the following system with different boundary conditions

(2.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=w|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}w|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

These two boundary conditions are compatible with the dissipative structure involved in these two systems, which allows us to obtain the following estimates for both systems.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
. Let
\varphi
be the solution to (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) with
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then we have

\bullet
If
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\|Uw\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

\bullet
If
(1+Y)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then it holds that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Yw\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|Y\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon\|Y(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}.

\bullet
If
(1+Y)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. If
f
is replaced by
\partial_{Y}f
or
f/Y
, then the solution
\varphi
to (
2.2
) satisfies

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\sqrt{U}w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Remark 2.3
.

The existence of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) can be obtained by the method of continuity. In fact, after replacing
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi
by
\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi-il(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi
with
l
large enough, one can obtain the existence of the solution, which along with the fact that the above estimates hold for any
l>0
implies the existence of (
2.2
) and (
2.3
).

Remark 2.4
.

Without the assumption
\varphi|_{Y=0}=0
, we can also obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\|Uw\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We first consider the case of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Let
\varphi
be the solution to (
2.2
) or (
2.3
). Taking inner product with
w
, we have

\displaystyle\langle f,w\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=

\displaystyle\langle Uw+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,w\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

By the structure assumption (
1.2
) on
U
, there exists
Y_{0}>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle U(Y)\geq C^{-1}Y,\quad\text{if}\quad Y\leq Y_{0};\qquad U(Y)\geq C^{-1},\quad\text{if}\quad Y\geq Y_{0},

then we have the following interpolation

(2.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|w\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\|w\|_{L^{2}\big([0,\epsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{0}]\big)}+\|w\|_{L^{2}\big([\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{0},+\infty)\big)}\\
&\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}Y_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}+C\max(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}Y_{0}^{-\frac{1}{2}},1)\|\sqrt{U}w\|_{L^{2}\big([\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}Y_{0},+\infty)\big)}\\
&\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{U}w\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Combining with (
2.4
) and using the fact that
\varepsilon\leq 1
, we get

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{12}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{4}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{3}{4}}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Applying (
2.4
) again, we obtain

(2.7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Multiplying
f
on both sides of the first equation in (
2.2
) or (
2.3
) and integrating by parts, we get

(2.8)

\displaystyle\|Uw\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\varepsilon\mathbf{Re}\big(\mathrm{i}\langle Uw,(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\rangle\big)=\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Noticing that

\displaystyle\big|\mathbf{Re}\big(\mathrm{i}\langle Uw,(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\rangle\big)\big|=\big|\mathbf{Im}\big(\langle U^{\prime}w,\partial_{Y}w\rangle+\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|U^{\prime}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-1}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used (
2.7
) in the last inequality. Then combining with (
2.8
) and (
2.7
), the above inequality implies

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|Uw\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

This proves the first statement of this lemma.

Now we turn to prove the second statement. Let
(1+Y)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
For this purpose, we introduce the following cut-off function. Let
\chi\geq 0
be a
C^{3}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
cut-off function, such that

\displaystyle\chi(Y)=1,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[0,1);\quad\chi(Y)=0,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[2,+\infty),

let
\chi_{R}(Y)=\chi(Y/R),\ R>1
, then

\displaystyle\chi_{R}(Y)=1,\

\displaystyle\text{if}\ Y\in[0,R);\quad\chi(Y)=0,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[2R,+\infty),

\displaystyle R|\partial_{Y}\chi_{R}|+R|\partial_{Y}^{2}\chi_{R}|\leq C,

which implies that

(2.10)

\displaystyle\ |\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}(Y))|+|\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R}(Y))|\leq C.

Hence,
Y\chi_{R}w
satisfies (
2.2
) with the source term
Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w
. That is,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})(Y\chi_{R}w)+U(Y\chi_{R}w)=Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w,\\
&(Y\chi_{R}w)|_{Y=0}=\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}(Y\chi_{R}w)=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Then by replacing
w
and
f
by
Y\chi_{R}w
and
Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w
respectively in (
2.9
), we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\big\|\big(\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}w),\alpha Y\chi_{R}w\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon\big\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\big(Y\chi_{R}w\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|Y\chi_{R}f\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

Then by (
2.9
) again, we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\big\|\big(\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}w),\alpha Y\chi_{R}w\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon\big\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\big(Y\chi_{R}w\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+C(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})\|f\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, by (
2.9
) and (
2.10
), we notice that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}w)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}-C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\geq\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}-C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R}w)\|_{L^{2}}\geq\varepsilon\|Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}-C\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}-C\varepsilon\|w\|_{L^{2}}\geq\varepsilon\|Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}-C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}Y\chi_{R}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\big\|\big(Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}w,\alpha Y\chi_{R}w\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\big\|Y\chi_{R}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}.

Leting
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we conclude

(2.11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|Yw\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\big\|\sqrt{U}Yw\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\varepsilon\|Y(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Now we are left with the estimate of
\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}
.
Taking inner product with
-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})
, we get

(2.12)

\displaystyle\big\langle Uw+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle=\big\langle f,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle.

We first notice that

\displaystyle\big\langle Uw,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle=

\displaystyle\langle w,-\varphi\rangle-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\langle w,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}-\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|w\bar{\varphi}/U\big\|_{L^{1}},

which implies that

(2.13)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq&\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|w\bar{\varphi}/U\big\|_{L^{1}}+\big|\big\langle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle\big|\\
&+\big|\big\langle f,\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle\big|.\end{split}

To deal with
\big\|w\bar{\varphi}/U\big\|_{L^{1}}
, we first notice that
1/U\leq C(1/Y+\sqrt{U})
from (
2.5
). Hence, we have

(2.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big\|w\bar{\varphi}/U\big\|_{L^{1}}&\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}}\big\|\varphi/Y\big\|_{L^{2}}+C\big\|\sqrt{U}Yw\big\|_{L^{2}}\big\|\varphi/Y\big\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+C\big\|\sqrt{U}Yw\big\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, by the fact
|U^{\prime}Y/U|\leq C
, we have

\displaystyle\big\|\partial_{Y}\big(\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|\varphi/Y\|_{L^{2}}\|U^{\prime}Y/U\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.

Since
\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})|_{Y=0}=0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\big|\big\langle\partial_{Y}w,\partial_{Y}\big(\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)\big\rangle\big|+\varepsilon\big|\langle\alpha^{2}w,-\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\rangle\big|

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\big\|\partial_{Y}\big(\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|\alpha w\|_{L^{2}}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}.

By the fact that
\big|Y/\big((1+Y)U\big)\big|\leq C
and
(1+Y)f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\big|\big\langle f,\varphi/(U+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}})\big\rangle\big|\leq&\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}\big\|Y/\big((1+Y)U\big)\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\varphi/Y\|_{L^{2}}\\
\leq&C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Summing up (
2.13
), (
2.14
), (
2.1
) and (
2.16
), we conclude

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq&C\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\big\|\sqrt{U}Yw\big\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

which along with (
2.7
) and (
2.11
) gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Finally, let us turn to show the last statement of this lemma. Since the case of
\partial_{Y}f
and
\frac{f}{Y}
are similar, here we only consider the case of
\frac{f}{Y}
. We first notice that

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|w/Y\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}.

Then we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\big\|\sqrt{U}w\big\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

From (
2.6
), we know that

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{U}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}},

which gives

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{2}{3}}\|f\|_{L^{2}}.

This completes the proof of this lemma.
∎

2.2.
Estimates for the Rayleigh equation

We consider the Rayleigh equation

(2.17)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=-\partial_{Y}f_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2},\\
&\varphi|_{Y=0},\quad\partial_{Y}f_{1}|_{Y=0}=\alpha^{2}f_{2}|_{Y=0}.\end{aligned}\right.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
(\varphi,f_{1},f_{2})
solve (
2.17
). Then we have

\displaystyle\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C(1+|\alpha|^{2})(|\alpha|^{-2}|f_{1}(0)|+\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}).

Proof.

We first construct two cut-off functions to correct
\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)
and
f_{1}(0)
. Let
\rho_{0}
be a cut-off function such that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho_{0}\mathrm{d}Y=1,\quad\rho_{0}\geq 0,\quad\rho_{0}(0)=0,\quad\left\|\dfrac{\rho_{0}}{U}\right\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{0}]\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,

where
\sigma[\rho_{0}]:=\int_{Y}^{\infty}\rho_{0}(Y_{1})dY_{1}
.
Let
\rho_{1}
be a cut-off function such that

\displaystyle\rho_{1}(0)=1,\quad\int_{0}^{+\infty}\rho_{1}\mathrm{d}Y=0,\quad\|\rho_{1}\|_{H^{1}}+\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{1}]\|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

Then we decompose
-\partial_{Y}f_{1}
as follow

(2.18)

\displaystyle-\partial_{Y}f_{1}=F_{1,1}+F_{1,2},

where

\displaystyle F_{1,1}(Y)=-\partial_{Y}f_{1}-f_{1}(0)\rho_{0}(Y)+\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}(Y),

\displaystyle F_{1,2}=f_{1}(0)\rho_{0}(Y)-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}(Y).

Clearly,
\int_{0}^{+\infty}F_{1,1}\mathrm{d}Y=0
,
F_{1,1}|_{Y=0}=0
.
We decompose
\varphi=\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2}
as

(2.19)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U\partial_{Y}^{2}\varphi_{1}-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi_{1}=F_{1,1},\\
&\varphi_{1}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi_{2}-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi_{2}=\alpha^{2}U\varphi_{1}+F_{1,2}+\alpha^{2}f_{2},\\
&\varphi_{2}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

We first show the estimate of
\varphi_{1}
. We notice that equation (
2.19
) can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\bigg[U^{2}\partial_{Y}\big(\dfrac{\varphi_{1}}{U}\big)\bigg]=F_{1,1},\quad\varphi_{1}|_{Y=0}=0.

Since
F_{1,1}
satisfies the compatible condition of (
2.19
), the solution
\varphi_{1}
to (
2.19
) can be represented by the following formula

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(Y)=U(Y)\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\dfrac{\int_{Y_{1}}^{+\infty}F_{1,1}(Y_{2})\mathrm{d}Y_{2}}{U^{2}(Y_{1})}\mathrm{d}Y_{1}=\mathcal{L}[\sigma[F_{1,1}]](Y),

where the linear operator
\mathcal{L}[\cdot]
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[f](Y)=U(Y)\int_{Y}^{\infty}\frac{f(Y_{1})}{U^{2}(Y_{1})}dY_{1}.

According to Lemma
A.4
and
F_{1,1}=-\partial_{Y}f_{1}-F_{1,2}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\big\|\mathcal{L}[\sigma[F_{1,1}]]\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[F_{1,1}]\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[\partial_{Y}f_{1}]\big\|_{L^{2}}+C\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[F_{1,2}]\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|(1+Y)f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+C\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[F_{1,2}]\big\|_{L^{2}}.

By the definition of
F_{1,2}
and the condition
\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)=\alpha^{2}f_{2}(0)
, we have that

\displaystyle\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[F_{1,2}]\big\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq|f_{1}(0)|\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{0}]\big\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}|f_{2}(0)|\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{1}]\big\|_{L^{2}},

which along with the fact
\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{0}]\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)\sigma[\rho_{1}]\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
implies that

\displaystyle\big\|(1+Y)\sigma[F_{1,2}]\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(|f_{1}(0)|+|\alpha^{2}f_{2}(0)|)\leq C(|f_{1}(0)|+\alpha^{2}\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}).

This shows that

(2.21)

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|(1+Y)f_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+|f_{1}(0)|+|\alpha|^{2}\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\big).

Now we turn to consider the estimate of
\varphi_{2}
. We also rewrite the equation as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{Y}\bigg[U^{2}\partial_{Y}\big(\dfrac{\varphi_{2}}{U}\big)\bigg]-\alpha^{2}U\varphi_{2}=\alpha^{2}U\varphi_{1}+F_{1,2}+\alpha^{2}f_{2},\\
&\varphi_{2}|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Multiplying both sides of the first equation by
-\varphi_{2}/U
, we get by integration by parts that

(2.22)

\displaystyle\left\|U\partial_{Y}\big(\dfrac{\varphi_{2}}{U}\big)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\alpha^{2}\langle\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\rangle-\langle F_{1,2}+\alpha^{2}f_{2},\varphi_{2}/U\rangle.

For the second term on the right side, we have

(2.23)

\displaystyle\langle F_{1,2}+\alpha^{2}f_{2},\varphi_{2}/U\rangle=f_{1}(0)\langle\rho_{0}/U,\varphi_{2}\rangle+\big\langle(-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2})/U,\varphi_{2}\big\rangle.

From the definition of
\rho_{0}
, we infer that

(2.24)

\displaystyle\big|f_{1}(0)\langle\rho_{0}/U,\varphi_{2}\rangle\big|\leq|f_{1}(0)|\left\|\rho_{0}/U\right\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|f_{1}(0)|\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

According to the boundary condition
(-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2})|_{Y=0}=0
and the fact that
U\geq C^{-1}Y/(1+Y)
, we get by Hardy’s inequality that

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle(-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2})/U,\varphi_{2}\big\rangle\big|\leq C\big\|\big((-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2})(1+Y)\big)/Y\big\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|-\partial_{Y}f_{1}(0)\rho_{1}+\alpha^{2}f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(|f_{2}(0)|+\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\big)\alpha^{2}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{2}\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Summing up with (
2.22
), (
2.23
) and (
2.24
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\left\|U\partial_{Y}\big(\dfrac{\varphi_{2}}{U}\big)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\alpha^{2}\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C|f_{1}(0)|\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+C\alpha^{2}\|f_{2}\|_{H^{1}}\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

which along with (

Lemma 2.6

Let

3.
Boundary layer corrector

This section is devoted to constructing the boundary layer corrector. That is, we need to construct the solution to the following homogeneous system

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})W_{b}+UW_{b}-U^{\prime\prime}\Phi_{b}=0,\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Phi_{b}=W_{b},\\
&\Phi_{b}|_{Y=0}=0,\ \partial_{Y}\Phi_{b}|_{Y=0}=1.\end{aligned}\right.

We construct the solution
W_{b}
by finding two special solution (fast and slow mode) to the homogeneous Orr-Sommerfeld equation to match the boundary condition in (
3.1
). The fast mode is a solution to the homogeneous Orr-Sommerfeld equation built around the Airy function, and the slow mode is built around a solution to the Rayleigh equation.

We shall see that for
1\leq|\alpha|<\infty

is a perturbation of the Airy function. In this case, the “stream fucntion”
\Phi_{a}
of Airy function is equipped with zero boundary condition. While, for

, we choose the fast decay part
\Phi_{a,f}
of
\Phi_{a}
as the “stream function” of Airy function. Since
\Phi_{a,f}(0)\neq 0

We always assume
\varepsilon|\alpha|^{3}\ll 1
throughout this section.

0<|\alpha|\leq 1
, then we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|W_{b}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\alpha\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

Proof.

We first consider the case of
1\leq|\alpha|<\infty
. We define
\Phi_{b}(Y)=\Phi(Y)/\partial_{Y}\Phi(0)
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|W_{b}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

Now we turn to the case of
0<|\alpha|\leq 1
. We may assume
0<\alpha\leq 1
. Recall that

\displaystyle\Phi_{f}(0)=\Phi_{a,f}(0)=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}W_{a}(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z.

We define

\displaystyle\Phi_{b}=A\Phi_{f}+B\phi_{s},

where

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{\partial_{Y}\Phi_{f}(0)-\Phi_{f}(0)\partial_{Y}\phi_{s}(0)},\quad B=-\frac{\Phi_{f}(0)}{\partial_{Y}\Phi_{f}(0)-\Phi_{f}(0)\partial_{Y}\phi_{s}(0)}.

It is easy to check that
\Phi_{b}(0)=0,\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b}(0)=1
.
From Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
3.7
, we infer that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\Phi_{f}(0)-\Phi_{f}(0)\partial_{Y}\phi_{s}(0)=-\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{3(U^{\prime}(0))^{\frac{2}{3}}Ai^{\prime}(0)}+\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{3}}\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}Ai(0)(U^{\prime}(0))^{\frac{2}{3}}}{Ai^{\prime}(0)\alpha}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon+\varepsilon^{\frac{17}{12}}\alpha^{-1}).

On the other hand, notice that

\displaystyle\left|-\dfrac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{3(U^{\prime}(0))^{\frac{2}{3}}Ai^{\prime}(0)}+\dfrac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{3}}\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}Ai(0)(U^{\prime}(0))^{\frac{2}{3}}}{Ai^{\prime}(0)\alpha}\right|\geq C\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}}{\alpha}\right),

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{Y}\Phi_{f}(0)-\Phi_{f}(0)\partial_{Y}\phi_{s}(0)\right|\geq C\Big(\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}+\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{4}{3}}}{\alpha}\Big).

Thus,

\displaystyle|A|\leq C\frac{\alpha}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}(\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}})},\quad|B|\leq C\frac{\alpha\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}},

which along with Lemma
3.3
and Proposition
3.7
imply that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\frac{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}+\alpha\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}}{\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}+\alpha},\quad\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\Phi_{b}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}},\quad\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.
Orr-Sommerfeld equation with non-slip boundary condition

This section is devoted to solve the Orr-Sommerfeld equation (
1.11
). Since the source term of (
1.11
) belongs to
H^{-1}
, we decompose the solution
\phi
as
\phi=\phi_{0}+\phi_{1}
, where
\phi_{0}
and
\phi_{1}
solve the following system respectively,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{Y}(U\partial_{Y}\phi_{0})-\alpha^{2}U\phi_{0}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi_{0}=-f_{2}-\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}f_{1},\\
&\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}=0,\end{aligned}\right.

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{1}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\phi_{1}-U^{\prime\prime}\phi_{1}=\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0}),\\
&\phi_{1}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\phi_{1}|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 4.1
.

The main reason that we use this decomposition is as follows.

•

All of terms on the left hand side of (
4.1
) are the divergence form, so that we can obtain good estimates by simple energy method.

•

The source term in (
4.2
) satisfies
\int_{0}^{\infty}\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0})dY^{\prime}=0
, which reduces
|\alpha|^{-2}
order loss compared with general
L^{2}
source term.

Therefore, our main task of this section is to solve (
4.2
). For this purpose, we first focus on the Orr-Sommerfeld equation with general type source term:

(4.3)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi=f,\quad f(0)=0,\\
&w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\quad\varphi|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\varphi|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

For small
\varepsilon
, we could obtain estimates of the solution to (
4.3
) by applying the results in previous section. However, for general
\varepsilon>0
, we apply compactness argument under our spectral assumption
0\notin\sigma(\mathcal{L}_{\nu})
.

4.1.
Estimates for small viscosity
\varepsilon

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
c_{2},\ \delta_{*}>0
be the constants in Proposition
3.8
. Then if
\varepsilon|\alpha|^{3}\leq\delta_{*},\ 0<\varepsilon\leq c_{2}
, then for any
f\in H^{1}
with
f(0)=0
, there exists a unique solution
\varphi\in H^{4}\cap H^{2}_{0}
to the system (
4.3
) satisfying

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).

Proof.

Let
(w_{ar},\varphi_{ar})
solve the following OS equation with Neumann’s type boundary condition

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi_{ar}-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi_{ar}+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{2}\varphi_{ar}=f,\quad f(0)=0,\\
&w_{ar}=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi_{ar},\quad\varphi_{ar}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}w_{ar}|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

We take
(W_{b},\Phi_{b})
to be the boundary layer corrector satisfying (
3.1
).
By comparing the boundary conditions, we can decompose
w
as

(4.4)

\displaystyle w=w_{ar}-\partial_{Y}\varphi_{ar}(0)W_{b},\qquad\varphi=\varphi_{ar}-\partial_{Y}\varphi_{ar}(0)\Phi_{b}.

We point out that the existence of
(w_{ar},\varphi_{ar})
and
(W_{b},\Phi_{b})
is guaranteed by Proposition
2.1
and Proposition
3.8
respectively.
By Proposition
2.1
, we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w_{ar}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi_{ar},\alpha\varphi_{ar})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).

By the interpolation, we get

(4.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|\partial_{Y}\phi_{ar}(0)|\leq&\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi_{ar},\alpha\varphi_{ar})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|w_{ar}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi_{ar},\alpha\varphi_{ar})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\\
\leq&C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).\end{split}

For
(W_{b},\Phi_{b})
, we get by Proposition
3.8
that if
1\leq|\alpha|<+\infty
,

(4.7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|W_{b}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C,

and if
0<|\alpha|\leq 1
,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|W_{b}\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\alpha\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\Phi_{b},\alpha\Phi_{b})\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

Summing up (
4.4
)-(
4.8
) , we conclude that for
1\leq|\alpha|<\infty
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right),

and for
0<|\alpha|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle C\frac{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\alpha+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}}\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}})\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}f\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right).

This finishes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
. Then for any
f\in L^{2}
, there exists a unique solution to (
4.1
) satisfying

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|\alpha|^{-1}\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

This elliptic problem is uniquely solvable. Hence, it suffices to provide a priori estimate.
Taking the inner product with
\phi_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{+\infty}-U\big(|\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|^{2}+\alpha^{2}|\phi_{0}|^{2}\big)\mathrm{d}Y+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{2}+\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}f_{1})\phi_{0}\mathrm{d}Y.

Taking the real part and imaginary part of the above equation, we obtain

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\big\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}(f_{2}+\dfrac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}\partial_{Y}f_{1})\phi_{0}\mathrm{d}Y\right|\\
&\leq C|\alpha|^{-1}\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, using the structure assumptions on
U
, we can actually obtain the control of
\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
from
\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}
via the interpolation. Indeed, we notice that
1\leq C(U/Y)^{\frac{1}{3}}+C\sqrt{U(Y)}
and
\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}=\phi_{0}|_{Y=0}
, which implies that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|(U/Y)^{\frac{1}{3}}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}+C\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{|\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|}{Y})^{\frac{2}{3}}(U^{\frac{2}{3}}|\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}|^{\frac{4}{3}})dY\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}+C\alpha\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\frac{|\phi_{0}|}{Y})^{\frac{2}{3}}(U^{\frac{2}{3}}|\phi_{0}|^{\frac{4}{3}})dY\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+C\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})/Y\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}+C\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2}{3}}\left\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{3}}+C\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}},

which along with (
4.9
) gives

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{6}}+1\big)|\alpha|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Applying (
4.9
) again and using
\varepsilon\leq 1
, we conclude

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|\sqrt{U}(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C|\alpha|^{-1}\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f=(f_{1},f_{2})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{2})
. Then there exist small positive number
\delta_{*},c_{*}
such that for any
\varepsilon|\alpha|^{3}\leq\delta_{*}
,
0<\varepsilon\leq c_{*}
, there exists a unique solution
\phi\in H^{4}\cap H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to (
1.11
) satisfying

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}|\alpha|\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}|\alpha|\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}|\alpha|\|(\partial_{Y}\phi,\alpha\phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

We decompose
\phi
as
\phi=\phi_{0}+\phi_{1}
, where
\phi_{0},\ \phi_{1}
solve (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) respectively. By Lemma
4.2
, we get

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{6}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{1},\alpha\phi_{1})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{1},\alpha\phi_{1})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|(1+Y)^{2}\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}+\dfrac{1}{|\alpha|^{2}}\bigg|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{Y}(U^{\prime}\phi_{0})\mathrm{d}Y\bigg|\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\big\|\big(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\phi_{0}/(1+Y)\big)\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\partial_{Y}\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}.

We get by Lemma
4.3
and the interpolation that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{\infty}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}\phi_{0},\alpha\phi_{0})\|_{L^{2}}++\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C|\alpha|^{-1}\|(f_{1},f_{2})\|_{L^{2}}.

Summing up the above two inequalities, we finish the proof.
∎

4.2.
Estimates when
c_{2}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1

Definition 4.5
.

We say that
\varphi
is a weak solution to (
4.3
) if

\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R_{+}}),\ \partial_{Y}\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
, and for any
\xi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R_{+}})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\langle w,(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\xi\rangle+\langle Uw,\xi\rangle-\langle U^{\prime\prime}\varphi,\xi\rangle=\langle f,\xi\rangle,\quad w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi.

In the rest of this part, we always assume the following solvability.

Solvalbility Assumption (S-A)
: for
c_{2}\leq\varepsilon\leq 1
and
\alpha\neq 0
, there is no nontrivial weak solution to the homogeneous equation

(4.10)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w+Uw-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=0,\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi=w,\quad\partial_{Y}\varphi|_{Y=0}=\varphi|_{Y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Remark 4.6
.

The solvalbility assumption is ensured by our spectral condition.
For small
0<\varepsilon\leq c_{2}
, Lemma
4.2
can ensure that there only exists zero solution to (
4.10
). When
\alpha=0
, it is easy to find by the energy method that for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, there only exists zero solution to (
4.10
) .

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leq 1,\ \alpha\neq 0
. Let
(1+Y)^{2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
f(0)=0
be given. Suppose that
\varphi
is the solution to (
4.3
) with source term
f
and
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then we have

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\big(\varepsilon\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\big),

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Yw\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Proof.

First of all, we construct a
C^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
cut-off function
\chi\geq 0,
such that

\displaystyle\chi(Y)=1,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[0,1);\quad\chi(Y)=0,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[2,+\infty).

Let
\chi_{R}(Y)=\chi(Y/R),\ R>1
. Then

\displaystyle\chi_{R}(Y)=1,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[0,R);\quad\chi(Y)=0,\ \text{if}\ Y\in[2R,+\infty),

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1,2}|R^{k}\partial_{Y}^{k}\chi_{R}|\leq C,

which implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1,2}|(1+Y)^{k}\partial_{Y}^{k}\chi_{R}|\leq C.

Taking the inner production with
-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\in L^{2}
, we get

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\big\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle+\langle w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi\rangle+\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{U^{\prime\prime}Y\chi_{R}}{U}}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big\langle f,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle.

Thanks to
|\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})|\leq CR^{-1}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathbf{Re}\left(\langle w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Re}\left(\langle\partial_{Y}\varphi,\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\varphi\rangle+\langle\partial_{Y}\varphi,Y\chi_{R}\partial_{Y}\varphi\rangle+\alpha^{2}\langle\varphi,Y\chi_{R}\varphi\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\partial_{Y}|\varphi|^{2}/2,\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\big\rangle+\left\|\sqrt{Y\chi_{R}}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})|\varphi|^{2}\mathrm{d}Y+\left\|\sqrt{Y\chi_{R}}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq-CR^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{Y\chi_{R}}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we obtain

(4.12)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\sqrt{Y\chi_{R}}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq&\mathbf{Re}\left(\langle w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi\rangle\right)+CR^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&\varepsilon\left|\big\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle\right|+\left|\langle f,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\rangle\right|\\
&-\Big\|\sqrt{\frac{U^{\prime\prime}Y\chi_{R}}{U}}\varphi\Big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+CR^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\leq&\varepsilon\left|\big\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle\right|+\left|\langle f,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\rangle\right|\\
&+CR^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

We get by integration by parts that

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle\right|=\left|\big\langle\partial_{Y}w,\partial_{Y}\big(Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big)\big\rangle+\alpha^{2}\langle w,Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\big\langle(1+Y)\partial_{Y}w,\big(Y\chi_{R}/U(1+Y)\big)\partial_{Y}\varphi\big\rangle+\langle\alpha(1+Y)w,\big(Y\chi_{R}/U(1+Y)\big)\alpha\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|\langle\partial_{Y}w,\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}/U)\varphi\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\dfrac{Y\chi_{R}}{(1+Y)U}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\|(1+Y)\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)\alpha w\|_{L^{2}}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\quad+\left\|\dfrac{Y\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}/U)}{(1+Y)}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(1+Y)\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi/Y\|_{L^{2}},

which along with the fact that
U\geq C^{-1}Y/(1+Y)
,
\big|\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R}/U)\big|\leq C(1+Y)/Y
, implies

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left|\big\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\big\rangle\right|\leq C\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}.

Using the fact
U\geq C^{-1}Y/(1+Y)
again, we have

(4.14)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left|\langle f,-Y\chi_{R}\varphi/U\rangle\right|&\leq\left\|\dfrac{Y\chi_{R}}{(1+Y)U}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\|(1+Y)Yf\|_{L^{2}}\|\varphi/Y\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

Summing up (
4.12
), (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{Y\chi_{R}}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\big(R^{-1}|\alpha|^{-2}\|\alpha\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\big),

which gives by taking
R\rightarrow+\infty
that

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\big(\varepsilon\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}}\big).

This shows the first inequality of the lemma.

On the other hand,
Y\chi_{R}w
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})(Y\chi_{R}w)+U(Y\chi_{R}w)=Y\chi_{R}U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w.

For the source term on the right hand side, we have

\displaystyle\left\|Y\chi_{R}U^{\prime\prime}\varphi+Y\chi_{R}f+2\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}(Y\chi_{R})\partial_{Y}w+\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\partial_{Y}^{2}(Y\chi_{R})w\right\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+R^{-1}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Thanks to
(Y\chi_{R}w)|_{Y=0}=0
, we get by Lemma
2.2
that

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|(\partial_{Y},\alpha)(Y\chi_{R}w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Y\chi_{R}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+R^{-1}\|w\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Taking
R\rightarrow+\infty
, we obtain

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\frac{2}{3}}\|Y(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}\|Yw\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\big).

∎

Now we consider the case when
|\alpha|
has upper bound and
\varepsilon
has lower bound.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
M_{1}>0,\ 0<c_{3}\leq 1
and
0\neq|\alpha|\leq M_{1}
. Assume that the solvalbility assumption (S-A) holds. Let
\varphi
be the solution to (
4.3
) with
(1+Y)^{2}f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then it holds that for any
1\geq\varepsilon\geq c_{3}
,

\displaystyle\|w\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(1+Y)^{2}f\|_{L^{2}},

where constant
C
depends on
c_{3}
and
M_{1}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\alpha\geq 0
. We prove this proposition by a contradiction argument.
Assume that this proposition is not true. Then there exists a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha_{n},\ \varphi^{(n)},\ w^{(n)},\ f^{(n)}\}
satisfying

(4.15)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &c_{3}\leq\varepsilon_{n}\leq 1,\quad 0\neq|\alpha_{n}|\leq M_{1},\\
&\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2})w^{(n)}+Uw^{(n)}-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{(n)}=f^{(n)},\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}_{n})\varphi^{(n)}=w^{(n)},\quad\varphi^{(n)}|_{Y=0}=\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}|_{Y=0}=0,\\
&\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}=1,\quad\|(1+Y)^{2}f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\rightarrow 0,\end{aligned}\right.

such that we can take a subsequence
\{\varepsilon_{n},\ \alpha_{n},\ \varphi^{(n)},\ w^{(n)},\ f^{(n)}\}
( denoted by the same index) satisfying

(4.16)

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\rightarrow\varepsilon\geq c_{3},\quad\alpha_{n}\rightarrow\alpha\in[0,M_{1}],\quad\mathrm{as}\quad n\to\infty.

Then we obtain the following weak convergence results when
n\to\infty
:

•

If
\alpha=0
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}})\leq C.

Then

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}\rightharpoonup u_{1}\quad\text{in}\ H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\quad\text{with}\ u_{1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

\displaystyle\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)}\rightharpoonup\mathrm{i}u_{2}\quad\text{in}\ H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\quad\text{with}\ \mathrm{i}u_{2}\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

hence,

(4.17)

\displaystyle(w^{(n)},\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\rightharpoonup(\partial_{Y}u_{1},u_{1},\mathrm{i}u_{2})\quad\text{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\quad\text{with}\ (u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

•

If
\alpha\neq 0,
we may assume that
\alpha_{n}>0,
and thanks to
\alpha_{n}\geq\alpha/2
for large
n
, we can deduce that

\displaystyle\|\varphi^{(n)}\|_{H^{2}}\leq(|\alpha_{n}|^{-1}+1)\big(\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\big)\leq(2\alpha^{-1}+1).

Then
\|\varphi^{(n)}\|_{H^{2}}
is uniformly bounded, hence there exists
\varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, such that
\varphi^{(n)}\rightharpoonup\varphi
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. We get

(4.18)

\displaystyle(w^{(n)},\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\rightharpoonup((\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi,\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi):=(w,u_{1},\mathrm{i}u_{2})\quad\text{in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

\displaystyle\text{with}\ \varphi\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\,\textrm{and}\,(w,u_{1},\mathrm{i}u_{2})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Step 1. Strong convergence
.

Since
w,\varphi
and
f
all belong to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, we know that
(\partial_{y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w
is also in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Moreover, the following bound holds

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2})w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq

\displaystyle\|Uw^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|U^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}})+\|f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}.

Thanks to
\varepsilon_{n}\geq c_{3}>0
, we deduce that
\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2})w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}
are uniformly bounded with respect to
n
. We get by Lemma
A.2
that

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}_{n})w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Then we have

(4.19)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\ \text{are uniformly bounded}.

Thanks to
\|\partial_{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
, we get

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\ \text{are uniformly bounded}.

Thanks to Lemma
4.7
, we get

\displaystyle\big\|\sqrt{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\big(\varepsilon_{n}\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}w^{(n)},\alpha_{n}w^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}+\|(1+Y)^{2}f^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\big),

and

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{n}\|Y(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2})w^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{3}}_{n}\|Yw^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yf^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\varepsilon_{n}\|\partial_{Y}w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}\big).

Summing up, we arrive at

(4.21)

\displaystyle\|(1+Y)(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha_{n}^{2})w^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\big\|\sqrt{Y}(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\big\|_{L^{2}}+\|Yw^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}

are uniformly bounded. Then

(4.22)

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\sup_{n}\big(\|(w^{(n)},\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}[R,+\infty)}\big)=0.

Along with (
4.19
), (
4.20
) and (
4.22
), we deduce that as
n\to+\infty
,

(4.23)

\displaystyle(w^{(n)},\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\rightarrow(\partial_{Y}u_{1},u_{1},\mathrm{i}u_{2})\quad\text{strongly in}\ L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Moreover if
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

(4.24)

\displaystyle\varphi^{(n)}\rightarrow\varphi\quad\text{strongly in}\ H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

Step 2. The limit equation.

Let
\xi
be any
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
test function. Then we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}\langle w^{(n)},(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}_{n})\xi\rangle+\langle Uw^{(n)},\xi\rangle-\langle U^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{(n)},\xi\rangle=\langle f^{(n)},\xi\rangle,\quad w^{(n)}=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}_{n})\varphi^{(n)}.

•

If
\alpha\neq 0
, by (
4.24
) and passing to the limit for
n\to\infty
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\langle w,(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\xi\rangle+\langle Uw,\xi\rangle-\langle U^{\prime\prime}\varphi,\xi\rangle=0,\quad w=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi.

For the boundary condition, thanks to
\varphi^{(n)}\in H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\ \partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and (
4.24
), we deduce that
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\ \partial_{Y}\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.
This means that
\varphi
is a weak solution to (
4.10
). By the solvability assumption (S-A), we get
\varphi=0.

•

If
\alpha=0,
we define
\varphi(Y)=\int_{0}^{Y}u_{1}(Z)\mathrm{d}Z
. By (
4.23
), we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon_{n}\langle w^{(n)},(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2}_{n})\xi\rangle+\langle Uw^{(n)},\xi\rangle\longrightarrow\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\langle\partial_{Y}u_{1},\partial_{Y}^{2}\xi\rangle+\langle U\partial_{Y}u_{1},\xi\rangle.

On the other hand, we notice that

\displaystyle\big\|U^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{(n)}-U^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{Y}u_{1}(Z)\mathrm{d}Z\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\bigg\|\dfrac{\varphi^{(n)}-\int_{0}^{Y}u_{1}(Z)\mathrm{d}Z}{Y}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)}-u_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\longrightarrow 0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\langle U^{\prime\prime}\varphi^{n},\xi\rangle\longrightarrow\Big\langle U^{\prime\prime}\int_{0}^{Y}u_{1}(Z)\mathrm{d}Z,\xi\Big\rangle,\quad\langle f^{n},\xi\rangle\longrightarrow 0.

Thus, we have

,
\varphi\in H^{2}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
\partial_{Y}\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\varepsilon\langle w,\partial_{Y}^{2}\xi\rangle+\langle Uw,\xi\rangle-\langle U^{\prime\prime}\varphi,\xi\rangle=0.

Then
\varphi
is a weak solution to
4.10
with
\alpha=0
, and
\varphi=0
by (S-A).

Step 3. Contradiction.

•

If
\alpha\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle(1+\alpha^{2}_{n})\|\varphi^{(n)}\|_{H^{2}}\geq C^{-1}\big(\|w^{(n)}\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)},\alpha_{n}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\big)\geq C^{-1},

which along with (

) and

\displaystyle 0=(1+\alpha^{2})\|\varphi\|_{H^{2}}=\lim_{n}(1+\alpha^{2}_{n})\|\varphi^{(n)}\|_{H^{2}}\geq C^{-1}>0,

which leads to a contradiction.

•

If
\alpha=0
, by (
4.23
) and
\varphi=0
,

\displaystyle 0=\|(\partial^{2}_{Y}\varphi,\partial_{Y}\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\|(w^{(n)},\partial_{Y}\varphi^{(n)})\|_{L^{2}}\geq C^{-1}>0.

This is a contradiction.

This finishes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.9

Let

and

∎

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem

5.1.
Estimate for zero mode

In this case, the linearized system (
1.10
) can be written as

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\nu\partial_{y}^{2}u_{0,1}=f_{0,1},\quad u_{2,0}=0,\quad\partial_{y}p_{0}=f_{0,2},\\
&u_{0,1}|_{y=0}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
f_{0,1}=\partial_{y}F_{0,1}
with
F_{0,1}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
. Then there exists a unique solution
u_{0}=(u_{0,1},0)
to (
1.10
) with
\tilde{n}=0
such that

\displaystyle\|u_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\nu^{-1}\|F_{0,1}\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle\|\partial_{y}u_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\nu^{-1}\|F_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{y\to+\infty}u_{0,1}=\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}F_{0,1}dy.

Proof.

Notice that
u_{0,1}
can be represented as

\displaystyle u_{0,1}(y)=-\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{y}\int_{y^{\prime}}^{+\infty}f_{0,1}(y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}dy^{\prime}=\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{y}F_{0,1}(y^{\prime})dy^{\prime},

which along with
F_{0,1}\in L^{1}
implies that
\|u_{0,1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\nu^{-1}\|F_{0,1}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\lim_{y\to+\infty}u_{0,1}=\nu^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}F_{0,1}dy
. Moreover, since
\partial_{y}u_{0,1}(y)=\nu^{-1}F_{0,1}(y)
and
F_{0,1}\in L^{2}
, we have
\|\partial_{y}u_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\nu^{-1}\|F_{0,1}\|_{L^{2}}
.
∎

5.2.

Proposition 5.2
.

(2)

For the case

Appendix A
Some basic inequalities

Lemma A.1
.

If
w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y)
, and
\phi\in H^{1}_{0}\cap H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies

\displaystyle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi=w,\ \alpha>0,

then it holds that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\phi(Y)=e^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z-\cosh(\alpha Y)\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z,

\displaystyle\alpha\phi(Y)=-\sinh(\alpha Y)\int_{0}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{+\infty}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z+\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z.

Specially, we have

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\phi(0)=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}w(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y.

Proof.

Integration by parts gives

\displaystyle\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z=\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\big((\partial_{Z}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi(Z)\big)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle=\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\phi\big((\partial_{Z}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\big)\mathrm{d}Z+\big((\partial_{Z}\phi)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\big)\big|_{Y}^{+\infty}-\big(\phi\partial_{Z}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\big)\big|_{Y}^{+\infty}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{Y}\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}-\alpha\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}.

That is,

(A.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}+\alpha\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}

\displaystyle=-\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z.

Specially, we have

(A.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\phi(0)=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}w(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y.

Integration by parts again gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z=\int_{0}^{Y}\big((\partial_{Z}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\phi(Z)\big)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle=\big(\partial_{Z}\phi(Z)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z}\big)\big|_{0}^{Y}-\big(\phi(Z)\partial_{Z}(\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z})\big)\big|_{0}^{Y}=\partial_{Y}\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y}-\alpha\phi(Y)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y}-\partial_{Y}\phi(0).

Along with (
A.1
) and (
A.2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\phi(Y)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z+\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\partial_{Y}\phi(0)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z-\cosh(\alpha Y)\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z.

Similarly, we have

\displaystyle\alpha\phi(Y)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z-\dfrac{1}{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\partial_{Y}\phi(0)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{Y}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z-\sinh(\alpha Y)\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Z}\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\int_{0}^{+\infty}w(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z+\int_{Y}^{+\infty}w(Z)\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z.

∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\alpha\neq 0
. Suppose that
(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi=w,\ \partial_{Y}\varphi|_{Y=0}=\varphi|_{Y}=0
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{3}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+|\alpha|\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume
\alpha>0
. Clearly,
\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, and
(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}=0
.
Let
(w_{d},\varphi_{d})
be the solution to the following elliptic equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w_{d}=(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,\\
&(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi_{d}=w_{d},\ w_{d}|_{Y=0}=\varphi_{d}|_{Y}=0.\end{aligned}\right.

Using the energy method and the interpolation, it is easy to show that

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{\frac{5}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{d}\|_{L^{\infty}}+|\alpha|^{3}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi_{d},\alpha\varphi_{d})\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|w_{d}\|_{L^{2}}+|\alpha|\|\partial_{Y}w_{d}\|_{L^{2}}

(A.3)

\displaystyle\quad+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w_{d}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}.

On the other hand, by Lemma
A.1
, we know that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})^{-1}(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y})|_{Y=0}=-1/(2\alpha).

Then
w=w_{d}+2\alpha\partial_{Y}\varphi_{d}(0)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}
, which along with (
A
) implies that

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{3}\|(\partial_{Y}\varphi,\alpha\varphi)\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+|\alpha|\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}.

This gives the first inequality of the lemma.

For the second inequality, we first notice that

\displaystyle\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,-w\rangle=\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\partial_{Y}w(0)\overline{w(0)},

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq|\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,w\rangle|+\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{\infty}}\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Thanks to
\partial_{Y}w,w\in L^{2}
, we have
\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}w(Y)=\lim_{Y\rightarrow+\infty}\partial_{Y}w(Y)=0
, hence,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq 2\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}},\quad\|w\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq 2\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

Summing up, we obtain

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle|\langle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w,w\rangle|+2\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}+2\|\partial_{Y}^{2}w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\partial_{Y}w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{1}{2}},

which implies that

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}w,\alpha w)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})w\|_{L^{2}}\|w\|_{L^{2}}.

∎

Lemma A.3
.

There exists a positive constant
C>0
, such that for any
z\in\mathbb{C},\ t>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|z-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq C^{-1}|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}t.

Proof.

Let
z_{r}=\mathbf{Re}(z),\ z_{i}=\mathbf{Im}(z)
. Let us first claim that

(A.4)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq C^{-1}|z_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}}t.

Once (
A.4
) holds, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|z-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq C^{-1}\big(\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s+\int_{0}^{t}|z_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\big)\geq C^{-1}\big(|z_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}}t+|z_{i}|^{\frac{1}{2}}t\big)\geq C^{-1}|z|^{\frac{1}{2}}t.

It remains to prove (
A.4
).

Case 1
.
z_{r}\leq 0
. In this case, we have

\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s=|z_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}}t.

Case 2
.
0\leq z_{r}\leq t/2
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{t/2}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{t/2}^{t}(s-z_{r})^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq\int_{t/2}^{t}(s-t/2)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\dfrac{2(t-t/2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}=\dfrac{2(t/2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}{3}\geq\dfrac{z_{r}^{\frac{1}{2}}t}{3}=\dfrac{|z_{r}|^{\frac{1}{2}}t}{3}.

Case 3
.
z_{r}\geq t/2
. In this case, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq\int_{0}^{t/4}|z_{r}-s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s\geq\int_{0}^{t/4}(z_{r}-t/4)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(z_{r}-t/4)^{\frac{1}{2}}t}{4}\geq\dfrac{|z_{r}/2|^{\frac{1}{2}}t}{4},

here we used
z_{r}-t/4\geq z_{r}/2=|z_{r}|/2
. Combining three cases, we conclude our result.
∎

\displaystyle A_{0}(z)=\int_{\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/6}z}^{+\infty}Ai(t)\mathrm{d}t=\mathrm{e}^{{i}\pi/6}\int_{z}^{+\infty}Ai(\mathrm{e}^{{\mathrm{i}}\pi/6}t)\mathrm{d}t.

The following lemma comes from
[
5
]
.

Lemma B.1
.

There exists
c>0
and
\delta_{0}>0
so that for
\textbf{Im}(z)\leq\delta_{0}
,

(B.1)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\right|\lesssim 1+|z|^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad{\rm Re}\frac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\leq\min(-1/3,-c(1+|z|^{\frac{1}{2}})).

Moreover, for
{{\bf Im}z}\leq\delta_{0}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{A_{0}^{\prime\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\Big|\leq C(1+|z|).

We denote

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(Y)=Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa(Y+\eta))/Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta),

where
\kappa>0
and
\mathbf{Im}\eta<0
. We define
\tilde{\Phi}(Y)
as the solution of

\displaystyle(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\tilde{\Phi}=\tilde{A},\quad\tilde{\Phi}(0)=0.

Under the assumption
\tilde{A}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{e}^{\alpha Y})
, by Lemma
A.1
, we know that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z+\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z.

We define the fast decay part of
\tilde{\Phi}(Y)
as

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)=\dfrac{1}{\alpha}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z.

Then
(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\tilde{\Phi}_{f}=\tilde{A}
.

Lemma B.2
.

Let
\kappa>0
and
\mathbf{Im}\eta<0
. Then there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle|\tilde{A}(Y)|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)},

\displaystyle\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\beta}{2}},\quad\beta\geq 0,

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi},\alpha\tilde{\Phi})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Moreover, if
c\kappa\geq 2\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)|\leq C\kappa^{-2}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)/2},

\displaystyle|\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)|\leq C\kappa^{-1}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)/2},

\displaystyle\big\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{4}},\quad\beta\geq 0,

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f},\alpha\tilde{\Phi}_{f})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{3}{4}},

and

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}(Y)|\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y},

\displaystyle\big\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{\Phi}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\alpha^{-\frac{2\beta+1}{2}},\quad\beta\geq 0.

Proof.

By Lemma
B.1
and Lemma
A.3
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{A_{0}(t+B)}{A_{0}(B)}\right|=\left|\exp\big(\ln(A_{0}\big(t+B\big))-\ln(A_{0}(B))\big)\right|=\left|\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}\big(s+B\big)}{A_{0}\big(s+B\big)}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\exp\bigg(\int_{0}^{t}\mathbf{Re}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}\big(s+B\big)}{A_{0}\big(s+B\big)}\mathrm{d}s\bigg)\leq\exp\bigg(-\int_{0}^{t}\max\big(1/3,c(1+|s+B|^{\frac{1}{2}})\big)\mathrm{d}s\bigg),

which along with Lemma
A.3
implies

(B.2)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{A_{0}(t+B)}{A_{0}(B)}\right|\leq\exp\left(-\max\big(t/3,c(1+|B|^{\frac{1}{2}})t\big)\right).

Thanks to
\mathbf{Re}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\leq\min(-1/3,-c(1+|z|^{\frac{1}{2}})\big)<0
, we deduce that
\left|\mathbf{Re}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\right|\geq c(1+|z|^{\frac{1}{2}})
and

(B.3)

\displaystyle\left|\dfrac{A_{0}(z)}{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}\right|=\left|\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\right|^{-1}\leq\left|\mathbf{Re}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}(z)}{A_{0}(z)}\right|^{-1}\leq c^{-1}(1+|z|^{\frac{1}{2}})^{-1}.

Now we are ready to show the estimates of
\tilde{A}(Y)
.
Lemma
B.1
gives

\displaystyle|\tilde{A}(Y)|

\displaystyle=\left|\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)}\right|=\left|\dfrac{A_{0}(\kappa\eta)}{A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta}\right|\left|\dfrac{A_{0}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{A_{0}(\kappa\eta)}\right|\left|\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{A_{0}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|+\kappa Y\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}

\displaystyle\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}.

Then it is easy to find that for
\beta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big\|\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2}-\beta}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{\beta}{2}}.

Now we turn to deal with
\tilde{\Phi}(Y)
. By Hardy’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi},\alpha\tilde{\Phi})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\big|\langle\tilde{A},\tilde{\Phi}\rangle\big|\leq\|Y\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2}}\left\|\tilde{\Phi}/Y\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|Y\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{Y}\Phi\|_{L^{2}},

which gives

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi},\alpha\tilde{\Phi})\|_{L^{2}}\leq 2\|Y\tilde{A}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Thanks to the definition of
\tilde{\Phi}_{f}
and
|\tilde{A}(Y)|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Y}
, we have

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)|

\displaystyle=\alpha^{-1}\left|\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-1}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Z}\sinh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle=C\kappa^{-1}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-1}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Z}\cosh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{-1}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Z}\exp\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{-1}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)-\alpha\big)^{-1}\exp\big(-\big(c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)-\alpha\big)Y\big)

Therefore, for
c\kappa\geq 2\alpha>0
, we have
c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)-\alpha\geq c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}})/2
and

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}})Y/2}.

which implies that for
\beta\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\big\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{4}}.

Notice that

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\int_{Y}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\cosh\big(\alpha(Z-Y)\big)\mathrm{d}Z.

Similarly, we can obtain

\displaystyle|\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y)|\leq C\kappa^{-1}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|\big)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa Y(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}})/2},

\displaystyle\big\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{2\beta+5}{4}},

\displaystyle\|(\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f},\alpha\tilde{\Phi}_{f})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-\frac{3}{2}}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{3}{4}}.

Thanks to
|\tilde{A}(Y)|\leq C\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Y}
, we get

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Y}\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle=C\kappa^{-1}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Y}\cosh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{-1}\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)Y}2\exp(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z

\displaystyle\leq C\kappa^{-1}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\big(c\kappa\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)-\alpha\big)^{-1}.

Then for
c\kappa\geq 2\alpha>0
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\left|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z\right|\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}.

Recall that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}(Y)

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}}{\alpha}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Z)\sinh(\alpha Z)\mathrm{d}Z+\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(Y).

Then we obtain

\displaystyle|\tilde{\Phi}(Y)|\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}+\mathrm{e}^{-c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{\frac{1}{2}}Y/2}\big)\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y},

which yields that

\displaystyle\big\|Y^{\beta}\tilde{\Phi}\big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\kappa^{-2}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-1}\alpha^{-\frac{2\beta+1}{2}}.

∎

Lemma B.3
.

Let
c
be the constant in Lemma
B.2
,
c\kappa\geq 2\alpha>0
and
\mathbf{Im}\eta<0
. Then it holds that

\displaystyle|\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}(0)|\geq C^{-1}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\kappa+3\alpha)^{-1},

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)=-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}}{3\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\eta}{Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{1/2}-\alpha)^{3}}\Big),

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)=\mathrm{i}\kappa^{-3}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-1}\eta)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{1/2}-\alpha)^{4}}\Big).

Proof.

By Lemma
A.1
, we have

\displaystyle-\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}(0)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{Ai\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{Ai\big(\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta\big)}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{A_{0}^{\prime}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)}\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=-\dfrac{A_{0}(\kappa\eta)}{\kappa A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)}-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\dfrac{A_{0}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)}{\kappa A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)}\partial_{Y}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\big)\mathrm{d}Y,

which along with Lemma
B.2
gives

\displaystyle\kappa|A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)||\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}(0)|

\displaystyle\geq\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\big|A_{0}\big(\kappa(Y+\eta)\big)\big|\big|\partial_{Y}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\big)\big|\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle\geq\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa Y/3}\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|\big|\partial_{Y}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\big)\big|\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|+\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa Y/3}\partial_{Y}\big(\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha Y}\big)\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\kappa}{3}\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-\kappa Y/3-\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|\dfrac{\kappa}{\kappa+3\alpha}.

This along with Lemma
B.1
shows that

\displaystyle|\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}(0)|\geq\dfrac{\big|A_{0}(\kappa\eta)\big|}{(\kappa+3\alpha)|A_{0}^{\prime}(\kappa\eta)|}\geq C^{-1}(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\kappa+3\alpha)^{-1}.

Now we estimate
\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)
. We first have

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\cosh(\alpha Y)\mathrm{d}Y=I+II,

where

\displaystyle I=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\mathrm{d}Y,\quad II=-\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)(\cosh(\alpha Y)-1)\mathrm{d}Y.

For
I
, we notice that by the definition of
\tilde{A}(Y)
,

\displaystyle I=

\displaystyle-Ai^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)\int_{0}^{+\infty}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa(Y+\eta)\mathrm{d}Y=-\kappa^{-1}Ai^{-1}(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)\int_{\kappa\eta}^{+\infty}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}Z\mathrm{)}dZ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}Z\mathrm{)}dZ+\frac{1}{\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\int_{0}^{\kappa\eta}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}Z\mathrm{)}dZ

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}}{\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\int_{0}^{+\infty}Ai(Y)\mathrm{d}Y+\frac{1}{\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\int_{0}^{\kappa\eta}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}Z\mathrm{)}dZ.

On the other hand, we know that
\int_{0}^{+\infty}Ai(Y)\mathrm{d}Y=1/3
and observe that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{0}^{\kappa\eta}Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}Z\mathrm{)}dZ\Big|\lesssim\kappa\eta.

Then we obtain

(B.4)

\displaystyle I=-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}}{3\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\eta}{Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\Big).

For
II
, we notice that by Lemma
B.2
,

\displaystyle|II|\leq C\int_{0}^{+\infty}\alpha^{2}Y^{2}e^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}e^{\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y\leq C\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}})-\alpha)^{3}},

which along with (
B.4
) implies

\displaystyle\partial_{Y}\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)=-\frac{e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}}{3\kappa Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\eta}{Ai(e^{\mathrm{i}\frac{\pi}{6}}\kappa\eta)}\Big)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{1/2}-\alpha)^{3}}\Big).

Now we turn to the estimate of
\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)
. We notice that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\sinh(\alpha Y)\mathrm{d}Y=I_{0}+II_{0},

where

\displaystyle I_{0}=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\alpha Y\mathrm{d}Y,\quad II_{0}=\alpha^{-1}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)(\sinh(\alpha Y)-\alpha Y)\mathrm{d}Y.

Thanks to the definition of
\tilde{A}(Y)
, we have

\displaystyle Y\tilde{A}(Y)=-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\kappa^{3}}\partial_{Y}^{2}\tilde{A}(Y)-\eta\tilde{A}(Y).

Then we get

\displaystyle I_{0}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\kappa^{3}}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\partial_{Y}^{2}\tilde{A}(Y)\mathrm{d}Y-\eta\int_{0}^{+\infty}\tilde{A}(Y)\mathrm{d}Y

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\kappa^{-3}\partial_{Y}\tilde{A}(0)+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-1}\eta)=\mathrm{i}\kappa^{-3}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-1}\eta),

and

\displaystyle|II_{0}|\leq C\alpha^{2}\int_{0}^{+\infty}Y^{3}e^{-c\kappa Y\big(1+|\kappa\eta|^{\frac{1}{2}}\big)}e^{\alpha Y}\mathrm{d}Y\leq C\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{1/2}-\alpha)^{4}}.

Thus, we conclude that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{f}(0)=\mathrm{i}\kappa^{-3}+\mathcal{O}(\kappa^{-1}\eta)+\mathcal{O}\Big(\frac{\alpha^{2}}{(c\kappa(1+|\kappa\eta|)^{1/2}-\alpha)^{4}}\Big).

∎

Appendix C
The Homogeneous Rayleigh equation

Here we recall a result about the homogeneous Rayleigh equation from
[
9
]
.

Proposition C.1
.

For any
0<\alpha<1
, there exists a function
\varphi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
such that

\displaystyle U(\partial_{Y}^{2}-\alpha^{2})\varphi-U^{\prime\prime}\varphi=0,\quad Y>0,

and there holds the following properties:
\varphi=\varphi_{0}+\varphi_{2}+\varphi_{3}
with

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}=Ue^{-\alpha Y},\quad\varphi_{1}|_{Y=0}=\frac{\alpha}{U^{\prime}(0)}+\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2}),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\alpha,

\displaystyle\|\partial_{Y}\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}+\alpha\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\alpha^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Here
C
is independent of
\alpha
. If
\frac{U^{\prime\prime}}{U}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
in addition, then
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
beong to
H^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

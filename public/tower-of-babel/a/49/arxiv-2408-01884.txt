Optimal Power-Weighted Birman–Hardy–Rellich-type Inequalities on Finite Intervals and Annuli Thanks: To appear in Bulletin London Math. Soc.
Fritz Gesztesy, Michael M. H. Pang

Abstract
. We derive an optimal power-weighted Hardy-type inequality in integral form on finite intervals and subsequently prove the analogous inequality in differential form. We note that the optimal constant of the latter inequality differs from the former. Moreover, by iterating these inequalities we derive the sequence of power-weighted Birman–Hardy–Rellich-type inequalities in integral form on finite intervals and then also prove the analogous sequence of inequalities in differential form. We use the one-dimensional Hardy-type result in differential form to derive an optimal multi-dimensional version of the power-weighted Hardy inequality in differential form on annuli (i.e., spherical shell domains), and once more employ an iteration procedure to derive the Birman–Hardy–Rellich-type sequence of power-weighted higher-order Hardy-type inequalities for annuli. In the limit as the annulus approaches ℝ n \ { 0 } {\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\} , we recover well-known prior results on Rellich-type inequalities on ℝ n \ { 0 } {\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\} .

2.
Optimal Power-Weighted Hardy Inequalities on Finite Intervals in Integral Form

In this section we prove an optimal power-weighted Hardy inequality on a finite interval in integral form and subsequently derive its iterated versions, that is, finite interval higher-order power-weighted Hardy inequalities in integral form.

We start with some preliminary results.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
b\in(1,\infty)
,
L\in\big(4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2},\infty\big)
, and

C=2^{-1}\big[4L-(1-\alpha)^{2}\big]^{1/2}
. Then the following items
(i)
–
(iii)
hold:

(i)
The differential equation

-\big(x^{2-\alpha}\big(x^{\alpha}y\big)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}(x)=Ly(x),\quad x\in[1,b],

(2.1)

has the linearly independent solutions

y_{1}(x)=x^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\cos(C\,\text{\rm ln}(x)),\quad y_{2}(x)=x^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\sin(C\,\text{\rm ln}(x)),\quad x\in(1,b].

(2.2)

(ii)
Assume in addition that

\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{1\}

(2.3)

and that
C_{0}
is the unique solution of the equation

[2/(1-\alpha)]C+\tan(C\,\text{\rm ln}(b))=0\,\text{ in }\,(0,\pi/\text{\rm ln}(b))\backslash\{\pi/[2\,\text{\rm ln}(b)]\}.

(2.4)

Then

y_{0}(x)=x^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\{[2/(1-\alpha)]C_{0}\cos(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))+\sin(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))\},\quad x\in[1,b],

(2.5)

is a solution of the regular boundary value problem

\displaystyle\begin{split}&-\big(x^{2-\alpha}\big(x^{\alpha}y\big)^{\prime}\big)^{\prime}(x)=L_{0}y(x),\quad L_{0}=4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}+C_{0}^{2},\;x\in[1,b],\\
&\,y^{\prime}(1)+\alpha y(1)=0,\quad y(b)=0.\end{split}

(2.6)

In addition,

y_{0}(x)\neq 0,\quad x\in[1,b).

(2.7)

(iii)
Assuming once more that in addition (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) hold, then

\int_{x}^{b}ds\,s^{\alpha-2}\int_{1}^{s}dt\,y_{0}(t)=L_{0}^{-1}x^{\alpha}y_{0}(x),\quad x\in[1,b].

(2.8)

Proof.

Since items
(i)
,
(iii)
and most of item
(ii)
are based on direct computations, it suffices to focus on the non-vanishing property (
2.7
). We start by noting that
0\leqslant C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x)<\pi
for
x\in[1,b)
and that
y_{0}(1)=C_{0}\,2/(1-\alpha)\neq 0
. Moreover, if for some
x_{0}\in(1,b)
one has
\cos(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x_{0}))=0
, then
C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x_{0})=\pi/2
and hence

y_{0}(x_{0})=x_{0}^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\neq 0
. Thus it remains to consider
x\in(1,b)
with
\cos(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))\neq 0
. Suppose first that
\tan(C_{0}\text{\rm ln}(b))>0
(i.e.,
\alpha\in(1,\infty)
). Then

\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))=\tan(C_{0}[\text{\rm ln}(x)/\text{\rm ln}(b)]\text{\rm ln}(b))<\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(b))=[2/(\alpha-1)]C_{0},

(2.9)

and hence,

y_{0}(x)=x^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\cos(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))\{[2/(1-\alpha)]C_{0}+\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))\}\neq 0,\quad x\in[1,b).

(2.10)

Next, suppose that
\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(b))<0
(i.e.,
\alpha\in(-\infty,1)
). If
\pi/2<C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x)<\pi
, then once again (
2.9
) and (
2.10
) hold. On the other hand, if
0<C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x)<\pi/2
, then

\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))>0>\tan(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(b))=[2/(\alpha-1)]C_{0},

(2.11)

and hence once more (
2.10
) holds.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{1\}
,
b\in(1,\infty)
,
L_{0}=4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}+C_{0}^{2}
, where
C_{0}
is the unique solution of (
2.4
).
Then

\int_{1}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|f(x)|^{2}\geqslant L_{0}\int_{1}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|\bigg)^{2},\quad f\in L^{2}((1,b);dx).

(2.12)

Moreover, equality holds in (
2.12
) for

f_{\alpha,b}(x)=x^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\{[2/(1-\alpha)]C_{0}\,\cos(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))+\sin(C_{0}\,\text{\rm ln}(x))\},\quad x\in[1,b],

(2.13)

observing the fact that

,
and hence the constant

in (

) is optimal.

Proof.

Let

be real-valued and suppose that

\bigg(\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|\bigg)^{2}\leqslant\int_{1}^{x}ds\,|g(s)|^{2}\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|^{2}\big/|g(t)|^{2},\quad x\in(1,b).

(2.14)

Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|\bigg)^{2}\leqslant\int_{1}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\int_{1}^{x}ds\,g(s)^{2}\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|^{2}\big/g(t)^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{1}^{b}dt\,|f(t)|^{2}g(t)^{-2}\int_{t}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\int_{1}^{x}ds\,g(s)^{2},

(2.15)

upon interchanging the order of integration. Introducing

G_{\alpha}(g;t)=g(t)^{-2}\int_{t}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\int_{1}^{x}ds\,g(s)^{2},\quad t\in(1,b),

(2.16)

(
2.15
) implies

\int_{1}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{1}^{x}dt\,|f(t)|\bigg)^{2}\leqslant\int_{1}^{b}dt\,G_{\alpha}(g;t)|f(t)|^{2},\quad f\in L^{2}((1,b);dx).

(2.17)

With

as in (
2.5
), we may choose

g(t)=|y_{0}(t)|^{1/2},\quad t\in[1,b],

(2.18)

and then (

) yields

G_{\alpha}(g;t)=|y_{0}(t)|^{-1}\int_{t}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\int_{1}^{x}ds\,|y_{0}(s)|=L_{0}^{-1}t^{\alpha},\quad t\in(1,b).

(2.19)

Consequently, (

) and (

) yield

proving (

).

Finally, choosing

(cf. (

) and (

Appropriate scaling then yields the following result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{1\}
. In addition, suppose that
\beta_{\alpha}\in{\mathbb{R}}
is the unique solution of

(2.21)

Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|h(x)|^{2}\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha}^{2}\big\}\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}\bigg(\int_{a}^{x}dt\,|h(t)|\bigg)^{2},&\\
h\in L^{2}((a,b);dx).&\end{split}

(2.22)

Moreover, equality holds in (
2.22
) for

\displaystyle h_{\alpha,a,b}(x)=(x/a)^{-(\alpha+1)/2}\{[2/(1-\alpha)]\beta_{\alpha}\,\cos(\beta_{\alpha}\,\text{\rm ln}(x/a))+\sin(\beta_{\alpha}\,\text{\rm ln}(x/a))\},

\displaystyle\hskip 270.30118ptx\in[a,b],

(2.23)

observing the fact that
h_{\alpha,a,b}\in L^{2}((a,b);dx)
,
and hence the constant
4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha}^{2}
in (

) is optimal.

Proof.

This follows from the following scaling argument: Let
h\in L^{2}((a,b);dx)
, and
\widehat{h}\in L^{2}((1,b/a);dx)

\widehat{h}(t)=h(at)

\displaystyle h_{0}(x)=|h(x)|,\quad h_{j}(x)=\int_{a}^{x}dt\,h_{j-1}(t),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}},\;x\in(a,b).

(2.24)

By Cauchy’s inequality and induction one concludes that

h_{j}\in L^{\infty}((a,b);dx)\subset L^{2}((a,b);dx),\quad j\in{\mathbb{N}}.

(2.25)

In addition, for
j\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{2j-1\}
, we denote by
\beta_{\alpha,j}\in{\mathbb{R}}
the unique solution of

[2/(2j-1-\alpha)]\beta+\tan(\beta\text{\rm ln}(b/a))=0\,\text{ in }\,(0,\pi/\text{\rm ln}(b/a))\backslash\{\pi/[2\text{\rm ln}(b/a)]\}.

(2.26)

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
,
h\in L^{2}((a,b);dx)
,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{2k-1\}_{1\leqslant k\leqslant m}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|h(x)|^{2}\geqslant\prod_{j=1}^{m}\big\{4^{-1}(2j-1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha,j}^{2}\big\}\\
&\quad\times\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2m}\Bigg(\int_{a}^{x}dt_{m}\int_{a}^{t_{m}}dt_{m-1}\cdots\int_{a}^{t_{2}}dt_{1}\,|h(t_{1})|\Bigg)^{2}.\end{split}

(2.27)

Proof.

Inequality (
2.27
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|h(x)|^{2}\geqslant\prod_{j=1}^{m}\big\{4^{-1}(2j-1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha,j}^{2}\big\}\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2m}h_{m}(x)^{2},\quad m\in{\mathbb{N}},

(2.28)

which we will now prove by induction on
m
. Clearly, (
2.28
) holds for
m=1
by Theorem
2.3
. Next, suppose (
2.28
) holds for some
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
and that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}\backslash\{2k-1\}_{1\leqslant k\leqslant m_{0}+1}
.
Applying Theorem
2.3
to the integral on the right-hand side of (
2.28
) and exploiting (
2.24
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|h(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}}\big\{4^{-1}(2j-1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha,j}^{2}\big\}\big\{4^{-1}(2m_{0}+1-\alpha)^{2}+\beta_{\alpha,m_{0}+1}^{2}\big\}

\displaystyle\quad\times\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2m_{0}-2}h_{m_{0}+1}(x)^{2}

3.
Optimal Power-Weighted Hardy Inequalities on Finite Intervals in Differential Form

In this section we prove an optimal power-weighted Hardy inequality on a finite interval in differential form and then discuss its iterations, that is, finite interval higher-order power-weighted Hardy inequalities on a finite interval.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
0<a<b<\infty
and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha}|f^{\prime}(x)|^{2}\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(1-\alpha)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(b/a)]^{2}\big\}\int_{a}^{b}dx\,x^{\alpha-2}|f(x)|^{2},&\\
f\in W_{0}^{1,2}((a,b)).&\end{split}

(3.1)

Moreover, equality holds in (

(3.2)

observing the fact that

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle(T_{\alpha,D}f)(x)=(\tau_{\alpha}f)(x),\quad x\in[a,b],

\displaystyle f\in\dom(T_{\alpha,D})=\big\{g\in L^{2}\big((a,b);x^{\alpha-2}dx\big)\,\big|\,g,g^{[1]}\in AC([a,b]);

\displaystyle\hskip 89.626ptg(a)=0=g(b);\,\tau_{\alpha}g\in L^{2}\big((a,b);x^{\alpha-2}dx\big)\big\}.

Here

represents the absolutely continuous functions on

\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}((a,b)).&\end{split}

4.

3.1
, and Theorem
3.3
, one obtains

\displaystyle\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-1}\big|-r^{1-n}\big(r^{n-1}f^{\prime}(r)\big)^{\prime}+\lambda r^{-2}f(r)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-1}|f^{\prime\prime}(r)|^{2}+[2\lambda+(n-1)(1-\alpha)]\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-3}|f^{\prime}(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\lambda[\lambda+(\alpha+n-4)(2-\alpha)]\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|f(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big[4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}]\big]\big[4^{-1}(\alpha+n-4)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|f(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+[2\lambda+(n-1)(1-\alpha)]\big[4^{-1}(\alpha+n-4)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big]

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\times\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|f(r)|^{2}+\lambda[\lambda+(\alpha+n-4)(2-\alpha)]\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|f(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\bigg\{\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha}+\lambda\big)^{2}+\Big(2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big]+2\lambda\Big)[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\;\;\,+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\bigg\}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|f(r)|^{2}.

(4.14)

∎

The power-weighted Rellich inequality on an annulus then reads as follows:

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2}))
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\Big\{\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}+2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big][\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4}|f(x)|^{2},

(4.15)

where

\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}=\min\Big\{\big[\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha}+\lambda_{j}\big]^{2}\,\Big|\,j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\Big\},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(4.16)

Proof.

It suffices to prove (
4.15
) for real-valued
f
only. By Lemmas
4.2

(ii)
and
4.4
one obtains

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}r^{n-1}dr\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}r^{\alpha}\big|-r^{1-n}\big(r^{n-1}F_{f,j,\ell}^{\,\prime}(r)\big)^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}^{2}r^{-2}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}|\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-1}\big|-r^{1-n}\big(r^{n-1}F_{f,j,\ell}^{\,\prime}(r)\big)^{\prime}+\lambda_{j}^{2}r^{-2}F_{f,j,\ell}(r)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\bigg\{\big(\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha}+\lambda_{j}\big)^{2}+\Big(2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big]+2\lambda_{j}\Big)[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 72.55461pt+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\bigg\}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\Big\{\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}+2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big][\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big\{\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}+2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big][\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}}\sum_{\ell=1}^{m(\lambda_{j})}\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}dr\int_{{\mathbb{S}}^{n-1}}d^{n-1}\omega(\theta)\,r^{\alpha+n-5}|F_{f,j,\ell}(r)|^{2}|\varphi_{j,\ell}(\theta)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\Big\{\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}+2^{-1}\big[(\alpha-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big][\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4}\Big\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4}|f(x)|^{2}.

(4.17)

∎

Remark 4.6
.

We note that as
r_{1}\downarrow 0
and
r_{2}\uparrow\infty
in inequality (
4.15
), one recovers the optimal Rellich inequality of Caldirola and Musina
[
4
, Theorem 3.1, eqs. (1.6), (1.7)]
and
[

]
. Also, keeping
r_{2}>0
fixed and letting

in (

\diamond

An appropriate iteration procedure then yields the following higher-order power-weighted Rellich inequalities.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geqslant 2
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big((-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\geqslant\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-4(j-1))\Bigg)\\
&\quad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4m}|f(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})),\end{split}

(4.18)

where

D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\gamma)=\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\gamma}+2^{-1}\big[(\gamma-2)^{2}+(n-2)^{2}\big][\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{4},\quad\gamma\in{\mathbb{R}}.

(4.19)

Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big((-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\geqslant\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha-4(j-1)}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4m}|f(x)|^{2},&\\
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).&\end{split}

(4.20)

Proof.

We will employ induction on
m
. Clearly, Theorem
4.5
implies that (
4.18
) holds for
m=1
. Assuming (
4.18
) holds for some
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, one obtains upon applying (
4.18
) to
-\Delta f
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big((-\Delta)^{m_{0}+1}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\geqslant\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-4(j-1))\Bigg)\\
&\quad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4m_{0}}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})).\end{split}

(4.21)

Employing Theorem
4.5
to the integral on the right-hand side of (
4.21
) results in

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big((-\Delta)^{m_{0}+1}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\geqslant\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-4(j-1))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\times D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-4m_{0})\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4m_{0}-4}|f(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad=\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}+1}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-4(j-1))\Bigg)\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4(m_{0}+1)}|f(x)|^{2},

(4.22)

\displaystyle\hskip 219.08612ptf\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})),

and hence in (
4.18
) for
m_{0}+1
.

Inequality (
4.20
) follows upon observing that

\lim_{r_{1}\downarrow 0,\,r_{2}\uparrow\infty}D(\alpha-4(j-1))=\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha-4(j-1)},\quad\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}},\;j=1,\dots,m.

(4.23)

∎

Similarly, an iteration procedure also yields the following sequence of power-weighted Hardy–Rellich-type inequalities.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geqslant 2
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2-4(j-1))\Bigg)

(4.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4m}|f(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})).

Moreover,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\\
&\quad\geqslant 4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha-2-4(j-1)}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4m}|f(x)|^{2},\\
&\hskip 221.93158ptf\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).\end{split}

(4.25)

Proof.

Once more we will employ induction on
m
. Applying Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
one obtains for
m=1
,

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}|(\nabla(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-6}|f(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})),

(4.26)

proving (
4.24
) for
m=1
. Next, suppose that (
4.24
) holds for some
m_{0}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then, applying (
4.24
) to
-\Delta f
yields

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{m_{0}+1}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2-4(j-1))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4m_{0}}|(-\Delta f)(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})).

(4.27)

An application of Theorem
4.5
to the last integral in (

\displaystyle\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{m_{0}+1}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\geqslant\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2-4(j-1))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\times D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2-4m_{0})\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4m_{0}-4}|f(x)|^{2},

\displaystyle\quad=\big\{4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}+[\pi/\text{\rm ln}(r_{2}/r_{1})]^{2}\big\}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m_{0}+1}D_{n,r_{1},r_{2}}(\alpha-2-4(j-1))\Bigg)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4(m_{0}+1)}|f(x)|^{2},\quad f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(A_{n}(r_{1},r_{2})),

and hence (
4.24
) for

Inequality (

Corollary 4.9
.

Let
m,n\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
n\geqslant 2
, and
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the following items
(i)
and
(ii)
hold.

(i)
If
\alpha\in[4m-n,n]
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big((-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\geqslant\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha-4(j-1)}^{2}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-4m}|f(x)|^{2},&\\
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).&\end{split}

(4.30)

(ii)
If
\alpha\in[2+4m-n,n+2]
, then

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha}\big|\big(\nabla(-\Delta)^{m}f\big)(x)\big|^{2}\\
&\quad\geqslant 4^{-1}(\alpha+n-2)^{2}\Bigg(\prod_{j=1}^{m}\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha-2-4(j-1)}^{2}\Bigg)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}d^{n}x\,|x|^{\alpha-2-4m}|f(x)|^{2},\\
&\hskip 221.93158ptf\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}\backslash\{0\}).\end{split}

(4.31)

Proof.

We start by noting that according to (
4.13
),
\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha}\in[0,\infty)
if and only if

\alpha\in[4-n,n]
. Thus, by (
4.16
),

\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha}^{2},\quad\alpha\in[4-n,n].

(4.32)

(i)
By (
4.20
), in order to prove (
4.30
) it suffices to show that

\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha-4(j-1)}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha-4(j-1)}^{2},\quad\alpha\in[4m-n,n],\;j=1,\dots,m.

(4.33)

By (
4.32
), one obtains (
4.33
) if

\alpha-4(j-1)\in[4-n,n],\quad j=1,\dots,m.

(4.34)

But (
4.34
) follows from the assumption
\alpha\in[4m-n,n]

(ii)

4.25
), in order to prove (
4.31

\widetilde{\beta}_{n,\alpha-2-4(j-1)}=\widetilde{\gamma}_{n,\alpha-2-4(j-1)}^{2},\quad\alpha\in[2+4m-n,n+2],\;j=1,\dots,m.

4.32

4.35

\alpha-2-4(j-1)\in[4-n,n],\quad j=1,\dots,m.

(4.36)

However, (
4.36

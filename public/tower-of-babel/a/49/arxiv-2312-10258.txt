Brauer pairs for splendid Rickard complexes
Jadyn V. Breland, Sam K. Miller

Abstract
. We define the notion of a Brauer pair of a chain complex, extending the notion of a Brauer pair of a p p -permutation module introduced by Boltje and Perepelitsky. In fact, the Brauer pairs of a splendid Rickard complex C C coincide with the set of Brauer pairs of the corresponding p p -permutation equivalence Λ ⁡ ( C ) \Lambda(C) induced by C C . As a result, we derive structural results for splendid Rickard complexes which are analogous to known structural properties for p p -permutation equivalences. In particular, we show splendid Rickard equivalences induce local splendid Rickard equivalences between normalizer block algebras as well as centralizer block algebras.

1.
Introduction

Broué’s abelian defect group conjecture is one of the hallmark conjectures of modular representation theory. It predicts an “equivalence” between a block with abelian defect group, and a corresponding block associated to the normalizer of the defect group. Moreover, such equivalences are predicted to induce “local equivalences” as well, in that for these block algebras, there are corresponding blocks associated to centralizer subgroups which are also equivalent in the same manner.

Historically, these equivalences were first predicted by Broué to exist as
isotypies
, a bouquet of character-theoretic equivalences called “perfect isometries” for pairs of local subgroups. However, it was suspected that isotypies were the “shadow” of a categorical equivalence from which all of the components of the isotypy are induced. Rickard refined this conjecture in
[
16
]
by introducing “splendid” derived equivalences, objects which are now known as splendid Rickard complexes. Splendid Rickard complexes are chain complexes of
p
-permutation bimodules which have “diagonal” vertices, and induce equivalences between the derived categories corresponding to the block algebras. The key insight of Rickard was that
p
-permutation was the correct property to require for the existence of equivalences between local subgroups. In this case, the Brauer construction, a common construction when working with
p
-permutation modules, induces the local equivalences. Such complexes were later extended to the language of (almost) source algebras by Linckelmann in
[
11
]
.

In
[
5
]
, Boltje and Xu introduced
p
-permutation equivalences, a type of equivalence for blocks which exists on the representation ring level. Such equivalences live between isotypies and splendid Rickard complexes, in the sense that in order to induce an isotypy from a splendid Rickard equivalence, one first constructs a corresponding
p
-permutation equivalence by taking the Lefschetz invariant of the chain complex, i.e. taking an alternating sum of terms. Much of the theory of splendid Rickard complexes and
p
-permutation equivalences should be expected to coincide - for instance, to induce local equivalences between centralizer block algebras for both types of equivalences, one applies the Brauer construction in the same manner. However, a splendid Rickard complex has additional structure that a
p
-permutation equivalence does not - namely, the differentials of the chain complex, and a grading of components.

The preliminary notion of
p
-permutation equivalence in
[
5
]
made restrictive assumptions, namely that the blocks have a common defect group and that certain fusion systems are equivalent. The main results were obtained by assuming that the defect group was abelian. Boltje and Perepelistky in
[
4
]
gave a wider definition for
p
-permutation equivalences, allowing for direct sums of blocks, removing the assumption of a common defect group, and allowing the vertices of any indecomposable summand to be “twisted diagonal.” Under these looser assumptions, it is initially unclear how one would obtain local equivalences, or if the stricter structural properties of a
p
-permutation equivalence would hold in the case of block equivalences. The machinery of Brauer pairs was necessary to resolve these questions, and in fact, many of the structural invariants, such as blocks having isomorphic fusion systems, continue to hold. By applying the Brauer construction to a
p
-permutation equivalence at certain Brauer pairs, one obtains a local
p
-permutation equivalence, leading to the notion of a Brauer pair of a
p
-permutation equivalence. If
A
and
B
are direct summands of group algebras
kG
and
kH
and
\gamma
is a
p
-permutation equivalence for
A
and
B
, we say the pair
(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})
, where
\Delta(P,\phi,Q)\leq G\times H
is a twisted diagonal subgroup,
e
a block of
kC_{G}(P)
, and
f
a block of
kC_{H}(Q)
, is a
\gamma
-Brauer pair
if
e\gamma(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))f\neq 0
.

Boltje and Perepelitisky developed this theory further, deducing the structure of the poset of Brauer pairs for
p
-permutation equivalences. Moreover, they showed that the local equivalences induced extend beyond blocks of centralizer group algebras - they may be extended to stronger equivalences for normalizer blocks as well. A key observation was that applying the Brauer construction to a
p
-permutation equivalence does not immediately lead to a centralizer
p
-permutation equivalence, but rather a module which is “invertible” under an extended tensor product. This suggests that there is more structure which is missed by only considering local centralizer
p
-permutation equivalences.

The goal of this paper is adapting much of the theory in
[
4
]
to splendid Rickard complexes. We adopt a similarly flexible definition for splendid Rickard complexes, allowing for twisted diagonal vertices and for the equivalence to be between sums of blocks. We then introduce the notion of a Brauer pair for a splendid Rickard complex. Given a splendid Rickard complex
C
for direct summands
A
and
B
of group algebras
kG
and
kH
, respectively, we say

is a

eC(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))f
is a noncontractible chain complex. Our main result is the following, which is Corollary

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces some preliminary definitions involving direct products of groups. Section 3 reviews some prerequisites on
p
-permutation modules and the Brauer construction. Section 4 is a review of block theory, Brauer pairs, and the fusion system of a block. Section 5 establishes the definitions of
p
-permutation equivalences and splendid Rickard complexes we will use for the paper, and proves a few basic results for splendid Rickard complexes which hold for more restrictive definitions. Section 6 reviews preliminaries on extended tensor products with a focus on extended tensor products of
p
-permutation bimodules. Section 7 recalls the definition of a Brauer pair for a
p
-permutation module and a
p
-permutation equivalence as defined by Perepelitsky and Boltje. Section 8 states and proves the main results of the paper regarding the notion of a Brauer pair for a splendid Rickard complex. Finally, Section 9 proves as corollaries a few structural results about the modules which appear in splendid Rickard complexes, which are analogous to results for
p
-permutation equivalences.

2.
Subgroups of direct products of groups

We begin by recalling some constructions related to subgroups of direct products of groups. We refer the reader to
[
6

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G,H,K
be finite groups, let
X\leq G\times H
and
Y\leq H\times K
.

(a)

Denote by
p_{1}:G\times H\to G
and
p_{2}:G\times H\to H
the canonical projections. Setting

k_{1}(X):=\{g\mid(g,1)\in X\}\text{ and }k_{2}(X):=\{h\mid(1,h)\in H\},

we obtain normal subgroups
k_{i}(X)\trianglelefteq p_{i}(X)
and canonical isomorphisms
X/(k_{1}(X)\times k_{2}(X))\xrightarrow{\sim}p_{i}(X)/k_{i}(X)
for
i\in\{1,2\}
induced by the projection maps
p_{i}
. The resulting isomorphism
\nu_{X}:p_{2}(X)/k_{2}(X)\xrightarrow{\sim}p_{1}(X)/k_{1}(X)
satisfies
\nu_{X}(hk_{2}(X))=gk_{1}(X)
if and only if
(g,h)\in X
, where
(g,h)\in p_{1}(X)\times p_{2}(X)
.

(b)

If
\phi:Q\xrightarrow{\sim}P
is an isomorphism between subgroups
Q\leq H
and
P\leq G
, then

\Delta(P,\phi,Q):=\{(\phi(q),q)\mid q\in Q\}

is a subgroup of
G\times H
. Subgroups of this form will be called
twisted diagonal subgroups
of
G\times H
. For
P\leq G
, we set
\Delta(P):=\Delta(P,\operatorname{id},P)\leq G\times G
. It is easy to verify that
X\leq G\times H
is twisted diagonal if and only if
|k_{1}(X)|=1
and
|k_{2}(X)|=1
, and that for
(g,h)\in G\times H
, one has

{}^{(g,h)}\Delta(P,\phi,Q)=\Delta({}^{g}P,c_{g}\phi c_{h^{-1}},{}^{h}Q).

Moreover, the set of twisted diagonal subgroups of
G\times H
is closed under taking subgroups.

(c)

The subgroup
X^{\circ}:=\{(h,g)\in H\times G\mid(g,h)\in X\}
is called the
opposite subgroup
of
X
.

(d)

The
composition
of
X
and
Y
is defined as

X\ast Y:=\{(g,k)\in G\times K\mid\exists h\in H:(g,h)\in X\text{ and }(h,k)\in Y\}.

It is a routine verification that composition is associative. If
\Delta(P,\phi,Q)\leq G\times H
and
\Delta(Q,\psi,R)\leq H\times K
are twisted diagonal subgroups, then

\Delta(P,\phi,Q)\ast\Delta(Q,\psi,R)=\Delta(P,\phi\circ\psi,R).

Note that
(X\ast Y)^{\circ}=Y^{\circ}\ast X^{\circ}
.

The next lemma is routine to verify.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
G,H,K
be finite groups, let
X\leq G\times H
and
Y\leq H\times K
.

(a)

We have
X\ast X^{\circ}=\Delta(p_{1}(X))\cdot(k_{1}(X)\times\{1\})=\Delta(p_{1}(X))\cdot(\{1\}\times k_{1}(X))=\Delta(p_{1}(X))\cdot(k_{1}(X)\times k_{1}(X))
.

(b)

We have
X\ast X^{\circ}\ast X=X.

(c)

For any
g\in G,h\in H,k\in K
, we have
{}^{(g,h)}X\ast{}^{(h,k)}Y={}^{(g,k)}(X\ast Y)
.

.

Let

and

be finite groups, let

be a twisted diagonal subgroup of

, and let

and

The following are routine to verify, and will be important in the sequel.

Lemma 2.4

Let

Throughout, let

Remark 4.1

(a)

(b)

5.
Trivial source rings,
p
-permutation equivalences, and splendid Rickard equivalences

Throughout this section we assume that
G
and
H
are finite groups and that
(K,\mathcal{O},k)
is a
p
-modular system large enough for
G
and
H
. We denote by
R
either
\mathcal{O}
or
k
. Let
e
be a central idempotent of
RG
and let
f
be a central idempotent of
RH

Definition 5.1
.

(a)

We denote by
T(RGe)
the
trivial source ring
, which is the Grothendieck group of the category
{}_{RGe}\textbf{triv}
with respect to direct sums. The group
T(RGe)
is free as an abelian group with standard basis given by the elements
[M]
, where
M
runs through a set of representatives of isomorphism classes of trivial source
RG
-modules. It forms a commutative ring under multiplication induced by
\otimes_{R}
, denoted
\cdot_{R}
. We always view
T(RGe)
as a subgroup of
T(RG)
in the natural way. Moreover, multiplying a
p
-permutation
RG
-module by

induces a projection
T(RG)\to T(RGe)
. In this way, if
b_{1},\dots,b_{k}
are the blocks of
RG
, then we have a direct sum decomposition of abelian groups
T(RG)=T(RGb_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus T(RGb_{k})
.

Taking duals induces a self-inverse group isomorphism
T(RG)\to T(RG)
. We write
O(T(RG))
for the
orthogonal unit group
of the trivial source ring, that is, the subgroup of
T(RG)^{\times}
consisting of all virtual
p
-permutation modules
\gamma
whose inverse is its dual, i.e.
\gamma\cdot_{R}\gamma^{*}=[R]=\gamma^{*}\cdot_{R}\gamma
. This notion does not extend to idempotents because if
\gamma\in O(T(RGe))
, then
\gamma^{*}\in O(T(RGe^{*}))
, where
(-)^{*}
is the map induced by the involution
g\mapsto g^{-1}
.

(b)

Similarly, we define
T(RGe,RHf):=T(R[G\times H])(e\otimes f^{*})
where we identify
R[G\times H]
with
RG\otimes_{R}RH
in the natural way. In the case where
e=f
,
T(RGe,RGe)
has a ring structure induced by the tensor product
\otimes_{RGe}
, written
\cdot_{G}
. We denote by
T^{\Delta}(RGe,RHf)
the subgroup of
T(RGe,RHf)
the subgroup generated by all trivial source
(RGe,RHf)
-bimodules which have twisted diagonal vertices.
Taking bimodule duals induces a group isomorphism
T(RGe,RHf)\to T(RHf,RGe)
, which has inverse again induced by taking duals. Denote by
O(T(RGe,RGe))
the subgroup of
T(RGe,RGe)^{\times}
consisting of all virtual
p
-permutation bimodules
\gamma
whose inverse is its dual, i.e.
\gamma\cdot_{G}\gamma^{*}=[RGe]=\gamma^{*}\cdot_{G}\gamma
.

Finally, observe that any
M\in T(RGe,RHf)
induces a group homomorphism
M\cdot_{H}-:T(RHf)\to T(RGe)
. If
M\in T(RGe,RHf)^{\times}
, it is an isomorphism.

(c)

The following definition is due to Boltje and Xu in
[
5
]
. A

-permutation equivalence between
RGe
and
RHf
is an element
\gamma\in T^{\Delta}(RGe,RHf)
satisfying

\gamma\cdot_{H}\gamma^{*}=[RGe]\in T^{\Delta}(RGe,RGe)\text{ and }\gamma^{*}\cdot_{G}\gamma=[RHf]\in T^{\Delta}(RHf,RHf).

Denote the set of
p
-permutation equivalences between
RGe
and
RHf
by
T^{\Delta}_{o}(RGe,RHf)
. In the case

,
T^{\Delta}_{o}(RGe,RGe)=O(T(RGe,RGe))\cap T^{\Delta}(RGe,RGe)
. We denote this by
O(T^{\Delta}(RGe,RGe))
as well. It follows that any

-permutation equivalence between
RGe
and
RHf
induces a group isomorphism
T(RGe)\cong T(RHf)
.

If
\mathcal{A}
is an additive category, then we denote by
\operatorname{Ch}^{b}(\mathcal{A})
the category of bounded chain complexes in
\mathcal{A}
and by

the bounded homotopy category of chain complexes in

.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
A
be a sum of blocks of
RG
and
B
a sum of blocks of
RH
. A
splendid Rickard complex
for

and

is a bounded chain complex

of
p
-permutation

-bimodules which satisfy the following conditions:

(a)

For every

, each indecomposable direct summand of

has a twisted diagonal subgroup of

as a vertex.

(b)

C\otimes_{B}C^{*}\simeq A
in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{A})
and
C^{*}\otimes_{A}C\simeq B
in
K^{b}({}_{B}\mathbf{mod}_{B})
, where

and
B
are considered as chain complexes concentrated in degree
0
.

A splendid Rickard complex
C
of
(A,B)
-bimodules induces an equivalence of categories
C\otimes_{B}-:K^{b}({}_{B}\textbf{mod})\to K^{b}({}_{A}\textbf{mod})
. We call this resulting equivalence a
splendid equivalence between

and

.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
A
and
B
be symmetric
R
-algebras. Suppose that
C
is a bounded chain complex of
(A,B)
-bimodules which are projective as right
B
-modules. Then
C\otimes_{B}C^{*}\simeq 0
in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{A})
if and only if
C\simeq 0
in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{B})
.

Proof.

Since
B
is symmetric and the terms of
C
are projective as right
B
-modules, we have an isomorphism
C\otimes_{B}C^{*}\cong\mathrm{End}_{B^{op}}(C)
of chain complexes of
(A,A)
-bimodules. Therefore,
\mathrm{End}_{B^{op}}(C)
is contractible by the hypothesis. The complex
\mathrm{End}_{A\otimes_{k}B^{op}}(C)
is obtained from the complex
\mathrm{End}_{B^{op}}(C)
by taking
A
-fixed points. Since taking
A
-fixed points is an additive functor,
\mathrm{End}_{A\otimes_{R}B^{op}}(C)
is also contractible. Taking homology we obtain

0=H_{0}(\mathrm{End}_{A\otimes_{R}B^{op}}(C))\cong\operatorname{End}_{K({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{B})}(C).

We conclude that
C\simeq 0
in
K({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{B})
. The converse is immediate.
∎

If
A
and
B
are blocks of
RG
and

, then a splendid Rickard complex for

and

is an indecomposable object in the homotopy category of chain complexes of

-bimodules.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
A
is a block of
RG
and
B
is a block of
RH
. Let
C
be a splendid Rickard complex for
A
and
B
. Then
C
is indecomposable in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{B})
. In particular,
C
has, up to isomorphism, a unique indecomposable noncontractible direct summand which is a splendid Rickard equivalence for
A
and
B
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3
,
C
is not contractible. Using the Krull-Schmidt theorem in
\operatorname{Ch}^{b}({}_{A}\mathrm{mod}_{B})
, write
C=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
where each
C_{i}
is indecomposable. Then

A\simeq C\otimes_{B}C^{*}=\bigoplus_{1\leq i,j\leq n}C_{i}\otimes C_{j}^{*}.

Since
A
is indecomposable as an
(A,A)
-bimodule, the Krull-Schmidt theorem in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathrm{mod}_{A})
implies that there is a unique pair

such that

and

for all
(k,l)\neq(i,j)
. Thus, if

for all

which forces

by Lemma

. This implies that

, which is a contradiction. We conclude that

and

for all

is indecomposable in
K^{b}({}_{A}\mathbf{mod}_{B})
. Using the other homotopy equivalence, we also obtain that
B\simeq C^{*}\otimes_{A}C=C_{i}^{*}\otimes_{A}C_{i}
so that

is a splendid Rickard equivalence for

and

.
∎

Definition 6.1
.

-bimodule. Note that

-module structure such that for

(h,k)\in Y

We denote this

-\underset{RH}{\overset{X,Y}{\otimes}}-:{}_{RX}\mathbf{mod}\times{}_{RY}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{R[X\ast Y]}\mathbf{mod}

Some quick calculations determine that this construction is associative in the following way: if

Remark 6.4
.

This construction originally appears in

Theorem 6.5

[

8.
Brauer pairs for splendid Rickard complexes

Each splendid Rickard complex
C
induces a
p
-permutation equivalence
\gamma:=\Lambda(C)
, and therefore has an associated set of
\gamma
-Brauer pairs. As with
p
-permutation equivalences, in general applying the Brauer construction at an arbitrary subgroup to

will not yield a local splendid Rickard complex. We may define a Brauer pair for a splendid Rickard complex in the same way as we have for
p
-permutation equivalences, treating contractible complexes as zero objects. In fact, the Lefschetz invariant of a contractible complex is zero in the corresponding Grothendieck group, but the converse of the statement rarely holds.

We will show that applying the Brauer construction at the Brauer pairs for the corresponding
p
-permutation equivalence
\gamma
do indeed yield local splendid equivalences. Moreover, we demonstrate that applying the Brauer construction at any non-Brauer pair results in a contractible complex. The main result of this paper is the corollary of the two above results, which is that the Brauer pairs for a splendid Rickard complex and its corresponding
p
-permutation equivalence are the same.

For this section, suppose

are direct summands of group algebras

and

be a splendid Rickard complex inducing a splendid equivalence between

and

, and set

. Given a Brauer pair

, denote by

the “opposite” Brauer pair
(\Delta(Q,\phi^{-1},P),f\otimes e^{*}).

We will make liberal use of the following standard homological algebra facts.

Proposition 8.2

.

(b)

The Mackey formula is natural and therefore extends to chain complexes. We have

\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}C\cong\bigoplus_{g\in[H\backslash G/H]}\operatorname{Ind}^{H}_{H\cap{}^{g}H}\operatorname{Res}^{H}_{H\cap{}^{g}H}C.

Suppose
\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}C\simeq 0
. Then the left-hand side is contractible since restriction preserves contractibility. All summands on the right-hand side are contractible as well, since direct summands of contractible chain complexes are again contractible. Since there is a coset representative in
[H\backslash G/H]
belonging to
H
,
C
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
\operatorname{Res}^{G}_{H}\operatorname{Ind}^{G}_{H}C
, and thus is contractible. The reverse direction is clear since
\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{G}
is an additive functor.

∎

Theorem 8.7
.

Suppose
C
is a splendid Rickard complex for
A
and
B
, with
\gamma=\Lambda(C)
.
If
\omega:=(\Delta(R,\psi,Q),d\otimes f^{*})\in\mathcal{BP}^{\Delta}(A,B)
is not a
\gamma
-Brauer pair, then
C(\omega):=dC(\Delta(R,\psi,Q))f
is contractible as a chain complex of
k[N_{G\times H}(\Delta(R,\psi,Q),d\otimes f^{*})](d\otimes f^{*})
-modules. In particular,
\omega
is not a
C
-Brauer pair.

Proof.

There exists an
A
-Brauer pair
(P,e)
and an isomorphism
\phi:Q\to P
such that
\lambda:=(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})
is a
\gamma
-Brauer pair. Set
I=N_{G}(P,e),\ J=N_{H}(Q,f)
,
K=N_{G}(R,d)
,
X=N_{G\times H}(\lambda)
, and
Y=N_{G\times H}(\omega)^{\circ}
. By Theorem
8.5
,
\operatorname{Ind}_{X}^{I\times J}(C(\lambda))
is a splendid Rickard complex for
kIe
and
kJf
. In order to prove the claim, it suffices to show that

\operatorname{Ind}_{X}^{I\times J}(C(\lambda))\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kJ}\operatorname{Ind}_{Y}^{J\times K}(C(\omega)^{*})\simeq 0.

If so, Lemma
8.6
(a) implies that
\operatorname{Ind}_{Y}^{J\times K}(C(\omega)^{*})\simeq 0
. Then Lemma
8.6
(b) implies that
C(\omega)^{*}\simeq 0
. By taking duals, we obtain
C(\omega)\simeq 0
, as desired.

In order to prove the above claim, we apply Theorem
6.2
to obtain

\operatorname{Ind}_{X}^{I\times J}(C(\lambda))\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kJ}\operatorname{Ind}_{Y}^{J\times K}(C(\omega)^{*})\cong\bigoplus_{t\in[p_{2}(X)\backslash J/p_{1}(Y)]}\operatorname{Ind}_{X\ast{}^{(t,1)}Y}^{I\times K}\left(C(\lambda)\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kJ}^{X,{}^{(t,1)}Y}{}^{(t,1)}(C(\omega)^{*})\right).

Thus, it suffices to show that every complex on the right hand side is contractible. Let
t\in J
. Set
\omega_{t}={}^{(1,t)}\omega
. Notice that
\omega_{t}^{\circ}=(\Delta(Q,c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R),f\otimes d^{*})
since
t\in J
.
For ease of notation, write

\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ}:=(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R),e\otimes d^{*}).

Let
\Omega
denote the set of triples
(\sigma,(S,a),\tau)
where
(S,a)
is a
kH
-Brauer pair and
\tau:R\to S
and
\sigma:S\to P
are isomorphisms for which
\sigma\circ\tau=\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1}
. Choose a set
\hat{\Omega}
of
N_{I\times K}(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R)\times H
-orbit representatives of
\Omega
which contains
(\phi,(Q,f),c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1})
. Then Theorem
6.7
(c) gives an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{\nu\in\hat{\Omega}}\operatorname{Ind}_{X(\nu)\ast Y(\nu)}^{N_{I\times K}(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R))}\left(C(\nu)\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kH}^{X(\nu),Y(\nu)}C^{*}(\nu)\right)\cong(C\otimes_{kH}C^{*})(\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ}),

where for
\nu=(\sigma,(S,a),\tau))
, we set
X(\nu):=N_{I\times H}(\Delta(P,\sigma,S),e\otimes a^{*})
,
Y(\nu):=N_{H\times K}(\Delta(S,\tau,R),a\otimes d^{*})
,
C(\nu):=eC(\Delta(P,\sigma,S))a
and
C^{*}(\nu):=aC^{*}(\Delta(S,\tau,R))d
. In particular, when
\nu=(\phi,(Q,f),c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1})
, we have
X(\nu)=X
,
Y(\nu)={}^{(t,1)}Y
,
C(\nu)=C(\lambda)
and, by Proposition
3.4
,
C^{*}(\nu)\cong{}^{(t,1)}(C(\omega)^{*})
. Thus, since
(\phi,(Q,f),c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1})\in\hat{\Omega}
, we conclude that

\operatorname{Ind}_{X\ast{}^{(t,1)}Y}^{N_{I\times K}(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R))}\left(C(\lambda)\mathop{\otimes}_{kH}^{X,{}^{(t,1)}Y}{}^{(t,1)}(C(\omega)^{*})\right)

is homotopy equivalent to a direct summand of

(C\otimes_{kH}C^{*})(\lambda\circ\omega^{\circ})\simeq A(\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ}).

We claim
A(\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ})=eA(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R))d=0
. Suppose to the contrary that
A(\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ})\neq 0
. Then
\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ}
is a Brauer pair for
A
. However, the Brauer pairs of any block algebra (regarded as bimodule) are up to
G\times G
-conjugacy given by pairs of the form
(\Delta(S),e_{S}\otimes e_{S}^{*})
for some
S\leq G
and some block
e_{S}
. So there exist
(g_{1},g_{2})\in G\times G
for which

(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R),e\otimes d^{*})={}^{(g_{1},g_{2})}(\Delta(S),e_{S}\otimes e_{S}^{*})=(\Delta({}^{g_{1}}S,c_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}},{}^{g_{2}}S),{}^{g_{1}}e_{S}\otimes{}^{g_{2}}e_{S}^{*}).

It is a straightforward computation that

{}^{g_{1}}e_{S}=e,{}^{g_{2}}e_{S}=d,P={}^{g_{1}}S,R={}^{g_{2}}S,\phi\circ c_{t}=c_{g_{1}g_{2}^{-1}}\circ\psi.

In this case,

\displaystyle{}^{(g_{2}g_{1}^{-1},1)}\lambda

\displaystyle={}^{(g_{2}g_{1}^{-1},1)}(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})

\displaystyle=(\Delta({}^{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}}P,c_{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}}\circ\phi,Q),{}^{g_{2}g_{1}^{-1}}e\otimes f^{*})

\displaystyle=(\Delta(R,\psi\circ c_{t^{-1}},Q),d\otimes f^{*})=\omega_{t}.

This implies that
\omega_{t}
is a
\gamma
-Brauer pair since it is conjugate to the
\gamma
-Brauer pair
\lambda
. However,
\omega
and
\omega_{t}
are also conjugate, so we conclude that
\omega
is a
\gamma
-Brauer pair, a contradiction. Thus
A(\lambda\circ\omega_{t}^{\circ})=0
. Since direct summands of contractible complexes are contractible, we obtain

\operatorname{Ind}_{X\ast{}^{(t,1)}Y}^{N_{I\times K}(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R))}\left(C(\lambda)\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kJ}^{X,{}^{(t,1)}Y}{}^{(t,1)}(C(\omega)^{*})\right)\simeq 0.

Finally, by applying the functor
\operatorname{Ind}_{N_{I\times K}(\Delta(P,\phi\circ c_{t}\circ\psi^{-1},R))}^{I\times K}(-)
to this equation, we obtain

\operatorname{Ind}_{X\ast{}^{(t,1)}Y}^{I\times K}\left(C(\lambda)\mathop{\otimes}\limits_{kJ}^{X,{}^{(t,1)}Y}{}^{(t,1)}(C(\omega)^{*})\right)\simeq 0

for all
t\in J
. This completes the proof.

∎

As a corollary of the preceding theorem, we obtain that the Brauer pairs for a splendid Rickard equivalence and the Brauer pairs for its Lefschetz invariant coincide.

Corollary 8.8
.

Let
C
be a splendid Rickard complex between
A
and
B
and let
\gamma:=\Lambda(C)
be its corresponding
p
-permutation equivalence. Then
\mathcal{BP}(C)=\mathcal{BP}(\gamma)
. In particular,

(a)

\mathcal{BP}(C)
is a
G\times H
-stable ideal in the poset of
A\otimes B^{*}
-Brauer pairs.

(b)

If
A
and
B
are blocks, then any two maximal
C
-Brauer pairs are
G\times H
-conjugate.

(c)

The following are equivalent for
(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)
:

(i)

(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})
is a maximal
C
-Brauer pair.

(ii)

(P,e)
is a maximal
A
-Brauer pair.

(iii)

(Q,f)
is a maximal
B
-Brauer pair.

(d)

Suppose
A
and
B
are blocks and
(\Delta(D,\phi,E),e_{D}\otimes f_{E}^{*})
is a maximal
C
-Brauer pair. Let
\mathcal{A}
denote the fusion system of
A
associated to
(D,e_{D})
and let
\mathcal{B}
denote the fusion system of
B
associated to
(E,f_{E})
. Then
\phi:E\to D
is an isomorphism of the fusion systems
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
7.7
and Theorem
8.4
, we may assume that
A
and
B
are blocks. Then the fact that
\mathcal{BP}(C)\subseteq\mathcal{BP}(\gamma)
follows from Theorem
8.7
. The other inclusion
\mathcal{BP}(\gamma)\subseteq\mathcal{BP}(C)
was already established at the beginning of this section. Claims (a)-(c) above follow immediately from
[
4
, Theorem 10.11]
. Claim (d) follows from
[
4
, Theorem 11.2]
.
∎

Another easy corollary we obtain is that splendid Rickard complexes induce local normalizer splendid equivalences.

Corollary 8.9
.

Let
C
be a splendid Rickard complex between blocks
A\subseteq kG
and
B\subseteq kH
and let
(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})
be a
C
-Brauer pair. Set
I=N_{G}(P,e)
and
J=N_{H}(Q,f)
and let
\hat{e}
and
\hat{f}
denote the unique block idempotents of
k[N_{G}(P)]
and
k[N_{H}(Q)]
which cover
e
and
f
, respectively. Then,

\hat{e}k[N_{G}(P)]e\otimes_{kI}\operatorname{Ind}^{I\times J}_{N_{I\times J}(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))}\big(eC(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))f\big)\otimes_{kJ}fk[N_{H}(Q)]\hat{f}

is a splendid Rickard complex for blocks algebras
k[N_{G}(P)]\hat{e}
and
k[N_{H}(Q)]\hat{f}
.

Proof.

Given any Brauer pair
(P,e)
of
kG
, we have a Morita equivalence
{}_{k[N_{G}(P)]\hat{e}}\mathbf{mod}\to{}_{kIe}\mathbf{mod}
, where
\hat{e}
is the unique block of
kN_{G}(P)
covering
e
. Explicitly,
\hat{e}=\operatorname{tr}^{N_{G}(P)}_{I}(e)
, and the Morita equivalence is induced by the
(kIe,k[N_{G}(P)]\hat{e})
-bimodule
e[N_{G}(P)]\hat{e}
(see
[
15
]
). The result now follows since

\operatorname{Ind}^{I\times J}_{N_{I\times J}(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))}\big(eC(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))f\big)

is a splendid equivalence for
kIe
and
kJf
.
∎

9.
Structural properties of splendid Rickard complexes

In this section, we deduce an analogous result to the following theorem of Boltje and Perepelitsky.

Theorem 9.1
.

[
4
, Theorem 14.1, 14.3]

Let
\gamma
be a
p
-permutation equivalence between blocks
A
and
B
of
kG
and
kH
, respectively. Let
N
be an indecomposable
(A,B)
-bimodule appearing in
\gamma
. Then
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(N)\subseteq\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(\gamma)
. Moreover, there is a unique indecomposable
(A,B)
-bimodule
M
appearing in
\gamma
with multiplicity
\pm 1
whose vertex is of the form
\Delta(D,\phi,E)
where
D
is a defect group of
A
. If
M\not\cong N
, then
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(N)\subset\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(M)
.

The module
M
as above is called the
maximal module
of
\gamma
. We begin proving the analogous statement with a block-wise reformulation of a theorem in
[
14
]
.

We first state an oft-used lemma. The proof of the following folklore lemma is from correspondence from P. Webb.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
C
be a chain complex of
kG
-modules and suppose that for some
i\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have decompositions of
kG
-modules

\displaystyle C_{i}

\displaystyle=U_{i}\oplus V_{i}

\displaystyle C_{i-1}

\displaystyle=U_{i-1}\oplus V_{i-1}

Let
\eta_{i}:U_{i}\to C_{i}
be inclusion and
\pi_{i-1}:C_{i-1}\to U_{i-1}
be projection. Suppose that
\pi_{i-1}d_{i}\eta_{i}:U_{i}\to U_{i-1}
is an isomorphism. Then there is a direct sum decomposition
C\cong D\oplus W
as complexes, where

W\cong 0\to U_{i}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{id}}U_{i}\to 0

is contractible.

Proof.

The map
d_{i}\eta_{i}:U_{i}\to C_{i-1}
is injective since
\pi_{i-1}d_{i}\eta_{i}
is, and we define

W_{i-1}=d_{i}\eta_{i}(U_{i})\cong U_{i}.

Now
C_{i-1}=W_{i-1}\oplus V_{i-1}
because
W_{i-1}
projects isomorphically onto
U_{i-1}
. We see that

0\to U_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{i}\mid_{U_{i}}}W_{i-1}\to 0

is a contractible subcomplex of
C
. This shows that we may assume

\displaystyle C_{i}

\displaystyle=U_{i}\oplus V_{i}

\displaystyle C_{i-1}

\displaystyle=U_{i-1}\oplus V_{i-1}

where
0\to U_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{i}\mid_{U_{i}}}U_{i-1}\to 0
is a contractible subcomplex of
C
, adjusting the rest of the notation to fit the assumption. We define

X_{i}=\{(-(d_{i}\mid_{U_{i}})^{-1}\pi^{i-1}d_{i}(x),x)\mid x\in V_{i}\}\subseteq C_{i},

and
C_{i}=U_{i}\oplus X_{i}
because
X_{i}
projects isomorphically to
V_{i}
. We calculate

\displaystyle d_{i}(X_{i})

\displaystyle=\{d_{i}(-d_{i}\mid_{U_{i}})^{-1}d_{i}(x)+d_{i}(x)\mid x\in V_{i}\}

\displaystyle=\{-\pi_{i-1}d_{i}(x)+d_{i}(x)\mid x\in V_{i}\}

\displaystyle\subseteq V_{i-1}

Thus
C
is the direct sum of the complexes

0\to U_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{i}\mid_{U_{i}}}U_{i-1}\to 0

and

\dots\to C_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\pi_{X_{i}}d_{i+1}}X_{i}\xrightarrow{d_{i}\mid_{X_{i}}}V_{i-1}\xrightarrow{d_{i-1}\mid_{V_{i-1}}}C_{i-2}\to\cdots.

∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
M
and
N
be
p
-permutation
kG
-modules and let
f:M\to N
be a
kG
-module homomorphism. Assume that
N=N_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus N_{k}
is a decomposition into indecomposable summands. For each
1\leq i\leq k
, let
(P_{i},e_{i})
be a maximal
N_{i}
-Brauer pair. Then
f
is split surjective if and only if
e_{i}\cdot f(P_{i})
is surjective for all
1\leq i\leq k
.

Proof.

The forward implication is immediate. We induct on the number of indecomposable summands of
N
. For the base case, suppose that
N
is indecomposable. Suppose that
(P,e)
is a maximal
N
-Brauer pair and that
e\cdot f(P)
is surjective. By Proposition
7.2
,
P
is a vertex of
N
and therefore
N(P)
is indecomposable. Since
(P,e)
is an
N
-Brauer pair,
eN(P)\neq 0
which implies that
eN(P)=N(P)
. Since
e\cdot f(P)
is surjective, it follows that
f(P)
is surjective as well. By
[
13
, 5.10.7]
,
f
is split surjective.

For the inductive step, suppose
N=N_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus N_{k}
. Consider the projection of
f
onto
N_{1}
, then by the base case, it follows that this is split surjective. Let
M=M_{1}\oplus M^{\prime}
, with the inclusion of
M_{1}
into
M
composed with
f
and projection onto
N_{1}
an isomorphism, and
M^{\prime}
a complement of
M_{1}
. It follows by Lemma
9.2
that we may find a corresponding direct sum decomposition of
N=N_{1}\oplus N^{\prime}
, with
f=f_{1}\oplus f^{\prime}
satisfying
f_{1}:M_{1}\to N_{1}
an isomorphism and
f^{\prime}:M^{\prime}\to N^{\prime}
a homomorphism. Since
N^{\prime}
is
p
-permutation with one less indecomposable summand, the inductive hypothesis completes the proof.

∎

Theorem 9.4
.

Let
C
be a chain complex of
p
-permutation
kG
-modules, and let
\mathcal{X}
be a subset of
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(kG)
which is
G
-stable and closed (under
\leq
). The following are equivalent:

(a)

For all
(P,e)\not\in\mathcal{X}
,
e\cdot C(P)
is acyclic.

(b)

There exists a chain complex
D
with
C\simeq D
such that every trivial source module occurring up to isomorphism in
D
has a maximal Brauer pair contained in
\mathcal{X}
.

If
C
is indecomposable and satisfies (a), then
C
itself has the property in (b).

Proof.

(b) implies (a) is straightforward. Let
n
be the minimum integer for which
C_{n}\neq 0
. For each nonzero
C_{n}
, write
C_{n}=X_{n}\oplus Y_{n}
, where
X_{n}
consists of all indecomposable summands which have a maximal pair contained in
\mathcal{X}
. Thus,
Y_{n}
consists of all indecomposable summands of
C_{n}
which have a maximal Brauer pair which is not contained in
\mathcal{X}
. We construct a chain complex
D
identical to
C
, except in degree
n+1
, where we set
D_{n+1}=X_{n}\oplus C_{n+1}
, and add the identity map on
X_{n}
to the differential of
D_{n+1}
,
d_{n+1}
. Notice that
f\cdot X_{n}(Q)=0
for all
(Q,f)\not\in\mathcal{X}
since
\mathcal{X}
is a
G
-stable ideal. Therefore,
f\cdot C(Q)\cong f\cdot D(Q)
. But since
f\cdot C(Q)
is acyclic for all
(Q,f)\not\in\mathcal{X}
by assumption, so is
f\cdot D(Q)
. Therefore
d_{n+1}
composed with projection onto
Y_{n}
is split surjective by Lemma
9.3
, and by construction, it follows that
d_{n+1}
is split surjective. Thus, we have that
D\cong D^{\prime}\oplus(C_{n}\xrightarrow{\sim}C_{n})
, where
D^{\prime}
has lowest nonzero degree
n+1
.

Note that
C
is homotopy equivalent to a shift of the mapping cone of the chain complex homomorphism
D^{\prime}\to X_{n}
. Moreover, if
(Q,f)\not\in\mathcal{X}
,
f\cdot C(Q)\cong f\cdot D(Q)\simeq f\cdot D^{\prime}(Q)
, and these complexes are by assumption acyclic. We now perform an inductive argument as follows. If the length of
C
is one, then (b) holds, since there is only one nonzero term which must vanish after applying the Brauer construction at all
(Q,f)\not\in\mathcal{X}
, and the rest follows by the previous lemma.

Now we assume (a) implies (b) holds for complexes of length at most
n
, and suppose
C
has length
n+1
. If the length of
C
is
n+1
, then
D^{\prime}
has length
n
. Since
f\cdot D^{\prime}(Q)
is also acyclic for all
(Q,f)\not\in\mathcal{X}
,
D^{\prime}
contains only modules with maximal Brauer pair contained in
\mathcal{X}
. Since
C
is homotopy equivalent to a shift of the mapping cone
D^{\prime}\to X_{n}
, and both
D^{\prime}
and
X_{n}
(as complex) are homotopy equivalent to complexes with all degrees containing only trivial source modules with maximal Brauer pairs contained in
\mathcal{X}
, we conclude
C
also satisfies this property, by invariance of the mapping cone under homotopy equivalences.

Lastly, suppose that
C
is indecomposable. Then there exists a contractible complex
C^{\prime}
such that
C\oplus C^{\prime}\cong D
. Hence,
C
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
D
and therefore
C
has the desired property.
∎

Corollary 9.5
.

Let
C
be an indecomposable splendid Rickard complex for block algebras
A\subseteq kG
and
B\subseteq kH
. Every trivial source bimodule occurring in
C
has a maximal Brauer pair contained in
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)
. In particular, the set consisting of all Brauer pairs for every trivial source bimodule appearing in
C
and the set of
C
-Brauer pairs coincide.

Proof.

By Corollary
8.8
,
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)
is a
G\times H
-stable ideal in
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(k[G\times H])
. Thus, by the preceding theorem, every trivial source bimodule occuring in
C
has a maximal Brauer pair contained in
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)
. Denoting the set of all Brauer pairs which occur in any trivial source bimodule in
C
by
\mathcal{X}
, it follows that
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)
. On the other hand, if
(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})
is a
C
-Brauer pair, then
eC(\Delta(P,\phi,Q))f\neq 0
. Thus, there exists a trivial source bimodule
M
appearing in
C
such that
(\Delta(P,\phi,Q),e\otimes f^{*})\in\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(M)
. Thus,
\mathcal{B}\mathcal{P}(C)\subseteq\mathcal{X}
.
∎

By Proposition
5.4
, the preceding result applies to any splendid Rickard equivalence between blocks after removing contractible direct summands. Moreover, by using Theorem
8.4
one obtains an obvious analogue for a splendid Rickard equivalence between sums of blocks. Finally, we note that Corollary
7.11
also holds for
C
-Brauer pairs as well.

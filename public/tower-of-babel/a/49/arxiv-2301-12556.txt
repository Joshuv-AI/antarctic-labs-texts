A Log-Sensitive Encoding of Turing Machines in the λ \lambda -Calculus
Beniamino Accattoli, Ugo Dal Lago, Gabriele Vanoni

Abstract
This note modifies the reference encoding of Turing machines in the λ \lambda -calculus by Dal Lago and Accattoli [ DLA17 ] , which is tuned for time efficiency, as to accommodate logarithmic space. There are two main changes: Turing machines now have two tapes, an input tape and a work tape, and the input tape is encoded differently, because the reference encoding comes with a linear space overhead for managing tapes, which is excessive for studying logarithmic space.



1
Introduction

This note presents a new encoding of Turing machines into the
\lambda
-calculus and and proves its correctness. It is based over Dal Lago and Accattoli’s reference encoding of single tape Turing machines

[
DLA17
]
. The new encoding is tuned for studying logarithmic space complexity even though such a study is not carried out here but in a companion paper. The aim of this note is to provide the formal definition of the encoding and the tedious calculations to prove its correctness.

The key points of the new encoding with respect to the reference one are:

•

Log-sensitivity
: the reference encoding cannot account for logarithmic space complexity because it is based on Turing machines with a single tape. Logarithmic space requires Turing machines with a read-only input tape, of input string
i
, and an ordinary work tape, and to only count the space used on the work tape—if one counts the space for
i
, it is impossible to use only
\mathcal{O}(\log|i|)
space. We refer to such a separation of tapes as to
log-sensitivity
. The reference encoding is log-
in
sensitive instead.

•

Different encoding of the input tape
: simply adapting the reference encoding to two tapes is not enough for preserving logarithmic space, because the reference encoding is tuned for time: it reads from tapes in
\mathcal{O}(1)
time but the reading mechanism comes with a
\mathcal{O}(|i|)
space overhead, while the input tape has to be handled with at most
\mathcal{O}(\log|i|)
space overhead. We then change the encoding and the reading mechanism of the input tape, trading time for space, as to read in
\mathcal{O}(|i|\log|i|)
time with
\mathcal{O}(\log|i|)
space overhead. The idea is that the position of the head is indicated by a pointer, given by the position index (of logarithmic size), and reading from the input requires scrolling the input tape sequentially, until the position index is reached.

•

Different time complexity
: by trading time for space, the new encoding is slower. If a Turing machine
\mathcal{M}
takes time
T_{\mathcal{M}}(|i|)
on input string
i
then the encoding evaluates in
\Theta((T_{\mathcal{M}}(|i|)+1)\cdot|i|\cdot\log{|i|})

\beta
-steps rather than in
\Theta(T_{\mathcal{M}}(|i|))
as in the reference encoding, because each transition reads from the input. The time complexity is however still polynomial and thus the encoding is reasonable for time (considered as the number of
\beta
-steps).

Intrinsic and Mathematical Tape Representations

A TM tape is a string plus a distinguished position, representing the head. There are two tape representations, dubbed
intrinsic
and
mathematical
by van Emde Boas in
[
vEB12
]
, described next.

•

The
intrinsic
one represents both the string
i
and the current position of the head as the triple
i=i_{l}\cdot h\cdot i_{r}
, where
i_{l}
and
i_{r}
are the prefix and suffix of
i
surrounding the character
h
read by the head.

The reference encoding of TMs in the
\lambda
-calculus uses the intrinsic representation of tapes and, as already mentioned, reading costs
\mathcal{O}(1)
time but the reading mechanism comes with a
\mathcal{O}(|i|)
space overhead.

•

The
mathematical
representation of tapes, instead, is simply given by the index
n\in\mathbb{N}
of the head position, that is, the triple
i_{l}\cdot h\cdot i_{r}
is replaced by the pair
(i,|i_{l}|+1)
. The index
|i_{l}|+1
has the role of a pointer, of logarithmic size when represented in binary.

An encoding in the
\lambda
-calculus based on the mathematical representation reads in
\mathcal{O}(|i|\log|i|)
time with
\mathcal{O}(\log|i|)
space overhead. The time cost is due to the fact that accessing the tape is done sequentially, by moving of one cell at a time on the tape and at each step decrementing of one the index, until the index is
0
. Therefore, accessing the right cell requires to decrement the index
|i_{l}|+1
times, each time requiring time
\log{|i_{l}|+1}
, because the index is represented in binary.

The new log-sensitive encoding keeps the intrinsic representation for the work tape, and instead adopts the mathematical representation for the input tape. This is because linear space overhead for the work tape is not a problem for log-sensitivity, while the mathematical representation makes it harder to write on the tape.

Binary Arithmetic and Literature.

For implementing on the
\lambda
-calculus the manipulation of the head index, one needs to develop an encoding of binary strings and three operations: successor, predecessor, and lookup of the
n
-th character of a string starting from the binary representation of
n
. We do this using the Scott encoding of binary string, but using a reversed representation of binary strings.

We developed our encoding of binary arithmetic from scratch, in a seemingly ad-hoc way. We discovered afterwards that our approach is a variation over encodings in the literature. Namely, it is quite similar to Mogensen’s encoding
[
Mog01
]
, itself building over Goldberg’s study
[
Gol00
]
.

The Deterministic
\lambda
-Calculus and the Scott Encoding of Strings

As it is the case for the reference encoding, also the new encodings has as image a restricted form of
\lambda
-calculus, what we refer to as the deterministic
\lambda
-calculus, where, in particular, call-by-name and call-by-value evaluation coincide.

Deterministic
\lambda
-Calculus.

The language and the evaluation contexts of the
deterministic
\lambda
-calculus
\Lambda_{\tt det}
are given by:

\begin{array}[]{r@{\hspace{.5cm}}rlllllll}\mbox{Terms}\hskip 14.22636pt&t,u,r,w&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&v\mid tv\\
\mbox{Values}\hskip 14.22636pt&v,w,v^{\prime}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x.t\mid x\\
\hskip 14.22636pt\\
\mbox{Evaluation Contexts}\hskip 14.22636pt&E&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\langle\cdot\rangle\mid Ev\end{array}

Note that

•

Arguments are values
: the right subterm of an application has to be a value, in contrast to what happens in the ordinary
\lambda
-calculus.

•

Weak evaluation
: evaluation contexts are
weak
, i.e. they do not enter inside abstractions.

Evaluation is then defined by:

\begin{array}[]{c@{\hspace{.5cm}}rll}\textsc{Rule at top level}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\lx@intercol\hfil\textsc{Contextual closure}\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\!(\lambda x.t)u\mapsto_{\beta}t\{x{\shortleftarrow}u\}\hfil\hskip 14.22636pt&\lx@intercol\hfil E\langle t\rangle\rightarrow_{det}E\langle u\rangle\textrm{\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ if }t\mapsto_{\beta}u\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\end{array}

Convention
: to improve readability we omit some parenthesis, giving precedence to application with respect to abstraction. Therefore
\lambda x.tu
stands for
\lambda x.(tu)
and not for
(\lambda x.t)u
, that instead requires parenthesis.

The name of this calculus is motivated by the following immediate lemma.

Lemma 2.1

(Determinism)
.

Let
t\in\Lambda_{\tt det}
. If
t\rightarrow_{det}u
and
t\rightarrow_{det}r
then
u=r
.

Proof.

By induction on
t
. If
t
is a variable or an abstraction then it cannot reduce. If
t=wv
then there are two cases:

•

w
is an abstraction
\lambda x.s
. Then
t=(\lambda x.s)v\rightarrow_{det}s\{x{\shortleftarrow}v\}
is the unique redex of
t
, that is,
u=r=s\{x{\shortleftarrow}v\}
.

•

w
is not an abstraction
. Then the two steps from
t
come from two steps
w\rightarrow_{det}u^{\prime}
and
w\rightarrow_{det}r^{\prime}
with
u=u^{\prime}v
and
r=r^{\prime}v
, because
\langle\cdot\rangle v
is the only possible evaluation context. By
i.h.
,
u^{\prime}=r^{\prime}
, that is,
u=r
.∎

Fixpoint.

The encoding of Turing machines requires a general form of recursion, that is usually implemented via a fixpoint combinator. We use Turing’s fixpoint combinator, in its call-by-value variant, that fits into
\Lambda_{\tt det}
and that returns a fixpoint up to
\eta
-equivalence. Let
\mathsf{fix}\,
be the term
\theta\theta
, where

\theta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x.\lambda y.y(\lambda z.xxyz).

Now, given a term
u
let us show that
\mathsf{fix}\,u
is a fixpoint of

u
up to
\eta
-equivalence.

\begin{array}[]{lcllll}\mathsf{fix}\,u&=&(\lambda x.\lambda y.y(\lambda z.xxyz))\theta u\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&(\lambda y.y(\lambda z.\theta\theta yz))u\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&u(\lambda z.\theta\theta uz)\\
&=&u(\lambda z.\mathsf{fix}\,uz)\\
&=_{\eta}&u(\mathsf{fix}\,u)\end{array}

It is well-known that
\eta
-equivalent terms are indistinguishable in the
\lambda
-calculus (this is Böhm’s theorem). Therefore, we will simply use the fact that
\mathsf{fix}\,u\rightarrow_{det}^{2}u(\lambda z.\mathsf{fix}\,uz)
without dealing with
\eta
-equivalence. This fact will not induce any complication.

Encoding alphabets.

Let
\Sigma=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\}
be a
finite alphabet. Elements of
\Sigma
are encoded
as follows:

\lceil a_{i}\rceil^{\Sigma}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x_{1}.\ldots.\lambda x_{n}.x_{i}\;.

When the alphabet will be clear from the context we will simply write

\lceil a\rceil_{i}
. Note that

1.

the representation fixes a total order
on
\Sigma
such that
a_{i}<a_{j}
iff
i<j
;

2.

the representation of an element
\lceil a_{i}\rceil^{\Sigma}
requires
space linear (and not logarithmic) in
|\Sigma|
. But, since
\Sigma

is fixed, it actually requires constant space.

Encoding strings.

A string in

s\in\Sigma^{*}
is represented by a term

\overline{s}^{\Sigma^{*}}
. Our encoding exploits the fact that a string is a concatenation of characters
followed by the empty string
\varepsilon
(which is generally omitted). For that, the encoding uses
|\Sigma|+1
abstractions, the extra one (
x_{\varepsilon}
in the definition below) being used to represent
\varepsilon
. The encoding is defined by induction on the structure of
s
as follows:

\displaystyle\overline{\varepsilon}^{\Sigma^{*}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x_{1}.\ldots\lambda x_{n}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{\varepsilon}\;,

\displaystyle\overline{a_{i}r}^{\Sigma^{*}}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x_{1}.\ldots\lambda x_{n}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{i}\overline{r}^{\Sigma^{*}}.

Note that the representation depends on the cardinality
of
\Sigma
. As before, however, the alphabet is a fixed parameter, and so such a
dependency is irrelevant. As an example, the encoding of the string
aba
with respect to the alphabet
\mathinner{\left\{a,b\right\}}
ordered as
a<b
is

\overline{aba}^{\mathinner{\left\{a,b\right\}}}=\lambda x_{a}.\lambda x_{b}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{a}(\lambda x_{a}.\lambda x_{b}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{b}(\lambda x_{a}.\lambda x_{b}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{a}(\lambda x_{a}.\lambda x_{b}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}})))

Lemma 2.2

(Appending a character in constant time)
.

Let
\Sigma
be an alphabet and
a\in\Sigma
one of its characters.
There is
a term
{\tt{append}}_{\Sigma}^{a}

such that for every
continuation term
k
and every string
s\in\Sigma^{*}
,

{\tt{append}}_{\Sigma}^{a}k\overline{s}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}k\overline{(as)}.

Proof.

Define the term
{\tt{append}}_{\Sigma}^{a}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.k^{\prime}(\lambda x_{1}.\ldots.\lambda x_{|\Sigma|}.\lambda{x_{\varepsilon}}.x_{i_{a}}s^{\prime})
where

i_{a}
is the index of
a
in
the ordering of
\Sigma
fixed by its encoding, that appends the character

a
to the string
s^{\prime}
relatively to the alphabet
\Sigma
. We
have:

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}{\tt{append}}_{\Sigma}^{a}k\overline{s}&=&(\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.k^{\prime}(\lambda x_{1}.\ldots.\lambda x_{|\Sigma|}.\lambda{x_{\varepsilon}}.x_{i_{a}}s^{\prime}))k\overline{s}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{2}&k(\lambda x_{1}.\ldots.\lambda x_{|\Sigma|}.\lambda{x_{\varepsilon}}.x_{i_{a}}\overline{s})\\
&=&k\overline{(as)}.\end{array}

∎

2.1
Binary Arithmetic

In order to navigate the input word, we consider a counter (in binary). Moving
the head left (respectively right) amounts to decrement (respectively
increment) the counter by one. The starting idea is to see a number as its
binary string representation and to use the Scott encoding of strings. Since it
is tricky to define the successor and predecessor on such an encoding, we
actually define an ad-hoc encoding.

The first unusual aspect of our encoding is that the binary string is
represented in reverse order, so that the representation of
2
is
01
and not

10
. This is done to ease the definition of the successor and predecessor
functions as
\lambda
-terms, which have to process strings from left to right, and
that with the standard representation would have to go to the end of the string
and then potentially back from right to left. With a reversed representation,
these functions need to process the string only once from left to right.

The second unusual aspect is that, in order to avoid problems with strings made
out of all
0
s and strings having many
0
s on the right (which are not
meaningful), we collapse all suffixes made out of all
0
on to the empty
string. A consequence is that the number
0
is then represented with the empty
string. Non-rightmost
0
bits are instead represented with the usual Scott
encoding.

If
n\in\mathbb{N}
we write
\lfloor n\rfloor
for the binary string representing
n
. Then
we have:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lfloor 0\rfloor&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\varepsilon\\[3.0pt]
\lfloor 1\rfloor&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&1\\[3.0pt]
\lfloor 2\rfloor&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&01\\[3.0pt]
\lfloor 3\rfloor&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&11\\[3.0pt]
\lfloor 4\rfloor&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&001\\
\end{array}

And so on. Binary strings are then encoded as
\lambda
-terms using the Scott
encoding, as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lll}\overline{\varepsilon}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}}}}\\[3.0pt]
\overline{0{\cdot}s}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{0}\overline{s}}}\\[3.0pt]
\overline{1{\cdot}s}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{1}\overline{s}}}\\
\end{array}

Successor Function.

The successor function
succ
on the reversed
binary representation can be defined as follows (in Haskell-like syntax):

\begin{array}[]{llll}\textsc{succ}&\varepsilon&=&1\\
\textsc{succ}&0{\cdot}s&=&1{\cdot}s\\
\textsc{succ}&1{\cdot}s&=&0{\cdot}(\textsc{succ}\,s)\\
\end{array}

For which we have
\textsc{succ}(\lfloor n\rfloor)=\lfloor n+1\rfloor
.

Lemma 2.3
.

There is
a
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{succ}

such that for every
continuation term
k
and every natural number
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathtt{succ}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(\log n)}k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}.

Proof.

Define

\mathtt{succ}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\Theta\texttt{succaux}
and

\texttt{succaux}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{\lambda n^{\prime}.{n^{\prime}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}fk^{\prime}}}}

where:

•

N_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{{\tt{append}}^{1}k^{\prime}s^{\prime}}}

•

N_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{f^{\prime}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}k^{\prime}z)s^{\prime}}}

•

N_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda k^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\,\overline{1{\cdot}\varepsilon}

We rather prove
\mathtt{succ}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(|\lfloor n\rfloor|)}k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}.
, where clearly
|\lfloor n\rfloor|=\log n
, because the proof is naturally by induction on the length of
\lfloor n\rfloor
as a string. The first steps of the evaluation of
\mathtt{succ}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}
are common to all natural numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\mathtt{succ}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}&=&\mathsf{fix}\,\texttt{succaux}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{2}&\texttt{succaux}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\\
&=&(\lambda f.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{\lambda n^{\prime}.{n^{\prime}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}fk^{\prime}}}})(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\end{array}

Cases of
n
:

•

Zero
, that is,
n=0
,
\lfloor n\rfloor=\varepsilon
, and
\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}=\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}}}}
: then

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda k^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\,\overline{1{\cdot}\varepsilon})k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&k\,\overline{1{\cdot}\varepsilon}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,\lfloor 0\rfloor}\end{array}

•

Not zero
. Then there are two sub-cases, depending on the first character of the string
\lfloor n\rfloor
:

–

0
character
, i.e.
\lfloor n\rfloor=0{\cdot}s
: then

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}&&\overline{0{\cdot}r}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{0}\overline{s}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&N_{0}\overline{s}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{{\tt{append}}^{1}k^{\prime}s^{\prime}}})\overline{s}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&{\tt{append}}^{1}k\overline{s}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&k\,\overline{1{\cdot}s}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}\end{array}

–

1
character
, i.e.
\lfloor n\rfloor=1{\cdot}s
: then

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}&&\overline{1{\cdot}r}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{1}\overline{s}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&N_{1}\overline{s}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda f^{\prime}.\lambda s^{\prime}.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{f^{\prime}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}k^{\prime}z)s^{\prime}}})\overline{s}(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&(\lambda z.\mathtt{succ}\,z)\,(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{s}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&\mathtt{succ}\,(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{s}\\
(\textit{i.h.})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(|s|)}&(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{\textsc{succ}\,s}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&{\tt{append}}^{0}k\overline{\textsc{succ}\,s}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&k\,\overline{0{\cdot}(\textsc{succ}\,s)}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,(1{\cdot}s)}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{succ}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}\end{array}

∎

Predecessor Function.

We now define and implement a predecessor
function. We define it assuming that it shall only be applied to the enconding

\lfloor n\rfloor
of a natural number
n
different from
0
, as it shall indeed be
the case in the following. Such a predecessor function
pred
is defined as
follows on the reversed binary representation (in Haskell-like syntax):

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\textsc{pred}&0{\cdot}s&=&1{\cdot}(\textsc{pred}\,s)\\
\textsc{pred}&1{\cdot}\varepsilon&=&\varepsilon\\
\textsc{pred}&1{\cdot}b{\cdot}s&=&0{\cdot}b{\cdot}s\\
\end{array}

It is easily seen that
\textsc{pred}(\lfloor n\rfloor)=\lfloor n-1\rfloor
for all

0<n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that
\textsc{pred}(\lfloor n\rfloor)
does not introduce a rightmost
0

bit when it changes the rightmost bit of
\lfloor n\rfloor
, that is,
\textsc{pred}\,001=11
and not
110
.

{\lfloor n\rfloor}=1{\cdot}r
: then

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{pred}\,z)k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{1}\overline{r}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{pred}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&N_{1}\overline{r}(\lambda z.\mathtt{pred}\,z)k\\
&=&(\lambda r^{\prime}.\lambda f.{{r^{\prime}}M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}})\overline{r}(\lambda z.\mathtt{pred}\,z)k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&\overline{r}M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k\end{array}

There are three sub-cases, depending on the string
r
:

–

r
is empty, i.e.
r=\varepsilon
. Then:

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}\overline{\varepsilon}M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}}}})M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&M_{\varepsilon}k\\
&=&(\lambda k^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\overline{\varepsilon})k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&k\overline{\varepsilon}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&k\overline{\lfloor 0\rfloor}\\
&=&k\overline{\textsc{pred}\,{\lfloor 1\rfloor}}\end{array}

–

r
start with
0
, that is,

\lfloor n\rfloor=1{\cdot}r=1{\cdot}0{\cdot}p
. Then:

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}\overline{0{\cdot}p}\,M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{0}\overline{p}}})M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&M_{0}\overline{p}k\\
&=&(\lambda v.{\lambda k.{{\tt{append}}^{0}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)v}})\overline{p}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{2}&{\tt{append}}^{0}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{p}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{0{\cdot}p}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&{\tt{append}}^{0}k\,\overline{0{\cdot}p}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&k\,\overline{0{\cdot}0{\cdot}p}\\
&=&k\,\overline{0{\cdot}r}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{pred}\,{1{\cdot}r}}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{pred}\,{\lfloor n\rfloor}}\end{array}

–

r
start with
1
, that is,

\lfloor n\rfloor=1{\cdot}r=1{\cdot}1{\cdot}p
. Then:

\begin{array}[]{rlllllll}\overline{0{\cdot}p}\,M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{1}\overline{p}}})M_{0}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&M_{1}\overline{p}k\\
&=&(\lambda v.{\lambda k.{{\tt{append}}^{1}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)v}})\overline{p}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{2}&{\tt{append}}^{1}(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{p}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&(\lambda z.{\tt{append}}^{0}kz)\overline{1{\cdot}p}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}&{\tt{append}}^{0}k\,\overline{1{\cdot}p}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char})&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}&k\,\overline{0{\cdot}1{\cdot}p}\\
&=&k\,\overline{0{\cdot}r}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{pred}\,{1{\cdot}r}}\\
&=&k\,\overline{\textsc{pred}\,{\lfloor n\rfloor}}\end{array}

∎

Lookup Function.

Given a natural number
n
, we need to be
able to extract the
n+1
-th character from a non-empty string
s
.
The partial function
lookup
can be defined as follows (in Haskell-like
syntax):

\begin{array}[]{llllll}\textsc{lookup}&\lfloor 0\rfloor&(c{\cdot}s)&=&c\\
\textsc{lookup}&\lfloor n\rfloor&(c{\cdot}s)&=&\textsc{lookup}\ (\textsc{pred}\,\lfloor n\rfloor)\ s&\mbox{if }n>0\\
\end{array}

Lemma 2.5
.

There is a
\lambda
-term
\mathtt{lookup}
such that for every continuation term
k
,
every natural number
n
and every non-empty string
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
,

\mathtt{lookup}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}\ \ \rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(n\log n)}\ \ k\,\lceil\textsc{lookup}\,\lfloor n\rfloor i\rceil.

Proof.

We can now code the function
\mathtt{lookup}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathsf{fix}\,\mathtt{lookupaux}
where:

\mathtt{lookupaux}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{\lambda n^{\prime}.{\lambda i^{\prime}.{n^{\prime}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}fk^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}}}

where:

•

N_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.i^{\prime}M_{0}M_{0}M_{\varepsilon}p^{\prime}fk^{\prime}
, where

–

M_{0}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda r^{\prime}.\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.{\tt{append}}^{0}(\lambda z^{\prime\prime}.\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.fk^{\prime}z^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime})p^{\prime}r^{\prime}
;

–

M_{\varepsilon}
is whatever closed term.

•

N_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.i^{\prime}M_{1}M_{1}M_{\varepsilon}p^{\prime}fk^{\prime}
, where

–

M_{1}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda r^{\prime}.\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.{\tt{append}}^{1}(\lambda z^{\prime\prime}.\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.fk^{\prime}z^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime})p^{\prime}r^{\prime}
;

–

M_{\varepsilon}
is whatever closed term.

•

N_{\varepsilon}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.i^{\prime}O_{0}O_{1}O_{\mathsf{L}}O_{\mathsf{R}}O_{\varepsilon}k^{\prime}
, where

–

O_{b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda s^{\prime}.\lambda k^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\lceil b\rceil
;

–

O_{\varepsilon}
is whatever closed term.

The first steps of the evaluation of
\mathtt{lookup}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}
are common to all strings
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
and natural numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\mathtt{lookup}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}&=&\mathsf{fix}\,\mathtt{lookupaux}\,k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{2}&\mathtt{lookupaux}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}\\
&=&(\lambda f.{\lambda k^{\prime}.{\lambda n^{\prime}.{\lambda i^{\prime}.{n^{\prime}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}fk^{\prime}i^{\prime}}}}})(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{i}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{4}&\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\end{array}

Cases of
n
:

•

n=0
, and so
\lfloor n\rfloor=\varepsilon
: then

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}&&\overline{\varepsilon}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.{x_{\varepsilon}}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}{x_{\varepsilon}}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&=&(\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.i^{\prime}O_{0}O_{1}O_{\mathsf{L}}O_{\mathsf{R}}O_{\varepsilon}k^{\prime})(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{3}&\overline{i}O_{0}O_{1}O_{\mathsf{L}}O_{\mathsf{R}}O_{\varepsilon}k\end{array}

Let
i
start with
b\in\mathbb{B}
, that is,
i=b{\cdot}s
:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\overline{b{\cdot}s}\,O_{0}O_{1}O_{\mathsf{L}}O_{\mathsf{R}}O_{\varepsilon}k&=&(\lambda x_{0}.\lambda x_{1}.\lambda x._{\varepsilon}x_{b}\overline{s})O_{0}O_{1}O_{\mathsf{L}}O_{\mathsf{R}}O_{\varepsilon}k\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{3}&O_{b}\overline{s}k\\
&=&(\lambda s^{\prime}.\lambda k^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\lceil b\rceil)\overline{s}k\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{2}&k\lceil b\rceil\\
&=&k\lceil\textsc{lookup}\,\varepsilon(b{\cdot}s)\rceil\\
&=&k\lceil\textsc{lookup}\,\lfloor 0\rfloor i\rceil\end{array}

•

Non-empty string starting with
0
, that is,
n>0
and
\lfloor n\rfloor=0{\cdot}p
: then

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}&&\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&=&(\lambda x_{0}.{\lambda x_{1}.{\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{0}\overline{p}}})N_{0}N_{1}N_{\varepsilon}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{3}&N_{0}\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&=&(\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.i^{\prime}M_{0}M_{0}M_{\varepsilon}p^{\prime}fk^{\prime})\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\overline{i}\\
&\rightarrow_{\beta}^{4}&\overline{i}M_{0}M_{0}M_{\varepsilon}\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\end{array}

Let
i
start with
b\in\mathbb{B}
, that is,
i=b{\cdot}r
:

\begin{array}[]{llllllll}\overline{b{\cdot}r}M_{0}M_{0}M_{\varepsilon}k\\
=\\
(\lambda x_{0}.\lambda x_{1}.\lambda x_{\varepsilon}.x_{b}\overline{r})M_{0}M_{0}M_{\varepsilon}\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{|\Sigma|}\\
M_{0}\overline{r}\,\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\\
=\\
(\lambda r^{\prime}.\lambda p^{\prime}.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.{\tt{append}}^{0}(\lambda z^{\prime\prime}.\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.fk^{\prime}z^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime})p^{\prime}r^{\prime})\overline{r}\,\overline{p}(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)k\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{4}\\
{\tt{append}}^{0}(\lambda z^{\prime\prime}.\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)kz^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime})\overline{p}\,\overline{r}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:append-char}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(1)}\\
(\lambda z^{\prime\prime}.\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)kz^{\prime})z^{\prime\prime})\overline{0{\cdot}p}\,\overline{r}\\
\rightarrow_{det}\\
\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)kz^{\prime})\overline{0{\cdot}p}\,\overline{r}\\
=\\
\mathtt{pred}(\lambda z^{\prime}.(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)kz^{\prime})\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\,\overline{r}\\
(L.\penalty\ \ref{l:pred})\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(\log n)}\\
(\lambda z^{\prime}.(\lambda z.\mathtt{lookup}\,z)kz^{\prime})\overline{\textsc{pred}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}\,\overline{r}\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{2}\\
\mathtt{lookup}\,k\,\overline{\textsc{pred}\,\lfloor n\rfloor}\,\overline{r}\\
=\\
\mathtt{lookup}\,k\,\overline{\lfloor n-1\rfloor}\,\overline{r}\\
(\textit{i.h.})\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}((n-1)\cdot\log{(n-1)})}\\
k(\lceil\textsc{lookup}\,\lfloor n-1\rfloor r\rceil)\\
=\\
k(\lceil\textsc{lookup}\,\lfloor n\rfloor s\rceil)\end{array}

The number of
\beta
steps then is
\mathcal{O}(\log n)+\mathcal{O}((n-1)\cdot\log{(n-1)})+h
for a certain constant
h
, which is bounded by
\mathcal{O}(n\cdot\log{n})
, as required.

•

Non-empty string starting with
1
, that is,
n>0
and
\lfloor n\rfloor=1{\cdot}p
: same as the previous one, simply replacing
N_{0}
with
N_{1}
, and thus
M_{0}
with
M_{1}
. In particular, it takes the same number of steps.∎

2.2
The New Encoding of Turing Machines

Turing Machines.

Let
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathinner{\left\{0,1,\mathsf{L},\mathsf{R}\right\}}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathinner{\left\{0,1,\Box\right\}}
where
\mathsf{L}
and
\mathsf{R}
delimit the input (binary)
string, and

\Box
is our notation for the blank symbol.
A deterministic binary Turing machine
\mathcal{M}

with input
is a
tuple

(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
consisting of:

•

A finite set
Q=\{q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\}
of
states
;

•

A distinguished state
q_{\mathit{in}}\in Q
, called the
initial
state
;

•

Two distinguished states
Q_{\mathit{fin}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathinner{\left\{q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}}\right\}}\subseteq Q
,
called the

final
states
;

•

A partial
transition function

\delta\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}\times Q\rightharpoonup\{-1,+1,0\}\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}\times\{\leftarrow,\rightarrow,\downarrow\}\times Q

such that

\delta(b,a,q)
is defined only if
q\notin Q_{\mathit{fin}}
.

A configuration for
\mathcal{M}
is a tuple

(i,n,w_{l},a,w_{r},q)\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}\times\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}\times\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}\times Q

where:

•

i
is the
immutable input string and is formed as

i=\mathsf{L}{\cdot}s{\cdot}\mathsf{R},\ s\in\mathbb{B}^{*}
;

•

n\in\mathbb{N}
represents the position of the input head. It is
meant to be represented in binary (that is, as an element of
\mathbb{B}^{*}
), to
take space
\log n
, but for ease of reading we keep referring to it
as a number rather than as a string;

•

w_{l}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}
is the work tape on the left of the work head;

•

a\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}
is the element on the cell of the work tape read
by the work
head;

•

w_{r}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}
is the work tape on the right of the work head;

•

q\in Q
is the state of the machine.

For readability, we usually write a configuration
(i,n,w_{l},a,w_{r},q)
as
(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)
, separating the input components, the working components, and
the current state.

Given an input string
i\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}
(where

i=\mathsf{L}{\cdot}s{\cdot}\mathsf{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{B}^{*}
) we define:

•

the
initial configuration

C_{\tt in}(i)

for
i
is
C_{\tt in}(i)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(i,0\,|\,\varepsilon,\Box,\varepsilon\,|\,q_{\mathit{in}})
,

•

the
final configuration

C_{\tt fin}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(s,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)
, where

q\in Q_{\mathit{fin}}
.

For readability, a transition, say,
\delta(i_{n},a,q)=(-1,a^{\prime},\leftarrow,q^{\prime})
, is usually written as
(-1\,|\,a^{\prime},\leftarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})
to stress the three components corresponding
to those of configurations (input, work, state).

As in Goldreich, we assume that the machine never scans the input beyond the
boundaries of the input. This does not affects space complexity.

An example of transition
: if
\delta(i_{n},a,q)=(-1\,|\,a^{\prime},\leftarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})
, then
\mathcal{M}

evolves from
C=(i,n\,|\,w_{l}a^{\prime\prime},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)
,
where the
n
th character of
i
is
i_{n}
,
to

D=(i,n-1\,|\,w_{l},a^{\prime\prime},a^{\prime}w_{r}\,|\,q^{\prime})

and if
the tape on the
left of the work head is empty, i.e. if
C=(i,n\,|\,\varepsilon,a,w_{r}\,|\,q)
, then the content of
the
new head cell
is a blank symbol, that is,
D\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(i,n-1\,|\,\varepsilon,\Box,a^{\prime}w_{r}\,|\,q^{\prime})
. The
same happens if the tape on the right of the work head is empty. If
\mathcal{M}
has a
transition
from
C
to
D
we write
C\rightarrow_{\mathcal{M}}D
. A
configuration having as state a final state
q\in Q_{\mathit{fin}}
is

final
and cannot evolve.

A Turing machine
(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
computes the function

f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{B}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}

in time
T\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
and space
S\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}

if for every
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
, the
initial configuration for
i
evolves to a final configuration of state

q_{f(i)}
in
T(|i|)
steps and using at most
S(|i|)

cells on the work tape.

Encoding configurations.

A configuration

(i,n\,|\,s,a,r\,|\,q)
of a machine

\mathcal{M}=(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
is
represented by
the term

\overline{(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)}^{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x.(x\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}^{+}}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}^{\mathbb{B}}\;\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil a\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}}\;\overline{w_{r}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil q\rceil^{Q}).

where
w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}
is the string
w_{l}
with the elements in reverse
order.
We shall often rather write

\overline{(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x.(x\;\overline{i}\;\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\;\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\;\lceil a\rceil\;\overline{w_{r}}\;\lceil q\rceil).

letting the superscripts implicit. To ease the reading, we sometimes use the
following notation for tuples

\langle s,q\,|\,t,u,r\,|\,w\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda x.(xsqturw)
, so that

\overline{(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)}=\langle\overline{i},\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\,|\,\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}},\lceil a\rceil,\overline{w_{r}}\,|\,\lceil q\rceil\rangle
.

Encoding the transition functions

The transition function
\delta(b,a,q)
is implemented by looking up into a 3-dimensional table
T
having for coordinates:

•

Input
: the current bit
b
on the input tape, which is actually retrieved
from the input tape
i
and the counter
n
of the current input position,

•

Work
: the current character
a
on the work tape, and

•

State
: the current state
q
,

The transition function is encoded as a recursive
\lambda
-term
{\tt trans}
taking as argument the encodings of
i
, and
n
—to retrieve
b
—and
a
and
q
. It works as follows:

•

It first retrieves
b
from
n
and
i
by applying the
\mathtt{lookup}
function;

•

It has a subterm
A_{b}
for the four values of
b
. The right
sub-term is selected by applying the encoding
\lceil b\rceil
of
b
to

A_{0},A_{1},A_{\mathsf{L}}
and
A_{\mathsf{R}}
.

•

Each
A_{b}
in turn has a sub-term
B_{b,a}
for every character
a\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}
, corresponding to the working tape coordinates. The right sub-term is selected by applying the encoding
\lceil a\rceil
of the current character
a
on the work tape to
B_{b,0},B_{b,1},B_{b,\Box}
.

•

Each
B_{b,a}
in turn has a subterm
C_{b,a,q}
for every character
q
in
Q
. The right sub-term is selected by applying the encoding
\lceil q\rceil
of the current state
q
to
C_{b,a,q_{1}},\ldots,B_{b,a,q_{|Q|}}
.

•

The subterm
C_{b,a,q}
produces the (encoding of the) next configuration according to the transition function
\delta
. If
\delta
decreases (resp. increases) the counter for the input tape then
C_{b,a,q}
applies
\mathtt{pred}
(resp.
\mathtt{succ}
) to the input counter and then applies a term corresponding to the required action on the work tape, namely:

–

S
(for
stay
) if the head does not move. This case is easy,
S
simply produces the next configuration.

–

L
if it moves left. Let
w_{l}=wa^{\prime\prime}
,
a^{\prime}
the element that the transition has to write and
q^{\prime}
the new state. Then
L
has a subterm
L_{a^{\prime\prime}}^{a^{\prime},q^{\prime}}
for each
a^{\prime\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}
the task of which is to add
a^{\prime}
to the right part of the work tape, remove
a^{\prime\prime}
from the left part of the work tape (which becomes
w
), and make
a^{\prime\prime}
the character in the work head position.

–

R
if it moves right. Its structure is similar to the one of
L
.

In order to be as modular as possible we use the definition of
S
,
L
, and
R
for the cases when the input head moves also for the cases where it does not move, even if this requires a useless (but harmless) additional update of the counter
n
.

Define the term

{\tt trans}^{\mathcal{M}}
, or
simply

{\tt trans}
, as follows.

\begin{array}[]{rclll}{\tt transaux}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda C^{\prime}.C^{\prime}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,Ki^{\prime}n^{\prime})\\
\\
{\tt trans}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\mathsf{fix}\,{\tt transaux},\end{array}

where:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}T&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda b^{\prime}.b^{\prime}\,A_{0}A_{1}A_{\mathsf{L}}A_{\mathsf{R}}a^{\prime}q^{\prime}xk^{\prime}i^{\prime}n^{\prime}w_{l}^{\prime}w_{r}^{\prime}\\
A_{b}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda a^{\prime}.a^{\prime}B_{b,0}B_{b,1}B_{b,\Box}\\
\\
B_{b,a}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}C_{b,a,q_{1}}\ldots C_{b,a,q_{|Q|}}\\
C_{b,a,q}&\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=&\lambda x.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}k^{\prime}\langle i^{\prime},n^{\prime}\,|\,w_{l}^{\prime},\lceil a\rceil,w_{r}^{\prime}\,|\,\lceil q\rceil\rangle\hfill\mbox{if $q\in Q_{\mathit{fin}}$}\\[3.0pt]
Sn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{\qquad if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(0\,|\,a^{\prime},\downarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\\[3.0pt]
Ln^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(0\,|\,a^{\prime},\leftarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\\[3.0pt]
Rn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(0\,|\,a^{\prime},\rightarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{pred}Sn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{\qquad if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(-1\,|\,a^{\prime},\downarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{pred}Ln^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(-1\,|\,a^{\prime},\leftarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$
}\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{pred}Rn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(-1\,|\,a^{\prime},\rightarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\par\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{succ}Sn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{\qquad if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(+1\,|\,a^{\prime},\downarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{succ}Ln^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(+1\,|\,a^{\prime},\leftarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$
}\\[3.0pt]
\mathtt{succ}Rn^{\prime}\hfill\mbox{if
$\delta(b,a,q)=(+1\,|\,a^{\prime},\rightarrow\,|\,q^{\prime})$}\end{array}\right.\par\\[3.0pt]

3
Time Correctness of the Encoding

Turning the input string into the initial configuration.

The
following lemma provides the term
{\tt init}
that builds the initial
configuration.

Lemma 3.1

(Turning the input string into the initial configuration)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
be a Turing machine. There is a term

{\tt init}^{\mathcal{M}}
, or
simply
{\tt init}
, such that for every continuation term
k
and for every input string
i\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}

(where

i=\mathsf{L}{\cdot}s{\cdot}\mathsf{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{B}^{*}
):

{\tt init}\,k\,\overline{i}\ \ \rightarrow_{det}^{\Theta(1)}\ \ k\,\overline{C_{\tt in}(i)}

where
C_{\tt in}(i)
is the initial configuration of
\mathcal{M}
for
i
.

Proof.

Define

{\tt init}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\lambda d.\lambda e.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\langle i^{\prime},d\,|\,e,\lceil\Box\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}},f\,|\,\lceil q_{\mathit{in}}\rceil^{Q}\rangle)\overline{\lfloor 0\rfloor}\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}

Please note that the term is not in normal form. This is for technical
reasons that will be clear next.
Then

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}{\tt init}\,k\,\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}}&=&(\lambda d.\lambda e.\lambda f.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda i^{\prime}.k^{\prime}\langle i^{\prime},d\,|\,e,\lceil\Box\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}},f\,|\,\lceil q_{\mathit{in}}\rceil^{Q}\rangle)\overline{\lfloor 0\rfloor}\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}k\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{5}&k\,\langle\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{I}}^{*}},\overline{\lfloor 0\rfloor}\,|\,\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}},\lceil\Box\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}},\overline{\varepsilon}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\,|\,\lceil q_{\mathit{in}}\rceil^{Q}\rangle\\
&=&k\,\overline{(i,0\,|\,\varepsilon,\Box,\varepsilon\,|\,q_{\mathit{in}})}\\
&=&k\,\overline{C_{\tt in}(i)}\end{array}

∎

Extracting the output from the final configuration.

Lemma 3.2

(Extracting the output from the final configuration)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
be a Turing machine. There is
a term
{\tt final}^{\mathcal{M}}
, or simply
{\tt final}
, such that for every continuation term
k
and for every final
configuration

C
of state
q\in Q_{\mathit{fin}}
:

{\tt final}\,k\,\overline{C}\rightarrow_{det}^{\Theta(|Q|)}\begin{cases}k(\lambda x.{\lambda y.x)}&\text{ if }q=q_{\mathit{T}}\\
k(\lambda x.{\lambda y.y)}&\text{ if }q=q_{\mathit{F}}\end{cases}

Proof.

Define

\displaystyle{\tt final}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda C^{\prime}.C^{\prime}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k^{\prime})

where:

N_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\begin{cases}\lambda k^{\prime}.{k^{\prime}(\lambda x.{\lambda y.x)}}&\text{ if }q_{i}=q_{\mathit{T}}\\
\lambda k^{\prime}.{k^{\prime}(\lambda x.{\lambda y.y)}}&\text{ if }q_{i}=q_{\mathit{F}}\\
\mbox{whatever closed term (say, the identity)}&\text{ otherwise }\end{cases}

Then:

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}&&{\tt final}\,k\,\overline{C}\\
&=&(\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda C^{\prime}.C^{\prime}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k^{\prime}))k\overline{C}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{2}&\overline{C}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k)\\
&=&\overline{(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k)\\
&=&(\lambda x.x\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}^{*}}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\;\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil a\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}}\;\overline{w_{r}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil q\rceil^{Q})(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k)\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k)\overline{i}^{\mathbb{B}^{*}}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\;\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil a\rceil^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}}\;\overline{w_{r}}^{\mathbb{B}_{\mathsf{W}}^{*}}\;\lceil q\rceil^{Q}\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{6}&\lceil q\rceil^{Q}N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k\\
&=&(\lambda x_{1}\ldots x_{|Q|}.{x_{j}})N_{1}\ldots N_{|Q|}k\\
&\rightarrow_{det}^{|Q|}&N_{j}k\end{array}

If
q=q_{\mathit{T}}
, then:

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}N_{j}k&=&(\lambda k^{\prime}.{k^{\prime}(\lambda x.{\lambda y.x})})k\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&k(\lambda x.{\lambda y.x})\end{array}

If
q=q_{\mathit{F}}
, then:

\begin{array}[]{rclcl}N_{j}k&=&(\lambda k^{\prime}.{k^{\prime}(\lambda x.{\lambda y.y})})k\\
&\rightarrow_{det}&k(\lambda x.{\lambda y.y})\end{array}

∎

Simulation of a machine transition.

Now we show that the given encoding of the
transition function
\delta
of a Turing machine as a
\lambda
-term simulates every single transition in
\mathcal{O}(|i|\log{|i|})
time, where
i
is the input string. This is the heart of the
encoding, and the most involved proof.

Lemma 3.3

(Simulation of a machine transition)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=(Q,q_{\mathit{in}},q_{\mathit{T}},q_{\mathit{F}},\delta)
be a Turing
machine. The term

{\tt trans}^{\mathcal{M}}
is
such that for every continuation term
k
and for every configuration

C
of input string
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
:

•

Final configuration
:
if
C
is a final configuration
then
{\tt trans}\,k\,\overline{C}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(|i|\log{|i|})}k\,\overline{C}
;

•

Non-final configuration
: if
C\rightarrow_{\mathcal{M}}D
then

{\tt trans}\,k\,\overline{C}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(|i|\log{|i|})}{\tt trans}\,k\,\overline{D}
.

Proof.

Let
C=(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)
. We are now
going to show the details of how the
\lambda
-calculus simulates the transition
function. At the level of the number of steps, the main cost is payed at the
beginning, by the
\mathtt{lookup}
function that looks up the
n
-th character
of the input string
i
. The cost of one such call is

\mathcal{O}(n\log n)
, but since
n
can vary and
n\leq|i|
,
such a cost is bound by
\mathcal{O}(|i|\log{|i|})
. The cases of
transition where the position on the input tape does not change have a constant
cost. Those where the input position changes require to change the counter

n
via
\mathtt{pred}
or
\mathtt{succ}
, which requires
\mathcal{O}(\log n)
,
itself bound by the cost
\mathcal{O}(|i|\log{|i|})
of the previous
look-up.

Now, if
\textsc{lookup}\,\lfloor n\rfloor i=b
then:

\begin{array}[]{llllll}{\tt trans}\,k\,\overline{C}\\
=\\
\mathsf{fix}\,{\tt transaux}k\overline{C}\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{2}\\
{\tt transaux}(\lambda z.\mathsf{fix}\,{\tt transaux}z)k\overline{C}\\
=\\
{\tt transaux}(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{C}\\
=\\
(\lambda x.\lambda k^{\prime}.\lambda C^{\prime}.C^{\prime}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,Ti^{\prime}n^{\prime}))(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{C}\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{3}\\
\overline{C}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,T\{x{\shortleftarrow}\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z\}i^{\prime}n^{\prime})\\
=\\
\overline{(i,n\,|\,w_{l},a,w_{r}\,|\,q)}(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,T\{x{\shortleftarrow}\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z\}i^{\prime}n^{\prime})\\
=\\
(\lambda x.x\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\;\lceil a\rceil\;\overline{w_{r}}\;\lceil q\rceil)(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,T\{x{\shortleftarrow}\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z\}i^{\prime}n^{\prime})\par\\
=\\
(\lambda i^{\prime}.\lambda n^{\prime}.\lambda w_{l}^{\prime}.\lambda a^{\prime}.\lambda w_{r}^{\prime}.\lambda q^{\prime}.\mathtt{lookup}\,T\{x{\shortleftarrow}\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z\}i^{\prime}n^{\prime})\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\;\lceil a\rceil\;\overline{w_{r}}\;\lceil q\rceil\par\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{6}\\
\mathtt{lookup}\,(\lambda b^{\prime}.b^{\prime}\,A_{0}A_{1}A_{\mathsf{L}}A_{\mathsf{R}}\lceil a\rceil\,\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k^{\prime}\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}})\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\par\\
L.\penalty\ \ref{l:look}\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}(n\log n)}\\
(\lambda b^{\prime}.b^{\prime}\,A_{0}A_{1}A_{\mathsf{L}}A_{\mathsf{R}}\lceil a\rceil\,\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k^{\prime}\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}})\lceil b\rceil\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}\\
\lceil b\rceil\,A_{0}A_{1}A_{\mathsf{L}}A_{\mathsf{R}}\lceil a\rceil\,\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{4}\\
A_{b}\lceil a\rceil\,\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\;\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
=\\
(\lambda a^{\prime}.a^{\prime}B_{b,0}B_{b,1}B_{b,\Box})\lceil a\rceil\,\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\;\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}\\
\lceil a\rceil B_{b,0}B_{b,1}B_{b,\Box}\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{3}\\
B_{b,a}\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
=\\
(\lambda q^{\prime}.q^{\prime}C_{b,a,q_{1}}\ldots C_{b,a,q_{|Q|}})\lceil q\rceil(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}\\
\lceil q\rceil C_{b,a,q_{1}}\ldots C_{b,a,q_{|Q|}}(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\\
\rightarrow_{det}^{|Q|}\\
C_{b,a,q}(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\par\end{array}

Now, consider the following
four cases, depending on the value of
\delta(b,a,q)
:

1.

Final state
:
if
\delta(b,a,q)
is undefined, then
q\in Q_{\mathit{fin}}
and replacing

\begin{array}[]{llllll}C_{b,a,q}(\lambda z.{\tt trans}\,z)k\overline{i}\overline{\lfloor n\rfloor}\overline{w_{l}^{\textsc{r}}}\overline{w_{r}}\\

Straightforward inductions on the length of executions provide the following
corollaries.

Corollary 3.4

(Executions)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a Turing machine. Then there exist a term
{\tt trans}
encoding

\mathcal{M}

as given by Lemma
3.3
such that
for every configuration
C
of input string
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}

1.

Finite computation
:
if
D
is a final configuration reachable from
C
in

n

transition steps
then there exists a derivation
\rho
such that
\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}{\tt trans}\,k\lceil C\rceil\rightarrow_{det}^{\mathcal{O}((n+1)|i|\log{|i|})}k\lceil D\rceil
;

2.

Diverging computation
: if there is
no final configuration reachable from
C
then
{\tt trans}\,k\lceil C\rceil
diverges.

The Simulation Theorem.

We now have all the ingredients for the final theorem of this note.

Theorem 3.5

(Simulation)
.

Let
f\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbb{B}^{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}
a function computed by a Turing machine

\mathcal{M}
in time
T_{\mathcal{M}}
. Then there is an encoding
\lceil\cdot\rceil
into

\Lambda_{\tt det}
of
\mathbb{B}
, strings, and Turing machines over
\mathbb{B}
such that
for every
i\in\mathbb{B}^{+}
, there exists
\rho
such that

\rho\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\lceil\mathcal{M}\rceil\lceil i\rceil\rightarrow_{det}^{n}\lceil f(i)\rceil

where
n=\Theta((T_{\mathcal{M}}(|i|)+1)\cdot|i|\cdot\log{|i|})
.

Proof.

Intuitively, the term is simply

\overline{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\tt init}({\tt trans}({\tt final}\mathsf{I})

where the identity
\mathsf{I}
plays the role of the initial continuation.

Such a term however does not belong to the deterministic
\lambda
-calculus,
because the right subterms of applications are not always values. The
solution is simple, it is enough to
\eta
-expand the arguments. Thus,
define

\overline{\mathcal{M}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}={\tt init}(\lambda y.{\tt trans}(\lambda x.{\tt final}\mathsf{I}x)y)

Then

\begin{array}[]{lll}\overline{\mathcal{M}}\lceil i\rceil&=&\\
{\tt init}(\lambda y.{\tt trans}(\lambda x.{\tt final}\mathsf{I}x)y)\lceil i\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}^{\Theta(1)}&(\mbox{by }L.\penalty\ \ref{l:init-config})\\
(\lambda y.{\tt trans}(\lambda x.{\tt final}\mathsf{I}x)y)\lceil C_{\tt in}^{\mathcal{M}}(s)\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}\\
{\tt trans}(\lambda x.{\tt final}\mathsf{I}x)\lceil C_{\tt in}^{\mathcal{M}}(s)\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}^{\Theta((T_{\mathcal{M}}(|i|)+1)\cdot|i|\cdot\log{|i|})}&(\mbox{by }Cor.\penalty\ \ref{coro:exec})\\
(\lambda x.{\tt final}\mathsf{I}x)\lceil C_{\tt fin}(f(i))\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}\\
{\tt final}\mathsf{I}\lceil C_{\tt fin}(f(i))\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}^{\Theta(|Q|)}&(\mbox{by
}L.\penalty\ \ref{l:final-config})\\
\mathsf{I}\lceil f(i)\rceil&\rightarrow_{det}&\\
\lceil f(i)\rceil\end{array}

∎

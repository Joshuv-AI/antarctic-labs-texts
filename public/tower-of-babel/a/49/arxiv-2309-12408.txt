Abstract

0.1
Introduction

Measuring standard model (SM) parameters with high precision in various processes may reveal yet unknown physics phenomena as deviations from SM predictions.
One of the fundamental parameters is the effective weak mixing angle
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
, which leads to
different couplings for right- and left-handed fermions in weak neutral currents.
A consequence of this difference is the effective
polarization of fermion-antifermion pairs in
\PZ
boson decays.
The
\PGtm
lepton polarization is defined as
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}=(\sigma_{+}-\sigma_{-})/(\sigma_{+}+\sigma_{-})
, where
\sigma_{+}
and
\sigma_{-}

are the cross sections for the production of
\PGtm
leptons with positive and negative helicities, respectively.

The helicity of
\PGt
leptons from
\PZ
boson decays can be measured from energy and angular distributions of the

\PGt
lepton decay products.
The polarization measures the ratio of vector to axial-vector neutral current
couplings of the
\PGt
lepton,
and therefore this ratio provides a further
measurement of the weak mixing angle solely from
\PGt
lepton couplings as detailed in the following.

Figure 1:
The four possible helicity states of incoming quarks and outgoing
\PGt
leptons. Thin arrows depict the
direction of movement and the thick arrows show the spin of the particles. The angle
\theta_{\PGt}
is the scattering angle of the
\PGtm
lepton with respect to the quark momentum in the rest frame of the
\PZ
boson.

In
\PZ\to\PGtp\PGtm
events, the helicity of the
\PGtp
lepton is expected to have a sign opposite to that of the
\PGtm
helicity.
The differential cross section for the process
\Pq\Paq\to\PZ\to\PGtp\PGtm
can be expressed at the lowest order
[
1
]
:

\frac{\rd\sigma}{\rd\cos\theta_{\PGt}}=F_{0}(\hat{s})(1+\cos^{2}\theta_{\PGt})+2F_{1}(\hat{s})\cos\theta_{\PGt}-\lambda_{\PGt}[F_{2}(\hat{s})(1+\cos^{2}\theta_{\PGt})+2F_{3}(\hat{s})\cos\theta_{\PGt}].

(1)

Here
\theta_{\PGt}
is the scattering angle of the
\PGtm
with respect to the quark momentum in the rest frame of the

\PZ
boson, as illustrated in Fig.
1
. The symbol
\lambda_{\PGt}
denotes the sign of positive or negative helicity of the
\PGtm
lepton and

\hat{s}
is the squared center-of-mass energy of the
\Pq\Paq
pair.
The
F_{i}(\hat{s})
are structure functions for the initial and final fermion pairs describing the strength and shape of the
\PZ
resonance and its dependence on the
vector and axial-vector neutral-current coupling constants.
The total cross section is:

\sigma=\sum\limits_{\lambda_{\PGt}=\pm 1}\int\frac{\rd\sigma}{\rd\cos\theta_{\PGt}}\rd\cos\theta_{\PGt}.

(2)

From the cross section in Eq. (
1
), the following quantities can be determined: the asymmetry
A_{\text{FB}}

of the forward-backward cross sections, the polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}
of the
\PGtm
lepton,
and the forward-backward polarization asymmetry
A_{\text{FB}}^{\text{pol}}
:

\begin{split}&A_{\text{FB}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}[\sigma(\cos\theta_{\PGt}>0)-\sigma(\cos\theta_{\PGt}<0)]=\frac{3F_{1}(\hat{s})}{4F_{0}(\hat{s})},\\
&\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}=\frac{1}{\sigma}[\sigma(\lambda_{\PGt}=+1)-\sigma(\lambda_{\PGt}=-1)]=-\frac{F_{2}(\hat{s})}{F_{0}(\hat{s})},\\
&A_{\text{FB}}^{\text{pol}}=\frac{1}{\sigma}[\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}(\cos\theta_{\PGt}>0)-\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}(\cos\theta_{\PGt}<0)]=-\frac{3F_{3}(\hat{s})}{4F_{0}(\hat{s})},\end{split}

(3)

where the symbols
F_{i}
correspond to the structure functions in Eq. (
1
).

The cross section includes the contributions from

\PZ
boson exchange, photon exchange, and photon-
\PZ
interference.
If
\sqrt{\hat{s}}
is equal to the Z boson mass
M_{\PZ}
, the contributions from photon exchange cancel in the numerator of the asymmetries and the following holds:

\begin{split}&A_{\text{FB}}=\frac{3}{4}A_{\text{f}}A_{\PGt},\\
&\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}=-A_{\PGt},\\
&A_{\text{FB}}^{\text{pol}}=\frac{3}{4}A_{\text{f}},\end{split}

(4)

where
A_{\PGt}=2v_{\PGt}a_{\PGt}/(v^{2}_{\PGt}+a^{2}_{\PGt})
and
A_{\text{f}}=2v_{\text{f}}a_{\text{f}}/(v^{2}_{\text{f}}+a^{2}_{\text{f}})
are the asymmetry parameters
defined by the effective neutral current vector and axial-vector couplings

v_{\PGt}
and
a_{\PGt}
for the
\PGt
lepton, and
v_{\text{f}}
and
a_{\text{f}}
, the parameters for the initial-state fermions, respectively.
In the limit
v_{\PGt}\ll a_{\PGt}
, the polarization can be written as
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}\approx-2v_{\PGt}/a_{\PGt}
, and is simply related to
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
:

\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}=-A_{\PGt}=-\frac{2v_{\PGt}a_{\PGt}}{v^{2}_{\PGt}+a^{2}_{\PGt}}\approx-2\frac{v_{\PGt}}{a_{\PGt}}=-2(1-4\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}).

(5)

Hence, a measurement of the polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}
can provide a precise determination of
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
using only
\PGt
lepton couplings.
Comparison with the value of
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
measured in the process
\Pep\Pem\to\PZ
at LEP
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]

tests the lepton universality of the weak neutral current.

Since
\PGt
leptons decay rapidly inside the detector, their polarization is measured by analyzing the energy and direction of their decay products.
Observables used for this analysis are the angles and momenta of the daughter particles with respect to the
boost direction of the
\PGt
lepton or intermediate particles in the decay of the
\PGt
lepton.

Measurements of the
\PGt
lepton polarization have been performed in
\Pep\Pem
annihilation by the four LEP experiments at center-of-mass energies
near
M_{\PZ}

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
, at the linear collider experiment SLD
[
6
]
, and
in proton-proton (
\Pp\Pp
) collisions by the ATLAS experiment at the CERN LHC at
\sqrt{s}=8\TeV

[
7
]
.

The measurement of the
\PGt
lepton polarization in
\PZ
boson decays was pioneered at LEP
[
1
]
, where the polarization could be measured in a very small window around the
\PZ
pole and as a function of the polar emission angle
\cos\theta_{\PGt}
of the
\PGt
lepton with respect to the incoming electrons. A maximum likelihood fit to this angular dependence provides the best measurement of the polarization.

A complication occurs when the polarization is measured in
\Pp\Pp

collisions; in contrast to
\Pep\Pem
collisions, the polar emission angle
\theta_{\PGt}
is not, or only very poorly, known and can not be used in the analysis.
Furthermore, instead of a fixed value, measurements are always an average over a limited range of the center-of-mass energy of the
\PQd\PAQd
and
\PQu\PAQu
quark-antiquark pairs. The measurement is effectively an integration over the experimental width of the
\PZ
boson and a fairly wide range of its rapidity and transverse momentum
\pt
. The ranges are determined by the parton distribution functions (PDFs) of the proton and the acceptance of the experiment.

In this paper, a measurement of the
\PGtm
lepton polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}

is presented, based on
\PZ\to\PGt\PGt
events in pp collisions with parton-parton center-of-mass energies

\sqrt{\hat{s}}
of 75–120
\GeV
.
The measurement is performed by
using the following combinations of leptonic and hadronic decay modes of the
\PGt
leptons:
\PGt_{\Pe}\PGt_{\PGm}
,
\PGt_{\Pe}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\PGt_{\PGm}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
, where the symbol
\PGt_{\Pe}
refers to the decay
\PGt^{-}\to\Pe^{-}\PAGne\PGnGt
, the symbol
\PGt_{\PGm}
refers to the decay
\PGt^{-}\to\PGm^{-}\PAGnGm\PGnGt
, and the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
to the hadronic
\PGt
decay modes.

Following a short description of the CMS experiment in Section
0.2
and of the Data and Monte Carlo samples (Section
0.3
), the
event reconstruction and the event selection are detailed in Sections
0.4
and
0.5
, respectively.
The background estimates are described in Section
0.6
, before the
\PGt
lepton spin variables are discussed in Section
0.7
.
After a discussion of systematic uncertainties in Section
0.8
, the measurement of the
\PGtm
lepton polarization
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}
is described in Section
0.9
.
Finally, from the measurement of
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}
, averaged over the mass range of the
\PZ
boson, the effective weak mixing angle
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
is derived and presented in Section
0.10
.
The paper ends with a summary in Section
0.11
.

0.2
The CMS detector

The central feature of the CMS apparatus is a superconducting solenoid of 6
\unit
m internal diameter, providing a magnetic field of 3.8
\unit
T. Within the magnetic volume are a silicon pixel and strip tracker, a lead tungstate crystal electromagnetic calorimeter (ECAL),
and a brass and scintillator hadron calorimeter (HCAL), each composed of a barrel and two endcap sections.
Forward calorimeters extend the pseudorapidity (
\eta
) coverage provided by the barrel and endcap detectors.
Muons are detected in gas-ionization chambers embedded in the steel flux-return yoke outside the solenoid. A more detailed description of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematical variables,
are reported in Ref.
[
8
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system. The first level (L1), composed of custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select events at a rate of around 100
\unit
kHz within a fixed latency of 4
\mus

[
9
]
. The second level, known as the high-level trigger (HLT), consists of a farm of processors running a version of the full event reconstruction software optimized for fast processing, and reduces the event rate to around 1
\unit
kHz before data storage
[
10
]
.

Differences in the mass and

Since

0.4
Event reconstruction

The particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
25
]
reconstructs and identifies each individual particle in an event, with an optimized combination of information from the various elements of the CMS detector. The energy of photons is obtained from the ECAL measurement. The energy of electrons is determined from a combination of the electron momentum at the primary vertex (PV) as determined by the tracker, the energy of a corresponding ECAL cluster, and the energy sum of all bremsstrahlung photons spatially compatible with originating from the electron track. The energy of muons is obtained from the curvature of the corresponding track. The energy of charged hadrons is determined from a combination of their momentum measured in the tracker and the matching ECAL and HCAL energy deposits, corrected for the response function of the calorimeters to hadronic showers.
Finally, the energy of neutral hadrons is obtained correspondingly from the corrected ECAL and HCAL energies.

The PV is taken to be the vertex corresponding to the hardest scattering in the event, evaluated from the largest value of summed physics-object
\pt^{2}
as described in Ref.
[
19
]
.

For each event, hadronic jets are clustered from these reconstructed particles based on the infrared- and collinear-safe anti-
\kt
algorithm
[
26
,
27
]
with a distance parameter of 0.4. The jet momentum is determined as the vector sum of all particle momenta in the jet, and is found from simulation to be, on average, within 5-10% of the generated momentum over the entire
\pt
spectrum and detector acceptance.
Pileup interactions can lead to additional tracks and calorimetric energy depositions to the jet momentum. To mitigate this effect, charged particles identified to be originating from pileup vertices are discarded, and an offset correction is applied to correct for remaining contributions. Jet energy corrections are derived from simulation to bring the measured response of jets to that of particle level jets on average. In situ measurements of the momentum balance in dijet, photon+jet,
\PZ
+jet, and multijet events are used to account for any residual differences in the jet energy scale between data and simulation. The jet energy resolution amounts typically to 15–20% at 30
\GeV
, 10% at 100
\GeV
, and 5% at 1
\TeV

[
28
]
. Additional selection criteria are applied to each jet to remove jets potentially dominated by anomalous contributions from various subdetector components or reconstruction failures.

Electrons are reconstructed within the geometric acceptance
\abs{\eta}<2.5
. The momentum resolution for electrons with
\pt\approx 45\GeV
from
\PZ\to\Pep\Pem
decays ranges from 1.6 to 5.0%. It is generally better in the barrel region than in the endcaps, and also depends on the bremsstrahlung energy emitted by the electron before reaching the ECAL
[
29
]
.

Muons are measured in the range
\abs{\eta}<2.4
, with detection planes made out of three types of gas-ionization detectors: drift tubes, cathode strip chambers, and
resistive-plate chambers.
Matching muons to tracks measured in the silicon tracker results in a relative transverse momentum resolution of 1% in the barrel and 3% in the endcaps for muons with
\pt
up to 100
\GeV

[
30
]
.

The missing transverse momentum vector
\ptvecmiss
in the event is defined as the negative vector
sum of the momenta of all reconstructed particles in an event projected onto the plane perpendicular to the beam axis.
It is computed from the PF candidates weighted by their probability to originate from the PV
[
31
]
.
Recoil corrections are applied to account for mismodeling of
\ptvecmiss
in the simulated samples of
Drell–Yan and
\PW
+jets production.
The vector
\ptvecmiss
is further modified to account for corrections to the energy scale of the reconstructed jets in the event. The pileup-per-particle identification
algorithm
[
32
]
is applied to reduce the pileup dependence of the
\ptvecmiss
observable.
The magnitude of
\ptvecmiss
is referred to as
\ptmiss
.

Leptonic
\PGt
decays,
\PGt_{\Pe}
and
\PGt_{\PGm}
, are identified as isolated electrons and muons.
The lepton
\ell
(
\Pe
,
\PGm
) isolation
I_{\text{rel}}(\ell)
is defined by the following equation:

I_{\text{rel}}(\ell)=\frac{\sum_{\text{ch. had}}\pt+\max\left(0,\;\sum_{\text{n. had.}}\ET+\sum_{\gamma}\ET-\beta(\text{PU})\right)}{\pt^{\ell}}.

(6)

In this expression,
\sum_{\text{ch. had}}\pt

is the scalar transverse momentum sum of the charged hadrons originating from the PV within a cone of size

\Delta R=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\Delta\eta)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}}=0.3
or 0.4 for electrons and muons, respectively, centered on the lepton.
The quantities
\Delta\eta
and
\Delta\phi
measure the particle separation in
\eta
and of the azimutal angle
\phi
in radians.
The sum
\sum_{\text{n. had.}}\ET+\sum_{\gamma}\ET
in Eq. (
6
)
represents a similar quantity for neutral particles.

To estimate the contribution of
neutral particles due to pileup the so-called effective-area method is used for identified electrons.
The pileup in this method is estimated as
\beta(\text{PU})
=
\rho A_{\text{eff}}
, where
\rho
is the event-specific average pileup energy
density per unit area in the
\eta-\phi
plane and
A_{\text{eff}}
is the effective area specific to the given type
of isolation.

A different method is used for the selected muons;
the contribution of photons and neutral hadrons originating
from pileup vertices
\beta(\text{PU})
is estimated from the scalar transverse momentum sum of charged
hadrons originating from pileup vertices:
\beta(\text{PU})=0.5\sum_{\text{PU}}\pt
.
The sum
\sum_{\text{PU}}\pt
is multiplied
by a factor of 0.5, which corresponds approximately to the ratio of neutral-to-charged hadron
production, as estimated from simulation.

Lepton isolation and identification efficiencies are measured with a tag-and-probe method
[
33
,
34
]
using
\PZ\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
events in lepton
\pt
and
\eta
bins from samples collected based on single-lepton triggers.

The reconstruction of the hadronically decaying
\PGt
leptons (
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates) is performed by the hadron-plus-strips algorithm (HPS)
[
34
]
.
Jets are reconstructed from PF constituents with the anti-
\kt
algorithm
[
26
]

with a distance parameter of
\Delta R=0.4
.
They are considered as
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates if they comprise one or three charged hadrons with a net
charge of
\pm
1, and up to two neutral pions. Dedicated attention is given to photons originating from
\PGpz\to\gamma\gamma

decays likely to convert to
\Pep\Pem
pairs, by collecting the photon and electron constituents, called strips, in an area
around the jet direction in the
\eta-\phi
plane.
The size of the strip varies as a function of the
\pt
of the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
To further characterize the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
signature, its visible reconstructed mass is required to be compatible with that of the

\PGrP{770}
resonance for
\PGtm\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGpz
signatures, and the
\PaDoP
1260 resonance for
\PGtm\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\PGpz\PGpz
and
\PGtm\to\mathrm{h}^{-}\mathrm{h}^{-}\mathrm{h}^{+}
signatures, with corresponding channels for the
\PGtp
.

Energy scale corrections are computed for each decay mode using either the visible mass of both
\PGt
leptons or the
invariant mass distributions of the
\PGppm\PGpz
,
\PGppm\PGpz\PGpz
, and
\PGpp\PGpm\PGppm
systems. The correction
factors are chosen such that either the visible mass distributions of both
\PGt
leptons of the MC events match the data distribution, or the invariant mass distributions of the

\PGppm\PGpz
,
\PGppm\PGpz\PGpz
, and
\PGpp\PGpm\PGppm
systems
match the shapes of the
\PGr
and
{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace
resonances
[
34
]
.

In this analysis, since the decay channels of the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
have
different polarization sensitivity, it is fundamental to identify these channels correctly.
To increase the purity of the reconstructed decay channels, a multi-class multivariate analysis (MVA)
with a boosted decision tree algorithm (implemented using the
XGBoost
library) is applied
in addition to the HPS algorithm.
The multivariate algorithm was developed for the analysis of the charge-conjugation and parity (
CP
) properties of the Higgs boson in
\PGtp\PGtm
decays
[
35
]
,
which is also very sensitive to migrations between reconstructed decay modes. Based on the simulated DY signal sample, the use of the multivariate algorithm in addition to the HPS algorithm increased the purity of the
\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGn
and
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\uprho}{}{-}}\PGn
channels in the analysis presented here from 63 to 83% and from 63 to 77%, respectively. For reconstructed
\PGt
leptons the efficiencies for the correct decay mode identifications are 76, 82 and 94% for the decays
\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGn
,
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\uprho}{}{-}}\PGn
and
\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGpp\PGpm\PGn
, respectively.

The
DeepTau
discriminator
[
36
]
is used in this analysis in order to distinguish
\tauh

\tauh

The so-called Medium Working Point (Med

WP) is used for
\tauh
as referred to in Table
0.5
of the next section.

Correction factors were measured in Ref.
[
34

for the different working points of anti-jet, anti-electron, and
anti-muon discriminators used to identify the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}

\PZ\to\PGt_{\PGm}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
events,
considering the visible mass of the muon or
\PGt
lepton candidate as observables (or alternatively the number of tracks in the signal
and isolation cones).

Background estimation

The largest background comes from multijet events where one of the jets is misidentified as a
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
Based on simulation it represents
about 84% of the expected background in the
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
channel, and 16 (20%) in the

\PGt_{\Pe}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
(
\PGt_{\PGm}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
) channels.
Drell–Yan events in dilepton final states may contribute if either a lepton is identified as a
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
or an additional jet is misidentified as
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
.
These contributions are suppressed by rejecting events containing lepton pairs with same flavor and
opposite electric charge and applying the
DeepTau
discriminants to suppress electrons and muons wrongly reconstructed as
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
candidates.
Whereas control samples are used in data to evaluate the multijet background, other contributions such as
\ttbar
and electroweak processes (EWK: single top quark,
\PZ
+jets, and dibosons) rely on simulation. The
\PW
+jets background is significant in the
\PGt_{\PGm}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
and
\PGt_{\Pe}\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
final states (respectively about 31 and 27% of the expected background) and is evaluated from data as described below.

N^{\text{QCD}}_{\text{low-}\mT}(A)=N^{\text{QCD}}_{\text{low-}\mT}(B)\ f^{\text{OS/SS}}_{\text{low-}\mT}.

(8)

Here

(9)

\PW

\PW
+jets yield in the signal region

0.7.1
Decay angles

The spin of the
\PGt
lepton is transferred into the total angular momentum of its decay products. Therefore, the orientation of the spin of the
\PGt
lepton can be revealed from the
angular distributions of the decay products relative to the direction of the
\PGt
lepton momentum and relative to each other.

\PGtm

The first angle to consider is the angle
\theta
between the boost direction of the
\PGt
lepton projected
onto the
\PGt
lepton rest frame and the momentum direction of the hadron.

\cos\theta=\vec{n}_{\PGt}\cdot\vec{n}_{\Ph^{\pm}}=2\frac{E(\Ph^{\pm})}{E(\tau^{\pm})}-1,

(13)

where

\vec{n}_{\PGt}
and

\vec{n}_{\Ph^{\pm}}
are unit vectors in the
\PGt
lepton rest frame pointing in the
\PGt
boost direction and along the hadron momentum, respectively.
The quantities
E(\Ph^{\pm})
and
E(\PGt^{\pm)}
are the energies of the hadron and
\PGt
lepton in the laboratory frame, respectively.
In the case of a leptonic
\PGtm
lepton decay, the vector of the hadron is replaced by the one of the lepton, but two neutrinos are emitted instead of one, leading to a more complicated situation.
In the experimentally favored case where the charged lepton takes most of the
\PGtm
lepton energy, the neutrino and anti-neutrino are emitted collinear and the pair is opposite to the charged lepton, and their spins will add up to zero.
The charged lepton will carry the spin of the
\PGtm
lepton, and it is preferentially emitted against the direction of
\PGtm
lepton spin.

In the decay

\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGn
,
there is zero orbital momentum in the two-body
\PGtm
lepton decay and, since the pion carries no spin,
angular momentum conservation requires the neutrino to carry the spin of the
\PGtm
lepton.
In the
\PGtm
lepton rest frame, the neutrino and pion are emitted back-to-back. Since the
neutrino is always left-handed, the
\PGpm
prefers small values for the angle
\theta
in the decay of a right-handed
\PGtm
lepton (
\HepParticle
\uptau
R-) and large values in case the
\PGtm
lepton is left-handed (
\HepParticle
\uptau
L-).
In this case, the angle
\theta
carries full spin information.

The decay into a spin-one resonance,
\PGr
or
{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace

offers the simplicity of a two-body decay, like the
\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGn
decay, but with
more complicated dynamics since the
\PGr
and
{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace

Conservation of angular momentum allows the
\PGr
and
{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace

\lambda_{\mathrm{V}}=0
or
-1
.

If the
\PGtm
lepton is in the right-handed state, the
\mathrm{V}
(
\mathrm{V}=\PGr,{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace

\lambda_{\mathrm{V}}=0

Conversely, if the
\PGtm
lepton is left-handed,
the
\mathrm{V}
is preferably transversely polarized (
\lambda_{\mathrm{V}}=-1
). Combining the spin amplitudes for all possible configurations of the
\mathrm{V}
resonance and
\PGtm
lepton helicities, one gets
[
37
]

\frac{1}{\Gamma}\frac{\rd\Gamma}{\rd\cos\theta}\propto 1+\alpha_{\mathrm{V}}\lambda_{\PGt}\cos\theta,

where the dilution factor
\alpha_{\mathrm{V}}=(\abs{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{2}-\abs{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{T}}}^{2})/(\abs{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{T}}}^{2}+\abs{\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}}}^{2})=(m^{2}_{\PGt}-2\unit{m}^{2}_{\mathrm{V}})/(m^{2}_{\PGt}+2\unit{m}^{2}_{\mathrm{V}})

result of the presence of the transverse amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{T}}
of the
\mathrm{V}
resonance in addition to the longitudinal amplitude
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{L}}

\alpha_{\mathrm{V}}
characterizes the sensitivity of the
\cos\theta

For comparison, in the
\PGtm

{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace

\alpha_{{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace}=0.021

\PGr

\alpha_{\PGp}=1

\PGtm
lepton helicity in
\tau\to\mathrm{V}\PGn

\theta

The spin of
\mathrm{V}
is transformed into the total angular momentum of the decay products and thus can be retrieved by analyzing the subsequent
\mathrm{V}

0.7.2
Optimal observables

Optimal variables to measure the helicity of
\PGtm
leptons have been widely explored at LEP and are explained in Ref.
[
39
]
.
The idea is to replace the multidimensional fits over all relevant decay angles by a one dimensional fit to a unique variable
\omega(\theta,\alpha,\beta,\gamma,...)
, which can be derived from the squared matrix elements of the decay for positive and negative helicity.
This procedure is equivalent to the use of the concept of the polarimetric vector.

In general, the differential width of any decay of a polarized
\PGtm
lepton can be described using the polarimetric vector and has the simplified form
[
37
,
15
,
38
]
:

\rd\Gamma=\frac{\abs{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}^{2}}{2m_{\PGt}}(1-h_{\mu}s^{\mu})\rd S,

(17)

where
\rd S
is the element of Lorentz-invariant phase space,
s
is the four-vector of the spin of the
\PGtm
lepton and
h
is the polarimetric four-vector.
In the rest frame of the
\PGtm
lepton, assuming that the
\PGtm
lepton spin is aligned with the
\PGtm
lepton momentum (all transverse
\PGtm
spin components are zero), this reads:

\frac{\rd\Gamma}{\rd\cos\zeta_{h}}\propto\frac{1}{2}(1+\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}\cos\zeta_{h}),

(18)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}
is the
\PGtm
lepton polarization
for a given
\PGtm
lepton decay and

\zeta_{h}
is the angle between the space-like
polarimetric vector
\vec{h}_{\PGt}
and
\vec{n}_{\PGt}
, the direction of the
\PGtm
lepton as defined above.

The polarimetric vector
\vec{h}_{\PGt}

depends on the particular final state. This vector describes
how the tau decay products propagate depending on the tau spin;
for
\PGtm
lepton decays into hadrons it depends on
the momenta of all the hadrons in the decay chain and the decay mode of the intermediate resonance.

In the case of
\PGtm\to\PGpm\PGn
, the polarimetric vector is aligned with the direction of the
\PGpm
meson:
\vec{h}_{\PGt}=\vec{n}_{\PGp}
, where
\vec{n}_{\PGp}
is a unit vector pointing along the momentum of the pion.

The situation in
\PGtm\to({\HepParticle{\uprho}{}{-}}\to\PGpm\PGpz)\PGn
and
\PGtm\to({\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{-}}\Xspace\to\PGpm\PGpp\PGpm)\PGn
decays is more complicated due to
the resonance structure. The general expressions for
polarimetric vectors in the decays
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\uprho}{}{-}}\PGn
and
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{-}}\Xspace\PGn
is reported in Ref.
[
38
]
and references therein, including a discussion of the hadronic form factor in
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{-}}\Xspace\PGn
decays.

We denote the optimal observable for a single
\PGtm
lepton decay as
\omega(\Ph)
, which is defined in Ref.
[
39
]
:

\omega(\Ph)=\cos\zeta_{h}.

(19)

The optimal observable
\omega(\Ph)
carries the full sensitivity and has the same unique distribution distinguishing left and right
\PGt
lepton helicities for all hadronic decay modes.
The simple linear distribution of
\omega(\Ph)
(for
\Ph=\PGp,\PGr,{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace
) is shown in Fig. 5 of Ref.
[
38
]
. The use of such an optimal
variable simplifies the extraction of the polarization and combines all spin
sensitive angles, even those with low discrimination power, into a one-dimensional quantity.

For the
\PGtm\to{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{-}}\Xspace\PGn
decay, we use the parametrization provided by the CLEO
Collaboration
[
40
]
for the hadronic form factor,
which includes in total seven resonances that describe the intermediate states of the
{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{-}}\Xspace\to\PGpm\PGpp\PGpm
decay. The CLEO parametrization for this decay
mode is also used in the
\TAUOLA

[
15
,
16
]
and
\PYTHIA
8
[
13
]
Monte Carlo generators.

0.7.3

The sensitivity to the polarization measurement can be improved by exploiting the anti-correlation of helicity within a
\PGt

The method, proposed in Ref.
[

, defines a new optimal observable for the
\PGt

0.9
Measurement of the
\PGt
lepton polarization

The average
\PGt
lepton polarization
\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle
is defined as

\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle=\frac{N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})-N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}})}{N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})+N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}})},

(22)

where
N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})
and
N(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}})
are the numbers of
\PZ
boson decays into
\PGt
leptons with positive and negative
\PGtm
lepton helicity, respectively.
The average polarization depends on
the accepted mass range of the
\PGt
lepton pair.
The numbers
\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L/R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R/L}}{+}}
are obtained by a maximum likelihood fit of representative templates to the distributions of the observables listed in
Table
0.7.4
. The signal templates are derived from simulated signal events.
The background templates are composed of the contributions estimated from data or from simulation, as described in Section
0.6
.

Figure 4:
Some examples of templates for six
\PGtm
lepton decays:
\Pe+\PGr,\Pe+{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace,\Pe+\PGp
in the upper row and
\PGm+\PGr,\PGm+{\HepParticle{\Pa}{1}{}}\Xspace,\PGm+\PGp
in the lower row. The blue and red lines indicate right and left-handed
\PGtm
leptons, respectively. The templates have been rescaled to correspond to zero polarization at the generator level. The uncertainty bars are statistical only and correspond to the limited MC samples after all selections. The shapes of the templates depend on the decay mode and on the nature of the chosen discriminating observables.

The extracted numbers from these maximum likelihood fits and thus the polarization are always an average over a range of intrinsic polarization given by

\mathcal{P}_{\tau}=\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}},\hat{s},\PQq\right),

(23)

which depends on the effective weak mixing angle, the quark-antiquark center-of-mass energy
\sqrt{\hat{s}}
and the quark type
\PQq
specifying whether the
\PZ
boson has been produced via a pair of up- or down-type quarks.

The measured average polarization can be written as

\left\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}})\right\rangle=\frac{\sum_{\PQq=\PQu,\PQd}\int\rd\hat{s}\;\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}},\hat{s},\PQq\right)w\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}},\hat{s},\PQq\right)}{\sum_{\PQq=\PQu,\PQd}\int\rd\hat{s}\,w\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}},\hat{s},\PQq\right)}.

(24)

Here
w\left(\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}},\hat{s},\PQq\right)
denotes the weights in the averaging. These weights include two effects: (i) the dependence of the cross section
\sigma
on the quark-antiquark squared center-of-mass energy
\hat{s}
and the quark type
\PQq
for a given value of the mixing angle
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
; and (ii) the acceptance of the detector and the selection efficiency in the analysis, which both depend on
\hat{s}
and quark
\PQq
as well.

Figure 5:
Variation of the templates for negative (left) and positive (middle)
\PGtm

\Pe+\PGr
(upper row) and

The graphs on the right show the ratio of the ratios of the changes for negative and positive helicities, which is flat and centered exactly at 1 demonstrating that a possible PDF effect on the polarization is very small. The drawn error bars are large because the events in the ratios are correlated, so only the fluctuations of the points are relevant and are within approximately 1%, much smaller than other systematic uncertainties in this analysis.

The efficiencies for right- and left-handed
\PGtm
leptons are different and depend strongly on the decay mode and the selection applied in the analysis, especially on transverse momenta of the
\PGtm
lepton decay products.
Therefore, we adopt a method where these effects are included by using templates for right- and left-handed
\PGtm
leptons. The templates are histograms of the
\PGtm
polarization observables for each decay category of the
\PGtm
lepton. They are generated by applying all selections and corrections of the analysis chain to the simulated events of the signal.
Generator-level information is used to construct
separate templates for right- and left-handed
\PGtm
leptons.

These templates are normalized such that they correspond to zero polarization for a given range (75–120
\GeV
) of
\sqrt{\hat{s}}
at the generator level.
The normalization proceeds in the following way.
First the templates are defined using the simulated data samples described above for signal and background:

\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{simulation})=\mathcal{T}(\text{sig.},\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle,r)+\mathcal{T}(\text{bkg.}).

(25)

The signal template
\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{sig.},\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle,r)
contains the two parts
\mathcal{T}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})

and
\mathcal{T}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}})
for the two helicity states and depends on two parameters of interest, the average
\PGt
polarization
\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle
and an overall signal strength modifier
r
as given in the following equation,

\mathcal{T}(\mathrm{sig.},\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle,r)=r\left[\frac{1+\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle}{2}\mathcal{T}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})+\frac{1-\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle}{2}\mathcal{T}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}})\right].

(26)

To extract the average
\PGtm
lepton polarization directly from the fit, the signal templates entering the fit must be rescaled such that they correspond to an unpolarized sample of
\PZ\to\PGtp\PGtm
events.
This is achieved by renormalizing the integrals of the templates
\mathcal{T}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L/R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R/L}}{+}})
with the product of
two factors,
s_{\mathrm{LR}/\mathrm{RL}}
and
s_{\mathrm{tot}}
, where

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{LR}/\mathrm{RL}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N_{\text{gen}}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L/R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R/L}}{+}})},

(27)

\displaystyle s_{\mathrm{tot}}

\displaystyle=N_{\text{gen}}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{+}})+N_{\text{gen}}(\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R}}{+}}).

(28)

These normalization factors are obtained from event counts at the generator level for each
N_{\mathrm{gen}}
before any selections are applied. The scale factor
s_{\mathrm{LR}/\mathrm{RL}}
produces an unpolarized sample before the event selection and the second factor
s_{\mathrm{tot}}
ensures that the overall number of signal events remains unchanged.
In this way, the templates for right- and left-handed helicities incorporate all
biases due to the trigger and analysis selections, and
the average polarization can be read off directly from the fit results according to Eq. (
26
).

All experimental selections are contained in the templates and all systematic uncertainties discussed in the previous section are applied simultaneously to the signal and background templates in the global maximum likelihood fits presented in Section
0.10
. The significance of the specific normalization of the templates means that they refer to a null average polarisation within a mass window of 75 to 120
\GeV
at the generator level.

A global maximum-likelihood fit is performed in the 11 event categories given in Table
0.7.4
in
the
\PGt_{\Pe}

\PGt_{\PGm}
,
\PGt_{\Pe}

\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
,
\PGt_{\PGm}

\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
, and
\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}

\PGt_{\mathrm{h}}
channels. We treat the signal strength
r
as a free parameter in this fit in order to ensure that only the shape information is used to estimate the average
\PGt
lepton polarization
\langle\mathcal{P}_{\tau}\rangle
.

The templates of the two signal contributions
\PZ\to{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{L/R}}{-}}{\HepParticle{\uptau}{\mathrm{R/L}}{+}}
reflect the bias from detector acceptance and event selection, and thus the average polarization is retrieved correctly by the fit according to Eqs. (
25
) and (
26
).

A closure test using MC simulated events was performed to verify that the procedure for measuring the
\PGt
lepton polarization is unbiased.

Representative templates for three of the 11 decay categories are presented in Fig.
4
.
The difference in the templates between right- and left-handed
\PGtm
leptons is clearly visible.
The distinctive shapes in different decay channels are partially distorted by the trigger and analysis selection. This is particularly true for the lepton plus pion channel where the
\pt
selection suppresses the left-hand part of the distribution. From this, it is evident that various categories have different discrimination power.
The distributions also demonstrate some bin-by-bin fluctuations that sometimes exceed the statistical fluctuations of the MC samples. Those fluctuations are included when treating the systematic uncertainties, whereas the plots contain only the statistical uncertainties.

To test the stability of the templates with respect to different PDF choices, events are reweighted by the relative weights of 13 different sets of PDFs available for the analysis and new templates are created from the reweighted events.
The change in the normalization and shape of the templates was studied with respect to the nominal PDF set (NNPDF3.0), finding that
only the overall normalization changes by at most a few percent, whereas the shapes remain unchanged, affecting the templates for positive and negative helicity in the same way, so that the relative ratio between the templates remained constant.
In other words, the average polarization extracted from the ratio of templates according to Eq. (
26
) does not change.

In Fig.
5
we compare templates with NNPDF3.0 and CTEQ6L1 PDFs for both negative and positive
\PGtm
lepton helicities in the
\Pe+\PGr
and
\PGm+\PGr
categories.
The change is uniform and of a few percent, and
the double ratio of the relative overall changes is identical to unity for all studied variations and less than 1% on the polarization measurement, as shown by the third graph of Fig.
5
.

A further test of the stability of the procedure consisted in a subdivision of the data into three bins of
pseudorapidity of the decaying
\PZ
boson:
\abs\eta^{\PZ}<1.3
,
1.3<\abs{\eta^{\PZ}}<2.2
, and
\abs{\eta^{\PZ}}>2.2
. This test was of particular importance to assure that
there is no bias in the polarization measurement in different kinematic regions.

\PZ

A_{\PGt}

\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}(\PZ)=-A_{\PGt}=-0.144\pm 0.006\stat\pm 0.014\syst=-0.144\pm 0.015.

[

\PZ

A_{\PGt}

Initial-state gluon radiation of the incoming quarks, which is more important than photon radiation, is included in the
\MGvATNLO

No further NLO corrections have been applied to the asymmetry parameter, since it is expected that their effect will be significantly smaller than the systematic uncertainties.
It is the most precise measurement of
A_{\PGt}
at the LHC and of comparable precision as the SLD experiment.

A_{\PGt}

A_{\PGt}=0.1439\pm 0.0043

[

and

A_{\ell}=0.1468\pm 0.0003

[

.

.

The polarization of
\PGtm
leptons produced in
\PZ
bosons decays is a direct consequence of the structure of the weak interaction, namely the mixing of the
\PBz
and the
\PW^{0}
fields, which is parametrized by the effective mixing angle,
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}
. In the analysis it is not possible to distinguish between
\PZ\to\PGtp\PGtm
and
\gamma\to\PGtp\PGtm
, because they produce exactly the same final state. The
\gamma\to\PGtp\PGtm
process results in unpolarized
\PGtm
leptons.
Because the relative fraction of
\PZ/\gamma\to\PGtp\PGtm
events changes as a function of the quark-antiquark invariant mass

\sqrt{\hat{s}}
, the polarization has to be considered as a function of this mass, the effective mixing angle, and the quark type, specifying whether the
\PZ
has been produced via a pair of up- or down-type quarks, see Eq. (
23
). This average has been accounted for in our procedure described in Section
0.9
to correct the measured polarization to the
\PZ
pole.
This result can be directly used in the relation of Eq. (
5
) to obtain a value for the effective electroweak mixing angle:

\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}=0.2319\pm 0.0008\stat\pm 0.0018\syst=0.2319\pm 0.0019.

(31)

Our result is in good agreement with the most precise value of the effective weak mixing angle measured in
\PZ\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}
decays obtained by the combination of the LEP and SLD results
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}=0.2315\pm 0.0002

[
43
]
.

0.11
Summary

The CMS detector was used to measure the polarization of
\PGtm
leptons in the decay of
\PZ
bosons produced in proton-proton collisions
at the LHC at
\sqrt{s}=13\TeV
in a data sample corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 36.3
\fbinv
.
Eleven different combinations of decay modes of the
\PGt
leptons were used to study the polarization.

The measured
\PGtm
lepton polarization,
\mathcal{P}_{\PGt}(\PZ)=-0.144\pm 0.006\stat\pm 0.014\syst=-0.144\pm 0.015
,
is in good agreement with the SLD, LEP and ATLAS results.
It is also compatible with the world average value of the lepton asymmetry parameter
A_{\ell}

[
45
]
.
This result is at present the most precise measurement at hadron colliders
and reaches a similar precision to the SLD experiment.

The measured polarization constrains the effective couplings of
\PGtm
leptons to the
\PZ
boson and determines the effective weak mixing angle
to be
\sin^{2}\theta_{\mathrm{W}}^{\text{eff}}=0.2319\pm 0.0019
. This result has a precision of 0.8% and is independent of the production process of the
\PZ
boson.

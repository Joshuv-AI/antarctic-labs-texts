Decomposition of Jacobian varieties and Shimura subvarieties in A g A_{g}
Abolfazl Mohajer

Abstract
. Using the decomposition of Jacobians with group action, we prove the non-existence of some Shimura subvarieties in the moduli space of ppav A g A_{g} arising from families of dihedral and quaternionic covers of the complex projective line ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} .

1.
Introduction

Consider a family of non-singular complex projective curves
f:C\to T
of genus
g
. The Torelli map associated to this family is the map
\tau:T\to A_{g}
which sends a curve
[C]
to its Jacobian
[J(C)]
. It is known by the classical Torelli theorem that this map is injective. The moduli space
A_{g}
of
g
-dimensional complex principally polarized abelian varieties has the structure of a
Shimura variety
. Indeed,
A_{g}=A_{g,l}=\Gamma_{g}(l)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
, where
{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
is the Siegel uper half-space of genus
g
and
\Gamma_{g}(l)
is the principal congruence subgroup of level-
l
in
Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})
which is the kernel of the natural map
Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}})\to\Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{Z}}/l)
, where
l\geq 3
is an odd integer. In this case
\Gamma_{g}(l)
is torsion-free and the quotient
\Gamma_{g}(l)\backslash{\mathbb{H}}_{g}
is a smooth complex submanifold. Note that
{\mathbb{H}}_{g}=\Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{R}})/U(g)
. Inside
A_{g}
there are specific subvarieties called
special subvarieties
or
Shimura subvarieties
. These are algebraic subvarieties of the form
\Gamma_{g}(l)\backslash{\mathbb{L}}
, where
{\mathbb{L}}
comes from an algebraic subgroup
G({\mathbb{R}})\hookrightarrow\Sp_{2g}({\mathbb{R}})
. Special subvarieties have many interesting properties. They are for instance
totally geodesic
subvarieties. In this paper, we consider families
f:C\to T
of
G
-Galois covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
that generate special subvarieties in
A_{g}
. The special subvarieties arising from families of curves and in particular by Galois covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
have been studied by many authors for instance in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
,
[
6
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
and
[

]
. Below, we will describe our main tools for studying such families in this paper.

1.1.
Group algebra decomposition of abelian varieties

For a more detailed presentation of the content of this subsection, we refer to the book of Birkenhake and Lange,
[
2
]
, chapter §13.6. Another reference is
[
11
]
by Lange and Rodriguez. Let
A
be an abelian variety over the complex numbers and suppose that a finite group
G
acts on
A
. This induces an algebra homomorphism

(1.0.1)

\rho:{\mathbb{Q}}[G]\to\en_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(A),

where
{\mathbb{Q}}[G]
denotes the rational group algebra of
G
. This homomorphism induces a decomposition of the abelian variety
A
upto isogeny. Although the representation
\rho
may not be faithful, we do not distinguish between elements of
{\mathbb{Q}}[G]
and their images in
\en_{{\mathbb{Q}}}(A)
. An element
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Q}}[G]
defines an abelian subvariety
A^{\alpha}:=\ima(m\alpha)\subseteq A
, where
m
is some positive integer such that
m\alpha\in\en(A)
. We remark that
{\mathbb{Q}}[G]
is a finite dimensional semisimple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebra and therefore admits a unique decomposition

(1.0.2)

{\mathbb{Q}}[G]=Q_{1}\times\cdots\times Q_{r}

with simple
{\mathbb{Q}}
-algebras
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}
. The following theorem gives the decomposition of abelian varieties with a
G
-group action and is key to our further analysis.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a fintie group acting on an abelian variety
A
. Let
W_{1},\ldots,W_{r}
denote the irreducible
{\mathbb{Q}}
-representations of
G
and
n_{i}:=\dim_{D_{i}}(W_{i})
with
D_{i}:=\en_{G}(W_{i})
for
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then there are abelian subvarieties
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{r}
of
A
and an isogeny.

A\sim Y^{n_{1}}\times\cdots\times Y^{n_{r}}
.

This decomposition is called the
group algebra decomposition
of the abelian variety
A
with respect to
G
. For more details about the above results we refer to Birkenhake, Lange’s book
[
2
]
, §13.6. In this article we are mainly interested in the case that
X
is a smooth projective curve over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
A=J(X)
is the Jacobian of
X
which is a principally polarized abelian variety over the complex numbers.

1.2.
Families of Galois covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

In this paper we deal with families of Galois covers of the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Let us briefly describe such covers. Consider a finite subset
\Delta=\{t_{1},\dots,t_{r}\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{1}
. It is then well-known that the fundamental group
\pi_{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\Delta)
has the following presentation

(1.1.1)

\pi_{1}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\Delta):=\Gamma_{r}=\langle g_{1},\dots,g_{r}\mid g_{1}\cdots g_{r}=1\rangle

If
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a Galois cover of curves with branch locus
\Delta
, let us set
U:={\mathbb{P}}^{1}\setminus\Delta
and
V:=f^{-1}(U)
. Then
f|_{V}:V\to U
is an unramified Galois covering. If
G
is the Galois group of
f
, then there is an epimorphism
\pi_{1}(U)\to G
or in other words an epimorphism
\Gamma_{r}\to G
. We therefore make following defintion.

Definition 1.2
.

A datum is a triple
(\mi,G,\Phi)
, where
\mi=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{r})
is an
r
-tuple of integers
m_{i}\geq 2
,
G
is a finite group and
\Phi:\Gamma_{r}\to G
is an epimorphism such that
\Phi(\gamma_{i})
has order
m_{i}
for each
i
.

The Riemann’s existence theorem asserts that a cover
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
can be completely determined by a datum. The following, is one of our main tools for excluding Shimura varieties generated by families of Galois covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
.

Lemma 1.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{C}}\to T
be a family of
G
-covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
given by the datum
(\mi,G,\Phi)
. Suppose that
N\lhd G
a normal subgroup and write
G^{\prime}=G/N
. Consider the family of quotients
\overline{C}=C/N\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
of
G^{\prime}
-covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
gcorresponding to the datum
(\mi^{\prime},G^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
. If
(\mi,G,\Phi)
gives rise to a Shimura subvariety in the Torelli locus
T_{g}
, then
(\mi^{\prime},G^{\prime},\Phi^{\prime})
is also a Shimura subvariety in
T_{g^{\prime}}
.

Proof.

This is a generalization of
[
14
]
, Lemma 4.4. Let

be a Galois

is the quotient cover with the notations as above.

Remark 1.4
.

If the family
f:Y\rightarrow T
gives rise to a Shimura subvariety in
A_{g}
then the connected monodromy group
\mon^{0}
is a normal subgroup of the generic Mumford-Tate group

. In fact in this case
\mon^{0}=M^{der}
. See for example
[

]
. Consequently, if
f
gives rise to a Shimura subvariety and

as a product of simple Lie groups then
\mon^{0,ad}_{\mathbb{R}}=\prod_{1}^{l}Q_{i}
.

Remark 1.5
.

Let
f:C\to T
be a family of curves. Let
M
be the generic Mumford-Tate group of the family
f:C\rightarrow T
. For the definition and construction of the generic Mumford-Tate group we refer to Rohde’s book
[
16
]
. Note that
M
is a reductive
\mathbb{Q}
-algebraic group . Let

be the natural Shimura variety associated to

is in fact the

of

in
A_{g}
. The dimension

. If

which can be read from Table V of
[
9

. If

. We remark that for

\delta(Q)=pq
and for

2.
Shimura subvarieties

2.1.
Families of dihedral covers

Throughout this note, we fix the following presentation for the dihedral group of order
2n

(2.0.1)

D_{n}=\langle r,s|r^{n}=s^{2}=(rs)^{2}=1\rangle

The elements of the form
r^{u}
with
1\leq u\leq n
are called
rotation
and elements of the form
r^{a}s
with
a\geq 0
are called
reflection
. Reflections are elements of order 2. We begin our analysis of dihedral covers by the following lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a
D_{n}
-cover. Then the number of branch points whose ramification is given by reflections
r^{a}s
as mentioned above is a non-zero even number.

Proof.

For this and more general results on dihedral and non-abelian covers we refer to
[
13
]
, especially Proposition 3.12. Note that we always assume that the cover is not ramified over the infinity.
∎

Let
C
be a curve with
D_{2p}
-action, where
p
is an odd prime. If
C\to Y=C/D_{2p}
is the quotient cover, then by results of
[
10
]
, §4.4 we have

(2.1.1)

J(C)\sim J(Y)\times P(C_{\langle r\rangle}/Y)\times P(C_{D_{p}}/Y)\times P(C_{\widetilde{D}_{p}}/Y)\times B

Here
D_{p}=\langle r^{2},s\rangle,\widetilde{D}_{p}=\langle r^{2},rs\rangle
and
\langle r\rangle
are normal subgroups of index 2. The factor
B
is a product of abelian varieties which we do not need in the sequel.

In the case that
n=2p
, with
p
an odd prime number, one obtains the following statement about 1-dimensional families of dihedral covers.

In the following, we exclude all of the families of dimension greater than 12 and with a special ramifications type.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that we have a family of
D_{2p}
-covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with
l>12
branch points (hence a family of dimension
\dim>9
) and such that all of the ramifications in
\langle r\rangle
(i.e., given by rotations) are of the form
r^{u}
with
u
an odd number (the notation being as in Presentation
2.0.1
). Then the image of this family under the Torelli map is not a Shimura subvariety.

Proof.

Suppose that the family is given by the branch points
(t_{1},\ldots,t_{l})
with
l>9
. Now consider the the normal subgroup
N:=\langle r\rangle
of index 2 and the quotient family of double covers
C_{t}/N\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
. Let
k
be the number of branch points whose ramification is a reflection, i.e., of the form
r^{a}s
with
a\geq 0
. Then the above quotient family corresponds to the family of covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
with monodromy data
(2,(\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{k},0,\ldots,0))
, where the number of 1’s is equal to
k
. This family has an eigenspace
{\mathcal{L}}
of the type
(\frac{k}{2}-1,\frac{k}{2}-1)
and hence
\delta({\mathcal{L}})=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{k^{2}}{4}-1)
, where
\delta({\mathcal{L}})
is introduced in Remark
1.5
. On the other hand considering the normal subgroup
D_{p}=\langle r^{2},s\rangle
of index 2, the quotient family
C_{t}/D_{p}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
yields a cover with the monodromy
(2,(\underbrace{1,1,\ldots,1}_{\geq l-k},0\ldots,0))
. As before, this family has an eigenspace
{\mathcal{M}}
with
\delta({\mathcal{M}})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{(l-k)^{2}}{4}-1)
as in Remark
1.5
. Using the decomposition
2.1.1
and the above formula, we see that our in the decomposition of the adjoint group of our original family factors
{\mathcal{L}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
occure and therefore the dimension of the smallest Shimura subvariety containing this family is at least
\delta({\mathcal{L}})+\delta({\mathcal{M}})
. Therefore if we have

(2.2.1)

\delta({\mathcal{L}})+\delta({\mathcal{M}})\geq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{k^{2}}{4}-1)+\frac{1}{2}(\frac{(l-k)^{2}}{4}-1)>l-3,

the family is not special. Now
2.2.1
, is equivalent to
k^{2}+(l-k)^{2}>8(l-2)
. This in turn yields the inequality
l^{2}-2(k+4)l+2(k^{2}+8)>0
. It is easy to verify that by our assumptions and conditions the above inequality holds true.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

From the (1-dimensional) families of
D_{2p}
- covers of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
in
[
7
]
which give rise to Shimura subvariesties, none have the property that the ramifications in
\langle r\rangle
are all of the form
r^{u}
with

odd. Therefore in view of Proposition

one expects that such Shimura families should not exist.

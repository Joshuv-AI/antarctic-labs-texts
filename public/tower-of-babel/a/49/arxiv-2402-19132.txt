Weighted least ℓ p \ell_{p} approximation on compact Riemannian manifolds
Jiansong Li, Yun Ling, Jiaxin Geng, Heping Wang

Abstract
. Given a sequence of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities in L 2 L_{2} on a compact space, Gröchenig in [ 16 ] discussed weighted least squares approximation and least squares quadrature. Inspired by this work, for all 1 ≤ p ≤ ∞ 1\leq p\leq\infty , we develop weighted least ℓ p \ell_{p} approximation induced by a sequence of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities in L p L_{p} on a compact smooth Riemannian manifold 𝕄 \mathbb{M} with normalized Riemannian measure (typical examples are the torus and the sphere). In this paper we derive corresponding approximation theorems with the error measured in L q , 1 ≤ q ≤ ∞ L_{q},\,1\leq q\leq\infty , and least quadrature errors for both Sobolev spaces H p r ​ ( 𝕄 ) , r > d / p H_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),\,r>d/p generated by eigenfunctions associated with the Laplace-Beltrami operator and Besov spaces B p , τ r ​ ( 𝕄 ) , 0 < τ ≤ ∞ , r > d / p B_{p,\tau}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),\,0<\tau\leq\infty,r>d/p defined by best polynomial approximation. Finally, we discuss the optimality of the obtained results by giving sharp estimates of sampling numbers and optimal quadrature errors for the aforementioned spaces.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with constructive polynomial approximation
on a compact Riemannian manifold that uses function values (the
samples) at selected well-distributed points (standard
information). Here the
well-distributed
points indicate that
those points constitute an
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, an
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family on a compact space is a double-indexed set of points
\{{\bf x}_{n,k}\}

with a family of positive numbers
\{\tau_{n,k}\}
such that the
sampled
\ell_{p}
norm of the
n
-th layer

\left(\sum_{k}\tau_{n,k}|Q({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
is
equivalent to the
L_{p}
norm for all “polynomials”
Q
of degree at most

n
with uniform constants.
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund
families on various compact spaces have been considered in large
number of papers and widely used in approximation theory, number
theory, signal processing, etc. The existence of

L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund families has been established in
many compact spaces, such as the torus, the unit sphere, unit
ball, and more general compact manifolds. Additionally, necessary
and sufficient density conditions for
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund
families on these domains have been discussed. See e.g.

[
2
,
7
,
11
,
15
,
33
,
34
,
35
,
41
,
42
,
44
,
49
]
.

Based on
L_{2}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund families on a compact space

M
, Gröchenig in
[
16
]
explored the weighted least squares
approximation operators and least squares quadrature rules.
Remarkably, he derived approximation theorems and least squares
quadrature errors for Sobolev spaces. Specifically, the following
results were obtained.

Theorem

(
[
16
]
)
.

Assume that
{M}
is a compact space with a probability measure
\nu
. Let
L_{n}
be
the weighted least squares approximation operator and
I_{n}
be the
least squares quadrature rule induced by an

L_{2}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family
\mathcal{X}
on
M
with
associated weights
\tau
and global condition number
\kappa
.
Under the hypothesis of Weyl’s law, consider the Sobolev space

H^{r}({M})
generated by eigenfunctions associated to an unbounded
positive operator
A
on
L_{2}({M})
. We have for all
f\in H^{r}(M)

with
r>d/2
,

(1.1)

\|f-L_{n}(f)\|_{L_{2}(M)}\leq c(1+\kappa^{2})^{1/2}n^{-r+d/2}\|f\|_{H^{r}({M})},

and

\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\nu({\bf x})-I_{n}(f)\right|\leq c(1+\kappa^{1/2})n^{-r+d/2}\|f\|_{H^{r}({M})},

where

is independent of
n
,
\kappa
,
f
, or
\mathcal{X}
. See
[
16
]
for details.

Several notable points in
[
16
]
should be mentioned.

•

Firstly, the least squares quadrature rules were derived from Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities in
L_{2}
by means of frame theory,
whereas traditional quadrature rules were often associated with Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund inequalities in
L_{1}
.

•

Secondly, the obtained error for the Sobolev spaces with smoothness index
r
is
\mathcal{O}(n^{-r+d/2})
,
which is almost optimal, and the constants depend only on the global condition number
\kappa
of the
L_{2}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family.

•

Thirdly, the proofs were based on the hypothesis of Weyl’s law, which is related to the critical Sobolev
exponent and leads to explicit and transparent error estimates.

However, the generality of the hypothesis results in suboptimal
error estimates. Additionally, Gröchenig remarks in
[
16
]

that this technique is certainly limited, which works only for

L_{2}
since it utilizes orthogonal projections, and may require
different techniques for general
p
-norms.

On the unit sphere
{S}^{d}
in
{R}^{d+1}
, a special case of
compact Riemannian manifolds, Lu and Wang
[

used the
orthogonality of projections and the regularity of discrete

approximation and least squares quadrature, which can be stated
as follows. However the technique in
[

works only for

(
[

Let

The purpose of this paper is to extend these results to general

p
-norm on compact Riemannian manifolds
{M}
. Our
contributions can be summarized in five aspects.

•

Firstly, instead of relying on the orthogonality of projections, we utilize the minimality of the weighted least
\ell_{p}

approximation operators along with the regularity of discrete measures (i.e. Lemma
2.6

•

Secondly, our error estimates,

approximation operators measured in

and
\mathcal{O}(n^{-r})

.
This holds particularly true for scenarios involving the

•

Thirdly, we improve the dependency on the global condition number

Fourthly, we also obtain sharp estimates of (linear) sampling numbers and optimal quadratures to elaborate the optimality of obtained errors.

•

Finally, we obtain the above results not only for Sobolev spaces

generated by eigenfunctions associated with the Laplace-Beltrami
operator and Besov spaces

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we will provide necessary materials required for
the remainder of the paper. We will begin by providing an overview
of fundamental concepts pertaining to Riemannian manifolds in a

\ell_{p}
approximation and least squares quadrature

2.1.
Compact Riemannian manifolds

Let
{M}
be a compact connected smooth
d
-dimensional
Riemannian manifold without boundary equipped with the normalized
Riemannian measure
\mu
. Typical examples are compact homogeneous
manifolds, which is of the form
G/H
. Here
G
is a compact Lie
group and
H
is its closed subgroup. For example, the
d
-torus

{T}^{d}
,
d=1,2,\dots
; the spheres
{S}^{d}
,

d=1,2,3,\dots
; the real projective spaces
{\rm P}^{d}({R}),d=2,3,\dots
; the complex projective spaces
{\rm P}^{d}({C}),d=4,6,8,\dots
the quaternion projective spaces
{\rm P}^{2d}({H}),d=4,6,8,\dots
; the Cayley elliptic plane
{\rm P}^{16}(Cay)
. See
[
9
,
17
,
20
,
21
]
for details.

Let
{\rm dist}_{{M}}({\bf x},{\bf y})
be the geodesic
distance of points
{\bf x}
and
{\bf y}
on
{M}
, and let

{\rm B}({\bf x},r)
denote the ball of radius
r>0
centred at

{\bf x}\in{M}
. Then
({M},\mu)
is
Ahlfors

d
-regular
which means that there exists a constant

C_{d}>0
depending only on the
dimension
d
such that

C_{d}^{-1}\,r^{d}\leq\mu\left({\rm B}({\bf x},r)\right)\leq C_{d}\,r^{d},\ {\rm for\ all}\ {\bf x}\in{M}\ {\rm and}\ 0<r\leq{\rm diam}({M}),

where
\mu(E)=\int_{E}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x})
for a measurable subset
E
of
{M}
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, let
L_{p}({M})
be the set of all complex
valued measurable function defined on
{M}
endowed with
finite norm

\|f\|_{L_{p}({M})}:=\left(\int_{{M}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\right)^{1/p},

and let
L_{\infty}({M})\equiv C({M})
be the set of all complex valued continuous functions defined on
{M}
endowed with uniform norm

\|f\|_{L_{\infty}({M})}:=\sup\limits_{{\bf x}\in{M}}|f({\bf x})|.

In particular,
L_{2}({M})
is a Hilbert space with inner product

\langle f,g\rangle_{L_{2}({M})}:=\int_{{M}}f({\bf x})\overline{g({\bf x})}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x}),\ {\rm for}\ f,g\in L_{2}({M}),

where
\overline{g}
is the complex conjugate to
g
.

Let
\Delta_{{M}}
be the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
{M}
, which is self-adjoint in
L_{2}({M})
. This operator has a sequence of
eigenvalues
\{-\lambda_{k}^{2}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
arranged by

0=\lambda_{0}\leq\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\dots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\dots\to\infty,

and a complete orthonormal system of eigenfunctions

\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
. Without loss of generality, we choose
each
\phi_{k}
to be real-valued and
\phi_{0}=1
. Then

\{\phi_{k}\}_{k=0}^{\infty}
can form an orthonormal basis for

L_{2}({M})
.
For each
n=0,1,2,\dots
,
with
\mathcal{P}_{n}
we denote the set of “polynomials” of degree
at most
n
on
{M}
by

\mathcal{P}_{n}:={\rm span}\left\{\phi_{k}:\,\lambda_{k}\leq n\right\}.

This space is called the
diffusion polynomial space of
degree
n

on
{M}
, and an element of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
is
called a
diffusion polynomial of degree at most
n
. In the
following, we will refer to diffusion polynomials simply as
polynomials.

For
f\in L_{p}({M})
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the formal Fourier
coefficients of
f
are

\mathaccent 866{f}(k):=\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\phi_{k}({\bf x})}{\rm d}\mu({\bf x}),\ k=0,1,2,\dots.

Then the
Sobolev space

H_{p}^{r}({M})
,
r>0
is defined by

H_{p}^{r}({M}):=\left\{f\in L_{p}({M}):\,(I-\Delta_{{M}})^{r/2}f\in L_{p}({M})\right\},

with Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{H_{p}^{r}({M})}:=\left\|(I-\Delta_{{M}})^{r/2}f\right\|_{L_{p}({M})}=\left\|\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\lambda_{k}^{2})^{r/2}\mathaccent 866{f}(k)\phi_{k}\right\|_{L_{p}({M})},

where
I
is the identity on
L_{p}({M})
. More about the Sobolev
spaces
H_{p}^{r}({M})
may be found in the paper
[
4
,
31
]
.

Remark 2.1
.

In particular, when
p=2
, it reduces to
H^{r}({M})\equiv H_{2}^{r}({M})
with the Sobolev norm

\|f\|_{H^{r}({M})}:=\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(1+\lambda_{k}^{2})^{r}|\mathaccent 866{f}(k)|^{2}\right)^{1/2},

as a special case in
[
16
, Section 3]
.
Under the conditions in this paper,
two-sides Wely’s law
always holds with exponent
d
and constants
C>c>0
, i.e.,

c\,n^{d}\leq\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}\leq n}|\phi_{k}({\bf x})|^{2}\leq C\,n^{d},

which, by integrating with respect to
{\bf x}
, implies that

\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}:=\#\{\phi_{k}:\,\lambda_{k}\leq n\}\asymp n^{d},

and the Sobolev space
H^{r}({M})
is continuously embedded into

C({M})
if and only if
r>d/2
(see e.g.
[
16
, Proposition
4.2]
).

Let

E_{n}(f)_{p}:=\inf_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\|f-g\|_{L_{p}({M})}

denote the
best approximation
of
f
in
L_{p}({M})
by
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.
Given
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
r>0
, and
0<\tau\leq\infty
, we can
define the
Besov space

B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})
to be the
space of all real functions
f
with finite quasi-norm

\|f\|_{B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}:=\|f\|_{L_{p}({M})}+\left(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{\infty}2^{jr\tau}E_{2^{j}}(f)_{p}^{\tau}\right)^{1/\tau}.

There are other equivalent definitions of the Besov spaces that
are equivalent (see e.g.
[
18
,
19
,
43
]
).

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Sobolev space
H_{p}^{r}({M})
is
continuously embedded into
C({M})
if and only if
r>d/p
(see
e.g.
[
1
, Section 2.7]
or
[
4
]
). Meanwhile, the Besov
space
B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})
is also continuously embedded into

C({M})
if
r>d/p
. These embedding relations guarantee that
any function in the above Sobolev and Besov spacea has a
representation by a continuous function. In the sequel, we denote

X_{p}^{r}({M})
to be the Sobolev space
H_{p}^{r}({M})
or the
Besov space

B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})
.

2.2.
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund families

Now we give the precise definition of Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund families in
L_{p}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}=\{{\bf x}_{n,k}:\,k=1,2,\dots,N_{n},\,n=1,2,\dots\}

be a doubly-indexed set of points in
{M}
, and

\tau=\{\tau_{n,k}:\,k=1,2,\dots,N_{n},\,n=1,2,\dots\}
be a family of positive weights. Then

(i) for
1\leq p<\infty
,
\mathcal{X}
is
called an
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family on
{M}
, denoted by
L_{p}
-MZ, if there exist constants
A,\,B>0
independent
of
n
such that

(2.1)

A\|Q\|_{L_{p}({M})}^{p}\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}|Q({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{p}\leq B\|Q\|_{L_{p}({M})}^{p},\ {\rm for\ all}\ Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}.

(ii) for
p=\infty
,
\mathcal{X}
is called an

L_{\infty}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family on
{M}
, denoted by

L_{\infty}
-MZ, if there exist constants
A>0
independent of
n

such that

(2.2)

A\|Q\|_{L_{\infty}({M})}\leq\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{n}}|Q({\bf x}_{n,k})|\leq\|Q\|_{L_{\infty}({M})},\ {\rm for\ all}\ Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}.

The ratios
\kappa=B/A
for
1\leq p<\infty
and
\kappa=1/A
if

p=\infty
are the global condition numbers of the

L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family, and
\mathcal{X}_{n}:=\{{\bf x}_{n,k}:\,k=1,2,\dots,N_{n}\}
is the
n
-th layer of

\mathcal{X}
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be an
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family with associated
weights
\tau
on
{M}
. We set for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\mu_{n}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}\delta_{{\bf x}_{n,k}},

where
\delta_{\bf z}(g)=g({\bf z})
for a function
g
is the evaluation operator.
For any
f\in C({M})
, we define for
1\leq p<\infty
,

\|f\|_{(p)}:=\left(\int_{{M}}|f({\bf x})|^{p}{\rm d}\mu_{n}\right)^{1/p}=\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}|f({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{p}\right)^{1/p},

and for
p=\infty
,

\|f\|_{(\infty)}:=\max\limits_{1\leq k\leq N_{n}}|f({\bf x}_{n,k})|.

Hence, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

(\mathcal{P}_{n},\|\cdot\|_{(p)})
is a Banach space. Using the
inequalities (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we can conclude that
the
L_{p}
norm of a polynomial of degree at most
n
on
{M}

is comparable to its discrete version, which is given by the
weighted
\ell_{p}
norm of its restriction to
\mathcal{X}_{n}
.
Moreover, if
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
Q({\bf x}_{n,k})=0

for
k=1,\dots,N_{n}
, then
Q=0
, which means that
\mathcal{X}_{n}
contains more than
\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}
points.
Therefore,
\mathcal{X}_{n}
is not an interpolating set for
\mathcal{P}_{n}
.

Filbir and Mhaskar in
[
15
]
established the existence of

L_{p}
-MZ families on compact smooth connected Riemanian manifold

\mathbb{M}
.
Before we proceed to state this result, we introduce the concept of a maximal separated subset on
\mathbb{M}
.

Let
\Xi
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{M}
. We say that
\Xi

is
\varepsilon
-separated
if the minimum distance between
any two distinct points
\boldsymbol{\xi}
and
\boldsymbol{\eta}

in
\Xi
is at least
\varepsilon
. In other words, we require

q(\Xi):=\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Xi\\
\boldsymbol{\xi}\neq\boldsymbol{\eta}\end{subarray}}{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{M}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta})\geq\varepsilon.

Furthermore, if an
\varepsilon
-separated subset
\Xi
of

\mathbb{M}
satisfies

\delta(\Xi):=\max\limits_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\mathbb{M}}{\rm dist}_{\mathbb{M}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\Xi)\leq\varepsilon,

then we
refer to it as a
maximal
\varepsilon
-separated
subset of

\mathbb{M}
, where
{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\cdot,\Xi):\,{M}\to[0,\infty)
is the distance function of
\Xi
with respect to

{\rm dist}_{{M}}
defined by

{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\Xi):=\inf_{\boldsymbol{\eta}\in\Xi}{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\boldsymbol{\eta}).

In simpler terms, a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated subset
\Xi
is
an
\varepsilon
-separated subset of
\mathbb{M}
such that every point in
\mathbb{M}

lies within a distance of
\varepsilon
from at least one point in
\Xi
.
We can express this as

\mathbb{M}=\bigcup\limits_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi}{\rm B}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varepsilon).

It is worth noting that if
\Xi
is a maximal
\varepsilon
-separated subset of
\mathbb{M}
,
then there exists a constant
C_{d}>0
that solely depends on the dimension
d
,
such that for all
{\bf x}\in\mathbb{M}
, we have

(2.3)

1\leq\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in\Xi}\chi_{{\rm B}(\boldsymbol{\xi},\varepsilon)}({\bf x})\leq C_{d}.

We can conclude from Ahlfors
d
-regularity that
\#\Xi\asymp\varepsilon^{-d}
,
which essentially means that the cardinality of
\Xi
is proportional to the reciprocal of the
\varepsilon
raised to the power of the dimension
d
.

Filbir and Mhaskar gave the existence of

L_{p}
-MZ families on
\mathbb{M}
in
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
). For
our purpose we restated it as follows.

Lemma 2.3
.

(
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
).
Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Then there exists a constant
\delta_{0}>0
depending only on

and

given a maximal
\delta/n
-separated subset

of

with

2.3.
Weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation and least squares
quadrature.

For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, let
\mathcal{X}
be an
L_{p}
-MZ family with associated weights
\tau
. We want to
approximate
f
using only these sampling nodes. In line with the method of Gröchenig in
[
16
]
,
this can be done by solving the weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation
problem with samples taken from the
n
-th layer
\mathcal{X}_{n}

of
\mathcal{X}
. Therefore, we can define
the

weighted least
\ell_{p}
approximation
of
f\in C({{M}})

by

L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f):=\arg\min\limits_{g\in\mathcal{P}_{n}}\,\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}|f({\bf x}_{n,k})-g({\bf x}_{n,k})|^{p}\right)^{1/p}.

Obviously, the solution of this problem exists for all
f\in C({M})
. Further, if
1<p<\infty
, then the solution is
unique. If
p=1
or
\infty
, then the solution may be not unique
and we can choose any solution to be
L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f)
. It
can be seen that
L_{n,p}^{{M}}
is nonlinear except for the
case
p=2
.
That is, for
p\neq 2
, there exist

f_{1},f_{2}\in C({{M}})
such that

L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f_{1}+f_{2})\neq L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f_{1})+L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f_{2}).

However,
for
f\in C({{M}})
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
, by the definition of
L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f)
we have

\displaystyle\|f-L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f)\|_{(p)}

\displaystyle=\|(f-Q)-L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f-Q)\|_{(p)}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\leq\|f-Q\|_{(p)}.

This is a critical property. Besides, we have

(2.5)

L_{n,p}^{{M}}(Q)=Q,\ {\rm for\ all}\ Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}.

Now we turn to consider the most interesting case
p=2
in which

L_{n}^{{M}}\equiv L_{n,2}^{{M}}
has many good properties
(see
[
32
]
). Indeed,
L_{n}^{{M}}
is a bounded linear
operator on
C({{M}})
satisfying that
(L_{n}^{{M}})^{2}=L_{n}^{{M}}
, and the range of
L_{n}^{{M}}
is

\mathcal{P}_{n}
. This means that
L_{n}^{{M}}
is a projection
on
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. If we define the
weighted discretized
inner product
on
C({{M}})
by

\langle f,g\rangle_{(2)}:=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}f({\bf x}_{n,k})g({\bf x}_{n,k}),

then
L_{n}^{{M}}
is just the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{P}_{n}

with respect to the discretized inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(2)}
. Let
\varphi_{k},k=1,\dots,\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}
be an orthonormal basis for
\mathcal{P}_{n}

with respect to the weighted discrete scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(2)}
.
Such
\varphi_{k},k=1,\dots,{\rm dim}\mathcal{P}_{n}
can be obtained by applying Gram-Schmidt orthogonalization
process to the basis
\{\phi_{k}:k=0,\dots,\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}-1\}
of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
. We set

D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y}):=\sum_{k=1}^{\dim\mathcal{P}_{n}}\varphi_{k}({\bf x})\varphi_{k}({\bf y}).

Then
D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})
is the reproducing kernel of
\mathcal{P}_{n}

with respect to the weighted discretized inner product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{(2)}
satisfying the following properties:

(i) For any
{\bf x},{\bf y}\in{M}
,
D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})=D_{n}({\bf y},{\bf x})
;

(ii) For any fixed
{\bf y}\in{M}
,
D_{n}(\cdot,{\bf y})\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
;

(iii) For any
Q\in\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
{\bf x}\in{M}
,

Q({\bf x})=\langle Q,D_{n}({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{(2)}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}Q({\bf x}_{n,k})D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{n,k}).

Therefore, for
f\in C({{M}})
,

L_{n}^{{M}}(f)({\bf x})=\langle f,D_{n}({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{(2)}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{n}}\tau_{n,k}f({\bf x}_{n,k})D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{n,k}).

We call
L_{n}^{{M}}(f)
the
weighted least squares polynomial
and

L_{n}^{{M}}
the
weighted least squares operator
.

We also want to approximate the integral

\int_{{M}}f{\rm d}\mu
using only finite samples. To this end, Gröchenig in
[
16
]
used the frame theory
to construct
least squares quadrature rules

I_{n}^{{M}}(f)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{n}}w_{n,k}f({\bf x}_{n,k})

induced by an
L_{2}
-MZ
family. Lu and Wang in
[
32
]
proved that

w_{n,k}=\tau_{n,k}\int_{{M}}D_{n}({\bf x},{\bf x}_{n,k}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x}),\ \ \ I_{n}^{{M}}(f)=\int_{{M}}L_{n}^{{M}}(f)({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x}),

and

(2.6)

\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})-I_{n}^{{M}}(f)\right|\leq\left\|f-L_{n}^{{M}}(f)\right\|_{L_{2}({M})}.

Proposition 2.4

(

Let

Now we recall some knowledge about filtered polynomial
approximation on

. In the sequel, we always assume that

For any positive integer

K_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})\equiv K_{n,\eta}({\bf x},{\bf y})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\eta\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{n}\right)\phi_{k}({\bf x}){\phi_{k}({\bf y})}.

Then,
K_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})
satisfies the following properties (see e.g.
[

[
14

K_{n}({\bf x},{\bf y})=K_{n}({\bf y},{\bf x})

(ii) for any fixed

(iii) for any

(iv) for any

For

V_{n}(f)({\bf x}):=\langle f,K_{n}({\bf x},\cdot)\rangle_{L_{2}({M})}=\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}\leq 2n}\eta\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}}{n}\right)\mathaccent 866{f}(k)\phi_{k}({\bf x}),

Then,

]

(i) for all

(ii) for all

(iii) for all

It follows from

measured
in the

norm. We extend this estimate to more general

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and

. Then for

For any

, we define

holds in the uniform norm. Meanwhile, (

\displaystyle\leq\|f-V_{2^{j-1}}(f)\|_{L_{p}({M})}+\|f-V_{2^{j-2}}(f)\|_{L_{p}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim 2^{-jr}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}.

\displaystyle\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{jd\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{p}({M})}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(2^{-j}\right)^{r-d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}.

Given a positive integer
n
, choose a integer

such that

We recall that for all

Thus, since

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\left\{\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}+\|V_{n}(\sigma_{j}(f))\|_{L_{q}({M})}\right\}.

Then, by (

\displaystyle\|f-V_{n}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{j=m+1}^{\infty}\left(2^{-j}\right)^{r-d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}

\displaystyle\asymp n^{r-d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})},

which gives the estimates (

\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{(\infty)}\leq\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}({M})}\lesssim(2^{j})^{d/p}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{L_{p}({M})}\lesssim(2^{j})^{-r+d/p}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})},

and

\|L_{n,\infty}^{{M}}(f)-V_{2^{s-1}}(f)\|_{(\infty)}\lesssim\sum\limits_{j=s+1}^{\infty}\|\sigma_{j}(f)\|_{(\infty)}.

Together with (
2.2
) and (
3.9
), this will lead to

\displaystyle{\rm II}\,

\displaystyle\leq\|L_{n,\infty}^{{M}}(f)-V_{2^{s-1}}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}({M})}

\displaystyle\leq\kappa\,\|L_{n,\infty}^{{M}}(f)-V_{2^{s-1}}(f)\|_{(\infty)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa\,\sum\limits_{j=s+1}^{\infty}(2^{j})^{-r+d/p}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}

(3.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim\kappa\,n^{-r+d/p}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}.

Therefore, from (
3
), (
3
), and (
3
), we get

\displaystyle\|f-L_{n,\infty}^{{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-V_{2^{s-1}}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})}+\|L_{n,\infty}^{{M}}(f)-V_{2^{s-1}}(f)\|_{L_{\infty}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-r+d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}+\kappa n^{-r+d/p}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+\kappa)n^{-r+d/p}\|f\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})},

which proves (
3.2
).

This proof of Lemma
3.1
is now finished.
∎

4.
Optimality

This section is devoted to elaborating the optimality of the
obtained results in the previous sections by giving sharp
estimates of sampling numbers and optimal quadrature errors.

4.1.
Optimal recovery and quadrature

First we recall some basic concepts concerning with optimal
recovery and quadrature.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>d/p
. We denote
by
BX_{p}^{r}({M})
the unit ball of
X_{p}^{r}({M})
. Consider the
problem of recovering a function
f
from
BX_{p}^{r}({M})

measured in
L_{q}({M})
using only finitely many function
values. The minimal worst case error that can be achieved by a
given finite set of points
P\subset{M}
with the cardinality

\#P
is defined by

e(P;\,BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M})):=\inf_{A_{P}}\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}({M})}\|f-A_{P}(f)\|_{L_{q}({M})},

where the infimum is taking over all sampling operators of the form

A_{P}:BX_{p}^{r}({M})\to L_{q}({M}),\ \ \ A_{P}(f):=\varphi\left(f|_{P}\right),

and
\varphi
is an arbitrary mapping from
{R}^{\#P}
to

L_{q}({M})
. Note that

f|_{P}=\left(f(\boldsymbol{\xi})\right)_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in P}
is the
restriction of
f
to the point set
P
. Sometimes it may be
preferable to all only linear mappings

A_{P}^{\rm lin}(f)=\varphi^{\rm lin}(f|_{P})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in P}f(\boldsymbol{\xi})\,a_{\boldsymbol{\xi}},\ \ a_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in L_{q}({M}),

in which we write
e^{\rm lin}
instead of
e
. Furthermore, if
we impose a constraint on the cardinality of sampling points
P
,
allowing at most
N
points, we can define the
N
-th
sampling number (or the optimal recovery)
of
BX_{p}^{r}({M})

measured in the
L_{q}({M})
norm by

g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\ :=\inf_{\#P\leq N}e(P;\,BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M})).

The
N
-th linear sampling number

g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))
can be defined similarly.

Consider the problem of approximating integration of a function

f
from
BX_{p}^{r}({M})
. The minimal worst case error of

BX_{p}^{r}({M})
that can be achieved by a given finite set of
points
P\subset{M}
is denoted by

e(P;\,BX_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT}):=\inf_{A_{P}}\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}({M})}\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})-A_{P}(f)\right|,

where the infimum ranges over all sampling operators of the form

A_{P}:BX_{p}^{r}({M})\to{R},\ \ \ A_{P}(f):=\varphi\left(f|_{P}\right).

The
N
-th optimal quadrature error
of
BX_{p}^{r}({M})
are defined by

e_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT}):=\inf_{\#P\leq N}e(P;\,BX_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT}),

where the infimum is taken over all sampling points
P\subset{M}
with at most
N
points. Note that
BX_{p}^{r}({M})
is convex and balanced,
the infimum will not change when we only minimize over all linear
mappings
\varphi^{\rm lin}
. Therefore, it is no restriction to
assume that
A_{P}
is of the form

A_{P}^{\rm lin}(f)=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\xi}\in P}\lambda_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}f({\boldsymbol{\xi}}),\ \lambda_{\boldsymbol{\xi}}\in{R}.

This is a classical result by Smolyak and Bakhvalov (see, e.g.
[
40
, Theorem 4.7]
).

It is well known (see e.g
[
48
]
) that

(4.1)

g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\geq g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M})),

and

(4.2)

\displaystyle g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N}\in{M}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in BX_{p}^{r}({M})\\
f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N})=0\end{subarray}}\|f\|_{L_{q}({M})}.

Meanwhile, it is evident (see e.g
[
48
]
) that

(4.3)

e_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N}\in{M}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in BX_{p}^{r}({M})\\
f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N})=0\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\right|.

The study of optimal recovery and quadratures for
Sobolev and Besov spaces is of significant importance and
relevance in both theoretical and practical contexts. In recent
years, extensive research has been conducted on these topics, for
example, for the case of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
, see

[
10
,
13
,
45
,
46
,
47
]
; for the case of the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{d}
,
see
[
5
,
6
,
8
,
12
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
28
,
50
,
51
,
52
]
; for
other domains, see
[
3
,
4
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
.

An outstanding result in this field for the case of the compact
Riemannian manifold
{M}
is the work by D. Krieg and M.
Sonnleitner
[
31
]
, which characterized optimal recovery and
quadrature for the Sobolev spaces using general sampling point set

P
by the distance function from
P
. More specifically, their
results can be formulated as follows: Let
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
r>d/p
. Then for any nonempty and finite set
P

of points in
{M}
, we have

(4.4)

e(P;\,BH_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp e^{\rm lin}(P;\,BH_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp\left\|{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\cdot,P)\right\|_{L_{\gamma}({M})}^{r-d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}},

and

(4.5)

e(P;\,BH_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})\asymp\left\|{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\cdot,P)\right\|_{L_{rp^{*}}({M})}^{r},

where the implied constants are independent of
P
,
1/p+1/p^{*}=1
, and

\gamma:=\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &r(1/q-1/p)^{-1},&&q<p,\\
&\infty,&&q\geq p.\end{aligned}

Meanwhile, it follows from the Ahlfors-regularity that for all
0<\gamma\leq\infty
and
\alpha>0
,

\inf_{\#P\leq N}\left\|{\rm dist}_{{M}}(\cdot,P)\right\|_{L_{\gamma}({M})}^{\alpha}\asymp N^{-\alpha/d},

where the infimum is taken over all point sets of cardinality at
most
N
. Together with (
4.4
) and (
4.5
), it deduces
that for
1<p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
, and
r>d/p
,

(4.6)

g_{N}(BH_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BH_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}},

and

(4.7)

e_{N}(BH_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})\asymp N^{-r/d}.

Indeed, (
4.7
) still holds for
p\in\{1,\infty\}
(see
[
4
]
). However,
the validity of (
4.6
) for
p\in\{1,\infty\}
remains open.

In this section, we will give sharp estimates of sampling numbers
for the Sobolev classes
BH_{p}^{r}({M})
. We also obtain relevant
results for the Besov classes
BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
. Then

(i) for
r>d/p
, we have

g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}};

(ii) if
2
is between
p
and
q
, and if
r>d\max\{1/p,1/2\}
,
then we have

g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right)_{+}}.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>d/p
. Then

e_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})\asymp N^{-r/d}.

4.2.
Proofs of Theorems
4.1
and
4.2

This subsection is dedicated to proving the sharp estimates of
sampling numbers and optimal quadratures, which have been
partially proven in previous studies. However, in order to ensure
a comprehensive understanding, we will provide a complete proof
for both the upper and lower estimates in different and straight
way. The proof of the upper estimates is based on Lemma 3.1.
The proof of the lower estimates relies on the well-known technique of fooling functions. Namely,
we provide a function belonging to
BX_{p}^{r}({M})
that vanishes on the given points set
X_{N}
as constructed
below.

Construction of bump functions:

Let
\Xi
be a maximal
\varepsilon/n
-separated subset with the
cardinality
2C_{d}N\leq\#\Xi\asymp N
in
{M}
, where
C_{d}>0
is
the constant given in (
2.3
), and
\varepsilon
is a sufficiently
small positive number. Then
N\asymp n^{d}
. For an arbitrary finite
set of points
X_{N}:=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}:\,j=1,\dots,N\}
in
{M}
, we set

G:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j}:\,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}\notin{\rm B}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j},\varepsilon/n\right),\ {\rm for\ all}\ i=1,\dots,N\right\}.

By (
2.3
) we have

\#G\geq 2C_{d}N-C_{d}N\geq C_{d}N\geq N.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{1},\boldsymbol{\eta}_{2},\dots,\boldsymbol{\eta}_{N}\in G
.
Then for each
j=1,\dots,N
, we can construct a bump function

\psi_{j}({\bf x})
supported on
{\rm B}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{j},\varepsilon/n\right)
with

\left\|(-\Delta_{{M}})^{k}\psi_{j}\right\|_{L_{p}({M})}\leq CN^{2k/d},\ \ k=0,1,\dots,

and

\int_{{M}}\psi_{j}({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})=N^{-1}.

Thus, the bump functions
\psi_{j},j=1,\dots,N
have disjoint
supports and vanish on
X_{N}
.

The construction of such functions in
{R}^{d}
can be done by
translating and dilating any fixed nonnegative
C^{\infty}
smooth
function with compact support and integral
1
. Then one can
transport and normalize these functions to the local charts of the
manifold and, by compactness, the constant of
C
can be chosen
independent of
j
and
N
. See e.g.
[
50
, Lemma 2]
for the
case of
{S}^{d}
.

Also, it can be prove that for all
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
and
r>0
,

(4.8)

\left\|\psi_{j}\right\|_{H_{p}^{r}({M})}\lesssim N^{r/d-1/p},\ \ \|\psi_{j}\|_{L_{q}({M})}\asymp N^{-1/q}.

See e.g.
[
3
]
for details.

Moreover, we claim that for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>0
,

(4.9)

\left\|\psi_{j}\right\|_{B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}\lesssim N^{r/d-1/p}.

To prove this claim, we note that
E_{2^{j}}(\psi_{j})_{p}\leq\|\psi_{j}\|_{L_{p}({M})}
for any
j\geq 0
. Then by (
4.8
) we
obtain

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N^{1/d}}\Big(2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(\psi_{j})_{p}\Big)^{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{j}\|_{L_{p}({M})}^{\tau}\sum\limits_{2^{j}<N^{1/d}}2^{jr\tau}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{r\tau/d-\tau/p}.

By (
2.9
), we can choose a positive number
\upsilon>r
such
that for any
j\geq 0
,

E_{2^{j}}(\psi_{j})_{p}\lesssim 2^{-j\upsilon}\|\psi_{j}\|_{H_{p}^{\upsilon}({M})},

which, by (
4.8
), leads to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq N^{1/d}}\Big(2^{jr}E_{2^{j}}(\psi_{j})_{p}\Big)^{\tau}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi_{j}\|_{H_{p}^{\upsilon}({M})}^{\tau}\sum\limits_{2^{j}\geq N^{1/d}}2^{j(r-\upsilon)\tau}

\displaystyle\asymp N^{(r-\upsilon)\tau/d}\|\psi_{j}\|_{H_{p}^{\upsilon}({M})}^{\tau}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\lesssim N^{r\tau/d-\tau/p}.

Combing (
4.2
) with (
4.2
), we get (
4.9
).

Define
\psi({\bf x})=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\psi_{j}({\bf x})
. Then

\psi|_{X_{N}}=0
. Furthermore, since
(I-\Delta_{{M}})^{r/2}\psi_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,N
have disjoint supports, we can
immediately obtain

(4.12)

\|\psi\|_{H_{p}^{r}({M})}\lesssim N^{r/d},\ \ \ \int_{{M}}\psi({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})=1,\ \ \ \|\psi\|_{L_{p}({M})}\asymp 1.

Meanwhile, by the same methods, we can prove that

(4.13)

\|\psi\|_{B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}\lesssim N^{r/d}.

We keep the above notations in the following.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Lower estimates:
It follows from (
4.2
) that

g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}({M}),L_{q}({M}))\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N}\in{M}\end{subarray}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in BX_{p}^{r}({M})\\
f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{1})=\dots=f(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{N})=0\end{subarray}}\|f\|_{L_{q}({M})}.

Therefore, for arbitrary
N
points
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j},\,j=1,\dots,N
in
{M}
, it suffices to show that there exist functions
f_{0}
with

f_{0}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j})=0,\ \ j=1,\dots,N,

such that

\|f_{0}\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}\lesssim 1\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \|f_{0}\|_{L_{q}({M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}.

The following proof will be divided into two cases.

Case 1:
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.

Let
f_{0}({\bf x})=N^{-r/d}\psi({\bf x})
. Then

f_{0}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j})=0,\ \ j=1,\dots,N
. Meanwhile, by (
4.12
)
and (
4.13
) we have

\|f_{0}\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}\lesssim 1\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \|f_{0}\|_{L_{q}({M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}},

which proves the lower estimates for
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
.

Case 2:
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
.

Let
f_{0}({\bf x})=N^{-r/d+1/p}\psi_{1}({\bf x})
. Then

f_{0}(\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j})=0,\ \ j=1,\dots,N
. Meanwhile, by (
4.8
)
and (
4.9
) we have

\|f_{0}\|_{X_{p}^{r}({M})}\lesssim 1\ \ \ {\rm and}\ \ \ \|f_{0}\|_{L_{q}({M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)},

which proves the lower estimates for
1\leq p<q\leq\infty
.

Upper estimates:
According to Lemma
2.3
,
we know that for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, there exists an

L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family
\mathcal{X}
on
\mathbb{M}

with
N_{n}\asymp N\asymp n^{d}
. Let
L_{n,p}
be the weighted
least
\ell_{p}
approximation operator induced by
\mathcal{X}
.
Then for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
r>d/{p}
,

\displaystyle g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),L_{q}(\mathbb{M}))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\|f-L_{n,p}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-r+d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}.

In particular, assume that 2 is between
p
and
q
, and

r>d\max\{1/p,1/2\}
. Since
L_{n,2}^{{M}}
is linear, Lemma
3.1
gives

\displaystyle g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),L_{q}(\mathbb{M}))

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\|f-L_{n,2}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-r+d\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}.

Together with (
4.1
), we obtain the upper estimate of Theorems
4.1
.

The proof of Theorem
4.1
is completed.
\hfill\Box

Proof of Theorems
4.2
.

It suffices to verify
that for
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
, and
r>d/p
,

e_{N}(BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})\asymp N^{-r/d}.

For this purpose, let
X_{N}:=\{\boldsymbol{\xi}_{j}:\,j=1,\dots,N\}
be
any subset of
{M}
. Based on the construction of bumping
functions in the front, we set
f_{0}({\bf x})=N^{-r/d}\psi({\bf x})
. Then
f_{0}|_{X_{N}}=0
,

\|f_{0}\|_{B_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}\lesssim 1\ \ {\rm and}\ \ \int_{{M}}f_{0}({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\asymp N^{-r/d}.

Therefore, we can derive from (
4.3
) that

\displaystyle e_{N}(BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{X_{N}\subset{M}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})\\
f|_{X_{N}}=0\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\right|

(4.14)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\inf_{X_{N}\subset{M}}\left|\int_{{M}}f_{0}({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})\right|\gtrsim N^{-r/d}.

On the other hand, according to Lemma
2.3
, we know that
for any
1\leq p\leq\infty
, there exists an

L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family
\mathcal{X}
on
\mathbb{M}

with
N_{n}\asymp N\asymp n^{d}
. Let
L_{n,p}
be the weighted
least
\ell_{p}
approximation operator induced by
\mathcal{X}
and
let

A_{X_{N}}(f):=\int_{{M}}L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f)({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x}).

Then by the Hölder inequality and Theorem
1.2
we have

\displaystyle e_{N}(BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M}),{\rm INT})

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}\left|\int_{{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})-A_{X_{N}}(f)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{f\in BB_{p,\tau}^{r}({M})}\|f-L_{n,p}^{{M}}(f)\|_{L_{p}({M})}

\displaystyle\lesssim n^{-r}\asymp N^{-r/d},

which, combining with (
4.14
), completes the proof.

\hfill\Box

4.3.
Optimality

Let us now proceed to discuss the optimality of the weighted
least
\ell_{p}
approximation and the least squares quadrature.
According to Lemma
2.3
, we know that for any
1\leq q\leq\infty
, there exists an
L_{q}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund family
on
\mathbb{M}
with
N_{n}\asymp N\asymp n^{d}
. Based on Theorems

1.1
and
1.2
, we can conclude that for
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
r>d/{p}
,

\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\|f-L_{n,p}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\asymp g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),L_{q}(\mathbb{M})),

and for
1\leq p,q<\infty
,
r>d\max\{1/p,1/q\}
,

\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\|f-L_{n,q}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\asymp g_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),L_{q}(\mathbb{M})).

This implies that
the weighted least
\ell_{p}
(or
\ell_{q}
) approximation operator

L_{n,p}^{\mathbb{M}}
(or
L_{n,q}^{\mathbb{M}}
) serves as an
asymptotically optimal algorithm for optimal recovery of

BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})
measured in the
L_{q}({M})
norm,
subject to certain restrictions on
r
.

Also, assume that 2 is between
p
and
q
, and

r>d\max\{1/p,1/2\}
. It follows from Lemma 3.1 that

\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\|f-L_{n,2}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\|_{L_{q}(\mathbb{M})}\asymp N^{-\frac{r}{d}+\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\right)_{+}}\asymp g_{N}^{\rm lin}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),L_{q}(\mathbb{M})).

This implies that the weighted least squares
approximation operator
L_{n,2}^{\mathbb{M}}
is an asymptotically
optimal linear algorithm for optimal recovery of

BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})
measured in the
L_{q}({M})
norm on
the condition that 2 is between
p
and
q
, and

r>d\max\{1/p,1/2\}
.

For the least squares quadrature rule
I_{n}^{\mathbb{M}}
, it can be deduced from Theorem
4.2

that for
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>d/{2}
,

\sup_{f\in BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})}\left|\int_{\mathbb{M}}f({\bf x}){\rm d}\mu({\bf x})-I_{n}^{\mathbb{M}}(f)\right|\asymp N^{-r/{d}}\asymp e_{N}(BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M}),\rm{INT}).

This indicates that the least squares quadrature rule
I_{n}^{\mathbb{M}}
is asymptotically optimal quadrature
rule for
BX_{p}^{r}(\mathbb{M})
on
the condition that
2\leq p\leq\infty
and
r>d/{2}
.

We finish this section by considering two special cases
of compact Riemannian manifolds.

Example 4.3
.

Let
1\leq p,q\leq\infty
,
0<\tau\leq\infty
,

5.
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we consider the weighted least
\ell_{p}

approximation problem on compact Riemannian manifolds and derive
the approximation theorem for the weighted least
\ell_{p}

approximation for both Sobolev and Besov classes, which are
asymptotically optimal. The proofs are straightforward, although
it is worth noting their limitations.

Firstly, the proofs are specifically designed for the Riemannian
measure on compact Riemannian manifolds. Different techniques may
be required for general probability measures. Secondly, we aspire
to extend our results to general metric spaces where the existence
of
L_{p}
-Marcinkiewicz-Zygmund families has been established by
Filbir and Mhaskar in a series of papers
[
14
,
15
,
36
,
38
]
.

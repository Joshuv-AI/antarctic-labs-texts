A lower bound for the discrepancy in a Sato-Tate type measure
Jishu Das

Abstract
. Let S k ​ ( N ) S_{k}(N) denote the space of cusp forms of even integer weight k k and level N N . We prove an asymptotic for the Petersson trace formula for S k ​ ( N ) S_{k}(N) under an appropriate condition. Using the non-vanishing of a Kloosterman sum involved in the asymptotic, we give a lower bound for discrepancy in the Sato-Tate distribution for levels not divisible by 8 8 . This generalizes a result of Jung and Sardari [ JS20 , Theorem 1.6] for squarefree levels. An analogue of the Sato-Tate distribution was obtained by Omar and Mazhouda [ OM09 , Theorem 3] for the distribution of eigenvalues λ p 2 ​ ( f ) \lambda_{p^{2}}(f) where f f is a Hecke eigenform and p p is a prime number. As an application of the above-mentioned asymptotic, we obtain a sequence of weights k n k_{n} such that discrepancy in the analogue distribution obtained in [ OM09 ] has a lower bound.

1.
Introduction

Let
S_{k}(N)
denote the space of cusp forms of even integer weight
k
and level
N
. For
(n,N)=1,
the
n^{\text{th}}
normalised Hecke operator acting on
S_{k}(N)
is given by

T_{n}(f)(z)=n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}\sum_{ad=n,d>0}\frac{1}{d^{k}}\sum_{b\,(\text{mod}\,d)}f\Big(\frac{az+b}{d}\Big).

Let
\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
be an orthonormal basis of
S_{k}(N)
consisting only of joint eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
T_{n}
. The Fourier expansion of
f
at the cusp
\infty
is given by

f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}(f)n^{\frac{k-1}{2}}e^{2\pi inz}

for
f\in S_{k}(N).
We denote
\lambda_{n}(f)
to be the
n^{\text{th}}
normalised Hecke eigenvalue of
f
, i.e.
T_{n}(f)=\lambda_{n}(f)f
. The Fourier coefficient
a_{n}(f)
and
\lambda_{n}(f)
are related by the condition
a_{n}(f)=a_{1}(f)\lambda_{n}(f).
From the Ramanujan-Deligne bound
[
Del74
]
, we have

|\lambda_{n}(f)|\leq\tau(n),

where
\tau(n)
denotes the divisor function. In particular
|\lambda_{p}(f)|\leq 2
for a prime number
p.

A sequence of real numbers
x_{n}\in[a,b]
is equidistributed with respect to the probability measure
\mu
if for any
[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\subset[a,b],

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{|\{m\leq n\,:\,x_{m}\in[a^{\prime},b^{\prime}]\}|}{n}=\int_{a^{\prime}}^{b^{\prime}}d\mu.

Let

\mu_{p}(x):=\frac{p+1}{\pi}\frac{\big(1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{p^{-1}})^{2}-x^{2}}

and

(1.1)

\mu_{k,N}:=\frac{1}{\text{dim}(S_{k}(N))}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\delta_{\lambda_{p}(f)},

where
\delta_{x}
is the Dirac measure at
x
and
p
is a prime number with
(p,N)=1.

Using the Eichler-Selberg trace formula, for a fixed prime
p
, Serre
[
Ser97
]
proved
\{\lambda_{p}(f)\,:\,(p,N)=1
,
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)\}
is equidistributed in
[-2,2]
with respect to the measure
\mu_{p}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
.
In other words,
\mu_{k,N}
converges weakly to
\mu_{p}
in
[-2,2],
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
\gcd(p,N)=1.

Given two probability measures
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
on a closed interval
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R},
the discrepancy between
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{2}
is given by

D(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}):=\sup\{|\mu_{1}(I)-\mu_{2}(I)|\,:\,I=[a,b]\subset\Omega\}.

The best known upper bound for
D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p})
is

(1.2)

D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p})=O\Big((\log kN)^{-1}\Big),

as proved by Murty and Sinha
[
MS09
]
.

Let
S_{k}(N)^{*}
be the space of primitive cusp forms with even integer weight
k
and level
N
. Let
\tilde{T}_{n}
be the restriction of the Hecke operator
T_{n}
from
S_{k}(N)
to its subspace
S_{k}(N)^{*}.
Let

\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}
be an orthonormal basis of
S_{k}(N)^{*}
consisting only of joint eigenfunctions of the Hecke operators
\tilde{T}_{n}.

We denote
\mu_{k,N}^{*}
to be the corresponding measure associated to
\tilde{T}_{p}
i.e.

\mu_{k,N}^{*}:=\frac{1}{\text{dim}(S_{k}(N)^{*})}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}}\delta_{\lambda_{p}(f)}.

For a fixed prime
p
, it is not very difficult to deduce from Serre’s theorem that

\mu^{*}_{k,N}
converges weakly to
\mu_{p}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
\gcd(p,N)=1.

An upper bound similar to equation (
1.2
) can also be obtained for
D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
(see
[
MS10
]
). In recent work, Sarnak and Zubrilina
[
SZ22
]
give improved uniform estimates for
D(\mu_{2,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
with the help of a new technique involving the use of the Petersson trace formula.

We have been discussing upper bounds for the discrepancies
D(\mu_{k,N}^{*},\mu_{p})
and
D(\mu_{k,N},\mu_{p})
. A natural question that arises in this context is if one can find lower bounds or
\Omega
-type estimates for these discrepancies.
For a fixed squarefree level N, Jung and Sardari
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.1]
give us a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

(1.3)

D(\mu_{k_{n},N}^{*},\mu_{p})\gg\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\log^{2}k_{n}}.

We now shift our focus to the distribution of
\{\lambda_{p^{2}}(f):f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}\}
which has been well investigated.
Let

\mu_{p^{2}}(x)=\frac{p+1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{(\sqrt{p}+\sqrt{p^{-1}})^{2}-(x+1)}\sqrt{\frac{3-x}{x+1}}

for
x\in[-1,3]
.
In 2009, Omar and Mazhouda
[
OM09
, Theorem 1]
showed that
\{\lambda_{p^{2}}(f)\,:\,f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}\}
is equidistributed with respect to the measure
\mu_{p^{2}}(x)
as
k\rightarrow\infty.

Let

\mu^{*}_{k,N,2}=\frac{1}{\text{dim}(S_{k}(N)^{*})}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}}\delta_{\lambda_{p^{2}}(f)}

For
N=1
, Tang and Wang
[
TW16
, Theorem 1]
show the following analogue of the equation (
1.2
)

(1.4)

D(\mu^{*}_{k,1,2},\mu_{p^{2}})=\operatorname{O}\Big((\log k)^{-1}\Big).

Let us consider the harmonic weight given by

\omega_{k,f}:=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}|a_{1}(f)|^{2}

for
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
(or
\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}
). The associated weighted variant of the measure defined by equation (
1.1
) (see also equation (
2.1
)) is given by

(1.5)

\nu_{k,N}:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{k,f}\,\delta_{\lambda_{p}(f)}.

We denote
\nu_{k,N}^{*}
to be the corresponding weighted measure associated to
\tilde{\mathcal{T}}_{p},

i.e.

\nu_{k,N}^{*}:=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)^{*}}\omega_{k,f}\,\delta_{\lambda_{p}(f)}.

Let us consider the Sato-Tate measure defined by

\mu_{\infty}(x):=\frac{1}{\pi}\sqrt{1-\frac{x^{2}}{4}}

for
x\in[-2,2].

One can use the Petersson trace formula to show that both
\nu_{k,N}
and
\nu_{k,N}^{*}
converge weakly to
\mu_{\infty}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
\gcd(p,N)=1.

For fixed squarefree levels, Theorem 1.6 of
[
JS20
]
gives us a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
for which we have the following lower bound

(1.6)

D(\nu_{k_{n},N}^{*},\mu_{\infty})\gg\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\log^{2}k_{n}}.

We obtain the following variant of (
1.6
) in the context of the space
S_{k}(N).

Theorem 1
.

Let
N=2^{a}b
with
a=0,1,2
and
b
odd. There exists an infinite sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

D(\nu_{k_{n},N},\mu_{\infty})\gg_{N}\frac{1}{k_{n}^{\frac{1}{3}}\log^{2}k_{n}}.

One of the main ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1
that enables us to generalize levels to the above form is the non-vanishing aspect of a certain Kloosterman sum mentioned in Theorem
4
.

We now consider the measure

\mu_{\infty,2}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sqrt{\frac{3-x}{1+x}}

for
x\in[-1,3]
and an analogue of the measure
\nu_{k,N}
given by

\nu_{k,N,2}^{:}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{k,f}\,\delta_{\lambda_{p^{2}}(f)}.

As
p\rightarrow\infty,
just as
\mu_{p}(x)\rightarrow\mu_{\infty}(x),
we note that
\mu_{p^{2}}(x)\rightarrow\mu_{\infty,2}(x)
(see
[
OM09
, Theorem 3]
).
Using the Petersson trace formula, it can be shown that
\nu_{k,N,2}
converges weakly to
\mu_{\infty,2}
as
k+N\rightarrow\infty
with
\gcd(p,N)=1
(see
[
OM09
, Theorem 3]
).
In this article, we provide a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
for which a similar lower bound as in equation (
1.6
) for the discrepancy
D(\nu_{k_{n},N,2},\mu_{\infty,2})

holds. More precisely, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let the level
N
be fixed. Further let
N=2^{a}b
with
a=0,1,2
and
b
odd. Then there exists a sequence of weights
k_{n}
with
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

D(\mu_{\infty,2},\nu_{k_{n},N,2})\gg_{N}\frac{1}{(\log k_{n})^{3}(k_{n})^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Again, the non-vanishing aspect of a certain Kloosterman sum is used to arrive at the proof of Theorem
2
.

2.
An asymptotic formula for
\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)

In 1932, Petersson expressed a weighted sum of
\overline{a_{m}(f)}a_{n}(f)
over
f
with
f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)
in terms of a Bessel function of the first kind and a Kloosterman sum (see

[
Pet32
]
).
For Hecke operators, this is the earliest weighted trace formula,
about 22 years earlier than the unweighted trace formula which was proved by Selberg
[
Sel56
]
in 1956. We use the version of Petersson’s trace formula given by
[
ILS00
, Proposition 2.1]
.
Let

\rho_{n}(f)=\Bigg(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}(f)

and

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\overline{\rho_{m}(f)}\rho_{n}(f).

On taking
m=1
in the above, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,N}(1,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\overline{\rho_{1}(f)}\rho_{n}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)\overline{a_{1}(f)}a_{n}(f)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)|a_{1}(f)|^{2}\lambda_{n}(f),

since
a_{n}(f)=a_{1}(f)\lambda_{n}(f)
.
Hence

(2.1)

\Delta_{k,N}(1,n)=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\left(\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\right)|a_{1}(f)|^{2}\lambda_{n}(f).

Equation (
2.1
) serves two purposes.
It justifies the choice of variants
\nu_{k,N}
and
\nu^{*}_{k,N}
of
\mu_{k,N}
and
\mu^{*}_{k,N}
respectively. Further, it indicates how Petersson’s trace formula is indeed a weighted trace formula for Hecke operators. Given integer
m,n
and a natural number
c
, the Kloosterman sum
S(m,n,c)
is defined to be

S(m,n,c)=\sum_{x(\text{mod}\hskip 2.84526ptc),\,\gcd(x,c)=1}e\left(\frac{mx+n\overline{x}(c)}{c}\right)

where
\overline{x}(c)
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
x

modulo
c
and
e(x)=e^{2\pi ix}.

The Bessel functions of the first kind
J_{a}(x)
for
a\geq 0
, can be defined by the following power series representation

J_{a}(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{\Gamma(j+1)\Gamma(a+j+1)}\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{a+2j},

where
\Gamma(x)
denotes the Gamma function evaluated at
x
.
Now we recall Petersson’s trace formula for
S_{k}(N).

Theorem 3

(
[
ILS00
, Proposition 2.1]
)
.

Let
m,n,N
be natural numbers and
k
be an even natural number. We have

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\sum_{N|c,c>0}\frac{S(m,n;c)}{c}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Big).

Lemma 2.1

(
[
JS20
, Section 2.1.1]
)
.

We have the following estimates for the J-Bessel function.

(i) If
a\geq 0
and
0<x\leq 1
, we have

1\leq\frac{J_{a}(ax)}{x^{a}J_{a}(a)}\leq e^{a(1-x)},

(ii)

0<J_{a}(a)\ll\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}
as
a\rightarrow\infty
,

(iii) If
|d|<1
, then

\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}\ll J_{a}(a+da^{\frac{1}{3}})\ll\frac{1}{a^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Remark 1
.

It is worth noting that Lemma
2.1
(iii) is important for finding a lower bound for Theorem
4
.
The geometric origin of Lemma
2.1
(iii) has been discussed in
[
JS20
, Section 5]
.

Now we prove the following asymptotic of Petersson’s trace formula that will be heavily used in future sections.

Theorem 4
.

Let
m,n,N
be natural numbers and
k
be an even natural number. Let
N
be fixed and
m,n
be such that
\left|\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}-(k-1)\right|<(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}},
then

\displaystyle(i)\,\,\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n,N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right).

Furthermore, if
S(m,n,N)\neq 0
, then

\displaystyle(ii)\,\,\big|\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)-\delta(m,n)\big|\gg(k-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Proof.

Let us consider Petersson’s trace formula

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)

\displaystyle=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\sum_{N|c,c>0}\frac{S(m,n,c)}{c}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{c}\Big)

\displaystyle=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n,N)}{N}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\Big)+2\pi i^{k}\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n,bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big).

On using a trivial bound
|S(m,n,bN)|\leq\phi(bN),
we obtain

(2.2)

\left|\sum_{b\geq 2}\frac{S(m,n,bN)}{bN}J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\right|\leq\sum_{b\geq 2}\left|J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\right|.

Now
\left|\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}-(k-1)\right|<(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}
implies

(2.3)

1-\frac{1}{(k-1)^{\frac{2}{3}}}<\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)N}<1+\frac{1}{(k-1)^{\frac{2}{3}}}.

Using Lemma
2.1
(i) we get

\displaystyle\left|J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|J_{k-1}\left((k-1)\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}\right)\right|\leq e^{a(1-x)}x^{a}J_{a}(a)

where
a=k-1
and
x=\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}.

For
k>27,

Equation (
2.3
) and
b\geq 2
yields

\frac{8}{9b}<\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)bN}<\frac{10}{9b}

which consequently implies

\displaystyle e^{a(1-x)}x^{a}

\displaystyle=e^{(k-1)(1-x+\log x)}\leq e^{(k-1)\big(1-\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{10}{9b}\big)}.

Using Lemma
2.1
(ii) and proceeding from equation (
2.2
), we get

\displaystyle\sum_{b\geq 2}\Bigg|J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\Bigg|\ll\frac{e^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\sum_{b\geq 2}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}

(2.4)

=\frac{e^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\Bigg(\sum_{b=2}^{4}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg)+\frac{e^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\Bigg(\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg).

Since

(2.5)

\frac{e^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\Bigg(\sum_{b=2}^{4}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg)=\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right),

we estimate

\Bigg(\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg).

Let
f(x)=e^{-(k-1)\left(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\right)}
, then
f
is decreasing for
x>2.
By integral test,

(2.6)

\Bigg(\sum_{b\geq 5}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9b}+\log\frac{9b}{10}\big)}\Bigg)\leq\int_{4}^{\infty}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\big)}\,dx.

Let
y=\frac{8}{9x}
in the above integral so that
dy=-\frac{8}{9x^{2}}dx
and

e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\big)}=\frac{e^{-(k-1)y}}{(\frac{9x}{10})^{k-3}}\cdot\frac{100}{81x^{2}}=\frac{e^{-(k-1)y}}{(\frac{4}{5y})^{k-3}}\cdot\frac{100}{81x^{2}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\int_{4}^{\infty}e^{-(k-1)\big(\frac{8}{9x}+\log\frac{9x}{10}\big)}\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{25}{18}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{9}}e^{-(k-1)y}\left(\frac{5y}{4}\right)^{k-3}\,dy

\displaystyle\leq\frac{25}{18}\int_{0}^{\frac{2}{9}}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}e^{-(k-1)y}\,dy=\frac{25}{18}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}\Bigg(\frac{e^{\frac{-2(k-1)}{9}}}{-(k-1)}+\frac{1}{(k-1)}\Bigg).

Using the above bound for equation (
2.6
) with keeping equation (
2.2
) ,(
2.4
) and (
2.5
) in mind, we have

\sum_{b\geq 2}\Bigg|J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\Bigg|\ll e^{(k-1)(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5}))}+\frac{e^{(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k-3}\left(\frac{e^{\frac{-2(k-1)}{9}}}{-(k-1)}+\frac{1}{(k-1)}\right).

However,

e^{(k-1)}\Big(\frac{5}{18}\Big)^{k-3}\Bigg(\frac{e^{\frac{-2(k-1)}{9}}}{-(k-1)}+\frac{1}{(k-1)}\Bigg)\leq\frac{2e^{2}}{(k-1)}\Big(\frac{5e}{18}\Big)^{k-3}=\operatorname{o}\left(e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}\right),

which implies

(2.7)

\sum_{b\geq 2}\Bigg|J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{bN}\Big)\Bigg|=\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right).

On considering equation (
2.7
) and equation (
2.2
),

\hskip 5.69054pt\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n,N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right).

In order to show part(ii), let
\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}=(k-1)+d(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}
with
|d|<1
. On applying Lemma
2.1
(iii) we have

J_{k-1}\Big(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\Big)\gg\frac{1}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)-\delta(m,n)=2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n,N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\pi i^{k}\frac{S(m,n,N)}{N}J_{k-1}\left(\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{N}\right)+\operatorname{o}\left((k-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\right)\gg(k-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

∎

Remark 2

(generalising Theorem
4
)
.

Note that since
\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)<-0.03
, we have
e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}=\operatorname{o}\left((k-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\right).
When
S(m,n,N)=0,
we have

\Delta_{k,N}(m,n)=\delta(m,n)+\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{(k-1)\left(1-\frac{4}{9}-\log(\frac{9}{5})\right)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right).

On modifying the proof of Theorem
4
a bit, we can get a slightly better bound of
\operatorname{O}\left(\frac{e^{\left(\frac{1}{2}-\log 2\right)(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right).
On considering the equation (
2.3
), as
k\rightarrow\infty,
we can let
1-\epsilon<\frac{4\pi\sqrt{mn}}{(k-1)N}<1+\epsilon
in such a way that
\epsilon\rightarrow 0.
If we observe the proof carefully and work with this
\epsilon
, the
\operatorname{O}
-term turns out to be

\frac{e^{\left(1-\frac{(1-\epsilon)}{2}+\log\left(\frac{(1+\epsilon)}{2}\right)\right)(k-1)}}{(k-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

The error term in the above theorem has been worked out with
\epsilon=\frac{1}{9}.
However, there is a limit to which we can optimize the big
\operatorname{O}

3.
Non-vanishing of certain classical Kloosterman sums

Recall that the Kloosterman sum
S(m,n,c)
is given by

S(m,n,c)=\sum_{x(\text{mod}\hskip 2.84526ptc),\,\gcd(x,c)=1}e^{2\pi i\left(\frac{mx+n\overline{x}(c)}{c}\right)},

where
m,n
are integers,
c
is a natural number and
\overline{x}(c)
denotes the multiplicative inverse of
x
modulo
c
.
In 1926, Kloosterman sums were introduced as an application to solve the following problem. Given natural numbers
a,b,c,d
and
n
, Kloosterman obtained an asymptotic formula in
[
Klo27
]
for the number of representations of a natural number
n

in the form
ax^{2}+by^{2}+cz^{2}+dt^{2}.
The sums for the special case of
m=0
or
n=0
are called Ramanujan sums. For squarefree
N
, the main term in
[
JS20
, Theorem 1.7]
is

2\pi i^{-k}\frac{\mu(N)}{N}\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-2})J_{k-1}(4\pi\sqrt{mn}).

Note that
\mu(N)=S(1,0,N)
(see equation (3.4) of
[
IK04
]
) which is nonzero if and only if
N
is squarefree. Thus in order to have a Kloosterman sum as the main term, we need to ensure the nonvanishing of that particular Kloosterman sum (see Theorem
4
(ii)).

Throughout this section, we assume
N
to be coprime to prime
p.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{N},

(a,b)=1,
let
\overline{a}(b)
denote the multiplicative inverse of
a
in
(\mathbb{Z}/b\mathbb{Z})^{*}
i.e.
a\overline{a}(b)\equiv 1
(mod
b
).
Now we demonstrate a lemma regarding the multiplicative property of Kloosterman sums. The lemma is particularly useful to show the non-vanishing of a Kloosterman sum by reducing the task to show the nonvanishing for prime powers.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
N=q_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\,\dots\,q_{t}^{\beta_{t}},

where
q_{1},\,\dots\,,q_{t}
are distinct prime numbers,
q_{i}^{\beta_{i}}=l_{i}
and

c_{i}=\frac{N}{\prod_{j=1}^{i}l_{j}}

for
1\leq i\leq t.

Further let
m_{0}=1,

m_{i}=\prod_{j=1}^{i}\overline{l_{j}}({c_{j}})
for
1\leq i\leq t-1.

For a positive integer
n,
we have

(3.1)

S(1,p^{2n},N)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}S(m_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}}),m_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}})p^{2n},l_{i}).

Proof.

The main idea of the proof is to
use the multiplicative of the Kloosterman sum (see equation (1.59) of
[
IK04
]
) repetitively. The property is given by

(3.2)

S(a,b,cd)=S(a\overline{c}(d),b\overline{c}(d),d)S(a\overline{d}(c),b\overline{d}(c),c)

where
(c,d)=1
.
Let us consider the last two terms in the product given by equation (
3.1
) where the index
i
is written in increasing order,i.e.,

\displaystyle S(m_{t-1},m_{t-1}p^{2n},l_{t})S(m_{t-2}\overline{c_{t-1}}({l_{t-1}}),m_{t-2}\overline{c_{t-1}}({l_{t-1}})p^{2n},l_{t-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(m_{t-2}\overline{l_{t-1}}({c_{t-1}}),m_{t-2}\overline{l_{t-1}}({c_{t-1}})p^{2n},l_{t})S(m_{t-2}\overline{c_{t-1}}({l_{t-1}}),m_{t-2}\overline{c_{t-1}}({l_{t-1}})p^{2n},l_{t-1}).

By equation (
3.2
), the above product is equal to

S(m_{t-2},m_{t-2}p^{2n},l_{t-1}l_{t})

since
\overline{c_{t-1}}({l_{t-1}})l_{t}=\overline{l_{t}}({l_{t-1}})l_{t}=1
(mod
l_{t-1}
). In a similar fashion, we note that

\displaystyle S(m_{t-i},m_{t-i}p^{2n},l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t})S(m_{t-i-1}\overline{c_{t-i}}({l_{t-i}}),m_{t-i-1}\overline{c_{t-i}}({l_{t-i}})p^{2n},l_{t-i})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S(m_{t-i-1},m_{t-i-1}p^{2n},l_{t-i}l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t})

for
2\leq i\leq t.

Above equality is valid because
m_{t-i}=m_{t-i-1}\overline{l_{t-i}}({c_{t-i}})
and

\displaystyle\overline{c_{t-i}}({l_{t-i}})l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t}=\overline{\Bigg(\frac{N}{l_{1}\,...\,l_{t-i}}\Bigg)}({l_{t-i}})l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t}}({l_{t-i}})l_{t-i+1}\,...\,l_{t}=1

modulo
l_{t-i}
.
This proves

S(1,p^{2n},N)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}S(m_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}}),m_{i-1}\overline{c_{i}}({l_{i}})p^{2n},l_{i}).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Proof.

Let
1\leq i\leq n-1
be fixed. Using Petersson’s trace formula we get

\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4i+2})=2\pi i^{k}\sum_{N|c,c>0}\frac{S(1,p^{4i+2};c)}{c}J_{k_{n}-1}\left(\frac{4\pi p^{2i+1}}{c}\right).

Note that

\frac{4\pi p^{2i+1}}{(k_{n}-1)N}<\frac{1}{p^{2}}\left(1+(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{2}{3}}\right)

which implies
\frac{4\pi p^{2i+1}}{(k_{n}-1)N}<\frac{1}{4}\cdot\frac{10}{9}=\frac{5}{18}
. Now using Lemma
2.1
we get,

\displaystyle\Big|\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4i+2})\Big|\leq\left|J_{k_{n}-1}\left(\frac{4\pi p^{2i+1}}{N}\right)\right|+\sum_{b=2}^{\infty}\left|J_{k_{n}-1}\left(\frac{4\pi p^{2i+1}}{bN}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{(k_{n}-1)}J_{k_{n}-1}(k_{n}-1)\left(\left(\frac{5}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}+\sum_{b=2}^{\infty}\left(\frac{5}{18b}\right)^{k_{n}-1}\right)

\displaystyle\ll_{N}\left(\frac{5e}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\Bigg[1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)^{(k_{n}-1)}\,dx\Bigg]\ll_{N}\left(\frac{5e}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Thus

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4i+2})\right|\leq\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Big|\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4i+2})\Big|

\displaystyle\ll_{N}n\,\left(\frac{5e}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}\ll_{N}\log k_{n}\left(\frac{5e}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

By Theorem
4
(ii),

\left|\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4n+2})\right|\gg_{N}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

∎

Remark 8
.

Note that in order to prove Lemma
5.2
(ii) we use Theorem
4
(ii). However, we can not use Theorem
4
(i) directly to deduce Lemma
5.2
(i). This is because in the summation

\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{4i+2}),

for instance, the term
\Delta_{k_{n},N}(1,p^{2})
does not satisfy the assumption of Theorem
4
(i) for a fixed
N
and a very large value of
k_{n}
.

Recall that

\nu_{k,N,2}=\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}\omega_{k,f}\,\delta_{\lambda_{p^{2}}(f)}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
N=2^{a}b
with
a=0,1,2
and
b
be an odd number. There exists a sequence
k_{n}\rightarrow\infty
such that

\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{3}(Q_{2n+1})(t)\,d\nu_{k_{n},N,2}(t)\Bigg|\gg_{N}\frac{1}{(k_{n}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

Proof.

Consider

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{3}Q_{2n+1}(t)\,d\nu_{k_{n},N,2}(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}|a_{1}(f)|^{2}Q_{2n+1}(\lambda_{p^{2}}(f))

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}|a_{1}(f)|^{2}Q_{2n+1}(X_{2}(\lambda_{p}(f))

\displaystyle=\int_{-2}^{2}Q_{2n+1}\circ X_{2}(t)\,d\nu_{k_{n},N}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(k-1)}{(4\pi)^{k-1}}\sum_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{k}(N)}|a_{1}(f)|^{2}\lambda_{p^{4n+2}}(f)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\Delta_{k,N}(1,p^{4n+2}).

On taking
k_{n}=\left[\frac{4\pi p^{2n+1}}{N}\right],
the proof is complete by Lemma
5.2
and noting that

\log k_{n}\left(\frac{5e}{18}\right)^{k_{n}-1}=\operatorname{o}_{N}\left(1\right).

∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The proof is similar to that of Theorem
1
. Using equation (
5.2
) and Lemma
5.3
, we have

(5.3)

\int_{-1}^{3}Q_{2n+1}(x)\,d(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})(x)=\int_{-1}^{3}Q_{2n+1}(x)\,d\nu_{k_{n},N,2}(x)\gg_{N}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}.

Integration by parts implies

\displaystyle\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{3}Q_{2n+1}(x)\,d(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})(x)\Bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg|\big(Q_{2n+1}(-1)-Q_{2n+1}(3)\big)(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})([-1,3])\Bigg|+\Bigg|\int_{-1}^{3}Q^{\prime}_{2n+1}(x)\,(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})(x)dx\Bigg|.

By Lemma
5.1
the above expression is

\ll_{\epsilon}n^{3+\epsilon}\Big|(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})([-1,3])\Big|.

Hence

\displaystyle D(\nu_{k_{n},N,2},\mu_{\infty,2})\geq\Big|\nu_{k_{n},N,2}([-1,3])-\mu_{\infty,2}([-1,3])\Big|=\Big|(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})([-1,3])\Big|

\displaystyle\gg\frac{1}{n^{3}}\Bigg|\int_{-2}^{2}Q_{2n+1}(x)\,d(\nu_{k_{n},N,2}-\mu_{\infty,2})(x)\Bigg|\gg_{N}n^{-(3)}(k_{n}-1)^{-\frac{1}{3}}

where the last step follows from equation (
5.3
).
For the sequence
k_{n}=\left[\frac{4\pi p^{2n+1}}{N}\right]
, indeed,

D(\nu_{k_{n},N,2},\mu_{\infty,2})\gg_{N}\frac{1}{(\log k_{n})^{3}(k_{n}-1)^{\frac{1}{3}}}.

∎

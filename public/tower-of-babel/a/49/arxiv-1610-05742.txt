Product measures without utilizing integrals
Hasan Gül, Süleyman Önal

Abstract




Abstract:
We show that product measures can be handled without resorting to integrals.

MSC:
28A35

Keywords:
Analysis; Classical measure theory; Measures and integrals in product spaces

1
Introduction

In many textbooks on Real Analysis or Measure Theory, if not in all, product measures on product spaces are handled by utilizing the monotone and dominated convergence theorems which involve integrals with respect to some measures (cf.
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
). Of course, we do not find this approach to be circular, but feel a certain uneasiness.

The necessary foundation for studying a measure space
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
is the characterization of null sets with respect to
\mu
and the establishment of
\sigma
-additivity of it on the elements of the semiring (
\sigma
-algebra)
\Sigma
.

In this short note, we show that an introduction to product measure and laying the aforementioned foundation without resorting to integrals is possible in a very simple way.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall some basic definitions.

Let
X
be a nonempty set.

Definition 2.1.
A collection
\Sigma
of subsets of
X
is called a
semiring
if it satisfies the following properties:

1.
\emptyset\in\Sigma
,

2. If
A,B\in\Sigma
, then
A\cap B\in\Sigma
,

3. For every
A,B\in\Sigma
, there exist
C_{1},...,C_{n}\in\Sigma
such that
A\setminus B=\cup_{i=1}^{n}C_{i}
and
C_{i}\cap C_{j}=\emptyset
if
i\not=j
.

Definition 2.2.
A nonempty collection
\Sigma
of subsets of
X
which is closed under finite intersections and complementation is called an
algebra
of sets. An algebra
\Sigma
of subsets of
X
is called a
\mathbf{\sigma}
-algebra
if every union of a countable collection of elements of
\Sigma
is again in
\Sigma
.

Definition 2.3.
Let
\Sigma
be a semiring of subsets of
X
. A set function

\mu:\Sigma\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is called a
measure
on
\Sigma
if it satisfies the following properties:

1.
\mu(\emptyset)=0
,

2. If
\{A_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is a disjoint
sequence in
\Sigma
satisfying
\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\Sigma
, then

\mu(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n})=\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})

holds.

Definition 2.4.
A triplet
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
, where
\Sigma
is a semiring of subsets of
X
and
\mu
is a measure on
\Sigma
is called a
measure space
.

Definition 2.5.
Let
(X,\Sigma,\mu)
be a measure space and
A\subseteq X
. If
\mu(A)<\infty
, then
A
is said to be of
finite measure
. If
A=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}A_{n}
, where each
A_{n}
is of finite measure, then
A
is said to be of
\sigma
-finite measure
.

Definition 2.6.
A set function
\mu:P(X)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
defined on the power set
\mathcal{P}(X)
of
X
is called an
outer measure
if it satisfies these properties:

l.
\mu(\emptyset)=0
,

2. If
A\subseteq B
, then
\mu(A)\leq\mu(B)
,

3.
\mu(\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n})\leq\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n})
holds for every sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of subsets of X.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a collection of subsets of
X
containing the empty set. Also, let
\mu:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
be a set function such that
\mu(\emptyset)=0
. For every subset
A
of
X
we define

\mu^{*}(A)=\inf\{\Sigma_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu(A_{n}):(A_{n})\text{ \rm is a sequence of }\mathcal{F}\text{ \rm with}A\subseteq\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\}.

If there is no sequence
(A_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathcal{F}
such that
A\subseteq\cup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}
, then we let
\mu^{*}(A)=\infty
.

The set function
\mu^{*}:P(X)\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is an outer measure (called the
outer measure generated
by the set function
\mu:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow[0,\infty])
satisfying

\mu^{*}(A)\leq\mu(A)

for each
A\in\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 2.7.
Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
and

(Y,\Sigma_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be measurable spaces. The subset

\Sigma_{X}\otimes\Sigma_{Y}:=\{A\times B:A\in\Sigma_{X},B\in\Sigma_{Y}\}

of
X\times Y
is called the
product semiring
. The set function

\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y}:\Sigma_{X}\otimes\Sigma_{Y}\rightarrow[0,\infty]
defined by

\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y}(A\times B)=\mu_{X}(A)\cdot\mu_{Y}(B)\ \ \text{\rm for all }A\times B\in\Sigma_{X}\otimes\Sigma_{Y}.

is a measure on
\Sigma_{X}\otimes\Sigma_{Y}
and is called the
product measure
of
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
and is finitely additive.

3
Product measures

Theorem 1
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
and

(Y,\Sigma_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be measurable spaces where
\Sigma_{X},\;\Sigma_{Y}
are semi-rings (
\sigma-
algebras) and
\mu_{X},\;\mu_{Y}
are
\sigma-
additive measures. Let
D\subseteq X\times Y
and suppose
D\subseteq\cup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}\times C_{n}
where
\{B_{n}\times C_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
are pairwise disjoint sequences with
B_{n}\in\Sigma_{X}
and
C_{n}\in\Sigma_{Y}
. Define
D^{>r}:=\{x\in X:\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})>r\}
for
r\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and
D^{x}:=\{y:(x,y)\in D\}
. Let
\mu_{X}^{*},\;\mu_{Y}^{*}
be outer measures generated by
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
, respectively, and suppose
\mu_{X}^{*}(D^{>r})>s
for some
s\in\mathbb{R}^{+},
Then, there exists a finite subset
F\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that

rs<\underset{n\in F}{\Sigma}\mu_{X}(B_{n})\mu_{Y}(C_{n})

.

Proof.

Let
x\in D^{>r}
and define
N_{x}:=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x\in B_{n}\}
. Then,
D^{x}\subseteq\bigcup_{n\in N_{x}}C_{n}
. Now

r<\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})\leq\Sigma_{n\in N_{x}}\mu_{Y}^{*}(C_{n})\leq\Sigma_{n\in N_{x}}\mu_{Y}(C_{n}).

Let
M_{x}
be a finite subset of
N_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
such that
r<\Sigma_{n\in M_{x}}\mu_{Y}(C_{n})
. Note that
x\in B_{n}
for all
n\in M_{x}
. Thus,
D^{>r}\subseteq\bigcup_{x\in D^{>r}}(\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cap}B_{n})
and the family
\{\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cap}B_{n}:x\in D^{>r}\}\subseteq\Sigma_{X}
is countable since
\{M_{x}:M_{x}\subseteq\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{<\aleph_{o}}(\mathbb{N}).

We have

s<\mu_{X}^{*}(D^{>r})\leq\mu_{X}^{*}(\bigcup_{x\in D^{>r}}(\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cap}B_{n})).

Thus, there exist a finite subset
M
of
D^{>r}
with

s<\mu_{X}^{*}(\underset{x\in M}{\bigcup}(\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cap}B_{n}).

Set
F:=\bigcup_{x\in M}M_{x}
. Since
\underset{x\in M}{\bigcup}(\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cap}B_{n}\times\underset{n\in M_{x}}{\cup}C_{n})\subseteq\underset{n\in F}{\bigcup}(B_{n}\times C_{n})
we have

rs<\underset{n\in F}{\Sigma}\mu_{X}(B_{n})\mu_{Y}(C_{n})
by the finite additivity of
\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y}
on the semiring

\Sigma_{X}\otimes\Sigma_{Y}
.

∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,\Sigma_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be measurable spaces where
\Sigma_{X},\;\Sigma_{Y}
are semi-rings (
\sigma-
algebras) and
\mu_{X}
and
\mu_{Y}
are
\sigma-
additive measures. Then
\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y}
is countably additive on
X\times Y
.

Proof.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,\Sigma_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be measurable spaces where
\Sigma_{X}

,\Sigma_{Y}
are semi-rings (
\sigma-
algebras) and
\mu_{X},\;\mu_{Y}
are
\sigma-
additive measures. Let
D=B\times C=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(B_{n}\times C_{n})
with

B_{n},B\in\Sigma_{X}
and
C_{n},C\in\Sigma_{Y}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
B_{n}\times C_{n}
are pairwise disjoint for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

We may suppose, without loss of generality,
\mu(B)>0
and also,
\mu(C)>0.

Suppose
t<\mu_{X}(B)\cdot\mu_{Y}(C)
. We may find
r,s\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
0<r<\mu_{X}(B)
and
0<s<\mu_{Y}(C)
such
that
t<r\cdot s
. Consider
(B\times C)^{x}
where
x\in B.
Clearly,

\mu^{*}((B\times C)^{x})=\mu(C)
and
D^{>r}=B
. Thus,
r<\mu(C)
and
s<\mu^{*}(D^{>r}).
By the theorem above, we may find a finite subset
F
of
\mathbb{N}
such that

t<\underset{n\in F}{\Sigma}\mu(A_{n})\cdot\mu(B_{n}).

∎

Corollary 2
.

Let
(X,\Sigma_{X},\mu_{X})
and
(Y,\Sigma_{Y},\mu_{Y})
be measurable spaces where
\Sigma_{X},\;\Sigma_{Y}
are semi-rings (
\sigma-
algebras) and
\mu_{X},\;\mu_{Y}
are
\sigma-
additive measures. If
D\subseteq X\times Y

and
(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D)=0
then
\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})=0
for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x\in X
. Conversely, if
D
is

(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}
measurable and
\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})=0
for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x
, then
(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D)=0
, provided

\mu_{X},\;\mu_{Y}
are
\sigma
-finite measures.

Proof.

If
D\subseteq X\times Y

and
(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D)=0
then
\mu^{*}_{X}(D^{>r})=0
for all
r>0
by the theorem. Hence
\mu_{X}^{*}(\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}D^{>\frac{1}{n}})=0
. Thus,
\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})=0
for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x\in X
.

Conversely, let
\mu_{X},\;\mu_{Y}
be
\sigma
-finite measures. Suppose
D
is

(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}
measurable and for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x
,
\mu_{Y}^{*}(D^{x})=0
. Then
(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D)=0
.

By the
\sigma
-additivity of measures, we may assume
\mu_{X}(X),\;\mu_{Y}(Y)<\infty
. Suppose
\mu_{Y}(D^{x})=0
for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x\in X
. Since
D^{c}=X\times Y\setminus D
, we have
\mu_{Y}((D^{c})^{x})=\mu_{Y}(Y)
for
\mu_{X}
-almost all
x\in X
. Thus,

\mu_{X}^{*}(\{x:\mu_{Y}((D^{c})^{x})=\mu_{Y}(Y)\})=\mu_{X}(X)

Hence, by the theorem,
(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D^{c})=\mu_{X}(X)\cdot\mu_{Y}(Y)
. Therefore,

(\mu_{X}\times\mu_{Y})^{*}(D)=0
.

Note that both monotone and dominated convergence theorems themselves may be proved with this approach, which is, using measure theory instead of integration with a slightly different interpretation.

∎

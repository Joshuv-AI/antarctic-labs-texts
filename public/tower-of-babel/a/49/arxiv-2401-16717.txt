Scattering for the dispersion managed nonlinear Schrödinger equation
Mi-Ran Choi, Kiyeon Lee, Young-Ran Lee

Abstract
. We consider the dispersion managed nonlinear Schrd̈inger equations with quintic and cubic nonlinearities in one and two dimensions, respectively. We prove the global well-posedness and scattering in L x 2 L_{x}^{2} for small initial data employing the U p U^{p} and V p V^{p} spaces.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
35Q55,37K60,35Q60

1.
Introduction

We consider the Cauchy problem for the dispersion managed nonlinear Schrödinger equation (NLS) in one and two dimensions

\begin{cases}\displaystyle{i\partial_{t}u+{d_{\mathrm{av}}}\Delta u+\int_{0}^{1}e^{-ir\Delta}(|e^{ir\Delta}u|^{2\sigma}e^{ir\Delta}u)dr=0},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u=u(t,x):\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{C}
,
d=1,2
,
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma>0
, and
e^{ir{\Delta}}
is the linear propagator of the Schrödinger equation.
The equation in (
1.1
) is related to the NLS with a periodically varying coefficient

i\partial_{t}u+d(t)\Delta u+|u|^{2\sigma}u=0.

(1.2)

In the regime of strong dispersion management, the dispersion
d(t)
is a periodic function given by

d(t)={d_{\mathrm{av}}}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}d_{0}\left(\frac{t}{\varepsilon}\right),

where
d_{0}
is its mean zero part over one period,
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}
the average component, and
\varepsilon
a small positive parameter. Through an appropriate change of variables and averaging over one period, the resulting equation takes the form of (
1.1
) in the model case, that is,
d_{0}
is the
2
-periodic function with
d_{0}(t)=1
on
[0,1)
and
d_{0}(t)=-1
on
[1,2)
, see
[
9
,
10
]
. The validity of this averaging process is confirmed in
[
6
,
7
,
24
]
.

Equation (
1.2
) arises in various physical scenarios, such as the propagation of electromagnetic pulses in optical fiber communication, beam propagation in waveguide arrays, and the study of nonlinear matter waves of Bose-Einstein condensates, see
[
1
,
19
,
20
]
. The balance between dispersion and nonlinearity is crucial for the existence of stable pulses. Dispersion management has proven incredibly successful in producing stable soliton-like pulses, see the review
[
21
,
22
]
. Numerous well-known results regarding ground states, which generate solitons of equation (
1.1
), can be found in the literature, refer to
[
4
,
5
,
24
]
for example.

The one-dimensional Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\sigma>0
when
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}<0
; for all
0<\sigma<4
when
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}>0
; for all
0<\sigma<2
in the singular case, i.e., when
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}=0
, in
[
2
,
5
,
24
]
. Recently, the existence of finite time blowup solutions has been shown in
[
8
]
for all
\sigma\geq 4
when
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}>0
.
We expect that analogous results can be shown for the two-dimensional problem, however, as of now, there are no such results.

We are interested in analyzing the long-time behavior of the solution for (
1.1
) when
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}\neq 0
.
In
[
18
]
, the authors established the optimal decay bound and
modified
scattering of the solution for (
1.1
) with small initial data in an appropriate weighted function space for the Kerr nonlinearity,
\sigma=1
, in one dimension. They proved this by adapting the Kato-Pusateri method from
[
14
]
. However, to the best of our knowledge, there are no results available in the literature about the
linear
scattering phenomenon of the solution and this is our main motivation for the present study. Now we present our main result on the global well-posedness and
linear
scattering in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for small initial data.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume
{d_{\mathrm{av}}}\neq 0
and
\sigma=2/d
for
d=1,2
.
Given initial data
u_{0}\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with small enough
L_{x}^{2}
-norm, the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) is globally well-posed in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Moreover, the solution
u(t)
scatters in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is,
there exist
{\varphi}_{\pm}\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\|u(t)-e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it{\Delta}}{\varphi}_{+}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lim_{t\to-\infty}\left\|u(t)-e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it{\Delta}}{\varphi}_{-}\right\|_{L_{x}^{2}}=0.

Remark 1.2
.

The global well-posedness and scattering in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
s>0
, can analogously be shown for small initial data, see Remark
4.2
.

To prove this theorem, as usual, we consider the Duhamel formula

\displaystyle u(t)=e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}i(t-s)\Delta}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-ir\Delta}\left(|e^{ir\Delta}u|^{4/d}e^{ir\Delta}u\right)\!(s)\,drds.

(1.3)

The global well-posedness of (
1.3
) holds for
every
initial data in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
without imposing any restriction on the size of the initial data. This can be shown by applying the contraction mapping argument combined with mass conservation, relying on the estimates in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for the nonlinear part of (
1.3
), see, e.g.,
[
5
, Lemma 2.5]
for the one dimensional case. Such an approach is standard for proving the well-posedness of the classical NLS as well as the dispersion managed NLS.

However, it is not possible to prove the scattering result for the Cauchy problem (
1.1
) via the well-known argument for the classical NLS, based on Strichartz’s estimates.
This limitation arises from the effect of the linear propagator
e^{-ir\Delta}
in nonlinearity, which restricts the choice of the Strichartz admissible pairs for the entire time interval. Consequently, we are unable to use the contraction mapping argument for the suitable space-time norms to obtain the scattering result.
Fortunately, we managed to overcome this difficulty by deriving bilinear estimates in Proposition
3.3
for functions in the adapted
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
spaces when
\sigma=2/d
. The
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
spaces were first introduced by H. Koch with D. Tataru
[
15
,
16
]
and N. Wiener
[
23
]
, respectively. This scattering in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for small initial data, stated in Theorem
1.1
, is the first result on the linear scattering for the dispersion managed NLS. Furthermore, our approach establishes well-posedness and scattering for (
1.1
) with
\sigma=2/d+k
in
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
s=d/2-1/\sigma
.

The plan for the paper is as follows:
In the next section, we introduce function spaces and their properties with the notations which are used in the paper frequently. In Section
3
, we prove bilinear estimates which are main ingredients for proving our main result. We close the paper by giving the proof of Theorem
1.1
via contraction mapping argument in Section
4
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notations

We use the convention
f\lesssim g
or
g\gtrsim f
, if there exists a finite constant
C>0
such that
f\leq Cg
. If
f\lesssim g
and
f\gtrsim g
, we write
f\approx g
.

We denote by
L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, for
1\leq q,r<\infty
and an interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, the space of all functions
u
for which

\|u\|_{L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}=\left(\int_{I}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u(t,x)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}

is finite. If
q=\infty
or
r=\infty
, use the essential supremum instead, respectively.
For notational simplicity, we write
L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}
for
L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
and
L^{q}_{t,x}
or
L_{t,x}^{q}(I\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
L_{t}^{q}(I;L_{x}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we define the dual exponent
1\leq p^{\prime}\leq\infty
by

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1.

The Fourier transform is defined by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\mathcal{F}(f)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}f(x)\,dx

and the space-time Fourier transform is defined by

\mathcal{F}_{t,x}(F)(\tau,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-it\tau}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}F(t,x)\,dxdt.

Let
\rho
be a Littlewood-Paley function, i.e., a smooth cut-off function satisfying
\rho(\xi)=1
for
|\xi|\leq 1
and supported in
|\xi|\leq 2
.
Denote by
2^{\mathbb{Z}}
the set of dyadic numbers
N=2^{j}
where
j\in\mathbb{Z}
.
We define
\rho_{1}(\xi):=\rho(\xi)
and
\rho_{\raisebox{-2.3pt}{$\scriptstyle N$}}(\xi):=\rho(\xi/N)-\rho(2\xi/N)
for a dyadic number
N\geq 2
.
Note that
\sum_{N\geq 1}\rho_{\raisebox{-2.3pt}{$\scriptstyle N$}}(\xi)=1
.
Then the frequency projection
P_{N}
is defined by

\mathcal{F}(P_{N}f)(\xi):=\rho_{\raisebox{-2.3pt}{$\scriptstyle N$}}(\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi).

For any
N_{1},N_{2}\in 2^{\mathbb{Z}}
,
N_{1}\sim N_{2}
means
2^{-10}N_{1}<N_{2}<2^{10}N_{1}
while
N_{1}\ll N_{2}
or
N_{2}\gg N_{1}
means
2^{10}N_{1}\leq N_{2}
. We also denote
\max(N_{1},N_{2})
and
\min(N_{1},N_{2})
by
N_{12}^{\max}
and
N_{12}^{\min}
, respectively.

2.2.
U^{p}-V^{p}
and adapted function spaces

As we mentioned above, we introduce
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
spaces and discuss their properties. These function spaces were introduced by Koch-Tataru
[
15
,
16
]
for NLS.
Later these spaces have been exploited to show the low regularity well-posedness and scattering results of several dispersive equations and related ones, as a useful replacement of
X^{s,b}
-spaces in the limiting cases. We refer to
[
11
,
12
,
17
]
for these properties and explicit definitions for general dispersive equations.

To state the definitions we need, let
\mathcal{P}
be the collection of finite partitions
\{t_{0},\cdots,t_{K}\}
satisfying

-\infty<t_{0}<\cdots<t_{K}\leq\infty.

If
t_{K}=\infty
, by convention,
u(t_{K}):=0
for any
u:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
For
1\leq p<\infty
, let us define a
U^{p}
-atom by a step function
a:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of the form

a(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\phi_{k}\chi_{[t_{k-1},t_{k})}(t)\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{ with }\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|\phi_{k}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{p}=1.

Then the
U^{p}
space is defined by

U^{p}:=\Biggl\{u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}_{j}a_{j}:a_{j}\mbox{ are }U^{p}\mbox{-atoms, }{\lambda}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\mbox{ with }\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\lambda}_{j}|<\infty\Biggr\}

with the equipped norm

\|u\|_{U^{p}}:=\inf\left\{\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}|{\lambda}_{j}|:u=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\lambda}_{j}a_{j},\ a_{j}\mbox{ are }U^{p}\mbox{-atoms, }{\lambda}_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

We next define
V^{p}
as the space of all right-continuous functions
v:\mathbb{R}\to L_{x}^{2}
satisfying
v(t)\to 0
as
t\to-\infty
and the norm

\|v\|_{V^{p}}:=\sup_{\{t_{k}\}\in\mathcal{P}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

is finite.

We gather the properties of the
U^{p}
and
V^{p}
spaces we need, including embeddings, duality, and logarithmic interpolation. We begin with the embedding property which follows from Proposition 2.2 and Corollary 2.6 in
[
11
]
. To obtain this embedding property, the conditions that
v
is right-continuous and that
v(t)\to 0
as
t\to-\infty
in the definition of
V^{p}
space are necessary, see
[
11
]
for more details.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq p<q<\infty
, then

(i)

U^{p}
and
V^{p}
are Banach spaces.

(ii)

The embeddings
U^{p}\hookrightarrow V^{p}\hookrightarrow U^{q}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{2})
are continuous.

Lemma 2.2

(Corollary 4.24. of
[
17
]
)
.

For
1<p<\infty
, if
u\in U^{p}
is absolutely continuous, then

\|u\|_{U^{p}}=\sup\left\{\left|\int\left<u^{\prime}(t),v(t)\right>_{L_{x}^{2}}dt\right|:v\in C_{0}^{\infty},\;\;\|v\|_{V^{p^{\prime}}}=1\right\}.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
1\leq p<\infty
, if
v\in V^{p}
, then the limits of
v(t)
in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
exist as
t\to\pm\infty
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we consider the limit of
v(t)
as
t\to\infty
when
\|v\|_{V^{p}}=1
. Given
0<\varepsilon<1
, there exists a partition
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\in\mathcal{P}
with
t_{K}<\infty
such that

\big(\|v\|_{V^{p}}-\varepsilon\big)^{\frac{1}{p}}<\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\|v\|_{V^{p}}=1

which gives

\|v\|_{V^{p}}<\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}+\varepsilon.

Then for any
t,t^{\prime}
with
t>t^{\prime}>t_{K}
, we have
\{t_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}\cap\{t^{\prime},t\}\in\mathcal{P}
and this implies that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|v(t^{\prime})-v(t_{K})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}+\|v(t)-v(t^{\prime})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|v\|_{V^{p}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\|v(t_{k})-v(t_{k-1})\|^{p}_{L_{x}^{2}}+\varepsilon,

and therefore
\|v(t)-v(t^{\prime})\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p}}
.
∎

Motivated by the Duhamel formula (
1.3
), we define the adapted function spaces
U_{{\Delta}}^{p}
and
V_{{\Delta}}^{p}
for
1\leq p<\infty
by

U_{\Delta}^{p}:=\left\{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u:u\in U^{p}\right\}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;V_{\Delta}^{p}:=\left\{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}v:v\in V^{p}\right\}

with norms

\|u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{p}}:=\|e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u\|_{U^{p}}\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\|v\|_{V_{\Delta}^{p}}:=\|e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}v\|_{V^{p}}.

Then, it follows from Lemma
2.1
that
U_{{\Delta}}^{p}
and
V_{{\Delta}}^{p}
are Banach spaces and that the embedding property holds, i.e.,
U_{{\Delta}}^{p}\hookrightarrow V_{{\Delta}}^{p}\hookrightarrow U_{{\Delta}}^{q}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};L_{x}^{2})
are continuous for all
1\leq p<q<\infty
.
Moreover, it is obvious to see that the
V^{p}
norm is preserved under the unitary operator
e^{ir\Delta}
for any
r\in\mathbb{R}
in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and so is the
V_{\Delta}^{p}
norm, that is,

\left\|e^{ir\Delta}v\right\|_{V_{\Delta}^{p}}=\|v\|_{V_{\Delta}^{p}}

(2.1)

for all
v\in V_{\Delta}^{p}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. The
U_{\Delta}^{p}
norm is, also, preserved under
e^{ir\Delta}
, i.e.,

\|e^{ir\Delta}u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{p}}=\|u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{p}}

(2.2)

for all
u\in{U_{\Delta}^{p}}
and
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Indeed, for any
U^{p}
-atom
a
, it is clear that
e^{ir\Delta}a
is a
U^{p}
-atom since
e^{ir\Delta}
is a unitary operator in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Thus, for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
,
\|e^{ir\Delta}u\|_{U^{p}}=\|u\|_{U^{p}}
and therefore
\|e^{ir\Delta}u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{p}}=\|u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{p}}
.
We close this section by presenting two lemmas adapted from
[
17
, Lemma 4.12]
and
[
11
, Proposition 2.19]
.

Lemma 2.4

(Logarithmic interpolation)
.

Let
p>2
. Then there is a constant
\kappa=\kappa(p)>0
such that for all
v\in{V_{\Delta}^{2}}
and
M\geq 1
there exist
u\in{U_{\Delta}^{2}}
and
w\in U_{{\Delta}}^{p}
with

v=u+w

for which

\frac{\kappa}{M}\|u\|_{{U_{\Delta}^{2}}}+e^{M}\|w\|_{U_{{\Delta}}^{p}}\leq\|v\|_{{V_{\Delta}^{2}}}.

Lemma 2.5

(Transference principle)
.

If a
k
-linear operator

T_{0}:L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times\cdots\times L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\to L_{loc}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

satisfies

\left\|T_{0}\left(e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it{\Delta}}f_{1},e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it{\Delta}}f_{2},\cdots,e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it{\Delta}}f_{k}\right)\right\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

for some
1\leq q,r\leq\infty
, then there exists
T:U^{p}_{\Delta}\times\cdots\times U^{p}_{\Delta}\to L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}
satisfying

\|T(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{k})\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|u_{j}\|_{U_{{\Delta}}^{q}}

such that

T(u_{1},\cdots,u_{k})(t,\cdot)=T_{0}(u_{1}(t),\cdots,u_{k}(t))
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

3.
Bilinear estimates

In this section, we provide the essential tools for establishing the proof of our main theorem.
We begin with invoking the standard Strichartz estimate, well-known in arbitrary dimensions. However, we need its one and two dimensional versions only, as stated below.

Lemma 3.1

(Strichartz estimate)
.

If
(q,r)
satisfies
\frac{2}{q}=d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r}\right)
and
(d,q,r)\neq(2,2,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\|e^{it\Delta}f\|_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

(3.1)

for all
f\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

To estimate the nonlinear part of Duhamel’s formula (
1.3
), we need the following bilinear Strichartz estimate

\|e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}f\,e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}g\|_{L_{t,x}^{1+2/d}}\lesssim\|f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|g\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

(3.2)

for all
f,g\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, which follows from the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the Strichartz estimate with
q=r
.
To account for high-low frequency interactions, we establish additional bilinear estimate.
Its proof is quite standard, but we provide the proof for the reader’s convenience. The bilinear estimate in
L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
is well-known, as seen in
[
3
]
, and an analogous bilinear estimate in
L^{2}_{t,x}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
holds for any dimension, see, e.g.,
[
17
, Theorem 2.9]
.

Lemma 3.2

(Bilinear Strichartz estimate)
.

If
N_{1}\nsim N_{2}
, then

\left\|e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}P_{N_{1}}f\,e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}P_{N_{2}}g\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{1+2/d}}\lesssim\left(\frac{N_{12}^{\min}}{N_{12}^{\max}}\right)^{\beta(d)}\|P_{N_{1}}f\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\|P_{N_{2}}g\|_{L_{x}^{2}}

(3.3)

for all
f,g\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\beta(1)=1/6
and
\beta(2)=1/2
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
N_{1}\gg N_{2}
. Note that the left side of (
3.3
) does not change when we replace
e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}P_{N_{2}}g
by its complex conjugate. First, we show that the space-time Fourier transform is

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\left[e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}f\,\overline{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}g}\right](\tau,\xi)\\
&=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d-1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\widehat{f}(\xi+\eta)\;\overline{\widehat{g}(\eta)}\;{\delta}(\tau+{d_{\mathrm{av}}}|\xi+\eta|^{2}-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}|\eta|^{2})d\eta.\end{aligned}

(3.4)

Indeed, using the formula of the linear propagator

\left(e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}{\varphi}\right)(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{ix\cdot\xi}e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{{\varphi}}(\xi)d\xi,

we first see that the space Fourier transform as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left[e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}f\,\overline{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}g}\right](\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{2d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot(\xi-\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})}dx\right)\,e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\left(|\xi_{1}|^{2}-|\xi_{2}|^{2}\right)}\widehat{f}(\xi_{1})\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi_{2})}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}\delta(\xi-\xi_{1}+\xi_{2})e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\left(|\xi_{1}|^{2}-|\xi_{2}|^{2}\right)}\widehat{f}(\xi_{1})\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi_{2})}d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\left(|\xi+\xi_{2}|^{2}-|\xi_{2}|^{2}\right)}\widehat{f}(\xi+\xi_{2})\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi_{2})}d\xi_{2}

where we used the Dirac delta function
\delta(\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-ix\cdot\xi}dx
as distribution. Subsequently, applying the Fourier transform in time yields (
3.4
) in a similar fashion.

Now we consider the one dimensional case. Since the Dirac delta function
\delta
is homogeneous of degree
-1
, that is,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\varphi}(x)\delta(ax)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\varphi}(y)\frac{1}{|a|}\delta(y)dy,

it follows from (
3.4
)
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{t,x}\left[e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}f\,\overline{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}g}\right](\tau,\xi)=\frac{1}{2|{d_{\mathrm{av}}}||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|}\widehat{f}(\xi_{1})\overline{\widehat{g}(\xi_{2})}

where
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is the solution of

\displaystyle{d_{\mathrm{av}}}(\xi_{2}^{2}-\xi_{1}^{2})=\tau\mbox{ and }\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}=\xi.

Thus, using the Hausdorff-Young inequality, we see that
|\xi_{2}-\xi_{1}|=|\xi|\gtrsim N_{1}
, and

d\tau d\xi=2|{d_{\mathrm{av}}}||\xi_{1}-\xi_{2}|d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},

we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}P_{N_{1}}f\,\overline{e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}P_{N_{2}}g}\right\|_{L_{t,x}^{3}}

(B_{\delta},d)
by

\displaystyle\Phi(u)=e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u_{0}+i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{1}e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}i(t-s){\Delta}}e^{-ir\Delta}(|e^{ir\Delta}u|^{\frac{4}{d}}\,e^{ir\Delta}u)(s)drds.

Its linear part can be bounded as

\displaystyle\|e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{X_{[0,\infty)}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u_{0}\|_{{U_{\Delta}^{2}}([0,\infty))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}u_{0}\|_{U^{2}([0,\infty))}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2}\approx\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}^{2},

since any normalized function in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
U^{2}
-atom.
Thus, by Lemma
4.1
, there exists a positive constant
C
such that

\|\Phi(u)\|_{X_{[0,\infty)}}\leq C\left(\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}+{\delta}^{1+4/d}\right)

(4.10)

and

d(\Phi(u)-\Phi(v))\leq C{\delta}^{4/d}d(u,v)

(4.11)

for all
u,v\in B_{\delta}
.
If
{\delta}>0
is chosen to be small enough so that
2C{\delta}^{4/d}<1
and the initial data
u_{0}
satisfies
2C\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\leq{\delta}
, then it follows from (
4.10
) and (
4.11
) that
\Phi
is a self-contraction on
(B_{\delta},d)
.
Thus,
\Phi
has a fixed point
u
which is the unique global solution to (
1.1
) in
B_{\delta}
.

Now, we show the scattering result in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. For the initial data
u_{0}\in L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\|u_{0}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\lesssim\delta
, the global solution
u
satisfies

u(t)=e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}\left(u_{0}+ie^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}\mathcal{N}(u)(t)\right).

Moreover, it follows from (
4.1
) and the embedding
U_{\Delta}^{2}\hookrightarrow V_{\Delta}^{2}
that

\displaystyle\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}\mathcal{N}(u)\|_{{V_{\Delta}^{2}}}^{2}\lesssim\sum_{N\geq 1}\|P_{N}\mathcal{N}(u)\|_{{U_{\Delta}^{2}}}^{2}\lesssim 1.

By applying Lemma
2.3
, there exists a limit

\displaystyle u_{0}+e^{-{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}\sum_{N\geq 1}P_{N}\mathcal{N}(u)(t)\to u_{+}

in
L_{x}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
t\to\infty
, which implies the following scattering phenomenon

\displaystyle\|u(t)-e^{{d_{\mathrm{av}}}it\Delta}u_{+}\|_{L_{x}^{2}}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}0.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Regarding the scattering phenomenon on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
s>0
, we can apply a similar approach as described above. More precisely, we replace
X_{[0,\infty)}
by the Banach space
X^{s}_{[0,\infty)}
defined by

\displaystyle X^{s}_{[0,\infty)}:=\Bigg\{u\in C([0,\infty),H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))\cap U^{2}_{\Delta}([0,\infty)):\|u\|_{X^{s}_{[0,\infty)}}<\infty\Bigg\}

equipped with the norm

\|u\|_{X^{s}_{[0,\infty)}}:=\Bigg(\sum_{N\geq 1}N^{2s}\|P_{N}u\|_{U_{\Delta}^{2}([0,\infty))}^{2}\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{2}},

and obtain improved bilinear estimates. This leads to a small data global well-posedness and scattering on
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
s>0
via the contraction mapping argument.

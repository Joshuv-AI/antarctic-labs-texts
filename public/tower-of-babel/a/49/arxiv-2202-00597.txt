Positive definite maps on amenable groups
Mikaël Pichot, Erik Séguin

Abstract
. We describe conditions that characterize amenability for groups in terms of positive definite functions valued in a von Neumann algebra.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a (discrete) group,
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space,
B(\mathcal{H})
be the algebra of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
endowed with the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
, and
U(\mathcal{H})
be the group of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
.
Let
\varepsilon\geq 0
.
A map
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})

is called an
\varepsilon
-representation
of
G
if

\|\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
x,y\in G
; we also use the term
\varepsilon
-multiplicative
to refer to such maps.

We are interested in the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Does there exist a non-amenable group
G
such that
for every
{\delta>0}
there exists some
{\varepsilon>0}
such that for every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|<\delta

for all
{x\in G}
?

The conclusion, that there must exist a unitary representation
\pi
approximating
\varphi
to within
\delta
, is a “stability condition” in the sense of Ulam (see
[
19
]
) for the unitary representations of
G
. It is a well-known theorem of Kazhdan (see
[
12
, Theorem 1]
) that discrete amenable groups are stable in this sense, but the converse remains open (see e.g.,
[
2
,
4
,
5
]
).

In the present paper, we give new characterizations of amenability which are “intermediate” between the usual definition of amenability (in terms of the existence of an invariant mean) and this stability condition. Roughly speaking, amenability can be viewed as a property ensuring the existence of positive definitive maps from
G
to
B(\mathcal{H})
with certain properties. We refer the reader to
[
9
]
or
[
14
]
for an introduction to amenable groups. Several well-known characterizations can be found in
[
3
, Theorem 2.6.8]
.

Stability conditions for the unitary representations of a group can be reformulated in terms of unital positive definite maps. Let us note that it is equivalent to prove the existence of a unitary representation
\pi
close to a given
\varepsilon
-multiplicative map
\varphi
, or that of a unital positive definite map
\psi
close to
\varphi
which is also
\delta
-multiplicative, provided that
\delta>0
can be chosen to be sufficiently small compared to
\varepsilon
. The following proposition (discussed further in §
3
) makes this statement more precise:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exist real numbers
\kappa>0
and
0<\delta<1
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon

for all
x\in G
;

(2)

there exist real numbers
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
,
0<\delta<1
, and
p>1
such that for every
0<\varepsilon<1
such that
\kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{p-1}\leq\delta^{p-1}
and every
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unital positive definite
{\kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{p}}
-representation
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\leq\kappa_{2}\varepsilon

for all
{x\in G}
.

A map
{\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
is said to be
uniformly bounded
if

{\|{\varphi}\|:=\sup_{x\in G}\,\|{\varphi(x)}\|<\infty}.

We let
{\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
denote the complex vector space of uniformly bounded maps from
G
to
B(\mathcal{H})
.

A version of Prop.
1.2
for uniformly bounded representations is discussed in §
4
. These arguments can be use to establish stability (see e.g.
[
2
]
and
[
17
]
). Unital positive definite maps arise as follows.

If the group
G
is amenable, then every invariant mean
\ell^{\infty}(G)
can be extended in a natural way to an invariant mean on
\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))
. It has been shown by Chiffre, Ozawa, and Thom in
[
5
, §2]
that if
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is a uniformly bounded map and
\mathbb{E}
is such a mean, then the map
\psi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
defined by

\psi(x):=\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(xy)\varphi(y)^{*},\ \ \ \ x\in G

is positive definite (see also
[
16
,
8
]
).

The following is our main result:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space. The following are equivalent:

(A)

G
is amenable;

(B)

there exists a
B(\mathcal{H})
-valued positive semi-definite left-sesquilinear form
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
and a positive real number
{\kappa>0}
such that

(a)

{\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0}
, where
\mathbf{1}\in\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))
refers to the constant map equal to
\Id_{\mathcal{H}}
for all
x\in G
;

(b)

{\|{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}\|\leq\kappa\,\|{\varphi}\|^{2}}
for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
;

(c)

{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,)\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}
is positive definite for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
;

(C)

there exists a state
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
such that for every every uniformly bounded map
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}
.

Remark 1.4
.

If
\varphi
takes values in a von Neumann algebra
M
, then the map
\psi
in Condition (C) can be taken to also map into
M
.

Conditions (B) and (C) describe ways to construct a positive definite map
\psi
from the given uniformly bounded map
\varphi
; the construction method is explicit (although it depends on the axiom of choice in general), through the form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
in (B) or the state
\tau
in (C). By contrast, the stability condition does not yield a method for constructing the map
\psi
from
\varphi
; it only requires that a
\psi
exists which is close to
\varphi
in norm.

Observe that the equality

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})},\qquad\forall x\in G,\,\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}

in Condition (C) implies that
\psi
is close to
\varphi
if the latter is almost multiplicative. However, it is more precise, because it determines
\psi
uniquely from
\varphi
and
\tau
in many cases. In a sense, (B) and (C) can be viewed as characterizations of the invariance of a mean on
G
in terms of positive definite maps.

Accordingly, one can introduce the notion of a “partially invariant mean” by requiring that Condition (C) holds only for some subset of
{\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
for some state
\tau
. For example, the stability condition applies only to almost multiplicative maps and would correspond to a partial invariance condition in this sense.

In §
9
, we discuss this sort of partial invariance by restricting Condition (C) to almost multiplicative maps, and deduce the following from the proof of Kazhdan’s theorem and the stability theorems established in
[
2
]
.

Proposition 1.5
.

Suppose that
G
contains a non-abelian free group. For every state
\tau
on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
, every Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that for every positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists some normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))\neq\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for some
{x\in G}
.

We conclude the paper with a remark on unitarily invariant norms. While the
classical stability theorems
[
10
,
7
,
12
]
focus on the operator norm, several interesting results have been established in more general situations
[
10
,
8
,
5
]
, in particular with respect to ultraweakly lower semi-continuous unitary invariant norms. One can deduce (see Prop.
10.1
) estimates for such norms from Condition (C).

2.
\delta
-unitary maps on groups

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
.
A map
\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is said to be
\delta
-unitary
if
\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}\|\leq\delta
and
\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)\|\leq\delta
for every
x\in G
.

The present section contains basic results on
\delta
-unitary maps, in particular in relation with positive definite mappings, representations, and
\varepsilon
-representations. While the techniques and computations are elementary and well-known, we provide complete proofs for the convenience of the reader. We recall that a map
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is said to be positive definite if for every finite set
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\subset G
, the operator matrix
(\varphi(x_{i}^{-1}x_{j}))_{ij}
is positive.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and suppose
\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is unital positive definite. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is
\delta
-unitary.

(2)

\varphi
is
\delta
-multiplicative.

Proof.

(2)
\impl
(1): we have

\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}\|=\|\varphi(e)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x^{-1})\|\leq\delta

for every
x\in G
, and similarly,
\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)\|\leq\delta
.

(1)
\impl
(2): This follows from Lemma
2.3
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

If
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
is a positive definite map such that
{\|\varphi(e)\|\leq 1}
, then

\|\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)\|\leq\|\varphi(e)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}\|^{1/2}\,\|\varphi(e)-\varphi(y)^{*}\,\varphi(y)\|^{1/2}

for all
{x,y\in G}
.

Proof.

It follows by Stinespring’s dilation theorem that there exists a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, a bounded operator
{U:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}}
, and a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{K})}
such that
{\varphi(x)=U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,U}
for all
{x\in G}
. As
{UU^{*}}
is a positive operator such that

\|UU^{*}\|=\|U^{*}\|^{2}=\|U\|^{2}=\|\varphi(e)\|\leq 1

it follows that
{\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*}}
is a positive operator, and thus
{(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}}
is well-defined, so

\displaystyle\|\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)\|

\displaystyle=\|U^{*}\,\pi(xy)\,U-U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,UU^{*}\,\pi(y)\,U\|

\displaystyle=\|U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\,(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\,\pi(y)\,U\|

\displaystyle\leq\|U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\|\,\|(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\,\pi(y)\,U\|

\displaystyle=\|(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\,\pi(x)^{*}\,U\|\,\|(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})^{1/2}\,\pi(y)\,U\|

\displaystyle=\|U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})\,\pi(x)^{*}\,U\|^{1/2}\,\|U^{*}\,\pi(y)^{*}\,(\Id_{\mathcal{K}}-UU^{*})\,\pi(y)\,U\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|U^{*}U-U^{*}\,\pi(x)\,UU^{*}\,\pi(x)^{*}\,U\|^{1/2}\,\|U^{*}U-U^{*}\,\pi(y)^{*}\,UU^{*}\,\pi(y)\,U\|^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi(e)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}\|^{1/2}\,\|\varphi(e)-\varphi(y)^{*}\,\varphi(y)\|^{1/2}

for all
{x,y\in G}
.
∎

We shall use the following terminology for convenience.
We say that a bounded operator
T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
between Hilbert spaces is
\delta
-isometric
if
\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T\|\leq\delta
, and
\delta
-unitary
if
T
and
T^{*}
are
\delta
-isometric; a map
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is
\delta
-isometric if
\varphi(x)
is
\delta
-isometric for every
x\in G
. We define
\varphi^{*}\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
by
\varphi^{*}(x):=\varphi(x)^{*}
for all
x\in G
; clearly,
\varphi
is
\delta
-unitary if and only if
\varphi
and
\varphi^{*}
are
\delta
-isometric if and only if
\varphi(x)
is
\delta
-unitary for every
x\in G
; if
\varphi
is positive definite, then
\varphi^{*}(x)=\varphi(x^{-1})
for all
x\in G
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and suppose
\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is positive definite. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\varphi
is
\delta
-unitary.

(2)

\varphi
is
\delta
-isometric.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and suppose that
T\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{K}
is a bounded operator between Hilbert spaces. Then

(1)

If
T
is
\delta
-isometric, then
1-\delta\leq\|T\|^{2}\leq 1+\delta
.

(2)

If
T
is invertible,
\|T\|^{2}\leq 1+\delta
, and
\|T^{-1}\|^{2}\leq 1+\delta
, then
T
is
\delta
-unitary.

Proof.

(1) We have

\displaystyle\|T\|^{2}=\|T^{*}T\|=\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T)\|

\displaystyle\leq 1+\delta

\displaystyle\geq 1-\delta.

(2) For every unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
we have

1=\|T^{-1}(T^{-1})^{*}T^{*}T\xi\|\leq(1+\delta)\|T^{*}T\xi\|\leq(1+\delta)^{2}

therefore
\|T^{*}T\xi\|\in[(1+\delta)^{-1},1+\delta]\subset[1-\delta,1+\delta]
. This implies that
\mathrm{Sp}(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{*}T)\subset[-\delta,\delta]
, so
T
is
\delta
-isometric. Exchanging the role of
T
and
T^{*}
, it follows identically that
T^{*}
is
\delta
-isometric. Therefore,
T
is
\delta
-unitary.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\delta\geq 0
and suppose
\pi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is a uniformly bounded representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}
. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\|\pi\|^{2}\leq 1+\delta
.

(2)

\pi
is
\delta
-unitary.

(3)

\pi
is
\delta
-isometric.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of Lemma
2.5
.
∎

For the remainder of this section we fix nonnegative real numbers
\varepsilon,\delta,\eta\geq 0
and bounded maps
{\varphi,\,\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

If
\varphi
is
\delta
-isometric and
\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq\eta
, then
\psi
is
{(\delta+(\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\eta)}
-isometric.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)\|+\|\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)\|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\|\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)-\psi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)\|+\|\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)-\psi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)\|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)^{*}\|\,\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|+\|\varphi(x)^{*}-\psi(x)^{*}\|\,\|\psi(x)\|+\delta

\displaystyle=\|\varphi(x)\|\,\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|+\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\,\|\psi(x)\|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\|{\varphi}\|\,\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|+\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\,\|{\psi}\|+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\left(\|{\varphi}\|+\|{\psi}\|\right)\eta+\delta

for all
{x\in G}
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

If
\varphi
is
\delta
-unitary and
\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq\varepsilon
, then
\psi
is
{(\delta+(\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\varepsilon)}
-unitary.

Proof.

This follows from Lemma
2.7
and the fact that
\|\varphi^{*}-\psi^{*}\|=\|\varphi-\psi\|
.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

If
\varphi
is a
\delta
-unitary
\varepsilon
-representation and
\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq\eta
, then
\psi
is a
{(\delta+(\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\,\eta)}
-unitary
{(\varepsilon+(1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\,\eta)}
-representation.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.8
,
\psi
is
{(\delta+(\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\,\eta)}
-unitary. Furthermore, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\psi(xy)-\psi(x)\,\psi(y)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi(xy)-\varphi(xy)\|+\|\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)\|

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ +\|\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\psi(x)\,\psi(y)\|

\displaystyle<\|\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\psi(x)\,\psi(y)\|+\varepsilon+\eta

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\,\psi(y)\|+\|\varphi(x)\,\psi(y)-\psi(x)\,\psi(y)\|+\varepsilon+\eta

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi(x)\|\,\|\varphi(y)-\psi(y)\|+\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\,\|\psi(y)\|+\varepsilon+\eta

\displaystyle\leq\|\varphi\|\,\|\varphi(y)-\psi(y)\|+\|\varphi(x)-\psi(x)\|\,\|\psi\|+\varepsilon+\eta

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|\right)\eta+\varepsilon

for all
{x,y\in G}
, and thus
\psi
is
{(\varepsilon+(1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\,\eta))}
-multiplicative.
∎

If
{\varphi,\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
we write
{\psi\prec\varphi}
if
{\psi(s)}
belongs to the von Neumann algebra generated by
{\varphi(G)}
for all
{s\in G}
. This is a preorder on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,B(\mathcal{H}))}
.

We shall require the following lemma (compare the proof Theorem 3.1 of
[
5
]
).

Lemma 2.10
.

If
{\varphi\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{H})}
is a
\delta
-unitary
\varepsilon
-representation, then there exists an
{(\varepsilon+(1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\,\delta)}
-representation
{\psi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\|{\varphi-\psi}\|\leq\delta}
.
Furthermore,
{\psi\prec\varphi}
.

Proof.

Let
{\varphi(x)=U_{x}\,|{\varphi(x)}|}
be the polar decomposition of
{\varphi(x)}
; as
{\varphi(x)}
is invertible,
{U_{x}}
can be taken to be unitary. Let
{\psi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
be the unitary map defined by
{\psi(x)=U_{x}}
. As
{|{\varphi(x)}|}
is a positive operator for all
{x\in G}
and
{|{1-t}|\leq|{1-t^{2}}|}
for all
{t\in[0,\infty)}
, we have

\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|\|\leq\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|^{2}\|

therefore

\displaystyle\|\psi(x)-\varphi(x)\|

\displaystyle=\|\psi(x)-\psi(x)\,|{\varphi(x)}|\|

\displaystyle=\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|^{2}\|

\displaystyle=\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)\|\leq\delta

for all
{x\in G}
. It follows by Lemma
2.9
that
\psi
is an
{(\varepsilon+(1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|)\delta)}
-representation.
∎

We also remark (following
[
5
]
) that the inequality

\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|\|\leq\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-|{\varphi(x)}|^{2}\|,

used in the previous lemma for the operator norm, holds in fact for an arbitrary unitarily invariant norm:

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a separable Hilbert space and
\|\cdot\|
be a unitarily invariant norm on
{B(\mathcal{H})}
. If
{T\in B(\mathcal{H})}
is a positive operator, then

{\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T}\|\leq\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{2}}}\|

Proof.

As
{|{1-t}|\leq|{1-t^{2}}}|
for all
{t\in[0,\infty)}
, it follows that

{\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T}\|=\|{|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T}}|\|\leq\|{|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{2}}}|\|=\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-T^{2}}}\|

by Proposition 2.6 in
[
5
]
.
∎

Lemma 2.12
.

Suppose
\delta<1
. If
{\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
is a
\delta
-unitary
\varepsilon
-representation, then there exists an
{(\varepsilon+4\delta)}
-representation
{\psi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\|{\varphi-\psi}\|\leq\delta}
.
Furthermore,
{\psi\prec\varphi}
.

Proof.

As
\varphi
is
\delta
-unitary and
{\delta<1}
, we have

{\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)\|<1,\qquad\|\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}\|<1}

for all
{x\in G}
, so
{\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(x)}
and
{\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}}
are invertible for all
{x\in G}
, and thus in turn
{\varphi(x)}
and
{\varphi(x)^{*}}
are invertible for all
{x\in G}
. As

{1+\|\varphi\|+\|\psi\|=2+\|\varphi\|\leq 2+\sqrt{1+\delta}<2+\sqrt{2}},

it follows by Lemma
2.10
that
\psi
is an
{(\varepsilon+4\delta)}
-representation.
∎

3.
Stability and positive definite maps

Let
G
be a group. Consider the following stability condition, which depends on a positive real number
\kappa>0
and a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
:

there exists a real number
0<\delta<1
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that
\|\varphi-\pi\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon
.

Let us say that
G
is
stable
if this condition holds for some
\kappa
and all
\mathcal{H}
. The theorem of Kazhdan
[
12
]
mentioned in the introduction states:

Theorem 3.1

(Kazhdan, 1982)
.

Every amenable group is stable (with constant
\kappa=2
).

(Here and below, by amenable group, we mean a group which is amenable as a discrete group.)

In the present section, we note that the stability condition is equivalent to every such map
p
being approximated by a positive definite almost unitary map defined over the same Hilbert space, rather than a unitary representation.

In the following proposition
\mathcal{H}
is fixed, while the real numbers
\kappa,\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2},p,\delta
may vary.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space. The following are equivalent:

(1)

there exists real numbers
\kappa>0
and
0<\delta<1
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon<\delta}
and every unitary
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a unitary representation
{\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that

\|\varphi(x)-\pi(x)\|\leq\kappa\varepsilon

for every
x\in G
;

(2)

there exist real numbers
\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}>0
,
0<\delta<1
, and

4.
Remarks on uniformly bounded representations

Let

\varepsilon

\varepsilon>0

(1)

\psi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
(fixing multiplicativity), and

(2)

then take
\psi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
to a unitary representation
\pi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})
(fixing unitarity)

in such a way that
\|\varphi-\psi\|
and

In Prop.
3.2

Observe that the first step may result in a map

there exist positive real number
\kappa,M\geq 1
and
{0<\delta<1}
such that for every
{0<\varepsilon\leq\delta}
and every unital uniformly bounded
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
there exists a representation
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that
{\|{\psi}\|\leq M\|{\varphi}}\|
and

{\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|\leq\kappa\,\|{\varphi}\|\,\varepsilon}

for all

.

We have the following implication:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Suppose that there exist positive real numbers
{\kappa_{1},\kappa_{2}\geq 1}
,
{0<\delta<1}
, and
{p>1}
, and a non-decreasing function

such that for every

and every unital uniformly bounded
\varepsilon
-representation

{\kappa_{1}\varepsilon^{p}}
-representation

such that
{\|{\psi}\|\leq F(\varepsilon)\,\|{\varphi}\|}
and

{\|{\varphi-\psi}\|\leq\kappa_{2}\|{\varphi}\|\,\varepsilon}.

Let
{\psi_{0}=\varphi}
, let
{\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon}
, and for every

, let
{\psi_{n}}
be the map yielded by applying the condition to
{\psi_{n-1}}
; then

-representation where

\varepsilon_{n}=\kappa_{1}\varepsilon_{n-1}^{p}=\kappa_{1}^{1+p+\cdots+p^{n-1}}\varepsilon^{p^{n}}=\kappa_{1}^{(p-1)^{-1}(p^{n}-1)}\varepsilon^{p^{n}}=\kappa_{1}^{-(p-1)^{-1}}\big(\kappa_{1}^{(p-1)^{-1}}\varepsilon\big)^{p^{n}}\leq\delta^{p^{n}}

for all

converges for all

5.
Semi-unitarizability

Let
M
be a von Neumann algebra acting on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and let
V
be a normal dual Banach right
M
-module (i.e., such that the maps
x\mapsto vx
is weak
∗
-continuous). We write
B_{M}(V)
for the set of bounded
M
-module maps on
V
.

Definition 5.1
.

A uniformly bounded linear representation
\pi\colon G\to B_{M}(V)
is
semi-unitarizable
if there exists an
M
-valued
positive semi-definite sesquilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle\colon V\times V\to M
which is

(1)

continuous:

\|\langle u,\ v\rangle\|\leq C\|u\|\|v\|\ \ \forall u,v\in V,\text{ for some constant }C

(2)

invariant:

\langle\pi(s)u,\pi(s)v\rangle=\langle u,v\rangle\ \ \forall u,v\in V,

(3)

non-degenerate:

\langle u,u\rangle\neq 0,\ \forall u\in V^{G},\ u\neq 0.

We say that
\pi
is
unitarizable
if
\langle\,,\,\rangle
can furthermore be chosen to be positive definite, i.e.,
\langle u,\ u\rangle\neq 0,\ \forall u\in V
,
u\neq 0
.

It is well-known (considering the case
M=\mathbb{C}
of trivial coefficients) that every representation of a finite group on a finite dimensional vector space is unitarizable, and every uniformly bounded representation of an amenable group on a Hilbert space is unitarizable. The Dixmier problem asks whether this property characterizes amenable groups.

Consider the complex vector space
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
of uniformly bounded maps from
G
to
M
, which we view as a normal Banach
M
-bimodule under the action defined by
(S\varphi T)(x)=S\varphi(x)T
for every
x\in G
and
S,T\in M
.

Proposition 5.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

the left regular representation

\displaystyle\lambda\colon G\curvearrowright\ell^{\infty}(G,M),\ \lambda_{x}(\varphi)(y):=\varphi(x^{-1}y),\ \varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M),\ x,y\in G

is semi-unitarizable.

We shall use the following notion of an invariant mean on the space
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. An
invariant mean
on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
is a linear map
{\mathbb{E}:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M}
such that
{\|\mathbb{E}\|=1}
,
{\mathbb{E}(T\mathbf{1})=T}
for all
{T\in M}
, and

{\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(xy)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(y)}

for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and
{x\in G}
.

Lemma 5.4
.

An invariant mean
\mathbb{E}
in the sense of Def.
5.3
is positive and satisfies

{\mathbb{E}(S\varphi T)=S\,\mathbb{E}(\varphi)\,T}

for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and
{S,T\in M}
.

Proof.

This is an obvious application of Tomiyama’s theorem. Namely, consider the canonical inclusion map
{i\colon\mathrm{M}\hookrightarrow\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{M})}
. As
{(i\circ\mathbb{E})(T\mathbf{1})=i(T)=T\mathbf{1}}
for all
{T\in M}
and
{\|{i\circ\mathbb{E}}\|=1}
, it follows by Theorem 1 in
[
18
]
that

{(i\circ\mathbb{E})(S\mskip 1.5mu\varphi\mskip 1.5muT)=i(S)\,(i\circ\mathbb{E})(\varphi)\,i(T)=i(S\,\mathbb{E}(\varphi)\,T)}

for all
{S,T\in\mathrm{M}}
and
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{M})}
; as
i
is an embedding, this implies that

{\mathbb{E}(S\mskip 1.5mu\varphi\mskip 1.5muT)=S\,\mathbb{E}(\varphi)T}

for all
{S,T\in\mathrm{M}}
and
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{M})}
. Furthermore, as
{i\circ\mathbb{E}}
is positive and
i
is an isometric *-homomorphism, it follows that
\mathbb{E}
is positive.
∎

Every invariant mean
\mathbb{E}\in\ell^{\infty}(G)^{*}
extends to an invariant mean
\mathbb{E}\colon\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M=(M_{*})^{*}
defined by

\Phi(\mathbb{E}_{x}\varphi(x))=\mathbb{E}_{x}\Phi(\varphi(x)),\forall\Phi\in M_{*}

(see e.g.,
[
12
,
16
,
17
,
2
,
5
]
). Explicitly, for each
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and
{\eta\in\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(M)}
, define a bounded linear functional
{\psi_{\varphi,\eta}\in\mathcal{H}^{*}}
by

\psi_{\varphi,\eta}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}_{x}\langle\xi,\varphi(x)\,\eta\rangle,

where
\mathbb{E}
is an invariant mean on
\ell^{\infty}(G)
. It follows by the Riesz representation theorem that a map
{\mathbb{E}:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to B(\mathcal{H})}
can be defined by

\psi_{\varphi,\eta}(\xi)=\langle\xi,\mathbb{E}(\varphi)(\eta)\rangle

Clearly,
\mathbb{E}(\varphi)\in M^{\prime\prime}=M
, so
\mathbb{E}
defines an invariant mean on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
.

Lemma 5.5
.

The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

there exists an invariant mean on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
;

(3)

there exists a bounded linear map
{\mathbb{E}:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M}
such that

{\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1})\neq 0}
and

{\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(xy)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(y)}

for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and
{x\in G}
.

Proof.

(1)
\impl
(2) was noted above and (2)
\impl
(3) is obvious. To show (3)
\impl
(1), fix a unit vector
{\xi\in(\ker\,\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1})^{1/2})^{\perp}}
and let
{\psi:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to\mathbf{C}}
be the linear map defined by

\psi(f)=\langle\mathbb{E}(f)\,\xi,{\xi}\rangle.

The restriction
\psi_{|\ell^{\infty}(G)}
to the canonical embedding
\ell^{\infty}(G)\subset\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
defines a nontrivial left invariant bounded linear functional on

. Therefore,
G
is amenable.
∎

Remark 5.6
.

The invariant mean
\mathbb{E}^{M}
defined in (1)
\impl
(2) satisfies

{\mathbb{E}_{x}\langle\xi,\varphi(x)\,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\mathbb{E}_{x}^{M}\varphi(x)\,\eta\rangle}

{\mathbb{E}_{x}\langle\varphi(x)\,\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\mathbb{E}_{x}^{M}\varphi(x)\,\xi,\eta\rangle}

for every
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and

, where

is a fixed invariant mean on

. We shall assume that these properties hold and simply write

.

Proof of Prop.
5.2
.

If
G
is amenable and
\mathbb{E}
is a left invariant mean on
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
, then
\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle:=\mathbb{E}_{x}\varphi(x)^{*}\psi(x)
is a continuous non-degenerate positive

\langle\lambda_{x}\varphi,\ \lambda_{x}\psi\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{x}{\varphi(x^{-1}y)}^{*}\psi(x^{-1}y)=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle

for every

,

.

Conversely, if

, then the linear map

is left invariant on

6.
semi-definite sesquilinear forms

Let
G
be a group,
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space, and
V
be a complex vector space.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
{X\subset V}
be a subset stable under multiplication by
i
, and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
be an
{B(\mathcal{H})}
-valued positive semi-definite sesquilinear form on
V
. Let
{u\in V}
be a vector. If
\pi
is a linear representation of
G
on
V
such that the map
{\langle\pi(\,\cdot\,)\,(u+v),u+v\rangle}
is positive definite from
G
to

for all

, then

\langle\pi(s)\,u,v\rangle=\langle u,\pi(s^{-1})\,v\rangle

for all
{v\in X}
and
{s\in G}
.

Proof.

As
{\langle\pi(\,\cdot\,)\,(u+v),u+v\rangle}
is positive definite for all
{v\in X}
, it follows that

{\langle\pi(s)\,(u+v),u+v\rangle=\langle\pi(s^{-1})\,(u+v),u+v\rangle^{*}=\langle u+v,\pi(s^{-1})\,(u+v)\rangle}

for all
{v\in E}
and
{s\in G}
; as

is stable under multiplication by

, this implies that

\displaystyle\langle\pi(s)\,u,v\rangle

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}\,(\langle\pi(s)\,(u+v),u+v\rangle-\langle\pi(s)\,(u-v),u-v\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -i\langle\pi(s)\,(u+iv),u+iv\rangle+i\,\langle\pi(s)\,(u-iv),u-iv\rangle)

\displaystyle=\textstyle\frac{1}{4}\,(\langle u+v,\pi(s^{-1})\,(u+v)\rangle-\langle u-v,\pi(s^{-1})\,(u-v)\rangle

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -i\,\langle u+iv,\pi(s^{-1})\,u+iv\rangle+i\,\langle u-iv,\pi(s^{-1})\,u-iv\rangle)

\displaystyle=\langle u,\pi(s^{-1})\,v\rangle

for all
{v\in E}
and
{s\in G}
.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\pi
be a linear representation of
G
on
V
and
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
be a
{B(\mathcal{H})}
-valued positive semi-definite left-sesquilinear form on

from

is positive definite for all

\langle\pi(s)\,u,v\rangle=\langle u,\pi(s^{-1})\,v\rangle

for all
{u,v\in X}
and
{s\in G}
.

Proof.

It suffices to apply Lemma

with
{X=V}
.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

there exists an
M
-valued positive semi-definite left-sesquilinear form
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
and
{\kappa>0}
such that

(a)

{\langle 1_{M},1_{M}\rangle\neq 0}

(b)

{\|{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle}\|\leq\kappa\,\|{\varphi}\|^{2}}
for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}

(c)

{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,)\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}
is positive definite for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}

Proof.

Suppose that

is amenable. Then

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle:=\mathbb{E}_{x}\varphi(x)^{*}\psi(x)

is an invariant continuous non-degenerate positive

. As

\langle\lambda(x)\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{y}(\lambda(x)\,\varphi)(y)^{*}\varphi(y)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(x^{-1}y)^{*}\,\varphi(y)=\mathbb{E}_{y}\varphi(y)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)

for all

and

, it follows by the proof of Prop. 2.2 in

[

that

is positive definite for all

The converse follows by Lemma

∎

Remark 6.4
.

In connection with this, an equivalence between the invariance of an

Let

(1)

(2)

on

and

{\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle\neq 0}
;

(b)

{|{\langle f,f\rangle}|\leq\kappa\,\|{f}\|_{\infty}^{2}}

(c)

7.
Remarks on Prop.
5.2
and Prop.
6.3

As mentioned in the introduction, one difference between the stability condition and the two characterizations of amenability in Prop.
5.2
and Prop.
6.3
, is that in the latter cases amenability can be used to transform an arbitrary uniformly bounded map (which needs not be an
\varepsilon
-representation for small
\varepsilon
) into a positive definite map.
If the set of
\varepsilon
-representations of a group
G
is too “thin”, one may not be able to reconstruct an invariant mean from the equivalent stability conditions stated in Prop.
3.2
.

On the other hand, the full set
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
of maps is not required to prove the two propositions. For example, it is sufficient to consider maps
\varphi\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)
, where
\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)
denotes the set of invertible operators, to establish Prop.
5.2
:

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

there exists a continuous nondegenerate
M
-valued
positive semi-definite sesquilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle\colon\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\times\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M
such that

\langle\lambda_{x}\varphi,\lambda_{x}\psi\rangle=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle

for all
x\in G
and all
\varphi,\psi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M))
;

Proof.

The direct implication follows by Prop.
5.2
. Conversely, using the notation of Prop.
5.2
, the restriction of the linear map
\mathbb{E}(\varphi):=\langle\mathbf{1},\varphi\rangle
to
\ell^{\infty}(G)

satisfies
\mathbb{E}(\lambda_{x}\varphi)=\mathbb{E}(\varphi)

for every
x\in G
and every
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
such that
\varphi(y)\neq 0
for every
y\in G
. Since
\mathbb{E}
is bounded, this equality holds for every
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G)
, and since
\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{1})\neq 0
, it follows that
G
is amenable.
∎

We shall require the following strengthening of Prop.
6.3
in the proof of Prop.
1.5
and Prop.
8.1
(see §
8
and §
9
).

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable;

(2)

there exists a continuous nondegenerate
M
-valued
positive semi-definite sesquilinear form
\langle\,,\,\rangle\colon\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\times\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M
such that

{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,)\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}
is positive definite for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M))}
.

It is clear that this proposition holds identically with
\lambda(\cdot)
replaced by
\lambda(\cdot^{-1})
.

Proof.

The direct implication follows by Prop.
6.3
. Conversely, let
{\Psi:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M}
be the linear map defined by

\Psi(\varphi)=\langle\mathbf{1},\varphi\rangle

Let

{X=\{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M):\|{\varphi}\|<1\}}

As
{\|{\varphi(x)}\|<1}
for all
{\varphi\in X}
and
{x\in G}
, in turn
{\mathbf{1}+\varphi(x)}
is invertible for all
{\varphi\in X}
and
{x\in G}
, and thus
{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,)(\mathbf{1}+\varphi),\mathbf{1}+\varphi\rangle}
is positive definite for all
{\varphi\in X}
. As
X
is stable under multiplication by
i
, it follows by Lemma
6.1
that

{\Psi(\lambda(s)\,\varphi)=\langle\mathbf{1},\lambda(s)\,\varphi\rangle=\langle\lambda(s^{-1})\,\mathbf{1},\varphi\rangle=\langle\mathbf{1},f\rangle=\Psi(\varphi)}

for all
{s\in G}
and
{\varphi\in X}
. By linearity, it follows that
{\Psi(\lambda(s)\,\varphi)=\Psi(\varphi)}
for all
{s\in G}
and
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
, and thus
\Psi
is left invariant on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
. Furthermore, it follows by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Prop. 2.3 in
[
13
]
that

{|{\Psi(\varphi)}|\leq\|{\langle\mathbf{1},\varphi\rangle}\|\,\|{\eta}\|^{2}\leq\|{\langle\mathbf{1},\mathbf{1}\rangle}\|^{1/2}\,\|{\langle\varphi,\varphi\rangle\|^{1/2}\leq\kappa\,\|{\varphi}\|}}

for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
, and thus
\Psi
is bounded. As
\Psi(\mathbf{1})\neq 0
, it follows by Lemma
5.5
that
G
is amenable.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

The arguments rely on the fact that the set of maps
\varphi\colon G\to\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M)
is large enough to generate
\ell^{\infty}(G,M)
, and they can be extended to other sets of maps with similar properties (e.g., the set of
M
-valued unitary maps).

8.
A positive definite characterization of the invariance of a mean

The following result characterizes amenability as a property that yields for every uniformly
bounded map
\varphi
into a positive definite map
\psi
which is close to it if
\varphi
is
\varepsilon
-multiplicative:

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
M
be a von Neumann algebra. The following are equivalent:

(1)

G
is amenable

(2)

there exists a state
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
such that for every every uniformly bounded map
{\varphi:G\to M}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to M}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in M_{*}}

Proof.

The proof of the implication
{(1)\Rightarrow(2)}
is standard. Suppose that
{(2)}
holds and let
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
be a state witnessing
{(2)}
. We let
{\Psi_{\tau}:\ell^{\infty}(G,M)\to M}
be the positive bounded linear map defined by

\Phi(\Psi_{\tau}(\varphi))=\tau_{x}\,\Phi(\varphi(x))

for
{\Phi\in M_{*}}
and
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
, where we identify
M
with
{(M_{*})^{*}}
.
Let
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
be the
M
-valued positive semi-definite left-sesquilinear form on
{\ell^{\infty}(G,M)}
defined by

\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle=\Psi_{\tau}(\varphi^{*}\psi).

By Prop.
7.2
, it suffices to show that
{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,^{-1})\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}
is a positive definite map on
G
for all
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M))}
.

Let
{\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M))}
. By assumption, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to M}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)\,\psi(x))=\Phi((\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\varphi(x)\,\varphi(xy)^{*}\,\varphi(y))}=\Phi(\varphi(x)(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\varphi(xy)^{*}\,\varphi(y))

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in M_{*}}
. Since
\varphi(x)
is invertible for all
x\in G
, it follows that

\psi(x^{-1})=(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,\varphi(x^{-1}y)^{*}\,\varphi(y)={\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,)\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}.

This shows that
{\langle\lambda(\,\cdot\,^{-1})\,\varphi,\varphi\rangle}
is positive definite.
∎

Remark 8.2
.

Suppose
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
is a state and
\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})
is a unitary map,
\psi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
is a uniformly bounded map such that the identity

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

is verified for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}}
; then

\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|\leq\sup_{y\in G}\|{\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\varphi(xy)}\|

for all
x\in G
. In particular, if
\varphi
is an
\varepsilon
-representation then

\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq\varepsilon.

Proof.

We have

{|{\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))}|=|{\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}}|

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}}
, and thus

\displaystyle\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|

\displaystyle=\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|{\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))|}:\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*},\,\|{\Phi\|}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|{\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})|}:\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*},\,\|{\Phi}\|\leq 1\}

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}}\|:y\in G\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|{\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\varphi(xy)}\|:y\in G\}

for all
{x\in G}
.
∎

Question 8.3
.

Does there exist a non-amenable group
G
such that for every von Neumann algebra
M
and every uniformly bounded map
{\varphi:G\to U(M)}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to M}
such that

\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|\leq\sup_{y\in G}\|{\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\varphi(xy)}\|

for all
x\in G
?

9.
Restricting to
\varepsilon
-representations

We now consider the situation where we restrict the set of maps
\varphi\colon G\to B(\mathcal{H})
for which a positive definite map
\psi
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}\ \ \forall x\in G,\ \forall\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}

must exist to the set of
\delta
-representations
\varphi\colon G\to U(\mathcal{H})
for some
\delta>0
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
G
be a group and
\mathcal{H}
be a Hilbert space.
Suppose that there exists a state
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
and
0<\delta<1
such that for every
\delta
-representation
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists a positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}
. Then
G
is stable.

Proof.

We establish (4) in Prop.
3.2
. We fix
\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))
and
0<\delta<1
witnessing the assumptions of the proposition. Let
0<\varepsilon<\delta
, let
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
be an
\varepsilon
-representation, and let
\psi
be a positive definite map such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})_{*}}
. Since
\varphi\in\ell^{\infty}(G,\mathrm{GL}_{1}(M))
, the proof of Prop.
8.1
show that
\psi
is uniquely determined by the formula

\psi(x)=(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}

for all
x\in G
. Since
\varphi
is an
\varepsilon
-representation, Remark
8.2
shows that

\|\varphi-\psi\|\leq\varepsilon.

We show that
\psi
is
2\varepsilon^{2}
-unitary, following the proof of Kazhdan’s theorem given in
[
2
]
. Thus, (4) in Prop.
3.2
holds with
\kappa_{1}=2
,
\kappa_{2}=1
,
p=2
.

Since

\displaystyle(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}(\varphi(xy)

\displaystyle-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}

\displaystyle=(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(xy)^{*}-\varphi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}-\psi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}+\varphi(x)\,\varphi(x)^{*}

\displaystyle=\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}+(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}

it follows that

\displaystyle\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\|{(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|\|{(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\|{\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)}\,\|\|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}\|

\displaystyle=\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|^{2}+(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\|{\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)}\|^{2}

\displaystyle<\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}=2\varepsilon^{2}

for all
{x\in G}
.
∎

Remark 9.2
.

One can in fact establish (4) in Prop.
3.2
with
\kappa_{1}=1
,
\kappa_{2}=1
,
p=2
. Indeed, in the notation of the proof,
as
{\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}}
is positive and

{\|{\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}}\|\leq\|{\psi}\|^{2}=\|{\psi(e)}\|=1}

for all
{x\in G}
, it follows that
{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}}
is positive for all
{x\in G}
. Furthermore,
{|({\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}}|^{2}}
is also positive for all
{x\in G}
and the equality

{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}+|{(\varphi(x)-\psi(x))^{*}}|^{2}=(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}|^{2}}

for all
{x\in G}
shows that

{0\leq\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}\leq(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}|^{2}}

for all
{x\in G}
. This then implies that

\displaystyle\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\psi(x)\,\psi(x)^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|{(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}|^{2}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\sup\{\|{|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}|^{2}}:y\in G\}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|{(\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y))^{*}}\|^{2}:y\in G\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\|{\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)}\|^{2}:y\in G\}\leq\varepsilon^{2}

for all
{x\in G}
.

In
[
12
]
Kazhdan proves that the compact surface groups of genus
\geq 2
are not stable.
More recently, an interesting construction of Rolli
[
15
]
gives for every finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and every
\varepsilon>0
an
\varepsilon
-representation
F_{n}\to U(\mathcal{H})
, where
F_{n}
is the free group on
n\geq 2
generators, which is not close to a unitary representation. By using the results of Burger, Ozawa and Thom
[
2
]
, we can obtain the following result for groups that contain
F_{2}
.

Corollary 9.3
.

Suppose that
G
contains a non-abelian free group. For every state
{\tau\in\mathop{\mathrm{St}}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
, Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and small enough
\varepsilon>0
, there exists an
\varepsilon
-representation
{\varphi:G\to U(\mathcal{H})}
such that for every positive definite map
{\psi:G\to B(\mathcal{H})}
, there exists some normal linear functional
{\Phi\in B(\mathcal{H})^{*}}
such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))\neq\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for some
{x\in G}
.

This follows by Cor. 3.8 in
[
2
]
. In fact, it is sufficient to suppose that the comparison map

H^{2}_{b}(G,\mathbb{R})\to H^{2}(G,\mathbb{R})

from bounded 2-cohomology to usual 2-cohomology is not injective, by Cor. 3.5 in
[
2
]
.

10.
Remarks on unitarily invariant norms

Finally, we note that the equality

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}\ \forall x\in G,\ \forall\Phi\in M_{*}

implies a norm estimate on the distance between
\varphi
and
\psi
, for every unitarily invariant norm on a finite factor
M
.

Proposition 10.1
.

Let
G
be a countable group,
M
be a finite factor,
{\tau\in\mathrm{St}(\ell^{\infty}(G))}
be a state,
{\varphi,\psi:G\to M}
be uniformly bounded maps such that

{\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))=\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}

for all
{x\in G}
and every normal linear functional
{\Phi\in M_{*}}
, and
\|\cdot\|
be an ultraweakly lower semi-continuous unitarily invariant norm on
M
. Then

{\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|\leq\tau_{y}\,\|{\varphi(xy)-\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)}}\|

for all
{x\in G}
.

Proof.

It follows by Theorem C in
[
6
]
that

\displaystyle\|{\varphi(x)-\psi(x)}\|

\displaystyle=\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x)}\|

\displaystyle=\sup\{|{\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\psi(x))}|:\Phi\in M_{*},\,\|{X_{\Phi}}\|_{*}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|{\tau_{y}\,\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})|}:\Phi\in M_{*},\,\|{X_{\Phi}}\|_{*}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{|{\Phi(\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*})}|:\Phi\in M_{*},\,\|{X_{\Phi}}\|_{*}\leq 1\}

\displaystyle=\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-(\Psi_{\tau})_{y}\,\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\tau_{y}\,\|{\Id_{\mathcal{H}}-\varphi(x)^{*}\,\varphi(xy)\,\varphi(y)^{*}}\|

\displaystyle=\tau_{y}\,\|{\varphi(x)\,\varphi(y)-\varphi(xy)}\|

for all
{x\in G}
, where
{\|\cdot\|_{*}}
denotes the dual norm of
\|\cdot\|
and
{X_{\Phi}}
denotes the bounded operator in
M
giving rise to the functional
\Phi
.
∎

Remark 10.2
.

The assumption that
\|\cdot\|
be ultraweakly lower semi-continuous is redundant, since Theorem C in
[
6
]
implies that every unitarily invariant norm on a finite factor is ultraweakly lower semi-continuous.

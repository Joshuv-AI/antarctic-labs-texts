Abstract
. We give efficient conditions under which a C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -subalgebra A ⊆ B A\subseteq B separates ideals in a C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -algebra B B , and B B is purely infinite if every positive element in A A is properly infinite in B B . We specialise to the case when B B is a crossed product for an inverse semigroup action by Hilbert bimodules or a section C ∗ \textup{C}^{*} -algebra of a Fell bundle over an étale, possibly non-Hausdorff, groupoid. Then our theory works provided B B is the recently introduced essential crossed product and the action is essentially exact and residually aperiodic or residually topologically free. These last notions are developed in the article.

1.
Introduction

Many authors have given sufficient criteria for crossed products by
discrete group actions or for
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras associated to
étale locally compact groupoids to be purely infinite (see, for
instance,
[
2
,
35
,
19
,
43
,
38
,
23
,
31
,
6
,
41
,
36
]
). These
articles mostly deal with the case when the bigger

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
is simple or when the

\textup{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
A\subseteq B
on which the action takes
place is commutative and has totally disconnected spectrum. In
addition, étale groupoids are required to be Hausdorff. These pure
infiniteness criteria also imply that
A
separates ideals
in
B
. Then the ideal lattice of
B
is
isomorphic to the lattice of invariant ideals in
A
. Here we
formulate sufficient conditions for
A
to separate ideals
in
B
and for
B
to be purely infinite, which allow
A
to be
noncommutative and which impose no Hausdorffness restrictions. In
this generality, it is natural to study actions of inverse
semigroups by Hilbert bimodules
(see
[
11
]
) or, equivalently, section
algebras of Fell bundles over inverse semigroups. This contains
Fell bundles over discrete groups and over étale groupoids
– possibly non-Hausdorff – as special cases. Another special case
are Exel’s noncommutative Cartan
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusions
(see
[
15
,
26
]
), which
generalise Renault’s (commutative) Cartan subalgebras.

This article is based on our recent papers,

[
27
,
25
,
29
,
28
]
, where two key concepts are
developed. The first one is the
essential crossed product

introduced in
[
28
]
, which is a
variation on the reduced crossed product that “always” has the
expected ideal structure – even for general actions of inverse
semigroups and for actions of non-Hausdorff groupoids. The second
concept is
aperiodicity
. It is a strong regularity property,
abstracted from the work of Kishimoto, Olesen–Pedersen, and others,
defined for a general
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion
A\subseteq B

in
[
28
]
. As shown
in
[
29
]
,
aperiodicity implies that there is a
unique
pseudo-expectation
– a unique generalised conditional
expectation for
A\subseteq B
taking values in Hamana’s injective
hull of
A
. If in addition this pseudo-expectation is almost
faithful, then
A

supports

B
in the sense that every
element in
B^{+}\setminus\{0\}
is supported by an element in

A^{+}\setminus\{0\}
in the Cuntz preorder
of
[
12
]
. This, in turn, implies
that
A

detects ideals
in
B
, that is,

J\cap A\neq 0
for any ideal
0\neq J\mathrel{\triangleleft}B
. All these
properties are closely related. In fact, when
B
is an essential
crossed product and
A
is separable or of Type I, then the
following conditions are equivalent: aperiodicity, unique
pseudo-expectation, supporting positive elements in all intermediate

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras, detection of ideals in all intermediate

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras, and
topological freeness
of the dual
groupoid
(see
[
29
]
).

In the present paper, we study “residual” versions of these
conditions, that is, when they hold for quotient inclusions

A/I\subseteq B/BIB
for all ideals
I\mathrel{\triangleleft}A
that are
restricted from
B
. The relationship between ideals in
B
and

restricted ideals
in
A
is thoroughly studied
in
[
27
]
. For a crossed product
inclusion, the restricted ideals in
A
are exactly those that are

invariant
under the action that produces
B
from
A
.
The residual version of detection of ideals is separation of ideals.
We say that
A

separates ideals
in
B
if

I\cap A=J\cap A
for ideals
I,J\mathrel{\triangleleft}B
implies
I=J
.
This identifies the ideal lattice of
B
with the lattice of
restricted ideals in
A
. Under some extra assumptions, we may
also identify the primitive ideal space of
B
with the

quasi-orbit space
of the induced action on the primitive
ideal space of
A
(see
[
27
]
).
A residual version of supporting is closely related to the formally
stronger condition called
filling
, which was used in

[
23
,
22
]
to prove strong pure
infiniteness. For a large class of
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion

A\subseteq B
, we show that
A
residually supports
B
if
and only if
A
fills
B
. Namely, this holds for the

symmetric
inclusions defined
in
[
27
]
, which include all sorts
of
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusions coming from crossed products. For general
residually supporting
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusions
A\subseteq B
and a
family
\mathcal{F}\subseteq A^{+}
of elements in
A
that are
properly infinite in
B
, we give sufficient conditions for
B

to be purely infinite (see
Theorem
2.37
below).

To ensure that
A^{+}
residually supports
B
we assume that the
inclusion
A\subseteq B
is
residually aperiodic
, in the
sense that for any restricted ideal
I\mathrel{\triangleleft}A
, the inclusion

A/I\to B/BIB
is aperiodic. We also need to assume that the
pseudo-expectations for these quotient inclusions are almost
faithful. For actions by discrete groups this “residual
faithfulness of conditional expectations” is also called

exactness
(see

[
44
,
1
,
31
]
) and
for groupoids inner exactness (see

[
3
,
6
]
).
We generalise this concept to inverse semigroup actions by Hilbert
bimodules and Fell bundles over étale groupoids.
In particular,
we prove that the full crossed product is an exact functor and the
reduced crossed product is an injective functor, but only when restricted
to special homomorphisms between actions (Propositions

4.2
and
4.15
).
The essential crossed product is not functorial. Therefore,
exactness for these crossed products, which we call
essential
exactness
, is more subtle.

As an appetizer, we formulate here a theorem that summarises and
illustrates some of our results. An action of a unital inverse
semigroup

by Hilbert bimodules on a
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

is a semigroup
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}

fibre
\mathcal{E}_{t}
is a Hilbert

S

\widehat{A}

and the primitive ideal space
\check{A}
. The corresponding
transformation groupoids
\widehat{A}\rtimes S
and

\check{A}\rtimes S
are called

of

call
\mathcal{E}

A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}
be an inverse semigroup action by
Hilbert bimodules on a
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
. Assume
that
\mathcal{E}
is essentially exact
(
or exact when the
action is closed
)
and residually aperiodic
(
this
holds when
\widehat{A}\rtimes S
is residually topologically
free
)
. Then

(1)

A
fills the essential crossed product
A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S
.
So
A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S
is strongly purely infinite if and only
if every pair of elements in
A^{+}
satisfies the matrix
factorisation property
of
[
22
]
;

(2)

the ideal lattice of
A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S
is isomorphic to the

of
\mathcal{E}
-invariant ideals
in
A
. If
\check{A}

is homeomorphic to the
quasi-orbit space
\check{A}/{\sim}
of the dual groupoid

\check{A}\rtimes S

(3)

\mathcal{E}

2.1.
Separation and detection of ideals

Let
A\subseteq B
be a
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion. We recall some
results from
[
27
]
that relate the
ideal structure of the two
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras
A
and
B
.
(In
[
27
]
, we also consider the
more general situation of an inclusion
A\to\mathcal{M}(B)
into the
multiplier algebra of
B
.)

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathbb{I}(A)
and
\mathbb{I}(B)
be the complete lattices of
(closed, two-sided) ideals in
A
and
B
, respectively. For

I\in\mathbb{I}(A)
, let
BIB\in\mathbb{I}(B)
be the ideal in
B

generated by
I
. We define the
restriction map
r

and the
induction map
i
by

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle\colon\mathbb{I}(B)\to\mathbb{I}(A),

\displaystyle\qquad J

\displaystyle\mapsto J\cap A,

\displaystyle i

\displaystyle\colon\mathbb{I}(A)\to\mathbb{I}(B),

\displaystyle\qquad I

\displaystyle\mapsto BIB.

We call
I\in\mathbb{I}(A)

restricted
if
I=r(J)
for
some
J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
. We call
J\in\mathbb{I}(B)

induced
if
J=i(I)
for some
I\in\mathbb{I}(A)
. Let

\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{I}(A)
and

\mathbb{I}^{A}(B)\subseteq\mathbb{I}(B)
be the subsets of restricted
and induced ideals, respectively.

The maps
r
and
i
form a (monotone)
Galois connection
,
that is, if
I\in\mathbb{I}(A)
,
J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
, then

I\subseteq r(J)
if and only if
i(I)\subseteq J
. This
observation goes back to Green
[
17
]
. It has a
number of consequences. For instance, the maps
i
and
r
are
monotone and satisfy
r\circ i(I)\supseteq I
and

i\circ r\circ i(I)=i(I)
for all
I\in\mathbb{I}(A)
, and

i\circ r(J)\subseteq J
and
r\circ i\circ r(J)=r(J)
for all

J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
. The map
i
preserves joins and
r

preserves meets. The maps

r\colon\mathbb{I}(B)\to\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)\subseteq\mathbb{I}(A)
and

i\colon\mathbb{I}(A)\to\mathbb{I}^{A}(B)\subseteq\mathbb{I}(B)
restrict
to mutually inverse isomorphisms of partially ordered sets

\mathbb{I}^{A}(B)\cong\mathbb{I}^{B}(A).

Thus
\mathbb{I}^{A}(B)\cong\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
are complete lattices with
inclusion as the partial order. The map
r
is injective if and
only if
i
is surjective, if and only if

\mathbb{I}(B)=\mathbb{I}^{A}(B)
. Subalgebras with this property are
said to separate ideals.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that
A

separates ideals
in
B
if

J_{1}\cap A\neq J_{2}\cap A
for all
J_{1},J_{2}\mathrel{\triangleleft}B
with

J_{1}\neq J_{2}
or, equivalently,
r
is injective. We say
that
A

detects ideals
in
B
if
J\cap A\neq 0
for
all
J\mathrel{\triangleleft}B
with
J\neq 0
or, equivalently,

r^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
.

Detection of ideals is sometimes called the intersection property.
Separation of ideals is a residual version of detection of ideals.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
27
]
*Proposition 2.12)
.

A
\textup{C}^{*}
-subalgebra
A\subseteq B
separates ideals if and
only if
A/I\subseteq B/BIB
detects ideals for all restricted
ideals
I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
.

2.2.
Symmetric and regular

\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusions

We will be mainly interested in regular inclusions, and these are

Definition 2.5

(
[
27

*Definition 5.2)

A
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion

nondegenerate

if
AB=B

(This is equivalent to

Let
\check{A}

\check{B}

A

\Prime^{B}(A)

\{U_{I}\}_{I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)}

The next theorem summarises several desirable properties of
symmetric inclusions.

Theorem 2.6

(
[

Let

\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion.

(1)

If
\mathfrak{p}

(2)

If
\mathfrak{p}

A
,

A

\mathfrak{p}
,

\pi(\mathfrak{p})

\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)

-module with the induced multiplication and the inner
product

\langle a+B_{t}I\,|\,\mathopen{}b+B_{t}I\rangle\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\langle a\,|\,\mathopen{}b\rangle+I=q(a^{*}b)\in A/I
for
a,b\in B_{t}
. We claim that the norm defined by this
inner product is equal to the quotient norm on
B_{t}/B_{t}I
. To
show this, let
(u_{n})_{n\in N}
be an approximate unit
for
I
. Then

\lVert q(b)\rVert^{2}=\lim{}\lVert b-bu_{i}\rVert^{2}=\lim{}\lVert(1-u_{i})b^{*}b(1-u_{i})\rVert=\lVert q(b^{*}b)\rVert=\lVert\langle b+B_{t}I\,|\,\mathopen{}b+B_{t}I\rangle\rVert.

This finishes the proof of
(1)
.
Assertion
(2)
follows
from
(1)
and
Proposition
2.11
.
∎

2.3.
Generalised expectations

Definition 2.15
.

A
generalised expectation
for a
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion

A\subseteq B
consists of another
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion

A\subseteq\tilde{A}
and a completely positive, contractive
map
B\to\tilde{A}
that restricts to the identity map
on
A
. If
\tilde{A}=A
,
\tilde{A}=A^{\prime\prime}
, or

\tilde{A}=\mathrm{I}(A)
is Hamana’s injective envelope of
A
,
then we speak of a
conditional expectation
, a
weak
expectation
, or a
pseudo-expectation
, respectively.

Let
E\colon B\to\tilde{A}\supseteq A
be a generalised
expectation. It is called
faithful
if
E(b^{*}b)=0
for
some
b\in B
implies
b=0
,
almost
faithful
if
E((bc)^{*}bc)=0
for all
c\in B
and some

b\in B
implies
b=0
, and
symmetric
if

E(b^{*}b)=0
for some
b\in B
implies
E(bb^{*})=0
.
The largest two-sided ideal in
B
contained in
\ker E
is
equal to

\mathcal{N}_{E}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\{b\in B\,{:}\,\mathopen{}E((bc)^{*}bc)=0\text{ for all }c\in B\}=\{b\in B\,{:}\,\mathopen{}E(xby)=0\text{ for all }x,y\in B\}

(see
[
28
]
*Proposition 3.6), and

\mathcal{N}_{E}=0
if and only if
E
is almost faithful.

Since
E|_{A}=\textup{Id}_{A}
and
E|_{\mathcal{N}_{E}}=0
, it follows that

A\cap\mathcal{N}_{E}=0
. Hence the composite map

A\to B\to B/\mathcal{N}_{E}
is injective and we may identify
A

with its image in
B_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}B/\mathcal{N}_{E}
. The map
E

descends to a generalised expectation

E_{\mathrm{r}}\colon B_{\mathrm{r}}\to\tilde{A}\supseteq A
that we call the

reduced generalised expectation
associated to
E

(see
[
28
]
*Definition 3.5). The
reduced generalised expectation
E_{\mathrm{r}}
is always almost
faithful. It is faithful if and only if
E
is symmetric (see

[
28
]
*Corollary 3.8).

We will mainly work with pseudo-expectations below. The injectivity
of
\mathrm{I}(A)
implies that any
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion has a
pseudo-expectation. The following lemma links detection of ideals
to almost faithfulness of pseudo-expectations:

Lemma 2.16
.

Let
A\subseteq B
be a
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion. The following
are equivalent:

(1)

A
detects ideals in
B
;

(2)

every generalised expectation for the
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion

A\subseteq B
is almost faithful;

(3)

every pseudo-expectation for
A\subseteq B
is almost
faithful.

Proof.

Let
E\colon B\to\widetilde{A}\supseteq A
be a generalised
expectation. Since
\mathcal{N}_{E}\cap A=0
, we must have

\mathcal{N}_{E}=0
if
A
detects ideals in
B
. That is,

(1)

implies
(2)
.
That
(2)

implies
(3)
is
obvious. We prove by contradiction
that
(3)

implies
(1)
. Assume
that
A
does not detect ideals in
B
. Then there is a
nonzero ideal
\mathcal{N}
in
B
with
\mathcal{N}\cap A=0
. The
inclusion
A\hookrightarrow B/\mathcal{N}
has a
pseudo-expectation
E\colon B/\mathcal{N}\to\mathrm{I}(A)
. Let

q\colon B\to B/\mathcal{N}
be the quotient map. Then

E\circ q\colon B\to\mathrm{I}(A)
is a pseudo-expectation for the
inclusion
A\subseteq B
. It is not almost faithful because

0\neq\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{E\circ q}
.
∎

Remark 2.17

(
[
40
]
*Theorem 3.5)
.

Every pseudo-expectation for
A\subseteq B
is faithful – not
only almost faithful – if and only if
A
detects ideals
in
C
for each intermediate
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

A\subseteq C\subseteq B
.

Lemma
2.4
says that
A
separates ideals
in
B
if and only if it “residually detects” ideals in
B
.
The residual version of
Lemma
2.16
says that
A

separates ideals in
B
if and only if the following happens: if

I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
is a restricted ideal and

E^{I}\colon B/BIB\to\mathrm{I}(A/I)
is a pseudo-expectation for the
inclusion
A/I\hookrightarrow B/BIB
, then
E^{I}
is almost
faithful.

In general, it is not practical to check that
all

pseudo-expectations
B\to\mathrm{I}(A)
are faithful or almost
faithful. And the residual version of this statement looks even
more hopeless. There are, however, inclusions with a unique
pseudo-expectation. This
is the case for aperiodic inclusions by
[
29
]
*Theorem 3.6. When the inclusion is even
“residually” aperiodic, then the inclusions

A/I\hookrightarrow B/BIB
have a unique pseudo-expectation for
all
I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
. And if we know pseudo-expectations

E^{I}\colon B/BIB\to\mathrm{I}(A/I)
for all
I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
,
then it becomes possible to check whether they are all (almost)
faithful (see Theorem
2.34
below).

The residual version of
Lemma
2.16
discussed above
uses pseudo-expectations for the inclusion

A/I\hookrightarrow B/BIB
for
I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
. The
following examples show that these need not be closely related to
pseudo-expectations for the original inclusion
A\subseteq B
. In
fact, for inclusions that are not symmetric, even a genuine
conditional expectation
E\colon B\to A
need not “induce” a
conditional expectation
E\colon B/BIB\to A/I
.

Example 2.18
.

Let
B=\mathbb{B}(H)
be the algebra of bounded operators on a
separable Hilbert space
H
, and let
A=\mathbb{K}(H)+1
be the
minimal unitisation of the compacts. The inclusion

A\subseteq B
is symmetric,

\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)=\{0,\mathbb{K}(H),A\}
, and

\mathrm{I}(A)=\mathbb{B}(H)=B
. We may take the identity map as the
pseudo-expectation
E\colon B\to B
for
A\subseteq B
. Let

I\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\mathbb{K}(H)
. Then, on the one hand,
E
descends to
the identity map
E^{I}\colon B/I\to B/I
on the Calkin algebra.
On the other hand, pseudo-expectations for

\mathbb{C}\cong A/I\subseteq B/I
are just states on the Calkin
algebra. It seems that there is no universal way how to produce a
state from the identity map.

Example 2.19

(see
[
27
]
*Examples
2.16 and 7.9)
.

Let
B\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{M}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and consider
the commutative
\textup{C}^{*}
-subalgebra

spanned
by the orthogonal diagonal projections
(P_{00},0)
,

(0,P_{00})
, and
(P_{11},P_{11})
. Let

be any faithful conditional
expectation. For instance,

E\left(\begin{pmatrix}a_{00}&a_{01}\\
a_{10}&a_{11}\end{pmatrix}\oplus\begin{pmatrix}b_{00}&b_{01}\\
b_{10}&b_{11}\end{pmatrix}\right)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}2a_{00}&0\\
0&a_{11}+b_{11}\end{pmatrix}\oplus\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}2b_{00}&0\\
0&a_{11}+b_{11}\end{pmatrix}.

Let

. Then

is a restricted ideal in

. We have

and

E(J)=\{\lambda_{1}(P_{00},0)+\lambda_{2}(P_{11},P_{11})\,{:}\,\mathopen{}\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\in\mathbb{C}\}\not\subseteq I
. Hence

E\colon B\to A
does not factor through a map
B/J\to A/I
.
Note that the inclusion
A\subseteq B
is not symmetric.

Lemma 2.20
.

Let
E\colon B\to A
be a conditional expectation for a
symmetric
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion
A\subseteq B
and let

I\in\mathbb{I}^{B}(A)
. Then

descends to a conditional

,

, for the inclusion

.

Proof.

Let

and put

.
Since

is symmetric we have

.
Thus

because
E

is
A
-bilinear.
Since

always holds, this is equivalent to

. Then

is
well defined.
∎

Many
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras, including section algebras for Fell bundles

,

,

,

,

.

Proposition 2.21
.

Let

be a symmetric
\textup{C}^{*}
-inclusion with a

which is residually

in

is symmetric. Then

separates ideals in

if and only

is residually faithful – that is,

– and

– that is,

for each

. Lemmas

and

imply

∎

2.4.
Supporting and filling families

Let
B^{+}
be the set of positive elements in a

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
. We equip
B^{+}
with the

Cuntz preorder

\precsim
introduced
in
[
12
]
: for

a,b\in B^{+}
, we write
a\precsim b
and say
that
a

supports

b
if, for every
\varepsilon>0
,
there is
x\in B
with
\lVert a-x^{*}bx\rVert<\varepsilon
. We
call
a,b\in B^{+}

Cuntz equivalent
and write

a\approx b
if
a\precsim b
and
b\precsim a
. We call

a,b\in B^{+}

Murray–von Neumann equivalent

and write
a\sim b
if there is
z\in B
with
a=z^{*}z
and

b=zz^{*}
. Both
\sim
and
\approx
are equivalence
relations, and
a\sim b
implies
a\approx b
. In the converse
direction, only a weaker result is true (see

[
21
]
*Lemma 2.3(iv)).
Namely, for
\varepsilon>0
and
a\in B^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, let

(a-\varepsilon)_{+}\in B
be the positive part of

a-\varepsilon\cdot 1\in\mathcal{M}(B)
. Let
bBb
be the
hereditary subalgebra generated by
b
, that is, the closure of

\{bxb\,{:}\,\mathopen{}x\in A\}
. Then
a\precsim b
if and only if
every
\varepsilon
-cut-down of
a
is Murray–von Neumann
equivalent to an element in
bBb
, that is

a\precsim b\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\forall_{\varepsilon>0}\ \exists_{z\in B}\quad(a-\varepsilon)_{+}=z^{*}z\ \text{and}\ zz^{*}\in bBb.

In particular,
a\in bBb
implies
a\precsim b
,

aBa=bBb
implies
a\approx b
, and
a\precsim b
implies

a\in BbB
. A
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
B
is
purely
infinite

[
20
]
if it admits no
characters and
a,b\in B^{+}\setminus\{0\}
satisfy
a\preceq b

if and only if
a\in BbB
.

Definition 2.22

(compare
[
34
]
*Definition 2.39)
.

A subset
\mathcal{F}\subseteq B^{+}

supports
B
if,
for each
b\in B^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, there is

a\in\mathcal{F}\setminus\{0\}
with
a\precsim b
. It

residually supports
B
if, for each
J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
,
the image of
\mathcal{F}
in the quotient
B/J

supports
B/J
.

Lemma 2.23
.

Let
B
be a
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra and let

\mathcal{F}\subseteq B^{+}
.

(1)

Let
\mathcal{F}
support
B
. If
J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
,
then
J\cap\mathcal{F}
supports
J
. So
\mathcal{F}

detects ideals in
B
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{F}
residually support
B
. If

J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
, then
J\cap\mathcal{F}
residually
supports
J
. In addition,
\mathcal{F}
separates ideals
in
B
.

Proof.

We first prove
(1)
. Let

J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
and
b\in J^{+}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

x^{*}bx\in J
for each
x\in B
. If
a\precsim b
for some

a\in B^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, then
a=\lim x_{k}^{*}bx_{k}
for some
sequence
(x_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
in
B
. Therefore, any

a\in\mathcal{F}^{+}\setminus\{0\}
with
a\preceq b
and

b\in J^{+}\setminus\{0\}
belongs to
J
. So
if
\mathcal{F}
supports
B
and
J\neq\{0\}
, then

J\cap\mathcal{F}\neq\{0\}
.

Next we prove
(2)
. Let

J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
and
I\in\mathbb{I}(J)
. Let

q\colon B\to B/I
be the quotient map. For any

b\in q(J)^{+}\setminus\{0\}
, there is
a\in\mathcal{F}
with

0\neq q(a)\precsim b
. The proof above shows that

q(a)\in q(J)
. Since
q(a)\neq 0
, we have

a\in J\setminus I
. Thus
J\cap\mathcal{F}
residually
supports
J
. If
I,J\in\mathbb{I}(B)
and
I\neq J
, then

I\cap J
is a proper ideal of
I
or
J
. Assume, say,
that
I\cap J\subsetneq J
. As we have seen above, there is

a\in(J\cap\mathcal{F})\setminus I
. Hence

I\cap\mathcal{F}\neq J\cap\mathcal{F}
.
∎

.

Let
A\subseteq B
be a
\textup{C}^{*}

\mathcal{F}\subseteq A^{+}

if and only

*Lemma 2.4. The

Example 3.17

(
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras of Fell bundles over groupoids)
.

Retain the notation from

. The
∗
-algebra
associated to the Fell bundle
\mathcal{A}
over the étale
groupoid
G

\mathfrak{S}(G,\mathcal{A})
. It is
the linear span of compactly supported continuous sections

for all bisections

with
a convolution and involution given by

(f*g)(\gamma)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}f(\eta)\cdot g(\eta^{-1}\cdot\gamma),\qquad(f^{*})(\gamma)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}f(\gamma^{-1})^{*}

for all

,
\gamma\in G
. The

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

\textup{C}^{*}(G,\mathcal{A})
is
defined as the maximal
\textup{C}^{*}
-completion of the

∗
-algebra
\mathfrak{S}(G,\mathcal{A})
. This
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
contains

as a
\textup{C}^{*}
-subalgebra. It
is equipped with a generalised expectation

, where

\mathfrak{B}(X,\mathcal{A})
is the
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra of bounded Borel
sections of the
\textup{C}^{*}
-bundle

and

\mathfrak{S}(G,\mathcal{A})
restricts sections
to

. The
reduced section
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
can be
defined as the quotient

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G,\mathcal{A})\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\textup{C}^{*}(G,\mathcal{A})/\mathcal{N}_{E}.

All this follows from

[
28
]
*Proposition 7.10. Let

S\subseteq\Bis(G)
be a unital, inverse subsemigroup of
bisections of
G
which is
wide
in the sense that

\bigcup S=G
and
U\cap V
is a union of bisections
in
S
for all
U,V\in S
. If
\mathcal{A}
is saturated, then the spaces

(A_{U})_{U\in S}
, with operations inherited from

\mathfrak{S}(G,\mathcal{A})
, form an action of
S
on
A
by
Hilbert bimodules, and the associated crossed products are
isomorphic to the corresponding section
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras. In
general, we modify the construction as follows. The convolution
and multiplication from

make

an action by

, and there are natural isomorphisms

\textup{C}^{*}(G,\mathcal{A})\cong A\rtimes\tilde{S},\qquad\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G,\mathcal{A})\cong A\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}\tilde{S}

(see
[
28
]
*Propositions 7.6 and 7.9).
If the groupoid

is Hausdorff, that is, the unit

is closed in

, then the inverse semigroup
action

is closed. The converse implication holds
if
\mathcal{A}
is a Fell line bundle. Fell line bundles over

are equivalent to twists of

. If

is a

the corresponding line bundle, then

and

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G,\Sigma)\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G,\mathcal{L})\cong A\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S
. That is, twisted groupoid

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras are also modelled by inverse semigroup

The

of

, where

The reduced section
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

usually defined through the regular representation, which is the
direct sum of representations

Here

of

\langle f\,|\,\mathopen{}g\rangle\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{s(\gamma)=x}f(\gamma)^{*}g(\gamma)

For

and

, define

\lambda_{x}(f)(g)(\gamma)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sum_{r(\eta)=r(\gamma)}f(\eta)g(\eta^{-1}\gamma)

is

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

is isomorphic to the
completion of
\mathfrak{S}(H,\mathcal{A})
in the reduced norm

\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{r}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sup_{x\in X}{}\lVert\lambda_{x}(f)\rVert
. We
now describe essential algebras in a similar fashion:

Definition 3.23

(

]
*Definition 7.14)

in

that converges towards two different points

in

Proposition 3.24
.

Let

be a continuous Fell bundle over an étale

with locally compact and Hausdorff unit

That is,

\mathfrak{S}(G,\mathcal{A})
in the seminorm

\lVert f\rVert_{\mathrm{ess}}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\sup_{x\in X\setminus D}{}\lVert\lambda_{x}(f)\rVert

For each

and

Thus

\ker\lambda_{x}=\{f\in\textup{C}^{*}(G,\mathcal{A})\,{:}\,\mathopen{}E(f^{*}f)(r(G_{x}))=0\}

*Proposition 7.18.

4.
Exactness of inverse semigroup actions

4.1.
Functoriality

For group actions, the full and reduced crossed products are
functors, and the reduced one preserves injective homomorphisms. We
extend this to inverse semigroup actions by Hilbert bimodules.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

\mathcal{E}=\bigl((\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S},(\mu_{t,u})_{t,u\in S}\bigr)

and

\mathcal{F}=\bigl((\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in S},(\nu_{t,u})_{t,u\in S}\bigr)
be two actions of
S
by Hilbert bimodules on
A

and
B
, respectively. A
homomorphism

\psi

from
\mathcal{E}
to
\mathcal{F}
is a family of linear maps

\psi_{t}\colon\mathcal{E}_{t}\to\mathcal{F}_{t}
for
t\in S
such that for all
t,u\in S
,
\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{t}
,
\eta\in\mathcal{E}_{u}
we have

\psi_{tu}(\mu_{t,u}(\xi\otimes\eta))=\nu_{t,u}(\psi_{t}(\xi)\otimes\psi_{u}(\eta)),\,\,\langle\psi_{t}(\xi)\,|\,\mathopen{}\psi_{t}(\eta)\rangle=\psi_{1}(\langle\xi\,|\,\mathopen{}\eta\rangle),\,\,\langle\!\langle\psi_{t}(\xi)\,|\,\mathopen{}\psi_{t}(\eta)\rangle\!\rangle=\psi_{1}(\langle\!\langle\xi\,|\,\mathopen{}\eta\rangle\!\rangle).

The maps
\psi_{t}
are always contractive. We call
\psi

injective
if
\psi_{1}
is injective; then the
maps
\psi_{t}
are isometric for all
t\in S
. We
call
\psi
an

if all
\psi_{t}
are
isomorphisms.

We use the superscripts

distinguish between objects defined for the actions
{\mathcal{E}}

and
\mathcal{F}

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\psi
be a homomorphism from an action

\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}

\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\in S}

∗
-homomorphism

\xi\in\mathcal{E}_{t}
,

t\in S

\psi

inclusions maps on
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{F}

\psi\rtimes S

∗

(1)

\psi\rtimes S

comutes

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S}

(resp.

(2)

\psi\rtimes_{\mathrm{alg}}S

\psi_{1}^{\prime\prime}

Proof.

The Hilbert bimodules
\mathcal{F}_{t}

in

in

*Proposition 2.9. This restricts

∗

Exact actions

Proposition 4.15
.

Let
\mathcal{E}

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra

Let

Proof.

Note that
\iota(I_{1,t}^{\mathcal{E}I})=I_{1,t}^{\mathcal{E}}\cdot I=\iota(s(\mathcal{E}_{t}I))\cdot I_{1,t}^{\mathcal{E}}

and

\kappa(I_{1,t}^{\mathcal{E}})=I_{1,t}^{{\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}I}}

. Hence

and

4.2

by the last part of Remark

Thus, by Proposition
4.2
, not only the
∗
-homomorphisms

\kappa\rtimes S

The maps

and

The composite maps

The essential crossed product is not functorial (see

Only the quotient maps cause extra problems:

Lemma 4.20
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an action of
S
by Hilbert bimodules on a

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
and let
I\in\mathbb{I}^{\mathcal{E}}(A)
.
The injective homomorphism
\iota
from
\mathcal{E}|_{I}

into
\mathcal{E}
induces an injective
∗
-homomorphism

\iota\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S\colon I\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S\to A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S

Its image is the ideal in
A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S
generated by

Proof.

If
J
is an essential ideal in
I
, then

is an essential ideal in

. The obvious inclusions

\mathcal{M}(J)\to\mathcal{M}(J\oplus I^{\bot})
for the essential ideals

induce a natural isomorphism from
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)

onto an ideal in
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)
. Let

EL_{I}\colon I\rtimes S\to\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)
be the canonical
essential expectation. We are going to prove below that the
following diagram commutes:

(4.21)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I\rtimes S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\rtimes S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(I)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{loc}}(A)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota\rtimes S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces EL_{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces EL}

Then

\mathcal{N}_{EL}\cap\iota\rtimes S(I\rtimes S)=\iota\rtimes S(\mathcal{N}_{EL_{I}})
because
\iota\rtimes S(I\rtimes S)
is an
ideal in

. This, in turn, implies that the

\iota\rtimes S
factors through an
injective homomorphism

\iota\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S\colon I\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S=I\rtimes S/\mathcal{N}_{EL_{I}}\to A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S=A\rtimes S/\mathcal{N}_{EL}
.

To check (

), let

. If

I_{t,1}=\overline{\sum_{v\leq t,1}s(\mathcal{E}_{v})}
then

I_{t,1}I=\overline{\sum_{v\leq t,1}s(\mathcal{E}_{v}I)}
and the

(defined in (

)) to

coincides with the corresponding map defined for the restricted

. Hence for each

the element

acts on

).

The following are equivalent:

(2)

We illustrate by an example what can go wrong with the exactness of
essential crossed products. Our example is closely related to the
Reeb foliation or, more precisely, to its restriction to a
transversal.

Example 4.27
.

Let
\vartheta\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a homeomorphism with

\vartheta(t)=t
for
t\leq 0
and
\vartheta(t)>t
for
all
t>0
. Let
G
be the germ groupoid of the transformation
groupoid
\mathbb{R}\times_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}
. We claim that

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G)\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G)
. To see this, we use that the
restrictions of
G
to
[0,\infty)
and
(-\infty,0)
are
Hausdorff. Indeed,
G|_{(0,\infty)}
is the Hausdorff
transformation groupoid
(0,\infty)\rtimes_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}

because
\mathbb{Z}
acts freely (and properly) on
(0,\infty)
.
Similarly,
G|_{[0,\infty)}
is the Hausdorff transformation
groupoid
[0,\infty)\rtimes_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}
; the action of
\mathbb{Z}

on
[0,\infty)
fixes
0
, but the germ of any
n\in\mathbb{Z}

at
0
is nontrivial because
\vartheta^{n}
acts nontrivially
on
(0,\infty)
. Therefore, the support of any nonzero element
of
\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G)
must intersect
[0,\infty)
and

(-\infty,0)
in relatively open subsets. Hence the support
cannot be meagre, and this proves our claim. This equality of
reduced and essential groupoid
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras for
G
is
not inherited by the restriction to the closed invariant
subset
(-\infty,0]
. Indeed, the restriction of
G
to

(-\infty,0]
is the non-Hausdorff group bundle with trivial
fibre over
(-\infty,0)
and the fibre
\mathbb{Z}
at
0
(see

[
28
]
*Example 4.7 and
Example
4.22
). In this case, the full and
reduced groupoid
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebras are obtained by gluing
together
\textup{C}_{0}((-\infty,0))
and the fibre
\textup{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})

at
0
. However,

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{(-\infty,0]})=\textup{C}_{0}((-\infty,0])
, so

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{(-\infty,0]})\not\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{(-\infty,0]}).

Since the essential and reduced crossed products coincide for

G
and
G|_{(0,\infty)}
, but not for
G|_{(-\infty,0]}
,
the following sequence of essential crossed products exists, but fails to be
exact:

0\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{(0,\infty)})\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G)\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{(-\infty,0]})\to 0.

This is one way how essential crossed products may fail to be
exact. The restriction
G|_{\{0\}}
is simply the group
\mathbb{Z}
.
So

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{\{0\}})=\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{\{0\}})\cong\textup{C}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
.
The restriction
∗
-homomorphism

\textup{C}^{*}(G|_{(-\infty,0]})\to\textup{C}^{*}(G|_{\{0\}})
does not descend
to the essential crossed products. That is, there is no canonical
map from
\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{(-\infty,0]})
to

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G|_{\{0\}})
. This is the second way how essential
crossed products may fail to be exact.

Since
G|_{[0,\infty)}\cong[0,\infty)\rtimes_{\vartheta}\mathbb{Z}
, it
follows that

\textup{C}^{*}(G|_{[0,\infty)})\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{[0,\infty)})
. By a
diagram, it follows that the sequence

0\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{(-\infty,0)})\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G)\to\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{[0,\infty)})\to 0

is exact. Since also

\textup{C}^{*}(G|_{(-\infty,0)})\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{(-\infty,0)})
, the
Five Lemma shows that
\textup{C}^{*}(G)\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G)
. A similar
argument shows
\textup{C}^{*}(G|_{U})\cong\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G|_{U})
for all
locally closed
G
-invariant subsets
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
.
Therefore,
G
is inner exact.

Remark 4.29

By (

Lemma 4.30
.

Let

be an

-action by Hilbert bimodules on a

-algebra

and let

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Lambda^{A/I}}

\Lambda^{A/I}
is injective, then

\ker(\kappa\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S)=\Lambda^{A}\left(\ker(\kappa\rtimes S)\right)=\Lambda^{A}\left(\iota\rtimes S(I\rtimes S)\right)=\iota\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S(I\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S).

Hence

I\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S\rightarrowtail A\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S\twoheadrightarrow A/I\rtimes_{\mathrm{r}}S

is exact. In general, if this sequence is exact, the Snake Lemma
from homological algebra yields a short exact sequence

\ker(\Lambda^{I})\rightarrowtail\ker(\Lambda^{A})\twoheadrightarrow\ker(\Lambda^{A/I}).

Hence
\ker(\Lambda^{A})=0
if and only if
\ker(\Lambda^{I})=0

and
\ker(\Lambda^{A/I})=0
.

Ideals and pure infiniteness for inverse semigroup crossed

In this section we present efficient criteria for separation of

5.1.
Residual aperiodicity and ideal structure

Let
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}
be an action of a unital inverse
semigroup
S
by Hilbert bimodules on a
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
A
.

Definition 5.1

(
[
28
]
*Definition 6.1)
.

The action
\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}
is
aperiodic
if the
Hilbert

-bimodules
\mathcal{E}_{t}\cdot I_{1,t}^{\bot}
are
aperiodic for all
t\in S
, where
I_{1,t}
is defined
in (
3.7
).

Proposition 5.2

(
[
28
]
*Proposition 6.3)
.

Let
B
be an
S
-graded
\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra with a grading

\mathcal{E}=(\mathcal{E}_{t})_{t\in S}
. Let
A\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\mathcal{E}_{1}\subseteq B

and turn
\mathcal{E}
into an
S
-action on

. If this
action is aperiodic, then the inclusion
A\subseteq B
is
aperiodic. The converse holds if the grading on
B
is
topological, that is, the canonical quotient map

A\rtimes S\twoheadrightarrow A\rtimes_{\mathrm{ess}}S
factors through

.

Definition 5.3

(
[
28
]
*Definition 2.20)
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid and
X\subseteq G
its unit
space. The
isotropy group
of a point

is

G(x)\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}s^{-1}(x)\cap r^{-1}(x)\subseteq G
. We
call

topologically free
if, for every open

, the set

\{x\in X\,{:}\,\mathopen{}G(x)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
has empty
interior.

Remark 5.4
.

A groupoid

is
effective
if any open subset

U\subseteq G
with

is contained in
X
.
Effective groupoids are topologically free. The converse
implication holds if

is closed in

, but not in
general.

Aperiodicity is related in
[
25
,
29
,
28
]
to topological freeness and several
other conditions. We now introduce residual versions of
aperiodicity and topological freeness.

Definition 5.5
.

The action
\mathcal{E}
is
residually aperiodic
if, for each

I\in\mathbb{I}^{\mathcal{E}}(A)
, the restricted action
\mathcal{E}|_{A/I}

is aperiodic.

Definition 5.6
.

An étale groupoid

with unit space

is

residually topologically free
if, for each nonempty closed

G
-invariant subset

, the restricted
groupoid
r^{-1}(Y)=s^{-1}(Y)
is topologically free.

The following lemma may help to show that a transformation groupoid
is (residually) topologically free.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G
be an étale groupoid. Let
X
and
Y
be
topological spaces with
G
-actions and let

f\colon X\to Y
be a continuous
G
-equivariant map.

(1)

if
Y
is closed in
G\ltimes Y
, then
X
is closed in
G\ltimes X

(2)

if
f\colon X\to Y
is open and
G\ltimes Y
is topologically free, then so is

G\ltimes X
.

(3)

if
f\colon X\to Y
is open, maps closed
G
-invariant sets to closed sets, and
G\ltimes Y
is residually topologically free, then so is

G\ltimes X
.

Proof.

The map
f
induces a continuous groupoid homomorphism

f_{*}\colon G\ltimes X\to G\ltimes Y
such that
f_{*}^{-1}(Y)=X
. So

(1)
follows. The map
f_{*}
is open if and only if
f
is open.
Assume this.
To show
(2)
assume that
G\ltimes Y
is topologically free and let

U\subseteq(G\ltimes X)\setminus X
. Then
f_{*}(U)
is open
and contained in
(G\ltimes Y)\setminus Y
. Let

I_{U}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\{x\in X\,{:}\,\mathopen{}G(x)\cap U\neq\emptyset\}
and

I_{f^{*}(U)}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}\{y\in Y\,{:}\,\mathopen{}G(y)\cap f_{*}(U)\neq\emptyset\}
. We claim that
f(I_{U})=I_{f^{*}(U)}
. To see
this, let
x\in I_{U}
. Then there is an arrow
(x,g,x)\in U
;
here
g\in G
is such that
\varrho(x)=s(g)
and

g\cdot x=x
. Since
f
is
G
-equivariant, then

(f(x),g,f(x))\in f_{*}(U)
. This witnesses that

f(x)\in I_{f^{*}(U)}
. Since
G\ltimes Y
is topologically
free,
I_{f^{*}(U)}
has empty interior. Since
f
is open, the
preimage of
I_{f^{*}(U)}
in
X
has empty interior as well. It follows
that
I_{U}
has empty interior. This witnesses
that
G\ltimes X
is topologically free and proves
(2)
.

Finally, for any
G
-invariant set
D\subseteq X
the set
f(D)
is
G
-invariant. If we assume
f(D)
is closed in
Y
and
G\ltimes Y
is residually topologically free,
then
G\ltimes f(D)
is topologically free. Since the restriction of
f_{*}
to
G\ltimes D
is a continuous open map onto
G\ltimes f(D)
,

(2)
implies that
G\ltimes D
is topologically free. This
proves
(3)
.
∎

\{x\in X\,{:}\,\mathopen{}(a_{i}^{*}a_{i})(x)>0\}=s(U_{i})
, the equalities

a=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}
and

a=\sum_{i=n+1}^{n+m}a_{i}^{*}a_{i}
imply

V=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}s(U_{i})
and

V=\bigsqcup_{i=n+1}^{n+m}s(U_{i})
. Hence the family

U_{i}\in\Bis(G)
for
1\leq i\leq n+m
has all the desired
properties, except that
U_{i}
need not be compact. However,
since
G
is ample, every
U_{i}
is a union of some compact
open bisections. Since
V
is compact and

V=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}s(U_{i})=\bigsqcup_{i=n+1}^{n+m}s(U_{i})
, we may, in fact, replace each
U_{i}
for

1\leq i\leq n+m
by a finite union of compact open bisections.
This gives a compact open bisection.

Conversely, let
U_{i}\subseteq G
for
1\leq i\leq n+m
be a
family of bisection as in Definition
5.27
. Let

S^{\prime}\subseteq\Bis(G)
be the family of open compact bisections
that trivialise the twist, that is, the restrictions of the
associated line bundle over
G
to sets in
S^{\prime}
are trivial.
Note that
S^{\prime}
forms an inverse semigroup and a basis for the
topology of
G
; this holds for the family of all open
bisections that trivialise the twist, by the proof of

[
8
]
*Theorem 7.2, and for the
family of all compact open bisections because
G
is ample.
Since

V=\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{n}s(U_{i})=\bigsqcup_{j=n+1}^{n+m}s(U_{j})

is compact, for each
i=1,\dotsc,n
we may find a finite family
of sets
(U_{i,j})_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\subseteq S^{\prime}
such that

\bigcup_{j=1}^{n_{i}}U_{i,j}\subseteq U_{i}
and

V=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\bigcup_{j=1}^{n_{i}}s(U_{i,j})
. Since the
bisections
(U_{i,j})_{j=1}^{n_{i}}\subseteq U_{i}
are closed and
open, we may arrange that the sets
(s(U_{i,j}))_{j=1}^{n_{i}}

are pairwise disjoint. Then the sets

(U_{i,j})_{i=1,j=1}^{n,n_{i}}
are pairwise disjoint, and since

\bigcup_{j=1}^{n_{i}}U_{i,j}\subseteq U_{i}
, for

i=1,\dotsc,n
, also
(r(U_{i,j}))_{i=1,j=1}^{n,n_{i}}
are
pairwise disjoint. We put
a_{i,j}\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}1_{U_{i,j}}
, for

i=1,\dots,n
,
j=1,\dotsc,n_{i}
. By the choice of bisections
in
S^{\prime}
, we may treat
a_{i,j}
as an element of the space

\textup{C}_{\textup{c}}(U_{ij})
of sections of the line bundle over
G
.
By the construction of the Fell bundle over
\widehat{A}\rtimes S
,
by passing if necessary to smaller sets, we may assume that each
space
\textup{C}_{\textup{c}}(U_{i,j})
is contained in
\mathcal{E}_{t_{ij}}
for
some
t_{ij}\in S
. Hence
a_{i,j}\in\mathcal{E}_{t_{ij}}
for all

i,j
. Using the Fell bundle structure, we get

\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{n,n_{i}}a_{i,j}^{*}\cdot a_{i,j}=\sum_{i=1,j=1}^{n,n_{i}}1_{s(U_{i,j})}=1_{V}=a.

Similarly, we get

\sum_{i=n+1,j=1}^{n+m,n_{i}}a_{i,j}^{*}\cdot a_{i,j}=a
and

a_{i,j}^{*}a_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}=0
for all
(i,j)\neq(i^{\prime},j^{\prime})
. Hence

a
is
\mathcal{E}
-paradoxical.
∎

Corollary 5.29
.

Let
(G,\Sigma)
be an essentially exact twisted groupoid
where
G
is ample and residually topologically free with
locally compact Hausdorff
X\mathrel{\vcentcolon=}G^{0}
. If every compact
open subset of
X
is
(2,1)
-paradoxical, then the essential

\textup{C}^{*}
-algebra
\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,\Sigma)
is purely infinite

(
and has the ideal property
)
.

Proof.

View
\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,\Sigma)
as the essential crossed product
by an inverse semigroup action
\mathcal{E}
on
\textup{C}_{0}(X)
as in
Examples
3.21

and
3.22
. The assertion follows
from Proposition
5.28
and
Corollary
5.22
.
∎

Remark 5.30
.

When
G
is Hausdorff, then

\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{ess}}(G,\Sigma)=\textup{C}^{*}_{\mathrm{r}}(G,\Sigma)
and

(G,\Sigma)
is essentially exact if and only if it is inner
exact. Thus Corollary
5.29

generalises the pure infiniteness criteria in

[
6
,
41
]
, where the
authors considered Hausdorff ample groupoids without a twist.
They proved, in addition, that if the type semigroup associated
to
G
is almost unperforated, then the implication in
Corollary
5.29
may be reversed. We
will generalise this and some other results of
Ma
[
36
]
to étale twisted groupoids
in the forthcoming paper
[
30
]
.

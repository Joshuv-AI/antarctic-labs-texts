The Cassels-Tate pairing on 2-Selmer groups of elliptic curves Thanks: HS was supported by Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG)-Grant STO 299/17-1 during this research.
Himanshu Shukla, Michael Stoll

Abstract
. We explicitly compute the Cassels-Tate pairing on the 2-Selmer group of an elliptic curve using the Albanese-Albanese definition of the pairing given by Poonen and Stoll. This leads to a new proof that a pairing defined by Cassels on the 2-Selmer groups of elliptic curves agrees with the Cassels-Tate pairing.



1  Introduction

Let
E
be an elliptic curve over an algebraic number field
K
. Then
according to the Mordell-Weil theorem, the set
E(K)
of
K
-rational
points on
E
is a finitely generated abelian group, so that

E(K)\cong E(K)_{{\mathrm{tors}}}\oplus\mathbf{Z}^{r_{E/K}}

with finite torsion group
E(K)_{{\mathrm{tors}}}
and an integer
r_{E/K}\geq 0
, which
is called the

or the
Mordell-Weil rank
of
E/K
.
It is then a natural problem to determine the group structure
of
E(K)
for a given elliptic curve
E/K
.
In practice, determining the torsion subgroup

for

is not hard
(see
[
11
]
*Theorem VII.3.4). On the other hand, so far there
is no algorithm known that provably determines the rank

By exhibiting independent points in

It is also possible to obtain upper bounds on

this gives, for each

It is a standard conjecture that

, is at most

;
in this case the algebraic rank agrees with the analytic rank (this is
the weak Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture). But in general, the
conjecture is wide open.

It is easy to see that the natural map

This shows that the image of

in

is contained

. So we may be able to improve our bound on the rank
if we can determine this image. This can be done by computing the

-Selmer

which is perfect after dividing out the infinitely
divisible subgroup (which conjecturally is trivial; see above).
We will use the abbreviation CTP to refer to the pairing.
By pull-back, we obtain a pairing on
\mathrm{S}^{(n)}(E/K)
, which we will also call CTP.
The properties of the CTP imply that the image of

is the same
as the (left or right) kernel of the CTP on
\mathrm{S}^{(n)}(E/K)
.
So if we can compute the CTP on the

can get a bound on

that is equivalent to the bound
obtained from the
n^{2}
-Selmer group.

See
[
7
]
for detailed information on the CTP (which can
be defined more generally on
\Sha(A/K)\times\Sha(A^{\vee}/K)
, where

A/K
is an abelian variety with dual abelian variety
A^{\vee}
).
In particular, this paper provides four different definitions of the
pairing (and shows that they are all equivalent), among them
the
Weil-pairing
definition
[
7
]
*§12.2,
the
homogeneous space definition

[
7
]
*§3.1
and the
Albanese-Albanese definition

[
7
]
*§12.1.

Cassels
[
1
]
defined a pairing on

\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)
using explicit models for the

2
-coverings representing elements of the
2
-Selmer group, which was
shown to be the same as the CTP in
[
4
]
.
This pairing of Cassels can be seen as lying somewhere in between
the Weil-pairing and the homogeneous space definitions. Later Cassels’s
approach was generalized by Swinnerton-Dyer
[
13
]
,
Fisher and Newton
[
3
]
and Fisher and van Beek
[
14
]

to compute the CTP on
\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(2^{m})}(E/K)
,

\mathrm{S}^{(3)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(3)}(E/K)
and for Selmer groups of
isogenies of odd prime degrees, respectively.
Using an approach of Donnelly based on the
homogeneous space definition of the CTP,
Fisher
[
5
]
has given another way of computing the CTP
on
\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)
. Fisher also has an approach based
on the homogeneous space definition for
\mathrm{S}^{(3)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(3)}(E/K)
(unpublished).

The aim of this article is to compute the CTP on
\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)\times\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E/K)

using the Albanese-Albanese definition. It was remarked in
[
7
]
that designing
an algorithm to compute the CTP on the Selmer groups associated to the Jacobian of a curve
via the Albanese-Albanese definition may be easier than via the homogeneous space
or Weil pairing definitions, since it involves
dealing only with divisors on the curve; to the best of our knowledge this article is
the first attempt to do so.

We introduce two technical innovations. The first is to extend the two
Galois-equivariant pairings (between principal divisors and degree zero
divisors) used in the Albanese-Albanese definition to make them everywhere
defined, rather than only on divisors with disjoint support. This helps us
avoid some complications which arise from the assumption used in the original
definition, which demands that the lifts of certain elements of
{\mathrm{Pic}\,}^{0}(E)
to

{\mathrm{Div}}^{0}(E)
have disjoint support.
The second innovation is to write an element
a\in\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E)
as a
sum of quantities in
{\mathrm{H}}^{1}(K,E(\bar{K})[2])
not necessarily belonging
to
\mathrm{S}^{(2)}(E)
. Using this we construct some maps (that may depend
on choices made) that mimic the construction of the CTP, and show that
the CTP is the sum of these functions (see Section
5.1
).
By making the choices carefully, we obtain a fairly simple proof of
the equivalence of the CTP with the pairing defined by Cassels in
[
1
]
.

Building on the methods of this article, the
first author has developed an algorithm to compute the CTP on

\mathrm{S}^{2}(J)\times\mathrm{S}^{2}(J)
, where
J
is the Jacobian
of a hyperelliptic curve having an odd-degree model.
This will be described in a forthcoming article
[
10
]
.

The organization of the paper is as follows.
Sections 2 and 3 provide the necessary notations and preliminaries.
In Section 4, we compute the pairing in a special case, and then show
in Section 5 that the general case follows from the special case.
In the same section we also show that the formula for the
CTP obtained using the Albanese-Albanese definition agrees with
the definition given by Cassels.

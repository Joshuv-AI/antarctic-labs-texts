Differential and linear integral operators representing polynomial covariance commutation relations in C ∞ C^{\infty}
Domingos Djinja, Sergei Silvestrov, Alex Behakanira Tumwesigye

Abstract
Representations of polynomial covariance commutation relations by pairs of linear integral and differential operators are constructed in the space of infinitely continuously differentiable functions. Representations of polynomial covariance commutation relations by pairs consisting of a differential and linear integral operator are considered including conditions on kernels and coefficients of operators, and examples. Lemma 1

Let

be a normed vector space. Let

be a continuous linear functional defined on
E

M\subseteq E

M
))=
E
, where cl
(\Pi)
is the closure of the set
\Pi
. If
H(x)=0
for all

then

H(x)=0
for all
x\in E
.

Proof

Let
x\in
span
(M)
, such that
x=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}x_{i}
, where
x_{i}\in M
,
\alpha_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
. Then, by using linearity of

we have

H(x)=H\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}x_{i}\right)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\alpha_{i}H(x_{i})=0.

Then
H(x)=0
for all
x\in
span(
M
). Suppose that

, and since cl(span
(M)
)
=E
, there exist

) such that
y_{n}\to y
, when

, that is,

. Therefore, by using linearity and continuity of

Hence,

for all

Theorem 3.1

Consider linear operators

\displaystyle(Ax)(t)=\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,s)x(s)ds,\quad(Bx)(t)=b(t)\frac{dx}{dt},

\displaystyle k_{0}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(t,s),\quad k_{1}(t,s)=\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,\tau)k(\tau,s)d\tau,

\displaystyle k_{m}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,\tau)k_{m-1}(\tau,s)d\tau,\quad m=1,\ldots,n,

\displaystyle F_{m}(k(t,s))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\delta_{j}k_{j-1}(t,s),\quad m=1,\ldots,n.

\displaystyle k(t,\beta)b(\beta)x(\beta)-k(t,\alpha)b(\alpha)x(\alpha)-\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial[b(s)k(t,s)]}{\partial s}x(s)ds

\displaystyle=\delta_{0}b(t)x^{\prime}(t)+b(t)\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial F_{n}(k(t,s))}{\partial t}x(s)ds.

(3)

If

\displaystyle-\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial[b(s)k(t,s)]}{\partial s}x(s)ds=\delta_{0}b(t)x^{\prime}(t)+b(t)\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial F_{n}(k(t,s))}{\partial t}x(s)ds.

holds if and only if

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial[b(s)k(t,s)]}{\partial s}=b(t)\frac{\partial F_{n}(k(t,s))}{\partial t},\quad(t,s)\in[\alpha,\beta]^{2}.

(5)

where

\displaystyle k_{1}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,\tau)k(\tau,s)d\tau,\quad k_{0}(t,s)=k(t,s),

\displaystyle k_{n}(t,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,\tau)k_{n-1}(\tau,s)d\tau,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Thus we have

\displaystyle(F(A)x)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}x(t)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}(A^{j}x)(t)=\delta_{0}x(t)+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k_{j-1}(t,s)x(s)ds

\displaystyle\delta_{0}x(t)+\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}F_{n}(k(t,s))x(s)ds,

where

\displaystyle F_{n}(k(t,s))=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}k_{j-1}(t,s),\quad n\in\mathbb{N}

and

\displaystyle(BF(A)x)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{0}b(t)x^{\prime}(t)+b(t)\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial F_{n}(k(t,s))}{\partial t}x(s)ds

\displaystyle(ABx)(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,s)(Bx)(s)ds=\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}k(t,s)b(s)x^{\prime}(s)ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle k(t,\beta)b(\beta)x(\beta)-k(t,\alpha)b(\alpha)x(\alpha)-\int\limits_{\alpha}^{\beta}\frac{\partial[b(s)k(t,s)]}{\partial s}x(s)ds.

Then, for

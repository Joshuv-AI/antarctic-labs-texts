Event-triggered Observability: A Set-membership Perspective
Kaikai Zheng, Dawei Shi, Tongwen Chen

Abstract
This work attempts to discuss the observability of linear time-invariant systems with event-triggered measurements. A new notion of observability, namely, ϵ \epsilon -observability is defined with parameter ϵ \epsilon , which relates to the worst-case performance of inferring the initial state based on not only the received measurement but also the implicit information in the event-triggering conditions at no-event instants. A criterion is developed to test the proposed ϵ \epsilon -observability of discrete-time linear systems, based on which an iterative event-triggered set-membership observer is designed to evaluate a set containing all possible values of the state. The proposed set-membership observer is designed as the outer approximation of the ellipsoids predicted based on previous state estimates and the ellipsoids inferred by fusing the received measurement and communication conditions, which is optimal in the sense of trace at each step and is proved to be asymptotically bounded. The efficiency of the proposed event-triggered set-membership state observer is verified by numerical experiments.



keywords
Observability, Event-triggered state estimation, Set-membership filtering.

Introduction

With the increase of size and functionality, networked control systems are limited by communication and energy resources
(
Zhang et al. 2001
;
Hespanha et al. 2007
;
Gupta and Chow 2009
;
Zhang et al. 2015
)
. Event-triggered communication has received considerable attention as an effective method to mitigate the resource constraints by actively select the most valuable information according to predefined event-triggering conditions
(
Åarzén 1999
;
Astrom and Bernhardsson 2002
;
Heemels et al. 2012
)
.

The scope of this work belongs to event-triggered state estimation, which aims to analyze the capacity of reconstructing the state of systems with event-triggered sensor measurements
(
Shi et al. 2016b
)
. In event-triggered state estimation, two related agents are usually considered in networked control systems with event-triggered communication channels: one is an intelligent sensor that observes the state periodically and makes decisions as to whether to transmit the state, and the other one is the event-triggered state estimator which attempts to reconstruct the state sequentially with received information
(
Imer and Basar 2005
;
Sijs et al. 2013
;
Shi et al. 2016b
)
. Minimum mean square error (MMSE) estimators for Gaussian systems were designed in earlier investigations
(
Li et al. 2010
)
. In
Wu et al. 2012
, the measurements were sent to the observer when the system innovation was beyond a pre-set threshold, and a closed-form approximate MMSE estimator was developed.
Shi et al. 2014
proposed an optimal sensor fusion approach for multiple sensors with separate event-triggering conditions under a truncated Gaussian assumption of the conditional distribution of the state. To overcome the issue caused by the Gaussian assumption of the conditional distribution, stochastic event-triggering mechanisms were designed, and corresponding event-triggered state estimators were also developed
(
Wu et al. 2014
;
Han et al. 2015
;
Han et al. 2017
)
. Later investigations considered event-triggered communication channels with packet dropout, and the nonexistence of event-based triggers that preserve Gaussian state
(
Kung et al. 2017
)
was proved, which promoted the development of event-triggered state estimation using hidden Markov models. For hidden Markov models, closed-form representations for estimators and average sensor-to-estimator communication rates were obtained using the reference measure approach
(
Shi et al. 2016a
;
Chen et al. 2017
)
for lossy communication channels. More recently, the effect of model uncertainty was considered and risk-sensitive event-triggered filtering
(
Xu et al. 2019
;
Huang et al. 2019
)
and smoothing
(
Cheng et al. 2021
)
approaches were developed by optimizing exponential objective functions.

In networked control systems with event-triggered communication channels, sensor measurements arrive non-uniformly. To deal with this issue, several interesting investigations for the observability of linear systems with non-uniform measurements exist in the literature
(
Xu et al. 2008
;
Li et al. 2011
;
Ding et al. 2009
)
.
For instance, a state reconstruction problem was considered for non-uniform samplings in
Xu et al. 2008
for systems with binary-valued sensors. Utilizing the threshold and crossing time of the sensor, the system was proved to be observable with
n
binary-sensor switching points for
n
th order observable continuous linear time-invariant (LTI) systems. A similar approach was adopted in
Li et al. 2011
, where authors proved that if the original system is observable, the non-uniform sampled system will be observable provided that the sampling density is higher than some critical frequency, independent of the actual time sequences. In
Ding et al. 2009
, the control input and the measurement output were modeled to be non-uniform through a zero-order hold from continuous-time linear systems, and the related observability issues were studied. The authors in
Ding et al. 2009
pointed out that the designed non-uniform sampling pattern does not change the observability of linear systems even if the sampling interval was pathological. In
Ben Nasser et al. 2021
, the observability problem for linear dynamic systems on non-uniform time domains was considered, and the duality relationship between observability and reachability was established. It is interesting to note that the observability problems considered in aforementioned investigations only considered the received measurements for state reconstruction with non-uniform sampled sensor measurements. In event-triggered state estimation, not receiving the exact sensor measurement value at a no-event instance does not equivalently mean that nothing can be inferred for the current measurement, as important information can be provided by exploiting the event-triggering conditions
(
Wu et al. 2012
;
Shi et al. 2014
;
Han et al. 2015
)
. This fact, however, still remain unexplored in existing studies, which motivates the investigation of this work.

In this paper, we attempt to discuss the worst-case performance of estimating the initial state of LTI systems in observability analysis for event-triggered state estimation, namely, determining a bounded region that contains the initial state, which is different from the observability aforementioned ensuring unique determination of the initial state. In order to utilize the set-valued information, a set-membership filtering approach by
Witsenhausen 1968
is used to characterize a set of all possible values of the state compatible with the measurement of outputs. We consider LTI systems with bounded process disturbance and measurement noise, and the measurements of systems are sent to an estimator through a send-on-delta event-triggered communication channel. The estimator is designed using the set-membership approach
(
Durieu et al. 2001
;
Yang and Li 2009
;
Li et al. 2020
)
to obtain optimal ellipsoid approximations of system states. The main contribution of this paper is summarized as follows:

1.

A novel
\epsilon
-observability is defined for discrete-time LTI systems, which can evaluate how accurate we can reconstruct the initial state under the event-triggered communication strategy. The proposed
\epsilon
-observability notion focuses on the possible range determined by received measurements and information in event-triggering mechanisms at the no-event instants.

2.

A criterion is developed using a set-membership approach for the proposed
\epsilon
-observability notion, which can be used for observability analysis and observer design. A calculation approach to determinate an upper bound of parameter
\epsilon
is provided, which can evaluate the worst-case performance of determining the initial state for LTI systems with event-triggered communication channels.

3.

An iterative set-membership observer is designed based on the proposed
\epsilon
-observability notion for LTI systems, which consists of predictive ellipsoids inferred from the last state, ellipsoids from measurement and event-triggering conditions, and the optimal outer approximation of intersection of such two ellipsoids in the sense of trace at each step. The estimated ellipsoids are proved to be asymptotically bounded, which is verified by several numerical examples.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
presents the system description, the definition of
\epsilon
-observability, and the problem formulation. A criterion to judge the
\epsilon
-observability of LTI systems and an approach to calculate an upper bound of the parameter
\epsilon
are provided in Section
3
. Based on the proposed
\epsilon
-observability and the developed criterion, an iterative set-membership observer is designed in Section
4
. Implementation issues and numerical verification of the results are presented in Section
5
, followed by some conclusions in Section
6
.

2
Problem Formulation

Conseder the following LTI system with a single output

\begin{split}{\bm{x}}_{k}&=A{\bm{x}}_{k-1}+{\bm{w}}_{k-1},\\
y_{k}&=C{\bm{x}}_{k}+v_{k},\end{split}

(1)

where
{\bm{x}}_{k}=[x_{k}^{1},\ldots,x_{k}^{n}]^{\rm T}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the state,
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
is the measurement, and
A\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n},C\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times n}
are system parameters. The process disturbance
{\bm{w}}_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and measurement noise
v_{k}\in\mathbb{R}
are assumed to be two series of random variables satisfying

\displaystyle{\bm{w}}_{k}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{W}:=\{{\bm{w}}:{\bm{w}}^{\rm T}Q^{-1}{\bm{w}}\leq 1\}=\mathcal{E}(0,Q),

(2)

\displaystyle{v}_{k}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{V}:=\{{v}:{v}^{\rm T}R^{-1}{v}\leq 1\}=\mathcal{E}(0,R),

(3)

with
Q
and
R
being real symmetric positive definite matrices and
\mathcal{E}(\cdot,\cdot)
being an ellipsoid. Note that the results obtained in this work can be extended to systems with multiple outputs, which is stated at Remark
13
in Section
4
.

In this work, the measurement
y_{k}
is transmitted by a deterministic event-triggered mechanism described as

\gamma_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1,&{\text{if}}~(y_{k}-y_{\tau k})^{\rm T}\Gamma^{-1}(y_{k}-y_{\tau k})>1,\\
0,&{\text{if}}~(y_{k}-y_{\tau k})^{\rm T}\Gamma^{-1}(y_{k}-y_{\tau k})\leq 1,\end{array}\right.

(4)

where
\gamma_{k}=1
denotes that the measurement
y_{k}
is transmitted to the observer and
\gamma_{k}=0
the otherwise,
\Gamma>0
and
y_{\tau k}
are known parameters. For send-on-delta mechanism
(
Miskowicz 2006
;
Shi et al. 2014
)
,
y_{\tau k}
is the previously transmitted measurement.

Let
\mathcal{Y}_{ok}
denotes the set-valued information for the observer inferred based on the received measurement and event-triggering condition, which satisfies

\mathcal{Y}_{ok}:=\{y:(y-y_{\tau k})^{\rm T}M_{k}^{-1}(y-y_{\tau k})\leq 1\}.

(5)

The matrix
M_{k}
describes the information of the measurement which can be write as

M_{k}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\Gamma,&\text{if}~\gamma_{k}=0,\\
\Gamma_{e},&\text{if}~\gamma_{k}=1,\end{array}\right.

(6)

where
\Gamma_{e}
is the measurement or transmission error. For example, if the measurement is transmitted by a digital wireless communication channel including an analog to digital converter (ADC) and a digital to analog converter (DAC),
\Gamma_{e}
can be used to describe the quantization error of the ADC. Generally, if there is no measurement error or transmission error,
\Gamma_{e}
can be set to be sufficiently small.

This work attempts to investigate the observability of system (
1
) under event-triggered mechanism (
4
) using received information (
5
), especially when the measurement is not transmitted. To enable our further analysis, we introduce the notion of
\epsilon
-observability as follows.

Definition 1

(
\epsilon
-observability) Given
\epsilon>0
, the system in (
1
) is said to be
\epsilon
-observable if for any initial state
{\bm{x}}_{0}
, there exists a finite
K>0
such that the knowledge of the measurement information
\{\mathcal{Y}_{o0},\mathcal{Y}_{o1},\ldots,\mathcal{Y}_{oK}\}
suffices to determine a bounded ellipsoidal area which contains the initial state

{\bm{x}}_{0}\in\mathcal{E}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{0},\hat{P}_{0}),

(7)

with
{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{0})\leq\epsilon
. Otherwise, the system is said to be
\epsilon-
unobservable.

The definition above is an extension of classical observability as in Definition 6.D2 in
Chen 1999
, from uniquely determining the initial state to the determining a bounded ellipsoidal area containing the initial state. It can be observed from Definition
1
that
\epsilon
is an important parameter to describe the degree of estimation accuracy of system (
1
).

Based on the descriptions above, the objective of this work is to investigate two problems:

1.

How to determine the
\epsilon
-observability of LTI system (
1
), especially the accuracy parameter
\epsilon
?

2.

How to design an iterative state estimator according to the
\epsilon
-observability of system (
1
)?

3
\epsilon
-Observability Criterion

In this section, the estimation of initial state
{\bm{x}}_{0}
utilizing the set-valued measurement information is first discussed, and then a criterion for
\epsilon
-observability of system (
1
) is developed based on the discussion.

For convenience, several useful lemmas for ellipsoids including affine transformation, geometrical (Minkowski) sum, and intersection are stated as follows.

Lemma 1
.

(Affine transformation) For
{\bm{x}}\in\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}},Q)
, the affine transformation
A{\bm{x}}+{\bm{b}}
satisfy

A{\bm{x}}+{\bm{b}}\in A\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}},Q)+b=\mathcal{E}(A{\bm{a}}+{\bm{b}},AQA^{\rm T}).

Lemma 2
.

(Minkowski sum) For
{\bm{x}}_{1}\in\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})
,
{\bm{x}}_{2}\in\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
, the sum
{\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{x}}_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{x}}_{2}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\oplus\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{a}}_{2},f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2};p)),

where

\displaystyle f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2};p)

\displaystyle:=(1+p^{-1})Q_{1}+(1+p)Q_{2},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle\in[\lambda_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}},\lambda_{max}^{\frac{1}{2}}],

with
\lambda_{min}
and
\lambda_{max}
being minimal and maximal roots of the equation
\det(Q_{1}-\lambda Q_{2})=0
, respectively.

Remark 3
.

In Lemma
2
, a set of ellipsoids containing the Minkowski sum of ellipsoids
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
are represented using parameter
p
, which can be seen as external approximations of the Minkowski sum. In this work, an optimal choice
p^{*}
of parameter
p
is utilized to minimize
{\rm Tr}(f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2},p))
as

\begin{split}p^{*}&=\arg\min\limits_{p}{\rm Tr}(f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2},p)),\\
{\rm s.t.}~~~p&\in\left[\lambda_{min}^{\frac{1}{2}},\lambda_{max}^{\frac{1}{2}}\right].\end{split}

(8)

To solve Problem
8
, let
\frac{{\rm d}{\rm Tr}(f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2},p))}{{\rm d}p}=0
, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm d}{\rm Tr}\left((1+p^{-1})Q_{1}+(1+p)Q_{2}\right)}{{\rm d}p}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle{\rm d}\frac{1+p^{-1}}{{\rm d}p}{\rm Tr}(Q_{1})+{\rm d}\frac{1+p}{{\rm d}p}{\rm Tr}(Q_{2})

\displaystyle=0

Thus we obtain

p^{*}=\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q_{1})}}{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q_{2})}}.

(9)

In this work, we denote

\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\oplus\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})\subset\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{a}}_{2},f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2};p^{*})),

and
f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2}):=f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2};p^{*})
for simplification. The function
f_{+}(\cdot,\cdot)
is used to describe the Minkowski sum of two ellipsoids, based on which the Minkowski sum of more ellipsoids can be performed successively. For example, the Minkowski sum of three ellipsoids
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1}),\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
, and
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{3},Q_{3})
can be obtained as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\oplus\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})\oplus\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{3},Q_{3})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1}+{\bm{a}}_{2}+{\bm{a}}_{3},f_{+}(f_{+}(Q_{1},Q_{2}),Q_{3})).

Then we define

\displaystyle f_{+}^{[3]}(\cdot,\cdot,\cdot)

\displaystyle:=f_{+}\left(f_{+}(\cdot,\cdot),\cdot\right),

\displaystyle f_{+}^{[i]}(\cdots)

\displaystyle:=f_{+}\left(f_{+}^{[i-1]}(\cdots),\cdot\right).

Lemma 4
.

(Intersection) For ellipsoids
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})
and
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
, the intersection
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\cap\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
satisfies

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\cap\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left(M{\bm{a}}_{1},MQ_{1}M^{\rm T}\right)

\displaystyle~~\oplus\mathcal{E}\left((I-M){\bm{a}}_{2},(I-M)Q_{2}(I-M)^{\rm T}\right),

where
M
is a matrix parameter with appropriate dimension.

Remark 5
.

Lemma
4
proposes an external ellipsoidal estimation of intersection
\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{1},Q_{1})\cap\mathcal{E}({\bm{a}}_{2},Q_{2})
with matrix parameter
M
. In this work, an optimal choice
M^{*}
is chosen to minimize the trace of the ellipsoid as

\displaystyle M^{*}=

\displaystyle\arg\min\limits_{M}{\rm Tr}\left(MQ_{1}M^{\rm T}\right)

\displaystyle~~+{\rm Tr}\left((I-M)Q_{2}(I-M)^{\rm T}\right),

(10)

which can be solved according to the equation

\frac{{\rm d}{\rm Tr}\left(MQ_{1}M^{\rm T}\right)}{{\rm d}M}+\frac{{\rm d}{\rm Tr}\left((I-M)Q_{2}(I-M)^{\rm T}\right)}{{\rm d}M}=0.

By direct derivation, the optimal choice
M^{*}
of Problem (
5
) is

M^{*}=(Q_{1}+Q_{2})^{-1}Q_{2}.

Based on the lemmas above, the
\epsilon
-observability of system (
1
) is investigated by analyzing the relationship between initial state
{\bm{x}}_{0}
and set-valued measurement information
\{\mathcal{Y}_{o0},\mathcal{Y}_{o1},\ldots\}
as follows.

For time instant
i>0
, the output of system (
1
) can be written as

y_{i}=CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}+\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}CA^{i-j-1}{\bm{w}}_{j}+v_{i}.

(11)

After defining that for
i=0
, the item
\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}CA^{i-j-1}{\bm{w}}_{j}
equals
0
, equation (
11
) works for
i\in\left\{0,1,\ldots\right\}
. Then equivalently, we have

CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}=y_{i}-\sum\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}CA^{i-j-1}{\bm{w}}_{j}-v_{i}.

(12)

Note that for
\forall{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{W}
and
\forall v\in\mathcal{V}
, the corresponding negative elements satisfy
-{\bm{w}}\in\mathcal{W}
and
-v\in\mathcal{V}
, respectively. Thus according to the definition of Minkowski sum stated in Lemma
2
, equation (
12
) leads to

\displaystyle CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}

\displaystyle\in

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{oi}\bigoplus\limits_{j=0}^{i-1}CA^{i-j-1}\mathcal{W}\oplus\mathcal{V}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}\left(y_{\tau i},f_{+}^{[i+1]}(M_{i},CA^{i-1}Q(A^{i-1})^{\rm T}C^{\rm T},\ldots,CQC^{\rm T},R)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\!:\mathcal{E}(y_{\tau i},W_{0,i}).

(13)

By defining the Minkowski sum of several ellipsoids in equation (
13
), the corresponding inequalities can be written as

\displaystyle(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})^{\rm T}W_{0,i}^{-1}(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})\leq 1.

(14)

In order to combine several inequalities together, we introduce
a=[a_{0},\ldots,a_{n-1}]
satisfying

\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}=1,a_{i}>0,i\in\{0,1,\ldots,n-1\}.

(15)

Then by multiplying
a_{i}
, inequality (
14
) turns to

(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})^{\rm T}a_{i}W_{0,i}^{-1}(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})\leq a_{i}.

(16)

For
i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}
, after defining

\displaystyle O

\displaystyle:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}C\\
CA\\
\vdots\\
CA^{n-1}\end{array}\right],~~~Y_{\tau 0}:=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}y_{\tau 0}\\
y_{\tau 1}\\
\vdots\\
y_{\tau n-1}\end{array}\right],

\displaystyle Q_{0}(a)

\displaystyle:=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccc}a_{0}W_{0,0}^{-1}&0&\cdots&0\\
0&a_{1}W_{0,1}^{-1}&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&a_{n-1}W_{0,n-1}^{-1}\end{array}\right],

with
Q_{0}(a)
being a diagonal matrix, sum of inequalities as form (
16
) can be written as

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})^{\rm T}a_{i}W_{0,i}^{-1}(CA^{i}{\bm{x}}_{0}-y_{\tau i})

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{i=0}^{n-1}a_{i}

\displaystyle(O{\bm{x}}_{0}-Y_{\tau 0})^{\rm T}Q_{0}(a)(O{\bm{x}}_{0}-Y_{\tau 0})

\displaystyle\leq 1,

(29)

and the matrix
O
is called observability matrix.

Based on the inequalities aforementioned, the
\epsilon
-observability criterion of system (
1
) is stated as Theorem
6
.

Theorem 6
.

An LTI system in (
1
) is
\epsilon
-observable if matrix
O
defined in equation (
3
) has full column rank and
K=n-1
. The parameter
\epsilon
satisfies

\epsilon=\max\limits_{\gamma_{i}}{\rm Tr}\left(O^{-1}Q_{0}^{-1}(a)(O^{\rm T})^{-1}\right),i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\}.

PROOF.

If the matrix
O\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is column full rank, the inequality (
29
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\left[O({\bm{x}}_{0}-O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0})\right]^{\rm T}Q_{0}(a)\left[O({\bm{x}}_{0}-O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0})\right]

\displaystyle\leq 1

\displaystyle({\bm{x}}_{0}-O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0})^{\rm T}\left[O^{\rm T}Q_{0}(a)O\right]({\bm{x}}_{0}-O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0})

\displaystyle\leq 1.

Thus we obtain an ellipsoidal estimation of the initial state
{\bm{x}}_{0}
as

{\bm{x}}_{0}\in\mathcal{E}(O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0},O^{-1}Q_{0}^{-1}(a)(O^{\rm T})^{-1}).

(30)

Let
\hat{\bm{x}}_{0}=O^{-1}Y_{\tau 0}
, and
\hat{P}_{0}=O^{-1}Q_{0}^{-1}(a)(O^{\rm T})^{-1}
, then equation (
7
) is satisfied. Observing the definition of
W_{0,i}
in equation (
13
), it can be find that
W_{0,i}
is defined based on the Minkowski sum of several ellipsoids, where
M_{i}
is the only uncertain matrix. The definition of
M_{i}
in equation (
6
) shows that
M_{i}
equals either
\Gamma
or
\Gamma_{e}
, which is determined by
\gamma_{i}
. Thus for a fixed parameter
a
, there are
2^{n}
possible values for the matrix
Q_{0}(a)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. After defining
\epsilon
as

\epsilon:=\max\limits_{\gamma_{i}}{\rm Tr}\left(O^{-1}Q_{0}^{-1}(a)(O^{\rm T})^{-1}\right),i\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},

we have
{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{0})\leq\epsilon
.

According to the Definition
1
, the
\epsilon
-observability of system (
1
) is proved.

Remark 7
.

Theorem
6
proposes a criterion of the
\epsilon
-observability for system in (
1
). The parameter
\epsilon
is an indicator to evaluate the uncertainty on estimating the initial state
{\bm{x}}_{0}
inferred based on the received measurement and the knowledge of the event-triggered communication channel. Note that the parameter
\epsilon
is determined by the characteristic of the system

, the parameter of the communication channel

, and the designed parameter

. The parameter
\epsilon
does not concern the specific communication condition
{\rm\gamma}
, which reflects the worst case of determining the region of the initial state.

4
Event-triggered Set-membership Observer

This section aims to design an estimator for system (
1
), which could calculate an external ellipsoidal approximation of state
{\bm{x}}_{k}
iteratively. This section consists of four parts: analysis of predictive possible region of
{\bm{x}}_{k}
based on the estimated region of
{\bm{x}}_{k-1}
(prior set), analysis of the possible region of
{\bm{x}}_{k}
inferred from set-valued measurement information (measurement information set), combination of prior set and measurement information set (iterative state estimator), and the analysis of the convergence of the proposed method.

4.1
Prior Set

Assume that at time instant
k
, we have obtained an ellipsoidal approximation for
{\bm{x}}_{k-1}
as

{\bm{x}}_{k-1}\in\hat{X}_{k-1}:=\mathcal{E}(\hat{\bm{x}}_{k-1},\hat{P}_{k-1}),

(31)

where
\hat{X}_{k-1}
is an estimated ellipsoid with parameters
\hat{\bm{x}}_{k-1}
and
\hat{P}_{k-1}
. Then according to Lemma
1
, we have

A{\bm{x}}_{k-1}\in\mathcal{E}(A\hat{\bm{x}}_{k-1},A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T}).

(32)

Considering the disturbance
{\bm{w}}_{k-1}\in\mathcal{W}
, the prior estimation of
{\bm{x}}_{k}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\bm{x}}_{k}

\displaystyle=A{\bm{x}}_{k-1}+{\bm{w}}_{k-1}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{E}(A\hat{\bm{x}}_{k-1},A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})\oplus\mathcal{W}

4.4
Convergence Analysis

In the SubSection
4.3
, an ellipsoidal set-membership state estimator is developed in Algorithm
1
by combining the measurement information set
\bar{X}_{k}
proposed in Section
4.2
and the prior set
\check{X}_{k}
proposed in Section
4.1
. This subsection focuses on the convergence of the proposed algorithm, which can be analyzed by investigating the asymptotic property of the posterior set
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})
.

In this subsection, the trace variation of the matrix
\hat{P}_{k}
is firstly derived. Then two inequalities are proved, which is useful for analyzing the asymptotic property of the posterior set. At the end of this subsection,
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})
is proved to be bounded and an upper bound is given.

From equation (
54
), the trace of
\hat{P}_{k}
can be written as

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left(f_{+}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T},(I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}((1+\frac{1}{p^{*}})M^{*}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}(M^{*}_{k})^{\rm T})

\displaystyle~~+{\rm Tr}((1+p^{*})(I-M^{*}_{k})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T}).

(58)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}((1+\frac{1}{p^{*}})M^{*}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}(M^{*}_{k})^{\rm T})+{\rm Tr}((1+p^{*})(I-M^{*}_{k})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}}{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}}{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}}{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}}{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}\right)^{2}

(59)

Substitute
p^{*}
in equation (
52
) into equation (
58
), then the relationship between
\hat{P}_{k}
,
\bar{P}_{k}
, and
\check{P}_{k}
is stated in equation (
59
), which can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle~~+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}.

(60)

Noting that
\check{P}_{k}=f_{+}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T},Q)
, the relationship between
\hat{P}_{k}
and
\hat{P}_{k-1}
is implied in equation (
60
), which is difficult to analyze. Thus two inequalities are proposed to scale equation (
60
), which are proved by a constructive method as follows.

Lemma 10
.

Terms in equation (
60
) satisfy

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})},

(61)

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\check{P}_{k})},

(62)

where
M_{k}^{*}
is given by equation (
50
).

PROOF.

The proof of inequality (
61
) is firstly stated as follows.
Since
\bar{P}_{k}
and
\check{P}_{k}
are symmetric positive semi-definite, the products of them
\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}
,
\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}
and
\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}
are positive semi-definite, i.e.,

\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}\geq 0,

and thus

\displaystyle\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}~~~~~~~

\displaystyle-\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}\geq 0

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})\bar{P}_{k}(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T}-\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}\geq 0.

Since the congruent transformation does not change the positive exponential inertial and the negative exponential inertial of a matrix, we obtain

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})\bar{P}_{k}(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T})^{-1}

\displaystyle~~-(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T})^{-1}\geq 0,

and

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T})^{-1}-\bar{P}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 0

\displaystyle M^{*}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}(M^{*}_{k})^{\rm T}-\bar{P}_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 0.

(63)

According to trace property of matrices
{\rm Tr}(\cdot)
, the inequality in (
63
) leads to

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(M^{*}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}(M^{*}_{k})^{\rm T})-{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})\leq 0,

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M^{*}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}(M^{*}_{k})^{\rm T})}\leq\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})},

which completes the proof of inequality (
61
).

Then the proof of the inequality (
62
) can be performed similar to the proof of the inequality (
61
), which is briefly stated as follows.
According to equation (
50
), we have

\displaystyle(I-M_{k}^{*})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})-(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\check{P}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\bar{P}_{k}.

(64)

The symmetric positive semi-definiteness of matrices
\bar{P}_{k}
and
\check{P}_{k}
leads to

\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\check{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}+\check{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}^{\rm T}\geq 0.

(65)

By adding and subtracting
\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}
to the left side of inequality in (
65
), we have

\displaystyle(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})\check{P}_{k}(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{\rm T}-\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\check{P}_{k}-(\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1})^{\rm T}

\displaystyle\geq 0

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1}\bar{P}_{k}\check{P}_{k}\bar{P}_{k}^{\rm T}((\bar{P}_{k}+\check{P}_{k})^{-1})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\check{P}_{k})}

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\check{P}_{k})},

where the last inequality can be obtained according to equation (
64
), and the proof of inequality (
62
) is completed.

Utilizing the scaling inequalities in Lemma
10
, the asymptotic property of the posterior set
\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})
can be analyzed based on equation (
60
) as the following theorem.

Theorem 11
.

For the system in (
1
), if the spectral norm
\|A\|<1
, the trace of the posterior ellipsoidal estimation set
{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})
is asymptotically bounded as

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})}\leq\max\limits_{\bm{\gamma}}\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}}{1-\|A\|}.

PROOF.

By substituting inequalities (
61
) and (
62
) into equation (
60
), we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})}=

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(M_{k}^{*}\bar{P}_{k}(M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle~~+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}((I-M_{k}^{*})\check{P}_{k}(I-M_{k}^{*})^{\rm T})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\check{P}_{k})}.

(66)

According to the definition of matrix
\check{P}_{k}
in equation (
34
), the trace of
\check{P}_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(\check{P}_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(f_{+}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T},Q))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}}{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}}{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})

\displaystyle~~+\frac{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}}{\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}}{{\rm Tr}(Q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})+{\rm Tr}(Q)+2\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}\right)^{2}.

(67)

After combining inequality (
66
) and equation (
67
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(A\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k-1}A^{\rm T}A)}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}

\displaystyle\leq\|A\|\sqrt{{\rm Tr}\hat{P}_{k-1}}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P}_{k})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}.

Matrix
\bar{P}_{k}
has
2^{n}
possible values, which is determined by the event-triggering conditions
{\bm{\gamma}}
according to the definition of
\bar{P}_{k}
in equation (
48
) and the definition of
Q_{k}(a)
in equation (
4.2
), where
{\bm{\gamma}}
is a series of event-triggering conditions involved by
Q_{k}(a)
.

For the case that
\|A\|<1
, we have

\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow+\infty}\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\hat{P}_{k})}\leq\frac{\max\limits_{\bm{\gamma}}\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(\bar{P})}+\sqrt{{\rm Tr}(Q)}}{1-\|A\|},

which completes the proof.

Remark 13
.

In this work, the

-observability and set-membership state estimator are discussed for systems with one dimensional measurement

in Theorem
6
and Theorem
11
, respectively. The measurement of the system is designed to be one dimensional to make it convenience to establish the relationship between the column full rank and invertible of matrix
O
. For a system with multiple outputs, the measurement matrix
C
satisfies
C\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
, and thus the observability matrix satisfies
O=[C^{\rm T},(CA)^{\rm T},\ldots,(CA^{n-1})^{\rm T}]^{\rm T}\in\mathbb{R}^{mn\times n}
. For column full rank matrix
O\in\mathbb{R}^{mn\times n}
, an invertible square matrix can be obtained by choosing appropriate rows of matrix
O
. Then the proposed
\epsilon
-observability criterion and the designed set-membership state estimator can be extended to systems with
m
dimensional measurements
y_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we evaluate the observability of LTI systems with event-triggered communication channels utilizing a set-membership approach. A new notion,
\epsilon
-observability, is proposed as a generalization of classical observability, which focuses on the potential possibility of determining a set containing all possible values of the initial state. To judge the proposed
\epsilon
-observability, a testing criterion and the corresponding calculation method for parameter
\epsilon
are developed using the intersection and sum of ellipsoids, based on which an event-triggered set-membership state observer is designed. The asymptotic property of the designed observer is analyzed and verified by numerical examples. In our current study, we focus on deterministic event-triggering mechanisms. In our next step, stochastic event-triggering conditions will be further investigated.

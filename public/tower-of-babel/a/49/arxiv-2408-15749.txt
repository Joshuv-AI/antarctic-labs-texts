Local well-posedness of a system coupling compressible atmospheric dynamics and a micro-physics model of moisture in air
Sabine Doppler, Rupert Klein, Xin Liu, Edriss S. Titi

Abstract
We establish here the local-in-time well-posedness of strong solutions with large initial data to a system coupling compressible atmospheric dynamics with a refined moisture model introduced in Hittmeir and Klein [ 10 ] (Theor. Comput. Fluid Dyn. 32:137–164, 2018). This micro-physics moisture model is a refinement of the one communicated by Klein and Majda [ 15 ] (Theor. Comput. Fluid Dyn. 20:525–551, 2006), which serves as a foundation of multi-scale asymptotic expansions for moist deep convection in the atmosphere. Specifically, we show, in this paper, that the system of compressible Navier–Stokes equations coupled with the moisture dynamics is well-posed. In particular, the micro-physics of moisture model incorporates the phase changes of water in moist air. Keywords: well-posedness; strong solutions; phase changes; micro-physics moisture model; moist atmospheric flows. MSC2010: 35M86; 35Q30; 35Q35; 35Q86; 76N10.

Introduction

Weather prediction, which affects our daily life closely, has been developed extensively thanks to modern computers. However, due to the complex dynamics involved, it still remains one of the most challenging topics in meteorology, both analytically and numerically. In particular, the thermodynamics of water and dry air, as well as the hydrodynamics coupling, calls for sophisticated modeling and careful mathematical analysis. Our goal in this paper is to perform rigorous analysis on an atmospheric model describing the complex phase changes in the moist atmosphere, and provide a solid mathematical foundation for applications in numerical simulation.

Water in the atmosphere exists in the basic forms of vapor water, cloud water, and rain water. Briefly speaking, evaporation changes the rain water into vapor water; condensation turns vapor water into cloud water; both accumulation and collection by rain convert cloud water into rain water. Notably, water eventually exits the dynamics in this model by raining. In figure

4.1
Well-posedness of the associated linear system (
4.2
) – (
4.6
)

By assuming

, and
\{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathrm{j}}^{o}\}_{\mathrm{j}\in\{\mathrm{v},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r}\}}
to be smooth enough, (

) can be solved by the standard characteristic method, and the well-posedness of (
4.2
) – (
4.6
) follows from a standard Galerkin method. Such a Galerkin scheme is similar to the one employed in
[
19
]
.

To be more precise, let
\{\Psi_{\mathrm{j}}\}_{\mathrm{j}=1,2,3,\cdots}
and
\{\Phi_{\mathrm{j}}\}_{\mathrm{j}=1,2,3,\cdots}
be the eigenfunctions of the Laplacian subject to the Dirichlet and the Neumann boundary conditions, respectively, i.e., for
\mathrm{j}=1,2,3,\cdots
,

\displaystyle-\Delta\Psi_{\mathrm{j}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{j}\Psi_{\mathrm{j}},\qquad

\displaystyle\Psi_{\mathrm{j}}|_{z=0,1}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle-\Delta\Phi_{\mathrm{j}}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{j}\Phi_{\mathrm{j}},\qquad

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\Phi_{\mathrm{j}}|_{z=0,1}

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\int\Phi_{\mathrm{j}}\,dx=1,

where
\lambda_{\mathrm{j}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{j}}
are the corresponding eigenvalues, labeled in the increasing order. Then the Galerkin approximation scheme can be described as follows: Let
K=1,2,3,\cdots
be an arbitrary finite integer. Let
v=v^{K},\mathfrak{T}=\mathfrak{T}^{K},\{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathrm{j}}=\mathfrak{q}_{\mathrm{j}}^{K}\}_{\mathrm{j}\in\{\mathrm{v},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r}\}}
be in the finite dimensional vector space spanned by
\{\Phi_{j},\mathrm{j}=1,2,3,\cdots,K\}
and
w=w^{K}
be in the finite dimensional vector space spanned by
\{\Psi_{\mathrm{j}},\mathrm{j}=1,2,3,\cdots,K\}
. Then by restricting (
4.2
)–(
4.6
) in the corresponding finite dimensional space, it forms a system of linear, non-degenerate ordinary differential equations (ODEs) with source terms. Then the existence of local-in-time solutions for this system of ODEs follows from the standard existence theory. Moreover, the standard
L^{2}
-estimates implies the uniform-in-
K
bounds of
v^{K},w^{K},\mathfrak{T}^{K}
, and
\{\mathfrak{q}_{\mathrm{j}}^{K}\}_{\mathrm{j}\in\{\mathrm{v},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{r}\}}
. After sending
K\rightarrow\infty
, one can conclude the existence of solutions to (
4.2
) – (
4.6
). The details are omitted here.

6.1
An extension lemma

Lemma 1
.

Let
s\geq 0
and
h\in\bigl\Arrowvert h\bigr\Arrowvert_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}
, where
\Gamma:=2\mathbb{T}^{2}\times\{z=0,1\}=\partial\Omega
is the boundary of the domain
\Omega
.
Then there exists an extension operator
\Psi
such that
\partial_{z}\Psi(h)\big|_{z=0,1}=h
and

\bigl\Arrowvert\Psi({h})\bigr\Arrowvert_{H^{s+3/2}(\Omega)}\leq C_{\chi_{0},s}\bigl\Arrowvert h\bigr\Arrowvert_{H^{s}(\Gamma)},

(6.1)

where the positive constant
C_{\chi_{0},s}
depends on
s
and the cut-off function
\chi_{0}
defined, below, in (
6.2
).

Proof.

After writing
h
in terms of the Fourier expansion in horizontal variables,

h(\mathbf{x}_{h},z,t)=\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\beta_{\mathbf{k}}^{z}(t)e^{i\pi\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{h}},~z\in\{0,1\}.

Next, define
\Phi_{0}(h)
and
\Phi_{1}(h)
by

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(h):=

\displaystyle\beta^{0}_{(0,0)}(t)z-\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|\mathbf{k}|\neq 0}\dfrac{\beta_{\mathbf{k}}^{0}(t)}{|\mathbf{k}|}e^{i\pi\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{h}}e^{-|\mathbf{k}|z},

\displaystyle\Phi_{1}(h):=

\displaystyle\beta^{1}_{(0,0)}(t)z+\sum_{\mathbf{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},|\mathbf{k}|\neq 0}\dfrac{\beta_{\mathbf{k}}^{1}(t)}{|\mathbf{k}|}e^{i\pi\mathbf{k}\cdot\mathbf{x}_{h}}e^{-|\mathbf{k}|(1-z)}.

Also, define a cut-off function
\chi_{0}=\chi_{0}(z)
by

\chi_{0}(z)=\begin{cases}1,&z\in(0,1/4),\\
\text{smooth, {monotonically} decreasing},&z\in(1/4,3/4),\\
0,&z\in(3/4,1).\end{cases}

(6.2)

Then
\Psi(h)
is defined by

\Psi(h):=\chi_{0}\Phi_{0}(h)+(1-\chi_{0})\Phi_{1}(h).

(6.3)

Then
\Psi({h})
is smooth, and satisfies (
6.1
).
∎

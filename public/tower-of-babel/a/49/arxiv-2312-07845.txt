Gap Theorem on manifolds with small curvature concentration
Pak-Yeung Chan, Man-Chun Lee

Abstract
. In this work, we show that complete non-compact manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature, Euclidean volume growth and sufficiently small curvature concentration are necessarily flat Euclidean space.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 53E20

1.
Introduction

One of the fundamental goal of Riemannian geometry is to investigate the underlying structure of a manifold. In this work, we are interested in the structure of manifolds with pinched curvature in the term of curvature concentration. The consideration of manifolds with bounded integral curvature dates back to the study of volume non-collapsed Einstein manifolds or more generally manifolds with bounded Ricci curvature, for example see
[
1
,
3
,
4
]
. Curvature bound in form of
||\text{\rm Rm}||_{L^{n/2}}
is particularly interesting since this is scaling invariant. In the content of Einstein manifolds, bounded curvature concentration often imposes sufficiently strong constraints so that only finitely many singularities can arise thanks to the Einstein structure, for example see
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
.

In this work, we are interested in studying complete non-compact manifolds with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
. In order to study its asymptotic, it is more often to consider its tangent cone at infinity. This is a pointed Gromov-Hausdorff limit of the sequence
(M^{n},g_{i},x_{0})
where
g_{i}=R_{i}^{-2}g
for some divergent sequence
R_{i}\to+\infty
. The existence of such limit is guaranteed by Gromov’s compactness Theorem. If
g
is of Euclidean volume growth, the sequence of manifolds
(M,g_{i},x_{0})
will be non-collapsed uniformly. If in addition
g
is Ricci-flat with bounded
||\text{\rm Rm}||_{L^{n/2}}
, it follows from
[
4
, Theorem 1.5 & Remark 2]
that
M^{n}
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if
n
is odd. This is in spirit similar to the gap Theorem of Anderson
[
2
]
. Motivated by this, we are interested to ask if a manifold is of
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
, Euclidean volume growth and has bounded curvature concentration, then is it necessarily flat?
In
[
9
, Theorem 6.10]
, a deep result of Cheeger together with Colding’s volume continuity Theorem
[
12
]
shows a gap Theorem under
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
, Euclidean volume growth and bounded curvature concentration when the dimension is odd. It is also immediate to observe from the Eguchi-Hanson metric that a general gap Theorem cannot be expected to hold for all dimension. In comparison with the Eguchi-Hanson metric, we consider manifolds with small curvature concentration which in theory rule out the singularities. Indeed, it was known by
[
9
, Theorem 8.3]
that this is the case under additional Ricci curvature decay assumption, see also
[
9
, Theorem 4.32]
. It is therefore tempting to study the gap phenomenon
without
any point-wise upper bound on the Ricci curvature but with small curvature concentration in
all dimension
.

Apart from the aforementioned static elliptic approach, the curvature concentration condition is shown to be a particularly well adapted quantity to the parabolic geometric flow. In
[
5
,
6
]
, a quantitative Ricci flow existence theory on manifold with small curvature concentration is developed and is applied to investigate the topological rigidity of such manifolds without bounded curvature conditions (in fact under a weaker curvature conditions), see also
[
7
]
for a gap theorem without volume non-collapsing conditions. In this work, we shall further exploit the monotonicity of the curvature concentration along the Ricci flow and show the following gap theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
n\geq 4,v>0
, there exists
\delta(n,v)>0
such that if
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold with

(i)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq 0
;

(ii)

The asymptotic volume ratio of
(M,g_{0})
satisfies

\mathrm{AVR}(g)\geq v>0;

(iii)

Small curvature concentration, i.e.

\left(\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}<\delta,

then
(M,g_{0})
is isometric to flat Euclidean space.

The method of this work is based on the Ricci flow which is motivated by the following picture. By the volume comparison, it is known that the tangent cone at infinity is a metric cone thanks to the Euclidean volume growth. If the metric cone is smooth away from the tips, the curvature concentration must be unbounded unless it is flat. In particular, the Ricci flow coming out of the cone is not expected to have bounded curvature concentration unless it is flat. This will be made precise using the quantitative existence theory developed in
[
6
]
.

2.
Some preliminaries on Ricci flow

We want to regularize
g_{0}
using the Ricci flow and obtain contradiction from long-time asymptotic of the flow. We first collect some preliminaries of Ricci flow result.

2.1.
Perelman’s entropy and its localization

The entropy along the Ricci flow was introduced by Perelman
[
15
]
and its definition was further localized by Wang
[
17
]
. Suppose
(M^{n},g)
is an
n
dimensional complete (not necessarily compact) Riemannian manifold and
\Omega
is a connected domain on
M
with smooth boundary (boundaryless if
M=\Omega
). Similar to the notation in
[
17
]
, we let

\displaystyle D_{g}(\Omega)

\displaystyle:=\left\{u:u\in W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega),u\geq 0\text{ and }\|u\|_{2}=1\right\},

\displaystyle W(\Omega,g,u,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}\tau(\mathcal{R}\,u^{2}+4|\nabla u|^{2})-2u^{2}\log u\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n,

\displaystyle\nu(\Omega,g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{u\in D_{g}(\Omega),s\in(0,\tau]}W(\Omega,g,u,s),

\displaystyle\nu(\Omega,g)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\nu(\Omega,g,\tau).

We refer the readers to
[
15
]
and
[
17
]
for the (almost) monotonicity of these quantities along the bounded curvature Ricci flow and their applications. Analogously, we can define the standard entropy without the scalar curvature term, which is closely related to the classical Log Sobolev inequality.

\displaystyle\bar{W}(\Omega,g,u,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}4\tau|\nabla u|^{2}-2u^{2}\log u\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g}-\frac{n}{2}\log(4\pi\tau)-n,

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{u\in D_{g}(\Omega),s\in(0,\tau]}\bar{W}(\Omega,g,u,s),

\displaystyle\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g)

\displaystyle:=\inf_{\tau\in(0,\infty)}\bar{\nu}(\Omega,g,\tau).

2.2.
Existence of immortal Ricci flow from
g_{0}

We start with the long-time existence of the Ricci flow with no bounded curvature assumption obtained by the authors and Huang
[
6
]
(see also
[
5
]
for the case of bounded curvature).

Proposition 2.1
.

For any
n\geq 4,v,\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta_{0}(n,v,\varepsilon),C_{0}(n)>0
such that if
(M^{n},g_{0})
is a complete non-compact manifold with

(i)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq 0
;

(ii)

The asymptotic volume ratio of
(M,g_{0})
satisfies

\mathrm{AVR}(g)\geq v>0;

(iii)

\left(\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{0})|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{0}}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}<\delta<\delta_{0},

then the asymptotic volume ratio of
g_{0}
must satisfy
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{0})\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Moreover, there exists a complete Ricci flow
g(t)
on
M\times[0,+\infty)
with
g(0)=g_{0}
such that for all
t\in(0,+\infty)
,

(a)

\sup_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\leq\varepsilon t^{-1}
;

(b)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(t)}(x_{0},\sqrt{t}\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\omega_{n}t^{n/2}
for all
x_{0}\in M
;

(c)

The Perelman’s
\nu
-entropy (of all scale) satisfies

\nu(M,g(t))\geq-\varepsilon;

(d)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\leq C_{0}\delta
;

(e)

For any compactly supported
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M,g(t))
,

\left(\int_{M}|u|^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\leq C_{0}\left(\int_{M}4|\nabla u|_{g(t)}^{2}+\mathcal{R}_{g(t)}u^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right);

(f)

The scalar curvature satisfies
\mathcal{R}(g(t))\geq 0
,

where
\omega_{n}
is the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Proof.

This was implicitly proved in
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
and
[
5
]
. We give a sketch here. Since the Ricci curvature of
g_{0}
is non-negative, by
[
17
, Theorem 3.6]
the Euclidean volume growth
\mathrm{AVR}(g_{0})\geq v
implies that there exists
C(n,v)>0
such that the (standard)
\bar{\nu}
-entropy of
g_{0}
is bounded from below by
-C
for all scale, i.e.
\bar{\nu}(M,g_{0})>-C
. It follows from
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
that the asymptotic volume ratio of
g_{0}
satisfies
\mathrm{AVR}(M,g_{0})\geq 1-\varepsilon
if
\delta_{0}
is sufficiently small and hence without loss of generality we might assume
\bar{\nu}(M,g_{0})\geq-\varepsilon
by
[
18
, Theorem 5.9]
. By applying
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
to
R^{-2}g_{0}
and rescaled the resulting solution back, we obtain a sequence of Ricci flow
g_{R}(t)
with
g_{R}(0)=g_{0}
on
M\times[0,T\cdot R^{2}]
and desired estimate (a), (b), (d) and (f) for some uniform
T>0
and for all
R\to+\infty
. The constant
C_{0}
in (e) will depend only on
n
since
\bar{\nu}(M,R^{-2}g_{0})\geq-\varepsilon
and condition (iii) is scaling invariant. By
[
10
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
11
, Theorem 14.16]
, we might let
R\to+\infty
to obtain the immortal solution
g(t)
with (a), (b), (d) and (f), see the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
. The Sobolev inequality (e) will follow from (c) using the argument in
[
19
,
20
]
. One can also apply a scaling argument using
[
5
, Theorem 2.2]
to show the uniform Sobolev inequality on
M
. If the flow has bounded curvature for all
t\geq 0
, (c) follows from the classical
\nu
-monotonicity of Perelman
[
15
]
using the heat kernel estimate from
[
8
]
.
To accommodate the unboundedness of curvature as
t\to 0
, we might argue as follows. Fix
\tau,t_{0}>0
, since
g(t)
is of bounded curvature for
t>0
and
g(t)\to g_{0}
in
C^{\infty}_{loc}
as
t\to 0
,
[
17
, Theorem 5.4]
implies

(2.1)

\begin{split}\nu\left(B_{g(t_{0})}(x_{0},8A\sqrt{t_{0}}),g(t_{0}),\tau\right)&\geq-A^{-2}+\nu\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},20A\sqrt{t_{0}}),g_{0},\tau+t_{0}\right)\\
&\geq-A^{-2}+\nu\left(M,g_{0},\tau+t_{0}\right)\\
&\geq-A^{-2}-\varepsilon\end{split}

for all
A
large. By letting
A\to+\infty
, since
\tau
is arbitrary we see that (c) holds, for instances see
[
17
, Proposition 2.3]
for the proof.
∎

Remark 2.1
.

It is important to note that the proof to our main result relies on existence of a long-time solution to the Ricci flow with small curvature concentration preserved. Such solutions has been constructed by the authors and Huang
[
6
]
when
n\geq 4
. When
n=3
, existence of Ricci flow from
g_{0}
with
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
and Euclidean volume growth was shown by Simon-Topping
[
16
]
. We expect that their constructed solution will remain to have small curvature concentration by carefully examining the partial Ricci flow construction. The three dimensional case can however be taken care using the deep result of Cheeger
[
9
]
and thus we do not pursue the parabolic approach here.

2.3.
Positivity of scalar curvature

Along the Ricci flow constructed from Proposition
2.1
, it follows easily from the strong maximum principle that the scalar curvature of
g(t)
is either strictly positive or
\text{\rm Ric}(g(t))\equiv 0
for
t>0
. In the latter case, the Ricci flow is static and hence the curvature estimate in Proposition
2.1
implies that
g(t)\equiv g_{0}
is flat. The following is from
[
18
, Proposition 5.12]
which says that the scalar curvature of
g(t)
is quantitatively positive if the initial metric
g_{0}
is non-flat. Since we will apply this to Ricci flow
g(t)
with possibly unbounded curvature at
t=0
, we need a slightly modified version.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists
\varepsilon_{0}(n)>0
such that the following holds. For any
\delta>0
, there exists
\frac{1}{2}>\xi(n,\delta)>0
and
S(n,\delta)>0
such that if
g(t)
is a complete Ricci flow on
M\times[0,r^{2}]
with
g(0)=g_{0}
for some
r>0
and satisfies

(i)

\nu(M,g(t),r^{2})\geq-\varepsilon_{0}
for all
t\in[0,r^{2}]
;

(ii)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{0})\geq-(n-1)\xi r^{-2}
on
B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},\xi^{-1}r)
;

(iii)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},s)\right)\leq(1-\delta)\omega_{n}s^{n}
for all
s\in[r,\xi^{-1}r]

for some
x_{0}\in M
. Then the scalar curvature satisfies

\mathcal{R}(x,t)\geq\xi t^{-1}

for all
x\in B_{g(t)}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})
and
t\in[\frac{1}{2}r^{2},r^{2}]
.

Proof.

The proof is almost identical to that of
[
18
, Proposition 5.12]
, we give a sketch for readers’ convenience. We may assume
r=1
by considering
r^{-2}g(r^{2}t),t\in[0,1]
. Suppose on the contrary, we can find a sequence of
\xi_{i}\to 0
and
(M_{i},g_{i}(t),x_{i})
,
0\leq t\leq 1
such that

(1)

\nu(M_{i},g_{i}(t),1)\geq-\varepsilon_{0}
;

(2)

\text{\rm Ric}(g_{i}(0))\geq-(n-1)\xi_{i}\text{ on }B_{g_{i}(0)}(x_{i},\xi_{i}^{-1})
;

(3)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{i}(0)}\left(B_{g_{i}(0)}(x_{i},s)\right)\leq(1-\delta)\omega_{n}s^{n}
for all
s\in[1,\xi_{i}^{-1}]
;

and for some
t_{i}\in[1/2,1]
and
y_{i}\in B_{g_{i}(t_{i})}(x_{i},\sqrt{t_{i}})
we have

t_{i}\mathcal{R}_{i}(y_{i},t_{i})<\xi_{i}\to 0.

By
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
, if
\varepsilon_{0}
is sufficiently small, then each
g_{i}(t)
satisfies

(2.2)

|\text{\rm Rm}(g_{i}(t))|\leq t^{-1}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{inj}(g_{i}(t))\geq\sqrt{t}

on
M_{i}\times(0,1]
. And hence we might apply Hamilton compactness theorem
[
13
]
to see that after passing to subsequence,

(M_{i},g_{i}(t),x_{i}),t\in(0,1]\to(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty}),t\in(0,1]

in the smooth Cheeger-Gromov sense. We also have
t_{i}\to t_{\infty}\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
and
y_{i}\to y_{\infty}\in\overline{B_{g_{\infty}(t_{\infty})}(x_{\infty},\sqrt{t_{\infty}})}
. Moreover by the local maximum principle
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
and Ricci lower bound at
t=0
,
R_{\infty}(x,t)\geq 0
on
M_{\infty}\times(0,1]
. So that
R_{\infty}(y_{\infty},t_{\infty})=0
. By the strong maximum principle,
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t),x_{\infty}),0<t\leq 1
is a static flow with
\text{\rm Ric}\equiv 0
.
Hence by Andersen gap theorem
[
2
]
(see also
[
18
, Theorem 3.5]
),
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(1))
is flat Euclidean space if
\varepsilon_{0}
is small enough and thus the entire static flow
(M_{\infty},g_{\infty}(t))
is isometric to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
t>0
. In particular, this shows that

(2.3)

\lim_{i\to+\infty}\int^{1}_{0}\int_{B_{g_{i}(0)}(x_{i},L)}|\mathcal{R}_{i}|\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g_{i}(\tau)}d\tau=0

for all
L>0
by considering the evolution of volume, see
[
18
, (5.28)]
. This will imply

(M,g_{i}(0),x_{i})\to(\mathbb{R}^{n},\delta,z_{\infty})

in pointed Gromov Hausdorff sense, where
\delta
denotes the flat metric. But this contradicts with (3) by Colding’s Volume Convergence Theorem
[
12
]
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

2.4.
Monotonicity of
||\text{\rm Rm}||_{n/2}
along Ricci flows

Next, we need a global monotonicity of curvature concentration. The following
localized form is proven in
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
n\geq 3
and
(M,g(t))
is a complete solution to the Ricci flow for
t\in[0,T]
. Then there exist
C_{0}(n)>0
such that for any compactly supported function
\phi(x,t)
in space-time, we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\;d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\leq&-C_{0}^{-1}\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{4}})|^{2}\;d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&+C_{0}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\;d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&+C_{0}\int_{M}|\nabla\phi|^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\;d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&+\int_{M}2\phi|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\phi\cdot d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}.\end{split}

We now apply Lemma
2.1
to the Ricci flow
g(t)
obtained from Proposition
2.1
to obtain a monotonicity of global
||\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))||_{n/2}
. We denote

(2.5)

E(t):=\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|^{n/2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}

for
t\in[0,+\infty)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under the assumption of Proposition
2.1
, we have

(2.6)

E(t)+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(\tau)}\,d\tau\leq E(s)

for all
0\leq s\leq t<+\infty
.

Proof.

We fix
0\leq s<t<+\infty
and
x_{0}\in M
. In what follows, we will use
C_{i}
to denote constants which depend only on
n
. Let
\eta(x,t)=d_{g(t)}(x,x_{0})+c_{n}\sqrt{t}
and define
\phi(x,t)=e^{-10t\rho^{-2}}\varphi\left(\frac{\eta(x,t)}{\rho}\right)
where
\rho\gg 1
and
\varphi(s)
is a cutoff function on
\mathbb{R}
so that
\varphi\equiv 1
on
(-\infty,\frac{1}{2}]
,
\varphi\equiv 0
outside
(-\infty,1]
and satisfies
\varphi^{\prime\prime}\geq-10\varphi
,
0\geq\varphi^{\prime}\geq-10\sqrt{\varphi}
. By choosing
c_{n}
large enough, we have from
[
15
, Lemma 8.3]
that

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\Delta_{g(t)}\right)\phi\leq 0.

Moreover,
|\nabla_{g(t)}\phi|^{2}\leq C_{n}\rho^{-2}

and
1\geq\phi\to 1
on
M\times[s,t]
as
\rho\to+\infty
.

Thanks to Lemma
2.1
,

(2.8)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\leq&C_{1}\delta^{n/2}\rho^{-2}-C_{1}^{-1}\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{4}})|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&+C_{1}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
=&C_{1}\delta^{n/2}\rho^{-2}-C_{1}^{-1}\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{4}})|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&+(C_{1}+1)\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}-\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}.\end{split}

By Hölder inequality,

(2.9)

\begin{split}&\quad(C_{1}+1)\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&\leq(C_{1}+1)\left(\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\\
&\leq C_{2}\delta\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.\end{split}

On the other hand, we use the uniform Sobolev inequality, i.e. (e) in Proposition
2.1
together with (
2.9
), to see that if we further require
\delta
to be smaller than a dimensional constant
\delta_{0}(n)
, then

(2.10)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{M}|\nabla(\phi|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{4}})|^{2}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&\geq C_{3}^{-1}\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}-\frac{1}{4}\int_{M}\phi^{2}\mathcal{R}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&\geq\left(C_{3}^{-1}-C_{4}\delta\right)\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2C_{3}}\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}.\end{split}

Therefore, we arrive at the following almost monotonicity inequality: if we shrink
\delta_{0}
further, then

(2.11)

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}&\leq C_{1}\delta^{n/2}\rho^{-2}-\frac{1}{2C_{3}C_{1}}\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\\
&+C_{2}\delta\left(\int_{M}\phi^{\frac{2n}{n-2}}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n^{2}}{2(n-2)}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{n}}\\
&-\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}\\
&\leq-\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}+C_{1}\rho^{-2}.\end{split}

By integrating the above differential inequality over
[s,t]
, we see that

(2.12)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(t)}+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(\tau)}\,d\tau\\
&\leq\int_{M}\phi^{2}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(s)}+C_{1}\rho^{-2}(t-s).\end{split}

Since
|\text{\rm Rm}(g(t))|\in L^{n/2}
for all
t\geq 0
, result follows from the dominated convergence theorem and by letting
\rho\to+\infty
.
∎

With the above preparations, we are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We let
g(t)
be the immortal Ricci flow constructed from Proposition
2.1
with initial data
g(0)=g_{0}
. We will assume
\delta
to be sufficiently small. We first note from Proposition
2.1
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we might choose
\delta
so that

(2.13)

\mathrm{AVR}(M,g_{0})=\lim_{r\to+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq 1-\varepsilon.

Suppose on the contrary that
g_{0}
is non-flat. From
\text{\rm Ric}\geq 0
and the rigidity of volume comparison Theorem we know that the asymptotic volume ratio of
g_{0}
satisfies

(2.14)

\mathrm{AVR}(M,g_{0})=1-{\beta}

for some
{\beta}>0
which will be small by shrinking
\delta
. Fix
x_{0}\in M
, there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
r>r_{0}
,

(2.15)

1-\frac{{\beta}}{2}\geq\frac{\mathrm{Vol}_{g_{0}}\left(B_{g_{0}}(x_{0},r)\right)}{\omega_{n}r^{n}}\geq 1-{\beta}.

By Proposition
2.2
, there exists
\xi>0
so that for all
x\in B_{g(t)}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})
and
t>r_{0}^{2}
,

(2.16)

\mathcal{R}(x,t)\geq\xi t^{-1}.

Using also

(2.17)

\mathrm{Vol}_{g(t)}\left(B_{g(t)}(x_{0},\sqrt{t})\right)\geq(1-\varepsilon)\omega_{n}t^{n/2}

for all
t>0
,
we see that for all
t>r_{0}^{2}
,

(2.18)

\begin{split}&\quad\int_{r_{0}^{2}}^{t}\int_{M}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(\tau)}\,d\tau\\
&\geq\int_{r_{0}^{2}}^{t}\int_{B_{g(\tau)}(x_{0},\sqrt{\tau})}|\text{\rm Rm}|^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\,d\mathrm{vol}_{g(\tau)}\,d\tau\\
&\geq(1-\varepsilon)\omega_{n}\xi^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\int_{r_{0}^{2}}^{t}\tau^{-1}d\tau=(1-\varepsilon)\omega_{n}\xi^{\frac{n}{2}+1}\log\left(\frac{t}{r_{0}^{2}}\right).\end{split}

But this contradicts with Lemma
2.2
since the right hand side tends to
+\infty
as
t\to+\infty
while the left hand side is uniformly bounded. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

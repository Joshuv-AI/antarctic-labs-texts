Weights for ℓ \ell -local compact groups
Jason Semeraro

Abstract
. In this note, we initiate the study of ℱ \mathcal{F} -weights for an ℓ \ell -local compact group ℱ \mathcal{F} over a discrete ℓ \ell -toral group S S with discrete torus T T . Motivated by Alperin’s Weight Conjecture for simple groups of Lie-type, we conjecture that when T T is the unique maximal abelian subgroup of S S up to ℱ \mathcal{F} -conjugacy and every element of S S is ℱ \mathcal{F} -fused into T T , the number of weights of ℱ \mathcal{F} is bounded above by the number of ordinary irreducible characters of its Weyl group. By combining the structure theory of ℱ \mathcal{F} with the theory of blocks with cyclic defect group, we are able to give a proof of this conjecture in the case when ℱ \mathcal{F} is simple and | S : T | = ℓ |S:T|=\ell . We also propose and give evidence for an analogue of the height zero case of Robinson’s Ordinary Weight conjecture in this setting.

1.
Introduction

An
\ell
-compact group
is, broadly speaking, the
\ell
-local homotopy theoretic analogue of a compact Lie group with a ‘Weyl group’
W
which is a
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
-reflection group. In
[
14
]
, the authors introduce and prove a version of Alperin’s Weight Conjecture (AWC) for fusion systems associated to homotopy fixed point spaces of connected
\ell
-compact groups under the action of unstable Adams operations. For such a fusion system
\mathcal{F}
associated to a simply connected
\ell
-compact group with Weyl group
W
, under some mild hypotheses this conjecture asserts that
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
where
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
is the number of
weights
(defect zero characters of
\mathcal{F}
-automorphism groups of
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroups up to
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy). One consequence is that the number of weights appears to be an invariant of the underlying
\ell
-compact group, as opposed to the space of fixed points. In an attempt to understand this, we introduce the study of weights for
\ell
-compact groups by appealing to the more general theory of
\ell
-local compact groups

[
3
]
. Here, we view an
\ell
-local compact group as a fusion system
\mathcal{F}
on an
\ell
-group
S
which contains a finite index infinite torus
T
(see Definition
2.2
). Since
\mathcal{F}
has finitely many classes of
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroups, each with a finite
\mathcal{F}
-outer automorphism group (see Proposition
2.3
), we obtain an integer invariant
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
which, by analogy with the finite case, we call the ‘number of weights’ of
\mathcal{F}
(see Definition
2.4
).

An
\ell
-local compact group
\mathcal{F}
is
connected
if
T
is the unique maximal abelian subgroup of
S
up to
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy and every element of the underlying
\ell
-group is
\mathcal{F}
-conjugate to an element of
T
. In this case we refer to the group
W={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)
as the
Weyl group
of
\mathcal{F}
. Based on
[
14
, Theorem 1]
for finite fusion systems, and observations made in the present paper, we make the following conjecture which may be regarded as a weak analogue of Alperin’s Weight Conjecture for connected
\ell
-local compact groups.

Conjecture 1.1

(Weak analogue of AWC)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a connected
\ell
-local compact group with Weyl group
W
. Then

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})\leq|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|.

Markus Linckelmann has asked for conditions on a fusion system
\mathcal{G}
which imply the existence of a subgroup
T
with
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{G})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}({{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{G}}(T))|
so Conjecture
1.1
would provide a partial answer to this. Letting
v_{\ell}(-)
denote the
\ell
-adic valuation, it is a straightforward observation that equality holds in Conjecture
1.1
when
v_{\ell}(|W|)=0
. Indeed,
\mathcal{F}
is automatically connected in this case (since
S=T
) and hence
T
is the unique
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroup in
\mathcal{F}
. Thus
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
is the number of defect zero characters of
W
, which is
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
since
v_{\ell}(|W|)=0
. Our main result concerns the case
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{F}
is a simple
\ell
-local compact group and
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
. Then
\mathcal{F}
is connected and

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|.

Here,
\mathcal{F}
is
simple
if it possesses no non-trivial normal subsystems. Theorem
1.2
initially inspired the author to conjecture that an equality
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})={{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)

always
holds for connected
\ell
-local compact groups, but this was later shown to be false in joint work with Kessar and Malle
[
15
]
. For example if
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{2}(\operatorname{Sp}(2))
, then
4=\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})\lneq|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(D_{8}))|=5
(see
[
15
, Example 4.2]
). On the other hand,
[
15
, Theorem 1]
shows that that
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|
where
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(\mathbf{G})
for
any
compact connected Lie group
\mathbf{G}
for which the prime
\ell
is good. Thus an equality-predicting refinement of Conjecture
1.1
might be obtained from an appropriate generalisation of “good” to connected
\ell
-local compact groups; we do not pursue that here.

Next, we introduce a version of the height zero case of Robinson’s Ordinary Weight Conjecture (OWC) for connected
\ell
-local compact groups. Via deep results in modular representation theory, Conjecture
1.3
below may be understood as a generalisation of height zero OWC for Lie-type groups with Weyl group
W
when
\ell
is a very good prime and
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
. This connection is made formally at the end of Section
2
.

Conjecture 1.3

(Analogue of height zero OWC)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a connected
\ell
-local compact group on
S
with Weyl group
W
and set

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})&=&\{(\psi,\chi)\mid\psi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S^{ab}),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)}(\psi))\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}}\\
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})&=&\{(s,\chi)\mid s\in Z(S),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(W(s))\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}}\end{array}

where for each
s\in Z(S)
,
W(s)
denotes the Weyl group of
C_{\mathcal{F}}(s)
. Then there is an
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
-equivariant bijection

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}).

Here
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
denotes the quotient
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})/{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
, where
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})\leq{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
is the group of
\mathcal{F}
-fusion preserving automorphisms of
S
. The above equivalences
\sim_{\mathcal{F}}
are defined precisely in Section
2
, as is the natural action of
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
on the equivalence classes
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})
. The conjectured existence of an
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
-equivariant bijection is partially inspired by the
inductive McKay condition
for simple groups due to Isaacs–Malle–Navarro
[
11
]
; indeed it seems likely that the natural map
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(G)\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
(see
[
1
, Section 4]
) can be used to exhibit a precise connection with that condition when
\mathcal{F}
is realisable by a simple group
G
, but we do not attempt that here. As with Conjecture
1.1
, it is relatively straightforward to prove Conjecture
1.3
when
v_{\ell}(|W|)=0
(see Proposition
2.9
). Our second main result supplies some evidence for the case
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
. We first recall the fact, due to González
[
10
, Theorem 1]
that any
\ell
-local finite group
\mathcal{F}
on
S
can be approximated as a direct limit of a sequence of (categorical) inclusions of saturated fusion systems on finite
\ell
-groups

(\mathcal{F}_{1},S_{1})\rightarrow(\mathcal{F}_{2},S_{2})\rightarrow(\mathcal{F}_{3},S_{3})\rightarrow\cdots

with
S=\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}S_{n}
. We use this to establish a ‘truncated’ version of
Conjecture
1.3
in the case
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a simple
\ell
-local compact group with Weyl group
W
and assume
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
. Then there is a sequence of fusion system inclusions
(\mathcal{F}_{n},S_{n})_{n\geq 1}
for which
\mathcal{F}=\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}_{n}
where for each
n\geq 1
,
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is a finite fusion system on the
\ell^{n}
-torsion subgroup
S_{n}
of
S
. Moreover, for each
n\geq 1
, there is a bijection

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}_{n}).

We prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
in Section
5
. The proofs only require the character theory of finite groups with Sylow
\ell
-subgroups of order
\ell
and particular information concerning the
\mathcal{F}
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroups mostly established in
[
16
]
(see Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
). The connectedness of
\mathcal{F}
is crucial to ensuring that there are a sufficiently small number of classes of
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroups.

We expect Theorem
1.4
to follow from Conjecture
1.3
. In
[
12
]
, Junod, Levi and Libman prove that for any
\ell
-local compact group
\mathcal{F}
with torus
T
and any
\zeta\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}^{\times}
with
v_{\ell}(\zeta-1)>\!\!>0
,
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
contains an
unstable Adams operation

\Psi=\Psi(\zeta)
with the property that
\Psi|_{T}(g)=g^{\zeta}
for each
g\in T
. Given such an operation
\Psi
, computations of González
[
9
]
seem to indicate that the homotopy fixed points of powers of
\Psi
stratify
\mathcal{F}
as a direct limit of finite fusion systems
\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\mid n\geq 1\}
where
\mathcal{F}_{n}
is the fusion subsystem of
\mathcal{F}
fixed by
\Psi^{n}
(so
\mathcal{F}=\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}_{n}
). Thus the
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
-equivariance in Conjecture
1.3
should imply the existence of bijections between sets fixed by
\Psi^{n}\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
as in Theorem
1.4
.

2.
\ell
-local compact groups and weights

For the rest of the paper,
\ell
is a prime and
k
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\ell
. We refer the reader to
[
2
]
for basic notation and results in the theory of fusion systems. We begin by introducing the class of
\ell
-groups on which we focus our attention.

Definition 2.1
.

Write
{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{\infty}
for the infinite union of the cyclic
\ell
-groups
{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{n}
under the obvious inclusions. A group isomorphic to a direct product of finitely many copies of
{\mathbb{Z}}/\ell^{\infty}
is called a
discrete
\ell
-torus
.
A discrete
\ell
-toral group
is a group
S
which contains a discrete
\ell
-torus
T
as a normal subgroup of finite
\ell
-power index.

As observed in
[
3
, Lemma 1.3]
, any quotient, subgroup or extension of an infinite discrete
\ell
-toral group
S
is a discrete
\ell
-toral group and
S
contains only finitely many conjugacy classes of subgroups of order
\ell^{n}
for every
n\geq 0
(
[
3
, Lemma 1.4]
). By
[
3
, Lemma 1.9]
there is an increasing sequence
S_{1}\leq S_{2}\leq S_{3}\leq\cdots
of
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
-invariant
\ell^{n}
-torsion subgroups of
S
, whose direct limit is
S=\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}S_{n}
. When
S
is abelian, the sequence

\cdots\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(S_{3},{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(S_{2},{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(S_{1},{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(S_{1},{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})

is
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
-invariant, so that
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
acts naturally on the corresponding inverse limit
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S)=\displaystyle\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longleftarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}{{\operatorname{Hom}}}(S_{n},{\mathbb{C}}^{\times})
.

A
fusion system

\mathcal{F}
over
S
with discrete torus
T
is defined by analogy with the finite case
[
3
, Definition 2.1]
. What it means for
\mathcal{F}
to be
saturated
is almost analogous, provided one makes an additional assumption that morphisms behave well with respect to filtrations of subgroups (see
[
3
, Definition 2.2 (III)]
).

Definition 2.2
.

Let
S
be a discrete
\ell
-toral group with discrete torus
T
. An
\ell
-local compact group
is a saturated fusion system on
S
.
\mathcal{F}
is, in addition,
connected
if
T
is the unique maximal abelian subgroup of
S
up to
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy and every element of
S
is
\mathcal{F}
-conjugate to an element of
T
. In this case we refer to the group
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(T)
as the
Weyl group
of
\mathcal{F}
.

Note that the definition of
connected
here differs slightly to that found in
[
8
, Definition 3.1.4]
. The definitions of
fully
\mathcal{F}
-centralised
,
fully
\mathcal{F}
-normalised
,
\mathcal{F}
-centric
and
\mathcal{F}
-radical
are unchanged from those for fusion systems on finite groups. We write
\mathcal{F}^{cr}
for the set of
\mathcal{F}
-centric radical subgroups. As in
[
16
, Definition 1.4]
, we say that
\mathcal{F}
is
simple
if it possesses no non-trivial normal subsystems. Using a certain “bullet construction” first considered by Benson (see
[
3
, Definition 3.1]
) it is possible to prove the following result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
\ell
-local compact group on
S
. Then
{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(P)
is finite for all
P\leq S
. Moreover,
\mathcal{F}
has only finitely many conjugacy classes of
\mathcal{F}
-centric,
\mathcal{F}
-radical subgroups.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Proposition 2.3 and Corollary 3.5]
.
∎

In particular the Weyl group of
\mathcal{F}
is finite. We are interested in the following invariant.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be an
\ell
-local compact group on
S
. The
number of weights

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
of
\mathcal{F}
is given by:

\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F}):=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{F}^{cr}/\mathcal{F}}z(k{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(P)),

where the sum runs over a set of
\mathcal{F}
-conjugacy class representatives of
\mathcal{F}
-centric,
\mathcal{F}
-radical subgroups and for a group
H
,
z(kH)
denotes the number of projective simple
kH
-modules up to isomorphism.

By Proposition
2.3
,
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
is finite. For the remainder of this section we assume that
\mathcal{F}
be a
connected

\ell
-local compact group on
S
.

Definition 2.5
.

The set
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
of
ordinary weights of height
0
is the set of equivalence classes

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})=\{(\psi,\chi)\mid\psi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S^{ab}),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)}(\psi))\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}},

where
(\psi,\chi)\sim_{\mathcal{F}}(\psi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
if there is
\rho\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
with
\psi^{\rho}=\psi^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\rho^{-1}}=\chi^{\prime}
; we write
[(\psi,\chi)]
for the class of
(\psi,\chi)
.

Recall that an element
\phi\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
is an
automorphism of
\mathcal{F}
if it induces an autofunctor
\Phi:\mathcal{F}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}
where:

(1)

for each
P\in
Ob
(\mathcal{F})
,
\Phi(P)=\phi(P)
;

(2)

for each
\psi\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(P,Q)
,

\Phi(\psi)=\phi|_{Q}\circ\psi\circ(\phi|_{\phi(P)})^{-1}:\phi(P)\rightarrow\phi(Q)

is an element of
{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(P),\phi(Q))
.

Write
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})\leq{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(S)
for the set of all automorphisms of
S
which induce automorphisms of
\mathcal{F}
. Since
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)\unlhd{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
, we define
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F}):={{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})/{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
to be the
outer automorphism group of
\mathcal{F}
and denote by
[\phi]\in{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
the class of an element
\phi
of
{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
. If
s\in Z(S)
then
s
is fully
\mathcal{F}
-centralised, so
C_{\mathcal{F}}(s)
is also a saturated fusion system on
S
and we write
W(s):=C_{W}(s)
.

Lemma 2.6
.

For any
s\in Z(S)
and
\phi\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
, the induced autofunctor
\Phi
restricts to an isomorphism
C_{\mathcal{F}}(s)\rightarrow C_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(s))
which sends
W(s)
to
W(\phi(s))
.

Proof.

Let
P\leq S
, and
\psi\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{C_{\mathcal{F}}(s)}(P,S)
, then by definition of
C_{\mathcal{F}}(s)
,
\psi
extends to a map
\overline{\psi}\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(P\langle s\rangle,S)
which fixes
s
; hence

\phi\circ\overline{\psi}\circ\phi^{-1}\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(P\langle s\rangle),S)={{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(P)\langle\phi(s)\rangle,S)

and this latter map clearly fixes
\phi(s)
and restricts to
\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1}\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(P),S)
. We deduce that
\phi\circ\psi\circ\phi^{-1}\in{{\operatorname{Hom}}}_{C_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(s))}(\phi(P),S)
. Moreover, since
\Phi
fixes
T
,
\phi|_{T}\circ W\circ(\phi|_{T})^{-1}=W
and hence
\phi|_{T}W(s)(\phi|_{T})^{-1}=W(\phi(s))
.
∎

Definition 2.7
.

The set
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})
is the set of equivalence classes

\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}):=\{(s,\chi)\mid s\in Z(S),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(W(s))\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}},

where
(s,\chi)\sim_{\mathcal{F}}(s^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
if and only if there is
\phi\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
with
s^{\prime}=\phi(s)
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi^{\Phi^{-1}|_{W(\phi(s))}}
.

Proposition 2.8
.

{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
acts on
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})
via

[\phi]\cdot[(s,\chi)]=[(\phi(s),\chi^{\Phi^{-1}|_{W(\phi(s))}})],

for each
[\phi]\in{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
and class
[(s,\chi)]\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})
.

Proof.

It suffices to check the action of
[\phi]
on
\chi
is independent of the choice of representative
\phi
. If
\phi^{\prime}\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}(\mathcal{F})
is such that
[\phi^{\prime}]=[\phi]
then
\gamma\circ\phi^{\prime}=\phi
for some
\gamma\in{{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
so

[\phi^{\prime}]\cdot[(s,\chi)]=[(\phi^{\prime}(s),\chi^{\Phi^{\prime-1}})]\sim_{\mathcal{F}}[(\phi(s),\chi^{\Phi^{-1}})]

since
(\gamma\circ\phi^{\prime})(s)=\phi(s)
and
\chi^{\Phi^{\prime-1}\Gamma^{-1}}=\chi^{\Phi^{-1}}
where
\Gamma
is the functor
C_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi^{\prime}(s))\rightarrow C_{\mathcal{F}}(\phi(s))
induced by
\gamma
.
∎

Similarly,
{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
acts on
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
via
[\phi]\cdot[(\psi,\chi)]=[(\psi^{\phi},\chi^{\phi^{-1}}]
for each
[\phi]\in{{\operatorname{Out}}}(\mathcal{F})
and class
[(\psi,\chi)]\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})
and thus the statement of Conjecture
1.3
makes sense. In particular we can prove:

Proposition 2.9
.

Conjecture
1.3
holds if
S
is abelian.

Proof.

Since
|W|
is coprime with
\ell
, by the Glauberman correspondence, there exists an infinite family of
W
-equivariant bijections
S_{n}\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S_{n})
which, by the remarks following Definition
2.2
, induces a
W
-equivariant bijection
S\rightarrow{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S)
. The result follows immediately from this.
∎

We conclude this section by explaining the sense in which Conjecture
1.3
may be regarded as an analogue of OWC for height zero characters. Let
B
be the principal
\ell
-block of a finite group
G
,
S\in{{\operatorname{Syl}}}_{\ell}(G)
be its defect group and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}_{S}(G)
be its fusion system. The maximal defect case of OWC for
B
(see
[
13
, Section 2]
) predicts an equality

(1)

\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{F},d)={{\operatorname{Irr}}}^{d}(B),

for
d=v_{\ell}(|S|)
where
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}^{d}(B)
denotes the set of characters of
\ell
-defect
d
in
B
. We have

(2)

\mathbf{m}(\mathcal{F},v_{\ell}(|S|))=\sum_{\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(S)/{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)}|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)}(\chi))|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S^{ab}:{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S))|=|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})|,

where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.2
. If
G
is of Lie-type we have the following result.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
B=B_{0}(G)
is the principal
\ell
-block of
G=\mathbf{G}^{F}
with defect group
S
, where
\mathbf{G}
is a semisimple algebraic group defined over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
(for which
\ell
is very good) with respect to a Frobenius endomorphism
F:\mathbf{G}\rightarrow\mathbf{G}
and let
\mathcal{F}
be the
\ell
-fusion system of
G
on
S
. If
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
then
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}^{d}(B)|=|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})|
.

Proof.

The proof of
[
14
, Proposition 6.8]
shows that there is a degree-preserving bijection between
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(B_{0}(G))
and the set of irreducible characters in the principal
\ell
-block
B_{0}
of the associated
{\mathbb{Z}}_{\ell}
-spets
{\mathbb{G}}(q)
(see
[
14
, Definition 6.7]
). The number of characters of defect
d
in the latter set is computed in
[
14
, Proposition 6.11]
. In the notation of that result, we see that for each
s\in S
,
u_{s}=0
by
[
14
, Proposition 5.7]
and
W(s)_{\phi_{s}^{-1}\zeta^{-1}}=W(s)
since
q\equiv 1\pmod{\ell}
. Since maximal defect characters only occur when

, the result follows.
∎

Thus, in the situation of Lemma
2.10
, by (
2

is exactly the equality
|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F})|=|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F})|
.

3.
Some character theory

We need an elementary consequence of the character theory of groups with a Sylow
\ell
-subgroup of order
\ell
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
W
be a finite group with a cyclic Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
U
of order
\ell
. Then

|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|-z(kW)=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(N_{W}(U))|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(N_{W}(U))|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(W)|.

Proof.

This follows from the existence of a bijection between blocks of
W
and
N_{W}(U)
with non-trivial defect which preserves the number of irreducible characters (see
[
7
, VII.2.12]
). Hence summing over all such blocks we obtain,

|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|-z(kW)=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(N_{W}(U))|-z(kN_{W}(U))=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(N_{W}(U))|,

since
z(kN_{W}(U))=0
, as required.

∎

The following is sometimes referred to as the “Method of Little Groups.”

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H\unlhd G
be finite groups and
\Theta:={{\operatorname{Irr}}}(H)/G
be a set of
G
-orbit representatives for
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(H)
. Suppose that each
\theta\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(H)
extends to a character
\widehat{\theta}\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(I_{G}(\theta))
, where
I_{G}(\theta)
denotes the inertia subgroup of
\theta
in
G
. Then there is a bijection

\{(\theta,\beta)\mid\theta\in\Theta,\beta\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(I_{G}(\theta)/H)\}\longrightarrow{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(G),\mbox{ given by }(\theta,\beta)\mapsto(\hat{\theta}\beta)_{I_{G}(\theta)}^{G}.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
5
, Theorem 11.5]
.
∎

The next lemma concerning characters of the extension of a normal subgroup by an
\ell^{\prime}
-group is well-known.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
G
be a finite group with
N\unlhd G
. Suppose that
V
is an inertial projective simple
kN
-module and that
G/N
is a cyclic
\ell^{\prime}
-group. Then
G
has exactly
|G:N|
projective simple modules lying over
V
. In particular if
N=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)\leq G\leq\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)
, then
z(kG)=|G:\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)|
.

Proof.

See, for example,
[
13
, Lemma 4.12]
.
∎

The following technical result is used in our calculation of
\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})
in Section
4
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X_{1}
be a finite
\ell^{\prime}
-group and
X_{2}
be a finite group and suppose
H
is a normal subgroup of
X_{1}\times X_{2}
with
(X_{1}\times X_{2})/H
cyclic of order
e\mid\ell-1
. The following hold:

(1)

If
X_{2}=\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)
and
O^{\ell^{\prime}}(H)=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)
then
z(kO^{\ell^{\prime}}(H)X_{1})=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X_{1})|\cdot(\ell-1)/e
.

(2)

If
X_{2}=N_{\operatorname{GL}_{2}(\ell)}(U)
for some Sylow
\ell
-subgroup
U
of
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)
and
O^{\ell^{\prime}}(H)=U
then
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(H)|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X_{1})|\cdot\ell\cdot(\ell-1)/e
.

Proof.

Suppose the hypotheses of (1) hold. Since
X_{1}
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-group,
z(k(X_{1}\times X_{2}))=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X_{1})|\cdot(\ell-1)
by Lemma
3.3
. On the other hand, Lemma
3.3
applied to

and

yields
z(k(X_{1}\times X_{2}))=z(kO^{\ell^{\prime}}(H)X_{1})\cdot e
and (1) follows from this. Part (2) is proved similarly: in this case

, and this group has
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X_{1})|\ell(\ell-1)
irreducible characters. Now Lemma
3.2
implies that
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X_{1})|\ell(\ell-1)=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(H)|e
, as needed.
∎

We first deal with the subgroups

we have,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|-\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})&=&|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|-(z(kW)+z(k{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q))+z(k{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S))\\
&=&|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(N_{W}(U))|-|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}({{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S))|-z(k{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)).\end{array}

The descriptions of the groups
N_{W}(U),{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S)
and
{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(Q)
in Theorem
4.5
combined with Lemma
3.4
then yield:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(W)|-\mathbf{w}(\mathcal{F})&=&\ell|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X)|-(\ell-1)|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X)|-z(k(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)X))\\
&=&|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(X)|-z(k(\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\ell)X))\\
&=&0,\end{array}

where
X
is as in Lemma
4.3
. The result follows from this.
∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a simple
\ell
-local compact group with
v_{\ell}(|W|)=1
and write
\displaystyle\mathcal{F}=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}\longrightarrow\\
n\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathcal{F}_{n}
for saturated fusion systems
\mathcal{F}_{n}
on
S_{n}\leq S
as described in Proposition
4.6
. For each
n
we have,

|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{n})|=|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}_{n})|.

Proof.

Let
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
be fixed and set
T_{n}:=T\cap S_{n}
,
W={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(T_{n})={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(T_{n})
and
U={{\operatorname{Aut}}}_{S_{n}}(T_{n})\leq W
. We have two bijections

\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{n})\longleftrightarrow{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(S_{n}^{ab}:{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S_{n}))\longleftrightarrow{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(T_{n}N_{W}(U)),

the first from the discussion before Lemma
2.10
and the second from the isomorphism
S_{n}^{ab}:{{\operatorname{Out}}}_{\mathcal{F}}(S_{n})\cong T_{n}N_{W}(U)
(implied by Theorems
4.1
and
4.5
). By Lemma
3.2
this latter set is in bijection with equivalence classes of pairs

\{(\psi,\chi)\mid\psi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(T_{n}),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{N_{W}(U)}(\psi))\mid[S_{n},S_{n}]\leq\ker(\psi)\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

where
(\psi,\chi)\sim_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(\psi^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
if there is
\rho\in N_{W}(U)
with
\psi^{\rho}=\psi^{\prime}
and
\chi^{\rho^{-1}}=\chi^{\prime}
. By the Glauberman-Isaacs correspondence there exists a
N_{W}(U)
-equivariant bijection between
T_{n}
and
{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(T_{n})
which maps
Z(S_{n})=C_{T_{n}}(U)
to
\{\psi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(T_{n})\mid[S_{n},S_{n}]\leq\ker(\psi)\}
, and hence
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{n})
is in bijection with

\{(s,\chi)\mid s\in Z(S_{n}),\chi\in{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{N_{W}(U)}(s))\}/\sim_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}

where
(s,\chi)\sim_{\mathcal{F}_{n}}(s^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
if and only if there is
\phi\in N_{W}(U)
with
s^{\prime}=\phi(s)
and
\chi^{\prime}=\chi^{\phi^{-1}}
. Now since every morphism between elements of
Z(S_{n})
extends to an
\mathcal{F}_{n}
-automorphism of
S_{n}
and
|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{N_{W}(U)}(s))|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(C_{N_{W}(U)}(s))|=|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(C_{W}(s))|
for each
s\in Z(S_{n})
(by Lemma
3.1
), we have
|\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{F}_{n})|=

\sum_{s\in Z(S_{n})/N_{W}(U)}|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}(C_{N_{W}(U)}(s))|=\sum_{s\in Z(S_{n})/\mathcal{F}_{n}}|{{\operatorname{Irr}}}_{0}(C_{W}(s))|=|\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{F}_{n})|,

as required.

∎

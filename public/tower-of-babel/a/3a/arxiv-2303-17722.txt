Semiclassical estimates for measure potentials on the real line
Andrés Larraín-Hubach, Jacob Shapiro

Abstract
. We prove an explicit weighted estimate for the semiclassical Schrödinger operator P = − h 2 ∂ x 2 + V ( x ; h ) P=-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{x}+V(x;h) on L 2 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{2}(\mathbb{R}) , with V ⁡ ( x , h ) V(x;h) a finite signed measure, and where h > 0 h>0 is the semiclassical parameter. The proof is a one dimensional instance of the spherical energy method, which has been used to prove Carleman estimates in higher dimensions and in more complicated geometries. The novelty of our result is that the potential need not be absolutely continuous with respect to Lebesgue measure. Two consequences of the weighted estimate are the absence of positive eigenvalues for P P , and a limiting absorption resolvent estimate with sharp h h -dependence. The resolvent estimate implies exponential time-decay of the local energy for solutions to the corresponding wave equation with a compactly supported measure potential, provided there are no negative eigenvalues and no zero resonance, and provided the initial data have compact support.



1.
Introduction and statement of results

The goal of this note is to study the spectral and scattering theory for the one dimensional semiclassical Schrödinger operator,

(1.1)

P=P(h)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}+V(x;h):L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\qquad h>0,

with potential
V=V(x;h)
a real, finite signed Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
, which may depend on the semiclassical parameter
h
. Here and below,
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is the usual Hilbert space of equivalence classes of functions
u:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
which are measurable with respect to the Lebesgue sigma algebra on
\mathbb{R}
, and for which
\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}dx<\infty
, where
dx
denotes Lebesgue measure.

Self-adjointness of singular Sturm-Liouville operators encompassing (
1.1
) was systematically addressed in earlier works
[
HrMy01
,
HrMy12
,
EGNT13
]
. With the objective of being self contained, we proceed in elementary fashion to specify the domain
\mathcal{D}
of
P
as a certain dense subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
which is contained in the Sobolev space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Recall each
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
has a (unique) continuous, bounded representative, which we denote by
u_{c}
. Thus, for
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, we prescribe the product of
u
and
V
to be the complex Borel measure
u_{c}V
, and define the expression

(1.2)

Pu\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{x}u+u_{c}V

in the sense of distributions on
\mathbb{R}
. Using the calculus of functions of bounded variation (which we review in Section
2
), we show in Section
3
that (for all
h>0
)
P
is self-adjoint with respect to

(1.3)

\mathcal{D}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}):u^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\text{ and }Pu\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}.

The main result of this note, whose proof appears in Section
4
, is the following weighted estimate on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix
\delta>0
. For all
E=E(h)>0
(which may depend on
h
),
\varepsilon\in[0,1]
,
h>0
, and
u\in\mathcal{D}
with
(|x|+1)^{(1+\delta)/2}(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

(1.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}(E|u|^{2}&+|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&\leq C(V,E,h,\delta)\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|x|+1)^{1+\delta}|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Here,

(1.5)

\displaystyle C(V,E,h,\delta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big(\big(\frac{4}{h^{2}}+\frac{2}{h}+\frac{1}{Eh^{2}}\big(2+2E+\frac{\|V\|^{2}}{h^{2}}\big)^{2}e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big),

(1.6)

\displaystyle C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2\delta^{-1}+E^{-1/2}h^{-1}\|V\|,

and
\|V\|\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|V|(\mathbb{R})
, with
|V|
the total variation of
V
, defined by

|V|=V^{+}+V^{-},

where
\{V^{+},V^{-}\}
is the Jordan decomposition of

(see, e.g.,
[
Fo99
, Theorem 3.4]
).

Remark 1.2
.

In the case that
E>0
is fixed independent of
h
, and we restrict

, the constant (
1.5
) is bounded from above by the more succinct expression

(1.7)

\exp(\tilde{C}(E,\delta)(1+\|V\|)/h),\qquad\text{for some $\tilde{C}(E,\delta)>0$ depending on $E$ and $\delta$.}

Two consequences of Theorem
1.1
are the absence of positive eigenvalues of

, and a weighted limiting absorption resolvent bound.

Corollary 1.3
.

The operator
P
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, given by (
1.2
) and equipped with domain (
1.3
), has no positive eigenvalues.

Corollary 1.4
.

Fix
\delta>0
. Then for all
E=E(h)>0
(which may depend on
h
),
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, and

(1.8)

\|(|x|+1)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}(|x|+1)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\Big(\frac{C(V,E,h,\delta)}{E}\Big)^{1/2},

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we employ a positive commutator-style argument in the context of the so-called spherical energy method. This strategy has long been used to prove Carleman and related estimates
[
CaVo02
,
Da14
,
KlVo19
,
DaSh20
,
GaSh22b
,
Ob23
]
. In fact, as we work in one dimension, it suffices to use the pointwise energy

(1.10)

\begin{gathered}F(x)=F_{\pm}[u](x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|hu^{\prime}(x)|^{2}+E|u(x)|^{2},\\
u\in\mathcal{D}\text{ such that }(|x|+1)^{(1+\delta)/2}(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).\end{gathered}

The goal is to construct a suitable weight
w(x)
having locally bounded variation, so that, roughly speaking, the distributional derivative of
wF
is bounded from above by a term involving

2w\mathop{\rm Re}((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u\overline{u}^{\prime})
, and bounded from below by
w(E|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2})
(see (
4.6
) for the precise estimate). To attain the upper bound,
V
needs to be reintroduced after differentiation of
wF
, at the cost of a perturbation term (see (
4.4
), and note
V
does not appear in
F
in the first place because its distributional derivative may be irregular). This perturbation can be controlled, yielding the desired the lower bound, by designing
w
appropriately. In particular, since
V
may have discrete part
V_{d}
(i.e., countably many point masses which are absolutely summable), we use a family of weights
w_{\eta}(x)
depending on a parameter
\eta>0
. Each
w_{\eta}
controls a certain Gaussian approximation of
|V_{d}|
(see (
4.2
)). We then show that the needed estimates hold uniformly as
\eta\to 0^{+}
.

When

is compactly supported, it is well known that Corollary

is related to the distribution of scattering resonances for the operator

. As in

(1.11)

\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}+V-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D},\qquad\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,1]),\chi\equiv 1\text{ on }\mathop{\rm supp}V,

which continues meromorphically from
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\gg 1
to the complex plane. In Section
6
, we combine (
1.8
) with a resolvent identity argument of Vodev
[

, Theorem 1.5]
to show

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
V
is a finite signed Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
, which is supported in
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
for some
R_{0}>0
. Fix
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\chi=1
near
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
, and fix
\lambda_{0}>0
. There exist
C,\varepsilon_{0}>0
so that for all
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
, and
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
,

(1.12)

\|\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}+V-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to H^{k}}\leq C|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{k-1},\qquad k=0,\,1\,(H^{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R})),

and

(1.13)

\|\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}+V-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\leq C(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1),

where
\mathcal{D}
is equipped with the graph norm
\|u\|_{\mathcal{D}}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\|(-\partial^{2}_{x}+V)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}})^{1/2}

The existence of resonance free regions below the real axis is a long-studied problem:

Review of BV

To keep the notation concise, for the rest of the article, we use “prime” notation to denote differentiation with respect to
x

u^{\prime}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\partial_{x}u

In Section
2

2.2
, while proofs of Propostions

, and

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
be a function of locally bounded variation. For all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, put

(2.1)

f^{L}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f(x-\delta),\qquad f^{R}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\lim_{\delta\to 0^{+}}f(x+\delta),\qquad f^{A}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(f^{L}(x)+f^{R}(x))/2,

where the limits exist because both the real and imaginary parts of
f
are a difference of two increasing functions. Recall that
f
is differentiable Lebesgue almost everywhere, so
f(x)=f^{L}(x)=f^{R}(x)=f^{A}(x)
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We may decompose
f
as

(2.2)

f=f_{r,+}-f_{r,-}+i(f_{i,+}-f_{i,-}),

where the
f_{\sigma,\pm}
,
\sigma\in\{r,i\}
, are increasing functions on
\mathbb{R}
. Each

uniquely determines a regular Borel measure
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}
on
\mathbb{R}
satisfying
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{1},x_{2}]=f^{R}_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{2})-f^{R}_{\sigma,\pm}(x_{1})
, see
[

, Theorem 1.16]
. We put

(2.3)

df\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\mu_{r,+}-\mu_{r,-}+i(\mu_{i,+}-\mu_{i,-}),

which is a complex measure when restricted to any bounded Borel subset. For any
a<b
,

(2.4)

\begin{gathered}\int_{(a,b]}df=f^{R}(b)-f^{R}(a),\\
\int_{(a,b)}df=f^{L}(b)-f^{R}(a).\end{gathered}

Proposition 2.1

(integration by parts)
.

Let
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
have locally BV. For any
a<b
, and any continuous
\varphi

\int_{(a,b]}\varphi df+\int_{(a,b]}\varphi^{\prime}fdx=f^{R}(b)\varphi(b)-f^{R}(a)\varphi(a).

Proposition 2.2

(funadmental theorem of calculus)
.

Let
\mu_{\sigma,\pm}
,
\sigma\in\{r,\,i\}
be positive Borel measures on
\mathbb{R}
which are finite on all bounded Borel subsets of

. Suppose

\mu=\mu_{r,+}-\mu_{r,-}+i(\mu_{i,+}-\mu_{i,-})

f_{\mu}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\begin{cases}\int_{[a,x]}d\mu&x\geq a,\\

We need to show that the function (

3.
Self-adjointness for
V
a measure

The goal of this section is to use the tools of Section

to show
(P,\mathcal{D})
is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, where
\mathcal{D}
is given by (
1.3
). This strategy sets the stage for several steps in the proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
4
. We demonstrate that
(P,\mathcal{D})
is merely the self-adjoint operator naturally associated to the quadratic form

(3.1)

q(u,v)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int h^{2}\overline{u}^{\prime}v^{\prime}dx+\int\overline{u_{c}}v_{c}V,\qquad u,\,v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

As mentioned in Section
1
, self-adjointness was addressed in greater generality elsewhere
[
HrMy01
,
HrMy12
,
EGNT13
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V=V(x;h)
be a real, finite signed Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\mathcal{D}
specified by (
1.3
) is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. The operator
P:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
given by (
1.2
) with domain
\mathcal{D}
is self-adjoint.

Proof.

Throughout the proof, we work with
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
that have locally integrable distributional derivative
u^{\prime}
. Such
u
have a (unique, locally absolutely) continuous representative
u_{c}
, hence we can define the product
u_{c}V
as a distribution on
\mathbb{R}
, since it is a complex measure when restricted to bounded Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}\supseteq\mathcal{D}
be the set of all
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
u^{\prime}\in L^{1}_{\textrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
and
Pu\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime}+u_{c}V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. By Proposition
2.2
, for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}
, we may fix a representative
u_{\text{bv}}^{\prime}
for
u^{\prime}
that has locally bounded variation. If necessary, we redefine
u_{\text{bv}}^{\prime}
on a set of Lebesgue measure zero so that
u_{\text{bv}}^{\prime}(x)=(u_{\text{bv}}^{\prime})^{A}(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
(this updated
u^{\prime}_{\text{bv}}
still has locally BV).

In the computations to follow, we always work with the representatives
u_{c}
and
u_{\text{bv}}^{\prime}
, but we drop subscripts to keep notation concise. This convention ensures that expressions like
u^{\prime}V
are well defined as complex Borel measures (on bounded Borel subsets), since locally BV functions are Borel measurable. It also simplifies some calculations that involve (
2.4
) or (
2.8
).

Our first step is to prove
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
. Since the reverse containment is trivial, we will conclude
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}=\mathcal{D}
. Indeed, for
u\in\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}
and any
a>0
,

(3.2)

\begin{split}\int_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx&=\int_{(-a,a)}u^{\prime}d(\overline{u})\\
&=\int_{(-a,a)}d(u^{\prime}\overline{u})-\overline{u}d(u^{\prime})\\
&=(u^{\prime}\bar{u})(a)-(u^{\prime}\bar{u})(-a)+h^{-2}\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}Pudx-h^{-2}\int_{(-a,a)}\overline{u}uV\\
&\leq 2\sup_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|+h^{-2}\|V\|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|^{2}+h^{-2}\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}},\end{split}

where
\|V\|\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=|V|(\mathbb{R})
, with
|V|
the total variation of
V
. The second line of (
3.2
) follows from (
2.8
) and
u=u^{A}
,
u^{\prime}=(u^{\prime})^{A}
; the third line follows from the fact that
-h^{2}d(u^{\prime})=Pu-uV
as Borel measures, which is a consequence of (
2.7
).

Since
u
is locally absolutely continuous,

(3.3)

\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|^{2}=\sup_{x\in(-a,a)}\Big(|u(0)|^{2}+2\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{0}^{x}u^{\prime}\overline{u}dx\Big)\leq|u(0)|^{2}+2\Big(\int_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Furthermore, if
x\in(0,a)
, then by (
2.4
), (
2.8
) and
u^{\prime}=(u^{\prime})^{A}
,

\begin{split}(u^{\prime}&\overline{u}^{\prime})(x)\\
&=(u^{\prime}\overline{u}^{\prime})(0)+\int_{(0,x]}d(u^{\prime}\overline{u}^{\prime})\\
&=|u^{\prime}|^{2}(0)-2h^{-2}\mathop{\rm Re}\Big(\int_{(0,x]}\overline{u}^{\prime}Pudx-\int_{(0,x]}\overline{u}^{\prime}uV\Big)\\
&\leq|u^{\prime}|^{2}(0)+2h^{-2}\|V\|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|+2h^{-2}\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\int_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2},\end{split}

while if
x\in(-a,0)
, we similarly find

\begin{split}(u^{\prime}&\overline{u}^{\prime})(x)\\
&=(u^{\prime}\overline{u}^{\prime})(0)-\int_{(x,0]}d(u^{\prime}\overline{u}^{\prime})\\
&\leq|u^{\prime}|^{2}(0)+2h^{-2}\|V\|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|\sup_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|+2h^{-2}\|Pu\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\int_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx\Big)^{1/2}.\end{split}

We thus arrive at a system of inequalities of the form
x^{2}\leq 2yz+Ay^{2}+B
,
y^{2}\leq C+Dx
,
z^{2}\leq E+Fyz+Gx
, where
x\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(\int_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx)^{1/2}
,
y\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup_{(-a,a)}|u|
, and
z\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\sup_{(-a,a)}|u^{\prime}|
. After using the second inequality to eliminate
y
, we obtain a system in
x
and
z
with quadratic left hand sides and subquadratic right hand sides. Hence
x
,
y
, and
z
are each bounded in terms of
A,B,\dots,G
. Letting
a\to\infty
, we conclude that
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and
u,\,u^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. Hence
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}\subseteq\mathcal{D}
as desired.

Next, we equip
P
with the domain
\mathcal{D}_{\textrm{max}}=\mathcal{D}
, and show that
P
is symmetric. Let
u,v\in\mathcal{D}
, and take
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}\subseteq C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
converging to
v
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Using the distributional definition of
Pu
,

(3.4)

\begin{split}\langle Pu,v\rangle_{L^{2}}&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\langle Pu,\varphi_{k}\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int\overline{u}(-h^{2}\varphi_{k}^{\prime\prime})dx+\int\overline{u}\varphi_{k}V\\
&=\lim_{k\to\infty}\int h^{2}\overline{u}^{\prime}\varphi_{k}^{\prime}dx+\int\overline{u}\varphi_{k}V\\
&=\int h^{2}\overline{u}^{\prime}v^{\prime}dx+\int\overline{u}vV,\end{split}

where the last equal sign follows since
|V|
is finite and
\|w\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|w\|_{L^{2}}\|w^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}
for any
w\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Approximating
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
by
C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
-functions, we similarly have
\langle u,Pv\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int h^{2}\overline{u}^{\prime}v^{\prime}dx+\int\overline{u}vV
. Thus
P
is symmetric.

The last step is to establish that
(P,\mathcal{D})
is densely defined and
P^{*}\subseteq P
. For this, define on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
the quadratic form (
3.1
). Since, for any
\gamma>0
,

(3.5)

\begin{split}\left|\int V|u|^{2}\right|&\leq\|V\|\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\\
&\leq\|V\|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|V\|\left(\tfrac{1}{2\gamma}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\right),\end{split}

setting
\gamma=h^{2}/\|V\|
yields

(3.6)

-\tfrac{\|V\|^{2}}{2h^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{h^{2}}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq q(u,u)\leq\tfrac{\|V\|^{2}}{2h^{2}}\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{3h^{2}}{2}\|u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

We thus conclude
q
is semibounded and closed.

By Friedrichs’ result
[
Te14
, Theorem 2.14]
, there is a unique (densely defined) self-adjoint operator
(A,\mathcal{D}_{1})
with

\begin{gathered}\mathcal{D}_{1}=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}):\text{there exists $\tilde{u}\in L^{2}$ with $q(u,v)=\langle\tilde{u},v\rangle_{L^{2}}$ for all $v\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})$}\},\\
Au=\tilde{u}.\end{gathered}

Revisiting the calculation (
3.4
), we see that for any
u\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
,
\tilde{u}=-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime}+uV
in the distributional sense. Thus
(A,\mathcal{D}_{1})\subseteq(P,\mathcal{D}_{\max})
, so
P^{*}\subseteq A^{*}=A\subseteq P
. Since we already showed
P\subseteq P^{*}
(symmetricity), we conclude
P^{*}=P
as desired.

∎

4.
Weighted estimate

The purpose of this Section is to prove Theorem
1.1
. As discussed in Section
1
, we do so by means of a positive commutator argument that leverages the energy method.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Our starting point is the pointwise energy
F
given by (
1.10
). As in the proof of Lemma
3.1
, we fix with a continuous representative of
u\in\mathcal{D}
, and fix a representative of
u^{\prime}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
that has locally bounded variation and
(u^{\prime})^{A}=u^{\prime}
(thus
F^{A}=F
).

Since the measure
V=V(x;h)
is finite, it can have only countably many point masses
\{x_{j}\}_{j}
, and moreover
\sum_{j}|V_{j}|<\infty
, where
V_{j}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=V(\{x_{j}\})
. Let us decompose

V=V_{c}+V_{d},\qquad V_{d}=\sum_{j}V_{j}\delta_{x_{j}},

into its discrete and continuous parts. Here,
\delta_{x_{j}}
denotes the dirac measure concentrated at
x_{j}
. A key technical feature of the ensuing calculations is our use of the weight function

(4.1)

w=w_{\eta}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\exp\Big(\int_{-\infty}^{x}\big[\frac{1}{E^{1/2}h}|V_{c}|+(\frac{1}{E^{1/2}h}V_{d,\eta}(x^{\prime})+(|x^{\prime}|+1)^{-1-\delta})\big]dx^{\prime}\Big),\qquad\eta>0,

where
|V_{c}|
denotes the total variation of
V_{c}
, and

(4.2)

V_{d,\eta}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\pi^{-1/2}\eta^{-1}\sum_{j}|V_{j}|e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}.

The exponent of
w
is a continuous function, thus we may compute
dw
using (
2.9
). Nearing the end of the argument, we manage to control a term involving
|V_{d}|
by sending
\eta\to 0^{+}
, essentially using that
\pi^{-1/2}\eta^{-1}\sum_{j}|V_{j}|e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}\to\sum_{j}|V_{j}|\delta_{x_{j}}
in the distribution sense. We note also that

(4.3)

\sup_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{\eta}(x)|\leq e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)},

with
C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)
given by (
1.6
).

From (
2.8
) and
u^{A}=u
,
(u^{\prime})^{A}=u^{\prime}
, we find, in the sense of measures on
\mathbb{R}
,

\begin{split}dF&=2h^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}(\overline{u}^{\prime}d(u^{\prime}))+2E\mathop{\rm Re}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)\\
&=-2\mathop{\rm Re}(((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}^{\prime})\mp 2\varepsilon\mathop{\rm Im}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)+2\mathop{\rm Re}(u\overline{u}^{\prime}V),\end{split}

where, to get the second line, we used that
-h^{2}d(u^{\prime})=Pu-uV
as Borel measures. Using (
2.8
) again, this time to expand
d(wF)
,

(4.4)

\begin{split}d(wF)&=Fdw+wdF\\
&=|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dw+E|u|^{2}dw\\
&-2w\mathop{\rm Re}(((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}^{\prime})\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)+2\mathop{\rm Re}w(u\overline{u}^{\prime}V)\\
&\geq-2w\mathop{\rm Re}(((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}^{\prime})\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)\\
&+(|hu^{\prime}|^{2}+E|u|^{2})dw-h^{-1}w(E^{1/2}|u|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})(|V_{c}|+\sum_{j}|V_{j}|\delta_{x_{j}}).\end{split}

By (
2.9
) and (
4.1
),

(4.5)

dw_{\eta}=h^{-1}w_{\eta}(E^{-1/2}|V_{c}|+E^{-1/2}\eta^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}\sum_{j}|V_{j}|e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}dx+h(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}dx).

Plugging (
4.5
) into the fifth line of (
4.4
) implies,

(4.6)

\begin{split}d(wF)&\geq-2w\mathop{\rm Re}(((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}^{\prime})\mp 2\varepsilon w\mathop{\rm Im}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)\\
&+w(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}(E|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&+\sum_{j}h^{-1}w(E^{1/2}|u|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})|V_{j}|(\eta^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}dx-\delta_{x_{j}})\end{split}

Next, we note there exist sequences
\{a^{\pm}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
tending to
\pm\infty
, along which
F(a^{\pm}_{n})=F^{R}(a^{\pm}_{n})=F^{L}(a^{\pm}_{n})\to 0
. This is because
F(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and is continuous off of a countable set. So, we integrate both sides of (
4.6
) over
(a^{-}_{n},a^{+}_{n}]
and send
n\to\infty
. By (
2.4
), the left side of (
4.6
) becomes zero. Hence, from (
4.3
) and
w\geq 1
,

(4.7)

\begin{split}\int&(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}(E|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&+\sum_{j}h^{-1}|V_{j}|\int w(E^{1/2}|u|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})(\eta^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}dx-\delta_{x_{j}})\\
&\leq e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\big(\int\frac{1}{\gamma h^{2}}(|x|+1)^{1+\delta}|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}+\gamma(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}|hu^{\prime}|^{2}\\
&+2\varepsilon\int|uu^{\prime}|dx\big),\qquad\gamma,\,h>0.\end{split}

The goal of the following calculations is to show that the second line of (
4.7
) is nonnegative in the limit as
\eta\to 0^{+}
. First notice that as
\eta\to 0^{+}
,

\begin{gathered}\int_{-\infty}^{x_{j}}V_{d,\eta}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=\pi^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell}|V_{\ell}|\int_{-\infty}^{(x_{j}-x_{\ell})/\eta}e^{-(x^{\prime})^{2}}dx^{\prime}\to\frac{1}{2}|V_{j}|+\sum_{x_{\ell}<x_{j}}|V_{\ell}|,\\
\int_{-\infty}^{x_{j}+\eta x}V_{d,\eta}(x^{\prime})dx^{\prime}=\pi^{-1/2}\sum_{\ell}|V_{\ell}|\int_{-\infty}^{\frac{x_{j}-x_{\ell}}{\eta}+x}e^{-(x^{\prime})^{2}}dx^{\prime}\to\pi^{-1/2}|V_{j}|\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-(x^{\prime})^{2}}dx^{\prime}+\sum_{x_{\ell}<x_{j}}|V_{\ell}|.\end{gathered}

This implies

\begin{gathered}w_{\eta}(x_{j})\to e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{2E^{1/2}h}\Big)\\
w_{\eta}(x_{j}+\eta x)\to e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{(\pi E)^{1/2}h}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-(x^{\prime})^{2}}dx^{\prime}\Big),\end{gathered}

where

e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\exp\Big(\frac{1}{E^{1/2}h}\sum_{x_{\ell}<x_{j}}|V_{\ell}|+\int_{-\infty}^{x_{j}}\big[\frac{1}{E^{1/2}h}|V_{c}|+(|x^{\prime}|+1)^{-1-\delta})dx^{\prime}\big]\Big).

Therefore,

\begin{split}h^{-1}|V_{j}|\int&w_{\eta}(E^{1/2}|u|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})\delta_{x_{j}}\\
&=h^{-1}|V_{j}|w_{\eta}(x_{j})(E^{1/2}|u(x_{j})|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}(x_{j})|^{2}),\\
&\to h^{-1}|V_{j}|(E^{1/2}|u(x_{j})|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}(x_{j})|^{2})e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{2E^{1/2}h}\Big),\end{split}

while

\begin{split}h^{-1}&\eta^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}|V_{j}|\int w_{\eta}(E^{1/2}|u|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})e^{-((x-x_{j})/\eta)^{2}}dx\\
&=h^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}|V_{j}|\int w_{\eta}(x_{j}+\eta x)(E^{1/2}|u(x_{j}+\eta x)|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}(x_{j}+\eta x)|^{2})e^{-x^{2}}dx,\\
&\to(E^{1/2}|u(x_{j})|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}(x_{j})|^{2})e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\\
&\cdot h^{-1}\pi^{-1/2}|V_{j}|\int\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{(\pi E)^{1/2}h}\int_{-\infty}^{x}e^{-(x^{\prime})^{2}}dx^{\prime}\Big)e^{-x^{2}}dx\\
&=E^{1/2}(E^{1/2}|u(x_{j})|^{2}+E^{-1/2}|hu^{\prime}(x_{j})|^{2})e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\big(\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{E^{1/2}h}\Big)-1\big).\end{split}

In summary, we have shown that the second line of (
4.7
), upon sending
\eta\to 0^{+}
, converges to

(4.8)

\sum_{j}(E|u(x_{j})|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}(x_{j})|^{2})e^{\Gamma(E,h,j)}\big(\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{E^{1/2}h}\Big)-1-\frac{|V_{j}|}{E^{1/2}h}\exp\Big(\frac{|V_{j}|}{2E^{1/2}h}\Big)\big)\geq 0.

The nonnegativity follows from the fact that
e^{x}-1-xe^{x/2}\geq 0
for all
x\geq 0
.

Returning to (
4.7
), we fix
\gamma=2^{-1}e^{-C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}
, so that we may absorb the first term in line three into the left side, and invoke (
4.8
), implying

(4.9)

\begin{split}\int(|x|&+1)^{-1-\delta}(E|u|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&\leq e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big(\frac{2e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}}{h^{2}}\int(|x|+1)^{1+\delta}|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx+2\varepsilon\int|uu^{\prime}|dx\Big),\qquad h>0.\end{split}

For the term in (
4.9
) having the factor of
\varepsilon
:

(4.10)

2\int|uu^{\prime}|dx\leq\frac{1}{h}\int|u|^{2}dx+\frac{1}{h}\int|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dx,\qquad h>0,

and

(4.11)

\begin{split}\int|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dx&=\mathop{\rm Re}\int((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}dx+E\int|u|^{2}dx+\int|u|^{2}V\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx+\big(\frac{1}{2}+E\big)\int|u|^{2}dx+\|V\|\|u\|_{L^{2}}\|u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx+\big(\frac{1}{2}+E+\frac{\|V\|}{2\gamma h^{2}}\big)\int|u|^{2}dx\\
&+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|V\|\int|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dx,\qquad\gamma,\,h>0.\end{split}

Fixing
\gamma=\|V\|^{-1}
in (
4.11
), implies

(4.12)

\int|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dx\leq\int|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx+(1+2E+\frac{\|V\|^{2}}{h^{2}})\int|u|^{2}dx,\qquad h>0.

Now, replace
\int|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dx
in (
4.10
) by the right side of (
4.12
). From this, we get a bound for
2\int|uu^{\prime}|dx
, which we insert into the in the last line of (
4.9
). We conclude

(4.13)

\begin{split}\int(|x|&+1)^{-1-\delta}(E|u|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&\leq e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big(\big(\frac{2e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}}{h^{2}}+\frac{1}{h}\big)\int(|x|+1)^{1+\delta}|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx\\
&+\frac{\varepsilon}{h}\big(2+2E+\frac{\|V\|^{2}}{h^{2}}\big)\int|u|^{2}dx\Big),\qquad\varepsilon\in[0,1],\,h>0.\end{split}

We absorb the last term of (
4.13
) into the left side by estimating

(4.14)

\begin{split}\varepsilon\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}&=\mp\mathop{\rm Im}\langle(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u,u\rangle_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\tfrac{\gamma^{-1}}{2}\|(|x|+1)^{\frac{1+\delta}{2}}(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\|(|x|+1)^{-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}},\end{split}

and then choosing
\gamma=Eh^{-1}(2+2E+h^{-2}\|V\|^{2})^{-1}e^{-C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}
. We then have

(4.15)

\begin{split}\int(|x|&+1)^{-1-\delta}(\tfrac{E}{2}|u|^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2})dx\\
&\leq e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big(\frac{2e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}}{h^{2}}+\frac{1}{h}+\frac{1}{2Eh^{2}}\big(2+2E+\frac{\|V\|^{2}}{h^{2}}\big)^{2}e^{C_{1}(V,E,h,\delta)}\Big)\\
&\cdot\int(|x|+1)^{1+\delta}|(P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u|^{2}dx,\qquad\varepsilon\in[0,1],\,h>0.\end{split}

which implies (
1.4
).

∎

5.
Exterior estimate

Proof of Theorem
1.7
.

We again start from (
1.10
), considering the case
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and putting

f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=(P(h)-E\pm i\varepsilon)^{-1}(|x|+1)^{-(1+\delta)/2}u.

This time, we pair
F
with a much simpler weight
w
. In particular we take
w
to be the continuous, odd function vanishing on
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
, and obeying

w(x)=1-\frac{(1+R_{0})^{\delta}}{(1+x)^{\delta}},\quad x>R_{0}\implies dw=w^{\prime}(x)=\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}(1+|x|)^{-1-\delta}\textbf{1}_{\geq R_{0}}.

Note that the same

was used in the proof of
[
DaSh20
, Theorem 2]
. Since

, we find, proceeding as in (

),

(5.1)

\begin{split}d(wF)=-2w^{A}\mathop{\rm Re}(((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}^{\prime})\mp 2\varepsilon w^{A}\mathop{\rm Im}\left(u\overline{u}^{\prime}\right)+|hu^{\prime}|^{2}dw+E|u|^{2}dw,\end{split}

and thus

(5.2)

\begin{split}\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}(|x|&+1)^{-1-\delta}\big(E|u|^{2}+|hu^{\prime}|^{2}\big)\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\gamma h^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|f|^{2}+\gamma\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}|hu^{\prime}|^{2}\\
&+2\varepsilon\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w|uu^{\prime}|\qquad\gamma,\,h>0.\end{split}

Taking
\gamma=2^{-1}\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}
, we absorb the second term on the right side of (
5.2
) into the left side. To handle the term involving
\varepsilon
, we proceed to find

(5.3)

2\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w|uu^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{h}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w^{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2},\qquad h>0,

and

(5.4)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}&w^{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2}=2h^{2}\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}ww^{\prime}u^{\prime}\overline{u}+\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w^{2}(-h^{2}u^{\prime\prime})\overline{u}\\
&\leq\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}h\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}\big(|u|^{2}+w^{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2}\big)\\
&+\mathop{\rm Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w^{2}((P-E\pm i\varepsilon)u)\overline{u}+E\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|u|^{2}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|f|^{2}+\big(\frac{1}{2}+E+\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}h)\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|u|^{2}\\
&+\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}h\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w^{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2},\qquad h>0.\end{split}

Putting
h_{0}\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=2^{-1}\delta^{-1}(1+R_{0})^{-\delta}
and restricting
h\in(0,h_{0}]
in (
5.4
) allows us to bound
\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w^{2}|hu^{\prime}|^{2}
in (
5.3
) by twice the fourth line of (
5.4
). Inserting the resulting estimate for
2\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}w|u\overline{u}^{\prime}|
into the right side of (
5.2
) yields, for
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
and
h\in(0,h_{0}]
,

(5.5)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}(|x|+1)^{-1-\delta}|u|^{2}\\
&\leq\big(\frac{2}{\delta^{2}E(1+R_{0})^{2\delta}h^{2}}+\frac{1}{E\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}h}\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|f|^{2}+\varepsilon\frac{3+2E}{E\delta(1+R_{0})^{\delta}h}\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}|u|^{2}.\\
\end{split}

The last term in line two of (

) may be estimated in manner similar (

), leading to (

6.
Uniform resolvent estimate and resonance free strips

In this Section, we prove Theorem
1.8
as an application of Corollary
1.4
. We are concerned with the self-adjoint operator

(6.1)

H\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=-\partial^{2}_{x}+V:\mathcal{D}\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where
V
remains a finite signed Borel measure, and has support in
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
for some
R_{0}>0
.

In this situation,
H
is a
black box Hamiltonian
in the sense of Sjöstrand and Zworski
[
SjZw91
]
, as defined in
[
DyZw19
, Definition 4.1]
. More precisely, in our setting this means the following. First, if
u\in\mathcal{D}
, then
u|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}])
. Second, for any
u\in\mathcal{D}
, we have
(Hu)|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]}=-(u|_{\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]})^{\prime\prime}
. Third, any
u\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
which vanishes on a neighborhood of
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
is also in
\mathcal{D}
. Fourth,
\textbf{1}_{[-R_{0},R_{0}]}(H+i)^{-1}
is compact on
\mathcal{H}
; this last condition follows from the fact that
\mathcal{D}\subseteq H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Then, by the analytic Fredholm theorem (see
[
DyZw19
, Theorem 4.4]
), we have the following. In
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0
, the resolvent
(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}
is meromorphic
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D}
;
\lambda
is a pole of
(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}
, if and only if
\lambda^{2}<0
is an eigenvalue of
H
. Furthermore, for
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
with
\chi=1
near
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
, the cutoff resolvent
\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi
continues meromorphically
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D}
from
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0
to
\mathbb{C}
. The poles of the continuation are known as its
resonances
.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Throughout the proof, we use
C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})
to denote a positive constant which may depend on
\|V\|
and
\lambda_{0}
, and whose value may change from line to line, but is always independent of
\lambda
.

We first show (
1.12
) for
k=0
,
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0
, and
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
. In this case, let us expand

(6.2)

\begin{split}\chi(H&-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\\
&=\chi(\partial^{2}_{x}+V-(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{2}+(\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)^{2}-i2\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda\cdot\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)^{-1}\chi\\
&=(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-2}\chi((\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-2}\partial^{2}_{x}+(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-2}V-1+(\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)^{2}(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-2}-i2(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-1}\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)^{-1}\chi.\end{split}

If
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\geq|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|/2
, then by the spectral theorem for self-adjoint operators,

(6.3)

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-2}\leq\lambda_{0}^{-1}|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-1},\qquad\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\geq|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|/2,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}.

If
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda<|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|/2
, we apply (
1.8
) to (
6.2
) (the notational correspondence is
\delta=1
,
E=1-(\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda)^{2}(\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda)^{-2}\geq 3/4
,
\varepsilon=2|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-1}\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\in(0,1]
,
h=|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-1}
, and

). Therefore,

(6.4)

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|^{-1},\qquad\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda>0,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}.

Next, we adapt the proof of
[
BGT04
, Proposition 2.5]
to show

(6.5)

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to H^{1}}\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0}),\qquad 0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}.

We employ the notation,

(6.6)

(H-\lambda^{2})u=\chi f,\qquad 0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0},\,f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\,u\in\mathcal{D},

and make use of additional cutoffs

(6.7)

\chi_{1},\,\chi_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,1]),\quad\chi_{1}=1\text{ on }\mathop{\rm supp}\chi,\quad\chi_{2}=1\text{ on }\mathop{\rm supp}\chi_{1}.

Observe

\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{H^{1}}\leq\|\chi u\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\chi u)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\|\chi_{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\chi_{1}u^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}),

where here and below,
C
is a positive constant, which may depend on
\|V\|
and the derivatives of the cutoffs, and which may change between lines, but stays independent of
\lambda
. So, by (
6.4
) it suffices to show

(6.8)

\|\chi_{1}u^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\big((|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{2}\|\chi_{2}u\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\chi_{2}f\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\big).

Multiplying (
6.6
) by
\chi^{2}_{1}\overline{u}
and integrating gives

\int\chi_{1}^{2}\chi f\overline{u}dx=\int\chi_{1}^{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx+2\int\chi_{1}^{\prime}\overline{u}\chi_{1}u^{\prime}dx+\int\chi^{2}_{1}|u|^{2}V-\lambda^{2}\int\chi^{2}_{1}|u|^{2}dx,

where (
3.4
) was used, and consequently

(6.9)

\begin{split}\int\chi_{1}^{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx&\leq\int|\chi_{1}^{2}\chi f\overline{u}|dx+2\int|\chi_{1}^{\prime}u^{\prime}\chi_{1}\overline{u}|dx+\|V\|\|\chi_{1}u\|_{L^{2}}\|(\chi_{1}u)^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{2}\int\chi^{2}_{1}|u|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C\big(\|\chi_{2}f\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{2}\|\chi_{2}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\int\chi_{1}^{2}|u^{\prime}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Absorbing the last term on the right side into the left side confirms (
6.8
).

With (
6.4
) and (
6.5
), for
0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
, and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\begin{split}\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{\mathcal{D}}&\leq\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+\|H\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+\|[-\partial^{2}_{x},\chi]\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|\chi((H-\lambda^{2})+\lambda^{2})(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&+\|[-\partial^{2}_{x},\chi]\chi_{1}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}+(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)^{2}\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi f\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})(|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|+1)\|f\|_{L^{2}}.\end{split}

This implies (
1.13
) for
0<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\leq 1,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
, and that continued resolvent

has no poles in

(since
\lambda_{0}>0
is arbitrary).

Now, we turn to showing (
1.13
) in strips in the lower half plane. For this, we use a resolvent identity argument due to Vodev
[
Vo14
, Theorem 1.5]
, adapted to the non-semiclassical case. It yields holomorphicity of
\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi:L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D}
in
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0},\,-\varepsilon_{0}<\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda<0
(
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small), with bounds in these strips of the form (
1.12
) and (
1.13
).

Fix
\tilde{\chi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\tilde{\chi}=1
near
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
and
\chi=1
near
\mathop{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}
. We are going to develop several resolvent identities, and let us work initially with
\lambda,\mu
such that
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda,\ \mathop{\rm Im}\mu>0
,
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\mu|\geq\lambda_{0}
(before sending these parameters into the the lower half plane). By the first resolvent identity,

\begin{split}(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}&=(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\\
&=(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\tilde{\chi}(2-\tilde{\chi})(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\\
&+(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(1-\tilde{\chi})^{2}(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}.\end{split}

As operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,

\begin{split}(1-\tilde{\chi})&(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})-(H-\lambda^{2})(1-\tilde{\chi})=[-\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}]\implies\\
&(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(1-\tilde{\chi})-(1-\tilde{\chi})(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}=(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}[-\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}](-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1},\end{split}

while as operators on
\mathcal{D}
,

\begin{split}(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})&(1-\tilde{\chi})-(1-\tilde{\chi})(H-\mu^{2})=[\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}]\implies\\
&(1-\tilde{\chi})(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}-(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}(1-\tilde{\chi})=(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}[\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}](H-\mu^{2})^{-1}.\end{split}

Using
\chi=1
on
\mathop{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}
and the three previous calculations,

(6.10)

\begin{split}\chi&(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi-\chi(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi=(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\tilde{\chi}(2-\tilde{\chi})(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi\\
&+(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})\chi((1-\tilde{\chi})(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}+(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}[-\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}](-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1})\\
&\cdot((-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}(1-\tilde{\chi})+(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}[\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}](H-\mu^{2})^{-1})\chi\\
&=(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\tilde{\chi}(2-\tilde{\chi})\chi(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi\\
&+(1-\tilde{\chi}-\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi[\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}])\\
&\cdot(\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi-\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi)\\
&\cdot(1-\tilde{\chi}+[\partial^{2}_{x},\tilde{\chi}]\chi(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi).\end{split}

To get the equality sign in line four of (
6.10
), we expanded the terms appearing in lines two and three, and repeatedly applied the first resolvent identity to
(\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2})(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}

Before proceeding to use (

) to estimate
\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}
in the lower half plane, we quote a well known estimate for the difference of continued free resolvents (see

[

(6.11)

\begin{gathered}\|\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi-\chi(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{k_{1}}\to H^{k_{2}}}\leq C(\lambda_{0})|\lambda-\mu|\sup_{\lambda^{\prime}\in\Gamma_{\lambda,\mu}}|\lambda^{\prime}|^{k_{2}-k_{1}-1},\\
k_{1}\in\{0,1\},\quad k_{2}\in\{0,1,2\},\quad|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|,\,|\mathop{\rm Re}\mu|\geq\lambda_{0},\quad\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda,\mathop{\rm Im}\mu\geq-1,\end{gathered}

where

denotes the line segment connecting
\lambda
and
\mu
.

The identity (
6.10
) continues to hold after meromorphically continuing both sides (

) to
\lambda,\,\mu\in\mathbb{C}
. Now, fix

with

; Assume

in the lower half plane is not a pole of the continued cutoff resolvent, and obeys

to be chosen. Then
\|\chi(H-\mu)^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to H^{k}}\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})|\mu|^{k-1}
,

,
\|\chi(H-\mu)^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})|\mu|
, (
6.10

) imply

(6.12)

\begin{split}\|\chi&(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\leq C(\|V\|,\lambda_{0})\Big(|\mu|+|\lambda^{2}-\mu^{2}||\mu|^{-1}\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\\
&+\|(1-\tilde{\chi})\chi((-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1})\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\\
&+\|\chi((-h^{2}\partial^{2}_{x}-\lambda^{2})^{-1}-(-\partial^{2}_{x}-\mu^{2})^{-1})\chi\|_{L^{2}\to H^{1}}\|\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{L^{2}\to\mathcal{D}}\\

\end{split}

Fixing

Starting from (

6.11
) and
\|\mu^{2}\chi(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{1}\to L^{2}},\,\|\mu\chi(H-\mu^{2})^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{1}\to H^{1}}\leq C
(
\mu\in\mathbb{R},|\mu|\geq\lambda_{0}
).

Finally, to show (
6.16
), we proceed as in the proof of
[
DyZw19
, Theorem 2.7]
. We omit the details, but take care to note that this argument does require that for each
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
, there is a unique solution
f
to
Hf=0
satisfying
f(x_{0})=a
and
f^{\prime}(x_{0})=b
; Even though
V
is only a measure in our setting, such well-posedness still holds for the initial value problem, see
[
EcTe13
, Theorem 3.1]
. (In general it is not necessary to prescribe the initial conditions outside the support of the measure, but this is sufficient for our purpose). The result is that near
\lambda=0
,

\chi(H-\lambda^{2})^{-1}\chi w_{1}=\frac{i}{\lambda}\langle\chi u_{0},w_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}\chi u_{0}+A(\lambda)w_{1},\qquad w_{1}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),

where
A(\lambda):L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathcal{D}
is holomorphic near zero, and for some
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}])
with
Hu_{0}=0
in the sense of distributions. Hence we have (
6.16
).

∎

7.
wave decay

In this Section, we combine Corollary
6.1
with an argument similar to those appearing in
[
Vo99
, Section 3]
and
[
DaSh23
, Section 4]
. We establish exponential local energy decay, modulo negative eigenvalues and a possible zero resonance, for solutions of the wave equation (
1.14
).

First, we represent the solution to (
1.14
) via the spectral theorem of for self-adjoint operators. Additionally, we use that the proof of Lemma
3.1
shows the form domain of
(H,\mathcal{D})
(i.e., the domain of
|H|^{1/2}
) is
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Thus, given initial conditions
w_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
,
w_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the unique function
w\in C^{2}((0,\infty),\mathcal{H})
with
w(0)=w_{0}
,
\partial_{t}w(0)=w_{1}
,
w(t)\in\mathcal{D}(H)
for all
t>0
, and
\partial^{2}_{t}w(t)+Hw(t)=0
, is

(7.1)

\begin{gathered}w(t)=w(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(\cdot,t)+\mathbf{1}_{<0}(H)w(\cdot,t),\\
\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)\big(\cos(t|H|^{1/2})w_{0}+\frac{\sin(t|H|^{1/2})}{|H|^{1/2}}w_{1}\big),\\
\mathbf{1}_{<0}(H)w(\cdot,t)=\mathbf{1}_{<0}(H)\big(\cos(it|H|^{1/2})w_{0}+\frac{\sin(it|H|^{1/2})}{i|H|^{1/2}}w_{1}\big).\\
\end{gathered}

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose
w_{0}\in\mathcal{D},\,w_{1}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathop{\rm supp}w_{0},\,\mathop{\rm supp}w_{1}\subseteq(-R,R)
for some
R>0
. Let
w(t)
be given by (
7.1
). For any
R_{1}>0
, there exist
C,c>0
so that

(7.2)

\begin{gathered}\|\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(\cdot,t)-w_{\infty}(x)\|_{H^{1}(-R_{1},R_{1})}+\|\partial_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(-R_{1},R_{1})}\\
\leq Ce^{-ct}(\|w_{0}\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}+\|w_{1}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}),\qquad t>0.\end{gathered}

Furthermore, if
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
is identically one near
[-R_{1},R_{1}]\cup[-R_{0},R_{0}]\cup[-R,R]
, then the function
w_{\infty}(x)
may be written as

(7.3)

w_{\infty}(x)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\chi(x)u_{0}(x)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi u_{0}w_{1},

for some real valued
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}])
with
Hu_{0}=0
in the sense of distributions (and if the continued operator (
6.14
) does not have a pole at
\lambda=0
, we may take
u_{0}\equiv 0
.)

Proof.

Choose
\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
such that
\chi=1
near
[-R_{1},R_{1}]\cup[-R,R]\cup[R_{0},R_{0}]
(where as before
\mathop{\rm supp}V\subseteq[-R_{0},R_{0}]
). From Corollary
6.1
, for any
\lambda_{0}>0
, there exist
C
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that

(7.4)

\|\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi f\|\leq C\|f\|,

whenever
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\geq\lambda_{0}
and
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, where here and for the rest of this Section, all norms are
H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
unless otherwise specified.

We have

\begin{gathered}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)\big(\cos(t|H|^{1/2})w_{0}+\sin(t|H|^{1/2})|H|^{-1/2}w_{1}\big),\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t)=\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)\big(-\sin(t|H|^{1/2})|H|^{1/2}w_{0}+\cos(t|H|^{1/2})w_{1}\big),\\
\partial_{t}^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t)=-H\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t).\end{gathered}

Consequently, after defining

f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}w_{0}\\
w_{1}\end{array}\right),\qquad U(t)f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t)\\
\partial_{t}\mathbf{1}_{\geq 0}(H)w(t)\end{array}\right),

we have

(7.5)

\|U(t)f\|\leq C(1+|t|)\|f\|,\qquad\partial_{t}U(t)f=iGU(t)f,\qquad U(t)U(s)f=U(t+s)f,

for all real
t
and
s
, and for some
C>0
independent of
t
and
f
.

Take
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
which is
0
near
(-\infty,1]
and
1
near
[2,\infty)
and put

W(t)f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\varphi(t)U(t)f=\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=\varepsilon}e^{-it\lambda}\check{W}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad\check{W}(\lambda)\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{is\lambda}W(s)fds,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

We compute
\partial_{t}W(t)f=\varphi^{\prime}(t)U(t)f+iGW(t)f
, and therefore find

(7.6)

W(t)f=\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=\varepsilon}e^{-it\lambda}(G+\lambda)^{-1}(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)\,d\lambda,\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Since
w_{0}
,
w_{1}
and
V
have compact support, finite propagation speeds holds for the solution (
7.1
). Therefore, increasing
R>0
if necessary, we have that,
x\mapsto U(t)f
is supported in
(-R,R)
for all
t\in[0,2]
. By continuity of integration, the same is true of
x\mapsto(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)
for every
\lambda
. Hence
\lambda\mapsto(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)
is entire and rapidly decaying as
|\mathop{\rm Re}\lambda|\to\infty
with
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|
remaining bounded and further
(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)=\chi(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)
.

By (
7.4
), there exists
\varepsilon>0
small enough so that, within the strip
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|<2\varepsilon
, either
\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi
has no poles, or just a pole at
\lambda=0
. Deforming the contour in (
7.6
), by the residue theorem, we find

\begin{split}\chi W&(t)f\\
&=-2\pi i\mathop{\rm Res}{}_{\lambda=0}(e^{-it\lambda}\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda))+\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=-\varepsilon}e^{-it\lambda}\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)d\lambda.\\
&=\lim_{\lambda\to 0}\lambda\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{\prime}(s)U(s)f\,ds+\int_{\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda=-\varepsilon}e^{-it\lambda}\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda)d\lambda.\end{split}

To simplify this, use (
6.16
) and put

W_{1}(t)f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\lambda}\chi(G+\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\,d\lambda,

to obtain

\chi W(t)f=\begin{pmatrix}\chi u_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{2}\chi(x)u_{0}(x)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\partial_{s}w(s,x)dsdx\\
0\end{pmatrix}+e^{-\varepsilon t}W_{1}(t)f.

To simplify the first term, we integrate by parts in
s
, using
\varphi^{\prime}=-(1-\varphi)^{\prime}
, to obtain

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{2}\chi(x)u_{0}(x)\varphi^{\prime}(s)\partial_{s}w(s,x)\,ds\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi u_{0}w_{1}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{2}\chi(x)(1-\varphi(s))\partial_{s}^{2}w(s,x)\,ds\,dx.

Now observe that
\partial_{s}^{2}w=-Hw
,
\chi u_{0}\in\mathcal{D}
, so

\langle\chi u_{0},Hw(s)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle H\chi u_{0},w(s)\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle([H,\chi]+H)u_{0},w(s)\rangle_{L^{2}}=0,\qquad\text{ for }s\in[0,2],

the last equality following from
\chi=1
near
[-R,R]
and
\mathop{\rm supp}w(s)\subseteq(-R,R)
for
s\in[0,2]
). Thus

\chi W(t)f=\begin{pmatrix}\langle\chi u_{0},w_{1}\rangle_{L^{2}}\chi u_{0}\\
0\end{pmatrix}+e^{-\varepsilon t}W_{1}(t)f.

It now suffices to show that

\|W_{1}(t)f\|\leq Ce^{\varepsilon t/2}\|f\|.

To prove this, we first use Plancherel’s theorem, along with the fact that by (
7.5
), the operator norm
\|U(t)\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
is uniformly bounded for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, as well as the fact that by Corollary
6.1
, the operator norm
\|\chi(G+\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi\|_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\oplus L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
is uniformly bounded for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, to obtain

(7.7)

\begin{split}\int\|W_{1}(t)f\|^{2}\,dt&=C\int\|\chi(G+\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\chi(\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\|^{2}\,d\lambda\\
&\leq C_{\varepsilon}\int\|(\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\|^{2}\,d\lambda\\
&=C_{\varepsilon}\int e^{2\varepsilon t}\|\varphi^{\prime}(t)U(t)f\|^{2}\,dt\leq C_{\varepsilon}\|f\|^{2}.\end{split}

Next, let
\tilde{\chi}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R};[0,1])
with
\tilde{\chi}=1
on
\mathop{\rm supp}\chi
. Observe that

(7.8)

G\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi=[G,\chi]\tilde{\chi}(G+\lambda+i\varepsilon)^{-1}\tilde{\chi}\chi-\lambda\chi(G+\lambda)^{-1}\chi+\chi^{2}

holds initially for
\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda\gg 1
by (
6.13
), and continues meromorphically to
\mathbb{C}
by (
6.14
). In particular, decreasing
\varepsilon
if necessary, (
6.15
) implies that (
7.8
) holds for everywhere in
|\mathop{\rm Im}\lambda|<2\varepsilon
, except possibly at
\lambda=0
. Therefore, setting,

\widetilde{W}_{1}(t)f\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{-it\lambda}\tilde{\chi}(G+\lambda-i\varepsilon)^{-1}\tilde{\chi}(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\,d\lambda,

we have

(\partial_{t}-iG)W_{1}(t)f=-i[G,\chi]\widetilde{W}_{1}(t)f+\varepsilon W_{1}(t)f-i\int e^{-it\lambda}(i\varphi^{\prime}Uf)\check{~}(\lambda-i\varepsilon)\,d\lambda=\mathrel{\vbox{\par\hbox{\scriptsize.}\hbox{\scriptsize.}}}W_{2}(t)f.

Integrating both sides of

\begin{split}\partial_{s}(U(t-s)W_{1}(s)f)&=-iGU(t-s)W_{1}(s)f+U(t-s)\big(iGW_{1}(s)f+W_{2}(s)f\big)\\
&=U(t-s)W_{2}(s)f\end{split}

from
s=0
to
s=t
gives

W_{1}(t)f=U(t)W_{1}(0)f+U(t)\int_{0}^{t}U(-s)W_{2}(s)f\,ds.

Thus

\begin{split}\|W_{1}(t)f\|&\leq C(1+t)\big(\|f\|+\int_{0}^{t}(1+s)\|W_{2}(s)f\|ds\big)\\
&\leq C(1+t)\big(\|f\|+\big(\frac{t^{3}}{3}+t^{2}+t\big)^{1/2}\Big(\int_{0}^{t}\|W_{2}(s)f\|^{2}ds\Big)^{1/2}\big).\end{split}

Now check that, since
\|[G,\chi]\widetilde{W}_{1}(t)f\|\leq C\|\widetilde{W}_{1}(t)f\|
, calculating as in (
7.7
), we obtain

\int\|W_{2}(s)f\|^{2}\,ds\leq C\|f\|^{2}
, and hence

\|W_{1}(t)f\|\leq C(1+t^{5/2})\|f\|

as desired.

∎

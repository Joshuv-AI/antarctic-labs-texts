Khavinson conjecture for hyperbolic harmonic functions on the unit ball
Adel Khalfallah, Fathi Haggui, Miodrag Mateljević

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the Khavinson conjecture for hyperbolic harmonic functions on the unit ball. This conjecture was partially solved in [ 5 ]



1.
introduction

For
\displaystyle n\geq 2
, let
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
denote the
\displaystyle n
-dimensional Euclidean space. We use

\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
and
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
to denote the unit ball and the unit sphere in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, respectively.

A mapping
\displaystyle u\in\mathcal{C}^{2}({\mathbb{B}}^{n},{\mathbb{R}})
is said to be hyperbolic harmonic if

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}u=0,

where
\displaystyle\Delta_{h}
is the hyperbolic Laplacian operator defined by

\Delta_{h}u(x)=(1-|x|^{2})^{2}\Delta u+2(n-2)(1-|x|^{2})\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}}(x),

here
\displaystyle\Delta
denotes the Laplacian on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.

Clearly for
\displaystyle n=2
, hyperbolic harmonic and harmonic functions coincide.

If
\displaystyle\phi\in L^{1}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},\mathbb{R})
, we define the invariant Poisson integral of
\displaystyle\phi
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{B}^{n}

\mathcal{P}_{h}[\phi](x)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathcal{P}_{h}(x,\zeta)\phi(\zeta)d\,\sigma(\zeta),

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h}(x,\zeta)=\left(\frac{1-|x|^{2}}{|x-\zeta|^{2}}\right)^{n-1}

is the Poisson kernel with respective to
\displaystyle\Delta_{h}
satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\mathcal{P}_{h}(x,\zeta)\,d\sigma(\zeta)=1.

For more information about hyperbolic harmonic functions we refer to Stoll

[
21
]
and Burgeth
[
3
,
4
]
.

2.
Khavinson Problem

Let
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
and let
\displaystyle q
be its conjugate. Assume that
\displaystyle u=\mathcal{P}_{h}[\phi]
, where
\displaystyle\phi\in L^{p}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},{\mathbb{R}})
. For
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\displaystyle\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
,
let
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x)
and
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)
denote the optimal numbers such that

|\nabla u(x)|\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x)\|\phi\|_{p},

and

|\langle\nabla u(x),\ell\rangle|\leq{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)\|\phi\|_{p}.

Since
\displaystyle\displaystyle|\nabla u(x)|=\sup_{\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}|\langle\nabla u(x),\ell\rangle|
, clearly we obtain

{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x)=\sup_{\ell\ \in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell).

We prove the Khavinson conjecture for hyperbolic harmonic functions, partially solved in
[
5
]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
,
\displaystyle n\geq 3
and
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x)=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;n_{x})&if $\displaystyle 1<p<n$,\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;t_{x})&if $\displaystyle p>n$,\end{cases}

where
\displaystyle n_{x}=\frac{x}{|x|}
, and
\displaystyle t_{x}
is any unit vector such that
\displaystyle\langle t_{x},x\rangle=0
.

Moreover, if
\displaystyle p=n
or
\displaystyle p=\infty
, then
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x,\ell)
does not depend on
\displaystyle\ell
.

In the planar case, i.e.,
\displaystyle n=2
, this conjecture was solved by Kalaj and Marković, see
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Khavinson
[
12
]
obtained a sharp pointwise estimate for the radial derivative of bounded harmonic functions on the unit ball of

\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and conjectured that the same estimate holds for the norm of the gradient of bounded harmonic functions.

For harmonic functions this conjecture was formulated by Kresin and Maz’ya in
[
14
]
and in
[
15
]
considered the half-space analogue of the above conjecture. See
[
16
, Chapter 6]
for
various Khavinson-type extremal problems for harmonic functions.

Kalaj
[
8
]
showed that the conjecture for
\displaystyle n=4
and Melentijević
[
19
]
confirmed the conjecture in
\displaystyle{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. Marković
[
18
]
solved the Khavinson problem for points near the boundary of the unit ball. The general conjecture was recently proved by Liu
[
17
]
.

By computing the gradient of the Poisson-Szegö kernel and using the Möbius transformation as a substitution, we obtain the following integral representation

Lemma 1
.

[
5
]

For any
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
,
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
and
\displaystyle l\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)=\frac{2(n-1)}{(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{n(q-1)+1}{q}}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta-x|^{2(n-1)(q-1)}|\langle\eta,l\rangle|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(2.1)

Moreover, one can easily deduce the following

Lemma 2
.

[
5
]

For any
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
,
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{B}^{n}
,
\displaystyle l\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
and unitary transformation
\displaystyle A
in
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)={\mathcal{C}}_{p}(Ax;A\ell).

(2.2)

For
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
and
\displaystyle\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, let

{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell)=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta-x|^{2(n-1)(q-1)}|\langle\eta,l\rangle|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta).

(2.3)

So in view of (
2.1
), we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)=\frac{2(n-1)}{(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{n(q-1)+1}{q}}}\left({\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell)\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

(2.4)

Our main result is the following theorem solving the Khavinson conjecture for hyperbolic harmonic functions.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
,
\displaystyle n\geq 3
and
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

\max_{\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;n_{x})&if $\displaystyle 1<p<n$,\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;t_{x})&if $\displaystyle p>n$.\end{cases}

\min_{\ell\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)=\begin{cases}{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;t_{x})&if $\displaystyle 1<p<n$,\\
{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;n_{x})&if $\displaystyle p>n$.\end{cases}

If
\displaystyle p=n
or
\displaystyle p=\infty
, then
\displaystyle\ell\mapsto{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x;\ell)
is constant.

One of our main tools is the method of slice integration on spheres.

Theorem A.

[
2
, Theorem A.5]

Let
\displaystyle f
be a Borel measurable, integrable function on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. If
\displaystyle 1\leq k<n
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}fd\sigma_{n}=\frac{k}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{k})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{n-k}}(1-|x|^{2})^{\frac{k-2}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{k-1}}f(x,\sqrt{1-|x|^{2}}\zeta)d\sigma_{k}(\zeta)\,dV_{n-k}(x),

where
\displaystyle V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})
denotes the volume of the ball, which is given by

V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})=\frac{\pi^{\frac{n}{2}}}{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}.

(2.5)

and
\displaystyle\sigma_{n}
denotes the normalized measure on the sphere
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
.

We will consider two special cases for
\displaystyle k=n-1
and
\displaystyle k=n-2
. The corresponding formulas are useful when the integrand function
\displaystyle f
depends only on one or two variables.

Corollary B.

Let
\displaystyle\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n})\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
and
\displaystyle f(\eta)
be a Borel measurable, integrable function on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
.

(1)

If
\displaystyle n\geq 2
and
\displaystyle f(\eta)
depends only on the first variable
\displaystyle\eta_{1}
, then

\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}f(\eta_{1})\,d\sigma_{n}(\eta)=\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}f(t)\,dt.

(2.6)

(2)

If
\displaystyle n\geq 3
and
\displaystyle f(\eta)
depends only on the first two variables
\displaystyle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}f(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\,d\sigma_{n}(\eta)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{2}}(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\,f(z)\,dA(z).

(2.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\pi}\int_{{\mathbb{B}}^{2}}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\,f(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)r\,drd\theta,

(2.8)

where
\displaystyle dA(z)
denotes the Lebesgue measure on the unit disc
\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}}^{2}
.

Using the invariance of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
by unitary transformations, see Lemma
2
, we may assume that:

x=|x|e_{1}\mbox{ and }\ell=\ell_{\gamma}=\cos\gamma e_{1}+\sin\gamma e_{2},\mbox{ with }\gamma\in[0,\pi].

Let
\displaystyle\eta=(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{n})\in{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
. Then

|\eta-x|^{2}=1+|x|^{2}-2|x|\eta_{1},

and

\langle\eta,\ell_{\gamma}\rangle=\eta_{1}\cos\gamma+\eta_{2}\sin\gamma.

For
\displaystyle r,\rho\in(0,1)
, introduce the notation

{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,\rho;\gamma)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(1+\rho^{2}-2\rho r\cos\theta\right)^{(n-1)(q-1)}\left|\cos(\theta-\gamma)\right|^{q}d\theta.

(2.9)

Lemma 3
.

Let
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
,
\displaystyle 1<p<\infty
and
\displaystyle q
its conjugate. Then

{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell)={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(|x|e_{1};\ell_{\gamma})=\frac{n-2}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}r^{q+1}{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,|x|;\gamma)\,dr.

Proof.

Using the invariance of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{p}
by unitary transformations, we may assume that

\displaystyle x=|x|e_{1}\mbox{ and }\ell=\ell_{\gamma}=\cos\gamma e_{1}+\sin\gamma e_{2},\mbox{ with }\gamma\in[0,\pi].

{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(|x|e_{1};\ell_{\gamma})=\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}\left(1+|x|^{2}-2|x|\eta_{1}\right)^{(n-1)(q-1)}\left|\eta_{1}\cos\gamma+\eta_{2}\sin\gamma\right|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta).

(2.10)

As the integrand function depends only on
\displaystyle\eta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\eta_{2}
, the method of slice integration on spheres reduces an integral on the sphere to some integral on the unit disc
\displaystyle{\mathbb{B}}^{2}
. Using polar coordinates on the unit disc, let us denote
\displaystyle\eta_{1}=r\cos\theta
and
\displaystyle\eta_{2}=r\sin\theta
. Thus

\langle\eta,\ell_{\gamma}\rangle=\eta_{1}\cos\gamma+\eta_{2}\sin\gamma=r\cos(\theta-\gamma).

∎

To find the extreme values of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,\rho;\gamma)
, we will consider the following more general integral

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}(\gamma)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(A-B\cos\theta)^{a}\,|\cos(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}\,d\theta.

(2.11)

The function
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
has the following properties

(1)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is
\displaystyle\pi
- periodic.

(2)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is an even function.

Thus, we will consider the behaviour of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
only on
\displaystyle[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
and we show that
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is a monotonic on
\displaystyle[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
, thus the extreme values are reached at
\displaystyle\gamma=0
and
\displaystyle\gamma=\frac{\pi}{2}
. A special case was considered in
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
\displaystyle A,B
be positive numbers such that
\displaystyle 0<B<A
, and
\displaystyle a
,
\displaystyle b
are real numbers such that
\displaystyle b>0
.

(1)

If
\displaystyle a=0
or
\displaystyle a=1
, then
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\gamma)
is constant.

(2)

If
\displaystyle a\in(0,1)
, then
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\gamma)
is increasing on
\displaystyle[0,\pi/2]
. Thus

\max_{\gamma\in[0,\pi/2]}{\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\gamma)={\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\pi/2).

(3)

If
\displaystyle a>1
, then
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\gamma)
is decreasing on
\displaystyle[0,\pi/2]
. Thus

\max_{\gamma\in[0,\pi/2]}{\mathcal{I}}(a,b;\gamma)={\mathcal{I}}(a,b;0).

Proof.

As the integrand function is
\displaystyle 2\pi
periodic with respect to
\displaystyle\theta
, we deduce that

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}(\gamma)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}(A-B\cos(\theta+\gamma))^{a}\,|\cos\theta|^{b}\,d\theta.

Therefore the mapping is differentiable and

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)=aB\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin(\theta+\gamma)(A-B\cos(\theta+\gamma))^{a-1}\,|\cos\theta|^{b}\,d\theta.

Again, using the
\displaystyle 2\pi
-periodicity of the integrand, we obtain

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)=aB\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\sin\theta(A-B\cos\theta)^{a-1}\,|\cos(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}\,d\theta.

Next, we split the integral into two parts from
\displaystyle 0
to
\displaystyle\pi
and from
\displaystyle\pi
to
\displaystyle 2\pi
. Using a substitution, we obtain

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)=aB\int_{0}^{\pi}\sin\theta\left[(A-B\cos\theta)^{a-1}-(A+B\cos\theta)^{a-1}\right]\,|\cos(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}\,d\theta.

By considering the substitution
\displaystyle u=\theta-\pi/2
, we get

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)=aB\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\left[(A+B\sin\theta)^{a-1}-(A-B\sin\theta)^{a-1}\right]\,|\sin(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}\,d\theta.

Next, we split the integral into two parts from
\displaystyle 0
to
\displaystyle\pi/2
and from
\displaystyle\pi/2
to
\displaystyle\pi
. Using the substitution
\displaystyle u=\pi-\theta
, we obtain

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)=aB\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos\theta\left[(A+B\sin\theta)^{a-1}-(A-B\sin\theta)^{a-1}\right]\,\left[|\sin(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}-|\sin(\theta+\gamma)|^{b}\right]d\theta.

Clearly, if
\displaystyle a=0
or
\displaystyle a=1
, then
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{z,b}^{\prime}=0
and the function
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is constant and

{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(0)={\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\frac{\pi}{2})=0.

For
\displaystyle\gamma,\theta\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
and
\displaystyle b>0
, then

|\sin(\theta-\gamma)|^{b}-|\sin(\theta+\gamma)|^{b}<0.

Indeed,
\displaystyle\sin(\theta-\gamma)-\sin(\theta+\gamma)=-2\cos(\theta)\sin(\gamma)<0
.

Therefore,

(1)

if
\displaystyle a\in(0,1)
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, then
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)>0
and the mapping
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is strictly increasing on
\displaystyle[0,\pi/2]
;

(2)

if
\displaystyle a\in(1,\infty)
and
\displaystyle\gamma\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
, then
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}^{\prime}(\gamma)<0
and the mapping
\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}_{a,b}
is strictly decreasing on
\displaystyle[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
.

∎

As a consequence, we get

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
\displaystyle r,\rho\in(0,1)
, and
\displaystyle q\geq 1
. Then the mapping
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,\rho;\gamma)
on
\displaystyle[0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
is

(1)

constant for
\displaystyle q=1
or
\displaystyle q=\frac{n}{n-1}
;

(2)

strictly increasing for
\displaystyle 1<q<\frac{n}{n-1}
;

(3)

strictly decreasing for
\displaystyle q>\frac{n}{n-1}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle p\in(1,\infty]
and
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
. Then

(1)

If
\displaystyle p=n
or
\displaystyle p=\infty
, then
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{\gamma})
is constant.

(2)

If
\displaystyle p\in(1,n)
, then

\max_{\gamma\in[0,\pi/2]}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{\gamma})={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{0})={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;n_{x}).

(3)

If
\displaystyle p\in(n,\infty)
, then

\max_{\gamma\in[0,\pi/2]}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{\gamma})={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{\pi/2})={\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;t_{x}).

Thus we obtain our main theorem.

3.
Computation of
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{p}(x)

We will start with two particular cases
\displaystyle p=n
or
\displaystyle p=\infty
.

3.1.
Case
\displaystyle p=\infty

In this case, the mapping
\displaystyle\gamma\mapsto{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x,\ell_{\gamma})
is constant, hence
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x,\ell_{\gamma})={\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x,\ell_{0})
, and

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x,\ell_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}}|\eta_{1}|\,d\sigma(\eta)

(3.1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}|t|\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2(n-1)}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{0}^{1}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}t\,dt.

Using the substitution
\displaystyle u=1-t^{2}
, we deduce

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x,\ell_{0})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{(n-1)}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{0}^{1}u^{\frac{n-3}{2}}\,du

(3.2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}.

By (
2.4
), we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}_{\infty}(x;\ell)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2(n-1)}{1-|x|^{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{\infty}(x;\ell)

(3.3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{4(n-1)}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{2(n-1)\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\frac{n}{2}+1})}\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}.

Hence, if
\displaystyle u=\mathcal{P}_{h}[\phi]
, where
\displaystyle\phi\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{S}^{n-1}},{\mathbb{R}})
, then

|\nabla u(x)|\leq\frac{2(n-1)\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\Gamma({\frac{n}{2}+1})}\frac{1}{1-|x|^{2}}\|\phi\|_{\infty}.\\

(3.4)

We should mention that the sharp inequality (
3.4
) can also be obtained as follows. According to
[
11
, Corollary 1.2]
, see also
[
3
]
, if
\displaystyle u
is a bounded hyperbolic harmonic function, with
\displaystyle|u|<1
, then

|\nabla u(0)|\leq\frac{4(n-1)}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}

(3.5)

Let
\displaystyle x\in{\mathbb{B}}^{n}
and
\displaystyle\varphi_{x}
be the Möbius transformation such that
\displaystyle\varphi_{x}(0)=x
, see
[
21
, p. 7 (2.1.4)]
. By the Möbius invariance of
\displaystyle\Delta_{h}
, the function
\displaystyle u\circ\varphi_{x}
is also bounded hyperbolic harmonic function with
\displaystyle\nabla(u\circ\varphi_{x})(0)=-(1-|x|^{2})\nabla u(x)
. Hence (
3.4
) follows by considering
\displaystyle u\circ\varphi_{x}
in (
3.5
).

3.2.
Case
\displaystyle p=n

The conjugate of
\displaystyle n
is
\displaystyle q=\frac{n}{n-1}
.

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,\rho;\gamma)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}\left(1+\rho^{2}-2\rho r\cos\theta\right)\left|\cos(\theta-\gamma)\right|^{q}d\theta.

(3.6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle(1+\rho^{2})\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}|\cos\theta|^{q}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 4(1+\rho^{2})\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{q}\theta\,d\theta.

Using

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(x;\ell_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}r^{q+1}{\mathcal{J}}_{q}(r,|x|;\gamma)\,dr

(3.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-r^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}r^{q+1}\,dr\right)\left(4(1+|x|^{2})\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{q}\theta\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{\pi}\left(\int_{0}^{1}(1-r)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}r^{\frac{q}{2}}\,dr\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\cos^{q}\theta\,d\theta\right)(1+|x|^{2}).

Recall some properties of the beta function. Let
\displaystyle a,b>0

{\mathrm{B}}(a,b)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{a-1}(1-t)^{b-1}\,dt=2\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin t)^{2a-1}(\cos t)^{2b-1}\,dt=\frac{\Gamma(a)\Gamma(b)}{\Gamma(a+b)}.

(3.8)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{n}(x;\ell_{\gamma})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\pi}{\mathrm{B}}(\frac{n}{2}-1,\frac{q}{2}+1){\mathrm{B}}(\frac{1+q}{2},\frac{1}{2})(1+|x|^{2})

(3.9)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{n-2}{2\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}-1)\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{n+q}{2})}(1+|x|^{2}).

3.3.
Case
\displaystyle 1<p<n

The following lemma is useful to compute
\displaystyle{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{0})
.

Lemma 5
.

For
\displaystyle a>-1
and
\displaystyle b>-1

(1)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt={\mathrm{B}}(\frac{a+1}{2},b+1).

(2)

If
\displaystyle|u|<1
and
\displaystyle\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-ut)^{-\alpha}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt={\mathrm{B}}(\frac{a+1}{2},b+1)\,\,{}_{3}F_{2}(\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{\alpha+1}{2},\frac{a+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{a+3}{2}+b;u^{2}).

Proof.

(1) Using the substitution
\displaystyle t=\sin\theta
and (
3.8
), we get

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}t^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{\pi/2}(\sin\theta)^{a}(\cos\theta)^{2b+1}d\theta

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathrm{B}}(\frac{a+1}{2},b+1).

(2) Since
\displaystyle|ut|\leq|u|<1
, for
\displaystyle|t|\leq 1
, we have

(1-ut)^{-\alpha}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{k}}{k!}u^{k}t^{k},

and this series converges uniformly in
\displaystyle[-1,1]
, thus

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}(1-ut)^{-\alpha}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(\alpha)_{k}}{k!}u^{k}t^{k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}\,dt

(3.10)

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}u^{2k}t^{2k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}\,dt

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}\,dt\right)u^{2k}.

Using Lemma
5
(1), we have

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt=\frac{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+1}{2})\Gamma(b+1)}{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+3}{2}+b)}.

Thus

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt\right)u^{2k}=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}\frac{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+1}{2})\Gamma(b+1)}{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+3}{2}+b)}u^{2k}.

Using

(\alpha)_{2k}=2^{2k}(\frac{\alpha}{2})_{k}(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})_{k},

(2k)!=\Gamma(2(k+1/2))=\frac{2^{2k}\Gamma(k+1/2)k!}{\sqrt{\pi}}=2^{2k}(1/2)_{k}k!,

and

(x)_{k}=\frac{\Gamma(x+k)}{\Gamma(x)},\mbox{ for }x\in{\mathbb{R}}\setminus{\mathbb{Z}}_{-},

we get

\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}=\frac{(\frac{\alpha}{2})_{k}(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})_{k}}{(\frac{1}{2})_{k}\,k!}.

(3.11)

Therefore

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\alpha)_{2k}}{(2k)!}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}t^{2k}|t|^{a}(1-t^{2})^{b}dt\right)u^{2k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\frac{\alpha}{2})_{k}(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})_{k}}{(1/2)_{k}k!}\frac{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+1}{2})\Gamma(b+1)}{\Gamma(k+\frac{a+3}{2}+b)}u^{2k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})\Gamma(b+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{a+3}{2}+b)}\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(\frac{\alpha}{2})_{k}(\frac{\alpha+1}{2})_{k}(\frac{a+1}{2})_{k}}{(1/2)_{k}(\frac{a+3}{2}+b)_{k}k!}u^{2k}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\displaystyle{\mathrm{B}}(\frac{a+1}{2},b+1)\,\,{}_{3}F_{2}(\frac{\alpha}{2},\frac{\alpha+1}{2},\frac{a+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{a+3}{2}+b;u^{2}).

∎

Recall that

{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{0})=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}|\eta-x|^{2(n-1)(q-1)}\left|\langle\eta,\ell\rangle\right|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta),

with
\displaystyle\ell_{0}=e_{1}=\frac{x}{|x|};\,x=|x|e_{1}
.

We have
\displaystyle|\eta-x|^{2}=1+|x|^{2}-2|x|\eta_{1}
and
\displaystyle\langle\eta,\ell_{0}\rangle=|\eta_{1}|.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha=(n-1)(1-q)
and
\displaystyle u=\frac{2|x|}{1+|x|^{2}}
.

\begin{array}[]{ccl}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{0})&=&\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}(1+|x|^{2}-2|x|\eta_{1})^{-\alpha}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta)\\
&=&(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(1-2\frac{|x|}{1+|x|^{2}}\eta_{1}\right)^{-\alpha}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta)\\
&=&(1+|x|^{2})^{-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(1-u\eta_{1}\right)^{-\alpha}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta).\end{array}

As we integrate a function which depends on one variable on
\displaystyle{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}
, then by the slice integration on spheres, we have

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(1-u\eta_{1}\right)^{-\alpha}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta)=\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-ut)^{-\alpha}|t|^{q}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt.

By Lemma
5
(2), it yields

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-ut)^{(n-1)(q-1)}|t|^{q}(1-t^{2})^{\frac{n-3}{2}}dt\\
=\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{q+n}{2})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)(1-q)}{2},\frac{(n-1)(1-q)+1}{2},\frac{q+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{q+n}{2};\frac{4|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}\right).\end{array}

Therefore

\begin{array}[]{l}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{n-1}}\left(1-u\eta_{1}\right)^{(n-1)(q-1)}|\eta_{1}|^{q}\,d\sigma(\eta)\\
=\frac{n-1}{n}\frac{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n-1})}{V({\mathbb{B}}^{n})}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n-1}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{q+n}{2})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)(1-q)}{2},\frac{(n-1)(1-q)+1}{2},\frac{q+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{q+n}{2};\frac{4|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}\right)\\
=\frac{2}{n\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2}+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{q+n}{2})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)(1-q)}{2},\frac{(n-1)(1-q)+1}{2},\frac{q+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{q+n}{2};\frac{4|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{q+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{n}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{q+n}{2})}\,{}_{3}F_{2}\left(\frac{(n-1)(1-q)}{2},\frac{(n-1)(1-q)+1}{2},\frac{q+1}{2};\frac{1}{2},\frac{q+n}{2};\frac{4|x|^{2}}{(1+|x|^{2})^{2}}\right).\end{array}

Finally,

\begin{array}[]{l}{\mathcal{K}}_{p}(x;\ell_{0})\\

1.
Introduction

1.1.

A system of vectors
\{x_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in a Banach space
X

is called a
Schauder basis
if every
x\in X
admits a unique series expansion

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}c_{n}x_{n}
where
\{c_{n}\}
are scalars.
It is well known that in this case there exist
biorthogonal continuous linear functionals
\{x_{n}^{*}\}

such that the coefficients of the series expansion are given by

c_{n}=x_{n}^{*}(x)
(see e.g.
[
You01
, Section 1.6]
).
If the series converges unconditionally
(i.e. if it converges for any rearrangement of its terms)
for every
x\in X
, then
\{x_{n}\}

is said to be an
unconditional
Schauder basis.

Given a function
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, we denote its translates by

(T_{\lambda}g)(x)=g(x-\lambda),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.1)

There is a long-standing open problem, asking
whether the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1<p<\infty
, admits
a Schauder basis formed by translates of a single function
(see
[
OZ92
]
,
[
OSSZ11
, Problem 4.4]
).
It is known that
unconditional
Schauder bases consisting of translates
do not exist in any of these spaces, see
[
OZ92
]
,

[
OSSZ11
]
,
[
FOSZ14
]
.

A sequence
\Lambda=\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of real numbers
is said to be
uniformly discrete
if

\inf_{n\neq m}|\lambda_{m}-\lambda_{n}|>0.

(1.2)

It was observed in
[
OZ92
, Theorem 1]

that the condition (
1.2
) is necessary for
a system of translates

\{T_{\lambda_{n}}g\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

to form a Schauder basis in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
It is also known
that in the space
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,
a system of uniformly discrete
translates cannot even be complete,
see
[
BOU06
]
.
Hence, no Schauder bases of translates
exist in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

1.2.

If
X
is a Banach space with dual space
X^{*}
, then a system

\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
in
X\times X^{*}
is called a

Schauder frame
(or a quasi-basis) if every
x\in X
has a series expansion

x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}x_{n}^{*}(x)x_{n}.

(1.3)

If the series (
1.3
)
converges unconditionally for every
x\in X
, then

\{(x_{n},x_{n}^{*})\}
is called an
unconditional
Schauder frame.
We note that the series expansion
(
1.3
) need not be unique and the coefficient functionals
\{x_{n}^{*}\}

need not be biorthogonal to
\{x_{n}\}
. Hence
Schauder frames form a wider class of representation systems than Schauder bases.

It was shown in
[
FOSZ14
]
that for every
p>2

there exists an unconditional Schauder frame
in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of translates, i.e. of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}
where
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,

\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
and

\{g_{n}^{*}\}
are continuous linear functionals on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
Moreover,
\{\lambda_{n}\}
may be chosen to be

an arbitrary unbounded sequence
, and
in particular, it may consist of integers, and
may increase arbitrarily fast.

To the contrary, we proved recently
[
LT25
]
that
if
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2
, then the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})

does not admit an unconditional Schauder frame
consisting of translates.

In
[
FPT21
, Section 4]
a construction was given
of Schauder frames (not unconditional)
of translates in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
.
In fact, it was proved
that whenever a system of translates
\{T_{\lambda}g\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda}

is complete in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, then there exists a Schauder frame

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

such that
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\Lambda
.
However the Schauder frames obtained by this construction
are
highly redundant
, as the sequence

\{\lambda_{n}\}
is composed
of countably many blocks of finite size,
such that each block gets repeated a higher and higher
number of times. The sequence

\{\lambda_{n}\}
thus “runs back and forth” through the set
\Lambda
.

1.3.

The following question was posed in
[
OSSZ11
, Problem 4.4]
:
does there exist a Schauder frame in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
formed by
a
uniformly discrete
sequence of translates?
The problem has remained open for
1<p\leqslant 2
.
We recently obtained
[
LT23
]
an affirmative answer for
p=2
,
i.e. we constructed in the space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

a Schauder frame of the form

\{(T_{\lambda_{n}}g,g_{n}^{*})\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

where
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a uniformly discrete real sequence.

The proof in
[
LT23
]

was based on the fact that the Fourier transform is a unitary mapping

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. This point breaks down
for
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
spaces,
1<p<2
.

The main goal of the present paper is to extend the result from
[
LT23
]

to certain values
of
p
within the range
1<p<2
. We will prove the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
. Then there exist a uniformly discrete
real sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

|\lambda_{n}|=n+o(1)
,
a function
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and a sequence
\{g^{*}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

in
(L^{p}(\mathbb{R}))^{*}
,
such that every
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
admits a series expansion

f(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g^{*}_{n}(f)g(x-\lambda_{n})

(1.4)

convergent in the
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
norm.

The question whether the result holds for every
p>1
, remains open.

Remarks

1. We refer the reader to
[
LT23
, Section 4]

where some extensions of the main result are given,
most of which apply to the result of the present paper as well.

2. Motivated by recent interest in nonnegative
coordinate systems in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
spaces, see

[
PS16
]
,
[
JS15
]
,
[
FPT21
]
,
one may ask whether there exists a Schauder
frame in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
formed
by a uniformly discrete sequence of translates
of some
nonnegative
function
g
.
We will show
that by a certain modification of our
proof, this additional requirement of
nonnegativity of
g
can
be achieved in Theorem
1.1

(see Section
5
).

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we present some necessary background
and fix notation that will be used throughout the paper.

2.1.

The
Schwartz space

\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
consists of all infinitely smooth
functions
\varphi
on
\mathbb{R}
such that for each
n,k\geqslant 0
, the seminorm

\|\varphi\|_{n,k}:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(1+|x|)^{n}|\varphi^{(k)}(x)|

(2.1)

is finite. A
tempered distribution
is a
linear functional on the Schwartz space
which is continuous with respect to the
topology generated by this family of seminorms.
We use
\alpha(\varphi)
to denote the action of
a tempered distribution
\alpha
on a
Schwartz function
\varphi
.

We denote by
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
the closed
support of a tempered distribution
\alpha
.

If
\varphi
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}
then we define its Fourier transform by

\widehat{\varphi}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(t)e^{-2\pi ixt}dt.

(2.2)

The Fourier transform of a tempered distribution

\alpha
is defined by

\widehat{\alpha}(\varphi)=\alpha(\widehat{\varphi})
.

If
\alpha
is a tempered distribution and if

\varphi
is a
Schwartz function, then
the product
\alpha\cdot\varphi
is
a tempered distribution defined
by
(\alpha\cdot\varphi)(\psi)=\alpha(\varphi\cdot\psi)
,

\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.
The convolution

\alpha\ast\varphi

of a tempered distribution

\alpha
and
a Schwartz function
\varphi
is
an infinitely smooth function
which is also
a tempered distribution, and
whose Fourier transform is
\widehat{\alpha}\cdot\widehat{\varphi}
.

2.2.

Let
A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, denote the Banach space of
Schwartz distributions
\alpha
on the circle
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}

whose Fourier coefficients
\{\widehat{\alpha}(n)\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
belong to
\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
, endowed with the norm

\|\alpha\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}:=\|\widehat{\alpha}\|_{\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})}
.
For
p=1
this is the classical Wiener algebra
A(\mathbb{T})

of continuous functions with an absolutely convergent
Fourier series.

We also use
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, to denote
the Banach space of
tempered distributions
\alpha
on
\mathbb{R}

whose Fourier transform
\widehat{\alpha}

is in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, with the norm

\|\alpha\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}:=\|\widehat{\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
.

Note that
A^{1}(\mathbb{T})
and
A^{1}(\mathbb{R})
are function spaces,
continuously embedded in
C(\mathbb{T})
and
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
respectively.
Similarly, for
1<p\leqslant 2
the space
A^{p}
(on either
\mathbb{T}
or
\mathbb{R}
)
is a function space, continuously embedded in
L^{q}
,

q=p/(p-1)
, by the Hausdorff-Young inequality.
On the other hand,
A^{p}
is not a function space for
p>2
.

2.3.

For
0<h<1/2
we denote by
\Delta_{h}
the “triangle function” on
\mathbb{T}
vanishing
outside
(-h,h)
, linear on
[-h,0]
and on
[0,h]
, and satisfying

\Delta_{h}(0)=1
. Then
\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(0)=h
, and

\|\Delta_{h}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant h^{(p-1)/p},\quad 1\leqslant p<\infty.

(2.3)

Indeed, to obtain the estimate (
2.3
) one can use the fact
that Fourier coefficients of
\Delta_{h}
are real and nonnegative,
hence
\|\Delta_{h}\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{n}\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)=\Delta_{h}(0)=1
.
Moreover, we have
\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)\leqslant\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Delta_{h}(t)dt=h
for every
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and so
\|\Delta_{h}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}=\sum_{n}\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)^{p}\leqslant h^{p-1}
.

For
0<h<1/4
we also use
\tau_{h}
to
denote the “trapezoid function” on
\mathbb{T}
which vanishes
outside
(-2h,2h)
, is equal to
1
on
[-h,h]
, and
is linear on
[-2h,-h]
and on
[h,2h]
. Then

\widehat{\tau}_{h}(0)=3h
, and

\|\tau_{h}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant 3h^{(p-1)/p},\quad 1\leqslant p<\infty,

(2.4)

which follows from (
2.3
) and the fact that

\tau_{h}(t)=\Delta_{h}(t+h)+\Delta_{h}(t)+\Delta_{h}(t-h)
.

2.4.

By a
trigonometric polynomial
we mean a finite sum of the form

P(t)=\sum_{j}a_{j}e^{2\pi i\sigma_{j}t},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},

(2.5)

where
\{\sigma_{j}\}
are distinct real numbers, and
\{a_{j}\}
are complex numbers.

By the
spectrum
of
P
we mean the set

\operatorname{spec}(P):=\{\sigma_{j}:a_{j}\neq 0\}
.
We observe that if
P

has integer spectrum,
\operatorname{spec}(P)\subset\mathbb{Z}
,
then
P
is
1
-periodic, that is,

P(t+1)=P(t)
. In this case,
P
may be
considered also as a function on
\mathbb{T}
.

By the
degree
of
P
we mean the number

\deg(P):=\min\{r\geqslant 0:\operatorname{spec}(P)\subset[-r,r]\}
.

The (symmetric)
partial sum

S_{r}(P)
of a trigonometric polynomial
(
2.5
) is defined by

S_{r}(P)(t)=\sum_{|\sigma_{j}|\leqslant r}a_{j}e^{2\pi i\sigma_{j}t}.

(2.6)

We say that
P
is
analytic

if we have
\operatorname{spec}(P)\subset[0,+\infty)
.

2.5.

For a trigonometric polynomial
(
2.5
) we use the notation

\|\widehat{P}\|_{p}=(\sum_{j}|a_{j}|^{p})^{1/p}

and
\|\widehat{P}\|_{\infty}=\max_{j}|a_{j}|
.
If
f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
and
P
is a trigonometric polynomial, then

\|f\cdot P\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|\widehat{P}\|_{1}\cdot\|f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

(2.7)

which follows by an application of the triangle inequality in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
using the fact that the Fourier transform of
f\cdot P
is given by

\sum_{j}a_{j}\widehat{f}(x-\sigma_{j})
.

In a similar way one can establish the inequality

\|f\cdot g\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\|f\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}\cdot\|g\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})},\quad f\in A(\mathbb{T}),\;g\in A^{p}(\mathbb{T}).

(2.8)

2.6.

We introduce an auxiliary norm
\|{{\cdot}}\|_{{*}}
on the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, defined by

\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}:=10\cdot\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}(1+x^{2})|\widehat{u}(x)|,\quad u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}).

(2.9)

If
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and

f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
,
then
f
may be
considered also as a
1
-periodic tempered distribution on
\mathbb{R}
,
so the product
u\cdot f
makes sense and is well defined.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and

f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Then

\|u\cdot f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}\|f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}.

(2.10)

Indeed, an application of Hölder’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\|u\cdot f\|_{A_{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{p}

\displaystyle=\int\Big|\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(n)\widehat{u}(x-n)\Big|^{p}\,dx

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leqslant\int\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{p}|\widehat{u}(x-n)|\Big)\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{u}(x-n)|\Big)^{p-1}dx

(2.12)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}^{p-1}\int\Big(\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{p}|\widehat{u}(x-n)|\Big)dx

(2.13)

\displaystyle\leqslant\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}^{p-1}\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\widehat{f}(n)|^{p}=\|{{u}}\|_{{*}}^{p}\|f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}^{p}.

(2.14)

If
f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
and
\nu
is a positive integer, then we use

f_{\nu}
to denote the element of the space

A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
whose Fourier series is given by

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{f}(n)e^{2\pi in\nu t}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
u\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{T})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
. Then

\lim_{\nu\to\infty}\|u\cdot f_{\nu}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\|u\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}.

(2.15)

This is obvious if
u
has a compactly supported Fourier transform
\widehat{u}
.
In the general case, (
2.15
)
can be proved by approximating
u
in the
\|{{\cdot}}\|_{{*}}
norm
by a Schwartz function
v
with a compactly supported Fourier transform
\widehat{v}
,
and using the inequality (
2.10
) to estimate the error.

2.7.

If
\alpha
is a tempered distribution on
\mathbb{R}

and
P
is a trigonometric polynomial, then the product

\alpha\cdot P
is a tempered distribution
defined by
(\alpha\cdot P)(\varphi)=\alpha(P\cdot\varphi)
,
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\alpha\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
1\leqslant p<\infty
, and
suppose that
P
is a nonzero trigonometric polynomial on
\mathbb{R}
.
If
\alpha\cdot P=0
, then
\alpha=0
.

Proof.

The condition
\alpha\cdot P=0

implies that
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
is contained in the
set of zeros of
P
, which is a discrete closed set in
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\chi
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}
with

\chi(t)=1
in a neighborhood of a point

a\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
, and

\chi(t)=0
in a neighborhood of
\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)\setminus\{a\}
.
The distribution
{\alpha}\cdot{\chi}
is then
supported at the point
a
and
coincides with
{\alpha}
in a neighborhood of
a
.
It is well known that a distribution supported at a single
point
a
is a finite linear combination of derivatives
of Dirac’s measure at the point
a
. In turn, this
implies that
(\widehat{\alpha}\ast\widehat{\chi})(x)=e^{-2\pi iax}q(x)

where
q
is a polynomial.
But
\widehat{\alpha}\ast\widehat{\chi}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, so this is possible only if

\widehat{\alpha}\ast\widehat{\chi}
, and hence also

\alpha\cdot\chi
, is zero. We conclude that
{\alpha}
must
vanish in a neighborhood of any point
a\in\operatorname{supp}(\alpha)
,
a contradiction unless
\alpha=0
.
∎

2.8.

We will use the known fact that
in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p>1
, there exist

complete systems
formed by uniformly discrete
translates of a single function.

It was proved in
[
AO96
]
that
for every
p>2
, there is a function
g\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R})

whose translates by the positive integers

\{g(x-n)\}
,
n=1,2,3,\dots
,
span the whole space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
, that is,
these translates are complete in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.
A similar result was proved also in the space
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
.
On the other hand, no system of integer translates
can be complete in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for
1\leqslant p\leqslant 2

(see e.g. Example 11.2 and Corollary 12.26 in
[
OU16
]
).

However, it was proved in

[
Ole97
]
that for any “small perturbation”
of the integers,

\lambda_{n}=n+\alpha_{n},\quad 0\neq\alpha_{n}\to 0\quad(|n|\to+\infty)

(2.16)

there exists
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})

such that the system

\{g(x-\lambda_{n})\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
is complete in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. It was moreover
shown in
[
OU04
]

that if the perturbations are exponentially small, i.e.

0<|\alpha_{n}|<Cr^{|n|},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z},

(2.17)

for some
0<r<1
and
C>0
,
then
g
can be chosen in the Schwartz class.

More recently, by a development of the approach
from
[
OU04
]
, the latter result was
extended to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
spaces
[
OU18a
]
, namely,
it was proved that there is a Schwartz function

g
such that if the sequence

\{\lambda_{n}\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
satisfies (
2.16
) and (
2.17
)
then the system
\{g(x-\lambda_{n})\}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
is complete in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for every
p>1

(see also
[
OU18b
]
).

In a recent paper
[
Lev24
]
, a different approach was given
for constructing a function
g
which spans the
space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p>1
, by uniformly discrete translates.
In fact, this approach allows to use only positive translates,
and moreover the completeness remains true for any subsystem
obtained by the removal of a finite number of elements.

Theorem 2.4

(see
[
Lev24
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

There is a real sequence

\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

\lambda_{n}=n+o(1)
, and there is
a Schwartz function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
,
such that for any
N
the system

\{g(x-\lambda_{n})\},\;n>N,

(2.18)

is complete in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
for every
p>1
.

3.
Approximation lemma

3.1.

A key ingredient in our proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
.
Given any
\varepsilon>0
there exist two real trigonometric polynomials

P
and
\gamma
with integer spectrum, such that

(i)

\widehat{P}(0)=0
,
\|\widehat{P}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
;

(ii)

\|\gamma-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon
;

(iii)

\|\gamma\cdot P-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon
;

(iv)

\max\limits_{l}\|\gamma\cdot S_{l}(P)\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<C_{p}
,

where
C_{p}
is a constant depending only on
p
.

Remarks
.

1. We do not know whether Lemma
3.1
holds for every
p>1
.
This is the reason for the restriction
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2

in Theorem
1.1
. A proof of
Lemma
3.1
for every
p>1

would imply the validity of
Theorem
1.1
for every
p>1
.

2. The proof establishes condition
(iv)
with
an absolute constant
C_{p}
which in fact does not depend on
p
.

3.2.

The following assertion will be used in our proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P_{0},\dots,P_{N-1}
be trigonometric polynomials
with integer spectrum, and let
\nu
be a positive
integer,
\nu>2\deg(P_{j})
,
0\leqslant j\leqslant N-1
.
Define

P(t):=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}P_{j}(\nu^{j}t)
.
Then we have

\widehat{P}(0)=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\widehat{P}_{j}(0)

and

\|P\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\|P_{j}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}
.

Indeed, expanding each
P_{j}
as a Fourier sum yields

P(t)=\sum\Big[\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}\widehat{P}_{j}(k_{j})\Big]e^{2\pi it\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}k_{j}\nu^{j}}

(3.1)

where the sum goes through all integer vectors

(k_{0},k_{1},\dots,k_{N-1})

with
|k_{j}|\leqslant\deg(P_{j})
.
The condition
\nu>2\deg(P_{j})
,
0\leqslant j\leqslant N-1
,
ensures that the exponentials in
(
3.1
) have distinct frequencies, so that
(
3.1
) is the Fourier expansion of
P
.
The conclusion of the lemma now follows
in a straightforward manner.

3.3.
Proof of Lemma
3.1

We divide the proof into several steps.

3.3.1.

With no loss of generality, we may additionally assume that
p<2
.
Let us choose
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p)>0
small enough, to be specified later.
We then choose and fix
0<h<1/3
and a positive integer
N

(both depending on
\varepsilon
,
\delta
and
p
) such that

N>\varepsilon^{-1},\quad(1+3^{p}h^{p-1})^{N}<1+\delta,\quad(1-3h)^{N}>1-\delta,\quad 4^{p}N^{-p}h^{-1}<1-\delta.

(3.2)

We show that such a choice of
h
and
N
exists. Indeed,
denote
\eta:=3^{-p}\log(1+\delta)
, and let
h=h(N,\eta,p)

be defined by the condition
N=\eta\cdot h^{1-p}
.
Then for
N
sufficiently large we have the inequalities

N>\varepsilon^{-1}
;

(1+3^{p}h^{p-1})^{N}<\exp(3^{p}\eta)=1+\delta
;

(1-3h)^{N}>1-3hN>1-\delta
, and lastly, using
the assumption

p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
, we can also ensure that

N^{-p}h^{-1}=\eta^{-p}h^{p(p-1)-1}<4^{-p}(1-\delta)
.

3.3.2.

Now observe that

(1-\tau_{h})\cdot(1-h^{-1}\Delta_{h})=1-\tau_{h}
.
Let
f
and
g
be Fourier partial sums
of
1-\tau_{h}
and
1-h^{-1}\Delta_{h}
respectively,
of sufficiently high order such that

\|f\cdot g-f\|_{A}<\delta.

(3.3)

Next, choose a positive integer
\nu
satisfying

\nu>2(\deg(f)+\deg(g)),

(3.4)

and define

\gamma(t):=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}f(\nu^{j}t),\quad P(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g(\nu^{j}t).

(3.5)

We will check that the
conditions
(i)
–
(iv)
are satisfied.

3.3.3.

First we note that
\widehat{P}(0)=0
. Also,
due to (
3.4
),

\|\widehat{P}\|_{\infty}=\frac{1}{N}\|\widehat{g}\|_{\infty}\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\sup_{n\neq 0}h^{-1}\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)\leqslant\frac{1}{N}\int_{\mathbb{T}}h^{-1}\Delta_{h}(t)dt=\frac{1}{N}<\varepsilon,

(3.6)

where the last inequality is due to (
3.2
).
Thus we obtain condition
(i)
.

3.3.4.

Next, due to (
2.4
) we have

\|f\|_{A^{p}}^{p}\leqslant\|1-\tau_{h}\|_{A^{p}}^{p}=(1-3h)^{p}+\sum_{n\neq 0}|\widehat{\tau}_{h}(n)|^{p}<1+3^{p}h^{p-1},

(3.7)

hence using (
3.2
), (
3.4
) we obtain

\|\gamma\|_{A^{p}}^{p}=\big(\|f\|_{A^{p}}^{p}\big)^{N}<(1+3^{p}h^{p-1})^{N}<1+\delta.

(3.8)

Also, again using (
3.2
), (
3.4
) we have

\widehat{\gamma}(0)=\widehat{f}(0)^{N}=(1-3h)^{N}>1-\delta,

(3.9)

and so it follows from (
3.8
), (
3.9
) that

\|\gamma-1\|_{A^{p}}^{p}=\|\gamma\|_{A^{p}}^{p}-\widehat{\gamma}(0)^{p}+(1-\widehat{\gamma}(0))^{p}<(1+\delta)-(1-\delta)^{p}+\delta^{p}<(\varepsilon/2)^{p},

(3.10)

provided that
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p)
is sufficiently small.
So condition
(ii)
follows.

3.3.5.

Next, we have

\gamma(t)(P(t)-1)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}(f(\nu^{j}t)g(\nu^{j}t)-f(\nu^{j}t))\prod_{k\neq j}f(\nu^{k}t),

(3.11)

and thus, recalling (
3.3
), (
3.4
),
(
3.7
), (
3.8
), this implies

\|\gamma\cdot(P-1)\|_{A^{p}}\leqslant\|f\cdot g-f\|_{A}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{A^{p}}\big)^{N-1}<\delta(1+\delta)^{1/p}<\varepsilon/2,

(3.12)

for
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p)
small enough.
We conclude from (
3.10
), (
3.12
) that

\|\gamma\cdot P-1\|_{A^{p}}\leqslant\|\gamma\cdot(P-1)\|_{A^{p}}+\|\gamma-1\|_{A^{p}}<\varepsilon/2+\varepsilon/2=\varepsilon,

(3.13)

and thus condition
(iii)
holds.

3.3.6.

Let us finally check that also
condition
(iv)
is satisfied.
Any partial sum
S_{l}(P)
can be decomposed as

S_{l}(P)(t)=A(t)+B(t)
, where

A(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}g(\nu^{j}t),\quad B(t):=\frac{1}{N}S_{m}(g)(\nu^{s}t).

(3.14)

Using the same argument as in
(
3.11
), (
3.12
) we can obtain

\|\gamma\cdot(A-s/N)\|_{A^{p}}\leqslant\frac{s}{N}\cdot\|f\cdot g-f\|_{A}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{A^{p}}\big)^{N-1}<\delta(1+\delta)^{1/p}<\varepsilon/2,

(3.15)

and consequently

\|\gamma\cdot A\|_{A^{p}}\leqslant\|\gamma\cdot(A-s/N)\|_{A^{p}}+\frac{s}{N}\cdot\|\gamma\|_{A^{p}}<\varepsilon/2+(1+\varepsilon)<2.

(3.16)

Next, we have

\gamma(t)B(t)=\frac{1}{N}(f\cdot S_{m}(g))(\nu^{s}t)\prod_{k\neq s}f(\nu^{k}t),

(3.17)

and therefore due to (
3.4
)

\|\gamma\cdot B\|_{A^{p}}=\frac{1}{N}\|f\cdot S_{m}(g)\|_{A^{p}}\cdot\big(\|f\|_{A^{p}}\big)^{N-1}.

(3.18)

We note using (
2.4
) that

\|f\|_{A}\leqslant\|1-\tau_{h}\|_{A}\leqslant 1+\|\tau_{h}\|_{A}\leqslant 4,

(3.19)

and due to (
2.3
),

\|S_{m}(g)\|_{A^{p}}^{p}\leqslant\|g\|_{A^{p}}^{p}\leqslant\|1-h^{-1}\Delta_{h}\|_{A^{p}}^{p}=h^{-p}\sum_{n\neq 0}\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)^{p}<h^{-p}h^{p-1}=h^{-1}.

(3.20)

Therefore, using
(
3.18
),
(
3.19
),
(
3.20
)
and (
3.2
),

\|\gamma\cdot B\|_{A^{p}}^{p}\leqslant 4^{p}N^{-p}h^{-1}(1+\delta)<(1-\delta)(1+\delta)<1.

(3.21)

We conclude that

\|\gamma\cdot S_{l}(P)\|_{A^{p}}\leqslant\|\gamma\cdot A\|_{A^{p}}+\|\gamma\cdot B\|_{A^{p}}<2+1=3.

(3.22)

Thus condition
(iv)
is established
and the lemma is proved.
∎

4.
Schauder frames of weighted exponentials

In this section we prove Theorem
1.1
.
First we note that the
Fourier transform is an isometric
isomorphism
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
which allows us to reformulate
Theorem
1.1

as a result about Schauder frames of weighted
exponentials in
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
. There exist

w\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
\{h^{*}_{j}\}\subset(A^{p}(\mathbb{R}))^{*}
,
and a
uniformly discrete real sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

satisfying
|\lambda_{n}|=n+o(1)
,
such that every
f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
admits a series expansion

f(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h^{*}_{j}(f)w(t)e^{2\pi i\lambda_{j}t}

(4.1)

convergent in the
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
norm.

The restriction
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
is needed
so that we can invoke
Lemma
3.1
, while otherwise
we only use the assumption
p>1
in the proof.

4.1.

We begin the proof by an application of
Theorem
2.4
, which implies the existence of
a function
u_{0}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
and
a real sequence
\{\sigma(n)\}_{n=1}^{\infty}

satisfying

\sigma(n)=n+o(1),\quad n\to+\infty,

(4.2)

such that for every
N
the system

\{u_{0}(t)e^{2\pi i\sigma(n)t}\}
,
n>N
,
is complete in
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

Next, we choose a normalized Schauder basis

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

for the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. (For example, one may take

\{\widehat{\varphi}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to be
the normalized basis of Haar functions in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
).
We then construct by induction a sequence of
Schwartz functions
\{u_{k}\}
on
\mathbb{R}
,
such that for each
k
and every
N
the system

\{u_{k}(t)e^{2\pi i\sigma(n)t}\},\;n>N,

(4.3)

is complete in the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

At the
k
’th step of the induction, given any
\eta_{k}>0
and
any positive integer
N_{k}
,
we use the completeness of the system

\{u_{k-1}(t)e^{2\pi i\sigma(n)t}\}
,
n>N_{k}
,
to find a trigonometric polynomial

Q_{k}(t)=\sum_{N_{k}<n<N^{\prime}_{k}}d_{n,k}e^{2\pi i\sigma(n)t}

(4.4)

such that

\|\varphi_{k}-u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}<\eta_{k}.

(4.5)

We choose a small number
\varepsilon_{k}>0
so that

\varepsilon_{k}\cdot\Big(1+\|{{u_{k-1}}}\|_{{*}}\Big)\Big(1+\|\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}\|\widehat{P}_{j}\|_{1}\cdot\|\widehat{Q}_{j}\|_{1}\Big)<2^{-k}\eta_{k},

(4.6)

and apply Lemma
3.1
in order to find real trigonometric polynomials

P_{k}
and
\gamma_{k}
with integer spectrum, such that

(i)

\widehat{P}_{k}(0)=0
,
\|\widehat{P}_{k}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon_{k}
;

(ii)

\|\gamma_{k}-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon_{k}
;

(iii)

\|\gamma_{k}\cdot P_{k}-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon_{k}
;

(iv)

\max\limits_{l}\|\gamma_{k}\cdot S_{l}(P_{k})\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<C_{p}
.

We now choose a large positive integer
\nu_{k}
(to be specified later) and set

\tilde{P}_{k}(t):=P_{k}(\nu_{k}t),\quad\tilde{\gamma}_{k}(t):=\gamma_{k}(\nu_{k}t).

(4.7)

Define
u_{k}:=u_{k-1}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}

which is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}
.
We claim that for every
N
the system (
4.3
)
is complete in the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. Indeed,
let
\alpha
be a tempered distribution belonging to the
dual space

(A^{p}(\mathbb{R}))^{*}=A^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
,
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
,
and suppose that
\alpha
annihilates the system (
4.3
).
This means that

\alpha\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}
,
which also lies in
A^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})
,
annihilates the system

\{u_{k-1}(t)e^{2\pi i\sigma(n)t}\}
,
n>N
.
By the completeness of the latter system in
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
it follows that
\alpha\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}=0
.
In turn, using Lemma
2.3
we conclude that

\alpha=0
. Hence the system (
4.3
)
is complete in
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

4.2.

It follows from
(ii)
and (
4.6
) that

\|u_{k}-u_{k-1}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=\|u_{k-1}(\tilde{\gamma_{k}}-1)\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|{{u_{k-1}}}\|_{{*}}\|\gamma_{k}-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<2^{-k},

(4.8)

hence the sequence
u_{k}
converges
in the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
to some element
w\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
.

4.3.

Next we claim that the estimate

\|\varphi_{k}-w\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}<3\eta_{k}

(4.9)

holds for all
k
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}-w\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|\varphi_{k}-u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\qquad+\|u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\cdot(1-\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k})\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}+\|(u_{k}-w)\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

(4.11)

The right hand side of (
4.10
) is less than
\eta_{k}
due to
(
4.5
). To estimate the first term in (
4.11
)
we apply inequalities (
2.7
) and (
2.10
), and
use
(iii)
and (
4.6
), to obtain

\|u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\cdot(1-\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k})\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|{{u_{k-1}}}\|_{{*}}\cdot\|\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{1}\cdot\|1-\gamma_{k}\cdot P_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\eta_{k}.

(4.12)

It remains to estimate the second term in (
4.11
).
We observe that for any fixed
k
we have

u_{j}\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\to w\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}
as
j\to+\infty
in the
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
norm, hence

\displaystyle\|(u_{k}-w)\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\|(u_{j-1}-u_{j})\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\qquad=\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\|u_{j-1}\cdot(1-\tilde{\gamma}_{j})\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}\|{{u_{j-1}}}\|_{{*}}\cdot\|\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{1}\cdot\|\widehat{P}_{k}\|_{1}\cdot\|1-\gamma_{j}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant\sum_{j=k+1}^{\infty}2^{-j}\eta_{j}<\eta_{k},

(4.16)

again using
(ii)
and (
4.6
), and
assuming (as we may do) that the sequence
\{\eta_{k}\}
is decreasing.
The estimate (
4.9
) thus follows.

4.4.

Let
\{\varphi^{*}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

be the sequence of continuous linear functionals on
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})

which is biorthogonal to the Schauder basis
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
.
It follows from (
4.9
) that if
we choose the sequence
\{\eta_{k}\}
to satisfy
3
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\|\varphi^{*}_{k}\|\cdot\eta_{k}<1
, then the system

\{w\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}

(4.17)

forms another Schauder basis in the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})

(see
[
You01
, Section 1.9]
).
Hence every
f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
has a series expansion

f=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\psi_{k}(f)\,w\cdot\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}

(4.18)

where
\{\psi_{k}\}
are the continuous linear functionals
on
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
which are biorthogonal to the system
(
4.17
). Moreover, recall that we have chosen the Schauder basis

\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
to be normalized,
so it follows from (
4.9
) that
the norms of the
elements of the system
(
4.17
) are bounded from below.
This implies that

\sup_{k}|\psi_{k}(f)|\leqslant K\|f\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\lim_{k\to\infty}\psi_{k}(f)=0

(4.19)

where
K
is a constant not depending on
f

(see
[
You01
, Section 1.6]
).

4.5.

Notice that we have

\tilde{P}_{k}(t)Q_{k}(t)=P_{k}(\nu_{k}t)Q_{k}(t)=\sum_{m\neq 0}\widehat{P}_{k}(m)Q_{k,m}(t),

(4.20)

where
Q_{k,m}
are trigonometric polynomials defined by

Q_{k,m}(t):=Q_{k}(t)e^{2\pi im\nu_{k}t}=\sum_{N_{k}<n<N^{\prime}_{k}}d_{n,k}e^{2\pi i(\sigma(n)+m\nu_{k})t}.

(4.21)

If we choose the sequence
\{\nu_{k}\}
increasing sufficiently fast, then the spectra
of the polynomials
Q_{k,m}
follow each other, meaning that

\max\operatorname{spec}(Q_{k,m_{1}})<\min\operatorname{spec}(Q_{k,m_{2}}),\quad m_{1}<m_{2}.

(4.22)

Moreover, there is a positive, increasing sequence
\{R_{k}\}
such that

\operatorname{spec}(\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k})=\bigcup_{m\in\operatorname{spec}(P_{k})}\operatorname{spec}(Q_{k,m})\subset(-R_{k+1},-R_{k})\cup(R_{k},R_{k+1}).

(4.23)

We now define

\Lambda:=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{spec}(\tilde{P}_{k}\cdot Q_{k}),

(4.24)

then each point
\lambda\in\Lambda
has a unique representation as

\lambda=m\cdot\nu_{k}+\sigma(n),\quad k\geqslant 1,\quad m\in\operatorname{spec}(P_{k}),\quad N_{k}<n<N^{\prime}_{k}.

(4.25)

We can use (
4.2
) to
choose
\{N_{k}\}
increasing fast enough, so that (say)

|\sigma(n)-n|<\tfrac{1}{10}\cdot k^{-1},\quad n>N_{k}.

(4.26)

This implies that
\Lambda
is a uniformly discrete set.
Moreover, if the elements of
\Lambda
are
enumerated as
\{\lambda_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}

by increasing modulus, that is,

0<|\lambda_{1}|\leqslant|\lambda_{2}|\leqslant\dots
, then
there are positive integers
n_{j}
such that

|\lambda_{j}|=n_{j}+o(1),\quad j\to+\infty.

(4.27)

We now observe that in fact we have

0<n_{1}<n_{2}<\dots
. Indeed,
the fact that the polynomials
Q_{k}

have positive spectra implies that if

\lambda\in\Lambda
is given by (
4.25
), then

|\lambda|=|m|\cdot\nu_{k}+\operatorname{sign}(m)\cdot\sigma(n)
,
where
\operatorname{sign}(m)
is
+1
or
-1
according to
whether
m
is positive or negative.
Hence, if
\lambda_{j}
and
\lambda_{l}
are
two distinct points of
\Lambda
then

n_{j}\neq n_{l}
.

4.6.

For
\lambda\in\Lambda
given by (
4.25
)
we define
h_{\lambda}^{*}\in(A^{p}(\mathbb{R}))^{*}
by

h_{\lambda}^{*}:=d_{n,k}\widehat{P}_{k}(m)\,\psi_{k}.

(4.28)

We will prove that each

f\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
admits a series representation

f(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}h_{\lambda_{j}}^{*}(f)w(t)e^{2\pi i\lambda_{j}t}

(4.29)

where the convergence is in the
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
norm.
This will establish Theorem
4.1
, and as a consequence,
Theorem
1.1
will also be proved.

Indeed,
any partial sum of the series (
4.29
)
can be decomposed as
S^{\prime}+S^{\prime\prime}+S^{\prime\prime\prime}
where

S^{\prime}(t)=\sum_{s=1}^{k-1}\psi_{s}(f)w(t)\tilde{P}_{s}(t)Q_{s}(t),

(4.30)

S^{\prime\prime}(t)=\psi_{k}(f)w(t)Q_{k}(t)S_{l}(P_{k})(\nu_{k}t),

(4.31)

and

S^{\prime\prime\prime}(t)=\psi_{k}(f)w(t)\big[\widehat{P}_{k}(l+1)S_{r}(Q_{k,l+1})(t)+\widehat{P}_{k}(-(l+1))S_{r}(Q_{k,-(l+1)})(t)\big]

(4.32)

for some
k
,
l
and
r
.

We have
\|f-S^{\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=o(1)
as
k\to\infty
due to (
4.18
).

In order to estimate
\|S^{\prime\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
we
denote
\tilde{S}_{k,l}(t):=S_{l}(P_{k})(\nu_{k}t)
, then

\|w\cdot\tilde{S}_{k,l}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant\|(w-u_{k})\cdot\tilde{S}_{k,l}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}+\|u_{k-1}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\cdot\tilde{S}_{k,l}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}.

(4.33)

The first summand on the right hand side
can be estimated similarly to the inequalities (
4.13
)–(
4.16
),
using the fact that
\|\widehat{S}_{k,l}\|_{1}\leqslant\|\widehat{P}_{k}\|_{1}
.
To estimate the second summand, we use Lemma
2.2

to conclude that if
\nu_{k}
is chosen sufficiently large, then

\|u_{k-1}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\cdot\tilde{S}_{k,l}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}<\|u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\|\gamma_{k}\cdot S_{k,l}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}+1.

(4.34)

It follows from (
4.5
) that

\|u_{k-1}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}<2
,
since the sequence
\{\varphi_{k}\}
is normalized in
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
while according to
(iv)
we have

\|\gamma_{k}\cdot S_{k,l}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<C_{p}
.
We conclude using (
4.33
) that

\|w\cdot\tilde{S}_{k,l}\cdot Q_{k}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=O(1)
.
In turn, together with (
4.19
), (
4.31
) this implies the desired estimate

\|S^{\prime\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=o(1)
as
k\to\infty
.

Finally, we estimate
\|S^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}
using
(i)
and the
inequalities (
2.7
), (
4.6
) and obtain

\|S^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 2|\psi_{k}(f)|\cdot\|\widehat{P}_{k}\|_{\infty}\|\widehat{Q}_{k}\|_{1}\|w\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}\leqslant 2\eta_{k}\cdot|\psi_{k}(f)|\cdot\|w\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})},

(4.35)

which shows that
\|S^{\prime\prime\prime}\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{R})}=o(1)
as
k\to\infty
as well.

We conclude that (
4.29
) indeed
holds, which completes the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

As a consequence, Theorem
1.1
is also established.

5.
Schauder frames of translates and nonnegativity

5.1.

In
[
PS16
]
the following question was considered: do there exist (unconditional or not) Schauder bases or Schauder frames in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
consisting of nonnegative functions? It turns out that it is not difficult to construct a Schauder frame formed by nonnegative functions, while unconditional Schauder frames of nonnegative functions do not exist in any
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
space.

In the paper
[
JS15
]
(which was written after
[
PS16
]
) some proofs from
[
PS16
]
were simplified, and also a Schauder basis consisting of nonnegative functions in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
was constructed. A Schauder basis of nonnegative functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
was constructed in
[
FPT21
]
. The existence of such a basis in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p\neq 1,2
, remains open.

Motivated by the recent interest in nonnegative coordinate systems in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
spaces, we consider the following question: does there exist a Schauder frame in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
formed by a uniformly discrete sequence of translates of a
nonnegative
function?

In this section, our goal is to show that
this additional requirement of nonnegativity can indeed be achieved
in our main result, namely:

Theorem 5.1
.

The function
g
in Theorem
1.1

can be chosen nonnegative.

To establish this, we will make certain modifications to
the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

5.2.

First, we recall that our construction
in Section
4
began with an application of
Theorem
2.4
, which yields
a real sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
satisfying

\lambda_{n}=n+o(1)
, and
a Schwartz function
g
on
\mathbb{R}
,
such that for any
N
the system

\{g(x-\lambda_{n})\},\;n>N,

(5.1)

is complete in the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p>1
.
So first, we need
to establish the following:

Lemma 5.2
.

The function
g
in
Theorem
2.4

can be chosen nonnegative.

This can be proved by adapting the approach in
[
Lev24
]
;
the details are omitted.

5.3.

The second ingredient which enables us to construct
a nonnegative function
g
in Theorem
1.1
,
is the following lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
a
and
h
be two positive real numbers,

2h<a<\frac{1}{2}-2h
. Then there is a nonnegative
function
\varphi\in A(\mathbb{T})
with the following properties:

(i)

\widehat{\varphi}(0)=1
,
\widehat{\varphi}(n)\geqslant 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

\varphi
vanishes on the set

[-a-h,-a+h]\cup[a-h,a+h]
;

(iii)

\|\varphi-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant 12\cdot h^{(p-1)/p}

for every
p\geqslant 1
.

Proof.

We consider the function

\psi(t):=6\cdot\Delta_{h}(t)-\tau_{h}(t+a)-\tau_{h}(t-a),

(5.2)

which is in
A(\mathbb{T})
. We observe that

\tau_{h}=\Delta_{h}\ast(\delta_{-h}+\delta_{0}+\delta_{h})
,
where
\delta_{t}
denotes the Dirac measure at a point
t\in\mathbb{T}
. Hence

\psi=6\cdot\Delta_{h}-\Delta_{h}\ast(\delta_{-h}+\delta_{0}+\delta_{h})\ast(\delta_{-a}+\delta_{a})=\Delta_{h}\ast(6\cdot\delta_{0}-\nu),

(5.3)

where
\nu=(\delta_{-h}+\delta_{0}+\delta_{h})\ast(\delta_{-a}+\delta_{a})
.
Then
\nu
is a positive measure on
\mathbb{T}
with total mass

\widehat{\nu}(0)=6
, and the Fourier coefficients
\widehat{\nu}(n)
are all real valued.
This implies that

-6\leqslant\widehat{\nu}(n)\leqslant 6,\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}.

(5.4)

It now follows from (
5.3
) that

\widehat{\psi}(n)=\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)(6-\widehat{\nu}(n))
,
and
since both terms in the product are nonnegative, we conclude
that
\widehat{\psi}(n)\geqslant 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. Furthermore,

\|\psi\|^{p}_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\psi}(n)^{p}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\widehat{\Delta}_{h}(n)^{p}(6-\widehat{\nu}(n))^{p}\leqslant 12^{p}\|\Delta_{h}\|^{p}_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant 12^{p}h^{p-1}

(5.5)

for every
p\geqslant 1
, due to (
2.3
).
Finally, set
\varphi:=1+\psi
.
Then it is obvious that condition
(i)
holds.
It follows from (
5.2
) that the function

\varphi
is nonnegative and satisfies
(ii)
.
Moreover, we obtain
(iii)
as a consequence
of (
5.5
). The lemma is thus proved.
∎

Remark 5.4
.

One can show that condition
(ii)
implies that

\|\varphi-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\geqslant h^{(p-1)/p}

for every
p\geqslant 1
, so the estimate

(iii)
is sharp up to the numerical
value of the constant.

5.4.

We now use Lemma
5.3
to strengthen Lemma
3.1
as follows.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
p>(1+\sqrt{5})/2
.
Given any
\varepsilon>0
there exist two real trigonometric polynomials

P
and
\gamma
with integer spectrum, such that

(i)

\widehat{\gamma}(0)=1
,
\widehat{\gamma}(n)\geqslant 0
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
;

(ii)

\widehat{P}(0)=0
,
\|\widehat{P}\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
;

(iii)

\|\gamma-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon
;

(iv)

\|\gamma\cdot P-1\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}<\varepsilon
;

(v)

\max\limits_{l}\|\gamma\cdot S_{l}(P)\|_{A^{p}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant 3
.

The novel part of this result compared to Lemma
3.1

is the addition of property

(i)
, that is, the requirement that
\gamma

have nonnegative Fourier coefficients.

Proof of Lemma
5.5
.

We first choose a small
\delta=\delta(\varepsilon,p)>0
, and
then choose and fix
0<h<1/8
and a positive integer
N

(both depending on
\varepsilon
,
\delta
and
p
) such that

N>\varepsilon^{-1},\quad(1+12^{p}h^{p-1})^{N}<1+\delta,\quad 13^{p}N^{-p}h^{-1}<1-\delta.

(5.6)

To see that such a choice of
h
and
N
exists, it suffices
to let
h=h(N,\delta,p)

be defined by the condition
N=12^{-p}h^{1-p}\log(1+\delta)
,
and take
N
to be sufficiently large.

We now choose an arbitrary number
a
satisfying

2h<a<\frac{1}{2}-2h
.
Let
\varphi
be the function of Lemma
5.3
,
and let
\psi=1-h^{-1}\Delta_{h}\ast\frac{1}{2}(\delta_{-a}+\delta_{a})
,
where again
\delta_{t}
is the Dirac measure at a point
t\in\mathbb{T}
.
We observe that

\varphi\cdot\psi=\varphi
.
Let
f
and
g
be Fourier partial sums
of
\varphi
and
\psi
respectively,
of sufficiently high order such that

\|f\cdot g-f\|_{A}<\delta
.

Next, choose a positive integer
\nu
satisfying

\nu>2(\deg(f)+\deg(g))
,
and define

\gamma(t):=\prod_{j=0}^{N-1}f(\nu^{j}t),\quad P(t):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}g(\nu^{j}t).

(5.7)

The proof can now continue in the same way as in
Lemma
3.1
, and it follows that this
choice of
\gamma
and
P
indeed satisfies
all the conditions
(i)
–
(v)

of Lemma
5.5
.
∎

5.5.

We can now adjust the proof of Theorem
1.1

given in Section
4

as follows. Recall that in the proof we have
constructed by induction a sequence of
Schwartz functions
u_{k}
.
Thanks to Lemma
5.2

we may now assume that the first Schwartz function
u_{0}

has a nonnegative Fourier transform
\widehat{u}_{0}
.
Next, at the
k
’th step of the induction
we now use Lemma
5.5
instead of
Lemma
3.1
, which yields a trigonometric polynomial

\gamma_{k}
with nonnegative Fourier coefficients.
As a consequence, it follows that the function

u_{k}:=u_{k-1}\cdot\tilde{\gamma}_{k}

is a Schwartz function whose
Fourier transform
\widehat{u}_{k}
is nonnegative.

In turn, the sequence
u_{k}

converges in the space
A^{p}(\mathbb{R})

to an element
w\in A^{p}(\mathbb{R})
whose Fourier
transform
\widehat{w}
is a nonnegative function
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
. We thus conclude that
Theorem
4.1
holds with the extra condition
that
w
have a nonnegative Fourier transform.
Finally, this means that Theorem
1.1

holds with the function
g=\widehat{w}
which is
nonnegative,
and so Theorem
5.1
is established.
∎

Remarks

1. In the space
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
,
p>2
, one
can adapt the technique
from
[
FOSZ14
, Section 3]
in order to
construct a Schauder frame (not unconditional)
formed by an
arbitrary unbounded

sequence of translates of a
nonnegative
function
g
.

2. One may also consider the
Banach space
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
of continuous functions on
\mathbb{R}
vanishing
at infinity, endowed with the norm
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup|f(x)|
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
First we note that this space
does not admit any
unconditional Schauder frames
. Indeed, a Banach space with an
unconditional Schauder frame is isomorphic to a complemented subspace of
a Banach space with an unconditional Schauder basis
[
CHL99
]
,
which is not the case for the space
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})

(see
[
Woj91
, Section II.D, Corollary 12]
).
On the other hand, again based on the technique
from
[
FOSZ14
, Section 3]
, one can show
that the space
C_{0}(\mathbb{R})
admits a Schauder frame
(not unconditional) formed by an arbitrary unbounded
sequence of translates of a function
g
, which can
moreover be chosen nonnegative.

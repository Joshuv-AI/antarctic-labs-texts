Teleparallel bigravity
Daniel Blixt, Manuel Hohmann, Tomi Koivisto, Luca Marzola

Abstract
We write down the teleparallel equivalent to Hassan-Rosen bigravity, which is written using a torsionful but curvature-free connection. The theories only differ by a boundary term. The equivalence was proven, both by using perturbation theory and Hamiltonian analysis. It is further shown how one can construct novel bigravity theories within the teleparallel framework. Some of those are analyzed through perturbation theory, and it is found that all of the considered novel bigravity theories suffer from pathologies. In particular, it is found that a construction with two copies of new general relativity leads to ghostly degrees of freedom which are not present in the single tetrad teleparallel corresponding theory. We demonstrate how the teleparallel framework allows to easily create theories with derivative interaction. However, it is shown through perturbation theory that the simplest model is not viable. Furthermore, we demonstrate some steps in the Hamiltonian analysis of teleparallel bigravity with two copies of new general relativity and some toy models. The results rule out some of the novel teleparallel bigravity theories, but also demonstrate techniques in perturbation theory and Hamiltonian analysis which could be further used for more profound theories in the future.



I
Introduction

Quantum field theory has successfully led to a consistent description of all fundamental forces of nature within the standard model of particle physics, except gravity. The standard model in its present form has passed numerous experimental tests, including most prominently the discovery of the Higgs boson almost half a century after its prediction. Modifying such a robust theory, to make it consistent with general relativity seems very difficult. A more natural approach would be to look at modifications of general relativity. The gauge structure encountered in the standard model and unexplained observations in Nature guide us towards the most motivated ways to explore theories beyond general relativity.

General relativity has also been very successful in passing experimental tests. It has successfully predicted the deflection of light from the sun and the precession of Mercury. One may ask why we should consider theories beyond general relativity. Beside a philosophically appealing idea, that there would exist a theory for everything, we need a quantum theory of gravity in order to understand the early universe and black holes. However, general relativity is a notoriously non-renormalizable theory, as is very nicely explained in
Zee:2003mt
, so that the usual quantization methods used in the standard model cannot be applied.

From observations at scales of galaxies and larger, we conclusively find evidence for the so-called dark sector of the universe. The dark sector consists of dark matter and dark energy, which make up about 95% of our observable universe. Dark energy gives rise to an expanding universe at an accelerating speed. The existence of dark matter can be inferred indirectly by its gravitational effects on the motion of stars within galaxies, gravitational lensing, and structure formation. However, neither of these components has been observed in any laboratory experiment searching for a suitable extension of the standard model. Since gravity is the dominant force at the scales where dark matter and dark energy are observed, it is motivating to look at extensions of general relativity which could give us an understanding of the dark sector of the universe.

II
Teleparallel bigravity

In this section we formulate a novel class of theories of gravity which we choose to call teleparallel bigravity. The basic idea of this theory is quite simple. By taking the Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity theory as our starting point, which is based on a formulation with a metric compatible and torsion-free connection, one can view this action in the teleparallel framework. In this formulation we are able to construct novel theories of gravity, as well as recovering equivalent theories, such as the teleparallel equivalent to Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity. In order to reach a construction of teleparallel bigravity we start by reviewing Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity in subsection

II.3
we give a short review for teleparallel equivalent to general relativity, and its extension to new general relativity is reviewed in section
II.4
. Section
II.5

II.2
Other bigravity theories

Our aim is to look at bigravity theories in the teleparallel framework, which assumes vanishing curvature. This includes novel theories of gravity. Recently, other novel bigravity theories have been proposed in articles where either the title is reminiscent to the concept of teleparallel gravity, or the structure of the theory is similar. Before going into the theory of teleparallel gravity, we highlight the main differences between these bigravity theories and teleparallel bigravity, in order to avoid any confusion. In
Damour:2019oru
;
Nikiforova:2020fbz
;
Nikiforova:2022pqy
a novel theory named torsion bigravity has been considered. Here, a theory of both curvature and torsion is formulated, where a massless spin-2 field is mediated by the curvature, whereas the massive spin-2 field is mediated by the torsion, which avoids the notion of two metrics (or tetrads) in the construction of a bigravity theory.

In
Markou:2018mhq
a bigravity theory is constructed with vierbeins as the fundamental field in order to yield an antisymmetric part of the field equations. It was motivated by the fact that antisymmetric field equations played a big role in the quantization of string theory, which suggests that it would make sense to include antisymmetric parts in a gravity theory. Their construction differs from ours by being curvature-based. However, we cannot exclude cases where the field equations might be equivalent. This theory was extended from the single vierbein massive gravity theory proposed in
Markou:2018wxo
. In
Alexandrov:2019dxm
the structure of the action seems to yield a physics similar to that of our theory, and even closer to that of
Li:2015iwc
. The crucial difference with respect to our approach is that our construction uses more than one connection. In teleparallel bigravity the action contains two connections (corresponding to the two tetrad fields), but only one spin connection. The construction of a bigravity theory with only one connection was found to yield one graviton, instead of two as in other bigravity theories, making this scheme significantly different from teleparllel bigravity
Alexandrov:2019dxm
.

III.2
Linear analysis of the teleparallel equivalent to Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity

As a first application of the general theory, we recover the standard bimetric theory in tetrad formulation. To this purpose we eliminate the new terms brought by the kinetic mixing in the Lagrangian setting
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{i}=0
. For the sake of visualization, we also rewrite the above Lagrangian in matrix formalism, using a basis where the field vector has the components
\Phi:=
(

\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}
,
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}

T

(58)

The matrices
\mathbb{K}

\mathbb{M}^{2}

Requiring the absence of linear terms for
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}
and
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}
in the bimetric Lagrangian yields the following conditions on the coefficients entering the interaction term

\begin{cases}4\beta_{0}=-3\beta_{1}-2\beta_{2}-\beta_{3}\\

\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}+\beta_{4}\equiv 0,

Hinterbichler:2012cn

\overset{int}{\mathcal{L}}_{1}=\overset{int}{\mathcal{L}}_{0}=0

\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{A}{}^{A}=\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{A}{}^{A}\equiv 0

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}G_{AB}\,\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}+\frac{1}{4}M_{AB}\,\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}

(72)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}M_{AB}\,\frac{\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}-\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}-\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}\left(M_{AB}M^{AB}-M^{2}\right)\,,

showing that the condition
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}
is necessary for a complete diagonalization of the symmetric sector. In fact, because the performed field redefinition is simply a rotation, the kinetic term matrix of the original field must be proportional to the unit matrix in order to be unaffected by the field redefinition.

III.2.2
Antisymmetric sector

As for the antisymmetric sector, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{bm}^{bb}&=-\frac{1}{4}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\,\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\right)\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}\,\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{DE}\\
&+\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}\frac{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\end{split}

(73)

which is diagonalized through a rotation analogous to that of the symmetric sector. In particular, by defining

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}E_{AB}\\
F_{AB}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\sqrt{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}}&-\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}{\sqrt{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}}\\
\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}{\sqrt{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}}&\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\sqrt{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\\
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}\end{pmatrix}

(74)

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{bm}^{bb}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}F_{AB}\,\frac{\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,F_{DE}

(75)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}E_{AB}\,\frac{\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,E_{DE}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}F_{AB}\,\frac{\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}-2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}-\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,E_{DE}+\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}E_{AB}E^{AB}\,.

In analogy with the case of the symmetric sector, the condition
2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}=2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}
ensures the decoupling of the modes.

III.2.3
Teleparallel Equivalent of Bimetric Gravity

In terms of the mass eigenstates we have identified and through the above conditions, the full Lagrangian then becomes:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{bm}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\,G_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\,M_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}-\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}\left(M_{AB}M^{AB}-M^{2}\right)

(76)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\right)\,F_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,F_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\right)\,E_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,E_{DE}+\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}E_{AB}E^{AB}\,.

Setting now the remaining coefficients to their TEGR values, we notice that
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}=-1
ensures a proper normalizes the Lichnerowicz operator
\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}
and casts the Lagrangian of the symmetric sector into the usual bimetric form:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{bm}^{hh}=\frac{1}{4}G_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}+\frac{1}{4}M_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}-\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}\left(M_{AB}M^{AB}-M^{2}\right)\,.

(77)

Differently, the choice
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}=C_{2}=1/2
prevents the propagation of all antisymmetric modes, reducing the corresponding Lagrangian to

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{bm}^{bb}=\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}E_{AB}E^{AB}\,.

(78)

The resulting equation of motion then forces constraint
E_{AB}=0
, which through eq.
(
74
)
recovers

\displaystyle\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}=\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\,.

(79)

At the first order of the perturbation expansion, this condition coincides with the requirement
\eta_{AB}{\mathfrak{e}^{A}}_{[\mu}{\mathfrak{f}^{B}}_{\nu]}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0
, often discussed in the literature of bigravity
Kocic:2018ddp
;
Kocic:2018yvr
;
Kocic:2019ahm
;
Hinterbichler:2012cn
.

We remark that the final form of the Lagrangian was obtained by using a rotation in field space, rather than a generic linear transformation, to diagonalize the mass term. Whether this might seem as an overly restrictive choice, we remind that in the teleparallel equivalent of bimetric gravity, the coefficients
C_{i}
of the two tetrads are set from the very beginning to their TEGR values and, as a consequence, the identified rotation is the only transformation that recasts the symmetric sector in the standard form.

We also notice that prior to setting the coefficients
C_{i}
to their TEGR values, the Lagrangian of the antisymmetric sector mirrors that of the symmetric one, albeit an important distinction. The mass terms of the massive modes, in fact, have opposite signs. Whether a generic theory containing propagating symmetric and antisymmetric modes would probably contain ghosts, at this stage it seems still possible to recover a regime of the theory characterized by the propagation of a massive and a massless antisymmetric mode and no symmetric perturbations.

III.3
Linear analysis of the full Lagrangian

We now face the full Lagrangian, allowing for the kinetic mixing terms that we neglected in the previous section. Note that in the limit
m_{FP}\to 0
of vanishing Fierz-Pauli mass, a kinetic coupling in the symmetric sector, which contains two massless spin-2 fields in this case, inevitably leads to the existence of a scalar ghost mode
Boulanger:2000rq
. For this reason, such kinetic coupling is not considered in the Hassan-Rosen bigravity
Hassan:2011zd
.

The treatment of the linear and 0-th order terms proceeds as in the standard bimetric case: enforcing the absence of tadpole term by setting

\displaystyle\begin{cases}4\beta_{0}=-3\beta_{1}-2\beta_{2}-\beta_{3}\\
4\beta_{4}=-3\beta_{3}-2\beta_{2}-\beta_{1}\end{cases}\,,

(80)

we obtain
\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}+\beta_{3}+\beta_{4}\equiv 0
, and the mass term consequently acquires again the form

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}^{2}=\dfrac{m^{4}_{int}\left(3\beta_{1}+4\beta_{2}+3\beta_{3}\right)}{96}\begin{pmatrix}-\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}}&\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}&0&0\\
\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}&-\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}&0&0\\
0&0&\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}}&-\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\\
0&0&-\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}&\dfrac{1}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\left(\eta^{AD}\eta^{BE}-\eta^{AB}\eta^{DE}\right)

(81)

in terms of the properly normalized fields given by

\displaystyle\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}\to\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}},\quad\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}\to\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}},\quad\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\to\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}},\quad\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}\to\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\,.

(82)

As for the kinetic term of the normalized fields, in this first analysis we choose to set

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{1}=\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\\
-2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{1}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\\
-2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{1}=\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\end{cases}

(83)

in order to obtain the same block diagonal structure of the mass term. Whereas this condition is in principle unnecessary, it allows us to tackle the symmetric and antisymmetric modes separately, with a considerable simplification of the diagonalization procedure. In order to further simplify the Lagrangian, we also opt to set
m^{2}_{\mathfrak{e}\mathfrak{f}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}
, obtaining:

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\begin{pmatrix}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}&\dfrac{\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}}{2}&0&0\\
\dfrac{\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}}{2}&\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}&0&0\\
0&0&(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3})&\dfrac{1}{2}(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})\\
0&0&\dfrac{1}{2}(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})&(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3})\end{pmatrix}\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,,

(84)

with the Lichnerowicz operator
\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}
defined as in the standard case. Because the interaction term is the same as in the standard bimetric gravity, the field rotations in eq.
(
71
)
and
(
74
)
certainly diagonalize it. However, if we restrict ourselves to these transformations, the coefficients in the kinetic term will have to be set in a way that the kinetic term becomes proportional to the unit matrix in field space. In particular, this choice will force the absence of kinetic mixing, leading inevitably to scenarios close to the standard bimetric case or that, at most, allow for the propagation of a massless and a negative mass antisymmetric mode. For this reason, in the following we consider instead generic superpositions of symmetric and antisymmetric perturbations rather than rotations in the corresponding field spaces.

III.3.1
Symmetric sector

Upon partial integration of the off-diagonal kinetic terms, the Lagrangian for the symmetric perturbation is written as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{hh}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{DE}

(85)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{m^{4}_{int}}{4}\left[\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)-\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)^{2}\right]

where, without loss of generality, we have set
\left(3\beta_{1}+4\beta_{2}+3\beta_{3}\right)=24
. In order to diagonalize the Lagrangian, we now consider the generic linear transformation

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}\\
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}M_{AB}\\
G_{AB}\end{pmatrix}

(86)

where
G_{AB}
and
M_{AB}
are respectively the graviton and massive bigraviton excitation. However generic the transformation be, we still require that an inverse exists by imposing that
the determinant of the transformation never vanishes
. We will verify that the solutions we identify indeed satisfy this criterion.

Analyzing the mass term in isolation, we see that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)=\left[\left(\frac{\alpha}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\gamma}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\,M_{AB}+\left(\frac{\beta}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\delta}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\,G_{AB}\right]

(87)

and analogous expressions hold for the remaining terms. It is then clear that setting

\displaystyle\delta=\frac{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}\beta

(88)

forbids a mass term for the
G
perturbation. Similarly, requiring that the kinetic terms for
G
and
M
have the canonical form, as well as the presence of no mixing, results in three further conditions that fully determine the parameters in eq.
(
86
)
. Explicitly, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{hh}&\supset-\frac{1}{4}\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\alpha^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\gamma^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\alpha\gamma\right)\,M_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\beta^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\delta^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\beta\delta\right)\,G_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}\\
&-\frac{1}{4}\left(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\alpha\,\beta+2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\gamma\,\delta+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}(\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma)\right)\,M_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE},\end{split}

(89)

where
\supset
denotes that some terms in the Lagrangian are omitted, and imposing the above condition, as well as

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\alpha^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\gamma^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\alpha\gamma=-1\\
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\beta^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\delta^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}\beta\delta=-1\\
2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\alpha\,\beta+2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\gamma\,\delta+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}(\alpha\delta+\beta\gamma)=0\end{cases}

(90)

results in four possible solutions for the involved parameters which differ only by the verse associated to the selected linear combinations in field space. In particular, the choice

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\alpha=-\dfrac{\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}+2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\sqrt{\left(\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}^{2}-4\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}\\
\beta=\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}{\sqrt{-\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}\\
\gamma=\dfrac{\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}+2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}}{\sqrt{\left(\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}^{2}-4\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}\\
\delta=\dfrac{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}{\sqrt{-\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}\end{cases}

(91)

correctly reduces to the (inverse of the) rotation matrix in eq.
(
71
)
. Real and finite coefficients are obtained when

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}<0\\
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}^{2}-4\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}<0\end{cases}

(92)

which also ensure that the transformation is invertible:

\displaystyle\det\begin{pmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{pmatrix}\overset{\text{eq.}\eqref{eq:goodsub}}{=}\frac{2\sqrt{-\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}{\sqrt{\left(\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}^{2}-4\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}\right)}}

(93)

We notice the following particular values of
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}
:

•

\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}=\pm 2\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}}
. In this case the kinetic term acquires the form

\mathcal{L}\supset A^{2}\,M_{AB}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}+B^{2}\,G_{AB}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}+2AB\,G_{AB}\,\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}
, implying that a vanishing mixing term necessarily requires at least either of the kinetic terms to vanish.

•

\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}=-(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2})/m_{e}m_{f}
. Once eq.
(
88
)
is imposed to prevent a mass term for the
G_{AB}
perturbation, this particular value of
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}
nullifies the kinetic term of the same perturbation.

Excluding the above values of
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}
, the Lagrangian of the symmetric sector simply becomes

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{hh}=\frac{1}{4}\,M_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,M_{DE}+\frac{1}{4}\,G_{AB}\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,G_{DE}-\frac{m_{FP}^{2}}{4}\left(M_{AB}M^{AB}-M^{2}\right)

(94)

where the involved perturbations are determined from the original
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{h}_{AB}
and
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{h}_{AB}
fields through the inverse of eq.
(
86
)
and where we have defined

\displaystyle m_{FP}^{2}:=m_{int}^{4}\left(\frac{\alpha}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\gamma}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)^{2}=m_{int}^{4}\frac{4\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{e}}m_{\mathfrak{f}}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}\right)}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}^{2}m_{\mathfrak{f}}^{2}\left(\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}^{2}-4\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}\right)}\,.

(95)

Notice that the Fierz-Pauli mass is positive defined if the conditions in eq.
(
92
)
are satisfied, and becomes vanishing or singular in correspondence of the two values of
\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3}
that make the transformation singular. This seems to imply that if a fluctuation does not propagate, the other must necessarily be massless.

III.3.2
Antisymmetric sector

Analogously, for the antisymmetric perturbations we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{bb}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\,(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3})\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}\,(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3})\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{DE}-\frac{1}{4}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\,(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})\,\mathcal{E}^{ABDE}\,\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{DE}

(96)

\displaystyle+\frac{m_{int}^{4}}{4}\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)\left(\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}^{AB}}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right),

where we used
\left(3\beta_{1}+4\beta_{2}+3\beta_{3}\right)=24
. In order to diagonalize the above Lagrangian we introduce the states
E_{AB}
and
F_{AB}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{b}_{AB}\\
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{b}_{AB}\end{pmatrix}=:\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}E_{AB}\\
F_{AB}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(97)

and express the mass term as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{bb}\supset\frac{m^{4}_{int}}{4}\left[\left(\frac{a}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{c}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)E_{AB}+\left(\frac{b}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{d}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)F_{AB}\right]\left[\left(\frac{a}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{c}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)E^{AB}+\left(\frac{b}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{d}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)F^{AB}\right]\,.

(98)

Imposing the absence of mass mixing then forces, again, the presence of a massless state. In analogy to the symmetric case, we choose here

\displaystyle\frac{b}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\frac{d}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}

(99)

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{bb}\supset\frac{m^{4}_{int}}{4}\left(\frac{a}{m_{\mathfrak{e}}}-\frac{c}{m_{\mathfrak{f}}}\right)^{2}E_{AB}E^{AB}=:\frac{\mu_{FP}^{2}}{4}E_{AB}E^{AB}\,.

(100)

Notice that
\mu^{2}_{FP}\geq 0
and consequently the mass term appears in the Lagrangian inevitably with the wrong sign. Leaving this issue aside for the moment, we proceed to express the kinetic term in terms of the newly defined perturbations

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{bb}

\displaystyle\supset-\frac{1}{4}\left((2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3})\,a^{2}+(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3})\,c^{2}+(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})\,ac\right)\,E_{AB}\,{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,E_{DE}

(101)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left((2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3})\,b^{2}+(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3})\,d^{2}+(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})\,bd\right)\,F_{AB}\,{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,F_{DE}+

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\left(2(2\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3})\,ab+2(2\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3})\,cd+(2\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{2}+\accentset{\textrm{\ae}}{C}_{3})\,(ad+bc)\right)\,E_{AB}\,{\mathcal{E}}^{ABDE}\,F_{DE}

(102)

.
Requiring now

As in the case with vanishing kinetic mixing, the general theory seems to admit the propagation of a ghost-like massive antisymmetric perturbation. Although it is certainly possible to force the kinetic terms of this problematic perturbation to vanish through a proper choice of the coefficients, we argue below that this might not be necessary. As antisymmetric perturbations correspond, in essence, to the propagation of scalar fields, the presence of a negative mass term could simply indicate the emergence of a spontaneously broken symmetry. In fact, for the sake of the viability of the theory, it could be enough that the terms of higher order in the perturbative expansion give rise to self interactions of the scalar field which result in a potential bounded from below. The situation is analogous to that of the Higgs boson in the standard model, where the negative mass term of the field triggers the electroweak symmetry breaking.

IV.2
Specific examples

In this section we go into two examples for which we write down the Hamiltonian (to the step before using Dirac’s algorithm to calculate Poisson brackets and find all constraints). The first example is two copies of new general relativity which is very easy to get from previous results
Blixt:2018znp
;
Blixt:2019ene
. The second is the
\overset{kin}{\mathcal{L}}
-term which share a lot of similarities with new general relativity. This theory, however, contains ghosts and only acts as a toy model. Note that
\mathcal{H}\left(\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{kin}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{int}{\mathcal{L}}\right)\neq\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{H}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{H}}+\overset{kin}{\mathcal{H}}+\overset{int}{\mathcal{H}}
. The inversion of velocities to conjugate momenta will in this case be different than simply the sum of them due to the derivative interactions between the tetrads in
\overset{kin}{\mathcal{L}}
. The interaction potential transforms under Legendre transformation just by changing its sign. We, hence, focus only on kinetic terms for the Hamiltonian analysis (until the step when Poisson brackets are calculated).

IV.2.1
Two copies of new general relativity:
\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{L}}

A straightforward example of a Hamiltonian for novel teleparallel bigravity terms is obtained by restricting to the case with no derivative interaction. Then the conjugate momenta for the tetrads will only be related to the velocity of the tetrad for which this conjugate momenta was defined. In this case, the Legendre transformation of the total Lagrangian equals the addition of the Lagrangians individually Legendre transformed,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}\left(\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{L}}\right)=\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{H}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{H}}.

(138)

Since we already know
\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{H}}
from the literature
Blixt:2018znp
;
Blixt:2019ene
;
Blixt:2019mkt
, and
\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{H}}
will just be another copy of the same Hamiltonian, but with respect to the
f
-tetrad we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{H}\left(\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathcal{L}}+\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{\mathcal{L}}\right)&=\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{N}m_{e}^{2}\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\gamma}}\left(-\frac{{}^{\mathcal{V}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{i}{}^{\mathcal{V}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}^{i}}{4A_{\mathcal{V}}}+\frac{{}^{\mathcal{A}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{ij}{}^{\mathcal{A}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}^{ij}}{4A_{\mathcal{A}}}+\frac{{}^{\mathcal{S}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{ij}{}^{\mathcal{S}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}^{ij}}{4A_{\mathcal{S}}}+\frac{3{}^{\mathcal{T}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}{}^{\mathcal{T}}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}}{4A_{\mathcal{T}}}-{}^{3}\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{\mathbb{T}}-\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\xi}^{A}\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{D}_{i}\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\pi}_{A}{}^{i}}{\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\gamma}}}\right)\\

&+\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\gamma}}\overset{\mathfrak{e}}{D}_{i}\left[\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\pi}_{A}{}^{i}}{\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\gamma}}}\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{N}\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{\xi}^{A}+\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{N}^{j}\mathfrak{e}^{A}{}_{j}\right)\right]+\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{\gamma}}\overset{\mathfrak{f}}{D}_{i}\left[\frac{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{\pi}_{A}{}^{i}}{\sqrt{\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{\gamma}}}\left(\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{N}\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{\xi}^{A}+\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{N}^{j}\mathfrak{f}^{A}{}_{j}\right)\right].\end{split}

(139)

Now the interaction potential should be added and it will modify the secondary constraints in lapse and shift. Since in general we do not have the special case where the antisymmetric modes become non-dynamical, we avoid that non-linearities in lapse and shift appear from solving the equations of motion for the antisymmetric tetrad components. However, one should still be cautious since for particular theories there exists constraints making certain tetrad components non-dynamical and we might end up in a similar situation. From this point Poisson brackets need to be calculated for whichever sub-theory one is interested in, from which eventual secondary, tertiary, …constraints can be found. In particular, this theory has two subcases. One is new general relativity in the single tetrad limit. The other is teleparallel equivalent to Hassan-Rosen bigravity (see eq. (
20

\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{1}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{4},\ \accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{2}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{2}=\frac{1}{2},\ \mathrm{and}\ \accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{C}_{3}=\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{C}_{3}=-1

V
Discussion and conclusions

The teleparallel equivalent of Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity (TEHR) has been formulated and it admits almost the same structure of constraints as the tetrad formulation of Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity. The primary constraints associated with the antisymmetric part of the conjugate momenta differ, however, by the presence of torsion terms originating from differences in the boundary
Blixt:2020ekl
. Nevertheless, the symmetries the theory admits are the same, and thus, the degrees of freedom coincide with other formulations of Hassan-Rosen bimetric gravity
Hassan:2011zd
;
Hinterbichler:2012cn
. With TEHR as a starting point we found several possibilities to create novel modified theories of gravity. For instance, one can make two copies of NGR. As an initial assumption, the NGR coefficients in the
\accentset{\mathfrak{e}}{g}
sector can be different from the
\accentset{\mathfrak{f}}{g}
sector. However, analyzing the perturbations around the Minkowski background reveals that the coefficients need to be proportional to each other for the modes to be decoupled. The only difference is absorbed in the mass constant in front of the respective kinetic term used to define the Fierz-Pauli mass. It is already known that NGR suffer from the strong coupling problem in Minkowski backgrounds
Cheng:1988zg
;
BeltranJimenez:2019nns
and ghost instabilities in the generic case
Ortin:2015hya
. We confirm this result and find that the problem worsens in the case of teleparallel bigravity since we find that ghostly modes propagate in this theory
7
7

7

The strong coupling problem could in theory be avoided by going to less trivial backgrounds and analyze the viability of the theory in more generic backgrounds. An example of such an attempt can be found in
f(T)
-gravity
Golovnev:2020nln
.
.

As another example, we introduce terms with derivative interactions. It appears that the teleparallel framework makes construction of such examples more easy, however, likewise the Riemannian case is known to introduce ghost instabilities in the symmetric sector
Boulanger:2000rq
in the massless case, this may be retained in the massive case. In future works, it would be interesting to investigate constructions with complex tetrads similar to what have been done in the Riemannian case
Apolo:2016ort
;
Apolo:2016vkn
. The motivation for this would be to investigate if viable theories with partially massless symmetry can be constructed in a viable way. Previous results suggest that this is strongly coupled around Minkowski backgrounds, and one would need to extend the linear analysis in this work to perturbations around complex De Sitter tetrads. It is also outlined how the Hamiltonian can be obtained in the model considered in this article, and with more details in a simpler model where the derivation is easier to carry out.

Furthermore, it is shown in app.
A
that as in other teleparallel theories
Blixt:2018znp
;
Blixt:2019mkt
;
Golovnev:2021omn
;
Blixt:2022rpl
;
Golovnev:2023yla
, the so-called Weitzenböck gauge can safely be assumed. One may easily be mistaken, trying to include two spin connections in the construction of teleparallel bigravity theories since bimetric gravity assumes two Levi-Civita connections. However, that would have little sense and miss the point of the theory covariant under Lorentz transformations
Golovnev:2017dox
;
Krssak:2015oua
. Overall, our work has made the initial investigations for teleparallel bigravity. Providing details about teleparallel and bimetric gravity and explained the difference between these theories and a couple of other theories that would appear to be similar. Some novel theories have been ruled out by our analysis, which also provides useful tools to further investigate perturbation theory and Hamiltonian analysis in the future.

Currently, the perhaps most promising avenue to pursue is paved by the parameterised field theory approach to gauging translations
Koivisto:2019ejt
;
Koivisto:2022uvd
. The conventional approach, adopted also in this paper, is well established to describe gauge interactions of fields and particles
in
spacetime, in continuum as well as in lattice models. However, if we would rather consider gravity as the gauge theory
of
spacetime, we should not assume the inhomogeneous extension of the symmetry group to begin with. In the parameterised field theory approach, the fundamental field giving rise to the spacetime metric is not its extension to a tetrad, but its reduction to a scalar. Recently, a viable theory with two interacting frame fields emerging from two scalar fields and giving rise to two independent but non-trivially coupled metrics, has been constructed in this approach, though not for bigravity but for the unification of gravity and the particle physics Yang-Mills theories
Gallagher:2022kvv
. Some of the methods and results we presented in this paper could be useful also in the potentially interesting application of the parameterised field theory approach to interacting multiple spin-2 field theories.

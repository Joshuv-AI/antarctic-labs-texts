1.
Introduction

The theory of noncommutatiive martingales is now well-established as a useful tool in various aspects of noncommutative analysis, quantum probability theory, and operator algebras. Various types of Hardy spaces arising from noncommutative martingales have played significant role in the development of the theory for the last few decades. We recall that the noncommutative Burkholder-Gundy inequalities proved by Pisier and Xu in
[
37
]
that triggered the modern phase of the study of noncommutative martingales were based on consideration of column/row Hardy spaces and how they should be mixed according the indices. Likewise, the noncommutative Burkholder inequalities due to Junge and Xu in
[
22
]
can be formulated using the conditioned versions of the ones from
[
37
]
together with
another type called diagonal Hardy spaces. We should also mention that the theory of maximal inequalities for noncommutative martingales developed by Junge in
[
20
]
generates another type of Hardy spaces in this context.

Interpolations between Hardy spaces from various fields of mathematics have a long history and proven to be very useful in many areas such as harmonic analysis, PDE’s, Banach space theory, among others. Although some of these Hardy spaces are closely related, we will only focus on those from martingale theory.
We refer to
[
13
,
50
,
53
,
52
]
for background concerning interpolations of Hardy spaces from classical martingale theory that are relevant for our purpose.

The primary objective of the present paper is to further advance the topic of interpolations between noncommutative column/row conditioned martingale Hardy spaces from noncommutative martingale theory.
We refer to the body of the paper for unexplained notation below.
We recall that the study of interpolations of noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces was initiated by Musat in
[
32
]
(see also
[
21
]
)
where the complex interpolation of the compatible couple
(\mathcal{H}_{1},{\mathcal{BMO}})
was given. Later, Bekjan
et al.
established in
[
3
]
that the analogue of Musat’s result is valid for couple of column/row conditioned Hardy spaces. More precisely, they obtained the corresponding result for the compatible couple
(\mathsf{h}_{1}^{c},\mathsf{bmo}^{c})
. More recently, the case of quasi-Banach space couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c},\mathsf{bmo}^{c})
for
0<p<\infty
were obtained in
[
41
]
using the real interpolation method. To the best of our knowledge, the articles
[
3
,
21
,
32
,
41
]
are the only available references in the literature that contain essential progress in understanding interpolation spaces of noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces to date.
A common theme in the above cited articles is that all couples considered have one of the endpoints consisting of appropriate types of noncommutative martingale
BMO
spaces. Naturally, the noncommutative Fefferman-Stein duality of
H_{1}
and
BMO
established in
[
22
,
37
]
in the setting of noncommutative martingale spaces plays prominent role in achieving the appropriate martingale BMO-space as endpoints of the various interpolation
couples considered thus far.

In the present paper, we deviate from the papers cited above and consider a previously untouched topic which we
may view as a version of the Peter Jones’s interpolation for the couple of classical Hardy spaces
(H_{1},H_{\infty})
in the setting of
Hardy spaces associated with noncommutative martingales. We refer to
[
4
,
18
,
19
,
36
]
for background concerning Peter Jones’s result.

To better explain our consideration, we make the notation a little more precise. Assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra equipped with a faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
. For
0<p\leq\infty
,
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the noncommutative column conditioned martingale Hardy space associated to the index
p
and fixed filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
for which we refer to the preliminary section below for detailed description. These spaces were heavily used in the articles
[
20
,
22
]
. The exact formulation of our primary result takes into account
a highly nontrivial fact proved by Junge in
[
20
]
that there exists a semifinite von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
with
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{N}
and such that for every
0<p\leq\infty
, the (quasi) Banach space
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
isometrically embeds into the noncommutative space
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
. Thus, it may be viewed as a subspace of
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
. The main theorem in the paper states that for
0<p<\infty
, the compatible couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple
(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{\infty}(\mathcal{N}))
in the sense of Pisier (
[
36
]
). We refer to Theorem
3.1
below for precise formulation. As an immediate consequence, we deduce that the family
\{\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\}_{0<p\leq\infty}
forms a real interpolation scale in the following sense: if
0<p<\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and

1/q=(1-\theta)/p
, then

\big(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,q}=\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

where
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\theta,q}
denotes the real interpolation method. In fact, our
K
-closed result is quite flexible that when combined with some known general results about
K
-functionals, it allows us to prove far more general interpolation results. For instance, we obtain an automatic transfer of interpolation of couples of symmetric function spaces to the corresponding couples of noncommutative conditioned column Hardy spaces. More precisely, if
E
is a quasi-Banach function space that is an interpolation space of the couple
(L_{p},L_{q})
for some
0<p<q<\infty
and
F
is an interpolation of the couple
(E,L_{\infty})
, then

\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is an interpolation space of the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
.
These type of results appeared to be new even for the classical martingale settings. We refer to Corollary
3.15
for precise formulation.

Our method of proof combines techniques from interpolation theory and martingale theory. We should note that duality arguments were used in the papers

[
3
,
21
,
32
]

in order to
reach
BMO
-spaces as one of the endpoints. This is no longer available when working with
\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. Our approach is based on explicit decompositions for martingales from
\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
in order to get specific formulae for
K
-functionals for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
. As expected, the so-called Cuculescu’s projections are instrumental in constructing such decompositions. Once we understand the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
, we combine it with some previously known results from
[
41
]
via a Wolff type theorem for
K
-closed couples due to Kislyakov and Xu to deduce the general statement for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
for any given
0<p<2
.
The generalization to the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
where
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space is achieved by a reiteration formula for interpolation with function space parameters.

Recently, there have been renewed interests on martingale Hardy spaces associated with Orlicz function spaces in the classical setting. We refer to recent articles
[
27
,
28
]
for more perseptive and background on real interpolations of couples involving martingale Orlicz-Hardy spaces in the classical setting. Motivated by these new developments, we show as an illustration of our unified approach of lifting interpolation results from couples of symmetric function spaces to the corresponding
noncommutative column Hardy spaces, that results from
[
27
,
28
]
admit noncommutative counterparts. These include real interpolations of the couples
(\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
and
(\mathsf{h}_{\Phi}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
for Orlicz function
\Phi
satisfying some natural conditions.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we give a brief introduction of noncommutative spaces and review the constructions of various Hardy spaces associated to symmetric function spaces. This section also includes discussions on some concepts from interpolation theory that we need in order to precisely state our main theorem.
Section 3 is where we provide the formulation and proof of our result in the form of
K
-closed couples together with some extensions and consequences. The section also contains two separate paragraphs dealing with the specific examples of noncommutative conditioned Hardy spaces associated with Orlicz function spaces and generalized Lorentz spaces respectively.

In the last part of the paper, we include an appendix section where we discuss some applications of the new development made in earlier sections to martingale inequalities. We provide improvement on all results from
[
41
, Section 4]
for inequalities involving martingales in symmetric spaces of measurable operators and corresponding moment inequalities associated with Orlicz functions. We single out here as one of the improvements we made is a resolution of a problem left open in
[
44
]
related to Davis type decomposition for martingales in symmetric spaces of measurable operators. We show that the noncommutative Davis decomposition applies to any martingale in
the Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
if and only if
E
is an interpolation of the couple
(L_{1},L_{2})
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some of the basic facts, notation and tools that will be used in the paper.

2.1.
Noncommutative symmetric spaces

Throughout,
\mathcal{M}\subseteq{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
will denote a semifinite von Neumann algebra on some Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
(here,
{\mathcal{B}}(\mathcal{H})
is the algebra of all bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
with the usual operator norm). It is assumed that
\mathcal{M}
is equipped with a fixed faithful normal semifinite trace
\tau
.
The identity in
\mathcal{M}
will be denoted by
{\bf 1}
. Recall that a linear operator
x:{\mathcal{D}}(x)\to\mathcal{H}
, with domain
\mathcal{D}(x)\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be
affiliated with

\mathcal{M}
if
xu\subseteq ux
for all unitary in the commutant
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{M}
. A densely defined operator
x
is affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
if and only if for every Borel set
B\subseteq\mathbb{R}
,
\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{B}(|x|)\in\mathcal{M}
where
\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{B}(|x|)
is the spectral projection of
|x|
associated with
B
.
The closed and densely defined operator
x
, affiliated with
\mathcal{M}
, is called
\tau
-measurable
if and only if there exists
s\geq 0
such that
\tau\big(\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)\big)<\infty
. The collection of all
\tau
-measurable operators is denoted by
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. For
\varepsilon,\delta>0
, we set
V(\varepsilon,\delta)
to be the set all

x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
for which there exists a projection
p\in\mathcal{M}
such that
p(\mathcal{H})\subseteq\mathcal{D}(x)
,
\|xp\|_{\infty}<\varepsilon
, and
\tau({\bf 1}-p)<\delta
.
The system
\{V(\varepsilon,\delta):\varepsilon,\delta>0\}
forms a
base at
0
for a metrizable Hausdorff topology in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, which is called the
measure topology
. Equipped with the measure topology,
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
is a complete topological
*
-algebra. These facts can be found in
[
33
]
.

For
x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, we recall that its
generalized singular value

\mu(x)

is the real-valued function defined by

\mu_{t}(x):=\inf\big\{s>0:\tau\big(\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(s,\infty)}(|x|)\big)\leq t\big\},\quad t>0.

It follows directly from the definition that the function
\mu(x)
is decreasing, right-continuous on the interval
[0,\infty)
and
\lim_{t\to 0^{+}}\mu_{t}(x)=\|x\|_{\infty}
.
We refer to
[
10
]
for more information on generalized singular values.

We observe that
if
\mathcal{M}
is the abelian von Neumann algebra
L_{\infty}(0,\infty)
with the trace given by integration with respect to the Lebesgue measure, then
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
becomes the space of all measurable complex functions on
(0,\infty)
which are bounded except on a set of finite measure and for
f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,
\mu(f)
is precisely the usual decreasing rearrangement of the function
|f|
. We also note that in this case, convergence for measure topology coincides with the usual notion of convergence in measure.

We will now review the construction of noncommutative symmetric spaces.
We denote by
L_{0}
, the space of all measurable functions on the interval
(0,\infty)
.
Recall that a quasi-Banach function space
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
of measurable functions on the interval
(0,\infty)
is called
symmetric
if for any
g\in E
and any
f\in L_{0}
with
\mu(f)\leq\mu(g)
, we have
f\in E
and
\|f\|_{E}\leq\|g\|_{E}
.
Throughout, all function spaces are assumed to be defined on the interval
(0,\infty)
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space. We define the corresponding noncommutative space by setting:

E(\mathcal{M},\tau)=\Big\{x\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\ :\ \mu(x)\in E\Big\}.

Equipped with the quasi-norm

\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M},\tau)}:=\|\mu(x)\|_{E}
, the linear space
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
becomes a complex quasi-Banach space (
[
24
,
51
]
) and is usually referred to as the
noncommutative symmetric space
associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
corresponding to
(E,\|\cdot\|_{E})
.
We remark that if
0<p<\infty
and
E=L_{p}
, then
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
is exactly the usual noncommutative
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
associated with
(\mathcal{M},\tau)
.
In the sequel,
E(\mathcal{M},\tau)
will be abbreviated to
E(\mathcal{M})
.

Beside
L_{p}
-spaces, Lorentz spaces are also very involved in the subsequent part of the paper.
Let
0<p,q\leq\infty
. The
Lorentz space

L_{p,q}
is the space of all
f\in L_{0}
for which
\|f\|_{p,q}<\infty
where

\big\|f\big\|_{p,q}=\begin{cases}\left(\displaystyle{\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{t}^{q}(f)\ d(t^{q/p})}\right)^{1/q},&0<q<\infty;\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{t>0}t^{1/p}\mu_{t}(f)},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

If
1\leq q\leq p<\infty
or
p=q=\infty
, then
L_{p,q}
is a symmetric Banach function space. If
1<p<\infty
and
p\leq q\leq\infty
, then
L_{p,q}
can be equivalently renormed to become a symmetric Banach function (
[
4
, Theorem 4.6]
). In general,
L_{p,q}
is only a symmetric quasi-Banach function space.

We conclude the subsection with a short introduction of the notion of submajorization in the sense of Hardy, Littlewood, and Polya.
If
x,y\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
then
x
is said to be
submajorized
by
y
if for every
t>0
, the inequality

\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{s}(x)\ ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\mu_{s}(y)\ ds

holds. In this case, we will write
x\prec\prec y
.

In the sequel, we will frequently use the submajorization inequality

(2.1)

\mu(x+y)\prec\prec\mu(x)+\mu(y).

Another fact that is important below is that if
T:L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}\longrightarrow L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
satisfies

\max\{\|T:L_{1}(\mathcal{M})\to L_{1}(\mathcal{M})\|;\|T:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}\|\}\leq 1
then for every
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
,
Tx\prec\prec x
. This fact can be found in
[
9
, Proposition 4.1]
. In particular, if
x\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
and
(p_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a sequence of mutually disjoint projections from
\mathcal{M}
then,

(2.2)

\sum_{k\geq 1}p_{k}xp_{k}\prec\prec x.

For more information on von Neumann algebras and noncommutative integration, we refer to
[
38
,
48
,
49
]
.

2.2.
Basic definitions and terminology from interpolations

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces in the sense that both
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
embed continuously into some topological vector space
\mathcal{Z}
. This allows us to define the spaces
A_{0}\cap A_{1}
and
A_{0}+A_{1}
. These are quasi-Banach spaces when equipped with quasi-norms:

\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}\cap A_{1}}=\max\Big\{\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}},\big\|x\big\|_{A_{1}}\Big\}

and

\big\|x\big\|_{A_{0}+A_{1}}=\inf\Big\{\big\|x_{0}\big\|_{A_{0}}+\big\|x_{1}\big\|_{A_{1}}:\,x=x_{0}+x_{1},\,x_{0}\in A_{0},\,x_{1}\in A_{1}\Big\},

respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

A quasi-Banach space
A
is called an
interpolation space
for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
if
A_{0}\cap A_{1}\subseteq A\subseteq A_{0}+A_{1}
and whenever a bounded linear operator
T:A_{0}+A_{1}\to A_{0}+A_{1}
is such that
T(A_{0})\subseteq A_{0}
and
T(A_{1})\subseteq A_{1}
, we have
T(A)\subseteq A

and

\big\|T:A\to A\big\|\leq c\max\left\{\big\|T:A_{0}\to A_{0}\big\|,\big\|T:A_{1}\to A_{1}\big\|\right\}

for some constant
c
.

If
A
is an interpolation space for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
, we write
A\in{\rm Int}(A_{0},A_{1})
. Below, we are primarily interested in an interpolation method generally referred to as the real method.

We begin with a short discussion of the
real interpolation
method.
A fundamental notion for the construction of real interpolation spaces is the
K
-functional
which we now describe. For
x\in A_{0}+A_{1}
, we define the
K
-functional by setting for
t>0
,

K(x,t)=K\big(x,t;A_{0},A_{1}\big)=\inf\Big\{\big\|x_{0}\big\|_{A_{0}}+t\big\|x_{1}\big\|_{A_{1}}:\,x=x_{0}+x_{1},\,x_{0}\in A_{0},\,x_{1}\in A_{1}\Big\}.

Note that for each
t>0
,
x\mapsto K(x,t)
gives an equivalent quasi-norm on
A_{0}+A_{1}
.

If
0<\theta<1
and
0<\gamma<\infty
, we recall that the real interpolation space
A_{\theta,\gamma}=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}
by
x\in A_{\theta,\gamma}
if and only if

\big\|x\big\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}}=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\big(t^{-\theta}K\big(x,t;A_{0},A_{1}\big)\big)^{\gamma}\ \frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/\gamma}<\infty.

If
\gamma=\infty
, we define
x\in A_{\theta,\infty}
if and only if

\big\|x\big\|_{(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\infty}}=\sup_{t>0}t^{-\theta}K(x,t;A_{0},A_{1})<\infty.

For
0<\theta<1
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, the functional
\|\cdot\|_{\theta,\gamma}
is a quasi-norm and
(A_{\theta,\gamma},\|\cdot\|_{\theta,\gamma})
is a quasi-Banach space. Moreover,
the space
A_{\theta,\gamma}
is an interpolation space for the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
in the sense of Definition
2.1
. There is also an equivalent description of
A_{\theta,\gamma}
using the dual notion of
J
-functionals but this will not be needed for our purpose below.

It is worth mentioning that the real interpolation method is well understood for the couple
(L_{p_{0}},L_{p_{1}})
for both the classical case and the noncommutative case. We record here that
Lorentz spaces can be realized as real interpolation spaces for the couple
(L_{p_{0}},L_{p_{1}})
. More precisely,
if
\mathcal{N}
is a semifinite von Neumann algebra,
0<p_{0}<p_{1}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
0<q\leq\infty
then, up to equivalent quasi-norms (independent of
\mathcal{N}
),

\big(L_{p_{0}}(\mathcal{N}),L_{p_{1}}(\mathcal{N})\big)_{\theta,q}=L_{p,q}(\mathcal{N})

where
1/p=(1-\theta)p_{0}+\theta/p_{1}
. By reiteration, if
0<\lambda,\gamma\leq\infty
, we have

(2.3)

\big(L_{p_{0},\lambda}(\mathcal{N}),L_{p_{1},\gamma}(\mathcal{N})\big)_{\theta,q}=L_{p,q}(\mathcal{N})

with equivalent quasi-norms. These facts can be found in
[
38
]
and will be used repeatedly throughout.

A more general real interpolation type spaces will be essential for our consideration below. Recall that a quasi-Banach function space
\mathcal{F}
has a monotone quasi-norm if whenever
f,g\in\mathcal{F}
,
|f|\leq|g|\implies\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\|g\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

Definition 2.2
.

An interpolation space
E
for a couple of quasi-Banach spaces
(E_{0},E_{1})
is said to be
given by a
K
-method
if there exists a quasi-Banach function space
\mathcal{F}
with monotone quasi-norm such that
x\in E
if and only if
t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\in\mathcal{F}

and there exists a constant
C_{E}>0
such that

C_{E}^{-1}\big\|t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}\leq\big\|x\big\|_{E}\leq C_{E}\big\|t\mapsto K(x,t;E_{0},E_{1})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

In this case, we write
E=(E_{0},E_{1})_{\mathcal{F};K}
.

The following fact will be used in the sequel.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
0<p<q\leq\infty
. Every interpolation space
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
is given by a
K
-method.

For the Banach space range, this fact is known as a result of Brudnyi and Krugliak (see
[
23
, Theorem 6.3]
). An argument for the quasi-Banach space range can be found in
[
41
]
.

We now review the primary topic of the paper.
The following concept on
K
-functionals was formally introduced by Pisier in
[
36
]
and will be essential in the subsequent discussions.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces and
B_{0}
(resp.
B_{1}
) be a subspace of
A_{0}
(resp.
A_{1}
). The couple
(B_{0},B_{1})
is said to be
K
-closed in the couple
(A_{0},A_{1})

if there exists a constant
C
such that for every
y\in B_{0}+B_{1}
and
t>0
,

K(y,t;B_{0},B_{1})\leq CK(y,t;A_{0},A_{1}).

Since the reverse inequality is always valid (with constant
1
),
K
-closedness means that
K
-functionals of the couples
(B_{0},B_{1})
and
(A_{0},A_{1})
are equivalent on
B_{0}+B_{1}
uniformly on
t>0
. Clearly, if
(B_{0},B_{1})
is
K
-closed in
(A_{0},A_{1})
then for every indices
0<\theta<1
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,

(B_{0},B_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}=(B_{0}+B_{1})\cap(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma}.

In fact, this is the case for more general interpolation method based on
K
-functionals: if
\mathcal{F}
is quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm, then

(B_{0},B_{1})_{\mathcal{F};K}=(B_{0}+B_{1})\cap(A_{0},A_{1})_{\mathcal{F};K}.

That is, one can deduce interpolation results for the couple
(B_{0},B_{1})
from the
corresponding results on the larger couple. For more information and background on
K
-closed couples, we refer to
[
36
,
25
,
26
]
.

A Wolff-type result for
K
-functionals was proved in
[
26
]

(see also
[
25
]
). We state it here for further use.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces and
B_{0}
(resp.
B_{1}
) be a subspace of
A_{0}
(resp.
A_{1}
). Assume that

0<\theta<\phi<1
and
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leq\infty
. Set

E_{0}=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,\gamma_{1}},\quad E_{1}=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\phi,\gamma_{2}}

and

F_{0}=(B_{0},B_{1})_{\theta,\gamma_{1}},\quad F_{1}=(B_{0},B_{1})_{\phi,\gamma_{2}}.

If
(B_{0},F_{1})
is
K
-closed in
(A_{0},E_{1})
and
(F_{0},B_{1})
is
K
-closed in
(E_{0},A_{1})
, then
(B_{0},B_{1})
is
K
-closed in
(A_{0},A_{1})
.

For convenience, we will make use of the following two definitions.

Definition 2.6
.

A family of quasi-Banach spaces
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
is said to
form a
real interpolation scale
if for every
0<p<q\leq\infty
,
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leq\infty
,
0<\theta<1
, and
1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
,

X_{r,\gamma}=(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta,\gamma}.

Definition 2.7
.

Assume that a family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
forms a real interpolation scale and for each
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
,
Y_{p,\gamma}
is a subspace of
X_{p,\gamma}
.
Let
I
be a subinterval of
(0,\infty]
. We say that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
is
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
on the interval
I
if for every
p,q\in I
and
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in(0,\infty]
,
the couple
(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})
is
K
-closed in the couple
(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})
.

According to (
2.3
), the family of Lorentz-spaces is an example of a family that forms a real interpolation scale.

The following result may be viewed as an extension of Theorem
2.5
to families of quasi-Banach spaces.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
and
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
be two families such that each forms a real interpolation scale. Assume that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
is
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
on two different intervals
I
and
J
. If

|I\cap J|>1
, then the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
is
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
on the interval
I\cup J
.

Proof.

We may assume that
I
and
J
are closed intervals. As
|I\cap J|>1
, assume that
\sup I>\inf J
and
I\cap J=[w_{1},w_{2}]
where
w_{1}=\inf J
and
w_{2}=\sup I
. Fix
p\in I\setminus J
and
q\in J\setminus I
. Then
p<w_{1}<w_{2}<q
. Let
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\leq\infty
. We need to verify that
(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})
is
K
-closed in
(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})
.

Let
1/w_{j}=(1-\theta_{j})/p+\theta_{j}/q
for
j=1,2
. One can easily see that
0<\theta_{1}<\theta_{2}<1
. We have by assumption that

X_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}}=(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta_{1},\gamma_{1}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ X_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}}=(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta_{2},\gamma_{2}}.

Similarly, we also have from the assumption that

Y_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}}=(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta_{1},\gamma_{1}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ Y_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}}=(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})_{\theta_{2},\gamma_{2}}.

Since both
p
and
w_{2}
belong to
I
, we have that
(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}})
is
K
-closed in

(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}})
. Similarly, since both
w_{1}
and
q
belong to
J
, we have

(Y_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})
is
K
-closed in

(X_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})
.

We apply Theorem
2.5
with
A_{0}=X_{p,\gamma_{1}}
,
A_{1}=X_{q,\gamma_{2}}
,

B_{0}=Y_{p,\gamma_{1}}
,
B_{1}=Y_{q,\gamma_{2}}
,
E_{0}=X_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}}
,
E_{1}=X_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}}
,

F_{0}=Y_{w_{1},\gamma_{1}}
, and
F_{1}=Y_{w_{2},\gamma_{2}}
, to conclude that

(Y_{p,\gamma_{1}},Y_{q,\gamma_{2}})
is
K
-closed in
(X_{p,\gamma_{1}},X_{q,\gamma_{2}})
as desired.
∎

2.3.
Noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces

By a filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we mean an
increasing sequence of von Neumann subalgebras of
{\mathcal{M}}

whose union is w*-dense in

\mathcal{M}
. Throughout, we will work with a fixed filtration
(\mathcal{M}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. For every
n\geq 1
, we assume further that there is a trace preserving conditional expectation
\mathcal{E}_{n}

from
{\mathcal{M}}
onto
{\mathcal{M}}_{n}
. This is the case if for every
n\geq 1
, the restriction of the trace
\tau
on
\mathcal{M}_{n}
is semifinite. It is well-know that for
1\leq p<\infty
, the
\mathcal{E}_{n}
’s extend to be bounded projections from
L_{p}(\mathcal{M},\tau)
onto
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n},\tau|_{\mathcal{M}_{n}})
. In particular, they are well-defined on
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
.

Definition 2.9
.

A sequence
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
is called
a
noncommutative martingale
with respect to the filtration
({\mathcal{M}}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
if for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathcal{E}_{n}(x_{n+1})=x_{n}.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space and
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be a martingale. If for every
n\geq 1
,
x_{n}\in E(\mathcal{M}_{n})
, then we say that
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is an
E(\mathcal{M})
-martingale. In this case, we set

\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\sup_{n\geq 1}\|x_{n}\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

If
\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}<\infty
, then
x
will be called
a bounded
E(\mathcal{M})
-martingale.

For a martingale
x=(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, we set
dx_{n}=x_{n}-x_{n-1}
for
n\geq 1
with the usual convention that
x_{0}=0
. The sequence
dx=(dx_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is called the
martingale difference sequence
of
x
. A martingale
x
is called a
finite martingale
if there exists
N
such that
dx_{n}=0
for all
n\geq N
.

Let us now review some basic definitions related to martingale Hardy spaces associated to noncommutative symmetric spaces.

Following
[
37
]
, we define the
column square functions
of a given
martingale
x=(x_{k})
by setting:

S_{c,n}(x)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad S_{c}(x)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}\,.

The conditioned versions were introduced in
[
22
]
. For a given
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
-martingale
(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}
, we set

s_{c,n}(x)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad s_{c}(x)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|dx_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

(here, we take
\mathcal{E}_{0}=\mathcal{E}_{1}
). The operator
s_{c}(x)
is called the
column conditioned square function
of
x
.
For convenience, we will use the notation

\mathcal{S}_{c,n}(a)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}|a_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}|a_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

and

\sigma_{c,n}(b)=\Big(\sum^{n}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|b_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2},\quad\sigma_{c}(b)=\Big(\sum^{\infty}_{k=1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|b_{k}|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}

for sequences
a=(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
and
b=(b_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
that are not necessarily martingale difference sequences.
It is worth pointing out that the infinite sums of positive operators stated above may not always make sense as operators but we only consider below special cases where they do converge in the sense of the topology in measure.

We will now describe various noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces associated with symmetric quasi-Banach function spaces.

We consider first the class of Hardy spaces associated with square functions.
Assume that
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space. We denote by
\mathcal{F}_{E}
the collection of all finite martingales in
E(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathcal{M}
.
For
x=(x_{k})_{k\geq 1}\in\mathcal{F}_{E}
, we set:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|{S}_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Then
(\mathcal{F}_{E},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}})
is a quasi-normed space.
If we denote by
(e_{i,j})_{i,j\geq 1}
the family of unit matrices in
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
, then
the correspondence
x\mapsto\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}\otimes e_{k,1}
maps
\mathcal{F}_{E}
isometrically into
a (not necessarily closed) linear subspace of
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
.
We define the column Hardy space

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
to be the completion of
(\mathcal{F}_{E},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}})
. It then follows that

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
embeds isometrically into a closed subspace of the quasi-Banach space
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
.

We remark that using the above definition for
L_{p}
where
0<p<\infty
, we recover the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
as defined in
[
37
]
. However, the case
p=\infty
is not covered by the above description since it requires separability. We define
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
as the collection of martingales in
\mathcal{M}
for which the column square functions exists in
\mathcal{M}
. The norm in
\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is defined by:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{c}}=\big\|S_{c}(x)\big\|_{\infty},\quad x\in\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

In the sequel, we will also make use of the more general space
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
which is defined as the set of all sequences
a=(a_{k})
in
E(\mathcal{M})
for which
\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)
exists in
E(\mathcal{M})
. In this case, we set

\big\|a\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Under the above quasi-norm, one can easily see that
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is a quasi-Banach space.
The closed subspace of
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
consisting of adapted sequences will be denoted by
E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. That is,

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\Big\{(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}):\forall n\geq 1,a_{n}\in E(\mathcal{M}_{n})\Big\}.

Note that for
1<p<\infty
, it follows from the noncommutative Stein inequality that
L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is a complemented subspace of
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. One should not expect such complementation if one merely assumes that
E
is a quasi-Banach symmetric function space.

We will now discuss conditioned versions of the spaces defined earlier. We should remind the reader that the conditioned situation is more delicate. The main technical difficulty one encounters in defining conditioned spaces lies in the ability of successfully defining conditioned square functions. When dealing with spaces that are not linear subset of
L_{2}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
, some type of approximations are needed.

Consider the linear space
\mathcal{FS}
consisting of all
x\in\mathcal{M}
such that there exists a projection
e\in\mathcal{M}_{1}
,
\tau(e)<\infty
, and
x=exe
. We should note that if
\mathcal{M}
is finite, then
\mathcal{FS}=\mathcal{M}
.
Let
n\geq 1
and
0<p\leq\infty
. For
x\in\mathcal{FS}
, we set

\big\|x\big\|_{L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})}=\big\|\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)\big\|_{p/2}^{1/2}.

We should emphasize here that if
x=exe\in\mathcal{FS}
is as described above, then
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)=e\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)e
is a well-defined operator in
\mathcal{M}
and since
\tau(e)<\infty
, it follows that
\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}x)\in L_{p/2}(\mathcal{M})
so the quasi-norm described above is well defined. For
0<p\leq\infty
,
we define the space
L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})
to be the completion of
\mathcal{FS}
with respect
to the above quasi-norm.

According to
[
20
]
, for every
0<p\leq\infty
, there exists an isometric right
\mathcal{M}_{n}
-module map
u_{n,p}:L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{E}_{n})\longrightarrow L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n};\ell_{2}^{c})
such that

(2.4)

u_{n,p}(x)^{*}u_{n,q}(y)=\mathcal{E}_{n}(x^{*}y)\otimes e_{1,1},

whenever
x\in L_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{n})
,
y\in L_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M};\mathcal{E}_{n})
, and
1/p+1/q\leq 1
.

An important fact about these maps is that they are independent of
p
as the index
p
in the presentation of
[
20
]
was only needed to accommodate the non-tracial case. Below, we will simply use
u_{n}
for
u_{n,p}
.

Let
\mathfrak{F}
be the collection of all finite sequences
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{FS}
. For
0<p\leq\infty
, we defined the
conditioned space

L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
to be the completion of the linear space
\mathfrak{F}
with respect to the quasi-norm:

(2.5)

\big\|a\big\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|\sigma_{c}(a)\big\|_{p}

A fact from

[
20
]
that is very crucial in the sequel is that
L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
can be isometrically embedded into an
L_{p}
-space associated to a semifinite von Neumann algebra by means of the following map:

U:L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))

defined by setting:

U((a_{n})_{n\geq 1})=\sum_{n\geq 1}u_{n-1}(a_{n})\otimes e_{n,1},\quad(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}.

The range of
U
may be viewed as a double indexed sequences
(x_{n,k})
such that
x_{n,k}\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M}_{n})
for all
k\geq 1
. As an operator affiliated with
\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
, this may be expressed as

\sum_{n,k}x_{n,k}\otimes e_{k,1}\otimes e_{n,1}
.
It is immediate from (
2.4
)
that if
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
and
(b_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
, then

(2.6)

U((a_{n}))^{*}U((b_{n}))=\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(a_{n}^{*}b_{n})\big)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}.

In particular, if
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
then
\|(a_{n})\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\|U((a_{n}))\|_{p}
and
hence
U
is indeed an isometry.

Now, we generalize the notion of conditioned spaces to the setting of symmetric spaces of measurable operators. This is done in two steps.

\bullet
First, we assume that
E
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space satisfying
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}\subseteq E\subseteq L_{p}+L_{\infty}
for some
0<p<\infty
and
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
. This is the case for instance when
E
is a separable fully symmetric quasi-Banach function space. For a given
sequence
a=(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in\mathfrak{F}
, we set:

\big\|(a_{n})\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|\sigma_{c}(a)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\big\|U((a_{n}))\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

This is well-defined and induces a quasi-norm on the linear space
\mathfrak{F}
. We define the quasi-Banach space
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
to be the completion of the quasi normed space
(\mathfrak{F},\|\cdot\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})})
. The space
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
will be called
the column conditioned space associated with
E
.
It is clear that

U
extends to an isometry from
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
into
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
which we will still denote by
U
.

\bullet
Assume now that
E\subseteq L_{p}+L_{q}
for some
0<p,q<\infty
that is not necessarily separable. Since
L_{p}+L_{q}
is separable, we can define
(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
using the previous case. We set

E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=\Big\{x\in(L_{p}+L_{q})^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}):U(x)\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))\Big\}

equipped with the quasi-norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|U(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

We refer to
[
41
]
for the fact that
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is a quasi-Banach space and
U
is an isometry from
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
into
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
.
Moreover,
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
is independent of the specific choice of
p
and
q
and if
E
is separable then
the above definition coincides with the one described in the previous case.

We now recall the construction of column conditioned martingale Hardy spaces.
As in the conditioned spaces, we describe the noncommutative conditioned Hardy spaces in steps. Let
\mathfrak{F}(M)
be the collection of all finite martingale
(x_{n})_{1\leq n\leq N}
for which
x_{N}\in\mathcal{FS}
.

\bullet
First, assume that
E\subseteq L_{2}+L_{\infty}
. In this case, column conditioned square functions are well-defined for bounded martingales in
E(\mathcal{M})
. We define

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
to be the collection of all bounded martingale
x
in
E(\mathcal{M})
for which
s_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M})
. We equip
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with the norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

One can easily verify that
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
is complete. In particular,

\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is defined in this fashion for
2\leq p\leq\infty
.

\bullet
Next, we consider quasi-Banach space
E
such that
L_{p}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
for some
0<p<\infty
. This is the case if
E
is separable.
Let
x\in\mathfrak{F}(M)
. As noted above,
s_{c}(x)\in L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathcal{M}
. In particular,
s_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M})
. We equip
\mathfrak{F}(M)
with the quasi-norm

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|s_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}=\big\|(dx_{n})\big\|_{E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}.

The column conditioned Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is the completion of
(\mathfrak{F}(M),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
. Clearly, the map
x\mapsto(dx_{n})
(from
\mathfrak{F}(M)
into

\mathfrak{F}
) extends to be an isometry from
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
into
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
which we denote by
\mathcal{D}_{c}
. In particular,
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is isometrically isomorphic to a subspace of
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
via the isometry
U\mathcal{D}_{c}
. This case provides in particular the formal definition of
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
for
0<p<2
or more generally
\mathsf{h}_{L_{p}+L_{q}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
for
0<p<q<\infty
.

We should note here that if
L_{2}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
and
E\subseteq L_{2}+L_{\infty}
, then the two definitions provide the same space.

\bullet
Assume now that
E\subset L_{q}+L_{q}
for
0<p<q<\infty
. As in the case of conditioned spaces, we set

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\Big\{x\in\mathsf{h}_{L_{p}+L_{q}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}):U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)\in E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))\Big\}

equipped with the quasi-norm:

\big\|x\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}=\big\|U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}.

Since the operator
U
is independent of the index, one can easily see that the space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is independent of the specific choice of
p
and
q
. Moreover, one can verify as in the case of conditioned spaces that the quasi-normed space
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}})
is complete. Furthermore, if
E
is such that
L_{2}\cap L_{\infty}
is dense in
E
then
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
coincides with the one defined through completion considered in the second bullet. We refer to
[
41
,
44
]
for more details.

In the sequel, noncommutative column Hardy spaces associated with the Lorentz space
L_{p,q}
will be denoted by
\mathcal{H}_{p,q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{h}_{p,q}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
.

For convenience, we record below essential properties of
\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
,
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, and
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
that are relevant for our purpose.

Proposition 2.10
.

Assume that
E
be a symmetric Banach function space so that
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
for
0<p<q<\infty
. Then:

(i)

U:E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
is an isometric embedding.

(ii)

U\mathcal{D}_{c}:\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\to E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
is an isometric embedding.

Moreover, if
1<p<q<\infty
, then

(iii)

\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
are complemented in
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
;

(iv)

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
is complemented in
E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
.

Similarly,
U:L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
and
U\mathcal{D}_{c}:\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\to\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
are isometric embeddings.

All definitions and statements above admit corresponding row versions by passing to adjoints. For instance, the row square function of a martingale
x
is defined as
S_{r}(x)=S_{c}(x^{*})
, and the row Hardy space
\mathcal{H}_{p}^{r}(\mathcal{M})
consists of all martingales
x
such that
x^{*}\in\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
.

A third type of Hardy spaces that we will use in the sequel are the diagonal Hardy spaces. For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, we recall that the diagonal Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{d}(\mathcal{M})
is the subspace of
\ell_{p}(L_{p}(\mathcal{M}))
consisting of martingale difference sequences. This definition can be easily extended to the case of symmetric spaces by setting
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})
as the space of all martingales whose martingale difference sequences belong to
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})
, equipped with the norm
\|x\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}}:=\|(dx_{n})\|_{E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})}
. We will denote by
\mathcal{D}_{d}
the isometric embedding of
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}
into
E(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\ell_{\infty})
given by
x\mapsto(dx_{n})_{n\geq 1}
.

We may also defined the mixed Hardy spaces by setting for
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{2})

with
0<p<2
,

\mathcal{H}_{E}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{H}_{E}^{r}(\mathcal{M})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathsf{h}_{E}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{r}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M}),

while for
F\in{\rm Int}(L_{2},L_{q})

with
2<q\leq\infty
,

\mathcal{H}_{F}(\mathcal{M})=\mathcal{H}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathcal{H}_{F}^{r}(\mathcal{M})\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathsf{h}_{F}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathsf{h}_{F}^{r}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathsf{h}_{F}^{d}(\mathcal{M}).

3.
Interpolations of martingale Hardy spaces: the real method

3.1.
Real interpolations of the couple
\bf{\big(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c},\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}\big)}
for
{\bf 0<p<\infty}

In this subsection, we prove the primary result of the paper. A version of P. Jones’s theorem for noncommutative column conditioned martingale Hardy spaces formulated here in the form of
K
-closed couple.

Theorem 3.1
.

For a given
0<p<\infty
, the compatible couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in
the couple
(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}))
where
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
.

Recall that
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
embed isometrically into
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
and
\mathcal{N}
respectively. Thus, we may view
\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
(resp.
\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
) as a subspace of
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
(resp.
\mathcal{N}
). Moreover, all spaces embed continuously into the topological vector space
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
, the space of all
\widetilde{\tau}
-measurable operators affiliated with the von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{N}
(here
\widetilde{\tau}
denotes the natural trace on
\mathcal{N}
). In particular,
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is a compatible couple which is a subcouple of
(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})
.

The main step in the proof of the theorem is for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
. The following constitutes the decisive ingredient in our argument.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
so that for every
x\in\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
t>0
, the following inequality holds:

K\big(x,t;\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\big(\mu_{u}(s_{c}(x))\big)^{2}\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
t>0
. Fix
\varepsilon>0
and set

\lambda=\frac{2+\varepsilon}{t}\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}(\mu_{u}(s_{c}(x)))^{2}\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

The two step construction used below is modeled after a similar decomposition considered in
[
6
]
which was inspired by an idea initially used in
[
34
]
. It is primarily based on the use of the so-called Cuculescu’s projections (
[
7
]
). Submajorizations also play prominent role in the argument.

\bullet
Step 1. (Initial construction)

We apply the construction of Cuculescu’s projections to the submartingale
(s_{c,k}^{2}(x))_{k\geq 1}
and the parameter
\lambda^{2}
. That is, we start with
q_{0}={\bf 1}
and for
k\geq 1
, we set

q_{k}:=q_{k-1}\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{[0,\lambda^{2}]}\big(q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}\big)=\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{[0,\lambda^{2}]}\big(q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}\big)q_{k-1}.

Then
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a decreasing sequence of projections in
\mathcal{M}
satisfying the following properties:

(1)

q_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-1}
for every
k\geq 1
;

(2)

q_{k}
commutes with
q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(3)

q_{k}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k}\leq\lambda^{2}q_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(4)

if we set
q=\bigwedge_{k\geq 1}q_{k}
, then

\lambda^{2}({\bf 1}-q)\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})s_{c,k}^{2}(x)(q_{k-1}-q_{k})
.

These facts on Cuculescu’s projections are now standard. Verifications can be found in
[
6
,
34
,
40
]
.

We consider the following two martingale difference sequences: for
k\geq 1
,

(3.1)

d\alpha_{k}=dx_{k}q_{k}\ \ \text{and}\ \ d\beta_{k}=dx_{k}q_{k-1}.

We denote the corresponding martingales by
\alpha=(\alpha_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and
\beta=(\beta_{k})_{k\geq 1}
respectively. We only use
\alpha
in the construction below but
\beta
will be needed in order to deduce some properties of
\alpha
. We record the next lemma for further use.

Lemma 3.3
.

The martingales
\alpha
and
\beta
satisfy the following properties:

\rm(i)

\|\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|d\alpha_{k}|^{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda^{2}
for all
k\geq 1
;

\rm(ii)

s_{c}^{2}(\beta)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(x)
;

\rm(iii)

s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)\prec\prec 4s_{c}^{2}(x)
.

Proof.

The first item can be deduced as follows: from (
3.1
), we have for
k\geq 1
,

\begin{split}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|d\alpha_{k}|^{2})&=\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(q_{k}|dx_{k}|^{2}q_{k})\\
&=q_{k}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|dx_{k}|^{2})q_{k}\\
&=q_{k}[s_{c,k}^{2}(x)-s_{c,k-1}^{2}(x)]q_{k}\\
&\leq q_{k}s^{2}_{c,k}(x)q_{k}\leq\lambda^{2}q_{k}\end{split}

where in the second identity, we use the fact that
(q_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a predictable sequence. The estimate clearly implies item (i).

For the second item, we have from the definition of
\beta
that for
m\geq 1
(with
s_{c,0}(x)=0
),

\displaystyle s_{c,m}^{2}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}q_{k-1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|dx_{k}|^{2})q_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}q_{k-1}[s_{c,k}^{2}(x)-s_{c,k-1}^{2}(x)]q_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{m}q_{k-1}s_{c,k-1}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}.

Performing some indexing shift, we obtain that

s_{c,m}^{2}(\beta)=q_{m-1}s_{c,m}^{2}(x)q_{m-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\big(q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}-q_{k}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k}\big).

From the fact that
q_{k}
commutes with
q_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(x)q_{k-1}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle s_{c,m}^{2}(\beta)

\displaystyle=q_{m-1}s_{c,m}^{2}(x)q_{m-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})s_{c,k}^{2}(x)(q_{k-1}-q_{k})

\displaystyle\leq q_{m-1}s_{c}^{2}(x)q_{m-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})s_{c}^{2}(x)(q_{k-1}-q_{k}).

Note that the finite family of projections
\{q_{k-1}-q_{k}:1\leq k\leq m-1\}\cup\{q_{m-1}\}
is mutually disjoint. We may deduce from (
2.2
) that for every
m\geq 1
,
s_{c,m}^{2}(\beta)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(x)
. We arrive at the desired conclusion by noticing that for every
w>0
, the monotone convergence theorem gives:

\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\beta))\ du=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c,m}^{2}(\beta))\ du\leq\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du.

That is,
s_{c}^{2}(\beta)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(x)
.

For the last item, we note first that a straightforward computation gives:

\displaystyle s_{c}^{2}(\beta-\alpha)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})[s_{c,k}^{2}(x)-s_{c,k-1}^{2}(x)](q_{k-1}-q_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}(q_{k-1}-q_{k})s_{c}^{2}(x)(q_{k-1}-q_{k})

and therefore we have from (
2.2
) that
s_{c}^{2}(\beta-\alpha)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(x)
. Using the elementary inequality
|a+b|^{2}\leq 2|a|^{2}+2|b|^{2}
for operators
a
and
b
, we can then conclude from (
2.1
) and the second item that

s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)\leq 2s_{c}^{2}(\beta-\alpha)+2s_{c}^{2}(\beta)\prec\prec 4s_{c}^{2}(x).

The lemma is verified.
∎

\bullet
Step 2. The decomposition that gives the desired estimate on the
K
-functional.

First, we apply the construction of Cuculescu’s projections to the submartingale
(s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha))_{k\geq 1}
and the parameter
\lambda^{2}
where
\alpha
is the martingale from (
3.1
).
That is, setting
\pi_{0}={\bf 1}
and for
k\geq 1
, we define:

\pi_{k}:=\pi_{k-1}\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{[0,\lambda^{2}]}\big(\pi_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}\big)=\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{[0,\lambda^{2}]}\big(\pi_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}\big)\pi_{k-1}.

Then
(\pi_{k})_{k\geq 1}
is a decreasing sequence of projections in
\mathcal{M}
. As before, it satisfies the following properties:

(1)

\pi_{k}\in\mathcal{M}_{k-1}
for every
k\geq 1
;

(2)

\pi_{k}
commutes with
\pi_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(3)

\pi_{k}s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{k}\leq\lambda^{2}\pi_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
;

(4)

if we set
\pi=\bigwedge_{k\geq 1}\pi_{k}
, then

\lambda^{2}({\bf 1}-\pi)\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})
.

Next, we define two martingales
y
and
z
by setting:

(3.2)

z=\sum_{k\geq 1}d\alpha_{k}\pi_{k-1}=\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}q_{k}\pi_{k-1}\ \ \text{and}\ \ y=\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}({\bf 1}-q_{k}\pi_{k-1}).

Clearly, we have the decomposition:

x=y+z.

We will show that this decomposition provides the desired estimate on the
K
-functional.
We consider first the martingale
z
. We claim that
z\in\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
with

(3.3)

\big\|z\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}}\leq\sqrt{2}\lambda.

To verify (
3.3
). Fix
m\geq 1
. From the definition of
z
, we have:

\displaystyle s_{c,m}^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}[\pi_{k-1}|d\alpha_{k}|^{2}\pi_{k-1}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\big(\pi_{k-1}s^{2}_{c,k}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k-1}s^{2}_{c,k-1}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}\pi_{k-1}s^{2}_{c,k}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\pi_{k}s^{2}_{c,k}(\alpha)\pi_{k}

\displaystyle=\pi_{m-1}s_{c,m}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{m-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})s^{2}_{c,k}(\alpha)(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k}\big)\,,

where the last equality follows from the commutativity between
\pi_{k}
and
\pi_{k-1}s^{2}_{c,k}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}
. Recall from Lemma
3.3
(i) that
\|\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|d\alpha_{k}|^{2})\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda^{2}
. Using this fact, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{k-1}s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\pi_{k-1}

\displaystyle=\pi_{k-1}[s_{c,k-1}^{2}(\alpha)+\mathcal{E}_{k-1}(|d\alpha_{k}|^{2})]\pi_{k-1}

\displaystyle\leq 2\lambda^{2}\pi_{k-1}.

Applying this inequality with the previous estimate, we obtain that for every
m\geq 1
,

s_{c,m}^{2}(z)\leq 2\lambda^{2}\pi_{m-1}+2\lambda^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})\leq 2\lambda^{2}{\bf 1}.

Since this holds for arbitrary
m\geq 1
, we have
s_{c}^{2}(z)\leq 2\lambda^{2}{\bf 1}

which shows that
\big\|z\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}}\leq\sqrt{2}\lambda
and thus proving the claim.

We now deal with the martingale
y
. We will estimate the norm of
y
in
\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. The following lemma is the most crucial part of the argument.

Lemma 3.4
.

The projections
q
and
\pi
satisfy the following property:

\max\big\{\tau({\bf 1}-q),\tau({\bf 1}-\pi)\big\}\leq t^{2}.

Proof.

We will only verify that
\tau({\bf 1}-\pi)\leq t^{2}
. The argument for
\tau({\bf 1}-q)
is identical so we will leave the details to the reader.

Fix
w>t^{2}
. We claim that
\mu_{w}({\bf 1}-\pi)=0
. Assume the opposite, i.e,
\mu_{w}({\bf 1}-\pi)=1
.
We start with the fact that

(3.4)

\lambda^{2}({\bf 1}-\pi)\leq\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k}).

Taking generalized singular values and integrals, inequality (
3.4
) gives

\lambda^{2}\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}({\bf 1}-\pi)\ du\leq\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)(\pi_{k-1}-\pi_{k})\big)\ du.

By submajorization and the fact that
\mu({\bf 1}-\pi)
is a characteristic function and therefore is identically equal to
1
on the interval
[0,w]
by assumption, we have,

\lambda^{2}w\leq\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha))\ du\leq 4\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du

where the second inequality comes from Lemma
3.3
(iii). Using the specific value of
\lambda
, this leads to

\displaystyle(2+\varepsilon)^{2}w\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{2}\int_{0}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du+4t^{2}\int_{t^{2}}^{w}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 4t^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du+4t^{2}(w-t^{2})\mu_{t^{2}}(s_{c}^{2}(x))

\displaystyle\leq(2+\varepsilon)^{2}t^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du+4t^{2}(w-t^{2})\mu_{t^{2}}(s_{c}^{2}(x)).

After rearrangement and division by
w-t^{2}
, we arrive at

(2+\varepsilon)^{2}\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du\leq 4t^{2}\mu_{t^{2}}(s_{c}^{2}(x)).

Since
\mu(s_{c}^{2}(x))
is decreasing, the left hand side of the preceding inequality is larger than
(2+\varepsilon)^{2}t^{2}\mu_{t^{2}}(s_{c}^{2}(x))
which is a contradiction. Thus, we must have

\mu_{w}({\bf 1}-\pi)=0
. This shows that
\tau({\bf 1}-\pi)\leq t^{2}
.
∎

Now we can estimate the norm of
y
as follows:
first, we split
y
into two parts:

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}({\bf 1}-q_{k}\pi_{k-1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 1}dx_{k}({\bf 1}-q_{k})+\sum_{k\geq 1}d\alpha_{k}({\bf 1}-\pi_{k-1})

\displaystyle=(x-\alpha)+(\alpha-z).

Recall that the martingale
z
is such that
dz_{k}=d\alpha_{k}\pi_{k-1}
for
k\geq 1
. We will use below that
s_{c}^{2}(z)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)
. The verification of this fact is identical to the martingale
\beta
in Lemma
3.3
(ii) so we omit the details.

We now proceed with the estimation of the norm:

\displaystyle\big\|y\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq\big\|x-\alpha\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}+\big\|\alpha-z\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}

\displaystyle=\big\|s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha)\big\|_{1}^{1/2}+\big\|s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z)\big\|_{1}^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha))\ du\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z))\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

The important fact here is that the operator
s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha)
(resp.
s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z)
) is supported by
the projection
({\bf 1}-q)
(resp.
({\bf 1}-\pi)
). By properties of generalized singular values and Lemma
3.4
, we have
\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha))=\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z))=0
for every
u>t^{2}
. Therefore, the last estimate can be refined as:

\big\|y\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}\leq\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha))\ du\Big)^{1/2}+\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z))\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

We estimate the two integrals on the right hand side separately. Using the elementary inequality
s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha)\leq 2s_{c}^{2}(x)+2s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)
and the submajorization stated in Lemma
3.3
(iii), we have:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x-\alpha))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du+2\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 10\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du.

Similarly, the second integral can be estimated as follows:

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha-z))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha))\ du+2\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(z))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(\alpha))\ du

\displaystyle\leq 16\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du,

where in the second inequality we have used the observation stated earlier that
s_{c}^{2}(z)\prec\prec s_{c}^{2}(\alpha)
.
Combining the estimates on the two integrals above, we get

(3.5)

\big\|y\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}\leq(\sqrt{10}+4)\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

We can now estimate the
K
-functional using the decomposition
x=y+z
. Indeed, by
combining (
3.3
) and (
3.5
), we have

\displaystyle K\big(x,t;\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)

\displaystyle\leq\big\|y\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}}+t\big\|z\big\|_{\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}}

\displaystyle\leq(\sqrt{10}+4+2\sqrt{2}+\sqrt{2}\varepsilon)\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we may conclude that

K\big(x,t;\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)\leq(\sqrt{10}+4+2\sqrt{2})\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\mu_{u}(s_{c}^{2}(x))\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

The proof of Proposition
3.2
is complete.
∎

We make the following remarks about the proof of Proposition
3.2
. First, we would like to point out that in order to obtain that
z\in\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, it is important that the sequences
\{s_{c,k}^{2}(x)\}_{k\geq 1}
and

\{s_{c,k}^{2}(\alpha)\}_{k\geq 1}
are used in constructing the Cuculescu projections.
Using any exponent strictly less than
2
is not sufficient for this goal. Second,
since submajorization is one of the main tool we use, it is also essential that the operator
s_{c}^{2}(x)\in L_{1}(\mathcal{M})+\mathcal{M}
.
Therefore, our argument cannot be carried out for the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
when
0<p<2
. This is in strong contrast with the stopping time and
atomic decompositions approach used for classical martingales as demonstrated in
[
50
, Theorem 5.9]
where actually the case
0<p\leq 1
was given.

We now proceed toward the proof of Theorem
3.1
. We recall that for
f\in L_{2}+L_{\infty}
and
t>0
, we have the equivalence:

K(f,t;L_{2},L_{\infty})\approx\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}(\mu_{u}(f))^{2}\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

We refer to
[
12
]
for this fact. As is well-known, the above equivalence extends to the corresponding couple of noncommutative spaces (see
[
38
]
). We note from (
2.6
)
that for
x\in\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
, we have
|U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)|=s_{c}(x)\otimes e_{1,1}\otimes e_{1,1}
. Thus, Proposition
3.2
can be restated in the following form: there exists a constant
C>0
so that for every
x\in\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
t>0
,

K\big(x,t;\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)\leq CK\big(U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x),t;L_{2}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}\big).

Consequently, Proposition
3.2
is equivalent to the following intermediate statement:

Remark 3.5
.

The couple
(\mathsf{h}_{2}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in
the couple
(L_{2}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})
.

As an immediate application of the preceding remark and (
2.3
), we have the following interpolation result:

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
2\leq q<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
. For
1/r=(1-\theta)/q
and
0<\lambda,\gamma\leq\infty
,

\big(\mathsf{h}_{q,\lambda}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,\gamma}=\mathsf{h}_{r,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with equivalent norms.

On the other hand, the corresponding result for finite indices is already known for the full interval
(0,\infty)
.

Proposition 3.7

(
[
41
]
)
.

Let
0<p,q<\infty
and
0<\theta<1
. For
1/r=(1-\theta)/p+\theta/q
and
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma\leq\infty
,

\big(\mathsf{h}_{p,\gamma_{1}}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q,\gamma_{2}}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,\gamma}=\mathsf{h}_{r,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with equivalent quasi-norms.

Combining Corollary
3.6
and Proposition
3.7
, we may deduce from
[
41
, Lemma 3.4]
that the family
\{\mathsf{h}_{p,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\}_{p\in(0,\infty],\gamma\in(0,\infty]}
forms a real interpolation scale on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
. The next statement should be compared with
[
41
, Theorem 3.5]
where
\mathsf{bmo}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
was used as one of the endpoints. Although it is implied by Theorem
3.1
, it is needed for the proof. We explicitly state it here for convenience.

Proposition 3.8
.

If
0<\theta<1
and
0<p<\infty
, then for
1/r=(1-\theta)/p
and
0<\lambda,\gamma\leq\infty
,

\big(\mathsf{h}_{p,\lambda}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)_{\theta,\gamma}=\mathsf{h}_{r,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})

with equivalent quasi-norms.

Let us now return to more results on
K
-closedness that we will need for the proof of Theorem
3.1
.
We recall that under a more restrictive conditions on the indices, a
K
-closedness result was already proved in
[
41
, Corollary 3.8, Remark 3.9]
. It can be stated as follows:

Lemma 3.9

(
[
41
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a integer with
\nu\geq 2
. Assume that
2/(\nu+1)<p\leq 2/\nu
and

p<q<2/(\nu-1)
. Then, the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple
(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N}))
.

The last piece we need can be easily deduced from the complementation property recorded in Proposition
2.10
:

Lemma 3.10
.

If
1<p<q<\infty
, then the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{q}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple
(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),L_{q}(\mathcal{N}))
.

End of the proof of Theorem
3.1
.

The argument consists of mixing the three different intervals stated in Remark
3.5
, Lemma
3.9
, and Lemma
3.10
.

For
0<p\leq\infty
and
0<\gamma\leq\infty
, let

X_{p,\gamma}:=L_{p,\gamma}(\mathcal{N})\ \ \text{and}\ \ Y_{p,\gamma}:=\mathsf{h}_{p,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

Here
L_{\infty,\gamma}(\mathcal{N})
(resp.
\mathsf{h}_{\infty,\gamma}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
) is simply
\mathcal{N}
(resp.
\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
).

Then, we may view
Y_{p,\gamma}
as a subspace of
X_{p,\gamma}
by the isometric embedding detailed in Subsection
2.3
.
We have from (
2.3
) that the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
forms a real interpolation scale. Similarly, we also have from Proposition
3.8
that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
forms a real interpolation scale. Thus, the assumptions of Proposition
2.8
are satisfied by the two families.

Consider the sequence of intervals
(I_{\nu})_{\nu\geq 0}
with

I_{0}=(2,\infty]
,

I_{1}=(1,\infty)
, and
for
\nu\geq 2
,

I_{\nu}=(\frac{2}{\nu+1},\frac{2}{\nu-1}).

By Remark
3.5
and Holmstedt formulae (see
[
12
, Theorem 2.1, Remark 2.1]
), the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
is
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
on the interval
I_{0}
. Also, Lemma
3.10
gives that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
in
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
on the interval
I_{1}
.

Next, for a given
\nu\geq 2
, it follows from Lemma
3.9
and
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
is
K
-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
on the interval
I_{\nu}
.

We note that
I_{0}\cap I_{1}=(2,\infty)
,

I_{1}\cap I_{2}=(1,2)
, and for
\nu\geq 2
,
I_{\nu}\cap I_{\nu+1}=(2/(\nu+1),2/\nu]
. In particular, for
\upsilon\geq 0
,
|I_{\upsilon}\cap I_{\upsilon+1}|>1
.

By applying Proposition
2.8
inductively, we deduce that the family
\{Y_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
is

-closed in the family
\{X_{p,\gamma}\}_{p,\gamma}
on the interval

which is the desired conclusion.
∎

.

The proof of Theorem

.

Remark 3.12
.

Assume that

. Theorem
3.1
can be reformulated as follows:
If

is a finite martingale in
\mathfrak{F}(M)
then for every

by density.

for the couple

3.2.
Generalization to the couple
\bf{\big(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c},\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}\big)}

In this subsection, we will extend Theorem
3.1
to more general couples. More precisely, noncommutative column conditioned Hardy spaces associated with general function spaces are used. It reads as follows:

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
0<p<q<\infty
and
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
.
Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm. If
E=(L_{p},L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{F};K}
, then the couple
(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple
(E(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})
.

Before we proceed, we remark that the assumption
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
where
0<p<q<\infty
is needed so that the Hardy space
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
can be defined as described in the preliminary section. The assumption implies a fortiori that
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{\infty})
and therefore the existence of the quasi-Banach function space
\mathcal{F}
is given by Proposition
2.3
.

The proof is based on the following more general form of Holmstedt formula for
K
-functionals. It will allow us to consider a wider class of function spaces beyond classical Lorentz spaces.

Proposition 3.14

(
[
1
]
)
.

Let
(A_{0},A_{1})
be a compatible couple of quasi-Banach spaces and
\mathcal{F}
be a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm. If
X=(A_{0},A_{1})_{\mathcal{F};K}
and
a\in X+A_{1}
, then for every
t>0
,

K(a,\rho(t);X,A_{1})\approx I(t,a)+\frac{\rho(t)}{t}K(a,t;A_{0},A_{1}),

where
I(t,a)=\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t)}(\cdot)K(a,\cdot\,;A_{0},A_{1})\|_{\mathcal{F}}
and

\rho(t)\approx t\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(t,\infty)}(\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{F}}+\|u\mapsto u\chi_{(0,t)}(u)\|_{\mathcal{F}}
.

We remark that some version of Proposition
3.14
already appeared in
[
30
]
for Banach spaces. We also note that Proposition
3.14
provides an alternative proof of the following fact: if
E=(L_{p},L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{F};K}
and
x\in E(\mathcal{N})+\mathcal{N}
then for every
t>0
,

(3.6)

K\big(x,t;E(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}\big)\approx_{E}K\big(\mu(x),t;E,L_{\infty}\big).

This equivalence is well-known for couples of noncommutative
L_{p}
-spaces (see
[
38
, Corollary 2.3]
and the remark immediately after). For the case where
E
is a Banach space, it is a consequence of a result on partial retract (see
[
38
, Corollary 2.2]
).

Proof of Theorem
3.13
.

Let
x\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
and
t>0
.
Set

I_{1}(t,x)=\big\|u\mapsto\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t)}(u)K(x,u;\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}

and

I_{2}(t,U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x))=\big\|u\mapsto\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t)}(u)K(U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x),u;L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})\big\|_{\mathcal{F}}.

Since
\mathcal{F}
has monotone quasi-norm, it follows from Theorem
3.1
that:

(3.7)

I_{1}(t,x)\approx I_{2}(t,U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x)).

Using (
3.7
) with Theorem
3.1
and Proposition
3.14
,
we may deduce that

\displaystyle K\big(x,\rho(t),\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)

\displaystyle\approx I_{1}(t,x)+\frac{\rho(t)}{t}K\big(x,t;\mathsf{h}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\big)

\displaystyle\approx I_{2}(t,U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x))+\frac{\rho(t)}{t}K\big(U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}\big)

\displaystyle\approx K\big(U\mathcal{D}_{c}(x),\rho(t),E(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}\big).

To conclude the proof, we will verify that the range of the function
\rho(\cdot)
is
[0,\infty)
. Indeed, one can easily see that

\rho(\cdot)
is continuous and
\rho(0)=0
. Moreover, for
t>0
, we have from Holmstedt’s formula that

K(\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t]},u;L_{p},L_{\infty})\approx t\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(t,\infty)}(u)+u\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t]}(u)
. It follows from the representation of
E
and quasi-triangle inequality on
\mathcal{F}
that

\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,t]}\|_{E}\lesssim\rho(t)
. On the other hand, by assumption,
E
is
r
-concave for some
r<\infty
. This implies that for every
n\geq 2
,

\big(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(k-1,k]}\|_{E}^{r}\big)^{1/r}\lesssim\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,n]}\|_{E}.

Since
E
is symmetric, the left hand side is equal to
n^{1/r}\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{(0,1]}\|_{E}
. We deduce that for every
t>1
,

(t-1)^{1/r}\|\raise 2.15277pt\hbox{$\chi$}_{0,1]}\|_{E}\lesssim\rho(t).

This clearly implies that

\lim_{t\to\infty}\rho(t)=\infty
. With this fact, the preceding equivalence of
K
-functionals is precisely the needed
K
-closedness.
∎

As an application of Theorem
3.13
, we have the following general scheme of lifting interpolation identities from a given couple of symmetric quasi-Banach function spaces to the corresponding couple of noncommutative conditioned column Hardy spaces. This appears to be new even for classical martingale Hardy spaces.

Corollary 3.15
.

Let
0<p<q<\infty
,
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
, and

\mathcal{G}
be a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm.
If
F=(E,L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{G};K}
and
F
is
r
-concave for some
r<\infty
, then

\mathsf{h}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))_{\mathcal{G};K}

with equivalent quasi-norms.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.3
, there is a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm
\mathcal{F}
so that
E=(L_{p},L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{F};K}
. Then by Theorem
3.13
, the couple

(\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathsf{h}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple
(E(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})
. On the other hand, we have from the assumption and (
3.6
) that
F(\mathcal{N})=(E(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N})_{\mathcal{G};K}
. The conclusion follows immediately from
K
-closedness.
∎

Next, we will point out that the ideas used for couples of noncommutative martingale Hardy spaces can be adapted to related compatible couples. Namely, we will consider couples of conditioned
L_{p}
-spaces and
couples of spaces of adapted sequences.

We consider first the couple of conditioned spaces
(L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M},\ell_{2}^{c}))
. We have a corresponding result to Proposition
3.2
.

Lemma 3.16
.

There exists a constant
C>0
so that for every
a\in L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
t>0
, the following inequality holds:

K\big(a,t;L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\big(\mu_{u}(\sigma_{c}(a))\big)^{2}\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

This can be verified by repeating the proof of Proposition
3.2
but using the sequence
(\sigma_{c,k}^{2}(a))_{k\geq 1}
in place of
(s_{c,k}^{2}(x))_{k\geq 1}
in the first step. We omit the details.

With Lemma
3.16
on hand, we can repeat mutatis mutandis the series of arguments leading up to the proof of Theorem
3.1
by using
[
41
, Proposition 3.7]
in place of
[
41
, Corollary 3.8]
and the fact from
[
20
]
that as in the case of Hardy spaces,
L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M})
identifies as a complemented subspace of
L_{p}(\mathcal{N})
whenever
1<p<\infty
. We leave the details to the reader.

We state below what we consider the two most important results that we should retain from these adjustments. One is the
K
-closedness result and the other is the automatic liftings of interpolations.

Proposition 3.17
.

(i)

For a given
0<p<\infty
, the couple
\big(L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)
is
K
-closed in the couple
\big(L_{p}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{N}\big)
.

(ii)

Let
0<p<q<\infty
,
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{q})
, and

\mathcal{G}
be a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm.
If
F=(E,L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{G};K}
and
F
is
r
-concave for some
r<\infty
, then

F^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=(E^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))_{\mathcal{G};K}.

Similarly, by using square functions in place of conditioned square functions, the proof of Proposition
3.2
can be adjusted to prove the following corresponding result for couples of spaces of adapted sequences.

Lemma 3.18
.

There exists a constant
C>0
so that for every
a\in L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+L_{\infty}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
t>0
, the following inequality holds:

K\big(a,t;L_{2}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)\leq C\Big(\int_{0}^{t^{2}}\big(\mu_{u}({\mathcal{S}}_{c}(a))\big)^{2}\ du\Big)^{1/2}.

As in the case of conditioned spaces, we can rewrite all the arguments and results from the case of column conditioned Hardy spaces to couples of spaces of adapted sequences.
Consequently, we obtain a much more general result than
[
41
, Theorem 3.13]
which we can state as follows:

Proposition 3.19
.

(i)

For a given
0<p<\infty
, the couple
\big(L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)
is
K
-closed in the couple
\big(L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\big)
.

(ii)

Let
0<p<\infty
,
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{\infty})
, and

\mathcal{G}
be a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm.
If
F=(E,L_{\infty})_{\mathcal{G};K}
, then

F^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=(E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{\infty}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))_{\mathcal{G};K}.

As already known from
[
41
]
, the case of adapted sequences implies some corresponding results on Hardy spaces with natural restrictions on the indices.

Corollary 3.20
.

(i)

If
1<p<\infty
, then the couple
(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in
the couple
(L_{1}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))
.

(ii)

Let
1<p<\infty
,
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{p})
, and

\mathcal{G}
be a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm.
If
F=(E,L_{p})_{\mathcal{G};K}
, then

\mathcal{H}_{F}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=(\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))_{\mathcal{G};K}.

Proof.

This follows from combining the result on adapted sequence with the noncommutative Stein inequality (
[
37
]
) and the noncommutative Lépingle-Yor inequality (
[
39
]
). Indeed, for
1\leq r<\infty
we may identify
\mathcal{H}_{r}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
as a complemented subspace of
L_{r}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
via the projection
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}\mapsto\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}-\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(a_{n})
. Thus, by complementation, the couple
(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H}_{p}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is
K
-closed in the couple

(L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{p}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))
. It follows further that it is
K
-closed in the couple
(L_{1}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))
. The second part also follows from complementation.
∎

Contrary to its conditioned counterpart, the preceding corollary only applies to the Banach space range. We refer to
[
41
]
for more details on this fact. It is also important to note that we do not have the corresponding result when the couple
(\mathcal{H}_{1}^{c}(\mathcal{M}),\mathcal{H}_{\infty}^{c}(\mathcal{M}))
is used.

As an illustration of the usefulness of Corollary
3.20
and its row version, we may easily deduce that one side of the Burkholder-Gundy inequality (
[
22
, Corollary 4.3]
) extends to a class of symmetric spaces of measurable operators. More precisely,
if
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
then for every
x\in\mathcal{H}_{E}(\mathcal{M})
, the following holds:

(3.8)

\|x\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{E}\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}}.

As an example, consider the Zygmund space
L\operatorname{log}L
. Recall that

L\operatorname{log}L=\big\{f\in L_{0}:\int_{0}^{\infty}|f(t)|\operatorname{log}^{+}|f(t)|\ dt<\infty\big\}.

Equipped with the norm
\|f\|_{L\operatorname{log}L}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mu_{t}(f)\operatorname{log}(1/t)\ dt
,
L\operatorname{log}L
is a symmetric Banach space.
It can be seen from
[
23
]
that
L\operatorname{log}L\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
. Therefore, (
3.8
) applies to martingales in
L\operatorname{log}L(\mathcal{M})
. More results in the spirit of (
3.8
) will be explored in the appendix section below.

3.3.
Some concrete illustrations

Below, we consider two concrete examples on how our general result from the previous two subsections can be applied.

3.3.1.
Noncommutative Orlicz spaces

We will first review the basics of Orlicz spaces.

A function
\Phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called an
Orlicz function
whenever it is strictly increasing, continuous,
\Phi(0)=0

The

We recall that for

3.3.2.
Generalized Lorentz spaces

We now examine the class of generalized Lorentz spaces. Our motivation for considering such class comes from
[
46
]
where classical martingale Hardy spaces associated with generalized Lorentz spaces were studied.

Let
\varphi:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a locally integrable function. Given
0<r\leq\infty
, the
Lorentz space

\Lambda^{r}(\varphi)
is the linear subspace of all function
f\in L_{0}
for which
\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{r}(\varphi)}<\infty
where

\big\|f\big\|_{\Lambda^{r}(\varphi)}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mu_{t}(f)\varphi(t))^{r}\ \frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/r}},&0<r<\infty;\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{t>0}\mu_{t}(f)\varphi(t)},&r=\infty.\end{cases}

The space
(\Lambda^{r}(\varphi),\|\cdot\|_{\Lambda^{r}(\varphi)})
is a symmetric quasi-Banach function space. We should note that there are other equivalent formulations of the generalized Lorentz spaces but we chose to follow the presentation of
[
35
]
since many properties we use below are taken directly from
[
35
]
.

Let
0<a_{1}<a_{2}
. Following
[
35
]
, we say that a function
\varphi
belongs to the class
Q[a_{1},a_{2}]
if
t\mapsto t^{-a_{1}}\varphi(t)
is nondecreasing and
t\mapsto t^{-a_{2}}\varphi(t)
is nonincreasing.

For simplicity, we denote by
\mathsf{h}_{r,\varphi}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
the noncommutative column conditioned
Hardy space associated with the Lorentz space
\Lambda_{r}(\varphi)
.

We will now describe an interpolation method that is suitable for Lorentz spaces. For a given function
\varrho\in Q(0,1)
and
0<q\leq\infty
, consider the function space
\mathcal{F}_{\varrho,q}
defined by the set of all functions
f\in L_{0}
satisfying
\|f\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\varrho,q}}<\infty
where

\big\|f\big\|_{\mathcal{F}_{\varrho,q}}:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}(|f(t)|/\varrho(t))^{q}\ \frac{dt}{t}\Big)^{1/q}},&0<q<\infty;\\
\displaystyle{\sup_{t>0}|f(t)|/\varrho(t)},&q=\infty.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\mathcal{F}_{\varrho,q}
equipped with the above quasi-norm is a quasi-Banach function space with monotone quasi-norm. Following
[
35
]
, for a compatible couple
(A_{0},A_{1})
, we denote by
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\varrho,q}
the interpolation space given by
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\mathcal{F}_{\varrho,q};K}
. If
\varrho(t)=t^{\theta}
for
0<\theta<1
, then
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\varrho,q}
reduces to
(A_{0},A_{1})_{\theta,q}
.

Our starting point is the following interpolation involving Lorentz spaces.

Proposition 3.26

(
[
35
, Proposition 6.2]
)

where
\varphi_{0}(t)=\varphi(t)/\varrho(\varphi(t))
.

As an immediate consequence of Corollary
3.15
, we have:

.

If

for some

Next, we will show as in the case of Orlicz spaces that the same result holds if we use

as one the endpoints in the interpolation in place of

In this appendix section, we apply results from the previous section to martingale inequalities.

Before we proceed,
we record the following result for further use. We may view this as a
\Phi
-moment analogue of the

-moment inequalities.

Proposition A.1
.

Let
0<p<q<\infty
and
\Phi
be a
p
-convex and
q
-concave Orlicz function.
Assume that
f
and
g
are positive functions in
L_{0}
such that
g\in L_{\Phi}
and
for every
t>0
, the inequality
K(f,t;L_{p},L_{q})\leq K(g,t;L_{p},L_{q})
holds. Then

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(f(t))\ dt\lesssim_{p,q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(g(t))\ dt.

Proof.

Assume first that
\Phi_{0}
is an Orlicz function that is
p_{0}
-convex and
q_{0}
-concave for some
1\leq p_{0}<q_{0}<\infty
. Let
f_{0}
and
g_{0}
be positive functions in
L_{0}
such that
g_{0}\in L_{\Phi_{0}}
and
for every
t>0
, the inequality
K(f_{0},t;L_{p_{0}},L_{q_{0}})\leq K(g_{0},t;L_{p_{0}},L_{q_{0}})
holds.

According to
[
47
]
, the pair
(L_{p_{0}},L_{q_{0}})
is a Calderón couple. That is, there exists an operator
T:L_{p_{0}}+L_{q_{0}}\to L_{p_{0}}+L_{q_{0}}
with
T(L_{p_{0}})\subseteq L_{p_{0}}
,
T(L_{q_{0}})\subseteq L_{q_{0}}
, and such that
Tg_{0}=f_{0}
.
A closer inspection of
[
47
]
reveals that
\max\{\|T:L_{p_{0}}\to L_{p_{0}}\|,\|T:L_{p_{0}}\to L_{p_{0}}\|\}\lesssim_{p_{0},q_{0}}1
. We can now appeal to
[
17
, Lemma 6.2]
(see also
[
45
, Lemma 3.10]
for a remark about the constant) to conclude that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}(f_{0}(t))\ dt\lesssim_{p_{0},q_{0}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}(g_{0}(t))\ dt.

Therefore, the proposition is verified for convex Orlicz functions.

Assume now that
0<p<1
and
p<q<\infty
. Consider an Orlicz function
\Phi
that is
p
-convex and
q
-concave. Define
\Phi_{0}(t)=\Phi(t^{1/p})
. Then
\Phi_{0}
is convex and
q/p
-concave. One can easily see from Holmsted’s formula
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
that for a given positive function

and
t>0
,
K(w,t^{1/p};L_{p},L_{q})\approx_{p,q}[K(w^{p},t;L_{1},L_{q/p})]^{1/p}
. Thus, if
f
and
g
are two functions satisfying the
assumption of the proposition, then we have for every
t>0
,

K(f^{p},t;L_{1},L_{q/p})\lesssim_{p,q}K(g^{p},t;L_{1},L_{q/p})
. We can apply the previous convex case to deduce that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}(f(t)^{p})\ dt\lesssim_{p,q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi_{0}(g(t)^{p})\ dt,

which is precisely the desired conclusion.
∎

\displaystyle\|x\|_{L_{r}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{r})}^{r}

\displaystyle=\|\sum_{n\geq 1}|x_{k}|^{2}\|_{r/2}^{r/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\|\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k-1}|x_{k}|^{2}\|_{r/2}^{r/2}

\displaystyle=4\|x\|_{L_{r}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}^{r}

where the inequality in the second line comes from
[
22
, Theorem 7.1]
. By interpolation, the preceding inequality lifts to
F\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{2})
for
0<p<2
.

For the case of the
\Phi
-moment, we start from stating that for
x\in\mathfrak{F}
, we have

(A.1)

K(x,t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))\lesssim_{p}K(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})),\quad t>0.

This can be deduced as follows: fix
x\in L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})+L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
t>0
. For
\varepsilon>0
, choose
x=y+z
, with
y\in L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and
z\in L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and such that

\|y\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+\|z\|_{L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}\leq K(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))+\varepsilon.

Using the fact that the above inequality holds for
L_{r}
for all
0<r\leq 2
, we have

\displaystyle K(x,t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))

\displaystyle\leq\|y\|_{L_{p}^{(}\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\|z\|_{L_{2}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\|y\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}+t\|z\|_{L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})}

\displaystyle\leq\max(C_{p},1)(K(x,t;L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{2}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))+\varepsilon).

Since
\varepsilon
is arbitrary, we have (
A.1
).

Next, it follows from Proposition
3.17
that (
A.1
) is equivalent to

K(\mathcal{S}_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{2}(\mathcal{M}))\lesssim_{p}K(\sigma_{c}(x),t;L_{p}(\mathcal{M}),L_{2}(\mathcal{M})).

On the other hand, according to
[
38
]
(see Corollary 2.3 and the remarks afterward), this is further equivalent to

K(\mu(\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)),t;L_{p},L_{2})\lesssim_{p}K(\mu(\sigma_{c}(x)),t;L_{p},L_{2}).

We can deduce from Proposition
A.1
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\mu_{t}(\mathcal{S}_{c}(x)))\ dt\lesssim_{p}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\mu_{t}(\sigma_{c}(x)))\ dt

and this is precisely the stated
\Phi
-moment.
∎

A.2
implies the following improvement of
[

, Theorem 7.1]
to symmetric spaces of measurable operators. The argument is identical to the proof of
[

Corollary A.3
.

Let
E
be a symmetric quasi-Banach function space with
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{p},L_{1})
for some
0<p<1
. There exists a constant
C_{E}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(a_{k})
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the following holds:

\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{E}\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(a_{k})\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Similarly, if
\Phi
is a concave Orlicz function that is
p
-convex for
0<p<1
, then there exists a constant
C_{p}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(a_{k})
in
\mathfrak{F}
, the following holds:

\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}a_{k}\big)\big]\leq C_{p}\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(a_{k})\big)\big].

We take the opportunity to present below a simple approach to the dual Doob inequality in the spirit of the approach to Corollary
A.3
. The result below strengthens
[
8
, Corollary 4.13]
.

Proposition A.4
.

Let
E
be a symmetric Banach function space with
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for some
1<q<\infty
. There exists a constant
C_{E}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(x_{k})
in
E(\mathcal{M})
, the following holds:

(A.2)

\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{E}\Big\|\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\Big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Similarly, if
\Phi
is a convex Orlicz function that is
q
-concave for some
1<q<\infty
, then there exists a constant
C_{q}
so that for any sequence of positive operators
(x_{k})
in
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
, the following holds:

\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})\big)\big]\leq C_{q}\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\big)\big].

Proof.

We only present the
\Phi
-moment case. For
2\leq p<\infty
, we set
L_{p}^{\rm cond_{+}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
to be the completion of the set of finite sequences in
L_{p}(\mathcal{M})\cap\mathcal{M}
under the norm

\big\|(a_{k})_{k\geq 1}\big\|_{L_{p}^{\rm cond_{+}}(\ell_{2}^{c})}=\big\|\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(|a_{k}|^{2})\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{p}.

This is a slight modification of the conditioned
L_{p}
-space
L_{p}^{\rm cond}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
using
(\mathcal{E}_{k})_{k\geq 1}
in place of
(\mathcal{E}_{k-1})_{k\geq 1}
. Using this variant, we still have that
L_{p}^{\rm cond_{+}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
embeds isometrically into
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
. We denote by
U^{+}
such isometry.

We now proceed with the proof. We start with the simple observation that for every

2\leq p<\infty
, the identity map
I:L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\to L_{p}^{\rm cond_{+}}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})

is bounded. This is equivalent to the noncommutative dual Doob inequality from
[
20
]
for the index
p/2
. Denote by
\Pi
the natural contractive projection from
L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
onto
L_{p}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Then

U^{+}I\Pi:L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))\to L_{p}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))
is bounded with norm depending only on
p
.

If
\varphi
is an Orlicz function that is
2
-convex and
2q
-concave then for every
\xi\in L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))
,

\tau\otimes\mbox{\rm Tr}\big[\varphi\big(|U^{+}I\Pi(\xi)|\big)\big]\lesssim_{q}\tau\otimes\mbox{\rm tr}\big[\varphi\big(|\xi|\big)\big]

where
tr
and
Tr
denote the usual trace on
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}))
and
\mathcal{B}(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2}))
respectively. Let
a=(a_{k})
be a sequence in
L_{\varphi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
. Using
\xi=\sum_{k}a_{k}\otimes e_{k,1}
, the above inequality becomes

(A.3)

\tau\big[\varphi\big(\sigma_{c}^{+}(a)\big)\big]\lesssim_{q}\tau\big[\varphi\big(\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)\big)\big]

where
\sigma_{c}^{+}(a)=\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(|a_{k}|^{2})\big)^{1/2}
.

To conclude the proof, let
\Phi
as in the statement and
(x_{k})_{k}
be a sequence of positive operators from
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M})
. For
t\geq 0
, set
\varphi(t)=\Phi(t^{2})
. Then
\varphi
is a
2
-convex and
2q
-concave Orlicz function. Consider the sequence
a=(x_{k}^{1/2})_{k\geq 1}
. It is easy to see that
\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)=\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\big)^{1/2}
and
\sigma_{c}^{+}(a)=\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})\big)^{1/2}
. It follows from (
A.3
) that

\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{E}_{k}(x_{k})\big)\big]=\tau\big[\varphi\big(\sigma_{c}^{+}(a)\big)\big]\lesssim_{q}\tau\big[\varphi\big(\mathcal{S}_{c}(a)\big)\big]=\tau\big[\Phi\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}x_{k}\big)\big].

This proves the
\Phi
-moment case. The case of symmetric space is obtain in a similar fashion by using the inequality

\big\|U^{+}I\Pi(\xi)\big\|_{F^{(2)}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N}^{2})))}\lesssim_{F}\big\|\xi\big\|_{F^{(2)}(\mathcal{M}\overline{\otimes}B(\ell_{2}(\mathbb{N})))}

where
F^{(2)}
is the
2
-convexfication of
F
. Alternatively, we may also deduce
(
A.2
) from the
\Phi
-moment case by using
[
23
, Theorem 7.1]
.
∎

Theorem A.6
.

Similarly, if

The next result is an extension of the noncommutative Lépingle-Yor inequality to symmetric spaces of measurable operators. This should be compared with the corresponding noncommutative Stein inequality treated in

Theorem A.7
.

Let
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for some
1<q<\infty
. There exists a constant
C_{E}
so that for every adapted sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in E(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, the following holds:

\big\|\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(\xi_{n})|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{E}\big\|\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big\|_{E(\mathcal{M})}.

Similarly, assume that
\Phi
is a convex Orlicz function that is
q
-concave for some
1<q<\infty
. For every adapted sequence
(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}\in L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
, the following holds:

\tau\Big(\Phi\big(\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(\xi_{n})|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big)\Big)\lesssim_{q}\tau\Big(\Phi\big(\big(\sum_{n\geq 1}|\xi_{n}|^{2}\big)^{1/2}\big)\Big).

Proof.

We recall that the first inequality holds for
E=L_{p}
when
1\leq p<\infty
(
[
37
,
39
]
). The case of general function space
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
is a simple consequence of the fact that the spaces of adapted sequences interpolate. For the second part, let
\xi=(\xi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an adapted sequence in
L_{\Phi}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2})
. Denote by
T(\xi)
the sequence
(\mathcal{E}_{n-1}(\xi_{n}))_{n\geq 1}
. One can easily check (using the first part) that for every
t>0
, the following holds:

K(T(\xi),t,L_{1}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}))\lesssim_{q}K(\xi,t,L_{1}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}),L_{q}^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})).

It follows from
K
-closedness that for every
t>0
,

K(\mathcal{S}_{c}(T(\xi)),t;L_{1}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M}))\lesssim_{q}K(\mathcal{S}_{c}(\xi),t;L_{1}(\mathcal{M}),L_{q}(\mathcal{M})).

We can deduce as in the last part of the proof of Theorem
A.2
that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\mu_{t}(\mathcal{S}_{c}(T(\xi))))\ dt\lesssim_{q}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Phi(\mu_{t}(\mathcal{S}_{c}(\xi)))\ dt

which is precisely the desired inequality.
∎

. Below, for a symmetric Banach function space

is used for the subspace of

Proposition A.8
.

Let
E
be a symmetric Banach function space.

(i)

If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{q})
for some
1<q<\infty
, then the following inclusion holds:

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})\subseteq E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})+E^{\rm cond,ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

(ii)

If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
, then following identity holds:

E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})=E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})+E^{\rm cond,ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c}).

We only indicate the adjustments in the argument as it follows verbatim the one used in
[
41
]
. First, we observe that the simultaneous decomposition in
[
41
, Proposition 4.9]
actually applies to all
2/3<p<p_{0}
for any given
2<p_{0}<\infty
. Then the proof of
[
41

can be carried out with arbitrary
1<q<\infty
since we no longer have any restriction on the
K
-closedness. This gives the inclusion in the first item.

On the other hand,
if
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
, one can easily see that
E(\oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}_{n})\subseteq E^{\rm ad}(\mathcal{M};\ell_{2}^{c})
and Theorem

gives

, we can answer problems from
[

and

Corollary A.9
.

Let
E
be a symmetric Banach function space. If
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
, then the following identity holds:

\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M}).

Consequently, the mixed Hardy spaces also coincide:
\mathcal{H}_{E}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{E}(\mathcal{M})
.

Clearly, the first identity follows from Proposition
A.8
(ii) while the second can easily be deduced from combining the first one with its row version.

We remark that Corollary
A.9
is sharp in the sense that if the conclusion is valid then
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
. This can seen as follows: if
\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})=\mathsf{h}_{E}^{d}(\mathcal{M})+\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
then a fortiori,
\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
. Therefore, there exists a constant
C
so that for any
x\in\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}(\mathcal{M})
,
\|x\|_{\mathcal{H}_{E}^{c}}\leq C\|x\|_{\mathsf{h}_{E}^{c}}
. Fix a
\sigma
-field
\mathcal{F}
whose atoms have finite measure. Denote by
\mathcal{E}(\cdot)
the conditional expectation
\mathbb{E}(\cdot|\mathcal{F})
.

Let
\mathcal{M}=L_{\infty}
. Fix
f\in E
and consider the finite martingale defined by setting
f_{1}=\mathcal{E}(f)
and
f_{2}=f
.
First, we note that since
E
is a symmetric Banach function space, by the boundedness of conditional expectations on
E
, we have
\|\mathcal{E}(|f|)\|_{E}\leq\|f\|_{E}
. On the other hand, we make the following estimates:

\displaystyle\|f\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\|f-f_{1}\|_{E}+\|f_{1}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq\|(|f_{1}|^{2}+|f-f_{1}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{E}+\|f_{1}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(|f_{1}|^{2}+\mathcal{E}|f-f_{1}|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{E}+\|f_{1}\|_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathcal{E}(|f|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{E}+\|f_{1}\|_{E}.

From the inequality
|f_{1}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{E}(|f|^{2})
, we get that
\|f\|_{E}\leq(C+1)\|(\mathcal{E}(|f|^{2})^{1/2}\|_{E}
.
We can now conclude from

[
23
, Theorem 7.2]
that
E\in{\rm Int}(L_{1},L_{2})
.

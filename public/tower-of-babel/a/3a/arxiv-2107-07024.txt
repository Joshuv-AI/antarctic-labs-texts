Rota’s Basis Conjecture for Matroids with Density Close to One
Sean McGuinness

Abstract
Rota’s basis conjecture (RBC) states that given a collection ℬ \mathcal{B} of n n bases in a matroid M M of rank n n , one can always find n n disjoint rainbow bases with respect to ℬ \mathcal{B} . We show that if M M is a matroid having n + k n+k elements, then one can construct n − k 3 n-k^{3} disjoint rainbow bases. Keywords : matroid, basis, Rota’s basis conjecture, girth. AMS Subject Classifications (2012) : 05D99,05B35.



1
Introduction

For basic concepts and notation pertaining to matroids, we follow Oxley
[
15
]
, and for graphs, we shall follow Bondy and Murty
[
2
]
. Let
M
be a matroid of rank
n
. A
base sequence
of
M
is an
m
-tuple
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},\ldots B_{m})\in\mathfrak{B}(M)^{m}
of bases of
M
, where for each
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,m\}
, we think of the base
B_{i}
as “coloured” with colour
i
. A
rainbow base
(
RB
) with respect to
\mathcal{B}
is a base of
M
all of whose elements have different colours. Two rainbow bases with respect to
\mathcal{B}
are said to be
disjoint
if for each colour
c
, if both bases have elements of colour
c
, then these elements are distinct. We let
t_{M}(\mathcal{B})
denote the cardinality of a largest set of disjoint rainbow bases with respect to
\mathcal{B}
, where the subscript is dropped when
M
is implicit.
In 1989, Rota made the following conjecture, first communicated in
[
13
]
:

Conjecture 1.1
(Rota’s Basis Conjecture (RBC))

Let
M
be a matroid of rank
n
, and let
\mathcal{B}
be a base sequence of
M
consisting of
n
bases. Then
t(\mathcal{B})=n
.

Due to the work of Drisko
[
8
]
, Glynn
[
12
]
, and Onn
[
14
]
on the Alon-Tarsi conjecture
[
1
]
, RBC is known to be true for
\mathbb{F}
-representable matroids of rank
p\pm 1
, where
\mathbb{F}
is a field of characteristic
0
, and
p
is an odd prime. See
[
9
]
for an overview of these results.
Chan
[
4
]
and Cheung
[
5
]
proved RBC for matroids of rank
3
and
4
, respectively. In
[
10
]
, it was shown that RBC is true for sparse paving matroids and Wild
[
18
]
proved the conjecture for strongly base-orderable matroids.

While RBC has attracted a significant amount of attention (see the polymath project
[
6
]
) and a number of different ideas have yielded different results, there are still very few classes of matroids for which it is known to be true. Even attempts to find lower bounds on the number of disjoint rainbow bases has been challenging. The bound established by Geelen and Webb
[
11
]
, who proved that
t(\mathcal{B})\geq\sqrt{n-1}
, was the only bound known until recently when it was improved by
Dong and Geelen
[
7
]
, who showed that
t(\mathcal{B})\geq\frac{n}{7\log n}
. Around the same time, a significant breakthrough was made on RBC by by Bucić et al.
[
3
]
who showed that
t(\mathcal{B})\geq(1/2-o(1))n
, provided
n
is large enough.
While a proof for RBC seems out of reach at the moment, a natural question raised in
[
3
]
asks whether one can improve the
(\frac{1}{2}-o(1))n
lower bound to say
(1-o(1))n
. This is a very challenging problem given that even finding a constant
c>\frac{1}{2}
for which
t(\mathcal{B})\geq cn
would be a significant step. As an example of when the
n-o(n)
bound can be achieved,
it was shown in
[
9
]
that if
M
is a rank-
n
matroid having girth at least
n-o(\sqrt{n})
, and no element of
M
belongs to more than
o(\sqrt{n})
bases in
\mathcal{B}
, then one can find at least
n-o(n)
disjoint rainbow bases with respect to
\mathcal{B}
. We mention also an interesting recent result in
[
16
]
where it is shown that one can find at least
n-o(n)
disjoint rainbow independent sets of size at least
n-o(n).

At this point in time, RBC remains unresolved even for matroids having rank
n
and a small number of elements. None of the methods introduced thus far apply to rank-
n
matroids with
n+k
elements, where
k
is fixed (for example,
k=5
). This is true even for graphic matroids.
Thus it is a natural question to ask, if for fixed
k
, is RBC true for rank-
n
matroids having
n+k
elements? Can one find even
n-o(n)
disjoint rainbow bases in this case? The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.2

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a sequence of
n
bases in a matroid of rank
n
having
n+k
elements. Then
t(\mathcal{B})\geq n-k^{3}.

As a consequence of the above theorem we obtain:

Corollary 1.3

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a sequence of
n
bases in a matroid of rank
n
having
n+o(n^{\frac{1}{3}})
elements.
Then
t(\mathcal{B})\geq n-o(n).

The method of proof of the main theorem is entirely new. In constructing disjoint rainbow bases, we ensure that at each step that a certain function defined on the flats of a rank-
k
matroid satisfies certain inequalities. When such inequalities are satisfied, one can use the matroid intersection theorem (see
[
15
]
) to guarantee the existence of the next rainbow base. In essence, the rainbow bases are constructed one-at-a-time largely by the special selection of matchings in a bipartite graph. Below we give a more detailed sketch.

1.1
Overview of the proof

Let
M
be a binary matroid of rank
n
where
\varepsilon(M)=|E(M)|=n+k.

Let
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
be a sequence of coloured bases of
M
. Let
m=n+k
and let
E(M)=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}.

We shall define a bipartite graph
G=G(\mathcal{B})
having bipartition
(U,V)
where
U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
, each vertex
u_{i}
representing
B_{i}
, and
V=E(M)
. We let
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
where
v_{i}=e_{i},\ i=1,\dots,m.
For all
i,j
,
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
are joined by an edge in
G
if and only if
e_{j}\in B_{i}.

Note that
d_{G}(u_{i})=n,\ i=1,\dots,n
and
d_{G}(v_{i})\leq n,\ i=1,\dots,n.

We colour the edges of
G
with colours
1,2,\dots,n
such that, for
i=1,\dots,n
, the edges incident to
u_{i}
receive colour
i.
A rainbow basis
B
in
M
corresponds to a
matching

W
in
G
which saturates all vertices in
U
and those elements in
V
saturated by
W
correspond to
B
.

Suppose that
G
can be partitioned into
n
matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
, where, for all
i
,
W_{i}
corresponds to a rainbow basis of
M
. For
i=1,\dots,n,
let
G_{i}=G-W_{1}-\dots-W_{i}.

We define the
deficit
of a vertex
v\in V
in
G_{i}
to be
\delta_{i}(v)=n-i-d_{G_{i}}(v).
Such a decomposition of
G
into matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
necessitates that the graph
G_{i}
satisfy certain conditions. First, we must have that for all
v\in V,

\delta_{i}(v)\geq 0.
For if
\delta_{i}(v)<0
for some vertex
v\in V,
then
d_{G_{i}}(v)>n-i.
However, this is impossible if the edges incident to
v
in
G_{i}
belong to the matchings
W_{i+1},\dots,W_{n}.
In addition, it can be shown that the deficit of a set cannot be too large. More precisely, for a set
A\subseteq E
let
\delta_{i}(A)=\delta_{i}(V_{A}).
It can be shown that for every subset
A\subseteq E,

\delta_{i}(A)\leq r_{N}(A)(n-i)
where
N
is a matroid of rank
k
derived from
M.
We refer to these as
deficit inequalities
.

Suppose now that we have only constructed disjoint matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{i}
, each corresponding to a rainbow basis. A key observation is that if
\delta_{i}
satisfies a deficit inequality for each
A\subseteq E,
then the matroid intersection theorem implies that there exists a matching
W_{i+1}
in
G_{i}
corresponding to a rainbow basis in
M.
Thus to guarantee that
G_{i}
has such a matching it suffices to choose the previous matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{i}
so that
\delta_{i}
satisfies these deficit inequalities. This is a daunting task. However, it turns out that if the deficit inequalities hold for
\delta_{i-1}
and
n-i
is large enough, then
one can choose
W_{i}
carefully so that the deficit inequalities will hold for
\delta_{i}
. Thus to construct disjoint rainbow bases, it suffices to show that at each step, one can first choose
W_{i}
corresponding to a rainbow basis, and then alter
W_{i}
so that the deficit inequalities hold for
\delta_{i}.
We alter
W_{i}
using sequences of
alternating paths
in
G_{i-1}
where one swaps the matching edges on the paths with the non-matching edges.
The major challenge is to alter the matchings so that the resulting matching corresponds to a rainbow basis. Secondly, the new matching must improve on the previous matching by reducing the number of deficit inequalities which are unsatisfied. We show that this can be achieved by constructing a
path-chain
of alternating paths. The existence of such path-chains will be dependent on
n-i
being much larger than
k
(more specifically,
n-i\geq k^{3}
). After each step
i
, the construction of the next matching
W_{i+1}
really only depends on the judicious selection of
W_{i}
and as such one can view the rainbow bases as being constructed one-at-a-time.

2
Notation

For a positive integer
k
, let
[k]
denote the set
\{1,\dots,k\}
. For a graph
G
, let
d_{G}(v)
denote the degree of
v.
For a subset
X\subseteq V(G)
, let
\partial_{G}(X)
denote the set of edges with one endpoint in
X
and the other in
V(G)\backslash X.
We let
d_{G}(X)=|\partial_{G}(X)|
. For sets
X
and
Y
of vertices, we let
E_{G}(X,Y)
denote the set of edges with one endpoint in
X
and the other in
Y
and let
e_{G}(X,Y)=|E_{G}[X,Y]|.

For a subset
X
of vertices, let
N_{G}(X)
denote the set of neighbours of vertices in
X
belonging to
V(G)\backslash X.
For a set
Y\subset V(G)
, we let
N_{G}(X,Y)=N_{G}(X)\cap Y.

For a directed graph
\overrightarrow{D},
we let
N_{\overrightarrow{D}}^{+}(v)
(resp.
N_{\overrightarrow{D}}^{-}(v)
) denote the set of out-neighbours (resp. in-neighbours) of
v.

For a set
X
, we let
\mathbf{1}_{X}
denote the indicator function for
X.
For sets
X
and
Y
, let
X\triangle Y
denote the symmetric difference of
X
and
Y.

For a matroid
M
, we let
\varepsilon(M)=|E(M)|,
the number of elements in
M
. For convenience, if we create a new basis from a basis
B
by deleting
X\subseteq B
and adding the elements of a set
Y,
then we denote the resulting basis by
B-X+Y.
In the case where
X=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}
and
Y=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\},
we will often write the new basis as
B-x_{1}-\cdots-x_{k}+y_{1}+\cdots+y_{k}.

For example, if
X=\{e\}
and
Y=\{f\},
then the new basis is just
B-e+f.

For a subset
A\subseteq E(M),
we let
\overline{A}
denote the set
E(M)\backslash A.

In several places in the paper, we shall use the (easily proven) observation that if
B
is a basis in a matroid,
C^{*}
is a cocircuit, and
e\in\overline{B}
is such that
(B+e)\cap C^{*}=\{e,f\},
then
B-f+e
is a basis.

3
The deficit function

Let
M
be a matroid of rank
n
where
\varepsilon(M)=|E(M)|=n+k.

Let
\mathcal{B}=(B_{1},\dots,B_{n})
be a sequence of coloured bases of
M
. Let
m=n+k
and let
E(M)=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}.

We shall define a bipartite graph
G=G(\mathcal{B})
having bipartition
(U,V)
where
U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\}
, each vertex
u_{i}
representing
B_{i}
, and
V=E(M)
. We let
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
, where
v_{i}=e_{i},\ i=1,\dots,m.
For all
i,j
, the vertices
u_{i}
and
v_{j}
are joined by an edge in
G
if and only if
e_{j}\in B_{i}.

Note that
d_{G}(u_{i})=n,\ i=1,\dots,n
and
d_{G}(v_{i})\leq n,\ i=1,\dots,n.

We colour the edges of
G
with colours
1,2,\dots,n
such that, for
i=1,\dots,n
, the edges incident to
u_{i}
receive colour
i.
A rainbow basis
B
corresponds to a
matching
in
G
which saturates
U
and those elements in
V
corresponding to
B
.

For a subset of edges
A\subseteq E(G)
, let
U_{A}\subseteq U
(respectively,
V_{A}\subseteq V
) denote the set of endvertices in
U
(resp.
V
) of edges in
A.

For a graph
H
of maximum degree
d
, we define the
\mathbf{H}
-deficit
of a vertex
v
to be
\delta_{H}(v):=d-d_{H}(v).
For a subset
X\subseteq V(H)
, we define the
H
-deficit of
X
to be

\delta_{H}(X):=\sum_{x\in X}\delta_{H}(x).
We define the deficit of
H
to be
\delta(H):=\delta(V(H)).

Suppose that
G
can be partitioned into
n
matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
, where, for all
i
,
V_{W_{i}}
is a basis of
M
; that is,
V_{W_{i}}
is a rainbow basis. For
i=1,\dots,n,
let
G_{i}=G-W_{1}-\dots-W_{i}.
For convenience, we let
\delta_{i}
denote the deficit function
\delta_{G_{i}}
and we let
d_{i}(v)=d_{G_{i}}(v).

Note that for all
j
,
d_{i}(u_{j})=n-i.
Also, since for each
v_{j}\in V,
the edges incident to
v_{j}
in
G_{i}
belong to the matchings
W_{i+1},\dots,W_{n},
it follows that for all
j,

d_{i}(v_{j})\leq n-i.

Thus
G_{i}
has maximum degree
n-i.

Lemma 3.1

The deficit of
G_{i}
equals
k(n-i).

We have

\displaystyle\delta(G_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V}\delta_{i}(v)=\sum_{v\in V}(n-i-d_{i}(v))=(n+k)(n-i)-\sum_{v\in V}d_{i}(v)

\displaystyle=(n+k)(n-i)-|E(G_{i})|=(n+k)(n-i)-(n^{2}-ni)=k(n-i).

\square

Lemma 3.2

Let
V^{\prime}\subseteq V.
Then

|V_{W_{i+1}}\cap V^{\prime}|=|V^{\prime}|-\alpha
if and only if
\delta_{i+1}(V^{\prime})=\delta_{i}(V^{\prime})-\alpha.

Suppose
|V_{W_{i+1}}\cap V^{\prime}|=|V^{\prime}|-\alpha
. Then

\displaystyle\delta_{i+1}(V^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}((n-i-1)-d_{i+1}(v))=(n-i-1)|V^{\prime}|-\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}d_{i+1}(v)

\displaystyle=(n-i-1)|V^{\prime}|-\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}d_{i}(v)+(|V^{\prime}|-\alpha))=(n-i)|V^{\prime}|-\sum_{v\in V^{\prime}}d_{i}(v)-\alpha

\displaystyle=\delta_{i}(V^{\prime})-\alpha.

The above also shows that if
\delta_{i+1}(V^{\prime})=\delta_{i}(V^{\prime})-\alpha,
then
|V_{W_{i+1}}\cap V^{\prime}|=|V^{\prime}|-\alpha
.

\square

4
The matroid
N

Our goal is to describe the
deficit inequalities
for
\delta_{i}
which will be a necessary condition for the existence of
n
disjoint rainbow bases. Note that
e
is a loop in
M^{*}
if and only if for every base
B
of
M
,
e\in B.

We let
N
be the matroid which is the simplification of the dual matroid
M^{*}
plus a loop
a_{loop}.
that is,
N=\widetilde{M^{*}}+a_{loop}.

We let
[a_{loop}]
denote the set of all loops of
M^{*}.

For each element
a\in E(N)-a_{loop}
, let
[a]
denote the
1
-flat in
M^{*}-[a_{loop}]
spanned by
a
. We define
\psi:V=E(M)\rightarrow E(N)
where
\psi(e)=a
if and only if
e\in[a].

For any subset
F\subseteq E(N)
, let
[F]=\bigcup_{a\in F}[a].

We have the following observations which have straightforward proofs:

Observation 4.1

Let
E=E(M).
Then we have

i)

C
is a circuit in
M
if and only if
\psi(C)
is a cocircuit in
N.

ii)

A subset
B\subseteq E
is a basis in
M
if and only if
\psi(E\backslash B)
is a basis in
N.

iii)

For a subset
X\subseteq E,

r_{N}(\psi(X))=|X|-n+r_{M}(E-X).
Equivalently,

r_{M}(X)=n-|E-X|+r_{N}(\psi(E-X)).

4.1
Necessary conditions for the existence of
n
rainbow bases

The existence of
n
disjoint rainbow bases necessitates that
N
satisfy certain conditions. These are given below in Lemma
4.2
.
Suppose
G
has a decomposition into matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{n}
, where for all
i\in[n],

V_{W_{i}}
is a basis in
M.
Let
G_{p}
be the graph obtained from
G
by removing
W_{1},\dots,W_{p}.

For each
a\in E(N),
we define
\delta_{p}(a):=\delta_{p}([a]).
As before, let
E=E(M).

For the remainder of the paper, we define
\eta:=n-p.
Note that for a matching
W
in
G_{p}
, the set
\overline{V}_{W}
denotes the vertices in
V
not saturated by
W.

Lemma 4.2

For all
e\in E,\ \delta_{p}(e)\geq 0,
and for all flats
F
in
N
we have

\delta_{p}(F)\leq r_{N}(F)\eta.

As observed before, the maximum degree of
G_{p}
equals
\eta
. Thus
\delta_{p}(e)\geq 0
for all elements
e\in E(M).
For each matching
W_{i},\ i=p+1,\dots,n
, we have
\psi(\overline{V}_{W_{i}})
is a basis of
N
(by Observation
4.1
ii)). Thus for all flats
F
in
N,

|\overline{V}_{W_{i}}\cap[F]|\leq r_{N}(F)
since
\psi(\overline{V}_{W_{i}}\cap[F])\subseteq F
is independent in
N.
It follows that

|V_{W_{i}}\cap[F]|\geq|[F]|-r_{N}(F).
Therefore, by Lemma
3.2
,
\delta_{i}([F])\geq\delta_{i-1}([F])-r_{N}(F)
and consequently,

\delta_{i}(F)\geq\delta_{i-1}(F)-r_{N}(F),\ i=p+1,\dots,n.

Thus
0=\delta_{n}(F)\geq\delta_{p}(F)-r_{N}(F)\eta
and hence
\delta_{p}(F)\leq r_{N}(F)\eta.

\square

5
Finding a rainbow basis

We shall use the following well-known theorem of Rado (see
[
17
]
):

Theorem 5.1

Suppose
X_{1},\dots,X_{\ell}
are subsets of elements of a matroid having rank function
r.
Then there exists an independent transversal
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{\ell}\}
where

x_{i}\in X_{i},\ i=1,\dots,\ell
if and only if for all subsets
J\subseteq[\ell],\ r\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}X_{i}\right)\geq|J|.

In this section, we will assume that only matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{p}
have been constructed, where for
i=1,\dots,p
,
V_{W_{i}}
is a basis of
M.
In addition, we shall assume the matchings
W_{1},\dots,W_{p}
are constructed such that
\forall e\in E(M),\ \delta_{p}(e)\geq 0
and for each flat
F
in
N
, the following
deficit inequality
holds for for each flat
F
:

\delta_{p}(F)\leq r_{N}(F)\eta

We shall prove the following key lemma:

Lemma 5.2

If
p\geq 1
,
\delta_{p}(e)\geq 0,\ \forall e\in E(M)
and (
5
) holds for all flats
F
in
N
, then there exists a matching
W_{p+1}
in
G_{p}
for which
V_{W_{p+1}}
is a basis of
M.

For
i=1,\dots,n,
let
X_{i}
be the set of neighbours of
u_{i}
in
V.
If one can find and independent transversal
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\}
where

x_{i}\in X_{i},\ i=1,\dots,n
, then the corresponding matching
W_{p+1}
will be such that
V_{W_{p+1}}
is a basis. To show such a transversal exists, it suffices to show (by Rado’s theorem) that for all
J\subseteq[n],

r_{M}\left(\bigcup_{i\in J}X_{i}\right)\geq|J|.
Let
J\subseteq[n]
and let
X=\bigcup_{i\in J}X_{i}.

By Observation
4.1
iii), we have
r_{M}(X)=n-|E-X|+r_{N}(\psi(E-X)).
We also have by (
5
) that

r_{N}(\psi(E-X))\geq\frac{\delta_{p}(E-X)}{\eta}=\frac{\sum_{v\in E-X}(\eta-d_{p}(v))}{\eta}=|E-X|-\frac{\sum_{v\in E-X}d_{p}(v)}{\eta}.

Thus
r_{M}(X)\geq n-\frac{\sum_{v\in E-X}d_{p}(v)}{\eta}.
Let
U_{J}=\{u_{i}\in U\ \big|\ i\in J\}
. Given that each vertex
v\in E-X
is only adjacent to vertices in
U-U_{J},
it follows that
\sum_{v\in E-X}d_{p}(v)\leq\sum_{u_{i}\in U_{J}}d_{p}(u_{i})\leq\eta(n-|J|).

Thus it follows from the above that
r_{M}(X)\geq n-\frac{\eta(n-|J|)}{\eta}=|J|.
This completes the proof.

\square

6
Changing matchings using alternating paths

We shall assume
\forall e\in E,

\delta_{p}(e)\geq 0
and for all flats
F
in
N
, (
5
) holds.
By Lemma
5.2
, one can find a matching
W=W_{p+1}
in
G_{p}
for which
V_{W}
is a basis in
M.
Our strategy is show that, provided
\eta=n-p\geq k^{3},
one can change
W
using
alternating paths
in such a way that when we delete it from
G_{p}
, the resulting graph
G_{p+1}
will also be such that
\forall e\in E(M),\ \delta_{p+1}(e)\geq 0
and (
5
) holds with
\eta-1
in place of
\eta.

It will then follow by Lemma
5.2
that
G_{p+1}
has a matching
W_{p+2}
for which
V_{W_{p+2}}
is a basis. The process then continues with
W_{p+2}
in place of
W_{p+1}.

Let
A=\psi(\overline{V}_{W})
, which (by Observation
4.1
ii)) is a basis in
N.
It follows by Lemma
3.2
that for all flats
F
in
N,

\delta_{p+1}(F)=\delta_{p}(F)-\nu\ \Longleftrightarrow\ |\overline{V}_{W}\cap[F]|=\nu.

In particular, to ensure that for all flats
F
in

Given a set of edges
X
forming a matching in a bipartite graph, an
alternating path
with respect to
X
is a path
P
whose edges alternate between matching edges and non-matching edges.

Let

be a matching in
G_{p}
where

is a basis. For a subset

we let

denote the set of vertices in

which are reachable by an alternating path with respect to

originating at some vertex in

where the first edge of the path is in

We are interested in

, the number of edges between
U_{W,Y}
and
\overline{V}_{W}.

In Lemmas
6.2

).

Lemma 6.1

e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})\geq\delta_{p}(Y).

Let
Y^{\prime}
be the set of vertices in
V_{W}
matched to vertices in
U_{W,Y}.
Note that
|Y^{\prime}|=|U_{W,Y}|.
Since each vertex in
U_{W,Y}
has degree
\eta,
we have

\displaystyle\eta|U_{W,Y}|

\displaystyle=d_{p}(U_{W,Y})=e_{p}(U_{W,Y},Y^{\prime}\cup\overline{V}_{W})=e_{p}(U_{W,Y},Y^{\prime})+e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})

\displaystyle\leq d_{p}(Y^{\prime})+e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})

\displaystyle=|Y^{\prime}|\eta-\delta_{p}(Y^{\prime})+e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})

\displaystyle=|U_{W,Y}|\eta-\delta_{p}(Y^{\prime})+e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})

From the above, we see that
e_{p}(U_{W,Y},\overline{V}_{W})\geq\delta_{p}(Y^{\prime})\geq\delta_{p}(Y).

\square

For a subset

let

An alternating path with respect to

Lemma 6.2

Let

\square

Lemma 6.3

Let

Lemma 6.4

Let
Y\subseteq V
and suppose that

If

\lceil\frac{\ell}{\eta}\rceil

for which there is a
Y
-path originating at

Since

\displaystyle\delta_{p}(Y)

\displaystyle=\delta_{p}(\widetilde{Y})+\delta_{p}(Y\cap\overline{V}_{W})\geq\ell+s\eta

\square

\overline{V}_{W}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}

and let

N
. We shall assume that for all flats
F
in
N

\delta_{p}(F)\leq r_{N}(F)\eta.
Our primary goal is to show that
W
can be chosen so that for all flats
F
in
N
, (
6
) holds.
When
\delta_{p}(F)\leq r_{N}(F)(\eta-1),
this inequality automatically holds and thus the focus will be on the flats
F
where

\delta_{p}(F)>r_{N}(F)(\eta-1).

be the set all flats
F
in
N
for which
\delta_{p}(F)>r_{N}(F)(\eta-1)

and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathfrak{F}
. Furthermore,
\mathfrak{F}
is closed under intersections.

Suppose

(where

) fails to hold for

so that for the corresponding

We will show that it is possible to choose

as such provided

For all

be the hyperplane in

, where

For

is a rank-

\delta_{p}(N_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}})\leq(k-s)\eta.
Thus
\delta_{p}\left(\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}C_{i_{j}}^{*}\right)=\delta_{p}(\overline{N_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}}})=\delta_{p}(N)-\delta_{p}(N_{i_{1}\cdots i_{s}})\geq s\eta.

\square

The next definition exploits the useful property that

is closed under taking intersections.

For all
i\in[k]
, there is a unique flat in

of least rank which contains

We shall denote such a flat by

Furthermore, we let

Lemma 8.2

Suppose that

are flats in

where for all

\delta_{p}(Q_{i})=r_{N}(Q_{i})\eta-j_{i}.

\displaystyle\delta_{p}(Q_{1}\cap Q_{2})

\displaystyle=\delta_{p}(Q_{1})+\delta_{p}(Q_{2})-\delta_{p}(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2})

\displaystyle=r_{N}(Q_{1})\eta-j_{1}+r_{N}(Q_{2})\eta-j_{2}-\delta_{p}(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2})

\displaystyle\geq(r_{N}(Q_{1})+r_{N}(Q_{2}))\eta-j_{1}-j_{2}-r_{N}(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2})\eta

\displaystyle=(r_{N}(Q_{1})+r_{N}(Q_{2})-r_{N}(Q_{1}\cup Q_{2}))\eta-j_{1}-j_{2}

\displaystyle\geq r_{N}(Q_{1}\cap Q_{2})\eta-j_{1}-j_{2}.

Path-tangles and path-chains

To alter
W
so that for all
F\in\mathfrak{F},

\omega_{W}(F)=r_{N}(F)
, we will do so incrementally using sequences of alternating paths in
G_{p}
which we refer to as

.

Recall that
\overline{V}_{W}=\{y_{1},\dots,y_{k}\}
,
\psi(\overline{V}_{W})=A=\{a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\}
(a basis for
N
) and for

C_{i}^{*}=\overline{H}_{i}
,
F_{i}^{\prime}=C_{i}^{*}\cap F_{i}
. Let

Suppose

are vertex-disjoint alternating paths with respect to

where for

,

(and

). Let

)

\overline{V}_{W^{\prime}}
is a basis. Then it follows that

N
. Our goal is to show that such paths
P_{1},\dots,P_{t}
can be found so that
\omega_{W^{\prime}}(F)>\omega_{W}(F)
(i.e.
|F\cap A^{\prime}|>|F\cap A|
) for certain flats

6
) being satisfied for all flats
F\in\mathfrak{F}.
To obtain
A^{\prime}
from

, for
i=1,\dots,t,
we replace
a_{i}
with
a_{i}^{\prime}
. Generally, to ensure that this procedure yields a basis

, we need to choose the path

strategically.
In particular, for

we will choose

such that

be a function and suppose

and terminating in

For all

y_{\beta(i)}\xrightarrow[W]{P_{i}}y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}
.

y_{\beta(1)}^{\prime}\in Y_{0}
.

iii)

or
y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}\in[C_{\beta(i^{\prime})}^{*}]
, for some
i^{\prime}<i.

K=(P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{t})
is called a

if instead of iii)

satisfies the stronger property:

iv)

For
i=2,\dots,t,

y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}\in[C_{\beta(i-1)}^{*}]
.

Let

be the path-tangle as described above. Consider
y_{\beta(i)}.
Within

one can find a path-chain

where
\gamma(s)=i
and

connects
y_{\beta(i)}
with
y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}
. If
y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}\in Y_{0},

is the desired path-chain. Suppose
y_{\beta(i)}^{\prime}\not\in Y_{0}.
Then

, for some

The path

connects
y_{\beta(i^{\prime})}
with
y_{\beta(i^{\prime})}^{\prime}.

, then

is the desired path-chain. Suppose
y_{\beta(i^{\prime})}^{\prime}\not\in Y_{0}
. Then
y_{\beta(i^{\prime})}^{\prime}\in[C_{\beta(i^{\prime\prime})}^{*}]
, for some

The path

connects
y_{\beta(i^{\prime\prime})}

. If

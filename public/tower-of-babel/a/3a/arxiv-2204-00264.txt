Minkowski and (A)dS ground states in general 2d dilaton gravity
Daniel Grumiller, Martin Laihartinger and Romain Ruzziconi

Abstract
We reorganize the derivative expansion of general (power-counting non-renormalizable) 2d dilaton gravity such that the mass function is integrable. As an example, we consider a three-parameter family of models and provide conditions on the parameters such that the ground state is either Minkowski, Rindler, or (A)dS.



1
Introduction to general 2d dilaton gravity

Two is the lowest spacetime dimension with enough room for space and time. Consequently, it is the lowest dimension that allows for lightcones, horizons, and black holes. Moreover, two is the lowest dimension permitting non-trivial intrinsic curvature and topology. For all these reasons, two-dimensional (2d) theories of gravity serve as minimal toy models to elucidate conceptual aspects of classical and quantum gravity, including black hole evaporation and holographic correspondences.

Since Einstein gravity does not exist in 2d, one has to resort to some alternative gravity theory. All known approaches

2
Classical solutions

To discuss the classical solutions of general 2d dilaton gravity (
1
), it is convenient to work in the first order formulation of the theory

;
Klosch:1995qv

I[\omega,\,e_{a},\,X,\,X^{a}]=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\int\Big(X\,\extdm\!\omega+X^{a}\,\big(\extdm\!e_{a}+\epsilon_{a}{}^{b}\,\omega\wedge e_{b}\big)+\epsilon\,{\cal V}(X,\,X^{c}X_{c})\Big)

(2)

\omega
, the zweibein
e_{a}
, the dilaton
X
, and auxiliary scalar fields
X^{a}
. We introduced the volume form
\epsilon=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon^{ab}e_{a}\wedge e_{b}
. In light-cone coordinates
(x^{+},x^{-})
for the Lorentz indices

\eta_{+-}=1

\eta_{\pm\pm}=0

\epsilon^{\pm}{}_{\pm}=\pm 1

\displaystyle\extdm\!X+X^{-}e^{+}-X^{+}e^{-}=0

(3a)

\displaystyle(\extdm\!\pm\omega)X^{\pm}\pm e^{\pm}\,\mathcal{V}=0

(3b)

\displaystyle\extdm\!\omega+\epsilon\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial X}=0

(3c)

\displaystyle(\extdm\!\pm\omega)e^{\pm}+\epsilon\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{V}}{\partial X^{\mp}}=0

(3d)

The constant dilaton sector of the theory is given by the solutions of (

)-(

{\cal V}(X,\,X^{a}X_{a})=0=X^{a}

(4)

Instead, we focus on the linear dilaton sector of the theory and summarize the solution algorithm discussed in

\extdm\!\,Y-\mathcal{V}(X,\,2Y)\,\extdm\!X=0\qquad\qquad Y:=X^{+}X^{-}\,.

(5)

As discussed below, this relation implies Casimir conservation
\extdm\!C=0

\omega=-\frac{\extdm\!X^{+}}{X^{+}}-Z\,\mathcal{V}

(6)

e^{-}=\frac{\extdm\!X}{X^{+}}+X^{-}Z

(7)

\extdm\!Z=\left(Z\wedge\extdm\!X\right)\frac{\partial{\cal V}}{\partial Y}\,.

(8)

Inserting

\frac{\extdm\!Q}{\extdm\!X}=-\frac{\partial{\cal V}}{\partial Y}

(9)

Q(X)=-\int^{X}\frac{\partial\cal V}{\partial Y}

(10)

\extdm\!s^{2}=2e^{+}e^{-}=2e^{Q}\,\extdm\!v\extdm\!X+2Ye^{2Q}\,\extdm\!v^{2}

3
Integrable conservation equation

In practice, given a potential
\mathcal{V}
, it might be difficult to integrate the conservation equation (
5
) and find an explicit solution for
Y
. Furthermore, it is not a priori obvious to determine which class of potentials
\mathcal{V}
will allow us to find explicit solutions and obtain a complete classification of the ground states. Instead, we will use another route and start from a class of exact differential equations for (
5
). They are of the form

p(X,\,Y)~\extdm\!X+q(X,\,Y)~\extdm\!Y=0

(13)

where
p(X,\,Y)
and
q(X,\,Y)
are functions satisfying the integrablity condition

\frac{\partial p}{\partial Y}=\frac{\partial q}{\partial X}\,.

(14)

With this condition, the conservation equation (
13
) can be rewitten in terms of the Casimir
C(X,\,Y)
as

\extdm\!C=0\qquad\textrm{with}\qquad p=\frac{\partial C}{\partial X}\qquad q=\frac{\partial C}{\partial Y}

(15)

which readily can be integrated. Comparing with (
5
), we identify

\mathcal{V}=-\frac{p}{q}\,.

(16)

Our approach is reminiscent of inverse scattering methods: instead of starting with some potential
\mathcal{V}
and determining the mass function
C
, we postulate some suitable mass function
C
, use (
15
) to get the functions
p
and
q
, and obtain the potential
\mathcal{V}
as output by virtue of (
16
).
3
3

3

The analog of the integrability condition (
14
) would be difficult to formulate if one started from a generic potential
\mathcal{V}
instead of starting from equation (
13
), which justifies our approach.

In terms of (
16
), equation (
10
) leads to

Q(X)=\int^{X}\frac{\partial}{\partial Y}\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\extdm\!X^{\prime}=\int^{X}\left(\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial p}{\partial Y}-\frac{p}{q}~\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial q}{\partial Y}\right)\extdm\!X^{\prime}~.

(17)

The first term in the integral on the right can be rewritten with the relation (
14
).

Q(X)=\int^{X}\left(\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial q}{\partial X^{\prime}}+\mathcal{V}~\frac{1}{q}\frac{\partial q}{\partial Y}\right)\extdm\!X^{\prime}

(18)

Performing the change of integration variable
\extdm\!X=\mathcal{V}^{-1}\extdm\!Y
in the second term, and using
\extdm\!q=\frac{\partial q}{\partial Y}~\extdm\!Y+\frac{\partial q}{\partial X}~\extdm\!X
, we finally obtain

Q(X)=\int\frac{1}{q}~\extdm\!q=\ln\,q(X)

(19)

where, as explained in the previous section, the integration constant does not contain physical information and has therefore been set to zero. In general,
Q(X)
depends on the Casimir
C
when
q
depends on
Y
, since we use the integrated version of (
5
) to express
Y
as function of
X
and the Casimir
C
. Power-counting renormalizable models,
\mathcal{V}(X,\,2Y)=V(X)-YU(X)
, are a notable exception: for these models, the integrating factor
Q(X)
is independent from the Casimir
C
.

Up to this stage, given
p
,
q
in equation (
13
) satisfying the integrability condition (
14
), we have shown how to write explicitly the line element (
11
) using (
19
) and the constraint on
Y
that can be readily obtained from the Casimir conservation deduced from (

). Now we intend to determine the ground states of these families of metrics, which are characterized by solutions with
(i)
constant curvature and
(ii)
vanishing Casimir function. The first condition is by choice — we are mostly interested in models that have a maximally symmetric ground state, like flat space, Rindler, or (A)dS
2
. The second condition is without loss of generality: we can always shift the whole spectrum in such a way that the value of the Casimir function for the ground state vanishes.

The Ricci scalar
R
is expressed in terms of the Killing norm
K
as

Zeros of the Killing norm
K
in (

R=\partial_{r}^{2}K\qquad\qquad K=2q^{2}Y\,.

(20)

We distinguish three types of vacua, depending on the values of the curvature and Killing norm:

•

Minkowski ground states
are characterized by vanishing Ricci scalar,

, and constant Killing norm,
K(r)=\rm const.

•

Rindler ground states
are given by vanishing Ricci scalar,

, and linear Killing norm,

.

•

(A)dS ground states
are characterized by positive (negative) Ricci scalar, so that the Killing norm is quadratic. We can distinguish between Poincaré (A)dS with

, global (A)dS with

and (A)dS Rindler where

for some constants

4
Three-parameter family as example

Power-counting renormalizable models involve potentials and Casimirs that are at most linear in
Y
. The ground states of these models have been extensively discussed in the literature (see e.g.
Katanaev:1996ni
). Here, we focus on the simplest general dilaton gravity that is not power-counting renormalizable by considering a Casimir

C(X,Y)=V_{0}(X)+V_{1}(X)\,Y+V_{2}(X)\,Y^{2}

(21)

where the potentials

,

are taken to be monomials in

V_{0}(X)=A~X^{a}\qquad\qquad V_{1}(X)=B~X^{b}\qquad\qquad V_{2}(X)=D~X^{c}

(22)

The parameters

,
B
,

and

,

,

are constant. The condition
(ii)
to obtain ground state solutions requires solving

for

Y^{\pm}=\frac{-V_{1}\pm\sqrt{V_{1}^{2}-4V_{2}V_{0}}}{2V_{2}}

(23)

Evaluating the function

on these solutions yields

q(X,Y^{\pm})=V_{1}+2V_{2}Y_{\pm}=\pm\sqrt{V_{1}^{2}-4V_{2}V_{0}}~.

(24)

Plugging (
23
) and (
24

K^{\pm}_{0}:=K^{\pm}|_{C=0}=(V_{1}^{2}-4V_{2}V_{0})\frac{-V_{1}\pm\sqrt{V_{1}^{2}-4V_{2}V_{0}}}{V_{2}}~.

(25)

For the three-parameter family (
22
), this reads as

K^{\pm}_{0}=\big(B^{2}X^{2b}-4AD~X^{a+c}\big)\Big(-\frac{B}{D}X^{b-c}\pm\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}}{D^{2}}X^{2b-2c}-4\frac{A}{D}X^{a-c}}\Big)~.

(26)

By expanding

for small values of

Y^{-}
is continuously connected to the linear solution, while the branch
Y^{+}
is not. This constitutes a criterion to select the branch
Y^{-}
rather than

We now focus on the condition
(i)
defining ground states. Following the classification of vacua provided at the end of the previous section, we treat each case separately. We determine first the conditions on the model parameters

such that the ground state of the corresponding model has the desired properties, then write down the potential associated with the model and finally display the metric (
12

\Delta:=B^{2}-4AD

4.1
Minkowski ground states

Minkowski ground states are found by requiring the expression of the Killing norm (
26
) to be constant. Rewriting (
26

K^{-}_{0}=X^{3b-c}\,\big(B^{2}-4AD~X^{a+c-2b}\big)\Big(-\frac{B}{D}-\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}}{D^{2}}-4\frac{A}{D}X^{a+c-2b}}\Big)=\textrm{const}~,

(27)

we see that this condition is satisfied for generic
A
,
B
and
D
only if each term in the product is constant, and therefore
a+c-2b=0
and
3b-c=0
. Minkowski ground state models then have to obey the relations

\boxed{\phantom{\Bigg(}\textrm{Minkowski\;ground\;state\;models:}\qquad\qquad a=-b\qquad\qquad c=3b\phantom{\Bigg)}}

(28)

between the parameters, yielding the potential

\boxed{\phantom{\Bigg(}{\cal V}_{\textrm{\tiny Mink}}\big(X,\,-(\partial X)^{2}\big)=a\,X^{2a-1}\,\frac{4A+2BX^{-2a}(\partial X)^{2}-3DX^{-4a}(\partial X)^{4}}{-4B+4DX^{-2a}(\partial X)^{2}}\,.\phantom{\Bigg)}}

(29)

The metric (
12
) for Minkowski ground state models simplifies to (
\Delta=B^{2}-4AD
)

\extdm\!s^{2}=2\extdm\!v\extdm\!r-I_{0}\,\Big(\Delta+4CDX^{-a}\Big)\Big(B+\sqrt{\Delta+4CDX^{-a}}\Big)\,\extdm\!v^{2}

(30)

where we labelled by
I_{0}
the freedom of fixing the integration constant in the function

given in (

). Demanding the terms proportional to the Casimir
C
to be subleading for large

establishes the convexity condition
a>0
. For positive
a
and a convenient choice of

, the metric (
30
) has the asymptotically flat expansion

\extdm\!s^{2}=2\extdm\!v\extdm\!r-\Big(1-MX^{-a}+{\cal O}\big(M^{2}X^{-2a}\big)\Big)\,\extdm\!v^{2}

(31)

where the mass parameter
M
is proportional to
C
. Demanding that large values of the radial coordinate also correspond to large values of the dilaton requires
a\leq 1
. Thus, standard Minkowski ground state models obey
a\in(0,1]
in addition to (

Non-generic cases for specific values of the constants
A
,
B
,
D
exist as well. If
A=0

, we get the ground state condition

with no further restriction. In the case of
B=0
and

leads to

. The case

leads to a two-parameter family of power-counting renormalizable models with the conditions on the parameter

, so that

. Comparing with the well-known two-parameter family

(see e.g.

\mathcal{V}_{(a^{\prime}b^{\prime})}(X,2Y)=\frac{a^{\prime}}{X}Y+X^{a^{\prime}+b^{\prime}}

4.2
Rindler ground states

With the radial coordinate defined by
r=\int^{X}q(X^{\prime},Y)\extdm\!X^{\prime}

\displaystyle K^{-}_{0}

\displaystyle=\big(B^{2}X^{2b}-4AD~X^{a+c}\big)\Big(-\frac{B}{D}X^{b-c}-\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}}{D^{2}}X^{2b-2c}-4\frac{A}{D}X^{a-c}}\Big)

\boxed{\phantom{\Bigg(}\textrm{Rindler\;ground\;state\;models:}\qquad\qquad a=1\qquad\qquad c=2b-1\phantom{\Bigg)}}

\boxed{\phantom{\Bigg(}{\cal V}_{\textrm{\tiny R}}\big(X,-(\partial X)^{2}\big)=X^{-b}\frac{4A-2bBX^{b-1}(\partial X)^{2}+(2b-1)DX^{2b-2}(\partial X)^{4}}{-4B+4DX^{b-1}(\partial X)^{2}}\,.\phantom{\Bigg)}}

Non-generic cases for specific values of the constants

(A)dS ground states

\displaystyle K^{-}_{0}

\displaystyle=X^{3b-c}\,\big(B^{2}-4AD~X^{a-2b+c}\big)\Big(-\frac{B}{D}-\sqrt{\frac{B^{2}}{D^{2}}-4\frac{A}{D}X^{a-2b+c}}\Big)

\boxed{{\cal V}_{\textrm{\tiny(A)dS}}\big(X,-(\partial X)^{2}\big)=X\frac{4aA-2(a-2)BX^{-2}(\partial X)^{2}+(a-4)DX^{-4}(\partial X)^{4}}{-4B+4DX^{-2}(\partial X)^{2}}\,.}

5
Generalizations

There are three types of generalizations of the standard Minkowski-, Rindler-, and (A)dS-ground state models discussed in this work.

1.

Beyond standard models.
One can relax the convexity conditions imposed as standard. For instance, one could consider Minkowski ground state models (
29
) where the parameter
a
lies outside the interval
(0,1]
. Then either the weak coupling region,
X\to\infty
, no longer corresponds to large values of the radial coordinate,
r\to\infty
, or the Killing norm is dominated asymptotically by the terms containing the Casimir
C
. In the latter case, generic solutions no longer are asymptotically flat. Similar remarks apply to Rindler and (A)dS ground state models. In the restricted class of power-counting renormalizable models, this issue was addressed in
Grumiller:2014oha
;
Bagchi:2014ava
.

2.

Beyond maximally symmetric ground states.
One can give up the requirement of having a maximally symmetric ground state and study all members of the family of models defined by (
21
) with (
22
). It could be interesting to scan this model space by imposing as constraint reasonable thermodynamics, along the lines of section 4.2 in
Grumiller:2007ju
.

3.

Beyond quadratic ansatz.
One can extend the discussion beyond the 3-parameter family considered in the present work. In particular, generalizing the ansatz (
21
) to
C(X,Y)=\sum_{n=0}^{4}V_{n}(X)Y^{n}
yields explicit (if cumbersome) expressions, and all our results generalize straightforwardly to this case. Generalizations beyond these algebraic examples require new techniques or special choices of
C(X,Y)
.

Besides these generalizations, there are also some evident potential applications of the models studied in the present work. We list three examples for possible follow-up work and expect that some of these points will be addressed in the near future.

1.

Thermodynamics and holographic renormalization.
Presently, it is unclear which boundary terms have to be added to the bulk action (
1
) to render the variational principle well-defined. For the class of power-counting renormalizable models, these boundary terms were constructed in
Grumiller:2007ju
(using the Hamilton–Jacobi method, see e.g.
Papadimitriou:2010as
). They play an important role in thermodynamics since in the Euclidean version of the theory the Helmholtz free energy is determined from the (holographically renormalized) on-shell action. It could be rewarding to extend the thermodynamical discussion of
Grumiller:2007ju
to generic 2d dilaton gravity models (
1
), like in section 5 of
Grumiller:2021cwg
.

2.

Asymptotic symmetries and boundary excitations.
Models with maximally symmetric ground states could allow for some standard boundary conditions, using notions of locally asymptotically flat or locally asymptotically (A)dS spacetimes, along the lines of e.g.
Grumiller:2017qao
;
Grumiller:2021cwg
.

3.

SYK-like correspondences.
Given the relation between Schwarzian actions and AdS isometries (see
Maldacena:2016hyu
and the reviews
Mertens:2018fds
;
Sarosi:2017ykf
;
Gu:2019jub
) one might expect SYK-like models
Kitaev:15ur
;
Sachdev:1992fk
dual to the AdS ground state models. Similarly, by analogy to
Afshar:2019axx
Rindler ground state models could lead to a twisted warped boundary action.

Appendix — Relation between dilaton and radial coordinate

The relation between dilaton and radial coordinate,

\extdm\!r=e^{Q(X)}\,\extdm\!X

(43)

can be integrated in closed form in terms of elementary functions and the hypergeometric function
{}_{2}F_{1}
for all models considered in our work. Dropping the integration constant in
Q(X)
, we list now these results, using again the definition
\Delta=B^{2}-AD
and introducing the definition
M=-4CD/\Delta
.

For Minkowski ground state models, we obtain

r=\frac{2X\sqrt{\Delta(1-MX^{-a})}}{(a-2)(3a-2)M^{2}}\,\Big(\frac{2a}{a+2}\,X^{a}\,_{2}F_{1}(1,1+\tfrac{1}{a},\tfrac{3}{2}+\tfrac{1}{a};\,X^{a}/M)\\
+M\big(a+(2-a)MX^{-a}\big)\Big)

(44)

which for large values of
X
and
a\in(0,1]
expands as

r\propto X^{1-a}+\dots

(45)

For Rindler ground state models, we obtain

r=\frac{2}{3}\,\sqrt{\Delta}\,\Big(\frac{1}{M}\Big)^{1/2-b}\,(X-M)^{3/2}\,_{2}F_{1}(\tfrac{3}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}-b,\tfrac{5}{2};\,1-X/M)

(46)

which for large values of
X
and
b>-1
expands as

r\propto X^{1+b}+\dots

(47)

For (A)dS ground state models, we obtain

r=\frac{a\sqrt{\Delta(1-MX^{-a})}X^{a-1}}{(a-2)(a-1)}\,\Big(\,_{2}F_{1}(1,1-\tfrac{1}{a},\tfrac{3}{2}-\tfrac{1}{a};\,X^{a}/M)+1-\tfrac{2}{a}\Big)

(48)

which for large values of
X
and
a>1
expands as

r\propto X^{-1+a}+\dots

(49)

In all cases, the omitted proportionality constants are independent from
M
and can be adjusted conveniently by fixing the integration constant in
Q(X)
.

Maximal commutative unipotent subgroups and a characterization of affine spherical varieties
Andriy Regeta and Immanuel van Santen

Abstract
. We describe maximal commutative unipotent subgroups of the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( X ) \mathrm{Aut}(X) of an irreducible affine variety X X . Further we show that a group isomorphism Aut ⁡ ( X ) → Aut ⁡ ( Y ) \mathrm{Aut}(X)\to\mathrm{Aut}(Y) maps unipotent elements to unipotent elements, where Y Y is irreducible and affine. Using this result, we show that the automorphism group detects sphericity and the weight-monoid. As an application, we show that an affine toric variety different from an algebraic torus is determined by its automorphism group among normal irreducible affine varieties and we show that a smooth affine spherical variety different from an algebraic torus is determined by its automorphism group (up to an automorphism of the base field) among smooth irreducible affine varieties. This generalizes results obtained by Cantat, Kraft, Liendo, Urech, Xie and the authors.



1.
Introduction

We work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. Let
X
be an irreducible affine variety.
In this paper we study subgroups of the automorphism group
\Aut(X)
of
X
.
As an application we study the question of characterizing affine spherical varieties via
their automorphism groups. This question is well-studied in the context of differentiable manifold theory,
see e.g.
[
Whi63
,
Fil82
,
Ryb95
,
Ryb02
]
.

In
[
Sha66
]
Shafarevich introduced the notion of
an
ind-group
, which is an infinite-dimensional analogue of an algebraic group.
It is known that the automorphism group
\Aut(X)
has a natural structure of an
ind-group (see
[
FK18
, Section 5]
or Sect.
2
for details).
In particular
\Aut(X)

has a natural topology induced by this ind-group structure.
Through the whole paper we consider only
linear
algebraic groups and therefore we will drop
the adjective “linear”.

An algebraic group
G
consists only of unipotent elements if and only if the neutral element is the
only finite order element in
G
(one can use the Jordan decomposition of an element, see e.g.
[
Hum75
, Theorem 5.3]
). This statement holds as well for closed connected commutative
subgroups
G
in
\Aut(X)
, where
\varphi\in\Aut(X)
is called
unipotent
if there is an
algebraic action of the one-dimensional additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
on
X
such that
\varphi
lies in the image of
the natural induced homomorphism
\mathbb{G}_{a}\to\Aut(X)

(see
[
CRX23
, Theorem B]
and Corollary
2.2
).
We call a subgroup of
\Aut(X)

unipotent
if it is closed and consists only of unipotent elements,
and we call it
algebraic
if it is the image under the natural homomorphism induced by an algebraic action
of an algebraic group on
X
. Note that unipotent subgroups of
\Aut(X)
are always connected
and algebraic subgroups of
\Aut(X)
are always closed.

In this paper we describe commutative unipotent subgroups of

\Aut(X)
that are maximal among such subgroups. This could be seen as a first step
towards a systematic study of maximal unipotent (or solvable) subgroups of
\Aut(X)
.

In order to state our first result we have to introduce some notation.
Let
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
be a unipotent algebraic subgroup and let
u\in U\setminus\{\id_{X}\}
.
Let
\rho\colon\mathbb{G}_{a}\times X\to X
be a
\mathbb{G}_{a}
-action on
X
such that
\rho(1,x)=u(x)

for all
x\in X
. For a rational
U
-invariant function
f\in\textbf{k}(X)^{U}
denote by

\dom(f)\subseteq X
its domain. Then

f\cdot u\colon\dom(f)\to\dom(f)\,,\quad x\mapsto\rho(f(x),x)

defines an automorphism of
\dom(f)
and hence
f\cdot u
is an element of
the group of birational transformations
\Bir(X)
of
X
. We define

\displaystyle\Repl_{X}(U)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left\{\,f\cdot u\in\Aut(X)\ |\ f\in\textbf{k}[X]^{U},u\in U\,\right\}\subseteq\Aut(X)\,,

\displaystyle\rRepl_{X}(U)

\displaystyle\coloneqq

\displaystyle\left\{\,f\cdot u\in\Bir(X)\ |\ f\in\textbf{k}(X)^{U},u\in U\,\right\}\subseteq\Bir(X)\,.

The term
``\Repl"
stands for “replica” (see e.g.
[
AFK+13
]
) and
``\rRepl"
stands for
“rational replica”.
Denote by
\langle\rRepl_{X}(U)\rangle
the subgroup
generated by
\rRepl_{X}(U)
inside
\Bir(X)
and let

\A_{X}(U)\coloneqq\Aut(X)\cap\langle\rRepl_{X}(U)\rangle\subseteq\Bir(X)\,.

Theorem A

(see Corollary
5.2
)
.

Assume
X
is an irreducible affine variety and let
G
be a unipotent
commutative subgroup in
\Aut(X)
.
Then the following statements are equivalent:

a)

G
is maximal among commutative subgroups in
\Aut(X)
;

b)

G
is maximal among unipotent commutative subgroups in
\Aut(X)
;

c)

G=\A_{X}(U)

for some commutative unipotent algebraic subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
.

The proof of Theorem
A
depends heavily on the fact that the centralizer
of
\Repl_{X}(U)
is equal to
\A_{X}(U)
which we prove in Proposition
4.1
.
As a consequence of Theorem
A
we get:

Corollary B

(see Corollary
5.3
)
.

Assume
k
is uncountable. Let
X,Y
be irreducible affine varieties and let

\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism. Then

\theta
maps unipotent elements to unipotent elements.

Corollary
B
provides an important tool to study the following question when the objects under consideration are affine varieties.

Question
.

Let
X
,
Y
be objects in some category
\mathscr{C}
.
If there exists a group isomorphism

\theta\colon\Aut_{\mathscr{C}}(X)\to\Aut_{\mathscr{C}}(Y)
,
are then
X
,
Y
isomorphic in
\mathscr{C}
?

Of course, one cannot hope for an affirmative answer of this question in the whole
category of varieties, as the automorphism groups of varieties are trivial in general.
However, if
X
is rather special, over the field of complex numbers
we have affirmative answers in the following instances
(when the morphisms in the category
\mathscr{C}
are not specified, we consider morphisms of varieties;
some of the results hold over more general fields than the complex numbers):

i)

X=\mathbb{P}^{n}
is the projective space, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible projective
n
-dimensional varieties together
with rational maps; see
[
Can14
, Theorem C]
;

ii)

X=\mathbb{A}^{n}
is the affine space, where
n\geq 1
,

\mathscr{C}
is the category of connected affine varieties
and
\theta
is an ind-group homomorphism; see
[
Kra17
, Theorem 1.1]
;

iii)

X
is a Danielewski surface, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible smooth affine surfaces and
\theta
is an ind-group isomorphism; see
[
LRU22
, Theorem 1 and Remark 1]
;

iv)

X
is a Danielewski surface, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible affine varieties and
\theta
is an ind-group isomorphism;
see
[
Reg22
, Theorem 2]
;

v)

X
is an affine toric variety that is non-isomorphic to an algebraic torus, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible normal affine varieties
and
\theta
is an ind-group isomorphism;
see
[
LRU23
, Theorem 1.4]
;

vi)

X
is a smooth affine spherical variety that is
non-isomorphic to an algebraic torus,
where
\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible smooth affine varieties
and
\theta
is an ind-group isomorphism;
see
[
RvS21
, Theorem A]
;

vii)

X=\mathbb{A}^{n}
, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of smooth irreducible quasi-projective varieties of dimension
n

such that the Euler characteristic doesn’t vanish and the Picard group is finite;
see
[
KRvS21
, Theorem]
;

viii)

X
is an affine toric surface, where

\mathscr{C}
is the category of irreducible normal affine surfaces;
see
[
LRU23
, Theorem 1.3]
;

ix)

X=\mathbb{A}^{n}
, where
n\geq 1
and
\mathscr{C}
is the category of
connected affine varieties; see
[
CRX23
, Theorem A]
.

Note that the assumption that
\Aut(X)
,
\Aut(Y)
are isomorphic as abstract groups is much weaker than the assumption that these groups are isomorphic as ind-groups
(as it is assumed in cases
ii)
-
vi)
).
Indeed, e.g. the automorphism groups of
two generic Danielewski surfaces are isomorphic as abstract groups,
but non-isomorphic as ind-groups; see
[
LR22
, Theorem 3 and Remark 7]
).

In Theorem
C
and Corollary
D

below we generalize statements
ii)
,
v)
-
ix)

to affine smooth spherical (affine toric) varieties
when
\mathscr{C}
is the category of smooth (normal) irreducible affine varieties.

Let
X

be an irreducible affine variety endowed with a faithful algebraic group action of
a connected reductive algebraic group
G

and let
B\subseteq G
be a Borel subgroup. Then
X
is called
G
-spherical
if

X
is normal and
B
acts with a dense open orbit on
X
.
We denote by
\mathfrak{X}(B)
the character group of
B
, i.e. the group of
algebraic group homomorphisms
B\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
The
weight monoid
of
X
is defined by

\Lambda_{B}^{+}(X)\coloneqq\{\,\lambda\in\mathfrak{X}(B)\ |\ \textbf{k}[X]_{\lambda}^{(B)}\neq 0\,\}\,,

where
\textbf{k}[X]_{\lambda}^{(B)}\subset\textbf{k}[X]

denotes the subspace of
B
-semi-invariants of weight
\lambda

of the coordinate ring
\textbf{k}[X]
.

In order to simplify the formulation, we state Theorem
C

only when
X
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
(we give a more general version in Theorem
9.1
).
Assume that
X
is a closed subvariety of some affine space
\mathbb{A}^{n}
and assume that

X
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, i.e.
X
is the zero set in
\mathbb{A}^{n}
of some polynomials with coefficients in
\mathbb{Q}
.
If
\tau
is a field automorphism of

k
, then the map
\mathbb{A}^{n}\to\mathbb{A}^{n}
,
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\mapsto(\tau(a_{1}),\ldots,\tau(a_{n}))

sends
X
onto itself and induces thus a map

\tau_{X}\colon X\to X

(in fact
\tau_{X}
only depends on
X
, not on the embedding of
X
into
\mathbb{A}^{n}
).
If
\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
is a group isomorphism, then

\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)\,,\quad\varphi\mapsto\theta(\tau_{X}^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\tau_{X})

is again a group isomorphism. This is the reason why field automorphisms
of
k
appear in the next theorem:

Theorem C
.

Assume that
k
is uncountable, let
X
,
Y
be irreducible affine varieties, where
X
is
not isomorphic to an algebraic torus, let

\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism
and let
G\subseteq\Aut(X)
be a connected reductive
algebraic subgroup.
If
X
is
G
-spherical and if
X
,
G
and the action of
G
on
X
are
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then there
exists a field automorphism
\tau
of
k
such that:

(1)

The image
\theta(G)
is an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
and

\theta|_{G}\circ\tau_{G}\colon G\to\theta(G)
is an isomorphism of algebraic groups.

For the following statements we endow
Y
with the
G
-action induced by
\theta|_{G}\circ\tau_{G}

and assume in addition that
Y
is normal.

(2)

The variety
Y
is
G
-spherical.

(3)

If
B\subseteq G
is a Borel subgroup defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then
the weight monoids
\Lambda^{+}_{B}(X)
and
\Lambda^{+}_{B}(Y)

are the same inside
\mathfrak{X}(B)
.

(4)

If
X
and
Y
are smooth, then they are
G
-equivariantly isomorphic.

Remark 1.1
.

In fact (up to isomorphism) every connected reductive algebraic group is defined over
\mathbb{Q}

by
[
DG11
, Exp. XXV, Corollaire 1.3 and Exp. XXII, Corollaire 2.4]
.
To the authors’ knowledge, it is not known, whether every (smooth) affine spherical variety is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

The proof of Theorem
C
is given at the end of Sect.
9
. As we already mentioned, it depends on
the fact that unipotent elements are mapped onto unipotent elements via
the group isomorphism
\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
. Moreover, we also prove that the
root subgroups of
\Aut(X)
correspond bijectively to the root subgroups of
\Aut(Y)
via
\theta

(see Theorem
8.5
). This
is another major ingredient in the proof of Theorem
C
.

For the following consequence of Theorem
C
, recall that a
toric variety is a
G
-spherical variety, where
G
is an algebraic torus.
We prove it at the end of Sect.
9
.

Corollary D
.

Assume that
k
is uncountable, let
X,Y
be irreducible normal
affine varieties such that
\Aut(X)
and
\Aut(Y)
are isomorphic as abstract groups.
If
X
is toric and non-isomorphic to an algebraic torus, then
Y
is isomorphic to
X
. ∎

Remark

(see Remark
9.2
)
.

In case
X,Y
are both affine toric varieties and
\Aut(X)
,
\Aut(Y)
are isomorphic as groups,
then
X,Y
are isomorphic as toric varieties.

Corollary
D
fails
if
X
is an algebraic torus:
For every irreducible smooth affine curve
C
with trivial
\Aut(C)

that admits no non-constant
invertible regular function
C\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
we get that

\Aut(X)
and
\Aut(X\times C)
are isomorphic by
[
LRU23
, Example 6.17]
.
However, if we assume that

\dim Y\leq\dim X
, then
Corollary
D
holds for an algebraic torus
X
as well (the proof is given
at the end of Sec.
10
):

Theorem E
.

Assume that
k
is uncountable.
Let
X
be an algebraic torus and let

Y
be an irreducible normal affine variety such that

\Aut(X),\Aut(Y)
are isomorphic as groups and
\dim Y\leq\dim X
.
Then
X
,
Y
are isomorphic as varieties.

If we replace in Corollary
D
the assumption that
X
,
Y

are affine by the assumption that
X
,

are projective, then Corollary

fails.
We give counterexamples in Sec.
11
.
Moreover, the normality assumption in Corollary

is also necessary: There exists an irreducible non-normal

such that its normalization

is an affine

and the normalization map
\tilde{Y}\to Y
induces a group isomorphism

, see
[

, Proposition 9.1]
and
[

, Theorem 4.5]
.
Also the irreducibility of

is any

2.
Preliminary results on ind-groups and algebraic subgroups

We refer to the survey
[
FK18
]
for the basic notions and results on ind-varieties and ind-groups. An
ind-variety

Z
is a set together with a countable
increasing filtration
of closed subvarieties

Z_{1}\subseteq Z_{2}\subseteq Z_{3}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq Z

such that
Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}Z_{i}
.
The ind-variety
Z
is called
affine
if
Z_{i}
is affine for all
i\geq 1
.
Declaring a subset
A\subseteq Z
to be
closed
,
if
A\cap Z_{i}
is closed in
Z_{i}
for all
i\geq 1
, turns
Z
into a topological space. Moreover,
a subset
S\subseteq Z
is called a
closed subvariety
of
Z
if
there exists
i\geq 1
such that
S\subseteq Z_{i}
and
S
is a closed subvariety in
Z_{i}
.
A
morphism of ind-varieties
is a map

f\colon Z=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}Z_{i}\to W=\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}W_{j}

such that for all
i\geq 1
there exists
j=j(i)\geq 1
with
f(Z_{i})\subseteq W_{j}
and

f|_{Z_{i}}\colon Z_{i}\to W_{j}
is a morphism of varieties.

An
ind-group
is a group
G
that is at the same time an affine
ind-variety and the group operations
are morphisms of ind-varieties. We denote by
G^{\circ}
the connected component of an ind-variety
G

that contains the neutral element. It is a countable index subgroup in
G
.
An
ind-group homomorphism

G\to H

is a homomorphism of groups that is at the same time a morphism of ind-varieties.

If
X
is an affine variety, then
\Aut(X)
admits in a natural way the structure of an ind-group
such that for all algebraic groups
G
the
algebraic group actions of
G
on
X
correspond to the ind-group
homomorphisms
G\to\Aut(X)
. The images of the ind-group homomorphisms

G\to\Aut(X)
are called
algebraic subgroups
of
\Aut(X)
.
It then turns out that a subgroup in
\Aut(X)
is algebraic if and only
if it is a closed subvariety in
\Aut(X)
.
For a subset
S\subseteq\Aut(X)
, we denote by
\langle S\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)

the subgroup of
\Aut(X)
that is generated by
S
.

All our theorems heavily depend on the following result due to Cantat, Xie and the first author:

Theorem 2.1

(see
[
CRX23
, Theorem B]
)
.

Let
X
be an affine variety and let
V
be an irreducible closed subvariety of
\Aut(X)

such that

\id_{X}\in V\quad\textrm{and}\quad\varphi\circ\psi=\psi\circ\varphi\quad\textrm{for all $\varphi,\psi\in V$}\,.

Then
\langle V\rangle\subseteq\Aut(X)
is an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(X)
. ∎

As a consequence of this theorem we may write closed commutative connected subgroups
in the automorphism group of an affine variety in a very nice form:

Corollary 2.2

(
[
CRX23
, Remark 1.1]
)
.

Let
X
be an affine variety and let

G
be a closed commutative connected subgroup of
\Aut(X)
. Then
G

is the countable union of an increasing
filtration by commutative connected algebraic subgroups of
\Aut(X)
.

Proof.

By
[
FK18
, Remark 2.2.3 and Proposition 1.6.3]

there exists an increasing filtration
by irreducible subvarieties
V_{1}\subseteq V_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G
such that

G=\bigcup_{k}V_{k}
and
\id_{X}\in V_{k}
for all
k\geq 1
. By Theorem
2.1
,
the subgroup
G_{k}\coloneqq\langle V_{k}\rangle
of
\Aut(X)
is algebraic for all
k\geq 1
.
By
[
Hum75
, §7.5 Proposition]
,
G_{k}
is connected for all
k\geq 1
.
Now,
G_{1}\subseteq G_{2}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq G
is our desired filtration.
∎

Let
H\subseteq\Aut(X)
be a subgroup, where
X
is an irreducible affine variety.
A non-trivial commutative unipotent algebraic subgroup
V
in
\Aut(X)
is a

generalized
H
-root subgroup of weight
\lambda\colon H\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
if for some (and hence every)
isomorphism of algebraic groups
\varepsilon\colon\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\dim V}\to V
we have

h\circ\varepsilon(s)\circ h^{-1}=\varepsilon(\lambda(h)\cdot s)\quad\textrm{for all $s\in\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\dim V}$ and all $h\in H$}\,.

In case
H
is an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(X)

the abstract group homomorphism
\lambda\colon H\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
is a homomorphism of algebraic groups.
In case
\lambda\colon H\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
is the trivial weight, we say that

V
is a
trivial generalized
H
-root subgroup
.
An
H
-root subgroup
is just a generalized
H
-root subgroup
V
where
\dim V=1
.

3.
Notation and setup concerning affine toric varieties

Throughout this section,
T
denotes an algebraic torus of dimension

n\geq 1
and
X
denotes an affine
T
-toric variety, i.e.
X
is a normal affine variety
endowed with a faithful
T
-action that admits a dense open orbit.
We see
T
as an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(X)
via the fixed
T
-action on
X
.
Moreover, we fix
x_{0}
in the open
T
-orbit of
X
and denote by

\eta_{x_{0}}\colon T\to X
,
t\mapsto tx_{0}
the orbit map with respect to
x_{0}
.
Hence,
\eta_{x_{0}}
is an open embedding of
T
into
X
.

We refer the reader to
[
Ful93
]

for the basic notions of affine toric varieties.

3.1.
General notions

Denote by
M=\mathfrak{X}(T)

the character group of
T
(which is additively written) and by
N\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(M,\mathbb{Z})
the group
of one-parameter subgroups of
T
. Moreover, let
M_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq M\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
,

N_{\mathbb{R}}\coloneqq N\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(N,\mathbb{R})
and denote by

M_{\mathbb{R}}\times N_{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}\,,\quad(u,v)\mapsto\langle u,v\rangle\coloneqq v(u)

the natural perfect pairing between
M_{\mathbb{R}}
and
N_{\mathbb{R}}
.
There exists a unique
strongly convex rational polyhedral cone
\sigma\subseteq N_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
its dual
\sigma^{\vee}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies

\textbf{k}[X]=\textbf{k}[\sigma^{\vee}\cap M]\subseteq\textbf{k}[M]=\textbf{k}[T]\,,

where
\textbf{k}[X]
denotes the coordinate ring of
X
,

\textbf{k}[M]
denotes the group algebra of
M
over
k
and the
inclusion
\textbf{k}[X]\subseteq\textbf{k}[M]
is given by the comorphism
of the open embedding
\eta_{x_{0}}\colon T\to X
. Hence,

\textbf{k}[X]
is the subalgebra of those functions in
\textbf{k}[M]
that extend to
X

via the open embedding
\eta_{x_{0}}\colon T\to X
.

If
\rho
is an extremal ray of
\sigma
, then
\rho^{\perp}\subseteq M_{\mathbb{R}}
denotes the subset of
all
u\in M_{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\langle u,v\rangle=0
for all
v\in\rho
.

3.2.
Weights of
T
-root subgroups

For an extremal ray
\rho\subseteq\sigma

define

S_{\rho}\coloneqq\left\{\,m\in(\sigma_{\rho})^{\vee}\cap M\ |\ \langle m,v_{\rho}\rangle=-1\,\right\}\subseteq M\,,

where
\sigma_{\rho}
denotes the rational convex polyhedral cone in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
spanned by
all the extremal rays of
\sigma
except
\rho
and
v_{\rho}
denotes the unique primitive
vector inside
\rho\cap N
.
If
\rho_{1},\ldots,\rho_{r}
denote the extremal rays
of
\sigma
, then
S_{\rho_{1}},\ldots,S_{\rho_{r}}
are pairwise disjoint, the union is equal to
the set of weights of
T
-root subgroups
and for every such weight
e
there exists only one
T
-root subgroup
of weight
e
, see
[
Lie10
, Lemma 2.6, Theorem 2.7]
.

3.3.
Basic results on affine toric varieties

In this section we gather some basic results on affine toric varieties, which will be
used later in this paper.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\rho
be an extremal ray of
\sigma
. Then the group generated by

\sigma^{\vee}\cap\rho^{\bot}\cap M
is equal to
\rho^{\bot}\cap M
.

Proof.

Let
S\subseteq\rho^{\bot}\cap M
be the subgroup generated by
\sigma^{\vee}\cap\rho^{\bot}\cap M
.
Let
m\in\rho^{\bot}\cap M
. Take

m_{1}\in M
in the relative interior of
\sigma^{\vee}\cap\rho^{\bot}
.
Then, there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that
\langle m+tm_{1},v_{\mu}\rangle\geq 0
for all extremal rays
\mu

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

in

K\coloneqq\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\ker(e_{i})\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}^{n-r}\times F\quad\textrm{and}\quad\mathfrak{X}(K)\simeq M/\Span_{\mathbb{Z}}\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{k}\}

Proof.

Consider the homomorphism

of algebraic groups.
The exact sequence

induces an exact sequence between character groups

\mathfrak{X}(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{k})\mathrel{{\mathop{\to}\limits}}M\to\mathfrak{X}(K)\to 0
, as

forms a basis of

for all diagonalizable algebraic groups

Writing

identifies with

.

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let

Lemma 3.4

Lemma 3.5

4.
Results on centralizers in an automorphism group

In this section we study centralizers of certain closed subgroups in the automorphism group of an irreducible affine variety
with a particular emphasis on centralizers of commutative unipotent subgroups.
The definitions of
\Repl_{X}(U)
,
\rRepl_{X}(U)
and
\A_{X}(U)
were given in the introduction.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
X
be an irreducible affine variety and let
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
be a commutative unipotent algebraic subgroup.
Then
\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\Repl_{X}(U))=\A_{X}(U)
and
\A_{X}(U)
is commutative.
In particular,
\A_{X}(U)
is maximal with respect to be a commutative subgroup of
\Aut(X)

and
\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\A_{X}(U))=\A_{X}(U)
.

As
\A_{X}(U)
is a commutative subgroup of
\Aut(X)
that contains
\Repl_{X}(U)
we have

\A_{X}(U)\subseteq\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\Repl_{X}(U))\,.

Thus we only have to prove the reverse inclusion. For the proof we need the following result
about the generic structure of the algebraic quotient by a unipotent algebraic group action on an irreducible
affine variety:

Proposition 4.2

(
[
GP93
, Proposition 1.6]
)
.

Let
U
be a unipotent algebraic group that acts on an irreducible affine variety
X
and let

\pi\colon X\to X/\!\!/U\coloneqq\Spec(\textbf{k}[X]^{U})

be the algebraic quotient. Then there exists a dense open
U
-invariant subset
X^{s}\subseteq X
such that

\pi(X^{s})
is an open dense subset in
X/\!\!/U
,
\pi|_{X^{s}}\colon X^{s}\to\pi(X^{s})

is a geometric quotient for the
U
-action on
X^{s}
and the following universal property is satisfied:
For all open subsets
V\subseteq X/\!\!/U
such that
the restriction

\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}\colon\pi^{-1}(V)\to V

is a geometric quotient for the
U
-action on
\pi^{-1}(V)
we have

\pi^{-1}(V)\subseteq X^{s}
.

Remark 4.3
.

By
[
GP93
, Remarks 1.7(1)]

it follows that there exists an open dense subset
V\subseteq X/\!\!/U
such that

\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(V)}\colon\pi^{-1}(V)\to V
is a geometric quotient for the
U
-action
on
\pi^{-1}(V)
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

As
U
is a commutative unipotent algebraic group we may identify it with a

-vector space where the
group-operation is given by addition and the closed subgroups of

are the
k
-subspaces.

Let
\varphi\in\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\Repl_{X}(U))
. We have to shows that
\varphi\in\A_{X}(U)
.

Claim 1
.

For all
g\in\textbf{k}[X]^{U}
we have
\varphi^{\ast}(g)=g
. In particular,
\varphi
fixes the fibres of
the algebraic quotient
\pi\colon X\to X/\!\!/U
.

Indeed, let
D_{u}
be the locally nilpotent derivation of
\textbf{k}[X]
associated to some

.
Hence
D_{u}
is non-zero and thus we may choose
h\in\textbf{k}[X]
with
D_{u}(h)\neq 0
. This gives
for all
g\in\textbf{k}[X]^{U}

\displaystyle g\varphi^{\ast}(D_{u}(h))=(gD_{u})(\varphi^{\ast}(h))=(\varphi^{\ast}\circ gD_{u})(h)=\varphi^{\ast}(g)\varphi^{\ast}(D_{u}(h))\,,

since
\varphi
commutes with
u
and
g\cdot u
. Since
\varphi^{\ast}(D_{u}(h))\neq 0
, the claim follows.

By Proposition
4.2
and Remark
4.3
,
there exists an open dense subset

B\subseteq X/\!\!/U
, such that the restriction

\pi_{0}\coloneqq\pi|_{\pi^{-1}(B)}\colon\pi^{-1}(B)\to B
is a geometric quotient and there exists an integer
n\geq 0
and
an isomorphism
\rho\colon\mathbb{A}^{n}\times B\to\pi^{-1}(B)
such that the composition

\pi_{0}\circ\rho\colon\mathbb{A}^{n}\times B\to B
is
the canonical projection to
B
. As
\varphi
fixes the fibres of
\pi
(see Claim
1
), it follows that

\varphi(\pi^{-1}(B))=\pi^{-1}(B)
. By shrinking

we may further assume that

is smooth and affine.

Let
b_{0}\in B
,
x_{0}=\rho(0,b_{0})\in\pi^{-1}(B)
and denote by

the stabilizer of

in

.
As

is a commutative unipotent group, there exists a closed subgroup

such that

and
U^{\prime}\cap U_{x_{0}}=\{0\}
.
In particular (as
\pi_{0}
is a geometric quotient),
\dim U^{\prime}=n
. Consider the following closed subvariety of

E\coloneqq\left\{\,(u^{\prime},b)\in U^{\prime}\times B\ |\ u^{\prime}\rho(0,b)=\rho(0,b)\,\right\}

and the projection

Then every fibre of

is irreducible and
p^{-1}(b_{0})=\{(0,b_{0})\}
.
Let

be the dimension of a general fibre of

(note that

is irreducible and thus such a

Let

and this map is dominant. As all fibres of

are irreducible, there exists an open dense subset

with

.
Since

and since

is closed in

, we get

\{0\}\times B\subseteq F
. Hence,
p^{-1}(b_{0})=\{(0,b_{0})\}\subseteq F
and thus

has at least one fibre of dimension

By the semi-continuity of the fibre dimension
[
GW20
, Theorem 14.112]
,
it follows that

(since

is irreducible). Hence, we may shrink

in such a way that

. This implies that

\eta\colon U^{\prime}\times B\to\pi^{-1}(B)\,,\quad(u^{\prime},b)\mapsto u^{\prime}\rho(0,b)

is a bijective

-equivariant morphism and since

that

-equivariant isomorphism.

Then

is an automorphism of

that fixes the fibres of the canonical projection

. Since
\varphi
commutes with the

commutes with the
U^{\prime}
-action on

for all

Since

\displaystyle\varphi(\eta(0,b))=\eta(\psi(0,b))

Remark 4.4
.

Lemma 4.5

from Sect.

As a consequence, we show for irreducible affine varieties

is uncountable the following: under a group isomorphism

, unipotent elements are mapped onto unipotent elements (see Corollary

Lemma 5.1
.

Assume

is an irreducible affine variety and let

be a closed commutative unipotent subgroup of
\Aut(X)
. Then there exists
a (commutative unipotent) algebraic subgroup
U\subseteq G
such that

and

\textbf{k}[X]^{U}=\textbf{k}[X]^{G}
.

Proof.

Write
G=\bigcup_{i\geq 0}G_{i}
, where
G_{i}
is a commutative unipotent algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(X)
,
see Corollary
2.2
.
Take successive algebraic subgroups
U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}\subseteq\cdots
inside
G
such that

\dim U_{i}=i
and
\textbf{k}[X]^{U_{i}}\subsetneq\textbf{k}[X]^{U_{i-1}}
for all
i\geq
1. By
[
Fre17
, Priniciple 11(e)]

we have that the Krull dimension of
\textbf{k}[X]^{U_{i-1}}
is one more than that of
\textbf{k}[X]^{U_{i}}
. Hence the sequence

U_{1}\subseteq U_{2}\subseteq\cdots
stops with some
U\coloneqq U_{k}
and
\textbf{k}[X]^{U}=\textbf{k}[X]^{G}
.
Therefore
\textbf{k}(X)^{U}=Q(\textbf{k}[X]^{U})=Q(\textbf{k}[X]^{G})
and every element
r\cdot u
with
u\in U
and
r\in\textbf{k}(X)^{U}
commutes with
G
.
This shows that
\A_{X}(U)
commutes with
G
. Because
\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\A_{X}(U))=\A_{X}(U)
(by Proposition
4.1
) it follows that

G\subseteq\A_{X}(U)
.
∎

Corollary 5.2
.

Assume
X
is an irreducible affine variety and let
G
be a commutative unipotent subgroup
in
\Aut(X)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

a)

G
is maximal among commutative subgroups in
\Aut(X)
;

b)

G
is maximal among commutative unipotent subgroups in
\Aut(X)
;

c)

G=\A_{X}(U)
for some commutative unipotent algebraic subgroup
U\subseteq\Aut(X)
.

Proof.

a)

\Rightarrow

b)
is obvious,
b)

\Rightarrow

c)
follows from
Lemma
5.1
and Remark
4.4
, and
c)

\Rightarrow

a)
follows from
Proposition
4.1
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Assume that
k
is uncountable. Let

X,Y
be irreducible affine varieties and let
\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism.
Then
\theta
maps unipotent elements to unipotent elements.

Proof.

Assume
u\in\Aut(X)
is a unipotent element, i.e.
U\coloneqq\overline{\langle u\rangle}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}
,
where the closure is taken inside
\Aut(X)
.
Since
\A_{X}(U)
is equal to its own centralizer in
\Aut(X)
(see Proposition
4.1
),
the image
\theta(\A_{X}(U))
is a closed commutative subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
.
As
\A_{X}(U)
contains no non-trivial element of finite order, the connected component

\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}
is a commutative unipotent subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
.

Claim 2
.

The subgroup
\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}
of
\Aut(Y)

is maximal among commutative connected subgroups of
\Aut(Y)
.

Indeed, let
H
be a commutative connected subgroup
of
\Aut(Y)
that contains
\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}
. Then
\theta^{-1}(H)
is a commutative subgroup
of
\Aut(X)
that contains
S\coloneqq\theta^{-1}(\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ})
. As
S

has countable index in
\A_{X}(U)
, since
\A_{X}(U)
is connected, and since
k
is uncountable,
it follows that
S
is dense in
\A_{X}(U)
, see e.g.
[
FK18
, Corollary 2.2.2]
.
Hence

\theta^{-1}(H)\subseteq\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(S)=\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(\A_{X}(U)){\mathrel{\to}^{\textrm{Prop.\penalty\ \ref{Prop.centralizer}}}}\A_{X}(U)\,.

As
H
is connected we get thus
H\subseteq\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}
.
This proves Claim
2
.

Using Claim

and Corollary

it follows that
\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}
is maximal among
commutative
subgroups of
\Aut(Y)
and hence
\theta(\A_{X}(U))^{\circ}=\theta(R_{X}(U))
. This implies that

\theta(u)
is unipotent in
\Aut(Y)
. ∎

Proof.

Let

. Note that

-weight of

Hence,

and by assumption,

Hence, there exists an isomorphism of algebraic groups

Then

For

∎

8.
Abstract group isomorphisms of automorphism groups

The aim of this section is to show that in certain situations
an abstract group isomorphism
\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
(where
X,Y
are irreducible and affine)
preserves certain algebraic subgroups.
Our main result in this direction is Theorem
8.5
.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
k
be uncountable, let
X,Y
be irreducible affine varieties and let

\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism.
Assume that
H\subseteq\Aut(X)
is a connected algebraic subgroup and let
V\subseteq\Aut(X)

be a non-trivial generalized
H
-root subgroup.
If

a)

\theta(H)
is an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
or

b)

H
has a dense open orbit in
X
,

then
\theta(V)\subseteq\Aut(Y)
is a non-trivial
\theta(H)
-root subgroup
of
\Aut(Y)
of the same dimension as
V
.

The lemma above is the key ingredient
in the proof of Proposition
8.3
and in the proof of
Theorem
8.5
(which itself constitutes a crucial step in the
proof of Theorem
C
).

Proof of Proposition
8.1
.

We establish first the statement in case
\dim V=1
. For this, we establish the following:

Claim 4
.

The subgroup
\theta(V)
is closed in
\Aut(Y)
.

Indeed, in case
a)
,
fix some
v\in V\setminus\{\id_{X}\}
. Then
H\to V\setminus\{\id_{X}\}
,

h\mapsto h\circ v\circ h^{-1}
is surjective, since
H
does not commute with
V
, and the map

(3)

\theta(H)\coprod\{\ast\}\to\theta(V)\,,\quad g\mapsto\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}g\circ\theta(v)\circ g^{-1}&\textrm{if $g\in\theta(H)$}\\
\id_{Y}&\textrm{if $g=\ast$}\end{array}\right.

is surjective as well. Since
\theta(H)
is an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
,
(
3
) is a morphism of a
variety into
\Aut(Y)
with image
\theta(V)
.
Hence
\theta(V)
is
an algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
, see

[
KRvS21
, Theorem 2.9]
. In particular,
\theta(V)
is closed
in
\Aut(Y)
. In case
b)
, the claim follows from
Lemma
4.5
.

Since
V
is a non-trivial
H
-root subgroup and since
H
is connected,
there exists a closed subgroup
\mathbb{G}_{m}\simeq D\subseteq H
that acts by conjugation on
V
with two orbits.
Thus
\theta(D)
acts with two orbits on
\theta(V)
by conjugation.
Let us write

\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}K_{i}\,,

where
K_{i}
is a commutative connected algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
for all
i\geq 1
.

By Claim
4
we know that
\theta(V)

is closed in
\Aut(Y)
and by Corollary
5.3

\theta(V)
consists of unipotent elements.
In particular
\theta(V)
is connected and by Corollary
2.2
we may write

\theta(V)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}U_{i}

where
U_{i}\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}^{k_{i}}
for all
i\geq 1
.
Let
U_{i}^{\prime}
be the closure of the group generated by all
K_{i}
-conjugates
of all elements of
U_{i}
. Then
U_{i}^{\prime}
is a closed subgroup of
U_{j(i)}
for some

j(i) \geq 1
. Hence we may replace every
U_{i}
by
U_{i}^{\prime}
and assume that
K_{i}
normalizes
U_{i}
.

Let
K\subseteq\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}
be the subgroup of elements that do commute with all
elements in
\theta(V)
.

Claim 5
.

\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}/K
acts on
\theta(V)
by conjugation and the stabilizer
of each element in
\theta(V)\setminus\{\id_{Y}\}
by this action is trivial.

Indeed, let
g\in\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}

with
g\circ\theta(v)\circ g^{-1}=\theta(v)
for some
\id_{X}\neq v\in V
. Then

\theta^{-1}(g)\circ v\circ\theta^{-1}(g^{-1})=v
and
hence,
a\cdot v
commutes with
\theta^{-1}(g)
for all
a\in\mathbb{Z}
. As
\mathbb{G}_{a}\simeq V
this
implies that
\theta^{-1}(g)
commutes with all elements of
V
. Hence
g
commutes with all
elements of
\theta(V)
, i.e.
g\in K
. This proves Claim
5
.

If
K_{i}/(K\cap K_{i})
contains a copy of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
for some
i\geq 1
, then by the Lie-Kolchin theorem
applied to the action by conjugation of this copy on
U_{i}
(see e.g.
[
Hum75
, Theorem 17.5]
),
this copy would be contained in the stabilizer of some non-trivial element of
U_{i}
, contradiction to
Claim
5
. Hence for all
i\geq 1
there exists
d_{i}\geq 0
such that

K_{i}/(K\cap K_{i})\simeq\mathbb{G}_{m}^{d_{i}}
.
Since
\theta(D)
acts with two orbits on
\theta(V)
by conjugation, it follows that
\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}

acts with at most countably many orbits on
\theta(V)
by conjugation. As
k
is uncountable, this implies that
\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}/K
is non-trivial
and thus
d_{i}\geq 1
for all
i
that are big enough.

Claim 6
.

We claim that
d_{i}=1
for all
i
that are big enough.

Indeed, if
d_{i}\geq 2
for some
i
, then there exists a subgroup
F_{i}\subseteq K_{i}/(K\cap K_{i})
that
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}
. Let
\pi_{i}\colon K_{i}\to K_{i}/(K\cap K_{i})
be the canonical projection.
Then

K\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(F_{i})=\left\{\,g\in\pi_{i}^{-1}(F_{i})\ |\ \textrm{$g$ commutes with all elements of $\theta(V)$}\,\right\}\,.

Hence,
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{2}\simeq\theta^{-1}(\pi_{i}^{-1}(F_{i}))/\theta^{-1}(K\cap\pi_{i}^{-1}(F_{i}))
acts faithfully on
V\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}
, contradiction.

By Claim
6
we get that the ind-group

\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}/K\simeq\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}K_{i}/(K\cap K_{i})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{m}
.
Since
\overline{\theta(D)}^{\circ}/K
acts via conjugation with at most countably many orbits on
\theta(V)
,
we conclude that
\theta(V)\simeq\mathbb{G}_{a}
, as
k
is uncountable,
see
[
FK18
, Lemma 1.3.1]
.

Now assume that
V\subset\Aut(X)
has arbitrary dimension. Then we may write
V
as a product
V=V_{1}\cdots V_{n}

in
\Aut(X)
for
H
-roots subgroups
V_{1},\ldots,V_{n}\subseteq\Aut(X)

and the multiplication map
V_{1}\times\cdots\times V_{n}\to V
is an isomorphism of algebraic groups.
By the proof above
\theta(V_{i})\subseteq\Aut(Y)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{a}
. Hence,

\theta(V)=\theta(V_{1})\cdots\theta(V_{n})
is a closed algebraic subgroup of
\Aut(Y)
and the
multiplication map
\theta(V_{1})\times\cdots\times\theta(V_{n})\to\theta(V)
is an isomorphism of algebraic groups.
Thus
\theta(V)
is isomorphic to
V
as an algebraic group.
∎

Lemma 8.2
.

Assume
k
is uncountable, let
X,Y
be irreducible affine varieties,
let
\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism
and let
D\subset\Aut(X)
be a diagonalizable subgroup such that
\theta(D)\subseteq\Aut(Y)
is closed. Then
\theta(D)^{\circ}
is a torus of dimension
\leq\dim D
.

Proof.

Since
\theta(D)\subset\Aut(Y)
is a closed subgroup,
\theta(D)^{\circ}\subset\Aut(Y)
is a closed connected subgroup. If
\theta(D)^{\circ}
would contain a
non-trivial unipotent element, then
\theta^{-1}(\theta(D)^{\circ})\subset D
would also contain a non-trivial unipotent element by Corollary
5.3
which is not the case (here we use that
k
is uncountable). Hence, every unipotent element in
\theta(D)^{\circ}
is trivial and
we conclude that
\theta(D)^{\circ}
is an algebraic subtorus of
\Aut(Y)
by Corollary
2.2
.
Since
the
p
-torsion of
\theta(D)
is isomorphic to
(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})^{\dim D}

for every prime

, we get
\dim\theta(D)^{\circ}\leq\dim D
.
∎

Proposition 8.3
.

Assume

is uncountable.
Let

be
G

of

Moreover, let
\theta\colon\Aut(X)\to\Aut(Y)

Then
\theta(\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(G)^{\circ})

of the same dimension

\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(G)^{\circ}

\theta(\Cent_{\Aut(X)}(G)^{\circ})=\Cent_{\Aut(Y)}(\theta(G))^{\circ}\,.

Let

we get that

\Aut(X)
. Denote

.

Due to Proposition

(

For all

Since
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
is finitely generated,
\Gamma
is finitely generated
as well by Schreier‘s lemma; see e.g.
[
Ser03
, Lemma 4.2.1]
.
Hence, we may find a subfield
\textbf{k}_{0}
of
k
that is finitely generated
over
\mathbb{Q}
such that there is an irreducible
quasi-affine variety
Z_{0}
, defined over the algebraic closure
\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}
with the following properties:

—

Z=Z_{0}\times_{\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}}\textbf{k}
(note that
\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}
embeds into
k
, as
k
is algebraically closed);

—

\Gamma
embeds into
\Aut_{\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}}(Z_{0})
.

As
\textbf{k}_{0}
is finitely generated over
\mathbb{Q}
, it embeds into
\mathbb{C}

and therefore
\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}
embeds into
\mathbb{C}
as well.
By
[
CX18
, Theorem A]
we get thus

\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z_{0}\times_{\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}}\mathbb{C}\geq n
. Since

\dim_{\textbf{k}}Z=\dim_{\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}}Z_{0}=\dim_{\mathbb{C}}Z_{0}\times_{\overline{\textbf{k}_{0}}}\mathbb{C}
,
the statement follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
E
.

Let
X=T
be a torus of dimension
n
.
We may assume
n\geq 1
.
For every prime
p
, denote by
\Gamma_{p}
the kernel of the natural homomorphism

\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})\to\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
. Note that
\Gamma_{p}
has finite index in

\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
for all primes
p
.
Since
\bigcap_{i}\Gamma_{p_{i}}
is the trivial group for all infinite sequences of primes

p_{1},p_{2},\ldots
, for every finite subgroup

F\subseteq\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
there exists a prime
p
with

(8)

F\cap\Gamma_{p}=\{I\}\,,

where
I
denotes the identity matrix in
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\theta\colon\Aut(T)\to\Aut(Y)
be a group isomorphism.
As
T
is its own centralizer in
\Aut(T)
(see Proposition
4.6
(
3
)), it follows
that
\theta(T)
is closed in
\Aut(Y)
. Hence,
\theta(T)^{\circ}

is a closed subtorus in
\Aut(Y)
of dimension
\leq n
by Lemma
8.2
.

Consider the natural group isomorphism

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\GL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})&\to&\Aut_{\grp}(T)\,,\\
(a_{ij})_{ij}&\mapsto&\left((t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\mapsto\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{1i}},\ldots,\prod_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}^{a_{ni}}\right)\right)\,,\end{array}

where
\Aut_{\grp}(T)
denotes the group of all algebraic group automorphisms of
T
.
Note that this homomorphism is induced by the action by conjugation of

\Aut_{\grp}(T)
on
T
inside
\Aut(T)
.
Using the isomorphism above, we may see
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
as a subgroup of
\Aut(T)

and this subgroup normalizes
T
. Hence,
\theta(\SL_{n})
normalizes
\theta(T)^{\circ}
.
We distinguish three cases, depending on the dimension of
\theta(T)^{\circ}
:

—

\dim\theta(T)^{\circ}=n
: As
\dim Y\leq n
,
Y
is an affine
n
-dimensional toric variety.
By Theorem
8.5

c)
,
d)

applied to

\theta
and by the characterization of the torus from Lemma
3.4
,

Y
is a torus. Since
\dim T=n=\dim Y
we get
Y\simeq T
as varieties.

—

0<\dim\theta(T)^{\circ}<n
:
As
\theta(\SL_{n})
normalizes
\theta(T)^{\circ}
, the
\theta(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))
-action
by conjugation on
\theta(T)^{\circ}
gives us a group homomorphism

\eta\colon\theta(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))\to\Aut_{\grp}(\theta(T)^{\circ})=\GL_{\dim\theta(T)^{\circ}}(\mathbb{Z})\,.

The case
n=1
is impossible. If
n=2
, then
\ker(\eta)

has finite index in
\theta(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))
and if
n\geq 3
, by Margulis’ normal
subgroup theorem
[
Zim84
, Theorem 8.1.2]
,

\ker(\eta)
is finite or has finite index in
\theta(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))
.
We distinguish these two cases:

a)

\ker(\eta)
is finite: By (
8
),
there exists a prime
p
such that
\ker(\eta)
intersects
\theta(\Gamma_{p})
trivially.
Hence,
\eta
restricts to an injection
\theta(\Gamma_{p})\to\Aut_{\grp}(\theta(T)^{\circ})
.
By Theorem
10.1
, we get
\dim\theta(T)^{\circ}\geq n
, contradiction.

b)

\ker(\eta)
has finite index in
\theta(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z}))
:
As
\theta^{-1}(\theta(T)^{\circ})
is uncountable (note that
k
is uncountable), there exists

t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in\theta^{-1}(\theta(T)^{\circ})
and
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

such that
t_{i}\in\mathbb{G}_{m}
has infinite order.
We may assume
i=n
.
As
\theta^{-1}(\ker(\eta))
has finite index in
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
, there exists
an integer
a\geq 1
such that the elementary matrix

E_{a}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1&\dots&a\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&\dots&1\end{pmatrix}\in\SL_{n}(\mathbb{Z})

lies in
\theta^{-1}(\ker(\eta))
. As
t_{n}
has infinite order,

E_{a}(t)=(t_{1}t_{n}^{a},t_{2},\ldots,t_{n})\neq t
.
This contradicts the fact that
\theta^{-1}(\ker(\eta))

acts trivially on
\theta^{-1}(\theta(T)^{\circ})
by conjugation.

—

\dim\theta(T)^{\circ}=0
: Since
\theta(T)^{\circ}
has countable index
in
\theta(T)
, it follows that
\theta(T)
is countable. However, since

k
is uncountable,
T
is uncountable as well (as
\dim T\geq 1
) and thus
we arrive at a contradiction. ∎

11.
Counterexamples in the projective case

In this small section we give counterexamples to Theorem
E

in the projective case. We assume that
k
is uncountable.
The following easy remark will be useful:

Remark 11.1
.

Two distinct irreducible curves of negative self-intersection number in a smooth projective
surface cannot be linearly equivalent and thus there are at most countably many of them. As
k
is uncountable,
each irreducible curve of negative self-intersection number in a smooth projective toric surface
is invariant under the torus action.

Let
X_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}^{2}
be endowed with the standard action of the torus
T=\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}
.
In this way,
X_{0}
becomes a
T
-toric variety. The complement
C_{0}
of the open
T
-orbit of
X_{0}
consists of the three
T
-invariant lines in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Let
\varphi_{1}\colon X_{1}\to X_{0}
be the blow-up of the three
T
-fixed points, i.e. the blow-up of the singular points of
C_{0}
. Then
X_{1}
is again
T
-toric under the
T
-action that comes from
X_{0}
. The complement
C_{1}
of the open
T
-orbit
in
X_{1}
consists of six
-1
-curves, arranged in a hexagon and there are no other irreducible
curves of negative self-intersection number in
X_{1}
.

We will inductively construct
T
-toric smooth projective surfaces
together with
T
-equivariant morphisms:

\ldots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i+2}}X_{i+1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i+1}}X_{i}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{i-1}}\ldots\xrightarrow{\varphi_{2}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{1}}X_{0}=\mathbb{P}^{2}\,.

For
i\geq 1
, assume that
X_{i}
is already constructed and
that the complement
C_{i}
of the open
T
-orbit in
X_{i}
is connected and it is the union of all irreducible curves of negative self-intersection number.
Moreover, the irreducible curves in
C_{i}
are smooth.
Let
\varphi_{i+1}\colon X_{i+1}\to X_{i}
be the blow-up of the singular points in
C_{i}
. Then
X_{i+1}
is
T
-toric
under the
T
-action that comes from
\varphi_{i+1}
.
Moreover, the complement
C_{i+1}
of the open
T
-orbit in
X_{i+1}
is connected and it is the union of all
irreducible curves of negative self-intersection number, by Remark
11.1
.
Moreover, all irreducible curves in
C_{i+1}
are smooth.
More precisely, the irreducible curves in
C_{i+1}

that lie in the strict transform of
C_{i}
under
\varphi_{i+1}
have self-intersection number
<-1
(as
C_{i}
is connected), whereas the other irreducible curves in
C_{i+1}
are the exceptional curves of
\varphi_{i+1}
. In particular, we get a group isomorphism

\Aut(X_{i+1})\to\Aut(X_{i})\,,\quad\psi\mapsto\varphi_{i+1}\circ\psi\circ\varphi_{i+1}^{-1}\,.

Hence, the smooth projective toric surfaces
X_{i}
,
i\geq 1
are pairwise non-isomorphic (as the rank of the Picard groups of them are distinct), but the automorphism groups of them are all isomorphic to

\Aut(X_{1})\simeq(\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}\rtimes S_{3})\rtimes(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
, where
S_{3}
denotes the symmetric group of three elements,
see e.g.
[
Bea07
, §3.3]
.

Remark 11.2
.

In fact, “almost all” smooth projective toric varieties have an automorphism group, which is equal to
the maximal torus acting on it, see e.g.
[
Cox95
, Corollary 4.7]
.

Abstract.

In this paper, we consider a class of initial-boundary value problems governed by pseudo-parabolic total variation flows. The principal characteristic of our problem lies in the velocity term of the diffusion flux, a feature that can bring about stronger regularity than what is found in standard parabolic PDEs. Meanwhile, our total variation flow contains singular diffusion, and this singularity may lead to a degeneration of the regularity of solution. The objective of this paper is to clarify the power balance between these conflicting effects. Consequently, we will present mathematical results concerning the well-posedness and regularity of the solution in the Main Theorems of this paper.

Introduction

This paper is devoted to the study of a class of initial-boundary value problems of pseudo-parabolic PDEs. Each initial-boundary value problem is denoted by
\mathrm{(P)}_{\varepsilon}
, with a constant
\varepsilon\geq 0
, and formulated as:

\displaystyle\mathrm{(P)}_{\varepsilon}\hskip 17.22217pt

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\mathrm{div}\bigl(\alpha(x)\partial\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)+\beta(x)\nabla\partial_{t}u\bigr)\ni 0~\mbox{ in $Q$,}\\[4.30554pt]
\bigl(\alpha(x)\partial\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u)+\beta(x)\nabla\partial_{t}u\bigr)\cdot n_{\Gamma}\ni 0~\mbox{ on $\Sigma$,}\\[4.30554pt]
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x),~~x\in\Omega.\end{cases}

The class of initial-boundary value problems
\bigl\{\mbox{(P)${}_{\varepsilon}$}\bigr\}_{\varepsilon\geq 0}
is considered under the following notations and assumptions:

(A0)

0<T<\infty
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
are fixed constants of time and spatial dimension, respectively.

(A1)

\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is a bounded domain such that the boundary
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
is smooth (
C^{\infty}
-class) when
N>1
. Also,
n_{\Gamma}
is the unit outer normal on
\Gamma
. Additionally, we let:

\displaystyle Q:=(0,T)\times\Omega,~~\Sigma:=(0,T)\times\Gamma,

\displaystyle~~H:=L^{2}(\Omega),~~\mbox{and}~~V:=H^{1}(\Omega).

(A2)

\alpha\in H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
\beta\in W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)
and
f\in{L^{2}(0,T;H)}
are fixed functions, such that:

\essinf\alpha(\Omega)\geq 0
, and
\delta_{*}:=\inf\beta(\Omega)>0
.

(A3)

\{\gamma_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\geq 0}
is a class of convex function on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, defined as:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varepsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\ni y\mapsto\gamma_{\varepsilon}(y):=\sqrt{\varepsilon^{2}+|y|^{2}}\in[0,\infty),~\mbox{for $\varepsilon\geq 0$.}

Also, for any
\varepsilon\geq 0
,

denotes the subdifferential of

.

(A4)

is a fixed function such that:

\displaystyle u_{0}\in W_{0}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l|l}w\in H^{2}(\Omega)&\nabla w\cdot n_{\Gamma}=0~\mbox{in $H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)$}\end{array}\right\}.

Besides, for each
\varepsilon\geq 0
, the solution to the problem (P)
ε
is defined in the following weak (variational) sense.

Definition 1

For any

, a function

is called a solution to (P)

\displaystyle u\in W^{1,2}(0,T;V),\mbox{ with $u(0)=u_{0}$ in $H$,}

(0.1)

and

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}u(t)

\displaystyle(u(t)-\varphi)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\beta(x)\nabla\partial_{t}u(t)\cdot\nabla\bigl(u(t)-\varphi\bigr)\,dx+\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla u(t))\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}\alpha(x)\gamma_{\varepsilon}(\nabla\varphi)\,dx,~\mbox{ for any $\varphi\in V$ and for a.e. $t\in(0,T)$}.

(0.3)

The class of problems

is motivated to establish a theory of pseudo-parabolic version for the weighted total variation flow:

which has been studied by a lot of mathematicians, as one of key-problems of mathematical models of image denoising processes

, and so on. According to the previous works, it is known that the weak solution to (

) admits the following regularity:

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\in W^{1,2}(0,T;H)~\mbox{ and }~|u(\cdot)|_{BV(\Omega)}\in L^{\infty}(0,T),

(0.5)

whenever

and

Comparing (

The objective of this paper is to clarify the power balance between these conflicting effects. In view of this, we have set the goal to prove the following two Main Theorems.

Main Theorem 1:

The content of this paper is as follows. Preliminaries are given in Section 1, and on this basis, the Main Theorems are stated in Section 2. For the proofs of Main Theorems, we prepare Section 3 to setting up of the regularity theory for an auxiliary elliptic boundary value problem. The auxiliary problem is associated with the time-discretization scheme of our problem (P)

Definition 2

\hat{w}_{n}\to\hat{w}

), as follows:

\varphi_{\delta}^{(0)}:=\rho_{\delta}*(\tilde{\eta}_{0}v)\mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega},\mbox{ for all }\delta>0.

(5.25)

Next, we consider the case when
\ell>0
. Let us fix
\ell\in\{1,\dots,M\}
, and set:

w_{\ell}:=(\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}v)\circ\Xi_{\ell}^{-1}\circ\Theta_{\ell}^{-1}\mbox{ in }H^{1}(W_{\ell}^{+}).

(5.26)

On this basis, we define an extension
\tilde{w}_{\ell}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
w_{\ell}
, by letting:

\displaystyle\tilde{w}_{\ell}(y^{\prime},y_{N}):=\begin{cases}w_{\ell}(y^{\prime},y_{N}),&\mbox{if }y=[y^{\prime},y_{N}]\in W_{\ell}^{+},\\
w_{\ell}(y^{\prime},-y_{N}),&\mbox{if }y=[y^{\prime},y_{N}]\in W_{\ell}^{-},\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise,}\end{cases}

(5.27)

\displaystyle\mbox{for a.e. }y=[y^{\prime},y_{N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\mbox{ with }y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}\mbox{ and }y_{N}\in\mathbb{R}.

(5.28)

Since
v\in V
implies
\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}v\in V
, we immediately see from (
5.26
) and (
5.27
) that
\tilde{w}_{\ell}\in H^{1}(W_{\ell})
. Additionally, we can say that:

(**)

if
v\in W_{0}
, then
\tilde{w}_{\ell}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and
\partial_{N}\tilde{w}_{\ell}(z^{\prime},0)=0
for a.e.
z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.

In fact, by virtue of (
1.5
), (
5.1
), (
5.26
), (
5.27
), and
v\in W_{0}
, it is observed that:

\tilde{w}_{\ell}\in H^{2}(W_{\ell}^{+}\cup W_{\ell}^{-}),\mbox{ with }\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\tilde{w}_{\ell}\subset W_{\ell},

(5.29)

and

\displaystyle\partial_{N}w_{\ell}(z^{\prime},0)~

\displaystyle=-\bigl[\nabla(\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}v)\cdot n_{\Gamma}\bigr](x)=-\bigl[\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}\nabla(v\cdot n_{\Gamma})\bigr](x)-\bigl[v\nabla(\tilde{\eta}_{\ell}\cdot n_{\Gamma})\bigr](x)

(5.30)

\displaystyle=0,\mbox{ for a.e. $z^{\prime}\in r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}$ and $x=\Theta_{\ell}\Xi_{\ell}[z^{\prime},0]\in\Gamma$.}

(5.31)

Furthermore, for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(W_{\ell})
, the properties (
5.29
) and (
5.31
) enable us to compute as follows:

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{N}^{2}\tilde{w}_{\ell},\varphi\rangle=-\int_{W_{\ell}}\partial_{N}\tilde{w}_{\ell}\partial_{N}\varphi dy

(5.32)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{h_{\ell}}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},y_{N})\partial_{N}\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\,dy_{N}dy^{\prime}

(5.33)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{-{h_{\ell}}}^{0}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},-y_{N})\partial_{N}\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\,dy_{N}dy^{\prime}

(5.34)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{h_{\ell}}\partial_{N}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},y_{N})\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\,dy_{N}dy^{\prime}

(5.35)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{-{h_{\ell}}}^{0}\partial_{N}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},-y_{N})\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\,dy_{N}dy^{\prime}

(5.36)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\Bigl[[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},y_{N})\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\Bigr]_{0}^{h_{\ell}}\,dy^{\prime}

(5.37)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\Bigl[[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},-y_{N})\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\Bigr]_{-h_{\ell}}^{0}\,dy^{\prime}

(5.38)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{W_{\ell}}\biggl([\partial_{N}^{2}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},y_{N})\chi_{W_{\ell}^{+}}(y^{\prime},y_{N})+[\partial_{N}^{2}w_{\ell}](y^{\prime},-y_{N})\chi_{W_{\ell}^{-}}(y^{\prime},y_{N})\biggr)\varphi(y^{\prime},y_{N})\,dy.

(5.39)

This variational identity implies that:

\displaystyle\partial_{N}^{2}\tilde{w}_{\ell}=\partial_{N}^{2}w_{\ell}\circ\Pi_{0}\in H,\mbox{ in }\mathscr{D}^{\prime}(W_{\ell}),

(5.40)

\displaystyle\mbox{with a Lipschitz transform }\Pi_{0}:\,y=[y^{\prime},y_{N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\Pi_{0}y:=[y^{\prime},|y_{N}|].

(5.41)

The item
(**)
will be ensured as a consequence of (
5.29
) and (
5.31
), and (
5.40
).

Now, let us define a sequence
\{\varphi_{\delta}^{(\ell)}\}_{\delta>0}\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, as follows:

\varphi_{\delta}^{(\ell)}:=(\rho_{\delta}*\tilde{w}_{\ell})\circ\Xi_{\ell}^{-1}\circ\Theta_{\ell}^{-1}\mbox{ on }\overline{\Omega},\mbox{ for all }\delta>0.

(5.42)

Then, in view of the arguments as in (

)–(

), we can say that the item (

) has been verified, except for the boundary condition:

\nabla\varphi_{\delta}^{(\ell)}\cdot n_{\Gamma}=0\mbox{ on }\Gamma,\mbox{ for all }\delta>0\mbox{ and }\ell=1,\dots,M.

(5.43)

included in

. However, with Remark

in mind, this boundary condition will be verified as a consequence of the following computation:

\displaystyle\partial_{N}(\rho_{\delta}*\tilde{w}_{\ell})(y^{\prime},0)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\partial_{N}\tilde{w}_{\ell}(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{N})\rho_{\delta}(y^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime},-\xi_{N})\,d\xi

(5.44)

\displaystyle\qquad=\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{0}^{h_{\ell}}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](\xi^{\prime},\xi_{N})\rho_{\delta}(y^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime},-\xi_{N})\,d\xi_{N}d\xi^{\prime}

(5.45)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}}\int_{-h_{\ell}}^{0}[\partial_{N}w_{\ell}](\xi^{\prime},-\xi_{N})\rho_{\delta}(y^{\prime}-\xi^{\prime},-\xi_{N})\,d\xi

(5.46)

\displaystyle\qquad=0,\quad\mbox{ for a.e. }y^{\prime}\in r_{\ell}\mathbb{B}^{N-1}.

(5.47)

Thus, we complete the proof of this lemma.

Lemma 5.3
.

For any
r>0
, there exists a constant
C_{r}\geq 0
, depending on
r
, such that:

|v|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq r|\nabla v|_{[H]^{N}}^{2}+C_{r}|v|^{2}_{H},\mbox{ for all }v\in V.

(5.48)

Proof.

We prove this lemma by a contradiction. If Lemma
5.3
does not hold, then there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
v_{n}\in V
satisfying:

|v_{n}|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}>r_{0}|\nabla v_{n}|_{[H]^{N}}^{2}+n|v_{n}|_{H}^{2}.

(5.49)

Here, let us set:

\hat{v}_{n}:=\frac{v_{n}}{|v_{n}|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}},\quad{\rm for~any}~n\in\mathbb{N}.

(5.50)

Then, from (
5.49
), we can see that
\{\hat{v}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded in
V
. On account of the compact embedding
V\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)
, there exists a subsequence
\{n_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\{n\}
and a function
\hat{v}\in V
such that:

\hat{v}_{n_{j}}\to\hat{v}~{\rm in~}L^{2}(\Gamma)\mbox{ as }j\to\infty,

(5.51)

and hence

|\hat{v}|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=\lim_{j\to\infty}|\hat{v}_{n_{j}}|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=1.

(5.52)

Meanwhile, (
5.49
) and (
5.52
) will lead to
\hat{v}=0
in
H
, i.e.
\hat{v}=0
in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
. This contradicts (
5.52
).

Thus, we finish the proof of this lemma.
∎

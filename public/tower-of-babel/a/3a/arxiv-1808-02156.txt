Duality between Final-Seed and Initial-Seed Mutations in Cluster Algebras
Shogo FUJIWARA and Yasuaki GYODA

Abstract
We study the duality between the mutations and the initial-seed mutations in cluster algebras, where the initial-seed mutations are the transformations of rational expressions of cluster variables in terms of the initial cluster under the change of the initial cluster. In particular, we define the maximal degree matrices of the F F -polynomials called the F F -matrices and show that the F F -matrices have the self-duality which is analogous to the duality between the C C - and G G -matrices.

Introduction

Cluster algebras
are a class of commutative algebras introduced by
[
5
]
, which are generated by some distinguished elements called the
cluster variables
.
The cluster variables are given by applying the
mutations
repeatedly starting from the initial cluster variables. Through the description of mutations as transformations on quivers or triangulations, the cluster algebras are applied in various areas of mathematics such that representation theory of quivers
[
1
]
, hyperbolic geometry
[
3
,
4
]
, etc.

Let us briefly recall the notion of mutations, which is essential in cluster algebra theory. For simplicity, we consider here a cluster pattern
without coefficients

[
5
]
. Let
\mathbb{T}_{n}
be the
n
-regular tree. Then, for each vertex
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, cluster variables
\mathbf{x}_{t}=(x_{1;t},\dots,x_{n;t})
are attached such that for any adjacent vertices

and
t^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, the variables
\mathbf{x}_{t}
and
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{t}
are related by a rational transformation called a

. Let
t_{0}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
be a given point in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
called the
initial point
. Then, repeating mutations starting from the initial variables, we have the expression

\displaystyle x_{i;t}=\mathcal{X}^{t_{0}}_{i;t}(\mathbf{x}_{t_{0}}),

where the rational function
\mathcal{X}^{t_{0}}_{i;t}
depends on
t
and
t_{0}
. For a vertex
t^{\prime}
which is adjacent to
t
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, the mutation transforms the function
\mathcal{X}^{t_{0}}_{i;t}
to the function
\mathcal{X}^{t_{0}}_{i;t^{\prime}}
. On the other hand, for a vertex

which is adjacent to
t_{0}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, the mutation also transforms the function
\mathcal{X}^{t_{0}}_{i;t}
to the function
\mathcal{X}^{t_{1}}_{i;t}
. We call this transformation the
initial-seed mutations
. (In contrast to the initial-seed mutations, we call the ordinary mutations also as the
final-seed mutations
in this paper.)

Thanks to the
separation formulas
of
[
6
]
, the cluster variables and the coefficients are described by the
C
-matrices
, the
G
-matrices
, and the
F
-polynomials
, where the
C
- and
G
-matrices are the “
tropical part
”, while the
F
-polynomials are the “

”. The final-seed and initial-seed mutations of the cluster variables and the coefficients reduce to the ones for the
C
- and
G
-matrices and the
F
-polynomials
[
6
]
.
In
[
8
]
, the duality between the
C
- and
G
-matrices under the final-seed and initial-seed mutations was established (under the assumption of the sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices, which is established in
[
7
]
). In
[
9
]
, an analogous duality was established also for the
denominator vectors
for some classes of cluster algebras.
In this paper, we treat the nontropical part of the seeds, namely the
F
-polynomials. Since the
F
-polynomials are rather complicated, we consider the “degree matrices” of
F
-polynomials (
F-matrices
). Under applying the sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices, a column vectors of the
F
-matrices correspond to the exponent vector of the unique monomial with maximal degree of a
F
-polynomials.
Then, we establish the duality of those matrices under the final-seed and initial-seed mutations, which is parallel to the ones in
[
8
,
9
]
. This is the main result of the paper. Also, in a process of proving the main result, we have a nice relation between the
C
-,
G
-,
F
-matrices under the exchange of the initial matrix
B
and
-B
.

We also exhibit the final-seed and initial-seed mutation formulas of the
C
- and
G
-matrices systematically, some of which are new in the literature. We stress that the duality of the final-seed and initial-seed mutations becomes manifest only after applying the sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices
[
7
]
.

The organisation of the paper is as follows:
In Section
2
, we start with recalling the definitions of the seed mutations and the cluster patterns according to
[
6
]
and give the final-seed mutations.
Also, using the sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices, we obtain a reduced form of the final-seed mutations. The formulas involving the
F
-matrices are new results.
In Section
3
, the initial-seed mutation formulas are given using the

-matrices
of
[
6
]
. By sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices and the duality of the

- and

-matrices of
[

, we prove the conjecture
[
6

-matrices are expressed by the

-matrices. Using this result, we obtain the duality of the

In Section

We start with recalling the definitions of the seed mutations and the cluster patterns according to

[

Let

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}u_{j}^{a_{j}}\oplus\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}u_{j}^{b_{j}}=\prod_{j=1}^{\ell}u_{j}^{\min(a_{j},b_{j})}.

We call it the

of

[

For any semifield

\displaystyle\pi\colon\ \mathbb{Q}_{\text{sf}}(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell})

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{P},

\displaystyle y_{i}

\displaystyle\longmapsto p_{i}.

For

\displaystyle F|_{\mathbb{P}}(p_{1},\dots,p_{\ell}):=\pi(F(y_{1},\dots,y_{\ell}))

Throughout the paper, for an integer

\displaystyle b=[b]_{+}-[-b]_{+}.

(2.1)

Let

, and let

. The

•

The entries of

\displaystyle b^{\prime}_{ij}=\begin{cases}-b_{ij}&\text{if $i=k$ or $j=k$,}\\
b_{ij}+\left[b_{ik}\right]_{+}b_{kj}+b_{ik}\left[-b_{kj}\right]_{+}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.2)

•

The coefficients

\displaystyle y^{\prime}_{j}=\begin{cases}y_{k}^{-1}&\text{if $j=k$,}\\
y_{j}y_{k}^{[b_{kj}]_{+}}(y_{k}\oplus 1)^{-b_{kj}}&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

(2.3)

•

The cluster variables

(2.5)

be the

\displaystyle=\big(J_{\ell}+[\varepsilon_{\ell}\big(G^{-B^{T}_{t};t}_{t_{0}}\big)B_{t}^{T}]^{\ell\bullet}_{+}\big)\big(F^{B;t_{0}}_{t}\big)^{T}+\big[\varepsilon_{\ell}\big(G^{-B_{t}^{T};t}_{t_{0}}\big)G^{B_{t}^{T};t}_{t_{0}}\big]^{\ell\bullet}_{+}

\displaystyle=\big(J_{\ell}+[\varepsilon_{\ell}\big(G^{-B_{t}^{T};t}_{t_{0}}\big)B_{t}^{T}]^{\ell\bullet}_{+}\big)F^{B^{T}_{t};t}_{t_{0}}+\big[\varepsilon_{\ell}\big(G^{-B_{t}^{T};t}_{t_{0}}\big)G^{B_{t}^{T};t}_{t_{0}}\big]^{\ell\bullet}_{+}=F_{t_{0}}^{B_{t^{\prime}}^{T};t^{\prime}}

as desired.
∎

4
Properties on principal extension of exchange matrices

In this section, we present some properties of the principal extension of the exchange matrices. These properties yield alternative derivations of some equalities in the previous sections and are interesting for their own right.

4.1
Principal extension

For an exchange matrix
B_{t}
,
\tilde{B_{t}}=\left[\begin{smallmatrix}B_{t}\\
C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\end{smallmatrix}\right]

(t\in\mathbb{T}_{n})
is the extended exchange matrix
[
5
, Section 5]
. We fix the initial vertex
t_{0}\in\mathbb{T}_{2n}
. Let us regard
\mathbb{T}_{n}
as the subtree of
\mathbb{T}_{2n}
induced by vertices which are reachable from
t_{0}
via edges labeled by
1,\dots,n
. Let us consider the following “full extension of
B
”. We set

\displaystyle\overline{B}:=\begin{bmatrix}B&-I_{n}\\
I_{n}&O\end{bmatrix}.

(4.1)

Note that
\overline{B}
is regular. Then, we obtain a family of
2n\times 2n
skew-symmetrizable matrices
\{\overline{B}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{2n}}
such that
\overline{B}=\overline{B}_{t_{0}}

(t_{0}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}\subset\mathbb{T}_{2n})
and they are related by the mutation (
2.2
). Then, the left half of
\overline{B}_{t}
is
\tilde{B}_{t}
for
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, and also
\overline{B}_{t}
is regular. We call
\{\overline{B}_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{2n}}
the
principal extension
of
\{B_{t}\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
. The following proposition gives the explicit expression of
\overline{B}_{t}

(t\in\mathbb{T}_{n})
:

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}=(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
be a cluster pattern with the initial vertex
t_{0}
. For any mutation series
\mu_{i_{m}}\mu_{i_{m-1}}\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}\ (i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in\{1,\dots,n\})
of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, we set
t_{1},\dots,t_{{m-1}},t_{m},t
as
\textstyle{t_{0}}
\textstyle{t_{1}}
\textstyle{\cdots}
\textstyle{t_{m-1}}
\textstyle{t_{m}=t}
\scriptstyle{i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{i_{2}}
\scriptstyle{i_{m-1}}
\scriptstyle{i_{m}}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Then, the following equality holds:

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{t}&-D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\\
C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&O\end{bmatrix},

(4.2)

where
D
is a skew-symmetrizer of
B
.

Proof.

We start with the proof of the following equality:

\displaystyle\overline{B}_{t}=\begin{bmatrix}B_{t}&-D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\\
C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{s=0}^{m-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big[{-}D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big]_{+}^{i_{s+1}\bullet}-\big[{-}C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big]_{+}^{\bullet i_{s+1}}D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big)\end{bmatrix}.

(4.3)

We prove (
4.3
) by the induction on the distance between
t
and
t_{0}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. The base case
m=0
is immediate as
\overline{B_{t_{0}}}=\overline{B}
. It remains to show that if (
4.3
) holds for some
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, then it also holds
for
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
such that
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\ell}
. The left
2n\times n
submatrix of
\overline{B}_{t}
coincides with the extended matrix
\tilde{B}_{t}
in
[
5
, Section 5]
. Therefore, it suffices to prove the right
2n\times n
submatrix of
\overline{B}_{t}
. For any
1\leq i\leq n
and
n+1\leq j\leq 2n
, we have

\displaystyle b_{ij;t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-b_{ij;t}&\text{if $i=\ell$,}\\
b_{ij;t}+b_{i\ell;t}[b_{\ell j;t}]_{+}+[-b_{i\ell;t}]_{+}b_{\ell j;t}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}d_{i}^{-1}c_{j-n,i;t}d_{j}&\text{if $i=\ell$,}\\
-d_{i}^{-1}c_{j-n,i;t}d_{j}+b_{i\ell;t}\big[{-}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}\big]_{+}-[-b_{i\ell;t}]_{+}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}-d_{i}^{-1}c_{j-n,i;t^{\prime}}d_{j}&\text{if $i=\ell$,}\\
-d_{i}^{-1}(c_{j-n,i;t}+b_{\ell i;t}[-c_{j-n,\ell;t}]_{+}+[b_{\ell i;t}]_{+}c_{j-n,\ell;t})d_{j}&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

\displaystyle=-d_{i}^{-1}c_{j-n,i;t^{\prime}}d_{j}.

By using the equality

\displaystyle\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big[{-}D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big]_{+}^{\ell\bullet}-\big[{-}C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big]_{+}^{\bullet\ell}D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big)_{i-n,j-n}

\displaystyle\qquad{}=c_{i-n,\ell;t}\big[{-}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}\big]_{+}-[-c_{i-n,\ell;t}]_{+}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}

and the inductive assumption, for any
n+1\leq i,j\leq 2n
, we have

\displaystyle b_{ij;t^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=b_{ij;t}+b_{i\ell}[b_{\ell j;t}]_{+}+[-b_{i\ell;t}]_{+}b_{\ell j;t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{{m-1}}\big(c_{i-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}\big[{-}d_{i_{s+1}}^{-1}c_{j-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}d_{j}\big]_{+}-[-c_{i-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}]_{+}d_{i_{s+1}}^{-1}c_{j-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}d_{j}\big)

\displaystyle\quad{}+c_{i-n,\ell;t}\big[{-}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}\big]_{+}-[-c_{i-n,\ell;t}]_{+}d_{\ell}^{-1}c_{j-n,\ell;t}d_{j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\big(c_{i-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}\big[{-}d_{i_{s+1}}^{-1}c_{j-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}d_{j}\big]_{+}-[-c_{i-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}]_{+}d_{i_{s+1}}^{-1}c_{j-n,{i_{s+1}};t_{s}}d_{j}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{s=0}^{m}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big[{-}D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big]_{+}^{{i_{s+1}}\bullet}-\big[{-}C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big]_{+}^{\bullet{i_{s+1}}}D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big)_{i-n,j-n},

(4.4)

where
t_{m}=t
and
\ i_{m+1}=\ell
. Thus we have (
4.3
). Moreover, applying the sign-coherence of the
C
-matrices, the third equality of (
4.4
) is always zero. Therefore, we have (
4.2
).
∎

Let us study the
C
-,
G
- and
F
-matrices for the initial matrix
\overline{B}
in (
4.1
), especially for the subtree
\mathbb{T}_{n}
in
\mathbb{T}_{2n}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\{\Sigma_{t}=(\mathbf{x}_{t},\mathbf{y}_{t},B_{t})\}_{t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}}
be a cluster pattern with the initial vertex
t_{0}
. For any mutation series
\mu_{i_{m}}\mu_{i_{m-1}}\cdots\mu_{i_{1}}\ (i_{1},\dots,i_{m}\in\{1,\dots,n\})
of
\mathbb{T}_{n}
, we set
t_{1},\dots,t_{{m-1}},t_{m},t
as
\textstyle{t_{0}}
\textstyle{t_{1}}
\textstyle{\cdots}
\textstyle{t_{m-1}}
\textstyle{t_{m}=t}
\scriptstyle{i_{1}}
\scriptstyle{i_{2}}
\scriptstyle{i_{m-1}}
\scriptstyle{i_{m}}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. Then, the following equalities hold:

\displaystyle C_{t}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&O\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix},

(4.5)

\displaystyle G_{t}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix}G_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&O\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix},

(4.6)

\displaystyle F_{\ell;t}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}(\mathbf{y})=\begin{cases}F_{\ell;t}^{B;t_{0}}(\mathbf{y})&\text{if }\ell\in\{1,\dots,n\},\\
1&\text{if }\ell\in\{n+1,\dots,2n\}.\end{cases}

(4.7)

Proof.

Firstly, we start the proof of the following equality:

\displaystyle C_{t}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&\mathop{\sum}\limits_{s=0}^{m-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big[D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big]_{+}^{i_{s+1}\bullet}-\big[C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big]_{+}^{\bullet i_{s+1}}D^{-1}\big(C_{t_{s}}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\big)\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}.

(4.8)

We prove (
4.8
) by the induction on the distance between
t
and
t_{0}
in
\mathbb{T}_{n}
. The base case
m=0
is immediate as
C_{t_{0}}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}=I_{2n}
. It remains to show that if (
4.8
) holds for some
t\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
, then it also holds
for
t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{T}_{n}
such that
\textstyle{t}
\textstyle{t^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\ell}
.
By (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle C_{t^{\prime}}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}

\displaystyle=C_{t}^{\overline{B};t_{0}}\big(J_{\ell}+[-\overline{B}_{t}]_{+}^{\ell\bullet}\big)+\big[C^{\overline{B};t_{0}}_{t}\big]_{+}^{\bullet\ell}\overline{B}_{t}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&X\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}\left(\begin{bmatrix}J_{\ell}&O\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}[-B_{t}]_{+}^{\ell\bullet}&[D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D]_{+}^{\ell\bullet}\\
O&O\end{bmatrix}\right)

\displaystyle\quad{}+\begin{bmatrix}\big[C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big]_{+}^{\bullet\ell}&O\\
O&O\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}B_{t}&-D^{-1}\big(C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big)^{T}D\\
C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}&Y\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}C_{t}^{B;t_{0}}\big(J_{\ell}+[-{B}_{t}]_{+}^{\ell\bullet}\big)+\big[C^{B;t_{0}}_{t}\big]_{+}^{\bullet\ell}{B}_{t}&Z\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}C_{t^{\prime}}^{B;t_{0}}&Z\\
O&I_{n}\end{bmatrix},

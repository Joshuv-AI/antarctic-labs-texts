Hodge numbers of hypergeometric data
Ling Long, Yifan Yang

Abstract
. In this paper, based on the toric hypergeometric model given in a paper by Beukers–Cohen–Mellit, we provide two other ways to explain why the zig-zag diagram method can be used to compute Hodge numbers for hypergeometric data defined over ℚ \mathbb{Q} .



1.
Introduction

Let
HD=\{\alpha=\{a_{1},a_{2},\cdots,a_{m}\},\beta=\{1,b_{2},\cdots,b_{m}\}\}
with
a_{i},b_{j}\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1]
be a hypergeometric datum defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which means
\prod_{i=1}^{m}(X-e^{2\pi ia_{i}})\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
and
\prod_{i=1}^{m}(X-e^{2\pi ib_{i}})\in\mathbb{Z}[X]

The purpose of this note is to compute the Hodge-Tate weights arising from the generic newton polygon of

in terms of combinatorics via the Hodge numbers of toric varieties. They form a set of fundamental invariants when we consider the hypergeometric Galois representations arising from
HD

[
2
]
and
[
6
]

k

\{0,k-1\}

Write

\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(X-e^{2\pi ia_{i}})}{\prod_{i=1}^{m}(X-e^{2\pi ib_{i}})}=\frac{(X^{p_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{p_{r}}-1)}{(X^{q_{1}}-1)\cdots(X^{q_{s}}-1)}

(1)

\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{s}q_{j}:=L(HD).

We call

of

We also refer to

of
HD
. Here we assume
q_{s}=1
. Further we let

be the

of

{\bf{e}}_{i}=&[1,0,\cdots,0,\underset{i+1}{1},0,\cdots,0],\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1\\

Let

be the convex polyhedron with vertices

Let

They form a set of useful invariants when we study the toric varieties parameterized by

as follows.
Given a gamma vector

Following

2.
Main statement

Given a hypergeometric gamma vector
\Gamma=[p_{1},\cdots,p_{r},-q_{1},\dots,-q_{s}]
, let

(8)

HD_{\Gamma}=\left\{\alpha=\left\{\bigcup_{j=1}^{r}\left\{\frac{i}{p_{j}}\right\}_{j=1}^{p_{i}}\right\},\quad\beta=\left\{\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\left\{\frac{i}{q_{j}}\right\}_{i=1}^{q_{j}}\right\}\right\}.

Each multi-set has length
L:=L(HD)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}p_{i}
. By construction, the multiplicity
n_{b,\beta}
of any element

in
\beta

(9)

Sort the combined elements in both
\alpha
and
\beta
into a new list satisfying

\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{2L},

\mu_{2L}=1

\alpha
.

Further let
\mu_{0}=0
.

Given
\Gamma
, we define an arithmetic function
\Phi

(11)

\Phi(0)=\Phi_{HD}(0)=r(\Gamma(HD)),\quad\Phi_{HD}=\Phi_{HD}(i)=\begin{cases}\Phi_{HD}(i-1)+1,&\text{ if }\quad\mu_{i}\in\alpha;\\
\Phi_{HD}(i-1)-1,&\text{ if }\quad\mu_{i}\in\beta.\end{cases}

Theorem 1
.

Let

(12)

HT(HD):=\{\Phi(i)\mid\text{ if }\mu_{i}\in\beta\}.

They are the Hodge numbers of
HD
.

Equivalently,

Theorem 2
.

Given a hypergeometric vector
\Gamma
as (
2
), let

as in (

). Then the Hodge numbers

of the convex polyhedron

(to be defined in (

Removing overlapping elements from both

, denoted by

3.
Zig-zag diagrams and
p
-adic orders

The zig-zag diagram is related to the
p
-adic order of

(14)

\displaystyle A_{HD}(k):=\frac{\prod_{a\in\alpha}(a)_{k}}{\prod_{b\in\beta}(b)_{k}},\quad\text{where}\quad(a)_{k}:=\frac{\Gamma(a+k)}{\Gamma(a)}=a(a+1)\cdots(a+k-1).

It is related to the discussion in
[
7
]
. Here we recast the idea. For
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, use
[a]_{0}
to denote the first
p
-adic digit of
a
. If
a=\frac{c}{d}
where
c,d\in\mathbb{N},1\leq c<d<p
, and
p\equiv 1\mod d
then

(15)

\left[-\frac{c}{d}\right]_{0}=\frac{cp-c}{d}=\frac{c}{d}(p-1).

For
a\in(0,1]
and
1\leq k<p
,

(16)

\text{ord}_{p}(a)_{k}=-\left\lfloor a-\frac{k}{p-1}\right\rfloor=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }1\leq k\leq[-a]_{0};\\
1&\text{if }[-a]_{0}<k<p.\end{cases}

Let
p\equiv 1\mod M(HD)
be a fixed prime throughout this paper. It follows that

(17)

0\leq[-\mu_{1}]_{0}\leq[-\mu_{2}]_{0}\leq\cdots\leq[-\mu_{2L}]_{0}\leq[-1]_{0}=p-1.

When
k
goes from 0 to
p-1
,
\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD}(k)
is a step function starting from 0, and when
k
passes through
[-\mu_{i}]_{0}
, its value will change by
n_{\mu_{i},\alpha}-n_{\mu_{i},\beta}
where
n_{\mu_{i},\alpha}
(resp.
n_{\mu_{i},\beta}
) denotes the multiplicity of
\mu_{i}
in
\alpha
(resp.
\beta
). We summarize the above discussion into the next Proposition.

Proposition 1
.

For a given datum
HD=\{\alpha=\{a_{1},\cdots,a_{L}\},\beta=\{b_{1},\cdots,b_{L}\}\}
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
where
a_{i},b_{j}\in(0,1]
,
b_{1}=1
, whose Gamma vector is
\Gamma(HD)
, let
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{2L}=1
be the sorted combined list. For any prime
p\equiv 1\mod M(HD)

(18)

\Phi_{HD}(i)=\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD}(k)+r(\Gamma(HD)),\quad\text{if}\quad[-\mu_{i}]_{0}<k\leq[-\mu_{i+1}]_{0}.

In particular, for each
i
such that
\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}
, by (
15
) and (
18
),

(19)

\Phi_{HD}(i)=\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD}(\mu_{i+1}(p-1))+r(\Gamma(HD)).

Next we will consider the first term on the right hand side of (
18
) by recalling some useful facts.
For
r\in\mathbb{R}
, let
\{r\}:=r-\lfloor r\rfloor
. Then

(20)

\{r\}+\{-r\}=\begin{cases}0&r\in\mathbb{Z};\\
1&r\notin\mathbb{Z}.\end{cases}

Also (from the multiplication formula of Gauss sums and the Gross-Koblitz formula), for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}

\{rm\}+\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\{\frac{i}{m}\right\}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\{r+\frac{i}{m}\right\}.

Equivalently,

(21)

\{rm\}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\{r+\frac{i}{m}\right\}-\frac{m-1}{2}.

For
1\leq k\leq p-1,
from (
16
)

(22)

\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD}(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{L}-\left\lfloor a_{i}-\frac{k}{p-1}\right\rfloor+\left\lfloor b_{i}-\frac{k}{p-1}\right\rfloor.

In particular,
\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD}(p-1)=s-r
.

Lemma 1
.

Given a Gamma vector
\Gamma
, let
HD_{\Gamma}
be as in (
8
). For
p\equiv 1\mod M(HD_{\Gamma})
,
\mu\in\alpha\cup\beta,

(23)

\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD_{\Gamma}}(\mu(p-1))=s-r+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\{-\mu p_{i}\}-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\{-\mu q_{j}\}.

Proof.

R.H.S.

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:fr}}{=}

\displaystyle s-r+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{p_{i}}\left\{-\mu+\frac{l}{p_{i}}\right\}-\frac{p_{i}-1}{2}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{q_{i}}\left\{-\mu+\frac{l}{q_{j}}\right\}-\frac{q_{j}-1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{s-r}{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{l=1}^{p_{i}}\left(-\mu+\frac{l}{p_{i}}-\left\lfloor-\mu+\frac{l}{p_{i}}\right\rfloor\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{l=1}^{q_{i}}\left(-\mu+\frac{l}{q_{j}}-\left\lfloor-\mu+\frac{l}{q_{j}}\right\rfloor\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{k=1}^{p_{i}}-\left\lfloor\mu-\frac{k}{p_{i}}\right\rfloor+\sum_{j=1}^{s}\sum_{k=1}^{q_{j}}\left\lfloor\mu-\frac{k}{q_{i}}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:ordpA}}{=}

\displaystyle\text{ord}_{p}A_{HD_{\Gamma}}(\mu(p-1))\in\mathbb{Z}.

∎

4.
Hodge numbers of convex cones

4.1.
Background

The background for this section is from
[
9
]
by Wan.
Let
\Delta
be a polyhedron in

whose vertices are points in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. For each
{\bf{v}}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its

, denoted by

is the sum of its negative entries. For the computation below we assume the interior of

so that its denominator

. For each lattice point

in this cone, the

\Delta

.
Given

, let

and for

Then the Hodge numbers of the cone is computed by the inductive formula

(24)

H_{\Delta}(k):=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\binom{n}{i}w_{\Delta}(k-i).

And

for

, see
[
9
]
.

Now let
\Delta(HD)
be the polyhedron with vertices

4.2.
Primitive elements and a generating set

From construction,

\sum_{j\in T}a_{j}=\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j\in S_{+}}\{-p_{j}c\}_{d}+\sum_{j\in S_{-}}\{p_{j}c\}_{d}\right)=w(c/d).

Therefore,

g(x;T)=\frac{\sum_{c=0}^{d-1}x^{w(c/d)}}{(1-x)^{|T|}}.

This proves the lemma for the case
0,n\in T
.

Finally, assume that
n\in T
but
0\notin T
. As before, we find
that for each integer
c
with
0\leq c\leq d-1
, there is exactly a
primitive element
u=\sum_{j\in T}a_{j}{\bf{e}}_{j}
with
a_{n+1}=c/d
and

da_{j}=\begin{cases}\{-p_{j}c\}_{d},&\text{if }j\in T_{+},\\
\{p_{j}c\}_{d},&\text{if }j\in T_{-}.\end{cases}

Then the weight of
u
is equal to

\begin{split}\sum_{j\in T}a_{j}&=\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j\in T_{+}}\{-p_{j}c\}_{d}+\sum_{j\in T_{-}}\{p_{j}c\}_{d}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{d}\left(\sum_{j\in S_{+}}\{-p_{j}c\}_{d}+\sum_{j\in S_{-}}\{p_{j}c\}_{d}\right)=w(c/d).\end{split}

Again, we have
g(x;T)=f(x;T)/(1-x)^{|T|}
with
f(x;T)
given by
(
35
). This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Corollary 1
.

For a proper subset
T
of
S
, let
f(x;T)
be defined by
(
35
). Then the generating function

g(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}m(k)x^{k}
for the number
m(k)
of points of
weight
k
in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap C(\Delta)
is equal to
f(x)/(1-x)^{n}
, where

(36)

\begin{split}f(x)&=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-1-k}\sum_{T\subset S_{+},|T|=k}f(x;T\cup S_{-})(1-x)^{r-1-k}\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{s-1}(-1)^{s-1-k}\sum_{T\subset S_{-},|T|=k}f(x;S_{+}\cup T)(1-x)^{s-1-k}.\end{split}

Consequently, the Hodge number multiplicity
H_{\Delta}(k)

.

Example 8.
Let

with

and

f(x)
in

By Lemma

We now deduce an alternative expression for
f(x)
, from which we will
see that it is equal to the Hodge polynomial from the zigzag
algorithm. Before that, let us remark that since
w(t)
is periodic of
period
1
, the polynomial
f(x;T)
in (
35
) can also be
written as

f(x;T)=\sum_{c=1}^{d}x^{w(c/d)}.

We let
\mathscr{T}
denote the set

\mathscr{T}:=\{t\in\mathbb{Q}:0<t\leq 1,~p_{j}t\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for some }j\in S_{+}\}.

For
t\in\mathscr{T}
, we let

T(t):=\{j\in S_{+}:p_{j}t\in\mathbb{Z}\},\qquad m(t):=|T(t)|.

That is,

\mathscr{T}=\bigcup_{j\in S_{+}}\left\{\frac{k}{p_{j}}:k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq k\leq p_{j}\right\},

T(t)
is the set of indices
j
such that
t
is contained in
the set
\{k/p_{j}:k\in\mathbb{Z},1\leq k\leq p_{j}\}
.

Recall from (
36
) that

f(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}(-1)^{r-1-k}\sum_{T\subset S_{+},|T|=k}f(x;T\cup S_{-})(1-x)^{r-1-k},

which is equal to

\sum_{c/d\in\mathscr{T},(c,d)=1}x^{w(c/d)}\sum_{T\subsetneq S_{+},d|d_{T\cup S_{-}}}(x-1)^{r-1-|T|}.

Now for
T\subset S_{+}
, we have
d|d_{T\cup S_{-}}
if and only if

T^{\prime}:=S\setminus(T\cup S_{-})
satisfies
T^{\prime}\subseteq T(c/d)
. Thus,

(37)

\begin{split}f(x)&=\sum_{t\in\mathscr{T}}x^{w(t)}\sum_{T^{\prime}\subseteq T(t),T^{\prime}\neq\emptyset}(x-1)^{|T^{\prime}|-1}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathscr{T}}x^{w(t)}\sum_{j=1}^{m(t)}\binom{m(t)}{j}(x-1)^{j}\\
&=\sum_{t\in\mathscr{T}}x^{w(t)}(1+x+\cdots+x^{m(t)-1}).\end{split}

This means that for each fraction
t
in
\{k/p_{j}:1\leq k\leq p_{j}\}
,
j\in S_{+}
, if we let
m(t)
denote the number of times
it appears in the sets, then
t
contributes
m(t)
terms continually
to
f(x)
, starting from the exponent
w(t)
and ending at the
exponent
w(t)+m(t)-1
. Now we have

(38)

\begin{split}w(t)&=\sum_{j\in S_{+}}(-p_{j}t-\lfloor-p_{j}t\rfloor)+\sum_{j\in S_{-}}(p_{j}t-\lfloor p_{j}t\rfloor)\\
&=-\sum_{j\in S_{+}}\lfloor-p_{j}t\rfloor-\sum_{j\in S_{-}}\lfloor p_{j}t\rfloor.\end{split}

Using

\lfloor-p_{j}t\rfloor=-\#\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:0<\frac{k}{p_{j}}<t\right\}-1

and

\lfloor p_{j}t\rfloor=\#\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:0<\frac{k}{p_{j}}\leq t\right\},

we find that

(39)

w(t)=\sum_{j\in S_{+}}\#\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:0<\frac{k}{p_{j}}<t\right\}-\sum_{j\in S_{-}}\#\left\{k\in\mathbb{Z}:0<\frac{k}{p_{j}}\leq t\right\}+r.

From this, we see that
f(x)
is precisely equal to the polynomial
from the zigzag algorithm.

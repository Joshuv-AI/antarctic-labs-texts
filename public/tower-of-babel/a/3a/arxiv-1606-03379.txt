A mixed finite element approximation for Darcy-Forchheimer flows of slightly compressible fluids
Thinh Kieu

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the generalized Forchheimer flows for slightly compressible fluids in porous media. Using Muskat’s and Ward’s general form of Forchheimer equations, we describe the flow of a single-phase fluid in ℝ d , d ≥ 2 \mathbb{R}^{d},d\geq 2 by a nonlinear degenerate system of density and momentum. A mixed finite element method is proposed for the approximation of the solution of the above system. The stability of the approximations are proved; the error estimates are derived for the numerical approximations for both continuous and discrete time procedures. The continuous dependence of numerical solutions on physical parameters are demonstrated. Experimental studies are presented regarding convergence rates and showing the dependence of the solution on the physical parameters.



keywords

Porous media, error analysis, slightly compressible fluid, dependence on parameters, numerical analysis.

Introduction

The fluid flow through porous materials, e.g. soil, sand, aquifers, oil reservoir, plants, wood, bones, etc., is a great interest in the research community such as engineering, oil recovery, environmental and groundwater hydrology and medicine. Darcy law, which is the linear relation between the velocity vector and the pressure gradient, is used to describe fluid flow under low velocity and low porosity conditions, see in
[
3
]

Consequently, the attention has been attracted to the nonlinear equations for describing of this kind of flow. Dupuit and Forchheimer proposed a modified equation, known as Darcy- Forchheimer equation or generalized Forchheimer equation, by adding the nonlinear terms of velocity to Darcy law, see
[
16

It is well known that the mixed finite element method is among the popular numerical methods for the modeling flow in porous media because it produces the accurate results for both scalar (density or pressure) and vector (velocity or momentum) functions, see
[

The objective of this paper is to analyze mixed finite element approximations to the solutions of the system of equations modeling the flows of a single-phase compressible fluid in porous media subject to the generalized Forchheimer law. This is a nonlinear degenerate system with coefficients depending on the density gradient and degenerating to zero as it approaches to infinity. 2\Big(\phi(\rho_{h}^{i}-\rho_{h}^{i-1}),\rho_{h}^{i}\Big)=\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+\|{\rho_{h}^{i}-\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2},

we obtain

\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+\|{\rho_{h}^{i}-\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+2\Delta t\left(K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}\right)=2\Delta t(f^{i},\rho_{h}^{i})-2\Delta t\left\langle\psi^{i},\rho_{h}^{i}\right\rangle.

(114)

It follows from (
18
) that

2\Delta t\left(K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}\right)\geq 2c_{2}\Delta t\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}-2c_{2}\Delta t|\Omega|.

(115)

Using (
36
) and Hölder’s inequality to the RHS of (
114
) shows that

2\Delta t\Big(\left(f^{i},\rho_{h}^{i}\right)-\left\langle\psi^{i},\rho_{h}^{i}\right\rangle\Big)\\
\leq 2\Delta t\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}\|{f^{i}}\|_{\phi^{-1}}+2\Delta t\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\Big\{C_{1}\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|+\varepsilon\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}+C_{1}\varepsilon^{-\frac{1}{\beta-1}}\Big\}.

(116)

Combining (
114
)–(
116
), then selecting
\varepsilon=\frac{c_{2}}{2}(\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+1)^{-1}
yields

\begin{split}&\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+\|{\rho_{h}^{i}-\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+c_{2}\Delta t\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}\\
&\qquad\leq 2\Delta t\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}\|{f^{i}}\|_{\phi^{-1}}+C\Delta t\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|+C\Delta t\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}(\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+1)^{\frac{1}{\beta-1}}+C\Delta t\\
&\qquad\leq\Delta t\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+C\Delta t\Big(1+\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}+\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\frac{\beta}{\beta-1}}+\|{f^{i}}\|^{2}\Big).\end{split}

We simplify the RHS of the above estimate using the inequality
\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)},\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{2}\leq C(1+\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\lambda})
to obtain

\frac{\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\rho_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}}{\Delta t}-\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+c_{2}\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}\leq C\Big(1+\|{\psi^{i}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\lambda}+\|{f^{i}}\|^{2}\Big).

According to discrete Gronwall’s inequality in Lemma
4
,

\|{\rho_{h}^{i}}\|^{2}+\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}\leq C(1-\Delta t)^{-i}\|{\rho_{h}^{0}}\|^{2}+\Delta t\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1-\Delta t)^{-i+j-1}\Big(1+\|{\psi^{j}}\|{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}^{\lambda}+\|{f^{j}}\|^{2}\Big).

(117)

Now selecting
z=m_{h}^{i}
in the first equation (
46
) gives

\|{m_{h}^{i}}\|^{2}+\left(K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},m_{i}\right)=0,

hence

\|{m_{h}^{i}}\|\leq C\|{K^{1/2}(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|\leq\|{\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}}\|_{0,\beta}^{\beta}.

(118)

Putting (
117
) and (
118
) together with note that
\|{\rho_{h}^{0}}\|^{2}\leq\|{\rho^{0}}\|^{2}
implies (
112
). The proof is complete.

Theorem 7.18
.

Proof 7.19
.

Evaluating equation (

) at

gives

\begin{split}\left(m^{i},z\right)+\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i},z\right)=0,\\
\left(\phi\rho_{t}^{i},r\right)-\left(m^{i},\nabla r\right)=\left(f^{i},r\right)-\left\langle\psi^{i},r\right\rangle.\end{split}

(120)

Subtracting (
46
) from (

), we obtain

\begin{split}\left(m^{i}-m_{h}^{i},z\right)+\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},z\right)=0,\quad\forall z\in\mathcal{M}_{h},\\
\left(\phi(\rho_{t}^{i}-\partial\rho_{h}^{i}),r\right)-\left(m^{i}-m_{h}^{i},\nabla r\right)=0,\quad\forall r\in\mathcal{R}_{h}.\end{split}

(121)

Choosing

, and adding the two equations shows that

\left(\phi(\rho_{t}^{i}-\partial\rho_{h}^{i}),\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right)+\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right)=0.

(122)

Since

we rewrite (
122
) in the form

\begin{split}&(\partial\vartheta_{h}^{i},\vartheta_{h}^{i})+\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\rho^{i}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}\right)\\
&\qquad\qquad=\left(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\theta^{i}\right)+\left(\phi(\rho_{t}^{i}-\partial\rho^{i}),\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right)+\left(\phi\partial\theta^{i},\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right).\end{split}

(123)

We will evaluate (

) term by term.

• For the first term, we use the identity

\begin{split}(\partial\vartheta_{h}^{i},\vartheta_{h}^{i})=\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}\right)+\frac{\Delta t}{2}\|{\partial\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}.\end{split}

(124)

• For the second term, the monotonicity of
K(\cdot)
in (

) yields

(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\rho^{i}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{i})\geq(\beta-1)\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}K(|\gamma^{i}(s)|)ds\Big)|\nabla\rho^{i}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|^{2}dx,

(125)

where

• For the third term, using (

) gives

(K(|\nabla\rho^{i}|)\nabla\rho^{i}-K(|\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|)\nabla\rho_{h}^{i},\nabla\theta^{i})\leq\frac{\beta-1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}K(|\gamma^{i}(s)|)ds\Big)|\nabla\rho^{i}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|^{2}dx+C\|{\nabla\theta^{i}}\|^{2}.

(126)

• For the fourth term, using Taylor expand we see that

\begin{split}\left(\phi(\rho_{t}^{i}-\partial\rho^{i}),\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right)&\leq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\|{\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\rho_{tt}(\tau)(\tau-t_{i-1})d\tau}\|_{\phi}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left(\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\rho_{tt}(\tau)}\|_{\phi}^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}(\tau-t_{i-1})^{2}d\tau\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}\\
&\leq C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\rho_{tt}(\tau)}\|_{\phi}^{2}d\tau+\frac{1}{4}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}.\end{split}

(127)

• For the last term, using Young’s and Hölder’s inequalities, we find that

\displaystyle\left(\phi\partial\theta^{i},\vartheta_{h}^{i}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|{\partial\theta^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}=C\|{\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\theta_{t}(\tau)d\tau}\|_{\phi}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}

(128)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\theta_{t}(\tau)}\|d\tau\Big)^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\theta_{t}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+\frac{1}{4}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}.

In view of (
124
)–(
128
), (
123
) yields

\frac{1}{2\Delta t}\left(\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}\right)+\frac{\beta-1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\Big(\int_{0}^{1}K(|\gamma^{i}(s)|)ds\Big)|\nabla\rho^{i}-\nabla\rho_{h}^{i}|^{2}dx\\
\leq C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\rho_{tt}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+\frac{1}{2}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}+C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\theta_{t}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+C\|{\nabla\theta^{i}}\|^{2},

(129)

which leads to

\frac{\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}-\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i-1}}\|_{\phi}^{2}}{\Delta t}-\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|_{\phi}^{2}\leq C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\rho_{tt}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+C\Delta t\int_{t_{i-1}}^{t_{i}}\|{\theta_{t}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+C\|{\nabla\theta^{i}}\|^{2}.

(130)

By mean of discrete Gronwall’s inequality in Lemma
4
and the fact

\begin{split}\|{\vartheta_{h}^{i}}\|^{2}&\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{i}(1-\Delta t)^{-i+j-1}\Big(\Delta t\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|{\rho_{tt}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+\Delta t\int_{t_{j-1}}^{t_{j}}\|{\theta_{t}(\tau)}\|^{2}d\tau+\|{\nabla\theta^{j}}\|^{2}\Big)\\

since

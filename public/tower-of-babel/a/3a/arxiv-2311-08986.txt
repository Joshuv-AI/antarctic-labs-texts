Physical scale adiabatic regularization in cosmological spacetimes
Antonio Ferreiro, Samuel Monin, Francisco Torrenti

Abstract
We develop a new regularization method for the stress-energy tensor and the two-point function of free quantum scalar fields propagating in cosmological spacetimes. We proceed by extending the adiabatic regularization scheme with the introduction of two additional mass scales. By setting them to the order of the physical scale of the studied scenario, we obtain ultraviolet-regularized quantities that do not distort the power spectra amplitude at the infrared scales amplified by the expansion of the universe. This is not ensured by the standard adiabatic approach. We also show how our proposed subtraction terms can be interpreted as a renormalization of coupling constants in the Einstein equations. We finally illustrate our proposed regularization method in two scenarios of cosmological interest: de Sitter inflation and geometric reheating.



I
Introduction

Quantum field theory in curved spacetime provides the most adequate framework to study the dynamics of quantum fields propagating on classical background spacetimes
Parker and Toms 2009
;
Wald 1995
;
Birrell and Davies 1984
;
Hu and Verdaguer 2020
. A fundamental implication of the theory is the one of gravitational particle production, which takes place e.g. when the Universe expands non-adiabatically
Parker 1968
;
Parker 1969
;
Parker 1971
or near the event horizon of a black hole
Hawking 1975
. In the cosmological context, this provides a natural mechanism for the excitation of metric fluctuations during inflation
Guth 1981
;
Linde 1982
;
Albrecht and Steinhardt 1982
;
Starobinsky 1980
, which constitute the seeds for the formation of structure in the universe
Sasaki 1986
;
Mukhanov 1988
. The excited tensor perturbations also constitute a relevant source of primordial gravitational waves, whose amplitude is observationally upper bounded by CMB experiments
Ade et al. 2021
. Gravitational effects can also play an important role in the production of particles at the end of inflation
Ford 1987
or during reheating
Bassett and Liberati 1998
. For a recent review on cosmological particle production, see
Ford 2021
.

An essential problem when working with quantum fields in curved spacetimes is the one of renormalization, which is challenging even for free fields. For instance, if we consider a free scalar field
\phi
and compute the expectation value of its stress-energy tensor
\langle T_{\mu\nu}(x)\rangle
, we find new ultraviolet divergences that are not present in flat spacetime, and hence cannot be removed by a normal ordering procedure. A similar problem appears in the two-point function
\langle\phi(x)\phi(x^{\prime})\rangle
when evaluated at coincident spacetime points
x\to x^{\prime}
. Several regularization and renormalization methods to deal with these divergences have been developed for quantum fields in curved spacetimes, see e.g.
Parker and Toms 2009
;
Birrell and Davies 1984
for standard textbooks on the subject.

In this work we will focus on regularization in cosmological spacetimes, described by Friedmann-Lemaitre-Robertson-Walker (FLRW) metrics. An extensively used regularization scheme in these spacetimes is
adiabatic regularization

Parker and Fulling 1974
;
Fulling and Parker 1974
;
Fulling et al. 1974
, which is based on an adiabatic WKB-like expansion of the field modes. Given an unregularized quantity, one can identify its ultraviolet-divergent contributions by expanding it adiabatically up to a certain order, and then subtract the obtained terms to obtain a finite expression. Despite being specific to FLRW metrics, the method is equivalent to general curved background constructions
del Rio and Navarro-Salas 2015
;
Beltrán-Palau et al. 2021
, which ensures that observables are constructed in a local covariant way. It is also very convenient to use in numerical computations (see e.g.
Anderson and Parker 1987
;
Habib et al. 2000
;
Zago and Kosowsky 2019
) and can also be applied to the construction of preferred vacuum states
Agullo et al. 2015
;
Ferreiro and Pla 2022
. The method has been extended to the regularization of spin-1/2 fields
Landete et al. 2013
;
Landete et al. 2014
;
del Rio et al. 2014
;
Barbero G. et al. 2018
and spin-1 fields
Marañón González and Navarro-Salas 2023
.

However, although the subtraction terms obtained through the adiabatic expansion successfully remove the ultraviolet divergences, it has been shown that they can distort the amplitude at infrared scales, especially in the case of light scalar fields. For example, the regularized power spectrum of a light field in de Sitter spacetime (
m\ll H
) gets significantly suppressed at scales
k\gtrsim am
, and it is exactly zero in the massless limit
m\rightarrow 0

Parker 2007
. These results can potentially change the standard observable predictions of slow-roll inflation
Agullo et al. 2008
;
Agullo et al. 2009
;
Agullo et al. 2010
, and have been critically examined in several works
Finelli et al. 2007
;
Durrer et al. 2009
;
Urakawa and Starobinsky 2009
;
Marozzi et al. 2011
;
Agullo et al. 2011
;
Bastero-Gil et al. 2013
(see also
Woodard 2014
). This is still considered an open problem, as reflected by more recent studies that have tackled this issue from different perspectives. For example, in
Markkanen 2018
it was emphasized that different renormalizations can be found by expressing the subtraction terms in de Sitter space as counterterms in the Lagrangian. In
Animali et al. 2022
, an infrared cutoff was introduced in the adiabatic subtraction terms to tame the infrared distortions, and in
Corbà and Sorbo 2023
the authors developed a different method based on a resummation of the entire adiabatic expansion.

In any case, it is important to emphasize that the regularization program is in principle ambiguous, and different methods can indeed produce different results. This is already the case of perturbative quantum field theory in Minkowski spacetime, where this ambiguity is encapsulated in the renormalized coupling constants (we will later come back to this point in the case of the gravitational coupling constants). Motivated by this idea, in Ref.
Ferreiro and Torrenti 2023
we developed a new regularization method for the two-point function at coincident spacetime points, based on an extension of the standard adiabatic scheme. We proposed a new set of subtraction terms that successfully cancel the ultraviolet divergences but also minimize the introduced distortions at momenta scales
k\lesssim M
, where
M
is an arbitrary mass scale fixed by our choice of regularization scheme. This new method is compatible with the ambiguities allowed by the renormalization program and is consistent with local covariance (unlike e.g. introducing a hard infrared cutoff in the adiabatic subtraction terms, as shown in the Appendix of
Ferreiro and Torrenti 2023
). Our scheme yields, for a light scalar field in de Sitter space (
m\ll H
), a scale-invariant regularized power spectrum
\Delta_{\phi}^{\rm(reg)}\simeq H^{2}/(4\pi^{2})
at super-Hubble scales.

In this work we continue the program initiated in Ref.
Ferreiro and Torrenti 2023
, by extending the method to the regularization of the scalar field’s stress-energy tensor. As we shall see, in this case the subtraction terms will depend instead on two arbitrary mass scales
M_{\mathit{2}}
and
M_{\mathit{4}}
, which minimize the infrared distortions if they take sufficiently large values. As we shall see, their minimum required value to overcome the problem of the infrared distortions is around the physical scale of the studied scenario, e.g. the Hubble parameter for de Sitter inflation or the inflaton’s oscillation frequency for geometric reheating (see Sect.
V
for more details). For this reason, we have named our proposed regularization scheme
physical scale adiabatic regularization
(or ‘PSAR’).

The structure of the paper is as follows. In Sect.
II
we present the different equations describing the dynamics of scalars field in cosmological spacetimes and review the standard adiabatic regularization method. In Sect.
III
we present our proposed PSAR method and apply it to the regularization of the stress-energy tensor and the two-point function. In Sect.
IV
we interpret our regularization scheme in terms of renormalization of coupling constants, which we then fix according to a physically motivated renormalization condition. In Sect.
V
we illustrate our proposed regularization method in two scenarios of cosmological interest: de Sitter expansion and geometric reheating after inflation. In Sect.
VI
we discuss our results and conclude.

II.1
Adiabatic regularization

Let us now review the adiabatic regularization method for scalar fields
Parker 1968
;
Parker 1969
;
Parker 1971
, which allows us to obtain a set of subtraction terms with the appropriate ultraviolet behavior. The method is based on a WKB-like adiabatic expansion of the field modes, which up to
n
th order takes the form

\displaystyle\chi_{k}^{(n)}\equiv a\,h_{k}^{(n)}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2W_{k}^{(n)}(\tau)}}e^{-i\int^{\tau}W_{k}^{(n)}(\tau^{\prime})d\tau}\ ,\hskip 11.38092ptW_{k}^{(n)}\equiv\omega_{k}^{(0)}+\omega_{k}^{(1)}+...+\omega_{k}^{(n)}\ .

(20)

The superscripts in the different
\omega_{k}^{(j)}
terms (with
j=0,\dots n
) indicate their adiabatic order, defined as the number of time derivatives of the scale factor they contain (e.g.
a^{\prime\prime\prime}
is of order three, while
a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}
is of order four). We fix the zeroth order of the expansion to
\omega_{k}^{(0)}=\omega\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}}
. By substituting (
20
) into (
6
) and solving the equation iteratively for increasingly higher adiabatic orders, we can derive the expansion up to any finite order

, obtaining this way a unique expression of
\chi_{k}^{(n)}
. The first terms of the expansion are

\displaystyle\omega_{k}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\omega\ ,

(21)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{k}^{(3)}=0\ ,

(22)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\omega^{-1/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\omega^{-1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\omega^{-1}\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)a^{2}R\ ,

(23)

\displaystyle\omega_{k}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\omega^{(2)}\omega^{-3/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\omega^{-1/2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega^{-1}(\omega_{k}^{(2)})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\omega^{1/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\left(\omega^{-3/2}\omega^{(2)}\right)\ .

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{2}}=\left(\langle\phi_{k}^{2}\rangle[\chi_{k}^{(2)}]\right)^{(0-2)}=\left(\frac{1}{2a^{2}W_{k}}\right)^{(0-2)}=\frac{1}{2a^{2}\omega}-\frac{\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)R}{4\omega^{3}}-\frac{3}{16}\frac{\omega^{\prime 2}}{a^{2}\omega^{5}}+\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}}{8a^{2}\omega^{4}}.

III
Regularization without infrared distortions

In this paper we wish to construct a regularization scheme for the stress-energy tensor
that removes its ultraviolet divergences, while simultaneously fulfilling the following three conditions:

(i)

It must yield
\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle|_{\mathcal{M}}=0
in the Minkowskian limit. In other words, the regularization scheme must be equivalent to the well-known normal-ordering prescription in Minkowski spacetime.

(ii)

The regularized stress-energy tensor must be conserved, i.e.
\nabla^{a}\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle=0
.

(iii)

The regularization procedure must not significantly distort the amplitude of the power spectra (
17
) and (
18
) at the momenta scales amplified by the non-adiabatic expansion of the universe. More specifically, we require that
:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{c}\hskip-2.84544pt:\hskip-1.42271pt(k)\approx\Delta_{c}^{\rm(0s)}(k)
(with
c=\rho,p
) for all infrared modes
k\lesssim k_{+}
, where
k_{+}
is the maximum amplified momentum.

The standard adiabatic scheme reviewed in Sect.
II.1
does indeed satisfy conditions (i) and (ii). However, it fails to satisfy condition (iii). The reason is apparent if we inspect e.g. the subtraction terms for the two-point function (
25
): for momenta
k\gtrsim am
, contributions to the momentum integral from second- and higher-order terms of the expansion behave as
\sim k^{2}\cdot\omega^{-q/2}\sim k^{2-q}
with
q\gtrsim 3
. Therefore, if the maximum amplified physical momentum is larger than the field mass
ma\lesssim k_{+}
, the subtractions will introduce spurious infrared distortions. We shall see an example of this in Sect.
V.1
, where we consider a field with mass
m\ll H
(where
H
is the Hubble parameter) propagating in de Sitter spacetime. In this scenario we have
k_{+}\approx H
, so the adiabatic scheme does indeed introduce unwanted distortions.

The root of the problem lies in the zeroth-order term of the expansion, which in the standard adiabatic approach is set to
\omega_{k}^{(0)}=\omega=(k^{2}+a^{2}m^{2})^{1/2}
. It is thus desirable to construct a regularization scheme that incorporates the advantages of adiabatic regularization but also gets rid of this assumption, so that we are able to reproduce the spectrum at infrared scales. A first step in this direction was taken by some of us in
Ferreiro and Torrenti 2023
, where we developed such a construction for the regularized two-point function at coincident spacetime points. Here we generalize our results to the more complicated regularized stress-energy tensor.

Note that an important result in renormalization theory in curved spacetime is that two different regularization methods for the stress-energy tensor compatible with locality and covariance (or more generally satisfying the Wald axioms
Wald 1995
) can differ by a finite amount of geometrical terms

\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle-\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}=\alpha g_{ab}+\beta G_{ab}+\gamma\,\,\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}\ ,

(28)

where
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}
are three dimensionless parameters,
G_{ab}
is the Einstein tensor, and
\hskip-1.42271pt{}^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=2R_{;ab}-2g_{ab}\Box R-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R^{2}+2RR_{ab}
is a higher-order geometrical tensor
2
2

2

Note that if we were working in a general curved background, we would need to include a second higher-order tensor
\hskip-1.42271pt{}^{{}^{(2)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=R_{;ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}\Box R-\Box R_{ab}-\frac{1}{2}g_{ab}R_{ab}R^{ab}+2R^{cd}R_{cdab}
in (
28
). However,
\hskip-1.42271pt{}^{{}^{(2)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}
is proportional to
\hskip-1.42271pt{}^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}
in a FLRW spacetime so it is not independent.
.

We can make use of this arbitrariness to construct an alternative subtraction scheme that is equivalent to adiabatic regularization up to the geometrical terms in (
28
). Since adiabatic regularization has been shown to be equivalent to methods in general curved spacetimes
del Rio and Navarro-Salas 2015
;
Beltrán-Palau et al. 2021
, the new method will also be equivalent to these up to geometric arbitrariness. In the following section we show how to incorporate the allowed arbitrariness in the subtraction terms of
\langle\rho\rangle
,
\langle p\rangle
and
\langle\phi^{2}\rangle
.

III.1
Construction of the physical scale adiabatic regularization (PSAR) method

We now construct an alternative regularization method that fulfills all three conditions of the above list, using as a basis the arbitrariness allowed by Eq. (
28
). Our starting point is the work carried out in Ref.
Ferreiro and Navarro-Salas 2019
, in which the standard adiabatic expansion was generalized with the introduction of an arbitrary mass scale

\displaystyle\chi_{k}^{\prime\prime}+\left(\omega_{\mu}^{2}-\mu^{2}a^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)a^{2}R\right)\chi_{k}=0\ ,\hskip 14.22636pt\omega_{\mu}\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+\mu^{2}a^{2}}\ ,

(29)

where the mass parameter
\mu
has been introduced. As in the standard adiabatic procedure, we expand the field modes with the WKB-like template

\displaystyle\overline{\chi}_{k}^{(n)}\equiv a\,\overline{h}_{k}^{(n)}\sim\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\overline{W}_{k}^{(n)}(\tau)}}e^{-i\int^{\tau}\overline{W}_{k}^{(n)}(\tau^{\prime})d\tau}\ ,\hskip 11.38092pt\overline{W}_{k}^{(n)}\equiv\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(0)}+\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(1)}+...+\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(n)}\ ,

(30)

but now the zeroth order of the expansion is fixed to be
\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(0)}=\omega_{\mu}
(the overline notation will be used to denote terms constructed from this generalized
\mu
-adiabatic expansion). Higher-order terms of the expansion can be obtained by substituting the ansatz (
30
) into (
29
) and solving order by order, where the rest of the parentheses of (
29
) must be considered of adiabatic order two (i.e. in this expansion both
m^{2}
and
\mu^{2}
are of order two). We obtain

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(0)}

\displaystyle=\omega_{\mu}\ ,

(31)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(3)}=0\ ,

(32)

\displaystyle\hskip 11.38092pt\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu}^{-1/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\omega_{\mu}^{-1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu}^{-1}\left[\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)a^{2}R+a^{2}m^{2}-a^{2}\mu^{2}\right]\ ,

(33)

\displaystyle\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(4)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(2)}\omega_{\mu}^{-3/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\omega_{\mu}^{-1/2}-\frac{1}{2}\omega_{\mu}^{-1}(\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(2)})^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu}^{1/2}\frac{d^{2}}{d\tau^{2}}\left(\omega_{\mu}^{-3/2}\overline{\omega}_{k}^{(2)}\right)\ .

(34)

Note that these terms coincide with the standard adiabatic ones (
21
)-(
24
) for the choice
\mu=m
.

The
\mu
-dependent subtraction terms for the two-point function are obtained, as before, by expanding
\langle\phi_{k}^{2}\rangle
up to second order,

\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{2}}}\equiv\overline{\langle\phi_{k}^{2}\rangle}^{(0-2)}=\frac{1}{2a^{2}\omega_{\mu}}+\frac{(\mu^{2}-m^{2})}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}}-\frac{(\xi-\frac{1}{6})R}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}}-\frac{3}{16}\frac{\omega^{\prime 2}_{\mu}}{a^{2}\omega_{\mu}^{5}}+\frac{\omega^{\prime\prime}_{\mu}}{8a^{2}\omega_{\mu}^{4}}\ .

(35)

Similarly, one could in principle construct subtraction terms for the stress-energy tensor by simply expanding
\langle\rho_{k}\rangle^{(0-4)}
and
\langle p_{k}\rangle^{(0-4)}
up to fourth order. However, one can show that the regularized stress-energy tensor obtained this way is not conserved. We can solve this issue by modifying the subtraction terms as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{\rho}

\displaystyle\equiv\overline{\langle\rho_{k}\rangle}^{(0-4)}+\frac{\mu^{2}}{2a^{2}}\overline{\langle\phi_{k}^{2}\rangle}^{(4)}\ ,

(36)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p}

\displaystyle\equiv\overline{\langle p_{k}\rangle}^{(0-4)}-\frac{(1-4\xi)\mu^{2}}{2a^{2}}\overline{\langle\phi_{k}^{2}\rangle}^{(4)}\ ,

(37)

where the extra terms in
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\rho}}
and
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{p}}
are chosen such that the condition
\nabla^{a}\overline{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}=0
holds. Observe that the subtraction terms
\overline{\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{2}}}
,
\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{\rho}
and
\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p}
coincide with the standard adiabatic ones
\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{2}}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
and
\mathcal{S}_{p}
for
\mu=m
.

Note that some of the fourth-order subtraction terms obtained with (
36
) and (
37
) will be finite after integration in momenta and can be integrated out. They are hence not strictly necessary in order to remove the ultraviolet divergences of the stress-energy tensor. In that regard, it is convenient to decompose the subtraction terms as
\overline{S}_{c}=\overline{S}_{c}^{\rm(d)}+\overline{S}_{c}^{\rm(f)}
for
c=\rho,p
, where
\overline{S}_{c}^{\rm(d)}
contains all the divergent subtraction terms, and
\overline{S}_{c}^{\rm(f)}
is composed by the finite terms that can be expressed as geometric tensors after integration in momenta. The regularized stress-energy tensor can then be written as,

\displaystyle\overline{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int d\log k\left(-(\Delta_{p}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p})g_{ab}+(\Delta_{p}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p}+\Delta_{\rho}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{\rho})u_{a}u_{b}\right)

(38)

\displaystyle=\int d\log k\left(-(\Delta_{p}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p}^{\rm(d)})g_{ab}+(\Delta_{p}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{p}^{\rm(d)}+\Delta_{\rho}-\mathcal{\overline{S}}_{\rho}^{\rm(d)})u_{a}u_{b}\right)+T^{\rm(f)}_{ab}+\frac{\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)}{288\pi^{2}}\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}\ ,\hskip-8.5359pt

where in the second line we have defined the following geometric tensor,

\displaystyle T^{\rm(f)}_{ab}\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{45}R_{ac}R^{c}_{b}-\frac{1}{45}RR_{ab}-\frac{1}{135}\nabla_{a}\nabla_{b}R-\frac{1}{90}R_{cd}R^{cd}g_{ab}+\frac{1}{135}R^{2}g_{ab}+\frac{1}{135}\Box Rg_{ab}\right).\,\,\,\,\hskip-14.22636pt

(39)

Let us explicitly write the divergent part of the subtraction terms. For the energy density we obtain the following sum of zeroth-, second-, and fourth-order terms,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d})}_{\rho}\equiv

\displaystyle\,\,\,\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},0)}_{\rho}+\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},2)}_{\rho}+\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},4)}_{\rho}\ ,

(40)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},0)}_{\rho}=-\frac{\mu^{2}}{4\omega_{\mu}a^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{2a^{4}}+\frac{m^{2}}{4\omega_{\mu}a^{2}}-\frac{\mu^{4}}{16\omega_{\mu}^{3}}+\frac{\mu^{2}m^{2}}{8\omega_{\mu}^{3}}-\frac{m^{4}}{16\omega_{\mu}^{3}}\ ,

(41)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},2)}_{\rho}=\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(-\frac{9\mu^{4}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{2}}-\frac{3\mu^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{4}}-\frac{3a^{\prime 2}}{2\omega_{\mu}a^{6}}+\frac{9\mu^{2}m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{2}}-\frac{3m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{4}}\right)\ ,

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},4)}_{\rho}=\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{27\mu^{2}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{5}}+\frac{9a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{7}}-\frac{9a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{6}}+\frac{9a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{6}}\right)\ ,

(43)

while for the pressure density we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d})}_{p}\equiv

\displaystyle\,\,\,\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},0)}_{p}+\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},2)}_{p}+\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},4)}_{p}\ ,

(44)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},0)}_{p}=-\frac{\mu^{6}a^{2}}{16\omega_{\mu}^{5}}-\frac{\mu^{2}}{12\omega_{\mu}a^{2}}+\frac{\omega_{\mu}}{6a^{4}}+\frac{\mu^{4}m^{2}a^{2}}{8\omega_{\mu}^{5}}-\frac{\mu^{2}m^{4}a^{2}}{16\omega_{\mu}^{5}}-\frac{m^{2}}{12\omega_{\mu}a^{2}}-\frac{\mu^{4}}{48\omega_{\mu}^{3}}-\frac{\mu^{2}m^{2}}{24\omega_{\mu}^{3}}+\frac{m^{4}}{16\omega_{\mu}^{3}},

(45)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},2)}_{p}=\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(-\frac{15\mu^{6}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{7}}-\frac{3\mu^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{4}}+\frac{3\mu^{4}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{5}a}+\frac{\mu^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{3}}-\frac{3a^{\prime 2}}{2\omega_{\mu}a^{6}}+\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{\omega_{\mu}a^{5}}+\frac{15\mu^{4}m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{7}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 47.51604pt\left.-\frac{3\mu^{2}m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{2}}-\frac{3\mu^{2}m^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{5}a}-\frac{m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{4}}+\frac{m^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{3}}\right)\ ,

(46)

\displaystyle\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{({\rm d},4)}_{p}=\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{45\mu^{4}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{7}a^{3}}+\frac{18\mu^{2}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{5}}-\frac{9\mu^{2}a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{4}}-\frac{27\mu^{2}a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{5}a^{4}}+\frac{12a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{7}}-\frac{15a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{6}}\right.

\displaystyle\hskip 47.51604pt\left.-\frac{15a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{6}}+\frac{3a^{(4)}}{2\omega_{\mu}^{3}a^{5}}\right)\ .

(47)

One can show that the difference between the stress-energy tensors regularized with the adiabatic and
\mu
-adiabatic approaches can be written as in Eq. (
28
), where
\alpha
,
\beta
and
\gamma
depend on
\mu
. Therefore, both methods satisfy the Wald axioms. More generally, Eq. (
28
) allows us to introduce different arbitrary mass scales at zeroth, second, and fourth adiabatic orders, which we denote as
M_{0}
,
M_{2}
and
M_{4}
respectively. We can then change the subtraction terms as follows,

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,0)}_{\rho}(\mu),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,0)}_{p}(\mu)\}\,\,\,\,\,\,\to\,\,\,\,\,\,\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,0)}_{\rho}(M_{0}),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,0)}_{p}(M_{0})\}\ ,

(48)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,2)}_{\rho}(\mu),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,2)}_{p}(\mu)\}\,\,\,\,\,\,\to\,\,\,\,\,\,\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,2)}_{\rho}(M_{2}),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,2)}_{p}(M_{2})\}\ ,

(49)

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,4)}_{\rho}(\mu),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,4)}_{p}(\mu)\}\,\,\,\,\,\,\to\,\,\,\,\,\,\{\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,4)}_{\rho}(M_{4}),\mathcal{\overline{S}}^{(d,4)}_{p}(M_{4})\}\ .

(50)

Let us now analyze if these subtraction terms fulfill the three conditions listed above. Regarding condition (ii), the resulting regularized stress-energy tensor is conserved by construction. Regarding condition (i), the correct Minkowskian limit is satisfied only if we fix
M_{0}=m
at zeroth order, so we impose that condition from now on. Finally, regarding condition (iii), these terms guarantee that the distortions introduced at infrared scales are minimized for large enough values of
M_{2}
and
M_{4}
(we will illustrate this in two specific examples in Sect.
V
). Therefore, we define our definitive subtraction terms for the regularized stress-energy tensor as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{S}}^{({\rm d})}_{\rho}=

\displaystyle\,\frac{\omega}{2a^{4}}+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(\frac{9M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{2}w_{\mathit{2}}^{\,5}}-\frac{3m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{4}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}-\frac{9M_{\mathit{2}}^{4}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{2}w_{\mathit{2}}^{5}}-\frac{3M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{4}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}-\frac{3a^{\prime 2}}{2a^{6}w_{\mathit{2}}}\right)

(51)

\displaystyle+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{27M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2a^{5}w_{\mathit{4}}^{5}}+\frac{9a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{a^{7}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}-\frac{9a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{2a^{6}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}+\frac{9a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4a^{6}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}\right)\ ,

\displaystyle\mathcal{\widetilde{S}}^{({\rm d})}_{p}=

\displaystyle\,-\frac{m^{2}}{6a^{2}\omega}+\frac{\omega}{6a^{4}}+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(\frac{3M_{\mathit{2}}^{4}a^{\prime\prime}}{2aw_{\mathit{2}}^{5}}+\frac{M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2a^{3}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}+\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a^{5}w_{\mathit{2}}}-\frac{15M_{\mathit{2}}^{6}a^{\prime 2}}{4w_{\mathit{2}}^{7}}-\frac{3M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{4}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}-\frac{3a^{\prime 2}}{2a^{6}w_{\mathit{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)\left(-\frac{3m^{2}M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2aw_{\mathit{2}}^{5}}+\frac{m^{2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2a^{3}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}+\frac{15m^{2}M_{\mathit{2}}^{4}a^{\prime 2}}{4w_{\mathit{2}}^{7}}-\frac{3m^{2}M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{2a^{2}w_{\mathit{2}}^{5}}-\frac{m^{2}a^{\prime 2}}{4a^{4}w_{\mathit{2}}^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle+\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{3a^{(4)}}{2a^{5}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}-\frac{27M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4a^{4}w_{\mathit{4}}^{5}}-\frac{15a^{\prime\prime 2}}{4a^{6}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}-\frac{9M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{a^{4}w_{\mathit{4}}^{5}}-\frac{15a^{(3)}a^{\prime}}{2a^{6}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}+\frac{45M_{\mathit{4}}^{4}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{2a^{3}w_{\mathit{4}}^{7}}\right.

(52)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{18M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{a^{5}w_{\mathit{4}}^{5}}+\frac{12a^{\prime 2}a^{\prime\prime}}{a^{7}w_{\mathit{4}}^{3}}\right)\ ,

where we have defined
\omega\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}}
,
w_{\mathit{2}}\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}a^{2}}
and
w_{\mathit{4}}\equiv\sqrt{k^{2}+M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}a^{2}}
.

We name this new regularization method
physical scale adiabatic regularization
(or ‘PSAR’), since the two extra parameters are conceived to be of order of the physical energy scale of the system we are trying to probe, as we shall see in Sect.
IV
. This method does not only provide a consistent regularization mechanism, but it is also physical in the sense that it does not act on the infrared amplification of the quantum fluctuations, but only on the ultraviolet part. Note that the difference between the stress-energy tensors regularized with the adiabatic and PSAR schemes can be written as in Eq. (
28
), and hence the PSAR scheme is also compatible with the Wald axioms.

A similar exercise can be carried out for the two-point function at coincident spacetime coordinates. The subtraction terms provided by the
\mu
-regularization prescription have been given in Eq. (
35
). Analogously to the stress-energy tensor, we set
\mu=m
in the zeroth-order terms in order to get
:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{\phi^{2}}\hskip-2.84544pt:|_{\mathcal{M}}=0
in the Minkowskian limit, as well as remove the last two subtraction terms because they only provide a finite contribution (see Ref.
Ferreiro and Torrenti 2023
for details). We then have

\widetilde{\mathcal{S}_{\phi^{2}}}\equiv\frac{1}{2a^{2}\sqrt{k^{2}+m^{2}a^{2}}}-\frac{(\xi-\frac{1}{6})R}{4(k^{2}+M^{2}a^{2})^{3/2}}\ ,

(53)

where
M
is an arbitrary mass scale. Equations (
51
)-(
53
) are the most important results of this work.

IV
Renormalization conditions and coupling constants

We have constructed a regularized stress-energy tensor
\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle_{\bf{M}}}
that depends on the vector of arbitrary parameters
{\bf{M}}=(M_{\mathit{2}},M_{\mathit{4}})
through the subtraction terms (
51
)-(
52
). However, these parameters are in principle completely arbitrary and hence not physical, so we need to impose some renormalization conditions on
\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{M}}
in order to fix their observational value consistently. The quantity
\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{M}}
couples to the gravitational field via the semiclassical Einstein equations,

\displaystyle G_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}+\alpha\,\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=-8\pi G\left(\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{M}}+T_{ab}^{\rm clas}\right),

(54)

where we have included a term proportional to the second-order curvature tensor
\hskip-1.42271pt{}^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}
because it is necessary for reabsorbing the divergences of
\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle_{\bf{M}}}
. We have also added an unspecified classical stress-energy tensor
T_{ab}^{\rm clas}
in the source term for completeness. We wish to impose renormalization conditions so that the coupling constants
\{G,\Lambda,\alpha\}
in the Einstein equations can be exchanged by their physical values
\{G_{\rm o},\Lambda_{\rm o},\alpha_{\rm o}\}
observed today.

Let us consider a massive scalar field with mass
m
such that
\mathcal{R}\ll m^{2}
at late times in the evolution of the Universe, where
\mathcal{R}
includes all possible combinations of curvature tensors (e.g.
R
,
R_{ab}R^{ab}
, etc). We can then use the adiabatic expansion (
20
) to approximate the modes
\chi_{k}
at these late times. If we fix
M_{\mathit{2}}=M_{\mathit{4}}=m
for now, the subtraction terms in the PSAR scheme coincide with the standard adiabatic ones. It is hence not difficult to show that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{m}}=\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle\approx m^{-2}\mathrm{T_{ab}}+\mathcal{O}(m^{-4})\ ,

(55)

where
{\bf m}=(m,m)
and
\mathrm{T_{ab}}
is a tensor of adiabatic order six and therefore of dimension six. Therefore, in the late-time regime
\mathcal{R}\ll m^{2}
we can neglect the contribution of the quantum vacuum to the stress-energy tensor, and the semiclassical Einstein’s equations reduce to the classical ones used to measure the coupling constants
\{G_{\rm o},\Lambda_{\rm o},\alpha_{\rm o}\}
,

\displaystyle G_{ab}+\Lambda_{\rm o}g_{ab}+\alpha_{\rm o}\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=-8\pi G_{\rm o}T_{ab}^{\rm clas}\ .

(56)

In general we have

\displaystyle G_{ab}+\Lambda_{\rm o}g_{ab}+\alpha_{\rm o}\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=-8\pi G_{\rm o}\left(\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf m}+T_{ab}^{\rm clas}\right)\ ,

(57)

which is valid also when
\mathcal{R}\gtrsim m^{2}
(like in many early-universe scenarios).

We are now interested in incorporating the quantum regularized stress-energy tensor at arbitrary scales
M_{\mathit{2}}
and
M_{\mathit{4}}
. We then need to change the coupling constants adequately,

G_{ab}+\Lambda({\bf{M}})g_{ab}+\alpha({\bf{M}})\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}=-8\pi G({\bf{M}})\left(\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{M}}+T_{ab}^{\rm clas}\right)\ .

(58)

The difference between the stress-energy tensors regularized at scales
{\bf M}
and
{\bf m}
can be computed to be

\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf{M}}-\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf m}=\frac{\xi-\frac{1}{6}}{16\pi^{2}}\left(m^{2}-M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}+m^{2}\log{\left(\frac{M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)}\right)G_{ab}+\frac{(\xi-\frac{1}{6})^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\log{\left(\frac{M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)}\,\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}\ .\hskip 28.45274pt\hskip-28.45274pt

(59)

By subtracting Eq. (
57
) from (
58
) and substituting (
59
) into the resulting expression, we can obtain the following relations between the running couplings constants (which depend on
{\bf M}
) and their observed values today:

\displaystyle G(M_{\mathit{4}})=\frac{G_{\rm o}}{1+\frac{G_{\rm o}(\xi-\frac{1}{6})}{2\pi}\left(M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}-m^{2}-m^{2}\log{\left(\frac{M_{\mathit{2}}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)}\right)}\ ,

(60)

\displaystyle\Lambda(M_{\mathit{2}})=\frac{\Lambda_{\rm o}}{G_{\rm o}}G(M_{\mathit{2}})\ ,

(61)

\displaystyle\alpha(M_{\mathit{2}},M_{\mathit{4}})=\frac{G(M_{\mathit{2}})}{G_{\rm o}}\left(\alpha_{\rm o}-\frac{(\xi-1/6)^{2}}{\pi}\log{\left(\frac{M_{\mathit{4}}^{2}}{m^{2}}\right)}\right)\ .

(62)

The running of the coupling constants must be evaluated on a case-by-case basis. Taking into account that
G_{\rm o}=m_{\rm p}^{2}
, one can see that the change in the gravitational coupling constant is negligible if
(\xi-\frac{1}{6})(M_{\mathit{2}}/m_{p})^{2}\ll 1
, and hence in the cosmological constant
\Lambda
via Eq. (
61
). For example, in Sect.
V.1
we will consider the case of a scalar field with
m\ll H
and minimal coupling
\xi=0
in de Sitter space, where
H
is the Hubble parameter. As we shall see, we need to fix
M_{\mathit{2}}\sim H(\ll m_{p})
in order to minimize the infrared distortions, so this condition holds and the change in the coupling constants is negligible.

The running of the couplings
with scaling
\mu\sim H
has been widely studied in several works
Moreno-Pulido and Sola 2020
;
Moreno-Pulido and Sola Peracaula 2022a
;
Moreno-Pulido and Sola Peracaula 2022b
(including also fermions
Moreno-Pulido et al. 2023
) in the context of the running vacuum model, see
Sola Peracaula 2022
for a review. The possible cosmological consequences have also been studied in
Solà Peracaula et al. 2021
;
Sola Peracaula et al. 2023
.

We can show that the trace of the vacuum expectation value of the stress-energy tensor is given by

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T^{a}_{a}\,{:}\rangle}_{\bf M}=

\displaystyle(3(\xi-\frac{1}{6})\Box+m^{2})\left(\langle{:}\,\phi^{2}\,{:}\rangle_{\bf M}-\frac{R}{288\pi^{2}}\right)+{\rm Tr}_{a}^{a}-\frac{(m^{2}-M_{\mathit{2}}^{2})(\xi-1/6)}{16\pi^{2}}R

\displaystyle+\frac{m^{2}(\xi-1/6)}{8\pi^{2}}R\log\left(\frac{M}{M_{\mathit{2}}}\right)+\frac{3(\xi-1/6)^{2}}{8\pi^{2}}\Box R\log\left(\frac{M}{M_{\mathit{4}}}\right)\ ,

(63)

where we have defined

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{a}^{a}\equiv\frac{1}{64\pi^{2}}\left(-\frac{1}{45}R_{ab}R^{ab}+\frac{1}{135}R^{2}+\frac{1}{45}\Box R\right)-\frac{(\xi-1/6)^{2}}{32\pi^{2}}R^{2}\ .

(64)

One can observe that the relation between the trace of the stress-energy tensor and the two-point function is still maintained at the quantum level, up to renormalization freedom and the contribution of (
64
), which is nothing but the well-known trace anomaly in the conformal coupling limit
\xi=1/6
.

Finally, let us address an important consequence
of result (
59
). Let us recall that standard adiabatic regularization has been shown to be equivalent to Hadamard/DeWitt Schwinger regularization in four dimensions, see
del Rio and Navarro-Salas 2015
;
Pla and Winstanley 2023
. Since the difference between the stress-energy tensors regularized with the PSAR method and thestandard adiabatic one is a sum of covariant tensors, we can affirm that the PSAR method also yields a covariant stress-energy tensor.

V
Examples

In this section we apply our proposed PSAR method to two cosmological scenarios of interest: a light scalar field propagating in de Sitter spacetime (see Sect.
V.1
), and a scalar field non-minimally coupled to curvature getting excited through a process of geometric reheating after inflation (see Sect.
V.2
). In these examples we define the regularized power spectrum for the energy and pressure densities as
:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{c}\hskip-2.84544pt:\,\equiv k^{3}(\langle c_{k}\rangle-\mathcal{S}^{\rm(d)}_{c})/(2\pi^{2})
(where
c=\rho,p
), such that the regularized power spectrum takes the following form [see Eq. (
38
)]:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\langle{:}\,T_{ab}\,{:}\rangle}

\displaystyle=\int d\log k\left(-:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{p}\hskip-2.84544pt:g_{ab}+(:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{p}\hskip-2.84544pt:+:\hskip-2.84544pt\Delta_{\rho}\hskip-2.84544pt:)u_{a}u_{b}\right)+T^{\rm(f)}_{ab}+\frac{\left(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\right)}{288\pi^{2}}\,\hskip-1.42271pt^{{}^{(1)}\hskip-2.84544pt}H_{ab}\ .\hskip-8.5359pt

(65)

V.2
Geometric reheating

Let us now consider the regularization of a free scalar field after inflation when it is non-minimally coupled to the scalar curvature. This scenario was originally studied in Ref.
Bassett and Liberati 1998
, which coined the term ‘geometric reheating’ (see also
Tsujikawa et al. 1999a
;
Tsujikawa et al. 1999b
). In this setup, the post-inflationary oscillations of the inflaton give rise to tachyonic oscillations of the Ricci scalar, which generate field instabilities similar to the tachoynic preheating phenomenon studied in
Dufaux et al. 2006
. This has been recently studied as a mechanism for dark matter production in the early universe
Markkanen and Nurmi 2017
;
Fairbairn et al. 2019
;
Cembranos et al. 2020
;
Babichev et al. 2020
;
Lebedev et al. 2023
. In order to fully capture the non-linear dynamics of geometric reheating, one would need to simulate the fields with lattice simulations
Figueroa et al. 2023
. However, here we restrict ourselves to a linearized analysis, which is a good approximation at early times (when backreaction effects from the excited field onto the background fields can be neglected).

Let us denote as
\Phi
the inflaton field sourcing the inflationary expansion of the Universe. As an example we consider an inflationary chaotic model with quadratic potential
V(\Phi)=\frac{1}{2}m_{\Phi}^{2}\Phi^{2}
. We take the inflaton as homogeneous,
\Phi=\Phi(\tau)
, which is a valid approximation during the initial oscillations. The coupled equations of motion for the inflaton and scale factor are

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime\prime}+2H\Phi^{\prime}+m_{\Phi}^{2}a^{2}\Phi=0\ ,

(74)

\displaystyle H^{2}(\tau)\equiv\left(\frac{a^{\prime}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{3m_{p}^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{\prime 2}+\frac{1}{2}m_{\Phi}^{2}a^{2}\Phi^{2}\right)\ ,

(75)

and the evolution of the Ricci scalar is given by

R(\tau)=6\frac{a^{\prime\prime}}{a^{3}}=\frac{1}{m_{p}^{2}}\left(2m_{\Phi}^{2}\Phi^{2}-\frac{{\Phi^{\prime}}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right)\ .

(76)

We fix our initial conditions at the time when inflation ends
\tau_{*}
, defined when the first slow-roll parameter becomes
\epsilon_{\mathrm{v}}(\tau_{*})=1
. This condition holds when
\Phi\left(\tau_{*}\right)=\sqrt{2}m_{p}
. In order to obtain the inflaton time derivative at this time
\Phi^{\prime}\left(\tau_{*}\right)
, we solve the coupled equations (
74

75
) with initial conditions deep inside the slow-roll regime, and evolve them until
\tau=\tau_{*}
. From this we obtain
\Phi^{\prime}\left(\tau_{*}\right)\simeq-0.75m_{\Phi}m_{p}

a(\tau_{*})=1
.

VI
Summary and discussion

During the last decades, the development of a self-consistent renormalization program for free quantum fields in curved spacetimes compatible with covariance and locality has been successfully achieved for scalars, fermions and vector fields. The different designed methods produce unique stress-energy tensors and two-point functions up to arbitrary finite geometrical terms as expressed in Eq. (
28
). The ambiguous subtracted divergent terms can then be reabsorbed in the coupling constants in the Einstein’s equations, which can be measured experimentally. Although a mathematical framework for this method has been successfully established, less effort has been made in the development of an efficient methodology to do actual computations, both analytically and numerically. This work has aimed to fill in this gap in the context of cosmological spacetimes.

Even in the simplest case of free scalar fields, the expectation value of the stress-energy tensor (or the two-point function at coincident spacetime points) has ultraviolet divergences that cannot be removed through a normal ordering procedure as in Minkowski spacetime. A well-established regularization method when working in cosmological spacetimes is adiabatic regularization, which allows to identify the divergent terms of any quantity by means of an adiabatic expansion of the field modes. The method is compatible with locality and covariance, and is equivalent to regularization methods for generic curved backgrounds when particularized to FLRW spacetimes
del Rio and Navarro-Salas 2015
;
Beltrán-Palau et al. 2021
. It is also feasible to use in numerical computations, see e.g.
Anderson and Parker 1987
;
Habib et al. 2000
;
Zago and Kosowsky 2019
. However, the adiabatic subtraction terms for a given quantity can substantially modify the amplitude of its power spectrum at the momenta scales amplified by the non-adiabatic expansion, which we expect to be classical and then not affected by the quantum subtraction terms. We have denoted these modifications as ‘infrared distortions’ (in contrast with the ultraviolet tail, which is the one we expect to remove through regularization).

The aim of this work has been to build a new regularization method that both includes the ambiguities allowed by Eq. (
28
) and allows to build observables without infrared distortions. The result has been the set of subtraction terms (
51
)-(
52
) (for the stress-energy tensor) and (
53
) (for the two-point function), derived in Sect.
III
. The method is equivalent to the normal-ordering procedure in Minkowski spacetime, produces a conserved stress-energy tensor, and allows to construct regularized observables without infrared distortions. We have named our proposed method
physical scale adiabatic regularization
(PSAR), in order to differentiate it with respect to the standard scheme. Our method includes a set of arbitrary mass scales (
M
,
M_{\mathit{2}}
,
M_{\mathit{4}}
), allowed by Eq. (
28
), which can tame the infrared distortions of any observable if set large enough. Moreover, in Sect.
IV
we have interpreted the subtraction terms as redefinitions of the coupling constants set by an appropriately chosen renormalization condition. Finally, in Sect.
V
we have illustrated our method in two examples of cosmological interest: a scalar field propagating in a de Sitter space, and a scalar field being excited after inflation through a process of geometric reheating.

Let us now comment about possible extensions and applications of our work. First, in this work we have developed the PSAR method for scalar fields, but it would be interesting to extend our proposed method to other field species such as fermions. The standard adiabatic expansion for spin-1/2 fields was introduced in Ref.
Landete et al. 2013
;
Landete et al. 2014
;
del Rio et al. 2014
;
Barbero G. et al. 2018
and, unlike scalars, it is not based on a WKB template. However, it is not clear to us if a method to minimize infrared distortions is possible (or even physically meaningful) in this case: their occupation number is always
n_{k}<1
due to Pauli blocking, so there is not such a clear hierarchy between infrared and ultraviolet modes like there is for scalars. Similarly, it would be interesting to generalize our method to quantized fields coupled to homogeneous time-dependent backgrounds like in preheating scenarios, including both scalar preheating
Kofman et al. 1994
;
Kofman et al. 1997
;
Greene et al. 1997
and fermionic preheating
Greene and Kofman 1999
;
Giudice et al. 1999
;
Greene and Kofman 2000
;
Peloso and Sorbo 2000
. Adiabatic expansions for these scenarios have been developed for scalars
Anderson et al. 2008
and fermions
del Rio et al. 2017
, which could be a nice starting point to build a PSAR method for these setups. Our method could also be potentially extended to the case of quantum fields with interactions to classical electric fields

Ferreiro and Navarro-Salas 2018
;
Ferreiro et al. 2018
;
Beltrán-Palau et al. 2020
.

Finally, it would be interesting to apply our regularization method to the inflationary correlation functions. Their regularity at infrared scales has been extensively studied in the literature, see e.g.
Tanaka and Urakawa 2013
;
Hu 2018
;
Hu and Verdaguer 2020
and references therein, but ultraviolet effects are typically neglected. Our results suggest that this might be a reasonable assumption. However, note that here we have restricted ourselves to free scalar fields evolving in fixed FLRW metrics, while for such study we would need to consider both field interactions and metric perturbations.

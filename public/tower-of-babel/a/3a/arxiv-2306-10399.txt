New techniques for Gauge Theories in Projective Superspace
Ariunzul Davgadorj, Ulf Lindström, Rikard von Unge

Abstract
We introduce new techniques for calculations in Gauge theories with extended supersymmetry. We are working in Projective Superspace where the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) R-symmetry is realized geometrically by including an auxilliary ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} component in the superspace. Different gauge representations are associated with different dependence on the ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} coordinate ζ \zeta and using contour integrals on ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \mathbb{CP}^{1} we define natural projection operators on these different representations which leads to elegant formulas for all relevant objects. The new techniques lead to compact expressions for lagrangians and field strengths in terms of the gauge prepotential but also to effective ways of reducing superspace expressions to components, i.e. to write them in terms of fields transforming covariantly only under a subgroup of the supersymmetry group. We illustrate our findings in several examples in three and four dimensions.

II
Gauge Theory in Projective superspace

II.1
Projective superspace

Projective superspace is available as soon as there is an
SU(2)
R-symmetry in the theory. The R-symmetry is kept manifest by introducing an auxilliary projective space,
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, with coordinate
\zeta
. All superfields transform as sections of some holomorphic line bundle over this
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
.

The basic matter multiplet is a superfield
\Upsilon(\zeta)
, analytic around the north pole of the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
so it can be expanded as

\displaystyle\Upsilon(\zeta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Upsilon_{n}\zeta^{n}~.

(1)

Fields with this
\zeta
dependence are called Arctic multiplets. There are also fields that are analytic around the south pole of the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
, they can be expanded as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Upsilon}(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{0}\widetilde{\Upsilon}_{n}\zeta^{n}~,

(2)

and are called Antarctic multiplets.

Further, there are multiplets with polynomial dependence on
\zeta
. The
{\mathcal{O}}(2k)
multiplet is defined as

\displaystyle\eta(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-k}^{k}\eta_{n}\zeta^{n}~.

(3)

Of particular importance is the case where
k\rightarrow\infty
which is called the Tropical multiplet since it is singular at both the north- and the south-pole.

Conjugation of a generic superfield
T(\zeta)=\sum_{n}t_{n}\zeta^{n}
is given by hermitian conjugation of the coefficients
t_{n}
combined with the antipodal map on the
\mathbb{CP}^{1}

\displaystyle\overline{T}(\zeta)=\overline{\sum_{n}t_{n}\zeta^{n}}=\sum_{n}t^{\dagger}_{n}\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)^{n}~.

(4)

Using the conjugation it makes sense to define self-conjugate (or ”real”)
{\mathcal{O}}(2k)
multiplets by imposing
\overline{\eta}=\eta
which implies

\displaystyle\sum_{n=-k}^{k}\eta_{n}\zeta^{n}=\sum_{n=-k}^{k}(-1)^{n}\eta_{-n}^{\dagger}\zeta^{n}~.

(5)

This makes sense also for the Tropical multiplet where
k\rightarrow\infty
.

It is also possible to define
\zeta
dependent supercovariant derivatives that transform as sections of a line bundle of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
. The particular line bundle (and thus the
\zeta
dependence) is given by the representation of the derivatives under the R-symmetry. In the four dimensional theory with eight supercharges, there are two
SU(2)_{R}
doublets of supercovariant derivatives
D^{i}_{\alpha}
and
\overline{D}_{i\dot{\alpha}}
. They transform in the fundamental and antifundamental representation of
SU(2)_{R}
. Therefore, the definition of the
projective
supercovariant derivatives becomes

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=D^{1}_{\alpha}+\zeta D^{2}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\overline{D}_{2\dot{\alpha}}-\zeta\overline{D}_{1\dot{\alpha}}~,

(6)

and they anticommute among themselves. In the general discussion of this paper, we will often illustrate the new techniques on examples involving this particular situation, i.e. on the case of
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetric gauge theory in four dimensions. However, our results are more generally true. In other cases the projective derivatives look slightly different (see for instance equation (
102
) or (
145
)) but the main conclusions remain valid.

Since the supercovariant derivatives constructed in this way anticommute with each other they can be used to define constrained superfields.
In the
{\mathcal{N}}=2
case defined above the supercovariant projective derivatives (
6
) can be used to define constrained superfields
\Upsilon
as the kernel of this set of operators

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Upsilon=0~,\;\;\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Upsilon=0~.

(7)

We call such fields
projective
superfields

2

Sometimes also referred to as projectively chiral superfields.
. The most common instance is if the field
\Upsilon
is complex and has arctic
\zeta
dependence. It is then a projective version of the hypermultiplet, or some generalization thereof. In this case we also have the conjugate field
\overline{\Upsilon}
which is projective but with antarctic
\zeta

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xd^{4}\theta\oint\frac{d\zeta}{2\pi i\zeta}\overline{\Upsilon}\Upsilon

(8)

where the integral is over only half of the superspace since the fields are constrained. In what follows, we will suppress the

II.2
Gauge transformations and Gauge invariance

In this section we mimic the usual superspace gauging of super Yang-Mills as far as possible.

Given an arctic projecive field it can be transformed by a global phase rotation where
\Lambda
is a constant

\displaystyle\Upsilon\rightarrow e^{i\Lambda}\Upsilon~.

(9)

and the action (
8
) is invariant under this global symmetry.
To make this transformation local, we allow the parameter
\Lambda
to become a superfield itself. In order to respect the properties of
\Upsilon
we need
\Lambda
to also be projective with arctic
\zeta
dependence. Similarly we have

\displaystyle\overline{\Upsilon}\rightarrow\overline{\Upsilon}e^{-i\overline{\Lambda}}~,

(10)

where
\overline{\Lambda}
is the conjugate of
\Lambda
and thus projective with antarctic
\zeta
dependence.
The superspace lagrangian

\displaystyle\overline{\Upsilon}\Upsilon~,

(11)

which is invariant under the global phase rotation, is not invariant in the local case. In order to make it invariant under the local transformation we introduce the projective superspace gauge field
V(\zeta)
which is projective, self conjugate and with tropical
\zeta
dependence. Under gauge transformations it transforms as

\displaystyle e^{V}\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\Lambda}}e^{V}e^{-i\Lambda}~,

(12)

so that the expression

\displaystyle\overline{\Upsilon}e^{V}\Upsilon

(13)

is invariant.
Since
V
and the gauge parameters are projective superfields, the supercovariant derivatives
\nabla,\overline{\nabla}
are automatically also gauge covariant without adding any connection coefficients. Using
e^{V}
we may redefine fields to transform with only the arctic parameter
\Lambda
, or, alternatively with the antarctic parameter
\overline{\Lambda}
only. This we call the arctic/antarctic representation where derivatives of gauge covariant fields are also gauge covariant.

Alternatively one may split
e^{V}
into parts with only positive or negative powers of
\zeta

\displaystyle e^{V}=e^{\overline{U}}e^{U}~,

(14)

where
e^{U}
contains only non-negative powers of
\zeta
and its conjugate
e^{\overline{U}}
contains only non-positive powers. Under gauge transformations they transform

\displaystyle e^{U}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{iK}e^{U}e^{-i\Lambda}

\displaystyle e^{\overline{U}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\Lambda}}e^{\overline{U}}e^{-iK}~,

(15)

where
K
is a real
\zeta
independent superfield. Using
e^{U}
and
e^{\overline{U}}
we may convert all fields in the theory to transform with the parameter
K
only. This we call the vector representation. The transformation of the covariant derivatives in going from the polar to the vector representation is given by a similarity transformation

\displaystyle[\nabla_{\alpha}]_{v}=e^{U}\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-U}=e^{-\overline{U}}\nabla_{\alpha}e^{\overline{U}}=\nabla_{\alpha}+\Gamma_{\alpha}~.

(16)

The second equality follows from the fact that
V
is projective since then

\displaystyle 0=\nabla_{\alpha}e^{V}=\nabla_{\alpha}(e^{\overline{U}}e^{U})=(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{\overline{U}})e^{U}+e^{\overline{U}}(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{U})~.

(17)

We thus find that in the arctic/antarctic representations the supercovariant derivatives do not develop any nontrivial connection coefficients and they stay the same as the supercovariant derivatives in the ungauged theory. This should be contrasted to the situation in the vector representation in which the gauged supercovariant derivaties do have a nontrivial connection coefficient piece.

The field strength

\displaystyle e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}~,

(48)

with inverse

\displaystyle e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}~,

(49)

which can be written compactly defining the Hermitian and
\zeta
independent quantities

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}~,

\displaystyle L^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}~,

(50)

as

\displaystyle e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\Gamma^{(n)}~,

\displaystyle e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}

\displaystyle=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}L^{(n)}~.

(51)

Using the rules for conjugation of the fields including the contour integrals and the
\epsilon
-regulated
\zeta
-denominators given in Appendix
A
we can check that
\Gamma^{(n)}
and
L^{(n)}
are self conjugate and furthermore that

\displaystyle L^{(n)}-\Gamma^{(n)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\Gamma^{(k)}L^{(n-k)}=\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}L^{(k)}\Gamma^{(n-k)}~.

(52)

This we show by writing the left hand side using (
III.1
) as

\displaystyle L^{(n)}-\Gamma^{(n)}=\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}\right)~,

(53)

and then rewriting the
\zeta
factors in two different ways

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{n}}\left[(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n-1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1})\right]~,

(54)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n-1}}{\zeta_{n-1}\zeta_{n}}+\ldots+\frac{\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}}~,

and then canceling the
\zeta
-factors
(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{k-1})
that appears with the
\epsilon
-regulated
\frac{1}{\zeta_{k,k-1}}
factors in the numerator. That this is possible is again proven in Appendix
A
. We conclude that (
48
) and (
49
) are in fact inverses of each other.

In the following, it will also be useful to introduce the additional
\zeta
independent but Hermitian conjugate pair

\displaystyle A^{(n)}

\displaystyle=\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}~,

\displaystyle\overline{A}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=-\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n}}~.

(55)

Notice that since
e^{P}
is
\zeta
independent we may use any representation to calculate the connection coefficient of the
\zeta
derivative

\displaystyle{\cal A}=e^{-U}\partial_{\zeta}e^{U}=e^{-\widehat{U}}\partial_{\zeta}e^{\widehat{U}}=e^{-\widecheck{U}}\partial_{\zeta}e^{\widecheck{U}}~,

(56)

which can also be checked explicitly using the expressions (
38
),(
41
),(
43
) and (
44
).

It is also interesting to check how (
48
) and (
49
) transform under gauge transformations. An explicit calculation gives

\displaystyle\delta\left(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}\right)

\displaystyle=i\overline{\lambda}_{0}e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}-e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}i\lambda_{0}~,

(57)

\displaystyle\delta\left(e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}\right)

\displaystyle=i{\lambda}_{0}e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}-e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}i\overline{\lambda}_{0}~,

where
\lambda_{0}
and
\overline{\lambda}_{0}
are the
\zeta
-independent pieces of
\Lambda(\zeta)
and
\overline{\Lambda}(\zeta)
and are precisely the gauge parameters of the
\zeta
-
components
of the superfield. More precisely, the gauge field
V
transforms under gauge transformations as

\displaystyle\delta V=i\overline{\Lambda}-i\Lambda+\mathcal{O}(V)~.

(58)

This implies the
\zeta
-component transformations

\displaystyle\delta v_{0}

\displaystyle=i\overline{\lambda}_{0}-i\lambda_{0}+\mathcal{O}(v)~.

(59)

This allows us to identify
v_{0}
as the component gauge field with the standard component gauge transformation with
\lambda_{0},\overline{\lambda}_{0}
as the parameter.

Given the definition of
e^{P}=e^{\widehat{U}}e^{-U}
and the transformation properties of
e^{U}\rightarrow e^{iK}e^{U}e^{-i\Lambda}
we find that
e^{P}
and
e^{\overline{P}}
must transform as

\displaystyle e^{P}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\lambda}_{0}}e^{P}e^{-iK}~,

(60)

\displaystyle e^{\overline{P}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{iK}e^{\overline{P}}e^{-i\lambda_{0}}~,

and furthermore that

\displaystyle e^{\widehat{U}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\lambda}_{0}}e^{\widehat{U}}e^{-i\Lambda}~,

(61)

\displaystyle e^{\widecheck{U}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\lambda_{0}}e^{\widecheck{U}}e^{-i\Lambda}~,

which again can be confirmed by explicit calculation. This gives a nice interpretation of the fields
e^{\widehat{U}},e^{\widecheck{U}},e^{P},e^{\overline{P}}
as converters between the arctic, vector, chiral/component and antichiral/component representations.

III.2
The basic potentials

To illustrate the power of our new formalism we will calculate
\zeta
-independent expressions for the connection coefficients of the four dimensional (vector representation) gauge covariant derivatives as defined in (
16
)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=e^{U}(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-U})=e^{-P}e^{\widehat{U}}\nabla_{\alpha}(e^{-\widehat{U}}e^{P})=e^{-P}(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{P})+e^{-P}e^{\widehat{U}}(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-\widehat{U}})e^{P}

\displaystyle=e^{\overline{P}}e^{\widecheck{U}}\nabla_{\alpha}(e^{-\widecheck{U}}e^{-\overline{P}})=e^{\overline{P}}\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-\overline{P}}+e^{\overline{P}}e^{\widecheck{U}}(\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-\widecheck{U}})e^{-\overline{P}}~,

(62)

where
\nabla_{\alpha}=D^{1}_{\alpha}+\zeta D^{2}_{\alpha}=:D_{\alpha}+\zeta Q_{\alpha}
, thus defining
D_{\alpha}
and
Q_{\alpha}
. (See section
IV
for a thorough discussion of the four dimensional case.)
We would like to calculate
\nabla_{\alpha}e^{-\widecheck{U}}
. For notational simplicity, let us denote the
\zeta
coordinate on which they depend by
\zeta_{0}
. Then we need to calculate

\displaystyle\nabla_{0\alpha}e^{-\widecheck{U}(\zeta_{0})}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{0\alpha}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots\nabla_{0\alpha}X_{k}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{n}}~.

(63)

We may use that
X=e^{V}-1
is a projective superfield to write

\displaystyle\nabla_{0\alpha}X_{k}=\left(1-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{k}}\right)D_{\alpha}X_{k}=\left(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{k}\right)Q_{\alpha}X_{k}~,

(64)

By expanding the
\zeta
factors at hand as

\displaystyle(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{k})

\displaystyle=(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1})+(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})+\ldots+(\zeta_{k-1}-\zeta_{k})

(65)

if we decide to expand using
Q_{\alpha}
, or

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{0})}{\zeta_{0}\zeta_{1}}+\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1})}{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}}+\ldots+\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{k-1})}{\zeta_{k-1}\zeta_{k}}~,

(66)

if we decide to expand using
D_{\alpha}
. Then
canceling the
\epsilon
-regulated
\zeta
-factors in the denominator of (
63
) with the
\zeta
-factors from (
65
) or (
66
) we find a number of relations which we may reorganize to read

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\alpha}=e^{\overline{P}}D_{\alpha}e^{-\overline{P}}+\zeta e^{-P}Q_{\alpha}e^{P}~,

(67)

as well as

\displaystyle e^{-P}Q_{\alpha}e^{P}

\displaystyle=e^{\overline{P}}Q_{\alpha}e^{-\overline{P}}-e^{\overline{P}}D_{\alpha}\overline{A}e^{-\overline{P}}~,

(68)

\displaystyle e^{\overline{P}}D_{\alpha}e^{-\overline{P}}

\displaystyle=e^{-P}D_{\alpha}e^{P}+e^{-P}Q_{\alpha}Ae^{P}~,

where we have defined

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}A^{(n)}

\displaystyle\overline{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\overline{A}^{(n)}~,

(69)

using the definition (
III.1
).

Again it is useful to look at how these fields transform under gauge transformations. An explicit calculation gives

\displaystyle\delta A

\displaystyle=i\overline{\lambda}_{0}A-Ai\overline{\lambda}_{0}-i\overline{\lambda}_{-1}~,

(70)

\displaystyle\delta\overline{A}

\displaystyle=i\lambda_{0}\overline{A}-\overline{A}i\lambda_{0}-i\lambda_{1}~.

From the projectiveness of
\Lambda
and
\overline{\Lambda}
we find that

\displaystyle D_{\alpha}\lambda_{0}=\overline{D}_{\alpha}\overline{\lambda}_{0}=0~,

(71)

\displaystyle D^{2}\lambda_{1}=\overline{D}^{2}\overline{\lambda}_{1}=0~,

which means that
D^{2}\overline{A}
and
\overline{D}^{2}A
transform covaraintly as

\displaystyle D^{2}\overline{A}

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\lambda_{0}}D^{2}\overline{A}e^{-i\lambda_{0}}~,

(72)

\displaystyle\overline{D}^{2}A

\displaystyle\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\lambda}_{0}}\overline{D}^{2}Ae^{-i\overline{\lambda}_{0}}~,

appropriate for the component field strength in the chiral or antichiral representation. This will be confirmed in the next section.

III.3
The field strength

Similarly we may calculate expressions for the field strength (
22
) from the
\zeta
-connection coefficient (
26
)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{0}^{\dagger}=-\nabla_{0}^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{0}=-\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{\nabla_{0}^{2}[X_{1}\ldots X_{n}]}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n0}}~.

(73)

We let the projective derivatives act and like in (
64
) use that
X
is projective.
When we let the derivatives act there will be terms of the type

\displaystyle X_{1}\ldots\nabla_{0}^{\alpha}X_{l}\ldots\nabla_{0\alpha}X_{r}\ldots X_{n}~,

(74)

as well as terms where both derivatives act on the same
X

\displaystyle X_{1}\ldots\nabla_{0}^{2}X_{k}\ldots X_{n}~.

(75)

For the leftmost derivative we rewrite the
\zeta
-factor as in (
65
) or (
66
) but the for the derivative on the right we instead use

\displaystyle(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{r})

\displaystyle=(\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{n})+(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{n-1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{r+1}-\zeta_{r})~,

(76)

in the case we expand using
Q_{\alpha}
, or

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{r}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{0})}{\zeta_{0}\zeta_{n}}+\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{n-1}-\zeta_{n})}{\zeta_{n-1}\zeta_{n}}+\ldots+\frac{\zeta_{0}(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{r+1})}{\zeta_{r}\zeta_{r+1}}~,

(77)

in the case we expand using
D_{\alpha}
.
For the terms of type (
75
) we expand one of the
\zeta
factors according to (
65
) or (
66
) but the other one as in (
76
) or (
77
).
If we again cancel the differences of
\zeta
-factors in the numerator with the
\epsilon
-regulated
\zeta
-factors in the denominator we find that each term in the field strength can be written as a product of three factors which sum up to give

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\dagger}=e^{-\widecheck{U}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{\widecheck{U}}=e^{-\widehat{U}}(Q^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}}~.

(78)

All of the
\zeta
dependence is contained in the outer factors and could be removed by a similarity transformation. In a trace with similar objects, these factors drop out. Furthemore we can find the expression for the field strength in the vector representation by

\displaystyle{\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{U}{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}e^{-U}=e^{U}e^{-\widecheck{U}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{\widecheck{U}}e^{-U}=e^{\overline{P}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{-\overline{P}}

\displaystyle=e^{U}e^{-\widehat{U}}(Q^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}}e^{-U}=e^{-P}(Q^{2}A)e^{P}~,

(79)

which is manifestly
\zeta
independent.

III.4
The Lagrangian

The superspace Lagrangian is given by the interesting expression

\displaystyle{\cal L}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}{\rm Tr}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{1n}}~,

(80)

which was shown to be gauge invariant in
Jain and Siegel 2012b
. It can be thought of as a generalized Fayet-Iliopoulos term since if we would ignore the zeta dependence the sum could be performed with the result
\ln(1+X)=\ln(e^{V})=V
. However, the nontrivial
\zeta
dependence makes the behavior much more interesting as we shall see in the next section.

It is also interesting to investigate how this term transforms under gauge transformations. Since the basic field
X
transforms as

\displaystyle\delta X=i\overline{\Lambda}-i\Lambda+i\overline{\Lambda}X-Xi\Lambda

(81)

one finds that there has to be cancellation between terms of different order of
X
. In particular one finds that at order
X^{0}
, the term transforms as an ordinary FI-term, with a ”Kähler gauge transformation”.

IV
Examples

IV.1
{\cal N}=2
in four dimensions

IV.1.1
The algebra

In
4d
the
{\cal N}=2
superspace has an
SU(2)
R-symmetry. The covariant derivatives transform in the fundamental and antifundamental representation of the R-symmetry. This leads to the algebra

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{D}}^{i}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{D}}^{k}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon^{ik}C_{\alpha\beta}{\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}~,

\displaystyle\{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{i\dot{\alpha}},\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{k\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{ik}C_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{\mathbb{W}}~,

(82)

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{D}}^{i}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{k\dot{\beta}}\}

\displaystyle=i\delta^{i}_{k}\nabla_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}~.

From the Bianchi identities we learn that

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{i\dot{\alpha}}{\mathbb{W}}={\mathcal{D}}^{i}_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}=0~.

(83)

Introducing the projective derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}=D^{1}_{\alpha}+\zeta D^{2}_{\alpha}=:D_{\alpha}+\zeta Q_{\alpha}~,\;\;\;~~~~~\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}}=\overline{D}_{2\dot{\alpha}}-\zeta\overline{D}_{1\dot{\alpha}}=:\overline{Q}_{\dot{\alpha}}-\zeta\overline{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}~,

(84)

analytic around the north pole of
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
and anticommuting among themselves, we may introduce constrained superfields by

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Upsilon=\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Upsilon=0~.

(85)

As mentioned below (
6
),
such fields will be called projective superfields. Although the projective derivatives anticommute taken at the same point in
\zeta
, when we take the anticommutator between projective derivatives at different
\zeta
coordinates, they do not anticommute any more (c.f. (
II.3
))

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta_{1}),\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta_{2})\}

\displaystyle=-C_{\alpha\beta}(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1}){\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}~,

(86)

\displaystyle\{\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}}(\zeta_{1}),\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\beta}}(\zeta_{2})\}

\displaystyle=C_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1}){\mathbb{W}}~,

which gives us the possibility to express the field strength by introducing the
\zeta
derivative as

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha},[\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta},\nabla_{\beta}]\}

\displaystyle=-C_{\alpha\beta}{\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}~,

(87)

\displaystyle\{\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\alpha}},[\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta},\overline{\nabla}_{\dot{\beta}}]\}

\displaystyle=C_{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}{\mathbb{W}}~.

IV.1.2
The field strength

As we have seen, this leads to the field strength in the Arctic representation given by

\displaystyle{\cal W}

\displaystyle=\overline{\nabla}^{2}{\cal A}~,

(88)

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=-\nabla^{2}{\cal A}~,

and, as we showed in section
III.3
, we can write

\displaystyle{\cal W}

\displaystyle=e^{-\widehat{U}}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}}=e^{-\widecheck{U}}(\overline{Q}^{2}\overline{A})e^{\widecheck{U}}=e^{-U}e^{-P}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{P}e^{U}~,

(89)

\displaystyle{\cal W}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{-\widecheck{U}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{\widecheck{U}}=e^{-\widehat{U}}(Q^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}}=e^{-U}e^{\overline{P}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{-\overline{P}}e^{U}~,

where
A
and its conjugate
\overline{A}
are
\zeta
independent fields defined in (
III.2
).
As shown in section
III.3
, the field strength in the vector representation can be written as

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}

\displaystyle=e^{-P}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{P}~,

(90)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{W}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=e^{\overline{P}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{-\overline{P}}~.

IV.1.3
The action

The action is the generalized Fayet-Iliopoulos term we introduced in section
III.4
. It is integrated over the full superspace which in this case is

\displaystyle\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta=\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\overline{Q}^{2}~.

(91)

We may write the final
\overline{Q}^{2}
in the measure as
D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\overline{Q}^{2}=D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}(\overline{Q}-\zeta\overline{D})^{2}=D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\overline{\nabla}^{2}
since the terms that were added are dependent on
\overline{D}
and are projected out by the rest of the measure. We may also choose the
\zeta
coordinate of the
\nabla^{2}
operator in the measure arbitrarily since the terms containing
\zeta
are projected out in the same way.
Taking this into account and choosing the
\zeta
in
\nabla
to be
\zeta_{1}
we write the action as

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\int d^{4}x\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\overline{\nabla}_{1}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}X_{2}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{1n}}\right]

(92)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\int d^{4}x\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\overline{\nabla}_{1}^{2}(X_{2}\ldots X_{n})}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{1n}}\right]\;.

Concentrating on the expression in the trace we act with the
\overline{\nabla}_{1}
derivatives and, analogously to the procedure in section
III.3
, use that on any projective field
\overline{\nabla}_{1}X_{k}=(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{1})\overline{D}X_{k}

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\overline{\nabla}_{1}^{2}(X_{2}\ldots X_{n})}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{1n}}\right]\;

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=2}^{n-1}\sum_{r=l+1}^{n}(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{1})(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{1})\;{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\ldots\overline{D}^{\dot{\alpha}}X_{l}\ldots\overline{D}_{\dot{\alpha}}X_{r}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{1n}}\right]\;

(93)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{n}(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{1})^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\ldots\overline{D}^{2}X_{k}\ldots\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{1n}}\right]\;.

Following the procedure described in section
III.3
we then rewrite
(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{1})=(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{l-1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1})
and
(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{1})=(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{r+1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{1})
as well as
(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{1})^{2}=\left[(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{k-1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1})\right]\times\left[(\zeta_{k}-\zeta_{k+1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{1})\right]
.
Canceling the
\zeta
factors in the numerator with the factors in the denominator and using the cyclicity of the trace we get

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{k}{n}A^{(k)}\overline{D}^{2}A^{(n-k)}~.

(94)

Integrating by parts, the expression (
94
) can be rewritten

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{n-k}{n}A^{(k)}\overline{D}^{2}A^{(n-k)}~.

(95)

Averaging over the two alternative expressions we get

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}A^{(k)}\overline{D}^{2}A^{(n-k)}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\;{\rm Tr}A\overline{D}^{2}A=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\overline{D}^{2}A\overline{D}^{2}A~,

(96)

where
A=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}A^{(k)}
as defined before. This is indeed the Yang-Mills action

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\;{\rm Tr}\mathbb{W}\mathbb{W}=-\frac{1}{2}\int d^{4}xD^{2}Q^{2}\oint\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}{\rm Tr}\mathcal{W}\mathcal{W}~,

(97)

with
{\mathbb{W}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-P}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{P}
and
{\mathcal{W}}=e^{-\widehat{U}}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}}
. In
Davgadorj and von Unge 2018
we presented a proof of the equivalence of the Yang-Mills action in the form (
80
) and (
97
) which was rather contrived. Using the new methods introduced in this paper we have presented a straightforward proof.

IV.2
{\cal N}=3
in three dimensions

This theory has been studied before in Harmonic superspace by Zupnik
Zupnik:1997nn
and in Projective superspace in
Arai and Sasaki 2012
;
Arai and Sasaki 2013
;
Arai and Sasaki 2014
albeit in an
{\mathcal{N}}=3
unitary gauge.

IV.2.1
The algebra

The
\mathcal{N}=3
superspace has
SO(3)
R-symmetry where the three supercovariant derivatives transform as a triplet. We index them with a symmetric pair of fundamental
SU(2)
indices. Including a Yang-Mills field and using the symmetry properties of the covariant derivatives, we can write down the most general algebra which gives the algebra of the gauge super covariant derivtives

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{D}}^{(ik)}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{D}}^{(lm)}_{\beta}\}=\left(\epsilon^{il}\epsilon^{km}+\epsilon^{im}\epsilon^{kl}\right)i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}+\frac{i}{2}C_{\alpha\beta}\left(\epsilon^{il}W^{km}+\epsilon^{kl}W^{im}+\epsilon^{im}W^{kl}+\epsilon^{km}W^{il}\right)

(98)

where the field strengths
W^{ik}
is again given by an
SU(2)_{R}
triplet of fields encoded by a symmetric pair of fundamental
SU(2)
indices. Renaming the derivatives as
\mathcal{D}^{11}\equiv\mathcal{D},\;\;\mathcal{D}^{22}\equiv\overline{\mathcal{D}}\;\;
and
i\mathcal{D}^{12}\equiv\mathcal{D}^{0}
and the field strengths as
W^{11}\equiv{W}^{\dagger},\;\;W^{22}\equiv W
and
iW^{12}\equiv W^{0}
we get

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{D}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\beta}\right\}=2i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}+2C_{\alpha\beta}W^{0}~,

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\alpha},\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\beta}\right\}=i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}~,

(99)

\displaystyle\left\{\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\alpha},\mathcal{D}_{\beta}\right\}=C_{\alpha\beta}{W}^{\dagger}~,

\displaystyle\left\{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},\mathcal{D}^{0}_{\beta}\right\}=C_{\alpha\beta}W~.

As a consequence of the Bianchi identities we then have additional conditions for the field strengths

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}=0=\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W\qquad(chirality)~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{0}{W}^{\dagger}=-\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}W^{0}~,

(100)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{0}W=-\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W^{0}~,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}^{0}W^{0}=\frac{1}{4}\left(\mathcal{D}_{\alpha}W+\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}\right)~,

which further imply that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}^{2}W^{0}=\overline{\mathcal{D}}^{2}W^{0}=0~,

(101)

meaning that
W^{0}
is a real linear superfield from the point of view of the
{\cal N}=2
superfield components.

These relations clearly show that the supercovariant derivatives can be put together into a projective supercovariant derivative that transforms as an
{\mathcal{O}}(2)
multiplet

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta)=\frac{1}{\zeta}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}+2{\mathcal{D}}^{0}_{\alpha}-\zeta\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}~,

(102)

and anticommute among themselves

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta),\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta)\}=0~.

(103)

Similarly the three field strengths can also be put into an
{\mathcal{O}}(2)
multiplet

\displaystyle{\mathbb{W}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}{W}^{\dagger}+2W^{0}-\zeta W~.

(104)

Using this description, the Bianchi identities can be compactly written

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}=0~,

(105)

i.e. by saying that the field strength is Projective. This theory has been discussed before in Projective superspace
Arai and Sasaki 2012
;
Arai and Sasaki 2013
;
Arai and Sasaki 2014
where they however were working in
\mathcal{N}=3
unitary gauge where all the components of
V(\zeta)
except for
v_{-1},v_{0}
and
v_{1}
are gauged away.

IV.2.2
The field strength

The projective supercovariant derivatives anticommute even when the algebra is gauged. To get nontrivial results one needs to look at the anti commutators between derivatives at different
\zeta
points

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta_{1}),\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta_{2})\}

\displaystyle=-\frac{(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})^{2}}{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}}2i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+2C_{\alpha\beta}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{2}}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\right)\overline{W}+\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{2}}-\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}\right)W^{0}+\left(\zeta_{2}-\zeta_{1}\right)W\right)

(106)

which, by introducing the
\zeta
derivative, can be used to write the field strength as

\displaystyle C_{\alpha\beta}\frac{2}{\zeta}{\mathbb{W}}(\zeta)=-\{\nabla_{\alpha},[\partial_{\zeta},\nabla_{\beta}]\}~,

(107)

or, by going to the arctic representation as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}(\zeta)=-\frac{\zeta}{2}\nabla^{2}{\mathcal{A}}=\frac{1}{\zeta}{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}+2{\mathcal{W}}^{0}-\zeta{\mathcal{W}}~,

(108)

where again
{\mathcal{A}}=e^{-U}\partial_{\zeta}e^{U}
.

We may now calculate explicit expressions for the field strength using this formula. Here we will use that when acting on a projective superfield we can write

\displaystyle\nabla_{0\alpha}X_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{0})\overline{D}_{\alpha}X_{1}+\frac{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{0}\zeta_{1}}D_{\alpha}X_{1}\right]~.

(109)

We now calculate the field strength from (
108
) using (
26
)

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{0}=-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\nabla_{0}^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{0}=-\frac{\zeta_{0}}{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n+1}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{\nabla_{0}^{2}[X_{1}\ldots X_{n}]}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n0}}\right]~.

(110)

Concentrating on the expression in the trace we get

\displaystyle\nabla_{0}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n0}}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{n-1}\sum_{r=l+1}^{n}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots\nabla_{0}^{\alpha}X_{l}\ldots\nabla_{0\alpha}X_{r}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n0}}\right]

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}\frac{X_{1}\ldots\nabla_{0}^{2}X_{k}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}}\frac{1}{\zeta_{n0}}\right]~.

(111)

Using (
109
) there will be three different types of contributions with different
\zeta_{0}
dependence. One with two
D
derivatives, one with two
\overline{D}
derivatives and a mixed one with one
D
and one
\overline{D}
. The terms with two
\overline{D}
or two
D
operators are treated completely analogously to what we did before. Identifying components by comparing the powers of
\zeta
in (
108
) the result is

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\widehat{U}}\overline{D}^{2}A\;e^{\widehat{U}}~,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\widecheck{U}}D^{2}\overline{A}\;e^{\widecheck{U}}~.

(112)

This result is given in the Arctic representation. Going back to the vector representation by conjugating with
e^{U}
we get

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{-P}\overline{D}^{2}A\;e^{P}~,

\displaystyle{W}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}e^{\overline{P}}D^{2}\overline{A}e^{-\overline{P}}~.

(113)

The mixed term is harder to analyze. The
\zeta
factors will be either

\displaystyle(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{0})\times\frac{\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{0}\zeta_{r}}~,

(114)

if the
\overline{D}
operator is on the left of the
D
operator or

\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{0}\zeta_{l}}\times(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{0})~,

(115)

in the opposite case. In both cases we rewrite

\displaystyle(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{0})(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{0})=\left[(\zeta_{l}-\zeta_{l-1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{0})\right]\times\left[(\zeta_{r}-\zeta_{r+1})+\ldots+(\zeta_{n}-\zeta_{0})\right]

(116)

and we also always expand

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{l}}=\frac{\zeta_{l+1}-\zeta_{l}}{\zeta_{l}\zeta_{l+1}}+\ldots+\frac{\zeta_{f}-\zeta_{f-1}}{\zeta_{f-1}\zeta_{f}}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{f}}~,

(117)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{r}}=\frac{\zeta_{r+1}-\zeta_{r}}{\zeta_{r}\zeta_{r+1}}+\ldots+\frac{\zeta_{f}-\zeta_{f-1}}{\zeta_{f-1}\zeta_{f}}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{f}}~,

where
\zeta_{f}
is the
\zeta
coordinate of the
X_{f}
where the denominator
\frac{1}{\zeta_{f+1,f}}
was canceled by a
(\zeta_{f+1}-\zeta_{f})
from the expansion (
116
).
Keeping track of signs and factors, this can be summed to

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\widehat{U}}\overline{D}^{\alpha}\left(D_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}})e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-{P}}\right)e^{\widehat{U}}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-\widecheck{U}}D^{\alpha}\left(e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}\overline{D}_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}})\right)e^{\widecheck{U}}~,

(118)

or in the vector representation

\displaystyle W^{0}=\frac{1}{2}e^{-P}\overline{D}^{\alpha}\left(D_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}})e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-{P}}\right)e^{P}=\frac{1}{2}e^{\overline{P}}D^{\alpha}\left(e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}\overline{D}_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}})\right)e^{-\overline{P}}~.

(119)

IV.2.3
The action

The Yang-Mills action is constructed using the projective superfield
\bm{\mathcal{W}}
. Since the field is projective the action is integrated with the projective measure
D^{2}\overline{D}^{2}

\displaystyle I_{N3YM}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\oint\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}{\rm Tr}(\bm{\mathcal{W}}^{2}(\zeta))=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}(({\mathcal{W}}^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{\mathcal{W}}{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}((W^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}W{W}^{\dagger})~.

(120)

Using the Bianchi identities (
IV.2.1
) this can equivalently be written

\displaystyle I_{N3YM}=\int d^{3}xD^{2}(D^{0})^{2}{\rm Tr}W^{2}=\int d^{3}x\overline{D}^{2}(D^{0})^{2}{\rm Tr}({W}^{\dagger})^{2}~.

(121)

Our next question is what theory the FI term describes. In
{\mathcal{N}}=3
in three dimensions, the full measure
d^{3}xd^{6}\theta
is dimensionless. Since the generalized FI term is also dimensionless, there is no need to introduce a dimensionfull coupling constant which indicates that the theory defined in this way could be Chern-Simons theory. To investigate this we now go to components

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xd^{6}\theta\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{n}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{1n}}\right]~.

(122)

Writing the measure
d^{6}\theta=D^{2}\overline{D}^{2}(D^{0})^{2}\propto D^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\nabla_{1}^{2}
we act with the
\nabla^{2}
again using that
X
is projective

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{n}{\rm Tr}\left[\frac{X_{1}\nabla_{1}^{2}(X_{2}\ldots X_{n})}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{n,n-1}\zeta_{1n}}\right]~.

(123)

We expand the
\zeta
factors in the same way as for the field strength with the result

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left[A\overline{D}^{2}A+\overline{A}D^{2}\overline{A}+f_{D,\overline{D}}\right]~,

(124)

where
f_{D,\overline{D}}
denotes the mixed term with one
D
and one
\overline{D}
which will require more work to analyze. Note that the first two terms can be written as superpotentials

\displaystyle\int d^{3}xD^{2}{\rm Tr}(W^{2})+\int d^{3}x\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}(({W}^{\dagger})^{2})~.

(125)

As stated, the mixed term is more complicated. It can be written as

\displaystyle\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{0\leq k+l\leq n}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}{\rm Tr}\left[R^{(k)}\overline{D}^{\alpha}((D_{\alpha}\Gamma^{(l)})L^{(n-k-l)})\right]~,

(126)

where

\displaystyle R^{(k)}=\oint d\zeta_{1}\ldots d\zeta_{k}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}+\ldots+\frac{1}{\zeta_{k}}\right)\frac{X_{1}\ldots X_{k}}{\zeta_{21}\ldots\zeta_{k,k-1}}~.

(127)

We have not been able to perform the sum but to at least partly analyze the meaning of this term we put all components of
V=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}v_{n}\zeta^{n}
to zero except for
v_{0}
which is the
{\mathcal{N}}=2
gauge field. Then
X\rightarrow e^{v_{0}}-1
becomes
\zeta
independent and thus commute with each other. Also, many of the
\zeta
integrals now vanish, the only nonzero integrals are

\displaystyle L^{(k)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow X^{k}

\displaystyle\Gamma^{(1)}

\displaystyle\rightarrow X

(128)

Using this we can write the mixed term as

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}(X^{k-1}\overline{D}^{\alpha}((D_{\alpha}X)X^{n-k})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}{\rm Tr}((\overline{D}^{\alpha}X^{k-1})(D_{\alpha}X)X^{n-k})~.

(129)

To be able to perform the sum it is useful to write the expression in operator formalism where

\displaystyle DX\rightarrow[D,X]~.

(130)

Then the mixed term becomes

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}{\rm Tr}([D^{\alpha},X]\sum_{k=1}^{n}X^{n-k}[\overline{D}_{\alpha},X^{k-1}])~,

(131)

and writing out the commutators explicitly the sum that we would like to do is

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{n+1}}{n}\left(\overline{D}_{\alpha}X^{n-1}+X\overline{D}_{\alpha}X^{n-2}+\ldots+X^{n-1}\overline{D}_{\alpha}-nX^{n-1}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)~.

(132)

In the last term the factor
n
cancels the
\frac{1}{n}
and gives a geometric series. The first
n
terms also gives a sumable expression in the following way. If we would replace the
\overline{D}
with a
\delta X
the result of the sum would be
\delta\ln(1+X)=\delta v_{0}
. The idea is then to rewrite
\delta v_{0}
in therms of
\delta X=\delta e^{v_{0}}
where finally we can change back
\delta X\rightarrow\overline{D}
for the final result. We do this using the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
defined as
{\mathcal{L}}X=[v_{0},X]
as explained in
Gates et al. 1983

\displaystyle\delta v_{0}=\frac{\mathcal{L}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}e^{-v_{0}}\delta e^{v_{0}}=\frac{\mathcal{L}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}e^{-v_{0}}\delta X~.

(133)

Following our argument we now conclude that the first
n
terms sum to
\frac{\mathcal{L}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}e^{-v_{0}}\overline{D}_{\alpha}
and that the full result can be written as

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},X]\left\{\frac{\mathcal{L}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}e^{-v_{0}}-\frac{1}{1+X}\right\}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)~,

(134)

where the first term in the curly brackets comes from summing the the first
n
terms of (
132
) and the second term is the result of the geometric sum from the last term.
Since
1+X=e^{v_{0}}
this can be written as

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},e^{v_{0}}]e^{-v_{0}}\left\{\frac{\mathcal{L}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}-1\right\}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},e^{v_{0}}]e^{-v_{0}}\left\{\frac{{\mathcal{L}}-1+e^{-\mathcal{L}}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}\right\}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)~.

(135)

Similarly we may rewrite
[D^{\alpha},e^{v_{0}}]e^{-v_{0}}=\frac{e^{\mathcal{L}}-1}{\mathcal{L}}[D^{\alpha},v_{0}]
. Finally, using the cyclicity of the trace implying
{\rm Tr}(({\mathcal{L}}B)C)={\rm Tr}([v_{0},B]C)=-{\rm Tr}(B[v_{0},C])={\rm Tr}(B(-{\mathcal{L}}C))
, where
B
and
C
are arbitrary expressions, we write

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},v_{0}]\frac{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}{\mathcal{L}}\frac{{\mathcal{L}}-1+e^{-\mathcal{L}}}{1-e^{-\mathcal{L}}}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},v_{0}]\frac{{\mathcal{L}}-1+e^{-\mathcal{L}}}{\mathcal{L}}\overline{D}_{\alpha}\right)

(136)

\displaystyle=-\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left([D^{\alpha},v_{0}]\frac{{\mathcal{L}}-1+e^{-\mathcal{L}}}{{\mathcal{L}}^{2}}[\overline{D}_{\alpha},v_{0}]\right)

which, if we go back to standard notation
[\overline{D}_{\alpha},v_{0}]\rightarrow\overline{D}_{\alpha}v_{0}
and expand the function of
{\mathcal{L}}
in a power series can be written

\displaystyle S_{D\overline{D}}(v_{0})=-\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}\left(\frac{1}{2}D^{\alpha}v_{0}\overline{D}_{\alpha}v_{0}-\frac{1}{6}D^{\alpha}v_{0}[v_{0},\overline{D}_{\alpha}v_{0}]+\frac{1}{24}D^{\alpha}v_{0}[v_{0},[v_{0},\overline{D}_{\alpha}v_{0}]]+\ldots\right)

(137)

which is indeed a known form for the action of
{\mathcal{N}}=2
Chern-Simons theory
Ivanov 1991
;
Gates and Nishino 1992
;
Nishino and Gates 1993
. We thus conclude that the action given by the generalized FI term, which in other theories gives the action for Yang-Mills theory, in the
{\mathcal{N}}=3
theory surprisingly gives the action for Chern-Simons theory.

IV.3
{\cal N}=4
in three dimensions

This topic has appeared in the literature before. The fact that there are two inequivalent vector multiplets was observed in
Brooks:1994nn
and was later treated in an off-shell fashion in Harmonic superspace in
Zupnik 1999
. It was subsequently discussed in Projective superspace in
Kuzenko:2011xg
;
Kuzenko:2014mva
in an
AdS_{3}
background.

IV.3.1
The algebra

The
{\mathcal{N}}=4
algebra has an
SO(4)\equiv SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
R-symmetry. The supercovariant derivatives transforms in the fundamental representation of each of the R-symmetry groups. The most general algebra involving also a Yang-Mills field is

\displaystyle\{{\mathcal{D}}^{ia}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{D}}^{kb}_{\beta}\}=\epsilon^{ik}\epsilon^{ab}i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}+iC_{\alpha\beta}\left(\epsilon^{ik}W_{R}^{ab}+\epsilon^{ab}W_{L}^{ik}\right)~,

(138)

where
W_{L}^{ik}
and
W_{R}^{ab}
are symmetric
SU(2)_{L}
and
SU(2)_{R}
triplets. The Bianchi identities are

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}^{i(a}W_{R}^{bc)}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}^{(i|a|}W_{L}^{kl)}

\displaystyle=0~.

(139)

The
{\mathcal{N}}=4
theory is the dimensional reduction of the
{\mathcal{N}}=2
theory in four dimensions. With the identification

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}={\mathcal{D}}^{11}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}=i{\mathcal{D}}^{21}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}=i{\mathcal{D}}^{12}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}={\mathcal{D}}^{22}_{\alpha}~,

(140)

and

\displaystyle{W}_{L}^{\dagger}=W^{11}_{L}\;\;\;W_{L}=W^{22}_{L}\;\;\;W_{L}^{0}=iW^{12}_{L}

\displaystyle{W}_{R}^{\dagger}=W^{11}_{R}\;\;\;W_{R}=W^{22}_{R}\;\;\;W_{R}^{0}=iW^{12}_{R}~,

(141)

the algebra becomes

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\beta}\right\}=i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+C_{\alpha\beta}(W_{R}^{0}+W_{L}^{0})

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}\right\}=i\nabla_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+C_{\alpha\beta}(W_{R}^{0}-W_{L}^{0})

(142)

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}\right\}=-C_{\alpha\beta}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}\;;

\displaystyle\;\;\left\{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}\right\}=C_{\alpha\beta}W_{R}

\displaystyle\left\{{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}\right\}=-C_{\alpha\beta}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}\;;

\displaystyle\;\;\left\{\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathcal{Q}}_{\beta}\right\}=C_{\alpha\beta}W_{L}~,

and the Bianchi identities for the
{\mathbb{W}}_{R}
multiplet become

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}^{\dagger}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}^{\dagger}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}=\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}-2\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{R}

\displaystyle=0

(143)

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}+2{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{R}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}-2{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{R}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}_{R}+2\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{R}

\displaystyle=0~,

and for the
{\mathbb{W}}_{L}
multiplet we get

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}=\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}_{L}={\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}_{L}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}_{L}-2{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{L}

\displaystyle=0

(144)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}+2{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{L}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}-2\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{L}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}{W}_{L}+2\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}W^{0}_{L}

\displaystyle=0~.

The fact that the
R
-symmetry group now consists of two
SU(2)
groups
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}
leads us to introduce two auxilliary
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
manifolds
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{L}\times\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{R}
with coordintes
\zeta_{L}
and
\zeta_{R}
.
It is then natural to define the projective supercovariant derivatives

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}+\zeta_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha})+\zeta_{R}(\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}-\zeta_{L}\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=({\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}+\zeta_{L}{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha})+\zeta_{R}(-\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}+\zeta_{L}\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha})

(145)

\displaystyle\Delta_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}({\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}-\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=(\overline{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha})+\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}(-{\mathcal{Q}}_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{\mathcal{D}}_{\alpha})~.

Even when taken at the same point at both
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{L}
and
\mathbb{CP}^{1}_{R}
the gauged supercovariant derivatives do not anticommute. Instead the anticommutation relations are

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=2C_{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{L}\zeta_{R}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}+2W^{0}_{L}-\zeta_{L}W_{L}\right)=2C_{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{L}\zeta_{R}{\mathbb{W}}_{L}(\zeta_{L})

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=2C_{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}+2W^{0}_{R}-\zeta_{R}W_{R}\right)=2C_{\alpha\beta}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{R})

(146)

\displaystyle\{\Delta_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=2C_{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}-2W^{0}_{R}-\zeta_{R}W_{R}\right)

\displaystyle\{\Delta_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\beta}\}

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{\alpha\beta}}{\zeta_{L}\zeta_{R}}\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}-2W^{0}_{L}-\zeta_{L}W_{L}\right)~,

where we have defined the
{\mathcal{O}}(2)
field strengths

\displaystyle{\mathbb{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{R})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}{W}^{\dagger}_{R}+2W^{0}_{R}-\zeta_{R}W_{R}

(147)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{W}}_{L}(\zeta_{L})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{W}^{\dagger}_{L}+2W^{0}_{L}-\zeta_{L}W_{L}~.

Furthermore, using these definitions, the Bianchi identities can be compactly written as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}_{L}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}_{L}=0

(148)

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}

\displaystyle=\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\alpha}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}=0~.

Notice that the projective derivatives
\nabla_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}
form an anticommuting set only if
{\mathbb{W}}_{L}=0
and that
\nabla_{\alpha},\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\alpha}
anticommute only if
{\mathbb{W}}_{R}=0
. Thus, if and only if
{\mathbb{W}}_{L}=0
we may consistently define left projective superfields
T_{L}
as fields satisfying the constraint

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}T_{L}=\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}T_{L}=0~,

(149)

and similarly, if and only if
{\mathbb{W}}_{R}=0
we may consistently define right projective superfields
T_{R}
as fields satisfying

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}T_{R}=\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleright$}_{\alpha}T_{R}=0~.

(150)

This theory was studied before in Projective superspace
Arai and Sasaki 2013
;
Arai and Sasaki 2014
but in
\mathcal{N}=4
unitary gauge.

IV.3.2
The field strength

If we assume the
{\mathbb{W}}_{L}=0
so that we may consistently define left projective matter superfields
\Upsilon_{L}(\zeta_{L})
, independent of
\zeta_{R}
and with polar dependence on
\zeta_{L}

\displaystyle\nabla_{\alpha}\Upsilon_{L}=\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}\Upsilon_{L}=0~.

(151)

Such fields transform under gauge transformations as

\displaystyle\Upsilon_{L}\rightarrow e^{i\Lambda_{L}}\Upsilon_{L}~,

(152)

where also
\Lambda_{L}
is left projective. In this case the gauge field
e^{V_{L}}
is introduced as a tropical left projective superfield transforming under gauge transformations as

\displaystyle e^{V_{L}}\rightarrow e^{i\overline{\Lambda}_{L}}e^{V_{L}}e^{-i\Lambda_{L}}~.

(153)

We can repeat what we did in the previous sections by observing that if we take the anticommutator of two
\nabla
derivatives at different
\zeta_{L}
positions we have

\displaystyle\{\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta_{1L},\zeta_{R}),\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta_{2L},\zeta_{R})\}=(\zeta_{1L}-\zeta_{2L})C_{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{R}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{R})~,

(154)

which, introducing the derivative operator
\partial_{L}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial\zeta_{L}}
, can also be written

\displaystyle\{[\partial_{L},\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta_{L},\zeta_{R})],\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta_{L},\zeta_{R})\}=C_{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{R}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{R})~.

(155)

Splitting the gauge field into polar parts

\displaystyle e^{V_{L}}=e^{\overline{U}_{L}}e^{U_{L}}~,

(156)

we define the field strength in arctic representation

\displaystyle\{[e^{-U_{L}}\partial_{L}e^{U_{L}},\nabla_{\alpha}(\zeta_{L},\zeta_{R})],\nabla_{\beta}(\zeta_{L},\zeta_{R})\}=C_{\alpha\beta}e^{-U_{L}}\zeta_{R}{\mathbb{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{R})e^{U_{L}}=C_{\alpha\beta}\zeta_{R}\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{L},\zeta_{R})~.

(157)

We see that in the arctic representation, the field strength is an
{\mathcal{O}}(2)
multiplet in
\zeta_{R}
but has an arctic dependence on
\zeta_{L}
.
By defining the
\zeta_{L}
covariant derivative

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{L}=e^{-U_{L}}\partial_{L}e^{U_{L}}=\partial_{L}+{\mathcal{A}}_{L}~,

(158)

we can write the field strength in the arctic representation as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{R}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}\nabla^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{L}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}{\mathcal{W}}_{R}^{\dagger}+2{\mathcal{W}}_{R}^{0}-\zeta_{R}{\mathcal{W}}_{R}=

\displaystyle-\left(\frac{1}{\zeta_{R}}(D+\zeta_{L}Q)^{2}+(D+\zeta_{L}Q)^{\alpha}(\overline{Q}-\zeta_{L}\overline{D})_{\alpha}+\zeta_{R}(\overline{Q}-\zeta_{L}\overline{D})^{2}\right){\mathcal{A}}_{L}~,

(159)

giving the component field strengths

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}_{R}

\displaystyle=-(D+\zeta_{L}Q)^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{L}

\displaystyle 2{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{R}

\displaystyle=-(D+\zeta_{L}Q)^{\alpha}(\overline{Q}-\zeta_{L}\overline{D})_{\alpha}{\mathcal{A}}_{L}

(160)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{R}

\displaystyle=(\overline{Q}-\zeta_{L}\overline{D})^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{L}~.

The new techniques introduced in this paper allow us to find the components of these expressions. We find that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}_{R}(\zeta_{L})

\displaystyle=e^{-\widecheck{U}_{L}}(D^{2}\overline{A})e^{\widecheck{U}_{L}}

\displaystyle 2{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{R}(\zeta_{L})

\displaystyle=-e^{-\widehat{U}_{L}}\overline{D}^{\alpha}(D_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}})e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P})e^{\widehat{U}_{L}}=-e^{-\widecheck{U}_{L}}D^{\alpha}(e^{-\overline{P}}e^{-P}\overline{D}_{\alpha}(e^{P}e^{\overline{P}}))e^{\widecheck{U}_{L}}

(161)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{R}(\zeta_{L})

\displaystyle=e^{-\widehat{U}_{L}}(\overline{D}^{2}A)e^{\widehat{U}_{L}}~,

where the fields depend on
\zeta_{L}
. Notice that the expressions for
{\mathcal{W}}_{R},{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}_{R}
are exactly the same as for the field strength in four dimensions.

An entirely analogous argument starting with
{\mathbb{W}}_{R}=0
and right projective matter superfields gives us the expression for the field strength in the right projective sector as

\displaystyle\bm{\mathcal{W}}_{L}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}\nabla^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{R}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{L}}{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}_{L}+2{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{L}-\zeta_{L}{\mathcal{W}}_{L}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{R}

\displaystyle=e^{-U_{R}}(\partial_{R}e^{U_{R}})~,

(162)

leading to the expressions for the components of the field strengths

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}^{\dagger}_{L}

\displaystyle=-(D+\zeta_{R}\overline{Q})^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{R}

\displaystyle 2{\mathcal{W}}^{0}_{L}

\displaystyle=-(D+\zeta_{R}\overline{Q})^{\alpha}(Q-\zeta_{R}\overline{D})_{\alpha}{\mathcal{A}}_{R}

(163)

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}}_{L}

\displaystyle=(Q-\zeta_{R}\overline{D})^{2}{\mathcal{A}}_{R}~.

The expressions for the field strengths when expressed using only the
D
and
\overline{D}
operators are identical to (
IV.3.2
) but with all the expressions computed from
e^{U_{R}}
instead of
e^{U_{L}}
.

IV.3.3
The action

Using the projective field strength (
147
) we can write a gauge invariant action using the projective measure

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\oint\frac{d\zeta_{R}}{\zeta_{R}}{\rm Tr}({\mathbb{W}}_{R}^{2})=\int d^{3}xD^{2}\overline{D}^{2}{\rm Tr}(4(W_{R}^{0})^{2}-2W_{R}{W}^{\dagger}_{R})~.

(164)

If we let the
\overline{D}^{2}
derivatives act and use the Bianchi identities (
IV.3.1
) the action can be rewritten in chiral form as

\displaystyle S=\int d^{3}xD^{2}Q^{2}{\rm Tr}(W_{R}^{2})~,

(165)

where
W_{R}
is the component of
{\mathbb{W}}_{R}
that multiplies
\zeta
.
To show that the generalized Fayet-Iliopoulos term is equivalent to this action we follow exactly the same steps as in the four dimensional case. We write the full superspace measure as
D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}\overline{Q}^{2}=D^{2}Q^{2}\overline{D}^{2}(\overline{Q}-\zeta\overline{D})^{2}
and use that since a left-projective superfield is annihilated by both
\nabla_{\alpha}
and
\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha}
it is also annihilated by
\frac{1}{2\zeta_{R}}(\nabla_{\alpha}-\hbox{\,\Large$\triangleleft$}_{\alpha})=(\overline{Q}-\zeta\overline{D})_{\alpha}
. The formulas are precisely the formulas from the four dimensional example (
93
) with the same result (
96
) showing that the generalized Fayet-Iliopoulos term again gives the action of Yang-Mills theory.
An analogous story holds in the right-projective case.

It is possible to reduce expressions in
{\mathcal{N}}=4
superspace to
{\mathcal{N}}=3
superspace by rewriting the complex supercovariant derivative
Q_{\alpha}
in terms of real and imaginary part as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha}=D^{0}_{\alpha}+iQ^{0}_{\alpha}~,

(166)

and then use that on left projective superfields, after setting
\zeta_{L}=\zeta_{R}=\zeta

\displaystyle Q^{0}_{\alpha}=\frac{1}{\zeta}D_{\alpha}+\zeta\overline{D}_{\alpha}~.

(167)

Starting from the Yang-Mills action, if we expand the
\zeta
factors according to the rules developed in this paper, we end up with the
{\mathcal{N}}=3
action for Yang-Mills theory (
121
).

V
Conclusions

We introduced a new method for manipulating gauge fields in Projective superspace. The main idea was to expand all expressions in powers of
X=e^{V}-1
(and thus indirectly in powers of the unconstrained gauge prepotential
V
) and then to use the epsilon prescription when projecting fields to positive or negative powers of
\zeta
leading to an asymmetric splitting which, however, is much more convenient to calculate with. This new approach led to elegant formulas for gauge potentials and field strengths. In particular, it becomes straightforward to rewrite the expressions in components, i.e. in a superspace with less manifest supersymmetry (
{\mathcal{N}}=1
in four dimensions,
{\mathcal{N}}=2
in three dimensions) which also made it possible to analyze various superspace Lagrangians in detail. We illustrated our results in several explicit examples.

Using the techniques introduced in this paper we get formulations of a wide range of theories in terms of the unconstrained gauge prepotential
V
. This makes it possible to calculate perturbative quantum corrections to these theories keeping the supersymmetry manifest.

We have not investigated the dimensional reduction of our theories to two dimensions. In two dimensions there are even more possibilities for Projective techniques since a theory with
(p,q)
supersymmetry has a
SO(p)\times SO(q)

R-symmetry group. For a theory with
(4,4)
supersymmetry that means that four auxilliary
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
manifolds could be introduced
Ivanov 1991
. It would be interesting to see how the different
{\mathcal{N}}=3
and
{\mathcal{N}}=4
theories fit into this picture.

Another area where the methods from this paper should be useful is in studying mirror symmetry of three dimensional
{\mathcal{N}}=4
supersymmetric theories
Intriligator and Seiberg 1996
. The mirror symmetry exchanges a left projective theory with a (twisted) right projective theory through a duality procedure that can be explicitly performed in the path integral. The coupling between the original theory and the twisted theory is through a BF type term which we know how to write in our formalism. Since both the Coulomb and the Higgs branches of the mirror symmetric theories are Hyperkähler manifolds, this might lead to new constructions of and relations between Hyperkähler manifolds.

Similarly our methods are well suited to investigate ABJM theory
Aharony et al. 2008
in superspace since there one describes the
{\mathcal{N}}=6
supersymmetric theory in terms of two
{\mathcal{N}}=3
Chern-Simons theories together with a pair of bifundamental hypermultiplets. This was first done in
{\mathcal{N}}=3
superspace in
Buchbinder et al. 2009
. The possibility to perform perturbative calculations with manifest
{\mathcal{N}}=3
supersymmetry and to extensively analyze the result should lead to new insights.

Another situation where our newly developed procedures may come in handy is for Yang-Mills theories, or in particular Chern-Simons theory, in five dimensions
Zupnik 1999
;
Kuzenko and Linch 2006
;
Kuzenko 2007
. In
Kuzenko 2007
an action for five dimensional Chern-Simons was given in the abelian case whereas for the non-abelian only the variation of the action was given. We believe that using the techniques introduced in this paper and suitably adapted to five dimensions will allow us to integrate this variation and find a covariant expression for the full nonabelian Chern-Simons action.

Appendix A
The epsilon prescription

A.1
Definition

In the paper we employ a regularization method of the poles appearing in some of the contour integrations. It was originally introduced in
Jain and Siegel 2009
;
Jain and Siegel 2011
and called the
\epsilon
-prescription. Following
Jain and Siegel 2009
;
Jain and Siegel 2011
we redefine

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\zeta_{12}}~,

(168)

where

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{10}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{1}}\right)^{n}\left(=\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{0}}\;\;\;{\rm if}\;\;\;\left|\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{1}}\right|<1\right)

(169)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{01}}

\displaystyle\equiv\frac{1}{\zeta_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{0}}\right)^{n}\left(=\frac{1}{\zeta_{0}-\zeta_{1}}\;\;\;{\rm if}\;\;\;\left|\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{0}}\right|<1\right)~.

Alternatively, we may define the factors using a small parameter
\epsilon
(hence the name) that we will let go to zero at the end of all calculations

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{12}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}+\epsilon(\zeta_{1}+\zeta_{2})}~.

(170)

In this expression
\zeta_{1}
and
\zeta_{2}
should be thought of as having the same magnitude
|\zeta_{1}|=|\zeta_{2}|
so that the
\zeta_{1}
contour would actually meet
\zeta_{2}
. By introducing the
\epsilon
, the pole is deformed away from the integration contour and the integral can be performed. The
\epsilon
-regulated denominators give rize to projection operators, more precisely, given a superfield
T(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}t_{n}\zeta^{n}
the contour integrals are

\displaystyle\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{T(\zeta_{1})}{\zeta_{10}}

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\zeta_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{0}}{\zeta_{1}}\right)^{n}T(\zeta_{1})=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}t_{k}\zeta_{0}^{k}

\displaystyle\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{T(\zeta_{1})}{\zeta_{01}}

\displaystyle=\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{1}{\zeta_{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{0}}\right)^{n}T(\zeta_{1})=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{-1}t_{k}\zeta_{0}^{k}~,

(171)

Notice that the projection defined in this way does not treat the
\zeta
-independent terms in a symmetrical way. They are always projected together with the positive powers of
\zeta
and thus
\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{T(\zeta_{1})}{\zeta_{10}}
and
\oint\frac{d\zeta_{1}}{2\pi i}\frac{T(\zeta_{1})}{\zeta_{01}}
are not conjugates of each other. To avoid cluttering we will suppress any factors of
2\pi i
in the measure,
\frac{d\zeta}{2\pi i}\rightarrow d\zeta
, in the rest of the paper.

A.2
Cancellation

In several places we cancel factors of
(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})
against
\epsilon
-regulated denominators
\frac{1}{\zeta_{12}}
. To prove that this is possible we explicitly calculate the integral

\displaystyle I=\oint d\zeta_{1}\frac{\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{12}}T(\zeta_{1})~,

(172)

for an arbitrary field
T(\zeta)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}t_{n}\zeta^{n}
. We start by splitting the integral into two integrals

\displaystyle I=\oint d\zeta_{1}\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{12}}T(\zeta_{1})-\oint d\zeta_{1}\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{12}}T(\zeta_{1})=\sum_{n=-1}^{\infty}t_{n}\zeta_{2}^{n+1}-\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}t_{n}\zeta_{2}^{n+1}=t_{-1}~,

(173)

which is precisely the result for the integral
I
one would get by canceling the
(\zeta_{1}-\zeta_{2})
against the
\frac{1}{\zeta_{12}}
before performing the contour integral. An analogous argument can be made for the factor
\frac{1}{\zeta_{21}}
in the denominator.

A.3
Conjugation

The basic operation of conjugation is always accompanied by a transformation on the auxiliary
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
manifold. A general field
T(\zeta)=\sum_{n}t_{n}\zeta^{n}
transforms as

\displaystyle\overline{T}(\zeta)=\sum t_{n}^{\dagger}\left(-\frac{1}{\zeta}\right)^{n}~.

(174)

An epsilon-regulated
\zeta
denominator transforms as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\zeta_{21}}=\frac{1}{\zeta_{2}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{1}}{\zeta_{2}}\right)^{n}\rightarrow-\frac{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{1}}\right)^{n}=-\frac{\zeta_{1}\zeta_{2}}{\zeta_{12}}~,

(175)

and the contour integral measure transforms as

\displaystyle\oint d\zeta\rightarrow-\oint\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta^{2}}~.

(176)

Notice the additional minus sign from a change in orientation of the contour.

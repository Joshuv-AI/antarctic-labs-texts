Degree Differences in the Eta Correspondences
Shu-Yen Pan

Abstract
. A sub-relation of the Θ \Theta -correspondence called the η \eta -correspondence is defined by Gurevich-Howe for a finite reductive dual pair in stable range. In this paper we propose an extension of the correspondence to general finite reductive dual pairs. Then we determine the domain the correspondence and prove a formula on the difference of degrees in q q of two irreducible characters paired by the correspondence.

Primary: 20C33; Secondary: 22E50

1.
Introduction

1.1.

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
be a reductive dual pair over a finite field
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
of
q
elements
where
q
is a power of an odd prime.
By restricting the character of the Weil representation to the dual pair,
we obtain a decomposition

\omega_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G),\ \rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})}m_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\rho\otimes\rho^{\prime}

where
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
denotes the set of irreducible character of the finite group
G

of rational points of
G
.
The subset

\Theta_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}=\{\,(\rho,\rho^{\prime})\mid m_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\neq 0\,\}\subset{\mathcal{E}}(G)\times{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})

gives a relation between
{\mathcal{E}}(G)
and
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
, and is called the
\Theta
-correspondence

for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
.
We say that
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})

occurs
in the
\Theta
-correspondence for
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})

if the set

\Theta(\rho^{\prime})=\{\,\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)\mid m_{\rho,\rho^{\prime}}\neq 0\,\}

is not empty.

For a non-negative integer
k
, let
\text{\bf G}_{k}
denote one of the following types of classical groups:
a general linear group
{\rm GL}_{k}
, a unitary group
{\rm U}_{k}
, a symplectic group
{\rm Sp}_{k}
(only when
k
is even),
or an orthogonal group
{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k}
where
\epsilon=+
or
-
.
For
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})
, the
\Theta
-rank
of
\rho^{\prime}
,
denoted by
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})
,
is defined to be the smallest number
k
such that
\rho^{\prime}\chi^{\prime}
occurs in the
\Theta
-correspondence
for some dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
and for some linear character
\chi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}

(
cf
. (
3.11
), (
3.12
) and (
4.9
)).

For a dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
is stable range,
Gurevich and Howe prove in
[
GH17
]
and
[
GH20
]
that for
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})

there is a unique
\rho^{\prime}\in\Theta(\rho)
such that
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=k
.
The character
\rho^{\prime}
is denoted by
\eta(\rho)
,
and the mapping
\rho\mapsto\eta(\rho)
from
{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime}_{n})
is injective
and is called the
\eta
-correspondence
for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
in stable range.

In
[
Pan20a
]
and
[
Pan20b
]
,
a one-to-one sub-correspondence
\underline{\theta}
of the
\Theta
-correspondence between

{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
and
{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime}_{n})
for a general dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
is defined (
cf
. (
3.6
)),
and it is shown that
\eta=\underline{\theta}
if dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
is in stable range.
Therefore, for general dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
(i.e., not necessarily in stable range),
it might be reasonable to define
\eta(\rho)=\underline{\theta}(\rho)
on those
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})

such that
\underline{\theta}(\rho)
is defined and
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=k
.

For a non-negative integer
\ell
,
a condition on irreducible characters
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
called

\ell
-admissible for a dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
is defined in
Subsections
3.2
,
4.2
and
5.1
.
Then we have our first main result which gives complete description of the domain of the
“
\eta
-correspondence” for a general dual pair:

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
0\leq k\leq n
.

(i)

If
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
is
(n-k)
-admissible for the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
,
then
\underline{\theta}(\rho)
is defined and
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))=k
.

(ii)

If
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime}_{n})
is of
\Theta
-rank
k
,
then
\rho^{\prime}=\underline{\theta}(\rho)\chi^{\prime}
for

•

some linear character
\chi^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}_{n}
; and

•

some group
\text{\bf G}_{k}
and an irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
which is
(n-k)
-admissible for
the dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
.

Remark 1.2
.

(1)

A condition on an irreducible character
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm GL}_{n}(q))
of
\Theta
-rank
k

is given in
[
GH20
]
theorem 9.2.3.

(2)

A similar condition on an irreducible character
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{n}(q))
of
\Theta
-rank
k

can be found in
[
GLT20
]
theorem 3.9.

1.2.

For
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
,
it is known that
\rho(1)
is a polynomial in
q
.
Let
\deg_{q}(\rho)
denote the degree of this polynomial.
It turns out that we have a very elegant formula on the difference between

\deg_{q}(\rho)
and
\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))
when
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))=k
:

Theorem 1.3
.

Consider a dual pair
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
If
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
is
(n-k)
-admissible for
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})
,
then

\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))=\deg_{q}(\rho)+\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}k(n-k+1),&\text{if\/ $(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})=({\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})$};\\
\frac{1}{2}k(n-k-1),&\text{if\/ $(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})=({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{n})$};\\
k(n-k),&\text{if\/ $(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})$}.\end{cases}

Remark 1.4
.

If
(\text{\bf G}_{k},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}_{n})=({\rm O}_{k}^{\epsilon},{\rm Sp}_{n})
and the dual pair is in the stable range,
then the
\eta
-correspondence and the
\underline{\theta}
-correspondence coincide,
and every
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{k})
is
(n-k)
-admissible (
cf
. Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
5.12
).
For this case, the above formula is already known in
[
GH17
]
, (1.11) and theorem 4.1.1.

An application of the above theorem is to determine the maximum and minimum values of the set

\{\,\deg_{q}(\rho)\mid\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G_{n}),\ \Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho)=k\,\}

where
\text{\bf G}_{n}={\rm Sp}_{n}
,
{\rm O}_{n}^{\epsilon}
or
{\rm U}_{n}
, and
k\leq n
.
This problem will be investigated in another papers of the author.
A lower bound and an upper bound of the above set can be found in
[
GLT19
]
and
[
GLT20
]
.

\deg_{q}(\rho)

for any unipotent characters in terms of Lusztig symbols.
In Section 3, we investigate the

-correspondence and define
\ell
-admissibility

\Theta

in the final section.

2.
Degrees in
q
of Unipotent Characters

2.1.
Basic notations

Let
G
be a classical group defined over
{\mathbf{F}}_{q}
,
and let
G=\text{\bf G}(q)
denote the finite group of rational points.

For a polynomial
f
in
q
, let
\deg_{q}(f)
denote the degree of the polynomial
f
.
Let
|G|_{p^{\prime}}
denote the part of the order
|G|
prime to
p
.
Now
|G|_{p^{\prime}}
is a polynomial in
q
and it is well-known that

\deg_{q}(|G|_{p^{\prime}})=\begin{cases}\tfrac{1}{4}k(k+2),&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{k}$ or ${\rm O}_{k+1}$, $k$ even};\\
\tfrac{1}{4}k^{2},&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k}$, $k$ even};\\
\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1),&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{k}$}.\end{cases}

2.2.
Symbols and unipotent characters

Let
\mu=[\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{k}]
and
\mu=[\nu_{1},\ldots,\nu_{l}]
be two partitions.
We may assume that
k=l
by adding some
0
’s if necessary.
Then we denote

(2.1)

\mu\preccurlyeq\nu\quad\text{ if \ }\nu_{1}\geq\mu_{1}\geq\nu_{2}\geq\mu_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\nu_{k}\geq\mu_{k}.

An ordered pair
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
of two partitions is called a
bi-partition
of
n

if

\left|\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}\right|:=|\mu|+|\nu|=n.

The set of bi-partitions of
n
is denoted by
{\mathcal{P}}_{2}(n)
.
For a bi-partition
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
, we define its
transpose
by

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}^{\rm t}=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu}{\mu}
.

A
\beta
-set is a finite subset
A=\{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m}\}
of non-negative integers
written strictly decreasingly, i.e.,
a_{1}>a_{2}>\cdots>a_{m}
.
A symbol
\Lambda=\binom{A}{B}
is an ordered pair of two
\beta
-sets.
Let
{\mathcal{S}}
denote the set of symbols
\binom{A}{B}
such that
0\not\in A\cap B
.
For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,
we define its
defect
and
rank
by

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\rm def}(\Lambda)&=m_{1}-m_{2},\\
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)&=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}b_{j}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor.\end{split}

We also define the
transpose
of a symbol by
\binom{A}{B}^{\rm t}=\binom{B}{A}
.
There is a mapping
\Upsilon
from the set of symbols to the set of bi-partitions:

(2.3)

\Upsilon\colon\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\mapsto\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{a_1-(m_1-1),a_2-(m_1-2),\ldots,a_{m_1-1}-1,a_{m_1}}{b_1-(m_2-1),b_2-(m_2-2),\ldots,b_{m_2-1}-1,b_{m_2}}.

It is easy to check that

(2.4)

|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\begin{cases}{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\tfrac{1}{4}({\rm def}(\Lambda)-1)({\rm def}(\Lambda)+1),&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is odd};\\
{\rm rk}(\Lambda)-\tfrac{1}{4}({\rm def}(\Lambda))^{2},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is even}.\end{cases}

For a non-negative even integer
k
, we define a set of symbols associated to a symplectic group
or an even orthogonal group:

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{+}_{k}}&=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=\tfrac{k}{2},\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{4}\,\};\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k}}&=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=\tfrac{k}{2},\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}\,\};\\
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{-}_{k}}&=\{\,\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}\mid{\rm rk}(\Lambda)=\tfrac{k}{2},\ {\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 2\pmod{4}\,\}.\end{split}

From
[
Lus77
]
it is known that there exists a bijection from
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
to the set
{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}

of unipotent characters of
G
denoted by
\Lambda\mapsto\rho_{\Lambda}
.

Remark 2.6
.

The set
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k}}
of symbols considered here is slightly different from the original one
in
[
Lus77
]
.
Please see
[
Pan21b
]
subsection 3.3 to see the equivalence of the two parametrizations.

Suppose all the entries
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}},b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}
in a symbol

\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
are

z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}
with
z_{1}\geq z_{2}\geq\cdots\geq z_{m}

where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
.
The following lemma is
[
Pan20a
]
lemma 2.12:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
where

\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{k}
or
{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k}
and
k
is even.
Then
\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})
is equal to

\sum_{i=1}^{m}(m-i)z_{i}-z_{i}(z_{i}+1)+\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}k(k+2)-\frac{1}{24}(m-1)(m-3)(2m-1),&\text{if $m$ is odd};\\
\frac{1}{4}k^{2}-\frac{1}{24}m(m-2)(2m-5),&\text{if $m$ is even}.\end{cases}

2.3.
Unipotent characters of a unitary group

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}
,
we define

(2.8)

{\rm rk}_{\rm U}(\Lambda)=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}+\frac{|m_{1}-m_{2}|}{2}-\frac{(m_{1}+m_{2})(m_{1}+m_{2}-2)}{2}.

For a non-negative integer
k
,
we let
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
be the set of symbols
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}
such that

•

{\rm def}(\Lambda)
is either even and non-negative, or odd and negative;

•

{\rm rk}_{\rm U}(\Lambda)=k
.

Again, there is a parametrization from
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
to
{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))_{1}

denoted by
\Lambda\mapsto\rho_{\Lambda}
(
cf
.
[
FS90
]
or
[
Pan20b
]
).

For a symbol
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,
we define two
\beta
-sets:

(2.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X^{0}=X^{0}_{\Lambda}&=\begin{cases}\{2b_{1},2b_{2},\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}\},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is even};\\
\{2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}\},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is odd},\end{cases}\\
X^{1}=X^{1}_{\Lambda}&=\begin{cases}\{2a_{1}+1,2a_{2}+1,\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}+1\},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is even};\\
\{2b_{1}+1,2b_{2}+1,\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}+1\},&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)$ is odd}.\end{cases}\end{split}

For a
\beta
-set
A
or two
\beta
-sets
A,B
, we define the following polynomials in
q
:

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Delta(A)&=\prod_{a,a^{\prime}\in A,\ a>a^{\prime}}(q^{a}-q^{a^{\prime}}),\\
\Theta(A)&=\prod_{a\in A}\prod_{h=1}^{a}(q^{h}-(-1)^{h}),\\
\Xi(A,B)&=\prod_{a\in A,\ b\in B}(q^{a}+q^{b}).\end{split}

For
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
,
it is well known that (
cf
. for example,
[
Pan20b
]
proposition 2.15):

(2.11)

\rho_{\Lambda}(1)=\frac{\Delta(X^{0})\Delta(X^{1})\Xi(X^{0},X^{1})|{\rm U}_{k}(q)|_{p^{\prime}}}{\Theta(X^{0})\Theta(X^{1})q^{\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-1}{2}+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-2}{2}+\cdots+\binom{2}{2}}}

where
\binom{a}{b}
denotes the binomial coefficients.

Lemma 2.12
.

For
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
,
write
X_{\Lambda}=X^{0}_{\Lambda}\cup X^{1}_{\Lambda}=\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}\}
such that

z_{1}>z_{2}>\cdots>z_{m}
where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
.
Then

\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(m-i)z_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{z_{i}(z_{i}+1)}{2}+\frac{k(k+1)}{2}-\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{6}.

Proof.

Because now
z_{1}>z_{2}>\cdots>z_{m}
,
from (
2.10
) it is not difficult to see that

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\Delta(X^{0})\Delta(X^{1})\Pi(X^{0},X^{1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{(z_{i},z_{j})\in X\times X,\ i\neq j}\max(z_{i},z_{j})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(m-i)z_{i},

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\Theta(X^{0})\Theta(X^{1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{z_{i}(z_{i}+1)}{2}.

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(|{\rm U}_{k}(q)|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\frac{k(k+1)}{2},

\displaystyle\binom{m-1}{2}+\binom{m-2}{2}+\cdots+\binom{2}{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{m(m-1)(m-2)}{6}.

Then the lemma follows from (
2.11
) directly.
∎

3.
Unipotent Characters of Symplectic or Orthogonal Groups

3.1.
Finite theta correspondence of unipotent characters

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
be a reductive dual pair of a symplectic group and an even orthogonal group.
For symbols
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
,
we write
\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
and
\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu'}{\nu'}
.
Now we define a relation on the set of symbols:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{+}_{n}}&=\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k}}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{+}_{n}}\mid\nu\preccurlyeq\mu^{\prime},\ \nu^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\mu,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda^{\prime})=-{\rm def}(\Lambda)+1\,\};\\
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{-}_{n}}&=\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm Sp}_{k}}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm O}^{-}_{n}}\mid\mu\preccurlyeq\nu^{\prime},\ \mu^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\nu,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda^{\prime})=-{\rm def}(\Lambda)-1\,\}\end{split}

where both
k,n
are even.
It is known that the unipotent characters are preserved by the correspondence

\Theta_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
(
cf
.
[
AM93
]
theorem 3.5).
The following result on the
\Theta
-correspondence of unipotent characters
is from
[
Pan19a
]
theorem 1.8:

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})
be a reductive dual pair of a symplectic group and an even orthogonal group.
Then
(\rho_{\Lambda},\rho_{\Lambda})\in\Theta_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
if and only if

(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
.

3.2.
\ell
-admissible symbols

Consider a dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{n})
or
({\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})

where both
n,k
are even and
k\leq n
.
Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
, and write

(3.3)

\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},\qquad\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}.

Then from (
2.5
) we can write

(3.4)

{\rm def}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}4d,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{+}_{k}$};\\
4d+1,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm Sp}_{k}$};\\
4d+2,&\text{if $\text{\bf G}={\rm O}^{-}_{k}$}\end{cases}

for some
d\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
and we define an integer

(3.5)

\tau=\begin{cases}\tfrac{n-k}{2}+2d,&\text{if $(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm O}^{+}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})$ or $({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{+}_{n})$};\\
\tfrac{n-k}{2}-(2d+1),&\text{if $(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm O}^{-}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})$ or $({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{-}_{n})$}.\end{cases}

Note that the number
\tau
depends on
k,n
and
{\rm def}(\Lambda)
.
If
\tau\geq 0
,
as in
[
Pan20a
]
,

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)
is defined to be the symbol in
{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}

such that

(3.6)

\Upsilon(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=\begin{cases}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}\cup\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\tau}{-},&\text{if $\epsilon=+$};\\
\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}\cup\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{-}{\tau},&\text{if $\epsilon=-$}.\end{cases}

Moreover, if
\tau\geq\nu_{1}
when
\epsilon=+
; or
\tau\geq\mu_{1}
when
\epsilon=-
, we have

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\begin{cases}\binom{\tau+m_{2},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}},&\text{if $\epsilon=+$};\\
\binom{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{\tau+m_{1},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}},&\text{if $\epsilon=-$}.\end{cases}

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
with
\Upsilon(\Lambda)
given as in (
3.3
)
and
{\rm def}(\Lambda)
given as in (
3.4
),
and let
\ell
be a non-negative even integer.
Then
\Lambda
is called
\ell
-admissible

Lemma 3.9
.

Consider the dual pair

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{k}{2}-2d(2d+1)=\frac{k}{2}-2d-4d^{2}\leq\frac{k}{2}-2d,

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{k}{2}-2d(2d+1)\leq\frac{k}{2}+2d+1.

is also
(n-k)
-admissible.

.
Let
\{z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,z^{\prime}_{m+1}\}
denote the set of entries of
\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)

where
z^{\prime}_{1}\geq z^{\prime}_{2}\geq\cdots\geq z^{\prime}_{m+1}
.
From (
2.2
), we know that

(3.17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{k}{2}={\rm rk}(\Lambda)&=\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor,\\
\frac{n}{2}={\rm rk}(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))&=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}z^{\prime}_{i}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor.\end{split}

Now we consider the following cases:

(1)

Suppose that
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm O}^{+}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})
.
Now by (
3.1
) and (
3.6
) we can write

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\binom{b^{\prime}_{1},b^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,b^{\prime}_{m_{2}+1}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}
for some
b^{\prime}_{i}
.
The assumption that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible
implies that
b^{\prime}_{1}=\tau+m_{2}
,
b^{\prime}_{j+1}=b_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,m_{2}
, and
b^{\prime}_{1}\geq a_{1}
.
Hence
z_{1}^{\prime}=b_{1}^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime}_{i+1}=z_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Now
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 0\pmod{4}
, so
m
is even.
From (
3.17
), we obtain

z^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{n}{2}+\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{k}{2}-\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m-1}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor=\frac{1}{2}(n-k+m).

Now
\deg_{q}(|{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{k}(q)|_{p^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{4}k^{2}
and

\deg_{q}(|{\rm Sp}_{n}(q)|_{p^{\prime}})=\frac{1}{4}n(n+2)
.
Moreover,

(m+1-(i+1))z^{\prime}_{i+1}-z^{\prime}_{i+1}(z^{\prime}_{i+1}+1)=(m-i)z_{i}-z_{i}(z_{i}+1)

for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Then by Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda}))-\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=mz^{\prime}_{1}-z^{\prime}_{1}(z^{\prime}_{1}+1)-\tfrac{1}{4}m(m-2)+\tfrac{1}{4}n(n+2)-\tfrac{1}{4}k^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}m(n-k+m)-\tfrac{1}{4}(n-k+m)(n-k+m+2)-\tfrac{1}{4}m(m-2)+\tfrac{1}{4}n(n+2)-\tfrac{1}{4}k^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}k(n-k+1).

(2)

Suppose that
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm O}^{-}_{k},{\rm Sp}_{n})
.
Now we can write

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\binom{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a^{\prime}_{1},a^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{m_{1}+1}}
.
The assumption that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible
implies that
a^{\prime}_{1}=\tau+m_{1}
,
a^{\prime}_{j+1}=a_{j}
for
j=1,2,\ldots,m_{1}
, and
a^{\prime}_{1}\geq b_{1}
.
Hence
z_{1}^{\prime}=a_{1}^{\prime}
and
z^{\prime}_{i+1}=z_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Then the remaining argument is exactly the same as in the above case.

(3)

Suppose that
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm Sp}_{k},{\rm O}^{\epsilon}_{n})
.
Now
\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)
is written as in case (1) if
\epsilon=+
;
and as in case (2) if
\epsilon=-
.
Now
{\rm def}(\Lambda)\equiv 1\pmod{4}
, so
m
is odd.
The assumption that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible implies that

z^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{n}{2}+\left\lfloor\left(\frac{m}{2}\right)^{2}\right\rfloor-\frac{k}{2}-\left(\frac{m-1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}(n-k+m-1)

and
z^{\prime}_{i+1}=z_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,m
.
Then by Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda}))-\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=mz^{\prime}_{1}-z^{\prime}_{1}(z^{\prime}_{1}+1)-\tfrac{1}{4}(m-1)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}k(k+2)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}m(n-k+m-1)-\tfrac{1}{4}(n-k+m-1)(n-k+m+1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad-\tfrac{1}{4}(m-1)^{2}+\tfrac{1}{4}n^{2}-\tfrac{1}{4}k(k+2)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}k(n-k-1).

∎

4.
Unipotent Characters of Unitary Groups

4.1.
Finite theta correspondence of unipotent characters

Let
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
be a reductive dual pair of two unitary groups.
For symbols
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}}
and
\Lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}
,
we write
\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu}{\nu}
and
\Upsilon(\Lambda^{\prime})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu'}{\nu'}
.
Now we define
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}
as follows:

•

if
k+n
is even, then

{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}=\Biggl\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{n}}\mid\nu\preccurlyeq\mu^{\prime},\ \nu^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\mu,\\
{\rm def}(\Lambda^{\prime})=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)=0$};\\
-{\rm def}(\Lambda)+1,&\text{if ${\rm def}(\Lambda)\neq 0$}\end{cases}\,\Biggr\}.

•

if
k+n
is odd, then

{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}=\{\,(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}\times{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{n}}\mid\mu\preccurlyeq\nu^{\prime},\ \mu^{\prime}\preccurlyeq\nu,\ {\rm def}(\Lambda^{\prime})=-{\rm def}(\Lambda)-1\,\}.

Here the relation
\mu\preccurlyeq\nu^{\prime}
is given in (
2.1
).
It is known that the unipotent characters are preserved by the
\Theta_{\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime}}

(
cf
.
[
AM93
]
theorem 3.5).
The following proposition on the
\Theta
-correspondence of unipotent characters
for a unitary dual pair
is rephrased from
[
AMR96
]
théorème 5.15 (see also
[
Pan19b
]
proposition 5.13):

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))_{1}
and
\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{n}(q))_{1}
.
Then
(\rho_{\Lambda},\rho_{\Lambda^{\prime}})\in\Theta_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}
if and only if

(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}
.

4.2.
\ell
-admissible unipotent characters

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
such that
k\leq n
.
Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
, and write

\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}},\qquad\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}.

Suppose that
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})\in{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}
.
Let
d=|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|
and
d^{\prime}=|{\rm def}(\Lambda^{\prime})|
.
Define

(4.2)

\tau=\frac{1}{2}\left[\left(n-\frac{d^{\prime}(d^{\prime}+1)}{2}\right)-\left(k-\frac{d(d+1)}{2}\right)\right].

Then from the definition of
{\mathcal{B}}_{{\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n}}
we can check that

(4.3)

\tau=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(n-k+d),&\text{if $k+n+d$ is even};\\
\frac{1}{2}(n-k-d-1),&\text{if $k+n+d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

Note that
\tau
is an integer depending on
k,n
and
{\rm def}(\Lambda)
.
If
\tau\geq 0
,
then
\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)
is defined to be the symbol in
{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{n}}
such that

\Upsilon(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=\begin{cases}\Upsilon(\Lambda)^{\rm t}\cup\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\tau}{-},&\text{if $k+n$ is even};\\
\Upsilon(\Lambda)^{\rm t}\cup\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{-}{\tau},&\text{if $k+n$ is odd}.\end{cases}

For a non-negative integer
\ell
,
a symbol
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
is called
\ell
-admissible
if the following conditions hold:

(4.4)

\begin{cases}\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell-d)\text{ and }\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell+d),&\text{if $\ell$ is even, $d$ is even};\\
\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell+d+1)\text{ and }\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell-d-1),&\text{if $\ell$ is even, $d$ is odd};\\
\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell-d-1)\text{ and }\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell+d+1),&\text{if $\ell$ is odd, $d$ is even};\\
\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell+d)\text{ and }\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\ell-d),&\text{if $\ell$ is odd, $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

It is clear that if
\Lambda
is
\ell
-admissible, then it is also
\ell^{\prime}
-admissible
for any
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
.
A unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))_{1}
is called
\ell
-admissible

if
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
is
\ell
-admissible.

Lemma 4.5
.

Every unipotent character
\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))_{1}
is
k
-admissible.
Consequently, if the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
is in stable range,
then every
\rho_{\Lambda}\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}
is
(n-k)
-admissible.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
,
d=|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|
,
and write
\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,

\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}
.

(1)

Suppose that
k
is even.
Then

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}(k-d(d+1))\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(k-d),&\text{if $d$ is even};\\
\frac{1}{2}(k+d+1),&\text{if $d$ is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}(k-d(d+1))\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(k+d),&\text{if $d$ is even};\\
\frac{1}{2}(k-d-1),&\text{if $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

(2)

Suppose that
k
is odd.
Then

\displaystyle\mu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}(k-d(d+1))\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(k-d-1),&\text{if $d$ is even};\\
\frac{2}{2}(k+d),&\text{if $d$ is odd},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\nu_{1}

\displaystyle\leq|\Upsilon(\Lambda)|=\frac{1}{2}(k-d(d+1))\leq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}(k+d+1),&\text{if $d$ is even};\\
\frac{2}{2}(k-d),&\text{if $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

For two cases,
\Lambda
is always
k
-admissible.

Now if
({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
is in stable range, then we have
k\leq n-k
.
Therefore,
\Lambda
is also
(n-k)
-admissible.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

For a non-negative integer
d
,
let
\rho_{d}=\rho_{\Lambda_{d}}
denote the unipotent cuspidal character of
{\rm U}_{k}(q)
where

k=\frac{1}{2}d(d+1)
.
Then
\Lambda_{d}
is
\ell
-admissible for
\ell\geq d
if
\ell-d
is even;
and is
\ell
-admissible for
\ell\geq d+1
if
\ell-d
is odd.

Proof.

It is known that
\Lambda_{d}=\binom{d-1,d-2,\ldots,0}{-}
if
d
is even; and

\Lambda_{d}=\binom{-}{d-1,d-2,\ldots,0}
if
d
is odd,
and
\Upsilon(\Lambda_{d})=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{0}{0}
, i.e.,
\mu_{1}=\nu_{1}=0
.
Note that
{\rm def}(\Lambda_{d})=d
if
d
is even; and
{\rm def}(\Lambda_{d})=-d
if
d
is odd.
Then the lemma follows from (
4.4
) immediately.
∎

.

The condition that

from (

).

Lemma 4.10
.

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
If
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
is
(n-k)
-admissible,
then
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
is of
\Theta
-rank
k
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
is
(n-k)
-admissible for
({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
.
From the previous lemma, we know that
\tau\geq 0
and so
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
is defined.
Let
d=|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|
.

(1)

Suppose that
k+n
is even.
Now we have
\tau\geq\nu_{1}
.
Then

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\binom{\tau+m_{2},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}},\qquad\Upsilon(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\tau,\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}.

(a)

Suppose that
d
is even.
Then the condition
\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-k+d)
implies that
\rho_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}

does not occur in the

-correspondence for the dual pair

\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
of
{\rm U}_{n}(q)

\Theta
-correspondence
for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k^{\prime\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})
for any
k^{\prime\prime}<k
such that
k^{\prime\prime}+n
is odd.

(b)

Suppose that
d
is odd.
The condition
\nu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-k-d-1)
implies that
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})

does not occur in the
\Theta
-correspondence for the dual pair

({\rm U}_{k^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})
for any
k^{\prime}<k

k^{\prime}+n
is even.

\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-k+d+1)
implies
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})

\Theta
-correspondence
for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k^{\prime\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})

k^{\prime\prime}<k

(2)

k+n

.

(a)

d
is even.

\mu_{1}\leq\frac{1}{2}(n-k-d-1)

\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})

\Theta
-correspondence for the dual pair

\text{\rm rk}_{\rm U}\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}\leq\text{\rm rk}_{\rm U}\binom{\tau+m_{1},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}.

(b)

Proposition 4.11
.

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
A unipotent character
\rho^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{n}(q))_{1}
is of
\Theta
-rank
k
if and only if

\rho^{\prime}=\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
for some
(n-k)
-admissible symbol
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho^{\prime}
be a unipotent character of
{\rm U}_{n}(q)
.
Suppose that
\rho^{\prime}=\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
for some
(n-k)
-admissible
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
.
Then
\Theta\text{\rm-rk}(\rho^{\prime})=k
by Lemma
4.10
.

Now we suppose that
\rho^{\prime}
is of
\Theta
-rank
k
.
From
[
Pan21a
]
, we know that
\rho^{\prime}=\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})

for some symbol
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
.
We need to show that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible.

\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}

and let
d=|{\rm def}(\Lambda)|
.
From
[
Pan19b
]
section 8, we can conclude the following.

(1)

Suppose that
k+n
is even and
d
is even.

(a)

If
\mu_{1}>\frac{1}{2}(n-k-d)
, we know that
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
occurs
in the
\Theta
-correspondence for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})
for some
k^{\prime}<k

such that
k^{\prime}+n
is even.

(b)

If
\nu_{1}>\frac{1}{2}(n-k+d)
, then
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
occurs
in the
\Theta
-correspondence for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k^{\prime\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})
for some
k^{\prime\prime}<k

such that
k^{\prime\prime}+n
is odd.

(2)

Suppose that
k+n
is even and
d
is odd.

(a)

If
\mu_{1}>\frac{1}{2}(n-k+d+1)
, we know that
\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda})
occurs
in the
\Theta
-correspondence for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k^{\prime}},{\rm U}_{n})
for some
k^{\prime}<k

such that
k^{\prime}+n
is even.

4.3.
Degree differences for unitary dual pairs

Proposition 4.12
.

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
If
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
is
(n-k)
-admissible,
then

\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda}))=\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})+k(n-k).

Proof.

Let
\Lambda\in{\mathcal{S}}_{{\rm U}_{k}}
be
(n-k)
-admissible, and write

\Lambda=\binom{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}}{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}
,

\Upsilon(\Lambda)=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}
.

(1)

Suppose that
k+n
is odd.
The assumption that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible means that

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\binom{b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{\tau+m_{1},a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}},\qquad\Upsilon(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}{\tau,\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}.

(a)

Suppose that
m_{1}+m_{2}
is even.
Then
{\rm def}(\Lambda)=m_{1}-m_{2}
is even and non-negative, and

{\rm def}(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=m_{2}-m_{1}-1
is odd and negative.
By (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}-\frac{1}{2}m(m-2)+\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}-m_{2})

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=2(\tau+m_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(m+1)(m-1)+\frac{1}{2}(m_{1}-m_{2}+1)

where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
.
Then we have

2(\tau+m_{1})+1=n-k+m.

Now by (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle X^{0}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\{2b_{1},2b_{2},\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}\},

\displaystyle X^{1}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\{2a_{1}+1,2a_{2}+1,\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}+1\};

\displaystyle X^{0}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\{2b_{1},2b_{2},\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}\},

\displaystyle X^{1}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\{2(\tau+m_{1})+1,2a_{1}+1,2a_{2}+1,\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}+1\}.

Write

\displaystyle X^{0}_{\Lambda}\cup X^{1}_{\Lambda}=\{z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{m}\},\qquad X^{0}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}\cup X^{1}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}=\{z^{\prime}_{1},z^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,z^{\prime}_{m+1}\}

with
z_{1}>z_{2}>\cdots>z_{m}
and
z^{\prime}_{1}>z^{\prime}_{2}>\cdots>z^{\prime}_{m+1}
.
Now
z^{\prime}_{i+1}=z_{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,m
, and
z^{\prime}_{1}=n-k+m
.
Hence by Lemma
2.12
,
we have

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho_{\Lambda}))-\deg_{q}(\rho_{\Lambda})

\displaystyle=mz^{\prime}_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}z^{\prime}_{1}(z^{\prime}_{1}+1)+\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}m(m-1)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(n-k+m)(m-(n-k)-1)+\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}m(m-1)

\displaystyle=k(n-k).

(b)

Suppose that
m_{1}+m_{2}
is odd.
Then
{\rm def}(\Lambda)=m_{1}-m_{2}
is odd and negative, and

{\rm def}(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=m_{2}-m_{1}-1
is even and non-negative.
By (
2.8
), we have

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}-\frac{1}{2}m(m-2)+\frac{1}{2}(m_{2}-m_{1})

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle=2(\tau+m_{1})+\sum_{i=1}^{m_{1}}2a_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m_{2}}2b_{j}-\frac{1}{2}(m+1)(m-1)+\frac{1}{2}(m_{2}-m_{1}-1)

where
m=m_{1}+m_{2}
.
Then we have

2(\tau+m_{1})=n-k+m.

Now by (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle X^{0}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\{2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}\},

\displaystyle X^{1}_{\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\{2b_{1}+1,2b_{2}+1,\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}+1\};

\displaystyle X^{0}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\{2(\tau+m_{1}),2a_{1},2a_{2},\ldots,2a_{m_{1}}\},

\displaystyle X^{1}_{\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)}

\displaystyle=\{2b_{1}+1,2b_{2}+1,\ldots,2b_{m_{2}}+1\}.

The remaining proof is the same as in case (a).

(2)

Suppose that
k+n
is even.
The assumption that
\Lambda
is
(n-k)
-admissible means that

\underline{\theta}(\Lambda)=\binom{\tau+m_{2},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots,b_{m_{2}}}{a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{m_{1}}},\qquad\Upsilon(\underline{\theta}(\Lambda))=\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{\tau,\nu_1,\nu_2,\ldots,\nu_{m_2}}{\mu_1,\mu_2,\ldots,\mu_{m_1}}.

Now the proof is similar to that in case (1).
The only difference is that the element
\tau+m_{1}
is replaced by
\tau+m_{2}
.

∎

5.2.

Let
\text{\bf G}={\rm U}_{k}
.
For
s\in G^{*}={\rm U}_{k}(q)
, we can write

(5.10)

C_{G^{*}}(s)=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{t_{i}}{\rm GL}_{k_{ij}}^{(-1)^{i}}(q^{i})

for some non-negative integers

such that
\sum_{i=1}^{r}\sum_{j=1}^{t_{i}}ik_{ij}=k
where

{\rm GL}^{+1}_{k_{ij}}={\rm GL}^{+}_{k_{ij}}:={\rm GL}_{k_{ij}}

and
{\rm GL}^{-1}_{k_{ij}}={\rm GL}^{-}_{k_{ij}}:={\rm U}_{k_{ij}}

For
i=1
, we can let
t_{1}=q+1
and each
j=1,\ldots,t_{1}
corresponds an
eigenvalue
\lambda_{j}\in\overline{\text{\bf f}}_{q}
of
s
such that
\lambda_{j}^{q+1}=1
.
Let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
and for the Lusztig correspondence

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}\colon{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{1}\rightarrow{\mathcal{E}}(C_{G^{*}}(s))_{1}
we can write

(5.11)

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\rho^{(ij)}

for some
\rho^{(ij)}\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm GL}_{k_{ij}}^{(-1)^{i}}(q^{i}))_{1}
.
Note that
\rho^{(1j)}
is a unipotent character of
{\rm U}_{k_{1j}}(q)
.

Now for a non-negative integer
\ell
,
an irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))
is called
\ell
-admissible

if each
\rho^{(1j)}
is
\ell
-admissible for
j=1,\ldots,t_{1}
.
It is clear that if
\rho
is
\ell
-admissible,
then
\rho
is also
\ell^{\prime}
-admissible for any
\ell^{\prime}\geq\ell
,
and
\rho\chi
is also
\ell
-admissible for any linear character
\chi
of
{\rm U}_{k}(q)
.

Lemma 5.12
.

If the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
is in stable range,
then every irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
is
(n-k)
-admissible.

Proof.

Suppose that the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
is in stable range,
and let
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
for some
s
.
Write
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{r}\bigotimes_{j=1}^{t_{i}}\rho^{(ij)}
as above.
Let
k_{ij}
be given as in (
5.10
).
Then
k_{1j}\leq k\leq n-k
and hence
\rho^{(1j)}
is
(n-k)
-admissible by Lemma
4.5

for each

.
Hence
\rho
is
(n-k)
-admissible by the definition above.
∎

Lemma 5.13
.

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
If
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)
is
(n-k)
-admissible,
then
\underline{\theta}(\rho)
is defined and of
\Theta
-rank
k
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be an
(n-k)
-admissible character of
{\rm U}_{k}(q)
.
Suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
for some
s
.
Let
\text{\bf G}^{(1)}=U_{k_{1,1}}
be the component in (
5.10
) corresponding the eigenvalue
1
,
and let
\text{\bf G}^{(0)}
be the product of the other components.
So we have
C_{G^{*}}(s)=G^{(0)}\times G^{(1)}
and

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}
where
\rho^{(1)}=\rho^{(1,1)}

and
\rho^{(0)}
is the tensor product of the components
\rho^{(ij)}
with
(i,j)\neq(1,1)
.

Let
n_{1,1}
be such that
n_{1,1}-k_{1,1}=n-k
,
so the unipotent character
\rho^{(1,1)}
is
(n_{1,1}-k_{1,1})
-admissible.
Then
\underline{\theta}(\rho^{(1)})
is defined for the dual pair

({\rm U}_{k_{1,1}},{\rm U}_{n_{1,1}})
by Lemma
4.8
.
Hence
\underline{\theta}(\rho)
is defined for the dual pair
({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})

via the following commutative diagram

(5.14)

\begin{CD}\rho @>{\underline{\theta}}>{}>\rho^{\prime}\\
@V{{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}}V{}V@V{}V{{\mathfrak{L}}_{s^{\prime}}}V\\
\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}@>{{\rm id}\otimes\underline{\theta}}>{}>\rho^{(0)}\otimes\underline{\theta}(\rho^{(1)})\\
\end{CD}

where
s^{\prime}=(s,1_{n-k})\in{\rm U}_{k}(q)\times{\rm U}_{n-k}(q)\subset{\rm U}_{n}(q)

Let
\chi
be a linear character of
{\rm U}_{n}(q)
and suppose that
\underline{\theta}(\rho)\chi

is in

Proposition 5.16
.

Consider the dual pair
(\text{\bf G},\text{\bf G}^{\prime})=({\rm U}_{k},{\rm U}_{n})
where
k\leq n
.
If an irreducible character
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}({\rm U}_{k}(q))
is
(n-k)
-admissible,
then

\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))=\deg_{q}(\rho)+k(n-k).

Proof.

Let
\rho
be an
(n-k)
-admissible irreducible character of
{\rm U}_{k}(q)
.
Suppose that
\rho\in{\mathcal{E}}(G)_{s}
for some
s
and write
{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}(\rho)=\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}
.
Let
\rho^{\prime}=\underline{\theta}(\rho)\in{\mathcal{E}}(G^{\prime})_{s^{\prime}}
for some
s^{\prime}
and write

{\mathfrak{L}}_{s^{\prime}}(\rho^{\prime})=\rho^{\prime(0)}\otimes\rho^{\prime(1)}
as above.
Now we have a commutative diagram

\begin{CD}\rho @>{\underline{\theta}}>{}>\rho^{\prime}\\
@V{{\mathfrak{L}}_{s}}V{}V@V{}V{{\mathfrak{L}}_{s^{\prime}}}V\\
\rho^{(0)}\otimes\rho^{(1)}@>{{\rm id}\otimes\underline{\theta}}>{}>\rho^{\prime(0)}\otimes\rho^{\prime(1)}\\
\end{CD}

We know that

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))

\displaystyle=|G^{\prime}|_{p^{\prime}}+\deg_{q}(\rho^{\prime(0)})+\deg_{q}(\rho^{\prime(1)})-|G^{\prime(0)}|_{p^{\prime}}-|G^{\prime(1)}|_{p^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\rho)

\displaystyle=|G|_{p^{\prime}}+\deg_{q}(\rho^{(0)})+\deg_{q}(\rho^{(1)})-|G^{(0)}|_{p^{\prime}}-|G^{(1)}|_{p^{\prime}}.

Now we have
\text{\bf G}^{(1)}={\rm U}_{k-l}
and
\text{\bf G}^{\prime(1)}={\rm U}_{n-l}
for some non-negative integer
l\leq k
,

G^{(0)}\simeq G^{\prime(0)}
,
\rho^{(0)}=\rho^{\prime(0)}
.
Then by Proposition
4.12
, we have

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\rho^{\prime(1)})-\deg_{q}(\rho^{(1)})

\displaystyle=(k-l)(n-l-(k-l))=(k-l)(n-k),

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(|G^{\prime}|_{p^{\prime}})-\deg_{q}(|G|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1),

\displaystyle-\deg_{q}(|G^{\prime(1)}|_{p^{\prime}})+\deg_{q}(|G^{(1)}|_{p^{\prime}})

\displaystyle=-\tfrac{1}{2}(n-l)(n-l+1)+\tfrac{1}{2}(k-l)(k-l+1).

Therefore

\displaystyle\deg_{q}(\underline{\theta}(\rho))-\deg_{q}(\rho)

\displaystyle=(k-l)(n-k)+\tfrac{1}{2}n(n+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}k(k+1)-\tfrac{1}{2}(n-l)(n-l+1)+\tfrac{1}{2}(k-l)(k-l+1)

\displaystyle=k(n-k).

∎

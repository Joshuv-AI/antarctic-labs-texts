Berger-Coburn-Lebow representation for pure isometric representations of product system over ℕ 0 2 \mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
Dimple Saini *, Harsh Trivedi, Shankar Veerabathiran

Abstract
. We obtain Berger-Coburn-Lebow (BCL)-representation for pure isometric covariant representation of product system over ℕ 0 2 \mathbb{N}_{0}^{2} . Then the corresponding complete set of (joint) unitary invariants is studied, and the BCL- representations are compared with other canonical multi-analytic descriptions of the pure isometric covariant representation. We characterize the invariant subspaces for the pure isometric covariant representation. Also, we study the connection between the joint defect operators and Fringe operators, and the Fredholm index is introduced in this case. Finally, we introduce the notion of congruence relation to classify the isometric covariant representations of the product system over ℕ 0 2 \mathbb{N}_{0}^{2} .

Introduction

The Wold-von Neumann decomposition (cf.
[
15
,
26
]
) of an isometry is a fundamental tool in Operator theory. Berger, Coburn, and Lebow
[
3
]
considered
C^{*}
-algebras generated by
n
commuting isometries and proposed its Fredholm and representation theory. Yang
[
27
]
explored the BCL index and related it to the Fredholm tuple (cf.
[
6
]
). The classification problem is one of the important issues in the study of pairs of commuting isometries. Bercovici, Douglas, and Foias
[
2
]
classified the pairs of commuting isometries by pivotal operators. He, Qin, and Yang
[
9
]
introduced a classification using the congruence relation.

In
[
3
]
, a finite set of unitary invariants is derived, corresponding to given
n
commuting isometries, to show that there exist infinitely many
C^{*}
-algebras generated by pairs of commuting isometric tuples which are non-isomorphic algebras. Bercovici, Douglas, and Foias
[
2
]
gave a full classification of the product of
n
commuting isometries
V=V_{1}V_{2}\ldots V_{n},
when
\mbox{dim}(kerV^{*})<\infty.

Weber
[
25
]
used a deformation of the tensor product and generalized commutation relation of two isometries to show the non-exactness of the
C^{*}
-algebra, which is a tensor twist of the given isometric pair. In
[
5
]
Cuntz studied
C^{*}
-algebras generated by row isometries. Popescu
[
18
]
explored doubly
\Lambda
-commuting row isometries and its classification program.

Pimsner
[
17
]
generalized the construction of Cuntz algebras from
[
5
]
using isometric covariant representations. Muhly and Solel
[
14
]
presented the Wold decomposition for an isometric covariant representation. Arveson started the classification program for
E_{0}
-semigroups using the (tensor) product system of Hilbert spaces in
[
1
]
. The notion of a discrete product system of Hilbert bi-modules is due to Fowler
[
8
]
. Covariant representation of a product system (see
[
21
,
22
]
) is an active field of study in operator theory and operator algebras. It provides a unified approach for studying operator tuples on Hilbert spaces in a commuting case on the one hand and the non-commuting case on the other hand, and therefore generalize the setup of Popescu
[
18
]
(for comparison, see
[
24
]
).

In this paper, we study BCL-representation for a pure isometric covariant representation of a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
(see Theorems
2.4
and
2.5
) and the classification due to Bercovici, Douglas, and Foias (see Theorems
2.8
), and our approach is based on
[
2
,
12
,
27
,
9
]
. The section-wise plan is as follows: In Section 1, we recall the basic setup and the classical Wold-von Neumann decomposition for an isometric covariant representation. In Section 2, we obtain the BCL-representation for a pure isometric covariant representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
and study the connection with wandering subspaces. In Section 3, we studied a characterization of invariant subspaces for a pure isometric covariant representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. Sections 4 and 5 explore the theory of joint defect operator, Fringe operators, and Fredholm index for an isometric covariant representation of product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. Also, we introduce the notion of the congruence relation to classify the isometric covariant representations, with finite defect, of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
.

1.1.
\mathbf{Preliminaries\>\>and\>\>Notations}

Here we shall recall some basic definitions and results from
[
16
,
10
,
13
]
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a
C^{*}
-algebra and
E
be a Hilbert
C^{*}
-module over
\mathcal{A}.
We denote
\mathcal{L}(E)
to be the collection of all adjointable operators on
E,
then
\mathcal{L}(E)
is a
C^{*}
-algebra.
The Hilbert module
E
is said to be
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
(or Hilbert
\mathcal{A}
-
\mathcal{A}
-module
) if
E
has a left
\mathcal{A}
-module structure induced by a non-zero
*
-homomorphism

\phi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{L}(E),
that is,

a\eta=\phi(a)\eta\quad\quad(a\in\mathcal{A},\eta\in E).

In this paper, we assume that each
*
-homomorphism is nondegenerate, that is, the closed linear span of
\phi(\mathcal{A})E
equals
E.
Recall that the Hilbert module
E
comes equipped with the operator space structure that inherits as a subspace of the linking algebra
[
10
]
. If
F
is an another
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A},
we denote the interior tensor product of
F
and
E
by
F\otimes_{\phi}E
(cf.
[
10
]
) that satisfies

\langle\eta_{1}\otimes\xi_{1},\eta_{2}\otimes\xi_{2}\rangle=\langle\xi_{1},\phi(\langle\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\rangle)\xi_{2}\rangle,\>\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in F,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in E.

Unless it is necessary, we simply write
F\otimes E
instead of
F\otimes_{\phi}E.

Definition 1.1
.

Suppose that
E
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space. Let
V:E\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a linear map and
\sigma:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a representation. The pair
(\sigma,V)
is
called a
covariant representation
(cf.
[
13
]
) of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
if

V(b\eta a)=\sigma(b)V(\eta)\sigma(a)\quad\quad(a,b\in\mathcal{A},\eta\in E).

Then
(\sigma,V)
is
completely bounded covariant representation
(simply say,
c.b.c. representation
) if
V
is completely bounded.
Further,
(\sigma,V)
is called
isometric
if
V(\eta)^{*}V(\xi)=\sigma(\langle\eta,\xi\rangle)
for all
\xi,\eta\in E.

The following lemma is due to Muhly and Solel which is useful to classify the covariant representation of a
C^{*}
-correspondence.

Lemma 1.2
.

[
13
, Lemma 3.5]

The map
(\sigma,V)\mapsto\widetilde{V}
provides a one-to-one correspondence between the set of all c.b.c. representations
(\sigma,V)

and the set of all bounded linear maps
\widetilde{V}:E\otimes_{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
defined by

\widetilde{V}(\eta\otimes h)=V(\eta)h\quad\quad(h\in\mathcal{H},\eta\in E),

such that
\sigma(a)\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}(\phi(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})
,
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Moreover,
\widetilde{V}
is
isometry
if and only if
(\sigma,V)
is isometric.

The c.b.c. representation
(\sigma,V)
is called fully co-isometric if
\widetilde{V}
is co-isometry, i.e.,
\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

Suppose that
E
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
. Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
,

E^{\otimes n}=E\otimes_{\phi}\cdots\otimes_{\phi}E
(
n
-times) (here
E^{\otimes 0}=\mathcal{A}
) is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A}
in a natural way, with the left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
E^{\otimes n}
defined as

\phi_{n}(a)(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n})=a\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n},\>\>\>\>\xi_{i}\in E,1\leq i\leq n.

For
n\in\mathbb{N},
define
\widetilde{V}_{n}:E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by

\widetilde{V}_{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h)=V(\xi_{1})\cdots V(\xi_{n})h\quad\quad\quad(\xi_{i}\in E,h\in\mathcal{H}).

The
Fock space
of
E
(cf.
[
8
]
),
\mathcal{F}(E)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}E^{\otimes n},
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A},
where the left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by

\phi_{\infty}(a)\left(\oplus_{n\geq 0}\eta_{n}\right)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}\phi_{n}(a)\eta_{n},\>\>\eta_{n}\in E^{\otimes n}.

For
\xi\in E,
the
creation operator

V_{\xi}
determined by
\xi
on
\mathcal{F}(E)
is defined by

V_{\xi}(\eta)=\xi\otimes\eta,\>\>\eta\in E^{\otimes n},n\geq 0.

Note that
\|V_{\xi}\|=\|\xi\|
for all
\xi\in E.

Suppose that
\pi
is a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
. An isometric covariant representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
defined by

\displaystyle S(\xi)=V_{\xi}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\quad and\quad\rho(a)=\phi_{\infty}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}},\>\>\xi\in E,a\in\mathcal{A}

is called an
induced representation
(cf.
[
20
]
) induced by
\pi
. Suppose there exists a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
such that
U\sigma(a)=\rho(a)U
and
UV(\xi)=S(\xi)U,a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\xi\in E,
that is,
(\sigma,V)
is isomorphic to
(\rho,S),
then in this case also we say that
(\sigma,V)
is an
induced representation
.

Definition 1.3
.

(1)

Let
(\sigma,V)
be a c.b.c. representation of
{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
A non-zero closed subspace
\mathcal{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is said to be
(\sigma,V)
-
invariant

(resp.(\sigma,V)
-
reducing
) if it is
\sigma
-invariant and (resp. both
\mathcal{K},\mathcal{K}^{\perp}
) is invariant by each operator
V(\xi),\xi\in E.
The restriction gives a new c.b.c. representation
(\sigma,V)|_{\mathcal{K}}
of
E
on
\mathcal{K}.

(2)

A closed subspace
\mathcal{W}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is a called
wandering subspace
for
(\sigma,V)
if
\mathcal{W}
is orthogonal to

\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. We say that the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
is
generating
for
(\sigma,V)

if

\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}).

Next, we recall the Wold-von Neumann decomposition given in
[
14
, Theorem 2.9]
. We will denote
I
for
I_{\mathcal{H}}.

Theorem 1.4

(
Muhly-Solel).

Suppose that
(\sigma,V)
is an isometric covariant representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
(\sigma,V)
uniquely decomposes into a direct sum
(\sigma_{s},V_{s})\bigoplus(\sigma_{u},V_{u})
on
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}\bigoplus\mathcal{H}_{u}
such that
(\sigma_{u},V_{u})=(\sigma,V)|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}}
is a fully co-isometric covariant representation and
(\sigma_{s},V_{s})=(\sigma,V)|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}}
is an induced representation. Moreover,

\mathcal{H}_{s}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\mathcal{H}_{u}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{H}),

where
\mathcal{W}
is a wandering subspace for
(\sigma,V).

Suppose that
(\sigma,V)
is an isometric covariant representation of
E
on
\mathcal{H}
. Then by Theorem
1.4
, there exists a unitary
\Pi_{V}:\mathcal{H}

(=\mathcal{H}_{s}{\oplus}\mathcal{H}_{u})\to(\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus\mathcal{H}_{u}
such that

\displaystyle\Pi_{V}\begin{bmatrix}V_{s}(\xi)&0\\
0&V_{u}(\xi)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}S(\xi)&0\\
0&V_{u}(\xi)\\
\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{V}\quad and\quad\Pi_{V}\begin{bmatrix}\sigma_{s}(a)&0\\
0&\sigma_{u}(a)\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}\rho(a)&0\\
0&\sigma_{u}(a)\end{bmatrix}\Pi_{V}.

In fact,
\Pi_{V}(\widetilde{V}_{n}(\eta_{n}\otimes h)\oplus f)=\widetilde{S}_{n}(\eta_{n}\otimes h)\oplus f,\quad\eta_{n}\in E^{\otimes n},h\in\mathcal{W},f\in\mathcal{H}_{u}.

Moreover, if
(\sigma,V)
is pure (i.e., SOT-
\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\widetilde{V}_{n}\widetilde{V}^{*}_{n}=0
), then
\mathcal{H}_{u}
=
\{0\}
and we have

\Pi_{V}\sigma(a)=\rho(a)\Pi_{V}\quad,\quad\Pi_{V}V(\xi)=S(\xi)\Pi_{V}.

Therefore, an isometric covariant representation
(\sigma,V)
of
E
on
{\mathcal{H}}
is pure if and only
if it is isomorphic to the induced representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
on
\mathcal{F}(E)\otimes\mathcal{W}
for some Hilbert space
\mathcal{W}.

We say that
\Pi_{V}
is the
Wold-von Neumann decomposition
of the pure isometric covariant representation
(\sigma,V)
with the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}
.

The aim of this paper is to examine the following Berger-Coburn-Lebow representation (see
[
2
,
3
,
12
]
) for the pure isometric covariant representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
:

Theorem 1.5

(
Berger-Coburn-Lebow).

Suppose that

(V_{1},V_{2})
is a pair of commuting isometries on
\mathcal{H}
and
V=V_{1}V_{2}
is pure. Then there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K},
an orthogonal projection
P,
and a unitary map
U
on
\mathcal{K}
such that

{\Phi_{1}}(z)=U^{*}(zP^{\perp}+P)\quad\text{and}\quad{\Phi_{2}}(z)=(zP+P^{\perp})U\quad\quad(z\in\mathbb{D}),

are commuting inner functions in
H^{\infty}_{B(\mathcal{K})}(\mathbb{D})
and the triple
(M_{\Phi_{1}},M_{\Phi_{2}},M_{z})
is unitarily equivalent to
(V_{1},V_{2},V),
where
H^{\infty}_{B(\mathcal{K})}(\mathbb{D})
is the set of all
B(\mathcal{K})
- valued bounded analytic functions on
\mathbb{D}.

2.
BCL-representation for a pure isometric covariant representation of a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}

The classification program for commuting
n
-isometries was proposed and analyzed first by Berger, Coburn, and Lebow
[
3
]
. Later on, Bercovici, Douglas, and Foias
[
2
]
gave a complete classification for the commuting
n
-isometries under the assumption that the joint wandering subspace has a finite dimension. Popovici
[
19
]
obtained a Wold-type decomposition for a pair of commuting isometries on
\mathcal{H}
. Skalski and Zacharias
[
21
]
discussed the Popovici-Wold-type decomposition for an isometric covariant representation of product system
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}.
In
[
12
]
, an explicit version of the BCL-representation in terms of multipliers with exact coefficients is given. In this section, we will study BCL-representation for pure isometric covariant representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
and describe a complete set of (joint) unitary invariants based on
[
12
]
.

In this paper, we use
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}.
A
product system

\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
is a family of
C^{*}
-correspondences
\{E_{1},E_{2}\}
with unitary isomorphism
t_{2,1}:E_{2}\otimes E_{1}\to E_{1}\otimes E_{2}.
Now, define
t_{i,i}={\textrm{id}}_{E_{i}\otimes E_{i}},i=1,2
and
t_{1,2}=t_{2,1}^{-1}.
Therefore, for all

{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
the
C^{*}
- correspondence
\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}})
is identified with
E_{1}^{\otimes^{n_{1}}}\otimes E_{2}^{\otimes^{n_{2}}}
(for more details see
[
8
,
22
,
23
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}
is a product system over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}
is a Hilbert space. Let
V^{(i)}:E_{i}\to B(\mathcal{H})
,
1\leq i\leq 2
be the linear maps and
\sigma:\mathcal{A}\to B(\mathcal{H})
be a representation. The triple
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is called
completely bounded covariant representation
(simply say,
c.b.c. representation
) of
\mathbb{E}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H},
if each tuple
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
is a c.b.c. representation of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{H}
and satisfy the commutative relation

(2.1)

\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})=\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,j}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\quad\mbox{for}\quad 1\leq i,j\leq 2.

Moreover, the covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is said to be
isometric (resp. fully co-isometric)
if each pair
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
is isometric (resp. fully co-isometric).

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq i\leq 2,
define
\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{n}:E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes_{\sigma}\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
by the formula

\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{n}(\xi_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h)=V^{(i)}(\xi_{1})\cdots V^{(i)}(\xi_{n})h,\quad\quad(\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{n}\in E_{i},h\in\mathcal{H}).

For
{\bf{n}}=(n_{1},n_{2})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2},
we use notation
\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}},
where
\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}:\mathbb{E}(\bf{n})\otimes\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
is given by

\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}_{n_{1}}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n_{1}}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}_{n_{2}}).

Using Lemma
1.2
, let us define the bi-module map
V_{\bf{n}}:\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}})\rightarrow B(\mathcal{H})
by

V_{\bf{n}}(\xi)h=\widetilde{V}_{\bf{n}}(\xi\otimes h),\>\>\>h\in\mathcal{H},\xi\in\mathbb{E}({\bf{n}}),{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}.

Define the
Fock space

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})
of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}
by

\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})=\bigoplus_{{\bf n}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}}\mathbb{E}({\bf n}).

Then
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})
is a
C^{*}
-correspondence over
\mathcal{A},
with the left module action
\phi_{\infty}
given by
\phi_{\infty}(a)(\oplus_{{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\xi_{\bf{n}})=\oplus_{{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}\phi_{\bf{n}}(a)\xi_{\bf{n}},\>a\in\mathcal{A}
and
\xi_{\bf{n}}\in\mathbb{E}(\bf{n})
and
\phi_{\bf{n}}
is the natural left module action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathbb{E}(\bf{n}).

Let
\mathcal{K}
be a Hilbert space and
\pi
be a representation of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{K}.
For
1\leq i\leq 2,
define an isometric covariant representation
(\rho,S^{(i)})
of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by

\displaystyle\rho(a)=\phi_{\infty}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}}\quad and\quad S^{(i)}(\xi_{i})=V_{{\xi}_{i}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}},\>\>\>a\in\mathcal{A}\>,\xi_{i}\in E_{i},

where
V_{\xi_{i}}
is the creation operator determined by
\xi_{i}
on
\mathcal{F}(\mathbb{E}).
The above covariant representation
(\rho,S^{(1)},S^{(2)})
is called an
induced representation
of
\mathbb{E}
induced by
\pi.
Any covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
which is isomorphic to the induced representation
(\rho,S^{(1)},S^{(2)})
is also called an induced representation.
More generally, we say two such a covariant representations
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
of
\mathbb{E}
on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively, are
isomorphic
(cf.
[
21
]
) if there exists a unitary
U:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
U\sigma(a)=\sigma^{\prime}(a)U
and
V^{(i)^{\prime}}(\xi_{i})U=UV^{(i)}(\xi_{i}),a\in\mathcal{A},\xi_{i}\in E_{i},1\leq i\leq 2.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}
is a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. Let
\Theta:E_{2}\longrightarrow B(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
be a completely bounded
bi-module map
, that is,
\Theta
is completely bounded and
\Theta(a\xi b)=\rho(a)\Theta(\xi)\rho(b),
where
\xi\in E_{2},a,b\in\mathcal{A}.
Define a bounded linear map
\widetilde{\Theta}:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
by
\widetilde{\Theta}(\xi\otimes h)=\Theta(\xi)h
for all
\xi\in E_{2},h\in\mathcal{K},
and it satisfies
\widetilde{\Theta}(\phi_{2}(a)\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})=\rho(a)\widetilde{\Theta},
where
\phi_{2}
is the left action of
\mathcal{A}
on
E_{2}
and
a\in\mathcal{A}.

We define a corresponding completely bounded bi-module map
M_{\Theta}:E_{2}\longrightarrow B(\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
by

\displaystyle M_{\Theta}(\xi)(S_{n}^{(1)}(\xi_{n})h)=\widetilde{S}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\xi\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h),

where
\xi\in E_{2},\xi_{n}\in E_{1}^{\otimes n},h\in\mathcal{K},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}:E_{2}\otimes E_{1}^{\otimes n}\rightarrow E_{1}^{\otimes n}\otimes E_{2}
is an isomorphism which is a composition of the isomorphisms
\{t_{i,j}\>:\>1\leq i,j\leq 2\}.

Clearly
M_{\Theta}(\xi)|_{\mathcal{K}}=\Theta(\xi)
for each
\xi\in E_{2},

(\rho,{M}_{\Theta})
is a c.b.c. representation of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K},
and it satisfies

(2.2)

\displaystyle M_{\Theta}(\xi)\left(\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\xi_{n}\otimes h_{n}\right)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\xi\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h_{n}),

where
\xi\in E_{2},\xi_{n}\in E_{1}^{\otimes n},h_{n}\in\mathcal{K}.
Let
(\rho,S)
be the induced representation of
E_{1}
induced by
\pi,
then it is easy to see that

\widetilde{M}_{\Theta}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{S})=\widetilde{S}(I_{{E}_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{M}_{\Theta})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}}).
That is,
(\rho,S,M_{\Theta})
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}.

Also, observe that
\widetilde{\Theta}
is an isometry if and only if
(\rho,M_{\Theta})
is an isometric covariant representation. In this section, we only focus on
\Theta
such that
(\rho,M_{\Theta})
is a c.b.c. representation.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}
is a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. Let
(\rho,S)
be the induced representation of
E_{1}
induced by
\pi
and let
(\rho,V)
be a c.b.c. representation of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}.
Then
(\rho,S,V)
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}
if and only if there exists a completely bounded bi-module map
\Theta:E_{2}\longrightarrow B(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
such that
V=M_{\Theta}.

Proof.

Suppose that
(\rho,S,V)
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}.
Then, for
n\in\mathbb{N}

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{S}_{n})=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{{E}_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{K}}).

Define
\Theta:E_{2}\longrightarrow{B}(\mathcal{K},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K})
by
\Theta(\xi)=V(\xi)|_{\mathcal{K}},\>\xi\in E_{2}.
By Equation (
2.2
), for each
\xi\in E_{2}
,
V(\xi)
is uniquely determined by
\Theta(\xi).

Indeed, since
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{K}=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(E_{1}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{K})
and for each
\xi\in E_{2},\xi_{n}\in E_{1}^{\otimes n},h\in\mathcal{K},
we have

\displaystyle V(\xi)S_{n}(\xi_{n})h

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}(\xi\otimes S_{n}(\xi_{n})h)=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\xi\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta})(t_{2,1}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{K}})(\xi\otimes\xi_{n}\otimes h)=M_{\Theta}(\xi)S_{n}(\xi_{n})h.

It follows that
V
is uniquely determined by
\Theta
and hence
V=M_{{\Theta}}.
The converse part follows from Equation (
2.2
).
∎

Theorem 2.3
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}
is a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
. Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)})
be a pure isometric covariant representation of
E_{1}
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\Pi_{V^{(1)}}
be the Wold-von Neumann decomposition of
(\sigma,V^{(1)})
with the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}_{1}
. Let
(\sigma,V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Define an isometric covariant representation
(\rho,T)
of
E_{2}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{W}_{1}
by

T(\xi)=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}V^{(2)}(\xi)\Pi_{V^{(1)}}^{*},

where
\pi=\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}_{1}},\rho
is defined as above and
\xi\in E_{2}.

Then
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
if and only if there exists an isometric bi-module map
\Theta:E_{2}\rightarrow{B}(\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes_{\pi}\mathcal{W}_{1})
(i.e.,
\widetilde{\Theta}
is an isometry) such that
T=M_{\Theta}.
Moreover,
\Theta(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}V^{(2)}(\xi),~\xi\in E_{2}
and
P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}
is an orthogonal projection of
\mathcal{H}
onto
\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Proof.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Since
\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}}),
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{S})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{S})=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V^{(1)}}))

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{2}\otimes E_{1}}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V^{(1)}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}})(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})(t_{2,1}\otimes\Pi^{*}_{V^{(1)}})=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{T})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}}).

Therefore
(\rho,S,T)
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}
. By Lemma
2.2
, there exists an isometric bi-module map
\Theta:E_{2}\rightarrow B(\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})
such that
T=M_{\Theta}.
Since
(\sigma,V^{(1)})
is pure, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}))\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}=I_{\mathcal{H}}.

If
w\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
, then
{\Pi^{*}_{V^{(1)}}}(w)=w
and it follows from the previous equality that

V^{(2)}(\xi)w=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}V^{(2)}(\xi)w,\>\>\xi\in E_{2}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\Pi_{V^{(1)}}V^{(2)}(\xi)w

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}V^{(2)}(\xi)w=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}V^{(2)}(\xi)w.

Hence for
\xi\in E_{2},
we have
\Theta(\xi)=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E_{1}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(1)^{*}}V^{(2)}(\xi).

Conversely, suppose that
T=M_{\Theta},
then by Lemma
2.2
,
(\rho,S,T)
is a c.b.c. representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}.
Further, we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}^{*}\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{T}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}}))

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}^{*}\widetilde{T}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{S})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{F}(E_{1})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}})(I_{E_{1}\otimes E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}})

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V^{(1)}}^{*}\widetilde{T}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}}^{*}))(t_{1,2}\otimes\Pi_{V^{(1)}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}).

Therefore
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.

∎

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Define
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}),

1.2

(\sigma,V)

E:=E_{1}\otimes E_{2}.

If
(\sigma,V)

Throughout this paper, we will use the following symbols unless otherwise stated:

\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{H})\>\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\>\mathcal{W}_{i}=\mathcal{H}\ominus\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}),\quad 1\leq i\leq 2.

Note that

\displaystyle I-\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}

\displaystyle=I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}+\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}})\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}

\displaystyle=I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}+\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}))\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}.

Similarly,
I-\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*}=I-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes(I_{\mathcal{H}}-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}))\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}.
Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\oplus\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})\oplus\mathcal{W}_{2}.

For
1\leq i\leq 2,
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(I_{E_{i}}\otimes I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,1}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}))(t_{i,1}\otimes I_{E_{2}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}))(t_{i,1}\otimes I_{E_{2}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})((I_{E_{1}}\otimes t_{i,2})(t_{i,1}\otimes I_{E_{2}})\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})=\widetilde{V}(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,\bf{1}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}}),

where

is an isomorphism. Therefore
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)},V)
is also an isometric covariant representation of the product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{3}
determined by the
C^{*}
-correspondences

on
\mathcal{H}.

Suppose that

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be a pure isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
and
(\rho,M_{{\Theta_{1}}},M_{{\Theta_{2}}})
be the BCL-representation of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)}).
Then

(2.6)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}=\widetilde{S}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})})\oplus\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}}

and

(2.7)

\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}=\widetilde{S}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})})\oplus\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}},

where
(\rho,S)
is the induced representation of
E
induced by
\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{W}
is
\sigma
-invariant subspace, consider the induced representation
(\rho,S)
of
E
induced by
\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}}.
Let
w\in\mathcal{W}={\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})}\oplus\mathcal{W}_{1},
then there exist
w_{1}\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\eta_{1}\in E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}
such that
w=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+w_{1},
therefore
\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}w=\eta_{1}.
It follows that for
\xi_{2}\in E_{2},
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{1})+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{1})+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})\>\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w)-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(\xi_{2}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}w)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes(I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}))(\xi_{2}\otimes w)=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})(\xi_{2}\otimes w),

where
P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{W}_{1}.

Using Equation (
2.4
), we get
\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})\in\mathcal{W}
and hence

\displaystyle{\Theta_{2}}(\xi_{2})w

\displaystyle=M_{{\Theta}_{2}}(\xi_{2})w=\Pi_{V}V^{(2)}(\xi_{2})\Pi_{V}^{*}w=\Pi_{V}V^{(2)}(\xi_{2})w

\displaystyle=\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{1})+\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}(I_{E}\otimes\Pi_{V})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{1})+\Pi_{V}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(\xi_{2}\otimes\eta_{1})+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\xi_{2}\otimes w_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(\xi_{2}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}w)+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}})(\xi_{2}\otimes w),

the last equality follows from
\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}w=\eta_{1}.
Therefore

\widetilde{\Theta}_{2}=\widetilde{S}(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})})\oplus\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}}.

Similarly, we get the relation for
\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}.

∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
be two pure isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. Then
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
are isomorphic if and only if
(\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}},\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}},I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})})
and
(\sigma^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}},\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{\prime}}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\prime}},I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime*}}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime}}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime})})
are isomorphic, where
\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
are the wandering subspaces for
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(i)^{\prime}})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{\prime}),
respectively.

Proof.

Consider the induced representations
(\rho,S)
and
(\rho^{\prime},S^{\prime})
of
E
induced by
\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}}
and
\sigma^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{W^{\prime}}},
respectively. Let
(\rho,M_{{\Theta_{1}}},M_{{\Theta_{2}}})
and
(\rho^{\prime},M_{{\Theta_{1}^{\prime}}},M_{{\Theta_{2}^{\prime}}})
be the BCL-representation of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}}),
respectively. Suppose
(\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}},\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}},I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})})
and
(\sigma^{\prime}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}},\\
\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{\prime}}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\prime}},I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime*}}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime}}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime})})
are isomorphic, this means that, there exists a unitary operator
X:\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
such that
X\sigma(a)|_{\mathcal{W}}=\sigma^{\prime}(a)X,

(I_{E_{2}}\otimes X){\psi}_{1}={\psi}_{1}^{\prime}X
and
X{\psi}_{2}={\psi}_{2}^{\prime}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes X),
where
{\psi}_{1}=\widetilde{V}^{(2)*}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})}
,
{\psi}_{2}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}},

{\psi}_{1}^{\prime}=\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime*}}|_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{\prime}}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}^{\prime})}
and
{\psi}_{2}^{\prime}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{\prime}}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}^{\prime}}.
Then

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{{\Theta}_{1}}|_{E_{1}\otimes E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes\widetilde{\Theta}_{1})(t_{1,{\bf 1}}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})=\widetilde{S}_{n}(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes((I_{E_{1}}\otimes{\psi}_{1})\oplus{\psi}_{2}))(t_{1,{\bf 1}}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})

\displaystyle=(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes((I_{E}\otimes X^{*})\oplus X^{*})(I_{E_{1}}\otimes{\psi}_{1}^{\prime}\oplus{\psi}_{2}^{\prime})(I_{E_{1}}\otimes X))(t_{1,{\bf 1}}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})

\displaystyle=(I_{E^{\otimes n}}\otimes((I_{E}\otimes X^{*})\oplus X^{*})\widetilde{\Theta}_{1}^{\prime}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes X))(t_{1,{\bf 1}}^{(1,n)}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}}),

where
t_{1,{\bf 1}}^{(1,n)}:E_{1}\otimes E^{\otimes n}\rightarrow E^{\otimes n}\otimes E_{1}
is an isomorphism which is a composition of
\{t_{i,j}\>:\>1\leq i,j\leq 2\}.
Define a unitary operator
U:\mathcal{F}({E})\otimes\mathcal{W}\to\mathcal{F}({E})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
by

\displaystyle\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}(\mathcal{W}^{\prime})=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{1}}^{*}(\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})\cap\mathcal{W}^{\prime})\subseteq E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}.

Then
\mathcal{M}
reduces
(\rho,M_{\Theta_{1}}),
and hence
\mathcal{M}
reduces
(\rho,M_{\Theta_{1}},M_{\Theta_{2}}).

∎

.

Let

Definition 3.4
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
be c.b.c. representations of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. A bounded operator
B:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
is said to be
multi-analytic
for the covariant representations
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
if

intertwine these two representations, that is,

B\sigma(a)=\sigma^{\prime}(a)B\quad\mbox{ and}\quad BV^{(i)}(\xi_{i})=V^{(i)^{\prime}}(\xi_{i})B,\quad a\in\mathcal{A},\xi_{i}\in E_{i},1\leq i\leq 2.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
are the isometric covariant representations of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively, such that for some
1\leq i\leq 2,

(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(i)^{\prime}})
are pure. Then by Theorem
1.4
,

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(i)}(E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i})\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(i)^{\prime}}(E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}),

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}
are the generating wandering subspaces for
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(i)^{\prime}}),
respectively. Let
B:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be a multi-analytic operator, then

is uniquely determined by the operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{i}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
satisfying
\Psi\sigma(a)h=\sigma^{\prime}(a)\Psi(h),h\in\mathcal{W}_{i}
where

Indeed, for

we have
BV^{(i)}_{{n}}(\eta_{{n}})h=V_{n}^{(i)^{\prime}}(\eta_{{n}})\Psi h
and
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{n}(E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}).
Suppose that
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{i}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\left(=\bigoplus_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{n}^{(i)^{\prime}}(E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime})\right)
is an operator which satisfies
\sigma^{\prime}(a)\Psi h=\Psi\sigma(a)h,h\in\mathcal{W}_{i}.
Define an operator
M_{\Psi}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
by

M_{\Psi}V_{n}^{(i)}(\eta_{{n}})h=V_{{n}}^{\prime}(\eta_{{n}})\Psi h=V_{n}^{(i)^{\prime}}(\eta_{{n}})M_{\Psi}h\>\>\>\eta_{{n}}\in E_{i}^{\otimes n},h\in\mathcal{W}_{i},

where
\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}_{n}(E_{i}^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}).
Then
M_{\Psi}
is multi-analytic for
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(i)^{\prime}}),
but it is not multi-analytic for
(\sigma,V^{(j)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(j)^{\prime}})
for
1\leq j\leq 2
with

It happens if

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}}\widetilde{V}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W})\>\>\mbox{and}\>\>\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{2}_{0}}\widetilde{V}^{\prime}_{\mathbf{n}}(\mathbb{E}(\mathbf{n})\otimes\mathcal{W}^{\prime}),

(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
are doubly commuting isometries such that
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(i)^{\prime}})
are pure, for all
1\leq i\leq 2
(see
[
24

). This shows that
M_{\Psi}
is multi-analytic for
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}}).

An operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{i}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
\sigma^{\prime}(a)\Psi h=\Psi\sigma(a)h,h\in\mathcal{W}_{i},

is
inner
if

is an isometry. Note that
\Psi

is an isometry and

The following theorem is a characterization of the invariant subspaces for pure isometric covariant representation of a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}

which is an analogue of
[
11
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
24
, Theorem 4.4]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
such that
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
is pure for some
1\leq i\leq 2
. Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}
be a closed subspace. Then
\mathcal{M}
is invariant for
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}},{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}})
if and only if there exist a Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, an isometric covariant represenation
(\varrho_{i},V^{(i)^{\prime}},V^{(j)^{\prime}})
,
1\leq j\leq 2
with
i\neq j
, of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{K}
and an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}\to\mathcal{W}_{i}
for
(\varrho_{i},V^{(i)^{\prime}})
and
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}})
such that
(\varrho_{i},V^{(i)^{\prime}})
is pure,
M_{\Psi}\widetilde{V}^{(j)^{\prime}}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes M_{\Psi})
and

\mathcal{M}=M_{\Psi}\mathcal{K},

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}
and
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}
are the generating wandering subspaces for
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}})|_{\mathcal{M}},
respectively. In particular,
M_{\Psi}
is multi-analytic for
(\varrho_{i},V^{(i)^{\prime}},V^{(j)^{\prime}})
and
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}},{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}}).

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i\leq 2
and let
1\leq j\leq 2
with
i\neq j
. Let
\mathcal{M}\subseteq\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}
be a closed invariant subspace for
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}},{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}})
of
{\mathbb{E}}
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}.
Define an isometric covariant representation
(\pi_{i},T^{(i)},T^{(j)})=(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}},{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}})|_{\mathcal{M}}
of
\mathbb{E}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{M}
. Clearly
(\pi_{i},T^{(i)},T^{(j)})
is pure, since
(\pi_{i},T^{(i)})
is pure. Let
\Pi_{T^{(i)}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{F}(E_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}
be the Wold-von Neumann decomposition of
(\pi_{i},T^{(i)})
on
\mathcal{M},
where
\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}=\mathcal{M}\ominus\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{M})
is a generating wandering
subspace for
(\pi_{i},T^{(i)})
. That is,
\Pi_{T^{(i)}}
is unitary and satisfies

(3.4)

\Pi_{T^{(i)}}\pi_{i}(a)=\rho^{\prime}_{i}(a)\Pi_{T^{(i)}}\quad and\quad\Pi_{T^{(i)}}\widetilde{T}^{(i)}=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{T^{(i)}}),\quad a\in\mathcal{A},

where
(\rho^{\prime}_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}})
is the induced representation induced by
\pi_{i}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}}.
Then by Theorem
2.3
,
there exists an isometric bi-module map
\Theta_{T^{(j)}}:E_{j}\to B(\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime},\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime})
such that

(3.5)

\Pi_{T^{(i)}}\widetilde{T}^{(j)}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{T^{(j)}}}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{T^{(i)}}),

where
\Theta_{T^{(j)}}(\xi_{j})=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\widetilde{S}_{n}^{\mathcal{W}^{\prime}_{i}}(I_{E_{i}^{\otimes n}}\otimes P_{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}})\widetilde{T}_{n}^{(i)^{*}}T^{(j)}(\xi_{j})|_{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}},~\xi_{j}\in E_{j}.
Therefore
(\rho^{\prime}_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}},{M}_{\Theta_{T^{(j)}}})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}.

Let
i_{\mathcal{M}}
denotes the inclusion map from
{\mathcal{M}}
to
\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}.
Define an isometry
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}:\mathcal{F}(E_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}({E}_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}
by
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}=i_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi_{T^{(i)}}^{*},
then
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}=i_{\mathcal{M}}i_{\mathcal{M}}^{*}
and hence the range of
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}
equals
{\mathcal{M}}.
Note that
i_{\mathcal{M}}\pi_{i}(a)=\rho_{i}(a)i_{\mathcal{M}},

i_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{T}^{(i)}=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{i}}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes i_{\mathcal{M}})
and
i_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{T}^{(j)}=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes i_{\mathcal{M}}),
we have

\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}\rho^{\prime}_{i}(a)=\rho_{i}(a)\Pi_{\mathcal{M}},\quad\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}}=i_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{T}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{T^{(i)}}^{*})=\widetilde{S}^{\mathcal{W}_{i}}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\Pi_{\mathcal{M}})

and

\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{T^{(j)}}}=i_{\mathcal{M}}\widetilde{T}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{T^{(i)}}^{*})=\widetilde{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}).

Then
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}
is a multi-analytic operator from
(\rho^{\prime}_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}},{M}_{\Theta_{{T}^{(j)}}})
to
(\rho_{i},S^{\mathcal{W}_{i}},{M}_{\Theta_{{V}^{(j)}}}),
and hence there exists an inner operator
\Psi:\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{W}_{i}
such that
\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}=M_{\Psi}
. Therefore

\mathcal{M}=\Pi_{\mathcal{M}}(\mathcal{F}(E_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime})=M_{\Psi}(\mathcal{F}(E_{i})\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}^{\prime}).

The converse part is obvious.
∎

4.
Connection between the Defect Operator and Fringe Operators for an isometric covariant representation of a product system over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}

In this section, we will introduce the notion of
Fringe operators
and
joint defect operator
, and discuss the relationship between them. Define the
joint defect operator

C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
(or, simply
C
) of the isometric covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
by

C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})=I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}+\widetilde{V}\widetilde{V}^{*},

where
\widetilde{V}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)}).
From Equation
(\ref{WWW}),
we get

(4.1)

\displaystyle P_{\mathcal{W}}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}\oplus P_{\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})}=P_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})}\oplus P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}.

This implies that

(4.2)

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}+P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}-P_{\mathcal{W}}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}-P_{\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1})}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}-P_{\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2})}.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
. If
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)\oplus\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)
is the Wold-decomposition of
(\sigma,V),
then the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
and
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)
are
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
-reducing,

\mathcal{H}_{s}(V^{(i)})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)\quad and\quad\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{u}(V^{(i)}),\quad 1\leq i\leq 2.

Proof.

Observe that
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{u}(V^{(i)})
and thus
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V^{(i)})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}(V).
Therefore, it is enough to show that
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
reduces
(\sigma,V^{(i)}),1\leq i\leq 2.
From Equation
(\ref{WWW})
and for
1\leq j\leq 2
with
i\neq j,
we get

(4.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j})\oplus\widetilde{V}({E}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i})\subseteq\mathcal{W}\oplus\widetilde{V}({E}\otimes\mathcal{W}).

For each
\xi_{i}\in E_{i}
and
\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{W},
we have

\displaystyle V^{(i)}(\xi_{i})\widetilde{V}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V})(\xi_{i}\otimes\eta)=\widetilde{V}(I_{E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)})(t_{i,{\bf{1}}}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(\xi_{i}\otimes\eta)

\displaystyle\in\widetilde{V}({E}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}))\subseteq\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus\widetilde{V}_{2}(E^{\otimes 2}\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}(V).

Continuing in this way, we get

V^{(i)}(\xi_{i})\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus\widetilde{V}_{n+1}(E^{\otimes n+1}\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{s}(V),
for
n\geq 0.
Since
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
is
\sigma
-invariant, the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
is
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
-invariant. Observe that
\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\mathcal{W}=E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}\subseteq E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}
and by using Equation (
4.3
), we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{W}})(E\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq E_{i}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(E_{j}\otimes\mathcal{W})

\displaystyle\subseteq E_{i}\otimes(\mathcal{W}\oplus\widetilde{V}(E\otimes\mathcal{W}))\subseteq E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s}(V).

Similarly, we can easily prove that

\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W})\subseteq E_{i}\otimes(\widetilde{V}_{n-1}(E^{\otimes n-1}\otimes\mathcal{W})\oplus\widetilde{V}_{n}(E^{\otimes n}\otimes\mathcal{W}))\subseteq E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
for each
n\geq 1.
This shows that
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
reduces
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
and hence
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)
is also
(\sigma,V^{(i)})
-reduces for
1\leq i\leq 2.

∎

Definition 4.2
.

A c.b.c. representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be
doubly
commuting
(cf.
[
23
]
) if

(4.4)

\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}=(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}).

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then by Equation (
4.2
),
\mathcal{W}_{1}
is an invariant subspace for
(\sigma,V^{(2)})
if and only if
\mathcal{W}_{2}
is an invariant subspace for
(\sigma,V^{(1)}).

The following lemma shows that the relation between doubly commuting isometric covariant representation and the wandering subspace
\mathcal{W}_{i}
for
(\sigma,V^{(i)}).
We denote by
R(\widetilde{V})
and
N(\widetilde{V})
, the range of
\widetilde{V}
and the kernel of
\widetilde{V},
respectively.

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
. The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is doubly commuting;

(2)

for
i\neq j,\mathcal{W}_{i}
is an invariant subspace for
(\sigma,V^{(j)}).

Proof.

Fix
1\leq i,j\leq 2
with
i\neq j.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
Let
\eta_{j}\in E_{j}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}
and by doubly commutivity of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(\eta_{j})

\displaystyle=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}})({\eta_{j}})=0.

Therefore
{\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(E_{j}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i})}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{i}\>(=N(\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}})),
that is,
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is an invariant subspace for
(\sigma,V^{(j)}).

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
From Theorem
1.4
, let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)\oplus\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)
be the Wold-decomposition of
(\sigma,V),
then by Lemma
4.1
the subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)
and
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)
are
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
-reducing and
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{u}(V^{(i)}),1\leq i\leq 2.
This shows that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}
is an isometric as well as fully co-isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}_{u}(V).
Since
\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}=I
on
{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}
and
\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{u}(V))\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{u}(V),

\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})=(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\quad on\quad{E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}.

Thus
\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)})=(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})
on
{E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}.
Since
(\sigma,V^{(1)})|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}
is fully co-isometric, we obtain

\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}=(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})(t_{2,1}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)})(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}})\quad on\quad{E_{1}\otimes E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}.

Therefore
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})|_{\mathcal{H}_{u}(V)}
is doubly commuting. Now we need to show that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)}
is doubly commuting.
For
n\in\mathbb{N},
consider the equation

\displaystyle(\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(j)}-(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}))(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n})

\displaystyle=(\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(j)}(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V})-(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}})(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}))(I_{E_{j}\otimes E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n-1})

\displaystyle=((I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})-(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)}))(I_{E_{j}\otimes E}\otimes\widetilde{V}_{n-1})=0.

Then from Equation
(\ref{WWW})
and by hypothesis
(2)
, it is easy to verify that
\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(j)}=(I_{E_{i}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(j)})(t_{j,i}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})(I_{E_{j}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}})
on

Hence, it follows from the above observations and by the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{s}(V),
the representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})|_{\mathcal{H}_{s}(V)}
is doubly commuting.
∎

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Define the
Fringe operators

\widetilde{F}^{(1)}:E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}\to\mathcal{W}_{2}
and
\widetilde{F}^{(2)}:E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}\to\mathcal{W}_{1}
by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{(1)}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{2}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{2}}\quad and\quad\widetilde{F}^{(2)}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}}.

Since
\mathcal{W}_{i}
is
\sigma
-invariant, clearly
(\sigma_{i},F^{(i)})
is a covariant representation of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{W}_{j},
where
\sigma_{i}=\sigma|_{\mathcal{W}_{j}},1\leq j\leq 2
with

Then

\displaystyle I_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}-\widetilde{F}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{F}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=I_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}-P_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}P_{\mathcal{W}_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}|_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}},

\displaystyle=P_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}\widetilde{V}^{(i)^{*}}P_{\widetilde{V}^{(j)}({E_{j}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}})}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}|_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}},

where the last Equality follows from Equation (
4.1
). Therefore

(4.6)

\displaystyle\widetilde{F}^{(i)^{*}}\widetilde{F}^{(i)}=I_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}\Leftrightarrow P_{\widetilde{V}^{(j)}({E_{j}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{i}})}\widetilde{V}^{(i)}|_{E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j}}=0\Leftrightarrow{\widetilde{V}^{(i)}(E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{j})}\subseteq\mathcal{W}_{j},

for
1\leq i,j\leq 2
with
i\neq j.
That is,
(\sigma_{i},F^{(i)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
E_{i}
on
\mathcal{W}_{j}
if and only if

\mathcal{H},
then for
E:=E_{1}\otimes E_{2},
there is a short sequence

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E\otimes\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H})\oplus(E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{H}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0,}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d_{2}}

where

d_{1}(\eta)=(-(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta,(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\eta)\quad\>\mbox{and}\>\quad d_{2}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2},

for
\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H}
and
\eta_{i}\in E_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H},1\leq i\leq 2.

It is easy to see that
d_{2}d_{1}=0.
Indeed, let
\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H},
then we have

\displaystyle d_{2}d_{1}\eta

\displaystyle=d_{2}(-(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta,(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\eta)

\displaystyle=-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\eta=0.

Definition 5.1
.

A covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
is said to be (jointly)
Fredholm
if both
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
have closed range and

dim(N(d_{1}))+dim[N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1})]+dim[\mathcal{H}\ominus R(d_{2})]<\infty.

The
index
of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is defined by

ind(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})=-dim(N(d_{1}))+dim[N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1})]-dim[\mathcal{H}\ominus R(d_{2})].

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H},
then
it is easy to see that
N(d_{1})=0.
The following lemma is helpful to derive the proof of Theorem
5.4
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then

N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1})=\{(\eta_{1},-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}):\eta_{1}\in R(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})^{\perp},\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}\in R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\}.

Proof.

Let
y=(\eta_{1},-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1})
in the R.H.S. of the above Equation, then we have

\displaystyle d_{2}(\eta_{1},-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1})

\displaystyle=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}=0.

For each
\eta\in E\otimes\mathcal{H},
we get

\displaystyle\langle(\eta_{1},-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}),d_{1}(\eta)\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\eta_{1},(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta\rangle-\langle\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1},(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)})(t_{1,2}\otimes I_{\mathcal{H}})\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\eta_{1},(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta\rangle-\langle\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1},\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\langle\eta_{1},(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})\eta\rangle=0.

On the other hand, let
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1}),
then

\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}=d_{2}(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})=0
and thus
\eta_{2}=-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}
and
\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}\in R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}).
Since
(\eta_{1},\eta_{2})\in R(d_{1})^{\perp},

\eta_{1}\in R(I_{E_{1}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(2)})^{\perp}.

∎

The following lemma establishes the range relation between the Fringe operator and the isometric covariant representation of the product system
\mathbb{E}
over
\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
, which is an analogue of
[
28
, Lemma 1.1]
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then

R(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})=(R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}))\ominus R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)}).

Moreover,
R(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})
is closed if and only if
R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)})
is closed. Also,

N(\widetilde{F}^{(2)^{*}})=\mathcal{H}\ominus(R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)})).

Proof.

Let
\eta_{2}\in E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1},

\widetilde{F}^{(2)}\eta_{2}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}=\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}.
That is,

R(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})\subseteq(R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}))\ominus R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)}).

On the other hand, let
h\in(R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}))\ominus R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)}),
then there exist
\eta_{1}\in E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}
and
\eta_{2}\in E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{H}
such that
h=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}.
Since
h\in\mathcal{W}_{1}
and for each
\eta^{\prime}_{1}\in E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H},
we get

\displaystyle\langle\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2},\eta^{\prime}_{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\eta_{1},\eta^{\prime}_{1}\rangle+\langle\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2},\eta^{\prime}_{1}\rangle=\langle\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2},\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}^{\prime}\rangle=0.

It follows that
\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}=0
and hence

h=\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta_{1}+\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}=(I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}.
Since
\eta_{2}=(I_{E_{2}}\otimes(I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}))\eta_{2}+(I_{E_{2}}\otimes\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}})\eta_{2}
and using the commutant relation of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)}),
we have

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle=(I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}})\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(I_{E_{2}}\otimes(I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\widetilde{V}^{(1)^{*}}))\eta_{2}=P_{\mathcal{W}_{1}}\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes{\mathcal{W}_{1}}}\eta_{2}=\widetilde{F}_{2}(\eta_{2}).

Therefore
(R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})+R(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}))\ominus R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})\subseteq R(\widetilde{F}^{(2)}).

∎

By the definition of Fringe operator, it is easy to see that

(5.1)

\displaystyle N(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})=\{\eta_{2}\in E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{W}_{1}:\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}\in R(\widetilde{V}^{(1)})\}.

From Lemma
5.2
and Equation
(\ref{K6}),
define a bijection map from
N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1})
to
N(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})
by
(\eta,-\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta)\mapsto\widetilde{V}^{(2)^{*}}\widetilde{V}^{(1)}\eta
and thus dim
(N(\widetilde{F}^{(2)}))
=dim
(N(d_{2})\ominus R(d_{1})).
This gives the following theorem which is a generalization of
[
28
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem 5.4
.

Suppose that
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
Then
(\sigma_{2},F^{(2)})
is Fredholm if and only if
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is Fredholm. Moreover,

ind(\sigma_{2},F^{(2)})=ind(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)}).

For the joint defect operator
C
of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and by using Theorem
4.10
, the map
\eta_{2}\mapsto\widetilde{V}^{(2)}\eta_{2}
from
N(\widetilde{F}^{(2)})
to
\mathcal{E}_{-1}(C)
is well defined bijection. The following theorem follows from the above, and using Theorems
4.5
and
4.10
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
If the joint defect operator
C
is compact, then
(\sigma_{2},F^{(2)})
is Fredholm and

ind(\sigma_{2},F^{(2)})=dim\mathcal{E}_{-1}(C)-dim\mathcal{E}_{1}(C).

Let
\mathcal{K}
be an invariant subspace of an isometric covariant representation
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
We denote the joint defect operator
C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
restricted to
\mathcal{K}
by
C^{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)},V^{(2)}),
where
\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma|_{\mathcal{K}}.
That is,

C^{\mathcal{K}}(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)},V^{(2)})=I-\widetilde{V}^{(1)}(\widetilde{V}^{(1)}|_{E_{1}\otimes\mathcal{K}})^{*}-\widetilde{V}^{(2)}(\widetilde{V}^{(2)}|_{E_{2}\otimes\mathcal{K}})^{*}-\widetilde{V}(\widetilde{V}|_{E\otimes\mathcal{K}})^{*}.

Definition 5.6
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
be an isometric covariant representation of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}.
The two invariant subspaces
{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
of
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
are said to be
congruent
if there exists an invertible module map
L:{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\to{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
(i.e.,
L(ah)=aL(h)
and
L
is invertible,
a\in\mathcal{A},h\in\mathcal{K}
) such that

C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}=LC^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}L^{*}.

Suppose that the joint defect operator
C
is finite rank, then it can be written as a symmetric invertible matrix (see Theorem
4.12
) on
{N(C)^{\perp}}
. An invertible symmetric matrix
T

is said to have
signature

(p,q)
if there is an invertible matrix
B
such that
BTB^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}I_{p}&0\\
0&-I_{q}\\
\end{pmatrix}.

The following theorem is a generalization of
[
29
, Proposition 3.1]
.

Theorem 5.7
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{1}
and
\mathcal{K}_{2}
be
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
-invariant subspaces of
\mathcal{H}
such that the dim
(\mathcal{K}_{1})
=dim
(\mathcal{K}_{2})
and let
C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
has finite rank. Then
\mathcal{K}_{2}
is congruent to
\mathcal{K}_{1}
if and only if both
C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
have the same signature.

Proof.

We can write the subspaces
{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
as

\mathcal{K}_{1}=N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})\oplus N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})^{\perp}\quad and\quad\mathcal{K}_{2}=N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})\oplus N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})^{\perp}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{K}_{1}
is congruent to
\mathcal{K}_{2},
then there exists an invertible module map
L:{\mathcal{K}_{1}}\to{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
such that
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}=LC^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}L^{*}.
It is easy to verify that
L^{*}
maps
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})
onto
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})
and
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}=LC^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}L^{*}
on
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})^{\perp}.
Therefore both
C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
have the same signature.

Conversely, let
C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}
and
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}
have the same signature. That means, the joint defect operators restricted to the orthogonal complement of their kernels have the same signature. Then there exists an invertible module map
L_{1}
from
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})^{\perp}
onto
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})^{\perp}
such that
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}|_{N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})^{\perp}}=L_{1}C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}L_{1}^{*}.
Since dim
(N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})^{\perp})
=dim
(N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}})^{\perp})<\infty,
choose

L_{2}
to be any invertible module map from
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{1}})
onto
N(C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}).
Consider
L=L_{1}\oplus L_{2},
we get
C^{\mathcal{K}_{2}}=LC^{\mathcal{K}_{1}}L^{*}.

∎

Corollary 5.8
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
be an isometric covariant representations of
\mathbb{E}
on the Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively such that dim
(\mathcal{H})
=dim
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
and let
C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
has finite rank. Then
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
is congruent to
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
(that is, there is an invertible module map
L:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that
C(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})=LC(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})L^{*}
and
L\sigma(a)=\sigma^{\prime}(a)L
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
) if and only if both
C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
C(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
have the same signature.

The following corollary follows from the above corollary and Theorems
5.4
and
5.5
.

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
be an isometric covariant representations of
\mathbb{E}
on
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime},
respectively. Suppose that dim
(\mathcal{H})
=dim
(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
and both
C(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)})
and
C(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})
are finite rank and have the same signature. Then

ind(\sigma^{\prime},V^{(1)^{\prime}},V^{(2)^{\prime}})=ind(\sigma,V^{(1)},V^{(2)}).

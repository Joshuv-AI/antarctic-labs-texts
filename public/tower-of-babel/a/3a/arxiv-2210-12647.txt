Binary sequences with a low correlation via cyclotomic function fields with odd characteristics
Lingfei Jin, Liming Ma, Chaoping Xing

Abstract
. Sequences with a low correlation have very important applications in communications, cryptography, and compressed sensing. In the literature, many efforts have been made to construct good sequences with various lengths where binary sequences attracts great attention. As a result, various constructions of good binary sequences have been proposed. However, most of the known constructions made use of the multiplicative cyclic group structure of finite field 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} for a prime p p and a positive integer n n . In fact, all p n + 1 p^{n}+1 rational places including the place at infinity of the rational function field over 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} form a cyclic structure under an automorphism of order p n + 1 p^{n}+1 . In this paper, we make use of this cyclic structure to provide an explicit construction of binary sequences with a low correlation of length p n + 1 p^{n}+1 via cyclotomic function fields over 𝔽 p n \mathbb{F}_{p^{n}} for any odd prime p p . Each family of binary sequences has size p n − 2 p^{n}-2 and its correlation is upper bounded by 4 + ⌊ 2 ⋅ p n / 2 ⌋ 4+\lfloor 2\cdot p^{n/2}\rfloor . To the best of our knowledge, this is the first construction of binary sequences with a low correlation of length p n + 1 p^{n}+1 for odd prime p p . Moreover, our sequences can be constructed explicitly and have competitive parameters.

1.1.
Our main result and techniques

In this paper, we provide an explicit construction of binary sequences with a low correlation of length
p^{n}+1
via cyclotomic function fields over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
for any odd prime
p
. The correlation of this family of binary sequences is upper bounded by
4+\lfloor 2\cdot p^{n/2}\rfloor
. To our best knowledge, this is the first construction of binary sequences with a low correlation of length type
p^{n}+1
. In Table I, we list many families of binary sequences with low correlations for comparison.
It turns out that our binary sequences have competitive parameters.

In order to understand the main idea of this article better, we give a high-level description of our techniques.
In the language of function fields, we denote
P_{\alpha}
by the zero of
x-\alpha
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
in the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
.
We employ all rational places include the infinity place of the rational function field to obtain binary sequences with length
q+1
as given in
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
.
The automorphism group of the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is isomorphic to the projective general linear group
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
and there is an automorphism
\sigma
with order
q+1
such that
\{\sigma^{j}(P_{0})\}_{j=0}^{q}
consists of all
q+1
rational places of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)

[
7
,
9
]
.
Our binary sequences are constructed via the quadratic multiplicative character of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and evaluations of carefully chosen rational functions
z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{S}\in E=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
on such cyclically ordered rational places
\{\sigma^{j}(P_{0})\}_{j=0}^{q}
.
The correlation of this family of binary sequences is converted to determining the upper bound of the number of rational places of Kummer extension
E_{i}=E(y)
with
y^{2}=z_{i}\cdot\sigma^{-t}(z_{i})
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant S
and
1\leqslant t\leqslant q
and Kummer extension
E_{i,j}=E(y)
with
y^{2}=z_{i}\cdot\sigma^{-t}(z_{j})
for
1\leqslant i\neq j\leqslant S
and
0\leqslant t\leqslant q
.

In order to obtain binary sequences with a low correlation, the number of rational places of
E_{i}
or
E_{i,j}
can not be large.
Hence,
z_{i}\cdot\sigma^{-t}(z_{j})
can not be elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
except the case
t=0
and
i=j
, since there are much more rational places in the constant field extensions. To overcome this problem, we introduce an equivalence relation and choose at most one element in each equivalence class. Moreover, the genus of
E_{i}
or
E_{i,j}
can not be large, we need to choose functions
z_{i}
such that
z_{i}
and
\sigma^{-t}(z_{j})
have the same pole. In particular,
z_{i}
can be chosen from a Riemann-Roch space associated to some place which is invariant under the automorphism
\sigma
.

In particular, we employ the cyclotomic function fields over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
with modulus
p(x)
which is a primitive quadratic irreducible polynomial to construct such an explicit family of binary sequences with a low correlation. The Galois group of this cyclotomic function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
is a cyclic group of order
q^{2}-1
. There is a unique subgroup of order
q-1
, its fixed subfield is the function field
E
and the Galois group of
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
is a cyclic group of
q+1
which is generated by
\sigma
.
Let
Q
be the unique totally ramified place of
E
lying over
p(x)
and
\mathcal{L}(Q)
be the Riemann-Roch space of
Q
. Hence,
\sigma(Q)=Q
and rational functions
z_{1},z_{2},\cdots,z_{S}
can be chosen as representative elements of some equivalence classes of an equivalence relation on
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
. This family of binary sequences with a low correlation via cyclotomic function fields over finite fields with odd characteristics are distinct from the even case given in
[
11
]
, since there are non-negligible differences among the determination of cyclic automorphisms
\sigma
, the equivalence relations on
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
, representative elements of equivalence classes, the construction of binary sequences and the estimation of their correlations.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we present some preliminaries on definitions and basic theory of binary sequences and their correlation, rational function fields, extension theory of function fields, cyclotomic function fields and Kummer extensions.

2.1.
Binary sequences and their correlation

Let
N
be a positive integer. Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a family of binary sequences with length
N
. For every sequence
{\bf s}=(s_{0},s_{1},\dots,s_{N-1})\in{\mathcal{S}}
with
s_{i}\in\{1,-1\}
, we define the autocorrelation of
{\bf s}
at delay
t
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant N-1
by

(1)

A_{t}({\bf s}):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}s_{i}s_{i+t},

where
i+t
means the least non-negative integer after taking modulo
N
. Consider two distinct binary sequences
{\bf u}=(u_{0},u_{1},\dots,u_{N-1})
and
{\bf v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\dots,v_{N-1})
in
{\mathcal{S}}
, we define the cross-correlation of
{\bf u}
and
{\bf v}
at delay
0\leqslant t\leqslant N-1
by

(2)

C_{t}({\bf u},{\bf v}):=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}u_{i}v_{i+t}.

The correlation of the family of sequences
{\mathcal{S}}
is defined by

(3)

Cor({\mathcal{S}}):=\max\left\{\max_{{\bf s}\in{\mathcal{S}},1\leqslant t\leqslant N-1}\{|A_{t}({\bf s})|\},\max_{{\bf u}\neq{\bf v}\in{\mathcal{S}},0\leqslant t\leqslant N-1}\{|C_{t}({\bf u},{\bf v})|\}\right\}.

2.2.
Rational function fields

In this subsection, we introduce basic facts of the rational function field. The reader may refer to
[
7
,
9
,
10
,
28
]
for more details.
Let
q
be a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements.
Let
K
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, where
x
is a transcendental element over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Every finite place
P
of
K
corresponds to a monic irreducible polynomial
p(x)
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and its degree
\deg(P)
is equal to the degree of polynomial
p(x)
.
The pole of
x
is called the infinity place of
K
and denoted by
P_{\infty}
.
In fact, there are exactly
q+1
rational places of
K
, i.e., the place
P_{\alpha}
corresponding to
x-\alpha
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the infinite place
P_{\infty}
.

Let
P
be a rational place of
K
and let
\mathcal{O}_{P}
be its valuation ring. For any
f\in\mathcal{O}_{P}
,
f(P)
is defined to be the residue class of
f
modulo
P
in
\mathcal{O}_{P}/P\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
; otherwise
f(P)=\infty
for any
f\in K\setminus\mathcal{O}_{P}
.
If
f(x)=g(x)/h(x)\in K
is written as a quotient of relatively prime polynomials, then the residue class map can be determined explicitly as follows

f(P_{\alpha})=\begin{cases}g(\alpha)/h(\alpha)&\text{ if }h(\alpha)\neq 0\\
\infty&\text{ if }h(\alpha)=0\end{cases}

for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Moreover, if
\deg(g(x))<\deg(h(x))
, then
f(P_{\infty})=0.

The automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(K/\mathbb{F}_{q})
of the rational function field
K
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is isomorphic to the projective general linear group
{\rm PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Any automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(K/\mathbb{F}_{q})
is uniquely determined by
\sigma(x)
with the form

\sigma(x)=\frac{ax+b}{cx+d}

for some constants
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
with
ad-bc\neq 0
.
In particular, there exists an automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(K/\mathbb{F}_{q})
with order
q+1
such that
\sigma
acts cyclically on all rational places of
K
from
[
9
]
.

2.3.
Extension theory of function fields

Let
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an algebraic function field with genus
g
over the full constant field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\mathbb{P}_{F}
denote the set of places of
F
. Any place with degree one is called rational. From the Serre bound
[
28
, Theorem 5.3.1]
, the number
N(F)
of rational places of
F
is upper bounded by

|N(F)-q-1|\leqslant g\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Here
\lfloor x\rfloor
stands for the integer part of
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Let
\nu_{P}
be the normalized discrete valuation of
F
with respect to the place
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
. The principal divisor of a nonzero element
z\in F
is given by
(z):=\sum_{P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}}\nu_{P}(z)P
.
For a divisor
G
of
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the Riemann-Roch space associated to
G
is defined by

\mathcal{L}(G):=\{z\in F^{*}:\;(z)+G\geqslant 0\}\cup\{0\}.

If
\deg(G)\geqslant 2g-1
, then
\mathcal{L}(G)
is a vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of dimension
\deg(G)-g+1
from the Riemann-Roch theorem
[
28
, Theorem 1.5.17]
.

Let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite extension of function field
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. The Hurwitz genus formula
[
28
, Theorem 3.4.13]
yields

2{g(E)}-2=[E:F]\cdot(2g(F)-2)+\deg\text{ Diff}(E/F),

where
\text{Diff}(E/F)
stands for the different of
E/F
.
For
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{E}
with
Q|P
, let
d(Q|P),e(Q|P)
be the different exponent and ramification index of
Q|P
, respectively. Then the different of
E/F
can be given by

\text{Diff}(E/F)=\sum_{Q\in\mathbb{P}_{E}}d(Q|P)Q.

If
p\nmid e(Q|P)
, then
d(Q|P)=e(Q|P)-1
from Dedekind’s Different Theorem
[
28
, Theorem 3.5.1]
.

Let
{\rm Aut}(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
denote the automorphism group of
F
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, i.e.,

{\rm Aut}(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})=\{\sigma:F\rightarrow F|\;\sigma\mbox{ is an }\mathbb{F}_{q}\mbox{-automorphism of }F\}.

We can consider the group action of automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
on the set of places of
F
.
From
[
22
, Lemma 1]
, we have the following results.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any automorphism
\sigma\in{\rm Aut}(F/\mathbb{F}_{q})
,
P\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
and
f\in F
, we have

(1)

\deg(\sigma(P))=\deg(P)
;

(2)

\nu_{\sigma(P)}(\sigma(f))=\nu_{P}(f)
;

(3)

\sigma(f)(\sigma(P))=f(P)
provided that
\nu_{P}(f)\geqslant 0
.

From Lemma
2.1
, we have
\sigma(\mathcal{L}(D))=\mathcal{L}(\sigma(D))
for any divisor
D
of
F
. In particular, if
\sigma(P)=P
, then
\sigma(\mathcal{L}(rP))=\mathcal{L}(\sigma(rP))=\mathcal{L}(rP)
for any
r\in\mathbb{N}
.

2.4.
Cyclotomic function fields

The basic theory of cyclotomic function fields was developed in the language of function fields by Hayes
[
6
]
.
Let
x
be an indeterminate over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
R
be the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
,
K
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
, and
K^{ac}
be the algebraic closure of
K
. Let
\varphi
be the endomorphism of
K^{ac}
given by

\varphi(z)=z^{q}+xz

for all
z\in K^{ac}
. Define a ring homomorphism

R\rightarrow\text{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(K^{ac}),f(x)\mapsto f(\varphi).

Then the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space of
K^{ac}
is made into an
R
-module by introducing the following action of
R
on
K^{ac}
, that is,

z^{f(x)}=f(\varphi)(z)

for all
f(x)\in R
and
z\in K^{ac}
. For a nonzero polynomial
M\in R
, we consider the set of
M
-torsion points of
K^{ac}
defined by

\Lambda_{M}=\{z\in K^{ac}|z^{M}=0\}.

The cyclotomic function field over
K
with modulus
M
is defined by the subfield of
K^{ac}
generated over
K
by all elements of
\Lambda_{M}
, and it is denoted by
K(\Lambda_{M})
. Let
p(x)=x^{2}+ax+b
be an irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
. In particular, we have the following facts from
[
20
,
11
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
p(x)=x^{2}+ax+b
be an irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Let
F
be the cyclotomic function field
K(\Lambda_{p(x)})
with modulus
p(x)
over
K
.
Then the following results hold:

(i)

[F:K]=q^{2}-1
and
F=K(\lambda),\text{ where }\lambda^{q^{2}-1}+(x^{q}+x+a)\lambda^{q-1}+x^{2}+ax+b=0.

(ii)

There is a unique place of
F
lying over
p(x)
which is totally ramified in
F/K
.

(iii)

The infinite place
\infty
of
K
splits into
q+1
rational places, each with ramification index
q-1
in the extension
F/K
.

(iv)

All other places of
K
except
p(x)
and
\infty
are unramified in
F/K
.

(v)

The Galois group of
F
over
K
is
{\rm Gal}(F/K)\cong(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(p(x)))^{*}
. Moreover, the automorphism
\sigma_{f}\in{\rm Gal}(F/K)
associated to
\overline{f}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]/(p(x)))^{*}
is determined by
\sigma_{f}(\lambda)=\lambda^{f}
.

2.5.
Kummer extensions

The theory of Kummer extension of function fields can be summarized as follows from
[
28
, Proposition 3.7.3 and Corollary 3.7.4]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
K
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
and
n
be a positive divisor of
q-1
. Suppose that
u\in K
is an element satisfying
u\neq\omega^{d}\text{ for all }\omega\in F\text{ and }d|n
with
d>1.

Let
F
be the Kummer extension over
K
defined by

F=K(y)\text{ with }y^{n}=u.

Then we have:

(a)

The polynomial
\phi(T)=T^{n}-u
is the minimal polynomial of
y
over
K
. The extension
F/K
is a cyclic extension of degree
n
, and the automorphisms of
F/K
are given by
\sigma(y)=\zeta y
, where
\zeta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
is an
n
-th root of unity.

(b)

Let
Q\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
be an extension of
P\in\mathbb{P}_{K}
. Let
r_{P}
be the greatest common divisor of
n
and
\nu_{P}(u)
, i.e.,
r_{P}=\gcd(n,\nu_{P}(u))
. Then one has

e(Q|P)=\frac{n}{r_{P}}\quad\text{and}\quad d(Q|P)=\frac{n}{r_{P}}-1.

(c)

Assume that there is a place
R\in\mathbb{P}_{F}
such that
\gcd(\nu_{Q}(u),n)=1
. Then
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the full constant field of
F
.

3.
Binary sequences with a low correlation of length
q+1

Let
p
be an odd prime,
m
be a positive integer,
q=p^{m}
be a prime power and
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements.
In this section, we will construct a family of binary sequences with a low correlation of length
q+1
via cyclotomic function fields over the finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with an odd characteristic
p
.

3.1.
Fixed subfields of cyclotomic function fields

Let
K
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Let
p(x)=x^{2}+ax+b
be a primitive irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Let
F
be the cyclotomic function field
K(\Lambda_{p(x)})
with modulus
p(x)
over
K
.
From Proposition
2.2
, we have
F=K(\lambda)
and the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
is an abelian group of order
q^{2}-1
.
Moreover, we have the following result which is similar as
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
for the case of odd characteristics.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
K
be the rational function field
\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
.
Let
p(x)=x^{2}+ax+b
be a primitive irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Let
F
be the cyclotomic function field
K(\Lambda_{p(x)})
with modulus
p(x)
over
K
.
Then the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
is a cyclic group of order
q^{2}-1
.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be the
K
-automorphism of
F
defined by
\eta(\lambda)=\lambda^{x}
from Proposition
2.2
.
Then
\eta^{i}
can be determined by
\eta^{i}(\lambda)=\lambda^{x^{i}}
for any positive integer
i
.
Hence,
\eta^{i}=id
if and only if
x^{i}\equiv 1\ (\text{mod }p(x))
.
Since
p(x)
is primitive, the order of
\eta
is
q^{2}-1
.
∎

From Lemma
3.1
, there exists a unique subgroup
G
of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
with order
q-1
. Such a unique subgroup
G
can be determined explicitly as follows.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\eta
be the generator of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
determined by
\eta(\lambda)=\lambda^{x}
and let
\tau=\eta^{q+1}
be an automorphism of
F
. Then the unique subgroup
G
of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
with order
q-1
is the cyclic group generated by
\tau
, i.e.,

G=\langle\tau\rangle=\{\tau_{c}\in{\rm Gal}(F/K):\tau_{c}(\lambda)=c\lambda\text{ for }c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}.

Proof.

From the Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion
[
28
, Proposition 3.1.15]
, we have
x^{2}+ax+b
divides
x^{q}+x+a
, i.e.,

x^{q}\equiv-x-a\ (\text{mod }x^{2}+ax+b).

Let
\tau=\eta^{q+1}
. It is easy to verify that
\tau(\lambda)=\eta^{q+1}(\lambda)=\lambda^{x^{q+1}}=\lambda^{-x(x+a)}=\lambda^{b}=b\lambda
, since
x^{q+1}=x\cdot x^{q}\equiv-x(x+a)\equiv b\ (\text{mod }x^{2}+ax+b).

From
[
16
, Lemma 3.17]
, the constant
b
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.
Hence, the order of
\tau
is
q-1
and
G=\langle\tau\rangle=\{\tau_{c}\in{\rm Gal}(F/K):\tau_{c}(\lambda)=c\lambda\text{ for }c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}
from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Let
E
be the fixed subfield of
F
with respect to
G
, that is,
E=F^{G}=\{z\in F:\sigma(z)=z\text{ for any }\sigma\in G\}.

From Galois theory,
E/K
is an abelian extension of degree
q+1
. In particular, the fixed subfield
E
can be characterized explicitly as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u=\lambda^{x}/x=\lambda^{q-1}+x
. Then we have
F=K(\lambda)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u,\lambda)
and
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
is a Kummer extension determined by

\lambda^{q-1}=-\frac{u^{2}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}.

Moreover,
u
and
x
satisfy the following equation

\frac{u^{q+1}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}=x.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.2
or
[
20
, Section 4]
, the cyclotomic function field
F=K(\Lambda_{p(x)})
is given by
F=K(\lambda)
with

\lambda^{q^{2}-1}+(x^{q}+x+a)\lambda^{q-1}+x^{2}+ax+b=0.

Substituting
x
with
u-\lambda^{q-1}
, one has

\lambda^{q^{2}-1}+[(u-\lambda^{q-1})^{q}+(u-\lambda^{q-1})+a]\lambda^{q-1}+(u-\lambda^{q-1})^{2}+a(u-\lambda^{q-1})+b=0.

It follows that

\lambda^{q-1}=-\frac{u^{2}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}.

Since
x=u-\lambda^{q-1}
, we have
F=K(\lambda)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,\lambda)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u,\lambda)
and

x=u-\lambda^{q-1}=u+\frac{u^{2}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}=\frac{u^{q+1}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}.

∎

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\eta
be the generator of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
determined by
\eta(\lambda)=\lambda^{x}
,
\tau=\eta^{q+1}
and
G=\langle\tau\rangle
. Then the fixed subfield of
F
with respect to
G
is
E=F^{G}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
.

Proof.

It is easy to verify that
\tau(\lambda^{q-1})=(\tau(\lambda))^{q-1}=(b\lambda)^{q-1}=\lambda^{q-1}
. From Proposition
3.3
, we have
\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u,x)=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,x+\lambda^{q-1})=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x,\lambda^{q-1})\subseteq F^{\langle\tau\rangle}=F^{G}.

From Galois theory, the degree of extension
F/F^{G}
is
[F:F^{G}]=|G|=q-1
.
From the proof of Proposition
3.3
,
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
is a Kummer extension given by

\lambda^{q-1}=-\frac{u^{2}+au+b}{u^{q}-u}.

From Proposition
2.3
, we have
[F:\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)]=q-1
.
Hence, we have
E=F^{G}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
.
∎

Let
Q
be a place of
E
lying over
p(x)
. From Proposition
2.2
,
Q|p(x)
is totally ramified in
E/K
with ramification index
e(Q|p(x))=q+1
and different exponent
d(Q|p(x))=q
.
From the Hurwitz genus formula
[
28
, Theorem 3.4.13]
, we obtain

-2=2g(E)-2\geqslant(q+1)[2g(K)-2]+2q.

It follows that all other places of
E
except
Q
are unramified in
E/K
.
Moreover, the place
Q
can be characterized similarly as
[
11
, Proposition 4.2]
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
Q
be the unique place of
E
lying over
p(x)
. Then the place
Q
corresponds to the monic quadratic irreducible polynomial
p(u)=u^{2}+au+b
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.2
,
p(x)
is totally ramified in
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
.
Hence,
Q
is totally ramified in
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
with
\deg(Q)=2
.
From Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
3.3
, the zeros of
u^{2}+au+b
,
1/u
and
u-\alpha
with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
are all totally ramified in the extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
. However, the zero of
u^{2}+au+b
is the unique totally ramified place of
\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
in the extension
F/\mathbb{F}_{q}(u)
with degree
2
.
Hence, the place
Q
corresponds to the monic quadratic polynomial
p(u)=u^{2}+au+b
.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\eta
be the generator of
{\rm Gal}(F/K)
determined by
\eta(\lambda)=\lambda^{x}
and let
\sigma=\eta^{q}|_{E}
.
Then
\sigma
is a
K
-automorphism of
E
with order
q+1
and the automorphism
\sigma
can be determined explicitly by

\sigma(u)=\frac{-b}{u+a}.

In particular, the Galois group of
E/K
is a cyclic group given by
{\rm Gal}(E/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle.

Proof.

From Galois theory, we have
{\rm Gal}(E/K)\cong{\rm Gal}(F/K)/{\rm Gal}(F/E)=\langle\eta\rangle/\langle\eta^{q+1}\rangle
.
Thus, the order of
\eta
is
q+1
in the group
{\rm Gal}(E/K)
. Since
\gcd(q,q+1)=1
, the order of
\sigma=\eta^{q}|_{E}
is
q+1
.
It is easy to verify that
\sigma(u)=\sigma(\lambda^{x}/\lambda)=\lambda^{x^{q+1}}/\lambda^{x^{q}}
.
From the proof of Proposition
3.2
, we have
x^{q}\equiv-x-a\ (\text{mod }x^{2}+ax+b)
and
x^{q+1}=x\cdot x^{q}\equiv x(-x-a)\equiv b\ (\text{mod }x^{2}+ax+b)
.
It follows that
\lambda^{x^{q}}=\lambda^{-x-a}
,
\lambda^{x^{q+1}}=\lambda^{b}
and

\displaystyle\sigma(u)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{x^{q+1}}}{\lambda^{x^{q}}}=\frac{\lambda^{b}}{\lambda^{-x-a}}=\frac{b\lambda}{-\lambda^{x}-a\lambda}=\frac{-b}{u+a}\in E.

Hence,
\sigma
is a
K
-automorphism of
E
and
{\rm Gal}(E/K)=\langle\sigma\rangle.

∎

3.2.
An equivalence relation on
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}

Let
Q
be the place of
E
with degree two corresponding to the irreducible polynomial
p(u)=u^{2}+au+b
from Lemma
3.5
.
Since the degree of
Q
is
\deg(Q)=2\geqslant 2g(E)-1=-1
, the dimension of Riemann-Roch space
\mathcal{L}(Q)
is
\ell(Q)=\deg(Q)-g(E)+1=3
from the Riemann-Roch theorem. It is clear that
\mathbb{F}_{q}=\mathcal{L}(0)\subseteq\mathcal{L}(Q)
.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{L}(Q)
can be determined explicitly as

\mathcal{L}(Q)=\left\{\frac{c_{0}+c_{1}u+c_{2}u^{2}}{u^{2}+au+b}\in E:c_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{ for }0\leqslant i\leqslant 2\right\}.

Now we can define a relation
\sim
on
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
as follows: for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
,

z_{1}\sim z_{2}\Leftrightarrow\exists\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)\text{ such that }z_{1}\cdot\tau(z_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}.

Here
E^{2}
stands for the set
\{z^{2}:z\in E\}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

The relation
\sim
defined as above is an equivalence relation on the set
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

It is clear that three axioms of an equivalence relation are satisfied:

(1)

Reflexivity:
For any
z\in\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
,
z\cdot id(z)=z\cdot z=z^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
. Hence, we have
z\sim z
.

(2)

Symmetry:
If
z_{1}\sim z_{2}
, then there exists
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)
such that
z_{1}\cdot\tau(z_{2})=\alpha\cdot v^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
v\in E
.
It follows that
z_{2}\cdot\tau^{-1}(z_{1})=\tau^{-1}(\alpha\cdot v^{2})=\tau^{-1}(\alpha)\cdot\tau^{-1}(v^{2})=\alpha\cdot(\tau^{-1}(v))^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
. Hence, we have
z_{2}\sim z_{1}
.

(3)

Transitivity:
If
z_{1}\sim z_{2}
and
z_{2}\sim z_{3}
, there exist
\tau_{1}
and
\tau_{2}
in
{\rm Gal}(E/K)
such that
z_{1}\cdot\tau_{1}(z_{2})=\alpha_{1}\cdot v_{1}^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
and
z_{2}\cdot\tau_{2}(z_{3})=\alpha_{2}\cdot v_{2}^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for some
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
It follows that
z_{1}\cdot\tau_{1}(z_{2})\cdot\tau_{1}(z_{2}\cdot\tau_{2}(z_{3}))=z_{1}\cdot(\tau_{1}(z_{2}))^{2}\cdot\tau_{1}\tau_{2}(z_{3})=\alpha_{1}\alpha_{2}\cdot v_{1}^{2}(\tau_{1}(v_{2}))^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
, i.e.,
z_{1}\cdot\tau_{1}\tau_{2}(z_{3})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
. Hence, we have
z_{1}\sim z_{3}
.

∎

For any element
z\in\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
, let
[z]
denote the equivalence class
\{x\in V:x\sim z\}
containing
z
.
In this subsection, we want to determine representative elements of equivalence classes of
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
under the relation
\sim
. Since
\alpha\cdot z\in[z]
for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, the relation
\sim
induces an equivalence relation on the set
V:=(\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\})/\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
.
Hence, it will be sufficient to determine representative elements of equivalent classes of
V
.
Let
S_{1}
be the set

S_{1}=\left\{\frac{u^{2}+cu+d}{u^{2}+au+b}\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}:u^{2}+cu+d\text{ is an irreducible polynomial in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]\right\},

let
S_{2}
be the set

S_{2}=\left\{\frac{u-\alpha}{u^{2}+au+b}:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{(u-\alpha)(u-\beta)}{u^{2}+au+b}:\alpha\neq\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\},

and let
S_{3}
be the set

S_{3}=\left\{\frac{1}{u^{2}+au+b}\right\}\cup\left\{\frac{(u-\alpha)^{2}}{u^{2}+au+b}:\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\right\}.

From
[
16
, Corollary 3.21]
, the number of monic irreducible polynomials of degree
2
in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]
is
(q^{2}-q)/2
. Hence, the cardinality of
S_{1}
is
|S_{1}|=(q^{2}-q)/2-1=(q-2)(q+1)/2.

By choosing representatives with monic leading coefficients of numerators of elements in
V
, we can identify
V
as
S_{1}\cup S_{2}\cup S_{3}\cup\{1\}
.

It is easy to see that all elements in
S_{3}
are equivalent to
1/(u^{2}+au+b)
.
In order to determine the equivalence classes of the set
S_{1}
under the relation
\sim
, we need to study the ramification behavior of places of
E
with degree
2
in the extension
E/K
. The following result can be found from
[
21
, Proposition 1.4.12]
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
F/K
be an abelian extension of function fields and let
E
be an intermediate field of
F/K
.
Assume that the place
P
of
K
is unramified in
F/K
. Then
P
splits completely in
E/K
if and only if the Artin symbol
[\frac{F/K}{P}]
belongs to
{\rm Gal}(F/E)
.

Now let us study the ramification behavior of places of
K
with degree
2
in the abelian extension
E/K
.

Proposition 3.9
.

There are exactly
(q-3)/2
distinct places of
K
with degree
2
which split completely in
E
, and there exists a unique rational place of
K
which splits into
(q+1)/2
places of
E
with degree
2
. All such places of
E
with degree
2
correspond to the numerators of elements in
S_{1}
.

Proof.

There are
(q^{2}-q)/2
places of the rational function field
E
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
with degree
2
in total from
[
16
, Corollary 3.21]
and
[
28
, Proposition 1.2.1]
.
Let
Q_{i}
be all pairwise distinct places of
E
with degree
2
other than
Q
and let
P_{i}
be its restriction to
K
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-2)(q+1)/2
.
From Proposition
2.2
,
Q_{i}|P_{i}
is unramified in
E/K
and the Artin symbol of
P_{i}\in\mathbb{P}_{K}
in the abelian extension
F/K
is given by

\left[\frac{F/K}{P_{i}}\right](\lambda)=\lambda^{P_{i}}.

Since
E
is the fixed subfield of
F
with respect to
G
from Proposition
3.2
and Proposition
3.4
, the Galois group of
F/E
is
{\rm Gal}(F/E)=G=\{\tau_{c}\in{\rm Gal}(F/K):\tau_{c}(\lambda)=c\lambda\text{ for }c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\}.

From Lemma
3.8
,
P_{i}
splits completely in
E/K
if and only if there exists an element
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that

\left[\frac{F/K}{P_{i}}\right](\lambda)=\lambda^{P_{i}}=\lambda^{\delta_{i}}.

Hence,
P_{i}=x^{2}+ax+b+\delta_{i}
must be a quadratic irreducible polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
In fact, the quadratic polynomial
P_{i}
is irreducible if and only if there doesn’t exist
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
such that
\alpha^{2}+a\alpha+b+\delta_{i}=0
, i.e., for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, we have

\delta_{i}\neq-(\alpha^{2}+a\alpha+b)=-\left(\alpha+\frac{a}{2}\right)^{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{4}-b\in-\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{4}-b.

Hence,
P_{i}=x^{2}+ax+b+\delta_{i}
with
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus(-\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}+a^{2}/4-b)
are irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
.
Since the cardinality of
-\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}+a^{2}/4-b
is
(q+1)/2
, there are exactly
q-1-(q+1)/2=(q-3)/2
different choices of
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
such that
P_{i}
is irreducible, i.e., there are exactly
(q-3)/2
places of
K
with degree
2
which split completely into
(q-3)(q+1)/2
places of
E
with degree
2
.

Let
P
be a place of
K
which is unramified in
F/K
and let
R
be any place of
F
lying over
P
. The order of Artin symbol
\left[\frac{F/K}{P}\right]
is
f(R|P)=\deg(R)/\deg(P)
.
Since there are
(q-2)(q+1)/2
places of
E
with degree
2
except
Q
, the remaining
(q+1)/2
places of
E
with degree
2
must lie over a rational place of
K
from
[
28
, Theorem 3.7.2]
.
∎

From Proposition
3.9
,
P_{i}=x^{2}+ax+b+\delta_{i}
with
\delta_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}\setminus(-\mathbb{F}_{q}^{2}+a^{2}/4-b)
are distinct places of
K
of degree
2
which split completely in
E/K
. Let
Q_{i}=u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[u]
be any place of
E
lying over
P_{i}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-3)/2
.
Now we try to characterize the equivalence classes of
S_{1}
.
Let
R_{1}
be the set defined by

R_{1}:=\left\{z_{i}=\frac{u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i}}{u^{2}+au+b}:1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-3)/2\right\}.

Proposition 3.10
.

For any
z_{i}\neq z_{j}\in R_{1}
, one has
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{j})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for any
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)
, i.e.,
[z_{i}]
are distinct equivalence classes of
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-3)/2
.

Proof.

The principal divisor of
z_{i}=(u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i})/(u^{2}+au+b)
is given by

(z_{i})=\left(\frac{u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i}}{u^{2}+au+b}\right)=Q_{i}-Q.

From Lemma
2.1
, the principal divisor of
\tau(z_{i})
is
(\tau(z_{i}))=\tau((z_{i}))=\tau(Q_{i}-Q)=\tau(Q_{i})-\tau(Q).

Since
Q
is totally ramified in
E/K
, one has
\tau(Q)=Q
for
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)
.
For
z_{i}\neq z_{j}\in R_{1}
, we have
Q_{i}\neq Q_{j}
and the principal divisor of
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{j})
is given by

(z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{j}))=Q_{i}-Q+\tau(Q_{j})-\tau(Q)=Q_{i}+\tau(Q_{j})-2Q.

Since
Q_{i}
and
Q_{j}
lie over distinct places of
K
with degree
2
from Proposition
3.9
, we have
\tau(Q_{j})\neq Q_{i}
for any
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)
. Hence, we have
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{j})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
.
∎

From Proposition
3.10
,
[z_{i}]
are pairwise distinct equivalence classes of
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-3)/2
.
In particular, their representative elements satisfy the following property.

Proposition 3.11
.

For any
z_{i}\in R_{1}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant(q-3)/2
and any automorphism
\tau\in{\rm Gal}(E/K)\setminus\{id\}
, one has
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{i})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
.

Proof.

The principal divisor of
z_{i}=(u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i})/(u^{2}+au+b)
is given by

(z_{i})=\left(\frac{u^{2}+c_{i}u+d_{i}}{u^{2}+au+b}\right)=Q_{i}-Q.

From Lemma
2.1
and Proposition
3.10
, the principal divisor of
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{i})
is given by

\left(z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{i})\right)=Q_{i}-Q+\tau(Q_{i})-\tau(Q)=Q_{i}+\tau(Q_{i})-2Q.

Since
P_{i}
splits completely in
E/K
from Proposition
3.9
, we have
\tau(Q_{i})\neq Q_{i}
. Hence, one has
z_{i}\cdot\tau(z_{i})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
.
∎

In the following, we want to determine the equivalence classes of
S_{2}
.
Since
E
is a rational function field over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, there exist exactly
q+1
rational places which are places of
E
lying over the infinity place
\infty
of
K
from Proposition
2.2
.
From Theorem
3.6
, the Galois group
{\rm Gal}(E/K)
is a cyclic group generated by an automorphism
\sigma
with order
q+1
.
Let
P_{0}
be the zero of
u
in
E
and
P_{\sigma^{j}}=\sigma^{j}(P_{0})
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant q
. From
[
28
, Theorem 3.7.1]
,
P_{\sigma^{j}}
are distinct rational places of
E
for
0\leqslant j\leqslant q
.
In particular, we have
P_{\sigma^{1}}=\sigma(P_{0})=P_{\infty}
from Theorem

Let
\alpha_{j}
be the element

in
\mathbb{F}_{q}

It is easy to see that

are pairwise distinct elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
for
0\leqslant j\neq 1\leqslant q
. Let
w_{j}=(u-\alpha_{j})/(u^{2}+au+b)
for

and let

be a set defined by

Proposition 3.12
.

For
w_{i}\neq w_{j}\in R_{2}
, one has
w_{i}\cdot\sigma^{t}(w_{j})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant q
, i.e.,
[w_{j}]
are pairwise distinct equivalence classes of
\mathcal{L}(Q)\setminus\{0\}
for
2\leqslant j\leqslant(q+1)/2
.

Proof.

The principal divisor of
w_{i}
is given by

(w_{i})=P_{\sigma^{i}}+P_{\infty}-Q=\sigma^{i}(P_{0})+\sigma(P_{0})-Q.

From Lemma
2.1
, the principal divisor of
\sigma^{t}(w_{j})
for each
0\leqslant t\leqslant q
is

(\sigma^{t}(w_{j}))=\sigma^{t}(P_{\sigma^{j}})+\sigma^{t}(P_{\infty})-\sigma^{t}(Q)=\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})+\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})-Q.

If
w_{i}\sim w_{j}
for
2\leqslant j\neq i\leqslant(q+1)/2
, i.e.,
w_{i}\cdot\sigma^{t}(w_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for some
0\leqslant t\leqslant q
, then we have

\begin{cases}\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})=\sigma^{i}(P_{0})\\
\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})=\sigma(P_{0})\end{cases}\text{ or }\quad\begin{cases}\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})=\sigma(P_{0})\\
\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})=\sigma^{i}(P_{0})\end{cases}.

If
\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})=\sigma(P_{0})
, then we have
t=0
. It follows that
j=i
which is impossible.
If
\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})=\sigma^{i}(P_{0})
, then there exists an integer
t
with
1\leqslant t\leqslant q
such that
t+1=i
and
t+j=1+q+1
.
Hence, we obtain
i+j=q+3
which is a contradiction.
∎

Now we want to classify all elements of
S_{2}
which are equivalent to
w_{j}
for each
2\leqslant j\leqslant(q+1)/2
.
From the definition of the equivalence relation
\sim
, it is clear that
\sigma^{t}(w_{j})
is equivalent to
w_{j}
for any
0\leqslant t\leqslant q
.
From Lemma
2.1
, the principal divisor of
\sigma^{t}(w_{j})
for each
1\leqslant t\leqslant q
is given by

(\sigma^{t}(w_{j}))=\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})+\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})-Q.

If
\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})=P_{\infty}
, i.e.,
t+j=1+(q+1)
, then
t+1=q+3-j
and
w_{j}\sim w_{q+3-j}
from the proof of Proposition
3.12
.
If

q+1

For

and
[w_{j}]
contains at least
(q+1)/2

is
q(q+1)/2

have the following property.

Proposition 3.13
.

For any
w_{j}\in R_{2}
with

The principal divisor of

\displaystyle=P_{\sigma^{j}}+P_{\infty}-Q+\sigma^{t}(P_{\sigma^{j}})+\sigma^{t}(P_{\infty})-\sigma^{t}(Q)

\displaystyle=\sigma^{j}(P_{0})+\sigma(P_{0})+\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})+\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})-2Q.

Assume that
w_{j}\cdot\sigma^{t}(w_{j})\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
. Since

, we must have
\sigma^{t+1}(P_{0})=\sigma^{j}(P_{0})
and
\sigma^{t+j}(P_{0})=\sigma(P_{0})

and

.
Hence, we obtain

which is a contradiction.

∎

Theorem 3.14
.

For any

, we have

for

for

From Proposition

From Proposition

For simplicity, let

for

are pairwise distinct equivalence classes of

Let

\eta(\alpha)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\alpha\text{ is a square in }\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*},\\

Assume that

\eta
to a map from
\mathbb{F}_{q}

\eta(\alpha)=\begin{cases}1&\text{ if }\alpha\text{ is a square in }\mathbb{F}_{q},\\

Let
P_{0}
be the zero of
u
in
E
and
P_{\sigma^{j}}=\sigma^{j}(P_{0})
for

For each equivalence class

for

In the following, we will show that this family of binary sequences

with length

Proposition 3.15
.

If
q=p^{m}
is a power of odd prime, then the autocorrelation of
s_{i}
with
1\leqslant i\leqslant q-2
at delay
t
for

is upper bounded by

|A_{t}(s_{i})|\leqslant 4+\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Proof.

The autocorrelation of
s_{i}
at delay
t
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant q
is given by

\displaystyle A_{t}(s_{i})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{q}s_{i,j}s_{i,j+t}=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\eta(z_{i}(P_{\sigma^{j}}))\cdot\eta(z_{i}(P_{\sigma^{j+t}}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{q}\eta(z_{i}(P_{\sigma^{j}}))\cdot\eta((\sigma^{-t}(z_{i}))(P_{\sigma^{j}})).

Let
Z_{i}=\{0\leqslant j\leqslant q:(z_{i}\sigma^{-t}(z_{i}))(P_{\sigma^{j}})=0\}
. It is easy to see that

, since
z_{i}\in\mathcal{L}(Q)
and
z_{i}\sigma^{-t}(z_{i})\in\mathcal{L}(2Q)
from Proposition
3.11
and Proposition
3.13
. Thus, we have

For any
z_{i}\in R_{1}\cup R_{2}
, we have
z_{i}\sigma^{-t}(z_{i})\notin\mathbb{F}_{q}\cdot E^{2}
for
1\leqslant t\leqslant q
from Proposition
3.11
and Proposition
3.13
.
Consider the Kummer extension

given by

with

If

from Proposition
3.11
. Hence, there are two places of

with degree

from Proposition
2.3

denote the cardinality of the set

From
[

q+1-\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor\leqslant N(E_{i})=2N_{1}+|Z_{i}|\leqslant q+1+\lfloor 2\sqrt{q}\rfloor.

Hence, we have

∎

4.
Algorithm and numerical results

The previous section provides a theoretical construction of binary sequences with a low correlation via cyclotomic function fields over finite fields with odd characteristics.
In fact, such a family of binary sequences constructed in Section
3

From Theorem
3.6
, the Galois group

\sigma

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

a_{1}=0,b_{1}=-b,c_{1}=1,d_{1}=a

be the automorphism of

\begin{cases}a_{j+1}=a_{1}a_{j}+c_{1}b_{j}=b_{j},\\
b_{j+1}=b_{1}a_{j}+d_{1}b_{j}=-ba_{j}+ab_{j},\\
c_{j+1}=a_{1}c_{j}+c_{1}d_{j}=d_{j},\\
d_{j+1}=b_{1}c_{j}+d_{1}d_{j}=-bc_{j}+ad_{j}.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.2

Let
P_{0}

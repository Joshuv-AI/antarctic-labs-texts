Dual Cheeger constant for weighted graphs over ordered fields Thanks: Keywords : weighted graphs, Laplace operator, non-Archimedean field, ordered field, Levi-Civita field, dual Cheeger constant. Mathematics Subject Classification 2010: 05C50, 05C22, 47A75, 12J15, 39A12
Anna Muranova

Abstract
. We consider a dual Cheeger constant h ¯ \overline{h} for finite graphs with edge weights from an arbitrary real-closed ordered field. We obtain estimates of h ¯ \overline{h} in terms of number of vertices in graph. Further, we estimate the largest eigenvalue for the discrete Laplace operator in terms of h ¯ \overline{h} and show the sharpness of estimates. As an example we consider graphs over non-Archimedean field of the Levi-Civita numbers.



Introduction

The Cheeger isoperimetric constant
h
was firstly introduced for compact Riemannian manifold by Jeff Cheeger
[
4
]
in 1969 and then considered for graphs (e.g.
[
5
,
11
]
). The dual Cheeger constant
\overline{h}
was introduced directly on weighted graphs in
[
1
]
. The dual Cheeger constant shows how close the graph is to bipartite and it is used in estimation of the largest eigenvalue of discrete Laplacian. Classical approach to weighted graph assume, that edge weights are positive real numbers. In this paper we consider the model, which was introduced in previous works by author (see
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
), where edge weights are positive elements of an arbitrary real-closed ordered field
(\mathbb{K},\succ)
. We introduce the notion of dual Cheeger constant
\overline{h}
for finite graphs analogously to the classical case of field
\mathbb{R}
and prove its estimates in terms of number
N
of vertices in graph. We show, that for even
N
holds

\overline{h}\succeq\dfrac{N}{2(N-1)}

and for odd
N
holds

\overline{h}\succeq\dfrac{N+1}{2N}.

These estimates improve the result in
[
1
]
, which states that
\overline{h}\geq\dfrac{1}{2}
for the field
\mathbb{R}
. The sharpness of our result follows from examples in
[
1
]
, since the equality holds for a complete graph with all the weights equal
1
over
\mathbb{R}
(and over any real-closed field, due to the same calculations).

Further, we show that for any
\mathbb{K}
the largest eigenvalue of the Laplace operator can be estimated as

(1)

2\overline{h}\preceq\lambda_{N-1}\preceq 1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}},

where the both equalities hold for any bipartite graph.

In Section
4
, we consider graphs over a non-Archimedean field of the Levi-Civita numbers
\mathcal{R}
and show, that left inequality in can hold also for complete graphs, while the right one is precise for them in a sense, that there is a sequence of graphs, for which the difference
|\lambda_{N-1}-1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}}|
converges in an order topology.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Real-closed ordered field

Firstly we remind a concept of real-closed ordered field. More detailed description can be found in textbooks on Algebra see e. g.
[
9
,
18
]
.

Definition 1
.

A field
\mathbb{K}
is called
ordered
, if there exist a subset
\mathbb{K}^{+}~\subset\mathbb{K}
with the following properties:

(1)

0\not\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
,

(2)

for any non-zero element
k\in\mathbb{K}
either
k\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
or
-k\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
,

(3)

if
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
, then
k_{1}+k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
and
k_{1}k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
.

The total order in the ordered field is then defined as follows: for
k_{1},k_{2}\in\mathbb{K}
we write
k_{1}\succ k_{2}
(or, equivalently,
k_{2}\prec k_{1}
) if
(k_{1}-k_{2})\in\mathbb{K}^{+}
. Further
k_{1}\succeq k_{2}
(equiv.
k_{1}\preceq k_{2}
) if
(k_{1}-k_{2})\in\mathbb{K}^{+}\cup\{0\}
.

An ordered field
\mathbb{K}
is
non-Archimedean
if there exist an
infinitesimal
, i.e.
\epsilon\in\mathbb{K}
such that

{\epsilon}\prec\dfrac{1}{n}=\dfrac{1}{\underbrace{1+\dots+1}_{n\mbox{ times}}}

for any
n\in\mathbb{N}\subset\mathbb{K}
. Otherwise, the field is called
Archimedean
. All Archimedean fields are isomorphic to some (not necessary proper) subfield of real numbers. Examples of non-Archimedean fields are ordered field of rational functions
[
3
, p. A.VI.21]
, Levi-Civita field
[
2
,
8
]
, super-real field
[
6
]
.

By
absolute value of
k\in\mathbb{K}
we mean
|k|\in\mathbb{K}^{+}\cup\{0\}
defined as

|k|=\begin{cases}k,\mbox{ if }k\succeq 0\\
-k,\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The convergence of sequence
\{k_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
to
k
in ordered topology of the ordered field means by definition, that for any element
a\in\mathbb{K}
exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
n\geq N_{0}
we have

|k_{n}-k|\preceq a.

There are several equivalent definitions of a real-closed field
[
6
, p. 38]
. We remind some of them.

An ordered field
\mathbb{K}
is
real-closed
if one of the following holds:

(1)

\mathbb{K}
has no proper algebraic extension to an ordered field,

(2)

the complexification of
\mathbb{K}
is algebraically closed,

(3)

every positive element in
\mathbb{K}
has a square root and every polynomial over
\mathbb{K}
of odd degree has a root in
\mathbb{K}
.

It is well known, that order can be introduced in any real-closed field. Moreover, any ordered field has an algebraic real-closed extension closure, which is unique up to isomorphism, preserving the order (Artin-Schreier Theorem, see
[
6
, p. 38]
). Therefore, further in this note we consider exclusively real-closed ordered fields, e.g. algebraic numbers, real numbers, Levi-Civita field.

1.2.
Graphs over ordered field

The concept of graph over an ordered field was introduced in previous works by author (see
[
12
,
13
]
). Graphs over the field
\mathbb{R}
of real numbers are classical weighted graphs. Here we shortly remind the definition. field.

Definition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be a real-closed ordered.
A graph oven an ordered field
K
(OF-graph)
is a couple
(V,b)
, where
V
is a set of vertices (i.e. arbitrary set)
and
b:V\times V\rightarrow\mathbb{K}^{+}\cup\{0\}
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

b(x,y)=b(y,x)
for any
x,y\in V
,

(2)

b(x,x)=0
for any
x\in V
.

The last property mean that we consider graphs
without loops
. We write
x\sim y
whenever
b(x,y)\neq 0
and say that there is an
edge
between
x
and
y
.

A
subgraph
of a graph
(V,b)
is any graph
(U,b\left|{}_{U\times U}\right.)
, where
U\subset V
.

A
path
between any vertices
x,y\in V
is any sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n},n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

x=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim x_{2}\sim\dots\sim x_{n}=y.

A graph is called
connected
, if there exists a path between any two vertices of it.

A
connected component
of a graph is any its connected subgraph that is not part of any larger connected subgraph. Therefore, set of vertices of graph is a disjoint union of vertices of all its connected components.

Further in this note we consider finite (
\#V<\infty
) graphs. For the sake of brevity futher we refer to a graph by its set of vertices (e.g.
V
).

A graph is called
bipartite
, if there exist a partition
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
of vertices,
such that from
x\sim y
follows that
x\in V_{1},y\in V_{2}
or
x\in V_{2},y\in V_{1}
.

A graph is called
complete
, if
x\sim y
for any two
x,y\in V
with
x\neq y
.

The
normalized
weight on edges is defined by

(2)

p(x,y)=\dfrac{b(x,y)}{b(x)},\mbox{ for any }x,y\in V,

where
b(x)=\sum_{y}b(x,y)
. If
b(x)=0
then the vertex
x
is called
isolated vertex
. Further we consider graphs without isolated vertices, therefore
p(x,y)
is well defined.

For any
V_{1}\subset V
we denote

b(V_{1})=\sum_{x\in V_{1}}b(x)

and for any
V_{1},V_{2}\subset V
with
V_{1}\cap~V_{2}=\emptyset
we denote

b(V_{1},V_{2})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V_{1},\\
y\in V_{2}\end{subarray}}b(x,y).

1.3.
Laplace operator

Our main interest is normalized Laplace operator (Laplacian) defined on the set of functions

\mathfrak{F}_{V}=\{f\mid f:V\to\mathbb{K}\}

by

(3)

\mathcal{L}f(x)=\sum_{y\in V}(f(x)-f(y))\dfrac{b(x,y)}{b(x)}=\sum_{y\in V}(f(x)-f(y))p(x,y).

An
eigenvalue
of
\mathcal{L}
is defined as
\lambda
such that
\mathcal{L}f=\lambda v
, where
v
is called
eigenfunction
.
It is proven in
[
14
]
that all
N=\#V
eigenvalues of the Laplacian for a connected graph belong to the same field
\mathbb{K}
as edge weights and all the eigenfunctions are in
\mathfrak{F}_{V}
. Moreover, it is shown there that the largest eigenvalue of the connected graph
satisfies

\dfrac{N}{N-1}\preceq\lambda_{N-1}\preceq 2

and
\lambda_{N-1}=2
if and only if the graph is bipartite.

For non-connected graph the eigenvalues are union of eigenvalues of its connected components (with multiplicities) and eigenfunctions are extension of eigenfunctions of connected components to the whole graph, which are
0
on the other components.

Here and further we order eigenvalues of Laplacian as

0=\lambda_{0}\preceq\lambda_{1}\preceq\lambda_{2}\preceq\dots\preceq\lambda_{N-1}\preceq 2.

The multiplicity of the eigenvalue
0
is equal to the number of connected components of the graph.

For any function
f\in\mathfrak{F}_{V}
we denote

V_{f}^{+}=\{x\in V\mid f(x)\succ 0\}

and

V_{f}^{-}=\{x\in V\mid f(x)\prec 0\}.

We introduce an analogue of scalar product on
\mathfrak{F}_{V}
by

\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)g(x)b(x).

for any
f,g\in\mathfrak{F}_{V}
.
The Green formula (see e.g.
[
12
]
) states, that

\langle\mathcal{L}f,g\rangle=\dfrac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in V}(f(x)-f(y))(g(x)-g(y))b(x,y).

2.
Dual Cheeger constant

The dual Cheeger constant for weighted graphs was introduced in
[
1
]
. Here we introduce it in the same way for OF-graphs.

Definition 3
.

Let
V
be an OF-graph with
N=\#V
vertices. Then its
dual Cheeger constant
is defined by

(4)

\overline{h}=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}\emptyset\neq V_{1}\subset V,\\
\emptyset\neq V_{2}\subset V,\\
V_{1}\cap V_{2}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}\dfrac{2b(V_{1},V_{2})}{b(V_{1})+b(V_{2})},

i.e. the maximum is taken over all pairs of non-empty disjoint subsets of
V
.

Lemma 1
.

For any connected graph the dual Cheeger constant satisfies

(5)

\overline{h}\preceq 1.

Moreover,
\overline{h}=1
if and only if the graph is bipartite.

The proof follows the same outline as in
[
1
]
.

Proof.

The inequality (
5
) follows immediately, since
b(V_{1},V_{2})\preceq b(V_{l})
for
l=1,2.

Further, let
V
is bipartite with the partition
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
. Then equality in (
5
) is attained on this partition.
Let now
\overline{h}=1
. Then

2b(V_{1},V_{2})=b(V_{1})+b(V_{2}),

i.e. there is no edges between the vertices from the same
V_{l}
, for
l=1,2
. Moreover, since the graph is connected,
V_{3}=\emptyset
. Therefore, the graph is bipartite and
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
is its partition.
∎

Theorem 1
.

(a)
For a graph
V
with
N=2K\geq 2
vertices and at least one edge, we have

\overline{h}\succeq\frac{N}{2(N-1)}.

(b)
For a graph
V
with
N=2K+1>2
vertices and at least one edge, we have

\overline{h}\succeq\dfrac{N+1}{2N}.

Proof.

(a) Let us consider only such
V_{1},V_{2}\subset V
that
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
is a partition of the graph with
\#V_{1}=\#V_{2}=K
. There are
{N\choose K}=\frac{N!}{K!(N-K)!}
such partitions. For each such
V_{1},V_{2}
we define

\overline{h}(V_{1},V_{2}):=\frac{2b(V_{1},V_{2})}{b(V_{1})+b(V_{2})}.

therefore
\overline{h}\succeq\overline{h}(V_{1},V_{2})
.

For every such a partition
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
we have

\overline{h}(V_{1},V_{2})=\frac{2\sum_{x\in V_{1},y\in V_{2}}b(x,y)}{b(V)}=\frac{2\sum_{x\in V_{1},y\in V_{2}}b(x,y)}{\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)},

and thus

(6)

\frac{2\sum_{x\in V_{1},y\in V_{2}}b(x,y)}{\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)}\preceq\overline{h}

Note that
b({x_{0}},y_{0})
for some
x_{0},y_{0}\in V
is in numerator if and only if
{x_{0}}\in V_{1}
and
{y_{0}}\in V_{2}
. It’s easy to count that among all the considered partitions
there are
{{N-2}\choose{K-1}}
such partitions for any given ordered pair
(x_{0},y_{0})
.
Now we sum up all inequalities (
6
), rewrite the numerator using the last observation and obtain

{{N-2}\choose{K-1}}\frac{2\sum_{x_{0},y_{0}\in V}b(x_{0},y_{0})}{\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)}\preceq\overline{h}{N\choose K}

from where follows

2{{N-2}\choose{K-1}}\preceq\overline{h}{N\choose K}

and, further,

\overline{h}\succeq\dfrac{2(N-K)K}{N(N-1)}=\dfrac{K}{(N-1)}=\dfrac{N}{2(N-1)},

since
N=2K
.

(b) The idea of the proof is the same, as in the part (a), but the corresponding partitions are
\#V_{1}=K,\#V_{2}=K+1
. Then, following the same outline as in (a), we get

\overline{h}\succeq\dfrac{2(N-K)K}{N(N-1)}=\dfrac{N-K}{N}=\dfrac{N+1}{2N},

since
N=2K+1
.

∎

Therefore, the following Corollary, which improves the result
\overline{h}\geq\frac{1}{2}
in
[
1
]
, holds:

Corollary 1
.

For any finite graph with at least one edge

\overline{h}\succ\dfrac{1}{2}.

3.
Largest eigenvalue of the graph Laplacian

In this section we show the relation between dual Cheeger constant and the largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{N-1}
of the discrete Laplacian for the finite graph over an ordered field. For the field
\mathbb{R}
it is done in
[
1
]
and the result is similar to the result, which relate Cheeger constant and smallest non-zero eigenvalue (see e. g.
[
5
]
,
[
7
]
for
\mathbb{R}
and
[
14
]
for ordered fields).

Theorem 2
.

The largest eigenvalue
\lambda_{N-1}
of the graph Laplacian satisfies

2\overline{h}\preceq\lambda_{N-1}\preceq 1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}}.

For the proof of this theorem we need the following important lemma, which is proved in
[
1
]
for
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
, but there the proof uses integrals.
Here we present the proof of the lemma, which is analogous to the prove of Theorem 2 in
[
14
]
and uses the technique, used in
[
5
]
for the proof of the relation between the smallest non-zero eigenvalue of Laplacian and Cheeger constant.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(V,b)
be a graph without isolated vertices and
f\in\mathfrak{F}_{V}
be such that
V^{+}_{f}\neq\emptyset
and
V\setminus V^{+}_{f}\neq\emptyset
. Let

h(f)=\min_{\emptyset\neq S\subset V^{+}_{f}}\dfrac{b(S,V\setminus S)}{b(S)}

and

f_{+}(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),x\in V^{+}_{f},\\
0,\mbox{ otherwise},\end{cases}

then

1-\sqrt{1-h^{2}(f)}\preceq\dfrac{\langle\mathcal{L}f_{+},f_{+}\rangle}{\langle f_{+},f_{+}\rangle}\preceq 1+\sqrt{1-h^{2}(f)}.

Proof.

Let us denote

W:=\dfrac{\langle\mathcal{L}f_{+},f_{+}\rangle}{\langle f_{+},f_{+}\rangle}.

By Green formula we can rewrite
W
as

W=\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x,y\in V}(f_{+}(x)-f_{+}(y))^{2}b(x,y)}{\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)}.

Then multiplying the numerator and the denominator by
\sum_{x,y\in V}(f_{+}(x)+f_{+}(y))^{2}b(x,y)
and using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

(7)

W\succeq\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)\left|f_{+}^{2}(x)-f_{+}^{2}(y)\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)\right)\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}(f_{+}(x)+f_{+}(y))^{2}b(x,y)\right)}.

From the other hand,

\displaystyle W\cdot{\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)}=\langle\mathcal{L}f_{+},f_{+}\rangle

\displaystyle=2\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)-\left(\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)+\sum_{x,y\in V}f_{+}(x)f_{+}(y)b(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)-\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}f_{+}(x)(f_{+}(x)+f_{+}(y))b(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)-\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}f_{+}(y)(f_{+}(x)+f_{+}(y))b(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=2\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}(f_{+}(x)+f_{+}(y))^{2}b(x,y)\right),

where in the last two lines we have switched notations for
x,y
in the second sum and sum up two lines and divided by two in the last line. Using this in denominator of (
7
) we get

(8)

W\succeq\dfrac{\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)\left|f_{+}^{2}(x)-f_{+}^{2}(y)\right|\right)^{2}}{\left(\sum_{x\in V}f_{+}^{2}(x)b(x)\right)^{2}\left(4-2W\right)}.

Now our goal is to estimate the numerator of (
8
)

A:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{x,y\in V}b(x,y)\left|f_{+}^{2}(x)-f_{+}^{2}(y)\right|\right)^{2}

in terms of
h(f)
.
Firstly, let us order all the vertices of the graph as
x_{1},x_{2}\dots,x_{N}
in the way that
f(x_{i})\preceq f(x_{i+1})
. Further, let us denote

S_{k}=\{x_{k+1},\dots,x_{N}\}\subset~V\;\;\;\mbox{ and }\;\;\;n_{0}=\max_{i}\{i\mid x_{i}\not\in V^{+}_{f},0\leq i<N\},

i.e. for any
k\geq n_{0}
we have
S_{k}\subset~V^{+}_{f}
.

Now we can rewrite
A
as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}b(x_{i},x_{j})\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{j})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{i})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}b(x_{i},x_{j})\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{j=i+1}^{N}\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}b(x_{i},x_{j})\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=[\mbox{change limits of summation for $k$ and $j$}]

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\sum_{k=i}^{N-1}\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}b(x_{i},x_{j})\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=[\mbox{change limits of summation for $i$ and $k$}]

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=k+1}^{N}b(x_{i},x_{j})}_{b(S_{k},V\setminus S_{k})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N-1}{b(S_{k},V\setminus S_{k})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2\left(\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{N-1}{b(S_{k},V\setminus S_{k})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\succeq 2\left(\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{N-1}{h(f)b(S_{k})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2h^{2}(f)\left(\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{N-1}{\sum_{i=k+1}^{N}b(x_{i})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=[\mbox{change limits of summation for $i$ and $k$}]

\displaystyle=2h^{2}(f)\left(\sum_{i=n_{0}+1}^{N}{\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{i-1}b(x_{i})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{k+1})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{k})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2h^{2}(f)\left(\sum_{i=n_{0}+1}^{N}{b(x_{i})}\left(f_{+}^{2}(x_{i})-f_{+}^{2}(x_{n_{0}})\right)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=2h^{2}(f)\left(\sum_{i=n_{0}+1}^{N}{b(x_{i})}f_{+}^{2}(x_{i})\right)^{2}=2h^{2}(f)\left(\sum_{x\in V}{b(x)}f_{+}^{2}(x)\right)^{2},

where we have used several times that
f_{+}(x_{k})=0
for any
k\leq n_{0}
.
Applying the last estimate to (
8
) we obtain

W\succeq\dfrac{2h^{2}(f)}{(4-2W)},

from where immediately follows the statement of the lemma.
∎

Proof of Theorem
2
.

The proof follows exactly the same outline as proof of the Theorem 3.2 in
[
1
]
and we do not repeat it here.
Note that the facts that

\lambda_{N-1}=\sup_{f\neq 0}\dfrac{\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle}=\max_{f\neq 0}\dfrac{\langle\mathcal{L}f,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle},

where maximum is attained on the corresponding eigenfunction and

2-\lambda_{N-1}=\inf_{f\neq 0}\dfrac{\langle(2\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{L})f,f\rangle}{\langle f,f\rangle},

where
\mathcal{I}:\mathfrak{F}_{V}\rightarrow\mathfrak{F}_{V}
is the identical operator, follow from decomposition of
f
by orthonormal basis, consisting of eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}
(for more details see
[
14
]
).

∎

4.
Examples

4.1.
Levi-Civita field

The Levi-Civita field was introduced by Tullio Levi-Civita in 1862
[
10
]
.
The Levi-Civita field is the smallest non-Archimedean real-closed ordered field, which is Cauchy complete in the order topology. Moreover, it has a subfield, isomorphic to the field of rational functions, whose elements naturally appear as edge weights in the theory of electrical networks (see e.g.
[
12
,
13
]
).

Firstly, we recall the definition and main notations for the Levi-Civita field
\mathcal{R}
(see e.g.
[
2
,
8
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
).

Definition 4
.

A subset
M
of the rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
is called
left-finite
if for every number
r\in\mathbb{Q}
there are only finitely many elements of
M
that are smaller than
r
.

Definition 5
.

[
10
]

We define the Levi-Civita field
\mathcal{R}
as a field of formal power series

(9)

a=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}a_{i}\epsilon^{q_{i}},

with
a_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and
Q=\{q_{i}\}_{i=0}^{\infty}\subset\mathbb{Q}
being left-finite.

Addition and multiplication are defined naturally as for formal power series. The order is defined as follows:
a\succ 0
if
a_{0}>0
.

Note that
\epsilon
here is considered as a ‘fixed’ variable. It is infinitesimal, as well as e.g.
\epsilon^{2}
,
5\epsilon^{\frac{1}{2}}+2\epsilon
, etc.

From the definition of elements of the Levi-Civita field follows that to prove the convergence
r_{n}\to r\in\mathcal{R}
it is enough to prove, that for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
exists
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|r_{n}-r|<\epsilon^{m}
for any
n>N_{0}
.

Example 1
.

Let us consider a family of complete graphs
V(n)
with
3
vertices and weights as at the Figure

(i.e.
b(x,y)=\epsilon^{n}
in
V(n)

One can check, that non-constant eigenvectors of the Laplacian are

v_{1}(n)=(-1,1,0)\mbox{ and }v_{2}(n)=\left(-\dfrac{1}{1+\epsilon^{n}},-\dfrac{1}{1+\epsilon^{n}},1\right)

with the corresponding eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(n)=\dfrac{1+2\epsilon^{n}}{1+\epsilon^{n}}\mbox{ and }\lambda_{2}(n)=\dfrac{2+\epsilon^{n}}{1+\epsilon^{n}}=2-\epsilon^{n}+\epsilon^{2n}-\epsilon^{3n}\dots,

where for
\lambda_{2}(n)
we have represented ratio as formal power series.

Considering all the posibilities, one can see that the dual Cheeger constant

\overline{h}(n)=\dfrac{2}{2+\epsilon^{n}}=1-\dfrac{\epsilon^{n}}{2}+\dfrac{\epsilon^{2n}}{4}-\dfrac{\epsilon^{3n}}{8}\dots

is attained on the partition of vertices
\{x,y\}\cup\{z\}
.
Then

1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h}(n))^{2}}=2-\dfrac{\epsilon^{2n}}{8}+\dfrac{\epsilon^{3n}}{8}\dots.

Note that the difference

1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h}(n))^{2}}-2\overline{h}(n)\to 0,\mbox{ as }n\to\infty

in a sence of the ordered topology. Indeed, the graph
V(n)
differ from a bipartite graph by the edge
x\sim y
, whose weight
b(x,y)=\epsilon^{n}
is infinitesimal and the weights converge to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

Example 2
.

Let us consider a complete graph
V
with
N=2K
vertices and the following edge weights: let fix a partition of vertices
V=V_{1}\cup V_{2}
with
\#V_{1}=\#V_{2}=K
and let
b(x,y)=\epsilon^{n}
(where
n\in\mathbb{N}
is fixed) if
x,y
belong to the same
V_{i}
and
b(x,y)=1
otherwise. Firstly we calculate the dual Cheeger constant for this graph. For any
x\in V
we have
b(x)={K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}}
. Let
U_{1},U_{2}
be disjoint subsets of
V
.

1 case.
Let
\#U_{1}=K_{1}>K
and then
\#U_{2}=K_{2}<K
. In this case

\overline{h}(U_{1},U_{2}):=\dfrac{2\sum_{x\in U_{1},y\in U_{2}}b(x,y)}{(K_{1}+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})}

is maximazed for fixed
K_{1},K_{2}
as

\overline{h}(K_{1},K_{2}):=\dfrac{KK_{2}+(K_{1}-K_{2})\epsilon^{n}}{(K_{1}+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})},

i.e. when
U_{1}\supset V_{1}
(or
U_{1}\supset V_{2}
). Further,

(10)

\displaystyle\overline{h}(K_{1},K_{2})=

\displaystyle\dfrac{2(KK_{2}+(K_{1}-K)\epsilon^{n})}{(K_{1}+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})}

(11)

\displaystyle\preceq

\displaystyle\dfrac{2K(K_{2}+1)}{(K+1+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})},

since
K_{1}\succeq K+1
and
(K_{1}-K)\epsilon^{n}
is infinitesimal. Therefore,

\overline{h}(K_{1},K_{2})\leq\overline{h}(V_{1},U),

where
\#U=K_{2}+1,U\subset V_{2}
and it is enough to consider the following case.

2 case.
Let
\#U_{1}=K_{1}\leq K
and
\#U_{2}=K_{2}\leq K
. If

\overline{h}(U_{1},U_{2}):=\dfrac{2\sum_{x\in U_{1},y\in U_{2}}b(x,y)}{(K_{1}+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})}

then the maximum of
\overline{h}(U_{1},U_{2})
for fixed
K_{1},K_{2}
is attained when all the edges between
U_{1}
and
U_{2}
have weight
1
(which is possible for
U_{1}\subset V_{1}
and
U_{2}\subset V_{2}
). This maximum is

\overline{h}(K_{1},K_{2})=\dfrac{2K_{1}K_{2}}{(K_{1}+K_{2})(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})}

and further, maximazing the ratio
{K_{1}K_{2}}/(K_{1}+K_{2})
gives

\max_{K_{1}\leq K,K_{2}\leq K}\overline{h}(K_{1},K_{2})=\dfrac{2K^{2}}{2K(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})}=\dfrac{K}{K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}},

where the maximum is attained on
K_{1}=K_{2}=K
.

From all the above, the dual Cheeger constant for the given graph is

\displaystyle\overline{h}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{K}{K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}}

\displaystyle=1-\left(\frac{K-1}{K}\right)\epsilon^{n}+\left(\frac{K-1}{K}\right)^{2}\epsilon^{2n}+\left(\frac{K-1}{K}\right)^{3}\epsilon^{3n}\dots,

where we have represented ratio as formal power series.

Therefore, taking
n
large enough we can get
|1-\overline{h}|\prec r
for any given
r\in\mathcal{R}
. It is an intuitively clear result, since such a graph is close to bipartite (all edges in the same part of partition have weight
\epsilon^{n}
, while all edges between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
have weight
1
).

The matrix of Laplacian in this case is

\frac{1}{B}\left(\begin{array}[]{ccccc|ccccc}B&-\epsilon^{n}&\dots&-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}&-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
-\epsilon^{n}&B&\dots&-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}&-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}&\dots&B&-\epsilon^{n}&-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}&\dots&-\epsilon^{n}&B&-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1\\
\hline\cr-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1&B&-\epsilon^{n}&\dots&-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}\\
-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1&-\epsilon^{n}&B&\dots&-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}\\
\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots&\dots\\
-1&-1&\dots&-1&-1&-\epsilon^{n}&-\epsilon^{n}&\dots&B&-\epsilon^{n}\\
\lx@intercol\hfil\underbrace{-1\;\;\;\;-1\;\;\;\dots\;\;\;-1\;\;-1}_{K}\hfil\lx@intercol\vrule\lx@intercol&\lx@intercol\hfil\underbrace{-\epsilon^{n}\;\;-\epsilon^{n}\;\;\dots\;\;\;-\epsilon^{n}\;\;\;\;B}_{K}\hfil\lx@intercol\\
\end{array}\right),

where
B=b(x)={K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}}
. Then the eigenvectors are:

•

v_{0}=(1,1,\dots,1,1)

with an eigenvalue
\lambda_{0}=0
;

•

v_{m}=(-1,0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0),
where
1
stays at the place
(m+~1)
for
m=1,\dots,K-1
with eigenvalues

\lambda_{m}=\dfrac{K+K\epsilon^{n}}{K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}};

•

v_{m}=(\underbrace{0,0,0\dots 0}_{K},-1,0,\dots,0,1,0,\dots,0),

where
1
stays at the place
(m+2)
for
m=K,\dots,N-2

with the same eigenvalues as in the previous case

\lambda_{m}=\dfrac{K+K\epsilon^{n}}{K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}};

•

v_{N-1}=({\underbrace{1,1,\dots,1,1}_{K}},{\underbrace{-1,-1,\dots,-1,-1}_{K}}),

with the eigenvalue

\lambda_{N-1}=\dfrac{2K}{K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n}}.

Note that
\lambda_{N-1}=2\overline{h}
and can be written as formal power series as

\displaystyle\lambda_{N-1}=2-2\frac{K-1}{K}\epsilon^{n}+2\left(\frac{K-1}{K}\right)^{2}\epsilon^{2n}-2\left(\frac{K-1}{K}\right)^{3}\epsilon^{3n}\dots.

From the other hand,

\displaystyle 1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}}

\displaystyle=1+\sqrt{\dfrac{K^{2}+2K(K-1)\epsilon^{n}}{(K+(K-1)\epsilon^{n})^{2}}}

\displaystyle=2-\frac{(K-1)^{2}}{2K^{2}}\epsilon^{2n}+\frac{(K-1)^{3}}{K^{3}}\epsilon^{3n}\dots.

Therefore, the estimate

\lambda_{N-1}\preceq 1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}}

is precise up to the order
\epsilon^{n}
for the considered graph and the difference

1+\sqrt{1-(1-\overline{h})^{2}}-\lambda_{N-1}

converge to
0
in a sence of convergence in order topology in
\mathcal{R}
if we consider a family of graphs with
n\to\infty
.

Given a configuration manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
, a smooth Lagrangian

L\colon\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
, and a (generally non-involutive) distribution

{\mathcal{D}}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
, the evolutions
q(t)
,
t\in[a,b]
in the

Lagrange d’Alembert model
are defined by the (fixed endpoint)
variational problem

\displaystyle dS\bigl(q(t)\bigr)\cdot\delta q(t)=0

for all
\displaystyle\delta q(t)\in{\mathcal{D}}
,
with the additional constraint
\displaystyle q^{\prime}(t)\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

(1)

where the action
S
is defined by (
q^{\prime}(t)
denotes the geometric derivative in

\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}
, i.e., including the base point)

S\bigl(q(t)\bigr)=\int_{a}^{b}L\bigl(q^{\prime}(t)\bigr)\,dt.

(2)

The model is called
holonomic
if
{\mathcal{D}}
is integrable, and otherwise it
is
nonholonomic
. The usual energy is conserved, although the usual
symmetry-associated momentum may not be. For further information on such
models, see

[
\citeauthoryearBates and SniatyckiBates and
Sniatycki1993
,
\citeauthoryearMarleMarle1995
,
\citeauthoryearMarleMarle1998
,
\citeauthoryearSniatyckiSniatycki1998
,
\citeauthoryearPatrickPatrick2007
]
, and the references therein.

Given natural regularity
conditions on
L
, the critical curved of the Lagrange d’Alembert model
correspond to (projections to
{\mathcal{Q}}
) of the integral curves of a vector
field
Y_{E}
on the phase space
{\mathcal{D}}
itself. If
{\mathcal{D}}=\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}
then the
differential equations defined by
Y_{E}
are the Euler-Lagrange equations, and
otherwise they are the Lagrange-d’Alembert equations. This note provides a
derivation of
Y_{E}
for a single rigid body rolling on a surface in Euclidean
space; see (
29
) and (
32
).

The derivation here uses the semi-symplectic formalism, where
\mathord{Y_{\mskip-2.0muE}}
is
determined by the system energy and a nondegenerate antisymmetric two from on
a
distribution
of the relevant phase space. If the body rolls on a
horizontal plane then the semi-symplectic system admits an
{\mathit{SE}(2)}
symmetry and
a semi-symplectic reduction shows that the well-known differential equations of
this planar system are semi-symplectic.

Lagrangian formalism

Assume a reference body with moments of inertia
I
and center of mass at the
origin of the reference frame. Let the surface of the body in the reference
frame be the 2-submanifold
{\mathcal{M}}
and let
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
be the
2-submanifold on which the body rolls. Configuration of the body may be
determined by elements
(A,a,s)\in{\mathit{SE}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}
, with interpretation that a
point
X
in the reference body is located at
AX+a
, and that the body
contacts the surface at
As+a
. Left translating,
\Omega=A^{-1}\dot{A}
and

v=A^{-1}\dot{a}
, and the Lagrangian on
T({\mathit{SE}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}})
is the left
invariant

L=\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{t}I\,\Omega+\frac{1}{2}m|v|^{2}-mga\cdot{\bm{{k}}}.

(3)

Establishing contact of the the body with the fixed surface means imposing
constraints. First, the (holonomic) constraint
As+a\in{\mathcal{H}}
imposes that the
contact point lies on the surface. Second, assuming
\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}
and
\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}
are
respectively smooth choices of unit normal for
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
(so both
surfaces are assumed orientable), the (holonomic) constraint

A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(As+a)
imposes that the surfaces do not infinitesimally
interpenetrate at the contact point (global body-surface interpenetration
issues are not considered here). Defining
x=As+a
, and replacing
a
, the
configuration space is

{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigl\{\mskip 1.0mu(A,s,x)\in{\mathit{SO}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\bigm|A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\mskip 1.0mu\bigr\}.

(4)

{\mathcal{Q}}
and
\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}
are inserted into
{\mathit{SE}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}
and
T({\mathit{SE}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}})

by the equations

a=x-As,\qquad v=A^{-1}\dot{a}=A^{-1}(\dot{x}-\dot{A}s-A\dot{s})=A^{-1}\dot{x}-\dot{s}-\Omega\times s,

(5)

and the Lagrangian is the pullback of (
3
) by these, i.e., the
result of substitution. It should be noted that the constraint

A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)
implies physical meaning to the choice of the normals
for
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{H}}
. For example, if
{\mathcal{H}}
is the plane
z=0
,
and
\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}=-{\bm{{k}}}
, then the choice of the
outward normal
for
{\mathcal{M}}

places the body
above the plane
, whereas the choice
\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}={\bm{{k}}}
places
it
below
.

Recall that the Weingarten map of
{\mathcal{M}}
is the vector bundle map (over the
identity)
\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}\colon\mathord{T\mskip-2.25mu{\mathcal{M}}}\rightarrow\mathord{T\mskip-2.25mu{\mathcal{M}}}
defined by

\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}\,\frac{ds}{dt}=-\frac{d}{dt}\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}\bigl(s(t)\bigr),

(6)

where
s(t)
is a smooth curve in
{\mathcal{M}}
. Similarly,
\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}
denotes the
Weingarten map of
{\mathcal{H}}
. If
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
then
x^{\wedge}
denotes the
3\times 3
matrix such that
x^{\wedge}y=x\times y
for all
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
.

Lemma 7

(The configuration space is a manifold).
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a
5
dimensional submanifold of
{\mathit{SO}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{H}}

with tangent bundle

\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}=\bigl\{\mskip 1.0mu\bigl(\,(A,s,x),\,(\Omega,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus},\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux})\,\bigr)\bigm|(A,s,x)\in{\mathcal{Q}},\;\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}-A^{-1}\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux}\mskip 1.0mu\bigr\},

(8)

and the projection
(A,s,x)\mapsto(s,x)
is a (trivial) principle

{\mathit{SO}(2)}
-bundle.

Proof.
The map
{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow S^{2}
defined by
(A,s,x)\mapsto A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)
has
derivative (use left translation on the factor
{\mathit{SO}(3)}
)

\bigl(\,(A,s,x),\,(\Omega,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus},\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux})\,\bigr)\mapsto\frac{d}{dt}\biggr|_{t=0}A\operatorname{exp}\bigl(t\Omega^{\wedge})\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s(t)\bigr)=A\bigl(\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)-\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}\bigr)

(9)

This is a submersion: take
\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}=0
and then

\Omega\mapsto\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)
is clearly onto the orthogonal
complement of
\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)
, i.e., onto
T_{s}S^{2}
. So,

(A,s,x)\mapsto A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)
and
(A,s,x)\mapsto\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)
are transversal, and
similarly differentiating the second of these,
{\mathcal{Q}}
is smooth with tangent
space at
(A,s,x)
the solutions
(\Omega,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus},\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux})
to

A\bigl(\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)-\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}\bigr)=-\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux},

(10)

and
\operatorname{dim}{\mathcal{Q}}=\operatorname{dim}({\mathit{SO}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{H}})-\operatorname{dim}S^{2}=(3+2+2)-2=5
. If
\theta\in{\mathbb{R}}
then

\theta\cdot(A,s,x)=(A\operatorname{exp}(-\theta\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)^{\wedge}),s,x)
defines a
right action of
{\mathit{SO}(2)}
which, for fixed
s
and
x
, is free and transitive on
the
A\in{\mathit{SO}(3)}
such that
A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)
; the assignment of the
identity of
{\mathit{SO}(3)}
to each
(s,x)
is a global section.
\blacksquare

The holonomic Lagrangian (
3
) is not regular because it does
not involve
s
— there is no interaction of the body and the surface. To
include that interaction, impose the rolling constraint that the point on the
body at
As+a
is instantaneously at rest, i.e.,

\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(A(t)s+a(t)\bigr)=\dot{A}s+\dot{a}=0.

(11)

Ideal rolling without slipping means zero velocity of the physical location of
the fixed point on the body (at
s
) in the (inertial) frame of the surface, so

s
is not differentiated here. Converting to the variable
x
,
\dot{a}=\dot{x}-\dot{A}s-A\dot{s}=-\dot{A}s
, and the rolling constraint becomes
\dot{x}-A\dot{s}=0
.

Summarizing: the nonholonomic system for a body with surface
{\mathcal{M}}
rolling on a surface
{\mathcal{H}}

is the lagrangian system

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigl\{\mskip 1.0mu(A,s,x)\in{\mathit{SO}(3)}\times{\mathcal{M}}\times{\mathcal{H}}\bigm|A\,\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{n_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\mskip 1.0mu\bigr\},\\
&\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}=\bigl\{\mskip 1.0mu\bigl(\,(A,s,x),\,(\Omega,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus},\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux})\,\bigr)\bigm|(A,s,x)\in{\mathcal{Q}},\;\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)=\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}-A^{-1}\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux}\mskip 1.0mu\bigr\},\\
&{\mathcal{D}}=\bigl\{\mskip 1.0mu\bigl(\,(A,s,x),\,(\Omega,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus},\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux})\,\bigr)\in\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}\bigm|\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.125mux}=A\,\mathord{\delta\mskip-0.5mus}\mskip 1.0mu\bigr\},\\
&L=\frac{1}{2}\Omega^{t}I\,\Omega+\frac{1}{2}m|v|^{2}-mga\cdot{\bm{{k}}},\quad a=x-As,\quad v=A^{-1}\,\dot{x}-\Omega\times s-\dot{s}.\end{split}

(12)

Semi-symplectic derivation of the vector field

Lagrange-d’Alembert models have an equivalent semi-symplectic
formalism (
[
\citeauthoryearBates and SniatyckiBates and
Sniatycki1993
,
\citeauthoryearSniatyckiSniatycki1998
,
\citeauthoryearPatrickPatrick2007
]
): Given
{\mathcal{Q}}
and
{\mathcal{D}}
,
a lagrangian
L\colon{\mathcal{Q}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
is called
{\mathcal{D}}
-regular if its
second fiber derivative is nonsingular when restricted to
{\mathcal{D}}
. The
distribution
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}\equiv\mathord{T\mskip 0.5mu{\mathcal{D}}}\cap(\mathord{T\mskip-0.125mu\mathord{\tau_{\mskip-1.75mu{\mathcal{Q}}}}})^{-1}{\mathcal{D}}
, where

\mathord{\tau_{\mskip-1.75mu{\mathcal{Q}}}}\colon\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Q}}
is the projection, has fiber dimension
twice that of
{\mathcal{D}}
, and
\mathord{\omega_{\mskip-0.75muL}}
is nonsingular on
{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}
if and only
if
L
is
{\mathcal{D}}
-regular, in which case

\displaystyle(i_{\mathord{Y_{\mskip-2.0muE}}}\mathord{\omega_{\mskip-0.75muL}}-dE)\bigl|{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{D}}=0
,
where
\displaystyle\mathord{Y_{\mskip-2.0muE}}({\mathcal{Q}})\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}_{D}
,

(13)

defines a vector field
\mathord{Y_{\mskip-2.0muE}}
with integral curves exactly the solutions of the
Lagrange-d’Alembert variational principle. In general one is led to a category
defined by a nondegenerate antisymetric two form with domain a (generally
nonintegrable) distribution. The semi-symplectic formulation is advantageous
because it has this formula for the evolution vector field — the
Lagrange-d’Alembert equations have already been geometrically determined
as (
13
).

Since
L
is fiberwise bilinear, regularity is equivalent to
\Omega=0
,

\dot{s}=0
, and
\dot{x}=0
whenever
\Omega^{t}I\,\Omega+m|v|^{2}=0
. Assuming
I

is positive definite and
m>0
, the latter is equivalent to
\Omega=0

and
v=0
, i.e.,
\dot{x}=A\dot{s}
(within
\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}
). Restricting to
\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}

leads to
\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)\dot{s}-A^{-1}\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\,A\dot{s}=0
, so
L
is
{\mathcal{D}}
-regular
if and only

\Lambda_{A,s,x}\colon\mathord{T_{\mskip-1.5mus}\mskip-0.25mu{\mathcal{M}}}\to\mathord{T_{\mskip-1.5mus}\mskip-0.25mu{\mathcal{M}}},\qquad\Lambda_{A,s,x}\equiv\mathord{L_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)-A^{-1}\mathord{L_{\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{H}}}}(x)\,A

(14)

is fiberwise nonsingular for all
(A,s,x)\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Another advantage of the semi-symplectic formalism is an early clear emphasis
and identification of the relevant phase space
{\mathcal{D}}
, which by
Lemma
9
is the subset of
\mathord{T\mskip-1.0mu{\mathcal{Q}}}
satisfying

\dot{x}=A\dot{s}
and
\Lambda_{A,s,x}\dot{s}=\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)
, and which,
if
L
is regular, may be identified with
{\mathcal{P}}={\mathcal{Q}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
by

(A,s,x,\Omega)\leftrightarrow\bigl(\,(A,s,x),\,(\Omega,\Lambda_{A,s,x}^{-1}(\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s)),A\Lambda_{A,s,x}^{-1}(\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}}(s))\,\bigr).

(15)

So from the outset one seeks differential equations for
dA/dt
,
ds/dt
,

dx/dt
, and
d\Omega/dt
, which is not entirely obvious apriori because from
the variational principle one might have anticipated second order differential
equations for
s
or

. Since every evolution has derivative in
{\mathcal{D}}
and is
second order, three of the required differential equations are known:

\frac{dA}{dt}=A^{-1}\Omega,\qquad\Lambda_{A,s,x}\frac{ds}{dt}=\Omega\times\mathord{n_{\mskip-3.0mu{\mathcal{M}}}},\qquad\frac{dx}{dt}=A\,\frac{ds}{dt}.

(16)

Only the differential equation for
d\Omega/dt
need be determined.

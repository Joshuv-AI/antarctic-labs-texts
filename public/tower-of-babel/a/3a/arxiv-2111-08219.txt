Exhaustion of hyperbolic complex manifolds and relations to the squeezing function
Ninh Van Thu, Trinh Huy Vu and Nguyen Quang Dieu 1,2

Abstract
. The purpose of this article is twofold. The first aim is to characterize an n n -dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold M M exhausted by a sequence { Ω j } \{\Omega_{j}\} of domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} via an exhausting sequence { f j : Ω j → M } \{f_{j}\colon\Omega_{j}\to M\} such that f j − 1 ​ ( a ) f_{j}^{-1}(a) converges to a boundary point ξ 0 ∈ ∂ Ω \xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega for some point a ∈ M a\in M . Then, our second aim is to show that any spherically extreme boundary point must be strongly pseudoconvex.

1.
introduction

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold. Let
\{M_{j}\}
and
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be two sequences of open subsets in
M
and
\mathbb{C}^{n}
respectively. Suppose that
M

can be exhausted by

\{\Omega_{j}\}
via an exhausting sequence
\{f_{j}\colon\Omega_{j}\to M_{j}\subset M\}
in the sense that
f_{j}
is a biholomorphism from
\Omega_{j}
onto
M_{j}
for all
j\geq 1
and
\displaystyle\cup_{j=1}^{\infty}M_{j}=M
. Then it is a natural problem is to describe
M
in terms of
\{\Omega_{j}\}
. In the case that
\Omega_{j}=\Omega
for all
j\geq 1
, this problem is called the
union problem
(cf.
[
BBMV21
,
FSi81
]
). In
1977
, J. E. Fornæss and L. Stout
[
FS77
]
proved that if
\Omega
is the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|z|<1\}
, then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
. More generally,
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
\Omega
if it is a homogeneous bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For further results about the union problem the reader may also consult the references
[
FSi81
,
FM95
,
Liu18
,
NT21
,
BBMV21
]
.

Now let us fix a point
a\in M
and assume that
\lim\Omega_{j}=\Omega
(see Definition
2.1
for the notion of this limit). Then, we consider the behavior of the sequence
\{f_{j}^{-1}(a)\}\subset\Omega
. In the case when
\{f_{j}^{-1}(a)\}
converges to a point
p\in\Omega
,
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
\Omega
(cf. Corollary
2.4
in Section
2
). Therefore, we especially pay attention to the case that
\{f_{j}^{-1}(a)\}
converges to a boundary point
\xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega
.

In the first part of this paper, we give a characterization of our manifold
M
in term of the behavior of the orbit
\{f_{j}^{-1}(a)\}
. To do this, let us fix positive integers
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n-1}
and let
P(z^{\prime})
be a
(1/m_{1},\ldots,1/m_{n-1})
-homogeneous polynomial given by

P(z^{\prime})=\sum_{wt(K)=wt(L)=1/2}a_{KL}{z^{\prime}}^{K}\bar{z}^{\prime L},

where
a_{KL}\in\mathbb{C}
with
a_{KL}=\bar{a}_{LK}
, satisfying that
P(z^{\prime})>0
whenever
z^{\prime}\neq 0
. Here and in what follows,
z^{\prime}:=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n-1})
and
wt(K):=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{k_{j}}{2m_{j}}
denotes the weight of any multi-index
K=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})\in\mathbb{N}^{n-1}
with respect to
\Lambda:=(1/m_{1},\ldots,1/m_{n-1})
. Then the general ellipsoid
D_{P}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\;(n\geq 2)
, defined in
[
NNTK19
]
by

\begin{split}D_{P}&:=\{(z^{\prime},z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|z_{n}|^{2}+P(z^{\prime})<1\}.\end{split}

We emphasize here that

(1)

P(a^{1/m_{1}}z_{1},a^{1/m_{2}}z_{2},\ldots,a^{1/m_{n-1}}z_{n-1})=P(z^{\prime}),\;\forall z^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}^{n-1},\forall a\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

Hence, the automorphism group of
D_{P}
(denoted by
\mathrm{Aut}(D_{P})
) contains the automorphisms
\phi_{a,\theta}
, defined by

(z^{\prime},z_{n})\mapsto\left(\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2m_{1}}}{(1-\bar{a}z_{n})^{1/m_{1}}}z_{1},\ldots,\frac{(1-|a|^{2})^{1/2m_{n-1}}}{(1-\bar{a}z_{n})^{1/m_{n-1}}}z_{n-1},e^{i\theta}\frac{z_{n}-a}{1-\bar{a}z_{n}}\right),

where
a\in\Delta:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon|z|<1\}
and
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
(see Lemma
3.1
in Section
2
).

Throughout this paper, we assume that the domain
D_{P}
is a WB-domain, i.e.,
D_{P}
is strongly pseudoconvex at every boundary point outside the set
\{(0^{\prime},e^{i\theta})\colon\theta\in\mathbb{R}\}
(cf.
[
AGK16
]
).

For any
s,r\in(0,1]
, inspired by
[
Liu18
, Lemma
2.5
]
let us define
D^{s}_{P},D^{s,r}_{P}
, and
D_{P,r}
respectively by

\begin{split}D^{s}_{P}&:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|z_{n}-b|^{2}+sP(z^{\prime})<s^{2}\};\\
D^{s,r}_{P}&:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|z_{n}-b|^{2}+\dfrac{s}{r}P(z^{\prime})<s^{2}\};\\
D_{P,r}:=D_{P/r}&=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon|z_{n}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{r}P(z^{\prime})<1\Big\},\end{split}

where
s=1-b
. We note that
D_{P}^{s,r}\subset D_{P}^{s}\subset D_{P}
(see Remark
3.1
),
D_{P}^{s,1}=D_{P}^{s}
, and the property that
\lim\psi_{j}^{-1}(D^{s}_{P})=D_{P}
for a certain family
\{\psi_{j}\}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(D_{P})
(cf. Lemma
3.2
in Section
3
) plays a key role in the proofs of our main theorems below.

In what follows, let us denote by
\Delta
the unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
and for a sequence
\{a_{j}\}\subset\Delta
converging to
1\in\partial\Delta
we always denote by
x_{j}:=1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})
and
y_{j}:=\mathrm{Im}(a_{j})
for
j\geq 1
. To state our first result, we need the following definition (see Section
3
for its application).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that the sequence
\{a_{j}\}\subset\Delta
converges
parabolically nontangentially
to
1
in
\Delta
if
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{y_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}}=0
.

Our first main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold and let
\Omega
be a pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be a sequence of subdomains of
D_{P}
such that
D^{s}_{P}\subset\Omega_{j}\subset D_{P},j\geq 1
. Suppose also that
M
can be exhausted by
\{\Omega_{j}\}
via an exhausting sequence
\{f_{j}\colon D_{P}\supset\Omega_{j}\to M_{j}\subset M\}
. If there exist a point
a\in M
and a number
r\in(0,1)
such that the sequence
D^{s,r}_{P}\ni\eta_{j}=(\eta_{j},\eta_{jn}):=f_{j}^{-1}(a)
converges to
(0^{\prime},1)
in
D_{P}
and
\{\eta_{jn}\}\subset\Delta
converges parabolically nontangentially to
1\in\partial\Delta
, then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
D_{P}
.

Remark 1.1
.

Notice that the point
p=(0^{\prime},1)
is
(P,s)
-extreme for each domain
\Omega_{j}
(cf.
[
NNC21
, Definition
1.1
]
for the notion of
(P,s)
-extreme points) and the convergence of a sequence of points in
D_{P}^{s,r}
to
p
, with its
n
-th component converging nontangentially to
1\in\partial\Delta
, is exactly the
\Lambda
-nontangential convergence introduced in
[
NN19
, Definition
3.4
]
. Therefore, in this situation
[
NT21
, Theorem
1.1
]
yields Theorem
1.1
for the case that
\Omega_{j}=D_{P}
for all
j\geq 1
. However, our proof here is quite different and more simple. In particular, we do not need to assume the convergence of
\Omega_{j}
to
D_{P}
and the proof shows that
D_{P}
may not be a WB-domain.

Remark 1.2
.

The parabolically nontangential convergence requirement appears necessary, because without this condition our manifold
M
may not be biholomorphically equivalent to
D_{P}
(cf. Remark
3.4
).

Now we move to the definition of
\Lambda
-tangential convergence.

Definition 1.2
.

We say that
\{\eta_{j}\}\subset D_{P}\cap U
converges
\Lambda
-tangentially
to
p=(0^{\prime},1)
if for any
0<r<1
there exists
j_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\eta_{j}\not\in D_{P,r}
for all
j\geq j_{r}
.

For the case that
\{\eta_{j}\}\subset\Omega\cap U
converges
\Lambda
-tangentially to
p=(0^{\prime},1)
, we do not know whether
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
D_{P}
. However, the following theorem shows that
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
provided that all
\partial\Omega_{j}
share a small neighborhood of the point
(0^{\prime},1)
with
\partial D_{P}
to which the sequence of points converges
\Lambda
-tangentially. More precisely, our second main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold. Let
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be a sequence of subdomains of
D_{P}
such that
\Omega_{j}\cap U=D_{P}\cap U
,
j\geq 1
, for a fixed neighborhood
U
of
(0^{\prime},1)
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose also that
M
can be exhausted by
\{\Omega_{j}\}
via an exhausting sequence
\{f_{j}\colon D_{P}\supset\Omega_{j}\to M_{j}\subset M\}
. If there exists a point
a\in M
such that the sequence
\eta_{j}:=f_{j}^{-1}(a)
converges
\Lambda
-tangentially to
(0^{\prime},1)
in
D_{P}
, then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
may be summarized roughly in the following dichotomy: if the convergence of
f_{j}^{-1}(a)
to
p
is
\Lambda
-nontangential (see page 2) then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to
D_{P}
, otherwise if the convergence is
\Lambda
-tangential then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to the ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Remark 1.4
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.2
, we shall show without loss of generality that the sequence
\{\psi_{j}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\}
converges nontangentially to some boundary point
p\in\partial D_{P}\cap\{z_{n}=0\}
. Thanks to the fact that
D_{P}
is a WB-domain, that point is strongly pseudoconvex and hence the proof easily follows from Theorem
3.6
. However, if
D_{P}
is not a WB-domain, i.e. the point
p
may not be strongly pseudoconvex, but it is
h
-extendible in the sense of J. Yu (cf.
[
Yu95
]
), then
M
would be biholomorphically equivalent to some model by Theorem
1.1
in
[
NT21
]
.

Let
\Omega
be a pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose that
\xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is a boundary orbit accumulation point, i.e. there exists a sequence
\{\varphi_{j}\}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(\Omega)
such that
\eta_{j}:=\varphi_{j}(a)
converges to
\xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega
for some point
a\in\Omega
. Here and in what follows,
\mathrm{Aut}(\Omega)
denotes the automorphism group of
\Omega
. Then, one notices that our domain
\Omega
is exhausted by itself via the sequence
\varphi_{j}\colon\Omega\to\Omega
. In addition, as an application of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we show that if
\Omega
is a subdomain of
D_{P}
with
\Omega\cap U=D_{P}\cap U
for a fixed neighborhood
U
of
(0^{\prime},1)
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then
\Omega
must be biholomorphically equivalent to either
D_{P}
or
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(cf. Corollary
3.7
in Section
3
).

Now we move to the second part of this paper. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
and
q\in\Omega
. For a holomorphic embedding
f\colon\Omega\to\mathbb{B}^{n}:=\mathbb{B}(0;1)
with
f(q)=0
, one sets

\sigma_{\Omega,f}(q):=\sup\left\{r>0\colon B(0;r)\subset f(\Omega)\right\},

where
\mathbb{B}^{n}(z;r)\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
denotes the ball of radius
r
with center at
z
. Then the
squeezing function

\sigma_{\Omega}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is defined in
[
DGZ12
]
as

\sigma_{\Omega}(q):=\sup_{f}\left\{\sigma_{\Omega,f}(q)\right\}.

Note that
0<\sigma_{\Omega}(z)\leq 1
for any
z\in\Omega
and it is obvious that the squeezing function is invariant under biholomorphisms.

Now we recall the definition of spherically extreme boundary points (cf.
[
KZ16
]
). Indeed, a boundary point
p\in\partial\Omega
is said to be locally spherically extreme if there exist a neighborhood
U
of
p
and a ball
\mathbb{B}(c(p);R)
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
of some radius
R
, center at some point
c(p)
such that
\partial\Omega\cap U
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth,
\Omega\cap U\subset\mathbb{B}(c(p);R)
, and
p\in\partial\Omega\cap\partial\mathbb{B}(c(p);R)
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold and let
\Omega
be a pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose that
\partial\Omega
admits a spherically extreme boundary point
\xi_{0}
in a neighborhood of which the boundary
\partial\Omega
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth. In addition, let
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be a subdomains of
\Omega
such that
\Omega_{j}\cap U=\Omega\cap U
,
j\geq 1
, for some neighborhood
U
of
\xi_{0}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Suppose also that
M
can be exhausted by
\{\Omega_{j}\}
via an exhausting sequence
\{f_{j}:\Omega\supset\Omega_{j}\to M_{j}\subset M\}
. If there exists a point
a\in M
such that the sequence
\eta_{j}:=f_{j}^{-1}(a)
converges to
\xi_{0}
, then
M
is biholomorphically equivalent to the unit ball
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

By using the scaling method, K.-T. Kim and L. Zhang
[
KZ16
, Theorem
3.1
]
proved that if a domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
admits a locally spherically extreme boundary point
p
, then

\lim\limits_{\Omega\cap U\ni q\to p}\sigma_{\Omega\cap U}(q)=1,

where
U
is a small neighborhood of
p
. Of course, we may not have that
\lim\limits_{\Omega\ni q\to p}\sigma_{\Omega}(q)=1
(see
[
FN21
, Theorem
1
]
). It is known that every strongly convex boundary point is locally spherically extreme. Conversely, we will show in the last section
(Proposition
4.1
) that every locally spherically extreme point is actually strongly pseudoconvex.

As an application of Proposition
4.1
, in the union problem, the Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold
M
must be biholomorphically equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
provided that
\{f_{j}^{-1}(a)\}
converges to a spherically extreme boundary point
\xi_{0}\in\partial\Omega
(see Theorem
3.6
and the proof of Theorem
1.3
in Sections
3
and
4
respectively for more details). More generally, if
\lim\limits_{z\to\xi_{0}}\sigma_{\Omega}(z)=1
, then
M
is also biholomorphically equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
(cf. Corollary
4.3
).

and Theorem

in Section

2.
The normality

First of all, we recall the following definition (see
[
GK87
]
or
[
DN09
]
).

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of open sets in a complex manifold
M
and
\Omega_{0}
be an open set of
M
. The sequence
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is said to converge to
\Omega_{0}
(written
\lim\Omega_{i}=\Omega_{0}
) if and only if

(i)

For any compact set
K\subset\Omega_{0},
there is an
i_{0}=i_{0}(K)
such that
i\geq i_{0}
implies that
K\subset\Omega_{i}
; and

(ii)

If
K
is a compact set which is contained in
\Omega_{i}
for all sufficiently large
i,
then
K\subset\Omega_{0}
.

Next, we recall the following proposition, which is a generalization of the theorem of H. Cartan (see
[
DN09
,
GK87
,
DT04
]
).

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\{A_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
and
\{\Omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be sequences of domains in complex manifolds
M
and
N
respectively with
\dim M=\dim N
,
\lim A_{i}=A_{0}
, and
\lim\Omega_{i}=\Omega_{0}
for some (uniquely determined) domains
A_{0}
in
M
and
\Omega_{0}
in
N
. Suppose that
\{f_{i}:A_{i}\to\Omega_{i}\}
is a sequence of biholomorphic maps. Suppose also that the sequence
\{f_{i}:A_{i}\to N\}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
A_{0}
to a holomorphic map
F:A_{0}\to N
and the sequence
\{g_{i}:=f^{-1}_{i}:\Omega_{i}\to M\}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
\Omega_{0}
to a holomorphic map
G:\Omega_{0}\to M
. Then, one of the following assertions holds:

(i)

The sequence
\{f_{i}\}
is compactly divergent, i.e., for each compact set
K\subset A_{0}
and each compact set
L\subset\Omega_{0}
, there exists an integer
i_{0}
such that
f_{i}(K)\cap L=\emptyset
for
i\geq i_{0}
; or

(ii)

There exists a subsequence
\{f_{i_{j}}\}\subset\{f_{i}\}
such that the sequence
\{f_{i_{j}}\}
converges uniformly on compact subsets of
A_{0}
to a biholomorphic map
F:A_{0}\to\Omega_{0}
.

Remark 2.1
.

In
[
DN09
]
, Do Duc Thai and the first author proved the above proposition for the case that
M=N
, but the same proof can give the result in the above proposition.

Now let us recall the definition of the Kobayashi infinitesimal pseudometric. For a point
p,q
be two points in a complex manifold
M
and let
X\in T_{p}M
, the
Kobayashi infinitesimal pseudometric

F_{M}(p,X)
is defined by

F_{M}(p,X)=\inf\{\alpha>0~|~\exists g\in\mathrm{Hol}(\Delta,\Omega),g(0)=p,g^{\prime}(0)=X/\alpha\}.

The
Kobayashi pseudodistance

d^{K}_{M}(p,q)
is defined by

d^{K}_{M}(p,q)=\inf\int_{a}^{b}F_{M}(\gamma(t),\gamma^{\prime}(t))dt,

where the infimum is taken over all differentiable curves
\gamma:[a,b]\to M
such that
\gamma(a)=p
and
\gamma(b)=q
. A complex manifold
M
is called Kobayashi hyperbolic if
d^{K}_{M}(p,q)\neq 0
whenever
p\neq q
.

The following proposition is inspired by Theorem
3.2
in
[
FSi81
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X,Y
be two complex manifolds of dimension
n.

Let
\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of domains in
X
that converges to a domain
M

and
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be a sequence of domains in
Y
that converges to a taut domain
\Omega
.
Let
\{f_{j}:M_{j}\to\Omega_{j}\}
be a normal sequence of biholomorphisms.
Suppose that there exists a point
a\in M
satisfying the following conditions:

(i)

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}f_{j}(a)=b\in\Omega
;

(ii)

\varlimsup\limits_{j\to\infty}F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)>0,\ \forall\xi\neq 0
.

Then,
\{f_{j}\}
contains a subsequence that converges uniformly on compacta to a biholomorphic map
f:M\to\Omega
.

Remark 2.2
.

The condition
\mathrm{(ii)}
always holds for the case that
X
is contained in a Kobayashi hyperbolic domain in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

We claim no originality for the following fact which is a slight extension of Hurwitz’s theorem.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X,Y
be complex manifolds of complex dimension
n
and
let
\{f_{j}\}
be a sequence of injective holomorphic maps from
X
to
Y.
Suppose that
\{f_{j}\}
converges locally uniformly to a
non-constant holomorphic map
f:X\to Y.
Then,
f
is also injective on
X
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exist two distinct points
z,w\in X
such that
f(z)=f(w)=\lambda\in Y
.
Then we choose disjoint neighbourhoods
U,V
of
z
and
w,
respectively, such that each of them is biholomorphic to the unit ball in
\mathbb{C}^{n}.
By Hurwitz’s theorem there exists
n_{0}
such that
\lambda\in f_{n_{0}}(U)\cap f_{n_{0}}(V)
. This is a contradiction to injectivity of
f_{n_{0}}.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Since
\{f_{j}\}
is normal and
f_{j}(a)\to b\in\Omega
as
j\to\infty
, without loss of generality we may assume that
f_{j}
converges uniformly on compacta to a holomorphic map
f\colon M\to\overline{\Omega}
such that
f(a)=b
.
By the invariance property of the infinitesimal Kobayshi metric, we obtain

F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)=F_{\Omega_{j}}(f_{j}(a),f_{j}^{\prime}(a)\xi),\;\forall j\geq 1,\,\forall\xi\in T_{a}^{\mathbb{C}}(M).

Since
\Omega
is taut, by letting
j\to\infty
while applying Lemma
3.1
in
[
NT21
]
we obtain for each
\xi\neq 0
that

0<\varlimsup\limits_{j\to\infty}F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)=F_{\Omega}(b,f^{\prime}(a)\xi).

It implies that
f^{\prime}
is invertible at
a
, and hence at every point of
M
by Hurwitz’s theorem. Therefore, by the inverse function theorem,
f
is an open map on
M
, and so
f(M)\subset\Omega
.
By Lemma
2.3

f
is indeed one to one entirely on
M
.

Finally, because of the biholomorphism from
M
to
f(M)\subset\Omega
and the tautness of
\Omega
, it follows that the sequence
f_{j}^{-1}:\Omega_{j}\to M_{j}\subset M
is normal. Moreover, since
f_{j}(a)\to b\in\Omega
, it yields the sequences
f_{j}
and
f_{j}^{-1}
are not compactly divergent. Therefore, by Proposition
2.1
, after passing to a subsequence,
f_{j}
converges uniformly on compacta to a biholomorphic map
f:M\to\Omega
.

∎

By Proposition
2.2
, we obtain the following corollary, which is a generalization of
[
Fr83
, Lemma
1.1
]
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\{M_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of domains in an
n
-dimensional Kobayashi hyperbolic complex manifold
M
such that
\lim M_{j}=M
and
\{\Omega_{j}\}
be a sequence of domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
converging to a taut domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Let
\{f_{j}:M_{j}\to\Omega_{j}\}
be a normal sequence of biholomorphisms. If there exists a point
a\in M
such that
f_{j}(a)
converges to a point
b\in\Omega
, then
\{f_{j}\}
contains a subsequence that converges uniformly on compacta to a biholomorphic map
f:M\to\Omega
.

Proof.

We first prove that
F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)\to F_{M}(a,\xi)
for all
a\in M
and for all and
\xi\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a}(M)
. Indeed, by the decreasing property of the infinitesimal Kobayashi distance we see that the sequence

\{F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)\}_{j\geq 1}
is decreasing and bounded from below by
F_{M}(a,\xi).

On the other hand, for each
j\geq 1
, let
f_{j}:\Delta\to M
be a holomorphic map with

f_{j}(0)=a,f_{j}^{\prime}(0)=\lambda\xi,\frac{1}{\lambda}\leq F_{M}(a,\xi)+\frac{1}{j}.

Notice that
f_{j}((1-\frac{1}{j})\Delta)
is relatively compact in
M
for any
j\geq 1
, and thus it is included in some
M_{k(j)}
.
By considering the map
\tilde{f}_{j}:\Delta\to M_{k(j)}
defined by

\tilde{f}_{j}(z):=f_{j}\Big((1-\frac{1}{j})z\Big),\ z\in\Delta,

we obtain

F_{M_{k(j)}}(a,\xi)\leq\frac{1}{\lambda\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}\Big)},\;j\geq 1.

Therefore, one has that

\Big(1-\frac{1}{j}\Big)F_{M_{k(j)}}(a,\xi)\leq F_{M}(a,\xi)+\frac{1}{j},\;j\geq 1.

By letting
j\to\infty
, we get

\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)\leq F_{M}(a,\xi).

Hence,
\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}F_{M_{j}}(a,\xi)=F_{M}(a,\xi)
for all
a\in M
and
\xi\in T^{\mathbb{C}}_{a}(M)
, as desired.
The proof now follows from Proposition
2.2
.
∎

Lemma 3.1

(see

Let

Next, we need the following lemma which is a generalization of

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\{\psi_{j}\}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(D_{P})
be a sequence of automorphisms

Remark 3.1
.

We have that

. Indeed, let

be arbitrary. Then, one sees that

|z_{n}-1|^{2}+2s\mathrm{Re}(z_{n}-1)+sP(z^{\prime})<0,

or equivalently

\frac{1}{s}\ |z_{n}-1|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}(z_{n}-1)+P(z^{\prime})<0.

Since

|z_{n}-1|^{2}+2s\mathrm{Re}(z_{n}-1)+sP(z^{\prime})\leq\frac{1}{s}\ |z_{n}-1|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}(z_{n}-1)+P(z^{\prime})<0,

which implies that

.

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
Liu18
, Lemma
2.5
]
, B. Liu consider the case that

and

Now, in order to prove Lemma
3.2
, one needs the following lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}
be a sequence in
\Delta
such that
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{\left(\mathrm{Im}(a_{j})\right)^{2}}{1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})}=\alpha\in[0,2)
and
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}a_{j}=1
. Then we have

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2-\alpha};

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{(1-\bar{a}_{j})^{2}}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}=\frac{-\alpha}{2-\alpha}.

(iii)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{|1-a_{j}|^{2}}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}=\frac{\alpha}{2-\alpha}.

Proof.

We have
x_{j}\to 0^{+}
,
y_{j}\to 0
, and
y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}\to\alpha
as
j\to\infty
, where
x_{j}:=1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}),y_{j}:=\mathrm{Im}(a_{j})
as mentioned in the introduction. Moreover, a computation shows that

\displaystyle\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{j}}{1-(1-x_{j})^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{x_{j}}{2x_{j}-x_{j}^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{1}{2-x_{j}-y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}};

\displaystyle\frac{(1-\bar{a}_{j})^{2}}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(x_{j}+iy_{j})^{2}}{1-(1-x_{j})^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{x_{j}^{2}-y_{j}^{2}+2ix_{j}y_{j}}{2x_{j}-x_{j}^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{x_{j}-y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}+2iy_{j}}{2-x_{j}-y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}};

\displaystyle\frac{|1-a_{j}|^{2}}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{1-(1-x_{j})^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}}{2x_{j}-x_{j}^{2}-y_{j}^{2}}=\frac{x_{j}+y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}}{2-x_{j}-y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}},\;\forall j\geq 1.

Therefore, the assertions follow since
x_{j}\to 0^{+}
and
y_{j}^{2}/x_{j}\to\alpha
as
j\to\infty
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}\subset\Delta
be a sequence converging to
1\in\partial\Delta
, i.e.
x_{j}\to 1^{+},y_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
. Moreover,
\displaystyle\frac{y_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}}<2-x_{j}
as
a_{j}\in\Delta
for every

. Therefore, if the limit of
\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{y_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}}
exists, then its limit, say
\alpha
, is in
[0,2]
. One sees that our sequence
\{a_{j}\}
converges “very tangentially” to
1
in
\Delta
for the case
\alpha=2
.

We note that Definition
1.1
deals with the case that
\alpha=0
. In this situation, by Lemma
3.3
we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\{a_{j}\}\subset\Delta
be a sequence converging parabolically nontangentially to
1\in\partial\Delta
. Then we have

(i)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}=\frac{1}{2};

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{|1-a_{j}|^{2}}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}=0.

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Let us recall that
b=1-s
or
s=1-b\in(0,1)
.
Then using the property (
1
), a computation shows that

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt\left|\dfrac{z_{n}+a_{j}}{1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}}-b\right|^{2}+sP\left(\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{1/2m_{1}}}{(1-\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})^{1/m_{1}}}z_{1},\ldots,\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{1/2m_{n-1}}}{(1-\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})^{1/m_{n-1}}}z_{n-1}\right)<s^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left|\dfrac{z_{n}+a_{j}}{1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}}-b\right|^{2}+s\dfrac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}|^{2}}P(z^{\prime})<s^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left|\dfrac{z_{n}+a_{j}-b(1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})}{1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}}\right|^{2}+s\dfrac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}{|1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}|^{2}}P(z^{\prime})<s^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left|z_{n}+a_{j}-b(1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})\right|^{2}+s(1-|a_{j}|^{2})P(z^{\prime})<s^{2}|1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left|z_{n}(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)+a_{j}-b\right|^{2}+s(1-|a_{j}|^{2})P(z^{\prime})<s^{2}|1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow|z_{n}|^{2}|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}\left[(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)z_{n}\right]+|a_{j}-b|^{2}+(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})P(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt<s^{2}\left(|a_{j}|^{2}|z_{n}|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}[\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}]+1\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow|z_{n}|^{2}\left(|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}\right)+2\mathrm{Re}\left[\left((\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}\right)z_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})P(z^{\prime})<(1-b)^{2}-|a_{j}-b|^{2}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow|z_{n}|^{2}+2\mathrm{Re}\left[\dfrac{(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}z_{n}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt+\dfrac{(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}P(z^{\prime})<\dfrac{(1-b)^{2}-|a_{j}-b|^{2}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\left|z_{n}+\dfrac{(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}\right|^{2}+\dfrac{(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}P(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt<\dfrac{(1-b)^{2}-|a_{j}-b|^{2}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}+\left|\dfrac{(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}\right|^{2}.

Moreover, by a straightforward calculation, one has that

\displaystyle(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}

\displaystyle=\bar{a}_{j}-b-\bar{a}_{j}^{2}b+\bar{a}_{j}b^{2}-\bar{a}_{j}+2\bar{a}_{j}b-\bar{a}_{j}b^{2}=-b(1-\bar{a}_{j})^{2};

\displaystyle(1-b)^{2}-|a_{j}-b|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-2b+b^{2}-|a_{j}|^{2}+2b\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})-b^{2}

\displaystyle=1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}));

\displaystyle|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-2\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}b)+|a_{j}|^{2}b^{2}-|a_{j}|^{2}+2b|a_{j}|^{2}-b^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b\left(\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b\left(\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})-1+1-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\left[1-2b\Big(1-\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}\Big)\right].

Hence, Corollary
3.4
implies that

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{(\bar{a}_{j}-b)(1-\bar{a}_{j}b)-(1-b)^{2}\bar{a}_{j}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{-b(1-\bar{a}_{j})^{2}/(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{1-2b\left[1-(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}))/(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right]}=0;

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{(1-b)(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b\left(\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})-1+1-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{1-b}{1-2b\left[1-(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}))/(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right]}=1;

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{(1-b)^{2}-|a_{j}-b|^{2}}{|1-\bar{a}_{j}b|^{2}-(1-b)^{2}|a_{j}|^{2}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}))}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}-2b\left(\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})-1+1-|a_{j}|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\dfrac{1-2b(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}))/(1-|a_{j}|^{2})}{1-2b\left[1-(1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}))/(1-|a_{j}|^{2})\right]}=1.

This yields
\psi_{j}^{-1}(D_{P}^{s})\to D_{P}
as
j\to\infty
.

Lemma 3.5

Let

as

such that

|\eta_{jn}|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{r_{j}}P(\eta_{j}^{\prime})\geq 1,\;\forall j\geq 1,

or equivalently

P(\eta_{j}^{\prime})\geq r_{j}(1-|a_{j}|^{2}),\;\forall j\geq 1.

This implies that

\begin{split}1>P(b_{j}^{\prime})&=\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{j}}P(\eta_{j}^{\prime})=\dfrac{1}{1-|a_{j}|^{2}}P(\eta_{j}^{\prime})\geq r_{j}\end{split}

for all

. Therefore, we obtain that

as
j\to\infty
, and hence by passing to a subsequence if necessary, we may assume that
\psi_{j}^{-1}(\eta_{j})
converges to some strongly pseudoconvex boundary point

(see Figure

below). Thus, by Theorem
3.6
we conclude that
\psi_{j}^{-1}\circ f_{j}^{-1}
converges uniformly on compacta to a biholomorphic map
F\colon M\to\mathbb{B}^{n}
, and thus the proof is complete.

Corollary 3.7
.

Let
\Omega

D_{P}
and
\Omega\cap U=D_{P}\cap U
for a fixed neighborhood

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

For simplicity, let us denote by

and by
x_{j}:=1-\mathrm{Re}(a_{j})>0,y_{j}:=\mathrm{Im}(a_{j})
for

\displaystyle\psi_{j}(z)=\left(\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{1/2m_{1}}}{(1-\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})^{1/m_{1}}}z_{1},\ldots,\frac{(1-|a_{j}|^{2})^{1/2m_{n-1}}}{(1-\bar{a}_{j}z_{n})^{1/m_{n-1}}}z_{n-1},\dfrac{z_{n}+a_{j}}{1+\bar{a}_{j}z_{n}}\right),\;j\geq 1.

\psi_{j}(0^{\prime},0)=(0^{\prime},a_{j})\to(0^{\prime},1)

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\psi_{j}^{-1}(\Omega)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\psi_{j}^{-1}(\Omega\cap U)=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\psi_{j}^{-1}(D_{P}\cap U)=D_{P}.\end{split}

Now we consider the limit of
\{\psi_{j}^{-1}(\eta_{j})\}
as
j\to\infty
in terms of the boundary behavior of
\{a_{j}\}
tending to
1\in\partial\Delta
. Indeed, we first assume that
\{\eta_{j}\}\subset D^{s}_{P}
for some

. Then as in Remark

|a_{j}-1|^{2}<-2s\mathrm{Re}(a_{j}-1),\;\text{ for }j\geq 1,

or equivalently
x_{j}^{2}+y_{j}^{2}<2sx_{j},\;\text{ for }j\geq 1
. Therefore, after taking some subsequence we may assume that there exists

\alpha:=\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\frac{y_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}}\leq 2s<2.

Thus we consider the following three cases:

Case 1.

\alpha=0

for some

. Then by Theorem
1.1
, one has
\Omega
is biholomorphically equivalent to

.

Case 2.

\alpha=0

\{\eta_{j}\}
converges
\Lambda
-tangentially to
(0^{\prime},1)
. Then by Theorem
1.2
, one has
\Omega
is biholomorphically equivalent to
\mathbb{B}^{n}
.

Case 3.

0<\alpha<2
. Then by Lemma

, we have

\begin{split}\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}\psi_{j}^{-1}(D_{P}^{s})=D_{P}^{s}(\alpha),\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\displaystyle D_{P}^{s}(\alpha)

\displaystyle=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\colon\left|z_{n}+\frac{b\alpha}{(1-b)(2-\alpha)+b\alpha}\right|^{2}+\frac{(1-b)(2-\alpha)}{(1-b)(2-\alpha)+b\alpha}P(z^{\prime})

where

\psi_{j}^{-1}(\eta_{j})=\Big(\dfrac{\eta_{j1}}{\lambda_{j}^{1/2m_{1}}},\ldots,\dfrac{\eta_{j(n-1)}}{\lambda_{j}^{1/2m_{n-1}}},0\Big)\in D_{P}^{s}(\alpha)\cap\{z_{n}=0\}

converges to some point

\psi_{j}^{-1}\circ f_{j}^{-1}

\{\eta_{j}\}\not\subset D^{s}_{P}

4.
Spherically extreme boundary points

We start with the following result which is the main step of the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
smooth boundary near some point
p\in\partial\Omega
. Suppose that
p
is a locally spherically extreme point. Then,
\Omega
is strongly convex at
p
.

Proof.

We write
p=(p^{\prime},p_{N}).

By the assumption there is a point
c(p)\in\Omega
and
r>0
such that
B(c(p),r)\subset\Omega

and
\partial B(c(p),r)\cap\partial\Omega=\{p\}.

After a translation we may assume
c(p)
is the origin,
then by a permutation of coordinates and the implicit function theorem we may assume that
near
p,\Omega
admits a local defining function
\rho
taking the form

(3)

\rho(x):=x_{N}-\varphi(x^{\prime}),\ x^{\prime}=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{N-1})

where
\varphi
is
\mathcal{C}^{2}
smooth defined on a small neighbourhood
U
of
p^{\prime}=(p_{1},\cdots,p_{N-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{N-1}
.
Furthermore, after applying a reflection if necessary we may achieve that
p_{N}\geq 0

Then for
x=(x^{\prime},x_{N})\in\partial\Omega
with
x^{\prime}\in U
we have

(4)

\|x\|^{2}=\psi(x^{\prime}):=\|x^{\prime}\|^{2}+\varphi(x^{\prime})^{2}.

Thus, by the assumption that
p
is a locally spherical extreme point of
\partial\Omega,

we see that the function
\psi

attains its local maximum at the point
p^{\prime}
. Hence

(5)

\nabla\psi|_{(p^{\prime})}=0.

Now we suppose that
\partial\Omega
is
not
strictly convex at
p.
Then we can find a
tangent vector
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N})\in T_{p}(\partial\Omega),\lambda\neq 0
such that

\text{Hess}\ (\rho)(p,\lambda):=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq N}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial x_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}(p)\lambda_{j}{\lambda_{k}}\leq 0.

By (
3
) we obtain

\text{Hess}\ (\varphi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime}):=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq N-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\varphi}{\partial x_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}(p^{\prime})\lambda_{j}{\lambda_{k}}\geq 0.

Now we wish to exploit further the assumption that
\psi
attains its local maximum at the point
(p^{\prime}).

Then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
small enough we have

(6)

\psi(p^{\prime})\geq\psi(p^{\prime}+t\lambda^{\prime}),\ \lambda^{\prime}=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N-1}).

Using Taylor expansion theorem on the right hand side while taking into account (
5
) we get

(7)

\psi(p^{\prime}+t\lambda^{\prime})=\psi(p^{\prime})+\text{Hess}(\psi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})t^{2}+o(t^{2}),

where

\text{Hess}(\psi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})=\sum_{1\leq j,k\leq N-1}\frac{\partial^{2}\psi}{\partial x_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}(p^{\prime})\lambda_{j}{\lambda_{k}}.

Putting (
6
) and (
7
) together we see that

\text{Hess}(\psi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\leq 0.

By direct computation, using (
4
) and the fact that
\varphi(p^{\prime})=p_{N},
we arrive at

(8)

\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{j}^{2}+2p_{N}\text{Hess}\ (\varphi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})+2\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(p^{\prime})\lambda_{j}\Big)^{2}\leq 0.

Next, we look at the right most term on the left hand side. Since

(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N})\in T_{p}(\partial\Omega)
we have

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\partial\rho}{\partial x_{j}}(p)\lambda_{j}=0.

In view of (
3
) the above equation takes the form

\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{j}}(p)\lambda_{j}=\lambda_{n}.

Plugging this equation into (
8
) we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{j}^{2}+2p_{n}\text{Hess}\ (\varphi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})+2\lambda_{N}^{2}\leq 0.

Since
p_{N}\geq 0
and since
\text{Hess}\ (\varphi)(p^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})\leq 0

we infer that
\lambda_{1}=\cdots=\lambda_{N}=0,
which is absurd. So we are done.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\Omega

We now consider the family

\begin{cases}w_{n}&=\dfrac{\epsilon z_{n}}{2-\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)z_{n}}\\
w^{\prime}&=\dfrac{\sqrt{\epsilon(2-\epsilon)}}{2-\epsilon+(1-\epsilon)z_{n}}z^{\prime}.\end{cases}

n

Definite fillings of lens spaces
Paolo Aceto, Duncan McCoy, JungHwan Park

Abstract
. This paper considers the problem of determining the smallest (as measured by the second Betti number) smooth negative-definite filling of a lens space. The main result is to classify those lens spaces for which the associated negative-definite canonical plumbing is minimal. The classification takes the form of a list of 10 “forbidden” subgraphs that cannot appear in the plumbing graph if the corresponding plumbed 4-manifold is minimal. We also show that whenever the plumbing is minimal any other negative-definite filling for the given lens space has the same intersection form up to addition of diagonal summands. Consequences regarding smooth embeddings of lens spaces in 4-manifolds are also discussed.

1.1.
Embeddings in 4-manifolds

As any 3-manifold smoothly embeds in
S^{5}

[
Hir61
,
Roh65
,
Wal65
]
and, hence, in any 5-manifold, it is natural to investigate the existence of embeddings of 3-manifolds in 4-manifolds. We will restrict ourselves to separating embeddings.
2
2

2

For many commonly considered 4-manifolds, specifically those with
b_{1}(X)=0
, there is no loss of generality in considering only separating embeddings. If a
4
-manifold
X
contains an embedded closed 3-manifold

that is not separating, then one can glue together infinitely many copies of
X
cut along

to construct an infinite cover of

with deck group
\mathbb{Z}
. Such a covering corresponds to a nontrivial homomorphism

\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
. As this homomorphism necessarily factors through

, we have
b_{1}(X)\geq 1
.
As a particularly simple class of 3-manifolds, the embeddings of lens spaces (and connected sums thereof) have often been studied. For example, it is known that no lens space embeds in
S^{4}

[
Han38
]
and the connected sums of lens spaces smoothly embedding in
S^{4}
have been classified by Donald
[
Don15
]
(see also
[
KK80
,
GL83
,
FS87
]
). Embeddings of lens spaces in
\mathbb{CP}^{2}
have also been studied (see, for example,
[
Owe20
]
).

Question 1
.

Is there a smooth closed 4-manifold
X
such that every lens space smoothly embeds in
X
as a separating submanifold?

This question is somewhat subtle in several ways. The smooth hypothesis is important; Edmonds has shown that every lens space embeds topologically locally flatly into
\#_{4}S^{2}\times S^{2},\#_{8}\mathbb{CP}^{2}
,
\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}
, and
\#_{2}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#_{2}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}

[
Edm05
, Theorem 1.2]
.
3
3

3

Although not explicitly stated by Edmonds, the case of
\#_{5}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}
also follows from the same methods.
Moreover, every punctured lens space smoothly embeds in
S^{2}\times S^{2}

[
EL96
, Proposition 2.3]
.

As evidence for a negative answer to Question

, we have the following result.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
X
is a smooth closed 4-manifold such that every lens space smoothly embeds in

as a separating manifold, then
X
is not spin and

|\sigma(X)|\leq b_{2}(X)-4.

In particular, for any positive integer

or
\#_{n}\mathbb{CP}^{2}\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{2}

The condition the

is not spin is straightforward. It was shown in
[

]
using the 10/8-Theorem
[

]
that there is no positive integer

such that every lens space smoothly embeds in

Theorem 1.6
.

For any positive integer

, there is a lens space

such that if

is a negative-definite filling of

or

, then

Given Theorem

and the results of Edmonds
[

]
, it is natural to ask whether every lens space smoothly embeds in

Comparison with the analytic and the symplectic settings

There are also some links between Theorem
1.1
and the theories of symplectic fillings

4

In the ensuing discussion, symplectic filling refers to the notion that is sometimes called a weak symplectic filling.
for lens spaces and smoothings of cyclic quotient singularities. The symplectic fillings for the standard (i.e. universally tight) contact structures on lens spaces were classified by Lisca
[
Lis08

Let

Suppose

Is it possible to characterize all lens spaces

An

(L,Q_{L})

L

Q_{L}:L\times L\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
is a symmetric bilinear pairing. A

\phi:(L,Q_{L})\rightarrow(L^{\prime},Q_{L^{\prime}})

preserving the bilinear pairings. In particular, the intersection form of a compact oriented 4-manifold

defines a lattice
(H_{2}(X)/\tors,Q_{X})
, which we will often refer to as just
Q_{X}
.

Recall that any connected sum of lens spaces
L=\#_{i}L(p_{i},q_{i})
bounds the boundary connected sum of canonical plumbings
X=\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
. The intersection form of
X
will be discussed in further detail in Section
3

) that will be proven later.

Lemma 2.1
.

If
X=\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
is the boundary connected sum of canonical plumbings, then for any
x\in H_{2}(X)
with
x\neq 0
, we have
Q_{X}(x,x)\leq-2
. In particular,
X
is negative-definite and the intersection form does not contain any elements with
Q_{X}(x,x)=-1
.

Proof.

The intersection form of
-X
, that is,
X
with the opposite orientation, is isomorphic to a lattice associated to a weighted plumbing graph in which every vertex has weight greater than or equal to two. Lemma
3.4
applied to such a plumbing graph shows that
Q_{-X}(x,x)=-Q_{X}(x,x)\geq 2
for every nonzero

.
∎

2.2.
Rigidity and the intersection form

In this section, we discuss the intersection forms of definite fillings.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
Y
be a closed oriented rational homology sphere which is the boundary of compact 4-manifolds
P
and
X
, where
H_{1}(P)=0
. If
W
is the closed oriented 4-manifold
W=P\cup_{Y}-X
, then the inclusions
P,-X\hookrightarrow W
induce a map of lattices

\iota:Q_{P}\oplus-Q_{X}\rightarrow Q_{W}

such that
-Q_{X}\cong\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}\subseteq Q_{W}
, where
\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}
is the orthogonal complement of
\iota(Q_{P})
in
Q_{W}
.

Proof.

Using a portion of the long exact Mayer-Vietoris sequence, we obtain the exact sequence

0=H_{2}(Y)\rightarrow H_{2}(P)\oplus H_{2}(-X)\rightarrow H_{2}(W).

Since the maps induced by inclusion preserve the intersection pairing, this gives an embedding of lattices
\iota:Q_{P}\oplus Q_{-X}\rightarrow Q_{W}
such that the image has full rank in
Q_{W}
. This implies that
\iota\left(Q_{-X}\right)
has finite index in
\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}
. Consider now the exact sequence

H_{2}(-X)\rightarrow H_{2}(W)\rightarrow H_{2}(W,-X)

Note that
H_{2}(W,-X)
is torsion-free since it is isomorphic to
H_{2}(P,Y)\cong H^{2}(P)
and the torsion subgroup of
H^{2}(P)
is isomorphic to the torsion subgroup of
H_{1}(P)=0
. Thus the quotient of
H_{2}(W)
by the image of
H_{2}(-X)
is torsion-free. However the quotient of
H_{2}(W)
by the image of
H_{2}(-X)
contains a subgroup isomorphic to the quotient
\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}/\iota(Q_{-X})
and so we conclude that
\iota(Q_{-X})=\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}
. Since
\iota\left(Q_{-X}\right)
is isomorphic to
-Q_{X}
, the lemma follows.
∎

The following concept will play a key role in proving Theorem
1.1
.

Definition 2.5
.

A lattice
(L,Q_{L})
is
rigid
, if for any positive integer
N
and any pair of lattice maps
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:(L,Q_{L})\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}^{N},\langle 1\rangle^{N})
, there is an automorphism
\alpha\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{N})
such that
\phi^{\prime}=\alpha\circ\phi
.

The relevance of this definition is that it gives a criterion for proving that for certain 3-manifolds the intersection form of any negative-definite filling is unique up to stabilisation with
\langle-1\rangle
summands.

Proposition 2.6
.

If
Y
is a rational homology sphere which bounds a smooth positive-definite 4-manifold
P
with
H_{1}(P)=0
such that the intersection form of
P
is rigid, then for any pair of smooth negative-definite fillings
X
and
X^{\prime}
with
b_{2}(X)\geq b_{2}(X^{\prime})
, we have

Q_{X}\cong Q_{X^{\prime}}\oplus\langle-1\rangle^{b_{2}(X)-b_{2}(X^{\prime})}.

Proof.

The closed manifolds
W=P\cup-X
and
W^{\prime}=P\cup-X^{\prime}
are smooth and positive-definite and hence diagonalizable by Donaldson’s theorem. Let
n=b_{2}(W)=b_{2}(X)+b_{2}(P)
and
n^{\prime}=b_{2}(W^{\prime})=b_{2}(X^{\prime})+b_{2}(P)
. Note that
n-n^{\prime}=b_{2}(X)-b_{2}(X^{\prime})
. Thus by Lemma
2.4
we have embeddings

\iota:Q_{P}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\langle 1\rangle^{n})\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\iota^{\prime}:Q_{P}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}^{n^{\prime}},\langle 1\rangle^{n^{\prime}})

such that the orthogonal complements give us isomorphisms

-Q_{X}\cong\iota(Q_{P})^{\bot}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad-Q_{X^{\prime}}\cong\iota^{\prime}(Q_{P})^{\bot}.

By embedding
(\mathbb{Z}^{n^{\prime}},\langle 1\rangle^{n^{\prime}})
in
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\langle 1\rangle^{n})
in the natural way, we further get an embedding

\iota^{\prime\prime}:Q_{P}\hookrightarrow(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\langle 1\rangle^{n})

such that
\iota^{\prime\prime}(Q_{P})^{\bot}
is isomorphic to
-Q_{X^{\prime}}\oplus\langle 1\rangle^{n-n^{\prime}}
. However since the intersection form of
P
is rigid, there is an automorphism of
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\langle 1\rangle^{n})
carrying
\iota^{\prime\prime}(Q_{P})
to
\iota(Q_{P})
. This automorphism restricts to give isomorphism between the orthogonal complements.
∎

Any connected sum of lens spaces
L=\#_{i}L(p_{i},q_{i})
is the boundary of the manifold
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
, which is positive-definite by Lemma
2.1
. The key ingredient in the proof of the implication
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
of Theorem
1.1
is the following result which will be established in Section
4
.

Theorem 2.7
.

If
L=\#_{i}L(p_{i},q_{i})
is a connected sum of lens spaces such that the corresponding plumbing graph does not contain any of the configurations in Theorem
1.1
(iii)
, then the intersection form of
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
is a rigid lattice.

Modulo the proof of Theorem
2.7
, we are ready to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The implication
(ii)
\Rightarrow
(i)
is evident. The implication
(i)
\Rightarrow
(iv)
is established via its contrapositive in Lemma
2.2
. The implication
(iv)
\Rightarrow
(iii)
is established via its contrapositive in Lemma
2.3
. Finally, we establish
(iii)
\Rightarrow
(ii)
. If
L=\#_{i}L(p_{i},q_{i})
is a connected sum of lens spaces satisfying the hypotheses of
(iii)
, then the intersection form of
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
is rigid by Theorem
2.7
. Thus Proposition
2.6
implies that any pair of smooth negative-definite fillings of
L
have the same intersection form up to stabilising with
\langle-1\rangle
summands. By Lemma
2.1
,
\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
is a smooth negative-definite filling whose intersection form does not contain any elements of length
-1
. Thus we see that every other possible intersection form must be obtained by stabilising the intersection form of
\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
.
∎

3.
Canonical plumbings and rigidity

In this section we explore the relationship between the intersection form of
\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
and the intersection form of
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
. We begin with some definitions.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
P
be a disjoint union of linear plumbing graphs and
V(P)
be the set of its vertices. We define the
lattice associated to
P
to be the lattice

(\mathbb{Z}V(P),Q_{P}),

i.e. the group is the free abelian group generated by the vertices of
P
and the pairing

Q_{P}
is defined by the rule that for all vertices
u,v\in V(P)
we have

Q_{P}(u,v)=\begin{cases}w(v)&\text{if $u=v$}\\
-1&\text{if $u,v$ adjacent}\\
0&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
w(v)
is the weight of
v
.
For brevity, we often refer to
(\mathbb{Z}V(P),Q_{P})
simply as
Q_{P}
.

It will frequently be convenient to think in terms of adjusted weights which are defined as follows.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
P
be a disjoint union of linear plumbing graphs. We define the
adjusted weight
of a vertex
v\in P
to be

w^{\prime}(v):=w(v)-d(v),

where
d(v)
is the degree of
v
.

Notation 3.3
.

When depicting a vertex in a plumbing graph we use a solid dot to indicate that the labelling is the weight
w(v)
\bullet
and a hollow dot to indicate that the labelling is the adjusted weight
w^{\prime}(v)
\circ
.

For example, with this notational convention

1
2
2
\circ
\circ
\circ

and

2
4
3
\bullet
\bullet
\bullet

both depict the same plumbing graph.

If a 4-manifold
X
is obtained by plumbing
D^{2}
-bundles over
S^{2}
according to a weighted plumbing graph
P
, then the intersection form of
X
is isomorphic to the lattice
(\mathbb{Z}V(P),Q_{P})
, where the isomorphism sends a vertex to the corresponding spherical generator in
H_{2}(X)
.

The following lemma taken with
M=2
implies Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
P
is a disjoint union of linear plumbing graphs in which every vertex has weight
w(v)\geq M
for some integer
M\geq 2
, then for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}V(P)
with
x\neq 0
, we have
Q_{P}(x,x)\geq M
.

Proof.

If
P
can be decomposed into connected components
P=\bigsqcup P_{i}
, then we have a decomposition of the lattice
Q_{P}=\bigoplus Q_{P_{i}}
. Thus it suffices to prove the lemma when
P
is connected. In this case, suppose that we have vertices
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}
, where these are ordered so that
v_{i}
and
v_{i+1}
are adjacent for
i=1,\dots,n-1
. By hypothesis,
w(v_{i})\geq M
for all
i
. Thus if we take
x=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}v_{i}
for integers
c_{i}
, then completing the square reveals that for any
1\leq j\leq n
we have

\displaystyle Q_{P}(x,x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(w(v_{i})-2)c_{i}^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(c_{i}-c_{i+1})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(w(v_{j})-2)c_{j}^{2}+c_{1}^{2}+(c_{1}-c_{2})^{2}+\dots+(c_{n-1}-c_{n})^{2}+c_{n}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq(w(v_{j})-2)|c_{j}|+|c_{1}|+|c_{1}-c_{2}|+\dots+|c_{n-1}-c_{n}|+|c_{n}|

\displaystyle\geq(w(v_{j})-2)|c_{j}|+2|c_{j}|

\displaystyle=w(v_{j})|c_{j}|

\displaystyle\geq M|c_{j}|

where we applied the triangle inequality to show:

|c_{j}|\leq|c_{1}|+|c_{1}-c_{2}|+\dots+|c_{j-1}-c_{j}|

and

|c_{j}|\leq|c_{j}-c_{j+1}|+\dots+|c_{n-1}-c_{n}|+|c_{n}|.

If
x\neq 0
, then
c_{j}\neq 0
for at least one
j
and so we have the required bound.
∎

3.1.
The dual plumbing

A connected sum of lens spaces
\#_{i}L(p_{i},q_{i})
arises as the boundary the plumbings
\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
and
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
. If the manifold
\natural_{i}X(p_{i},q_{i})
is obtained by plumbing according to a canonical plumbing graph
P
and
-\natural_{i}X(p_{i},p_{i}-q_{i})
is obtained by plumbing along a canonical plumbing graph
P^{*}
, then we say that
P^{*}
is the
dual
of
P
. The task of this section is to relate
P
and
P^{*}
explicitly.

We will work with negative continued fractions and use the following notation

[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]^{-}=a_{1}-\cfrac{1}{a_{2}-\cfrac{1}{\ddots-\cfrac{1}{a_{n}}}}.

Here and throughout the article, we will use
a^{[m]}
to denote the tuple

\underbrace{a,a,\dots,a,a}_{\text{$m$ times}},

with the understanding that
a^{[0]}
denotes the empty tuple. The Riemenschneider point rule
[

]
tells us that if

has the continued fraction of the form

where the

and
b_{i}
are integers satisfying
a_{i}\geq 0
and

, then

takes the form

(3.1)

\frac{p}{p-q}=\begin{cases}\left[a_{0}+1\right]^{-}&\text{if $k=0$}\\
\left[a_{0}+2,2^{[b_{1}-3]},a_{1}+3,2^{[b_{2}-3]},\dots,2^{[b_{k}-3]},a_{k}+2\right]^{-}&\text{if $k\geq 1$}.\end{cases}

4.
Rigid linear lattices

The objective of this section is to prove Theorem
2.7
. Throughout this section, we will take
\Gamma
to be a disjoint union of linear plumbing graphs. The aim is to show that if
\Gamma
satisfies the Working Conditions, then the corresponding lattice
Q_{\Gamma}
is rigid. By Lemma
3.6
the dual of a plumbing graph satisfying Theorem
1.1
(iii)
satisfies the Working Conditions, therefore this is sufficient to establish Theorem
2.7
.

To this end, we introduce some terminology specific to the problem of mapping linear lattices into diagonal lattices. Throughout this section, for simplicity we refer to the standard positive-definite lattice
(\mathbb{Z}^{n},\langle 1\rangle^{n})
as
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Definition 4.1
.

An
embedding
of
\Gamma
into
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is a function

\phi:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}

such that for all
u,v\in V(\Gamma)
, we have

\phi(u)\cdot\phi(v)=\begin{cases}w(u)&\text{if $u=v$}\\
-1&\text{if $u$ and $v$ adjacent}\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Since lattice maps of
Q_{\Gamma}
into
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are determined by the image of the vertices, embeddings of
\Gamma
into
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are in bijection with lattice maps of
Q_{\Gamma}
into
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Similarly, we define what it means for a plumbing graph to be rigid.

Definition 4.2
.

A plumbing graph
\Gamma
is
rigid
if for any integer
n\geq 1
and any pair of embeddings
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, there is an element
\alpha\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
such that
\phi^{\prime}(v)=\alpha(\phi(v))
for each
v\in V(\Gamma)
.

Thus the lattice
Q_{\Gamma}
is rigid in the sense of Definition
2.5
if and only if
\Gamma
is rigid as defined in terms of embeddings here.

For a vector
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we define the
support
of
v
to be

\supp(v):=\{e\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\,|\,\lVert e\rVert^{2}=1\,\text{ and }\,e\cdot v\neq 0\}/(e\sim-e)

that is the set of unit vectors pairing nontrivially with
v
up to sign. For vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, we define the support of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}
to be

\supp(v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}):=\supp(v_{1})\cup\cdots\cup\supp(v_{k}).

Remark 4.3
.

We quotient out the support by the relation
e\sim-e
, so as to avoid explicitly choosing an orthonormal basis for
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. However, we see that for any choice of orthonormal basis
\supp(v)
contains one element for each nonzero coordinate of
v
. This gives the inequality

(4.1)

\lVert v\rVert^{2}\geq|\supp(v)|

with equality if and only if
v=e_{1}+\dots+e_{k}
for some collection of distinct orthogonal unit vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Definition 4.4
.

An embedding
\phi:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
is
standard
if

|\supp(\phi(u))\cap\supp(\phi(v))|=\begin{cases}w(v)&\text{if $u=v$}\\
1&\text{if $u$ and $v$ are adjacent}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for all vertices
u
and
v
in
V(\Gamma)
.

Remark 4.5
.

We make the following observations regarding standard embeddings:

(a)

The plumbing graph
\Gamma
always admits standard embeddings in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
provided
n
is sufficiently large. Explicitly one can calculate that a standard embedding requires
-E+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}w(v)
distinct orthogonal unit vectors, where
E
is the number of edges in
\Gamma
. Thus a standard embedding exists if and only if

n\geq-E+\sum_{v\in V(\Gamma)}w(v).

(b)

If
\phi
is a standard embedding of
\Gamma
and
\alpha\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
is an automorphism, then
\alpha\circ\phi
is also standard. Conversely any pair of standard embeddings in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
are related by an automorphism. Thus a plumbing graph
\Gamma
is rigid if and only if every embedding of
\Gamma
is standard.

Next we define a relative version of rigidity.

Definition 4.6
.

We say that a subgraph
\Gamma^{\prime}
of a plumbing graph
\Gamma
is
rigid
if for any integer
n\geq 1
and for any pair of embeddings
\phi,\phi^{\prime}:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, there is an element
\alpha\in\aut(\mathbb{Z}^{n})
such that
\phi^{\prime}(v)=\alpha(\phi(v))
for each
v\in V(\Gamma^{\prime})
.

For the rest of the article, whenever we are considering a plumbing graph
\Gamma
together with a lattice embedding
\phi:V(\Gamma)\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we will, by abuse of notation, denote
\phi(v)
by

6.
Embedding lens spaces in 4-manifolds

We now turn our attention to embedding lens spaces in 4-manifolds.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
L_{k}
be a lens space as given in Theorem
1.6
. If
L_{k}
embeds as a separating submanifold of a smooth closed 4-manifold
X
with
|\sigma(X)|\geq b_{2}(X)-2
, then
k\leq b_{2}(X)
.

Proof.

By hypothesis and Theorem
1.6
, any smooth negative-definite filling of
L_{k}
or
-L_{k}
has
b_{2}\geq k
.
Suppose that
L_{k}
smoothly embeds in a closed
4
-manifold
X
as a separating submanifold and that
|\sigma(X)|\geq b_{2}(X)-2
. By changing the orientation of
X
if necessary we may assume that
\sigma(X)\leq 2-b_{2}(X)
and hence that
b_{2}^{+}(X)\leq 1
. The embedding of
L_{k}
in
X
gives a decomposition of
X
into two pieces
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
with
\partial X_{1}\cong-\partial X_{2}\cong L_{k}
. Moreover, by Novikov additivity and the fact that a lens space is a rational homology sphere, we have that
b_{2}^{+}(X)=b_{2}^{+}(X_{1})+b_{2}^{+}(X_{2})
and
b_{2}^{-}(X)=b_{2}^{-}(X_{1})+b_{2}^{-}(X_{2})
. Thus, we have that at least one of
b_{2}^{+}(X_{1})
or
b_{2}^{+}(X_{2})
is zero. That is, at least one of
X_{1}
or
X_{2}
is negative-definite. This implies that
b_{2}(X_{1})\geq k
or
b_{2}(X_{2})\geq k
and hence that
b_{2}(X)\geq k
.
∎

Mimicking arguments that can be found in
[
AGL17
]
, we note that there is no spin 4-manifold that contains every lens space as a separating submanifold.

Proposition 6.2
.

If the lens space
L(n,n-1)
with
n\geq 1
odd smoothly embeds in a closed spin 4-manifold
X
as a separating submanifold, then

n\leq 9b_{2}(X)+1.

Proof.

Note that since
n
is odd, the lens space
L(n,n-1)
admits a unique spin structure. Cutting
X
along the embedded
L(n,n-1)
yields a spin 4-manifold
Z
filling
-L(n,n-1)
. On the other hand, the manifold
X(n,n-1)
is negative-definite and spin with
b_{2}(X(n,n-1))=n-1
. Since the spin structure on
L(n,n-1)
is unique, the closed 4-manifold
W=X(n,n-1)\cup_{L(n,n-1)}Z
is spin. We have

b_{2}(W)=b_{2}(Z)+n-1\leq b_{2}(X)+n-1

and

|\sigma(W)|=|\sigma(Z)-n+1|\geq n-1-b_{2}(X).

Thus by Furuta’s theorem
[
Fur01
]
, we have

b_{2}(X)+n-1\geq b_{2}(W)\geq\frac{5}{4}|\sigma(W)|\geq\frac{5}{4}(n-1-b_{2}(X)).

Rearranging implies that
n\leq 9b_{2}(X)+1
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Let
X
be a smooth closed 4-manifold that contains every lens space as a smoothly embedded separating submanifold. Proposition
6.2
implies that
X
cannot be spin. Proposition
6.1
implies that
|\sigma(X)|<b_{2}(X)-2

|\sigma(X)|\leq b_{2}(X)-4

ListEmbeddings:= function(coefs)
local embeddings, k, M;

M:=DiagonalMat(coefs);
for k in [1..Length(coefs)-1] do
M[k][k+1]:=-1;
M[k+1][k]:=-1;
od;

embed:=OrthogonalEmbeddings(M);

Print("----", coefs ,"----","\n");
Print("Number of embeddings:",Length(embed.solutions),"\n");
for k in [1..Length(embed.solutions)] do
Print(TransposedMat(embed.vectors{embed.solutions[k]}),"\n");
od;
Print("--------","\n");
end;

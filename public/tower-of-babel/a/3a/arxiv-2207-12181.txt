Restricted universal groups for right-angled buildings
Jens Bossaert, Tom De Medts

Abstract
. In 2000, Marc Burger and Shahar Mozes introduced universal groups acting on trees. Such groups provide interesting examples of totally disconnected locally compact groups. Intuitively, these are the largest groups for which all local actions satisfy a prescribed behavior. Since then, their study has evolved in various directions. In particular, Adrien Le Boudec has studied restricted universal groups, where the prescribed behavior is allowed to be violated in a finite number of vertices. On the other hand, we have been studying universal groups acting on right-angled buildings , a class of geometric objects with a much more general structure than trees. The aim of the current paper is to combine both ideas: we will study restricted universal groups acting on right-angled buildings. We show several permutational and topological properties of those groups, with as main result a precise criterion for when these groups are simple.

1.
Introduction

In their seminal paper
[
burgermozes2000
]
, Marc Burger and Shahar Mozes have introduced
universal groups
acting on trees. More precisely, given any permutation group
F
acting on a set
S
, there is a “largest” group
\U(F)
acting on the regular tree of degree
|S|
such that all local actions (i.e., the induced actions on the stars around a vertex and its image) belong to
F
.

If
S
is finite (an assumption which is often, but certainly not always, imposed), then these universal groups are
totally disconnected locally compact
(tdlc, for short) with respect to the permutation topology. They provide an interesting class of examples, and the study of and the interplay between their permutational and topological properties leads to a better understanding of tdlc groups; see, for instance,
[
CDM11
]
and the more recent survey
[
universal-survey
]
.

In 2016, Adrien Le Boudec
[
leboudec
]
has generalized the notion to so-called
restricted
universal groups—the terminology is due to
[
caprace_dense
]
—by allowing for a finite number of
singularities
, i.e., vertices where the local action does not belong to
F
. This gives rise to non-closed subgroups of the automorphism group of the tree, but with the correct topology, these groups are again tdlc groups.

In a different direction, we have been studying universal groups for
right-angled buildings
. These geometric objects can be seen as higher-dimensional analogues of trees, but their combinatorics are governed by a (right-angled) Coxeter diagram, allowing for many different possible structures.
The starting point is our joint work with Ana C. Silva and Koen Struyve in
[
silva1
]
, which has been continued in
[
silva2
,
bossaert
,
cityproducts
]
.
This builds upon earlier results (for the full automorphism group) by Pierre-Emmanuel Caprace in
[
caprace2014
]
; in particular, he had shown that the full automorphism group of a right-angled building is always simple.

In the current paper, we apply Le Boudec’s idea on the setting of locally finite right-angled buildings. We introduce restricted universal groups
\G(\boldsymbol{F}\mkern-2.0mu,\boldsymbol{\smash{\acute{F}}})
acting on a right-angled building
\Delta
(of rank
n\geq 2
) with respect to “local data”
\boldsymbol{F}
and
\boldsymbol{\smash{\acute{F}}}
, each of which consists of
n
permutation groups. Again, with the correct topology, this gives rise to new examples of tdlc groups.

The combinatorics of the Coxeter diagram enter the picture in interesting ways. An important aspect is the occurrence of
ladders
and their associated
rungs
in the diagram, which are nodes for which the generators commute with (at least) two non-commuting generators; see
below. The main result of our paper (
), which gaves a good flavor for the kind of conditions that arise, is the following.

Theorem
.

Let
\Delta
be a thick irreducible right-angled building over an index set
I
with
\lvert I\rvert\geq 2
. Let
\boldsymbol{F}
and
\boldsymbol{\smash{\acute{F}}}
be the local data as in
. Assume that
F_{i}=\acute{F}_{i}
for every
i\in I
that is the type of a rung. Moreover, assume that not all local groups
\acute{F}_{i}
are free.

Then the restricted universal group
\G(\boldsymbol{F}\mkern-2.0mu,\boldsymbol{\smash{\acute{F}}})
is virtually simple if and only if
\acute{F}_{i}
is generated by point stabilizers for every
i\in I
and transitive for every
i
in some vertex cover of the diagram of
\Delta
.

Along the way, we show several other permutational and topological properties for these restricted universal groups.

We should point out that this is (hopefully) not the end of the story. Indeed, in
[
leboudec
]
, these restricted universal groups for trees are studied more deeply, in particular with regard to their asymptotic dimension and the (non-)existence of lattices. It is not unlikely that our new class of tdlc groups admits a similar behavior as the groups introduced and studied by Le Boudec.

Automorphism groups of right-angled buildings come equipped with a natural topology.

Definition 2.10
.

We define the

The following notion will play an important role later.

Definition 2.11

(1)

To every partition of a set

Assume that there is a second chamber

(because of the gate property applied to
\mathcal{P}
), contradicting the minimality of

.
Hence we must have
\proj_{C}(c)\neq\proj_{C}(d)
. Those projections are
i
-adjacent; let
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
be the
i
-panel through them. By
Proposition

4
,
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
are parallel, and since

is panel-closed, we have
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\subseteq C
.
In particular, all chambers in
\mathcal{P}
are at the same distance from

\mathcal{P}\subseteq\operatorname{\mathsf{S}}_{n}(C)

Assume now that

\mathcal{P}

can certainly not be parallel to any panel in

.
In particular,

2.8

\mathcal{P}

Lemma 3.8

(Closing squares)
.

Let

be panel-closed.

(1)

Let

be such that
\dist(C,c_{1})=\dist(C,c_{3})=n

\dist(C,c_{1})=\dist(C,c_{2})=n+1
,
\dist(C,c_{3})=n
, and
c_{1}\sim_{i}c_{2}\sim_{j}c_{3}
for
i\neq j
. Then
m_{ij}=2
and there exists

such that
\dist(C,d)=n
and
c_{1}\sim_{j}d\sim_{i}c_{3}
.

Proof.

(1)

By
Lemma

3.7
, the chambers
c_{1}
,
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
all have the same projection on
C
.
We can thus immediately apply
Lemma

3.2
1
.

(2)

By
Lemma

3.7
, the chambers
c_{2}
and
c_{3}
have the same projection

on

.
Let

.
If also

, then we can apply
Lemma

3.2
2
.
If

, then the
i
-panel
\mathcal{P}
through

\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
through

Proposition 3.9

(Concave galleries)
.

Let
C\subseteq\Delta
be panel-closed and let
c_{1},c_{2}\in\Delta
. Then there exists a minimal gallery

\gamma=(v_{0},\dots,v_{\ell}),\quad\text{with }v_{0}=c_{1},\ v_{\ell}=c_{2},

such that there are numbers
0\leq j\leq k\leq\ell
satisfying:

•

\dist(C,v_{i})<\dist(C,v_{i-1})
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,j\}
,

•

\dist(C,v_{i})=\dist(C,v_{i-1})
for all
i\in\{j+1,\dots,k\}
,

•

\dist(C,v_{i})>\dist(C,v_{i-1})
for all
i\in\{k+1,\dots,\ell\}
.

Such a minimal gallery from
c_{1}
to
c_{2}
will be called
concave (with respect to
C
)
.

Proof.

The proof from
[
silva1
, Lemma 2.11]
for the case
C=\{c\}
only relies on the closing squares lemmas. By
Lemma

3.8
, the proof carries over
mutatis mutandis
to the case where
C
is any panel-closed subset of
\Delta
.
∎

Abstract
. As a continuation of our study (Y.N., S.Y., arXiv:2209.14617) on the algebraic operad of SUSY vertex algebras, we introduce the SUSY coisson operad, which encodes the structures of SUSY Poisson vertex algebras. Our operad is a natural SUSY analogue of the operad encoding the structures of Poisson vertex algebras introduced by Bakalov, De Sole, Heluani and Kac (2019). We also give an embedding of the associated graded of the SUSY chiral operad into the SUSY coisson operad in the filtered case.

Introduction

This is a continuation of our study
[
NY
]
of algebraic operads concerning SUSY vertex algebras.
There we introduced the superoperads
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{W}=N}}
and
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{K}=N}}
called the SUSY chiral operad, which encodes the structures of
N_{W}=N
and
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebras in the sense of Heluani and Kac
[
HK07
]
. The main statement in
[
NY
]
is the bijection

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathsf{Op}}(\oLie,\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{\bullet}=N}_{\Pi^{N+1}V}})_{\overline{1}}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\{\text{$N_{\bullet}=N$ SUSY vertex algebra structures on $(V,\nabla)$}\},\quad\bullet=W\text{ or }K.

for each supermodule
(V,\nabla)
over a certain superalgebra
\mathcal{H}_{W}
or
\mathcal{H}_{K}
, where
\oLie
denotes the Lie operad and
\Pi
denotes the parity change functor. These superoperads are natural SUSY extensions of the chiral operad
P^{\textup{ch}}
introduced by Bakalov, De Sole, Heluani and Kac in
[
BDHK19
]
, which encodes the structure of vertex algebras.

In this note, we introduce the superoperads
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{cl}N_{W}=N}_{V}}
and
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{cl}N_{K}=N}_{V}}
(
2.2.1
), called
the SUSY coisson operads
. They encode the structures of SUSY Poisson vertex algebras. More precisely, there are bijections (
2.2.10
and
2.3.2
)

(0.0.1)

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathsf{Op}}(\oLie,\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{cl}N_{\bullet}=N}_{\Pi^{N+1}V}})_{\overline{1}}\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\{\text{$N=N_{\bullet}$ SUSY Poisson vertex algebra structures on $(V,\nabla)$}\},\quad\bullet=W\text{ or }K.

The word “coisson” is a synonym of “Poisson vertex”, borrowed from
[
BD04
, 2.7]
.

These superoperads are natural SUSY extensions of the operad
P^{\textup{cl}}
in
[
BDHK19
, §10]
which encodes the structures of Poisson vertex algebras. Also, similarly as in the non-SUSY case
[
BDHK19
, §10.4]
, we have a filtration on the SUSY chiral operads
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{\bullet}=N}_{V}}
for a filtered supermodule
V=(V,\nabla)
, and for the associated graded
\gr\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{\bullet}=N}_{V}}
we have a natural embedding (
3.3.8
)

(0.0.2)

\displaystyle\gr\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{ch}N_{\bullet}=N}_{V}}\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{cl}N_{\bullet}=N}_{V}}\quad\bullet=W\text{ or }K.

Our construction of SUSY coisson operads basically follow the non-SUSY case in
[
BDHK19
, §10]
, and the bosonic part of the formulas are essentially the same with those in
[
BDHK19
]
. However, to treat the fermionic part nicely, we give non-trivial extensions in several points. See
2.2.2
,
2.2.6
and the beginning of
section

3
, for example.

The approach to Poisson vertex algebra structures taken in loc. cit. is unique and non-standard in the following sense: It is known (see
[
F06
]
and
[
LV12
, 13.3]
for example) that a Poisson algebra structure on a linear space
V
corresponds bijectively to an operad morphism
\oPoi\to\oHom_{V}
from the Poisson operad
\oPoi
to the endomorphism operad
\oHom_{V}
. Thus, one may guess that Poisson vertex algebra structures correspond to operad morphism from
\oPoi
to some operad. The approach of
[
BDHK19
]
is to consider operad morphisms from
\oLie
instead, as (
0.0.1
) indicates.

In
appendix

A
, we give a remark on the above approach to Poisson-like algebra structures.
In
[
BDHK19
, §10.5]
, it is argued that there is a finite analogue
P^{\textup{fn}}_{V}
of the operad
P^{\textup{cl}}_{V}
encoding the structures of Poisson algebra, and there is a bijection between two sets of operad morphisms for an even linear space
V
:

\Hom_{\mathsf{Op}}(\oPoi,\oHom_{V})\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\Hom_{\mathsf{Op}}(\oLie,P^{\textup{fn}}_{V}).

Then, given a Poisson algebra
A=(V,\cdot,\{\,,\,\})
, we have two cohomology complexes
\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn}}_{A}
and
C_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)
, which arise from
P^{\textup{fn}}
and
\oPoi
, respectively. According to the work of Fresse
[
F06
]
, the latter complex has a bigrading
C_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A,A)
. Now it is natural to ask whether the two complexes can be identified in a natural way. In
A.3.2
, we show that the complex
\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn}}_{A}
arising from the finite operad
P^{\textup{fn}}_{V}
has a bigrading
(\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn}}_{A})^{\bullet,\bullet}
, and the two bicomplexes coincide up to shift:

(\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn}}_{A})^{\bullet,\bullet}\cong C_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}^{\bullet,\bullet+1}.

This statement can be seen as a strengthened form of
[
BDHK19
, Theorem 10.16]
.

The operad
P^{\textup{cl}}
in
[
BDHK19
]
was introduced to reduce the computation of the cohomology of vertex algebras to that of Poisson vertex algebras, as demonstrated in the series of works
[
BDHK20
,
BDK20
,
BDK21
,
BDKV21
]
. We expect that a similar calculation can be made using our SUSY coisson operads for SUSY vertex algebras, but leave it for future work.

1.
Preliminaries

1.1.
Super terminology

We use the same super terminology as in
[
NY
, §1.1]
.
Here we only recall some symbols for symmetric groups and their modules.

•

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
the
n
-th symmetric group with the convention
\mathfrak{S}_{0}\coloneqq\{e\}
.
We consider the group algebra
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
as a purely even superalgebra.
A linear superspace equipped with a left (resp. right)
\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
-supermodule structure is just called a left (resp. right)
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-supermodule.

•

Let
V
be a linear superspace.
For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the linear superspace
V^{\otimes n}
is a left
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-supermodule by letting
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}
act on
v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n}\in V^{\otimes n}
by

\displaystyle\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n})\coloneqq\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i<j\leq n\\
\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\end{subarray}}(-1)^{p(v_{i})p(v_{j})}\cdot v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}.

Here
p(v)\in\{0,1\}
denotes the parity of
v\in V
. In what follows, we always regard
V^{\otimes n}
as this left
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-supermodule unless otherwise specified.

•

An
\mathfrak{S}
-supermodule
M=\bigl(M(n)\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a collection of right
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-supermodules
M(n)
.

The notion of operads (see
[
LV12
]
for example) is naturally extended to the super setting.
We call it a superoperad, following the terminology in
[
BDHK19
]
.
More precisely, a superoperad is an
\mathfrak{S}
-module
\oP=\bigl(\oP(n)\bigr)_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
equipped with an even element
1\in\oP(1)_{\overline{0}}
and a family of even linear maps
\gamma_{\nu}\colon\oP(m)\otimes\oP(n_{1})\otimes\dotsb\otimes\oP(n_{m})\to\oP(n)
for each
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\nu=(n_{1},\dotsc,n_{m})\in\mathbb{N}^{m}
with
n_{1}+\dotsb+n_{m}=n
called the composition maps, satisfying some axiom. We express the composition of the operations
f\in\oP(m)
and
g_{1}\in\oP(n_{1}),\dotsc,g_{m}\in\oP(n_{m})
by
\gamma_{\nu}
as

f\circ(g_{1}\odot\dotsb\odot g_{m})\coloneqq\gamma_{\nu}(f\otimes g_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes g_{m})\in\oP(n).

We refer to
[
NY
, Definition 1.1.1]
for the detail.

1.2.
SUSY Lie conformal algebras and SUSY vertex algebras

The notion of SUSY vertex algebras and the related algebraic structures are introduced by Heluani and Kac in
[
HK07
]
, and some of their aspects are reviewed in
[
NY
, §§2.1–2.3, §§3.1–3.3]
.
We have two types of SUSY vertex algebras, the
N_{W}=N
and the
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebras.
Here we only give the definitions of SUSY Lie conformal algebras and SUSY vertex algebras in the
N_{W}=N
case, and briefly explain the definitions in the
N_{K}=N
case.

Throughout this
section

1.2
, we fix a non-negative integer
N
.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
A
be an index set, and
\Lambda_{\alpha}=(\lambda_{\alpha},\theta_{\alpha}^{1},\ldots,\theta_{\alpha}^{N})
be a sequence of letters for each
\alpha\in A
. We denote by
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
the free commutative
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by even
\lambda_{\alpha}

(\alpha\in A)
and odd
\theta_{\alpha}^{i}

(\alpha\in A,i\in[N])
, i.e, the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by these elements with relations

(1.2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\lambda_{\alpha}\theta_{\beta}^{i}-\theta_{\beta}^{i}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\theta_{\alpha}^{i}\theta_{\beta}^{j}+\theta_{\beta}^{j}\theta_{\alpha}^{i}=0\quad(\alpha,\beta\in A,\ i,j\in[N]).

Each
\Lambda_{\alpha}
for
\alpha\in A
is called a
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable, and the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
is called the
N_{W}=N
polynomial superalgebra of the supervariables
(\Lambda_{\alpha})_{\alpha\in A}
.

In the case
A=[n]=\{1,\dotsc,n\}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, we often denote the polynomial superalgebra by
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}
instead of
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k\in[n]}
. If
A
consists of one element, then we suppress the subscript
\alpha\in A
and denote the polynomial superalgebra by
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
.

For a
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable
\Lambda=(\lambda,\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{N})
and a subset
I=\{i_{1}<\cdots<i_{r}\}\subset[N]
, we denote
\theta^{I}\coloneqq\theta^{i_{1}}\cdots\theta^{i_{r}}\in\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
. Also, we define
\sigma(I,J)\in\{0,\pm 1\}
for
I,J\subset[N]
by the relation
\theta^{I}\theta^{J}=\sigma(I,J)\theta^{I\cup J}
, and set
\sigma(I)\coloneqq\sigma(I,[N]\mathbin{\setminus}I)
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
I\subset[N]
, we denote
\Lambda^{m|I}\coloneqq\lambda^{m}\theta^{I}\in\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
.

For a linear superspace
V
and
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariables
\Lambda_{\alpha}
(
\alpha\in A
),
we denote
V[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}V
,
which regarded as a left
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{\alpha}]_{\alpha\in A}
-supermodule.
We will often use it in the particular case
A=[n]
:

V[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}=\mathbb{K}[\Lambda_{k}]_{k=1}^{n}\otimes_{\mathbb{K}}V.

Definition 1.2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{W}
be the free commutative
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by even
T
and odd
S^{i}
(
i\in[N]
), i.e., the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by these elements with relations

(1.2.2)

\displaystyle TS^{i}-S^{i}T=0,\quad S^{i}S^{j}+S^{j}S^{i}=0\quad(i,j\in[N]).

For simplicity, we set

(1.2.3)

\displaystyle\nabla\coloneqq(T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N}).

We also denote a linear superspace
V
equipped with a left
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule structure as

(V,\nabla)=(V,T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N}),

where
T
is regarded as an even linear transformation on
V
and
S^{i}
as an odd linear transformation, satisfying the relations (
1.2.2
).

In the remaining of this
section

1.2
, let us fix a
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable
\Lambda=(\lambda,\theta^{1},\ldots,\theta^{N})
.

Note that
\mathcal{H}_{W}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{K}[\Lambda]
as a superalgebra by the homomorphism defined by

\displaystyle T\longmapsto-\lambda,\quad S^{i}\longmapsto-\theta^{i}\quad(i\in[N]).

Since
\mathcal{H}_{W}
is a commutative superalgebra, we suppress the word ‘left’ of an
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule hereafter.

Definition 1.2.3

(
[
HK07
, Definition 3.2.2]
)
.

Let
(V,\nabla)=(V,T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N})
be an
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule and
[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot]\colon V\otimes V\to V[\Lambda]
be a linear map of parity
\overline{N}
. A triple
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
is called an
N_{W}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra if it satisfies the following conditions:

1

(sesquilinearity) For any
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}&[Ta_{\Lambda}b]=-\lambda[a_{\Lambda}b],\hskip 43.0pt[a_{\Lambda}Tb]=(\lambda+T)[a_{\Lambda}b],\\
&[S^{i}a_{\Lambda}b]=-(-1)^{N}\theta^{i}[a_{\Lambda}b],\quad[a_{\Lambda}S^{i}b]=(-1)^{p(a)+\overline{N}}(\theta^{i}+S^{i})[a_{\Lambda}b]\quad(i\in[N]).\end{split}

2

(skew-symmetry) For any
a,b\in V
,

(1.2.4)

\displaystyle[b_{\Lambda}a]=-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)+\overline{N}}[a_{-\Lambda-\nabla}b],

where we used
\nabla\coloneqq(T,S^{1},\ldots,S^{N})
in (
1.2.3
).

3

(Jacobi identity) For any
a,b,c\in V
,

(1.2.5)

\displaystyle[a_{\Lambda_{1}}[b_{\Lambda_{2}}c]]=(-1)^{(p(a)+\overline{N})\overline{N}}[[a_{\Lambda_{1}}b]_{\Lambda_{1}+\Lambda_{2}}c]+(-1)^{(p(a)+\overline{N})(p(b)+\overline{N})}[b_{\Lambda_{2}}[a_{\Lambda_{1}}c]],

where
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
are
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariables.

For simplicity, we say
(V,\nabla)
, or more simply
V
, is an
N_{W}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra.
The linear map
[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot]
is called the
\Lambda
-bracket of the
\mathcal{H}_{W}
-supermodule
(V,\nabla)
.

For even linear transformations
F
and
G
on a linear superspace
V
, we define a linear map
\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\colon V[\Lambda]\to V
by

(1.2.6)

\displaystyle\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\,\Lambda^{m|I}v\coloneqq\frac{\delta_{I,[N]}}{m+1}(G^{m+1}v-F^{m+1}v)\quad(m\in\mathbb{N},\,I\subset[N],\,v\in V).

The linear map
\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda
has the parity
\overline{N}
.
Also, if
V
is a superalgebra (not necessarily unital nor associative), we define a linear map

\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\,a\colon V[\Lambda]\to V
for
a\in V
by

\displaystyle\Bigl(\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\,a\Bigr)\Lambda^{m|I}v\coloneqq\Bigl(\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\ \Lambda^{m|I}a\Bigr)v,

where the term
\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\ \Lambda^{m|I}a
in the right hand side is given by (
1.2.6
). Then the linear map
\int_{F}^{G}d\Lambda\,a
has the parity
p(a)
.
Using this integral, we introduce:

Definition 1.2.4

(
[
HK07
, Definition 3.3.15]
)
.

Let
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])=(V,T,S^{1},\dotsc,S^{N},[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot])
be an
N_{W}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra (
1.2.3
) and
\mu\colon V\otimes V\to V
be an even linear map. We denote
ab\coloneqq\mu(a\otimes b)
for
a,b\in V
. A tuple
(V,\nabla,[\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot],\mu)
is called a non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra (non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY VA for short) if it satisfies the following conditions:

1

For any
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle T(ab)=(Ta)b+a(Tb),\quad S^{i}(ab)=(S^{i}a)b+(-1)^{p(a)}a(S^{i}b)\quad(i\in[N]).

2

(quasi-commutativity) For any
a,b\in V
,

\displaystyle ab-(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}ba=\int_{-T}^{0}d\Lambda[a_{\Lambda}b].

3

(quasi-associativity) For any
a,b,c\in V
,

\displaystyle(ab)c-a(bc)=\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}d\Lambda a\Bigr)[b_{\Lambda}c]+(-1)^{p(a)p(b)}\Bigl(\int_{0}^{T}d\Lambda b\Bigr)[a_{\Lambda}c].

4

(Wick formula) For any
a,b,c\in V
,

\displaystyle[a_{\Lambda}bc]=[a_{\Lambda}b]c+(-1)^{(p(a)+\overline{N})p(b)}b[a_{\Lambda}c]+\int_{0}^{\lambda}d\Gamma[[a_{\Lambda}b]_{\Gamma}c],

where
\Gamma
is an additional
(1|N)_{W}
-supervariable.

For simplicity, we say
(V,\nabla)
, or more simply
V
, is a non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra. The map
\mu
is called the multiplication of
V
.

Definition 1.2.5
.

A non-unital
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra
V
is called an
N_{W}=N
SUSY vertex algebra if there exists an even element
\ket{0}\in V
such that
a\ket{0}=\ket{0}a=a
for all
a\in V
.

We close this subsection by a brief comment on

SUSY vertex algebras.

) of the

-supervariables

with those of the

-supervariables:

(1.2.7)

\displaystyle\lambda_{\alpha}\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\beta}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\lambda_{\alpha}\theta_{\beta}^{i}-\theta_{\beta}^{i}\lambda_{\alpha}=0,\quad\theta_{\alpha}^{i}\theta_{\beta}^{j}+\theta_{\beta}^{j}\theta_{\alpha}^{i}=-2\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\delta_{i,j}\lambda_{\alpha},

and replacing the commutative superalgebras
\mathcal{H}_{W}
by the following superalgebra

.

Definition 1.2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{K}
be the
\mathbb{K}
-superalgebra generated by an even generator

and odd generators

(i\in[N])
with relations

\displaystyle TS^{i}-S^{i}T=0,\quad S^{i}S^{j}+S^{j}S^{i}=2\delta_{i,j}T\quad(i,j\in[N]).

Then we have the notion of an
N_{K}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra and that of an
N_{K}=N
SUSY vertex algebra.

Let
(V,\nabla,\{\cdot_{\Lambda}\cdot\})
be an
N_{W}=N
SUSY Lie conformal algebra and
\mu\colon V\otimes V\to V

ab\coloneqq\mu(a\otimes b)

Appendix A
Poisson cohomology bicomplex and finite cohomology complex

The purpose of this

is to give a remark on the finite operad

[
BDHK19
, §10.5]
, and the content is logically independent of the main text. Hereafter we use the calligraphy symbol
\mathcal{P}^{\textup{cl}}
for the finite operad for consistency with the main text.

By the theory of algebraic operads (see
[
LV12
]
for example), a Poisson algebra structure on a given linear space
V
corresponds bijectively to an operad morphism
\alpha\colon\oPoi\to\oHom_{V}
from the Poisson operad
\oPoi

[
LV12
, §13.3]
to the endomorphism operad
\oHom_{V}
.
The Poisson operad
\oPoi
is a binary homogeneous quadratic operad,
and can be constructed out of the Lie operad
\oLie
and the commutative operad
\oCom
by means of a distributive law:
\oPoi=\oCom\circ\oLie
.
As an
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-module, we have
\oPoi(n)=(\oLie\circ\oCom)(n)\simeq\oAss(n)=\mathbb{K}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
, where
\oAss
denotes the associative operad.

By the operadic cohomology theory
[
LV12
, Chap. 6]
, given a Poisson algebra
A
corresponding to an operad morphism
\alpha\colon\oPoi\to\oHom_{V}
, we have the Andre-Quillen type cohomology complex with differential induced by
\alpha
(see
section

A.1.1
for a brief review).
This complex has a complicated nature, but by the work of Fresse
[
F06
]
, it has a bicomplex structure
C^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)
whose vertical and horizontal complexes are essentially the same as the Chevalley-Eilenberg (Lie algebra) cohomology complex and the Harrison (commutative algebra) cohomology complex, respectively. See
section

A.1.3
for an explanation.

In
[
BDHK19
, §10.5]
, it is argued that a Poisson algebra structure
A
on a linear space
V
corresponds bijectively to an operad morphism
X\colon\oLie\to\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}
from the Lie operad to what is called the finite operad. Then, we have the Chevalley-Eilenberg-type cohomology complex
\mathfrak{g}(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{A}})
with differential induced by
X
.

So we may ask how the two cohomology complexes
C^{\bullet,\bullet}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)
and
\mathfrak{g}(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{A}})
relate.
In
A.3.2
, we will show that
\mathfrak{g}(\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{A}})
has a bicomplex structure, and the two bicomplexes coincide up to vertical shift.

We will work over a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic
0
, and all the objects (linear spaces, algebras and so on) are defined over
\mathbb{K}
unless otherwise stated.
For
\mathbb{K}
-modules
V
and
W
(i.e., linear spaces over
\mathbb{K}
), we simplify the notation as
\Hom(V,W)\coloneqq\Hom_{\mathbb{K}}(V,W)
,

and so on.

A.1.
Poisson cohomology bicomplex

A.1.1.
Poisson cohomology

Let
A=(A,-\cdot-)
be a commutative algebra with multiplication
-\cdot-\colon A\otimes A\to A
.
A
Poisson bracket
on
A
is an antisymmetric biderivation
\{-,-\}\colon A\otimes A\to A
which satisfies the Jacobi relation. A
Poisson algebra
is a commutative algebra equipped with a Poisson bracket. Thus, a Poisson algebra
(A,-\cdot-,\{-,-\})
has both a structure
(A,-\cdot-)
of a commutative algebra and a structure
(A,\{-,-\})
of a Lie algebra.

Let
A=(A,-\cdot-,[-,-])
be a Poisson algebra.
A
Poisson
A
-module
is a module
M
over the commutative algebra
(A,-\cdot-)
equipped with an antisymmetric bilinear map
[-,-]\colon M\otimes A\oplus A\otimes M\to M
which is a biderivation with respect to the algebra action
A\to\End(M)
, making
M
a representation of the Lie algebra
(A,[-,-])
.
We also have the natural notion of
morphisms of Poisson
A
-modules
, and the corresponding category
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}_{A}
of Poisson
A
-modules.
There exists an associative algebra
U_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A)
, called the
enveloping algebra of the Poisson algebra
A
, such that
\mathsf{Mod}^{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}_{A}
is equivalent to the category of left
U_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A)
-modules.
See
[
F06
, 1.1.2–1.1.4]
for the detail, and also
[
LV12
, 12.3.1–12.3.4]
for the general argument applicable to algebras over any operad.

Next, we briefly recall the Poisson cohomology, following
[
F06
, 1.2]
, which gives a Poisson analogue of the André-Quillen (co)homology theory.
See also
[
LV12
, 12.3]
which can be applied to an arbitrary operad.
Let
A
be a Poisson algebra, and
M
be a Poisson
A
-module.
We say that a linear map
d\colon A\to M
is a
Poisson derivation
if it is a derivation with respect to both the multiplication and the Poisson bracket.
We denote by
\Der_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,M)
the module of Poisson derivations
A\to M
.
Roughly speaking, the Poisson cohomology is defined to be the “derived functor of
A\mapsto\Der_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,M)
”.
More precisely, there exists a Poisson
A
-module
\Omega_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}^{1}(A)
such that
\Der_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,M)=\Hom_{U_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A)}(\Omega_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}^{1}(A),M)
for any Poisson
A
-module
M
.
We then replace
A
by its quasi-free resolution
R
,
and define the
p
-th Poisson cohomology of
A
with coefficients in
M
is defined to be

(A.1.1)

\displaystyle H^{p}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,M)\coloneqq H^{p}\bigl(\Hom_{U_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(R)}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(R),M)^{\bullet}\bigr).

Here, a quasi-free resolution of
A
is a dg Poisson algebra
R=(R_{\bullet},\partial,-\cdot-,[-,-])
equipped with a surjective quasi-isomorphism
R\to A
whose underlying graded Poisson algebra
(R_{\bullet},-\cdot-,[-,-])
is the reduced free graded Poisson algebra
\overline{P}(V)
of some submodule
V\subset R
stable under the derivation
\partial\colon R_{\bullet}\to R_{\bullet-1}
.
The reduced free Poisson algebra
\overline{\oP}(V)
is the augmentation ideal of the free Poisson algebra
\oP(V)
, which is given by
\oP(V)=\clS\bigl(\clL(V)\bigr)
, the symmetric algebra of the free Lie algebra
\clL(V)
of the module
V
.
Back to (
A.1.1
), the module
\Hom_{U_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(R)}(\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(R),M)
has a grading induced by the homological grading
R_{\bullet}
, and is equipped with a differential induced by
\partial
. Thus, it is a cohomological complex, and we can take the
p
-th cohomology.

A.1.2.
Eulerian decomposition

As a preliminary of the construction explained in the next
Section

A.1.3
, we recall the
Eulerian
(also called
Hodge-type
)
decomposition
of the Hochschild complex of a commutative algebra
[
GS87
,
Lo89
]
.
We follow the explanation in
[
Lo98
, §4.5]
and
[
LV12
, §1.3.5]

For a linear space
V
, we denote by
\clT^{c}=\clT^{c}(V)
the
tensor coalgebra
.
As a module, it is given by
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}V^{\otimes n}
.
We denote the naturally
\mathbb{N}
-grading as
\clT^{c}_{n}\coloneqq V^{\otimes n}

We denote its element as

(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\coloneqq v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n}\in V^{\otimes n}
.
The comultiplication is given by the
deconcatenation
:

\Delta(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{n}(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{i})\otimes(v_{i-1},\dotsc,v_{n})\in\clT^{c}\otimes\clT^{c}.

The coalgebra
\clT^{c}
has a commutative Hopf algebra structure whose multiplication is given by the
shuffle product

\mu
:

\mu\bigl((v_{1}\dotsm v_{m})\otimes(v_{m+1},\dotsc,v_{m+n})\bigr)\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in\Sh_{m,n}}(-1)^{\sigma}v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\dotsm v_{\sigma^{-1}(m+n)},

where
\Sh_{m,n}
is the subgroup of the
(m+n)
-th symmetric group
\mathfrak{S}_{m+n}
consisting of
(m,n)
-shuffles
, i.e.,

(A.1.2)

\displaystyle\Sh_{m,n}\coloneqq\{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{m+n}\mid\sigma(1)<\dotsb<\sigma(m),\,\sigma(m+1)<\dotsb<\sigma(m+n)\}.

We denote the unit and counit by
u
and
\varepsilon
, respectively.
See
[
LV12
, §1.2]
for their precise definitions.

Using the Hopf algebra
(\clT^{c},\mu,\Delta,u,\varepsilon)
, we define the
convolution
of two linear endomorphisms
f,g\in\End(\clT^{c})
to be

f*g\coloneqq\mu\circ(f\otimes g)\circ\Delta.

If
f
and
g
are algebra endomorphism, then
f*g
is also an algebra endomorphism.
We also note that if
f
is an algebra endomorphism with
f(1)=0
for
1\in\mathbb{K}
, then
f^{*k}\coloneqq f*\dotsb*f
(
k
times) is
0
when restricted to
V^{\otimes n}
for
n<k
.

Let us consider the iterative convolution
\id^{*k}
.
It is an algebra endomorphism of
\clT^{c}
, and we have
\id^{*k}=\mu^{k}\circ\Delta^{k}
, where we denoted by
\mu^{k}\colon V^{\otimes k}\to V
and
\Delta^{k}\colon V\to V^{\otimes k}
the iterative composition of
\mu
’s and
\Delta
’s, respectively.
Note that
\mu^{2}=\mu
and
\Delta^{2}=\Delta
in our convention.
Now we define

e^{(1)}\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{k}\frac{\id^{*k}}{k}.

Due to the equality
(\id-u\varepsilon)(1)=0
for
1\in\mathbb{K}
, the above remark claims that
e^{(1)}
is a well-defined algebra endomorphism on
\clT^{c}
.
We further define

e^{(p)}\coloneqq\frac{1}{p!}(e^{(1)})^{*p},\quad e^{(p)}_{n}\coloneqq\left.e^{(p)}\right|_{\clT^{c}_{n}}.

We have
e^{(0)}_{0}=1
and
e^{(p)}_{n}=0
for
p>n
.

By a general argument
[
Lo98
, 4.5.3 Proposition]
, the elements
e^{(p)}_{n}\in\End\bigl(\clT^{c}_{n}\bigr)
enjoys the following properties.

1

e^{(1)}_{n}+\dotsb+e^{(n)}_{n}=\id
.

2

e^{(p)}_{n}e^{(j)}_{n}=\delta_{i,j}e_{n}^{(p)}
, i.e, they are orthogonal idempotents.

These elements are called the
Eulerian idempotents
.

Since each Eulerian idempotent
a=e^{(p)}_{n}
(
p=1,\dotsc,n
) acts on the homogeneous component
\clT^{c}_{n}=V^{\otimes n}
, the image of
(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})\in V^{\otimes n}
is of the form
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}a(\sigma)\sigma.(v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n})
, and we can identify
a
with
\sum_{\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n}}a(\sigma)\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
.
Under this identification, we have
e^{(p)}_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
(
i=1,\dotsc,n
), for which there is known an explicit formula using the descents of a permutation.
We also have
e^{(p)}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}e_{n}^{(p)}\in\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
,
regarded as a completion of
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{Q}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
,
We refer to
[
Lo98
, §4.5.5]
for the detail.

Now we can explain the decomposition of Hochschild cochain complex of a commutative algebra.
Let
A
be a commutative algebra, and
M
be a symmetric
A
-bimodule, i.e., an
A
-bimodule satisfying the condition
a.m=m.a
for any
a\in A
and
m\in M
.
We also denote by
\Sigma
the suspension of a homologically graded module.
Thus
(\Sigma V)_{p}=V_{p-1}
for
p\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Let

(A.1.3)

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}^{\bullet}(A,M)\coloneqq\Hom(\clT^{c}(\Sigma A),M\bigr)

be the Hochschild cochain complex. Each graded component is

C_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}^{n}(A,M)\simeq\Hom(A^{\otimes n},M)
(
n\in\mathbb{N}
),
and the coboundary map
b\colon C_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}^{n}(A,M)\to C_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}^{n+1}(A,M)
is given by

(A.1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(bf)(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n+1})\coloneqq a_{1}f(a_{2},\dotsc,a_{n+1})&+\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}f(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{i}a_{i+1},\dotsc,a_{n+1})\\
&+(-1)^{n+1}f(a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n})a_{n+1}.\end{split}

Then the Eulerian idempotents
e_{n}^{(p)}
induces the following decomposition of
C^{\bullet}(A,M)
, established by
[
GS87
,
Lo89
]
. See also
[
F06
, 4.2]
for an explanation.

Fact A.1.1
.

The Hochschild cochain complex of a commutative algebra
A
with coefficients in a symmetric
A
-bimodule
M
has a decomposition into subcomplexes

C_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}^{\bullet}(A,M)=\bigoplus_{s\in\mathbb{N}}C_{(p)}^{\bullet}(A,M),\quad C_{(p)}^{\bullet}(A,M)\coloneqq\Hom(e^{(p)}\clT^{c}(\Sigma A),M).

Furthermore, we have

(A.1.5)

\displaystyle C_{(p)}^{\bullet}(A,M)\simeq\Hom\bigl(\clS_{p}\bigl(\clL^{c}(\Sigma A)\bigr),M\bigr)

for each
p\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\clL^{c}(\Sigma A)
is the cofree Lie coalgebra of the graded module
\Sigma A
, and
\clS=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}\clS_{p}
denotes the free commutative algebra functor, i.e., the symmetric tensor product.
In particular, the
p=1
part is isomorphic to the Harrison cochain complex:

(A.1.6)

\displaystyle C_{(1)}^{\bullet}(A,M)\simeq\Hom(\clL^{c}(\Sigma A),M)=C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}(A,M).

For later use, let us describe the Harrison (co)chain complex explicitly. Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
, and for
r=1,\dotsc,n
, we set
s_{r,n-r}\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in\Sh(r,n-r)}\sgn(\sigma)\sigma\in\mathbb{Z}[\mathfrak{S}_{n}]
, which acts on
A^{\otimes n}
by permuting tensor factors. Then we define

\textup{ch}_{n}(A)\coloneqq A^{\otimes n}/\langle s_{r,n-r}(a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n})\mid r=1,\dotsc,n,\,a_{i}\in A\rangle_{\text{$A$-mod}}.

We will denote by
[a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n}]\in\textup{ch}_{n}(A)
the element associated to
a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n}\in A^{\otimes n}
. Now, for an
A
-module
M
, we set

(A.1.7)

\displaystyle\textup{ch}_{n}(A;M)\coloneqq\textup{ch}_{n}(A)\otimes M.

The graded module
\bigoplus_{n>0}\textup{ch}_{n}(A;M)
is equipped with the boundary map
\partial\colon\textup{ch}_{n}(A;M)\to\textup{ch}_{n-1}(A;M)
defined by

(A.1.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\partial_{n}([a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n}]\otimes m)\coloneqq[a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n-1}]\otimes a_{n}.m&+\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}[a_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{i}a_{i+1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n}]\otimes m\\
&+(-1)^{n}[a_{2}\otimes\dotsb\otimes a_{n}]\otimes a_{1}.m.\end{split}

The obtained homology complex
(\textup{ch}_{\bullet}(A;M),\partial)
is the Harrison chain complex.
On the other hand, the Harrison cochain complex
C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}(A,M)
is given by

(A.1.9)

\displaystyle C^{n}_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}(A,M)\coloneqq\Hom(\textup{ch}_{n}(A),M)

with the coboundary map
d\colon C^{n}_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}(A,M)\to C^{n+1}_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}(A,M)
being the same one with the Hochschild coboundary map (
A.1.4
).

Remark A.1.2
.

Some comments on references are in order.

[

]
using monotone permutations, which are also used in
[

]
. On the other hand, the cofree Lie coalgebra is constructed as the indecomposables of the tensor coalgebra with respect to the shuffle product (see

for example). These two objects are shown to be equivalent in

]
. The above description (

) and (

.

A.1.3.
Poisson cohomology bicomplex

The Poisson cohomology complex is introduced in
[

]
to calculate the Poisson cohomology (

, §1.3]

Let
A=(A,-\cdot-,[-,-])
be a Poisson algebra, and
M
be a Poisson

Then

can be regarded as a symmetric module over the underlying commutative algebra
A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}\coloneqq(A,-\cdot-)
, and we have the Hochschild cochain complex

Recall the Eulerian decomposition of the Hochschild cochain complex:

C^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{A}\mathit{ssoc}}(A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}},M)=\bigoplus_{p\in\mathbb{N}}C^{\bullet}_{(p)}(A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}},M),\quad C^{\bullet}_{(p)}(A,M)\simeq\Hom(\clS_{p}\bigl(\clL^{c}(\Sigma A)\bigr),M)^{\bullet}.

For

, we set

We denote the restriction of the Hochschild coboundary map (

) by the same symbols as

By

, it gives the vertical differential of the double complex.

In order to introduce the horizontal differential, we need to recall the Chevalley-Eilenberg cochain complex of a Lie algebra. Let
L=(L,[-,-])
be a Lie algebra, and

be an

-module with
[-,-]\colon L\otimes M\to M

(C_{\mathcal{L}{\kern-0.40974pt}\mathit{ie}}^{\bullet}(L,M),\delta_{\textup{CE}})
is given by

(A.1.10)

\displaystyle C_{\mathcal{L}{\kern-0.40974pt}\mathit{ie}}^{n}(L,M)\coloneqq\Hom(\clS_{n}(\Sigma L),M),

and for

and

,

(A.1.11)

\displaystyle\delta_{\textup{CE}}(f)(u_{0}\dotsm u_{n})=\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}[u_{i},f(u_{0}\dotsm\widehat{u_{i}}\dotsm u_{n})]+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq n}(-1)^{i+j}f([u_{i},u_{j}]u_{0}\dotsm\widehat{u_{i}}\dotsm\widehat{u_{j}}\dotsm u_{n}).

By
[
F06
, 1.3.5]
, the Poisson bracket on
A
induced a
(-1)
-shifted Lie bracket
[-,-]
on
\clL^{c}(\Sigma A)
, i.e.,

is a graded Lie algebra.
Also, by
[
F06
, 1.3.7]
, the Poisson

-module structure on

induces a module structure over the
(-1)
-shifted Lie algebra
(\clL^{c}(\Sigma A),[-,-])

\mathbb{N}
-grading

\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}V(n)=\bigoplus_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\gr^{r}\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}(n)

on the finite operad
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}
by

(A.3.2)

\displaystyle\gr^{r}\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}(n)\coloneqq\{f\in\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}(n)\mid f^{\Gamma}=0\text{ for any $n$-quiver $\Gamma$ with $\#E(\Gamma)\neq r$}\}.

Note that we have
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}(n)=\bigoplus_{r=0}^{n-1}\gr^{r}\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}(n)
by the cycle conditions. Now
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}
is a graded operad, i.e., the
\mathfrak{S}
-module structure and the composition map
\gamma
respect the grading.

Next, using the notation in
Section

A.2
, let us choose and fix a Poisson algebra
A=(V,-\cdot-,\{-,-\})=(V,X)
with underlying linear space
V
and
X\in\MC(\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn}}_{V})
. For simplicity, let us denote by

\mathfrak{g}\coloneqq\mathfrak{g}^{\textup{fn},X}_{A}

the cohomology complex (
A.3.1
). By (
A.2.10
), we have

\mathfrak{g}^{n-1}=\left\{f\in\Hom\bigl((\mathbb{K}\oQ(n)/R(n))\otimes V^{\otimes n},V\bigr)\mid\forall\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},\ f^{\sigma}=\sgn(\sigma)f\right\},

and by
A.2.1
, the space
\mathbb{K}\oQ(n)/R(n)
has a basis
\clL(n)
consisting of disconnected unions of lines
\Lambda_{I}
where
I
runs over partitions of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
. Then the grading (
A.3.2
) on
\mathop{\mathcal{P}^{\textup{fn}}_{V}}
induces the following double grading on
\mathfrak{g}
.

Lemma A.3.3
.

For a partition
I=\{i^{1}_{1},\dotsc,i^{1}_{m_{1}}\}\sqcup\{i^{2}_{1},\dotsc,i^{2}_{m_{2}}\}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup\{i^{p}_{1},\dotsc,i^{p}_{m_{p}}\}
of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, we denote
\pi_{0}(I)\coloneqq p
. Then (
A.3.2
) induces the
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
-grading
\mathfrak{g}=\bigoplus_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}}\mathfrak{g}^{p,q}
by

(A.3.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{p,q}\coloneqq\{f\in\mathfrak{g}^{p+q}\mid f^{\Lambda_{I}}=0\text{ for any partition $I$ of $\{1,\dotsc,n\}$ with $\pi_{0}(I)\neq p$}\}.

We have
\mathfrak{g}^{n-1}=\bigoplus_{p=1}^{n}\mathfrak{g}^{p,n-1-p}=\mathfrak{g}^{1,n-2}\oplus\dotsb\oplus\mathfrak{g}^{n,-1}
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
.

Proof.

The claim follows from
A.2.1
and the fact that
\pi_{0}(I)=p
if and only if
\#E(\Lambda_{I})=n-p
for a partition
I
of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
.
∎

We can identify the doubly-graded linear spaces
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet,\bullet}
and
C_{\textup{Pois}}^{\bullet,\bullet}
in the following way.

Lemma A.3.4
.

We have

(A.3.4)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}^{p,q}\simeq C^{p,q+1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)

as linear spaces for any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

We may assume
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and
q+1\in\mathbb{N}
. Let us denote by
\textup{ch}_{\bullet}\coloneqq\clL^{c}(\Sigma A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}})_{\bullet}
the Harrison chain complex of the commutative algebra
A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}}=(V,-\cdot-)
.
Then,
A.1.3
claims

\displaystyle C^{p,q+1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)=\Hom\bigl(\clS_{p}(\textup{ch}_{\bullet}),V\bigr)^{p+q+1}=\bigoplus_{J}\Hom(\textup{ch}_{J},V),

where the summation index
J=\{j_{1},\dotsc,j_{l}\}
runs over
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
,
j_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
(
k=1,\dotsc,l
),
\sum_{k=1}^{l}j_{k}=p
and
\sum_{k=1}^{l}kj_{k}=p+q+1
, and

\textup{ch}_{J}\coloneqq\wedge^{j_{1}}\textup{ch}_{1}\otimes\wedge^{j_{2}}\textup{ch}_{2}\otimes\dotsb\otimes\wedge^{j_{l}}\textup{ch}_{l}.

Such
J
is in one-to-one correspondence with the equivalent class of a partition
I
of
\{1,\dotsc,p+q+1\}
with
\pi_{0}(I)=p
under the natural
\mathfrak{S}_{n}
-action. Recalling that we have
\textup{ch}_{\bullet}\simeq\clL^{c}(\Sigma A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}})\simeq e^{(1)}T^{c}(\Sigma A_{\mathcal{C}\!\mathit{om}})
by (
A.1.6
), we find that this bijection induces

\Hom(\textup{ch}_{J},V)\xrightarrow{\,\sim\,}\left\{f=f^{I}\in\Hom(\mathbb{K}\Lambda_{I}\otimes V^{\otimes n},V)\mid\forall\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{n},\ f^{\sigma}=\sgn(\sigma)f\right\}.

Since
\clL(n)
is a basis of
\mathbb{K}\oQ(n)/R(n)
, we see that
\bigoplus_{J}\Hom(\textup{ch}_{J},V)
is isomorphic to the right hand side of (
A.3.3
). Thus we have the conclusion.
∎

As for the differential
d_{X}=[X,-]
of
\mathfrak{g}
, we have the following
A.3.5
,
which will finish the proof of
A.3.2
.

Lemma A.3.5
.

Using the basis (
A.2.5
), we decompose
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{1}
as

X=X^{\{1\}\sqcup\{2\}}+X^{\{1,2\}},\quad X^{\{1\}\sqcup\{2\}}\in\mathfrak{g}^{2,-1},\quad X^{\{1,2\}}\in\mathfrak{g}^{1,0}.

Then the induced decomposition

d_{X}=d_{h}+d_{v}\quad\text{with }d_{h}\coloneqq[X^{\{1\}\sqcup\{2\}},-],\quad d_{v}\coloneqq[X^{\{1,2\}},-]

satisfies

d_{h}(\mathfrak{g}^{p,q})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{p+1,q},\quad d_{v}(\mathfrak{g}^{p,q})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{p,q+1}.

Moreover, under the identification
\mathfrak{g}^{p,q}\simeq C^{p,q+1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)
in (
A.3.4
) and
X_{1}=\{-,-\}
,
X_{2}=-\cdot-
in (
A.2.11
), we have

(\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet,\bullet},d_{h},d_{v})\simeq(C_{\textup{Pois}}^{\bullet,\bullet+1}(A,A),\delta_{\textup{CE}},d)

as bicomplexes.

Before starting the proof, we note that the part
d_{v}=d
is due to
[
BDKV21
, Lemma 4.10]
(c.f.
A.3.1
).

Proof.

For simplicity, let us denote
X_{h}\coloneqq X^{\{1\}\sqcup\{2\}}
and
X_{v}\coloneqq X^{\{1,2\}}
.
We want to calculate
d_{i}(Y)=[X_{i},Y]=X\square Y-(-1)^{n-1}Y\square X_{i}
for
i=h,v
,
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{p,q}\subset\mathfrak{g}^{n-1}
with
n\coloneqq p+q+1
. By
A.2.1
, we may assume
Y^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{I}
where
I
is a partition of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
with
\pi_{0}(I)=p
. Let us set

I=I_{1}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup I_{p},\quad I_{a}\coloneqq\{i^{a}_{1},\dotsc,i^{a}_{m_{a}}\}\quad(a=1,\dotsc,p).

Recalling the description (
A.2.8
) of the pre-Lie product
\square
,
we find that
X_{i}\square Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{n}
is expressed as

X_{i}\square Y=\sum_{\sigma\in\Sh(n,1)}(X_{i}\circ_{1}Y)^{\sigma^{-1}},

where each
\sigma\in\Sh(n,1)
can be written as

\sigma=\begin{pmatrix}1&\cdots&&\cdots&n&n+1\\
1&\cdots&\widehat{j}&\cdots&n+1&j\end{pmatrix}\quad(1\leq j\leq n+1).

Recalling the parity change
\pc
in
\mathfrak{g}
(c.f. (
A.2.10
)) and unfolding the infinitesimal composition
\circ_{1}
, we have

(A.3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(X_{i}\circ_{1}Y)^{\sigma^{-1}}(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1})&=\sgn(\sigma)\cdot\bigl(X_{i}(Y\otimes\id)\bigr)(v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{\sigma(n+1)})\\
&=\sgn(\sigma)\cdot X_{i}\bigl(Y(v_{\sigma(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma(2)}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{\sigma(n)})\otimes v_{\sigma(n+1)}\bigr)\end{split}

for
v_{1},\dotsc,v_{n+1}\in V
. Similarly, we have
Y\square X_{i}\in\mathfrak{g}^{n}
with

Y\square X_{i}=\sum_{\tau\in\Sh(2,n-1)}(Y\circ_{1}X_{i})^{\tau^{-1}},

and each
\tau\in\Sh(2,n-1)
can be written as

\tau=\begin{pmatrix}1&2&\cdots&&\cdots&&\cdots&n+1\\
j&k&\cdots&\widehat{j}&\cdots&\widehat{k}&\cdots&n+1\end{pmatrix}\quad(1\leq j<k\leq n+1).

The infinitesimal composition
(Y\circ_{1}X_{i})^{\tau^{-1}}
is given by

(A.3.6)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&(Y\circ_{1}X_{i})^{\tau^{-1}}(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1})\\
&=\sgn(\tau)\cdot\bigl(Y(X_{i}\otimes\underbrace{\id\otimes\dotsb\otimes\id}_{n-1})\bigr)(v_{\tau(1)}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{\tau(n+1)}\bigr)\\
&=\sgn(\tau)\cdot Y\bigl(X_{i}(v_{\tau(1)}\otimes v_{\tau(2)})\otimes v_{\tau(3)}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{\tau(n+1)}\bigr).\end{split}

Now we focus on
X_{v}=X^{\{1,2\}}
. By the assumption
Y^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{I}
, and by the description of the cocomposition map (
A.2.2
) and (
A.2.4
), we have
(X_{v}\circ_{1}Y)^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{J^{v}(a)}
,
a=1,\dotsc,n
, where

J^{v}(a)
is a partition of
\{1,\dotsc,n+1\}
obtained from
I
by replacing the entry
a
by
a
and
n+1
. In the case
\Gamma=\Lambda_{J^{v}(a)}
, we have

(X_{v}\circ_{1}Y)^{\Lambda_{J^{v}(a)}}=\pm X_{v}(Y\otimes\id).

Similarly, we have
(Y\circ_{1}X_{v})^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{K^{v}}
, where
K^{v}
is a partition of
\{1,\dotsc,n+1\}
obtained from
I
by replacing the entry
1
by
1
and
2
, and the entries
2,\dotsc,n
by
3,\dotsc,n+1
, respectively. If
\Gamma=\Lambda_{K^{v}}
, then

(Y\circ_{1}X_{v})^{\Lambda_{K^{v}}}=Y(X_{v}\otimes\id^{\otimes(n-1)}).

Thus we find
(X_{v}\square Y)^{\Gamma}=0
and
(Y\square X_{v})^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{g}^{p,q+1}
, which yields
d_{v}(\mathfrak{g}^{p,q})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{p,q+1}
.
Moreover, by (
A.3.5
) and (
A.3.6
), and after the careful calculation on the sign shown in
[
BDKV21
, Lemma 4.10]
, we have

\displaystyle d_{v}(Y)(v_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes v_{n+1})=(-1)^{n+1}\Bigl(X_{v}\bigl(v_{1}\otimes Y(v_{2}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(-1)^{j}Y\bigl(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes X_{v}(v_{j}\otimes v_{j+1})\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1}\bigr)+(-1)^{n+1}X_{v}\bigl(Y(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\otimes v_{n})\otimes v_{n+1}\bigr)\Bigr).

Under the identification
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet,\bullet}\simeq C^{\bullet,\bullet+1}_{\mathcal{P}\!\mathit{ois}}(A,A)
and
X_{v}=-\cdot-
,
this is nothing but the Harrison (or Hochschild) coboundary map, i.e.,
the vertical differential
d
in
C_{\textup{Pois}}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A,A)
.

Next, for
X_{h}=X^{\{1\}\sqcup\{2\}}
, we have
(X_{h}\circ_{1}Y)^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{J^{h}}
with
J^{h}\coloneqq J\sqcup\{n+1\}
, and

(X_{h}\circ_{1}Y)^{\Lambda_{J^{h}}}=X_{h}(Y\otimes\id).

We also have
(Y\circ_{1}X_{h})^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma=\Lambda_{K^{h}}
, where
K^{h}
is the partition of
\{1,\dotsc,n+1\}
given by

K^{h}\coloneqq\{1\}\sqcup I_{1}^{\prime\prime}\sqcup\dotsb\sqcup I_{p}^{\prime\prime},\quad I_{a}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq\{i^{a}_{1}+1,\dotsc,i^{a}_{m_{a}}+1\}\quad(a=1,\dotsc,p),

and in the case
\Gamma=\Lambda_{K^{h}}
, we have

(Y\circ_{1}X_{h})^{\Lambda_{K^{h}}}=Y(X_{h}\otimes\id^{\otimes(n-1)}).

Hence we have
(X_{h}\square Y)^{\Gamma}=0
and
(Y\square X_{h})^{\Gamma}=0
unless
\Gamma\in\mathfrak{g}^{p+1,q}
, which yields
d_{h}(\mathfrak{g}^{p,q})\subset\mathfrak{g}^{p+1,q}
.
By (
A.3.5
) and (
A.3.6
), we have

\displaystyle d_{h}(Y)(v_{1}\otimes\dotsc\otimes v_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-1}X_{h}\bigl(Y(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\widehat{v}_{j}\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1})\otimes v_{j}\bigr)

\displaystyle-(-1)^{n-1}\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n+1}(-1)^{j+k}Y\bigl(X_{h}(v_{j}\otimes v_{k})\otimes v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\widehat{v}_{j}\dotsb\widehat{v}_{k}\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1}\bigr)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{n}\biggl(\sum_{j=1}^{n+1}(-1)^{j-1}X_{h}\bigl(v_{j}\otimes Y(v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\widehat{v}_{j}\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1})\bigr)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq j<k\leq n+1}(-1)^{j+k}Y\bigl(X_{h}(v_{j}\otimes v_{k})\otimes v_{1}\otimes\dotsb\widehat{v}_{j}\dotsb\widehat{v}_{k}\dotsb\otimes v_{n+1}\bigr)\biggr),

and under the identification
\mathfrak{g}^{\bullet,\bullet}\simeq C_{\textup{Pois}}^{\bullet,\bullet+1}(A,A)
and
X_{h}=\{-,-\}
, this is nothing but the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary, i.e., the horizontal differential
\delta_{\textup{CE}}
in
C_{\textup{Pois}}^{\bullet,\bullet}(A,A)
.
∎

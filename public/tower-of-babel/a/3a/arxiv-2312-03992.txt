Analytic Approach to the Non-Preemptive Markovian Priority Queue
Josef Zuk, David Kirszenblat

Abstract
A new approach is developed for the joint queue-length distribution of the two-level non-preemptive Markovian priority queue that allows explicit and exact results to be obtained. Marginal distributions are derived for the general multi-level problem. The results are based on a representation of the joint queue-length probability mass function as a single-variable complex contour integral, that reduces to a real integral on a finite interval arising from a cut on the real axis. Both numerical quadrature rules and exact finite sums, involving Legendre polynomials and their generalization, are presented for the joint and marginal distributions. A high level of accuracy is demonstrated across the entire ergodic region. Relationships are established with the waiting-time distributions. Asymptotic behaviour in the large queue-length regime is extracted.

Introduction

This work is concerned with both algebraically exact and highly accurate numerical evaluations
of the joint and marginal queue length distributions for the non-preemptive
Markovian priority queue, based on a representation of the joint queue-length
probability mass function (PMF) as a single-variable complex contour integral,
making use of its pole and cut singularities on the real axis.

There exist numerous real-world applications of the non-preemptive priority queue,
including telecommunications
[
5
]
,
health care
[
1
,
15
,
28
]
,
beam scheduling in phased-array radars
[
21
,
22
]
, and
air traffic control
[
23
]
, to name just a few.
Here, we revisit the two-level problem from a different perspective
to that considered by us in
[
31
]
and the previous literature
cited therein.
The two-level case is amenable to a number of exact analytical approaches
that are not easily accessible to the general multi-level problem.
Furthermore, the two-level problem occupies a distinguished position,
because knowledge of the low-priority marginal queue-length distribution for
the two-level case is sufficient for the evaluation of all the marginal queue-length
distributions in the general problem.
For this reason, the two-level problem justifies some special attention.

The two-level problem was recently discussed in
[
31
]
,
where a detailed survey of previous work was also given.
The most recent work on the problem, prior to our own, appeared in
[
27
]
.
The multi-level problem for an arbitrary number of priority levels was
solved in
[
32
]
.
In that work, an explicit exact expression was derived for the multi-variate
joint queue-length probability generating function (PGF),
but the extraction of the joint and marginal queue-length
PMFs from the PGF was numerical, rather than analytical.
Here, we describe an analytical approach to the two-level problem.
It is, at present, not clear whether an analogous analytic method could be easily
extended to the general multi-level problem.

In the present approach, both the marginal and joint PMFs are represented as
one-dimensional real-valued integrals over a finite interval.
The integral representation is amenable to efficient numerical evaluation
via Gaussian quadrature in an iterative form that allows a desired level of accuracy
to be set
a priori
.
It is equally amenable to an exact evaluation as a finite sum of easily
computable functions that generalize the familiar associated Legendre functions
in a novel way.
Both methods produce highly accurate results for parameter values spanning the entire
ergodic region, including values of the total traffic intensity close to unity.

The analytical structure of the integrals arising here is very similar to that
encountered for the partial busy period of an M/M/
c
queue
[
17
,
30
]
,
and for the waiting-time marginal distributions in
[
6
]
.
In fact, we are able to provide alternative derivations of the
waiting-time marginal distributions,
either from the queue-length marginal PGFs, or from the queue-length marginal PMFs.
In the latter case, we find a new representation as a convergent Laguerre series,
where the Laguerre polynomials carry the time dependence, while the sequence
of coefficients is a binomial transform of the queue-length PMF.

For multi-class non-priority queueing models,

Bertsimas and Mourtzinou 1997
derive a representation of the multi-variate joint PGF
for the queue-length distribution as a multi-dimensional time integral over all the
waiting-time marginals, based on the distributional form of Little’s law.
However, this approach does not extend to the priority queue because the
underlying no-overtaking assumption is violated.
Consequently, in the present problem, the joint queue-length distribution cannot
be derived from the waiting-time marginals.
Here, and in
[
32
]
, a direct derivation of the joint queue-length
distribution is provided.

The integral representations of the marginal and joint PMFs facilitate the
extraction of the asymptotic behaviour in the large queue-length limits;
something that appears to be difficult to achieve from other starting points.
The integral representations are also robust to the inclusion of various
modelling complications.
An example arises in the application to the ambulance ramping problem
[
1
]
.
Here, the priority levels are determined by patient acuity levels,
but patients also fall into separate arrival classes depending on whether they
are entering the hospital emergency department directly from an ambulance, from
an ambulance offload zone or as walk-ins. Each priority level may span a number of
arrival classes, and each arrival class has a distinct mean arrival rate.
This results in a multi-level, multi-class queueing model
with priorities and classes entwined.
The queue-length PMFs acquire a somewhat more complex integral representation,
but the simple quadrature rules derived for the present model remain applicable
and efficient. We shall make use of this in a forthcoming paper.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
,
we introduce an approach to the problem based on a PGF
of a single complex variable that sums the joint distribution only over the low-priority
indices (
i.e.
queue lengths), and we elucidate the analytic structure of the PGF
in the complex plane.
In Section
3
, we derive an integral representation for the low-priority
marginal queue-length distribution, noting that the high-priority marginal is trivially geometric,
and proceed to establish iterative quadrature rules.
We show that the results are related to one of Appell’s bivariate hypergeometric functions.
The asymptotic behaviour in the limit
of large queue length is also derived directly from the integral representation.
In Section
4
, we discuss the marginal waiting-time distributions
for the general multi-level non-preemptive priority problem.
As a by-product of our results on the queue-length marginal distributions, we are able to invoke
the distributional form of Little’s law to reproduce a previously established integral
form for the waiting-time marginals, which may be related to a bivariate confluent
hypergeometric function of Humbert,
as well as deriving a new representation as a Laguerre series.
In Section
5
, we derive an integral representation for the joint
queue-length distribution, and establish the relevant iterative quadrature rules.
In Section
6
, we first show that the low-priority marginal PMF can be
expressed as a finite sum of Legendre polynomials.
For the joint PMF, an exact representation as a finite sum from a family of functions
Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)

is derived. These functions reduce to the Legendre polynomials in the special case

Q_{m}^{0}(\chi)=P_{m}(\chi)
,
and are closely related to the associated Legendre functions when

\absolutevalue{\ell}\leq m
,
but are defined for all integral values of
\ell
.
Various properties of these functions are elucidated.
The section concludes with an efficient and robust numerical implementation of the exact expressions.
In Section
7
, we introduce a number of measures of performance (MOP) to study the
numerical behaviour of the two approaches developed in the present paper, testing them
against one other, against known exact relationships, and comparing with two other algorithms
previously derived for this problem
[
31
]
.
Some concluding remarks follow, and
various technical details appear in the appendices.

2
Probability Generating Function

As in
[
31
]
, we consider a non-preemptive priority queue with

K
priority levels, each with a distinct
Poisson arrival rate
\lambda_{k}
,

k=1,2,\ldots,K

and corresponding level traffic intensity
1
1

1

Consistent with
[
8
,
16
]
,
the notation
\rho_{k}
reserved for
\rho_{k}\equiv\lambda_{k}/\mu
, so that
r_{k}=\rho_{k}/c
.

r_{k}=\lambda_{k}/(c\mu)
,
leading to a total traffic intensity for the aggregation of all arrivals of

r=\sum_{k=1}^{K}r_{k}
.
We adopt the usual convention that smaller priority-level indices
k

represent higher priorities. Thus,
r_{1}
denotes the traffic intensity
associated with the highest priority level.
We assume a common exponential service rate
\mu

among all priority levels.
The number of servers is arbitrary and is denoted by
c
.
For the discussion of the joint queue-distribution, we limit the number of priority levels to

K=2
, and adopt the notation

r_{\text{hi}}\equiv r_{1}
,

r_{\text{lo}}\equiv r_{2}
,
so that

r=r_{\text{hi}}+r_{\text{lo}}
.
We also denote the fraction of high-priority arrivals (abbreviated ‘hifrac’) by

\nu\equiv r_{\text{hi}}/r
,
so that

0\leq\nu\leq 1
.
This allows us to study all possibilities for a given total traffic intensity
r
by
independently varying
\nu
over its full range.

Let

P_{\text{full}}(\ell,m)

denote the unconditional queue-length PMF with
\ell
high-priority
clients and
m
low-priority clients in the queue.
Let

P(\ell,m)

denote the same queue-length PMF conditioned on all servers being busy,
to which we shall refer as the wait-conditional PMF.
These two quantities are related by

P_{\text{full}}(\ell,m)=P_{\text{NW}}\delta_{\ell 0}\delta_{m0}+(1-P_{\text{NW}})P(\ell,m)\;,

(1)

where
P_{\text{NW}}
is the no-wait (or delay) probability.
An expression for its value is derived in Appendix
A
.
The wait-conditional PMF does not directly depend on the
number of servers, but only indirectly through its dependence on the
total traffic intensity.
Therefore, the model for

P(\ell,m)

depends on just two parameters, namely,
r
and
\nu
,
that may be varied independently in the intervals

0\leq r<1
,

0\leq\nu\leq 1
.

Cohen 1956
introduced a PGF with a discrete index
for the unconditional PMF given by

G_{\ell}(z)\equiv\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}P_{\text{full}}(\ell,m)\;.

(2)

The corresponding wait-conditional PGF

g_{\ell}(z)

is related to the full PGF by

G_{\ell}(z)=P_{\text{NW}}\delta_{\ell 0}+(1-P_{\text{NW}})g_{\ell}(z)\;.

(3)

To obtain an expression for

g_{\ell}(z)
,
we introduce

\zeta\equiv\zeta_{\pm}(z)

as the two branches that solve the
z
-dependent quadratic equation

\zeta^{2}-(1+r-r_{\text{lo}}z)\zeta+r_{\text{hi}}=0\;.

(4)

Thus, we can write

\zeta_{\pm}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left[b(z)\pm\sqrt{\Delta(z)}\right]\;,

(5)

where

\Delta(z)\equiv b^{2}(z)-4r_{\text{hi}}\;,\quad b(z)\equiv 1+r-r_{\text{lo}}z\;,

(6)

and it is useful to note the quadratic identities

\zeta_{+}(z)+\zeta_{-}(z)=b(z)\;,\quad\zeta_{+}(z){\cdot}\zeta_{-}(z)=r_{\text{hi}}\;,

(7)

which also imply that

[1-\zeta_{+}(z)]{\cdot}[1-\zeta_{-}(z)]=r_{\text{lo}}(z-1)\;.

(8)

Notable special values are

\zeta_{+}(1)=1
,

\zeta_{-}(1)=r_{\text{hi}}
.
Following
Cohen 1956
, the wait-conditional PGF is given by

g_{\ell}(z)=g_{\text{lo}}(z){\cdot}\left[1-\zeta_{-}(z)\right]\zeta_{-}^{\ell}(z)\;,\quad g_{\text{lo}}(z)=\frac{1-r}{\zeta_{+}(z)-r}\;.

(9)

The quantity

g_{\text{lo}}(z)

represents the PGF for the low-priority marginal distribution, as can be seen from

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}g_{\ell}(z)=g_{\text{lo}}(z)\;.

(10)

These results also follow as a special case of the multi-variate joint PGF
for the general multi-level non-preemptive queue derived in
[
30
]
.

The discriminant

\Delta(z)

can be factorized according to

\Delta(z)=r_{\text{lo}}^{2}(z-x_{+})(z-x_{-})\;,

(11)

with real strictly positive roots

x_{\pm}=1+(1\pm\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}})^{2}/r_{\text{lo}}\;.

(12)

It is useful to introduce their difference

x_{\text{dif}}\equiv x_{+}-x_{-}=4\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}/r_{\text{lo}}\;.

(13)

One should observe that

\Delta(x)<0

for

x_{-}<x<x_{+}
,
which implies the presence of a cut on this finite real interval,
and that

\Delta(1/r)=(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}})^{2}/r^{2}>0
.
Thus, the pole at
x=1/r

does not lie in the open cut interval

(x_{-},x_{+})
.

3
Marginal Distributions

The high and low priority marginal PMFs are given, respectively, by

P_{\text{hi}}(\ell)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}P(\ell,m)\;,\quad P_{\text{lo}}(m)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}P(\ell,m)\;.

(14)

An immediate consequence of (
9
) is the simple geometric form
for the high-priority marginal

P_{\text{hi}}(\ell)=g_{\ell}(1)=(1-r_{\text{hi}})r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell}\;.

(15)

Thus, only the low-priority marginal

p_{n}\equiv P_{\text{lo}}(n)

is of interest for further analysis.
By Cauchy’s integral theorem, we have

p_{n}=\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\frac{1}{z^{n+1}}g_{\text{lo}}(z)\;,

(16)

where the integration contour
\mathcal{C}
is taken to be an anti-clockwise circle
centred on the origin, of radius
\eta
within the radius of convergence of the function

g_{\text{lo}}(z)
,
namely

0<\eta<1/r
.
Our approach is to evaluate this integral directly.

We begin by expanding the contour to infinity, while avoiding the pole and cut
by wrapping around them in the usual way.
However, in order to deform the circular contour out to infinity, we must be able to continue
the integrand to the entire
z
-plane in a way that it remains analytic.
For any given

z\in\mathbb{C}
,
the solution of the corresponding quadratic equation has two distinct branches, but
it is
a priori
arbitrary how to assign the plus and minus signs, that multiply
the discriminant term, to the branches.
The most general form of
\zeta_{\pm}(z)
is

\zeta_{\pm}(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}\left[b(z)\pm\xi(z)\left(\Delta(z)\right)^{1/2}\right]\;,

(17)

where
\xi(z)
is an arbitrary function such that

\xi^{2}(z)\equiv 1
,
and the principal branch of the square-root function is adopted.
Analyticity of
\zeta_{\pm}(z)
everywhere in the complex
z
-plane away from the
positive real axis is achieved by the choice

\xi(z)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}+1&\quad\text{for}&\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z\leq(x_{+}+x_{-})/2\\
-1&\quad\text{for}&\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z>(x_{+}+x_{-})/2\end{array}\right.\;.

(18)

With this definition, we observe that

\zeta_{-}(1/r)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}r&\quad\text{for}&r_{\text{hi}}>r^{2}\\
r_{\text{hi}}/r&\quad\text{for}&r_{\text{hi}}\leq r^{2}\end{array}\right.\;,

(19)

which implies that there is no pole when

r_{\text{hi}}\geq r^{2}
.
The behaviour of
\zeta_{+}(1/r)
is the opposite and follows directly from (
7
).
With the choice in (
18
), we also obtain the uniform asymptotic behaviour

\zeta_{+}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{|z|\to\infty}}-r_{\text{lo}}z\;,\quad\zeta_{-}(z)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{|z|\to\infty}}-\frac{r_{\text{hi}}}{r_{\text{lo}}z}\;,

(20)

which is sufficient to ensure the vanishing of the contour at infinity.

After deformation of the contour,
p_{n}

may be expressed as the sum of a pole contribution and a cut integral

p_{n}=P_{\text{pol}}(n)+P_{\text{cut}}(n)
, where

P_{\text{pol}}(n)=\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}})\left[1-\frac{r(1-r)}{r_{\text{lo}}}\right]{\cdot}(1-r)r^{n-1}\;,

(21)

and

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=\frac{1-r}{2\pi r}\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}\frac{dx}{x^{n+1}}\,\frac{\sqrt{(x_{+}-x)(x-x_{-})}}{x-1/r}\;.

(22)

The change of integration variable
x\mapsto u
,

0\leq u\leq 1
,
according to

x=x_{-}+(x_{+}-x_{-})u\;,

(23)

yields

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=\frac{1-r}{2\pi rx_{\text{dif}}^{n}}\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{(u+a)^{n+1}(u+b)}\;,

(24)

with

a\equiv x_{-}/x_{\text{dif}}\;,\quad b\equiv(x_{-}-1/r)/x_{\text{dif}}\;,\quad x_{\text{dif}}\equiv x_{+}-x_{-}\;,

(25)

so that

1/x_{\text{dif}}=r(a-b)
.
These parameters may also be conveniently expressed in terms of the model inputs as

a=\frac{1+r}{4\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}-\frac{1}{2}\;,\quad b=\frac{1}{4}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}+\frac{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}{r}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\;.

(26)

In terms of Appell’s
F_{1}
bivariate hypergeometric function, we may write

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\text{cut}}(n)&=\frac{1-r}{16rx_{\text{dif}}^{n}}{\cdot}\frac{1}{a^{n+1}b}{\cdot}\frac{1}{B(3/2,3/2)}\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{\left(1+u/a\right)^{n+1}\left(1+u/b\right)}\\
&=\frac{(1-r)r^{n-1}}{16ab}{\cdot}\left(1-b/a\right)^{n}F_{1}(3/2,n+1,1,3;-1/a,-1/b)\;,\end{aligned}

(27)

where it is useful to note that

1-b/a=1/(rx_{-})
,
and with

B(\mu,\nu)

denoting the beta function.
This follows from Picard’s integral representation
[
24
]

F_{1}(\lambda,\rho,\sigma,\lambda+\mu;u,v)=\frac{1}{B(\mu,\lambda)}\int_{0}^{1}dx\,\frac{x^{\lambda-1}(1-x)^{\mu-1}}{(1-ux)^{\rho}(1-vx)^{\sigma}}\;,

(28)

as reproduced in
[
13
, sections 3.211, 9.18]
.
When, as in the present application, the arguments
u,v
are real and negative,
the Appell
F_{1}
-hypergeometric function can be efficiently evaluated by means of a
Gauss-Jacobi quadrature rule
[
10
,
11
]
.
Furthermore, in the present application, where

(\mu,\lambda)=(3/2,3/2)
,
the Gauss-Jacobi quadrature specializes to a Gauss-Chebyshev quadrature of the second kind.
To see this, we make the further change of integration variable

v=2u-1
, which casts the integral into the form

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=C_{n}\int_{-1}^{+1}\frac{dv}{\pi/2}\,\frac{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}{(v+\alpha)^{n+1}(v+\beta)}\;,\quad C_{n}\equiv\frac{1-r}{4r}{\cdot}\left(\frac{2}{x_{\text{dif}}}\right)^{n}\;,

(29)

where we have introduced the parameters

\alpha\equiv 2a+1\;,\quad\beta\equiv 2b+1\;.

(30)

Thus,

\alpha=\frac{1+r}{2\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}\;,\quad\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}+\frac{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}{r}\right)\;.

(31)

It is known that Gauss-Chebyshev quadrature of the second kind
is equivalent to the trapezoidal rule on the unit circle
[
4
]
.
Accordingly, if we set

v=\cos(\pi\tau)
,
so that

0\leq\tau\leq 1
,
and subsequently uniformly discretize the unit interval into
L
sub-intervals
each terminated at the point

\tau_{k}=k/L
,

k=1,2,\ldots,L
,
then we obtain the
L
-point quadrature rule for the cut integral given by

P_{\text{cut}}(n)\simeq C_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{w_{k}}{[\cos(\pi\tau_k)+\alpha]^{n+1}{\cdot}[\cos(\pi\tau_k)+\beta]}\;,\quad w_{k}\equiv(2/L){\cdot}\sin^{2}(\pi\tau_{k})\;.

(32)

This structure mirrors the discussion of the distribution of the partial busy period
for a multi-server Markovian queue found in
[
32
]
.
An alternative way of expressing this, along the lines of
[
32
]
, is

P_{\text{cut}}(n)\simeq\frac{x_{\text{dif}}}{4}{\cdot}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{W_{k}}{\left[x_{\text{dif}}(U_{k}+a)\right]^{n+1}}\;,

(33)

with

U_{k}\equiv\cos^{2}(\pi\tau_{k}/2)\;,\quad W_{k}\equiv\frac{2(1-r)}{rL}{\cdot}\frac{1-U_{k}}{1+b/U_{k}}\;.

(34)

The case

\beta=1
,
equivalently

b=0
,
is problematic for the computation of the integral in (
29
) via the Gauss-Chebyshev
quadrature of the second kind as the denominator vanishes at an integration end-point,
where the weight function also vanishes.
Thus, a removable singularity appears.
At this critical point, which corresponds to parameter choice

r_{\text{hi}}=r^{2}

— the same point at which the pole contribution disappears —
(
29
) may be cast into a form suitable for Gauss-Chebyshev quadrature of the first kind:

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=C_{n}\int_{-1}^{+1}\frac{dv}{\pi/2}\,\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}{\cdot}\frac{1-v}{(v+\alpha)^{n+1}}\;.

(35)

The loss of accuracy away from the critical point but close to it may be addressed by
noting that there is no impediment to evaluating (
29
) by Gauss quadrature of the
first kind in the general case, noting, in particular, that it does not place a node at

v=-1
.
Furthermore, it is known that Gauss-Chebyshev quadrature of the first kind is
equivalent to the mid-point rule on the unit circle.
In fact, the quadrature rule expressed by (
33
) and (
34
)
holds equally for the midpoint rule,
provided that one now chooses

\tau_{k}=(k-1/2)/L

for

k=1,2,\ldots,L
.
The advantage of the associations with the trapezoidal and mid-point rules is that
they can both be applied iteratively, in order to attain a pre-assigned level of
accuracy
[
26
]
.
Generally, in a Gaussian quadrature scheme, the nodes and weights are generated for a
pre-selected quadrature order
L
, and it is difficult to estimate
a priori

the accuracy that will result for any particular problem.
While one can successively increase the order to refine the integration grid,
thus achieving greater accuracy,
the nodes from the previous iteration cannot be re-used.
The mapping of a Gaussian quadrature to the trapezoidal rule allows node re-use if the
grid is doubled on each refinement and, for the mid-point rule, node re-use results
from tripling the grid on each refinement
[
25
]
.
While this makes the trapezoidal rule more efficient than the mid-point rule
in reaching a desired level of convergence, in the present problem,
a price is paid for the singular behaviour around the critical point.
It should be appreciated that the trapezoidal and mid-point rules do not generally
implement Gaussian quadratures.
The fact that it is so in the present case leads to an exponential rate of
convergence
[
29
]
.

in the special case

r_{\text{hi}}=r^{2}
,
we have

3.1
Asymptotic Limits

Let us consider the integral

\mathcal{I}_{n}(a,b)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{(u+a)^{n+1}(u+b)}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u}}{(u+a)^{n+1}}\;,

(38)

and continue the limiting procedure with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u}}{(u+a)^{n+1}}&\;\;=\;\;\frac{2}{a^{n-1/2}}\int_{0}^{a^{2}}dv\,\frac{v^{2}}{(1+v^{2})^{n+1}}\\
&\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{2}{a^{n-1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,\frac{v^{2}}{(1+v^{2})^{n+1}}\\
&\;\;=\;\;\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2a^{n-1/2}}{\cdot}\frac{\Gamma(n-1/2)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\;.\end{aligned}

(39)

Hence, using the result that

\Gamma(n+c)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\Gamma(n)n^{c}
,
we obtain

\mathcal{I}_{n}(a,b)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2ba^{n-1/2}}{\cdot}\frac{1}{n^{3/2}}\;,

(40)

and so we conclude that

P_{\text{cut}}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{1-r}{4rb}\sqrt{\frac{a}{\pi n^{3}}}{\cdot}\left[(1-b/a)r\right]^{n}\;,

(41)

provided

b\neq 0
.
The situation where

b=0

occurs when

r_{\text{hi}}=r^{2}
.
In this case, we must consider

\mathcal{I}_{n}(a)\equiv\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{1}{(u+a)^{n+1}}\sqrt{\frac{1-u}{u}}\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u}}\,\frac{1}{(u+a)^{n+1}}\;.

(42)

Now,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\sqrt{u}}\,\frac{1}{(u+a)^{n+1}}&\;\;=\;\;\frac{2}{a^{n+1/2}}\int_{0}^{a^{2}}dv\,\frac{1}{(1+v^{2})^{n+1}}\\
&\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{2}{a^{n+1/2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}dv\,\frac{1}{(1+v^{2})^{n+1}}\\
&\;\;=\;\;\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{a^{n+1/2}}{\cdot}\frac{\Gamma(n+1/2)}{\Gamma(n+1)}\;,\end{aligned}

(43)

and so it follows that

\mathcal{I}_{n}(a)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\frac{1}{a^{n+1/2}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{n}}\;.

(44)

Therefore, when

r_{\text{hi}}=r^{2}
,
in which case

a=(1-r)/(4r)
,
we obtain

P_{\text{cut}}(n)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\sqrt{\frac{1-r}{\pi r}}{\cdot}\frac{r^{n}}{\sqrt{n}}\;.

(45)

4
Waiting-Time Distribution

It is known that
the queue-length marginals can be recovered from the waiting-time marginals
by appealing to the distributional form of Little’s law
[
3
,
18
]
,
bearing in mind the
caveat
that it is applicable to priority queues only within a given priority level.
Let us denote the Poisson PMF for arrival rate
\lambda
and time
t
by

\Lambda_{n}(\lambda t)\equiv\frac{(\lambda t)^{n}}{n!}e^{-\lambda t}\;,

(46)

for arrival numbers

n=0,1,2,\ldots
.
Then, one has

P_{\mathscr{L}}(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)\Lambda_{n}(r_{\text{lo}}t)\;,

(47)

where
\mathscr{L},\mathscr{W}
denote the queue-length and waiting-time
random variables (RVs), respectively.
This can be inverted, hence enabling us to derive the waiting-time probability density
function (PDF)
P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)

as a by-product of our derivation of the queue-length marginal PMF
P_{\mathscr{L}}(n)
.
It is implicit in (
47
) that we are dealing with the low-priority level
and that we have, without loss of generality, set

c\mu=1
,
which means that

\lambda_{\text{lo}}=r_{\text{lo}}
.
This simply implies that time is being measured in units of
c\mu
.

Let us consider

g_{\text{lo}}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}P_{\mathscr{L}}(n)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)K(z,r_{\text{lo}}t)\;,

(48)

where
K(z,\lambda t)

is the PGF of the Poisson distribution

K(z,\lambda t)\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}z^{n}\Lambda_{n}(\lambda t)=e^{\lambda t(z-1)}\;.

(49)

It follows immediately that the moment generating function (MGF) of the waiting-time RV,

M_{\mathscr{W}}(z)\equiv\langle e^{z\mathscr{W}}\rangle_{\mathscr{W}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)e^{zt}\;,

(50)

is given by

M_{\mathscr{W}}(z)=g_{\text{lo}}(1+z/r_{\text{lo}})
.
The waiting-time PDF can now be recovered via the inverse Laplace transform as

P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,e^{-tz}g_{\text{lo}}(1+z/r_{\text{lo}})\;,

(51)

with Bromwich contour
\Gamma
such that

z=\gamma+iy
,
for

-\infty<y<+\infty

with any given real constant

\gamma
such that

0<\gamma<(1/r-1)r_{\text{lo}}
.
After a scaling and shift of the integration variable
z
, and a subsequent deformation
of the resulting Bromwich contour so that it wraps around the pole and cut of the function

g_{\text{lo}}(z)

on the real axis, we arrive at the representation

P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)dt=r_{\text{lo}}dt{\cdot}\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{pol}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\text{cut}}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,e^{-r_{\text{lo}}t(z-1)}g_{\text{lo}}(z)\;,

(52)

noting that

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z>1

everywhere on

\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\text{pol}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\text{cut}}
.
An alternative derivation is presented in Appendix
B
. While more complex,
it has the merit of establishing a novel representation for the waiting-time
PDF in terms of the queue-length PMF as a convergent series of Laguerre polynomials
whose coefficients are finite linear combinations of queue-length probabilities,
corresponding to a binomial transform.

Let us consider the closed contour integral, for an arbitrary analytic function

f(z)
,
given by

\mathcal{I}[f]\equiv\oint_{\mathcal{C}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,f(z)g_{\text{lo}}(z)\;,

(53)

with

g_{\text{lo}}(z)=\frac{1-r}{\zeta_{+}(z)-r}=\frac{1}{r_{\text{lo}}r}{\cdot}\frac{1-r}{z-1/r}{\cdot}\left[\zeta_{-}(z)-r\right]\;,

(54)

where we recall that

\lim_{z\to 1/r}\left(z-1/r\right)g_{\text{lo}}(z)=\frac{1-r}{r^{2}}\left[\frac{r(1-r)}{r_{\text{lo}}}-1\right]\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}})\;.

(55)

The chosen contour deformation leads to the pole/cut decomposition

\mathcal{I}[f]=\int_{\mathcal{C}_{\text{pol}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\text{cut}}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,f(z)g_{\text{lo}}(z)=\mathcal{I}_{\text{pol}}[f]+\mathcal{I}_{\text{cut}}[f]\;,

(56)

with

\mathcal{I}_{\text{pol}}[f]=\frac{1-r}{r^{2}}\left[1-\frac{r(1-r)}{r_{\text{lo}}}\right]\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}}){\cdot}f(1/r)\;,

(57)

and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \mathcal{I}_{\text{cut}}[f]&=\frac{1-r}{2\pi r}\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}dx\,\frac{\sqrt{(x_{+}-x)(x-x_{-})}}{x-1/r}f(x)\\
&=\frac{(1-r)x_{\text{dif}}}{2\pi r}\int_{0}^{1}du\,\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{u+b}f(x_{\text{dif}}(u+a))\;.\end{aligned}

(58)

It should be noted that
\mathcal{C}_{\text{pol}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\text{cut}}

are both clockwise contours about the pole at

z=1/r

and cut along

z\in[x_{-},x_{+}]
,
respectively.
Application to the waiting-time PDF entails considering

f(z;t)=r_{\text{lo}}e^{-r_{\text{lo}}t(z-1)}\;,

(59)

in which case
the wait-conditional waiting-time PDF, given by

P_{W}(t)=\mathcal{I}[f](t)
,
becomes

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)&=-(1-r)\left(\frac{r_{\text{hi}}}{r^{2}}-1\right)\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}})e^{-tr_{\text{lo}}(1/r-1)}\\
&\quad{}+\frac{2(1-r)\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}{\pi r}e^{-t(4\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}a-r_{\text{lo}})}\int_{0}^{1}du\,e^{-4t\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}{\cdot}u}{\cdot}\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{u+b}\;,\end{aligned}

(60)

where we have observed that

r_{\text{lo}}x_{\text{dif}}=4\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}
.
The integral appearing here is an Euler-type integral representation for a special case
of the Humbert series
\Phi_{1}
, given by
[
7
]
as

\Phi_{1}(a,b,c;x,y)=\frac{1}{B(a,c-a)}\int_{0}^{1}dt\,e^{yt}{\cdot}\frac{t^{a-1}(1-t)^{c-a-1}}{(1-xt)^{b}}\;.

(61)

are bivariate generalizations of Kummer’s confluent hypergeometric function

P_{\mathscr{W}}(0)=1-r

\left(\sqrt{b+1}-\sqrt{b}\right)^{2}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}/r&\quad\text{for}&r_{\text{hi}}\leq r^{2}\;,\\

\mu

[

(65)

so that

\sigma_{K}=r_{\text{agg}}
,
and make the associations with an effective two-level problem

r_{\text{lo}}=r_{\kappa}\;,\quad r_{\text{hi}}=\sigma_{\kappa-1}\;,\quad r=r_{\text{lo}}+r_{\text{hi}}\;,

Then
P_{\mathscr{W}_{\kappa}}(t)

(

,

5
Joint Distribution

For the wait-conditional joint queue-length distribution,
the pole/cut decomposition reads

P(\ell,m)=P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,m)+P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)\;,

(68)

with

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,m)&=-\lim_{z\to 1/r}\left(z-1/r\right)g_{\ell}(z)/z^{m+1}\;,\\
P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)&=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}\frac{dx}{x^{m+1}}\,\left[g_{\ell}(x+i\epsilon)-g_{\ell}(x-i\epsilon)\right]\;,\end{aligned}

(69)

where
\epsilon
is a positive infinitesimal.
On inserting the explicit form of the PGF, the cut contribution reads

P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)=\frac{1-r}{2\pi i}\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}dx\,\frac{i\sqrt{|\Delta(x)|}}{x^{m+1}(1-rx)}\chi_{\ell}(x)-\frac{1-r}{2\pi i}\int_{x_{-}}^{x_{+}}dx\,\frac{i\sqrt{|\Delta(x)|}}{x^{m}(1-rx)}\chi_{\ell+1}(x)\;,

(70)

where

\chi_{\ell}(x)\equiv-\frac{\zeta^{\prime\ell}_{-}(x)-\zeta^{\prime\ell}_{+}(x)}{i\sqrt{|\Delta(x)|}}\;,\quad\zeta^{\prime}_{\pm}(x)\equiv\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\zeta_{\pm}(x-i\epsilon)\;,

(71)

is an analytic function.
On the cut, we can write

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \zeta_{\pm}(x)&=\frac{r_{\text{lo}}}{4}\left(\sqrt{x_{+}-x}\pm i\sqrt{x-x_{-}}\right)^{2}\\
&=\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}\left(\sqrt{1-u}\pm i\sqrt{u}\right)^{2}\;.\end{aligned}

(72)

We also have

|\Delta(x)|=r_{\text{lo}}^{2}x_{\text{dif}}^{2}u(1-u)=16r_{\text{hi}}u(1-u)\;.

(73)

This leads to the result

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \chi_{\ell}(x(u))&=(-1)^{\ell-1}r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-1)/2}{\cdot}\frac{\left(y+\sqrt{y^{2}-1}\right)^{\ell}-\left(y-\sqrt{y^{2}-1}\right)^{\ell}}{2\sqrt{y^{2}-1}}\\
&=(-1)^{\ell-1}r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-1)/2}{\cdot}U_{\ell-1}(y)\\
&=(-1)^{\ell-1}r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-1)/2}{\cdot}U_{\ell-1}(2u-1)\\
&=r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-1)/2}{\cdot}\frac{\sin(2\ell\asin\sqrt{u})}{\sin(2\asin\sqrt{u})}\;,\end{aligned}

(74)

where we had temporarily set

y=2u-1

and used some standard representations for the Chebyshev polynomial
U_{\ell}(y)
of the
second kind of order
\ell
.
One may observe that

\chi_{0}(x)=0
,

\chi_{1}(x)=1
.
From the Chebyshev generating function

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}t^{\ell}U_{\ell}(y)=\frac{1}{1-2ty+t^{2}}\;,

(75)

one obtains

\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\chi_{\ell}(x(u))=\frac{1}{1+2\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}(2u-1)+r_{\text{hi}}}\;,

(76)

which is useful for verifying that the low-priority marginal is recovered
when one sums over the high-priority indices
\ell
.

After some algebraic manipulation, we arrive at the expression

\begin{split}P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)=&-C_{1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\pi}{\cdot}\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{(u+a)^{m+1}(u+b)}{\cdot}\frac{\sin(2\ell\asin\sqrt{u})}{\sin(2\asin\sqrt{u})}\\
&{}+C_{2}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{du}{\pi}{\cdot}\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{(u+a)^{m}(u+b)}{\cdot}\frac{\sin(2(\ell+1)\asin\sqrt{u})}{\sin(2\asin\sqrt{u})}\;,\end{split}

(77)

with coefficients

C_{1}\equiv\frac{2(1-r)}{r}\left(\frac{r_{\text{lo}}}{4}\right)^{m+1}{\cdot}r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-m-1)/2}\;,\quad C_{2}\equiv\frac{2(1-r)}{r}\left(\frac{r_{\text{lo}}}{4}\right)^{m}{\cdot}r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-m+1)/2}\;.

(78)

As for the marginal PMF, we set

u=(1+v)/2
,

v=\cos(\pi\tau)
,

0\leq\tau\leq 1
,
so that

u(\tau)=\left[1+\cos(\pi\tau)\right]/2=\cos^{2}(\pi\tau/2)\;,

(79)

and

\frac{du}{\pi}{\cdot}\frac{\sqrt{u(1-u)}}{u}=-d\tau{\cdot}[1-u(\tau)]\;.

(80)

We also have

C_{\ell}(u)\equiv\frac{\sin(2\ell\asin\sqrt{u})}{\sin(2\asin\sqrt{u})}=(-1)^{\ell-1}\frac{\sin(\ell\pi\tau)}{\sin(\pi\tau)}=(-1)^{\ell-1}\ell{\cdot}\frac{\mathop{\rm sinc}\nolimits(\ell\pi\tau)}{\mathop{\rm sinc}\nolimits(\pi\tau)}\;,

(81)

and we can write

P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)=\Lambda(\ell+1,m)-\Lambda(\ell,m+1)
,
where

\Lambda(\ell,m)\equiv\frac{2(1-r)}{r}{\cdot}\frac{r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell/2}}{x_{-}^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}d\tau\,\frac{[1-u(\tau)]C_{\ell}(u(\tau))}{[1+u(\tau)/a]^{m}[1+b/u(\tau)]}\;.

(82)

The
L
-point quadrature with nodes
\tau_{k}
,

k=1,2,\ldots,L
,
corresponding to the trapezoidal rule is obtained by introducing
the integration grid

\tau_{k}=k/L
,
while the mid-point rule corresponds to

\tau_{k}=(k-1/2)/L
.
In both cases, the resulting quadrature rule may be expressed as

\Lambda(\ell,m)\simeq r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell/2}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{W_{k}C_{\ell}(U_{k})}{[x_{-}(1+U_{k}/a)]^{m}}\;,

(83)

where the quadrature nodes and weights
U_{k}
,
W_{k}
, respectively, are given by (
34
).
As a sanity check, we may note that the identity for the exclusively-low probabilities

P_{\text{cut}}(0,m+1)=r_{\text{lo}}P_{\text{cut}}(m)

implies that

\Lambda(1,m+1)=r_{\text{lo}}P_{\text{cut}}(m)
.
Recalling that

x_{-}=ax_{\text{dif}}
,
it is straightforward to verify that the quadrature rules (
83
) and (
33
)
applied to the left-hand and right-hand sides, respectively,
reproduce this equality.
The explicit result for the pole contribution is easily obtained as

P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,m)=\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}}){\cdot}(1-r)r^{m}\left(1-\frac{r_{\text{hi}}}{r^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{r_{\text{hi}}}{r}\right)^{\ell}\;.

(84)

6
Exact Expressions

6.1
Marginal Distribution

In order to facilitate an exact representation of the low-priority marginal PMF,
we introduce a family of integrals to which we shall refer as the
marginal integrals. These are given by

M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{1-\cos^{2}\theta}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{n+1}(\beta+\cos\theta)}\;,

(85)

for

n=-1,0,\ldots
,
so that

M_{-1}(\alpha,\beta)=\pi(\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1})
.
It is assumed that

|\alpha|,|\beta|>1
.
In terms of the marginal integral, we have

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=\frac{(1-r)r^{n-1}}{2\pi}{\cdot}(\alpha-\beta)^{n}M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\;,

(86)

with the parameters

\alpha,\beta

defined in (
30
).
The marginal integral may be evaluated in terms of Legendre polynomials

P_{n}(\chi)

due to the identity
[
13
, section 3.661.4]

\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{n+1}}=\frac{1}{(\alpha^{2}-1)^{(n+1)/2}}P_{n}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)\;.

(87)

for

\alpha>1

and

n=-1,0,1,\ldots
,
where we formally set

P_{-1}(\chi)\equiv 1
.
After a partial fraction decomposition of the integrand, one obtains
for

n=0,1,\ldots
,

M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\pi}{(\alpha-\beta)^{n+1}}\sum_{k=-1}^{n}\psi_{k}(n)P_{k}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)\;,

(88)

with

\psi_{k}(n)=\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^{k}}{(\alpha^{2}-1)^{(k+1)/2}}\left[\beta^{2}-1+(\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2})\delta_{k,n}-(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\delta_{k,n-1}\right]\;,

(89)

for

k=1,2,\ldots,n
,
and

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \psi_{-1}(n)&=(\beta-\alpha)\delta_{n,0}\;,\\
\psi_{0}(n)&=(\beta^{2}-1)\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1}}\right)+\frac{\alpha^{2}-\beta^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\delta_{n,0}-\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\delta_{n,1}\;.\end{aligned}

(90)

An immediate consequence is that

M_{0}(\alpha,\beta)/\pi=\frac{\psi_{-1}(0)+\psi_{0}(0)}{\alpha-\beta}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}+\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1}}-1\;,

(91)

which, as expected, is symmetric in
\alpha
and
\beta
.

The representation of the marginal PMF arising from (
88
) is clearly singular in the limit

\alpha-\beta\to 0^{+}
,
noting that, in the present queueing application, we always have

\alpha>\beta>1
.
This limit is encountered as

r_{\text{lo}}\to 0

or, equivalently,

\nu\to 1
.
It is also singular in the limit

\alpha\to 1^{+}
,
which is encountered when

r_{\text{lo}}\to 0
,

r_{\text{hi}}\to 1

simultaneously.
The representation may be re-arranged and scaled to make it numerically robust
throughout the entire ergodic region.
The final algorithm is detailed in the implementation summary at the end of this section.

6.2
Joint Distribution

In order to facilitate an exact representation of the joint marginal PMF,
we introduce a family of integrals to which we shall refer as the
joint integrals. These are defined by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)&\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{1-\cos^{2}\theta}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{m+1}(\beta+\cos\theta)}U_{\ell}(\cos\theta)\\
&=\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{\sin\theta{\cdot}\sin((\ell+1)\theta)}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{m+1}(\beta+\cos\theta)}\;.\end{aligned}

(92)

A relationship with the marginal integrals is given by

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{m}^{0}(\alpha,\beta)&=M_{m}(\alpha,\beta)\;,\\
J_{m}^{1}(\alpha,\beta)&=2M_{m-1}(\alpha,\beta)-2\alpha M_{m}(\alpha,\beta)\;.\end{aligned}

(93)

In the general case, the recurrence relation for the Chebyshev polynomial

U_{\ell}(\cos\theta)

implies the recurrence relation for the joint integral

J_{m}^{\ell+1}(\alpha,\beta)=2J_{m-1}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)-2\alpha J_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)-J_{m}^{\ell-1}(\alpha,\beta)\;,

(94)

for

\ell=0,1,\ldots
,
where we take the seed values

J_{m}^{-1}(\alpha,\beta)\equiv 0\;,\quad J_{m}^{0}(\alpha,\beta)=M_{m}(\alpha,\beta)\;,

(95)

for

m=-1,0,1,\ldots
,
noting that

M_{-1}(\alpha,\beta)=\pi(\beta-\sqrt{\beta^{2}-1})
.

Next, we introduce the intermediate integrals

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)&\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{\cos(\ell\theta)}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{m+1}(\beta+\cos\theta)}\;,\\
I_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha)&\equiv\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{\cos(\ell\theta)}{(\alpha+\cos\theta)^{m+1}}\;,\end{aligned}

(96)

for

m=-1,0,\ldots
,

\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.
One may observe that

M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)=\left[I_{n}^{0}(\alpha,\beta)-I_{n}^{2}(\alpha,\beta)\right]/2

and that

I_{-1}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)=I_{0}^{\ell}(\beta)
,

I_{-1}^{\ell}(\alpha)=\pi\delta_{\ell 0}
.
A partial fraction decomposition yields

I_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)=c_{m}I_{0}^{\ell}(\beta)-\sum_{k=0}^{m}c_{k}I_{m-k}^{\ell}(\alpha)\;,

(97)

where we have set

c_{m}\equiv 1/(\alpha-\beta)^{m+1}
.
The joint integral may be expressed in terms of the intermediate integrals as

J_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)=I_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)+\beta I_{m}^{\ell+1}(\alpha,\beta)-I_{m}^{\ell+1}(\alpha)\;.

(98)

In terms of the joint integrals, we can write

P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)=\frac{(1-r)r_{\text{lo}}}{2\pi r}\left[\frac{(-\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}})^{\ell}}{(x_{\text{dif}}/2)^{m-1}}J_{m-1}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)-\frac{(-\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}})^{\ell-1}}{(x_{\text{dif}}/2)^{m}}J_{m}^{\ell-1}(\alpha,\beta)\right]\;.

(99)

6.3
Q
-Functions

To proceed further, we define a family of functions

Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)
,
that generalize the Legendre polynomials
and are related closely to the associated Legendre functions,
by means of the integral representation

Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)\equiv\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\chi+1}{\chi-1}\right)^{\ell/2}\int_{0}^{\pi}d\theta\,\frac{\cos(\ell\theta)}{(\chi+\sqrt{\chi^{2}-1}\cos\theta)^{m+1}}\;.

(100)

This may be compared with the integral representation of the associated Legendre function
given by
[
13
, section 8.711.2]

P_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{\Gamma(m+1)}{\Gamma(m-\ell+1)}\int_{0}^{\pi}\frac{d\theta}{\pi}\,\frac{\cos(\ell\theta)}{(\chi+\sqrt{\chi^{2}-1}\cos\theta)^{m+1}}\;,

(101)

noting also that the Legendre polynomials are recovered according to

P_{m}(\chi)=P_{m}^{0}(\chi)
.
Clearly

Q_{-1}^{\ell}(\chi)=\delta_{\ell 0}
,

Q_{0}^{\ell}(\chi)=1

and

Q_{m}^{0}(\chi)=P_{m}(\chi)
.
It also follows that

Q_{m}^{1}(\chi)=(\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1})\left[\alpha P_{m}(\chi)-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}P_{m-1}(\chi)\right]\;,

(102)

for

m=0,1,\ldots
.
From their definition and the trigonometric identity

\cos[(\ell+1)\theta]+\cos[(\ell-1)\theta]=2\cos(\ell\theta)\cos\theta\;,

(103)

it is easily shown
that the
Q
-functions satisfy the recurrence relation

Q_{m}^{\ell+1}(\chi)=\frac{2\chi}{\chi-1}Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)-\frac{2}{\chi-1}Q_{m-1}^{\ell}(\chi)-\frac{\chi+1}{\chi-1}Q_{m}^{\ell-1}(\chi)\;,

(104)

for

m=0,1,\ldots
,

\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
.
An explicit expansion of the
Q
-function is given by

Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\binom{m+\ell}{k+\ell}\binom{k+m}{m}\left(\frac{\chi-1}{2}\right)^{k}\;.

(105)

The relationship with associated Legendre functions is established by noting that,
for

\ell\leq m
,

Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)=\frac{(m-\ell)!}{m!}\left(\frac{\chi+1}{\chi-1}\right)^{\ell/2}P_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)\;.

(106)

Another recurrence relation,
which follows directly from the recurrence relations for the associated Legendre functions, is
given by

Q_{m+1}^{\ell}(\chi)=\frac{2m+1}{m+1}\chi Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)-\frac{m^{2}-\ell^{2}}{m(m+1)}Q_{m-1}^{\ell}(\chi)\;,

(107)

for

m=1,2,\ldots
,
with the first few functions being

Q_{0}^{\ell}(\chi)=1\;,\quad Q_{1}^{\ell}(\chi)=\chi+\ell\;,\quad Q_{2}^{\ell}(\chi)=\tfrac{3}{2}\chi^{2}+\tfrac{3}{2}\ell\chi+\tfrac{1}{2}(\ell^{2}-1)\;.

(108)

This recurrence is particularly useful for practical purposes since, while superficially appearing
to be a two-dimensional recurrence, it is actually a family of independent one-dimensional recurrences
in
m
, each indexed by the value of
\ell
.

The generating function for the
Q
-functions is found to be

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{z^{2}-2\chi z+1}}\left(\frac{\chi-z-\sqrt{z^{2}-2\chi z+1}}{\chi-1}\right)^{\ell}\;.

(109)

Using

\chi=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\;,\quad z=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\;,

(110)

and noting that

z^{2}-2\chi z+1=(\beta^{2}-1)/(\alpha^{2}-1)
,
this may be cast in the alternative form

\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}z^{m}Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)=\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}-1}{\beta^{2}-1}}{\cdot}\frac{d_{\ell}(\beta)}{d_{\ell}(\alpha)}\;,

(111)

where we have set

d_{\ell}(\alpha)\equiv(\alpha-\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1})^{\ell}=\frac{1}{(\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1})^{\ell}}\;,

(112)

and analogously for

d_{\ell}(\beta)
.

The single-argument intermediate integral can be expressed in terms of the
Q
-function as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} I_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha)&=\frac{\pi(-1)^{\ell}}{(\alpha+\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1})^{\ell}(\alpha^{2}-1)^{(m+1)/2}}Q_{m}^{\ell}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)\\
&=\pi(-1)^{\ell}\left(\frac{\chi-1}{\chi+1}\right)^{\ell/2}{\cdot}(\chi^{2}-1)^{(m+1)/2}Q_{m}^{\ell}(\chi)\;.\end{aligned}

(113)

Consequently, we may express the joint integral in terms of the
Q
-functions,
according to

\begin{split}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}J^{\ell}_{m}(\alpha,\beta)}{\pi c_{m}}&=d_{\ell+1}(\beta)+d_{\ell+1}(\alpha)\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)^{m+1}Q_{m}^{\ell+1}(\chi)\\
&\quad{}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)^{k}\left[d_{\ell}(\alpha)Q_{k}^{\ell}(\chi)-\beta d_{\ell+1}(\alpha)Q_{k}^{\ell+1}(\chi)\right]\;.\end{split}

(114)

6.4
Implementation

We conclude this section by giving complete, self-contained
specifications of the algorithms
for obtaining exact closed-form algebraic expressions for the marginal and joint
queue-length PMFs that are also numerically efficient and robust.
Let us first recall that

\alpha=\frac{r+1}{2\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}\;,\quad\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{r}{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}+\frac{\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}{r}\right)\;,

(115)

and that

\chi=\frac{\alpha}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\;,\quad z=\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\;,

(116)

so that

\chi=\frac{1+r}{\sqrt{(1+r)^{2}-4r_{\text{hi}}}}\;,\quad z=\frac{1-r_{\text{hi}}/r}{\sqrt{(1+r)^{2}-4r_{\text{hi}}}}\;,\quad t\equiv\chi z\;.

(117)

It is useful to note that

d_{1}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}}\left[1+r-\sqrt{(1+r)^{2}-4r_{\text{hi}}}\right]=\zeta_{-}(0)/\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}\;,

(118)

and that

d_{1}(\beta)=\min\left(r/\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}},\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}/r\right)\;.

(119)

It is also useful to note the bounds on
z
given by

0\leq\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\leq\frac{1}{1+r}<1\;,

(120)

which shows that, while
z
never becomes large, it can be very small.
The lower and upper endpoints for
z
correspond to

r_{\text{lo}}=0

and

r_{\text{hi}}=0
,
respectively.
We also have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 1&\leq\chi\leq(1+r)/(1-r)\;,\\
0&\leq\chi z\leq 1/(1+r)\;.\end{aligned}

(121)

enumerates the singular limits of the marginal and

6.4.1
Marginal Distribution

We introduce scaled Legendre polynomials

\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)\equiv z^{n}P_{n}(t/z)

that are computed according to the recurrence relation

\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)=(2-1/n)t\hat{P}_{n-1}(t;z)-(1-1/n)z^{2}\hat{P}_{n-2}(t;z)\;,

(122)

for

n=1,2,\ldots
,
subject to the boundary values

\hat{P}_{-1}(t;z)=1/z\;,\quad\hat{P}_{0}(t;z)=1\;.

(123)

The scaled marginal integral

M_{\text{scl}}(n)\equiv\pi^{-1}(\alpha-\beta)^{n}M_{n}(\alpha,\beta)\;,

(124)

in terms of which the cut contribution to the low-priority marginal PMF is expressed as

P_{\text{cut}}(n)=\frac{(1-r)r^{n-1}}{2}{\cdot}M_{\text{scl}}(n)\;,

(125)

is computed from the scaled Legendre polynomials as follows.
With

D\equiv z^{2}-2\chi z+1=(\beta^{2}-1)/(\alpha^{2}-1)\;,

(126)

we can write

M_{\text{scl}}(n)=-z\hat{P}_{n-1}(t;z)+(2\chi-z)\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)-\frac{\sqrt{D}}{z}\biggl[1-\sqrt{D}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{P}_{k}(t;z)\biggr]\;.

(127)

It is instructive to note the asymptotic behaviour

1-\sqrt{D}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{P}_{k}(t;z)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{n\to\infty}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-1}}z\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)\;,

(128)

provided

\beta\neq 1
.
When

\beta=1
,
the foregoing expression is identically unity for all

n<\infty

since

\sqrt{D}=0
.
This enables us to establish the bounding property

0<\frac{\sqrt{D}}{z}\biggl[1-\sqrt{D}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{P}_{k}(t;z)\biggr]<\sqrt{\frac{\beta+1}{\beta-1}}{\cdot}\sqrt{D}\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)\;.

(129)

The generating function for the Legendre polynomials implies that

\sqrt{D}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{k}(t;z)=1\;.

(130)

Use of this identity leads directly to an
alternative expression for the scaled marginal integral, given by

M_{\text{scl}}(n)=-z\hat{P}_{n-1}(t;z)+(2\chi-z)\hat{P}_{n}(t;z)-\frac{D}{z}\sum_{k=n+1}^{\infty}\hat{P}_{k}(t;z)\;.

(131)

In the numerical implementation, we construct the last term of (
127
)
and test whether it satisfies the bound given by (
129
).
If the test is passed, then the scaled marginal integral is evaluated via (
127
).
If it fails (due to arithmetic underflow),
then the scaled marginal integral is evaluated instead via (
131
),
where the sum is computed by sequentially adding terms until the desired level of
convergence is attained.

6.4.2
Joint Distribution

To deal with the joint distribution,
we introduce scaled
Q
-functions according to

\hat{Q}^{\ell}_{m}(t;z)\equiv z^{m}Q^{\ell}_{m}(t/z)
.
Let

A_{m}\equiv\frac{2m+1}{m+1}{\cdot}t\;,\quad B_{m}^{\ell}\equiv\frac{m^{2}-\ell^{2}}{m(m+1)}{\cdot}z^{2}\;.

(132)

Then,

\hat{Q}_{m+1}^{\ell}(t,z)=A_{m}\hat{Q}_{m}^{\ell}(t,z)-B_{m}^{\ell}\hat{Q}_{m-1}^{\ell}(t,z)\;,

(133)

for

\ell=0,1,\ldots
,

m=1,2,\ldots
,
subject to the boundary values

\hat{Q}_{0}^{\ell}(t,z)=1\;,\quad\hat{Q}_{1}^{\ell}(t,z)=t+\ell z\;.

(134)

We use the scaled
Q
-functions to construct the scaled joint integrals

J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m)\equiv\pi^{-1}(-1)^{\ell}(\alpha-\beta)^{m+1}J_{m}^{\ell}(\alpha,\beta)\;,

(135)

as

\begin{split}J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m)&=d_{\ell+1}(\beta)+d_{\ell+1}(\alpha)\biggl[z\hat{Q}_{m}^{\ell+1}(\chi z,z)\\
&\quad{}-(1+\chi)\biggl(\sum_{k=0}^{m}\hat{Q}_{k}^{\ell}(\chi z,z)-\frac{1-z/\chi}{1+1/\chi}\sum_{k=0}^{m}\hat{Q}_{k}^{\ell+1}(\chi z,z)\biggr)\biggr]\;,\end{split}

(136)

and we may note the generalization of (
130
), given by

\sqrt{D}\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{Q}_{k}^{\ell}(t;z)=\frac{d_{\ell}(\beta)}{d_{\ell}(\alpha)}\;.

(137)

One can also confirm that

J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,0)=d_{\ell+1}(\beta)-d_{\ell+1}(\alpha)
.

Given the definition of the scaled joint integrals in (
135
),
the cut contribution to the wait-conditional joint PMF becomes

P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,m)=(1-r)\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}\left[r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell/2}r^{m-1}J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m-1)-r_{\text{hi}}^{(\ell-1)/2}r^{m}J_{\text{scl}}(\ell-1,m)\right]\;.

(138)

As a sanity check, one can confirm, with the aid of (
118
) and (
119
),
that this expression leads to the results

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\text{cut}}(0,0)&=(1-r){\cdot}\min\left(1,r_{\text{hi}}/r^{2}\right)\,,\\
P_{\text{cut}}(0,m+1)&=r_{\text{lo}}{\cdot}P_{\text{cut}}(m)\;,\\
P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,0)&=P_{\text{xhi}}(\ell)-P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,0)\;,\end{aligned}

(139)

where the cut contribution to the low-priority marginal PMF

P_{\text{cut}}(m)
is given by (
125
),

P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,0)
follows from (
84
)
and

P_{\text{xhi}}(\ell)\equiv P(\ell,0)=(1-r)\zeta_{-}^{\ell}(0)

[
32
]
.
An intermediate step in deriving the last relationship is

P_{\text{cut}}(\ell,0)=(1-r)r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell/2}\left[d_{\ell}(\alpha)-d_{\ell}(\beta)+(\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}/r){\cdot}d_{\ell+1}(\beta)\right]\;,

(140)

which is established with the aid of the identity

I_{0}^{\ell}(\gamma)+\gamma I_{0}^{\ell+1}(\gamma)=\pi(-1)^{\ell}d_{\ell+1}(\gamma)\;,

(141)

with

\gamma=\alpha,\beta
.
In fact, it may be noted that

P_{\text{pol}}(\ell,m)=(1-r)r^{m-1}r_{\text{hi}}^{\ell/2}\left[rd_{\ell}(\beta)-\sqrt{r_{\text{hi}}}d_{\ell+1}(\beta)\right]\;.

(142)

Finally, we mention that the large-
\ell
asymptotics of the scaled joint integrals are given by

J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{\ell\to\infty}}d_{\ell+1}(\beta)-\frac{(\ell+1)^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)^{m}d_{\ell+1}(\alpha)\;.

(143)

A derivation is presented in Appendix
C
.
The large-
\ell
behaviour of the joint PMF
P(\ell,m)

for fixed
m
follows directly from this.

8
Conclusions

The analytic approach to the calculation of the joint and marginal queue-length distributions
for the two-level non-preemptive priority queue studied here
leads to integral representations that are
amenable to both efficient numerical quadrature rules and exact expressions as finite sums
of easily computable special functions that generalize the Legendre polynomials.
One benefit of integral forms obtained here
is that the large queue-length asymptotic behaviour becomes apparent.
Another is that there is scope for extending the methodology to cater for additional complications,
such as multiple arrival classes.

Appendix A
No-Wait Probability

Let
c
denote the number of servers, and let
\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}}
be the
RV for system occupancy. It is well-known that

\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = n)=Ar^{n}

for all

n\geq c
,
for some constant
A

and, by definition, we have

P_{\text{NW}}=\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} \leq c-1)

for the no-wait probability.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} P_{\text{NW}}&=\sum_{n=0}^{c-1}\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = n)\\
&=1-\sum_{n=c}^{\infty}\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = n)\\
&=1-Ar^{c}/(1-r)\\
&=1-\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = c)/(1-r)\;.\end{aligned}

(154)

Furthermore,

\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = c)=[(rc)^{c}/c!]\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = 0)
,
and
[
12
]

\frac{1}{\Pr(\mathscr{N}_{\text{sys}} = 0)}=\sum_{n=0}^{c}\frac{(rc)^{n}}{n!}+\frac{(rc)^{c}}{c!(1/r-1)}\;.

(155)

The result for
P_{\text{NW}}
follows, and may be compactly stated as

\frac{1}{1-P_{\text{NW}}}=1+\frac{(1-r)c!}{(rc)^{c}}\sum_{n=0}^{c-1}\frac{(rc)^{n}}{n!}\;.

(156)

Appendix B
Laguerre Series for the Waiting Time

Let us write

p_{n}\equiv P_{\mathscr{L}}(n)

so that

p_{n}=\frac{1}{n!}{\cdot}\left.\frac{d^{n}}{dp^{n}}g_{\text{lo}}(z)\right|_{z=0}=\oint_{\mathcal{C}_{\eta}}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\frac{g_{\text{lo}}(z)}{z^{n+1}}\;,

(157)

where the closed anti-clockwise traversed integration contour
\mathcal{C}_{\eta}
, initially taken to be a
circle centred on the origin of radius

0<\eta<1/r
,
is deformed into the Bromwich contour
\Gamma
on which

z=\delta+iy

with

-\infty<y<+\infty
,
for some

1<\delta<1/r

so that

\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits z>1
.
Then, we consider the Binomial transform of the
p_{n}
sequence

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \gamma_{n}&\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}p_{k}\\
&=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,g_{\text{lo}}(z)\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\frac{1}{z^{k+1}}\\
&=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,\frac{g_{\text{lo}}(z)}{z}{\cdot}(1-1/z)^{n}\;.\end{aligned}

(158)

We now recall the generating function for the Laguerre polynomials
L_{n}(x)
, given by

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tau^{n}L_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{1-\tau}e^{-\tau x/(1-\tau)}\;.

(159)

On setting

\tau=1-1/z

and

x=r_{\text{lo}}t
,
this reads

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(1-1/z\right)^{n}L_{n}(r_{\text{lo}}t)=ze^{-r_{\text{lo}}t(z-1)}\;,

(160)

from which it follows that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}L_{n}(r_{\text{lo}}t)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,g_{\text{lo}}(z)e^{-r_{\text{lo}}t(z-1)}\;.

(161)

Now, (
47
) can be written as

P_{\mathscr{L}}(k)=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\,P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t^{\prime})\frac{t^{\prime k}}{k!}e^{-t^{\prime}}\;,

(162)

where

t^{\prime}=r_{\text{lo}}t

and

P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}=P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)dt
.
We also have

L_{n}(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{k!}t^{k}\;.

(163)

These may be combined to yield

\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\,e^{-t^{\prime}}L_{n}(t^{\prime})P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t^{\prime})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\binom{n}{k}(-1)^{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}dt^{\prime}\,P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t^{\prime})\frac{t^{\prime k}}{k!}e^{-t^{\prime}}=\gamma_{n}\;.

(164)

The completeness relation
[
20
]

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}L_{n}(t)L_{n}(t^{\prime})=e^{(t+t^{\prime})/2}{\cdot}\delta(t-t^{\prime})\;,

(165)

\delta(t)

being the Dirac delta function,
implies the convergent Laguerre-series representation

P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t^{\prime})=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{n}L_{n}(t^{\prime})\;,

(166)

and we conclude, via (
161
), that

P_{\mathscr{W}}(t)dt=r_{\text{lo}}dt{\cdot}\int_{\Gamma}\frac{dz}{2\pi i}\,g_{\text{lo}}(z)e^{-r_{\text{lo}}t(z-1)}\;.

(167)

The Bromwich contour
\Gamma
, with

\real z>1
,
may now be deformed as

\Gamma\mapsto\mathcal{C}_{\text{pol}}\cup\mathcal{C}_{\text{cut}}

so that it wraps around the pole and cut of
g_{\text{lo}}(z)
.
This serves to reproduce (
52
).

The convergence claim for (
166
) follows from the theorem that
the formal expansion

\gamma(t)\equiv\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma_{k}L_{k}(t)

(168)

converges in
L_{2}
if and only if

\|\gamma\|^{2}_{L_{2}}<\infty
,
where

\|\gamma\|^{2}_{L_{2}}\equiv\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-t}|\gamma(t)|^{2}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|\gamma_{k}|^{2}\;.

(169)

In the present application, we have

\gamma(t)=P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t)

for which, trivially,

\|P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}\|_{L_{2}}^{2}=\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-t}\left[P^{\prime}_{\mathscr{W}}(t)\right]^{2}<\int_{0}^{\infty}dt\,e^{-t}=1\;.

(170)

One should note that, while the coefficients
\gamma_{n}
of the Laguerre series
(
166
) are constructed as (potentially)
numerically problematic alternating combinatorial sums, the last equality in
(
158
) shows that they can be efficiently computed by means of the very same
iterative quadrature method derived for the queue-length probabilities
p_{n}
.
Explicitly,

\begin{split}\gamma_{n}&=\Theta(r^{2}-r_{\text{hi}})\left[1-\frac{r(1-r)}{r_{\text{lo}}}\right]{\cdot}\frac{(1-r)^{n+1}}{r}\\
&\quad{}+\frac{1-r}{2\pi r}\int_{0}^{1}du\,\sqrt{u(1-u)}{\cdot}\frac{(u+c)^{n}}{(u+a)^{n+1}(u+b)}\;,\end{split}

(171)

where

c\equiv(x_{-}-1)/x_{\text{dif}}=(1-r)a+rb
.

Appendix C
Joint Asymptotics

To determine the large-
\ell
behaviour of the joint integral (
92
),
we use the symmetry properties and periodicity of the integrand
to write it as an integral over the interval

\theta\in[0,2\pi)
.
We then promote the integration
variable
\theta
to the complex plane, and deform the contour from

\theta=0

to

\theta=2\pi

into three straight legs: (i) from
0
to
0+i\infty
,
(ii) from
0+i\infty
to
2\pi+i\infty
, and
(iii) from
2\pi+i\infty
to
2\pi
,
making small excursions on the vertical legs around the points

\theta=\arccos(\alpha),\arccos(\beta)
.
The contribution from the horizontal contour vanishes,
while the vertical contours cancel apart from the excursions around the singular points.
As a result, we obtain the decomposition

J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m)=J_{\text{scl}}^{\alpha}(\ell,m)+J_{\text{scl}}^{\beta}(\ell,m)

where, on writing

\theta=it
,
we have

J_{\text{scl}}^{\alpha,\beta}(\ell,m)=(\beta-\alpha)^{m+1}\oint_{C_{\alpha,\beta}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\,\frac{\sinh(t)e^{-\ell t}}{(\cosh(t)-\alpha)^{m+1}(\cosh(t)-\beta)}\;,

(172)

where
C_{\alpha}
is an infinitesimal anti-clockwise circle about the point

t=\acosh(\alpha)
,
and likewise for
C_{\beta}
about the point

t=\acosh(\beta)
.

It is clear that

J_{\text{scl}}^{\beta}(\ell,m)=d_{\ell+1}(\beta)
.
To evaluate
J_{\text{scl}}^{\alpha}(\ell,m)
,
we set

\alpha\equiv\cosh(s)

so that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} J_{\text{scl}}^{\alpha}(\ell{-}1,m)&=\left(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)^{m+1}\!\!e^{-\ell s}\oint_{C_{\alpha}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\,\frac{e^{-\ell(t-s)}}{\sinh^{m+1}((t-s)/2)}\\
&\quad{}\times\frac{\sinh(t)}{\sinh^{m+1}((t+s)/2)[\cosh(t)-\beta]}\\
&=\left(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{2}\right)^{m+1}\frac{e^{-\ell s}}{\ell}\oint_{C_{0}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\,\frac{e^{-t}}{\sinh^{m+1}(t/(2\ell))}\\
&\quad{}\times\frac{\sinh(s+t/\ell)}{\sinh^{m+1}(s+t/(2\ell))[\cosh(s+t/\ell)-\beta]}\;.\end{aligned}

(173)

Taking the limit yields

J_{\text{scl}}^{\alpha}(\ell{-}1,m)\mathrel{\mathop{\sim}_{\ell\to\infty}}\frac{(\beta-\alpha)^{m+1}\ell^{m}e^{-\ell s}}{\sinh^{m}(s)[\cosh(s)-\beta]}\oint_{C_{0}}\frac{dt}{2\pi i}\,\frac{e^{-t}}{t^{m+1}}=-\frac{\ell^{m}}{m!}\left(\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}-1}}\right)^{m}d_{\ell}(\alpha)\,,

(174)

where
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is an infinitesimal anti-clockwise circle about the origin.
The result in the main text follows.
A by-product of this discussion is an alternative representation of the joint integral,
given by

J_{\text{scl}}(\ell,m)=d_{\ell+1}(\beta)+\oint_{C_{\alpha}}\frac{dw}{2\pi i}\,\frac{d_{\ell+1}(w)}{w-\beta}\left(\frac{\beta-\alpha}{w-\alpha}\right)^{m+1}\;.

(175)

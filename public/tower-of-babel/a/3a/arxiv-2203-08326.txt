Algebras of Lebesgue and KB regular operators in Banach lattices


Abstract
It is shown that the regularly Lebesgue and regularly (quasi) K ​ B KB operators in a Banach lattice E E form operator algebras and, for Dedekind complete E E , even Banach lattice algebras. We also prove that a Banach lattice E E with order continuous norm is a K ​ B KB -space iff each positive compact operator in E E is a K ​ B KB operator.

Introduction

Several operator versions of Banach lattice properties
like the property to be a
KB
-space were introduced and
studied in recent papers
[
2
,
3
,
6
]
.
The main idea of such operator versions lies in
a redistribution of topological and order properties
between domain and range of operators. Since
the order convergence is not topological in infinite
dimensional vector lattices
[
5
]
, the important
operator versions emerge when order and norm convergences
are involved simultaneously. The case of regular operators
acting in a Banach lattice is of special interest.

1.1

In the present paper,
E
is a real Banach lattice and
\text{\rm L}(E)

is the algebra of all continuous linear operators in
E
.
The space
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
of regular
operators in
E
is an ordered subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}(E)
.
In general,
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
is not a vector lattice.
However,
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
is a Banach space
under the
regular norm

\|T\|_{r}=\inf\{\|S\|:\pm T\leq S\in\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)\}

[
7
, Proposition 1.3.6]
.
If
E
is Dedekind complete, then
(\text{\rm L}_{r}(E),\|\cdot\|_{r})

is a Banach lattice algebra, and

\|T\|_{r}=\|~|T|~\|\ \ \ \ \ (\forall T\in\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)).

[formula]

1.2

The following is an adopted variant of Definition 1.1
from
[
2
]
in the Banach lattice setting.

Definition 1
.

An operator
T\in\text{\rm L}(E)
is said to be:

(i)

Lebesgue (
\sigma
-Lebesgue) if
\|Tx_{\alpha}\|\to 0

for every net (sequence)
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E
such that

x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
.
The set of all such operators is denoted by
\text{\rm L}_{Leb}(E)

(resp.,
\text{\rm L}^{\sigma}_{Leb}(E)
).

(ii)

on
-continuous (
on\sigma
-continuous) if

\|Tx_{\alpha}\|\to 0
for every net (sequence)
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E

such that
x_{\alpha}\convo 0
(cf.,
[
6
, p.1315]
).
The set of all such operators is denoted by
\text{\rm L}_{on}(E)

(resp.,
\text{\rm L}^{\sigma}_{on}(E)
).

(iii)

KB
(
\sigma
-
KB
) if, for every norm-bounded
increasing net (sequence)
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E_{+}
,
there exists (not necessarily unique)
x\in E
such that
\|Tx_{\alpha}-Tx\|\to 0
.
The set of all such operators is denoted by
\text{\rm L}_{KB}(E)

(resp.,
\text{\rm L}^{\sigma}_{KB}(E)
).

(iv)

quasi
KB
(quasi
\sigma
-
KB
) if
T
takes
norm-bounded increasing nets (sequences) in
E_{+}
to norm Cauchy nets.
The set of all such operators is denoted by
\text{\rm L}_{qKB}(E)

(resp.,
\text{\rm L}^{\sigma}_{qKB}(E)
) (cf.
[
3
, Definition 1.1]
).

The identity operator in
c_{0}
is Lebesgue yet not
KB
.
The next example shows that
KB
operators also need
not to be Lebesgue.

Example 1
.

Let
E
be a Banach lattice of all bounded real functions on
[0,1]

that differ from a constant on at most countable subset of
[0,1]
.
Let
T:E\to E
be an operator that assigns to each
f\in E
the constant function

Tf
on
[0,1]
such that the set
\{x\in[0,1]:f(x)\neq(Tf)(x)\}
is at most
countable. Since
T
is rank one and continuous,
T
is
KB
.
Consider the net
(f_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E
, indexed by
finite subsets of
[0,1]
, defined by:

f_{\alpha}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}1&\text{ if }&x\not\in\alpha\\
0&\text{ if }&x\in\alpha\end{array}\right..

Then
f_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
in
E
, yet
\|f_{\alpha}\|_{\infty}=1

for all
\alpha
. Thus
T
is not a Lebesgue operator.
However,
T
is
\sigma
-Lebesgue.

The following example shows that quasi
KB
operators need not to be
KB
.

Example 2
.

Let
T
be an operator in
E=C[0,1]\oplus L_{1}[0,1]
defined by

T\bigl((\phi,\psi)\bigl)=(0,\phi)
for
\phi\in C[0,1]
and
\psi\in L_{1}[0,1]
.
Clearly,
T
is
a quasi
KB
operator. But
T
is not
KB
.
Indeed, let a continuous function
\phi_{n}
be equal
to 1 on
[0,\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{n}}]
,
to 0 on
[\frac{1}{2},1]
, and linear otherwise.
Let
f_{n}:=(\phi_{n},0)
. Then
f_{n}\uparrow
in
E
with

\|f_{n}\|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
Tf_{n}\convn(0,g)
,
where
g\in L_{1}[0,1]
is the indicator function of
[0,\frac{1}{2}]
.
Since
g\not\in C[0,1]
, there is no element
x
of
E
satisfying

Tx=(0,g)
, and hence
T
is not
KB
.

1.3

The operators belonging to the linear span of positive compact operators
(r-compact operators) were investigated by Z.L. Chen and A.W. Wickstead in
[
4
]
.
The class of r-compact operators is closed under neither the operator nor
regular norm. However, there is a special norm on r-compact operators
that makes this class closed (see
[
4
, Proposition 2.2]
). This norm occurs
in application of Proposition 1.3.6 of
[
7
]
to compact rather
than continuous operators. The general case, that is a quite straightforward
extension of
[
7
, Proposition 1.3.6]
, is described in
Lemma
1
below. First, we need the following notion.

Definition 2
.

Given a set
P
of operators in
E

(
briefly, the set
P
consists of
P
-operators
)
. An operator

T:E\to E
is called a regularly
P
-operator
(
shortly, an

r-P
-operator
)
, whenever there exist two positive

P
-operators
T_{1},T_{2}
such that
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
.

As examples of regularly P-operators one can consider:
r-
positive
and r-
regular operators

that are just regular; r-
finite rank operators
;
r-
\sigma
-Lebesgue
;
r-
compact
; r-
KB

operators
; etc.

The importance of regularly P-operators in the present paper is justified by the fact that
most of the key results below, like Propositions
1
,
4
, yields for

r-P
-operators.

Lemma 1
.

Let
{\rm P}
be a closed in the operator norm subspace
of
\text{\rm L}(E)
. Then the formula

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}=\inf\{\|S\|:\pm T\leq S\in{\rm P}\}

[formula]

defines a norm on the space
\text{\rm r-P}(E)
of all

r-P
-operators in
E
. Furthermore,

\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\geq\|T\|_{r}\geq\|T\|
for all
T\in\text{\rm r-P}(E)
, and

(\text{\rm r-P}(E),\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}})
is a Banach space.

Proof.

We skip routine checking that
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}
is a norm on

\text{\rm r-P}(E)
satisfying
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\geq\|\cdot\|_{r}\geq\|\cdot\|
.
Take any
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}
-Cauchy sequence
(T_{n})_{n}
in
\text{\rm r-P}(E)
.
Then
T_{n}=G_{n}-R_{n}
for some positive P-operators
G_{n},R_{n}
.
We can assume also that
\|T_{n+1}-T_{n}\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}<2^{-n}
for all

n\in\mathbb{N}
. As
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\geq\|\cdot\|
, there exists

T\in\text{\rm L}(E)
such that
\|T-T_{n}\|\to 0
.
Since
P
is closed in the operator norm,
T\in\text{\rm P}
.
Pick
S_{n}\in\text{\rm P}
with
\|S_{n}\|<2^{-n}
and
\pm(T_{n+1}-T_{n})\leq S_{n}
. Then

T_{n+1}x^{+}-T_{n}x^{+}\leq Sx^{+}\ \ \text{and}\ \ -T_{n+1}x^{-}+T_{n}x^{-}\leq Sx^{-}

[formula]

for each
x\in E
. Summing up the inequalities in (3) gives

T_{n+1}x-T_{n}x\leq S_{n}|x|
. Replacing
x
by
-x

gives
T_{n}x-T_{n+1}x\leq S_{n}|x|
, and hence

|(T_{n+1}-T_{n})x|\leq S_{n}|x|\ \ \ (\forall x\in E).

[formula]

As
P
is closed in the operator norm,

Q_{n}:=\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}S_{k}\in\text{\rm P}
for all
n
.
By (4), we obtain

|(T-T_{n})x|=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}|(T_{k}-T_{n})x|\leq

\sum\limits_{k=n}^{\infty}|(T_{k+1}-T_{n})x|\leq Q_{n}|x|\ \ \ \ \ (x\in E),

and hence
\pm(T-T_{n})\leq Q_{n}
. Then

-Q_{n}\leq(T-T_{n})\leq Q_{n}\ \text{\rm and}\ 0\leq(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore

T=[(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}]+[T_{n}-Q_{n}]=

[(T-T_{n})+Q_{n}+G_{n}]-[R_{n}+Q_{n}]\in\text{\rm r-P}(E),

and hence
(T-T_{n})\in\text{\rm r-P}(E)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We conclude from
\|T-T_{n}\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\leq\|Q_{n}\|<2^{1-n}

that
T_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}}}{{\longrightarrow}}T
.
∎

Algebraic and lattice properties of regularly Lebesgue and KB operators

2.1

The following proposition contains a useful simple observation
(cf.
[
6
, Lemma 1]
and
[
2
, Lemma 2.1]
).

Proposition 1
.

Every
r
-Lebesgue
(resp.\
r
-
\sigma
-Lebesgue
)

operator is order continuous
(
resp.
\sigma
-continuous
)
.

Proof.

Let
T
be a regularly Lebesgue operator in
E
,
and let
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
in
E
. As
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
with
both
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
positive Lebesgue operators,

T_{1}x_{\alpha}\downarrow
and
\|T_{1}x_{\alpha}\|\to 0
.
By
[
1
, Theorem 2.14]
,
T_{1}x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
.
Also,
T_{2}x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
. Then
Tx_{\alpha}\convo 0
.
The
r
-
\sigma
-Lebesgue case is similar.
∎

Lemma 2
.

Let a net
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
in
\text{\rm L}_{Leb}(E)

satisfy
T_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\longrightarrow}}T

for some
T\in\text{\rm L}(E)
. Then
T\in\text{\rm L}_{Leb}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
in
E
.
Passing to a tail of the net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
,
we may assume that
\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq M
for some
M>0

and for all
\alpha
. Take an
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
be such that

\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}-T\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{M}
.
Since
T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}
is Lebesgue,

\lim_{\alpha}\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}x_{\alpha}\|=0
.
There exists
\alpha_{\varepsilon}
with

\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}x_{\alpha}\|\leq\varepsilon

for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\varepsilon}
, and hence

\|Tx_{\alpha}\|\leq\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}x_{\alpha}\|+\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}x_{\alpha}-Tx_{\alpha}\|\leq\varepsilon+\|T_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}-T\|\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq 2\varepsilon

for
\alpha\geq\alpha_{\varepsilon}
. Since
\varepsilon>0
is arbitrary,

T
is Lebesgue.
∎

Recall that P-operators satisfy the
domination property
if

0\leq S\leq T\in\text{\rm P}
implies
S\in\text{\rm P}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
E
be a Banach lattice. Then
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)

and
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}^{\sigma}(E)
are both left algebraic ideals
and hence subalgebras of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
. Furthermore
:

i)

\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)

(
resp.,
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}^{\sigma}(E)
)

coincides with
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
iff the norm of
E
is order
continuous
(
resp.,
\sigma
-order continuous
)
.

ii)

If
E
is Dedekind complete then
\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}=\|T\|_{r}

for each
T\in P:=\text{\rm L}_{Leb}(E)
, and

(\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}})\ \ \ \ (\text{\rm resp.,}\ (\text{\rm r-L}^{\sigma}_{Leb}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}))

is a Banach lattice algebra.

Proof.

We consider only
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
,
the case of
\text{\rm r-L}^{\sigma}_{Leb}(E)
is similar,
and skip routine direct checking that
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)

is a vector subspace of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
Let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
and
S\in\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
In order to show that
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
is a left
algebraic ideal, we need to show that
ST
is r-Lebesgue.
WLOG, we may assume
S,T\geq 0
.
Let
x_{\alpha}\downarrow 0
in
E
. Then
\|Tx_{\alpha}\|\to 0
,

\|S(Tx_{\alpha})\|\to 0
, and hence
ST
is r-Lebesgue.
As each left ideal is a subalgebra,
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)

is a subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
. Note that by the similar argument,

\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
is a left ideal of
\text{\rm L}(E)
.

i)
If
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
then the identity
operator
I
in
E
is Lebesgue, and hence the norm in
E
is order continuous.
Conversely, if the norm in
E
is order continuous then

I\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
and,
since
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
is a left ideal of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)

containing
I
then
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.

ii)
By Lemmas
1
and
2
,

(\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}})
is a Banach space.
Let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
, say
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
,
with
0\leq T_{1},T_{2}\in\text{\rm L}_{Leb}(E)
. Then
|T|\leq T_{1}+T_{2}
.
Since the Lebesgue operators satisfy the domination property,

|T|\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
. Hence
\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
is a vector lattice,
and
\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}=\|~|T|~\|
.
By formula (1),
\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}=\|T\|_{r}
.

For proving that
(\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}})

is a Banach lattice, we need to show

|S|\leq|T|\ \Rightarrow\ \|S\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\leq\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\ \ \ (\forall S,T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)).

[formula]

Let
S,T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
satisfy
|S|\leq|T|
.
Since
|S|,|T|\in{\text{\rm r-}}{\text{\cal L}}_{Leb}(E)
then

\|S\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}=\|~|S|~\|\leq\|~|T|~\|=\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}
,
that proves (5).

It remains to show that the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}
is submultiplicative.
Let
S,T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
. It follows from

\pm STx\leq|STx|\leq|S||Tx|\leq|S||T|x\ \ \ (\forall x\in E_{+})

that
\pm ST\leq|S||T|
. Thus,

\|ST\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\leq\|~|S||T|~\|\leq\|~|S|~\|\cdot\|~|T|~\|=\|S\|_{\text{\rm r-P}}\|T\|_{\text{\rm r-P}},

as desired.
∎

Proposition 2

Let

\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)=\text{\rm r-L}_{on}(E)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \text{\rm r-L}^{\sigma}_{Leb}(E)=\text{\rm r-L}^{\sigma}_{on}(E),

[formula]

where
\text{\rm r-L}_{on}(E)

(

\text{\rm r-L}^{\sigma}_{on}(E)

is the space of all

r-on
-continuous
(
resp.,
r-on
\sigma
-continuous
)
operators in

We restrict ourselves to the first equality. Since

\text{\rm r-L}_{on}(E)\subseteq\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)

\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)\subseteq\text{\rm r-L}_{on}(E)

Let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{Leb}(E)
, and let
x_{\alpha}\convo 0
.
WLOG, suppose
T\geq 0
.
There exists a net
y_{\beta}\downarrow 0

in
E
such that, for every

\alpha_{\beta}
with

for

for

\|Ty_{\beta}\|\to 0
and hence
\|T((x_{\alpha})^{\pm})\|\to 0
,
that implies
\|Tx_{\alpha}\|\to 0
.

Now we investigate algebraic structure of

Lemma 3
.

Let a net
(T_{\gamma})_{\gamma}
in
\text{\rm L}_{qKB}(E)
satisfies

T_{\gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\|\cdot\|}}{{\longrightarrow}}T

for some
T\in\text{\rm L}(E)
. Then
T\in\text{\rm L}_{qKB}(E)
.

Proof.

Let
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be an increasing norm bounded net in
E
.
WLOG,
\|x_{\alpha}\|\leq 1
for all
\alpha
.
Take
\varepsilon>0
and choose
\gamma_{0}
satisfying

\|T_{\gamma_{0}}-T\|\leq\varepsilon
.
Choose
\alpha_{0}
such that

\|T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}-T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|\leq\varepsilon

for
\alpha^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\geq\alpha_{0}
. Then

\|Tx_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}-Tx_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|\leq

\|Tx_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}-T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}\|+\|T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}-T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|+\|T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}-Tx_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|\leq

\|T_{\gamma_{0}}-T\|\|x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}\|+\|T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime}}}-T_{\gamma_{0}}x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|+\|T_{\gamma_{0}}-T\|\|x_{\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}}\|\leq 3\varepsilon

for all
\alpha^{{}^{\prime}},\alpha^{{}^{\prime\prime}}\geq\alpha_{0}
, and hence
T
is quasi
KB
.
∎

Theorem 2
.

\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)

(
resp.,
\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
)

is a left
(
resp., two sides
)
algebraic ideal of

\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
, and hence is a subalgebra of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
Furthermore
:

i)

The algebra
\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
is unital iff

\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
is unital iff
E
is a KB-space.

ii)

If
E
is Dedekind complete then

(\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E),\ \|\cdot\|_{\text{\rm r-P}})

is a Banach lattice algebra for
\text{\rm P}=\text{\rm L}_{qKB}(E)
.

Proof.

We omit the straightforward proof that

\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
and

\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)

are both vector subspaces of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
We need to show that
\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)

(resp.,
\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
) is closed under left (two sides)
multiplication by elements of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.

A)
Firstly consider
\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
.
Let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
and
S\in\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.
WLOG, suppose
T\geq 0
and
S\geq 0
. If
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

be an increasing norm bounded net in
E_{+}
, then
\|Tx_{\alpha}-Tx\|\to 0

for some
x\in E
and, since
S
is continuous, we obtain

\|STx_{\alpha}-STx\|\to 0
. Then
ST\in\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
,
showing that
\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
is a left ideal of
\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
.

B)
Now, let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
and
S\in\text{\rm L}_{r}(E)
,
and let
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be a norm bounded increasing net in
E_{+}
.
Then the net
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is
\|.\|
-Cauchy and, since
S
is continuous,
the net
(STx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is also
\|.\|
-Cauchy, showing

ST\in\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
.

Since the net
Sx_{\alpha}
is increasing and norm bounded in

,
we obtain that the net
(TSx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

\|.\|

Thus,
\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)

i)

ii)

3
Relations between KB and compact operators

Here we discuss relations between KB and compact operators in
E
.

3.1

We begin with the following fact.

Proposition 3
.

Every continuous finite rank operator
on a Banach lattice
E
is a regularly
KB
operator.

Proof.

Let
T\in\text{\rm L}(E)
and
\dim(TE)<\infty
. Then

T=\sum_{k=1}^{n}x_{k}\otimes f_{k}\ \text{for}\ x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in E\ \text{and}\ f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in E^{\ast}.

WLOG, we can assume
T=x_{1}\otimes f_{1}
.
Since the dual
E^{\ast}
is Dedekind complete,

f_{1}
is regular, and hence
T
is also regular.
WLOG, suppose
x_{1}\geq 0

and
f_{1}\geq 0
. Then
T
is positive, and hence regular.
Let
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
be an increasing
norm bounded net in
E_{+}
. Since

Tx_{\alpha}=(x_{1}\otimes f_{1})(x_{\alpha})=f_{1}(x_{\alpha})x_{1}\uparrow

and norm bounded, then
Tx_{\alpha}\to y\in\text{\rm span}(x_{1})

and
Tx_{\alpha}\convn Tx
for some
x\in E
.
Hence
T
is
r-KB
.
∎

It is worth to notice that, for a positive compact operator

T
in the Banach lattice
c
of real convergent sequences,
and, for a norm bounded increasing net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
c_{+}
, a vector
x\in c
,
satisfying
\|Tx_{\alpha}-Tx\|\to 0
, need not to be an upper bound
of
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
. Indeed, let
f
be a positive functional on
c
that
assigns to any element of
c
its limit.
Define
T:c\to c
by
Tx:=f(x)\cdot\mathbb{1}
, where
\mathbb{1}

is the real sequence identically equal to
1
.
Then
T
is a
KB
operator by Proposition
3
. Take

x_{n}:=(\underbrace{1,1,...,1}_{n},0,0,0,...)
in
c_{+}
.
Then
x_{n}\uparrow
and
\|x_{n}\|=1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Yet, for every
x\in c
satisfying
x_{n}\uparrow\leq x
, we have
\|Tx\|\geq 1

and hence
\|Tx_{n}-Tx\|=\|Tx\|\geq 1
for each
n
.

Proposition 4
.

Every r-compact operator
T
in a Banach lattice
E
is regularly quasi
KB
.

Proof.

WLOG, we restrict ourselves to the case when
T

is a positive compact operator.
Let a net
(x_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
in
E
be norm bounded
and
0\leq x_{\alpha}\uparrow
.
Since
T
is compact, the increasing net
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
has a subnet

(Tx_{\alpha_{\beta}})_{\beta}
with
\left\|Tx_{\alpha_{\beta}}-y\right\|\to 0

for some
y\in E
. In particular,
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
has a
\|.\|
-Cauchy subnet,
and hence
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
is also
\|.\|
-Cauchy, showing that
T
is quasi
KB
,
and since
T\geq 0
,
T
is
r-qKB
.
∎

3.2

The next example shows that the inclusion

\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)\subseteq\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)

can be proper.

Example 3
.

Denote elements of
c_{0}
by
x=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}\cdot e_{n}
,
where
e_{n}
are orts placed at
n
and
(a_{n})_{n}
is a real sequence converging to zero.
Define an operator
T:c_{0}\to c_{0}
by

T\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}e_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{n}}{n}e_{n}.

Then
T
is positive and compact, and hence a quasi
KB
operator
by Proposition
4
. Thus
T\in{\text{\rm r-}}{\text{\cal L}}_{qKB}(E)
.
Take an increasing norm bounded sequence
(x_{n})_{n}
in
c_{0}
by

x_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}
. The sequence

(Tx_{n})_{n}=\bigl(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}\bigl)_{n}
is norm-Cauchy in
c_{0}
,
however there is no element
\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}e_{k}
in

satisfying

T\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}e_{k}\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}=\|\cdot\|_{c_{0}}\text{-}\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}Tx_{n}.

-operator.

3.3

Our next result develops the idea of Example
3
.

Theorem 3
.

In a Banach lattice
E
with order continuous norm the following are equivalent
:

1)

E
is a
KB
-space
;

2)

every positive compact operator in
E
is
KB
.

Proof.

The implication
1)\Rightarrow 2)
is obvious, as any continuous operator
on a
KB
-space is a
KB
-operator.

2)\Rightarrow 1)
Assume, in contrary, that
E
be not a
KB
-space.
Then there is a homeomorphic lattice embedding

c_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\longrightarrow}}E
, say

i((a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}\cdot e_{k}
,
where
(a_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}\in c_{0}
. WLOG, assume that
the sequence
(e_{k})_{k}
in
E_{+}
is pairwise disjoint and normalized.
Let
f_{k}
be norm-one functionals on
E
such that
f_{k}(e_{k})=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then
|f_{k}|
are norm-one positive functionals on
E
with

|f_{k}|(e_{k})=1
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Indeed
\||f_{k}|\|=\|f_{k}\|=1
and

1=|f_{k}(e_{k})|\leq|f_{k}|(e_{k})=\sup\{|f_{k}(x)|:|x|\leq e_{k}\}\leq

\sup\{|f_{k}(x)|:\|x\|\leq 1\}=\|f_{k}\|=1

for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
P_{k}
be the band projection onto the principle
band
B_{e_{k}}
generated by
e_{k}
. Define a positive operator
T:E\to E
by

Tx=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{|f_{k}|(P_{k}(x))}{k}e_{k}.

[formula]

Since
||f_{k}|(P_{k}(x))|\leq\||f_{k}|\|\cdot\|P_{k}(x)\|\leq\|x\|
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
the formula (7) implies

T(B_{E})\subseteq i([-u,u]),

[formula]

where
u:=\bigl(\frac{1}{k}\bigl)_{k}\in c_{0}
.
As order intervals of
c_{0}
are compact,
the set
i([-u,u])
is compact in
E
.
It follows from (8) that
T
is a compact operator.
By the assumption
2)
,
T
is a
KB
-operator.
Consider an increasing norm bounded sequence
y_{n}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}e_{k}
in
E
.
Then there is some
y\in E
such that
\|Ty_{n}-Ty\|\to 0
. Since

Ty_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|f_{k}|(P_{k}(y_{n}))}{k}e_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{|f_{k}|(e_{k})}{k}e_{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
Ty=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}
.
Let
w_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}P_{k}(y)
. Then
w_{n}\convo y
.
Since the norm in
E
is order continuous,
\|w_{n}-y\|\to 0
.
Then the sequence
(w_{n})_{n}
is norm-Cauchy.
It follows from
\|Ty_{n}-Ty\|\to 0
,
Ty_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}
, and

Ty=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}e_{k}
that
|f_{n}|(P_{n}(y_{n}))=|f_{n}|(P_{n}(y))
.
Then

1=|f_{n}|(e_{n})=|f_{n}|(P_{n}(y_{n}))=|f_{n}|(P_{n}(y))\leq\|P_{n}(y)\|,

violating
\|P_{n}(y)\|=\|w_{n}-w_{n-1}\|\to 0
.
The obtained contradiction completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
E
be a Banach lattice with order continuous norm.
The following conditions are equivalent.

1)

E
is a
KB
-space.

2)

\text{\rm L}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{KB}(E)
.

3)

\text{\rm L}_{qKB}(E)=\text{\rm L}_{KB}(E)
.

4)

\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)=\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
.

5)

(\text{\rm L}_{qKB})_{+}(E)=(\text{\rm L}_{KB})_{+}(E)
.

6)

Every positive compact operator in
E
is
KB
.

Proof.

1)\Rightarrow 2)\Rightarrow 3)
is obvious.

3)\Rightarrow 4)
Let
T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{qKB}(E)
, say
T=T_{1}-T_{2}
with

T_{1},T_{2}\in(\text{\rm L}_{qKB})_{+}(E)
. Then

T_{1},T_{2}\in(\text{\rm L}_{KB})_{+}(E)
, and hence

T\in\text{\rm r-L}_{KB}(E)
.

4)\Rightarrow 5)
is obvious.

5)\Rightarrow 6)
Let
T
be a positive compact operator in
E
.
Let
(B_{E})_{+}\ni x_{\alpha}\uparrow
. By compactness of
T
,
the net
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}
contains a norm convergent
subnet, and, since
Tx_{\alpha}\uparrow
then the net
(Tx_{\alpha})_{\alpha}

is norm convergent. Thus
T\in(\text{\rm L}_{qKB})_{+}(E)
, and hence

T\in(\text{\rm L}_{KB})(E)
.

6)\Rightarrow 1)
follows from Theorem
3
.
∎

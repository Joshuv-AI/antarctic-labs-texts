Abstract
. In this article, we study two different types of operators, the localization operator and Weyl transform, on the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} . The group 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} is a quotient group of non-isotropic Heisenberg group with multidimensional center ℋ m {\mathcal{H}}^{m} by its center subgroup. Firstly, we define the localization operator using a wavelet transform on 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} and obtain the product formula for the localization operators. Next, we define the Weyl transform associated to the Wigner transform on 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} with the operator-valued symbol. Finally, we have shown that the Weyl transform is not only a bounded operator but also a compact operator when the operator-valued symbol is in L p , 1 ≤ p ≤ 2 , L^{p},1\leq p\leq 2, and it is an unbounded operator when p > 2 p>2 .

1.
Introduction

In
[
5
]
, I. Daubechies introduced the localization operator using the short-time Fourier transform.
These operators are used to localize the signal on a time-frequency plane to get information about the position and frequency in signal analysis, image processing, etc., (see
[
6
,
5
]
). The localization operators are pseudo-differential operators associated with the so called anti-wick symbols and their applications can be found in the framework of PDEs and quantum mechanics,
[
7
,
9
]
.
In
[
20
,
10
]
Wong et al. studied localization operators corresponding to wavelet transforms and proved that under suitable conditions on the symbols the operators are paracommutators, paraproducts and Fourier multipliers.

Recall that the symbol of the composition of pseudo-differential operators on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be expressed in terms of the asymptotic expansion. Similar kind of results for localization operators are obtained in
[
4
]
,
whereas in
[
8
]
, Wong et al., expressed the symbol of the product of localization operators in terms of some new convolution using the Weyl transform on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Motivated by
[
8
,
4
]
, first we establish the localization operator with respect to the wavelet transform on the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center,
{\mathcal{G}}
, and obtain the product formula of two localization operators. We expressed the product formula using a new convolution among the corresponding symbols. To obtain this result the important tool is the
k
-Weyl transform which has been extensively studied in
[
11
]
.

The classical Weyl transform was first introduced by H. Weyl in
[
18
]
to solve the quantization problem in quantum mechanics. The Weyl transform has been broadly used in the field of PDEs, harmonic analysis, time-frequency analysis, etc., (see
[
15
,
16
]
). It is one of the powerful tool in solving problems like regularity, ellipticity, spectral asymptotic, etc., the reader can follow the book
[
19
]
for their applications.

Let
\sigma
be a suitable function on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Then the classical Weyl transform
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
is an operator on the Schwartz space
S(\mathbb{R}^{n})
defined by

({\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}f)(x)=(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{i(x-y)\cdot\xi}\sigma(\frac{x+y}{2},\xi)f(y)dyd\xi,\quad f\in S(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Also we can represent the Weyl transform,
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}
, in terms of Wigner transform, for any
f,g\in S(\mathbb{R}^{n})

\langle{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\langle{\mathcal{W}}(f,g),\overline{\sigma}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})},

where

{\mathcal{W}}(f,g)(x,\xi)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-i\xi\cdot p}f(x+p/2)\overline{g(x-p/2)}dp,

for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Here we recall the following theorem,
[
19
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\sigma
be in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Then the Weyl transform
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator.

Assume
\sigma\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n}),1\leq p\leq 2
, then the Weyl transform
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a compact operator. But for each
2<p<\infty
, there exists a symbol
\sigma\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
such that the Weyl transform
{\mathcal{W}}_{\sigma}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is not bounded operator. One can refer Wong’s book
[
19
]
for the proofs .

The above results for Weyl transform associated with the Wigner transform have been also studied in other settings like the Heisenberg group
[
12
]
, upper half plane
[
13
]
, quaternion Heisenberg group
[
3
]
. In
[
14
,
21
]
, authors defined the Weyl transform associated to Wigner transform using spherical mean operator and also studied their properties. In Heisenberg group the group center,
Z({\mathcal{H}})
, coincide with the set

\{(0,0,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. Here the periodicity in
t\in\mathbb{R}
of the Schrödinger representation,
\rho_{\lambda}
can be inconvenient in the context of some applications. It is neither faithful nor square integrable. Therefore it is useful to occasionally use the so called reduced Heisenberg group,

{\mathcal{H}}_{\text{red}}={\mathcal{H}}/\{(0,0,t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Here we consider the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center, denoted by
{\mathcal{G}}
. Our aim is to study the Weyl transform using the operator-valued symbol on the reduced Heisenberg group
{\mathcal{G}}
, and then investigate all aforementioned properties of Weyl transform for this group
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Apart from the introduction the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we describe the square-integrable representation, group Fourier transform, Plancherel formula and inverse Fourier transform. In Section
3
, we define the localization operator and also find their connection with the
k
-Weyl transform. Then obtain the product formula of the localization operators. In the final section, we define the Weyl transform on the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center associated to the Wigner transform and characterize symbol spaces in terms of boundedness and unboundedness of the Weyl transform(operator).

2.
Harmonic Analysis on Reduced Heisenberg group with multi-dimensional center

Let us fix
(c_{1},c_{2},\cdots,c_{n})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The nonisotropic Heisenberg group on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
is defined by

(x,y,t)\ast(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\Big(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}c_{j}(x_{j}y_{j}^{\prime}-x_{j}^{\prime}y_{j})\Big),

(2.1)

for all
(x,y,t),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
. Put
c_{j}=1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
. Then we get the ordinary Heisenberg group (see
[
17
]
), with one dimensional center,

Z=\{(0,0,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
. One can observe that the group operation in (
2.1
) does not contain
x_{i}x_{j}^{\prime},i\neq j
. In
[
11
]
, the author introduced the terms
x_{i}x_{j}^{\prime},i\neq j
, in (
2.1
) in a suitable manner by taking some orthogonal matrices in the group operation (
2.1
). More precisely, the group is defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}

with the operation

(x,y,t)\ast(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\Big(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[z,z^{\prime}]\Big),

for all
(x,y,t),(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
z=(x,y),z^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}
. Here
[z,z^{\prime}]
in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
, is defined by

[z,z^{\prime}]_{j}=x^{\prime}\cdot B_{j}y-x\cdot B_{j}y^{\prime},j=1,2,\cdots,m,

where
B_{1},B_{2},\cdots,B_{m}
are
n\times n
orthogonal matrices such that

B_{j}^{-1}B_{k}=-B_{k}^{-1}B_{j},j\neq k.

We denote the group by
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
, and the dimension of the center
Z
, given by

Z=\{(0,0,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}:t\in\mathbb{R}^{m}\},

is
m
. This group
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
is known as nonisotropic Heisenberg group with multi-dimensional center. The relation between dimensions
n
and
m
is
m\leq n^{2}
,
[
11
, Proposition 2]
.
The irreducible, unitary representations of
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
, namely the Schrödinger representations on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, are given by

\pi_{\lambda}(q,p,t)\varphi(x)=e^{i\lambda\cdot t}e^{iq\cdot B_{\lambda}(x+p/2)}\varphi(x+p),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n},0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{m\ast},

(2.2)

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(q,p,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m},
where
B_{\lambda}=\lambda_{1}B_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}B_{m},
and
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. It is easy to check that

B_{\lambda}B_{\lambda}^{t}=|\lambda|^{2}I,

where
I
is the identity
n\times n
matrix, and
|\lambda|=\sqrt{\lambda_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{m}^{2}}
. In particular
\det B_{\lambda}=|\lambda|^{n}
.

Let
H=\{(0,0,2\pi t):t\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}\}
. It can be easily shown that, this is a normal subgroup of
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
under the operation
\ast
. Now define the quotient group
{\mathcal{G}}={{\mathcal{H}}^{m}}/{H}
with the same operation
\ast
,

(x,y,t)\ast(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\Big(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[z,z^{\prime}]\Big),

where the coordinates of
t+t^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}[z,z^{\prime}]
are modulo
2\pi
. We call the group,
{\mathcal{G}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
, the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center, where
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
is the
m
-dimensional torus.

The one-dimensional representations of the nonisotropic Heisenberg group
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
are still representations
of
{\mathcal{G}}
but the Schrödinger representations,
\pi_{\lambda}
of
{\mathcal{H}}^{m}
are representations
of
{\mathcal{G}}
only when
0\neq\lambda\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
. For better understanding we refer
[
17
]
. These representations are relevant to the Plancherel theorem.

Definition 2.1

(Fourier transform)
.

Let
f
be in
L^{1}({\mathcal{G}})
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
. Define the group Fourier transform
({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)
of
f
at
k
by

({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\varphi=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(q,p,t)\pi_{k}(q,p,t)\varphi d\mu(q,p,t),\quad\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
d\mu(q,p,t)=\dfrac{dqdpdt}{(2\pi)^{m}}
.

For
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}
, let us denote

f^{k}(q,p):=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{m}}e^{ik\cdot t}f(q,p,t)\frac{dt}{(2\pi)^{m}},

the inverse Fourier transform of
f
in
t=(t_{1},t_{2},\cdots,t_{m})
on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
. So the group Fourier transform can be rewritten

({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{k}(q,p)\pi_{k}(q,p)dqdp,

where
\pi_{k}(q,p)\varphi(x)=e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(x+p/2)}\varphi(x+p),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Next we prove the Plancherel formula for
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Theorem 2.2

(Plancherel Theorem)
.

Let
f
be in
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
. Then
({\mathcal{F}}f)(k):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a Hilbert-Schmidt operator and

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{2}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}=\|f-f^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}^{2},

(2.3)

where
\|k\|_{2}=\left(|k_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|k_{m}|^{2}\right)^{1/2},
and

f^{0}(q,p,t)=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{T}}^{m}}f(q,p,t)\dfrac{dt}{(2\pi)^{m}}.

Furthermore, for
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
, we have

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\operatorname{tr}\left[({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)({\mathcal{F}}g)(k)^{\ast}\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}=\langle f-f^{0},g-g^{0}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then by using the Fourier transform of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{k}(q,p)\pi_{k}(q,p)\varphi(x)dqdp

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f^{k}(q,p)e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(x+\frac{p}{2})}\varphi(x+p)dqdp

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{k}(q,p-x)e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(\frac{x+p}{2})}dq\right)\varphi(p)dp

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}N_{k}(x,p)\varphi(p)dp,

where

N_{k}(x,p)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f^{k}(q,p-x)e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(\frac{x+p}{2})}dq=(2\pi)^{n/2}\widehat{f^{k}}\left(-B_{k}\left(\frac{x+p}{2}\right),p-x\right).

By using the Plancherel theorem for the Fourier transform on
\mathbb{R}^{2n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|N_{k}(x,p)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\widehat{f^{k}}\left(-B_{k}\left(\frac{x+p}{2}\right),p-x\right)\right|^{2}dxdp

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\left|\widehat{f^{k}}\left(x,p\right)\right|^{2}\|k\|_{2}^{-n}dxdp

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\|k\|_{2}^{-n}\|f^{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}^{2}<\infty.

Using the Plancherel formula for
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{2}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|f^{k}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}|f^{k}(q,p)|^{2}dqdp

(2.4)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}|h^{k}(q,p)|^{2}dqdp

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{m}}|h(q,p,t)|^{2}\dfrac{dqdpdt}{(2\pi)^{m}}=\|f-f^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}^{2},

(2.5)

where
h(q,p,t)=f(q,p,t)-f^{0}(q,p,t)
, and then
h\in L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
and

h^{k}(q,p)=\begin{cases}f^{k}(q,p),&\text{for}\quad k\neq 0\\
0,&\text{for}\quad k=0.\end{cases}

∎

Remark 2.3
.

If we add
\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|f^{0}(q,p)|^{2}dqdp
in (
2.4
), we obtain the exact analogue of Plancherel formula

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\|_{{\mathcal{HS}}}^{2}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|\widehat{(f^{0})}(x,y)|^{2}dxdy=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}|f(q,p,t)|^{2}\frac{dqdpdt}{(2\pi)^{m}}.

(2.6)

In the next theorem we derive the inverse Fourier transform.

Theorem 2.4

(Inverse Fourier transform)
.

Let
f
be in the Schwartz space
S({\mathcal{G}})
. Then the inversion formula is given by

f(q,p,t)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right)(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}+f^{0}(q,p,t).

Moreover,

\displaystyle f(q,p,t)=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\operatorname{tr}\left(\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right)(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}+(2\pi)^{-n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{i(q\cdot x+q\cdot y)}\widehat{(f^{0})}(x,y)dxdy.

Proof.

For all
(q,p,t)
in
{\mathcal{G}}
and
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
,

\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)=\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}g(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(2.7)

where
g_{(q,p,t)}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})=e^{-i\frac{k}{2}\cdot[z,z^{\prime}]}f(q^{\prime}+q,p^{\prime}+p,t^{\prime}+t)
and
z=(q,p),z^{\prime}=(q^{\prime},p^{\prime}).
Then (
2.7
) can rewrite as

\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)=({\mathcal{F}}g_{(q,p,t)})(k).

The kernel of
({\mathcal{F}}g_{(q,p,t)})(k)
is given by

N_{k}^{(q,p,t)}(x,y)=(2\pi)^{n/2}\widehat{g_{(q,p,t)}^{k}}\left(-B_{k}\left(\frac{x+y}{2}\right),y-x\right).

Also

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left(\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}N_{k}^{(q,p,t)}(x,x)dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n/2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{g^{k}_{(q,p,t)}}\left(-B_{k}x,0\right)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{iy\cdot B_{k}x}g^{k}_{(q,p,t)}(y,0)dy.

On the other hand, we can find

g_{(q,p,t)}^{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime})=e^{-i\frac{k}{2}\cdot[z,z^{\prime}]}e^{-ik\cdot t}f^{k}(q^{\prime}+q,p^{\prime}+p).

Then

g_{(q,p,t)}^{k}(y,0)=e^{-i\frac{y}{2}\cdot B_{k}p}e^{-ik.t}f^{k}(y+q,p).

Thus

\displaystyle\text{tr}\left(\pi_{k}(q,p,t)^{\ast}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{iy\cdot B_{k}x}e^{-i\frac{y}{2}\cdot B_{k}p}e^{-ik.t}f^{k}(y+q,p)dxdy

\displaystyle=e^{-ik.t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{i(y-q)\cdot B_{k}x}e^{-i\frac{y-q}{2}\cdot B_{k}p}f^{k}(y,p)dxdy

\displaystyle=e^{-ik.t}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}e^{-iq\cdot B_{k}x}e^{iy\cdot\left(B_{k}x-B_{k}\frac{p}{2}\right)}f^{k}(y,p)dxdy

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-ik\cdot t}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}\frac{p}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-iq\cdot B_{k}x}\left({\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{-1}f^{k}\right)\left(B_{k}x-B_{k}\frac{p}{2},p\right)dx

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n/2}e^{-ik.\cdot t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{-iq\cdot x}\left({\mathcal{F}}_{1}^{-1}f^{k}\right)(x,p)\frac{dx}{\|k\|_{2}^{n}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{n}\|k\|_{2}^{-n}e^{-ik\cdot t}f^{k}(q,p).

By using the inverse Fourier transform on
{\mathbb{T}}^{m}
, we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\pi_{k}^{\ast}(q,p,t)({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}e^{-ik\cdot t}f^{k}(q,p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m}}e^{-ik\cdot t}h^{k}(q,p)

\displaystyle=h(q,p,t)=(f-f^{0})(q,p,t).

∎

The next theorem shows that all the Schrödinger representations
\pi_{k}
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
, are square integrable representations.

Theorem 2.5
.

For
k=(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
,

\pi_{k}(q,p,t)\varphi(x)=e^{ik\cdot t}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(x+p/2)}\varphi(x+p),x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for all
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
(q,p,t)\in{\mathcal{G}},
where
B_{k}=k_{1}B_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}B_{m},
are square integrable representations.

Proof.

Using the Plancherel theorem for
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}|\langle\varphi,\pi_{k}(q,p,t)\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|^{2}dpdqdt

\displaystyle=\int_{[0,2\pi]^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)e^{-ik\cdot t}e^{-iq\cdot B_{k}(x+p/2)}\overline{\phi(x+p)}dx\Big\rvert^{2}dpdqdt

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)e^{-iq\cdot B_{k}(x+p/2)}\overline{\phi(x+p)}dx\Big\rvert^{2}dpdq

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{m}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\lvert\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(\varphi T_{-p}\overline{\varphi})(x)e^{-iB_{k}^{t}(q)\cdot x}e^{-iB_{k}(p/2)}dx\Big\rvert^{2}dpdq

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{m+n}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\Big\lvert\widehat{(\varphi T_{-p}\overline{\varphi})}(B_{k}^{t}q)\Big\rvert^{2}dpdq

\displaystyle=|\det(B_{k})|^{-1}(2\pi)^{m+n}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{4}<\infty,

where
(\varphi T_{-p}{\varphi})(x)=\varphi(x)\varphi(x+p).

∎

Let
f,g
be in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Recall the
k
-Fourier Wigner transform
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(f,g)
of
f
and
g
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, are defined by

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(f,g)(q,p)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\langle\pi_{k}(q,p)f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

We can relate the ordinary Fourier Wigner transform
{\mathcal{V}}(f,g)
with the
k
-Fourier Wigner transform
{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(f,g)
by

{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(f,g)(q,p)={\mathcal{V}}(f,g)(B_{k}^{t}q,p).

Then the
k
-Wigner transform
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}(f,g)
of
f
and
g
is defined as the Fourier transform of
k
-Fourier Wigner transform, i.e.,

{\mathcal{W}}^{k}(f,g)=\widehat{{\mathcal{V}}_{k}(f,g)},

for all
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Again one can relate the
k
-Wigner transform
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}(f,g)
with ordinary Wigner transform
{\mathcal{W}}(f,g)
by

{\mathcal{W}}^{k}(f,g)(x,\xi)=\|k\|_{2}^{n}{\mathcal{W}}(f,g)\left(\frac{1}{|k|^{2}}B_{k}^{t}x,\xi\right),

for all
x,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Let
\sigma
be in the Schwartz space,
S(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n})
, of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the
k
-Weyl transform
{\mathcal{W}}^{k}_{\sigma}

\displaystyle-c\left(|B_{k}(\xi-q)|^{2}+|\eta-p|^{2}\right)-c\left(|B_{k}q|^{2}+|p|^{2}\right)+{\frac{1}{4}\left(|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|q|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|q|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=-c\Big(\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|q|^{2}-2\langle B_{k}\xi,B_{k}q\rangle+|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}-2\langle\eta,p\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle-c\left(\|k\|_{2}^{2}|q|^{2}+|p|^{2}\right)+{\frac{1}{4}\left(|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|q|^{2}\right)}-\frac{1}{2}\left(|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|q|^{2}\right).

(3.7)

For each
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle 2c\langle B_{k}\xi,B_{k}q\rangle

\displaystyle\leq 2c|B_{k}\xi||B_{k}q|

\displaystyle=2c\sqrt{\epsilon}|B_{k}\xi|\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{\epsilon}}|B_{k}q|\leq c\|k\|_{2}^{2}\left(\epsilon|\xi|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}|q|^{2}\right),

and similarly

\displaystyle 2c\langle\eta,p\rangle\leq c\left(\epsilon|\eta|^{2}+\dfrac{1}{\epsilon}|p|^{2}\right).

For each
\epsilon>0

\displaystyle-c\left(|B_{k}(\xi-q)|^{2}+|\eta-p|^{2}\right)-c\left(|B_{k}q|^{2}+|p|^{2}\right)+{\frac{1}{4}\left(|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|q|^{2}\right)}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\left(|p|^{2}+\|k\|_{2}^{2}|q|^{2}\right)\leq\left(\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\right)\left(-c+c\epsilon+\frac{1}{4}\right)+\left(|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}\right)\left(-2c+\frac{c}{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{4}\right).

Now the inequality (

\displaystyle\left|\Big(\widehat{F^{k}}\circledast\widehat{G^{k}}\Big)(\xi,\eta)\right|

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\left(c-c\epsilon-\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(\|k\|_{2}^{2}|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2}\right)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}|f(\xi-q,\eta-p)||g(q,p)|e^{-\left(2c-\frac{c}{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{4}\right)\left(|\eta|^{2}+|p|^{2}\right)}dqdp.

(3.8)

Hence from (

) and (
3

H\in W_{c_{\epsilon}}^{k}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Thus by the fact,
W_{c}^{k}\subseteq W_{d}^{k}
if

H\in\bigcap_{0<d\leq c_{\epsilon}}W_{d}^{k}.

∎

4.
Weyl Transform on Reduced Heisenberg group with Multi-dimensional center

In this section, we study the boundedness of Weyl transform associated to the Wigner transform on the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Let us denote
L^{r}\left({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{r},(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|^{n}_{2}d\mu(q,p,t)\right),

1\leq r<\infty
, the space of all operator valued functions
F
on
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
such that

\|F\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}^{r}=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|F(q,p,t,k)\|_{S_{r}}^{r}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|^{n}_{2}d\mu(q,p,t)<\infty,

and for
r=\infty

\|F\|_{\infty,\mu\otimes\alpha}=\operatorname{ess~sup}\limits_{(q,p,t,k)\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|F(q,p,t,k)\|_{S_{\infty}}<\infty,

where
\mu\otimes\alpha
is the product measure on
{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
, given by

d(\mu\otimes\alpha)=d\mu(q,p,t)d\alpha(k)=d\mu(q,p,t)(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}=\|k\|_{2}^{n}\dfrac{dqdpdt}{(2\pi)^{m+n}},

and
S_{r}
denotes
r
-Schatten-von Neumann class.

We want to show, the Weyl transform is a bounded operator on
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
when the symbol belongs to
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{r},d(\mu\otimes\alpha)),

1\leq r\leq 2
, and for every
2<r<\infty
, there exists an unbounded Weyl transform with symbol in
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{r},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))
.

Definition 4.1
.

The translation operator on
L^{1}({\mathcal{G}})
is given by

\tau_{(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})}f(q,p,t)=f((q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{-1}\ast(q,p,t)),

for all
(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime}),(q,p,t)\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
f,g
be in
S({\mathcal{G}})
. Then the Wigner transform associated to the reduced Heisenberg with multidimensional center
{\mathcal{G}}
is defined by

V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\tau_{(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})}g(q,p,t)\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\quad(q,p,t)\in{\mathcal{G}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}.

Now we can write the Wigner transform as the group Fourier transform.

V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)={({\mathcal{F}}f\cdot\tau_{\cdot}g(q,p,t))}(k),(q,p,t)\in{\mathcal{G}},k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
f_{1},f_{2},g_{1},g_{2}
be in
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)
. Then

\langle V(f_{1},g_{1}),V(f_{2},g_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{2},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))}=\langle f_{1}-f_{1}^{0},f_{2}-f_{2}^{0}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)}\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)}.

Proof.

Now by definitions
4.2
,
4.1
and Theorem
2.2
, we get

\displaystyle\langle V(f_{1},g_{1}),V(f_{2},g_{2})\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{2},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\text{tr}\left[V(f_{1},g_{1})(q,p,t,k)V(f_{2},g_{2})(q,p,t,k)^{\ast}\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[{\mathcal{F}}(f_{1}\tau g_{1}(q,p,t))(k){\mathcal{F}}(f_{2}\tau g_{2}(q,p,t))(k)^{\ast}\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\langle f_{1}-f_{1}^{0},f_{2}-f_{2}^{0}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)}\langle g_{1},g_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)}.

∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
f
be in
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)
and
g
be in
L^{r^{\prime}}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)
, for
r\in[1,2]
. Then

\|V(f,g)\|_{r^{\prime},\mu\otimes\alpha}\leq c_{r}\|f\|_{L^{r}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}({\mathcal{G}})},

where
c_{r}
is a constant depending on
r
.

Proof.

For
r=2
, by Proposition
4.3
, we get

\|V(f,g)\|_{2,\mu\otimes\alpha}=\|f-f^{0}\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\leq 2\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}.

For
r=1
,

\displaystyle\|V(f,g)\|_{\infty,\mu\otimes\alpha}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ess~sup}\limits_{(q,p,t,k)\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)\|_{S_{\infty}},

\displaystyle=\operatorname{ess~sup}\limits_{(q,p,t,k)\in{\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\|{\mathcal{F}}(f\tau g(q,p,t)(k))\|_{S_{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq c\operatorname{ess~sup}\limits_{(q,p,t)\in{\mathcal{G}}}\|f\tau g(q,p,t)\|_{L^{1}({\mathcal{G}})}

\displaystyle=c\|f\|_{L^{1}(G)}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(G)}.

(4.1)

Now the proof can be followed from interpolation theory of operators,
[
2
]
.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
f,g
be in
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
. Then

V(f,g)\in L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{r},d(\mu\otimes\alpha)),\quad r\in[2,\infty].

Proof.

The proof follows from Proposition
4.4
and using the fact
f,g\in L^{2}(G)
, then
fg\in L^{1}(G)
.

∎

The following theorem represents the group Fourier transform in terms of the Wigner transform.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
f,g
be in
L^{1}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)\cap L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)
and

C=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}g(q,p,t)d\mu(q,p,t)\neq 0
. Then for any
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
, we have

({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)=\frac{1}{C}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t).

Proof.

Using the definition of translation operator and the group Fourier transform, we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{C}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{C}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})g\left((q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})^{-1}(q,p,t)\right)\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime}t^{\prime})d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{C}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})g(x,y,s)\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right)d\mu(x,y,s)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{1}{C}({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}g(x,y,s)d\mu(x,y,s)=({\mathcal{F}}f)(k).

∎

The following corollary is the inversion formula for the Wigner transform.

Corollary 4.7
.

With the hypothesis of Theorem
4.6
and if
{{\mathcal{F}}f}(k)\in\ell^{1}({\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{1},(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n})
, then we have

f(q,p,t)-f^{0}(q,p,t)=C^{-1}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\operatorname{tr}\left[\pi_{k}^{\ast}(q,p,t)\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,g)(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime},k)d\mu(q^{\prime}p^{\prime}t^{\prime})\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}.

Proof.

By Fourier inversion theorem and Theorem
4.6

\displaystyle f(q,p,t)-f^{0}(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\pi_{k}^{\ast}(q,p,t)({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=C^{-1}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\pi_{k}^{\ast}(q,p,t)\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,g)(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime},k)d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}.

∎

Now we study the Weyl transform associated to Wigner transform on the reduced Heisenberg group with multidimensional center.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
\sigma
be in
L^{p}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{p},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))
. Then the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
of
\sigma
, an operator on some space of
G
, is defined by

\displaystyle\langle W_{\sigma}f,\overline{g}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}},d\mu)}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}W_{\sigma}f(q,p,t)g(q,p,t)d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\langle V(f,g),\sigma\rangle_{\mu\otimes\alpha}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\sigma^{\ast}(q,p,t,k)V(f,g)(q,p,t,k)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t),

(4.2)

for all
f,g
in
S(G)
.

The kernel representation of the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
is given by

W_{\sigma}f(q,p,t)=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}K(q,p,t,q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})f(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime}),

(4.3)

with the kernel

K(q,p,t,q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\sigma^{\ast}\left((q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})(q,p,t),k\right)\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}.

The following two theorems state the boundedness of Weyl transform when the symbol
\sigma
belonging to
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{1},d(\mu\otimes\alpha)),1\leq r\leq 2.

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
\sigma
be in
L^{1}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{1},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))
. Then the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
is in Schatten-von Neumann class
S_{1}
.

Proof.

Using the fact,
|\text{tr}(T)|\leq\text{tr}(|T|)
, we get

\displaystyle\|W_{\sigma}\|_{S_{1}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}|K(q,p,t,q,p,t)|d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\sigma^{\ast}\left((q,p,t)(q,p,t),k\right)\pi_{k}(q,p,t)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right|d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\sigma^{\ast}\left(q,p,t,k\right)\pi_{k}(q/2,p/2,t/2)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right|2^{-2n-m}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[|\sigma^{\ast}(q,p,t,k)|\right]2^{-2n-m}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=2^{-2n-m}\|\sigma\|_{1,\mu\otimes\alpha}.

∎

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
\sigma
be in
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{2},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))
. Then the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
is in the Schatten-von Neumann class
S_{2}
.

Proof.

By using the fact
\text{tr}(A^{\ast})=\text{tr}(A)
, the group Fourier inversion transform and Plancherel formula, we get

\displaystyle\|W_{\sigma}\|^{2}_{S_{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}|K(q,p,t,q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})|^{2}d\mu(q,p,t)d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\sigma^{\ast}((q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})(q,p,t),k)\pi_{k}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right|^{2}d\mu(q,p,t)d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left|\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\text{tr}\left[\pi_{k}^{\ast}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\sigma(q,p,t,k)\right](2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right|^{2}d\mu(q,p,t)d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left|\left({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\sigma(q,p,t,\cdot)\right)(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})-\left({\mathcal{F}}^{-1}\sigma(q,p,t,\cdot)\right)^{0}(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right|^{2}d\mu(q,p,t)d\mu(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\sigma(q,p,t,k)\right\rVert_{S_{2}}^{2}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=\|\sigma\|_{2,\mu\otimes\alpha}.

∎

Here we recall that Hermite polynomials
H_{k}(x)
are of the form

H_{k}(x)=(-1)^{k}\dfrac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}(e^{-x^{2}})e^{x^{2}},\quad k=0,1,2,\cdots

on the real line. Then the Hermite functions
\widetilde{h_{k}}
are defined by

\widetilde{h_{k}}(x)=H_{k}(x)e^{\frac{-1}{2}x^{2}},k=0,1,2,\cdots,

and the normalized Hermite functions are given by

h_{k}(x)=(2^{k}k!\sqrt{\pi})^{\frac{-1}{2}}\widetilde{h_{k}}(x).

Now we can define the Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by taking the product of
n
Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\gamma
be a multi-index and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The Hermite function
\Phi_{\gamma}
is given by

\Phi_{\gamma}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}h_{\gamma_{j}}(x_{j}).

The set
\{\Phi_{\gamma}:\gamma\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}_{+}\}
of all Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
forms an orthonormal system for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. These functions are eigen vectors of the Hermite operator
H=\Delta+\|x\|^{2}_{2}
, i.e.,

H\Phi_{\gamma}=(2|\gamma|+n)\Phi_{\gamma},

where
|\gamma|=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\cdots+\gamma_{n}
and
\|x\|^{2}=|x_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}+\cdots+|x_{n}|^{2}
.

Let
\alpha>-1
. Then for
k=0,1,2,\cdots,
the Laguerre polynomial of degree
k
and order
\alpha
,
L_{k}^{\alpha}
on
(0,\infty)
is defined by

L_{k}^{\alpha}(x)=\frac{x^{-\alpha}e^{x}}{k!}\frac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}(e^{-x}x^{\alpha+k}).

We now recall the special Hermite functions as the Fourier-Wigner transform of the Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For each pair of multi-indices
\gamma
and
\eta
, we define
\Phi_{\gamma\eta}(z)={\mathcal{V}}(\Phi_{\gamma},\Phi_{\eta})(z)
. One can show that the special Hermite functions
\Phi_{\gamma\eta}
forms a complete orthonormal system in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2n})
. Also there is a relation between special Hermite functions and Laguerre polynomials, which is,

\Phi_{\gamma\gamma}(z)=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}L_{\gamma_{j}}^{0}(\frac{1}{2}|z_{j}|^{2})e^{\frac{-1}{4}|z_{j}|^{2}},\quad z_{j}=(x_{j},y_{j})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}.

(4.4)

In the next theorem we prove the unboundedness of the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
.

Theorem 4.11
.

For
r\in(2,\infty)
, there exists an symbol
\sigma
in
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast},S_{r},d(\mu\otimes\alpha))
such that
W_{\sigma}
on
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
is an unbounded operator.

To prove Theorem
4.11
, we follow the following lemmas.

Lemma 4.12
.

Suppose that the Weyl transform,
W_{\sigma}
is bounded linear operator on
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
for all
\sigma
in
L^{r}({\mathcal{G}}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m},S_{r},d(\mu\otimes\alpha)),2<r<\infty
. Then there exists a
c>0
such that

\|W_{\sigma}\|\leq c\|\sigma\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}.

(4.5)

Proof.

The proof follows from Banach-Steinhauss Theorem.
∎

Lemma 4.13
.

Suppose the inequality (
4.5
) holds for all
r
,
2<r<\infty
. Then

\|V(f,g)\|_{r^{\prime},\mu\otimes\alpha}\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}(G)},\quad\dfrac{1}{r}+\dfrac{1}{r^{\prime}}=1,

(4.6)

for all
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Let
f,g
be in
L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
. Then by the definition of the Weyl transform
W_{\sigma}
and inequality (
4.5
), we get

\displaystyle\|V(f,g)\|_{r^{\prime},\mu\otimes\alpha}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\sigma\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}=1}|\langle V(f,g),\sigma\rangle_{\mu\otimes\alpha}|

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|\sigma\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}=1}|\langle W_{\sigma}f,\overline{g}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|\sigma\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}=1}\|W_{\sigma}f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}

\displaystyle\leq c\|f\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}\|g\|_{L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})}.

∎

Lemma 4.14
.

Suppose that the inequality (
4.6
) holds. Then

\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}<\infty,

for all compact support and integrable function
f
, on
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Proof.

Let
f
be an integrable and compactly supported function in
\Omega\subset{\mathcal{G}}
. Then
V(f,f)
is supported in
\overline{\Omega}\times{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}
, where
\overline{\Omega}=\{(q,p,t)\ast(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime}):(q,p,t),(q^{\prime},p^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\Omega\}
. Using Minkowski inequality for integral, Hölder inequality and (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left(\sup_{\|\sigma\|_{r,\alpha}=1}\left|\langle\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,f)(q,p,t,\cdot)d\mu(q,p,t),\sigma(\cdot)\rangle\right|\right)^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left(\sup_{|\sigma\|_{r,\alpha}=1}\left|\text{tr}\left[\sigma(k)^{\ast}\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right]\right|\right)^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left(\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\sup_{\|\sigma\|_{r,\mu\otimes\alpha}=1}\left|\text{tr}\left[\sigma(q,p,t,k)^{\ast}V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)\right]\right|d\mu(q,p,t)\right)^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}\left(\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}d\mu(q,p,t)\right)^{1/r}\left(\int_{\Omega}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}d\mu(q,p,t)\right)^{1/r^{\prime}}<\infty.

∎

Proof of Theorem
4.11
.

Let
f
be an integrable function with compact support on
{\mathcal{G}}
and
\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}f(q,p,t)d\mu(q,p,t)\neq 0
. Now

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|c|}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f,f)(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\right\rVert_{S_{\infty}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left|\langle({\mathcal{F}}f)(k)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right|^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n},

(4.7)

where
\Phi_{\gamma}^{k}(x)=\|k\|_{2}^{\frac{n}{4}}\Phi_{\gamma}(\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}x)
and
\Phi_{\gamma}
are Hermite functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now using the relation (
4.4
), we get

\displaystyle\langle\pi_{k}(q,p,t)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{ik\cdot t}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}(x+p/2)}\Phi_{0}^{k}(x+p)\overline{\Phi_{0}^{k}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=e^{ik\cdot t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}y}\Phi_{0}^{k}(y+p/2)\overline{\Phi_{0}^{k}(y-p/2)}dy

\displaystyle=\|k\|_{2}^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{ik\cdot t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{iq\cdot B_{k}y}\Phi_{0}\left(\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}(y+p/2)\right)\overline{\Phi_{0}\left(\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}(y-p/2)\right)}dy

\displaystyle=e^{ik\cdot t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{iy\cdot(\frac{B_{k}^{t}q}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}})}\Phi_{0}\left(y+\frac{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}p}{2}\right)\overline{\Phi_{0}\left(y-\frac{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}p}{2}\right)}dy

\displaystyle=e^{ik\cdot t}V(\Phi_{0},\Phi_{0})\left(\frac{B_{k}^{t}q}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}},\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}p\right)

\displaystyle=e^{ik\cdot t}\Phi_{00}\left(\frac{B_{k}^{t}q}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}},\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}p\right)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{ik\cdot t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}L_{0}^{0}\left(\frac{|z_{j}|^{2}}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{|z_{j}|^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{ik\cdot t}\prod_{j=1}^{n}L_{0}^{0}\left(\frac{|z_{j}|^{2}}{2}\right)e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}|(q,p)|^{2}}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}e^{ik\cdot t}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}|(q,p)|^{2}},

(4.8)

where
z_{j}=\left(\frac{B_{k}^{t}q}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}},\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}p\right)_{j},j=1,2,\cdots,n
and
L_{0}^{0}\left(\frac{|z_{j}|^{2}}{2}\right)=1
.

Suppose

f_{\alpha}=\begin{cases}\prod_{\iota=1}^{m}|t_{\iota}|^{\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|q_{j}|^{\alpha}\prod_{l=1}^{n}|p_{l}|^{\alpha},&\text{ when $(q,p,t)\in A$ and $q_{j},p_{l},t_{\iota}\neq 0$},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(4.9)

where
A=\{(q,p,t):t_{\iota}\in[0,2\pi],|q_{j}|\leq 1,|p_{l}|\leq 1\}
and
\frac{-1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}-1
. Then
f_{\alpha}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{G}})
. From (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\langle({\mathcal{F}}f_{\alpha})(k)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{m}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\prod_{\iota=1}^{m}|t_{\iota}|^{\alpha}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|q_{j}|^{\alpha}\prod_{l=1}^{n}|p_{l}|^{\alpha}e^{ik\cdot t}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}|(q,p)|^{2}}d\mu(q,p,t)

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\prod_{\iota=1}^{m}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}e^{ik_{\iota}t_{\iota}}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\right)\prod_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|q_{j}|^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}q_{j}^{2}}dq_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\prod_{l=1}^{n}\left(\int_{-1}^{1}|p_{l}|^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}p_{l}^{2}}dp_{l}\right).

(4.10)

Consider, for
k_{\iota}>0,{\iota}=1,2,\cdots,m,

\int_{0}^{2\pi}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}e^{ik_{\iota}t_{\iota}}dt_{\iota}=\dfrac{1}{k_{\iota}^{\alpha+1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{j}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi},

and

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}|q_{j}|^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}q_{j}^{2}}dq_{j}

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}q_{j}^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}q_{j}^{2}}dq_{j}

\displaystyle=\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}}\right)^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}q_{j}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-q_{j}}dq_{j}.

Similarly,

\int_{-1}^{1}|p_{l}|^{\alpha}e^{-\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}p_{l}^{2}}dp_{l}=\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}}\right)^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}p_{l}^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-p_{l}}dp_{l}.

Hence for
k_{\iota}>0,{\iota}=1,2,\cdots,m
, the equation (
4
) becomes

\displaystyle\langle({\mathcal{F}}f_{\alpha})(k)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\prod_{{\iota}=1}^{m}\dfrac{1}{k_{\iota}^{\alpha+1}}\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\times\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}}\right)^{2n(\alpha+1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle\geq(2\pi)^{-n/2}\dfrac{1}{\|k\|_{2}^{m(\alpha+1)}}\prod_{{\iota}=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\times\left(\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{\|k\|_{2}}}\right)^{2n(\alpha+1)}\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx\right)^{2n}

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{-n/2}\dfrac{2^{2n(\alpha+1)}}{\|k\|_{2}^{(m+n)(\alpha+1)}}\prod_{{\iota}=1}^{m}\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\times\left(\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx\right)^{2n}.

(4.11)

Since

\int_{0}^{\infty}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}=\Gamma(\alpha)\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\alpha\right)

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx=\Gamma\left(\dfrac{\alpha+1}{2}\right).

So there exist natural numbers
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m},a
and positive number
M
such that

\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\right|\geq M,\quad\text{for}\quad k_{\iota}>\lambda_{\iota},{\iota}=1,2,\cdots,m,

and

\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx\geq M,\quad\text{for}\quad\|k\|_{2}>a.

Choose
\lambda_{0}=\max\{\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{m},a\}.
Then

\left|\int_{0}^{2\pi k_{\iota}}t_{\iota}^{\alpha}\cos t_{\iota}\frac{dt_{\iota}}{2\pi}\right|\geq M,\int_{0}^{\frac{\|k\|_{2}}{4}}x^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}e^{-x}dx\geq M,\quad\text{for}\quad k_{\iota}>\lambda_{0},{\iota}=1,2,\cdots,m.

Thus the inequality (
4
) becomes

\left|\langle({\mathcal{F}}f_{\alpha})(k)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right|\geq\dfrac{2^{2n(\alpha+1)}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\times\dfrac{M^{2n+m}}{\|k\|_{2}^{(m+n)(\alpha+1)}},\quad\text{for}\quad k_{\iota}>\lambda_{0},{\iota}=1,2,\cdots,m.

(4.12)

Now using the inequality (
4.12
) in (
4
), we get

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{|c|}\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left|\langle({\mathcal{F}}f_{\alpha})(k)\Phi_{0}^{k},\Phi_{0}^{k}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right|^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}

\displaystyle\geq\dfrac{2^{2n(\alpha+1)}}{(2\pi)^{n/2}}\sum_{k_{1}>\lambda_{1}}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}>\lambda_{0}}\dfrac{M^{(2n+m)r^{\prime}}}{\|(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})\|_{2}^{[(m+n)(\alpha+1)]r^{\prime}}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|(k_{1},\cdots,k_{m})\|_{2}^{n}=\infty,

(4.13)

when
(m+n)(\alpha+1)r^{\prime}-n\leq m
, i.e., for
\alpha\leq\frac{1}{m+n}\left(\frac{n+m}{r^{\prime}}\right)-1
. The divergence of the inequality (
4
) is because of Remark
4.15
. Hence for each
r
,
2<r<\infty
, we have an integrable, compactly supported function
f_{\alpha},\frac{-1}{2}<\alpha\leq\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}-1
such that

\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{m\ast}}\left\lVert\int_{{\mathcal{G}}}V(f_{\alpha},f_{\alpha})(q,p,t,k)d\mu(q,p,t)\right\rVert_{S_{r^{\prime}}}^{r^{\prime}}(2\pi)^{-n}\|k\|_{2}^{n}=\infty,

which contradict Lemma
4.14

∎

Remark 4.15
.

Let
f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})=\dfrac{1}{(x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\cdots+x_{m}^{2})^{p/2}}
, for all
x_{i}\geq 1,i=1,2,\cdots,m.
Let
k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m}
be positive integers. Then

f(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})\geq f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m}),

(4.14)

when
x_{i}
are in the closed intervals
[k_{i},k_{i}+1],i=1,2,\cdots,m
.
Now taking integration on both side of the inequality (
4.14
) over an rectangle
[k_{1},k_{1}+1]\times[k_{2},k_{2}+1]\times\cdots\times[k_{m},k_{m}+1]
, we obtain

f(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})\geq\int_{k_{1}}^{k_{1}+1}\int_{k_{2}}^{k_{2}+1}\cdots\int_{k_{m}}^{k_{m}+1}f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{m}

Summing the above inequality from
1
to
\infty
, we get

\sum_{k_{1}=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k_{2}=1}^{\infty}\cdots\sum_{k_{m}=1}^{\infty}f(k_{1},k_{2},\cdots,k_{m})\geq\int_{1}^{\infty}\int_{1}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{1}^{\infty}f(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{m})dx_{1}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{m}.

(4.15)

The right hand side of inequality (
4.15
) diverges when
p\leq m
. Hence the left hand side series of (
4.15
) diverges for
p\leq m.

Observation 1

J. Harju, C. Vastel, O. Sipilä, E. Redaelli, P. Caselli, J. E. Pineda, A. Belloche, F. Wyrowski

Abstract
Aims. We aim to test the use of three common molecular ions, ortho- H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} (o H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} ), N 2 ​ H + \rm N_{2}H^{+} , and DCO + \rm DCO^{+} as probes of the internal structure and kinematics of a dense, starless molecular cloud core. Methods. The prestellar core H-MM1 in Ophiuchus was mapped in the o H 2 ​ D + ​ ( 1 10 − 1 11 ) \rm H_{2}D^{+}(1_{10}-1_{11}) , N 2 ​ H + ​ ( 4 − 3 ) \rm N_{2}H^{+}(4-3) , and DCO + ​ ( 5 − 4 ) \rm DCO^{+}(5-4) lines with the Large APEX sub-Millimeter Array (LAsMA) multibeam receiver of the Atacama Pathfinder EXperiment (APEX) telescope. We also ran a series of chemistry models to predict the abundance distributions of the observed molecules, and to estimate the effect of the cosmic-ray ionisation rate on their abundances. Results. The three line maps show different distributions. The o H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} map is extended and outlines the general structure of the core, while N 2 ​ H + \rm N_{2}H^{+} mainly shows the density maxima, and the DCO + \rm DCO^{+} emission peaks are shifted towards one edge of the core where a region of enhanced desorption has been found previously. According to the chemical simulation, the fractional o H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} abundance remains relatively high in the centre of the core, and its column density correlates strongly with the cosmic-ray ionisation rate, ζ H 2 \zeta_{\rm H_{2}} . Simulated line maps constrain the cosmic-ray ionisation rate to be low, between 5 × 10 − 18 ​ s − 1 5\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1} and 1 × 10 − 17 ​ s − 1 1\times 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1} in the H-MM1 core. This estimate agrees with the gas temperature measured in the core. Conclusions. The present observations show that very dense, cold gas in molecular clouds can be traced by mapping in the ground-state line of o H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} , and high- J J transitions of DCO + \rm DCO^{+} and N 2 ​ H + \rm N_{2}H^{+} , despite the severe depletion of the latter two molecules. Modelling line emission of o H 2 ​ D + \rm H_{2}D^{+} provides a straightforward method of determining the cosmic-ray ionisation rate in dense clouds, where the primary ion, H 3 + \rm H_{3}^{+} , is not observable.

Introduction

Simple molecular ions, such as
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, found in dense interstellar clouds are manifestations of gas-phase chemistry driven by cosmic-ray ionisation. The ionisation of
\rm H_{2}
, the principal constituent of these clouds, leads immediately to the formation of the trihydrogen cation,
\rm H_{3}^{+}
. This triangular cation is observable by its rovibronic spectrum in diffuse clouds in absorption against strong infrared background sources, but is difficult to detect in highly obscured, dense regions. On the other hand,
\rm H_{3}^{+}
is easily destroyed in exothermic proton transfer reactions with the common neutral species CO, N
2
, and HD, to give
\rm HCO^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
, which are observable in cold, dark clouds owing to their pure rotational spectra. Reactions of CO and N
2
with
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
, or the doubly and triply deuterated forms
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm D_{3}^{+}
, give then also
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}
. Because of the relatively simple chemical network connecting these molecules to the primary ions,
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and more recently also
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
, have been used to estimate the electron abundance,
X({\rm e^{-}})
, and the cosmic-ray ionisation rate of
\rm H_{2}
,
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
, in dense interstellar clouds. The methods generally involve estimates of the abundances of CO, N
2
, and other major destroyers of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
, and often combine these with the fractionation ratios
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
or
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
to derive the electron fraction and the
\rm H_{3}^{+}
abundance in the cloud (e.g.,
Guelin et al. 1977
;
Wootten et al. 1979
;
Caselli et al. 1998
;
van der Tak & van Dishoeck 2000
;
Caselli et al. 2002
;
Caselli et al. 2008
;
Bovino et al. 2020
;
Redaelli et al. 2021
;
Sabatini et al. 2023
). The usefulness of analytic formulas to derive
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
from the observed fractionation ratios is discussed in
Redaelli et al. 2024
. Ionising collisions with energetic particles have a fundamental influence on the physics and chemistry of interstellar molecular clouds (
Padovani et al. 2020
). The ionisation of
\rm H_{2}
is dominated by low-energy (
<1
GeV/nucleon) particles, mainly protons and their secondary electrons. Cosmic ray propagation models predict that the total ionisation rate of
\rm H_{2}
decreases with an increasing
\rm H_{2}
column density of the medium, from values estimated in diffuse clouds,
\sim 10^{-16}-10^{-15}\,\rm s^{-1}
(
Indriolo & McCall 2012
;
Neufeld & Wolfire 2017
), down to
\sim 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
at column densities
N(\rm H_{2})\sim 10^{23}\,\rm cm^{-2}
characteristic of dense cores (
Padovani et al. 2009
;
Ivlev et al. 2015
;
Padovani et al. 2018
;
Ivlev et al. 2021
;
Padovani et al. 2024
). Besides its central role in astrochemistry, cosmic-ray ionisation is also the dominant direct heating mechanism of gas in quiescent regions shielded from UV radiation. The gas temperature in these regions is determined by equilibrium between the cosmic-ray ionisation rate, the gas cooling rate by line emission, and the energy transfer rate by collisions between gas particles and dust grains (
Burke & Hollenbach 1983
;
Goldsmith 2001
;
Galli et al. 2002
). Conversely, gas temperature measurements can also be used to constrain the cosmic-ray ionisation rate (
Galli & Padovani 2015
;
Ivlev et al. 2019
).

In addition to their potential use for estimating the cosmic-ray ionisation rate, the deuterated isotopologues of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
are predicted to be useful probes of the interiors of molecular cloud cores (
Caselli et al. 2003
;
Roberts et al. 2003
;
Walmsley et al. 2004
;
Vastel et al. 2006b
). The detection of
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
in centrally condensed starless cores (
Caselli et al. 2003
;
Vastel et al. 2004
;
Vastel et al. 2006a
;
Parise et al. 2011
) show that these light deuterated ions with permanent electric dipole moments can retain high abundances in very dense, cold gas, where many C-, N-, and O-bearing molecules are frozen onto dust grains.

Here we present maps of the prestellar core Ophiuchus/H-MM1 in the
\lambda=0.8
mm lines of ortho-
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
(hereafter o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
),
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm DCO^{+}
, obtained using the Large APEX sub-Millimeter Array (LAsMA) of the Atacama Pathfinder EXperiment (APEX) telescope. The ground-state lines of o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and para-
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
(hereafter p
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
) have been previously detected towards this core (
Parise et al. 2011
), and a VLA mapping shows severe depletion of ammonia in its centre (
Pineda et al. 2022
). The goal of the present observations is to derive the distributions of the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm DCO^{+}
in the core, test the predictions of chemistry models concerning their abundances, and put constraints on the cosmic-ray ionisation rate in the region.

The line parameters derived by Gaussian fits to the hyperfine structure components are presented in Table

To predict line intensity maps from the core model requires assumptions about molecular abundances. The simplest assumption is that the fractional abundance of a molecule is constant throughout the cloud. We tested this assumption using the estimates towards our (0,0) position from the LTE method described in Sect.
3
. While the predicted line intensities using these constant abundances are comparable to those observed in the core centre, the extent of the emission is clearly more compact than observed. This indicates that the fractional abundances of all three molecules are higher in the outer parts than in the centre of the core. In what follows, we use a chemical model to predict the abundance profiles of the observed molecules in the core, given the density and temperature distributions derived above.

.

We use for the simulations the gas-grain chemical model pyRate described in
Sipilä et al. 2019
,
Riedel et al. 2023
, and
Harju et al. 2024
. We assume constant chemical desorption with 1% of exothermic surface reactions leading to desorption, and adopt the proton hop chemical network in the gas phase
(
Sipilä et al. 2019
)
. In this model, proton donation and abstraction reactions involving O, C, and N are assumed to proceed via the exchange of one proton (or deuteron) between the reactants, without multiple atom exchanges. On the other hand, full scrambling is assumed for the
\rm H_{3}^{+}+\rm H_{2}
reaction system and its deuterated variants, and the rate coefficients and branching ratios for these reactions are adopted from
Hugo et al. 2009
. In the chemical calculations we employ a one-dimensional physical model for H-MM1, which is constructed using the average densities and temperatures over concentric shells shown in Fig.
6
. We adopt the same initial abundances as in
Sipilä et al. 2019
, and the simulation parameters are also the same as in that paper except for the cosmic-ray ionisation rate of
\rm H_{2}
,
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
, for which we test here different values ranging from
10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}
to
10^{-15}\,\rm s^{-1}
. We assume that
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
is constant throughout the core, because the column densities in the core region (at the resolution achieved here) are confined to a narrow range,
N(\rm H_{2})\sim 10^{22}\,\rm cm^{-2}-6\times 10^{22}\,\rm cm^{-2}
, where attenuation models (e.g.,
Padovani et al. 2018
) do not predict drastic changes in
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
. Besides, the angular resolution of the observations is not sufficient for studying spatial variations of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
. The adopted abundances of oxygen, carbon, and nitrogen relative to hydrogen are
{\rm O/H}=1.8\times 10^{-4}
,
{\rm C/H}=7.3\times 10^{-5}
, and
{\rm N/H}=5.3\times 10^{-5}
. Previous observations of ammonia and its deuterated forms towards H-MM1 have been reproduced well by these abundances (
Harju et al. 2017
;
Pineda et al. 2022
;
Harju et al. 2024
). We also tested higher elemental abundances (
{\rm O/H}=2.56\times 10^{-4}
,
{\rm C/H}=1.2\times 10^{-4}
, and
{\rm N/H}=7.6\times 10^{-5}
) used previously in chemical models for the prestellar core L1544 (e.g.,
Riedel et al. 2023
). The chemistry model assumes a unique grain radius, and this is fiducially set to 0.1
\mu
m. We also examined how increasing the grain radius would affect the abundances of the observed molecules. The results of tests with different elemental abundances and grain radii are discussed at the end of Sect.
6
.

Simulation results for the one-dimensional core model with our fiducial set of parameters are shown in Fig.

. There we show the average fractional abundances of o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm DCO^{+}
as functions of the cosmic-ray ionisation rate
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
at a certain time of simulation (Fig

a), and the temporal evolution of these abundances for
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
(Fig

b). The abundances derived using the LTE approximation in Sect.
3
are shown with dashed lines in panels a and b. The bottom panel (Fig
7
c) shows the fractional o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm DCO^{+}
abundances as functions of the local density in the core at the simulation time
5\times 10^{5}
yr assuming
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
. In panels a and b, the average fractional abundance is the column density ratio
N({\rm mol})/N(\rm H_{2})
computed along a pencil beam going through the core centre. The best-fit abundances discussed in Sect.
6

The chemical model predicts that o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
is clearly more abundant than the
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
ions, except for the core boundary, and that its abundance strongly correlates with the cosmic-ray ionisation rate. The abundance derived in Sect.

by the LTE method,
X({\rm o}\rm H_{2}D^{+})\sim 2.2\times 10^{-10}
would agree with
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}

\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
and at all times clearly lower than that derived from the observations (suggesting
X(\rm DCO^{+})\sim 3.3\times 10^{-10}

\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance is, however, shallower than for
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}

5\times 10^{5}

\rm H_{2}D^{+}

\sim 10^{4}\,\rm cm^{-3}

and
\rm DCO^{+}
abundances in the outer parts of the clouds (their Fig. 5).

6
Simulated line maps

The chemical abundances calculated using the one-dimensional core model at different times and for different values of the cosmic-ray ionisation rate
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
were interpolated to the three-dimensional model described in Sect.
4
. In the interpolation it was assumed that the abundance of a molecule depends only on the density. This means that the temperature variations in a certain density regime owing to the anisotropic external radiation field was ignored. The simulations were done using the LOC radiative transfer program
(
Juvela 2020
)
. The goodness of the simulation result was evaluated by comparing the simulated integrated intensity maps with the observed ones. For this purpose, we used the image cubes from GILDAS mentioned in Sect.
3
that gave a pixel size of
5\aas@@fstack{\prime\prime}6
. The simulated integrated intensity maps were resampled to the same grid, and the
\chi^{2}
statistic was evaluated over pixels where the observed integrated intensity exceeded a certain threshold. The threshold was set to
0.1\,\rm K\,km\,s^{-1}
for
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}
, and
0.2\,\rm K\,km\,s^{-1}
for o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
. This selection gave
\sim 70-80
pixels for each map. For the
\sigma
we used the RMS integrated intensity on the outskirts of the observed maps, where the surface brightness is dominated by noise.

The best agreement between the simulated and observed integrated o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}(1_{10}-1_{11})
intensity map in terms of
\chi^{2}
was found with
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=5.5\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}
at the simulation time
5\times 10^{5}
yr. Nearly as good agreement is obtained with
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=7.4\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}
at
6.3\times 10^{5}
yr. With the latter value of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
, the agreement with the observed o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
map does not depend strongly on the simulation time after
6.3\times 10^{5}
yr. Image-style plots of
\chi^{2}
as functions of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
and time are shown in Fig.

\rm H_{2}D^{+}

The average fractional o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance of the best-fit models (with

) is
\sim 2.2\times 10^{-10}
towards the centre of the core, derived from column densities convolved to the angular resolution of the observations. The value is close to that estimated in Sect.

. As can be anticipated from Fig.

at the angular resolution of the observations. Maps and spectra towards a few selected positions calculated from the model with

We also tested the set of higher O, C, and N abundances mentioned in Sect.

\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance, and also for
\rm N_{2}H^{+}

7\times 10^{-22}\,{\rm cm^{2}}
per H atom. Yet another series of simulations was made varying the grain radius. The tested values were 0.2
\mu
m, 0.3
\mu
m, and 0.4
\mu

and

\mu

7
Discussion

The observed o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
map follows the general structure of the H-MM1 core seen in the far-infrared and sub-millimetre continuum (Figs.
1
and
3
). The core model misses the secondary density peak near the offset
+10\arcsec,+30\arcsec
from the core centre (Fig.
11
). Besides the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
maps presented here, the presence of the secondary peak is also evident on the previous high-resolution
\rm NH_{2}D
and
\rm NH_{3}
maps of the core (
Harju et al. 2020
;
Pineda et al. 2022
). According to the chemistry model, the fractional o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance decreases typically by one order of magnitude from the outer parts to the centre of the core (Fig.
7
c). For
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, the decrease in the fractional abundance is about two orders of magnitude. Combined with the density model of the core (Fig.
6
), this means that while the number densities of
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
(
\rm cm^{-3}
) remain approximately constant throughout the core, the number density of o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
increases by a factor of ten towards the centre. Provided that it is not optically thick, or absorbed in an ambient cloud, the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}(1_{10}-1_{11})
line is therefore a useful probe of the interior parts of dense cores. In H-MM1, we do not see signs of self-absorption in this line.

The centroid velocity and velocity dispersion maps of o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
shown in Fig.
9
do not bring new information about the gas kinematics in the centre of the core. The previous maps derived from the
\rm NH_{3}(1,1)
line observed with the VLA are superior thanks to their high spatial resolution (
6\arcsec
versus
18\arcsec
achieved here) and sensitivity, despite the heavy depletion of ammonia in this region (
Pineda et al. 2022
). The sudden drop in the velocity dispersion seen at a declination offset of about
+30\arcsec
in
\rm NH_{3}
,
\rm DCO^{+}
, and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
is less pronounced in o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
because the linewidth of this molecule is dominated by thermal motions. All three maps observed here confirm the presence of red-shifted gas in a narrow region pointing to the centre of the core. This probably represents a stream of gas falling into the centre of gravity, resembling thus larger-scale accretion streams discovered previously in association with protostars (
Pineda et al. 2020
;
Valdivia-Mena et al. 2022
;
Valdivia-Mena et al. 2023
). The putative stream, best discernible on the
\rm NH_{3}(1,1)
velocity map (Fig.
9
, top right panel), may have a role in the conception of a protostar in this core.

The shift between the
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
maps, illustrated in Fig.
4
, is reminiscent of what was seen in high-resolution maps of
\rm CH_{3}OH
and
\rm NH_{2}D
towards this core (
Harju et al. 2020
). The bright methanol emission at its shaded, eastern boundary was suggested to be caused by vigorous desorption, possibly induced by grain-grain collisions in a layer of strong velocity shear. Methanol formation requires CO-rich ice (e.g.,
Vasyunin et al. 2017
), and carbon monoxide eventually released in this process could also have led to an intensified production of
\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}
. In low-mass star-forming regions, bright
\rm DCO^{+}
emission sometimes marks interaction with a low-velocity outflow
(
Lis et al. 2016
)
. To examine the possible connection of the methanol feature to the
\rm DCO^{+}
”displacement”, we have overlaid in Fig.
14
contours of strong methanol emission on the integrated intensity maps of the three lines observed here. The figure shows that the
\rm DCO^{+}
peaks are not found in the direction of the brightest methanol emission, but lie west of it, slightly inward from the core edge. Also the
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
emission peak seems to reside, at least in projection, next to methanol contours. On the whole, the differences between the distributions of
\rm DCO^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
relative to
\rm CH_{3}OH
are small. Nevertheless, the fact that
\rm DCO^{+}
emission peaks on the same side of the core where also most methanol is found can probably be traced to their common precursor, CO. It is possible that the distributions have been shaped by the asymmetric radiation field owing to nearby B-type stars, which has led to a measurable difference in the dust temperature between the western and eastern boundaries of the core (
Harju et al. 2020
, Sect. 7.1). The effects of uneven illumination on carbon chemistry and the formation of methanol in prestellar and starless cores have been discussed in
Spezzano et al. 2016
and
Spezzano et al. 2020
; the latter paper also presents a single-dish methanol map of H-MM1. The large-scale distribution of methanol may be understood in terms favourable conditions for the formation of CO, and CO-rich ice, on the sheltered eastern side of the core, and relative scarcity of CO on the western side side which is exposed to strong radiation. How these conditions influence the
\rm DCO^{+}
distribution is not clear to us. The present chemistry model does not include local variations of the desorption efficiency or anisotropies of the external radiation field. This may partly explain the discrepancy between the observed and modelled
\rm DCO^{+}
abundances.

The decrease in the fractional abundances of electrons and molecular ions towards higher densities predicted by the chemistry model (Fig.
7
c) is a natural consequence of more frequent recombination reactions; the recombination rate (
\rm cm^{-3}\,\rm s^{-1}
) between molecular ions and electrons is approximately proportional to the cosmic-ray ionisation rate (here assumed to be constant) and the number density of
\rm H_{2}
(
McKee 1989
, Appendix B). For
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
the decrease is accentuated by the disappearance of their precursors CO and N
2
owing to freezing onto dust grains. In contrast, o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and the other forms of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
benefit from the depletion of these molecules, and the density dependence of their fractional abundance is in our model slightly shallower than
\sim n^{-0.5}
predicted by
McKee 1989
(see also Fig. 13 of
Pineda et al. 2022
).

\rm H_{3}^{+}
, its deuterated isotopologues, and electrons in the core model as functions of the cosmic-ray ionisation rate
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
. The solid red curve shows the total (ortho + para) abundance of
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
, whereas the dashed red curve shows separately the fractional o

\rm H_{3}^{+}
, denoted
\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}}
in the text, is shown with the dashed cyan curve.

The average o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance is predicted to be nearly constant in time and to correlate strongly with the cosmic-ray ionisation rate (Fig.
7
a and b). The same is true for
\rm H_{3}^{+}
and for the combined abundance of all its modifications. In Fig.
8
, we show the average fractional abundances of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
,
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
,
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
,
\rm D_{3}^{+}
as functions of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
. The fractional abundances of the observable o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and p
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
cations are shown there with dashed red and green curves, respectively. Also shown is the total abundance of all
\rm H_{3}^{+}
isotopologues (cyan dashed curve; denoted
\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}}
). The fractional o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance follows remarkably well the total abundance of
\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}}
, at about one order of magnitude lower level. This is true also for other simulation times.

The constancy and the correlation of the average o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance with the cosmic-ray ionisation rate is a consequence of the fact that the formation rate of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
is practically equal to
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}\times n(\rm H_{2})
. The
\rm H_{3}^{+}
ion is deuterated through reactions with HD, starting with
\rm H_{3}^{+}+{\rm HD}\leftrightharpoons{}\rm H_{2}D^{+}+\rm H_{2}
. In dense gas, the fractional HD abundance is reduced slowly from the initial value
\sim 3\times 10^{-5}
(
Sipilä et al. 2013
), and deuteration is boosted when the fractional CO abundance decreases below that of HD. At high cosmic-ray ionisation rates, deuterium fractionation is, however, suppressed as
\rm H_{3}^{+}
is destroyed in electron recombination reactions rather than reactions with HD. In this respect, the situation resembles that in diffuse clouds. On the other hand, at low values of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
,
\rm D_{3}^{+}
can become the dominant ion. The isotopologues of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
together form by far the most abundant group of cations in our core model, and the total
\rm H_{3}^{+}
abundance,
X(\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}})
, is nearly equal to the electron fraction,
X({\rm e^{-}})
. Therefore, it seems reasonable to apply the approximation presented by
Watson 1976
for the minimum fractional ionisation (his Eq. 4.50) to the total
\rm H_{3}^{+}
abundance, and write
X(\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}})\approx X({\rm e^{-}})\approx\sqrt{\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}/(\alpha\bar{n}(\rm H_{2}))}
, where
\alpha
is the rate coefficient of dissociative electron recombination, and
\bar{n}(\rm H_{2})
is the average
\rm H_{2}
density. In our core model
\bar{n}(\rm H_{2})\approx 2\times 10^{5}\,\rm cm^{-3}
. The formula approximates the dependency of
\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}}
on
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
very well in our models if we set
\alpha=1.9\times 10^{-6}\,{\rm cm^{3}}\,\rm s^{-1}
, giving
X(\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}})\approx 1.6\sqrt{\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}/\rm s^{-1}}
. The
\alpha
value quoted is, however, about ten times larger than typical recombination rate coefficients for
\rm H_{3}^{+}
and its variants at 10 K (
Pagani et al. 2009b
; their Table B.1), and it can be thought to represent the removal of molecules from the sequence
\rm H_{3}^{+}\leftrightharpoons{}\rm H_{2}D^{+}\leftrightharpoons{}\rm D_{2}H^{+}\leftrightharpoons{}\rm D_{3}^{+}
by both dissociative electron recombination (including those on grains) and proton transfer reactions, primarily to CO and N
2
. Inspection of the main production and destruction pathways during the simulation reveals that at
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
, the share of electron recombination reactions is approximately 60% of the total destruction rate of the
\rm H_{3}^{+}
family, whereas CO and N
2
together are responsible for about 40% of it. The relative importance of these reaction types change with
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
, but our simulations suggest that their combined effect on
X(\widetilde{\rm H_{3}^{+}})
remains approximately constant. A great majority of reactions involving the altogether nine isotopologues and spin modifications of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
is, however, conversions between these forms induced by HD and ortho- or para-
\rm H_{2}
.

The cosmic-ray ionisation rate derived in H-MM1,
5\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}\lesssim\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}\lesssim 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
, is somewhat lower than previous estimates for dense clouds (
1-5\times 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
; e.g.,
Dalgarno 2006
;
Padovani et al. 2024
). Direct comparison with previous results is difficult, though, because different molecular tracers, and a number of assumptions about their abundances, have been employed (see below). Cosmic ray propagation models predict that the flux of particles responsible for
\rm H_{2}
ionisation is attenuated by large columns of gas (
Padovani et al. 2009
;
Ivlev et al. 2015
;
Padovani et al. 2018
;
Ivlev et al. 2021
;
Padovani et al. 2024
). According to attenuation models,
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
should stay above
10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
at column densities typical for starless dense cores (
N(\rm H_{2})\sim 10^{23}\,\rm cm^{-2}
,
\Sigma\sim 0.5\,{\rm g\,cm^{-2}}
) (e.g.,
Padovani et al. 2018
, their Appendix F). The upper limits for the cosmic-ray ionisation
rates derived by
Indriolo & McCall 2012
towards diffuse sight lines
in the Ophiuchus-Scorpius region are some of the lowest in their
sample, suggesting that Ophiuchus clouds may be exposed to a weaker
than average cosmic ray flux (see their Section 6.2.4). The results presented here
only pertain the local value of
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
in the dense core H-MM1. Our simulations with two different sets of elemental O, C, and N abundances, and different grain sizes (affecting the total surface area of grains, and thus the depletion of CO and N
2
and other molecules with heavy elements) gave similar best-fit
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
values, based on the agreement with the observed o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance, which correlates strongly with
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
. There is no such simple relationship between the cosmic-ray ionisation rate and the abundances of the two other molecules observed here. The failure to fit simultaneously the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
maps, and the underestimation of the
\rm DCO^{+}
abundance are disturbing, but do not undermine the
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
estimate.

The problem that our chemical model under-predicts the
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
abundance has been previously encountered by
Redaelli et al. 2019
and
Redaelli et al. 2021
in the analysis of observations towards the prestellar core L1544. The improvement gained by reducing the grain surface area (end of Sect.
6
) suggests that N
2
is excessively depleted in the model. Effective grain radii exceeding 0.1
\mu
m, which were tested in Sect.
6
, would imply that the dust grain size distribution is shifted towards larger grains from the classic dust model of
Mathis et al. 1977
, with
n(a)\propto a^{-3.5}da
in the range
a_{\rm min}=50\AA
,
a_{\rm max}=0.25\,\mu
m. Growth of grains to micrometer sizes owing to condensation of icy mantles and coagulation, leading to a decrease in the total surface area of the particles and hence to a lower degree of depletion, is predicted by theoretical models of gas-dust dynamics (e.g.,
Ossenkopf & Henning 1994
;
Ormel et al. 2009
;
Lefèvre et al. 2020
;
Hirashita et al. 2021
). Observational evidence for the presence of large dust particles in dense cores comes from measurements of mid-infrared extinction, the discovery of scattered mid-infrared radiation, variation in the dust opacity at millimetre wavelengths, and changes in the ice band profiles in mid-infrared spectra (e.g.,
Steinacker et al. 2010
;
Chacón-Tanarro et al. 2019
;

Dartois et al. 2024
).

To test if the derived low cosmic-ray ionisation rate can possibly be true, we make a quick calculation of the gas temperature,
T_{\rm gas}
, in the centre of the core and at its outer edge, using the density and the dust temperature distributions of the core model shown in Fig.
6
. Assuming that the line cooling rate is very low in the core centre, the cosmic-ray heating rate,
\Gamma_{\rm cr}
, is approximately equal to the gas-dust energy transfer rate,
\Lambda_{\rm gd}
, and adopting the expressions for these quantities from
Goldsmith 2001
and
Galli et al. 2002
(Eqs. 8 and 10 in the latter paper), the equilibrium
T_{\rm gas}
corresponding to
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=7.4\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}
is 7.2 K. Because of the strong gas-dust coupling at densities prevailing in the centre of core,
T_{\rm gas}
would not rise much above
T_{\rm dust}
even for substantially higher cosmic-ray ionisation rates. The value measured at the outer edge of the core is, therefore, critical. There, line cooling is important, and
T_{\rm gas}
is obtained from the equation
\Gamma_{\rm cr}-\Lambda_{\rm g}-\Lambda_{\rm gd}=0
, where
\Lambda_{\rm g}
is the gas cooling rate by molecular and atomic lines. Again, we adopt the parametrization of
Goldsmith 2001
for this quantity,
\Lambda_{\rm g}=\alpha(T_{\rm gas}/10\,{\rm K})^{\beta}
. According to our chemistry model, the fractional CO abundance at the outer edge of the core is
\sim 6\times 10^{-6}
, which corresponds to a depletion factor (DF) of 10. Consequently, we use the values of
\alpha
and
\beta
given in Table 4 of
Goldsmith 2001
for model DF10, interpolated to the density
n(\rm H_{2})=2\times 10^{4}\,\rm cm^{-3}
, characteristic of the outer edge of the core (giving
\alpha=6\times 10^{-24}\,{\rm ergs\,cm^{-3}\,s^{-1}}
,
\beta=2.7
). The dust temperature there is
\sim 15
K. Substituting these numbers into the thermal balance equation one finds that
T_{\rm gas}=11.2
K for
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}=7.4\times 10^{-18}
. This agrees with the gas temperature model shown in Fig.
6
, which is based on the VLA ammonia map of
Pineda et al. 2022
. At the outer edge, the gas is heated by dust grains (
\Lambda_{\rm gd}<0
), otherwise the adopted
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
value would imply a lower gas temperature (
\sim 9.3
K, if gas-dust energy transfer would be neglected).

Finally, we briefly discuss assumptions made in analytical methods of deriving the electron abundance and the cosmic-ray ionisation from observed abundances. For a thorough analysis of these methods, we refer to
Redaelli et al. 2024
. The analytic methods commonly assume steady state, and derive the abundance of the dominant ion,
\rm H_{3}^{+}
, from that of an abundant observable ion, such as
\rm HCO^{+}
or o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
. Here, one has to consider the importance of proton transfer reactions (for example,
\rm H_{3}^{+}+{\rm CO}\rightarrow\rm HCO^{+}+\rm H_{2}
) relative to the dissociative electron recombination (
\rm H_{3}^{+}+{\rm e^{-}}\rightarrow\rm H_{2}+{\rm H}
) in the destruction of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
(or its deuterated forms). When high densities are concerned, a proper judgement on this issue would require modelling. If o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
or p
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
are used as substitutes of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
, the difficulty is that neither the ortho/para ratios of these molecules nor the fractionation ratios
\rm H_{2}D^{+}/\rm H_{3}^{+}
and
\rm D_{2}H^{+}/\rm H_{3}^{+}
are known a priori (Fig.
8
). A common assumption is that
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
are
\sim 1/3\times\rm H_{2}D^{+}/\rm H_{3}^{+}
. This is based on the idea that
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
is the principal deuterated form of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
, and that one third of the reactions
{\rm CO}+\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and
{\rm N_{2}}+\rm H_{2}D^{+}
leads to
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}
, respectively. The fact that also
\rm D_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm D_{3}^{+}
contribute to the formation of
\rm DCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}
, are taken into account in the steady-state formula for the fractionation ratios
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
presented by
Caselli et al. 2008
and
Pagani et al. 2009b
(their Eqs. 13 and 10, respectively):

\frac{\rm N_{2}D^{+}}{\rm N_{2}H^{+}}\approx\frac{\rm DCO^{+}}{\rm HCO^{+}}\approx\frac{\rm H_{2}D^{+}+2\rm D_{2}H^{+}+3\rm D_{3}^{+}}{3\rm H_{3}^{+}+2\rm H_{2}D^{+}+\rm D_{2}H^{+}}\;.

In Fig.
15
we show the ratios
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
from our models as functions of the cosmic-ray ionisation rate and time, along with the prediction from the equation above. Also shown is the ratio
1/3\times\rm H_{2}D^{+}/\rm H_{3}^{+}
, which often is assumed to be equal to
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
or
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
. Besides the model with the fiducial set of parameters, diagrams are shown for the model with the grain radius
a=0.3\,\mu
m, which can approximately reproduce both o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
intensities. While the steady-state formula shown above gives a nearly perfect match, one can see that the
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
ratios do not serve as good templates of the quantity
1/3\times\rm H_{2}D^{+}/\rm H_{3}^{+}
in this case. The discrepancy is a factor of 2-3, and thus still within the estimated accuracy of the common approximation according to
Redaelli et al. 2024
. For targets with lower average densities the agreement is probably better. Unfortunately, the present observations do not include
\rm HCO^{+}
or
\rm N_{2}D^{+}
lines to test the predictions for the fractionation ratios.

8
Conclusions

Each of the three common molecular ions, o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
,
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
, and
\rm DCO^{+}
, presents a different picture of the prestellar core H-MM1. The o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}(1_{10}-1_{11})
emission follows rather closely the general structure of the core seen in the far-infrared and in ammonia maps. The line is excited already at densities around
10^{5}\,\rm cm^{-3}
, but its optical thickness remains moderate also towards the densest regions, partly because of the broad thermal linewidth and the hyperfine structure. The
\rm N_{2}H^{+}(4-3)
emission is more compact, concentrating on the density maxima. Like
\rm N_{2}H^{+}(4-3)
, also the
\rm DCO^{+}(5-4)
line has a high critical density, but its emission maxima are shifted eastward from the density peaks, towards the more sheltered side of the core.

The chemistry model used to interpret the observed map brings out two advantages of o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
observations: 1) the fractional o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance remains relatively high also in the interiors of the core, where
\rm N_{2}H^{+}
and
\rm DCO^{+}
are depleted by a factor of hundred or more; 2) the average o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance is nearly constant in time, and correlates strongly with the cosmic-ray ionisation rate owing to its direct relationship to the primary ionisation product
\rm H_{3}^{+}
. The observed o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
line emission constrains the cosmic-ray ionisation rate to be
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}\sim 5\times 10^{-18}\,\rm s^{-1}-1\times 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
. A substantial increase in the assumed elemental O, C, and N abundances, or a decrease in the total surface area of the dust grains in the chemistry model, do not change the best-fit
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
value markedly, and the estimate
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}\lesssim 10^{-17}\,\rm s^{-1}
in H-MM1 seems robust. The estimate is further supported by the low gas temperature measured on the outskirts of the core. The
\rm H_{2}
column density of the ambient cloud around the core is of the order of
10^{22}\,\rm cm^{-2}
, and the low
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
value derived in H-MM1 compared with estimates in diffuse clouds can probably be understood in terms of attenuation of the cosmic ray flux in dense gas (e.g.,
Padovani et al. 2018
), although propagation models predict higher ionisation rate at this column density. Our modelling results corroborate the conclusion from the analysis presented in
Bovino et al. 2020
and
Redaelli et al. 2024
that o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
observations are useful for deriving the cosmic-ray ionisation rate in dense dark clouds where
\rm H_{3}^{+}
is not observable. However, the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
abundance relative to that of
\rm H_{3}^{+}
cannot be reliably obtained from the observable fractionation ratios
\rm DCO^{+}/\rm HCO^{+}
and
\rm N_{2}D^{+}/\rm N_{2}H^{+}
. Modelling the o
\rm H_{2}D^{+}
line emission using
\zeta_{\rm H_{2}}
as a parameter provides a more straightforward method.

Abstract
The increasing availability of geometric data has motivated the need for information processing over non-Euclidean domains modeled as manifolds. The building block for information processing architectures with desirable theoretical properties such as invariance and stability is convolutional filtering. Manifold convolutional filters are defined from the manifold diffusion sequence, constructed by successive applications of the Laplace-Beltrami operator to manifold signals. However, the continuous manifold model can only be accessed by sampling discrete points and building an approximate graph model from the sampled manifold. Effective linear information processing on the manifold requires quantifying the error incurred when approximating manifold convolutions with graph convolutions. In this paper, we derive a non-asymptotic error bound for this approximation, showing that convolutional filtering on the sampled manifold converges to continuous manifold filtering. Our findings are further demonstrated empirically on a problem of navigation control.

Contributions.
In this paper, we define manifold filtering as a convolutional operation based on diffusions that are defined as exponentials of the Laplace-Beltrami (LB) operator

2
Preliminary definitions

In order to relate graph filters and manifold filters, we start by reviewing the basic setup as well as the graph signal processing and manifold signal processing frameworks.

2.1
Graph signal processing: graph filters

We consider an undirected graph
{\mathbf{G}}=({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{W}})
with
{\mathcal{V}},|{\mathcal{V}}|=n
, representing the set of nodes and
{\mathcal{E}}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}\times{\mathcal{V}}
the set of edges. The weight function
{\mathcal{W}}:{\mathcal{E}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
assigns weights to the edges. Graph signals
{\mathbf{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
are defined as data supported on the graph, with the
i
-th element
x_{i}
representing the datum at node
i

[
6
,
14
]
.

The so-called graph shift operator (GSO)
{\mathbf{S}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
is typically selected to be the Laplacian matrix, the normalized Laplacian or the adjacency matrix, which encodes the graph sparsity pattern. The application of the GSO to a graph signal leads to a diffusion of the data over the edges. Specifically, for each node
i
, the GSO diffuses the signals on the neighboring nodes
x_{j}
scaled with the proximity measure
[{\mathbf{S}}]_{ij}
to this node
i
. I.e.
[{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{x}}]_{i}=\sum_{j,(i,j)\in{\mathcal{E}}}[{\mathbf{S}}]_{ij}x_{j}
. Based on this notion of diffusion, we can then define graph convolutional filters as a shift-and-sum operation

{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}h_{k}{\mathbf{S}}^{k}{\mathbf{x}},

(1)

where
\{h_{k}\}_{k=0}^{K-1}
are the filter coefficients
[
7
,
6
]
.

For an undirected graph
{\mathbf{G}}
, the GSO is symmetric and thus can be diagonalized as
{\mathbf{S}}={\mathbf{V}}\bm{\Lambda}{\mathbf{V}}^{H}
, with
\bm{\Lambda}
containing the graph eigenvalues and
{\mathbf{V}}
the graph eigenvectors. By replacing the GSO by its eigendecomposition, we can thus write the spectral representation of the graph convolution as

{\mathbf{V}}^{H}{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{x}}=\sum_{k=1}^{K-1}h_{k}\bm{\Lambda}^{k}{\mathbf{V}}^{H}{\mathbf{x}}=h(\bm{\Lambda}){\mathbf{V}}^{H}{\mathbf{x}}.

(2)

The frequency response of graph convolution can therefore be written as
h(\lambda)=\sum_{k=0}^{K-1}h_{k}\lambda^{k}
, which only depends on the coefficients
h_{k}
and the eigenvalues of
{\mathbf{S}}
.

2.2
Manifold signal processing: manifold filters

We consider a compact smooth differentiable
d
-dimensional submanifold
{\mathcal{M}}
embedded in some Euclidean space
{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathsf{N}}
, and a measure
\mu
over the manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
. Manifold signals are defined as scalar functions
f:{\mathcal{M}}\rightarrow{\mathbb{R}}
supported on
{\mathcal{M}}
. The inner product of functions
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
can be defined as

\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})}=\int_{\mathcal{M}}f(x)g(x)\text{d}\mu(x)

(3)

with
\text{d}\mu(x)
representing the volume element with respect to measure
\mu
. Therefore, the energy of manifold signal
f
is naturally given as

\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})}={\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})}}
. We consider manifold signals with finite energy, i.e.,
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
.

The manifold is locally Euclidean and the local homeomorphic Euclidean space around each point
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
is defined as the tangent space
T_{x}{\mathcal{M}}
. The disjoint union of all tangent spaces over
{\mathcal{M}}
is denoted
T{\mathcal{M}}
. The
intrinsic gradient

\nabla:L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L^{2}(T{\mathcal{M}})
is the differentiation operator and maps scalar functions to tangent vector functions over
{\mathcal{M}}

[
10
]
. The adjoint of
\nabla
is the
intrinsic divergence
, which is defined as
\text{div}:L^{2}(T{\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
. The Laplace-Beltrami (LB) operator
{\mathcal{L}}:L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})\to L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
can be defined as the intrinsic divergence of the intrinsic gradient
[
18
]
. More formally,

{\mathcal{L}}f=-\text{div}\circ\nabla f.

(4)

The LB operator measures the difference between the function value at a point and the average function value over the point’s neighborhood
[
10
]
, similar to graph Laplacian matrix.

The manifold convolution of
{\tilde{h}}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}
and the manifold signal
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
is called a manifold filter with impulse response
{\tilde{h}}
, and is defined by leveraging the heat diffusion dynamics
[
8
]
. Explicitly, this filter, denoted
{\mathbf{h}}
, is given by

\displaystyle g(x)=({\mathbf{h}}f)(x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\tilde{h}}(t)e^{-t{\mathcal{L}}}f(x)\text{d}t={\mathbf{h}}({\mathcal{L}})f(x)\text{.}

(5)

This is a spatial manifold convolution operator because it is parametrized by the operator
{\mathcal{L}}
and directly operates on points
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

The LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is self-adjoint and positive-semidefinite. These facts, together with the compactness of
{\mathcal{M}}
, imply that
{\mathcal{L}}
has a real, positive and discrete eigenvalue spectrum
\{\lambda_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
such that
{\mathcal{L}}\bm{\phi}_{i}=\lambda_{i}\bm{\phi}_{i}
, where
\bm{\phi}_{i}
is the eigenfunction associated with eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
. The eigenvalues are ordered in increasing order as
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\lambda_{3}\leq\ldots
. The eigenfunctions are orthonormal and thus form an eigenbasis of
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
in the intrinsic sense.

Denoting
[\hat{f}]_{i}=\langle f,\bm{\phi}_{i}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})}=\int_{{\mathcal{M}}}f(x)\bm{\phi}_{i}(x)\text{d}\mu(x)
and
e^{-t{\mathcal{L}}}\bm{\phi}_{i}=e^{-t\lambda_{i}}\bm{\phi}_{i}
, the manifold convolution can be represented in the manifold frequency domain as

\displaystyle[\hat{g}]_{i}=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\tilde{h}}(t)e^{-t\lambda_{i}}\text{d}t[\hat{f}]_{i}\text{.}

(6)

Hence, the frequency response of the filter
{\mathbf{h}}({\mathcal{L}})
is given by
\hat{h}(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}{\tilde{h}}(t)e^{-t\lambda}\text{d}t
, which depends only on the function
\tilde{h}
and on the eigenvalues of the LB operator.
Summing over all
i
, we thus get

g={\mathbf{h}}({\mathcal{L}})f=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\hat{h}(\lambda_{i})[\hat{f}]_{i}\bm{\phi}_{i}.

(7)

3
Convergence of filtering on sampled manifolds

Our goal is to derive a non-asymptotic approximation bound for manifold filtering on sampled manifolds, providing theoretical guarantees when the sampled manifold is modeled as a graph and showing that graph filtering can be used to approximate manifold filtering. We start by constructing the graph corresponding to the sampled manifold.

3.1
Sampled manifolds as graphs

Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots x_{n}\}
be a set of
n
points sampled uniformly and independently at random from the
d
-dimensional manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
according to measure
\mu
. The empirical measure associated with
\text{d}\mu
is
p_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{i}}
, where
\delta_{x_{i}}
is the Dirac measure supported on
x_{i}
. Similar to the definition of inner product in
L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
(
3
), the inner product in
L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})
is defined as

\langle u,v\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}=\int u(x)v(x)\text{d}p_{n}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}u(x_{i})v(x_{i}).

(8)

The norm in
L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})
with measure
p_{n}
is therefore

\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}=\langle u,u\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}

for
u,v\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
. For signals
{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})
, the inner product is

\langle{\mathbf{u}},{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}[{\mathbf{u}}]_{i}[{\mathbf{v}}]_{i}.

From
X
, we construct an undirected graph
{\mathbf{G}}_{n}
by seeing the sampled points as nodes. The weights of the edges connecting nodes
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
are determined by

w_{ij}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\epsilon(4\pi\epsilon)^{d/2}}\exp\left(-\frac{\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|^{2}}{4\epsilon}\right),

(9)

where
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|
stands for the Euclidean distance between points
x_{i}
and
x_{j}
and
\epsilon
is a parameter related to the Gaussian kernel
[
19
]
. The adjacency matrix
{\mathbf{A}}_{n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n\times n}
can be written as
[{\mathbf{A}}_{n}]_{ij}=w_{ij}
for
1\leq i,j\leq n
. Thus, the graph Laplacian associated with this graph can be written as

{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}_{n}=\mbox{diag}({\mathbf{A}}_{n}\bm{1})-{\mathbf{A}}_{n}

with
[{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}_{n}]_{ij}=-w_{ij}
if
i\neq j
and
[{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}_{n}]_{ii}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}w_{ik}

[
20
]
.

To discretize manifold signals
f\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
on the sampled manifold, we define a uniform sampling operator
{\mathbf{P}}_{n}:L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})\rightarrow L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})
yielding signals
{\mathbf{f}}\in L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})
defined explicitly as

{\mathbf{f}}={\mathbf{P}}_{n}f\text{ with }{\mathbf{f}}(x_{i})=f(x_{i}),\quad x_{i}\in X.

(10)

This indicates that
{\mathbf{f}}
shares the function values of the manifold signal
f
at the sample points
X
.
Seeing the graph Laplacian as an operator acting on
{\mathbf{f}}:X\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we can write the diffusion operation on each point
x
explicitly as

{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}_{n}{\mathbf{f}}(x_{i})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{ij}\left({\mathbf{f}}(x_{i})-{\mathbf{f}}(x_{j})\right),\quad i=1,2,\ldots n.

(11)

It can be seen that this formulation can extend to a continuous manifold signal
f
, which we denote as

{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}_{n}f(x)=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\epsilon(4\pi\epsilon)^{d/2}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(f(x)-f(x_{j})\right)e^{-\frac{\|x-x_{j}\|^{2}}{4\epsilon}},\;x\in{\mathcal{M}}.

(12)

If we further extend the set of sample points from
X
to all the points on the manifold, we finally get the functional approximation of the graph Laplacian,

{\mathbf{L}}^{\epsilon}f(x)=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{\epsilon(4\pi\epsilon)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\left(f(x)-f(y)\right)e^{-\frac{\|x-y\|^{2}}{4\epsilon}}\text{d}\mu(y),

(13)

which operates on all
x\in{\mathcal{M}}
.

3.2
Graph Laplacian approximation of the LB operator

We first demonstrate the consistency of the discrete graph Laplacians defined in (
12
) when operating on the eigenfunctions
\bm{\phi}_{i}\in L^{2}({\mathcal{M}})
of the LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
. The quality of the approximation given by the discrete Laplace operator on the sampled manifold is quantified by the following non-asymptotic result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathsf{N}}
be a compact smooth differentiable
d
-dimensional manifold with LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
, whose spectrum is given by
\{\lambda_{i},\bm{\phi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
, and assume
\bm{\phi}_{i}\in C({\mathcal{M}})
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}_{n}
be the discrete graph connecting points
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
sampled uniformly and independently at random from
{\mathcal{M}}
, with edge weights as in (
9
) and
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>n^{-1/(d+4)}
. Then, it holds

|{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}\bm{\phi}_{i}(x)-{\mathcal{L}}\bm{\phi}_{i}(x)|\leq\left(C_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\ln(1/\delta)}{2n}}+C_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon}\right)\lambda_{i}^{\frac{d+2}{4}},

(14)

with probability at least
1-\delta
. The constants
C_{1}
,
C_{2}
depend on the volume of the manifold.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.1
.
∎

This theorem provides a point-wise upper bound for the error incurred when using the discrete graph Laplacian to approximate the LB operator operating on the eigenfunctions of
{\mathcal{L}}
. We can see that this bound is not only related to the number of sampling points
n
, but also grows with the corresponding eigenvalue
\lambda_{i}
. This makes sense considering that higher eigenvalues correspond to eigenfunctions with high oscillation
[
21
]
.

Based on this approximation result, we can further derive a non-asymptotic approximation bound relating the spectra of the graph Laplacian and the LB operator using the Davis-Khan theorem
[
22
]
. This result, stated in Theorem
2
, will allows us to analyze the approximation of a manifold filter by a graph filter through their spectral representations.

Theorem 2
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{\mathsf{N}}
be a compact smooth differentiable
d
-dimensional manifold with LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
whose spectrum is given by
\{\lambda_{i},\bm{\phi}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
. Let
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}
be a discrete graph Laplacian defined as in (
12
) with
\{x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}\}
sampled uniformly and independently at random from
{\mathcal{M}}
whose spectrum is given by
\{\lambda_{i,n}^{\epsilon},\bm{\phi}_{i,n}^{\epsilon}\}_{i=1}^{n}
.
Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
and asumme that
n
is sufficiently large so that
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)>n^{-1/(d+4)}
. Then, with probability at least
1-2e^{-n}
, we have

|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i,n}^{\epsilon}|\leq\Omega_{1}\sqrt{\epsilon},\quad\|a_{i}\bm{\phi}_{i,n}^{\epsilon}-\bm{\phi}_{i}\|\leq\Omega_{2}\sqrt{\epsilon},

(15)

with
a_{i}=\{-1,1\}
for all
i<K
. The constants
\Omega_{1}
,
\Omega_{2}
depend on
\lambda_{K}
, the eigengap of
{\mathcal{L}}
i.e.,
\theta=\min_{1\leq i\leq K}\{\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{i-1},\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_{i}\}
,
d
and the volume of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.2
.
∎

3.3
Graph filtering approximation of manifold filtering

Equipped with theoretical approximation results for both the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions of the Laplacian operators, we can now prove that graph filters can approximate manifold filters well in the spectral domain. We first show that we can generalize the definition of manifold convolutional filters to sampled manifolds using the same impulse response
\tilde{h}
.

From the definition in (
5
), recall that the manifold filter is parametric on the LB operator when the impulse response
\tilde{h}(t)
is fixed. By replacing the LB operator with the discrete graph Laplacian defined in (
12
), we can then obtain a graph convolutional filter

{\mathbf{g}}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\tilde{h}(t)e^{-t{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}}{\mathbf{f}}\text{d}t={\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}){\mathbf{f}},\quad{\mathbf{g}},{\mathbf{f}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

(16)

This can be seen as a
continuous-time
graph filtering process, different from the
discrete-time
graph filtering process
{\mathbf{h}}_{\mathbf{G}}
in (
1
), where the exponential term
e^{-{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}}
can be seen as the graph shift operator.
The frequency representation of this graph filter can be written as

{\mathbf{g}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\hat{h}(\lambda_{i,n}^{\epsilon})\langle{\mathbf{f}},\bm{\phi}_{i,n}^{\epsilon}\rangle_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}\bm{\phi}_{i,n}^{\epsilon},

(17)

which reveals the full dependency on the eigendecomposition of
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}
. Thus, we can relate graph filtering and manifold filtering using the spectral approximation results for the graph Laplacian and LB operator presented in Theorem
2
.

Unlike the finite spectrum of the graph Laplacian, the LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
possesses an infinite spectrum. We consider Weyl’s law
[
23
]
when analyzing the spectral properties of
{\mathcal{L}}
. This classical result states that the eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of
{\mathcal{L}}
grow in the order of
i^{2/d}
. Therefore, the difference between neighboring eigenvalues becomes quite small in the high frequency spectrum, i.e., large eigenvalues accumulate. Leveraging this fact, we can use a partition strategy to separate the spectrum into finite groups as in Definition
1
.

Definition 1
.

[
13
]
(
\alpha
-separated spectrum)
The
\alpha
-separated spectrum of LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
is defined as the partition
\Lambda_{1}(\alpha)\cup\ldots\cup\Lambda_{N}(\alpha)
such that
\lambda_{i}\in\Lambda_{k}(\alpha)
and
\lambda_{j}\in\Lambda_{l}(\alpha)
,
k\neq l
, satisfying

|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|>\alpha
.

The following defined
\alpha
-FDT filter allows obtaining the
\alpha
-separated spectrum as in Definition
1
.

Definition 2
.

[
13
]
(
\alpha
-FDT filter)
The
\alpha
-frequency difference threshold (
\alpha
-FDT) filter is defined as a filter
{\mathbf{h}}({\mathcal{L}})
whose frequency response satisfies

|{\hat{h}}(\lambda_{i})-{\hat{h}}(\lambda_{j})|\leq\gamma_{k}\mbox{ for all }\lambda_{i},\lambda_{j}\in\Lambda_{k}(\alpha)

(18)

with
\gamma_{k}\leq\gamma
for
k=1,\ldots,N
.

To derive an approximation bound, we will also need the manifold filter to have a Lipschitz frequency response as in Definition
3
.

Definition 3

(Lipschitz filter)
.

A filter is
A_{h}
-Lispchitz if its frequency response is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant
A_{h}
,

|{\hat{h}}(a)-{\hat{h}}(b)|\leq A_{h}|a-b|\text{ for all }a,b\in(0,\infty)\text{.}

(19)

Equipped with the above requirements for the filter frequency response, we can finally establish the upper bound on the manifold filter approximation error on the sampled manifold.

Theorem 3
.

Let
X=\{x_{1},x_{2},...x_{n}\}
be a set of
n
points sampled by an operator
{\mathbf{P}}_{n}
(
10
) from a
d
-dimensional manifold
{\mathcal{M}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Let
{\mathbf{G}}_{n}
be a discrete graph approximation of
{\mathcal{M}}
constructed from
X
with weight values as in (
9
) with
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)\geq n^{-1/(d+4)}
. Let
{\mathbf{h}}(\cdot)
be the convolutional filter parameterized by the LB operator
{\mathcal{L}}
of the manifold
{\mathcal{M}}
(
5
) or by the discrete graph Laplacian operator
{\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}
of the graph
{\mathbf{G}}_{n}
. Under the assumption that the frequency response of filter
{\mathbf{h}}
is Lipschitz continuous and
\alpha
-FDT with
\alpha^{2}\gg\epsilon
and
\gamma=\Omega_{2}^{\prime}\sqrt{\epsilon}/\alpha
, with probability at least
1-2n^{-2}
it holds that

\displaystyle\|{\mathbf{h}}({\mathbf{L}}_{n}^{\epsilon}){\mathbf{P}}_{n}f-{\mathbf{P}}_{n}{\mathbf{h}}({\mathcal{L}})f\|_{L^{2}({\mathbf{G}}_{n})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq\left(\frac{N\Omega_{2}^{\prime}}{\alpha}+A_{h}\Omega_{1}\right)\sqrt{\epsilon}+C_{gc}\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{{n}}}

(20)

where
N
is the partition size of
\alpha
-FDT filter and
C_{gc}
is related with
d
and the volume of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.3
.
∎

From this theorem, we see that if we take
\epsilon=n^{-1/(d+4)}
, the difference between filtering on the manifold and the sampled manifold is in the order of
O(n^{-1/(2d+8)})
. Thus, with a large enough number of sampling points, this approximation provides a good accuracy with high probability. This provides a formal theoretical guarantee for the ability of graph filters to approximate manifold filters on sampled manifolds, enabling approximate linear information processing on continuous non-Euclidean domains.

Numerical experiments

We consider a problem of automatic navigation control of an agent
[

]
. We intend to control an agent to find a path to the goal without colliding to obstacles. We sample grid points over the free space which means avoiding the obstacle areas to construct a graph structure. We generate four trajectories with Dijkstra’s shortest path algorithm leading a starting point to the goal. Each point along the trajectory is labeled with the direction of the velocity. The adjacency matrix can be calculated based on the weight value defined in (

We note that if the direct path between two points goes through the obstacles, we set the distance between them as infinity, i.e. the weight value as zero, so as to better capture the geometric structure. The input graph signal is the position of the nodes and the output of the graph filter is the direction of this node leading to the goal. We construct a one-layer and two-layer graph filters with
5
filter taps in each layer. We train all the architectures for

epochs with SGD optimizer with the learning rate set as
0.0002
. The performance of the learned graph filter is testified by randomly generating
100
starting points and computing the trajectories. If the trajectory can reach the goal without colliding into the obstacles, the trajectory is marked as “success”.

Conclusions

In this paper, we introduced manifold convolutional filters defined with the exponential Laplace-Beltrami operator to process geometric data. To access the manifold model, we sample uniformly over the manifold and construct a graph model as a sampled manifold. We first prove the non-asymptotic approximation error bound of discrete graph Laplacian to LB operator in both operator and spectral aspects. By transferring the manifold filtering to the sampled manifold, the approximation of the graph filtering to the manifold filtering can be derived. The non-asymptotic error bound decreases with the number of sampling points growing. The approximation of graph filtering is verified empirically on a navigation control problem over a manifold.

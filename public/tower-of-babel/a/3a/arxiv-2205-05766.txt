Weierstrass type projective Riccati equation expansion method and solutions of KdV equation
Sirendaoreji

Abstract
This paper presents two new Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of the projective Riccati equations and four conversion formulas for converting the Weierstrass elliptic functions to the hyperbolic and trigonometric functions. The Weierstrass elliptic function solutions to the projective Riccati equations and the conversion formulas are used to propose the called Weierstrass type projective Riccati equation expansion method. The Weierstrass elliptic function solutions, the solitary wave and the periodic wave solutions of the KdV equation are constructed by using the proposed method. The solitary wave like and the periodic wave solutions of the KdV equation are shown through some figures.

Introduction

Exact solutions to nonlinear evolution equations (NLEEs) play an important role
in applied mathematics, mathematical physics and nonlinear science. Therefore,
the problem of how to find new methods for solving NLEEs has become a hot topic
in soliton theory. In the past decades, various direct methods such as the
tanh–function method
[
1
]
, the auxiliary equation method
[
2
]
,
the Riccati equation expansion method
[
3
]
, the unified Riccati equation
expansion method
[
4
]
, the Jacobi elliptic function expansion
method
[
5
]
, the Weierstrass elliptic function method
[
6
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
and the projective Riccati
equation expansion method
[
11
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
, and so on,
have been proposed to find exact traveling wave solutions of NLEEs. Among them,
the projective Riccati equation expansion method can usually give some new special
solutions of NLEEs. Therefore, the researchers give more attention to find new
solutions of the projective Riccati equation. So far, two sets of Weierstrass
elliptic function solutions of the projective Riccati equation have been given and
applied to construct the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of NLEEs
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
. But most of these Weierstrass elliptic
function solutions cannot be transformed into the hyperbolic and trigonometric
function solutions because the previously used conversion formulas were
determined by the roots of the third order polynomial equation
w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3}=0
.
Therefore, this paper aims to give more Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of
the projective Riccati equations and establish the so–called conversion formulas
which can transform the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of NLEEs into the
hyperbolic and trigonometric function solutions. Further, we shall use the Weierstrass
elliptic function solutions of the projective Riccati equations and the conversion
formulas to propose a direct method which we named as the Weierstrass type projective
Riccati equation expansion method. Finally, we shall take the KdV equation as an
illustrative example to show the effectiveness of our method.

This paper is organized as follows. In the next section, four sets of Weierstrass
elliptic function solutions of the projective Riccati equations and four conversion
formulas to transform the Weierstrass elliptic function into the hyperbolic and
trigonometric function are constructed. In addition, the Weierstrass type projective
Riccati equation expansion method is proposed to find exact traveling wave solutions
to NLEEs.
In Sec.
3
, the proposed method is applied to construct the Weierstrass
elliptic function solutions, the solitary and periodic wave solutions of the KdV
equation. The conclusions are given in Sec.
4
.

2
Weierstrass type projective Riccati equation expansion method

The Weierstrass elliptic function
w=\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})
is defined as the inverse
function of the Weierstrass elliptic integral

\displaystyle\xi=\int_{\infty}^{w}{\frac{dt}{\sqrt{4t^{3}-g_{2}t-g_{3}}}},

(1)

or the solution of the following nonlinear ordinary differential equation

\displaystyle{\frac{dw}{d\xi}}=4w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3},

(2)

where
g_{2},g_{3}
are real parameters and called invariants.

In the following we shall consider the projective Riccati equations of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &F^{\prime}(\xi)=pF(\xi)G(\xi),\\
&G^{\prime}(\xi)=q+pG^{2}(\xi)-rF(\xi),\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

where
F
,
G
are unknown functions of the variable
\xi
and
p,q,r

are constants. By using the direct assumption approach we can construct
the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of Eqs.(
3
) as following

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &F(\xi)={\frac{q}{6r}}+{\frac{2}{pr}}\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3}),\\
&G(\xi)={\frac{12\wp^{\prime}(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}{p\left[pq+12\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]}},\end{aligned}\right.

(4)

\displaystyle{\qquad}G^{2}(\xi)=-{\frac{q}{p}}+{\frac{2r}{p}}F(\xi),

(5)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &F(\xi)={\frac{5q}{6r}}+{\frac{5q^{2}p}{72r\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}},\\
&G(\xi)={\frac{-q\wp^{\prime}(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}{\left[qp+12\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}},\end{aligned}\right.

(6)

\displaystyle{\qquad}G^{2}(\xi)=-{\frac{q}{p}}+{\frac{2r}{p}}F(\xi)-{\frac{24r^{2}}{25pq}}F^{2}(\xi),

(7)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &F(\xi)={\frac{(2+q)\left[pq+12\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]}{r\left[12p+pq+12\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]}},\\
&G(\xi)={\frac{\wp^{\prime}(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}{\left(\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})+{\frac{p}{2}}+{\frac{pq}{12}}\right)^{2}-{\frac{p^{2}}{4}}}},\end{aligned}\right.

(8)

\displaystyle{\qquad}G^{2}(\xi)=-{\frac{q}{p}}+{\frac{2r}{p}}F(\xi)-{\frac{r^{2}(q+4)}{p(q+2)^{2}}}F^{2}(\xi),

(9)

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &F(\xi)={\frac{q(p^{2}+2)\left[pq+12\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]}{pr\left[12q+p^{2}q+12p\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})\right]}},\\
&G(\xi)={\frac{q\wp^{\prime}(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})}{\left(p\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})+{\frac{q}{2}}+{\frac{p^{2}q}{12}}\right)^{2}-{\frac{q^{2}}{4}}}},\end{aligned}\right.

(10)

\displaystyle{\qquad}G^{2}(\xi)=-{\frac{q}{p}}+{\frac{2r}{p}}F(\xi)-{\frac{pr^{2}(p^{2}+4)}{q(p+2)^{2}}}F^{2}(\xi),

(11)

where the invariants
g_{2}
and
g_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle g_{2}={\frac{p^{2}q^{2}}{12}},g_{3}={\frac{p^{3}q^{3}}{216}}.

(12)

Here we point out that the solutions (
4
) and (
6
) are
previously known
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, but the solutions (
8
) and
(

We find that the Weierstrass elliptic function can degenerate to the hyperbolic and
trigonometric function by using the following conversion formulas

\displaystyle\wp(\xi,{\frac{\theta^{2}}{12}},-{\frac{\theta^{3}}{216}})={\frac{\theta}{12}}-{\frac{\theta}{4}}\rm sech^{2}({\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2}}\,\xi),\theta>0,

(13)

\displaystyle\wp(\xi,{\frac{\theta^{2}}{12}},-{\frac{\theta^{3}}{216}})={\frac{\theta}{12}}+{\frac{\theta}{4}}\rm csch^{2}({\frac{\sqrt{\theta}}{2}}\,\xi),\theta>0,

(14)

\displaystyle\wp(\xi,{\frac{\theta^{2}}{12}},-{\frac{\theta^{3}}{216}})={\frac{\theta}{12}}-{\frac{\theta}{4}}\sec^{2}({\frac{\sqrt{-\theta}}{2}}\,\xi),\theta<0,

(15)

\displaystyle\wp(\xi,{\frac{\theta^{2}}{12}},-{\frac{\theta^{3}}{216}})={\frac{\theta}{12}}-{\frac{\theta}{4}}\csc^{2}({\frac{\sqrt{-\theta}}{2}}\,\xi),\theta<0,

(16)

where
\theta
is a real number.

A given NLEE with respect to two variables
x
and
t
is of the form

\displaystyle P(u,u_{x},u_{t},u_{xx},u_{xt},u_{tt},u_{xxx},\cdots)=0,

(17)

where the subscripts denote the partial derivatives,
P
is a polynomial in unknown
function
u(x,t)
and its derivatives.

The Weierstrass type projective Riccati equation expansion method proposed
here follows the following five steps.

Step 1:
Making the wave transformation

\displaystyle u(x,t)=u(\xi),\xi=x-{\omega}t,

(18)

we may exchange the Eq. (
17
) into the following ODE

\displaystyle H(u,u^{\prime},u^{\prime\prime},\cdots)=0,

(19)

where primes denote the derivatives with respect to
\xi
.

Step 2:
Assume that the Eq. (
19
) has the truncated formal
series solution

\displaystyle u(\xi)=a_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}F^{i-1}(\xi)\left(a_{i}F(\xi)+b_{i}G(\xi)\right),

(20)

where
F(\xi)
and
G(\xi)
are the Weierstrass elliptic function solution
of the projective Riccati Eqs. (
3
),
n
is an integer number which
can be determined by balancing the highest order derivative terms with the
highest power nonlinear terms in (
19
),
a_{0}
,
a_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,2,\cdots,n)

are undetermined constants and
a_{n}^{2}+b_{n}^{2}\not=0
.

Step 3:
Substituting (
20
), (
3
) together with one
of the relations (
5
), (
7
), (
9
), (
11
)
into (
19
) and equating the coefficients of like
powers of
F^{i}(\xi)G^{j}(\xi)
to zero yields a set of algebraic equations.
Solving this set of algebraic equations with the aid of Maple or Mathematica
we can determine the values of
\omega,a_{0}
and
a_{i},b_{i}~(i=1,2,\cdots,n)
.

Step 4:
Putting each solutions of the algebraic equations obtained in

Step 3
together with the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions
F(\xi),G(\xi)

of the projective Riccati equations into (
20
) and using (
18
),
we obtain the Weierstrass type traveling wave solutions of the Eq. (
17
).

Step 5:
Taking the conversion formulas into the Weierstrass elliptic function
type traveling wave solutions obtained in
Step 4
, we get the solitary wave
and the periodic wave solutions of the Eq. (
17
).

4
Conclusions

In the present paper, the Weierstrass type projective Riccati equation
expansion method is proposed to construct Weierstrass elliptic function
solutions of NLEEs. At the same time, the conversion formulas are also
proposed to transform these Weierstrass elliptic function solutions into
the hyperbolic and trigonometric function solutions of NLEEs.
Our method is more applicable than the projective Riccati equation
expansion method and it can be regarded as an extension of the projective
Riccati equation expansion method. In order to explain our method more clear
we need to point out the following three points.

(1) The Weierstrass elliptic function solutions (
4
) and (
6
)
of the projective Riccati equations are known
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
,
and the solutions (
8
) and (
10
) are new.

(2) The known conversion formula such as
[
16
,
17
]

\displaystyle\wp(\xi,g_{2},g_{3})=e_{2}-(e_{2}-e_{3})\mathrm{cn}^{2}\left(\sqrt{e_{1}-e_{3}}\,\xi,m\right),

(33)

is related to the roots
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
of the third order polynomial
equation
w^{3}-g_{2}w-g_{3}=0
,but our conversion formulas (
13
)–
(
16
) don’t require these roots. Using formula (
33
),
we can transform the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of NLEEs
into the Jacobian elliptic function solutions, and then the hyperbolic
and trigonometric function solutions are obtained by taking the limit of
modulus
m\rightarrow{1}
and
m\rightarrow{0}
, but they may be
incorrect
[
19
,
20
]
.
In contrast, our formulas (
13
)–(
16
) can directly convert
the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions of NLEEs into the hyperbolic
and trigonometric function solutions, and ensure that the obtained solutions
are correct.
In addition, the conversion formulas (
13
)–(
16
) also
can be used in other Weierstrass elliptic function methods to transform
the Weierstrass elliptic function solutions into the hyperbolic and trigonometric
function solutions.

(3) Although we have concerned with the KdV equation, our method can be
applied to construct the exact solitary wave and periodic wave solutions
of a wide class of NLEEs.

Multi-pulse edge-localized states on quantum graphs
Adilbek Kairzhan, Dmitry E. Pelinovsky

Abstract
. Edge-localized stationary states of the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation on a general quantum graph are considered in the limit of large mass. Compared to the previous works, we include arbitrary multi-pulse positive states which approach asymptotically to a composition of N N solitons, each sitting on a bounded (pendant, looping, or internal) edge. Not only we prove that such states exist in the limit of large mass, but also we compute the precise Morse index (the number of negative eigenvalues in the corresponding linearized operator). In the case of the edge-localized N N -soliton states on the pendant and looping edges, we prove that the Morse index is exactly N N . The technical novelty of this work is achieved by avoiding elliptic functions (and related exponentially small scalings) and closing the existence arguments in terms of the Dirichlet-to-Neumann maps for relevant parts of the given graph.



1.
Introduction

We address standing waves of the focusing
NLS (nonlinear Schrödinger) equation posed on a quantum graph

\Gamma=\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V}\}
, where
\mathcal{E}
is the set of edges
and
\mathcal{V}
is the set of vertices (see
[
21
,
22
]
for review). The evolution system can be written in the normalized form:

i\Psi_{t}+\Delta\Psi+2|\Psi|^{2}\Psi=0,

(1.1)

where the Laplacian
\Delta
and the nonlinear term are defined
componentwise on edges
\mathcal{E}

subject to proper boundary conditions on the vertices
\mathcal{V}

(see
[
6
,
15
]
for introduction to linear differential equations
on quantum graphs).

The quantum graph
\Gamma=\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V}\}
is assumed to consist of a finite number
|\mathcal{E}|
of bounded and unbounded edges. Enumerating every edge in
\Gamma
uniquely gives the set

\mathcal{E}=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{|\mathcal{E}|}\}
. The function
\Psi
on
\Gamma
can be represented as a vector with
|\mathcal{E}|
components,

\Psi=\{\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\dots,\psi_{|\mathcal{E}|}\},

(1.2)

where
\psi_{j}
is defined on the edge
e_{j}
only. The function
\Psi
can be defined in the Hilbert
space of square-integrable functions
L^{2}(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{E}}L^{2}(e)
.

Weak (resp. strong) solutions of the NLS time flow (
1.1
)
are well defined in the
L^{2}
-based Sobolev spaces
H^{1}(\Gamma)
(resp.
H^{2}(\Gamma)
), where
H^{1,2}(\Gamma)=\bigoplus_{e\in\mathcal{E}}H^{1,2}(e)
,
provided the boundary conditions on
\mathcal{V}
are symmetric. Since
|\mathcal{E}|<\infty
, the NLS time flow (
1.1
) is essentially the evolution problem in one spatial dimension, which is globally well-posed both in
H^{1}(\Gamma)
and
H^{2}(\Gamma)
due to the cubic (
L^{2}
-subcritical) nonlinearity.

We consider
the natural Neumann–Kirchhoff (NK) boundary conditions

at each vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
given by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\Psi\text{ is continuous on }\Gamma,\\
\sum_{e\sim v}\partial\Psi(v)=0\text{ for every vertex }v\in\mathcal{V},\end{array}\right.

(1.3)

where the derivatives
\partial
are directed away from the vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}

and
e\sim v
denotes the edges
e\in\mathcal{E}
adjacent to the vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
.

Consistent with the boundary conditions (
1.3
),
weak solutions of the NLS equation (
1.1
) are defined
in the energy space
H^{1}_{C}(\Gamma):=H^{1}(\Gamma)\cap C^{0}(\Gamma)
,
where
C^{0}(\Gamma)
denotes the space of functions continuous on all edges in
\mathcal{E}
and across all vertex points in
\mathcal{V}
. These weak solutions
conserve the energy and mass functionals given respectively by

E(\Psi)=\|\nabla\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}-\|\Psi\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\Gamma)},\quad Q(\Psi)=\|\Psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}.

(1.4)

Standing waves of the NLS equation (
1.1
)
are given by the solutions of the form

\Psi(t,x)=\Phi(x)e^{-i\omega t}
,
where
\Phi\in H^{1}_{C}(\Gamma)
is a weak solution of
the stationary NLS equation

\omega\Phi=-\Delta\Phi-2|\Phi|^{2}\Phi,

(1.5)

for a given
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
. By bootstrapping arguments,
every weak solution of the stationary NLS equation (
1.5
)
is also a strong solution satisfying the natural NK conditions (
1.3
). Hence, we can use the vector representation
\Phi=(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{|\mathcal{E}|})
and rewrite the stationary NLS equation (
1.5
) on every edge
e_{j}
in
\mathcal{E}

as a collection of differential equations:

\omega\phi_{j}(x)=-\phi_{j}^{\prime\prime}(x)-2|\phi_{j}(x)|^{2}\phi_{j}(x),\quad x\in e_{j},

(1.6)

which satisfy the boundary conditions (
1.3
) on every vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
.
We write
\Phi\in H^{2}_{\rm NK}(\Gamma)
if
\Phi\in H^{2}(\Gamma)

satisfies the NK conditions (
1.3
). Only

real-valued
solutions of the stationary NLS equation are considered
but we write the modulus sign for easy generalizations.

Among all possible real-valued solutions of the stationary NLS equation (
1.5
), we are particularly interested
in the
positive edge-localized states
which satisfy the following conditions:

•

\Phi(x)>0
for every
x\in\Gamma
;

•

on each bounded edge
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}
, there is at most one local
critical point of
\phi_{j}
(either maximum or minimum) inside the edge;

•

on each unbounded edge
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}
, the function

\phi_{j}
is monotonically decreasing and has exponential decay to
0
at infinity.

Depending on the topological properties of the quantum graph
\Gamma
,
the positive edge-localized states could become
the ground state
,
the state of the least energy
E(\Psi)
at fixed mass
Q(\Psi)

[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

Although the set of conditions on
\Phi
seems to be restrictive, the positive edge-localized states exist
in the limit of large mass

[
4
,
7
,
10
,
18
]
.
This limit for large
\mu=Q(\Phi)
can be recast as the limit of large negative
\omega
in the stationary NLS equation (
1.5
) for the cubic (
L^{2}
-subcritical) nonlinearity. Existence of such edge-localized
states was confirmed analytically and/or numerically for the tadpole graph
[
2
,
8
,
9
,
23
,
24
]
, the dumbbell graph
[
12
,
20
]
, and the flower graph
[
16
]
. The importance of the positive edge-localized states is motivated by their (possible) orbital stability in the NLS time flow.

Bounded edges of
\mathcal{E}
are divided into pendant, looping, and internal edges
according to the following classification (see illustrations on Fig.
1
):

•

A pendant edge
of length
\ell
is associated
with the segment
[0,\ell]
, where the left end is isolated from
the rest of
\Gamma
subject to the Neumann boundary condition
and the right end is connected with the rest of
\Gamma
at a vertex
v
.

•

A looping edge
of length
2\ell
is associated with the segment
[-\ell,\ell]
,
where both ends at connected to the rest of
\Gamma
at a single vertex
v
,
hence contributing twice to the derivative condition in (
1.3
).

•

An internal edge
of length
2\ell
is associated with the segment
[-\ell,\ell]
,
where different ends at connected to the rest of
\Gamma
at two different vertices
v_{-}
and
v_{+}
.

Each unbounded edge is associated with the half-line
[0,\infty)
.
The edge-localized states considered in
[
7
]
consist of a single
large-amplitude component
\phi_{e}
on a fixed looping or internal
edge
e
of length
2\ell
such that
\phi_{e}
has a single local maximum inside
e
, monotone from its maximum to the vertices of
e
, and
concentrated on
e
in the following sense

\frac{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(e)}}{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}}\geq 1-Ce^{-2\epsilon\ell},

(1.7)

where
\epsilon:=\sqrt{|\omega|}
is a large parameter
and the constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon
. Moreover,
\Phi
has no internal maxima on the remainder of graph
\Gamma\backslash\{e\}
.
The same result also holds for the pendant edge
e
except that
\phi_{e}
has a single local maximum at the terminal vertex. The edge-localized states considered in
[
7
]
are illustrated on Fig.
2
.

Schematic illustration of edge-localized states on a pendant (left), a looping edge (middle), and an internal edge (right).

The construction of the edge-localized states in
[
7
]
relies
on the properties of elliptic functions and on careful rescaling
of exponentially small terms for the elliptic modulus. In addition,
the states of lowest energy at fixed mass were analyzed
in
[
7
]
by comparing the exponentially small terms in the
expansion of
\mu:=Q(\Phi)
in
\epsilon:=\sqrt{|\omega|}
.

A similar result on the edge-localized states in the large-mass limit was obtained independently in
[
4
]
using variational methods (for unbounded graphs only). Since the edge-localized states
were identified in
[
4
]
as local energy minimizers in the restricted space of functions in
H^{1}_{C}(\Gamma)
that attain their maximum on a given edge
e
, the Morse index for such states is exactly one, where
the Morse index
is the number of negative eigenvalues of the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}:H^{2}_{\rm NK}(\Gamma)\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
given by

\mathcal{L}=-\Delta-\omega-6|\Phi|^{2}.

(1.8)

If the graph
\Gamma
is unbounded,
\Phi
is bounded and decays to
0
at infinity exponentially fast, and
\omega<0
, then the linearized operator

\mathcal{L}
is self-adjoint and the absolutely continuous part of the spectrum is strictly
positive and bounded away from zero by
|\omega|
by Weyl’s theorem on
essential spectrum. Therefore, the Morse index is well-defined as the finite
number of negative eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}
accounting for
their finite multiplicity. We denote this number by
n(\mathcal{L})
.
We also denote the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}

by
z(\mathcal{L})
.

If the graph
\Gamma
is bounded, then the spectrum of
\mathcal{L}

is purely discrete and the numbers
n(\mathcal{L})
and
z(\mathcal{L})

are again well-defined since
\mathcal{L}
is bounded from below.

Additional results related to the edge-localized states in the limit of large energy were obtained in
[
10
]
, where bounded graphs with the pendant edges were considered and convergence of the edge-localized states to the half-solitons was proven. Multi-pulse states were also studied in
[
10
]
, all pulses localize at the terminal vertices. Edge-localized states were considered in
[
18
]
by recasting the existence problem to the semi-classical limit of an elliptic problem. It was proven in
[
18
]
that the location of the edge-localized state with a single maximum as the ground state of energy at fixed mass is determined by the longest pendant edge of a bounded graph (or the longest internal edge if no pendant and looping edges are present). These results are included in those in
[
7
]
but they are obtained in a more general setting in
[
18
]
.

The main result of this work concerns the construction of the
multi-pulse edge-localized states in the limit of
large mass (large negative
\omega
).
As
\omega\to-\infty
, the multi-pulse states approach asymptotically a composition of
N
solitons,
each sitting on a bounded (pendant, looping, or internal) edge.
The edges of the graph, where the multi-pulse state is localized,
must satisfy the following non-degeneracy assumptions.

Assumption 1
.

Let
\mathcal{E}_{N}:=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{N}\}
be the set of
N
edges
connected to
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
at
|B|
boundary vertices
\mathcal{V}_{B}=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{|B|}\}
. Let
\ell_{\rm min}
be the length of the shortest edge in
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
and
\ell_{j,{\rm min}}
be the minimal half-length of the looping and internal edges or the minimal length of the pendant edges adjacent to the boundary vertex
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
from
\mathcal{E}_{N}
,
1\leq j\leq|B|
.
The edge lengths must satisfy the constraints:

\ell_{\rm min}+\min_{1\leq i\leq|B|}\ell_{i,{\rm min}}>\ell_{j,{\rm min}}\quad 1\leq j\leq|B|

(1.9)

and

3\min_{1\leq i\leq|B|}\ell_{i,{\rm min}}>\ell_{j,{\rm min}},\quad 1\leq j\leq|B|.

(1.10)

Assumption 2
.

Each internal edge has no common boundary vertices with other internal edges in
\mathcal{E}_{N}
and its half-length is strictly minimal
among the half-lengths of the looping edges and the lengths of the pendant edges
in
\mathcal{E}_{N}
adjacent to its two boundary vertices.

Remark 1
.

In the definition of the edge lengths, we assume that each vertex has degree
3
or higher, except for the terminal vertices of the pendant edges which have degree
1
. In other words, we do not allow fake vertices of degree
2
in the graph
\Gamma
.

The following theorem gives the existence result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\Gamma=\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V}\}
be a graph with finitely many edges and
satisfying NK conditions at its vertices. For any
N
edges
\mathcal{E}_{N}:=\{e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{N}\}

of finite lengths satisfying Assumptions
1
and
2
and for large enough
\epsilon:=\sqrt{-\omega}

there exists a positive edge-localized state
\Phi
with the following properties:

(1)

\Phi|_{e_{j}}
has a single local maximum and is monotone
from its maximum to the end vertices of
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
, for
1\leq j\leq N
;

(2)

\Phi
has no internal maxima on the remainder
of the graph
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
;

(3)

\Phi
is concentrated on
\mathcal{E}_{N}
in the following sense

\frac{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N})}}{\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})}}\leq Ce^{-\epsilon\ell_{N}},

(1.11)

where the constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon

and
\ell_{N}=\min\{\ell_{1,{\rm min}},\ell_{2,{\rm min}},\dots,\ell_{|B|,{\rm min}}\}
.

Remark 2
.

For the pendant edge
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
, the maximum of
\Phi|_{e_{j}}
occurs
at the left edge with the Neumann boundary conditions.
For the looping edge
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
, the maximum of
\Phi|_{e_{j}}
occurs exactly at the middle point. For the internal edge
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
, the maximum of
\Phi|_{e_{j}}

is generally shifted from the middle point. The non-degeneracy condition
in Assumption
2
allows us to control
the shift of the maximum of
\Phi|_{e_{j}}
at the internal edge.
See Fig.
2
for illustration of edge-localized states on each bounded edge in
\mathcal{E}_{N}
.

Remark 3
.

Condition (
1.11
) is equivalent to (
1.7
) in the case
N=1
and
\mathcal{E}_{N}=\{e\}
. Indeed, it follows from (
1.11
) with
\ell_{N=1}=\ell
that

\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}+\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma\backslash\{e\})}\leq(1+C^{2}e^{-2\epsilon\ell})\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)},

(1.12)

which yields (
1.7
). It follows from Theorems 3.1, 3.3, and 3.5 in
[
7
]
that

|\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}-\epsilon|\leq C\epsilon^{2}e^{-2\epsilon\ell}

(1.13)

for the pendant edge and

|\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e)}-2\epsilon|\leq C\epsilon^{2}e^{-2\epsilon\ell}

(1.14)

for the looping and internal edges, where the constant
C
is independent of
\epsilon
. In the case of
N
edges, we have

\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})}=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{j})}

(1.15)

with similar estimates for
\|\Phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}(e_{j})}
, where
e_{j}\in\mathcal{E}_{N}
.

Remark 4
.

Compared to the work in
[
7
]
, we do not use
elliptic functions and exponentially small scalings,
which makes our results more general and the proofs simpler.
We partition the graph
\Gamma
into
\mathcal{E}_{N}
and
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}

and reduce the existence problem to a system of equations for
Dirichlet data on the boundary vertices in
\mathcal{V}_{B}
.
Then, we show that all these
equations can be solved independently of each other under
the constraints (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) on the lengths of edges
in Assumption
1
.
These constraints
provide compatability of asymptotic solutions for large
\epsilon

and allow us to ignore the Dirichlet data at other boundary vertices.

Our second main result is the precise characterization of
the Morse index
n(\mathcal{L})
and degeneracy index
z(\mathcal{L})

of the multi-pulse edge-localized states in the limit of
large mass (large negative
\omega
).
This characterization was not provided in
[
7
]
even in the case
of
N=1
. The following theorem gives the result
when the set
\mathcal{E}_{N}
does not include internal edges.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\Phi
be the positive
N
-pulse edge-localized state of Theorem
1
for large enough
\epsilon

and assume that the set
\mathcal{E}_{N}
contains only pendant and looping edges.
Then,
n(\mathcal{L})=N
and
z(\mathcal{L})=0
,
where
\mathcal{L}
is the linearized operator in (
1.8
).

Remark 5
.

To obtain the exact count of the Morse index and the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}
, we use a homotopy argument relating the non-positive spectra of the operators
\mathcal{L}:H_{\rm NK}^{2}(\Gamma)\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
and
\mathcal{L}_{D}:H_{\rm D}^{2}(\Gamma)\subset L^{2}(\Gamma)\to L^{2}(\Gamma)
where
\mathcal{L}_{D}
has the same differential representation as
\mathcal{L}
and
H_{\rm D}^{2}(\Gamma)
differs from
H_{\rm NK}^{2}(\Gamma)
by the Dirichlet conditions at the boundary vertices in
\mathcal{V}_{B}
instead of the NK conditions. The technique resembles the surgery principle widely used in the spectral analysis of differential operators on graphs
[
5
,
19
]
.

Remark 6
.

The implication of Theorem
2
to the time evolution
of perturbations to the positive
N
-pulse edge-localized states
of Theorem
1
is that these states are orbitally
unstable under the NLS time flow (
1.1
) if
N\geq 2
.
This follows from an easy application of the main results of
[
13
]
and
[
25
]
. In agreement with the variational
characterization of the single-pulse states on unbounded graphs
\Gamma
in
[
4
]
, the positive edge-localized states with
N=1

are orbitally stable. This follows via the standard
orbital stability theory
[
14
]
from the monotonicity of the mapping

\epsilon\mapsto\left\|\Phi\right\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})}

(1.16)

for large
\epsilon
and the exponential smallness of
\left\|\Phi\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{E}_{N})}
due to the decomposition (
1.15
) with bounds (
1.11
), (
1.13
) and (
1.14
). Note that it was proven in
[
7
]
that the mapping (
1.16
) is
C^{1}
.

Remark 7
.

If an internal edge is present in the set
\mathcal{E}_{N}
, then the explicit count of the Morse index in Theorem
2
is no longer applicable, as shown
in Example

. Therefore, characterization of the Morse index
and the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue of

is still an open problem in the case of multi-pulse states localized on internal edges.

2.
Preliminary results

Since
\epsilon:=\sqrt{-\omega}
is considered to be large,
we rescale the stationary NLS equation (
1.5
)
with the transformation
\Phi(x)=\epsilon U(\epsilon x)
,
x\in\Gamma
.
As a result, the graph
\Gamma=\{\mathcal{E},\mathcal{V}\}
is transformed to the

\epsilon
-scaled graph
\Gamma_{\epsilon}=\{\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon},\mathcal{V}\}

for which every bounded edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
of length
\ell_{e}

transforms to the edge
e_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
of length
\epsilon\ell_{e}
but the unbounded edge
e\in\mathcal{E}
remains the same as
e\in\mathcal{E}_{\epsilon}
. The stationary NLS equation (
1.5
)
is rewritten in the parameter-free form

(1-\Delta)U-2|U|^{2}U=0.

(2.1)

Writing
U=(u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{|\mathcal{E}|})

gives a collection of differential equations

-u_{j}^{\prime\prime}(z)+u_{j}(z)-2|u_{j}(z)|^{2}u_{j}(z)=0,\quad z\in e_{j,\epsilon},

(2.2)

subject to the boundary conditions (
1.3
) at vertices
v\in\mathcal{V}
.

We select
N
edges
\mathcal{E}_{N}:=\{e_{1},e_{2},\ldots,e_{N}\}
with
N\leq|\mathcal{E}|
in the original graph
\Gamma

and partition the graph into two parts
\mathcal{E}_{N}
and
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
.
The two graphs intersect at the vertices in the set
\mathcal{V}_{B}:=\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{|B|}\}
, which are referred to as
boundary vertices
.
After rescaling of
\Gamma
to
\Gamma_{\epsilon}
,
we obtain
\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
and
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
.

We introduce the Dirichlet data on the boundary vertices

\vec{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{|B|})
and the Neumann data

\vec{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{|B|})
for the graph
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
with

p_{j}:=u_{e\sim v_{j}}(v_{j}),\quad q_{j}:=\sum_{e\sim v_{j}}\partial u_{e}(v_{j}),\quad v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B},

(2.3)

where the derivatives
\partial
are directed away from
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
and
e\sim v_{j}
lists all edges
e\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
incident to the vertex
v_{j}
.
We are looking for solutions of the differential equations
(
2.2
) such that

\sup_{z\in e_{j,\epsilon}}|u_{j}(z)|<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad e_{j,\epsilon}\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon},

(2.4)

and

\sup_{z\in e_{j,\epsilon}}|u_{j}(z)|>\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}},\quad e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}

(2.5)

where
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
is the
constant solution of the differential equations in (
2.2
).

The following two lemmas were proven in
[
7
]
(Theorem 2.9 and Lemma 2.12).

Lemma 1
.

There exist
C_{0}>0
,
p_{0}>0
, and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for every

\vec{p}
with
\|\vec{p}\|<p_{0}
and every

\epsilon>\epsilon_{0}
, there exists a solution
U\in H^{2}_{\rm NK}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})

to the stationary NLS equation (
2.1
)
on
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
subject
to the Dirichlet data on
\mathcal{V}_{B}
which
is unique among functions satisfying (
2.4
).
The solution satisfies the estimate

\|U\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}\leq C_{0}\|\vec{p}\|,

(2.6)

while its Neumann data satisfies

|q_{j}-D_{j}p_{j}|\leq C_{0}\left(\|\vec{p}\|e^{-\epsilon\ell_{\rm min}}+\|\vec{p}\|^{3}\right),\qquad 1\leq j\leq|B|,

(2.7)

where
D_{j}
is the degree of the
j
-th boundary vertex
in
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
and
\ell_{\rm min}

is the length of the shortest edge in
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
.
Furthermore, if
p_{j}\geq 0
for every
j
, then
U(z)\geq 0
for
all
z\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
and

has no internal local maxima in

3.
Existence of multi-pulse loop-localized states

Here we explain the asymptotic construction of the multi-pulse loop-localized
states and give the proof of Theorem

For each boundary vertex

with

we use the Dirichlet data

as the unknown variable
and write the flux boundary condition to determine

.
The main advantage of this method is that the value of

can be found independently from the conditions at the other boundary vertices.

Fix

in
1\leq j\leq|B|
.
Assume that the boundary vertex

is in contact with

\mathcal{E}_{j,{\rm loop}}

2

are centered at

By Lemma

directed away from

By Lemma

\displaystyle q_{j}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(K_{j}+2L_{j}+M_{j})p_{j}-4\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{j,{\rm pend}}}e^{-\epsilon\ell_{e}}-8\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{j,{\rm loop}}}e^{-\epsilon\ell_{e}}

Applying the scaling transformation

subject to the boundary conditions (

4.1.
Idea of the proof

Recall that
\mathcal{V}_{B}
is the set of boundary vertices separating

and
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}

Then for every

H^{2}_{\rm NK}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})\equiv H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}=0}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
and
H^{2}_{\rm D}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})\equiv H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
, where
H^{2}_{\rm D}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})

stands for the domain with the Dirichlet conditions at the boundary vertices:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}W\text{ is continuous on }\Gamma_{\epsilon},\\
\sum_{e\sim v}\partial W(v)=0\text{ for every vertex }v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{B},\\
W(v_{j})=0\text{ for every vertex }v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}.\end{array}\right.

(4.3)

We also introduce the linearized operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}
,
such that the spectral problem
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=\lambda W
is still represented
by the differential equations (
4.1
), but the domain of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}
is
H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})\subset L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
.

The proof of Theorem
2
relies
on the continuity argument from
\bm{\alpha}=\infty
to
\bm{\alpha}=0

given by the following proposition.

Proposition 1
.

Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=0
admits no solutions
W\in H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
for every
\bm{\alpha}\in[0,\infty)^{|B|}
.
Then,

n(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=0})=n(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}),\qquad z(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=0})=z(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}).

Proof.

The proof relies on the standard perturbation theory of linear operators given in Chapter 7 of
[
17
]
. In short, since
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}
is a holomorphic family of self-adjoint operators, each isolated eigenvalue of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}
depends continuously on
\alpha
. Therefore, if no eigenvalues of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}
cross zero when
\bm{\alpha}

traverses from
\bm{\alpha}=\infty
to
\bm{\alpha}=0
, then the number of negative eigenvalues as well as the multiplicity of the zero eigenvalue remains the same.
∎

By using Proposition
1
, Theorem
2
holds if we can verify the following claims for large
\epsilon
:

\displaystyle\text{\bf Claim 1:}\qquad n(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty})=N\text{ and }z(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty})=0,

\displaystyle\text{\bf Claim 2:}\qquad\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=0\text{ admits no solutions }W\in H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})\text{ with }\alpha\in[0,\infty)^{|B|}.

Below we give proofs of these claims.

4.2.
Proof of Claim 1

The proof is based on the Sturm’s Comparison theorem
(see Section 5.5 in
[
26
]
). For simplicity, we assume that
\mathcal{E}_{N}
contains only looping edges. Due to the symmetry of solutions in the looping edges, the proof extends to the pendant edges with minor modifications.

The spectral problem for the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}
can be expressed by the second-order differential equations
(
4.1
) equipped with the Dirichlet conditions at the boundary vertices given by (
4.3
).
The spectral problem for
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}
is given by
N
uncoupled problems on the
N
edges in
\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
and another uncoupled problem in
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
. On each edge
e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
, we have

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}-w_{j}^{\prime\prime}(z)+w_{j}(z)-6u_{j}(z)^{2}w_{j}(z)=\lambda w_{j}(z),\quad z\in(-\epsilon\ell_{j},\epsilon\ell_{j}),\\
w_{j}(-\epsilon\ell_{j})=w_{j}(\epsilon\ell_{j})=0,\end{array}\right.

(4.4)

where
u_{j}(z)
satisfies the condition (
2.5
).
Since
\epsilon
is large,
p:=u(\epsilon\ell_{j})
and
q:=-u^{\prime}(\epsilon\ell_{j})
are small and positive by Lemma
2
.
For
\lambda=0
, the homogeneous equation (
4.4
) is solved by the superposition formula (
2.15
) in Lemma
4
, where
the even solution
s(z)
has exactly one nodal point on
(0,\epsilon\ell_{j})

and the odd solution
u^{\prime}(z)
has no nodal points on
(0,\epsilon\ell_{j})
. By Sturm’s Comparison theorem, the spectral problem (
4.4
) admits exactly one simple negative eigenvalue and no zero eigenvalue. Since eigenvalues of all
N
spectral problems (
4.4
) also appear in the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}
, we have
n(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty})\geq N
.

Remark 13
.

If
e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
is a pendant edge, the Neumann condition at one end implies that the spectral problem (
4.4
) is solved in the space of even functions, where it still admits exactly one simple negative eigenvalue and no zero eigenvalue.

Next, we show that the uncoupled problem in
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
given by

-W^{\prime\prime}+W-6U^{2}W=\lambda W,\quad z\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon},

(4.5)

subject to the boundary conditions (
4.3
) has a strictly positive spectrum.
Since
U
on
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
satisfies the bound (
2.6
)
with small
\vec{p}
given by (
3.4
),
we obtain from (
4.5
) after multiplication by
W
and integration over
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}

with the boundary conditions (
4.3
):

\lambda\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}=\|W\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}-6\|UW\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}+\|W^{\prime}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}\geq C\|W\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})},

for some
C>0
. Hence,
\lambda\geq C>0
for every solution
W\in H^{2}_{\rm D}((\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})
, and the spectral problem (
4.5
) does not contribute into the nonpositive part of the spectrum of the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty}
. This completes the proof of
n(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty})=N
and
z(\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}=\infty})=0
.

4.3.
Proof of Claim 2

Let
W
be a solution of
\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=0
in
H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
with large enough
\epsilon
.
For every looping edge
e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
associated with the segment
[-\epsilon\ell_{j},\epsilon\ell_{j}]
, the restriction of
W
to the edge
e_{j,\epsilon}
denoted by
w_{j}
solves the second-order differential equation

-w_{j}^{\prime\prime}(z)+w_{j}(z)-6u_{j}(z)^{2}w_{j}(z)=0,\quad z\in(-\epsilon\ell_{j},\epsilon\ell_{j}).

(4.6)

where
u_{j}(z)
satisfies the condition (
2.5
).
The following lemma computes the
contributions of the solution of the differential equation
(
4.6
) to the last boundary condition
in (
4.2
).

Lemma 5
.

If
e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
is a looping edge with
\epsilon
large enough, then either
w_{j}\equiv 0
on the entire edge or

\alpha_{\pm}(e_{j,\epsilon}):=\mp\frac{w_{j}^{\prime}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})}{w_{j}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})}<0.

(4.7)

Proof.

By Lemma
4
, the general solution is
given by (
2.15
) rewritten again as

w_{j}(z)=Au_{j}^{\prime}(z)+Bs_{j}(z)

(4.8)

for some parameters
A
and
B
.
Since
u_{j}^{\prime}(z)
is odd,
s_{j}(z)
is even, and
u_{j}^{\prime}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})\neq 0
, the continuity condition
w_{j}(-\epsilon\ell_{j})=w_{j}(\epsilon\ell_{j})
necessarily implies
A=0
.

If
B=0
, then
w_{j}\equiv 0
on the entire edge. If
B\neq 0
,
then
w_{j}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})\neq 0
and
\alpha_{\pm}(e_{j,\epsilon})
in (
4.7
)
is defined by
s_{j}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})
and
s_{j}^{\prime}(\pm\epsilon\ell_{j})
. It follows
from the second inequality in (
2.16
) and the symmetry of
s_{j}(z)
that
\alpha_{\pm}(e_{j,\epsilon})<0
.
∎

Remark 14
.

If
e_{j,\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
is a pendant edge, the Neumann condition at one end still implies that
A=0
, after which the proof of Lemma
5
holds and gives
\alpha_{+}(e_{j,\epsilon})<0
.

We denote the value of
W
at each boundary point
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
as

\mathfrak{p}_{j}:=W(v_{j})
. Then on the subgraph
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
,
W
solves the homogeneous equation

-W^{\prime\prime}+W-6U^{2}W=0,\quad z\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon},

(4.9)

subject to the nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}W\text{ is continuous on }\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon},\\
\sum_{e\sim v}\partial W(v)=0\text{ for every vertex }v\in\mathcal{V}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{B},\\
W(v_{j})=\mathfrak{p}_{j}\text{ for every vertex }v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}.\end{array}\right.

(4.10)

The following lemma computes the
contributions of the solution of the boundary-value problem
(
4.9
)–(
4.10
)
to the last boundary condition
in (
4.2
).

Lemma 6
.

There are
C_{0}>0
and
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that for every
\vec{\mathfrak{p}}=(\mathfrak{p}_{1},\mathfrak{p}_{2},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{|B|})
and every
\epsilon>\epsilon_{0}
, the unique solution
W\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})
of the boundary-value problem (
4.9
)–(
4.10
)
satisfies

\|W\|_{H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}\leq C_{0}\|\vec{\mathfrak{p}}\|

(4.11)

and

|\mathfrak{q}_{j}-D_{j}\mathfrak{p}_{j}|\leq C_{0}\|\vec{\mathfrak{p}}\|e^{-\epsilon\ell_{\rm min}},

(4.12)

where
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
is the Neumann data directed away from
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
,
D_{j}
is the degree of the
j
-th boundary vertex
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
in
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
, and
\ell_{\rm min}
is the length of the shortest edge in
\Gamma\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N}
.

Proof.

Since
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}
is small for large enough
\epsilon
by the bound (
2.6
)
with
\vec{p}
given by (
3.4
), the boundary-value problem (
4.9
)–(
4.10
) can be analyzed with Theorem 2.1 and Lemma 2.3 in
[
7
]
for
g=0
. The bound (
4.12
) follows from the bound (2.4) in
[
7
]
due to the smallness of
\|U\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon})}
.
∎

Remark 15
.

The Neumann data
\mathfrak{q}_{j}
in (
4.12
) is directed in the opposite direction compared to
\partial\Psi(v_{j})
in (
4.2
). In other words,

\sum_{e\sim v_{j},e\in\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}}\partial\Psi(v_{j})=-\mathfrak{q}_{j}=-D_{j}\mathfrak{p}_{j}+\mathcal{O}(\|\vec{\mathfrak{p}}\|e^{\epsilon\ell_{\rm min}}).

(4.13)

We are now ready to show that no solutions
W\in H^{2}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
of

\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=0
satisfy the last boundary condition in (
4.2
) with
\alpha_{j}
being nonnegative.

First, we consider the case when
\mathfrak{p}_{j}=0
for all
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
. Then, the right hand side of the last boundary conditions in (
4.2
) vanishes and
W
satisfies the Neumann-Kirchhoff (NK) boundary conditions. Moreover, by Lemma
5
, the

\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
-components of the solution
W
are entirely zero, and make no contribution into the last condition in (
4.2
).
In what follows, the restriction of
W
to
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
solves the second-order differential equation (
4.9
) subject to the Dirichlet conditions at the boundary
vertices. By Lemma
6
due to the bound (
4.11
), the only solution on
\Gamma_{\epsilon}\backslash\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
is zero,
hence
W=0
on
\Gamma_{\epsilon}
.

Therefore, there exists at least one boundary vertex
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
such that the corresponding
\mathfrak{p}_{j}\neq 0
. Up to multiplication by a constant, we may assume that
\mathfrak{p}_{j}=1
. Using Lemmas
5
and
6
and the expansion (
4.13
), the last condition in (
4.2
) for large enough
\epsilon
becomes

\alpha_{j}=\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{j,\epsilon}}\alpha_{-}(e)+\sum_{e\in\mathcal{E}_{j,\epsilon}}\alpha_{+}(e)-\mathfrak{q}_{j}<0,

(4.14)

where
\mathcal{E}_{j,\epsilon}\subset\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
is the set of edges in
\mathcal{E}_{N,\epsilon}
adjacent to the vertex
v_{j}\in\mathcal{V}_{B}
.
This contradicts to the assumption that
\alpha_{j}\in[0,\infty)
. Hence no

W\in H^{2}_{\bm{\alpha}}(\Gamma_{\epsilon})
exists such that

\mathcal{L}_{\bm{\alpha}}W=0
for
\alpha\in[0,\infty)^{|B|}
.

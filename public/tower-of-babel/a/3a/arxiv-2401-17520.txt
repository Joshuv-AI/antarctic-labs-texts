Abstract
. Given a finite Blaschke product B B we prove asymptotically sharp estimates on the ℓ ∞ \ell^{\infty} -norm of the sequence of the Fourier coefficients of B n B^{n} as n n tends to ∞ \infty . We provide constructive examples which show that our estimates are sharp. As an application we construct a sequence of n × n n\times n invertible matrices T T with arbitrary spectrum in the unit disk and such that the quantity | det T | ⋅ ‖ T − 1 ‖ ⋅ ‖ T ‖ 1 − n |\det{T}|\cdot\|T^{-1}\|\cdot\|T\|^{1-n} grows as a power of n n . This is motivated by Schäffer’s question on norms of inverses.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{D}
be the unit disk of the complex plane,
m\geq 1
, let
\sigma=\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}\right)\in\mathbb{D}^{m}

and let
B
be the finite Blaschke product of degree
m\geq 1
associated
to
\sigma
:

B(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}b_{\lambda_{j}}(z),

where
b_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{z-\lambda}{1-\bar{\lambda}z}
is the
Blaschke factor corresponding to
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
.
Here and later on we omit the standard unimodular factor
\bar{\lambda}/|\lambda|
which is of no importance for the questions we study here. Furthermore, we assume that
0\not\in\sigma
.
For
k\geq 0

we consider the
k^{{\rm th}}
-Fourier coefficient of
B^{n}
defined
by:

\widehat{B^{n}}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}B^{n}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})e^{-\rm ik\theta}\,d\theta.

We study the asymptotic behavior of the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm
of the sequence
\bigl(\widehat{B^{n}}(k)\bigr)_{k\geq 0}
:

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}:=\sup_{k\geq 0}|\widehat{B^{n}}(k)|,

as
n
grows large. We use some standard notation
from asymptotic analysis. For two positive functions

f,g
we say that
f
is
dominated by
g
, denoted by
f\lesssim g
, if there is a constant

c>0
such that
f\leq cg
. We say that
f
and
g
are comparable,
denoted by
f\asymp g
, if both
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

The case
B=b_{\lambda}
,
\lambda
being arbitrary in
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
,
is well-studied
[
1
,
16
,
2
,
10
]
and it is known that

(1.1)

\|\widehat{b_{\lambda}^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\asymp n^{-1/3}.

We mention in passing that Y. Meyer recently rediscovered the upper bound in (
1.1
) in view of applying it to some sparse crystalline measure constructions
[
10
]
.
Precise asymptotic formulas for the
k^{{\rm th}}
Fourier
coefficients of
b_{\lambda}^{n}
,
k\in[0,\infty)
as
n\rightarrow\infty

have been recently obtained in
[
2
]
where the authors distinguish
several regions of different asymptotic behavior of
\widehat{b_{\lambda}^{n}}(k)

in terms of
k
and
n.
These asymptotic formulas are applied to
the construction of strongly annular functions with Taylor coefficients
satisfying sharp summation properties, which improves and generalizes
the results in
[
1
]
.

Information about those coefficients has also recently
been exploited in operator theory
[
8
]
to identify decreasing
weights for which composition operators are bounded on weighted Hardy
spaces of Hilbert type. Observe also that a connection was earlier
established in
[
3
]
between the asymptotics of the Fourier coefficients
of
B^{n}
and the boundedness of the composition operator
\mathcal{C}_{B}
,
\mathcal{C}_{B}(f)=f\circ B

on the analytic Beurling–Sobolev space of analytic functions
in
\mathbb{D}
whose sequence of Taylor coefficients belongs to

l^{p},

p\in[1,\infty]
. Generally speaking, to verify whether

\mathcal{C}_{B}
is a bounded linear operator from one Banach space
X

of analytic functions into another, say
Y
, it is often enough to
know the asymptotic behavior of
\|B^{n}\|_{Y}
. It is shown in
particular in
[
3
]
that for any finite Blaschke product
B

(1.2)

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{p}}\asymp n^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}\ \textnormal{for}\ p\in[1,2]

and that

(1.3)

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{p}}\gtrsim n^{\frac{2-p}{2p}}\ \textnormal{for}\ p\in[2,\infty].

The study of the case
p=1
in (
1.2
) with a single Blaschke factor
B=b_{\lambda}

was probably initiated by J.-P. Kahane
[
6
]
. Applying van
der Corput type estimates on
\widehat{b_{\lambda}^{n}}(k)

[
6
, p. 253]
,
he proved that

which is in line with (

). Notice that his motivation
[
6
, Theorem 1]

was different from the above: he generalized a theorem by Z. K. Leibenson

[
7
]
, which is a special case of a theorem
[
13
, Theorem 4.1.3]

about homomorphisms of group algebras due to P. T. Cohen. The exact
asymptotic

-dependency of

for

was described in
[
16
]
and a change of asymptotic behavior
at

was in particular discovered
[
16
,
2
]
. In this paper
we focus on the case
p=\infty
: in this case the estimate (
1.3
)
reads

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\gtrsim n^{-1/2}

for any finite Blaschke product
B
. We will prove that

is never of order
n^{-1/2}
. More precisely, for any finite Blaschke product

, there exists an integer

such that

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\asymp n^{-1/N}.

For every integer

of degree

It is known
[

– respectively

Finally we apply (

invertible matrices

such that

\frac{|\det{T}|\cdot\|T^{-1}\|}{\|T\|^{n-1}}\gtrsim n^{1/N}

This is motivated by Schäffer’s question on the

invertible matrices, see Section 3.

Notation and statement of our results

In this section we give sharp asymptotics for the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm of the sequence
\bigl(\widehat{B^{n}}(k)\bigr)_{k\geq 0}
,

1

We denote by

B(e^{{\rm i}\theta})=\exp\left({\rm i}\psi_{B}(\theta)\right)

Let

3.
Application to operator theory

A well-established circle of questions in Operator Theory concerns estimating the norm of the inverse of an invertible
n\times n
matrix
T
on an
n
-dimensional Banach space
X
. In the early 1970’s, B. L. Van der Waerden, W. A. Coppel and J. J. Schäffer
[
14
]
studied the smallest quantity
C
, denoted
C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)
,
such that

|\det{T}|\cdot\|T^{-1}\|\leq C\|T\|^{n-1}

for any invertible
T
and for any
X
.
In 1970, Schäffer
[
14
]
proved the estimate
C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\leq\sqrt{en}
,
and showed that the inequality

(3.1)

|\det T|\cdot\|T^{-1}\|\leq 2\|T\|^{n-1}

holds for any invertible
T
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
endowed
with the
\ell^{1}
-norm or with the
\ell^{\infty}
-norm (and that
(
3.1
) is optimal in both cases). This led him to
make the conjecture – nowadays known as
Schäffer’s
conjecture
– that
C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)=2
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
The latter was disproved first by E. Gluskin, M. Meyer and A. Pajor

[
4
]
, J. Bourgain
[
4
]

1
1

1

The same article
[
4
]
contains an appendix
with a stronger estimate due to Bourgain.
and later by H. Queffélec
[
12
]
who also proved
that
C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
. The above mentioned results
make use of the following analytic expression for
C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)
,
given in
[
4
]
, in terms of a “max-min-type”
optimization problem :

C_{\mathcal{B}}(n)=\sup_{(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\in\mathbb{D}^{n}}\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}),

where

\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\\
:=\inf\biggl\{\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|:f(z)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k},\,f(\lambda_{j})=0,\,j=1,\ldots,n\biggr\}.

The proof by Queffélec makes use of Bourgain’s lower bound on
\Phi

[
18
, Inequality (2.2)]
,
which he combines with a number theoretic argument to prove the existence
of a family
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
on the circle of
radius
1-1/n
such that
\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
.

The question of finding a concrete exemple of a sequence
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

in
\mathbb{D}^{n}
such that
\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})\gtrsim\sqrt{n}

has been recently addressed by O. Szehr–R. Zarouf
[
18
]
who
proved that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
we have
\Phi(\lambda,\ldots,\lambda)\gtrsim\sqrt{n}
,
where
\lambda
is repeated
n
times.
As a consequence, they construct in
[
18
]
an explicit class
of counterexamples to Schäffer’s conjecture: a sequence of invertible lower triangular
Toeplitz matrices
T_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{M}_{n}
with singleton
spectrum
\{\lambda\}\subset\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
such that

|\lambda|^{n}\|T_{\lambda}^{-1}\|\geq c(\lambda)\sqrt{n}\|T_{\lambda}\|^{n-1},

where
c(\lambda)>0
depends only on
\lambda
.

Furthermore, Gluskin–Meyer–Pajor
[
4
, p. 2, lines 25–26]

mention that it is of interest to find concrete examples
(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})

for which
\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
grows. We will
combine the approach in
[
18
]
with Theorem
1

to prove that given a fixed
m\geq 1
and an arbitrary sequence
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{m}
,
the sequence

(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{n\times m}

(i.e. each
\lambda_{i}
is repeated according to its multiplicity

n
) satisfies the estimate

\Phi(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\gtrsim n^{1/N}

for some integer
N\geq 3
, as
n
tends to infinity. As a consequence, we obtain a family of invertible lower triangular
matrices
T\in\mathcal{M}_{nm}
with arbitrary spectrum
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})

such that

|\det T|\cdot\|T^{-1}\|\gtrsim n^{1/N}\|T\|^{n-1}

for some integer
N\geq 3
, as
n
tends to infinity. Our construction of
T
is given in Section
3.2
.

3.1.
Background

We denote by
H(\mathbb{D})
the space of the functions analytic in the
open unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, and by
H^{\infty}
the Banach algebra of bounded analytic functions
in
\mathbb{D}
, endowed with the supremum norm. We recall that the Hardy space
H^{2}
is defined as the
subspace of
H(\mathbb{D})
consisting of the functions
f
such
that

\|f\|_{H^{2}}^{2}:=\sup_{0\leq r<1}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\left|f(rz)\right|^{2}\,d\nu(z)<\infty,

where
\nu
is the normalized Lebesgue measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\,|z|=1\right\}
.
Let
\sigma
be a finite sequence of points in
\mathbb{D}.
The
finite Blaschke product
B=B_{\sigma}
corresponding to
\sigma

is defined by

B=B_{\sigma}=\prod_{\lambda\in\sigma}b_{\lambda},

where
b_{\lambda}=\frac{z-\lambda}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}
is the
Blaschke factor corresponding to
\lambda\in\mathbb{D}
. Then one
defines the model space
K_{B}
as the finite dimensional subspace
of
H^{2}
given by

K_{B}:=\left(BH^{2}\right)^{\perp}=H^{2}\ominus BH^{2}.

Let
\sigma=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{q})\in\mathbb{D}^{q}
. We set
f_{k}=\dfrac{1}{1-\overline{\lambda_{k}}z}
,
k=1,\ldots,q
.
Observe that
\|f_{k}\|_{H^{2}}=\left(1-|\lambda_{k}|^{2}\right)^{-1/2}
. The family
(f_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}
is a basis of
K_{B}
.
Furthermore, the family
(e_{k})_{1\leq k\leq n}

given by

e_{1}=\frac{f_{1}}{\|f_{1}\|_{H^{2}}}\,\quad\mbox{and}\quad e_{k}=\frac{f_{k}}{\|f_{k}\|_{H^{2}}}{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}}b_{\lambda_{j}},\quad k=2,\ldots,q,

is an orthonormal basis of
K_{B}
(known as the Malmquist–Walsh
basis, see
[
11
]
).

The backward shift operator
S:f\mapsto(f-f(0))/z
acts on
K_{B}
.

3.2.
Constructive counterexamples to Schäffer’s conjecture

A
We put

\sigma=(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{2},\dots,\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{nm},

where distinct
\lambda_{i}
are arbitrary in
\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}
and are repeated
n
times.
We consider the Malmquist–Walsh basis of
K_{B}
given by

e_{sn+t}=\biggl(\prod_{j=1}^{s}b^{n}_{\lambda_{j}}\biggr)\frac{f_{s+1}}{\|f_{s+1}\|_{H^{2}}}b_{\lambda_{s+1}}^{t-1},\qquad 0\leq s<m,\,1\leq t\leq n.

The backward shift operator
S
has a lower triangular matrix with respect to this basis, with diagonal

(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m})
, see
[
15
]
for the entry-wise description of this matrix.
Hence,

(3.2)

\det S=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda^{n}_{j}.

We will use the duality method from
[
18
]
and combine
it with our upper estimates on the Fourier coefficients of the
n^{\text{th}}

power of a finite Blaschke product
B
(see Theorem
1
)
to show that there exists a Banach space norm on
K_{B}

such that the operator
S
with spectrum
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m})
acting
there, satisfies the asymptotic relation

|\det S|\cdot\|S^{-1}\|\cdot\|S\|^{1-n}\gtrsim n^{1/N}
.

Following
[
4
]
, we choose the norm

on

\|f\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}}=\|\widehat{f}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}.

Then
\|S\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}\to\ell^{\infty}_{A}}\leq 1
. Furthermore, if
\dim K_{B}>1
, then
\|S\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}\to\ell^{\infty}_{A}}=1
.
We are now ready to state the corresponding result.

Theorem 3
.

Let

\sigma=(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{m},\ldots,\lambda_{m})\in\mathbb{D}^{nm},

where distinct
\lambda_{i}
are arbitrary in
\mathbb{D}\backslash\{0\}

and are repeated

(i)

|\det S|\cdot\|S^{-1}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}\to\ell^{\infty}_{A}}\gtrsim{n^{1/N}}

(ii)

Here

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

The proof of Theorem
1
makes use of the following
van der Corput lemmata, see, for example,
[
5
, Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2, p. 56]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let
g
be a real function continuously
differentiable on the interval
[a,b]\subset\mathbb{R}
such that

g
and
g^{\prime}
are monotone and
g^{\prime}
does not vanish on
[a,b].

Then

\left|\int_{a}^{b}e^{{\rm i}g(t)}\,dt\right|\leq\frac{2}{|g^{\prime}(a)|}+\frac{2}{|g^{\prime}(b)|}.

Lemma 5
.

Let
F(x)
be a real, twice differentiable function
in
[a,b]
such that
F^{\prime\prime}(x)\geq\mu>0
or
F^{\prime\prime}(x)\leq-\mu<0
. Let

G(x)
be a positive monotonic function in
[a,b]
such that
G(x)\leq M
.
Then

\left|\int_{a}^{b}G(x)e^{{\rm i}F(x)}\,dx\right|\leq\frac{8M}{\sqrt{\mu}}.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

For
j=1,\ldots,m
we write
\lambda_{j}=\rho_{j}\exp({\rm i}\theta_{j})
, where
\rho_{j}\in(0,1)

and
\theta_{j}\in(-\pi,\pi]
. A direct computation shows that

\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\theta)=|B^{\prime}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})|=\Bigl|\frac{B^{\prime}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})}{B(e^{{\rm i}\theta})}\Bigr|=\biggl|\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}\frac{b^{\prime}_{\lambda_{j}}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})}{b_{\lambda_{j}}(e^{{\rm i}\theta})}\biggr|.

Since

\frac{zb^{\prime}_{\lambda}(z)}{b_{\lambda}(z)}=\Re\frac{1+\overline{\lambda}z}{1-\overline{\lambda}z}\geq 0,\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{D},\,z\in\mathbb{T},

we have

(4.1)

\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\theta)=\Re\sum_{1\leq j\leq m}\frac{1+\overline{\lambda_{j}}e^{{\rm i}\theta}}{1-\overline{\lambda_{j}}e^{{\rm i}\theta}}.

Therefore,

\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\theta)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1-\rho_{j}^{2}}{1+\rho_{j}^{2}-2\rho_{j}\cos\left(\theta-\theta_{j}\right)}.

The
k^{th}
-Fourier coefficient of
B^{n}
is given by

\widehat{B^{n}}(k)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left({\rm i}\left(n\psi_{B}(\theta)-k\theta\right)\right)\,d\theta.

We put

f(\theta)=\psi_{B}(\theta)-\frac{k}{n}\theta,

and given
\varepsilon>0
we write (recall that
\xi_{1}>0
)

\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right){\rm d}\theta=\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\\
+\sum_{\ell=1}^{s-1}\int_{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\ell+1}-\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{\xi_{1}-\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\\
+\int_{\xi_{s}+\varepsilon}^{2\pi}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta.

We put

\varepsilon=n^{-1/N}\rightarrow 0,\qquad n\rightarrow\infty.

Clearly

\biggl|\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\biggr|\leq 2\varepsilon,

and therefore the first sum is bounded from above by

\biggl|\sum_{\ell=1}^{s}\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\biggr|\lesssim n^{-1/N}.

Writing the Taylor expansion (of order
N_{\ell}-2
) of
\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}

near
\xi_{\ell}
we obtain that

\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)=\frac{\psi_{B}^{(N_{\ell})}(\xi_{\ell})}{(N_{\ell}-2)!}\left(\theta-\xi_{\ell}\right)^{N_{\ell}-2}\left(1+\mathcal{O}\left(\theta-\xi_{\ell}\right)\right),\qquad\theta\to\xi_{\ell}.

Therefore, if
\theta
is close to
\xi_{\ell}
but satisfies
|\theta-\xi_{\ell}|\geq\varepsilon
,
then

(4.2)

|\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim n^{-(N_{l}-2)/N}.

Since
\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}
does not vanish on the intervals
(0,\xi_{1}-\varepsilon)
,

(\xi_{s}+\varepsilon,2\pi)
and
(\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon,\xi_{\ell+1}-\varepsilon)

for
\ell=1,\ldots,s-1
, we obtain that (
4.2
) holds everywhere
outside of the intervals
[\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon,\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon]
. Now
we can apply Lemma
5
to the remaining integrals

\int_{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon}^{\xi_{\ell+1}-\varepsilon}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta,\qquad\int_{0}^{\xi_{1}-\varepsilon}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta,\qquad\int_{\xi_{s}+\varepsilon}^{2\pi}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta.

Since

|nf^{\prime\prime}|\gtrsim n^{2/N}

on these intervals, these integrals are also
\mathcal{O}\left(n^{-1/N}\right)
.
Thus,

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\lesssim n^{-1/N}.

Let
r\in\{1,\ldots,s\}
be such that
N_{r}=N
and define

\mathcal{L}=\bigl\{\ell:1\leq\ell\leq s,\,N_{\ell}=N,\,\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{\ell})=\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})\bigr\},\quad D=\mathop{\rm Card}\mathcal{L}.

We will show that the maximal value of
|\widehat{B^{n}}(k)|
for
k\geq 0

is (asymptotically as
n
grows large) attained at
k=k_{d}=k_{d}(n)=[n\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})]+d
,
0\leq d<D

(where
\left[A\right]
means the integer part of
A
), that is,

\max_{0\leq d<D}|\widehat{B^{n}}(k_{d}(n))|\asymp n^{-1/N}.

Recall that the
k_{d}^{th}
-Fourier coefficient of
B^{n}
is given
by

\widehat{B^{n}}(k_{d})=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,

where

f_{d}(\theta)=\psi_{B}(\theta)-\frac{k_{d}}{n}\theta.

Let
(\varepsilon_{\ell})_{\ell=1}^{s}
be a sequence of nonnegative numbers.
Each
\varepsilon_{\ell}
for
\ell=1,\ldots,s
will be chosen below over the
proof, depending on the nature of
\xi_{\ell}
.
Let
\tau_{0}=0
,
\tau_{\ell}\in(\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell},\xi_{\ell+1}-\varepsilon_{\ell+1})
,
1\leq\ell\leq s-1
,
\tau_{s}=2\pi
,

J_{\ell}=[\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell},\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}]
,
1\leq\ell\leq s
. Then

[0,2\pi)\setminus\bigsqcup_{1\leq\ell\leq s}J_{\ell}=\bigsqcup_{1\leq\ell\leq s}[\tau_{\ell-1},\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell})\sqcup(\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell},\tau_{\ell})=:\bigsqcup_{1\leq\ell\leq s}(J^{\prime}_{\ell}\sqcup J^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}).

We have

\int_{0}^{2\pi}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta=\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq s}\int_{J_{\ell}}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta+\sum_{1\leq\ell\leq s}\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}\sqcup J^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}}e^{{\rm i}n\,f(\theta)}\,d\theta.

We divide our argument into three steps in order to show that the main
contribution of
\int_{0}^{2\pi}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta

is due to
J_{\ell}
,
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
.

The content of Step
1 is computing an asymptotic formula for
\Sigma=\Sigma_{d}
, the sum of the integrals

I_{\ell,d}=\int_{J_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,\qquad\ell\in\mathcal{L},

with
0\leq d<D
, for suitable choices of
\varepsilon_{\ell}
. In Step 2 we estimate from
above the integrals

\int_{J_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,

for
1\leq\ell\leq s
,
\ell\not\in\mathcal{L}
, and show that they are asymptotically
much smaller than
\Omega=\max_{0\leq d<D}|\Sigma_{d}|
, again for suitable choices of
\varepsilon_{\ell}
.
Finally the goal of Step 3 is to show – in the same spirit as in
Step 2 – that the integrals

\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,\qquad\int_{J^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,

are also asymptotically much smaller than
\Omega
.

Step 1. To make the notation less cluttered we set
\xi=\xi_{\ell}
,
F=F_{d}=nf_{d}
,
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\ell}
, so that

I=I_{\ell}=I_{\ell,d}=\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}F_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta.

Without loss of generality we may assume that

\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)>0.

In the opposite case the argument is analogous.

Furthermore, we fix
\delta\in(0,1/(2N^{2}))
and set

(4.3)

\varepsilon=n^{\delta-\frac{1}{N}}.

This choice of
\varepsilon
is motivated below in the proofs of formulas (
4.4
) and (
4.5
).

We will first establish that:

(a) If
N
is even, then

(4.4)

I_{\ell,d}=\frac{2}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\times\\
\times\exp\left({\rm i}n\psi_{B}(\xi)-{\rm i}k_{d}\xi+\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right).

(b) If
N
is odd, then

(4.5)

I_{\ell,d}=\frac{2}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\times\\
\times\exp\left({\rm i}n\psi_{B}(\xi)-{\rm i}k_{d}\xi\right)\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right).

Here and later on,
\Gamma
is the Gamma function.

Writing the Taylor expansion of
\psi_{B}
near

\xi
we obtain that

\psi_{B}(\theta)=\psi_{B}(\xi)+(\theta-\xi)\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi)\\
+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\psi_{B}^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi)),

for some
t_{\theta}\in(0,1)
.
Therefore,

\psi_{B}(\theta)-\frac{k}{n}\theta=\psi_{B}(\xi)-\frac{k}{n}\xi+(\theta-\xi)\left(\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi)-\frac{k}{n}\right)+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)\\
+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}\psi_{B}^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi)),

and

F(\theta)=F(\xi)+(\theta-\xi)F^{\prime}(\xi)+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\\
+\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}F^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi)).

Thus, going back to the integral
I
we obtain that if
n

is sufficiently large, then

I=\exp\left({\rm i}F(\xi)\right)\cdot\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\right)\times\\
\times\exp\left({\rm i}(\theta-\xi)F^{\prime}(\xi)+{\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}F^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi))\right)\,d\theta.

Furthermore,

\exp\left({\rm i}(\theta-\xi)F^{\prime}(\xi)+{\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}F^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi))\right)\\
=1+\mathcal{O}\left(|(\theta-\xi)F^{\prime}(\xi)|+\frac{|\theta-\xi|^{N+1}}{(N+1)!}|F^{(N+1)}(\xi+t_{\theta}(\theta-\xi))|\right)\\
=1+\mathcal{O}\left(n|\theta-\xi|\left(|f^{\prime}(\xi)|+|\theta-\xi|^{N}\right)\right),

for
\theta
in a small neighborhood of
\xi
. This gives

(4.6)

\exp\left(-{\rm i}F(\xi)\right)I\\
=\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\right)\,d\theta+\mathcal{O}\left(n\varepsilon^{2}|f^{\prime}(\xi)|+n\varepsilon^{N+2}\right).

Let us verify that with our choice of
\varepsilon
we have

(i)

n\varepsilon^{2}|f^{\prime}(\xi)|=o\left(n^{-1/N}\right)
,

(ii)

n\varepsilon^{N+2}=o\left(n^{-1/N}\right)
,

(iii)

n\varepsilon^{N}\rightarrow\infty
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

Since
k\leq n\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi)<k+D
, we have
n|f^{\prime}(\xi)|\lesssim 1
.
Now, (i)–(iii) follow from (
4.3
).

Thus,

(4.7)

n\varepsilon^{2}|f^{\prime}(\xi)|+n\varepsilon^{N+2}=o(n^{-1/N}).

Next we consider

J=\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\right)\,d\theta\\
\\
=\int_{\xi}^{\xi+\varepsilon}\exp\left({\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\right)\,d\theta+\int_{\xi-\varepsilon}^{\xi}\exp\left({\rm i}\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)\right)\,d\theta\\
\\
=J_{1}+J_{2}.

First we estimate
J_{1}
. Changing the variable

u=\frac{(\theta-\xi)^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi),

we obtain that

J_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{F^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\int_{0}^{\frac{\varepsilon^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\,du\\
\\
=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{F^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\,du-\int_{\frac{\varepsilon^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\,du\right)\\
\\
\!=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{F^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\!\!\left(\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{(\varepsilon^{N}F^{(N)}(\xi))^{1-1/N}}\right)\right).

Here we use that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\,du=\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N),

and (via integration by parts, with
\gamma>0
) that

-\int_{\gamma}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\,du=\left[{\rm i}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{1-1/N}}\right]_{\gamma}^{\infty}+\left(1-\frac{1}{N}\right){\rm i}\int_{\gamma}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\left({\rm i}u\right)}{u^{2-1/N}}\,du\\
=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{1-1/N}}\right).

Here
\gamma=\frac{\varepsilon^{N}}{N!}F^{(N)}(\xi)
and thus
\gamma^{(N-1)/N}\asymp n^{\delta(N-1)}
.

This gives

J_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{F^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\left(\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\delta(N-1)}}\right)\right)\\
\\
=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right).

If
N
is even, then
J_{2}=J_{1}
,

J=\frac{2}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\exp\left(\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right),

and asymptotic formula (
4.4
) follows from the above
equality combined with (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).

For odd
N
we obtain in an analogous way that

J_{2}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\exp\left(-\frac{{\rm i}\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right).

Thus, if
N
is odd, then

J=\frac{2}{N}\left(\frac{N!}{n\psi_{B}^{(N)}(\xi)}\right)^{1/N}\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{2N}\right)\Gamma(1/N)+o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right),

and asymptotic formula (
4.5
) follows from the above
equality combined with (
4.6
) and (
4.7
).

By (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) we obtain that

\Sigma=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}I_{\ell,d}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}c_{\ell}\exp(-{\rm i}d\xi_{\ell}),\qquad 0\leq d<D.

The points
\xi_{\ell}
are pairwise disjoint, and hence the square matrix

\bigl(e^{-{\rm i}d\xi_{\ell}}\bigr)_{\ell\in\mathcal{L},\,0\leq d<D}
is invertible. Since it does not depend on
n
,
we conclude that

\Omega=\max_{0\leq d<D}\Bigl|\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}I_{\ell,d}\Bigr|\gtrsim\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}|c_{\ell}|\asymp n^{-1/N}.

Step 2. Now we deal with
the
s-D
integrals

\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell}}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta,

1\leq\ell\leq s
,
N_{\ell}\neq N
or
\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\xi_{\ell})\neq\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\xi_{r})
, and choose
\varepsilon_{\ell}
in such a way that the corresponding integrals are
o\left(\frac{1}{n^{1/N}}\right)
. We distinguish
the following two cases.

(1) If
N_{\ell}=N
, then the integral

\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell}}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f(\theta)\right)\,d\theta

is treated as follows. By continuity of
\psi_{B}^{\prime}
at the point
\xi_{\ell}

there exists
\eta_{\ell}>0
(independent of
n
) such that

|\theta-\xi_{\ell}|\leq\eta_{\ell}\implies|\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{\ell})-\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\theta)|\leq|\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{\ell})-\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})|/2.

We choose
\varepsilon_{\ell}=\eta_{\ell}
and observe that

n|f^{\prime}_{d}(\theta)|\geq n|\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{\ell})-\psi_{B}^{\prime}(\xi_{r})|/2-D\gtrsim n,\qquad|\theta-\xi_{\ell}|\leq\varepsilon_{\ell},\,0\leq d<D.

Moreover,
f=f_{d}
is monotonic over the interval
[\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell},\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}]

and
f^{\prime}
is monotonic over
[\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell},\xi_{\ell}]
and

[\xi_{\ell},\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}]
(because
f^{\prime\prime}=\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}
vanishes
at
\xi_{\ell}
and nowhere else on the interval
[\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell},\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}]
).
The assumptions of Lemma
4
are therefore satisfied
and an application of this lemma gives:

\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell}}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta=\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{1}{n}\right).

(2) If
N_{\ell}<N
, then the situation is even simpler: we set
\varepsilon_{\ell}=n^{-1/N_{\ell}}
,
and estimate directly

\biggl|\int_{\xi_{\ell}-\varepsilon_{\ell}}^{\xi_{\ell}+\varepsilon_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\biggr|\lesssim n^{-1/N_{\ell}}.

Step 3. We need to verify that

\biggl|\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\biggr|+\biggl|\int_{J^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta\biggr|\\
=o(n^{-1/N}),\quad 1\leq\ell\leq s,\,0\leq d<D.

Given
J^{\prime}_{\ell}
(or
J^{\prime\prime}_{\ell}
with an analogous argument), we consider the following cases.

(i)
\ell\in\mathcal{L}
. Then
\varepsilon_{\ell}=n^{\delta-(1/N)}
, and considering the Taylor expansion (of order
N-2
) of
\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}

near
\xi_{\ell}
, we obtain for large
n
and for
\theta
close to
\xi_{\ell}
satisfying
|\theta-\xi_{\ell}|\geq\varepsilon_{\ell}
that

|\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim\varepsilon_{\ell}^{N-2}=n^{\delta(N-2)-(N-2)/N}.

Since
\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}
does not vanish on the
interval
J^{\prime}_{\ell}
, we conclude that

n|f_{d}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim n^{(2/N)+\delta(N-2)},\qquad\theta\in J^{\prime}_{\ell},\,0\leq d<D.

An application of Lemma
5
gives

\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta=o(n^{-1/N}).

(ii)
\ell\not\in\mathcal{L}
and
N_{\ell}=N
. Since
\varepsilon_{\ell}
does not depend on
n
, we have

|\psi_{B}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim 1,\qquad\theta\in J^{\prime}_{\ell},

and

n|f_{d}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim n,\qquad\theta\in J^{\prime}_{\ell},\,0\leq d<D.

Therefore, by Lemma
5
, we have

\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{-1/2}\right).

(iii)
N_{\ell}<N
. Here
\varepsilon_{\ell}=n^{-1/N_{\ell}}
, and, arguing as above, we conclude that

n|f_{d}^{\prime\prime}(\theta)|\gtrsim n^{2/N_{\ell}},\qquad\theta\in J^{\prime}_{\ell},\,0\leq d<D,

and

\int_{J^{\prime}_{\ell}}\exp\left({\rm i}n\,f_{d}(\theta)\right)\,d\theta=\mathcal{O}\left(n^{-1/N_{\ell}}\right).

Summing up, Steps 1–3 give us that

\max_{0\leq d<D}|\widehat{B^{n}}(k_{d})|\gtrsim n^{-1/N},

which completes the proof.
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
2

5.1.
The general case
N\geq 3

Proof of Theorem
2
, formula (
2.1
).

Let
N\geq 3
and let
B=\prod_{1\leq j\leq N}b_{\lambda_{j}}
be a finite Blaschke product.
The argument

\psi_{B}(\theta)
of
B(e^{{\rm i}\theta})
is determined modulo
2\pi
. Furthermore,

\psi^{\prime}_{B}
is a real analytic
2\pi
-periodic function,
and by (
4.1
), we have

\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\theta)=\Re\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{1+\overline{\lambda_{j}}e^{{\rm i}\theta}}{1-\overline{\lambda_{j}}e^{{\rm i}\theta}}.

Since
\psi^{\prime}_{B}
is real analytic,
the function
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}
has only finite number of zeros on
[0,2\pi]
.
By Theorem
1
, to obtain that

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\asymp n^{-1/N},

it suffices to verify that
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}(0)=\ldots=\psi_{B}^{(N-1)}(0)=0
,

\psi_{B}^{(N)}(0)\not=0
, and
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}
has no zeros of order
N-1
.

Set
u=\exp(\frac{2\pi{\rm i}}{N})
. For
t\in(0,1/(2\pi))

to be chosen later on, we set
\zeta=t\exp(\frac{\pi{\rm i}(N-1)}{2N})
and

\lambda_{j}=\frac{\overline{\zeta u^{j}}}{1+\overline{\zeta u^{j}}}\in\mathbb{D},\qquad 1\leq j\leq N.

Then

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime}_{B}(\theta)

\displaystyle=-N+2\Re\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{1}{1-\overline{\lambda_{j}}e^{{\rm i}\theta}}

\displaystyle=-N+2\Re\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}\frac{1+\zeta u^{j}}{1-\zeta u^{j}(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)}

\displaystyle=-N+2\Re\sum_{s\geq 0}\sum_{1\leq j\leq N}(1+\zeta u^{j})\zeta^{s}u^{js}(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{s}

\displaystyle=N+2N\Re\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta^{kN}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{kN-1}

\displaystyle=N+2N\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{kN}\Re\Bigl({\rm i}^{k(N-1)}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{kN-1}\Bigr).

Furthermore,

\displaystyle\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}(\theta)

\displaystyle=2N\Re\sum_{k\geq 1}\zeta^{kN}{\rm i}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(kNe^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{kN-2}

\displaystyle=2N\sum_{k\geq 1}t^{kN}\Re\Bigl({\rm i}^{k(N-1)+1}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(kNe^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{kN-2}\Bigr).

It is clear that (independently of
t
) we have
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}(0)=\ldots=\psi_{B}^{(N-1)}(0)=0
.
Since
{\rm i}^{2N-2}\in\mathbb{R}
, for some
c=c(t)\not=0
we have
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}(\theta)\sim c\theta^{N-2}

at
0
and, hence,
\psi_{B}^{(N)}(0)\not=0
.

Set

\displaystyle h(\theta)

\displaystyle={\rm i}^{N}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(Ne^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{N-2}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{N-1}e^{{\rm i}\theta(N+2)/2}(N-e^{-{\rm i}\theta})\cdot 2^{N-2}(\sin(\theta/2))^{N-2}.

Then
h(0)=0
if and only if
\theta\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, and

\frac{h^{\prime}(\theta)}{h(\theta)}=\frac{N+2}{2}{\rm i}+\frac{{\rm i}e^{-{\rm i}\theta}}{N-e^{-{\rm i}\theta}}+\frac{N-2}{2}\cdot\frac{\cos(\theta/2)}{\sin(\theta/2)}.

Hence,

\Im\frac{h^{\prime}(\theta)}{h(\theta)}\geq\frac{N+2}{2}-\frac{1}{N-1}>0,\qquad 0<|\theta|\leq\pi.

Thus, at every point
\theta\in]0,2\pi[
we have

|\Re h(\theta)|+|\Re h^{\prime}(\theta)|>0.

Since
\Re h^{(N-2)}(0)=(-1)^{N-1}(N-1)!\not=0
, we have

\sum_{s=0}^{N-2}|\Re h^{(s)}(\theta)|>0,\qquad|\theta|\leq\pi.

By continuity, we can find
\delta>0
such that

\sum_{s=0}^{N-2}|\Re h^{(s)}(\theta)|\geq\delta,\qquad|\theta|\leq\pi.

Now we use that

\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}(\theta)=2Nt^{N}\Re h(\theta)\\
+2Nt^{2N}\sum_{k\geq 2}t^{(k-2)N}\Re\Bigl({\rm i}^{N}e^{{\rm i}\theta}(kNe^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)(e^{{\rm i}\theta}-1)^{kN-2}\Bigr).

Therefore, we can fix a small positive
t
,
t<1/(2\pi)
, such that

\sum_{s=2}^{N}|\psi_{B}^{(s)}(\theta)|\geq\delta Nt^{N},\qquad|\theta|\leq\pi.

Now,
\psi^{\prime\prime}_{B}
has no zeros of
order
N-1
.
Thus, by Theorem
1
, for every
N\geq 3
, we have constructed a Blaschke product

B_{N}
of order
N
such that
\|\widehat{B_{N}^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\asymp n^{-1/N}
.
∎

5.2.
The case
N=5

In this section we give two explicit examples of finite Blaschke
product
B
of degree 2 satisfying the estimate

\|\widehat{B^{n}}\|_{\ell^{\infty}}\asymp n^{-1/5}.

In the first example, the zeros of
B
are of the same modulus, while in the second example they are on the same diameter of the unit disk.

5.2.1.
Example 1

Let
w\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{1\}
be such that
\Re w>0
,

(5.1)

\Re(w)=\Re(w^{3})\not=\Re(w^{5})

and

(5.2)

\Re(w^{-1})\not=\Re(w^{-3}).

Set
w_{1}=w
,
w_{2}=\overline{w}
,

\lambda_{j}=\frac{w_{j}-1}{w_{j}+1},\qquad j=1,2,

and consider

B=\prod_{1\leq j\leq 2}b_{\lambda_{j}}.

For example, we can choose

h(\lambda_{j})=h^{\prime}(\lambda_{j})=\ldots=h^{(n-1)}(\lambda_{j})=0,\qquad j=1,\dots,m,

h/B^{n}\in W
.
With this observation we rewrite the Gluskin–Meyer–Pajor expression
for
\Phi

(6.5)

\Phi(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m})\\
=\inf\biggl\{\|h\|_{W}-|h(0)|:h\in B^{n}W,\,h(0)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}^{n}\biggr\}.

Let

L^{2}(\partial\mathbb{D})
be the usual
L^{2}
space on the unit circle

\partial\mathbb{D}
, equipped with the standard scalar product

\left\langle f,g\right\rangle:=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}f(e^{{\rm i}\varphi})\overline{g(e^{{\rm i}\varphi})}\,\frac{d\varphi}{2\pi}.

For
f=\sum_{k}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}
,
g=\sum_{k}\hat{g}(k)z^{k}\in H^{2}
,
it is well known
[
9
]
that the
L^{2}(\partial\mathbb{D})

scalar product can be written as

\left\langle f,\,g\right\rangle=\sum_{j\geq 0}\hat{f}(j)\overline{\hat{g}(j)}.

Let
h=B^{n}g
with
g\in W
and
h(0)=\prod_{j=1}^{m}\lambda_{j}^{n}
.
Then

\|h\|_{W}\cdot\|B^{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}}\geq\bigl|\bigl\langle h,B^{n}\bigr\rangle\bigr|=\bigl|\bigl\langle g,1\bigr\rangle\bigr|=|g(0)|=\Bigl|\frac{h(0)}{B^{n}(0)}\Bigr|=1.

It follows that any candidate function
h
in (
6.5
)
satisfies

\|h\|_{W}\geq\frac{1}{\|B^{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}}},

and consequently

\Phi\left(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m}\right)\geq\frac{1}{\|B^{n}\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{A}}}-\prod_{j=1}^{m}|\lambda_{j}|^{n}.

It remains to apply Theorem
1
to obtain that

\Phi(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m},\dots,\lambda_{m})\gtrsim n^{1/N},\qquad n\to\infty.

The Burgers equation driven by a stochastic measure


Abstract
We study the class of one-dimensional equations driven by a stochastic measure μ \mu . For μ \mu we assume only σ \sigma -additivity in probability. This class of equations include the Burgers equation and the heat equation. The existence and uniqueness of the solution are proved, and the averaging principle for the equation is studied.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider the stochastic equation, which can formally be written as

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+f(t,x,u(t,x))+\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}(t,x,u(t,x))+\sigma(t,x)\frac{\partial\mu}{\partial x},\quad u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)

(1)

where
(t,x)\in[0,\ T]\times\mathbb{R}
, and
\mu
is a stochastic measure defined on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
(Borel

\sigma
-algebra in
\mathbb{R}
). For
\mu
we assume only
\sigma-
additivity in probability, assumptions for
f,\ g,\ \sigma

and
u_{0}
are given in Section
3

1
) in the mild
form (see (
5
) below).

If
f=0
and
g(x,t,v)=v^{2}/2
then (
1
) is the Burgers equation, for
g={\rm const}
we get the heat equation.

In the paper, we prove the existence and uniqueness of the solution and obtain its
{\sf L}^{2}
-continuity. In addition, we obtain that the averaging principle holds for equation (
1
).

Equation (
1
) driven by the Wiener process was studied in
[
7

for

[

[

Stochastic equations driven by stochastic measures were studied, for example, in
[

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we give basic information concerning stochastic measures in a general setting. In equation (
1
),
\mu
is defined on Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
.

Let
{\sf L}_{0}={\sf L}_{0}(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{{\sf{P}}})
be the set of all real-valued
random variables defined on the complete probability space
(\Omega,{\mathcal{F}},{{\sf{P}}})
. Convergence in
{\sf L}_{0}
means the convergence in probability. Let
{{\sf X}}
be an arbitrary set and
{\mathcal{B}}
a
\sigma
-algebra of subsets of
{{\sf X}}
.

Definition 1
.

A
\sigma
-additive mapping
\mu:\ {\mathcal{B}}\to{\sf L}_{0}
is called
stochastic measure
(SM).

We do not assume the moment existence or martingale properties for SM. In other words,
\mu
is
{\sf L}_{0}
–valued measure.

Important examples of SMs are orthogonal stochastic measures,
\alpha
-stable random measures defined on a
\sigma
-algebra for
\alpha\in(0,1)\cup(1,2]
(see
[
21
, Chapter 3]
). Theorem 8.3.1 of
[
10
]
states the conditions under which the increments
of a real-valued process with independent increments generate an SM.

Many examples of the SMs on the Borel subsets of
[0,T]
may be given by the Wiener-type integral

\mu(A)=\int_{[0,T]}{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(t)\,{d}X_{t}.

(2)

We note the following cases of processes
X_{t}
in (
2
) that generate SM.

1.

X_{t}
– any square integrable martingale.

2.

X_{t}=W_{t}^{H}
– the fractional Brownian motion with Hurst index
H>1/2
, see Theorem 1.1
[
15
]
.

3.

X_{t}=S_{t}^{k}
– the sub-fractional Brownian motion for
k=H-1/2,\ 1/2<H<1
, see Theorem 3.2 (ii) and Remark 3.3 c) in
[
23
]
.

4.

X_{t}=Z_{H}^{k}(t)
– the Hermite process,
1/2<H<1
,
k\geq 1
, see
[
12
]
,
[
24
, Section 3.1.3]
.
Z_{H}^{2}(t)
is known as the Rosenblatt process, see
[
22
, Section 3]
.

The following analogue of the Nikodym theorem is satisfied for SMs.

Theorem 1
.

(
[
5
, Theorem 8.6]
)
Let
\mu_{n}
are SMs on
{\mathcal{B}}
,
n\geq 1
, and

\forall\,{A}\in{\mathcal{B}}\ \exists\ \mu({A})={\rm p}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu_{n}({A}).

Then
\mu
is a SM on
{\mathcal{B}}
.

Applying Theorem
1
, we can construct the following example of the SM on all Borel subsets of
\mathbb{R}
:

\mu(A)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(t)\xi(t)\,{d}W_{t}:=\lim_{T\to\infty}\int_{[0,T]}{\mathbf{1}}_{A}(t)\xi(t)\,{d}W_{t},

(3)

where
W_{t}
is the Wiener process,
\xi(t)
is an adapted process such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\sf E}\xi(t)^{2}\,dt<\infty
, the limit is taken in
{\sf L}^{2}(\Omega)
.

For deterministic measurable functions
g:{\sf X}\to\mathbb{R}
, an integral of the form
\int_{{\sf X}}g\,d\mu
is studied
in
[
20
, Chapter 1]
(see also
[
10
, Chapter 7]
). In particular, every bounded measurable
g
is integrable w. r. t. any SM
\mu
.

To estimate the stochastic integral, we will use the following statement.

Lemma 1
.

(Lemma 3.1
[
17
]
, Lemma 2.3
[
20
]
)
Let
f_{l}:\ {{\sf X}}\to\mathbb{R},\ l\geq 1
, be measurable functions such that

\bar{f}(x)=\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}|{f_{l}}(x)|
is integrable w.r.t.
\mu
. Then

\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}\Bigl(\int_{{\sf X}}f_{l}\,d\mu\Bigr)^{2}<\infty\quad\mbox{{\penalty\ }a.\penalty\ s.}

We consider the Besov spaces
B^{\alpha}_{22}([c,d])
. Recall that the norm in this classical space for

0<\alpha<1
may be introduced by

\|g\|_{B^{\alpha}_{22}([c,d])}=\|g\|_{{\sf L}_{2}([c,d])}+\Bigl(\int_{0}^{d-c}\ {(w_{2}(g,r))^{2}}{r^{-2\alpha-1}}\,dr\Bigr)^{1/2},

(4)

where

w_{2}(g,r)=\sup_{0\leq h\leq r}\Bigl(\int_{c}^{d-h}|g(y+h)-g(y)|^{2}\,dy\Bigr)^{1/2}.

By
C
and
C(\omega)
we will denote positive constants and positive random constants respectively whose values may change.

3
The problem

We consider (
1
) in the mild form

\begin{split}u(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x-y)u_{0}(y)\,dy+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p(t-s,x-y)f(s,y,u(s,y))\,dy\,ds\\
-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}(t-s,x-y)g(s,y,u(s,y))\,dy\,ds+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y),\end{split}

(5)

where equality holds a. s. for each
t\in[0,T]
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
u\in\mathbb{C}([0,T],{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Here

{p}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi t}}\,e^{-\frac{x^{2}}{4t}}

is the heat kernel, and
\mu
is an SM on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
.

We will refer to the following assumptions on elements of (
5
).

Assumption A 1
.

u_{0}(y)=u_{0}(y,\omega):{\mathbb{R}}\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable and
u_{0}(y)=u_{0}(y,\omega)\in{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for each fixed
\omega
.

Assumption A 2
.

f(s,y,r):[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Borel function satisfying the linear growth and Lipschitz condition:

\displaystyle|f(s,y,r)|\leq a_{1}(y)+K|r|,

\displaystyle|f(s,y,r_{1})-f(s,y,r_{2})|\leq(a_{2}(y)+L|r_{1}|+L|r_{2}|)|r_{1}-r_{2}|

for all
s\in[0,T]
,
y,r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and for some constants
K,L
and non-negative functions
a_{i}\in{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Assumption A 3
.

The function
g
is of the form

g(s,y,r)=g_{1}(s,y,r)+g_{2}(s,r),

where

g_{1}(s,y,r):[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to\mathbb{R},\quad g_{2}(s,r):[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}

are Borel functions satisfying the following linear and quadratic growth conditions:

\displaystyle|g_{1}(s,y,r)|\leq b_{1}(y)+b_{2}(y)|r|,

\displaystyle|g_{2}(s,r)|\leq K|r|^{2},

for all
s\in[0,T]
,
y,r\in\mathbb{R}
, where
K
is a constant, and

b_{1}\in{\sf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\quad b_{2}\in{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})\cap{\sf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})

are nonnegative functions. Moreover,
g
satisfies the following Lipschitz condition:

|g(s,y,r_{1})-g(s,y,r_{2})|\leq(b_{3}(y)+L|r_{1}|+L|r_{2}|)|r_{1}-r_{2}|

for all
s\in[0,T]
,
y,r_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
and for some constant
L
and non-negative function
b_{3}\in{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Assumption A 4
.

\sigma(s,y):[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}\to\mathbb{R}
is measurable, and

|\sigma(s,y)|\leq C_{\sigma},\quad|\sigma(s,y_{1})-\sigma(s,y_{2})|\leq L_{\sigma}|y_{1}-y_{2}|^{\beta(\sigma)}

for
1/2<\beta(\sigma)<1
and some constants
C_{\sigma},\ L_{\sigma}
.

Assumption A 5
.

y^{\tau}
is integrable w. r. t.
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}
for some
\tau>1/2
.

For example, Assumption A
5
holds for SM defined in (
3
) and given
\tau
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}}t^{2\tau}{\sf E}\xi(t)^{2}\,dt<\infty.

If A
4
and A
5
hold, then, by Theorem of
[
1
]
(or Theorem 3.1
[
20
]
), the random function

\vartheta(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y)

has a version
\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,x)
such that for any fixed

\delta\in(0,T),\quad M>0,\quad 0<\gamma_{1}<1/2,\quad 0<\gamma_{2}<1/4

there exists
L_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\omega)
such that

\begin{split}|\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{1},x_{1})-\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{2},x_{2})|\leq L_{\widetilde{\vartheta}}(\omega)(|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\gamma_{2}}+|x_{1}-x_{2}|^{\gamma_{1}}),\\
t_{i}\in[\delta,T],\quad|x_{i}|\leq M,\quad i=1,\ 2.\end{split}

(6)

In the sequel, we will use that

\Bigl|\frac{\partial p}{\partial y}(t-s,x-y)\Bigr|\leq\frac{C_{\lambda}}{t-s}e^{-\frac{\lambda(x-y)^{2}}{t-s}}

(7)

for any
\lambda\in(0,1/4)
and some constant
C_{\lambda}
.

4
Regularity property of the stochastic integral

In this section, we obtain some properties of
\widetilde{\vartheta}
.

For any
j\in\mathbb{R}
and all
n\geq 0
, put

d_{kn}^{(j)}=j+k2^{-n},\quad 0\leq k\leq 2^{n},\quad\Delta_{kn}^{(j)}=(d_{(k-1)n}^{(j)},d_{kn}^{(j)}],\quad 1\leq k\leq 2^{n}\,.

A following estimate is a key tool for the estimates of the stochastic integral.

Lemma 2
.

(Lemma 3.2
[
17
]
)
Let
\mu
be defined on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R})
,
Z
be an arbitrary set, and
q(z,x):Z\times[j,j+1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a function such that for some
1/2<\alpha<1
and for each
z\in Z

q(z,\cdot)\in B^{\alpha}_{22}\left([j,j+1]\right)
. Then the random function

\eta(z)=\int_{[j,j+1]}q(z,x)\,d\mu(x),\quad z\in Z,

has a version
\tilde{\eta}(z)
such that for some constant
C
(independent of
z,\ j,\ \omega
) and each

\omega\in\Omega
,

\begin{split}|\tilde{\eta}(z)|\leq|q(z,j)\mu([j,j+1])|+C\|q(z)\|_{B^{\alpha}_{22}([j,j+1])}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{n(1-2\alpha)}\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2^{n}}|\mu(\Delta_{kn}^{(j)})|^{2}\Bigr\}^{1/2}.\end{split}

(8)

Further, we study the properties of stochastic integral
\vartheta

Lemma 3
.

Let Assumptions A
4
and A
5
hold. Then for version
\widetilde{\vartheta}
that satisfies (
6

\widetilde{\vartheta}\in\mathbb{C}([0,T],{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}))

Proof.

To prove the continuity in
{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, for fixed
\omega\in\Omega
and arbitrary
t_{n}\to t_{0}
, consider

\begin{split}\|\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{n})-\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{0})\|_{{\sf L}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{n},x)-\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{0},x)|^{2}\,dx.\end{split}

By (
6
),
\widetilde{\vartheta}
is continuous in both variables, therefore
\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{n},x)\to\widetilde{\vartheta}(t_{0},x)
for each
x
.

We will find
g\in{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that

|\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,x)|=\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y)\Bigr|\leq Cg(x),

(9)

then the dominated convergence theorem implies our statement.

We apply Lemma
2
for

q(z,y)=\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds,\quad z=(t,x)

Our considerations will imply that the function
q(z,y)
is continuous in
y
(see (
14
) and (
15
) below), thus the conditions of Lemma
2
are satisfied.

We fix
\omega\in\Omega
,
\theta>1
and estimate

\begin{split}|\widetilde{\vartheta}(t,x)|^{2}=\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y)\Bigr|^{2}\\
=\Bigl|\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{(j,j+1]}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y)\Bigr|^{2}\\
\leq\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(|j|+1)^{-\theta}\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(|j|+1)^{\theta}\Bigl|\int_{(j,j+1]}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\,d\mu(y)\Bigr|^{2}\\
=C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(|j|+1)^{\theta}\Bigl|\int_{(j,j+1]}q(t,x,y)\,d\mu(y)\Bigr|^{2}\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:esqm}}}{{\leq}}C\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}(|j|+1)^{\theta}\Bigl(|q(t,x,j)\mu((j,j+1])|^{2}+\|q(t)\|^{2}_{B^{2,2}_{\alpha}([j,j+1])}\Bigl\{\sum_{n\geq 1}2^{-n(2\alpha-1)}\sum_{1\leq k\leq 2^{n}}|\mu(\Delta_{kn}^{(j)})|^{2}\Bigr\}\Bigr).\end{split}

(10)

Below we will use the following simple estimates:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{r}e^{-\frac{b}{r}}dr\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{b}/{r}=z}}{{=}}\int_{b/t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{z}e^{-z}\,dz\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{b\geq t\}}\int_{b/t}^{\infty}e^{-z}\,dz+\mathbf{1}_{\{b<t\}}\Bigl(\int_{b/t}^{1}\frac{1}{z}\,dz+\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-z}\,dz\Bigr)\\
\leq\mathbf{1}_{\{b\geq t\}}e^{-{b/t}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{b<t\}}\Bigl(\ln{\frac{t}{b}}+1\Bigr),\end{split}

(11)

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}e^{-\frac{b}{t-s}}ds\leq\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t-s}}e^{-\frac{b}{T}}ds\leq Ce^{-\frac{b}{T}}.\end{split}

(12)

We have that

\begin{split}|q(t,x,y)|=\Bigl|\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\sigma(s,y)\,ds\Bigr|\leq C_{\sigma}C\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\sqrt{t-s}}\,ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estbss}}}{{\leq}}Ce^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4T}}.\end{split}

(13)

To estimate
\|q(t)\|_{B^{2,2}_{\alpha}([j,j+1])}
, we consider

\begin{split}q(t,x,y+h)-q(t,x,y)=\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)(\sigma(s,y+h)-\sigma(s,y))\,ds\\
+\int_{0}^{t}(p(t-s,x-y-h)-p(t-s,x-y))\sigma(s,y+h)\,ds:=I_{1}+I_{2}.\end{split}

For
I_{1}
, we obtain

\begin{split}|I_{1}|\leq L_{\sigma}h^{\beta(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{t}p(t-s,x-y)\,ds=Ch^{\beta(\sigma)}\int_{0}^{t}\dfrac{e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4(t-s)}}}{\sqrt{t-s}}\,ds\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estbss}}}{{\leq}}Ch^{\beta(\sigma)}e^{-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{4T}}.\end{split}

(14)

For

\begin{split}|I_{2}|\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm A\ref{assxs}}}{{\leq}}C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}|p(t-s,x-y-h)-p(t-s,x-y)|\,ds\\
=C_{\sigma}\int_{0}^{t}\Bigl|\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}\dfrac{\partial p(t-s,v)}{\partial v}\,dv\Bigr|\,ds\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estpx}}}{{\leq}}C_{\sigma}C_{\lambda}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}e^{-\frac{\lambda v^{2}}{t-s}}\,dv\,ds\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:estb}}}{{\leq}}C\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}e^{-\frac{\lambda v^{2}}{t}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda v^{2}\geq t\}}\,dv+C\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}\Bigl(\Bigl|\ln{\frac{T}{\lambda v^{2}}}\Bigr|+1\Bigr)\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda v^{2}<t\}}dv\\
{\leq}C\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}e^{-\frac{\lambda v^{2}}{t}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda v^{2}\geq t\}}\,dv+C\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}|\ln{|v|}|\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda v^{2}<t\}}dv\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{\leq}}C\int_{x-y-h}^{x-y}e^{-\frac{\lambda v^{2}}{t}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\lambda v^{2}\geq t\}}\,dv+C\mathbf{1}_{\{|x-y|<\sqrt{t/\lambda}+1\}}\int_{0}^{h/2}|\ln z|\,dz\\
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(**)}}{{\leq}}Che^{-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}}{T}}+C\mathbf{1}_{\{|x-y|<\sqrt{t/\lambda}+1\}}(z-z\ln z)\Big|_{0}^{h/2}\\
\leq Che^{-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}}{T}}+Ch|\ln h|\mathbf{1}_{\{|x-y|<\sqrt{t/\lambda}+1\}}\\
\leq Che^{-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}}{T}}+Ch^{\beta(\sigma)}\mathbf{1}_{\{|x-y|<\sqrt{T/\lambda}+1\}}\leq Ch^{\beta(\sigma)}e^{-\frac{\tilde{\lambda}(x-y)^{2}}{T}}.\end{split}

(15)

Step 1 (existence and uniqueness of the solution).

\begin{split}\|p(t-s,x-y)\|^{2}_{{\sf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}=C(t-s)^{-1/2},\\

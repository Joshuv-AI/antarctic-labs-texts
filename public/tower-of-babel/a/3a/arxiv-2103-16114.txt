Variational approach to the existence of solutions for non-instantaneous impulsive differential equations with perturbation Journal: arXiv
Wangjin Yao, Liping Dong, Jing Zeng

Abstract
In this paper, we study the existence of solutions for second-order non-instantaneous impulsive differential equations with a perturbation term. By variational approach, we obtain the problem has at least one solution under assumptions that the nonlinearities are super-quadratic at infinity, and sub-quadratic at the origin.

\displaystyle I(u)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i})u(t_{i})

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}p(t)(u(t)-u(t_{i+1}))dt-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}F_{i}(t,u(t)-u(t_{i+1}))dt,

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|u\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i}|-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}M_{i}(t)dt-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\sqrt{t_{i+1}-s_{i}}\|p\|_{L^{2}}.

By
(H3)
,
I(\frac{1}{2\beta})>0
and satisfies the condition (1) in Theorem
2.2
. Let
\xi>0
and
w\in H_{0}^{1}(0,T)
with
\|w\|=1
. We can see that
w(t)
is not a constant for
a.e.~[s_{i},t_{i+1}]
. By Lemma
2.3
,

\begin{split}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}F_{i}(t,(w(t)-w(t_{i+1}))\xi)dt\geq&\left(\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}m_{i}(t)|w(t)-w(t_{i+1})|^{\mu_{i}}dt\right)\xi^{\mu_{i}}\\
&-\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}b_{i}(t)dt.\end{split}

Let
W_{i}:=\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}m_{i}(t)|w(t)-w(t_{i+1})|^{\mu_{i}}dt
, then

0\leq W_{i}\leq(2\beta)^{\mu_{i}}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}m_{i}(t)dt,~W_{0}\geq 0.

We can select the interval
[0,t_{1}]
and prove
w(t)
is not a constant for
a.e.~[0,t_{1}]
. In fact, we suppose that
\int_{0}^{t_{1}}m_{0}(t)|w(t)-w(t_{1})|^{\mu_{0}}dt=0
. Since
m_{0}(t)
is positive, then
w(t)=w(t_{1})
for
a.e.~[0,t_{1}]
. A
contradiction with the assumption on
w
.

By (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle I(\xi w)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}w^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}(\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i})w(t_{i})\xi-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}p(t)(w(t)-w(t_{i+1})\xi)dt

\displaystyle-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}F_{i}(t,(w(t)-w(t_{i+1}))\xi)dt,

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\xi^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}|\alpha_{i-1}-\alpha_{i}|\beta\xi+2\beta\xi\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\sqrt{t_{i+1}-s_{i}}\|p\|_{L^{2}}-\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}W_{i}\xi^{\mu_{i}}

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}\int_{s_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}b_{i}(t)dt.

Since
\mu_{i}>2
, the above inequation implies that
I(\xi w)\rightarrow-\infty
as
\xi\rightarrow\infty
, that is, there exists a
\xi\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
such that
\|\xi w\|>\frac{1}{2\beta}
and
I(\xi w)\leq 0
. The proof of Theorem
1.2
is completed.
\Box

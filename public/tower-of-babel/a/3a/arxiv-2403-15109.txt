Natural density of rank- 2 2 Drinfeld modules with big Galois image
Chien-Hua Chen

Abstract
In this paper, we compute the natural density of rank- 1 1 Drinfeld module over 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[T] with surjective adelic Galois representation; and the natural density of rank- 2 2 Drinfeld modules over 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] \mathbb{F}_{q}[T] whose 𝔩 \mathfrak{l} -adic Galois image containing the special linear subgroup for finitely many prime ideal 𝔩 \mathfrak{l} .



1
Introduction

In the groundbreaking work
[
Jon10
]
, Jones investigated the natural density of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
with adelic Galois image that are index-
2
subgroups of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\hat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Zywina
[
Zyw10
]
later extended this result to include elliptic curves over arbitrary number fields. It turns out that the natural density of elliptic curves over a number field with maximal adelic Galois image is equal to one.

Expanding upon this line of research,
[
LSTX19
]
generalized the natural density problem to encompass Abelian varieties with “big monodromy”. The techniques in these results all involved in classification of maximal subgroups in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
( or
{\rm GSP}_{2n}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell)
for abelian varieties), Serre’s large sieve method, and Messer-Wüstholz bound for irreducibility of mod-
\ell
Galois representations.

In the function field analogy, Pink and Rütsche
[
PR09
]
proved the open image theorem for Drinfeld modules of arbitrary rank without complex multiplication. And
[
Zyw11a
]
Zywina proved the existence of rank-
2
Drinfeld modules with surjective adelic Galois representation. The result is later on generalized by the author to Drinfeld modules of rank
3
case
[
Che22
]
and prime rank case
[
Che21
]
. Therefore, it is reasonable to ask the natural density of Drinfeld modules of prime rank with surjective adelic Galois representation. Due to Van der Heiden’s result
[
vdH04
]
on determinant of adelic Galois representation for Drinfeld modules, the natural density estimation can be split into two cases:

Case 1:

Natural density of rank-
1
Drinfeld modules with surjective adelic Galois representation.

Case 2:

Natural density of rank-
r
Drinfeld modules whose adelic Galois image containing
\mathrm{SL}_{r}(\widehat{\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]})
.

In this paper, we compute and give a brief estimation for case 1.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
3.3
& Corollary
3.5
)
.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
||\cdot||
to be the absolute value
|\cdot|_{\infty}
of
F_{\infty}
. Define

B_{1}(x)=\{\Delta\in A\mid\Delta\neq 0,||\Delta||<x\}.

The upper natural density of rank-
1
Drinfeld modules with surjective adelic Galois representation is equal to

\begin{array}[]{lll}1-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}\\
\\
\\
=1-\frac{\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,t\mid q-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}\left(q^{ti+1}\cdot\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor\right)+\left(q\cdot 1\right)\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{q-1}\rfloor}\left(q^{(q-1)i+1}\right)}{\#B_{1}(x)}\\
\\
\\
\leqslant 1-\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,{t\mid q-1}}\frac{q-1}{(q^{t}-1)\cdot t}\end{array}

The computation is based on Gekeler’s
[
Gek16
]
characterization of adelic Galois image for rank-
1
Drinfeld modules, which he called twisted Carlitz modules.

On the other hand, for case 2 we only compute the natural density of rank-
2
Drinfeld modules with
\mathfrak{l}
-adic Galois image containing
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]_{\mathfrak{l}})
for finitely many prime ideal
\mathfrak{l}
.

Theorem 2

(Corollary
4.11
)
.

Let
q\geqslant 4
be a prime power, and
S
be a finite set of prime ideals of
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
. Then we have

\displaystyle L_{2}^{S,low}

\displaystyle:=\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in B_{2}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{(g_{1},g_{2})},\mathfrak{l}}\supset{\rm SL}_{2}(A_{\mathfrak{l}})\textrm{ for }\mathfrak{l}\in S\}}{\#B_{2}(x)}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1+\sum_{i=1}^{|S|}(-1)^{i}\cdot\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{l}_{1},\cdots,\mathfrak{l}_{i}\in S\textrm{ distinct }}\frac{1}{q^{\deg_{T}\mathfrak{l}_{1}+\cdots+\mathfrak{l}_{i}}}\right).

The reason why we can only estimate the natural density for finitely many
\mathfrak{l}
-adic Galois representations is because of the followings:

First, the technical difficulty on the criterion to determine whether a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/\mathfrak{l})
contains
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/\mathfrak{l})
, one can compare Proposition
4.2
with the elliptic curve situation (
[
Zyw10
]
, Lemma A.8). Second, there is no function field analogue of Messer-Wüstholz bound for surjective mod
\mathfrak{l}
Galois representations (see
[
Zyw11a
]
, Theorem 5.8 for the elliptic curve case). Currently, in
[
Che23
]
we have derived Drinfeld module analogue of Messer-Wüstholz bound for irreducible mod
\mathfrak{l}
Galois representations, but there is no such a bound for a Drinfeld module
\phi
whose mod
\mathfrak{l}
Galois image lies in a normalizer of Cartan subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/\mathfrak{l})
. The current existed bound needs to assume that
\phi

\mathfrak{l}
, see
[
CL19
]
Theorem 2.1 (2).

Concluding the introduction, we mention that Ray
[
Ray23
]
formulated and proved a lower bound for natural density of rank-
2
Drinfeld modules with surjective
(T)
-adic Galois representation. He found directly that a special type of rank-2 Drinfeld module satisfies Pink’s criteria (
[
PR09
]

[

(T)

>q^{-7}
).

Apart from his result, we give a stronger estimation (see Corollary

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in B_{2}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{(g_{1},g_{2})},(T)}={\rm GL}_{2}(A_{(T)})\}}{\#B_{2}(x)}\geqslant(q-1)\cdot(\frac{1}{q}-\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,{t\mid q-1}}\frac{1}{(q^{t}-1)}).

2.2
Drinfeld modules and Galois representations

Let

be the polynomial ring over finite field with

be the fractional field of

, and

be a finite extension over

. Set
F\{\tau\}
to be the twisted polynomial ring with usual addition rule, and the multiplication rule is defined to be
\tau\alpha=\alpha^{q}\tau\text{ for any }\alpha\in K
.

Definition 2.1
.

A Drinfeld
A
-module of rank
r
over
F
is a ring homomorphism

\phi:A\rightarrow F\{\tau\}

such that

(i)

\phi(a):=\phi_{a}
satisfies
{\rm deg}_{\tau}\phi_{a}=r\cdot{\rm deg}_{T}a

(ii)

Denote
\partial:F\{\tau\}\rightarrow F
by
\partial(\sum a_{i}\tau^{i})=a_{0}
, then
\phi
satisfies
\gamma=\partial\circ\phi
.

From the definition of Drinfeld
A
-module, we can characterize a Drinfeld module
\phi
by writing down

\phi_{T}=T+g_{1}\tau+\cdots+g_{r-1}\tau^{r-1}+g_{r}\tau^{r},\textrm{ where }g_{i}\in F\textrm{ and }g_{r}\in F^{*}.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\phi
and
\psi
be two rank-
r
Drinfeld
A
-modules over
K
. A
morphism

u:\phi\rightarrow\psi
over
K
is a twisted polynomial
u\in K\{\tau\}
such that

u\phi_{a}=\psi_{a}u\text{ \rm for all }a\in A.

A non-zero morphism
u:\phi\rightarrow\psi
is called an isogeny. A morphism
u:\phi\rightarrow\psi
is called an
isomorphism
if its inverse exists.

Definition 2.3
.

(i)

Let
\phi
be a Drinfeld
A
-module over
K
. The Drinfeld module
\phi
gives
K^{{\rm{alg}}}
an
A
-module structure, where
a\in A
acts on
K^{{\rm{alg}}}
via
\phi_{a}
.

(ii)

Let
\mathfrak{a}
be an ideal of
A
with monic generator
a
.The
\mathfrak{a}
-torsion of
\phi
is defined to be the set

\phi[\mathfrak{a}]=\phi[a]:=\{{\rm{zeros}}\ {\rm{of}}\ \phi_{a}(x)=\gamma(a)x+\sum^{r\cdot{\rm{deg}}(a)}_{i=1}g_{i}(a)x^{q^{i}}\}\subseteq K^{alg}.

The action
b\cdot\alpha=\phi_{b}(\alpha)\ \forall\ b\in A,\forall\alpha\in\phi[\mathfrak{a}]
also gives
\phi[\mathfrak{a}]
an
A
-module structure.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
\phi
be a rank

Drinfeld module over

\mathfrak{a}

,
, then the

∎

Let
\phi
be a rank
r
Drinfeld module over
F
of generic characteristic, then

.
This implies that

Theorem 3.1

(
[
Gek16
]
, Corollary 3.14)
.

Let
\phi^{\Delta}
be the rank-
1
Drinfeld
A
-module determined by

\phi^{\Delta}_{T}=T+\Delta\tau,

where
\Delta\in A-\{0\}
. Then the image of adelic Galois representation of
\phi^{\Delta}
is a subgroup of
\hat{A}^{*}
of index

{\rm def}(\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}})=g.c.d.(d-1,q-1,k_{0}^{*}).

Here we write
\Delta=c^{k_{0}}P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{s}^{e_{s}}
to be the prime factorization of
\Delta
in
A
, and
c
is a generator of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. The notation
d:=\deg_{T}\Delta
, and

k_{0}^{*}=\begin{cases}k_{0},&\text{if $q$ or $d=\deg\Delta$ is even}\\
\\
\text{the unique $k\equiv k_{0}+(q-1)/2\mod q-1$ with $0\leqslant k<q-1$},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

Now we define the natural density of rank-
1
Drinfeld module with surjective adelic representation:

Definition 3.2
.

1.

For each
x\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, let
||\cdot||
to be the absolute value
|\cdot|_{\infty}
of
F_{\infty}
. Define

B_{1}(x)=\{\Delta\in A\mid\Delta\neq 0,||\Delta||<x\}.

2.

The natural density (if exists) of rank-
1
Drinfeld module with surjective adelic representation is defined to be

L_{1}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}=\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}.

Theorem 3.3
.

The number of
\Delta\in B_{1}(x)
with
{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}
is equal to

\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,t\mid q-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}\left(q^{ti+1}\cdot\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor\right)+\left(q\cdot 1\right)\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{q-1}\rfloor}\left(q^{(q-1)i+1}\right)

Proof.

From Theorem
3.1
, those
\Delta\in A
such that
{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}
have the following property:
\deg_{T}\Delta-1
,
q-1
and
k_{0}^{*}
have a nontrivial common divisor. Thus we know that the possible common divisor can only be those
2\leqslant t\leqslant q-1
with
t\mid q-1
. Now we count the number of
\Delta\in B_{1}(x)
with
{\rm def}(\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}})=g.c.d.(d-1,q-1,k_{0}^{*})=t.

We write
\Delta=c^{k_{0}}P_{1}^{e_{1}}\cdots P_{s}^{e_{s}}
. In the first summation, for each
2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,\textrm{ and }t\mid q-1
, we consider
1\leqslant i\leqslant\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor
. The number
q^{ti+1}
represents the number of monic
\Delta\in B_{1}(x)
whose degree is equal to
ti+1
, and the summation

\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor

represents the choices of
c^{k_{0}}
such that
k_{0}^{*}
is a multiple of
t
but
k_{0}^{*}/t
is coprime to
i
. The term
(q\cdot 1)
comes from the extreme case when
\Delta
has degree equal to
1
, it forces
k_{0}^{*}
to be equal to
t
.

In the second sum, it presents the case when
t=q-1
, so we must have
k_{0}^{*}=0
, and the degree of
\Delta
must be a multiple of
q-1
.

Hence the result follows

∎

Remark 3.4
.

One can check that, as a function of
x
, the summation

\frac{\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,t\mid q-1}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}\left(q^{ti+1}\cdot\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor\right)+\left(q\cdot 1\right)\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{q-1}\rfloor}\left(q^{(q-1)i+1}\right)}{\#B_{1}(x)}

is monotone decreasing and lower bounded by zero. Hence
\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}
exists, and we get

L_{1}=1-\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}.

Corollary 3.5
.

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}\geqslant\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,\\
{t\mid q-1}}\frac{q-1}{(q^{t}-1)\cdot t}

Proof.

The inequality is obtained by direct computation and the fact that

q-1\geqslant\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor\geqslant\frac{q-1}{t}.

Therefore, on the right inequality we have

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{\Delta\in B_{1}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\rho_{\phi^{\Delta}}\neq\hat{A}^{*}\}}{\#B_{1}(x)}\\
\\
\\
\geqslant\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,t\mid q-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}\left(q^{ti+1}\cdot\sum_{j\mid i}\mu(j)\lfloor\frac{q-1}{tj}\rfloor\right)}{\#B_{1}(x)}\\
\\
\\
\geqslant\sum_{2\leqslant t\leqslant q-2,{t\mid q-1}}\frac{q-1}{t}\cdot\left(\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}q^{ti+1}}{q^{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor}-1}\right).\end{array}

Now we compute the limit

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{i=0}^{\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}q^{ti+1}}{q^{\lfloor x\rfloor}-1}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q\cdot(q^{t\cdot\lfloor\frac{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor-1}{t}\rfloor}-1)}{(q^{t}-1)(q^{\lfloor{\rm log}_{q}x\rfloor}-1)}=\frac{1}{q^{t}-1},

hence the inequality in the statement follows.

∎

\widehat{L}_{2,\mathfrak{l}}=\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\#\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in B_{2}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\bar{\rho}_{\phi^{(g_{1},g_{2})},\mathfrak{l}}\not\supset{\rm SL}_{2}(A/\mathfrak{l})\textrm{ for }\mathfrak{l}\in S\}}{\#B_{2}(x)}.

In section the following section, we will show that
\widehat{L}_{2,\mathfrak{l}}
exists and is equal to
0
.
From Proposition
4.2
, we have

\widehat{L}_{2,\mathfrak{l}}\leqslant\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\frac{\#\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in B_{2}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\bar{\rho}_{\phi^{(g_{1},g_{2})}}\cap C=\emptyset\}}{\#B_{2}(x)}

4.2
Large sieve inequality

In this subsection, we apply function field analogue of large sieve method to estimate the number
\#Y_{C}(x)=\#\{(g_{1},g_{2})\in B_{2}(x)\mid{\rm Im}\bar{\rho}_{\phi^{(g_{1},g_{2})}}\cap C=\emptyset\}
when
x
is large.

Theorem 4.6

(
[
Hsu96
]
, Theorem 3.2)
.

Let
K
be a positive integer. For each monic irreducible polynomial
\mathbf{p}\in A
with
\deg\mathbf{p}\geqslant 1
, let
a_{\mathbf{p}}
be a real number such that
0<a_{\mathbf{p}}\leqslant 1
. Set
X\subset A^{2}
to be a subset satisfies the following property:

\#X_{\mathbf{p}}\leqslant a_{\mathbf{p}}\cdot q^{2\deg_{T}\mathbf{p}}=a_{\mathbf{p}}\cdot\#({A^{2}/\mathbf{p}\cdot A^{2}}),

where
X_{\mathbf{p}}\subset A^{2}/\mathbf{p}A^{2}
denotes the canonical image of
X
in the quotient group
A^{2}/\mathbf{p}A^{2}
.

Then

\#(X\cap B_{2}(x))\cdot L(K)\leqslant q^{2(m_{0}+1)},

where

\begin{array}[]{lll}L(K)=1+\sum_{M\in MS,1\leqslant\deg_{T}M\leqslant K}\prod_{\mathbf{p}\in MI,\mathbf{p}\mid M}\left(\frac{1-a_{\mathbf{p}}}{a_{\mathbf{p}}}\right),\\
\\
MS=\textrm{ all monic square-free polynomials in }\mathbb{F}_{q}[T],\\
\\
MI=\textrm{ all monic irreducible polynomials with positive degree in}A,\\
\\
m_{0}={\rm sup}\{{\rm log}\ x,2K-1\}\end{array}

In order to apply the large sieve method, set
K=\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x
and
X\cap B_{2}(x)=Y_{C}(x)
. Let
\sum{(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}=\{\mathbf{p}\neq\mathfrak{l}\textrm{ prime ideal of }A\mid\deg_{T}\mathbf{p}\leqslant\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x\}
. For
\mathbf{p}\not\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)
, define
\alpha_{\mathbf{p}}=0
. And for
\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)
, define

\begin{array}[]{lll}\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C}=\{(\bar{g}_{1},\bar{g}_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}^{2}\mid\bar{g}_{2}\neq 0,\bar{\rho}_{\phi^{(\bar{g}_{1},\bar{g}_{2})},\mathfrak{l}}({\rm Frob}_{\mathbf{p}})\in C\}\\
\\
\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}=\frac{\#\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C}}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}}\end{array}.

Now we examine that
Y_{C}(x)
satisfies the assumption of the above theorem. Let
Y_{{\bf{p}},C}
be its canonical image modulo
\mathbf{p}
, then

Y_{{\bf{p}},C}\subset\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}^{2}-\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C}\Rightarrow\#Y_{{\bf{p}},C}\leqslant(1-\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C})|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}.

Therefore, Theorem
4.6
implies

Y_{C}(x)\cdot L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)\leqslant q^{2({\rm log}x+1)},

and
L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)=1+\sum_{M\in MS,1\leqslant\deg_{T}M\leqslant\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x}\prod_{\mathbf{p}\in MI,\mathbf{p}\mid M}\left(\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}}{1-\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}}\right)
.

Note that

L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)\geqslant\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}}{1-\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}}\geqslant\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}.

The next step is to estimate the number

\alpha_{\mathbf{p},C}=\frac{\#\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C}}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}}.

By Corollary of Proposition 7 in
[
Yu95
]
, we have the following property:

Proposition 4.7
.

The number of isomorphism classes of rank-
2
Drinfeld module
\phi\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}
such that
\bar{\rho}_{\phi\otimes\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}},\mathfrak{l}}({\rm Frob}_{\mathbf{p}})\in C
is equal to
h(\mathcal{O})=\#{\rm Pic}(\mathcal{O})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is an imaginary quadratic order over
A
depending only on
C
.

As a corollary, we immediately have

\#\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C}=\frac{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}^{*}|}{|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}h(\mathcal{O})

Theorem 4.8
.

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}Y_{C}(x)}{B_{2}(x)}=0.

Proof.

Since
L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)\geqslant\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\alpha_{\mathbf{p}}
and
\#\Omega_{\mathbf{p},C_{i}}=\frac{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}^{*}|}{|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}h(\mathcal{O})
, we have

L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)\geqslant\frac{h(\mathcal{O})}{|\mathcal{O^{*}}|}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}^{*}|}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|^{2}}\geqslant\frac{h(\mathcal{O})}{2|\mathcal{O^{*}}|}\cdot\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|}.

Therefore, we get

\frac{1}{L(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\leqslant\frac{2|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}{h(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|}}.

This implies

Y_{C}(x)\leqslant\frac{x^{2}q^{2}\cdot 2|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}{h(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|}}.

On the other hand,
\#B_{2}(x)=(q^{{\rm log}x})^{2}=x^{2}
. Thus

\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}Y_{C}(x)}{B_{2}(x)}\leqslant\sum_{C\in\mathcal{C}}\lim_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{q^{2}\cdot 2|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}{h(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{\mathbf{p}\in\sum(\frac{1}{2}{\rm log}x)}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|}}=\frac{q^{2}\cdot 2|\mathcal{O}^{*}|}{h(\mathcal{O})}\cdot\frac{1}{\sum_{\mathbf{p}\neq\mathfrak{l}\textrm{ prime of }A}\frac{1}{|\mathbb{F}_{\mathbf{p}}|}}=0

∎

As a conclusion, we have

\widehat{L}_{2,\mathfrak{l}}=0.

Zeros of S S -characters
Gunter Malle

Abstract
. The concept of S S -characters of finite groups was introduced by Zhmud as a generalisation of transitive permutation characters. Any non-trivial S S -character takes a zero value on some group element. By a deep result depending on the classification of finite simple groups a non-trivial transitive permutation character even vanishes on some element of prime power order. We present an example that this does not generalise to S S -characters, thereby answering a question posed by J.-P. Serre.

The concept of
S
-characters of finite groups was introduced by Zhmud
[
7
]

as a far reaching generalisation of, for example, transitive permutation
characters. More precisely let
G
be a finite group. Then an

S
-character of
G
is a virtual character
\psi
of
G
(i.e., an
integral linear combination of irreducible complex characters of
G
) such that

(1)

\psi
contains the trivial character
1_{G}
exactly once, and

(2)

all values
\psi(g)
,
g\in G
, are non-negative real numbers.

Examples of
S
-characters are, as mentioned before, the characters of transitive
permutation representations of
G
which obviously satisfy the above conditions,
but also
\psi=\chi\overline{\chi}
for
\chi\in{\operatorname{Irr}}(G)
, where
\overline{\chi}

denotes the complex conjugate character of
\chi
.

Any
S
-character
\psi\neq 1_{G}
vanishes on some group element
[
7
, Thm 1]
;
this can be seen by an easy adaptation of Burnside’s proof of the corresponding
statement for non-linear irreducible characters.

It was shown by Fein, Kantor and Schacher in a much cited paper
[
2
]
,
using the classification of finite simple groups, that non-trivial transitive
permutation characters even vanish on some element of prime power order. (See
also Giudici
[
3
]
for a more complete proof of a stronger statement).
Similarly, in
[
4
]
we showed, building upon
[
2
]
, that any non-linear
irreducible character
\chi
of a finite group (and hence any
S
-character

\chi\overline{\chi}
) vanishes on some element of prime power order. This
naturally leads to the question, posed by J.-P. Serre
[
5
]
, whether the
conclusion continues to hold for
S
-characters.

This is not the case, indicating that the conclusions of
[
2
]
and

[
4
]
might be rather subtle:

Proposition
.

The sporadic simple Janko group
J_{1}
has a non-trivial ordinary

S
-character which does not vanish on any element of prime power order.

Here, an
S
-character is called
ordinary
if it is an actual character.

[
6
]
to
run through the rationalised character

S

irreducible character at most three times. The first (and only) example that

. Write
{\operatorname{Irr}}(J_{1})=\{\chi_{1},\ldots,\chi_{15}\}

(\chi_{i}(1))=(1,56,56,76,76,77,77,77,120,120,120,133,133,133,209).

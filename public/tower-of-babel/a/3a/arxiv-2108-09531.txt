Sefika Kuzgun, David Nualart

Abstract
. In this paper, we consider the one-dimensional stochastic heat equation driven by a space time white noise. In two different scenarios: (i) initial condition u 0 = 1 u_{0}=1 and general nonlinear coefficient σ \sigma and (ii) : initial condition u 0 = δ 0 u_{0}=\delta_{0} and σ ⁡ ( x ) = x \sigma(x)=x (Parabolic Anderson Model), we establish rates of convergence for the uniform distance between the density of (renormalized) spatial averages and the standard normal density. These results are based on the combination of Stein method for normal approximations and Malliavin calculus techniques. A key ingredient in Case (i) is a new estimate on the L p L^{p} -norm of the second Malliavin derivative. Mathematics Subject Classifications (2020) : 60H15, 60H07. Keywords and Phrases : Stochastic heat equation. Malliavin calculus. Stein’s method.



1.
Introduction

Consider the one-dimensional stochastic heat equation

(1.1)

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+\sigma(u)\dot{W},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\,\,t>0,

with initial condition
u(0,x)=u_{0}(x)
, where
\dot{W}
is a space-time white noise. This is to say, informally,
that
\dot{W}=\{\dot{W}(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\}
is a centered Gaussian random field with covariance

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left(\dot{W}(t_{1},x_{1})\dot{W}(t_{2},x_{2})\right)=\delta_{0}(t_{1}-t_{2})\delta_{0}(x_{1}-x_{2}),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac delta measure at zero.

The existence and uniqueness of a mild solution
u(t,x)
to equation (
1.1
) has been proved by Chen and Dalang in
[
3
]
(see also the lecture notes by Walsh
[
15
]
in the case where
u_{0}
is a bounded function), assuming that
\sigma
is Lipschitz and
u_{0}
is a signed measure that satisfies the following integrability condition for any
t>0

(1.2)

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{0}|(dx)p_{t}(x)<\infty.

Here and along the paper we will make use of the notation
p_{t}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-x^{2}/2t}
for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

We are interested in the asymptotic behavior of the spatial averages of the solution to equation (
1.1
) in the following two particular cases:

Case 1:

u_{0}\equiv 1
and
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz function such that
\sigma(1)\not=0
.

Case 2:

u_{0}=\delta_{0}
and
\sigma(x)=x
.

We observe that, for any
t>0
, in Case 1, the process
\{u(t,x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
is stationary and in Case 2, the process
\{U(t,x):x\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where

\displaystyle U(t,x):=\frac{u(t,x)}{p_{t}(x)},

is also stationary (see Amir, Corwin and Quastel
[
1
]
).

Fix
R>0
and consider the corresponding centered and normalized spatial averages defined by

(1.3)

\displaystyle F_{R,t}:=\frac{1}{\sigma_{R,t}}\left(\int_{-R}^{R}u(t,x)dx-2R\right),\text{ where }\sigma^{2}_{R,t}:={\mathrm{Var}}\left(\int_{-R}^{R}u(t,x)dx\right)

in Case 1, and

(1.4)

\displaystyle G_{R,t}:=\frac{1}{\Sigma_{R,t}}\left(\int_{-R}^{R}U(t,x)dx-2R\right),\text{ where }\Sigma^{2}_{R,t}:={\mathrm{Var}}\left(\int_{-R}^{R}U(t,x)dx\right)

in Case 2.

Huang, Nualart and Viitasaari
[
10
]
and Chen, Khoshnevisan, Nualart and Pu
[
7
]
, studied the limiting behavior of
F_{R,t}
and
G_{R,t}
, respectively, as
R
tends to infinity. In these papers, functional central limit theorems have been established. Moreover, by the Malliavin-Stein approach, introduced by Nourdin and Peccati (see
[
12
]
), upper bounds for the total variation distance have been obtained. More precisely, it has been proven that, for any fixed
t>0
and for all
R\geq 1
,

(1.5)

d_{\text{TV}}\left(F_{R,t}\,,N(0,1)\right)\leq\frac{C_{t}}{\sqrt{R}},

and

(1.6)

d_{\text{TV}}\left(G_{R,t}\,,N(0,1)\right)\leq\frac{C_{t}\sqrt{\log R}}{\sqrt{R}},

where
d_{\text{TV}}
denotes the total variation distance and
C_{t}
is a constant depending on
t
.

The purpose of this paper is to derive upper bounds for the rate of convergence of the uniform distance of densities in the two cases above mentioned. Upper bounds for the uniform distance of densities using techniques of Malliavin calculus were first derived by Hu, Lu and Nualart in
[
9
]
. Inspired by the methodology introduced in this reference, we have been able to obtain the following two main results. In Case 1, we will make use of the following hypothesis on
\sigma
:

(H1)
:

\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is a twice continuously differentiable function with
\sigma^{\prime}
bounded and
|\sigma^{\prime\prime}(x)|\leq C(1+|x|^{m})
, for some
m>0
.

Theorem 1.1
.

In Case 1, let
u=\left\{u(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right\}
be the mild solution to the stochastic heat equation (
1.1
). Assume that
\sigma
satisfies hypothesis
(H1)
. Suppose also that for some
q>10
,
\mathrm{E}\left[|\sigma(u(t,0))|^{-q}\right]<\infty
.
Fix
t>0
and
let
F_{R,t}
be defined as in (
1.3
). Then, for all
R>0
,

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_{F_{R,t}}(x)-\phi(x)|\leq\frac{C_{t}}{\sqrt{R}},

where
f_{F_{R,t}}
and
\phi
are the densities of
F_{R,t}
and
N(0,1)
, respectively.

We remark that condition
\mathrm{E}\left[|\sigma(u(t,0))|^{-q}\right]<\infty
holds if
\sigma
is bounded away from zero or if
|\sigma(x)|\leq\Lambda|x|
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
and for some constant
\Lambda>0
(see, for instance,
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

In Case 2, assume that the random field
u=\left\{u(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right\}
solves the stochastic heat equation

\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x^{2}}+u\dot{W},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R},\,\,t>0,

with the initial condition
u_{0}(x)=\delta_{0}
. Let
G_{R,t}
be defined as in (
1.4
). Fix
\gamma>\frac{19}{2}
.
Then, there exists an
R_{0}\geq 1
such that for all
R\geq R_{0}

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_{G_{R,t}}(x)-\phi(x)|\leq\frac{C_{t}(\log R)^{\gamma}}{\sqrt{R}},

where
f_{G_{R,t}}
and
\phi
are the densities of
G_{R,t}
and
N(0,1)
, respectively.

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section
2
we introduce some preliminaries on the definition of mild solution and we recall some basic elements of Malliavin calculus. In Section

. Theorem 6.2
in

can be considered as a particular case of Theorem

when

, where

and Section

.
Finally, the Appendix contains some technical lemmas used along the paper.

We remark that a fundamental ingredient in the proof of the upper bounds (

) is the estimate of the

-norm of the Malliavin derivative of the solution. More precisely, the upper bounds

,

inequality (

) in

, we need similar estimates but for the

-norm of the second Malliavin derivative. If
\sigma(x)=x
an upper bound for

\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u(t,x)\|_{p}
has been obtained by Chen, Khoshnevisan, Nualart and Pu
[
6

when
u_{0}=1
and by Kuzgun and Nualart in
[

2.
Preliminaries

We first introduce the white noise on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
and denote by
\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right)
the collection of Borel sets
A\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
with finite Lebesgue measure, denoted by
m(A)
. Consider a family of centered Gaussian random variables
\left\{W(A):A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}\left(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right)\right\}
, defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathrm{P})
, with covariance

\mathrm{E}\left[W(A)W(B)\right]=m(A\cap B)\text{ for all }A,B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}).

The Wiener integrals

W(h):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}h(s,y)W(ds,dy),\quad h\in\mathcal{H},

define an
isonormal Gaussian process
on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.

For
t>0
, let
\mathcal{F}_{t}
denote the
\sigma
-field generated by
\left\{W([0,s]\times A):0\leq s\leq t,\ A\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R})\right\}
and the
P
-null sets.
A random field
X=\left\{X(s,y):(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right\}
is said to be
adapted
if
X(s,y)
is
\mathcal{F}_{s}
-measurable for each
(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.
For any adapted random field
X
, that is also jointly measurable and square integrable, i.e.

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}\left[X^{2}(s,y)\right]dyds<\infty,

the stochastic integral

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}X(s,y)W(ds,dy)

is well defined (see
[
15
]
).

The following proposition (see
[
3
,
15
]
) ensures the existence and uniqueness of a
mild solution
to equation (
1.1
) in Case 1 and Case 2.

Proposition 2.1
.

In Case 1, there exists a unique measurable and adapted random field
u=\left\{u(t,x):(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}\right\}
such that for all
T>0
and
p\geq 2

(2.1)

\sup_{(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}\left[|u(t,x)|^{p}\right]=C_{T,p}\,,

and for all
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

(2.2)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)\sigma(u(s,y))W(ds,dy).

In Case 2, there exists a unique measurable and adapted random field
u=\left\{u(t,x):(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\right\}
such that for all
T>0
,
t\in(0,T]
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\geq 2

(2.3)

\mathrm{E}\left[|u(t,x)|^{p}\right]\leq C_{T,p}p_{t}(x)\,,

and for all
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}

(2.4)

\displaystyle u(t,x)=p_{t}(x)+\int_{[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)W(ds,dy).

2.1.
Malliavin Calculus

We recall some basic facts from Malliavin calculus. For a broader exposition, we refer to
[
13
]
.
We recall that
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R})
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the collection of smooth and cylindrical random variables of the form

(2.5)

F=f\left(W(h_{1}),\dots,W(h_{n})\right),

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, that is,
f
is infinitely differentiable and all its partial derivatives are bounded, and
h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\in\mathcal{H}
.
For a random variable
F\in\mathcal{S}
of the form (
2.5
), we define its Malliavin derivative by putting

D_{t,x}F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}(W(h_{1}),\cdots,W(h_{n}))h_{i}(t,x)

for
t\geq 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
.
For any real number
p\geq 1
and any integer
k\geq 1
, let
\mathbb{D}^{k,p}
be the closure of
S
with respect to the norm

\|F\|_{k,p}=\left(\mathrm{E}\left[\left|F\right|^{p}\right]+\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mathrm{E}\left[\left\|D^{j}F\right\|^{p}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes j}}\right]\right)^{1/p},

where
D^{j}
denotes the
j
th iterated derivative. If
V
is a real separable Hilbert space, similarly we can introduce the corresponding Sobolev spaces

\mathbb{D}^{k,p}(V)
of
V
-valued random variables.

The divergence operator
\delta
is defined as the adjoint of the derivative operator
D
as an unbounded operator from
L^{1}(\Omega)
to
L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Namely, an element
v\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
belongs to the domain of
\delta
, denoted by
{\mathrm{Dom}}\,\delta
, if there exists an integrable random variable
\delta(v)
verifying

\mathrm{E}\left[F\delta(v)\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[\langle DF,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right]\,,

for any
F\in\mathcal{S}
. A crucial property of divergence is the fact that
any adapted and square integrable process
v
belongs to
{\mathrm{Dom}}\,\delta
and
\delta(v)
coincides with the stochastic integral:

\delta(v)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}v(s,y)W(ds,dy).

The following lemma is a factorization property of the divergence operator, obtained in this generality in
[
2
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Fix
p,p^{\prime}>1
with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
. Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p^{\prime}}
,
v\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
, be such that
v\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}))
and
\delta(v)\in L^{p}(\Omega)
. Then
Fv\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
, and

\delta(Fv)=F\delta(v)-\langle DF,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

Because
\delta
is a continuous linear operator from
D^{1,p}(\mathcal{H})
to
L^{p}(\Omega)
, Lemma (
2.2
) holds true provided
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p^{\prime}}
and

v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(\mathcal{H})
.

The operators
D
and
\delta
satisfy the following commutation relation

(2.6)

\displaystyle D_{s,y}(\delta(v))=v(s,y)+\delta(D_{s,y}v),\quad

for almost all
(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
, provided

v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
is such that for almost all
(s,y)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
,

D_{s,y}v
belongs to the domain of the divergence in
L^{2}

and
\mathrm{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}}|\delta(D_{s,y}v)|^{2}dsdy\right]<\infty
(see
[
13
, Proposition 1.3.2]
).

2.2.
Notation

Recall that
p_{t}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}e^{-x^{2}/2t}
for
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
. We will make use of the following identity. For
0<s<t
,
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

(2.7)

\displaystyle p_{t-s}(a)p_{s}(b)=p_{t}(a+b)p_{\frac{s(t-s)}{t}}(b-\frac{s}{t}(a+b)).

For a Lipschitz function
f
, let

\textrm{Lip}_{f}:=\sup_{x\neq y}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|}<\infty
. We set

(2.8)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{t,x}:=\sigma(u(t,x)),\quad\Sigma^{(1)}_{t,x}:=\sigma^{\prime}(u(t,x)),\quad\Sigma^{(2)}_{t,x}:=\sigma^{\prime\prime}(u(t,x)).

Set
Q_{R}:=[-R,R]
, and define for
0<s<t
and
y\in\mathbb{R}

(2.9)

\displaystyle\phi_{R,t}(s,y):=\frac{1}{\sigma_{R,t}}\int_{Q_{R}}{p_{t-s}(x-y)}dx,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,

and

(2.10)

\displaystyle\varphi_{R,t}(s,y):=\frac{1}{\Sigma_{R,t}}\int_{Q_{R}}{p_{s(t-s)/t}(y-\frac{s}{t}x)}dx.

For an
\mathcal{H}
-valued random variable
v:\Omega\to\mathcal{H}
and
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
, we put

D_{v}F:=\left\langle DF,v\right\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}.

3.
Existence and estimates of densities

In this section, we recall the basic density formula for one-dimensional random variables, and state and prove an estimate on the uniform distance between the density of a random variable and the standard normal density, see Theorem
3.2
. The proof of this theorem follows the lines of the proof Theorem 6.2 of
[
9
]
. For the sake of completeness, we include here some details of the proof. These results will be later applied in the context of spatial averages of the stochastic heat equation.

The results of this section are valid in the framework of a general isonormal Gaussian process
W=\{W(h):h\in\mathcal{H}\}
on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
The following density formula under general assumptions on the random variable has been proved in
[
2
, Proposition 1]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F\in\mathbb{D}^{1,1}
and
v\in L^{1}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
be such that
D_{v}F\neq 0
a.s. Assume that
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
. Then the law of
F
has a continuous and bounded density given by

f_{F}(x)=\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}\delta\left(\dfrac{v}{D_{v}F}\right)\right].

Using Lemma
2.2
, in the context of Proposition
3.1
, the following constitute sufficient conditions for
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
, for some
p,p^{\prime}
with
1/p+1/p^{\prime}=1
(see
[
2
, Lemma 3]
):

(i)

\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-1}\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p^{\prime}}
.

(ii)

v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,p}(\mathcal{H})
.

In view of
[
2
, Lemma 4]
, a sufficient condition for (i) is
\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-1}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega)
and

\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-2}\left[\|D^{2}F\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|Dv\|_{\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathcal{H}}\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}\right]\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega).

Therefore, assuming that
F\in\mathbb{D}^{2,p}
and
(D_{v}F)^{-1}\in L^{q}(\Omega)
, then condition (i) holds if
2/q+3/p=1
for some
p>3
and
q>2
.
In particular, we can take
q=4
and
p=6
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Assume that
v\in\mathbb{D}^{1,6}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
and
F=\delta(v)\in\mathbb{D}^{2,6}
with
\mathrm{E}\left[F\right]=0
,
\mathrm{E}\left[F^{2}\right]=1
and
\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-1}\in L^{4}(\Omega)
. Then,
v/D_{v}F\in{\rm Dom}\,\delta
,
F
admits a density
f_{F}(x)
and the following inequality holds true

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_{F}(x)-\phi(x)|\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left\|{F}\right\|_{4}\left\|(D_{v}F)^{-1}\right\|_{4}+2\right)\left\|1-D_{v}F\right\|_{2}

(3.1)

\displaystyle+\left\|(D_{v}F)^{-1}\right\|_{4}^{2}\left\|D_{v}\left({D_{v}F}\right)\right\|_{2},

where
\phi(x)
is the density of the law
N(0,1)
.

Proof.

First, note that, by Proposition
3.1
,
F
admits a density
f_{F}(x)=\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}\delta\left(\bar{v}\right)\right]
, where
\bar{v}=v/D_{v}F
.
As a consequence, we can write

(3.2)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|f_{F}(x)-\phi(x)|=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}\delta\left(\bar{v}\right)\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{N>x\}}N\right]\right|,

where
N
denotes a
N(0,1)
random variable.
We have

(3.3)

\delta(\bar{v})=\delta\left(\frac{v}{D_{v}F}\right)=\frac{F}{D_{v}F}-D_{v}\left(\frac{1}{D_{v}F}\right)=\frac{F}{D_{v}F}+\frac{D_{v}\left(D_{v}F\right)}{\left(D_{v}F\right)^{2}}.

Indeed, the second equality follows from Lemma
2.2
together with
F=\delta(v)
, and the third one follows from the chain rule. Then,
substituting (
3.3
) into (
3.2
), yields

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}:=

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}\delta\left(\bar{v}\right)\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{N>x\}}N\right]\right|

(3.4)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}F}{D_{v}F}\right]-\mathrm{E}\left[\frac{\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}D_{v}\left(D_{v}F\right)}{\left(D_{v}F\right)^{2}}\right]-E\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{N>x\}}N\right]\right|.

Adding and subtracting
\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}F\right]
in (
3.4
), we get

(3.5)

\Phi_{x}\leq\mathrm{E}\left[\left|\frac{(1-D_{v}F)F}{D_{v}F}\right|\right]+\mathrm{E}\left[\frac{|D_{v}\left(D_{v}F\right)|}{\left(D_{v}F\right)^{2}}\right]+\left|\mathrm{E}\left[F\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}-N\mathbf{1}_{\{N>x\}}\right]\right|.

Applying Hölder’s inequlity to the first term, we obtain

(3.6)

\mathrm{E}\left[\left|\frac{(1-D_{v}F)F}{D_{v}F}\right|\right]\leq\left\|{F}\right\|_{4}\left\|\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-1}\right\|_{4}\left\|1-D_{v}F\right\|_{2}.

Meanwhile, applying Hölder’s inequality to the second term, we get

(3.7)

\mathrm{E}\left[\frac{|D_{v}\left(D_{v}F\right)|}{\left(D_{v}F\right)^{2}}\right]\leq\left\|\left(D_{v}F\right)^{-1}\right\|_{4}^{2}\left\|D_{v}\left(D_{v}F\right)\right\|_{2}.

Finally, applying Stein’s method with
h(y)=y\mathbf{1}_{\{y>x\}}
(see
[
12
, Chapter 3]
), and using the integration by parts formula
\mathrm{E}\left[Ff(F)\right]=\mathrm{E}\left[f^{\prime}(F)\langle DF,v\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\right]

\displaystyle\left|\mathrm{E}\left[F\mathbf{1}_{\{F>x\}}-N\mathbf{1}_{\{N>x\}}\right]\right|

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\left[f_{h}^{\prime}(F)-Ff_{h}(F)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\left[f_{h}^{\prime}(F)\left(1-D_{v}F\right)\right]\leq\|f_{h}^{\prime}(F)\|_{2}\|1-D_{v}F\|_{2},

where

\displaystyle\|f_{h}^{\prime}(F)\|_{2}\leq 2.

Then, substituting (

Moment estimates of the second derivative of u

Proposition 4.1

We will make use of the Picard iteration scheme.

(4.6)

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|u_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq c_{T,p}.

This result is proved in
[
13

We apply the properties of the divergence operator, namely using (

It has also been proven in
[
10

\displaystyle\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|D_{s,y}u_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq c_{T,p}p_{t-s}(x-y).

Once again using (

) and (

\displaystyle D_{r,z}D_{s,y}

\displaystyle u_{n+1}(t,x)=p_{t-s}(x-y)\Sigma^{(1)}_{n,s,y}D_{r,z}u_{n}(s,y)

\displaystyle+\int_{[s,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t-\tau}(x-\xi)\Sigma^{(2)}_{n,\tau,\xi}D_{r,z}u_{n}(\tau,\xi)D_{s,y}u_{n}(\tau,\xi)W(d\tau,d\xi)

(4.9)

\displaystyle+\int_{[s,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p_{t-\tau}(x-\xi)\Sigma^{(1)}_{n,\tau,\xi}D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n}(\tau,\xi)W(d\tau,d\xi),

\displaystyle\left\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n+1}(t,x)\right\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle+C_{T,p}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{t-\tau}(x-\xi)p^{2}_{\tau-r}(\xi-z)p^{2}_{\tau-s}(\xi-y)d\xi d\tau\

for some constant

,

and
\sigma
.
Let

\displaystyle J(d\tau,d\xi)

\displaystyle:=p^{2}_{{\tau-r}}(\xi-z)\delta_{s,y}(d\tau,d\xi)+p_{\tau-r}^{2}(\xi-z)p^{2}_{\tau-s}(\xi-y)d\tau d\xi.

\displaystyle\left\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n+1}(t,x)\right\|^{2}_{p}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{T,p}\int_{[s,t]\times\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{t-\tau}(x-\xi)\left\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n}(\tau,\xi)\right\|^{2}_{p}d\tau d\xi.

\displaystyle\left\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n+1}(t,x)\right\|^{2}_{p}\leq C_{T,p}\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{t-s_{1}}(x-y_{1})J(ds_{1},dy_{1})

\displaystyle\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n+1}(t,x)\|^{2}_{p}\leq C_{T,p}K^{2}(t,x)

\displaystyle\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u_{n+1}(t,x)\|^{2}_{p}\leq C_{T,p}K^{2}(t,x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n-1}\frac{C_{T,p}^{k+1}}{(2\pi)^{\frac{k+1}{2}}}\int_{s<s_{k+1}<\cdots<s_{2}<s_{1}<t}ds_{k}\cdots ds_{1}

Using Lemma

, we arrive at the upper-bound

Since

converges in

for all

.
By the same arguments as in the proof of Theorem 6.4 in

\|D_{r,z}D_{s,y}u(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\widetilde{C}_{T,p}\Phi_{r,z,s,y}(t,x)

for all

,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(\int_{t_{\alpha}}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,R}^{2}(s,y)U^{2}(s,y)dyds\right)^{-q}\right]

Step 2:

Lemma A.1
.

For
0<r<s<t
and
y,z,x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle K_{r,z,s,y}(t,x)\leq C_{t}\Phi_{r,z,s,y}(t,x),

where
\Phi
and
K
are defined in (
4.5
) and (
4.11
) respectively.

Proof.

Using the identity
p_{t}^{2}(a)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}p_{t/2}(a)
, we see that the first term in
K_{r,z,s,y}(t,x)
is bounded by a constant depending on
t
times the first term in
\Phi_{r,z,s,y}(t,x)
. So, we estimate the integral term in
K_{r,z,s,y}(t,x)
that we denote by
I
.
Using the above identity for the square of the Gaussian together with the identity
(
2.7
) we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}p^{2}_{t-\theta}(x-w)p^{2}_{\theta-s}(w-y)p^{2}_{\theta-r}(w-z)\,dwd\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}(t-\theta)(\theta-s)(\theta-r)}}p_{\frac{t-\theta}{2}}(x-w)p_{\frac{\theta-s}{2}}(w-y)p_{\frac{\theta-r}{2}}(w-z)\,dwd\theta

\displaystyle=p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(2\pi)^{3}(t-\theta)(\theta-s)(\theta-r)}}p_{\frac{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)}{2(t-s)}}(w-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))

\displaystyle\qquad\times p_{\frac{\theta-r}{2}}(w-z)\,dwd\theta.

Now, applying the semigroup property,

\displaystyle I=\frac{p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{1}{\sqrt{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)(\theta-r)}}p_{\frac{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)}{2(t-s)}+\frac{\theta-r}{2}}(z-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))d\theta.

Since for
r<s<\theta<t

\frac{\theta-r}{2}\leq\frac{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)}{2(t-s)}+\frac{\theta-r}{2}\leq\frac{t-r}{2},

we have

p_{\frac{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)}{2(t-s)}+\frac{\theta-r}{2}}(z-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))\leq\frac{\sqrt{t-r}}{\sqrt{\theta-r}}p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))

and

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{t-r}}{\sqrt{(t-\theta)(\theta-r)^{2}(\theta-s)}}p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{t-r}p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}J,

where

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle=\int_{s}^{t}\frac{\sqrt{t-r}}{(\theta-r)\sqrt{(t-\theta)(\theta-s)}}p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y-\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}(x-y))d\theta.

Making the change of variables
\frac{\theta-s}{t-s}=\gamma
and putting
\beta=\frac{{s-r}}{t-s}>0
yields
\theta-r=\theta-s+s-r=(t-s)(\gamma+\beta)
and

J=\frac{1}{t-s}\int_{0}^{1}(1-\gamma)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\gamma^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\gamma+\beta)^{-1}p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y+\gamma(y-x))d\gamma.

We consider two cases:

Case 1:
If
z-y
and
z-x
have same sign, then

p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y+\gamma(y-x))\leq p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y)+p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-x).

Case 2:
If
z-y
and
z-x
have different sign, suppose firstly that

z-y>0
and
z-x=z-y+y-x<0
. Then,
0<z-y<-(y-x)
; so
|z-y|<|y-x|
and

p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y+\gamma(y-x))\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi(t-r)}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|y-x|>|z-y|\}}.

Similarly, if
z-y<0
and
z-x=z-y+y-x>0
, then
0>z-y>-(y-x)
, which implies
|z-y|<|y-x|
and we end up with the same inequality.

Finally, noting that for
\beta=\frac{{s-r}}{t-s}>0

\int_{0}^{1}(1-\gamma)^{-1/2}\gamma^{-1/2}(\gamma+\beta)^{-1}d\gamma=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\beta(\beta+1)}}=\frac{t-s}{\sqrt{(t-r)(s-r)}},

we get

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\sqrt{t-r}p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)}{(2\pi)^{3/2}}J

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}\frac{p_{\frac{t-s}{2}}(x-y)}{\sqrt{s-r}}\left(p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-y)+p_{\frac{t-r}{2}}(z-x)+\mathbf{1}_{\{|y-x|>|z-y|\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C^{\prime}_{T}\frac{p^{2}_{t-s}(x-y)}{\sqrt{s-r}}\left(p^{2}_{t-r}(z-y)+p^{2}_{t-r}(z-x)+\mathbf{1}_{\{|y-x|>|z-y|\}}\right),

which then completes our proof by taking the square roots on both sides.
∎

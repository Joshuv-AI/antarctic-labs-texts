Polynomial growth of Betti sequences over local rings Thanks: Research partly supported by National Science Foundation grants DMS-1103176, DMS–210122 and National Science Foundation of China grant 12001384.
Luchezar L. Avramov, Alexandra Seceleanu, Zheng Yang

Abstract
. This is a study of the sequences of Betti numbers of finitely generated modules over a complete intersection local ring, R R . The subsequences ( β i R ​ ( M ) ) (\beta^{R}_{i}(M)) with even, respectively, odd i i are known to be eventually given by polynomials in i i with equal leading terms. We show that these polynomials coincide if I □ {{I}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}} , the ideal generated by the quadratic relations of the associated graded ring of R R , satisfies height I □ ≥ codim R − 1 \operatorname{height}{{I}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}\geq\operatorname{codim}R-1 , and that the converse holds if R R is homogeneous or codim ⁡ R ≤ 4 \operatorname{codim}R\leq 4 . Subsequently Avramov, Packauskas, and Walker proved that the terms of degree j > codim R − height I □ j>\operatorname{codim}R-\operatorname{height}{{I}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}} of the even and odd Betti polynomials are equal. We give a new proof of that result, based on an intrinsic characterization of residue rings of c.i. local rings of minimal multiplicity obtained in this paper. We also show that that bound is optimal.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with free resolutions of finitely generated modules
M
over a
commutative noetherian ring
R
with unique maximal ideal,
{\mathfrak{m}}
. Each such module
has a unique up to isomorphism
minimal
free resolution. The rank
\beta^{R}_{i}(M)

of the
i
th module in such a resolution is called the
i
th
Betti number
of
M
.

The asymptotic patterns of
Betti sequences

(\beta^{R}_{i}(M))
reflect and affect the
singularity of
R
. This dynamic is best understood when the ring
R
is
complete
intersection
, abbreviated to
c.i.
; that is, when the
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion
\widehat{R}
is
isomorphic to the residue ring of some regular local ring modulo an ideal generated by a regular
set; the smallest cardinality of such a set is equal to
\operatorname{codim}R
, the
codimension
of
R
.

Gulliksen
[
24
]
proved that if
R
is c.i., then for every
M
there exist
Betti polynomials
,

\beta^{R,M}_{0}
and
\beta^{R,M}_{1}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
with
\operatorname{deg}(\beta^{R,M}_{j})<\operatorname{codim}R
(where

\operatorname{deg}(0):=-1
, by convention) such that
\beta^{R}_{i}(M)=\beta^{R,M}_{j}(i)
for
i\gg 0
and

i\equiv j\pmod{2}
. The hypothesis on
R
cannot be relaxed, as
\beta^{R}_{i}(k)\leq b(i)
with

k:=R/{\mathfrak{m}}
and
b\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
implies
R
is c.i. (Gulliksen,
[
25
]
), nor can the conclusion
on
\beta^{R,M}_{j}
be tightened, for
\beta^{R,k}_{{\mathsf{even}}}=\beta^{R,k}_{{\mathsf{odd}}}
and

\operatorname{deg}(\beta^{R,k}_{{\mathsf{even}}})=\operatorname{codim}R-1
hold when
R
is c.i. (Tate,
[
40
]
).

Eisenbud
[
18
]
showed that if
R
is c.i. and
\operatorname{codim}R\leq 1
, then
(\beta^{R}_{i}(M))
is eventually
constant for every
M
; this was an early sign of possible connections between
\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{even}}}
and

\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{odd}}}
. The general property is that these polynomials have equal degrees and leading
coefficients over every c.i. ring; see Avramov
[
3
]
. The present work is a study of the
discrepancy between
\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{even}}}
and
\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{odd}}}
as measured by a number,

\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M):=\operatorname{deg}\big(\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{even}}}-\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{odd}}}\big)+1\,,

that we call the
granularity
of
M
over
R
. The least value,
\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)=0
, is attained when

(\beta^{R}_{i}(M))
is
eventually polynomial
; that is, when
\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{even}}}=\beta^{R,M}_{{\mathsf{odd}}}
.

Our main results link the granularities of
R
-modules and the structure of
R
.
Let
R{}^{\mathsf{g}}
denote the associated graded ring of
R
and
\pi\colon\operatorname{Sym}_{k}({R^{\mathsf{g}}_{1}})\twoheadrightarrow R{}^{\mathsf{g}}

the canonical map. We write
\operatorname{codim}{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}
for the height of the ideal generated by the
quadratic forms in
\operatorname{Ker}(\pi)
and call that number the
quadratic codimension
of
R
.

1.1 Theorem

(Theorem
4.1
)
.

Every module
M
over a c.i. local ring
R
satisfies

\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)\leq\max\{\operatorname{codim}R-\operatorname{codim}{{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}}-1\,,\,0\}\,.

This theorem subsumes a number of contributions, related in time and content as follows. It was
proved in
[
5
]
for local rings with
\operatorname{codim}R=\operatorname{codim}{{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}}
. When
R
and
M

are
homogeneous
(that is, localizations of
R{}^{\mathsf{g}}
and of a graded
R{}^{\mathsf{g}}
-module at the
maximal ideal
(R{}^{\mathsf{g}}_{1})
) and
\operatorname{codim}R=\operatorname{codim}{{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}}+1=2
, it was obtained by Avramov
and Zheng
[
13
]
using methods not available in other cases. Theorem
1.1
was
proved for all c.i. rings
R
with
\operatorname{codim}R=\operatorname{codim}{{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}}+1
in unpublished joint work
of the authors of this paper. Motivated by that result, Avramov, Packauskas, and Walker
(
[
11
]
, to appear) subsequently proved the full theorem by different techniques.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
, given below, extends our original approach. It relies on
the following structure theorem for rings of given quadratic codimension.

1.2 Theorem

(Part of Theorem
3.7
)
.

A local ring
R
with infinite residue field has
\operatorname{codim}{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}=q
if and only if
\widehat{R}
is a homomorphic
image of some c.i. local ring
Q
with
\operatorname{codim}Q=q
, multiplicity
2^{q}
, and
\operatorname{edim}Q=\operatorname{edim}R
.

This result is of independent interest, as every local c.i. ring of codimension
q
has multiplicity at
least
2^{q}
, and those
of minimal multiplicity
are to regular local rings what
complete intersections of quadrics are to polynomial rings.

In the second half of the paper we explore possibilities of relaxing the hypothesis or tightening the
conclusion of Theorem
1.1
, and completely settle the second issue:

1.3 Theorem

(Abstracted from Theorem
5.2
)
.

The upper bound in the inequality in Theorem
1.1
is optimal: For every pair
(c,q)

of integers with
c\geq q\geq 0
there exist a c.i. local ring
R
and a cyclic
R
-module
S
that satisfy

(\operatorname{codim}R,\operatorname{codim}{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}})=(c,q)\ \text{ and }\ \operatorname{gn}_{R}(S)=\max\{c-q-1,0\}\,.

In order to probe the tightness of the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
we search for partial
converses to its statement. Below we focus on those rings over which all finite modules have
granularity zero and obtain the following result:

1.4 Theorem

(Contained in Theorems
6.1
and
6.2
)
.

Let
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
be a c.i. local ring such that the Betti sequence of each finite
R
-module is eventually
polynomial.

If
R
is homogeneous, or
\operatorname{codim}R\leq 4
and
k
is algebraically closed, then one has

\operatorname{codim}R\leq\operatorname{codim}{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}+1\,.

The proofs of Theorems
1.3
and
1.4
hinge upon identifying and constructing families
of residue rings
S
of
R
, where the import of invariants of the rings
R
and
S
on the values
of
\operatorname{gn}_{R}(S)
can be traced explicitly. The relevant arguments involve hard computations that
draw on a number of different techniques.

The results in this work and in
[
11
]
open up a new narrative concerning the patterns of Betti
sequences of modules over a given c.i. ring. The methods of proof in these papers suggest possible
approaches and specific questions. Here is a sample.

1.5 Question
.

Let
R
be a c.i. local ring
R
and set
n:=\operatorname{codim}R-\operatorname{codim}{{R}{}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}
.

Does

?

2.
Complexity and granularity

We first overview notation, constructions, and results that will be used throughout the
main text of the paper. The statement that
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
is a
local ring
here means
that
R
is a commutative noetherian ring with unique maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}
and
k
is the
residue field
R/{\mathfrak{m}}
. As usual,
\dim R
denotes the (Krull) dimension of
R
and
\operatorname{edim}R

its
embedding dimension
(that is, the minimal number of generators of
{\mathfrak{m}}
); the
(
embedding
)
codimension
of
R
is the number
\operatorname{codim}R:=\operatorname{edim}R-\dim R
,
and the number
\operatorname{codepth}R:=\operatorname{edim}R-\operatorname{depth}R
is its (
embedding
)
codepth
.

When
(R^{\prime},{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is a local ring, a ring homomorphism
\varphi\colon R\to R^{\prime}
is
local

if
\varphi({\mathfrak{m}})
lies in
{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
. The map
\varphi
is faithfully flat if and only if it is flat and local.
Surjective homomorphisms are assumed to induce the identity map on the residue fields.

For our purposes, it is often convenient to introduce invariants through non-canonical
presentations of modifications of
R
, or of its
{\mathfrak{m}}
-adic completion,
\widehat{R}
.

2.1
.

A
regular presentation
of
R
is a surjective ring map
R\twoheadleftarrow P:\!\rho
with
P
local
and regular; we use the same name for an isomorphism
R\cong P/I
, with
P
as above.

Every regular presentation
\rho
factors through one that is
minimal
, meaning

\operatorname{edim}P=\operatorname{edim}R
or, equivalently,
I\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}
, where
{\mathfrak{p}}
is the maximal ideal of
P
.
Indeed, one has
I/(I\cap{\mathfrak{p}}^{2})\cong\operatorname{Ker}({\mathfrak{p}}/{\mathfrak{p}}^{2}\to{\mathfrak{m}}/{\mathfrak{m}}^{2})
. Lifting a
k
-basis of this kernel to a
subset
{\boldsymbol{t}}
of
I
yields a minimal presentation
R\cong\overline{P}/\overline{I}
, with
\overline{P}:=P/P{\boldsymbol{t}}
;
the ring
\overline{P}
is regular because
{\boldsymbol{t}}
extends to a regular system of parameters of
P
.

By Cohen’s Structure Theorem, regular presentations
\widehat{R}\cong P/I
exist, and such

Cohen presentations
also produce minimal ones. Minimal Cohen presentation need
not be isomorphic, but
\operatorname{rank}_{k}(I/{\mathfrak{p}}I)
is the same for all of them (see
2.2
below);
we let
\operatorname{rel}R
denote that common value and call it the
number of relations
of
R
.

Throughout the paper,
M
denotes a finite, that is, finitely generated
R
-module.
We review some numerical invariants of minimal free resolutions of modules over local
rings. For general information on free resolutions we refer to
[
6
]
.

2.2
.

The
i
th
Betti number

\beta^{R}_{i}(M)
of
M
is the rank of the
i
th module in a(ny)
minimal free
R
-resolution
F
of
M
. It can be computed in different ways:

\beta^{R}_{i}(M)=\operatorname{rank}_{k}(F\otimes_{R}k)=\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{R}(M,k)=\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(M,k)\,.

One measure of the growth of the
Betti sequence

(\beta^{R}_{i}(M))
is given by the number

\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M):=\inf\{n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}\mid\beta^{R}_{i}(M)\leq ai^{n-1}\text{ for $i\gg 0$ and some }a>0\}\,,

called the
complexity
of
M
over
R
. Thus
\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)=0
means that
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{R}M
is finite
and
\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)=\infty
that
i\mapsto\beta^{R}_{i}(M)
cannot be bounded above by a polynomial.

The Betti numbers of
M
are handily packed into its
Poincaré series
, given by

P^{R}_{M}:=\sum_{i{\geqslant}0}\beta^{R}_{i}(M)z^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[[z]]\,.

If
\widehat{R}\cong P/I
is a minimal Cohen presentation, then the series
P^{P}_{I}
lies in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}[z]

and it is an invariant of
R
; in particular, so is the number
\operatorname{rank}_{k}(I/{\mathfrak{p}}I)
; see
[
6
, 4.1.3]
.

We study the asymptotic behavior of a Betti sequence in terms of its Poincaré series, complexity,
and granularity—a new invariant that we introduce next.

2.3
.

The sequence
(\beta^{R}_{i}(M))
is
linearly recursive
if and only if the series
P^{R}_{M}
is

rational
; that is, if and only if
p\cdot P^{R}_{M}
lies in
{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
for some nonzero
p\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
.

If
P^{R}_{M}
is rational and
\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)
is finite, then the poles of
P^{R}_{M}
are at roots of unity, that
of highest non-negative order is at
1
, and its order equals
\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)
; see
[
2
, 2.4]
.

We say that
M
has
granularity

g
and write
\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)=g
if
P^{R}_{M}
is rational
and has a pole of order
g\geq 0
at
-1
. Formulas involving granularity are stated or used with
the tacit assumption that the relevant modules have rational Poincaré series.

The definitions of complexity and granularity given in
2.3
and those used in the
introduction will soon be reconciled; see
2.5
.

2.4
.

The properties of Poincaré series and of complexity, listed below, hold without restrictions;
the formulas for granularity follow from those for Poincaré series.

(1)

If
N
is an
n
th syzygy module of
M
over
R
, then one has

P^{R}_{M}-z^{n}P^{R}_{N}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]\,,\quad\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{cx}_{R}(N)\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{gn}_{R}(N)\,.

(2)

If
R\to(R^{\prime},{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
is a local ring homomorphism,
M^{\prime}
denotes the
R^{\prime}
-module
R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}M
, and

\operatorname{Tor}^{R}_{i}(R^{\prime},M)=0
holds for
i\geq 1
, then one has

P^{R}_{M}=P^{R^{\prime}}_{M^{\prime}}\,,\ \ \ \operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{cx}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})\,,\ \text{ and }\ \,\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)=\operatorname{gn}_{R^{\prime}}(M^{\prime})\,.

This is the case, in particular, if
R^{\prime}
is flat over
R
, or if
R^{\prime}=R/R{\boldsymbol{g}}
for some
R
-regular set

{\boldsymbol{g}}
that is also
M
-regular.

(3)

A (
codimension
n
)
deformation
of
R
to
Q
is an isomorphism
R\cong Q/Q{\boldsymbol{f}}
, with

(Q,{\mathfrak{q}},k)
local and
{\boldsymbol{f}}
a
Q
-regular set (of
n
elements); it is
embedded
if
{\boldsymbol{f}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{q}}^{2}
.
We use the same name(s) also for the canonical homomorphism
R\twoheadleftarrow Q
.

Betti sequences whose asymptotic patterns are (almost) completely determined by
complexity and granularity admit several descriptions:

2.5
.

Let
R
be a local ring and
M
a nonzero
R
-module.

The following conditions on an integer
c\geq 0
are equivalent.

(i)

There is an inclusion
(1-z^{2})^{c}\cdot P^{R}_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
.

(ii)

There exists a unique
p^{R}_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]
with
p^{R}_{M}(1)>0
such that

\qquad P^{R}_{M}=\frac{p^{R}_{M}}{(1+z)^{\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)}(1-z)^{\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)}}\quad\text{and}\quad 0\leq\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)<\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)\leq c\,.

(iii)

There exist unique polynomials
\beta^{R,M}_{j}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
for
j=0,1
that
satisfying the following conditions, where the convention
\operatorname{deg}(0)=-1
is used:

\displaystyle\beta^{R}_{i}(M)

\displaystyle=\beta^{R,M}_{j}(i)\quad\text{for}\quad i\gg 0\quad\text{when}\quad i\equiv j\pmod{2}\,,\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\qquad\operatorname{gn}_{R}(M)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{deg}\big(\beta^{R,M}_{0}-\beta^{R,M}_{1}\big)+1<\operatorname{deg}\big(\beta^{R,M}_{j}\big)+1=\operatorname{cx}_{R}(M)\leq c\,.

Indeed, it is shown in
[
3
, Proof of Theorem 4.1]
that (i) implies (ii). Partial fraction decomposition
yields the implications (ii)
\implies
(iii)
\implies
(i).

2.6
.

A collection of known results illustrates the conditions in
2.5
.

(1)

If
\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}(\widehat{M})
is finite for some codimension
c
deformation
\widehat{R}\cong Q/Q{\boldsymbol{f}}
, then

2.5
(i) holds; see
[
24
, 4.2(i)]
; this is a major source of modules whose Poincaré
series have poles only at
\pm 1
. However,
R
-modules

with
P^{R}_{M}=2/(1-z)
may exist
over rings
R
that admit no non-trivial deformations; see
[
9
]
.

(2)

The ring
R
is said to be
complete intersection
, or
c.i.
, if
\widehat{R}
admits a
deformation to some regular local ring. When
\widehat{R}\cong P/I
is a minimal Cohen presentation,

is c.i. if and only if

can be minimally generated by some
P
-regular set, if and only if

\operatorname{rel}R=\operatorname{codim}R
(i.e.,

).

(3)

The following conditions are equivalent: (i)

is c.i.; (ii)
(1-z^{2})^{c}\cdot P^{R}_{M}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[z]

for every
R
-module

; (iii)
P^{R}_{k}=(1+t)^{\dim R}/(1-t)^{\operatorname{codim}R}
; (iv)
\operatorname{cx}_{R}(k)<\infty
.

See (1) for (i)
\implies
(ii),
[
40
, Theorem 6]
for (i)
\implies
(iii), and
[
25
, 2.3]
for (iv)
\implies
(i).

2.7
.

Maps of local rings
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)\to(R^{\prime},{\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime},k^{\prime})
that are flat with
{\mathfrak{m}}R^{\prime}={\mathfrak{m}}^{\prime}
will be called

adjustments
of
R
; for every
R
-module

and
M^{\prime}:=R^{\prime}\otimes_{R}M
one has

H_{M^{\prime}}=H_{M}\,,\quad\dim_{R^{\prime}}M^{\prime}=\dim_{R}M\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\,\operatorname{edim}R^{\prime}=\operatorname{edim}R^{\prime}\,.

Any adjustment

, composed with the completion map
R^{\prime}\to\widehat{R^{\prime}}
and some
minimal Cohen presentation
\widehat{R^{\prime}}\cong P/I
yields an adjustment

with
(P,{\mathfrak{p}},k^{\prime})

\operatorname{edim}P=\operatorname{edim}R
. In adjustments

.

Grothendieck
[

proved that every field extension

occurs as the

, called

.

3.
Associated quadratic rings

Recall that
(R,{\mathfrak{m}},k)
denotes a local ring and
M
a finite
R
-module.

In this section we introduce and study invariants of
R
that are defined in terms of its associated graded ring
and appear in the main results of the paper. We first record terminology and notation used when dealing with
associated graded objects; for general background on graded rings and their modules; see
7.1
.

3.1
.

Set
M{}^{\mathsf{g}}_{j}={\mathfrak{m}}^{j}M/{\mathfrak{m}}^{j+1}M
for

, and
M{}^{\mathsf{g}}=\bigoplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}M{}^{\mathsf{g}}_{j}
. Thus,

For

. The image of

in

is called

of

and is denoted by

; in addition, we set

.

We write
H_{M}
for
\sum_{j{\geqslant}0}\operatorname{rank}_{k}{{M^{\mathsf{g}}_{j}}}(z)
. Since

is generated by
{R^{\mathsf{g}}_{1}}

over
{R^{\mathsf{g}}_{0}}=k
, the Hilbert-Serre Theorem yields

, with

The integer
h^{R}_{M}(1)
, called the

of

, is denoted by

;
one has

, with equality if and only if

; set

3.2
.

Let
\widehat{R}\twoheadleftarrow P{\ :\,}\rho

R{}^{\mathsf{g}}\twoheadleftarrow P{}^{\mathsf{g}}{\ :\,}\rho{}^{\mathsf{g}}

Set

and call the isomorphism
R{}^{\mathsf{g}}\cong P{}^{\mathsf{g}}/I^{*}
the

of

As

\operatorname{height}I^{*}=\operatorname{codim}R{}^{\mathsf{g}}=\operatorname{codim}R=\operatorname{height}I\leq\operatorname{rel}R\leq\operatorname{rel}R{}^{\mathsf{g}}\,.

We define the

It is an invariant of

3.5

Let

(1)

[

(2)

When

-regular; see

(3)

Assume

-regular if and only

-regular and equality holds in (2) above; such an equivalence is

[

(4)

(5)

The ring

3.6 Proposition

The following conditions on a local ring

(5)

(ii)

(iii)

(iv)

(2)

(v)

(3)

(vi)

\{{{g}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}},\dots,{{g}_{s}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}\}

-regular.

Proof.

We set

(4)) that

(i)
\implies

(vi)
\implies

This implication follows from the hypothesis, as

(v)
\implies
(iv).
The ring

(iii)

3.8 Theorem
.

Let
(P,{\mathfrak{p}},k)
be a local ring; let
\overline{a}
denote the image in

Given

-regular for some

is

-regular, then the following set is Zariski-open and not empty:

{\mathcal{U}}:=\{\overline{{\boldsymbol{a}}}\in\mathbb{A}^{r-1}_{k}\mid P/(f^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{1},\dots,f^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{q})\text{ is c.i.\ of minimal multiplicity}\,\}\,.

Proof.

Let
k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
be the polynomial ring with indeterminates

.

The ring
P/(f^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{1},\dots,f^{{\boldsymbol{a}}}_{q})
is c.i. of minimal multiplicity if and only if the set

{{{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}:=\{{{f}_{1}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}-\overline{a}_{1}{{f}_{r}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}},\ldots,{{f}_{q}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}-\overline{a}_{q}{{f}_{r}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}\}
is

-regular; see (i)
\iff
(vi) in

. The set
{{{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}
is regular if and only if

holds with
{P^{\mathsf{g}}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}:=P{}^{\mathsf{g}}/P{}^{\mathsf{g}}{{{\boldsymbol{f}}}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}
and

. The algebra
{P^{\mathsf{g}}_{{\boldsymbol{a}}}}
is the fiber
of the canonical map

6.1 Theorem

(iv)

6.3

Let

, as seen from
the relations

\frac{I+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}}{{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}}={{I}_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}\subseteq D_{2}={{L}_{2}^{\scriptscriptstyle\square}}=\frac{L+{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}}{{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}}=\frac{\widetilde{J}}{{\mathfrak{p}}^{3}}\,.

Put

7.
A family of Golod homomorphisms

This section does not rely on material in earlier parts of the paper. The goal is Theorem

7.9
, which contains results crucial to the proofs in Sections
5
and
6
.

7.1
.

In this section
k
denotes a field,
{\boldsymbol{x}}
a finite set of indeterminates of degree one, and
A
a
k
algebra
isomorphic to
k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]/I
, where
I
is a homogeneous ideal in
({\boldsymbol{x}})^{2}
. Furthermore,
N
denotes a graded

A
-module; we set
\inf N:=\inf\{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid N_{j}\neq 0\}
if
N\neq 0
and
\inf 0=\infty
; abusing notation,
we write
k
for
A/(A_{1})
.

A blanket hypothesis is that all
A
-modules are graded and finitely generated,
their submodules are homogeneous, and their homomorphisms preserve degrees.

Natural gradings
\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(N,k)=\bigoplus_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(N,k)_{j}
are inherited from
resolutions by free graded
A
-modules. The
graded Betti numbers

\beta^{A}_{i,j}(N):=\operatorname{rank}_{k}\operatorname{Tor}^{A}_{i}(N,k)_{j}
satisfy the conditions

\beta^{A}_{i,j}(N)=0
for
i\notin[0,\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{A}N]
,
j<i+\inf N
, and
j\gg i
.

We write
P^{A}_{N}(y,z)
, or
P^{A}_{N}
, for the
graded Poincaré series
of
N
, defined to be

P^{A}_{N}(y,z):=\sum_{i{\geqslant}0}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta^{A}_{i,j}(N)y^{j}z^{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[y^{\pm 1}][[z]]\,.

(1)

Localization at
(A_{1})
, denoted here by
?\leadsto?^{\ell}
, is a faithfully exact functor from
graded
A
-modules to
A^{\ell}
-modules; it preserves freeness and minimality, whence

P^{A^{\ell}}_{N^{\ell}}(z)=P^{A}_{N}(1,z)\,.

Three relevant properties of graded algebras are defined in terms of
P^{A}_{k}
.

(2)

The algebra
A
is said to be
Koszul
if it satisfies the condition

H_{A}(-yz)\cdot P^{A}_{k}(y,z)=1\,;

see also
7.2
(1). In particular,
k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
is Koszul and
P^{k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}_{k}(y,z)=(1+yz)^{|{\boldsymbol{x}}|}
.

(3)

The algebra
A
is said to be a
graded complete intersection
if
I=k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]\,{\boldsymbol{g}}
for some

k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
-regular set of forms,
{\boldsymbol{g}}
; by a graded version of
2.6
(3), this is equivalent to

P^{A}_{k}(y,z)\cdot\prod_{g\in{\boldsymbol{g}}}(1-y^{\operatorname{deg}(g)}z^{2})=(1+yz)^{|{\boldsymbol{x}}|}\,.

(4)

The algebra
A
is said to be
Golod
if it satisfies the condition

P^{A}_{k}(y,z)\cdot\left(1-z^{2}P^{k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]}_{I}(y,z)\right)=(1+yz)^{|{\boldsymbol{x}}|}\,.

(Note: This equality differs from that in
[
28
]
, where Formula (2.2) is incorrect.)

In view of
7.1
(1), the algebra
A
is c.i., respectively, Golod if and only if the local ring

A^{\ell}
has the corresponding property; cf.
2.6
, respectively,
5.3
.

The focus in this section is on specific properties of polynomial ideals.

7.2
.

Let
B:=k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
be a polynomial ring,
I
an ideal of
B
, and
t
an integer.

We say that
I
is
t
-
linear
(or,
I

has an
t
-linear resolution
) if
\beta^{B}_{i,j}(I)=0

holds for
j\neq i+t
; for instance,
({\boldsymbol{x}})
is
1
-linear. The ideal
I
is
linear
if it is
t
-linear
for some
t
; when
I
is linear, it is
(\inf I)
-linear if
I\neq 0
, and
t
-linear for each
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
if

I=0
.

Following Herzog and Hibi

[
26
]
, we say
I
is
componentwise linear
if the ideal
{I}_{\langle j\rangle}:=BI_{j}
is
j
-linear for each

j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We list a few relevant properties.

(1)

If
I
is
t
-linear, then the following equality holds:

\displaystyle(-z)^{t}P^{B}_{I}(y,z)

\displaystyle=(1+yz)^{|{\boldsymbol{x}}|}H_{I}(-yz)\,.

(2)

If
I
is linear, then it is componentwise linear.

(3)

If
I
is componentwise linear, then the following equality holds:

\beta^{B}_{i,j}(I)=\sum_{h\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta_{i,h}({I}_{\langle j\rangle})-\sum_{h\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta_{i,h}(B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle})\quad\text{for}\quad i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\,.

Part (1) can be read off Polishchuk and Positselski
[
35
, Proof of Proposition 2.2]
.
(2) is well known; e.g.,
[
35
, 1.1]
or
[
28
, Lemma 1]
. Part (3) is proved in
[
26
, 1.3]
.

We reduce computation of Poincaré series of componentwise linear ideals to a
potentially simpler task – computing Hilbert series of finitely many residue rings.

7.3 Proposition
.

Let
B:=k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
be a polynomial ring,
I
an ideal of
B
, and put

(7.3.1)

e:=|{\boldsymbol{x}}|\,,\ n_{j}:=\operatorname{rank}_{k}I_{j}\,,\ {\mathbb{J}}:=\{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}\mid I_{j}\neq B_{1}I_{j-1}\}\,,\ \text{and}\,\ t:=\max{\mathbb{J}}\,.

If
I
is componentwise linear, then
P^{B}_{I}(z,y)
is given by the following formula:

(7.3.2)

\displaystyle P^{B}_{I}(y,z)

\displaystyle=(1+yz)^{e}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{-j}\big(H_{B/{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}(-yz)-H_{B/{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}(-yz)\big)

\displaystyle}{\displaystyle+(1+yz)^{e}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{-j}n_{j-1}(-yz)^{j-1}\,.

Proof.

Both
{I}_{\langle j\rangle}
and
B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}
are
j
-linear, by the assumption on
I
and by

7.2
(2), respectively; thus, the formula in
7.2
(3) is shorthand for
a family of equalities:

\beta^{B}_{i,j}(I)=\begin{cases}\beta^{B}_{i,i+j}({I}_{\langle j\rangle})-\beta^{B}_{i,i+j}(B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle})&\text{for }j\in{\mathbb{J}}\,;\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Multiply the
i
th equality by
y^{i+j}z^{i}
and sum up over
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
; the result is

\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\beta^{B}_{i,j}(I)y^{i+j}z^{i}=\begin{cases}P^{B}_{{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}-P^{B}_{B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}&\text{for }j\in{\mathbb{J}}\,;\\
0&\text{otherwise}\,.\end{cases}

Now multiply each power series by
(-z)^{t}
, and aggregate the products:

\displaystyle(-z)^{t}P^{B}_{I}=(-z)^{t}\sum_{i,j}\beta^{B}_{i,j}(I)y^{i+j}z^{i}=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{t-j}\big((-z)^{j}P^{B}_{{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}-(-z)^{j}P^{B}_{B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}\big)\,.

Multiply the last equality by
H_{B}(-yz)
and invoke
7.2
(1) to get

\displaystyle(-z)^{t}H_{B}(-yz)P^{B}_{I}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{t-j}\big((-z)^{j}H_{B}(-yz)P^{B}_{{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}-(-z)^{j}H_{B}(-yz)P^{B}_{B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{t-j}(H_{{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}(-yz)-H_{B_{1}{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}(-yz))

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{t-j}\big(H_{{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}(-yz)-(H_{{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}(-yz)-n_{j-1}(-yz)^{j-1})\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{J}}}(-z)^{t-j}(H_{B/{I}_{\langle j-1\rangle}}(-yz)-H_{B/{I}_{\langle j\rangle}}(-yz)+n_{j-1}(-yz)^{j-1})

The preceding equalities, multiplied by
(-z)^{-t}(1+yz)^{e}
, yield (
7.3.2
); see
7.1
(2).
∎

7.4
.

Let
Q
be a noetherian ring and
L
a nonzero
Q
-module. The maximal length of
Q
-regular sequences
in
\operatorname{Ann}_{Q}(L)
is called the
grade
of
L
; it is denoted by
\operatorname{grade}L
and satisfies

\operatorname{grade}L\leq\operatorname{proj\,dim}_{Q}L
. If equality holds (and
\operatorname{grade}L=g
), then
L
is said to be
perfect
(of
grade
g
). When
Q
is regular,
\operatorname{grade}L=\operatorname{height}\operatorname{Ann}_{Q}(L)
holds, and
L
is perfect of grade
g
if and
only if
L
is Cohen-Macaulay and
\dim L=\dim B-g
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{x}}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{e}\}
be a set of nonzero elements of
Q
. Let
X=\begin{bmatrix}x_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}

be an
s\times(s+h-1)
matrix with entries from
{\boldsymbol{x}}\cup\{0\}
, and put

{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{h}\}\,,\ \Delta^{X}_{n}:=\{x_{i,j}\}_{j-i+1=n}\text{ for }n\in[1,h]\,,\text{ and }\,\Delta^{X}:=\bigcup_{n=1}^{h}\Delta^{X}_{n}.

We say that
X
is
adequate for

{\boldsymbol{x}}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

\Delta^{X}\subseteq{\boldsymbol{x}}\,;\quad x_{l}\in\Delta^{X}_{n}\iff l=n\in[1,h]\,;\quad\big|\{\,(i,j):x_{i,j}=x_{n}\in{\boldsymbol{x}}\setminus{\boldsymbol{x}}^{\prime}\big\}\big|\leq 1\,.

Let
I(X,Q)
denote the ideal of
Q
generated by the
s\times s
minors of
X
.

Eagon and Northcott
[
16
, Theorem 2]
proved that
I(X)
is perfect of grade
h
if the entries of

X
are distinct indeterminates. In the results that follow we describe families of ideals with similar
properties that are parametrized by fewer variables.

7.5 Lemma
.

Let
k
be a field,
{\boldsymbol{x}}:=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{e}\}
a set of indeterminates, and put
B:=k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
. Let
X

be an
s\times(s+h-1)
matrix that is adequate for
{\boldsymbol{x}}
(see
7.4
).

The module
A(X):=B/I(X)
is perfect of grade
h
, the ideal
I(X)
is
s
-linear, and

(7.5.1)

(-z)^{s}P^{B}_{I(X)}(-yz)=1-(1+yz)^{h}\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{h-1+i}{i}(-yz)^{i}\,.

In the special case
h=e
one has
I(X)=({\boldsymbol{x}})^{s}
.

Proof.

Put
A(X):=k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]/I(X)
. For each
n\in[1,h]
choose
i\in[1,s]
such that
x_{i,i+n-1}=x_{n}
.
Let
\sigma\colon k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]\to k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]
be the
k
-algebra map that swaps
x_{1,i+n-1}
and

x_{1,n}
for
i\in[1,h]
and fixes the rest of
{\boldsymbol{x}}
. As
\sigma
induces an isomorphism of

k
-algebras
A(X)\cong A(\sigma(X))
, we may suppose that
x_{1,n}=x_{n}
holds for
n\in[1,h]
.

Let
Y=\begin{bmatrix}y_{i,j}\end{bmatrix}
be an
s\times(s+h-1)
matrix with distinct entries
from a set
{\boldsymbol{y}}
of
s\times(s+h-1)
indeterminates such that
{\boldsymbol{y}}\cap{\boldsymbol{x}}=\emptyset
; put

{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\prime}:=\{y_{1,1},\dots,y_{1,h}\}
and
C:=k[{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\prime}]
. The module
A(Y)
is perfect of grade
h
,
the following is
A(Y)
-regular

{\boldsymbol{z}}_{Y}:=\{y_{i,i+n-1}-y_{1,n}\}_{i\neq 1,n\in[1,h]}\cup\{y_{i,j}\notin\Delta_{Y}\}\,,

and
A(Y)/{\boldsymbol{z}}_{Y}A(Y)\cong C/(C_{1})^{s}
holds; see Eagon
[
15
, Proof of Theorem 1]
.

Let
\varkappa\colon k[{\boldsymbol{y}}]\to k[{\boldsymbol{x}}]=B
be the
k
-algebra map with
y_{i,j}\mapsto x_{i,j}
. Since

{\boldsymbol{x}}
is adequate for
X
, the ideal
\operatorname{Ker}(\varkappa)
is generated by the following set of linear forms:

{\boldsymbol{z}}_{X}:=\{\,y_{i,i+n-1}-y_{1,n}\mid x_{i,i+n-1}=x_{n}\,\}_{i\neq 1,n\in[1,h]}\cup\{\,y_{i,j}\mid x_{i,j}=0\,\}

The set
{\boldsymbol{z}}_{X}
is
A(Y)
-regular (as it is a part of
{\boldsymbol{z}}_{Y}
) and
A(X)\cong A(Y)/{\boldsymbol{z}}_{X}A(Y)
holds;
thus
A(X)
is perfect of grade
h
. In addition, the set of linear forms

{\boldsymbol{z}}:=\varkappa({\boldsymbol{z}}_{Y}\setminus{\boldsymbol{z}}_{X})
is
A(X)
-regular with
A(X)/{\boldsymbol{z}}A(X)\cong C/(C_{1})^{s}
.
Therefore
A(X)
is perfect of grade
h
and
P^{B}_{A(X)}=P^{C}_{C/(C_{1})^{s}}
holds. This implies

P^{B}_{I(X)}=P^{C}_{(C_{1})^{s}}
; in particular,
I(X)
is
B
-linear, by
7.2
(2). Setting

{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\prime\prime}:={\boldsymbol{y}}\setminus{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\prime}
and applying (
7.3.2
) with
{\mathbb{J}}=\{s\}
yields

(-z)^{-s}P^{B}_{I}(y,z)=(1+yz)^{e}\big(H_{k[{\boldsymbol{y}}]}(-yz)-H_{k[{\boldsymbol{y}}^{\prime\prime}]}(-yz)H_{C/(C_{1})^{s}}(-yz)\big)\,.

It remains to plug in the well known expressions of the Hilbert series involved.
∎

7.6
.

Let
{\boldsymbol{x}}=\{x_{1},\dots,e_{e}\}
be a set of indeterminates,

,
I
a homogeneous ideal, and

g:=\operatorname{grade}D/I
. When

(1)

(3)

Let

.

7.9 Theorem
.

Let
(Q,{\mathfrak{q}},k)
be a local ring,
{\boldsymbol{u}}=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{e}\}
a

-regular subset of
Q
, and

an

;

. Let

be the ideal of

(1)

The ideal

is perfect of grade
h
, and

; see (
7.5.1
).

(2)

The ideal

\displaystyle z^{2}P^{Q}_{J}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-z)^{s-2}}+\frac{(1+z)^{h+1}}{(-z)^{s-1}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s-1}\binom{h-1+i}{i}(-z)^{i}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{(1+z)^{e}}{(-z)^{s-1}}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{s}\binom{e-1+i}{i}(-z)^{i}-\binom{h-1+s}{s}(-z)^{s}\right).

It is clear that

(2) The ideal

(3) The map

(see

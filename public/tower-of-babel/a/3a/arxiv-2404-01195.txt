Abstract
In this paper, we investigate joint 3-dimensional (3D) trajectory planning and resource allocation for rotary-wing unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) synthetic aperture radar (SAR) sensing. To support emerging real-time SAR applications and enable live mission control, we incorporate real-time communication with a ground station (GS). The UAV’s main mission is the mapping of large areas of interest (AoIs) using an onboard SAR system and transferring the unprocessed raw radar data to the ground in real time. We propose a robust trajectory and resource allocation design that takes into account random UAV trajectory deviations. To this end, we model the UAV trajectory deviations and study their effect on the radar coverage. Then, we formulate a robust non-convex mixed-integer non-linear program (MINLP) such that the UAV 3D trajectory and resources are jointly optimized for maximization of the radar ground coverage. A low-complexity sub-optimal solution for the formulated problem is presented. Furthermore, to assess the performance of the sub-optimal algorithm, we derive an upper bound on the optimal solution based on monotonic optimization theory. Simulation results show that the proposed sub-optimal algorithm achieves close-to-optimal performance and not only outperforms several benchmark schemes but is also robust with respect to UAV trajectory deviations.

INTRODUCTION

Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) is a moving radar system that uses the flight path of a moving platform to simulate a large aperture
[
1
,
2
]
. In classical radar systems, the spatial resolution depends on the ratio of the wavelength of the radar signal to the physical length of the antenna. This means that spatial resolution can be improved by using large antenna arrays, which are however often not practical. synthetic aperture radar overcomes this hurdle by using the motion of the moving platform to form a synthetic aperture where observations in different time slots, corresponding to different positions of the trajectory, are jointly processed to create the final image of the target area
[
3
,
4
]
. synthetic aperture radar can provide high-resolution two-dimensional and even three-dimensional images
[
5
,
6
]
. The benefits of synthetic aperture radar also include its resilience to harsh weather conditions and its ability to operate at day and night. In addition, synthetic aperture radar is non-invasive, which is useful for applications where penetration or direct contact with the area of interest is not possible or not desirable, such as imaging of glaciers and wetlands
[
5
]
. Thanks to these features, synthetic aperture radar sensing has been employed in a variety of applications, such as environmental monitoring, agriculture, and surveillance.

synthetic aperture radar sensing has traditionally relied on airborne and spaceborne systems such as satellites and aircraft. However, these types of platforms lack flexibility and their revisit time for a given area of interest is often large
[
2
]
. In this context, the recent growth of the drone industry has motivated the use of synthetic aperture radar onboard small and lightweight unmanned aerial vehicles, which is promising and challenging at the same time. On the one hand, synthetic aperture radar sensing benefits from the high flexibility and the easy deployment of small drones at relatively short distances from area of interests. Consequently, unmanned aerial vehicle-based synthetic aperture radar sensing, which is now possible at low cost, facilitates short revisit times and enables various new applications, such as the monitoring of highly dynamic environments. On the other hand, the drones’ limited hardware capacity is confronted with highly computationally complex synthetic aperture radar image processing algorithms
[
7
]
. This makes real-time synthetic aperture radar, which is an emerging synthetic aperture radar application
[
8
,
9
]
, difficult to realize onboard the flying platform. In this regard, one potential solution is to transmit the synthetic aperture radar raw data to the ground for processing, which requires a reliable communication link between the unmanned aerial vehicle and the ground
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. In real-time synthetic aperture radar, the radar raw data received for each sensing pulse is processed with minimum delay such that the synthetic aperture radar image frame can be generated as a function of the received synthetic aperture radar raw data. This facilitates immediate adjustments based on the processed synthetic aperture radar data. Moreover, unmanned aerial vehicle instability presents another challenge facing unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems. In fact, lightweight drones are vulnerable to external perturbations such as wind and atmospheric turbulence
[
13
]
. In this context, robust trajectory and resource allocation design with respect to unmanned aerial vehicle jittering were investigated for unmanned aerial vehicle-based communication systems
[
14
,
15
]
. Another challenge is the drones’ limited battery capacity
[
16
]
. Optimizing the unmanned aerial vehicle’s energy consumption requires a careful trajectory design as energy consumption and synthetic aperture radar sensing performance are tightly coupled.

In general, trajectory design and resource allocation for unmanned aerial vehicles have been exhaustively studied in the literature
[
17
,
18
]
and different optimization techniques ranging from classical optimization
[
19
]
to learning-based methods
[
20
,
21
]
have been used to tackle the problem. However, for the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar use case, trajectory design and resource allocation have not been comprehensively addressed. In fact, existing experimental works on unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems only investigate the practical deployment of the synthetic aperture radar sensors onboard small drones and report results of experimental measurement campaigns with intuitive pre-planned flight paths with fixed velocity and altitude
[
22
,
23
,
24
]
. Furthermore, existing experimental research works do not account for real-time synthetic aperture radar applications and are limited to offline image processing without considering a potential wireless communication link to the ground. Among the few works targeting the optimization of unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems, the authors in
[
25
]
jointly optimized a focus measure for the synthetic aperture radar images and the estimation of the platform trajectory. However, while the estimation error of the actual trajectory was minimized based on a nominal trajectory, the trajectory itself was not optimized in
[
25
]
, and communication with the ground was not considered. Furthermore, for a bistatic unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar system, the authors in
[
26
]
investigated trajectory optimization for a cellular-connected bistatic unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar system, where the area of interest was illuminated by a ground base station, and the energy consumption was minimized. Yet, the proposed solution is limited to bistatic systems with a stationary ground transmitter and a moving receiver, making it inapplicable for active monostatic systems, where both transmitter and receiver are mounted on the same moving platform. The authors in
[
27
,
28
]
investigated the use of a geosynchronous synthetic aperture radar system, where the area of interest was illuminated by a satellite and echoes were collected by a passive sensing unmanned aerial vehicle. For instance, in
[
27
]
, a multi-objective optimization problem for minimization of the distance traveled by the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar was formulated and solved. However, the trajectory design of passive bistatic unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems is fundamentally different from that of active monostatic unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems.

In this paper, we investigate the joint three-dimensional trajectory and resource allocation design for communication-assisted unmanned aerial vehicle-based synthetic aperture radar sensing. Different from other works, we focus on active monostatic synthetic aperture radar sensing, where the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar enables real-time synthetic aperture radar applications by transmitting the collected radar data instantaneously to the ground for processing. To this end, the unmanned aerial vehicle three-dimensional trajectory and resources are jointly optimized to maximize the radar ground coverage under communication, radar, and energy constraints. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

The joint three-dimensional trajectory and resource allocation algorithm design is formulated as a non-convex mixed-integer non-linear program. Our problem formulation takes into account the constraints imposed by both the radar and communication subsystems.

•

The unavoidable unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations are modeled based on their statistics and their effect on the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar ground radar coverage is unveiled.

•

Exploiting successive convex approximation (SCA), we propose a low-complexity sub-optimal solution for the formulated problem, which is robust with respect to unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations. Furthermore, based on monotonic optimization theory, we provide an upper bound on the optimal solution to assess the performance of the proposed sub-optimal algorithm.

•

Our simulation results show that the proposed sub-optimal algorithm achieves close-to-optimal performance. In addition, we demonstrate the superiority of the proposed solution in terms of radar coverage compared to several benchmark schemes and confirm its robustness against random trajectory deviations.

II
System Model

Fig. 1:
System model for unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar system with real-time data offloading to a GS.

We consider a rotary-wing unmanned aerial vehicle equipped with a side-looking radar system designed to perform synthetic aperture radar. The main mission of the unmanned aerial vehicle is to scan a certain area of interest on the ground. To enable live mission control of the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar system, real-time synthetic aperture radar processing is facilitated by transmitting the sensing data to a ground station in real-time.

This creates overlapping scans and allows covering the area of interest without gaps.

III-A
1
Cross-range error

The deviation in
y
-direction, denoted by
\bm{\Delta}_{y}=\mathbf{y}^{a}-\mathbf{y}=

[\Delta_{y}(1),...,\Delta_{y}(NM)]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{NM\times 1}
, has the least effect on the synthetic aperture radar ground coverage. Although this error can affect the synthetic aperture radar resolution by influencing the illumination time of a given reference point on the ground, it has no effect on the radar coverage thanks to the radar beam width in
y
-direction. In fact, the main idea behind synthetic aperture radar systems is to use this direction to create the synthetic aperture such that any reference point on the ground is illuminated multiple times from different angles along the
y
-axis
[

. Therefore, in what follows, we neglect the effect of this error on the radar coverage and focus on unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations in the
xz

III-A
2
In-range error

This error is caused by the unmanned aerial vehicle position deviation in
x
-direction and is denoted by vector
\bm{\Delta}_{x}=\mathbf{x}^{a}-\mathbf{x}=

[\Delta_{x}(1),...,\Delta_{x}(NM)]^{T}

\in

\mathbb{R}^{NM\times 1}
, see Figure

representing the intersection between the far-range slant distance line and the

3
b. Any deviation from the ideal unmanned aerial vehicle position in the

III-A
3
Altitude error

A deviation of the trajectory in
z
-direction, given by
\bm{\Delta}_{z}=\mathbf{z}^{a}-\mathbf{z}=[\Delta_{z}(1),...,\Delta_{z}(NM)]^{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{NM\times 1}

As shown in Figure

\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}(n)=\Delta_{x}(n)+c_{1}\Delta_{z}(n).

(20)

Similarly, in time slot

\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}(n)=\Delta_{x}(n)+c_{2}\Delta_{z}(n).

(21)

III-A
4
Trajectory deviation model

In this work, based on measurements in
[
35
,
36
]
, we propose to model the unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar trajectory deviations as independent Gaussian random variables such that, in each time slot
n
,
\Delta_{x}(n)\sim\mathcal{N}(o_{x},\sigma)
and
\Delta_{z}(n)\sim\mathcal{N}(o_{z},\sigma)
,
\forall n
. The standard deviation, denoted by
\sigma
, depends on the stability of the unmanned aerial vehicle in the presence of external perturbations caused, e.g., by adverse weather conditions. The offsets in
x
- and
z
-direction, denoted by
o_{x}
and
o_{z}
, respectively, represent a possible constant error between the unmanned aerial vehicle’s actual trajectory and its ideal intended trajectory and depend on the unmanned aerial vehicle localization accuracy. For the special case,

, the unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations reduce to random fluctuations around the ideal trajectory, also referred to as unmanned aerial vehicle jittering
[

III-B

Proposed Robust Trajectory Design

Both positive near-range errors, i.e.,
\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}(n)>0
, and negative far-range errors, i.e.,
\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}(n)<0
, result in uncovered areas, whereas the reverse result in an overlap between successive scans, see Figure
3
e. For robust trajectory design, uncovered areas are avoided by tolerating an overlap between adjacent azimuth scans. This can be achieved by shifting the actual footprint of the sensing beam such that the near-range and far-range errors are compensated. Since excessive overlap between scans reduces the synthetic aperture radar coverage, the overlap should be limited to the degree needed for compensation of unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations. To this end, we define the coverage reliability level
r
,
0\leq r\leq 1
, as the probability of having no gaps between successive synthetic aperture radar azimuth scans. The compensation that is required in the near range of the sensing beam footprint to achieve coverage reliability level
r
is denoted by
\bm{\delta}_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}^{r}=[\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{\scriptscriptstyle N}}^{r}(1),...,\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}^{r}(NM)]^{T}

\in\mathbb{R}^{NM\times 1}
, and is obtained as:

\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}^{r}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\argmin_{s}\Big\{|s|\Big|s\in\mathbb{R},\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{N}}(n)+s\leq 0\right)\geq r\Big\}.

(22)

Similarly, the far-range compensation, which we denote by
\bm{\delta}_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}^{r}=

[\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}^{r}(1),...,\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}^{r}(NM)]^{T}

\in\mathbb{R}^{NM\times 1}
, is obtained as:

\delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}^{r}(n)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{=}}\argmin_{s}\Big\{|s|\Big|s\in\mathbb{R},\mathbb{P}\left(\Delta_{\mathbf{p}_{F}}(n)+s\geq 0\right)\geq r\Big\}.

(23)

Note that, on the one hand, to avoid coverage holes, coverage reliability values close to 1 are desirable. On the other hand, if
r
is chosen too close to 1, this might result in a reduction of the total coverage, as a larger swath overlap is required to compensate for random trajectory deviations. Based on our simulations,
r=0.95
provides a good compromise between coverage reliability and total coverage.

IV
Robust Resource Allocation and Trajectory Optimization Framework

In this section, we formulate an optimization problem for joint three-dimensional trajectory and resource allocation design. The formulated problem is solved offline where random unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations are accounted for via the robust trajectory given in (
29
). In particular, for a given coverage reliability level
r
, we provide a low-complexity sub-optimal solution of the formulated problem based on SCA. Moreover, to assess the performance of the proposed sub-optimal scheme, we derive an upper bound for the optimal solution based on monotonic optimization theory
[
41
]
.

IV-A

Problem Formulation

Our objective is the maximization of the drone ground coverage
C
while satisfying the constraints imposed by the radar and communication subsystems. We assume Gaussian trajectory deviation as described in Section
III-A
. To achieve a coverage reliability level
r
, the unmanned aerial vehicle follows the robust trajectory
\mathbf{u}^{r}
provided in (
29
). We optimize the drone trajectory
\{\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x},N\}
and the power allocation
\{\mathbf{q},\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{com}}},\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{sar}}}\}
, where vectors
\mathbf{z}
,
\mathbf{x}
,
\mathbf{q}
,
\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{com}}}
, and
\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{sar}}}
, represent the collections of all
z(n),x(n),q(n),P_{\rm com}(n),
and
P_{\rm sar}(n),\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM}
, respectively, and

is the energy remaining in the drone’s battery in time slot
n
. To this end, the following optimization problem is formulated:

\displaystyle\!(\mathrm{P.1}):\max_{\mathbf{z},\mathbf{x},\mathbf{q},\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{sar}}},\mathbf{P_{\mathrm{com}}},N}C(\mathbf{u}^{r})

\displaystyle\text{s.t.}\hskip 5.69054pt\mathrm{C1-C4,}

\displaystyle\mathrm{C5:}\;P_{\mathrm{sar}}(n+1)=P_{\mathrm{sar}}(n),\forall n\in\mathcal{A},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C6}:\;z_{\mathrm{min}}\leq z^{r}(n)\leq z_{\mathrm{max}},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C7}:\mathrm{SNR}_{n}(\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{sar}},\mathbf{u}^{r})\geq\mathrm{SNR_{min}},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C8}:R_{n}(\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{com}},\mathbf{u}^{r})\geq R_{\mathrm{min},n}(\mathbf{u}^{r})+R_{\mathrm{sl}},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C9}:0\leq P_{\mathrm{sar}}(n)\leq P_{\mathrm{sar}}^{\mathrm{max}},0\leq P_{\mathrm{com}}(n)\leq P_{\mathrm{com}}^{\mathrm{max}},\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C10}:q(1)=q_{\mathrm{start}},\;q(n)\geq 0,\forall n\in\mathbb{N}_{NM},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C11}:q(n+1)=q(n)-E_{n}(\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{sar}},\mathbf{P}_{\mathrm{com}}),\forall n\in{\mathbb{N}_{NM-1}},

\displaystyle\mathrm{C12}:N\in\mathbb{N}.

Problem

is a non-convex mixed-integer non-linear program due to non-convex constraint

based on SCA.

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we investigated robust joint three-dimensional trajectory and resource allocation design for unmanned aerial vehicle-synthetic aperture radar systems, where real-time transmission of radar data to the ground was taken into account. Random unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations were modeled and their effect on the ground radar coverage was derived based on their statistics. Moreover, the robust three-dimensional trajectory and resource allocation design was formulated as a mixed-integer non-linear optimization problem for maximization of the unmanned aerial vehicle radar coverage. A low-complexity sub-optimal solution based on successive convex approximation was provided. To assess the performance of the proposed algorithm, we derived an upper bound for the optimal solution based on monotonic optimization theory. Simulation results confirmed that the proposed robust sub-optimal solution achieves close-to-optimal performance. The superior performance of the proposed scheme compared to three benchmark schemes was established and the robustness of the proposed solution with respect to unmanned aerial vehicle trajectory deviations was confirmed. Finally, we highlighted the impact of ground station placement and the power available for communication on unmanned aerial vehicle radar performance. In this paper, we assumed a deterministic line-of-sight link between the unmanned aerial vehicle and the ground station. An interesting topic for future work is the extension to other types of air-to-ground channels, including Ricean fading channels, based on online optimization techniques, see, e.g.,
[
39
]
. Furthermore, while we used classical optimization methods and focused on single-unmanned aerial vehicle synthetic aperture radar sensing, the extension to multi-unmanned aerial vehicle synthetic aperture radar sensing
[
44
]
and the exploration of learning-based methods for the trajectory design and resource allocation are promising research directions.

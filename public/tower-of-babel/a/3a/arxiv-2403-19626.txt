On the large interaction asymptotics of the free energy density of the Ising chain with disordered centered external field
Orphée Collin

Abstract
This article completes [ 6 ] by identifying explicitly the leading coefficient in the asymptotic development of the free energy density of the centered Random Field Ising Chain as the spin-spin interaction of the chain goes to infinity. The assumptions on the disorder law are much weaker than in [ 6 ] : only polynomial moments are required. Introduction

We denote by
\mathbb{N}:=\{1,2,\dots,\}
the set of all positive integers. Let
\mu
be a law on
\mathbb{R}
, with a finite first moment.
We consider, under a probability measure denoted
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
, an i.i.d. sequence
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of real random variables following law
\mu
.

For every realization of the sequence
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, for every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
and every
J\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we define the partition function of the Ising chain on
\{1,\dots,N\}
with external field
h
, boundary conditions
(a,b)
and spin-spin interaction
J
as:

Z_{N,h}^{a,b}(J):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}\\
\text{s.t. }\sigma_{0}=a,\,\sigma_{N}=b\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-2J\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sigma_{n}h_{n}\Big)\,.

(1)

We say that
configuration

\sigma
exhibits a
spin flip
at position
n
if
\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}
.

Using Kingman’s sub-additive ergodic theorem, it is straightforward to show the existence, for every
J\in\mathbb{R}
, of a real number
F_{\mu}(J)
such that for almost every realization of
h
(i.e.
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
-almost surely), for every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
, we have

\frac{1}{N}\log Z_{N,h}^{a,b}(J)\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}F_{\mu}(J)\,.

(2)

The number
F_{\mu}(J)
is called the (quenched) free energy density of the Random Field Ising Chain (RFIC) with disorder law
\mu
and interaction
J
. We are interested in the asymptotics of
F_{\mu}(J)
as
J\to\infty
.

From now on, we assume that
\mu
has a finite second moment, is centered and is different from
\delta_{0}
. We denote its variance by
\vartheta^{2}:=\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[h_{1}^{2}]
, with
\vartheta\in(0,\infty)
.
We will consider in particular the case of the centered Gaussian law with variance
\vartheta^{2}
, which we denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
.
The aim of this article is to show that

F_{{\mu}}(J)\sim\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}\,,\quad\text{ as }J\to\infty.

(3)

To prove (
3
) we will proceed in two steps. To begin with, we will cover the special case of disorder law
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
through the use of a continuum analogue of the RFIC, introduced as a weak disorder limit in
[
8
]
, and for which the free energy density is known exactly. Then we will show (
3
) under general integrability assumptions on
\mu
using a coarse-graining argument, together with the central limit theorem. We say that
\mu
admits finite exponential moments, if
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(th_{1})]<\infty
for all
t
in a neighbourhood of 0, and, for
p
positive real number, that
\mu
is in
L^{p}
- or has a
p
-th moment - if
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[|h_{1}|^{p}]<\infty
. We are going to show the following three theorems.

Theorem 1

(Gaussian disorder)
.

As
J\to\infty
,

F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J)=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{J^{2}}\right)\,.

(4)

Theorem 2

(Disorder with finite exponential moments)
.

If
\mu
admits finite exponential moments, then

F_{\mu}(J)=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{3}}}{J^{\frac{4}{3}}}\right)\,,\quad\text{as }J\to\infty\,.

(5)

Theorem 3

(Disorder with finite polynomial moments)
.

Let
p\in[3,\infty)
. If
\mu
is in
L^{p}
, then

\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{3}}}{J^{\frac{4}{3}}}\right)\leq F_{{\mu}}(J)\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{1}{J^{\frac{4p}{3p+2}}}\right)\,,\quad\text{as }J\to\infty\,.

(6)

Let
p\in[2,3)
. If
\mu
is in
L^{p}
, then

\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{p-1}{2p}}}{J^{\frac{2(p-1)}{p}}}\right)\leq F_{{\mu}}(J)\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+O\left(\frac{1}{J^{\frac{2p(p-1)}{p^{2}+p-1}}}\right)\,,\quad\text{as }J\to\infty\,.

(7)

In particular, if
\mu
is in
L^{p}
for some
p>\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\approx 2.618
, then the error terms are
o\left(\frac{1}{J}\right)
and (
3
) holds.

Often, in statistical physics, properties concerning the typical configurations of the system can be extracted from the free energy density. We are now going to discuss some general properties of the free energy density of the RFIC, and use our results to derive bounds on the mean density of spin changes in the RFIC in the thermodynamic limit.

Let us denote by
P^{a,b}_{N,h,J}
the RFIC measure, i.e., the Gibbs measure on
\{-1,+1\}^{N}
associated to the partition function
Z^{a,b}_{N,h}(J)
. A classical computation yields expressions for the expectation and the variance of the density of spin changes under the RFIC measure:

E^{a,b}_{N,h,J}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}\right]=-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{J}\left(\frac{1}{N}\log Z^{a,b}_{N,h}(J)\right)\,.

(8)

and

\mathrm{Var}^{a,b}_{N,h,J}\left[\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}\right]=\frac{1}{4N}\partial_{J}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{N}\log Z^{a,b}_{N,h}(J)\right)\,.

(9)

Since the left-hand sides are non-negative, we see that
J\mapsto\frac{1}{N}\log Z^{a,b}_{N,h}(J)
is non-increasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}
. Taking the
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
-almost sure limit, as
N\to\infty
, we obtain that also
F_{\mu}
is non-increasing and convex on
\mathbb{R}
. We mention also that
F_{\mu}
is non-negative: this can be seen by lower bounding the partition function by the contribution given in (
1
) by
\sigma=(a,b,b,\dots,b,b)
; the resulting bound goes to 0 as
N\to\infty
by the law of large numbers.

Furthermore, the two-by-two matrix
(Z_{N,h}^{a,b}(J))_{(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}}
can be expressed (see
[
1
]
) as a product of i.i.d. random matrices, distributed under a law determined by
\mu
. Consequently, Furstenberg’s theory can be invoked: the free energy density
F_{\mu}(J)
is the top Lyapunov exponent of this random matrix product and has an integral expression involving the invariant measure of a certain Markov chain on
(0,\infty)
. Then, using results by D. Ruelle (see
[
11
]
, or also
[
4
]
),
F_{\mu}
is analytic on
(0,\infty)
.

As a consequence of convexity and twice differentiability, we can take the limit
N\to\infty
in (
8
) and (
9
). We see that the variance goes to 0 as
N\to\infty
, so we obtain, for all
J>0
and all
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
,
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
-almost surely, the convergence

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}\underset{N\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}_{{\mu}}(J)\,

(10)

in
P^{a,b}_{N,h,J}
-probability.

From our main theorems, we derive the following asymptotic results on the limiting quantity appearing in (
10
).

Corollary 4
.

As
J\to\infty
,

-\frac{1}{2}F^{\prime}_{\mu}(J)=\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{4J^{2}}+O(\varepsilon_{J})\,,

(11)

where
\varepsilon_{J}
is equal,

•

in the case of Gaussian disorder, to
\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{4}}}{J^{\frac{5}{2}}}
,

•

in the case of exponential integrability, to
\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{6}}}{J^{\frac{13}{6}}}
,

•

and, in the case of polynomial integrability, denoting by
\eta_{p}
the exponent in the upper bound in Theorem
3
, to
\frac{1}{J^{\frac{3+\eta_{p}}{2}}}
for
p\in(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2},\infty)
and to
\frac{1}{J^{\eta_{p}+1}}
for
p\in[2,\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}]
.

Proof.

By convexity, for every
J>0
and every
a\in(0,J]
, we have

\frac{F_{{\mu}}(J)-F_{{\mu}}(J-a)}{a}\leq F^{\prime}_{{\mu}}(J)\leq\frac{F_{{\mu}}(J+a)-F_{{\mu}}(J)}{a}\,.

(12)

In the Gaussian case, apply this with
a=J^{\frac{1}{2}}(\log J)^{\frac{1}{4}}
to derive (
11
) from Theorem
1
. In the case of exponential integrability, apply it instead with
a=J^{\frac{5}{6}}(\log J)^{\frac{1}{6}}
to derive (
11
) from Theorem
2
. Finally, in the case of polynomial integrability, when
\eta_{p}>1
, take
a=J^{\frac{3-\eta_{p}}{2}}
to derive (
11
) from Theorem
3
, and when
\eta_{p}\leq 1
, recall that
F_{\mu}^{\prime}
is non-positive, and use the lower bound in (
12
) with
a=J/2
and the non-negativity of
F_{\mu}
.
∎

Let us now discuss the links with the existing literature.
In
[
9
]
, the free energy density
F_{\mu}(J)
is computed exactly for two specific choices of the law
{\mu}
.
In
[
6
]
, under the assumptions that
{\mu}
admits finite exponential moments, that
{\mu}
has a density with respect to the Lebesgue measure and that this density is
uniformly Hölder continuous, a much more detailed asymptotic development for
F_{{\mu}}(J)
is obtained, precisely:

F_{{\mu}}(J)=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{2J+\kappa_{2}}+O\left(\exp(-cJ)\right),\quad\text{ as }J\to\infty,

(13)

with constants
\kappa_{1}\in(0,\infty)
,
\kappa_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
and
c\in(0,\infty)
depending on
\mu
.
A formula appears in
[
6
]
for the value of
\kappa_{1}
, but it is not very explicit: it is in terms of an integral involving the invariant measure of a certain Markov chain on
(0,\infty)
, see equation (3.12) in
[
6
]
.
In this perspective, the contribution of the present work is to show that
\kappa_{1}
is simply the variance of
{\mu}
, which comes as a surprise: one would rather only expect that it is close to the variance
asymptotically
(in the weak disorder limit, i.e., when the variance goes to 0). We mention that in
[
9
]
the leading coefficient is not recognized as being the variance of
{\mu}
. Finally, we stress that our result holds in a much more general framework than that of
[
6
]
, regarding the assumptions required on
{\mu}
.

A description of the typical configurations of the RFIC was proposed in the physical literature, by D. Fisher, P. Le Doussal and C. Monthus, in
[
5
]
. The typical configurations are conjectured to be
close
to a given configuration which we call
the Fisher configuration
, obtained through a renormalization procedure. This configuration is determined by the disorder sequence
h
and has a density of spin changes which is equivalent to
\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{4J^{2}}
as
J\to\infty
. For more details on this, see
[
3
]
, where an estimation of the discrepancy density between typical configurations and the Fisher configuration is obtained. Our present results concerning the free energy density and the mean density of spin changes are in accordance with the predictions of Fisher, and are beyond the reach of
[
3
]
.
In the present article, we do not make use of Fisher’s approach. Let us however mention that it is possible to use the Fisher configuration to derive the lower bound
F_{\mu}(J)\geq\frac{\vartheta^{2}+o(1)}{2J}
, as
J\to\infty
: simply by lower bounding the partition function by the contribution given only by the Fisher configuration, whose spin domains are well-understood, at least to first order, see Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
.

2
Comparison of the discrete partition function with Gaussian disorder with the continuum partition function

In this section, we are going to prove Theorem
1
. In order to do so, we turn to the continuous analogue of the model. The continuum RFIC was introduced in
[
8
]
at the level of the free energy as a weak disorder limit of the discrete RFIC. The partition function and the continuum model themselves have been introduced in
[
2
]
. We now present the definitions of the partition function and of the (quenched) free energy density.

Consider, under a probability
\mathbb{P}
, a standard Brownian motion
B=(B_{t})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
.
For every
J\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in\{-1,+1\}
, denote by
\mathbf{P}_{J}^{a}
the law of a Poisson process
(s_{t})_{t\geq 0}
taking values in
\{-1,1\}
, with
s_{0}=a
and jump rate equal to
\exp(-2J)
, and denote by
\mathbf{E}_{J}^{a}
the associated expectation. Then, for every realization of
B
, every
\mathcalb{l}\geq 0
, every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
and every
J\geq 0
, we define the partition function of the continuum Ising chain on
[0,\mathcalb{l}]
with external field
B
, boundary conditions
(a,b)
and interaction
J
as:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalb{l},B}^{a,b}(J):=\exp(\mathcalb{l}e^{-2J})\;\mathbf{E}_{J}^{a}\left[\exp\left(\int_{0}{l}s_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{s{l}=b\}}\right]\,.

(14)

For almost every realization of
B
, i.e.
\mathbb{P}
-almost surely, for every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
, we have (see
[
1
]
together with Lemma 5.1 of
[
2
]
):

\frac{1}{\mathcalb{l}}\log{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalb{l},J,B}^{a,b}(J)\underset{\mathcalb{l}\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}{\mathcal{F}}(J)\,,

(15)

where
{\mathcal{F}}(J)
is called the (quenched) free energy density of the continuum RFIC with interaction
J
, and:

{\mathcal{F}}(J):=\frac{e^{-2J}K_{-1}(e^{-2J})}{K_{0}(e^{-2J})}\sim\frac{1}{2J+\log 2+\gamma_{EM}}+O(e^{-4J}),\quad\text{ as }J\to\infty.

(16)

In this expression,
K_{-1}
and
K_{0}
are the modified Bessel functions of second kind of index respectively -1 and 0, and
\gamma_{EM}
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant. Only the developement
{\mathcal{F}}(J)=\frac{1}{2J}+O(\frac{1}{J^{2}})
is relevant for our purpose.

For the sake of simplicity, we choose to prove for now Theorem
1
when
\vartheta=1
. Remark
8
will be devoted to generalizing the proof to any
\vartheta\in(0,\infty)
.

We are going to compare the partition function of the discrete Ising chain with disorder law
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
with the continuum partition function we just introduced.
This comparison is based on the following heuristic. Splitting
\mathbb{R}^{+}
into the segments
[n-1,n],n\in\mathbb{N}
, we observe that:

•

inside each segment, the Poisson parameter of the number of jumps is
e^{-2J}
,

•

the disorder energies over the segments, i.e., the variables
B_{n}-B_{n-1}
, are i.i.d. and distributed under
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
.

Thus, it seems reasonable to modify the configurations by artificially moving the positions of the spin changes to their integer part (this means slightly modifiying the energy
\int_{0}{l}s_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}
collected): then, we see the
discrete
partition function associated to the sequence
(B_{n}-B_{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
appear. Keeping in mind that we expect the mean number of jumps to be of order
\frac{1}{J^{2}}
, it seems likely that the error concerning the collected disorder energy is not too big, and that this method should yield the desired estimate.

First, we need to slightly generalize the definition of this continuum partition function. For every real numbers
0\leq\mathcalb{l}\leq\mathcalc{r}
, we set:

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalb{l},\mathcalc{r},B}^{a,b}(J)

\displaystyle:=\exp((\mathcalc{r}-\mathcalb{l})e^{-2J})\mathbf{E}_{J}^{a}\left[\exp\left(\int{l}{r}s_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{s{r}=b\}}\Big|s{l}=a\right]\,.

(17)

Observe that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalb{l},B}^{a,b}(J)={\mathcal{Z}}_{0,\mathcalb{l},B}^{a,b}(J)
and that
{\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalb{l},\mathcalc{r},B}^{a,b}(J)={\mathcal{Z}}_{\mathcalc{r}-\mathcalb{l},\Theta{l}B}^{a,b}(J)
if we set
\Theta{l}B:=(B_{\mathcalb{l}+t}-B_{\mathcalb{l}})_{t\in[0,\infty)}
.

Following our heuristic, we rewrite the continuum partition function defined in (
14
) in the following way. Take
N
a positive integer and to any (cadlag) trajectory
(s_{t})_{0\leq t\leq N}\in\{-1,1\}^{[0,N]}
, associate the configuration
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}
defined by:

\sigma_{k}=s_{k},\qquad k=0,\dots,N.

To each
\sigma
corresponds a set of trajectories
(s_{t})_{0\leq t\leq N}
, we rewrite the expectation in (
14
) according to this partitioning to obtain:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{N,B}^{a,b}(J)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}\\
\text{s.t. }\sigma_{0}=a,\,\sigma_{N}=b\end{subarray}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}{\mathcal{Z}}_{n-1,n,B}^{\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n}}(J)\,.

(18)

We define the sequence
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by setting
h_{n}:=B_{n}-B_{n-1}
. As already stressed,
h
is i.i.d with law
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
. Our aim is to compare the above sum with:

Z_{N,h}^{a,b}(J)=\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}:\sigma_{0}=a,\sigma_{N}=b}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\exp(-2J{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n-1}\neq\sigma_{n}\}}+\sigma_{n}h_{n})\,.

(19)

so naturally we are going to compare the continuum partition function on the
block

[n-1,n]
, i.e
{\mathcal{Z}}_{n-1,n,B}^{\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n}}(J)
, with
\exp(-2J{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n-1}\neq\sigma_{n}\}}+\sigma_{n}h_{n})
. This comparison will be provided by the next lemma, that without loss of generality we formulate only for the first block
[0,1]
.
We introduce the random variable

H:=\max_{0\leq s\leq t\leq 1}|B_{s}-B_{t}|\,.

(20)

Lemma 5
.

For every realization of
B
, for every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
, for every
J\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
, we have

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)\geq\exp\left(-2(J+M){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bB_{1}-2(H-M)_{+}\right)\,,

(21)

and

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)\leq\exp\left(-2(J-M){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bB_{1}+2(H-M)_{+}+e^{2(H-J)}\right)\,.

(22)

Proof.

The proof is based on the following observation: for any (cadlag) trajectory
(s_{t})_{0\leq t\leq 1}\in\{-1,1\}^{[0,1]}
satisfying
s_{0}=a
and
s_{1}=b
, denoting by
j
its number of jumps on
[0,1]
, we have

\left|\int_{0}^{1}s_{t}\mathrm{d}B_{t}-bB_{1}\right|\leq 2jH\,.

(23)

We first handle the lower bound. If
a=b
(respectively, if
a\neq b
), we lower bound the partition function
{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)
by restricting the expectation
\mathbf{E}_{J}^{a}
to the set of trajectories with no jump in
[0,1]
(respectively with exactly one jump in
[0,1]
).
Recalling the factor in front of the expectation in (
14
), we obtain:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)\geq\exp(-2(J+H){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bB_{1})\,.

(24)

Using
H\leq M+(H-M)_{+}
we derive the desired lower bound.

We now turn to the proof of the upper bound. Using the observation (
23
), we have:

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)\leq\sum_{j}\frac{(e^{-2J})^{j}}{j!}\exp({bB_{1}+2jH})\,,

(25)

where the sum is over non-negative even integers if
a=b
and over positive odd integers if
a\neq b
. In the first case, let us bound the sum by the sum over all non-negative integers, in the second case let us bound by the sum over all positive integers, and use
j!\geq(j-1)!
. We obtain :

{\mathcal{Z}}_{1,B}^{a,b}(J)\leq\exp\left(bB_{1}-2(J-H){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+e^{2(H-J)}\right)\,.

(26)

Using
H\leq M+(H-M)_{+}
we see that the upper bound in the lemma follows.
∎

From Lemma
5
, we derive the following lemma concerning the free energy densities.

Lemma 6
.

For every
M\geq 0
and
J\geq 0
, we have:

{\mathcal{F}}(J)\geq F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}(J+M)-\mathbb{E}[2(H-M)_{+}].

(27)

and

{\mathcal{F}}(J)\leq F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}(J-M)+e^{2(M-J)}+\mathbb{E}\left[2(H-M)_{+}+e^{2(H-J)}\right].

(28)

Replacing
J
by
J-M
(respectively
J+M
), Lemma
6
immediately gives the following corollary.

Corollary 7
.

For every
M\geq 0
and
J\geq 0
, we have:

F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}(J)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(J-M)+\mathbb{E}[2(H-M)_{+}],

(29)

and

F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}(J)\geq{\mathcal{F}}(J+M)-e^{-2J}-\mathbb{E}\left[\left(2(H-M)+e^{2(H-M-J)}\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{H>M\}}\right].

(30)

Proof of Lemma
6
.

Recall the observation that, under
\mathbb{P}
, sequence
h=(B_{n}-B_{n-1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. with law
{\mathcal{N}}_{1}
.
The lower bound is obtained using (
21
) in (
18
), taking the
\log
, dividing by
N
and finally sending
N
to infinity and using (
15
) and the law of large numbers.
The upper bound is obtained similarly, using (
22
) instead of (
21
).
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1
when
\vartheta=1
: it is just a matter of making a choice for
M
, depending on
J
, and controlling the error terms appearing in Corollary
7
.

Proof of Theorem
1
when
\vartheta=1
.

We are going to use the following fact:

\mathbb{P}[H\geq\lambda]=O\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}\right)\right)\,,\qquad\text{as }\lambda\to\infty\,.

(31)

This can be shown by noticing that
H\geq\lambda
implies that
\max_{[0,1]}B_{\cdot}\geq\frac{\lambda}{2}
or
\min_{[0,1]}B_{\cdot}\leq-\frac{\lambda}{2}
and by using the fact that
\max_{[0,1]}B_{\cdot}
and
-\min_{[0,1]}B_{\cdot}
are distributed as the absolute values of standard Gaussian variables (by the reflection principle).
Equation (
31
) yields in particular that
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2H}\right]
and
\mathbb{E}[H^{2}]
are finite.

For every
J\geq 1
, let us set
M_{J}:=6(\log J)^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Then we have
\mathbb{E}\left[e^{2(H-M_{J}-J)}\right]=O(e^{-2J})
,
and, using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\mathbb{E}\left[(H-M_{J})_{+}]\leq\mathbb{E}[H{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H>M_{J}\}}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}[H^{2}]^{\frac{1}{2}}\mathbb{P}[H>M_{J}]^{\frac{1}{2}}=O(J^{-2})\,.

(32)

Thus, Corollary
7
yields:

{\mathcal{F}}(J+M_{J})+O(J^{-2})\leq F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{1}}(J)\leq{\mathcal{F}}(J-M_{J})+O(J^{-2})\,.

(33)

Recalling (
16
), this concludes the proof of Theorem
1
in the case where
\vartheta=1
.
∎

Remark 8
.

To conclude the present section, we present a way to adapt it to prove Theorem
1
for any
\vartheta\in(0,\infty)
.
It consists in replacing the Browian trajectory
B
by
\vartheta B
. The variable
H
is thus replaced by
\vartheta H
. In Lemma
6
and in Corollary
7
,
{\mathcal{F}}
is replaced by
{\mathcal{F}}_{\vartheta}
, the free energy density of the continuum RFIC with external field
\vartheta B
(instead of
B
, see (
14
) and (
15
)). Using Brownian scaling and Poisson scaling, we see that for all
J\in\mathbb{R}
:

{\mathcal{F}}_{\vartheta}(J)=\vartheta^{2}{\mathcal{F}}(J+\log\vartheta).

(34)

The proof of Theorem 1 can then be conducted
mutatis mutandis
.

3
Gaussian approximation of the discrete RFIC partition function

Our aim is to compare the free energy density of the RFIC with disorder distributions
\mu
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
, in order to derive Theorems
2
and
3
from Theorem
1
. We first state a lemma which bounds the difference between the free energy densities for different disorder laws, with respect to their Wasserstein 1-distance.

Lemma 9
.

Consider
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
, two laws on
\mathbb{R}
. Then, for all
J\geq 0
,

|F_{\nu}(J)-F_{\nu^{\prime}}(J)|\leq W_{1}(\nu,\nu^{\prime}),

where
W_{1}(\cdot,\cdot)
is the Wasserstein 1-distance, i.e.:

W_{1}(\nu,\nu^{\prime})=\inf_{(h,h^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}[|h-h^{\prime}|].

with the infimum running over all couplings
(h,h^{\prime})
of
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let us consider a coupling of
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
and two sequences of random variables
h=(h_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
h^{\prime}=(h_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that the sequence
((h_{n},h_{n}^{\prime}))_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is i.i.d. and such that
(h_{1},h_{1}^{\prime})
is distributed according to the considered coupling.
Then, using
\sigma_{n}h_{n}\leq\sigma_{n}h_{n}^{\prime}+|h_{n}-h_{n}^{\prime}|
, we have:

Z_{N,h}(J)\leq Z_{N,h^{\prime}}(J)\exp\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}|h_{n}-h_{n}^{\prime}|\right).

Taking the
\log
, dividing by
N
and sending
N
to infinity, (
2
) yields that:

F_{\nu}(J)\leq F_{\nu^{\prime}}(J)+\mathbb{E}[|h_{1}-h_{1}^{\prime}|],

using the law of large numbers for the last term. The lemma follows via a minimization over the couplings of
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
, and by exchanging the roles of
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
.
∎

Since this lemma provides a bound which is uniform in
J
, it seems useless for our purpose. It will however become crucial once we have introduced the main idea of the present section, which is to make a
coarse graining of the Ising chain
. This coarse-graining bears some similarities with the procedure used in the previous section to link the continuum RFIC to the Gaussian disorder discrete RFIC. It is also reminiscent of the block spin transform in the context of the renormalization group approach. We pick
L\in\mathbb{N}
. For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we “rigidify” the RFIC on
\{1,\dots,NL\}
by restricting it to configurations which exhibit spin changes only at locations
nL,n=1,\dots,N
. Then the collection
(\sigma_{nL})_{1\leq n\leq L}
is itself distributed as a RFIC on
\{1,.\dots,N\}
with disorder sequence
h^{L}=(h_{n}^{L})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by
h_{n}^{L}:=\sum_{i=(n-1)L+1}^{nL}h_{i}
. We finally observe that this sequence is i.i.d., we denote its law by
\mu^{\ast L}
, it is the law of the sum of
L
independent variables with law
\mu
.
This coarse-graining approach will yield comparison estimates on the free energy densities
F_{\mu}
and
F_{\mu^{\ast L}}
, see Lemma
12
. The lower bound is straight-forward to obtain, while the upper bound will require some work.

With these estimates at hand, the proofs of Theorems
2
and
3
will informally consist in the following approximations. We will let
L
depend on
J
and show, as
J\to\infty
that:

F_{{\mu}}(J)\approx\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J)\\
\approx\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}}}(J)\approx F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J)\approx\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}\,.

(35)

The first and third approximations will be given by our coarse-graining estimates, while for the fourth one we will use Theorem
1
. The second one will be provided by Lemma
9
together with the following lemma, which is a version of the central limit theorem with respect to the Wasserstein-1 distance.

Lemma 10
.

If
{\mu}
has a finite third moment, then the sequence
\left(W_{1}\left(\mu^{\ast L},\mathcal{N}_{L\vartheta^{2}}\right)\right)_{L\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}
is bounded.
If
{\mu}
has a finite
p
-th moment, with
p\in[2,3)
, then, as
L\to\infty
,

W_{1}\left(\mu^{\ast L},\mathcal{N}_{L\vartheta^{2}}\right)=O\left(L^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\right)\,.

(36)

Proof.

This is equivalent to showing that the Wasserstein 1-distance between the law of
(h_{1}+\dots+h_{n})/\sqrt{L}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
is
O(L^{-\frac{1}{2}})
, respectively
O(L^{1-\frac{p}{2}})
.
This is a known result, see
[
10
]
for example.
∎

Lemmas
9
and
10
yield that the error in the second approximation in (
35
) is
O(\frac{1}{L})
, respectively
O(\frac{1}{L^{\frac{p-1}{2}}})
. How large an
L
we will take will depend on the error terms coming from the coarse-graining estimates, which we present now.

3.1
Coarse-graining of the Ising chain

As annouced, in the present section, we pick
L\in\mathbb{N}
and, denoting by
{\mu}^{\ast L}
the law of
h_{1}+\dots+h_{L}
(recall that the
h_{n}
’s are i.i.d with law
{\mu}
), we will compare the free energy density
F_{\mu}
with the free energy density
F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}
by performing a “coarse-graining” of the Ising chain.

We need to slightly generalize the notation in (
1
). We introduce for positive integers
\mathcalb{l}
and
\mathcalc{r}
satisfying
\mathcalb{l}\leq\mathcalc{r}
the partition function on
\{\mathcalb{l},\mathcalb{l}+1,\dots,\mathcalc{r}\}
:

Z_{\mathcalb{l},\mathcalc{r},h}^{a,b}(J):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{\mathcalb{l}-1,\mathcalb{l},\dots,\mathcalc{r}\}}\\
\text{s.t. }\sigma_{\mathcalb{l}-1}=a,\,\sigma{r}=b\end{subarray}}\exp\Big(-2J\sum_{n=\mathcalb{l}}^{\mathcalc{r}}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}+\sum_{n=\mathcalb{l}}^{\mathcalc{r}}\sigma_{n}h_{n}\Big)\,.

(37)

Note that
Z_{N,h}^{a,b}(J)=Z_{1,N,h}^{a,b}(J)
and that
Z_{\mathcalb{l},\mathcalc{r},h}^{a,b}(J)=Z_{\mathcalc{r}-\mathcalb{l}+1,\Theta^{\mathcalb{l}-1}h}^{a,b}(J)
if we define
\Theta^{\mathcalb{l}-1}h
as the sequence
(h_{\mathcalb{l}-1+n}-h_{\mathcalb{l}-1})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.

The coarse-graining idea goes as follows: we define a sequence
h^{L}=(h^{L}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
by setting
h^{L}_{n}:=\sum_{i=(n-1)L+1}^{nL}h_{i}
. As already stressed, the sequence
h^{L}
is i.i.d. with law
{\mu}^{\ast L}
. For all
N\in\mathbb{N}
, we associate to any configuration
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,NL\}}
the configuration
\sigma^{L}\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}
defined by:

\sigma^{L}_{n}=\sigma_{nL},\qquad n=0,\dots,N.

(38)

Multiple configurations
\sigma
are sent to the same configuration
\sigma^{L}
, we rewrite the summation in (
1
) according to this partitioning and we obtain:

Z_{NL,h}^{a,b}(J)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}\\
\text{s.t. }\sigma_{0}=a,\,\sigma_{N}=b\end{subarray}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}Z_{(n-1)L+1,nL,h}^{\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n}}(J)\,.

(39)

Our aim is to compare this expression with

Z_{N,h^{L}}^{a,b}(J)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,N\}}\\
\text{s.t. }\sigma_{0}=a,\,\sigma_{N}=b\end{subarray}}\prod_{n=1}^{N}\exp(-2J{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n-1}\neq\sigma_{n}\}}+\sigma_{n}h_{n}^{L})\,,

(40)

so naturally we now want to compare the partition function on the
block

\{(n-1)L+1,\dots,nL\}
, i.e
Z_{(n-1)L+1,nL,h}^{\sigma_{n-1},\sigma_{n}}(J)
, with
\exp(-2J{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n-1}\neq\sigma_{n}\}}+\sigma_{n}h_{n}^{L})
. This will be provided by the following lemma, that without loss of generality we formulate only for the first block
\{1,\dots,L\}
.
We introduce the random variable

H_{L}:=\max_{1\leq n\leq m\leq L}\Big|\sum_{i=n}^{m}h_{i}\Big|\,.

(41)

Lemma 11
.

For every realization of
h
, for every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
, for every
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
, we have

Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)\geq\exp\left(-2J{\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bh_{1}^{L}\right)\,,

(42)

and

\displaystyle Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg[-2\left(J\!-\!M\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log\!L\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bh_{1}^{L}

(43)

\displaystyle+L\left(4(H_{L}-M)_{+}+e^{2(M-J)}\right)\Bigg]\,.

Proof.

For the lower bound, we simply lower bound the sum defining
Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)
in (
14
) by the term corresponding to
\sigma=(a,b,b,\dots,b)
.

We now turn to the upper bound. For given
\sigma\in\{-1,+1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,L\}}
such that
\sigma_{0}=a,\sigma_{L}=b
, denoting
j=\sum_{n=1}^{L}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{\sigma_{n}\neq\sigma_{n-1}\}}
the number of spin flips in
\sigma
, we observe that:

\sum_{n=1}^{L}\sigma_{n}h_{n}\leq bh^{L}_{1}+2jH_{L}

(44)

Furthermore, there are at most
\binom{L}{j}
configurations in
\{-1,+1\}^{\{0,1,\dots,L\}}
having
j
spin flips, with the convention
\binom{L}{j}=0
if
j>L
.
Therefore:

Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)\leq\sum_{j}\binom{L}{j}\exp(-2jJ+bh_{1}^{L}+2jH_{L})\,,

(45)

where the sum is over all non-negative even integers if
a=b
, over all positive odd integers if
a\neq b
. In the first case, let us upper bound the sum by the sum over all non-negative integers and in the second case let us bound it by the sum over all positive integers and use
\binom{L}{j}\leq L\binom{L}{j-1}
. Using the binomial formula, we get:

Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)\leq\exp\left(bh_{1}^{L}-2(J-H_{L}-\frac{1}{2}\log L){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}\right)\times(1+e^{2(H_{L}-J)})^{L}

(46)

We use
H_{L}\leq M+(H_{L}-M)_{+}
to bound
H_{L}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}
and we use it again to get:

1+e^{2(H_{L}-J)}\leq e^{2(H_{L}-M)_{+}}(1+e^{2(M-J)})

(47)

Using the fact that
1+x\leq e^{x}
for any real number
x
, we get:

Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)\leq\exp\left[-2\left(\!J\!-\!M\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log\!L\!\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bh_{1}^{L}+2(L+1)(H_{L}-M)_{+}+Le^{2(M-J)}\right]\,.

(48)

We bound
L+1
by
2L
to conclude the proof of the upper bound.
∎

From Lemma
11
, we derive the following lemma concerning the free energy densities.

Lemma 12
.

For every
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
, we have:

F_{{\mu}}(J)\geq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J).

(49)

and

F_{{\mu}}(J)\leq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J\!-\!M\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log L)+4\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]+e^{2(M-J)}\,.

(50)

Proof.

The lower bound is immediate, using (
49
) in (
39
), taking the
\log
, dividing by
NL
and finally sending
N
to infinity with the help of (
2
).
For the upper bound, we do the same, using (
50
) instead of (
49
) and using in addition the law of large numbers.
∎

Replacing
J
by
J\!+\!M\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log L
, Lemma
12
yields the following corollary.

Corollary 13
.

For every
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
,

\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J)\geq F_{{\mu}}(J\!+\!M\!+\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log L)-4\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]-\frac{e^{-2J}}{L}\,.

(51)

Remark 14
.

In the proofs of Theorem
2
of
3
, we will take
L
depending on
J
and large (as
J
goes to infinity). We will also let
M
depend on
J
, typically larger than scale
\sqrt{L}
, so as to make the event
\{H_{L}>M\}
unlikely,
but also smaller than scale
J
so that the bounds remain interesting for our purpose.

3.2
Proof of Theorem
2

Lemma 15
.

Assume that
\mu
admits some finite exponential moments and recall that it is centered with variance denoted
\vartheta^{2}
. Then, there exists a positive constant
c
such that, for all
L\in\mathbb{N}
and all
M>0
satisfying
M\leq cL
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[(H_{L}-M)_{+}]\leq\frac{4\vartheta^{2}L^{3}}{M}\exp\left(-\frac{M^{2}}{4\vartheta^{2}L}\right)\,.

(52)

Proof.

Let us denote by
\varphi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R},t\mapsto\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(th_{1})]
the moment generating function of
\mu
. Developing it around 0, we see that there exists a positive constant
c^{\prime}
such that:

\forall t\in[-c^{\prime},c^{\prime}]\,,\quad\varphi(t)\leq\exp(\vartheta^{2}t^{2})\,.

(53)

We pick any
t\in(0,c^{\prime})
. Recalling the definition of
H_{L}
in (
41
), we observe that:

\exp(tH_{L})\leq\sum_{1\leq n\leq m\leq L}\exp\left(\sum_{i=n}^{m}h_{i}\right)+\exp\left(-\sum_{i=n}^{m}h_{i}\right)

(54)

which, by taking the expectation under
\mathbb{P}_{\mu}
and using (
53
), yields:

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[\exp(tH_{L})]\leq\sum_{1\leq n\leq m\leq L}\varphi(t)^{(m-n+1)}+\varphi(-t)^{(m-n+1)}\leq 2L^{2}\exp(\vartheta^{2}t^{2}L)\,.

(55)

Using the fact that
(H_{L}-M)_{+}\leq\frac{1}{t}\exp(t(H_{L}-M))
, we obtain:

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[(H_{L}-M)_{+}]\leq\frac{2L^{2}}{t}\exp(\vartheta^{2}t^{2}L-Mt)\,.

(56)

Now, we see that the lemma holds with
c:=2\vartheta^{2}c^{\prime}
. Indeed, when
M\leq cL
, take
t=\frac{M}{2\vartheta^{2}L}
, which is not larger than
c^{\prime}
, to derive (
52
) from (
56
).
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
2
.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

In this proof, we use
{\widetilde{M}}
as a short-hand for
M+\frac{1}{2}\log L
.
Using the lower bound in Lemma
12
for law
\mu
, then Lemma
9
and finally Corollary

for law
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
, we derive a lower bound on
F_{{\mu}}(J)
:

\displaystyle F_{{\mu}}(J)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J)

(57)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}}}(J)-\frac{1}{L}W_{1}({\mu}^{\ast L},{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}})

\displaystyle\geq F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J+{\widetilde{M}})-4\mathbb{E}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]-\frac{e^{-2J}}{L}-\frac{1}{L}W_{1}({\mu}^{\ast L},{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}})\,.

Recall Theorem
1
. Using the fact that
\frac{1}{1+x}\geq 1-x
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, we have

F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J+{\widetilde{M}})\geq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}-\frac{\vartheta^{2}{\widetilde{M}}}{2J^{2}}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{J^{2}}\right)\,.

(58)

Similarly, we derive an upper bound on
F_{{\mu}}(J)
.
Applying the upper bound in Lemma
12
for law
\mu
, then Lemma
9
and finally the lower bound in Lemma
12
for law
{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}
, we have:

\displaystyle F_{{\mu}}(J)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mu}^{\ast L}}(J-{\widetilde{M}})+4\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]+e^{2(M-J)}

(59)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{L}F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}}}(J-{\widetilde{M}})+\frac{1}{L}W_{1}({\mu}^{\ast L},{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}})+4\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]+e^{2(M-J)}

\displaystyle\leq F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J-{\widetilde{M}})+\frac{1}{L}W_{1}({\mu}^{\ast L},{\mathcal{N}}_{L\vartheta^{2}})+4\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]+e^{2(M-J)}\,.

Recall Theorem
1
. We assume that
{\widetilde{M}}\leq\frac{J}{2}

\frac{1}{1-x}\leq 1+2x
for all

, we have

F_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}(J-{\widetilde{M}})\leq\frac{\vartheta^{2}}{2J}+\frac{\vartheta^{2}{\widetilde{M}}}{J^{2}}+O\left(\frac{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{J^{2}}\right)\,.

(60)

Now, we make a choice for

and

.
We set, for

:

L_{J}=\left\lfloor\frac{J^{\frac{4}{3}}}{(\log J)^{\frac{1}{3}}}\right\rfloor,\qquad M_{J}=6\vartheta J^{\frac{2}{3}}(\log J)^{\frac{1}{3}},\qquad

(61)

With this choice, we have
e^{2(M-J)}=O(J^{-2})
and, according to Lemma

, both

Proof of Theorem

Notice that in the proof of the lower bound in Theorem

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]
but not on
\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\left[(H_{L}-M)_{+}\right]

.

Proof of the lower bound in Theorem

.

We use the lower bounds (

) and (

) establised in the proof of Theorem

. For the case
\mu\in L^{3}
, we choose

and

, see (

, with

:

(62)

With these choices, we have

and, according to Lemma

\mathbb{E}_{{\mathcal{N}}_{\vartheta^{2}}}\left[(H_{L_{J}}-M_{J})_{+}\right]=O(J^{-2})

Now, we turn to the proof of the upper bound in Theorem

.

Lemma 16
.

Assume that
\mu\in L^{p}
, with
p\in[2,\infty)
. Then, for every
L\in\mathbb{N}
and every
M>0
, we have:

\mathbb{P}_{\mu}[H_{L}>M]=O\left(\frac{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}{M^{p}}\right)\,,

(63)

and

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}[(H_{L}-M)_{+}]=O\left(\frac{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}{M^{p-1}}\right)\,.

(64)

Proof.

Let us introduce
S_{n}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}
and observe that
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a martingale and
H_{L}\leq 2\sup_{1\leq n\leq L}|S_{n}|
. Doob’s inequality in
L^{p}
yields:

\mathbb{E}[H_{L}^{p}]\leq 2^{p}\mathbb{E}[(\sup_{1\leq n\leq L}|S_{n}|)^{p}]\leq 2^{p}\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}\mathbb{E}[|S_{L}|^{p}].

(65)

Now, Rosenthal’s inequality for independent centered random variables yields:

\mathbb{E}[|S_{L}|^{p}]=O(L^{\frac{p}{2}})
, hence we have shown that
\mathbb{E}[H_{L}^{p}]=O(L^{\frac{p}{2}})
.
The first statement of the lemma follows by Markov inequality. To show the second one, use instead the following inequality:

\mathbb{E}[(H_{L}-M)_{+}]\leq\mathbb{E}[H_{L}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[H_{L}\left(\frac{H_{L}}{M}\right)^{p-1}{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}\right]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[H_{L}^{p}]}{M^{p-1}}\,.

(66)

∎

With this lemma at hand, it is possible to adapt the proof of Theorem
2
to obtain an upper bound on
F_{\mu}(J)
. However, we present a different approach that slightly improves the final result. We modify the upper bounds in Lemmas
11
and
12
in the following way.

Lemma 17
.

For every realization of
h
, for every
(a,b)\in\{-1,+1\}^{2}
, for every
L\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
J\geq 0
and
M\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle Z_{L,h}^{a,b}(J)

\displaystyle\leq\exp\Bigg[-2\left(J\!-\!M\!-\!\frac{1}{2}\!\log\!L\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{a\neq b\}}+bh_{1}^{L}

(67)

\displaystyle+\left(L\log 2+2\sum_{n=1}^{L}|h_{n}|\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}+Le^{2(M-J)}\Bigg]\,,

and, consequently,

Lemma 18
.

Assume that
\mu\in L^{p}
, with
p\in[2,\infty)
. Then:

\mathbb{E}_{\mu}\Big[\left(\log 2+\frac{2}{L}\sum_{n=1}^{L}|h_{n}|\right){\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}\Big]=O\left(\frac{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}{M^{p}}+\frac{1}{M^{p-1}}\right)\,.

(71)

Proof.

Recall the bound
\mathbb{P}[H_{L}>M]=O\left(\frac{L^{\frac{p}{2}}}{M^{p}}\right)
which controls the first term.
We now bound for each
n=1,\dots,L
the term
\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}]
. We set

H_{L}^{(n)}:=\sup_{1\leq m\leq m^{\prime}\leq L}\left|\sum_{i\in\{m,\dots,m^{\prime}\}\setminus\{n\}}h_{i}\right|

(72)

and we observe that if
|h_{n}|\leq\frac{M}{2}
and
H_{L}>M
then
H_{L}^{(n)}>\frac{M}{2}
, so:

\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}>M\}}]\leq\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|{\mathbf{1}}_{\{H_{L}^{(n)}>\frac{M}{2}\}}]+\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|{\mathbf{1}}_{\{|h_{n}|\geq\frac{M}{2}\}}]\,.

(73)

We handle the first term in the right-hand side of (
73
) by noticing that
h_{n}
and
H_{L}^{(n)}
are independent, with
H_{L}^{(n)}
distributed as
H_{L-1}
, so that (
63
) can be used. For the second one we write, proceeding as in (
66
):

\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|{\mathbf{1}}_{\{|h_{n}|\geq\frac{M}{2}\}}]\leq\frac{\mathbb{E}[|h_{n}|^{p}]}{\left(\frac{M}{2}\right)^{p-1}}\,.

(74)

The lemma follows.

Proof of the upper bound in Theorem

.

We proceed as in the proof of the upper bound in Theorem

, but using Lemma

instead of Lemma

\mu

, with

and

For

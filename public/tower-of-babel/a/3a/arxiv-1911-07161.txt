1.
Introduction

Given a surface
\Sigma
embedded in a Riemannian three-manifold, it is natural to ask whether
\Sigma
contains a minimal surface representative in its isotopy class. If
\Sigma
is an incompressible surface (
\pi_{1}
-injective) embedded in a
3
-manifold, a result of Meeks-Simon-Yau
[
19
]
implies that one can minimize area in the isotopy class of
\Sigma
to obtain a stable (i.e., Morse index
0
) minimal surface.

Many
3
-manifolds contain no incompressible surfaces and so it may not be possible to construct minimal surfaces by minimization techniques. On the other hand, every three-manifold admits a Heegaard surface and one can try to represent such a surface by an index
1
minimal surface using min-max methods.

Roughly speaking, the idea is as follows. A Heegaard splitting determines a continuous family of surfaces foliating the manifold and whose two ends are graphs (the spines of the two handlebodies). Taking a sequence of such
1
-parameter smooth families of surfaces
\{\Sigma^{i}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
sweeping out the manifold, which are tighter and tighter in the sense that
\max_{t}Area(\Sigma^{i}_{t})
converges to the infimum possible among such Heegaard sweepouts, one might hope that a subsequence of surfaces
\Sigma^{j}_{t_{j}}
converges (in a weak sense) to a minimal surface. This was carried out by Simon and Smith in the 80s, who proved (the optimal genus bound was obtained in
[
11
]
):

Theorem 1

(L.Simon and F.Smith (1983))
.

If
H
is a Heegaard surface of genus
g
in an oriented Riemannian three-manifold, then there exists a sequence of surfaces
\Sigma_{i}
isotopic to
H
, and pairwise disjoint embedded minimal surfaces
\Gamma_{1},...,\Gamma_{k}
as well as positive integers
n_{1},...,n_{k}
so that

(1)

\Sigma_{i}\rightarrow\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}\Gamma_{j}\mbox{ in the sense of varifolds.}

Moreover, there holds

(2)

\sum_{i\in\mathcal{O}}n_{i}g(\Gamma_{i})+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{N}}n_{i}(g(\Gamma_{i})-1)\leq g,

where
\mathcal{O}
denotes the subcollection of indices

such that
\Gamma_{j}
is orientable,
\mathcal{N}
denotes the subcollection of indices

such that
\Gamma_{j}
is non-orientable and

denotes the genus of
\Gamma_{j}
when its orientable, and otherwise the number of cross-caps one must add in to a sphere to obtain a homeomorphic surface.

Note that the min-max minimal surface produced by Theorem
1
may be disconnected and some components may have integer multiplicities.

While Theorem
1
implies that every three-manifold contains an embedded minimal surface, only in the case of the three-sphere does Theorem
1
produce a minimal surface with prescribed topology, namely, a minimal two-sphere. Starting with a stabilized Heegaard splitting, one does not expect to obtain an isotopic minimal surface. For instance, considering genus
1
splittings of the round three-sphere, Theorem

produces a minimal sphere, and not a torus.

However, in the 80s Pitts-Rubinstein outlined a claim that in the case when the Heegaard surface
H
is assumed to be
strongly irreducible
, one could rule out degeneration of the min-max sequence.

A Heegaard surface
\Sigma
is called

if every closed curve on
\Sigma
bounding an essential disk in one of the handlebodies intersects every such curve bounding an essential disk in the other handlebody. Strongly irreducible splittings were introduced by Casson-Gordon
[

who proved that in non-Haken manifolds, one can always destabilize (or reduce the genus of) a Heegaard splitting until it is strongly irreducible. In other words, in non-Haken manifolds an irreducible Heegaard splitting is also strongly irreducible.

Precisely, Pitts-Rubinstein conjectured (
[

Case ii) can arise already when considering genus

Since in Theorem
1
the minimal surface obtained by min-max methods could be broken into several components and thus not isotopic to
H

[
26

one
of the handlebodies. If this degeneration occurs, one then can remove the handlebodies bounded by the several minimal surfaces to obtain a manifold with minimal boundary

. As one of the minimal boundary components should have index

\partial M^{\prime}
into

to get a new manifold

with stable boundary. One then applies min-max to
M^{\prime\prime}
and iterates. Since

has stable boundary, and the min-max limit should have an unstable component, at each stage of the iteration the manifold shrinks. If the process does not stop, one obtains infinitely many nested minimal surfaces with bounded genus, which gives rise to a Jacobi field. If the metric is bumpy (which White proved is a generic condition) then this gives a contradiction. Thus the process stops after finitely many steps at a minimal surface isotopic to the Heegaard surface.

The argument sketched by Pitts-Rubinstein was incomplete on two points. First, they assume that the min-max surface contains the topological information of the sweepouts in the sense that it arises from neck-pinch surgeries on the Heegaard surface. This was proved by the first-named author
[

of the manifold with stable minimal boundary
\partial M^{\prime\prime}

interior
of such a subdomain. This needs to be justified as a min-max procedure may just give rise to the boundary
\partial M^{\prime\prime}
where some
2

Let us now sketch some of the main ideas in the proof of Theorem
2
.

As remarked above, the essential missing piece in the iteration scheme of Pitts-Rubinstein is to show that when running a min-max procedure in a manifold

with strictly stable boundary, one can find a minimal surface in the interior of
N
. For simplicity, consider the case where

is a three-ball and
\partial N
is a strictly stable two-sphere. One can sweep out

by embedded two-spheres and hope to produce an embedded minimal two-sphere inside

is minimal, it acts as a good barrier and so there is no difficulty in running the min-max process in this local setting. The serious difficulty, however, is that one might just obtain
\partial N
with some integer multiplicity

using the Squeezing map (Section

[

]
.

We argue by contradiction and suppose that applying the min-max process to the three-ball

gives
\partial N
with multiplicity

. Consider a sequence of sweepouts
\{\Sigma^{i}_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
so that
\sup_{t\in[0,1]}Area(\Sigma^{i}_{t})
approaches the min-max value

(5)

W_{N}:=kArea(\partial N)

as

. As a min-max limit is the varifold
\partial N
counted with multiplicity

, the surfaces
\{\Sigma^{i}_{t}\}_{t\in[t_{0}-\varepsilon_{i},t_{0}+\varepsilon_{i}]}
are within a fixed

\partial N
with multiplicity

(in some metric on the space of varifolds). Suppose for simplicity that

(6)

Area(\Sigma^{i}_{t_{0}-\varepsilon})<W_{N}

and

(7)

Area(\Sigma^{i}_{t_{0}+\varepsilon})<W_{N}.

The key observation is that either

We then need to construct a sweepout supported near

(b)

(c)

Choosing

2.
Strongly irreducible Heegaard splittings

In this section we collect the facts about Heegaard splittings that we will need and show that a min-max procedure beginning from a strongly irreducible Heegaard surface can only degenerate in particular ways.

2.1.

Topological preliminaries

We begin with some basic definitions and notations in topology. A 3-manifold

is a
compression body
if there is a connected closed oriented surface
S
such that

is obtained from
S\times[0,1]
by attaching
2
-handles along mutually disjoint loops in
S\times\{1\}
and filling in some resulting
2
-sphere boundary components with
3
-handles. We do not require to fill in all the
2
-sphere boundary components. Denote

by
\partial_{+}W
and
\partial W\backslash\partial_{+}W

\partial_{-}W
. A compression body is called a

if
\partial_{-}W=\varnothing

.

Let

be a connected compact oriented

-manifold. When

is closed, an embedded connected orientable surface

is a

if

has two connected components each diffeomorphic to a handlebody. This notion can be generalized to the case when

as follows. Let
(\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
be a partition of the boundary components of

(W_{0},W_{1},H)
is called a

of

if

are compression bodies with

W_{0}\cup W_{1}=M,\quad\partial_{-}W_{0}=\partial_{0}M,\quad\partial_{-}W_{1}=\partial_{1}M

\text{ and }W_{0}\cap W_{1}=\partial_{+}W_{0}=\partial_{+}W_{1}=H.

The surface
H
is then also called a
generalized Heegaard splitting
of
M
. The
Heegaard genus
of

is the lowest possible genus of a generalized Heegaard splitting of
M
. Recall that any triplet
(\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
as above admits a generalized Heegaard splitting.

Let
S
be an orientable closed surface embedded in the interior of
M
as above. An
essential disk
in

is a disk

A generalized Heegaard splitting

We say that

(D_{0},\partial D_{0})\subset(W_{0},H)

(D_{1},\partial D_{1})\subset(W_{1},H)

Note that a strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting is automatically irreducible. Indeed, since every essential disks to opposite sides must intersect, if two such disks had the same boundary curve, after small perturbation one could make them disjoint, which contradicts the strong irreducibility.

Conversely, in non-Haken manifolds it follows from Casson-Gordon
[
3
]
that irreducible splittings are strongly irreducible.

is locally defined, and for any

In this case,

is a connected orientable surface which is a double cover of

under nearest point projection.

The following proposition shows how a strongly irreducible splitting may degenerate after surgeries:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(W_{0},W_{1},\Sigma)
be a generalized strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting of

(where

Then if

and

(2)

For any

If

Proof.

We prove the Proposition when

Recall first that the surface
\Sigma
divides

Let us prove statement (2). Thus suppose without loss of generality that

\Gamma_{1}

parallel ordered sheets each isotopic to
\Gamma_{1}

Since

\Sigma

from

(see
[

First we rule out the case

\Phi_{2}

and

cannot be greater than

.

It remains to rule out the case where

. In this case, the set

,

and

. Thus

It follows from Scharlemann-Thompson

, and on the other side, the set

and

on

so that

and

.

and suppose the genus of

is

in the usual way. Thus we may write

Let

Let

We now address statement (1). If

3.
Interpolation: statement and outline of proof

In this section, we introduce a smooth interpolation result that will be important in the proof of the Local Min-max Theorem (cf. Theorem
10
in Section
6
). The Interpolation Theorem enables us to deform a surface close in the flat topology to a stable minimal surface with multiplicity to a certain canonical position.

Let
M
be an oriented connected compact
3
-manifold possibly with boundary. Let
\Sigma
be an orientable connected surface, and let

(12)

\exp_{\Sigma}:\Sigma\times[-h,h]\rightarrow M

denote the normal exponential map.
Let

(13)

N_{h}(\Sigma)=\exp_{\Sigma}(\Sigma\times[-h,h])

denote an open
h
-tubular neighborhood
of submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
and let
p:N_{h}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\Sigma

be the projection map.
Given a positive integer
m
we will say that
a surface
\Gamma
has
\varepsilon
-multiplicity

m
if there exists a subset
U\subset\Sigma

with
Area(U)<\varepsilon
and for almost every

x\in\Sigma\setminus U
the set
\{p^{-1}(x)\cap\Gamma\}

has exactly
m
points.
We will say that a surface
\Gamma
has
\varepsilon
-even
(resp.
\varepsilon
-odd) multiplicity in
N_{h}(\Sigma)

if its
\varepsilon
-multiplicity

m
is even (resp. odd).
It is straightforward to check that
for all sufficiently small
h>0
and
\varepsilon>0
,
if
L(\Gamma\cap\partial N_{h}(\Sigma))\leq\frac{1}{100}\sqrt{\varepsilon}
,
then
\Gamma
is either
\varepsilon
-even or
\varepsilon
-odd in

N_{h}(\Sigma)
.

We can now state the smooth interpolation result.

Theorem 6

(Interpolation)
.

Let
M
be Riemannian three-manifold with partitioned boundary
(\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
so that the triple
(M,\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
has the structure of a generalized strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting
(W_{0},W_{1},\Gamma)
(as described in Section
2.1
). Suppose further that each component of
\partial M
is a strictly stable minimal surface.

Set
\Sigma=\sqcup_{k=1}^{\nu}\Sigma_{k}
, where each
\Sigma_{k}

is a
boundary component of
M
.

There exists
h_{0},\varepsilon_{0}>0
,
such that for all
h\in(0,h_{0})
and

\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
the following
holds.

Suppose that the surface
\Gamma
satisfies:

Area(\Gamma\setminus N_{h}(\Sigma))<\varepsilon.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
denote the union of the minimal surfaces among
\{\Sigma_{1},...,\Sigma_{\nu}\}

for which
\Gamma
is
\varepsilon
-odd in
N_{h}(\Sigma_{k_{j}})
.
Then for every
\delta>0
and
\tau>0
there exists an isotopy
\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
, such that:

(1)

\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma

(2)

Area(\Gamma_{t})<Area(\Gamma)+\delta

(3)

\mathbf{F}(\Gamma_{1},\Sigma^{\prime})\leq\tau
.

Here,
\mathbf{F}
denotes the varifold metric
as defined in
[
23
, page 66]
.

We note that it is possible
to strengthen conclusion (3) of
Proposition
6

as follows: we may assume that
\Gamma_{1}
can be decomposed into
a thin and thick part, where the thick part is
obtained by removing tiny discs from
each connected component of
\Sigma^{\prime}
and
the thin part is contained in a small tubular neighbourhood of a 1-dimensional graph. Moreover, the area of the thin part can be
assumed to be arbitrarily small.

3.1.
The case of connected stable minimal surface.

In the case of a three-ball bounded by a strictly stable two-sphere, Theorem
6
can be thought of as a quantitative form of Alexander’s Theorem or a combinatorial version of Mean Curvature Flow performed “by hand.”

It follows from Alexander’s theorem that any embedded two-sphere in
N_{\varepsilon}(\Sigma)\cong S^{2}\times[0,1]
can be isotoped to either a round point or else to
\Sigma
itself. The difficulty is to obtain such an isotopy obeying the area constraint (2). The reason that the
\delta
-constraint is important is that (as explained in the sketch in the Introduction) we will be gluing this interpolating isotopy into sweep-outs with maximal area approaching the width
W
and we want the maximal area of the resulting sweepout to still be close to
W
.

It is instructive to consider the analogous question in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
to that addressed in Theorem
6
. Suppose one is given two embeddings
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
of two-spheres into
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. We can ask whether for any
\delta>0
there exists
an isotopy
\Sigma_{t}
from
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
obeying the constraint (assuming
|\Sigma_{1}|>|\Sigma_{0}|
):

(14)

|\Sigma_{t}|\leq|\Sigma_{1}|+\delta\mbox{ for all }t.

It is easy to see that the answer is “yes”. Namely, one can even do better and find an isotopy satisfying

(15)

|\Sigma_{t}|\leq|\Sigma_{1}|\mbox{ for all }t.

To see this, one can first enclose
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
in a large ball about the origin
B_{R}
. By Alexander’s theorem there is an isotopy
\phi_{t}
between
\Sigma_{0}
and
\Sigma_{1}
increasing area by a factor at most
A
along the way. First shrink
B_{R}
into
B_{R/A}
, then perform the shrunken isotopy
(1/A)\phi_{t}
on
B_{R/A}
, and then rescale back to unit size.

Of course, in
3
-manifolds that we must deal with in Theorem
6
are
\mathbb{S}^{2}\times[0,1]
in which one does not have good global radial isotopies to exploit. However, the same idea of shrinking still applies if we first work locally in small balls to “straighten” our surface. We can also use a certain squeezing map (see Section
4
) to repeatedly press our surface closer to
\Sigma
in the flat topology while only decreasing area.

Let us explain the ideas in our proof of Theorem
6
in more detail.
There are two main steps. In the first, we introduce
a local area-nonincreasing deformation process in balls. The end result of applying this process in multiple balls centered around
\Sigma
is to produce an isotopic surface
\Gamma
consisting of
k
parallel graphical sheets to
\Sigma
joined by a thin set of potentially knotted and linked tubes.

The local deformation we introduce exploits the fact that in balls, we can using Shrinking Isotopies to “straighten” the surface while obeying the area constraint (a similar idea was used by Colding-De Lellis
[
5
]
in proving the regularity of
1/j
-minimizing sequences). Our deformation process is a kind of discrete area minimizing procedure, somewhat akin to Birkhoff’s curve shortening process. In the process, it “opens up” any
folds or unknotted necks that are contained in a single ball. However,
at this stage we can not open necks like on Figure
2
.

After the first stage of the process, we are left with
k
parallel graphical sheets arranged about
\Sigma
joined by potentially very complicated necks. If
k
is
1
or
0
, the proposition is proved. If not, the second step is to use a global deformation to deform the surface through sliding of necks to one in which two parallel sheets are joined by a neck contained in a single ball. Then we go back to Step 1 to open these necks. After iterating, eventually
k
is
1
or
0
.

The second stage is complicated by the fact that the necks joining the various sheets can be nastily nested, knotted or linked. We we need generalizations of the Light Bulb Theorem in topology to untangle this morass of “cables” and find a neck to open.
The version of the Light Bulb Theorem that will be most useful to us
is the following
(see Theorem
5.3
).
Given a 3-manifold
M
and two arcs,
\alpha
and
\beta
, with boundary
points in
\partial M
assume that one of the boundary points of
\alpha

lies in the boundary component of
M
diffeomorphic to a sphere.
Then
\alpha
and
\beta
are isotopic as free boundary curves
if and only if they are homotopic as free boundary curves.
We will apply this theorem in the situation when
\alpha

is a core arc of a collection of (partially) nested necks
in the tubular neighbourhood of
\alpha
.
In section
5.2
we prove some auxiliary
lemmas which allows us to treat these collections of tubes
almost as if it was an arc attached to the surface.

3.2.
The case of multiple connected components.

In Theorem
6
we need to consider the case that
\Gamma
clusters
around a minimal surface
\Sigma
that has multiple connected components.
This is illustrated in Figure

.
The surface
\Gamma
is mostly contained in the tubular neighborhood
of minimal surfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, while the part of

After the surface has been deformed into a canonical form in the neighborhood

Interpolation I: deformation to a stacked surface

4.1.
Isotopy with surgeries.

To describe our interpolation procedure
it will be convenient to introduce the notion
of
\varepsilon
-surgeries. We use this term to describe pinching small necks
or discarding small connected components of the surface.

More precisely, we will say that a surface

is obtained

In both cases we also require that the area of the symmetric difference
of
\Gamma

is bounded from above by

We have the following useful Lemma that relates

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\{\Gamma_{t}\}_{t=0}^{1}

For every
\delta>0

The proof is by induction

Suppose the statement of the theorem

Let

For any

Removing thin part of

The following lemma is useful for reducing the area of thin hair (see

Lemma 4.2

(Reducing the area of thin hair)

Let
\Sigma
be a surface in
M

then for all sufficiently small

the following holds.
There exists
\varepsilon(M,\Sigma,h)>0
with the following
property.
For every
\delta>0
and every surface
\Gamma
with

there exists
a smooth isotopy
\Gamma_{t}
with

\Gamma_{0}=\Gamma
,

We recall the following “bounded path” version of the

used in the min-max setting of Simon-Smith

]
(see
[

(which might not be smooth up to

5.1.
Light Bulb Theorem.

The difficulty in Proposition

Proposition 5.2

(Light Bulb Theorem
[

]
)

Let

(2)

The Light Bulb theorem can be interpreted physically as that one can untangle a lightbulb cord hanging from the ceiling and attached to a lightbulb by passing the cord around the bulb many times.

7.1.

H-compatible splittings and H-cores

Let

be a connected compact oriented
3

3
-manifolds

, and whose boundary is also a stable minimal surface. Our goal will be to find an innermost such splitting.

Let
(W_{0},W_{1},H)
be a generalized Heegaard splitting of
(M,\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
where
(\partial_{0}M,\partial_{1}M)
is a given partition of the components of
\partial M
.

Consider a triplet
(W_{0}^{\prime},W^{\prime}_{1},H^{\prime})
such that the following holds:

•

the surface
H^{\prime}
is embedded inside the interior of
M
and is obtained from
H
by an isotopy of
M
leaving
\partial M
unchanged,

•

W^{\prime}_{0}
,
W^{\prime}_{1}
are non empty compression bodies included in
M
so that
(W_{0}^{\prime},W^{\prime}_{1},H^{\prime})
is a generalized Heegaard splitting of

(W^{\prime}_{0}\cup W^{\prime}_{1},\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{0},\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{1}),

•

\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{0}
and
\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{1}
are two (not necessarily connected, possibly empty) oriented stable minimal surfaces.

Such a triplet
(W_{0}^{\prime},W^{\prime}_{1},H^{\prime})
is then called an
H
-compatible splitting
. Note that
(W_{0},W_{1},H)
is itself an
H
-compatible splitting. By the maximum principle, each component of
\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{0}\cup\partial_{-}W^{\prime}_{1}
is either disjoint from
\partial M
or entirely contained in it.

Given a metric

on

be the set of subsets

such that

for a certain

gives a partial order on

such that
\tilde{N}\subset N^{\prime}
and

.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be as above and suppose that
H
is a strongly irreducible generalized Heegaard splitting. Then

(1)

either

is isotopic to a stable minimal surface,

(2)

or
H
is isotopic to the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of a non-orientable minimal surface
\Sigma\subset M

\Sigma
has stable oriented double cover,

(3)

or for any

such that

Proof.

Let
N=N_{0}\in\mathcal{S}_{g}(H)
, and consider a sequence
\{N_{i}\}\subset\mathcal{S}_{g}(H)
such that

...\subset N_{2}\subset N_{1}\subset N_{0}\text{ and}

\Vol(N_{i+1})\leq\inf\{\Vol(N^{\prime});N^{\prime}\subset N_{i}\}+\frac{1}{i+1}.

We want to show that
\partial N_{i}
converges subsequentially to a minimal surface. By stability,
[
29
]
gives a uniform upper bound

on the second fundamental form of
\partial N_{i}
and hence a uniform lower bound
\delta_{0}>0
on the local focal distance of
\partial N_{i}
(the supremum of the
d
such that, given
\nu

S_{i}
, the map

\partial N_{i}\times(-d,d)�\to M

(x,s)\mapsto\exp_{x}(s\nu)

is locally a diffeomorphism). It suffices now to rule out the possibility that the area

is unbounded. Note that in our case the sequence of stable minimal surfaces is nested, i.e. for each

the other

are on one side of
\partial N_{i}
. Moreover since

is a converging sequence, for any

, there exists an integer

such that the volume of

is smaller than

if

. Let

be the unit normal of

pointing inside of

. If

then for any

,

,
\{\exp_{x}(s\nu);s\in[0,\delta_{0}]\}\cap(\partial N_{i_{0}}\cup\partial N_{j})\neq\varnothing
. So since

,

is locally a graph over

with bounded slope, and we conclude that the area

(

) is bounded in terms of

converges subsequentially to an embedded minimal surface

. When the limit of

is zero then either

is an oriented stable minimal surface isotopic to

or
\Sigma
is a connected non-orientable minimal surface bounding a compression body on one side. In the non-orientable case, the oriented double cover is stable. By irreducibility, the generalized Heegaard splitting

is isotopic to this double cover with a vertical handle attached
[
10
]
. When

of

7.2.

Main theorem

We now state the main theorem.

Theorem 13
.

Let
(M,g)
be a closed oriented
3

3
-sphere. Suppose that there is a strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting

i)

either

is isotopic to a minimal surface

of index at most one,

ii)

or isotopic to the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of a non-orientable minimal surface
\Sigma_{2}
with a vertical handle attached, and
\Sigma_{2}
has stable oriented double cover.

If moreover the metric is bumpy, then there is an oriented minimal surface of index one isotopic to
\Sigma_{1}
in the first case, and isotopic to the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma_{2}
in the second case. In particular its genus is equal to either

or
genus(H)-1
.

We list a few corollaries of Theorem
13
. We use “minimal Heegaard splitting” to denote a closed connected embedded minimal surface which is a Heegaard splitting.

Corollary 14
.

The conjecture of Pitts-Rubinstein is true.

Corollary 15
.

Any lens space not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
contains a minimal Heegaard splitting of genus one with index at most one.

Corollary 16
.

Any
(\mathbb{RP}^{3},g)
contains

•

either a Heegaard splitting of genus one and index at most one,

•

or a minimal
\mathbb{RP}^{2}
with stable oriented double cover.

If the metric
g
is bumpy, then

•

either there is an index one Heegaard splitting of genus one,

•

or there is an index one minimal sphere.

Example 7.3
.

Corollary

is optimal as shown by the following example. Consider a long cylindrical piece

, cap it on one side (say

) with a half-sphere, then take the quotient on the other side (

) by the usual

-action to get an

with positive scalar curvature. Note that any Heegaard torus must intersect the set

is not isotopic to a stable minimal surface. There is exactly one such unstable component
\Sigma^{\prime}
by the index bound
[
17
]
.

We now claim that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is isotopic to
H
. Suppose toward a contradiction that it is not. Using Meeks-Simon-Yau
[
19
]
, we could then minimize area for surface isotopic to
\Sigma^{\prime}
on the side
M^{\prime}\subset\interior(\mathfrak{C})
which is not a compression body, say the side of
\Gamma_{1}
(it exists because
M
is not a
3
-sphere). Observe that
\Sigma^{\prime}
is incompressible inside
M^{\prime}
, since if
\Sigma^{\prime}
is not isotopic to
H
, then it comes from
H
on which at least one essential neck-pinch surgery was done on the side different from
M^{\prime}
.
From Meeks-Simon-Yau we obtain that a minimizing sequence for area in
M^{\prime}
converges to a (maybe disconnected) stable minimal surface
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
with integer multiplicities.
Note that
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is achieved after surgeries form
\Sigma^{\prime}
(
\gamma
-reductions in the terminology of
[
19
]
), which itself was obtained by surgeries on
H
. By Proposition
2.1
, either a component of
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is non-orientable, then its oriented double cover is stable and by adding a vertical handle we get a surface isotopic to
H
: this case cannot occur by our assumption at the beginning of the proof. Or
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
is oriented: then there is a component
\Sigma^{\prime\prime\prime}
of
\Sigma^{\prime\prime}
that is isotopic to
\Sigma^{\prime}
(the other components are just spheres) and is in the interior of
\mathfrak{C}
, but we can now get an
H
-core smaller than
\mathfrak{C}
by replacing
\Gamma_{0}
with
(\Gamma_{0}\cup\Sigma^{\prime\prime\prime})\backslash\Sigma^{\prime}
, which contradicts the minimality of
\mathfrak{C}
.

To summarize, in the case when the metric is bumpy, we have proved that if
H
is not after a neck-pinch isotopic to a double cover of a stable minimal surface, then
H
is isotopic to either an index
1
or
0
minimal surface. Furthermore this minimal surface has area bounded above by the width
W(\mathfrak{C},\Pi)
of the
H
-core. We now show that if
H
is not after a neck-pinch isotopic to a double cover of a stable minimal surface, and is isotopic to an index
0
minimal surface
\Sigma
, then it is also isotopic to an index
1
minimal surface
\Sigma^{\prime}
.

Indeed, consider a handlebody body
W
bounded on one side by
\Sigma
. By the proof of Lemma
7.1
and bumpiness of the metric, there is a compression body
W^{\prime}
included in
W
such that
\partial W^{\prime}
is a stable minimal surface,
\partial W^{\prime}_{+}
is isotopic to
H
, and
W^{\prime}
is of minimal volume among such compression bodies. Then by applying Theorem
10
to
W^{\prime}
we obtain a minimal surface
\hat{\Sigma}\subset\interior(W^{\prime})
which, by minimality of
W^{\prime}
, is of index one and isotopic to
H
.

In the case finally where
H
after a neck-pinch is isotopic to a double cover of a stable minimal surface
\Sigma
, we verify that there is a minimal surface in
M
of genus
genus(H)-1
. To see this, we pass to a double cover
\tilde{M}
of
M
so that
\Sigma
lifts to an orientable stable minimal surface
\tilde{\Sigma}
in
\tilde{M}
bounding a handlebody whose interior projects diffeomorphically to the complement of
\Sigma
in
M
. By the previous paragraph we obtain an oriented index one minimal surface in this handlebody isotopic to
\tilde{\Sigma}
. This surface descends to an embedded surface in
M\backslash\Sigma
and has genus equal to
genus(H)-1
. This completes the proof of the theorem in the case when the metric is bumpy.

If the metric is not bumpy, we use Lemma
7.4
to approximate
g
by bumpy metrics
g_{m}
. For each
m
, there is a
C
-bounded
H
-core
\mathfrak{C}_{m}
and
W(\mathfrak{C}_{m},\Pi)
converges to
W(\mathfrak{C},\Pi)
. If
C
is chosen bigger than
2W(\mathfrak{C},\Pi)
, then we check without difficulty that the above arguments hold for a
C
-bounded
H
-core instead of an
H
-core for large
m
, since
\lim_{m}W(\mathfrak{C}_{m},\Pi)=W(\mathfrak{C},\Pi)
. We get for each
m
large enough

•

either a minimal surface of index at most one isotopic to
H
,

•

or a non-orientable minimal surface with stable oriented double cover, such that when we attach a vertical handle to the boundary of a tubular neighborhood, we get a surface isotopic to
H
.

These minimal surfaces have area at most
C
. Subsequentially this sequence converges by
[
31
]
to a minimal surface
\Sigma^{*}
. By strong irreducibility, either the limit is two-sided and the convergence is smooth, or the limit is one-sided, the oriented double cover of
\Sigma^{*}
is stable (by a Jacobi field argument, see
[
31
]
for instance) and
H
is isotopic to the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma^{*}
with a vertical handle attached (by
[
10
]
). So
\Sigma^{*}
is as in the first part of the statement of Theorem
13
and the theorem is proved.

∎

8.
Minimal Heegaard splittings in orientable 3-manifolds with positive scalar curvature

In this section, we give some applications to the case of positive scalar curvature. We suppose that
M
is a spherical space form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Assume that
M
is endowed with a metric
g
with scalar curvature at least
6
, then any embedded orientable stable surface is a sphere (see
[
30
]
) of area at most
4\pi/3
, and index one minimal surfaces also have an area bound
[
16
, Proposition A.1 (i)]
.

8.1.

Existence of index one minimal Heegaard splitting with minimal genus

A corollary of Theorem
13
is the following existence theorem for irreducible minimal Heegaard splittings in spherical space forms with positive scalar curvature.

Theorem 17
.

(1)

Let
(M,g)
be an
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
which has scalar curvature at least
6
. Then

•

either
M
contains an index one Heegaard splitting of genus one which has area less than
4\pi
,

•

or
M
contains a minimal
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
with stable oriented double cover and area less than
2\pi
.

(2)

Let
(M,g)
be a spherical space form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
, and with positive scalar curvature. Let
H
be a strongly irreducible Heegaard splitting of
M
. Then
M
contains an index one minimal surface isotopic to
H
.

Note that, since a spherical space form admits a positive curvature metric, it does not contain incompressible surfaces so any Heegaard splitting of minimal genus is strongly irreducible by
[
3
]
.

Proof.

Observe that since the scalar curvature is positive any oriented stable minimal surface is a sphere, and a non-orientable minimal surface with stable oriented double cover has to be an
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
. Then the theorem follows essentially from Theorem
13
. It remains to get the strict area upper bound in the case where
M
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
. First by
[
2
]
, the area of an area-minimizing
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
is smaller than
2\pi
whenever
M
is not round. The theorem is clearly true for the round
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
(consider the projection of a Clifford torus). So let
\Sigma
be such an area-minimizing projective plane and suppose that its oriented double cover is unstable. Attaching a vertical handle to the boundary of a thin tubular neighborhood of
\Sigma
, we get a genus one Heegaard splitting
H
and there is an
H
-core
\mathfrak{C}
containing
\Sigma
in its interior (by the arguments of Lemma
12
). As in the proof of Theorem
13
, we use Lemma
7.5
to find a smooth sweepout of
\mathfrak{C}
with maximum area less than
2Area(\Sigma)
, so the local min-max procedure gives either an index one Heegaard splitting of genus one and area less than
4\pi
, or a minimal projective plane with area less than
2\pi
and stable universal cover.

∎

8.2.

Existence of index one minimal Heegaard splitting with area less than
4\pi

The following result combined with Theorem
17
(1) can be thought of as an extension of Theorem 23 in
[
34
]
in the orientable case.

Theorem 18
.

Let
(M,g)
be a spherical space form
\mathbb{S}^{3}/\Gamma
not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
or
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
, and with scalar curvature at least
6
. Then
M
contains an index one minimal Heegaard splitting with area less than
4\pi
.

Proof.

Let
M
be a spherical space form not diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, with scalar curvature at least
6
. Recall that if
M
cannot contain an embedded
\mathbb{R}P^{2}
since then by irreducibility
M
would be an
\mathbb{RP}^{3}
. By Corollary
12
, there is a core
\mathfrak{C}\subset M
. Observe that the boundary components are all spheres.

We now work in the min-max setting of Almgren-Pitts: specifically we will use a local min-max theorem analogous to Theorem
10
in the Almgren-Pitts setting. That theorem is easier to prove and is essentially the combination of the usual min-max result with mean convex boundary and the lower index bound of Marques-Neves
[
17
]
, see the Appendix in
[
35
]
for definitions and statements.

Pick a homotopy class
\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}}\in\bigcup_{C_{0},C_{1}}\pi^{\sharp}_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{n}(M),C_{0},C_{1})
in the sense of Almgren-Pitts corresponding to the fundamental class of
\mathfrak{C}
, namely
\mathcal{A}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}})=[|\mathfrak{C}|]
(where
\mathcal{A}
is the Almgren map), with
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
going through the (finitely many) possible choices, . The width of the core is
\mathbf{L}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}})
. Suppose the latter to be minimal with respect to the choice of
C_{0}
,
C_{1}
.

Claim:
There is an embedded connected minimal surface of index 1, which is also a Heegaard splitting of
M
, such that

\Sigma\subset\interior(\mathfrak{C})\text{ and }Area(\Sigma)=\mathbf{L}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}}).

Indeed for a bumpy metric, we get a minimal surface
\Sigma
in the interior of the core thanks to the Appendix of
[
35
]
. Suppose that
\Sigma
is oriented unstable. It has to be a Heegaard splitting because minimizing its area in the core on one side, we get stable spheres (which have to bound
3
-balls in this side) so we can make its area go to zero in this side (see
[
19
, Proposition 1]
). Thus applying Lemma
7.5
on both sides of
\Sigma
, using interpolation and discretizing the sweepouts, we get that actually
Area(\Sigma)=\mathbf{L}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}})
. As in Theorems
13
and
17
we rule out the case where
\Sigma
is non-orientable. For general metrics, we use the approximation proved in Lemma
7.4
.

It remains to improve the area bound yielded by the usual Hersch trick. Suppose for simplicity that the metric is bumpy. We want to show
\mathbf{L}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}})<4\pi.
For that purpose, let us consider the lift
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
of
\mathfrak{C}
to
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. We apply Theorem
10
. There is a minimal sphere
\tilde{S}
in the interior of
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
and its index is at most one so by the Hersch trick,

Area(\tilde{S})\leq 4\pi.

After projecting on
M
, we get a non-embedded immersed minimal surface
S\subset\interior(\mathfrak{C})\subset M
. We want to apply the method of
[
34
, Proposition 19, Proposition 22]
to
S
. If at embedded points of
S
the projection is a local diffeomorphism then any generic closed curve in
\interior(\mathfrak{C})
intersecting
S
an odd number of times would lift to a closed curve intersecting
\tilde{S}\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
an odd number of times, which is absurd. Hence in that case, inspecting the proof of
[
34
, Lemma 16]
, we conclude that
S
satisfies the local separation property (
LS
). If at embedded points of
S
the projection is a double cover, then
2Area(S)\leq 4\pi
and we can simply reduce the boundary of every component of
\mathfrak{C}\backslash S
at the same time using
[
34
]
. So in every case we construct a homotopy sequence
\{\psi_{i}\}\in\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}}
such that

\mathbf{L}(\{\psi_{i}\})\leq 4\pi\text{ and }\mathbf{L}(\Pi_{\mathfrak{C}})<\mathbf{L}(\{\psi_{i}\}),

the strict inequality coming from the fact that
S
is not embedded while Almgren-Pitts min-max theory produces embedded surfaces (see
[
34
]
).

Finally, if the metric is not bumpy, we use Lemma
7.4
to get minimal spheres
\tilde{S}_{m}
in the interior of the lifts
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}_{m}
, with uniformly bounded area and index. Since
{\mathfrak{C}}_{m}
are cores,
\tilde{S}_{m}
cannot converge to a sphere of
\partial\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
so there is a limit minimal sphere in the interior of
\tilde{\mathfrak{C}}
, which projects to
S
in
M
. The end of the argument is the same as previously.

∎

, this is true by combining

]

]

Denote by

Since any sweepout

\quad(P_{1})_{\sharp}\hat{T}=\pm\Gamma_{0}\text{ and }\mathbf{M}(\Gamma_{0})\leq\mathbf{M}(\hat{T}).

For almost all

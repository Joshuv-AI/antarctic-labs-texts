A Modified weak Galerkin finite element method for the Maxwell equations on polyhedral meshes Thanks: The research of Chunmei Wang was partially supported by National Science Foundation Grants DMS-2136380 and DMS-2206332.
Chunmei Wang, Xiu Ye, Shangyou Zhang

Abstract
. We introduce a new numerical method for solving time-harmonic Maxwell’s equations via the modified weak Galerkin technique. The inter-element functions of the weak Galerkin finite elements are replaced by the average of the two discontinuous polynomial functions on the two sides of the polygon, in the modified weak Galerkin (MWG) finite element method. With the dependent inter-element functions, the weak curl and the weak gradient are defined directly on totally discontinuous polynomials. Optimal-order convergence of the method is proved. Numerical examples confirm the theory and show effectiveness of the modified weak Galerkin method over the existing methods.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we introduce a new numerical
method for solving the time-harmonic Maxwell equations in a heterogeneous
medium
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The mixed formulation of this model problem seeks unknown
functions
{\bf u}
and
p
satisfying

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\times(\mu\nabla\times{\bf u})-\epsilon\nabla p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf f}_{1}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,

(2)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\epsilon{\bf u})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{1}\quad\mbox{in}\;\Omega,

(3)

\displaystyle{\bf u}\times{\bf n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,

(4)

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\quad\mbox{on}\;\partial\Omega,

where the constant coefficients
\mu>0
and
\epsilon>0
are the magnetic
permeability and the electric permittivity of the medium,
respectively.
The formulations (
1
)-(
4
) of the Maxwell equations
have been used in
[
12
,
16
,
17
,
18
]
, for better numerical stability.

The space
H(\hbox{curl};D)
is defined as the set of vector-valued
functions on
D
which, together with their curl, are square
integrable; i.e.,

H({\rm curl};D)=\left\{{\bf v}:\ {\bf v}\in[L^{2}(D)]^{3},\nabla\times{\bf v}\in[L^{2}(D)]^{3}\right\}.

Denote
the subspace of

H({\rm curl};D)
with vanishing trace in the tangential component by

H_{0}({\rm curl};D)=\left\{{\bf v}:\ {\bf v}\in[L^{2}(D)]^{3},\nabla\times{\bf v}\in[L^{2}(D)]^{3}:{\bf v}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial D}=0\right\}.

A weak formulation for (
1
)-(
4
) seeks
({\bf u},p)\in H_{0}(\hbox{curl};\Omega)\times H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
such that

(5)

\displaystyle(\nu\nabla\times{\bf u},\ \nabla\times{\bf v})-({\bf v},\nabla p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf f},\ {\bf v}),\quad\forall{\bf v}\in H_{0}(\hbox{curl};\Omega)

(6)

\displaystyle({\bf u},\nabla q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(g,q),\quad\forall q\in H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),

where
\nu=\mu/\epsilon
,
{\bf f}={\bf f}_{1}/\epsilon
and

g=g_{1}/\epsilon
.

The numerical methods for the Maxwell equations have been studied extensively,
such as the
H(\hbox{curl};\Omega)
-conforming edge elements

[

,

,

,

,

]

.
A weak Galerkin finite element method is studied for the Maxwell equations

.

In the weak Galerkin finite element method, the discrete finite element
functions are generalized functions denoted by

u_{h}=\{u_{0},u_{b}\}
where
u_{0}
is a polynomial
on each polyhedron and
u_{b}
is an unrelated polynomial on each face-polygon.
Using the usual integration by parts, we can define weak derivatives of the generalized
function

as the polynomial
L^{2}
-projections of the standard generalized
derivatives.
The method was first introduced in

[

,
21
]
for second order elliptic equations, and
applied to other partial differential equations

[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

.

In this work, similar to the weak Galerkin finite element method in
[

]
,
the modified weak Galerkin finite element method, which was proposed in
[

]
and has been applied to various problems

[

,

,

,

]
, is applied to the Maxwell equations.
In the modified weak Galerkin finite element method, the weak function
on the inter-element polygons is replaced by the average on the two sides, i.e.,

u_{h}=\{u_{0},u_{b}\}=\{u_{0},\{u_{0}\}\}
.
In particular, it is proved in
[
5

that any convex or nonconvex combination
of two-side functions would work in the modified weak Galerkin finite element
method, i.e.,
u_{b}=\theta u_{1}+(1-\theta)u_{2}
for any real number
\theta
, instead of

\theta=1/2
only previously.

In this paper, we prove that the modified weak Galerkin finite elements are
inf-sup stable in solving the saddle-point problem (
5
)–(
6

Consequently the optimal order of convergence for
\mathbf{u}
and
p
is established.
Numerical examples are provided, showing the correctness of the theory and
the efficiency over the existing weak Galerkin method.

The space

2.
MWG finite element scheme

Let
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
be a partition of the domain
\Omega
with mesh
size
h
that consists of polyhedra of arbitrary shape. Assume that
the partition
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
is shape regular. Denote by
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}
the set of all
faces in
{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
and let
{\mathcal{E}}_{h}^{0}={\mathcal{E}}_{h}\backslash\partial\Omega
be the set of all interior faces.

For simplicity, we adopt the following notations; i.e.,

\displaystyle(v,w)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}(v,w)_{T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{T}vwd{\bf x},

\displaystyle{\langle}v,w{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}{\langle}v,w{\rangle}_{\partial T}=\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}\int_{\partial T}vwds.

Let
P_{k}(K)
consist all the polynomials of degree less or equal to
k
defined on
K
.

Let
k\geq 1
. We define two finite element spaces
V_{h}
and
W_{h}
as follows

\displaystyle V_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{{\bf v}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{3}:\ {\bf v}|_{T}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3},\;\;T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\},

\displaystyle W_{h}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{q\in L^{2}(\Omega):\ q|_{T}\in P_{k-1}(T),\;\;T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}\right\}.

We further introduce two subspaces of
V_{h}
and
W_{h}
with boundary conditions

\displaystyle V_{h}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{{\bf v}\in V_{h}:\ {\bf v}\times{\bf n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\},

\displaystyle W_{h}^{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{q\in W_{h}:\ q|_{\partial\Omega}=0\right\}.

For
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
, we define the jump of
\tau
as
[\tau]_{\partial T}=\frac{1}{2}(\tau|_{T}-\tau|_{T_{n}})
and the average of
\tau
as
\{\tau\}_{\partial T}=\frac{1}{2}(\tau|_{T}+\tau|_{T_{n}})
, where
T_{n}
denotes the elements neighboring to
T
. For
e\in{\partial T}\cap\partial\Omega
, we define
[\tau]_{e}=\tau
and
\{\tau\}_{e}=0
. Then we have the following identities

(7)

\displaystyle\langle{\bf q}\times{\bf n},\;{\bf v}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle[{\bf q}]\times{\bf n},\;{\bf v}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle\{{\bf q}\}\times{\bf n},\;{\bf v}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

(8)

\displaystyle\langle{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n},\;q\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n},\;[q]\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+\langle{\bf v}\cdot{\bf n},\;\{q\}\rangle_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

For a function
{\bf v}\in V_{h}
, its weak curl
\nabla_{w}\times{\bf v}
is defined as a piecewise vector-valued polynomial such that on each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
\nabla_{w}\times{\bf v}\in[P_{k-1}(T)]^{3}
satisfies

(9)

(\nabla_{w}\times{\bf v},{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=({\bf v},\nabla\times{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}-\langle\{{\bf v}\}\times{\bf n},{\bm{\varphi}}\rangle_{\partial T},\qquad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{k-1}(T)]^{3}.

For a function
q\in W_{h}
, its weak gradient
\nabla_{w}q
is defined as a piecewise vector-valued polynomial such that on each
T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}
,
\nabla_{w}q\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3}
satisfies

(10)

(\nabla_{w}q,{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}=-(q,\nabla\cdot{\bm{\varphi}})_{T}+\langle\{q\},{\bm{\varphi}}\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T},\qquad\forall{\bm{\varphi}}\in[P_{k}(T)]^{3}.

We define a bilinear form with an appropriate
stabilization term as follows:

(11)

\displaystyle a({\bf v},\ {\bf w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\nu\nabla_{w}\times{\bf v},\ \nabla_{w}\times{\bf w})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+s_{1}({\bf v},{\bf w}),

where

(12)

\displaystyle s_{1}({\bf v},\;{\bf w})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h^{-1}_{T}({\langle}[{\bf v}]\times{\bf n},\;\;[{\bf w}]\times{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial T}

\displaystyle\quad+{\langle}[{\bf v}]\cdot{\bf n},\;\;[{\bf w}]\cdot{\bf n}{\rangle}_{{\partial T}\setminus{\partial\Omega}}).

We introduce the following bilinear form

(13)

\displaystyle b({\bf v},\ q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf v},\nabla_{w}q)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

and a second stabilization term

(14)

\displaystyle s_{2}(p,\;q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}{\langle}[p],\;\;[q]{\rangle}_{\partial T}.

We are ready to present the MWG method for the weak formulation (
5
) of the time-harmonic Maxwell model equations (
1
).

Modified Weak Galerkin Algorithm 1
.

Find
{\bf u}_{h}\in V_{h}^{0}
and

p_{h}\in W_{h}^{0}
satisfying

(15)

\displaystyle a({\bf u}_{h},\ {\bf v})-b({\bf v},\;p_{h})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf f},\;{\bf v})\quad\forall\ {\bf v}\in V_{h}^{0},

(16)

\displaystyle b({\bf u}_{h},\;q)+s_{2}(p_{h},q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(g,q)\quad\forall\ q\in W_{h}^{0}.

Lemma 2.1
.

The modified weak Galerkin finite element algorithm (
15
)-(
16
)
has a unique solution.

Proof.

It suffices to show that zero is the only solution of
(
15
)-(

) if

and

\displaystyle\quad+{\langle}[{\bf u}_{h}]\cdot{\bf n},\;\;[{\bf u}_{h}]\cdot{\bf n}{\rangle}_{{\partial T}\setminus{\partial\Omega}})+\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}{\langle}[p_{h}],\ [p_{h}]{\rangle}_{\partial T}=0,

.

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle({\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf u}_{h},{\bf v})

\displaystyle({\nabla\times}{\bf u}_{h},\ {\bf v})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}},

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\bf u}_{h},\nabla_{w}q)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=-(\nabla\cdot{\bf u}_{h},q)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+{\langle}{\bf u}_{h}\cdot{\bf n},\ \{q\}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

.

\displaystyle 0

3.
Error Equations

Denote by

the element-wise defined

Lemma 3.1
.

For
{\bf v},{\bf w}\in V_{h}
, we have

(18)

({\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf v},{\bf w})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}=({\nabla\times}{\bf v},{\bf w})_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+{\langle}[{\bf v}]\times{\bf n},{\bf w}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}.

Using (

\displaystyle=

Lemma 3.2
.

Let

&\quad\ -E_{2}({\bf u},{\bf v})+E_{3}(p,{\bf v})+s_{1}({\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u},{\bf v}).\end{aligned}

Subtracting (

19

Testing (

gives

(\nabla\cdot{\bf u},\;q)=(g,\;q).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-({\bf u},\ \nabla q)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+{\langle}{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n},\,q{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-({\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u},\ \nabla q)_{{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}+{\langle}{\bf u}\cdot{\bf n},\,q{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}

Adding

This completes the proof of the lemma.

&\quad\ +s_{1}({\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u},{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})+E_{4}({\bm{\psi}},\epsilon_{h})-s_{2}(\epsilon_{h},\ Q_{k-1}\xi)\\
&\quad\ +E_{4}({\bf u},Q_{k-1}\xi)+s_{2}(Q_{k-1}p,Q_{k-1}\xi)-s_{1}({\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}},\ {\bf e}_{h})\\
&\quad\ +E_{2}({\bm{\psi}},{\bf e}_{h})+E_{1}({\bm{\psi}},{\bf e}_{h})+E_{3}(\xi,{\bf e}_{h}).\end{aligned}

We will provide the estimates for the twelve terms on the last line of (
50
). Among them, we can bound six of them by using Lemma
4.1
with
t=s
and (
41
),

\displaystyle|s_{1}({\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}},\ {\bf e}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\bf e}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s},

\displaystyle|s_{2}(\epsilon_{h},\ Q_{h}\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|\xi\|_{s}\ |\epsilon_{h}|_{0,h}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|\xi\|_{s},

\displaystyle|E_{1}({\bm{\psi}},{\bf v})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\bf e}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s},

\displaystyle|E_{2}({\bm{\psi}},{\bf e}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\bf e}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s},

\displaystyle|E_{3}(\xi,{\bf e}_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|\xi\|_{s}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}{\bf e}_{h}{|\hskip-1.4457pt|\hskip-1.4457pt|}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|\xi\|_{s},

\displaystyle|E_{4}({\bm{\psi}},\epsilon_{h})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{s}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}|\epsilon_{h}|_{0,h}\leq Ch^{t+s}(\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}+\|p\|_{t})\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}.

Using the trace inequality (
29
), Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and the properties of the projection operators
{\bf Q}_{k}
and
Q_{k-1}
, we have

\displaystyle|s_{1}({\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u},\ {\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}(\langle[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}]\times{\bf n},\;[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle+\langle[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}]\cdot{\bf n},\;[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}]\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{{\partial T}\setminus\partial\Omega})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}^{-1}(\langle[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}-{\bf u}]\times{\bf n},\;[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}\rangle_{\partial T}

\displaystyle+\langle[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}-{\bf u}]\cdot{\bf n},\;[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\psi}}]\cdot{\bf n}\rangle_{{\partial T}\setminus\partial\Omega})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s},

and

\displaystyle|s_{2}(Q_{k-1}p,\ Q_{k-1}\xi)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}\langle[Q_{k-1}p],\;[Q_{k-1}\xi]\rangle_{\partial T}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|\sum_{T\in{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}h_{T}|\langle[Q_{k-1}p-p],\;[Q_{k-1}\xi-\xi]\rangle_{\partial T}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|p\|_{t}\|\xi\|_{s}.

Similarly, we have that

\displaystyle|E_{1}({\bf u},\ {\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\nu{\nabla\times}{\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}(\nu{\nabla\times}{\bf u}),\,[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\nu{\nabla\times}{\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}(\nu{\nabla\times}{\bf u}),\,[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}.

and

\displaystyle|E_{3}(p,{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\{p-Q_{k-1}p\},\,[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}]\cdot{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\{p-Q_{k-1}p\},\,[{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}-{\bm{\psi}}]\cdot{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|p\|_{t}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}.

and

\displaystyle|E_{4}({\bf u},Q_{k-1}\xi)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}({\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf n},\,[Q_{k-1}\xi]{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}({\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u})\cdot{\bf n},\,[Q_{k-1}\xi-\xi]{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}\|\xi\|_{s}.

Using (
37
), (
38
), and the trace inequality (
29
), we have

\displaystyle|E_{2}({\bf u},\ {\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|({\bf Q}_{k}{\nabla\times}{\bf u}-{\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u},\ {\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\{{\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}\},[{\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|{\langle}\{{\bf u}-{\bf Q}_{k}{\bf u}\},[{\nabla_{w}\times}{\bf Q}_{k}{\bm{\psi}}-{\nabla\times}{\bm{\psi}}]\times{\bf n}{\rangle}_{\partial{\mathcal{T}}_{h}}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Ch^{t+s}\|{\bf u}\|_{t+1}\|{\bm{\psi}}\|_{1+s}.

Substituting all the estimates above into (
50
) and using (
47
)
imply the error estimate (
48
). This completes
the proof of the theorem.
∎

Genericity of Lyapunov spectrum of bounded random compact operators on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces
Thai Son Doan

Abstract
. This paper is devoted to study stability of Lyapunov exponents and simplicity of Lyapunov spectrum for bounded random compact operators on a separable infinite-dimensional Hilbert space from a generic point of view generated by the essential supremum norm. Firstly, we show the density of both the set of bounded random compact operators having finite number Lyapunov exponents and the set of bounded random compact operators having countably infinite number Lyapunov exponents. Meanwhile, the set of bounded random compact operators having no Lyapunov exponent is nowhere dense. Finally, for any k ∈ ℕ k\in\mathbb{N} we show that the set of bounded random compact operators satisfying that the Lyapunov spectral corresponding to their first k k Lyapunov exponents are simple and continuous contains an open and dense set.

Existing literature and the contribution on stability of Lyapunov exponent and simplicity of Lyapunov spectrum of random compact operators on infinite-dimensional spaces
:

In contrast to various known results about simplicity and continuity of Lyapunov exponents for random dynamical systems on a finite dimensional space, only a partial result was developed for the same problems but on an infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. We mentioned here the result obtained in
[
9
,
10
]
which can be considered as a generalization of Mañe-Bochi Theorem. Concretely, the authors in
[
9
,
10
]
showed that generically either product of random compact operators converges to the null operators or the corresponding Oseledets-Ruelle decomposition is dominated. Our first contribution in this paper is to show that the set of product of random compact operators converging to the null operators is nowhere dense. The main ingredient in the proof of this result is a development of Millionshchikov’s rotation technique on infinite-dimensional Hilbert spaces. Concerning the remaining possibility of the number of Lyapunov exponents of random compact operators, we show that the set of bounded random compact operators
having finite Lyapunov exponents and all of them are simple is dense, the set of bounded
random compact operators having infinitely countable numbers of Lyapunov exponents
is also dense.

Regarding the aspect of continuity and higher regularity of Lyapunov exponents, our second contribution of the paper is to show the existence of an open and dense set of the space of product of bounded random compact operators such that their first finite number of Lyapunov exponents are simple and depends analytically on the generators. The first ingredient in the proof of this result is a generalization of the Perron–Frobenius theorem and the Krein-Rutman and theorem for random compact operators established in
[
24
,
48
]
. The second ingredient of the proof is density of integral separation for random compact operators.

Preliminaries and the statement of the main results

Let
\mathcal{H}
be an infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space. Denote by
\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
the space of compact operators from
\mathcal{H}
into itself. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a non-atomic Lebesgue probability space and
\theta:\Omega\rightarrow\Omega
an invertible ergodic transformation preserving the probability
\mathbb{P}
. Denote by
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
the space of strongly measurable maps

1

The map
T:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
is called
strongly measurable
if for any
x\in\mathcal{H}
the map from
\Omega
to
\mathcal{H}
defined by
\omega\mapsto T(\omega)x
is measurable, see e.g.
[

T:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
endowed with the

-norm

\|T\|_{\infty}:=\mbox{ess}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|T(\omega)\|.

Then, the space
(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
is a Banach space and each
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
gives rise to an one-sided linear random dynamical system
T:\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
via

(2.1)

T(n,\omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\hbox{id},&\hbox{if }n=0,\\[6.45831pt]
T(\theta^{n-1}\omega)\dots T(\omega),&\hbox{else.}\end{array}\right.

For short, we use the notation
T_{\omega}^{n}
instead of
T(n,\omega)
and we also identify each random compact operator
T
with its generated linear random dynamical system
T^{n}_{\omega}
. Thanks to work in
[
49
,
29
,
30
]
, the following multiplicative ergodic theorem provides the asymptotic behavior of random compact operators over an invertible ergodic system. Note that these random compact operators are not needed to be assume to be invertible.

Theorem 2.1

(Multiplicative ergodic theorem for non-invertible random compact operators)
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Then, there exists a measurable set
\widehat{\Omega}
being of full probability and invariant under
\theta
such that the following statements hold:

(i)
Lyapunov exponents
: For every
\omega\in\widehat{\Omega}
, the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\big((T^{n}_{\omega})^{\mathrm{T}}T^{n}_{\omega}\big)^{\frac{1}{2n}}=\Lambda_{\omega}(T)
exists and is a compact operator. Let
e^{\lambda_{1}(T)}>e^{\lambda_{2}(T)}>\dots
denote the nonzero eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{\omega}(T)
. Then,

\lambda_{k}(T)
are real and independent of
\omega
and might terminate at
\lambda_{s(T)}(T)
; otherwise we write
s(T)=\infty
.

(ii)
Oseledets-Ruelle decomposition
: Let
F^{\infty}_{\omega}(T)
be the null space of
\Lambda_{\omega}(T)
, if it exists. Then,
\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T^{n}_{\omega}|_{F_{\omega}^{\infty}(T)}\|=-\infty
. There exists an equivariant decomposition

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{s(T)}\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T)\oplus F^{\infty}_{\omega}(T),

where the Oseledets-Ruelle subspaces
\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T),i=1,\dots,s,
are of finite dimension and equivariant, i.e.
T(\omega)\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T)\subset\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\theta\omega}(T)
, and

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T^{n}_{\omega}v\|=\lambda_{i}\qquad\hbox{for }v\in\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T)\setminus\{0\}.

According to the above theorem, three possibilities for the number of Lyapunov exponents of a random compact operator are finite numbers, infinitely countable numbers or no number, see also
[
38
]
. Our first result is to show the density of the set of random compact operators having finite numbers of Lyapunov exponents and all of them are simple, i.e. the Oseledets-Ruelle subspaces corresponding to all finite Lyapunov exponents are of dimension one. Next, we also show the density of the set of random compact operators having infinitely countable numbers of Lyapunov exponents. Concerning the remaining possibility, we show the nowhere density of the convergence to the null operator of random compact operators. These results are summarized in the following theorem.

Theorem A (Genericity on the number of Lyapunov exponents).
Define the following subsets of
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}

\displaystyle:=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}):s(T)<\infty\;\&\dim\mathcal{O}^{k}_{\omega}(T)=1\hbox{ for }k=1,\dots,s(T)\Big\},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}

\displaystyle:=\Big\{T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}):s(T)=\infty\Big\},

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}

\displaystyle:=\big\{T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}):\Lambda_{\omega}(T)=0\quad\hbox{for }\mathbb{P}-\hbox{a.e. }\omega\in\Omega\big\}.

Then, the following statements hold

(i)

The set
\mathcal{S}
is dense in
(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

(ii)

The set
\mathcal{I}
is dense in
(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H}),\|\cdot\|_{\infty})
.

(iii)

The set
\mathcal{N}
is nowhere dense in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
.

Next, we are interested in the simplicity and the continuity of the Lyapunov exponents of bounded random compact operators. More concretely, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
we show the genericity of the the simplicity and analyticity of the first
k
Lyapunov exponents.

Theorem B (Genericity on simplicity and analyticity of Lyapunov exponents).
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an open and dense set
\mathcal{G}_{k}\subset\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
such that for all
T\in\mathcal{G}_{k}
the following statements hold

(i)

The Oseledets-Ruelle subspaces corresponding to the first
k
Lyapunov exponent of
T
are simple, i.e.

\dim\mathcal{O}^{1}_{\omega}(T)=\dots=\dim\mathcal{O}^{k}_{\omega}(T)=1.

(ii)

For each
i=1,\dots,k
, the map
\lambda_{i}:\mathcal{G}_{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{R},T\mapsto\lambda_{i}(T)
is analytic.

3.
Preparatory results

3.1.
Krein-Rutman theorem for random compact operators

A subset
\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{H}
is called a
proper cone
if
tv\in\mathcal{C}
for all
t\geq 0,v\in\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}\cap(-\mathcal{C})=\{0\}
. For a closed proper convex cone
\mathcal{C}
, the dual cone
\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\prime}}
is defined by

(3.1)

\mathcal{C}^{{}^{\prime}}:=\{u\in\mathcal{H}:\langle u,v\rangle\geq 0\quad\hbox{for all }v\in\mathcal{C}\}.

A family of closed proper convex cones
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is assumed to satisfies an additional condition about the existence of unit interior points of these cones and their dual cones:

(C)

There are measurable mappings
c,c^{\prime}:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
\|c(\omega)\|=\|c^{\prime}(\omega)\|=1
and
B(c(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
,
B(c^{\prime}(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}

with
r
is independent of
\omega
.

In sequel, we collect examples of family of closed proper convex cones
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfying condition (C) used throughout the paper.

Example 3.1
.

(i) Let
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
be a family of closed proper convex cones satisfying condition (C). Denote by
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
the space of bounded operators from
\mathcal{H}
into itself. Let
P:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H})
be a strongly measurable map such that
P(\cdot)
is essentially bounded. Define

\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega}=P(\omega)\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\quad\hbox{for }\omega\in\Omega.

Then,
(\widetilde{\mathcal{C}}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is also a family of closed proper convex cones satisfying condition (C).

(ii) Let
e:\omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a random unit variable. For each
\omega\in\Omega
, let

(3.2)

\mathcal{C}_{\omega}:=\left\{v\in\mathcal{H}:\langle e(\omega),v\rangle\geq\frac{\|v\|}{2}\right\}.

Obviously,
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a family of proper closed convex cones. We show that
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
also satisfies condition (C) with the interior unit vector
e(\omega)
by verifying that
B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
and
B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
. Let
v\in B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})
be arbitrary. Then,
v=e(\omega)+x
for some
x\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|x\|\leq\frac{1}{6}
. Thus,
\|v\|\leq\frac{7}{6}
and

\langle e(\omega),v\rangle=1+\langle x,v\rangle\geq 1-\|x\|\|v\|\geq\frac{29}{36},

which implies that
\langle e(\omega),v\rangle\geq\frac{\|v\|}{2}
and therefore
B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})\subset\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
. To show
B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
, pick an arbitrary
u\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
with
\|u\|=1
. Then,

\langle v,u\rangle=\langle e(\omega),u\rangle+\langle x,u\rangle\geq\frac{\|u\|}{2}-\|x\|\|u\|>0,

which together with the definition of the dual cone
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
as in (
3.1
) implies that
v\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
. Consequently,
B(e(\omega),\frac{1}{6})\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
.

(iii) Let
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{O}(\omega)\oplus F(\omega)
be a random decomposition of
\mathcal{H}
with
\dim\mathcal{O}(\omega)=1
. Let
e(\omega)
be the random unit vector of
\mathcal{O}(\omega)
. Suppose that there exists
\eta\in(0,1)
such that

(3.3)

\frac{|\langle u,e(\omega)\rangle|}{\|u\|}\leq\eta\qquad\hbox{ for all }u\in F(\omega)\setminus\{0\}.

The above condition means an uniform separation from zero of the angle between two vectors in
\mathcal{O}(\omega)
and
F(\omega)
. This condition also implies the following estimate on the decomposition of an arbitrary unit vector in
\mathcal{H}
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be an arbitrary unit vector. Write
\xi=v+u
, where
v\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
and
u\in F(\omega)
. Then, using (
3.3
) we obtain that

(3.4)

\|v\|,\|u\|\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}.

For each
\omega\in\Omega
, let

\mathcal{C}_{\omega}:=\left\{u+\gamma e(\omega):u\in F(\omega)\quad\hbox{ with }\quad\|u\|\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}\right\}.

Obviously,
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a family of closed proper convex cones. We are proving that
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies condition (C) with the unit interior point
c(\omega)=c^{\prime}(\omega):=e(\omega)
. To see this, let

(3.5)

r:=\frac{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}{3}.

We verify that
B(e(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
and
B(e(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
. For this purpose, choose and fix an arbitrary
\beta\in(0,r)
and
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
with
\|\xi\|=1
. Write
\xi=v+u
with
v\in\mathcal{O}(\omega)
and
u\in F(\omega)
. Thus,

e(\omega)+\beta\xi=\beta u+(e(\omega)+\beta v).

Note that
e(\omega)+\beta v
is either
(1+\beta\|v\|)e(\omega)
or
(1-\beta\|v\|)e(\omega)
. In both cases, by (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and
\beta\in(0,r)
we have

\|\beta u\|\leq\frac{r}{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}\leq\frac{1-\frac{r}{\sqrt{1-\eta^{2}}}}{2}\leq\frac{1-\beta\|v\|}{2},

which implies that
e(\omega)+\beta\xi\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
and therefore
B(e(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
. To see
e(\omega)+\beta\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
, let
u+\gamma e(\omega)\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
with
u\in F(\omega)
and
\|u\|\leq\frac{\gamma}{2}
. Then, by (
3.5
)

\displaystyle\langle e(\omega)+\beta\xi,u+\gamma e(\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(1+\beta\langle\xi,e(\omega)\rangle)-\|u\|\|e(\omega)+\beta\xi\|

\displaystyle\geq\gamma(1-r)-\frac{\gamma}{2}(1+r)\geq 0,

which indicates that
e(\omega)+\beta\xi\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
. Thus,
B(e(\omega),r)\subset\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\omega}
.

In what follows, we recall the result from
[
24
]
and its slight improvement, see
[
22
, Theorem 8]
, about simplicity and analyticity of the top exponent for a random compact operator having an invariant random cone. To state this result, recall that the
top Lyapunov exponent function

\kappa:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty\}
is defined as

(3.6)

\kappa(T):=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|\qquad\hbox{for}\quad\mathbb{P}-\hbox{a.e. }\omega\in\Omega.

The
Hilbert metric

d_{\mathcal{C}}
on
\left(\mathcal{C}\cup-\mathcal{C}\right)\setminus\{0\}
is defined by

(3.7)

d_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v):=\log(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v))+\log(\beta_{\mathcal{C}}(v,u))\qquad\hbox{for }u,v\in\left(\mathcal{C}\cup-\mathcal{C}\right)\setminus\{0\},

with

(3.8)

\beta_{\mathcal{C}}(u,v):=\inf\{t>0:t|u|-|v|\in\mathcal{C}\},

where for any
w\in(\mathcal{C}\cup-\mathcal{C})
we let
|w|:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w&\hbox{ if }w\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\},\\[4.30554pt]
-w&\hbox{ if }w\in-\mathcal{C}\setminus\{0\},\end{array}\right.
.

Theorem 3.2

(Krein-Rutman theorem for random compact operators)
.

Consider a random compact operator
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Let
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
be a family of closed proper convex cones satisfying the condition (C). Suppose that
T(\omega)\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\subset\left(\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}\cup-\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}\right)
and there exists
R\in(0,\infty)
such that

(3.9)

d_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(T(\omega)v,c(\theta\omega))\leq R\qquad\hbox{for }\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}.

Then, the following statements hold:

(i)

The Oseledets-Ruelle subspace of
T
corresponding to the top Lyapunov exponent
\kappa(T)
is of dimensional one,

(ii)

The top Lyapunov exponent function
\kappa(\cdot)
is analytic at
T
.

Remark 3.3
.

Condition (
3.9
) implies that
T(\omega)
maps the cone
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
into the interior of
\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}
. For easier task in checking this condition later, condition (
3.9
) is replaced by a slightly stronger condition

(3.10)

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(T(\omega)v,c(\theta\omega))<R\qquad\hbox{for }\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\hbox{with }\|v\|=1,

where
R>0
is a positive constant. To see that condition (
3.10
) implies (
3.9
), choose and fix arbitrary
\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}
with
\|v\|=1
. Using property (C) of cone
\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}
and the fact that
\left\|\frac{T(\omega)v}{\|T\|_{\infty}}\right\|\leq 1
, we obtain that

c(\theta\omega)-\frac{r}{2}\frac{T(\omega)v}{\|T\|_{\infty}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega},

which implies that
\frac{2\|T\|_{\infty}}{r}c(\theta\omega)-T(\omega)v\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}
. Thus,

(3.11)

\log\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(c(\theta\omega),T(\omega)v)\leq\log\frac{2\|T\|_{\infty}}{r},

which together with (
3.10
) implies that

\displaystyle d_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(T(\omega)v,c(\theta\omega))

\displaystyle=\log\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(c(\theta\omega),T(\omega)v)+\log(\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta\omega}}(T(\omega)v),c(\theta\omega))

\displaystyle<\log R+\log\frac{2\|T\|_{\infty}}{r}.

It means that (
3.9
) holds with the positive constant
\log\frac{2R\|T\|_{\infty}}{r}
.

In the following result, Theorem
3.2
is strengthened at two aspects. The first improved point is to replace the condition (
3.10
) on invariance of cone under one iteration of random maps by the condition on invariance of cone under a finite iteration of random maps, see also
[
22
, Theorem 4]
for a similar argument. The second improved point is that the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
is not only true for a random compact operator having an invariance cone but also true for nearby random compact operators.

Proposition 3.4

(Generalized Krein-Rutman theorem for random compact operators)
.

Consider a random compact operator
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
. Let
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
be a family of closed proper convex cones satisfying the condition (C). Suppose that
T
satisfies the following assumptions

(A1)

There exists a prime number
N
such that

T^{N}_{\omega}\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\subset\left(\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}\cup-\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}\right)\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

(A2)

There exists a positive constant
R>0
such that

(3.12)

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}}(T^{N}_{\omega}v,c(\theta^{N}\omega))<R\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}\hbox{ with }\|v\|=1.

Then, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that for all
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
with
\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
the following statements hold

(i)

The Oseledets-Ruelle subspace of
S
corresponding to the top Lyapunov exponent
\kappa(S)
is of dimensional one,

(ii)

The top Lyapunov exponent function
\kappa(\cdot)
is analytic at
S
.

Proof.

Firstly, we show the assertions for the original random compact operator
T
under an additional assumption that
\theta^{N}
is an ergodic transformation from
\Omega
into itself. Define a map
\pi:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
by

(3.13)

\pi(S)(\omega):=S(\theta^{N-1}\omega)\dots S(\omega).

Note that
\pi
is a product of bounded linear operators
\pi_{i}:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H}),S\mapsto\pi_{i}(S)
with
\pi_{i}(S)(\omega):=S(\theta^{i}\omega)
and
i=1,\dots,N-1
and thus the function
\pi
is analytic. For each
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
, by ergodicity of
\theta^{N}
and Theorem
2.1
the top Lyapunov exponent
\kappa^{(N)}(\pi(S))
of the compact operator
\pi(S)\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{B})
over
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta^{N})
is given by

\kappa^{(N)}(\pi(S))=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\pi(S)(\theta^{(n-1)N}\omega)\dots\pi(S)(\theta^{N}\omega)\pi(S)(\omega)\|,

this together with (
3.13
) implies that

(3.14)

\lambda_{1}(S)=\frac{1}{N}\lambda_{1}^{(N)}(\pi(S)).

By (
3.12
) the random compact operator
\pi(T)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta^{N})
preserves the family of closed proper convex cones
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
. Using Theorem
3.2
and Remark
3.3
, the function
\kappa^{(N)}(\cdot)
is analytic at
\pi(T)
and this together with (
3.14
) and analyticity of the function
\pi
implies that
\kappa(\cdot)
is analytic at
T
. Additionally, by Theorem
3.2
and Remark
3.3
the dimension of the Oseledets-Ruelle subspace corresponding to top Lyapunov exponent of
\pi(T)
is
1
. Therefore, the Oseledets-Ruelle subspace corresponding to top Lyapunov exponent of
T
is also
1
. Hence, the conclusion of the proposition holds for
T
in the case that
\theta^{N}
is ergodic.

Now we deal with the case that
\theta^{N}
is not ergodic. Let
W
be a measurable set satisfying that

\mathbb{P}(W)\in(0,1)
and
W=\theta^{N}W
. Since
N
is a prime number, there exists a measurable set
\widehat{W}
such that
\theta^{N}\widehat{W}=\widehat{W}
and the restriction of the map
\theta^{N}
on

\widehat{W}
is ergodic, see
[
22
, Proof of Theorem 4]
. Thus, using a similar statement as in the case that
\theta^{N}
is ergodic on
\Omega
to the induced random compact operator
T
on
(\widehat{W},\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},\theta^{N})
yields the assertions for
T
in this case.

To conclude the proof, it is sufficient to show that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that all
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
with
\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
fulfill (
3.12
), i.e.
S^{N}_{\omega}\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
and there exists
K>0
such that

(3.15)

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}}(S^{N}_{\omega}v,c(\theta^{N}\omega))\leq K\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}\hbox{ with }\|v\|=1.

For this purpose, we show that for all
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
with
\|v\|=1
and
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
with
\|u\|=1

(3.16)

\langle u,T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle\geq\frac{r}{2R}\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

Choose and fix
\omega\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
with
\|v\|=1
and
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
with
\|u\|=1
. By (
3.12
) we derive that
R\,T^{N}_{\omega}v-c(\theta^{N}\omega)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
and thus
\langle u,R\,T^{N}_{\omega}v-c(\theta^{N}\omega)\geq 0
. So,

(3.17)

\langle u,T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle\geq\frac{\langle u,c(\theta^{N}\omega)\rangle}{R}.

By (C) and the fact that
\|\frac{r}{2}u\|=\frac{r}{2}
, we have
c(\theta^{N}\omega)-\frac{r}{2}u\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
. Thus,
\langle u,c(\theta^{N}\omega)-\frac{r}{2}u\rangle\geq 0
. This together with (
3.17
) implies that
\langle u,T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle\geq\frac{r}{2R}
, i.e. (
3.16
) is proved. By continuity argument, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that all
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
with
\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
satisfy that

(3.18)

\|T^{N}_{\omega}-S^{N}_{\omega}\|\leq\frac{r}{4R}\quad\hbox{for all }\quad\omega\in\Omega

and we show that this choice of
\varepsilon
satisfies (
3.15
). For this purpose, let
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
be arbitrary but fixed with
\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
. Let
\omega\in\Omega
and
v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
with
\|v\|=1
. Then, for all
u\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\theta^{N}\omega}

\langle u,S^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle\geq\langle u,T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle-\left|\langle u,(S^{N}_{\omega}-T^{N}_{\omega})v\rangle\right|\geq\frac{r}{2R}-\frac{r}{4R}>0.

It means that
S^{N}_{\omega}v
lies on the dual cone of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
and therefore
S^{N}_{\omega}v\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
. Furthermore, for any
t\geq\frac{4R}{r}
and

u\in\mathcal{C}^{\prime}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
with
\|u\|=1
we have

\langle u,tS^{N}_{\omega}v-c(\theta^{N}\omega)\rangle=\langle u,t\;T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle+\langle u,t\;(S^{N}_{\omega}-T^{N}_{\omega})v\rangle-\langle u,c(\theta^{N}\omega)\rangle.

This together with (
3.16
) and (
3.18
) implies that

\displaystyle\langle u,tS^{N}_{\omega}v-c(\theta^{N}\omega)\rangle

\displaystyle\geq t\langle u,T^{N}_{\omega}v\rangle-t\left|\langle u,(S^{N}_{\omega}-T^{N}_{\omega})v\rangle\right|-\left|\langle u,c(\theta^{N}\omega)\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\geq t\frac{r}{2R}-t\frac{r}{4R}-1

\displaystyle\geq 0.

Consequently, for all
t\geq\frac{4R}{r}
we have

tS^{N}_{\omega}v-c(\theta^{N}\omega)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
and thus

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}}(S^{N}_{\omega}v,c(\theta^{N}\omega))\leq\frac{4R}{r}.

Hence, (
3.15
) holds for
K=\frac{4R}{r}
and the proof is complete.
∎

We conclude this preparatory subsection by establishing a result on how to perturb a random compact operator possessing a random unit vector with good growth rate to the one possessing an invariant family of closed proper convex cones satisfying all assumptions of Proposition
3.4
. Random compact operators possessing a random unit vector with good growth rate will appear later as arbitrarily small perturbation of random compact operators converging to the null operator, see Proposition
4.1
. It is also noted that linear random dynamical systems having one invariant direction with good growth rate plays an important role in proving density of integrally separated bounded linear random dynamical systems, see
[
19
, Theorem 6]
.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
and
e(\omega)
a random unit vector of
\mathcal{H}
. Suppose that
T(\omega)e(\omega)
is collinear to
e(\theta\omega)
and there exist
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\rho,\alpha>0
such that

(3.19)

\|T(\omega)e(\omega)\|\geq\rho,\;\|T_{\omega}^{N}e(\omega)\|\geq e^{-\alpha N}\|T_{\omega}^{N}\|\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

Define
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
by for each
\omega\in\Omega

(3.20)

S(\omega)v=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}e^{2\alpha}\,T(\omega)e(\omega)&\hbox{ if }v=e(\omega),\\[4.30554pt]
T(\omega)v&\hbox{ if }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}.\end{array}\right.

Then, the following statements hold

(i)

\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq(e^{2\alpha}-1)\|T\|_{\infty}
,

(ii)

The random compact operator
S
satisfies all assumptions of Proposition
3.4
, i.e. there exist a family of closed proper convex cones
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfying the condition (C), a prime number
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
S^{N}_{\omega}\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}
and a positive constant
R>0
such that

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{N}\omega}}(S^{N}_{\omega}v,c(\theta^{N}\omega))<R\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}\hbox{ with }\|v\|=1.

Consequently, the top Lyapunov exponent function
\kappa(\cdot)
is analytic at
S
.

In the above proposition, there is no assumption on that
e(\omega)^{\perp}
is invariant under
S(\omega)
. This issue makes difficult to estimate the growth of the vectors starting from
e(\omega)^{\perp}
under iteration of
S(\omega)
. In order to overcome this difficulty,
S(\omega)
is transformed to the new random linear compact
S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
for which
e(\omega)^{\perp}
is almost invariant and the problem of existence of invariant cone for
S(\omega)
is shifted to the existence of invariant cone for the new one. To define
S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
, let
\Pi_{e}(\omega)
denote the projection onto
e(\omega)
along
e(\omega)^{\perp}
. For any
\varepsilon\in[0,1)
, let
S_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
be given as

(3.21)

S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)v=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}S(\omega)e(\omega)&\hbox{ if }v=e(\omega),\\[4.30554pt]
\varepsilon\;\Pi_{e}(\theta\omega)S(\omega)v+(\hbox{id}-\Pi_{e}(\theta\omega))S(\omega)v&\hbox{ if }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}.\end{array}\right.

To see a relation between
S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
and
S(\omega)
, we define a linear operator
P_{\varepsilon}(\omega):\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
by

P_{\varepsilon}(\omega)v=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\varepsilon e(\omega)&\hbox{ if }v=e(\omega),\\[4.30554pt]
v&\hbox{ if }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}.\end{array}\right.

Then,
P_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
and its inverse
P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\omega)
are bounded linear operators. In fact,
\|P_{\varepsilon}(\omega)\|=1
and
\|P_{\varepsilon}^{-1}(\omega)\|=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}
. By (
3.21
) and the fact that
S(\omega)e(\omega)
is collinear to
e(\theta\omega)
, we have

(3.22)

P_{\varepsilon}(\theta\omega)^{-1}S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)P_{\varepsilon}(\omega)=S(\omega).

Before going the proof of Proposition
3.5
, we establish some preparatory results on
S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)
.

Lemma 3.6
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\eta>0
, there exists
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
such that

\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}v\|\leq(1+\eta)\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|\|v\|\qquad\hbox{ for all }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp},\omega\in\Omega.

Proof.

Choose and fix arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N},\eta>0
. By (
3.21
), we have

\|S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)-S_{0}(\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon\|S(\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon\|S\|_{\infty}\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,

which implies that

(3.23)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}-S_{0,\omega}^{n}\|\right)=0.

Note that by (
3.19
) and invariance of
e(\omega)
we have

\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|\geq\|T_{\omega}^{n}e(\omega)\|\geq\rho^{n}\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

This together with (
3.23
) gives that there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.24)

\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}-S_{0,\omega}^{n}\|\leq\eta\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

By (
3.21
) and the fact that
T(\omega)
preserves the random unit vector
e(\omega)
, we
deduce that

S_{0,\omega}^{n}v=(\hbox{id}-\Pi_{e}(\theta^{n}\omega))T_{\omega}^{n}v\qquad\hbox{ for all }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}.

Thus,
\|S_{0,\omega}^{n}v\|\leq\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|
for all
v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}
with
\|v\|=1
. Combining this fact with (
3.24
), we obtain that for all
v\in e(\omega)^{\perp}
with
\|v\|=1

\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}v\|\leq\|S_{0,\omega}^{n}v\|+\eta\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|\leq(1+\eta)\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|.

The proof is complete.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Proposition
3.5
.

Proof of Proposition
3.5
.

(i) Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be abitrary with
\|\xi\|=1
. Then, for each
\omega\in\Omega
there exist
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
and
u\in e(\omega)^{\perp}
such that
\xi=\gamma e(\omega)+u
. Thus
\|\xi\|^{2}=\gamma^{2}+\|u\|^{2}
and hence
|\gamma|\leq 1
. Consequently, by (
3.20
) we have

\|T(\omega)\xi-S(\omega)\xi\|=\|T(\omega)u-S(\omega)u\|=|(e^{2\alpha}-1)\gamma|\leq(e^{2\alpha}-1),

which completes the proof of this part.

(ii) Thanks to (
3.22
) and Example
3.1
(i), it is sufficient to prove that for some
\varepsilon>0
the random compact operator
S_{\varepsilon}
defined as in (
3.21
) satisfies the assertion (ii), i.e. a long enough iteration of
S_{\varepsilon}
preserves a random cone
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfying the condition (C). Here, the family of closed convex cones
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
is given by

(3.25)

\mathcal{C}_{\omega}:=\left\{v\in\mathcal{H}:\langle e(\omega),v\rangle\geq\frac{\|v\|}{2}\right\}\qquad\hbox{for }\omega\in\Omega.

By Example
3.1
(ii),
(\mathcal{C}_{\omega})_{\omega\in\Omega}
satisfies the condition (C). Choose and fix
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
e^{\alpha kN}\geq 8
. Using Lemma
3.6
with
\eta:=\frac{1}{3}
, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

(3.26)

\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\|\leq\frac{4}{3}\|T_{\omega}^{kN}\|\|v\|\qquad\hbox{ for all }v\in e(\omega)^{\perp},\omega\in\Omega.

To conclude the proof, we show that

(3.27)

S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{kN}\omega}\cup(-\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{kN}\omega})\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,

and there exists
R>0
such that

(3.28)

\beta_{\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{kN}\omega}}(S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v,e(\theta^{kN}\omega))\leq R\qquad\hbox{for }v\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}\setminus\{0\}\hbox{ with }\|v\|=1.

For this purpose, choose and fix an arbitrary unit vector
v=\gamma e(\omega)+u\in\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
, where
u\in e(\omega)^{\perp}
. Thus,
\|v\|=\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+\|u\|^{2}}
and by (
3.25
), we have

(3.29)

\gamma=\langle e(\omega),v\rangle\geq\frac{\sqrt{\gamma^{2}+\|u\|^{2}}}{2}.

From
S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v=\gamma S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)+S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u
, we derive that

(3.30)

\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\|\leq|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|+\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|.

Since
T(\omega)e(\omega)
and hence
S_{\varepsilon}(\omega)e(\omega)
are collinear to
e(\theta\omega)
it follows that

|\langle e(\theta^{n}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}e(\omega)\rangle|=\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{n}e(\omega)\|\quad\hbox{ for }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Consequently,

\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\rangle=\gamma\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|+\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\rangle.

Thus,

(3.31)

\left|\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\rangle\right|\geq|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|-\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|.

Verification of (
3.27
)
: By the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\omega}
as in (
3.25
), to show (
3.27
) it is sufficient to
verify that
\left|\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\rangle\right|\geq\frac{\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\|}{2}
. By (
3.30
) and (
3.31
), it is sufficient to show that
|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq 3\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|
. In fact, we show a slightly stronger inequality

(3.32)

|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq 2\sqrt{3}\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|,

which is also useful for other estimate later. To gain this, firstly by (
3.20
), (
3.21
) and (
3.19
) we have

|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|=|\gamma|e^{2\alpha kN}\|T_{\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq|\gamma|e^{\alpha kN}\|T_{\omega}^{kN}\|.

Since
e^{\alpha kN}\geq 8
it follows that
|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq 8|\gamma|\|T_{\omega}^{kN}\|
. Secondly, by (
3.26
) we have
3\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|\leq 4\|T_{\omega}^{kN}\|\|u\|
. Finally, by (
3.29
) we have
\sqrt{3}|\gamma|\geq\|u\|
and thus (

) has been proved.

Verification of (
3.28
)
: We are now estimating
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v,e(\theta^{kN}\omega))
. For this purpose, for any
t>0
we have

\left|\left\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\right\rangle\right|\geq|t|\left|\left\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\right\rangle\right|-1,

which together with (
3.31
) implies that

\left|\left\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\right\rangle\right|\geq|t||\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|-|t|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|-1.

Meanwhile, using (
3.30

\frac{\|tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\|}{2}\leq\frac{t\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v\|+1}{2}\leq\frac{t|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|+\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|+1}{2}

Hence, in order to have the inclusion
tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta^{kN}\omega}
, equivalently

\left|\left\langle e(\theta^{kN}\omega),tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\right\rangle\right|\geq\frac{\|tS_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v-e(\theta^{kN}\omega)\|}{2},

the positive number
t
just only satisfies the following inequality

(3.33)

t|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq 3t|\gamma|\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}u\|+3.

From the fact that
\|v\|=1
and (
3.29

\gamma\geq\frac{1}{2}
. Thus, by virtue of (
3.32
) and the fact that
\|S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq\|T_{\omega}^{kN}e(\omega)\|\geq\rho^{kN}
, any

satisfying

also satisfy (

). Consequently,
\beta_{\mathcal{C}}(S_{\varepsilon,\omega}^{kN}v,e(\theta^{kN}\omega))\leq\frac{12}{(2-\sqrt{3})\rho^{kN}}
, which proves (

3.2.
Perturbation of random compact operators converging to null operator

In the following result, we show that corresponding to a random compact operator
T
converging to the null operator there exist a finite number of disjoint sets
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N}V
such that there exists a perturbed random compact linear operator which is arbitrarily close to
T
sending any given random vector space of dimension
1
starting from
V
to any given random vector space of dimension
1
starting from
\theta^{N}V
. As a corollary, the random compact operator
T
can be perturbed to a random compact operator having an invariant random subspace of dimension
1
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then there exists an integer
N^{*}(T,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
(depending only on
T,\varepsilon
) such that for all
N\geq N^{*}(T,\varepsilon)
there exists a set
V\in\mathcal{F}
of positive
\mathbb{P}
-probability such that the sets
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N}V
are disjoint and the following statements hold

(i) For any random unit vector
e:V\cup\theta^{N}V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, there exists
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
and a scalar random variable
\beta:\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
|\beta(\omega)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
such that

(p1)

Small perturbation
:

S(\omega)=T(\omega)\hbox{ for }\omega\not\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V\hbox{ and }\|S(\omega)-T(\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon\hbox{ for }\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V.

(p2)

Existence of invariant one-dimensional random subspace along the sets
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N}V
: There exists an extension of
e
to a random unit vector
e:\bigcup_{i=0}^{N}\theta^{i}V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that

S(\theta^{i}\omega)e(\theta^{i}\omega)=\beta(\theta^{i}\omega)e(\theta^{i+1}\omega)\quad\hbox{ for }\omega\in V,i=0,1,\dots,N-1.

(ii) Let
h:V\cup\theta^{N}V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a random unit vector. Then, there exist
R\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
, an extension of
h
to a random unit vector
h:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
and a scalar random variable
\gamma:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
|\gamma(\omega)|\geq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}
for
\omega\in\Omega
such that
\|R-T\|_{\infty}\leq\varepsilon
and
R(\omega)h(\omega)=\gamma(\omega)h(\theta\omega)
.

Proof.

Choose and fix an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
. Since
T\in\mathcal{N}
it follows that

(3.34)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T_{\omega}^{n}\|=-\infty\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

(3.35)

\Omega_{n}:=\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\|T_{\omega}^{k}\|<\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\right)^{k}\quad\hbox{for all }k\geq n\right\}.

Then,
\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega_{n+1}
and by (
3.34
) we have
\Omega=\bigcup_{n\geq 1}\Omega_{n}
. This implies that there exists
N^{*}(T,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
(depending on
T
and
\varepsilon
) such that
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{N-1})>0
for all
N\geq N^{*}(T,\varepsilon)
. Choose and fix an arbitrary
N\geq N^{*}(T,\varepsilon)
. By Theorem
6.4
, there exists a measurable set
V\subset\Omega_{N-1}
of positive probability such that
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N}V
are pairwise disjoint. We are now proving that the measurable set
V
satisfies the desired statements of the proposition:

(i) By Proposition
6.3
(iii), the set of random bijective compact operators is dense in the space of random compact operators. Then,
T(\omega)
, where
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
, can be perturbed to an arbitrarily closed random bijective compact linear operator still satisfies (
3.35
). Then, w.l.o.g. we assume that

\displaystyle\hskip 193.47882pt\hbox{ for }i=0,\dots,j-1\Big\}.

As a corollary of the preceding result, any random compact operator converging to null operator can be perturbed to one having a finite number of Lyapunov exponents.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{N}

\varepsilon>0

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0

3.7

and a set

\mathbb{P}

\widetilde{T}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})

\|\widetilde{T}-T\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}

S(\omega)|_{h(\omega)^{\perp}}
is of finite-rank and

which implies that

\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})

\widetilde{T}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})

\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{k}

\mathcal{H}=F^{\infty}_{\omega}(T)\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T),

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|T^{n}_{\omega}|_{F_{\omega}^{\infty}(T)}\|=

\displaystyle-\infty,

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}\qquad\hbox{for }v\in\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T)\setminus\{0\}.

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{O}^{i}_{\omega}(T)

\mathbb{P}\big(U\big)>0,\qquad\hbox{where }U:=\big\{\omega:\|\Pi(\omega)\|\leq M\big\}.

Density of bounded random compact operators having infinitely countable number of Lyapunov exponents

T(\omega)

(4.6)

\|T(\omega)|_{F(\omega)}\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{3}\quad\hbox{ for }\mathbb{P}\hbox{-a.e. }\omega\in\Omega.

4.3.
Negligibility for the convergence random compact operators to the null operator

The structure of the proof of the part (iii) of Theorem A is that we start with a random compact operator converging to the null operator. Then, we first perturb this random compact operator to the new one having an invariant direction with good growth rate, i.e. this new random compact operator satisfies all properties of Proposition
3.5
. Finally, we apply Proposition
3.5
to show an existence of an open set of random compact operators nearby to the original one such that any of them has a non-trivial Lyapunov spectrum.

Concerning a perturbation of a random compact linear operator converging to the null operator to the new one having an invariant direction with good growth rate, we follow the idea of the Millionschchikov rotation technique developed in
[
19
, Theorem 3.6]
, see also
[
6
]
. The content of this result is stated as follows.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{N}
be arbitrary and let
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N}V
be pairwise disjoint sets satisfying assertions of Proposition
3.7
. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
S\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
and an invariant unit random vector
e:\Omega\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
\|S-T\|_{\infty}\leq 3\varepsilon(1+\|T\|_{\infty})
and

(4.8)

\displaystyle\|S(\omega)e(\omega)\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{N+1}}{(1+2\|T\|_{\infty})^{N-1}}\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega,

(4.9)

\displaystyle\|S^{kN}_{\omega}e(\omega)\|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\varepsilon^{k+4N}}{(1+2\|T\|_{\infty})^{4N}}\|S_{\omega}^{kN}\|\qquad\hbox{ for }\omega\in\Omega.

To prove the above proposition, we show the following preparatory lemma about a perturbation result for a random compact operator along a finite disjoint sets.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}(\Omega,\mathcal{H})
be arbitrary. Let
V\subset\Omega
be a measurable set such that
V,\theta V,\dots,\theta^{N-1}V
are disjoint, where
N\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g:V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
be a unit random vector. Then, for any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{6})
there exists a strongly measurable map
S:\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V\rightarrow\mathcal{K}(\mathcal{H})
satisfying the following requirements:

(r1)

For
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
, we have
\|S(\omega)-T(\omega)\|\leq 3\varepsilon+3\varepsilon\|T\|_{\infty}
,

(r2)

For
\omega\in V
, we have

(4.10)

\|S_{\omega}^{N}g(\omega)\|\geq\varepsilon\|S_{\omega}^{N}\|\quad\hbox{and}\quad\|S_{\omega}^{N}\|\geq\varepsilon^{N+1}.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.3
(i), there exists a unit random vector
e:V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
\|T_{\omega}^{N}e(\omega)\|=\|T_{\omega}^{N}\|
for
\omega\in V
. In view of Proposition
6.3
(iii), by perturbing
T(\omega)
on
\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
, if necessary, we can assume that
T^{N}_{\omega}e(\omega)\not=0
for
\omega\in V
. We first perturb
T(\omega)
on
\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
to the new one
R(\omega)
such that
\|R^{N}_{\omega}\|
is uniformly separated from zero for
\omega\in V
. Precisely, for
\omega\in V
and
i=0,\dots,N-1
let
e_{i}(\omega)=\frac{T^{i-1}_{\omega}e(\omega)}{\|T^{i-1}_{\omega}e(\omega)\|}
. A random compact operator
R(\theta^{i}\omega)
is defined by

R(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}T(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)&\hbox{ if }\|T(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)\|\geq\varepsilon,\\[4.30554pt]
\frac{\varepsilon}{\|T(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)\|}T(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)&\hbox{ if }\|T(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)\|<\varepsilon,\end{array}\right.

and

R(\theta^{i}\omega)v=T(\theta^{i}\omega)v
for
v\in e_{i}(\omega)^{\perp}
. Then, it is easily seen that
\|R(\omega)-T(\omega)\|\leq 2\varepsilon
for
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
and

(4.11)

\|R^{N}_{\omega}e(\omega)\|=\prod_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|R(\theta^{i}\omega)e_{i}(\omega)\right\|\geq\varepsilon^{N}\quad\hbox{ for all }\omega\in V.

Now, we will perturb
R(\omega)
to obtain the desired random compact operator
S(\omega)
. For this purpose, using Proposition
6.3
(i), there exists a unit random vector
f:V\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
such that
\|R_{\omega}^{N}f(\omega)\|=\|R_{\omega}^{N}\|
for
\omega\in V
.
We write
g(\omega)=\alpha(\omega)f(\omega)+h(\omega)
, where

\alpha(\omega)=\langle g(\omega),f(\omega)\rangle,\quad h(\omega)=g(\omega)-\alpha(\omega)f(\omega)\in f(\omega)^{\perp}.

For any
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
, we let
S(\omega)=R(\omega)
. For any
\omega\in V
, we construct a compact operator
S(\omega)
as follows
S(\omega)v=R(\omega)v
for all
v\in g(\omega)^{\perp}
and
S(\omega)g(\omega)
is defined by

(4.12)

(\alpha(\omega)+3\varepsilon)R(\omega)f(\omega)+R(\omega)h(\omega))&\hbox{if }|\alpha(\omega)|<\varepsilon.\end{array}\right.

By the above construction, we have
\|S(\omega)-R(\omega)\|\leq 3\varepsilon\|R(\omega)\|
for
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V
. This together with the fact that
\|R(\omega)-T(\omega)\|\leq 2\varepsilon
for
\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V

\|S(\omega)-T(\omega)\|\leq 2\varepsilon+3\varepsilon(\|T(\omega)+2\varepsilon\|)\leq 3\varepsilon+3\varepsilon\|T(\omega)\|\quad\hbox{for }\omega\in\bigcup_{i=0}^{N-1}\theta^{i}V.

Thus, (r1) is fulfilled and to complete the proof it remains to verify (
4.10
) for
\omega\in V

\omega\in V
and consider two separated cases:

Case 1

Since

\langle R_{\omega}^{N}f(\omega),R_{\omega}^{N}h(\omega)\rangle=0

\|S_{\omega}^{N}g(\omega)\|^{2}=\alpha(\omega)^{2}\|R_{\omega}^{N}f(\omega)\|^{2}+\|R_{\omega}^{N}h(\omega)\|^{2}\geq\varepsilon^{2}\|R_{\omega}^{N}\|^{2},

which yields that
\|S_{\omega}^{N}g(\omega)\|\geq\varepsilon\|S_{\omega}^{N}\|
. This together with (

4.10
) in this case.

Case 2

\|S(\omega)g(\omega)-R(\omega)g(\omega)\|=\|3\varepsilon R(\omega)g(\omega)\|\leq 3\varepsilon\|R\|_{\infty},

which together with the fact that

for all

\|S(\omega)-R(\omega)\|\leq 3\varepsilon\|R\|_{\infty}
for all
\omega\in V

(4.13)

\|S_{\omega}^{N}g(\omega)\|\geq(\alpha(\omega)+3\varepsilon)\|R_{\omega}^{N}\|\geq 2\varepsilon\|R_{\omega}^{N}\|.

Thus, on the one hand, by (
4.11

\|S_{\omega}^{N}\|\geq\|S_{\omega}^{N}g(\omega)\|\geq\varepsilon^{N+1}.

On the other hand, by (
4.12
) and (r1) we have
S^{N}_{\omega}v=R^{N}_{\omega}v

5.1.1.
Dominated splitting and integral separation for linear random dynamical systems on a finite-dimensional space

This subsection is devoted to recall the notion and the result on genericity of dominated splitting and integral separation for linear random dynamical systems in a finite-dimensional space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see
[
14
,
19
,
18
]
for more details.

For each
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
denote the set of bounded measurable maps
A:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. Each random map
A\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
gives rise to an one-sided linear random dynamical system
\Phi_{A}:\mathbb{N}\times\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
via

\Phi_{A}(n,\omega):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}A(\theta^{n-1}\omega)\dots A(\omega),&\quad\hbox{ if }n>0,\\[6.45831pt]
\hbox{id},&\quad\hbox{ if }n=0,\end{array}\right.

where
id
denotes the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
. By virtue of the Multiplicative Ergodic Theorem for one-sided linear random dynamical systems defined on an invertible metric dynamical system, see e.g.
[
27
]
, there exist finite numbers of Lyapunov exponents
\lambda_{1}(A)>\dots>\lambda_{p(A)}(A)
, where
p(A)\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, and a forward invariant decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{d}=\mathcal{O}^{1}_{\omega}(A)\oplus\mathcal{O}^{2}_{\omega}(A)\oplus\dots\oplus\mathcal{O}^{p(A)}_{\omega}(A)

with the property that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\log\|\Phi_{A}(n,x)v\|=\lambda_{i}\quad\hbox{iff}\quad v\in\mathcal{O}^{i}_{A}(\omega)\setminus\{0\}.

When
p(A)=d
, we call that the Lyapunov spectrum of
A
is
simple
. We now recall a result on density of simple Lyapunov spectrum of linear random dynamical systems in a finite-dimensional space in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 5.1
.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
, let
A\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
and
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Then, for any measurable set

of positive probability, there exists
B\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{d\times d})
satisfying the following properties:

(i)

\mbox{ess}\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|B(\omega)-A(\omega)\|\leq\varepsilon
and
B(\omega)=A(\omega)
for all
\omega\in\Omega\setminus U
,

(ii)

The Lyapunov spectrum of

is simple.

Definition 5.2

(Dominated splitting)
.

Let

\mathbb{R}^{d}=E(\omega)\oplus F(\omega)

if there exist
K,\alpha,\delta>0
such that for
\mathbb{P}-\hbox{a.s. }\omega\in\Omega
and

the following statements hold

(5.1)

\displaystyle\|A(\omega)v\|

\displaystyle\geq\delta\|v\|\quad\hbox{for }v\in E(\omega)\setminus\{0\},

(5.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(n,\omega)v\|}{\|v\|}

\displaystyle\geq Ke^{\alpha n}\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(n,\omega)u\|}{\|u\|}\quad\hbox{for }v\in E(\omega)\setminus\{0\},u\in F(\omega)\setminus\{0\}.

Remark 5.3
.

(i) The condition (
5.2
) can be replaced by an equivalent statement that
there exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\rho>1
such that for
\mathbb{P}-\hbox{a.s. }\omega\in\Omega

(5.3)

\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(N,\omega)v\|}{\|v\|}\geq\rho\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(N,\omega)u\|}{\|u\|}\quad\hbox{for }v\in E(\omega)\setminus\{0\},u\in F(\omega)\setminus\{0\}.

Proof.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose that (
5.2
). Then, choose and fix and arbitrary
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
Ke^{\alpha N}<1
. Therefore, (
5.3
) holds for
\rho:=Ke^{\alpha N}
.

(
\Leftarrow
) In this direction, we assume that (
5.3
) holds. Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and

be arbitrary. We write
n=sN+r
, where

. Then, by cocycle property
\Phi_{A}(n+m,\omega)=\Phi_{A}(n,\theta^{m}\omega)\Phi_{A}(m,\omega)
and (

), (
5.3
) we have for
\mathbb{P}-\hbox{a.s. }\omega\in\Omega
and

\displaystyle\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(n,\omega)v\|}{\|v\|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(r,\theta^{sN}\omega)\Phi_{A}(sN,\omega)v\|}{\|\Phi_{A}(sN,\omega)v\|}\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(sN,\omega)v\|}{\|v\|}

\displaystyle\geq\delta^{r}\rho^{s}\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(sN,\omega)u\|}{\|u\|}

(5.4)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta^{r}}{\|A\|_{\infty}^{r}}\rho^{\frac{n}{N}-1}\frac{\|\Phi_{A}(n,\omega)u\|}{\|u\|}.

It means that (
5.2

K:=\frac{\delta^{r}}{\rho\|A\|_{\infty}^{r}}
and

(ii) There is characterization of dominated splitting in terms of exponential separation of singular values, see

Some new classes of (almost) perfect c c -nonlinear permutations
Mohit Pal

Abstract
. The concept of differential uniformity was recently extended to the c c -differential uniformity. An interesting problem in this area is the construction of functions with low c c -differential uniformity and a lot of research has been done in this direction in the recent past. Here, we present three classes of (almost) perfect c c -nonlinear permutations over finite fields of even characteristic.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
be the finite field with
q^{n}
elements, where
q
is a prime power and
n
is a positive integer. We denote by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
, the multiplicative cyclic group of non-zero elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Let
f
be a function from
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
to itself. Lagrange’s interpolation formula allows us to write
f
as a polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]/(X^{q^{n}}-X)
uniquely. Thus, we shall use the term function and polynomial for
f
interchangeably. A polynomial
f\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X]
is called a permutation polynomial if the associated mapping
c\mapsto f(c)
permutes the elements of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Here we consider permutation polynomials of the shape
f(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
, where
{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}
is the trace map given by
{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}X^{q^{i}}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{*}
. These permutation polynomials are interesting because of their simple algebraic structure. Charpin and Kyureghyan
[
2
]
first studied the polynomials of the form
f
and gave a complete characterization of permutation polynomials for
q=2
. In 2016, Kyureghyan and Zieve
[
10
]
studied permutation polynomials of the shape
X+\gamma{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
for odd
q
and constructed several classes of permutation polynomials. Motivated by the results of
[
2
,
10
]
, Li et al.
[
12
]
studied polynomials of the form
\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
over finite fields of even characteristic. To avoid repetitive work from
[
2
]
, the authors in
[
12
]
restricted themselves to the case
q>2
and constructed fifteen new classes of permutation polynomials. For more details on permutation polynomials of the shape
\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
, reader may refer to
[
2
,
6
,
9
,
10
,
12
,
16
]
and references therein.

Permutation polynomials have wide applications in the areas of mathematics and engineering such as coding theory
[
3
,
11
]
, cryptography
[
15
,
19
]
and combinatorial designs
[
4
]
. In symmetric key cryptography, permutation polynomials are widely applied to the design of block ciphers, where they are used in the design of so-called substitution boxes. In fact, permutation polynomials must be choosen in a way so as S-box becomes immune to some of the known attacks against block ciphers. For instance, the differential attack introduced by Biham and Shamir
[
1
]
is one of the most powerful attacks on block ciphers. In 1993, Nyberg
[
18
]
introduced the notion of differential uniformity to quantify the immunity of a function against differential attacks. Recently, Ellingsen et al.
[
5
]
generalised the notion of differential uniformity and defined the
c
-differential uniformity of a function
f
for any
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. For any
a,c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the
c
-derivative of
f
in the direction of
a
is given by
{}_{c}D_{f}(a,X):=f(X+a)-cf(X)
for all
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. For any
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, the
c
-Difference Distribution Table (
c
-DDT) entry at point
(a,b)
of
f
is given by
{}_{c}\Delta_{f}(a,b):=\lvert\{X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\mid{{}_{c}D}_{f}(a,X)=b\}\rvert
. The
c
-differential uniformity of
f
is defined as
{}_{c}\Delta_{f}:=\max\{_{c}\Delta_{f}(a,b)\mid a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}~\mbox{and}~a\neq 0~\mbox{when}~c=1\}
. When
{}_{c}\Delta_{f}=1,2,
then the function
f
is called perfect
c
-nonlinear (P
c
N) function and almost perfect
c
-nonlinear (AP
c
N) function, respectively. When
c=1
, then the notions of
c
-DDT and
c
-differential uniformity coincide with the classical notions of DDT and differential uniformity, respectively.

Since the appearence of the notion of the
c
-differential uniformity, several functions having low
c
-differential uniformity have been constructed in the recent past. The most recent list of power maps along with their
c
-differential uniformity can be found in
[
13
, Table 1]
, and the list of non-power maps with their
c
-differential uniformity is given in
[
13
, Table 2]
for
c\neq 1
. However, over finite fields of even characteristic, there are only few P
c
N and AP
c
N functions, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
7
,
8
,
17
,
20
]
. In this paper, we shall restrict ourselves to finite fields of even characteristic and study the
c
-differential uniformity of three classes of permutation polynomials of the shape
\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\backslash\{1\}
. The rationale behind excluding the case
c=1
is the fact that in this case linear term outside the trace function vanishes and the varible
X
remains only in the trace term. As a consequence, polynomials of the form
\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{n}/q}(X^{k})
will have very high differential uniformity.

The rest of the paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we consider the
c
-differential uniformity of the permutation polynomial
f(X):=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
i,n,t>0
are integers. We show that
f
is P
c
N for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
and if
\gcd(i,t)=1
then
{}_{c}\Delta_{f}\leq 2
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Since we are considering only permutation polynomials, and for
c=0
every permutation polynomial is P
c
N, we have excluded the trivial case
c=0
in rest of the paper. In Section
3
, we shall show that the permutation polynomial
g(X):=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
n,t>0
are integers, is P
c
N for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Section
4
is devoted to the
c
-differential uniformity of the permutation polynomial
h(X):=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
i,n,t>0
are integers and either
n
is even; or
n
is odd and
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\left(\frac{1}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i+1}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{\gcd(2^{i+1}-1,2^{t}-1)}}\neq 1
. We show that
h
is P
c
N for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, and if
\gcd(i,t)=1
then the
c
-differential uniformity of
h
is
\leq 2
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Finally, we summarize the paper with some concluding remarks in Section
5
.

2.
The
c
-differential uniformity of the function
f

In this section, we shall consider the
c
-differential uniformity of the function
f(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
i,n,t>0
are integers. It has been proved in
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
that
f
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. We first recall a lemma that will be used throughout this paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
14
]

Let
q=p^{t}
,
i
be a positive integer and
f(X)=X^{p^{i}}-\alpha X-\beta
where
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, in the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
f
has either zero, one or
p^{d}
roots where
d=\gcd(i,t)
.

The following theorem gives the
c
-differential uniformity of the function
f
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
q=2^{t}
and
f(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and
i,n,t>0
are integers. Then

(i)

f
is P
c
N over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

(ii)

{}_{c}\Delta_{f}\leq 2
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
when
\gcd(i,t)=1
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Recall that, for any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
, the
c
-DDT entry
{}_{c}\Delta_{f}(a,b)
is given by the number of solutions
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
of the following equation.

(2.1)

f(X+a)+cf(X)=b.

It is easy to see that when
a=0
then for each
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
, Equation (
2.1
) has a unique solution
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. For
a\neq 0
, Equation (
2.1
) can be written as

(2.2)

\begin{split}\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q}a+a^{q}X)^{2^{i}})&=B,\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B=C(b+\gamma a+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(a^{2^{i}(q+1)}))
and
\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{1+c}
. Notice that, when
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
, then the above equation reduces to

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})+a^{2^{i}}C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{2^{i}})^{q}+X^{2^{i}})=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)}),

which has a unique solution for each
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
(and hence for each
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
). Now, let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, from Equation (
2.2
) we have

(2.3)

\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{2}}+X)a^{q})^{2^{i}}\right)=B.

We shall now study the solutions of Equation (
2.3
), in three different cases, namely,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, respectively.

Case 1
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Now, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{2}}+X)a^{q})^{2^{i}}\right)
. Then, from Equation (
2.3
), we have
\displaystyle X=\frac{u+B}{\gamma}
. Putting the value of
X
into Equation (
2.3
), we have

\begin{split}u&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(u^{2}+(B^{q}+B)u+B^{q+1}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)+CB_{1}\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{2^{i}(q+1)})+CB_{1},\\
\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B_{1}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{(B^{q^{2}}+B)a^{q}}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)
and the first equality holds as
u,\gamma^{q+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and the second equality holds as
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(1)=0
and
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{q}+B)=0
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, Equation (
2.3
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 2
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})
and
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{2}}+X)a^{q})^{2^{i}}\right)
. Then, from Equation (
2.3
), we have
\displaystyle X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}
. Using the properties of the trace function, we shall now simplify
u
and
v
. Consider

\begin{split}u&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(u^{2}+((B+Cv)^{q}+B+Cv)u+C^{q+1}v^{2}+(B^{q}C+BC^{q})v+B^{q+1}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(((BC^{q})^{q}+BC^{q})v+B^{q+1}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(B^{2^{i}(q+1)}\right),\end{split}

where the first equality holds because
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
and the second equality holds because
C^{q+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{q\cdot 2^{i}})={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{2^{i}})
and the third equality holds as
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((BC^{q})^{q\cdot 2^{i}})={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((BC^{q})^{2^{i}})
. Now consider
v
which is given by

\begin{split}v&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{2}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)^{2^{i}}a^{q\cdot 2^{i}}\right)=B_{1},\end{split}

where the last equality holds as
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
C,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Thus,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Equation (
2.3
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 3
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Again, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q+1)})
,
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{2}}+X)a^{q})^{2^{i}}\right)
. Then, from Equation (
2.3
), we have

(2.4)

X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}.

We shall now simplify
u
and
v
. Consider
u
which is given by

\begin{split}u=&~\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left((u^{2}+((B+Cv)^{q}+B+Cv)u+C^{q+1}v^{2}+(BC^{q}+B^{q}C)v+B^{q+1})^{2^{i}}\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)}}\left(v^{2^{i+1}}{\mathcal{C}}+v^{2^{i}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{2^{i}(q+1)})\right),\\
\end{split}

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((BC^{q}+B^{q}C)^{2^{i}})
,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(C^{2^{i}(q+1)})
and the first equality holds because
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and last equality holds as
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{q\cdot 2^{i}})={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{2^{i}})
. Now consider
v
which is given by

\begin{split}v={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{2}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)^{2^{i}}a^{q\cdot 2^{i}}\right)=v^{2^{i}}C_{1}+B_{1},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle C_{1}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{(C^{q^{2}}+C)a^{q}}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)
and the last equality holds as
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. When
C_{1}=0
, then
v=B_{1}
and by putting the values of
u
and
v
in Equation (
2.4
), we get a unique solution

X=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}(q+1)+1}}\left((B_{1})^{2^{i+1}}{\mathcal{C}}+(B_{1})^{2^{i}}{\mathcal{B}}+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{2^{i}(q+1)})\right)+\frac{(CB_{1}+B)}{\gamma}

of Equation (
2.3
). In the event when
C_{1}\neq 0
, consider the equation

v^{2^{i}}+C_{1}^{-1}v+B_{1}C_{1}^{-1}=0.

Since
\gcd(i,t)=1
, from Lemma
2.1
the above equation can have at most two solutions
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, Equation (
2.3
) can have at most two solutions for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The
c
-differential uniformity of the function
g

In this section, we shall consider the
c
-differential uniformity of the function
g(X):=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
n,t>0
are integers. In
[
12
, Theorem 3.2]
, Li et al. proved that
g
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. The following theorem gives the
c
-differential uniformity of the function
g
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
q=2^{t}
and
g(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
and
n,t>0
are integers. Then
g
is P
c
N over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
the
c
-DDT entry
{}_{c}\Delta_{g}(a,b)
is given by the number of solutions
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
of the following equation.

(3.1)

\begin{split}\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{2}}a+a^{q^{2}}X))&=B,\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B=C\left(b+\gamma a+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(a^{q^{2}+1})\right)
and
\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{1+c}
. When
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then the above equation reduces to
\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})=B
, which has a unique solution for each
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. Now, let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
then Equation (
3.1
) can be rewritten as

(3.2)

\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})=B.

We shall split the analysis of the solutions of Equation (
3.2
) into three different cases, namely,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, respectively.

Case 1
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Now, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{q^{2}+1})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})
. Then, from Equation (
3.2
), we have
\displaystyle X=\frac{u+B}{\gamma}
. Putting the value of
X
into Equation (
3.2
), we have

\begin{split}u&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(u^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+B^{q^{2}+1})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{B^{q^{4}}+B}{\gamma}\right)a^{q^{2}}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{q^{2}+1})+CB_{1},\\
\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B_{1}:=\frac{1}{\gamma}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left((B^{q^{4}}+B)a^{q^{2}}\right)
and equalities hold because
u,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, Equation (
3.2
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 2
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this case, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{(q^{2}+1)})
and
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})
. Then, from Equation (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}
. Using properties of the trace function, we shall now simplify
u
and
v
. Consider
u
which is given by

\begin{split}u=&~\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(u^{2}+(Cv)^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+((BC)^{q^{2}}+BC)v+B^{q^{2}+1})\\
=&\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left(v^{2}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(C^{2})+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{q^{2}+1})\right),\\
\end{split}

where equalities hold because
u,v,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{q^{2}}+B)=0
and
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((BC)^{q^{2}}+BC)=0
. Now consider
v
which is given by

\begin{split}v&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{4}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)a^{q^{2}}\right)=B_{1}\end{split}

where last equality holds as
u,v,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Equation (
3.2
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 3
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Again, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{(q^{2}+1)})
and
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})
. Then, from Equation (
3.2
), we have

(3.3)

X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}.

Now, consider
u
which is given by

\begin{split}u=&~\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(u^{2}+((B+Cv)^{q^{2}}+B+Cv)u+C^{q^{2}+1}v^{2}+(BC^{q^{2}}+B^{q^{2}}C)v+B^{q^{2}+1}\right)\\
=&\frac{1}{\gamma^{2}}\left(v^{2}{\mathcal{C}}+v{\mathcal{B}}+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B^{q^{2}+1})\right),\\
\end{split}

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(BC^{q^{2}}+B^{q^{2}}C)
,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(C^{q^{2}+1})
and equalities hold because
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{q^{2}}+B+Cv)=0
. Now consider
v
which is given by

\begin{split}v&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{4}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)a^{q^{2}}\right)=vC_{1}+B_{1},\end{split}

where
\displaystyle C_{1}=\frac{1}{\gamma}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left((C^{q^{4}}+C)a^{q^{2}}\right)
and the last equality holds as
u,v,\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. From above equation we get a unique solution for
v
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, Equation (
3.2
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The
c
-differential uniformity of the function
h

In this section, we shall consider the
c
-differential uniformity of a more general class of permutations than
g
. Let
h(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
q=2^{t}
and
i,n,t>0
are integers. It has been proved in
[
12
, Theorem 3.3]
that
h
is a permutation polynomial over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
if either
n
is even; or
n
is odd and
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}+1}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{\gcd(2^{i+1}-1,2^{t}-1)}}\neq 1
. The following theorem gives the
c
-differential uniformity of the function
h
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
q=2^{t}
and
h(X)=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
,
i,n,t>0
are integers and either
n
is even; or
n
is odd and
\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i}+1}}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{\gcd(2^{i+1}-1,2^{t}-1)}}\neq 1
. Then

(i)

h
is P
c
N over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
,

(ii)

{}_{c}\Delta_{h}\leq 2
for all
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
when
\gcd(i,t)=1
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
. For any
(a,b)\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
the
c
-DDT entry
{}_{c}\Delta_{h}(a,b)
is given by the number of solutions
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
of the following equation.

(4.1)

\begin{split}\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((X^{q^{2}}a+a^{q^{2}}X)^{2^{i}})&=B,\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B=C(b+\gamma a+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(a^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}))
and
\displaystyle C=\frac{1}{1+c}
. Notice that when
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Equation (
4.1
) reduces to
B=\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})
, which has a unique solution for each
B\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. Now, let
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then, from Equation (
4.1
) we have

(4.2)

\gamma X+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})^{2^{i}}\right)=B.

Now we shall split the analysis of the solutions of Equation (
4.2
) into three different cases, namely,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
,
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, respectively.

Case 1
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
and
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)})+C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})^{2^{i}}\right).
Then, from Equation (
4.2
), we have
\displaystyle X=\frac{u+B}{\gamma}
. Putting the value of
X
into Equation (
4.2
), we have

\begin{split}u&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{u^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+B^{q^{2}+1}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)+C_{1}\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left({\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(\left(\frac{u^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+B^{q^{2}+1}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\right)+C_{1}\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\left(\frac{u}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i+1}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}(1)\right)+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{B}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)+C_{1}\\
&=\begin{cases}B_{1}+C_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is even}\\
u^{2^{i+1}}T_{1}+B_{1}+C_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is odd},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\displaystyle B_{1}:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{B}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)
,
\displaystyle C_{1}:=C\cdot{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{(B^{q^{4}}+B)a^{q^{2}}}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)
and
\displaystyle T_{1}:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}\right)
. Notice that when
n
is odd,
\displaystyle T_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{\gcd(2^{i+1}-1,2^{t}-1)}}\neq 1
and hence we get only one solution
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{2}
, Equation (
4.2
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 2
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)
and
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})^{2^{i}}\right).
Then, from Equation (
4.2
), we have

(4.3)

X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}.

Using the properties of the trace function, we shall now simplify
u
and
v
. Consider
u
which is given by

\begin{split}u&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{u^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+C^{2}v^{2}+((BC)^{q^{2}}+BC)v+B^{q^{2}+1}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(\left(u^{2}+(B^{q^{2}}+B)u+C^{2}v^{2}+((BC)^{q^{2}}+BC)v+B^{q^{2}+1}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\right)\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(\left(u^{2}+C^{2}v^{2}+B^{q^{2}+1}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\right)\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{u^{2^{i+1}}}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(1\right)+\frac{v^{2^{i+1}}}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(C^{2^{i+1}}\right)+\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q^{2}}\left(B^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)\right)\\
&=\begin{cases}v^{2^{i+1}}C_{2}+B_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is even}\\
u^{2^{i+1}}T_{1}+v^{2^{i+1}}C_{2}+B_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is odd},\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\displaystyle C_{2}:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{C}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i+1}}\right)
. Again, since
\displaystyle T_{1}^{\frac{q-1}{\gcd(2^{i+1}-1,2^{t}-1)}}\neq 1
when
n
is odd, for any fixed
v
, we get a unique solution
u\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
of the above equation. Now consider
v
which is given by

\begin{split}v&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{4}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)^{2^{i}}a^{q^{2}2^{i}}\right)=\frac{C_{1}}{C}.\end{split}

Now putting the values of
u,v
into Equation (
4.3
), we get a unique solution
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
of Equation (
4.2
). Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
, Equation (
4.2
) has a unique solution for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
.

Case 3
Let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
. Again, in this case too, let
\displaystyle u:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(X^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)
and
v:={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(((X^{q^{4}}+X)a^{q^{2}})^{2^{i}}\right).
Then, from Equation (
4.2
), we have
\displaystyle X=\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}.
Now consider
u
which is given by

(4.4)

\begin{split}u&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{u^{2}+((B+Cv)^{q^{2}}+B+Cv)u+C^{q^{2}+1}v^{2}+(C^{q^{2}}B+CB^{q^{2}})v+B^{q^{2}+1}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)\\
&={\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/{q^{2}}}\left((u^{2}+C^{q^{2}+1}v^{2}+(C^{q^{2}}B+CB^{q^{2}})v+B^{q^{2}+1})^{2^{i}}\right)\right)\\
&=u^{2^{i+1}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2}/q}\left(\frac{1}{\gamma^{2^{i+1}}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/{q^{2}}}(1)\right)+v^{2^{i+1}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/{q}}\left(\left(\frac{C}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)\\
&\quad\quad+v^{2^{i}}{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/{q}}\left(\left(\frac{C^{q^{2}}B+CB^{q^{2}}}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)+{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/{q}}\left(\left(\frac{B}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)\\
&=\begin{cases}v^{2^{i+1}}{\mathcal{C}}+v^{2^{i}}{\mathcal{B}}+B_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is even}\\
u^{2^{i+1}}T_{1}+v^{2^{i+1}}{\mathcal{C}}+v^{2^{i}}{\mathcal{B}}+B_{1}~&~\mbox{when}~n~\mbox{is odd},\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

where
\displaystyle{\mathcal{B}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{BC^{q^{2}}+B^{q^{2}}C}{\gamma^{2}}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)
,
\displaystyle{\mathcal{C}}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{C}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}(q^{2}+1)}\right)
and these equalities hold because
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
and
{\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}((B+Cv)^{q^{2}})={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}(B+Cv)
. Now consider
v
which is given by

(4.5)

v={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)^{q^{4}}+\left(\frac{u+Cv+B}{\gamma}\right)\right)a^{q^{2}}\right)=v^{2^{i}}C_{3}+\frac{C_{1}}{C},

where
\displaystyle C_{3}={\rm Tr}_{q^{2n}/q}\left(\left(\frac{(C^{q^{4}}+C)a^{q^{2}}}{\gamma}\right)^{2^{i}}\right)
and the second equality holds as
u,v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}^{*}
. Now, if
C_{3}=0
, then we have a unique solution
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
of Equation (
4.5
). Thus, Equation (
4.2
) has a unique solution in this case. If
C_{3}\neq 0
, then Equation (
4.5
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle v^{2^{i}}+\frac{v}{C_{3}}+\frac{C_{1}}{CC_{3}}=0.

From Lemma
2.1
, we know that if
\gcd(i,t)=1
, then the above equation can have at most two solutions
v\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Also, corresponding to each solution
v
of Equation (
4.5
), we get a unique solution
u
of Equation (
4.4
). Thus, for
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
, Equation (
4.2
) has at most two solutions for each
a\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}\backslash\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2n}}
. This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Conclusion

We have cosntructed three new classes of P
c
N permutations and two new classes of AP
c
N permutations for some specific values of
c
. Since there are only few P
c
N and AP
c
N permutations over finite fields of even characteristic, it would be an interesting problem to construct more such permutations.

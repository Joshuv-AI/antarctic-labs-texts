A Kripke Semantics for Hajek’s BL
Andrew Lewis-Smith

Abstract
We provide a generalisation of Kripke semantics for Petr Hajek’s Basic Logic and prove soundness and completeness of the same with respect to our semantics. We find this semantics easily specialises to the linearly ordered Kripke frames for Godel-Dummett logic which 𝐁𝐋 \bf BL properly contains. Our soundness, deduction theorem and completeness arguments further strengthen this analogy. This paper extends the insights of [ 11 ] from 𝐆𝐁𝐋 e ​ w ​ f {\bf GBL}_{ewf} to the case of 𝐁𝐋 \bf BL .

2.1
BL and MV-algebras

We situate the algebraic semantics characterising
\bf BL
in terms of the somewhat larger theory of residuated lattices.

Definition 4
.

\mathcal{A}=\langle A,\land,\lor,\otimes,1,\to\rangle
is called a
\emph{commutativeresiduatedlattice}
if

•

\langle A,\wedge,\vee,\otimes,1\rangle
is a commutative lattice-ordered monoid

•

x\otimes y\leq z
if and only if
x\leq y\rightarrow z

Definition 5

(
\bf BL
-algebras,
\bf BL
-chains)
.

A
\bf BL
-algebra
is a bounded, commutative residuated lattice which satisfies the
divisibility property
: if
x\leq y
then
y\otimes(y\rightarrow x)=x
; pre-linear:
(x\to y)\lor(y\to x)
; bounded from below by
\bot
, i.e.
\bot\leq x
for all
x\in A
, and integral in that
1
is the top element of the lattice, i.e.
x\leq 1
for all
x\in A
. In this case we also denote
1
by
\top
. Finally, we note the condition of the Divisibility property is equivalent to requiring that the residuated lattice satisfy the equation
x\otimes(x\rightarrow y)=y\otimes(y\rightarrow x)
. A
\bf BL
-chain is a totally-ordered
\bf BL
-algebra.

Definition 6

(
\bf MV
-algebra)
.

A
\bf BL
-algebra is called an
\bf MV
-algebra if the negation map (
\neg x=x\rightarrow\bot
) is an involution, i.e.
(x\rightarrow\bot)\rightarrow\bot=x
, for all
x
.

\bf MV
-algebras provide an algebraic semantics for classical Łukasiewicz logic. Here we are interested in a particular MV algebra which we will use in our Kripke semantics for
\bf BL
:

Definition 7

(Standard
\bf MV
-chain)
.

For
x\in[0,1]
, let
\overline{x}:=1-x
. The

\bf MV
-chain
, denoted
[0,1]_{{\rm MV}}
, is the
\bf MV
-algebra defined as follows: The domain of
[0,1]_{{\rm MV}}
is the unit interval
[0,1]

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\top&:=&1\\[5.69054pt]
\bot&:=&0\\[5.69054pt]
x\wedge y&:=&\min\{x,y\}\\[5.69054pt]
x\vee y&:=&\max\{x,y\}\\[5.69054pt]
x\otimes y&:=&\max\{0,\overline{\overline{x}+\overline{y}}\}\\[5.69054pt]

Definition 15
.

Let
\lfloor\cdot\rfloor
be the usual “floor" operation on the standard MV-chain
[0,1]_{{\rm MV}}
, corresponding to the case distinction

\lfloor x\rfloor:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\top&\mbox{if}\quad x=\top\\[5.69054pt]
\bot&\mbox{if}\quad x<\top\end{array}\right.

which is known as the “Monteiro-Baaz
\Delta
-operator”. Given a (not necessarily sloping) function
f\colon W\to[0,1]
and a
w\in W
, let us write
\inf_{v\succeq w}
for the following construction:

\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v):=\min\{f(w),\inf_{v\succ w}\lfloor f(v)\rfloor\}

where
\inf_{v\succ w}\lfloor f(v)\rfloor
is the infimum of the set
\{\lfloor f(v)\rfloor:v\succ w\}\subseteq[0,1]
.

Lemma 16
.

This definition of
\inf_{v\succeq w}
can also be equivalently written as

\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f(w)&\mbox{if}\quad\forall v\succ w(f(v)=\top)\\[5.69054pt]
\bot&\mbox{if}\quad\exists v\succ w(f(v)<\top)\end{array}\right.

and for any
f\colon W\to[0,1]
the function
\lambda w.\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v)
is a sloping function.

Proof.

First let us show that this is an equivalent definition. Consider two cases:

Case 1
.
\forall v\succ w(f(v)=\top)
. In this case
\inf_{v\succ w}\lfloor f(v)\rfloor=\top
and hence

\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v)=\min\{f(w),\top\}=f(w)

Case 2
.
\exists v\succ w(f(v)<\top)
. In this case
\inf_{v\succ w}\lfloor f(v)\rfloor=\bot

\inf_{v\succeq w}f(v)=\min\{f(w),\bot\}=\bot

In order to see that
\lambda w.\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v)
is a sloping function, assume that for some
w
we have
\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}f(v)>\bot
, and let
w^{\prime}\succ w
. By definition we have that
\forall v\succ w(f(v)=\top)
, and hence
f(w^{\prime})=\top
and
\forall v\succ w^{\prime}(f(v)=\top)
, which implies
\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w^{\prime}}f(v)=\top
.
∎

Lemma 20
.

(The sloping functions are linearly ordered in LBM’s.) Let
f,g:W\to[0,1]_{\bf MV}
be sloping for
\bf BL
. Then:

\forall v\succeq w:(f(v)\geq g(v))\lor\forall v\succeq w:(g(v)\geq f(v))

Proof.

We prove
\neg\forall v\succeq w:(f(v)\geq g(v))\Rightarrow\forall v\succeq w:(g(v)\geq f(v))
as this is classically equivalent to the above statement. So assume that
\neg\forall v\succeq w:(f(v)\geq g(v))
. Then
\exists v\succeq w:(f(v)<g(v))
. But then
g(v)>\bot
; and since
f,g
are sloping, this means for any

we have
g(v\prime)=\top
and so
g(v\prime)\geq f(v\prime)
. On the other hand, for any
v\prime\prec v
,
f(v\prime)=\bot
as

is sloping, and since this is the least element of the ordering, in particular we have
g(v\prime)\geq f(v\prime)
. In either case, we have
\forall v\succeq w:(g(v)\geq f(v))
as desired.
∎

translates directly into a universally quantifed expression, i.e. it is (again) a standard
\inf_{v\succeq w}
operation (on a set).

Therefore:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}w\Vdash^{\rm LK}\psi\to\chi&\equiv&\forall v\succeq w((v\Vdash^{\rm LK}\psi)\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LK}\chi))\\[2.84526pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textup{(IH)}}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}&\forall v\succeq w((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)=\top\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\chi)=\top)\\[5.69054pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{item:compositionality})}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}&\forall v\succeq w((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\chi)=\top)\\[5.69054pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{item:inf})}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}&{\bf\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor}_{v\succeq w}((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\chi))=\top\\[5.69054pt]
&\equiv&(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi\to\chi)=\top\end{array}

which concludes the proof.
∎

Soundness

We now prove the soundness of the Kripke semantics for
\bf BL
.

Theorem 27

(Soundness)
.

If
\Gamma\vdash_{\mbox{\scriptsize{\bf BL}}}\phi
then
\Gamma\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi
.

Proof.

By induction on the derivation of
\Gamma\vdash\phi
. Assume
\Gamma=\psi_{1},\ldots,\psi_{n}
and let
\otimes\Gamma:=\psi_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes\psi_{n}
. Fix a LBM-structure
\mathcal{M}=\langle{\cal W},\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\rangle
with
\mathcal{W}=\langle W,\succeq\rangle
, and let
w\in W
. We exhibit only one case, as the rest of the proof is analogous to that of
[
11
]
.

(PRELIN)
\Gamma\vdash(\phi\to\psi)\lor(\psi\to\phi)
. By Definition
22
, we need to show:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\otimes\Gamma)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\footnotesize{(\mbox{L.\ref{def:model}})}}}{{\leq}}&(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\phi\to\psi)\lor(\psi\to\phi))=\top\end{array}

which is equivalent to:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\otimes\Gamma)&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\footnotesize{(\mbox{L.\ref{std-mv-chain}})}}}{{\leq}}&\max\{(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\phi\to\psi)),(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\psi\to\phi))\}=\top\end{array}

where the right of the inequality means: Either
(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\phi\to\psi))=\top
or
(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\psi\to\phi))=\top
. Here we break into cases.

Case 1.

(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\phi\to\psi))=\top
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\phi\to\psi))=\top&\equiv&\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi)\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi))=\top\\[2.84526pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}&{\forall v:v\succeq w}((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi)\leq(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi))\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

Case 2.

(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\psi\to\phi))=\top
. We have:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(w\Vdash^{\rm LBM}(\psi\to\phi))=\top&\equiv&\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\to(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi))=\top\\[2.84526pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle}}{{\Leftrightarrow}}&{\forall v:v\succeq w}((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\leq(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi))\\[5.69054pt]
\end{array}

These latter cases show that we must then prove:

\forall v:v\succeq w((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi)\leq(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi))\lor\forall v:v\succeq w((v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\leq(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi))

(1)

But by
19
,
\lambda w.(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\phi)\colon W\to[0,1]_{\bf MV}
and
\lambda w.(v\Vdash^{\rm LBM}\psi)\colon W\to[0,1]_{\bf MV}
are sloping functions; and by
20
the sloping functions for LBM’s are linearly ordered, so that indeed
1
above holds.
∎

8
LBM’s and Poset Products

A
poset product
(cf.
[
2
]
and
[
9
]
) is defined over a poset
{\cal W}=\langle W,\preceq\rangle
as the algebra
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
of signature
\mathcal{L}_{\otimes}
whose elements are sloping functions
f\colon W\to[0,1]_{\bf MV}
and operations are defined as below:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(\bot)(w)&:=&\bot\\[5.69054pt]
(f_{1}\wedge f_{2})(w)&:=&\min\{f_{1}w,f_{2}w\}\\[5.69054pt]
(f_{1}\vee f_{2})(w)&:=&\max\{f_{1}w,f_{2}w\}\\[5.69054pt]
(f_{1}\otimes f_{2})(w)&:=&\max\{0,\overline{\overline{f_{1}w}+\overline{f_{2}w}}\}\\[5.69054pt]
(f_{1}\to f_{2})(w)&:=&\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}f_{1}(w)\to f_{2}(w)&\mbox{if}\quad\forall v\succ w(f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v))\\[5.69054pt]
\bot&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Since
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are sloping functions, we have that

\forall v\succ w(f_{1}(v)\leq f_{2}(v))\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\forall v\succ w((f_{1}(v)\to f_{2}(v))=\top)

Therefore, this last clause of the definition can be simplified to

\begin{array}[]{lcl}(f_{1}\to f_{2})(w)&:=&\lfloor\mathbf{inf}\rfloor_{v\succeq w}(f_{1}(v)\to f_{2}(v))\end{array}

Definition 28

(Poset Product semantics for
\mathcal{L}_{\otimes}
)
.

Let
{\cal W}=\langle W,\preceq\rangle
be a fixed linearly ordered poset, and
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
be the poset product described above. Given
h:Atoms\to{\bf A}_{\cal W}
an assignment of atomic formulas to elements of
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
, any formula
\phi
can be mapped to an element
\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}\in{\bf A}_{\cal W}
as follows:

\begin{array}[]{lcl}\llbracket p\rrbracket_{h}&:=&h(p)\quad(\mbox{for atomic formulas $p$})\\[5.69054pt]
\llbracket\bot\rrbracket_{h}&:=&\bot\\[5.69054pt]
\llbracket\phi\wedge\psi\rrbracket_{h}&:=&\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}\wedge\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{h}\\[5.69054pt]
\llbracket\phi\vee\psi\rrbracket_{h}&:=&\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}\vee\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{h}\\[5.69054pt]
\llbracket\phi\otimes\psi\rrbracket_{h}&:=&\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}\otimes\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{h}\\[5.69054pt]
\llbracket\phi\to\psi\rrbracket_{h}&:=&\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}\to\llbracket\psi\rrbracket_{h}\end{array}

A formula
\phi
is said to be
valid in
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
under
h
if for every
w\in W

\llbracket\phi\rrbracket_{h}^{{\bf A}_{\cal W}}(w)=\top

(which is
1
in
[0,1]_{{\rm MV}}
). A formula
\phi
is said to be
valid in
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
if it is valid in
{\bf A}_{\cal W}
under
h
for any possible mapping
h\colon Atoms\to{\bf A}_{\cal W}
.

10
Conclusion

The state of the art abounds with work deeply algebraic in character. By introducing generalisations of Kripke semantics adequate for
\bf BL
(and neighbours) we seek a new perspective on fuzzy logics such as
\bf BL
as constructive or semi-constructive systems. This analogy is justified by a relational semantics that specialises in the present case to
\bf GD
. Moreover, relational semantics typically come pregnant with connections to proof theory, decidability and model theory. We hope in the fullness of time the semantics developed here (and in
[
11
]
and
[
4
]
) will suggest new calculi for
\bf BL
which currently lacks a suitable analytic, syntactically-based proof theory. The existent analytic calculi for
\bf BL
rely crucially on semantic insights, e.g.
[
10
]
.

There are three main alternatives on offer. One approach is semantic tableaux, or refutation systems. One can exploit the notion of unsatisfiability in our relational semantics to devise a proof system, as is done in classical modal or Intuitionistic logic (see
[
14
]
). Another possibility is to consider labelled calculi, e.g.
[
13
]
or
[
5
]
. Labelled calculi have been well-tested in cases of classical modal logics, extensions of classical modal systems, substructural cases, as well as logics over an intuitionistic or minimal base, although as yet untested on cases involving of divisibility ((A7)-(A8) p.4). Still another alternative are the multi-type calculi of e.g.
[
6
]
. These calculi seem well-adapted to systems defined over distributive structures, e.g. chains, having strong algebraic underpinnings. This bodes well for
\bf BL
, which is complete for
\bf BL
-chains.

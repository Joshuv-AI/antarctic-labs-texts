𝒫 \mathcal{P} -canonical forms and Drazin inverses
M. MOUÇOUF

Abstract
. In this paper, 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -canonical forms of ( A k ) k (A^{k})_{k} (or simply of the matrix A A ) are defined and some of their properties are proved. It is also shown how we can deduce from them many interesting informations about the matrix A A . In addition, it is proved that the 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -canonical forms of A A can be written as a sum of two parts, the geometric and the non-geometric parts of A A , and that the 𝒫 \mathcal{P} -canonical form of the Drazin inverse A d A_{d} of A A can be deduced by simply plugging − k -k for k k in the geometric part of A A . Finally, several examples are provided to illustrate the obtained results.

Introduction

Let
A
be a nonsingular matrix over a field
F

A

k
with
-k
in certain forms of
A^{k}

k

A^{-1}
of
A
. However, this fact is not proven in general. The problem here is that the
k
th power of a matrix can be represented in several forms. In fact, It can happen that in certain forms of
A^{k}
it is not even possible to substitute
k=-1
and, in other forms we can substitute
k=-1
but we does not obtain
A^{-1}
. For example, Let
A
be an
r
-circulant matrix. The expressions of
A^{k}
given in Theorem 4.1 of
[
16
]
and Theorem 3.1 of
[
17
]
do not provide
A^{-1}
when we plug into them
k=-1
.

In this paper, we consider an arbitrary matrix
A
, singular or nonsingular, with entries in a field
F
and we prove that if we plug
-k
for
k
into the geometric part of
(A^{k})_{k}
we get the
k
th power of the Drazin inverse of
A
. In order to avoid any confusion that may arise by using this plugging-in operation, we begin by showing that the representations of
(A^{k})_{k}
into them we plug
-k
for
k
, are canonical.

An element

of an associative ring is said to have a Drazin inverse
[

]

a^{k}ba=a^{k},\hskip 42.67912ptab=ba,\hskip 42.67912ptbab=b

for some nonnegative integer

. It is well known that any element of any associative ring has at most one Drazin inverse (see
[
8
]
).

The theory of Drazin inverse has been extensively studied and successfully applied in many fields of science such as functional analysis, matrix
computations, combinatorial problems, numerical analysis, statistics, population models, differential equations, Markov chains, control theory, and cryptography, etc.
[
5
,
4
,

,

,
14
,

]
. For this reason, many interesting properties of the Drazin inverse have been obtained
[
8
,
7
,
22
,
3
,
14
]
and a variety of direct and iterative methods have been developed for computation of this type of generalized inverse
[

,

,

,
2
,
25
,
7
,
22
,

,

,
24
]
.

It should be noted that the method presented herein provides a closed-form formula for the
k
th power of the Drazin inverse
A_{d}
of
A
and gives other interesting information on the matrix
A
such as the minimal polynomial and the Jordan-Chevalley decomposition of
A
. But for this, a closed-form formula for the

The organization of this paper is as follows. In Section 2, some algebraic results are established for the
F
-algebra of all linear recurrence sequences over
F
whose characteristic polynomials split over

, and then, the definitions of the geometric part and the non-geometric part of a linear recurrence sequence are given. Results obtained there will be used in Section 3 to derive similar results for the set of all sequences of matrices, with coefficients in

Let

and

. We note that there exist matrices

3.

The result follows immediately from
2.
above together with Theorem

.

∎

As a particular consequence of the above theorem, we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
F
be a field of characteristic
0
and
\mathcal{H}=(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{i})_{i\geqslant 0}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
is the sequence
\boldsymbol{\Gamma}=(0,1,2,\ldots)
. Let
A
be a square matrix over
F
with characteristic polynomial splits over
F
. Then the sequence of matrices obtained by plugging
-k
for
k
in the geometric part of
A
relative to
(\mathcal{S}^{\ast},\mathcal{H})
is
\boldsymbol{A}_{d}-\pi_{0}\boldsymbol{0}_{0}
.

Proof.

It is well known that

(\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{i})_{i\geqslant 0}=T(\boldsymbol{\Lambda_{i}})_{i\geqslant 0}

where
T
is the infinite invertible lower triangular matrix
(m!S(n,m))_{n,m\geqslant 0}
and
S(n,m)
are the Stirling numbers of the second kind (see, e.g. Quaintance and Gould
[
18
]
).

From this result, we deduce on the one hand that
\mathcal{H}
is contained in the
F
-vector space spanned by
\mathcal{T}
and on the other hand that
\mathcal{H}
is, as is well known, a basis of the
F_{\mathcal{S}^{\ast}}
-module
F_{\mathcal{S}^{\ast}}\langle\mathcal{T}\rangle
. The corollary is then a consequence of Theorem
4.4
.
∎

Now let us consider the case where
F=\mathbb{C}
the complex field. Consider the following sets

•

\mathcal{S}^{\ast}=\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}=(\lambda^{k})_{k\geqslant 0}/\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\lambda\neq 0\}

•

\widetilde{S}=\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}/\lambda\in\mathbb{R},\lambda\neq 0\}

•

\mathcal{S}^{+}=\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}/Im(\lambda)>0\}

•

\mathcal{S}^{-}=\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{\ast}/Im(\lambda)<0\}=\{\boldsymbol{\overline{\lambda}}/\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\}

•

\mathcal{S}^{+}_{1}=\{\frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda}+\boldsymbol{\overline{\lambda}}}{2}/\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\}=\{[r,\cos(\theta)]/0\neq r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\theta\in]0,\pi[\}
, where
[r,\cos(\theta)]=(r^{k}\cos(k\theta))_{k}

•

\mathcal{S}^{+}_{2}=\{\frac{\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\overline{\lambda}}}{2\mathrm{i}}/\boldsymbol{\lambda}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\}=\{[r,\sin(\theta)]/0\neq r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\theta\in]0,\pi[\}
, where
[r,\sin(\theta)]=(r^{k}\sin(k\theta))_{k}

Clearly
\widetilde{S},\mathcal{S}^{+},\mathcal{S}^{-}
constitute a partition of
\mathcal{S}^{\ast}
, and then the set
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}\cup\mathbb{S}
,
\mathbb{S}=\widetilde{S}\bigcup\mathcal{S}^{+}_{1}\bigcup\mathcal{S}^{+}_{2}
, is
\mathbb{C}
-linearly independent and thus is
\mathbb{R}
-linearly independent.

From the the general theory of linear recurrence sequences we have the following:

•

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}}\bigoplus\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}
is the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of real linear recurrence sequences whose characteristic polynomials are of the form
X^{m}P(X)
where the polynomials
P(X)\in\mathbb{R}[X]
are square-free with nonzero constant terms.

•

\boldsymbol{\Gamma}
is transcendental over
\mathbb{C}_{\mathcal{S}^{\ast}}
and it is so over
\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}

•

\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}}\bigoplus\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}[\Gamma]
is the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space of real linear recurrence sequences whose characteristic polynomials
P(X)\in\mathbb{R}[X]
.

•

If
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}}\bigoplus\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}[\Gamma]
then there exist
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\rho}_{l}\in\widetilde{S}
,
\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{1}=(r_{1}^{k}e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta_{1}})_{k},\ldots,\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{m}=(r_{m}^{k}e^{\mathrm{i}k\theta_{m}})_{k}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
,
\theta_{1},\ldots,\theta_{m}\in]0,\pi[
,
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{q}\in\mathbb{R}
,
P_{1},\ldots,P_{l}\in\mathbb{R}[X]
and
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{m}\in\mathbb{C}[X]
such that, for all
k\geq 0
,

\displaystyle u(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu_{i}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}(k)+\sum_{i=0}^{l}P_{i}(k)\rho_{i}^{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{m}(Q_{j}(k)\lambda_{j}^{k}+\overline{Q}_{j}(k)\overline{\lambda}_{j}^{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu_{i}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}(k)+\sum_{i=0}^{l}P_{i}(k)\rho_{i}^{k}+\sum_{i=0}^{m}(Q_{i}(k)\lambda_{i}^{k}+\overline{Q_{i}(k)\lambda_{i}^{k}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{q}\mu_{i}\boldsymbol{0}_{i}(k)+\sum_{i=0}^{l}P_{i}(k)\rho_{i}^{k}+

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{m}2Re(Q_{i}(k))[r_{i},\cos(\theta_{i})](k)-\sum_{i=0}^{m}2Im(Q_{i}(k))[r_{i},\sin(\theta_{i})](k).

This means that
\mathcal{S}^{\circ}\bigcup\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{i}/0\neq\lambda\in\mathbb{R},i\in\mathbb{N}\}\bigcup\{[r,\cos(\theta)]\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{i},[r,\sin(\theta)]\boldsymbol{\Gamma}^{i}/i\in\mathbb{N},\,\,0\neq r\in\mathbb{R}^{+},\,\,\theta\in]0,\pi[\}
spans the
\mathbb{R}
-vector space
\mathbb{R}_{\mathcal{S}^{\circ}}\bigoplus\mathbb{R}_{\mathbb{S}}[\boldsymbol{\Gamma}]
, and hence is a basis for it.

With the above results and notations we can formulate the following.

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
A\in M_{q}(\mathbb{R})
be a real matrix and let

.

for

is
\boldsymbol{A}_{d}-\pi_{0}\boldsymbol{0}_{0}

The proof follows simply from the fact that the equalities

and

\boldsymbol{\lambda}-\boldsymbol{\overline{\lambda}}=2\mathrm{i}[r,\sin(\theta)]

still valid when we plug in
-k
for
k
.
∎

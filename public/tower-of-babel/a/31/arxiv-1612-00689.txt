Sharp bounds for composition with quasiconformal mappings in Sobolev spaces
Marcos Oliva and Martí Prats

Abstract
Let ϕ \phi be a quasiconformal mapping, and let T ϕ T_{\phi} be the composition operator which maps f f to f ∘ ϕ f\circ\phi . Since ϕ \phi may not be bi-Lipschitz, the composition operator need not map Sobolev spaces to themselves. The study begins with the behavior of T ϕ T_{\phi} on L p L^{p} and W 1 , p W^{1,p} for 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty . This cases are well understood but alternative proofs of some known results are provided. Using interpolation techniques it is seen that compactly supported Bessel potential functions in H s , p H^{s,p} are sent to H s , q H^{s,q} whenever 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 for appropriate values of q q . The techniques used lead to sharp results and they can be applied to Besov spaces as well.

Introduction

Given a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
between domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, we consider the composition operator
T_{\phi}
which maps every measurable function
f:\Omega^{\prime}\to{\mathbb{R}}
to
f\circ\phi
. It is well known that
\phi
lies in a certain Sobolev space
W^{1,p}_{loc}
with
p>n
, that is, the space of locally
p
-integrable functions with locally
p-
integrable derivatives, and in some Hölder class
C^{s}
with
0<s<1
, i.e. for any
K\subset\Omega
compact
\phi
is bounded and continuous in
K
with
|\phi(x)-\phi(y)|\leq C_{K}|x-y|^{s}
for
x,y\in K
. The composition operator
T_{\phi}
is a self-map of
W^{1,n}
. However, since
\phi
may not be bi-Lipschitz, the composition operator does not necessarily map other Sobolev spaces to themselves.

A characterization of homeomorphisms which give rise to bounded composition operators is given in
[
Ukh93
]
and
[
Kle12
]
.

Over the last decade, the study on the stability of the planar Calderón inverse conductivity problem raised questions on the range of
T_{\phi}
. The ground-breaking work of Astala and Päivärinta
[
AP06
]
showing uniqueness of the solution to the problem was adapted by Barceló, Faraco and Ruiz in
[
BFR07
]
to provide stability in Lipschitz domains with only Hölder a priori conditions on the conductivities. Some years later, Clop, Faraco and Ruiz weakened the a priori assumption on the conductivities to just a fractional Sobolev condition in
[
CFR10
]
, allowing the method to be applied to non-continuous conductivities, and later on the regularity assumptions on the boundary of the domain where severely reduced in
[
FR13
]
. A deeper knowledge of the behavior of the composition operator may lead to better numerical methods for the electric impedance tomography (see
[
AMPS10
]
, for instance).

We study quasiconformal mappings
\phi
whose Jacobian determinant
J_{\phi}
satisfies the estimates

\left(\int_{U}J_{\phi}(x)^{a}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{a}}\leq C_{a},\mbox{\quad\quad and \quad\quad}\left(\int_{U}J_{\phi}(x)^{-b}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{b}}\leq C_{b}

(1.1)

for values
a>1
and
b>0
, where
U\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
is a certain domain (open and connected set). The existence of these values for any domain
U
compactly contained in
\Omega
can be derived from quasiconformality itself, but we may have better exponents for particular mappings, and this will imply better behavior of the composition operator.

In
[
HK08
, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2]
, it is shown that, under the first condition in (
1.1
) the composition operator
T_{\phi}
sends compactly supported
W^{1,p}
functions to
W^{1,q}
for certain couples
n\leq q<p
, that is, some integrability of the function and its gradient is lost. The main result of the present paper shows that this loss of integrability is common to all the Bessel potential spaces
H^{s,p}
with
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
and Besov spaces
B^{s}_{p,r}
with
0<s<1
,
1<p<\infty
and
0<r\leq\infty
(see Section
3
for the definitions). Note that
H^{0,p}=L^{p}
and
H^{1,p}=W^{1,p}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Given a quasiconformal mapping
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
between two domains in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
, a ball
B
with
2B\subset\Omega
, a ball
B^{\prime}
with
\phi(2B)\subset B^{\prime}
and positive real numbers
a
,
b
,
C_{a}
and
C_{b}
satisfying (
1.1
) for
U=2B
, let
q
be defined by
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{c}\left|\frac{s}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right|
, where we take
c=a
if
sp\geq n
and
c=b
if
sp<n
.
If
q>1
, then there exists a constant
C
such that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{{H}^{s,q}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{{H}^{s,p}(B^{\prime})}

(1.2)

and, if in addition
s\notin\{0,1\}
, then

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,r}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{p,r}(B^{\prime})}

(1.3)

for every locally integrable function
f
and every
0<r\leq\infty
, with constants not depending on
\phi
. If
s\in\{0,1\}
and
p=\infty
, then (
1.2
) holds as well.

Previous results (
[
CFR10
, Proposition 4.2]
and
[
HK13
, Theorem 1.2]
) show that
T_{\phi}
sends compactly supported
H^{s,q}\cap L^{\infty}
functions (with
0<s<1
) to
H^{\beta,q}
functions for certain
\beta<s
, that is, with a loss on the smoothness parameter. More precisely, the statement of the latter theorem settles that question for mappings between diagonal Besov spaces
B^{s}_{q,q}\to B^{\beta}_{q,q}
, and it establishes the supremum of the admissible values of
\beta
as
\frac{bs}{b+1-\frac{sq}{n}}=\frac{\frac{s}{q}}{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}\right)}
.

These results can be recovered from Theorem
1.1
. Indeed, by
[
RS96
, Theorem 2.2.5]
, for
0<s<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
,
0<r,\ell\leq\infty
and
0<\Theta<1
,

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{F^{\Theta s}_{\frac{q}{\Theta},r}}\leq C_{s,q,r,\Theta}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{F^{s}_{q,\ell}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}\mbox{\quad and \quad}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{\Theta s}_{\frac{q}{\Theta},\frac{r}{\Theta}}}\leq C_{s,q,r,\Theta}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,r}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}.

For
sq<n
, taking
\beta:=\frac{\frac{s}{q}}{\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{s}{n}\right)}
and
p
so that
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\beta}{n}\right)
one can check that
\frac{\beta}{s}=\frac{1/p}{1/q}
. Thus, chosing
\Theta=\frac{q}{p}
,
r:=q
and given a function
f\in B^{s}_{q,q}\cap L^{\infty}
with compact support, we have that
{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{\beta}_{p,p}}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,q}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}.

Using Theorem
1.1
, it follows that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{B^{\beta}_{q,p}(B)}\leq C_{s,p,q}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{\beta}_{p,p}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,q}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}},

recovering
[
HK13
, Theorem 1.2]
by elementary embeddings and obtaining the end-point with a change on the secondary integrability index. In Bessel potential spaces the estimate reads as

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{H^{\beta,q}(B)}\leq C_{s,p,q}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{\beta,p}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,q}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}.

However, the result in
[
HK13
, Theorem 1.1]
can only be partially recovered. Indeed, condition (
1.1
) implies that
\phi^{-1}\in W^{1,n(b+1)}(U)
, arguing as in (
2.2
) below. Thus,
\phi^{-1}\in C^{\frac{b}{b+1}}(U)
. Therefore,
\phi
satisfies that

|\phi(B)|\geq C|B|^{\alpha}

(1.4)

for every ball
B\subset U
, with
\alpha=\frac{b+1}{b}
. According to
[
HK13
, Theorem 1.1]
this implies that given a function
f\in B^{s}_{q,q}(\phi(B))
, the composition operator maps it to
f\circ\phi\in B^{\beta}_{q,q}(B)
, where
\beta=\frac{n}{q}-\alpha\left(\frac{n}{q}-s\right)
.
As before, one can check that this statement when
\alpha=\frac{b+1}{b}
is a consequence of Theorem
1.1
using the embeddings

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{F^{\beta}_{p,r}}\leq C_{s,q,r,\ell}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{F^{s}_{q,\ell}}\mbox{\quad and \quad}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{\beta}_{p,r}}\leq C_{s,q,r}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,r}},

which hold because
\beta=s-\frac{n}{q}+\frac{n}{p}
(see
[
Tri83
, Theorem 2.7.1]
).
As the reader may note, there may be values of
\alpha<\frac{b+1}{b}
for which (
1.4
) holds but with no counterpart in the spirit of (
1.1
). The planar case illustrates how this result must be taken into account.

Indeed, given a
K
-quasiconformal mapping
\phi:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{C}}
, we may choose

-\mathbf{b_{K}}<-b<0<a<\mathbf{a_{K}}

where
\mathbf{b_{K}}:=\frac{1}{K-1}
and let
\mathbf{a_{K}}:=\frac{K}{K-1}
. In
[
AIM09
, Theorem 13.4.2]
it is shown that
J_{\phi}^{a}
and
J_{\phi}^{-b}
are locally integrable, with

\fint_{B}J_{\phi}^{a}\leq\frac{C_{K}}{1-\frac{a}{\mathbf{a_{K}}}}\left(\frac{|\phi(B)|}{|B|}\right)^{a},

and

\fint_{B}J_{\phi}^{-b}\leq\frac{C_{K}}{1-\frac{b}{\mathbf{b_{K}}}}\left(\frac{|B|}{|\phi(B)|}\right)^{b}.

On the other hand, although for
b=\mathbf{b_{K}}
this integral may blow up (see
[
AIM09
, Theorem 13.2.3]
, for instance), still we have the end-point Hölder regularity

|\phi(B)|\geq C|B|^{K}.

Combining Theorem
1.1
and
[
HK13
, Theorem 1.1]
we get the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
K\geq 1
. Let
0\leq s\leq 1
and
1<p<\infty
. Given domains
\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{C}}
, a
K
-quasiconformal mapping
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
, a ball
B
with
2B\subset\Omega
, a ball
B^{\prime}
with
\phi(2B)\subset B^{\prime}
, if
1>\frac{1}{q}>\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{\mathbf{c_{K}}}\left|\frac{s}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right|
(where we take
\mathbf{c_{K}}=\mathbf{a_{K}}
if
sp\geq 2
and
\mathbf{c_{K}}=\mathbf{b_{K}}
if
sp<2
), there exists a constant
C
such that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{H^{s,q}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}(B^{\prime})}

and, if
s\notin\{0,1\}
, then

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{q,r}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{p,r}(B^{\prime})}.

Moreover, whenever
sp<2
and
\beta=s-(K-1)\left(\frac{2}{p}-s\right)>0
, we have that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}f}\right\|}_{B^{\beta}_{p,p}(B)}\leq C{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{p,p}(B^{\prime})}.

The results in Theorem
1.1
and Corollary
1.2
are sharp concerning the loss of integrability. In the critical setting (
sp=n
), this fact is obvious since the composition is a self-map for these spaces. Both examples presented in
[
HK13
]
illustrate the sharpness of the subcritical setting (
sp<n
), adapting the arguments above. In Section
4
we check that this extends to the Bessel-potential subcritical setting and we adapt one of these examples to the supercritical setting (
sp>n
).

In conclusion, we have seen that better integrability properties of the derivatives of quasiconformal mappings imply less loss of integrability in the composition, while better Hölder regularity implies less loss of smoothness. In general, we expect the integrability properties to be an open condition for higher dimensions as it happens in dimension 2 (see
[
AIM09
, Section 13.4.1]
). In higher dimensions, it is conjectured that one can take
b<\mathbf{b_{K}}:=\frac{1}{K^{({1}/{n-1})}-1}
in (
1.1
). If this is the case, the Hölder condition of the inverse mapping of exponent
K^{-1/(n-1)}
, which coincides with the Sobolev embedding of the conjectured endpoint Sobolev space for the inverse mapping, is reached by quasisymmetry (see
[
Kos
]
), that is, a closed condition for the loss of smoothness. It remains to see if this extends to the supercritical case and what happens in the fractional spaces between both end-points. We believe that these techniques can be applied to the finite distortion setting described in
[
Kle12
]
.

The paper is structured as follows. In Section
2
we revisit the proofs of the loss of integrability in the classical Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces. In Section
3
, we derive the proof of Theorem
1.1
by a subtle interpolation of those classical results. Finally, in Section
4
, we check the sharpness of the main theorem.

Theorem 2.7

Proof.

By the chain rule (see
[

\displaystyle{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f(\phi(z)))|^{q}|D^{t}\phi(z)|^{q}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f(\phi(z)))|^{q}|J_{\phi}(z)|^{\frac{q}{n}}\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}.

In case

2.1
) we have shown that

{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\Omega)}\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\phi(\Omega))}.

We need to study the supercritical and the subcritical cases. Let
1<p<\infty
with
p\neq n
(the case

\displaystyle{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla f(\phi(z))|^{p}|J_{\phi}(z)|\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{\phi}(z)^{\left(\frac{q}{n}-\frac{q}{p}\right)\frac{p}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}

\displaystyle\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}\left(\int_{\Omega}J_{\phi}(z)^{\frac{p-n}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}}\right)^{\frac{p-q}{pq}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\phi(\Omega))},

and it remains to control the integral of the Jacobian.
For (
1.1

or

First we study the supercritical case,
p>n
.
Since
p>q
, we have that
\frac{p-n}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}>0
, and we only need to check that

\frac{p-n}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}=a,

that is,
\frac{p-q}{pq}=\frac{p-n}{nap}
, which is equivalent to our first assumption

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right).

Next we study the case
p<n
and
p>q
. Here
\frac{p-n}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}<0
, and we will check the other equality, that is
\frac{p-n}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}=-b
,
or, changing signs,

\frac{n-p}{n}\frac{q}{p-q}=b,

which is equivalent to
\frac{p-q}{pq}=\frac{n-p}{nbp}
and finally, to

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}\right).

∎

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
1<p\leq\infty
. Given a
K
-quasiconformal mapping
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
, let
B
be a ball with
2B\subset\Omega
, let
B^{\prime}
a ball with
\phi(2B)\subset B^{\prime}
, and let
b>0
and
C_{b}
satisfy (
1.1
) for
U=2B
. Let
q
be defined as before.
If
p>n
, then

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p}\to{W}^{1,q}}\leq C_{K,n,p,q,|B|,|B^{\prime}|}\left(1+C_{a}^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}\right).

If
p=n
, then

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,n}\to{W}^{1,n}}\leq C_{K,n,b,C_{b},|B|,|B^{\prime}|}.

If
p<n
, then

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p}\to{W}^{1,q}}\leq C_{K,n,p,q,|B|,|B^{\prime}|}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}.

Proof.

We will assume that
B=B^{\prime}=B_{1}
, which can be achieved by translation and rescaling. Let
f\in W^{1,p}
. First take
n>p>q
, and let
\frac{1}{q^{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{n}
. By Leibniz’ rule and Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle{\left\|{\nabla(\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|{\nabla\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}+{\left\|{\varphi\cdot\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|{\nabla\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{n}}{\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{q^{*}}(2B_{1})}+{\left\|{\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}.

(2.4)

Since
\frac{1}{q^{*}}=\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{n}=\frac{1}{p^{*}}+\frac{1}{b}\frac{1}{p^{*}}
, Lemma
2.4
and the Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality (see
[
Eva98
, Theorem 5.6.1/2]
, for instance) give

{\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{q^{*}}(2B_{1})}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p^{*}}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{p^{*}}(\phi(2B_{1}))}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{p^{*}}(B_{1})}\leq C_{p,n}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{W^{1,p}(B_{1})}.

Theorem
2.7
gives

{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\phi(2B_{1}))}.

Back to (
2.2
), we have shown that

\displaystyle{\left\|{\nabla(\varphi_{B}\cdot f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,n,p}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p}}.

By the Poincaré inequality,

\displaystyle{\left\|{\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C{\left\|{\nabla(\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}\leq C_{K,n,p}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p}}.

The critical case,
p=q=n
, instead of (
2.2
) we use

\displaystyle{\left\|{\nabla(\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{n}(2B_{1})}

\displaystyle\leq{\left\|{\nabla\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}{\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{n}(2B_{1})}+{\left\|{\varphi}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}}{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{n}(2B_{1})}.

Theorem
2.7
gives

{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{n}(2B_{1})}\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{n}(\phi(2B_{1}))}\leq K^{\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{n}}

and using Lemma
2.4
and the continuous embedding
W^{1,n}(B_{1})\subset L^{\frac{(b+1)n}{b}}(B_{1})
we obtain

{\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{n}(2B_{1})}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{b}{b+1}\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\frac{(b+1)n}{b}}(\phi(2B_{1}))}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{b}{b+1}\frac{1}{n}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\frac{(b+1)n}{b}}(B_{1})}\leq C_{b,n,C_{b}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{W^{1,n}}.

Using again the Poincaré inequality we end the proof.

In the supercritical case, we can use the algebra structure of
W^{1,q}(2B_{1})
(see
[
RS96
, Section 4.6.4]
, for instance) to get

\displaystyle{\left\|{\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{W^{1,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,q}{\left\|{\varphi}\right\|}_{W^{1,q}(2B_{1})}{\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{W^{1,q}(2B_{1})}=C_{n,q}\left({\left\|{f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}+{\left\|{\nabla(f\circ\phi)}\right\|}_{L^{q}(2B_{1})}\right).

By Theorem
2.7
we obtain that

\displaystyle{\left\|{\varphi\cdot f\circ\phi}\right\|}_{W^{1,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{n,q}\left({\left\|{f}\right\|}_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+K^{\frac{1}{n}}C_{a}^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}{\left\|{\nabla f}\right\|}_{L^{p}(\phi(2B_{1}))}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{K,n,p,q}\left(1+C_{a}^{\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}}\right){\left\|{f}\right\|}_{W^{1,p}}.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

The proof given above gives constants which blow up when
p\to n
, because they depend on Gagliardo-Niremberg and Morrey inequalities. However, one can show by interpolation with the critical case (see Theorem
3.5
below) that there exist a constant
C=C_{K,n,p,b,C_{b},a,C_{a},|B|,|B^{\prime}|}
which depends continuously on
p\in\left[\frac{nb+n}{nb+1},\infty\right)
such that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p}\to{W}^{1,q}}\leq C.

3
Fractional Spaces

3.1
A note on interpolation

When dealing with fractional smoothness there are two families which are studied the most, the Besov and the Triebel-Lizorkin scale, which include a number of classical function spaces, as the Hölder-Zygmund class, the Bessel potentials, the Sobolev traces… (see
[
Tri83
, Section 2.3.5]
). We recall their definition:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
be the collection of all the families
\Psi=\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=0}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm supp}\,\psi_{0}\subset{\mathbb{D}}(0,2),&\\
{\rm supp}\,\psi_{j}\subset{\mathbb{D}}(0,2^{j+1})\setminus{\mathbb{D}}(0,2^{j-1})&\mbox{ if $j\geq 1$},\\
\end{array}\right.

for all multiindex
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}^{n}
there exists a constant
c_{\alpha}
such that

{\left\|{D^{\alpha}\psi_{j}}\right\|}_{\infty}\leq\frac{c_{\alpha}}{2^{j|\alpha|}}\mbox{\,\,\, for every $j\geq 0$}

and

\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\psi_{j}(x)=1\mbox{\,\,\, for every $x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}$.}

Definition 3.2
.

Given any Schwartz function
\psi\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
, its Fourier transform is

F\psi(\zeta)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}e^{-2\pi ix\cdot\zeta}\psi(x)dm(x).

This notion extends to the tempered distributions
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})^{\prime}
by duality (see
[
Gra08
, Definition 2.3.7]
).

Definition 3.3
.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\Psi\in\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. For any tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we define its non-homogeneous Besov norm

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{B^{s}_{p,q}}^{\Psi}={\left\|{\left\{2^{sj}{\left\|{\left(\psi_{j}\widehat{f}\right)\widecheck{\,}\,}\right\|}_{L^{p}}\right\}}\right\|}_{l^{q}},

and we call
B^{s}_{p,q}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
to the set of tempered distributions such that this norm is finite.

Let
s\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
1\leq p<\infty
,
1\leq q\leq\infty
and
\Psi\in\Phi({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. For any tempered distribution
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})
we define its non-homogeneous Triebel-Lizorkin norm

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{F^{s}_{p,q}}^{\Psi}={\left\|{{\left\|{\left\{2^{sj}\left(\psi_{j}\widehat{f}\right)\widecheck{\,}\right\}}\right\|}_{l^{q}}}\right\|}_{L^{p}},

and we call
F^{s}_{p,q}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
to the set of tempered distributions such that this norm is finite.

These norms are equivalent for different choices of
\Psi
. Usually one works with radial
\psi_{j}
and such that
\psi_{j+1}(x)=\psi_{j}(x/2)
for
j\geq 1
. Of course we will omit
\Psi
in our notation since it plays no role (see
[
Tri83
, Section 2.3]
).

We will work with the so-called Bessel potential spaces

H^{s,p}:=F^{s}_{p,2}.

For
s\in{\mathbb{N}}
and
1<p<\infty
we have that
W^{s,p}=H^{s,p}
and
L^{p}=H^{0,p}
.

We recall a result from interpolation theory.

Theorem 3.4

(see
[
BL76
, Theorem 6.4.5 - (7)]
)
.

Let
0<s<1
, let
1<p_{0},p_{1}<\infty
. Let

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-s}{p_{0}}+\frac{s}{p_{1}}.

Then

(L^{p_{0}},W^{1,p_{1}})_{[s]}=H^{s,p},

where the interpolation space
(\cdot,\cdot)_{[s]}
is defined as in
[
BL76
, Chapter 4]
.

Next, the interpolation property.

Theorem 3.5

(See
[
BL76
, Theorem 4.1.2]
)
.

Let
0<s<1
, let
1<p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}<\infty
. Let

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1-s}{p_{0}}+\frac{s}{p_{1}},\quad\quad\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-s}{q_{0}}+\frac{s}{q_{1}}.

Then, given a linear operator
T:L^{p_{0}}+W^{1,p_{1}}\to L^{q_{0}}+W^{1,q_{1}}
, it satisfies that

{\left\|{T}\right\|}_{H^{s,p},H^{s,q}}\leq{\left\|{T}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}},L^{q_{0}}}^{1-s}{\left\|{T}\right\|}_{W^{1,p_{1}},W^{1,q_{1}}}^{s}.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let

(3.1)

Proof.

Since

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-s}{q_{0}}+\frac{s}{q_{1}}.

We define

\frac{1-\frac{1}{p}}{n-s}=\frac{1-\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}}{n-\lambda}\mbox{ \quad\quad and \quad\quad}\frac{1-\frac{1}{q}}{n-s}=\frac{1-\frac{1}{q_{\lambda}}}{n-\lambda}

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{p}

\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}\right)\quad\implies\quad\frac{1}{q_{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}-\frac{\lambda}{n}\right).

So we assume the condition and plugging (
3.4
) we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}\right)-\left(1-\frac{1}{q_{\lambda}}\right)=\frac{n-\lambda}{n-s}\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-\lambda}{n-s}\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}\right)=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{n-\lambda}{n-s}\left(\frac{1}{p}-1\right)+\frac{n-\lambda}{n-s}-\frac{(n-\lambda)s}{(n-s)n}\right).

Again by (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q_{\lambda}}-\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}-1+\frac{n-\lambda}{n-s}-\frac{(n-\lambda)s}{(n-s)n}\right)=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}+\frac{-n^{2}+ns+n^{2}-n\lambda-ns+\lambda s}{(n-s)n}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p_{\lambda}}-\frac{\lambda}{n}\right).

Thus, we can use again both Corollary
2.6

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p}_{0}}

(3.5)

and Corollary
2.8

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{W^{1,p_{1}}\to W^{1,q_{1}}}\leq C_{K,n,p,q}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{n}}.

(3.6)

Estimate (

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}\to H^{s,q}}\leq{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-s}{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{W^{1,p_{1}}\to W^{1,q_{1}}}^{s}\leq C_{K,n,p_{1},q_{1}}^{s}C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}(1-s)+\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)s}.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
0<s<\infty
and let
1<p_{0}<\infty
. Let
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
satisfy that for every
\varepsilon
, there exists
p
with
|p-p_{0}|<\varepsilon
such that

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}}\leq 1.

Then,

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p_{0}}}\leq C,

with C depending on
s
and
p_{0}
.

Proof.

By the lifting property (see
[
Tri83
, Theorem 2.3.8]
), the linear map
I_{s}=F^{-1}(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}F
induces an isomorphism between
H^{s,r}
and

. By interpolation, the norm of this map varies continuously on

. Let
\varepsilon>0
and let

with

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}}\leq 1.

Thus,

{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p_{0}}}\leq C_{s,p_{0}}{\left\|{I_{s}f}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}}=C_{s,p_{0}}\left({\left\|{I_{s}f}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}}-{\left\|{I_{s}f}\right\|}_{L^{p}}\right)+C_{s,p_{0}}{\left\|{I_{s}f}\right\|}_{L^{p}}.

The last term in the right-hand side above is uniformly bounded by the continuity of
I_{s}
and our hypothesis. Namely, for
\varepsilon
small enough, we can grant

{\left\|{I_{s}f}\right\|}_{L^{p}}\leq C_{s,p_{0}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}}\leq C_{s,p_{0}}.

On the other hand, by the monotone convergence theorem

\int|I_{s}f|^{p}=\int_{|I_{s}f|\geq 1}|I_{s}f|^{p}+\int_{|I_{s}f|<1}|I_{s}f|^{p}\xrightarrow{p\to p_{0}}\int_{|I_{s}f|\geq 1}|I_{s}f|^{p_{0}}+\int_{|I_{s}f|<1}|I_{s}f|^{p_{0}}=\int|I_{s}f|^{p_{0}}.

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
n\geq 2
,
K\geq 1
,
0\leq s\leq 1
. Given a
K
-quasiconformal mapping
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
between two domains in

,
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega^{\prime}
with

and
\phi(2B_{1})\subset B_{1}
, and real numbers
b
and
C_{b}
satisfying (
1.1
) for

, there exists a constant

such that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{{H}^{s,\frac{n}{s}}\to{H}^{s,\frac{n}{s}}}\leq C_{K,n,s,b,C_{b}}.

(3.7)

Proof.

Let

be such that

(3.8)

It follows immediately that

3.3
). Let we see that

. Using identities (

) and (

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-s}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{q}\right)=\frac{1}{1-s}\left(\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{n}+\frac{1}{n}-\frac{s}{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{1-s}\left(\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1-s}{n}\right)\right)=\frac{1}{n}-\frac{1}{p_{0}}-\frac{1}{b}\frac{1}{p_{0}},

so
\frac{1}{q_{0}}=\frac{1}{p_{0}}+\frac{1}{b}\frac{1}{p_{0}}
with
q_{0}>1
. By Corollary
2.6
, we get

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

By Corollary
2.8
we also have that

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,p_{1}}\to{W}^{1,q_{1}}}={\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{{W}^{1,n}\to{W}^{1,n}}\leq C_{K,n,b,C_{b}}.

Since (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) hold, using Theorem
3.5
,

{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}\to H^{s,q}}\leq{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{L^{p_{0}}\to L^{q_{0}}}^{1-s}{\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}}\right\|}_{W^{1,p_{1}}\to W^{1,q_{1}}}^{s}\leq C_{b}^{\frac{1-s}{p_{0}}}C_{K,n,b,C_{b}}^{s}.

Note that

Take

\displaystyle={\left\|{T_{\phi}^{B_{1}}\widetilde{f}}\right\|}_{H^{s,q}}\leq c_{0}{\left\|{\widetilde{f}}\right\|}_{H^{s,p}}\leq c_{0}C_{n,s}{\left\|{\widetilde{f}}\right\|}_{H^{s,\frac{n}{s}}}

\displaystyle\leq c_{0}C_{n,s}{\left\|{\varphi_{B_{1}}\cdot}\right\|}_{H^{s,\frac{n}{s}}\to H^{s,\frac{n}{s}}}{\left\|{f}\right\|}_{H^{s,\frac{n}{s}}},

where

Lemma

∎

4
Sharpness

In this section we provide examples that show the sharpness of Theorem
1.1
.

For
k>0
, let
\phi_{k}(x):=x|x|^{k-1}
. We have that

D\phi(x)=|x|^{k-1}\left((\alpha-1)\frac{xx^{t}}{|x|^{2}}+Id\right).

Hence, we obtain
J_{\phi_{k}}(x)=k|x|^{n(k-1)}
, and, therefore,
\phi_{k}
is
k^{n-1}
-quasiconformal if
k\geq 1
and
\frac{(2-k)^{n}}{k}
-quasiconformal if
k<1
. It also satisfies (
1.1
) for
a<\frac{1}{1-k}
and
b>0
if
k<1
and for
a>1
and
0<b<\frac{1}{k-1}
if
k>1
regardless of the chosen domain
U
.

Next we recover the example given in
[
HK13
]
to show the sharpness on the subcritical setting (that is, when
sp<n
) for the composition of an unbounded
B^{s}_{p,p}
function function with a quasiconformal mapping. Let

f_{\rho}(x):=\max\{|x|^{-\rho}-1,0\}\text{ with }\rho>0.

It is known that
f_{\rho}
belongs to the space
H^{s,p}
if and only if
0<\rho<\frac{n}{p}-s
(see
[
RS96
, Lemma 2.3.1]
). Let
b>0
and
q\geq 1
such that

\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}\right),

let

{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{b}\left(\frac{1}{p}-\frac{s}{n}\right)-\frac{1}{q}>0

(4.1)

and
\delta>0
such that

(1-\delta)^{2}\left(\frac{n}{q}-s+n{\varepsilon}\right)>\frac{n}{q}-s.

Define

k:=(1-\delta)\left(\frac{1}{b}+1\right)

(4.2)

and
\rho
such that

(1-\delta)\left(\frac{n}{p}-s\right)<\rho<\frac{n}{p}-s.

(4.3)

Then,
\phi_{k}
satisfies (
1.1
) for
b
, and
f_{\rho}\in H^{s,p}
. On the other hand we have
f_{\rho}\circ\phi_{k}=f_{k\rho}
and combining (
4.2
) and (

), we get

\displaystyle k\rho-\left(\frac{n}{q}-s\right)>(1-\delta)^{2}\left(\frac{1}{b}+1\right)\left(\frac{n}{p}-s\right)-\left(\frac{n}{q}-s\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\delta)^{2}\left(\frac{n}{q}-s+n{\varepsilon}\right)-\left(\frac{n}{q}-s\right)>0.

Therefore,

. The interested reader may find in
[

, Lemma 4.2]

For the supercritical case, i.e.

, we define

Then, using again

, Lemma 2.3.1]
we have that

belongs to the Triebel-Lizorkin space

if and only if
s-\frac{n}{p}<\rho
. Let

and
q\geq 1
such that
\frac{1}{q}<\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{s}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right),
let

{\varepsilon}:=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{a}\left(\frac{s}{n}-\frac{1}{p}\right)-\frac{1}{q}

and
\delta>0
such that

Define

with sufficient small

\rho

Then,
\phi_{k}
satisfies (
1.1
) for

, and
g_{\rho}\in H^{s,p}
. On the other hand we have
g_{\rho}\circ\phi_{k}=g_{k\rho}
and arguing as before we obtain

\displaystyle s-\frac{n}{q}-k\rho>s-\frac{n}{q}-(1+\delta)^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{a}\right)\left(s-\frac{n}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=s-\frac{n}{q}-(1+\delta)^{2}\left(s-\frac{n}{q}-n{\varepsilon}\right)>0.

Therefore,

Heavy photophobic ALP at the LHC Preprint: KEK–TH–2595
Masashi Aiko, Motoi Endo, and Kåre Fridell

Abstract
We study the photophobic ALP model in high-mass regions under LHC Run-II. Since the ALP is predominantly coupled with electroweak gauge bosons such as Z ​ Z ZZ , W ​ W WW , and Z ​ γ Z\gamma , and less with di-photon, the model may be probed via multi-boson final-state processes. We find that on-shell ALP productions with Z ​ γ Z\gamma final states currently provide the best sensitivities for m a > 40 ​ GeV m_{a}>40\,\mathrm{GeV} .

Introduction

Pseudo-scalar bosons are one of the most attractive candidates for new particles.
They often appear in models with extended scalar sectors.
In particular, an axion-like particle (ALP) has been widely studied.
Its mass is assumed to be generated by explicit violations of global symmetries, and couplings with gauge bosons in the Standard Model (SM) are characterized by remnant shift symmetries.

We consider photophobic ALPs, whose masses are large, and coupling to di-photon is suppressed (cf., Ref.
Craig:2018kne
).

2
Model

The photophobic ALP is assumed to be coupled to the
{\rm SU(2)}_{L}
and
{\rm U(1)}_{Y}
gauge bosons.
The other direct couplings with SM particles are suppressed.
The ALP Lagrangian is shown as
Georgi:1986df

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm ALP}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\mu}a\partial^{\mu}a-\frac{1}{2}m_{a}^{2}a^{2}-c_{WW}\frac{a}{f_{a}}W_{\mu\nu}^{a}\widetilde{W}^{a\mu\nu}-c_{BB}\frac{a}{f_{a}}B_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{B}^{\mu\nu},

(1)

where
f_{a}
is the ALP decay constant, and where we assume that
m_{a}\lesssim f_{a}
is satisfied, see Refs.
Aiko:2023trb
;
Aiko:2023nox
for our conventions.
After electroweak symmetry breaking, the interactions are generally expressed as

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{int}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4}g_{a\gamma\gamma}aF_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{a\gamma Z}aZ_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{F}^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{4}g_{aZZ}aZ_{\mu\nu}\widetilde{Z}^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{aWW}aW_{\mu\nu}^{+}\widetilde{W}^{-\mu\nu}+\cdots.

(2)

The coupling constants are related to
c_{WW}
and
c_{BB}
as

\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{f_{a}}\quantity(s_{W}^{2}{c}_{WW}+c_{W}^{2}{c}_{BB}),

(3)

\displaystyle g_{aZ\gamma}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{f_{a}}\quantity(c_{WW}-c_{BB})s_{2W},

(4)

\displaystyle g_{aZZ}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{f_{a}}\quantity(c_{W}^{2}c_{WW}+s_{W}^{2}c_{BB}),

(5)

\displaystyle g_{aWW}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{f_{a}}c_{WW},

(6)

where
c_{W}=\cos{\theta_{W}}
,
s_{W}=\sin{\theta_{W}}
,
c_{2W}=\cos{2\theta_{W}}
, and
s_{2W}=\sin{2\theta_{W}}
with the Weinberg angle
\theta_{W}
.
In the photophobic model, the ALP coupling to di-photon is absent at the tree level,
i.e.
,
s_{W}^{2}{c}_{WW}+c_{W}^{2}{c}_{BB}=0
is satisfied, yielding
g_{a\gamma\gamma}=0
.
Then,
g_{aZ\gamma}
,
g_{aZZ}
are denoted in terms of
g_{aWW}
as

\displaystyle g_{aZ\gamma}=\frac{s_{W}}{c_{W}}g_{aWW},\quad g_{aZZ}=\frac{c_{2W}}{c_{W}^{2}}g_{aWW}.

(7)

In the model, there are no direct ALP couplings with SM particles other than
ZZ
,
WW
, and
Z\gamma
.
In particular, no direct ALP-gluon coupling
g_{aGG}
or ALP-fermion couplings
g_{aff}
are introduced.
In UV models (see
e.g.
, Ref.
Craig:2018kne
),
g_{a\gamma\gamma}=g_{aGG}=g_{aff}=0
is realized at a
high scale.
If the ALP is massless, as a result of the shift symmetry,
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
and
g_{aGG}
are not generated via renormalization group evolution, but
g_{aff}
is generated at the one-loop level (see Ref.
Bauer:2020jbp
;
Chala:2020wvs
).
On the other hand, non-zero ALP mass generates
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
and
g_{aGG}
at the one- and two-loop levels, respectively
Bauer:2017ris
, since the symmetry is softly broken.
Nonetheless, such loop-induced
g_{a\gamma\gamma}
and
g_{aGG}
are considered to be less effective for LHC searches.

Although the original setup of the photophobic ALP model predicts
g_{aGG}=0
, its presence may affect the collider signatures significantly.
We investigate LHC signatures when
g_{aGG}
is turned on at tree level in Appendix

2.1
Decay of ALP

The ALP decays into a pair of SM gauge bosons.
The partial decay widths for
a\to V_{i}V_{j}\ (V_{i,j}=\gamma,Z,W^{\pm})
are obtained as
Bauer:2017ris
;
Craig:2018kne
;
Bonilla:2021ufe

\displaystyle\Gamma(a\to V_{i}V_{j})=\frac{m_{a}^{3}}{32\pi(1+\delta_{ij})}\lambda^{3/2}\quantity(\frac{m_{V_{i}}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}}, \frac{m_{V_{j}}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}})\absolutevalue{g_{aV_{i}V_{j}}^{\rm eff}}^{2},

(8)

with
\lambda(x,y)=(1-x-y)^{2}-4xy
.
Here
m_{V}
is the gauge-boson mass (
m_{\gamma}=0
).
Note that
\delta_{ij}=0
for
a\to Z\gamma
and
a\to W^{+}W^{-}
.

The ALP coupling to di-photon
g^{\rm eff}_{a\gamma\gamma}
is induced by loop corrections with
g_{aWW}
in Eq. (
6
) as
Bauer:2017ris

\displaystyle g_{a\gamma\gamma}^{\rm eff}=\frac{2\alpha}{\pi}g_{aWW}B_{2}(\tau_{W}),

(9)

where
\alpha\equiv e^{2}/(4\pi)
with the QED coupling
e=gs_{W}
.
The loop function
B_{2}
is defined as

\displaystyle B_{2}(\tau)=1-(\tau-1)f^{2}(\tau),

(10)

where
f(\tau)
is given by

\displaystyle f(\tau)=\left\{\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix
\arcsin{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\tau}}}, \\
\frac{\pi}{2}+\frac{i}{2}\log{\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\tau}}{1-\sqrt{1-\tau}}},
\endlx@physics@matrix}\right.\qquad\matrixquantity{\lx@physics@matrix
\text{for}~\tau\geq 1\\
\text{for}~\tau< 1
\endlx@physics@matrix},

(11)

with
\tau_{W}=4m_{W}^{2}/m_{a}^{2}
.
On the other hand, it is sufficient for us to evaluate the decay widths for
a\to Z\gamma,\,ZZ,\,W^{+}W^{-}
at the tree level,
i.e.
,
g_{aV_{i}V_{j}}^{\rm eff}=g_{aV_{i}V_{j}}
.

When the ALP is lighter than the
Z
boson,
the ALP decays into either a pair of SM fermions,
a\to f\bar{f}
, or the fermions with a photon,
a\to Z^{*}\gamma\to f\bar{f}\gamma
, by exchanging an off-shell
Z
boson.
Since the ALP does not couple with SM fermions directly, the former proceeds via radiative corrections.
On the other hand, even though the latter is a three-body decay, it proceeds at the tree level, and thus, its decay width can be comparable to the former.

For
m_{a}\ll m_{Z}
, the partial decay width of
a\to Z^{*}\gamma\to f\bar{f}\gamma
is obtained as

\displaystyle\Gamma(a\to Z^{*}\gamma\to f\bar{f}\gamma)=N_{c}^{f}\frac{g_{Z}^{2}g_{aZ\gamma}^{2}}{30720\pi^{3}}\quantity[(g_{V,f})^{2}+(g_{A,f})^{2}]\frac{m_{a}^{7}}{m_{Z}^{4}},

(12)

where
N_{c}^{f}=1~(3)
is the color factor for leptons (quarks), and
g_{Z}=g/c_{W}
is the
Z
-boson coupling constant.
The vector and axial form-factors of
Z
boson are defined as
g_{V,f}=I_{3}^{f}-2\,Q_{f}s_{W}^{2}
and
g_{A,f}=I_{3}^{f}
.
The formula valid for any
m_{a}
is provided in Ref.
Aiko:2023trb
.

The ALP decays into a pair of leptons and heavy quarks,
a\to f\bar{f}
, via gauge-boson loops.
The partial decay widths are obtained as
Bauer:2017ris
;
Bauer:2020jbp

\displaystyle\Gamma(a\to f\bar{f})=N_{c}^{f}\frac{m_{a}m_{f}^{2}}{8\pi}\absolutevalue{g_{aff}^{\rm eff}}^{2}\sqrt{1-\frac{4m_{f}^{2}}{m_{a}^{2}}}.

(13)

The effective coupling induced by radiative corrections is given by

\displaystyle g_{aff}^{\rm eff}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4s_{W}^{2}}\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}g_{aWW}\ln{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{W}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{s_{W}c_{W}}\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}g_{aZ\gamma}Q_{f}\quantity(I_{3}^{f}-2Q_{f}s_{W}^{2})\ln{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{3}{s_{W}^{2}c_{W}^{2}}\frac{\alpha}{4\pi}g_{aZZ}\quantity(Q_{f}^{2}s_{W}^{4}-I_{3}^{f}Q_{f}s_{W}^{2}+\frac{1}{8})\ln{\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m_{Z}^{2}}},

(14)

where
Q_{f}
and
I_{3}^{f}
are the electric charge and the weak isospin of the fermion
f
, and
\tau_{f}=4m_{f}^{2}/m_{a}^{2}
.
Here, we keep the terms enhanced by
\ln{(\Lambda^2/m_f^2)}
or
\ln{(\Lambda^2/m_{V}^2)}

(V=Z,W)
.
We take the cutoff scale
\Lambda
to be
4\pi f_{a}
in this paper.
Subleading terms are given in Refs.
Bauer:2017ris
;
Bauer:2020jbp
;
Bonilla:2021ufe

Arias-Aragon:2022iwl

On the other hand, the inclusive rate for the ALP decaying into light hadrons is shown as (cf., Ref.
Bauer:2017ris
)

\displaystyle\Gamma(a\to{\rm hadrons})

\displaystyle=32\pi\alpha_{s}^{2}m_{a}^{3}\quantity(1+\frac{83}{4}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{\pi})\absolutevalue{g_{aGG}^{\rm eff}}^{2}+\sum_{f=u,d,s}\Gamma(a\to f\bar{f}),

(15)

where
m_{a}\gg\Lambda_{\rm QCD}
is assumed.
The effective coupling with gluons is given by

\displaystyle g_{aGG}^{\rm eff}=\frac{1}{32\pi^{2}}\sum_{f=u,d,s}g_{aff}^{\rm eff}.

(16)

Note that the above expression is not valid for

3
LHC signatures

Light ALPs are constrained by cosmology and precision/collider experiments.
If the ALP mass is smaller than
\sim 1\,\mathrm{GeV}
, the model is severely limited by cosmological measurements
Craig:2018kne
, while heavier ALPs are constrained by flavor and collider experiments.
In particular, heavy meson decays are sensitive to
g_{aWW}
for
m_{a}\lesssim 4.8\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
The current status is summarized in Ref.
Aiko:2023trb
.
For
4.8\,\mathrm{GeV}\lesssim m_{a}\lesssim m_{Z}
, LEP results have provided the leading constraints via searches for
Z
-boson invisible decays and
Z\to\gamma a\to\gamma jj
.
The detailed analysis can be found in Ref.
Craig:2018kne
.

In this paper, we focus on heavy ALPs.
For
m_{a}\gtrsim m_{Z}
the LHC measurements are sensitive to ALP contributions and have provided constraints on
g_{aWW}
.
In the previous work
Craig:2018kne
, on-shell ALP productions have been studied extensively with the LHC results at
\sqrt{s}=8\,\mathrm{TeV}
and
\int\!\mathcal{L}\,dt\simeq 20\,{\rm fb}^{-1}
.
The sensitivities are degraded rapidly as the ALP mass is increased because the production cross section/luminosity is not large enough.
In the following, we reinterpret the latest LHC results obtained at
\sqrt{s}=13\,\mathrm{TeV}
.
In particular, we analyze
pp\to WWW
,
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
, and
pp\to Z\gamma\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma
, via on-shell ALP production.

#3

#3

There are briefly two approaches to studying ALP contributions at the LHC: searches for new resonances in event distributions and for event number excesses after kinematical cuts.
For the former, among the ALP decay channels,
a\to Z\gamma
provides a sensitive probe and will be studied in this paper. See Refs.
CMS:2021xor
;
CMS:2021itu
for
a\to ZZ,WW
.
For the latter approach, we analyze
pp\to WWW
and
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
proceeding via
a\to WW
and
Z\gamma
respectively.
Although other processes such as
pp\to WWZ,WZZ
, and
ZZZ
(see Ref.
CMS:2020hjs
) may contribute, their limits on the ALP models are expected to be weaker than those obtained in this paper.

To generate the event samples, we employ
MadGraph5_aMC@NLO 3.5.2

Alwall:2014hca
interfaced with
PYTHIA 8.3

Bierlich:2022pfr
for parton showering and hadronization.
The ALP model is implemented by using a UFO model file generated with
FeynRules 2.3

Alloul:2013bka
.
Detector simulations are performed by
DELPHES 3.5.0

deFavereau:2013fsa
.

3.1
Statistical method

The signals are investigated by comparing the number of ALP signal events (
n^{\rm sig}
) with the number of observed events (
n^{\rm obs}
) and the expected number of background events (
n^{\rm bkg}
).
For
pp\to WWW
and
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
, we evaluate the upper limits by using a modified frequentist approach, which is also called the
{\rm CL}_{s}
method
Junk:1999kv
;
Read:2002hq
.
#4
#4

#4

Such a prescription is not necessary for resonance searches via
pp\to Z\gamma\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma
. See Sec.
3.4
for details.

The events are assumed to follow Poisson distributions, and the
p
-value and
{\rm CL}_{s}
are defined as

\displaystyle p_{\alpha}=\sum_{n=0}^{n^{\rm obs}}\frac{\alpha^{n}e^{-\alpha}}{n!},\quad{\rm CL}_{s}=\frac{p_{s+n^{\rm bkg}}}{p_{n^{\rm bkg}}}.

(17)

The upper limit on the number of signal events at the 95% level,
n^{\rm sig}_{\rm up}
, is determined by solving
{\rm CL}_{s}=0.05
.
Then, the upper bound on
g_{aWW}
is obtained from

\displaystyle n^{\rm sig}=\sigma\cdot{\cal L}_{I}\cdot A\times\epsilon<n^{\rm sig}_{\rm up},

(18)

where
{\cal L}_{I}
is an integrated luminosity,
A
is the acceptance, and
\epsilon
accounts for the efficiency.
The production cross section
\sigma
is a function of
m_{a}
and
g_{aWW}
.
For a given ALP mass,
\sigma
scales as
g_{aWW}^{2}
when the process is dominated by on-shell ALP productions.
In practice, signal categories are defined by imposing cuts on the events, and an upper limit is obtained in each signal category.
Among the categories, the most stringent constraint is adopted as the result.

There are two comments in order.
First, although the
{\rm CL}_{s}
method does not always provide a real confidence interval, it is useful especially to accommodate the case where an experimental result appears to contain fewer events than the expected number of background events due to a fluctuation, which is likely to lead to too strong exclusions.
Such a situation occurs in some of the signal categories for
pp\to WWW
and
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
.
Next, Eq. (
17
) does not take account of systematic uncertainties.
In the literature, they are not provided explicitly for each signal category.
Besides, although there are potential correlations among the categories, such information is not shown, and we therefore omit them for simplicity.
As explained below, the validity of the above method is checked by deriving limits on new physics models that are explored in Refs.
CMS:2019mpq
;
ATLAS:2021pdg
and comparing the results with those in the literature.

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have studied the photophobic ALP model in high-mass regions using the LHC Run-II results.
Since the ALP-photon coupling is suppressed, the model can avoid tight constraints from searches for di-photon final states.
The ALPs are predominantly coupled with electroweak gauge bosons, and we investigated the multi-boson final-state signatures at the LHC.
We reinterpreted the LHC analyses of
pp\to WWW
,
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
, and
pp\to Z\gamma\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma
.
We found that the on-shell ALP productions via
pp\to Z\gamma\to\nu\bar{\nu}\gamma
shows the best sensitivity in the mass range,
40<m_{a}<220\,\mathrm{GeV}
.
For the mass region
220<m_{a}<3400\,\mathrm{GeV}
, the ALP resonance search via
pp\to Z\gamma\to\ell^{+}\ell^{-}\gamma
provides the most severe constrains.

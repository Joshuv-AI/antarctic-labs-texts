Equilibrium States for Random Zooming Systems Thanks: The third author was partially supported by CNPq, under the project with reference 409198/2021-8.
Rafael A. Bilbao, Marlon Oliveira, Eduardo Santana

Abstract
. In this work, we introduce the notion of random zooming systems and prove the existence of equilibrium states for which we call random zooming potentials, that include the hyperbolic ones, possibly with the presence of a critical set. The proof follows ideas of the work [ 4 ] . With a mild condition, we obtain uniqueness. We also prove that the classes of random zooming potentials and random hyperbolic potentials are equivalent and also contain the null potential, giving measures of maximal entropy.



1.
Introduction

The theory of equilibrium states on dynamical systems was firstly developed by Sinai, Ruelle and Bowen in the sixties and seventies.
It was based on applications of techniques of Statistical Mechanics to smooth dynamics.
Given a continuous map
f:M\to M
on a compact metric space
M
and a continuous potential
\phi:M\to\mathbb{R}
, an
equilibrium state
is an
invariant measure that satisfies a variational principle, that is, a measure
\mu
such that

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(f)+\int\phi d\mu=\sup_{\eta\in\mathcal{M}_{f}(M)}\bigg\{h_{\eta}(f)+\int\phi d\eta\bigg\},

where
\mathcal{M}_{f}(M)
is the set of
f
-invariant probabilities on
M
and
h_{\eta}(f)
is the so-called metric entropy of
\eta
.

In the context of uniform hyperbolicity, which includes uniformly expanding maps, equilibrium states do exist and are unique if the potential is Hölder continuous
and the map is transitive. In addition, the theory for finite shifts was developed and used to achieve the results for smooth dynamics.

Beyond uniform hyperbolicity, the theory is still far from complete. It was studied by several authors, including Bruin, Keller, Demers, Li, Rivera-Letelier, Iommi and Todd

[
6
,
9
,
10
,
13
]
for interval maps; Denker and Urbanski
[
8
]
for rational maps; Leplaideur, Oliveira and Rios

[
14
]
for partially hyperbolic horseshoes; Buzzi, Sarig and Yuri
[
7
,
25
]
, for countable Markov shifts and for piecewise expanding maps in one and higher dimensions.
For local diffeomorphisms with some kind of non-uniform expansion, there are results due to Oliveira
[
15
]
; Arbieto, Matheus and Oliveira
[
4
]
;
Varandas and Viana
[
23
]
. All of whom proved the existence and uniqueness of equilibrium states for potentials with low oscillation. Also, for this type of maps,
Ramos and Viana
[
20
]
proved it for potentials so-called
hyperbolic
, which includes the previous ones. The hyperbolicity of the potential is
characterized by the fact that the pressure emanates from the hyperbolic region. In all these studies the maps do not have the presence of critical sets and recently, Alves, Oliveira and Santana proved the existence of at most finitely many equilibrium states for hyperbolic potentials, possible with the presence of a critical set (see
[
3
]
). More recently, Santana completed this by showing uniqueness in
[
21
]
. Moreover, in this work it is proved that the class of hyperbolic potentials is equivalent to the class of continuous zooming potential (which satisfies a key inequality between free energies). It includes the null potential, which implies existence and uniqueness of measure of maximal entropy. A similar work is developed in
[
19
]
, where they deal with expanding measures and potentials.

In the context of random dynamics, some works has established existence and others uniqueness. We can cite
[
4
,
5
,
11
,
22
]
. All these works deal with dynamics with absence of critical sets. In this work, we generalize some of these results and extend them to the context of zooming contractions, obtaining random zooming systems. Based on the definition of zooming maps, introduced in
[
18
]
and developed equilibrium states for the deterministic context in
[
21
]
, we introduce random zooming systems.

We define what we mean by random zooming maps (which generalizes both deterministic zooming systems and random non-uniformly expanding maps), random zooming potentials and follow ideas of
[
4
]
to obtain equilibrium states. With a mild condition, we obtain uniqueness. Moreover, based on ideas of
[
21
]
we prove that the class of random zooming potentials is equivalent to the class of random hyperbolic potentials and contain the null potential, giving measures of maximal entropy.

In the Example section, we give the class of random Viana maps and construct random zooming potentials for maps with the presence of a fixed point in the random zooming set, which is the case of the random Viana maps. Finally, we prove that for random systems which have equilibrium states that are not measures of maximal entropy at the same time, we have the null potential as a random zooming potential.
We also present an example of random zooming system which is not random non-uniformly expanding.

2.
Preliminaries and Main Result

2.1.
Random Maps and Invariant Measures

Let
M
be a connected compact metric space and
\mathcal{C}^{0}:=\mathcal{C}^{0}(M,M)
the space of continuous maps on
M
. Let
(\Omega,T,\mathbb{P})
be a measure preserving system, where
T:\Omega\to\Omega
is
\mathbb{P}
-invariant (where
\mathbb{P}
is a Borel measure) and
\Omega
is a Polish space, i.e.,
\Omega
is a complete separable metric space. By a random transformation we mean a continuos map
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{C}^{0}
. Then, we define the skew-product generated by
f
:

\begin{array}[]{cccc}F\ :&\!\Omega\times M&\!\longrightarrow&\!\Omega\times M\\
&\!(w,x)&\!\longmapsto&\!(T(w),f_{w}(x))\end{array}

where the iterates of
F
are given by

F^{n}(w,x)=(T^{n}(w),f^{n}_{w}(x))

with,

f^{n}_{w}(x):=f_{T^{n-1}(w)}\circ\dots\circ f_{w}(x).

We denote by
\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega\times M)
the space of probability measures
\mu
on
\Omega\times M
such that the marginal of
\mu
on
\Omega
is
\mathbb{P}
. Let
\mathcal{M}(F,\Omega\times M)\subset\mathcal{P}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega\times M)
the space of probability measure
\mu
that are
F
-invariant. We denote the fiber at
w\in\Omega
by
M_{w}=\{w\}\times M
.

Because
M
is compact, invariant measures always exist and the property of
\mathbb{P}
being the
marginal on
\Omega
of an invariant measure can be characterized by its disintegration:

d\mu(w,x)=d\mu_{w}(x)d\mathbb{P}(x).

\mu_{w}
are called sample measures of
\mu
.

2.2.
Random Zooming Systems

Based on the definition of zooming maps, introduced in
[
18
]
and developed equilibrium states for the deterministic context in
[
21
]
, we introduce random zooming systems.

Definition 1
.

(Zooming contractions). A zooming contraction is a sequence of functions
\alpha_{n}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
such that

•

\alpha_{n}(r)<r,\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N},\text{for all}\,\,r>0.

•

\alpha_{n}(r)<\alpha_{n}(s),\,\,if\,\,0<r<s,\text{for all}\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\alpha_{m}\circ\alpha_{n}(r)\leq\alpha_{m+n}(r),\text{for all}\,\,r>0,\text{for all}\,\,m,n\in\mathbb{N}
.

•

\displaystyle\sup_{r\in(0,1)}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(r)<\infty
.

As defined in
[
19
]
, we call the contraction
(\alpha_{n})_{n}

exponential
if
\alpha_{n}(r)=e^{-\lambda n}r
for some
\lambda>0
and
Lipschitz
if
\alpha_{n}(r)=a_{n}r
with
0\leq a_{n}<1,a_{m}a_{n}\leq a_{m+n}
and
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}<\infty
. In particular, every exponential contraction is Lipschitz. We can also have the example with
a_{n}=(n+b)^{-a},a>1,b>0
.

We define a random
(\alpha,\delta)
-zooming time for
(w,x)\in\Omega\times M
. Fix a zooming contraction
\alpha
,
\delta>0
and
(w,x)\in\Omega\times M
. We say that
n\in\mathbb{N}
is a
(\alpha,\delta)
-
random zooming time
for
(w,x)
if

•

There exists a neighbourhood
V_{n}(w,x)\subset\{w\}\times M
of
(w,x)
such that
f^{n}_{w}
sends
V_{n}(w,x)
homeomorphically onto the ball
B_{\delta}(f^{n}_{w}(x))\subset\{T^{n}(w)\}\times M
;

•

d(f^{i}_{w}(y),f^{i}_{w}(z))\leq\alpha_{n-i}(d(f^{n}_{w}(y),f^{n}_{w}(z))),\forall\,\,y,z\in V_{n}(w,x),\forall\,\,0\leq i\leq n-1.

We require that it holds for
\mathbb{P}
almost every
w\in\Omega
.

The sets
V_{n}(w,x)
are called
random zooming pre-balls
and their images
f^{n}_{w}(V_{n}(w,x))=B_{\delta}(f^{n}_{w}(x))
,
random zooming balls
.

We observe that if
n
is a
(\alpha,\delta)
-random zooming time for
(w,x)
, then
n
is a
(\alpha,\delta^{\prime})
-random zooming time for
(w,x)
, for every
0<\delta^{\prime}<\delta
.

We say that
(w,x)\in\Omega\times M
has
positive frequency
of random zooming times if

(1)

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\#\bigg\{0\leq j\leq n-1\,\,|\,\,j\,\,\text{is a random zooming time for}\,\,(w,x)\bigg\}>0,

and define the
random zooming set

\Lambda=\{(w,x)\in\Omega\times M\mid\text{the frequency of}\,\,(\alpha,\delta)-\text{random zooming times of}\,\,(w,x)\,\,\text{is positive}\}.

We say that a Borel probability measure
\mu
on
\Omega\times M
is
random zooming
if
\mu(\Lambda)=1
.

A map with an associated random zooming measure is called a
random zooming map
. We will always consider random zooming maps admitting invariant random zooming measures.

2.3.
Entropy and Topological Pressure

We start by the definition of entropy and topological pressure for random transformations. The reader can consult more results and properties in Kifer
[
11
]
and Liu
[
12
]
.

Let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(F,\Omega\times M)
be an
F
-invariant measure. Given a finite measurable partition
\xi
of
M
we set

h_{\mu}(F|T;\xi):=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}H_{\mu_{w}}\left(\bigvee_{j=0}^{n-1}f_{w}^{-j}(\xi)\right)d\mathbb{P}(w)

where
H_{\nu}(\xi)=-\sum_{P\in\xi}\nu(P)\log\nu(P)
for a finite partition
\xi
and
\mu_{w}
is the sample measure of
\mu.

The
entropy
of
(F|T,\mu)
is

h_{\mu}(F|T):=\sup_{\xi}\{h_{\mu}(F|T;\xi)\}

where the supremum is taken over all finite measurable partitions of
M
.

Denote by
\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
the space of all measurable functions
\phi:\Omega\times M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that
\phi_{w}:M\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined by
\phi_{w}(x):=\phi(w,x)
is continuous for all
w\in\Omega
and
\lVert\phi\rVert_{1}=\int_{\Omega}\lVert\phi_{w}\rVert_{\infty}\,d\mathbb{P}(w)<+\infty
.

Fix
w\in\Omega
. Given
\varepsilon>0
and an integer
n\geq 1
, we say that a subset
F_{n}\subseteq M
is
(w,n,\varepsilon)-
separated if for every two distinct points
y,z\in F_{n}
there exists some
j\in\{0,1,...,n-1\}
such that
d(f^{j}_{w}(y),f^{j}_{w}(z))>\varepsilon
.

For
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
,
\varepsilon>0
and
n\geq 1
we consider

P_{F|T}(\phi)(w,n,\varepsilon)=\sup\left\{\sum_{y\in F_{n}}e^{S_{n}\phi(w,y)}\ ;\ F_{n}\ \mbox{is a}\ (w,n,\varepsilon)-\mbox{separated set}\right\}

where
S_{n}\phi(w,y):=\sum^{n-1}_{j=0}\phi_{\theta^{j}(w)}(f^{j}_{w}(y))
.

The
random topological pressure
of
\phi
relative to
\theta
is defined by

P_{F|T}(\phi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\limsup_{n\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{n}\int_{\Omega}\log P_{F|T}(\phi)(w,n,\varepsilon)\,d\mathbb{P}(w).

Thus it is well defined the pressure map as follows

\begin{array}[]{cccc}P_{F|T}\ :&\!\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))&\!\longrightarrow&\!\mathbb{R}\cup\{\infty\}\\
&\!\phi&\!\longmapsto&\!P_{F|T}(\phi)\end{array}

In particular, the topological entropy of
F
relative to
T
is
h_{top}(F|T)=P_{F|T}(0)
.

The topological pressure and the entropy are related by the well known Variational Principle. The reader can see a proof of this result in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 1

(Variational Principle)
.

Assume that
(\Omega,\mathbb{P})
is a Lebesgue space. Then for any
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
we have

(2)

P_{F|T}(\phi)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{P}}(F)}\biggl(h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi\ d\mu\biggr).

Moreover, when
\mathbb{P}
is ergodic, we can consider the supremum over ergodic measures.

Motivated by the Variational Principle, we say that an
F
-invariant measure
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times M)
is an
equilibrium state
for
(F|T,\phi)
relative to
T
if the supremum (
2
) is attained by
\mu
, i.e.,

P_{F|T}(\phi)=h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi\ d\mu.

2.4.
Topological Pressure and Variational Principle to noncompact set

Let
w\in\Omega
and consider
\mathcal{U}
a finite open cover of
M
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{U})
the set of all strings
{\bf{U}}=\{U_{i_{0}},\cdots,U_{i_{n-1}}\,;\,U_{i_{j}}\in\mathcal{U}\}
of length
n=n({\bf{U}})
and put
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{U})=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{U})
.
Given a string
{\bf{U}}=\{U_{i_{0}},\cdots,U_{i_{n-1}}\}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{U})
, we consider the cylinder

X_{w}=X_{w}({\bf{U}}):=\{x\in M\,\,;\,\,f^{j}_{w}(x)\in U_{i_{j}}\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,j=0,\cdots,n({\bf U})-1\}.

Let
\mathcal{F}_{(N,w)}
be the collection of all cylinders of depth at least
N
, i.e.,

\mathcal{F}_{(N,w)}=\mathcal{F}_{(N,w)}(\mathcal{U})=\{X_{w}({\bf{U}})\,\,;\,\,{\bf{U}}\in\mathcal{S}_{n}(\mathcal{U})\,\,\mbox{for}\,\,n\geq N\}.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
let

(3)

m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\mathcal{U},N)=\displaystyle{\inf_{\mathcal{F}}}\left\{\sum_{X_{w}\in\mathcal{F}_{(N,w)}}e^{-\beta n({\bf{U}})+S_{n({\bf{U}})}\phi(X_{w})}\right\},

where
S_{n({\bf{U}})}\phi(X_{w})=\sup_{y\in X_{w}}\sum_{j=0}^{n({\bf U})-1}\phi_{T^{j}(w)}(f^{j}_{w}(y))
and the infimum is taken over all finite families
\mathcal{F}
of
\mathcal{F}_{(N,w)}
in order that (
3
) is measurable in
w
(see e.g. Section 9 of
[
22
]
). As
N
goes to infinity we define

m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\mathcal{U})=\lim_{N\to\infty}m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\mathcal{U},N).

The existence of the limit above is guaranteed by the function
m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\mathcal{U},N)
to be increasing with
N
. Taking the infimum over
\beta
we call

P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\mathcal{U})=\inf\{\beta:m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\mathcal{U})=0\}.

Let
|\mathcal{U}|=\max\{\mbox{diam}U_{i}\,\,;\,\,U_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}\}
be the diameter of the cover
\mathcal{U}
and consider

P_{F|T}(w,\phi)=\lim_{|\mathcal{U}|\rightarrow 0}{P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\mathcal{U})}.

In [Theorem 11.1,
[
17
]
] it was showed that this quantity is well defined and does not depend on the cover
\mathcal{U}
. Moreover, since all quantities defined above are measurable functions of
w\in\Omega
(see e.g. Section 9 of
[
22
]
), we can define the
random topological pressure
of
(F|T,\phi)
as

P_{F|T}(\phi)=\int P_{F|T}(w,\phi)\ d\mathbb{P}(w).

In the following we present another way to define the random topological pressure. We fix
w\in\Omega
and
\varepsilon>0
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
x\in M
, let
B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon)
be the dynamic ball

B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon):=\{y\in M:d(f_{w}^{j}(x),f_{w}^{j}(y))<\varepsilon,\ \mbox{ for}\ \ 0\leq j\leq n\}.

We denote by
G_{(N,w)}
the collection of dynamic balls:

G_{(N,w)}:=\{B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon):x\in M\ \mbox{and}\ n\geq N\}.

Let
U_{w}
be a finite or countable family of
G_{(N,w)}
which covers
M
. For every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
let

m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\varepsilon,N)=\inf_{U_{w}\subset G_{(N,w)}}\left\{\sum_{B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon)\in U_{w}}e^{-\beta n+S_{n}\phi(B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon))}\right\},

where
S_{n}\phi(B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon))=\sup_{y\in B_{w}(x,n,\varepsilon)}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\phi_{T^{j}(w)}(f^{j}_{w}(y))
. When
N
goes to infinity we consider

m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\varepsilon)=\lim_{N\to\infty}m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\varepsilon,N).

and taking the infimum over
\beta
we define

P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\varepsilon)=\inf\{\beta:m_{\beta}(w,\phi,F|T,\varepsilon)=0\}.

Since
P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\varepsilon)
is decreasing on
\varepsilon
we can take the limit

P_{F|T}(w,\phi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\varepsilon)}.

Now if we consider a finite open cover
\mathcal{U}
of
M
with Lebesgue number
\varepsilon(\mathcal{U})
we have

B_{w}(x,n({\bf U}),\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon)\subset X_{w}({\bf U})\subset B_{w}(x,n({\bf U}),2|\mathcal{U}|)

which implies that

P_{F|T}(w,\phi)=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}{P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\varepsilon)}=\lim_{|\mathcal{U}|\rightarrow 0}{P_{F|T}(w,\phi,\mathcal{U})}.

Therefore the definition of random topological pressure via coverings and via dynamic balls coincides.

The topological pressure and the entropy are related by the well known Variational Principle. The reader can see a proof of this result in
[
22
]
.

Theorem 2
.

(Variational Principle to noncompact sets).
Let
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times M)
be a probability measure and
\Lambda\subset\Omega\times M
such that
F(\Lambda)\subseteq\Lambda
. Then

P_{F|T}(\phi,\Lambda)\geq\sup_{\mu(\Lambda)=1}\left\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int_{\Lambda}\phi d\mu\right\}.

2.5.
Random Zooming Potentials

Analogouly to what is given in
[
19
]
, we introduce the following definitions in the random context.

Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the set of random zooming measures on
\Omega\times M
. We say that a function
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
is a
random zooming potential
if the following inequality holds:

(4)

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\nu\bigg\}<\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu\bigg\}.

If
P(\phi)
denotes the topological pressure of
\phi
, by the Variational Principle, we have:

P(\phi)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{M}(\Omega\times M)}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu\bigg\}=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu\bigg\}.

It means that, if the there exists some equilibrium state for a random zooming potential, the equilibrium states must be random zooming measures.

2.6.
Random Hyperbolic Potentials

We say that a function
\phi\in\mathbb{L}^{1}_{\mathbb{P}}(\Omega,C^{0}(M))
is a
random hyperbolic potential
if the topological pressure
P_{F|T}(\phi)
is concentrated at the random zooming set
\Lambda
, that is, if the following inequality holds:

(5)

P_{F|T}(\phi,\Lambda^{c})<P_{F|T}(\phi,\Lambda)=P_{F|T}(\phi).

As a consequence of the Theorem
2
(Variational Principle), we have the following inequality

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\nu\bigg\}\leq P_{F|T}(\phi,\Lambda^{c})<P_{F|T}(\phi,\Lambda)=P_{F|T}(\phi)=\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu\bigg\}.

It means that every random hyperbolic potential is a random zooming potential.

2.7.
Main Result

Our main result concerns the existence and uniqueness of equilibrium states for random zooming systems.

Theorem A
.

Let
F:\Omega\times M\to\Omega\times M
be a random zooming map and
\phi:\Omega\times M\to\mathbb{R}
a random zooming potential. Then,

•

There exists some equilibrium state and every equilibrium state is a random zooming measure.

•

Suppose that the marginal
\mathbb{P}
is ergodic with full support. Also suppose that the map
F:\Omega\times M\to\Omega\times M
is such that the set
\{f^{n}_{w}(x)\,\,|\,\,n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is dense on
M
, for every
(w,x)\in\Lambda\cap M_{w}
,
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
w\in\Omega
. In this case, we have uniqueness of equilibrium state.

As a corollary of our main result concerns of the equivalence between the classes of random zooming potentials and random hyperbolic potentials.

Corollary 1
.

We have that every random zooming potential is also a random hyperbolic potential. These two classes of potentials are then equivalent.

5.
Examples

In this section, we give examples of random zooming systems, as the random non-uniformly expanding maps so-called random Viana maps and construct zooming potentials. Random non-uniformly expanding maps are examples of random zooming systems when the contractions are exponential. In this case, random zooming times are called random hyperbolic times.

We begin by constructing random zooming potentials for random maps which has zooming fixed points. The Dirac probability supported on this point will be the equilibrium state. After that, we show that the null potentials is random zooming, if we can find a potential with an equlibrium state which is not a measure of maximal entropy at the same time. Finally, we presente the class of random Viana maps and give an example of random zooming system which does not have exponential zooming contractions.

5.1.
Random Zooming Potentials

Let
F
be an random zooming map with a critical set such that the random zooming set
\Lambda
contains a fixed point
x_{0}
. We will construct a random zooming potential, that is, a potential
\varphi
such that

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int\varphi d\nu\bigg\}<\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\varphi d\mu\bigg\},

where
\mathcal{Z}
is the set of the zooming measures.

Since
x_{0}\in\Lambda
is a fixed point, the Dirac probability
\delta_{x_{0}}
at
x_{0}
is an invariant zooming measure.

Let
\phi\geq 0
be a continuous potential on the fibers such that
\max\phi=\phi(x_{0})>0
and it is integrable with respect to the marginal probability (for example, we can define it as constant on the fibers). We have that

\int\phi d\nu<\phi(x_{0})=\int\phi d\delta_{x_{0}},\forall\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}.

We assume that

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{\int\phi d\nu\bigg\}<\phi(x_{0}).

So, we can find
k>0
such that

\int k\phi d\delta_{x_{0}}-\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{\int k\phi d\nu\bigg\}>2h(F)\implies\int k\phi d\delta_{x_{0}}-\int k\phi d\nu>2h(F),\forall\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}

where
h(F)>0
is the topological entropy. It implies that

h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\nu\leq h(F)+\int k\phi d\nu<2h(F)+\int k\phi d\nu<\int k\phi d\delta_{x_{0}},

that is,

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\nu\bigg\}<\int k\phi d\delta_{x_{0}}\leq\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\mu\bigg\}.

Then, the potential
\varphi=k\phi
is random zoooming.

Also, in general, if there exist a potential
\phi
and a measure
\mu\in\mathcal{Z}
, such that

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{\int\phi d\nu\bigg\}<\int\phi d\mu,

we can find
k>0
such that

\int k\phi d\mu-\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{\int k\phi d\nu\bigg\}>2h(F)\implies\int k\phi d\mu-\int k\phi d\nu>2h(F),\forall\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}

where
h(F)>0
is the topological entropy. It implies that

h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\nu\leq h(F)+\int k\phi d\nu<2h(F)+\int k\phi d\nu<\int k\phi d\mu,

that is,

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\bigg\{h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\nu\bigg\}<\int k\phi d\mu\leq\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\bigg\{h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int k\phi d\mu\bigg\}.

Then, the potential
\varphi=k\phi
is random zooming.

5.2.
The Null Potential

We will show that for random maps and potentials for which there exist equilibrium states we have that the null potential is random zooming and, as a consequence, potentials with low variation are also random zooming. It is a consequence of the fact that the set of random zooming potentials is open.

In order to show it, we use the existence of equilibrium states which are zooming measures.

We recall that
\mathcal{Z}
denotes the set of zooming invariant measures. Assume, by contradiction, that

\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\{h_{\mu}(F|T)\}\leq\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\{h_{\nu}(F|T)\}.

Let
\phi_{0}
be a random zooming potential which has equilibrium states. There exists an ergodic equilibrium state
\mu\in\mathcal{Z}
. We have that
\mu
is also an equilibium state for
\phi:=\phi_{0}-P(\phi_{0})
which implies that
P(\phi)=0
and
h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu=0
and
-h_{\mu}(F|T)=\int\phi d\mu\leq 0
. Assume that there exists
\nu_{0}\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}
such that
h_{\mu}(F|T)<h_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)
. Since every equilibrium state is a zooming probability, we have that
\nu_{0}
is not an equilibrium state for
\phi
and

h_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)+\int\phi d\nu_{0}<h_{\mu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\mu=0,

which implies that
\int\phi d\nu_{0}<\int\phi d\mu=-h_{\mu}(F|T)
because
h_{\mu}(F|T)<h_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)
. For every
t\in(0,1)
and putting
\nu=(1-t)\mu+t\nu_{0}
we still have that
h_{\mu}(F|T)<h_{\nu}(F|T)
(we observe that
h_{\nu}(F|T)=(1-t)h_{\mu}(F|T)+th_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)
, because the entropy is an affine function, see
[
16
, Proposition 9.6.1]
for the deterministic case and by using the Abramov-Rokhlin Formula we obtain it for the relative entropy). Let
\epsilon>0
and
t\in(0,1),n\in\mathbb{N}
are chosen such that such that
0<h_{\nu}(F|T)-h_{\mu}(F|T)-\epsilon/n
and

\int\phi d\mu>\int\phi d\nu>\int\phi d\mu-\epsilon\implies-\epsilon(n-1)/n<h_{\nu}(F|T)-h_{\mu}(F|T)-\epsilon<h_{\nu}(F|T)+\int\phi d\nu<0,

using that
\int\phi d\mu=-h_{\mu}(F|T)
. It is possible because once
\mu
is an equilibrium state we have
h_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)-h_{\mu}(F|T)<\int\phi d\mu-\int\phi d\nu_{0}
and there exist
t\in(0,1),n\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\epsilon/n<h_{\nu}(F|T)-h_{\mu}(F|T)=t(h_{\nu_{0}}(F|T)-h_{\mu}(F|T))<t\bigg(\int\phi d\mu-\int\phi d\nu_{0}\bigg)=\int\phi d\mu-\int\phi d\nu<\epsilon.

We obtain a contradiction because
\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}
and
\epsilon>0
is arbitrary and the potential
\phi
is zooming. It shows that we have

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\{h_{\nu}(F|T)\}\leq h_{\mu}(F|T).

If
\mu_{0}
is an equilibrium state which is not a measure of maximal entropy, then we have

\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{Z}^{c}}\{h_{\nu}(F|T)\}\leq h_{\mu_{0}}(F|T)<\sup_{\mu\in\mathcal{Z}}\{h_{\mu}(F|T)\}=h(f).

Hence, the null potential is random zooming.

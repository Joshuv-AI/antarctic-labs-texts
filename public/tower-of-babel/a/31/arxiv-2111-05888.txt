Normalizers of chains of discrete p p -toral subgroups in compact Lie groups
Eva Belmont, Natàlia Castellana, Jelena Grbić, Kathryn Lesh, Michelle Strumila

Abstract
. In this paper we study the normalizer decomposition of a compact Lie group G G using the information of the fusion system ℱ {\mathcal{F}} of G G on a maximal discrete p p -toral subgroup. We prove that there is an injective map from the set of conjugacy classes of chains of ℱ {\mathcal{F}} -centric, ℱ {\mathcal{F}} -radical discrete p p -toral subgroups to the set of conjugacy classes of chains of p p -centric, p p -stubborn continuous p p -toral subgroups. The map is a bijection when π 0 ​ ( G ) \pi_{0}(G) is a finite p p -group. We also prove that the classifying space of the normalizer of a chain of discrete p p -toral subgroups of G G is mod p p equivalent to the classifying space of the normalizer of the corresponding chain of p p -toral subgroups.



1.
Introduction

In
[
Dwy97
]
, Dwyer formalized and unified three homology decompositions for the
p
-completed classifying space of
a finite group
G
based on a collection of
p
-subgroups: the centralizer decomposition, the subgroup decomposition, and the normalizer decomposition. The first two had been studied in
[
JM92
]
and
[
JMO92
]
for compact Lie groups, and the normalizer decomposition was new in this context.
Dwyer showed that for a given collection of subgroups of a finite group
G
, either all three decompositions give the correct homotopy type for
{BG}_{p}^{\wedge}
or none of them do. Such decompositions in the setting of Lie groups have since been studied by other authors, for example
[
CLN07
,
Lib11
,
So01
,
Str00
]
.
In particular, in
[
Lib11
]
Libman
gives a normalizer decomposition and then
unifies the three homology decompositions for Lie groups, as Dwyer did in the finite group case.

Recently, the homotopy theory of
p
-local compact groups

[
BLO07
, Defn 4.2]

has provided a new, more general framework for dealing with the homotopy type of
p
-completed classifying spaces of compact Lie groups,
in addition to other examples coming from finite loop spaces (
[
BLO14
]
).
One works with discrete
p
-toral groups
(Definition
2.1
) instead of
p
-toral groups.
The formal structure of a
p
-local compact group consists of a triple
(S,{\mathcal{F}},{\mathcal{L}})
where
{\mathcal{F}}
is a saturated fusion system over the discrete
p
-toral groups

and
{\mathcal{L}}
is a centric linking system associated to
{\mathcal{F}}
. But in view of
[
LL15
, Thm B]
a
p
-local compact group is equivalent to just a pair
(S,{\mathcal{F}})
, namely a saturated fusion system over a discrete
p
-toral group.

When the
p
-local compact group
arises from a compact Lie group
G
, it encodes
the essential
p
-local information needed to uniquely determine the homotopy type of
{BG}_{p}^{\wedge}
(see
[
BLO07
]
,
[
Che13
]
,
[
Oli13
]
,
[
LL15
]
).
A great advantage of studying Lie groups via this theory is that it reduces the study of a topological group to the study of a collection of
discrete
subgroups.
There are also other interesting examples
of
p
-local compact groups.
For example, one can construct a
p
-local compact group capturing the homotopy type of a
p
-compact group
(an
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-finite loop space together with a chosen
p
-complete delooping, see
[
DW94
]
).
Other examples
of
p
-local compact groups
are given in
[
BLO14
]
and
[
GLR19
]
.

To state the form of a normalizer decomposition more precisely,
consider a collection

of closed subgroups of a Lie group

.
Define
{{\overline{s}}d}({\mathcal{C}})
to be the poset of

.
One can construct a functor

to the constant functor with value

to induce a map

(1.1)

{\left(\underset{{{\overline{s}}d}({\mathcal{C}})}{\hocolim}\,\delta\right)\!}_{p}^{\wedge}\to{BG}_{p}^{\wedge}

such that
\delta({\bf H}_{*})\simeq BN_{G}({\bf H}_{0}\subset\cdots\subset{\bf H}_{k}):=B\big(\bigcap_{i}N_{G}({\bf H}_{i})\big)
.
The following statement collects results of

[
Lib11
, Thm C, D]
,
[

[

-completed homotopy type of

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

be either (i) the collection of nontrivial

-radical

-toral subgroups or (ii) the collection of

Our program’s goal, taken up in a forthcoming work
[
BCG+
]
, is a computable setup that generalizes the normalizer decomposition (

) from compact Lie groups to

-local compact groups. The formalism is a straightforward generalization of the earlier work of Libman
[

]
giving a normalizer decomposition for
p
-local

groups. In a result similar to Theorem

,
[
BCG+
]
will also show that if the

-local compact group corresponds to the fusion system

, then
the full subcategory of

{\mathcal{F}}
-centric and

-radical subgroups (Definition

)

sufficient to determine the homotopy type of the

-completed classifying space.
This result is in the literature for finite groups (
[
Gro02
, Thm 1.5]
) and

-local finite groups (
[

, Thm 3.5]
), but not for
p
-local compact groups.

When it comes to actual computations, the analysis of the spaces coming into our normalizer decompositions for
p
-local compact groups can be delicate. This paper is largely in service of understanding the spaces in the decompositions that we obtain in certain examples. In particular, we need to understand what happens in the case of a
p
-local compact group that arises from a compact Lie group, because we want to compare the decomposition we obtain in
[
BCG+
]
with the earlier one of Libman for the corresponding Lie group
[
Lib11
]
.

We turn to a description of the contents of this paper and how they fit into our program. Let
G
be a compact Lie group, and let

{\bf S}\subseteq G
be a maximal
p
-toral subgroup of

with maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup

. The corresponding fusion system

associated to

is the category whose objects are the discrete
p
-toral subgroups of

, and whose morphisms are given by homomorphisms induced by conjugation by elements of

The goal of this paper is to establish that the left side of (

), which is described in terms of chains of continuous

-toral groups and the action of

, can instead be described in terms of

-toral groups of

.
There are two issues: the indexing category, and the values of the functor

Our first theorem addresses the indexing category, by relating conjugacy classes of chains of

-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group

to conjugacy classes of chains of continuous

-toral subgroups of

.
The following theorem establishes that the desired classes of chains can all be found by considering the
p
-stubborn

-toral subgroups of

]
for classical groups.
(See Definition

for

-discretization.)

Theorem
4.3
.

Let
{\bf S}
be a maximal
p
-toral subgroup of a compact Lie group

, with
p
-discretization

. The closure map
P\mapsto{\overline{P}}
defines an injective map

The map is a one-to-one correspondence if
\pi_{0}G
is a
p
-group.

Our second theorem deals with the values of the functor

in (

). In particular, we
relate the mod

-toral subgroups to those of chains of continuous

-toral subgroups. Since our decomposition for

-local compact groups will involve the former, this theorem will relate

-local compact group methods

Theorem
5.1
.

Let
{\bf P}_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq{\bf P}_{k}

-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group

In summary, this paper provides the technical results necessary to compare

]

The two decompositions are related by taking closures of discrete

2.
p
-toral and discrete
p
-toral subgroups of Lie groups

In this section, we give background material on
p
-toral and
discrete
p
-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group
G
. First, the definitions.

Definition 2.1
.

(1)

A group is
p
-toral of rank
r
if it is an extension of a torus of rank
r
by a finite
p
-group.

(2)

A
discrete
p
-torus of rank
r
is a group isomorphic to a product
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{p}^{\infty}\right)^{r}
.

(3)

A
discrete
p
-toral group of rank
r
is an extension of a discrete
p
-torus of rank
r
by a finite
p
-group.

Remark
.

When we want to emphasize the difference between a
p
-toral group and a discrete
p
-toral group, we will sometimes refer to the former as a
continuous
p
-toral group
.

The
p
-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group
G
play a key role in the analysis of the mod
p
homology of the classifying space of
G
, analogous to the role played by the
p
-subgroups in the case of a finite group. However, there is a key difference between the finite and topological contexts:
subgroups of finite
p
-groups are finite
p
-groups, but subgroups of
p
-toral groups need not be
p
-toral.
For example,
S^{1}
is a
p
-toral group, but it has finite subgroups of order prime to
p
(certainly not
p
-toral) as well as the subgroup
{\mathbb{Z}}/{p}^{\infty}\subset S^{1}
(also not
p
-toral, for a different reason).
By contrast, a subgroup of a discrete
p
-toral group is necessarily another discrete
p
-toral group.
This feature of discrete
p
-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group
G
gives them an advantage over continuous
p
-toral subgroups as tools to approximate
G
.

A compact Lie group
G
admits both maximal
continuous
p
-toral subgroups and maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroups, both of which have properties analogous to those of the
Sylow
p
-subgroups of a finite group.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
BLO07
, Prop. 9.3]
)
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group.

(1)

Every
p
-toral subgroup (respectively, discrete
p
-toral subgroup) of
G
is contained in a maximal one.

(2)

All maximal
p
-toral subgroups (respectively, discrete
p
-toral subgroup) are conjugate in
G
.

Unfortunately, discrete
p
-toral subgroups will not be good approximations to continuous
p
-toral groups when their group theoretic properties interact badly with their embeddings. In particular,
a discrete
p
-toral group can have smaller rank than its closure, and this can occur even when the groups are
just tori. For example,
{\mathbb{Z}}/{p}^{\infty}
can be embedded via a homomorphism as a dense subgroup of
S^{1}\times S^{1}
.
To set apart good approximations from bad ones, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.3

(
[
BLO07
, Defn. 9.1]
)
.

A discrete
p
-toral subgroup
P\subseteq G
is
snugly embedded
if
P
is a maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup of
{\overline{P}}
.

Lemma 2.4

(
[
BLO07
, Prop. 9.2]
)
.

If
P\subseteq G
is a snugly embedded discrete
p
-toral group, then

P\hookrightarrow{\overline{P}}
induces a homotopy equivalence

{(BP)}_{p}^{\wedge}\simeq{\left(B{\overline{P}}\right)}_{p}^{\wedge}
.

Not all discrete
p
-toral subgroups of a compact Lie group
G
are snugly embedded. However, since any
p
-toral group possesses maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroups by Proposition
2.2
, a
p
-toral group
can always be approximated by a snugly embedded discrete
p
-toral group. A more compact terminology will be helpful.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\bf P}
be a
p
-toral subgroup of
G
, and let
P\subseteq{\bf P}
be a snugly embedded discrete
p
-toral subgroup with
{\overline{P}}={\bf P}
. We say that
P
is a
p
-discretization

of
{\bf P}
.

In particular, a
p
-discretization of
{\bf P}
is characterized by being a maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup of
{\bf P}
.

Example 2.6
.

A torus has only one
p
-discretization, namely the subgroup consisting of all
p
-torsion elements. Similarly, Proposition
2.2
(2) establishes that any abelian
p
-toral group has a unique
p
-discretization.

In general, however, a
p
-toral group
{\bf P}
that has
multiple components has many
p
-discretizations. If
P\subseteq{\bf P}
is one such, then the others are all conjugate to
P

in
{\bf P}
by Proposition
2.2
(2). The stabilizer of
P
is
N_{\bf P}(P)
, so the approximations are parametrized by
{\bf P}/N_{\bf P}(P)
. (See also Remark
3.7
.)

The simplest nontrivial example with more than one discretization is the
2
-toral group

{\bf P}=O(2)\cong S^{1}\rtimes\{\pm 1\}
, where
-1
is represented by
reflection over the
y
-axis.
An obvious
2
-discretization is given by the subgroup
P={\mathbb{Z}}/{2}^{\infty}\rtimes\{\pm 1\}
.
A direct matrix calculation shows that
N_{\bf P}(P)=P
, so in fact the
2
-discretizations are parametrized by
{\bf P}/P\cong S^{1}/\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{2}^{\infty}\right)
.
The other parametrizations are given by

(2.7)

P^{\prime}=\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{2}^{\infty}\times\{1\}\right)\ \sqcup\ \left(\xi\cdot{\mathbb{Z}}/{2}^{\infty}\times\{-1\}\right)

where
\xi
is any fixed element of
S^{1}
.
And indeed, the proof of
[
BLO07
, 9.3]
establishes that, in general, the different
p
-discretizations of a
p
-toral group
{\bf P}
can be obtained by conjugation by an element of the torus of
{\bf P}
.

We close this section by observing that, as in (
2.7
), any
p
-discretization must start with the unique
p
-discretization of the torus.

Lemma 2.8
.

If
{\bf P}
is a
p
-toral group with maximal torus
{\bf T}
, and
T_{p}
denotes the
p
-torsion elements of
{\bf T}
, then any
p
-discretization of
{\bf P}
must contain
T_{p}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
,
T_{p}
can be expanded to a
p
-discretization
P^{\prime}
of
{\bf P}
, and
P^{\prime}
is conjugate to
P
in
{\bf P}
. However,
T_{p}
is a normal subgroup of
{\bf P}
and hence is stabilized by the conjugation. Thus
T_{p}\subseteq P
as well.
∎

3.
p
-discretizations of pairs

Let
{\bf P}
be a
p
-toral group.
Proposition
2.2
tells us that all
p
-discretizations of
{\bf P}
are conjugate in
{\bf P}
. It also tells us that if
{\bf Q}\subseteq{\bf P}
is a
p
-toral subgroup
and
Q
is a
p
-discretization of
{\bf Q}
, then
Q
can be expanded to a
p
-discretization
P
of
{\bf P}
.
However, it is likely that there is more than one way to expand
Q
; that is, there can be different pairs of discrete
p
-toral subgroups
(Q,P_{1})
and
(Q,P_{2})
that are
p
-discretizations for the pair
({\bf Q},{\bf P})
. The main goal of this section is to establish the following proposition, which can be thought of as a uniqueness statement about
p
-discretizations
of pairs. The point is that
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are conjugate in
{\bf P}
by an element that fixes
Q
.
Hence the pair
(Q,P_{1})
is conjugate in
{\bf P}
to the pair
(Q,P_{2})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

If
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are
p
-discretizations of
{\bf P}
, and
Q\subseteq P_{1}\cap P_{2}
, then there exists
{\bf y}\in C_{{\bf P}}(Q)
such that
c_{{\bf y}}\left(P_{1}\right)=P_{2}
.

Our approach is based on a non-canonical (and non-topological) splitting of
p
-toral groups, for which we use the following
standard homological lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
K
be a finite group, and let

0\longrightarrow I\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow V\longrightarrow 0

be a short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}}[K]
-modules. If
V
is uniquely
|K|
-divisible and
I
is an injective
{\mathbb{Z}}
-module, then there is a splitting

X\cong I\times V
as
{\mathbb{Z}}[K]
-modules.

Proof.

Because
I
is an injective abelian group, there is a retraction of abelian groups
r\colon X\rightarrow I
,
which in turn defines a section
s\colon V\rightarrow X
of abelian groups. However,
\Hom_{{\mathbb{Z}}}(V,X)
is uniquely
|K|
-divisible
(because
V
is),
so we can define a new section
{\tilde{s}}\colon V\rightarrow X

by averaging over the elements of
K
,

{\tilde{s}}\,:=\frac{1}{|K|}\sum_{y\in K}y^{-1}sy.

Then
{\tilde{s}}
is a section of
X\rightarrow V
as
{\mathbb{Z}}[K]
-modules, which establishes the lemma.
∎

We use the following notation for the splitting result below.
Let
{\bf T}={\mathbb{R}}^{r}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
be a rank
r
torus,
whose subgroup of torsion elements is denoted

T_{{\mathbb{Q}}}:={\mathbb{Q}}^{r}/{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}
.
The quotient of
{\bf T}
by the torsion elements is denoted

T_{\infty}:={\bf T}/T_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. If
p
is a prime, then the
p
-torsion subgroup of
{\bf T}
is denoted
T_{p}:=\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{p}^{\infty}\right)^{r}
,
and we write
T_{p^{\prime}}
for the subgroup of
{\bf T}
consisting of torsion elements of order prime to
p
, i.e. the product of all the subgroups
\left({\mathbb{Z}}/{q}^{\infty}\right)^{r}
over primes
q\neq p
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
{\bf P}
be a
p
-toral group with
p
-discretization
P\subseteq{\bf P}
. There exists a (non-canonical, discontinuous) group homomorphism
{\bf P}\rightarrow P
that splits the inclusion
P\hookrightarrow{\bf P}
. Any such splitting has the property that if
P^{\prime}
is another
p
-discretization of
{\bf P}
, then
P^{\prime}\rightarrow{\bf P}\rightarrow P
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Let
{\bf T}
be the maximal torus of
{\bf P}
and let
K=\pi_{0}{\bf P}={\bf P}/{\bf T}
, a finite
p
-group. By Lemma
3.2
applied to
{\bf T}
(considered as a discrete group), there is a split short exact sequence of
{\mathbb{Z}}[K]
-modules

0\longrightarrow T_{p}\longrightarrow{\bf T}\longrightarrow T_{p^{\prime}}\times T_{\infty}\longrightarrow 0.

Note that by Lemma
2.8
,
T_{p}\subseteq P
. Further, because
T_{p^{\prime}}\times T_{\infty}
is split from
{\bf T}
as a
{\mathbb{Z}}[K]
-module, we know
T_{p^{\prime}}\times T_{\infty}
is normal in
{\bf P}
and we can define the quotient

{\tilde{P}}:={\bf P}/\left(T_{p^{\prime}}\times T_{\infty}\right)
, which is
a discrete
p
-toral group.
(We note here that we have completely discarded the topology on the torus.
The key tool resulting from Lemma
3.3

is Lemma
3.5
, and the topology is not needed there.)

Lemma 3.6
.

Let

-discretization of a
p
-toral subgroup

\Out_{G}(P)\cong\Out_{G}({\bf P})

Proof.

We want to prove that the natural map

c_{\bf n}(P)

{\bf P}
, there exists
{\bf x}\in{\bf P}
such that
c_{\bf x}(c_{\bf n}P)=P
. Therefore
{\bf n}\cdot{\bf x}\in N_{G}(P)

and it represents the same class as

\Out_{G}({\bf P})

To show injectivity, first suppose that

We would like to show that

. However, Lemma
3.5
tells us that the automorphism of

Hence

and

To finish, suppose that

n\in N_{G}(P)\cap\left[C_{G}({\bf P})\cdot{\bf P}\right]

with
{\bf c}\in C_{G}({\bf P})=C_{G}(P)

and
{\bf x}\in{\bf P}
. Then

. The previous argument shows that
{\bf x}\in C_{G}(P)\cdot P
, and hence
n={\bf c}\cdot{\bf x}\in C_{G}(P)\cdot P
, as required.
∎

4.
Chains of
p
-centric,
p
-stubborn subgroups of
G

In
[
BLO07
]
,
Broto, Levi and Oliver construct a saturated fusion system associated to a compact Lie group
G
, denoted
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}(G)
, where
S
is a maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup of
G
. It is a category whose objects are the subgroups of
S
and whose morphisms are homomorphisms induced by conjugation in
G
.
The purpose of this section is to
compare the collection of
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}(G)
-centric,
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}(G)
-radical subgroups (Definition
4.1
) with the analogous collection of continuous
p
-toral subgroups, namely the
p
-centric,
p
-stubborn subgroups. In our forthcoming normalizer decomposition for
p
-local compact groups
[
BCG+
]
, the indexing category will be conjugacy classes of chains of
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}(G)
-centric,
{\mathcal{F}}_{S}(G)
-radical subgroups of
S
. In this section, we show that when
\pi_{0}G
is a
p
-group, the set of such conjugacy classes is in one-to-one correspondence with conjugacy classes of chains of
p
-centric,
p
-stubborn subgroups of
G
(Theorem
4.3
). Further, even when
\pi_{0}G
is not a
p
-group, there is still an injection from the first set to the second.

First we need some definitions, taken from the definitions for a fusion system (see
[
BLO07
, Def. 2.6 and pp 380]
and
[
JMO92
, Def. 1.3]
). For streamlined notation, we suppress both
G
and the maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup
S
.

Definition 4.1
.

Fix a compact Lie group
G
and a maximal discrete
p
-toral
subgroup
S\subseteq G
.

(1)

For discrete
p
-toral groups

(a)

A subgroup
P\subseteq S
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-centric
if whenever
Q\subseteq S
is
G
-conjugate to
P
, we have
C_{S}(Q)=Z(P)
. (In particular,
C_{S}(P)=Z(P)
.)

(b)

A subgroup
P\subseteq S
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical
if
\Out_{G}(P):=N_{G}(P)/\left[C_{G}(P)\cdot P\right]

has no nontrivial normal subgroups.

(2)

For continuous
p
-toral groups

(a)

A
p
-toral subgroup
{\bf P}\subseteq G
is

p
-centric in
G
if
Z({\bf P})
is a maximal
p
-toral subgroup of
C_{G}({\bf P})
.

(b)

A
p
-toral subgroup
{\bf P}\subseteq G
is

p
-stubborn in
G
if
N_{G}({\bf P})/{\bf P}
is finite
and has no nontrivial normal
p
-subgroups.

Note that although the concepts of
{\mathcal{F}}
-centric and
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical depend on both
S
and
G
, we omit them from the notation because
S
and
G
are always clear from context, and the omission gives a slimmer notation. We also observe that the properties of being
p
-centric and
p
-stubborn are closed under
G
-conjugation since
c_{g}(C_{G}({\bf P}))=C_{G}(c_{g}({\bf P}))
and
c_{g}(N_{G}({\bf P}))=N_{G}(c_{g}({\bf P}))
for any
g\in G
.

Remark 4.2
.

A
p
-toral subgroup
{\bf P}\subseteq G
is
p
-radical
if
N_{G}({\bf P})/{\bf P}
has no nontrivial normal
p
-toral subgroups (no assumption that
N_{G}({\bf P})/{\bf P}
is finite). For finite groups, the collection of
p
-radical subgroups was introduced by Bouc. In the compact Lie case,
the
p
-radical subgroups were featured in
[
Lib11
]
.

However, when
{\bf P}\subseteq G
is
p
-centric, then
p
-radical and
p
-stubborn become equivalent, because

\Out_{G}({\bf P})
is finite (
[
BLO07
, Lemma 9.4]
) and
the short exact sequence

0\rightarrow C_{G}({\bf P})/Z({\bf P})\rightarrow N_{G}({\bf P})/{\bf P}\rightarrow\Out_{G}({\bf P})\rightarrow 0

has

finite of order prime to
p
.

With the necessary vocabulary in hand, we are able to state the main theorem for this section.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
{\bf S}
be a maximal

-toral subgroup of a compact Lie group

, with

-discretization

. The closure map
P\mapsto{\overline{P}}
defines an injective map

The map is a one-to-one correspondence if

p
-group.

The first task is to show that the map of

That is, we need to establish that the closure of a discrete

-centric and

-radical is
p
-centric and
p
-stubborn. (It is certainly

To use the results of Section
3

[

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
G
be a compact Lie group with maximal discrete

p
-toral subgroup
S
, and let
P
be a subgroup of
S
.
If
P
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-centric and
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical, then
P
is snugly embedded.

Proof.

Let
{\bf P}={\overline{P}}
, and expand

to a
p
-discretization

of
{\bf P}
. To prove that
P
is snugly embedded, we would like to prove that
Q^{\prime}=P
. Expand
Q^{\prime}
further to a
p
-discretization
S^{\prime}
of
{\bf S}
, so that we have

P\subseteq Q^{\prime}\subseteq S^{\prime}\subseteq{\bf S}
. Using Proposition
3.1

, choose
{\bf y}\in C_{\bf S}(P)
such that

, and let

. Now we have

and
Q
is a
p
-discretization of
{\bf P}
, and our goal has become to prove
Q=P
.

Consider the homomorphism

(4.5)

N_{Q}(P)/Z(P)\rightarrow N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P).

Because
P
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-centric by assumption,
C_{S}(P)=Z(P)
. Therefore the centralizer of
P
in
Q\subseteq S
is
Z(P)
as well, and (
4.5
) is a monomorphism. We assert that the image is a normal subgroup
of
N_{G}(P)/C_{G}(P)
. To prove this, we must take an element
g\in N_{G}(P)
and prove that we can adjust
g
by an element of
x\in C_{G}(P)
so that
g\cdot x
normalizes
N_{Q}(P)
. Given that
g\cdot x
would certainly normalize
P
, it is sufficient to construct
x
so that
g\cdot x
normalizes
Q
.

Let
Q^{\prime\prime}=c_{g}(Q)
. Because
g\in N_{G}(P)\subseteq N_{G}({\bf P})
, the groups
Q^{\prime\prime}
and

are both
p
-discretizations of
{\bf P}
, and we have
P\subseteq Q\cap Q^{\prime\prime}
. Proposition
3.1
gives us an element
x\in C_{\bf P}(P)
such that

c_{x}(Q^{\prime\prime})=Q
. Therefore
g\cdot x

Q
and

and we conclude that
(
4.5
) is the inclusion of a normal subgroup. Taking the quotient on both sides by
P
, we find that
N_{Q}(P)/P
is a normal subgroup of
\Out_{G}(P)
.

However, we have assumed that
P
is
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical, meaning that
\Out_{G}(P)
has no nontrivial
p
-subgroups. Therefore
N_{Q}(P)/P
must be the trivial group, that is,
N_{Q}(P)=P
. Because
P
and
Q
are discrete
p
-toral groups,

implies that the inclusion of
P
into

cannot be proper
[
BLO07

, so
P=Q
. Hence
P
is a maximal discrete
p
-toral subgroup of
{\bf P}
, as required.
∎

Now that we know that
{\mathcal{F}}
-centric and
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical subgroups of

must be snug, we can
use the results of Section

Our next proposition shows that the map of Theorem

can be defined.
That is, we show that the closure of an

-centric and
{\mathcal{F}}
-radical discrete

-stubborn. (See also the argument given in

for

Proposition 4.6
.

P

{\bf P}

Proof.

To show that

is

-toral subgroup of

Our strategy to prove Theorem

Definition 5.2

Note that the definition makes no restriction on the subgroups in the chain. In particular, in the next

To show that the map is an epimorphism, we induct on

The case

3.6

For the inductive hypothesis, assume that we have

,

N_{G}\left(P_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq P_{k}\right)\xlongrightarrow{\rm{\ epi}\ }\frac{N_{G}\left({\bf P}_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq{\bf P}_{k}\right)}{N_{{\bf P}_{k}}\left({\bf P}_{0}\subseteq\ldots\subseteq{\bf P}_{k}\right)},

To establish injectivity, consider

[n]

Integrable motion of anisotropic space curves and surfaces induced by the Landau-Lifshitz equation
Zhaidary Myrzakulova, Gulgassyl Nugmanova, Kuralay Yesmakhanova, and Ratbay Myrzakulov

Abstract
In this paper, we have studied the geometrical formulation of the Landau-Lifshitz equation (LLE) and established its geometrical equivalent counterpart as some generalized nonlinear Schrödinger equation. When the anisotropy vanishes, from this result we obtain the well-known results corresponding for the isotropic case, i.e. to the Heisenberg ferromagnet equation and the focusing nonlinear Schrödinger equation. The relations between the LLE and the differential geometry of space curves in the local and in nonlocal cases are studied. Using the well-known Sym-Tafel formula, the soliton surfaces induced by the LLE are briefly considered.



Key words
: Integrable equations, Nonlinear Schrödinger equation, Heisenberg ferromagnet equation, Landau-Lifshitz equation, space curves, soliton solution, soliton surfaces, nonlocal integrable equations.

Landau-Lifshitz equation

In this section we give some basic informations on the LLE like the equation of motion, three types of Lax pairs, Hamilton formulation, different parametrizations and limiting cases.

2.1
Equation

The LLE has been introduced in
[
1
]
and describes the dynamics of the orientation of the magnetization (or spin) in ferromagnetic materials. It constitutes nowadays a fundamental tool in the magnetic recording industry, due
to its applications to ferromagnets. The LLE has the form
[
10
]

{\bf S}_{t}={\bf S}\wedge{\bf S}_{xx}+{\bf S}\wedge J{\bf S},

(2.1)

where
J=diag(J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
and
J_{1}\leq J_{2}\leq J_{3}
. In components it takes the form

\displaystyle S_{1t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{2}S_{3xx}-S_{2xx}S_{3}+S_{2}J_{3}S_{3}-J_{2}S_{2}S_{3},

(2.2)

\displaystyle S_{2t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{3}S_{1xx}-S_{3xx}S_{1}+S_{3}J_{1}S_{1}-J_{3}S_{3}S_{1},

(2.3)

\displaystyle S_{3t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{1}S_{2xx}-S_{1xx}S_{2}+S_{1}J_{2}S_{2}-J_{1}S_{1}S_{2},

(2.4)

or

\displaystyle S_{1t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{2}S_{3xx}-S_{2xx}S_{3}-J_{23}S_{2}S_{3},

(2.5)

\displaystyle S_{2t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{3}S_{1xx}-S_{3xx}S_{1}-J_{31}S_{3}S_{1},

(2.6)

\displaystyle S_{3t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{1}S_{2xx}-S_{1xx}S_{2}-J_{12}S_{1}S_{2},

(2.7)

where
J_{ij}=J_{i}-J_{j},\quad I_{ij}=J_{i}+J_{j}
. We also use the following form of the LLE

\displaystyle iS_{t}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{+}S_{3xx}-S^{+}_{xx}S_{3}+J_{3}S_{3}S^{+}-\frac{1}{2}S_{3}[J_{1}(S^{+}+S^{-})+J_{2}(S^{+}-S^{-})],

(2.8)

\displaystyle iS^{-}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{-}_{xx}S_{3}-S^{-}S_{3xx}-J_{3}S_{3}S^{-}+\frac{1}{2}S_{3}[J_{1}(S^{+}+S^{-})-J_{2}(S^{+}-S^{-})],

(2.9)

\displaystyle iS_{3t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(S^{-}S^{+}_{xx}-S^{-}_{xx}S^{+})-i(J_{2}-J_{1})S_{1}S_{2},

(2.10)

or

\displaystyle iS_{t}^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{+}S_{3xx}-S^{+}_{xx}S_{3}+J_{3}S_{3}S^{+}-\frac{1}{2}S_{3}(I_{21}S^{+}-J_{21}S^{-}),

(2.11)

\displaystyle iS^{-}_{t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S^{-}_{xx}S_{3}-S^{-}S_{3xx}-J_{3}S_{3}S^{-}-\frac{1}{2}S_{3}(J_{21}S^{+}-I_{21}S^{-}),

(2.12)

\displaystyle iS_{3t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}(S^{-}S^{+}_{xx}-S^{-}_{xx}S^{+})-\frac{1}{4}J_{12}(S^{+2}-S^{-2}).

(2.13)

The matrix LLE reads as

iS_{t}=\frac{1}{2}[S,S_{xx}+S_{J}],

(2.14)

where

S=\sum_{k=1}^{3}S_{k}\sigma_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}S_{3}&S^{-}\\
S^{+}&-S_{3}\end{pmatrix},\quad S^{2}=I,\quad S^{\pm}=S_{1}\pm iS_{2}

(2.15)

S_{J}=\sum_{k=1}^{3}J_{k}S_{k}\sigma_{k}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{3}S_{3}&J_{1}S_{1}-iJ_{2}S_{2}\\
J_{1}S_{1}+iJ_{2}S_{2}&-J_{3}S_{3}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.16)

Here
\sigma_{j}
are Pauli matrices:

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

(2.17)

We note that the LLE is a nonlinear dispersive partial differential equation. The corresponding dispersion relation reads as
[
11
]

\omega(k)=\pm\sqrt{|k|^{4}+(J_{21}+J_{23})|k|^{2}+J_{21}J_{23}},

(2.18)

what corresponds to the solutions of the form
e^{i(kx-\omega t)}
that is to linear sinusoidal waves of frequency
\omega

2.3
Hamiltonian structure

The LLE can be written in the Hamiltonian form as
[
10
]

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{t}=\{H,{\bf S}\},

(2.36)

where the Hamilton function is given by

\displaystyle H=\frac{1}{2}\int\left({\bf S}_{x}^{2}-J_{1}S_{1}^{2}-J_{2}S_{2}^{2}-J_{3}S_{3}^{2}\right)dx.

(2.37)

The Poisson bracket for the two functionals
A,B
has the form

\displaystyle\{A,B\}=-\sum_{a,b,c=1}^{3}\epsilon_{abc}\int\frac{\delta A}{\delta S_{a}}\frac{\delta B}{\delta S_{b}}S_{c}dxdy.

(2.38)

In particular, for the components of the spin vector the corresponding Poisson brackets read as

\displaystyle\{S_{a}(x),S_{b}(y)\}=-\epsilon_{abc}S_{c}(x)\delta(x-y).

(2.39)

Quantum Heisenberg model

It is well-known that the LLE (1.1) is the continuous classical limit of the quantum Heisenberg XYZ model:

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(J_{1}\hat{S}_{1j}\hat{S}_{1(j+1)}+J_{2}\hat{S}_{2j}\hat{S}_{2(j+1)}+J_{3}\hat{S}_{3j}\hat{S}_{3(j+1)}+h\hat{S}_{3j}\right)

Conclusion

In this paper, we have
established that the LLE (1.1) is geometrical equivalent to the
generalized nonlinear Schrödinger like equation (3.9)-(3.11).
When
the anisotropy vanishes, from this result follows the well-known results corresponding for the continuous isotropic spin chain, i.e. the HFE (1.2). We have studied the relation between the LLE and the differential geometry of space curves in the local and nonlocal cases. Also the soliton surfaces induced by the LLE are briefly considered using the well-known Sym-Tafel formula.

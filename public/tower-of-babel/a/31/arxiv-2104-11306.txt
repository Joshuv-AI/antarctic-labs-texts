Homogenization of high-contrast composites under differential constraints
Elisa Davoli, Martin Kružík, Valerio Pagliari

Abstract
. We derive, by means of variational techniques, a limiting description for a class of integral functionals under linear differential constraints. The functionals are designed to encode the energy of a high-contrast composite, that is, a heterogeneous material which, at a microscopic level, consists of a periodically perforated matrix whose cavities are occupied by a filling with very different physical properties. Our main result provides a Γ \Gamma -convergence analysis as the periodicity tends to zero, and shows that the variational limit of the functionals at stake is the sum of two contributions, one resulting from the energy stored in the matrix and the other from the energy stored in the inclusions. As a consequence of the underlying high-contrast structure, the study is faced with a lack of coercivity with respect to the standard topologies in L p L^{p} , which we tackle by means of two-scale convergence techniques. In order to handle the differential constraints, instead, we establish new results about the existence of potentials and of constraint-preserving extension operators for linear, k k -th order, homogeneous differential operators with constant coefficients and constant rank.

1.
Introduction

The identification of materials capable
to switch, enhance, or tune their properties according to external stimuli
is one of the key catalysts
for the creation of new artificially engineered composites: the metamaterials.
In the present paper,
we contribute to the mathematical study of metamaterials
by providing a unified limiting description for a class of energies
that are inspired by the physics of high-contrast composites and
are evaluated on fields subject to suitable partial differential constraints.

High-contrast materials are characterized by the property that
their microscopic physical features may change abruptly from point to point.
In the case of a binary periodic medium of this kind,
we have two relevant microscales:
the periodicity of the microstructure and the high-contrast parameter,
which encodes the strong (high-contrast) difference in the properties of the two components.
Typically, the scale at which periodicity is observed is much smaller than the size of the specimen.
It is hence natural, from a mathematical viewpoint,
to study the asymptotics of the quantities that describe the system
in the limit of vanishing period.
This procedure is called homogenization.
It allows to make predictions on the effective character of heterogeneous media
when no experiments are available,
and eases numerical simulations
by reducing the degrees of freedom of the problem.
Therefore, mathematical homogenization has been a lively field
of investigation for many decades.
We refer e.g. to the monographs
[
10
,
34
,
84
]

for a thorough introduction to the subject.

In this paper, we tackle the case in which
both the periodicity and the high-contrast are quantified by a small number
\varepsilon>0

(see (
1.3
) and Remark
2.1
).
We assume that the vector fields that encode the significant quantities of the system
satisfy a linear first-order differential constraint.
The rationale behind this choice becomes transparent
if one focuses on specific theories:
in large strain elasticity
the deformation gradient is a significant strain measure, while
electromagnetism deals with the differential operators encoded by the Maxwell system.
In other settings,
the elastic behavior of some materials can be influenced
by electric currents or magnetic fields and vice versa
(see e.g.
[
80
]
for a recent contribution).
In all these cases,
the key quantities lie in the kernel of a suitable differential operator.
We therefore see that
the need of combining homogenization and differential constraints naturally arises and leads to generalized notions of convexity (see
[
13
]
for an overview).
It is well-known that all the differential constraints above (and many more) can be treated in a unified way
in the framework of

\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvexity
(see
[
50
]
and references therein).
Such a notion allows to extend various results
which were originally available only for Sobolev maps and their gradients
to the setting where admissible fields belong to the kernel of a linear first-order differential operator
\mathscr{A}
of constant rank.

The novelty of our contribution consists
in tackling homogenization of high-contrast materials in the setting of
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvexity.
In order to describe our main results, we need to introduce some basic notation.

For
d\in\mathbb{N}
,
d\geq 2
,
let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, connected, open set with Lipschitz boundary.
We regard
\Omega
as a reference configuration
for a composite with a high-contrast microstructure
obtained by periodically inserting inclusions of a specific kind in a surrounding matrix.
For instance, in the context of elasticity,

M. Cherdantsev & K. D. Cherednichenko

[
25
]
considered
a stiff matrix with embedded soft fillings.
In general, applications require that
the properties of the inclusions and of the matrix are tuned
so that the resulting effective behavior of the composite is the desired one.

To depict the fine texture of the material in mathematical terms,
we consider the periodicity cell
Q\coloneqq(0,1)^{d}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}

and an open connected subset with Lipschitz boundary
D_{0}\Subset Q
(see Figure
1
),
where the symbol

indicates that

is compactly contained in

.
The high-contrast behavior of the composite sitting in

is identified
by means of the sets
D_{0}
and
D_{1}\coloneqq Q\setminus\bar{D}_{0}

in the following way:
we introduce a small parameter
\varepsilon>0

representing the periodicity scale
and we define the sets

(1.1)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{z\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(D_{0}+z),\quad\text{with }Z_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\Set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\varepsilon(\bar{D}_{0}+z)\subset\Omega},

and

(1.2)

\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0,\varepsilon},

which correspond respectively
to the inclusions and to the surrounding matrix (see Figure

\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}

,

This use is informal and just meant to convey ideas,

The reader should however bear in mind that

The main goal of this contribution is to characterize the asymptotic behavior as
\varepsilon\to 0
in the sense of
\Gamma
-convergence
[
15
,
38
]
of the energy functionals

(1.3)

\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}f_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon u\right)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}}f_{1}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},u\right)\mathrm{d}x,

evaluated on fields
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying

\mathscr{A}u=0
,
where
\mathscr{A}
is a suitable linear first-order partial differential operator.
The precise class of constraints is presented in Subsection
2.2
below
and further investigated in Section
7
. In the expression above,
the family
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}
represents the energy densities stored in the ‘soft’ inclusions,
while
f_{1}
encodes the behavior of the ‘stiff’ matrix. The exact assumptions on
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
f_{1}
are collected in Subsection
2.1
.
We point out that
considering a sequence of
\varepsilon
-dependent energy densities for the ‘soft’ component is not merely a mathematical mannerism:
on the contrary, it is a modeling necessity
that arises when the admissible maps
u
are assumed to have the form
u=\nabla v
for suitable fields
v
, i.e., when
\mathscr{A}=
curl.
We refer to
[
25
, Remark 2]
for a discussion on this point.

When
f_{1}
does not oscillate and the parameter
\varepsilon
is fixed,
determining an optimal shape
D=\set{x\in\Omega:\chi(x)=1}
for the functionals

(u,\chi)\mapsto\int_{\Omega}\big[\chi f_{0}(u)+(1-\chi)f_{1}(u)\big]\mathrm{d}{x}

under a volume constraint amounts to solve a shape-optimization problem.
In this case, the aim is, for instance, finding
the optimal distribution in terms of compliance
for two materials with different stiffness in a given container
\Omega
.
By enforcing in the energy a dependence on
\varepsilon

like the one we consider,
we attempt to encode the fact that
the material properties in the set
D
and in its complement may be very different;
specifically, the one sitting in
D
becomes increasingly soft as
\varepsilon
vanishes.
In addition, finer and finer microstructures may be taken into account
by assuming as well a dependence on
\varepsilon
of the shapes,
so that
D
is replaced by
\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}
.
Problems of this kind are central, e.g.,
in the modeling of lattice materials for additive manufacturing.
We refer to the monograph
[
3
]
and the paper
[
4
]

for extensive discussions about the connections
between homogenization and topology optimization.

Even though
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}
and
f_{1}
in (
1.3
) are assumed to satisfy
p
-growth conditions from above and below, the fact that the ‘soft’ energies are evaluated on
\varepsilon u
rather than

results in a loss of coercivity. As a result, classical weak and strong
L^{p}
-topologies are not capable to capture the asymptotic behavior of the problem and one needs to resort to an
ad hoc
notion of ‘convergence in the high-contrast sense’, which instead keeps track of the finest features of the microstructure, cf. Definition
2.5
.
Denoting by
U_{1}
the set of maps in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
\Omega
, our main result consists in showing that the limiting behavior of the energies in (
1.3
) is encoded by the functional

\mathscr{F}\colon U_{1}\to\mathbb{R}

defined as

\mathscr{F}(u)\coloneqq\alpha_{0}+\int_{\Omega}f_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(u(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x.

In the formula above,

\alpha_{0}
is a constant determined by
f_{0}
(see (
2.8
)),

f_{\mathrm{hom}}
is a suitable energy density related to
f_{1}
(see (
2.9
)),
and the condition
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
\Omega
has to be interpreted in a distributional sense (see Subsection
2.2
).

Our first main result is presented in Theorem
2.6
, and,
loosely speaking, states the following:

Theorem
.

The functional
\mathscr{F}
is the
\Gamma
-limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the energies in (
1.3
)
restricted to
\mathscr{A}
-free fields with respect to the high-contrast convergence.

Key techniques to establish the theorem are
p
-equiintegrability arguments, as well as the notions of two-scale convergence
[
2
]
and of periodic unfolding
[
32
,
33
,
81
,
82
]
.
The proof relies essentially on the possibility of decoupling the behavior of the material in the ‘soft’ and ‘stiff’ contributions.
The
\Gamma
-convergence of the ‘stiff’ portion is a corollary of homogenization results in the setting of
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvex
[
46
]
.
The study of the ‘soft’ part is instead more challenging,
for the presence of the high-contrast microstructure and its interplay with the differential constraint result in the emergence of a second, hidden scale.
This makes
both the identification of a lower bound and the proof of its optimality
need a delicate combination
of two-scale results and weak-
L^{p}
compactness.

The decoupling of the system into regions having different material features hinges on careful compactness and splitting arguments. The latter are in turn based on two essential properties of the differential constraint
\mathscr{A}
: the fact that
\mathscr{A}
-free maps in the periodicity cell have null average and the existence of a suitable extension operator on perforated domains (see Assumptions
1
and
2
). In our general setting, these conditions are quite hard to ensure, in contrast to the well-understood case in which the set
\Omega
is contractible,
\mathscr{A}
is the curl operator, and the
\mathscr{A}
-free fields are gradients. In particular, to the authors’ knowledge, even for standard operators, it is still an open question whether extension operators preserving the
\mathscr{A}
-free constraint from a perforated domain to the ‘filled’ set exist. Similar results are available for gradients both in the Sobolev setting
[
1
]
and in the weaker one of antiplane fracture
[
20
]
. We also mention the recent result in
[
19
]
for the case of symmetric gradients in linearized fracture (in the space
GSBD

[
39
]
).

The analysis of the differential constraints satisfying the aforementioned Assumptions
1
and
2
is the focus of Section
7
. A special case in which the first requirement holds is that of operators
\mathscr{A}
admitting ‘potentials’, namely such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
if and only if
u=\mathscr{B}w
for a suitable differential operator
\mathscr{B}
and a field
w
. The second main contribution of this paper consists in showing that for a broad class of operators
\mathscr{A}
existence of potentials and existence of extension operators are closely related. Roughly speaking, we prove the following.

Theorem
.

Let
\Omega
be a ‘nice’ set and let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear homogeneous differential operator with constant coefficients and constant rank. If an extension operator from
\mathscr{A}
-free maps on
\Omega
to
\mathscr{A}
-free maps on the whole space exists, then
\mathscr{A}
admits a potential on
\Omega
.
Conversely, if
\mathscr{A}
admits a potential on
\Omega
for which a suitable Korn-type inequality holds true, then there exists an extension operator from
\Omega
to the whole space which preserves the property of being
\mathscr{A}
-free.

We refer to Theorems
2.10
and
2.11
for the precise formulation of the result,
as well as to Section
7
for a broader discussion on the topic.
We point out that assuming the existence of potentials imposes some topological constraints on the set
\Omega
.
An example is easily provided by the case
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{curl}
for
d=3
:
one needs to require at least
\Omega
to be simply connected, cf. Remark
7.2
and Examples
7.17
–
7.20
. Essential tools for the proof of Theorems
2.10
and
2.11
are the theory of Fourier multipliers and the notion of generalized Moore-Penrose inverse.

2.1.
Energy functionals

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

,

We denote by
\chi_{0,\varepsilon}
and

the characteristic functions respectively of
\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}
and
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
in
\Omega
(see (

) and (

)), i.e. for
i=0,1

(2.1)

\chi_{i,\varepsilon}(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\in\Omega_{i,\varepsilon},\\

Similarly, we let

\chi_{i}(y)\coloneqq\begin{cases}1&\text{if }y\in D_{i},\\
0&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

denote the characteristic function
\chi_{i}
of
D_{i}
in
Q
.

We assume that
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon>0}
and
f_{1}
in (

) fulfill the following set of hypotheses:

H1:

each
f_{0,\varepsilon}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
is continuous
and

H2:

f_{1}(\,\cdot\,,\xi)
is
Q
-periodic for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
;

H3:

there exist
a,\lambda,\Lambda>0
such that
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lambda(-a+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p})\leq f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)\leq\Lambda(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p}),

\displaystyle\lambda(-a+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p})\leq f_{1}(x,\xi)\leq\Lambda(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p}),

for a fixed
p\in(1,+\infty)
;

H4:

there exists
\mu>0
such that for a.e.
x\in\Omega
,
for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\lvert f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)-f_{0,\varepsilon}(\eta)\rvert\leq\mu\left(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\eta\rvert^{p-1}\right)\lvert\xi-\eta\rvert,

\displaystyle\lvert f_{1}(x,\xi)-f_{1}(x,\eta)\rvert\leq\mu\left(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\eta\rvert^{p-1}\right)\lvert\xi-\eta\rvert,

where

is the same as in

;

H5:

there exists

such that
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)=f_{0}(\xi).

Remark 2.1

(Degeneracy of the ‘soft’ component)
.

We draw again attention to the fact that,
in spite of the standard coercivity and growth of
f_{0,\varepsilon}
,
the problem we address features some degeneracy,
which is expressed by the factor
\varepsilon
multiplying the argument of the ‘soft’ integrand (see (
1.3
)).
From a modeling perspective,
this
\varepsilon
accounts for the high-contrast between the two components,
in that it makes the ‘soft’ component less and less sensitive to external stimuli.
This can be easily seen in the simple instance
f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)=f_{1}(\xi)=\lvert\xi\rvert^{p}
:
formula (
1.3
) becomes

\varepsilon^{p}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}f_{0,\varepsilon}(u)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}}f_{1}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},u\right)\mathrm{d}x,

and the ratio
1/\varepsilon^{p}
between the ‘stiffness coefficients’ of the two components blows up as
\varepsilon
vanishes.

Owing to the growth conditions prescribed by hypothesis
H3
,
the natural environment for our problem is the space
L^{p}
.
We will often work with
Q
-periodic functions, i.e.,
those
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
such that

u(x+z)=u(x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and all
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
We will use the subscript ‘
\mathrm{per}
’ to denote subspaces of
Q
-periodic maps;
for instance,

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})\coloneqq\Set{u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}):u\text{ is $Q$-periodic}}.

We endow the previous space with the norm of
L^{p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
With a slight abuse of notation,
we will tacitly identify
L^{p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

by mapping the unit cube into the unit torus in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and extending the corresponding fields by periodicity. Analogously, we will denote the subspace of
L^{p}_{\rm loc}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

containing all the functions
that are
Q
-periodic w.r.t. their second argument by

L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and implicitly identify this space with
L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

2.2.
The differential constraint

Let
p\in(1,+\infty)
be as in
H3
and
H4
above.
We suppose that
the constraint that we couple with the energy in (
1.3
)
falls within the framework of
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvexity
as described by
I. Fonseca & S. Müller

[
50
]
.
Namely, for
N,M\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{0}
,
we let
\mathscr{A}
be a partial differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{M}

whose action on a function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is given by

(2.2)

\mathscr{A}u\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}A^{(i)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},

where
A^{(i)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
are linear maps for all
i=1,\dots,d
.
In other words,

\mathscr{A}
is a linear first-order differential operator with constant coefficients.

Our fundamental requirement on
\mathscr{A}
is the
constant rank
assumption
introduced by
F. Murat

[
66
]
, that is,
we suppose that there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}
such that
the
symbol

\mathbb{A}
of
\mathscr{A}
satisfies the following:
for all
\omega=(\omega_{1},\dots,\omega_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
, the operator

(2.3)

\mathbb{A}[\omega]\coloneqq\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega_{i}A^{(i)}

has rank equal to
r
.

We observe that for every open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathscr{A}
can be regarded as an operator
from
L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
W^{-1,p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M})
, where

W^{-1,p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M})
denotes the dual
of the Sobolev space
W^{1,p/(p-1)}_{0}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.
To see this,
we introduce the formal adjoint of
\mathscr{A}
, denoted by
\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
. For
v\colon O\to\mathbb{R}^{M}
, we set

\mathscr{A}^{\ast}v\coloneqq-\sum_{i=1}^{d}A^{(i),\ast}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x_{i}},

where
A^{(i),\ast}
is the adjoint (i.e. the transpose) of
A^{(i)}
.
In this way,

\int_{O}\mathscr{A}\phi\cdot\psi\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{O}\phi\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi\,\mathrm{d}{x}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})\text{ and }\psi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{1}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

and for
u\in L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})
we define the pairing

\langle\mathscr{A}u,v\rangle\coloneqq\int_{O}u\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}v\,\mathrm{d}x\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

where
p^{\prime}
is the conjugate exponent to
p
.
In what follows, if
u\in L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
equalities of the form
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
O
are always tacitly understood
in the sense of
W^{-1,p}
, or, in other words,
in the sense that

(2.4)

\int_{O}u\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}v\,\mathrm{d}{x}=0\quad\text{for all }v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M}).

When such a relation holds,
we say that
u
is
\mathscr{A}
-free
on
O
.
Similarly, if
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
we say that it is
\mathscr{A}
-free on the unit torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}

when the equality in (
2.4
) is satisfied for
O=Q

and for all
v\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
;
in particular, if
u\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
\mathscr{A}
-free on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
then it is
\mathscr{A}
-free on
Q
as well.

In addition to the constant rank hypothesis,
we need to consider further restrictions on the class of operators
\mathscr{A}

for which our analysis will be performed.
We formulate two
ad hoc
assumptions:

Assumption 1

(Null-average)
.

For all
u\in L^{p}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

u=0
on
D_{1}
and

\mathscr{A}u=0
on
\mathbb{T}^{d}
,
it holds
\int_{Q}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}=0
.

Assumption 2

(
\mathscr{A}
-free extension)
.

There exists an
\varepsilon
-independent constant
c>0
and a sequence of operators
\set{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}}
, with
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that the following holds:
for all
\mathscr{A}
-free
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u=u
a.e. in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,

(3)

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u)=0
on
\Omega
, and

(4)

if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
p
-equiintegrable, then
\set{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
is also
p
-equiintegrable in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We recall that

\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
p
-equiintegrable
if for every
\eta>0
there exists
m>0
such that

\int_{E}\lvert u_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}<\eta\quad\text{for all }\varepsilon>0

whenever
E\subset\Omega
satisfies
\mathscr{L}^{d}(E)<m
.
Thanks to the Dunford-Pettis Theorem,
this is equivalent to the fact that

\set{\lvert u_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{p}}
admits a subsequence
that is weakly convergent in
L^{1}(\Omega)
.
We point out that in Assumption
2

it is essential to start with maps
u
which are
\mathscr{A}
-free in the whole set
\Omega
,
for this is related to the existence of ‘potentials’ for the operator
\mathscr{A}
.
We refer to the considerations after Theorem
2.11
in Subsection
2.3

for a discussion on this point,
while we collect some comments on Assumption
2
in the next remark .

Remark 2.2

(On Assumption
2
)
.

In problems involving perforated media
the use of extension techniques is fairly common, and
Assumption
2
is akin to others in the literature.
For comparison, we mention
[
47
, Definition 1.4]
.
The main differences consist in: 1) the fact that the extension operator here is assumed to exist on a periodically perforated domain, with constants independent of the periodicity parameter
\varepsilon
; 2) the fact that the existence of an extended
p
-equiintegrable sequence here is only required when starting from a sequence that also had
p
-equiintegrability properties. We refer to
[
47
]
for a general discussion on the connections between the existence of extension operators from an open set to a bigger surrounding domain, and the theory of DiPerna-Majda measures.

A notion of
\mathscr{A}
-free extension domain was also considered in
[
60
, Definition 4.4]
.
There, however, no
p
-equiintegrability conditions are prescribed. As pointed out in
[
60
, Section 4.2]
, such extension results are, in general, non-trivial. Additionally, they depend both on the topology of the set and on the operator
\mathscr{A}
under consideration. In particular, no such
\mathscr{A}
-free extension is possible when
\mathscr{A}
is the operator associated to the Cauchy-Riemann system, not even in the case of very regular sets
\Omega
.

As far as our analysis is concerned,
items (1)–(3) in Assumption
2
are among the main ingredients
in the proof of Proposition
4.1
, and
without them we would not be able
to establish high-contrast compactness for sequences with equibounded energy
(see Definition
2.5
).
On the contrary, the preservation of
p
-equiintegrability is not required at this stage,
whereas it is used in the proof of our main
\Gamma
-convergence result
(see the proofs of Propositions
2.9
and
4.4
).
Nonetheless, point (4) in Assumption
2
could be dispensed with
in some simplified versions of our problem,
e.g. when
f_{0,\varepsilon}=f_{0}
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
\Omega
is a rectangle.

For the sake of brevity,
it is convenient to give a name
to the class of operators addressed in our study:

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear first-order differential operator as in (
2.2
).
We say that
\mathscr{A}
is
admissible

if and only if it is of constant rank
and it satisfies Assumptions
1
and
2
.

It is well-known that
the family of constant rank operators is quite large
[
50
]
;
as for the subclass of the admissible ones,
we prove that it is non-empty in Section
7

where, in particular, we analyze the cases
of the curl, of the
\mathrm{curl\,curl}
operator, of the divergence,
as well as the setting associated with higher-order gradients.

2.3.
Main results

The first main result of this paper, Theorem
2.6
below, is the characterization
of the asymptotic behavior as
\varepsilon\to 0
of the functionals in (
1.3
)
when the family is restricted to
\mathscr{A}
-free fields.
In order to deduce also some information on the related energy minimizers,
we resort to a variational kind of convergence,
namely
\Gamma
-convergence (see e.g.
[
15
,
38
]
).
Here, we consider a quite abstract version of its definition:
indeed, we consider functionals defined on a certain set
for which it is only known that some of its sequences are converging,
the limit point being declared too;
no topological structure is provided, instead.
We summarize this situation by saying that
such a set is endowed with a notion of convergence.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a set endowed with a notion of convergence.
We say that the family
\set{F_{\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon>0}

of functions on
X
with values in
[-\infty,+\infty]

\Gamma
-converges
as
\varepsilon\to 0

to the function
F\colon X\to[-\infty,+\infty]

if for any
x\in X
and
for any sequence
\set{\varepsilon_{k}}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0

the following holds:

(1)

for any sequence
\set{x_{\varepsilon_{k}}}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X

such that
x_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to x
, we have

F(x)\leq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{\varepsilon_{k}});

(2)

there exists a sequence
\set{x_{\varepsilon_{k}}}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset X

such that
x_{\varepsilon_{k}}\to x
and

\limsup_{k\to+\infty}F_{\varepsilon_{k}}(x_{\varepsilon_{k}})\leq F(x).

We have already remarked that
the natural domain of the functionals (
2.10
) is contained in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
However, in our setting, the two kinds of convergence in this space
that are most frequently considered,
the strong and the weak one,
are not well-suited to perform a
\Gamma
-convergence analysis.
Indeed, they do not match the high-contrast nature of the problem and
would therefore not yield useful compactness results
for sequences with equibounded energy.
This leads us to introduce a peculiar notion of convergence.

Definition 2.5
.

We say that a family
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

converges in the high-contrast sense
to
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(relatively to the sequence of sets
\set{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}}
)
if
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and if there exists a second family
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
u_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
and

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Some comments are in order.
The reason why
the definition above is made up of two requirements
is that
there is no standard convergence which is able to capture alone
the behaviors of both components.
On one hand, the convergence of extensions is related to the coercivity of the ‘stiff’ part,
and it is a somehow customary requirement in homogenization
(see Subsection
2.4
);
on the other, the convergence
\varepsilon u\rightharpoonup 0
is needed
to keep track of the ‘soft’ component.
Here, in the light of Assumption
1
,
the
0
vector turns out to be the average of some two-scale limit.

If we select
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{curl}
and we assume that
\Omega
is simply connected,
the problem may be recast in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
.
In this setting, as far as the asymptotics of the ‘stiff’ part is concerned,
Rellich-Kondrachov’s Theorem allows to switch from weak convergence in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)
to strong convergence in
L^{p}(\Omega)
.
Therefore, the use of high-contrast convergence on the matrix leads to the standard homogenization results for perforated media.
At the same time, it allows to quantify the change in the effective energy of the perforated material
that occurs when ‘soft’ inclusions are added
(cf.
\alpha_{0}
in (
2.7
)).
Indeed, it can be readily shown
that
\varepsilon\nabla w_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0

if
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(\nabla w_{\varepsilon})\leq c

by well-known two-scale properties of gradients.

As a last remark on Definition
2.5
, note that
it is obvious that
the weak convergence in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
of a sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
of
\mathscr{A}
-free maps entails high-contrast convergence
(it suffices to set
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}
).
In particular, bounded sequences in
L^{p}
are pre-compact in the high-contrast sense.
As Proposition
4.1
proves, however,
a much weaker control is enough (see (
4.10
)),
at least for admissible differential operators.

It will be convenient to have a special notation
for spaces of functions satisfying the differential constraint encoded by
\mathscr{A}
.
For any open
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, we define

(2.5)

U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\coloneqq\big\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})):u=0\text{ if }y\in D_{1}\\
\text{ and }\mathscr{A}_{y}u=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}\big\}

and

(2.6)

\displaystyle U_{1}\coloneqq\set{u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}):\mathscr{A}u=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})}.

Note that limit points of high-contrast convergent sequences are automatically in
U_{1}
. Hereafter, we use the subscripts
x
and
y
to denote that
the operator
\mathscr{A}
acts on the variables
x
and
y
, respectively.
We will prove that
the limiting behavior of the
\varepsilon
-dependent energies constrained to
\mathscr{A}
-free fields is described
by the functional
\mathscr{F}\colon U_{1}\to\mathbb{R}

defined as

(2.7)

\mathscr{F}(u)\coloneqq\alpha_{0}+\int_{\Omega}f_{\mathrm{hom}}\big(u(x)\big)\mathrm{d}x

where

(2.8)

\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega}\inf_{u\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

and

(2.9)

f_{\mathrm{hom}}(\xi)\coloneqq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\inf\Bigg\{\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\int_{Q\cap k^{-1}(D_{1}+z)}f_{1}\big(ky,\xi+v(y)\big)\mathrm{d}y:v\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})\\
\text{ with }\int_{Q}v(z)\mathrm{d}z=0\text{ and }\mathscr{A}v=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\Bigg\}.

We are now ready to state our main convergence result.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
\mathscr{F}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))\to\mathbb{R}\cup\set{+\infty}
be the functional in (
2.7
) and, for
\varepsilon>0
,
let
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}\cup\set{+\infty}
be given by

(2.10)

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}f_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon u\right)\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}}f_{1}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},u\right)\mathrm{d}x}&\text{if }u\in U_{1},\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).\end{cases}

Let us assume that
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}
and
f_{1}
fulfill
H1
–
H5
,
and that
\mathscr{A}
is an admissible partial differential operator
in the sense of Definition
2.3
.
Then, the following properties hold:

(1)

If a family
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfies

\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
for all
\varepsilon>0
and for some
c\geq 0
, then it admits a subsequence
that is convergent in the high-contrast sense.

(2)

For any
u\in U_{1}
and for any sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

that converges to
u
in the high-contrast sense, it holds that

\mathscr{F}(u)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

(3)

For any
u\in U_{1}
there exists a sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset U_{1}

that converges to
u
in the high-contrast sense and satisfies

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathscr{F}(u).

By standard
\Gamma
-convergence techniques,
we find the following corollary of Theorem
2.6
:

Corollary 2.7
.

Under the same assumptions and notation of Theorem
2.6
,
if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a sequence of almost-minimizers for
\set{\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}}
, i.e., if

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-\inf_{u\in U_{1}}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)\right)=0,

then there exists a subsequence of
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}

that is convergent in the high-contrast sense to a minimum point of
\mathscr{F}
.
Moreover,

\inf_{u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)\to\min_{u\in U_{1}}\mathscr{F}(u).

Our proof of Theorem
2.6
relies on four main intermediate results:
1) a compactness analysis combined with the characterization of high-contrast limits of
\mathscr{A}
-free fields,
cf. Proposition

;
2) a splitting argument that allows

-limit of

;
3) the identification of an optimal lower bound for the ‘soft’ part of the energy, cf. Proposition

;

.
The proof of Theorem

is exposed in Section

.

The last section is focused on the study of admissible differential operators.

.

Theorem 2.10

(Existence of potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free maps)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}

-th order, homogeneous differential operator

-extension domain,
then there exists

, and a differential operator

An immediate consequence of this result is that

).

Our third and last main result shows that for operators admitting a potential

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
\mathscr{A}

Let also

where

and
\mathbb{B}
are
the symbols of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
, respectively.
We further assume that

•

for all
\mathscr{A}
-free

there exists

satisfying

;

•

for any bounded, connected and open set

\mathscr{B}
-free maps

\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}\colon W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})\to W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
as well as a constant
c>0
such that

(2.15)

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\mathscr{B}w\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad\text{for all }w\in W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M}).

Then, there exist a constant
c\coloneqq c(d,p,D_{1})>0
independent of
\varepsilon
and and
\Omega
, as well as a sequence of maps
\set{{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}}
, with

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
satisfying Assumption
2
.

It is fundamental to observe that the maps under consideration in the theorem above are already
a priori
in the kernel of the operator
\mathscr{A}
on the whole set
\Omega
. For this reason, it is meaningful to assume the existence of potentials
\mathscr{B}
. In the case in which, instead, our fields were in the kernel of the operator
\mathscr{A}
only in the perforated domain, its lack of contractibility would prevent the existence of a potential to hold even in the simple setting
d=3
and
\mathscr{A}={\rm curl}
. We refer once again to Remark
7.2
for a counterexample.

2.4.
Comparison with other works

Before getting to the heart of the matter, we take the chance to compare our main convergence result, Theorem
2.6
,
with similar ones in the literature.

When
\mathscr{A}
is the curl operator, our analysis is akin to the one performed by
M. Cherdantsev & K. D. Cherednichenko
in
[
25
]
.
However, our conclusions differ from theirs, also in the simple situation of a contractible
\Omega
. Indeed, even though under this topological assumption curl-free maps coincide with gradients, the notion of convergence that we use is much weaker than the strong two-scale convergence considered in
[
25
]
.
Another key difference with
[
25
]
is that
in our setting convergence of minimizers follows directly from Theorem
2.6
(see Corollary
2.7
), whereas in
[
25
]
it had to be shown
a posteriori
(see
[
25
, Section 10]
).
Actually, the introduction of high-contrast convergence is motivated exactly by the fact that sequences with uniformly bounded energy
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
are sequentially precompact in that sense.

This notion of convergence is inspired by the one considered by
X. Pellet, L. Scardia, & C. I. Zeppieri
in
[
68
]
to deal with high-contrast Mumford-Shah energies. Unlike that contribution, aside from the role of the differential constraint
\mathscr{A}
, we require additionally that
\set{\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}}
converges to zero, so as to comply with the delicate two-scale characterization in Proposition
4.1
.

We also stress that, in the absence of growth conditions from below on the ‘soft’ part of the energy, the weak
L^{p}
-topology alone would not ensure convergence of minimizers. We refer to
[
25
, Section 4]
and
[
17
, Section 4]
for a discussion on this topic.

For a general differential constraint
\mathscr{A}
, our approach does not correspond to the ones in
[
17
,
25
]
.
If
\mathscr{A}
does not admit a of suitable ‘potential’
\mathscr{B}
satisfying a Korn-type inequality (cf. Theorem
2.10
below), then
\mathscr{A}
-free maps and fields
u
of the form
u=\mathscr{B}w
are not interchangeable.
Another
caveat
involves the choice of the ‘soft’ effective energy. Looking at the results in
[
25
]
, one could expect that in our case the
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvex envelope of
f_{0}
is to be retrieved in the
\Gamma
-limit.
We recall that for a continuous function
g\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}
with
p
-growth the
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvex envelope of
g
is defined as

Q_{\mathscr{A}}g(\xi)\coloneqq\inf\left\{\int_{Q}g(\xi+v(z))\mathrm{d}z:v\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})\right.\\
\left.\text{ with }\int_{Q}v(z)\mathrm{d}z=0\text{ and }\mathscr{A}v=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\right\}.

The example below shows that
Q_{\mathscr{A}}f_{0}
in not the correct limiting energy density.

Example 2.12
.

Let
\set{{f}_{0,\varepsilon}}
satisfy
H1
–
H5
,
let
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
, and let
D\subset D_{0}
be open.
In general, if a family
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime};L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
satisfies

\displaystyle\set{w_{\varepsilon}}\text{ is }p\text{-equiintegrable},

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}w_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}

we cannot conclude that for all
\xi_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
it holds

(2.16)

\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D}Q_{\mathscr{A}}f_{0}(\xi_{0})\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\big(\xi_{0}+w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

as the following counterexample proves.

For
d=2
and
N=2\times 2
, up to translations and dilations,
we may assume
\Omega^{\prime}\coloneqq(0,1)\times(0,1)=Q
.
We focus on the case
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{curl}
, in which
the
\mathscr{A}
-quasiconvex envelope
Q_{\mathscr{A}}f
of the function
f
coincides
with the well-know quasiconvex envelope
Qf

[
35
, Section 6.3]
.
Given
\lambda>0
, as energy densities
we set
f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)=f_{0}(\xi)=\psi_{\lambda}(\xi)-\det(\xi)
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
where
\psi_{\lambda}\colon\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a convex function such that

\psi_{\lambda}(\xi)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\left|\xi\right|<2,\\
(2+\lambda)\left|\xi\right|^{2}&\text{if }\left|\xi\right|\geq 3.\end{cases}

In this way, requirements
H1
and
H3
–
H5
are met,
and
Qf_{0,\varepsilon}=Qf_{0}=f_{0}
, because
f_{0,\varepsilon}=f_{0}
is quasiconvex.

In order to disprove (
2.16
), we pick
\xi_{0}=0
and,
for
x=(x_{1},x_{2})\in(0,1)\times(0,1)
and
y\in D
,
we define

a(x)\coloneqq\begin{cases}-\mathbb{I}&\text{if }x_{1}\in\left(0,\dfrac{1}{2}\right]\\[11.0pt]
\mathbb{I}&\text{if }x_{1}\in\left(\dfrac{1}{2},1\right)\end{cases}\quad\text{and}\quad w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\coloneqq a\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+\varepsilon\mathbb{I},

where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2}
and

a
is extended by
Q
-periodicity.
Then,
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable and

w_{\varepsilon}{\rightharpoonup}0
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{2\times 2})
.
Besides,
\mathscr{A}_{y}w_{\varepsilon}=\mathrm{curl}_{y}w_{\varepsilon}=0
,
because the sequence does not depend on
y
.

For such particular choices, we see that the left-hand side of (
2.16
) equals
0
,
while for the right-hand side we find

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D}\Big[\psi_{\lambda}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)-\det\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\Big]\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D}\det\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x=-\mathscr{L}^{2}(D)<0.

Proposition 3.1

(Theorem III.6 in
[

]
)
.

Let

3.2.
\mathscr{A}
-free fields and two-scale limits

In this subsection we include some useful tools
for the variational analysis of problems
under differential constraints.

We recall two results about
(asymptotically)
\mathscr{A}
-free fields.
The operator
\mathscr{A}
is always assumed to be of the form (
2.2
) and of constant rank.

Lemma 3.2

(
\mathscr{A}
-free decomposition, Lemma 2.15 in
[
50
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, bounded domain, and let

. Let
\set{u_{k}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

Then, there exist a subsequence
\set{u_{j_{k}}}
and

such that the following holds:

(1)

\set{v_{k}}
is bounded,
\mathscr{A}
-free, and
p
-equiintegrable;

(2)

u_{j_{k}}-v_{k}\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every

.

Lemma 3.3

(
\mathscr{A}
-free periodic extension, Lemma 2.8 in
[
46
]
)
.

Let
D\subset Q
be open, and let

p\in(1,+\infty)
.
Let
\set{u_{k}}\subset L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})

p
-equiintegrable sequence such that

weakly in
L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\mathscr{A}u_{k}\to 0
in

there exists an
\mathscr{A}
-free sequence
\set{v_{k}}\subset L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

which is
p
-equiintegrable in
Q

\displaystyle v_{k}-u_{k}\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad v_{k}\to 0\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(Q\setminus D;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

\displaystyle\int_{Q}v_{k}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y=0,\quad\lVert v_{k}\|_{L^{p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c(\mathscr{A},D)\lVert u_{k}\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\quad\text{for all }k\in\mathbb{N}.

To perform our analysis,
we need to track
how the differential constraint behaves along sequences
that converge in the sense of Definition
2.5
above.
In this respect, the notion of two-scale convergence
[
67
,
2
]
will be crucial:

Definition 3.4
.

We say that

\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

weakly two-scale converges in
L^{p}

to a function
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))

if for all
v\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;C_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
it holds

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot v\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}\int_{Q}u(x,y)\cdot v(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x.

In this case we write
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
.

We say that
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

strongly two-scale converges in
L^{p}

to
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
if
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
in
L^{p}
and

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{N}))}
.
In this case we write
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\to}}u
strongly in
L^{p}
.

For the sake of completeness,
we record in the following lines the properties of two-scale convergence
to be exploited in this work;
we refer to
[
2
,
81
]
for further reading.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence.

(1)

If
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
is weakly two-scale convergent,
then it is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
; conversely,
if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
then, it possesses a subsequence
which is weakly two-scale convergent.

(2)

If
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
weakly two-scale in
L^{p}
,
then
u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\int_{Q}u(\,\cdot\,,y)\mathrm{d}y
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(3)

If
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
weakly two-scale in
L^{p}

and
\set{v_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is another sequence
with the property that

v_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\to}}v
strongly two-scale in
L^{p}
,
then
u_{\varepsilon}v_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}uv
weakly two scale in
L^{p}
.

(4)

If
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;C_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
or
u\in C(\bar{\Omega};L^{p}_{\rm per}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, then the sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
defined as

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=u\left(x,\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\quad\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega

is
p
-equiintegrable and
u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\to}}u
strongly in
L^{p}
.

It is well-known that
two-scale convergence in
L^{p}
can be related to
L^{p}
convergence
by means of the unfolding operator.

Lemma 3.6
.

For
\varepsilon>0
, let us define the
unfolding operator

\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}(\Omega)\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
as

\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\coloneqq\tilde{v}\left(\varepsilon\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+\varepsilon y\right),

where
\tilde{v}
denotes the extension of

by

outside
\Omega
.
Then, if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a bounded sequence,
the following holds:

(1)

u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u
weakly two-scale in
L^{p}
if and only if

\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(2)

u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\to}}u
strongly two-scale in
L^{p}
if and only if

\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\to u
strongly in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Moreover, if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable,
the sequence of unfoldings
\set{\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable too in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We refer to
[
32
,
33
,
81
,
82
]

for further properties of the unfolding operator.

A characterization of
weak two-scale limits of
\mathscr{A}
-free sequences was
established by
I. Fonseca & S. Krömer
.

Proposition 3.7

(Theorem 1.2 in
[
46
]
)
.

A function
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
is the weak two-scale limit
of an
\mathscr{A}
-free sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
if and only if

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{Q}u(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}u(x,y)=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

As a last technical tool, we show that
the unfolding of an
\mathscr{A}
-free map is in turn
\mathscr{A}
-free w.r.t. the periodicity variable.
We preliminarily introduce the set

(3.1)

\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{z\in\hat{Z}_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(Q+z),\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{Z}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\varepsilon(Q+z)\subset\Omega}.

Note that
\hat{Z}_{\varepsilon}\subset Z_{\varepsilon}
, where
Z_{\varepsilon}
is the collection of indices in (
1.1
).

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
v\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
\mathscr{A}v=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.
Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
there holds

(3.2)

\mathscr{A}_{y}(\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v)=0\quad\text{in $W^{-1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{M})$ for a.e. }x\in\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}.

Moreover, if also
v=0
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
, then

\mathscr{A}_{y}(\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v)=0\quad\text{in $W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})$ for a.e. }x\in\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon})
and
\psi\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{M})
. A change of variables yields

\displaystyle\int_{\hat{\Omega}^{\varepsilon}}\int_{Q}(\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v)(x,y)\cdot\eta(x)\mathscr{A}^{*}\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{Q}v\left(\varepsilon\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor+\varepsilon y\right)\cdot\eta(x)\mathscr{A}^{*}\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}\mathrm{d}{x}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{d-1}}\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\eta(x)\int_{\varepsilon\left(Q+\left\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}x\right\rfloor\right)}v(z)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{*}_{z}\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(z)\,\mathrm{d}z\mathrm{d}x,

where
\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x}(z)\coloneqq\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}z-\left\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}x\right\rfloor)

for a.e.

.
Since
\psi\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{M})
,

\psi_{\varepsilon}^{x}
belongs to
W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}\left(\varepsilon\left(Q+\left\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}x\right\rfloor\right);\mathbb{R}^{M}\right)

and it can be regarded as an element of
W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})

by extending it to
0
outside
\varepsilon\left(Q+\left\lfloor\varepsilon^{-1}x\right\rfloor\right)
.
In this step (
3.1
) needs to be used.
The conclusion follows now from (
3.3
),
because
v
is
\mathscr{A}
-free in
\Omega
.

Assume further that
v=0
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
.
By the definition of the unfolding operator,

\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)=0
for all
(x,y)\in\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}\times D_{1}
, so that
for any
\psi\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})

\int_{Q}\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{D_{0}}\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{for all }x\in\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}.

Let now
\eta\in C^{\infty}_{c}(Q;[0,1])
be a cut-off function which is constantly

.
From (

)
we conclude that

\displaystyle\int_{Q}\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{D_{0}}\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\big(\eta(y)\psi(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{Q}\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}v(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\big(\eta(y)\psi(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y=0

for almost every

.

3.3.
Fourier analysis

The study of the class of admissible operators in Section
7
is grounded on the theory of Fourier multipliers.
For a comprehensive treatment of the matter,
we refer to the monographs
[
78
,
53
]
;
here we limit ourselves to a short recollection of useful properties.

We let
\mathscr{S}
denote the Schwartz space of rapidly decreasing functions and
for
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we let

(3.4)

\mathcal{F}u(\omega)\coloneqq\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\omega\cdot x}u(x)\,\mathrm{d}x

be its Fourier transform.
We also denote by
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
the inverse transform and
by
\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
the space of linear maps from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
We recall that a measurable function
m\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is said to be an
L^{p}
-multiplier

if the linear operator
\mathsf{T}_{m}
defined as

\mathsf{T}_{m}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(m(\mathcal{F}u)\big)\quad\text{for }u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

can be extended to a bounded operator from
L^{p}
to
L^{p}
.
Given a sufficiently smooth
m
,

S.G. Mikhlin
’s multipliers theorem provides a condition for it to be a multiplier
in terms of the decay of its derivatives.
We firstly introduce some notation:
when
i
is a
d
-dimensional multi-index,
i.e.
i\coloneqq(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,
we set
\lvert i\rvert\coloneqq\sum_{j}i_{j}
and

\partial_{i}u(x)\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{\lvert i\rvert}u}{\partial^{i_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\partial^{i_{d}}x_{d}}(x).

In the scalar case, the criterion reads:

Proposition 3.9

(Theorem 2 in the Appendix of
[
65
]
))
.

Let
m\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function of class
C^{k}
with
k>d/2
.
If there exists
c\geq 0
such that

\lvert\partial_{i}m(x)\rvert\leq\frac{c}{\lvert x\rvert^{\lvert i\rvert}}\quad\text{whenever }\lvert i\rvert\leq k,

then
m
is an
L^{p}
-multiplier.

A helpful consequence of the previous result is:

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
m\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function of class
C^{k}
with
k>d/2
.
If
m
is
0
-homogeneous,
then it is an
L^{p}
-multiplier for all
p\in(1,+\infty)
.

Multipliers stand as basics examples of pseudo-differential operators.
Their theory, in turn, can be used to characterize Sobolev spaces as follows.
Given
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
we introduce for
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
the symbol
\mathbb{S}[\omega]\coloneqq(1+\lvert\omega\rvert^{2})^{k/2}
and
the associated pseudo-differential operator
(I-\Delta)^{k/2}
:

(I-\Delta)^{k/2}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathbb{S}(\mathcal{F}u)\big).

We then say that a distribution
u
belongs to
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
if

(I-\Delta)^{k/2}u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and we endow the space with the norm

\lVert u\|_{W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\coloneqq\lVert(I-\Delta)^{k/2}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
.
It turns out that
this definition of
W^{k,p}
is equivalent
to the one given in terms of weak derivatives and that
the norms are comparable too.
The same approach shows that
the dual space
W^{-k,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
coincides with the space of distributions for which it holds

(I-\Delta)^{-k/2}u\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})

and that the norms
\lVert u\|_{W^{-k,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
and
\lVert(I-\Delta)^{-k/2}u\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
coincide.
Here, we recall that
p^{\prime}\coloneqq p/(p-1)
and

(I-\Delta)^{-k/2}
is the pseudo-differential operator defined by the symbol
(1+\lvert\omega\rvert^{2})^{-k/2}
.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2.6

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.6
,
which stands as our principal result
about the asymptotics of the energy functionals
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
in (
2.10
).
For the moment being, we assume that
the limiting behaviors of the ‘soft’ and ‘stiff’ contributions are known, i.e.,
we suppose that Propositions
2.8
and
2.9
hold true.
Their proofs are dealt with in Sections
5
and
6
below.
Our main task in the current section is then to show that
sequences with equibounded energies are precompact in a suitable sense and that
the asymptotics of the global energy
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}
is determined by that of the functionals accounting for the ‘soft’ and ‘stiff’ parts.
In this respect,
it is useful to regard the total energy
\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u)
of
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

as the sum of
{\mathscr{F}}_{0,\varepsilon}(u)
and
\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(u)
,
which were defined in (
2.11
) and (
2.12
).

The section is organized as follows.
We first address the compactness result in Proposition
4.1
,
second we show how to reduce our problem to the sub-problems regarding the ‘soft’ and the ‘stiff’ components,
then we prove the
\Gamma
-convergence statement in Theorem
2.6
.

4.1.
Compactness

The next proposition shows that
sequences which are equibounded in energy
have high-contrast converging subsequences.
This kind of convergence was introduced in Definition
2.5
.
We remark that, owing to position (
2.10
),
only
\mathscr{A}
-free fields give rise to finite energy configurations.
We prove here that
high-contrast limits of
\mathscr{A}
-free sequences inherit differential constraints.

Proposition 4.1

(High-contrast limits of
\mathscr{A}
-free sequences)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a constant rank differential operator of the form (
2.2
).
Let also
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be such that
there exists
c\geq-a\lambda\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega)
for which
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
. Then,

(1)

There exist a (non-relabeled) subsequence,
as well as two maps
u_{0}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and
u_{1}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
such that
\set{\varepsilon\chi_{0,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
u_{0}
weakly two-scale and
that
\set{\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
u_{1}
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Moreover, up to subsequences,

\set{\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}}
and
\set{\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
weakly two-scale converge in
L^{p}
, respectively,
to
u_{0}
and
to a map
v_{1}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))

that satisfy

(4.1)

u_{0}(x,y)=0\text{ if }y\in D_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{Q}v_{1}(\cdot,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=u_{1}.

(2)

The following differential constraints are fulfilled:

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{D_{0}}u_{0}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}u_{0}(x,y)=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}v_{1}(x,y)=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(D_{1};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

(3)

If

\mathscr{A}
satisfies Assumption
2
,
setting
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
,
there exists
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
such that,
up to subsequences,
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
weakly two-scale converges to
\tilde{u}
in
L^{p}
,
and there holds

(4.5)

\displaystyle v_{1}(x,y)=\chi_{D_{1}}(y)\tilde{u}(x,y)\quad\text{almost everywhere in }\Omega\times Q,

(4.6)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}u_{1}=-\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{D_{0}}\tilde{u}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}\right)\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

(4.7)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}(\chi_{0}\tilde{u})=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(Q;\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

(4)

If additionally
\mathscr{A}
satisfies Assumption
1
, then

(4.8)

\mathscr{A}u_{1}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})

and
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u_{1}
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
In particular, up to subsequences,
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
u_{1}
in the high-contrast sense.

Proof.

If
{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
,
then
{\mathscr{F}}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
and
\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c

(cf. (
2.11
)–(
2.12
)).
The growth assumptions in
H3
yield

(4.9)

\lVert\varepsilon\chi_{0,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c,\quad\lVert\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c.

(1) By Lemma
3.5
, since bounded sequences in
L^{p}
are weakly two-scale precompact,
there exist
u,u_{0},v_{1}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))

such that, up to a (non-relabeled) subsequence,

(4.10)

\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u,\quad\varepsilon\chi_{0,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}u_{0},\quad\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}v_{1}\quad\text{ weakly two-scale in }L^{p}.

For what concerns (
4.1
),
we observe that,
thanks to the relation between weak two-scale convergence and weak
L^{p}
-convergence,
we have

(4.11)

\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u_{1}\coloneqq\int_{Q}v_{1}(\,\cdot\,,y)\,\mathrm{d}y\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Further, it holds that

(4.12)

\chi_{i,\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\to}}\chi_{i}\text{ strongly two-scale in }L^{p},

whence, by the first two convergences in (
4.10
), we find

u_{0}(x,y)=\chi_{0}(y)u(x,y)\quad\text{almost everywhere in }\Omega\times Q.

In particular, also the first equality in (
4.1
) is satisfied.

By linearity,
the previous considerations also entail that

\varepsilon\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}(1-\chi_{0})u
weakly two-scale in
L^{p}
.
On the other hand, from (
4.9
) we infer that

\set{\varepsilon\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
must actually converge to
0
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and,
a fortiori
, in the strong two-scale sense.
Thus,
(1-\chi_{0})u=0
,
which implies that
u(x,y)=0
if
y\in D_{1}
.
We therefore conclude that
u=u_{0}
.

(2) Since
\mathscr{A}u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega
for all
\varepsilon>0
,
Proposition
3.7
yields immediately (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
As for (
4.4
),
given any
\psi\in W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}(D_{1};\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
we extend it to the whole
\mathbb{R}^{d}

by setting
\psi=0
in
D_{0}
and

\psi(x+z)=\psi(x)
for all
x\in Q
and
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
Fix a function
\eta\in C^{1}_{c}(\Omega)
.
Since
\mathscr{A}u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
denoting by
\mathbb{A}^{\ast}
the symbol of
\mathscr{A}^{\ast}
(see Subsection
2.2
),
we have

\begin{split}0&=\left\langle\mathscr{A}u_{\varepsilon},\varepsilon\eta(\,\cdot\,)\psi\left(\,\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon}\,\right)\right\rangle_{W^{-1,p},W^{1,p^{\prime}}_{0}}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\left(\varepsilon\mathbb{A}^{*}[\nabla\eta(x)]\psi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+\eta(x)\mathscr{A}^{*}\psi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\chi_{1,\varepsilon}(x)u_{\varepsilon}(x)\cdot\left(\varepsilon\mathbb{A}^{*}[\nabla\eta(x)]\psi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)+\eta(x)\mathscr{A}^{*}\psi\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)\right)\mathrm{d}{x},\end{split}

where the latter equality is due to the choice of the support of
\psi
. Therefore, the third convergence in (
4.10
) yields

\int_{\Omega}\eta(x)\left(\int_{Q}v_{1}(x,y)\cdot\mathscr{A}^{*}\psi(y)\mathrm{d}{y}\right)\mathrm{d}{x}=0,

which in turn implies (
4.4
).

(3) For every
\varepsilon>0
let
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
denote the
\mathscr{A}
-free extension of
u_{\varepsilon}

provided by Assumption
2
.
Owing to (
4.9
), the sequence

\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
there exists
\tilde{u}\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
such that (up to subsequences)

(4.13)

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 2}}{{\rightharpoonup}}\tilde{u}\quad\text{ weakly two-scale in }L^{p}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{Q}\tilde{u}(x,y)\mathrm{d}{y}\right)=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}).

Since Assumption
2
grants that

\chi_{1,\varepsilon}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
,
we infer (
4.5
)
from (
4.10
) and (
4.12
).
Relationship (
4.5
) in turn rewrites as

\mathscr{A}u_{1}=\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{Q}v_{1}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)=\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{D_{1}}\tilde{u}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\right)=-\mathscr{A}_{x}\left(\int_{D_{0}}\tilde{u}(x,y)\mathrm{d}y\right),

i.e., (
4.6
) is proved.
Finally, to obtain (
4.7
),
we combine the fact that

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}+\chi_{0,\varepsilon}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}

almost everywhere in
\Omega

with the assumption that

\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
for every
\varepsilon>0
.
Using again Proposition
3.7
, as well as (
4.4
) and (
4.10
) – (
4.13
), we complete the proof of the third statement.

(4) By (
4.7
) and Assumption
1
, we deduce that
\int_{D_{0}}\tilde{u}(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}{y}=0
for almost every
x\in\Omega
. Then, by (
4.5
),

\mathscr{A}u_{1}=0
in
\Omega
.
Besides, in view of its weak two-scale convergence,

\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
converges weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
to

\int_{Q}\tilde{u}(\,\cdot\,,y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{Q}\chi_{D_{1}}(y)\tilde{u}(\,\cdot\,,y)\mathrm{d}y=\int_{Q}v_{1}(\,\cdot\,,y)\mathrm{d}y=u_{1}(\,\cdot\,),

where we used (
4.5
) and (
4.1
).
This also shows that
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
admits an extension on the ‘soft’ part
that converges to
u_{1}
weakly in
L^{p}
.
Therefore, to grant that there is a subsequence of
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}

that converges to
u_{1}
in the high-contrast sense,
we are only left to observe that
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\int_{Q}u_{0}(\,\cdot\,,y)\,\mathrm{d}y

and that such average vanishes because of (
4.1
), (
4.3
), and Assumption
1
.
The proof is now concluded.
∎

As already anticipated in Remark

,
we note that the preservation of
p
-equiintegrability granted by Assumption
2

is not needed to prove item (3) of the previous proposition.
We will instead resort to it to establish Proposition

below.

4.2.
Splitting

In the light of the of previous subsection,
we know that those sequences which are equibounded in energy
are precompact with respect to the high-contrast convergence, and that
their cluster points fulfill suitable differential constraints.
Following the approach of
M. Cherdantsev & K. D. Cherednichenko

[
25
]
,
the next step is to show that
the asymptotic behavior of the energy
along such sequences
coincides with the sum of those of the energies
of two decoupled systems,
one sitting on the ‘soft’ inclusions,
the other on the ‘stiff’ matrix.

To favor intuition,
let us consider a family
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
for some
c\geq-a\lambda\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega)
,
and assume that the operator
\mathscr{A}
is admissible in the sense of Definition
2.3
.
As Proposition
4.1
proves, the growth condition
H3
on the energy densities entails

\lVert\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c
.
Hence,
by exploiting Assumption
2
,
we retrieve the functions
\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that for all
\varepsilon

(1)

\chi_{1,\varepsilon}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}
almost everywhere in
\Omega
,

(2)

\lVert\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c
,

(3)

\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega
,

and also that

(4)

\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable if so is
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
.

Setting
v_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
,
we rewrite

{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})={\mathscr{F}}_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})+\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})+{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

where
{\mathscr{F}}_{0,\varepsilon}
and
\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}
are as in (
2.11
)–(
2.12
) and

(4.14)

{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\coloneqq\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left[{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\right)-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x.

(notice that
v_{\varepsilon}(x)=0
when
x\in\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
).
In the lemma below
we show that
the error made by substituting
{\mathscr{F}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
by the sum
{\mathscr{F}}_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})+\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})
, i.e.
{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
,
is asymptotically negligible
whenever high-contrast convergence holds.

Lemma 4.2

(Splitting)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a constant rank differential operator of the form (
2.2
).
Let also
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be the high-contrast limit of a family
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Explicitly, assume that
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and that
there is a family
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the properties that
\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
,

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

u_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
.
If
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
for some
c\geq-a\lambda_{0}\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega)
,
and if
v_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
, then
the following hold:

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}u=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }\Omega_{1,\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}v_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

\displaystyle\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})+\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})+\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

As a consequence of the fact that
\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega
for all
\varepsilon
, also the weak limit of
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
, i.e.
u
, must be
\mathscr{A}
-free.
As immediate consequences of the definition, we also find that

v_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
and that
\mathscr{A}v_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega
.
Being
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, we also deduce

\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup 0\quad\text{weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Only the estimates involving the semilimits are now left to prove.
It suffices to show that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=0
, with
\mathscr{E}_{\varepsilon}
as in (
4.14
).
To this aim, we observe that in view of
H4
, for almost every
x
we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\right)-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}\right)\right\rvert\leq\mu\left(1+\lvert\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{p-1}+\lvert\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rvert^{p-1}\right)\lvert\varepsilon\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rvert.

Hölder’s Inequality yields

\lvert{\mathscr{E}}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\rvert\leq c\varepsilon\lVert\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})},

and the conclusion is achieved
by exploiting again the boundedness of
\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
∎

We conclude this section
by showing that,
thanks to the
\mathscr{A}
-free decomposition procedure in Lemma
3.2
,
we can always reduce to the case in which
the sequence
\set{v_{\varepsilon}}
on the left-hand side of (
4.15
) is
p
-equiintegrable.
This motivates the
\Gamma
-liminif inequality
contained in Proposition
5.3
.
We premise a lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\set{f_{0,\varepsilon}}
and
f_{1}
satisfy hypotheses
H1
,
H3
, and
H4
.
Let also
\set{u_{\varepsilon}},\set{v_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be bounded sequences such that

u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in measure, and
that
\set{v_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable.
Then,

(4.16)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left[{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(u_{\varepsilon}\right)-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(v_{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x\geq 0,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}}\left[{f}_{1}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},u_{\varepsilon}\right)-{f}_{1}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon}\right)\right]\mathrm{d}x\geq 0.

Proof.

Despite the dependence of the energy density
f_{1}

on the oscillating variable,
up to a different notational realization,
the ‘stiff’ case is completely analogous to the ‘soft’ one.
For this reason, we
detail the proof of (
4.16
) only

We first note that
(
4.16
) is left unchanged
if we replace
{f}_{0,\varepsilon}
with
{f}_{0,\varepsilon}-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(0)
, and hence
we may assume that
{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(0)=0
. Upon extraction of a (non-relabeled) subsequence, we may also suppose that
the left-hand side in (
4.16
) is a limit.
In view of
[
48
, Lemma 8.13]
,
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
we can decompose the elements of such subsequence as
u_{\varepsilon}=u^{o}_{\varepsilon}+u^{c}_{\varepsilon}
,
where
\set{u^{o}_{\varepsilon}}
(the ‘oscillating’ part) is
p
-equiintegrable and
\set{u_{\varepsilon}^{c}}
(the ‘concentrating’ part) converges to zero in measure.
If we let
R_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\set{x\in\Omega:u^{o}_{\varepsilon}\neq u_{\varepsilon}}
,
we have that
\mathscr{L}^{d}(R_{\varepsilon})\to 0
, whence, by the current assumptions,

\set{u^{o}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable and converges to zero in measure.
Thanks to Vitali’s convergence theorem (see e.g.
[
48
, Theorem 2.24]
),
it follows that
u^{o}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}\to 0

strongly
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and,
in view of
H4
, we deduce

(4.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left|{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon})-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\right|\mathrm{d}x=0.

By employing the definition of
R_{\varepsilon}
,
H3
, and
H4
,
we find the estimate

\left|\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left[{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon})+{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{c}_{\varepsilon})\right]\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
=\left|\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon}+u^{c}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\left[{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon})+{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{c}_{\varepsilon})\right]\mathrm{d}x\right|\\
\leq\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\left|{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon}+u^{c}_{\varepsilon})-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{c}_{\varepsilon})\right|\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\left|{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon})\right|\mathrm{d}x\\
\leq\mu\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\big(1+|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}+|u^{c}_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}\big)|u^{o}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x+\Lambda\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\big(1+|u^{o}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\big)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Since
\set{u^{o}_{\varepsilon}}
and
\set{u^{c}_{\varepsilon}}
are bounded in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\set{u^{o}_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable,
we infer that the last term tends to zero as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Therefore, we see that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left[f_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})-f_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}\left[f_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{o}_{\varepsilon})+f_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{c}_{\varepsilon})-f_{0,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})\right]\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}f_{0,\varepsilon}(u^{c}_{\varepsilon})\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\cap R_{\varepsilon}}\lambda\big(-a+|u^{c}_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\big)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\geq 0,

where the second equality is a consequence of (
4.17
),
the second to last inequality is due to
H3
,
and the last one follows from
\mathscr{L}^{d}(R_{\varepsilon})\to 0
.
∎

The improved variant of Lemma
4.2
for the liminf inequality in (
4.15
) reads as follows.

Proposition 4.4

(Refined splitting for the liminf)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be an admissible differential operator.
Let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and consider two sequences
\set{u_{\varepsilon}},\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the property that

\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
,

\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and

u_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
.
If
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq c
for some
c\geq-a\lambda_{0}\mathscr{L}^{d}(\Omega)
,
then there exists
\set{\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that the following hold:

\displaystyle\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }\Omega_{1,\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}),

\displaystyle\set{\varepsilon\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}}\text{ is $p$-equiintegrable,}

(4.18)

\displaystyle\varepsilon\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0\text{ weakly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

(4.19)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon})+\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}),.

Proof.

The equiboundedness in energy entails that

(4.20)

\lVert\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert\chi_{1,\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c.

By Lemma
3.2
,
there exist a (not relabeled) subsequence of
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
and a sequence
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

(1)

\set{\varepsilon w_{\varepsilon}}
is bounded,
\mathscr{A}
-free, and
p
-equiintegrable;

(2)

\varepsilon(w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for every
q\in[1,p)
;

(3)

\varepsilon\chi_{1,\varepsilon}(w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})\to 0
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Note that the last property is not mentioned explicitly in the statement of Lemma
3.2
,
but it is a byproduct of the construction in its proof (see
[
50
, Lemma 2.15]
).

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define
\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}w_{\varepsilon}
,
with
\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}
as in Assumption
2
.
We then set
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq w_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}
.
In view of Assumption
2
,
we immediately obtain that

\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
vanishes on
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
, that it is
\mathscr{A}
-free in
\Omega
, and that
\set{\varepsilon\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable.
To prove (
4.18
), we consider the identity

(4.21)

\varepsilon\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon(w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon(\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}).

Thanks to (2) above,
\varepsilon(w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})\to 0
strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Additionally, by Assumption
2
,

\varepsilon\lVert\tilde{w}_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\varepsilon\lVert w_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\varepsilon\lVert w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}+c\varepsilon\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\to 0,

the convergence to
0
following from (3) above and (
4.20
).
In view of the assumptions on
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
and of (2), (
4.18
) is inferred.

To complete the proof of the corollary, it suffices now show that for
q\in[1,p)
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\varepsilon(\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\to 0\quad\text{ strongly in }L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
v_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq u_{\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}
.
Indeed, once this is proven, (
4.19
) is deduced from Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
.
To prove (
4.22
),
we notice that, by the definitions of
v_{\varepsilon}
and
\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}
, as well as by Hölder’s inequality,
for every
q\in[1,p)
it holds

\lVert\varepsilon(\tilde{v}_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\varepsilon\lVert w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}+c\varepsilon\lVert\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}(w_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Then, in view of (2), of Assumption
2
, and of (3), we deduce (
4.22
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

.

.

be fixed and assume that

\set{u_{\varepsilon}},\set{\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the property that

\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in

\mathscr{A}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}=0
in
\Omega

For

2.8
and

,

5.
Asymptotics for the soft component

This section is devoted to the proof of Proposition
2.8
.
We address the asymptotic analysis of the energy stored in the ‘soft’ component of the system, that is,
\set{\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}}
in (
2.11
),
and we prove that the limiting behavior is encoded by the functional
\mathscr{F}_{0}
in (
2.13
).
We recall that
\mathscr{F}_{0}
is finite only when
u=0

and in that case the value of the functional equals the constant
\alpha_{0}

given by

\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\sup_{\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega}\inf_{u\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

where the supremum is meant to run over all open sets that are compactly contained in
\Omega
,
and, for any open
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
,
U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
is as in (
2.5
).

Remark 5.1

(On the constant
\alpha_{0}
)
.

By the definition of
U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
in (
2.5
), we see that

(5.1)

U_{0}(\Omega)\subset U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}_{2})\subset U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}_{1})\quad\text{whenever }\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{2}\subset\Omega.

Since
\lambda(-a+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p})\leq f_{0}(\xi)
for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
from (
5.1
) we deduce that

\alpha_{0}\leq\inf_{u\in U_{0}}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x,

and, more generally, that

(5.2)

\inf_{u\in U_{0}\in(\Omega^{\prime}_{1})}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{1}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\inf_{u\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}_{2})}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{2}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

when
\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{2}\subset\Omega
.
In particular, the supremum in the definition of
\alpha_{0}
is not a maximum, in general.
Indeed, suppose that
\Omega^{\prime}_{1}\Subset\Omega
achieves such supremum.
Then, we would get a contradiction whenever there exists
\Omega^{\prime}_{2}\Subset\Omega
containing
\Omega^{\prime}_{1}

such that the inequality in (
5.2
) is strict.

We separate the discussion of the
\Gamma
-liminf and of the
\Gamma
-limsup inequalities.

5.1.
\Gamma
-liminf inequality

The proof of the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality relies
on the compactness and splitting results of Section
4
,
on unfolding techniques, as well as on the following intermediate statement.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\set{{f}_{0,\varepsilon}}
satisfy
H1
and
H3
–
H5
.
Suppose that
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
is a
p
-equiintegrable family satisfying the following properties:

(5.3)

\displaystyle w_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ if }y\in D_{1},

and for all open
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
there exists
\varepsilon^{\prime}\coloneqq\varepsilon^{\prime}(\Omega^{\prime})
such that

(5.4)

\displaystyle\mathscr{A}_{y}w_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}\text{ if }\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime}.

Then, it holds

(5.5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

Proof.

The proof is divided in two steps.

Step 1: a simplified setting.
We start by establishing an inequality for the case in which
the densities on the right-hand side do not depend on
\varepsilon
(cf.
[
25
, Lemma 20]
), that is, we first prove that

(5.6)

\alpha_{0}\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

According to the definition of
\alpha_{0}
and to Remark
5.1
,
for an arbitrary
\delta>0
, there is an open set
\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}\Subset\Omega
such that

\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega,\partial\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta})<\delta
and

\alpha_{0}\leq\inf_{u\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta})}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(u(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta.

In view of (
5.3
) and (
5.4
),

w_{\varepsilon}\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta})
for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small, whence

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta

\displaystyle=\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime}_{\delta}}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta.

Thanks to the
p
-equiintegrability of
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}
,
the conclusion (
5.6
) now follows by letting
\delta
vanish.

Step 2: the general case.

We adapt the argument in the proof of
[
38
, Thm. 5.14]
,
relying on the almost everywhere convergence of
\set{{f}_{0,\varepsilon}}
to
{f}_{0}
, as well as on the
p
-Lipschitz continuity of these energy densities (see
H4
and
H5
).

Fix
\delta>0
. By the
p
-equiintegrability of
\set{w_{\varepsilon}}
and by the
p
-growth assumptions on
f_{0}
, there exists
r>0
such that

(5.7)

\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\int_{\set{(x,y)\in\Omega\times D_{0}:\lvert w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\rvert>r}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\leq\delta.

Besides, since
f_{0,\varepsilon}
and
f_{0}
are
p
-Lipschitz, we find
\rho>0
such that

(5.8)

|f_{0}(\xi)-f_{0}(\eta)|+\sup_{\varepsilon>0}|f_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi)-f_{0,\varepsilon}(\eta)|\leq\delta\quad\text{for every }\xi,\eta\in B(0,\rho).

Let now
\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{K}\in B(0,r)
be such that

(5.9)

\overline{B(0,r)}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{K}B\left(\xi_{k},\rho\right).

In view of
H5
, for any such
\xi_{k}

there exist
\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}>0

such that

\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi_{k})-{f}_{0}(\xi_{k})\rvert\leq\delta

if
\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}_{k}
.
Letting

\bar{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\min\set{\bar{\varepsilon}_{1},\dots,\bar{\varepsilon}_{K}}
,
it follows that
for any
k=1,\dots,K

(5.10)

\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi_{k})-{f}_{0}(\xi_{k})\rvert\leq\delta\quad\text{if $\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}$.}

By (
5.9
),
for every
\eta\in\overline{B(0,r)}

there exists
k\in\{1,\dots,K\}
such that
\eta\in B(\xi_{k},\rho)
.
For this particular
k
,
the combination of the triangle inequality, (
5.8
), and (
5.10
) yields

(5.11)

\displaystyle\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\eta)-{f}_{0}(\eta)\rvert\leq\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\eta)-{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi_{k})\rvert+\lvert{f}_{0,\varepsilon}(\xi_{k})-{f}_{0}(\xi_{k})\rvert+\lvert{f}_{0}(\eta)-{f}_{0}(\xi_{k})\rvert\leq 3\delta,

for every
\eta\in\overline{B(0,r)}
and every
\varepsilon<\bar{\varepsilon}
.

In view of (
5.6
) we deduce

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\set{(x,y)\in\Omega\times D_{0}:\lvert w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\rvert\leq r}}f_{0}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\delta

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}f_{0,\varepsilon}\big(w_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x+3\delta\mathscr{L}^{2d}(\Omega\times D_{0})+\delta,

where the first inequality is due to (
5.7
), and the second one to (
5.11
).
The arbitrariness of
\delta>0
yields the conclusion.
∎

We are now ready to tackle the
\Gamma
-liminf inequality for the ‘soft’ contribution;
the argument is comparable to the one in
[
25
, Lemma 21]
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\set{{f}_{0,\varepsilon}}_{\varepsilon}
satisfy assumptions
H1
and
H3
–
H5
, and
let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Then, for every
p
-equiintegrable sequence
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
u_{\varepsilon}=0
on
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
,

\mathscr{A}u_{\varepsilon}=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
and
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup u
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
we have

\mathscr{F}_{0}(u)\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}).

Proof.

If the lower limit of
\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is not finite,
then there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise, without loss of generality,
we focus on the case in which the lower limit of
\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})
is finite, and
we assume that it is a limit.
Then, it follows by Proposition
4.1
and Assumption
1
that necessarily
u=0
.
Therefore, we are left to prove that

\alpha_{0}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}}f_{0,\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}(x)\big)\mathrm{d}{x},

where
\alpha_{0}
is given by (
2.8
) and
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup 0
weakly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We exhibit a formula for
\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})

that involves the unfolding operator introduced in Lemma
3.6
.
We let
\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}
be the unfolded map of
\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}
:

\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\coloneqq\varepsilon\mathsf{S}_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}(x,y),

whence

(5.12)

\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)=0\quad\text{for almost every }x\in\Omega\text{ and }y\in D_{1}.

Also, since the unfolding procedure preserves
p
-equiintegrability,

\set{\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega\times Q;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
p
-equiintegrable.
Finally, Lemma
3.8
grants that

\mathscr{A}_{y}\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon},

where
\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
is as in (
3.1
).
In particular, if
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
is an open set such that
the distance
\delta\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)
is strictly positive,
it is clear that
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
if
\sqrt{d}\varepsilon<\delta
.
Thus, for all open
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega

(5.13)

\mathscr{A}_{y}\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}\text{ if }\varepsilon<\varepsilon^{\prime},

where
\varepsilon^{\prime}>0
is a suitable threshold depending on
\Omega^{\prime}
.

By the definition of
\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}
(see (
1.1
)),
we have

\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{z\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\Big(\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon(y+z)\big)\Big)\mathrm{d}y.

Being
z
an integer-valued vector,
it holds
\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon z,y)=\varepsilon u_{\varepsilon}\big(\varepsilon(y+z)\big)
for every
z\in Z_{\varepsilon}
and
y\in D_{0}
. Hence, we obtain

\begin{split}\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})&=\varepsilon^{d}\sum_{z\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\big(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(\varepsilon z,y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&=\sum_{z\in Z_{\varepsilon}}\int_{\varepsilon(Q+z)}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon\left\lfloor\frac{x}{\varepsilon}\right\rfloor,y\right)\right)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\\
&\geq\int_{\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x\end{split}

because for all
x\in\varepsilon(Q+z)
,
\lfloor x/\varepsilon\rfloor=z
.
We can rewrite the last estimate as follows:

\mathscr{F}_{0,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\geq\int_{\Omega}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\left(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\right)\,\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega\setminus\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}\int_{D_{0}}{f}_{0,\varepsilon}\big(\hat{u}_{\varepsilon}(x,y)\big)\mathrm{d}y\,\mathrm{d}x.

From this, owing to (
5.12
) and (
5.13

6.

In this section, we characterize the limiting behavior of the family

We rely on the contribution by

about homogenization of multiple integrals under differential constraints:

Theorem 6.1

(Theorem 1.1 in
[

]
)
.

Let

-periodic with respect to its first argument.
Assume that
there exist

,

, and

satisfying

-c\leq g(y,\xi)\leq\Lambda(1+\lvert\xi\rvert^{p})\quad\text{for a.e. $y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ and all $\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$}.

Then, for all

such that
\mathscr{A}u=0

the functional

\Gamma
-converges with respect to the weak

-convergence to

g_{\mathrm{hom}}(\xi)\coloneqq\liminf_{k\to+\infty}\inf\Bigg\{\int_{Q}g\big(ky,\xi+v(y)\big)\,\mathrm{d}y:v\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})\\
\text{ with }\int_{Q}v(z)\mathrm{d}z=0\text{ and }\mathscr{A}v=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\Bigg\}.

As a corollary of the theorem above,
we derive the asymptotics of
\set{\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}}
.

given by Theorem
6.1
.
Thanks to Lemma
3.2
,
we get a
p
-equiintegrable family
\set{v_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\set{u_{\varepsilon}-v_{\varepsilon}}
converges to
0

strongly in
L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
q\in[1,p)
.
Therefore,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}\setminus\tilde{\Omega}_{1,\varepsilon}}\lvert v_{\varepsilon}(x)\rvert^{p}\mathrm{d}x=0.

Let now
\set{\varepsilon_{n}}
be such that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{\varepsilon_{n}}).

By Lemma
4.3
,
we obtain the desired upper limit inequality:

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathscr{F}_{1,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon})=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon_{n}}(v_{\varepsilon_{n}})\\
\leq\lim_{n\to+\infty}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon_{n}}(u_{\varepsilon_{n}})\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tilde{\mathscr{F}}_{1,\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\leq\mathscr{F}_{1}(u).

∎

7.
Admissible differential constraints

In what follows we deepen our analysis of constant rank operators.
We investigate the existence of Sobolev potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free maps and of extension operators on perforated domains. In particular, we prove Theorems
2.10
and
2.11
.

In contrast to the previous sections,
here we take into account also differential operators of order greater than
1
:
we treat the broader class
of linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operators with constant coefficients.
To fix the notation, given
k,N,M\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{0}
,
we consider linear maps
A^{(i)}\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}

for all
d
-dimensional multi-indices
i
with
\lvert i\rvert=k
and
we focus on the operators
whose action on a function
u\colon\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{M}

is given by

\mathscr{A}u\coloneqq\sum_{\lvert i\rvert=k}A^{(i)}\partial_{i}u.

Recall that if
i\coloneqq(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
is a multi-index, then

\partial_{i}u(x)\coloneqq\frac{\partial^{\lvert i\rvert}u}{\partial_{i_{1}}x_{1}\cdots\partial_{i_{d}}x_{d}}(x).

We keep in place the constant rank condition,
which currently reads:
there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}

such that the rank of
\mathbb{A}[\omega]
equals
r
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
,
where

\mathbb{A}[\omega]\coloneqq\sum_{\lvert i\rvert=k}\omega^{i}A^{(i)}

is the symbol of
\mathscr{A}

and
\omega^{i}=\Pi_{j=1}^{d}\omega_{j}^{i_{j}}

if
i=(i_{1},\dots,i_{d})
is a multi-index.
Note that when
k=1
we recover the setting of Subsection
2.2
. As before, we say that a map
u
is
\mathscr{A}
-free in
\Omega
if
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
W^{-k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.

7.1.
Existence of potentials

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
2.10
.
Loosely speaking, our goal is to prove that,
given an operator
\mathscr{A}
of constant rank,
there exists a second differential operator
\mathscr{B}

such that any
\mathscr{A}
-free map
u\in L^{p}

satisfies
u=\mathscr{B}w
for a suitable Sobolev function
w
.
When such a relation holds, we will say for brevity
that
w
is a
potential
for
u
and that
\mathscr{B}
is a
potential
for
\mathscr{A}
.
We are able to prove existence of potentials
for those functions that are
\mathscr{A}
-free
on a certain class of subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, which we introduce in the next definition.

Definition 7.1
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a homogeneous operator of order
k
. An open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain

if there exist
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

and
c>0
such that the following holds:
for all
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
W^{-k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
, we have

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
a.e. in
\Omega
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and

(3)

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

Analogous definitions concerning extensions of sequences that are (asimptotically)
\mathscr{A}
-free
are found in
[
47
, Definition 1.4]
and
[
46
, Lemma 2.8]
(i.e. Lemma
3.3
above), while the reader is referred to Section
7.2
below
for a more detailed discussion on extension problems. We also refer to
[
57
]
for a related study in the stochastic setting.
It is important to notice that
the property of being an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain carries some topological consequences.

Remark 7.2

(On the topology of
\mathscr{A}
-extension domains)
.

The requirements of Definition
7.1
may act as implicit restrictions on the topology of the set
\Omega
, according to the specific choice of
\mathscr{A}
.
Let us consider, for instance, the case in which
d=3
,
\mathscr{A}
=curl, and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\mathbb{R}e_{3}
,
\set{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}}
being the canonical basis in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
We assume by contradiction that
\Omega
is an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain.
Then, if for every
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega
we let

u(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\coloneqq\left(-\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},\frac{x_{1}}{x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}},0\right),

we find that
{\rm curl}\,u=0
almost everywhere in
\Omega
,
whence
{\rm curl}\,(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, if
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}
is the extension operator in Definition
7.1
for
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{curl}
. This fact, in turn, yields a potential
w
such that
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u=\nabla w
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and, from the requirement that
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
on
\Omega
, we conclude that
u=\nabla w
on
\Omega
. This leads to a contradiction, for the field
u
is not conservative.

For the proof of Theorem
2.10

we rely heavily on some recent contributions
by
B. Raiţă
and coauthors,
who established existence of potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free smooth maps and
Korn-type inequalities for constant rank operators.
Further related bibliographical comments are provided
when we discuss Propositions
7.4
and
7.5
below.
Before dealing with the proof, we pinpoint here that
Theorem
2.10
constitutes a sufficient condition for Assumption
1
to hold:

Corollary 7.3
.

Assume that

\mathscr{A}
is a first order operator satisfying the assumptions of Theorem
2.10

and that the unit cube
Q
is an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain.
Then, for any open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
, the space
U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
in (
2.5
) is characterized as follows:

(7.1)

U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})=\Bigg\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})):u=0\text{ if }y\in D_{1},\\
\mathscr{A}_{y}u=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime},\text{ and }\int_{Q}u(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=0\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}\Bigg\}.

Proof.

For the desired equality to hold,
we just need to prove that

U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})\subset\Bigg\{u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})):u=0\text{ if }y\in D_{1},\\
\mathscr{A}_{y}u=0\text{ in }W^{-1,p}(\mathbb{T}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime},\text{ and }\int_{Q}u(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=0\text{ for a.e. }x\in\Omega^{\prime}\Bigg\}.

Let us fix
u\in U_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})
.
Theorem
2.10
grants that
for a.e.
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
there exists
w_{x}\in W^{\ell,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})

such that
u(x,\cdot)=\mathscr{B}_{y}w_{x}
a.e. in
Q
.
To the aim of defining a measurable selection of the maps
\set{w_{x}}_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}
,
we exploit Proposition
3.1
.

We first observe that
for every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}))
and
x\in\Omega^{\prime}

the set

W(x)\coloneqq\Set{w\in W^{\ell,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M}):\mathscr{B}_{y}w(y)=u(x,y)\text{ for a.e. }y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}

is either empty or a closed (and hence complete) subspace of
W^{\ell,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
.
Besides, being
x\mapsto u(x,\,\cdot\,)\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
measurable, then for every open set
O\subset W^{\ell,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
the set

\Set{x\in\Omega^{\prime}:W(x)\cap O\neq\emptyset}

is measurable as well.
It follows that the multifunction
x\mapsto W(x)
admits a measurable selection,
which we denote by
w(x,\,\cdot\,)
.

Summing up,
we recovered a measurable function
w

from
\Omega^{\prime}
to
W^{\ell,p}_{\mathrm{per}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})

with the property that
u=\mathscr{B}_{y}w
for almost every
x\in\Omega^{\prime}
and
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We conclude that

\int_{Q}u(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{\lvert i\rvert=\ell}B^{(i)}\int_{Q}\partial_{y^{i}}w(x,y)\,\mathrm{d}y=0,

where the latter inequality follows from the periodicity of
w
in its second variable.
∎

We devote the remainder of the section
to the proof of Theorem
2.10

and to the pertaining tools.
We first highlight that

B. Raiţă

[
70
]
has recently pointed out that
the kernel of the symbol of
any
constant rank operator
coincides with the image of the symbol of a suitable operator
\mathscr{B}
, and that
the latter is actually a potential for
\mathscr{A}

when tools from Fourier analysis are applicable.
Precisely, we have:

Proposition 7.4

(Theorem 1 and Lemma 2 in
[
70
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear homogeneous differential operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with constant coefficients.
Then,
\mathscr{A}
is of constant rank
if and only if
there exists a linear homogeneous differential operator
\mathscr{B}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}

with constant coefficients such that

(7.2)

\ker\mathbb{A}[\omega]=\im\mathbb{B}[\omega]\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0},

where
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
are
the symbols of
\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
, respectively.

Moreover,
suppose that

\mathscr{A}
and
\mathscr{B}
are, respectively, operators from
\mathbb{R}^{N}
to
\mathbb{R}^{M}

and from
\mathbb{R}^{M}
to
\mathbb{R}^{N}
.
If (
7.2
) holds,
then, for all
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
,
there exists
w\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})

such that
u=\mathscr{B}w
.

Observe that if the operator
\mathscr{B}
satisfies (
7.2
),
then it is in turn of constant rank;
precisely,
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathbb{B}[\omega]=N-r
if
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathbb{A}[\omega]=r
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
.
Proposition
7.4
builds a unified framework
that encompasses some well-studied special cases,
such as the one of the curl and of the divergence.
Results in the same spirit had been previously obtained by
J. Van Schaftingen

[
79
, Proposition 4.2]
for elliptic operators,
which correspond to the subclass of operators whose symbol is injective.

A second relevant finding related to constant rank operators was obtained
by
A. Guerra & B. Raiţă

[
54
]
,
who showed that this class is exactly the one
in which a sort of Korn inequality holds.
To state their result,
we need to introduce the
projection

\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}

associated with the operator
\mathscr{A}
.
For
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
it is defined as

(7.3)

\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u(x)\coloneqq\big(\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathcal{F}u)\big)(x),

where
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{F}^{-1}
are the Fourier transform and its inverse (see (
3.4
)), and
the map

\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}\to\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{N})

associates to each
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
the orthogonal projection operator onto
\ker\mathbb{A}[\omega]
.
Here and in the rest of the paper,

\mathrm{Lin}(V;W)
is the set of linear operators
from the vector space
V
to the vector space
W
.
We have:

Proposition 7.5

(Korn-type inequality for constant rank operators
[
54
]
)
.

Let
p\in(1,\infty)

and let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients.
Then,
\mathscr{A}
is of constant rank if and only if
there exists
c\coloneqq c(d,p)
such that

\lVert\nabla^{k}(\phi-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}\phi)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{k}})}\leq c\lVert\mathscr{A}\phi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})}\quad\text{for all }\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

For future use, we remark that
the conclusion remains valid when
\phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
as the Fourier analysis
on which the proof is based
is still viable in this case,
or even when
\phi\in W^{k,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, by approximation.
The sufficiency of the constant rank condition had been already observed in the literature,
see for instance the bibliography in
[
54
]
or the paper by
D. Gustafson

[
56
]
,
where a proof for
first order operators is given.

To the purpose of constructing Sobolev potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free fields,
we first need to extend the projection operator
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}
in (
7.3
) to nonsmooth maps.
We invoke the following result by
T. Kato

[
58
]
,
which we present in a form due to
F. Murat
:

Lemma 7.6

(Lemma 3.7 in
[
66
]
)
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients and constant rank.
Then, the operator
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}
in (
7.3
) can be extended
to a bounded linear operator from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

In the proof of the lemma
the constant rank assumption is fundamental.
Indeed, it ensures that
the map
\omega\mapsto\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}[\omega]
introduced above
is analytic on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
(see
[
66
]
);
this, in combination with the
0
-homogeneity of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
,
allows the use of
S.G. Mikhlin
’s multipliers theorem
(cf. Corollary
3.10
).

Remark 7.7

(Self-adjointness of the projection operator)
.

As a consequence of Parseval’s formula and
of the self-adjointness of
\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}
,
by density there holds

(7.4)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u\cdot v\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\cdot\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}v\,\mathrm{d}{x}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

We next clarify why
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}
is entitled to be named projection:
Lemma
7.9
below shows that
for an
L^{p}
-function
u
one has that

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
, and that
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
if
\mathscr{A}u=0
.
We premise the instrumental notion of
Moore-Penrose generalized inverse

\Lambda^{\dagger}

of a linear map
\Lambda
.

Lemma 7.8

(Properties of the generalized inverse
[
54
,
23
]
)
.

Given the map
\Lambda\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{M})
,
we let
\Lambda^{\dagger}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathbb{R}^{N})

be defined as

\Lambda^{\dagger}\coloneqq\left(\Lambda\lfloor_{(\ker\Lambda)^{\perp}}\right)^{-1}\circ\mathbb{P}_{\im\Lambda},

where
(\ker\Lambda)^{\perp}=\im\Lambda^{\ast}
is the orthogonal complement of the kernel of
\Lambda
and

\mathbb{P}_{\im\Lambda}
is the orthogonal projection on the image of
\Lambda
.
Then, the following hold:

(1)

\Lambda^{\dagger}
is the unique element
in
\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{M};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\Lambda^{\dagger}\circ\Lambda=\mathbb{P}_{\im\Lambda^{\ast}}
and

\Lambda\circ\Lambda^{\dagger}=\mathbb{P}_{\im\Lambda}
.

(2)

Let
\mathbb{A}\colon O\to\mathrm{Lin}(\mathbb{R}^{N};\mathbb{R}^{M})
be a smooth map
on the open set
O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
If
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathbb{A}[\omega]
is constant for all
\omega\in O
,
then the map
O\ni\omega\mapsto(\mathbb{A}[\omega])^{\dagger}
is locally bounded and smooth.

(3)

If
\mathscr{A}
is a linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients,
then the map
\omega\mapsto(\mathbb{A}[\omega])^{\dagger}
is
(-k)
-homogeneous.

We can now characterize the properties of the projection
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}
.
Note that they are comparable
to the ones presented by
I. Fonseca & S. Müller

[
50
, Lemma 2.14]

for the periodic setting. We also refer to
[
62
, Subsection 2.8]
for an alternative projection operator on the unit torus for which no null-average conditions are imposed.

Lemma 7.9
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients and constant rank.
For every
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, there holds:

(1)

For all
\psi\in C_{c}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})
, we have

\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi)=0
and,
as a consequence,

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
.

(2)

If
h=0
and
u\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, or
if
h=1,\dots,k-1
and
u\in W^{h,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
there exists a constant
c\coloneqq c(p,h)>0
such that

(7.5)

\lVert\nabla^{h}(u-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{h}})}\leq c\lVert\mathscr{A}u\|_{W^{-(k-h),p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

In particular, when
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
then
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
a.e.

Proof.

When
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
the facts
that
\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
and
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
if
\mathscr{A}u=0

are simple consequences of (
7.3
).
To extend these properties to the case of a nonsmooth
u
,
we regard

\psi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
as a Schwartz function on the whole space and
we find

\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}(\mathbb{A}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}\psi)\big)=0,

because the image of
\mathbb{A}^{\ast}[\omega]
is orthogonal to
\ker\mathbb{A}[\omega]
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
It follows by (
7.4
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u\cdot\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi\,\mathrm{d}{x}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}u\cdot\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}\mathscr{A}^{\ast}\psi\,\mathrm{d}{x}=0

and statement (1) holds.

We now turn to point (2). We argue for
h=0
as the case
h>0
is analogous.
We consider at first
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
we compute the Fourier transform of
u-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u
.
Setting
\hat{u}=\mathcal{F}u
, we find

\hat{u}[\omega]-\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}[\omega]\hat{u}[\omega]=\mathbb{P}_{\im\mathbb{A}[\omega]^{\ast}}\hat{u}[\omega]=\mathbb{A}[\omega]^{\dagger}\big(\mathbb{A}[\omega]\hat{u}[\omega]\big)\quad\text{for all }\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where in the latter inequality we exploited statement (1) in Lemma
7.8
.

Since
\mathbb{A}
is
k
-homogeneous and
\mathbb{A}^{\dagger}
is
(-k)
-homogeneous,
we infer by Corollary
3.10
that
\mathbb{A}^{\dagger}\mathbb{A}
is an
L^{p}
-Fourier multiplier.
Therefore, the operator
\mathsf{T}

defined for
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
as
\mathsf{T}u\coloneqq\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\hat{u}-\mathbb{P}_{\mathscr{A}}\hat{u})
can be extended to a bounded linear operator
from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
which we still denote by
\mathsf{T}
.

For every
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
we write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\mathsf{T}u)[\omega]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{A}^{\dagger}[\omega]\big(\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{A}u)[\omega]\big)

\displaystyle=(1+\lvert\omega\rvert^{2})^{k/2}\mathbb{A}^{\dagger}[\omega]\big((1+\lvert\omega\rvert^{2})^{-k/2}\mathcal{F}(\mathscr{A}u)[\omega]\big),

whence, by the
(-k)
-homogeneity of
\mathscr{A}^{\dagger}
,
Mikhlin’s multipliers theorem yields

\|\mathsf{T}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\|(I-\Delta)^{-k/2}(\mathscr{A}u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

From the definition of
\mathsf{T}
and the characterization of
W^{-k,p}
recalled in Subsection
3.3
,
(
7.5
) follows for
u\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
The general assertion is then obtained by density.
∎

Eventually, we are able to establish
the existence of potentials in a nonsmooth setting and prove the first main result of this section.
We will use the following variant of Poincaré-Wirtinger’s inequality,
which can be derived as a corollary of Rellich-Kondrachov’s Theorem.

Lemma 7.10
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded, connected, open set with Lipschitz boundary.
There exists a constant
c>0
such that for every
u\in W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(7.6)

\lVert u-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\nabla^{\ell}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})},

where
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\nabla^{\ell}}
is the projection on the kernel of the operator
\nabla^{\ell}
.

Proof of Theorem
2.10
.

Let us fix
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
satisfying
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
W^{-k,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
.
Since we postulate that

\Omega
is an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain,
there exists an operator
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

as in Definition
7.1
.
In particular,
if we let
\tilde{u}\coloneqq\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u
,
then
\tilde{u}
is
\mathscr{A}
-free on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

As a first step,
we approximate
\tilde{u}
by maps in the image of
\mathscr{B}
.
By the definition of the projection
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}
,
there exist a sequence
\set{u_{k}}\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that

u_{k}\to\tilde{u}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{u}_{k}\coloneqq\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}u_{k}\to\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}\tilde{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

By construction,
the functions
\tilde{u}_{k}
belong to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and,
in view of Proposition
7.4
,
we recover a sequence
\set{w_{k}}\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\tilde{u}_{k}=\mathscr{B}w_{k}
.
Therefore,
recalling that
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{A}}\tilde{u}=\tilde{u}
because
\tilde{u}
is
\mathscr{A}
-free,
we deduce

(7.7)

\mathscr{B}w_{k}\to\tilde{u}\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Next, we proceed by applying Proposition
7.5
:
since
\mathscr{B}
has constant rank,

\left\|\nabla^{\ell}\big(w_{k}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{k}\big)\right\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{M})}\leq c\lVert\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})},

where
\ell
is the order of
\mathscr{B}
.
Note that the right-hand side is uniformly bounded in
k

because
\set{\tilde{u}_{k}}
is convergent in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
by (
7.7
).
As a consequence, inequality (
7.6
) yields
a function
w\in W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
such that (up to subsequences)

\tilde{w}_{k}\coloneqq w_{k}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{k}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\nabla^{\ell}}\big(w_{k}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{k}\big)\to w\quad\text{strongly in }L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M}).

Since the functions
w_{k}
are smooth,
the equality
\mathscr{B}w_{k}=\mathscr{B}\tilde{w}_{k}
holds pointwise and
we deduce from (
7.7
) that,
for all
\phi\in C_{\mathrm{c}}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}u(y)\cdot\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\lim_{k\to+\infty}\int_{\Omega}\tilde{w}_{k}\cdot\mathscr{B}^{\ast}\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{\Omega}w(y)\cdot\mathscr{B}^{\ast}\phi(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,\end{split}

where
\mathscr{B}^{\ast}
is the adjoint of
\mathscr{B}
.
The conclusion is then achieved.
∎

Remark 7.11
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be an
\mathscr{A}
-free map and
let
w\in W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
be a potential for
u
in the sense of Theorem
2.10
.
Then, there exist constants
c_{0},c_{1}>0
such that

(7.8)

\frac{1}{c_{0}}\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lVert w\|_{W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c_{1}\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

The first inequality easily follows from the identity

u=\mathscr{B}w
.
Conversely,
keeping in force the notations in the proof of Theorem
2.10
,
we have

\left\|\tilde{w}_{k}\right\|_{W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})}\leq c_{1}\lVert\tilde{u}_{k}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})},

for a suitable
c_{1}>0
,
whence, by taking the limit
k\to+\infty

and recalling requirement (2) in Definition
7.1
,
the second estimate in (
7.8
) is achieved.

7.2.
\mathscr{A}
-free extensions

The
\Gamma
-convergence analysis that we developed in Sections
4
–
6

is grounded on the splitting argument contained in Lemma
4.2
,
which in turn requires Assumption
2
.
For the reader’s convenience,
we recall the latter here:

Assumption 2

(
\mathscr{A}
-free extension)
.

There exists an
\varepsilon
-independent constant
c>0
and a sequence of operators
\set{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}}
, with
\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}\colon L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that the following holds:
for all
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
W^{-1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u=u
a.e. in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}
,

(3)

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u)=0
on
\Omega
, and

(4)

if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is
p
-equiintegrable, then
\set{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is also
p
-equiintegrable.

In contrast to the classical cases of the curl and of higher-order gradients
(see the examples at the end of this section),
we did not manage to ensure that
for a general operator
\mathscr{A}
the whole Assumption
2
holds
(cf. Theorem
2.11
).
Note that this assumption is designed to tackle the periodically perforated structure appearing in our problem.
The first step to prove properties (1)–(3) is thus to start
from the simpler scenario
in which the parameter
\varepsilon
is neglected.
In this respect, we establish the following:

Theorem 7.12

(Existence of
\mathscr{A}
-free extensions)
.

Let
D,O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open sets such that
D
is connected,
O
is bounded, and

\partial D\cap\bar{O}
is a Lipschitz boundary.
Let also
\mathscr{A}
be a linear,
k
-th order, homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients and constant rank, and
let
\mathscr{B}
be a linear,
\ell
-th order,
homogeneous differential operator
with constant coefficients such that (
7.2
) holds.
We further assume that

•

for all
\mathscr{A}
-free
u\in L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
there exists
w\in W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})
satisfying
u=\mathscr{B}w
;

•

there exist a projection operator on the subspace of
\mathscr{B}
-free maps
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}\colon W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})\to W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})
and a constant
c>0
such that

(7.9)

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\mathscr{B}w\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})}\quad\text{for all }w\in W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

Then, there exist a map

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}\colon L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and a constant
c\coloneqq c(d,p,\mathscr{A},D,O)
such that,
for all
u\in L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with
\mathscr{A}u=0
in
W^{-k,p}(D\cap O;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
a.e. in
D\cap O
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and

(3)

\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
on
O
.

Here, the projection
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}
on the kernel of
\mathscr{B}
has to be understood as an analogue of the one in (
7.3
) and Lemma
7.6
.
The main difference is that
\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}
acts on functions defined on a domain, and not on the whole space.

Remark 7.13
.

(On Korn-type inequalities)
After the first version of this manuscript was completed,

A. Arroyo-Rabasa
proved in the preprint
[
6
]
that
inequalities of the form (
2.15
)
(that is, (
7.9
) for every open bounded
D
)
hold whenever
\mathscr{B}
meets a suitable maximal rank requirement,
which entails, in particular, that
\mathscr{B}
has constant rank.
Therefore, if
\mathscr{A}
admits a potential
\mathscr{B}
in such class,
the assumptions in Theorem
7.12
are satisfied.
An example of maximal rank operator is the divergence, see Example
7.20
below.

We will comment on the relationships
between the previous theorem and the theory developed above
at the end of this section, see Remark
7.16
.
For the moment being,
let us just highlight that
the hypothesis concerning existence of potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free fields on
D

enables us to recast the problem
in terms of extension of Sobolev maps.
For the latter, an adaptation of well-established arguments yields the following:

Lemma 7.14

(cf. Lemma 2.6 in
[
1
]
)
.

Let
D,O\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be open sets.
If
D
is connected,
O
is bounded, and

\partial D\cap\bar{O}
is a Lipschitz boundary,
there exist a bounded linear map

{\mathsf{E}}\colon W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{\ell,p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})

and a constant
c\coloneqq c(d,p,D,O)
such that

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}u=u
a.e. in
D\cap O
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}u\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
, and

(3)

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(\mathsf{E}u)\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}u\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}
.

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines of
[
1
, Lemma 2.6]
.
Note that, in order to recover item (3) when
\ell>1
, Poincaré’s inequality has to be replaced with (
7.6
).
∎

We are now in a position to prove the first main result of this section.

Proof of Theorem
7.12
.

Let us fix
u\in L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
.
The current assumptions grant that
there is a Sobolev potential
w\in W^{\ell,p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M})
for
u
,
i.e.
u=\mathscr{B}w
,
and that it satisfies

(7.10)

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

If
\mathsf{E}
is the extension operator in Lemma
7.14
, we set

{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\coloneqq\mathscr{B}\big({\mathsf{E}}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\big).

By construction, then,

{\mathsf{E}}_{\mathscr{A}}u=u
almost everywhere in
D\cap O
. Additionally,
\mathscr{A}(\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}u)=0
on
O

by the definition of
\mathscr{A}
-free maps and (
7.2
).

To conclude, we need to show that

\lVert{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})}
.
By the definition of
{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}}u
, we have

\lVert{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lVert\mathscr{B}\big({\mathsf{E}}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\big)\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big({\mathsf{E}}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\big)\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})},

c
being a constant depending on
\mathscr{B}

(and hence on
\mathscr{A}
).
Thanks to Lemma
7.14
,
we obtain a bound in terms of the potential of
u
:

\lVert{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(O;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w\big)\|_{L^{p}(D;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}

for some
c\coloneqq c(d,p,\mathscr{A},D,O)
.
The conclusion is achieved by combining the above inequality with (
7.10
).
∎

Arguing as in the proof of Theorem
7.12
,
we ground the study about extensions from perforated domains on the corresponding result for Sobolev functions.
When the perforations are detached from the boundary,
the following holds:

Proposition 7.15

(cf. Lemma 8 in
[
25
]
and references therein)
.

Let
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
as in (
1.2
).
Then, there exist a constant
c\coloneqq c(d,p,D)

independent of
\varepsilon
and
\Omega
,
as well as a sequence of operators
\set{{\mathsf{E}}^{\varepsilon}}
,
with
{\mathsf{E}}^{\varepsilon}\colon W^{\ell,p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})\to W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
such that

(1)

{\mathsf{E}}^{\varepsilon}u=u
a.e. in
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
,

(2)

\lVert\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}
;

(3)

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}u)\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N\times d^{\ell}})}
, and

(4)

if
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}\subset L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is bounded and
\set{\nabla^{\ell}u_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable, then
\set{\nabla^{\ell}(\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon})}
is
p
-equiintegrable as well.

The proposition may be proved
by adapting the strategy in the seminal work by
E. Acerbi & Al.

[
1
]
.
Their result addresses only the case
\ell=1
, and the analogue of (4) is not mentioned;
we omit nonetheless the proof in the case
\ell>1
,
which is a natural adaptation of
[
1
, Proof of Theorem 2.1]
.
As for point (4), it is a mere consequence of the construction of
\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}
:
it suffices to note that reflections, dilations, and patching by partitions of unity preserve
p
-equiintegrability.

Thanks to the previous result,
we obtain that
Assumption
2
is fulfilled
by an operator
\mathscr{A}

as soon as it admits a potential for which a Korn-type inequality holds.
Note that it is fundamental that we start from fields
u
which are
\mathscr{A}
-free in the whole set
\Omega
:
in principle, the existence of a potential would be false
if we worked with maps that are
\mathscr{A}
-free just on the perforated set
\Omega_{1,\varepsilon}
, cf. Remark
7.2
.

Proof of Theorem
2.11
.

Let
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
be an
\mathscr{A}
-free map and
let
w\in W^{\ell,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{M})
be its potential.
In the same spirit of Theorem
7.12
, we set

\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}u\coloneqq\mathscr{B}\big({\mathsf{E}}^{\varepsilon}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\big),

where
\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}
is the extension operator in Proposition
7.15
.
As in the proof of Theorem
7.12
,
it easy to check that (1) and (3) in Assumption
2
hold.
We now turn to item (2), that is,

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\qquad\text{ for all $\mathscr{A}$-free }u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}).

By the definition of
\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}
in (
1.1
),
there exists an open set
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
with Lipschitz boundary such that

\Omega_{0,\varepsilon}\subset\Omega^{\prime}
and that
\delta\coloneqq\mathrm{dist}(\partial\Omega^{\prime},\partial\Omega)>0
.
In particular, recalling (
3.1
),

\Omega^{\prime}\subset\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
if
\sqrt{d}\varepsilon<\delta
, and

\set{\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon},\Omega\setminus\bar{\Omega}^{\prime}}
is an open cover of
\Omega
.
We observe that

\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}w=w\quad\text{a.e. in }\Omega\setminus\bar{\Omega}^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{1,\varepsilon},

whence, by the definition on
\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}
,

(7.11)

\lVert{\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}

For what concerns the contribution on
\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon}
, if we let

\hat{\Omega}_{1,\varepsilon}\coloneqq\bigcup_{z\in\hat{Z}_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon(D_{1}+z),\quad\text{with}\quad\hat{Z}_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq\set{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}:\varepsilon(Q+z)\subset\Omega},

we have

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big({\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}}(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w)\big)\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w\big)\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}.

The second inequality is a consequence of the construction of
\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}
,
which is obtained by patching together the extension operators from each ‘stiff’ unit
\varepsilon(D_{1}+z)
to the cell
\varepsilon(Q+z)
.
Thanks to the definition of
\hat{\Omega}_{1,\varepsilon}

we can further bound the last quantity from above
by invoking Korn’s inequality

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big(w-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w\big)\|_{L^{p}(D_{1};\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}\leq c\lVert\mathscr{B}w\|_{L^{p}(D_{1};\mathbb{R}^{N})},

which holds by assumption.
We obtain

\lVert{\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}_{\mathscr{A}}}u\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

On the whole, the last estimate and (
7.11
) yield

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\hat{\Omega}_{\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}+\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega\setminus\Omega^{\prime};\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{1,\varepsilon};\mathbb{R}^{N})}.

Eventually, we turn to (4).
For any
\varepsilon>0
, let
w_{\varepsilon}
be the potential of
u_{\varepsilon}
.
We fix
\eta>0
arbitrarily and
we let
E\subset\Omega
be a Lebesgue measurable set.
As a first step, we prove that
there exists
m>0
such that

\mathscr{L}^{d}(E)<m
implies

\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}<\eta.

Since
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
is
p
-equiintegrable,
there exists
\tilde{m}>0
with the property that

\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(F;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\eta
whenever
\mathscr{L}^{d}(F)<2\tilde{m}
.
Thanks to the outer regularity of the Lebesgue measure,
we can select a finite union of open rectangles
U\supset E

such that
\mathscr{L}^{d}(U)<\mathscr{L}^{d}(E)+\tilde{m}
.
Thus, if we set
m\coloneqq\tilde{m}
and we assume that
\mathscr{L}^{d}(E)<m
,
thanks to (
2.15
) we deduce

\displaystyle\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}\leq\lVert\nabla^{\ell}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})}\leq\lVert u_{\varepsilon}\|^{p}_{L^{p}(U;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\eta,

where the last inequality is a consequence of the
p
-equiintegrability of
\set{u_{\varepsilon}}
.

In conclusion, we infer the
p
-equiintegrability of
\set{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}}
from the one of
\set{\nabla^{\ell}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})}
.
According to the definition of
{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}}u
, we have

\lVert{\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\lVert\mathscr{B}\big({\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})\big)\|_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq c\lVert\nabla^{\ell}\big({\mathsf{E}^{\varepsilon}}(w_{\varepsilon}-\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathscr{B}}w_{\varepsilon})\big)\|_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{M\times d^{\ell}})},

whence, owing to point (4) in Proposition
7.15
,

\lVert\mathsf{E}_{\mathscr{A}}^{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(E;\mathbb{R}^{N})}<\eta

if
\mathscr{L}^{d}(E)
is sufficiently small, as desired.
∎

existence of potentials and of extension operators turn out
to be almost equivalent;
a key role in this respect is played by (

).
We elaborate on this point in the next remark.

Remark 7.16

(Relations between existence of potentials and of extension operators)
.

Let us compare the main results of the current section.

We let the operator
\mathscr{A}
be as above,
notably we assume that it has constant rank.
Thanks to Theorem
2.10
,
we know that
if
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a bounded, connected, open set with Lipschitz boundary
which is also an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain in the sense of Definition
7.1
,
then
\mathscr{A}
-free maps on
D
admit potentials.
In short,
for the class of operators under consideration and for sufficiently ‘nice’ open sets
D
,
it holds

(7.12)

D\text{ is an $\mathscr{A}$-extension domain}\implies\text{existence of potentials for $\mathscr{A}$-free fields on }D.

Conversely,
if
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an open bounded set with Lipschitz boundary
such that
all
\mathscr{A}
-free fields on
D
admit Sobolev potentials through the operator
\mathscr{B}

and if for the latter (
7.9
) holds,
then
D
is an
\mathscr{A}
-extension domain.
Indeed, by a slight adaptation of the proof,
a variant of Theorem
7.12
for the case
O=\mathbb{R}^{d}

can be established.
Schematically, again for sufficiently ‘nice’ open sets
D
,
we have

\left.\begin{aligned} \text{existence of potentials for $\mathscr{A}$-free fields on }D\\
\text{Korn-type inequality for $\mathscr{B}$ on }D\end{aligned}\right\}\implies D\text{ is an $\mathscr{A}$-extension domain}.

All in all, we see that,
given a constant rank operator
\mathscr{A}
and
a bounded, connected, open set with Lipschitz boundary
D
,
the existence of Sobolev potentials for
\mathscr{A}
-free maps on
D

and the existence of an
\mathscr{A}
-free extension operator from
D
to
\mathbb{R}^{d}

are nearly equivalent.
More specifically,
they would actually be equivalent
as soon as we knew that
the generalised Korn inequality (
7.9
) holds
when
D
is a ‘nice’ open set and
\mathscr{B}
is a constant rank operator.
In conclusion, we believe that
investigations about the validity of (
7.9
) constitute a very interesting line of research.

We conclude with a parade of examples.

Example 7.17

(Curl)
.

For the choice
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{curl}
,
we have (classically)
d=N=M=3
and

\mathrm{curl}\,u=\sum_{i=1}^{3}A^{(i)}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},

with

A^{(1)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0\\
0&0&-1\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A^{(2)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1\\
0&0&0\\
-1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad A^{(3)}\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&-1&0\\
1&0&0\\
0&0&0\end{pmatrix}.

The symbol of
\mathrm{curl}
is then

\mathbb{A}[\omega]=\begin{pmatrix}0&-\omega_{3}&\omega_{2}\\
\omega_{3}&0&-\omega_{1}\\
-\omega_{2}&\omega_{1}&0\end{pmatrix}

and
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{\mathbb{A}}[\omega]=2
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
.

It is easy to check that Assumption
1
holds.
Besides, when
\Omega
is simply connected,
in view of Proposition
7.15

all the conditions in Assumption
2
are fulfilled too.

Example 7.18

(Operators associated with higher-order gradients)
.

Let
\Omega
be simply connected.
For any
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\set{0}
,
a constant rank differential operator
\mathscr{A}
can be constructed
such that
\mathscr{A}u=0
if and only if
u=\nabla^{k}w
for a suitable
w

[
50
]
.
Then, similarly to the previous example,
for such operator
\mathscr{A}
Assumptions
1
and
2

are consequences, respectively, of a simple check and of Proposition
7.15
.

Example 7.19

(The
\mathrm{curl\,curl}
operator)
.

Let
d=M=N=3
and,
for all
i,j=1,2,3
,
let
E^{(i,j)}
be the matrix whose entries all
0
,
but for the one in position
(i,j)
, which equals
1
.
We consider the operator
\mathscr{A}={\rm curl\,curl}
, defined as

\mathrm{curl\,curl}\,u\coloneqq\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}A^{(i,j)}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{i}},

where

A^{(i,i)}=-E^{(i+1,i+1)}-E^{(i+2,i+2)}
(the indices are computed modulo
3
) and
A^{(i,j)}=E^{(i,j)}
.
The symbol of this operator is

\mathbb{A}[\omega]=\begin{pmatrix}-\omega^{2}_{2}-\omega^{2}_{3}&\omega_{1}\omega_{2}&\omega_{1}\omega_{3}\\
\omega_{1}\omega_{2}&-\omega^{2}_{1}-\omega^{2}_{3}&\omega_{2}\omega_{3}\\
\omega_{1}\omega_{3}&\omega_{3}\omega_{3}&-\omega^{2}_{1}-\omega^{2}_{2}\end{pmatrix}

and
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{\mathbb{A}}[\omega]=2
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
.

When
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded and simply connected domain with Lipschitz boundary, we have that

\mathscr{A}u=0
if and only if
u=\mathscr{B}w
for a suitable potential
w
, where
\mathscr{B}
is the symmetric gradient
\mathscr{B}w\coloneqq(\nabla w+(\nabla w)^{\mathsf{t}})/2
.
As a corollary of a recent result by
F. Cagnetti & al.

[
19
, Theorem 1.1]
, every such
\Omega
is a
\mathrm{curl\,curl}
-extension domain,
whence Assumption
1
is satisfied.
Classical Korn’s inequality grants also that Assumption
2
holds.

Example 7.20

(Divergence)
.

Let us choose
\mathscr{A}=\dvg
,

\dvg
being the standard divergence operator on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Then,
N=d
,
M=1
, and

\dvg u=\sum_{i=1}^{d}e_{i}^{\mathsf{t}}\cdot\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{i}},

where, for
i=1,\dots,d
,

e_{i}
is the
i
-th
element of the canonical basis of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and

e_{i}^{\mathsf{t}}
is its transpose.
The symbol of
\dvg
is

\mathbb{A}[\omega]=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\omega_{i}e_{i}^{\mathsf{t}},

thus
\mathrm{rank}\,\mathrm{\mathbb{A}}[\omega]=1
for all
\omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\set{0}
.
For what concerns Assumption
1
and the existence of extension operators,
we resort to a result by
T. Kato & al.
,
which we present in a simplified setting:

Proposition 7.21

(Extensions and potentials for divergence-free vector fields
[
59
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded and simply connected set with Lipschitz boundary.
Then, the following holds:

(1)

\Omega
is a
\dvg
-extension domain in the sense of Definition
7.1
.

(2)

For every
u\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\dvg u=0
in
\Omega
,
there exists
w\in W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathrm{Antisym}(d\times d))
satisfying

u=\mathrm{Div}\,w\coloneqq\sum_{i,j}\frac{\partial w_{i,j}}{\partial x_{j}}e_{i}\quad\text{in }\Omega,

where
\mathrm{Antisym}(d\times d)
is the space of
d\times d
antisymmetric matrices.
Besides,
w
can be selected in such a way that
the map
u\mapsto w
from
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
to
W^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d})
is linear and bounded.

It is interesting to notice that
the authors of
[
59
]
derive item (2) from (1), that is,
they prove an implication of the form (
7.12
).

It is proved in
[
6
, Subsection 4.1]
that
\mathrm{Div}
satisfies (
2.15
).
Hence, Theorem
7.12
holds for
\mathscr{A}=\mathrm{div}
and
Assumption
2
is fulfilled too.

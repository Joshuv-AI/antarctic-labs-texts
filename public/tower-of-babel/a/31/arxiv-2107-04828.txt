Minimal pairs, minimal fields and implicit constant fields
Arpan Dutta

Abstract
. Minimal pairs of definition were introduced by Alexandru, Popescu and Zaharescu [ References , References , References ] to study residue transcendental extensions. In this paper we obtain analogous results in the value transcendental case. We introduce the notion of minimal fields of definition for valuation transcendental extensions and show that they share some common ramification theoretic properties. The connection between minimal fields of definition and implicit constant fields is also investigated. Further, we explore the relationship between valuation transcendental extensions and pseudo-Cauchy sequences.

1.
Introduction

Given a valued field
(K,\nu)
, it is of interest to understand the set of all possible extensions of
\nu
to a rational function field
K(x)
. On the other hand, given an extension
\omega
of
\nu
to
K(x)
, it is important to give a complete description of the valuation
\omega
. These two problems are tightly connected and several objects have been introduced to tackle such problems. The notion of minimal pairs of definition was introduced by Alexandru, Popescu and Zaharescu to study residue transcendental extensions [
References
,
References
,
References
]. MacLane [
References
] introduced the notion of key polynomials, which was then generalized by Vaquié in [
References
]. An alternative form of key polynomials was given by Spivakovsky and Novacoski in [
References
]. The notion of pseudo-Cauchy sequences was introduced by Kaplansky in his celebrated paper [
References
]. All the relevant definitions will be provided in Section
2
.

An extension
\omega
of a valuation
\nu
on a field
K
to a rational function field
K(x)
satisfies the famous Abhyankar inequality:

\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,\omega K(x)/\nu K+\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(x)\omega:K\nu]\leq 1,

where
\omega K(x)
and
\nu K
denote the correpsonding value groups,
K(x)\omega
and
K\nu
denote the corresponding residue fields and
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,\omega K(x)/\nu K
is the
{\mathbb{Q}}
-dimension of the divisible hull
\frac{\omega K(x)}{\nu K}\otimes_{{\mathbb{Z}}}{\mathbb{Q}}
. This inequality is a consequence of Theorem 1 of [§10.3, Chapter VI,
References
]. The extension
\omega
is said to be
value transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm rat rk}\,\omega K(x)/\nu K=1
and
residue transcendental
if
\mbox{\rm trdeg}\,[K(x)\omega:K\nu]=1
. The extension
\omega
is said to be
valuation transcendental
if it is either value or residue transcendental. In Section
3
, we extend the results obtained in [
References
,
References
,
References
] to the case of value transcendental extensions. In particular, we show that a value transcendental extension is completely described by a minimal pair of definition [cf. Section
2
]. We also consider the question: given a pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for a valuation transcendental extension
\omega
, can we find a minimal pair of definition for it? Using the notion of complete distinguished chains, introduced by Popescu and Zaharescu in [
References
], we provide an answer to the above question in Theorem
3.9
, under the assumption that
K(a)|K
is a defectless extension of henselian valued fields [cf. Section
2
]. Further, in Theorem
3.12
we show that for a valuation transcendental extension
\omega
of
\nu
to
K(x)
and an extension
\overline{\nu}
of
\nu
to
\overline{K}
, the simultaneous extensions of
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
are tightly connected, where
\overline{K}
is a fixed algebraic closure of
K
. As a consequence we obtain that there are a finite number of such common extensions. Although some of the results obtained in this section are already known, we recreate them here for the sake of completeness.

In Section
4
we introduce the notion of minimal fields of definition. A minimal field of definition for a valuation transcendental extension
\omega
is defined to be of the form
K(a)
, where
(a,\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
. We show that the minimal fields of definition for
\omega
share some common ramification theoretic properties. The relationship between implicit constant fields, introduced by Kuhlmann in [
References
], and the minimal fields of definition is also explored. For a valued field extension
(K(x)|K,\omega)
and an extension of
\omega
to
\overline{K(x)}
, the
implicit constant field
is defined to be the relative algebraic closure of
K
in the henselization of
K(x)
and is denoted by
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)
. We obtain the following result in this paper:

Theorem 1.1
.

Take a valued field
(K,\nu)
, a valuation transcendental extension
\omega
of
\nu
to
K(x)
, and a minimal field of definition
K(a)
for
\omega
. Take an extension
\overline{\omega}
of
\omega
to
\overline{K}(x)
which has
(a,\gamma)
as a minimal pair of definition for some
\gamma\in\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)
, and fix an extension of
\overline{\omega}
to
\overline{K(x)}
. Take
b_{1},b_{2}\in\overline{K}
such that
K(b_{1})=K(a)\cap K^{i}
and
K(b_{2})=K(a)\cap K^{r}
, where
K^{i}
and
K^{r}
denote the absolute inertia field and the absolute ramification field of
(K,\nu)
. Write the henselizations of
K(a)
and
K(b_{i})
as
K(a)^{h}
and
K(b_{i})^{h}
.

If
\omega
is residue transcendental, then

(1)

K(b_{1})^{h}\subseteq IC(K(x)|K,\omega)\subseteq K(a)^{h}.

If
\omega
is value transcendental, then

(2)

K(b_{2})^{h}\subseteq IC(K(x)|K,\omega)\subseteq K(a)^{h}.

An important relevant problem is the following:

Question 1.2
.

Take notations and assumptions as in Theorem
1.1
. Give an explicit computation for
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)
.

The absolute inertia field and the absolute ramification field are separable-algebraic extensions of
K
. The problem of explicit computation of the implicit constant field when
K
admits an arbitrary separable-algebraic minimal field of definition for
\omega
remains open. A partial solution to that problem is provided in the following theorem:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let notations and asumptions be as in Theorem
1.1
. Assume that
a
is separable over
K
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i) Assume that
\gamma>\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
. Then
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)=K(a)^{h}
.

(ii) Assume that
\omega
is value transcendental,
\nu
admits a unique extension from
K
to
K(a)
and
\gamma<\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
. Then
(\overline{\omega}K(a,x):\omega K(x))=j
where
\overline{\omega}(a-a_{i})>\gamma
for exactly
j
many conjugates
a_{i}
of
a
, including
a
itself. Consequently,
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)\subsetneq K(a)^{h}
.

(iii) Assume that
\omega
is residue transcendental,
\nu
admits a unique extension from
K
to
K(a)
and
\gamma\leq\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
. Then either
\overline{\omega}K(a,x)\neq\omega K(x)
, or,
[K(a,x)\overline{\omega}:K(x)\omega]=j
where
\overline{\omega}(a-a_{i})\geq\gamma
for exactly
j
many conjugates
a_{i}
of
a
, including
a
itself. Consequently,
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)\subsetneq K(a)^{h}
.

In particular, Theorem
1.3
provides a partial solution to Question
1.2
when
(K,\nu)
is either henselian or has rank one (that is,
\nu K
is an ordered subgroup of
{\mathbb{R}}
), as observed in Corollary
6.4
.

On the other hand, if there is a minimal field of definition for
\omega
that is a purely inseparable extension of
K
, then we obtain that
IC(K(x)|K,\omega)
equals the henselization of
K
. The problem remains open when a minimal field of definition for
\omega
is an arbitrary inseparable extension of
K
.

Finally, in Theorem
7.2
we provide a complete solution to Question
1.2
when
\omega
admits a unique pair of definition. We show that in that situation the implicit constant field is the separable closure of
K
in the henselization of the minimal field of definition for
\omega
.

In Section
8
we provide several examples illustrating that the ramification or field theoretic structure of the minimal field of definition may not necessarily be recovered from the information of the implicit constant field. Examples showing that the implicit constant field may be a proper non-trivial subextension of the henselization of a minimal field of definition for
\omega
are also constructed.

Section
9
deals with the connection between valuation transcendental extensions and pseudo-Cauchy sequences. Specifically, we consider the case when
x
is a limit of a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
in
(K,\nu)
, and study its connection with the pairs of definition and key polynomials for
\omega
.

For basic information on valuation theory, we refer the reader to [
References
,

,

2.
Preliminaries

We adopt the following convention in the present paper:
(K,\nu)
denotes a field
K
equipped with a valuation
\nu
,
\omega
denotes some valuation transcendental extension of
\nu
to
K(x)
,
\overline{\nu}
denotes some extension of
\nu
to a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, and
\overline{\omega}
denotes some common extension of
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
. The existence of such a common extension is guaranteed by Lemma 3.2 of [
References
]. However,
\overline{\omega}
is not necessarily uniquely determined by
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
. The value group, residue field, valuation ring and maximal ideal of a valued field
(L,\nu)
are denoted by
\nu L
,
L\nu
,
\mathcal{O}_{L}
and
\mathcal{M}_{L}
. The value of an element
l\in L
is denoted by
\nu l
and its residue by
l\nu
. Denote a valued field extension as
(L|K,\nu)
where
L|K
is an extension of fields,
\nu
is a valuation on
L
and
K
is equipped with the restricted valuation. If
L
and
K
are subfields of a larger valued field
(F,\nu)
, then we will also write
(L|K,\nu)
to mean that they are equipped with the restricted valuations. Given an extension of valued fields
(K(y)|K,\nu)
, we define the set

\nu(y-K):=\{\nu(y-a)\mid a\in K\}.

The separable closure of a field
K_{1}
in some overfield
K_{2}
is denoted by
(K_{2}|K_{1})^{\mathrm{sep}}
.

2.1.
Ramification Theory

We recall some aspects of ramification theory and general valuation theory [cf.
References
,
References
,
References
,
References
]. Set
G:=\mathrm{Gal}\,(\overline{K}|K)
. The absolute decomposition group of the extension
(\overline{K}|K,\overline{\nu})
is defined as

G_{d}:=\{\sigma\in G\mid\overline{\nu}\circ\sigma=\overline{\nu}\text{ on }K^{\mathrm{sep}}\}.

The absolute inertia group is defined as

G_{i}:=\{\sigma\in G\mid\overline{\nu}(\sigma a-a)>0\text{ for all }a\in\mathcal{O}_{K^{\mathrm{sep}}}\}.

The absolute ramification group is defined as

G_{r}:=\{\sigma\in G\mid\overline{\nu}(\sigma a-a)>\overline{\nu}a\text{ for all }a\in K^{\mathrm{sep}}\setminus\{0\}\}.

The corresponding fixed fields in
K^{\mathrm{sep}}
will be denoted by
K^{d}
,
K^{i}
and
K^{r}
and they are called the
absolute decomposition field
,
absolute inertia field
and the
absolute ramification field
of
(K,\nu)
respectively. For an arbitrary algebraic extension of valued fields
(L|K,\overline{\nu})
, we have that
L^{d}=L.K^{d}
,
L^{i}=L.K^{i}
and
L^{r}=L.K^{r}
. The
henselization
of a valued field
K
is the smallest henselian field containing
K
, and we can consider it to be the same as the absolute decomposition field
K^{d}
. The henselization is also denoted by
K^{h}
. As a consequence, for algebraic extensions
L|K
, we have

L^{h}=L.K^{h}.

We state a simple form of the
fundamental inequality
here: for every finite extension
(L|K,\overline{\nu})
,

[L:K]\geq(\overline{\nu}L:\nu K)[L\overline{\nu}:K\nu].

The extension
(L|K,\overline{\nu})
is said to be
defectless
if we have equality in the above inequality.

The
characteristic exponent
of
(K,\nu)
, denoted by
p
, is defined to be equal to
\mathrm{char}\,K\nu
if
\mathrm{char}\,K\nu>0
, and
1
otherwise. The
Lemma of Ostrowski
states that whenever
\nu
admits a unique extension
\overline{\nu}
to a finite extension
E
of
K
, then

[E:K]=(\overline{\nu}E:\nu K)[E\overline{\nu}:K\nu]p^{n}

for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
. As a consequence, any extension of henselian valued fields of degree coprime to
p
is a defectless extension.

An algebraic extension
(L|K,\overline{\nu})
of henselian valued fields is said to be
tame
if every finite subextension
(E|K,\overline{\nu})
satisfies the following conditions:

(TE1)
\mathrm{char}\,K\nu
does not divide
(\overline{\nu}E:\nu K)
,

(TE2) the residue field extension
E\overline{\nu}|K\nu
is separable,

(TE3)
(E|K,\overline{\nu})
is defectless.

An algebraic extension
(F|K,\overline{\nu})
of henselian valued fields is said to be
purely wild
if
\overline{\nu}F/\nu K
is a
p
-group and
F\overline{\nu}|K\nu
is purely inseparable. Equivalently,
F\cap K^{r}=K
. An extension of valued fields is said to be
immediate
if there is no value group or residue field extension. In particular, immediate extensions and purely inseparable extensions of henselian valued fields are purely wild extensions.

2.2.
Krasner’s Lemma

Take a valued field
(K,\nu)
and set
G:=\mathrm{Gal}\,(\overline{K}|K)
. Choose
a\in\overline{K}\setminus K
which is not purely inseparable over
K
. The
Krasner constant
of
a
over
K
is defined as

\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K):=\max\{\overline{\nu}(\sigma a-\tau a)\mid\sigma,\,\tau\in G\text{ and }\sigma a\neq\tau a\}.

The fact that all extensions of
\nu
to
\overline{K}
are conjugate implies that
\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
is independent of the choice of the extension
\overline{\nu}
. For the same reason, whenever
\nu
admits a unique extension from
K
to
K(a)
, we can define the Krasner’s constant as

\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K):=\max\{\overline{\nu}(a-\sigma a)\mid\sigma\in G\text{ and }a\neq\sigma a\}.

We will now state a variant of the important Krasner’s Lemma [cf. Lemma 2.21,
References
].

Lemma 2.1
.

Take a separable-algebraic extension
K(a)|K
and
(K(a,y)|K,\nu)
be any valued extension such that

\nu(a-y)>\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K).

Then for every extension of
\nu
from
K(a)
to
\overline{K(a,y)}
, we have that
a\in K(y)^{h}
.

Take an algebraic extension
(L|K,\overline{\nu})
and a separable-algebraic element
a
over
K
. Then
a
is separable-algebraic over
L
. Further, the minimal polynomial of
a
over
L
divides that over
K
. It then follows from the definition of the Krasner constant that

\mathrm{kras}\,(a,L)\leq\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K).

2.3.
Artin-Schreier extensions

Take a valued field
(K,\nu)
with
\mathrm{char}\,K=p>0
and a polynomial
f(x):=x^{p}-x-c
over
K
. Take
a\in\overline{K}
such that
f(a)=0
. Such a polynomial is said to be an Artin-Schreier polynomial. When
K(a)|K
is a non-trivial extension, it is said to be an Artin-Schreier extension. Applying the Frobenius endomorphism, we observe that

f(a+i)=(a+i)^{p}-(a+i)-c=a^{p}+i^{p}-a-i-c=i^{p}-i=0

for all
i\in{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Thus the conjugates of
a
are
a,a+1,\dotsc,a+p-1
. It follows that
f(x)
is either irreducible, or it splits completely over
K
. Further, whenever
\overline{\nu}a\geq 0
, then
\nu c=\overline{\nu}(a^{p}-a)\geq 0
. Thus if we have
\nu c<0
, then
\overline{\nu}a<0
. It then follows from the triangle inequality that

\nu c<0\Longrightarrow\nu c=\overline{\nu}a^{p}=p\overline{\nu}a.

2.4.
Pseudo-Cauchy sequences

A well-ordered set
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
in a valued field
(K,\nu)
, where
\lambda
is some limit ordinal, is said to form a
pseudo-Cauchy sequence
if
\nu(z_{\mu_{1}}-z_{\mu_{2}})<\nu(z_{\mu_{2}}-z_{\mu_{3}})
for all
\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\mu_{3}<\lambda
. It follows from the triangle inequality that
\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\rho})=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})
for all
\mu<\rho<\lambda
. An element
l
in some valued field extension
(L,\nu)
of
(K,\nu)
is said to be a
limit
of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
if
\nu(l-z_{\mu})=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})
for all
\mu<\lambda
.

Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
and a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
over
K
. It follows from Lemma 5 of [
References
] that the sequence
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is either ultimately constant, or it is ultimately monotonically increasing. If
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant for all polynomials
f
over
K
, then
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
is said to be a pseudo-Cauchy sequence of
transcendental type
. Otherwise, it is of
algebraic type
. For a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
of algebraic type, a monic polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
of minimal degree such that
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately monotonically increasing is said to be an
associated minimal polynomial
of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Take an extension
(K(y)|K,\nu)
and a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
over
(K,\nu)
. Then either
y
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
, or,
(\nu(y-z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant.

Proof.

Consider the polynomial
f(x)=x-y\in K(y)(x)
. Observe that
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
is a pseudo-Cauchy sequence over
(K(y),\nu)
. Assume that
(\nu(y-z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}=(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is not ultimately constant. In light of Lemma 5 of [
References
] the sequence
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately monotonically increasing. So there exists some ordinal
\mu_{0}<\lambda
such that
\nu(y-z_{\mu_{2}})>\nu(y-z_{\mu_{1}})
for all
\mu_{0}<\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}<\lambda
. It follows from the triangle inequality that

\nu(y-z_{\mu})=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})\text{ for all }\mu>\mu_{0}.

Now take any ordinal
\mu^{\prime}<\mu_{0}
and
\mu>\mu_{0}
. Then
\nu(y-z_{\mu})=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})>\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu^{\prime}})
. As a consequence of the triangle inequality, we now obtain that

\nu(y-z_{\mu})=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})\text{ for all }\mu<\lambda.

Hence
y
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.
∎

2.5.
Pure and weakly pure extensions

An extension
(K(x)|K,\omega)
is said to be
pure
if it satisfies one of the following conditions:

(PE1)
\omega(x-a)
is not a torsion element modulo
\nu K
for some
a\in K
,

(PE2)
\omega b(x-a)=0
and
b(x-a)\omega
is transcendental over
K\nu
for some
a,b\in K
,

(PE3)
x
is the limit of some pseudo-Cauchy sequence of transcendental type in
(K,\nu)
.

It has been shown in Lemma 3.5 of [
References
] that
\omega K(x)/\nu K
is torsion free and
K\nu
is relatively algebraically closed in
K(x)\omega
when
(K(x)|K,\omega)
is a pure extension. As a consequence we obtain the following facts:

(i) In case (PE1),
\omega K(x)=\nu K\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\omega(x-a)\text{ and }K(x)\omega=K\nu
.

(ii) In case (PE2),
\omega K(x)=\nu K
and
K(x)\omega=K\nu(b(x-a)\omega)
.

An extension
(K(x)|K,\omega)
is always pure when
K
is algebraically closed [Lemma 3.6,
References
].

An extension
(K(x)|K,\omega)
is said to be
weakly pure
if it is either pure, or there exist
a,b\in K
and
e\in{\mathbb{N}}
such that
\omega b(x-a)^{e}=0
and
b(x-a)^{e}\omega
is transcendental over
K\nu
.

2.6.
Homogeneous Sequences

The notion of homogeneous sequences was introduced by Kuhlmann [
References
,
References
]. An element
a
is said to be
strongly homogeneous
over
(K,\nu)
if
a\in K^{\mathrm{sep}}\setminus K
, the extension of
\nu
from
K
to
K(a)
is unique and
\overline{\nu}a=\mathrm{kras}\,(a,K)
.

Let
(L|K,\nu)
be an extension of valued fields and
a,y\in L
. We will say that
a
is a
homogeneous approximation
of
y
over
K
if
a-d
is strongly homogeneous over
K
for some
d\in K
, and
\nu(y-a)>\nu(a-d)
. A sequence of elements
(a_{i})_{i\in S}
in
\overline{K}
, where
S
is an initial segment of
{\mathbb{N}}
and
a_{0}:=0
, is said to form a
homogeneous sequence for
y
if
a_{i}-a_{i-1}
is a homogeneous approximation of
y-a_{i-1}
over
K(a_{0},\dotsc,a_{i-1})
for all
i\in S
.

2.7.
Minimal pairs and key polynomials

Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
with
\deg f=n
and take
a\in K
. Write

f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}c_{i}(x-a)^{i}

where
c_{i}\in K
. Now take some
\gamma
in some ordered abelian group containing
\nu K
. Define the map
\nu_{a,\gamma}:K[x]\longrightarrow\nu K+{\mathbb{Z}}\gamma
by setting

\nu_{a,\gamma}f:=\min\{\nu c_{i}+i\gamma\}.

Extend
\nu_{a,\gamma}
canonically to
K(x)
. Then
\nu_{a,\gamma}
is a valuation on
K(x)
[Lemma 3.10,
References
]. By definition,
\nu_{a,\gamma}(x-a)=\gamma
. Take any
b\in K
. Then
\nu_{a,\gamma}(x-b)=\min\{\gamma,\nu(a-b)\}\leq\gamma
. It follows that

(3)

\nu_{a,\gamma}(x-a)=\gamma=\max\nu_{a,\gamma}(x-K).

It has been shown in Theorem 3.11 of [
References
] that
(K(x)|K,\omega)
is a valuation transcendental extension if and only if
\overline{\omega}=\overline{\nu}_{a,\gamma}
for some
a\in\overline{K}
and
\gamma\in\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)
. Further, the extension is value transcendental if and only if
\gamma\notin\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
and the extension is residue transcendental if and only if
\gamma\in\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Such a pair
(a,\gamma)
is said to be a
pair of definition
for
\omega
. A valuation transcendental extension can have multiple pairs of definition. The following lemma, proved in Proposition 3 of [
References
], gives us a relation between the different pairs of definition:

Lemma 2.3
.

Take
a,\,a^{\prime}\in\overline{K}
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}
in some ordered abelian group containing
\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Then,

\overline{\nu}_{a,\gamma}=\overline{\nu}_{a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime}}\text{ if and only if }\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}\text{ and }\overline{\nu}(a-a^{\prime})\geq\gamma.

The above lemma motivates the following definition:

Definition 2.4
.

A pair
(a,\gamma)
is said to be a
minimal pair of definition
for
\omega
if
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
and for any
b\in\overline{K}
,

\overline{\nu}(a-b)\geq\gamma\Longrightarrow[K(a):K]\leq[K(b):K].

In other words,
a
is of minimal degree over
K
with the property that
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
. It follows from Theorem 3.11 of [
References
] that every valuation transcendental extension admits a pair of definition. The well-ordering principle then implies that every valuation transcendental extension admits a minimal pair of definition.

Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
. We define

\delta(f):=\max\{\overline{\omega}(x-a)\mid f(a)=0\}.

This notion has been introduced by Novacoski in [
References
]. It has been observed in [
References
] that for a fixed valuation transcendental extension
\omega
, the value
\delta(f)
is independent of the extension
\overline{\omega}
of
\omega
to
\overline{K}(x)
. A root
a
of
f
such that
\delta(f)=\overline{\omega}(x-a)
will be referred to as a
maximal root
of
f
. A monic polynomial
Q(x)\in K[x]
is said to be a
key polynomial
of
\omega
over
K
if for any
f(x)\in K[x]
, we have that

\deg f<\deg Q\Longrightarrow\delta(f)<\delta(Q).

Now take a monic polynomial
Q(x)\in K[x]
. Then any polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
has a unique expansion, called the
Q
-expansion
, which is of the form

(4)

f=f_{0}+f_{1}Q+\dotsc+f_{r}Q^{r},

where
f_{i}(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg f_{i}<\deg Q
. We have the map
\nu_{Q}:K[x]\longrightarrow\omega K(x)
given by

\nu_{Q}f:=\min\{\omega f_{i}+i\omega Q\}.

We can extend
\nu_{Q}
by setting
\nu_{Q}(f/g):=\nu_{Q}f-\nu_{Q}g
. A sufficient condition for
\nu_{Q}
to be a valuation on
K(x)
is that
Q
be a key polynomial of
\omega
over
K
[Proposition 2.6,
References
], but it is not a necessary condition [Proposition 2.3,
References
]. The connection between minimal pairs of definition and key polynomials is explored in Theorem 1.1 of [
References
]:

Theorem 2.5
.

Take a monic irreducible polynomial
Q(x)\in K[x]
. Take a root
a
of
Q(x)
such that
\delta(Q)=\overline{\omega}(x-a)
. Then
(a,\delta(Q))
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
if and only if
\omega=\nu_{Q}
.

Corollary 2.6
.

Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
and consider the minimal polynomial
Q(x)
of
a
over
K
. Then
\omega=\nu_{Q}
. Further,
Q
is a key polynomial for
\omega
.

Proof.

By definition, we can take an extension
\overline{\omega}
of
\omega
to
\overline{K}(x)
which has
(a,\gamma)
as a minimal pair of definition, that is,
\overline{\omega}=\overline{\nu}_{a,\gamma}
. It follows that

\overline{\omega}(x-a)=\gamma=\max\overline{\omega}(x-\overline{K}).

The maximality of
\gamma
then implies that
\delta(Q)=\gamma
. It now follows from Theorem 1.1 of [
References
] that
\omega=\nu_{Q}
. Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg f<\deg Q
. The maximality of
\gamma
implies that
\delta(f)\leq\delta(Q)
. Suppose that
\delta(f)=\delta(Q)
. Then there exists some root
z
of
f
such that
\overline{\omega}(x-z)=\gamma
. However, this implies that
(z,\gamma)
is also a pair of definition for
\omega
, which contradicts the minimality of
(a,\gamma)
. Hence
\delta(f)<\delta(Q)
and thus
Q(x)
is a key polynomial for
\omega
.
∎

3.
Minimal pairs of definition for
\omega

Throughout the rest of the paper
,
\omega
denotes some valuation transcendental extension of
\nu
to
K(x)
,
\overline{\nu}
denotes some extension of
\nu
to a fixed algebraic closure
\overline{K}
of
K
, and
\overline{\omega}
denotes some common extension of
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
.

Minimal pairs of definition were used by Alexandru, Popescu and Zaharescu [
References
,
References
,
References
] to give a very satisfactory description of residue transcendental extensions. We first provide a brief summary of some of their important results. A proof of these statements can be obtained in Thereom 2.1 of [
References
].

Remark 3.1
.

Assume that
\omega
is a residue transcendental extension of
\nu
to
K(x)
with a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
. Take the minimal polynomial
Q(x)
of
a
over
K
. Take a polynomial
g(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg g<\deg Q
. Then

\omega g=\overline{\nu}g(a).

Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
with the
Q
-expansion
f=f_{0}+f_{1}Q+\dotsc+f_{r}Q^{r}
. Then,

\omega f=\min\{\overline{\nu}f_{i}(a)+i\omega Q\}.

We have that

\omega K(x)=\overline{\nu}K(a)+{\mathbb{Z}}\omega Q\text{ and }(\omega K(x):\overline{\nu}K(a))=e,

where
e
is the least natural number such that
e\omega Q\in\overline{\nu}K(a)
.

If
h_{1},h_{2}
are coprime polynomials over
K
, then we define the
order
of
\frac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}
as

\mathrm{ord}\,(\frac{h_{1}}{h_{2}}):=\max\{\deg h_{1},\deg h_{2}\}.

Now take
g(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg g<\deg Q
and
\overline{\nu}g(a)=\omega g=\omega Q^{e}
. Then
\frac{Q^{e}}{g}
is the element of
\mathcal{O}_{K(x)}
of the smallest order such that
\frac{Q^{e}}{g}\omega
is transcendental over
K\nu
. Further,
K(a)\overline{\nu}
can be canonically identified with the relative algebraic closure of
K\nu
in
K(x)\omega
. Moreover,

K(x)\omega=K(a)\overline{\nu}(\frac{Q^{e}}{g}\omega).

Now take some
h(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg h<\deg Q
and
\omega h=-e\omega Q
. Then
\omega Q^{e}h=0
and hence
(Q^{e}h)\omega\in K(x)\omega
. Further, observe that
\omega(gh)=0
and hence

(hQ^{e})\omega=((gh)\omega)(\frac{Q^{e}}{g}\omega).

Suppose that
(gh)\omega
is transcendental over
K\nu
. It follows from Proposition 1.1 of [
References
] that there is a root
b
of
gh\in K[x]
such that
(b,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
. So either
g(b)=0
or
h(b)=0
. However, the fact that
\deg g,\deg h<\deg Q
and the minimality of
(a,\gamma)
yields a contradiction to the above assertion. Hence
(gh)\omega
is algebraic over
K\nu
. The fact that
K(a)\overline{\nu}
is the relative algebraic closure of
K\nu
in
K(x)\omega
now implies that
(gh)\omega\in K(a)\overline{\nu}
. It follows that
K(a)\overline{\nu}(\frac{Q^{e}}{g}\omega)=K(a)\overline{\nu}((hQ^{e})\omega)
. We have thus obtained that

K(a)\overline{\nu}(\frac{Q^{e}}{g}\omega)=K(x)\omega=K(a)\overline{\nu}((hQ^{e})\omega).

We now show that a value transcendental extension is completely described by a minimal pair of definition. Recall that a minimal pair of definition always exists for a valuation transcendental extension.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
\omega
is value transcendental. Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
and the minimal polynomial
Q(x)
of
a
over
K
. Then
\omega Q\notin\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Take a polynomial
g(x)\in K[x]
such that
\deg g<\deg Q
. Then
\omega g=\overline{\nu}g(a)
. Take a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
with the
Q
-expansion
f=f_{0}+f_{1}Q+\dotsc+f_{r}Q^{r}
. Then,

\omega f=\min\{\overline{\nu}f_{i}(a)+i\omega Q\}.

Proof.

It follows from Corollary
2.6
that
\omega=\nu_{Q}
. So for a polynomial
f(x)\in K[x]
with the given
Q
-expansion, we have

(5)

\omega f=\min\{\omega f_{i}+i\omega Q\}.

Take
g(x)\in K[x]
with
\deg g<\deg Q
. Write
g(x)=c(x-b_{1})\dotsc(x-b_{m})
where
b_{i}\in\overline{K}
and
c\in K
. If
\overline{\nu}(a-b_{i})>\gamma
for some
i
, then
(b_{i},\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\overline{\omega}
. The minimality of
(a,\gamma)
then implies that
[K(b_{i}):K]\geq[K(a):K]
. But,
[K(b_{i}):K]\leq\deg g<\deg Q=[K(a):K]
. Hence
\overline{\nu}(a-b_{i})<\gamma
for each
i
, and thus,
\overline{\omega}(x-b_{i})=\overline{\nu}(a-b_{i})\in\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. It follows that,

(6)

\deg g<\deg Q\Longrightarrow\omega g=\overline{\nu}g(a)\in\overline{\nu}\overline{K}.

From (
5
) and (
6
) it follows that

\omega f=\min\{\overline{\nu}f_{i}(a)+i\omega Q\}.

Finally, we express
Q(x)=(x-a)(x-a_{2})\dotsc(x-a_{n})
. We have
\overline{\omega}(x-a)=\gamma\notin\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Further, for each
i
we have that
\overline{\omega}(x-a_{i})=\min\{\gamma,\overline{\nu}(a-a_{i})\}
. Hence
\omega Q\notin\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

Take notations and assumptions as in Lemma
3.2
. Then
\omega K(x)=\overline{\nu}K(a)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\omega Q
and
K(x)\omega=K(a)\overline{\nu}
. The equality of the value groups is straightforward. To see the equality of the residue fields, we first observe that
(K(a,x)|K(a),\overline{\omega})
is a pure value transcendental extension. Consequently,
K(a,x)\overline{\omega}=K(a)\overline{\nu}
. It follows that

K(x)\omega\subseteq K(a,x)\overline{\omega}=K(a)\overline{\nu}.

Now take
g(x)\in K[x]
with
\deg g<\deg Q
and
\omega g=0
. Consider the expansion

g(x)=g(a)+\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}(x-a)^{i}.

Observe that all the monomials in the above expansion have distinct values. It now follows from Lemma
3.2
and the triangle inequality that

\omega g=0=\overline{\nu}g(a)<\overline{\omega}[c_{i}(x-a)^{i}]\text{ for all }i.

Consequently,

\overline{\omega}(g(x)-g(a))=\min\{\overline{\omega}[c_{i}(x-a)^{i}]\mid i=1,\dotsc,m\}>0.

We have thus obtained that
g(x)\omega=g(a)\overline{\nu}
. Since this holds for any arbitrary
g(a)\overline{\nu}\in K(a)\overline{\nu}
, we have

K(a)\overline{\nu}\subseteq K(x)\omega.

Hence we have arrived at the equality

K(x)\omega=K(a)\overline{\nu}.

In Theorem 3.1 of [
References
] it is shown that every residue transcendental extension of
\nu
to
K(x)
has a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)\in\overline{K}\times\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
where
a
is separable over
K
. However, this is not necessarily true for a value transcendental extension.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
(a,\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
and
\gamma>\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Then
\omega
is value transcendental and
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
\omega
.

Proof.

The fact that
\gamma
is not contained in the divisible group
\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
implies that
\gamma
is torsion free modulo
\nu K
. Consequently,
\omega
is a value transcendental extension. Suppose that
(b,\gamma)
is another pair of definition for
\omega
for some
b\in\overline{K}
with
b\neq a
. Then
\overline{\nu}(a-b)>\gamma
, which contradicts our assumption. Hence
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
\omega
.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume that
K
is not perfect. Then there exists a value transcendental extension
\omega
of
\nu
to
K(x)
with a unique pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
such that
a
is not separable over
K
.

Proof.

Consider the ordered abelian group
\Gamma:={\mathbb{Z}}\oplus\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
equipped with the lexicographic order. Take some
a\in\overline{K}\setminus K^{\mathrm{sep}}
and set
\gamma:=(1,0)
. Take the extension
\overline{\omega}:=\overline{\nu}_{a,\gamma}
of
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
and set
\omega:=\overline{\omega}|_{K(x)}
. Observe that
\gamma>\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. The result now follows from the preceding lemma.
∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
\nu K
is cofinal in
\omega K(x)
. Then
\omega
has a minimal pair of definition
(b,\gamma)
such that
b
is separable over
K
.

Proof.

Observe that
\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)/\omega K(x)
is a torsion group and hence
\omega K(x)
is cofinal in
\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)
. Then the fact that
\nu K
is cofinal in
\omega K(x)
implies that
\nu K
is cofinal in
\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)
. The proof is now identical to the proof of Theorem 3.1 of [
References
].
∎

We combine the preceding observations in the next theorem.

Theorem 3.7
.

Take a minimal pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
. Then the following cases are possible:

(i)
\gamma>\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
. Then
\omega
is value transcendental and
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
\omega
.

(ii)
\nu K
is cofinal in
\omega K(x)
. Then there exists a minimal pair of definition
(b,\gamma)
for
\omega
such that
b
is separable over
K
.

We now consider the following question: given a pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
, can we construct a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
? We illustrate a solution in the particular case when
K(a)|K
is a defectless extension of henselian valued fields. Assume that
(K,\nu)
is henselian and take
a\in\overline{K}
. Consider the set

M(a):=\{\overline{\nu}(a-z)\mid z\in\overline{K}\text{ and }[K(z):K]<[K(a):K]\}.

The maximum of
M(a)
, whenever it exists, is denoted by
\delta(a,K)
. It has been shown in Theorem 1.3 of [
References
] that
M(a)
admits a maximum whenever
(K(a)|K,\overline{\nu})
is a defectless extension. A pair
(a,z)\in\overline{K}\times\overline{K}
is said to form a
distinguished pair
if
[K(z):K]<[K(a):K]
,
\overline{\nu}(a-z)=\delta(a,K)
and
[K(z):K]
is minimal with respect to this property. A chain
a:=a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}
of elements in
\overline{K}
is said to form a
complete distinguished chain
if
(a_{i},a_{i+1})
is a distinguished pair for each
i=0,\dotsc,n-1
and
a_{n}\in K
. Observe that in this case we have
\delta(a,K)>\delta(a_{1},K)>\dotsc>\delta(a_{n},K)=\nu a_{n}
. The following is Theorem 1.2 of [
References
]:

Theorem 3.8
.

Take a henselian valued field
(K,\nu)
. Then an element
a\in\overline{K}
has a complete distinguished chain if and only if
(K(a)|K,\overline{\nu})
is a defectless extension.

We now take a henselian valued field
(K,\nu)
and a pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
. When
\gamma>\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
then
(a,\gamma)
is the unique pair of definition for
\omega
. So we restrict our attention to the case when
\overline{\nu}\overline{K}
is cofinal in
\overline{\omega}\overline{K}(x)
. Assume that
(K(a)|K,\overline{\nu})
is a defectless extension. Then we have a complete distinguised chain
a=a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}
. We first consider the case when
\gamma>\delta(a,K)
. If
(b,\gamma)
is another pair of definition for
\omega
, then
\overline{\nu}(a-b)\geq\gamma>\delta(a,K)
and hence
[K(b):K]\geq[K(a):K]
. It follows that
(a,\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
. Now assume that
\gamma\leq\delta(a,K)
. Then
\overline{\nu}(a-a_{1})=\delta(a,K)\geq\gamma
and hence
(a_{1},\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
. If
\gamma>\delta(a_{1},K)
then
(a_{1},\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition from our preceding discussions. Otherwise
\gamma\leq\delta(a_{1},K)
. If
\gamma\leq\delta(a_{i},K)
for all
i=0,\dotsc,n-1
, then
\overline{\nu}(a_{n}-a_{n-1})=\delta(a_{n-1},K)\geq\gamma
and hence
(a_{n},\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
. The fact that
a_{n}\in K
implies that
(a_{n},\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
. Otherwise, we can choose some
i\in\{0,\dotsc,n-1\}
such that
\gamma>\delta(a_{i},K)
and
i
is minimal with respect to this property. We conclude from our preceding arguments that
(a_{i},\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
. We have thus obtained the following result:

Theorem 3.9
.

Take a pair of definition
(a,\gamma)
for
\omega
. Assume that
(K,\nu)
is henselian and
(K(a)|K,\overline{\nu})
is a defectless extension. Take a complete distinguished chain
a=a_{0},a_{1},\dotsc,a_{n}
of
a
. Then
(a_{i},\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
for some
i\in\{0,\dotsc,n\}
.

We now show that two simultaneous extensions of
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
are closely related.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
\overline{\omega}
and
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}
be two extensions of
\omega
to
\overline{K}(x)
with minimal pairs of definition
(a,\gamma)
and
(a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
respectively. Take the minimal polynomials
Q(x)
and
Q^{\prime}(x)
of
a
and
a^{\prime}
over
K
. Then
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
,
\deg Q=\deg Q^{\prime}
and
\omega Q=\omega Q^{\prime}
.

Proof.

From Corollary
2.6
we have that
\nu_{Q}=\omega=\nu_{Q^{\prime}}
and
Q
and
Q^{\prime}
are key polynomials for
\omega
. Proposition 2.10 of [
References
] gives us that

\deg Q<\deg Q^{\prime}\Longrightarrow\delta(Q)<\delta(Q^{\prime})\Longrightarrow\nu_{Q}Q^{\prime}<\omega Q^{\prime}.

Then the fact that
\omega=\nu_{Q}
implies
\deg Q\geq\deg Q^{\prime}
. Similarly we obtain
\deg Q^{\prime}\geq\deg Q
and hence
\deg Q=\deg Q^{\prime}
. We can further observe that
\delta(Q)=\delta(Q^{\prime})
, that is,
\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. Again from Proposition 2.10
(iii)
of [

] we have,

\omega Q<\omega Q^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\nu_{Q}Q^{\prime}<\omega Q^{\prime}\Longleftrightarrow\delta(Q)<\delta(Q^{\prime}).

It follows that
\omega Q=\omega Q^{\prime}
.
∎

The following is Theorem 2.2 of [
References
]:

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
\overline{\omega}
and
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}
be two residue transcendental extensions of
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

\overline{\omega}|_{K(x)}=\overline{\omega^{\prime}}|_{K(x)}
,

(ii) there exist minimal pairs of definition

.

In the next theorem, we show that analogous results hold in the case of value transcendental extensions as well.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
\overline{\omega}
and
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}
be two value transcendental extensions of
\overline{\nu}
to

. Then the following are equivalent:

(ii) there exist minimal pairs of definition
(a,\gamma)
and
(a^{\prime},\gamma^{\prime})
for
\overline{\omega}
and
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}

\gamma=\gamma^{\prime}
. Further, for any polynomial

\overline{\nu}g(a)=\overline{\nu}g(a^{\prime})

We first prove the forward direction of the equivalence. Set

Proposition 4.2

Take two extensions

and
\overline{\omega^{\prime}}

\omega
to

Proof.

Take the minimal polynomials

and

of
a
and
a^{\prime}
over

\overline{\omega}K(a)\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\omega Q=\omega K(x)=\overline{\omega^{\prime}}K(a^{\prime})\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}\omega Q

We now assume that

in

9.
Connection with pseudo-Cauchy sequences

We now explore the relationship between valuation transcendental extensions and pseudo-Cauchy sequences. Recall that we are working under the assumtions that
\omega
is a valuation transcendental extension of
\nu
to
K(x)
,
\overline{\nu}
is an extension of
\nu
to
\overline{K}
and
\overline{\omega}
is a common extension of
\omega
and
\overline{\nu}
to
\overline{K}(x)
. We first mention the following general result.

Lemma 9.1
.

Take a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
in
(K,\nu)
without a limit in
K
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i) Assume that
f(x)\in K[x]
is such that
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately monotonically increasing. Then at least one root of
f
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.

(ii) Assume that
c\in\overline{K}
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
. Take the minimal polynomial
g(x)
of
c
over
K
. Then
(\nu g(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately monotonically increasing.

Proof.

Write
f(x)=b\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-a_{i})
where
a_{i}\in\overline{K}
and
b\in K
. Then
\nu f(z_{\mu})=\nu b+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\nu}(z_{\mu}-a_{i})
. If none of the
a_{i}
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
, then it follows from Proposition
2.2
that
(\overline{\nu}(a_{i}-z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant for all
i
. It follows that
(\nu f(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately constant which is a contradiction. So at least one root of
f
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.

We now assume that
c\in\overline{K}
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
. By definition,
(\overline{\nu}(c-z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is a monotonically increasing sequence. Write
g(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(x-c_{i})
. Then
\nu g(z_{\mu})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\overline{\nu}(z_{\mu}-c_{i})
. We observe from Proposition
2.2
that
(\overline{\nu}(c_{i}-z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is monotonically increasing whenever
c_{i}
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
, and the sequence is ultimately constant otherwise. It follows that
(\nu g(z_{\mu}))_{\mu<\lambda}
is ultimately monotonically increasing.
∎

Theorem 9.2
.

Take a pseudo-Cauchy sequence
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
in
(K,\nu)
without a limit in
K
. Assume that
x
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
. Then the following statements hold true:

(i)
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
is a pseudo-Cauchy sequence of algebraic type.

(ii) Whenever
(a,\gamma)
is a pair of definition for
\omega
, we have that
a
is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.

(iii) Let
Q(x)\in K[x]
be a nonlinear key polynomial for
\omega
with a maximal root
b\in\overline{K}
. Then
b
is also a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
.

(iv) Take an associated minimal polynomial
m(x)\in K[x]
of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
. Then

is a key polynomial for
\omega
.

Conversely, assume that
(a,\gamma)
is a minimal pair of definition for
\omega
and that

is a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}
. Then
x
is also a limit of
(z_{\mu})_{\mu<\lambda}

Proof.

Set
\gamma_{\mu}:=\nu(z_{\mu}-z_{\mu+1})
for each
\mu<\lambda

\mu<\lambda

References

Let
(a,\gamma)
be a pair of definition for
\omega

\overline{\omega}(x-a)=\gamma=\max\overline{\omega}(x-\overline{K})

for each
\mu<\lambda

\gamma_{\mu}\in\omega(x-K)\subseteq\overline{\omega}(x-\overline{K})

for each

for each

.

.

Conversely, assume that

\overline{\nu}(a-z_{\mu})=\gamma_{\mu}

\overline{\nu}(x-z_{\mu})=\gamma_{\mu}

∎

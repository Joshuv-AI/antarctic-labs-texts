Abstract
Kawaler et al. 1985 present a variational expression for the eigenfrequencies associated with stellar oscillations. We highlight and correct a typographical error in the weight functions appearing in these expressions, and validate the correction numerically.



I
Introduction

Under the assumption of a vanishing surface density, the equations
governing linear, adiabatic, non-radial stellar oscillations are
self-adjoint and therefore obey a variational principle
(
Unno et al. 1989
, see,
e.g.)
. Variational expressions for mode
eigenfrequencies serve as a valuable computational check on numerical
solutions to the oscillation equations. Via so-called weight
functions, they also quantify how eigenfrequencies are sensitive to
different parts of a star’s internal structure; they are therefore key
tools in determining the asteroseismic probing potential of each mode.

Kawaler et al. 1985
present a variational expression that has been
adopted in many studies of stellar oscillations

(
Kawaler et al. 1986
;
Brassard et al. 1992a
;
Brassard et al. 1992b
;
Córsico & Benvenuto 2002
;
Timmes et al. 2018
, e.g.,)
. In
the present paper we highlight and correct a typographical error in
one of the weight functions appearing in their expression, and use
numerical calculations to demonstrate the validity of the correction.

II
Weight Functions

The
Kawaler et al. 1985
variational expression for the eigenfrequency

\sigma(\mathbf{y})
of a mode is

\sigma^{2}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{\int_{0}^{R}\left[\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y},r)+\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y},r)+\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{y},r)\right]\rho r^{2}\operatorname{d}\!{r}}{\int_{0}^{R}\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{y},r)\rho r^{2}\operatorname{d}\!{r}}.

(1)

Here,
\mathbf{y}(r)=\{y_{1}(r),\ldots,y_{4}(r)\}
is the vector of
eigenfunctions introduced by
Dziembowski 1971
,
r
is the
radial coordinate (with
R
the stellar radius), and
\rho
the
density. The three weight functions are

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}(\mathbf{y},r)

\displaystyle=g^{2}\ell(\ell+1)S_{\ell}^{-2}(y_{2}-y_{3})^{2},

(2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{y},r)

\displaystyle=r^{2}N^{2}y_{1}^{2},

(3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{y},r)

\displaystyle=-\frac{gr}{U}\left[y_{4}+\ell(\ell+1)y_{3}\right]^{2}.

(4)

where
\ell
is the model harmonic degree,
g
is the gravity,
N
the
Brunt-Väisälä frequency,
S_{\ell}
the Lamb frequency, and
U
the usual
homology invariant. The function

\mathcal{T}(\mathbf{y},r)=r^{2}\left[y_{1}^{2}+\ell(\ell+1)\left(\frac{g}{r\sigma^{2}}\right)^{2}y_{2}^{2}\right]

(5)

appearing in the denominator is proportional to the differential inertia of the mode.

By re-deriving these expressions from equations (13.13) and (13.15) of

Unno et al. 1979
1
1

1

The equivalent equations in

Unno et al. 1989
are (14.16) and (14.19).
, we find that the correct
expression for
\mathcal{G}
is

\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{y},r)=-\frac{gr}{U}\left[y_{4}+(\ell+1)y_{3}\right]^{2};

(6)

comparing this against equation (
4
), there is an extra factor of

\ell
multiplying the
y_{3}
term in the latter. We have confirmed that this error is typographical in nature; the spurious factor was not included in the calculations presented in
Kawaler et al. 1985
and follow-up papers by the same authors.

Numerical Validation

To validate our analysis we explore how well equation (
1
)
can reproduce the numerical eigenfrequencies of an
n=3
polytrope

\mathcal{G}

(
Townsend & Teitler 2013

Townsend et al. 2018

Goldstein & Townsend 2020

to calculate adiabatic eigenfrequencies and eigenfunctions for low-radial order,

\ell=0,\ldots,4

VACUUM

boundary condition (where the Lagrangian pressure perturbation
vanishes at the surface) is adopted, and the oscillation equations are
discretized using the
COLLOC_GL2
(second-order
Gauss-Legendre collocation) scheme on a radial grid of
\sim$1000$
points.

For each mode found we evaluate the relative frequency error

\Delta=\left|\frac{\sigma(\mathbf{y})-\sigma}{\sigma}\right|,

(7)

where
\sigma
is the numerical eigenfrequency reported by
GYRE. Figure
1
plots
\Delta
as a function of mode
radial order
n_{\rm pg}

(
Unno et al. 1989
, defined in the Eckart-Osaki-Scuflaire
classification scheme; see)
, for calculations based on
the original (equation
4
) and corrected
(equation
6
) expressions for
\mathcal{G}
. In the former case
(left panel) the errors are significant for
\ell\geq 2
modes,
reaching
\Delta\gtrsim 0.1

|n_{\rm pg}|

(right panel) the frequency errors are small for all modes,
\Delta\lesssim${10}^{-4}$

The original expression for
\mathcal{G}
happens to yield small
\Delta
for
both radial (
\ell=0
) and dipole (
\ell=1

\ell=1

spurious factor of
\ell

4

to the value of
\mathcal{G}

\ell=0

\delta\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{y})=\frac{gr}{U}y_{3}\left(y_{3}+2y_{4}\right)

(8)

\mathcal{G}

1

V_{r}(P_{r},Q_{r})^{2}=\left(\frac{Q_{r}}{P_{r}}\right)^{2r}V_{r}\left(\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{Q_{r}},\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{Q_{r}}\right)^{2}=Q\left(V_{r}\left(\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{Q_{r}}-2,1\right)+2\right)=P^{2}.

Therefore, we have

(4.0)

V_{r}(P_{r},Q_{r})=\pm P

If
V_{r}(P_{r},Q_{r})=P
,
(i)
holds. Suppose that
V_{r}(P_{r},Q_{r})=-P
.
If
r
is odd, (
i
) holds by replacing
P_{r}
with
-P_{r}
.
If

,

is also equal to
\frac{P^{2}}{Q}-2
. We take

to satisfy
\frac{P_{r}^{\prime 2}}{Q_{r}}-2=-x
. In the same way as in the proof of
(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
, we have

(4.0)

V_{r}(P_{r}^{\prime},Q_{r})=\left(\frac{Q_{r}}{P_{r}^{\prime}}\right)^{r}V_{r}\left(\frac{P_{r}^{\prime 2}}{Q_{r}},\frac{P_{r}^{\prime 2}}{Q_{r}}\right),

also. However, since
\frac{P_{r}^{\prime 2}}{Q_{r}}-2=-(\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{Q_{r}}-2)
,
V_{2}(P_{r}^{\prime},Q_{r})=P_{r}^{\prime 2}-2Q_{r}=-(P_{r}^{2}-2Q_{r})=-V_{2}(P_{r},Q_{r})
. Thus (
i
) holds by replacing
P_{r}
with
P_{r}^{\prime}
. Note that if
r=2

such that
(i)
holds and
\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{Q_{r}}-2=\pm x
.

When
r

) with

(4.0)

V_{2k}(P,Q)=V_{2}(V_{k}(P,Q),Q^{k})

(4.0)

\frac{P^{2}}{Q}-2=V_{k}(x,1)=V_{2}(V_{k}(x,1),1).

From the case

We prove Theorem

by reducing it to Theorem

.
In this section,

Lemma 5.1
.

(5.0)

V_{nm}(P,Q)=V_{n}(V_{m}(P,Q),Q^{m})

Proof.

The cases

and

are trivial, so it is sufficient to verify that

From (
2.0
) with replacing

(5.0)

V_{(n+1)m}=V_{m}V_{nm}-Q^{m}V_{(n-1)m}.

It is compatible with the difference equation of

.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

The period of
V_{n}(P,1)
modulo
p
divides
p-\epsilon
. That is,

V_{n+(p-\epsilon)}(P,1)\equiv V_{n}(P,1)\mod p

for any index
n
.

Proof.

Since
V_{0}=2
and
V_{1}=P
, it is sufficient to prove
V_{p-\epsilon}=2
and

.
From (
2.0
),

.
From (
2.0
) with

, we have

(5.0)

V_{n}=U_{n+1}-QU_{n-1}=PU_{n}-2QU_{n-1}=2U_{n+1}-PU_{n}.

\epsilon=1
, then

, since

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
h
be an integer co-prime to
p-\epsilon
. Then, the map
\mathbb{F}_{p}\to\mathbb{F}_{p}

(x\mapsto V_{h}(x,1))
is bijective.

Proof.

Since
h
is co-prime to

, there exists

such that

. From Lemmas
5.1
,

(5.0)

Therefore,

x\mapsto V_{h}(x,1)
. Thus, the map
x\mapsto V_{h}(x,1)

∎

Theorem

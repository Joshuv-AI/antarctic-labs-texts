Finitely generated saturated multi-Rees algebras
Suprajo Das, Sudeshna Roy

Abstract
. We study the question of finite generation of saturated multi-Rees algebras and investigate the asymptotic behaviour of related length functions. In the setup of excellent local domains, we show that the saturated multi-Rees algebra of a finite collection of ideals is finitely generated when the analytic spread is not maximal and the associated length function eventually agrees with a polynomial. Similar results are obtained when we restrict to two-dimensional local UFDs with no restrictions on the analytic spread. We further prove that the saturated multi-Rees algebra of finitely many monomial ideals in a polynomial ring modulo an irreducible monomial ideal, is always finitely generated. In this case, the corresponding length function is shown to exhibit piecewise quasi-polynomial behaviour. We also produce multi-ideal versions of a theorem of Amao.

Introduction

The inspiration of this manuscript mainly comes from three sources:
[
CHS10
]
by Cutkosky, Herzog and Srinivasan,
[
HPV08
]
by Herzog, Puthenpurakal and Verma and
[
HHT07
]
by Herzog, Hibi and Trung. Here we produce multi-ideal versions of some of the results obtained in the aforementioned papers.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local ring (or a standard graded Noetherian ring with graded maximal ideal) of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are ideals in
R
. We are interested in studying the asymptotic behaviour of the function
l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{r}^{n_{r}}\right)\right)
, where
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\in\mathbb{N}
. In Theorem
3.1
we show that this length function is bounded above by a numerical polynomial in
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
of total degree at most
d
. If we only consider a single ideal
I
then it is known previously that

(1)

\varepsilon(I):=\limsup\limits_{n\to\infty}\dfrac{l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/I^{n}\right)\right)}{n^{d}/d!}<\infty,

which is a special case of our result. Katz and Validashti
[
KV10
]
calls this invariant
\varepsilon(I)
the
epsilon multiplicity
of the ideal
I
. Now it begs the question if it is possible to define ‘
mixed
’ versions of the epsilon multiplicity.

The associated length function
l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/I^{n}\right)\right)
can exhibit very mysterious behaviour. An example constructed in
[
CHST05
]
shows that the invariant
\varepsilon(I)
can be an irrational number. In general, the sequence
\left\{l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/I^{n}\right)\right)\right\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
does not even have quasi-polynomial growth eventually and hence cannot arise from a finitely generated graded module. A very interesting question is to decide whether the
\limsup
in (
1
) can be replaced by a limit. Cutkosky
[
Cut13
]
has given a positive answer to this question when
R
is analytically unramified. A related problem is to describe instances where this limit can be a rational number. It turns out that if the saturated Rees algebra

S_{m_{R}}(I):=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}(I^{n}\colon_{R}m_{R}^{\infty})

is finitely generated then
\varepsilon(I)
is rational. However, the saturated Rees algebra need not always be finitely generated. More generally, we consider the
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded
R
-algebra

S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})=\bigoplus\limits_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\left(I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{r}^{n_{r}}\colon_{R}J^{\infty}\right)

where
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r},J
are ideals in
R
. We say that
S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
is the
saturated multi-Rees algebra
of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
with respect to
J
. If
J=m_{R}
then
S_{m_{R}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
will be called the
saturated multi-Rees algebra
of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
.

The fundamental difficulty in working with multigraded Noetherian algebras is that it might not contain a standard multigraded Veronese subalgebra which is also Noetherian. It is worth mentioning that the results in Sections
4
,
5
and
7
of this manuscript have a ‘
diagonal
’ flavour. We now describe certain situations when

is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded
R
-algebra.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is an excellent local domain of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r},J
are ideals in
R
. Assume that the analytic spread
\ell({I_{1}}_{P}\cdots{I_{r}}_{P})<\dim R_{P}

I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}

for all
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}\gg 0
.

The first statement of Theorem
1.1
was already proven for

by Cutkosky, Herzog and Srinivasan in
[
CHS10
]
. By generalizing their proof we show that

is contained inside the integral closure of the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})

S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
is a finite module over
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
, see Theorem
5.1

An immediate consequence of Theorem

for details. The next result concerns the saturated multi-Rees algebra of monomial ideals in a polynomial ring modulo an irreducible monomial ideal.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
R=K[X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}]

We shall prove this result in Section

. In the case

,

1.2
and Corollary

generalies these existing results but our methods differ from

]
and
[
HPV08
]
. We use a projection argument to obtain the first assertion of our theorem. For proving the second assertion we invoke a result from

In section

, we concern ourselves with two-dimensional local domains. More precisely, we show that if the ambient ring

2.
Notations and Preliminaries

We begin by fixing some notations and by recalling some basic facts about multi-Rees rings.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers including zero. Vectors in
\mathbb{Z}^{r}
will be written by bold-faced letters, e.g.,
\mathbf{a},\mathbf{n},\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{h},\ldots
. The norm of a multi-index
\mathbf{n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{Z}^{r}
is
|\mathbf{n}|=n_{1}+\cdots+n_{r}
. We denote by
\mathbf{0}
and
\mathbf{1}
the vectors
(0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
(1,\ldots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
respectively. For every
i=1,\ldots,r
let
\mathbf{e}_{i}
denote the standard unit vector in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
with
1
at the
i
-th spot and zeros elsewhere. If
m_{i}>n_{i}
(resp.
m_{i}\geq n_{i}
) for all
i=1,\ldots,r
then we write
\mathbf{m}>\mathbf{n}
(resp.
\mathbf{m}\geq\mathbf{n}
). If
n_{i}\gg 0
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
then we write
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
. For
\mathbf{i},\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we denote by
\mathbf{n}^{\mathbf{i}}=n_{1}^{i_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{i_{r}}
.

Let
S=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}S_{\mathbf{n}}
be an
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded ring. If
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
s\in S_{\mathbf{n}}
, we say that
s
is
homogeneous
of degree
\mathbf{n}
. We say that
S
is a
standard

\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded
S_{\mathbf{0}}
-algebra if
S
is generated over
S_{\mathbf{0}}
by elements in
S_{\mathbf{e}_{1}},\ldots,S_{\mathbf{e}_{r}}
.

Suppose that
R
is a ring,
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are ideals in
R
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
are variables over
R
. Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we write
\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}=I_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots I_{r}^{n_{r}}
and
\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}=t_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots t_{r}^{n_{r}}
for short. The
multi-Rees algebra of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
is defined as

\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}):=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}

and
\overline{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}
denotes the integral closure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
in its total ring of fractions. If we further assume that
R
is a Noetherian local ring with unique maximal ideal
m_{R}
then the analytic spread
\ell(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
is defined as

\ell(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}):=\dim\left(\dfrac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}{m_{R}\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}\right)=\dim\left(\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\dfrac{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}{m_{R}\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}\right).

Given an ideal
I\subseteq R
, let
\overline{I}
denote the integral closure of
I
in
R
.

3.
Multigraded Rees algebras and Hilbert functions

The following result is well-known and describes the integral closure of multi-Rees algebras. This is a multi-ideal version of
[
HS06
, Proposition 5.2.1]
and
[
HS06
, Proposition 5.2.4]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
R
is a ring,
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are ideals in
R
and
t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}
are variables over
R
. Then the integral closure of the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
in
R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
equals the
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded ring

\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Let
\overline{R}
denote the integral closure of
R
in its total ring of fractions. Then the integral closure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
in its total ring of fractions equals the
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded ring

\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\overline{R}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

Proof.

Let
S
denote the integral closure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
in
R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
. By
[
HS06
, Theorem 2.3.2]

S
is an
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded submodule of
R[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
. Let
s\in S_{\mathbf{n}}
where
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Write
s=s_{\mathbf{n}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}
for some
s_{\mathbf{n}}\in R
. As
s
is integral over
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
, there exists an integer
e>0
and
a_{i}\in\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
for all
i=1,\ldots,e,
such that

s_{\mathbf{n}}^{e}\mathbf{t}^{e\cdot\mathbf{n}}+a_{1}s_{\mathbf{n}}^{e-1}\mathbf{t}^{(e-1)\cdot\mathbf{n}}+\cdots+a_{e}=0.

Expand each

a_{i}=\sum_{\mathbf{j}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}a_{i,\mathbf{j}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{j}}

as a finite sum with
a_{i,\mathbf{j}}\in\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{j}}
. The homogeneous part of degree
e\cdot\mathbf{n}
in the equation above is exactly

\mathbf{t}^{e\cdot\mathbf{n}}\left(s_{\mathbf{n}}^{e}+a_{1,\mathbf{n}}s_{\mathbf{n}}^{e-1}+\cdots+a_{e,e\cdot\mathbf{n}}\right)=0.

As
a_{i,i\cdot\mathbf{n}}\in\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)^{i}
, this equation says that
s_{\mathbf{n}}
is integral over the ideal
\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}
. The other inclusion is easy to prove. This proves the first statement of the lemma.

For the proof of the second statement, observe that for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we have
\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\overline{R}}=\overline{\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}\;\overline{R}}
. The integral closure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
in its total ring of fractions clearly contains
\overline{R}[I_{1}\overline{R}t_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\overline{R}t_{r}]
. By the first statement of the lemma, we know that integral closure of
\overline{R}[I_{1}\overline{R}t_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\overline{R}t_{r}]
in
\overline{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
is

\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\overline{R}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}.

But
\overline{R}[t_{1},\ldots,t_{r}]
is integrally closed in its total ring of fractions, so that the integral closure of
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
is as displayed above.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

[
Gho16
, Lemma 3.4]

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local ring,
A=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}A_{\mathbf{n}}
is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded Noetherian
R
-algebra with
A_{\mathbf{0}}=R
and
M=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}M_{\mathbf{n}}
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded
A
-module. There exists a vector
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that
\mathrm{Ann}_{R}M_{\mathbf{n}}=\mathrm{Ann}_{R}M_{\mathbf{h}}
for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. In particular,
\dim M_{\mathbf{n}}=\dim M_{\mathbf{h}}
for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
.

In the setup of Proposition
3.1
,
t:=\dim_{R}M_{\mathbf{h}}
is called the
limit dimension
of the
R
-modules
M_{\mathbf{n}}
. The Hilbert-Samuel multiplicity of
M_{\mathbf{n}}
is given by

e(M_{\mathbf{n}}):=\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\dfrac{l_{R}\left(M_{\mathbf{n}}/m_{R}^{k}M_{\mathbf{n}}\right)}{k^{t}/t!}.

If
t=0
then the modules
M_{\mathbf{n}}
eventually become finite length
R
-modules. In that case we have
e(M_{\mathbf{n}})=l_{R}(M_{\mathbf{n}})
.

The following result generalises an observation due to Katz and Validashti
[
KV10
, Observation 4.1]
for multiple ideals.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are ideals in
R
. Then there exists a numerical polynomial
F\in\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
of total degree at most
d
such that

l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)\leq F(\mathbf{n})

for all
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
.

Proof.

Given an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and an integer
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we define

\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k):=\prod_{i=k}^{r}I_{i}^{n_{i}}.

Also set
\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},r+1):=R
and notice that
\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k)=I_{k}^{n_{k}}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)
for all
1\leq k\leq r
. Consider the chain of inclusions of the ideals

\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},1)\subseteq I_{1}^{n_{1}-1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},2)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},2)\subseteq I_{2}^{n_{2}-1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},3)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},r)\subseteq\cdots\subseteq I_{r}\subseteq R.

The subadditivity of
l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(-\right)\right)
on short exact sequences shows that

(2)

l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{r}\sum_{t=0}^{n_{k}-1}l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\dfrac{I_{k}^{t}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{I_{k}^{t+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}\right)\right).

Consider the
\mathbb{N}^{r-k+1}
-graded multi-Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}_{k}:=\bigoplus_{\mathbf{m}_{k}:=(m_{k},\ldots,m_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r-k+1}}I_{k}^{m_{k}}\cdots I_{r}^{m_{r}}

of the ideals
I_{k},\ldots,I_{r}
and the associated graded ring
\mathcal{G}_{k}:=\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
. Then
H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{k}\right)
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r-k+1}
-graded
\mathcal{G}_{k}
-module which is annihilated by
m_{R}^{c}
for some integer
c>0
. Therefore,
H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{k}\right)
can be regarded as a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r-k+1}
-graded module over
\mathcal{G}_{k}/m_{R}^{c}\mathcal{G}_{k}
, which is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{r-k+1}
-graded Noetherian algebra over the Artinian local ring
R/(m_{R}^{c}+I_{k})
. It then follows from
[
HHRT97
, Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
4.1
]
that there exists a numerical polynomial
P_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}[m_{k},\ldots,m_{r}]
of total degree at most
\dim\mathcal{G}_{k}-(r-k+1)\leq d-1
such that

l_{R}\left(\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\mathcal{G}_{k}\right)\right)_{\mathbf{m}_{k}}\right)=l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\dfrac{I_{k}^{m_{k}}\cdots I_{r}^{m_{r}}}{I_{k}^{m_{k}+1}I_{k+1}^{m_{k+1}}\cdots I_{r}^{m_{r}}}\right)\right)=P_{k}\big(m_{k},\ldots,m_{r}\big)

for all
m_{k},\ldots,m_{r}\gg 0
. Consider the sum

F_{k}(n_{k},\ldots,n_{r}):=\sum\limits_{t=0}^{n_{k}-1}l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\dfrac{I_{k}^{t}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{I_{k}^{t+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}\right)\right)

and observe that the difference function

F_{k}(n_{k},\ldots,n_{r})-F_{k}(n_{k}-1,n_{k+1},\ldots,n_{r})=l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(\dfrac{I_{k}^{n_{k}-1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{I_{k}^{n_{k}}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}\right)\right)

eventually agrees with the numerical polynomial
P_{k}(n_{k},\cdots,n_{r})
having total degree at most
d-1
. We now apply
[
CN08
, Proposition
2.3.6
]
to conclude that each
F_{k}(n_{k},\ldots,n_{r})
is eventually given by a numerical polynomial in
n_{k},\ldots,n_{r}
of total degree at most
d
. Finally we use (
2
) to reach our desired conclusion.
∎

4.
Multigraded generalisations of a theorem of Amao

The following theorem extends Amao’s result
[
Ama76
, Theorem 3.2]
to a finite number of ideals.

Theorem 4.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and
I_{1}\subseteq J_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subseteq J_{r}
are ideals in
R
such that
l_{R}(J_{i}/I_{i})<\infty
for all
i=1,\ldots,r
. Then the multigraded length function
l_{R}\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
of total degree at most
d
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. In particular,

\mathrm{total\;degree}\;l_{R}\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\mathrm{deg}\;l_{R}\left(\left(J_{1}\cdots J_{r}\right)^{n}/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)\leq d.

Proof.

Given an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and an integer
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we define

\mathbb{J}(k,\mathbf{n}):=\prod_{j=1}^{k}J_{j}^{n_{j}}\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k):=\prod_{i=k}^{r}I_{i}^{n_{i}}.

Also set
\mathbb{J}(0,\mathbf{n}):=R
and
\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},r+1):=R
. There exist inclusion of ideals

\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{t-1}I_{k}^{n_{k}-t+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)\subseteq\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{t}I_{k}^{n_{k}-t}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)

for each
k=1,\ldots,r
and for all
t=1,\ldots,n_{k}
. Using the additivity of lengths on short exact sequences, we can write

(3)

l_{R}\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{r}\sum\limits_{t=1}^{n_{k}}l_{R}\left(\dfrac{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{t}I_{k}^{n_{k}-t}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{t-1}I_{k}^{n_{k}-t+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}\right).

Given
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r},(u,v)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, we define an
(r+1)
-tuple by

\left(\mathbf{n}(k),u,v\right):=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{k-1},u,v,n_{k+1},\ldots,n_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}.

For each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
we consider the
\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
-graded multi-Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}_{k}:=\bigoplus_{\left(\mathbf{n}(k),u,v\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}}\mathbb{J}(\mathbf{n},k-1)J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{v}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)

of
r+1
ideals
J_{1},\ldots,J_{k},I_{k},\ldots,I_{r}
. Also consider the
\mathbb{N}^{r+1}
-graded ideals

\displaystyle I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(\mathbf{n}(k),u,v\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1}\\
u\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u-1}I_{k}^{v+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1),

\displaystyle J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}

\displaystyle=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(\mathbf{n}(k),u,v\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1},\\
u\geq 1\end{subarray}}\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{v}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)

of
\mathcal{R}_{k}
and their quotient

J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}\left(\mathbf{n}(k),u,v\right)\in\mathbb{N}^{r+1},\\
u\geq 1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{v}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u-1}I_{k}^{v+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}.

We now impose an
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-grading on
\mathcal{R}_{k}
by setting

\left(\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)_{\mathbf{n}}:=\bigoplus\limits_{u+v=n_{k}}\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{v}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)

and
\mathcal{R}_{k}
becomes a standard
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded Noetherian
R
-algebra with this new grading. Observe that
J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
becomes a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded
\mathcal{R}_{k}
-module, where the
\mathbf{n}
-th component of
J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
is given by

\left(J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}\right)_{\mathbf{n}}:=\bigoplus\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=n_{k},\\
u\geq 1\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{v}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u-1}I_{k}^{v+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}.

Moreover, each component of
J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
is an
R
-module of finite length. Therefore
J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
is annihilated by
m_{R}^{c}
for some integer
c>0
. So
J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}/I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}
can be regarded as a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over
\mathcal{R}_{k}/m_{R}^{c}\mathcal{R}_{k}
and the latter is a standard
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded Noetherian algebra over the Artinian local ring
R/m_{R}^{c}
. From
[
HHRT97
, Theorem
4.1
]
we know that for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
, there exists a numerical polynomial
F_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
such that

l_{R}\left(\left(\dfrac{J_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}}{I_{k}\mathcal{R}_{k}}\right)_{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\sum\limits_{u=1}^{n_{k}}l_{R}\left(\dfrac{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u}I_{k}^{n_{k}-u}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}{\mathbb{J}(k-1,\mathbf{n})J_{k}^{u-1}I_{k}^{n_{k}-u+1}\mathbb{I}(\mathbf{n},k+1)}\right)=F_{k}(\mathbf{n})

for all
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. From (
3
) it now follows that our desired length function
l_{R}\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
agrees with the numerical polynomial
F(\mathbf{n}):=\sum_{k=1}^{r}F_{k}(\mathbf{n})
for all
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
and all monomials of highest degree in
F(\mathbf{n})
also have nonnegative coefficients. From Amao’s theorem
[
Ama76
, Theorem 3.2]
we know that there exists a numerical polynomial
G(n)\in\mathbb{Q}[n]
of degree at most
d
such that

l_{R}\left(\left(J_{1}\cdots J_{r}\right)^{n}/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)=G(n)

for all
n\gg 0
. The last assertion then follows from the observation that
F(n,\ldots,n)=G(n)
for all
n\gg 0
.
∎

The next lemma ensures existence of limit dimensions of certain modules, also see
[
Ciu15
, Lemma
2.2
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
R
is a Noetherian ring and
I_{1}\subseteq J_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subseteq J_{r}
are ideals in
R
. Then there exists an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that

\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}=\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{h}}\right)}

for all
r
-tuples
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}
then
x^{c}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\subset\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}
for some integer
c>0
. Therefore

x^{2c}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}=x^{c}x^{c}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}J_{i}\subseteq x^{c}\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}J_{i}\subseteq x^{c}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}I_{i}\subseteq\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}I_{i}=\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}+\mathbf{e}_{i}}

for all
i=1,\ldots,r
. In other words,
\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}\subseteq\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\right)}
whenever
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\geq\mathbf{n}
. Now consider the set of ideals

\Lambda:=\left\{\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}\mid\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\right\}

which is partially ordered by inclusion. Since
R
is Noetherian, the set
\Lambda
has a maximal element with respect to inclusion. It can be easily verified that the maximal element of
\Lambda
is unique as
\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}\subseteq\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}^{\prime}}\right)}
whenever
\mathbf{n}^{\prime}\geq\mathbf{n}
. Let
\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{h}}\right)}
be the maximum element of
\Lambda
and our result follows.
∎

The following result further generalises Theorem
4.1
by producing a multigraded version of
[
Ciu15
, Proposition
2.4
]
or
[
HPV08
, Proposition
2.1
]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local ring of dimension
d
and
I_{1}\subseteq J_{1},\ldots,I_{r}\subseteq J_{r}
are ideals in
R
. In view of Lemma
4.1
, set

t:=\lim\limits_{\mathbf{n}\to\infty}\dim\dfrac{R}{\sqrt{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}}}=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dim\dfrac{R}{\sqrt{(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})^{n}\colon_{R}(J_{1}\cdots J_{r})^{n}}}.

Then the multigraded multiplicity function
e\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
of total degree at most
d-t
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. In particular,

\mathrm{total\;degree}\;e\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\mathrm{deg}\;e\left(\left(J_{1}\cdots J_{r}\right)^{n}/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)\leq d-t.

Proof.

From Lemma
4.1
we know that there exists an
r
-tuple
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that
\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)}=\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{h}}\right)}
for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. Then
t=\dim\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. Using the associativity formula, for
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
we have

e\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in V\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{h}}\right),\\
\dim R/P=t\end{subarray}}e(R/P)\cdot l_{R_{P}}\left({\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right).

For every prime ideal
P
which appears in the above sum, the
R_{P}
-module
{\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}
has finite length, and by Theorem
4.1
it follows that
l_{R_{P}}\left({\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial
F_{P}(\mathbf{n})
of total degree at most
\dim R_{P}
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. Then
e\left(\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{n}}/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
is eventually given by a numerical polynomial
F(\mathbf{n})
of total degree at most

\max\left\{\dim R_{P}\mid P\in V\left(\sqrt{\left(\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\colon_{R}\mathbf{J}^{\mathbf{h}}\right)}\right)\;\text{and}\;\dim R/P=t\right\}\leq d-t.

As each
e(R/P)>0
so every monomial of highest degree occuring in
F(\mathbf{n})
has nonnegative coefficient. From
[
Ciu15
, Proposition
2.4
]
or
[
HPV08
, Proposition
2.1
]
we know that there exists a numerical polynomial
G(n)\in\mathbb{Q}[n]
of degree at most
d-t
such that

e\left(\left(J_{1}\cdots J_{r}\right)^{n}/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)=G(n)

for all
n\gg 0
. The last claim then follows from the observation that
F(n,\ldots,n)=G(n)
for all
n\gg 0
.
∎

5.
Saturated multi-Rees algebras of ideals with low analytic spread

The following result subsumes Theorem
1.1
. This can be considered as a multi-ideal version of
[
CHS10
, Theorem
2.1
]
due to Cutkosky, Herzog and Srinivasan. We recall the definitions of saturated multi-Rees algebra and limit dimension as described in the Introduction and Proposition
3.1
respectively.

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is an excellent local domain of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r},J
are ideals in
R
. Assume that the analytic spread
\ell({I_{1}}_{P}\cdots{I_{r}}_{P})<\dim R_{P}
for all
P\in V(J)
. Then the following statements are true.

(a)

The saturated multi-Rees algebra
S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
of
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
with respect to
J
, is an
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded Noetherian
R
-algebra and also a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
.

(b)

In view of Proposition
3.1
, set

t:=\lim\limits_{\mathbf{n}\to\infty}\dim_{R}H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\dim_{R}H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right).

Then the multigraded multiplicity function
e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. Moreover,

\displaystyle\mathrm{total\;degree}\;e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{deg}\;e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq\min\left\{d-t-2,\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})+t-1\right\}.

Proof.

(a)
Since
(0)\colon_{R}J^{\infty}=(0)
, we may assume without any loss of generality that
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are all nonzero ideals. It suffices to show that there are
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded inclusions

\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\subset S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})\subset\overline{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\overline{R}}\mathbf{t}^{\mathbf{n}}

and the last equality is a consequence of Lemma
3.1
. Since

\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}J^{\infty}\subset\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}\colon_{\overline{R}}J^{\infty}\overline{R}

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, therefore it is enough to show that

\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}\colon_{\overline{R}}J^{\infty}\overline{R}=\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. We now claim that

(4)

\mathrm{grade}(J\overline{R},\overline{R}):=\inf\left\{\mathrm{depth}\;\overline{R}_{P}\mid P\in V(J\overline{R})\right\}\geq 2.

Let
P\in V(J\overline{R})
and
Q=P\cap R
. Then
Q\in V(J)
, and our assumptions imply that
\dim R_{Q}\geq\ell({I_{1}}_{Q}\cdots{I_{r}}_{Q})+1\geq 2
. As
R/Q\hookrightarrow\overline{R}/P
is an integral extension, we obtain that
\dim R/Q=\dim\overline{R}/P
. Hence, since
R
is an excellent domain, we get

\dim\overline{R}_{P}=\dim\overline{R}-\dim\overline{R}/P=\dim R-\dim R/Q=\dim R_{Q}\geq 2.

The ring
\overline{R}
being normal, satisfies Serre’s
S_{2}
criterion. Altogether we see that

\mathrm{depth}\;\overline{R}_{P}\geq\inf\{2,\dim\overline{R}_{P}\}\geq 2

for all
P\in V(J\overline{R})
and conclude that
\mathrm{grade}(J\overline{R},R)\geq 2
. Let
A=\overline{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}
and we further claim that

(5)

\mathrm{grade}(JA,A):=\inf\left\{\mathrm{depth}\;A_{P}\mid P\in V(JA)\right\}\geq 2.

Again, since
A
is normal, it satisfies Serre’s criterion
S_{2}
, and it remains to be shown that
\dim A_{P}\geq 2
for all
P\in V(JA)
. Let
Q=P\cap R
. Then
Q\in V(J)
. Localizing at
Q
we may assume that
Q=m_{R}
. Since
R
is excellent, we have

\dim A_{P}=\dim A-\dim A/P.

The excellent property ensures that
A
is a finite
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
-module and from the discussions in
[
HHRT97
]
, it follows that

\dim A=\dim\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})=\dim R+r.

Using
[
BAM20
, Corollary 3.10]
we see that

\dim\dfrac{A}{P}\leq\dim\dfrac{A}{QA}=\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}{m_{R}\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}=\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})+r-1.

The hypothesis
\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})\leq d-1
implies that

\dim A_{P}\geq(d+r)-(\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})+r-1)\geq 2.

From (
4
) we see that
\mathrm{grade}(J\overline{R},\overline{R})\geq 2
. This implies that
H^{0}_{J\overline{R}}(\overline{R})=H^{1}_{J\overline{R}}(\overline{R})=0
(see
[
BS12
, Theorem 6.2.7]
), and hence

\genfrac{}{}{0.75pt}{}{\overline{\II^{\nn} \;\overline{R}} \colon_{\overline{R}} J^{\infty}\overline{R}}{\overline{\II^{\nn} \;\overline{R}}}\cong H^{0}_{J\overline{R}}\left(\genfrac{}{}{0.75pt}{}{\overline{R}}{\overline{\II^{\nn} \;\overline{R}}}\right)\cong H^{1}_{J\overline{R}}\left(\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}\right)

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Again from (
5
) we see that
\mathrm{grade}(JA,A)\geq 2
and using
[
BS12
, Theorem 6.2.7]
, we obtain that

H^{1}_{JA}(A)=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}H^{1}_{J\overline{R}}\left(\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}\right)=0.

Combining the above results, we conclude that

\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}\colon_{\overline{R}}J^{\infty}\overline{R}=\overline{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\;\overline{R}}

for all
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. This proves part
(a)
of our theorem.

(b)
Again we may assume without any loss of generality that
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are nonzero ideals in
R
. It follows from part
(a)
of this theorem that

\dfrac{S_{J}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}{\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})}=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}\dfrac{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\colon_{R}J^{\infty}}{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}=\bigoplus\limits_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}H^{0}_{J}\left(\dfrac{R}{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}\right)

is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
. We know from Proposition
3.1
that there exists a vector
\mathbf{h}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
such that

\mathrm{Ann}_{R}\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)=\mathrm{Ann}_{R}\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{h}}\right)\right)=:Q

for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. Then
t=\dim R/Q
,
\sqrt{I+J}\subseteq\sqrt{Q}
and there exist equalities
H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)=H^{0}_{\sqrt{Q}}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)
for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. From the associativity formula we get

(6)

\displaystyle e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{P\in V(Q),\;\dim R/P=t}e(R/P)\cdot l_{R_{P}}\left(H^{0}_{P_{P}}\left(R_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right)\right)

for all
\mathbf{n}\geq\mathbf{h}
. For every prime ideal
P
appearing in the above sum (
6
), we have that
\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}H^{0}_{P_{P}}\left(R_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right)
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P})
and there exists an integer
c>0
such that
P_{P}^{c}
annihilates the module
\bigoplus_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}}H^{0}_{P_{P}}\left(R_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right)
. So it can be regarded as a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over
\mathcal{R}({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P})/P_{P}^{c}\mathcal{R}({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P})
and using
[
BAM20
, Corollary 3.10]
we notice that

(7)

\dim\dfrac{\mathcal{R}({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P})}{P_{P}^{c}\mathcal{R}({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P})}-r=\ell\left({I_{1}}_{P},\ldots,{I_{r}}_{P}\right)-r=\ell\left({I_{1}}_{P}\cdots{I_{r}}_{P}\right)-1.

From
[
HHRT97
, Lemma
1.1
and Theorem
4.1
]
and statement (
7
) we know that there exists a numerical polynomial
F_{P}(\mathbf{n})\in\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
of total degree at most
\ell\left({I_{1}}_{P}\cdots{I_{r}}_{P}\right)-1
such that

l_{R_{P}}\left(H^{0}_{P_{P}}\left(R_{P}/{\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}}_{P}\right)\right)=F_{P}(\mathbf{n})

for all
\mathbf{n}\gg\mathbf{0}
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. We finally conclude from (
6
) that
e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial
F(\mathbf{n}):=\sum\limits_{P\in V(Q),\;\dim R/P=t}F_{P}(\mathbf{n})
, of total degree at most

\displaystyle\max\left\{\ell\left({I_{1}}_{P}\cdots{I_{r}}_{P}\right)-1\mid P\in V(Q),\;\dim R/P=t\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\dim R_{P}-2\mid P\in V(Q),\;\dim R/P=t\right\}

\displaystyle\leq d-t-2

and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. From the proof of
[
CHS10
, Theorem
2.1(b)
]
we know that there exists a numerical polynomial
G(n)\in\mathbb{Q}[n]
of degree at most
\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})+t-1
such that

e\left(H^{0}_{J}\left(R/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)\right)=G(n)

for all
n\gg 0
. The last claim is a consequence of the observation that
F(n,\ldots,n)=G(n)
for all
n\gg 0
.
∎

A refinement of Theorem
5.1
is obtained when
J=m_{R}
and this observation is recorded below. It generalises and improves statement
(0.6)
of
[
CHS10
]
.

Corollary 5.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is an excellent local domain of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are ideals in
R
. Assume that the analytic spread
\ell({I_{1}}\cdots{I_{r}})<d
. Then the saturated multi-Rees algebra
S_{m_{R}}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
is an
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded Noetherian
R
-algebra and also a finitely generated
\mathbb{N}^{r}
-graded module over the multi-Rees algebra
\mathcal{R}(I_{1},\ldots,I_{r})
. The multigraded length function
l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)
eventually agrees with a numerical polynomial in
\mathbb{Q}[n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}]
and all monomials of highest degree in this polynomial have nonnegative coefficients. In particular,

\mathrm{total\;degree}\;l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/\mathbf{I}^{\mathbf{n}}\right)\right)=\mathrm{deg}\;l_{R}\left(H^{0}_{m_{R}}\left(R/\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)^{n}\right)\right)\leq\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})-1.

We collect some known facts about analytic spread of ideals.

Remarks 5.1
.

Suppose that
(R,m_{R})
is a Noetherian local domain of dimension
d
and
I_{1},\ldots,I_{r}
are nonzero ideals in
R
.

(i)

If
\ell\left(I_{1}\cdots I_{r}\right)<d
then it follows from
[
BAM20
, Corollary
3.12
]
that each
\ell(I_{j})<d
. However the converse need not be true. Consider the ring
R=K[X,Y,Z]
with ideals
I_{1}=(X,Y)
and
I_{2}=(Y,Z)
. In this case,
\ell(I_{1})=2
,
\ell(I_{2})=2
whereas
\ell(I_{1}I_{2})=3
.

(ii)

If each
\ell(I_{j})\leq(d-2)/r+1
then it again follows from
[
BAM20
, Corollary
3.12
]
that
\ell(I_{1}\cdots I_{r})\leq\sum_{j=1}^{r}\ell(I_{j})-r+1\leq d-1
.

(iii)

If at least one of the ideals
I_{j}

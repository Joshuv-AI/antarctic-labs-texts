The Euler-Bernoulli equation with distributional coefficients and forces
Robin Blommaert, Srd¯an Lazendić, Ljubica Oparnica

Abstract
In this work we investigate a very weak solution to the initial-boundary value problem of an Euler-Bernoulli beam model. We allow for bending stiffness, axial- and transversal forces as well as for initial conditions to be irregular functions or distributions. We prove the well-posedness of this problem in the very weak sense. More precisely, we define the very weak solution to the problem and show its existence and uniqueness. For regular enough coefficients we show consistency with the weak solution. Numerical analysis shows that the very weak solution coincides with the weak solution, when the latter exists, but also offers more insights into the behaviour of the very weak solution, when the weak solution doesn’t exist. Mathematics Subject Classification (2010): 35D30, 46F10, 35A15, 35Q74 Keywords: differential equations with discontinuous coefficients, very weak solutions to partial differential equations, functional analytic methods, energy estimates.

Introduction

In this paper we investigate the initial-boundary value problem which arises in the analysis of the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation that includes irregular coefficients and force terms. More precisely, we consider the initial-boundary value problem

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\left(c(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u\right)+b(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u+\partial_{t}^{2}u=g,

(1)

\displaystyle u|_{t=0}=f_{1},\qquad\partial_{t}u|_{t=0}=f_{2},

(2)

\displaystyle u|_{x=0}=u|_{x=1}=0,\qquad\partial_{x}u|_{x=0}=\partial_{x}u|_{x=1}=0,

(3)

where the coefficients
c,b
, the force term
g
and the initial data
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are irregular objects, such as Dirac-type distributions. The structure of the considered problem arises from mechanics and it describes the displacement of a beam under axial and transversal forces.

Similar problems, involving distributional and generalized solutions to the Euler-Bernoulli model have already been addressed in literature
[
6
,
11
,
12
,
23
]
. In the Colombeau algebra
[
10
]
setting, it was shown that if
c
,
b
,
g
and
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are Colombeau generalized functions, the problem (1)-(3) has a unique solution in the Colombeau functions based on families of weak solutions to the corresponding regularized problem
[
11
]
.

Recently introduced concept of the very weak solution
[
9
]
offers an alternative approach to deal with irregular objects. Since then the very weak solution has been used to successfully treat a wide range of problems
[
3
,
1
,
2
,
8
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
19
,
17
,
20
]
with irregular data. The basic idea is to regularize irregular objects by nets of regular functions with moderate asymptotics, find the weak solution to the net of regularized problems obtaining a net of solutions, which is called a
sequential solution
. When the sequential solution is of moderate growth, it is called a
very weak solution
.

To the best of our knowledge, the concept of the very weak solution for the Euler-Bernoulli beam initial-boundary value problem with irregular coefficients and forces has not been addressed so far. The results presented in
[
11
]
include a weak solution of the problem (1)-(3) for regular data together with the Colombeau generalized solutions. In comparison with the Colombeau algebra generalized solution approach
[
11
]
, in the concept of very weak solution, we do not require derivatives to be of moderate growth and the approximating nets are chosen more freely, independently of the fact whether the underlying space is an algebra or not
[
9
]
.

The outline of the article is as follows. First, we conclude the Introduction section by fixing some notations, recall main notions and definitions important for our work, and give a brief description of the mechanical model for the Euler-Bernoulli beam. In Section 2, we review the existing results on the weak solution for the

-coefficients obtained in
[

, and modify the existing energy estimate making it suitable for the very weak solution approach. In Section 3 we define and show existence of the very weak solution. Section 4 includes the uniqueness results of the very weak solution defined in an appropriate sense and the consistency results of the very weak solution with the weak solution, when the coefficients are regular enough. In Section 5 we conduct numerical analysis of the obtained theoretical results. Finally, we give a short conclusion and discuss possibilities for future research.

There are vector valued Sobolev spaces that are essential in the construction of weak and very weak solutions to PDEs, when working with time dependent problems. Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space, then
L^{2}([0,T],X)
is the space of functions
f
over
[0,T]
with values in

with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}([0,T],X)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{X}dt\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

The space

is the subspace consisting of all functions

in

for which the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{2}([0,T],X)}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\|f(t)\|_{X}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}f(t)\|_{X}dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\partial_{t}^{2}f(t)\|_{X}dt\right)^{1/2}

is finite, where

is interpreted as the weak derivative.
Similarly

is the space of continuous functions over

with values in

\displaystyle\|f\|_{C([0,T],X)}:=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|f(t)\|_{X}<\infty.

For our purposes, the Hilbert space

with the inner product given by

\langle u,v\rangle:=\int_{0}^{1}u(x)\overline{v(x)}\,dx

will be of special interest. Together with this inner product we consider the anti-dual action of

by
\langle f,v\rangle:=f(\overline{v}),
for all

For the sake of simplifying certain notations, we will also use the Landau asymptotic symbols. More precisely, we say that function

1.2
The Euler-Bernoulli beam model

In this subsection we will explain in more detail the model of an elastic rod behind the Euler-Bernoulli beam equation. We base ourselves on a general theory of elasticity, which can be found in
[
4
,
5
]
.

Let us consider a cylindrical beam of length
L
. A beam is often modeled by considering deviations from the beam axis. Let us assume that the beam is under influence of a vertical force
g_{1}
and an axial force
P
, but there are no lateral forces. The vertical displacement of the beam axis in this situation, depends on time
t
and position
x
and will be modeled by the function
u(x,t)
.

Material particles also experience displacements in the axial direction
w(x,z,t)
. Let
p
be a particle that starts at the position
(x,z)
at time
t=0
. Then
w(x,z,t)
is the horizontal displacement of
p
at time
t
. For small horizontal deformations we can approximate the horizontal deformation by
w(x,z,t)=z\partial_{x}u(x,t)
. When the elasticity of the beam is modeled by Hooke’s law, the stress
\sigma(x,t)
is then proportional to the relative extension of the material. Locally, the relative extension is given by the derivative
\partial_{x}w(x,z,t)
. This together implies that

\sigma(x,t)=E(x)\partial_{x}w(x,z,t),

where the proportionality constant
E(x)
is called the modulus of elasticity. The bending moment
M
is a stress resultant given by

M(x,t)=\int_{C(x)}\sigma(x,t)zdA
, with the integral over the cross-section of the beam
C(x)
at position
x
. In our case, we obtain

\displaystyle M(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{C(x)}E(x)\partial_{x}w(x,z,t)zdA=\int_{C(x)}E(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)z^{2}dA

\displaystyle=E(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)\int_{C(x)}z^{2}dydz=E(x)I(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t).

Note that
I(x):=\int_{C(x)}z^{2}dydz
is the second moment of area of the beam’s cross-section. A shearing force is defined as an excess horizontal force on the top part of the beam over the bottom part of the beam. The bending moment defines the shearing force as

Q_{1}(x,t)=-\partial^{2}_{x}M(x,t)=-\partial^{2}_{x}\left(E(x)I(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)\right).

The minus sign appears as an excess force in a position
x
with positive curvature results in a downwards force.
Next we have the shearing force due to the axial force, that is

Q_{2}(x,t)=-P(t)\partial^{2}_{x}u(x,t).

Using Newton’s second law on the vertical displacement
u(x,t)
and the vertical forces
g_{1}(x,t)
,
Q_{1}(x,t)
and
Q_{2}(x,t)
, results in the dynamic Euler-Bernoulli equation

R(x)\partial_{t}^{2}u=g_{1}(x,t)+Q_{2}(x,t)+Q_{2}(x,t).

(4)

The line density
R(x)
is a replacement for the proper mass, as we are working locally on a cross-section of infinitesimal width. It is defined as
\partial_{x}m(x)
with
m(x)
the mass of the beam contained in the interval
[0,x]
. By putting together the previous results, we obtain the dynamic Euler-Bernoulli equation of the form

\partial_{x}^{2}\left(A(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u\right)+P(t)\partial_{x}^{2}u+R(x)\partial_{t}^{2}u=g_{1}(x,t),\quad x\in(0,1),\quad t>0,

where
A(x):=E(x)I(x)
is the bending stiffness,
P(t)
is the axial force,
R(x)
is the line density and
g_{1}(x,t)
denotes the force term.

In this article we will consider the Euler-Bernoulli beam model where now the rod consists of two parts with uniform cross-section. Moreover, we consider
distributional
transversal force
g_{1}
and axial force
P
. Denote the vertical displacement of the beam axis at
(x,t)
by
u(x,t)
. The equation of motion then reads as follows

\partial_{x}^{2}\left(A(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u\right)+P(t)\partial_{x}^{2}u+R(x)\partial_{t}^{2}u=g_{1}(x,t),\quad x\in(0,1),\quad t>0,

(5)

where by taking into account that the beam consists of two parts, the following holds:

•

A
is the bending stiffness, given by
A(x)=EI_{1}+H(x-x_{0})(EI_{2}-EI_{1})
where
H
is the Heaviside jump function and
I_{1}\not=I_{2}
are are the second moments of area of the beam’s cross-section that correspond to the two parts of the beam,

•

R
is the line density of the beam of the form
R(x)=R_{0}+H(x-x_{0})(R_{2}-R_{1})
,

•

P
is an axial force of the form
P(t)=P_{0}+P_{1}\delta(t-t_{1})
,
P_{0},P_{1}>0
,

•

g_{1}
is the vertical force term.

Further, we consider the
clamped Euler-Bernoulli beam
, i.e., the case when the equation (
5
) is accompanied by the homogeneous boundary conditions

u(0,t)=u(1,t)=\partial_{x}u(0,t)=\partial_{x}u(1,t)=0.

Using a formal change of variables
t\mapsto\sqrt{R(x)}t
, we reduce the model (
5
) to the following initial-boundary value problem on the domain
X_{T}:=(0,T)\times(0,1)
.

Problem 1

Find a function
u
such that

\partial_{x}^{2}\left(c(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)\right)+b(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(x,t)+\partial_{t}^{2}u(x,t)=g(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in X_{T},

(EB)

satisfying the initial conditions

u(x,0)=f_{1}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=f_{2}(x),\quad x\in[0,1],

(IC)

and the boundary conditions

u(0,t)=\partial_{x}u(0,t)=0,\quad u(1,t)=\partial_{x}u(1,t)=0.

(BC)

Note that the function
c
in (
EB
) equals
A
, the function
b
is given by
b(x,t)=P(\sqrt{R(x)}t)
and
g(x,t)=g_{1}(x,\sqrt{R(x)}t)
.

In Section
3
we will consider Problem
1
with
c\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
,
b\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X_{T})
,
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X_{T})
,
f_{1},f_{2}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
. First, we will say a bit more about about regularization techniques, which are a common approach when dealing with irregular coefficients. Moreover, we will introduce the concept of the very weak solution.

1.3
A note on regularizations

A common approach in the functional analysis to deal with irregular objects is via regularization, which will provide us with a regularizing net of smooth functions. We will work with nets of functions
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in a function space
X
, which is any map
(0,\varepsilon_{0}]\to X:\varepsilon\mapsto u_{\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon_{0}>0
. In this article we are concerned by nets of smooth functions indexed by a parameter
\varepsilon
and obeying certain asymptotic growth estimates as
\varepsilon
tends to zero.

One can regularize a distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by means of a mollifier
\varphi
. A
Friedrichs mollifier
is a smooth function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\varphi\geq 0
and
\int\varphi=1
. The corresponding mollifying net
(\varphi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is

\displaystyle\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\varepsilon^{-d}\varphi(\varepsilon^{-1}x),\qquad\varepsilon>0.

Convolution with the mollifying net produces a regularizing net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of smooth functions by

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}=u*\varphi_{\varepsilon},\quad\varepsilon>0,

(6)

such that
u_{\varepsilon}\to u
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Notably, the regularizing nets admit asymptotic estimates in terms of the regularization parameter.

Definition 1.1
(Moderateness)

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space. A net of elements
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
in
X
is
X
-moderate if there exist
C>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq C\varepsilon^{-N},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 1.2

Specifically, the net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
defined by (
6
) and all nets of partial derivatives
(\partial^{\alpha}_{x}u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
are
L^{p}
-moderate on compact sets, for
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
Indeed, let
v\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By the structure theorem for compactly supported distributions of finite order
[
10
,
22
]
, we can write
v
as a finite sum of distributional derivatives

v=\sum_{\alpha\leq m}\partial^{\alpha}f,

on
\supp v
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
and a function
f\in C(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with compact support in an arbitrarily small neighborhood of
\supp v
.
Convolution with a Friedrichs mollifier then gives

\displaystyle v_{\varepsilon}=v*\varphi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\partial^{\alpha}f*\varphi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}f*\partial^{\alpha}\varphi_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}f*(\varepsilon^{-d}(\partial^{\alpha}\varphi)(x/\varepsilon)),

such that by Young’s inequality

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\varepsilon^{-|\alpha|}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\varepsilon^{-d}(\partial^{\alpha}\varphi)(x/\varepsilon)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{-m}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}\|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi(x)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Similarly, nets of any order of derivatives
(\partial^{\beta}v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
are
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-moderate. Now let us consider any compact
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a distribution
u\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. By writing
u
as a locally finite sum of compactly supported distributions one finds that the regularizing net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
L^{p}(K)
-moderate. We can conclude that nets with moderate asymptotics are well suited for representing distributional objects.

In our investigation of the very weak solution we will encounter also log-type moderate nets. Due to its importance in the sequel, we will also show that every distribution of finite order can be regularized such that
the obtained regularization net is of log-type.

Definition 1.3
(Moderateness of log-type)

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space. A net
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
X
-moderate of log-type if there is
C>0
such that

\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq C\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

Remark 1.4

It has been already shown
[
15
]
that log-type regularizations of distributions can be easily obtained by convolution with logarithmically scaled Friedrichs mollifiers. For the convenience of the reader we will include an explicit construction starting from a moderate net.

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space. Suppose that
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is an
X
-moderate net. By moderateness of
(a_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
we have the estimate

\|a_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq C_{1}\varepsilon^{-N},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0,

(7)

for
C_{1}>0
and
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. We will now change the parametrisation of
\varepsilon
to get a slower rate of growth. Let us consider the net given by

(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(a_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon},\quad\text{where}\quad\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\left(\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-\dfrac{1}{N}},\quad\varepsilon>0.

(8)

It is then clear that
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is moderate of log-type. Indeed, by (
7
) and (
8
) we obtain that when
\varepsilon\to 0
, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}=\|a_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}}\|_{X}\leq C_{1}\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-N}=C_{1}\left(\left(\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{-\dfrac{1}{N}}\right)^{-N}=C_{1}\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}.

If the constant
N
is unknown, then we can use the following reparametrisation instead. Let

(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(a_{\mu_{\varepsilon}})_{\varepsilon},\quad\text{with}\quad\mu_{\varepsilon}=\dfrac{1}{\log\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}},\quad\varepsilon>0.

By using the fact that

\left(\log x\right)^{N}\leq x,

for
x>0
sufficiently large, we obtain that
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is moderate of log-type, since for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
there holds

\displaystyle\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}=\|a_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}\|_{X}\leq C_{1}\left(\dfrac{1}{\log\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}\right)^{-N}\leq C_{1}\left(\log\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}\right)^{N}\leq C_{1}\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon},

for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small. This concludes the proof and the remark.

Definition 1.5
(Negligibility)

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a Banach space. A net
(v_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of elements in
X
is
X
-negligible if for all
q>0
there exist
C_{q}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|v_{\varepsilon}\|_{X}\leq C_{q}\varepsilon^{q},\quad\text{as }\varepsilon\to 0.

After introducing the notion of moderateness and neglibility, we have all the ingredients needed to formulate the very weak solution concept.

1.4
The very weak solution concept

Now we will explain the general idea behind the very weak solution concept. Although the introduced concepts of moderate and negligible nets are related to those in the Colombeau setting, it is worth noting that the very weak solution concept offers an alternative approach to rigorously analyze the sequential solutions to PDEs. It is also important to note that in the concept of very weak solution, there is no requirement for derivatives to be of moderate growth and the approximating nets can be chosen more freely.

•

The existence theorem generally has the following structure. Assume that we consider distributional data in the original initial-boundary value problem. Then, we prove that the sequential solution that corresponds to any moderate regularization of the coefficients and data that satisfy some minimal additional conditions, in our case uniform positivity of
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and log-type moderateness of
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
, is necessarily moderate. One has to investigate for which classes of irregular functions such regularizations with additional conditions exist and how to construct them.

•

The uniqueness theorem reads as follows. Let there be two very weak solutions for which the regularizing nets of coefficients and data satisfy the conditions from the existence theorem. The goal is go give sufficient conditions on when the two very weak solutions can be considered the same.
Usually, one shows that when corresponding regularizing nets of the coefficients and data have negligible differences, then the very weak solutions must have negligible differences as well, i.e., the solutions are unique in the negligibility sense.

•

The main idea of the consistency theorem can be summarized as follows.
The question is whether the very weak solution obtained in the existence theorem, for the case when the data are nice enough functions, coincides with the solution guaranteed by the classical or weak solution theorems. One seeks regularity conditions for the functions or for their regularizing nets such that the very weak solution converges to the known solution.

In the following section, we will first discuss the existence and uniqueness of an abstract variational problem and derive energy estimates that will play a fundamental role in the formal introduction of the concept of the very weak solution. After defining the very weak solution to a problem in question, we prove the existence, uniqueness and consistency theorems.

\displaystyle\langle u^{\prime\prime}(t),v\rangle+a(t,u(t),v)=\langle f(t),v\rangle,\quad\forall v\in V,\quad\forall t\in(0,T),

(9)

\displaystyle u(0)=u_{0},\quad u^{\prime}(0)=u_{1}.

(10)

\|u(t)\|_{V}^{2}+\|u^{\prime}(t)\|_{H}^{2}\leq\left(D_{T}\|u_{0}\|_{V}^{2}+\|u_{1}\|^{2}_{H}+\int_{0}^{t}\|f(\tau)\|^{2}_{V^{\prime}}d\tau\right)\cdot\exp(tF_{T}),\quad\forall t\in[0,T],

(13)

2.2
The weak solution

In the sequel we will apply the above abstract result to Problem
1
in the case when the coefficients appearing in the equation are regular enough. Thus, let us consider
H:=L^{2}(0,1)
with the standard scalar product and a Sobolev space
V:=H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
. Then,
V^{\prime}=H^{-2}(0,1)
and
V\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow V^{\prime}
forms a Gelfand triple.

Formally, taking inner product in (
EB
) with
v\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
and using (

) we obtain the following weak formulation of the original problem.

Problem 2

Find a function
u
such that

\displaystyle\langle\partial_{t}^{2}u(t),v\rangle+\langle c\partial_{x}^{2}u(t),\partial_{x}^{2}v\rangle+\langle b(t)\partial_{x}^{2}u(t),v\rangle=\langle g(t),v\rangle,\quad t\in(0,T),

(14)

satisfying for
f_{1}\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
and
f_{2}\in L^{2}(0,1)

\displaystyle u(x,0)=f_{1}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u(x,0)=f_{2}(x),\quad x\in[0,1].

(15)

Remark 2.3

Note that if
u(t)\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
, for all
t\in[0,T]
, then the boundary conditions (
BC
) are automatically satisfied, as we know that
H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
is continuously embedded in the space

\{v\in C^{1}[0,1]\mid v(0,t)=v(1,t)=\partial_{x}v(0,t)=\partial_{x}v(1,t)=0\}
.

We have now obtained all the ingredients needed to prove the existence and the uniqueness of the weak solution of Problem
1
. In particular, we are able to apply the abstract result from Theorem
2.1
to the initial-value problem specified in Problem
2
.

Thus, for the coefficients
c\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
and
b\in C([0,T],L^{\infty}(0,1))
, let us define the sesquilinear forms
a_{0}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
and
a_{1}(t,\cdot,\cdot)
on
V\times V
by

a_{0}(t,u,v):=\langle c(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u,\partial_{x}^{2}v\rangle,\quad a_{1}(t,u,v):=\langle b(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u,v\rangle,\quad u,v\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1),

(16)

and
a(t,\cdot,\cdot)
with
a(t,u,v):=a_{0}(t,u,v)+a_{1}(t,u,v)
.

Theorem 2.4
(Weak solution)

Let
c\in L^{\infty}(0,1)
,
b\in C([0,T],L^{\infty}(0,1))
and
g\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))
and suppose there exists a positive constant
c_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

0<c_{0}\leq c(x),\quad\text{for }x\in(0,1).

Let
f_{1}\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1),f_{2}\in L^{2}(0,1)
.
Then there exists a unique
u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1))
with

\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1)),\quad\partial_{t}^{2}u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1)),

(17)

that satisfies (
14
) and initial conditions
u(0)=f_{1}
and
\partial_{t}u(0)=f_{2}
. Additionally, for any
t\in[0,T]
the energy estimate

\|u(t)\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+\|\partial_{t}u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq\left(D_{T}\|f_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{-2}(0,1)}d\tau\right)\exp(tF_{T}),

(18)

holds with the constants

\displaystyle D_{T}=\left(\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right),

\displaystyle F_{T}=\left(\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{T})}+1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right).

The proof is a direct consequence of the Theorem
2.1
provided that sesquilinear forms (
16
) satisfy properties (i)-(v). We refer to
[
11
, Theorem 2.2]
for the explicit verification of those. The precise abstract constants in the energy estimates read as

C=\|c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)},\quad C_{0}=0,\quad C_{1}=\|b\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{T})},\quad\alpha=\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},\quad\lambda=c_{0}C_{1/2},

where
C_{1/2}>0
is an Ehrling’s constant, which is defined as the smallest number satisfying

\|v\|^{2}_{H^{1}(0,1)}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+C_{1/2}\|v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)},\quad v\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1).

From
u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1))
and
\partial_{t}u\in L^{2}([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1))
and Sobolev embedding theorems we have that
u\in C([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1))
, while (
17
) together with
[
7
, ch. XVIII, §1, Theorem 1, pg. 473]
implies that
\partial_{t}u\in C([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))
.
Thus, we have that the solution
u
belongs to the Banach space denoted by

W:=C([0,T],H^{2}_{0}(0,1))\cap C^{1}([0,T],L^{2}(0,1)),

endowed with the norm

\|u\|_{W}=\left(\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],H^{2}(0,1))}+\|\partial_{t}u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{2}(0,1))}\right)^{1/2}.

Finally, note that by taking the supremum over
t\in[0,T]
in the energy estimate inequality (
18
), we easily obtain that

\|u\|^{2}_{W}\leq\left(D_{T}\|f_{1}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+\|f_{2}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|g\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))}\right)\exp\left(TF_{T}\right).

(19)

3
The very weak solution

Now we turn our attention to the case when we have irregular data.
Let the coefficients and right hand side in (
EB
) be distributional, i.e.,

b,g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(X_{T}),\quad c\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1),

(20)

and initial data

f_{1}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1),f_{2}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1).

We introduce the definition of the very weak solution.

Definition 3.1
(Very weak solution)

A very weak solution to Problem
1
is a
W
-moderate net
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
if there exist regularizing nets
(f_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(f_{2,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of
f_{1},f_{2}
,
b,g
and
c
respectively such that for every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
,
the function
u_{\varepsilon}

satisfies the regularized governing equation

\langle c_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t),\partial_{x}^{2}v(x)\rangle+\langle b_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t),v(x)\rangle+\langle\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t),v(x)\rangle=\langle g_{\varepsilon}(x,t),v(x)\rangle,

(21)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, for all
v\in H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
, and the initial conditions

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=f_{1,\varepsilon}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=f_{2,\varepsilon}(x),\quad\forall x\in(0,1).

(22)

Note that the choice of regularizations of the coefficients
b
and
c
and right-hand side
g
are still unspecified.
Next, we show that the appropriate moderateness of the coefficients nets yields the existence of a
W
-moderate solution net. It then remains to construct such nets as regularization of the distributional data.

Theorem 3.2
(Existence)

Let
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
and let
b
,
c
and
g
satisfy (
20
).
Let
(f_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be
H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
-moderate and
(f_{2,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be
L^{2}(0,1)
-moderate regularizing
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
. Let
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be
C([0,T],L^{\infty}(0,1))
-moderate of log-type,
(g_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be
L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))
-moderate and let
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
be
L^{\infty}(0,1)
-moderate regularizing
b,g
and
c
, respectively.
Assume additionally that there exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that for every sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle 0<c_{0}\leq c_{\varepsilon}(x),\quad\text{for all }x\in[0,1].

(23)

Then there exists a very weak solution to Problem
1
.

Proof.
By the respective moderateness of the regularizing nets, there are positive constants

C_{f_{1}},C_{f_{2}},C_{c},C_{b},C_{g},N_{f_{1}},N_{f_{2}},N_{c},N_{g}
such that

\displaystyle\|f_{1,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}(0,1)}\leq C_{f_{1}}\varepsilon^{-N_{f_{1}}},

\displaystyle\|f_{2,\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}\leq C_{f_{2}}\varepsilon^{-N_{f_{2}}},

\displaystyle\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\leq C_{c}\varepsilon^{-N_{c}},

\displaystyle\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{T})},\leq C_{b}\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\|g_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))}\leq C_{g}\varepsilon^{-N_{g}}.

For fixed
\varepsilon>0
, all conditions of Theorem
2.4
are verbatim fulfilled. The
net of regularized problems
corresponding to Problem 1 is given by

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\left(c_{\varepsilon}(x)\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\right)+b_{\varepsilon}(x,t)\partial_{x}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)+\partial_{t}^{2}u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)=g_{\varepsilon}(x,t),\quad(x,t)\in X_{T},

(24)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=f_{1,_{\varepsilon}}(x),\quad\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)=f_{2,_{\varepsilon}}(x),\quad x\in[0,1],

(25)

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(0,t)=\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(0,t)=0,\quad u_{\varepsilon}(1,t)=\partial_{x}u_{\varepsilon}(1,t)=0.

(26)

Thus, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we find a unique weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to Problem 1. We claim that the net of solutions
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
W
-moderate. We use the energy estimate (
19
) of Theorem
2.4
.
It holds that

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W}

\displaystyle\leq\left(D^{\varepsilon}_{T}\|f_{1,\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+\|f_{2,\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|g_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))}\right)\exp(TF^{\varepsilon}_{T}).

Further, there exist constants
C_{D},C_{F},N_{F}\geq 0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}D^{\varepsilon}_{T}&=\left(\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)\\
&\leq C_{c}\varepsilon^{-N_{c}}\left(1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)\\
&=C_{D}\varepsilon^{-N_{c}},\end{split}

(27)

and

\displaystyle\begin{split}\exp\left(TF^{\varepsilon}_{T}\right)&=\exp\left(T\left(\|b_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{T})}+1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(\dfrac{T\left(1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)}{{\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)}}\right)\exp\left(\dfrac{TC_{b}\log\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon}}{\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)}\right)\\
&\leq\exp\left(\dfrac{T\left(1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T)\right)}{{\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)}}\right)\varepsilon^{\dfrac{-TC_{b}}{\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right)}}\\
&=:C_{F}\varepsilon^{-N_{F}}.\end{split}

(28)

Finally, we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{W}

\displaystyle\leq\left(D^{\varepsilon}_{T}\|f_{1,\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}+\|f_{2,\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,1)}+\|g_{\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{L^{2}([0,T],H^{-2}(0,1))}\right)\exp\left(TF^{\varepsilon}_{T}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle\leq\left(C_{D}C^{2}_{f_{1}}\varepsilon^{-N_{c}-2N_{f_{1}}}+C^{2}_{f_{2}}\varepsilon^{-2N_{f_{1}}}+C_{g}^{2}\varepsilon^{-2N_{g}}\right)C_{F}\varepsilon^{-N_{F}},

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-N},

for some constants
C,N>0
.
This proves that
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
is
W
-moderate.
\Box

Remark 3.3

By tracking the constants involved in the proof of Theorem
3.2
we obtain the exact moderateness estimate for
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{W}\leq C_{u}\varepsilon^{-N_{u}},

with constants
C_{u},N_{u}>0
given by

\displaystyle C_{u}^{2}=C_{g}^{2}+C^{2}_{f_{2}}+C_{c}C^{2}_{f_{1}}(1+c_{0}C_{1/2}(1+T))/\min\left(\dfrac{c_{0}}{2},1\right),

(30)

\displaystyle 2N_{u}=\max(N_{c}+2N_{f_{1}},2N_{f_{2}},2N_{g})+C_{b}T/\min\left(\frac{c_{0}}{2},1\right).

(31)

Remark 3.4

It is important to note that Theorem
3.2
does not cover all cases for which data regularizations correspond to a very weak solution. For example, assume that
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(f_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
are such that

is moderate, then the error estimate (
29
) still yields the moderateness of the sequential solution. This includes cases where
\|c_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}
has faster than moderate growth rate, while
\|f_{1,\varepsilon}\|^{2}_{H^{2}(0,1)}
is a zero sequence that vanishes quickly, and vice-versa. There are many other cases including zero sequences. However, a general study of these cases is of less importance since they highly depend on the specific growth and vanishing rates of the data regularizations.

It is most natural to consider the case where none of the distributional data are zero, and therefore none of the regularizations and their estimates are zero sequences.
In this case, Theorem
3.2
provides the existence results of a very weak solution while considering optimally large sets of regularizations for each of the data. Thus, let us further assume that none of the regularization estimates are zero sequences.

Remark 3.5

Definition 3.6

(a)

Let
C_{c}(\Omega)
be the space of continuous functions on an open subset
\Omega\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact support. A
Radon measure
is a linear functional on
C_{c}(\Omega)
such that for any compact subset
K\subset\Omega
, there is a constant
M_{K}\geq 0
such that, for all continuous functions
\phi
vanishing identically outside of
K
, there holds

|\langle f,\phi\rangle|\leq M_{K}\sup\limits_{x\in K}|\phi(x)|.

(b)

Distribution
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is a
positive distribution
if it satisfies

\langle f,\varphi\rangle\geq 0,

for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\varphi(x)\geq 0
.

Lemma 3.7

A positive distribution is a positive Radon measure.

Proof.
Consider
f\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega)
and let
K\subseteq\Omega
an arbitrary compact set.
Choose
\eta\in\mathcal{D}(\Omega)
such that
\eta(x)=1
on
K
. For any real-valued
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(K)
we have

-\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta(x)\leq\phi(x)\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta(x),\quad\forall x\in\Omega.

Since the the functions
\phi+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta
and
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta-\phi
are positive we have

\displaystyle\langle f,\phi+\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta\rangle\geq 0,\quad\langle f,\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\eta-\phi\rangle\geq 0.

We obtain

-\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\langle f,\eta\rangle\leq\langle f,\phi\rangle\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\langle f,\eta\rangle,

and therefore

|\langle f,\phi\rangle|\leq\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\langle f,\eta\rangle,

which means that
f
is a distribution of order zero. By
[
21
, Proposition 21.2]

f
is a Radon measure.
\Box

Lemma 3.8

Let
c\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
and
c_{0}>0
. Then there exists a regularization
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of
c
such that

0<c_{0}\leq c_{\varepsilon}(x),\quad\forall x\in[0,1],

(32)

if and only if there exists a positive distribution
\mu
such that
c=c_{0}+\mu
.

Proof.
Let
c\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
,
c_{0}>0
and
c_{\varepsilon}
such that
0<c_{0}\leq c_{\varepsilon}(x)
.
We have that

c_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(x)dx\leq\langle c_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle,

if
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(0,1)
with
\varphi\geq 0
and by passing to the limit

c_{0}\int_{0}^{1}\varphi(x)dx\leq\langle c,\varphi\rangle.

Set
\mu=c-c_{0}
. Then it is clear that
\mu
is a positive distribution.

Let now
c_{0}>0
and
c=c_{0}+\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
with
\mu\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,1)
a positive distribution. Consider the extension
\tilde{c}\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
given by
\tilde{c}=c_{0}+\mu
on the whole of
\mathbb{R}
. Any regularization of
\tilde{c}
is a regularization of
c
. Let
\varphi
be a Friedrichs mollifier and define
c_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{c}*\varphi_{\varepsilon}
. We estimate

c_{\varepsilon}(x)=c_{0}*\varphi_{\varepsilon}+\mu*\varphi_{\varepsilon}\geq c_{0}*\varphi_{\varepsilon}=c_{0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x+y)dy=c_{0},

since
\mu
is a positive distribution, so
\mu*\varphi_{\varepsilon}
is non-negative. This concludes the proof.

\Box

(Regularizations)

Proof.

\displaystyle(f_{1,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(f_{1}*\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},\qquad(f_{2,\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}=(f_{2}*\psi_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon},

For

\Box

By the above construction of regularizing nets we can guarantee the existence of a very weak solution. However this construction of regularizations by smooth nets is not applicable for the consistency Theorem

4
Uniqueness and consistency results

For each choice of regularizing nets which satisfy the conditions of Theorem
3.2
, one obtains a unique sequential solution
(u_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
. However since the choice of regularizing nets is arbitrary, the resulting sequential solutions can still be different.

4.1
Uniqueness

As previously mentioned, we will formulate the uniqueness results in the neglibility sense. In other words, we seek conditions under which the coefficients and initial conditions are close enough, such that we can guarantee that the resulting sequential solutions are close enough. In the case of a very weak solution, we find stability under negligible deviations of the regularizing nets.

\displaystyle\|f_{1,\varepsilon}-\tilde{f}_{1,\varepsilon}\|_{H^{2}(0,1)}

\displaystyle=o\left(\varepsilon^{\tfrac{N_{c}+N_{F}}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=o\left(\varepsilon^{\tfrac{N_{F}}{2}}\right),

(40)

Remark 4.4

(a)

If
b
and
c
are not continuous, the regularizing nets
(b_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
and
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
such that
\|b_{\varepsilon}-b\|_{L^{\infty}(X_{T})}\to 0
and
\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\to 0
cannot be constructed via mollification, as previously mentioned. This can be seen as follows. The regularizing functions
c_{\varepsilon}=c\,*\varphi_{\varepsilon}
are continuous. The property
\|c_{\varepsilon}-c\|_{L^{\infty}(0,1)}\to 0
implies also convergence in
C([0,1])
, where the essential supremum becomes just the ordinary supremum in case of continuous functions. Thus,
c
is in
C([0,1])
, which contradicts the assumption that
c
is not continuous. Similarly for
b
.

(b)

This problem can be handled in different ways. One possibility is to consider the regularizing nets
b_{\varepsilon}
and
c_{\varepsilon}
of
L^{\infty}
functions, abandoning constructions of regularizing nets via mollifiers and smooth functions. For example, for a given zero sequence
(p_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}\to 0
in
L^{\infty}(0,1)
, one can consider the net
(c_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
of the form
(c+p_{\varepsilon})_{\varepsilon}
. Similarly for
b
.

Another possibility is to restrict the consistency theorem to continuous
b,c
. Then
L^{\infty}
-convergence of regularizing nets can be obtained by the following construction.
Let
c\in C([0,1])
and let
\tilde{c}\in C(\mathbb{R})
such that
\tilde{c}|_{[0,1]}=c
. If
\varphi
is a Friedrichs mollifier and
c_{\varepsilon}=\tilde{c}*\varphi_{\varepsilon}
, then for any
x\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle\left|c_{\varepsilon}(x)-c(x)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\tilde{c}(x-y)-c(x)\right)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)dy\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\tilde{c}(x-y)-c(x)\right|\varphi_{\varepsilon}(y)dy

\displaystyle\leq\sup\limits_{y\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]}\left|\tilde{c}(x-y)-\tilde{c}(x)\right|\leq\epsilon

whenever
\varepsilon\leq\delta_{\epsilon}
, where
\delta_{\epsilon}
is such that

|x-z|\leq\delta_{\epsilon}\implies|\tilde{c}(x)-\tilde{c}(z)|\leq\epsilon
for any
x,z\in[-1,2]
.
Such
\delta_{\epsilon}
exists since
\tilde{c}
is continuous on
[-1,2]
and therefore uniformly continuous. Note also that interval
[-1,2]
can be replaced with an arbitrary closed neighbourhood
K
of
[0,1]
.

. More precisely, we will test performance of the numerical schemes for solving Problem 1.

We describe briefly the method we use our simulations. Firstly, we use a hybrid method in which we discretize the space dimension and apply a finite element method, while in the time dimension we work with a finite difference method. We discretize the space and time dimensions in
n
and
m
parts, respectively with discretization steps
h=1/n
and
h_{t}=1/m
. For the space discretization, we consider the finite element space
V_{h}\subset H^{2}_{0}(0,1)
of continuous functions
f
on
[0,1]
that are cubic on intervals of the type
[ih,(i+1)h]
,
i=0,\dots,n-1
and such that the boundary conditions (
1.2
) hold. Note that without the boundary conditions, this space is known as
V^{1,3}
. As our boundary conditions are equal to zero, we denote this space with
V^{1,3}_{0}
.

Let
u
denote a numerical solution and let
u_{ex}
be the exact solution, then in the following experiments we consider the following error:

E_{L^{2}}(u,u_{ex})=\max\left\{\|u(x,t)-u_{ex}(x,t)\|_{L^{2}(0,1)}:\,t=ih_{t},\,i=0,\dots,m\right\}.

(46)

5.1
Regularization

As we described previously in Section
1.3
, regularization plays the central role in our work. Now, we explain the numerical procedure for the regularization of the coefficients. The standard mollifier in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}A\exp\left(\dfrac{1}{1-\|x\|^{2}}\right),&\text{if }\|x\|<1,\\
0,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(47)

where
A
is obtained such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)dx=1.

Then
\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x)=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{n}}\varphi\left(\dfrac{x}{\varepsilon}\right)
is a mollifying net.
In the one-dimensional case we will employ the standard mollifier as in (
47
). For the ease of numerical simulations and analysis, instead of considering two-dimensional standard molifier, we employ the separable molifier of the form
\rho(x,t)=\varphi(x)\varphi(t)
. This mollifier has the nice property that
\delta(x)*\rho(x,t)=\varphi(t)
and similarly
\delta(t)*\rho(x,t)=\varphi(x)
.

Additionally we use an extension procedure as in Lemma
3.8

to mollify extensions of the distributions on their domain. This will mostly be restricted to a constant mollification of a constant function.

6
Conclusion

In this article we show the existence, uniqueness and consistency results of the very weak solution for the Euler-Bernoulli equation with distributional coefficients and initial conditions. We consider nets of non-smooth data to obtain a sufficiently regular solution net. Our treatment of this problem, broadens the scope of the very weak solutions concepts. For the Euler-Bernoulli problem, we extend the existing weak solution framework making it suitable for the very weak solution approach. We defined and showed existence of the very weak solution, together with the uniqueness results defined in an appropriate sense. Moreover, we give the characterization of the distributions that allow the existence of the solution of the discussed problem. Lastly, we showed the consistency of the very weak solution with the weak solution, when the irregular data were sufficiently nice. Numerical analysis conducted showed that the very weak solution coincides with the weak solution, when the latter exists. When the coefficients are irregular functions, we showed that numerical analysis can provide us with more insights into the behaviour of the very weak solution.

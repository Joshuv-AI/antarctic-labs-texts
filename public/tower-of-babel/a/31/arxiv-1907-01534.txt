Three-point functions at strong coupling in the BMN limit
Benjamin Basso, and De-liang Zhong

Abstract
We consider structure constants of single-trace operators at strong coupling in planar 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 SYM theory using the hexagon formalism. We concentrate on heavy-heavy-light correlators where the heavy operators are BMN operators, with large R-charges and finite anomalous dimensions, and the light one is a finite-charge chiral primary operator. They describe the couplings between two highly boosted strings and a supergravity mode in the bulk dual. In the hexagon framework, two sums over virtual magnons are needed to bind the hexagons together around the light operator. We evaluate these sums explicitly at strong coupling, for a certain choice of BMN operators, and show that they factorise into a ratio of Gamma functions and a simple stringy prefactor. The former originates from giant mirror magnons scanning the AdS geometry while the latter stems from small fluctuations around the BMN vacuum. The resulting structure constants have poles at positions where an enhanced mixing with double-trace operators is expected and zeros whenever the process is forbidden by supersymmetry. We also discuss the transition to the classical regime, when the length of the light operator scales like the string tension, where we observe similitudes with the Neumann coefficients of the pp-wave String Field Theory vertex.

2
Generalities

In this section we recall the general hexagon formulae for structure constants in the HHL regime. To begin with, we make more precise the operators that we shall be considering.

2.1
Operators

In this paper we consider planar 3pt functions between two BPS operators and one non-BPS operator using the hexagon formalism. The latter are single-trace chiral primary operators built out of complex scalar fields. Without loss of generality, we take them as

\mathcal{O}_{1}\sim\textrm{tr}\,\bar{\mathcal{Z}}^{L_{1}}\,,\qquad\mathcal{O}_{2}\sim\textrm{tr}\,(\mathcal{Z}+\bar{\mathcal{Z}}+\mathcal{Y}-\bar{\mathcal{Y}})^{L_{2}}\,,

(4)

where
\mathcal{Z},\bar{\mathcal{Z}}=\phi_{1}\pm i\phi_{2}
and
\mathcal{Y},\bar{\mathcal{Y}}=\phi_{3}\pm i\phi_{4}
. The fused operator is a single-trace chain of length
L
, with a non-zero anomalous dimension
\gamma
. It reads as a spin-chain state on the vacuum
\textrm{tr}\,\mathcal{Z}^{L}
,

\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\sim\textrm{tr}\,\mathcal{Z}\ldots\mathcal{Z}\chi_{1}\mathcal{Z}\ldots\mathcal{Z}\chi_{M}\mathcal{Z}\ldots\mathcal{Z}+\ldots\,,

(5)

with the extra fields
\chi_{i}
’s mapping to magnons. The dots indicate the need to smear the magnons, such as to obtain a conformal and R-symmetry primary.

Each magnon moves along the chain with a momentum
p
and a corresponding energy

E=\sqrt{1+16g^{2}\sin^{2}{\frac{p}{2}}}\,.

(6)

The total energy yields the anomalous dimension
\gamma
of the operator,
\sum_{i=1}^{M}E_{i}=M+\gamma
, up to exponentially small corrections at
L\rightarrow\infty
. As usual with integrable models, the most useful variables are not the momenta but the rapidities that uniformize the interactions. In the case at hand, we get two rapidities
x^{\pm}
, related to each other
x^{\pm}=x(u\pm i/2)
and to the more common Bethe rapidity
u
through the Zhukowski map

x(u)=\frac{1}{2g}(u+\sqrt{u^{2}-4g^{2}})\,.

(7)

The dispersion relation can be written in these terms using

E=1+2ig(1/x^{+}-1/x^{-})\,,\qquad p=-i\log{(x^{+}/x^{-})}\,.

(8)

At strong coupling the magnons can cover a wide range of energies, from
E\sim 1
to
E\sim g
. The BMN operators correspond to the low-lying states in this spectrum; they carry
\gamma\sim 1
and are composed of low-momentum modes
p_{i}\sim 1/g
. (This domain is also known as the plane-wave region, as the magnon S matrix goes to 1 at strong coupling for such momenta.) As well known, and as one can see from (
6
), this kinematics is relativistic. Relatedly, we can drop the
\pm
superscripts in (
8
) and proceed with a single Zhukowski variable, for each magnon,

x_{i}^{\pm}=x_{i}\pm\frac{ix_{i}^{2}}{2g(x_{i}^{2}-1)}+O(1/g^{2})\,,

(9)

with
|x_{i}|>1
, giving the relativistic spectrum in the form

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{2}{x_{i}^{2}-1}+O(1/g)\,,\qquad p_{i}=\frac{x_{i}}{g(x^{2}_{i}-1)}+O(1/g^{2})\,.

(10)

The variable
x
relates then to the more common hyperbolic rapidity via
x=\textrm{coth}{(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta)}
.

In the large volume limit,
L\gg g
, there is no need for the quantization of the momenta. Hence, until we relax the latter assumption, in Section
5
, the rapidities
\{x_{i}\}
will be treated as free parameters, as much as the energy
\gamma
. We shall find convenient however to impose the zero-momentum condition,

0=\sum_{i=1}^{M}p_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{M}\frac{x_{i}}{g(x_{i}^{2}-1)}\,,

(11)

implementing the cyclicity of the state, as it will lead to further simplifications.

Besides the rapidity, each magnon carries a bi-fundamental index for its transformation property under the centrally extended
PSU(2|2)^{2}
symmetry of the spin-chain vacuum
Beisert:2006qh
. In this paper we shall restrict this index to the graded subspace generated by

\chi\in\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\phi_{1}\\
\psi_{1}\end{array}\right)\otimes\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\phi}_{1}\\
\dot{\psi}_{1}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathcal{Y}&\bar{\Psi}\\
\Psi&\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Z}\end{array}\right)\,,

(12)

where
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{D}
are the scalar field and lightcone derivative introduced earlier, and
\Psi
and
\bar{\Psi}
are the gauginos they can mix with,
\mathcal{Y}\mathcal{D}\mathcal{Z}\sim\Psi\bar{\Psi}
.
The corresponding linear space of local operators is known as the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector, see
Beisert:2005fw
, and it is closed under renormalisation at any coupling.

We get rid of the flavour indices by building scattering eigenstates.
4
4

4

One could also work with indices in the decompactification limit. However, in the hexagon framework, the interactions on the pair-of-pants involve the magnon scattering amplitudes and their diagonalisation simplifies the algebra.

The procedure is performed in the usual manner using the nested Bethe ansatz
Beisert:2005tm
. We shall make use of the compact grading, treating the scalars as the main excitations and the rest as defining the nested layers, see
Beisert:2005fw
. Later on, we will convert the expressions so-obtained to the alternative, non-compact grading, where the derivatives play the leading role.

Two nested levels are needed here, for the left- and the right-handed fermions, and two sets of auxiliary rapidities
\{y_{i},i=1,\ldots,N\},\{\dot{y}_{i},i=1,\ldots\dot{N}\}
are introduced. At strong coupling, for scattering eigenstates, they obey the linearised Bethe ansatz equations
Beisert:2005fw

\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{(x^{2}_{j}-1)(y-x_{j})}=0\,,\qquad\forall y\in\{y_{i}\}\cup\{\dot{y}_{i}\}\,,

(13)

modulo terms that vanish for cyclic states (
11
). We will not need to know much about the solutions to these equations; enough to note, numerically, that their modules are greater than
1
, for all
|x_{j}|>1
. The exception to the rule is the root
y=0
, which always exists for cyclic states.

For application to structure constants with two half-BPS operators we can fold the wave functions as only the diagonal states with
\{y_{i}\}=\{\dot{y}_{i}\}
return a non-zero answer. This superselection rule was derived in
Basso:2017khq
from the diagonal
PSU(2|2)
Yangian symmetry of the hexagon form factors. It extends the global selection rule
N=\dot{N}
which states that only left-right symmetric representations show up in the OPE of two chiral primary operators. Hence, summarising, the diagonal operators to be studied look like

\mathcal{O}_{\gamma}\sim\textrm{tr}\,\mathcal{D}^{S}\mathcal{Z}^{L-Y}\mathcal{Y}^{Y}\,,

(14)

up to mixing. They carry Lorentz spin
S=N
and scalar charge
Y=M-N
, with
M,N
the excitation numbers in the compact grading.

Hexagon sums

\mathcal{A}

First,
\mathcal{N}_{L}

In particular, it takes into account the fact that the structure constants computed here are for operators that are canonically normalised.
It depends on the magnons’ rapidities,
u_{i}=g(x_{i}+1/x_{i})
, and reads, in absolute value,

|\mathcal{N}_{L}|^{2}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{G}_{L}}\prod_{i=1}^{M}|\frac{\mu(u_{i})}{dp(u_{i})/du}|\times\prod_{i<j}^{M}\Delta(u_{i},u_{j})\,,

(16)

where
\Delta(u_{i},u_{j})=h(u_{i},u_{j})h(u_{j},u_{i})
is the squared module of the two-scalar hexagon form factor and
\mu
is the hexagon measure. The former trivialises at strong coupling for (distinct) low-momentum magnons,

h,\Delta\rightarrow 1\,.

(17)

The momentum-space measure is a simple factor, which takes the same form at any coupling,

|\mu(u)\frac{du}{dp}|=\frac{4g^{2}}{E(E^{2}-1)}\,,

(18)

when expressed in terms of the magnon energy, see Eq. (
6

\mathcal{G}_{L}
is the Gaudin determinant normalising the asymptotic wave function at large
L
.

7

7

\mathcal{G}_{L}

\mathcal{G}_{L}=L^{M}
, with a factor of
L

\mathcal{N}_{L}\rightarrow\prod_{i=1}^{M}\frac{2g}{\sqrt{LE_{i}(E_{i}^{2}-1)}}\,,

(19)

and it is of order

\mathcal{B}(\ell)=1+\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)+\mathcal{B}_{2}(\ell)+\ldots\,,

\mathcal{B}_{n}(\ell)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\geqslant 1}\int\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{a_{i}}(u_{i})du_{i}}{2\pi}e^{-\ell\tilde{E}_{a_{i}}(u_{i})}\mathcal{F}_{a_{i}}(u_{i})\mathcal{T}_{a_{i}}(u_{i})\prod_{i<j}^{n}\tilde{\Delta}_{a_{i}a_{j}}(u_{i},u_{j})\,.

(21)

Each mirror magnon carries a rapidity
u
and a bound-state index
a=1,2,...
, labelling its
PSU(2|2)^{2}
module with dimension
(4a)^{2}
. Equivalently, it comes equipped with a pair of complex conjugated Zhukowski variables,

x^{[\pm a]}=x(u\pm ia/2)\qquad\rightarrow\qquad\frac{u\pm ia/2}{g}=x^{[\pm a]}+1/x^{[\pm a]}\,,

(22)

which determines its energy and momentum,

\tilde{E}_{a}(u)=\log{(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]})}\,,\qquad\tilde{p}_{a}(u)=2u-\frac{2g}{x^{[+a]}}-\frac{2g}{x^{[-a]}}\,.

(23)

The latter energy couples in (
21
) to the length
\ell
of the bridge crossed by the particle. It becomes relativistic at strong coupling, for a momentum
\tilde{p}\sim 1
,

\tilde{E}_{a}(\tilde{p})\sim\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{p}^{2}+a^{2}}}{2g}\,.

(24)

This low-energy limit corresponds to Zhukowski’s close to the unit circle,
x^{[-a]}\rightarrow 1/x^{[+a]}
.

The magnons are weighted in (
21
) with a multi-particle measure, which is the mirror image of the one in
\mathcal{N}_{L}
, up to the Gaudin norm. It comprises the individual measure

\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)du=\frac{ik(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})du}{(x^{[+a]}-1/x^{[+a]})(x^{[-a]}-1/x^{[-a]})}=\frac{8a^{2}g^{2}d\tilde{p}}{(\tilde{p}^{2}+a^{2})^{2}\sqrt{1+16g^{2}/(\tilde{p}^{2}+a^{2})}}\,,

(25)

and the pairwise interaction

\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}(u,v)=\prod_{\sigma,\tau=\pm}k(x^{[\sigma a]},y^{[\tau b]})\,,

(26)

where

k(x,y)=\frac{x-y}{1-xy}\,.

(27)

The above interaction is of order
O(g^{0})
at strong coupling in all the regimes of interest. One also notices that it goes away as soon as one magnon is relativistic,

\lim\limits_{x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}\rightarrow 1}\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}(u,v)\rightarrow 1\,,

(28)

much like its spin-chain analogue, see Eq. (
17
). This important property follows from the crossing involution of
k
,

k(x,y)k(1/x,y)=1\,,

(29)

which can be read out from (
27
).

The final ingredient stands for the interaction between a mirror magnon
x^{[\pm a]}
and the spin-chain magnons at the boundary. It is customarily split into a diagonal part and a matrix part,
\mathcal{F}_{a}
and
\mathcal{T}_{a}
. While everything before was rather universal, these ones depend on the channel under study. Their general expressions for the bottom channel are given in Appendix
A
. Below, we discuss them in the BMN regime.

The diagonal part can be expressed in terms of the BES dressing phase
Beisert:2006ez
which is a rather convoluted function for general kinematics. Fortunately, at strong coupling and for state with
\gamma\sim 1
, we only need the AFS phase
Arutyunov:2004vx
which is significantly simpler. Taking all factors into account, we find

\mathcal{F}_{a}(u)=e^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma\tilde{E}_{a}(u)}=(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]})^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma}\,,

(30)

up to
1/g
corrections. Its structure is reminiscent of the shock-wave scattering phase
S_{12}\sim e^{i(p_{1}E_{2}-p_{2}E_{1})}
, see e.g.
Dubovsky:2012sh
;
Camanho:2014apa
, after performing a double-Wick rotation of one leg to the mirror kinematics. Strikingly, the resulting amplitude grows with the energy as if the Wick rotation has been done in the wrong way.
8
8

8

This large asymptotic behaviour holds at any coupling, as shown in Appendix
A
.
This is a signature of the bottom channel amplitude and relates to the fact that the mirror magnons are not standing near the physical ones on the chain, that is on an adjacent mirror channel, but lie instead on the edge that is facing it, see figure
3
. As a result, in place of a (euclidean) time delay, we find that the mirror magnons exit the bridge earlier than expected, since (
30
) is equivalent to length shift

\ell\rightarrow\beta=\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma\,.

(31)

We will see the consequences of this shift later on.

The matrix part
\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)
is slightly more involved. It contains a sum over the
4a
diagonal flavours of the mirror magnon and, according to the conjectures in
Basso:2015zoa
, it can be expressed in terms of the diagonal matrix element of the
PSU(2|2)
transfer matrix. This eigenvalue was worked out in
Beisert:2006qh
for a general Bethe state and can be cast into the form

\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)=\mathcal{T}(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})=\mathcal{T}^{+}+\mathcal{T}^{-}+\mathcal{T}^{\,0}\,,

(32)

where each term here is a rational function of
x^{[\pm a]}
. Their general expressions are obtained by continuing the formula in
Beisert:2006qh
to the mirror kinematics and are given in Appendix
A
. Here, we give
\mathcal{T}
for a cyclic state with
\gamma=O(1)
in the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector at strong coupling. It yields

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}=\pm\frac{i\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}}{g}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{x_{j}}{(x_{j}^{2}-1)(x^{[\pm a]}x_{j}-1)}\,,

(33)

with
\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}
accounting for the rapidities on the nested level,

\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{x^{[\mp a]}-y_{k}}{1/x^{[\pm a]}-y_{k}}\,.

(34)

If not for the
\mathcal{Q}
factors, these are just generating functions for the higher conserved charges.
9
9

9

The charges are given by
Q_{r}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}x_{j}^{2-r}/(g(x_{j}^{2}-1))
and they are generated at both large or small
x^{[\pm a]}
. The expansion starts with the anomalous dimension
\gamma=2gQ_{2}
in either case, if the state obeys the zero-momentum condition (
11
), that is, if
Q_{1}=0
.

Notice that they are small at strong coupling,
\mathcal{T}^{\pm}=O(1/g)\,,\forall x^{[\pm a]}
, as expected for a state standing “close” to the BPS ground state. The last component,
\mathcal{T}^{\,0}
, is more bulky but plays fortunately a supporting role. It reads

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{\,0}=\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{0}}{\mathcal{Q}^{+}\mathcal{Q}^{-}}\mathcal{T}^{+}\mathcal{T}^{-}\,,

(35)

where

\mathcal{Q}^{0}=\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1/x^{[+a]}-y_{k})(1-1/x^{[-a]}y_{k})}\sum_{n=0}^{a-2}P(u^{[a-2-2n]})\,.

(36)

Here,
u^{[m]}=g(x^{[m]}+1/x^{[m]})
and
P(u)=\prod_{k=1}^{N}(x-y_{k})(1-1/xy_{k})
is a Baxter polynomial for the auxiliary rapidities. Notice that
\mathcal{T}^{\,0}
is quadratic in the charges and
\sim 1/g^{2}
. It is negligible in the semiclassical regime
\ell\sim g
but contributes when
\ell=O(1)
, as explained in Section
4
.

3
The one-particle integral

In this section we analyse the one-particle integral
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)
at strong coupling. This contribution controls the entire amplitude in the classical domain
\ell\sim g
, after exponentiation of the mirror series. It captures only a bit of the answer for bridge length
\ell\sim 1
, but hints nonetheless at some important simplifications.

3.1
Classical bridge

We shall first walk through the classical regime
\ell\sim g\gg 1
. Precisely, we take the strong coupling limit with
l=\ell/(2g)
kept fixed. Since the length is large, the integrals in (
21
) are dominated by the low-energy modes, with momenta
\tilde{p}_{i}=O(1)
, spins
a_{i}=O(1)
and relativistic energies (
24
). As said earlier, this kinematics corresponds to Zhukowski variables on the unit circle,

x^{[+a]}\rightarrow x\qquad x^{[-a]}\rightarrow 1/x\,,\qquad\forall a\,,

(37)

with
|x|=1
, and, in this variable, the measure (
25
) reads

\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)du\rightarrow\frac{du(x)}{a}\,,\qquad u(x)=g(x+1/x)\,,

(38)

while the energy is given by

\tilde{E}_{a}\rightarrow a\varepsilon(x)/2g\,,\qquad\varepsilon(x)=\frac{2ix}{x^{2}-1}\,.

(39)

The measure scale large
\sim g
at given
x
, but its scaling is compensated by the transfer matrix
\mathcal{T}=O(1/g)
. Hence, the resulting integrand is of order
O(1)
. The latter takes the same universal form, regardless of the favours of the magnons in the Bethe state. Namely, the flavour dependence drops out,
\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}\rightarrow 1
, when
x^{[\pm a]}
approach the unit circle, and we get, see (
32
) and (
33
),

\mathcal{T}_{a}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{ix_{j}^{2}(x^{2}-1)}{g(x_{j}^{2}-1)(xx_{j}-1)(x-x_{j})}\,,\qquad\forall a\,.

(40)

We can also disregard the diagonal factor,
\mathcal{F}_{a}\rightarrow 1
, since the length shift it produces is subleading here,
\beta\sim\ell
, see Eq. (
31
).

Moreover, there are no interactions among mirror magnons in this regime, see Eq. (
28
). As a result, the mirror sum exponentiates,
10
10

10

This is different than for a state with semiclassical energy
\gamma\sim\sqrt{\lambda}
. In that case,
\mathcal{T}=O(1)
and the pinch singularities in the multi-particle integrals produce sizeable one-particle-like contributions, see
Jiang:2016ulr
.

\log{\mathcal{B}(\ell)}\simeq\mathcal{B}_{1}+O(1/g)\,,

(41)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)

\displaystyle\simeq\int_{C^{+}}\frac{du(x)}{2\pi}\mathcal{T}(x)\sum_{a=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{a}e^{-al\varepsilon(x)}

(42)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{x_{j}^{2}}{x_{j}^{2}-1}\int\limits_{C^{+}}\frac{dx}{2\pi i}\frac{(x-1/x)^{2}}{(x-x_{j})(xx_{j}-1)}\log{(1-e^{-l\varepsilon(x)})}\,,

where
C^{+}
goes from
x=-1
to
x=+1
along the unit circle with positive imaginary part. Introducing hyperbolic rapidities,

x=\textrm{coth}{\,\tfrac{1}{2}(\theta-\tfrac{i\pi}{2})}\,,\qquad\varepsilon(x)=\cosh{\theta}\,,

(43)

and similarly for the Bethe roots, see relations listed after Eq. (
10
), we obtain the equivalent form

\lim\limits_{g\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{B}(\ell=2g\l)=\prod_{j=1}^{M}b_{l}(\theta_{j})\,,

(44)

where
b_{l}(\theta_{j})
is given by

\log{b_{l}(\theta_{j})}=\int\limits_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{d\theta}{2\pi}\frac{f(\theta_{j}+i\frac{\pi}{2})}{f(\theta)\cosh{(\theta-\theta_{j})}}\log{(1-e^{-l\cosh{\theta}})}\,,

(45)

with the weight
f(\theta)=\cosh^{2}{\theta}
. Note for later purposes that the coefficient
b_{l}(\theta)
obeys a simple functional relation,

b_{l}(\theta_{j}+i\pi)b_{l}(\theta_{j})=(1-e^{-il\sinh{\theta_{j}}})\,,\qquad\forall f\,.

(46)

It relates to the crossing property of the amplitude, see Section
5
.

The factorisation in (
44
) is reminiscent of the one observed for the Neumann coefficients of the pp-wave SFT vertex, see
Bajnok:2017mdf
;
Bajnok:2015hla
;
Bajnok:2015ftj
;
Lucietti:2004wy
and references therein. Furthermore, the coefficient
b_{l}(\theta)
in (
44
) and the one denoted
d_{l}(\theta)
in
Bajnok:2017mdf
which captures the bridge corrections to the Neumann coefficients appear quite similar. The latter also solves the crossing equation (
46
) and, as such, can be cast into the integral form (
45
). Yet it corresponds to a different solution, with a different weight
f
. Namely, in the SFT context, it is the boost-invariant solution
f=1
that is picked,

d_{l}(\theta)=b_{l}(\theta)|_{f\rightarrow 1}\,.

(47)

Despite this difference, there are many common points and as we will see in Section
5
the
b
’s enter the structure constants much like the
d
’s in the Neumann coefficients.

Formula (
44
) resums all the mirror corrections
\mathcal{B}_{n}\sim e^{-nl}
at strong coupling
\forall l\in(0,\infty)
. It shows that
\mathcal{B}
, which begins at
1
for
l\sim\infty
, grows with decreasing length
l
, all the way to
l=0
where it blows up. At this end point, it exhibits a power-law behaviour,

\mathcal{B}\sim\left(2l\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}\gamma}\,\prod_{j=1}^{M}\cosh{(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{j})}\,.

(48)

This is a rather common short-distance scaling for a form factor series. What is unusual is that it originates from the sum over the bound states and not from large rapidities. Yet another uncommon feature is that the exponent depends on the state at the boundary, through the anomalous dimension
\gamma=\sum_{j}(\cosh{\theta_{j}}-1)
. It can be traced back to the
\theta
dependence of
f
. It would be absent for a boost-invariant weight
f=1
, which also brings (
48
) for
l\sim 0
but with
\gamma\rightarrow-1
, no matter the state
\{\theta_{j}\}
.

To conclude, let us mention the connection with the string theory result
Kazama:2016cfl
. The latter holds for classical Bethe states with energy
\sim g
dual to classical strings in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. Picking a purely scalar state for simplicity, it predicts that

\log{C^{\circ\circ\bullet}}|_{\textrm{mirror}}=\mathsf{A}(l)+\textrm{other channels}\,,

(49)

where

\mathsf{A}(l)=\int_{C^{+}}\frac{du(x)}{2\pi}\{2\textrm{Li}_{2}(e^{-l\varepsilon(x)})-\textrm{Li}_{2}(q_{+}(x)e^{-l\varepsilon(x)})-\textrm{Li}_{2}(q_{-}(x)e^{-l\varepsilon(x)})\}\,,

(50)

with
\textrm{Li}_{2}
the dilogarithm. In this formulation, all the information about the (zero-momentum) state is encoded in the twists
q_{\pm}(x)
,

q_{\pm}(x)=\exp{\{\mp\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{ix_{j}}{g(x_{j}^{2}-1)(x^{\pm 1}x_{j}-1)}\}}\,.

(51)

Since the number of magnons is large for a classical string,
M\sim g\gg 1
, the sums here are of order
O(1)
and should be read as integrals over the dense support of the roots. Drawing inspiration from the discretisation of the spectral curve introduced in
Arutyunov:2004vx
, we may extrapolate the result to a state with fewer magnons, and energy
\sim 1
, by keeping the exponents as in (
51
) and expanding at large
g
. This readily maps
\mathsf{A}(l)
into (
42
), after linearising the twists and differentiating the dilogs. We could also check this reduction, with some more control on the approximation, directly at the level of the hexagon series, which was shown to reproduce
\mathsf{A}(l)
for classical states in
Jiang:2016ulr
. It links then to the map between the classical and the BMN transfer matrices,
\mathcal{T}(x)_{\textrm{classical}}=2-\sum_{i=\pm}\exp{\{-\mathcal{T}^{i}(x)_{\textrm{N-BMN}}\}}
, which embodies the exponentiation observed in (
51
).

3.2
Quantum bridge

We turn to the finite-bridge regime
\ell=O(1)
. Classically, this is the point
l=0
where the amplitude blows up. We will see here how this singularity is “resolved” at higher energy, when the non-relativistic corrections, to the dispersion relation notably, are taken into account.

Let us first work out the kinematics. For a finite bridge, the energy should be of order
O(1)
,

\tilde{E}_{a}^{2}\sim(\tilde{p}^{2}+a^{2})/4g^{2}\sim 1/\ell^{2}\,,

(52)

implying that
\tilde{p}
or
a
should be
\sim g
. In either case we observe that the Zhukowski’s
x^{[\pm a]}
’s are moving away from the unit circle. These magnons with large individual momenta are the mirror - or AdS - analogues of the spin-chain giant magnons
Hofman:2006xt
. They map to solitonic solutions of the double Wick rotated worldsheet theory and were constructed classically in
Arutyunov:2007tc
at small
a
.
11
11

11

The AdS soliton found in
Arutyunov:2007tc
carries a large momentum
\tilde{p}\sim g
but no spin. Its bound state analogue should also rotate in AdS when
a\sim g
.

Their characteristic feature is that they are non-compact: They stretch in AdS and reach the boundary at infinite momenta, as one can see classically using the soliton of
Arutyunov:2007tc
. As such, they can trigger short-distance singularities in the boundary theory, as we shall see later on.

In our case, it appears necessary to have
a\sim g
, as otherwise the measure would be suppressed,

\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)du\sim\frac{a^{2}d\tilde{p}}{g^{2}}\,.

(53)

see Eq. (
25
). This condition was also suggested by the limit
l\rightarrow 0
of the classical integral (
45
), which, as said earlier, is dominated by the large
a
’s. In these circumstances, we can approximate the bound-state sum by an integral,
\sum_{a}\rightarrow\int da
, and substitute to the sum over the 1-particle states the 2d integral over the Zhukowski’s,

\sum_{a}\int\frac{\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)du}{2\pi}\rightarrow ig\int\frac{dx^{[+a]}dx^{[-a]}}{\pi x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}}k(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})\,,

(54)

where we used (
25
) for the measure and performed a change of variables using (
22
). Recall that
k(x,y)=(x-y)/(1-xy)
.

Combining all the pieces together gives us the one-particle integral for a quantum bridge,

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\int\frac{dx^{[+a]}dx^{[-a]}}{\pi(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]})^{\beta+1}}k(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})\times ig\mathcal{T}(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})\,,

(55)

where
\beta=\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
and with
x^{[-a]}=(x^{[+a]})^{*}
. The domain of integration is the upper half plane for
x^{[+a]}
, minus the unit disk, see figure
4
, with the relativistic low-energy modes sitting on the unit circle at the boundary. Note that
g\mathcal{T},k
, and thus the whole integrand, are of order
O(g^{0})
throughout the entire domain.

Figure 4:
Graphs of the Zhukowski variable
x^{[+a]}=x(u+ia/2)
, for real
u
,
a=1,2,\ldots\,
and
g=10
. The short-distance regime is controlled by the continuum formed by this dense semi-infinite set, shown here in blue-grey. Because of a singularity at the boundary, the continuum approximation must stop somewhere over the rainbow, a short distance away from the unit circle.

A remarkable property of this integrand is its reflection symmetry under
a\rightarrow-a
. Namely, we see from (
33
) that

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}(x^{[-a]},x^{[+a]})=-\mathcal{T}^{\mp}(x^{[+a]},x^{[-a]})\,.

(56)

The sum
(\mathcal{T}^{+}+\mathcal{T}^{-})
is thus antisymmetric and so is the measure, since
k(x,y)=-k(y,x)
. The same can be said about
\mathcal{T}^{\,0}
, in the regime
a\sim g
, see Eq. (
80
) below. Hence, since the integrand is a symmetric function of
a
, we can extend the integration domain to the entire plane minus the disk. Introducing polar coordinates,

x^{[\pm a]}=r\,s^{\pm 1}\,,

(57)

with
s=e^{i\phi}
and
r\in[1,\infty]
, we arrive at

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2\beta+1}}\oint\limits_{|s|=1}\frac{ds}{2\pi is}k(rs,r/s)\times ig\mathcal{T}(rs,r/s)\,,

(58)

with the
s
-contour going clockwise along the unit circle.

We will now evaluate this two-fold integral for
\mathcal{T}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}^{+}+\mathcal{T}^{-}
, deferring the integration of
\mathcal{T}^{\,0}
to the next section. (The latter component is quadratic in the charges and combines naturally with the higher-magnon integrals.)

Given the parity property (
56
), we only need to consider the integral for
\mathcal{T}^{+}
. Focussing on the angular integral, and using (
33
), we get to evaluate

\mathcal{R}_{1}=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{x_{j}}{x_{j}^{2}-1}\oint\limits_{|s|=1}\frac{ds}{2\pi is}\frac{k(rs,r/s)}{1-rsx_{j}}\mathcal{Q}^{+}(rs,r/s)\,.

(59)

The integrand is a rational function of
s
with poles at

\{0,1/rx_{j},1/ry_{k},\infty\}\,.

(60)

Recall that all the roots are
>1
in absolute value; hence, if not for the last one, all the poles in the list above sit inside the unit disk.
12
12

12

Note that one can relax the condition that the
y
’s are outside the disk, as long as they obey the BAEs, since then the associated residues vanish.
So, by Cauchy theorem, the integral follows from its behaviour at infinity,

\lim\limits_{s\rightarrow\infty}\frac{k(rs,r/s)}{1-rsx_{j}}\mathcal{Q}^{+}(rs,r/s)=\frac{1}{x_{j}(r^{2}-1)}\,,

(61)

and thus

\mathcal{R}_{1}=\frac{1}{r^{2}-1}\times\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{(x_{j}^{2}-1)}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma}{r^{2}-1}\,.

(62)

Remarkably, all the charges in
\mathcal{T}^{+}
have been swept away by the angular average, if not for the leading one
\gamma
. We will see shortly that this phenomenon extends to the higher-magnon integrals, which turn out to be proportional to higher powers of
\gamma
only.

We proceed with the integration over
r
, after doubling (
62
) for
\mathcal{T}^{+}+\mathcal{T}^{-}
. Here, we face the problem that the integral does not converge, since
\mathcal{R}_{1}
has a pole at
r=1
. We regularise it by introducing a lower cut-off
r_{\textrm{min}}=1+\epsilon
, with
\epsilon\sim 0
. It gives

\mathcal{B}_{1}|_{\textrm{cut}}=2\int\limits_{1+\epsilon}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{1+2\beta}}\,\mathcal{R}_{1}=-\frac{\gamma}{2}\log{(2\epsilon e^{\gamma_{E}})}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log{\Gamma(1+\beta)}\,,

(63)

where
\gamma_{E}=-\log{\Gamma}^{\prime}(1)
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant.

The logarithmic divergence at
r=1
goes back to the
1/a
scaling of the measure in the classical regime, see Eq. (
38
). It indicates that our approximation (
54
) is not valid in this neighbourhood. Hence, to complete our calculation and get rid of the discrete cut-off dependence in (
63
), we shall reinstate the bound-state sum for the modes close to the disk.

Precisely, we divide the domain into two regions, for the IR and UV modes,

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathcal{B}_{1}|_{\textrm{discrete}}+\mathcal{B}_{1}|_{\textrm{continuum}}\,,

(64)

where the continuum part is nothing but the integral (
63
) with
r_{\textrm{min}}=1+\epsilon
. It is completed here by the sum over the low-lying bound states,
a=1,\ldots,a^{\textrm{max}}
, with
a^{\textrm{max}}
large but finite. To ensure the continuity at the boundary of the sum and the integral, we demand that

a^{\textrm{max}}=-ig(s-1/s)(r_{\textrm{min}}-1/r_{\textrm{min}})\simeq\frac{4g\epsilon}{\varepsilon(s)}\,,

(65)

using the coordinates transformations (
22
) and (
57
). This gluing condition is consistent with our assumptions, as long as
1\ll a^{\textrm{max}}\ll g
or equivalently
1/g\ll\epsilon\ll 1
. Note that it implies that
a^{\textrm{max}}
depends on the position
s
on the unit circle, through the classical energy (
39
). Other than that, the integrand of the discrete part is as for the classical limit, if not that the bridge is small. Namely, it reads as the first line of (
42
), with
l=0
,
x\rightarrow s
and with a sum up to
a^{\textrm{max}}
.
The sum is readily done

\sum_{a=1}^{a^{\textrm{max}}}\frac{1}{a}\simeq\log{(a^{\textrm{max}}e^{\gamma_{E}})}=\log{\big[\frac{4g\epsilon e^{\gamma_{E}}}{\varepsilon(s)}\big]}\,,

(66)

and so is the integral,

\mathcal{B}_{1}|_{\textrm{discrete}}=\int\limits_{C^{+}}\frac{du(s)}{2\pi}\mathcal{T}(s)\log{\big[\frac{4g\epsilon e^{\gamma_{E}}}{\varepsilon(s)}\big]}=\frac{\gamma}{2}\log{(2g\epsilon e^{\gamma_{E}})}+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\log{\cosh{(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{j}})}\,.

(67)

Hence, as expected, the logarithmic divergences cancel out between (
67
) and (
63
).

The final expression for the one-particle integral is obtained by collecting the finite parts in (
64
). It reads

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\log{C}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log{\Gamma(1+\beta)}\,,

(68)

with
\beta=\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
and with the
\ell
independent constant

C=g^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{2}(1+E_{j})}=g^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma}\prod_{j=1}^{M}\cosh{(\tfrac{1}{2}\theta_{j})}\,,

(69)

where
E_{j}=\cosh{\theta_{j}}
. The funny scaling of
C
with
g
is the imprint left by the singularity at
r=1
. It responds to the logarithmic singularity
\sim\frac{\gamma}{2}\log{(1/l)}
found classically. This singularity is replaced, as far as the
\ell
dependence is concerned, by a series of poles,

\log{\ell}\rightarrow\psi(1+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)\,,

(70)

with
\psi
the digamma function. This is the first step towards the Gamma functions mentioned in the introduction.

Let us mention finally that one can double check (
68
) using, instead of a hard cut-off, a modified measure

\frac{1}{x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}-1}\rightarrow\frac{1}{(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}-1)^{1-\alpha}}\,,

(71)

with
\alpha
a regulator, to be sent to zero at the end of the calculation. The analysis in this scheme is performed in Appendix
B
for completeness.

4
Summing the multi-particle integrals

In this section we generalise the analysis to the multi-particle exchanges and resum the full form factor series, for a boundary Bethe state in the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector.

4.1
Free energy

For more magnons, it proves convenient to use the Pfaffian formula for the hexagons
Basso:2017khq
, see also
Kostov:2019stn
;
Kostov:2019auq
for recent discussions. Namely, defining

z_{i}=x_{i}^{[+a_{i}]}\,,\qquad\bar{z}_{i}=x_{i}^{[-a_{i}]}\,,

(72)

for the variables of the
i
-th mirror magnons, we can write the interactions among magnons in the form

\prod_{i<j}^{n}\tilde{\Delta}_{a_{i},a_{j}}(u_{i},u_{j})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k(z_{i},\bar{z}_{i})}\times\mathtt{Pf}_{n}\,,

(73)

with
k
as in (
27
) and where
\mathtt{Pf}_{n}
is the Pfaffian of the
2n\times 2n
antisymmetric matrix with
ij
element

k(t_{i},t_{j})\,,\qquad\quad t_{i}=\begin{cases}z_{[i/2]}&i/2\ \text{non-integer}\\
\bar{z}_{[i/2]}&i/2\ \text{integer}\end{cases}\qquad i=1,\cdots,2n\,.

(74)

Explicitly,

\mathtt{Pf}_{n}=\frac{1}{2^{n}n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{2n}}(-1)^{|\sigma|}\prod_{j=1}^{n}k(t_{\sigma(2j-1)},t_{\sigma(2j)})\,,

(75)

where
S_{2n}
is the symmetric group of the
2n
variables
t_{j}
and
(-1)^{|\sigma|}
is the signature of the group element
\sigma
.

We should multiply (
75
) with a string of transfer matrices
\prod_{j}\mathcal{T}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})
and integrate each pair of variables with a measure. Combining them with the product of
1/k
’s coming from the interaction (
73
) yields

4.3
Evaluating the sum

In the end, we are left with the much simpler problem of evaluating the sum over the homogeneous cycles,

\log{\mathcal{B}(\ell)}=-\sum_{n\geqslant 1}\frac{2^{n}}{n}\int C_{n}\,ig\mathcal{T}_{1}^{+}ig\mathcal{T}_{2}^{+}\cdots ig\mathcal{T}_{n}^{+}\,.

(93)

As before, we will first carry out the integrals over the angular variables,

\mathcal{R}_{n}=(-1)^{n}\sum_{b_{1},b_{2},\cdots,b_{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{x_{b_{j}}}{x_{b_{j}}^{2}-1}\oint\limits_{|s_{1}|=1}\cdots\oint\limits_{|s_{n}|=1}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{ds_{j}}{2\pi is_{j}}\frac{k(\bar{z}_{j},z_{j+1})}{z_{j}x_{b_{j}}-1}\underbrace{\prod_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\bar{z}_{j}-y_{k}}{1/z_{j}-y_{k}}}_{=\mathcal{Q}_{j}^{+}}\,,

(94)

where
z_{n+1}=z_{1}
. We can integrate the variables recursively, from
s_{1}
to
s_{n-1}
, by picking up the residues of the poles outside the unit disk at each step. The latter poles only come from the string of
k
-factors,

1-\bar{z}_{j}z_{j+1}=0\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad s_{j}=r_{j}z_{j+1}\,.

(95)

Their contributions are read off using an anticlockwise contour and follow from

-\mathtt{Res}_{s_{j}=\,r_{j}z_{j+1}}\left[\frac{\mathcal{Q}^{+}_{j}k(\bar{z}_{j},z_{j+1})}{s_{j}(x_{b_{j}}z_{j}-1)}\right]=\mathcal{Q}^{+}(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\times\frac{(z_{j+1}-1/z_{j+1})}{z_{j+1}r^{2}_{j}x_{b_{j}}-1}\,,

(96)

with
\mathcal{Q}^{+}
evaluated at

(z_{j},\bar{z}_{j})\rightarrow(r_{j}^{2}z_{j+1},1/z_{j+1})\,.

(97)

We can prove recursively that these simple poles are the only ones that contribute. For example, looking at step 1 first, the relevant component of the integrand is

\frac{k(\bar{z}_{n},z_{1})\mathcal{Q}_{1}^{+}k(\bar{z}_{1},z_{2})}{s_{1}(z_{1}x_{b_{1}}-1)}\,.

(98)

It has poles at
s_{1}=\{r_{1}z_{2},0,1/r_{1}\bar{z}_{n},1/r_{1}x_{b_{1}},1/r_{1}y_{k}\}
, which all sit inside the unit disk, except for the first one.

15

15

We are using here that
r_{j},x_{b_{j}},y_{k}\geqslant 1
and disregard the exceptional situations where the poles are on the circle.
Note that there is no pole at infinity, since the above factor is of order

when

. Assuming a similar pole structure is found at step

where, for

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigg[\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{2\beta+2k+1}}\bigg]^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{n}}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(\beta+k)^{n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{2^{n}(n-1)!}\frac{\partial^{n}}{\partial\beta^{n}}\log{\Gamma(1+\beta)}\,.

5.1
Crossing

It remains to determine the amplitude

E_{j}\rightarrow-E_{j}\,,

(113)

instead of flipping the mirror particles. Geometrically, it corresponds to transporting the state along the contour of the octagon and maps
\mathcal{B}
into
\mathcal{A}

7

18
18

18

There are a few complications there as well. Firstly, since we are working with scattering eigenstates, we should pay attention to the crossing of the auxiliary roots. However, given that these ones drop out in the final result, we believe we can ignore them here. More importantly, one cannot cross all the magnons along the same path and at the same time satisfy the zero-momentum condition. The latter requirement imposes that magnons rotate in opposite directions. We can get around this problem without relaxing
P=0
by assuming that the magnons were split from the outset and laid on different edges, at the bottom and top of the octagon in the right panel of figure
7

5.3
Wrapping and string

We will now discuss what happens for a classical length
L\sim g
where one should in principle expect wrapping corrections to kick in. Recall first that for a classical length
\mathcal{L}=L/2g=O(1)
, the continuum of states breaks up into discrete energy levels. This follows from the quantization of the momenta,

\sinh{\theta_{i}}=2\pi n_{i}/\mathcal{L}\,,

(129)

with
n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}
, and leads to the well-known BMN spectrum,

\gamma=\sum_{i=1}^{M}(\sqrt{1+(\frac{2\pi n_{i}}{\mathcal{L}})^{2}}-1)\,.

(130)

Besides this effect, which accounts for corrections that are power suppressed in
\mathcal{L}
, there are wrapping corrections. The latter come from virtual magnons going around the chain and are exponentially small in the case at hand
Ambjorn:2005wa
;
Bajnok:2008bm
. A nice property of the BMN spectrum is that it is free from such corrections: Neither the energy formula (
130
) nor the quantization conditions (
129
) receive exponentially small additions. This is because the worldsheet theory is free at strong coupling. (There is not even a vacuum energy shift, because of supersymmetry.)

We will argue that something similar happens for the HHL structure constants of interest, meaning that one only needs to plug the quantized momenta in (
121
) to keep track of the full dependence on
\mathcal{L}
, as long as the third bridge length
\ell_{C}=L-\ell_{A}
is much bigger than the dimension of the half-BPS operator
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

To be more precise, let us write the structure constants in the form

C^{\circ\circ\bullet}/C^{\circ\circ\circ}=\mathcal{N}_{L}\mathcal{A}(\ell_{A})\mathcal{B}(\ell_{B})\mathcal{W}_{\ell_{A},\ell_{B}}(l_{C})\,,

(131)

where the first three factors are as before. The new factor
\mathcal{W}
incorporates the wrapping corrections when
l_{C}=\ell_{C}/2g\sim\mathcal{L}\sim\mathcal{L}_{1}
is held fixed. It drops out when
l_{C}\rightarrow\infty
,

\mathcal{W}\rightarrow 1\,,

(132)

since then nothing can pass through the bridge
C
. For
l_{C}=O(1)
, there are plenty of mirror magnons, with relativistic energies, going through, and one naively expects
\mathcal{W}
to be a complicated function of all the quantum numbers in the problem, i.e., the splitting lengths
\ell_{A,B}
and the Bethe roots. The claim is that
\mathcal{W}(l_{C})=1
for any
\ell_{A,B}=O(1)
and for any state with
\gamma=O(1)
.

As said earlier, we cannot rely entirely on the hexagon series for the wrapping corrections. To overcome this problem we will first extract information about them from the classical string analysis. This one requires that all lengths be classical. Hence, we shall take

6
Conclusion

In this paper we studied HHL structure constants at strong coupling using the hexagon framework. We found that the mirror sums describing these correlators simplify drastically and can be computed exactly for any bridge lengths. For finite bridges, we observed that the answer splits into a global factor, written in terms of Gamma functions, and an internal part, given by the product of the BMN energies. This factorisation mimics the separation between the two kinematical domains that contribute, namely the giant mirror magnons and the low-lying relativistic magnons.

We have seen that this factorisation is robust and applies to a large family of operators, containing derivatives and scalar fields. However, as broad as this family is, it does not include neutral pairs, like
\mathcal{D}\bar{\mathcal{D}}
or
\mathcal{Y}\bar{\mathcal{Y}}
. It would be interesting to consider these pairs as they might enable a more precise comparison with the form factors found in the context of the SFT vertex, which are singlets under the
O(8)
symmetry of the free worldsheet theory. (They might also be needed for applications to higher-point functions.) These pairs are associated to a deeper layer of auxiliary roots in the nested Bethe ansatz construction. They are described by more complicated transfer matrices and it is not obvious that the strategy followed in this paper will apply to them. Optimistically, the mirror sum analysis can be bypassed and the finite-bridge amplitudes be bootstrapped using analyticity, crossing, supersymmetry, etc.

It would also be nice to find the precise counterparts of all these amplitudes in the worldsheet theory. An operatorial definition might facilitate the calculation of more general HHL form factors and shed light on the absence of wrapping advocated in this paper. It may also help filling the gap with the near-flat space limit
Minahan:2012fh
;
Bargheer:2013faa
;
Minahan:2014usa
. One could perhaps reverse-engineer the formula obtained using integrability to reconstruct the worldsheet vertex.

Although we could not match our findings with direct worldsheet calculations, we observed that they have all the desired features to stand as a string correlators in
AdS_{5}\times S^{5}
. Notably, the singularities associated to the mixing with double-traces are packed inside Gamma functions, in agreement with general results from Witten diagrams.
24
24

24

Similar formulae were also found at weak coupling in the large spin limit
Alday:2013cwa
;
Alday:2016mxe
using conformal bootstrap ideas. They hold when
\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}\sim 0
for a flux-tube energy
\gamma\sim 2\Gamma_{\textrm{cusp}}\log{S}=O(1)
.

The latter in fact predicts more Gamma functions than we have found here. The missing ones come with large arguments and drop out in the HHL kinematics. The semiclassical string formula is hinting at their “reappearance” in the forms of ratios of
b
-coefficients, see Eq. (
136
), which, as we have seen, are the classical counterparts of the Gamma functions.

Double-trace induced Gamma functions are omnipresent in holographic calculations and enter very naturally in the Mellin integrands of CFT/AdS correlators
Mack:2009mi
;
Penedones:2010ue
;
Rastelli:2016nze
. It would be fascinating to establish a connection with the integrability formulae found here by considering large-charge correlators of the type shown in figure
10
. These HHLL correlators can be obtained by sewing two pair-of-pants together using a complete sum of BMN operators, modulo double-traces. It is tempting to see in this inclusive sum the start of a Mellin integral. If so, the formulae obtained in this paper could help exploring the Mellin amplitudes in a more stringy regime.

It would also be interesting to compare our findings with the formulae obtained in
Kostov:2019stn
;
Kostov:2019auq
for large-charge 4pt functions. Although both arise from hexagons the comparison is not immediate since they run with different transfer matrices, which is also why they describe different observables of the boundary theory. For the 4pt functions, the transfer matrix
\mathcal{T}
is twisted such as to accommodate for the cross ratios
Fleury:2016ykk
. This innocuous operation makes a difference for the strong coupling scaling, since then
\mathcal{T}
is of order
O(1)
, as for semiclassical states, and the sum over bound states is regularised. It means that the saddle point will be trapped in the relativistic region, for generic values of the cross ratios. To escape from it and connect to our story, one might have to scale the cross ratios and work very close to the OPE limit / BPS point
\mathcal{T}=0
. This is also suggested by the funny scaling
\sim g^{\frac{1}{2}\gamma}
that we observed in this paper. It would be worth exploring this connection further in view of understanding how the 4pt function hexagon formula resums the tower of higher-rank structure constants, as well as to extract useful information about the latter if possible. It would also be interesting to explore how the “softening” of the scaling with the coupling, which we observed here for the two-bridge amplitude, is realised in the hexagonalised 4pt functions. It is certainly hinting at the need to average over the various mirror channels. The latter operation proved to be important at weak coupling
Fleury:2016ykk
to reproduce properties of the gauge-theory correlators. Similar magics might also be key at strong coupling to move away from the classical regime and match with the supergravity correlators.

Transfer matrix

The general formula for the eigenvalue of the transfer matrix can be found in
Beisert:2006qh
. Performing three mirror rotations and fixing the normalisation appropriately, we find

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1/x^{[-a]}-y_{j})(1-1/x^{[+a]}y_{j})}

(156)

\displaystyle\times\big[t_{a+1}(u)-t_{a}(u^{+})f^{-}_{a}(u)-t_{a}(u^{-})f^{+}_{a}(u)+t_{a-1}(u)f^{+}_{a}(u)f^{-}_{a}(u)\big]\,,

with
u^{\pm}=u\pm i/2
. Here, the
y
’s are fermionic roots at the first nested level in the
\eta=+1
grading. The
f
’s are functions of the main roots only,

f^{\pm}_{a}(u)=\prod_{j=1}^{M}\xi_{j}^{\mp 1}\frac{1-1/x^{[\pm a]}x_{j}^{\mp}}{1-1/x^{[\pm a]}x_{j}^{\pm}}\,,

(157)

with
\xi_{j}=(x^{+}_{j}/x^{-}_{j})^{1/2}
and with
\prod_{j=1}^{M}\xi_{j}=1
for a cyclic state.
t_{a}(u)
is the eigenvalue of a
\mathfrak{sl}(2)
XXX transfer matrix with auxiliary spin
\tfrac{1}{2}(a-1)
. For a state in the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector, we get to consider its vacuum eigenvalue with the
y
’s acting as inhomogeneities. It reads

t_{a}(u)=\sum_{k=0}^{a-1}P(u^{[a-1-2k]})\,,

(158)

with
P
the Baxter polynomial for the fermionic roots, which we recall here for convenience,

P(u)=g^{-N}\prod_{i=1}^{N}(u-v_{i})=\prod_{i=1}^{N}(x-y_{i})(1-1/xy_{i})\,,

(159)

with
u=g(x+1/x)
and
v_{i}=g(y_{i}+1/y_{i})
. These formulae are written assuming that the roots
\{y_{i}\}
are non vanishing. The case
y=0
is obtained as a limit and leads to the relation (
110
).

We can group the four terms of the transfer matrix differently, as done in Eq. (
32
), by introducing

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}=\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}\,(1-f^{\pm}_{a}(u))\,,

(160)

with
\mathcal{Q}^{\pm}
as in Eq. (
34
), and

\mathcal{T}^{\,0}=\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{1}{(1/x^{[-a]}-y_{j})(1-1/x^{[+a]}y_{j})}t_{a-1}(u)\left(1-f^{+}_{a}(u)\right)\left(1-f^{-}_{a}(u)\right)\,.

(161)

This recasting relies on

t_{a+1}(u)-t_{a}(u^{+})-t_{a}(u^{-})+t_{a-1}(u)=0\,,

(162)

and on other simple recurrence relations, which all follow from the definition (
158
).

At strong coupling, when the roots
\{x_{j}\}
are of order
O(1)
, the terms in brackets in (
161
) and (
160
) are of order
O(1/g)
. Plugging (
9
) into (
157
) and expanding in

1-f^{\pm}_{a}(u)\simeq\pm\frac{i}{g}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{x_{j}}{(x_{j}^{2}-1)(x^{[\pm a]}x_{j}-1)}\,,

(163)

leading to the expressions (
33
) and (
35
).

Yet another regime where
f\sim 1
is weak coupling. Then
x^{[\pm a]}\sim u^{[\pm a]}/g
and

f^{\pm}_{a}(u)=1\mp\frac{i\gamma}{2u^{[\pm a]}}+O(g^{4})\,.

(164)

We have similarly that
y_{i}\sim v_{i}/g
and therefore,

\mathcal{T}^{\pm}\simeq\pm\frac{i\gamma P(u^{[\mp a]})}{2u^{[\pm a]}P(0)}\,,\qquad\mathcal{T}^{\,0}=O(g^{4})\,,

(165)

after using (
34
) and (

Appendix B
Analytic regularisation

In this appendix we evaluate the one-particle integral at strong coupling using a different regularisation. Namely, instead of introducing a hard cut-off for the IR and UV regions, we modify the behaviour of the measure close to
r^{2}=x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}\sim 1
, using

\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)\rightarrow\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}-1)^{\alpha}\,,

(166)

where
\alpha\sim 0
, and compute the integrals in the two regions (over a complete domain in each case).

In the IR region we keep
a=O(1)
and expand the integrand for
g\rightarrow\infty
. The additional
\alpha
-dependent factor yields

(x^{[+a]}x^{[-a]}-1)^{\alpha}=(e^{\tilde{E}_{a}}-1)^{\alpha}\sim\left(\frac{a\varepsilon(s)}{2g}\right)^{\alpha}\,,

(167)

using
\tilde{E}_{a}\sim a\varepsilon(s)/2g
, with
s
along the unit circle. The sum over
a
gives Riemann zeta function evaluated at
1-\alpha
,

\sum\limits_{a=1}^{\infty}a^{-1+\alpha}=\zeta(1-\alpha)\simeq-\frac{1}{\alpha}+\gamma_{E}\,,

(168)

and thus

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{IR}=\int\frac{du(s)}{2\pi}\mathcal{T}(s)\zeta(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{\varepsilon(s)}{2g}\right)^{\alpha}\simeq\int\frac{du(s)}{2\pi}\mathcal{T}(s)(-\frac{1}{\alpha}+\gamma_{E}+\log{(\varepsilon(s)/2g)})\,,

(169)

with domain of integration
\{|s|=1,\Im m(s)>0\}
. The integral is easily taken and yields

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{IR}=-\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}+\frac{\gamma\gamma_{E}}{2}+\log{C}\,,

(170)

with
C
as in (
69
).

For the UV region, we take
a\sim g
and replace the sum
\sum_{a}
by an integral. Performing the average over the angular variable, we obtain (
63
) integrated from
1
to
\infty
with the deformed weight,

\mathcal{B}_{1}^{UV}=\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\gamma dr}{r^{2\beta+1}(r^{2}-1)^{1-\alpha}}=\frac{\gamma\Gamma(1+\beta-\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha)}{2\Gamma(1+\beta)}=\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}-\frac{\gamma}{2}(\gamma_{E}+\psi(1+\beta))+O(\alpha)\,.

(171)

(Note that
\mathcal{B}^{UV}
is defined for
\alpha>0
and then continued, while
\mathcal{B}^{IR}
was defined for
\alpha<0
and then continued.)

Now, both
\mathcal{B}^{IR}
and
\mathcal{B}^{UV}
are singular when
\alpha\rightarrow 0
, but their poles readily cancel in the sum. We get

\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathcal{B}_{1}^{IR}+\mathcal{B}_{1}^{UV}=\log{C}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\psi(1+\beta)\,,

(172)

in agreement with the result obtained using a hard cut-off.

Appendix C
Near-extremal correlators at weak coupling

In this appendix we analyse the octagon amplitude
\mathcal{B}(\ell)
at weak coupling. First, recall the weak coupling scaling of the
m
-particle integral
\mathcal{B}_{m}
at weak coupling
Basso:2015eqa
;
Eden:2018vug

g^{2m(m+\ell)}\times\mathcal{T}^{m}

(173)

where
\mathcal{T}
is for the eigenvalue of the transfer matrix. In the following we shall study what happens when we analytically continue the integrals close to
\ell=-1
for a primary in the
\eta=+1
grading. This point is the turning point for the exponent of the one-particle integral. The higher-
m
integrals are still parametrically smaller and can be discarded in a first approximation. Hence, in the following we shall restrict our discussion to the vacuum and one-particle integral

\mathcal{B}(\ell)\simeq 1+\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)\,.

(174)

Figure 11:
The one-particle integral is well defined for any
\ell_{\eta}>0
to any order at weak coupling, with
\ell_{\eta}=\ell+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\eta)
. However, the weak coupling series only converges for
\ell_{\eta}>\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
. To see what happens for
\ell_{\eta}=0
one must analytically continue around the pole at
\ell_{\eta}=\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
. To leading order at weak coupling, the residue at the pole determines the value of the integral at
\ell_{\eta}=0
.

To begin with, let us consider a scalar state and expand all the ingredients at weak coupling. We get

\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)\simeq\frac{a\gamma}{2(u^{2}+a^{2}/4)}\,,\qquad\mathcal{F}_{a}(u)\simeq 1\,,\qquad\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)\simeq\frac{ag^{2}}{(u^{2}+a^{2}/4)^{2}}\,,

(175)

and, after combining all factors together,

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)=\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{a\geqslant 1}\int\frac{du}{2\pi}\frac{a^{2}g^{2+2\ell}}{(u^{2}+a^{2}/4)^{\beta+3}}\,,

(176)

where here
\beta=\ell
. The integral over
u
is easily taken and the sum is expressed in terms of the Riemann zeta function,

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)=\frac{\gamma 2^{3+2\beta}g^{2+2\ell}\Gamma(\frac{5}{2}+\beta)\zeta(3+2\beta)}{\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(3+\beta)}\,.

(177)

It is smooth for
\ell>-1
and, despite the many factors, analytically continued to a function with a single pole at
\beta=-1
, coming from the Riemann zeta function,

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)\sim\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma g^{2+2\ell}}{\beta+1}\,.

(178)

The singularity comes from the large
r=\sqrt{u^{2}+a^{2}/4}
behaviour of the integrand. We can verify it by converting the sum over
a
into an integral, as done earlier at strong coupling,

\sum_{a}\int\frac{du}{2\pi}\rightarrow\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}dr\,\oint\frac{ds}{2\pi is}\,,

(179)

with the polar coordinates
u^{[\pm a]}=rs^{\pm 1}
and with a cut off set at
r=1
for convenience. (Since we are only interested in the residue at the leading pole, only the large
r
behaviour matters and the precise value of the lower bound is irrelevant. We also symmetrised the domain of integration in
a
, using the parity symmetry of the integrand.) Straightforward algebra gives

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)\sim-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma g^{2(1+\ell)}\int\limits_{1}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^{3+2\beta}}\oint\frac{ds}{2\pi is}(s-1/s)^{2}=\frac{\gamma g^{2(1+\ell)}}{2(1+\beta)}\,,

(180)

in agreement with (
178
).

If we were to expand further the integrand at weak coupling we would find higher poles at
\beta=-1
. They originate from the logarithms in the
\mathcal{F}
-factor and can be resummed using the asymptotic behaviour (
148
), that is

\mathcal{F}_{a}(u)\sim F(g)(u^{2}+\frac{a^{2}}{4})^{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma}\,,

(181)

with
F(g)=1+O(g^{2})
. The rest is power suppressed at large
r
or just modifies the overall factor by subleading corrections in
g^{2}
. Hence, resumming the leading singularities is done by using
\beta=\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
in the formulae above.

In particular, we find that the singular part of the loop corrections reads

\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma g^{2(1+\ell)}(1+O(g^{2}))}{1+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma}\,.

(182)

This behaviour determines the result at
\ell=-1
to leading order at weak coupling. It gives
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell=-1)=-1
which cancels the tree-level part in (
174
). Hence, as expected, the amplitude vanishes at
\ell=-1
. (Checking the zero at higher loops would require to take into account multi-particle corrections at some point, as well as to expand further the one-particle integral around
\beta=-1
.)

This analysis generalises to a generic primary in the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector with
P(0)\neq 0
, after replacing the transfer matrix by its general expression, see Eq. (
165
),

\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)\simeq\frac{i\gamma}{2}\frac{u^{[-a]}P(u^{[-a]})-u^{[+a]}P(u^{[+a]})}{u^{[+a]}u^{[-a]}P(0)}\,.

(183)

It is saying that adding
y
’s reduces a priori the degree of convergency of the integral, since the numerator is then of a higher degree in both
u
and
a
. Nonetheless, we can still evaluate the integral for large enough
\beta
. For a generic
P
, we use

\sum\limits_{a=1}^{\infty}a\int\frac{du}{2\pi}\frac{(u\pm ia/2)^{2k+1}}{(u^{2}+a^{2}/4)^{3+\beta}}=\pm i\frac{4^{2+\beta-k}\Gamma(2k-\beta-1)\Gamma(\tfrac{5}{2}+\beta-k)}{2\Gamma(\tfrac{1}{2})\Gamma(k-\beta-1)\Gamma(3+\beta)}\zeta(3+2\beta-2k)\,,

(184)

together with the fact that only odd powers survive. The large
a
behaviour (i.e., power counting) of the sum in the LHS directly maps to the pole of the
\zeta
-function in the RHS. We note however that this pole at
\beta=k-1
is absent from the full result when
k\neq 0
, since it multiplies a zero of the polynomial
\Gamma(2k-\beta-1)/\Gamma(k-\beta-1)
. The sole exception is
k=0
, which brings us back to (
177
). Therefore, we find here again a single, simple pole at
\beta=-1
with the same residue as before. It predicts a zero at
\ell=-1
. We can verify all of that using the angular average, which returns

\oint\frac{ds}{2\pi is}a\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)=\frac{\gamma}{2r}\oint\frac{ds}{2\pi is}(s-1/s)\frac{(r/s)P(r/s)-(rs)P(rs)}{P(0)}=\gamma\,.

(185)

for any polynomial
P
such that
P(0)\neq 0
.

The bottom line is that the position of the pole and its residue are independent of the spin
N
, despite the fact that the integral is seemingly less convergent at higher
N
. It agrees with the analysis in
Basso:2015zoa
which shows that the convergency of the integral improves and becomes spin independent if one first takes the sum over
a
. We see the same phenomenon here as coming from the angular integration.

Note finally that the state independence of the pole is unlikely to hold beyond the
\mathfrak{su}(1,1|2)
sector. The next layers of magnons will add conjugate derivatives
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{n}
, or equivalently boxes, which increase the twist of the operator,
t=L+\gamma+2n
. One thus expects the leading pole to show up at
\ell=-1+n+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
, with a small residue
\sim g^{2(1+\ell)}\sim g^{2n}
.

Appendix D
Leading pole at finite coupling

In this appendix we study the leading pole of the amplitude
\mathcal{B}(\ell)
at finite coupling and its relation to the one-particle integral
\mathcal{B}_{1}(\ell)
. We consider a scalar state for simplicity. The asymptotic behaviour of the one-magnon integrand for large
u
and large
a
is then simply given by

e^{-\ell\tilde{E}_{a}(u)}\tilde{\mu}_{a}(u)\mathcal{F}_{a}(u)\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)\sim\frac{a^{2}g^{2(1+\ell)}F(g)\gamma(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)}{2(u^{2}+a^{2}/4)^{1+\beta}}\,,

(186)

with
\beta=1+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma
. We used here the asymptotic behaviour (
148

\mathcal{F}_{a}(u)
and the fact that
\mathcal{T}_{a}(u)\sim a\gamma(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)/(2u^{[+a]}u^{[-a]})
which holds for scalar states.
It implies, after integrating over
a
and
r
, that the leading pole in
\mathcal{B}_{1}
is given by

\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{1}=\frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma(1+\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)g^{\gamma}F(g)}{1+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma}\,,

(187)

with
F(g)
the state-dependent constant (
149

\mathcal{B}(\ell)
. This is not the case because of the interaction (

\lim\limits_{x^{[\pm a]}\rightarrow\infty}\tilde{\Delta}_{ab}(u,v)=(y^{[+b]}y^{[-b]})^{-2}\,.

(188)

In other words, the pole is triggered by a single mirror magnon, which decouples from the rest up to a length shift. Namely,

\mathcal{B}(\ell)\sim\mathcal{B}^{sing}_{1}\times\mathcal{B}(\ell+2)\,,

(189)

close to the pole, or equivalently

\mathtt{Res}_{\ell\,=\,-1+\frac{1}{2}\gamma}\,\mathcal{B}(\ell)=(1+\ell)(2+\ell)g^{\gamma}F(g)\mathcal{B}(\ell+2)\,,

(190)

using (
187
). This equation should hold at any coupling
g
. It is verified at weak coupling, see Appendix
C
, and at strong coupling, using (
106
) together with
F(g)\rightarrow g^{-\gamma}
. What is more, in the latter case, a recurrence relation holds away from the pole,

\mathcal{B}(\ell+2)/\mathcal{B}(\ell)|_{g\rightarrow\infty}=\frac{(1+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)(2+\ell-\tfrac{1}{2}\gamma)}{(1+\ell)(2+\ell)}\,,

(191)

according to Eq. (

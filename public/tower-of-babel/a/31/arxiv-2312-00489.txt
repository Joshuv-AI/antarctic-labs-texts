Abstract
. We analyze a goal-oriented adaptive algorithm that aims to efficiently compute the quantity of interest G ⁡ ( u ⋆ ) G(u^{\star}) with a linear goal functional G G and the solution u ⋆ u^{\star} to a general second-order nonsymmetric linear elliptic partial differential equation. The current state of the analysis of iterative algebraic solvers for nonsymmetric systems lacks the contraction property in the norms that are prescribed by the functional analytic setting. This seemingly prevents their application in the optimality analysis of goal-oriented adaptivity. As a remedy, this paper proposes a goal-oriented adaptive iteratively symmetrized finite element method (GOAISFEM). It employs a nested loop with a contractive symmetrization procedure, e.g., the Zarantonello iteration, and a contractive algebraic solver, e.g., an optimal multigrid solver. The various iterative procedures require well-designed stopping criteria such that the adaptive algorithm can effectively steer the local mesh refinement and the computation of the inexact discrete approximations. The main results consist of full linear convergence of the proposed adaptive algorithm and the proof of optimal convergence rates with respect to both degrees of freedom and total computational cost (i.e., optimal complexity). Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical results and investigate the selection of the parameters.

Introduction

Adaptive finite element methods (AFEMs) are a cornerstone in the numerical
solution of partial differential equations (PDEs). The abundant
literature
emphasizes significant progress and manifests a matured
understanding
of the
topic; see,
e.g.,
[
Dör96
,

,
BDD04
,
Ste07
,

,

,

,

,

]

for linear elliptic PDEs.

The variational formulation of a

nonsymmetric
second-order linear elliptic PDE
with bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)

and right-hand side functional
F

on the Sobolev space
\mathcal{X}\coloneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

seeks a weak solution
u^{\star}

to

b(u^{\star},v)=F(v)\quad\text{for all}\quad v\in\mathcal{X}.

(1)

While standard AFEM aims at an
efficient approximation of the solution

u^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}
,
goal-oriented AFEM (GOAFEM) strives only
to approximate a
quantity of interest

G(u^{\star})
; see
[
BR01
,
BR03
,
EEHJ95
,
GS02
]
for
early prominent contributions.
However,
to accurately approximate
G(u^{\star})
for a
continuous linear
goal functional

G\colon\mathcal{X}\to\mathbb{R}
,
following the generic approach
G(u_{H})\approx G(u^{\star})

leads to convergence rates
determined by the error of the approximation

u_{H}\approx u^{\star}

to the
primal problem
(
1
).
Instead, GOAFEM adopts a duality technique by
additionally approximating
z_{H}\approx z^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}

solving the
dual problem

b(v,z^{\star})=G(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{X}.

(2)

Following
[
GS02
]
,
a discrete approximation

G_{H}(u_{H},z_{H})\approx G(u^{\star})

enables the control of the error for
any
u_{H},z_{H}\in\mathcal{X}
by

|G(u^{\star})-G_{H}(u_{H},z_{H})|\leq|b(u^{\star}-u_{H},z^{\star}-z_{H})|\leq L\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-z_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|,

(3)

where
L>0
is the continuity constant
of
b(\cdot,\cdot)
with respect to the energy norm
|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,\cdot\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|
; see Section
2
for details.
As seen in (
3
), this approach allows
to add the convergence rates
of the primal and dual problem.
Moreover, it is not necessary
–
and may even lead to unnecessary computational expense
–
to compute approximations

u_{H}\approx u^{\star}

and
z_{H}\approx z^{\star}

across the entire domain with the same accuracy.
Instead, a
careful marking of elements for refinement
enables a considerable reduction of the
computational costs and makes GOAFEM
highly relevant in both practical applications
and mathematical research.

The first challenge in the analysis of the GOAISFEM
algorithm consists of the nonlinear product structure
attained by the combined quasi-error product as displayed in
Table
1
. The resulting nonlinear remainder term
significantly complicates the proof compared to treating
only the primal problem as in
[
BIM+23
]
and requires the
application of a novel proof strategy from
[
BFM+23
]
that only
utilizes summability of the
remainder, denoted as

tail-summability
throughout. The second
challenge arises from the combination of the primal and dual
marking leading to a merged marked set. Thereby, either only
the primal or only the dual estimator is guaranteed to
satisfy the estimator reduction property. Since the
estimator belongs to the quasi-error, this also leads to a
failure of contraction for one of the two involved
quasi-errors. While
[
BGIP23
]
solves this issue in the
symmetric case, the additional symmetrization loop results
in a more involved situation at hand.
Adapting the novel
approach of the tail-summability criterion from
[
BFM+23
]

enables the proof of full linear convergence and
optimal complexity for the nonlinear
quasi-error product in this paper.
The analysis employs the
generalized quasi-orthogonality from
[
Fei22
]

to remedy the lack of a Pythagorean identity
for nonsymmetric problems.

Our main result asserts full linear convergence of
the quasi-error product

\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}
with respect to the total step counter
|\cdot,\cdot,\cdot|

(measuring the total solver steps in the index set).
Therein, we allow for an arbitrary symmetrization stopping
parameter
\lambda_{\textnormal{sym}}
and only require a
small algebraic solver
parameter
\lambda_{\textnormal{alg}}
such that the product
\lambda_{\textnormal{sym}}\,\lambda_{\textnormal{alg}}
is
sufficiently small.
More precisely, Theorem
5.1

states that there exist constants
C_{\textnormal{lin}}>0
and
0<q_{\textnormal{lin}}<1
such that,
for all
(\ell,k,j),(\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}

with
|\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime}|\leq|\ell,k,j|
,

\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\leq C_{\textnormal{lin}}\,q_{\textnormal{lin}}^{|\ell,k,j|-|\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime}|}\,\mathrm{H}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}.

Note that, unlike
[
BIM+23
]
, where
full linear convergence is guaranteed only for sufficiently large
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the current result is stronger in the sense that the result holds for

\ell_{0}=0

owing to a generalized
quasi-orthogonality from
[
Fei22
]
.
An immediate consequence of full linear convergence and the
geometric series in Corollary
6.1
states that the rates
with respect to the degrees of
freedom coincide with the rates with respect to the cumulative computational
work (i.e., computational time),
i.e., for all
r>0
, there holds

\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell,k,j)\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\bigl(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bigr)^{r}\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\leq\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell,k,j)\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}\\
|\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime}|\leq|\ell,k,j|\end{subarray}}\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)^{r}\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\leq C_{\rm cost}\,\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell,k,j)\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\bigl(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\bigr)^{r}\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}

along the sequence of meshes
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
generated by the GOAISFEM algorithm.
The second main result of Theorem
6.2

proves that, for sufficiently small adaptivity parameters and any achievable
rates

s,t>0
of the primal resp. dual problem (stated in terms
of nonlinear approximation classes),
the algorithm guarantees optimal complexity, i.e.,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell,k,j)\in\mathcal{Q}\end{subarray}}\Bigl(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Q}\\
|\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime}|\leq|\ell,k,j|\end{subarray}}\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell^{\prime}}\Bigr)^{s+t}\,\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\leq C_{\textnormal{opt}}\,\max\{\|u^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{s}}\,\|z^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},\,\mathrm{H}_{0}^{0,0}\,\mathrm{Z}_{0}^{0,0}\}.

This means the convergence of the algorithm
attains the optimal rate
s+t

with respect to the overall
computational work, where
\|u^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{s}}<\infty
means that
u^{\star}
can be approximated at rate
s
(along a
sequence of
unavailable optimal meshes) and likewise for
z^{\star}
.

2.1.
Continuous model problem

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 1
be a polygonal Lipschitz domain.
Given right-hand sides

f\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\bm{f}\in[L^{2}(\Omega)]^{d}
, we consider a
general second-order linear elliptic PDE

-\div(\bm{A}\nabla u^{\star})+\bm{b}\cdot\nabla u^{\star}+c\,u^{\star}=f-\div(\bm{f})\quad\text{in }\Omega\quad\text{subject to}\quad u^{\star}=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

(4)

with a pointwise symmetric and positive definite diffusion matrix

\bm{A}\in\bigl[L^{\infty}(\Omega)\bigr]^{d\times d}_{\textup{sym}}
, a
convection coefficient
\bm{b}\in\bigl[L^{\infty}(\Omega)\bigr]^{d}
, and a
reaction
coefficient
c\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
For well-definedness of the
a posteriori
error estimator
in Section
2.6
below, we additionally
require that

\bm{A}|_{T}\in\bigl[W^{1,\infty}(T)\bigr]^{d\times d}_{\textup{sym}}
and
\bm{f}|_{T}\in\bigl[H^{1}(T)\bigr]^{d}
for
all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
, where

\mathcal{T}_{0}
is an initial triangulation that subdivides

\Omega
into compact simplices.
Let
\langle\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,\rangle
denote the
L^{2}(\Omega)
-scalar
product.
With the principal part
a(u,v)\coloneqq\langle\bm{A}\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle
, the variational formulation of (
4
)
seeks a solution
u^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}\coloneqq H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
to
the so-called
primal problem

b(u^{\star},v)\coloneqq a(u^{\star},v)+\langle\bm{b}\cdot\nabla u^{\star}+c\,u^{\star},v\rangle=\langle f,v\rangle+\langle\bm{f},\nabla v\rangle\eqqcolon F(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{X}.

(5)

We suppose that the bilinear form
b(\cdot,\cdot)

from (
5
) is continuous
and elliptic with respect to the norm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}

on
\mathcal{X}
, i.e., there exist constants
L^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}>0
such that

b(u,v)\leq L^{\prime}\,\|u\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}}\quad\text{and}\quad b(v,v)\geq\alpha^{\prime}\,\|v\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal{X}.

(6)

Then, the Lax–Milgram lemma proves existence and uniqueness of the solution

u^{\star}
to (
5
).
An elementary compactness argument shows
that (
6
) implies
ellipticity of the principal part
a(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
and thus

a(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
is a scalar
product on
\mathcal{X}
with induced energy norm

a(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)^{1/2}\eqqcolon|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,\cdot\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\simeq\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}}
, cf.
[
BHP17
, Remark 3]
.
Therefore,
b(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
is also continuous and
elliptic with respect to
|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,\cdot\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|
, i.e.,
there
exist constants
L,\alpha>0
such that

b(u,v)\leq L\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\quad\text{and}\quad b(v,v)\geq\alpha\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|^{2}\quad\text{for all }u,v\in\mathcal{X}.

(7)

In the present paper, we suppose that
the
quantity of interest

G
is linear
and reads for
given data
g\in L^{2}(\Omega)
and
\bm{g}\in\bigl[L^{2}(\Omega)\bigr]^{d}
,

G(v)\coloneqq\int\limits_{\Omega}\bigl(g\,v+\bm{g}\cdot\nabla v\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x.

In order to guarantee well-definedness of the error estimator
in Section
2.6
below, we
suppose
\bm{g}|_{T}\in\bigl[H^{1}(T)\bigr]^{d}
for all
initial simplices
T\in\mathcal{T}_{0}
.
In view of the continuity and coercivity of

b(\,\cdot\,,\,\cdot\,)
, the Lax–Milgram lemma yields existence and
uniqueness of the solution

z^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}

of the so-called
dual problem
: Find
z^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}
such that

b(v,z^{\star})=G(v)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathcal{X}.

(8)

2.2.
Finite element discretization and discrete goal

For a polynomial degree
p\in\mathbb{N}
and a conforming
simplicial
triangulation

\mathcal{T}_{H}
of
\Omega
, the discrete ansatz space
reads

\mathcal{X}_{H}\coloneqq\{v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}\colon\forall\,T\in\mathcal{T}_{H},\ v_{H}|_{T}\ \text{is a polynomial of total degree}\leq p\}.

(9)

Since
\mathcal{X}_{H}\subset\mathcal{X}
is conforming, the Lax–Milgram
lemma ensures the existence and uniqueness of primal and dual
discrete solutions
u_{H}^{\star}
,
z_{H}^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}

satisfying

b(u_{H}^{\star},v_{H})=F(v_{H})\quad\text{and}\quad b(v_{H},z_{H}^{\star})=G(v_{H})\quad\text{for all }v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}.

(10)

It is well-known that conforming FEMs are quasi-optimal, i.e., there
hold Céa-type estimates with constant

C_{\textnormal{C\'{e}a}}=L/\alpha

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{C\'{e}a}}\,\min_{v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-v_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\quad\text{ and }\quad|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-z_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{C\'{e}a}}\,\min_{v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-v_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|.

(11)

For arbitrary approximations

u_{H},z_{H},\in\mathcal{X}_{H}

the linearity of the quantity of interest
G

as well as the primal and the dual problem
(
1
)
and (
2
)
show that

\displaystyle G(u^{\star})-G(u_{H})=G(u^{\star}-u_{H})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:intro:dual_problem}}}}{{=}}b(u^{\star}-u_{H},z^{\star})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:intro:weak_formulation}}}}{{=}}b(u^{\star}-u_{H},z^{\star}-z_{H})+\bigl[F(z_{H})-b(u_{H},z_{H})\bigr].

The definition of the discrete goal quantity by

G_{H}(u_{H},z_{H})\coloneqq G(u_{H})+\bigl[F(z_{H})-b(u_{H},z_{H})\bigr]

allows to control the goal error by continuity of
b(\cdot,\cdot)

|G(u^{\star})-G_{H}(u_{H},z_{H})|\leq|b(u^{\star}-u_{H},z^{\star}-z_{H})|\leq L\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu||\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-z_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|.

(12)

We emphasize that (
12
) holds for any

u_{H},z_{H}

and, in particular, for those stemming from an iterative solution
step. Moreover, if
u_{H}=u_{H}^{\star}
, then
G(u_{H},z_{H})=G(u_{H}^{\star})
as expected.

2.3.
Zarantonello iteration

The discrete formulations (
10
) lead to
positive definite, but

nonsymmetric
linear systems of equations. To reduce the formulation to
symmetric and positive definite (SPD) problems, we follow previous
own work
[
BIM+23
]
for the primal problem and employ the Zarantonello
iteration
[
Zar60
]
. Typically, the
latter is used in the up-to-date proof of the Lax–Milgram lemma and also defines a
linearization
scheme for the treatment of a certain class of nonlinear
elliptic
PDEs (see, e.g.,
[
CW17
,
GHPS18
,
HPSV21
,
BFM+23
]
). In
its core, it is a fixed-point method, thus
also applicable in
the nonsymmetric setting at hand. For a
damping parameter

\delta>0
and given
u_{H},z_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
,
the Zarantonello iterations
\Phi_{H}^{u},\Phi_{H}^{z}\colon(0,\infty)\times\mathcal{X}_{H}\to\mathcal{X}_{H}
compute
the unique solutions
\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;u_{H})
,

\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;z_{H})\in\mathcal{X}_{H}

to the symmetric variational formulations

\displaystyle a(\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;u_{H}),v_{H})

\displaystyle=a(u_{H},v_{H})+\delta\,\bigl[F(v_{H})-b(u_{H},v_{H})\bigr]\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\quad v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H},

(13a)

\displaystyle a(v_{H},\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;z_{H}))

\displaystyle=a(v_{H},z_{H})+\delta\,\bigl[G(v_{H})-b(v_{H},z_{H})\bigr]\quad

\displaystyle\text{for all}\quad v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}.

(13b)

The Riesz–Fischer theorem (and also the Lax–Milgram lemma)
guarantees existence and uniqueness of

\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;u_{H})
,
\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;z_{H})\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
, i.e.,
the Zarantonello operators

\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;\cdot)
and

\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;\cdot)
are well-defined.
In particular, the exact discrete solutions
u_{H}^{\star}=\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;u_{H}^{\star})
and

z_{H}^{\star}=\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;z_{H}^{\star})
are the
unique fixed points for
all
\delta>0
.
Moreover, for a sufficiently small damping parameter
\delta
,
i.e.,

0<\delta<\delta^{\star}\coloneqq 2\alpha/L^{2},

the Banach fixed-point theorem
[
Zei90
, Section 25.4]
guarantees that

\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta,\cdot)
and
\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta,\cdot)
are
contractive with constant
0<q_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\star}\coloneqq\bigl[1-\delta\,(2\alpha-\delta L^{2})\bigr]^{1/2}<1
, i.e., for all functions

v_{H},w_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
,
it holds that

\displaystyle\max\bigl\{|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;v_{H})-\Phi_{H}^{u}(\delta;w_{H})|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;v_{H})-\Phi_{H}^{z}(\delta;w_{H})|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\bigr\}\leq q_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\star}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v_{H}-w_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|.

(14)

The optimal value
\delta_{\mathrm{opt}}=\alpha/L^{2}

yields the
minimal contraction value
q_{\mathrm{sym}}^{\star}=1-\alpha^{2}/L^{2}
.

2.4.
Algebraic solver

A canonical candidate for
solving (
10
) directly
is a generalized minimal
residual method
[
Saa03
,
SS86
]
with optimal preconditioner for the
symmetric part. While this guarantees uniform
contraction of the algebraic residuals in a
discrete vector norm
,
the link between the algebraic
residuals and the functional setting
is still open
[
BIM+23
]
.
Instead, after a symmetrization with the Zarantonello iteration, it
remains to solve the SPD systems (
13
).
Since large SPD problems are still computationally expensive
and the exact solution cannot be computed in linear
computational complexity,
we employ an
iterative algebraic solver whose iteration is expressed by the
operator

\Psi_{H}\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\times\mathcal{X}_{H}\to\mathcal{X}_{H}.

More precisely, given a bounded linear functional
\psi\in\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
and an approximation

w_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}

of the exact solution
w_{H}^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
to

a(w_{H}^{\star},v_{H})=\psi(v_{H})

for all
v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
,
the algebraic solver returns an improved approximation
\Psi_{H}(\psi;w_{H})\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
in the sense
that there exists
0<q_{\textnormal{alg}}<1
independent of
\psi
and
\mathcal{X}_{H}
such that

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|w_{H}^{\star}-\Psi_{H}(\psi;w_{H})|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq q_{\textnormal{alg}}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|w_{H}^{\star}-w_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\quad\text{for all}\quad w_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}.

(15)

To simplify notation, we shall
identify
\psi
with its Riesz representative

w_{H}^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
and write

\Psi_{H}(w_{H}^{\star};\cdot)

instead of
\Psi_{H}(\psi;\cdot)
, even though

w_{H}^{\star}
is
unknown in
practice and will only be approximated by an optimal algebraic
solver,
e.g.,
[
CNX12
,
WZ17
,
IMPS24
]
.
In the following, we use the
hp
-robust multigrid method from
[
IMPS24
]
with localized lowest-order smoothing on
intermediate levels and patchwise higher-order smoothing on the finest mesh
as an innermost algebraic solver loop.

2.5.
Mesh refinement

The mesh
refinement employs newest-vertex bisection (NVB). We refer to

[
Ste08
]
for NVB with admissible initial triangulation

\mathcal{T}_{0}
and
d\geq 2
, to

[
AFF+15
,
KPP13
]
for NVB with general
\mathcal{T}_{0}
for
d\in\{1,2\}
, and to the
recent work
[
DGS23
]
for NVB with general

in any dimension

. For each
triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{H}
and marked elements

\mathcal{M}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{H}
, let

\mathcal{T}_{h}\coloneqq\texttt{refine}(\mathcal{T}_{H},\mathcal{M}_{H})

be the coarsest conforming refinement of
\mathcal{T}_{H}
such that
at least all
T\in\mathcal{M}_{H}

have been refined, i.e.,

\mathcal{M}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We write

\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{H})

if
\mathcal{T}_{h}
can be obtained from
\mathcal{T}_{H}
by finitely many steps of

\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}_{N}(\mathcal{T}_{H})
if
\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{H})
with

\#\mathcal{T}_{h}-\#\mathcal{T}_{H}\leq N

with the number of additional elements

.
To simplify notation, we write
\mathbb{T}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{0})

and
\mathbb{T}_{N}\coloneqq\mathbb{T}_{N}(\mathcal{T}_{0})
. We note that the nestedness of meshes

\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{H})
implies nestedness of the corresponding
finite element spaces

\mathcal{X}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{h}\subset\mathcal{X}

from (
9
).

2.6.
A posteriori
error estimation

For a triangle

and

denote the outer unit normal
vector and
[\![\,\cdot\,]\!]
the jump
along
inner edges of
\mathcal{T}_{H}
.
We define the refinement indicators

\eta_{H}(T;v_{H})\geq 0

and
\zeta_{H}(T;v_{H})\geq 0
for the primal and dual
problem from (
10

\displaystyle\eta_{H}(T;v_{H})^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq|T|^{2/d}\,\|-\div(\bm{A}\nabla v_{H}-\bm{f})+\bm{b}\cdot\nabla v_{H}+c\,v_{H}-f\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}

(16a)

\displaystyle+|T|^{1/d}\,\|[\![\bigl(\bm{A}\nabla v_{H}-\bm{f}\bigr)\cdot\bm{n}]\!]\|_{L^{2}(\partial T\cap\Omega)}^{2},

\displaystyle\zeta_{H}(T;v_{H})^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq|T|^{2/d}\,\|-\div(\bm{A}\nabla v_{H}-\bm{g})-\bm{b}\cdot\nabla v_{H}+\bigl(c-\div(\bm{b})\bigr)\,v_{H}-g\|_{L^{2}(T)}^{2}

\displaystyle+|T|^{1/d}\,\|[\![\bigl(\bm{A}\nabla v_{H}-\bm{g}\bigr)\cdot\bm{n}]\!]\|_{L^{2}(\partial T\cap\Omega)}^{2}.

For any subset
\mathcal{U}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{H}

\eta_{H}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{H})^{2}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{U}_{H}}\eta_{H}(T;v_{H})^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta_{H}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{H})^{2}\coloneqq\sum\limits_{T\in\mathcal{U}_{H}}\zeta_{H}(T;v_{H})^{2}

(16b)

as well as

\eta_{H}(v_{H})\coloneqq\eta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H};v_{H})

and

\zeta_{H}(v_{H})\coloneqq\zeta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H};v_{H})

for all
v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
.

For details on residual-based error estimators, we refer to
[
AO00
,
Ver94
]
.
Throughout the paper, the index of the estimators refer to the underlying mesh,
e.g.,
\eta_{h}
and
\zeta_{h}
on the refinement
\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{H})

or
\eta_{\ell}
and
\zeta_{\ell}
on a sequence of meshes
\mathcal{T}_{\ell}
with
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.
It is well-known that
\eta_{H},\zeta_{H}
satisfy the
following
axioms of adaptivity
.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
CFPP14
, Section 6.1]
)
.

The error estimators
\eta_{H},\zeta_{H}
from (
16
)
satisfy the following properties with constants
C_{\textnormal{stab}},C_{\textnormal{rel}},C_{\textnormal{drel}},C_{\textnormal{mon}}>0
and
0<q_{\textnormal{red}}<1
for any
triangulation
\mathcal{T}_{H}\in\mathbb{T}
and any conforming refinement
\mathcal{T}_{h}\in\mathbb{T}(\mathcal{T}_{H})

with the corresponding Galerkin solutions
u_{H}^{\star},z_{H}^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
,
u_{h}^{\star},z_{h}^{\star}\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
to (
10
),
any subset
\mathcal{U}_{H}\subseteq\mathcal{T}_{H}\cap\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
and arbitrary
v_{H}\in\mathcal{X}_{H}
,
v_{h}\in\mathcal{X}_{h}
.

(A1)

stability:

|\eta_{h}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{h})-\eta_{H}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{H})|+|\zeta_{h}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{h})-\zeta_{H}(\mathcal{U}_{H};v_{H})|\leq C_{\textnormal{stab}}\,|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|v_{h}-v_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|.

(A2)

reduction:

\eta_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{H};v_{H})\leq q_{\textnormal{red}}\,\eta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h};v_{H})

and

\zeta_{h}(\mathcal{T}_{h}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{H};v_{H})\leq q_{\textnormal{red}}\zeta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H}\setminus\mathcal{T}_{h};v_{H})
.

(A3)

reliability:

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{rel}}\,\eta_{H}(u_{H}^{\star})

and

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-z_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{rel}}\,\zeta_{H}(z_{H}^{\star})
.

(A3
+
)

discrete reliability:

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u_{h}^{\star}-u_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{drel}}\,\eta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{h},u_{H}^{\star})

and

|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z_{h}^{\star}-z_{H}^{\star}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|\leq C_{\textnormal{drel}}\,\zeta_{H}(\mathcal{T}_{H}\backslash\mathcal{T}_{h},z_{H}^{\star})

.

(QM)

quasi-monotonicity:

\eta_{h}(u_{h}^{\star})\leq C_{\textnormal{mon}}\,\eta_{H}(u_{H}^{\star})
and

\zeta_{h}(z_{h}^{\star})\leq C_{\textnormal{mon}}\,\zeta_{H}(z_{H}^{\star})
.

The constant

depends only on the uniform

\gamma
-shape
regularity of

\mathcal{T}_{H}\in\mathbb{T}
and on
the space dimension

and

For NVB, reduction (

.
∎

Reliability (
A3

A1
)
verify

\displaystyle|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|u^{\star}-u_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|

\displaystyle|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|z^{\star}-z_{H}|\mkern-1.5mu|\mkern-1.5mu|

In combination with the
estimate (

for

(17)

which provides the core estimate of the proposed adaptive algorithm
in Section

The ellipticity of

-
\sup

3.
Adaptive algorithm

In this section, we introduce our goal-oriented adaptive
iteratively symmetrized algorithm. It utilizes specific stopping
indices
denoted by an underline, e.g.,
{\underline{\ell}},{\underline{m}}[\ell],{\underline{n}}[\ell,k]\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

Table
1
above.
However, we may omit the dependence whenever it is apparent from the
context, such

{\underline{n}}\coloneqq{\underline{n}}[\ell,m]
for

{algorithm}
[GOAISFEM]

z_{0}^{0,0}=z_{0}^{0,\underline{\nu}}\in\mathcal{X}_{0}.

For all
\ell=0,1,2,\dots
, repeat the following steps
(I)
–
(IV)
:

(I)

SOLVE & ESTIMATE (PRIMAL).
For all
m=1,2,3,\dots
, repeat
(I)(a)
–
(I)(c)
:

(a)

Set

.

(b)

For all

, repeat the following steps
(I)((b))(i)
–
(I)((b))(ii)
:

(i)

Compute

and corresponding refinement indicators

(19)

(c)

Terminate
m

(II)

SOLVE & ESTIMATE (DUAL).

(b)

For all
\nu=1,2,3,\dots

(II)((b))(i)
–
(II)((b))(ii)
:

(i)

|\ell^{\prime},k^{\prime},j^{\prime}|\leq|\ell,k,j|\end{subarray}}(\mathrm{H}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell^{\prime}}^{k^{\prime},j^{\prime}})^{-1/(s+t)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathclap{\eqref{eq:lin_cv_sum}}}}{{\lesssim}}\bigl(\|u^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{s}}\,\|z^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}\bigr)^{1/(s+t)}(\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j})^{-1/(s+t)}.

Rearranging the terms and noting that
1\leq\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}

implies

\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1\leq 2\,(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0})
, we
obtain, for
\ell>0
, that

(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1)^{s+t}\,\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\lesssim\|u^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{s}}\,\|z^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}}.

(86a)

Moreover, full linear
convergence (
47
) proves that

(\#\mathcal{T}_{0}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1)^{s+t}\,\mathrm{H}_{0}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{0}^{k,j}=\mathrm{H}_{0}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{0}^{k,j}\lesssim\mathrm{H}_{0}^{0,0}\,\mathrm{Z}_{0}^{0,0}.

(86b)

We recall from
[
BHP17
, Lemma 22]
that, for
all

\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{T}
, it holds

\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1\leq\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}\leq\#\mathcal{T}_{0}\,(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1).

(87)

This shows, for all
(\ell,k,j)\in\mathcal{Q}
,

(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell})^{s+t}\,\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:bhp-lemma22}}}{{\lesssim}}(\#\mathcal{T}_{\ell}-\#\mathcal{T}_{0}+1)^{s+t}\,\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:optimality}}}{{\lesssim}}\max\{\|u^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{s}}\,\|z^{\star}\|_{\mathbb{A}_{t}},\mathrm{H}_{0}^{0,0}\,\mathrm{Z}_{0}^{0,0}\}

and concludes the proof of (
78
).

8.
Summary

In this work, we developed a cost-optimal goal-oriented adaptive finite element
method for the efficient computation of the quantity of interest
G(u^{\star})

with solution
u^{\star}
to the general second-order linear elliptic partial
differential equation (
4
). Since the current analysis of
iterative algebraic solvers for nonsymmetric systems with optimal preconditioner
only leads to contraction of the residual in a vector norm, we proposed a
nested iterative solver for the primal and dual problem in parallel. The strategy consists
of the Zarantonello iteration (
13
) as an outer solver loop and
an optimal multigrid solver for the arising SPD system as an innermost solver loop. In recent own work
[
BFM+23
]
,
we have shown that the link between convergence rates with respect to the degrees
of freedom and the total computational cost is full linear convergence of the quasi-error
\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}
.
To this end, Theorem
5.1
shows that the proposed adaptive
algorithm contracts (up to a multiplicative constant) the quasi-error product
\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}

in every step, independently of the algorithmic decision to employ mesh refinement, symmetrization, or the algebraic
solver. A particular problem in the analysis is that the nested iterative solver procedure only guarantees contraction as long as
1\leq k<{\underline{k}}[\ell]
, whereas contraction for the final iterate is only guaranteed up to an estimator term (cf. (
29
)). Another difficulty arises from the nonsymmetric setting with a quasi-Pythagorean estimate (
18
) replacing the usual Pythagorean estimate.
Therefore, the proof of Theorem
5.1
employs the equivalence of R-linear convergence and tail-summability of the
quasi-error product
\mathrm{H}_{\ell}^{k,j}\,\mathrm{Z}_{\ell}^{k,j}
and leads to mild restriction on the product
\lambda_{\textnormal{sym}}\,\lambda_{\textnormal{alg}}
of the involved solver stopping parameters.
The key ingredients to cost-optimality are an
adaptive mesh-refinement algorithm with optimal
convergence rate with respect to the number of
degrees of freedom (under the assumption of exact
solution) and an algebraic solver for the
linear system of equations that is contractive
with respect to the underlying Sobolev norm.
In this regard, the analysis in this paper may guide
the generalization to conforming discretizations of
vector-valued elliptic problems.
Finally, the numerical experiments in
Section
7
suggest that the proposed
strategy allows for large stopping parameter in practice
and that a larger choice is beneficial in terms of total
runtime.
Admittedly, the development of an optimal solver for the
nonsymmetric problem (
10
)
would allow to prove full linear convergence with an
arbitrary selection of the stopping parameter.

1.
Introduction

Phase retrieval refers to the problem of determining a function given its modulus only.
Such kind of questions are encountered in a number of applications including
diffraction imaging
[
20
]
, quantum mechanics
[
10
]
, audio
[
22
]
and astronomy
[
11
]
.

More precisely, given
\Omega
a set and
V\subseteq{\mathbb{K}}^{\Omega}
a function class with
{\mathbb{K}}\in\{{\mathbb{R}},{\mathbb{C}}\}
, phase retrieval asks to

reconstruct
F\in V
, given its pointwise modulus
|F|
.

Typically
V
is a linear space. If
|\lambda|=1
,
F
and
\lambda F
can not be distinguished from the phaseless observations. Thus reconstruction is at best possible up to a constant phase factor. We say that
V
does phase retrieval if

F,H\in V:\,|F|=|H|\quad\Rightarrow\quad\exists\,|\lambda|=1:\,\,H=\lambda F.

As for any inverse problem, stability is a property of fundamental importance: In order for the reconstruction task to be a well-posed question one requires that similar measurements can only arise from similar inputs. More formally, this can be expressed in terms of Lipschitz stability inequality:
Fix
\|\cdot\|
a norm. We say that
V
does stable phase retrieval if there exists
C>0
such that

\forall F,H\in V:\quad\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|\leq C\||H|-|F|\|.

If
V
does phase retrieval and
\dim V<\infty
, then automatically
V
does stable phase retrieval.
In infinite dimensions however, the situation becomes more interesting and there exist results in two different directions.
On the one hand, infinite-dimensionsional spaces
V
that satisfy certain rather general conditions do not do stable phase retrieval
[
6
,
1
]
.
On the other hand, there are a couple of very recent constructions of infinite-dimensional spaces which do stable phase retrieval
[
7
,
9
,
13
]
.

In order to circumvent the issue of a lack of Lipschitz stability,
one may consider a more flexible concept by allowing the constant
C
to depend on
F
, i.e.,

\forall H\in V:\quad\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|\leq C(F)\||H|-|F|\|.

We point out that even deciding whether
C(F)<\infty
is a highly non-trivial matter.
It is the subject of the present article to study precisely this type of concept of local Lipschitz stability and investigate its behaviour.

1.1.
Related results

For a large class of phase retrieval problems instabilities can be built by employing ’multi-component’ constructions, cf.
[
6
,
1
]
. From an abstract point of view the idea is to pick a pair of functions
F_{1},F_{2}\in V
that essentially live on disjoint domains, i.e. their product
F_{1}F_{2}
is small. Then, adding two such components results in an instability since

|F_{1}+F_{2}|\approx|F_{1}|+|F_{2}|\approx|F_{1}-F_{2}|.

The availability of such disjoint components is of course dictated by the concrete choice of the space
V
.
In many cases, a common feature of multi-component instabilities is disconnectedness.
To make matters more concrete we introduce the following isoperimetric quantity.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
be a domain and let
w\in C(\Omega,[0,\infty))\cap L^{1}(\Omega)
be a weight.
The
Cheeger constant
of
w
on
\Omega
is defined by

(1.1)

h(w,\Omega)\coloneqq\inf_{E}\frac{\int_{\partial E\cap\Omega}w(s)\,\mbox{d}s}{\min\left\{\int_{E}w(x)\,\mbox{d}x,\,\int_{\Omega\setminus E}w(x)\,\mbox{d}x\right\}},

where
E
runs through the set of all subsets of
\Omega
with smooth interior boundary
\partial E\cap\Omega
.

The Cheeger constant
h(w,\Omega)
quantifies the connectedness of
w
on
\Omega
: A small Cheeger constant indicates that there exists a partition into
E
and
\Omega\setminus E
such that
w
has roughly the same
L^{1}
volume while the weight is rather small on the seperating boundary; this is the disconnected case. In the connected case however (to have a concrete example, think of a Gaussian) both of these objectives are not feasible at the same time.
We refer to the introductory section of
[
18
]
for more details on phase retrieval instabilities, the role of connectivity for phase retrieval as well as the Cheeger constant.

To provide some more context let us briefly discuss our results in
[
18
,
19
]
.
The central object in both of these articles is the short-time Fourier transform.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
The
short-time Fourier transform
(STFT) of
f
w.r.t. the window function
g
is defined as

(1.2)

{\mathcal{V}}_{g}f(x,\xi)\coloneqq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\xi t}\,\mbox{d}t,\quad x,\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}.

The results in
[
1
]
show – irrespectively of the choice of
g
– that
V={\mathcal{V}}_{g}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
allows for construction of multi-component instabilities.
The main insight of our earlier result is that disconnectedness of the spectrogram is the only source of instabilities for the STFT phase retrieval problem with Gaussian window function.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
19
]
, informal)
.

Let
\varphi(t)=e^{-\pi t^{2}}
and let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
a domain.
There exists a universal constant
c>0
such that for all
F,H\in{\mathcal{V}}_{\varphi}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
1
1

1

We identify
F,H
with their respective restrictions to the subdomain
\Omega
.

(1.3)

\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq c\big(1+h(|F|,\Omega)^{-1}\big)\||H|-|F|\|_{W(\Omega)},

with
\|\cdot\|_{W(\Omega)}
a certain first order weighted Sobolev norm.

1.2.
Aim of this work

Theorem
1.3
heavily relies on the fact that
{\mathcal{V}}_{\varphi}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
consists – up to a simple manipulation – of entire functions exclusively.
Once the Gaussian
\varphi
is replaced by any other window function this strong structural property goes missing and the proof method breaks down.
It is the purpose of this present work to establish the connection between local Lipschitz stability and the connectivity of the spectrogram in a more general setting and to show that this is actually not a consequence of the fact that the functions under consideration are holomorphic.

1.3.
Main results

Before we present the main results of this paper we need to settle quite some terminology.
We will work with a novel concept of stability based on the reformulation of phase retrieval in the lifted setting. That is,

reconstruct
F\otimes\overline{F}
, given
|F|^{2}
.

Note that
|F|^{2}
is just the restriction of
F\otimes\overline{F}
to the diagonal
D=\{(z,z),z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\}\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
. The task may therefore be understood as an extension problem from
D
to all of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}
.
From this point of view the natural quantitative concept looks as follows.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
V\subseteq L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a linear space and let
\rho:{\mathbb{R}}^{2d}\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable weight.
We say that
V
does
\rho
-stable lifted phase retrieval (
\rho
-SLPR) if there exists a finite constant
C>0
such that

(1.4)

\forall F,H\in V:\quad\left\|F\otimes\overline{F}-H\otimes\overline{H}\right\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}\leq C\left\||F|^{2}-|H|^{2}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

A central role will be played by Poincaré inequalities.
For the sake of maximal flexibility we work with a non-symmetric version.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
v\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be non-negative and let
w:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
be measurable.
We say that
v,w
support a
Poincaré inequality
if there exists a finite number
C>0
such that

(1.5)

\forall u\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\quad\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

The smallest possible constant
C
will be denoted by
C_{P}(v,w)
and is called the
Poincaré constant
2
2

2

this number may be finite or infinite
.
If
v=w
we just write
C_{P}(w)
instead of
C_{P}(v,w)
.

The philosophy is as follows: The ideal case is that
w
supports a Poincaré inequality. However, sometimes this is not the case (for example due to insufficient decay of
w
) and one needs to make amends. Weakening the requirement by replacing the weight
w
on the left hand side by
v\leq w
potentially changes things in the sense that
C_{P}(v,w)<\infty
while
C_{P}(w)=\infty
, cf. Section
2.2.1
.

A key tool that will appear at multiple places in the proofs is replacing weights by translates of themselves.
In order to have the correct control on the error made in these steps we introduce the following concept.

Definition 1.6
.

We say that
\gamma\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{d},[0,\infty))
is a
translation stable weight
(TSW) if there exists a locally bounded function
\mu:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
such that

(1.6)

\forall z,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\quad\gamma(z+\tau)\leq\mu(\tau)\gamma(z).

Prototypes of TSWs are
\gamma(t)=e^{-a|t|}
and
\gamma(t)=(1+|t|)^{-a}
where
a>0
, as well as products and tensor products of these types of functions.
Note that Gaussians are not TSWs.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
\gamma\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a non-negative weight on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
Associated to such a
\gamma
is another weight
\Gamma
on the product space
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
defined by

(1.7)

\Gamma(z,z^{\prime})=\Gamma[\gamma](z,z^{\prime})\coloneqq\left(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma\right)(z-z^{\prime}),\quad z,z^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

where
\mathcal{R}
denotes the reflection operator, i.e.
\mathcal{R}f(x)=f(-x)
.

Last but not least, we need to settle w.r.t. which distance notions we measure the respective errors in our stability analysis.

Definition 1.8
.

Given
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,\infty)
measurable we define a weighted mixed Lebesgue norm by

(1.8)

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\coloneqq\left\|\tau\mapsto\left\|F\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}(\tau))}\right\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}.

If
\chi=1
, we will write
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}}
instead of
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}
.
Moreover, we define

\mathfrak{d}(\phi,\psi):=\|\phi^{2}-\psi^{2}\|_{L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})}^{1/2},\quad\phi,\psi\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).

The following properties are easily verified:

•

(L^{4}_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\mathfrak{d})
forms a metric space with
L^{4}_{+}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\{\phi\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\,\phi\geq 0\,\,\text{a.e.}\}
.

•

\mathfrak{d}
is consistent w.r.t. scaling in the sense that

\mathfrak{d}(c\phi,c\psi)=c\mathfrak{d}(\phi,\psi)
if
c\geq 0
.

•

If
\chi
is bounded then
(L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)})
forms a normed vector space (this follows from observing that

\|F\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{4}=\||F|^{2}\ast\mathds{1}_{B_{1}}\|_{2}^{2}
and Young’s inequality).

Our first main result takes a rather general form. Its proof is given in Section
3.1
.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a measurable weight and suppose that

•

\gamma
is an integrable TSW and

•

V\subseteq L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
does
\Gamma
-SLPR, where
\Gamma=\Gamma[\gamma]
.

There exists
C>0
(depending on
d
,
V
and
\gamma
only) such that

(1.9)

\forall F,H\in V:\quad\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq C(1+C_{P}(w\chi,w))^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\mathfrak{d}(|F|,|H|),

with
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
.

Neglecting technical details, the content of Theorem
1.9
can be summarised as follows:
If
V
does stable lifted phase retrieval, then the local Lipschitz constant
C(F)
of the associated phase retrieval problem is controlled in terms of the Poincaré constant w.r.t. the weight
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
.
While the statement is pleasing as its rather general it contains the crucial assumption of
V
doing
\Gamma
-SLPR.
To fill the theorem with life we will show that it leads to stability estimates for STFT phase retrieval.
We subsume the configurations which we can deal with under the following notion of admissibilty.

Definition 1.10
.

A pair
(g,\gamma)
consisting of a window function
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and a TSW
\gamma
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
is called an
admissible pair
if

•

the modulus of the ambiguity function
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW, i.e.,

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|\leq\mu(\tau)|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z)|,\quad z,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

with
\mu
locally bounded and

•

the controlling function
\mu
satisfies the integrability condition
\mu\in L^{2}(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)
.

In order to highlight that the above notion of admissibility is a meaningful concept we show that it is satisfied by two natural window functions collected in the lemma below.

Lemma 1.11
.

Given
a,b>0
, let
\gamma(x,\xi)=\exp(-a|x|-b|\xi|)
.
Assume either of the following configurations:

(i)

Let
g(t)=\exp(t-e^{t})
,
a>2\pi^{2}
and
b>2
.

(ii)

Let
g(t)=e^{-t}\mathds{1}_{(0,\infty)}(t)
,
a>2
and
b>0
.

Then
(g,\gamma)
forms an admissible pair.

.

Remark 1.12

(Further windows)
.

The
fundamental identity of time-frequency analysis

[
14
, formula (3.10)]
states that

{\mathcal{V}}_{\phi}\psi(x,\xi)=e^{-2\pi ix\xi}\cdot{\mathcal{V}}_{\hat{\phi}}\hat{\psi}(\xi,-x),\quad\phi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}).

Therefore, with
\widetilde{\gamma}(x,\xi):=\gamma(\xi,-x)
we have that
(g,\gamma)
is an admissible pair if and only if
(\hat{g},\widetilde{\gamma})
is an admissible pair.
Hence, for every window
g
appearing in the list in Lemma
1.11
we could have included its Fourier transform
\hat{g}
as well.
More generally, one could also include the fractional Fourier transform of any such window by making use of the relation in
[
17
, Theorem 2.3]
.
For the sake of compactness we refrained from explicitly including these as further examples in Lemma
1.11
.

Our second main result establishes a link between local Lipschitz stability for STFT phase retrieval and Poincaré constants and is proven in Section
3.2
. The result reads as follows.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to[0,1]
be a measurable weight and let
(g,\gamma)
be an admissible pair.
There exists
C>0
(depending on
g
and
\gamma
only) such that for all

F,H\in{\mathcal{V}}_{g}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))

(1.10)

\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq C(1+C_{P}(w\chi,w))^{\frac{1}{4}}\cdot\mathfrak{d}(|F|,|H|),

with
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
.

Remark 1.14

(Comparison with earlier results)
.

In the default case
\chi\equiv 1
, the Poincaré constant appearing on the right is the ordinary one,
C_{P}(w)
.
The celebrated
Cheeger inequality

[
8
]
asserts that
Poincaré and Cheeger constants are related by

(1.11)

C_{P}(w)\leq\frac{4}{h(w,{\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}.

Thus, the Poincaré constant appearing on the right in (
1.9
) may be replaced by the squared reciprocal of the Cheeger constant.
Hence, Theorem
1.9
is in fact of the same flavour as Theorem
1.3
.
A major improvement compared to the results in
[
18
,
19
]

is that here the differences of the spectrograms is computed w.r.t.
\mathfrak{d}
instead of the heavier weighted first order Sobolev norm in Theorem
1.3
.

Another difference is the precise shape of the weight which goes into the Poincaré constant. Instead of taking the spectrogram as the weight, here it is a smoothed version of the spectrogram. Corollary
1.15
below suggests that this convolution might actually have a beneficial effect as it avoids issues arising from zeros of the weight function.

As an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.13
we get a statement on stable phase reconstruction on compact sets.

Corollary 1.15
.

Let
\chi=\mathds{1}_{K}
with
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
compact and let
(g,\gamma)
be an admissible pair.
Moreover, let
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and
F={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f
.
There exists a finite constant
L>0
(depending on
f,g,\gamma
and
K
) such that

\forall H\in{\mathcal{V}}_{g}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})):\quad\inf_{|\lambda|=1}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq L\cdot\mathfrak{d}(|F|,|H|).

As the proof is rather short we present it right here.

Proof.

If
f=0
, by Jensen’s inequality

\|H\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}^{4}\leq\|H\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{4}=\||H|^{2}\ast\mathds{1}_{B_{1}(0)}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\||H|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot\|\mathds{1}_{B_{1}(0)}\|_{L^{1}}^{2}=\mathfrak{d}(0,|H|)^{4}\cdot|B_{1}(0)|^{2}.

It remains to show that
C_{P}(w\chi,w)<\infty
if
F\neq 0
and
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
, because then the statement follows from Theorem
1.13
.
To that end, let
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be
open, connected and bounded with smooth boundary such that
\Omega\supseteq K
. Then, as
w\chi\leq w\mathds{1}_{\Omega}\leq w
we have that

C_{P}(w\chi,w)\leq C_{P}(w\mathds{1}_{\Omega})\leq\frac{\sup_{x\in\Omega}{w(x)}}{\inf_{x\in\Omega}w(x)}C_{P}(\mathds{1}_{\Omega}).

Note that
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
is continuous, bounded and strictly positive. Thus,
\inf_{x\in\Omega}w(x)>0
and
\sup_{x\in\Omega}w(x)<\infty
.
Since bounded, connected domains with sufficiently regular boundary support unweighted Poincaré inequalities
[
12
]
, we have that
C_{P}(\mathds{1}_{\Omega})<\infty
and are done.
∎

1.4.
Discussion and open question

1.4.1.
Extension to the multivariate case

We have restricted ourselves to the univariate setting in Theorem
1.13
.
However, it is straight forward to extend the result to the multivariate setting for the case where the window
g
is chosen to be a tensor product

g=g_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes g_{d}
with
(g_{1},\gamma_{1}),\ldots,(g_{d},\gamma_{d})
admissible pairs.
This is due to the fact that in this case the ambiguity function is precisely the tensor product of the respective one-dimensional ambiguity functions, i.e.,

{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g={\mathcal{V}}_{g_{1}}g_{1}\otimes\ldots\otimes{\mathcal{V}}_{g_{d}}g_{d}.

1.4.2.
SLPR beyond STFT

So far we only know how to establish
\Gamma
-SLPR when
V={\mathcal{V}}_{g}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
arises as the image of the STFT (and for very particular windows
g
only). For that case we have rather explicit knowledge what the relevant extension operator looks like (see Section
2.3
).
From this perspective it is natural to ask:

Can the general result, Theorem
1.9
be applied beyond the STFT setting?
Can the property of SLPR be established for any other natural function classes
V
?

1.4.3.
Concentration implies finite Poincaré constant?

Any weight
w
which supports a Poincaré inequality has exponential concentration, cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2]
.
The converse is of course not true: Just consider a weight which has compact but disconnected support. In our situation such weights are however not permitted as
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
is strictly positive.
One may therefore ask:

Is exponential concentration of
F={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f
sufficient for
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
to support a Poincaré inequality?

More formally, this can be phrased as follows:

Conjecture 1.18
.

Let
g(t)=\exp(t-e^{t})
, and let
\gamma(x,\xi)=\exp(-a|x|-b|\xi|)
,
a,b>0
.
Suppose that
f\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
has exponential time-frequency concentration, i.e.,

\exists\delta>0:\quad|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}f(z)|\cdot e^{\delta|z|}\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{2})

and let
w=(|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}f|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
.
Then
C_{P}(w)<\infty
.

Note that for
g
the one-sided exponential the above conjecture is an empty statement since there are no functions
f
(except for the zero function) for which the spectrogram
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}f|
has exponential concentration as the window is discontinuous.
This is why the conjecture has been formulated explicitly for
g(t)=\exp(t-e^{t})
.

1.5.
Notation

We say that
w
is a weight on a domain
\Omega
if
w
is a measurable and non-negative function on
\Omega
.
Given a function
f
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we denote its reflection by
\mathcal{R}f=f(-\cdot)
.
The indicator function of a set
A
is denoted by
\mathds{1}_{A}
.
By
B_{r}(x)\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we denote the open ball of radius

centered at
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We use the notation
f\otimes g
for the tensor product, that is,
(f\otimes g)(x,y)=f(x)g(y)
.
For
f,g:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
we write
f\lesssim g
if there exists a finite

such that
f(x)\leq cg(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
.
We use the notation
f\asymp g
in case
f\lesssim g
and
g\lesssim f
.

The Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is defined by

\hat{f}(\xi)=(\mathcal{F}f)(\xi)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2\pi i\xi x}\,\mbox{d}x,\quad\xi\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},

and is extended to
L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
in the usual way.
Given
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
measurable we denote the weighted
L^{p}
-norm of a measurable function
F
by

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\chi)}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|F(x)|^{p}\chi(x)\,\mbox{d}x\right)^{1/p}

Occasionally, there can appear either a set
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
or a measure
\mu
instead of
\chi
in the argument.
Perhaps unsurprisingly, this is then to be understood in the following ways:

\|F\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}=\|F\cdot\mathds{1}_{\Omega}\|_{L^{p}},\quad\text{and}\quad\|F\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}=\left(\int|F(x)|^{p}\,\mbox{d}\mu(x)\right)^{1/p}.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Key lemma

The following innocent result will play a rather crucial role.
It provides us with a quantitative link between standard phase retrieval and its lifted reformulation.

Lemma 2.1
.

For all
\phi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
it holds that

\frac{\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}}{2}\cdot\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|\psi-\lambda\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\psi\otimes\overline{\psi}-\phi\otimes\overline{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Proof.

We define three non-negative numbers by

a=\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
,

b=\|\psi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
and

c=|\langle\psi,\phi\rangle_{L^{2}}|
.
Elementary computations show that

\min_{|\lambda|=1}\|\psi-\lambda\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=a+b-2c

and that

\|\psi\otimes\overline{\psi}-\phi\otimes\overline{\phi}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}-2c^{2}.

As
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
and
a+b\geq 2c
we get that

\frac{a+b}{2}\cdot\left(a+b-2c\right)\leq a^{2}+b^{2}-2c^{2}-c(a+b-2c)\leq a^{2}+b^{2}-2c^{2}

and are done.
∎

2.2.
Prerequisites on Poincaré inequalities

2.2.1.
Estimates for Poincaré constants

In this section we collect a couple of sufficient conditions on the weights which guarantee that a Poincaré inequality holds.

First up are log-concave weights. Recall that a function
f:\Omega\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
with
\Omega\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
a convex domain is called log-concave if

\log f
is concave. Perhaps the simplest example of a log-concave function is a Gaussian.
The following classical result states that log-concave weights support Poincaré inequalities.

Theorem 2.2

(Bobkov
[
3
]
)
.

Let
w:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be log-concave weight which induces a probability measure (i.e.,
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}w(x)\,\mbox{d}x=1
).
There exists a universal constant
K>0
(independent of
w
and
d
) such that

C_{P}(w)\leq K\|x\mapsto|x-x_{0}|\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2},

where
x_{0}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}x\,w(x)\mbox{d}x
is the barycenter of the measure.

Next we consider an example of a weight which is not log-concave: The Cauchy distribution has the density

(2.1)

w_{\beta}(x)=\frac{1}{Z}\left(1+|x|^{2}\right)^{-\beta},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\,\beta>\frac{d}{2},

where
Z
is the appropriate normalization factor that turns
w_{\beta}
into a probability distribution.

Theorem 2.3

(Bobkov and Ledoux
[
4
]
)
.

If
\beta\geq d+1
, then
C_{P}\left(\frac{w_{\beta}(x)}{1+|x|^{2}},w_{\beta}(x)\right)\leq\frac{1}{2\beta}
.

The next result tells us how Poincaré constants behave under tensorisation.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}
be densities of probability measures on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}
and
{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}}
, respectively.
Let
w_{1},w_{2}
be weights such that
v_{1}\leq w_{1}
and
v_{2}\leq w_{2}
.
Then it holds that

C_{P}(v_{1}\otimes v_{2},w_{1}\otimes w_{2})\leq\max\{C_{P}(v_{1},w_{1}),C_{P}(v_{2},w_{2})\}.

For the symmetric case (i.e.,
v_{1}=w_{1}
and
v_{2}=w_{2}
) the above inequality is well-known, cf.
[
5
, Theorem 4.1]
.
The general asymmetric case follows by some minor adaptations.
For the sake of completeness we give a full proof.

Proof of Lemma
2.4
.

For
k\in\{1,2\}
, let us denote
\mbox{d}\mu_{k}(x)=w_{k}(x)\mbox{d}x
and
\mbox{d}\nu_{k}(x)=v_{k}(x)\mbox{d}x
.
Moreover, set
\mu=\mu_{1}\otimes\mu_{2}
and
\nu=\nu_{1}\otimes\nu_{2}
.

Note that if
\sigma
is a probability measure on some measure space and
u\in L^{2}(\sigma)
, then

\inf_{c}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}=\left\|u-\langle u,1\rangle_{L^{2}(\sigma)}1\right\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}-\left(\int u\,\mbox{d}\sigma\right)^{2}.

Let
u\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d_{1}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d_{2}},{\mathbb{R}})
arbitrary.
With this, rewrite

\displaystyle(\ast):=\inf_{c}\|u-

\displaystyle c\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int u(x,y)^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\right)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)-\left(\iint u(x,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\left(\int u(x,y)^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\right)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)-\int\left(\int u(x,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\right)^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)

\displaystyle\quad+\int\left(\int u(x,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\right)^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)-\left(\iint u(x,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)\right)^{2}.

With the notation
u_{y}(x)=u(x,y)
and
\phi(y)=\int u(\cdot,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}
, we further obtain

\displaystyle(\ast)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\|u_{y}\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{1})}^{2}-\left(\int u_{y}\mbox{d}\nu_{1}\right)^{2}\right)\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)+\left(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{2})}^{2}-\left(\int\phi\mbox{d}\nu_{2}\right)^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int\left(\inf_{c}\|u_{y}-c\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{1})}^{2}\right)\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)+\inf_{c}\|\phi-c\|_{L^{2}(\nu_{2})}^{2}.

Next we apply the respective Poincaré inequality to each of the terms and get that

(2.2)

\inf_{c}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(\nu)}^{2}\leq C_{P}(v_{1},w_{1})\int\|\nabla u_{y}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{1})}^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)+C_{P}(v_{2},w_{2})\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{2})}^{2}.

Now it suffices to prove that

(2.3)

\int\|\nabla u_{y}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{1})}^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)+\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{2})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

Indeed, (
2.3
) implies that (
2.2
) is bounded by
\max\{C_{P}(v_{1},w_{1}),C_{P}(v_{2},w_{2})\}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}
, which implies the statement of the lemma.

We use the notation
\nabla_{x}u=\left(\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{1}},\ldots,\frac{\partial u}{\partial x_{d_{1}}}\right)^{T}
, and
\nabla_{y}u
is defined accordingly.
Since
v_{2}\leq w_{2}
we have that

(2.4)

\int\|\nabla u_{y}\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{1})}^{2}\,\mbox{d}\nu_{2}(y)\leq\|\nabla_{x}u\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{1})}^{2}.

For the second term, interchanging order of integration and differentiation shows that

\nabla\phi(y)=\int\nabla_{y}u(x,y)\,\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x)
.
By Jensen’s inequality,

|\nabla\phi(y)|^{2}\leq\int|\nabla_{y}u(x,y)|^{2}\mbox{d}\nu_{1}(x).

Integrating both sides w.r.t.
\mu_{2}
and using that
v_{1}\leq w_{1}
, implies that

\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}(\mu_{1})}^{2}\leq\|\nabla_{y}u\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}.

This, together with (
2.4
) gives (
2.3
), and we are done.
∎

Remark 2.5

(Normalisation)
.

Later on we will use the results of this section to show that certain weights support Poincaré inequalities.
This means, we are interested in configurations which yield finite Poincaré constants without caring too much about the concrete values.
For this purpose the assumption that
w(x)\mbox{d}x
is a probability measure can always be neglected by rescaling.

2.2.2.
A Poincaré inequality without derivatives

The objective of this paragraph is to derive a version of a Poincaré inequality which can be applied to functions with little regularity.
As a substitute for the length of the gradient we introduce the following concept.

Definition 2.6
.

The
local deviation
of
u\in L_{loc}^{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is defined by

(2.5)

\delta_{1}[u](x)\coloneqq\left(\int_{B_{1}(x)}|u(y)-u(x)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}y\right)^{1/2},\quad x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}.

What we are interested in is the following version of a Poincaré inequality.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
v,w
be weights on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and assume that
v\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The
modified Poincaré
constant, denoted by
C_{P}^{*}(v,w)
is the smallest possible constant
C>0
such that

(2.6)

\forall u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\quad\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}\leq C\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

As we shall see next, if
v,w
support a Poincaré inequality (in the ordinary sense) then
C_{P}^{*}(v,w)
is finite as well.

Theorem 2.8
.

Let
v,w
be weights on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and assume that

v\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and that
v\leq w
pointwise.
There exists a finite constant
c
(depending on
d
only) such that

(2.7)

C_{P}^{*}(v,w)\leq c\cdot(1+C_{P}(v,w)).

Remark 2.9

(Motivation for looking at modified Poincaré inequalities)
.

The ordinary Poincaré inequality is ultimately about the question
’To what extent is the norm of the derivative indicative about how close a function is to a constant?’.
Figure
2
shows
two basic function on an interval of the real line.
The more interesting one is the oscillatory function: Its derivative will have a relatively large norm due to the oscillations while the function is close to a constant. This suggests that the quantity
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}
on the right hand side of a Poincaré inequality is unnecessarily excessive in this case.
Theorem
2.8
can be understood as an approval of this intuition as it effectively allows us to replace
\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}
by
\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}}
.

Figure 2
.
Example of a purely oscillatory function with a small amplitude (blue, solid line) and a function which actually deviates much from its mean (black, dashed line).

Proof of Theorem
2.8
.

Let
u\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be an arbitrary function.
Moreover, let
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a bump function, which satisfies that
\suppp\phi\subseteq B_{1}(0)
and that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\phi(x)\,\mbox{d}x=1
.
We set
\tilde{u}\coloneqq u\ast\phi
and notice that
\tilde{u}\in C_{b}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.
We apply the (ordinary) Poincaré inequality on
\tilde{u}
and get that

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}+\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|\tilde{u}-c\|_{L^{2}(v)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}+C_{P}(w)\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\right)

We will slightly abuse notation and use the letter
w
for the measure
w(x)\mbox{d}x
induced by the function
w
.
An application of Cauchy-Schwarz allows us to bound the first term on the right as follows.

\displaystyle\|u-\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(x)-\tilde{u}(x)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\int_{B_{1}(x)}(u(x)-u(y))\phi(x-y)\,\mbox{d}y\right|^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\delta_{1}[u](x)^{2}\cdot\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle=\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\cdot\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

To deal with the second term, observe that

\forall\ell\in\{1,\ldots,d\}:\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{\ell}}\,\mbox{d}x=0.

Denoting
\phi_{\ell}=\frac{\partial\phi}{\partial x_{\ell}}
, we therefore have that
k\ast\phi_{\ell}=0
whenever
k
is a constant function. Thus,

\displaystyle\|\nabla\tilde{u}\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\frac{\partial\tilde{u}}{\partial x_{\ell}}(x)\right|^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|(u\ast\phi_{\ell})(x)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left|\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(u(y)-u(x))\phi_{\ell}(x-y)\,\mbox{d}y\right|^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\delta_{1}[u](x)^{2}\cdot\|\phi_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mbox{d}w(x)

\displaystyle=\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\cdot\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

where we used Cauchy-Schwarz once more.
Combining the pieces, gives that

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{R}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\left(\|\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{P}(w)\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}(1+C_{P}(w))\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2},

which implies the claim.
∎

2.3.
SLPR for the STFT

The upcoming statement reveals how differences in the lifted setting can be expressed in terms of the respective ambiguity functions.

Lemma 2.10
.

Suppose that
f,g,h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and let us denote
F={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f
and
H={\mathcal{V}}_{g}h
.
For all
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
it holds that

(2.8)

\|F(\cdot)\overline{F(\cdot+\tau)}-H(\cdot)\overline{H(\cdot+\tau)}\|_{L^{2}}=\|[{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f-{\mathcal{V}}_{h}h](\cdot){\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(\cdot+\tau)\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

As a starting point we use the following fact which may be found in
[
15
]
. Let
f,g,\phi,\psi\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and let
\mathcal{J}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{pmatrix}
. Then

(2.9)

\left({\mathcal{V}}_{g}f\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{\psi}\phi}\right)^{\wedge}(\zeta)={\mathcal{V}}_{\phi}f(-\mathcal{J}\zeta)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{\psi}g(-\mathcal{J}\zeta)},\quad\zeta\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Fix
\tau=(p,q)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
and choose

\phi=M_{-q}f
and
\psi=T_{p}g
.
Notice that

(2.10)

{\mathcal{V}}_{\psi}\phi(z)=\langle M_{q}f,M_{z_{2}}T_{z_{1}}T_{p}g\rangle=\langle f,\pi(z+\tau)g\rangle={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f(z+\tau),

and that (with
\zeta=(u,v)
)

(2.11)

{\mathcal{V}}_{\phi}f(-\mathcal{J}\zeta)=\langle f,M_{u}T_{-v}M_{-q}f\rangle=\langle f,e^{-2\pi iq\cdot v}M_{u-q}T_{-v}f\rangle=e^{2\pi iq\cdot v}{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f(-v,u-q),

and further, that

(2.12)

{\mathcal{V}}_{\psi}g(-\mathcal{J}\zeta)=\langle g,M_{u}T_{-v}T_{p}g\rangle={\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(p-v,u).

Thus, equation (
2.9
) implies that

\left({\mathcal{V}}_{g}f(\cdot)\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{g}f(\cdot+\tau)}\right)^{\wedge}(\zeta)=e^{2\pi iq\cdot v}\cdot{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f(-v,u-q)\cdot\overline{{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(-v+p,u)}

Clearly
f
may be replaced by
h
. The statement now follows by taking differences, by applying Plancherel’s formula and by a change of variable.
∎

For window functions
g

such that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW we can deduce the following statement.

Proposition 2.11
.

Suppose that
g\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
is such that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW and let
\mu:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
be locally bounded such that

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|\leq\mu(\tau)|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z)|,\quad z,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}.

Moreover, assume that
\sigma
is a weight on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
such that

\mu\in L^{2}(\sigma)
.

Then
V={\mathcal{V}}_{g}(L^{2}({\mathbb{R}}))
does
\rho
-SLPR with
\rho(z,z^{\prime})=\sigma(z^{\prime}-z)
.
In particular, if
(g,\gamma)
is an admissible pair, then
V
does
\Gamma
-SLPR with
\Gamma=\Gamma[\gamma]
.

We require the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 2.12
.

Assume the conditions of Proposition
2.11
, let
f,h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
and denote
F={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f
and
H={\mathcal{V}}_{g}h
. For all
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
it holds that

(2.13)

\|F(\cdot)\overline{F(\cdot+\tau)}-H(\cdot)\overline{H(\cdot+\tau)}\|_{L^{2}}\leq\mu(\tau)\cdot\||F|^{2}-|H|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

According to Lemma
2.10
, the left hand side squared is

\displaystyle\|[{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f-{\mathcal{V}}_{h}h](\cdot){\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(\cdot+\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|[{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f-{\mathcal{V}}_{h}h](z)|^{2}\cdot|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}z

\displaystyle\leq\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|[{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f-{\mathcal{V}}_{h}h](z)|^{2}\cdot\mu(\tau)^{2}|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}z

\displaystyle=\mu(\tau)^{2}\cdot\|[{\mathcal{V}}_{f}f-{\mathcal{V}}_{h}h]{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Applying Lemma
2.10
once more and taking square roots implies the claim.
∎

Now, the only thing that is left is to integrate things up in the correct way.

Proof of Proposition
2.11
.

Let
F={\mathcal{V}}_{g}f
and
H={\mathcal{V}}_{g}h
for
f,h\in L^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
.
By Fubini, as
\rho(z,z+\tau)=\sigma(\tau)
and with Lemma
2.12
we get

\displaystyle\|F\otimes\overline{F}-H\otimes\overline{H}\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\left|F(z)\overline{F(z+\tau)}-H(z)\overline{H(z+\tau)}\right|^{2}\,\mbox{d}z\,\sigma(\tau)\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\||F|^{2}-|H|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\mu(\tau)^{2}\sigma(\tau)\mbox{d}\tau.

This implies the assertion.
∎

2.4.
Identifying admissible pairs

The goal of this section is to prove Lemma
1.11
.
Recall that for
(g,\gamma)
to be an admissible pair, the modulus of the ambiguity function
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
necessarily has to be a TSW. In particular,
{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g
cannot have any zeros.
The question of which windows yield zero-free ambiguity functions has been investigated by Gröchenig, Jaming and Malinnikova
[
16
]
.
The examples we consider here are based on their findings.

Proof of Lemma
1.11
.

For each case we will follow the same pattern:
First, identify a function
\mu
such that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|\leq\mu(\tau)\mu(z)
in order to derive that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW.
Secondly, pick
\gamma
accordingly in order to make sure that

\mu\in L^{2}(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)
.

Case (i):

g(t)=\exp(t-e^{t})
and
\gamma(x,\xi)=e^{-a|x|-b|\xi|}
, where
a>2\pi^{2}
and
b>2
.
The ambiguity function has been explicitly computed in
[
16
, Example 4]
, and its modulus is given by

(2.14)

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(x,\xi)|=\frac{1}{4}\sech\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}|\Gamma(2-2\pi i\xi)|.

We will make use of the fact that
\sech\asymp e^{-|\cdot|}
to estimate

(2.15)

\sech\left(\frac{x+t}{2}\right)^{2}\lesssim e^{-|x+t|}\leq e^{|t|-|x|}\lesssim e^{|t|}\sech\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{2}.

Moreover, with
\frac{t}{\sinh(t)}\asymp(1+|t|)e^{-|t|}
and the identity
|\Gamma(2+bi)|^{2}=\frac{\pi b}{\sinh(\pi b)}(1+b^{2})
we get

\displaystyle|\Gamma(2-2\pi i(y+t))|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\pi^{2}(y+t)}{\sinh(2\pi^{2}(y+t))}(1+4\pi^{2}(y+t)^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+2\pi^{2}|y+t|)e^{-2\pi^{2}|y+t|}(1+4\pi^{2}(y+t)^{2})

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|y+t|^{3})e^{-2\pi^{2}|y+t|}

\displaystyle\lesssim(1+|y|^{3})(1+|t|^{3})e^{2\pi^{2}|t|}e^{-2\pi^{2}|y|}

\displaystyle\leq(1+|t|^{3})e^{2\pi^{2}|t|}\cdot\frac{2\pi^{2}y}{\sinh(2\pi^{2}y)}(1+4\pi^{2}y^{2})

\displaystyle=(1+|t|^{3})e^{2\pi^{2}|t|}\cdot|\Gamma(2-2\pi iy)|^{2}.

Combining the two estimates implies that there exists a numerical constant
c>0
such that

(2.16)

\mu(\tau)=c(1+|\tau_{2}|^{\frac{3}{2}})e^{\pi^{2}|\tau_{2}|+|\tau_{1}|},\quad\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2}

satisfies
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|\leq\mu(\tau)|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z)|
.
Hence,
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW and it remains to check that
\mu
satisfies the integrability condition.
To that end, compute

(2.17)

(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)(x,\xi)=(a^{-1}+|x|)e^{-a|x|}\cdot(b^{-1}+|\xi|)e^{-b|\xi|}.

With this we see that

\mu\in L^{2}(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mu^{2}\cdot(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)\in L^{1}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad a>2\pi^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad b>2,

which settles case (i).

Case (ii):

g(t)=e^{-t}\mathds{1}_{(0,\infty)}(t)
and
\gamma(x,\xi)=e^{-a|x|-b|\xi|}
.

The modulus of the ambiguity function of the one-sided exponential is given by

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(x,\xi)|=\frac{e^{-|x|}}{2|1+i\pi\xi|},

see
[
16
, Example 3]
.
By triangle inequality, for all
\xi,\tau_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

|1+i\pi\xi|\leq|1+i\pi(\xi+\tau_{2})|+\pi|\tau_{2}|\leq|1+i\pi(\xi+\tau_{2})|\cdot(1+\pi|\tau_{2}|).

Hence, with
z=(x,\xi),\tau=(\tau_{1},\tau_{2})
,

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z+\tau)|\leq(1+\pi|\tau_{2}|)e^{-|\tau_{1}|}|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(z)|.

It remains to verify that
\mu(\tau)=e^{-|\tau_{1}|}(1+\pi|\tau_{2}|)
is square integrable w.r.t.
(\gamma\ast\mathcal{R}\gamma)
.
Using formula (
2.17
) once more, we see that this is the case if and only if
a>2
and
b>0
.
This settles case (ii).
∎

Remark 2.13

(Remaining candidates)
.

Besides
\exp(t-e^{t})
and the one-sided exponential, the article
[
16
]
contains further examples of functions whose ambiguity function is nowhere vanishing.
One class of examples are Gaussians.
The other examples are all built from one-sided exponentials. That is, with

\eta_{a}(t):=e^{-at}\mathds{1}_{[0,\infty)}(t)
,
all of the following choices

•

g(t)=e^{-ct^{2}}
with
c>0

•

g(t)=\eta_{a}\ast\eta_{b}
with
a,b>0
,
a\neq b

•

g(t)=\eta_{a}\ast\mathcal{R}\eta_{b}
with
a,b>0
,
a\neq b

•

g(t)=t^{n}\eta_{a}(t)
with
n\in\mathbb{N}

have the property that
{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g
has no zeros. If
g
is a Gaussian, then
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a Gaussian and hence cannot be a TSW.
The respective ambiguity functions of the other candidates are also explicitly available but unfortunately take a rather complicated form.
For these cases we did not manage to prove (and neither disprove) that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|
is a TSW.

3.
Proofs

3.1.
The abstract stability result

The first goal of this section is to establish the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\chi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to[0,1]
be a weight and let

\gamma\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
be a TSW.

There exists
c>0
(depending on
\gamma,\chi
and
d
only) such that for all
F,H\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})

(3.1)

\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}^{4}\leq c(1+C_{P}(w\chi,w))\|H\otimes\overline{H}-F\otimes\overline{F}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2},

with
w=(|F|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}
and
\Gamma=\Gamma[\gamma]
.

Proof.

Throughout
C
will denote a constant which depends on
\gamma
and
d
only but may change from one line to another.

Let us begin with some notation: Given
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
we introduce weighted versions of
F
and
H
by

F_{\tau}=F(\cdot)\sqrt{\gamma(\tau-\cdot)},\quad\text{and}\quad H_{\tau}=H(\cdot)\sqrt{\gamma(\tau-\cdot)}.

Note that
w(\tau)=\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}
.
Moreover, let
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to\mathbb{T}
be such that

(3.2)

\forall\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}:\quad u(\tau)\in\argmin_{c\in\mathbb{T}}\|H_{\tau}-cF_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}.

For all
G\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
we have that

\displaystyle\|G\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle=\|\tau\mapsto\|G\|_{L^{2}(B_{1}(\tau))}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\inf_{y\in B_{1}(0)}\gamma(y)}\|\tau\mapsto\|G\|_{L^{2}(\gamma(\tau-\cdot))}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}.

The positivity and continuity of
\gamma
ensure that the
\inf
is strictly positive.
Hence,

\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq C\cdot\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|\tau\mapsto\|H_{\tau}-cF_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}\\
\leq C\left(\|\tau\mapsto\|H_{\tau}-u(\tau)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}+\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|\tau\mapsto\|(u(\tau)-c)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}\right).

Since
u
attains its values on the unit circle exclusively, we get the following bound on the second term on the right.

\displaystyle\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|\tau\mapsto\|(u(\tau)-c)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}^{4}

\displaystyle=\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(\tau)-c|^{4}\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\chi(\tau)\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{D}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(\tau)-c|^{4}w(\tau)\chi(\tau)\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq 4\cdot\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{D}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(\tau)-c|^{2}w(\tau)\chi(\tau)\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle=4\cdot\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(w\chi)}^{2}.

As
\chi\leq 1
we have that
\|\cdot\|_{L^{4}(\chi)}\leq\|\cdot\|_{L^{4}}
.
Let us define two quantities by

(I)\coloneqq\|\tau\mapsto\|H_{\tau}-u(\tau)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\quad\text{and}\quad(II)\coloneqq\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|u-c\|_{L^{2}(w\chi)}^{2}.

In order to arrive at the desired conclusion we need to show that

(3.3)

(I),(II)\leq C(1+C_{P}(w\chi,w))\|H\otimes\overline{H}-F\otimes\overline{F}\|_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}^{2}.

We start with
(II)
. Applying the modified Poincaré inequality implies together with Theorem
2.8
that

(II)\leq C(1+C_{P}(w\chi,w))\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}.

We introduce the short-hand notation

\Lambda(\tau,y):=\|H_{\tau}-u(y)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

Suppose
y,\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
are such that
|y-\tau|\leq 1
.
As
\gamma
is a TSW, we get that there exists a function
\mu
which is locally bounded such that

\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|F(x)|^{2}\gamma(\tau-x)\,\mbox{d}x\leq\mu(\tau-y)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|F(x)|^{2}\gamma(y-x)\,\mbox{d}y=\mu(\tau-y)\|F_{y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

The same argument shows that
\|H_{\tau}-u(y)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mu(\tau-y)\|H_{y}-u(y)F_{y}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Since
\mu
is locally bounded we get for all
y,\tau
with
|y-\tau|\leq 1
that

\Lambda(\tau,y)\leq C\Lambda(y,y).

With this,

\displaystyle\delta_{1}[u](\tau)^{2}\cdot w(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}|u(y)-u(\tau)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}y\cdot\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u(y)F_{\tau}(z)-u(\tau)F_{\tau}(z)|^{2}\,\mbox{d}z\,\mbox{d}y\,\cdot\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left(\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\Lambda(\tau,y)+\Lambda(\tau,\tau)\,\mbox{d}y\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\Lambda(y,y)\,\mbox{d}y+\Lambda(\tau,\tau)\right).

It follows from Lemma
2.1
that

\Lambda(y,y)\leq 2\|H_{y}\otimes\overline{H_{y}}-F_{y}\otimes\overline{F_{y}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and likewise for
\Lambda(\tau,\tau)
.
Thus, we further have that

(3.4)

\delta_{1}[u](\tau)^{2}\cdot\omega(\tau)\leq C\left(\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\|H_{y}\otimes\overline{H_{y}}-F_{y}\otimes\overline{F_{y}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mbox{d}y+\|H_{\tau}\otimes\overline{H_{\tau}}-F_{\tau}\otimes\overline{F_{\tau}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right).

Integrating both sides w.r.t.
\tau\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, changing order of integration (and absorbing the constants) further gives

\|\delta_{1}[u]\|_{L^{2}(w)}^{2}\leq C\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|F(z)\overline{F(z^{\prime})}-H(z)\overline{H(z^{\prime})}|^{2}\\
\times\left\{\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left[\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\gamma(y-z)\gamma(y-z^{\prime})\,\mbox{d}y+\gamma(\tau-z)\gamma(\tau-z^{\prime})\right]\,\mbox{d}\tau\right\}\mbox{d}z\,\mbox{d}z^{\prime}

The second term inside
\{\ldots\}

produces
\Gamma(z,z^{\prime})
.
Furthermore, a change of variable reveals

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}(\tau)}\gamma(y-z)\gamma(y-z^{\prime})\,\mbox{d}y\,\mbox{d}\tau=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}(y)}\gamma(y-z)\gamma(y-z^{\prime})\,\mbox{d}\tau\,\mbox{d}y\\
=|B_{1}(0)|\cdot\Gamma(z,z^{\prime}).

This implies (
3.3
) for
(II)
.

The first term is significantly easier to deal with. Using Lemma
2.1
once more gives

\displaystyle(I)

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|H_{\tau}-u(\tau)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|H_{\tau}-u(\tau)F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\cdot 2(\|H_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|F_{\tau}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\,\mbox{d}\tau

\displaystyle\leq 4\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\|H_{\tau}\otimes\overline{H_{\tau}}-F_{\tau}\otimes\overline{F_{\tau}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\mbox{d}\tau.

From this point one can argue in the same way as above to arrive at (
3.3
) for
(I)
.
∎

We require a couple of auxiliary results.
First we compare two ways of how to measure phaseless differences.

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a finite constant
C>0
(depending on
d
only) such that

(3.5)

\forall F,H\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}):\quad\||H|-|F|\|_{\mathcal{L}}\leq C\||H|^{2}-|F|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

Proof.

An application of Jensen’s inequality and the
elementary inequality
(a-b)^{2}\leq|a^{2}-b^{2}|
for
a,b\geq 0
,
allows us to bound

\displaystyle\||H|-|F|\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\left(\int_{B_{1}(y)}(|H(x)|-|F(x)|)^{2}\,\mbox{d}x\right)^{2}\,\mbox{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{B_{1}(y)}(|H(x)|-|F(x)|)^{4}\,\mbox{d}x\,\mbox{d}y

\displaystyle=C|B_{1}(0)|\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(|H(x)|-|F(x)|)^{4}\,\mbox{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C|B_{1}(0)|\cdot\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}(|H(x)|^{2}-|F(x)|^{2})^{2}\,\mbox{d}x

\displaystyle=C|B_{1}(0)|\||H|^{2}-|F|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

which implies the claim.
∎

The next result provides us with some flexibility when it comes to the constraint on the constant in the distance.
We point out that a similar argument has been used in one of our previous articles
[
19
]
and that it could be stated much more generally.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\chi
be a bounded weight on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
and let
F,H\in L^{4}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then it holds that

\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq 2\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\||H|-|F|\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}.

Proof.

Given
\varepsilon>0
arbitrary, let
c_{\varepsilon}\in{\mathbb{C}}
be such that

(3.6)

\|H-c_{\varepsilon}F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\varepsilon.

By continuity we may assume w.l.o.g. that
c_{\varepsilon}\neq 0
.
For every
\varepsilon>0
,

\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq\left\|H-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{|c_{\varepsilon}|}F\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}\leq\|H-c_{\varepsilon}F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\left\|c_{\varepsilon}F-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{|c_{\varepsilon}|}F\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}.

While the first term on the right hand side can be bounded with (
3.6
), we get for the second one that

\displaystyle\left\|c_{\varepsilon}F-\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{|c_{\varepsilon}|}F\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle=\left\||c_{\varepsilon}F|-|F|\right\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle\leq\||c_{\varepsilon}F|-|H|\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\||F|-|H|\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle\leq\|c_{\varepsilon}F-H\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\||F|-|H|\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}.

The statement now follows by sending
\varepsilon\to 0
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

\displaystyle\inf_{\lambda\in\mathbb{T}}\|H-\lambda F\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{Lemma \ref{lem:replaceconstraint}}}}{{\leq}}2\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\||H|-|W|\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}

\displaystyle\quad\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\chi\leq 1}}{{\leq}}\quad 2\inf_{c\in{\mathbb{C}}}\|H-cF\|_{\mathcal{L}(\chi)}+\||H|-|W|\|_{\mathcal{L}}

Applying Proposition

and using the assumption that

3.3.
Finite Poincaré constants

This paragraph is dedicated to the proofs of Theorem
1.16
and Theorem
1.17

Proof of Theorem
1.16
.

Let us denote by
LC({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
the family of all log-concave, integrable functions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
.
We will make use of the fact that
LC({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
is closed under convolution
[
21
]
.
We claim that
|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|^{2}\in LC({\mathbb{R}}^{2})
if
g(t)=\exp(t-e^{t})
. Once we have verified this, we can argue

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|^{2}\in LC({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\quad\Rightarrow\quad|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|^{2}\ast\gamma\in LC({\mathbb{R}}^{2})\quad\Rightarrow\quad(|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|^{2}\ast\gamma)^{2}\in LC({\mathbb{R}}^{2}),

and Bobkov’s Theorem
2.2
implies the statement.

Recall that (cf. proof of Lemma
1.11
)

(3.7)

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(x,\xi)|^{2}=\frac{1}{16}\sech\left(\frac{x}{2}\right)^{4}|\Gamma(2-2\pi i\xi)|^{2}.

Thus, it suffices to show that both

(3.8)

\phi_{1}(x)=\sech(x),\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{2}(\xi)=|\Gamma(2-i\xi)|^{2}=\frac{\pi\xi}{\sinh(\pi\xi)}(1+\xi^{2})

are log-concave in the 1d sense.
Note that if
\phi:{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
is a smooth function then

(\ln\phi)^{\prime\prime}\geq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad(\phi^{\prime})^{2}-\phi^{\prime\prime}\phi\geq 0.

With this, since

(\phi_{1}^{\prime})^{2}-\phi_{1}^{\prime\prime}\phi=\tanh^{2}\sech^{2}-\sech^{2}\left(\tanh^{2}-\sech^{2}\right)=\sech^{4}>0.

we get that
\phi_{1}
is log-concave.

Proving log-concavity for the second function is a bit more involved. Note that by continuity and symmetry it suffices to show that
(\ln\phi_{2})^{\prime\prime}(\xi)\geq 0
only for
\xi>0
.
For
\xi>0
we have that

\displaystyle(\ln\phi_{2})^{\prime\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle=\left(\ln\pi+\ln\xi-\ln(\sinh(\pi\xi))+\ln(1+\xi^{2})\right)^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=-\xi^{-2}+\pi^{2}\csch^{2}(\pi\xi)-\frac{2(\xi^{2}-1)}{(1+\xi^{2})^{2}}

Next we multiply the above expression by the positive function

(\star)=\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)\xi^{2}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}

and obtain

\displaystyle(\ln\phi_{2})^{\prime\prime}\cdot(\star)

\displaystyle=-\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)(1+\xi^{2})^{2}+\pi^{2}\xi^{2}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}-2\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)\xi^{2}(\xi^{2}-1)

\displaystyle=-\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)\left[(1+\xi^{2})^{2}+2\xi^{2}(\xi^{2}-1)\right]+\pi^{2}\xi^{2}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=-\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)\left[3\xi^{4}+1\right]+\pi^{2}\xi^{2}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\sinh^{2}(\pi\xi)+\pi^{2}\xi^{2}(1+\xi^{2})^{2}

By substituting
\pi\xi=v
, we see that it is sufficient to show that

(3.9)

\forall v>0:\quad\sinh^{2}(v)\geq v^{2}\left(1+\frac{v^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\right)^{2}=v^{2}+\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}v^{4}+\frac{1}{\pi^{4}}v^{6}

The Taylor expansion of the function on the left hand side is

\sinh^{2}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\cosh(2v)-\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(2v)^{2\ell}}{(2\ell)!}-1\right)=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\frac{2^{2\ell-1}}{(2\ell)!}v^{2\ell}

Note that all the coefficients are positive, and that

\frac{2^{2-1}}{2!}=1,\quad\frac{2^{4-1}}{4!}>\frac{2}{\pi^{2}},\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{2^{6-1}}{6!}>\frac{1}{\pi^{4}},

which proves (
3.9
), and we are done.
∎

We continue with the proof of the second statement.

Proof of Theorem
1.17
.

First we shall show that

\sqrt{w}=(|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g|^{2}\ast\gamma)\asymp\phi\otimes\psi

with
\phi
log-concave and integrable and
\psi=\frac{1}{1+\cdot^{2}}
.
Recall (cf. proof of Lemma
1.11
) that

|{\mathcal{V}}_{g}g(x,\xi)|^{2}=\frac{e^{-2|x|}}{4(1+\pi^{2}\xi^{2})}.

Thus,

\sqrt{w}=\frac{1}{4}(e^{-2|\cdot|}\ast e^{-a|\cdot|})\otimes\left(\frac{1}{1+\pi^{2}\cdot^{2}}\ast e^{-b|\cdot|}\right)

The first term in the tensor product is a convolution of two log-concave functions, and hence log-concave itself.
Therefore, it remains to show that

(3.10)

\frac{1}{1+\pi^{2}\cdot^{2}}\ast e^{-b|\cdot|}\asymp\psi.

We will use that
\frac{1}{1+c\cdot^{2}}\asymp\psi
for any
c>0
(with the implicit constant depending on
c
only).
Moreover, since
e^{-b|\cdot|}\lesssim\psi
we get that

\frac{1}{1+\pi^{2}\cdot^{2}}\ast e^{-b|\cdot|}\lesssim\psi\ast\psi=\frac{2\pi}{4+\cdot^{2}}\lesssim\psi.

For the other direction, we have that

(3.11)

\left(\frac{1}{1+\pi^{2}\cdot^{2}}\ast e^{-b|\cdot|}\right)(x)\gtrsim(\psi\ast\mathds{1}_{(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2})})(x)=\int\limits_{x-\frac{1}{2}}^{x+\frac{1}{2}}\psi(t)\,\mbox{d}t.

Note that
\psi\geq\frac{1}{2}
on
[-1,1]
and that
\psi
is monotonically decreasing on
(0,\infty)
.
By symmetry, we further have that

\int\limits_{x-\frac{1}{2}}^{x+\frac{1}{2}}\psi(t)\,\mbox{d}t\geq\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2},\quad&|x|\leq\frac{1}{2},\\
\psi\left(x+\frac{1}{2}\right),\quad&|x|>\frac{1}{2},\end{cases}

which is
\asymp\psi
, and we proved (
3.10
).

Remember that we have to show that
C_{P}(w\chi,w)<\infty
with
\chi(x,\xi)=\psi(\xi)
.
Clearly, replacing the weight in either slot by an equivalent one does not change the property of yielding a finite Poincaré constant (however, in general the value will change). This means, it suffices to show that

C_{P}(\phi^{2}\otimes\psi^{3},\phi^{2}\otimes\psi^{2})<\infty.

According to the tensorisation property, Lemma
2.4
, it is enough to establish the one-dimensional Poincaré inequalities:
a)
C_{P}(\phi^{2})<\infty
and b)
C_{P}(\psi^{3},\psi^{2})<\infty
.
But, since
\phi
is log-concave, so is
\phi^{2}
and a) follows from Theorem
2.2
.
On the other hand, statement b) is a direct consequence of Theorem
2.3
with
\beta=2
.
∎

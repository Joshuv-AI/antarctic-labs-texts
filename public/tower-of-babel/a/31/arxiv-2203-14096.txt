Fourier Coefficients of Hilbert Modular Forms at Cusps
Tim Davis

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to study the fields generated by the Fourier coefficients of Hilbert newforms at arbitrary cusps. Precisely, given a cuspidal Hilbert newform f f and a matrix σ \sigma in (a suitable conjugate of) the Hilbert modular group, we give a cyclotomic extension of the field generated by the Fourier coefficients at infinity which contains all the Fourier coefficients of f | | k σ f||_{k}\sigma .



1.
Introduction

Let
f
be a normalised Hecke eigenform for
\Gamma_{0}(N)
of weight
k
. It is known that the field generated by the Fourier coefficients of
f
is a number field, see
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
. This number field we denote by
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
. Furthermore, for any matrix
g\in\SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
, an application of the
q
-expansion principle shows that the Fourier coefficients of
f|_{k}g
lie in the cyclotomic extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)(\zeta_{N})
, see
[
6
, Remark 12.3.5]
for more details. In recent years, algorithms have been developed to compute these Fourier coefficients. These were developed by several different authors, see
[
4
,
8
]
for more details about these algorithms. Their methods use the knowledge that these numbers are in fact algebraic numbers. This means if we know more about the number field where these Fourier coefficients lie, this could speed up the computations.

Therefore the question is given a Hecke eigenform (which we may assume to be a newform)
f
of level
N
and weight
k
and
\sigma\in\SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}})
; what is the number field that the Fourier coefficients of
f|_{k}\sigma
generate? Or a slightly weaker question is: can one write down an explicit subfield of
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)(\zeta_{N})
, depending on the entries of
\sigma
, which contains all the Fourier coefficients of
f|_{k}\sigma
? This was answered in a paper of Brunault and Neururer, who proved the following result
[
2
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Let
f
be a normalised newform on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
with weight
k
. Let
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
be the field generated by all the Fourier coefficients of
f
. Let
\sigma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\SL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then the Fourier coefficients of
f|_{k}\sigma
lie in the cyclotomic extension
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)(\zeta_{N^{\prime}})
where
N^{\prime}=N/(cd,N)
.

The method of Brunault and Neururer was classical. They use a result of Shimura
[
18
, Theorem 8]
, which studied the connections between two actions on spaces of modular forms: the action of
\GL_{2}^{+}({\mathbb{Q}})
via the slash-operator and the action of
\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
on the Fourier coefficient of a modular form. The proof of Brunault and Neururer also applies to modular forms of
\Gamma_{0}(N)
that are not necessarily newforms but in that case it is not known if the field is optimal. In this paper we give a new proof of the result of Brunault and Neuruer as well as a substantial generalisation (to the case of Hilbert modular forms) using adelic and local representation-theoretic methods. Specifically we use local Whittaker functions and their invariance properties. The starting point is an explicit formula for the Fourier coefficients of
f|_{k}\sigma
given in
[
5
, Section 3]
, in terms of local Whittaker functions. From there we find sufficient conditions for
\tau\in\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
to fix this product. The advantage of this method is that it gives an insight into how one could prove analogous results for other families of modular forms, specifically it can be generalised to Hilbert modular forms. One would suspect that a similar method can also be used to prove a result of the same style for Whittaker coefficients (or other factorisable periods) of automorphic forms lying in cohomological automorphic representations of higher rank groups.

The main result of this paper is an extension of Brunault and Neururer stated above to the case of cuspidal Hilbert newforms. Let
F
be a totally real number field of degree
n
with narrow class group of size
h
. Let
{\mathfrak{n}}
be a fixed integral ideal of
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
. For
\mu=1,...,h
, we define the congruence subgroup
\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})
of
\GL_{2}(F)
as

{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}=\bigg\{\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}:a,d\in{\mathcal{O}}_{F},b\in(t_{\mu})^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{F}^{-1},c\in{\mathfrak{n}}t_{\mu}\mathfrak{D}_{F},ad-bc\in{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}\bigg\},

where
{\mathfrak{D}}_{F}
is the absolute different of
F
and
t_{\mu}
is a representative of the narrow class group.

We identify
\alpha\in\GL_{2}(F)
with an element of
\GL_{2}({\mathbb{R}})^{n}
via the various embeddings of
F
in
{\mathbb{R}}
and for
y=(y_{1},...,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{C}}^{n}
and
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
we use the notation
y^{k}:=y_{1}^{k_{1}}...y_{n}^{k_{n}}
.

Then
f:{\mathbb{H}}^{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{C}}
is a Hilbert modular form of weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
and level
{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}
if
f
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and cusps and we have
f||_{k}\alpha(z):=\det\alpha^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}f(\alpha z)=f(z)
for every
\alpha=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})
. Any
f
of the above form has a Fourier expansion of the form

f(z)=\sum_{\xi}a(\xi;f)e^{2\pi i\Tr(\xi z)}.

Following Shimura
[
19
, 2.24]
, we define for
f
as above,
1\leq\mu\leq h
,

c_{\mu}(\xi;f)=N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}a(\xi;f)\xi^{(k_{0}\mathbf{1}-k)/2},

where
\mathbf{1}=(1,...,1)
and
k_{0}=\max\{k_{1},...,k_{n}\}
.

We say
f
is a Hilbert cuspform if the the constant term in the Fourier expansion of
f||_{k}\gamma
is zero for every
\gamma\in\GL_{2}^{+}(F)
. A cuspidal Hilbert newform of weight
k
and level
{\mathfrak{n}}
is a tuple
{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},...,f_{h})
where
f_{\mu}
is a Hilbert cuspform for
\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})
and such that
{\mathbf{f}}
satisfies additional properties (it does not come from a form of lower level and is a Hecke eigenform), see
[
19
, Section 2]
for more details. A normalised cuspidal Hilbert newform
{\mathbf{f}}
(also called primitive form) is one such that the first Fourier coefficient of
{\mathbf{f}}
(which is non-zero) is normalised to equal 1. A result of Shimura
[
19
, Proposition 2.8]
, tells us that the set of all
\{c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}):1\leq\mu\leq h\ \text{and}\ \xi\in F_{+}\}
generates a totally real or CM number field, denoted
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})
, under the assumption that
k_{1}\equiv...\equiv k_{n}\pmod{2}
. We prove the following result.

Theorem 1

(Theorem
6.1
)
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},...,f_{h})
be a normalised Hilbert newform of weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
with
k_{1}\equiv...\equiv k_{n}\pmod{2}
and level
{\mathfrak{n}}
. Let
1\leq\mu\leq h
and let
\sigma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{\mu}(1)
. Let
f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma
have the Fourier expansion

f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma(z)=\sum_{\xi}a_{\mu}(\xi;\sigma)e^{2\pi i\Tr(\xi z)},

and define

c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)=N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}a_{\mu}(\xi;\sigma)\xi^{(k_{0}\mathbf{1}-k)/2}.

Then
c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)
lie in the number field
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}})
where
N_{0}
is the integer such that
N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}={\mathfrak{n}}/(cdt_{\mu}^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{F}^{-1},{\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
.

To prove this theorem we study the classical action of
\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
on the Fourier coefficients via the action of
\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
on local Whittaker newforms. We give an explicit formula for the Fourier coefficients of a classical Hilbert automorphic form in terms of a global Whittaker function. We can then apply this general result to the specific newform in the theorem. This generalises two results of
[
5
]
, namely Lemma 3.1 and Propostion 3.2, where the authors proved analogous results for modular forms. The global Whittaker newform can be broken up into a product of local Whittaker newforms. Therefore we have that we can write the Fourier coefficients of
f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma
in terms of local Whittaker newforms. Thus studying the action of
\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
on these Fourier coefficients is equivalent to studying the action of
\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
on local Whittaker newforms.

4.1.
Fourier expansion

For a function

{\mathbb{H}}

Result for modular forms

Theorem 4.1
.

Let

Remark 4.2
.

Recall that to show a complex number

is an element of a number field it is equivalent to showing that

is fixed by the Galois group of that number field.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\Aut({\mathbb{C}})

\tau
fixes
{\mathbb{Q}}(f)
and all the
\zeta_{N/L}

Let

5.
Preliminary Results

In this section we define the classical Hilbert newform and how we can reinterpret this definition in the adelic setting. We then prove some general results needed to prove Theorem
1
. Specifically we show that the Fourier coefficients of Hilbert newform equals the global Whittaker newform.

5.1.
Hilbert Modular Forms

We define

{\mathbb{H}}^{n}=\{z=(z_{1},...,z_{n}):\Ima(z_{j})>0,\forall j=1,...,n\},

where we write
z=x+iy
to mean for
z=(z_{1},...,z_{n})
and
z_{j}=x_{j}+iy_{j}
for each
j
. We have that
\GL_{2}(F)
acts on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
in the following way

g\cdot z=\Bigg(\frac{\eta_{1}(a_{1})z_{1}+\eta_{1}(b_{1})}{\eta_{1}(c_{1})z_{1}+\eta_{1}(d_{1})},...,\frac{\eta_{n}(a_{n})z_{n}+\eta_{n}(b_{n})}{\eta_{n}(c_{n})z_{n}+\eta_{n}(d_{n})}\Bigg).

We define the congruence subgroup
{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}
of
\GL_{2}(F)
for each
\mu
as

(5)

{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}=x_{\mu}(\GL_{2}^{+}(F_{\infty})K_{0}({\mathfrak{n}}))x_{\mu}^{-1}\cap\GL_{2}(F),

where

(6)

K_{0}({\mathfrak{n}})=\prod_{v<\infty}K_{v}({\mathfrak{n}}).

Then

is a Hilbert modular form of level
{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}
and weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
if
f_{\mu}
is holomorphic on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}
and at cusps and

f_{\mu}||_{k}\alpha(z):=\det\alpha^{k/2}(cz+d)^{-k}f_{\mu}(\alpha\cdot z)=f_{\mu}(z),

for every
\alpha\in{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}
.

Remark 5.1
.

One can also consider this definition with a character
\chi
on
{\mathbb{A}}_{F}^{\times}/F^{\times}
, which we will assume is trivial. For a treatment of
\chi
being non-trivial see
[
15
, Section 4]
and the references therein.

If the constant term in the Fourier expansion of

is zero for every

then we say

is a cuspform. For the space of cuspforms to be nonempty we require that

, see
[

, Section 1.7]
for a proof of this fact. In this case

has a Fourier expansion at infinity of the form

The space of cuspforms of level
{\Gamma_{\mu}({\mathfrak{n}})}
and weight

is denoted

. For each
\mu
choose a cuspform in

and put

. Then we say that

is a cuspform of level
{\mathfrak{n}}
and weight

. The space of all such

is denoted by

and we have

An element

is said to be a cuspidal classical Hilbert newform if it lies in the orthogonal complement of the oldspace and is a common eigenfunction for the Hecke operators at almost all primes, see
[
19
, Section 2]
.

5.2.
Classical and adelic correspondence

Here we give a description on how one can go between classical Hilbert newforms and adelic newforms (as in Section

we only consider the case of trivial character

(12)

W_{\infty}(a(\xi)g_{z})=(\xi y)^{k/2}e(\Tr(\xi z)),

see for example
[
16
, Section 3]
.

6.
Main Result

In this section we prove our main result. We do this by applying a similar method to that used in the proof of Theorem
4.1
.

In Section
5
we showed that
a_{\mu}(\xi;\sigma)
can be written in terms of the global Whittaker function. Recall that the global Whittaker function can be broken up into a product of local Whittaker functions. This means that finding sufficient conditions for
\tau
to fix
c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)
, boils down to finding sufficient conditions such that
\tau
fixes the local Whittaker newforms. With this in mind we have the following decomposition

(13)

W_{\phi}(g)=C\prod_{v|\infty}W_{\infty}(g_{\infty})\prod_{v<\infty}W_{v}(g_{v}),

where
C
is a constant. This decomposition is due to the uniqueness of Whittaker functionals. We wish to compute the constant
C
. To do this let
g_{\infty}=g_{z}
and
g_{v}=x_{1,v}
for every
v<\infty
. Then using Proposition
5.5
and the fact that
{\mathbf{f}}
is normalised, that is,
a_{\mu}(1;1)=1
we have

W_{\phi}(g_{z}x_{1})=y^{k/2}a_{1}(1;1)e(\Tr(z))=y^{k/2}e(\Tr(z)).

On the other hand using (
12
), (
13
) and the fact that
W_{v}(1)=1
we obtain

W_{\phi}(g_{z}x_{1})=Cy^{k/2}e(\Tr(z)).

Thus
C=1
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},...,f_{h})
a normalised cuspidal Hilbert newform with weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
such that
k_{1}\equiv...\equiv k_{n}\pmod{2}
. We let
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})
denote the number field generated by
c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu})
as
\xi
varies over
F
and
\mu
varies over
1\leq\mu\leq h
. Then for
{\mathbf{f}},1\leq\mu\leq h,\ \text{and}\ \sigma\in\Gamma_{\mu}(1)
we let
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}},\mu,\sigma)
denote the field generated by
c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)
as
\xi
varies over
F
. Note that
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})
is the compositum of the fields
{\mathbb{Q}}(f,\mu,1)
as
\mu
varies over
1\leq\mu\leq h
.

We are now able to state and prove the main result of this article.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
{\mathbf{f}}=(f_{1},...,f_{h})
be a normalised cuspidal Hilbert newform of level
{\mathfrak{n}}
and weight
k=(k_{1},...,k_{n})
with
k_{1}\equiv...\equiv k_{n}\pmod{2}
. Let
1\leq\mu\leq h
and
\sigma=(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix})\in\Gamma_{\mu}(1)
. Then
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}},\mu,\sigma)
lies in the number field
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}})
where
N_{0}
is the integer such that
N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}={\mathfrak{n}}/(cdt_{\mu}^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{F}^{-1},{\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\Aut({\mathbb{C}})
fix
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}})
where
N_{0}
is the unique positive integer such that
N_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}={\mathfrak{n}}/(cdt_{\mu}^{-1}\mathfrak{D}_{F}^{-1},{\mathfrak{n}})\cap{\mathbb{Z}}
. Thus specifically
\tau
fixes
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})
and all the
\zeta_{N_{0}}
roots of unity.

From Proposition
5.5
and evaluating
W_{\infty}
we have that

\displaystyle a_{\mu}(\xi;\sigma)

\displaystyle=y^{-k/2}e(-\Tr(\xi z))W_{\phi}(a(\xi)g_{z}{\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})

\displaystyle=y^{-k/2}e(-\Tr(\xi z))(\xi y)^{k/2}e(\Tr(\xi z))\prod_{v<\infty}W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})

\displaystyle=\xi^{k/2}\prod_{v<\infty}W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu}).

Recall from (
11
) we have

c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)=N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}\xi^{(k_{0}\mathbf{1}-k)/2}a_{\mu}(\xi;\sigma).

Therefore

(14)

c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)=N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}\xi^{k_{0}\mathbf{1}/2}\prod_{v<\infty}W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu}).

From the fact that
\tau
fixes
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})
and using
[
19
, Proposition 2.6]
, multiplicity one and Lemma
5.2
we have that
{\mathbf{f}}={\mathbf{f}}^{\tau}
. Therefore, using Lemma
5.2
we have

\Pi_{v}\otimes|\ |_{v}^{k_{0}/2}\cong{}^{\tau}(\Pi_{v}\otimes|\ |_{v}^{k_{0}/2}).

So, by (
4
), we have that for each non-archimedean place
v

(15)

\tau(W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu}))\tau\big((|\xi|_{v}|t_{\mu}|_{v})^{k_{0}/2}\big)=W_{v}(a(\alpha_{\tau})a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})(|\xi|_{v}|t_{\mu}|_{v})^{k_{0}/2}.

Since all the
N_{0}
roots of unity are fixed by
\tau
we have that

\alpha_{\tau}\equiv 1\pmod{{\mathfrak{n}}_{v}/(c_{v}d_{v}\varpi_{v}^{-t_{v}-d_{v}},{\mathfrak{n}}_{v})},

where
c_{v}
and
d_{v}
are the
v
parts of
c
and
d
respectively. Consider the product

x_{\mu}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\tau}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}^{-1}x_{\mu}.

On using (
9
) we have this product is equal to

(16)

\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&\varpi_{v}^{d_{v}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&\varpi_{v}^{-d_{v}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{\tau}&\\
&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&\varpi_{v}^{d_{v}}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
&\varpi_{v}^{-d_{v}}\end{pmatrix}.

On evaluating (
16
) we have

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{-1}(a^{\prime}d^{\prime}\alpha_{\tau}-b^{\prime}c^{\prime})&\varepsilon^{-1}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}(1-\alpha_{\tau})\varpi_{v}^{-d_{v}}\\
\varepsilon^{-1}c^{\prime}d^{\prime}(\alpha_{\tau}-1)\varpi_{v}^{d_{v}}&\varepsilon^{-1}(a^{\prime}d^{\prime}-b^{\prime}c^{\prime}\alpha_{\tau})\end{pmatrix},\ \text{where}\ \varepsilon=\det(\begin{smallmatrix}a^{\prime}&b^{\prime}\\
c^{\prime}&d^{\prime}\end{smallmatrix}).

We want to show that this matrix is an element of
K_{v}({\mathfrak{n}})
. Therefore we need the following conditions to be satisfied

(1)

\varepsilon^{-1}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}(1-\alpha_{\tau})\varpi_{v}^{-dv}\in\mathfrak{D}_{v}^{-1}

(2)

\varepsilon^{-1}c^{\prime}d^{\prime}(\alpha_{\tau}-1)\varpi_{v}^{d_{v}}\in{\mathfrak{n}}_{v}\mathfrak{D}_{v}

(3)

\varepsilon^{-1}(a^{\prime}d^{\prime}-b^{\prime}c^{\prime}\alpha_{\tau})\in{\mathcal{O}}_{v}
.

Recall that
a^{\prime},b^{\prime},c^{\prime}
and
d^{\prime}
are elements of
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
and
\varepsilon^{-1}\in{\mathcal{O}}_{F}^{\times}
so conditions (1) and (3) are satisfied with no additional assumptions on
\alpha_{\tau}
. Now consider condition (2). From the fact we have
\alpha_{\tau}\equiv 1\pmod{{\mathfrak{n}}_{v}/(c_{v}d_{v}\varpi_{v}^{-t_{v}-d_{v}},{\mathfrak{n}}_{v})}
which is equivalent to
\alpha_{\tau}\equiv 1\pmod{n_{v}/(c^{\prime}_{v}d^{\prime}_{v},{\mathfrak{n}}_{v})}
, using Remark
5.4
. Thus we have that condition (2) is also satisfied. Therefore

\begin{pmatrix}\varepsilon^{-1}(a^{\prime}d^{\prime}\alpha_{\tau}-b^{\prime}c^{\prime})&\varepsilon^{-1}a^{\prime}b^{\prime}(1-\alpha_{\tau})\varpi_{v}^{-dv}\\
\varepsilon^{-1}c^{\prime}d^{\prime}(\alpha_{\tau}-1)\varpi_{v}^{d_{v}}&\varepsilon^{-1}(a^{\prime}d^{\prime}-b^{\prime}c^{\prime}\alpha_{\tau})\end{pmatrix}\in K_{v}({\mathfrak{n}}).

Recall that in general for
W_{v}(g_{1})=W_{v}(g_{2})
it suffices that
g_{1}^{-1}g_{2}\in K_{v}({\mathfrak{n}})
. Therefore we have that

(17)

W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})=W_{v}(a(\alpha_{\tau})a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu}),

for every finite place. Combining (
15
) and (
17
), we have

(18)

\frac{\tau\big(W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})\big)}{W_{v}(a(\xi){\iota_{\text{f}}(\sigma^{-1})}x_{\mu})}=\frac{|\xi t_{\mu}|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}}{\tau(|\xi t_{\mu}|_{v})^{k_{0}/2}}.

We now consider the quotient

\frac{\tau\big(c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)\big)}{c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)}.

To prove the result it now suffices to show this quotient is equal to one. Therefore by (
14
) and (
18
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\tau\big(c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)\big)}{c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau\big(N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}\big)\tau(\xi^{k_{0}/2})}{N(t_{\mu}{\mathcal{O}}_{F})^{-k_{0}/2}\xi^{k_{0}/2}}\prod_{v<\infty}\frac{|\xi t_{\mu}|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}}{\tau(|\xi t_{\mu}|_{v})^{k_{0}/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau\big(\prod_{v<\infty}|t_{\mu}|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}\big)}{\prod_{v<\infty}|t_{\mu}|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}}\frac{\tau\big(\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}\big)}{\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}/2}}\frac{\big(\prod_{v<\infty}|t_{\mu}|_{v}|\xi|_{v}\big)^{k_{0}/2}}{\tau\big(\prod_{v<\infty}|t_{\mu}|_{v}|\xi|_{v}\big)^{k_{0}/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau\big(\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}\big)}{\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}},\ \text{using global norm formula}.

We have that
\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}=|N(\xi)|\in{\mathbb{Q}}
. Hence

\frac{\tau\big(\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}\big)}{\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}}=\frac{\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}}{\prod_{v|\infty}|\xi|_{v}^{k_{0}}}=1.

Therefore we have that
\tau
fixes
c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)
and so

c_{\mu}(\xi;f_{\mu}||_{k}\sigma)\in{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}}).

∎

Remark 6.2
.

We see that in the special case of
F={\mathbb{Q}}
we obtain the result of Brunault and Neururer for newforms.

Question 6.3
.

Our method to prove Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
6.1
was to find sufficient conditions not necessary ones. Therefore one can ask if the number field
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}})
is optimal, that is, do we have
{\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}},\mu,\sigma)={\mathbb{Q}}({\mathbf{f}})(\zeta_{N_{0}})
?

Note that if
F={\mathbb{Q}}
this is known to be true
[
2
, Theorem 7.6]
, so one can restrict their attention to the case of
n>1
.

Question 6.4
.

It would be interesting to generalise the results in this paper to the setting of Hilbert newforms with non-trivial central character and specifically what will the number field be.

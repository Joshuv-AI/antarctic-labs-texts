Abstract
. We address the question of the analyticity of a rank one perturbation of an analytic operator. If ℳ z \mathscr{M}_{z} is the bounded operator of multiplication by z z on a functional Hilbert space ℋ κ \mathscr{H}_{\kappa} and f ∈ ℋ f\in\mathscr{H} with f ⁡ ( 0 ) = 0 , f(0)=0, then ℳ z + f ⊗ 1 \mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1 is always analytic. If f ⁡ ( 0 ) ≠ 0 , f(0)\neq 0, then the analyticity of ℳ z + f ⊗ 1 \mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1 is characterized in terms of the membership to ℋ κ \mathscr{H}_{\kappa} of the formal power series obtained by multiplying f ⁡ ( z ) f(z) by 1 f ⁡ ( 0 ) − z . \frac{1}{f(0)-z}. 1.
Analyticity and the invariance of the left spectrum

Several examples of multiplication operators on reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces suggest that the essential spectrum and the left spectrum coincide. Since the essential spectrum of a bounded linear operator is always invariant under compact perturbations (see
[
3
]
), it is reasonable to ask whether the left spectrum is also invariant under a compact or a finite rank perturbation ? On a testing ground, we address the above problem for rank one perturbations. The main result of this note exploits the Shimorin’s analytic model (see
[
9
]
) for analytic left invertible operators to provide a solution to this problem when the operator in question is a cyclic analytic left-invertible operator and the perturbation is of the form
f\otimes g
with
g\in\ker T^{*}.
We see that solution to the above problem is closely related to the description of the hyper-range of the rank one perturbation of analytic operators.
Before we state the main result, we fix some notations and collect the necessary preliminaries.

Let
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of complex numbers. For a positive real number
r,
let
\mathbb{D}_{r}
denote the open disc centered at
0
and of radius
r.
For simplicity, the open unit disc
\mathbb{D}_{1}
will be denoted by
\mathbb{D}.
For any holomorphic function
\phi:\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and an integer
n\geqslant 0,
let
\hat{\phi}(n)
denote the coefficient of
z^{n}
in the power series representation of
\phi.
Let
\overline{\partial}
denote the partial derivative with respect to
\overline{w}.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a nonzero complex Hilbert space and
let
T
be a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}.
Let
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
denote the set of bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}.
Recall that
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
is a unital
C^{*}
-algebra with unit

We denote by
\sigma_{p}(T),

\sigma(T),

\sigma_{l}(T)
and
\sigma_{e}(T)
the point spectrum, spectrum, left spectrum and essential spectrum of the bounded linear operator
T,
respectively. The spectral radius of
T
is denoted by

(refer to
[
3
]
for definitions and basic spectral theory).
For

the bounded linear operator
f\otimes g
on
\mathcal{H}
is given by

\displaystyle f\otimes g(h)=\langle{h},\,{g}\rangle f,\quad h\in\mathcal{H}.

A bounded linear operator
T
on
\mathcal{H}
is
left invertible

if there exists a bounded linear operator

on
\mathcal{H}
(a

) such that

For a positive integer

\mathcal{H}

of

is the closed linear span of

For simplicity, we refer
1
-cyclic operator as the

A bounded linear operator

if the

\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}{T^{n}}\mathcal{H}
of

\{0\}.
We

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}=\bigvee_{n\geqslant 0}T^{n}(\ker T^{*}).

Let
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
be a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of complex-valued holomorphic functions defined on the unit disc and let
\kappa:\mathbb{D}\times\mathbb{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be the reproducing kernel for
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},
that is,
\kappa(\cdot,w)\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
and

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{\kappa(\cdot,w)}\rangle_{\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}}=f(w),\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~w\in\mathbb{D}.

The reader is referred to
[
8
]
for the basics of reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces.
We call
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
a
functional Hilbert space
if the following hold:

(A1)

if

is a holomorphic function, then
h\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
if and only if
zh\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},

(A2)

\kappa
is normalized at the origin, that is,
\kappa(z,0)=1
for every
z\in\mathbb{D},

(A3)

Under the assumptions (A1) and (A2), the orthogonal complement of
\{zf:f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}\}
is spanned by the space of constant functions.

Remark 1.1
.

Assume that
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
is a functional Hilbert space.
By (A1) and the closed graph theorem, the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by the coordinate function
z
defines a bounded linear operator on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.
By (A2),

\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
consists of constant functions, and hence by (A1),

\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
contains the linear space of polynomials.
By (A3), the kernel of
\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}
is spanned by the constant function
1.

Here is the main result of this paper (cf.
[
7
, Proposition 4.2]
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
be a functional Hilbert space and and let
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
denote the operator of multiplication by
z
on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Then
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1
is analytic if and only if exactly one of the following holds
:

(i)

f(0)=0,

(ii)

f(0)\neq 0
and
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{f(0)^{i}}\Big)z^{j}~\mbox{does not belong to}~\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},
where
\hat{f}(n)
denotes the coefficient of
z^{n}
in the power series representation of
f.

Remark 1.3
.

Note that the formal power series in (ii) above is nothing but the product of
f
with the formal power series
\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{z}{f(0)}\Big)^{j}.

Although Theorem
1.2
is of independent interest, it helps understand the problem of the invariance of the left spectra under compact perturbations. To see some general facts pertaining to this problem, let
T,K
be bounded linear operators on
\mathcal{H}.
If
K
is a compact operator, then

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(T+K)\setminus\sigma_{p}(T+K)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T),\quad\sigma_{l}(T)\setminus\sigma_{p}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T+K).

(1.1)

Indeed, if
\lambda\notin\sigma_{l}(T),
then

\displaystyle\mbox{$T-\lambda$ is left-invertible}\Rightarrow\mbox{$T+K-\lambda$ is left semi-Fredholm}

\displaystyle\Rightarrow\mbox{$\mbox{ran}(T+K-\lambda)$ is closed and $\ker(T+K-\lambda)$ is finite dimensional},

and hence either
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\sigma_{l}(T+K)
or
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(T+K)
completing the verification of the first inclusion in (
1.1
). The second inclusion in (
1.1
) can be obtained along similar lines. The obvious question appears here is when equality occurs in these inclusions.
This plus little more yields the following:

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
T,K\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Assume that
K
is a compact operator. Then the following statements are valid
:

(i)

if
\sigma_{p}(T)=\emptyset,
then
\sigma_{l}(T+K)=\sigma_{l}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T+K),

(ii)

for every
\lambda\in\sigma_{p}(T+K)\backslash\sigma_{l}(T),

\ker(T+K-\lambda)
is finite dimensional.

Proof.

If
\sigma_{p}(T)=\emptyset,
then by (
1.1
),

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(T)=\sigma_{l}(T)\setminus\sigma_{p}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T+K)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T)\cup\sigma_{p}(T+K),

which yields (i). To see (ii), let
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\backslash\sigma_{l}(T).
For
h\in\mathcal{H},
note that

(T+K-\lambda)h=0
if and only if
(T-\lambda)h=-Kh.

(1.2)

Applying a left-inverse
L_{\lambda}
of
T-\lambda
on both sides, we get

\displaystyle h=-L_{\lambda}Kh.

(1.3)

Thus the dimension of
\ker(T+K-\lambda)
is the dimension of
\ker(L_{\lambda}K+I).
Since
L_{\lambda}K
is a compact operator,
\ker(T+K-\lambda)
is finite dimensional (see
[
3
, Corollary 1.2.3 and Theorem 1.3.3]
).
∎

Thus the problem of invariance of the left-spectra could be answered provided we determine eigenvalues of the compact perturbation. Let us analyse this problem for
K=f\otimes g
with
g\in\ker T^{*}.

Proposition 1.5
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and
f,g\in\mathcal{H}.
For
\lambda\notin\sigma_{l}(T),
the following statements are valid
:

(i)

the dimension of
\ker(T+f\otimes g-\lambda)
is at most
1,

(ii)

if
g\in\ker T^{*}
and
\lambda\neq\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle,
then
\lambda\notin\sigma_{p}(T+f\otimes g).

In particular, if
g\in\ker T^{*}
and
\sigma_{p}(T)=\emptyset,
then

\sigma_{p}(T+f\otimes g)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T)\cup\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}.

Proof.

By (
1.3
), any
h\in\ker(T+f\otimes g-\lambda)
is a multiple of
L_{\lambda}f,
and hence (i) follows.
If

\lambda
is an eigenvalue of
T+f\otimes g
with eigenvector
h,
then by (
1.3
),
\langle{h},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0,
and hence
by (
1.2
),
f=-\frac{(T-\lambda)h}{\langle{h},\,{g}\rangle}.
Taking inner-product with
g,
this implies that
\lambda=\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
(since
g\in\ker T^{*}
).
This yields (ii).
To see the remaining part, note that

\sigma_{p}(T+f\otimes g)\setminus\sigma_{l}(T)\subseteq\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}
(by (ii)) and apply
Proposition
1.4
.
∎

Thus a solution to the problem of the invariance of the left spectra for the aforementioned rank one perturbation boils down to deciding whether or not
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
is an eigenvalue of
T+f\otimes g.
This lies deeper!
Nevertheless,
as an application of the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we obtain the following
:

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
T
be a cyclic analytic left invertible operator in
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).
Assume that
f\in\mathcal{H}
and
g\in\ker T^{*}.
Then
\sigma_{l}(T)
is a subset of
\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)
such that

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)\backslash\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}=\sigma_{l}(T)\backslash\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\},

\displaystyle r(T+f\otimes g)=\max\{r(T),|\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle|\}.

Moreover, the following statements are valid
:

(i)

if
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
belongs to
\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)\backslash\{0\},
then it is a simple eigenvalue of
T+f\otimes g,

(ii)

either
\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)=\sigma_{l}(T)
or
\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)=\sigma_{l}(T)\cup\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}.

Remark 1.7
.

In general, the inclusion
\sigma_{l}(T)\subseteq\sigma_{l}(T+f\otimes g)
and the inequality
r(T+f\otimes g)\geqslant r(T)
are strict. Further, any of following possibilities can occur:
r(T+f\otimes g)<|\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle|,

r(T+f\otimes g)=|\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle|,

r(T+f\otimes g)>|\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle|.

The proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
occupy major portion of Section 2. In the remaining part of this section, we explain the essential difference between the problems of the invariance of the essential spectra and that of left spectra with the help of one concrete family of multiplication operators. Recall that the Fredholm index is invariant under compact perturbations (see
[
3
, Theorem 1.3.1]
). So if one can show that the dimension of the cokernel is not preserved under perturbations, then so is the dimension of the kernel, and hence the left spectrum may not be invariant under compact perturbations.
The following proposition supports these speculations (cf.
[
10
, Theorem 1]
).

Proposition 1.8
.

Let
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
be a functional Hilbert space and and let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
denote the operator of multiplication by
z.

Assume that
the kernel
\kappa
satisfies

\displaystyle\ker(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}-\overline{w})=\{\alpha\kappa(\cdot,w):\alpha\in\mathbb{C}\},\quad w\in\mathbb{D}.

(1.4)

If
f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
and
S:=\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1,

then the following statements are valid
:

(i)

if
f(0)=0,
then

\displaystyle\ker S^{*}=\begin{cases}\mbox{span}\{\kappa(\cdot,0),\overline{\partial}\kappa(\cdot,w)|_{w=0}\}&\mbox{if}~f^{\prime}(0)=-1,\\
\mbox{span}\{\kappa(\cdot,0)\}&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

(ii)

if
f(0)\neq 0,
then

\ker S^{*}=\mbox{span}\{(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})\kappa(\cdot,0)-\overline{f(0)}\,\overline{\partial}\kappa(\cdot,w)|_{w=0}\}.

In particular, if
f(0)=0
and
f^{\prime}(0)=-1,
then
S
is not cyclic.

Proof.

Recall from

[
5
, Lemma 4.1]
and
[
4
, Lemma 1.22(ii)]
that

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}&e_{n}:=\frac{\overline{\partial}^{n}\kappa(\cdot,w)}{n!}\Big|_{w=0}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~\langle{f},\,{e_{n}}\rangle=\frac{({\partial}^{n}f)(0)}{n!},\quad f\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa},~n\geqslant 0,\\
&\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}e_{n}=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if}~n=0,\\
e_{n-1}&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}\end{array}\right\}

We claim that

\ker S^{*}=\{ae_{0}+be_{1}:b(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})+a\overline{f(0)}=0\}.

(1.8)

To see this, note that since
\kappa(\cdot,0)=1,
by (
1
),

\displaystyle S^{*}(ae_{0}+be_{1})

\displaystyle=M^{*}_{z}(ae_{0}+be_{1})+1\otimes f(ae_{0}+be_{1})

\displaystyle=b(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})+a\overline{f(0)}.

(1.9)

Thus, if
b(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})+a\overline{f(0)}=0
, then

ae_{0}+be_{1}\in\ker S^{*}.
Conversely, let
h\in\ker S^{*}.
Note that

\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}h=-\langle{h},\,{f}\rangle,

and since
1\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z},

h
belongs to
\ker\mathscr{M}^{*2}_{z}.

Since
\kappa
satisfies (
1.4
), by
[
4
, Lemma 1.22(ii)]
,
h\in\mbox{span}\big\{e_{0},e_{1}\big\}.
This combined with (
1
)
yields (
1.8
).

Assume now that
f(0)=0.

Thus by (
1.8
),

\displaystyle\ker S^{*}=\{ae_{0}+be_{1}:b(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})=0\}.

In case
f^{\prime}(0)=-1,

\ker S^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{0},e_{1}\}.
Otherwise,
\ker S^{*}=\mbox{span}\{e_{0}\}.

If
f(0)\neq 0,
then by (
1.8
),
\ker S^{*}
consists of elements of the form
ae_{0}+be_{1}
where
a=-\frac{b(1+\overline{f^{\prime}(0)})}{\overline{f(0)}}
and we get the desired conclusion in (ii).
Since
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
contains all complex polynomials, it is easy to see using (
1
) that
\kappa(\cdot,0)
and
\overline{\partial}\kappa(\cdot,w)|_{w=0}
are linearly independent.
The remaining part now follows from (i) and
[
6
, Proposition 1(i)]
.
∎

2.
Proofs of Theorems
1.6
and
1.2

In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, we employ reproducing kernel techniques to describe the hyper-range of a rank one perturbation
f\otimes g,

g\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z},
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}.
This combined with
the Shimorin’s analytic model yields the proof of Theroem
1.6
.
Recall from

[
9
, Pg 154]
that any analytic left-invertible operator
T
is unitarily equivalent to the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}
of multiplication by
z
on a reproducing kernel Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
with kernel
\kappa.

Indeed,
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
consists of
\ker T^{*}
-valued holomorphic functions on a disc centered at the origin in the complex plane and
the reproducing kernel
\kappa
of
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
satisfies
\kappa(\cdot,0)=1
(see
[
9
, Eqn (2.6)]
).
If, in addition,
T
is cyclic, then
\ker T^{*}
is one-dimensional, and hence, in the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we may assume that
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
is a functional Hilbert space.
To prove Theorems
1.2
and
1.6
, we need several lemmas. The first one is of algebraic nature and holds for any bounded linear operator on a complex Hilbert space.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
and let
f,g\in\mathcal{H}.
If
T^{*}g=0,
then the following statements are valid
:

(i)

for any positive integer
n,

\displaystyle(T+f\otimes g)^{n}=T^{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}T^{j}f\otimes g,

(2.1)

(ii)

if
T
is cyclic, then
T+f\otimes g
is
2
-cyclic.

Proof.

Suppose that
T^{*}g=0.

(i) Clearly, the formula (
2.1
) holds for
n=1.
Assume the formula (
2.1
) for a positive integer
n\geqslant 2.
Since
T^{*}g=0,

(f\otimes g)T=f\otimes T^{*}g=0
and for any
h\in\mathcal{H},

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)(T^{j}f\otimes g)h=\langle{h},\,{g}\rangle\langle{T^{j}f},\,{g}\rangle f=0,\quad j\geqslant 1.

This combined with the induction hypothesis yields

\displaystyle(T+f\otimes g)(T+f\otimes g)^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{n+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}T^{j+1}f\otimes g

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(f\otimes g)T^{n}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}(f\otimes g)\,T^{j}f\otimes g

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{n+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}T^{j+1}f\otimes g

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-1}(f\otimes g)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{n+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{n}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j}T^{j}f\otimes g.

This completes the verification of (i).

(ii) Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}.
A routine verification using (
2.1
) shows that for any integer
n\geqslant 1,

\displaystyle(T+f\otimes g)^{n}\xi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle T^{n}\xi+\langle{\xi},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}T^{j}f,

\displaystyle(T+f\otimes g)^{n-1}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}T^{j}f.

It is immediate that

\displaystyle T^{n}\xi=(T+f\otimes g)^{n}\xi-\langle{\xi},\,{g}\rangle(T+f\otimes g)^{n-1}f.

Thus, if
\xi
is a cyclic vector for
T,
then
T+f\otimes g
is
2
-cyclic with the set of cyclic vectors equal to
\{\xi,f\}.

∎

The next lemma required in the proof of Theorem
1.6
describes the hyper-range of rank one perturbations of left invertible analytic operators (cf.
[
2
, Lemma 2.4]
).

Lemma 2.2
.

For
r>0,
let
\mathscr{H}
be a Hilbert space of complex-valued holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}_{r}
such that
\mathscr{H}
contains all complex polynomials in
z.

Assume that

h\in\mathscr{H}
if and only if
zh\in\mathscr{H}.

(2.2)

Let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
denote the operator of multiplication by
z
and let
f,g\in\mathscr{H}
be such that
g\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}.

Then the following statements are valid
:

(i)

if
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle=0,
then
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
is analytic,

(ii)

if
f(0)\neq 0
and
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0,
then the hyper-range
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
of
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
is given by

\displaystyle\mathscr{R}_{\infty}=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\mathrm{span}\Big\{h_{0}\Big\}&\mbox{if}~h_{0}~\mbox{belongs to}~\mathscr{H},\\
\{0\}&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

where

h_{0}:=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}}\Big)z^{j},

(iii)

if
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0
and
h_{0}
converges in
\mathscr{H},
then
h_{0}
is an eigenfunction of
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
with respect to the simple eigenvalue
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle.

Proof.

Let

\mathscr{M}_{f,g}:=\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g.
Note that

h\in\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
if and only if for every integer
n\geqslant 1,
there exists
h_{n}\in\mathscr{H}
such that

h=\mathscr{M}^{n}_{f,g}h_{n}.
Thus, by Lemma
2.1
(i),
h\in\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
if and only if there exists
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}\subseteq\mathscr{H}
such that

\displaystyle h=z^{n}h_{n}+\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}z^{j}f,\quad n\geqslant 1.

(2.3)

(i): In case
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle=0,

h=z^{n}h_{n}+\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle z^{n-1}f

for every positive integer
n,

and hence
h
has zero at
0
of arbitrary order. Thus
h=0
in this case.

(ii): Assume that
\hat{f}(0)\neq 0
and
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0.
Fix an integer
n\geqslant 1.
Letting
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}
in
(
2.3
), we get

\displaystyle h=z^{n}h_{n}+\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\hat{f}(k)\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-i-1}z^{i+k},\quad n\geqslant 1.

Comparing the coefficients on the both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{h}(j)=\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{i=0}^{j}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-i-1}\hat{f}(j-i),\quad j=0,\ldots,n-1,~n\geqslant 1.

(2.4)

Letting
j=0
in the above identity, we get
\hat{h}(0)=\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-1}\hat{f}(0).
Thus, (
2.4
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\hat{h}(j)=\frac{\hat{h}(0)}{\hat{f}(0)}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}},\quad j=0,\ldots,n-1,~n\geqslant 1,

where we used the assumption that
f(0)=\hat{f}(0)
and
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
are nonzero. Since
n
was arbitrary, we obtain that
h=\frac{\hat{h}(0)}{\hat{f}(0)}h_{0}.

Thus, if
h_{0}
does not belong to
\mathscr{H},
then
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}=\{0\}.

We now check that if
h_{0}\in\mathscr{H},
then
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
is spanned by
h_{0}.

To see this, assume that
h_{0}\in\mathscr{H}.
Note that
h_{0}
belongs to the hyper-range of
\mathscr{M}_{f,g}
provided there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
in
\mathscr{H}
such that (
2.3
) holds for
h=h_{0}
.
It follows from the definition of
\hat{h}_{0}(\cdot)
and (
2.2
) that
h_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{z^{j}f}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{j}}
is divisible by
z^{n}
in
\mathscr{H}.

Thus there exists a sequence
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
in
\mathscr{H}
such that

\displaystyle h_{0}-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{z^{j}f}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{j}}=z^{n}h_{n},\quad n\geqslant 1.

(2.5)

Fix
n\geqslant 1.

Comparing the coefficient of
z^{n}
on both sides, we obtain

\hat{h}_{0}(n)-\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\frac{\hat{f}(n-j)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{j}}=\hat{h}_{n}(0).

However, by the definition of
h_{0},
this simplifies to

\displaystyle\frac{\hat{f}(0)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n}}=\hat{h}_{n}(0).

(2.6)

Since any
\phi\in\mathscr{H}
can be written as
\phi=\hat{\phi}(0)+z\psi
for
\psi\in\mathscr{H}
(see (
2.2
)) and
g\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z},
we have
\langle{\phi},\,{g}\rangle=\langle{\hat{\phi}(0)},\,{g}\rangle,
and hence

\displaystyle\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle=\langle{\hat{h}_{n}(0)},\,{g}\rangle\overset{\eqref{2.6}}{=}\frac{\langle{\hat{f}(0)},\,{g}\rangle}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n}}=\frac{1}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-1}}.

Combining this with (
2.5
), we see that
\{h_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
satisfies

\displaystyle h_{0}=z^{n}h_{n}+\langle{h_{n}},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{n-j-1}z^{j}f,\quad n\geqslant 1.

This completes the proof of (ii).

(iii) Assume that
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0
and
h_{0}\in\mathscr{H}.
By (ii),
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
is one-dimensional invariant subspace of
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
, and hence there exists
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that

(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g)h_{0}=\lambda h_{0}.

Since
g\in\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z},

\displaystyle(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g)h_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}}\Big)z^{j+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}}\Big)\langle{z^{j}},\,{g}\rangle f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}}\Big)z^{j+1}+\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\hat{f}(k)z^{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle^{i}}\Big)z^{j}.

Comparing the constant terms on both sides, we get
\lambda=\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle.
Since
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
contains eigenfunction corresponding to any nonzero eigenvalue of
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
and since
\mathscr{R}_{\infty}
is one dimensional,
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle
is a simple eigenvalue of
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g,
completing the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
is a functional Hilbert space,
\langle{f},\,{1}\rangle=f(0).
The desired conclusion is now immediate from Lemma
2.2
(with
g=1
). ∎

Here is a particular case of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
. If
f
is a polynomial of degree
n,
then the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1
is analytic if and only if exactly one of the following holds
:

(i)

f(0)=0,

(i)

f(0)\neq 0,

f(f(0))\neq 0
and
\frac{1}{f(0)-z}\notin\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Proof.

Assume that
f(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{f}(k)z^{k},

z\in\mathbb{D},
and
f(0)\neq 0.

Since
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
contains all polynomials (see Remark
1.1
),

h_{0}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}

\Leftrightarrow

h_{1}:=\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{f(0)^{i}}\Big)z^{j}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

(2.7)

Also, since
\hat{f}(j)=0
for
j>n,

\displaystyle h_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\Big(\sum_{i=j-n}^{j}\frac{\hat{f}(j-i)}{f(0)^{i}}\Big)z^{j}

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{n}\hat{f}(k)f(0)^{k}\Big)\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{f(0)^{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(f(0))\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}\frac{z^{j}}{f(0)^{j}}.

This together with (
2.7
) shows that
h_{0}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
if and only if either
f(f(0))=0
or
\frac{1}{f(0)-z}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

One may now apply Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Assume that
f
is a polynomial such that
f(0)\neq 0
and
f(f(0))\neq 0.

By (
2.7
) and (
2.8
),

h_{0}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
if and only if
\frac{1}{f(0)-z}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

To complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
, we need another fact from the spectral theory.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

In view of the discussion prior to Lemma
2.1
, we may assume that
T=\mathscr{M}_{z}
is acting on a functional Hilbert space
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Since
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is analytic and injective,
\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z})=\emptyset.

Hence, by Proposition
1.4
,

\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g)=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z})\cup\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g).

Consider the following cases
:

\bullet

If
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle=0,
then by Lemma
2.2
(i),
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g
is analytic, and hence
\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g)\subseteq\{0\}=\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}.

\bullet

If
\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\neq 0,
then by Proposition
1.5
(ii) and Lemma
2.2
,,
\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes g)\subseteq\{\langle{f},\,{g}\rangle\}
(since
0\notin\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z})
).

All conclusions now follow from (i) and (ii) of Proposition
1.4
.
∎

The following is immediate from Theorem
1.6
.

Corollary 2.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
, if
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1
is analytic and
f(0)\neq 0,
then

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}),\quad r(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=r(\mathscr{M}_{z}).

Following
[
9
]
, we call the operator

T^{\prime}:=T(T^{*}T)^{-1}
the
Cauchy dual
of
T
whenever
T

is left-invertible. Here is an application to operators satisfying the so-called kernel condition (see
[
1
, Section 2]
).

Corollary 2.6
.

Assume the hypotheses of Theorem
1.2
. Assume that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
satisfies the kernel condition:

\displaystyle\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z}(\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z})\subseteq\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}.

If
\mathscr{M}_{z}
has the wandering subspace property, then the operator
\mathscr{M}_{z}+1\otimes 1
also has the wandering subspace property.

Proof.

Recall the fact that for any left-invertible operator
S
on
\mathcal{H},

S
is analytic if and only if the Cauchy dual
S^{\prime}
of
S
has the wandering subspace property (see
[
9
, Corollary 2.8]
). In view of this fact, it suffices to check that if
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}
is analytic, then so is
(\mathscr{M}_{z}+1\otimes 1)^{\prime}.

Let
T
be a left invertible operator and
S=T+f\otimes g
with
f\in\ker T^{*}
of unit norm and
g\in\mathcal{H}.
Note that

\displaystyle S^{*}S=(T^{*}+g\otimes f)(T+f\otimes g)=T^{*}T+g\otimes g.

It is easy to see using
T^{\prime*}T^{\prime}=(T^{*}T)^{-1}
that

\displaystyle(S^{*}S)^{-1}=(T^{*}T)^{-1}-(1+\|T^{\prime}g\|^{2})^{-1}(T^{*}T)^{-1}g\otimes(T^{*}T)^{-1}g.

One may now verify that
S^{\prime}
is given by

\displaystyle(T+f\otimes g)^{\prime}=T^{\prime}+(1+\|T^{\prime}g\|^{2})^{-1}(f-T^{\prime}g)\otimes(T^{*}T)^{-1}g.

(2.9)

Since
\mathscr{M}_{z}
satisfies the kernel condition, by
[
1
, Proposition 2.1]
,

(\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}\mathscr{M}_{z})^{-1}\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}=\ker\mathscr{M}^{*}_{z}.

Thus
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}1=\beta z
for some scalar
\beta.

Apply now the formula (
2.9
) to
f=g=1
to conclude that
(\mathscr{M}_{z}+1\otimes 1)^{\prime}
is of the form
\mathscr{M}^{\prime}_{z}+(\alpha-\beta z)\otimes 1
for some constants
\alpha,\beta.
The desired conclusion now follows from Theorem
1.2
.
∎

3.
An example

We conclude this note with one motivating example illustrating the general picture of the invariance of the left spectra.

Example 3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
be a functional Hilbert space such that all complex polynomials are dense in
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Let
\mathscr{M}_{z}
denote the operator of multiplication by
z
on
\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

For scalars
a,b\in\mathbb{C},
let
f(z)=az+b,

z\in\mathbb{D}.

By Corollary
2.3
,
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1
is analytic if and only if

b=0
or

b\neq 0,

a\neq-1
and
\frac{1}{b-z}\notin\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.

Assume that
\mathscr{M}_{z}
is left invertible.
Moreover, by Theorem
1.6
,

\displaystyle\left.\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)\backslash\{b\}=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z})\backslash\{b\},\\
r(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\max\{r(\mathscr{M}_{z}),|b|\}.\end{array}\right\}

Furthermore, we have the following
:

\bullet

Assume that
b=0
and
a\neq 0.
Then
f=az
and

\displaystyle(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)(1)=(1+a)z.

Thus
1\in\ker(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)
if and only if
a=-1.
Hence,
by Proposition
1.5
,
\ker(\mathscr{M}_{z}-z\otimes 1)
is spanned by
\{1\}.

In this case,

\mathscr{M}_{z}-z\otimes 1
is analytic and

\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}-z\otimes 1)=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z})\sqcup\{0\},\quad r(\mathscr{M}_{z}-z\otimes 1)=r(\mathscr{M}_{z}).

\bullet

Assume that
b\neq 0,

a\neq-1
and
\frac{1}{b-z}\in\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}
(so that
|b|\geqslant 1
). Then by Lemma
2.2
(iii) and Remark
2.4
,
b\in\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1),
and hence by (
3.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z})\cup\{b\},\quad r(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\max\{r(\mathscr{M}_{z}),|b|\}.

\bullet

Assume that
b\neq 0,

a\neq-1
and
\frac{1}{b-z}\notin\mathscr{H}_{\kappa}.
Then,
by Lemma
2.2
(ii) and Remark
2.4
,
\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1
is analytic. Also, by Proposition
1.5
(ii),
0
does not belong to
\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1).
Since the point spectrum of an analytic operator is contained in
\{0\},

\sigma_{p}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\emptyset.
It now follows from (
3.1
) that

\displaystyle\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=\sigma_{l}(\mathscr{M}_{z}),\quad r(\mathscr{M}_{z}+f\otimes 1)=r(\mathscr{M}_{z}).

It is evident that the above discussion extends, with suitable modifications, to the case when
f
is a polynomial.

\diamondsuit

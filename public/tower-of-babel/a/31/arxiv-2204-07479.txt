Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces and energy equality in the Navier-Stokes equations
Yanqing Wang, Xue Mei and Wei Wei

Abstract
In this paper, it is shown that an analogue in mixed norm spaces of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality is valid. As an application, some new criteria for energy conservation of Leray-Hopf weak solutions to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations are established in these spaces, which extend the corresponding known results.

42B35, 42B25, 35A23, 35L65, 76D05

Keywords:
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality; anisotropic Lebesgue spaces; Navier-Stokes equations; energy equality

Introduction

1.1
Sobolev-Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality

Starting from the appearance of the Sobolev embedding theorem, it has become an essential
tool in modern theory of partial differential equations.
The classical Sobolev inequality reads

\|f\|_{L^{\frac{rn}{n-r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\nabla f\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~1\leq r<n.

(1.1)

A celebrated generalization of Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) is the following Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality obtained independently by
[
15
]
and
[
24
]

\|D^{j}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|D^{m}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta},

(1.2)

where
j,m
are any integers satisfying
0\leq j<m
,
~1\leq q,r\leq\infty,~
and

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{j}{n}=\theta(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{m}{n})+(1-\theta)\frac{1}{q}

for all
\theta
in the interval
\frac{j}{m}\leq\theta\leq 1,
unless
1<r<\infty
and
m-j-\frac{n}{r}
is a nonnegative integer.

Almost at the same time, Benedek-Panzone
[
5
]
introduced mixed (anisotropic) Lebesgue spaces
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\overrightarrow{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n})

and extended the Sobolev inequality (
1.1
) to

\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~\text{with}~~\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)=s,~1<\overrightarrow{r}<\overrightarrow{p}<\infty.

(1.3)

It is clear that the anisotropic Lebesgue spaces
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are a generalization of usual Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.
Later on, in the framework of anisotropic Lebesgue spaces and anisotropic derivatives, Besov-Il’in-Nikolskiǐ
[
6
]
showed an anisotropic Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality below

\displaystyle

\displaystyle\|D^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p(0)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\mu_{0}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\|D^{l_{j}}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p(j)}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\mu_{j}},~~\text{with}~~\sum_{j=0}^{n}\mu_{j}=1,~0\leq\mu_{j}\leq 1,~

(1.4)

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}-\frac{1}{q_{i}}=(l_{i}-\frac{1}{p(i)_{i}})\mu_{i}-\sum_{0\leq j\leq n,\,j\neq i}\frac{\mu_{j}}{p(j)_{i}},~1\leq i\leq n,~~~\text{and}~~~\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq\sum^{n}_{j=0}\frac{\mu_{j}}{{p(j)}_{i}}.

The Besov-Il’in-Nikolskiǐ inequality (
1.4
) is a very powerful tool and
plays an important role in the study of partial differential equations (see
[
34
,
28
,
10
,
7
,
8
,
17
,
29
]
and references therein).
However, it seems that it is difficult to derive from both of the inequality
(
1.4
)
and its generalization in
[
20
,
14
]
that

\|u\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{4}L_{x_{2}}^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|u\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{2}L_{x_{2}}^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{\theta}\|\nabla u\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{2}L_{x_{2}}^{3}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{1-\theta},

(1.5)

owing to the condition
\eqref{BIN}_{2}
involving each component of vector-valued indices.
In fact, the standard Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
in mixed Lebesgue spaces without anisotropic derivatives such as
(
1.5
)
is still unknown in the literature.
The main objective of this paper is to establish
the most general form of standard Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
in mixed Lebesgue spaces. Our first result reads

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0\leq\sigma<s,

1<\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q},\overrightarrow{r}<\infty,

0\leq\theta\leq 1,

\Lambda^{s}=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
be defined via

\widehat{\Lambda^{s}f}(\xi)=|\xi|^{s}\hat{f}(\xi).

Then there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
u
such that

\|\Lambda^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta},

(1.6)

if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\theta}{q_{i}}+(1-\theta)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}-s\right),~\frac{1}{p_{i}}\leq\frac{\theta}{q_{i}}+\frac{1-\theta}{r_{i}},~0\leq\theta\leq 1-\frac{\sigma}{s}.

(1.7)

Remark 1.1
.

Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in mixed Lebesgue spaces
requires weaker restrictions than that of Besov-Il’in-Nikolskiǐ’s anisotropic interpolation inequality (
1.4
). In particular, the inequality
(
1.5
) can be deduced from (
1.6
) with
\theta=7/11
. Moreover, our result (
1.6
) with
\theta=0
improves the Sobolev inequality (
1.3
) in mixed Lebesgue spaces.

As a byproduct, one can immediately obtain the following corollary, which was frequently used in the regularity of the weak solutions of the Navier-Stokes equations (see
[
29
,
34
,
17
]
).

Corollary 1.2
.

Let
q_{1},q_{2},q_{3}\in[2,\infty)
and
\frac{1}{2}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq\frac{3}{2}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C=C(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})
such that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3}{2}-{\sum_{i}\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}\|u\|^{\sum_{i}\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(1.8)

Since the first condition in (
1.7
) for Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in anisotropic Lebesgue
spaces is posed indirectly on each component of indices, it seems that the
recent argument developed for Fourier-Herz spaces in
[
13
]
and Lorentz spaces in
[
32
]
, which requires many applications of interpolation inequality (
2.2
) involving each component, breaks down in the proof of Theorem
1.1
. To overcome this difficulty, we reformulate the desired inequality (
1.6
) as

\|\Lambda^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\Lambda^{s(1-\theta)}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\mathcal{M}u\|^{\theta}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathcal{M}(\Lambda^{s}u)\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{\beta}}=\frac{\theta}{\overrightarrow{q}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\overrightarrow{r}}.

Here the first inequality is partially motivated by
[
6
,
20
]
, and its proof relies on the celebrated
Marcinkiewicz-Lizorkin multiplier theorem together with a duality argument and Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality. The second inequality
rests on
the following pointwise estimate proved by the decomposition of low and high frequencies

\left|\Lambda^{s(1-\theta)}u(x)\right|\leq C(\mathcal{M}u(x))^{\theta}\left(\mathcal{M}\Lambda^{s}u(x)\right)^{1-\theta},

whose proof can be found in
[
1
, p.84]
. This helps us to achieve the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the past decades, important progress involving the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.2
) has been made in Besov spaces by Hajaiej-Molinet-Ozawa-Wang
[
18
]
, in Besov-Fourier-Herz spaces by Chikami
[
13
]
, and in Besov-Lorentz spaces by
[
32
]
(see also Byeon-Kim-Oh
[
9
]
). Inspired by this, a natural question arises whether Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.2
) can be extended in the context of anisotropic Besov
spaces. Our corresponding result is formulated as follows.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
0\leq\sigma<s<\infty
and
1\leq\overrightarrow{q},\overrightarrow{r},\overrightarrow{p}\leq\infty.
In addition, if

\min\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}\}<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\max\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}},\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}\}
or
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{q_{i}}
, we require that
there exists
\alpha\in[0,1]
such that

\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{p}}=\frac{\alpha}{\overrightarrow{q}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\overrightarrow{r}}.

(1.9)

Then there holds for any
u\in\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}^{s}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)\cap\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}^{0}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)
that

\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}^{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{1-\theta},

(1.10)

with

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sigma=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\theta}{q_{i}}+(1-\theta)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{r_{i}}-s\right),~s\neq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{r_{i}}-\frac{1}{q_{i}}\Big),~0<\theta<1-\frac{\sigma}{s}.

Remark 1.2
.

Under the condition (
1.9
), in view of the embedding relations

\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}^{s}\hookrightarrow\dot{H}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p}}\hookrightarrow\dot{B}_{\overrightarrow{p},\infty}^{s}
(see Lemma
2.2
), it can be seen that Theorem
1.3
partially implies Theorem
1.1
. It is an interesting question how to remove the additional condition (
1.9
).

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is close to that in the recent work
[
13
,
32
]
. The new ingredients are mainly the generalized Bernstein type inequalities for anisotropic Lebesgue spaces in Lemma
2.1
, which are of independent interest and may be applied to other topics.

Next, we give an application of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.1
in the study of energy conservation for weak solutions to the three-dimensional Navier-Stokes equations.

1.2
An application of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces to the Navier-Stokes system

The classical incompressible 3D Navier-Stokes equations can be written as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &v_{t}-\Delta v+v\cdot\nabla v+\nabla\Pi=0,\\
&\mathrm{div}\,v=0,\\
&v|_{t=0}=v_{0},\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

where the unknown vector
v=v(x,t)
describes the flow velocity field, and the scalar function
\Pi
represents the pressure.
The initial datum
v_{0}
is given and satisfies the divergence-free condition.
The global Leray-Hopf finite energy weak solution of the 3D Navier-Stokes system has been constructed by Leray
[
21
]
for the Cauchy problem and by Hopf
[
19
]
for Dirichlet problem, and obeys the energy inequality

\|v(T)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}ds\leq\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Unfortunately, both the conservation of energy
and the regularity for this type of weak solutions are unknown. Here, we focus on the results involving energy equality of the Leray-Hopf weak solutions.
The sufficient conditions for
energy equality of weak solutions to the 3D Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
) have attracted a lot of attention (see
[
22
,
27
,
2
,
12
,
33
,
32
,
30
,
31
]
and
references therein). Concretely speaking,
a weak solution to the Dirichlet problem on a bounded smooth domain
\Omega
of 3D Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
) satisfies energy equality if one of the following four conditions holds

•

Lions
[
22
]
:
v\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\Omega));

•

Shinbrot
[
27
]
:

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),~\text{with}~\frac{2}{p}+\frac{2}{q}=1~\text{and}~q\geq 4;

•

Beirao da Veiga-Yang
[
2
]
:

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1
and
3<q<4;

•

Berselli-Chiodaroli
[
4
]
, Beirao da Veiga-Yang
[
3
]
, Zhang
[
33
]
:

\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\Omega)),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2~\text{and}~\frac{3}{2}<q<\frac{9}{5},~\text{or}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{6}{5q}=1~\text{and}~q\geq\frac{9}{5}.

In
[
30
]
, the authors observed that all the aforementioned results on the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}
are a consequence of the following
energy conservation criterion via a combination of the velocity and the gradient of velocity

v\in L^{\frac{2k}{k-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2\ell}{\ell-1}}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))~~\text{and}~~\nabla v\in L^{k}(0,T;L^{\ell}(\mathbb{T}^{3})).

(1.12)

A parallel result of energy conservation criterion (
1.12
)
for both the Cauchy problem and Dirichlet problem is given in
[
31
]
. We also refer the readers to
[
12
,
11
,
32
,
31
]
for the sufficient conditions keeping the energy of Navier-Stokes equations on the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Finally, we present some new energy conservation criteria in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces via the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
).

Theorem 1.4
.

The Leray-Hopf weak solutions to
Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
)
are persistence of energy if one of the following four conditions is satisfied

(1)

v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;\,L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))~\text{and}~\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~1<p,\overrightarrow{q}\leq\infty
;

(2)

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}=1~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq 1,~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq 4
;

(3)

\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}=2~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq 2
,
~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq\frac{9}{5}
;

(4)

\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{5}(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})=1~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq\frac{5}{3},~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq 3.

Remark 1.3
.

The first criterion in Theorem
1.4
is a generalization of all the corresponding known results in terms of the velocity and the gradient of the velocity.
For the Dirichlet problem on a bounded smooth domain
\Omega
,
one may establish similar results to this theorem
by the approximation sequence developed in
[
16
,
27
,
22
,
4
,
31
]
.

Remark 1.4
.

In the spirit of
[

,

,

]
, we invoke the Littlewood-Paley theory in the proof of (1) in Theorem
1.4
to show that energy conservation class (
1.12
) can also be extended in context of anisotropic Lebesgue spaces.
The proof of (2) in this theorem is a combination of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) and Lions’s class
v\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).
We conclude the results in
(3) and (4) by (1) together with the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
).

Remark 1.5
.

The sufficient class (1) for the weak solutions keeping the energy covers (
1.12
) on the wholes space in
[

]
.
The criteria (2) and (3) in Theorem

are a generalization of corresponding results in
[

v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
with
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=1
and
3<q<4,
or
\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{3}{q}=2

2
Notations and key auxiliary lemmas

To begin with, we introduce some notations used in this paper.
For
p\in[1,\,\infty]
, the notation
L^{p}(0,T;X)
stands for the set of measurable functions
f(x,t)
on the interval
(0,T)
with values in
X
and
\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{X}
belonging to
L^{p}(0,T)
.
For a vector
\overrightarrow{p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{n})\in[1,\infty]^{n},
we denote
1/\overrightarrow{p}=(1/p_{1},1/p_{2},\ldots,1/p_{n}).
An inequality between the vector
\overrightarrow{p}
and a real number
\beta\in[1,\infty]
is understood as the corresponding inequality between each component
p_{j}
and the number
\beta
for
j=1,2,\ldots,n
. An inequality between vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
signifies the corresponding inequality between all the respective components. For simplicity, we write
\sum\limits_{i}f_{i}=:\sum f_{i}.
The sign function
sgn(x)
is defined by

sgn(x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} -1&\quad\text{ if }~x<0,\\
0&\quad\text{ if }~x=0,\\
1&\quad\text{ if }~x>0.\end{aligned}\right.

Throughout this paper,
C
is an absolute constant which may be different from line to line unless otherwise stated.
a\approx b
means that
C^{-1}b\leq a\leq Cb
for some constant
C>1
.
\chi_{\Omega}
stands for the characteristic function of the set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
|E|
represents the
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure of a set
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and its definition is given by

\mathcal{M}f(x)=\sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{|B(r)|}\int_{B(r)}|f(x-y)|dy,

where
f
is any locally integrable function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
B(r)
is the open ball centered at the origin with radius
r>0
.

2.1
Basic materials on anisotropic Lebesgue spaces

For
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\overrightarrow{q}=(q_{1},q_{2},\ldots,q_{n})\in[1,\infty]^{n}
,
a function
f(x)
belongs to the anisotropic Lebesgue space
L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

if

\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}}L^{q_{2}}\cdots L^{q_{n}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\Big\|\cdots\big\|\|f\|_{L_{x_{1}}^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\big\|_{L_{x_{2}}^{q_{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\cdots\Big\|_{L_{x_{n}}^{q_{n}}(\mathbb{R})}<\infty.

The study of anisotropic Lebesgue spaces first appears in Benedek-Panzone
[
5
]
.
We list several useful properties of anisotropic Lebesgue spaces as follows.

•

The Hölder inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces
[
5
]

\|fg\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}~~\text{with}~~\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{r}}+\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{s}}=1,~1\leq\overrightarrow{r}\leq\infty.

(2.1)

•

The interpolation inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces
[
5
]

\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|f\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{t}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}~~\text{with}~~\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{r}}=\frac{\alpha}{\overrightarrow{s}}+\frac{1-\alpha}{\overrightarrow{t}},~0\leq\alpha\leq 1,~1\leq\overrightarrow{s},\overrightarrow{t}\leq\infty.

(2.2)

•

Young’s inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces
[
5
]

\displaystyle\|f\ast g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}~~\text{with}~~1+\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{r}}=\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{p}}+\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{q}},~1\leq\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q}\leq\infty.

(2.3)

2.2
Generalized Bernstein inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces

As an application of Young’s inequality (
2.3
), we may derive the generalized Bernstein inequalities in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces as follows.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let a ball
B=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq R\right\}
with
0<R<\infty
and an annulus
\mathcal{C}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq r_{2}\right\}
with
0<r_{1}<r_{2}<\infty
. Then a constant
C>1
exists such that for any nonnegative integer
k,
any couple
(\overrightarrow{p},\overrightarrow{q})
with
1\leq\overrightarrow{p}\leq\overrightarrow{q}\leq\infty,
any
\lambda\in(0,\infty)
, and any function
u
in
L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, there hold

\displaystyle\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\lambda^{k+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right)}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}~~\text{with}~~\operatorname{supp}\widehat{u}\subset\lambda B,

(2.4)

\displaystyle C^{-1}\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\lambda^{k}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}~~\text{with}~~\operatorname{supp}\widehat{u}\subset\lambda\mathcal{C},

(2.5)

where
\lambda B=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq\lambda R\right\}
and
\lambda\mathcal{C}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda r_{1}\leq|\xi|\leq\lambda r_{2}\right\}
.

Proof.

(1)  Let
\psi
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\chi_{B}\leq\hat{\psi}\leq\chi_{2B}
. Since
\widehat{u}(\xi)=\hat{\psi}(\xi/\lambda)\widehat{u}(\xi)
when
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{u}\subset\lambda B
, we have

\partial^{\alpha}u=i^{|\alpha|}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\xi^{\alpha}\hat{u})=i^{|\alpha|}\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\xi^{\alpha}\hat{\psi}(\xi/\lambda)\widehat{u}(\xi))=\lambda^{|\alpha|}(\partial^{\alpha}\psi)_{\lambda}\ast u.

Here
(\partial^{\alpha}\psi)_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{n}\,\partial^{\alpha}\psi(\lambda x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Fix
|\alpha|=k
. Take
1/\overrightarrow{r}=1+1/\overrightarrow{q}-1/\overrightarrow{p}
, then the hypotheses on the indices imply that
1\leq\overrightarrow{r}\leq\infty
. In view of (
2.3
), we infer that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\alpha}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k}\left\|(\partial^{\alpha}\psi)_{\lambda}\ast u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\lambda^{k}\left\|(\partial^{\alpha}\psi)_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+n-\sum_{i}\frac{1}{r_{i}}}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda^{k+\sum_{i}\left(\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\frac{1}{q_{i}}\right)}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}\psi\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This together with the fact that
\partial^{\alpha}\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
yields (
2.4
).

(2)   Observe that (
2.4
) with
\overrightarrow{q}=\overrightarrow{p}
implies the second inequality of (
2.5
), it suffices to show the first inequality in (
2.5
). Let
\eta
be a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\chi_{\mathcal{C}}\leq\hat{\eta}\leq\chi_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}}
, where
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}/2\leq|\xi|\leq 2r_{2}\right\}
. It follows from
\operatorname{supp}\hat{u}\subset\lambda\mathcal{C}
that for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\hat{u}(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{(-i\xi)^{\alpha}}{|\xi|^{2k}}\hat{\eta}\left(\xi/\lambda\right)(i\xi)^{\alpha}\hat{u}(\xi)=\lambda^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\frac{(-i\xi/\lambda)^{\alpha}}{|\xi/\lambda|^{2k}}\hat{\eta}\left(\xi/\lambda\right)\mathcal{F}(\partial^{\alpha}u)(\xi).

Therefore, we may write

u=\lambda^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}(g_{\alpha})_{\lambda}\ast\partial^{\alpha}u,

where
(g_{\alpha})_{\lambda}(x)=\lambda^{n}g_{\alpha}(\lambda x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

g_{\alpha}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\left(\frac{(-i\xi)^{\alpha}}{|\xi|^{2k}}\hat{\eta}(\xi)\right)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

This together with Young’s inequality (
2.3
) yields that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|(g_{\alpha})_{\lambda}\ast\partial^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-k}\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|(g_{\alpha})_{\lambda}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{-k}\left(\sum_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|g_{\alpha}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)\sup_{|\alpha|=k}\left\|\partial^{\alpha}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

This concludes (
2.5
) and the proof is completed.
∎

2.3
Anisotropic Besov spaces and anisotropic Sobolev spaces

\mathcal{S}
represents the Schwartz class of rapidly decreasing functions,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the
space of tempered distributions,
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}/\mathcal{P}
the quotient space of tempered distributions which modulo polynomials.
We use
\mathcal{F}f
or
\widehat{f}
to denote the Fourier transform of a tempered distribution
f
.
To define anisotropic Besov space, we need the following dyadic unity partition
(see e.g.
[
1
]
). Choose two nonnegative radial
functions
\varrho
,
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be
supported respectively in the ball
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\leq\frac{4}{3}\}
and the shell
\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|\leq\frac{8}{3}\}
such that

\varrho(\xi)+\sum_{j\geq 0}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n};\qquad\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\varphi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\xi\neq 0.

The nonhomogeneous dyadic
blocks
\Delta_{j}
are defined by

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\Delta_{j}f:=0~~\text{if}~~j\leq-2,\\
&\Delta_{-1}f:=\varrho(D)f=(\varrho(\xi)\hat{f}(\xi))^{\vee},\\
&\Delta_{j}f:=\varphi\left(2^{-j}D\right)f=2^{jn}\check{\varphi}(2^{j}\cdot)\ast f~~\text{if}~~j\geq 0,\\
&S_{j}f:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\Delta_{k}f~~\text{for}~~j\in\mathbb{Z}.\end{aligned}\right.

The homogeneous Littlewood-Paley operators are defined as follows

\forall j\in\mathbb{Z},\quad\dot{\Delta}_{j}f:=\varphi(2^{-j}D)f\quad\text{and}\quad\dot{S}_{j}f:=\sum_{k\leq j-1}\dot{\Delta}_{k}f.

The homogeneous anisotropic Besov space
\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p},r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
r\in[1,\infty]
and
\overrightarrow{p}\in[1,\infty]^{n},
is the set of
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
such that

\left\|f\right\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p},r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\left\|\left\{2^{js}\left\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}f\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right\}\right\|_{\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})}<\infty,

where
\ell^{r}(\mathbb{Z})
represents the set of sequences with summable
r
-th powers.
The homogenous anisotropic Sobolev norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
is defined as
\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\Lambda^{s}f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

When
p_{1}=\cdots=p_{n}=p
, the anisotropic Sobolev spaces reduce to the classical Sobolev spaces
\dot{H}^{s}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
f\in\dot{H}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\overrightarrow{p}\in[1,\infty]^{n}
. Then there holds

\|f\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{\dot{H}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

(2.6)

where
C>0
depends only on
n,s
and
\varphi
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for all functions
g\in L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,

\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\rho=\left(|\xi|^{-s}\varphi(\xi)\right)^{\vee}
and
\rho_{j}(x)=2^{jn}\rho(2^{j}x)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

To this end, observe that
\rho
is a Schwartz function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<\infty.
This together with Young’s inequality (
2.3
) implies that

\displaystyle\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{j}\ast g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\sup_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\rho_{j}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\|\rho\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which concludes the proof.
∎

We end this section with an anisotropic version of approximate identity lemma, which will be used in the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Assume that
f(x,t)\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
with
p\in[1,\infty)
and
\overrightarrow{q}\in[1,\infty)^{n}
. Let
S_{j}f(x,t)=\varrho(2^{-j}D)f(x,t)=\mathcal{F}^{-1}_{\xi\rightarrow x}(\varrho(2^{-j}\xi)\,\mathcal{F}_{y\rightarrow\xi}(f(y,t)))
.
Then there holds

\big\|S_{j}f-f\big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))}\rightarrow 0,~\text{as}~j\rightarrow\infty.

Proof.

Note that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\check{\varrho}=\varrho(0)=1
, we have

\displaystyle S_{j}f(x,t)-f(x,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f(x-y,t)-f(x,t)\right)\check{\varrho}_{j}(y)dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(f(x-2^{-j}y,t)-f(x,t)\right)\check{\varrho}(y)dy

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t\in(0,T)
, where
\check{\varrho}_{j}(y)=2^{jn}\check{\varrho}(2^{j}y)
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then the generalized Minkowski inequality in
[
6
, Theorem 2.10]
enables us to arrive at

\displaystyle\big\|S_{j}f(\cdot,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\|f(\cdot-2^{-j}y,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\check{\varrho}(y)|dy.

Since the translation operator is strongly continuous on
L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\overrightarrow{q}\in[1,\infty)^{n}
(see
[
6
, Theorem 1.5]
), there holds for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\big\|f(\cdot-2^{-j}y,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.

Combining this with the fact that
\big\|f(\cdot-2^{-j}y,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\big\|f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
and
\check{\varrho}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, we may derive from Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem that for almost every
t\in(0,T)
,

\limsup_{j\rightarrow\infty}\big\|S_{j}f(\cdot,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\big\|f(\cdot-2^{-j}y,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}|\check{\varrho}(y)|dy=0.

This together with the observation that

\big\|S_{j}f(\cdot,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2\big\|f(\cdot,t)\big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\check{\varrho}(y)|dy\in L^{p}(0,T)

yields that

\lim_{j\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{T}\big\|S_{j}f(\cdot,t)-f(\cdot,t)\big\|^{p}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}dt=0.

Here we have used the Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem once again, which concludes the proof of this lemma.
∎

3
Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in mixed Lebesgue spaces

This section is devoted to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, which establishes Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Since the estimate (
1.6
) with
n=1
is the well-known fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in isotropic Lebesgue spaces on
\mathbb{R}
, we only need to consider the case that
n\geq 2.

By applying the celebrated Marcinkiewicz-Lizorkin multiplier theorem in
[
23
]
to the Fourier multiplier operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with each of the following three symbols

m_{1}(\xi)=\frac{|\xi_{i}|^{s}}{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}|^{s}}~~\text{with}~~1\leq i\leq n,~~m_{2}(\xi)=\frac{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}|\xi_{i}|^{s}}{|\xi|^{s}},~~m_{3}(\xi)=\frac{1}{m_{2}(\xi)},

we arrive at

\|\Lambda^{s}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\Lambda_{i}^{s}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\approx\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda_{i}^{s}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

for any
s\geq 0
and
1<\overrightarrow{p}<\infty
. Here
\Lambda=(-\Delta)^{1/2}
and
\Lambda_{i}=(-\partial_{i}^{2})^{1/2}
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

Hence, the desired inequality (
1.6
) is equivalent to

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda_{i}^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\|\Lambda_{i}^{s}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{1-\theta}.

Furthermore, this estimate together with the standard rescaling analysis implies the following equivalent inequality that for
k=1,2,\ldots,n
,

\|\Lambda_{k}^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\theta}\left(\lambda_{k}^{-\sigma}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{\tau_{j}}\right)\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}\|\Lambda_{i}^{s}u\|^{\frac{1}{s}}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\right)^{s(1-\theta)},

(3.1)

where
\tau_{j}=\frac{1}{p_{j}}-\frac{\theta}{q_{j}}-\frac{1-\theta}{r_{j}}
for
1\leq j\leq n
. For
n\geq 2
, this yields that
\sigma-s(1-\theta)\leq\tau_{j}\leq 0
with
1\leq j\leq n
, otherwise we can derive a contradiction for any nonzero function
u
by letting some positive
\lambda_{i}
in (
3.1
) tend to zero or infinity appropriately. Also, by taking
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{2}=\cdots=\lambda_{n}>0,
we infer that

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}(-\tau_{j})=s(1-\theta)-\sigma\geq 0.

This means the neccessity of condition (
1.7
) for anisotropic inequality (
1.6
) in Theorem
1.1
.

In what follows, we shall give a proof of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) under the condition (
1.7
).

Since the case
\theta=1
implies that
\sigma=0
and
\overrightarrow{q}=\overrightarrow{p}
, it suffices to prove (
1.6
) for the case
\theta<1
. The proof can be divided into two steps.

Firstly, we shall show

\|\Lambda^{\sigma}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\|\Lambda^{s(1-\theta)}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

or equivalently,

\Big\|(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda^{\tau_{j}})f\Big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\Big\|\Lambda^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}}f\Big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=\left\|\Lambda^{\sigma-s(1-\theta)}f\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where

\overrightarrow{\tau}=\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{p}}-\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{\beta}},\quad 1<\overrightarrow{\beta}\leq\overrightarrow{p}<\infty.

Note that the celebrated Marcinkiewicz-Lizorkin multiplier theorem in
[
23
]
applied to the Fourier multiplier operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the symbol
\frac{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}|\xi|^{\tau_{j}}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}|\xi_{j}|^{\tau_{j}}}
yields the estimate

\Big\|(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda^{\tau_{j}})f\Big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\Big\|(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda_{j}^{\tau_{j}})f\Big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

we only need to prove

\Big\|(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda_{j}^{\tau_{j}})f\Big\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

Indeed, the classical Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev inequality on fractional integration and the Hölder inequality enable us to deduce that

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\cdot(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda_{j}^{\tau_{j}})f(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\,dy_{1}\cdots dy_{n}\Big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n}}f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})g(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Big\{|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{sgn(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j}+1)}\delta(x_{j}-y_{j})^{sgn(\tau_{j})+1}B\}dx_{1}\cdots dx_{n}dy_{1}\cdots dy_{n}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{n}\left\{|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{sgn(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j}+1)}\delta(x_{j}-y_{j})^{sgn(\tau_{j})+1}\right\}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{n}

\displaystyle\times\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})|\cdot|g(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})|\cdot|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{sgn(\tau_{1})(\tau_{1}+1)}\delta(x_{1}-y_{1})^{sgn(\tau_{1})+1}dx_{1}dy_{1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2n-2}}\|f(\,\cdot,x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\|_{L_{1}^{\beta_{1}}(\mathbb{R})}\|g(\,\cdot,y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})\|_{L_{1}^{p_{1}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\times\prod\limits_{j=2}^{n}\left\{|x_{j}-y_{j}|^{sgn(\tau_{j})(\tau_{j}+1)}\delta(x_{j}-y_{j})^{sgn(\tau_{j})+1}\right\}dx_{2}\cdots dx_{n}dy_{2}\cdots dy_{n},

where
\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{p}^{\prime}}=1-\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{p}}\,
and
\delta
represents the Dirac delta function at the origin. By induction, it can easily be seen that

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}g(y)\cdot(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{n}\Lambda_{j}^{\tau_{j}})f(y)dy\right|\leq C\|f\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|g\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

which verifies the desired estimate.

Next, thanks to the decomposition of low and high frequencies, one can derive the following pointwise estimate

\left|\Lambda^{s(1-\theta)}u(x)\right|\leq C(\mathcal{M}u(x))^{\theta}\left(\mathcal{M}\Lambda^{s}u(x)\right)^{1-\theta},

(3.2)

whose proof can be found in
[
1
, p.84]
. As a consequence, it follows from the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\left\|\Lambda^{s(1-\theta)}u\right\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|(\mathcal{M}u)^{\theta}\left(\mathcal{M}\Lambda^{s}u\right)^{1-\theta}\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{\beta}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\mathcal{M}u\|^{\theta}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\mathcal{M}\left(\Lambda^{s}u\right)\|^{1-\theta}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
\frac{1}{\overrightarrow{\beta}}=\frac{\theta}{\overrightarrow{q}}+\frac{1-\theta}{\overrightarrow{r}}
. According to the boundedness of Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator in mixed Lebesgue spaces (see
[
26
, Theorem 6.9]
), we conclude the desired inequality (
1.6
).
The proof of Theorem
1.1
is completed.
∎

(

\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}

Note that

\displaystyle\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}

\displaystyle=(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{s}\frac{1}{r_{i}}+(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})(\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}})+(\sigma-s(1-\theta))

\displaystyle<(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\frac{\sigma}{s}\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\frac{\sigma}{s}\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

(
I_{13}
)
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
:

We observe that
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
and (

)
mean that
\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}
. Therefore, we need not consider the case that
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
here.

(
I_{14}
)
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
:

An easy computation yields that

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\sum\frac{\theta}{q_{i}}+\sum\frac{1-\theta}{r_{i}}-[(1-\theta)s-\sigma]<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}+(1-\theta)\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}.

(4.3)

It turns out that
\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}=\sum\frac{1}{r}_{i}
. This is a contradiction.

(
I_{15}
)
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
:

We assert that
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
and
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
yield a contradiction.
Note that

\Big(\frac{1-\frac{\sigma}{s}-\theta}{1-\frac{\sigma}{s}}\Big)\Big[\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}-(\sum\frac{1}{r}_{i}-({s-\sigma}))\Big]=\Big(\frac{\theta}{1-\frac{\sigma}{s}}\Big)\Big[(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}\Big].

(4.4)

When
(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}
, we see that
\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}=\sum\frac{1}{r}_{i}-s
. This contradicts the hypothesis that
s\neq\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{q}_{i})
.
As a conseuqence, we only need to consider the subcase that
(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}
or
(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}>\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}
.
On one hand, we derive from
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
and
\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}
that
\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
, which leads to a
contradiction. On the other hand, if there hold

\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}>\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
and
\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+\sum\frac{\sigma}{sr_{i}}
, then it follows from (
4.4
) that

\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}-(\sum\frac{1}{r}_{i}-({s-\sigma}))<0
, namely,

0<{s-\sigma}<\sum\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<0
. We get a contradiction once again.

(
I_{2}
)
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
:

(
I_{21}
)
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}
:

By means of (
1.9
), we
observe that

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{(1+\varepsilon)\overrightarrow{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\frac{1-\alpha}{q_{i}}+\frac{\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}},

(4.5)

where
\varepsilon>0
will be determined later.
We derive from this, Bernstein inequalities in mixed Lebesgue spaces and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}>0
that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{q,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{(1+\varepsilon)\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+n(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}+C\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s\alpha-\sum\frac{\varepsilon\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}}-\sigma]}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q}\infty}}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\alpha},

where we have used the fact that
\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})>0
.

Denote
\delta(\varepsilon)=s\alpha-\sum\frac{\varepsilon\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}}-\sigma
. From (
4.5
), we see that

\delta(\varepsilon)=\frac{s[(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}-(1+\varepsilon)q_{i}]}{(1+\varepsilon)(p_{i}-q_{i})}-\sum\frac{[\varepsilon(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}-(1+\varepsilon)q_{i}]}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}(1+\varepsilon)(p_{i}-q_{i})}-\sigma,

and
\delta(\varepsilon)
is a continuous function at neighborhood of
0
. Since
\delta(0)>0
, there exists a sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
such that
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
.
Then it follows from (
4.6
) that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{q,\infty,\infty}}+C\frac{-k[s\alpha-\sum\frac{\varepsilon\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}}-\sigma]}{1-2^{s\alpha-\sum\frac{\varepsilon\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}}-\sigma}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\alpha},

(4.7)

which implies that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

(
I_{22}
)
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}
:

From (
4.3
), we know that this subcase will not happen.

(
I_{23}
)
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
:

This subcase is similar to the subcase (
I_{15}
). We omit the details here.

(
I_{3}
)

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
:

(
I_{31}
)

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}
:

According to the Bernstein inequality in Lemma
2.1
,
\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})>0
and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}>0
, we observe that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R})^{n}}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R})^{n}}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}\|\Delta_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}+C\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}.

Thus the desired inequality

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,1,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}

(4.9)

follows by taking

{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}\approx{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty,\infty}}.

(
I_{32}
)
\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}
:

We proceed as in the proof of (
4.8
) and get

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{q,\infty}}+C\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r}\infty}},

where we have used the fact that
\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})>0
and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}>0
.

Consequently, as the derivation of (
4.9
), we find out that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,1,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q}}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

(
I_{33}
)
\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
:

Taking advantage of Bernstein inequalities in Lemma
2.1
and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}>0
, we know that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{\overrightarrow{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{q,\infty}}+\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}},

where we have used the fact that
\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})>0
.

Therefore, it follows from

\frac{2^{k[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{[\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{q,\infty}}\approx\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}

that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{p,1,1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma+\sum(\frac{1}{q}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})}+\frac{1}{s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})}\Big)\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

(
I_{34}
)
\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}=\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<\frac{1}{r}_{i}
:

It is worth remarking that this subcase implies that
\sigma>0
. In the same manner as (
4.5
), we see that

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{(1+\varepsilon)\overrightarrow{p},\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},~~\frac{1}{p_{i}}=\frac{1-\alpha}{q_{i}}+\frac{\alpha}{(1+\varepsilon)p_{i}},

(4.10)

where
\varepsilon>0
will be determined later.

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\Delta_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{(1+\varepsilon)\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+C\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma+\sum\frac{\varepsilon\alpha}{p_{i}(1+\varepsilon)}-s(1-\alpha)]}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\alpha}+C\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}},

As the arguments in (
I_{21}
), we can choose
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small to ensure that

\sigma+\frac{n\varepsilon\alpha}{p(1+\varepsilon)}-s(1-\alpha)>0
and get the desired inequality. We omit the details.

(
I_{35}
)
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
:

Notice that
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}+\sigma-s<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
and (
4.4
) mean that

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}<(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\frac{\sigma}{s}\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}.

(4.11)

This together with (
1.9
) leads to

\sigma-s(1-\alpha)>0

(4.12)

and

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.13)

By means of Bernstein inequalities, (
4.13
), (
4.12
) and
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}+\sigma-s<\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
, we infer that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\vec{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{-j[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq C\frac{2^{k[\sigma-s(1-\alpha)]}}{2^{[\sigma-s(1-\alpha)]}-1}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q}}}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\alpha}+C\frac{2^{-k[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{1-2^{-[s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{r_{i},\infty}}.

Therefore we may deduce that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{\sigma-s(1-\alpha)}+\frac{1}{s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})}\Big)\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

Case 2:

\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+{\sigma}<\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}
and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<0
.

It is clear that
\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+{\sigma}<\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}<\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}
.
From
\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}+\sigma-s>\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}
and (
4.4
), we see that

\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}>\sum(1-\frac{\sigma}{s})\frac{1}{q_{i}}+\frac{\sigma}{s}\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}},

which together with (
1.9
) leads to

s(1-\alpha)-\sigma>0

(4.14)

and

\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}.

(4.15)

In view of Bernstein inequalities, (
4.15
),
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}<0
and (
4.14
), we have

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{p}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\leq k}2^{j[\sigma-s+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}2^{js}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\sum_{j>k}2^{j\sigma}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\|\dot{\Delta}_{j}u\|^{1-\alpha}_{L^{\overrightarrow{r}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{k[\sigma-s+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]}}{2^{-[\sigma-s+\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})]-1}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}+\frac{2^{-k[s(1-\alpha)-\sigma]}}{1-2^{-[s(1-\alpha)-\sigma]}}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}^{\alpha}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\alpha}.

Repeating the deduction of (
4.9
), we know that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{\sigma}_{\overrightarrow{p},1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{1}{s-\sigma-\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{p}_{i})}+\frac{1}{s(1-\alpha)-\sigma}\Big)\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{0}_{\overrightarrow{q},\infty}}^{\theta}\|u\|_{\dot{B}^{s}_{\overrightarrow{r},\infty}}^{1-\theta}.

Case 3:

\sum\frac{1}{q}_{i}+{\sigma}=\sum\frac{1}{p}_{i}
and
{s-\sigma}+\sum\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sum\frac{1}{r_{i}}=0
. This case will not be considered in this theorem, owing to the hypothesis that
s\neq\sum(\frac{1}{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{q}_{i})
.

At this stage, we complete the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

5
An
application of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces

This section is concerned with the application of Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces to the
energy conservation criteria of 3D Navier-Stokes equations.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

(1)
v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;\,L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
,
1<p,\overrightarrow{q}\leq\infty.

For
N\in\mathbb{N},
applying nonhomogeneous Littlewood-Paley operator
S_{N}
to the Navier-Stokes equations (
1.11
) and taking the
L^{2}
inner product with
S_{N}v
, one arrives at

\frac{1}{2}\|S_{N}v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla S_{N}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds=\frac{1}{2}\|S_{N}v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}v_{i})S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds.

(5.1)

Thus, in order to prove energy equality

\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}+2\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}ds=\|v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

(5.2)

for all
t\in[0,T)
, it suffices to show that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}v_{i})S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds=0

by estimating the nonlinear term in (
5.1
).

Indeed, it follows from the divegence-free condition and integration by parts that

\begin{split}&-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}v_{i})S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds\\
=&-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}S^{2}_{N}v_{i})S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}[v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}]S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds\\
=&-\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}v_{j}\partial_{j}(S^{2}_{N}v_{i})^{2}\,dxds-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}\big[v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}\big]S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds\\
=&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{N}\big[v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}\big]S_{N}\partial_{j}v_{i}\,dxds,\end{split}

where the notation
\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}
represents the identity operator. We further employ the Hölder inequality (
2.1
) and Young’s inequality (
2.3
) in
anisotropic Lebesgue spaces to get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}S_{N}\big[v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}\big]S_{N}\partial_{j}v_{i}\,dxds\right|

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big\|S_{N}\big[v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}\big]\big\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\big\|S_{N}\partial_{j}v_{i}\big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big\|v_{j}(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S^{2}_{N})v_{i}\big\|_{L^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\big\|\partial_{j}v_{i}\big\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\big\|\big(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}+S_{N}\big)\big(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S_{N}\big)v\big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\times\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\big\|\big(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S_{N}\big)v\big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\times\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

In the light of Lemma
2.3
, we see that, as
N\rightarrow\infty
,

\big\|\big(\text{I}_{\textnormal{d}}-S_{N}\big)v\big\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;\,L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\rightarrow 0.

It turns out that, as
N\rightarrow\infty
,

-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\partial_{j}(v_{j}v_{i})S^{2}_{N}v_{i}\,dxds\rightarrow 0.

Hence, letting
N\rightarrow\infty
in (
5.1
), we conclude the energy equality (
5.2
).
The proof of this part is completed.

(2)
v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}=1~\text{and}~~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq 1,~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq 4.

Making use of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in mixed norm spaces, we
obtain

\|v\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{3}{4}}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}}\|v\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}},

where we have used the fact that
\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}\geq\frac{3}{4}
and
\frac{1}{4}\leq\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{3}{4}}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}})+\frac{1}{q_{j}}(\frac{\frac{1}{4}}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}})
for
j=1,2,3
.

Since
\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq 1
, we further conclude by the Hölder inequality that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\|v\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{4}dt\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{\sum\frac{4}{q_{i}}-3}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}}\|v\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}}dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt\Big)^{\frac{2(\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{3}{4})}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}}\Big(\int_{0}^{T}\|v\|^{\frac{1}{1-\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}}}_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}dt\Big)^{\frac{1-\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}}{\sum\frac{1}{q_{i}}-\frac{1}{2}}},

which leads to
v\in L^{4}(0,T;L^{4}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).
The Lions’s class for energy equality of weak solutions to Navier-Stokes equations allows us to complete the proof of this part.

(3)
\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}=2~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq 2
,
~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq\frac{9}{5}
.

According to the result of (1) in this theorem, it suffices to show that
v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})).
Thanks to the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) and the Sobolev embedding (
1.3
) in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces, we have

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})-5}{2(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})-3}}\|v\|_{L^{\frac{3q_{1}}{3-q_{1}}}L^{\frac{3q_{2}}{3-q_{2}}}L^{\frac{3q_{3}}{3-q_{3}}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{2-(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})}{2(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})-3}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{p-3}{p-2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{1}{p-2}},

which leads to

\displaystyle\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{p-3}{p-2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{1}{p-2}}.

Here the restriction
1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq\frac{9}{5}
guarantees that the second condition in (
1.7
) is valid.
Hence, we finish the proof of this part.

(4)
\nabla v\in L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})),~\text{with}~\frac{1}{p}+\frac{2}{5}(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})=1~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{3}\frac{1}{q_{i}}\leq\frac{5}{3},~1<\overrightarrow{q}\leq 3.

As mentioned above, in terms of the result of (1) in this theorem, it is enough to prove that
v\in L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
. To this end, in view of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.6
) in anisotropic Lebesgue spaces, we deduce that

\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{5-3(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})}{5-2(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}}}{5-2(\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\frac{1}{q_{2}}+\frac{1}{q_{3}})}}=C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{\frac{p-1}{2}},

which implies that

\|v\|_{L^{\frac{2p}{p-1}}(0,T;L^{\frac{2q_{1}}{q_{1}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{2}}{q_{2}-1}}L^{\frac{2q_{3}}{q_{3}-1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{3-p}{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{p}(0,T;L^{\overrightarrow{q}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}.

At this stage, all proofs of this theorem are given.
∎

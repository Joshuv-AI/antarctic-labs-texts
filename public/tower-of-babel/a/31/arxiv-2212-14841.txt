Abstract.
Let

A_{m,n}(a)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-4)^{j}{m+j\choose 2j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2j}(a+2k\pi/n)

and

B_{m,n}(a)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-4)^{j}{m+j+1\choose 2j+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2j+1}(a+2k\pi/n),

where
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
are integers and
a
is a real number. We present two proofs for the following results:

(i)   If
2m+1\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}n\sin((2m+1)a).

(ii)   If
2m+1\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then
A_{m,n}(a)=0
.

(iii)   If
2(m+1)\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then

B_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{n}{2}\sin(2(m+1)a).

(iv)   If
2(m+1)\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then
B_{m,n}(a)=0
.

Keywords.
Combinatorial identity, trigonometric function, Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind.

1.
Introduction and statement of the main result

The work on this note has been inspired by a remarkable paper on trigonometric identities published by
Chu and Marini
[
5
]
in 1999. One of their results states that

(1.1)

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\frac{\sin(a+2k\pi/n)}{1+x^{2}-2x\cos(a+2k\pi/n)}=G_{n,a}(x)

with

(1.2)

G_{n,a}(x)=\frac{nx^{n-1}\sin(na)}{1+x^{2n}-2x^{n}\cos(na)}.

Some authors used (1.1) and several similar identities to derive numerous interesting trigonometric summation formulas. We refer to
Andrica and Piticari
[
1
]
, Chamberland
[
2
]
, Chen
[
3
]
, Chu
[
4
]
, Wang and Zheng
[
8
]
,
and the references cited therein.

Here, we study the two closely related combinatorial sums

A_{m,n}(a)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-4)^{j}{m+j\choose 2j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2j}(a+2k\pi/n)

and

B_{m,n}(a)=\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-4)^{j}{m+j+1\choose 2j+1}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2j+1}(a+2k\pi/n),

where
m\geq 0
and
n\geq 1
are integers and
a
is a real number. We show that (1.1) and (1.2) can be applied to find identities for

A_{m,n}(a)
and
B_{m,n}(a)
. Our main result reads as follows.

Theorem.

Let
m\geq 0
,
n\geq 1
be integers and let
a
be a real number.

(i)
If

2m+1\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
,
then

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}n\sin((2m+1)a).

(ii)
If

2m+1\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
,
then

A_{m,n}(a)=0
.

(iii)
If

2(m+1)\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
,
then

B_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{n}{2}\sin(2(m+1)a).

(iv)
If

2(m+1)\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
,
then

B_{m,n}(a)=0
.

These formulas can be used to deduce further identities. For example, if we integrate
A_{m,n}(a)
from
a=0
to
a=\pi/2
, then we obtain

C_{m,n}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(-1)^{m}{n}/(2m+1),&\textrm{if \, $2m+1\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)$, }\\
0,&\textrm{if \, $2m+1\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)$, }\end{array}\right.

where

C_{m,n}=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\frac{(-4)^{j}}{2j+1}{m+j\choose 2j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\bigl(\sin^{2j+1}(2k\pi/n)+\cos^{2j+1}(2k\pi/n)\bigl).

In the following sections, we present two proofs of our Theorem. The first one makes use of a comparison of coefficients in series expansions, where (1.1) and (1.2) play a key role. The second proof applies properties of the classical Chebyshev polynomials.

2.
First proof

Let

z=x(x-2\cos(a+2k\pi/n)).

Then, for
|x|<\sqrt{2}-1
,

|z|\leq|x|(|x|+2)<1.

Using (1.1), the geometric series and the binomial formula yields

(2.1)

\displaystyle G_{n,a}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}(-x)^{\nu}(x-2\cos(a+2k\pi/n))^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)(-x)^{\nu}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}{\nu\choose j}x^{j}(-2\cos(a+2k\pi/n))^{\nu-j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}S(j,\nu)

with

S(j,\nu)=(-1)^{j}{\nu\choose j}2^{\nu-j}x^{\nu+j}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{\nu-j}(a+2k\pi/n).

Then,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}S(j,2m-j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2m}\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}{2m-j\choose j}2^{2(m-j)}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2(m-j)}(a+2k\pi/n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{m}A_{m,n}(a)x^{2m}

and

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{m}S(j,2m+1-j)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{2m+1}\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}{2m+1-j\choose j}2^{2(m-j)+1}

\displaystyle\times\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(a+2k\pi/n)\cos^{2(m-j)+1}(a+2k\pi/n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2(-1)^{m}B_{m,n}(a)x^{2m+1}.

Applying

\sum_{\nu=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{\nu}S(j,\nu)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}S(j,2m-j)+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{m}S(j,2m+1-j)

we conclude from (2.1), (2.2) and (2.3) that

G_{n,a}(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{G^{(2m)}_{n,a}(0)}{(2m)!}x^{2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{G^{(2m+1)}_{n,a}(0)}{(2m+1)!}x^{2m+1}=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{m}A_{m,n}(a)x^{2m}+\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}2(-1)^{m}B_{m,n}(a)x^{2m+1}.

Comparing the coefficients reveals that

(2.4)

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{G^{(2m)}_{n,a}(0)}{(2m)!}

and

(2.5)

B_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{G^{(2m+1)}_{n,a}(0)}{2\cdot(2m+1)!}.

Next, we use the formula

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sin(k\alpha)z^{k}=\frac{z\sin(\alpha)}{1+z^{2}-2z\cos(\alpha)};

see Prudnikov et al.
[
7
, Entry 5.4.12.1]
.
We set
z=x^{n}
,
\alpha=na
, multiply both sides by
n/x
and use (1.2). This gives

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}n\sin(kna)x^{nk-1}=G_{n,a}(x).

It follows that for nonnegative integers
p
we obtain

(2.6)

G^{(p)}_{n,a}(0)=0,\quad\mbox{if}\quad p+1\not\in n\mathbb{Z},

and

(2.7)

G^{(p)}_{n,a}(0)=p!n\sin(kna),\quad\mbox{if}\quad p=nk-1.

We consider four cases.

Case 1.

2m+1\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
.

Let
nk_{0}=2m+1
. Using (2.4) and (2.7) gives

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}n\sin(k_{0}na)=(-1)^{m}n\sin((2m+1)a).

Case 2.

2m+1\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
.

From (2.4) and (2.6) we conclude that
A_{m,n}(a)=0
.

Case 3.

2(m+1)\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
.

Let
nk_{1}=(2m+1)+1
. We apply (2.5) and (2.7). This yields

B_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{n}{2}\sin(nk_{1}a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{n}{2}\sin(2(m+1)a).

Case 4.

2(m+1)\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
.

Then,
(2m+1)+1\not\in n\mathbb{Z}
, so that (2.5) and (2.6) lead to
B_{m,n}(a)=0
.

The proof of the Theorem is complete.

3.
Second proof

The Chebyshev polynomials of the first and second kind are defined by

T_{n}(x)=\cos(n\theta)\quad\mbox{and}\quad U_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{n+1}T^{\prime}_{n+1}(x)=\frac{\sin((n+1)\theta)}{\sin(\theta)},\quad n=0,1,2,...,

where
\cos(\theta)=x
. Detailed information about these functions can be found, for example, in Mason and Handscomb
[
6
]
.

In what follows, we make use of the following known formulas:

(3.1)

T_{2n+1}(\sin(\theta))=(-1)^{n}\sin((2n+1)\theta),

(3.2)

\cos(\theta)U_{2n+1}(\sin(\theta))=(-1)^{n}\sin(2(n+1)\theta).

Moreover, we need the trigonometric identities

(3.3)

\cos((2n+1)\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))=(-1)^{n}\text{sign}(\sin(\theta))\sin((2n+1)\theta),

(3.4)

\sin(2n\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))=(-1)^{n+1}\text{sign}(\sin(\theta))\sin(2n\theta)

and the summation formula

(3.5)

\sum_{k=0}^{N}\sin(y+kz)=\frac{\sin(y+Nz/2)\sin((N+1)z/2)}{\sin(z/2)}.

We are now in a position to present our second proof of the Theorem. First, we show that (i) and (ii) are valid.
Let
x\in(-2,2)
. The following formula is given in Prudnikov et al.
[
7
, Entry 4.2.3.15]
:

(3.6)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}{m+j\choose 2j}x^{2j}=\frac{\cos((2m+1)\arcsin(x/2))}{\cos(\arcsin(x/2))}.

We apply (3.3) and (3.1). Then,

(3.7)

\frac{\cos((2m+1)\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))}{\cos(\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))}=\frac{T_{2m+1}(\sin(\theta))}{\sin(\theta)}.

Using (3.6) with
x=2\cos(a+2k\pi/n)
, (3.7) and (3.1) leads to

(3.8)

A_{m,n}(a)=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}T_{2m+1}(\sin(a+2k\pi/n))=(-1)^{m}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin((2m+1)(a+2k\pi/n)).

If
2m+1\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then we conclude from (3.8) that

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}n\sin((2m+1)a).

Let
2m+1\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
. We apply (3.8) and (3.5) with
N=n-1
,
y=(2m+1)a
,
z=(2m+1)2\pi/n
. Then we obtain

A_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m+1}\frac{\sin((2m+1)\pi)}{\sin((2m+1)\pi/n)}\sin((2m+1)(a-\pi/n))=0.

Next, we prove (iii) and (iv). We have

(3.9)

\sum_{j=0}^{m}(-1)^{j}{m+j+1\choose 2j+1}x^{2j+1}=\frac{\sin(2(m+1)\arcsin(x/2))}{\cos(\arcsin(x/2))};

see Prudnikov et al.
[
7
, Entry 4.2.3.17]
. Using (3.4), (3.3) and (3.2) gives

(3.10)

\frac{\sin(2(m+1)\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))}{\cos(\arcsin(\cos(\theta)))}=\frac{\cos(\theta)U_{2m+1}(\sin(\theta))}{\sin(\theta)}.

We set
x=2\cos(a+2k\pi/n)
. Then we conclude from (3.9), (3.10) and (3.2) that

\displaystyle B_{m,n}(a)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\cos(a+2k\pi/n)U_{2m+1}(\sin(a+2k\pi/n))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sin(2(m+1)(a+2k\pi/n)).

If
2(m+1)\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
, then we obtain

B_{m,n}(a)=(-1)^{m}\frac{n}{2}\sin((2(m+1)a).

Let
2(m+1)\not\equiv 0\,(\mbox{mod}\,n)
. Then, we conclude from (3.5) that

B_{m,n}(a)=\frac{(-1)^{m}}{2}\frac{\sin(2(m+1)\pi)}{\sin(2(m+1)\pi/n)}\sin(2(m+1)(a-\pi/n))=0.

This completes the proof of the Theorem.

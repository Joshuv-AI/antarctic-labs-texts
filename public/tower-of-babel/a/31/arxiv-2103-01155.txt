Abstract
. For a set E E of positive and finite length, we prove that if the Huovinen transform (the convolution operator with kernel z k / | z | k + 1 z^{k}/|z|^{k+1} for an odd number k k ) associated to E E exists in principal value, then E E is rectifiable.



1.
Introduction

We say that a subset
E\subset\mathbb{C}
is rectifiable if there exists Lipschitz maps
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
,
i=1,2,...
, such that

\mathcal{H}^{1}\Bigl(E\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}(\mathbb{R})\Bigl)=0,

where
\mathcal{H}^{1}
stands for the
1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure. A locally finite Borel measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{C}
is rectifiable if there exists a rectifiable set
E\subset\mathbb{C}
such that

\mu(\mathbb{C}\setminus E)=0.

The goal of this paper is to prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Fix an odd number
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Suppose that
\mu
is a finite Borel measure for which

(1.1)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r}\in(0,\infty)\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C}.

If the limit

(1.2)

\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{|z-\omega|>r}\frac{(z-\omega)^{k}}{|z-\omega|^{k+1}}d\mu(\omega)\text{ exists for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C},

then
\mu
is rectifiable.

If
k=1
then Theorem
1.1
was proved by Tolsa
[
To3
]
using the Menger-Melnikov curvature method
1
1

1

building upon a number of important results including
[
Me
,
MMV
,
Dav3
,
DM
,
MM
,
NTV2
]
; in this case the principal value integral is the Cauchy transform of the measure
\mu
. The curvature method is no longer directly applicable to this problem for
k\geq 3
, and it had been an open problem as to whether Theorem
1.1
holds in this case (see for instance
[
To5
]
).

If one replaces the limsup condition in (
1.1
) with the condition of positive lower density

(1.3)

\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r}>0\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C},

then the case
k=1
of Theorem
1.1
was proved earlier by Mattila
[
M
]
, and subsequently for all
k
odd by Huovinen
[
H
]
. It is for this reason that we call the integral transform given by convolution of a measure with the singular kernel
z\mapsto\frac{z^{k}}{|z|^{k+1}}

the Huovinen transform
.

Under the assumption (
1.3
), much stronger criteria for rectifiability are available in terms of tangent measures that no longer hold under the condition (
1.1
), see
[
P
, Section 5.8]
. Nevertheless, the tools introduced by Mattila and Huovinen are essential to our method
2
2

2

More precisely, these techniques play an important role in Theorem
A
below.
.

A natural higher dimensional generalization of Mattila’s result for
k=1
was proved by Mattila-Preiss
[
MP
]
, who showed that if
d\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[2,\infty)
,
s\in\mathbb{Z}\cap[1,d-1]
and
0<\liminf_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(x,r))}{r^{s}}<\infty
for
\mu
-a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the existence of the
s
-Riesz transform in principal value implies
s
-rectifiability
3
3

3

We say that a Borel measure
\mu
is
s
-rectifiable if there exist Lipschitz maps
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{s}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
i=0,1,...
, such that

\mu(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i=0}^{\infty}f_{i}(\mathbb{R}^{s}))=0
.
. Here the Riesz transform is the convolution of a measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the kernel
\frac{x}{|x|^{s+1}}
where
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
. The positive lower density assumption was later removed by Tolsa
[
To4
]
, who introduced a very novel variation of a scheme of Legér
[
L
]
(which in turn has its origins in the work of David-Semmes
[
DS
]
).

Villa
[
Vil
]
recently extended the results of
[
M
]
to perturbations of the Cauchy kernel, and it would be interesting to understand whether those results remain valid without the assumption of positive lower density. The Huovinen kernel does not fall within this perturbative theory, but our analysis does not appear to apply to perturbations in the generality that they are considered in
[
Vil
]
.

Broadly speaking, we proceed by adapting the scheme implemented by in Tolsa
[
To4
]
, but doing so required overcoming a basic difficulty. A measure
\mu
for which the Cauchy (or Riesz) transform exists in principal value enjoy some ‘local flattening’ properties
4
4

4

by this we mean that on a small scale either there is very little measure, or the support of the measure is close to a line/plane of appropriate dimension
on account of the fact that the only
symmetric measures
associated to these kernels with suitable growth are (the Hausdorff measures of) planes. However, there are symmetric measures associated to the Huovinen kernel which are not flat – the
spike measures
– which will appear often in our analysis. It appears that all variants of the Legér scheme (for instance in
[
L
,
CMPT
,
To5
,
JTV
]
) have relied on this local flattening property in one way or another. In this paper we circumvent these difficulties with a novel decomposition of a measure involving a modified density, relying significantly on our previous papers
[
JM1
,
JM2
]
, which we recall in the next sections.

1.1.
A first necessary condition for existence of principal value: Small local action and transportation coefficients

In the paper
[
JM1
]
we studied the geometric consequences of a weaker notion than existence of principal value called
small local action
.

For a homogeneous Calderón-Zygmund operator, one can characterize the small local action property geometrically in terms of the transportation distance to the class of symmetric measures associated to the kernel (Theorem 1.1 of
[
JM1
]
, building upon work of Mattila
[
M
,
M1
]
). We do not define these terms here, but rather state what it means for the Huovinen transform.

Definition 1.2
.

A
k
-spike measure associated to a line
D\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
(i.e. going through
0
) and the vertex
z\in\mathbb{C}
is a measure of the form, for some
c>0
,

\nu_{m,D,z}=c\sum_{n=0}^{m-1}\mathcal{H}_{e^{\pi in/m}D+z},

where
m
divides
k
(henceforth
m\mid k
). We set
\mathcal{S}_{k}
to be the collection of all such spike measures over
D\in\mathcal{G}_{0}
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
.

Fix the Lipschitz continuous function
\varphi
that satisfies
\varphi\equiv 1
on
[0,3)
,
\|\varphi\|_{Lip}=1
, and
\operatorname{supp}(\varphi)\subset[0,4)
.

Definition 1.3
.

Given a locally finite Borel measure
\mu
,
z\in\mathbb{C}
, and
r>0
, we define the transportation distance as

\alpha^{(k)}_{\mu}(B(z,r))=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{k}:\\
z\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\end{subarray}}\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(B(z,r)),

where, for a Borel measure
\nu
,

\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(B(z,r))=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in\lip_{0}(B(z,4r))\\
\|f\|_{\lip}\leq\frac{1}{r}\end{subarray}}\Bigl|\frac{1}{r}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\Bigl(\frac{|\cdot-z|}{r}\Bigl)f\;\,d(\mu-c_{\mu,\nu}\nu)\Bigl|,

and with the normalizing constant
c_{\mu,\nu}

c_{\mu,\nu}=\begin{cases}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\bigl(\frac{|\cdot-z|}{r}\bigl)d\mu\Bigl[\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\bigl(\frac{|\cdot-z|}{r}\bigl)d\nu\Bigl]^{-1}\text{ if }\int_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi\bigl(\frac{|\cdot-z|}{r}\bigl)d\nu\neq 0\\
0\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

The following result is an immediate consequence of Proposition A.1 and Theorem 1.5 of
[
JM1
]
, making essential use of the aforementioned work of Mattila
[
M
]
and Huovinen
[
H
]
.

Theorem A
.

[
JM1
]
Suppose that
\mu
is a finite Borel measure satisfying (
1.1
) and (
1.2
), then

(1.4)

\lim_{r\to 0}\alpha^{(k)}_{\mu}(B(z,r))=0\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C}.

This result provides valuable geometric information without which we would not be able to prove Theorem
1.1
, but the condition (
1.4
) alone does not imply that
\mu
is rectifiable, even if
k=1
– see for instance the examples in Section 5.8 of
[
P
]
.

1.2.
A second necessary condition for the existence of principal value: Operator boundedness

We set

K_{k}(z)=\frac{z^{k}}{|z|^{k+1}},\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}.

For a non-atomic Borel measure
\mu
, we say the Huovinen transform associated to
\mu
is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
if there exists
C>0
such that

(1.5)

\sup_{\varkappa>0}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{C}\backslash B(z,\varkappa)}K_{k}(z-\omega)f(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\Bigl|^{2}d\mu(z)\leq C\|f\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu)}

for every
f\in L^{2}(\mu)
.

A well-known consequence (see, for instance
[
Dav2
]
, page 56) of the
L^{2}
-boundedness condition (
1.5
) is that
\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C},r>0}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r}<\infty
.

A simple special case of much more general results of Nazarov-Treil-Volberg
[
NTV2
]
and Tolsa
[
To3
]
is the following theorem, valid for a wide class of Calderón-Zygmund operators.

Theorem B
.

[
NTV2
]
Suppose that
\mu
is a finite Borel measure satisfying (
1.2
) and

(1.6)

\limsup_{r\to 0}\frac{\mu(B(z,r))}{r}<\infty\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C}.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a set
E_{\varepsilon}
and a constant
C=C(\varepsilon)
such that
\mu(\mathbb{C}\backslash E_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon
and the measure
\mu|_{E_{\varepsilon}}
satisfies the
L^{2}
-boundedness condition (
1.5
).

The following Corollary is immediate from Theorem
B
.

Corollary 1.4
.

Suppose that
\mu
is a finite Borel measure satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.6
). There is a decomposition
\operatorname{supp}(\mu)=F\cup\bigcup_{j=1}^{\infty}E_{j}
, where
\mu(F)=0
and (
1.5
) holds with
\mu
replaced by
\mu|_{E_{j}}
for a constant
C=C(j)
.

We conclude that the notion of principal value is (although qualitative) stronger than the
L^{2}
-boundedness of the operator. It is actually
significantly stronger
: In
[
JN
]
an example was constructed of a (purely unrectifiable) measure for which the Huovinen transform is bounded in
L^{2}
, but fails to exist in principal value
6
6

6

This is another instance in which the Huovinen and Cauchy transforms behave very differently, since if
\mu
is a non-atomic measure for which the Cauchy transform is bounded in
L^{2}
, then the Cauchy transform exists in principal value (the same result here also holds for the
(d-1)
-Riesz transform in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, as can be seen by stringing together the results of
[
ENV
,
NToV
,
NToV2
,
MV
]
).
. Higher dimensional analogues of this example featuring kernels of spherical harmonics have recently been developed by Mateu and Prat
[
MaPr
]
. It would be very challenging to extend Theorem
1.1
to this higher dimensional setting – with the primary issue being to understand the structure of the set of symmetric measures associated to these higher dimensional kernels. Other examples of kernels for which
L^{2}
-boundedness does not imply existence of principal value can be found in
[
CH
,
Dav
]
.

We have thus far recorded two necessary conditions for the existence of principal value in Theorems
A
and
B
. Taken individually, neither condition needs to imply the existence of principal value, but by building on prior work of Mattila-Verdera
[
MV
]
, we showed in
[
JM2
]
that when combined, these two necessary conditions are indeed sufficient:

Theorem C
.

[
JM2
, Theorem 1.5]
Suppose that
\mu
is a finite non-atomic Borel measure satisfying the transportation coefficient condition (
1.4
), and the
L^{2}
-boundedness condition (
1.5
) holds. Then the principal value limit (
1.2
) exists.

1.3.
A revised statement

We conclude with a revised statement, which is essentially equivalent to Theorem
1.1
, and which will be our focus:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\mu
be a finite non-atomic Borel measure in the complex plane and whose support satisfies
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\operatorname{supp}(\mu))<\infty.

Suppose that the Huovinen transform is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
and

\lim_{r\to 0}\alpha^{(k)}_{\mu}(B(z,r))=0\text{ for }\mu\text{-a.e. }z\in\mathbb{C}.

Then
\mu
is rectifiable.

This result is only new if
k\geq 3
(for
k=1
it is a consequence of
[
To3
]
), but we will prove the statement for all odd
k
(although many of the statements of lemmas are automatically satisfied in the case
k=1
). In the case
k=1
, imposing the condition
\lim_{r\to 0}\alpha^{(k)}_{\mu}(B(z,r))=0
for
\mu
-a.e.
z\in\mathbb{C}
is unnecessary – the result still holds if one removes this statement, which is a theorem due to David
[
Dav3
]
, see also David-Mattila
[
DM
]
.
However, for
k\geq 3
the conclusion of rectifiability may fail without the additional assumption on the transportation numbers
(cf.
[
JN
]
).

1.4.
An overview of the proof

As we have already mentioned, the proof of Theorem
1.5
follows a similar scheme to the one in Tolsa
[
To4
]
:
We decompose our measure into different pieces, where an adapted version of the David-Léger-Semmes scheme
[
L
,
DS
]
may be applied construct a Lipschitz graph that approximates our measure. Finally, we revise Tolsa’s scheme in order to prove that the Lipschitz graph actually covers a good portion of our measure.

In order to carry both Léger’s and Tolsa’s schemes in a given scale, one needs, besides of course the analytic properties of the singular integral operator, two specific features from the measure: flatness with respect to lines and nearly maximal density. A priori, we are only equipped with spike flatness, i.e. our measure is in concentration close to either a line or to a spike. However, spikes allow big oscillations in density, making harder the search for suitable scales with nearly maximal density.

These issues are mainly bypassed with the decomposition of the measure (see Section
6
) and the development of a modified density (see Sections
4
and
5
). This new density moves us away from the center of the spikes (therefore it finds for us scales with regular flatness) and helps us to classify the spikes by the density in their rays.

In Sections
7
and
8
we carry out a variant of the Léger construction of an approximating Lipschitz curve, where the transportation coefficients play a central role.

The necessary geometric toolbox for Sections
4
–
8
is developed in Section
3
.

Sections
9
and
10
closely follow Tolsa
[
To4
]
, and mainly concern the Calderón-Zygmund theory required to show that the approximate Lipschitz curve (constructed in Section
7
) does not rotate too much.

2.3.
Basic Operator Notation

For a kernel function
K:\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}\backslash\{(z,\omega):z=\omega\}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
|K(z,\omega)(z-\omega)|
extends to a bounded function on
\mathbb{C}\times\mathbb{C}
, we set

P.V.\int_{\mathbb{C}}K(z,\omega)f(\omega)d\mu(\omega)=\lim_{r\to 0}\int_{|z-\omega|>r}K(z,\omega)f(\omega)d\mu(\omega)

provided that the right hand side exists. We say that
K
forms a principal value operator on
L^{p}(\mu)
(
1<p<\infty
) if there is a constant
C>0

(2.1)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Bigl|P.V.\int_{\mathbb{C}}K(z,\omega)f(\omega)d\mu(\omega)\Bigl|^{p}d\mu(z)\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p}(\mu)}^{p}

for all
f\in L^{p}(\mu)
. We call the least constant
C
such that (
2.1
) holds as the
principal value operator norm
.

It will prove very useful to define operators with a smoother cut-off. Define a function
\Psi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that
\Psi
is non-decreasing,
\Psi(t)\equiv 0
on
[0,1/2]
and
\Psi(t)=1
for
t\geq 1
, and
\|\Psi^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\lesssim 1
. Put, for
r>0
and any measure
\nu

\widehat{T}_{r}\nu(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|z-\omega|}{r}\Bigl)K_{k}(z-\omega)d\nu(\omega),

\widehat{T}^{\perp}_{r}\nu(x)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\Psi\Bigl(\frac{|z-\omega|}{r}\Bigl)K^{\perp}_{k}(z-\omega)d\nu(\omega),

where
K^{\perp}_{k}(z)=\displaystyle\frac{\Im(z^{k})}{|z|^{k+1}}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,

\widehat{T}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\nu(x)=\begin{cases}\widehat{T}_{r_{1}}\nu(x)-\widehat{T}_{r_{2}}\nu(x)\text{ if }r_{1}<r_{2}\\
0\text{ if }r_{2}\geq r_{1},\end{cases}

and

T^{\perp}_{r_{1},r_{2}}\nu(x)=\begin{cases}\widehat{T}^{\perp}_{r_{1}}\nu(x)-\widehat{T}^{\perp}_{r_{2}}\nu(x)\text{ if }r_{1}<r_{2}\\
0\text{ if }r_{2}\geq r_{1}.\end{cases}

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\mu
is a locally finite Borel measure.

(1)

If the principal value limit (
1.2
) exists at a given point
z\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\lim_{r\to 0}\widehat{T}_{r}(\mu)(z)
exists and is equal to the same limit.

(2)

If the Huovinen transform is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
(in the sense that (
1.5
) holds), then there is a constant
C
such that

(2.2)

\|\sup_{r>0}|\widehat{T}_{r}(f\mu)|\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mu)}^{2}\text{ for every }f\in L^{2}(\mu)

The proof of (1) is by direct calculation, while (2) is standard Calderón-Zygmund theory: one estimates the difference between the smooth and rough cut-off by a suitable maximal function, and applies a Cotlar type lemma to bound the maximal singular integral (see
[
To5
]
, Chapter 2).

In the event that there is a constant
C
such that (
2.2
) holds, we denote the least such constant by
\|\widehat{T}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu),L^{2}(\mu)}
.

We warn the reader here that, even if the associated principal value operator exists and is bounded in
L^{2}(\mu)
, then
\|\widehat{T}_{\mu}\|_{L^{2}(\mu),L^{2}(\mu)}
need not be comparable with the principal value operator norm.

3.
Transportation coefficients tool box

Now we proceed to record a series of estimates regarding the transportation coefficients that will be used throughout the paper.

Throughout this section,
\nu
will denote a locally finite Borel measure.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma>0
and suppose
s\in(0,r)
,
B(z,s)\subset B(x,3r)
,
\operatorname{dist}(z,\operatorname{supp}(\nu))\geq 2s
, and

\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(B(x,r))\leq\gamma\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)).

Then

\delta_{\mu}(B(z,s))\leq\gamma\Bigl(\frac{r}{s}\Bigl)^{2}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)).

Proof.

Choose
f\equiv 1
on
B(z,s)
with
\operatorname{supp}(f)\subset B(z,2s)
and
\|f\|_{\operatorname{Lip}}\leq\frac{1}{s}
. Then
\frac{s}{r}f\in\mathcal{F}_{x,r}
. Since
\alpha_{\mu,\nu}(B(x,r))\leq\gamma\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r))
, but
\operatorname{supp}(f)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\nu)=\varnothing
,

\frac{1}{r}\cdot\frac{s}{r}\cdot\mu(B(z,s))\leq\gamma\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)),

4.
Density ratio

For a non-zero measure we set

(4.1)

D_{\nu}=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}r,s>0\\
x,z\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\end{subarray}}\frac{\delta_{\nu}(B(x,r))}{\delta_{\nu}(B(z,s))}.

Observe that

•

for any non-zero measure

,

,

•

if
\nu
is a line measure, then

, and

•

if

, then

.

Lemma 4.1
.

Given a measure
\nu
and
x\in\mathbb{C}
,

\delta_{\nu}(B(x,r))\leq 3D_{\nu}\cdot\delta_{\nu}(B(z,s))\text{ for every }r,s>0\text{ and }z\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu).

Moreover, if
\nu
is a line measure, then

\delta_{\nu}(B(x,r))\leq\delta_{\nu}(B(z,s))\text{ for every }r,s>0\text{ and }z\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu).

Proof.

If
\nu(B(x,r))=0
then there is nothing to prove. Otherwise
r>\operatorname{dist}(x,\operatorname{supp}(\nu))
, and fix
x_{\nu}\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)
to be the closest point to

. The first statement follows from noticing that
B(x,r)\subset B(x_{\nu},3r)
. For the second statement, merely observe that if
\nu
is a line measure, then
B(x,r)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\subset B(x_{\nu},r)\cap\operatorname{supp}(\nu)
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\gamma>0
and suppose

,

Now, without loss of generality we set

,

0\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)

Bringing (

Lemma 4.3

5.
Navigating through spikes: a modified density

We introduce a density that enables one to find a flat piece of a measure
\mu
given that
\mu
is close to a spike in transportation distance.

For
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{k}\setminus\{\nu\not\equiv 0\}
, set

\lambda_{\nu}=\!\!\!\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu)\\
r>0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{r}\sup\left\{t\in(0,r):\!\!\!\begin{array}[]{l}\text{ there are }B(z,t)\subset B(x,r),\,z\in\operatorname{supp}(\nu),\\
\text{ a line }D\in\mathcal{G}_{z},\text{ and }c>0,\text{ such that}\\
\;\nu_{|B(z,4\cdot 30t)}=c\mathcal{H}^{1}_{|D\cap(B(z,4\cdot 30t))}\end{array}\!\!\right\}.

and

\lambda_{k}=\inf_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\nu\not\equiv 0}\lambda_{\nu}.

We will often use the simple observation that
\lambda_{k}\gtrsim 1
.

Now recall the density ratio (
4.1
). We define

D_{k}=\sup_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{k},\,\nu\not\equiv 0}D_{\nu}.

Observe that
1\leq D_{k}\leq k\lesssim 1
.

Fix
\varepsilon\ll 1
. For
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
, set

\displaystyle S_{x,r}(\varepsilon)=\left\{B:\!\!\begin{array}[]{l}B\text{ a ball},B\subset B(x,r),\delta_{\mu}(B)\geq\frac{1}{2D_{k}}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)),\\
r(B)\geq\frac{\lambda_{k}}{2}r,\text{ and }\alpha_{\mu}(30B)\leq\varepsilon\delta_{\mu}(B)\end{array}\right\}.

We then define the modified density

\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu,\varepsilon}(B(x,r))=\begin{cases}\;\inf_{B\in S_{x,r}(\varepsilon)}\delta_{\mu}(B)\text{ if }S_{x,r}(\varepsilon)\neq\varnothing\\
\;0\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

We will usually just drop the subscript
\varepsilon
, and write
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu}(B(x,r))
instead of
\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu,\varepsilon}(B(x,r))
. Observe that we have, for any ball
B(x,r)
and
B\in S_{x,r}(\varepsilon)
,

(5.1)

\delta_{\mu}(B)\leq\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)),\text{ and so }\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu}(B(x,r))\leq\frac{2}{\lambda_{k}}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)).

.

Let
x\in\mathbb{C}
and
r>0
be such that

\alpha^{(k)}_{\mu}(B(x,30r))\ll\varepsilon\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r)).

Then we have that

\frac{1}{C_{k}}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r))\leq\widetilde{\delta}_{\mu}(B(x,r))\leq C_{k}\delta_{\mu}(B(x,r))

Without loss of generality, we assume that
x=0
,

and

First, we note that it suffices to verify that

Now we proceed to prove that

4.3

10.
Calderón-Zygmund operators on Lipschitz graphs with small constant

Like in Tolsa’s work
[
To4
]
, the behavior of Calderón-Zygmund operators on Lipschitz graphs with small Lipschitz constant plays an important role in our work. Here we carry out a suitable adaptation to the Huovinen kernels. The main point is that, on a Lipschitz graph with small constant, the normal component of the Huovinen kernel behaves like a small perturbation of the normal component of the Cauchy kernel.

Recall that

K_{k}^{\perp}(z)=\frac{\Im(z^{k})}{|z|^{k+1}}\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}.

Throughout this section, we will denote by
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
a compactly supported Lipschitz continuous function with
\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq 1
. We set
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)=(t,\mathcal{A}(t))(=t+i\mathcal{A}(t)\in\mathbb{C})
, and
\Gamma=\{\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t):t\in\mathbb{R}\}
.

The goal of the section is to derive the following result:

Theorem 10.1
.

There exists constants
C,c>0
and
\alpha_{0}>0
depending on
k
such that if
\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{0}
, and
\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{A}))\lesssim 1
, then

(1)

for every
p\in(1,\infty)
, the principal value operator associated
K_{k}^{\perp}
has operator norm at most
C_{p}\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
, and

(2)

we have the lower bound

(10.1)

\int_{\Gamma}\Bigl|P.V.\int_{\Gamma}K_{k}^{\perp}(z-\omega)d\mathcal{H}^{1}(\omega)\Bigl|^{2}d\mathcal{H}^{1}(z)\geq c\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}-C\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{4}.

For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we shall set

J(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}})(t)=\sqrt{1+\mathcal{A}^{\prime}(t)^{2}},

so that for any
f\in L^{1}(\Gamma)
,

(10.2)

\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(\omega)d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{|\Gamma}(\omega)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t))J(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}})(t)\,dm_{1}(t),

Using (
10.2
), we shall prove bounds of the the operator norm in
L^{2}(\Gamma)
of the Calderón-Zygmund operator

T^{\perp}(f\mathcal{H}^{1}_{|\Gamma})(z)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{C}}K_{k}^{\perp}(z-\omega)f(\omega)d\mathcal{H}^{1}_{|\Gamma}(\omega)

by first considering the principal value operator norm in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
of the operator

T_{A}(g)(t)=P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{k}^{\perp}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(s))g(s)\,dm_{1}(s),\;\;t\in\mathbb{R}.

The following theorem is a well known result regarding Calderón commutators, see
[
Dav2
, Chapter 2]
for an exposition including several approaches to how it can be proved.

Theorem 10.2

(Boundedness of Calderón commutators)
.

There exists
C_{1}>0
such that for every
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the CZO acting on
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
with kernel

K(t,s)=\frac{1}{t-s}\Bigl(\frac{\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s)}{t-s}\Bigl)^{\ell}

is a bounded principal value operator in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
with norm
\lesssim_{p}C_{1}^{\ell}\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|^{\ell}
.

We next recall an important tool in our argument, which is a special case of
[
To4
, Theorem 1.3]
, relying ultimately on a Fourier analytic argument.

Theorem 10.3
.

There exists
\alpha_{0}>0
such that if
\|A^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{0}
, then

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s)}{(t-s)^{2}}\,dm_{1}(s)\Bigl|^{2}\,dm_{1}(t)\gtrsim\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

We now examine the difference between normal components of the Huovinen and Cauchy transforms. For
|s|<|t|
, we may expand the kernel

(10.3)

\begin{split}K_{k}^{\perp}(t+is)&=\frac{\Im[(t+is)^{k}]}{(t^{2}+s^{2})^{(k+1)/2}}=\sum_{\ell\in\mathbb{N},\ell\text{ odd }}c_{k,\ell}\frac{s^{\ell}}{t^{\ell+1}},\end{split}

where
c_{k,\ell}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

(10.4)

c_{k,1}=k\text{ and }\sum_{\ell}|c_{k,\ell}|\Bigl(\frac{1}{2}\Bigl)^{\ell}\lesssim_{k}1.

Consequently, we see that

(10.5)

K_{k}^{\perp}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(s))=k\frac{\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s)}{(t-s)^{2}}+\sum_{\ell\geq 3,\ell\text{ odd}}c_{k,\ell}\frac{(\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s))^{\ell}}{(t-s)^{\ell+1}}.

Now, if
\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leq\alpha_{0}
for a small enough
\alpha_{0}
, the kernel

K_{\text{tail}}(t,s)=\sum_{\ell\geq 3,\ell\text{ odd}}c_{k,\ell}\frac{(\mathcal{A}(t)-\mathcal{A}(s))^{\ell}}{(t-s)^{\ell+1}}

is a Calderón-Zygmund kernel, and Theorem
10.2
ensures that, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, the associated principal value operator is bounded in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
with norm
\lesssim_{p,k}\|A^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{3}
. Therefore

(a)

the principal value operator with kernel
K_{k}^{\perp}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(t)-\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}(s))
has
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
operator norm
\lesssim_{k,p}\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
,

(b)

employing a simple localization argument yields that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Bigl|\int_{\mathbb{R}}K_{\text{tail}}(t,s)\,dm_{1}(s)\Bigl|^{2}\,dm_{1}(t)\lesssim\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}^{6}\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{A})),

(c)

if
\|\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
is small enough and
\operatorname{diam}(\operatorname{supp}(\mathcal{A}))\lesssim 1
, then part (b) and Theorem
10.3
ensures that there are constants
C,c
depending on
k
such that

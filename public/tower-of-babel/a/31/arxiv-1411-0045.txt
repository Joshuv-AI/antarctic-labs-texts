Computation of Standardized Residuals for MARSS Models
E. E. Holmes

Abstract
This report shows how to compute the variance of the joint conditional model and state residuals for multivariate autoregressive Gaussian state-space (MARSS) models. The bulk of the report focuses on ‘smoothations’, which are the residuals conditioned on all the data t = 1 t=1 to T T . The final part of the report covers ‘innovations’, which are residuals conditioned on the data t = 1 t=1 to t − 1 t-1 . The MARSS model can be written: 𝒙 t = 𝐁 𝒙 t − 1 + 𝐮 + 𝒘 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}_{t}=\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}_{t-1}+\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}+\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}_{t} , 𝒚 t = 𝐙 𝒙 t + 𝐳 + 𝒗 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}_{t}=\mbox{$\mathbf{Z}$}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}_{t}+\mbox{$\mathbf{z}$}+\mbox{$\boldsymbol{v}$}_{t} , where 𝒘 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}_{t} and 𝒗 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{v}$}_{t} are independent multivariate Gaussian error-terms with variance-covariance matrices 𝐐 t \mbox{$\mathbf{Q}$}_{t} and 𝐑 t \mbox{$\mathbf{R}$}_{t} respectively. The joint conditional residuals are the 𝒘 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}_{t} and 𝒗 t \mbox{$\boldsymbol{v}$}_{t} conditioned on the observed data, which may be incomplete (missing values). Harvey, Koopman and Penzer (1998) show a recursive algorithm for the smoothation residuals (conditioned on all the data). I show an alternate algorithm to compute these residuals using the conditional variances of the states and the conditional covariance between unobserved data and states. This allows one to compute the variance of un-observed residuals (residuals associated with missing or left-out data), which is needed for leave-one-out cross-validation tests. I show how to modify the Harvey et al. algorithm in the case of missing values and how to modify it to return the non-normalized conditional residuals.



Keywords: Time-series analysis, Kalman filter, residuals, maximum-likelihood, vector autoregressive model, dynamic linear model, parameter estimation, state-space

citation: Holmes, E. E. 2014. Computation of standardized residuals for (MARSS) models. Technical Report. arXiv:1411.0045

(23)

Let’s solve the inner part for a specific

\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]

so the middle term reduces to 0.
\mbox{$\mathbf{a}$}_{t}
is also fixed and its variance is also 0. Thus for a specific

\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}

\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathbf{U}$}}_{t}^{T}
for all
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}

\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathbf{U}$}}_{t}^{T}

\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]=\widetilde{\mbox{$\mathbf{U}$}}_{t}^{T}.

(24)

2.2.3
Putting together the first and second terms

We can now put the first and second terms in Equation

(25)

\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}
and
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
and by extension

)

2.3
State residuals conditioned on the data

The state residuals are
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}_{t}-(\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}_{t-1}+\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t})=\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}_{t}
. The unconditional expected value of the state residuals is
\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-(\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}+\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t})]=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=0
and the unconditional variance of the state residuals is

\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-(\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}+\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t})]=\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=\mbox{$\mathbf{Q}$}_{t}

(27)

based on the definition of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
in Equation
1
.
The conditional state residuals (conditioned on the data from
t=1
to
t=T
) are defined as

\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}}_{t}=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}.

(28)

where
\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}=E[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
and
\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}=E[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
.
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}}_{t}
is a sample from the random variable
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}
; random over different possible data sets. The expected value of
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}
is 0, and we are concerned with computing its variance.

We can write the variance of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
(no hat) using the law of total variance.

\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=\,\textup{{var}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]+\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(29)

Notice that

\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{E}}[(\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t})|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}=\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}}_{t}.

(30)

This is true for all
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
, thus
\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]
is
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}
, and
\,\textup{{var}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]=\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]
. Equation
29
can thus be written

\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]+\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(31)

Solve for
\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]
:

\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]-\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(32)

The variance in the expectation on the far right for a specific
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}=\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
is

\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{var}}[(\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t})|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}].

(33)

\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}
is not a random variable and can be dropped. Thus
8
8

8

\,\textup{{var}}[A-B]=\,\textup{{var}}[A]+\,\textup{{var}}[B]+\,\textup{{cov}}[A,-B]+\,\textup{{cov}}[-B,A]
. Be careful about the signs in this case as they are a little non-intuitive.
,

\begin{split}\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}&|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{var}}[(\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1})|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]\\
&=\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]+\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]+\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t},-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]+\,\textup{{cov}}[-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]\\
&=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}^{T}+\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}-\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t,t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1,t}^{T}.\end{split}

(34)

Again this is conditional multivariate Normal variance, and its value does not depend on the value,
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
that we are conditioning on. It depends only on the parameters values,
\mathbf{Q}
,
\mathbf{B}
,
\mathbf{R}
, etc., and is the same for all values of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
. So
\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]=\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
, using any value of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
. Thus

\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}^{T}+\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}-\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t,t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1,t}^{T}.

(35)

Putting
\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]
from Equation

and
\,\textup{{var}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=\mbox{$\mathbf{Q}$}_{t}
into Equation

, the variance of the conditional state residuals is

\,\textup{{var}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=\mbox{$\mathbf{Q}$}_{t}-\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}+\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t,t-1}^{T}\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}^{\top}+\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t-1,t}^{T}.

(36)

2.4
Covariance of the conditional model and state residuals

The unconditional model and state residuals,
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}
and
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
, are independent by definition
9
9

9

This independence is specific to the way the MARSS model for this report (Equation
1
). It is possible for the model and state residuals to covary. In the MARSS model written in
Harvey et al., 1998
form, they do covary.
(in Equation
1
), i.e.,
\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=0
. However the conditional model and state residuals,
\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]
, are not independent since both depend on
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
.
Using the law of total covariance, we can write

\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=\,\textup{{cov}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}],\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]+\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(37)

For a specific value of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}=\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
, the covariance in the second term on the right is
\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
. Conditioned on a specific value of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}
,
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}
is a fixed value,
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}}_{t}=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}
, and conditioned on
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
,
\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}
and
\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}
are fixed values.
\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}
is also fixed; it is a parameter.
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}
is not a fixed value because it has
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}^{(2)}
and that is a random variable. Thus
\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
is the covariance between a random variable and a fixed variable and thus the covariance is 0. This is true for all
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
.
Thus the second right-side term in Equation
37
is zero, and the equation reduces to

\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=\,\textup{{cov}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}],\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(38)

Notice that
\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
and
\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]
since

\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}=\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t-1}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}=\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{w}$}}_{t}=\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]

(39)

and

\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]-\mbox{$\mathbf{Z}$}_{t}\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]-\mbox{$\mathbf{a}$}_{t}=\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]-\mbox{$\mathbf{Z}$}_{t}\widetilde{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{x}$}}_{t}^{T}-\mbox{$\mathbf{a}$}_{t}=\,\textup{{E}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}].

(40)

Thus the right side of Equation
38
can be written in terms of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}
and
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
instead of
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t}
and
\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}
:

\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=\,\textup{{cov}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}],\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(41)

Using the law of total covariance, we can write:

\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}]=\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]+\,\textup{{cov}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}],\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(42)

The unconditional covariance of
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}
and
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}
is 0. Thus the left side of Equation

is 0 and we can rearrange the equation as

\,\textup{{cov}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}],\,\textup{{E}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]]=-\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]].

(43)

Combining Equation
41
and
43

\,\textup{{cov}}[\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}}_{t},\widehat{\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}}_{t}]=-\,\textup{{E}}_{Y^{(1)}}[\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}]],

(44)

and our problem reduces to solving for the conditional covariance of the model and state residuals (right side of Equation
44
).

For a specific
\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}^{(1)}=\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}
, the conditional covariance
\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]

\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{Z}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{a}$}_{t},\,\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}-\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}].

(45)

\mbox{$\mathbf{a}$}_{t}
and
\mbox{$\mathbf{u}$}_{t}
are fixed values and can be dropped. Thus
10
10

10

\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}\mathbf{A},\mbox{$\mathbf{C}$}\mathbf{D}]=\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}\,\textup{{cov}}[\mathbf{A},\mathbf{D}]\mbox{$\mathbf{C}$}^{\top}
.

\begin{split}\,\textup{{cov}}&[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{V}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{W}$}_{t}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]=\,\textup{{cov}}[\mbox{$\boldsymbol{Y}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{Z}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t},\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t}-\mbox{$\mathbf{B}$}_{t}\mbox{$\boldsymbol{X}$}_{t-1}|\mbox{$\boldsymbol{y}$}^{(1)}]\\

(46)

2.5
Joint distribution of the conditional residuals

We now can write the variance of the joint distribution of the conditional residuals. Define

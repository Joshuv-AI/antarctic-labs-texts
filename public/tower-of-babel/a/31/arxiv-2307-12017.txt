The algebra of higher homotopy operations
Samik Basu, David Blanc, and Debasis Sen

Abstract
. We explain how the simplicial higher order unstable homotopy operations defined in [ BBS2 ] may be composed and inserted one in another, thus forming a coherent if complicated algebraic structure.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary: 55Q35; secondary: 55Q40, 55Q15, 55S30

Introduction

Secondary and higher order operations have played an important role in homotopy theory
and other areas of mathematics since Massey products and Toda brackets first appeared
in
[
Mas
]
and
[
Tod
]
, respectively. Many generalizations of these two examples
have been proposed over the years (see, e.g.,

[
Sp
,
Mau
,
Wa
,
BMa
,
BBG
,
BJT2
,
BBS1
]
), but we shall
concentrate here on the simplicial version considered in
[
BBS2
]
, which
were shown there to generate all elements in the unstable homotopy groups of spheres
(starting with a small set of indecomposables), in the same way that higher Toda
brackets generate the stable homotopy groups from the Hopf maps (as Joel Cohen showed
in
[
C
]
).

In both cases, the sense in which the higher operations
generate
all homotopy
classes was left somewhat opaque, and our goal here is to clarify this point by
defining an
algebra
of higher homotopy operations, in which they are shown to
interact in certain specified ways, beyond the usual primary composition structure.

We start with the definition of the simplicial higher order
homotopy operations from
[
BBS2
, §1]
, stated in the language of

\infty
-categories, along the lines indicated in
[
BMe
]
.
Even though here we are concerned only with higher homotopy operations in the
strict sense – that is, acting on the homotopy groups of pointed topological
spaces – much of what we do is applicable in wider settings, where we may not
even have a model category structure available.

The interpretation in terms of spectral sequences helps to clarify the relation between
the lower level indeterminacy (in terms of inductive choices of higher homotopies),
and the higher level indeterminacy in terms of the algebraic resolutions or cell
structure (see
[
BJT1
, §3]
and compare
[
BS
, §3]
).

We do not claim these simplicial higher operations are the most general possible – see
[
BMa
,
BJT2
]
for other options. Nevertheless, they appear to be
sufficient for the following purposes:

(a)

If
f:{\mathbf{S}}^{n}\to{\mathbf{X}}
is a value of a higher order operation, we show how addition,
post- or pre-composition with
f
, and insertion of
f
in another operation,
can be expressed as the value of another such operation.

(b)

We explain how (iterated) Whitehead products can be expressed quite explicitly
as values of higher operations (in a variety of ways); this is relatively formal.

(c)

Finally, we use Hilton’s Theorem on the general form of the
primary

homotopy operations to provide a “higher Hilton decomposition” of a general
higher order operation (using the formal description of the Whitehead product
component).

As an application of our methods we study the only known regular sequence of
higher order operations: namely, the higher order Whitehead products.
We were able to obtain a closed formula (using our simplicial approach) only for
the rational version, but we conjecture that it may be valid integrally. Finally,
we indicate how the more general Lie-Massey products may be treated by our methods.

0.1 Notation
.

Let
\Delta
denote the category of non-empty finite ordered sets and order-preserving
maps (cf.
[
May
, §2]
),
\Delta_{\operatorname{res}}
the subcategory with the same
objects but only monic maps, and
\Delta_{\langle{n}\rangle}
the subcategory of
\Delta_{\operatorname{res}}
consisting of
non-empty ordered sets with at most
n+1
elements; we denote the ordered set

0<1<\dotsc<n
with
n+1
elements by
[\mathbf{n}]
. Similarly,
\Delta_{+}
is the
category of
all
finite ordered sets, with the empty set denoted by
[-\mathbf{1}]
.
Write
\Delta_{\operatorname{res},+}
for the corresponding subcategory of monic maps, and
\Delta_{\langle{n+}\rangle}
for
the subcategory of
\Delta_{\operatorname{res},+}
consisting of ordered sets with at most
n+1
elements.

A
simplicial object

V_{\bullet}
in a category
\mathcal{C}
is a functor

\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
and an
augmented simplicial object
is a functor

\Delta_{+}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
, usually written
V_{\bullet}\to X
(where
X
is the value at
[-\mathbf{1}]
).
Similarly, an
n
-truncated restricted
simplicial object is a functor

\Delta_{\langle{n}\rangle}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
, and an
n
-truncated restricted augmented
simplicial object
is a functor
\Delta_{\langle{n+}\rangle}^{\operatorname{op}}\to\mathcal{C}
. There is a natural embedding

c({-})_{\bullet}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
, with
c({A})_{\bullet}
the constant simplicial object, and
for each
n\geq 0
, a truncation functor

\tau_{n}:\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{\operatorname{res},+}^{\operatorname{op}}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{\langle{n+}\rangle}^{\operatorname{op}}}
(and similarly for the unrestricted
or augmented versions).

In each case,
V_{n}
is the value of
V_{\bullet}
at
[\mathbf{n}]

and
d_{i}:V_{n}\to V_{n-1}
for the
i
-th face map (corresponding
to the map
[\mathbf{n}-\mathbf{1}]\to[\mathbf{n}]
which skips
i
in the target). For
V_{\bullet}\to X

an augmented object, we write
\varepsilon:V_{0}\to X
for the augmentation (corresponding
to the unique map
[-\mathbf{1}]\to[\mathbf{0}]
in
\Delta_{+}
).
The inclusion
\Delta\to\Delta_{+}
induces
\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{+}^{\operatorname{op}}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
(forgetting
the augmentation), with right adjoint the décalage

\operatorname{Dec}:\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{+}^{\operatorname{op}}}
(omitting the last face and degeneracy maps
in each dimension
n\geq 0
and decreasing
n
by one). When
\mathcal{C}
is pointed, we have a functor

T:\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{\operatorname{res},+}^{\operatorname{op}}}\to\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{\operatorname{res}}^{\operatorname{op}}}
, right inverse to
\operatorname{Dec}
,
which adds an extra (zero) face map
d_{n+1}:V_{n}\to V_{n-1}

in each dimension
n\geq-1
and increases
n
by one.

The category of topological spaces will be denoted by
Top
,
that of pointed spaces by
\mbox{\sf Top}_{\ast}
, and that of pointed connected
spaces by
\mbox{\sf Top}_{0}
. The category of simplicial sets will be
denoted by
\mathcal{S}=\mbox{\sf Set}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
, that of pointed simplicial
sets by
\mathcal{S}_{\ast}=\mbox{\sf Set}_{\ast}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
, that of reduced simplicial
sets by
\mathcal{S}^{\operatorname{red}}
(see
[

), and that of simplicial groups
by
\mathcal{G}=\mbox{\sf Gp}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
, with

[
GJ

), and
\mathcal{U}:\mathcal{G}\to\mathcal{S}_{\ast}
the forgetful functor (so
\mathcal{U}GK

is a model for the loop space on
K
). We write
\operatorname{map}_{\ast}({\mathbf{X}},{\mathbf{Y}})
for the standard
function complex in
\mathcal{S}_{\ast}
,
\mbox{\sf Top}_{0}
, or
\mathcal{G}
(see
[
GJ

).

For any category

with enough colimits,

is simplicial (that is
(co)tensored over
\mathcal{S}
) in the sense of

[

Models of

-categories.

There are a number of versions of

(a)

1.2 Remark
.

Note that the composition
\mbox{\sf Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(y,z)\times\mbox{\sf Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,y)\to\mbox{\sf Map}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,z)

in
\mathcal{C}
need be neither well-defined nor strictly associative, so a diagram in
\mathcal{C}
,
indexed by
I\in\mbox{\sf Cat}
, say, must come equipped with all higher coherences,
encoded by a functor
F:\mathcal{B}I\to\mathcal{C}
in
\mathcal{M}
. These also figure in
the universal property of the (co)limit of
F
, when it exists.

In this paper we are interested only in an
\infty
-category model
\mathcal{C}
for pointed
topological spaces with all countable (co)limits, which may be identified with the
corresponding homotopical (co)limits in
\mathscr{C}
(in the sense of
[
DHKS
]
).
In particular
\mathcal{C}
will be (co)tensored over finite simplicial sets.

Thus in what follows, the reader may simply take
\mathcal{C}
to be
\mbox{\sf Top}_{\ast}
with the usual
simplicial enrichment. It is important to keep in mind, however, that in this section
diagrams need not commute on the nose, but instead have specified (higher) homotopies
as needed. They can be strictified, of course, but in the course of doing so the maps
and spaces may change up to homotopy.

In this setting, we may formulate
[
BBS2
, Definition 1.12]
as follows:

2.
Simplicial resolutions

Although our formalism works in the generality of Definition
1.3
, in fact we
shall be mostly concerned with simplicial spaces
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
which are resolutions of a
given space
{\mathbf{X}}
(often itself a sphere). For this purpose we require the following
notions:

2.1 Definition
.

In a pointed and complete category
\mathcal{C}
, the
n
-th
Moore chains
object
of a restricted augmented simplicial object
G_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta_{\operatorname{res},+}^{\operatorname{op}}}
is defined to be:

(2.2)

{}C_{n}G_{\bullet}~:=~\cap_{i=1}^{n}\operatorname{Ker}\{d_{i}:G_{n}\to G_{n-1}\}~,

with differential

\partial_{n}:=d_{0}\lvert_{C_{n}G_{\bullet}}:C_{n}G_{\bullet}\to C_{n-1}G_{\bullet}
.
The
n
-th
Moore cycles
object is
Z_{n}G_{\bullet}:=\operatorname{Ker}(\partial_{n})
.
Write
w_{n}:C_{n}G_{\bullet}\hookrightarrow G_{n-1}
and

v_{n}:Z_{n}G_{\bullet}\hookrightarrow C_{n}G_{\bullet}
for the inclusions.

2.3 Definition
.

For a (possibly
(n-1)
-truncated) simplicial object
G_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}
in a
cocomplete category
\mathcal{C}
, the
n
-th
latching object
for
G_{\bullet}

is

(2.4)

{}L_{n}G_{\bullet}~:=~\raisebox{-7.3194pt}{$\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textstyle\operatorname{colim}}}{{\scriptstyle{\theta^{\operatorname{op}}:[\mathbf{k}]\to[\mathbf{n}]}}}$}\,G_{k}~,

where
\theta
ranges over the surjective maps
[\mathbf{n}]\to[\mathbf{k}]
in

\mathbf{\Delta}
(for
k<n
). There is a natural map

\sigma_{n}:L_{n}G_{\bullet}\to G_{n}
induced by the indexing maps
\theta

of the colimit for any
n
-truncated simplicial object, and any iterated
degeneracy map
s_{I}=\theta_{\ast}:G_{k}\to G_{n}

factors as
s_{I}=\sigma_{n}\circ\operatorname{inc}_{\theta}
,
where
\operatorname{inc}_{\theta}:G_{k}\to L_{n}G_{\bullet}
is the structure
map for the copy of
G_{k}
indexed by
\theta
.

2.5 Definition
.

A
CW object
in a pointed category
\mathcal{C}
is a simplicial object
G_{\bullet}\in\mathcal{C}^{\Delta^{\operatorname{op}}}

equipped with a
CW basis

(\overline{G}_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{C}
such that

G_{n}=\overline{G}_{n}\amalg L_{n}G_{\bullet}
, and

(2.6)

{}d_{i}\lvert_{\overline{G}_{n}}~=~0\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for}\hskip 5.69054pt1\leq i\leq n~.

Observe that the restriction
d_{0}\lvert_{\overline{G}_{n}}:\overline{G}_{n}\to G_{n-1}

of the
0
-th face map factors through

,
which we call the
n
-th attaching map
for
G_{\bullet}
.

We then have an explicit description of the
n
-th latching object of
G_{\bullet}
,
given by:

(2.7)

{}L_{n}G_{\bullet}~:=~\coprod_{0\leq k\leq n-1}~~\coprod_{0\leq i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{n-k-1}\leq n-1}~\overline{G}_{k}~,

where the iterated degeneracy map

s_{i_{n-k-1}}\dotsc s_{i_{2}}s_{i_{1}}
, restricted to the
basis
\overline{G}_{k}

\overline{G}_{k}
indexed by

(in the first coproduct) and
(i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n-k-1})

2.8 Remark
.

As shown in
[
BJT1
, Theorem 2.29]
, any algebraic resolution

(in the sense of
[
BJT1
, §1]

has a
realization by a

: that is, an augmented simplicial space

with a CW basis
(\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{n})_{n=0}^{\infty}
such that each

\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{n}\subseteq{\mathbf{W}}_{n}
is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres,
with
\pi_{\ast}{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}=V_{\bullet}
. In this particular case the augmentation

\varepsilon:\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}\|\to{\mathbf{X}}
is a weak equivalence.

2.9
.

A combinatorial description.

The above discussion provides a homotopy-invariant interpretation
of Kan’s “combinatorial” approach to homotopy groups (see
[
K
]
),
in the following sense:

Let
{\mathbf{K}}
be any pointed connected simplicial set, which we may assume to be reduced,
and
G{\mathbf{K}}
the simplicial group obtained by applying Kan’s
G
-functor (a model of

\Omega|{\mathbf{K}}|
– see
[
GJ
, V,§5]
).
Thus
GK_{n}
is a free group, for each

, which can be thought
of as a discrete simplicial group – a model for a wedge of circles indexed by
the pointed set
K_{n}
(thought of as a discrete pointed simplicial set) under
Milnor’s
F
functor (a model of
\Omega\Sigma|{\mathbf{K}}|
– see
[
GJ
, V,§6]
).
Thus applying the classifying space functor (cf.
[
GJ
, V,§4]
)
dimensionwise to

yields a simplicial space
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}:=\overline{W}G{\mathbf{K}}
,
with each

a wedge of circles and
\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}\|\simeq{\mathbf{K}}
.

The Bousfield-Friedlander spectral sequence for
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
has

E^{2}_{{s},{t}}~=~\pi^{h}_{s}\pi^{v}_{t}{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}\cong~\begin{cases}\pi_{s+1}{\mathbf{K}}&\text{if}~t=1\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(see §
1.6
), so it collapses at the

-term. The formalism of

[
BBS2
, §3]
(see also
[
BMe
, Theorem 3.11]
) then allows us to interpret
each element of
\pi_{s+1}{\mathbf{K}}
as the value of an
(s+1)
-st order homotopy
operation associated to the simplicial space
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
.

Of course, this interpretation has no computational advantage; however, it shows that
the combinatorial description of the homotopy groups of a simplicial group
in
[
K
]
has a homotopy-invariant meaning. See
[
BBS2
, (7.9)]
for an example
(due to Kan).

At the other extreme, we note the following

2.10 Proposition
.

Let
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
be a simplicial space in which each
{\mathbf{W}}_{n}
is weakly equivalent to a
wedge of simply-connected rational spheres. Then the Bousfield-Friedlander
spectral sequence for
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
collapses at the

-term.

Proof.

Using the differential graded Lie model for
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
, we see that each

{\mathbf{W}}_{n}
is

(that is, has a cofibrant model with

differential). Thus given an element

represented by a Moore cycle

we may assume by induction on

that a representative
\hat{f}
for

fits into an

-commutative diagram of the form
(

) ending in

into

(that is, the

-differential) must vanish. Thus

is in fact represented in degree

of the cycles by

∎

2.11 Remark
.

Of course, when
{\mathbf{X}}=\|{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}\|
itself is not coformal, the induced map

in (

2.12 Corollary
.

If
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
is a simplicial space in which each

is a wedge of

3.
Combining higher order homotopy operations

Because we are interested in higher order operations in the context of homotopy groups,
we now specialize to the case where
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{S}_{\ast}
and all the diagrams described above
consist of (finite) wedges of spheres – except possibly
{\mathbf{X}}
. In this situation
we consider the higher order constructions described above to be higher
homotopy

operations, in the strict sense of the term.

In
[
BBS2
, Theorem 7.16]
, we showed that all elements in the homotopy groups
of a finite wedge of simply-connected spheres are “generated of higher order” by the
fundamental classes and their Whitehead products: that is, all such elements
can be obtained recursively from these “generators” under the group operation
and composition from values of higher order operations (as defined in §
1.3
).
Our goal here is to make this recursive construction more explicit.

3.1
.

Addition.

When
A
in Definition
1.3
is a coproduct

of two homotopy cogroup objects, diagram (
1.5
) also splits as a coproduct
of the corresponding diagrams for

and

the coherence homotopies restricted to each summand are independent of each other.
In particular, when

is a sphere (or a wedge of spheres) and

6.
Higher order Whitehead products

A well-known infinite sequence of non-trivial higher homotopy operations is
provided by the higher Whitehead products (see
[
Ha
,
AA
]
).
These may actually be defined for any collection of suspensions, as in
[
P1
]
,
and further generalized by the
polyhedral products
of Buchstaber and Panov
(see
[
BP
]
), but for our purposes we shall make do with the following

6.1 Definition
.

Given a finite collection
\mathcal{S}=({\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}})_{i=1}^{m}
of simply-connected
pointed spheres, for each
0\leq k<m
we define the
k
-th order (fat) wedge

of
\mathcal{S}
to be

T_{k}(\mathcal{S}):=\{(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{m})\in\prod_{i=1}^{m}\ {\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}}|\ \exists 1\leq i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{k}\leq m,\ x_{i_{j}}=\ast\hskip 5.69054pt\text{for\ all\hskip 5.69054pt}1\leq j\leq k\}~.

Thus
T_{m-1}(\mathcal{S})
is the wedge

\bigvee_{i=1}^{m}\ {\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}}
,
T_{1}(\mathcal{S})
is the usual fat wedge,
while
T_{0}(\mathcal{S})
is the product
\prod_{i=1}^{m}\ {\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}}
.

We have inclusions
T_{m-1}(\mathcal{S})\subset\dotsc T_{1}(\mathcal{S})\subset T_{0}(\mathcal{S})
,
which provide a cell structure for
T_{0}(\mathcal{S})
:
if we let
N=N_{\mathcal{S}}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ n_{i}
, then
T_{1}(\mathcal{S})
is always
the
(n-2)
-CW skeleton of the product (and
when
n_{1}=\dotsc=n_{m}
, all the
T_{k}(\mathcal{S})
are standard skeleta).
Thus we have attaching maps

\varphi^{k}_{\mathcal{S}}:{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}\to T_{k}(\mathcal{S})
,
with
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}={\mathbf{Z}}_{k}(\mathcal{S})
a wedge of spheres, such that
T_{k-1}(\mathcal{S})

is the homotopy cofiber of
\varphi^{k}_{\mathcal{S}}
. The maps
\varphi^{k}_{\mathcal{S}}

(
1<k<m
) are the
universal higher order Whitehead product maps
.

6.2 Fact
.

For each
m>k\geq 2
, the space
{\mathbf{Z}}_{k}(\mathcal{S})
is a wedge of spheres

{\mathbf{S}}^{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}-1}
, as
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
ranges over all sub-collections of

({\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}})_{i=1}^{m}
with
m-k+1
elements, and
on each such summand the map
\varphi^{k}_{\mathcal{S}}
is just

\varphi^{1}_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}:{\mathbf{S}}^{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}-1}\to T_{1}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

6.3 Example
.

In particular,
{\mathbf{Z}}_{1}={\mathbf{S}}^{N-1}
, and
when
m=2
,
\varphi^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}
is the ordinary Whitehead product
(see
[
Wh
, X, §7]
).

6.4 Definition
.

Given a pointed space
{\mathbf{X}}
and a finite list of homotopy classes

\vec{\alpha}=(\alpha_{i}\in\pi_{n_{i}}{\mathbf{X}})_{i=1}^{m}
, with each

n_{i}\geq 2
, we may represent
\vec{\alpha}
by a map
a:T_{m-1}(\mathcal{S})\to{\mathbf{X}}
,
where
\mathcal{S}=({\mathbf{S}}^{n_{i}})_{i=1}^{m}
.
The
m
-th order Whitehead product
of
\vec{\alpha}
(in the indexing of
[
P3
]
)
is the (possibly empty) collection of homotopy classes of the form

[g\circ\varphi^{1}_{\mathcal{S}}]\in\pi_{N-1}{\mathbf{X}}
, where

N:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\ n_{i}
and
g:T_{1}(\mathcal{S})\to{\mathbf{X}}
runs over all possible
extension of
a
to
T_{1}(\mathcal{S})
.

6.5 Definition
.

Given a fixed ordered set
(p_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
of
n
integers with each

p_{i}\geq 1
, the
Koszul sign

\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma})
of a permutation

\sigma\in S_{n}
is defined recursively by setting
\operatorname{gsn}({\operatorname{Id}})=1
, and
requiring that each adjacent transposition
(i,j)
contributes a factor of

(-1)^{p_{i}p_{j}+1}
(thus combining the graded and ungraded signs).

6.6 Remark
.

Given
(p_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
as in §
6.5
, for any

(k,l)
-shuffle

(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})
with
\sigma^{\prime}=(i_{1}<\dotsc<i_{k})
and

\sigma^{\prime\prime}=(j_{1}<\dotsc<j_{\ell})
, write

\deg(\sigma^{\prime}):=p_{i_{1}}+\dotsc+p_{i_{k}}

and
\deg(\sigma^{\prime\prime}):=p_{j_{1}}+\dotsc+p_{j_{\ell}}
.
We then have:

(6.7)

{}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}})~=~(-1)^{\deg(\sigma^{\prime})\deg(\sigma^{\prime\prime})+k\ell}\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime\prime},\sigma^{\prime}})~.

Now for any two disjoint ordered subsets

\sigma=(\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{k})
and

\tau=(\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{\ell})
of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
,
denote the ordered subset obtained by rearranging

\{\sigma_{1},\dotsc,\sigma_{k},\tau_{1},\dotsc,\tau_{\ell}\}

in ascending order by
\sigma\sqcup\tau
. If (
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
) are
pairwise disjoint sets of
\{1,\dotsc,n\}
, by considering the transpositions
needed to arrange the concatenation of the three in ascending order, we see that

(6.8)

{}\operatorname{gsn}({\alpha,\beta})\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\alpha\sqcup\beta,\gamma})~=~\operatorname{gsn}({\alpha,\beta,\gamma})~=~\operatorname{gsn}({\beta,\gamma})\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\alpha,\beta\sqcup\gamma})~.

Thus if
(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})
is a
(k,l)
-shuffle and

(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})
is a further partition of
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
into two disjoint sets
(so
\sigma^{\prime\prime}=\tau^{\prime}\sqcup\tau^{\prime\prime}
), then

(6.9)

{}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime}\sqcup\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}})~=~\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime},\tau^{\prime}})\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}})\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime}})~.

6.10 Notation
.

Following
[
BBS2
, §8.11]
, we extend the notation of
§
4.1
as follows:

(i)

If
I
and
J
are disjoint subsets of
\{0,1,2,\dotsc,N\}
, each written in
ascending order let

\psi:I\sqcup J\to\{1,2,\dotsc,n\}
be an order-preserving
isomorphism, with
\operatorname{sgn}({I,J}):=\operatorname{sgn}({\psi[I],\psi[J]})
.

(ii)

More generally, if
I
and
J
are any two finite sets of non-negative integers,
let
I^{\prime}:=I\setminus J
and
J^{\prime}:=J\setminus I
, and set

\operatorname{sgn}({I,J}):=\operatorname{sgn}({I^{\prime},J^{\prime}})
.

6.11
.

Properties of
\operatorname{sgn}({I,J})
.

We recall from
[
BBS2
, §8]
a number of basic properties of the sign
of a collection of subsets of
\{0,1,2,\dotsc,N\}
:

(1)

If
(I^{\prime},J^{\prime})
is obtained from
(I,J)
by switching a
pair of indices between
I
and
J
, then
\operatorname{sgn}({I^{\prime},J^{\prime}})=-\operatorname{sgn}({I,J})
.

(2)

If
I\setminus J
has cardinality
k
and
J\setminus I

has cardinality
\ell
, then

(6.12)

{}\operatorname{sgn}({I,J})~=~(-1)^{k\cdot\ell}\operatorname{sgn}({J,I})

(3)

For subsets
K
and
L
of
\{0,\dotsc,n\}
with
|K|=k
and

|L|=\ell
, let
\widehat{K}
and
\widehat{L}
denote the subsets of
\{1,\dotsc,n+1\}

obtained by adding
1
to each element of
K
(respectively,
L
), and

\widehat{\widehat{K}}:=\widehat{K}\cup\{0\}
. Then

(6.13)

{}\operatorname{sgn}({\widehat{\widehat{K}},\widehat{L}})~=~\operatorname{sgn}({K,L})~,

and so by (
6.12
) and (
6.13
):

(6.14)

{}\begin{split}\operatorname{sgn}({\widehat{L},\widehat{\widehat{K}}})~=&~(-1)^{\ell(k+1)}\operatorname{sgn}({\widehat{\widehat{K}},\widehat{L}})~=~(-1)^{\ell(k+1)}\operatorname{sgn}({K,L})\\
=&~(-1)^{\ell(k+1)}(-1)^{\ell\cdot k}\operatorname{sgn}({L,K})~=~(-1)^{\ell}\operatorname{sgn}({L,K})~.\end{split}

(4)

If
M=(m_{1},\dotsc,m_{k})
is an ordered set of natural numbers (in
ascending order), denote its underlying set by
\underline{M}
, and conversely. In particular,
if
\underline{M}
and
\underline{N}
are disjoint sets of natural numbers,
M\sqcup N=N\sqcup M

will denote the disjoint union
\underline{M}\sqcup\underline{N}
, arranged in ascending order.

For any decomposition
\underline{M}\sqcup\underline{N}\sqcup\underline{P}=\{0,\dotsc,n-1\}
, consider the
corresponding three-fold shuffle
(M,N,P)
. As in (
6.8
), we have

(6.15)

{}\operatorname{sgn}({M,N\sqcup P})\cdot\operatorname{sgn}({N,P})~=~\operatorname{sgn}({M,N,P})~=~\operatorname{sgn}({M\sqcup N,P})\cdot\operatorname{sgn}({M,N})~.

6.16 Definition
.

Assume given an ordered collection
\mathcal{S}=({\mathbf{S}}^{p_{i}+1})_{i=1}^{n}
of
simply-connected spheres, we define a rational simplicial space
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}={\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{S})

by inductively choosing a CW-basis
(\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{m})_{m=0}^{\infty}
, as follows:

For each
(m,\ell)
- shuffle

(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})=(\tau^{\prime}_{1},\dotsc,\tau^{\prime}_{m},\tau^{\prime\prime}_{1},\dotsc,\tau^{\prime\prime}_{\ell})

with
m+\ell=n
(or equivalently, for each subset

(6.17)

{}\mathcal{S}^{\prime}~=~({\mathbf{S}}^{p_{\tau^{\prime}_{1}}+1},\dotsc,{\mathbf{S}}^{p_{\tau^{\prime}_{m}+1}})

of
\mathcal{S}
of cardinality
1\leq m\leq n
), a rational sphere

{\mathbf{S}}^{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}}_{(\tau^{\prime})}
, where
N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}:=\deg(\tau^{\prime})+1
.
We denote its fundamental class by

\iota_{\tau^{\prime}}\in\pi_{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{S}}^{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}}_{(\tau^{\prime})}
,
and set
\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{m-1}:=\bigvee_{(\tau^{\prime},\tau^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{I}^{n}_{m-1}}~{\mathbf{S}}^{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}}_{(\tau^{\prime})}
.

The attaching map
\overline{\partial}_{W_{m-1}}:\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{m-1}\to{\mathbf{W}}_{m-2}
sends
\iota_{\tau^{\prime}}
to

(6.18)

{}\phi_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor\frac{m}{2}\rfloor}\sum_{(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\widehat{\mathcal{I}}^{m}_{m-k}}\ (-1)^{\deg(\sigma^{\prime})+k}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}})\sum_{(I,J)\in\mathcal{J}^{m-2}_{k-1}}\ \operatorname{sgn}({I,J})[s_{I}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]

in
\pi_{N_{\mathcal{S}^{\prime}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{m-2}
, for
\widehat{\mathcal{I}}^{m}_{m-k}
and

\mathcal{J}^{m-2}_{k-1}
as in §
4.1
. We refer to
(-1)^{\deg(\sigma^{\prime})+k}

as the
global sign
for the summand

[s_{I}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]
.

6.19 Proposition
.

Given a collection
\mathcal{S}=({\mathbf{S}}^{p_{i}+1})_{i=1}^{n}
of simply-connected
spheres, the rational simplicial space
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}={\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{S})
of §
6.16
is
a resolution of the rational fat wedge
T_{1}(\mathcal{S})
, and the class

[\phi_{\mathcal{S}}]
of (
6.18
) (for
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}
and
m=n
) represents
the rational
n
-th order Whitehead product for
\mathcal{S}
in the homotopy spectral sequence
for
{\mathbf{W}}_{\bullet}
.

Proof.

Since there are no non-degenerate elements in
E^{1}_{\ast,n-1}
for

\ast\leq N_{\mathcal{S}}
, if we show that
[\phi_{\mathcal{S}}]\in E^{1}_{N_{\mathcal{S}},n-2}

is a Moore cycle, it cannot bound, so we may deduce from Proposition
2.10

that it represents the rational
n
-th order Whitehead product.

We do so by induction on
n\geq 2
, starting with

\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{0}=T_{n-1}(\mathcal{S})=\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\ {\mathbf{S}}^{p_{i}+1}

and
\overline{{\mathbf{W}}}_{1}=\bigvee_{i<j}\ {\mathbf{S}}^{p_{i}+p_{j}+1}_{(i,j)}
, where

\overline{\partial}_{W_{1}}
is given on
{\mathbf{S}}^{p_{i}+p_{j}+1}_{(i,j)}
by

[\iota^{p_{i}+1},\iota^{p_{j}+1}]

.

Step I.
First observe that
\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
is a Moore
chain. For this purpose, assume that

(6.20)

{}\widehat{A}~=~[s_{I}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]~\in\pi_{N_{\mathcal{S}}}{\mathbf{W}}_{n-2}

is one summand, which appears in (
6.18
) with coefficient

(6.21)

{}(-1)^{\deg(\sigma^{\prime})+k}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime}})\cdot\operatorname{sgn}({I,J})~,

where

(6.22)

{}\begin{split}&(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})~\text{is an}\ (n-k,k)\text{-shuffle, with}\ \sigma^{\prime}_{1}=1\ \text{if}\ n=2k\\
&(I,J)\ \text{is a}\ (k-1,n-k-1)\text{-partition of}~\{0,\dotsc,n-3\}~.\end{split}

We then must show that
d_{\ell}(\widehat{A})=0
for each
\ell\geq 1
; to do so,
consider two cases:

(a)

If
\ell\in I
and
\ell-1\in J
, or conversely, let

(\hat{I},\hat{J})
be obtained from
(I,J)
by switching
\ell
and

\ell-1
. Then for any

(\sigma^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime\prime})\in\mathcal{I}^{n}_{n-k}
, we have

d_{\ell}[s_{I}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]=d_{\ell}[s_{\hat{I}}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{\hat{J}}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]

but
\operatorname{sgn}({I,J})=-\operatorname{sgn}({\hat{I},\hat{J}})
, so the two expressions
cancel in the sum for
d_{\ell}\circ\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
.

(b)

If
\ell,\ell-1\in J
, applying
d_{\ell}
to

s_{I}=s_{i_{\ell-1}}\dotsc s_{i_{t}}\dotsc s_{i_{1}}
,
where
\ell<i_{t}
and
\ell>i_{t-1}-1
, yields

s_{i_{\ell-1}-1}\dotsc s_{i_{t}-1}d_{\ell}s_{i_{t-1}}\dotsc s_{i_{1}}~=~s_{i_{\ell-1}-1}\dotsc s_{i_{t}-1}s_{i_{t-1}}\dotsc s_{i_{1}}d_{\ell-t+1}~.

Since
\ell>t-1
, necessarily
d_{\ell-t+1}
is not
d_{0}
, so
by (
2.6
)
d_{\ell-t+1}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}=0
.

Similarly if
\ell,\ell-1\in I

.

To show that
\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
is in fact a Moore cycle,
note that for
\widehat{A}
as in (
6.20
):

(a)

if
0\in I
,

d_{0}(\widehat{A})=[s_{I^{\prime}}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J^{\prime}}d_{0}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]
, and

(b)

if
0\in J
,

d_{0}(\widehat{A})=[s_{I^{\prime}}d_{0}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}},s_{J^{\prime}}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}]
,

where
I^{\prime}
and
J^{\prime}
are obtained from
I
and
J
, respectively,
by decreasing all indices by
1
(and omitting
0
).

By Definition
6.16
we see that
d_{0}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime}}
and

d_{0}\iota_{\sigma^{\prime\prime}}
are themselves sums of the form
(
6.18
), with
\mathcal{S}
replaced by
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
or
\mathcal{S}^{\prime\prime}
(the subset of spheres
appearing in
\sigma^{\prime}
or
\sigma^{\prime\prime}
, respectively)

. Therefore, the terms which occur in
d_{0}(\phi_{\mathcal{S}})
are of the form

A~=~[[s_{P}\iota_{\sigma},s_{Q}\iota_{\tau}],s_{R}\iota_{\rho}]~,

with a certain coefficient
\pm 1
which we proceed to calculate below.

We may assume that the number of spheres in
\sigma
is greater than that in
\tau
.
and if they are equal then
1\in\sigma
.
We use the following notation

L:=P\cap Q,\hskip 25.60747ptM:=P\setminus L,\hskip 25.60747ptN=Q\setminus L~,

so that
R=M\sqcup N
. We also write
k_{\sigma}
(respectively,
k_{\tau}
and

k_{\rho}
) for the cardinality of
\sigma
(respectively
\tau
and
\rho
).
It follows that

\#(L)=k_{\rho}-1,~\#(M)=k_{\tau}-1,~\#(N)=k_{\sigma}-1,~\#(R)=n-k_{\rho}-2.

Step II.
We assume first that
k_{\sigma}+k_{\tau}\geq n/2
,
and if equality holds, then
1\in\sigma\sqcup\tau
.
This implies that the term
A
appears in
d_{0}(\widehat{A})
where
\widehat{A}
is given in
(
6.20
) with

\sigma^{\prime}=\sigma\sqcup\tau,~\sigma^{\prime\prime}=\rho,~J=\widehat{\widehat{R}},~I=\widehat{L}.

The sign of
\widehat{A}
in
\phi_{\mathcal{S}}
is

(-1)^{k_{\rho}+\deg(\sigma)+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma\sqcup\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({\widehat{L},\widehat{\widehat{R}}})=-(-1)^{\deg(\sigma)+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma\sqcup\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,R}).

The sign of
A
in
d_{0}(\widehat{A})
is

(-1)^{k_{\tau}}(-1)^{\deg(\sigma)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,N}).

Thus the sign of
A
in
d_{0}(\phi_{\mathcal{S}})
is

-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})~.

Step III.
Now assume that for the term
A
,

k_{\sigma}+k_{\tau}\leq n/2
and in case of equality,

1\notin\sigma\sqcup\tau
.
The term appears after a reordering via (
4.6
) in
d_{0}(A^{\prime})
, where

A^{\prime}=[s_{\widehat{\widehat{R}}}\iota_{\rho},s_{\widehat{L}}\iota_{\sigma\sqcup\tau}].

The sign of
A^{\prime}
in
d_{0}(\phi_{\mathcal{S}})
is

(-1)^{k_{\sigma}+k_{\tau}}(-1)^{\deg(\rho)}\operatorname{gsn}({\rho,\sigma\sqcup\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({\widehat{\widehat{R}},\widehat{L}})~=~(-1)^{k_{\sigma}+k_{\tau}}(-1)^{\deg(\rho)}\operatorname{gsn}({\rho,\sigma\sqcup\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({R,L})~.

The sign of
[s_{R}\iota_{\rho},[s_{P}\iota_{\sigma},s_{Q}\iota_{\tau}]]
in
d_{0}(A^{\prime})
is

(-1)^{k_{\tau}}(-1)^{\deg(\sigma)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,N}).

The skew symmetry involved in obtaining
A
has sign

(-1)^{(\deg(\rho)+1)(\deg(\sigma)+\deg(\tau)+1)}.

Therefore, the total sign of
A
in this case is

\begin{split}(-1)^{k_{\sigma}+k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)}&\operatorname{gsn}({\rho,\sigma\sqcup\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({R,L})(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\sigma)}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt\cdot\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,N})(-1)^{(\deg(\rho)+1)(\deg(\sigma)+\deg(\tau)+1)}\\
=~&-(-1)^{k_{\sigma}+\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho)(\deg(\sigma)+\deg(\tau))}\operatorname{gsn}({\rho,\sigma,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,N,L}).\end{split}

Step IV.
We assume that

k_{\sigma}\geq k_{\tau}\geq k_{\rho}
,
and if equality occurs,
1
belongs to the subset on the left. Observe that the terms

A~:=~[[s_{P}\iota_{\sigma},s_{Q}\iota_{\tau}],s_{R}\iota_{\rho}],\hskip 14.22636ptB~:=~[[s_{Q}\iota_{\tau},s_{R}\iota_{\rho}],s_{P}\iota_{\sigma}],\hskip 14.22636pt\text{and}\hskip 14.22636ptC~:=~[[s_{P}\iota_{\sigma},s_{R}\iota_{\rho}],s_{Q}\iota_{\tau}]~,

together with their signs in
d_{0}(\phi_{\mathcal{S}})
, cancel out via the
Jacobi identity.

There are two different cases to consider:

a)

k_{\sigma}\leq n/2
, and in case of equality, we have

1\notin\sigma
.

b)

k_{\sigma}\geq n/2
, and in case of equality, we have
1\in\sigma
.

In case 1), all the signs are as computed in Step II. Thus,

\begin{split}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}({B})}{\operatorname{sgn}({A})}~&=~\frac{-(-1)^{k_{\rho}+\deg(\rho)}\operatorname{gsn}({\tau,\rho,\sigma})\operatorname{sgn}({N,L,M})}{-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})}\\
&=~\frac{(-1)^{k_{\rho}+k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\tau\sqcup\rho,\sigma})\operatorname{sgn}({N,L\sqcup M})}{\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau\sqcup\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L\sqcup M,N})}\\
&=~(-1)^{k_{\rho}+k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\tau)}(-1)^{(\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho))(\deg(\sigma))+(k_{\tau}+k_{\rho})k_{\sigma}}(-1)^{(k_{\rho}+k_{\tau})(k_{\sigma}-1)}\\
&=~(-1)^{(\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho))(\deg(\sigma)+1)}~.\end{split}

Analogously,

\begin{split}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}({C})}{\operatorname{sgn}({A})}~&=~\frac{-(-1)^{k_{\rho}+\deg(\rho)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\rho,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,L,N})}{-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})}\\
&=~\frac{(-1)^{k_{\rho}+k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\rho,\tau})\operatorname{sgn}({M,L})}{\operatorname{gsn}({\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M})}\\
&=~(-1)^{k_{\rho}+k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\tau)}(-1)^{\deg(\tau)\deg(\rho)+k_{\tau}k_{\rho}}(-1)^{(k_{\rho}-1)(k_{\tau}-1)}\\
&=~(-1)^{(\deg(\tau)+1)(\deg(\rho)+1)}~.\end{split}

It follows that the three terms cancel out by the Jacobi identity.

In the case 2), the signs of
A
and
C
are calculated using Step II,
while the sign of
B
is calculated by Step III. This turns out to be

-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\sigma)(\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho))}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})~.

Therefore, once again we have

\begin{split}\frac{\operatorname{sgn}({B})}{\operatorname{sgn}({A})}&=\frac{-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\rho)+\deg(\sigma)(\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho))}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})}{-(-1)^{k_{\tau}+\deg(\tau)}\operatorname{gsn}({\sigma,\tau,\rho})\operatorname{sgn}({L,M,N})}\\
&=(-1)^{(\deg(\tau)+\deg(\rho))(\deg(\sigma)+1)}~.\end{split}

This concludes the proof.

7.
Lie-Massey products

A more general collection of rational higher order operations is provided by the Lie
analogue of higher Massey products. These can be described in terms of the
{\mathcal{L}}_{\infty}

model for rational homotopy theory (see
[
LS
,
LM
]
), but we shall use Retakh’s
original description for a DG Lie algebra
(L_{\ast},d)
in
[
Ret
]

(here with the usual
d:L_{i}\to L_{i-1}
).

7.1
.

Construction of the Lie-Massey products.

Assume given an ordered set
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{n})
of cycles in
L_{\ast}
,
such that for each ordered set
I=(i_{1},i_{2},\dotsc,i_{m})
with

1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}<\dotsc<i_{m}\leq n
, there is an element

x_{I}\in L_{\ast}
with
d(x_{I})=\widetilde{x_{I}}
for

(7.2)

{}\widetilde{x}_{I}~:=~\sum_{(J,K)}\ (-1)^{\varepsilon(J,K)+|x_{J}|+1}\cdot[x_{J},x_{K}]~.

Here
J=(j_{1},j_{2},\dotsc,j_{s})

8.9
) yields
(m+2)!
summands

[s_{I}\iota^{m+3-k},\,s_{J}\iota^{k+1}]
, with signs

\begin{split}(-1)^{m+2}&\cdot(-1)^{(m+1)\cdot(k+1)}\cdot(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{m+1-k}{2}\rfloor}\cdot(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{k-1}{2}\rfloor}=\\
~=&~\begin{cases}(-1)^{\frac{m-k}{2}+\frac{k-2}{2}+1}=(-1)^{\frac{m}{2}}=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{2}\rfloor}&\text{$m$ and $k$ even}\\
(-1)^{\frac{m-k+1}{2}+\frac{k-1}{2}}=(-1)^{\frac{m}{2}}=(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{2}\rfloor}&\text{$m$ even, $k$ odd}\\
(-1)\cdot(-1)^{\frac{m-k}{2}+\frac{k-1}{2}}=(-1)^{\frac{m-1}{2}+1}=(-1)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}&\text{$m$ and $k$ odd}\\
(-1)\cdot(-1)^{\frac{m+1-k}{2}+\frac{k-2}{2}}=(-1)^{\frac{m-1}{2}+1}=(-1)^{\frac{m+1}{2}}&\text{$m$ odd, $k$ even~,}\\
\end{cases}\end{split}

which again equals
(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{m+1}{2}\rfloor}
in all cases.
This completes the induction step, and thus the proof.
∎

8.10 Corollary
.

For each
n\geq 1
,
(-1)^{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(n+1)!
times the class of
the rational Hopf map

(n+1)

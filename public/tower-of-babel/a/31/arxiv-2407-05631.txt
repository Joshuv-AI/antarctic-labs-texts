Gradient regularity for a class of doubly nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations
Michael Strunk

Abstract
. In this paper, we study the local gradient regularity of non-negative weak solutions to doubly nonlinear parabolic partial differential equations of the type ∂ t u q − div ⁡ A ⁡ ( x , t , D ​ u ) = 0 in Ω T , \displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{q}-\divv A(x,t,Du)=0\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $\Omega_{T}$}, with q > 0 q>0 , Ω T ≔ Ω × ( 0 , T ) ⊂ ℝ n + 1 \Omega_{T}\coloneqq\Omega\times(0,T)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1} a space-time cylinder, and A = A ⁡ ( x , t , ξ ) A=A(x,t,\xi) a vector field satisfying standard p p -growth conditions. Our main result establishes the local Hölder continuity of the spatial gradient of non-negative weak solutions in the super-critical fast diffusion regime 0 < p − 1 < q < n ⁡ ( p − 1 ) ( n − p ) + . 0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}. This result is achieved by utilizing a time-insensitive Harnack inequality and Schauder estimates that are developed for equations of parabolic p p -Laplacian type. Additionally, we establish a local L ∞ L^{\infty} -bound for the spatial gradient.

1.
Introduction and main results

The parabolic partial differential equation

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{q}-\divv{A(x,t,Du)}=0\qquad\mbox{in\penalty\ $\Omega_{T}$},

with an arbitrary exponent
q>0
, represents a generalized version of the prototype equation which involves the
p
-Laplacian as the diffusion part and that is given by

(1.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{q}-\divv{(|Du|^{p-2}Du)}=0\qquad\text{in $\Omega_{T}$},

where
p>1
. Here and in the following,
\Omega_{T}=\Omega\times(0,T)
denotes a space-time cylinder taken over a bounded domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
and a finite time
T>0
. Treating the general equation (
1.1
), we assume the vector field
A
to satisfy the standard
p
-growth structure conditions (
1.2
) introduced in Section
1.2
below. Based on a nonlinear behaviour in both, the term involving the time derivative as well well as the term that incorporates the spatial derivative, equations of type (
1.1
) are referred to as doubly nonlinear. For the prototype equation (
1.2
) some well known special cases arise. Only with the specific choice
q=1
and
p=2
, it is linear and yields the heat equation. If
q=p-1
, it is homogeneous with respect to multiplication and is sometimes called Trudinger’s equation. In the case
p=2
, we obtain the porous medium equation, whereas the case
q=1
yields the parabolic
p
-Laplace equation.

The aim of this paper is to extend the regularity results obtained for the prototype equation (
1.2
) to the broader scope of the doubly nonlinear parabolic equation (
1.1
), where the vector field
A
is assumed to satisfy standard
p
-growth structure conditions.
Indeed, this generalization turns out to hold true, resulting in a Lipschitz estimate for weak solutions within the space-time domain
\Omega_{T}
based on a pointwise value of the solution. Additionally, we obtain a Hölder estimate for the spatial gradient of weak solutions in
\Omega_{T}
, where the Hölder exponent
\alpha
, which lies within the range of
(0,1)
, is dependent on the provided data. However, it is important to note that we are only able to provide statements regarding the regularity of the spatial gradient of solutions to (
1.1
), as our notion of solution according to Definition
2.1
does not encompass any weak time derivatives.

At this point in time, only few attempts concerning gradient regularity of weak solutions to equation (

1.2.
Structure conditions

Throughout this paper, we consistently assume the following set of structure conditions to hold true. We consider a vector field

A\colon\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n},

such that the mapping
\xi\mapsto A(x,t,\xi)
is differentiable. Moreover, the maps
A(x,t,\xi)
as well as
\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\xi)
are assumed to be Carathéodory functions, i.e.

\displaystyle(x,t)

\displaystyle\mapsto A(x,t,\xi),\,\,(x,t)\mapsto\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\xi)\,\,\mbox{are measurable}

for every
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and

\displaystyle\xi

\displaystyle\mapsto A(x,t,\xi),\,\,\xi\mapsto\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\xi)\,\,\mbox{are continuous}

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
. It is notable that we do not impose any higher regularity than measurability for the mapping
t\mapsto A(x,t,\xi)
. Furthermore, the vector field
A(x,t,\xi)
is assumed to satisfy the following standard
p
-growth and ellipticity conditions

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}|A(x,t,\xi)|+(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}|\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\xi)|\leq C_{1}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\\[3.0pt]
\langle\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\geq C_{2}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\eta|^{2}\\[3.0pt]
|A_{i}(x,t,\xi)-A_{i}(y,t,\xi)|\leq C_{3}|x-y|^{\alpha}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\end{array}\right.

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
,
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, any
\eta,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and an Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Here, we let
p>1
,
\mu\in[0,1]
, while
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
represent positive constants. The parameter
\mu\in[0,1]
serves as a regularizing quantity that distinguishes between the degenerate resp. singular case if
\mu=0
and the non-degenerate resp. non-singular case when
\mu\in(0,1]
. Note that for the standard example we have in mind, which is the
p
-Laplacian, conditions
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
and
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{2}
are satisfied in the case where
\mu=0
with constants
C_{1}=C_{1}(p)=1+|p-2|
,
C_{2}=C_{2}(p)=1-(2-p)_{+}
, while condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
is satisfied regardless with
C_{3}=0
. Moreover, the vector field incorporated in the
p
-Laplacian may also contain some bounded coefficients
a\in L^{\infty}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\gamma^{-1}_{1}\leq a(x,t)\leq\gamma_{1}
and
a(x,t)-a(y,t)|\leq\gamma_{2}|x-y|^{\alpha}
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
, where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
denote some positive constants and
\alpha\in(0,1)
is the Hölder exponent of
a
with respect to the spatial variable
x
, i.e.
A(x,t,\xi)=a(x,t)|\xi|^{p-2}\xi
.

1.3.
Main result

Imposing the standard
p

1.2
) on the vector field

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
and
u
be a non-negative weak solution to (
1.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
) with
\mu=0
. Then, there exist
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{1}(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
and
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)>1
, such that: for any compact subset
\mathcal{K}_{1}\Subset\Omega_{T}
, let

\displaystyle\rho_{1}\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}_{1}\\
(y,s)\in\partial(\Omega_{T})\end{subarray}}\big(|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}}\big)>0.

Moreover, we set

\displaystyle K_{1}\coloneqq\max\Big\{1,\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}u\Big\},\quad K_{2}\coloneqq\max\Big\{1,\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}u\Big\},

where
\mathcal{K}_{2}\Supset\mathcal{K}_{1}

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{2}\coloneqq\Big\{(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}:\penalty\ \inf\limits_{(y,s)\in\partial(\Omega_{T})}\big(|x-y|+|t-s|^{\frac{1}{p}}\big)\geq\frac{\rho_{1}}{2}\Big\}\Subset\Omega_{T}.

Then, there hold the gradient bound

(1.6)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{1}}|Du|\leq C\frac{K_{1}K_{2}^{\frac{q+1-p}{p}}}{\rho_{1}}

as well as the gradient Hölder estimate

(1.7)

\displaystyle|Du(z_{1})-Du(z_{2})|\leq C\frac{K_{1}K_{2}^{\frac{q+1-p}{p}}}{\rho_{1}}\Bigg[K_{2}^{\frac{q+1-p}{p}}\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{\rho_{1}}+\Big(\frac{K_{2}}{K_{1}}\Big)^{\frac{q+1-p}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{\rho_{1}^{p}}}\,\Bigg]^{\alpha_{1}}

1.4.
Strategy of the proof

Regarding the overall course of this paper, a few words are in order. The main focus is to establish the main regularity Theorem
1.1
. One essential tool that we exploit on our path towards the main regularity result is the Harnack inequality of elliptic nature mentioned above that solely holds true in the super-critical fast diffusion regime
0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
. This inequality allows us to properly handle the nonlinearity present in the evolution term of (
1.1
). To specify, by employing the transformation
v\coloneqq u^{q}
in (
1.1
), there holds
Du=\frac{1}{q}v^{\frac{1-q}{q}}Dv
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
. Now, due to the Harnack inequality mentioned above, there exists a universal constant
\gamma>1
that depends on
n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2}
, such that on an intrinsic cylinder
\mathcal{Q}\subset\Omega_{T}
not further specified at this point,
v
is bounded below by
\gamma^{-1}
and bounded above by
\gamma
. Therefore, the coefficients
a(x,t)\coloneqq\textstyle{\frac{1}{q}}v^{\frac{1-q}{q}}
satisfy the bounds

C^{-1}\leq a(x,t)\leq C\quad\mbox{for a.e.\penalty\ $(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}$}

with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2})
. As a result, we achieve that
v
is a weak solution to a parabolic equation of
p
-Laplacian type and we are able to break down the original equation into the following version

(1.8)

\partial_{t}v-\divv\tilde{A}(x,t,Dv)=0\quad\mbox{in\penalty\ $\mathcal{Q}$},

where the vector field
\tilde{A}
is given by

\tilde{A}(x,t,\xi)\coloneqq A(x,t,a(x,t)\xi)

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\mathcal{Q}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Later on in Section
5
, it will turn out that the vector field
\tilde{A}
again satisfies the
p
-growth conditions (
1.2
) with
\mu=0
, and with positive constants
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2},\tilde{C}_{3}
and an Hölder exponent
\tilde{\alpha}\in(0,1)
, where
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
depend on
n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2}
, while
\tilde{C}_{3}
exhibits the same dependence but additionally also depends on
C_{3}
. The Hölder exponent
\tilde{\alpha}\in(0,1)
depends on
n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha
but not on the structure constant
C_{3}
. In particular, the evolutionary term no longer exhibits nonlinearity, which leads us to focus on developing Schauder estimates for weak solutions to parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations. As an initial step, our aim in Section
3
is to demonstrate that any weak solution to equations of the form (
5.9
) exhibits a spatial derivative that is locally bounded in
\Omega_{T}
, i.e. satisfies
|Du|\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T})
. Throughout Section
3
, structure assumption
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
is replaced by assuming that
A
is differentiable with respect to the spatial variable
x
, allowing us to differentiate the equation. In the case where
p>\frac{2n}{n+2}
, previous works have established this result for the super-quadratic case
p\geq 2
in
[
9
, Theorem 1.2]
and for the sub-quadratic-super-critical case
\frac{2n}{n+2}<p<2
in
[
46
, Theorem 1.3]
. However, at this point in time, the sub-critical range
p\leq\frac{2n}{n+2}
remains unaddressed to our knowledge. Our results yield the very same quantitative estimates as those in
[
6
, Proposition 9.1 & Proposition 9.2]
, differing from the ones in
[

, Theorem 1.2]
and
[

, Theorem 1.3]
. Subsequently, we aim to establish an

. It should be noted that the authors in
[

also utilized a similar

[

[

in our setting as it applies to systems of Uhlenbeck structure. On the contrary, we consider equations without Uhlenbeck structure in general, and rather utilize the complete machinery involving both the non-degenerate and degenerate regime in order to ultimately obtain a similar

[
35
, Theorem 3.3]
in the sub-quadratic case and in
[
36
, Theorem 3.2]
in the super-quadratic case, comparable

[

in a cohesive manner by exploiting the fact that the function

with respect to the spatial variable

. In particular, we subsequently consider vector fields

and utilize the energy estimates derived in

1.5.
Plan of the paper

The paper is organized as follows: in Section

) and additional supplementary material required later on. As a preliminary step towards Theorem

, we first treat weak solutions to the parabolic

), which does not exhibit a nonlinearity in the evolution term. Additionally, we assume that the vector field

is differentiable with respect to the spatial variable

, in contrast to the general structural conditions (

). Under these assumptions (
3
) on the vector field

, we obtain that any weak solution

to (

, i.e. there holds

. Thereby, we obtain an

with respect to the spatial variable
x
, without distinguishing between them. According to this notion, the weak partial derivative of any weakly differentiable function
u\colon\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
with respect to
i\in\{1,...,n\}
will be denoted by
D_{i}u
. To distinguish between spatial and time derivatives, the latter will be denoted as
\partial_{t}u
, given that
u\colon\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}
admits a weak derivative with respect to the time variable
t
. It is worth noting that no distinction between classical and weak derivatives will be made, with the context clarifying the intended meaning.
Moreover, we will regard the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
for
n\geq 2
and the absolute value
|\cdot|
on
\mathbb{R}
as interchangeable. In this paper, both will be referred to as
|\cdot|
with the specific context providing clarity regarding its denotation. At certain stages we will also employ the maximum norm
|x|_{\infty}\coloneqq\max\limits_{i=1,...,n}|x_{i}|
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and recall the equivalence of norms

|x|_{\infty}\leq|x|\leq\sqrt{n}|x|_{\infty}

for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The standard scalar product on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be denoted by
\displaystyle\langle\cdot\rangle
and the dyadic product of two vectors
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
\xi\otimes\eta
. The positive part of a real quantity
a\in\mathbb{R}
is denoted as
a_{+}=\max\{a,0\}
, while the negative part is denoted as
a_{-}=\max\{-a,0\}
. Constants are consistently represented as
C
or
C(\cdot)
and their dependence is described solely on their variables, not their specific values. However, it is possible for constants to vary from one line to another without further clarification.

We define the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with radius
\rho>0
and center
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

B_{\rho}(x_{0})\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\colon\penalty\ |x-x_{0}|<\rho\}.

By a point
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, we always refer to
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})
where
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. If
x_{0}=0
, it will be common to simplify notation by writing
B_{\rho}
instead of
B_{\rho}(x_{0})
.
Additionally, we may omit the vertex of the cylinder for convenience. The
general (backward) parabolic cylinder
with vertex
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
is defined as

Q_{R,S}(z_{0})\coloneqq B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-S,t_{0}],

with the standard parabolic cylinder given by

Q_{\rho}(z_{0})\coloneqq B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\rho^{2},t_{0}].

The following
(backward) intrinsic parabolic cylinders
lead to homogeneous estimates that incorporate the spatial gradient
Du
. Given
\lambda>0
, we set

Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})\coloneqq B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(t_{0})

with

\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(t_{0})\coloneqq(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}\rho^{2},t_{0}].

Next, we will introduce different concepts of distances. Consider two points
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1})
,
z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})\in\Omega_{T}
. The
parabolic distance
between
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
is defined as

d_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})\coloneqq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\sqrt{|t_{1}-t_{2}|},

while the
intrinsic parabolic distance
is denoted by

d^{(\lambda)}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})\coloneqq|x_{1}-x_{2}|+\sqrt{\lambda^{p-2}|t_{1}-t_{2}|}.

Finally, for a subset
E\subset\Omega_{T}
, the parabolic distance from
E
to the parabolic boundary
\partial_{p}\Omega_{T}
is defined as

\dist_{p}(E,\partial_{p}\Omega_{T})\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}z_{1}\in E\\
z_{2}\in\partial_{p}\Omega_{T}\end{subarray}}d_{\p}(z_{1},z_{2}).

Since our notion of solution according to Definition (
2.1
) does not involve spatial weak derivatives of second order, it becomes useful to utilize the customary difference quotient technique. In the case of
\mu\in(0,1]
, this approach will yield the existence and
p
-integrability of second order spatial derivatives. Let
f\in L^{1}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
i\in\{1,...,n\}
,
k\in\mathbb{N}
, and
h\neq 0
. We define the difference quotient of
f
for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
in the spatial direction
e_{i}
by

(2.1)

\displaystyle\Delta_{h}^{(i)}f(x,t)\coloneqq\frac{f(x+he_{i},t)-f(x,t)}{h},

where
|h|>0
is chosen sufficiently small, such that
(x+he_{i})\in\Omega_{T}
holds true. Let
\mu\in[0,1]
. The following functions will turn out to be expedient in the course of this paper:

(2.2)

\displaystyle h_{\mu}(s)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mu^{2}+s^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\mu}^{(p)}(\xi)

\displaystyle\coloneqq(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{4}}\xi=h_{\mu}(|\xi|)^{\frac{1}{2}}\xi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mu}^{(i)}(h)(x,t)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mu^{2}+|Du(x,t)|^{2}+|Du(x+he_{i},t)|^{2}

for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
s\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
,
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
, and
|h|>0
small enough. Further, for
\xi,\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
the following bilinear form on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will be employed

(2.3)

\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}(\cdot,\xi)(\eta,\zeta)\coloneqq\langle\partial_{\xi}A(\cdot,\xi)\eta,\zeta\rangle.

For any
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we use the common abbreviation for the average integral of
f
. Let
A\subset\Omega
such that
\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)>0
. Then, we define the slice-wise mean
(f)_{A}\colon(0,T)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
as follows

\displaystyle(f)_{A}(t)\coloneqq\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle-$ }}\kern-7.24571pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.65785pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-5.07782pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle-$ }}\kern-4.91403pt}}\!\int_{A}f(\cdot,t),\mathrm{d}x,\quad\mbox{for a.e.\penalty\ $t\in(0,T)$}.

Since most of the functions in this paper are continuous with respect to the time variable, that is,
f\in C(0,T;L^{1}(\Omega))
, the slice-wise mean value is well-defined for all
t\in(0,T)
. In a similar fashion, we denote the mean value of
f
on some measurable set
E\subset\Omega_{T}
, such that
\mathcal{L}^{n+1}(E)>0
, by

\displaystyle(f)_{E}\coloneqq\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{E}f(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

If
E
is an intrinsic parabolic cylinder of the form
Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})
, then we abbreviate the mean value of
f
on
Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})
by
(f)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\rho}
, meaning

\displaystyle(f)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\rho}\coloneqq\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})}f(x,t)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

If it is evident from the context, the vertex
z_{0}
may sometimes be omitted to simplify notation.

2.2.
Definition of weak solution

While it is standard, we subsequently state the employed definition of a weak solution in our paper.

Definition 2.1

(Weak solution)
.

Let
q>0
,
p>1
. A non-negative measurable function
u\colon\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in the class

u\in C\big([0,T];L^{q+1}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega)\big)

under assumptions (
1.2
) is a non-negative weak sub(super)-solution of (
1.1
), if

(2.4)

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\big[-u^{q}\partial_{t}\phi+\langle A(x,t,Du),D\phi\rangle\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq(\geq)\,0

for any non-negative function

\phi\in W^{1,q+1}_{0}\big(0,T;L^{q+1}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big).

A non-negative function
u
is a non-negative weak solution of (
1.1
), if it is both, a weak sub-solution and a weak super-solution.

This ensures the convergence of all the integrals in (
2.4
). According to our Definition
2.1
, weak sub/super-solutions belong to the space

C\big([0,T];L^{q+1}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega)\big),

meaning they are continuous functions with respect to the time variable for a.e.
x\in\Omega
. However, this property is not restrictive, as pointed out in
[
6
, Proposition 4.9]
. We will now state the notion of a weak solution to the following Cauchy-Dirichlet problem of equation (
1.1
):

(2.5)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u^{q}-\divv A(x,t,Du)=0&\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$},\\
u=g&\mbox{on $\partial\Omega\times(0,T)$},\\
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}&\mbox{in $\Omega$},\end{cases}

where
g\in L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}(\Omega)\big)
with
\partial_{t}g\in L^{p^{\prime}}\big(0,T;W^{-1,p^{\prime}}(\Omega)\big)
and
u_{0}\in L^{q+1}(\Omega)
. Here,
p^{\prime}\coloneqq\frac{p}{p-1}
in the usual manner denotes the Hölder conjugate of
p
.

Definition 2.2

(Weak solution to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem)
.

Let
q>0
,
p>1
. A non-negative measurable function
u\colon\Omega_{T}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
in the class

u\in C\big([0,T];L^{q+1}(\Omega)\big)\cap\big(g+L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big)\big)

under assumptions (
1.2
) is a non-negative weak solution to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem (
2.5
), if

(2.6)

\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\big[(u_{0}^{q}-u^{q})\partial_{t}\phi+\langle A(x,t,Du),D\phi\rangle\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=0

for any non-negative function

\phi\in W^{1,q+1}\big(0,T;L^{q+1}(\Omega)\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\big),

such that
\phi(\cdot,T)\equiv 0
.

According to this definition, if
u
is a weak solution to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem (
2.5
), then it can be inferred from
[
6
, Proposition 4.9]
that
u(\cdot,0)=u_{0}
a.e. in
\Omega
. The existence of weak solutions to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem (
2.5
) has been established in
[
2
]
.

2.3.
Mollification in time

According to our definition, weak solutions may not necessarily exhibit weak differentiability with respect to the time variable. To address this challenge, we commonly resort to specific regularization techniques. In this regard, we will utilize Steklov-averages, referring to
[
14
]
for further insights into their properties. For a function
f\in L^{1}(\Omega_{T})
and
0<h<T
, we define its
Steklov-average

[f]_{h}
by

(2.7)

[f]_{h}(x,t)\coloneqq\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\displaystyle{\frac{1}{h}\int_{t}^{t+h}f(x,\tau)\,\mathrm{d}\tau,}&t\in(0,T-h),\\[9.0pt]
0,&t\in[T-h,T)\,.\end{array}\right.

Rewriting inequality (
2.4
) in terms of Steklov-means
[u]_{h}
of
u
, yields

(2.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega\times\{t\}}\big[\partial_{t}[u^{q}]_{h}\phi+\langle[A(x,t,Du)]_{h},D\phi\rangle\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\leq(\geq)\,0

for any non-negative function
\phi\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
and any
t\in(0,T)
.

2.4.
Auxiliary material

The aim of this section is to present several useful preliminary results. As the majority of the subsequent material is commonly known, we will largely omit providing proofs.

2.4.1.
Difference quotients

The following initial lemmas recall standard estimates for the difference quotients, as introduced in (
2.1
). To state a reference, we refer the reader to
[
22
, Chapter 5.8, Theorem 3]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
i\in\{1,...,n\}
,
0<r<\rho
, and
D_{i}u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho})
. Then, there holds

\lim\limits_{\rho-r>|h|\downarrow 0}\big\|\Delta_{h}^{(i)}u-D_{i}u\big\|_{L^{p}(B_{r})}=0.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
i\in\{1,...,n\}
,
0<r<\rho
, and
u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho})
. Furthermore, suppose there exists a constant
K\geq 0
, such that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\big|\Delta_{h}^{(i)}u\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq K,\quad\mbox{for any $|h|<\rho-r$}.

Then, there holds
D_{i}u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho})
including the quantitative estimate

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq K.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1<p<\infty
,
i\in\{1,...,n\}
,
0<r<\rho
,
|h|<\rho-r
, and
u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho})
. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|u(x+he_{i})|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Furthermore, if
D_{i}u\in L^{p}(B_{\rho})
, then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}\big|\Delta_{h}^{(i)}u\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|D_{i}u|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The subsequent lemma presents a sort of discrete integration by parts.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Further, let
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
,
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, and
0<|h|<\dist(\supp\phi,\partial\Omega)
. For any
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, there holds

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta_{h}^{(i)}F,\phi\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\langle F(x+hse_{i}),D_{i}\phi(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x.

Proof..

First, we consider
F\in C^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
. For
x\in\supp\phi
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta^{(i)}_{h}F(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{h}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}F(x+hse_{i})\,\mathrm{d}s=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}D_{i}F(x+hse_{i})\,\mathrm{d}s.

This implies

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta^{(i)}_{h}F(x),\phi(x)\big\rangle\,dx

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\langle D_{i}F(x+hse_{i}),\phi(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\langle D_{i}F(x+hse_{i}),\phi(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\langle F(x+hse_{i}),D_{i}\phi(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{1}\langle F(x+hse_{i}),D_{i}\phi(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x.

Given
F\in L^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
, we consider a sequence
(F_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{k})
with

\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F-F_{k}|\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Then, there hold

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta^{(i)}_{h}F,\xi\big\rangle\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big\langle\Delta^{(i)}_{h}F_{k},\xi\big\rangle\,dx\bigg|\leq\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\frac{2\left\|\phi\right\|_{\infty}}{|h|}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F-F_{k}|\,\mathrm{d}x=0

as well as

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}

\displaystyle\bigg|\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left\langle F(x+hse_{i}),D_{i}\right\rangle\xi\,dx-\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle F_{k}(x+hse_{i}),D_{i}\xi\rangle\,dx\bigg|

\displaystyle\leq\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\left\|D\phi\right\|_{\infty}\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|F-F_{k}|\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

2.4.2.
Structure estimates

In this section, we will present several commonly used estimates that primarily incorporate the regularizing parameter
\mu\in[0,1]
. For the next lemma, we refer to
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
in the case
\alpha\geq 0
, and to
[
1
, Lemma 2.1]
in the case
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha<0
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
-\frac{1}{2}<\alpha
and
\mu\in[0,1]
. Then, there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\alpha}\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\mu^{2}+|\xi+s(\eta-\xi)|^{2})^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}s\leq C(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\alpha}

for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
C=C(\alpha)>0
.

As an immediate consequence of the preceding Lemma
2.7
, we obtain the following structure properties for the vector field
A
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
p>1
,
\mu\in(0,1]
, and
A\colon\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}-\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{2}
. For any
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there hold

\displaystyle|A(x,t,\xi)-A(x,t,\eta)|\leq C(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|

with
C=C(p,C_{1})
, and

\displaystyle\langle A(x,t,\xi)-A(x,t,\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle\geq\frac{1}{C}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|^{2}

with
C=C(p,C_{2})
. The estimates remain valid, if either
\xi
or
\eta
is non-zero, even when
\mu
equals zero and
1<p<2
.

Proof..

If
\xi=\eta
, there is nothing to prove. Hence, we assume
\xi\neq\eta
. We start with the first estimate. By utilizing
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
and Lemma
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle|A(x,t,\xi)-A(x,t,\eta)|

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}A(x,t,\eta+s(\xi-\eta))\Big|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}|\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\eta+s(\xi-\eta))||\xi-\eta|\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\mu^{2}+|\eta+s(\xi-\eta)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,|\xi-\eta|

\displaystyle\leq C(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|.

The second inequality follows from
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{2}
and again Lemma
2.7

\displaystyle\langle A(x,t,\xi)-A(x,t,\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\Big\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}A(x,t,\eta+s(\xi-\eta)),\xi-\eta\Big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\langle\partial_{\xi}A(x,t,\eta+s(\xi-\eta))(\xi-\eta),\xi-\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geq C_{2}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}(\mu^{2}+|\eta+s(\xi-\eta)|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}s\,|\xi-\eta|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2}+|\eta|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\xi-\eta|^{2}.

∎

Exploiting condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
, we derive another useful lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
p>1
,
\mu\in[0,1]
, and
A\colon\Omega_{T}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
satisfy

|\partial_{x}A(x,t,\xi)|\leq C(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, with
C\geq 0
. For any
t\in(0,T)
,
x,y\in\Omega
with
[x,y]\subset\Omega
, where

[x,y]\coloneqq\{v\in\Omega\colon\penalty\ v=sx+(1-s)y,\,s\in[0,1]\},

and any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, there holds

\displaystyle|\langle A(x,t,\xi)-A(y,t,\xi),\eta\rangle|\leq C(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|x-y||\eta|

with
C=C(n,C_{3})
.

Proof..

An application of the mean value theorem yields the existence of
z\in[x,y]
with

A(x,t,\xi)-A(y,t,\xi)=\langle\partial_{x}A(z,t,\xi),x-y\rangle

for a.e.
t\in(0,T)
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Employing
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we thus immediately obtain the claimed estimate

\displaystyle|\langle A(x,t,\xi)-A(y,t,\xi),\eta\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq|A(x,t,\xi)-A(y,t,\xi)||\eta|

\displaystyle\leq|\partial_{x}A(z,t,\xi)||x-y||\eta|

\displaystyle\leq nC_{3}(\mu^{2}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|x-y||\eta|.

∎

Lemma 2.13

\displaystyle C_{1}(p)h_{\mu}(|Du|)|D^{2}u|^{2}\leq\big|D\mathcal{V}^{(p)}_{\mu}(Du)\big|^{2}\leq C_{2}(p)h_{\mu}(|Du|)|D^{2}u|^{2}\quad\mbox{a.e. in $B_{R}(z_{0})$}.

Proof..

The second inequality follows from

a.e. in

Now, if

\displaystyle 1+\frac{h^{\prime}_{\mu}(t)t}{h_{\mu}(t)}+\frac{h^{\prime}_{\mu}(t)^{2}t^{2}}{4h_{\mu}(t)^{2}}=\frac{4p\mu^{2}t^{2}+4\mu^{4}+p^{2}t^{4}}{4(\mu^{2}+t^{2})^{2}}

3.

In this section, we establish local boundedness of the spatial gradient of weak solutions to parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations. Moreover, we provide quantitative estimates for the whole parameter range

in the case

for a.e.

1.2
)

\eqref{voraussetzungendiffbarkeit}_{3}
deviates from its counterpart in (

). The differentiability of

with respect to

3.1
)

and the local gradient boundedness, combined with the quantitative estimates from Propositions

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{S+2|h|}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p+2\alpha}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

We insert inequality (

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{t\in\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{R}(t_{0})}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|D_{i}u(x,t)|}(T_{k}\circ\Phi_{\alpha})(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|D_{i}u|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|D_{i}Du|^{2}(T_{k}\circ\Phi_{\alpha})(|D_{i}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\alpha+1)}{(S-R)^{2}}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{S}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p+2\alpha}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C\lambda^{p-2}}{S^{2}-R^{2}}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{S}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{1+\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

By passing to the limit

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{|D_{i}u(x,t)|}\Phi_{\alpha}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|D_{i}u|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|D_{i}Du|^{2}\Phi_{\alpha}(|D_{i}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\alpha+1)}{(S-R)^{2}}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{S}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p+2\alpha}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{C\lambda^{p-2}}{S^{2}-R^{2}}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{S}(z_{0})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{1+\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Exploiting the definition of

4.
Schauder estimates

In this section, we will once again consider weak solutions to parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations of the form

(4.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\divv A(x,t,Du)=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$,}

where
\mu\in[0,1]
and
A
satisfies structure conditions (
1.2
). Throughout this section, weak solutions may still exhibit sign changes. Our objective is to derive a local gradient Hölder regularity result for weak solutions to (
4.1
). To begin with, we establish an
a priori
gradient Hölder estimate for weak solutions when

is independent of the spatial variable
x
. By employing a comparison argument, we then extend this regularity result to weak solutions of the more general equation (
4.1
) with
A
satisfying the given structure conditions (
1.2
). The assumed Hölder continuity of
A
with respect to the spatial variable
x
is crucial in transferring regularity from weak solutions with the vector field
A
independent of the spatial variable
x
to weak solutions of more general structure. Finally, we address the case where
\mu=0
in Section
4.4
, leading to the following theorem as a main result.

Theorem 4.1

(Schauder estimate for parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations)
.

Let
u
be a bounded weak solution to (
4.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
) in the range
p>1
and
\mu\in[0,1]
. Then, there exist a Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
and a constant
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)\geq 1
, such that there holds

Du\in C^{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}/2}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Moreover, for any
E\subset\Omega_{T}
such that
r\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\dist_{p}(E,\partial_{p}\Omega_{T})>0
, and any
z_{1},z_{2}\in E
, there hold
the quantitative gradient estimate

(4.2)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{E}|Du|\leq C\bigg[\frac{\omega}{r}+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\mu\bigg]\eqqcolon\lambda

and the gradient Hölder estimate

(4.3)

\displaystyle|Du(z_{1})-Du(z_{2})|\leq C\lambda\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda)}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}},

where
\omega\coloneqq\essosc\limits_{\Omega_{T}}u
.

4.1.
An
a priori
gradient estimate

In this paragraph, we pursue our goal of an
a priori
gradient estimate for weak solutions to parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations. We specifically consider equations of the following type

(4.4)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-\divv A(t,Du)=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$,}

where the the vector field

is independent of the spatial variable

and

In the following, we consider
Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})\Subset\Omega_{T}
and a parameter

\displaystyle E^{(\lambda),i}_{\rho}(z_{0},\nu)\coloneqq\big\{z\in Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0}):\,|D_{i}u(z)|>(1-\nu)\lambda_{\mu}\big\}.

The main result in this section is the following Campanato type estimate.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

,
\mu\in(0,1]
, and

and a Hölder exponent
\beta\in(0,1)

to (
4.4

(4.7)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{r}(z_{0})}\big|Du-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0};r}\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{p\beta}\lambda^{p}_{\mu}

holds true for at least one

Conversely, in the degenerate regime, we consider a subset of points with

far from its supremum, which is large in measure.

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{r}(z_{0})}|Du-\Gamma_{z_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{2\beta}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}

) holds true. We note that in view of equivalence of norms, the general bound (
4.5
) yields an estimate for the excess

(4.18)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho)\leq 4n\lambda^{2}_{\mu}.

We will now show inductively that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
there holds

(4.19)

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta^{i}\rho)\leq\theta^{2\beta i}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho),

where we refer to
\eqref{induktioneins}_{i}
in the
i
-th step respectively. We commence by treating the case
i=1
. The choice of
\theta
and an application of Lemma
4.6
yields

\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta\rho)\leq\tilde{C}\theta^{2\tilde{\beta}}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho)=\theta^{2\beta}\tilde{C}\theta^{2(\tilde{\beta}-\beta)}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho)\leq\theta^{2\beta}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho),

which establishes that
\eqref{induktioneins}_{1}
holds true. Next, we consider the case
i>1
and assume that
\eqref{induktioneins}_{i-1}
is satisfied. Additionally, we apply Lemma
4.6
and utilize the choice of
\theta
once more, to obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta^{i}\rho)\leq\tilde{C}\theta^{2\tilde{\beta}}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho)\leq\tilde{C}\theta^{2\tilde{\beta}}\theta^{2\beta(i-1)}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho)\leq\theta^{2\beta i}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\rho).

This establishes
\eqref{induktioneins}_{i}
. We continue by exploiting
\eqref{induktioneins}_{i-1}
together with
(
4.18
)
and again the choice of
\theta
, which yields

(4.20)

\displaystyle\big|(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{i}\rho}-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\theta^{i}\rho}(z_{0})}\big|Du-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho}\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\theta^{-(n+2)}\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta^{i-1}\rho)

\displaystyle\leq 4n\theta^{-(n+2)}\theta^{2\beta(i-1)}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}.

Let us now consider natural numbers
l<m
and take roots in the preceding estimate (
4.20
). Due to the fact that
\theta^{\beta}\leq\frac{1}{2}
, we thus obtain

(4.21)

\displaystyle\big|(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{m}\rho}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{j=l+1}^{m}\big|(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{j}\rho}-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{j-1}\rho}\big|

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{n}\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+2)}\lambda_{\mu}\sum\limits_{j=l+1}^{m}\theta^{\beta(j-1)}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{n}\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+2)}\lambda_{\mu}\frac{\theta^{\beta l}}{(1-\theta^{\beta})}

\displaystyle\leq 4\sqrt{n}\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+2)}\theta^{\beta l}\lambda_{\mu}.

After passing to the limit
l\to\infty
in the preceding estimate, we have established that
\big((Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{j}\rho}\big)_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let us denote its limit by

\Gamma_{z_{0}}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{j\to\infty}(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{j}\rho}.

Next, we pass to the limit
m\to\infty
in (
4.21
) to derive

\big|(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}-\Gamma_{z_{0}}\big|\leq 4\sqrt{n}\theta^{-\frac{1}{2}(n+2)}\theta^{\beta l}\lambda_{\mu}\qquad\mbox{for any\penalty\ $l\in\mathbb{N}$}.

Together with estimate
(
4.18
)
and
\eqref{induktioneins}_{l}
, the following excess-decay estimate for the cylinders
Q^{(\lambda)}_{\theta^{l}\rho}(z_{0})
, where
l\in\mathbb{N}
, can be inferred from the preceding inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\theta^{l}\rho}(z_{0})}|Du-\Gamma_{z_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\Phi_{\lambda}(z_{0},\theta^{l}\rho)+2\big|\Gamma_{z_{0}}-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},\theta^{l}\rho}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\theta^{2\beta l}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}+32n\theta^{-(n+2)}\theta^{2\beta l}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{2\beta l}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}

with a constant
C=C(n,p,L,C_{1},C_{2})
. As a last step, this estimate is converted into the excess-decay (
4.12
). Let
r\in(0,\rho]
and choose
l\in\mathbb{N}
, such that
\theta^{l+1}\rho<r\leq\theta^{l}\rho
holds true. The choice of
\theta
and the preceding estimate yield

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{r}(z_{0})}|Du-\Gamma_{z_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\theta^{n+2}}\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\theta^{l}\rho}(z_{0})}|Du-\Gamma_{z_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\theta^{2\beta(l+1)}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{2\beta}\lambda^{2}_{\mu}

with
C=C(n,p,L,C_{1},C_{2})
. After an application of Jensen’s inequality, this implies

\displaystyle\big|(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},r}-\Gamma_{z_{0}}\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{r}(z_{0})}|Du-\Gamma_{z_{0}}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{r}{\rho}\Big)^{2\beta}\lambda^{2}_{\mu},

which further leads to

\displaystyle\Gamma_{z_{0}}=\lim\limits_{r\downarrow 0}(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{z_{0},r}.

Finally, the bounds (
4.13
) are an immediate consequence of Lemma
4.6
and assumption (
4.5
).
∎

4.1.2.
The degenerate regime

Our objective in this section is the proof of Proposition
4.5
, which presents the main result in the degenerate regime. The fundamental measure-theoretic information in this section is given by

\displaystyle\big|Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})\setminus E^{(\lambda),i}_{\rho}(z_{0},\nu)\big|\geq\nu\big|Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}(z_{0})\big|

for any
i=1,\ldots,n
. To commence, we will establish that a certain function of
D_{i}u
, where
i=1,...,n
, is a sub-solution to a linear parabolic equation. By incorporating further modifications, we subsequently obtain a weak sub-solution to a linear parabolic equation that is uniformly elliptic. Through a standard argument, two parabolic De Giorgi class type estimates are then obtained, which serve as the principal tool for the subsequent technique of expansion of positivity.

We start with the following lemma, which asserts that for any
i=1,...,n
, the function

\Big(|D_{i}u|^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\mu}}{4}\Big)^{2}_{+}

is a weak sub-solution to a linear parabolic equation. For this matter, we recall the bilinear form
\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}
introduced in (
2.3
).

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
u
be a weak solution to (
4.4
) and consider the function

\displaystyle v\coloneqq\Big(|D_{i}u|^{2}-\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\mu}}{4}\Big)^{2}_{+},

where
i\in\{1,...,n\}
is arbitrary. Then,
v
is a weak sub-solution to a linear parabolic equation in the sense that
v
satisfies the integral inequality

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\big[-v\partial_{t}\phi+\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}(Du)(Dv,D\phi)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq 0

for any non-negative test function

\phi\in W^{1,2}_{0}\big(\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(t_{0});L^{2}\big(B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\big)\big)\cap L^{2}\big(\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(t_{0});W^{1,2}_{0}\big(B_{2\rho}(x_{0})\big)\big).

Proof..

We differentiate the weak form of (
4.1
) by testing the latter with the function
\varphi\coloneqq D_{i}\big[\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\big]
. Here,
\Phi\in W^{1,\infty}_{\loc}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})
denotes a non-negative and non-decreasing locally Lipschitz function, while
\phi\in C^{1}_{0}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0}))
is an arbitrary non-negative smooth cut-off function. By omitting a Steklov-average procedure, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}

\displaystyle\Big[u\partial_{t}\big[D_{i}\big[\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\big]\big]

\displaystyle-\big\langle A(t,Du),D_{i}\big[D\big[\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\big]\big]\big\rangle\Big]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=0.

Integrating by parts in both, the term involving the time derivative and also the diffusion term, leads to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}

\displaystyle\Big[\partial_{t}[D_{i}u]\,\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})

\displaystyle+\big\langle D_{i}A(x,t,Du),D\big[\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\big]\big\rangle\Big]\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t=0.

First, we treat the term involving the time derivative by following the approach taken in
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
. Thus, we achieve

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\partial_{t}[D_{i}u]\,\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\partial_{t}|D_{i}u|^{2}\phi\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\partial_{t}v\,\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}v\partial_{t}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Next, we turn our attention to the diffusion term, where we exploit the fact that

D_{i}uD_{i}Du=\frac{1}{2}D|D_{i}u|^{2}\qquad\mbox{a.e. in\penalty\ $Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})$}.

Moreover, we utilize the bilinearity of the bilinearform
\mathcal{B}
, to obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}

\displaystyle\big\langle D_{i}A(t,Du),D\big[\phi D_{i}u\,\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\big]\big\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\mathcal{B}(Du)(D_{i}Du,D_{i}Du)\rangle\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{B}(Du)(D|D_{i}u|^{2},D\phi)\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{B}(Du)(D|D_{i}u|^{2},D|D_{i}u|^{2})\Phi^{\prime}(|D_{i}u|^{2})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

The first quantity on the right-hand side of the preceding estimate may be bounded below by exploiting structure condition
(\ref{voraussetzungen})_{2}
.
Therefore, by combining our calculations for the term involving the time derivative and the diffusion term, we arrive at

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint

{}_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\Big[-\frac{1}{2}v\partial_{t}\phi+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{B}(Du)(D|D_{i}u|^{2},D\phi)\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}C_{2}h_{\mu}(|Du|)|D_{i}Du|^{2}\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})}\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{B}(Du)(D|D_{i}u|^{2},D|D_{i}u|^{2})\Phi^{\prime}(|D_{i}u|^{2})\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 0.

Finally, we choose the locally Lipschitz function

\Phi(t)\coloneqq 2\Big(t-\frac{\lambda^{2}_{\mu}}{4}\Big)_{+}

and note that
Dv=D|D_{i}u|^{2}\Phi(|D_{i}u|^{2})
a.e. in
Q^{(\lambda)}_{2\rho}(z_{0})
. Due to a non-negative contribution of the second and third integral term on the left-hand side of the preceding inequality, which can be inferred from Lemma
2.14
, and the fact that
\Phi
is non-decreasing, we discard both quantities to derive that
v
is a weak sub-solution to the linear parabolic equation given above.
∎

Remark 4.8
.

The employment of Lemma
4.7
allows us to directly address the quantity
|D_{i}u|
, for
i=1,...,n
, in light of the measure-theoretic information (
4.14
). In contrast to previous results in the literature, such as those presented in
[
35
,
36
]
for the parabolic case and
[
42
]
for the elliptic case, which required a separate analysis based on the sign of

, our approach offers a simplified strategy in a unified manner.

Next, we re-scale

and
\phi
to
\tilde{u}
and
\tilde{\phi}
, defined on
Q_{2\rho}\coloneqq B_{2\rho}\times(-(2\rho)^{2},0]
, by setting

\displaystyle\tilde{u}(x,\tau)\coloneqq\frac{u(x_{0}+x,t_{0}+\lambda^{2-p}\tau)}{\lambda_{\mu}},\qquad\tilde{\phi}(x,\tau)\coloneqq\phi(x_{0}+x,t_{0}+\lambda^{2-p}\tau).

As Lemma
4.7
holds true for any arbitrary
i\in\{1,...,n\}
, throughout this section we fix one such
i\in\{1,...,n\}
. Moreover, we define

(4.22)

\displaystyle w(x,\tau)\coloneqq\frac{7}{16}+\frac{v(x_{0}+x,t_{0}+\lambda^{2-p}\tau)}{\lambda^{4}_{\mu}}=\frac{7}{16}+\Big(|D_{i}\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\Big)^{2}_{+}

for

. In this setting, assumption (
4.5
) implies the bounds

(4.23)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{Q_{2\rho}}|D_{i}\tilde{u}|\leq 1\quad\&\quad\esssup\limits_{Q_{2\rho}}w\leq 1

and due to Lemma
4.7
we infer, by a change of variables together by exploiting the bilinearity of
\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}
, that
w
is a sub-solution to a re-scaled linear parabolic equation and satisfies the integral inequality

(4.24)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{2\rho}}\big[-w\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}+\tilde{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(Dw,D\tilde{\phi})\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq 0

for any non-negative

\tilde{\phi}\in W^{1,2}_{0}\big((-(2\rho)^{2},0]);L^{2}(B_{2\rho})\big)\cap L^{2}\big((-(2\rho)^{2},0]);W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{2\rho})\big).

Here, we abbreviated

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(x,\tau)(\xi,\eta)\coloneqq\lambda^{2-p}\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}(\lambda_{\mu}D\tilde{u}(x,\tau))(\xi,\eta)

for
(x,\tau)\in Q_{2\rho}
, and
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We note that
w
remains constant on the set

and thus does not impact the diffusion part. Due to the set inclusion

\Big\{(x,\tau)\in Q_{2\rho}\colon\penalty\ |D\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\}\subset\Big\{(x,\tau)\in Q_{2\rho}\colon\penalty\ |D_{i}\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big\},

we may redefine
\tilde{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}
by incorporating

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(x,\tau)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{n}&\mbox{on $\big\{(x,\tau)\in Q_{2\rho}\colon\penalty\ |D_{i}\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|\leq\frac{1}{2}\big\}$}\\
\tilde{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(x,\tau)&\mbox{on $\big\{(x,\tau)\in Q_{2\rho}\colon\penalty\ |D_{i}\tilde{u}(x,\tau)|>\frac{1}{2}\big\}$},\end{cases}

where
\hat{\mathbf{I}}_{n}(\xi,\eta)\coloneqq\langle\mathbf{I}_{n}\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\xi,\eta\rangle
for any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This way, the preceding weak form translates to

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{2\rho}}\big[-w\partial_{t}\tilde{\phi}+\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}\big(Dw,D\tilde{\phi}\big)\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq 0

for any non-negative test function

\tilde{\phi}\in W^{1,2}_{0}\big((-(2\rho)^{2},0]);L^{2}(B_{\rho})\big)\cap L^{2}\big((-(2\rho)^{2},0]);W^{1,2}_{0}(B_{2\rho})\big).

Furthermore, the coefficients

\displaystyle\frac{1}{C}|\zeta|^{2}\leq\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(x,\tau)(\zeta,\zeta)\leq C|\zeta|^{2}

) clearly holds true with constant

and
|\cdot|_{\infty}
on

as well as assumption (

4.25

p,L,C_{2}
. In turn, this yields (
4.25
) with the claimed dependence of

. Concluding, both inequalities (

is a weak sub-solution to a linear parabolic equation with uniformly elliptic and bounded coefficients. This fact enables us to derive two energy estimates, which serve as the starting point for a measure-theoretic approach inspired by De Giorgi. To maintain simplicity, we return to employ

as the time variable of
w
throughout the subsequent discussion.

Lemma 4.9

(A De Giorgi class type estimate for a sub-solution)
.

Let
w
denote the weak sub-solution defined in (
4.22
). There exists
C=C(n,p,L,C_{1},C_{2})
, such that for any
k>0
,
\sigma\in(0,1)
, and any cylinder
Q_{R,S}(0,\tau_{0})=B_{R}\times(\tau_{0}-S,\tau_{0}]\subset Q_{\rho}
there hold

(4.26)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{\tau\in(\tau_{0}-\sigma S,\tau_{0}]}\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma R}\times\{\tau\}}(w-k)^{2}_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x+\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{\sigma R,\sigma S}(0,\tau_{0})}|D(w-k)_{+}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}(0,\tau_{0})}\bigg[\frac{(w-k)^{2}_{+}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}R^{2}}+\frac{(w-k)^{2}_{+}}{(1-\sigma)S}\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

and

(4.27)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{\tau\in(\tau_{0}-S,\tau_{0}]}\displaystyle

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\sigma R}\times\{\tau\}}(w-k)^{2}_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}\times\{\tau_{0}-S\}}(w-k)^{2}_{+}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+C\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}(0,\tau_{0})}\frac{(w-k)^{2}_{+}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}R^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Proof..

To simplify notation, we write
Q_{R,S}
instead of
Q_{R,S}(0,\tau_{0})
. We test the weak form (
4.24
) with
\phi=\varphi^{2}\chi(w-k)_{+}
, where
\varphi\in C^{1}_{0}(Q_{R},[0,1])
denotes a spatial cut-off function satisfying
\varphi\equiv 1
on
B_{\sigma R}
and
|D\varphi|\leq\frac{2}{(1-\sigma)R}
. Furthermore,
\chi\in W^{1,\infty}((\tau_{0}-S,\tau_{0}],[0,1])
denotes a Lipschitz cut-off function in time vanishing at the end points of the interval
(\tau_{0}-S,\tau_{0}]
. By initially considering the quantity involving the time derivative and omitting a Steklov-average procedure, we obtain

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}w\partial_{t}\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\partial_{t}w\phi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\partial_{t}(w-k)^{2}_{+}\varphi^{2}\chi\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\partial_{t}\chi(w-k)^{2}_{+}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Next, the diffusion part is estimated by

\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(Dw,D\phi)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(Dw,\chi(w-k)_{+}2\varphi D\varphi+\chi\varphi^{2}D(w-k)_{+})\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\big[2\chi\varphi(w-k)_{+}\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(D(w-k)_{+},D\varphi)+\chi\varphi^{2}\hat{\mathbf{\mathcal{B}}}(D(w-k)_{+},D(w-k)_{+})\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\bigg[\frac{1}{C}\varphi^{2}\chi|D(w-k)_{+}|^{2}-C\varphi\chi(w-k)_{+}|D(w-k)_{+}||D\varphi|\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{C}\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\varphi^{2}\chi|D(w-k)_{+}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t-C\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\frac{(w-k)^{2}_{+}}{(1-\sigma)^{2}R^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

with
C=C(p,L,C_{1},C_{2})
. In the penultimate step we utilized (
4.25
) and then applied Young’s inequality. Combining both estimates for the term involving the time derivative and the diffusion term respectively, we end up with

(4.28)

\displaystyle-\displaystyle\iint_{Q_{R,S}}\partial_{t}\chi(w-k)^{2}_{+}\varphi^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\mathbf{II}
, we have

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{II}=\displaystyle\iint_{B_{S}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}S^{2},t_{1})}\partial_{t}\zeta^{p}|u-\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Next, we will treat the diffusion term. By exploiting structure condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
, Lemma
2.8
and Lemma
2.10
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lim\limits_{h\downarrow 0}\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\langle[A(x,t,Du)]_{h},D\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\psi_{\varepsilon}[\langle A(x,t,Du)-A(x,t,0),Du\rangle\zeta^{p}+\langle A(x,t,0),Du\rangle\zeta^{p}]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\langle A(x,t,Du),D\zeta\rangle p\zeta^{p-1}(u-\xi)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq C\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\Big[(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|Du|^{2}\zeta^{p}-\mu^{p-1}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{p}\Big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-C\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}p\zeta^{p-1}\psi_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p-1}{2}}|D\zeta||u-\xi|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\geq C\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\psi_{\varepsilon}\zeta^{p}\big[(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}-\mu^{p}\big]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\quad-C\displaystyle\iint_{\Omega_{T}}\psi_{\varepsilon}|D\zeta|^{p}|u-\xi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
. In the last step, we first applied Lemma
2.10
, and subsequently Young’s inequality twice with exponents
(p,\frac{p}{p-1})
and a suitable choice of constants in order to reabsorb the term involving the spatial derivative
Du
twice. Passing to the limit
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
and combining both estimates for the quantity involving the time derivative and for the diffusion term respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{S}(x_{0})\times\{t_{1}\}}\zeta^{p}|u-\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\displaystyle\iint_{B_{S}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}S^{2},t_{1})}\zeta^{p}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\displaystyle\iint_{B_{S}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}S^{2},t_{1})}[|D\zeta|^{p}|u-\xi|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t+\partial_{t}\zeta^{p}|u-\xi|^{2}+\mu^{p}]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
. At this point, a standard argument finishes the proof. On the left-hand side we first discard the second integral and afterwards take the essential supremum with respect to
t_{1}\in t_{0}+\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{R}
. Due to the assumptions made on the cut-off function
\zeta
, we end up with

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{t\in(t_{0}-\lambda^{2-p}R^{2},t_{0}]}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}|u-\xi|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\displaystyle\iint_{z_{0}+Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\displaystyle\iint_{z_{0}+Q^{(\lambda)}_{S}}\bigg[\frac{|u-\xi|^{p}}{(S-R)^{p}}+\lambda^{p-2}\frac{|u-\xi|^{2}}{S^{2}-R^{2}}+\mu^{p}\bigg]\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
. Finally, taking mean values on both sides, together with exploiting the assumption
\frac{1}{2}S\leq R<S
, finishes the proof.
∎

4.3.
Schauder estimates for parabolic
p
-Laplacian type equations

In this section, we will show that the gradient of any bounded weak solution
u
to (
4.1
) in the case
\mu\in(0,1]
and under the given structure conditions (
1.2
), exhibits local Hölder continuity within
\Omega_{T}
. The strategy is to start by freezing the spatial variable
x
, allowing us to utilize the
a priori
gradient regularity result from
4.2
for a weak solution to a Cauchy-Dirichlet problem involving a more regular equation. This unique weak solution serves as a comparison function for the solution
u
itself. Exploiting the comparison estimates in
4.2.1
, the regularity of the comparison function is then transferred to the weak solution
u
itself. We assume
a priori
throughout this section that
|Du|\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T})
holds true. The main result of this section is summarized in form of the following theorem.

Theorem 4.18
.

Let
u
be a bounded weak solution to (
4.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
) in the range
p>1
and
\mu\in(0,1]
. Furthermore, assume that there holds
|Du|\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T})
. Then, there exist a Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
and a constant
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)\geq 1
, such that there holds

Du\in C^{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}/2}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T},\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Moreover, for any
E\subset\Omega_{T}
, such that
r\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\dist_{p}(E,\partial_{p}\Omega_{T})>0
and any
z_{1},z_{2}\in E
there hold
the quantitative gradient estimate

(4.40)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{E}|Du|\leq C\bigg[\frac{\omega}{r}+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\mu\bigg]\eqqcolon\lambda

and the gradient Hölder estimate

(4.41)

\displaystyle|Du(z_{1})-Du(z_{2})|\leq C\lambda\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda)}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}},

where
\omega\coloneqq\essosc\limits_{\Omega_{T}}u
.

Proof..

Instead of providing a detailed proof of Theorem
4.18
, we will only outline the major steps since our approach closely resembles the approach taken for the prototype equation, which is performed in
[
6
, 10.5 Schauder estimates for Lipschitz solutions]
. We start by freezing the spatial variable. Let us consider nested cylinders
Q_{\rho_{1}}(\hat{z})\subset Q_{\rho_{2}}(\hat{z})\Subset\Omega_{T}
for arbitrary radii
0<\rho_{1}<\rho_{2}\leq 1
. Further, let
\lambda\geq\mu>0
, and assume that the following upper bound for the gradient is satisfied

(4.42)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{Q_{\rho_{2}}(\hat{z})}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\lambda.

We consider a radius
\rho\in(0,\frac{\rho_{0}}{8})
,
where

\rho_{0}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\min\big\{1,\lambda^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}).

This choice implies that for any
z_{0}\in Q_{\rho_{1}}(\hat{z})
there holds
Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}(z_{0})\subset Q_{\rho_{2}}(\hat{z})
, as long as
\frac{R}{2}\leq\rho_{0}
. For some
\kappa\in(0,1)
fixed later, we set

(4.43)

\displaystyle R\coloneqq\Big(\frac{8\rho}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\kappa}\rho_{0}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\rho=\frac{1}{8}\Big(\frac{R}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{\kappa}}\rho_{0},

which implies
\rho<\frac{R}{8}
and also
R<\rho_{0}
. Since
z_{0}
is now fixed, we will omit the vertex
z_{0}
from all cylinders in the following. As before, let

v\in C\big(\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{R};L^{2}(B_{R})\big)\cap L^{p}\big(\Lambda^{(\lambda)}_{R};W^{1,p}(B_{R})\big)

denote the unique weak solution to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

(4.44)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v-\divv A(x_{0},t,Dv)=0&\,\,\mbox{in $Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}$,}\\[3.0pt]
v=u&\,\,\mbox{on $\partial_{p}Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}$},\\[3.0pt]
\end{cases}

where the boundary values
u
are taken in the sense of Definition
2.2
.

Our next aim is to utilize the
a priori
estimate (
4.7
) from Theorem
4.2
and derive a Campanato type estimate for the comparison function
v
first. Since
u\in L^{\infty}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{R})
and due to
v=u
on
\partial_{p}Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}
, the comparison principle from Lemma
4.13
implies that
v\in L^{\infty}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{R})
and, moreover, yields the estimate

\essosc\limits_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}v\leq\essosc\limits_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}u\eqqcolon\omega^{(\lambda)}_{R}.

We note that structure conditions (
4.2.1
) are satisfied for
A(x,t,\xi)
and
A(x_{0},t,\xi)
due to Lemma
2.8
. Therefore, we are in position to apply Lemma
4.12
and the mean value theorem. This yields

(4.45)

\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}|Du-Dv|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|A(\cdot,Du)-A(x_{0},\cdot,Du)\right\|^{p^{\prime}}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{R})}

\displaystyle\quad+\scalebox{1.3}{$\chi$}_{(1,2)}(p)\,C\left\|A(\cdot,Du)-A(x_{0},\cdot,Du)\right\|^{p}_{L^{p^{\prime}}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{R})}\bigg[\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{2-p}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{\alpha_{*}p}\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

as well as the estimate

(4.46)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\displaystyle\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

with
C=C(n,p,C_{2},C_{3})
, and

(4.47)

\displaystyle\alpha_{*}=\begin{cases}\alpha,&\mbox{if $1<p<2$}\\
\frac{\alpha}{p-1},&\mbox{if $p\geq 2$}.\end{cases}

Note that by defining
\alpha_{*}
, we exploited the assumption
R\leq 1
. Next, we aim to verify that condition (
4.5
) is satisfied for the comparison solution
v
. Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
imply
|Dv|\in L^{\infty}(Q^{(\lambda)}_{R})
for any
p>1
. Furthermore, there exist quantitative estimates. In the range
1<p<2
, Proposition
3.1
yields for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
the estimate

(4.48)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\frac{R}{4}}}|Dv|\leq C\varepsilon\lambda

\displaystyle+\frac{C\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{\theta}}\bigg[\Big(\frac{\omega^{(\lambda)}_{R}}{R\lambda}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\frac{\omega^{(\lambda)}_{R}}{R\lambda}+\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\bigg]^{\frac{n(2-p)+2p}{4p}}

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg[\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2p}}

with
\theta=\theta(n,p)>0
, and
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
. Note that
C
indeed is independent of structure constant
C_{6}
due to the frozen spatial variable
x_{0}
in the vector field
A(x_{0},t,\xi)
, such that assumption
\eqref{voraussetzungendiffbarkeit}_{3}
holds true with the choice
C_{6}=0
. By employing the oscillation estimate from Lemma
4.16
, together with inequality (
4.42
), we have the bounds

\frac{\omega^{(\lambda)}_{R}}{R}\leq C(n,C_{1})\lambda,\qquad\frac{\mu}{\lambda}\leq 1.

Thus, the reasoning above and inequality (
4.46
) imply for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
the following

(4.49)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\frac{R}{4}}}|Dv|\leq C\varepsilon\lambda

\displaystyle+\frac{C\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\varepsilon^{\theta}}\bigg[\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1}{2p}},

where
\theta=\theta(n,p)>0
, and
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
. In the case
p\geq 2
, Proposition
3.2
together with (
4.42
) and (
4.46
),
yield for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
the very same estimate (
4.49
). In what follows, we will consistently refer to equation (
4.49
) in a comprehensive manner encompassing both scenarios, where
1<p<2
and
p\geq 2
. An application of (
4.49
) with the choice
\varepsilon=1
and exploiting the bound (
4.42
) yields the following estimate

(4.50)

\esssup\limits_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\frac{R}{4}}}(\mu^{2}+|Dv|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mu+2C\lambda\leq C\lambda

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})\geq 1
. Due to (
4.50
), we are in position to apply the
a priori
gradient estimate from Theorem
4.2
with
L\coloneqq C
. The latter yields the bound

\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}}\big|Dv-(Dv)^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{\rho}{R}\Big)^{p\beta}\lambda^{p}

for any
0<\rho<\frac{R}{8}
, with
\beta=\beta(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})\in(0,1)
, and
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})>0
. Combining inequality (
4.49
) and the preceding
a priori
gradient estimate, this yields for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]

(4.51)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}}

\displaystyle\big|Dv-(Dv)^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}\big|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\frac{\rho}{R}\Big)^{\beta}\bigg[\varepsilon^{p-1}\lambda^{p}+\varepsilon^{-\hat{\theta}}\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{R}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

with
\hat{\theta}=\hat{\theta}(n,p)
, and
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2})
.

Subsequently, as already mentioned above, we derive a campanato type estimate for the spatial derivative of the weak solution
u
itself. Due to the Campanato type estimate (
4.51
), we are in position to exploit the comparison inequality (
4.45
) and the comparison estimates from Lemma
4.12
. This necessitates the selection of the free parameter
\kappa
as

\kappa\coloneqq\frac{n+2+\beta}{n+2+\beta+\alpha_{*}p}

and the Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}
as

(4.52)

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}\coloneqq\frac{\alpha_{*}\beta}{2(n+2+\beta+\alpha_{*}p)}\in(0,1).

Accordingly, the Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}
only depends on the data
n,p,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha
, but is independent of structure constant
C_{3}
. Furthermore, we obtain for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0})
and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
the estimate

(4.53)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}}\big|Du-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}\big|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\alpha_{0}p}\bigg[\varepsilon^{p-1}\lambda^{p}+\varepsilon^{-\tilde{\theta}p}\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]

with
\tilde{\theta}>0
, and
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
.
In particular, the choice
\varepsilon=1
and the upper bound (
4.42
), together with the preceding estimate, imply

(4.54)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}}\big|Du-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{\rho}\big|\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\leq C\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\alpha_{0}p}\lambda^{p}

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3})
.

Next, a local gradient bound for
u
can be obtained. First, we achieve that the limit

\Gamma_{z_{0}}\coloneqq\lim\limits_{i\to\infty}(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{r_{i}},

where
r_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\rho_{0}}{2^{i}}
and
\rho_{0}
denotes the radius from the Campanato type estimate (
4.53
), exists and that for any
\rho\in(0,\rho_{0}]
and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
, there holds

(4.55)

\displaystyle\big|\Gamma_{z_{0}}-(Du)^{(\lambda)}_{r}\big|\leq C\Big(\frac{\rho}{\rho_{0}}\Big)^{\alpha_{0}}\Bigg[\varepsilon^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\lambda+\varepsilon^{-\tilde{\theta}}\bigg[\displaystyle\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-6.23328pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-5.58163pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.09418pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{-}\mkern-3.5mu{-}$}}\kern-3.32918pt}}\!\iint_{Q^{(\lambda)}_{\rho_{0}}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}\Bigg]

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
, where
\tilde{\theta}>0
still denotes the parameter from estimate (
4.53
). We note that the constant
C
additionally exhibits a dependence on the Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. This reasoning shows that
\Gamma_{z_{0}}
is the Lebesgue representative of
Du
in
z_{0}
. To obtain an upper bound for the gradient, we define

(4.56)

\displaystyle r\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\dist_{\p}(E,\partial_{p}\Omega_{T}),

where
E\subset\Omega_{T}
. With this choice, there holds
Q_{2r}(\hat{z})\Subset\Omega_{T}
for any
\hat{z}\in E
. Setting

E_{\p}\coloneqq\displaystyle\bigcup\limits_{\hat{z}\in E}Q_{r}(\hat{z}),

the energy estimate from Lemma
4.17
in turn yields the bound

(4.57)

\esssup\limits_{E_{\p}}|Du|<\esssup\limits_{E_{\p}}(\mu^{2}+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\bigg[\frac{\omega}{r}+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\mu\bigg]

with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
, where
\omega\coloneqq\essosc\limits_{\Omega_{T}}u
. Since
E\subset E_{p}
, this establishes the local gradient bound (
4.40
).

Finally, the local Hölder continuity of the gradient
Du
can be obtained, due the fact that inequalities (
4.42
), (
4.54
), (
4.55
), (
4.57
), the definition of
r
, and the assumption
|Du|\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T})
all are at our disposal. In turn, this leads to the gradient Hölder estimate (
4.41
) and finishes the proof idea of Theorem
4.18
.
∎

4.4.
Approximation: The case
\mu=0

In this section, we will relinquish the assumption
|Du|\in L^{\infty}_{\loc}(\Omega_{T})
from the previous section and treat the missing case
\mu=0
by an approximation argument. As a result, we derive Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let the vector field
A
satisfy structure conditions (
1.2
) in the case where
\mu=0
. Further, let
E\subset\Omega_{T}
, such that
r\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\dist_{p}(E,\partial_{p}\Omega_{T})>0
holds true. The idea is to approximate the vector field
A
by a sequence of vector fields
A_{\varepsilon_{j}}
satisfying the set of assumptions (
1.2
) and additionally
\eqref{voraussetzungendiffbarkeit}_{3}
for some parameter
\varepsilon_{j}>0
. We consider the subset
\tilde{E}\coloneqq\{x\in E:\,\text{dist}(x,\partial E)>\varepsilon\}
for a parameter
0<\varepsilon<r
and take some sequence
(\varepsilon_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\in(0,\varepsilon)
with
\varepsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
as
j\to\infty
. Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
denote a standard mollifier in space with
\int_{B_{1}}\phi(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=1
and consider its scaled version
\phi_{\varepsilon_{j}}(\cdot)\coloneqq\varepsilon^{-n}_{j}\phi(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon_{j}})
that is compactly supported in
B_{\varepsilon_{j}}
. We define the
i
-th component of the regularized vector field
A_{\varepsilon_{j}}
by

A_{\varepsilon_{j},i}(x,t,\xi)\coloneqq\int_{B_{\varepsilon_{j}}}A_{i}(y,t,\xi)\phi_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x-y)\,\mathrm{d}y

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\tilde{E}_{T}\coloneqq\tilde{E}\times(0,T)
,
i=1,...,n
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is finite due to the
p
-growth assumption
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
on
A
. The Hölder regularity with respect to the spatial variable
x
of
A
according to assumption
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
yields the estimate

(4.58)

|A_{i}(x,t,\xi)-A_{\varepsilon_{j},i}(x,t,\xi)|\leq C_{3}\varepsilon^{\alpha}_{j}|\xi|^{\frac{p-1}{2}}

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\tilde{E}_{T}
,
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In order to perform the approximation, we consider unique weak solutions

u_{j}\in C\big([0,T];L^{2}(\tilde{E})\big)\cap L^{p}\big(0,T;W^{1,p}(\tilde{E})\big)

to the Cauchy-Dirichlet problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u_{j}-\divv\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x,t,Du_{j})=0&\,\,\mbox{in $\tilde{E}$,}\\[3.0pt]
u_{j}=u&\,\,\mbox{on $\partial_{p}\tilde{E}_{T}$,}\\[3.0pt]
\end{cases}

where the approximating vector fields are given by

(4.59)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x,t,\xi)\coloneqq A_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x,t,\xi)+\varepsilon_{j}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi

for
(x,t)\in\tilde{E}_{T}
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The solutions
u_{j}
will serve as comparison functions for
u
. Note that by construction,
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
additionally admits a bounded derivative with respect to the spatial variable
x
, i.e. condition
\eqref{voraussetzungendiffbarkeit}_{3}
is satisfied due to the bound

|\partial_{x}\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j},i}(x,t,\xi)|\leq\frac{C_{1}\|D\phi\|_{L^{1}}}{\varepsilon_{j}}|\xi|^{p-1}

for any
i=1,...,n
,
(x,t)\in\tilde{E}_{T}
, and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Further, a direct calculation verifies

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi}\Big[\varepsilon_{j}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi\Big]=\varepsilon_{j}\Big[(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\mathbf{I}_{n}+(p-2)(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-4}{2}}(\xi\otimes\xi)\Big].

Due to our construction, the approximating vector fields
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
satisfy the
p
-growth structure conditions (
1.2
) with the parameter
\mu=0
replaced by
\varepsilon_{j}
, and with positive structure constants
\tilde{C}_{1}=\tilde{C}_{1}(p,C_{1}),\tilde{C}_{2}=\tilde{C}_{2}(p,C_{2}),C_{3}
, and a Hölder exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
. In particular, the ellipticity condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{2}
holds true with a constant
\tilde{C}_{2}
that is independent of the approximating parameter
\varepsilon_{j}
, i.e. we have

\langle\partial_{\xi}\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x,t,\xi)\eta,\eta\rangle\geq\tilde{C}(p,C_{2})(\varepsilon^{2}_{j}+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}|\eta|^{2}

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\tilde{E}_{T}
, and any
\xi,\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. In fact, it is worth noting that for the treatment of the super-quadratic case where
p>2
, we need to replace the additional term
\varepsilon_{j}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi
in the approximating vector fields
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
with the quantity
\varepsilon^{p-2}_{j}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi
. This adjustment allows us to obtain homogeneous estimates, and through a convexity argument, it can be verified that the vector fields
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
indeed satisfy the
p
-growth structure conditions (
1.2
). However, in order to avoid a separate treatment of the cases where
p\leq 2
and
p>2
, we adopt a unified approach by only considering the vector fields
\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}
with the quantity
\varepsilon_{j}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\xi
as described above. Consequently, we apply the comparison estimate (
4.35
) from Lemma
4.12
with the vector field
V(x,t,\xi)=\tilde{A}_{\varepsilon_{j}}(x,t,\xi)
and with structure constants
C_{7}=\tilde{C}_{1}
,
C_{8}=\tilde{C}_{2}
. Together with growth condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
, this yields for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
the following estimate

\displaystyle\displaystyle\iint_{\tilde{E}_{T}}|Du-Du_{j}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\varepsilon_{j}^{\alpha p}+\varepsilon_{j}^{\alpha p^{\prime}}+\varepsilon^{p}_{j}+\varepsilon^{p^{\prime}}_{j}\big)\displaystyle\iint_{\tilde{E}_{T}}(1+|Du|^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}

with
C=C(p,C_{2})
. Passing to the limit
j\to\infty
in the preceding estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle Du_{j}\to Du\qquad\mbox{in $L^{p}(\tilde{E}_{T},\mathbb{R}^{n})$\,\,as $j\to\infty$}.

Furthermore, Lemma
2.8
ensures that the assumptions of the comparison principle from Lemma
4.13
are satisfied, and we obtain the bound

(4.60)

\displaystyle\essosc\limits_{\tilde{E}_{T}}u_{j}\leq\essosc\limits_{\tilde{E}_{T}}u<\infty\qquad\mbox{for any $j\in\mathbb{N}$}.

The preceding estimate implies
u_{j}\in L^{\infty}(\tilde{E}_{T})
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, the gradient bounds from Proposition
3.1
and Proposition
3.2
are at our disposal, and we infer
|Du_{j}|\in L^{\infty}(\tilde{E}_{T})
for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
. At this point, we are in position to apply Theorem
4.18
. In particular, we obtain

(4.61)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{E}|Du_{j}|\leq C\bigg[\frac{\omega_{j}}{r}+\Big(\frac{\omega_{j}}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}+\varepsilon_{j}\bigg]\eqqcolon\lambda_{j}

and

(4.62)

\displaystyle|Du_{j}(z_{1})-Du_{j}(z_{2})|\leq C\lambda_{j}\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda_{j})}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda_{j}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}}

for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in E
, with
C=C(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)\geq 1
and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
, where
\omega_{j}\coloneqq\essosc\limits_{\Omega_{T}}u_{j}
. Due to (
4.60
), this implies

(4.63)

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{j\to\infty}\lambda_{j}\leq C\bigg[\frac{\omega}{r}+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\bigg]\eqqcolon\lambda.

A similar reasoning to
[
6
, 10.6. Approximation]
establishes that the mapping

\displaystyle\lambda_{j}\mapsto C\lambda\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda_{j})}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda_{j}^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}}

is monotonically increasing. Passing to the limit
j\to\infty
in (
4.62
) and exploiting (
4.63
) thus leads to

(4.64)

\displaystyle\limsup\limits_{j\to\infty}|Du_{j}(z_{1})-Du_{j}(z_{2})|\leq C\lambda\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda)}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}}

for any
z_{1},z_{2}\in E
. The inequalities (
4.61
), (
4.63
), and (
4.64
) together show that the sequence
(Du_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}/2}(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is uniformly bounded and equicontinuous. Due to the fact that
Du_{j}\to Du
in
L^{p}(E,\mathbb{R}^{n})
as
j\to\infty
, the theorem of Arzelà-Ascoli yields the estimates (
4.2
) and (
4.3
). Overall, we have proven the theorem.
∎

5.
Hölder continuity of the gradient

In this section, we finally show that the gradient of any non-negative weak solution to equations of the type

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u^{q}-\divv A(x,t,Du)=0\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$},

assuming the structure conditions (
1.2
), is locally Hölder continuous in
\Omega_{T}
, and give the proof our main regularity result that is Theorem
1.1
. This is accomplished by utilizing the Schauder estimates for equations of parabolic
p
-Laplacian type from Section
4
and the time-insensitive Harnack inequality from
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
, which is valid in the super-critical fast diffusion regime
0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
.
To state the Harnack inequality, we require the following
p
-growth and coercivity assumptions

(5.1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}|A(x,t,\xi)|\leq\tilde{C}_{1}|\xi|^{p-1}&\\
\langle A(x,t,\xi),\xi\rangle\geq\tilde{C}_{2}|\xi|^{p}&\end{cases}

for a.e.
(x,t)\in\Omega_{T}
and any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
denote positive constants.

Theorem 5.1

(Harnack inequality)
.

Let
0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
and
u
be a non-negative and continuous weak solution to (
1.1
) under assumptions (
5.1
). Then, there exist
\gamma>1
and
\sigma\in(0,1)
, both depending on
n,p,q,\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
, such that: if
u(x_{0},t_{0})>0
and the set inclusion

(5.2)

\displaystyle B_{8\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(8\rho)^{p},t_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(8\rho)^{p}\big)\subset\Omega_{T},

where

\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\esssup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u(\cdot,t_{0}),

holds true, then for any

(x,t)\in B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p},t_{0}+\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}\big)

there holds

(5.3)

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}u(x_{0},t_{0})\leq u(x,t)\leq\gamma u(x_{0},t_{0}).

Remark 5.2
.

In the case
\mu=0
, assumptions (
1.2
) together with Lemma
2.8
ensure that both conditions (
5.1
) are always fulfilled. Hence, the Harnack inequality stated in Theorem
5.1
is at our disposal. Moreover, it is worth mentioning that the Harnack inequality in
[
6
, Theorem 1.10]
is presented for parabolic cylinders defined with spatial cubes instead of spatial balls. However, this modification is negligible and the Harnack inequality still remains valid in the form of Theorem
5.1
for parabolic cylinders composed of spatial balls.

The following proposition serves as an intermediate result on our path to Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
0<p-1<q<\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
and
u
be a non-negative, continuous weak solution to (
1.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
) with
\mu=0
. Then, there exist
\gamma=\gamma(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2})>1
,
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)>1
, and
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
, such that: if
u(x_{0},t_{0})>0
and the set inclusion

(5.4)

\displaystyle B_{\gamma\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(\gamma\rho)^{p},t_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(\gamma\rho)^{p}\big)\subset\Omega_{T},

where

\mathcal{L}\coloneqq\esssup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x_{0})}u(\cdot,t_{0}),

holds true, then we have the gradient bound

(5.5)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{Q_{z_{0}}}|Du|\leq C\frac{u(x_{0},t_{0})}{\rho},

with

Q_{z_{0}}\coloneqq B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p},t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}\big).

Furthermore, there hold the Lipschitz estimate

(5.6)

\displaystyle|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|\leq Cu(x_{0},t_{0})\Bigg[\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{\rho}+\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}}}\,\Bigg]

as well as the gradient Hölder estimate

(5.7)

\displaystyle|Du(z_{1})-Du(z_{2})|\leq C\frac{u(x_{0},t_{0})}{\rho}\Bigg[\frac{|x_{1}-x_{2}|}{\rho}+\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}}}\,\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}},

for any
z_{1}=(x_{1},t_{1})
,
z_{2}=(x_{2},t_{2})\in Q_{z_{0}}
.

Remark 5.4
.

The continuity assumption on
u
in Proposition
5.3
is only required to ensure the well-definedness of the pointwise evaluation
u(x_{0},t_{0})
. However, this requirement is not restrictive, as any locally bounded weak solution to (
1.1
) under assumptions (
1.2
) admits an upper semi-continuous representative
u^{*}
with
u=u^{*}
a.e. in
\Omega_{T}
. A reference for this result can be found in
[
38
, Theorem 2.3]
.

Remark 5.5
.

The bounds provided in (
5.5
), (
5.6
), and (
5.7
) are analogous to those stated in
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
. However, the set inclusion condition (
5.4
) assumed in Proposition
5.3
is more stringent than its counterpart for the prototype equation (
1.2
). This difference arises from the more general structure of (
1.1
), and despite the greater constraint, it is a natural requirement.
In fact, it is noteworthy that the assumption (
5.4
) is a consequence of the time-insensitive Harnack inequality stated in form of Theorem
5.1
, while the same Harnack inequality for the prototype equation (
1.2
) in
[
6
, Theorem 1.11]
applies under a weaker condition.

Remark 5.6
.

The optimality of the range of exponents in Proposition
5.3
can be inferred from
[
6
, Section 11.1]
. In particular, the statement of the proposition breaks down in the borderline cases
q=p-1
and
q=\frac{n(p-1)}{(n-p)_{+}}
, which essentially arises due to the fact that the Harnack inequality from Theorem
5.1
holds true solely within the specified range of parameters.

Proof of Proposition
5.3
.

To begin with, we refer to Corollary 1.7 in
[
6
]
, which implies that
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega_{T}
. Consider
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\Omega_{T}
with
u(x_{0},t_{0})>0
. Let
\sigma\in(0,1)
and
\gamma>1
both depending on
n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2}
, denote the constants from Theorem
5.1
and set
\tilde{\gamma}\coloneqq\frac{8}{\sigma}>1
. Further, consider
\rho>0
, such that the set inclusion

B_{\tilde{\gamma}\rho}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(\tilde{\gamma}\rho)^{p},t_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}(\tilde{\gamma}\rho)^{p})\subset\Omega_{T}

holds true. In fact, due to
u
being locally bounded and therefore
\mathcal{L}
being finite, this assumption is justifiable for
\rho>0
small enough. The choices above imply the set inclusion

B_{8\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}\big[8\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p},t_{0}+\mathcal{L}^{q-p+1}\big[8\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p}\big)\subset\Omega_{T}.

Moreover, due to the Harnack inequality from Theorem
5.1
, the bound (
5.3
) holds true a.e. in

B_{\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\big[\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p},t_{0}+\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\big[\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p}\big).

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\quad B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p},t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}\big)

\displaystyle\subset B_{\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p},t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}\big)

\displaystyle=B_{\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\big[\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p},t_{0}+\sigma[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\big[\rho/\sigma^{\frac{1}{p}}\big]^{p}\big),

which allows an application of the Harnack inequality on
Q_{z_{0}}
. Therefore, we achieve

(5.8)

\displaystyle\gamma^{-1}u(x_{0},t_{0})\leq u(x,t)\leq\gamma u(x_{0},t_{0})

for any
(x,t)\in Q_{z_{0}}
. To relinquish the quantity
u(x_{0},t_{0})
in (
5.8
) and obtain bounds independent of the pointwise value of
u
, we re-scale to the unit cylinder
\mathcal{Q}_{1}\coloneqq B_{1}(0)\times(-1,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
and consider

\displaystyle\hat{u}(y,s)\coloneqq

\displaystyle{\textstyle\frac{1}{u(x_{0},t_{0})}}u\big(x_{0}+\rho y,t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}s\big),\quad(y,s)\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}.

Then,
\hat{u}
is a weak solution to the doubly nonlinear equation

(5.9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\hat{u}^{q}-\divv\hat{A}(y,s,D\hat{u})=0\quad\mbox{in $\mathcal{Q}_{1}$},

where we defined

(5.10)

\displaystyle\hat{A}(y,s,\xi)\coloneqq A(x_{0}+\rho y,t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}s,\xi)

for
(y,s)\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Hence, there holds

(5.11)

\gamma^{-1}\leq\hat{u}(y,s)\leq\gamma

for any
(y,s)\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}
. With the Schauder estimates considered in the preceding section in mind, we proceed to replace
\hat{u}^{q}
with
\tilde{u}
. Therefore, equation (
5.9
) transforms to

(5.12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tilde{u}-\divv\tilde{A}(y,s,D\tilde{u})=0\quad\mbox{in $\mathcal{Q}_{1}$}

with vector field

(5.13)

\displaystyle\tilde{A}(y,s,\xi)\coloneqq\hat{A}\big(y,s,q^{-1}\tilde{u}(y,s)^{\frac{1-q}{q}}\xi\big).

By employing the abbreviation

a(y,s)\coloneqq q^{-1}\tilde{u}(y,s)^{\frac{1-q}{q}}

and utilizing the bound (
5.11
), we have the following lower and upper bound for the coefficients

q^{-1}\gamma^{-|1-q|}\leq a(y,s)\leq q^{-1}\gamma^{|1-q|}\qquad\mbox{for any\penalty\ $(y,s)\in\mathcal{Q}_{1}$.}

Consequently, we are in position to apply a classical result found in
[
16
, Chapter III, §1., Theorem 1.1]
, which asserts that
v
is locally Hölder continuous in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
with some Hölder exponent
\beta\in(0,1)
that depends on
n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2}
. Due to the upper and lower bounds for the vector field
A
, it readily follows that the structure conditions
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
–
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{2}
are once again satisfied, with certain positive constants
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
. Note that
\tilde{C}_{1}
depends only on
\gamma,q
, while
\tilde{C}_{2}
depends on
\gamma,q
. Furthermore, the local Hölder continuity of
v
in
\mathcal{Q}_{1}
, together with the
p
-growth condition
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{1}
, implies that the vector field
A
also satisfies
\eqref{voraussetzungen}_{3}
in
\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}\coloneqq B_{\frac{1}{2}}(0)\times(-\frac{1}{4},\frac{1}{4})
with a constant
\tilde{C}_{3}
that depends on
\gamma,\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2},C_{3}
, and with a Hölder exponent
\tilde{\alpha}\in(0,1)
depending on
n,p,q,\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2},\alpha
. As a result, we are now in position to apply Theorem
4.1
, which yields

D\tilde{u}\in C^{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{0}/2}\big(\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}},\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)

for some
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
. Due to the dependence of the constants
\tilde{C}_{1},\tilde{C}_{2}
there holds
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)
. Furthermore, Theorem
4.1
yields quantitative estimates: there exists a constant
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)\geq 1
and a Hölder exponent
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},\alpha)\in(0,1)
, such that for any
E\subset\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}
with
r\coloneqq\frac{1}{4}\dist_{\p}\big(E,\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}\big)>0
, the following quantitative estimates

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{E}|D\tilde{u}|\leq C\Big[\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big]\eqqcolon\lambda

and

\displaystyle|D\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{1})-D\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{2})|\leq C\lambda\Bigg[\frac{d^{(\lambda)}_{p}(z_{1},z_{2})}{\min\big\{1,\lambda^{\frac{p-2}{2}}\big\}r}\Bigg]^{\alpha_{0}}

hold true, where
\omega\coloneqq\essosc\limits_{\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{2}}}\tilde{u}
,
\tilde{z}_{1}=(\tilde{x}_{1},\tilde{t}_{1})
,
\tilde{z}_{2}=(\tilde{x}_{2},\tilde{t}_{2})\in E
. We now choose

. In turn, we have

and there holds
\omega\leq\gamma^{q}
. This implies

\displaystyle\lambda=C\Big[\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)+\Big(\frac{\omega}{r}\Big)^{\frac{2}{p}}\Big]\leq C

and moreover

(5.14)

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{4}}}|D\tilde{u}|\leq C

with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
. Furthermore, there holds

(5.15)

\displaystyle|D\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{1})-D\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{2})|\leq C\mathrm{d}_{\p}(\tilde{z}_{1},\tilde{z}_{2})^{\alpha_{0}}

for any
\tilde{z}_{1}
,
\tilde{z}_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{4}}
, with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
. The previous estimate is obvious in the case
p\leq 2
, whereas in the case
p>2
we utilized
\alpha_{0}\leq\frac{2}{p-2}
, which is an immediate consequence of the definition of
\alpha_{0}
according to (
4.52
). To proceed, we consider two points
\tilde{z}_{1}
,
\tilde{z}_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{8}}=B_{\frac{1}{8}}(0)\times(-\frac{1}{64},\frac{1}{64})
with
\tilde{t}_{1}\leq\tilde{t}_{2}
, and a cylinder

, such that
\overline{x}=\frac{1}{2}(\tilde{x}_{1}+\tilde{x}_{2})
and
\frac{1}{2}|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|\leq\rho\leq\frac{1}{8}
. Now, an application of Lemma
4.15
with the choice
\mu=0
on the cylinder
Q
, together with (
5.14
), yield the following

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{1})-\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\essosc\limits_{Q}\tilde{u}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg[\rho\|D\tilde{u}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+C\frac{\tilde{t}_{2}-\tilde{t}_{1}}{\rho}\|D\tilde{u}\|^{p-1}_{L^{\infty}(Q)}\bigg]

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg[\rho+\frac{\tilde{t}_{2}-\tilde{t}_{1}}{\rho}\bigg]

with

. Distinguishing between both cases
\sqrt{\tilde{t}_{2}-\tilde{t}_{1}}\leq|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|
and
\sqrt{\tilde{t}_{2}-\tilde{t}_{1}}>|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|
, this overall leads to the bound

|\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{1})-\tilde{u}(\tilde{z}_{2})|\leq C\Big[|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|+\sqrt{|\tilde{t}_{1}-\tilde{t}_{2}|}\Big]

for any
\tilde{z}_{1}
,
\tilde{z}_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{8}}
, with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
. Exploiting (
5.13
) and Lemma
2.17
, we establish

(5.16)

\displaystyle|\tilde{u}^{\beta}(\tilde{z}_{1})-\tilde{u}^{\beta}(\tilde{z}_{2})|\leq C\gamma^{q|\beta-1|}\Big[|\tilde{x}_{1}-\tilde{x}_{2}|+\sqrt{|\tilde{t}_{1}-\tilde{t}_{2}|}\Big]

for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and any
\tilde{z}_{1}
,
\tilde{z}_{2}\in\mathcal{Q}_{\frac{1}{8}}
, with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha,\beta)
. By following the final steps performed in
[
6
, Proof of Theorem 1.1]
, we transform back to the original solution
u
, which yields the claimed estimates
\eqref{hauptresultatgradientbound}-\eqref{hauptresultathölder}
. It is worth mentioning that the dependence on
\beta
of
C
is eliminated at the end by the specific choices
\beta=\frac{1}{q}
and
\beta=\frac{1-q}{q}
.
∎

Finally, we are in position to provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\tilde{\gamma}>1
denote the constant from Proposition
5.3
. As
u
is locally bounded in
\Omega_{T}
, there holds
\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}u<\infty
. We now choose the radius
\rho\coloneqq\frac{\tilde{K}^{-\frac{q-p+1}{p}}\rho_{1}}{4\tilde{\gamma}}
, which implies

\displaystyle\esssup\limits_{B_{\rho}(x)}u(\cdot,t)\leq\esssup\limits_{B_{\frac{\rho_{1}}{4}}(x)}u(\cdot,t)\leq\esssup\limits_{\mathcal{K}_{2}}u\leq K_{2}

for any
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
. Additionally, we have the set inclusion

\displaystyle B_{\tilde{\gamma}\rho}(x)\times(t-\tilde{K}^{q-p+1}(\tilde{\gamma}\rho)^{p},t+\tilde{K}^{q-p+1}(\tilde{\gamma}\rho)^{p})

\displaystyle\subset B_{\frac{\rho_{1}}{4}(x)}\times\Big(t-\Big(\frac{\rho_{1}}{4}\Big)^{p},t+\Big(\frac{\rho_{1}}{4}\Big)^{p}\Big)\subset\mathcal{K}_{2}\Subset\Omega_{T}

for any
(x,t)\in\mathcal{K}_{2}
. Let us now fix a point
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathcal{K}_{1}
. If
u(x_{0},t_{0})>0
, the preceding inequalities enable us to apply the gradient bound (
5.5
) from Proposition
5.3
on the cylinder

Q_{z_{0}}\coloneqq B_{\rho}(x_{0})\times\big(t_{0}-[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p},t_{0}+[u(x_{0},t_{0})]^{q-p+1}\rho^{p}\big)

to obtain

(5.17)

\displaystyle|Du(x_{0},t_{0})|\leq C\frac{u(x_{0},t_{0})}{\rho}\leq C\frac{K_{1}}{\rho}=C\frac{K_{1}K_{2}^{\frac{q-p+1}{p}}}{\rho_{1}}

with
C=C(n,p,q,C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},\alpha)
. If
u(x_{0},t_{0})=0
, then the Harnack inequality from Theorem
5.1
implies
u(\cdot,t_{0})\equiv 0
in
\Omega
, and in particular
Du(x_{0},t_{0})=0
. Since
z_{0}
was chosen arbitrarily, by combining the calculations from both cases, we obtain the stated gradient bound (
1.6
). Following the strategy in
[
6
, Proof of Corollary 1.3]
, we also obtain the local gradient Hölder estimate (
1.7
), which finishes the proof.
∎

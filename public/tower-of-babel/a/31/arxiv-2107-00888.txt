Moments of Subsets of General Equiangular Tight Frames
Marina Haikin, Ram Zamir

Abstract
This note outlines the steps for proving that the moments of a randomly-selected subset of a general ETF (complex, with aspect ratio 0 < γ < 1 0<\gamma<1 ) converge to the corresponding MANOVA moments. We bring here an extension for the proof of the ’Kesten-Mckay’ moments (real ETF, γ = 1 / 2 \gamma=1/2 ) [ 1 ] . In particular, we establish a recursive computation of the r r th moment, for r = 1 , 2 , … r=1,2,\ldots , and verify, using a symbolic program, that the recursion output coincides with the MANOVA moments.

Let
F
be a unit-norm equiangular tight frame comprised of
n

{\cal C}^{m}
,
\gamma=\frac{m}{n}<1

We define
s\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{=}}(1-x)/2\sqrt{x}
, where
x=\gamma^{-1}-1

and denote a generalized conference matrix
as

S=\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{{x}}}\cdot\left(F^{\prime}F-\frac{1}{2\gamma}\cdot I_{n}\right)

(i)

S_{i,i}
= constant =
\sqrt{n-1}(1-x)/2\sqrt{x}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta}}{{=}}\sqrt{n-1}\cdot s

(ii)

S

|S_{i,j}|=1

i\neq j

(iii)

S^{\prime}S\propto I_{n}

are zero, while the diagonal elements are

(n-1)(x+1)^{2}/4x
.

x/(n-1)

\gamma=0.5
,

S^{\prime}S=(n-1)I

[
1
]
.

(4)

.

.

V_{n}(\pi)\rightarrow s^{r}\cdot V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})

(12)

For example a partition 122234422333341 translated to 1234234 with a factor of

.

Now we focus on estimation of
V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})
.
G_{\pi^{\prime}}
is a loop-free graph and thus Lemma 6 in

]
holds for the same reasons. Lemma 12(iv),12(v) also holds due to similar the properties of

.

We follow the proof by induction of Lemma 16 in
[
1
]
. Lemma 12(i) holds again by

As for

Assuming that the (loops-removed) partition

As we deal with

V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})\rightarrow A_{l_{1}}\cdots\ A_{l_{m^{\prime}}}.

Case I of the proof refers to the case when the singleton block resides in a cycle of length 2, i.e., if the singleton is

\pi(1)=\pi(k)
. A similar derivation as in
[

]
implies

V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})=V_{n}(\pi^{\prime\prime})+o(1).

(14)

Our induction hypothesis and (

), imply

(15)

Since

Case II of the proof refers to the case when

]
we get:

V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})=-\sum_{i=2}^{l}V_{n}({\pi^{\prime}}^{i})+o(1).

Assuming

V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})\rightarrow-A_{l_{1}}\cdots\ A_{l_{m^{\prime}-1}}\cdot\sum_{i=2}^{l}A_{l-1}A_{l-i+1}=-A_{l_{l}}\cdots\ A_{l_{m^{\prime}-1}}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{l-1}A_{l}A_{l-i}.

(17)

Applying the identity (
6
), we get
V_{n}(\pi^{\prime})\rightarrow A_{l_{1}}\cdots\ A_{l_{m^{\prime}}}
, thereby establishing (
13
).

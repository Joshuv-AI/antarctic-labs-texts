Abstract
. Given an action of the one-dimensional torus on a projective variety, the associated Chow quotient arises as a natural parameter space of invariant 1 1 -cycles, which dominates the GIT quotients of the variety. In this paper we explore the relation between the Chow and the GIT quotients of a variety, showing how to construct explicitly the former upon the latter via successive blowups under suitable assumptions. We also discuss conditions for the smoothness of the Chow quotient, and present some examples in which it is singular.

2.
Preliminaries on
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions and GIT quotients

Along this section
X
will denote a normal complex projective variety, with a nontrivial
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.

2.1.
Fixed point components

We denote by
X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}
the fixed locus of the action, and by
{\mathcal{Y}}
the set of irreducible fixed point components:

X^{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}}=\bigsqcup_{Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}}Y.

The
sink
and the
source
of the action are the fixed point components containing, respectively, the limiting points of a general
x\in X
:

\lim_{t\to 0}t^{-1}x,\quad\lim_{t\to 0}tx.

If
X
is smooth and projective, every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
is smooth (cf.
[
18
]
).

2.2.
Linearizations and weight maps

Given a line bundle
L\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
, one may find a linearization of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on it (cf.
[
21
]
), so that for every
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
acts on
L_{|Y}
by multiplication with a character
m\in\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*})=\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*},{\mathbb{C}}^{*})
, called
weight of the linearization on
Y
. Fixing an isomorphism
\mathop{\rm\hskip 0.0ptM}\nolimits({\mathbb{C}}^{*})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
, a linearization defines a map
\mu_{L}:{\mathcal{Y}}\to{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that

\mu_{kL}(Y)=k\mu_{L}(Y),\quad\mu_{L+L^{\prime}}(Y)=\mu_{L}(Y)+\mu_{L^{\prime}}(Y),

for every
L,L^{\prime}\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
,
k\in{\mathbb{Z}}
,
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
.

2.3.
Actions on polarized pairs

A
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on the polarized pair
(X,L)

is a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on a projective variety
X
, together with the weight map
\mu_{L}
determined by an ample line bundle
L
. Since two linearizations differ by a character of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, for
L\in\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)
there exists a unique linearization (called
normalized
) whose smaller weight is equal to zero.
In this case we denote by

0=a_{0}<\dots<a_{r},

the weights
\mu_{L}(Y)
,
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}
,
and set:

Y_{i}:=\bigcup_{\mu_{L}(Y)=a_{i}}Y.

The values
a_{i}
will be called the
critical values
and the number
r
will be called the
criticality
of the action.
The minimum and maximum of the critical values are achieved only at the sink and the source of the action, which are then equal to
Y_{0}
and
Y_{r}
, respectively (see
[
32
, Remark 2.12]
). The value
\delta=a_{r}
is called

of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
(X,L)
. The fixed point components different from
Y_{0}
,
Y_{r}
are called
inner
, and their set is denoted by

{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}:={\mathcal{Y}}\setminus\{Y_{0},Y_{r}\}.

2.4.
Geometric quotients

Example 2.1
.

An obvious source of examples of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions is the category of toric varieties. An algebraic variety

if it admits an action of an algebraic torus

with an open orbit. As usual, we denote by

If

; the lattice polytope determines the fan

Actions of

on a projective toric variety

as above can be obtained by considering 1-parameter subgroups

, which are determined by projections
\nu:M\otimes\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\otimes\mathbb{R}
. The critical values of the corresponding
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action (together with the induced linearization on

) are the images of vertices of
\Delta

. The associated GIT quotients of the action on

are then toric varieties, with respect to the quotient torus

. They can be described as the toric varieties determined by the polytopes

, obtained by slicing

. We refer to

]
for details.

-dimensional orbit, whose sink and source are denoted by
x_{-}
and
x_{+}
. Then
C
is a smooth rational curve of
L
-degree equal to
\mu_{L}(x_{+})-\mu_{L}(x_{-})
.

2.7.
B-type actions and bordisms

Following
[
32
, Section 3]
, we call
B-type action
a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action whose extremal fixed point components
Y_{0},Y_{r}
have codimension one.

If
X
is smooth and projective, by the Białynicki-Birula decomposition, the restriction maps
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y_{j})
,
j=0,r
are surjective (cf.
[
8
, Theorem 3]
). We will say that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is a
bordism
(cf.
[
32
, Definition 3.8]
) if the restriction maps
\imath_{j}^{*}:\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)\to\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y_{j})
,
j=0,r
, fit into two short exact sequences:

\begin{array}[]{c}0~{\ \rightarrow\ }~{\mathbb{Z}}[Y_{r}]~\longrightarrow~\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)~\longrightarrow~\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y_{0})~{\ \rightarrow\ }~0\\[2.0pt]
0~{\ \rightarrow\ }~{\mathbb{Z}}[Y_{0}]~\longrightarrow~\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(X)~\longrightarrow~\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(Y_{r})~{\ \rightarrow\ }~0\end{array}

The following equivalence, which is a consequence of
[
8
, Theorem 3]
has been proved in
[
32
, Corollary 3.7]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety admitting a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action of B-type. The action is a bordism if and only if
\nu^{\pm}(Y)=\mathop{\rm codim}\nolimits(B^{\pm}(Y))\geq 2
for every inner fixed point component
Y\in{\mathcal{Y}}^{\circ}
.

2.8.
A digression on Grassmannians and
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions

In this section we discuss an example that we will use later on.

Let
V
be a finite dimensional complex vector space with a nontrivial linear action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
with weights
a_{0}<\dots<a_{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, inducing a decomposition:

V=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{s}V_{k},\qquad V_{k}=\{v\in V|\,\,t(v)=t^{a_{k}}v\,\,\mbox{for all }t\in{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\}.

The induced action on the (Grothendieck) projectivization
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
of
V
has exactly
s+1
fixed-point components
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k})\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)
. The sink and the source of the action are
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{0}),{\mathbb{P}}(V_{s})
, and there exists a linearization of the action on the tautological line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}(1)
such that:

\mu_{{\mathcal{O}}(1)}({\mathbb{P}}(V_{k}))=a_{k}.

Let us denote by
{\mathbb{G}}(s,{\mathbb{P}}(V))
the Grassmannian of
s
-dimensional projective subspaces of
{\mathbb{P}}(V)
and consider the induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action. One of its fixed point components is the variety parametrizing subspaces meeting each
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k})
in one point, which is isomorphic to the Segre variety
\prod_{k=0}^{s}{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k})
; we will denote it by

\Sigma(V):=\{{\mathbb{P}}(W)\subset{\mathbb{P}}(V)|\,\,\dim({\mathbb{P}}(W)\cap{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k}))=0,\,\,\forall k\}\subset{\mathbb{G}}(s,{\mathbb{P}}(V))

(7)

Now let us consider two given indices
k_{-},k_{+}
with
0\leq k_{-}<k_{+}\leq s
, and set:

V_{k_{-},k_{+}}:=\bigoplus_{k=k_{-}}^{k_{+}}V_{k}.

We have induced
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions on
V_{k_{-},k_{+}}
(with weights
a_{k_{-}}<\dots<a_{k_{+}}
),
{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k_{-},k_{+}})
and the Grassmannian
{\mathbb{G}}(k_{+}-k_{-},{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k_{-},k_{+}}))
. One of the fixed-point components of the action on
{\mathbb{G}}(k_{+}-k_{-},{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k_{-},k_{+}}))
is
\Sigma(V_{k_{-},k_{+}})
, which is isomorphic to
\prod_{k=k_{-}}^{k_{+}}{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k})
; note also that the restriction of the Plücker embedding of the Grassmannian
{\mathbb{G}}(k_{+}-k_{-},{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k_{-},k_{+}}))
to
\Sigma(V_{k_{-},k_{+}})
is the Segre embedding.

The linear projection
\varphi_{k_{-},k_{+}}:{\mathbb{P}}(V)\dashrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k_{-},k_{+}})
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant and induces a surjective morphism

\pi_{k_{-},k_{+}}:\Sigma(V)\longrightarrow\Sigma(V_{k_{-},k_{+}}),

which corresponds to the natural projection

\prod_{k=0}^{s}{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k})\longrightarrow\prod_{k={k_{-}}}^{k_{+}}{\mathbb{P}}(V_{k}),\qquad(P_{0},\dots,P_{s})\mapsto(P_{k_{-}},\dots,P_{k_{+}}).

3.
Prunings

Following
[
31
]
, the GIT quotients of
X
can be seen as the extremal fixed point components of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant birational modifications of
X
, that are referred to as
prunings
of

in
[

]

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair, and let
\tau_{-}<\tau_{+}
be two rational numbers in
[0,\delta]
. We define the
pruning
of
(X,L)
with respect to
\tau_{-},\tau_{+}
as:

X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+}):=\mathop{\rm Proj}\nolimits A(\tau_{-},\tau_{+}),\ \mbox{where}\ A(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})=\bigoplus_{m\in d{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\bigoplus_{k\in[m\tau_{-},m\tau_{+}]}\HH^{0}(X,mL)_{k},

and
d
is the minimum positive integer such that
d\tau_{\pm}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Remark 3.2
.

Note that
A(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
is finitely generated by
[
3
, Lemma 3.2]
, guaranteeing the good definition of
X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
.

Example 3.3
.

Assume that

is a projective toric variety, polarized with an ample line bundle
L
, associated with a lattice polytope
\Delta\subset M\otimes{\mathbb{R}}
, with a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action determined by a
1
-parameter subgroup
\nu
. Then, with the notation of Example
2.1
, the prunings of
X
are the toric varieties associated with polytopes of the form
\nu^{-1}[a,b]\cap\Delta
where
a<b
are rationals contained in
\nu(\Delta)

In the case in which
X
is smooth and the action is equalized, the varieties
X(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})
can be easily written as birational modifications of
X
, as follows. Let us denote by
\beta:X^{\flat}\longrightarrow X
the blowup of

along the sink and the source
Y_{0}
,
Y_{r}
. The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
extends via
\beta
to an action on
X^{\flat}
, whose sink and source are the exceptional divisors over
Y_{0},Y_{r}
, that we denote by
Y^{\flat}_{0},Y^{\flat}_{r}
. Let us define

L(\tau_{-},\tau_{+}):=\beta^{*}L-\tau_{-}Y^{\flat}_{0}-(\delta-\tau_{+})Y^{\flat}_{r}.

Then, following
[
31
, Lemma 2.5]
, we can write:

A(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})=\bigoplus_{m\in d{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}}\HH^{0}(X^{\flat},mL(\tau_{-},\tau_{+})).

Remark 3.4
.

In particular, for
\tau_{-}
,
\tau_{+}
sufficiently close to

\delta

-invariant cycles were previously proposed by Białynicki-Birula and Sommese,
[
5
]
, inspired by a construction by Fujiki (cf.
[
14
]
).

Following Kapranov’s definition, we may find a nonempty open set
V\subset X
of points
x
such that the cycles
\overline{{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\cdot x}
belong to a single homology class in
X
. By shrinking
V
, if necessary, the geometric quotient of
V
by the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
exists. Moreover, taking closures in
X
of the orbits of points of
V
, we obtain an algebraic irreducible family of rational curves in
X
, therefore a morphism
\psi:V/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\to\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
.

Definition 4.1
.

With the above notation, the
Chow quotient
of
X
by the action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
is defined as the closure:

\overline{\mathcal{C}\!X}:=\overline{\psi(V/{\mathbb{C}}^{*})}\subset\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X).

We will denote by
\mathcal{C}\!X
the normalization of
\overline{\mathcal{C}\!X}
, and call it the
normalized Chow quotient
of
X
by the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.
The pullback to
\mathcal{C}\!X
of the universal family of
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
will be denoted by
p:{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{C}\!X
, with evaluation morphism
q:{\mathcal{U}}\to X
.

The
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
extends naturally to an action on
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
and the corresponding universal family of cycles. This action restricts to the trivial action on
V/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
, hence to the trivial action on
\mathcal{C}\!X
. Consequently we have an action on
{\mathcal{U}}
, so that the maps
p:{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{C}\!X
,
q:{\mathcal{U}}\to X
are
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-equivariant, and every cycle parametrized by
\mathcal{C}\!X
is
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant.

Example 4.2
.

In the case of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-actions on toric varieties (see Example
2.1
), Chow quotients are known to be toric varieties, that have been described combinatorially by Kapranov, Sturmfels and Zelevinsky in
[
19
]
.

Throughout the rest of the paper we will assume that the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action is equalized and that
X
is smooth. Let us start by observing that in this case every
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant cycle parametrized by
\mathcal{C}\!X
is a chain of rational curves with simple normal crossings
(see
[
26
, Lemma 3.12]
for a similar statement for the parameter space of stable maps):

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair, with
X
smooth, admitting an equalized
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.
Let
z\in\mathcal{C}\!X
be any point, and let
Z=p^{-1}(z)
be the corresponding cycle.
Then
Z
is reduced, and its dual graph
is of type
{\rm A}
.

Proof.

Note that
Z
is necessarily a connected cycle meeting the sink and the source, of
L
-degree equal to the bandwidth
\delta=a_{r}
of the action, by Lemma
2.3
. If
Z_{j}
is an irreducible component of
Z
, denoting by
z_{j}^{-},z_{j}^{+}
its sink and source, the equalization assumption implies that the
L
-degree of the reduced scheme of
Z_{j}
is equal to
\mu_{L}(z_{j}^{+})-\mu_{L}(z_{j}^{-})
(again by Lemma
2.3
), and this number is of type
a_{i}-a_{j}
for some
i,j\in\{0,\dots,r\}
. We conclude by summing on the components of
Z
.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Note that the proof of Lemma
4.3
also implies that an irreducible component
Z_{k}
of
Z=p^{-1}(z)
is a rational curve of degree
\mu_{L}(z_{k}^{+})-\mu_{L}(z_{k}^{-})
, where
z_{k}^{-},z_{k}^{+}
denote the sink and the source of
Z_{k}
, respectively. In particular, there are no components of
Z
contained in a fixed-point component.

A consequence of the reducedness given by Lemma
4.3
is the following statement, which can be obtained by applying standard arguments on parameter spaces:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair, with
X
smooth, with an equalized
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.
Then the universal family
p:{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{C}\!X
is flat.

Proof.

Let
Z=p^{-1}(z)
be any fiber of
p
. By Lemma
4.3
, the components of
Z
are all reduced, so that
Z
is normal at the general point of each component and, since
\mathcal{C}\!X
is normal, we may apply
[
22
, Theorem I.6.6]
to claim that
p
is flat at the general point of any component of
Z
. We then conclude by applying
[
22
, Theorem I.7.3.1]
.
∎

Remark 4.6
.

As a consequence of the flatness of
{\mathcal{U}}\to\mathcal{C}\!X
, we obtain a morphism
\mathcal{C}\!X\to\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
such that
{\mathcal{U}}
is the pullback to
\mathcal{C}\!X
of the universal family over
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
. By shrinking the open subset
V
introduced above, we can assume that the image of
\mathcal{C}\!X\to\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
is the closure of the image of the natural map from
V/{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
to
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
, whose normalization
\mathcal{H}\!X
we call the
Hilbert quotient
of the action. On the other hand we have a natural morphism from
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
to
\mathop{\rm Chow}\nolimits(X)
(cf.
[
22
, Theorem I.6.3]
), so it turns out that
\mathcal{C}\!X
coincides with the Hilbert quotient of
X
. The idea of considering a flat family of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-invariant cycles, whose general element is the closure of a general orbit –in the complex geometric setting– goes back to Fujiki,
[
14
]
(see also
[
5
, Theorem 0.1.2]
).

Remark 4.7
.

Given an equalized
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
X
, every cycle parametrized by
\mathcal{C}\!X
has a unique lift-up to the blowup
X^{\flat}
of
X
along the sink and the source. In particular
\mathcal{C}\!X\simeq\mathcal{C}\!X^{\flat}
.

Proposition 4.8
.

If the action is of B-type and faithful, then
\mathcal{C}\!X
is the normalization of the component of the Chow/Hilbert scheme containing the class of the closure of a general orbit of the action.

Proof.

The faithfulness implies that the action is equalized at the sink and the source (see
[
32
, Remark 3.2]
).
If
C{\ \cong\ }\mathbb{P}^{1}
is the closure of a general orbit, then we can use
[
32
, Lemma 2.16]
to compute the splitting type of the normal bundle of
C
in
X
. Since the weights of the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on
{\mathcal{N}}_{C/X}
are
0^{(\dim X-1)}
both at the sink and at the source of
C
,
it follows that
{\mathcal{N}}_{C/X}{\ \cong\ }{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{1}}^{\oplus n-1}
and therefore the component of
\mathop{\rm Hilb}\nolimits(X)
containing the point
[C]
is smooth at
[C]
and of dimension
n-1=\dim\mathcal{C}\!X
.
∎

4.1.
The universal bundle of a
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair, with
X
smooth, supporting an equalized
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.
We will now construct a vector bundle on
\mathcal{C}\!X
, whose fibers represent, roughly speaking, the linear spans of the cycles parametrized by
\mathcal{C}\!X
in a certain embedding of
X
. This will allow us to identify
\mathcal{C}\!X
with a subvariety of a Grassmannian.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(X,L)
be a polarized pair, with
X
smooth, supporting an equalized
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action.
Then
p_{*}(q^{*}(L))
is locally free of rank
\delta+1
.

Proof.

We use the equalization of the action to ensure, by Lemma
4.5
, that
p
is flat. In particular,
\chi(C,L)
is the same for every cycle
C
of the family parametrized by
\mathcal{C}\!X
, considered as a scheme with its reduced structure. We will show that
\HH^{1}(C,L)=0
for every
C
, so that
\dim\HH^{0}(C,L)
will be equal to
\delta+1
, and we may then apply
[
16
, Corollary III.12.9]
to obtain the claimed result.

In order to do so, since the dual graph of
C
is of type
{\rm A}
by Lemma
4.3
, we may number the irreducible components of a given element
C
of
\mathcal{C}\!X
as
C_{1},\dots,C_{k}
so that
C_{i}
intersects (transversally)
C_{i+1}
in a point
p_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,k-1
, and
\{\mu_{L}(p_{i})\}
is strictly increasing. Note that we have an exact sequence:

0\to\HH^{0}(C,L)\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\HH^{0}(C_{i},L_{|C_{i}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mathbb{C}}_{p_{i}}\longrightarrow\HH^{1}(C,L)\to 0.

(9)

Then, since
L
is ample, one may easily check that the restriction map

\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}\HH^{0}(C_{i},L_{|C_{i}})\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k-1}{\mathbb{C}}_{p_{i}}

is surjective, hence
\HH^{1}(C,L)
is equal to zero. This completes the proof.
∎

Definition 4.10
.

The vector bundle

associated to the
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
-action on

Lemma 4.11
.

In the setting of Lemma

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

{\mathcal{E}}=\bigoplus_{u=0}^{\delta}{\mathcal{L}}_{u},

Proof.

The action of
{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

{\mathbb{C}}^{*}

6.1.
The case of convex varieties

We finish this section by presenting a sufficient condition for the smoothness of the normalized Chow quotient

Definition 6.2

The main example of convex varieties are rational homogeneous spaces

We will now prove Theorem

among them are smooth blowups (Proposition

Under the hypotheses of Theorem

, assume moreover that

Proof.

Let us prove the statement for the quotients

Note that the variety

Example 7.3

Let

The associated fan in the lattice

We take the ample line bundle

Let us now compute the weights of the

X^{n}_{i,j}
(
i\leq k\leq j
) is the pruning of
B^{\pm}(x_{k})
, that is:

\varphi_{i,j}(B^{+}(x_{k}))\simeq X^{k}_{i,k},\qquad\varphi_{i,j}(B^{-}(x_{k}))\simeq X^{n-k}_{k,j}.

Now we observe that, with the notation of Remark
5.10
,
S_{i,k}
is the Chow quotient of the (disjoint) union over the fixed points of weight
k
of
\varphi_{i,k}(B^{+}(x_{k}))\simeq X^{k}_{i,k}
and, similarly,
D_{k,j}
is the Chow quotient of the (disjoint) union over the fixed points of weight
k
of
\varphi_{k,j}(B^{-}(x_{k}))\simeq X^{k}_{k,j}
, so the center of the blowup
s:\mathcal{C}\!X^{n}_{i,j}\to\mathcal{C}\!X^{n}_{i,j-1}
is a disjoint union of varieties isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}\!X^{j-1}_{i,j-1}
, and the center of
the blowup
d:\mathcal{C}\!X^{n}_{i,j}\to\mathcal{C}\!X^{n}_{i+1,j}
is a disjoint union of varieties isomorphic to
\mathcal{C}\!X^{i+1}_{i+1,j}
.
By the inductive assumption, since
1\leq i<j\leq n-1
, the centers of the blowups are smooth and the proof is concluded.
∎

.

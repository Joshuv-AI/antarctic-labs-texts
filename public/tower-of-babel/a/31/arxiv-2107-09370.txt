An embedding of ReLU networks and an analysis of their identifiability
Pierre Stock and Rémi Gribonval

Abstract
. Neural networks with the Rectified Linear Unit (ReLU) nonlinearity are described by a vector of parameters θ \theta , and realized as a piecewise linear continuous function 𝑹 θ : x ∈ ℝ d ↦ 𝑹 θ ​ ( x ) ∈ ℝ k \boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}:x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\mapsto\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}(x)\in\mathbb{R}^{k} . Natural scalings and permutations operations on the parameters θ \theta leave the realization unchanged, leading to equivalence classes of parameters that yield the same realization. These considerations in turn lead to the notion of identifiability – the ability to recover (the equivalence class of) θ \theta from the sole knowledge of its realization 𝑹 θ \boldsymbol{R}_{\theta} . The overall objective of this paper is to introduce an embedding for ReLU neural networks of any depth, 𝚽 ⁡ ( θ ) \boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta) , that is invariant to scalings and that provides a locally linear parameterization of the realization of the network. Leveraging these two key properties, we derive some conditions under which a deep ReLU network is indeed locally identifiable from the knowledge of the realization on a finite set of samples x i ∈ ℝ d x_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d} . We study the shallow case in more depth, establishing necessary and sufficient conditions for the network to be identifiable from a bounded subset 𝒳 ⊆ ℝ d \mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d} .

Introduction

The empirical success of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) for traditional machine learning tasks such as image classification is a well-known fact for the research community
[
1
]
. While this empirical success percolates to areas ranging from protein folding to symbolic mathematics, a second well-known fact is that the theoretical tools to grasp DNNs and uncover the reasons of their success are still lagging behind the fast-paced experimental results. We argue that a deeper understanding of the expressivity and stability properties of such networks could lead to practical improvements
[
2

3

In the remainder of this section, we first list the papers tackling identifiability of neural networks with a given non-linearity function. We next present some related work that construct an embedding for ReLU networks. Finally, we list applications directly or indirectly derived from the two previous theoretical considerations.

2.
General setting and main results

We consider fully-connected feedforward ReLU neural networks with
L\geq 2
affine layers.
Each network is supported on a graph
G=(E,V)
with vertex set
V
composed of neurons
\nu
and edge set
E
composed of connections. The set of neurons
V
is partitioned into the input layer
N_{0}
,
L-1
hidden layers
N_{\ell}
,
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
, and the output layer
N_{L}
. Hidden neurons compose the set
H=\cup_{\ell=1}^{L-1}N_{\ell}
. Since we focus on fully-connected networks, the set of connections
E
is made of all oriented edges
e=\nu\to\nu^{\prime}
between neurons belonging to consecutive layers,
\nu\in N_{\ell-1}
,
\nu^{\prime}\in N_{\ell}
for some
1\leq\ell\leq L
. The subset of incoming edges of neuron
\nu
is denoted
\bullet\to\nu
, while
\nu\to\bullet
denotes its set of outgoing edges.

Each edge
e\in E
is equipped with a weight
w_{e}
and each hidden neuron
\nu\in H
with a bias
b_{\nu}
. Output neurons, i.e. neurons from the last layer
\eta\in N_{L}
, are also equipped with a bias
b_{\eta}
, which is sometimes constrained to be zero. The set of all neurons equipped with biases is
\bar{H}:=H\cup N_{L}
. Parameters (weights and biases) are gathered in a parameter vector
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
where
E\cup\bar{H}
indexes all possible weights and biases including biases on the output layer. For brevity we may denote
\theta_{e}
for weights and
\theta_{\nu}
for biases.
When needed we also write
\theta=(\theta_{i})_{i\in E\cup\bar{H}}
.
Since we consider a fully connected architecture (this does not prevent some weights to possibly vanish on some edges),
\theta
can also be represented as a set of
L
matrices
\boldsymbol{W}_{\ell}=(w_{\nu\to\nu^{\prime}})_{\nu^{\prime}\in N_{\ell},\nu\in N_{\ell-1}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}\times N_{\ell-1}}
,
1\leq\ell\leq L
and
L
vectors
\boldsymbol{b}_{\ell}=(b_{\nu})_{\nu\in N_{\ell}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}}
,
1\leq\ell\leq L
.

2.1.
Network architectures

Many of the notions of parameter identifiability or non-degeneracy that will be considered are relative to a choice of network “architecture”. This is represented both by the graph
G
(which determines how many layers there are, and how wide they are) but also by a possibly restricted set
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
of network parameters, which may for example account for the following type of constraints:

•

restricting to a convolutional structure;

•

restricting to sparse networks, possibly with structured sparsity patterns;

•

restricting to networks without output biases (
b_{\eta}=0
for every
\eta\in N_{L}
);

•

restricting to networks without biases (
b_{\nu}=0
for every
\nu\in H\cup N_{L}
).

2.2.
Realization of a network

Given a parameter
\theta
and an input vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
, we sequentially define
\boldsymbol{y}_{0}(\theta,x)=x
and for each
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
the pre-activation
\boldsymbol{z}_{\ell}(\theta,x)=\boldsymbol{W}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{y}_{\ell-1}(\theta,x)+\boldsymbol{b}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}}
, the post-activation
\boldsymbol{y}_{\ell}(\theta,x)=\mathtt{ReLU}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\ell}(\theta,x))\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}}
where the rectified linear unit (ReLU) activation function,
\mathtt{ReLU}(t)=\max(t,0)
, is applied entrywise. Finally we define the realization of the network as the function
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}:x\mapsto\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}(x):=\boldsymbol{z}_{L}(\theta,x)=\boldsymbol{W}_{L}\boldsymbol{y}_{L-1}(\theta,x)+\boldsymbol{b}_{L}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{L}}
. When needed we will use
neuron-wise
versions of these notations, e.g.
y_{\nu}(\theta,x)=(\boldsymbol{y}_{\ell}(\theta,x))_{\nu}
where
\nu\in N_{\ell}
. Note the general convention to denote scalar-valued quantities in plain font to distinguish them from quantities that can be vector-valued, which are generally denoted in bold.

2.3.
Invariance to permutation and scaling

A well known fact
[
23
]
is that the realization of any ReLU-network is invariant to permutations and scalings of the parameter
\theta
. The invariance to permutations is not specific to ReLU-networks, while the scaling-invariance is due to the homogeneity of the ReLU:
\mathtt{ReLU}(\lambda\cdot)=\lambda\mathtt{ReLU}(\cdot)
for every
\lambda>0
and is also valid for other variants such as the leaky-ReLU. While various definitions coexist in the literature
[
24
,
25
,
26
]
,
it is convenient to focus first on the practical
per-neuron
rescaling equivalence
[
23
]
as stated below.

Rescaling equivalence

Let
\nu\in H
and
\lambda_{\nu}>0
. A neuron-wise scaling multiplies the incoming weights and the bias of
\nu
by
\lambda_{\nu}
, and divides the outgoing weights by
\lambda_{\nu}
. It is formally defined as
s_{\nu,\lambda_{\nu}}:\theta=(w,b)\mapsto\theta^{\prime}=(w^{\prime},b^{\prime})
where for every connection
e\in E
,

(1)

\forall e\in E,\quad w^{\prime}_{e}=\begin{cases}w_{e}\lambda_{\nu}&\mbox{if }e\in\bullet\to\nu\\
\frac{1}{\lambda_{\nu}}w_{e}&\mbox{if }e\in\nu\to\bullet\\
w_{e}&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}\qquad\forall\nu\in H,\quad b^{\prime}_{\nu}=b_{\nu}\lambda_{\nu}.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of neuron-wise scalings. We observe that neuron-wise rescalings commute and are invertible, the inverse of
s_{\nu,\lambda_{\nu}}
being
s_{\nu,1/\lambda_{\nu}}
. Let
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
be the commutative group generated by
\mathcal{S}
. Every
s\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
can be uniquely represented as the composition

s=\Circ_{\nu\in H}s_{\nu,\lambda_{\nu}}

where the
\lambda_{\nu}
are strictly positive. Note that in this representation, every hidden neuron
\nu
is associated to exactly one neuron-wise rescaling
\lambda_{\nu}
.

Definition 1
.

\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
are rescaling equivalent if there exists
s\in\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
such that
\theta^{\prime}=s(\theta)
. We then denote
\theta\sim_{S}\theta^{\prime}
.

Notice that if
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{S}\theta
, then the output biases are equal:
\theta^{\prime}_{\eta}=\theta_{\eta}
for all
\eta\in N_{L}
.

Fact 1
.

\theta^{\prime}\sim_{S}\theta
if, and only if, there exists diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}\times N_{\ell}}
with positive entries,
0\leq\ell\leq L
such that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{0}=\boldsymbol{I}_{N_{0}}
,
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{L}=\boldsymbol{I}_{N_{L}}
, and for every layer
1\leq\ell\leq L

(2)

\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell-1}^{-1}\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{b}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{b}_{\ell}.

Permutation equivalence

Consider
\pi:=(\pi_{1},\ldots,\pi_{\ell})
where
\pi_{\ell}\in\mathfrak{S}_{N_{\ell}}
is a permutation of the
\ell
-th hidden layer (input and output layers are never permuted),
1\leq\ell\leq L-1
. Denote
\mathfrak{S}_{G}=\mathfrak{S}_{N_{1}}\times\ldots\times\mathfrak{S}_{N_{L-1}}
the group of all such tuples of permutations. One can define a natural action of the group
\mathfrak{S}_{G}
on parameterizations via
\theta\mapsto\pi\circ\theta:=\theta^{\prime}
where each weight matrix
\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}_{\ell}
is obtained from
\boldsymbol{W}_{\ell}
by permuting rows according to
\pi_{\ell}
and columns according to
\pi_{\ell-1}
, while bias vector
\boldsymbol{b}^{\prime}_{\ell}
is a permuted version of
\boldsymbol{b}_{\ell}
according to
\pi_{\ell}
.

Definition 2
.

Two parameters
\theta,\theta^{\prime}
are
permutation-equivalent
if, and only if, there exists
\pi\in\mathfrak{S}_{G}
such that
\theta^{\prime}=\pi\circ\theta
. This is denoted
\theta\sim_{P}\theta^{\prime}
.

The parameters are
permutation-scaling equivalent
if, and only if, there exists
\theta^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta\sim_{S}\theta^{\prime\prime}\sim_{P}\theta^{\prime}
. This is denoted
\theta\sim_{PS}\theta^{\prime}
.

The parameters are
scaling-permutation equivalent
if, and only if, there exists
\theta^{\prime\prime}
such that
\theta\sim_{P}\theta^{\prime\prime}\sim_{S}\theta^{\prime}
. This is denoted
\theta\sim_{SP}\theta^{\prime}
.

Fact 2
.

\theta^{\prime}\sim_{PS}\theta
if, and only if,
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{SP}\theta
, if and only if there exists diagonal matrices
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}\times N_{\ell}}
with positive entries and permutation matrices
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}\times N_{\ell}}
,
0\leq\ell\leq L
,
such that
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{0}=\boldsymbol{I}_{N_{0}}
,
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{L}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{L}=\boldsymbol{I}_{N_{L}}
, and for every layer
1\leq\ell\leq L

(3)

\boldsymbol{W}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{W}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell-1}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\ell-1}^{-1}\ \text{and}\ \boldsymbol{b}^{\prime}_{\ell}=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{\ell}\boldsymbol{b}_{\ell}.

As widely documented

[
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
, PS-equivalent parameters share their realization as proven, e.g., in
[
9
]
[Lemma 1].

Lemma 1
.

For any
\theta,\theta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
, if
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{PS}\theta
then
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
.

A natural question is to determine conditions for the
identifiability
of (the equivalence class up to scaling and permutation of)
\theta
from
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
.
To be more specific, we consider identifiability with respect to a family of parameters
\Theta
, from a set
\mathcal{X}
. A case of particular interest will be when
\mathcal{X}
is finite, in order to characterize whether
\theta
can be recovered (up to scaling and permutations) from finitely many samples of the network realization
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
.

Definition 3

(PS-identifiability)
.

A parameter
\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
is PS-identifiable with respect to
\Theta
from
\mathcal{X}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
if for every
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta
, the equality
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{X}
implies
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{PS}\theta
. When considering
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
,
\theta
is simply said to be PS-identifiable with respect to
\Theta
.
When considering
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
,
\theta
is simply said to be PS-identifiable (from
\mathcal{X}
).

A trivial observation is that if all outgoing weights of a hidden neuron are zero, then the realization of the network is unchanged under arbitrary modifications of the incoming weights and of the bias
of this neuron
, hence the corresponding parameter
\theta

cannot
be PS-identifiable with respect to
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
. A similar phenomenon occurs if all incoming weights to a hidden neuron are zero. This motivates the definition of admissible parameters and proves
Lemma 2
below.

Definition 4
.

\theta
is admissible if for each hidden neuron
\nu\in H
we have
\boldsymbol{w}_{\bullet\to\nu}\neq 0
and
\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu\to\bullet}\neq 0
. Equivalently, every hidden neuron belongs to a full path with nonzero weights.

Lemma 2
.

If
\theta
is PS-identifiable
from
\mathcal{X}
with respect to
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
, then it is admissible.

) calls for an invariant representation of equivalence classes of network parameters. A central tool is a representation
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
mapping a network parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
to a vector
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
in a space indexed by paths of the network,
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}}

Definition 6
.

Given

, the value of a path is

(4)

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{p}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\Pi_{e\in p}\theta_{e},\ \text{for each full path}\ p\in\mathcal{P}_{0},

(5)

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{p}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\theta_{p_{\ell}}\Pi_{e\in p}\theta_{e},\text{for}\ p=(p_{\ell},\ldots,p_{L})\in\mathcal{P}_{\ell},1\leq\ell\leq L.

\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta):=({\Phi}_{p}(\theta))_{p\in\mathcal{P}}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}}

This representation,
combined with the entrywise sign of

characterizes the classes of scaling-equivalent admissible parameters.

Theorem 1
.

Consider any
\theta^{\prime},\theta\in\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
.

a)

Assume that
\theta\sim_{S}\theta^{\prime}
. Then
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta^{\prime})
and
\mathtt{sign}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathtt{sign}(\theta)
.

b)

Assume that
\theta
is admissible, that
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
, and that
\mathtt{sign}(\theta^{\prime}_{E})=\mathtt{sign}(\theta_{E})
.

Then
\theta\sim_{S}\theta^{\prime}
and
\theta^{\prime}
is also admissible.

The proof is in Section
3
. A similar result is proven in

without considering the biases and by replacing the condition on the signs by a condition on the activation statuses of all partial paths, which depend on the input variable

besides

.

Remark 2
.

The map
\theta\mapsto\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
will be referred to as an
embedding
of network parameters. Stricto-sensu, as this map is not an injective function of network parameters, it does not match the definition of an embedding. However, since it characterizes equivalence classes of rescaling-equivalent admissible parameters, it can be used to define without ambiguity an embedding of these equivalence classes in
\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{P}}
.

2.5.
Some consequences of PS-identifiability

Using the embedding
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\cdot)
, we show that if
\theta
is PS-identifiable then it is
locally
identifiable
up to scaling only
.
Locality is measured in the sense of open balls
B(\boldsymbol{c},r)=\{\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}:\|\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{c}\|_{\infty}<r\}
, where the ambient linear space, equipped with the sup-norm, should always be clear from context.

Definition 7

(local S-identifiability)
.

Given
\epsilon>0
, a parameter
\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
is

\epsilon
-locally S-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
with respect to
\Theta
, if for every
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta\cap B(\theta,\epsilon)
, the identity
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{X}
implies
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{S}\theta
.
If there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
\theta
is
\epsilon
-locally S-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}
then
\theta
is locally S-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}
.
When
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
and/or
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
we adopt the same simplified terminology as with the notion of PS-identifiability.

Remark 3
.

If

Definition 8

5.
Identifiability for shallow neural networks

In this section we focus on shallow networks, for which the set
\mathcal{Q}=\mathcal{Q}_{1}
of paths is in bijection with the set
H=N_{1}
of hidden neurons. Identifying these sets the activation vectors also coincide
\boldsymbol{\alpha}(\theta,x)=\boldsymbol{a}_{1}(\theta,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathbb{R}^{N_{1}}=\mathbb{R}^{H}

using the notion of twin neurons, we show that the absence of twin neurons implies non-degeneracy (hence local S-identifiability), and that its combination with irreducibility implies PS-identifiability. Finally, we discuss what happens in the presence of twin neurons.

5.1.

Whenever
\theta
is admissible, each hidden neuron

is not dead, i.e.

and

Each equivalence class

\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}:=B(x^{\prime}_{\nu},\epsilon^{\prime}_{\nu})\subseteq\Omega_{\nu}
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\cap\bar{\mathcal{T}^{\prime}}=\emptyset
and
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\cap\mathcal{V}_{\nu}=\mathcal{V}_{\nu}
. Since
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\cap\bar{\mathcal{T}}=\emptyset
, the function
\boldsymbol{\psi}
is affine linear on
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}
, hence it has constant Jacobian on
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}
.
Denote
\Omega_{\nu}^{+}:=\{x\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}:\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu}>0\}
,
\Omega_{\nu}^{-}:=\{x\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}:\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu}<0\}
, and observe that both sets are non-empty.
For any
x\in\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{\nu}=\Omega_{\nu}^{+}\cup\Omega_{\nu}^{-}
, the function
\boldsymbol{\varphi}
is differentiable and its Jacobian is

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x)=\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu})+\boldsymbol{d}

where
\boldsymbol{d}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and

\displaystyle H(t):=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if}\ t>0\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For each
x_{\nu}^{+}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{+},x_{\nu}^{-}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{-}
we have
H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{+}\rangle+b_{\nu})-H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{-}\rangle+b_{\nu})=1
, hence
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{+})-\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{-})=\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}
. As
\boldsymbol{\psi}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{X}\supseteq\Omega_{\nu}\supseteq\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}
and
\boldsymbol{\psi}
has constant Jacobian on
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}
, it follows that
\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}=0
, which contradicts our assumptions.
Hence, there is
\bar{\mu}\in\bar{N}_{1}
such that
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bar{\mu}}=\mathcal{V}_{\nu}
. Since the hyperplanes
\{\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\bar{\nu}}\}_{\bar{\nu}\in\bar{N}_{1}}
are pairwise disjoint by construction, such a
\bar{\mu}
is unique and we define
\pi(\nu):=\bar{\mu}
. Since this holds for every
\nu\in N_{1}
, we can define the map
\pi:N_{1}\to\bar{N}_{1}
with
\pi(\nu):=\bar{\mu}
. For
\nu\neq\nu^{\prime}
we have
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\pi(\nu^{\prime})}=\mathcal{V}_{\nu^{\prime}}\neq\mathcal{V}_{\nu}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\pi(\nu)}
since the hyperplanes
\{\mathcal{V}_{\nu}\}_{\nu\in N_{1}}
are pairwise distinct. This proves the injectivity of
\pi
. As we have seen, this means that indeed
\pi
is a permutation of
N_{1}
. Without loss of generality, to simplify notations, we assume from now on that
\pi
is the identity.

For each
\nu\in N_{1}
, since
\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\nu}=\mathcal{V}^{\prime}_{\pi(\nu)}=\mathcal{V}_{\nu}
there is a nonzero
\lambda_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},b^{\prime}_{\nu})=\lambda_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},b_{\nu}).

Reasoning as above, with
\Omega_{\nu}^{\pm}
defined using
\Omega^{\prime}_{\nu}:=\Omega_{\nu}
, we obtain that

\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{+})-\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{-})=\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}.

for each
x_{\nu}^{+}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{+},x_{\nu}^{-}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{-}
, and that for each
x\in\Omega_{\nu}\backslash\mathcal{V}_{\nu}
, the Jacobian of
\psi
satisfies
\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\prime}(x)=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu})^{\top}H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu})+\boldsymbol{d}^{\prime}
with some
\boldsymbol{d}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
,
hence for each
x_{\nu}^{+}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{+},x_{\nu}^{-}\in\Omega_{\nu}^{-}

\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{+})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{-})=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu})^{\top}\left(H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{+}\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu})-H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{-}\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu})\right).

Since
(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},b^{\prime}_{\nu})=\lambda_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},b_{\nu})
and
\mathtt{sign}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{\pm}\rangle+b_{\nu})=\pm 1
, we have

\displaystyle H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{+}\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu})-H(\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x_{\nu}^{-}\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu})

\displaystyle=\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu}).

Moreover, as
\boldsymbol{\psi}=\boldsymbol{\varphi}
on
\mathcal{X}
, we have
\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{+})-\boldsymbol{\varphi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{-})=\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{+})-\boldsymbol{\psi}^{\prime}(x_{\nu}^{-})
, hence

\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu})^{\top}\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu}).

Since
\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu}=\lambda_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}
, this simplifies to

(40)

\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}(\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu})^{\top}\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}\lambda_{\nu}\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=|\lambda_{\nu}|\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}

Hence,
\boldsymbol{v}^{\prime}_{\nu}=\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}/|\lambda_{\nu}|
for each
\nu\in N_{1}
.

To conclude, it is enough to prove that
\lambda_{\nu}>0
for every
\nu\in N_{\nu}
. Using (
40
), we can re-write the equality
\boldsymbol{\varphi}(x)=\boldsymbol{\psi}(x)
for every
x
as follows:

(41)

\sum_{\nu\in{N_{1}}}\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\Big[\mathtt{ReLU}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu})-|\lambda_{\nu}|^{-1}\mathtt{ReLU}(\underbrace{\langle\boldsymbol{w}^{\prime}_{\nu},x\rangle+b^{\prime}_{\nu}}_{\lambda_{\nu}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu})})\Big]+\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}=0.

Now, we observe that

(42)

\mathtt{ReLU}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu})-|\lambda_{\nu}|^{-1}\mathtt{ReLU}(\lambda_{\nu}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu}))=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=1\\
\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu}&\text{if }\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=-1\end{cases}

We now show that
{T}:=\{\nu\in{N_{1}}\mid\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=-1\}=\emptyset
. Using (
42
), we re-write (
41
) as:

(43)

\sum_{\nu\in{T}}\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}(\langle\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu},x\rangle+b_{\nu})+\boldsymbol{c}-\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}=0.

Since this is valid for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{0}}
we get
\boldsymbol{c}=\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}
and
\sum_{\nu\in{T}}\boldsymbol{v}_{\nu}\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu}^{\top}=0
. In light of (
38
) the latter implies
{T}=\emptyset
, hence
\mathtt{sign}(\lambda_{\nu})=1
for all
\nu\in{N_{1}}
.
∎

5.4.
Local S-identifiability despite the presence of twins

It is natural to wonder if there exists shallow networks with twins that are nevertheless either non-degenerate, or locally S-identifiable, or PS-identifiable. Positive twins are excluded (for any network depth) by
Lemma 3
, hence we can focus on the case where there are
K\geq 1
nontrivial classes of twins, each made of a single pair of (distinct) negative twins (as any equivalence class with at least three twins necessarily contains two positive ones). We detail here the case
K=1
and leave to future work a more detailed analysis of what happens for
K\geq 2
.

Lemma 16

(
Single pair of negative twins).

Consider a shallow network architecture. If
\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
is admissible with a single pair of negative twins,
\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}\subseteq H
, then

(44)

\mathtt{A}^{\perp}(\theta)=\{0\}\ \text{and}\ \mathtt{\bar{A}}^{\perp}(\theta)=\mathtt{span}\left\{\delta_{\nu_{1}}+\delta_{\nu_{2}}-\delta_{\star}\right\}\neq\{0\}

where
\delta_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}^{H+1},\nu\in H
is the
\nu
-th canonical eigenvector, and
\delta_{\star}=(\boldsymbol{0}_{H},1)
.

Moreover, if at least one of the following conditions holds:

i)

\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu_{1}\to\bullet}
,
\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu_{2}\to\bullet}
are linearly independent (which is only possible if
|N_{2}|\geq 2
); or

ii)

\Theta
is contained in the set of parameters with zero output bias;

then
\theta
is non-degenerate with respect to
\Theta
. Conversely, if

iii)

\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu_{1}\to\bullet}
,
\boldsymbol{w}_{\nu_{2}\to\bullet}
are linearly dependent and
\theta
belongs to the interior of
\Theta
,

then
\theta
is degenerate with respect to
\Theta
.

Remark 7
.

Inspecting the proof shows that the assumption in iii) that
\theta
is in the interior of
\Theta
can be relaxed to: for small enough
\epsilon
, each parameter
\theta^{\prime}\in B(\theta,\epsilon)
differing from
\theta
only in terms of biases belongs to
\Theta\cap B(\theta,\epsilon)
.

The proof is in Appendix
H
. We are now equipped to show with an example that non-degeneracy and local-identifiability are distinct concepts.

Example 4

(Absolute value)
.

Consider a shallow architecture with scalar input and output and two hidden neurons. The absolute value can be written as
|x|=\mathtt{ReLU}(x)+\mathtt{ReLU}(-x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
where
\theta=(w_{\mu\to\nu_{1}}=1,w_{\mu\to\nu_{2}}=-1,b_{\nu_{1}}=b_{\nu_{2}}=0,w_{\nu_{1}\to\eta}=w_{\nu_{2}\to\eta}=1,b_{\eta}=0)
has a single pair of negative twins.
This parameter
\theta
satisfies the following properties

i)

it is not PS-identifiable from any bounded set
\mathcal{X}\subset\mathbb{R}
(by
Lemma 3
);

ii)

it is not locally S-identifiable from any finite
F\subset\mathcal{X}_{\theta}
(i.e., it is degenerate, see below);

iii)

it is PS-identifiable (hence locally S-identifiable)
from
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}
;

iv)

it is locally S-identifiable from
F\cup\{0\}
for some finite set
F\subset\mathcal{X}_{\theta}
;

The last two points are detailed in
Appendix I
. Let us detail ii) here. Since
|N_{2}|=1
, by
Lemma 16
-iii) we get that
\theta
is degenerate with respect to
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
, i.e. not locally S-identifiable from any finite
F\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\theta}
. Indeed, if
F\subseteq\mathcal{X}_{\theta}=\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
is finite then
F\subset(-\infty,-t]\cup[t,+\infty)
for some
t>0
, and
\mathtt{abs}
coincides on
F
with (see
Figure 2
-(c))

\mathtt{ReLU}(x-t)+\mathtt{ReLU}(-(x+t))+t=\begin{cases}-x,&x\leq-t\\
t,&|x|\leq t\\
x,&x\geq t.\end{cases}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)

where
\theta^{\prime}
has nonzero biases, so that
\theta^{\prime}\not\sim_{PS}\theta
.

Example 5

(Revisiting the identity function from
Example 1
)
.

The identity function from
Example 1
is another example with a single pair of twin neurons. With
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
the parameter
\theta_{0}
is not locally S-identifiable (from
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}
) as already explained in
Example 1
.
With
\Theta=\Theta_{0}\subsetneq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
the set of parameters with zero output bias,
\theta_{0}
is on the contrary PS-identifiable from
\mathbb{R}
(see details in
Appendix J
). It can also be shown that

\theta_{0}
is
non-degenerate with respect to
\Theta_{0}
, using arguments similar to those used in
Appendix I
to prove item iv) of

. This illustrates the fact that, in the presence of a pair of negative twins, many things can happen: the parameter can be PS-identifiable and non- degenerate, or not even locally S-identifiable.

5.5.
Discussion of the role of activation spaces

For shallow irreducible networks, PS-identifiability from a bounded set is equivalent (
Theorem 3
) to the absence of twin neurons, which corresponds (
Lemma 15
) to a completeness property of the activation space that reads
\mathtt{\bar{A}}^{\perp}(\theta)=\{0\}
. The property
\mathtt{\bar{A}}^{\perp}(\theta)=\{0\}
also implies non-degeneracy (
Lemma 14
), yet a consequence of
Lemma 16
is that the converse does not generally hold
(and that the weaker assumption
\mathtt{A}^{\perp}(\theta)=\{0\}
is no longer sufficient to imply non-degeneracy). An exception occurs for scalar-valued shallow networks.

Lemma 17
.

Consider a
scalar-valued
shallow architecture (
|N_{2}|=1
). If
\theta
belongs to the interior of
\Theta\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
and is non-degenerate with respect to
\Theta
then

\mathtt{\bar{A}}^{\perp}(\theta)=\{0\}
.

This exception is a consequence of the following result.

Lemma 18
.

Consider a scalar-valued shallow network architecture. If
\theta
is admissible then there is
0<C<\infty
such that: for each
\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{Q}+1}
, there exists

(45)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{i}}_{\eta}(\theta^{\prime})-\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{i}}_{\eta}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}\times N_{0}},

(46)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{h}}_{\eta}(\theta^{\prime})-\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{h}}_{\eta}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{z}

where
\eta
is the single output neuron constituting the output layer
N_{L}
.
The parameters
\theta
and
\theta^{\prime}
differ only in terms of biases.

Proof.

Write
\boldsymbol{z}=(\boldsymbol{y},\gamma)
with
\boldsymbol{y}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathbb{R}^{H}
and
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. For each hidden neuron
\nu\in H=N_{1}
,
denote

the unique weight from neuron
\nu
to the single output neuron.
For each input neuron
\mu\in N_{0}
, the
\mu
-th column of

is
\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{i}}_{\mu\to\eta}(\theta):=(w_{\mu\to\nu}v_{\nu})_{\nu\in H}

\boldsymbol{\Phi}^{\mathtt{h}}_{\eta}(\theta)=\left((b_{\nu}v_{\nu})_{\nu\in H},b_{\eta}\right)^{\top}
.
To prove the result we define
\theta^{\prime}
with identical weights as

driven by

∎

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{1}x)+\alpha_{2}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{2}x)+b^{\prime}_{\eta},\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R}.

If we had
s_{1}=s_{2}
, the realization would be
(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{1}x)+b^{\prime}_{\eta}
, which cannot match
\mathtt{abs}
, hence
s_{2}=-s_{1}
. Without loss of generality (up to a permutation of indices of the hidden layer)
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=-1
. Now, for
x<0
we have
-x=|x|=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=-\alpha_{2}x+b^{\prime}_{\eta}
while for
x>0
we get
x=|x|=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}x+b_{\eta}
, hence
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=1
,
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
. Overall, up to the possible permutation of the hidden layer, we obtain
\mathtt{sign}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathtt{sign}(\theta)
and
\alpha_{1}s_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}s_{2}=-1
,
\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}=\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}=0
,
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
, hence
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
. Since
\theta
is admissible, it follows by
Theorem 1
that
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{PS}\theta
. Since this holds for any
\theta^{\prime}
such that
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
, this shows that
\theta
is PS-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\Theta=\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
.

\theta
is locally S-identifiable from some finite set
F\subset\mathbb{R}
(with
0\in F
)

With the same notations as above, observe that there is
\epsilon>0
such that for every
\theta^{\prime}\in B(\theta,\epsilon)
we have
s_{i}:=\mathtt{sign}(w^{\prime}_{\mu\to\nu_{i}})=\mathtt{sign}(w_{\mu\to\nu_{i}})
,
i=1,2
, and
\max(|\alpha_{1}-1|,|\alpha_{2}-1|,|\beta_{1}|,|\beta_{2}|,|b^{\prime}_{\eta}|)\leq 1/2
. Consider
\theta^{\prime}\in B(\theta,\epsilon)
such that
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}
on
F=\{-3,-2,-1,0,1,2,3\}
.
We have
s_{1}=+1
,
s_{2}=-1
hence

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}\mathtt{ReLU}(x-\beta_{1})+\alpha_{2}\mathtt{ReLU}(-x-\beta_{2})+b^{\prime}_{\eta}.

Since
|\beta_{i}|\leq 1/2
for
i=1,2
, we have

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}(x-\beta_{1})+b^{\prime}_{\eta}

for every
x\geq 1/2
, hence
\alpha_{1}(y-x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(y)-\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}(y)-\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}(x)=|y|-|x|=y-x
for
(x,y)=(1,2)
. Therefore
\alpha_{1}=1
. A similar reasoning
with
(x,y)=(-2,-1)
shows that
\alpha_{2}=1
, hence

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\mathtt{ReLU}(x-\beta_{1})+\mathtt{ReLU}(-x-\beta_{2})+b^{\prime}_{\eta}

Specializing to
x=1
, since
x-\beta_{1}>0
and
-x-\beta_{2}<0
we get
1=|x|=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=x-\beta_{1}+b^{\prime}_{\eta}=1-\beta_{1}+b^{\prime}_{\eta}
hence
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=\beta_{1}
. Similarly, with
x=-1
, we get
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=\beta_{2}
hence

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\mathtt{ReLU}(x-b^{\prime}_{\eta})+\mathtt{ReLU}(-x-b^{\prime}_{\eta})+b^{\prime}_{\eta}.

Specializing to
x=0\in F
yields

0=|x|=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=b^{\prime}_{\eta}+2\mathtt{ReLU}(-b^{\prime}_{\eta})=\begin{cases}b^{\prime}_{\eta}&\text{if}\ b^{\prime}_{\eta}\geq 0\\
-b^{\prime}_{\eta}&\text{if}\ b^{\prime}_{\eta}\leq 0\end{cases}=|b^{\prime}_{\eta}|

hence
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
. Overall we have shown that for every
\theta^{\prime}\in B(\theta,\epsilon)
such that

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}
on
F=\{-2,-1,0,1,2\}
we have
\alpha_{1}=\alpha_{2}=1
,
s_{1}=1,s_{2}=-1
,
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
. These imply
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta)
and
\mathtt{sign}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathtt{sign}(\theta)
hence
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{S}\theta
. In other words,
\theta
is locally S-identifiable from
F
.

Appendix J
Details on
Example 5

Here we show, as claimed in
Example 5
that the parameter
\theta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{E\cup\bar{H}}
from
Example 1
is PS-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}
with respect to the set
\Theta_{0}
of parameters with zero output biases. Consider an arbitrary
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{0}
. If
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta_{0}}(x)=x
on
\mathcal{X}
then
\theta^{\prime}
is admissible (otherwise its realization would be, up to an additive constant, proportional to a single shifted version of the ReLU, which would prevent it from being equal to
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta}=\mathtt{id}
) hence
w^{\prime}_{\nu\to\nu_{i}}\neq 0
,
i=1,2
. Writing
\alpha_{i}=|w^{\prime}_{\mu\to\nu_{i}}|\ w^{\prime}_{\nu_{i}\to\eta}
and
\beta_{i}=-b^{\prime}_{\nu_{i}}/|w^{\prime}_{\mu\to\nu_{i}}|
, and
s_{i}=\mathtt{sign}(w^{\prime}_{\mu\to\nu_{i}})\in\{-1,+1\}
for
i=1,2
, we have
\alpha_{i}\neq 0
and since the output bias is zero

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{1}(x-s_{1}\beta_{1}))+\alpha_{2}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{2}(x-s_{2}\beta_{2})),\ \forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

If we had
s_{1}\beta_{1}\neq s_{2}\beta_{2}
,
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}
would be non-differentiable at two distinct points
s_{1}\beta_{1},s_{2}\beta_{2}
. However
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta_{0}}=\mathtt{id}
is differentiable on
\mathbb{R}
, hence
s_{1}\beta_{1}=s_{2}\beta_{2}
. It follows that
s_{2}\beta_{1}=s_{1}\beta_{1}=\mathtt{id}(s_{1}\beta_{1})=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(s_{1}\beta_{1})=0
. Since
|s_{i}|=1
, we get
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
and

\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{1}x)+\alpha_{2}\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{2}x),\ \forall x\in\mathcal{X}.

If we had
s_{1}=s_{2}
, the realization would be
(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})\mathtt{ReLU}(s_{1}x)
, which cannot match
\mathtt{id}
on
\mathcal{X}
, hence
s_{2}=-s_{1}
. Without loss of generality (up to a permutation of indices of the hidden layer)
s_{1}=1
,
s_{2}=-1
. Now, for
x<0
we have
x=\mathtt{id}(x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=-\alpha_{2}x
while for
x>0
we get
x=\mathtt{id}(x)=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}(x)=\alpha_{1}x
, hence
\alpha_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}=-1
(and
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
because
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{0}
). Overall, up to the possible permutation of the hidden layer, we obtain
\mathtt{sign}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathtt{sign}(\theta_{0})
and
\alpha_{1}s_{1}=1
,
\alpha_{2}s_{2}=-1
,
\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}=\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}=0
,
b^{\prime}_{\eta}=0
, hence
\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\Phi}(\theta_{0})
. Since
\theta_{0}
is admissible, it follows by
Theorem 1
that
\theta^{\prime}\sim_{PS}\theta_{0}
. Since this holds for any
\theta^{\prime}\in\Theta_{0}
such that
\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{R}_{\theta_{0}}
, this shows that
\theta
is PS-identifiable from
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}
with respect to
\Theta_{0}
.

ON THE CODERIVATIVE OF THE PROJECTION OPERATOR ONTO THE POSITIVE CONE IN HILBERT SPACES
Le Van Hien, Nguyen Viet Quan

Abstract
In this paper, we study the generalized differentiability of the metric projection operator onto the positive cone in Hilbert spaces. We first establish the formula for exactly computing the regular coderivative and the Mordukhovich coderivative of the metric projection operator onto the positive cone in Euclidean spaces. Then, these results are also established for the projection operator onto the positive cone in the real Hilbert space l 2 l_{2} .



Key words.
metric projection, positive cone, regular coderivative, Mordukhovich coderivative, Hilbert spaces

Preliminaries

We first give the following notations that will be used throughout the paper. Let
X
be Hilbert space with scalar product
\langle.,.\rangle
and norm
\|.\|
. Given a set
C\subset X,
we denote by
C^{o}
its interior. Denote
t\downarrow 0
means that
t\to 0
with
t>0
. The symbol
\mathbb{N}
is the set of natural numbers.

Below are basic notions and facts from variational analysis, which are frequently used in the following; see
[
1
,
14
,
21
,
25
,
26
]
for more details.

First, We recall the concepts of Gâteaux directional differentiability and the Fréchet differentiability of the metric projection operator
P_{C}:H\rightarrow C.
For
\bar{x}\in X
and
w\in X\backslash\{\theta\}
, if the following limit exists,

P_{C}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{x})(w)=\lim\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{P_{C}(\bar{x}+tw)-P_{C}(\bar{x})}{t},

then,
P_{C}
is said to be Gâteaux directionally differentiable at point
\bar{x}
along direction
w
and
P_{C}^{{}^{\prime}}(\bar{x})(w)
is called the Gâteaux directional derivative of
P_{C}
at point
\bar{x}
along direction
w
.

P_{C}
is called Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}\in X
if there is a linear continuous mapping
\nabla P_{C}(\bar{x}):X\to X
such that

\lim\limits_{h\to 0}\dfrac{\|P_{C}(\bar{x}+h)-P_{C}(\bar{x})-\nabla P_{C}(\bar{x})(h)\|}{\|h\|}=0.

Then,
\nabla P_{C}(\bar{x})
is said to be the Fréchet derivative of
P_{C}
at
\bar{x}
.

In particular, the mapping
P_{C}
is called strictly Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}\in X
if

\lim\limits_{(u,v)\to(\bar{x},\bar{x})}\dfrac{\|P_{C}(u)-P_{C}(v)-\nabla P_{C}(\bar{x})(u-v)\|}{\|u-v\|}=0.

The mapping
P_{C}
is called Gâteaux directionally differentiable (Fréchet differentiable, strictly Fréchet differentiable) on
\Omega\subset X
if
P_{C}
is Gâteaux directionally differentiable (Fréchet differentiable, strictly Fréchet differentiable, respectively) at every point
x\in\Omega
.

Definition 2.1
.

(see
[
21
,
25
]
)
Let
\Omega
be a nonempty subset of a Hilbert space
H
and
\bar{x}\in\Omega
, we denote by
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
the
Fréchet (regular) normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, defined by

\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\Big\{z\in H|\limsup\limits_{u\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\Big\}.

The
limiting (Mordukhovich) normal cone
to
\Omega
at
\bar{x}
, denoted
N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
, is defined by

N_{\Omega}(\bar{x}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}\limits_{u\xrightarrow{\Omega}\bar{x}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(x)

where "Lim sup" is the Painlevé-Kuratowski outer limit of sets (see
[
25
]
)

If
\bar{x}\not\in\Omega
, one puts

\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=\emptyset
by convention. When the set
\Omega
is convex,
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{x})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{x})
amounts to the classic normal cone in the sense of
convex analysis.

Definition 2.2
.

(see
[
21
,
25
]
)
Let
H
be Hilbert space and
\Phi:H\rightrightarrows H
be a set-valued mapping with its graph gph
\Phi:=\{(x,y)|y\in\Phi(x)\}
and its domain Dom
\Phi:=\{x|\Phi(x)\not=\emptyset\}
. The multifunction
\widehat{D}^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y}):H\rightrightarrows H
, defined by

\widehat{D}^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})(w):=\big\{v\in H|(v,-w)\in\widehat{N}_{\mbox{\rm gph}\Phi}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in H

is called
regular coderivative
of
\Phi
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

Analogously, the multifunction

D^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y}):H\rightrightarrows H
, defined by

D^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})(w):=\big\{v\in H|(v,-w)\in N_{\mbox{\rm gph}\Phi}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}\ \mbox{for all}\ w\in H

is called
limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative
of
\Phi
at
(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

In the case
\Phi(\bar{x})=\{\bar{y}\},
we usually write
\widehat{D}^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x})(D^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x})).
If
\Phi:H\rightarrow H
is strictly Fréchet differentiable at
\bar{x}
, then
D^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x})(y)=\widehat{D}^{\ast}\Phi(\bar{x})(y)=\{\nabla\Phi(\bar{x})(y)\},\ \mbox{for all}\ y\in H.

Note that gph
\Phi\subset H\times H
, where
H\times H
be the orthogonal product of
H
equipped with the inner product
\langle.,.\rangle_{H\times H}
, which is abbreviated as
\langle.,.\rangle
. It is defined by,

\langle(x,y),(u,v)\rangle=\langle x,v\rangle+\langle y,v\rangle,\ \ \mbox{for any}\ (x,y),(u,v)\in H\times H.

Let
\|.\|_{H\times H}
be the norm in
H\times H
induced by the inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
. Then, we have

\|(x,y)\|_{H\times H}=\sqrt{\langle(x,y),(x,y)\rangle}=\sqrt{\|x\|^{2}+\|y\|^{2}}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x,y\in H.

The following result is given in
[
6
]
, which provides a useful formula to calculate the Fréchet coderivatives of single-valued mappings on Hilbert spaces.

Lemma 2.3
.

(see
[
6
]
)

Let
H
be a Hilbert space,
f:H\rightarrow H
be a Lipschitz continuous mapping on
H
. Then, the regular coderivatives of
f
at a point
\bar{x}\in H
satisfies that, for any
y\in H
,

\widehat{D}^{\ast}f(\bar{x})(y)=\left\{z\in H\big|\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,f(u)-f(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0\right\}.

3
Coderivative of the metric projection onto the positive cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n}
denote the n-dimensional Euclidean space with origin
\theta
. We recall some notations and concepts from
[
14
]
, which will be used in this section. For any
x=(x_{1},...,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define three subsets of the set
\{1,2,...,n\}
with respect to the given
x
by

x^{+}:=\{i\in\{1,...,n\}:x_{i}>0\};

x^{-}:=\{i\in\{1,...,n\}:x_{i}<0\};

x^{\bullet}:=\{i\in\{1,...,n\}:x_{i}=0\}.

Let K denote the positive cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, which is defined by

K:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{-}=\emptyset\}.

Then,
K
is a pointed closed and convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with the interior given by

K^{o}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{-}=x^{\bullet}=\emptyset\}.

Define

\widehat{K}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{+}\not=\emptyset,x^{-}\not=\emptyset,x^{\bullet}=\emptyset\},

and

\Delta\mathbb{R}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset\}.

For any given fixed
x\in\widehat{K}
, we define a mapping
b(x;.):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by

b(x;w)_{i}:=\begin{cases}w_{i}\ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in x^{+},\\
0\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\not\in x^{+},\end{cases}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

It follows from
[
14
]
that the metric projection operator
P_{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow K
has the following properties:

(i)

P_{K}(x)_{i}:=\begin{cases}x_{i}\ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in x^{+},\\
0\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\not\in x^{+},\end{cases}\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)
P_{K}
is a Lipschitz continuous mapping on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lipschitz constant
L=1
, i.e

\|P_{K}(x)-P_{K}(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|,\ \ \mbox{for all}\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iii)
P_{K}
is strictly Fréchet differentiable on
K^{o},(-K)^{o}
and
\widehat{K}
;
P_{K}
is Gâteaux directionally differentiable but not Fréchet differentiable on
\Delta\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The following result provides the formula for calculating the regular coderivatives of the metric projection onto the positive cone in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
K
be the positive cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the regular coderivative of the projection operator
P_{K}
at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(3.1)

Proof.

Let
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, thanks to the Lipschitz continuity of
P_{K}
, by Lemma
2.3
, we have

z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\iff\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0.

(3.2)

Suppose that
z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)
. We take a directional line segment in the limit in (
3.2
),
u=(u_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i}+t(z_{i}-y_{i}),\ \mbox{for}\ i\in\bar{x}^{+};\ u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i}+tz_{i},\ \mbox{for}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-};\ u_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
, where
t\downarrow 0
. It follows that

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{t\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})^{2}+t\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}^{2}}{\sqrt{t^{2}\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})^{2}+t^{2}\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}^{2}}}\\
&=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})^{2}+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}^{2}}.\end{array}

This implies that

z_{i}=y_{i},\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{+}\ \ \mbox{and}\ \ z_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}.

(3.3)

Assume, by the way of contradiction, that that there exists
i_{0}\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
z_{i_{0}}>y_{i_{0}}
. Choosing
u=(u_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},\ \mbox{for }\ i\not=i_{0};\ u_{i_{0}}=t
for
t\downarrow 0
. We have

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{t\left(z_{i_{0}}-y_{i_{0}}\right)}{t}\\
&=z_{i_{0}}-y_{i_{0}}.\end{array}

This means
z_{i_{0}}\leq y_{i_{0}}
. This contradiction proves that

z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}.

(3.4)

Similarly, suppose there exists
i_{0}\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
z_{i_{0}}<0
. By choosing
u=(u_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},\ \mbox{for }\ i\not=i_{0};\ u_{i_{0}}=-t
for
t\downarrow 0
. We have

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\ast}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{-tz_{i_{0}}}{t}\\
&=-z_{i_{0}}.\end{array}

This shows that

z_{i}\geq 0,\ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}.

(3.5)

By (
3.3
), (
3.4
) and (
3.5
) together, we have

z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\Longrightarrow\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in\bar{x}^{+}\\
z_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}\\
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\end{cases}

(3.6)

Next, we prove the converse, assuming
z=(z_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
z_{i}=y_{i},\ i\in\bar{x}^{+};
z_{i}=0,\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}
and
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
. We need to show that

\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0.

Indeed, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\cap u^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})u_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\backslash u^{+}}z_{i}u_{i}}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\leq 0\ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\{\bar{x}\}.\end{array}

So,
\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0
. This means

\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in\bar{x}^{+}\\
z_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}\\
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\end{cases}\Longrightarrow z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).

(3.7)

Then, by (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), (
3.1
) is proved.
\square

Using Theorem
3.1
, we obtain the following result, part of which was given by Li in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

(see
[
15
, Theorem 4.1]
)

Let
K
be the positive cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with negative cone
-K
. Then, the regular coderivative of the projection operator
P_{K}
have the following representations.

(i) For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)=\begin{cases}\{y\}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \bar{x}\in K^{o}\\
\{\theta\}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \bar{x}\in(-K)^{o}\\
\{b(\bar{x};y)\}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \bar{x}\in\widehat{K}\\
\end{cases}

(ii) For any
\bar{x}\in\Delta\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

(a)
\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(\theta)=\{\theta\};

(b) For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

y^{-}\cap\bar{x}^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset\Longrightarrow\lambda y\not\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\ \mbox{for all}\ \lambda<1.

In particular,

y^{-}\cap\bar{x}^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset\Longrightarrow\theta\not\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).

(c) For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y^{-}\cap\bar{x}^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset\Longrightarrow\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)=\emptyset.

(d)
\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(\bar{x})=\{P_{K}(\bar{x})\}
.

(e)
\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\theta)(y)=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}[0,y_{i}].

Proof.

(i) For any
\bar{x}\in K^{o}
, we have
\bar{x}^{-}=\bar{x}^{\bullet}=\emptyset
. For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using (
3.1
), we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+}\right\}.\\
&=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},\forall i=1,2,...,n\}\\
&=\{y\}.\end{array}

For any
\bar{x}\in(-K)^{o}
, we have
\bar{x}^{+}=\bar{x}^{\bullet}=\emptyset
. For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using (
3.1
), we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-}\right\}.\\
&=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=0,\forall i=1,2,...,n\}\\
&=\{\theta\}.\end{array}

For any
\bar{x}\in\widehat{K}
, we have
\bar{x}^{\bullet}=\emptyset
. For any
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, using (
3.1
), we get

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-}\right\}.\\
&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\not\in\bar{x}^{+}\right\}.\\
&=\{b(\bar{x};y)\}.\end{array}

(ii.a) Using (
3.1
) with
y=0
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(\theta)&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq 0,i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\}\\
&=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=0,\forall i=1,2,...,n\}\\
&=\{\theta\}.\end{array}

(ii.b) If
y^{-}\cap\bar{x}^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset
, then there exists
i_{0}\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
y_{i_{0}}<0
. We have
\lambda y_{i_{0}}>y_{i_{0}}
for all
\lambda<1
. By (
3.1
),
\lambda y\not\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\ \mbox{for all}\ \lambda<1.
In particular, for
\lambda=0
, we get
\theta\not\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).

(ii.c) Since
y^{-}\cap\bar{x}^{\bullet}\not=\emptyset
, there exists
i_{0}\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
y_{i_{0}}<0
. This means that there does not exist
z=(z_{i})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
0\leq z_{i_{0}}\leq y_{i_{0}}
, and
\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)=\emptyset.

(ii.d) Using (
3.1
) with
y=\bar{x}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(\bar{x})&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq 0,i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\}\\
&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\not\in\bar{x}^{+}\right\}\\
&=\{P_{K}(\bar{x})\}.\end{array}

(ii.e) Using (
3.1
) with
\bar{x}=\theta

\bar{x}^{+}=\bar{x}^{-}=\emptyset,\ \bar{x}^{\bullet}=\{1,2,...,n\}
and

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\theta)(y)&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\}.\\
&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},\forall i=1,2,...,n\right\}\\
&=\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{n}[0,y_{i}].\end{array}

\square

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
K
be the positive cone of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then, the Mordukhovich coderivative of the projection operator
P_{K}
at
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by

\begin{array}[]{rl}D^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)&=\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\\
&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\},\end{array}

(3.11)

\mbox{for all}\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Proof.
Since
\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\subset D^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)
, it suffices to prove

D^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\subset\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).

Take
z\in D^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)
. Then by definition, there exist
y^{k}\rightarrow y,x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
and
z^{k}\rightarrow z
such that
z^{k}\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(x^{k})(y^{k})
, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
By (
3.1
), we have

\begin{cases}z^{k}_{i}=y^{k}_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in(x^{k})^{+}\\
z^{k}_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in(x^{k})^{-}\\
0\leq z^{k}_{i}\leq y^{k}_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in(x^{k})^{\bullet}\end{cases}\ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ k\in\mathbb{N}.

Since
x^{k}\rightarrow\bar{x}
, we have
\bar{x}^{+}\subset(x^{k})^{+},\bar{x}^{-}\subset(x^{k})^{-}
and
(x^{k})^{\bullet}\subset\bar{x}^{\bullet}
. So, for all
i\in\bar{x}^{+}\subset(x^{k})^{+}
, we have

z_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}z^{k}_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}y^{k}_{i}=y_{i}.

(3.12)

For all
i\in\bar{x}^{-}\subset(x^{k})^{-}
,

z_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}z^{k}_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}0=0.

(3.13)

For
i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
, using a subsequence if necessary, we consider the following cases.

Case 1.

i\in(x^{k})^{+},
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
z_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}z^{k}_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}y^{k}_{i}=y_{i}.

Case 2.

i\in(x^{k})^{-},
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
z_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}z^{k}_{i}=\lim\limits_{k\rightarrow\infty}0=0.

Case 3.

i\in(x^{k})^{\bullet},
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We have
0\leq z^{k}_{i}\leq y^{k}_{i}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}.
Taking the limits as
k\rightarrow\infty
, we get
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}
.

Thus, for any
i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
,

0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}

(3.14)

Then, by (
3.12
), (
3.13
), (
3.14
) and (
3.1
),
z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).
The proof is complete.
\square

4
Coderivative of the metric projection onto the positive cone in real Hilbert space
l_{2}

In this section, we consider the real Hilbert space
l_{2}
with norm
\|.\|
and inner product
\langle.,.\rangle
. Let
\mathbb{K}
denote the positive cone of
l_{2}
defined by

\mathbb{K}:=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in l_{2}:x_{i}\geq 0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Let
P_{\mathbb{K}}:l_{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{K}
be the metric projection operator from
l_{2}
onto
\mathbb{K}
. It follows from
[
14
]
that for any
x\in l_{2}
,
P_{\mathbb{K}}(x)
is represented as follows

P_{\mathbb{K}}(x)_{i}=\begin{cases}x_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ x_{i}>0;\\
0\ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ x_{i}\leq 0;\end{cases}\mbox{for}\ i\in\mathbb{N}.

Furthermore,
P_{\mathbb{K}}
is a Lipschitz continuous mapping on
l_{2}
with Lipschitz constant
L=1
, i.e

\|P_{\mathbb{K}}(x)-P_{\mathbb{K}}(y)\|\leq\|x-y\|,\ \mbox{for all }\ x,y\in l_{2}.

And
P_{\mathbb{K}}
is positive homogeneous, i.e

P_{\mathbb{K}}(\lambda x)=\lambda P_{\mathbb{K}}(x),\ \ \mbox{for any}\ \ x\in l_{2}\ \mbox{and}\ \lambda>0.

By
[
14
, Theorem 5.3]
,
P_{\mathbb{K}}
is Gâteaux directionally differentiable but not Fréchet differentiable on sets
\mathbb{K}^{+},\mathbb{K}^{-}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{K}}
. Where,

\mathbb{K}^{+}:=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in l_{2}:x_{i}>0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}\right\};

\mathbb{K}^{-}:=\left\{x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in l_{2}:x_{i}<0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}\right\};

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{\mathbb{K}}:=\{&x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in l_{2}:|x_{i}|>0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\mathbb{N}\ \mbox{and}\\
&\mbox{there are at least one pair}\ j,k\in\mathbb{N}\ \mbox{with}\ x_{j}x_{k}<0\}.\end{array}

For any
x=(x_{1},x_{2},...)\in l_{2}
, define

x^{+}:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:x_{i}>0\};

x^{-}:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:x_{i}<0\};

x^{\bullet}:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:x_{i}<0\}.

The following result provides the formula for calculating the regular coderivatives of the metric projection onto the positive cone in
l_{2}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be the positive cone of
l_{2}
. Then, the regular coderivative of the projection operator
P_{\mathbb{K}}
at
\bar{x}\in l_{2}
is given by

\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)=\left\{z\in l_{2}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\},\ \ \mbox{for all}\ y\in l_{2}.

(4.1)

Proof.
Similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
. Take
\bar{x}\in l_{2}
.

Let
y\in l_{2}
, thanks to the Lipschitz continuity of
P_{K}
, by Lemma
2.3
, we have

z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\iff\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0.

(4.2)

Suppose that
z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)
, we prove

\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in\bar{x}^{+},\\
z_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-},\\
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}.\end{cases}

Indeed, suppose there exists
j\in\bar{x}^{+}
such that
z_{j}\not=y_{j},
by choosing
u\in l_{2}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},
for
i\not=j
and
u_{j}=x_{j}+t(z_{j}-y_{j}),
for
t\downarrow 0.
We have
u\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
t\downarrow 0
and

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{t(z_{j}-y_{j})^{2}}{t|z_{j}-y_{j}|}\\
&=|z_{j}-y_{j}|.\end{array}

This implies that

z_{i}=y_{i},\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{+}.

(4.3)

Suppose there exists
j\in\bar{x}^{-}
such that
z_{j}\not=0,
by choosing
u\in l_{2}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},
for
i\not=j
and
u_{j}=x_{j}+tz_{j},
for
t\downarrow 0.
We have
u\rightarrow\bar{x}
as
t\downarrow 0
and

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{tz_{j}^{2}}{t|z_{j}|}\\
&=|z_{j}|.\end{array}

This implies that

z_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}.

(4.4)

Assume, by the way of contradiction, that that there exists
j\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
z_{j}>y_{j}
. Choosing
u=(u_{i})\in l_{2}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i}
, for
i\not=j
and
u_{j}=t
, for
t\downarrow 0
. We have
u\rightarrow\bar{x}
and

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\ast}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{(z_{j}u_{j}-y_{j}(P_{K}(u))_{j})}{t}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{t\left(z_{j}-y_{j}\right)}{t}\\
&=z_{j}-y_{j}.\end{array}

This means
z_{j}\leq y_{j}
. This contradiction proves that

z_{i}\leq y_{i},\ \ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}.

(4.5)

Similarly, suppose there exists
j\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}
such that
z_{j}<0
. By choosing
u=(u_{i})\in l_{2}
with
u_{i}=\bar{x}_{i},
for
j\not=j
and
u_{j}=-t
, for
t\downarrow 0.
We get
u\rightarrow\bar{x}
and

\begin{array}[]{rl}0&\geq\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\ast}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\geq\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{(z_{j}u_{j}-y_{j}(P_{K}(u))_{j})}{t}\\
&=\limsup\limits_{t\downarrow 0}\dfrac{-tz_{j}}{t}\\
&=-z_{j}.\end{array}

This shows that

z_{i}\geq 0,\ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}.

(4.6)

By (
4.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) together, we have

z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y)\Longrightarrow\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in\bar{x}^{+}\\
z_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}\\
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\end{cases}

(4.7)

Next, we prove the converse, assuming
z\in l_{2}
with
z_{i}=y_{i}
, for
i\in\bar{x}^{+},

z_{i}=0,
for
i\in\bar{x}^{-}
and
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},
for
i\in~\bar{x}^{\bullet}
. We need to show that

\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0.

Indeed, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}&\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{-}}z_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{x}_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}}(z_{i}u_{i}-y_{i}(P_{K}(u))_{i})}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&=\dfrac{\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\cap u^{+}}(z_{i}-y_{i})u_{i}+\sum\limits_{i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\backslash u^{+}}z_{i}u_{i}}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\\
&\leq 0\ \ \ \ \mbox{for all}\ u\in l_{2}\backslash\{\bar{x}\}.\end{array}

So,
\limsup\limits_{u\rightarrow\bar{x}}\dfrac{\langle z,u-\bar{x}\rangle-\langle y,P_{K}(u)-P_{K}(\bar{x})\rangle}{\|u-\bar{x}\|}\leq 0
. This means

\begin{cases}z_{i}=y_{i}\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ \ i\in\bar{x}^{+}\\
z_{i}=0\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{-}\\
0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i}\ \ \ \mbox{if}\ i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\end{cases}\Longrightarrow z\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{K}(\bar{x})(y).

(4.8)

Then, by (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), (
4.1
) is proved.
\square

Let
N
be a nonempty subset of
\mathbb{N}
with complementary
\bar{N}
. We define some subsets and an ordering relation in
l_{2}
with respect to
N
.

\mathbb{R}^{N}=\left\{x=(x_{i})\in l_{2}:x_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for}\ i\in\bar{N}\right\};

\mathbb{K}_{N}=\left\{x=(x_{i})\in l_{2}:x_{i}\geq 0,\ \mbox{for}\ i\in N\right\};

\partial\mathbb{K}_{N}=\left\{x=(x_{i})\in\mathbb{K}_{N}:x_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for}\ i\in N\right\};

\mathbb{Z}_{N}=\left\{x=(x_{i})\in l_{2}:x_{i}>0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in N\ \mbox{and}\ x_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{N}\right\};

z\preceq_{N}y\Leftrightarrow z_{i}\leq y_{i},\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in N\ \mbox{and}\ z_{i}=y_{i},\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{N}.

Using Theorem
4.1
, we obtain the following result, which was given by Li in
[
15
]
.

Corollary 4.2
.

(see
[
15
, Theorem 5.1]
)

Let
M
be a nonempty finite subset of
\mathbb{N}
with complementary
\bar{M}
. The regular coderivative of
P_{\mathbb{K}}
have the following representations.

(i) For any
\bar{x}\in l_{2}
, we have

\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(\theta)=\{\theta\};

(ii) Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}.
For any
y\in l_{2}
,

y\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}\Longleftrightarrow y\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y);

(4.9)

(iii) Let
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}.
For any
y\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}
, we have

\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}:z\preceq_{M}y\right\}.

(4.10)

In particular,

y\in\partial\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}\Longrightarrow\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)=\{y\}.

Proof.

(i) Using (
4.1
) with
y=\theta
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(\theta)&=\left\{z\in l_{2}:z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq 0,i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\}\\
&=\{z\in l_{2}:z_{i}=0,\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\}\\
&=\{\theta\}.\end{array}

(ii, iii) Since
\bar{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{M}
,
\bar{x}^{+}=M,\bar{x}^{-}=\emptyset
and
\bar{x}^{\bullet}=\bar{M}.
By (
4.1
), for any
y\in l_{2}
, we have

\begin{array}[]{rl}\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)&=\left\{z\in l_{2}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{+};z_{i}=0,i\in\bar{x}^{-};0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{x}^{\bullet}\right\}\\
&=\left\{z\in l_{2}:z_{i}=y_{i},i\in M;0\leq z_{i}\leq y_{i},i\in\bar{M}\right\}\\
&=\{z\in l_{2}:z\preceq_{\bar{M}}y,\ z_{i}\geq 0,i\in\bar{M}\}\\
&=\left\{z\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}:z\preceq_{\bar{M}}y\right\}.\end{array}

So,

\begin{array}[]{rl}y\in\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)&\Longleftrightarrow y\in\left\{z\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}:z\preceq_{\bar{M}}y\right\}\\
&\Longleftrightarrow y\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}.\end{array}

In particular,

\begin{array}[]{rl}y\in\partial\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}&\Longrightarrow y_{i}=0,\ \mbox{for all}\ i\in\bar{M}\\
&\Longrightarrow\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)=\left\{z\in\mathbb{K}_{\bar{M}}:z\preceq_{\bar{M}}y\right\}\\
&\Longrightarrow\widehat{D}^{\ast}P_{\mathbb{K}}(\bar{x})(y)=\{y\}.\end{array}

\square

Stationary measures for SL 2 ⁡ ( ℝ ) \operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) -actions on homogeneous bundles over flag varieties Thanks: A.G. and J.L. were supported by the SNF grant 200021–182089; C.S. was supported by SNF Ambizione grant 193481
Alexander Gorodnik, Jialun Li, Cagri Sert

Abstract
. Let G G be a real semisimple Lie group with finite centre and without compact factors, Q < G Q<G a parabolic subgroup and X X a homogeneous space of G G admitting an equivariant projection on the flag variety G / Q G/Q with fibres given by copies of lattice quotients of a semisimple factor of Q Q . Given a probability measure μ \mu , Zariski-dense in a copy of H = SL 2 ⁡ ( ℝ ) H=\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R}) in G G , we give a description of μ \mu -stationary probability measures on X X and prove corresponding equidistribution results. Contrary to the results of Benoist–Quint corresponding to the case G = Q G=Q , the type of stationary measures that μ \mu admits depends strongly on the position of H H relative to Q Q . We describe possible cases and treat all but one of them, among others using ideas from the works of Eskin–Mirzakhani and Eskin–Lindenstrauss.



1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a real Lie group and
R<G
a closed subgroup. The actions of subgroups of
G
on the homogeneous space
X=G/R
constitute a natural class of dynamical systems whose (topological, statistical etc.) properties are of key relevance to various problems in mathematics. Accordingly, the study of such dynamical systems has a rich history; it has prompted the introduction of various new techniques and contains major results. The nature of these systems varies according to the acting subgroup and the group
R
to be factored out, ranging over classes such as partially hyperbolic, parabolic, proximal dynamics, etc.

One type of ambient homogeneous space
X
is obtained by considering quotients by discrete subgroups
R=\Lambda<G
. For the actions of connected subgroups of
G
generated by unipotents (e.g. semisimple subgroups) on these quotients
X
, settling conjectures of Raghunathan and Dani, definitive results were obtained by Ratner
[
28
,
29
]
. Her results can be considered as a vast generalization of classical results on vector flows on the tori
\mathbb{T}^{d}
and have far-reaching consequences. A key step/result in Ratner’s works — corresponding to Dani’s conjecture — is the classification of measures invariant under unipotent flows. To obtain this result, Ratner introduced an important technique, the polynomial drift argument.

Setting aside the actions of commuting diagonal flows, a next major step for actions on quotients
X
by lattices
\Lambda<G
is reached by the seminal work of Benoist–Quint
[
3
,
5
,
4
,
6
]
. Their work involved describing dynamics of actions by subgroups
\Gamma
whose algebraic (Zariski) closure has semisimplicity but the subgroups themselves can be genuinely irregular, e.g. discrete. Focusing on stationary measures of random walks on quotients
X
, they developed the exponential drift argument used to obtain a description of all stationary measures. The drift argument of Benoist–Quint requires a
precise control of random matrix products
(e.g. local limit theorem), a feature not readily available without a semisimplicity assumption. Benoist–Quint’s ideas were then remarkably modified in a non-homogeneous setting by Eskin–Mirzakhani
[
12
]
who managed to set up a much more flexible argument bypassing for example the need for a local limit theorem. This development enabled further extensions of Benoist–Quint’s results in several directions by Eskin–Lindenstrauss
[
10
,
11
]
. Some of our arguments in this article (e.g. the
six points drift argument) draws on the ideas of the latter works
[
12
,
10
]
and in fact can be seen as a slightly modified and simpler version of them.

Continuing to expound elements of our setting, a second type of homogeneous space is obtained by considering quotients by parabolic subgroups
R=Q<G
, giving rise to flag varieties
X=G/Q
. The dynamics on these quotients are quite different from those on quotients by discrete subgroups; in particular, when the acting group has semisimple (non-compact) Zariski-closure, the action is proximal (if the group is split) and the space supports no invariant measures. Starting with the pioneering works of Furstenberg
[
13
,
14
,
15
]
on random matrix products and boundary theory, a thorough qualitative description of dynamics is established by Guivarc’h–Raugi
[
16
]
and Benoist–Quint
[
7
]
.

The homogeneous space
X=G/R
considered in this article is a combination of the two types of classical homogeneous spaces discussed above; it has the structure of a fibre bundle over the flag variety
G/Q
with fibres given by a homogeneous space
S/\Lambda
, where
S
is a semisimple group and
\Lambda
is a discrete subgroup. To illustrate and motivate this structure, recall that a standard example for the first kind of spaces (obtained as quotients by discrete subgroups) is provided by the space

of rank-

of rank-

\mathbb{R}^{d}
(which is a standard example of a space realized as a quotient by a parabolic subgroup) with fibres given by copies of

Theorem 1.1

(Case 2.2: Diagonal flow invariance and product structure)
.

Let the space

and groups
G,Q,R_{0},R,S\simeq Q/R_{0},\Lambda\simeq R/R_{0}

Before finishing the introduction, we give a brief overview of the ideas of proofs used to obtain the main results of this paper.

Case 2.3 (measure classification): Decomposable action.
In some sense, the key difficulty in this case is to suspect the possibility of the existence of a decomposable action in our setting. Once one has this possibility in mind, one can use ideas about cocycles going back to Mackey

The key point that enables us to obtain the equidistribution result (Theorem

) and an alternative proof of Theorem

under a stronger moment assumption, is the fact that thanks to the particular situation we have precise control of random matrix products in the form of a uniform quantitative renewal theorem and exponential large deviation estimates. More precisely, under suitable trivialization, the Cesàro average can be expressed as

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\delta(g\theta,\alpha(\sigma_{\chi}(g,\theta))z)\ d\mu^{*k}(g),

with respect to compactly supported continuous functions. Combined with exponential large deviation estimates and good error estimates from the uniform quantitative renewal theorem, we can prove the equidistribution result.

2.
Preliminaries: Cocycles, decomposable actions and stationary measures

This section contains a collection of preliminaries for the proofs in the following parts. We adopt a general setting. In §

2.1.
Generalities on cocycles and decomposable actions

Let

be a closed subgroup and suppose we have a measurable

as a fibre bundle over

. A fruitful way to describe the

[

]
(see Varadarajan
[

2.1.1.
Cocycles defined by actions and vice versa

Given a bundle over
G/Q
with

-action, let

be a copy of the fibre above

\pi^{-1}(Q)
endowed with the

\phi(gx)=(g\phi_{1}(x),\alpha(g,\phi_{1}(x))\phi_{2}(x))

has a natural

-action. Recall that a cocycle

is a map satisfying
\alpha(g_{1}g_{2},f)=\alpha(g_{1},g_{2}f)\alpha(g_{2},f)
for every

and

(this corresponds to what is called a strict cocycle in
[
31
]
). By using the cocycle relation, one sees that any cocycle

endows the space

with a

-action. We shall denote the space

endowed with a
G
-action induced by a

. Therefore, a
(G,Q)
-bundle trivialization is a

-equivariant isomorphism between

and

for some

-valued cocycle
\alpha
.

In the rest of this paper, the ambient space

which we will work with will be, in particular, a homogeneous space of a lcsc group

. For

, we denote by

the stability group

so that we have a

of a base point

so that we have a continuous

-equivariant surjection

turning the homogeneous space

into a bundle over

with

-action. The choice of the base point

identifies the fibre

with the

-homogeneous space

. In this setting, any Borel section

yields a trivialization of

given by the Borel isomorphism

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\to G/Q\times Q/R

(2.1)

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(\pi(x),s(\pi(x))^{-1}x).

Here the Borel section

is a section of the principal

, i.e.
s(gQ)Q=gQ
.
Such a section

induces a Borel cocycle

(2.2)

which makes the trivialization (

-bundle trivialization.

For a

-bundle trivialization

In the sequel, we will assume further structure on the groups making up the space

2.1.2.
Equivalence of cocycles

Let

be a lcsc group. Two

are said to be equivalent, denoted

such that for every

and

, we have

\alpha(g,x)=\phi(gx)^{-1}\beta(g,x)\phi(x).

(2.3)

It is clear that for two

-valued cocycles
\alpha
and

over

, then the associated

and

are isomorphic. Accordingly,

-valued cocycles obtained from different sections

via (

) are equivalent.

In the sequel, we will be interested in actions of subgroups

of

on a

-homogeneous bundle

and the associated subbundles. We will use similar terminology for cocycles restricted to

. Let

be a closed subgroup of

and
\mathcal{C}\subseteq G/Q
an

-homogeneous closed subset of

. Up to replacing

by a conjugate, suppose

so that

where

. In that case

is an

-invariant closed (not necessarily

-homogeneous) subset of

giving rise to a bundle over

with

-action.

Example 2.1
.

The above situation appears in (and is motivated by) the setting of the work of Sargent–Shapira
[
30
]
: Let

be the set of homothety-equivalence classes of rank-2 lattices in

. The set

has a natural lcsc topology and the group

acts continuously and transitively on

. The connected component

of the stabilizer

consists of the solvable radical of a maximal parabolic group

in

and we have a surjective map

, where

. One can then consider the action of the subgroup

on

in

.

2.1.3.
Induced morphisms and decomposable subbundle actions

Recall the notion of a decomposable bundle in Definition
1.3
. We provide a criterion to ensure that an
H
-homogeneous bundle
X_{\mathcal{C}}
is decomposable.

Proposition 2.2
.

If the morphism
Q_{H}\hookrightarrow Q\twoheadrightarrow S
extends to a morphism
H\to S
, then
X_{\mathcal{C}}
is decomposable.

The following statement is a version of
[
32
, Proposition 4.2.16]
and provides a useful characterization of a decomposable action in our setting. Let
\operatorname{Co}(H,Q_{H},S)
denote the set of Borel cocycles
H\times H/Q_{H}\to S
. Given
\alpha\in\operatorname{Co}(H,Q_{H},S)
, the map
\rho_{\alpha}
defined by
\rho_{\alpha}(p):=\alpha(p,Q_{H})
for
p\in Q_{H}
defines a Borel (hence continuous) morphism from
Q_{H}\to S
.

The proof is based on an important observation of Mackey which characterizes equivalence classes of Borel cocycles
\operatorname{Co}(H,P,G^{\prime})
by conjugacy classes of induced morphisms
P\to G^{\prime}
where conjugacy is understood by an element of
G^{\prime}
in the target. We have the following result from
[
31
, Theorem 5.27]
that we adapt to our setting here.

Lemma 2.3
.

The map

\displaystyle\operatorname{Co}(H,P,G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{Hom}(P,G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho_{\alpha}

is a surjective map that descends to a bijection when we quotient
\operatorname{Co}(H,P,G^{\prime})
by equivalence of cocycles and
\operatorname{Hom}(P,G^{\prime})
by conjugation in
G^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Fix a Borel section
s:H/P\to H
with
s(P)=\operatorname{id}
. Given a morphism
\rho:P\to G^{\prime}
, the map given by

\alpha(h_{1},hP)=\rho(s(h_{1}hP)^{-1}h_{1}s(hP))

(2.4)

is a cocycle whose restriction to
P\simeq P\times\{P\}
recovers
\rho:P\to G^{\prime}
. This shows that the map is surjective.

Note that if two cocycles
H\times H/P\to G^{\prime}
are equivalent, then it is clear that the morphisms
P\to G^{\prime}
that they induce are conjugate by an element of
G^{\prime}
. In particular, the map
\alpha\mapsto\rho_{\alpha}
descends to a (surjective) map on the equivalence classes of cocycles.

To show that this map is a bijection, let
\alpha
and
\beta
be two cocycles
H\times H/P\to G^{\prime}
and suppose that
\rho_{\alpha}(.)=l\rho_{\beta}(.)l^{-1}
for some element
l\in G^{\prime}
. By direct computation, we have

\alpha(s(h_{1}hP),P)^{-1}\alpha(h_{1},hP)\alpha(s(hP),P)=\rho_{\alpha}(s(h_{1}hP)^{-1}h_{1}s(hP))^{-1}.

(2.5)

Writing the equivalent of (
2.5
) for
\beta
(substituting
\rho_{\beta}
for
\rho_{\alpha}
) and combining it with (
2.5
), we get

\alpha(h_{1},hP)=\alpha(s(h_{1}hP),P)l^{-1}\beta(s(h_{1}hP),P)^{-1}\beta(h_{1},hP)\beta(s(hP),P)l\alpha(s(hP),P)^{-1}.

This shows that
\alpha\sim\beta
via the fibre automorphisms given by the map

hP\mapsto\beta(s(hP),P)l\alpha(s(hP),P)^{-1}

which proves the claim.
∎

We say that a cocycle
\alpha:H\times H/P\to S
is of morphism-type with morphism
\rho
if there exists a Borel morphism
\rho:H\to S
such that
\alpha(h_{1},hP)=\rho(h_{1})
for every
h_{1},h\in H
.
We can now give proof of the decomposability criterion.

Proof of Proposition
2.2
.

Choose a Borel section
s:G/Q\to G
with
s(Q)=\operatorname{id}
.
Let
\tilde{\alpha}:G\times G/Q\to Q
be the associated cocycle. Recall
\pi_{1}
is the quotient map
Q\to S=Q/R_{0}
. The associated morphism
\rho_{\tilde{\alpha}}:Q\to Q
is then the identity map and hence the map
\pi_{1}\circ\rho_{\tilde{\alpha}}:Q_{H}\to Q\to S
extends to a morphism
\tau:H\to S
by assumption. We thus get a morphism-type cocycle
\beta:H\times H/Q_{H}\to S
by taking
\beta(h,c)=\tau(h)
. By Lemma
2.3
, the cocycle
\alpha:=\pi_{1}\circ\tilde{\alpha}
restricted to
H\times H/Q_{H}
and
\beta
are equivalent. Since equivalent cocycles induce isomorphic bundles, we are done.
∎

2.2.
Skew-product systems and stationary measures

Let
H^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the set of two-sided sequences of elements of
H
. We denote an element (bi-infinite word) of

by

where, by convention, we consider

and

. The sequence

, also denoted

will be referred to as the past of

and

, also denoted

, as the future of

. We denote by

the shift map on

taking one step forward to future, i.e.

, where

is the concatenation
(\ldots,b_{-1},a_{0})
and

is the image of

under the usual shift map, also denoted

, where

.

Let

be a probability measure on

and
\nu
a
\mu
-stationary probability measure on a locally compact and second countable

. It follows from the martingale convergence theorem that the limit as

of

exists for

-almost every

; it will be denoted by

, or sometimes

. These limit measures satisfy a key equivariance property which says that for
\mu^{-\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
-a.e.

, we have
\nu_{T^{-1}b}=b_{-1}^{-1}\nu_{b}
or equivalently, for
\mu^{-\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
-a.e.

and

-a.e.

, we have
\nu_{ba_{0}}=a_{0}\nu_{b}
.

Stationary measures can also be seen as part of invariant measures on skew-product systems. Let

denote the product

-invariant measure on

that projects onto

\hat{\nu}=\int\delta_{w}\otimes\nu_{w}d\mu^{\mathbb{Z}}(w)

defines a

-invariant measure. The measure

2.3.

\mu
-proximal. Then, we have

P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(Y)\simeq P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(Y_{0})\times P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(F).

More precisely, the map

\nu\mapsto(\overline{\nu},\nu^{F})

(2.6)

is a bijection where the latters are, respectively, pushforwards of
\nu
by the projections
Y\to Y_{0}
and
Y\to F
.

The assumption of proximality induces a certain disjointness between two factors; it is clear that without such an assumption the conclusion fails (e.g. if
Y_{0}
and
F
have a common non-trivial factor).

Proof.

Note that since the projections
Y\to Y_{0}
and
Y\to F
are both
H
-equivariant, the pushforward measures
\overline{\nu}
and
\nu^{F}
are both
\mu
-stationary. Moreover, it is clear that if
\nu
is ergodic, then so are
\overline{\nu}
and
\nu^{F}
. Let us show that the map
P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(Y)\ni\nu\to P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(Y_{0})\times P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(F)
given by
\nu\mapsto(\overline{\nu},\nu^{F})
yields the desired bijection.

Without loss of generality, let us suppose that the
H
-action on
Y_{0}
is
\mu
-proximal and show that the above map is injective. Given
\nu\in P_{\mu}^{\operatorname{erg}}(Y)
, since the projections to each factor commute with the
H
-action, we have
\mu^{-\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
-a.s.
\overline{\nu_{b}}=(\overline{\nu})_{b}
and
(\nu_{b})^{F}=(\nu^{F})_{b}
. Moreover, since the
H
-action on
Y_{0}
is
\mu
-proximal and
\overline{\nu}
is ergodic, there exists a boundary map
\xi:B\to Y_{0}
such that
(\overline{\nu})_{b}=\delta_{\xi(b)}
for
\mu^{-\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
-a.s.

b\in H^{-\mathbb{N}^{\ast}}
. Therefore, the probability measure
\nu_{b}
is given by
\delta_{\xi(b)}\otimes(\nu^{F})_{b}
and hence by the Furstenberg decomposition, we can recover the measure
\nu
as
\nu=\int\delta_{\xi(b)}\otimes(\nu^{F})_{b}d\beta(b)
. This shows that the map
\nu\mapsto(\overline{\nu},\nu^{F})
is injective.

Surjectivity does not use the
\mu
-proximality assumption and follows from the fact that both projections
\overline{\nu}
and
\nu^{F}
are
\mu
-stationary and ergodic. Indeed, one readily checks that
\int(\overline{\nu})_{b}\otimes(\nu^{F})_{b}d\beta(b)
is a
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measure on
Y
.
∎

3.
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-Zariski closure: measure classification

We now begin the main part on classifying stationary measures on homogeneous bundles over flag varieties. Following the scheme exposed in the Introduction, in §
3.1
, we start by distinguishing Case 1 (Dirac base) and Case 2 (Furstenberg base) according to the classification in the base followed by a precise description of various possibilities that occur (see Figure
1
) in Case 2. In the rest, we focus on Case 2. §
3.2
treats the trivial fibre case. In §
3.3
, we treat the diagonal fibre action case and prove Theorem
1.1
. Finally, in §
3.4
, we prove Theorem
1.4
and provide an example for Case 2.3.b.

3.1.
The setting, classification on the base and the cases

Let us start by recalling the notations from the introduction. Let
G
be a semisimple real Lie group with finite centre and
Q<G
a parabolic subgroup. Let
R_{0}\unlhd Q
be a normal algebraic subgroup and
R<Q
be a closed subgroup containing
R_{0}
such that
S:=Q/R_{0}
is semisimple with finite centre and without compact factors and
\Lambda:=R/R_{0}
is a discrete subgroup of

. We denote by
X
the quotient space
G/R
. As explained in the introduction, the space
X
has a natural
G
-equivariant projection to
G/Q
endowing it with a fibre-bundle structure over the flag variety
G/Q
with fibres given by copies of
S/\Lambda
.

A convenient way to find such subgroup
R_{0}\unlhd Q
as above is by considering the refined Langlands decomposition
Q=S_{Q}E_{Q}A_{Q}N_{Q}
of

(see e.g.
[
22
, VII,7]
), where
S_{Q}
is a semisimple subgroup of
Q
without compact factors,
E_{Q}
is a compact subgroup commuting to
S_{Q}
,
A_{Q}
is a maximal
\mathbb{R}
-split diagonalizable subgroup commuting with
S_{Q}E_{Q}
, and
N_{Q}
is the unipotent radical of
Q
. One can then take the normal subgroup
R_{0}
of

to be of the form
S^{\prime}_{Q}E_{Q}A_{Q}N_{Q}
where
S^{\prime}_{Q}
is a simple factor of
S_{Q}
or the trivial group.

Let
\mu
be a probability measure on
G
with finite first moment,
\Gamma_{\mu}
the closed semigroup generated by the support of
\mu
and suppose that the Zariski-closure
\overline{\Gamma}_{\mu}^{Z}
of
\Gamma_{\mu}
is a copy of either
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
or
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
in
G
. We will denote this Zariski closure by

. On our way to establishing a description of
\mu
-stationary probability measures
\nu
on
X
, we remark that a measure
\nu
on

determines, and in turn is determined by, its projection on
G/Q
via

and the fibre measures of this projection. Therefore, we proceed by first discussing the possible
\mu
-stationary measures on the base

.

3.1.1.
Stationary measures on the base

Since the projection
X\to G/Q
is
G
-equivariant, any
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\nu
on
X
projects down to
\mu
-stationary probability measure
\overline{\nu}
on the base
G/Q
. The description of stationary measures on the base is handled by Guivarc’h–Raugi
[
19
]
and by Benoist–Quint
[
7
]
. We have the following.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a bijection between
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measures on
G/Q
and compact
H
-orbits on
G/Q
.

Proof.

It is clear that any compact
H
-orbit carries a
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measure. Conversely, let
\nu
be a
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measure. By Chacon–Ornstein ergodic theorem, there exists
x\in G/Q
such that
\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\mu^{\ast k}\ast\delta_{x}\to\nu
as
N\to\infty
. So, in particular,
\nu
is supported in the compact
\overline{\Gamma_{\mu}x}
. Since
G/Q
is a flag variety, the orbit
Hx
is locally closed (see e.g.
[
32
, Theorem 3.1.1]
). Hence the compact
\overline{\Gamma_{\mu}x}
is contained in
Hx
. Moreover, since
Hx
is locally compact, up to conjugating
Q
, it is (
H
-equivariantly) homeomorphic to
H/Q_{H}
where
Q_{H}=H\cap Q
. It now follows from
[
7
, Proposition 5.5]
that
Q_{H}
is cocompact in
H
and
\nu
is the unique
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measure supported in
H/Q_{H}\simeq Hx
. This concludes the proof.
∎

It follows from this result that there are two types of
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measures on the base
G/Q
. The first type, which we will refer to as
Case 1
, is Dirac measures. This happens if and only if

is contained in a conjugate of
Q
. The second type (
Case 2
) is the Furstenberg measure supported in a copy of
H/P
in
G/Q
where
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
H
. This happens if and only if
H
intersects a conjugate of
Q
in a parabolic subgroup. Note that both types of stationary measures can be simultaneously present in a
G
-homogeneous bundle

.

Our study will primarily concern the analysis of stationary probability measures falling in Case 2. Indeed, as we now discuss, Case 1 is handled precisely by the seminal works of Benoist–Quint
[
3
,
4
]
and Eskin–Lindenstrauss
[
11
]
.

3.1.2.
Case 1: Dirac base

Let us observe that the results of Benoist–Quint
[
3
,
4
]
and Eskin–Lindenstrauss
[
11
]
imply the following.

Proposition 3.2
.

Keep the setting above and let
\nu
be a
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability on

whose projection onto

is a Dirac measure. Then the fibre measure
\nu^{F}
of

on

is homogeneous.

This result follows from a direct application of
[
11
, Theorem 1.3]
which extends the main measure classification results of Benoist–Quint
[
4
]
with regards to the moment assumption and the fact that the group

is only required to be discrete.

Proof.

By assumption, the projection
\overline{\nu}
of

is a Dirac

on
G/Q
. Replacing if necessary

by a conjugate, we can suppose
H<Q
and that

. The fibre of the map

above

identifies

. Identifying

with its image in

, the measure

is

-stationary and ergodic. Therefore, it follows from

that

is a homogeneous measure.
∎

3.1.3.
Case 2: Furstenberg base

The rest of this section is devoted to the analysis of the remaining case, i.e. the description of a
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability on
X
whose projection to
G/Q
is non-atomic (and which is consequently the Furstenberg measure on a copy of
H/P
in
G/Q
, where
P
is a parabolic subgroup of
H
, see Lemma
3.1
). In this case
H
intersects a conjugate
gQg^{-1}
of
Q
in a parabolic subgroup. By conjugating
Q
if necessary, we can and will suppose that

, i.e.
P=Q_{H}:=H\cap Q
and

lives in

. As before, the analysis of stationary measure will vary depending on the relative position of

with respect to the parabolic group

within the ambient group

. We will distinguish three cases that will be dealt with separately in the following subsections.

•
Case 2.1
: Trivial fibre action. This is a trivial case that occurs when the parabolic subgroup

of

is contained in

.

•
Case 2.2
: Diagonal fibre action. This is the case when

is a proper non-trivial subgroup of

. As we shall see, this positioning gives rise to a situation where the

-valued cocycle describing the fibre action has values in a diagonal subgroup of

.

•
Case 2.3
: This is the remaining case, i.e. the case where

is trivial. Interestingly, the analysis in this case depends on further properties

with respect to

that we will explain. Accordingly, our analysis will involve two subcases (
2.3.a
and
2.3.b
). In this paper, we will not be able to give the full description of stationary measures in the last of these two subcases.

3.2.
Case 2.1: Trivial fibre action

We express the description in this simple case in the following result.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let the space
X
and groups
G,Q,R_{0},R,S\simeq Q/R_{0},\Lambda\simeq R/R_{0}
and
H
be as defined before. Suppose that
Q_{H}^{o}
is contained in
R_{0}
. Then, there exists a standard trivialization
X\simeq G/Q\times S/\Lambda
such that any
\mu
-stationary and ergodic probability measure
\nu
on
X_{\mathcal{C}}
can be written as
\overline{\nu}_{F}\otimes\delta_{q\Lambda}
a product of the Furstenberg measure
\overline{\nu}_{F}
with a Dirac measure
\delta_{q\Lambda}
for some
q\in Q
.

Proof.

Start by noting that since
Q_{H}
is a (Zariski) connected algebraic group,
Q_{H}^{o}<R_{0}
implies that
Q_{H}<R_{0}
. Now fix any standard trivialization
X_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq H/Q_{H}\times S/\Lambda
, let
\beta
be the associated cocycle. The hypothesis

then entails that the associated morphism
\rho_{\beta}:Q_{H}\to S
has trivial image. In particular
\rho_{\beta}
extends trivially to a morphism

and hence by Proposition
2.2
, the
H
-action on
X_{\mathcal{C}}
is decomposable.
Therefore there exists a standard trivialization
X_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq H/Q_{H}\times S/\Lambda
for which the associated cocycle is morphism-type with trivial morphism. The result follows.
∎

Here are two examples where the trivial fibre action situation arises.

.

Case 2.2: Diagonal fibre action

3.3.1.
Iwasawa cocycle and representation theory

Let
H
denote either the group
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
or
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
H
and
P
be a minimal parabolic subgroup so that we have the decomposition
H=K^{o}P
. Let
D
be the maximal connected diagonal subgroup of
P
,
N
the unipotent radical of
P
and
M=K\cap P
. Let
H/P
be the flag variety of
H
. Given
h\in H
and
\xi=kP\in H/P
, we denote by
\sigma(h,\xi)
the unique element of
D
such that

hk\in K\sigma(h,\xi)N.

(3.1)

This map
\sigma:H\times H/P\to D
defines a continuous cocycle (see
[
8
, Lemma 8.2]
), called the
Iwasawa cocycle
. The morphism
\rho_{\sigma}
associated with the Iwasawa cocycle is simply the projection
P\to D\simeq P/MN
.

An alternative way, more in the spirit of Section
2
, to construct the Iwasawa cocycle is as follows. Consider
H
as a fibre bundle over
H/P
and let
s
be a section
s:H/P\to H
given by the Iwasawa decomposition, namely
s(kP)\in kM
for
k\in K^{\circ}
. We then get a trivialization
H\simeq H/P\times P
(see (
2.1
)) and an associated cocycle
\tilde{\sigma}:H\times H/P\to P
(see (
2.2
)). It is not hard to verify that the cocycle obtained by composing
\tilde{\sigma}
with the projection
P\to P/MN\simeq D
satisfies the characterizing property (
3.1
) of the Iwasawa cocycle. Regarding the section
s
, for
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
case, we can define it canonically to have values in
K^{\circ}
. For
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
case, we need to make a choice in
kM
so that
s(kP)
is a Borel section. Even though the cocycle
\tilde{\sigma}
depends on
s
, by (
2.2
), since the ambiguity

is in the centre, we know that Iwasawa cocycle does not depend on the choice of the value of
s(kP)
in
kM
.

In the course of our proofs, sometimes it will be more convenient to switch to the additive notation for cocycles. Let
\mathfrak{d}
be the Lie algebra of
D
. For a

-valued cocycle
\alpha
, we will denote by
\overline{\alpha}
, the
\mathfrak{d}
-valued cocycle obtained by composing
\alpha
with the logarithm map
D\to\mathfrak{d}
.

Given an algebraic irreducible representation
\rho:H\to\operatorname{GL}(V)
of
H
in a finite dimensional real vector space

, for every character
\chi
of
D
, the associated weight space is

V^{\chi}=\{v\in V:\rho(a)v=\chi(a)v\;\;\text{for every}\;a\in D\}
. The set of characters
\chi
for which
V^{\chi}\neq\{0\}
is called the set of (restricted) weights of
(V,\rho)
and denoted
\Sigma(\rho)
. For a character
\chi
of
D
, we denote by
\overline{\chi}
the corresponding additive character on
\mathfrak{d}
. The set
\Sigma(\rho)
is endowed with an order:
\overline{\chi}_{1}\geqslant\overline{\chi}_{2}
if and only if
\overline{\chi}_{1}-\overline{\chi}_{2}
is a sum of positive roots of
H
in
\mathfrak{d}
.
\Sigma(\rho)
has a largest element
\chi
, called the highest weight of
\rho
. The corresponding eigenspace is the subspace
V^{N}
of
N
-fixed vectors. Since

is
\mathbb{R}
-split, this is a line in

. For an element
\eta=gP
in the flag variety

, we denote by

the line

in

constructing a map

.

The following lemma will be important for our considerations; it will allow us to control the Iwasawa cocycle.

Lemma 3.7
.

[
8
, Lemma 6.33]

Let
(V,\rho)
be an algebraic irreducible representation of
H
with the highest weight
\chi
. Then, there exists a

-invariant Euclidean norm
\|.\|
on
V
such that for every element

,
\rho(a)
is a symmetric endomorphism of

. Moreover, for every
\eta\in H/P
, non-zero

and

, we have

\overline{\chi}(\overline{\sigma}(h,\eta))=\log\frac{\|\rho(h)v\|}{\|v\|}.

The goal of this part is to obtain a lemma (Lemma

3.3.4.

To obtain the required

[

Remark 4.2

The conclusion of Proposition

also holds if we replace the Cesàro average

by the sequence of empirical measures. More precisely, for every

Proof.

It is clear that any limit point

\varepsilon_{1}>0
, there exist constants
C>0
and
c>0
such that

\mu^{*n}\{g\in G:|\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)-\lambda_{\mu}n|\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}n\}\leqslant Ce^{-cn}.

We can now give

Proof of Proposition
4.5
.

We fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
\lambda_{\mu}\geqslant 5/n
(recall that the Lyapunov exponent
\lambda_{\mu}
is positive, a well-known result of Furstenberg), fix
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varphi
and
\omega
as in the statement. We will estimate the left-hand side of (
4.1
) separately for
\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)
inside three different intervals
[(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n,\infty)
,
[\varepsilon_{1}n,(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n]
and
(-\infty,\varepsilon_{1}n]
. The second interval will give us the main term, other intervals will yield the error term. Take a smooth cutoff
\chi
which equals
1
on
[\varepsilon_{1}n,(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n]
and equals
0
outside of
[\varepsilon_{1}n-1,(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n+1]
so that we have
\mathds{1}-\chi\leqslant\mathds{1}_{s<\varepsilon_{1}n}+\mathds{1}_{s>(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}
. Then, we can write

\begin{split}&\left|\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\ d\mu^{*k}(g)-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\chi(\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\ d\mu^{*k}(g)\right|\\
\leqslant&\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)<\varepsilon_{1}n}\ d\mu^{*k}(g)\right|\\
&+\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)>(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}\ d\mu^{*k}(g)\right|.\end{split}

(4.3)

Main term:

Let
t=n\lambda_{\mu}
and
f(w,s)=\varphi(w,s+t)\chi(s+t)
. Then by (
4.2
)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\chi(\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\ d\mu^{*k}(g)\\
=&\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int f(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)-t)\mu^{*k}(g)\\
=&\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{-t}^{\infty}f\ dLeb\ d\nu_{w}+\frac{1}{n}O\big(e^{-\eta(t-|\operatorname{Supp}f|)}|f|_{W^{1,2}C^{\gamma}}\big),\end{split}

(4.4)

where in the error term, we have
t-|\operatorname{Supp}f|=t-(t-\varepsilon_{1}n)=\varepsilon_{1}n
. For the main term, using the formula of
f
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\lambda_{\mu}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\varphi(y,s)\chi(s)\ dLeb(s)d\nu_{w}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n\lambda_{\mu}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{\varepsilon_{1}n}^{(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}\varphi(y,s)\chi(s)\ dLeb(s)d\nu_{w}(y)+|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{2}{n\lambda_{\mu}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(y,s)\ dLeb(s)\ d\nu_{w}(y)+|\varphi|_{\infty}O(\varepsilon_{1}+\frac{1}{n}).

For the error term in (
4.4
), we have

\frac{1}{n}|f|_{W^{1,2}C^{\gamma}}\leqslant\sup_{s}\{|\varphi|_{C^{\gamma}}\}+\sup_{s}\{|\partial_{ss}\varphi|_{C^{\gamma}}\}\leqslant|\varphi|_{C^{3}}.

Now, we give an upper bound of the sum over
{k>n}
:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k>n}^{\infty}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\chi(\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))d\mu^{*k}(g)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k>n}^{\infty}\mu^{*k}(\{g,\ \sigma_{\chi}(g,w)<(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n+1\}).

Due to the assumption
\varepsilon_{1}n\geqslant 2
, we obtain

\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)-\lambda_{\mu}n\leqslant-\varepsilon_{1}n+1\leqslant-\varepsilon_{1}n/2.

We use the large deviation estimate (Theorem
4.8
) to obtain

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k>n}^{\infty}\mu^{*k}(\{g:\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)<(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n+1\})\leqslant\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k>n}Ce^{-ck}=\frac{Ce^{-cn}}{n(1-e^{-c})},

where the constants
C,c
depend on
\varepsilon_{1}
.

Collecting above estimates, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\chi(\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))d\mu^{*k}(g)=&\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(y,s)\ dLeb(s)\ d\nu_{w}(y)\\
&\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!+O\left(e^{-\eta\varepsilon_{1}n}|\varphi|_{C^{3}}+|\varphi|_{\infty}\left(\varepsilon_{1}+\frac{1}{n}+\frac{Ce^{-cn}}{n(1-e^{-c})}\right)\right).\end{split}

(4.5)

Error term I:

For
k<n_{0}:=\frac{\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{1}}n
, we have
k(\lambda_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{1})<(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n
.
By the large deviation estimates (Theorem
4.8
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\geqslant(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}d\mu^{*k}(g)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}\mu^{*k}(\{g\in G:\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)>(\lambda_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{1})k\})

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\varphi|_{\infty}C\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{0}}e^{-ck}\leqslant\frac{|\varphi|_{\infty}C}{n(1-e^{-c})}.

For the part
n_{0}\leqslant k\leqslant n
, we use the absolute value to bound

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=n_{0}}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\geqslant(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}d\mu^{*k}(g)\leqslant|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{n-n_{0}}{n}=|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{1}}.

Thus, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\geqslant(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})n}d\mu^{*k}(g)\leqslant\frac{|\varphi|_{\infty}C}{n(1-e^{-c})}+|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}+\varepsilon_{1}}.

(4.6)

Error term II:
If
k>n_{1}:=\varepsilon_{1}n/(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})
, then we have
\varepsilon_{1}n<k(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})
and hence we can apply the large deviation estimate to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}n}d\mu^{*k}(g)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=n_{1}}^{n}\mu^{*k}(\{g\in G:\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)<(\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1})k\})\leqslant\frac{C|\varphi|_{\infty}e^{-cn_{1}}}{n(1-e^{-c})}.

For the part
k\leqslant n_{1}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{1}}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}n}d\mu^{*k}(g)\leqslant|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{n_{1}}{n}=|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1}}.

Thus, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\mathds{1}_{\sigma_{\chi}(g,w)\leqslant\varepsilon_{1}n}d\mu^{*k}(g)\leqslant|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{C|\varphi|_{\infty}e^{-cn_{1}}}{n(1-e^{-c})}.

(4.7)

Finally, combining (
4.3
), (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.7
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\int\varphi(gw,\sigma_{\chi}(g,w))\ d\mu^{*k}(g)=\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(y,s)\ dLeb(s)\ d\nu_{w}(y)

\displaystyle+O\left(e^{-\eta\varepsilon_{1}n}|\varphi|_{C^{3}}+|\varphi|_{\infty}\frac{\varepsilon_{1}}{\lambda_{\mu}-\varepsilon_{1}}+\frac{C|\varphi|_{\infty}}{n(1-e^{-c})}\right).

∎

4.2.2.
Equidistribution on
X_{\mathcal{C}}

We now use the equidistribution on
K\times\mathbb{R}
(Proposition
4.5
) to deduce the equidistribution on
X_{\mathcal{C}}
, that is, to give

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

Let
K\times_{\sigma}D
be the fiber bundle with
H
action, the action of
H
is given by
h(k,d)=(hk,\sigma(h,k)d)
, where we identify
K\simeq\mathbb{S}^{1}\simeq H/AN
. We define a map
p
from
K\times_{\sigma}D
to
H/Q_{H}\times_{\alpha}D^{\pm}
by

p(k,d)=(kM,\mathrm{sg}(k)d),

where
\mathrm{sg}(k)
is the sign element in
M
. By Lemma
3.8
, we have

Lemma 4.9
.

The map
p
is an
H
-equivariant map from
K\times_{\sigma}D
to
H/Q_{H}\times_{\alpha}D^{\pm}
.

We denote by
\mathcal{G}(r,\mathrm{sg}(w))
the element
(e^{r},\mathrm{sg}(w))\in D^{\pm}
and we use additive parameter
r\in\mathbb{R}
. Under this parametrization, the equidistribution of
D
or
D^{\pm}
-orbits of
z\in S/\Lambda
to some measure
m
means, respectively, that the measure
\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\mathcal{G}(r,1)z}\ dr
or the measure
\frac{1}{2t}\int_{0}^{t}\delta_{\mathcal{G}(r,1)z}+\delta_{\mathcal{G}(r,-1)z}\ dr
converges to
m
as
t\to\infty
.

Let
\psi
be
C^{3}
function on
X_{\mathcal{C}}\simeq H/Q_{H}\times S/\Lambda
and
z\in S/\Lambda
. Set
\varphi(w,r):=\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,\mathrm{sg}(w))z)
where
\eta
is the projection of
w
on
H/Q_{H}\simeq K/M
. Thanks to Lemma
4.9
, we have the relation

\varphi(gs(\eta),\sigma_{\chi}(g,\eta))=\psi(g\eta,\alpha(g,\eta)z)=\psi(gx),

(4.8)

for
x=(\eta,z)\in X_{\mathcal{C}}
and where, we recall,
s:K/M\to K
is the section. Therefore, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}(\psi)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\int\varphi(gs(\eta),\sigma_{\chi}(g,\eta))\ d\mu^{*k}(g).

(4.9)

We know that for any
\psi\in C^{3}(X_{\mathcal{C}})
with bounded
C^{3}
norm, with suitable choice of
\varepsilon_{1}
depending on
n
, thanks to Proposition
4.5
and the relation (
4.9
), for
t=\lambda_{\mu}n
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}(\psi)-\frac{1}{t}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}\varphi(k^{\prime},r)\ dr\ d\nu_{s(\eta)}(k^{\prime})\rightarrow 0

as
n
, equivalently
t
, tends to
\infty
.

On the other hand, by construction of the function
\varphi(\cdot,\cdot)
and the measures
\nu_{w}
for
w\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\varphi(k^{\prime},r)\ d\nu_{w}(k^{\prime})=

\displaystyle p_{1}(w)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\psi(k^{\prime}M,\mathcal{G}(r,\mathrm{sg}(k^{\prime}))z)\ d\nu_{1}(k^{\prime})

\displaystyle+p_{2}(w)\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\psi(k^{\prime}M,\mathcal{G}(r,\mathrm{sg}(k^{\prime}))z)d\nu_{2}(k^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{1}(w)\int\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,1)z)\ d\overline{\nu}_{F}(\eta)+p_{2}(w)\int\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,-1)z)\ d\overline{\nu}_{F}(\eta).

We get that

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}(\psi)-\frac{1}{t}\int\int_{0}^{t}p_{1}(s(\eta))\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,1)z)+p_{2}(s(\eta))\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,-1)z)\ dr\ d\overline{\nu}_{F}(\eta)\rightarrow 0.

Recall that for Case 2.2.a, the section
s
is chosen so that its image contains the support of
\nu_{1}
. In particular,
p_{1}(s(\eta))=1
and
p_{2}(s(\eta))=0
for every
\eta
in the support of the Furstenberg measure
\overline{\nu}_{F}
. Therefore, in Case 2.2.a, if the
\eta
coordinate of
x=(\eta,z)
belongs to the support of
\overline{\nu}_{F}
, then we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}(\psi)-\frac{1}{t}\int\int_{0}^{t}\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,1)z)\ dr\ d\bar{\nu}_{F}(\eta)\rightarrow 0.

(4.10)

In Case 2.2.b, by a similar computation and using the fact that the unique measure
\nu_{K}
on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
writes as
\nu_{K}=\frac{1}{2}\int(\delta_{w}+\delta_{-w})d\overline{\nu}_{F}(\mathbb{R}w)
, we have

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}(\psi)-\frac{1}{2t}\int\int_{0}^{t}(\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,1)z)+\psi(\eta,\mathcal{G}(r,-1)z))\ dr\ d\overline{\nu}_{F}(\eta)\rightarrow 0.

(4.11)

By density of
C^{3}(X_{\mathcal{C}})
in
C(X_{\mathcal{C}})
, we deduce from (
4.10
) and (

) that
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{1\leqslant k\leqslant n}\mu^{*k}*\delta_{x}
converges weakly to a measure
\bar{\nu}_{F}\otimes m
if and only if the
D
-orbit or the
D^{\pm}
- orbit (respectively in Case 2.2.a or Case 2.2.b) starting at

equidistributes to the measure
m
.
∎

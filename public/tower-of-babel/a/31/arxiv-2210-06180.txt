Abstract
. We introduce the class of dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebras as a generalisation of higher Auslander algebras and minimal Auslander-Gorenstein algebras, and give their basic properties. We also introduce mixed (pre)cluster tilting modules as a generalisation of (pre)cluster tilting modules, and establish an Auslander type correspondence by showing that dominant Auslander-Gorenstein (respectively, Auslander-regular) algebras correspond bijectively with mixed precluster (respectively, cluster) tilting modules. We show that every trivial extension algebra T ⁡ ( A ) T(A) of a d d -representation-finite algebra A A admits a mixed cluster tilting module and show that this can be seen as a generalisation of the well known result that d d -representation-finite algebras are fractionally Calabi-Yau. We show that iterated SGC-extensions of a gendo-symmetric dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra admit mixed precluster tilting modules.

2.
Dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebras and basic properties

2.1.
The definition and the first properties

Definition 2.1
.

An algebra
A
is a
dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra
if it is Iwanaga-Gorenstein and
\operatorname{idim}P\leq\operatorname{domdim}P
for every indecomposable projective module
P
.
If, furthermore,
\operatorname{gldim}A<\infty
, then we call it a
dominant Auslander-regular algebra
.

Note that for a dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra
\operatorname{idim}P<\operatorname{domdim}P
holds if and only if
P
is also injective; otherwise, we have
\operatorname{domdim}P=\operatorname{idim}P
.
In particular, a dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra is self-injective if and only if we have strict inequality for all
P
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose
A
is a dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra. Then the following hold.

(1)

For every indecomposable projective
A
-module
P
with
d:=\operatorname{idim}P\geq 0
, the minimal injective coresolution

\displaystyle 0\to P\to T^{0}\to T^{1}\to\cdots\to T^{d-1}\to I\to 0

has indecomposable injective
I
and projective-injective

for all
0\leq k<d
. In particular, this is also the the minimal projective resolution of
I
.

(2)

The map
P\mapsto I=\Omega^{-\operatorname{idim}P}(P)
defines a bijection
\pi:\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{proj}A)\to\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{inj}A)
with inverse
I\mapsto\Omega^{\operatorname{pdim}I}(I)
.

(3)

A^{\mathrm{op}}
is also dominant Auslander-Gorenstein.

(4)

\operatorname{DN}(A)=\{\operatorname{idim}P\mid P\text{ indecomposable projective}\}
.

(5)

A
is Auslander-Gorenstein.

Proof.

By assumption
A
is Iwanaga-Gorenstein. Let
n:=\operatorname{idim}A
denote the self-injective dimension of
A
. If
n=0
, then
A
is self-injective and the claim is trivial; hence, we will assume
n>0
from now on.
Label the indecomposable projective (resp. injective)
A
-modules by
P_{1},\ldots,P_{m}
(resp.
I_{1},\ldots,I_{m}
) so that
\operatorname{\mathrm{top}}P_{i}=\operatorname{\mathrm{soc}}I_{i}
for all
i
.

(1) When
P_{i}
is injective, the claim is trivial.
Consider a non-injective
P_{i}
with
d_{i}:=\operatorname{domdim}P_{i}=\operatorname{idim}P_{i}
, so there is the following minimal injective coresolution:

0\rightarrow P_{i}\rightarrow T_{i}^{0}\rightarrow T_{i}^{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow T_{i}^{d_{i}}\rightarrow 0.

Since
P_{i}
is non-injective, we have
\operatorname{domdim}P_{i}=\operatorname{idim}P_{i}
, which then means that the module
T_{i}^{k}
are projective-injective for
0\leq k<d_{i}
.
Now
T_{i}^{d_{i}}
is injective and has no projective direct summands or else the resolution would split off this summand and would not be minimal. If
T_{i}^{d_{i}}=X_{1}\oplus X_{2}
for two non-zero direct summands

and

then those summands would have both codominant dimension at least

by the above minimal coresolution of

. But then

and
\Omega^{d_{i}}(X_{2})
would both be non-zero, contradicting that

is indecomposable. Thus

must be indecomposable.

(2) This follows from (1) immediately.

(3) By (1), we have
\operatorname{codomdim}I_{\pi(i)}\leq\operatorname{pdim}I_{\pi(i)}
with strict inequality if and only if
I_{\pi(i)}\simeq P_{i}\in\operatorname{proj}A
. By
K
-linear duality this means that
A^{\mathrm{op}}
is dominant Auslander-Gorenstein.

(4) As
\operatorname{pdim}T_{i}^{k}=0
for all
0\leq k<d_{i}
and
\operatorname{pdim}T_{i}^{d_{i}}=\operatorname{pdim}I_{\pi(i)}=d_{i}
, the claim on
\operatorname{DN}(A)
now follows.

(5) Since the minimal injective coresolution
(I^{k})_{k\geq 0}
of
A
is the direct sum of those for

over all
i
’s, we have
\operatorname{pdim}I^{k}=\operatorname{pdim}\bigoplus_{i}T_{i}^{k}
, which is
k
if
k=d_{i}
, otherwise,
0
. Thus, it immediately follows that
A
is Auslander-Gorenstein.
∎

By Theorem
1.6
, let
A
be a dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra with
\operatorname{DN}(A)=\{d_{1}<d_{2}<\cdots d_{r}\}
, then for each

\operatorname{Gr}_{d_{i}+1}(A)=\operatorname{Gr}_{d_{i}+2}(A)=\cdots=\operatorname{Gr}_{d_{i+1}}(A)
.
In other words, knowledge of the subcategories

Proposition 2.3
.

Let

By Theorem

2.2.
Relation with the Gorenstein condition

The
Gorenstein condition
is a non-commutative modification of a property of commutative Gorenstein rings and was introduced in the study of Artin-Schelter regular algebras.
The following condition is an analogue of the Gorenstein condition, and was studied under the additional assumption that
S
has finite projective dimension in
[

,

]
where it is called

-regular.

Definition 2.4
.

A simple module
S
over an algebra

is called

-Gorenstein
if for

,
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(S,A)\neq 0
if and only if

and
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{k}(S,A)
is a simple
A^{\mathrm{op}}
-module. We will consider the following condition:

(G)

A
is Iwanaga-Gorenstein and a simple
A
-module
S
is
k
-Gorenstein for some
k\geq 1
if the injective envelope
I(S)
of

is not projective.

We give the following example, which shows that 0-Gorenstein simple modules correspond to simple algebra direct summands for algebras of finite global dimension.

Example 2.5
.

Let
A
be a finite dimensional algebra. Any simple
A
-module corresponding to a simple algebra direct summand of

is 0-Gorenstein. Conversely, if
A
has finite global dimension, then any
0
-Gorenstein simple
A
-module is obtained in this way.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear. We prove the second one. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
A
is not simple.
It is basic that
\operatorname{pdim}M=\sup\{i\geq 0\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,A)\neq 0\}
holds for each
M\in\operatorname{mod}A
with
\operatorname{pdim}M<\infty
.
In particular, each simple module
S
that is 0-Gorenstein has to be projective since
A
is assumed to have finite global dimension. Thus we can write
S=eA
for a primitive idempotent
e\in A
. Since
Ae=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(S,A)
is simple, we have
(1-e)Ae=0=eA(1-e)
.
Thus
S
is given by a simple algebra direct summand

.
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Assume that

satisfies the condition (G). For every indecomposable projective non-injective

We consider a map

As in the case of precluster tilting modules, we have the following equivalence.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let

be a dominant Auslander-Gorenstein algebra with dominant dimension at least two corresponding to

.
Then we have a commutative diagram of equivalences of categories:

\textstyle{\mathcal{Z}(M)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(M,-)}
\textstyle{\operatorname{CM}A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\scriptstyle{-\otimes_{A}M}

For an Iwanaga-Gorenstein algebra

We want to show that

when

is dominant Auslander-Gorenstein with corresponding mixed precluster tilting pair

Let

Recall from Proposition

Let us now show that

Recall that

4.
Mixed cluster tilting for trivial extensions of
d
-representation finite algebras

Recall that an algebra
A
is called
d
-representation-finite
if
A
has global dimension at most
d
and a
d
-cluster tilting module.
The
trivial extension

T(A)
of a finite dimensional
K
-algebra
A
is defined as the algebra with vector space structure
A\oplus DA
and multiplication
(a,f)(b,g)=(ab,ag+fb)
, where
DA=\operatorname{Hom}_{K}(A,K)
.
The trivial extension algebra
T(A)
is a symmetric algebra, that is
A\simeq DA
as
A
-bimodules.
We will often use in this section that, for self-injective algebras
A
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(M,N)\simeq\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(\Omega_{A}^{i}(M),N)
for all
i\geq 0
and
\tau_{d}(M)\simeq\nu\Omega^{d+1}(M)
for all
d\geq 1
.
Note that an algebra is symmetric exactly when the Nakayama functor is isomorphic to the identity functor.
We refer for example to
[
SY
]
for more information on trivial extension algebras and symmetric algebras.

Another fact we will use about
T(A)
is that
T(A)
can be
\mathbb{Z}
-graded with
\deg A=0
and
\deg DA=1
. Moreover, we have an equivalence of triangulated categories
\underline{\operatorname{mod}}^{\mathbb{Z}}T(A)\simeq\mathrm{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}A)
between the stable category of
\mathbb{Z}
-graded finitely generated
T(A)
-module and the bounded derived category of
\operatorname{mod}A

[
Hap
]
. Under this equivalence, the autoequivalence
\Omega_{T(A)}\circ(1)
given by composing the syzygy with the grading-shift corresponds to the derived Nakayama functor
\nu_{A}
; see
[
CDIM
, Section 2.2, 2.3]
for the details.

The following theorem shows the existence of a canonical mixed cluster tilting module for the trivial extension algebra of a
d
-representation-finite algebra.

Theorem 4.1
.

For
d\geq 1
, let
A
be a
d
-representation-finite algebra with basic
d
-cluster tilting
A
-module
A\oplus N
, and let
B=T(A)
be the trivial extension of
A
.
Then the
B
-module
B\oplus N
is a mixed cluster tilting
B
-module.
In this case, for each
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N)
,
\ell_{X}
is given by

\ell_{X}=d+t_{X}\ \mbox{ where $t_{X}=\min\{l\geq 0\mid\nu_{A}^{l}(\tau_{d}^{A}(X))$ is not projective$\}$.}

We start with recalling a basic result of
d
-representation-finite algebras.

Proposition 4.2
.

[
I6
, Lemma 2.3]

Let
A
be a
d
-representation-finite algebra with basic
d
-cluster tilting
A
-module
M=A\oplus N
. Then we have a bijection
\tau_{d}:\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}M)\setminus\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{proj}A)\simeq\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}M)\setminus\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{inj}A)
, and we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,A)=0
.

To prove the previous theorem, we need the following two lemmas:

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
A
be an algebra with trivial extension
B=T(A)
, and take
X\in\operatorname{mod}A
satisfying

\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,A)=0
for each
0\leq i<d:=\operatorname{pdim}X_{A}
. Then the following hold.

(1)

\tau_{d}^{A}(X)\simeq\tau_{d}^{B}(X)
.

(2)

Take a minimal projective resolution of
X
as an
A
-module:

(4.3.1)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow P_{d}\xrightarrow{f_{d}}P_{d-1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0.

Applying
-\otimes_{A}B
, we obtain an exact sequence of
B
-modules:

(4.3.2)

\displaystyle 0\to\tau_{d}^{A}(X)\to P_{d}\otimes_{A}B\xrightarrow{}P_{d-1}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0.

Proof.

Since
\tau_{d}^{B}(X)=\Omega_{B}^{d+1}(X)
, it suffices to prove (2).
Applying
-\otimes_{A}B
to (
4.3.1
), we get the start of a projective resolution

(4.3.3)

\displaystyle P_{d}\otimes_{A}B\xrightarrow{f_{d}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{id}_{B}}P_{d-1}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow X\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow 0.

This is exact since
B\simeq A\oplus DA
as
A
-modules and hence
\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(X,B)=\operatorname{Tor}_{i}^{A}(X,DA)\simeq D\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(X,A)=0
holds for
1\leq i<d
, which means that
X\otimes_{A}B\simeq X
holds as
X\otimes_{A}DA\simeq D\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(X,A)=0
.

Now we claim that the kernel of the map
f_{d}\otimes_{A}\operatorname{id}_{B}
is isomorphic to
\tau_{d}^{A}(X)
as
B
-modules.
Since
P_{i}\otimes_{A}B\simeq P_{i}\oplus(P_{i}\otimes_{A}DA)
as
A
-modules, the sequence (
4.3.3
) regarded as a sequence of
A
-modules is isomorphic to the direct sum of (
4.3.1
) and the
exact sequence

0\rightarrow\tau_{d}^{A}(X)\rightarrow P_{n}\otimes_{A}DA\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\otimes_{A}DA\rightarrow X\otimes_{A}DA\rightarrow 0

obtained by applying
-\otimes_{A}DA
to (
4.3.1
). Consequently, (
4.3.2
) is exact.
∎

The following observation is clear.

Lemma 4.4
.

For each
P\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{proj}A)
, let
t=\min\{l\geq 0\mid\nu_{A}^{l}(P)
is not projective
\}
. Then combining the projective covers

\displaystyle 0\to\nu^{i+1}(P)\to\nu^{i}(P)\otimes_{A}B\to\nu^{i}(P)\to 0

for each
0\leq i<t
, we obtain an exact sequence

(4.4.1)

\displaystyle 0\to\nu^{t}(P)\to\nu^{t-1}(P)\otimes_{A}B\to\cdots\to\nu(P)\otimes_{A}B\to P\otimes_{A}B\to P\rightarrow 0.

We will prove Theorem
4.1
in two steps. First, we show in the following lemma that
B\oplus N
is mixed precluster tilting with the claimed value of
\ell_{X}
. Afterwards we show that the arising dominant Auslander-Gorenstein has finite global dimension.

Lemma 4.5
.

Under the setting of Theorem
4.1
,
B\oplus N
is a mixed precluster tilting
B
-module with
\ell_{X}=d+t_{X}

for all
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N)
.

Proof.

We can assume for simplicity that
A
and
N
are basic.
By Proposition
4.2
, we have
\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(N,A)=0
for all
0\leq i<d
.
We now calculate
\ell_{X}
for
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}M)
; we do this for two separate cases:

(i) Let
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N)
such that
\tau_{d}(X)
is non-projective. We will show that
\ell_{X}=d
, i.e.
\tau_{d}^{B}(X)\in\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N
and
X\in{}^{\perp d-1}(B\oplus N)
.
The first required property already follows from Lemma
4.3
which asserts that
\tau_{d}^{B}(X)\simeq\tau_{d}^{A}(X)\in\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N
, so it remains to show orthogonality.

Let (
4.3.1
) be a minimal projective resolution of
X
.
Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,N)
to (
4.3.1
) and
\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(-,N)
to (
4.3.2
) and comparing them via a functorial isomorphism
\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(-\otimes_{A}B,N)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(-,N)
, we have a commutative diagram of exact sequences

\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P_{0},N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P_{1},N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(P_{d},N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\wr}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(P_{0}\otimes_{A}B,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(P_{1}\otimes_{A}B,N)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\cdots\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(P_{d}\otimes_{A}B,N).}

Comparing the cohomologies, we obtain that
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{B}(X,B\oplus N)=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{B}(X,N)\simeq\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{A}(X,N)=0
for each
1\leq i<d
.

(ii) Let
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N)
such that
P:=\tau_{d}^{A}(X)
is a projective
A
-module and
t:=t_{X}
.
We have

\tau_{d+t}^{B}(X)=\Omega_{B}^{d+t+1}(X)=\Omega_{B}^{t}(\tau_{d}^{B}(X))=\Omega_{B}^{t}\tau_{d}^{A}(X)=\nu_{A}^{t}(\tau_{d}^{A}(X)).

Here, the third equality follows from Lemma
4.3
, and the last equality follows from the correspondence between
\Omega_{B}\circ(1)
and
\nu_{A}
(and then apply the grading forgetful functor).
Since
\nu_{A}^{t-1}(\tau_{d}^{A}X)\in\operatorname{proj}A
by assumption,
\tau_{d+t}^{B}(X)=\nu_{A}^{t}(\tau_{d}^{A}X)\in\operatorname{inj}A\setminus\operatorname{proj}A\subset\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N
.

For Ext-orthogonality, as in case (i),
we have
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{B}(X,N)=0
for each
1\leq i<d
.
Moreover, since
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{A}(X,N)\simeq D\overline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{A}(N,P)=0
by Proposition
4.2
, the rightmost map in the upper sequence of (
4
) is surjective, and hence so is the rightmost map in the lower sequence of (
4
). This implies that
\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{B}(X,N)=0
.

Let
\nu:=\nu_{A}
.
Gluing (
4.3.2
) and (
4.4.1
),
we obtain an exact sequence

0\to\nu^{t}(P)\to\nu^{t-1}(P)\otimes_{A}B\to\cdots\to\nu(P)\otimes_{A}B\to P\otimes_{A}B\to P_{d}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow P_{0}\otimes_{A}B\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0.

Thus we have

\tau_{d+t}^{B}(X)=\Omega^{d+t+1}_{B}(X)=\nu^{t}(P)\in\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N\ \mbox{ and }\ \Omega_{B}^{i}(X)=\nu^{i-d-1}(P)

for each
d+1\leq i\leq d+t-1
. Hence, for such an
i
,
\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{B}(X,N)=\underline{\operatorname{Hom}}_{B}(\Omega_{B}^{i}(X),N)
is a quotient of

\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(\nu^{i-d-1}(P),N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(\nu^{i-d-1}(P),N)=D\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,\nu^{i-d}(P)),

which is zero by Proposition
4.2
as
\nu^{i-d}(P)\in\operatorname{proj}A
. Now the assertion follows.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We have shown in Lemma
4.5
that
B\oplus N
is mixed precluster tilting, and so it remains to show that the global dimension of
C:=\operatorname{End}_{B}(B\oplus N)
is finite, i.e. every simple
C
-modules
S_{X}:=\operatorname{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(B\oplus N,X)
for indecomposable
X\in\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}(B\oplus N)
have finite projective dimension.
We calculate this in two cases.

(i) First we consider the case
X\in\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}N
.

Take a
d
-almost split sequence in
\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}(A\oplus N)

(4.5.5)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow\tau_{d}^{A}(X)\rightarrow E_{d-1}\rightarrow\cdots\to E_{0}\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0.

Since
B
is a projective
B
-module, applying
{}_{B}(N,-):=\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N,-)
to (
4.5.5
) yields an exact sequence

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow{}_{B}(N,\tau_{d}^{A}(X))\rightarrow{}_{B}(N,E_{d-1})\rightarrow\cdots\to{}_{B}(N,E_{0})\rightarrow{}_{B}(N,X)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathrm{top}}{}_{B}(N,X)\rightarrow 0.

Now let
t:=t_{X}
.
Gluing (
4.5.5
) and (
4.4.1
), we obtain an exact sequence

(4.5.6)

\displaystyle 0\to\nu^{t}(P)\to\nu^{t-1}(P)\otimes_{A}B\to\cdots\to\nu(P)\otimes_{A}B\to P\otimes_{A}B\to E_{d-1}\rightarrow\cdots\to E_{0}\rightarrow X\rightarrow 0

with terms in
\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}(B\oplus N)
. By Proposition
4.2
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,\nu^{i}(P))=0
for each
0\leq i<t
and
{}_{B}(N,\nu^{i}(P)\otimes_{A}B)=\operatorname{Hom}_{A}(N,\nu^{i+1}(P))=0
for each
0\leq i\leq t-2
.
Hence, by applying
{}_{B}(N,-):=\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N,-)
to (
4.5.6

(4.5.7)

\displaystyle 0\rightarrow{}_{B}(N,\nu^{t}(P))

\displaystyle\to{}_{B}(N,\nu^{t-1}(P)\otimes_{A}B)\to 0\to\cdots\to 0

\displaystyle\to{}_{B}(N,E_{d-1})\rightarrow\cdots\to{}_{B}(N,E_{0})\rightarrow{}_{B}(N,X)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathrm{top}}\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N,X)\rightarrow 0.

Applying
\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(B\oplus N,-)=(-)\oplus\operatorname{Hom}_{B}(N,-)
to (
4.5.6

(ii) Next we consider the case
X\in\operatorname{proj}B

5.
Mixed precluster tilting for gendo-symmetric algebras

The next theorem gives an iterative construction of mixed precluster tilting modules.
For clarity, given a mixed precluster tilting
A
-module
M
and
X\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{\mathrm{add}}M)
, we use
\ell_{X}^{M}
and
r_{X}^{M}
to denote the associated numbers that were notated as
\ell_{X}
and
r_{X}
respectively.
1
1

1

Recall from Proposition
3.4
that we have
\operatorname{idim}_{B}P_{X}=r_{X}^{M}+1
and
\operatorname{pdim}_{B}I_{X}=\ell_{X}^{M}+1
, where
B:=\operatorname{End}_{A}(M)
and for
P_{X}
and
I_{X}
are the projective and injective
B
-modules corresponding to
X
respectively. We specifically clarify this as the condition (b) and (c) looks similar but is different from this formula.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
A
be an algebra. We call
A

splendid
if the following conditions are satisfied.

(1)

M:=A\oplus DA
is a mixed precluster tiliting
A
-module.

(2)

For each
P\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{proj}A)\setminus\operatorname{inj}A
, we have
\operatorname{idim}_{A}P=r_{P}^{M}+1
.

(3)

For each
I\in\operatorname{ind}\nolimits(\operatorname{inj}A)\setminus\operatorname{proj}A
, we have
\operatorname{pdim}_{A}I=\ell_{I}^{M}+1
.

We give a family of examples of splendid algebras.
Recall from
[

]
that an algebra
A
is
gendo-symmetric
if
A\simeq\operatorname{End}_{B}(M)
for some generator(-cogenerator)
M
over a symmetric algebra
B
. Recall that the invariants
\ell_{X}^{M}
and
r_{X}^{M}
are always strictly positive.

Proposition 5.2
.

Each gendo-symmetric algebra
A
that is dominant Auslander-Gorenstein
is splendid.

Proof.

Recall from
[
FK
, Prop 2.11]
and
[
Ma
, Prop 4.3]
that, for any
X\in\operatorname{mod}A
with dominant dimension at least two, one has

(5.2.1)

\displaystyle\operatorname{domdim}X=\inf\{i\geq 1\mid\operatorname{Ext}_{A}^{i}(DA,X)\neq 0\}+1\;\;\text{ and }\;\;\tau^{-}(X)\simeq\Omega^{-2}(X).

Dual formulas hold for modules

\{\operatorname{idim}C,\operatorname{idim}P_{x}^{B}\mid x\in Q_{B}\setminus\{v_{j}\}_{j}\}
and
\{\operatorname{idim}P^{B}_{v_{i}}+\operatorname{pdim}I^{C}_{v_{i}}\mid 1\leq i\leq n\}
, then we have
\operatorname{idim}A=\max\mathcal{D}
and
\operatorname{domdim}A=\min\mathcal{D}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we omit the superscript
A
for
A
-modules.
Let

be an indecomposable projective
A
-module corresponding to the vertex
x\in Q
.
Note that if

(resp.

) for any

, then
P_{y}\in\operatorname{inj}A
(resp.

).

If
x\in Q_{C}
, then the minimal projective resolution for
I_{x}^{C}
lifts to that of
I_{x}
in
\operatorname{mod}A
naturally (i.e. we can just remove the superscript

Suppose that

and let

.

Solutions of the Loop Equations of a Class of Generalized Frobenius Manifolds
Si-Qi Liu, Haonan Qu, Yuewei Wang, Youjin Zhang

Abstract
. We prove the existence and uniqueness of solution of the loop equation associated with a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity, and show, for a particular example of one dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold, that the deformation of the Principal Hierarchy induced by the solution of the loop equation is the extended q q -deformed KdV hierarchy.



1.
Introduction

Since Dubrovin introduced the notion of Frobenius manifold in the early 1990s
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, the relationship between Frobenius manifolds and hierarchies of integrable evolutionary PDEs has been an important subject in the research field of mathematical physics, see for example
[
2
,
3
,
4
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
15
,
20
,
21
,
22
,
23
,
24
,
27
,
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
40
,
41
]
and references therein. As it was shown by Dubrovin
[
16
,
17
,
18
]
, one can associate with any given Frobenius manifold a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of hydrodynamic type, which is called the Principal Hierarchy of the Frobenius manifold. Under the assumption of semisimplicity of the Frobenius manifold, it is shown in
[
21
]
that the Principal Hierarchy has a so-called topological deformation which is a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of KdV type
[
44
]
. If the semisimple Frobenius manifold arises from the quantum cohomology of a smooth projective variety
X
, then we know from the construction of the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy that the logarithm of a particular tau function of the integrable Hierarchy yields the generating function of the Gromov-Witten invariants of
X
. In particular, when
X
is a point we have the one-dimensional Frobenius manifold, and the bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy is the KdV hierarchy
[
36
,
48
]
; when
X
is the complex projective line, the Frobenius manifold is two-dimensional, and the associated
bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy is the extended Toda hierarchy
[
14
,
22
,
31
,
46
,
49
]
.
For a semisimple Frobenius manifold, the Principal Hierarchy is related with its topological deformation via a quasi-Miura transformation
which is given by the unique solution of its loop equation. As it is introduced in
[
21
]
, the loop equation of a Frobenius manifold encodes the condition that the actions of the Virasoro symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy on the tau function of its topological deformation are linear, i.e., they are given by the actions of certain second order linear operators, which are called the Virasoro operators, on the tau function.

An analogue of the relationship between semisimple Frobenius manifolds and bihamiltonian integrable hierarchies is proposed in
[
41
]
for a class of generalized Frobenius manifolds, which satisfy all the axioms of Dubrovin’s definition of Frobenius manifolds but the flatness of the unit vector fields
[
18
]
, i.e., the unit vector fields are non-flat with respect to the flat metrics of the manifolds. For such a generalized Frobenius manifold
M
, one can
construct a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of hydrodynamic type via the deformed flat coordinates of
M
, just as one does for the construction of the Principal Hierarchy of a usual Frobenius manifold. However, due to the non-flatness of the unit vector field, this bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy does not contain the flow that is given by the translation along the spatial variable. By applying the bihamiltonian recursion relation to this special flow one obtains an infinite number of flows which are symmetries of the integrable hierarchy. These flows play an important rule in our understanding of properties of the Virasoro symmetries of the bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of hydrodynamic type, and they are added to this integrable hierarchy to form the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold.

The Principal Hierarchy of a generalized Frobenius manifold also possesses a tau structure, and its Virasoro symmetries acts nonlinearly on the tau function. As for a usual Frobenius manifold, we construct the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy by requiring that the Virasoro symmetries acts linearly on the tau function of the deformed integrable hierarchy. Namely, if we denote by
\tau^{[0]}
and
\tau
the tau functions of the Principal Hierarchy and its topological deformation, then we require that these tau functions are related by the following genus expansion formula:

\log\tau=\varepsilon^{-2}\log\tau^{[0]}+\sum_{k\geq 1}\varepsilon^{k-2}\mathcal{F}^{[k]}(v;v_{x},\dots,v^{(N_{k})}),

and the infinitesimal actions of the Virasoro symmetries of the Principal Hierarchy on the tau function of the deformed integrable hierarchy are given by

\tau\to\tau+\delta L_{m}\tau,\quad m=-1,0,1,\dots.

Here
v=(v^{1},\dots,v^{n})
is a solution of the Principal Hierarchy that depends on the spatial variable
x
and time variables
t^{\alpha,p}
and
L_{m}
, called the Virasoro operators, are second order linear differential operators of the time variables. This condition of linearization of the Virasoro symmetries leads to a system of linear equations for the gradients of the functions
\mathcal{F}^{[k]}
for
k\geq 1
, and we call
it the loop equation of the generalized Frobenius manifold. See Theorem 10.5 of
[
41
]
for the explicit form of the loop equation.

In the present paper, we prove the existence and uniqueness of solution of the loop equation of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold, and prove the Conjecture 11.1 of
[
41
]
. This conjecture asserts that the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of a certain one-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold yields the Volterra hierarchy and the
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy.

We organize the paper as follows: In Sect. 2, we present some important properties of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold. In Sect. 3, we prove the existence and uniqueness of solution of the loop equation, the main results are given in Theorem
3.9
and Theorem
3.15
. In Sect. 4, we prove the above-mentioned conjecture on the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of a one-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold. In Sect. 5, we give some concluding remarks.

2.
Semisimple generalized Frobenius manifolds

In this section, we present some basic properties of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity, which will be used in Sect. 3 to prove the uniqueness and existence of solution of the associated loop equation. Most of these properties are shared by a usual semisimple Frobenius manifold, we refer readers to
[
18
,
19
]
for details of their derivations.

Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity
[
18
,
41
]
. We will simply call it a generalized Frobenius manifold or GFM for short in what follows. Then we have an operation of multiplication
``\cdot"
on the tangent spaces of
M
which yields, together the unit vector field
e
and the flat metric
\eta
of
M
, a Frobenius algebra structure on each tangent space of
M
. Denote by
\nabla
the Levi-Civita connection of
\eta
, then the non-flatness of the unit vector field
e
means that
\nabla e\neq 0
. We also have the Euler vector field
E
which is associated with the quasi-homogeneity properties of the flat metric
\eta
and the operation of multiplication on the tangent spaces of
M
, and satisfies the condition
\nabla^{2}E=0
. As for a usual Frobenius manifold, we have the deformed flat connection
\tilde{\nabla}
on
M
which is defined by

\tilde{\nabla}_{a}b=\nabla_{a}b+za\cdot b,\quad a,b\in\textrm{Vect}(M).

It can be extended to a flat connection of
M\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
by defining

\displaystyle\tilde{\nabla}_{a}\frac{\nd}{\nd z}=0,\quad\tilde{\nabla}_{\frac{\nd}{\nd z}}\frac{\nd}{\nd z}=0,\quad\tilde{\nabla}_{\frac{\nd}{\nd z}}b=\partial_{z}b+E\cdot b-\frac{1}{z}\mu b,

here
a,b
are vector fields on
M\times\mathbb{C}^{*}
with zero components along
\mathbb{C}^{*}
,
z
is the coordinate of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, and
\mu
is the grading operator

(2.1)

\mu=\frac{2-d}{2}-\nabla E

with
d
being the charge of
M
.

2.1.
The canonical coordinates of a semisimple GFM

Following the definition of the semisimplicity of a usual Frobenius manifold
[
18
,
19
]
, we call the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
semisimple if the Frobenius algebras defined on
T_{p}M
are semisimple for generic
p\in M
. As it is shown in
[
18
,
19
]
, one can find a system of local coordinates
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}
in a certain neighborhood of a generic point of
M
such that

(2.2)

\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}=\delta_{ij}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}},\quad i,j=1,\dots,n.

These coordinates are called canonical coordinates of
M
, in which the unit vector field has the form

e=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}

and the flat metric
\eta
is diagonal, i.e.,

(2.3)

\left\langle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\right\rangle=\eta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\right)=h_{i}\delta_{ij},\quad i,j=1,\dots,n.

It is shown in
[
41
]
that the unit vector field
e
is the gradient of a function
\theta_{0,0}(u)
with respect to the metric
\eta
, so the functions
h_{i}
can be represented in the form

(2.4)

h_{i}=\frac{\partial\theta_{0,0}}{\partial u_{i}},\quad i=1,2,...,n.

From
[
18
,
19
]
we know that one can choose the canonical coordinates of
M
in such a way that in these coordinates the Euler vector field has the form

E=\sum_{i=1}^{n}u_{i}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}.

In another word, we can take the eigenvalues of the
operator
of multiplication by the Euler vector field
E
as canonical coordinates of
M
.
We will fix such a choice of canonical coordinates of
M
which is assumed to be semisimple in what follows.

Let us also fix a system of local flat coordinates
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
of
M
with respect to its flat metric
\eta
, and denote
by
f_{1},...,f_{n}
the orthonormal frame

(2.5)

f_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{i}}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

Then we have

(2.6)

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{1}},\dots,\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{n}}\right)=(f_{1},\dots,f_{n})\Psi,

where
\Psi=(\psi_{i\alpha})
is an
n\times n
matrix valued function
which satisfies the relation

(2.7)

\Psi^{{\mathrm{T}}}\Psi=\eta.

Here and in what follows we also use
\eta
to denote the constant matrix
(\eta_{\alpha\beta})
with

\eta_{\alpha\beta}=\eta\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}},\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\beta}}\right).

In terms of the matrix
\Psi
, the elements of the Jacobi matrix of the change of coordinates between the canonical and the flat ones are given by

(2.8)

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial v^{\alpha}}=\frac{\psi_{i\alpha}}{\sqrt{h_{i}}},\quad\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial u_{i}}=\sqrt{h_{i}}\psi_{i}^{\alpha},\quad i,\alpha=1,\dots,n,

where
\psi^{\alpha}_{i}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\psi_{i\gamma}
,
(\eta^{\alpha\beta})=(\eta_{\alpha\beta})^{-1}
, and we assume summation with respect to repeated upper and lower Greek indices henceforth. The structure constants of the multiplication

\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\beta}}=c^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\gamma}},\quad\alpha,\beta=1,\dots,n

can be represented in the form

(2.9)

c_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\psi_{i\alpha}\psi_{i\beta}\psi_{i}^{\gamma}}{\sqrt{h_{i}}}.

From the relation (
2.6
) we know that the functions
\psi_{i\alpha}
satisfy the equations

(2.10)

\partial_{i}\psi_{j\alpha}=\gamma_{ij}\psi_{i\alpha},\quad\partial_{i}\psi_{i\alpha}=-\sum_{k\neq i}\gamma_{ki}\psi_{k\alpha},\quad i\neq j,

here

(2.11)

\gamma_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{j}}}\frac{\partial\sqrt{h_{i}}}{\partial u_{j}},\quad i\neq j

are the rotation coefficients of the diagonal metric
\eta=\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{i}\td u_{i}^{2}
, which has the symmetry property

(2.12)

\gamma_{ij}=\gamma_{ji},\quad i\neq j

due to (
2.4
), and satisfy the Darboux-Egoroff system

(2.13)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\gamma_{ij}}{\partial u_{k}}=\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{kj},\quad\textrm{for distinct}\ i,j,k=1,\dots,n,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\gamma_{ij}}{\partial u_{i}}+\frac{\partial\gamma_{ij}}{\partial u_{j}}=-\sum_{l\neq i,j}\gamma_{il}\gamma_{lj},\quad\textrm{for distinct}\ i,j=1,\dots,n.

From (
2.10
) it also follows that

\partial_{e}\Psi=0,

here
e
is the unit vector field. If we represent
e
in terms of the flat coordinates as follows:

e=e^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}},

then from the relations

\displaystyle f_{i}

\displaystyle=\,e\cdot f_{i}=e^{\alpha}\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}\cdot f_{i}=e^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\psi_{j\alpha}f_{j}\cdot f_{i}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{h_{i}}}e^{\alpha}\psi_{i\alpha}f_{i}

we arrive at

(2.15)

\sqrt{h_{i}}=e^{\alpha}\psi_{i\alpha},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

2.2.
The isomonodromic tau-function of a GFM

Recall that the horizontal section
\xi=(\xi^{1},...,\xi^{n})^{{\mathrm{T}}}

of the deformed flat connection
\tilde{\nabla}
of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
satisfies the linear system

[
18
,
19
,
21
,
41
]

(2.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{\alpha}\xi

\displaystyle=z\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}\xi,

(2.17)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}\xi

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{U}+\frac{\mu}{z}\right)\xi,

here the matrices
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}
and
\mathcal{U}
are representations, in the frame given by the flat coordinates, of the operators of mutiplication by the vector field
\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}

and the Euler vector field
E
respectively.
After the gauge transformation
y=\Psi\xi
, the system (
2.16
), (
2.17
) reads

(2.18)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}y

\displaystyle=\,(zE_{i}+V_{i})y,

(2.19)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial z}y

\displaystyle=\,\left(U+\frac{V}{z}\right)y,

where the matrices
E_{i}
are given by
(E_{i})_{kl}=\delta_{ik}\delta_{il}
, and

U=\,\Psi\mathcal{U}\Psi^{-1}=\diag(u_{1},\dots,u_{n}),\quad V=\Psi\mu\Psi^{-1},\quad V_{i}=\ad_{U}^{-1}[E_{i},V].

The matrix
V
is anti-symmetric and it is related with the rotation coefficients by

(2.20)

V=[\Gamma,U],

here
\Gamma=(\gamma_{ij})
. It satisfies the isomonodromy deformation equations

(2.21)

\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{i}}=[V_{i},V],\quad i=1,\dots,n

of the linear system (
2.19
). These equations can be represented as the following Hamiltonian systems:

\frac{\partial V}{\partial u_{i}}=\{V,H_{i}\},\quad i=1,\dots,n

with the linear Poisson bracket on
so(n)
defined by

\{V_{ij},V_{kl}\}=V_{il}\delta_{jk}-V_{jl}\delta_{ik}+V_{jk}\delta_{il}-V_{ik}\delta_{jl},

and the Hamiltonians given by

(2.22)

H_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{u_{i}-u_{j}},\quad i=1,\dots,n.

The tau function
\tau_{I}=\tau_{I}(u)
of these Hamiltonian systems defined by

(2.23)

\td\log\tau_{I}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{i}\td u_{i}

is called the isomonodromic tau function of the semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold
M
.

2.3.
Periods of a semisimple GFM

Let
g
be the intersection form of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
. In the flat coordinates it is represented by the matrix

g=(g^{\alpha\beta})
, where

g^{\alpha\beta}=\imath_{E}\left(\nd v^{\alpha}\cdot\nd v^{\beta}\right)=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\mathcal{U}^{\beta}_{\gamma}.

We denote

(2.24)

\Sigma_{\lambda}=\left\{v\in M\,\middle|\,\det(g^{\alpha\beta}-\lambda\eta^{\alpha\beta})|_{v}=0\right\},\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

then the inverse matrix

(g_{\alpha\beta}(\lambda))=(g^{\alpha\beta}-\lambda\eta^{\alpha\beta})^{-1}

defines a flat metric
(\ ,\,)_{\lambda}
on
M\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}
. In the flat coordinates
v^{1},\dots,v^{n}
of
M
, the contravariant components of the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla(\lambda)
of this metric are given by

\nabla(\lambda)^{\alpha}\nd v^{\beta}=(g^{\alpha\gamma}-\lambda\eta^{\alpha\gamma})\nabla(\lambda)_{\gamma}\nd v^{\beta}=\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}\nd v^{\gamma},

where
\Gamma^{\alpha\beta}_{\gamma}
are the contravariant coefficients of the Levi-Civita connection of
g
.
There exists a system of flat local coordinates

p_{1}(v;\lambda),\dots,p_{n}(v;\lambda)
of
(\ ,\,)_{\lambda}
on an open subset of
M\setminus\Sigma_{\lambda}
,
called the
periods
of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
, which satisfy the equations

(2.25)

\nabla(\lambda)\nd p_{\alpha}=0,\quad\alpha=1,\dots,n.

Let
p
be a period of the semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold
M
, then its gradient with respect to the flat metric
\eta
can be represented in the orthonormal frame as follows:

(2.26)

\nabla p=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}f_{i},

then we have

(2.27)

\partial_{\alpha}p=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\psi_{i\alpha}\phi_{i},\qquad\partial_{i}p=\sqrt{h_{i}}\phi_{i},

where we use the short notation
\partial_{\alpha}=\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha}}
and
\partial_{i}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The period
p
of
M
satisfies the following system of Gauss-Manin equations:

(2.28)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{j}}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{ij}\phi_{i},\quad i\neq j,

(2.29)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial u_{i}}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{1}{\lambda-u_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{ij}\phi_{j}\right)-\sum_{j\neq i}\gamma_{ij}\phi_{j},

(2.30)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\phi_{i}}{\partial\lambda}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{\lambda-u_{i}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{ij}\phi_{j}\right),

where
V_{ij}
is the
(i,j)
-th element of the matrix
V
.

Proof.

Recall that the gradient
\nabla p=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i}f_{i}
of the period
p

satisfies the following Gauss-Manin equations
[
19
,
41
]

(2.31)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{U}-\lambda)\nabla_{i}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}f_{j}\right)+\mathcal{C}_{i}\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}f_{j}\right)=0,

(2.32)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{U}-\lambda)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}f_{j}\right)=\left(\mu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\phi_{j}f_{j}\right),

where the operators
\mathcal{U}
,
\mathcal{C}_{i}
and
\mu
are defined by

\mathcal{U}f_{j}=E\cdot f_{j},\quad\mathcal{C}_{i}f_{j}=\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\cdot f_{j}=\delta_{ij}f_{j},\quad\mu f_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}V_{ij}f_{i}.

Then this proposition is obtained from (
2.10
)
by a straightforward calculation.
∎

Now let us fix a basis of periods
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}
, and denote by
G=(G^{\alpha\beta})
the corresponding Gram matrix

[
21
,
41
]
.
We represent the gradients of these periods in the orthonomal frame as follows:

(2.33)

\nabla p_{\alpha}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\phi_{i\alpha}f_{i},

where
\phi_{i\alpha}=\phi_{i\alpha}(u;\lambda)
,
then we have

(2.34)

\phi_{i\alpha}G^{\alpha\beta}\phi_{j\beta}=\frac{\delta_{ij}}{u_{i}-\lambda},\quad i,j=1,\dots,n.

3.
The solution of the loop equation

In this section, we first recall briefly the derivation of the loop equation of a generalized Frobenius manifold
M
, and then give the proof of uniqueness and existence of solution of this equation by following the approach of
[
21
]
for a usual semisimple Frobenius manifold.

3.1.
Derivation of the loop equation

Recall that the tau cover of the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold
M
has the form
[
41
]

(3.1)

\frac{\partial f}{\partial t^{j,q}}=f_{j,q},\quad\frac{\partial f_{i,p}}{\partial t^{j,q}}=\Omega_{i,p;j,q},\quad\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{j,q}}=\eta^{\alpha\gamma}\partial_{x}\Omega_{\gamma,0;j,q}.

It has unknown functions
f,f_{i,p},v^{\alpha}
, where

(3.2)

\alpha\in\{1,2\dots,n\},\quad(i,p)\in\mathcal{I}=\Big(\{1,\dots,n\}\times\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\Big)\cup\Big(\{0\}\times\mathbb{Z}\Big),

and
\left\{\Omega_{I,J}\,\middle|\,I,J\in\mathcal{I}\right\}
are the 2-point functions on
M
.
Since the flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{0,0}}
is given by the translation along the spatial variable
x
, we will identify the time variable
t^{0,0}
with
x
.

The tau cover (
3.1
) of the Principal Hierarchy of
M
possesses a family of Virasoro symmetries
\left\{\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\mid m\geq-1\right\}
of the following form
[
41
]
:

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}}

\displaystyle=a_{m}^{I,J}f_{I}f_{J}+b_{m;J}^{I}t^{J}f_{I}+\hat{c}_{m;I,J}t^{I}t^{J},

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f_{I}}{\partial s_{m}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}}=(2a_{m}^{K,L}\Omega_{I,K}+b^{L}_{m;I})f_{L}+(b^{K}_{m;L}\Omega_{I,K}+2\hat{c}_{m;I,L})t^{L},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{m}}

\displaystyle=\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_{x}\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{\beta,0}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}},

where
a_{m}^{I,J}
,
b^{I}_{m;J}
and
\hat{c}_{m;I,J}
are certain constants which only depend on the monodromy data
\eta,\mu,R
of
M
at
z=0
,
and they are called the
extended Virasoro coefficients
of
M
.
Here and in what follows summation over repeated upper and lower capital Latin indices with range
\mathcal{I}
is assumed.
Note that the coefficients
a_{m}^{I,J}
,
\hat{c}_{m;I,J}
and the 2-point functions
\Omega_{I,J}

satisfy the following symmetry properties

(3.6)

a_{m}^{I,J}=a_{m}^{J,I},\quad\hat{c}_{m;I,J}=\hat{c}_{m;J,I},\quad\Omega_{I,J}=\Omega_{J,I}

for all
m\geq-1
and
I,J\in\mathcal{I}
.
Each flow
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\,(m\geq-1)
commutes with all the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}
, i.e.,

(3.7)

\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\right]=0,\quad m\geq-1,\,I\in\mathcal{I}.

Introduce the following
\mathbb{C}
-algebra

(3.8)

\mathscr{A}=C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(M))[f,f_{I}][\![t^{I}]\!],

here
f
and
f_{I},t^{I}\,(I\in\mathcal{I})
are independent formal variables.
Then
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\,(I\in\mathcal{I})
can be regraded as a derivation on
\mathscr{A}
in a natural way,
i.e.,
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}}\in\Der(\mathscr{A})
acts on
f
,
f_{J}
and
v^{\alpha}
as in (
3.1
), and

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial t^{I}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,s}}\partial_{x}^{s}\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial t^{I}}

for
\mathcal{F}\in C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(M))
.
Similarly,
\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\,(m\geq-1)
can also be regarded as elements in

\Der(\mathscr{A})
by assigning
\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_{m}}
,

\frac{\partial f_{I}}{\partial s_{m}}
,
\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{m}}
as in (
3.3
)–(
3.5
), and

(3.9)

\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial s_{m}}=\sum_{s\geq 0}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}}{\partial v^{\alpha,s}}\partial_{x}^{s}\frac{\partial v^{\alpha}}{\partial s_{m}},\quad\frac{\partial t^{I}}{\partial s_{m}}=0

for
\mathcal{F}\in C^{\infty}(J^{\infty}(M))
and
I\in\mathcal{I}
. In this sense,
the relations
\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}},\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{J}}\right]=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{I}},\frac{\partial}{\partial s_{m}}\right]=0
still hold true.

The Virasoro symmetries can be represented in terms of

Lemma 3.6

Let

Moreover, the coefficient of the highest order pole

Proof.

Fix an

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{k}\phi_{i\alpha}

\displaystyle=\,\frac{(2k-1)!!}{2^{k}}\frac{(u_{i,x})^{k}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{k}}\left(\phi_{i\alpha}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{ij}\phi_{j\alpha}\right)

(3.35)

\displaystyle\quad+\text{lower-degree terms of $\frac{1}{\lambda-u_{i}}$}.

Note that

\displaystyle(s+1)\partial_{x}^{s}\left(\frac{1}{E-\lambda e}\right)^{\gamma}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{\sqrt{h_{i}}\psi_{i}^{\gamma}(u_{i,x})^{s}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{s+1}}(s+1)!+\cdots,

where “

Using (

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{s-k}\partial^{\gamma}p_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x}^{s-k}\left(\psi_{i}^{\gamma}\phi_{i\alpha}\right)=\psi_{i}^{\gamma}\partial_{x}^{s-k}\phi_{i\alpha}+\cdots

\displaystyle=\frac{\psi_{i}^{\gamma}(u_{i,x})^{s-k}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{s-k}}\cdot\frac{(2s-2k-1)!!}{2^{s-k}}\left(\phi_{i\alpha}+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}V_{ij}\phi_{j\alpha}\right)+\cdots,

Lemma 3.7

\sum_{s=0}^{N_{1}^{\prime}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{1}}{\partial v^{\gamma,s}}K^{\gamma,s}=\frac{1}{2}G^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}-\frac{1}{4\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right).

Lemma 3.10
.

For a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold and an arbitrary basis of its periods
p_{1}(v,\lambda),\dots,p_{n}(v,\lambda)

with the associated Gram matrix
(G^{\alpha\beta})
, the following identity holds true

(3.46)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{2}}-\sum_{i<j}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{(\lambda-u_{i})(\lambda-u_{j})}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right).

Proof.

Recall that the formula

(3.47)

\nabla(f*g)=\nabla f\cdot\nabla g

for the star product
*
holds true
[
21
,
41
]
for any smooth functions
f(v;\lambda)
and
g(v;\lambda)
,
where
\nabla
is the gradient with respect to the flat metric
\eta
,
and
``\cdot"
is the multiplication of the Frobenius algebra.
Then by (
2.27
) we have

\displaystyle\nabla\left(\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}\right)=\left(\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\cdot\left(\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}\right)G^{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h_{i}h_{j}}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}\right)G^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\cdot\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{j}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial\phi_{i\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}\frac{\partial\phi_{i\beta}}{\partial\lambda}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}.

On the other hand,

\nabla\left(\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{h_{i}}\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}\right)\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}.

Therefore, by using the relations (
2.30
), (
2.34
) we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial u_{i}}\left(\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_{i\alpha}+\sum_{j\neq i}V_{ij}\phi_{j\alpha}\right)G^{\alpha\beta}\left(\frac{1}{2}\phi_{i\beta}+\sum_{k\neq i}V_{ik}\phi_{k\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{u_{i}-\lambda}+\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{u_{j}-\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{4}\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{3}}-\sum_{j\neq i}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{2}(\lambda-u_{j})}.

Then due to (
2.21
), there exists a function
c=c(\lambda)
such that

(3.48)

\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{2}}-\sum_{i<j}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{(\lambda-u_{i})(\lambda-u_{j})}+c(\lambda).

On the other hand, from
[
41
]
we know that the left-hand side of the above formula can be represented as

\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}=-2\sum_{(i,p),(j,q)\in\mathcal{I}}a^{i,p;j,q}(\lambda)\Omega_{i,p;j,q}(v),

where

a^{i,p;j,q}(\lambda)=\sum_{m\geq-1}\frac{1}{\lambda^{m+2}}a_{m}^{i,p;j,q}

are the generating function of Virasoro coefficients, and
\Omega_{i,p;j,q}(v)
are the two-point functions of the generalized Frobenius manifold.
Note that
a_{-1}^{i,p;j,q}=a_{0}^{i,p;j,q}=0
,
therefore

\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}=O\left(\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}}\right)\quad\text{when $\lambda\to\infty$},

hence we obtain

c(\lambda)=\frac{n}{8\lambda^{2}}+\sum_{i<j}\frac{V_{ij}^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-V^{2}\right)=\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right).

The lemma is proved.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

The formula (
3.46
) can be rewritten as

(3.49)

\frac{\partial p_{\alpha}}{\partial\lambda}*\frac{\partial p_{\beta}}{\partial\lambda}G^{\alpha\beta}=-\frac{1}{8}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{U}-\lambda I}\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{U}-\lambda I}\mu\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2\lambda^{2}}\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{1}{4}-\mu^{2}\right),

We conjecture that it holds true for a generalized Frobenius manifold without the semisimplicity condition.

3.4.
The existence theorem

We are now to prove the existence of a solution to the loop equation (
3.25
) in the form

\Delta\mathcal{F}=\sum_{g\geq 1}\varepsilon^{2g-2}\mathcal{F}_{g}(v,v_{x},...,v^{(3g-2)}).

We are to prove the existence of
\mathcal{F}_{g}
by induction on
g
.
The genus one free energy
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is already given in (
3.50
).
For
g\geq 2
, suppose
\mathcal{F}_{g^{\prime}}
has already been solved for each
1\leq g^{\prime}\leq g-1
,
then from the
\varepsilon^{2g-2}
-coefficients of (
3.25
) we know that
\mathcal{F}_{g}
satisfies the following equation:

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{3g-2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{g}}{\partial v^{\gamma,s}}K^{\gamma,s}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=0}^{3g-5}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{g-1}}{\partial v^{\beta,l}}\right)\right)(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\partial^{\alpha}p_{\omega})G^{\omega\rho}(\partial_{x}^{l+1}\partial^{\beta}p_{\rho})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{{g^{\prime},g^{\prime\prime}\geq 1\atop g^{\prime}+g^{\prime\prime}=g}}\sum_{k=0}^{3g^{\prime}-2}\sum_{l=0}^{3g^{\prime\prime}-2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{g^{\prime}}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{g^{\prime\prime}}}{\partial v^{\beta,l}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\partial^{\alpha}p_{\sigma})G^{\sigma\rho}(\partial_{x}^{l+1}\partial^{\beta}p_{\rho})

(3.58)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{3g-5}\frac{\partial\mathcal{F}_{g-1}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\left[\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\sigma}}{\partial\lambda}\cdot\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\rho}}{\partial\lambda}\cdot v_{x}\right]^{\alpha}G^{\sigma\rho}.

We are to show the existence of

,
and then prove that the solutions

satisfy the following compatibility condition:

(3.60)

\frac{\partial H_{g;\alpha,r}}{\partial v^{\beta,s}}=\frac{\partial H_{g;\beta,s}}{\partial v^{\alpha,r}}.

Lemma 3.13
.

The linear system (
3.58

Now let

, and suppose that the linear systems (
3.58

3.58
)

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{3g-2}H_{g;\gamma,s}K^{\gamma,s}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k,l=0}^{3g-5}\frac{\partial H_{g-1;\beta,l}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}(\partial_{x}^{k+1}\partial^{\alpha}p_{\sigma})G^{\sigma\rho}(\partial_{x}^{l+1}\partial^{\beta}p_{\rho})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=0}^{3g-5}H_{g-1;\alpha,k}\partial_{x}^{k+1}\left[\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\sigma}}{\partial\lambda}\cdot\nabla\frac{\partial p_{\rho}}{\partial\lambda}\cdot v_{x}\right]^{\alpha}G^{\sigma\rho}.

which vanish at

and all the possible poles of them are at

.
Due to Lemma
3.6
,
the functions
K^{\gamma,s}
can be written in the form

(3.62)

K^{\gamma,s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{s+1}\frac{Q^{\gamma,s}_{i,k}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{k}},

Using (
2.28

2.30

\sum_{s=0}^{3g-2}H_{g;\gamma,s}K^{\gamma,s}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{3g-2}\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{k+1}}\sum_{s=k}^{3g-2}H_{g;\gamma,s}Q^{\gamma,s}_{i,k+1}.

By using the following formulae of change of coordinates on the jet spaces

\frac{\partial u_{i}^{(p)}}{\partial v^{\gamma,r}}={p\choose r}\partial_{x}^{p-r}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial v^{\gamma}}

\frac{\partial H_{g-1;\beta,l}}{\partial v^{\alpha,k}}=0\quad\text{if $k+l\geq 3g-3$}.

Then by (
2.28
)–(
2.30
) and (
2.34
),
the order of poles
\lambda=u_{i}
in the right-hand side of (

) is at most

, therefore

(3.67)

\text{RHS of \eqref{new linear system}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=1}^{3g-1}\frac{P_{g;i,k}}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{k}}

for some functions

on the jet space

.

(3.68)

Due to (
3.64
), (
3.67
),
the equation (
3.61
) for the unknowns

can be rewritten as

(3.69)

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{k=0}^{3g-2}\frac{1}{(\lambda-u_{i})^{k+1}}\left(\sum_{s=k}^{3g-2}H_{g;\gamma,s}Q^{\gamma,s}_{i,k+1}-P_{g;i,k+1}\right)=0,

Moreover, from (

4.1.
The bihamiltonian structure of the positive and negative flows

Let us introduce the following operators:

(4.18)

Lemma 4.2
.

The flows (
4.6
) and (
4.7
) can be rewritten as

(4.19)

\displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{1,p}}

\displaystyle=c_{1,p}U(1-\Lambda)\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right),

(4.20)

\displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,-p}}

\displaystyle=c_{0,-p}(\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p}\right).

Proof.

From (
4.6
) it follows that

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{c_{1,p}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{1,p}}=\res\left(\left[(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}})_{+},L\right]\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left((L^{p+\frac{1}{2}})_{+}(\Lambda+U)-(\Lambda^{2}+U\Lambda)(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}})_{+}\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,U\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}-\Lambda L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1}\right)=U(1-\Lambda)\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Similarly, from (
4.7
) we obtain the relations

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon}{c_{0,-p}}\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,-p}}=\res\left(\left[(M^{p})_{-},L\right]\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left((M^{p})_{-}(\Lambda+U)\right)-\res\left((\Lambda^{2}+U\Lambda)(M^{p})_{-}\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left(M^{p}\Lambda\right)-\res\left(\Lambda^{2}M^{p}\Lambda^{-1}\right)=(\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p}\right).

Thus the lemma is proved.

Proposition 4.3
.

The positive and negative flows (
4.6
)–(
4.7
) are Hamiltonian systems with respect to the Poisson operator
\mathcal{P}_{1}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{1,p}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{1}\frac{\delta H_{1,p}}{\delta U},\quad p\geq 0,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,-p}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{1}\frac{\delta H_{0,-p}}{\delta U},\quad p\geq 1,

where the densities of the Hamiltonians

\displaystyle H_{1,p}

\displaystyle=\int h_{1,p}\td x,\quad p\geq 0,

\displaystyle H_{0,-p}

\displaystyle=\int h_{0,-p}\td x,\quad p\geq 1

(4.23)

\displaystyle h_{1,p}=\frac{4c_{1,p}}{2p+3}\res\left(L^{p+\frac{3}{2}}\right),\quad p\geq 0,

(4.24)

\displaystyle h_{0,-1}=\frac{1}{2}\log V,\quad h_{0,-p}=\frac{2c_{0,-p}}{p-1}\res\left(M^{p-1}\right),\quad\text{$p\geq 2$}.

By using the relations

\delta h_{1,p}\sim 2c_{1,p}\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\delta L\right)=2c_{1,p}\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\delta U\cdot\Lambda\right)\sim 2c_{1,p}\res\left(\delta U\cdot\Lambda L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\frac{\delta H_{1,p}}{\delta U}=2c_{1,p}\res\left(\Lambda L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad p\geq 0.

Here we define the equivalence relation
``\sim"
as follows: a 1-form on the jet space is equivalent to zero if it is the
x
-derivative of another 1-form. Thus (
4.21
) follows from (
4.19
)
and the relations

\displaystyle\,U(1-\Lambda)\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\res\left(UL^{p+\frac{1}{2}}-U\Lambda L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}U+(\Lambda^{2}-L)L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}U+\Lambda^{2}L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{-1}-L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}(\Lambda+U)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1})\res\left(\Lambda L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\delta h_{0,-p}

Then by using (

Proposition 4.4
.

The positive and negative flows (

From the relations

\displaystyle\res\left(U\Lambda L^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\res\left((L-\Lambda^{2})L^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right)-\Lambda^{2}\res\left(L^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\Lambda^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-\Lambda^{2})\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right)+\Lambda\res\left(U\Lambda L^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(1-\Lambda)\res\left(U\Lambda L^{p-\frac{1}{2}}\right)=(1-\Lambda^{2})\res\left(L^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

U\frac{1}{1+\Lambda}

For

\displaystyle=\,\res\left(M^{p+1}\Lambda^{2}\right)-\res\left(\Lambda^{2}M^{p+1}\right)+\res\left(M^{p+1}U\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=\,\left(\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1}\right)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p+1}\Lambda\right)+\Lambda^{-1}\res\left(U\Lambda M^{p+1}\right)

\left(1-\Lambda^{-1}\right)\res\left(U\Lambda M^{p+1}\right)=\left(\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda\right)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p+1}\Lambda\right).

Applying
U\frac{\Lambda}{\Lambda+1}
to both sides of the above identity, we arrive at

\displaystyle U\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}\res\left(U\Lambda M^{p+1}\right)=U(1-\Lambda)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p+1}\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\res\left(U\Lambda M^{p+1}\Lambda\right)-\res\left(U\Lambda^{2}M^{p+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\res\left((L-\Lambda^{2})M^{p+1}\Lambda\right)-\res\left(U\Lambda^{2}M^{p+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left(M^{p}\Lambda\right)-\res\left(\Lambda^{2}M^{p+1}(\Lambda+U)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\res\left(M^{p}\Lambda\right)-\res\left(\Lambda^{2}M^{p+1}L\Lambda^{-1}\right)=\left(\Lambda^{-1}-\Lambda\right)\res\left(\Lambda M^{p}\right).

Then by using (
4.20
), (
4.26
) and (
4.29
) we obtain (
4.28
).
The proposition is proved.

Proposition 4.7
.

The logarithmic flows (

(4.38)

\mathcal{R}\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p-1}}=p\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p}},\quad p\geq 1,

\mathcal{R}=\mathcal{P}_{2}\mathcal{P}_{1}^{-1}=4U\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}U\frac{1}{\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1}}

is the recursion operator given by the bihamiltonian structure
(\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2})
defined in (
4.18
).

Proof.

Denote

(4.39)

L^{p}\log L=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{Z}}f^{[p]}_{s}\Lambda^{s},\quad p\geq 0,

then from (
4.8
) we obtain

\displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p}}

\displaystyle=\,c_{0,p}\res\left(\left[(L^{p}\log L)_{+},L\right]\Lambda^{-1}\right)=c_{0,p}\res\left([f_{0}^{[p]},U\Lambda]\Lambda^{-1}\right)

(4.40)

\displaystyle=\,c_{0,p}U(1-\Lambda)f_{0}^{[p]}.

On the other hand, since
[L,\log L]=0
, the flows
\frac{\partial}{\partial t^{0,p}}
can also be rewritten as

\displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p}}

\displaystyle=-c_{0,p}\res\left(\left[(L^{p}\log L)_{-},L\right]\Lambda^{-1}\right)=-c_{0,p}\res\left(\left[f_{-1}^{[p]}\Lambda^{-1},\Lambda^{2}\right]\Lambda^{-1}\right)

(4.41)

\displaystyle=c_{0,p}(\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1})\left(f_{-1}^{[p]}\right)^{+}.

Note that

\displaystyle f_{0}^{[p]}

\displaystyle=\res\left(L^{p}\log L\right)=\res\left(L^{p-1}\log L\cdot L\right)

\displaystyle=\res\left((f_{-2}^{[p-1]}\Lambda^{-2}+f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\Lambda^{-1})(\Lambda^{2}+U\Lambda)\right)

(4.42)

\displaystyle=\,f_{-2}^{[p-1]}+U^{-}f_{-1}^{[p-1]}.

On the other hand, we also have

\displaystyle f_{0}^{[p]}

\displaystyle=\res\left(L\cdot L^{p-1}\log L\right)=\res\left((\Lambda^{2}+U\Lambda)(f_{-2}^{[p-1]}\Lambda^{-2}+f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\Lambda^{-1})\right)

(4.43)

\displaystyle=\left(f_{-2}^{[p-1]}\right)^{++}+U\left(f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\right)^{+}.

Applying the operator
\Lambda^{2}
on both sides of (

(\Lambda^{2}-1)f_{0}^{[p]}=(\Lambda-1)\left(U\left(f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\right)^{+}\right),

therefore

(4.44)

Thus from (
4.40
)–(
4.41
) and (

\displaystyle\epsilon\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p}}

\displaystyle=\,c_{0,p}U(1-\Lambda)f_{0}^{[p]}=-c_{0,p}\left(U\circ\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}\circ U\right)\left(f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle=\,-\frac{c_{0,p}}{c_{0,p-1}}\left(U\circ\frac{\Lambda-1}{\Lambda+1}\circ U\circ\frac{1}{\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1}}\right)\left(c_{0,p-1}(\Lambda-\Lambda^{-1})\left(f_{-1}^{[p-1]}\right)^{+}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\epsilon}{p}\mathcal{R}\frac{\partial V}{\partial t^{0,p-1}},\quad p\geq 1.

The proposition is proved.

Theorem 4.8
.

The logarithmic flows (
4.8

(4.45)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial U}{\partial t^{0,p}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{1}\frac{\delta H_{0,p}}{\delta U},

\displaystyle H_{0,-1}=\frac{1}{4}\int\res(\log L)\td x=\frac{1}{4}\int\frac{2\epsilon\partial_{x}}{\Lambda-1}(\log U)\td x=\frac{1}{2}\int\log U\td x,

Proof of Theorem

By following the approach used in the proof of Lemma 3.5 of
[

\delta(\log_{+}L-\epsilon\partial_{x})=\delta(\log_{+}L)=\frac{\ad_{\log_{+}L}}{1-\exp(-\ad_{\log_{+}L})}(M\delta L),

is defined in (

), (
4.13

for any operators

Therefore for each

\displaystyle+\int\res\left(L^{p+1}\cdot\frac{\ad_{\log_{+}L}}{1-\exp(-\ad_{\log_{+}L})}(M\delta L)\right)\td x

\Lambda_{3}

Another important case of the fractional Volterra hierarchy (

5.
Conclusion

In this paper we prove the existence and uniqueness of solution to the loop equation of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity. The solution to the loop equation yields a quasi-Miura transformation which defines the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of the generalized Frobenius manifold. We show that the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of a special 1-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold is the extended
q
-deformed KdV hierarchy, part of this result gives a proof of the Conjecture 11.1 of
[
41
]
. It is also conjectured in
[
41
]
that the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of a special 2-dimensional generalized Frobenius manifold contains the flows of the Ablowitz–Ladik hierarchy. We will give a proof of this conjecture in a separate publication.

As we already know, for a usual semisimple Frobenius manifold the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy is a bihamiltonian integrable hierarchy of KdV type, i.e., both of the flows and the bihamiltonian structure can be represented in terms of differential polynomials of the unknown functions
[
21
,
9
,
34
,
44
]
. This polynomiality of the integrable hierarchy is quite nontrivial, since the coefficients of
\varepsilon^{2g}
of the quasi-Miura transformation that relates
the Principal Hierarchy and its topological deformation are rational functions of the
x
-derivatives of the unknown functions.
Nonetheless, we conjecture that the topological deformation of the Principal Hierarchy of a semisimple generalized Frobenius manifold with non-flat unity also possesses such a property of polynomiality, and we will study this topic in subsequent publications.

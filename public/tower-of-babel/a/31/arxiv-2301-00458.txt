Presentation of an Iwasawa algebra: The pro- p p -Iwahori of reductive groups
Aranya Lahiri, Jishnu Ray

Abstract
. In this article we generalize results of Clozel and Ray (for S ​ L 2 SL_{2} and S ​ L n SL_{n} respectively) to give explicit ring-theoretic presentation in terms of a complete set of generators and relations of the Iwasawa algebra of the pro- p p Iwahori subgroup of a connected, split, reductive group 𝔾 {\mathbb{G}} over ℚ p {{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}} .

Primary: 11R23, 20G25 Secondary: 16L30.

1.
Introduction

In this article we are interested in the Iwasawa algebra of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of the
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
-valued points
G:={\bf G}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
, of a connected reductive group
{\bf G}
defined and split over
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
.

Let’s recall that for a pro-
p
group
G
and a finite extension
L/{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
with ring of integers
{\mathcal{O}}
, the projective limit over the set of open normal subgroups
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
of
G
,

\Lambda(G):={\mathcal{O}}[[G]]:=\varprojlim_{N\in{\mathcal{N}}(G)}{\mathcal{O}}[G/N],

equipped with the appropriate projective limit topology, is called the
completed group ring
or the
Iwasawa algebra
of
G
over
{\mathcal{O}}
. It is a complete pseudo-compact topological ring that plays an important role in the study of representations of
p
-adic groups and number theory via it’s connection to the
p
-adic Local Langlands program. The reader may find in the excellent survey article
[
AB06
]
a collection of several ring theoretic properties of Iwasawa algebras of a
p
-adic group and several open problems on them.

In this article we give a ring theoretic presentation of
\Lambda(I)
, where
I
is the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of a connected reductive group
G
satisfying the assumptions above. We work under the assumption that
p>h+1
where
h
is the Coxeter number of the reductive group (see Section
2.1
). Under this assumption the pro-
p
Iwahori
I
is
p
-saturated in the sense of Lazard (see Theorem
2.4.1
,
[
LS22
]
). The main result of the article is,

Theorem 1.0.1
.

For
p>h+1
, the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(I)
is naturally isomorphic as topological rings to
{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{R}}
(see Theorem
6.0.2
).

Here
{\mathcal{A}}
is the noncommutative power series ring over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
in a certain number of variables (depending on
G
) and
{\mathcal{R}}
is a two-sided ideal of
{\mathcal{A}}
coming from the Chevalley relations on the
p
-adic group
G
.

The first result in this direction was carried out by Clozel in
[
Clo11
]
, then taken up in
[
Clo17
]
and used in
[
Clo18
]
in context of a proposed ‘
p
-adic base change map’. There he gives an explicit ring theoretic presentation of the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(I_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
of
SL_{2}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
and of
\Lambda(I_{L})
, the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I_{L}
of
SL_{2}(L)
for some unramified extension
L/{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
. Later, the second author of this article generalized the result to the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup of
SL_{n}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
(see
[
Ray20
]
). He also solved the case of a general uniform pro-
p
group in
[
Ray18a
]
.

Here we follow the circle of ideas of Clozel and the second authors’ work for
SL_{2}
and
SL_{n}
. The main technical challenge here is that the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup can be equipped with a
p
-valuation with respect to which it is
p
-saturated but in general it is not a uniform group. Essentially, the main difference being the associated graded algebra in the case of a uniform pro-
p
group is a commutative polynomial ring, while that is not the case in our setup.

Clozel could carry out his calculations by hand because of a relatively small number of variables and the relations between them being simple and easily calculable. The techniques used by the second author in
[
Ray20
]
were also explicit but involved somewhat tedious computations involving large matrices.

Clozel’s main interest in the explicit ring theoretic presentation of the Iwasawa algebras seem to stem from the fact that he could propose a formal candidate for the
p
-adic base change map between
\Lambda(I_{L})\rightarrow\Lambda(I_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
. But even Clozel himself notes that the right setup for such a base change map perhaps involves the rigid analytic distribution algebras associated to the corresponding rigid analytic groups of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroups. And as of now, we are not sure of the significance of such a formal base change map and thus for the purpose of exposition and ease of calculations we have restricted ourselves to groups defined over
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
. The same technique should generalize to groups defined over an unramified extension
L/{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
as well, and using the explicit presentation it should not be difficult to define a ‘formal base change map’ between
\Lambda(I_{L})\rightarrow\Lambda(I_{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
.

1.1.
Sketch of ideas for the main theorem

Before starting to write things formally we give a brief and slightly vague account of the main ideas going into the proof of the main theorem.

The natural
p
-valuation on the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I
of
G
makes it a
p
-saturated group, which in turn implies that
I
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}
as a
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
-analytic manifold. Thus the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(I)
is isomorphic as a
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-module to
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}]]
. Now the Chevalley relations between the Chevalley basis of the Lie algebra of
I
induces relations between the variables
X_{i}
. We show here that this is a complete set of generator and relations.

A key observation towards the proof is to realize that it is enough to prove an analogous statement for the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra
\Omega(I):=\Lambda(I)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
. Reduction mod-
p
makes the Chevalley relations much easier to work with. The proof then boils down to a dimension counting of associated graded vector space of
\Omega(I)
, as executed in Theorem
4.0.3
.

We point out some useful context and contrast for the reader of this article:

•

By using results of a previous paper by the first author and Sorensen (
[
LS22
]
) we conceptualize the calculations significantly. The key input being a systematic way of writing down a
p
-valuation and an ordered basis for the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup. This was missing in literature when the second author published
[
Ray20
]
, which caused him to resort to the long calculations. This simplification also allows us to deal with the Chevalley relations without having to break them into too many cases.

•

We replace the lexicographic ordering on the set of positive roots in
[
Ray20
]
by any additive ordering as constructed by Papi in
[
Pap94
]
. In the process perhaps we shade more light on the seemingly adhoc choice of order in the ordered basis for
I
in
[
Ray20
]
.

•

We also provide a more conceptual proof of Theorem
4.0.3
, which is in fact the technical heart of the article.

•

In
[
WE21
]
the author also gives a ring theoretic presentation of the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra. But their methods are very different from ours, and we do not know the connection between these two approaches yet.

1.2.
Applications in Iwasawa theory

Let
E
be an elliptic curve without complex multiplication, over a number field
F
with good ordinary reduction at places of
F
above
p
. Consider the
trivializing extension

F_{\infty}:=F[E_{p^{\infty}}]=\cup_{n\geq 1}F[E_{p^{n}}]
, where
E_{p^{n}}
is the
p^{n}
-torsion points of
E(\overline{F})
. This trivializing extension has Galois group
G
which is a compact open subgroup of
GL(2,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
for all primes
p
; and
G=GL(2,{\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
for all but finitely many primes
p
. The Pontryagin dual
\mathcal{X}_{\infty}
of the Selmer group
\widehat{\mathrm{Sel}(E/F_{\infty})}
over this trivializing extension carries several arithmetic information of
E
and its structure as an Iwasawa module has deep number theoretic consequences. It is conjectured (see
[
Coa99
, Conjecture 1.7]
) that
\mathcal{X}_{\infty}
is a finitely generated torsion
\Lambda(G)
-module. See Theorem 1.10 and the example following it of
(loc.cit)
for cases
when the conjecture holds. A partial result giving the explicit structure of this dual Selmer group as an Iwasawa module is also known. More precisely,
\mathcal{X}_{\infty}
is pseudo-isomorphic to a direct sum of cyclic Iwasawa modules:

\mathcal{X}_{\infty}\sim\oplus_{i=1}^{m}\Lambda(G)/L_{i},

where
L_{i}
are
left
reflexive ideals in the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(G)
(see the structure theorem in
[
CSS03
, pg. 74]
). The explicit description of these
left
reflexive ideals is not known. So a natural question is whether one can use the explicit presentation of the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(G)
to explicitly describe these reflexive ideals that are of arithmetic interest. Several progress has been made in the literature regarding the nonexistence of the
two-sided
reflexive ideals and over the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra
\Omega(G)
. An element
x\in\Omega(G)
is called
normal
if
x\Omega(G)=\Omega(G)x
and the ideal generated by a normal element is a two-sided reflexive ideal of
\Omega(G)
. When
G
is the first congruence kernel of a split, semi-simple, simply connected Chevalley group over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, under certain hypothesis, the explicit presentation of its Iwasawa algebra given by the second author in
[
Ray18b
]
can be used to show that every nonzero normal element of
\Omega(G)
must be a unit (see
[
HRW21
, Theorem 5.1]
). Hence there cannot be any two-sided non-trivial reflexive ideal generated by a normal element in the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra. Note that this technique of using the explicit presentation to prove such a result on the normal element (
loc.cit
) also works for the prime
p=3
and hence generalizes an earlier result by Ardakov, Wei and Jhang; their techniques were also entirely different (see
[
AWZ08
, Theorem A]
).
Such an explicit presentation of Iwasawa algebra could also be used to reprove known results regarding the center of the mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra (see
[
HRW21
, Proposition 7.1]
). We must mention that the techniques in
[
HRW21
]
concerns only the first congruence kernel and hence the underlying group is uniform pro-
p
. It is a natural question, which we believe should have an affirmative answer, to generalize the results in
(loc.cit)
and investigate whether the explicit presentation of the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup presented in this paper could be used to explore one-sided or two-sided reflexive ideals of the correponding Iwasawa algebra.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Iwahori factorization

Let
p
be a prime. For the sake of clarity we will discuss everything with the assumption that our ground field is
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
and just note at the beginning that most of the results in this preliminary section are usually stated in their corresponding sources in an appropriately generalized fashion for a finite extension
L/{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
.

We fix a connected reductive group
{\bf{G}}
which is split over
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
, and choose an
{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
-split maximal torus
{\bf{T}}\subset{\bf{G}}
. Let

(X^{*}({\bf{T}}),\Phi,X_{*}({\bf{T}}),\Phi^{\vee})
be the associated root datum. We fix a system of positive roots
\Phi^{+}
with simple roots

\Delta\subset\Phi^{+}
and let
\Phi^{-}=-\Phi^{+}
. We let
\text{ht}(\cdot)
be the height function on
\Phi
, and we recall that the
Coxeter number of
{\bf{G}}
is
h=1+\text{ht}(\theta)
where
\theta
is the highest root.

Let
G={\bf{G}}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
with the usual locally profinite topology, and similarly
T={\bf{T}}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
. Further we will denote the maximal compact subgroup by
T^{0}\subset T
and its Sylow pro-
p
-subgroup by
T^{1}
. Recall that to each
\alpha\in\Phi
one can attach a unipotent root subgroup

{\bf{U}}_{\alpha}
normalized by
{\bf{T}}
. We let
U_{\alpha}={\bf{U}}_{\alpha}({{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}})
and note that it comes with a natural isomorphism

u_{\alpha}:{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}U_{\alpha}
such that
tu_{\alpha}(x)t^{-1}=u_{\alpha}(\alpha(t)x)
for all
t\in T
and
x\in{{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}}
. This gives a filtration of
U_{\alpha}
by the subgroups
U_{\alpha,r}=u_{\alpha}((p^{r}))
for varying
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We consider the full Iwahori subgroup
J
corresponding to
\Phi^{+}
. If
{\bf G}
is assumed to be semisimple and simply connected then
J
has the geometric interpretation of being the stabilizer of the maximal facet (or alcove) corresponding to the root datum of the Bruhat-Tits building
{\mathcal{B}}(G)
of
{\bf G}
. We don’t pursue the geometric aspects of
J
and instead just note that the so-called Iwahori factorization says that multiplication defines a homeomorphism,

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha,1}\times T^{0}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha,0}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}J.

We concentrate our attention on the Sylow pro-
p
subgroup of
J
, namely the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I
. Again multiplication gives a homeomorphism,

(2.1.0)

\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\alpha,1}\times T^{1}\times\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}U_{\alpha,0}\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}I.

Here the products over
\Phi^{-}
and
\Phi^{+}
are ordered in an arbitrarily chosen way, see the proof of
[
OS19
, Lem. 2.3]
and Lemma 2.1, part i, in
[
OS19
]
. From Section
2.4
, we will fix an ordering of this product to discuss ordered basis and subsequent developments that depend on the ordered basis.

2.2.
p
-saturated groups

To make the article somewhat self-contained we collect here a few definitions related to
p
-valuation on a group
G
which can be any pro-
p
, torsion free, compact
p
-adic Lie group. Let us recall that a
p
-valuation

[
Sch11
, Chapter 23, pg 169]

\omega
on the group
G
is a real valued function,

\omega:G\setminus\{1\}\rightarrow(\frac{1}{p-1},\infty)
with the convention
\omega(1)=\infty
, satisfying

(1)

\omega(g^{-1}h)\geq\min(\omega(g),\omega(h))
,

(2)

\omega([g,h])\geq\omega(g)+\omega(h)
,

(3)

\omega(g^{p})=\omega(g)+1
.

A sequence of elements
(g_{1},\cdots,g_{r})
in a group
G
equipped with a
p
-valuation
\omega
is called an
ordered
basis

[
Sch11
, Def, Chapter 26, pg 182]
of
(G,\omega)
if it satisfies

(1)

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}\rightarrow G,(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\rightarrow g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}}
is a homeomorphism,

(2)

\omega(g_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots g_{d}^{x_{d}})=\min_{1\leq i\leq d}(\omega(g_{i})+v_{p}(x_{i}))
for any
x_{1},\cdots,x_{d}\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
.

Finally a
p
-valuable group
(G,\omega)
equipped with the valuation
\omega
is
saturated

[
Sch11
, Def, chapter 26, pg 187]
if any
g
with
\omega(g)>\frac{p}{p-1}
is a
p
-th power of some element of
G
.

2.3.
Additive ordering on the set of positive roots

We recollect results from
[
Pap94
]
in a form we will need to prove Lemma
5.3.1
later.

Let
W
be the associated Weyl group to the root system
\Phi
. Let
\Delta=\{\delta_{1},\cdots,\delta_{n}\}
be a set of simple roots and
\Phi^{+}
cooresponding set of positive roots and
\Phi^{-}=-\Phi^{+}
. And let
s_{1},\cdots,s_{n}
be the reflections corresponding to simple roots.

Definition 2.3.1
.

If
S:=\{\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{r}\}\subset\Phi^{+}
is a subset of positive roots of
\Phi
we say that
S
is associated to
w\in W
if there is a reduced expression
w=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{r}}
of
w
such that
\sigma_{1}=\delta_{i_{1}},\sigma_{j}=s_{i_{1}}\cdots s_{i_{j-1}}(\delta_{i_{j}})
,
\delta_{i_{j}}\in\Delta
.

In particular note that, if
w_{0}
is the longest element of the Weyl group then
\Phi^{+}
is associated to
w_{0}
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

An ordered subset
(S,<)
of
\Phi^{+}
is said to be
compatibly ordered
if the following two conditions hold.

(1) If
\lambda,\mu\in S
with
\lambda<\mu
, and
\lambda+\mu\in\Phi
then
\lambda+\mu\in S
and
\lambda<\lambda+\mu<\mu
.

(2) If
\lambda+\mu\in S
,
\lambda,\mu\in\Phi^{+}
then
\lambda
or
\mu
(or both) belong to
S
and one of them precedes
\lambda+\mu
.

The main result of
[
Pap94
]
is that

Theorem 2.3.3

(Theorem in pg 662 of
[
Pap94
]
)
.

A subset
S\subset\Phi^{+}
can be given a compatible ordering if and only if it is associated to some
w
of
W
.

Since we already observed that
\Phi^{+}
is associated to
w_{0}
, as a corollary of
2.3.3
we get

Corollary 2.3.4
.

We can impose a compatible ordering on
\Phi^{+}
.

2.4.
Ordered basis of pro-
p
Iwahori

We recall what we need from
[
LS22
, Section 3]
with slight modification and without any proofs.

In
[
LS22
]
it was shown that the pro-
p
Iwahori subgroup
I
can be equipped with a
p
-valuation
\omega
with respect to which
I
is saturated. For us it will be important to work with a choice of an ordered basis for
(I,\omega)
and to be able to explicitly write down the valuations of these basis elements.

Let us fix any ordering on the elements of
\Phi^{-}
with increasing height function on the roots, the elements of
\Delta
in an arbitrary way and the elements of
\Phi^{+}
with respect to an arbitrary but fixed compatible ordering in the sense of Definition
2.3.2
. And finally we combine this ordering to a totally ordered
\Phi
in a way such that
for any
\beta\in\Phi^{-},\delta\in\Delta,\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
we have
\beta<\delta<\alpha
.

Theorem 2.4.1
.

[
LS22

With
p-1>h,
an ordered basis with their respective valuations for
(I,\omega)
is given by

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}&\bullet&&u_{\beta}(p)_{\beta\in\Phi^{-}}&,&\omega(u_{\beta}(p))&=&1+\frac{{\rm ht}(\beta)}{h},&\\
&\bullet&&\delta^{\vee}(1+p)_{\delta\in\Delta}&,&\omega(\delta^{\vee}(1+p))&=&1,&\\
&\bullet&&u_{\alpha}(1)_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}},&,&\omega(u_{\alpha}(1))&=&\frac{{\rm ht}(\alpha)}{h}.&\end{array}

where we order the elements of the ordered basis corresponding to a total ordering of
\Phi
chosen as above.

Note that the description given in
[
LS22
]
chooses

as an ordered basis of

. Here to make the calculations more straight forward the natural choice seems to be
1+p
. From this point onward we change our notation to write
h_{\delta}
instead of
\delta^{\vee}
to match with the notation used in
[
Ray20
]
.

Remark 2.4.2
.

For future reference we note that for any element

of the ordered basis just mentioned above we have
\frac{1}{h}\leq\omega(g)\leq 1.

2.5.
Iwasawa Algebra

Let
G
be any profinite group and let
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
the set of open normal subgroups in
G
. Then we know that

G=\varprojlim_{N\in{\mathcal{N}}(G)}G/N

is the projective limit, as a topological group, of the finite groups
G/N
equipped witb the discrete topology.
By functoriality of associated group rings, the algebraic group rings
{\mathcal{O}}[G/N]
form, for varying
N
in
{\mathcal{N}}(G)
, a projective system of rings. The projective limit

\Lambda(G):={\mathcal{O}}[[G]]:=\varprojlim_{N\in{\mathcal{N}}(G)}{\mathcal{O}}[G/N]

is called the
completed group ring
or the
Iwasawa algebra
of
G
over
{\mathcal{O}}
. Equipping
\Lambda(G)
with the projective limit topology of the natural topologies of
{\mathcal{O}}[G/N]
makes it a complete pseudo-compact topological ring.

The embedding of
G\rightarrow\Lambda(G)
given by
g\rightarrow\delta_{g}
promotes to an injective embedding of the group ring
{\mathcal{O}}[G]
into
\Lambda(G)
with dense image.

Now let
(G,\omega)
be a
p
-saturated group over
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
with a
p
-valuation
\omega
and a choice of ordered basis
(h_{1},\cdots,h_{d})
. In this situation we get a homeomorphism of analytic manifolds

\displaystyle c:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d}

\displaystyle\rightarrow G

\displaystyle(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})

\displaystyle\mapsto h_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots h_{d}^{x_{d}}.

We use the standard notation
b_{j}=h_{j}-1\in\Lambda(G)
, and for
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{d})\in{\bf N}^{d}
we let
{\bf b}^{\alpha}=b_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots b_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
. Then any element
\lambda\in\Lambda(G)
can be written as
\lambda:=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}{\bf b}^{\alpha}
with
c_{\alpha}\in{\mathcal{O}}
and we can naturally equip it with the valuation,

(2.5.0)

\tilde{\omega}_{\Lambda}(\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}{\bf b}^{\alpha})=\inf_{\alpha}(v(c_{\alpha})+\sum_{i=1}^{d}\alpha_{i}\omega(h_{i})).

After pulling back and dualizing we get an isomorphism of topological
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-modules

\displaystyle c_{\star}:\Lambda(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d})

\displaystyle\cong\Lambda(G)

\displaystyle\delta_{e_{i}}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\delta_{h_{i}}.

By
[
Sch11
, 20.1]
we know that
\Lambda(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{d})\cong{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}]]
the power series ring in
d
variables, as a topological ring.

Using
2.4.1
we thus rewrite for
I
,

Lemma 2.5.1

(Lazard)
.

The following are isomorphic as
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-modules with an isomorphism given by,

\displaystyle\tilde{c}:{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[U_{\beta},V_{\alpha},W_{\delta}:\beta\in\Phi^{-},\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\delta\in\Delta]]\cong\Lambda(I)

\displaystyle 1+V_{\alpha}\rightarrow u_{\alpha}(1),

\displaystyle 1+W_{\delta}\rightarrow h_{\delta}(1+p),

\displaystyle 1+U_{\beta}\rightarrow u_{\beta}(p).

2.6.
The graded structure

For any
p
-valued group
G
, the valuation
\tilde{\omega}
induces a natural filtration of
\Lambda(G)
as follows. Define for
v\geq 0
,

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\Lambda(G)_{v}&:=\big\{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)\big|\tilde{\omega}(\lambda)\geq v\big\},\\
\Lambda(G)_{v^{+}}&:=\big\{\lambda\in\Lambda(G)\big|\tilde{\omega}(\lambda)>v\big\}.\end{array}

This gives a filtration and the associated graded algebra

{\rm gr}^{v}\Lambda(G):=\Lambda(G)_{v}/\Lambda(G)_{v^{+}};\hskip 5.69046pt{\rm gr}\Lambda(G)=\bigoplus_{v\geq 0}{\rm gr}^{v}\Lambda(G).

Under this filtration
\Lambda(I)
is filtered by
\frac{1}{h}{\mathbb{N}}
.

This follows from the fact that,
{\rm gr}^{v}\Lambda(I)
is generated by the independent elements

p^{s}\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\beta}^{n_{\beta}}\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}W_{\delta}^{n_{\delta}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}V_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}
such that

s+\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{-}}\frac{h+{{\rm ht}(\beta)}}{h}n_{\beta}+\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\frac{1}{h}n_{\delta}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{{{\rm ht}(\alpha)}}{h}n_{\alpha}=v.

The filtration on
\Lambda(I)
induces a natural filtration and thus a grading on
\Omega(I)=\Lambda(I)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
. We will use this to compute the dimension of
{\rm gr}^{v}\Omega(I)
as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space.
We know that
{\rm gr}^{v}\Omega(I)
is generated by
\prod_{\beta\in\Phi^{-}}U_{\beta}^{n_{\beta}}\prod_{\delta\in\Delta}W_{\delta}^{n_{\delta}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}V_{\alpha}^{n_{\alpha}}
with

\sum_{\beta\in\Phi^{-}}\frac{h+{{\rm ht}(\beta)}}{h}n_{\beta}+\sum_{\delta\in\Delta}\frac{1}{h}n_{\delta}+\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{{{\rm ht}(\alpha)}}{h}n_{\alpha}=v.

We don’t change the filtered pieces if we replace
v\in\frac{1}{h}{\mathbb{N}}
by
m=vh
. This change assigns the following valuations (which we still denote by
\tilde{\omega}
by abuse of notation) to the generators of the Iwasawa algebra.

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccccc}&\tilde{\omega}(u_{\beta}(p))&=&h+{{\rm ht}(\beta)},&\\
&\tilde{\omega}((h_{\delta}(1+p))&=&h,&\\
&\tilde{\omega}(u_{\alpha}(1))&=&{{\rm ht}(\alpha)}.&\end{array}

The remarks above recover the following fact which is really due to Lazard.

Theorem 2.6.1
.

The dimension of
{\rm gr}^{m}\Omega(I)
as an
{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
-vector space is equal to the dimension of space of homogeneous symmetric polynomials of degree
m
in the variables
\{U_{\beta},V_{\alpha},W_{\delta}:\beta\in\Phi^{-},\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\delta\in\Delta\}
where the variables are assigned the degrees equal to the corresponding shifted valuations of the ordered basis,
\deg(U_{\beta})=h+{{\rm ht}(\beta)},\deg(W_{\delta})=h
and
\deg(V_{\alpha})={{\rm ht}(\alpha)}
.

3.
Relations in the Iwasawa Algebra

3.1.
The Chevalley relations

The Chevalley relations on the Chevalley basis of
I
translate to relations between the non-commutative variables defining the Iwasawa algebra. As remarked in the introduction the main result of this article is that these are a defining set of relations for the Iwasawa algebra.

The Chevalley relations are the following (see
[
Ste16
, pg 23]
):

(
Diag
)
h_{\alpha}(u)h_{\beta}(t)=h_{\beta}(t)h_{\alpha}(u).

(
Diag-uni
)
h_{\delta}(t)x_{\alpha}(u)h_{\delta}(t)^{-1}=x_{\alpha}(t^{\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle}u)
where
\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(
Uni-1
)
If
\alpha+\beta\neq 0
and
\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi
, then we have

x_{\alpha_{1}}(t)x_{\alpha_{2}}(u)=x_{\alpha_{2}}(u)x_{\alpha_{1}}(t).

(
Uni-2
)
If
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\neq 0
and
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}\in\Phi
, then we have
x_{\alpha}(t)x_{\beta}(u)=\prod_{i,j>0}x_{i\alpha+j\beta}(c_{ij}t^{i}u^{j})x_{\beta}(u)x_{\alpha}(t),
where
c_{ij}
’s are unique integers depending on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

(
Uni-3
)
Let
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Writing
\alpha=\sum_{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}n_{i}\delta_{i}
, we have
h_{\alpha}(t)=\prod_{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}h_{\delta_{i}}(t)^{n_{i}}
for some non-negative integers
n_{i}
. We obtain

x_{\alpha}(1)x_{-\alpha}(p)=x_{-\alpha}(p)^{Q}\prod_{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}h_{\delta_{i}}(1+p)^{n_{i}}x_{\alpha}(1)^{Q},

where
Q=(1+p)^{-1}.

We include a proof of
(Uni-3)
; the other relations are exactly as in
[
Ste16
]
. For any root
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
, we have a homomorphism

\phi_{\alpha}:SL_{2}({\mathbb{Q}}_{p})\rightarrow\langle x_{\alpha}(t),x_{-\alpha}(t);t\in{\mathbb{Q}}_{p}\rangle

such that

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}(1+tE_{12})

\displaystyle=x_{\alpha}(t),

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}(1+tE_{21})

\displaystyle=x_{-\alpha}(t),

\displaystyle\phi_{\alpha}(tE_{11}+t^{-1}E_{22})

\displaystyle=h_{\alpha}(t),

where
E_{ij}
is the
2\times 2
matrix with 1 in the
(i,j)
-th entry and zero elsewhere.
In
SL_{2}({\mathbb{Z}}_{p})
we have the following matrix relation

(1+E_{12})(1+pE_{21})=\big(1+p(1+p)^{-1}E_{21}\big)\big((1+p)E_{11}+(1+p)^{-1}E_{22}\big)\big(1+(1+p)^{-1}E_{12}\big).

Since
\phi_{\alpha}
is a homomorphism we obtain

x_{\alpha}(1)x_{-\alpha}(p)=x_{-\alpha}(p(1+p)^{-1})h_{\alpha}(1+p)x_{\alpha}((1+p)^{-1}),

which implies

x_{\alpha}(1)x_{-\alpha}(p)=x_{-\alpha}(p)^{(1+p)^{-1}}\prod_{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}h_{\delta_{i}}(1+p)^{n_{i}}x_{\alpha}(1)^{(1+p)^{-1}}.

3.2.
Relations in the Iwasawa algebra

The corresponding relations in the Iwasawa algebra are

(
R-1
)

(1+W_{\delta_{1}})(1+W_{\delta_{2}})=(1+W_{\delta_{2}})(1+W_{\delta_{1}}).

(
R-2
)

(1+W_{\delta})(1+V_{\alpha})=(1+V_{\alpha})^{M}(1+W_{\delta}),\alpha\in\Phi^{+},M=(1+p)^{\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle}.

(
R-3
)

(1+W_{\delta})(1+U_{\beta})=(1+U_{\beta})^{M}(1+W_{\delta}),\beta\in\Phi^{-},M=(1+p)^{\langle\beta,\delta\rangle}.

(
R-4
)
V_{\alpha}U_{\beta}=U_{\beta}V_{\alpha},\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\beta\in\Phi^{-},\alpha+\beta\neq 0,\alpha+\beta\notin\Phi.

(
R-5
)
For
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
,

(1+V_{\alpha_{1}})(1+V_{\alpha_{2}})=\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}\in\Phi}}}{\prod}(1+V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}})^{c_{ij}}(1+V_{\alpha_{2}})(1+V_{\alpha_{1}}).

(
R-6
)
For
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{-}
,

(1+U_{\beta_{1}})(1+U_{\beta_{2}})=\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}\in\Phi}}}{\prod}(1+U_{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}})^{c_{ij}p^{i+j-1}}(1+U_{\beta_{2}})(1+U_{\beta_{1}}).

(
R-7
)
For
\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\beta\in\Phi^{-}
,

(1+V_{\alpha})(1+U_{\beta})=\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha+j\beta\in\Phi^{-}}}}{\prod}(1+U_{i\alpha+j\beta})^{c_{ij}p^{j-1}}\underset{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha+j\beta\in\Phi^{+}}}}{\prod}(1+V_{i\alpha+j\beta})^{c_{ij}p^{j}}(1+U_{\beta})(1+V_{\alpha}).

(
R-8
)
With
\alpha=\sum_{i}n_{i}\delta_{i},

(1+V_{\alpha})(1+U_{-\alpha})=(1+U_{-\alpha})^{Q}\underset{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}{\prod}(1+W_{\delta_{i}})^{n_{i}}(1+V_{\alpha})^{Q},

where
Q=(1+p)^{-1}.

4.
Explicit presentation of the Iwasawa algebra

Let
|\Phi|+|\Delta|=d
and let us totally order
S:=\{V_{\alpha},W_{\delta},U_{\beta},\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\delta\in\Delta,\beta\in\Phi^{-}\}
with an order as prescribed in Theorem
2.4.1
. Let
X:=\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\}
. It is clear that there is a natural bijection between
\psi:X\rightarrow S
sending
X_{j}
to the
j
-th element of
S
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be the universal
p
-adic algebra of non-commutative power series in the variables
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with the ordering as in set
S
. Let us note that any monomial of degree
n
in
{\mathcal{A}}
can be expressed as
a_{i}X_{i(1)}\cdots X_{i(n)}
where
i
is a map
i:\{1,\cdots,n\}\rightarrow\{1,\cdots,d\}
. Thus, writing
X_{i}:=X_{i(1)}\cdots X_{i(n)}
we can write

{\mathcal{A}}:=\big\{f=\sum_{n}\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i}\big|\,a_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\big\}.

The topology of
{\mathcal{A}}
is given by the valuation

v_{{\mathcal{A}}}(f=\sum_{n}\sum_{i}a_{i}X_{i})=\inf_{i}(v_{p}(a_{i})+|i|),

where
|i|=i(1)+\cdots+i(n)
.
The powers of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{{\mathcal{A}}}:=(p,X_{1},\cdots,X_{d})
gives a fundamental system of neighborhoods of
0
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}\subset{\mathcal{A}}
be the closed two-sided ideal generated in
{\mathcal{A}}
by the relations
(R-1)
-
(R-8)
(after we have translated them as a relation between the variables
X_{i}
’s using
\psi
). Let
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
be the reduction modulo
p
of
{\mathcal{A}}
. The same proof as
[
Clo11
, lemma 1.3]
gives us

Lemma 4.0.1
.

Let
\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}
be the image of
{\mathcal{R}}
in
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. Then
\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}
is the closed two-sided ideal in
\overline{A}
generated by the relations
(R-1)
-
(R-8)
.

Let
A:={\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\{X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}\}
be the non-commutative polynomial ring in the variables
X_{1},\cdots,X_{d}
. The natural inclusion map
A\rightarrow{\mathcal{A}}
has dense image.

Lemma 4.0.2
.

Let us (by abuse of notation) define a natural map
\psi:A\rightarrow\Lambda(I)
mapping
X_{i}\in A
to the corresponding
i
-th element among
S:=\{V_{\alpha},W_{\delta},U_{\beta},\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\delta\in\Delta,\beta\in\Phi^{-}\}
in the Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(I)
. Then
\psi
extends continuously to a surjective homomorphism
{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\Lambda(I)
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
2.5.1
and identifying via
\psi
we can write an element
\mu\in\Lambda(I)
as
\mu=\sum_{i}a_{i}\psi(X_{i})
and we have

\begin{array}[]{cccc}\tilde{\omega}_{\Lambda}(\mu)&=&\inf_{i}(v_{p}(a_{i})+\sum_{j=1}^{d}i(j)\omega(h_{j}))\text{ }(\text{from }\eqref{eq:evaluate}),\\
&\geq&\inf_{i}(\frac{v_{p}(a_{i})}{h}+\sum_{j=1}^{d}i(j)\frac{1}{h})\;(\text{as }\omega(h_{d})\geq\frac{1}{h},\;\text{remark }\ref{bound on valuation of basis}),\\
&\geq&\frac{1}{h}v_{\mathcal{A}}(\sum_{i}a_{i}(X_{i})).\end{array}

Hence the map is continuous. Surjectivity is almost automatic from the definition of
\psi
.
∎

Consider the the natural filtration of
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
by powers of the maximal ideal
{\mathfrak{m}}_{\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}}
, which we denote by
F^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. We have
F^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}/F^{n+1}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}={\rm gr}^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. The filtration
F^{n}
induces a filtration on
\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}=\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}/\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}

F^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}=F^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}+\overline{{\mathcal{R}}},

and hence a gradation

{\rm gr}^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}=F^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}/F^{n+1}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}.

Theorem 4.0.3
.

With the notation as above, for
n\geq 0
, we have

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}\leq\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{n}\Omega(I).

The proof of this theorem is the main heart of the paper and occupies the whole of Section
5
.

5.
Bounding the dimension

5.1.
Plan for the proof of Theorem
4.0.3

The theorem is obviously true for
n=0
as
\mathrm{gr}^{0}
is
{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}
and both sides equal
1
. For
n=1
,
{\rm gr}^{1}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}
is a quotient of the space

F^{1}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}/F^{2}\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
with basis
\{V_{\alpha},U_{\beta}\}
where
\alpha
varies over all the simple roots (i.e.
\mathrm{ht}(\alpha)=1
) and
\beta
is the negative of the highest root (hence
h+\mathrm{ht}(\beta)=1
). Hence

\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{1}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}\leq|\Delta|+1=\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{1}\Omega(I).

For the general case, we first reduce all the relations
\textbf{(R-1)}-\textbf{(R-8)}
modulo appropriate filtrations (see Proposition
5.2.1
in Section
5.2
). Proposition
5.2.1
tells us how the product of two variables in the
wrong
order is related to the product of the variables in the
right
order (the right order is the one prescribed by Theorem
2.4.1
). Next we show that we can arrange the variables in the wrong order in
{\rm gr}^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}
, by a sequence of transpositions and put them in the right order, and at each step we reduce the number of inversions (see Lemma
5.3.1
in Section
5.3
).
This shows that
\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{n}\overline{{\mathcal{B}}}\leq\dim_{{{\mathbb{F}}_{p}}}{\rm gr}^{n}\Omega(I)
since

{\rm gr}^{n}\Omega(I)
consists of
homogeneous symmetric polynomials
.

Let us start the proof of Theorem
4.0.3
by analyzing the mod-
p
reduction of certain binomial coefficients which will be necessary to study the relations between the variables modulo filtrations of an appropriate degree inside
\overline{\mathcal{R}}.

Lemma 5.1.1
.

Let
1\leq c\leq p-1
,
k\geq 1
,
p^{k}\nmid r
and
1\leq r<p^{k}
then we have

{cp^{k}\choose r}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)

Proof.

For any positive integer
n
, write
n
in base
p
, i.e.
n=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}p^{d},
where
0\leq a_{i}\leq p-1
and
a_{d}\neq 0
. Define the function
s_{p}(n):=\sum_{i=0}^{d}a_{i}.

Let us recall
v_{p}(n!)=\frac{n-s_{p}(n)}{p-1}
. Thus we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}v_{p}({cp^{k}\choose r})&=\frac{cp^{k}-s_{p}(cp^{k})}{p-1}-\frac{r-s_{p}(r)}{p-1}-\frac{cp^{k}-r-s_{p}(cp^{k}-r)}{p-1}&\\
&=\frac{s_{p}(cp^{k}-r)+s_{p}(r)-s_{p}(cp^{k})}{p-1}\end{array}

From
[
HLS11
, Proposition 2.1]
we get

\begin{array}[]{cccc}s_{p}(cp^{k}-r)&=&s_{p}(c-1)+(p-1)k-s_{p}(r-1)\\
s_{p}(cp^{k}-r)+s_{p}(r)-s_{p}(cp^{k})&=&s_{p}(c-1)+(p-1)k-s_{p}(r-1)+s_{p}(r)-c\\
=c-1+(p-1)k-s_{p}(r-1)+s_{p}(r)-c&=&(p-1)k+s_{p}(r)-s_{p}(r-1)-1\end{array}

Since we assumed
r<p^{k}
we have
s_{p}(r-1)<(p-1)k
and of course
s_{p}(r)-1\geq 0
. Thus we have

v_{p}({cp^{k}\choose r})=(p-1)k+s_{p}(r)-s_{p}(r-1)-1>(p-1)k-(p-1)k=0

And since it is an integer we get
v_{p}({cp^{k}\choose r})\geq 1
, which is what we needed to show.

∎

5.2.
Relations modulo appropriate filtrations

Recall that
\deg(W_{\delta})=h,\deg(V_{\alpha})=0pt(\alpha),\deg(U_{\beta})=h+0pt(\beta)
.

The variables in relations
(R-1)
and
(R-4)
commute modulo any filtration. In this following proposition we compute the other relations modulo appropriate filtrations. We will see that we are reduced to only three relations
(R-5’)
,
(R-7’)
and
(R-8’)
given below where the variables don’t commute modulo filtration of an appropriate order.

Proposition 5.2.1
.

We have

(R-2’)

W_{\delta}V_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha}W_{\delta}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha)+h+1}).

(R-3’)

W_{\delta}U_{\beta}=U_{\beta}W_{\delta}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta)+1}).

(R-5’)

V_{\alpha_{1}}V_{\alpha_{2}}=\underset{\underset{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}\in\Phi}{i,j>0}}{\sum}c_{ij}V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}}+V_{\alpha_{2}}V_{\alpha_{1}}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha_{1})+0pt(\alpha_{2})+1}).

(R-6’)

U_{\beta_{1}}U_{\beta_{2}}=U_{\beta_{2}}U_{\beta_{1}}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})+1}).

(R-7’)

V_{\alpha}U_{\beta}=c_{11}U_{\alpha+\beta}+U_{\beta}V_{\alpha}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{h+0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta)+1}).

(R-8’)

V_{\alpha}U_{-\alpha}=\underset{\delta_{i}\in\Delta}{\sum}n_{i}W_{\delta_{i}}+U_{-\alpha}V_{\alpha}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{h+1}).

Proof.

Consider relation

(R-2)

(1+W_{\delta})(1+V_{\alpha})=(1+V_{\alpha})^{M}(1+W_{\delta}),\alpha\in\Phi^{+},M=(1+p)^{\langle\alpha,\delta\rangle}.

Note that
\deg(W_{\delta}V_{\alpha})=h+0pt(\alpha)
and so we are interested in studying relation
(R-1)
modulo
{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha)+h+1}
.

We want to show that relation

(R-2)
reduces to

(1+W_{\delta})(1+V_{\alpha})=(1+V_{\alpha})(1+W_{\delta})\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha)+h+1}).

We will show that by proving

(1+V_{\alpha})^{M}\equiv(1+V_{\alpha})\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha)+h+1}).

We note that
M\equiv 1\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)
and

(1+V_{\alpha})^{M}=1+MV_{\alpha}+{M\choose 2}V_{\alpha}^{2}+\cdots

Since degree of
V_{\alpha}^{m}=0pt(\alpha)m
for any
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
we see that
V_{\alpha}^{m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha)+h+1})
as long as
0pt(\alpha)m>0pt(\alpha)+h
. So we will be done if we can show
{M\choose m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)
for
m\geq 2
and
0pt(\alpha)m\leq 0pt(\alpha)+h
or rewriting,
0pt(\alpha)(m-1)\leq h
then we are done. But note that, if
0pt(\alpha)(m-1)\leq h
then certainly
m-1\leq h
. Thus if we can prove
{M\choose m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)
for
m-1\leq h
we are done. By assumption,
h<p-1
, thus if
m-1\leq h<p-1
then
m<p
. So it’s clear in that case
{M\choose m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)
.

This proves
(R-2’)
.

Next we want to show that relation

(R-3)

(1+W_{\delta})(1+U_{\beta})=(1+U_{\beta})^{M}(1+W_{\delta}),\beta\in\Phi^{-},M=(1+p)^{\langle\beta,\delta\rangle},

reduces to

(1+W_{\delta})(1+U_{\beta})=(1+U_{\beta})(1+W_{\delta})\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta)+1}).

Just like the last relation we want to show

(1+U_{\beta})^{M}\equiv 1+U_{\beta}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta)+1}).

When
(0pt(\beta)+h)m>2h+0pt(\beta)
then
U_{\beta}^{m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta)+1})
thus we need to worry about
m\geq 2
and
(0pt(\beta)+h)m\leq 2h+0pt(\beta)
or rewriting
(0pt(\beta)+h)(m-1)\leq h
. But pretty much by the same argument as above we are reduced to the case
m-1\leq h
and the above calculation goes through which proves
(R-3’)
.

Next consider relation
(R-6)
.
We want to show the relations,
for
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\Phi^{-}
,

(1+U_{\beta_{1}})(1+U_{\beta_{2}})=\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}\in\Phi}}}(1+U_{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}})^{c_{ij}p^{i+j-1}}(1+U_{\beta_{2}})(1+U_{\beta_{1}}),

reduces to

(1+U_{\beta_{1}})(1+U_{\beta_{2}})=(1+U_{\beta_{2}})(1+U_{\beta_{1}})\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})+1}).

We need to analyze
(1+U_{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}})^{c_{ij}p^{i+j-1}}
carefully. As usual

\deg(U_{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}}^{m})=m(h+0pt(i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2})).

Thus for any
m
such that

m(h+0pt(i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}))>2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})
, we have

\deg(U_{i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}}^{m})\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})}+1).

From now on we safely make the assumption,

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}m(h+0pt(i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2}))&\leq&2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})\\
\text{gives}\;m&\leq&\frac{2h+0pt(\beta_{1})+0pt(\beta_{2})}{h+0pt(i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2})}\\
&\leq&2h-2\;(\text{as }h+0pt(i\beta_{1}+j\beta_{2})\geq 1)\\
&\leq&2(p-1)-2=2p-4\;(\text{as }p>1+h).\end{array}

For any prime
p\geq 3
and any
k\geq 2
we have
p^{k}>2p-4
.
Thus we have

{c_{ij}p^{i+j-1}\choose m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p),\;m\leq 2p-4\;\text{and}\;i+j-1\geq 2.

We are left to deal with the case
i+j=2
or
i=1,j=1
. Note that by Lemma
5.1.1
,
{c_{ij}p\choose m}\equiv 0\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }p)
for
m<p
. Thus we can make the assumption
m\geq p
. But in this case we have

\begin{array}[]{ccccccc}m(h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}))&\geq&p(h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}))\\
&>&(1+h)(h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}))=h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})+h(h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}))\\
&\geq&h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})+h\text{ }(\text{as }h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2})\geq 1)\\
&=&2h+0pt(\beta_{1}+\beta_{2}).\end{array}

Hence this proves
(R-6’)
.

Next consider relation
(R-5)
. We want to show the relation,
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\in\Phi^{+}
,

(1+V_{\alpha_{1}})(1+V_{\alpha_{2}})=\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}\in\Phi}}}(1+V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}})^{c_{ij}}(1+V_{\alpha_{2}})(1+V_{\alpha_{1}}),

reduces to

V_{\alpha_{1}}V_{\alpha_{2}}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}\in\Phi}}}c_{ij}V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}}+V_{\alpha_{2}}V_{\alpha_{1}}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha_{1})+0pt(\alpha_{2})+1}).

This follows from the observations that

(1+V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}})^{c_{ij}}\equiv 1+c_{ij}V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}}\text{ }(\mathrm{mod}\text{ }{\rm Fil}^{0pt(\alpha_{1})+0pt(\alpha_{2})+1})

by degree calculation and that
\deg(V_{i\alpha_{1}+j\alpha_{2}})+\deg(V_{\alpha_{i}})>0pt(\alpha_{1})+0pt(\alpha_{2})
for
i\in\{1,2\}
.
This shows
(R-5’)
.

Next consider the relation
(R-7)
. For
\alpha\in\Phi^{+},\beta\in\Phi^{-}
,

(1+V_{\alpha})(1+U_{\beta})=\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha+j\beta\in\Phi^{-}}}}(1+U_{i\alpha+j\beta})^{c_{ij}p^{j-1}}\prod_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i,j>0}}{{i\alpha+j\beta\in\Phi^{+}}}}(1+V_{i\alpha+j\beta})^{c_{ij}p^{j}}(1+U_{\beta})(1+V_{\alpha}).

Let us first deal with the case
i\alpha+j\beta\in\Phi^{-}
. For

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}m(h+0pt(i\alpha+j\beta))&\leq&h+0pt(\beta)+0pt(\alpha)\\
\text{gives}\;m&\leq&\frac{h+0pt(\beta)+0pt(\alpha)}{h+0pt(i\alpha+j\beta)}\\
&\leq&2h-2\;(\text{as }h+0pt(i\alpha+j\beta)\geq 1,0pt(\beta)\leq-1,0pt(\alpha)\leq h-1)\\
&\leq&2(p-1)-2=2p-4\;(\text{as }p>1+h).\end{array}

By Lemma

, similar to the argument we gave while deducing

, we see that if

, then with this restriction on

.

When

or

\deg(U_{i\alpha+2\beta})^{p}=p(h+0pt(i\alpha+2\beta))>(h+1)(h+0pt(i\alpha+2\beta))

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta)&\geq&1\\
\text{hence }\text{ }\text{ }i0pt(\alpha)&\geq&i(1-0pt(\beta))\\
\text{which gives }\text{ }\text{ }i0pt(\alpha)+20pt(\beta)&\geq&i(1-0pt(\beta))+20pt(\beta)=\textit{i}+(2-\textit{i})0pt(\beta).\end{array}

So if

then the right hand side is definitely

, but this is not possible as
i\alpha+2\beta
was assumed to be a negative root. Thus we see that only value

can take is

. But in that case

,

\displaystyle p(h+0pt(\alpha)+20pt(\beta))

\displaystyle>(h+1)(h+0pt(\alpha)+20pt(\beta))

\displaystyle=(h+0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta))+h\big(h+0pt(\alpha)+20pt(\beta)\big)+0pt(\beta)

\displaystyle=(h+0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta))+h\big(h+0pt(\alpha+2\beta)\big)+0pt(\beta)

\displaystyle>(h+0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta))+(h+0pt(\beta))\text{ }(\text{as }h+0pt(\alpha+2\beta)>1)

\displaystyle>h+0pt(\alpha)+0pt(\beta)\text{ }(\text{as }h+0pt(\beta)>1).

When

Now to look at the contribution coming from positive roots part of the equation

When

In this case we have

6.
Proof of the main theorem

In this section we recall and prove the main theorem in two steps. Both the proofs are exactly same proof as that of Clozel (see
[
Clo11
, pg. 554, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4]
), we recall them here for the purpose of completion.

Theorem 6.0.1
.

The mod-
p
Iwasawa algebra
\Omega(I)
is naturally isomorphic to
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}/\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}
.

Proof.

The natural map
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\Lambda(I)
sends
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{{\mathcal{A}}}
to
{\mathcal{M}}^{n}_{\Lambda(I)}
, where
{\mathcal{M}}_{\Lambda(I)}
is the maximal ideal of
\Lambda(I)
. The reduction mod-
p
of
\psi
respects the natural filtration on both sides and thus induce a natural map
{\rm gr}^{\bullet}\psi:{\rm gr}^{\bullet}{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\rm gr}^{\bullet}\Lambda(I)
. The induced map on the associated graded is surjective since
\psi
is surjective. Thus it is an isomorphism by Theorem
4.0.3
. This then imply the theorem since the filtration on
{\mathcal{B}}
is complete.
∎

Theorem 6.0.2
.

The Iwasawa algebra
\Lambda(I)
is naturally isomorphic to
{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{R}}
.

We replicate the proof which is also the same argument as Clozel’s corresponding statement.

Proof.

The reduction of
\psi:{\mathcal{A}}/{\mathcal{R}}\rightarrow\Lambda(I)
is
\overline{\psi}
. Recall that
\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}
is the image of
{\mathcal{R}}
in
\overline{{\mathcal{A}}}
. Assume
f\in{\mathcal{A}}
satisfies
\psi(f)=0
. We then have
\overline{f}\in\overline{{\mathcal{R}}}
by Theorem
6.0.1
. So
f=r_{1}+pf_{1}
,
r_{1}\in{\mathcal{R}},f_{1}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Then by applying
\psi
to both sides we get
\psi(f_{1})=0
. Inductively, we obtain an expression
f=r^{n}+p^{n}f_{n}
with
r_{n}\in{\mathcal{R}}
and
f_{n}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Since
p^{n}f_{n}
tends to
0
in
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{R}}
is closed, we see that
f\in{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

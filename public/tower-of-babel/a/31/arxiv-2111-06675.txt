Construction of Spectrally-Null-Constrained Zero-Correlation Zone Sequences with Flexible Support
Nishant Kumar, Palash Sarkar, Sudhan Majhi

Abstract
In recent years, traditional zero-correlation zone (ZCZ) sequences are being studied due to support interference-free quasi-synchronous code division multiple access (QS-CDMA) systems. However, in cognitive radio (CR) network, it is desirable to design ZCZ sequences having spectral null constraint (SNC) property to achieve low spectral density profile. This paper focuses on the construction of SNC-ZCZ sequences having flexible support, where support refers to a collection of indices corresponding to non-zero entries in the sequence. The proposed SNC-ZCZ sequences reduce to traditional ZCZ sequences when the size of the support becomes equal to the length of the sequence. To obtain ZCZ sequences, we first propose construction of traditional/SNC-Complete complementary codes (SNC-CCCs) using a class of extended Boolean functions (EBFs). With the help of this class we propose another class of EBFs that generates asymptotically optimal traditional/SNC-ZCZ sequences of prime-power lengths with respect to Tang-Fan-Matsufuzi bound. Furthermore, a relation between the second-order cosets of first-order generalized Reed-Muller code and the proposed traditional ZCZ sequences is also established.



keywords
Quasi-synchronous code division multiple access (QS-CDMA), spectrally null constraint (SNC), complete complementary code (CCC), extended Boolean functions (EBFs), ZCZ sequences, generalized Reed-Muller (GRM) code.

1
Introduction

The construction of sequences possessing favorable correlation properties assumes a crucial significance in the realm of wireless communications and radar sensing. Such sequences find widespread utility across various applications, including but not limited to channel estimation, synchronization, spread spectrum communications, surveillance, and ranging
Fan and Darnell 1996
. The polyphase sequences having zero-correlation zone (ZCZ) properties have been found suitable for application in quasi-synchronous-code division multiple access (QS-CDMA) system to provide interference free environment. A ZCZ is characterized by a region of zero auto- and cross-correlations centered around the in-phase timing position. These traditional ZCZ sequences (having non-zero entries) assume that there are continuous spectral bands available, so the energy of the sequence can be distributed across all the carriers within that spectral band. Since there’s a limited and valuable amount of available radio spectrum for wireless communication and radar sensing, researchers are trying to find better ways to use it efficiently
Zhao and Sadler 2007
. This emerging concept is referred to as cognitive radio (CR)
Haykin 2005
or cognitive radar
Haykin 2006
. A significant challenge in cognitive radio (CR) systems is achieving interference-free multiple access communications with a low spectral density profile and robust anti-jamming capability. In pursuit of this goal, a potential strategy involves the utilization of spread-spectrum CDMA techniques, employing sequences possessing the ZCZ property. Exploiting the correlation attribute of ZCZ sequences, a QS-CDMA system utilizing ZCZ sequences as its spreading codes can achieve interference-free performance, contingent on the condition that all received signals align with the ZCZ
Suehiro 1994
. In the literature, ZCZ sequences were first proposed in
Fan and Darnell 1996
, and have been well developed by many researchers
Chen and Wu 2018
;
Liu et al. 2014
;
Tang et al. 2010
;
Hayashi 2009
;
Zhou et al. 2018
;
Zhang et al. 2020
. Nonetheless, traditional ZCZ sequences encounter a challenge when applied directly in CR systems. This arises due to their design presumption, which typically assumes the presence of the entire spectral band, rather than the specific non-contiguous spectral bands dictated by the spectrum hole constraint within a CR system. Imposing this constraint for spectral nulling jeopardizes the ZCZ property within a traditional ZCZ sequence set, leading to potential loss or degradation of its intended benefits. In this paper, sequences with constraints of spectral nulls are referred
to as spectrally-null-constrained (SNC) sequences. The term ”support” pertains to a set of indices that correspond to elements within the sequence that have non-zero values. Therefore, SNC-Sequences can also be defined as the sequences whose size of the support is not equal to the length of the sequence. Additionally, if the size of the support equals to the length of the sequence, then the sequence is referred to as a traditional sequence.

In the realm of systematic SNC sequence construction, the pioneering work of Tsai
et al.

Tsai et al. 2011
introduced the initial SNC-ZCZ family by utilizing a comb-like sequence distribution in the frequency domain. In 2018, Liu
et al.

Liu et al. 2018
achieved SNC sequences with minimal autocorrelation values through carefully selected ternary frequency-domain duals possessing zero periodic autocorrelation sidelobes. A recent contribution by Tian
et al.

Tian et al. 2020
presented an analytical construction of SNC sequences in single-channel polyphase configurations. The work by Li
et al.

Li et al. 2022
proposed the construction of both SNC-ZCZ sequences and SNC-Z-periodic complementary set (SNC-ZPCS) sequences. Furthermore, in a recent study, Ye
et al.

Ye et al. 2022
introduced a method for constructing SNC-ZCZ sequences based on a circular Florentine rectangle and an interleaving approach.

This paper is driven by the limited availability of design approaches for SNC-ZCZ sequences in the existing literature. Due to their mathematical properties, Extended Boolean functions (EBFs) are widely recognized in the literature for their ability to generate sequences with flexible parameters. This motivates us to study EBFs as a way to overcome the current limitations in achieving flexible parameters for traditional/SNC-ZCZ sequences. In this study, we first propose a class of EBFs to generate traditional/SNC-CCC which further been extended to another class of EBFs to achieve traditional/SNC-ZCZ sequences of parameter
(p^{t},(p-1)p^{n},p^{n+t+1})
. The proposed ZCZ sequences has length in the form of prime-power and alphabet set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Furthermore, the proposed traditional/SNC-ZCZ sequences are asymptotically optimal as the obtained parameters attain the Tang-Fan-Matsufuzi bound asymptotically. Moreover, we also establish a relationship between traditional ZCZ sequences and GRM codes. The remaining sections of this paper are structured as follows.

Section 2 presents some basic definitions and notations which will be used throughout the paper. The proposed constructions of traditional/SNC-CCC and traditional/SNC-ZCZ sequences are provided in Section 3 and Section 4 respectively. Section 5 establishes the relation between proposed traditional ZCZ sequences and GRM codes. In Section 6 we compare the proposed construction with some existing construction. Lastly, we conclude the paper in Section 7.

2.2
Definition and Correlation Functions

Let
\boldsymbol{a}=\left(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{N-1}\right)

\gamma(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})(u)=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1-u}a_{i}b^{*}_{(i+u)},&0\leq u<L,\\
\sum_{i=0}^{L+u-1}a_{(i-u)}b^{*}_{i},&-L<u<0.\end{cases}

(3)

The functions
\gamma(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})
is called aperiodic cross-correlation function (ACCF) of
\boldsymbol{a}
and
\boldsymbol{b}
. Moreover, if
\boldsymbol{a}=\mathbf{b}

Further, the periodic cross-correlation function (PCCF) of
\boldsymbol{a}

\boldsymbol{b}

\phi(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})(u)=\begin{cases}\sum_{i=0}^{L}a_{i}b^{*}_{(i+u)\mod~L},&0\leq u<L,\\
\sum_{i=0}^{L}a_{(i-u)\mod~L}b^{*}_{i},&-L<u<0.\end{cases}

(4)

When
\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{b}
, then this function is called periodic auto-correlation function (PACF). We can also define PCCF in terms of ACCF as

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\phi}(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})(u)=\gamma(\boldsymbol{a},\boldsymbol{b})(u)+\gamma^{*}(\boldsymbol{b},\boldsymbol{a})(L-u).

(5)

For a sequence
\boldsymbol{a}
, its polynomial representation can be given by

p_{\boldsymbol{a}}(z)=a_{0}+a_{1}z+\ldots+a_{N-1}z^{N-1},

(6)

where
z\in\{e^{j2\pi t:~0\leq t<1}\}
is a complex number. Further. the following property is easy to derive

\arrowvert p_{\boldsymbol{a}}(z)\arrowvert^{2}=~\gamma(\boldsymbol{a})(0)+\sum_{u=1}^{N-1}{(\gamma_{\boldsymbol{a}}(u)z^{-u}+\gamma^{*}_{\boldsymbol{a}}(u)z^{u})}.

(7)

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathbf{C}=\{\mathbf{C}_{0},\mathbf{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{C}_{K-1}\}
be a collection of

\nu
th constituent sequence and

is transpose operator. Then the ACCF of two codes
\mathbf{C_{\mu_{1}}},\mathbf{C_{\mu_{2}}}\in\textbf{C}
is defined as

\gamma(\mathbf{C_{\mu_{1}}},\mathbf{C_{\mu_{2}}})(u)=\sum_{\nu=0}^{M-1}{\gamma(\mathbf{a}_{\nu}^{\mu_{1}},\mathbf{a}_{\nu}^{\mu_{2}})(u)}.

(8)

Definition 2
.

Let
C
be a code set defined as above and satisfying the following correlation properties,

\gamma(\mathbf{C_{\mu_{1}}},\mathbf{C_{\mu_{2}}})(u)=\begin{cases}{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}LM},&u=0,\ \mu_{1}=\mu_{2},\\
0,&0<|u|<L,\ \mu_{1}=\mu_{2},\\
0,&|u|<L,\mu_{1}\neq\mu_{2}.\end{cases}

(9)

Then

is said to be a
(K,M,L)
-MOGCS. Moreover,

For a GCS

\arrowvert p_{\mathbf{a}_{0}^{\mu}}(z)\arrowvert^{2}+\arrowvert p_{\mathbf{a}_{1}^{\mu}}(z)\arrowvert^{2}+\ldots+\arrowvert p_{\mathbf{a}_{M-1}^{\mu}}(z)\arrowvert^{2}=~\text{Constant}.

(10)

Definition 3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}}=\{\mathbf{z}_{0},\mathbf{z}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{N-1}\}
be a set of
N
sequences of length
L
each, i.e.,
\mathbf{z}_{i}=(\mathbf{z}_{i0},\mathbf{z}_{i1},\ldots,\mathbf{z}_{iL-1}),\ \ 0\leq i\leq N-.
Then,

.

Restricted and Squeezed Vector/Sequence

Let
p
be a prime and
f:\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\rightarrow

\mathbb{Z}_{p}

n

\mathbf{x}_{J}=

\left(x_{j_{1}},x_{j_{2}},\cdots,x_{j_{k}}\right)

2.4
Spectrally-Null-Constrained (SNC) Sequences
Shen et al. 2023

Let
\boldsymbol{a}=\left(a_{0},a_{1},\cdots,a_{N-1}\right)
be a complex-valued sequence, the support of
a
is
\Omega=\left\{x\in\mathbb{Z}_{N}:a_{x}\neq 0\right\}

2.5
Generalised Reed-Muller (GRM) Code

For a prime
p
, consider the polynomial ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}]

l

\mathbb{F}_{p}
is a field with

Definition 5
.

The
\vartheta
-th order
\vartheta\leq p
,
p
-ary GRM code,
GRM_{p}(l,\vartheta)
, is defined as the following set of
p
-ary vectors

GRM_{p}(l,\vartheta)=\{\xi(f):f\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{l}],~deg(f)<\vartheta\}.

(12)

Furthermore, it is noteworthy that we always have

in
\mathbb{F}_{p}

is no larger than

\mathcal{N}(r)=\Big\{x_{1}^{j_{1}}x_{2}^{j_{2}},\ldots x_{l}^{j_{l}}:j=0,1,2\ldots,p-~\text{and}~\sum_{i=1}^{l}{j_{i}}\leq\vartheta\Big\}.

(13)

Using combinatorics, we can always prove that the number of monomials in the set is
\mid\mathcal{N}\mid=\sum_{d=0}^{\vartheta}{{l-1+d\choose d}}
. It is well known that

\mathbb{F}_{p}

is its subspace. Therefore,

is a linear code with code length

and code dimension

as the rows of a matrix, then this matrix forms a generator matrix of

Lemma
1

(
Kasami et al. 1968
)
.

For
\lambda=p^{n}
and

, minimum Hamming distance of

is

where

Proposed Construction of SNC-CCC

Relation Between Proposed traditional ZCZ Sequences and GRM Codes

This section establishes a relation between constructed ZCZ sequences and GRM codes. For ease of notation, we assume
n+t+1=n^{\prime}
and
\mathbf{z}_{h}
refers to function instead of sequence as shown in (
37

Corollary

1
we can write

\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}}=\left\{\mathbf{z}_{h}=\mathcal{Q}+\sum_{j=1}^{n}{d_{\alpha}x_{\alpha}}+\sum_{\beta=1}^{t+1}{e_{\beta}x_{n+\beta}}+\sum_{r=1}^{t}{x_{\pi_{r}(m_{r})}h_{r}}+e^{\prime}:h_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\right\},

(37)

where

\mathcal{Q}=\underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{t}{\sum_{j=1}^{m_{r}-1}{x_{\pi_{r}(j)}x_{\pi_{r}(j+1)}}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}+\underbrace{\sum_{r=2}^{t+1}{c_{r}x_{n+r}x_{n+1}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{2}}+\hskip-4.26773pt\underbrace{\sum_{2\leq\mu<\nu\leq t}\hskip-8.5359pt{d_{\mu\nu}x_{n+\mu}x_{n+\nu}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{3}}\\
+\underbrace{\sum_{r=1}^{t}{x_{\pi_{r}(1)}x_{n+r}}}_{\mathcal{Q}_{4}}.

In the following corollary, we see that ZCZ sequence sets with fixed quadratic form

\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)

Corollary
2
.

Let
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
be second order coset of the first order GRM code. Then for a fixed quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}
,
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
contains
p^{n^{\prime}+1-t}
distinct ZCZ sequence sets.

Proof.

From equation (
37
), it is clear that for a fixed quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}
, the parameters which contribute to different ZCZ sequence sets are
d_{\alpha},~e_{\beta},
and
e^{\prime}
. But, whenever
x_{\pi_{r}(m_{r})}=x_{\alpha}
, then respective
d_{\alpha}
will no longer contribute to generate different ZCZ sequence sets because
\sum_{r=1}^{t}x_{\pi_{r}(m_{r})}h_{r}
contributes to different sequences in a particular ZCZ sequence sets. Therefore, total variables which contributes to different ZCZ sequence sets are
n^{\prime}+1-t
. Hence total number of different ZCZ sequence sets in
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
equals to
p^{n^{\prime}+1-t}
.
∎

It can be obtained that if we have a sequence in
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}}
, then it is a codeword belonging to second-order coset
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
where
\mathcal{Q}
is given by (
38
). Moreover, if any codeword belongs to the coset
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
then it belongs to a certain
(p^{t},(p-1)p^{n},p^{n+t+1})
-ZCZ sequence set. Hence ZCZ sequence sets with fixed quadratic form
\mathcal{Q}
, partition
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
.

In the following corollary, we explicitly determine the number of second-order cosets representatives having the form
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
.

Corollary
3
.

The number of coset representative
\mathcal{Q}
in the second-order coset of the form
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
where
\mathcal{Q}
is as defined in (
38
) is given by

(p-1)p^{\frac{(t-1)t}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{t}=n}{\prod_{r=1}^{t}{\frac{m_{r}!}{2}}}\bigg).

(39)

Proof.

One can easily see that the two permutations
\pi_{r}
and
\pi^{\prime}_{r}
, where
\pi^{\prime}_{r}(k)=\pi_{r}(m_{r}+k-1)
will generate the same quadratic form. Therefore, we have
\frac{m_{r}!}{2}
different quadratic forms of the type

x_{\pi_{r}(1)}x_{\pi_{r}(2)}+x_{\pi_{r}(2)}x_{\pi_{r}(3)}+\cdots+x_{\pi_{r}(m_{r}-1)}x_{\pi_{r}(m_{r})},~r=1,2,\ldots t,

(40)

where notations are the same as used in
Lemma

15
.
In addition, we have the condition
m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{t}=n

where
1\leq m_{r}\leq n
. Therefore, we can explicitly determine

\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{t}=n}{\prod_{r=1}^{t}{\frac{m_{r}!}{2}}}

number of distinct coset representatives of the type
{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
. It is to be noted that once we fix
{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
then
{\mathcal{Q}_{2}},{\mathcal{Q}_{3}},~\text{and}~{\mathcal{Q}_{4}}
are also fixed except their coefficients, i.e.,
c_{r}
and
d_{\mu\nu}
where
c_{r},d_{\mu\nu}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
,
c_{t+1}\neq 0
. Since
{\mathcal{Q}_{2}}
and
{\mathcal{Q}_{3}}
contain
t
and
\frac{(t-2)(t-1)}{2}
terms respectively and
c_{t+1}
can take only
p-1
values. Therefore, We have
(p-1)p^{t-1}p^{\frac{(t-2)(t-1)}{2}}=(p-1)p^{\frac{(t-1)t}{2}}
number of distinct coset representative of the type
{(\mathcal{Q}_{2}}+{\mathcal{Q}_{3}}+{\mathcal{Q}_{4}})+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
for the fixed value of
{\mathcal{Q}_{1}}
. Hence, combining all the discussion, we can conclude that the total number of coset representative of the form
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
is

(p-1)p^{\frac{(t-1)t}{2}}\bigg(\sum_{m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{t}=n}{\prod_{r=1}^{t}{\frac{m_{r}!}{2}}}\bigg).

∎

In the next corollary, it has been concluded that the ZCZ sequence sets lying inside the different cosets of
GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
are different.

Corollary
4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}^{\prime}}
be two ZCZ sequence sets obtained from
Corollary

1
, contained in
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)

\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
are different quadratic forms. Then,
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathscr{Z}^{\prime}}
are different ZCZ sequence sets.

Proof.

It Suffices to prove that the cosets
\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
are either disjoint or same. Let us assume that there exist an element
c
such that
c\in\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)\cap\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
. Then

c=\mathcal{Q}+c_{1}=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+c_{2},
for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1).
Which implies
\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=c_{2}-c_{1}
. Since,
c_{1},c_{2}\in GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
and
GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
is linear code therefore,
c_{2}-c_{1}\in GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
, which further implies
\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\in GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)
. Now for some

let
\mathcal{Q}-\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}=w
. Then,

\mathcal{Q}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+w+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1)=\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}+GRM_{p}(n^{\prime},1).

Which follows the result.
∎

Remark 4
.

From

Conclusion

In this paper, we introduce a construction method for SNC-ZCZ sequences of prime-power length based on EBFs having flexible support. The proposed SNC-ZCZ sequences are asymptotically optimal. Additionally, we establish a relationship between traditional ZCZ sequences and GRM codes.

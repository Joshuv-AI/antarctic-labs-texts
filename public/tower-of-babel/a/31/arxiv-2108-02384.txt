2
The Associated Simplicial Complexes and The Embedded Homology for Hypergraphs

In this section, we review some definitions as well as some basic properties about hypergraphs, their (lower-)associated simplicial complexes, and the embedded homology. We also give some examples and show that the homology of the associated simplicial complexes, the homology of the lower-associated simplicial complexes, and the embedded homology of the hypergraphs detect the topology of hypergraphs from different aspects.

Definition 13

A function

(ii).

\#\{\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}\mid f(\gamma)\geq f(\alpha),\gamma\in\mathcal{H}\}\leq 1

\displaystyle\#\{\beta^{(n+1)}>\alpha^{(n)}\mid f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha),\beta\in\mathcal{H}\}=0

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}\mid f(\gamma)\geq f(\alpha),\gamma\in\mathcal{H}\}=0.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\#\{\beta^{(n+1)}>\alpha^{(n)}\mid f^{\prime}(\beta)\leq f^{\prime}(\alpha),\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\}=0

and

\displaystyle\#\{\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}\mid f^{\prime}(\gamma)\geq f^{\prime}(\alpha),\gamma\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\}=0.

Therefore, we obtain
\alpha\in M(f^{\prime},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
. The lemma is proved.
∎

.

\displaystyle M(f,\mathcal{H}),

(3.1)

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle M({\underline{f}},\delta\mathcal{H}),

\displaystyle M(f,\mathcal{H})\cap\delta\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle\subseteq

\displaystyle M({\underline{f}},\delta\mathcal{H}).

4
Discrete Gradient Vector Fields on Hypergraphs

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph. In this section, we study the discrete gradient vector fields on
\mathcal{H}
.

4.1
Abstract Discrete Gradient Vector Fields on Hypergraphs

Definition 16
.

An
(abstract) discrete gradient vector field

\mathbb{V}
on
\mathcal{H}
is a collection of pairs
\{\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}\}
of hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
,
n\geq 0
, such that there is no non-trivial closed paths of the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}^{(n)},\beta_{0}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{1}^{(n)},\beta_{1}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{2}^{(n)},\cdots,\alpha_{r}^{(n)},\beta_{r}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{r+1}^{(n)}=\alpha_{0}^{(n)},

where for each
0\leq i\leq r
, we have
\{\alpha_{i}^{(n)}<\beta_{i}^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V}
,
\{\alpha_{i+1}^{(n)}<\beta_{i}^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V}
and
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{i+1}
.

Definition 17
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
. If
there does not exist any
n\geq 1
and any triple
(\gamma^{(n-1)},\alpha^{(n)},\beta^{(n+1)})
of hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}

and

\displaystyle\{\gamma<\alpha\}\in\mathbb{V}~~~{\rm and}~~~\{\alpha<\beta\}\in\mathbb{V},

then we call
\mathbb{V}
a
semi-proper
discrete gradient vector field.

Definition 18
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
. If
each hyperedge of
\mathcal{H}
is in at most one pair in
\mathbb{V}
, then we call
\mathbb{V}
a
proper
discrete gradient vector field.

Remark 5
:

By Definition
17
and Definition
18
, it is direct to see that a proper discrete gradient vector field is semi-proper while semi-proper discrete gradient vector field may not be proper.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 19
.

We define an
R
-linear map

\displaystyle R(\mathbb{V}):~~~R(\mathcal{H})_{n}\longrightarrow R(\mathcal{H})_{n+1}

(4.1)

induced from
\mathbb{V}
where for each
\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
, the
R
-linear map
R(\mathbb{V})
sends
\alpha
to

•

-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta
if
\{\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V}
,

•

0
if
\{\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}\}\notin V
.

Remark 6
:

In Definition
19
, the notion
\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle
is the incidence number of
\beta
and
\alpha
in the chain complex
C_{*}(\Delta\mathcal{H};R)
(cf.
[
9
, p. 98]
). Note that
\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle
takes the values
\pm 1
where
1
refers to the multiplicative unity of
R
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\mathbb{V}
is semi-proper if and only if the induced
R
-linear map
R(\mathbb{V})
satisfies

\displaystyle R(\mathbb{V})\circ R(\mathbb{V})=0.

(4.2)

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
. Let
R(\mathbb{V})
be the induced
R
-linear map.

(
\Longrightarrow
): Suppose
\mathbb{V}
is semi-proper. In order to prove (
4.2
), we take an arbitrary
\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
. By the linear property of
R(\mathbb{V})\circ R(\mathbb{V})
, it suffices to prove

\displaystyle R(\mathbb{V})\circ R(\mathbb{V})(\gamma^{(n)})=0.

(4.3)

Case 1
.
R(\mathbb{V})(\gamma^{(n)})=0
.

Then (
4.3
) follows immediately.

Case 2
.
R(\mathbb{V})(\gamma^{(n)})\neq 0
.

Suppose
\alpha^{(n+1)}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\{\gamma^{(n)}<\alpha^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V}

and
\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\neq 0
.
Since
\mathbb{V}
is semi-proper and
\{\gamma^{(n)}<\alpha^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V}
, we have that
\alpha
is not in any other pairs of the form
\{\alpha^{(n+1)}<\beta^{(n+2)}\}
in
V
. Thus we have
R(\mathbb{V})(\alpha)=0
. Consequently,

\displaystyle R(\mathbb{V})\circ R(\mathbb{V})(\gamma^{(n)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\mathbb{V})\Big(\sum_{\{\gamma^{(n)}<\alpha^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V},\atop\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\neq 0}-\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\alpha\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\{\gamma^{(n)}<\alpha^{(n+1)}\}\in\mathbb{V},\atop\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\neq 0}-\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle R(\mathbb{V})(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0.

Summarizing both Case 1 and Case 2, we obtain (
4.3
). This implies (
4.2
).

(
\Longleftarrow
): Suppose
R(\mathbb{V})
satisfies (
4.2
). In order to prove that
\mathbb{V}
is semi-proper, we suppose to the contrary that
\mathbb{V}
is not semi-proper. Then by Definition
17
, there exist
n\geq 1
and a triple triple
(\gamma^{(n-1)},\alpha^{(n)},\beta^{(n+1)})
of hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
such that

\displaystyle\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}

and

\displaystyle\{\gamma<\alpha\}\in\mathbb{V}~~~{\rm and}~~~\{\alpha<\beta\}\in\mathbb{V}.

Consequently,

\displaystyle\langle R(\mathbb{V})\circ R(\mathbb{V})(\gamma^{(n-1)}),\beta^{(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\langle R(\mathbb{V})(\alpha^{(n)}),\beta^{(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\langle\beta^{(n+1)},\beta^{(n+1)}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle\partial\alpha,\gamma\rangle\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pm 1.

This contradicts with our assumption that
R(\mathbb{V})
satisfies (
4.2
).
Therefore,
\mathbb{V}
is semi-proper.
∎

Remark 7
:

In particular, if
\mathcal{H}
is a simplicial complex, then the
R
-linear map (
4.1
) is defined in
[
9
, Definition 6.1]
.

4.2
Discrete Gradient Vector Fields of Discrete Morse Functions on Hypergraphs

Let
f
be a discrete Morse function on
\mathcal{H}
.

Definition 20
.

The
discrete gradient vector field of
f
, denoted as
\grad f
, is the collection of all the pairs
\{\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}\}
(
n\geq 0
) of the hyperedges in
\mathcal{H}
such that
f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha)
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be a discrete Morse function on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\grad f
is a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

By Definition
16
, to prove that
\grad f
is a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
, we only need to prove that there is no nontrivial closed paths of the form

\displaystyle\alpha_{0}^{(n)},\beta_{0}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{1}^{(n)},\beta_{1}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{2}^{(n)},\cdots,\alpha_{r}^{(n)},\beta_{r}^{(n+1)},\alpha_{r+1}^{(n)}=\alpha_{0}^{(n)},

where for each
0\leq i\leq r
, we have
\{\alpha_{i}^{(n)}<\beta_{i}^{(n+1)}\}\in\grad f
,
\{\alpha_{i+1}^{(n)}<\beta_{i}^{(n+1)}\}\in\grad f
and
\alpha_{i}\neq\alpha_{i+1}
. Suppose to the contrary, there exists such a closed path. Then by Definition
20
, we have

\displaystyle f(\beta_{i})\leq f(\alpha_{i})~~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~~f(\beta_{i})\leq f(\alpha_{i+1}).

This contradicts with Definition
13
. Therefore, there is no such closed paths, which implies that
\grad f
is a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f
be a discrete Morse function on
\mathcal{H}
. Then for any
n\geq 0
and any
\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
, the induced
R
-linear map
R(\grad f)
of
\grad f
is given as follows:

(i).

If there exists
\beta^{(n+1)}>\alpha^{(n)}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{H}
, such that
f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha)
, then

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta;

(ii).

If there does not exist any
\beta^{(n+1)}>\alpha^{(n)}
,
\beta\in\mathcal{H}
, such that
f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha)
, then

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha)=0.

Proof.

The lemma follows directly from Definition
19
.
∎

The next example shows that the discrete gradient vector fields of discrete Morse functions on hypergraphs may not be semi-proper.

Example 4.4
.

Consider the hypergraph
\mathcal{H}
and the discrete Morse function
f
given in Example
3.10
.
Let
\grad f
be the discrete gradient vector field of
f
. Then

\displaystyle(\grad f)(\{v_{0}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\{v_{0},v_{1}\},

\displaystyle(\grad f)(\{v_{0},v_{1}\})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\}.

Hence
(\grad f)\circ(\grad f)(\{v_{0}\})=-\{v_{0},v_{1},v_{2}\}\neq 0
.

Nevertheless, the next proposition shows that by imposing the condition (C) in Proposition

) on the hypergraphs, the discrete gradient vector fields of discrete Morse functions must be proper.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{H}
be a hypergraph satisfying the condition (C) in Proposition
3.11
. Let
f
be a discrete Morse function on
\mathcal{H}
. Then
\grad f
is a proper discrete gradient vector field.

Proof.

Firstly, we prove that
\grad f
is semi-proper. By Lemma
4.1
, it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle R(\grad f)\circ R(\grad f)=0.

(4.4)

The proof is similar with the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 6.3 (1)]
. Suppose
\gamma^{(n-1)},\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
and

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\gamma^{(n-1)})=\pm\alpha^{(n)}.

Then
\gamma^{(n-1)}<\alpha^{(n)}
and
f(\gamma^{(n-1)})\geq f(\alpha^{(n)})
. Since
\mathcal{H}
satisfies the condition (C), by Proposition
3.11
,
we see that there does not exist any
\beta^{(n+1)}\in\mathcal{H}
such that
\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}
and
f(\beta^{(n+1)})\leq f(\alpha^{(n)})
. Thus

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha^{(n)})=0,

which implies

\displaystyle R(\grad f)\circ R(\grad f)(\gamma^{(n-1)})=0.

Therefore, we obtain (
4.4
).

Secondly, we prove that
\grad f
is proper. Suppose to the contrary,
\grad f
is not proper. Then since
\grad f
is semi-proper, it follows from Definition
18
and Definition
17
that at least one of the followings is satisfied:

(a).

There exist
\gamma_{1}^{(n-1)},\gamma_{2}^{(n-1)},\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
with
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\{\gamma_{1}<\alpha\}\in\grad f~~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~~\{\gamma_{2}<\alpha\}\in\grad f;

(b).

There exist
\beta_{1}^{(n+1)},\beta_{2}^{(n+1)},\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
with
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\{\alpha<\beta_{1}\}\in\grad f~~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~~\{\alpha<\beta_{2}\}\in\grad f.

Equivalently, we can re-state (a) and (b) respectively as (a)’ and (b)’:

(a)’.

There exist
\gamma_{1}^{(n-1)},\gamma_{2}^{(n-1)},\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
with
\gamma_{1}\neq\gamma_{2}
such that

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\gamma_{1}^{(n-1)})=\pm\alpha^{(n)}~~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~~R(\grad f)(\gamma_{2}^{(n-1)})=\pm\alpha^{(n)}.

(b)’.

There exist
\beta_{1}^{(n+1)},\beta_{2}^{(n+1)},\alpha^{(n)}\in\mathcal{H}
with
\beta_{1}\neq\beta_{2}
such that

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha^{(n)})=\pm\beta_{1}^{(n+1)}~~~~~~{\rm and}~~~~~~R(\grad f)(\alpha^{(n)})=\pm\beta_{2}^{(n+1)}.

Since

is an

-linear map, we see that (b)’ is impossible. On the other hand, (a)’ implies that
\gamma_{i}<\alpha
and
f(\alpha)\leq f(\gamma_{i})
for

.
This contradicts that

is a discrete Morse function on

. Therefore,

must be proper.
∎

Let
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
be two hypergraphs such that
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{H}

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathbb{V}
is also a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{V}
be a discrete gradient vector field on
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. We generalize the Hasse diagram construction (cf.
[
13
, Section 6]
) of simplicial complexes and apply a similar argument to hypergraphs. Specifically,

•

We construct a digraph
D_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
) as follows:

–

The Vertices of
D_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
)
: the vertices of
D_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
) are in 1-1 correspondence with the hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}
(resp.
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
);

–

The Directed Edges of
D_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp.
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
)
: for any hyperedges
\alpha
and
\beta
of
\mathcal{H}
(resp. of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
), we assign a directed edge in
D_{\mathcal{H}}
(resp. in
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
) from
\beta
to
\alpha
, denoted as
\beta\to\alpha
, iff.
\alpha^{(n)}<\beta^{(n+1)}
for some
n\geq 0
.

•

We construct a digraph
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}}
as follows:

–

The Vertices of
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}}
: the vertices of
D_{\mathcal{H}^{\prime},\mathbb{V}}
are in 1-1 correspondence with the hyperedges of
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
;

–

be discrete Morse functions on

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be the canonical projection from
R(\mathcal{H})_{*}
to
R(\mathcal{H}^{\prime})_{*}
sending an
R
-linear combination of hyperedges

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime},x_{i}\in R}x_{i}\sigma_{i}+\sum_{\tau_{j}\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\prime},y_{j}\in R}y_{j}\tau_{j}

to the
R
-linear combination of hyperedges

\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{i}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime},x_{i}\in R}x_{i}\sigma_{i}.

Then

\displaystyle R(\grad f^{\prime})=\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\circ R(\grad f).

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
\alpha^{(n)},\beta^{(n+1)}\in\mathcal{H}
. We divide the proof into the following steps.

Step 1
. Suppose
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta

if and only if both of the followings are satisfied:

•

\alpha<\beta
;

•

f^{\prime}(\beta)\leq f^{\prime}(\alpha)

if and only if both of the followings are satisfied:

•

\alpha<\beta
;

•

f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha)

if and only if

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta.

Step 2
. Suppose
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then

\displaystyle R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=0

if and only if there does not exist any
\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that both of the followings are satisfied:

•

\alpha<\beta
;

•

f^{\prime}(\beta)\leq f^{\prime}(\alpha)

if and only if there does not exist any
\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that both of the followings are satisfied:

•

\alpha<\beta
;

•

f(\beta)\leq f(\alpha)

if and only if either of the followings is satisfied:

•

there does not exist any
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}
such that both of the followings are satisfied:

–

\alpha<\gamma
;

–

f(\gamma)\leq f(\alpha)

•

there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that both of the followings are satisfied:

–

\alpha<\gamma
;

–

f(\gamma)\leq f(\alpha)

if and only if either of the followings is satisfied:

•

R(\grad f)(\alpha)=0
;

•

there exists
\gamma\in\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
such that

R(\grad f)(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\gamma,\alpha\rangle\gamma
.

Step 3
. Suppose
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. Then either of the followings is satisfied:

•

R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=0
;

•

R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.

Step 4
. Suppose
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
.
We consider the following cases:

Case 1
.
R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=0
.

Subcase 1.1
.
R(\grad f)(\alpha)=0
.

Then

.

Subcase 1.2
.

.

Then by the last line in Step 2, we have that

\displaystyle R(\grad f)(\alpha)=-\langle\partial\gamma,\alpha\rangle\gamma

for some

. Since

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})(-\langle\partial\gamma,\alpha\rangle\gamma)=0,

it follows that

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\circ R(\grad f)(\alpha)=0=R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha).

Case 2
.
R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)\neq 0
.

Then by Step 3, we have that

for some
\beta\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
. By Step 1, we have

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\circ R(\grad f)(\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})(-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle\partial\beta,\alpha\rangle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha).

Summarizing the cases, it follows that for any
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
we always have

\displaystyle R(\grad f^{\prime})(\alpha)=\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})\circ R(\grad f)(\alpha).

Step 5
. By the last line in Step 4 as well as the linear property of
R(\grad f)
,
R(\grad f^{\prime})
and
\pi(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
, we obtain (
5.1
) and finish the proof.

5.2
(i), we have
\grad f=\grad g
. Thus
M(g,\mathcal{H})=M(f,\mathcal{H})
. By Proposition
5.2
(i) and (ii), we have

\displaystyle R(\overline{\grad f})(\alpha)=R(\grad f)(\alpha)

for
\alpha\in\mathcal{H}
and

\displaystyle R(\overline{\grad f})(\alpha)=0

for
\alpha\in\Delta\mathcal{H}\setminus\mathcal{H}
. Thus by Corollary
5.5
, we have
M(f,\mathcal{H})=M(\bar{f},\Delta\mathcal{H})\cap\mathcal{H}
. We finish the proof.
∎

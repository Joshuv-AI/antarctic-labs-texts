Euler and Betti curves are stable under Wasserstein deformations of distributions of stochastic processes
Daniel Perez

Abstract
Euler and Betti curves of stochastic processes defined on a d d -dimensional compact Riemannian manifold which are almost surely in a Sobolev space W n , s ​ ( X , ℝ ) W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R}) (with d < n d<n ) are stable under perturbations of the distributions of said processes in a Wasserstein metric. Moreover, Wasserstein stability is shown to hold for all p > d n p>\frac{d}{n} for persistence diagrams stemming from functions in W n , s ​ ( X , ℝ ) W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R}) .

Introduction

The study of random fields by Adler and Taylor using geometric and topological methods gave rise to the following celebrated and interesting result.

Theorem 1.1

(Adler, Taylor, Theorem 12.4.1
[
2
]
)
.

Let
f
be a centered unit variance Gaussian field on a
d
-dimensional
C^{2}
-submanifold
X
of a
C^{3}
manifold. Suppose
f
satisfies the conditions of
[
2
, Corollary 11.3.5]
(henceforth the GKF conditions), then,

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\chi(\{f\geq x\})\right]=\sum_{j=0}^{d}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(X)\,\rho_{j}(x)\,\,,

(1.1)

where
\chi
denotes the Euler characteristic and
\rho_{j}(x)
denotes some known functions of
x
and
{\mathcal{L}}_{j}
denote the Lipschitz-Killing curvatures of
X
.

In recent years, much work has been done to weaken the Gaussian assumption of the above theorem. A notable step in this direction is the following remarkable result by Bobrowski and Borman.

Theorem 1.2

(Bobrowski, Borman, Theorem 4.1,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact d-dimensional stratified space, and let
f:X\to\mathbb{R}^{k}
be a
k
-dimensional Gaussian random field satisfying the GKF
conditions. For a piecewise
C^{2}
-function
G:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and let
g=G\circ f
. Setting
D_{u}=G^{-1}\,]-\infty,u]
, we have

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\int_{X}g\,d\chi\right]=\chi(X)\mathbb{E}\!\left[g\right]-\sum_{j=1}^{d}(2\pi)^{j/2}{\mathcal{L}}_{j}(X)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{M}_{j}(D_{u})\;du\,,

(1.2)

where
\mathbb{E}\!\left[g\right]
has constant mean.

As pointed out by the authors of the above theorem in their original paper
[
3
]
, the difficulty in evaluating the expression above lies in computing the Minkowski functionals
\mathcal{M}_{j}(D_{u})
. In this paper, we aim to somewhat compensate this difficulty by providing a stability result regarding the above expectations, and moreover, the distributions of the Euler curves (
i.e.
the curves
x\mapsto\chi(\{f\geq x\}))
. More precisely, our main result is the following.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
X
be a compact
d
-dimensional manifold and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Consider two stochastic processes
f,g:\Omega\to W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
d<n
. Then, the Euler curves of
f
and
g
are well-defined as elements of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and as distributions, and for every
\frac{d}{n}<\alpha<1
, there exists a finite constant
C_{X,n,s}
such that

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\!\left[\chi(\{f\geq x\})\right]-\mathbb{E}\!\left[\chi(\{g\geq x\})\right]\right\rVert_{L^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C_{X,n,s}\left[\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,W^{n,s})}^{\alpha}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,W^{n,s})}^{\alpha}\right]W_{1,L^{\infty}}^{1-\alpha}(f_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},g_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\,,

(1.3)

where
W_{1,L^{\infty}}
denotes the Wasserstein distance on the space of probability measures on the space
(L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})\cap W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R}),\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})
. Moreover, the same statement holds for all Betti curves.

The key will be to adopt the point of view of persistent homology, for which we refer the unfamiliar reader to the now classical references on the subject for an introduction
[
5
,
10
]
. The relevance of the study of persistence diagrams was already understood by Bobrowski and Borman, and was later also elaborated upon by different authors
[
1
,
9
]
.

Parallel to this development, the following result was recently shown by Buhovsky
et al.

Theorem 1.4

(Buhovsky, Payette, Polterovich, Polterovich, Shelukhin, Stojisavljević,
[
4
]
)
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
and let
f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
and
n>\frac{d}{s}
. Then,
f
is continuous and for all
\varepsilon>0

N^{\varepsilon}_{H_{k}(X,f)}\leq C_{X,n,s}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{d/n}\;\varepsilon^{-d/n}+\dim H_{k}(X)\,,

(1.4)

where
N^{\varepsilon}_{H_{k}(X,f)}
denotes the number of bars of length
\geq\varepsilon
in the barcode of the persistent homology of degree
k
,
H_{k}(X,f)
.

A similar bound was shown in
[
11
]
for
\alpha
-Hölder functions and for a wider class of metric spaces. The results of this paper can be seen as direct consequences of the theory developped in
[
11
]
, but which deserve to be made explicit.

Remark 1.5
.

Using the Sobolev injection
W^{n,s}\xhookrightarrow{}C^{r+\alpha}

, we can rewrite the exponent as
\frac{d}{r+\alpha+\frac{d}{s}}

]

Unless otherwise specified, we shall always consider the (infinite) bars of the barcode to be truncated. That is,

(1.5)

Notation 1.10

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{2}

and define the distance from a point
(x,y)

.

sub

use the following notation for superlevel and sublevel sets respectively:

•

\mathcal{X}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,|\,x<y\}

\overline{\mathcal{X}}

2
Stability results

2.1
Preliminary results

Lemma 2.1
.

\Pers_{p}^{p}(H_{k}(X,f))=p\int_{0}^{\infty}\varepsilon^{p-1}N^{\varepsilon}_{H_{k}(X,f)}\;d\varepsilon\,.

(2.7)

Proof.

The length of a bar
b\in H_{k}(X,f)
can be written as

\ell(b)^{p}=p\int_{0}^{\infty}1_{[0,\ell(b)]}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{p-1}\;d\varepsilon\,.

(2.8)

The result follows by summing over all bars
b
in the barcode of
H_{k}(X,f)
and applying the Fubini theorem.
∎

A natural corollary of the above facts is that if
f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then we can bound the
\Pers_{p}^{p}
-functional of
H_{k}(X,f)
by a universal constant and
\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}
as soon as
p>\frac{d}{n}
.

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
, then for
p>\frac{d}{n}
, there exists a finite constant
C_{X,n,s}
such that

\Pers_{p}^{p}(H_{k}(X,f))\leq C_{X,n,s}\,\frac{pn}{d}\,\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{p}+(2\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty})^{p}\dim H_{k}(X)\,.

(2.9)

Remark 2.3
.

Corollary
2.2
means

f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})\implies\Dgm_{k}(f)\in\bigcap_{p>d/n}\mathcal{D}_{p}\,.

(2.10)

Remark 2.4
.

Examining the limiting case where
n\to\infty
and
s\to\infty
, one can wonder whether
C^{\infty}
-functions have finite
\Pers_{0}
(in other words, a finite number of bars). However, this is not the case as is shown by the example
e^{-1/x^{2}}\sin(1/x)
on
[0,1]
.

The answer of which is the optimal degree of regularity for which
\Pers_{0}
is finite is an interesting question. A sufficient condition found through discussions with François Petit is that the functions be subanalytic in an
o
-minimal structure. Once
\Pers_{0}
is finite, it follows that
\Pers_{p}^{p}
is a holomorphic function everywhere on
\mathbb{C}
.

On the other side of the story, we can ask whether the
C^{\alpha}
(up to reparametrization by a homeomorphism) condition is necessary to establish that
\Pers_{p}^{p}
is finite for some
p
. This question has been positively answered in
[
11
]
in the 1D case (in fact, the correct notion of regularity is the
p
-variation, but is equivalent to asking that the function be
C^{\alpha}
for some
\alpha
, up to reparametrization).

One can ask whether the bound
p>\frac{d}{n}
for the finiteness of
\Pers_{p}^{p}
is sharp within the degree of regularity considered. This question amounts to asking whether the asymptotics provided by theorem
1.4
are in some sense sharp. Indeed, note that lemma
2.1
entails

Proposition 2.5
.

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N^{\varepsilon}_{H_{k}(X,f)}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}=\inf\{p\,|\,\Pers_{p}(H_{k}(X,f))<\infty\}\,.

(2.11)

The sharpness of the asymptotics of
N^{\varepsilon}_{H_{k}(X,f)}
was already partially adressed in
[
4
, §1.5]
and positively answer the question. For
\alpha
-Hölder functions, this sharpness was already thoroughly adressed in
[
11
]
, where it was shown that

\inf\{p\,|\,\Pers_{p}(H_{k}(X,f))<\infty\}=\frac{d}{\alpha}\quad\text{generically in }C^{\alpha}\,.

(2.12)

This discussion suggests the following conjecture, which might be adressed in a later work.

Conjecture 2.6
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
, then

\inf\{p\,|\,\Pers_{p}(H_{k}(X,f))<\infty\}\leq\frac{d}{r+\alpha}

(2.13)

and this inequality is saturated generically in the sense of Baire in
C^{r+\alpha}
.

Remark 2.7
.

By the work performed in
[
11
]
, it suffices to exhibit a function
g
on the cube (or the ball), such that the bound is saturated. We obtain a dense family by perturbating any dense family to make it constant on a small open ball
B\subset X
and adding
g
to
f
on this ball. For
H_{0}
, such a function may be constructed by considering packings and smoothened versions of distances to point clouds. In higher degrees of homology, exhibiting explicit examples might be more difficult. Experience shows that it might be easier to find a random field which saturates the bound, as done in
[
11
]
.

2.2
Stability theorems

Having bounded
\Pers_{p}^{p}
, a stability result follows from the standard argument considered in
[
6
]
.

Theorem 2.8

(Wasserstein stability on compact Riemannian manifolds)
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
d
and let
f,g\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
. Then, for all
p>q>\frac{d}{n}
,

d_{p}^{p}(\Dgm_{k}(f),\Dgm_{k}(g))\leq C_{X,n,s}\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{q}\vee\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{q}\right)\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{\infty}^{p-q}\,.

(2.14)

Proof.

Following the proof of the stability theorem shown in
[
6
]
, it is sufficient to show that
\Pers_{q}(\Dgm_{k}(f))
and
\Pers_{q}(\Dgm_{k}(g))
can be bounded. This bound is provided by corollary
2.2
, yielding the result.
∎

Similarly, using
[
11
]
, this bound immediately entails a stochastic stability result.

Theorem 2.9

(Stochastic Wasserstein Stability)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two a.s.
W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
stochastic processes on a
d
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
X
, defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Then, for any
0\leq k<d
, every
\frac{d}{n}<q<p<\infty
and any
r,s\in\;]1,\infty[
satisfying
\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{s}=1
and
(p-q)s\geq 1
, there exists a constant
C=C_{X,n,s}
such that

\displaystyle W_{p,d_{p}}((\Dgm_{k}\circ f)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},(\Dgm_{k}\circ g)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{qr}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{qr}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}W_{(p-q)s,\infty}^{1-\frac{q}{p}}(f_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},g_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})

(2.15)

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{qr}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}+\mathbb{E}\!\left[\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{W^{n,s}}^{qr}\right]^{\frac{1}{r}}\right]^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\lVert f-g\right\rVert_{L^{(p-q)s}(\Omega,L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}))}^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\,.

(2.16)

Proof.

Here, we follow the proof of
[
11
, Theorem 5.9]
. The proof is the same, replacing the
C^{\alpha}
-seminorms by the Sobolev norms.
∎

Theorem 2.10

(Stochastic stability of representations,
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a Banach space and
\Psi:\mathcal{D}_{p}\to\mathcal{B}
be an
\alpha
-Hölder continuous functional. Let
\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q}\in\mathcal{P}_{\alpha q}(\mathcal{D}_{p})
, then

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{P}}[\Psi]-\mathbb{E}_{\mathbb{Q}}[\Psi]\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}\leq W_{q,\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\mathcal{B}}}(\Psi_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},\Psi_{\sharp}\mathbb{Q})\leq\left\lVert\Psi\right\rVert_{C^{\alpha}(\mathcal{D}_{p},\mathcal{B})}W_{q\alpha,d_{p}}^{\alpha}(\mathbb{P},\mathbb{Q})\,.

(2.17)

3
Euler and Betti curves

Degree-by-degree, we define the so-called Betti and Euler curves.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
n>\frac{d}{s}
. The
k
th degree Betti curve of
f
, denoted
\beta_{k}(x,f)
is defined as

\beta_{k}(f)(x):=\beta_{k}(f,x):=\Dgm_{k}(f)(R_{x})\,,

(3.18)

where
\Dgm_{k}(f)
is seen as a persistence measure. The
Euler curve of
f
is defined as

\chi(f)(x):=\chi(f,x):=\sum_{k=0}^{d}(-1)^{k}\Dgm_{k}(f)(R_{x})

(3.19)

Considered pointwise, showing a stability result for
\beta_{k}(f,x)
is hopeless. This follows from the fact that we may perturb
f
so that we displace the points in the diagram pushing them over the boundary of
R_{x}
, thereby introducing a perturbation in
\beta_{k}(f,x)
of a potentially infinite number of points.

The correct point of view is to allow ourselves to consider the
\beta_{k}(f,x)
as an element of
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, or more generally as a distribution.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a
d
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and let
f\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
for
d<n
. Then, for every
k
,
\beta_{k}(f),\chi(f)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and thus also in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Moreover,

\left\lVert\beta_{k}(f)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=\Pers_{1}(\Dgm_{k}(f))\,.

(3.20)

Remark 3.3
.

\beta_{k}(f)
can be infinite for some values of
x
. Nonetheless, the degree of regularity considered ensures that we can still make sense of average values of
\beta_{k}
around small neighbourhoods of a level
x
. This justifies that we look at the Betti and Euler curves as distributions.

Proof.

We do the proof for sublevel sets, noticing that the proof for superlevel sets is completely analogous. Since
\beta_{k}(f)
is a positive function,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\beta_{k}(f)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\beta_{k}(f,x)\;dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Dgm_{k}(f)(R_{x})\;dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}}1_{R_{x}}(z)\;d\Dgm(f)(z)\right]\;dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{R_{x}}(z)\;dx\right]\;d\Dgm(f)(z)\,.

For any
z=(z_{1},z_{2})\in\overline{\mathcal{X}}
,

1_{R_{x}}(z_{1},z_{2})=1_{[0,\infty[}(z_{2}-x)1_{[0,\infty[}(x-z_{1})\,.

(3.21)

Using the translation invariance of the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
, we get

\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{R_{x}}(z_{1},z_{2})\;dx=z_{2}-z_{1}\,.

(3.22)

This is nothing other than the distance
d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}
from
(z_{1},z_{2})
to the diagonal, so

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}}(z_{2}-z_{1})\;d\Dgm(f)(z_{1},z_{2})=\Pers_{1}(\Dgm_{k}(f))<\infty\,,

(3.23)

as soon as
\frac{d}{n}<1
by virtue of corollary
2.2
.
∎

Corollary 3.4

(No stability for
d_{p}
-stability for
p>1
)
.

Let
p>1
and consider
\beta_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{p}\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. Then,
\beta_{k}
is discontinuous.

Remark 3.5
.

This suggests that
\chi:\mathcal{D}_{p}\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is discontinuous as well.

Proof.

We give two different arguments which prove the statement. First, consider a diagram
D\notin\mathcal{D}_{1}
such that
D\in\mathcal{D}_{p}
and any other diagram
D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}\cap\mathcal{D}_{p}
. Then,
d_{p}(D,D^{\prime})<\infty
, but the reverse triangle inequality entails

\left\lVert\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D)\right\rVert-\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D^{\prime})\right\rVert\right\rVert_{L^{1}}\leq\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D)-\beta_{k}(D^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{1}}=\infty\,,

(3.24)

since the lower bound is infinite.
Second, suppose that
\beta_{k}
is continuous. Then the map
\mathcal{D}_{p}\to\mathbb{R}
, given by
D\mapsto\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D)\right\rVert_{L^{1}}
must be continuous by continuity of the norm. However, by
[
8
, Proposition 5.1]
this map cannot be continuous, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}}1_{R_{x}}(z_{1},z_{2})\;dx=z_{2}-z_{1}

(3.25)

is linear in the distance to the diagonal, and in particular not
O(\left\lvert z_{2}-z_{1}\right\rvert^{p})
as would be required for continuity with respect to
d_{p}
.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

Divol and Lacombe’s work
[
8
, Proposition 5.1]
also explain why it is necessary – even within
\mathcal{D}_{1}
– to regard the Betti and Euler curves as distributions. Indeed, notice that pointwise in
x
,
1_{R_{x}}
doesn’t decrease to
0
at the required rate.

Corollary 3.7

(
d_{1}
-stability of Betti and Euler curves)
.

Let
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
, then

\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D)-\beta_{k}(D^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\,d_{1}(D,D^{\prime})\,.

(3.26)

Remark 3.8
.

This shows that
\beta_{k}:\mathcal{D}_{1}\to L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a
2
-Lipschitz representation.

Proof.

Consider a transport map
\pi\in\Gamma(D,D^{\prime})
. Then,

\displaystyle\left\lVert\beta_{k}(D)-\beta_{k}(D^{\prime})\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert 1_{R_{x}}(z)-1_{R_{x}}(w)\right\rvert\;dx\right]\;d\pi(z,w)\,.

We carefully check that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left\lvert 1_{R_{x}}(z)-1_{R_{x}}(w)\right\rvert\;dx\leq 2\,d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}(z,w)\,,

(3.27)

which entails the result by taking
\pi
to be an optimal transport for
d_{1}
.
∎

Proposition 3.9

(Interpolation for optimal transport)
.

Let
0<p<q\leq\infty
and
\theta\in\,]0,1[
. Define
p_{\theta}
by

\frac{1}{p_{\theta}}=\frac{\theta}{p}+\frac{1-\theta}{q}\,.

(3.28)

Then, for
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{p}\cap\mathcal{D}_{q}

\displaystyle d_{p_{\theta}}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1-\theta}\;d_{p}^{\theta}(\mu,\nu)\,(\Pers_{q}(\mu)+\Pers_{q}(\nu))^{1-\theta}

(3.29)

\displaystyle d_{p_{\theta}}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{\theta}\;d_{q}(\mu,\nu)^{1-\theta}\,(\Pers_{p}(\mu)+\Pers_{p}(\nu))^{\theta}\,.

(3.30)

Consequently, if
p\leq r\leq q
, then
\mathcal{D}_{p}\cap\mathcal{D}_{q}\subset\mathcal{D}_{r}
.

Proof.

We prove the first inequality, the second one following by analogy, by interverting the roles of
p
and
q
. Let
\pi
be an optimal transport for
d_{p}
. Applying Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle d_{p_{\theta}}(\mu,\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}\right\rVert_{L^{p_{\theta}}(\pi)}\leq\left\lVert d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\pi)}^{\theta}\left\lVert d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}\right\rVert_{L^{q}(\pi)}^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle=d_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{\theta}\left[\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}^{q}(z,z^{\prime})\;d\pi(z,z^{\prime})\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq d_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{\theta}\left[2^{q}\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{2}}d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}^{q}(z,\Delta)+d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}^{q}(\Delta,z^{\prime})\;d\pi(z,z^{\prime})\right]^{\frac{1-\theta}{q}}\,,

where the inequality on the last line holds everywhere on the support of
\pi
. This can be shown by defining

S=\{(z,z^{\prime})\in\overline{\mathcal{X}}^{2}\cap\supp(\pi)\,|\,d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}(z,z^{\prime})>d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}(z,\Delta)+d_{\mathbb{R}^{2},\infty}(z^{\prime},\Delta)\}\,.

(3.31)

This set
S
either has null or positive measure. If it has positive measure, then we can modify the transport plan
\pi
by sending the projections of
S
to the diagonal, thereby producing a transport plan of strictly inferior cost to that of
\pi
, which is a contradiction. Hence,
S
is of null measure, so the equality holds over the support of the measure. This entails

\displaystyle d_{p_{\theta}}(\mu,\nu)\leq 2^{1-\theta}\;d_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{\theta}\,(\Pers_{q}(\mu)+\Pers_{q}(\nu))^{1-\theta}\,.

If
q=\infty
, since
\pi
is an optimal transport between
\mu
and
\nu
and
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{D}_{\infty}
,
\pi
itself must have compact support and the diameter of the support is bounded above by
\Pers_{\infty}(\mu)\vee\Pers_{\infty}(\nu)
, so the inequality of the proposition follows.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

Taking
0<p<1\leq q

3.9
allows us to say that, despite

Theorem 3.11

(Stability of Euler and Betti curves under perturbations in distribution)
.

Let
f
and
g
be two stochastic processes on a
d
-dimensional compact Riemannian manifold
X
, defined on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
such that almost surely
f,g\in W^{n,s}(X,\mathbb{R})
with
\frac{d}{n}<1
. Then, for every
\frac{d}{n}<\alpha<1
, there exists a constant
C=C_{X,n,s}
such that,

W_{1,L^{1}}(\beta_{k}(f)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},\beta_{k}(g)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\leq C\left[\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,W^{n,s})}^{\alpha}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\Omega,W^{n,s})}^{\alpha}\right]W_{1,L^{\infty}}^{1-\alpha}(f_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},g_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\,,

(3.32)

and a similar inequality holds by replacing the Betti curves with the Euler curves
\chi
.

Proof.

Recognizing that
\beta_{k}
are Lipschitz representations, it is a simple application of the stochastic stability theorem and its analog for representations, where we set
q=\alpha
,
p=1
and
s=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}
and
r=\frac{1}{\alpha}
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

Using the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality, a lower bound of
W_{1,L^{1}}
can be easily established. Recall that
[
12
, Particular case 5.16]

\sup_{\psi\in\Lip_{1}(L^{1}(\mathbb{R}),\mathbb{R})}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\psi(\chi(f))-\psi(\chi(g))\right]\leq W_{1,L^{1}}(\chi(f)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},\chi(g)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\,.

(3.33)

This point of view further justifies our view of
\beta_{k}
and
\chi
as distributions. Indeed, for
\varphi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R})
, the map

\psi:\chi\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}\chi(x)\varphi(x)dx\,,

(3.34)

is clearly Lipschitz. This distributional view of the Euler and Betti curves is (unsurprisingly) also stable with respect to perturbations in the distributions of the random fields from which they stem from.

A particularly interesting family of Lipschitz functionals (in light of recent developments
[
9
]
) to consider is

\psi_{\theta}:\chi\mapsto\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-ix\theta}\chi(x)\;dx\,.

(3.35)

Using the Kantorovich-Rubenstein duality on the real and imaginary components of the above integral separately, and taking the supremum over all
\theta
we arrive that the conclusion that

\left\lVert\mathbb{E}\!\left[\mathcal{F}\chi(f)-\mathcal{F}\chi(g))\right]\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\sqrt{2}\,W_{1,L^{1}}(\chi(f)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P},\chi(g)_{\sharp}\mathbb{P})\,,

(3.36)

where
\mathcal{F}
denotes the Fourier transform. Interestingly, one can apply the same reasoning for different integral transforms, provided that their kernel is such that the transform is Lipschitz on
L^{1}
. Of course, these arguments are all also valid for the Betti curves as well. Applying the stability of the Euler and Betti curves under perturbations in the distributions of
f
and
g
, we arrive at the conclusion that these transforms are stable with respect to changes in distribution.

Remark 3.13
.

Looking at Euler curves, it is possible to recover the Euler characteristic of level sets of
f
. Since for every
\varepsilon>0
,
\{f>x-\varepsilon\}\cap\{f<x+\varepsilon\}=\{x-\varepsilon<f<x+\varepsilon\}
and that
\{f>x-\varepsilon\}\cup\{f<x+\varepsilon\}=X
, by the valuation property of the Euler characteristic, we recover

\chi(\{x-\varepsilon<f<x+\varepsilon\})+\chi(X)=\chi(f,x-\varepsilon)+\chi(-f,-x-\varepsilon)\,,

(3.37)

by taking
\varepsilon\to 0
, we obtain
\chi(f=x)
, whenever it is defined.

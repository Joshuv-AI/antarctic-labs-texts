Legendrian links and déjà vu moments Thanks: This work was partially supported by the SFB TRR 191 Symplectic Structures in Geometry, Algebra and Dynamics , funded by the DFG (Projektnummer 281071066 – TRR 191).
Stefan Nemirovski

Abstract
. A Legendrian link is called a déjà vu link if its components can be connected by a positive Legendrian isotopy but this isotopy cannot be embedded. This is the contact geometric analogue of a pair of events in a spacetime such that there are déjà vu moments on every future-directed timelike path between them. We construct déjà vu links in several geometrically relevant situations and discuss their basic properties.



1.
Introduction and overview

Let
(Y,\xi)
be a contact manifold with a co-oriented contact structure.
An isotopy of Legendrian submanifolds
\iota:L\times[0,1]\to Y

is said to be
embedded
if the map
\iota
is an embedding
and
positive
if the trajectories of points on
L

are positively transverse to the co-oriented contact distribution,
see §
2.1
for a discussion of different types of
Legendrian isotopies. Every positive isotopy is embedded locally.
A basic example of a positive isotopy is given by the action of the Reeb flow
of a contact form defining
\xi
. This isotopy is embedded if
there are no Reeb chords of action
\leq 1
for
L_{0}=\iota(L\times\{0\})
.

For the purposes of this paper, a
Legendrian link
is
an ordered pair
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
of disjoint closed
connected Legendrian submanifolds in
Y
.

Definition 1.1
.

A Legendrian link
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
is called a
déjà vu link
if
it satisfies the following two conditions:

(P)

There is a positive isotopy connecting
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
.

(DjV)

There is
no
embedded positive isotopy connecting
\Lambda_{1}
to
\Lambda_{2}
.

The definition and terminology are motivated by the connection with
Lorentz geometry recalled in §
4.1
. The
sky

(or
celestial sphere
) of a point in a reasonable spacetime
\mathcal{X}

is a Legendrian sphere in the contact manifold of light rays of
\mathcal{X}
.
Future-directed timelike curves in
\mathcal{X}
induce positive Legendrian
isotopies of skies. If the skies
of two points in
\mathcal{X}
form a déjà vu link, then every future-directed
timelike curve connecting these points contains
déjà vu moments
,
that is, pairs of distinct points lying on the same light ray,
see §
4.2
.

If the Legendrian isotopy class of the components
\Lambda_{1}
and
\Lambda_{2}
is fixed,
there is only one Legendrian isotopy class of
non
déjà vu links satisfying
condition (P) by Corollary
2.13
. Legendrian links satisfying (P)
and not smoothly isotopic to links in this ‘primitive’ class are clearly déjà vu.
Proposition
3.3
shows that this observation can lead to
a topological description of déjà vu links in certain cases
but being déjà vu is not a topological property in general,
see Example
3.6
.

The components of a déjà vu link may sometimes be connected
by an embedded Legendrian isotopy which is not positive, see §§
3.1

and
3.2
. It seems unlikely, however, that this could happen
if the Legendrian isotopy class of the components of the link
is orderable. A partial result in this direction is obtained
in Proposition
2.14
based on a partial answer
to Question
2.4
.

Déjà vu links with components in the most basic orderable Legendrian
isotopy class of the zero section of a one-jet bundle are constructed
in §
3.4
using generating function methods recalled briefly
in §
2.2
. It is a simple example of a problem in which one must use
the ‘non-persistent’ finite bars of the barcode of a generating
function rather than the infinite bars and the associated spectral
invariants.

2.
Legendrian submanifolds

2.1.
Legendrian isotopies

A parametrised Legendrian isotopy in a contact manifold
(Y,\xi)

is a smooth map

\iota:L\times[0,1]\longrightarrow Y

such that
\iota|_{L\times\{t\}}:L\times\{t\}\hookrightarrow Y
is an embedding
and the submanifold
\Lambda_{t}=\iota(L\times\{t\})
is Legendrian for all
t\in[0,1]
.
Two parametrised isotopies are equivalent if they differ by
a diffeomorphism
\varphi
of
L\times[0,1]
such that
\varphi(L\times\{t\})=L\times\{t\}

for all
t\in[0,1]
. A Legendrian isotopy is an equivalence class of parametrised
Legendrian isotopies.

By the Legendrian isotopy extension theorem
[
16
, Theorem 2.6.2]
,
a Legendrian isotopy of closed Legendrian submanifolds can be extended
to a compactly supported contact isotopy of the ambient contact
manifold. In particular, Legendrian isotopic Legendrian links are ambiently
contactomorphic.

Let us assume henceforth that Legendrian submanifolds are closed
(i.e. compact and without boundary) and connected.

A Legendrian isotopy parametrised by
\iota:L\times[0,1]\longrightarrow Y

in a co-oriented contact manifold
(Y,\xi=\ker\alpha)
is called

non-negative
if
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})\geq 0
on
L\times[0,1]
.
If the inequality is everywhere strict, the isotopy is called
positive
.
Both properties do not depend on the parametrisation and on the choice
of a contact form defining the co-oriented contact structure;
they are also invariant under (co-orientation preserving) contactomorphisms.

We write
\Lambda\preccurlyeq\Lambda^{\prime}
if there is a non-negative Legendrian isotopy
from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
and
\Lambda\llcurly\Lambda^{\prime}
if
there is a positive one. The relation
\preccurlyeq
is clearly reflexive and transitive;
if it is also antisymmetric on a Legendrian isotopy class
\mathcal{L}
,
then this class is called
orderable
. By
[
7
, Proposition 4.7]
,

\mathcal{L}
is orderable if and only if it does not contain a positive
Legendrian loop, i.e. if and only if
\llcurly
is
not
reflexive on
\mathcal{L}
.

A Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is called
\preccurlyeq
-
monotone

if
\Lambda_{t_{1}}\preccurlyeq\Lambda_{t_{2}}
for all
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

A Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
such that
\Lambda_{0}\preccurlyeq\Lambda_{1}

and
\Lambda_{t_{1}}\cap\Lambda_{t_{2}}=\varnothing
for all
t_{1}\neq t_{2}

is
\preccurlyeq
-monotone.

Proof.

The Legendrian links
(\Lambda_{t_{1}},\Lambda_{t_{2}})
are Legendrian
isotopic and hence contactomorphic for all
0\leq t_{1}<t_{2}\leq 1
.
The result follows because
\preccurlyeq
is preserved by contactomorphisms.
∎

Remark 2.2
.

Recall from
[
6
, Lemma 2.2]
that
\llcurly
is equivalent to
\preccurlyeq
for disjoint
\Lambda_{t}
’s.
Hence, the isotopy in the lemma is in fact
\llcurly
-monotone.

A non-negative isotopy is obviously
\preccurlyeq
-monotone but the converse
is not true in general.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a
non
orderable Legendrian isotopy class
(e.g. any class containing a
loose
Legendrian
[
21
]

or any class in a contact manifold admitting a periodic Reeb flow).
Then there is a positive Legendrian loop based at every
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}
,
which implies that
\Lambda\llcurly\Lambda^{\prime}

for every
\Lambda^{\prime}
sufficiently
C^{1}
-close to
\Lambda
,
cf.
[
6
, Proof of Corollary 8.1]
. If now
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}

is any Legendrian isotopy in
\mathcal{L}
such that
\Lambda_{t}
are pairwise
disjoint, then it is
\llcurly
-monotone by the proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Question 2.4
.

Are
\preccurlyeq
-monotone isotopies non-negative in every
orderable
Legendrian isotopy class?

For the Legendrian isotopy class of the zero section of the
1
-jet bundle
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

of a closed manifold
L
, the positive answer to the above question
follows easily from
[
5
, Corollary 5.4]
,
which is essentially equivalent to the orderability
of that class
[
5
, Corollary 5.5]
. There is
another case in which we are now going to show that
\preccurlyeq
-monotone
Legendrian isotopies are non-negative.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be an orderable Legendrian isotopy class of spheres.
A
\preccurlyeq
-monotone Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}\subset\mathcal{L}

is non-negative.

Proof.

Suppose that the isotopy is not non-negative at some
\tau\in[0,1]
and denote
\Lambda=\Lambda_{\tau}
.
Fix a contactomorphism
\Psi
from a neighbourhood
U\supset\Lambda
to a tubular neighbourhood of the zero section
in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\Lambda)
mapping
\Lambda
onto the zero section
\mathrm{O}
, see
[
16
, Example 2.5.11]
.
For
t
close enough to
\tau
, the Legendrian

\Lambda_{t}
corresponds to the graph of the
1
-jet of a smooth function
f_{t}:\Lambda\to\mathbb{R}
.
We may now assume that
f_{t}
is
C^{1}
-small and negative somewhere on
\Lambda

but
\Lambda\preccurlyeq\Lambda_{t}=\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(f_{t}))
.

Let
F
be a smooth function on
\Lambda
such that

a)

F\geq f_{t}
;

b)

\{F<0\}
is a ball in
\Lambda
;

c)

zero is not a critical value of
F
(i.e.
j^{1}(F)\cap\mathrm{O}=\varnothing
);

d)

j^{1}(F)\subset\Psi(U)
.

F
may be defined as a regularised maximum (see
[
13
, Lemma I.5.18]
)
of
f_{t}
and a
C^{1}
-small function
\varphi
such that
\{\varphi\leq 0\}
is a closed ball
contained in
\{f_{t}<0\}\neq\varnothing
.

Property (a) implies that
\Lambda\preccurlyeq\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(f_{t}))\preccurlyeq\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(F))
.
The space of functions satisfying (b)–(d) is connected for any
\Lambda
.
If
\Lambda\cong S^{n}
, the function
-F
satisfies (b)–(d) too.
Hence, the links
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(F))
and
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(-F))

are Legendrian isotopic in
\Psi(U)
. The latter link is Legendrian
isotopic to
(j^{1}(F),\mathrm{O})
via the ‘shift’ contact isotopy

(q,p,u)\mapsto\left(q,p+s\tfrac{\partial F}{\partial q},u+sF(q)\right),\quad s\in[0,1].

Thus,
(\Lambda,\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(F)))
is Legendrian isotopic to
(\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(F)),\Lambda)

and therefore we have both
\Lambda\preccurlyeq\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(F))
and
\Psi^{-1}(j^{1}(F))\preccurlyeq\Lambda
,
which contradicts the assumption that
\mathcal{L}
is orderable.
∎

Remark 2.6

(Irreversible Legendrian links)
.

The key point in the above proof will not work
if
\Lambda
is not a homology sphere, i.e. if there
exists a non-zero homology class
\beta\in\mathsf{H}_{k}(\Lambda;\mathbbm{k})

of degree
k\neq 0,\dim\Lambda
. Namely, the links

(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(F))
and
(j^{1}(F),\mathrm{O})

will
not
be Legendrian isotopic in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\Lambda)

for any function
F
satisfying (b) and (c).
To see this, observe that if
S_{F}
is any
quadratic at infinity generating function for
j^{1}(F)

(see §
2.2
), then
c_{\beta}(S_{F})=c_{\beta}(F)>0

and
c_{\beta}(-S_{F})=c_{\beta}(-F)<0
, where
c_{\beta}
is the spectral invariant
defined in Remark
2.19
. Hence, one can apply
Traynor’s argument from
[
30
, §5]

with
c_{\beta}
instead of
c_{+}=c_{[\Lambda]}
.

Remark 2.7

(Lorentzian comparison)
.

The statement analogous to Lemma
2.5
in Lorentz geometry
(in the sense explained in §
4.1
) asserts that a causally
monotone curve in a
distinguishing
spacetime is future-directed,
see e.g.
[
24
, Proposition 3.19]
. Note that orderability
is formally analogous to causality, so the Lorentzian statement
requires a stronger assumption.

A Legendrian isotopy parametrised by
\iota:L\times[0,1]\longrightarrow Y

is called
immersed
or
embedded
if the map
\iota
is an immersion
or an embedding. Clearly, an immersed isotopy is embedded if and only if
\iota

is injective. These notions are obviously independent of the choice of
a parametrisation. The property of a Legendrian isotopy to be
immersed

can also be expressed in terms of the pull-back of a contact form.

Lemma 2.8
.

A Legendrian isotopy
\iota:L\times[0,1]\to(Y,\xi=\ker\alpha)

is
not
immersed at a point
(q,\tau)
if and only if
(q,\tau)
is a
critical point of the function
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})

restricted to
L\times\{\tau\}
with critical value zero.

Proof.

The condition
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})=0
is equivalent to
\iota_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\in\xi_{\iota(q,\tau)}
.
The point
(q,\tau)
is critical for
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})
on
L\times\{\tau\}
if and only if

X\left(\iota^{*}\alpha(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t})\right)=0

at
(q,\tau)
for all
vertical
vector fields
X
on
L\times[0,1]
.
For a vertical
X
, the commutator
[X,\frac{\partial}{\partial t}]

is also vertical and
\iota^{*}\alpha(X)=\alpha(\iota_{*}X)=0
. Hence,

\begin{array}[]{rcl}X\left(\iota^{*}\alpha(\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t})\right)&=&d(\iota^{*}\alpha)\left(X,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)+\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}(\iota^{*}\alpha(X))+\iota^{*}\alpha\left([X,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}]\right)\\[2.0pt]
&=&d(\iota^{*}\alpha)\left(X,\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)\\[2.0pt]
&=&d\alpha\left(\iota_{*}X,\iota_{*}\tfrac{\partial}{\partial t}\right).\end{array}

Thus, our assumptions are equivalent to
\iota_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

being skew-orthogonal to the Lagrangian subspace
\iota_{*}(T_{q}L)
in the symplectic
vector space
(\xi_{\iota(q,\tau)},d\alpha)
. This means precisely
that
\iota_{*}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\in\iota_{*}(T_{q}L)
and the rank
of
\iota
is not maximal at
(q,\tau)
.
∎

As an application, we show that being immersed characterises positive
Legendrian isotopies among non-negative ones.

Corollary 2.9
.

A non-negative Legendrian isotopy is positive if and only if it is immersed.

Proof.

A positive Legendrian isotopy is obviously immersed. To prove the ‘if’ part,
let
\iota:L\times[0,1]\to(Y,\xi=\ker\alpha)
be a parametrisation
of an immersed non-negative isotopy. Suppose that the isotopy isn’t positive.
Then
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})=0

at some point
(q,\tau)\in L\times[0,1]
.
Since
\iota^{*}\alpha(\frac{\partial}{\partial t})\geq 0
, it follows that this function
attains its minimum equal to zero at
(q,\tau)
. Hence, the isotopy
is not immersed at that point by Lemma
2.8
.
∎

Remark 2.10
.

A related argument may be found in
[
17
, Lemma 4.12(i)]
.

The existence of an embedded isotopy between two Legendrians
may be inferred from a seemingly weaker assumption.

Lemma 2.11
.

If
\Lambda_{0}\cap\Lambda_{t}=\varnothing
for all
t>0

in a Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
,
then
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
are connected
by an embedded Legendrian isotopy.
If
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
is also positive at
t=0
,
then
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{1}
are connected
by a positive embedded Legendrian isotopy.

Proof.

If we identify a neighbourhood
of
\Lambda_{0}
with a neighbourhood of the zero section
in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\Lambda_{0})
, then
\Lambda_{\tau}
for small enough
\tau>0

corresponds to
j^{1}(f_{\tau})
for a smooth function
f_{\tau}
on
\Lambda_{0}

such that zero is not its critical value. Hence, an embedded
isotopy connecting
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{\tau}
can be defined
by
\{j^{1}(sf_{\tau})\}_{s\in[0,1]}
. If the given isotopy
is positive at
t=0
, then it is positive and embedded on
[0,\tau]

for small enough
\tau>0
.
(This corresponds to
f_{\tau^{\prime}}>f_{\tau^{\prime\prime}}>0
on
\Lambda_{0}
for
\tau\geq\tau^{\prime}>\tau^{\prime\prime}>0
.)
It remains to observe that
\Lambda_{\tau}
is Legendrian isotopic
to
\Lambda_{1}
in the complement of
\Lambda_{0}
and therefore
there exists a contact isotopy
\{\varphi_{t}\}_{t\in[\tau,1]}

such that
\Lambda_{0}\cap\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\varphi_{t}=\varnothing

and
\varphi_{t}(\Lambda_{\tau})=\Lambda_{t}
. Applying
\varphi_{1}

to the Legendrian isotopies from
\Lambda_{0}
to
\Lambda_{\tau}

constructed above completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.12
.

The embedded isotopy obtained in the lemma is homotopic
to the original Legendrian isotopy through Legendrian
isotopies. If the original isotopy is positive,
this homotopy is through positive isotopies.

Since the space of positive functions on a manifold is
connected, the proof of Lemma
2.11
implies
the following result.

Corollary 2.13
.

All
non
déjà vu Legendrian links
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})

with
\Lambda\llcurly\Lambda^{\prime}
and a given
\Lambda

are Legendrian isotopic by an isotopy fixing
\Lambda
.

It follows that the Legendrian isotopy class of a non déjà vu link
with
\Lambda\llcurly\Lambda^{\prime}
is completely determined by the Legendrian
isotopy class of its components. One way of representing this class
of links is to take the link formed by a Legendrian and its sufficiently small
shift along the Reeb flow of a contact form.

Proposition 2.14
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}
is either the Legendrian isotopy class
of the zero section in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
or an orderable Legendrian
isotopy class of spheres. Let
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})
be
a Legendrian link with components in
\mathcal{L}
.

(i)

If
\Lambda\llcurly\Lambda^{\prime}
, then every embedded isotopy
from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
is positive.

(ii)

If
(\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime})
is a déjà vu Legendrian link,
then for every Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
with

\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda
and
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda^{\prime}
there exists
a
t_{0}>0
such that
\Lambda\cap\Lambda_{t_{0}}\neq\varnothing

and a
t_{1}<1
such that
\Lambda^{\prime}\cap\Lambda_{t_{1}}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

(i) An embedded Legendrian isotopy from
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
is
\preccurlyeq
-monotone by Lemma
2.1
.
Any
\preccurlyeq
-monotone isotopy in
\mathcal{L}
is non-negative
by Lemma
2.5
and the discussion preceding it.
Finally, every embedded non-negative isotopy is positive by Corollary
2.9
.

(ii) Assume that there is a Legendrian isotopy connecting
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}

which is disjoint from
\Lambda
for all
t>0
or
from
\Lambda^{\prime}
for all
t<1
. Then by Lemma
2.11

there is an embedded isotopy connecting
\Lambda
to
\Lambda^{\prime}
.
Applying (i) we obtain a contradiction with the definition of a déjà vu link.
∎

Remark 2.15
.

Proposition
2.14
holds true for every Legendrian isotopy class
in which
\preccurlyeq
-monotone isotopies are non-negative. So it will hold for every
orderable Legendrian isotopy class if Question
2.4
can be
answered in the positive.

Remark 2.16
.

A version of Proposition
2.14

can be formulated for
universally orderable

Legendrian isotopy classes, see
[
7
, §4.3]
. Namely,
one has to assume that the Legendrian isotopies in (i) and (ii)
are
a priori
homotopic through Legendrian isotopies
to positive isotopies. A careful inspection of the proofs in this section
shows that the modified statement is true for a universally orderable
class of Legendrian spheres. On the other hand, the result can be false
without this additional assumption, see §
3.1
and §
3.2
.

2.2.
Quadratic at infinity functions and Legendrians

Let
L
be a closed connected manifold. A smooth function

S=S(q,\xi):L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

is said to be
quadratic at infinity
if

S(q,\xi)=\sigma(q,\xi)+Q(\xi),

where
\sigma
has compact support in
L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}

and
Q
is a non-degenerate quadratic
form on
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{\xi}
. Denote by

S^{c}:=\{(q,\xi)\in L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\mid S(q,\xi)<c\}

the sublevel sets of
S
and let
S^{-\infty}
be the set
S^{c}

for a sufficiently negative
c\ll 0
. The following standard lemma
is an immediate consequence of the isotopy extension theorem.

Lemma 2.17
.

Let
S_{t}=\sigma_{t}+Q
,
t\in[0,1]
be a smooth family of quadratic at infinity
functions. Suppose that
a\in\mathbb{R}
is not a critical value of
S_{t}
for all
t
.
Then the inclusions
\imath^{a}_{t}:S^{a}_{t}\hookrightarrow L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
are
isotopic by a compactly supported isotopy constant on
S_{t}^{-\infty}
.
In particular, the relative homology groups
\mathsf{H}_{*}(S_{t}^{a},S_{t}^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})

are isomorphic for all
t
and the induced homomorphisms

\left(\imath^{a}_{t}\right)_{*}:\mathsf{H}_{*}(S_{t}^{a},S_{t}^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})\longrightarrow\mathsf{H}_{*}(L\times\mathbb{R}^{N},S_{t}^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})

do not depend on
t
.

Remark 2.18

(Persistence and barcodes)
.

If
S
is a Morse function (i.e. its critical points are non-degenerate),
then the relative homology groups
\mathsf{H}_{*}(S^{c},S^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
together with the homomorphisms induced by the inclusions

\imath^{c,c^{\prime}}:S^{c}\hookrightarrow S^{c^{\prime}}
for
c\leq c^{\prime}

form a
persistence module
in the sense of
[
27
, Definition 1.1.1]
.
Lemma
2.17
implies that if two quadratic at infinity Morse functions
can be connected by a family such that
a\in\mathbb{R}
is never a critical value, then
in the
barcodes
associated to their persistence modules
by
[
27
, Theorem 2.1.1]
the number of bars containing
a
is the same.

Remark 2.19

(Spectral invariants)
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{N}=V_{+}\times V_{-}
be a decomposition into linear subspaces such that
Q

is positive definite on
V_{+}
and negative definite on
V_{-}
. The dimension

\nu=\dim V_{-}
is the (negative) index of
Q
. The map

\mathsf{H}_{*}(L;\mathbbm{k})\ni\beta\longmapsto\beta\times[V_{-}]\in\mathsf{H}_{*+\nu}(L\times\mathbb{R}^{N},S^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})

is an isomorphism between the homology of
L
and the shifted homology
of
L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
relative to
S^{-\infty}
. Following Viterbo
[
31
, §2]
,
one can therefore associate critical values of
S
to homology classes on
L
.
Namely, for
\beta\in\mathsf{H}_{k}(L;\mathbbm{k})
, define

c_{\beta}(S):=\inf\bigl\{c\in\mathbb{R}\mid\beta\times[V_{-}]\in\left(\imath^{c}\right)_{*}\mathsf{H}_{k+\nu}(S^{c},S^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})\bigr\},

where
\imath^{c}:S^{c}\to L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the inclusion. This is a critical value of
S

by basic Morse theory. Moreover,
c_{\beta}(S_{t})
is a continuous function of
t

for any smooth family of quadratic at infinity functions, cf.
[
31
, Proposition 2.5]
.
In the barcode associated to
S
, the numbers
c_{\beta}(S)
are precisely the endpoints
of the
infinite
bars.

Let
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
denote the
1
-jet bundle of
L
equipped with
the standard contact form
du-\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}
,
where
u
is the fibre coordinate in
{\mathcal{J}}^{0}(L)
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}=p\,dq

is the Liouville form on
T^{*}L
.

Definition 2.20
.

A smooth function

S=S(q,\xi):L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

is a
generating function
for a Legendrian submanifold
\Lambda\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

if zero is a regular value of the partial differential
d_{\xi}S
and the map

\{d_{\xi}S(q,\xi)=0\}\ni(q,\xi)\longmapsto(q,d_{q}S(q,\xi),S(q,\xi))\in{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

(2.1)

is a diffeomorphism onto
\Lambda
.

Note that (Morse) critical points of
S

as a function of both variables
(q,\xi)
are in one-to-one
correspondence with (transverse) intersection points of
\Lambda

with
\{p=0\}\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
. In particular, the intersection
of
\Lambda
with the zero section is parametrised by the
critical points of
S
with critical value zero.

By Chekanov’s theorem
[
4
]
, for any Legendrian isotopy

\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
such that
\Lambda_{0}
is the zero section
\mathrm{O}\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
,
there exists a smooth family of
quadratic at infinity

generating functions

S_{t}=\sigma_{t}+Q:L\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}

for
\Lambda_{t}
with
\sigma_{0}\equiv 0
.
Furthermore, this family is unique up to stabilisations and fibrewise
diffeomorphisms by the Viterbo–Théret theorem
[
31
,
28
,
29
]
.

3.
Examples of déjà vu links

3.1.
Déjà vu
versus
Wiedersehen

Let
M
be a connected manifold,
\dim M\geq 2
,
and let
ST^{*}M
be its co-sphere bundle with the canonical contact structure.
The fibres of
ST^{*}M
are Legendrian spheres and the
links formed by any two of them are Legendrian isotopic.

The link of two fibres of
ST^{*}M
may satisfy condition (P) for some
M
. Suppose, for instance, that there
is a Riemannian metric on
M
making it into a
Y^{x}_{\ell}
-manifold,
i.e. such that all unit speed geodesics from
x\in M
return to
x

at time
\ell>0
.
(Examples of
Y^{x}_{\ell}
-manifolds include compact rank one symmetric spaces and
certain exotic spheres, see
[
3
, §7.C]
.)
The co-geodesic flow of this metric defines a positive
Legendrian loop based at the fibre
ST_{x}^{*}M
.
Hence, there is a positive Legendrian isotopy between
(any) two fibres, see
[
6
, §8]
.

In the special case when
M
is diffeomorphic to the sphere,
the link of two fibres satisfies (P) but is
not

déjà vu. Indeed, any two points
x\neq y
on
M

are antipodal for the pull-back of the
standard round metric by some diffeomorphism.
The co-geodesic flow of this metric defines an

embedded
positive Legendrian isotopy
from
ST_{x}^{*}M
to
ST_{y}^{*}M
.
(The term ‘Wiedersehen’ refers to Riemannian manifolds
on which the cut locus of every point is another point;
the only such manifolds are the standard round spheres
[
20
,
32
]
.)

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
M
be a connected manifold not homeomorphic to the sphere.
If there is a positive Legendrian isotopy connecting
two different fibres of
ST^{*}M
, then they form
a déjà vu Legendrian link.

Proof.

Let us assume that there is an embedded positive Legendrian
isotopy from
ST_{x}^{*}M
to
ST_{y}^{*}M
,
x\neq y\in M
,
and prove that
M
must be homeomorphic to the sphere.

As in the proof of Lemma
2.11
, we may arrange that
the embedded isotopy has a standard form near its endpoints.
In the case of
ST^{*}M
, this standard form may be taken to be
the action of a co-geodesic flow. It follows that if

\pi:ST^{*}M\to M
is the bundle projection and
\iota:S^{n-1}\times[0,1]\to ST^{*}M

is a parametrisation of the isotopy, then the map
\pi\circ\iota:S^{n-1}\times(0,1)\to M

extends to a smooth map
f:S^{n}\to M
,
n=\dim M
. Moreover, there is a contractible
neighbourhood
U\ni x
such that
f:f^{-1}(U)\to U
is one-to-one.

The result now follows from a standard topological argument, cf. e.g.
[
23
, §3.8]
.
Let
p:\widetilde{M}\to M
be the universal covering, then
f
lifts to a map
\widetilde{f}:S^{n}\to\widetilde{M}
.
There is a connected component
V
of
\pi^{-1}(U)
such that
{\widetilde{f}}^{-1}(V)=f^{-1}(U)

and the map
\widetilde{f}
is one-to-one over
V
. Therefore
\widetilde{f}

has topological degree one. Since
S^{n}
is compact, it follows that

\widetilde{f}
is surjective onto
\widetilde{M}
.
Hence,
p^{-1}(U)=\widetilde{f}(f^{-1}(U))=V
and so
p
is the trivial covering.
This shows that
M=\widetilde{M}
is simply connected and closed.
If
\alpha\in\mathsf{H}^{k}(M;\mathbbm{k})
is a non-zero cohomology class with
k\neq 0,n

and coefficients in any field
\mathbbm{k}
,
then by Poincaré duality (on the closed orientable manifold
M
)
there is a class
\beta\in\mathsf{H}^{n-k}(M;\mathbbm{k})
such that
\alpha\cup\beta\neq 0\in\mathsf{H}^{n}(M;\mathbbm{k})
.
Then
0\neq f^{*}(\alpha\cup\beta)=f^{*}(\alpha)\cup f^{*}(\beta)
,
which is impossible since the cohomology of
S^{n}
is trivial in degrees
k\neq 0,n
.
Thus the cohomology of
M
with any (e.g. integer) coefficients
vanishes in all degrees
k\neq 0,n
. This shows that
M
is homeomorphic
to the sphere by the classification of surfaces for
n=2

and by the theorems of Perelman, Freedman, and Smale for

n=3,4
, and
\geq 5
, respectively.
∎

The existence of a positive Legendrian loop based at a fibre
of
ST^{*}M
implies that the universal cover
\widetilde{M}
is
compact
[
6
]
and the integral cohomology ring
\mathsf{H}^{*}(\widetilde{M};\mathbb{Z})

is isomorphic to that of a CROSS
[
15
]
. If the
loop does not intersect the fibre except at its basepoint,
then
M
is either simply connected or homotopy equivalent
to the real projective space
\mathbb{R}\mathrm{P}^{n}
by
[
12
]
.
The above proposition shows that the existence of an
embedded positive Legendrian isotopy between two fibres imposes
an even stronger topological condition on
M
.
Note also that the proof does not use any tools from
contact topology such as generating functions or
Rabinowitz–Floer homology.

There are obvious embedded Legendrian isotopies
between two fibres of
ST^{*}M
obtained by moving
the first fibre along an embedded curve
connecting the corresponding points in
M
.
(Such isotopies are not positive or non-negative,
see
[
5
, §6]
.) The Legendrian isotopy class
of the fibre of
ST^{*}M
is always universally orderable
[
7
]

and so Remark
2.16
shows that
those obvious isotopies are not homotopic to
a positive one.

3.2.
Déjà vu Legendrian links in
T^{*}L\times S^{1}
(cf.
[
11
, §8]
)

Let
Y:=T^{*}L\times S^{1}
be the quotient of
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
by the
\mathbb{Z}
-action
generated by the shift
(q,p,u)\mapsto(q,p,u+1)
and denote by
\pi:{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)\to Y

the projection to the quotient. The canonical contact form
du-\lambda_{\mathrm{can}}
is invariant under
this action and descends to a contact form with periodic Reeb flow on
Y
.
In particular, every Legendrian isotopy class in
Y
is nonorderable.

Let
f:L\to\mathbb{R}
be a smooth function such that

1)

\max_{L}|f|<\tfrac{1}{2}
;

2)

zero is not a critical value of
f
;

3)

f
changes sign on
L
.

The first two conditions guarantee that
\pi(j^{1}(f))
is a Legendrian submanifold of
Y

disjoint from
\pi(\mathrm{O})
.

Proposition 3.2
.

\bigl(\pi(\mathrm{O}),\pi(j^{1}(f))\bigr)
is a déjà vu link in
Y
.

Proof.

First, taking the projection to
Y
of the positive linear isotopy from
\mathrm{O}
to
j^{1}(f+1)

shows that
\pi(\mathrm{O})\llcurly\pi(j^{1}(f))
.
Secondly, we need to prove that a positive isotopy from
\pi(\mathrm{O})
to
\pi(j^{1}(f))

cannot be embedded. Such an isotopy lifts to a positive isotopy
from
\mathrm{O}
to
j^{1}(f+k)
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.
By
[
5
, Corollary 5.4]
, we have
f+k\geq 0
on
L
and therefore
k>0
.
Consider now a family
S_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
, of quadratic at infinity generating functions
for the lifted isotopy and the spectral invariant
c_{[L]}
associated
to the fundamental class
[L]\in\mathsf{H}_{\dim L}(L;\mathbb{Z}/2)
.
Then
c_{[L]}(S_{0})=0
and
c_{[L]}(S_{1})=\max_{L}(f+k)>k
so that

c_{[L]}(S_{\tau})=k
for some
\tau\in(0,1)
by the continuity of the spectral invariant.
Hence,
k
is a critical value of
S_{\tau}
or, in other words, the Legendrian generated
by
S_{\tau}
intersects
j^{1}(k)
. The projection of this Legendrian to
Y
intersects

\pi(j^{1}(k))=\pi(\mathrm{O})
and thus the positive isotopy from
\pi(\mathrm{O})

to
\pi(j^{1}(f))
in
Y
is not embedded as claimed.
∎

The linear isotopy from the zero section
\mathrm{O}
to
j^{1}(f)
projects
to an
embedded
Legendrian isotopy from
\pi(\mathrm{O})

to
\pi(j^{1}(f))
in
Y
, which shows that Proposition
2.14

does not hold in this case. Note, however, that such an embedded isotopy
cannot be homotopic to a positive isotopy. (Otherwise it would lift to
an isotopy from
\mathrm{O}
to
j^{1}(f+k)
for
k>0
and the last
part of the proof of the proposition would again lead to a contradiction.)
The Legendrian isotopy class of
\pi(\mathrm{O})
is
universally

orderable, so this agrees with Remark
2.16
.

The starting point of the construction is the positive Legendrian
isotopy in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

. The figure shows

to the
(q,u)
-plane. The dashed line represents the zero section

.

to the

.

The Legendrian link
(\mathrm{O},\Lambda)
is a déjà vu link in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(S^{1})

for a purely topological reason. Indeed, suppose not. Then
\Lambda
is
Legendrian isotopic to
j^{1}(1)
in the complement of
\mathrm{O}
by Corollary
2.13
.
The two knots are however not even homotopic there because the winding number
of
\Lambda
around
\mathrm{O}

(u,p)

minus the origin is
\pm 1
.

The construction of
\Lambda
can be modified to make its winding number

.
The link

is déjà vu by Proposition

but one can also show that it is not even smoothly isotopic to
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))
.

These two examples illustrate the following general fact based on
the results of Ding and Geiges
[
14
]
and similar to
[
5
, Theorem B]
.

Proposition 3.3
.

A Legendrian link
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(S^{1})
such that its components
are Legendrian isotopic to the zero section and
\Lambda_{1}\llcurly\Lambda_{2}

is a déjà vu link if and only if it is not smoothly isotopic to
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))
.

Proof.

The ‘if’ part follows from the definitions because as observed above
a non déjà vu link is even Legendrian isotopic to
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))

by Corollary
2.13
.

A Legendrian link in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(S^{1})
such that its components are Legendrian isotopic
to the zero section is smoothly isotopic to
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))

if and only if it is Legendrian isotopic to either
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))

or
(j^{1}(1),\mathrm{O})
by the main result of
[
14
]
.

However,
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
cannot be Legendrian isotopic to
(j^{1}(1),\mathrm{O})

because
j^{1}(1)\ggcurly\mathrm{O}
and the class of the zero section is orderable
[
5
,
10
]
.
So if
(\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2})
is smoothly isotopic to
(\mathrm{O},j^{1}(1))
,
then it is Legendrian isotopic to it and is not déjà vu,
which proves the ‘only if’ part.
∎

This topological characterisation of condition (DjV) in the definition of
déjà vu links is specific to dimension
3
, see Example
3.6
.

3.4.
Déjà vu Legendrian links in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

In order to construct déjà vu links in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

for an arbitrary closed manifold
L
of dimension
n
,
we ‘thicken’ a given Legendrian in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})

to a similar Legendrian in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
using
the Legendrian suspension construction
[
7
, §3.3]
.
We will only consider Legendrians in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

that are equal to 1-jets of functions outside of
a compact set and Legendrian isotopies within
this class.

Let
\Lambda\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be a Legendrian properly
isotopic to the zero section and equal to the 1-jet of
a bounded positive function outside of a compact subset.
Choose a positive Legendrian isotopy
\{\Lambda_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}

in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda

and
\Lambda_{1}=j^{1}(C)
for a positive constant
C
.
(Such an isotopy can be constructed by taking any isotopy
from
\Lambda
to
\mathrm{O}
and composing it with
appropriate positive shifts in the
u
-direction, see e.g.
[
11
, §4]
.)
Let
\chi:[0,+\infty)\to[0,1]
be a smooth function such that

\chi(t)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}t&\text{ for }t<\varepsilon\\
1&\text{ for }t\geq 1-\varepsilon\end{array}\right.\quad\text{ and }\quad\chi^{\prime}(t)>0\text{ for }t<1-\varepsilon

and consider the Legendrian family
\{\Lambda_{\chi(\|x\|^{2})}\}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}

with base
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
, see
[
7
, §3.1]
.
(Here
\|x\|
denotes the Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
.)
This family is transverse on
0<\|x\|^{2}<1-\varepsilon
and constant on
\|x\|^{2}\geq 1-\varepsilon
.
Its Legendrian suspension is a Legendrian submanifold

\widetilde{\Lambda}\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})={\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\times T^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n-1}

with the following properties:

(1)

\widetilde{\Lambda}
is the 1-jet of a positive function outside of a compact set
K
.

(2)

Critical points of
\widetilde{\Lambda}
in
K
are of the form
((q,0),0,u)\in{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
where
(q,0,u)\in{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
is a critical point of
\Lambda
.

(3)

If
\mathrm{O}\llcurly\Lambda
, then
\mathrm{O}\llcurly\widetilde{\Lambda}
.

By property (1),
\widetilde{\Lambda}
can be completed to a Legendrian in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)

for any
n
-dimensional closed manifold
L
so that no additional critical
points in
\{u\leq 0\}
are created and property (3) is preserved.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a Legendrian in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\mathrm{O}\llcurly\Lambda

and
\Lambda
has
\varkappa\geq 1
transverse critical points in
\{u\leq 0\}
with the same
negative critical value.
(
For instance, one may take the Legendrian
\Lambda^{\prime}

in Fig.
2
and
\varkappa=2
.
)

Then
(\mathrm{O},\widetilde{\Lambda})
is a déjà vu link in
{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
.

Proof.

Any quadratic at infinity generating function
S
for
\widetilde{\Lambda}

will have
\varkappa
Morse critical points in
\{S\leq 0\}

with the same negative critical value.
Therefore, the relative homology

\mathsf{H}_{*}(S^{0},S^{-\infty};\mathbb{Z}/2)
will have
\varkappa

independent generators (in the degrees equal
to the indices of those critical points).

On the other hand, let
\{S_{t}\}_{t\in[0,1]}
be a family
of quadratic at infinity generating functions for
a positive isotopy from
\mathrm{O}
to
\widetilde{\Lambda}
.
For small
t
, the Legendrians in the isotopy
are 1-jets of positive functions and hence
all critical values of
S_{t}
are positive
and
\mathsf{H}_{*}(S_{t}^{0},S_{t}^{-\infty};\mathbb{Z}/2)=0
.
So by Lemma
2.17
, zero must be a critical
value of
S_{\tau}
for some
\tau>0
and the corresponding
Legendrian intersects
\mathrm{O}
, which shows that
the isotopy is not embedded.
∎

The argument in the proof uses the positivity of the
embedded isotopy only for small
t>0
. This agrees
with Lemma
2.11
.

Spectral invariants are increasing along positive isotopies
(see
[
10
, Proposition 2]
or
[
5
, Lemma 5.2]
) and hence cannot be used
to detect the intersection of such an isotopy with
\mathrm{O}
.
In other words, the image of the homomorphism

\mathsf{H}_{*}(S^{0},S^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})\to\mathsf{H}_{*}(L\times\mathbb{R}^{N},S^{-\infty};\mathbbm{k})

is trivial and therefore to invoke Lemma
2.17

we need to know that the kernel of this homomorphism is non-trivial.
This general principle may be stated in terms of
the
finite bars
in the barcode of a generating function.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\Lambda\subset{\mathcal{J}}^{1}(L)
be a Legendrian submanifold
such that
\mathrm{O}\llcurly\Lambda
and
\mathrm{O}\cap\Lambda=\varnothing
.
If for some
(
and then any
)
quadratic at infinity
generating Morse function of
\Lambda
there is a finite bar
in its barcode containing
0\in\mathbb{R}
, then
(\mathrm{O},\Lambda)

is a déjà vu link.

4.
Lorentz geometry

4.1.
Spacetimes and spaces of null geodesics

A
spacetime
is a connected time-oriented Lorentz manifold
(\mathcal{X},\langle\text{ },\!\text{ }\rangle)
.
The Lorentz metric is taken to be of signature
(+,-,\dots,-)
so that
\langle v,v\rangle>0

for timelike vectors and
\langle v,v\rangle<0
for spacelike vectors.
The time-orientation is a continuous choice of the future hemicone

C^{\uparrow}_{x}\subset\{v\in T_{x}\mathcal{X}\mid\langle v,v\rangle\geq 0,v\neq 0\}

in the cone of non-spacelike vectors at each point
x\in\mathcal{X}
.
The vectors in
C^{\uparrow}_{x}
are called
future-pointing
.
A piecewise smooth curve in
\mathcal{X}
is
future-directed

if all its tangent vectors are future-pointing.

The
causality relation

\leq
on
\mathcal{X}
is defined
by setting
x\leq y
if either
x=y
or there is a future-directed
curve connecting
x
to
y
.
The
chronology relation

\ll
is defined similarly
by writing
x\ll y
if there is a future-directed timelike curve
connecting
x
to
y
.

\mathcal{X}
is
causal
if it does not contain closed
future-directed curves. (This is equivalent to requiring
that
\leq
is a partial order.) A causal spacetime
\mathcal{X}

is
globally hyperbolic
if the causal interval
\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid x\leq z\leq y\}

is compact for every
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
, see
[
2
]

for the equivalence of this definition and the more classical one
[
26
, Definition 5.24]
or
[
19
, §6.6]
.
By the smooth splitting theorem
[
1
]
,
a globally hyperbolic spacetime is foliated by smooth spacelike
Cauchy (hyper)surfaces, where a
Cauchy surface
is a subset
of a spacetime such that every endless future-directed curve
intersects it exactly once.

The
space of null geodesics
of
\mathcal{X}
is the set
\mathfrak{N}_{\mathcal{X}}

of equivalence classes of endless future-directed null geodesics
up to an orientation preserving affine reparametrisation
[
22
]
.
For a globally hyperbolic
\mathcal{X}
of dimension
\geq 3
, this space is a contact manifold
and to every Cauchy surface
M\subset\mathcal{X}
there is associated
a contactomorphism
\rho_{M}:\mathfrak{N}_{\mathcal{X}}\overset{\cong}{\longrightarrow}ST^{*}M

onto the co-sphere bundle of
M
, see e.g.
[
25
, pp. 252–253]

or
[
8
, §§1-2]
.

The set
\mathfrak{S}_{x}\subset\mathfrak{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
of all null geodesics
passing through a point
x\in\mathcal{X}
is a Legendrian sphere in
\mathfrak{N}_{\mathcal{X}}

called the
sky
(or the
celestial sphere
) of that point.
For any Cauchy surface
M\subset\mathcal{X}
and a point
x\in M
,

\rho_{M}(\mathfrak{S}_{x})=ST_{x}^{*}M
and hence all skies are
mapped by
\rho_{M}
to the Legendrian isotopy class of the fibre of
ST^{*}M
, see
[
5
, §4]
.

The twistor map
x\mapsto\mathfrak{S}_{x}
has the following properties
summarised in this form in
[
9
, §4.2]
.
If
x\leq y
, then
\mathfrak{S}_{x}\preccurlyeq\mathfrak{S}_{y}
, and if
x\ll y
, then

\mathfrak{S}_{x}\llcurly\mathfrak{S}_{y}
, where
\preccurlyeq
and
\llcurly

are the relations on Legendrians introduced in §
2.1
.
Moreover, if the Legendrian isotopy class of the fibre of
ST^{*}M

is orderable (for instance, if
M
is non-compact
[
6
]
), then
the converse implications hold as well.

4.2.
Déjà vu moments

Let
\gamma=\gamma(t)
be a future-directed timelike curve
in a causal spacetime
\mathcal{X}
. A
déjà vu moment
is
a point
x_{+}=\gamma(t_{+})
such that there exists a point

x_{-}=\gamma(t_{-})
with
t_{-}<t_{+}
and a null geodesic
connecting
x_{-}
to
x_{+}
. (Causality implies that this null
geodesic will be future-directed.)
The curve
\gamma
may be thought of as the world line
of an observer who is receiving the same light ray
at inner time
t_{+}
as at the time
t_{-}
in the past.

Proposition 4.1
.

If the skies of two points in a globally hyperbolic spacetime
\mathcal{X}
form a déjà vu
Legendrian link
(\mathfrak{S}_{x},\mathfrak{S}_{y})
, then there are déjà vu
moments on every future-directed timelike curve from
x
to
y
.

Proof.

Such a curve
\gamma=\gamma(t)
,
t\in[0,1]
,
defines a piecewise smooth isotopy of skies
\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma(t)}

connecting
\mathfrak{S}_{x}
to
\mathfrak{S}_{y}

that is positive on its smooth segments by
[
9
, Proposition 4.3]
.
Since there is no positive embedded isotopy between those skies,
it follows from Lemma
2.11
that there exist
t_{0}\in(0,1]
and
t_{1}\in[0,1)

such that
\mathfrak{S}_{x}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma(t_{0})}\neq\varnothing

and
\mathfrak{S}_{\gamma(t_{1})}\cap\mathfrak{S}_{y}\neq\varnothing
.
But this means exactly that there are null geodesics connecting

x
to
\gamma(t_{0})
and
\gamma(t_{1})
to
y
. The skies of
x

and
y
are disjoint, so they are not connected by a null geodesic.
Hence, we get at least two déjà vu moments at
\gamma(t_{0})

and
y=\gamma(1)
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The proof of Proposition
4.1
shows, in other words, that every future-directed timelike curve from
x
to
y

must intersect the exponentiated future null cone of
x
and the exponentiated
past null cone of
y
.

Déjà vu links in
ST^{*}\mathbb{R}^{n}
of the types shown on Fig.
3
(d) and Fig.
4

appear as links of skies in globally hyperbolic static spacetimes of the form
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R},-g\oplus dt^{2})
,
where
g
is a ‘bumpy’ Riemannian metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
creating scattering obstacles.
More advanced examples of this kind may be derived from
[
25
, Fig. 5]
.

If the Legendrian isotopy class of skies
(i.e. the fibre class in
ST^{*}M
for a Cauchy surface
M\subset\mathcal{X}
) is
orderable
,
then the proposition can be strengthened in two ways.
First, there actually exists
a future-directed timelike curve from
x
to
y
because

\mathfrak{S}_{x}\llcurly\mathfrak{S}_{y}
implies
x\ll y
.
Secondly, intersections with the exponentiated null cones
exist for
every
curve connecting
x
and
y

by Proposition
2.14
(ii). The first part
of the following example shows that without the orderability
assumption both assertions may be false.

Example 4.3
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian
Y^{x}_{\ell}
-manifold, which implies that
the fibre class in
ST^{*}M
is
not
orderable, see §
3.1
.
The Lorentz direct product
(M\times\mathbb{R},-g\oplus dt^{2})
is a globally
hyperbolic spacetime.

(i)
If
M
is not homeomorphic to the sphere, then the skies
of two points
x=(\underline{x},0)
and
y=(\underline{y},0)

on the same Cauchy surface
M\times\{0\}
form a déjà vu link by Proposition
3.1
.
At the same time, the points are not causally related and can be connected by a (spacelike) curve
inside
M\times\{0\}
which does not intersect their exponentiated null cones.

(ii)
If
M
is diffeomorphic to the sphere, then
(\mathfrak{S}_{x},\mathfrak{S}_{y})

is
not
a déjà vu link. Let us consider the point
y^{\prime}=(\underline{y},\ell)
.
If
\underline{y}
is close to
\underline{x}
, then obviously
x\ll y^{\prime}
.
Note also that
\mathfrak{S}_{y^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{S}_{y}
because null geodesics in
\mathcal{X}

are of the form
(\beta(s),s)
, where
\beta=\beta(s)
is a naturally parametrised Riemannian geodesic in
(M,g)
.
So
(\mathfrak{S}_{x},\mathfrak{S}_{y^{\prime}})
is
not
a déjà vu link.
Nevertheless, the conclusion of Proposition
4.1

is valid for
x
and
y^{\prime}
(non-vacuously because
x\ll y^{\prime}
). To see this,
note that
x\ll x^{\prime}=(\underline{x},\ell)
whereas
y^{\prime}
is causally unrelated to
x^{\prime}
.
Hence, every curve connecting
x
and
y^{\prime}
intersects the boundary of
I^{-}(x^{\prime})=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid z\ll x^{\prime}\}
.
In a globally hyperbolic spacetime, this boundary is contained in the

closed
set
J^{-}(x^{\prime})=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid z\leq x^{\prime}\}

and is covered by null geodesics passing through
x^{\prime}
(and hence through
x
)
by
[
24
, Proposition 3.71]
and
[
26
, Proposition 2.20]
.
It follows that every future-directed curve from
x
to
y^{\prime}

must intersect the exponentiated future null cone of
x
.
Applying the same argument to
I^{+}(y)=\{z\in\mathcal{X}\mid y\ll z\}

shows that such a curve must also intersect the exponentiated
past null cone of
y^{\prime}
.∎

The second part of the preceding example shows that the converse
to Proposition
4.1
need not hold in general.
The example is very special, however, so one may venture the
following (optimistic) conjecture:

Conjecture 4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime such that its Cauchy surface
is
not
homeomorphic to the sphere. If there are déjà vu moments
on every future-directed timelike curve connecting two causally related points
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
,
then their skies either intersect or form a déjà vu Legendrian link
in
\mathfrak{N}_{\mathcal{X}}
.

If this conjecture is true, it will have a number of purely
geometric consequences which may be used to support (or refute) it.
Assume, for instance, that
x
is connected to
y
by a future-directed
timelike curve that does not intersect the exponentiated
future null cone of
x
. Then
\mathfrak{S}_{x}\llcurly\mathfrak{S}_{y}

but the link
(\mathfrak{S}_{x},\mathfrak{S}_{y})
is not déjà vu
by Lemma
2.11
. The conjecture would imply
that there exists a (maybe different) future-directed timelike curve from
x
to
y

without déjà vu moments and, in particular, not intersecting
the exponentiated past null cone of
y
.

Abstract
. Enumerating integral orbits in prehomogeneous vector spaces plays an important role in arithmetic statistics. We describe a method of proving subconvexity of the zeta function enumerating the integral orbits, illustrated by proving a subconvex estimate for the Shintani ζ \zeta function enumerating class numbers of binary cubic forms.

The subconvexity problem is one of the important problems in the theory of zeta and

1.1.
Discussion of method

As usual in the theory of zeta functions, the proof of Theorem
1
begins by expressing the zeta function in the critical strip via an approximate functional equation which expresses the zeta function as the sum of two Dirichlet polynomials, each of length square root of the conductor. The theory of such functional equations for prehomogeneous vector space zeta functions was first developed by Sato and Shintani
[
26
]
, with extensions to larger classes of zeta functions in works of Yukie
[
35
]
and Saito
[
22
]
. The coefficients in these Dirichlet polynomials are expressed as a sum over binary cubic forms restricted to a fundamental domain for the action of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\backslash\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Bhargava’s averaging trick
[
2
]
is used to average over many fundamental domains in order to bound the contribution of the lattice points in the cusp. Having trimmed away the cusps, the points now lie essentially within a compact neighborhood of the identity; van der Corput’s inequality is used to obtain cancellation in exponential sums in which the phase has controlled partial derivatives.

1.2.
Background and related work

The zeta function of a prehomogeneous vector space was introduced by Sato and Shintani
[
26
]
in the 70s, with the case of binary cubic forms studied in detail by Shintani
[
27
]
who determined poles and residues along with the meromorphic continuation. The class of functions have been extended over time. F. Sato introduced series in multiple complex variables
[
23
]
,
[
24
]
, treating Eisenstein series associated to irrational indefinite quadratic forms. Later he introduced a twisting automorphic form and showed a wide class of familiar number theoretic objects can be constructed in this way
[
25
]
including Langlands standard L-functions, and Dirichlet series of Maass constructed from systems of quadratic forms. Datskovsky, Wright and Yukie developed the adelization with applications to low degree number fields
[
11
]
,
[
34
]
,
[
35
]
. Saito completed part of Yukie’s work
[
22
]
by establishing criteria which guarantee the convergence and meromorphic continuation of the zeta functions. Taniguchi and Thorne developed the local theory of the binary cubic form case more carefully
[
30
]
, using this to prove a secondary main term in the Davenport-Heilbronn Theorem counting cubic number fields ordered by discriminant. Recently Wen-Wei Li has further developed the automorphic twisted zeta functions
[
19
]
proving local functional equations and giving an indication of the size of the class of objects considered.

The theory of subconvexity of zeta and L-functions is a very active current area of number theory. After pioneering work of Duke, Friedlander and Iwaniec
[
13
]
subconvexity has frequently been established by the amplification method, which calculates a moment of the zeta or L-function in a family multiplied by a Dirichlet polynomial that selects for the function of interest. Due to the high degree of the prehomogeneous vector space zeta functions, calculating moments is not quickly available and so the proof here uses a truncation method, together with an approach similar to the classical approach to the Riemann zeta function using van der Corput’s method. An interesting parallel result has been proved by Blomer recently for Epstein zeta functions
[
10
]
, which are prehomogeneous zeta functions. In principle, similar bounds to Blomer’s can be obtained from methods for PVS zeta functions, we intend to return to this issue in a forthcoming publication.

\sum_{j=1}^{N}e^{2\pi i\alpha j}\ll\min\left(N,\frac{1}{\|\alpha\|_{\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}}}\right).

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}\sum_{f\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}}\int_{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{h\in H_{\pm}:f=d_{\lambda}gh\cdot f_{\pm}}\omega(h\cdot f_{\pm})F(g)dg\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}

where
M_{\pm}=\int_{B\cap V_{\pm}}\frac{\omega(v)dv}{|\operatorname{Disc}(v)|}
.

Next introduce a smooth partition of unity to control the size of the discriminant. Let
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
satisfy
\sigma\geq 0
and

(65)

\sum_{n\geq A}\sigma\left(\frac{x}{2^{n}}\right)=1,\qquad x\geq 1.

Thus

(66)

\displaystyle\Sigma=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}\sum_{f\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}\sum_{n\geq A}\sigma\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{2^{n}}\right)\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{h\in H_{\pm}:f=d_{\lambda}gh\cdot f_{\pm}}\omega(h\cdot f_{\pm})F(g)dg\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.

We may assume
Y>\tau^{2-\frac{2}{99}}
, bounding the initial part of the sum trivially, with acceptable error.

Next truncate in
t
. Let
T_{1}=T_{1}(Y)
and
T_{2}=T_{2}(Y)
be parameters. Recall that
f=n_{u}a_{t}k_{\theta}\cdot f_{\pm}
. By Lemma
11
the expected number of classes of forms
f
with
a\neq 0
and
t>T_{1}
,
|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|\asymp Y
is
O\left(\frac{Y}{T_{1}}+Y^{\frac{5}{6}}\right)
, while the number of forms with
a=0
and
t<T_{2}
is
O\left(Y^{\frac{3}{4}}T_{2}\right)
. Let
\Sigma_{1}(Y)
indicate the sum over forms with
a\neq 0
and
t\leq T_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}(Y)
the sum over
a=0
with
t\geq T_{2}
. Thus

(67)

\Sigma(Y)=\Sigma_{1}(Y)+\Sigma_{2}(Y)+O\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{Y}}{T_{1}}+Y^{\frac{1}{3}}+Y^{\frac{1}{4}}T_{2}\right)\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}\right).

In
\Sigma_{1}(Y)
, introduce a space average. Let
R=R(Y)
be a parameter satisfying
R\ll\frac{Y^{\frac{1}{4}}}{T_{1}^{3}}
and let
B_{R}=\{y\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}:\|y\|_{\infty}\leq R\}
. Note that by Lemmas
8
and
9
, for
y\in B_{R}
,
|\operatorname{Disc}(f+y)|\asymp|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|
and
t_{f+y}\asymp t_{f}
. The averaged sum is, with
′
indicating
a\neq 0
and
t\leq T_{1}
,

(68)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(Y)=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}{\sum_{f\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}}}^{\prime}\mathbf{E}_{y\in B_{R(Y)}}\sigma\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f+y)|}{Y}\right)\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f+y)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f+y)|^{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}}

\displaystyle\times\int_{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{h\in H_{\pm}:f+y=d_{\lambda}gh\cdot f_{\pm}}\omega(h\cdot f_{\pm})F(g)dg\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.

Notice that in
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(Y)
, the sum over
f
is restricted to forms
f
with
t\leq T_{1}
, which is different than the condition
t_{f+y}\leq T_{1}
. This introduces a difference between
\Sigma_{1}(Y)
and
\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(Y)
.
We have,

|\Sigma_{1}(Y)-\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(Y)|=O\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{Y}}{T_{1}}+Y^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}\right),
since the forms where the two sums differ have
|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|\asymp Y
and
t_{f}\asymp T_{1}
.

Let

(69)

W(f)=\int_{g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{0}^{\infty}\sum_{h\in H_{\pm}:f=d_{\lambda}gh\cdot f_{\pm}}\omega(h\cdot f_{\pm})F(g)dg\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}.

Lemma 15
.

Let
v\in\mathbb{R}^{4}
be a unit vector. For
f=d_{\lambda}n_{u}a_{t}k_{\theta}\cdot f_{\pm}
with
\lambda\geq 1
and
t\gg 1
, we have the bound for partial derivatives,

(70)

\partial_{v}\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)\sigma\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{Y}\right)W(f)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ll\frac{t^{5}}{\lambda^{3}}\left(1+\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}.

Proof.

Since we can write
W(f)
as a group convolution, we can pass the derivative inside the integral, and thus we bound

(71)

\displaystyle\partial_{v}W(f)

\displaystyle=\int_{h\in H_{\pm},d_{\lambda}gh\cdot f_{\pm}=f}\omega(h\cdot f_{\pm})\partial_{v}F(g)dh.

Notice that
H_{\pm}
is
K
-invariant, so we can eliminate the dependence on
\theta
. Also, the integral is
\lambda
-invariant, so the derivative depends only on
u
and
t
. Thus write

\partial_{v}F=\frac{\partial{F}}{\partial u}\partial_{v}u+\frac{\partial{F}}{\partial t}\partial_{v}t
and bound
\partial_{v}u\ll\frac{t^{5}}{\lambda}
,
\partial_{v}t\ll\frac{t^{4}}{\lambda}
to bound
\partial_{v}W\ll\frac{t^{5}}{\lambda}
. The remainder of the claim follows from the estimate for the derivatives of
V
in Lemma
3
, together with the bound for the partial derivatives of the discriminant.
∎

Let

(72)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}(Y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}{\sum_{f}}^{\prime}\sigma\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{Y}\right)\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)W(f)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\mathbf{E}_{y\in B_{R(Y)}}\left[|\operatorname{Disc}(f+y)|^{-i\tau}\right].

Applying the derivative bound,

(73)

|\Sigma_{1}^{\prime}(Y)-\Sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}(Y)|\ll Y^{\frac{1}{4}}T_{1}^{5}R\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}.

By the bound for exponential sums in Lemma
13
, with

,

(74)

|\Sigma_{1}^{\prime\prime}(Y)|\ll\frac{T_{1}^{\frac{8}{3}}\sqrt{Y}}{\tau^{\frac{1}{27}-\epsilon}}\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}.

Notice that, for the choice of
R
, this dominates the error in (

It follows that

(75)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}(Y)=O\left(\left(\frac{\sqrt{Y}}{T_{1}}+Y^{\frac{1}{3}}+\frac{T_{1}^{\frac{8}{3}}\sqrt{Y}}{\tau^{\frac{1}{27}-\epsilon}}\right)\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}\right).

Choose, optimally,

. Summed over

obtains a bound of

.

We next bound
\Sigma_{2}
.
Let

. Write

Let

(78)

\Sigma_{2}^{\prime}(Y)=\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}\\

Then

We next estimate the derivative of the weight function with respect to

.

Lemma 16
.

For
f=d_{\lambda}n_{u}a_{t}k_{\theta}\cdot f_{\pm}
with
\lambda\geq 1
and
t\gg 1
, and
a=0
. We have the bound for partial derivatives,

(79)

\partial_{d}\frac{V_{\frac{1}{2}+i\tau}\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\sqrt{432}}\right)\sigma\left(\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{Y}\right)W(f)}{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|^{\frac{1}{2}}}\ll\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}T_{2}^{3}}\left(1+\frac{|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}.

Proof.

We have
\frac{\partial}{\partial d}|\operatorname{Disc}(f)|=b^{3}=O\left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{T_{2}}\right)^{3}\right)
. Meanwhile,

(80)

\frac{\partial}{\partial d}W(f)=\frac{\partial}{\partial d}\int_{g\cdot v=f}F(g)\omega(v)\frac{dv}{|\operatorname{Disc}(v)|}.

If

. Pass this estimate for the derivative under the integral to obtain the same estimate for the derivative of the integral. Combining these estimates proves the claim.
∎

Let

(81)

\Sigma_{2}^{\prime\prime}(Y)=\frac{1}{n_{\pm}M_{\pm}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in V_{\mathbb{Z}}\cap V_{\pm}\\

By the estimate for exponential sums in Lemma

\Sigma_{2}(Y)\ll\left(Y^{\frac{1}{4}}T_{2}+\frac{Y^{\frac{3}{4}}}{\tau^{\frac{2}{3}}T_{2}}+\frac{Y}{\tau^{\frac{1}{3}}T_{2}^{2}}\right)\left(1+\frac{Y}{\tau^{2}}\right)^{-A}.

Mutations of ordinary torsion theories and generalized torsion theories connected by a Serre subcategory
Takeshi Yoshizawa

Abstract
. Understanding how torsion theories are described and constructed is crucial to the study of torsion theory. Mutations of torsion theories have been studied as a method of constructing another torsion theory from a given one. We have already obtained how to mutate ordinary torsion theories into generalized torsion theories associated with a Serre subcategory. The paper investigates when the generalized torsion theories give ordinary torsion theories and when ordinary torsion theories provide each other.

1.
Introduction

Dickson
[
4
]
introduced the concept of torsion theory in an abelian category.
Owing to its applicability, the torsion theory is highly valuable in various fields, including ring theory, representation theory, and category theory.
For instance, in representation theory, we investigate the behavior of the category of modules using the Hasse quiver of the complete lattice of torsion classes.

The study of how to describe and construct torsion theories is crucial.
An indispensable result is Gabriel’s classification of hereditary torsion theories in the category of modules with specialization closed subsets of the spectrum of ring, as presented in his work
[
6
]
. (See also
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
and
[
9
, Corollary 2.7]
.)

On the other hand, it is also important to investigate how to construct another torsion theory or t-structure from a given one in the category of modules or a triangulated category.

As a specific example, a torsion theory can mutate into another torsion theory using brick modules.
Let us consider the complete lattice
\mathrm{tors}\,A
of torsion classes in the category of finitely generated
A
-modules over a finite-dimensional
k
-algebra
A
for a field
k
and the
heart

\mathcal{H}_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}=\mathcal{U}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{T}
of the interval
[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]
in
\mathrm{tors}\,A
.

[
3
, Theorems 3.3 and 3.4]
deduce that there exists an arrow
q:\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{U}
in the Hasse quiver
\mathrm{Hasse}(\mathrm{tors}\,A)
if and only if there is a unique brick
S_{q}\in\mathrm{brick}\,\mathcal{H}_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
up to isomorphism.
In this case, one has the equality
\mathcal{H}_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}=\mathrm{Filt}\,S_{q}
.
This brick
S_{q}
is called the

of the arrow
q
.
The brick labeling plays a useful role in the computation of
\mathrm{Hasse}(\mathrm{tors}A)

Indeed, one has the equality
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{U}*\mathcal{H}_{[\mathcal{U},\mathcal{T}]}
, in other words, the torsion class
\mathcal{U}
mutates into another torsion class
\mathcal{T}
by the brick
S_{q}
. (See also the dual version of
[
5
, Lemma 2.8]
.)

As another example, let
\mathcal{H}_{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}=\mathcal{X}[-1]\cap\mathcal{Y}
be the
heart
of
t
-structure
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
in a triangulated category.
Happel, Reiten, and Smal

[
7

introduced methods to construct another

-structure from the given

-structure

using a torsion theory

in the heart
\mathcal{H}_{(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})}

To be more precise, the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
mutates into the

(\mathcal{X}[1]*\mathcal{U}[1],\mathcal{V}[1]*\mathcal{Y})
and the

(\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}*(\mathcal{Y}[-1]))

There has been a growing interest in how a given torsion theory mutates into another one.
Therefore, the paper focuses on the mutation of torsion theories in the category

As a method of mutation of torsion theory, the paper uses generalized torsion theories connected by a Serre subcategory

In analogy with the definition of ordinary torsion theory, the paper

Furthermore, in this case, the Serre subcategory

is called the

\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory

[

-connection of the pair

By contrast, the purpose of this paper is to investigate when
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theories give ordinary torsion theories and when ordinary ones provide each other.
Specifically, as mutations for a given

-torsion theory

Therefore, it is natural to investigate when each separation becomes an ordinary torsion theory in

Let us now make three preparations for stating our main results.
Firstly, if each of the above separations is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
, then the separations
\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))

Secondly, an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is called

if the equality
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
holds.
Finally, we say that the heart
\mathcal{S}
of an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory

is

(respectively,

) if one has the equality

(respectively, the equality
(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F})*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}

Preliminaries

Throughout this paper, all rings are commutative Noetherian, and all modules are unitary.
For a ring

Additionally, we suppose that all subcategories of

First of all, we recall the concept of torsion theory introduced by Dickson

\mathcal{Y}

(2)

(3)

We denote by
\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
the
right
\mathcal{S}
-separation
of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.
We say that
\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))
is the
right mutation at the heart
\mathcal{S}
of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
if the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

(4)

We denote by
\Psi^{M}_{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=((\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}))
the
middle
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
-separation
of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.
We say that
\Psi^{M}_{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))
is the
middle mutation at the triple
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
of
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
if the pair
((\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V}*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}))
is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

(5)

The pairs
\Psi^{CL}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{S}})((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
,
\Psi^{CR}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{S}})((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
, and
\Psi^{CM}_{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\Psi^{M}_{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{S}})((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
are respectively called the
left
\mathcal{S}
-mutation
, the
right
\mathcal{S}
-mutation
, and the
middle
(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
-mutation
of
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
if each pair is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

Remark 2.7
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
,
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
, and
(\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})
be as in Definition
2.6
.
When we investigate mutations, we are interested in the case when the heart
\mathcal{S}
is not the zero subcategory, in other words, when the heart
\mathcal{S}
has a non-zero module.
Indeed, if we suppose that the heart
\mathcal{S}=0
, then we have the equalities
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
,
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
,
\mathcal{U}=0
, and
\mathcal{V}=0
.
Therefore, we have the equalities
\Phi_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
,
\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=\Psi^{M}_{(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{V})}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.

3.
The left mutation and the right mutation

In this section we investigate when an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory has the left mutation or right mutation at the heart
\mathcal{S}
.

We begin with a necessary and sufficient condition for an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory to have the left mutation at the heart
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The left
\mathcal{S}
-separation
\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{F})
is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

(2)

The pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical and the heart
\mathcal{S}
is left canonical.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
:
We start by showing the equality
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{F})=0
.
Let us take
T\in\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
,
F\in\mathcal{F}
, and
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(T,F)
.
Then we will see the equality
\varphi=0
.

Since the map
\varphi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, the module
\varphi(T)
lies in
\mathcal{S}
by condition (TT1) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.
We now decompose the map
\varphi
into the following form

\begin{CD}T@>{\psi}>{}>\varphi(T)@>{i}>{}>F,\end{CD}

where
i
is the inclusion map.
Then this map
\psi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}({}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S})=0
.
We thus achieve the equalities
\varphi=i\circ\psi=0
.

Secondly, we suppose that an
R
-module
M
satisfies
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,\mathcal{F})=0
.
Then we will verify that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
.
For each map
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,F)
with
F\in\mathcal{F}
, our assumption for the module
M
yields the equality
\varphi=0
, and we thus obtain that
\varphi(M)=0\in\mathcal{S}
.
Therefore, condition (TT2) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
implies that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{T}
.
On the other hand, Lemma
2.4
(3) and the assumption for the module
M
deduce that
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,\mathcal{F})=0
.
Therefore, the module
M
also lies in
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
.

Finally, we take an
R
-module
M
with
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},M)=0
, and let us show that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{F}
.
Assumption (2) gives the equalities
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
and
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{T}
.
Thus, the module
M
has a short exact sequence
0\to T\overset{\varphi}{\to}M\to F\to 0
with
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
Furthermore, the module
T
gives a short exact sequence
0\to T_{1}\overset{\psi}{\to}T\to T_{2}\to 0
with
T_{1}\in\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S}
.

We now note that the composition
\varphi\circ\psi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},M)
.
Then the assumption for the module
M
implies that
\varphi\circ\psi=0
, and thus the equality
(\varphi\circ\psi)(T_{1})=0
holds.
The injectivity of the maps
\varphi
and
\psi
yields the equality
T_{1}=0
.
Therefore, the second short exact sequence provides an isomorphism
T\cong T_{2}\in\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
.
Consequently, the first short exact sequence and Lemma
2.4
(2) deduce that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{F}
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
By Remark
2.2
(3), the torsion theory
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{F})
provides that
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}=(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}\subseteq R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
This implies the equality
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
In other words, the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical.

Next, to prove that the heart
\mathcal{S}
is left canonical, we only have to show the relation
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S})\supseteq\mathcal{T}
by Lemma
2.4
(4).
Let us take an
R
-module
T\in\mathcal{T}
.
Remark
2.2
(3) implies that the torsion theory
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{F})
satisfies the equality
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
Then there exists a short exact sequence
0\to T^{\prime}\to T\overset{\varphi}{\to}F\to 0
with
T^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
Since the map
\varphi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, the module
F=\varphi(T)
lies in
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S}
by condition (TT1) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
and Lemma
2.4
(3).
Therefore, the above short exact sequence implies that the module
T
lies in
(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S})
.
This completes the proof of our claim.
∎

A consequence of Theorem
3.1
characterizes when a torsion theory
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
has the left
\mathcal{S}
-mutation
\Psi^{CL}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
, that is, when the pair
\Psi^{CL}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
For a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{S}
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
has the left
\mathcal{S}
-mutation

\Psi^{CL}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=((\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{S})\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{Y}).

(2)

The heart
\mathcal{S}
of the
\mathcal{S}
-connection
\Phi_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
is left canonical.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{Y}
.
Then we have the equalities
\Psi^{CL}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{S}})((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=\Psi^{L}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))
.
If we can verify that the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is a canonical
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory, then our statements hold by Theorem
3.1
.

[
14
, Proposition 5.1]
deduces that the
\mathcal{S}
-connection
\Phi_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory.
Moreover, Remark
2.2
(3) implies that the torsion theory
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
yields the relations
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}=\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{Y}\subseteq(\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{S})*(\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{Y})\subseteq R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
This means the equality
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
Consequently, the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical.
∎

Next, we investigate when an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory has the right mutation at the heart
\mathcal{S}
.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The right
\mathcal{S}
-separation
\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))=(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.

(2)

The pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical and the heart
\mathcal{S}
is right canonical.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
:
First of all, let us show the equality
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})=0
.
Namely, we take a map
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(T,F)
with
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}
, and we will prove the equality
\varphi=0
.

Since the map
\varphi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, the module
\varphi(T)
lies in
\mathcal{S}
by condition (TT1) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.
Now let us decompose the map
\varphi
into the following form

\begin{CD}T@>{\psi}>{}>\varphi(T)@>{i}>{}>F,\end{CD}

where
i
is the inclusion map.
Then the map
i
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\perp})=0
.
Therefore, we obtain the equality
\varphi=i\circ\psi=0
.

Secondly, we suppose that an
R
-module
M
satisfies
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})=0
.
Then we will see that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{T}
.
Assumption (2) deduces that the equalities
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
and
(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F})*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})=\mathcal{F}
.
We thus have a short exact sequence
0\to T\to M\overset{\varphi}{\to}F\to 0
with
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F\in\mathcal{F}
.
Moreover, the module
F
provides a short exact sequence
0\to F_{1}\to F\overset{\psi}{\to}F_{2}\to 0
with
F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F}
and
F_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}
.

Since the composition
\psi\circ\varphi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
, the assumption for the module
M
yields the equality
\psi\circ\varphi=0
.
The surjectivity of the maps
\varphi
and
\psi
implies that the equalities
F_{2}=(\psi\circ\varphi)(M)=0
.
Then the second short exact sequence gives an isomorphism
F\cong F_{1}\in\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{S}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
(1), the first short exact sequence concludes that
M
lies in
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}
.

Finally, we take an
R
-module
M
with
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},M)=0
, and let us show that the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}
.
Lemma
2.4
(3) and the assumption for the module
M
imply that
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{S},M)\subseteq\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},M)=0
.
Therefore, the module
M
lies in
\mathcal{S}^{\perp}
.
On the other hand, for each map
\varphi\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(T,M)
with
T\in\mathcal{T}
, our assumption for the module
M
gives the equality
\varphi=0
.
This equality implies that
\varphi(T)=0\in\mathcal{S}
.
Consequently, we can conclude that the module
M
also lies in
\mathcal{F}
by condition (TT3) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
By Remark
2.2
(3), the torsion theory
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
satisfies that
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}=\mathcal{T}*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}\subseteq R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
Then we achieve the equality
\mathcal{T}*\mathcal{F}=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
, and thus the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical.

Next, let us prove that the heart
\mathcal{S}
is right canonical.
By Lemma
2.4
(4), it suffices to show that
(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F})*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})\supseteq\mathcal{F}
.
We suppose that an
R
-module
F
lies in
\mathcal{F}
.
Remark
2.2
(3) provides the equality
\mathcal{T}*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})=R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
for the torsion theory
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
.
This equality yields a short exact sequence
0\to T\overset{\varphi}{\to}F\to F^{\prime}\to 0
with
T\in\mathcal{T}
and
F^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F}
.
Since the map
\varphi
belongs to
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
, the module
T\cong\varphi(T)
lies in
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F}
by condition (TT1) for the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
and Lemma
2.4
(3).
Then the above short exact sequence deduces that the module
F
lies in
(\mathcal{S}\cap\mathcal{F})*(\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap\mathcal{F})
.
This completes the proof of our inclusion relation.
∎

We close this section by characterizing when a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
has the right
\mathcal{S}
-mutation, which is a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
The following characterization is a consequence of Theorem
3.3
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
be a torsion theory in
R\text{-}\mathrm{Mod}
.
For a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{S}
, the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The pair
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y})
has the right
\mathcal{S}
-mutation

\Psi^{CR}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\perp}\cap(\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{Y})).

(2)

The heart
\mathcal{S}
of the
\mathcal{S}
-connection
\Phi_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))
is right canonical.

Proof.

For
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{X}*\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{S}*\mathcal{Y}
, we have the equalities
\Psi^{CR}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=(\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}\circ\Phi_{\mathcal{S}})((\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}))=\Psi^{R}_{\mathcal{S}}((\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F}))
.
Therefore, by the same argument as the proof of Corollary
3.2
, our statements follow from Theorem
3.3
.
∎

4.
The middle mutation

Our purpose here is to provide necessary and sufficient conditions for a generalized torsion theory to have the middle mutation at a triple of subcategories.

We begin with the following three lemmas, which will be necessary conditions concerned with the above purpose.
The first lemma investigates whether an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory is canonical when a middle mutation exists.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let

Lemma

2.2
(3).
Consequently, the pair
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
is canonical.
∎

5.
An example of mutations

The purpose of this section is to give an example of left, right and middle mutations.
Although Example
5.3
below is basic, we can provide an example of these three mutations that differ from one another.

We begin by preparing to illustrate this example.
The following two lemmas state that Serre subcategories with a special injective module over a local ring are quite restricted to the behavior of the left orthogonal subcategories and the situation in which they are left canonical.

We recall that a Serre subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is called
stable
if
\mathcal{S}
satisfies the following condition: if an
R
-module
M
lies in
\mathcal{S}
, then the injective hull
E_{R}(M)
of
M
also lies in
\mathcal{S}
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
R
be a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
.
If the injective hull
E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})
of the
R
-module
R/\mathfrak{m}
lies in a subcategory
\mathcal{C}
, then one has the equality
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}=0
.
In particular, a stable Serre subcategory
\mathcal{S}
with a non-zero
R
-module satisfies the equality
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}=0
.

Proof.

We recall that any
R
-module
M
has the injective
R
-homomorphism

\varphi_{M}:M\to\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})),E_{R}(E/\mathfrak{m}))

for which
(\varphi_{M}(x))(f)=f(x)
for all
x\in M
and
f\in\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))
. (For example, see
[
1
, Remarks 10.2.2 (i)]
.)

We now take an
R
-module
M\in{}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}
.
Since our assumption says that the module
E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})
lies in
\mathcal{C}
, we have the equality
\mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M,E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m}))=0
.
Therefore, the injective map
\varphi_{M}
gives the equality
M=0
.

Next suppose that
\mathcal{S}
is a stable Serre subcategory with a non-zero
R
-module
S
.
Then we can take a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\in\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(S)
with an injective map
R/\mathfrak{p}\to S
.
Additionally, we have the natural surjective map
R/\mathfrak{p}\to R/\mathfrak{m}
.
We note that the stable Serre subcategory
\mathcal{S}
is closed under submodules, quotient modules, and injective hulls.
Hence, we obtain that
E_{R}(R/\mathfrak{m})\in\mathcal{S}
.
Consequently, the above argument yields the equality
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}=0
.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
R
be a local ring with maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
and
(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{F})
be an
\mathcal{S}
-torsion theory.
If the heart

(1)

The heart
\mathcal{S}
is left canonical.

(2)

One has the equality
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2)
:
Since the injective
R

lies in the heart
\mathcal{S}
, Lemma
5.1
yields that the equality
{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S}=0
.
Lemma
2.4
(3) deduces that the left canonical heart
\mathcal{S}
provides the equalities
\mathcal{T}=(\mathcal{T}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{S})*(\mathcal{T}\cap\mathcal{S})=0*\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}

(2)\Rightarrow(1)

We note that any subcategory
\mathcal{C}
has the equality
\mathcal{C}\cap{}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}=0
.
Indeed, for each module

\mathrm{Hom}_{R}({}^{\perp}\mathcal{C},\mathcal{C})=0

We thus have

If we suppose that the equality

.
Therefore, the heart
\mathcal{S}
is left canonical.
∎

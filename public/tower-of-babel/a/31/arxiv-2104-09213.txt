A computational proof of the existence of the Dual Isogeny
Markos Karameris

Abstract
For E E an elliptic curve over a perfect field K K , we present a proof of the existence of the dual isogeny ϕ ^ \hat{\phi} using computational methods linked to Vélu’s formulae instead of the standard Galois correspondence method.



1
Preliminaries and Notation

Let
E(K)
denote an elliptic curve over a perfect field
K
.
For simplicicty we assume that
char(K)\neq 2
or
3
. We will mostly preserve the notation of [
References
].

To a finite subgroup of
E
we can associate an isogeny
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
. This isogeny is not uniquely determined but if we require the isogeny to be normalized then we do get a unique morphism.

Definition 1

If
\omega^{\prime}
is the invariant differential of
E^{\prime}
then the pullback
\phi^{*}(\omega^{\prime})
is a holomorphic differential of
E
and since the space
\Omega_{E}
is
1-
dimensional we have
\phi^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=c\omega
for some
c\in K
. We call
\phi
normalized iff
c=1
.

The proof of the existence is presented in [
References
] in two parts:

Proposition 1

Let
\phi:E\to E_{1}
and
\psi:E\to E_{2}
be two isogenies with
\phi
seperable and
ker(\phi)\subset ker(\psi)
. Then there exists a unique isogeny
\lambda:E_{1}\to E_{2}
with
\psi=\lambda\circ\phi
.

The theorem is then proved in Theorem 6.1 using the above proposition for
\psi=[m]
. Notice that requiring the isogeny to be seperable is natural as an inseperable isogeny cannot be normal.
The primary tools we will be using are Vélu’s formulae:
Let
G
be a subgroup of
E(K)
and write
P=(x_{P},y_{P})
then as in [
References
] the formula we will be using is:

\phi(P)=(x(P)+\sum_{Q\in{G}\neq{O}}x(P+Q)-x(Q),y(P)+\sum_{Q\in{G}\neq{O}}y(P+Q)-y(Q)),P\not\in G

(1)

where we also set
\phi(P)=O,\forall P\in G
by definition.

Remark 1

The above definition is invariant under the action of
G
and it’s kernel is exactly
G
as intended. It can only be used to describe a normalized isogeny
\phi
which follows from the uniqueness property.

2
Existence of the Dual Isogeny

The first result we will prove is the same as that of Proposition
1
but in the case of normalized elliptic curve isogenies
\phi,\psi
.

Proposition 2

Let
\phi:E\to E_{1}
and
\psi:E\to E_{2}
be two normalized isogenies with
ker(\phi)\subset ker(\psi)
. Then there exists a unique isogeny
\lambda:E_{1}\to E_{2}
with
\psi=\lambda\circ\phi
.

Proof

If
\phi:E\to E_{1}
is normalized then we have that
\phi(P)=(\sum_{Q\in{G^{\prime}}}(x_{P+Q}-x_{Q}),\sum_{Q\in{G^{\prime}}}(y_{P+Q}-y_{Q}))
. But then

\lambda(P)=(\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(x_{P+\phi(g)}-\phi_{x}(g)),\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(y_{P+\phi(g)}-\phi_{y}(g)))
.
We observe that:
x_{\phi(P)+\phi(g)}=x_{\phi(P+g)}
and similarly for
y
we get:
\lambda\circ\phi(P)=(\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(\phi_{x}(P+g)-\phi_{x}(g)),\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(\phi_{y}(P+g)-\phi_{y}(g)))=\\
(\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(\sum_{Q\in{G^{\prime}}}(x_{P+g+Q}-x_{g+Q})),\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}(\sum_{Q\in{G^{\prime}}}(y_{P+g+Q}-y_{g+Q}))=\\
(\sum_{Q\in{G}}(x_{P+Q}-x_{Q}),\sum_{Q\in{G}}(y_{P+Q}-y_{Q}))

(for a homomorphism
h
with normal subgroup
G^{\prime}
,
\sum_{g\in{G}}h(g)=\sum_{g\in{G/G^{\prime}}}\sum_{Q\in{G}}h(q+Q)
). And thus we obtain a normal isogeny with kernel
G
which is also unique as it is normalized with
\lambda\circ\phi(P)=\psi(P)
.
The fact
\lambda
is an isogeny
E_{1}\to E_{2}
is immediate

\lambda(O)=\lambda(\phi(O))=\psi(O)=O
and

\lambda(E_{2})=\lambda(\phi(E_{1}))=\psi(E_{1})=E_{3}
as
\phi,\psi
are onto.

So far we restricted our results to normal isogenies however:

Proposition 3

For every seperable isogeny
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
there exists an isomorphism
i:E^{\prime}\to E^{\prime\prime}
such that
i\circ\phi
is normalized

Proof

for a proof see [
References
].

We are now ready to prove the original statement of Proposition
1
. for seperable isogenies.

Proof

Let
i_{\phi},i_{\psi}
be the corresponding isomorphisms such that
\phi^{\prime}=i_{\phi}\circ\phi
and
\psi^{\prime}=i_{\psi}\circ\psi
are both normalized. Then by Proposition
2
there is a
\lambda
such that
\psi^{\prime}=\lambda\circ\phi^{\prime}
. Then
i_{\psi}^{-1}\circ\lambda\circ i_{\phi}
is the required isogeny
E_{1}\to E_{2}
.

Remark 2

The fact that we require both
\phi
and
\psi
to be seperable does not cause any problems as in the case of inseperable
\psi
we can simply write
\psi=\pi^{n}\circ\psi_{sep}
where
\pi
is the Frobenius endomorphism (this is possible because we are in a perfect field or even over a finite field of characteristic
p
essentially). Applying our result to
\phi,\psi_{sep}
we get an isogeny
\lambda:\lambda\circ\phi=\psi_{sep}\implies\pi^{n}\circ\lambda\circ\phi=\psi
. The required isogeny is thus
\lambda^{\prime}=\pi^{n}\circ\lambda
.

The above remark settles Proposition
1
.

3
Computational Aspects

The proposed approach also offers a way to compute the dual isogeny directly using Vélu’s formulae. Let
\phi:E\to E/G
be an isogeny over
K
, then the required steps are:

1.

compute
\phi_{sep}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\phi=\phi_{sep}\circ\pi^{n}

2.

compute
\hat{\pi}
via the
[p]
endomorphism

3.

compute
[m]_{sep}
and
e\in\mathbb{N}
such that
[m]=\pi^{e}\circ[m]_{sep}

4.

normalize
\phi_{sep}
using
i_{\phi}
into
\phi_{norm}

5.

normalize
[m]_{sep}
using
i_{[m]}
into
[m]_{norm}

6.

using Vélu’s formulae this gives
\lambda:\lambda\circ\phi_{norm}=[m]_{norm}

7.

set
\widehat{\phi_{sep}}=i_{[m]}^{-1}\circ\pi^{e}\circ\lambda\circ i_{\phi}
, the dual isogeny is
\widehat{\phi}={\hat{\pi}}^{n}\circ\widehat{\phi_{sep}}

Seperating the seperable and inseperable part is a straightforward procedure: writing
\phi
in terms of rational functions it simply means finding the greatest power of
p
dividing all the exponents. The second part is also immediate from
References
, simply write
[p]=[p]_{sep}\circ\pi^{k}
and take
\hat{\pi}=[p]_{s}ep\circ\pi^{k-1}
. The only remaining step is the normalization proccess which we will describe explicitly.

Remark 3

Given an isogeny
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
, we are looking for an isomorphism
i_{\phi}:E^{\prime}\to E^{\prime\prime}
such that
(i_{\phi}\circ\phi)^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=\omega
. Writing
\omega=\frac{dx}{2y+a_{1}x+a_{3}},\omega^{\prime}=\frac{dx}{2y+{a_{1}}^{\prime}x+{a_{3}}^{\prime}}
and
\phi(x,y)=(r(x),s(x)y+z(x))
in rational form, abusing notation we get the equation
\frac{d(i_{\phi}(r(x)))}{2i_{\phi}(s(x)y+z(x))+{a_{1}}^{\prime}i_{\phi}(r(x))+{a_{3}}^{\prime}}=\frac{dx}{2y+a_{1}x+a_{3}}

We can tackle this problem by noting that
i_{\phi}(x,y)=(u^{2}x+t_{1},u^{3}y+t_{2}u^{2}x+t_{3})
for some
t_{i}\in K
which leads us to the following lemma:

Lemma 1

Let
i_{\phi}:E\to E^{\prime}
be an isomorphism as above, then
i_{\phi}^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=\frac{1}{u}\omega

Proof

The proof is a direct computation along with the observation that the nominator has a term of the form
u^{3}
in the denominator and
u^{2}
in the nominator.

Using the Lemma above we immediately obtain the desired result:

•

for the isogeny
[m]_{sep}
we simply set
u=(\frac{m}{p^{e}})^{-1}
where
p^{e+1}\not|m

•

for
\phi_{sep}
we compute
\phi_{sep}^{*}(\omega^{\prime})=c\omega
and set
u=c^{-1}

Combining these results enables us to compute explicitly the dual isogeny without resorting to the usual method of computing via the preimage of the arbitary isogeny
\phi
.

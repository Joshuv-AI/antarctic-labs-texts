Comments on A note on stability of fractional logistic maps , Appl. Math. Lett. 125 (2022) 107787 Journal: Applied Mathematics Letters
Mark Edelman

Abstract
In this paper we show that the stability analysis in the paper A note on stability of fractional logistic maps , Appl. Math. Lett. 125 (2022) 107787 is incorrect and repeat a proof of a theorem on convergence of a convolution of the product of a converging series with a converging sequence. We also mention that the paper commented on should have been edited more carefully.



1
The main results of the article commented on

The paper commented on

is an attempt to obtain at least some of the sufficient stbility conditions for at least one fractional map. This is an important problem, which is not solved yet.

Results of the prior investigations

The main difficulty here is the slow,
as a power law (as
n^{-\alpha}
, where
n
is the number of iterations and

0<\alpha<1

[

(see Fig. 6.8). The first attempt to derive stability conditions for fractional maps was done in
[
2
]
for the fractional standard map. The method of generating functions was proposed in
[
2
]
to analyze the stability of fractional maps but the stability conditions for fractional maps have never been obtained.

It is possible to find the equations which define asymptotically periodic points for any fractional maps (see
[
8
]
). For the asymptotically period two (
T=2
) trajectories in fractional logistic map, the equations were obtained in
[
9
]
(see Eq. (60)–Eq. (62)). These equations do not have real solutions for the values of the parameter
of the logistic map
\lambda

\displaystyle 1-\lambda_{C1s}(\alpha)<\lambda<1+\lambda_{C1s}(\alpha),

(1)

where
\lambda_{C1s}(\alpha)

\lambda

[
9

in Section

where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

1.2
The major flaws

The authors rewrite the linearized equations for the perturbations
x_{n}=X_{n}-x_{f}
from the fixed points
x_{f}
in the form

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}[a_{0}(n-k,\alpha)-a_{0}(n-k-1,\alpha)]x_{k}+a_{0}(0,\alpha)x_{n},

(3)

where

\displaystyle a_{0}(n,\alpha)=1+\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)(n+1)^{1-\alpha}}.

(4)

The authors show that if
x_{0}
is positive, then
x_{n}
is positive and bounded and show that

\displaystyle a_{0}(n,\alpha)-a_{0}(n-1,\alpha)\rightarrow 0\ \ {\rm when}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty.

(5)

Then they claim (without a proof) that for large values of
n
the total in Eq. (
3
) may be ignored and that in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=a_{0}(n,\alpha)x_{n}.

(6)

If true, this would imply the exponential convergence of trajectories to the fixed point and non-zero Lyapunov exponent of the fractional logistic map and contradict to all known numerical results. To show that the assumption made by the authors is incorrect we will employ the following theorem, implicitly proven in
[
8
]
(we present the explicit proof in the following section):

Theorem 1.1

If

\displaystyle\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}x_{k}=X,\ \ \lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N}a_{k}=S,

(7)

and the series is converging absolutely, then

\displaystyle\Sigma=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{N}x_{N-k}a_{k}=XS,

(8)

where
k,N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
x_{k},a_{k},X,S,\Sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
S=\infty
, and
\Sigma
is finite, then
X=0
.

Assuming that the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
of
x_{n}
exists (equal to
X
), the limiting value of the sum in Eq. (
3
) can be written as

\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n-1}_{k=0}[a_{0}(n-k,\alpha)-a_{0}(n-k-1,\alpha)]x_{n-k}=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n}_{k=1}[a_{0}(k,\alpha)

\displaystyle-a_{0}(k-1,\alpha)]x_{k}=\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}X\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\sum^{n}_{k=1}[(k+1)^{\alpha-1}-k^{\alpha-1}]

\displaystyle=-X\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}=X[1-a_{0}(0,\alpha)].

(9)

Then, instead of Eq. (
6
), in the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
we will have a trivial equality

\displaystyle x_{n+1}=x_{n}

(10)

consistent with the power-law convergence.

2
Proof of the theorem

A proof of the second statement of Theorem
1.1
is well described in
[
8
]
between Eq. (20) and Eq. (23). So, here we will prove only the first statement.

Let us consider the difference

\displaystyle\Bigl|\Sigma-\sum_{k=0}^{N}x_{N-k}a_{k}\Bigr|=\Bigl|XS-\sum^{N_{1}}_{k=0}x_{N-k}a_{k}-\sum^{N}_{k=N_{1}+1}x_{N-k}a_{k}\Bigr|,

(11)

where
N>N_{1}
. If for
\forall<\varepsilon
we may find
N_{l}
, such that this difference is less than
\varepsilon
,
\forall N>N_{l}
, then the theorem is proven.

According to our assumption,
x_{n}
is converging and it must be bounded:
|X-x_{n}|<C1
. Absolute convergence of the total
\Sigma_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|
implies that for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists
N_{1}
such that
\Sigma_{k=N_{1}+1}^{N}|a_{k}|<\varepsilon/(3C1)
,
\forall N>N_{1}
.

Because
x_{n}
is converging, for any
\varepsilon>0
we may find
N_{l1}
such that
|X-x_{N-k}|<\varepsilon/(3\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|)
,
\forall N>N_{l1}
and
k\leq N_{1}
.
We may also find
N_{l2}
such that
\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}|a_{k}|<\varepsilon/(3*|X|)
. Now, for
N>\max\{N_{l1},N_{l2}\}
, the following chain of inequalities will hold

\displaystyle\Bigl|XS-\sum^{N_{1}}_{k=0}x_{N-k}a_{k}-\sum^{N}_{k=N_{1}+1}x_{N-k}a_{k}\Bigr|=\Bigl|\sum_{k=0}^{N_{1}}(X-x_{N-k})a_{k}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=N_{1}+1}^{N}(X-x_{N-k})a_{k}+X\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}a_{k}\Bigr|<\Bigl|\sum_{k=0}^{N_{1}}(X-x_{N-k})a_{k}\Bigr|

\displaystyle+\Bigl|\sum_{k=N_{1}+1}^{N}(X-x_{N-k})a_{k}\Bigr|+|X|\Bigl|\sum_{k=N+1}^{\infty}a_{k}\Bigr|

(12)

\displaystyle<\frac{\varepsilon}{3\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}|a_{k}|}\sum_{k=0}^{N_{1}}|a_{k}|+C1\frac{\varepsilon}{3C1}+|X|\frac{\varepsilon}{3|X|}\leq\varepsilon.

(13)

This completes the proof of the theorem.

3
Conclusion

From the investigation of the period-two points (see Eq. (62) in

[
9
]
and Eq. (49) in
[
8
]
) and numerical simulations,
the
x=0
fixed point is stable when

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[(2k+1)^{\alpha-1}-(2k+2)^{\alpha-1}\Bigr]}<\lambda<1

(14)

and the
x=(\lambda-1)/\lambda
fixed point is stable when

\displaystyle 1<\lambda<1+\frac{\Gamma(\alpha)}{\sum^{\infty}_{k=0}\Bigl[(2k+1)^{\alpha-1}-(2k+2)^{\alpha-1}\Bigr]},

(15)

where the total in the denominator, called
S_{2}
in
[
8
]
, has values between approximately 0.7, for
\alpha=0
, and 0.5,
for
\alpha=1
(see Table 3 in
[
8
]
). The intervals of stability are much wider than those considered in the paper commented on.

The methods that were previously used to analyze the stability of fractional systems include applications of generating functions
[
2
]
and
\mathbb{Z}
-transform (which works better in the case of fractional difference equations, see, e.g.,
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
). The generating function
\tilde{X}(t)
is defined as

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}x_{n}t^{n}.

(16)

Multiplication of Eq. (
3
) by
t^{n}
and summation from zero to infinity gives

\displaystyle\tilde{X}(t)=\frac{x_{0}}{1-\Bigl[\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\tilde{J}(t)+\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigr)\Bigr]t},

(17)

where

\displaystyle\tilde{J}(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\Bigl[(j+1)^{\alpha-1}-j^{\alpha-1}\Bigr]t^{j}.

(18)

When
\lambda=1
, the coefficients of Taylor series of
\tilde{X}(t)
are
x_{n}=x_{0}
and it seems that this is a critical value at which stability conditions change but there is no proof. The
\mathbb{Z}
-transform is defined as

\displaystyle\bar{X}(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}x_{n}t^{-n}.

(19)

From Eq. (
3
)

\displaystyle\bar{X}(z)=\frac{x_{0}}{1-\Bigl[\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\bar{J}(z)+\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda-1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\Bigr)\Bigr]z^{-1}},

(20)

where

\displaystyle\bar{J}(z)=\sum^{\infty}_{j=1}\Bigl[(j+1)^{\alpha-1}-j^{\alpha-1}\Bigr]z^{-j}.

(21)

The stability conditions Eq. (
14
) and the power-law convergence are not obtained yet neither from the Taylor expansion of the generating function Eq. (
17
) nor from the application of the inverse
\mathbb{Z}
-transform to
\bar{X}(z)
Eq. (
20
).

The authors brought up an important problem. They made a mistake but, as I know (from the private communication with the authors), they found a different way to investigate stability of the fixed points within the interval
1-\Gamma(\alpha)<\lambda<1+\Gamma(\alpha)
. The original paper should not have been edited more carefully to avoid mistakes.

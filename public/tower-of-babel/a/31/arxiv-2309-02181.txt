Null-controllability for a fourth order parabolic equation under general boundary conditions
Emmanuel Wend-Benedo Zongo, Luc Robbiano

Abstract
In this paper, we consider a fourth order inner-controlled parabolic equation on an open bounded subset of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} , or a smooth compact manifold with boundary, along with general boundary operators fulfilling the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition. We derive a spectral inequality for the solution of the parabolic system that yields a null-controllability result. The spectral inequality is a consequence of an interpolation inequality obtained via a Carleman inequality for the bi-Laplace operator under the considered boundary conditions. Keywords: Carleman estimates; spectral inequality; Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition; interpolation inequality. AMS 2020 subject classification: 35B45; 35J30; 35J40; 74K20; 93D15.



1
Introduction

In this paper, our aim is to study the interior null-controllability for a fourth order parabolic equation. A kind of fourth order parabolic equations describes the epitaxial growth of nanoscale thin films which has recently received increasing interest in materials science because compositions like
YBa_{2}Cu_{3}O_{7-\delta}
(YBCO) are expected to be high-temperature super-conducting and could be used in the design of semi-conductors (see for instance
[
7
]
and the references therein). Therefore, studying the features of fourth order parabolic equations has realistic meanings.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded connected open subset in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, or a smooth bounded connected
d
-dimensional manifold, with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
.
We consider the following controlled parabolic equation

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y+\Delta^{2}y=\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma}v~~~~\text{in}~(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\Omega,\\
B_{1}y_{|\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega}=B_{2}y_{|\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\partial\Omega}=0,\\
y_{|t=0}=y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega).\end{cases}

(1.1)

Here,
\Sigma
is a nonempty open subset of
\Omega
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
denote two boundary differential operators. The function
v
is the control and lies in
L^{2}((0,\infty)\times\Omega).
It only acts on the solution
y
in
\Sigma.
The function
y
may represent a scalled film height and the term
\Delta^{2}y
represents the capillarity-driven surface diffusion (see
[
3
]
).

The two boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are of order
k_{j}
,
j=1,2
respectively, yet at most of order
3
in the direction
normal to the boundary
\partial\Omega
. They
are chosen such that the two following properties are fulfilled:

1.

the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary condition holds (this condition is fully
described in what follows);

2.

along with the homogeneous boundary conditions given above the
bi-Laplace operator is self-adjoint and nonnegative.

We are concerned with the question of null-controllability for this controlled parabolic equation which is the following:

For a given initial data
y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),
and a given time
T>0,
can one find
v\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Sigma)
such
y(T)=0?

The answer to this question rely on the derivation of a spectral inequality. With the second property, associated with the operator is then a Hilbert basis
(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
L^{2}(\Omega).
In the case of “clamped” boundary condition (
B_{1}y_{|\partial\Omega}=y_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}y_{|\partial\Omega}=\partial_{\nu}y_{|\partial\Omega}
) the following spectral inequality was proved by J. Le Rousseau and L. Robbiano in
[
9
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(spectral inequality for the “clamped” bi-Laplace operator)
.

Let
\varnothing
be an open subset of
\Omega
. There exists
C>0
such that

\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ce^{C\mu^{1/4}}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\varnothing)},~~\mu>0,~~y\in\Span\{\varphi_{j};~\mu_{j}\leq\mu\},~~~\text{with}~~\Delta^{2}\varphi_{j}=\mu_{j}\varphi_{j}.

The proof of this theorem is based on a Carleman inequality for the augmented fourth-order elliptic operator

D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2}
, that is, after the addition of a variable
s.
We are interested in proving such a spectral
inequality for general boundary condition, that is, the operator
\Delta^{2}
along with the general boundary
operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
satisfy the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition.
By considering for instance, the boundary operators
B_{1}(x,D)=D_{d}^{2}+\alpha R(x,D^{\prime})
and
B_{2}(x,D)=D_{d}
,
where
D_{d}=-i\partial_{d},

\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
and
R(x,D^{\prime})
a tangential elliptic operator of order
2,
we remark
that having the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(\Delta^{2},B_{1},B_{2})
does not imply that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds true

(D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2},B_{1},B_{2}).

With this approach, some boundary conditions that satisfy the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition cannot be used to prove the Carleman inequality required to obtain the spectral inequality. However, some boundary conditions allow this, for instance the clamped boundary condition. So, we have determined the boundary conditions allowing to prove such Carleman inequality and thus the spectral inequality.

Our first goal is thus the derivation of the Carleman inequality for
the operator
Q=D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2}
near the boundary under the boundary conditions given by
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
.

Then, from the Carleman estimate one deduces an interpolation inequality
for the operator
Q
in the case of the prescribed boundary
conditions. The spectral inequality then follows from this interpolation inequality.

Finally as an application of the spectral inequality one deduces a null-controllability result. The method we used is called the Lebeau-Robbiano method which is a quantitative unique continuation property for the sum of eigenfunctions of the operator
\Delta^{2}.

Null-controllability of a fourth order parabolic equation in dimension greater or equal to two was studied in
[
3
,
12
]
under the boundary conditions
B_{1}y_{|\partial\Omega}=y_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}y_{|\partial\Omega}=\Delta y_{|\partial\Omega},
see also the references therein for some interesting results concerning the null-controllability of this kind of equations. In
[
3
]
, the authors proved the null-controllability of system (
1.1
) (with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions on the solution and the laplacian of the solution), by establishing an observability inequality for the non-homogeneous adjoint system associated to (
1.1
), obtained from a Carleman estimate. The null interior controllability for a fourth order parabolic equation under the boundary conditions
B_{1}y_{|\partial\Omega}=y_{|\partial\Omega}
and
B_{2}y_{|\partial\Omega}=\Delta y_{|\partial\Omega},
obtained in
[
12
]
is based on Lebeau-Robbiano method.

1.1
On Carleman estimate for higher-order elliptic operators

If
B
is an elliptic operator of even order
k,
and
\varphi
is a Carleman weight function such that the couple
(B,\varphi)
satisfies the so-called sub-ellipticity condition, then a Carleman estimate can be obtained, even at the boundary, for instance from the results of
[
1
]
.

If
k\geq 4,
it is however quite natural not to have sub-ellipticity, in particular if
B
is a product, say
B=B_{1}B_{2}.
Denote by
b,b_{1}
and
b_{2}
the principal symbols of
B,B_{1}
and
B_{2}
respectively. The conjugated operator
B_{\varphi}=e^{\tau\varphi}Be^{-\tau\varphi}
reads
B_{\varphi}=B_{1,\varphi}B_{2,\varphi}
with
B_{\ell,\varphi}=e^{\tau\varphi}B_{\ell}e^{-\tau\varphi}
for
\ell=1,2.
If the semi-classical characteristic set
Char(B_{1,\varphi})\cap Char(B_{2,\varphi})\neq\emptyset,
then the sub-ellipticity property fails to holds. We recall that the semi-classical characteristic set of a differential operator
B
with principal symbol
b(\varrho)
is defined as

Char(B)=\{~\varrho=(z,\zeta,\tau)~:~(\zeta,\tau)\neq(0,0)~;~b(\varrho)=0\}.

In fact, if
b_{\varphi},b_{1,\varphi}
and
b_{2,\varphi}
are respectively the semi-classical symbols of
B_{\varphi},B_{1,\varphi}
and
B_{2,\varphi},
that is,

b_{\varphi}=b(z,\zeta+i\tau d\varphi(z))~~\text{and}~~b_{\ell,\varphi}=b_{\ell}(z,\zeta+i\tau d\varphi(z)),~\ell=1,2,

we can write

\{\overline{b}_{\varphi},b_{\varphi}\}=\{\overline{b_{1,\varphi}b_{2,\varphi}},b_{1,\varphi}b_{2,\varphi}\}=|b_{1,\varphi}|^{2}\{\overline{b}_{2,\varphi},b_{2,\varphi}\}+|b_{2,\varphi}|^{2}\{\overline{b}_{1,\varphi},b_{1,\varphi}\}+f|b_{1,\varphi}||b_{2,\varphi}|

for some function
f
and where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes the Poisson bracket.

Hence
\{\overline{b}_{\varphi},b_{\varphi}\}
vanishes if
b_{1,\varphi}=b_{2,\varphi}=0
and thus the sub-ellipticity property cannot hold for
B.
Observe that in this example, we have
d_{z,\zeta}b(z,\zeta+i\tau d\varphi(z))=0
if
b_{1}(z,\zeta+i\tau d\varphi(z))=b_{2}(z,\zeta+i\tau d\varphi(z))=0.
In such cases of symbol “flatness”, the Carleman estimate we can derive for
B
exhibits at least a loss of one full derivative.

1.2
Geometrical setting

On
\Omega
we consider a Riemannian metric
\mathsf{g}_{x}=(\mathsf{g}_{ij}(x))
, with
associated cometric
(\mathsf{g}^{ij}(x))=(\mathsf{g}_{x})^{-1}
.
It stands as a bilinear form that act on vector fields,

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}_{x}(u_{x},v_{x})=\mathsf{g}_{ij}(x)u_{x}^{i}v_{x}^{j},\quad u_{x}=u_{x}^{i}\partial_{x_{i}},\ v_{x}=v_{x}^{i}\partial_{x_{i}}.

For
x\in\partial\Omega
we denote by
\nu_{x}
the unit outward
pointing
normal vector
at
x
, unitary in the sense of the
metric
\mathsf{g}
, that is

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}_{x}(\nu_{x},\nu_{x})=1\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mathsf{g}_{x}(\nu_{x},u_{x})=0\quad\forall u_{x}\in T_{x}\partial\Omega.

We denote by
\partial_{\nu}
the associated derivative at the boundary,
that is,
\partial_{\nu}f(x)=\nu_{x}(f)
.
We also denote by
n_{x}
the unit outward
pointing
conormal vector
at
x
, that is,

n_{x}=\nu_{x}^{\flat}
, that is,
(n_{x})_{i}=\mathsf{g}_{ij}\nu_{x}^{j}
.

Near a boundary point, we shall often use normal geodesic coordinates
where
\Omega
is locally given by
\{x_{d}>0\}
and the
metric
\mathsf{g}
takes the form

\displaystyle\mathsf{g}=dx^{d}\otimes dx^{d}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d-1}\mathsf{g}_{ij}dx^{i}\otimes dx^{j}.

Then, the vector field
\nu_{x}
is locally given by
(0,\dots,0,-1)
. The same for the one form
n_{x}
.

Normal geodesic coordinates allow us to locally formulate
boundary problems in a half-space geometry. We write

\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ x_{d}>0\}\qquad\text{where}\ x=(x^{\prime},x_{d})\ \text{with}\ x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},x_{d}\in\mathbb{R}.

We shall naturally denote its closure by
\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}
, that is,

\overline{\mathbb{R}^{d}_{+}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};x_{d}\geq 0\}
.

The Laplace-Beltrami operator
is given by

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\mathsf{g}}f)(x)=(\det\mathsf{g}_{x})^{-1/2}\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\partial_{x_{i}}\big((\det\mathsf{g}_{x})^{1/2}\mathsf{g}^{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{j}}f\big)(x).

in local coordinates. Its principal part is given by

\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\mathsf{g}^{ij}(x)\partial_{x_{i}}\partial_{x_{j}}
and
its principal symbol by
\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\mathsf{g}^{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}
.
The bi-Laplace operator is
P=\Delta^{2}_{\mathsf{g}}
.

In the main text of the
article we shall write
\Delta
,
\Delta^{2}
in place of
\Delta_{\mathsf{g}}
,
\Delta_{\mathsf{g}}^{2}
.

.

If

So, in the proof of the spectral inequality, we may therefore assume that

1.4
Some perspectives

In the light of the results obtained here, it would be of interest to consider more general polyharmonic operators on
\Omega
with more general Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro conditions. More precisely, one can replace

by

for

along with

that satisfy the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro boundary condition. It would also be very interesting to investigate the boundary null-controllability of system (
1.1
) under a specify boundary conditions, let say in the case

and
B_{2}y=\Delta y
or
\partial_{n}y
on
\partial\Omega.

A class of semi-classical symbols

We introduce the following class of tangential symbols depending on

Definition 2.1

As opposed to the usual semi-classical symbols, some regularity is required with respect to the semi-classical parameter that is a vector of

The above class of symbols will not be used as such to define a class of pseudo-differential operators but rather to generate other classes of symbols and associated operators in a more refined semi-classical culculus that we present below.

.

We have
\tilde{\tau}=\tau\gamma\varphi_{\gamma,\varepsilon}\in S(\tilde{\tau},g)\cap S(\tilde{\tau},g_{\mathsf{T}}).

Proof.

We first remark that only differentiation with respect to

2.2

A differential quadradic form acts on a function and involves
differentiations of various degrees of the function. One can associate
to these forms a symbol and positivity inequality that can be obtained in
the form of a Gårding inequality.
Such forms appear in proofs of Carleman estimates in the pioneering work
of Hörmander
[
4
, Section 8.2]
.

Here differential quadradic forms are
defined at the boundary. The result we
present here without proof can be found in
[
1
]
and
[
11
]

Definition 2.5
.

\xi,

3.1

f(\omega_{d})
with complex coefficients, there exists
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and a polynomial function
h(\omega_{d})
with complex coefficients such that, for all
\omega_{d}\in\mathbb{C},

f(\omega_{d})=\sum\limits_{1\leq j\leq 2}c_{j}b_{j}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})+h(\omega_{d})p^{+}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d}).

We say that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
at
x\in\partial\Omega
if it holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
for all
\omega^{\prime}\in T^{*}_{x}\partial\Omega
with
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0.

The general boundary differential operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are then given by

B_{\ell}(x,D)=\sum\limits_{0\leq j\leq\min(3,k_{\ell})}B_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,D^{\prime})(i\partial_{\nu})^{j},\qquad\ell=1,2,

with
B_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,D^{\prime})
differential operators acting in the
tangential variables.
We denote by
b_{1}(x,\omega)
and
b_{2}(x,\omega)
the principal symbols of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
respectively. For
(x,\omega^{\prime})\in T^{*}\partial\Omega,
we set

b_{\ell}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})=\sum\limits_{0\leq j\leq\min(3,k_{\ell})}b_{\ell}^{k_{\ell}-j}(x,\omega^{\prime})\omega_{d}^{j},\qquad\ell=1,2.

(3.1)

We recall that the principal symbol of
P
is given by

p(x,\omega)=|\omega|^{4}_{\mathsf{g}}.
One thus has

p(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})=p(x,\omega^{\prime}-\omega_{d}n_{x})=\big(\omega_{d}^{2}+|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}}^{2}\big)^{2}.

Therefore

p(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})=(\omega_{d}-i|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}})^{2}(\omega_{d}+i|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}})^{2}
. According
to the above definition we set

p^{+}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})=(\omega_{d}-i|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}})^{2}.
Thus, the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
with
\omega^{\prime}\neq 0
if
and only if for any function
f(\omega_{d})
the complex number
i|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}}

is a root of the polynomial function
\omega_{d}\mapsto f(\omega_{d})-c_{1}b_{1}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})-c_{2}b_{2}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d})
and its
derivative for some
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
. This leads to the
following determinant condition

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
P=\Delta^{2}
on
\Omega
,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
be two boundary differential
operators. If
x\in\partial\Omega
,
\omega^{\prime}\in T_{x}^{*}\partial\Omega
, with

\omega^{\prime}\neq 0
, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
if and only if

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\omega_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\omega_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\omega^{\prime},\omega_{d}=i|\omega^{\prime}|_{\mathsf{g}})\neq 0.

(3.2)

Remark 3.3
.

With the determinant condition and homogeneity, we note that if the
Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\omega^{\prime})
it also holds
in a conic neighborhood of
(x,\omega^{\prime})
by continuity. If it holds at
x\in\Omega,
it also holds in a neighborhood of
x
.

3.2
Formulation in normal geodesic coordinates

Near a boundary point
x\in\partial\Omega
, we shall use normal
geodesic coordinates. Then the principal symbols of
\Delta
and

\Delta^{2}
are given by
\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime})

(\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}))^{2}
respectively, where

is the

of order 2, with

r(x,\xi^{\prime})=\sum\limits_{1\leq i,j\leq d-1}\mathsf{g}^{ij}(x)\xi^{\prime}_{i}\xi^{\prime}_{j}\ \ \text{and}\ \ r(x,\xi^{\prime})\geq C|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}.

Here
\mathsf{g}^{ij}
is the inverse of the metric
\mathsf{g}_{ij}.
Below, we shall
often write
|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=r(x,\xi^{\prime})
and we shall also write

|\xi|^{2}_{x}=\xi_{d}^{2}+r(x,\xi^{\prime}),
for
\xi=(\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}).

If
b_{1}(x,\xi)
and
b_{2}(x,\xi)
are the principal symbols of the

B_{1}
and
B_{2}
in the normal geodesic
coordinates then the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
with
P=\Delta^{2}
at
(x,\xi^{\prime})
reads

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\neq 0,

(3.3)

if
|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}\neq 0
according to Lemma
3.2
. If the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at some
x^{0}
,
because of homogeneity, there exists

such that

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|(x^{0},\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\geq C_{0}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1},\qquad\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

(3.4)

3.3
Boundary operators yielding symmetry

We give some examples of pairs of boundary operators
B_{1},B_{2}
that

1.

fulfill the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition,

2.

yield symmetry for the bi-Laplace operator
P=\Delta^{2}
, that is,

\displaystyle(Pu,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,Pv)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

for
u,v\in H^{4}(\Omega)
such that
B_{j}u_{|\partial\Omega}=B_{j}v_{|\partial\Omega}=0
,
j=1,2
.

We first recall that following Green formula

\displaystyle(\Delta u,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,\Delta v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(3.5)

which applied twice gives

(Pu,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,Pv)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+T(u,v)

with

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(\Delta u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},\Delta v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}\Delta v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(3.6)

Using normal geodesic coordinates in a neighborhood of the whole boundary

\partial\Omega
allows one to write
\Delta=\partial_{n}^{2}+\Delta^{\prime}

where
\Delta^{\prime}
is the resulting Laplace operator on the boundary,
that is, associated with the trace of the metric on

\partial\Omega
. Since
\Delta^{\prime}
is selfadjoint on
\partial\Omega

this allows one to write formula (
3.3
)
in the alternative forms

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{n}^{3}u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-((\partial_{n}^{2}+2\Delta^{\prime})u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},(\partial_{n}^{2}+2\Delta^{\prime})v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}^{3}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)},

(3.7)

or

\displaystyle T(u,v)

\displaystyle=((\partial_{n}^{3}+2\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{n})u_{|\partial\Omega},v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(\partial_{n}^{2}u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+(\partial_{n}u_{|\partial\Omega},\partial_{n}^{2}v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}-(u_{|\partial\Omega},(\partial_{n}^{3}+2\Delta^{\prime}\partial_{n})v_{|\partial\Omega})_{L^{2}(\partial\Omega)}.

(3.8)

With this computations, one can provide a list of examples in which the boundary operators yield symmetry and fulfill the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition. This can be found in

Example 3.4

3.4
Some properties of the bi-Laplace operator

Let
(\mathsf{P},D(\mathsf{P}))
be the unbounded operator on

with

\displaystyle D(\mathsf{P})=\big\{u\in H^{4}(\Omega);\ B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=0\big\},

and given by
\mathsf{P}u=\Delta^{2}u
for

.
The boundary operators
B_{1}

and
B_{2}
of orders
k_{j}
,
j=1,2
, less than or equal to
3
in the normal direction are chosen so that

(i)

the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition of Definition

(P,B_{1},B_{2})

(ii)

the operator

\displaystyle(Pu,v)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=(u,Pv)_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for

such that

\partial\Omega
,

With the assumed Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition the operator

\displaystyle L:H^{4}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\to L^{2}(\Omega)\oplus H^{7/2-k_{1}}(\partial\Omega)\oplus H^{7/2-k_{2}}(\partial\Omega),

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle\mapsto(Pu,B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega},B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}),

(3.11)

is Fredholm.

(iii)

We shall further assume that the Fredholm index of the operator
L
is zero.

The previous symmetry property gives

(Pu,u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\in\mathbb{R}
. We further assume the following
nonnegativity property:

(iv)

For

such that

on
\partial\Omega
,

one has

\displaystyle(Pu,u)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\geq 0.

Associated with
P
and the boundary operators
B_{1}

and
B_{2}
is the operator
(\mathsf{P},D(\mathsf{P}))
on
L^{2}(\Omega)
, with domain

\displaystyle D(\mathsf{P})=\big\{u\in L^{2}(\Omega);\ Pu\in L^{2}(\Omega),\ B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}=B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega}=0\big\},

and given by

for

.
The definition of

makes sense since having

for

are well defined for

Since the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds on

one has

as in

\displaystyle\mapsto(Pu+u,B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega},B_{2}u_{|\partial\Omega})

\range(\mathsf{P}+\id)=L^{2}(\Omega)
.
One thus concludes that

is maximal monotone.
From the assumed symmetry property (

) and
one finds that
\mathsf{P}
is selfadjoint, using

[

).

being compact on

has a sequence of

Well-posedness for the parabolic system (

Corollary 3.7

The operator
(\mathsf{P},D(\mathsf{P}))
generates an analytic

semigroup

on

For
T>0,

, and

3.5
The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for the augmented operator

We consider the augmented operator
Q=D_{s}^{4}+\Delta^{2},
with
s\in\mathbb{R}.
Let
q
denote the principal symbol of
Q.
We have

q(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=\sigma^{4}+(\xi^{2}_{d}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x})^{2}=(\xi^{2}_{d}-i\sigma^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x})(\xi^{2}_{d}+i\sigma^{2}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}).

We look for the roots of
q(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})
as a polynomial in
\xi_{d}.
We recall that for a given complex number
z=a+ib,
the square root of
z
is given by:

\pm\left(\sqrt{\frac{m+a}{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{m-a}{2}}\right)~~\text{if}~b>0,~~\pm\left(\sqrt{\frac{m+a}{2}}-i\sqrt{\frac{m-a}{2}}\right)~~\text{if}~b<0,

where
m=\sqrt{a^{2}+b^{2}}=|z|.
Consequently the roots of
q(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})
are

\pm\left(\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}\right)

\pm\left(\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}-i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}\right).

Let
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
denote the roots with positive imaginary part, then

\rho_{1}:=\rho_{1}(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})=-\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}

(3.13)

\rho_{2}:=\rho_{2}(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})=\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}+i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}.

(3.14)

We observe that
\rho_{j}(x,\lambda\sigma,\lambda\xi^{\prime})=\lambda\rho_{j}(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
for

or
2
, then the roots
\rho_{j}
are homogeneous of degree one in the variable
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime}).

Remark 3.8
.

The roots
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are equals if and only if
\sigma=0.

Set
q^{+}(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=(\xi_{d}-\rho_{1})(\xi_{d}-\rho_{2}).
According to Definition
3.1
, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is satisfies for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at the point
(x,\xi^{\prime},\sigma)
with
(\xi^{\prime},\sigma)\neq(0,0)
if for every polynomial function
f(\xi_{d})
, there exist
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and a polynomial function

such that

f(\xi_{d})=c_{1}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})+c_{2}b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})+h(\xi_{d})(\xi_{d}-\rho_{1})(\xi_{d}-\rho_{2}).

Therefore the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds if the complex numbers
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are roots of the polynomial function
f(\xi_{d})-c_{1}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})-c_{2}b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}).
This leads to the following determinant condition

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})\\[2.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

(3.15)

Remark 3.9
.

We have for
\ell=1
or
2

b_{\ell}(x,\lambda\xi^{\prime},\rho_{\ell}(x,\lambda\sigma,\lambda\xi^{\prime}))=\lambda^{k_{\ell}}b_{\ell}(x,\xi^{\prime},\rho_{\ell}(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime}))

and

b_{\ell}(x,\lambda\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda},\rho_{\ell}(x,\lambda\frac{\sigma}{\lambda},\lambda\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda}))=\lambda^{k_{\ell}}b_{\ell}(x,\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda},\rho_{\ell}(x,\frac{\sigma}{\lambda},\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda})).

But writing

\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\lambda}+\frac{\sigma}{\lambda}=\underbrace{\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{(|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{4}+\sigma^{4})^{1/4}}}_{:=X}+\underbrace{\frac{\sigma}{(|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{4}+\sigma^{4})^{1/4}}}_{:=Y}

with
\lambda=(|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{4}+\sigma^{4})^{1/4},
we have
|X|^{4}+Y^{4}=1.

If the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds at some
(x^{0},\sigma^{0},\xi^{0})
where
\sigma^{0}\neq 0
,
because of homogeneity, there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\left|\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})\\[2.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})\end{pmatrix}\right|\geq C_{0}\Lambda^{k_{1}+k_{2}},

(3.16)

with
\Lambda=(\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x})^{1/4}=\frac{1}{2}(|\rho_{1}|+|\rho_{2}|)
in a conic neighborhood
\mathscr{U}
of
(x^{0},\sigma^{0},\xi^{0}).

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
(x,\xi^{\prime})\in T^{*}\partial\Omega\cong\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0).
Suppose that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})

(x,\xi^{\prime}).
Then the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition also holds true for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
for
\sigma
near zero.

Before the proof of this proposition we make the following
key
observation.

Observation 3.11
.

Consider the following two boundary operators

B_{1}(x,D)=D^{2}_{d}+\alpha R(x,D^{\prime})~~~~\text{and}~~~~B_{2}(x,D)=D_{d},~~~\text{where}~~D_{d}=-i\partial_{d},

and with respective principal symbols

b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=\xi_{d}^{2}+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}~~\text{and}~~b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=\xi_{d}.

Here
R(x,D^{\prime})
denote a tangential differential elliptic operator of order
2
with principal symbol
r(x,\xi^{\prime})=|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}.
The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
(the case
\sigma=0
) reads as follows (with
\rho=i|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}
):

\begin{vmatrix}\rho^{2}+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}&~~~~\rho\\
\\
2\rho&1\end{vmatrix}=-\rho^{2}+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=(1+\alpha)|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}\neq 0,~~\text{if}~~\alpha\neq-1.

On the other hand, the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
(the case
\sigma\neq 0
) reads as follows

\begin{vmatrix}\rho^{2}_{1}+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}&~~~~\rho_{1}\\
\\
\rho^{2}_{2}+\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}&~~\rho_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\rho^{2}_{1}\rho_{2}+\alpha\rho_{2}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}-\rho_{1}\rho^{2}_{2}-\alpha\rho_{1}|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}\rho_{2}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}).

(3.17)

We recall that
\rho_{1}=-\overline{\rho}_{2}
and so
\rho_{1}\rho_{2}=-|\rho_{1}|^{2}=-\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}
. Therefore equation (
3.17

(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(\rho_{1}\rho_{2}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x})=(\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})(-\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}).

If
\alpha<-1,
then for
\sigma^{4}=(\alpha^{2}-1)|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x},
we have
-\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=0
while for
\sigma=0,

-\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}-\alpha|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}=-(\alpha+1)|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}>0.
This means that having Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
does not imply necessarily that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition also holds true for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2}).

More generally, we set

K=\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})\\[2.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})\end{pmatrix}~~\text{and}~~K^{\prime}=\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho).

Using the notation (
3.1
), straightforward computations gives:

\displaystyle K

\displaystyle=(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}}\right]

\displaystyle+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}}\right](\rho_{2}+\rho_{1})

\displaystyle+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\right](\rho_{1}\rho_{2})

\displaystyle+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}-3}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-3}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\right](\rho_{1}\rho_{2})(\rho_{2}+\rho_{1})

\displaystyle+(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}-3}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-3}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}\right](\rho_{1}\rho_{2})^{2}

\displaystyle K^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}}\right]+2\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}}\right]\rho

\displaystyle+3\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}}b_{2}^{k_{2}-3}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-3}b_{2}^{k_{2}}\right]\rho^{2}+\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\right]\rho^{2}

\displaystyle+2\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-1}b_{2}^{k_{2}-3}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-3}b_{2}^{k_{2}-1}\right]\rho^{3}+3\left[b_{1}^{k_{1}-2}b_{2}^{k_{2}-3}-b_{1}^{k_{1}-3}b_{2}^{k_{2}-2}\right]\rho^{4}.

Here we recall that
\rho=i|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x},

\rho_{1}
and

are given (

) and (

For

Proof of Proposition
3.10
.

Set
\rho_{2}=\rho_{1}+h
with

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})\\[2.0pt]

3.6
Some examples

In connection with the examples listed in Section
3.3
, we check the validity of the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
for some boundary conditions
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
at
(\xi^{\prime},\sigma)\neq(0,0).

Example 3.12

(Hinged boundary conditions)
.

With
B_{1}u=u
and
B_{2}u=\Delta u,
we then have the principal symbols

b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=1
and
b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=-\xi_{d}^{2}.
The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2},B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
is equivalent to have

\begin{vmatrix}1&-\rho^{2}_{1}\\
\\
1&-\rho^{2}_{2}\end{vmatrix}=-\rho^{2}_{2}+\rho^{2}_{1}=-(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})(\rho_{2}+\rho_{1})\neq 0.

We note that
\rho_{1}=-\bar{\rho_{2}}
and therefore

\begin{vmatrix}1&-\rho^{2}_{1}\\
\\
1&-\rho^{2}_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\bar{\rho}^{2}_{2}-\rho^{2}_{2}=(\bar{\rho_{2}}-\rho_{2})(\bar{\rho_{2}}+\rho_{2}).

But
\bar{\rho_{2}}^{2}-\rho^{2}_{2}=-4i\Re\rho_{2}\Im\rho_{2}=-4i\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}\times\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}=-2i\sigma^{2}\neq 0
if
\sigma\neq 0.

Example 3.13

(Clamped boundary conditions)
.

With
B_{1}u=u
and
B_{2}u=\partial_{n}u,
we then have the principal symbols of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
given respectively by

b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=1
and
b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=-i\xi_{d}.
The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2},B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
is equivalent to have

\begin{vmatrix}1&-i\rho_{1}\\
1&-i\rho_{2}\end{vmatrix}=-i\rho_{2}+i\rho_{1}=-i(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\neq 0.

But
\rho_{2}-\rho_{1}=2\sqrt{\frac{\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}-|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}}{2}}
which is different from zero if
\sigma\neq 0.

Example 3.14
.

Let
B_{1}u=\partial_{n}u
and
B_{2}u=\partial_{n}\Delta u
. We have
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=-i\xi_{d}
and
b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=i\xi^{3}_{d}.

The Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2},B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
is equivalent to have

\begin{vmatrix}-i\rho_{1}&i\rho^{3}_{1}\\
\\
-i\rho_{2}&i\rho^{3}_{2}\end{vmatrix}=\rho_{1}\rho^{3}_{2}-\rho_{2}\rho^{3}_{1}=(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})(\rho_{2}+\rho_{1})(\rho_{2}\rho_{1})\neq 0.

We note that
\rho_{1}=-\bar{\rho_{2}}
and then

\rho_{1}\rho^{3}_{2}-\rho_{2}\rho^{3}_{1}=-4i|\rho_{2}|^{2}\Re\rho_{2}\Im\rho_{2}=-2i\sigma^{2}\sqrt{\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|^{4}_{x}}\neq 0~~\text{if}~\sigma\neq 0.

We give an additional example which is totally different from those boundary conditions listed in Section
3.3
.

Example 3.15
.

Let us consider the two boundary differential operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
to be of order zero and one respectively with nonvanishing principal symbols

b_{1}(x,\xi)=\langle\xi,\nu_{x}\rangle~~\text{and}~~b_{2}(x,\xi)=\langle\xi,t_{x}\rangle+i\langle\xi,v_{x}\rangle

where
t_{x}
and
v_{x}
are two real vector fields on
\Omega
along
\partial\Omega,
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the Euclidean scalar product. We write

v_{x}=v^{\nu}_{x}\nu_{x}+v^{\prime}_{x}~~\text{and}~~t_{x}=t^{\nu}_{x}\nu_{x}+t^{\prime}_{x},

with
v^{\nu}_{x},t^{\nu}_{x}\in\mathbb{R}
and
v^{\prime}_{x},t^{\prime}_{x}\in T_{x}\partial\Omega\cong\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

\nu_{x}
denote the unitary outward pointing vector on
\Omega
along
\partial\Omega.
We set

b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=b_{j}(x,\xi^{\prime}-\xi_{d}n_{x}),

where
n_{x}
denotes the outward unit pointing conormal vector at
x.

Therefore, we have

b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=-i\xi_{d}

and

b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d})=\langle\xi^{\prime}-\xi_{d}n_{x},t_{x}\rangle+i\langle\xi^{\prime}-\xi_{d}n_{x},v_{x}\rangle=\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle-\xi_{d}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x}).

For
\xi^{\prime}\in T^{*}_{x}\partial\Omega
such that
\xi^{\prime}\neq 0,
the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
if and only if

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}&b_{2}\\[2.0pt]
\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{1}&\partial_{\xi_{d}}b_{2}\end{pmatrix}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x})\neq 0.

This is equivalent to have

\begin{vmatrix}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}&~~~~\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle-i|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x})\\
\\
-i&-t^{\nu}_{x}-iv^{\nu}_{x}\end{vmatrix}\neq 0.

That is,

-|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x})+i\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle+|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x})\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}\rangle+i\langle\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{x}\rangle\neq 0.

Hence, this holds if and only if

\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}\rangle\neq 0~~\text{and}~~\langle\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{x}\rangle\neq 0.

Therefore the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\xi^{\prime})
with
\xi^{\prime}\neq 0
if and only if
(\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}\rangle,\langle\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{x}\rangle)\neq(0,0).

We say that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime})
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
if and only if

\det\begin{pmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{1})\\[2.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho_{2})\end{pmatrix}\neq 0.

Therefore we have

\begin{vmatrix}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\rho)\\[2.0pt]
b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\theta)&b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=\theta)\end{vmatrix}=\begin{vmatrix}-i\rho_{1}&~~~~\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle-\rho_{1}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x})\\
\\
-i\rho_{2}&~~~~\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle-\rho_{2}(t^{\nu}_{x}+iv^{\nu}_{x})\end{vmatrix}\neq 0,

i.e.,

i(\rho_{2}-\rho_{1})\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}+iv^{\prime}_{x}\rangle\neq 0\Longleftrightarrow\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}\rangle+i\langle\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{x}\rangle\neq 0,

if
\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\neq 0,
i.e.,
\sigma\neq 0.
Hence the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at
(x,\sigma,\xi)
with
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime})\neq(0,0)
if and only if
(\langle\xi^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{x}\rangle,\langle\xi^{\prime},v^{\prime}_{x}\rangle)\neq(0,0).

3.7
Stability of the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition

To prepare the study of how the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition behave under conjugation with Carleman exponential weight function, we show that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
is stable under small perturbations.

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
V^{0}
be a compact set of
\partial\Omega
such that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition holds for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at the point
(x^{0},\sigma^{0},\xi^{0})
of
V^{0}
, then the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition remains valid for
(Q,B_{1},B_{2})
at every point
(x,\sigma,\xi)
of
V^{0}.
That is to say that the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition is stable under small perturbations of the coefficients.

Proof.

According to Definition
3.1
, let

f(\mathbf{z})=c_{1}b_{1}(x,\xi^{\prime},\mathbf{z})+c_{2}b_{2}(x,\xi^{\prime},\mathbf{z})+g(z)h(x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\mathbf{z}),

for all
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

x\in V^{0},

\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{C}.

We suppose that
h
is a polynomial function (in
\mathbf{z}
) of order 2 and plays the same role as
q^{+}.

In addition, we consider
f
to be at most of degree one, and

The augmented operator

3.8.2
Roots properties and configuration for each factor

We consider the factors

\displaystyle\ell_{j}(\varrho)=(\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+\gamma_{j}(\varrho^{\prime}),\ \ j=1,2.

Second, we consider the case

. There exists a unique
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
such
that

\Re\alpha_{j}>0~~\text{and}~~\alpha_{j}^{2}:=\alpha_{j}^{2}(\varrho^{\prime})=\gamma_{j}(\varrho^{\prime}),~~~~j=1,2.

We have

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}^{2}

\displaystyle=r(x,\xi^{\prime}+i\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}i(\sigma+i\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2})

\displaystyle=r(x,\xi^{\prime})-\tau^{2}r(x,d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+2i\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}i(\sigma+i\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2},

\displaystyle=r(x,\xi^{\prime})-\tau^{2}r(x,d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+2i\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+(-1)^{j}i\sigma^{2}+2(-1)^{j+1}\sigma\tau\partial_{s}\varphi+(-1)^{j+1}i(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}

We may then write

\ell_{j}(\varrho)=(\xi_{d}+i\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}+\alpha_{j}^{2}=(\xi_{d}-\pi_{j,+}(\varrho^{\prime}))(\xi_{d}-\pi_{j,-}(\varrho^{\prime})),

may cross the real line.

Remark 3.17

Suppose that

Let

We recall the definition of

Lemma 3.18
.

Assume that
\partial_{d}\varphi>0
, there exists
K_{0}>0
sufficiently small such that
|\partial_{s}\varphi|+|d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|_{x}\leq K_{0}|\partial_{d}\varphi|
. There exist
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that, for
j=1,2,
if we have

(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}\geq C_{1}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)+C_{2}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\rho_{j}|^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}.

then

\Im\pi_{j,+}<0
, for
j=1,2
.

Proof.

From (
3.24
) one has
\Im\pi_{j,+}<0
if and only if

\Re\alpha_{j}<\tau\partial_{d}\varphi=|\tau\partial_{d}\varphi|
, that is, if
and only if

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\Re\alpha_{j}^{2}-4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}+(\Im\alpha_{j}^{2})^{2}<0,

by Lemma
3.19
below. With (
3.8.2
), we have

\begin{cases}\Re\alpha_{j}^{2}&=r(x,\xi^{\prime})-r(x,\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)-2(-1)^{j}\sigma(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)\\
(\Im\alpha_{j}^{2})^{2}&=4\tau^{2}\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}+\sigma^{4}+4(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)\\
&-4(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)-2\sigma^{2}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}.\end{cases}

Therefore the inequality

4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\Re\alpha_{j}^{2}-4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}+(\Im\alpha_{j}^{2})^{2}<0

is equivalent to

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+4\tau^{2}\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}

\displaystyle\quad<4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}+4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+8(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\sigma(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)+2\sigma^{2}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad-4(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)+4(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad<4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\tau d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+8(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\sigma(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)+2\sigma^{2}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}-4(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad+4(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)

(3.25)

using the fact that
|\tau d_{x}\varphi|_{x}^{2}=|\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|_{x}^{2}+(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}.

On the one hand, since
|d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|\leq K_{0}|\partial_{d}\varphi|
one has

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}r(x,\xi^{\prime})+4\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq(4+4K_{0}^{2})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}

\displaystyle\leq(4+4K_{0}^{2})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\rho_{j}|^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4},

(3.26)

since
|\rho_{j}|^{2}=(\sigma^{4}+|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{4})^{1/2}\geq|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}^{2}
,
r(x,\xi^{\prime})=|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}
and
|\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)^{2}|\leq|\xi^{\prime}|^{2}_{x}|\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|_{x}^{2}.

On the other hand one has

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\tau d_{x}\varphi|^{2}+8(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}\sigma(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)+2\sigma^{2}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}-4(-1)^{j}\sigma^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad-4(-1)^{j}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}\tau\tilde{r}(x,\xi^{\prime},d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-8(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\sigma||\tau\partial_{s}\varphi|-4\sigma^{2}|\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|_{x}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}-4|\tau d_{x^{\prime}}\varphi|_{x}|\xi^{\prime}|_{x}(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-8K_{0}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{3}|\rho_{j}|-4K_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)-4K^{3}_{0}|\rho_{j}|(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{3}

\displaystyle\qquad\geq 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-(8K_{0}+4K^{3}_{0})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{3}|\rho_{j}|-4K_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi).

Using
xy^{2}\leq\frac{1}{3}x^{3}+\frac{2}{3}y^{3}
, we then have

\displaystyle 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-(8K_{0}+4K^{3}_{0})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{3}|\rho_{j}|-4K_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\geq 4(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-(8K_{0}+4K^{3}_{0})(\frac{1}{3}|\rho_{j}|^{3}+\frac{2}{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{3})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)-4K_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\geq(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-CK_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)

(3.27)

for
K_{0}
sufficiently small and some
C>0.
From (
3.8.2
)
and (
3.8.2
), (
3.8.2

(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}-CK_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)\geq(4+4K_{0}^{2})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\rho_{j}|^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4},

that is

(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{4}\geq CK_{0}|\rho_{j}|^{3}(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)+(4+4K_{0}^{2})(\tau\partial_{d}\varphi)^{2}|\rho_{j}|^{2}+\sigma^{4}+(\tau\partial_{s}\varphi)^{4}.

This ends the proof.

Lemma 3.19
.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}
such that
m=z^{2}.
For
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such
that
x_{0}\neq 0
, we have

\displaystyle|\Re z|\lesseqqgtr|x_{0}|\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad 4x_{0}^{2}\Re m-4x_{0}^{4}+(\Im m)^{2}\lesseqqgtr 0.

Proof.

Let
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}.
On the one hand we have

z^{2}=x^{2}-y^{2}+2ixy=m
and
\Re m=x^{2}-y^{2},

\Im m=2xy.
On the other
hand we have

\displaystyle 4x_{0}^{2}\Re m-4x_{0}^{4}+(\Im m)^{2}

\displaystyle=4x_{0}^{2}(x^{2}-y^{2})-4x_{0}^{4}+4x^{2}y^{2}

\displaystyle=4(x_{0}^{2}+y^{2})(x^{2}-x_{0}^{2}),

thus with the same sign as
(x^{2}-x_{0}^{2})
.
Since
|\Re z|\lesseqqgtr|x_{0}|\ \Leftrightarrow\ x^{2}-x_{0}^{2}\lesseqqgtr 0
the conclusion follows.

3.8.3
Discussion on the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition

With the assumption that

for any point

(s,x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\tau)

•

holds trivially at
(s,x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\tau)

•

Case 2 : One root lying in the upper complex closed
half-plane. Let us denote by
r^{+}
that root, then

q_{\varphi}^{+}(s,x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d},\tau)=\xi_{d}-r^{+}.
With
Definition
3.21
, for any choice of

well

\begin{cases}f(r^{+}_{1})=c_{1}b_{1,\varphi}(s,x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=r^{+}_{1},\tau)+c_{2}b_{2,\varphi}(s,x,\xi^{\prime},\xi_{d}=r^{+}_{1},\tau),\\

this root; one has

\{x_{d}>0\}
and
S_{0}>0
. Assume that
(Q,B_{1},B_{2},\varphi)
satisfies the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition of Definition
3.21
at
\varrho^{\prime}=(s,x,\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\gamma,\varepsilon)
for all
(\sigma,\xi^{\prime},\tau,\gamma,\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\times(0,+\infty)\times[1,+\infty)\times(0,1].

Let
\varphi(z)=\varphi_{\gamma,\varepsilon}(z)
be define as in section
2.1
. There exists an open neighborhood
W
of
z_{0}
in
(0,S_{0})\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
W\subset V
, and there exist
\tau_{0}\geq\tau_{*},\gamma\geq 1,\varepsilon_{0}\in(0,1]
such that

\gamma{\|\tilde{\tau}^{-1}e^{\tau\varphi}u\|}_{4,0,\tilde{\tau}}+{|\trace(e^{\tau\varphi}u)|}_{3,1/2,\tilde{\tau}}\lesssim{\|e^{\tau\varphi}Qu\|}_{+}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}{|e^{\tau\varphi}B_{j}u_{|x_{N}=0^{+}}|}_{7/2-k_{j},\tilde{\tau}},

for
\tau\geq\tau_{0},

\gamma\geq\gamma_{0},

\varepsilon\geq\varepsilon_{0},
and
u\in\overline{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}_{c}(W_{+}).

We recall that the notion of the function space is introduced in (
1.4
).

Using the notations of Section
2
for semi-classical norms, we can write the Carleman inequality of Theorem
5.2
as follows:

\gamma\sum_{|\alpha|\leq 4}\|\tilde{\tau}^{3-|\alpha|}e^{\tau\varphi}D^{\alpha}_{s,x}u\|_{+}+\sum\limits_{r=0}^{3}|e^{\tau\varphi}D^{r}_{x_{N}}u_{|\partial Z}|_{7/2-r,\tilde{\tau}}\lesssim{\|e^{\tau\varphi}Qu\|}_{+}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}{|e^{\tau\varphi}B_{j}u_{|x_{N}=0^{+}}|}_{7/2-k_{j},\tilde{\tau}}.

6
Spectral inequality and an application

Before we state and prove the spectral inequality, we start by stating an interpolation-type inequality which follows from the Carleman inequality of Theorem
5.2
. Finally, as an application of such a spectral inequality, we deduce a null-controllability result of the fourth order parabolic equation (
1.1
).

6.1
An interpolation inequality

Let
S_{0}>0
and
\alpha\in(0,S_{0}/2)
. We introduce the cylinder
Z=(0,S_{0})\times\Omega
and we define
Y=(\alpha,S_{0}-\alpha)\times\Omega
for some
\alpha>0.
We recall that
Q
denotes the augmented elliptic operator
Q:=D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2}.

Following the proof the the interpolation inequality of Theorem 5.1 in
[
9
, Section 5]
, one can derive the following interpolation inequality.

Theorem 6.1
.

(interpolation inequality).
Let
\mathscr{O}
be nomempty subset of
\Omega.
There exist
C>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for
u\in H^{4}(Z)
that satisfies

B_{1}u_{|{x\in\partial\Omega}}=0,~~~~B_{2}u_{|{x\in\partial\Omega}}=0,~~~~~~~~~~~~~~s\in(0,S_{0}),

we have

\|u\|_{H^{3}(Y)}\leq C\|u\|^{1-\delta}_{H^{3}(Z)}\left(\|Qu\|_{L^{2}(Z)}+\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}|B_{j}u_{|s=0}|_{H^{3-k_{j}}(\mathscr{O})}\right)^{\delta},

(6.1)

where
H^{m}(\Omega)
denote the classical Sobolev spaces in
\Omega.

6.2
Spectral inequality

Let
\phi_{j}
and
\mu_{j}
be eigenfunctions and associated eigenvalues of the bi-Laplace operator
P=\Delta^{2}
with the boundary operators
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, which form a Hilbert basis for
L^{2}(\Omega)
(see section
3.3
), with

\displaystyle 0<\mu_{0}\leq\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{k}\leq\cdots.

We now prove the spectral inequality of Theorem
1.3
, viz.,
for some
K>0,

\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq Ke^{K\mu^{1/4}}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\omega)},~~\mu>0,~~y\in\Span\{\phi_{j};~\mu_{j}\leq\mu\}.

(6.2)

We use the following corollary, whose proof can be adapted to the proof of Corollary 3.3 in
[
11
, Section 3.1]
.

Corollary 6.2
.

Assume that
(P,B_{1},B_{2})
fulfills the Lopatinskiĭ-Šapiro condition on
\partial\Omega.
Then there exists
C>0
such that

\|u\|_{H^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\|Pu\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+|B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}|_{H^{7/2-k_{1}}(\partial\Omega)}+|B_{1}u_{|\partial\Omega}|_{H^{7/2-k_{2}}(\partial\Omega)}\right)

for all
u\in H^{4}(\Omega).

We recall that
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
denote respectively the orders of
B_{1}
and
B_{2}.

Proof.

Let
\mu>0
and
y\in\Span\{\phi_{j};~\mu_{j}\leq\mu\},
meaning that
y=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\alpha_{j}\phi_{j}.
We set

u(s,x)=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\alpha_{j}\mu_{j}^{-3/4}f(s\mu_{j}^{1/4})\phi_{j}(x).

Here
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
for
j=0,\dots,k
with
k\in\mathbb{N},
where
f(s)=\beta\sin(\beta s)\cosh(\beta s)-\beta\cos(\beta s)\sinh(\beta s)
with
\beta=\sqrt{2}/2.
Straightforward computation shows that
D_{s}^{4}f=\partial^{4}_{s}f=-f.
Therefore, we have
Qu=0
with
Q=D^{4}_{s}+\Delta^{2}
. Moreover, we note that

f(0)=f^{\prime}(0)=f^{(2)}(0)=0,f^{(3)}(0)=1

and

f(s)=h(\beta s)=\frac{1}{2}\left(e^{-s}\cos(s-\frac{\pi}{4})-e^{s}\cos(s+\frac{\pi}{4})\right).

Applying inequality (
6.1
) of Theorem
6.1
to
u
reads

\|u\|_{H^{3}(Y)}\leq C\|u\|^{1-\delta}_{H^{3}(Z)}\left(\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}|B_{j}u_{|s=0}|_{H^{3-k_{j}}(\mathscr{O})}\right)^{\delta}.

As
\partial_{s}^{3}u_{|s=0}=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\alpha_{j}\phi_{j}(x)=y,
we have
\sum\limits_{j=1}^{2}|B_{j}u_{|s=0}|_{H^{3-k_{j}}(\mathscr{O})}\asymp\|y\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O})}.
Then

\|u\|_{H^{3}(Y)}\leq C\|u\|^{1-\delta}_{H^{3}(Z)}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O})}^{\delta}.

We also have
\|u\|_{L^{2}(Y)}\lesssim\|u\|_{H^{3}(Y)}
with

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\mu_{j}^{-3/2}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\int_{\alpha}^{S_{0}-\alpha}f(\mu_{j}^{1/4}s)^{2}ds.

Using the change of variable
t=\beta\mu_{j}^{1/4}s,
it follows that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{2}(Y)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\mu_{j}^{-7/4}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\int_{\alpha\gamma\mu_{j}^{1/4}}^{(S_{0}-\alpha)\gamma\mu_{j}^{1/4}}f(\gamma^{-1}t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\mu_{j}^{-7/4}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\int_{\alpha\gamma\mu_{j}^{1/4}}^{(S_{0}-\alpha)\gamma\mu_{j}^{1/4}}h(t)^{2}dt

\displaystyle\gtrsim\mu^{-7/4}\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}=\mu^{-7/4}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)};

we have used the following lemma, whose proof can be found in
[
9
, Lemma 5.7]
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
0<a<b
and
t_{0}>0.
There exists
C>0
such that
\int_{at}^{bt}h(s)^{2}ds\geq C_{0}
for
t\geq t_{0}.

Hence we obtain

\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\lesssim\mu^{7/4}\|u\|^{1-\delta}_{H^{3}(Z)}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\mathscr{O})}^{\delta}.

(6.3)

In order to complete the proof, we now estimate
\|u\|_{H^{3}(Z)}
with the use of the following lemma, which by estimate (
6.3
), allows to conclude the proof of Theorem
1.3
.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

There exists
K>0
such that
\|u\|_{H^{3}(Z)}\leq Ke^{K\mu^{1/4}}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Proof.

We have

\|u\|_{H^{3}(Z)}\asymp\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{3}\int_{0}^{S_{0}}\|\partial^{\ell}_{s}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{3-\ell}(\Omega)}ds\lesssim\sum\limits_{\ell=0}^{3}\int_{0}^{S_{0}}\|\partial^{\ell}_{s}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}ds.

Recalling that if
B_{1}u_{|{\partial\Omega}}=B_{2}u_{|{\partial\Omega}}=0,
by Corollary
6.2
we find

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\ell}_{s}u(s,\cdot)\|^{2}_{H^{4}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta^{2}\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\alpha_{j}\mu_{j}^{(\ell-3)/4}f^{(\ell)}(\mu_{j}^{1/4}s)\phi_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}\alpha_{j}\mu_{j}^{(\ell+1)/4}f^{(\ell)}(\mu_{j}^{1/4}s)\phi_{j}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}\mu_{j}^{(\ell+1)/2}f^{(\ell)}(\mu_{j}^{1/4}s)^{2}\lesssim\mu^{2}e^{S_{0}\mu^{1/4}}\sum\limits_{\mu_{j}\leq\mu}|\alpha_{j}|^{2}.

Integrating this estimate over
(0,S_{0})
and summing over
\ell
yields

\|u\|_{H^{3}(Z)}\leq Ke^{K\mu^{1/4}}\|y\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

∎

6.3
A null-controllability result

Let
T>0,
we consider the controlled evolution equation on
(0,T)\times\Omega
under general boundary conditions:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}y+\Delta^{2}y=\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma}v~~~~\text{in}~(t,x)\in(0,T)\times\Omega,\\
B_{1}y_{|(0,T)\times\partial\Omega}=B_{2}y_{|(0,T)\times\partial\Omega}=0,\\
y_{|t=0}=y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),\end{cases}

(6.4)

where
\Sigma
is an open subset of
\Omega
and
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma}\in L^{\infty}
is such that
\mathbbm{1}_{\Sigma}>0
on
\Sigma.
We recall that he function
v\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Sigma)
is the control function. One may ask the following question: can one choose
v
to drive the solution from its initial condition
y^{0}
to zero in final time
T?
Thanks to the spectral inequality of Theorem
1.3
one can answer to this null-controllability question in the affirmative.

Theorem 6.5
.

(Null-controllability)
There exists
C>0
such that for any
y^{0}\in L^{2}(\Omega),
there exists
v\in L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)
such that the solution to (
6.4
) vanishes at time
T
and moreover the control
v
satisfies the bound
\|v\|_{L^{2}((0,T)\times\Omega)}\leq C\|y^{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

The proof can be adapted in a straightforward manner from the proof scheme in section 6.1 of
[
8
]
developed for the heat equation.

On the asymptotic behavior of the colored Jones polynomial of the figure-eight knot associated with a real number Thanks: The first author is supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP16H03927, JP22H01117, JP20K03601, JP20K03931. Hitoshi Murakami, Anh T. Tran

Abstract
. We study the asymptotic behavior of the N N -dimensional colored Jones polynomial evaluated at exp ⁡ ( ξ / N ) \exp(\xi/N) for a real number ξ \xi greater than a certain constant. We prove that, from the asymptotic behavior, we can extract the SL ⁡ ( 2 , ℂ ) \rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C}) Chern–Simons invariant and the Reidemeister torsion twisted by the adjoint action both associated with a representation determined by ξ \xi .

1.
Introduction

Let
N\geq 2
be an integer.

In
[
10
]
, R. Kashaev introduced a link invariant
\langle K\rangle_{N}
for a knot
K
in the three-sphere
S^{3}
by using the so-called quantum dilogarithm.
In
[
11
]
, he studied its asymptotic behavior when
N\to\infty
for several hyperbolic knots and conjectured that it would determine its hyperbolic volume for any
hyperbolic
knot, where a knot is called hyperbolic if its complement possesses a complete hyperbolic structure with finite volume.
More precisely he conjectured

Conjecture 1.1

(Kashaev’s conjecture)
.

Let
H
be a hyperbolic knot in
S^{3}
.
Then the following equality holds.

(1.1)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|\langle H\rangle_{N}\right|}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{V}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)}{2\pi},

where
\operatorname{V}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)
is the hyperbolic volume.

Let
J_{N}(K;q)
be the colored Jones polynomial associated with the
N
-dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2;\mathbb{C})

[
12
,
32
]
.
We normalize it so that
J_{N}(U;q)=1
for the unknot
U
, and that
J_{2}(K;q)
is the ordinary Jones polynomial
[
9
]
.
In
[
23
]
, J. Murakami and the first author proved that Kashaev’s invariant
\langle K\rangle_{N}
coincides with the
N
-dimensional colored Jones polynomial
J_{N}(K;q)
evaluated at
q=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}
.
They also generalized Kashaev’s conjecture for general knots:

Conjecture 1.2

(Volume Conjecture)
.

For any knot
K\subset{S^{3}}
, we have

(1.2)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log\left|J_{N}\left(K;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}\right)\right|}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{Vol}\left(S^{3}\setminus{K}\right)}{2\pi}.

Here,
\operatorname{Vol}(S^{3}\setminus{K})
is the simplicial volume, also known as the Gromov norm
[
6
]

(
see also
[
33
]
)
, which is normalized so that
\operatorname{Vol}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)=\operatorname{V}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)
if
H
is hyperbolic.

Kashaev’s conjecture was complexified by T. Takata, J. Murakami, M. Okamoto, Y. Yokota and the first author in
[
24
]
by dropping the absolute value symbol (and replacing
\langle H\rangle_{N}
with
J_{N}\left(H;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}\right)
) in (
1.1
).

Conjecture 1.3

(Complexification of Kashaev’s conjecture)
.

For a hyperbolic knot
H
, we have

(1.3)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}\left(H;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}\right)}{N}=\frac{\operatorname{V}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)+\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{CS}(S^{3}\setminus{H})}{2\pi},

where
\operatorname{CS}
is the
SO(3)
Chern–Simons invariant of
H
associated with the Levi-Civita connection
[
16
]
.

More detailed asymptotic formulas are known for several knots
[
1
,
28
,
30
]
:

\langle H\rangle_{N}\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\omega(H)N^{3/2}\exp\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\operatorname{CV}(H)\right),

where
\operatorname{CV}(H):=\operatorname{Vol}\left(S^{3}\setminus{H}\right)+\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{CS}(S^{3}\setminus{H})
is the complex volume and
2\sqrt{-1}\omega(H)^{2}
is the adjoint (cohomological) Reidemeister torsion twisted by the holonomy representation of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus{H})
to
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})

[
29
]
.

It was also generalized by replacing
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
with another complex number
\xi

[
2
,
21
]
.

Conjecture 1.4

(Generalized Volume Conjecture)
.

Let
\xi\neq 2\pi\sqrt{-1}
be a complex number close to
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
, and
H
a hyperbolic knot.
Then we have

(1.4)

J_{N}\left(H;e^{\xi/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{C}{\sinh(\xi)}\times\tau(\xi)^{1/2}\left(\frac{N}{\xi}\right)^{d}\exp\left(\frac{N}{\xi}S(\xi)\right)

with
d
a constant.
Here
S(\xi)
and
\tau(\xi)
are related to the
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
Chern–Simons invariant and the adjoint Reidemeister torsion, respectively, both associated with a certain representation of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus{H})
to
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
.

In this paper, we are mainly interested in the figure-eight knot.
We first list known results for the asymptotic behavior of the colored Jones polynomial of the figure-eight knot
E
.

We define a function
\varphi
as

\varphi(\xi):=\operatorname{arccosh}\left(\cosh(\xi)-\frac{1}{2}\right)

for a complex number
\xi
, where we use the following branch of
\operatorname{arccosh}
:

\operatorname{arccosh}(x):=\log\left(x-\sqrt{-1}\sqrt{1-x^{2}}\right),

and we choose the branch cut of
\log
as
(-\infty,0)
.
Note that
\varphi(0)=-\pi\sqrt{-1}/3
and
\varphi(\kappa)=0
, where
\kappa:=\log\left(\frac{3+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)
.
We also define

\displaystyle S(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-\xi-\varphi(\xi)})-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-\xi+\varphi(\xi)})+\xi\varphi(\xi),

\displaystyle\tilde{S}(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-\xi-\varphi(\xi)})-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-\xi+\varphi(\xi)})+(\xi-2\pi\sqrt{-1})(\varphi(\xi)+2\pi\sqrt{-1}),

\displaystyle T(\xi)

\displaystyle:=\frac{2}{\sqrt{(2\cosh(\xi)+1)(2\cosh(\xi)-3)}},

where

\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z):=-\int_{0}^{z}\frac{\log(1-x)}{x}\,dx

is the dilogarithm function, where we choose the branch cut as
(1,\infty)
.
Note that
S(0)=\tilde{S}(2\pi\sqrt{-1})=\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{\pi\sqrt{-1}/3})-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{-\pi\sqrt{-1}/3})=\sqrt{-1}\times 2.02988...
.

The quantities
S(\xi)
,
\tilde{S}(\xi)
, and
T(\xi)
are related to the Chern–Simons invariant and the adjoint Reidemeister torsion associated with a certain representation of
\pi_{1}\left(S^{3}\setminus{E}\right)
to
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
.
See Section
5
for details.

(1)

\xi=2\pi\sqrt{-1}
:

This corresponds to the case of the Volume Conjecture.
Kashaev sketched a proof of (
1.2
) in
[
11
]
, and then T. Ekholm gave a detailed proof of (
1.2
).
See for example
[
27
, Section 3.2]
for Ekholm’s proof.
Later, J.E. Andersen and S.K. Hansen
[
1
, Theorem 1]
followed Kashaev’s method to prove the following asymptotic formula.

J_{N}\left(E;e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}2\pi^{3/2}\left(\frac{N}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)^{3/2}T(2\pi\sqrt{-1})^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{N}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\tilde{S}(2\pi\sqrt{-1})\right).

Since
S(0)=\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Vol}\left(S^{3}\setminus{E}\right)
, this refines the Volume Conjecture for the figure-eight knot.

(2)

\xi
is close to
2\pi\sqrt{-1}
and not purely imaginary:

Y. Yokota and the first author proved the following formula
[
26
]
.

(1.5)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})}{N}=\frac{\tilde{S}(\xi)}{\xi}.

(3)

\xi
is purely imaginary with
5\pi/3<|\xi|<7\pi/3
, and
2\pi/|\xi|
is irrational with finite irrationality measure:

In
[
17
]
(see
[
22
]
for correction), the first author proved the following formula.

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})}{N}=\frac{\tilde{S}(\xi)}{\xi}.

See also
[
19
, 6.2.2]
.

(4)

\xi
is of the form
2\pi\sqrt{-1}+u
for a real number
u
with
0<|u|<\kappa
:

The first author proved the following asymptotic formula
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
:

(1.6)

J_{N}\left(E;e^{\xi/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{-\pi}}{2\sinh(u/2)}T(\xi)^{1/2}\left(\frac{N}{\xi}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{N}{\xi}\tilde{S}(\xi)\right).

Note that this refines (
1.5
) when
\xi
is as above.

(5)

\xi
satisfies the inequalities
|2\cosh{\xi}-2|<1
and
|\operatorname{Im}{\xi}|<\pi/3
:

In this case,
J_{N}\left(E;e^{\xi/N}\right)
converges.
In fact, we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}J_{N}\left(E;e^{\xi/N}\right)=\frac{1}{\Delta(E;e^{\xi})},

where
\Delta(E;t)=-t+3-t^{-1}
is the normalized Alexander polynomial of the figure-eight knot
E

[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
.

See
[
5
]
for general knots.

(6)

\xi=\pm\kappa
:

In this case,
J_{N}\left(E;e^{\kappa/N}\right)
grows polynomially.
More precisely, we have

J_{N}\left(E;e^{\kappa/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\Gamma(1/3)}{(3\kappa)^{2/3}}N^{2/3},

where
\Gamma(z)
is the Gamma function
[
8
, Theorem 1.1]
.

(7)

\xi
is real and
|\xi|>\kappa
:

The first author proved the following formula
[
17
, Theorem 8.1]
(see also
[
20
, Theorem 3.2]
and
[
19
, Lemma 6.7]
).

(1.7)

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{\log J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})}{N}=\frac{S(\xi)}{|\xi|}.

The purpose of this paper is to refine (
1.7
).
We will show

Theorem 1.5
.

If
\xi
is real and
|\xi|>\kappa
, then we have

J_{N}\left(E;e^{\xi/N}\right)\underset{N\to\infty}{\sim}\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sinh(|\xi|/2)}T(\xi)^{1/2}\left(\frac{N}{|\xi|}\right)^{1/2}\exp\left(\frac{N}{|\xi|}S(\xi)\right).

Moreover, we can show that
T(\xi)
is the Reidemeister torsion twisted by the adjoint action of a representation
\rho
of
\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus{E})
to
\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
determined by
\xi
, and that
S(\xi)-\xi\eta/2
is the Chern–Simons invariant of
\rho
associated with the meridian and the preferred longitude of
E\subset S^{3}
.
See Section
5
for details.

Remark 1.6
.

Suppose that
u:=\xi-2\pi\sqrt{-1}
is a small complex number or a real number with
|u|<\kappa
.
In this case
\operatorname{Im}\varphi(u)<0
.
By using a well-known formula

\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z)+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(z^{-1})+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(-z)\right)^{2}=0.

we have

\begin{split}&\tilde{S}(\xi)\\
=&-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{\xi+\varphi(\xi)})+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{\xi-\varphi(\xi)})-\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(-e^{\xi+\varphi(\xi)})\right)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\log(-e^{\xi-\varphi(\xi)})\right)^{2}\\
&+u(\varphi(\xi)+2\pi\sqrt{-1})\\
\\
=&-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{u+\varphi(u)})+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{u-\varphi(u)})-\frac{1}{2}\left(u+\varphi(u)+\pi\sqrt{-1}\right)^{2}\\
&+\frac{1}{2}\left(u-\varphi(u)-\pi\sqrt{-1}\right)^{2}+(2\pi\sqrt{-1}+u)\varphi(u)\\
=&-\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{u+\varphi(u)})+\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{u-\varphi(u)})-u\varphi(u).\end{split}

In the second equality, we use the fact that
\operatorname{Im}\varphi(u)<0
.
Therefore (
1.6
) coincides with the formula appearing in
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we first introduce the colored Jones polynomial, and then we define a quantum dilogarithm, and variants of the logarithm and the dilogarithm.
We also describe some of their properties.

For a knot
K
in the three-sphere
S^{3}
, we denote by
J_{N}(K;q)
the colored Jones polynomial of
K
associated with the
N
-dimensional irreducible representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2;\mathbb{C})

[
12
,
32
]
.
We normalize it so that
J_{N}(U;q)=1
for the unknot
U\subset S^{3}
.
Note that
J_{2}(K;q)
is the original Jones polynomial
[
9
]
.

Let
E
be the figure-eight knot.
We use the following formula obtained by K. Habiro
[
7
, P. 36 (1)]
and T.T.Q. Le
[
14
, P. 129]
.
See also
[
15
]
.

(2.1)

\begin{split}J_{N}(E;q)&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(q^{(N-l)/2}-q^{-(N-l)/2}\right)\left(q^{(N+l)/2}-q^{(N+l)/2}\right)\\
&=\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}q^{-kN}\prod_{l=1}^{k}\left(1-q^{N-l}\right)\left(1-q^{N+l}\right).\end{split}

Next, we define functions
T_{N}(z)
,
\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)
,
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
, and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
, which are related as

\displaystyle T_{N}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)+O(1/N)\quad(N\to\infty),

\displaystyle\frac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{2}}{d\,z}(z)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(z),

\displaystyle\frac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{1}}{d\,z}(z)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{0}(z).

Let
\xi
be a positive real number.
We define
C_{0}:=(-\infty,-1]\cup\{e^{\tau\sqrt{-1}}\mid 0\leq\tau\leq\pi\}\cup[1,\infty)
and
C_{\theta}:=e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}C_{0}
, where
\theta
is a positive real number with
\tan\theta<\frac{\pi}{\xi}
and we orient
C_{0}
from left to right.

Consider the following integrals:

\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x)}\,dx,\quad\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx,

where
m=0,1,2
,
\gamma:=\frac{\xi}{2N\pi\sqrt{-1}}
for an integer
N\geq 2
, and
z
is a complex number with
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
.
Here we follow
[
4
]
to introduce
T_{N}(z)
, which plays an important role in the paper.
Note that the set of the poles of the integrands are

\{k\pi\sqrt{-1}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\}\cup\{2lN\pi^{2}/\xi\mid l\in\mathbb{Z}\}\quad\text{and}\quad\{k\pi\sqrt{-1}\mid k\in\mathbb{Z}\},

respectively.
Therefore, if
N
is large enough, then the only pole inside the unit circle centered at the origin is
0\in\mathbb{C}
and so the path of integral
C_{\theta}
avoids the poles.
Proofs of their convergences (Lemmas
2.1
and
2.2
below) are given in Section
6
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The integral
\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x)}\,dx
converges if
z\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
-\frac{\xi\sin\theta}{4N\pi}<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})<\cos\theta+\frac{\xi\sin\theta}{4N\pi}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
z\in\mathbb{C}
satisfies
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})<\cos\theta
, then the integral
\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx
converges for
m=0,1,2
.

Now define functions
T_{N}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{m}(z)
(
m=0,1,2
) by using the integrals above.

Definition 2.3
.

Fix an integer
N\geq 2
and put
\gamma:=\frac{\xi}{2N\pi\sqrt{-1}}
.
We define

T_{N}(z):=\frac{1}{4}\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x)}\,dx,

for a complex number
z
with
-\frac{\xi\sin\theta}{4N\pi}<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})<\cos\theta+\frac{\xi\sin\theta}{4N\pi}
.

Definition 2.4
.

For a complex number
z
with
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle:=\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle:=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dx,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle:=\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\,dx.

Lemma 2.5
.

For
m=0,1,2
, we calculate
\mathcal{L}_{m}(z)
as follows.

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\log\left(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$,}\\[8.53581pt]
\pi\sqrt{-1}(2z-1)+\log\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$,}\end{cases}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0$,}\\[8.53581pt]
\pi^{2}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)-\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)&\text{if $\operatorname{Im}{z}<0$}.\end{cases}

Proofs are also given in Section
6
.

Remark 2.6
.

In (
2.3
) and (
2.4
), we use
\log(1-x)
and
\operatorname{Li}_{2}(x)
only for
|x|\leq 1
(
x\neq 1
).

Since

\begin{split}T_{N}(z-\gamma/2)-T_{N}(z+\gamma/2)&=\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-\gamma-1)x}-e^{(2z+\gamma-1)x}}{4x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x)}\,dx\\
&=-\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{2x\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
&=\mathcal{L}_{1}(z),\end{split}

we have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.7
.

If
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})<\cos\theta
, then we have

(2.5)

\frac{\exp\left(T_{N}(z-\gamma/2)\right)}{\exp\left(T_{N}(z+\gamma/2)\right)}=1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}.

We will show relations among the functions
T_{N}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{m}(z)
(
m=0,1,2
).

We can prove that
\frac{1}{N}T_{N}(z)
uniformly converges to
\frac{1}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
.
More precisely, we have the following proposition.
See
[
28
, Proposition A.1]
.

Proposition 2.8
.

For any positive real number
M
and a sufficiently small positive real number
\nu
, we have

T_{N}(z)=\frac{N}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)+O(1/N)\quad(N\to\infty)

in the region

\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\nu\leq\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})\leq\cos\theta-\nu,|\operatorname{Im}{z}|\leq M\}.

In particular, the function
\frac{1}{N}T_{N}(z)
uniformly converges to
\frac{1}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
in the region above.

A proof is given in Section
6
.
Since we may regard
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
as a variant of the dilogarithm from Lemma
2.5
, the function
T_{N}(z)
is another quantum dilogarithm.

The functions
\mathcal{L}_{0}(z)
,
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
, and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
are related as follows.

Lemma 2.9
.

The derivatives of
\mathcal{L}_{1}(z)
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
are given as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{2}}{d\,z}(z)

\displaystyle=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(z),

\displaystyle\dfrac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{1}}{d\,z}(z)

\displaystyle=-\mathcal{L}_{0}(z).

Proof.

We have

\begin{split}\frac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{2}}{d\,z}(z)&=\frac{\pi\sqrt{-1}}{2}\int_{C_{\theta}}\left(\frac{d}{d\,z}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\right)\,dx\\
&=\pi\sqrt{-1}\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
&=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\mathcal{L}_{1}(z),\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\frac{d\,\mathcal{L}_{1}}{d\,z}(z)&=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{C_{\theta}}\left(\frac{d}{d\,z}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\right)\,dx\\
&=-\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
&=-\mathcal{L}_{0}(z),\end{split}

completing the proof.
∎

3.
Summation

In this section, we use the quantum dilogarithm
T_{N}(z)
to express
J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})
without the product of a sequence.

Since the figure-eight knot
E
is amphicheiral, its colored Jones polynomial is symmetric, that is,
J_{N}(E;q)=J_{N}(E;q^{-1})
.
So we have
J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})=J_{N}(E;e^{-\xi/N})
and we do not need to consider the case where
\xi<0
.

Recall that we choose a positive real number
\theta
so that
\tan\theta<\frac{\pi}{\xi}
and that we put
\gamma:=\frac{\xi}{2N\pi\sqrt{-1}}
.
Putting
z:=\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(1-l/N)
(
0<l<N
) in (
2.5
), we have

\frac{\exp\left(T_{N}\left(\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(1-l/N)-\frac{\xi}{4N\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)\right)}{\exp\left(T_{N}\left(\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(1-l/N)+\frac{\xi}{4N\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)\right)}=1-e^{\xi(1-l/N)}

since

\operatorname{Re}\left(\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}\left(1-\frac{l}{N}\right)e^{\theta\sqrt{-1}}\right)=\frac{\xi}{2\pi}\left(1-\frac{l}{N}\right)\sin\theta<\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{l}{N}\right)\cos\theta,

which is between
0
and

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)\leq k/N\leq(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(k/N)}\\
=\int_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)}^{(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(z)}\,dz+O\left(e^{-N\varepsilon}\right).

Since
f_{N}(z+\varphi(\xi)/\xi+1/(2N))-S(\xi)/\xi
uniformly converges to
\psi(z)
, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)\leq k/N\leq(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\left(f_{N}(k/N+\varphi(\xi)/\xi+1/(2N))-S(\xi)/\xi\right)}\\
=\int_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)}^{(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(z)}\,dz+O(e^{-N\varepsilon})

from
[
28
, Remark 4.4]
.
Putting
l/N:=k/N+\varphi(\xi)/\xi
, we also have

\sum_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)\leq k/N\leq(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\left(f_{N}\left(k/N+\varphi(\xi)/\xi+1/(2N)\right)-S(\xi)/\xi\right)}\\
=e^{-NS(\xi)/\xi}\sum_{\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)\leq l/N\leq(1+\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}\exp\left(N\times f_{N}\left(\frac{2l+1}{2N}\right)\right).

Thus we obtain

(4.7)

\sum_{\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)\leq k/N\leq(1+\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}\exp\left(N\times f_{N}\left(\frac{2k+1}{2N}\right)\right)\\
=N\times e^{NS(\xi)/\xi}\left(\int_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)}^{(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(z)}\,dz+O\left(e^{-N\varepsilon}\right)\right).

Now by using the saddle point method
[
28
, Proposition 3.2]
(see also
[
28
, Remark 3.3]
), we have

(4.8)

\int_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)}^{(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(z)}\,dz=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{2}\sqrt{(2\cosh(\xi)-1)^{2}-4}}\sqrt{N}}\left(1+O(N^{-1})\right).

Putting
c_{-}:=\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)
and
c_{+}:=(1+\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2
in (
3.6
), we finally have

\begin{split}&J_{N}\bigl(E;\exp(\xi/N)\bigr)\\
=&\frac{N\times e^{NS(\xi)/\xi}}{2\sinh(\xi/2)}\left(\int_{-\varphi(\xi)/(2\xi)}^{(1-\varphi(\xi)/\xi)/2}e^{N\psi(z)}\,dz+O\left(e^{-N\min\{\varepsilon,\delta^{\prime}\}}\right)\right)\\
=&\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\sqrt{N}e^{N\times S(\xi)/\xi}}{2\sinh(\xi/2)\sqrt{\frac{\xi}{2}\sqrt{(2\cosh(\xi)-1)^{2}-4}}}\left(1+O(N^{-1})\right).\end{split}

where we use (
4.7
) at the first equality and (
4.8
) at the second.
Letting
T(\xi)
denote
\frac{2}{\sqrt{(2\cosh(\xi)-1)^{2}-4}}
, we have

J_{N}\bigl(E;\exp(\xi/N)\bigr)=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sinh(\xi/2)}\sqrt{T(\xi)}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\xi}}\times e^{\frac{N}{\xi}S(\xi)}\left(1+O(N^{-1})\right).

Since
J_{N}(E;e^{-\xi/N})=J_{N}(E;e^{\xi/N})
we obtain the required formula.
∎

The fundamental group

Let

5.3.
Chern–Simons invariant

Let
M
be a three-manifold with boundary a torus
T
, and
\{\mu,\lambda\}
be generators of

For a representation
\rho\colon\pi_{1}(M)\to\rm{SL}(2;\mathbb{C})
, we can define the Chern–Simons invariant as follows.

Let
A
be an
\mathfrak{sl}(2;\mathbb{C})
-valued
1
-form
A
on

that defines the flat connection corresponding to
\rho

Assume that
\rho\Bigm|_{T}
is diagonalizable for simplicity.
Then by a suitable conjugation, one has

\rho(\mu)=\begin{pmatrix}e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}\alpha}&0\\

0&e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\beta}\end{pmatrix}.

Then up to gauge equivalence, we can assume that

is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-1}\alpha&0\\
0&-\sqrt{-1}\alpha\end{pmatrix}\,dx+\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{-1}\beta&0\\

near

, where

and

are the

-forms corresponding to

and

respectively.
Then the Chern–Simons invariant
\operatorname{cs}_{M}(\rho;\alpha,\beta)
of

associated with

is defined by

\operatorname{CS}_{M}(\rho;\alpha,\beta):=\frac{-1}{8}\int_{M}\operatorname{Tr}\left(dA\wedge A+\frac{2}{3}A\wedge A\wedge A\right)\in\mathbb{C}/(\pi^{2}\mathbb{Z}).

Note that
\operatorname{Im}\operatorname{CS}_{M}(\rho_{0};0,0)
coincides with the hyperbolic volume if the interior of

\rho_{0}
is the Levi–Civita connection.
See
[
13
]
for details.

Lemma 6.1
.

If
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})<\cos\theta
, then we have

\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx=\frac{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}}{1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}}.

Proof.

Put
C_{0}^{r}:=C_{0}\setminus\Bigl((-\infty,-r)\cup(r,\infty)\Bigr)
for
r>1
,
C_{\theta}^{r}:=e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}C_{0}^{r}
, and
C_{\theta}^{r}+\pi\sqrt{-1}:=\{w+\pi\sqrt{-1}\mid w\in C_{\theta}^{r}\}
.
We first note that

\begin{split}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx&=\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}+\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)(x-\pi\sqrt{-1})}}{\sinh(x-\pi\sqrt{-1})}\,d(x-\pi\sqrt{-1})\\
&=e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}+\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx.\end{split}

Hence we have

\begin{split}&\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
=&e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\left(-\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx+\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}+\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\right)\\
=&e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\left(\int_{V_{r}^{+}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx-\int_{V_{r}^{-}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\right)\\
&-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}2\pi\sqrt{-1}\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)};x=\pi\sqrt{-1}\right),\end{split}

where
V_{r}^{\pm}
is the vertical segment connecting
\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}
and
\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}
, oriented upward.
Since
\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)};x=\pi\sqrt{-1}\right)=\lim_{x\to\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}(x-\pi\sqrt{-1})}{\sinh(x)}=-e^{(2z-1)\pi\sqrt{-1}}
, we have

\begin{split}&\left(1-e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\right)\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
=&e^{-2\pi\sqrt{-1}z}\left(\int_{V_{r}^{+}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx-\int_{V_{r}^{-}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\right)-2\pi\sqrt{-1}.\end{split}

We will show that
\displaystyle\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{V_{r}^{\pm}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx=0
.

\int_{V_{r}^{\pm}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx=\pi\sqrt{-1}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{(2z-1)(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}}{\sinh(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}\,ds

for any

\begin{split}\left|\int_{V_{r}^{\pm}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|&\leq\pi\int_{0}^{1}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}}{\sinh(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}\right|\,ds\\
&\leq\pi\int_{0}^{1}\frac{2e^{\operatorname{Re}((2z-1)(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s))}}{\left|e^{\operatorname{Re}(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}-e^{-\operatorname{Re}(\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+\pi\sqrt{-1}s)}\right|}\,ds\\
&=\frac{2\pi e^{\pm r\operatorname{Re}((2z-1)e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})}}{e^{r\cos\theta}-e^{-r\cos\theta}}\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\pi s\operatorname{Im}(2z-1)}\,ds.\end{split}

Lemma 6.2
.

\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0

\overline{U}_{r}^{\pm}

\begin{split}&\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx-\int_{\overline{H}_{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\\

for

\leq&\frac{e^{-2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\\
&\quad\times\left(2M+\int_{-r}^{-1}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-y\cos\theta}}{e^{-y\cos\theta}-e^{y\cos\theta}}\,dy+\int_{1}^{r}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-y\cos\theta}}{e^{y\cos\theta}-e^{-y\cos\theta}}\,dy\right)\\
=&\frac{2e^{-2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left(M+\int_{-r}^{-1}\frac{e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{1-e^{2y\cos\theta}}\,dy+\int_{1}^{r}\frac{e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2y\cos\theta}}{1-e^{-2y\cos\theta}}\,dy\right)\\
\leq&\frac{2e^{-2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left(M+\frac{\int_{-r}^{-1}e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}\,dy}{1-e^{-2\cos\theta}}+\frac{\int_{1}^{r}e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2y\cos\theta}\,dy}{1-e^{-2\cos\theta}}\right)\\
=&\frac{2e^{-2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\Biggl(M+\frac{e^{-2\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}-e^{-2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{2\left(1-e^{-2\cos\theta}\right)\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}\\
&\phantom{\frac{4e^{-r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\Biggl(}\quad+\frac{e^{r\left(2\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2\cos\theta\right)}-e^{2\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2\cos\theta}}{2\left(1-e^{-2\cos\theta}\right)\left(\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-\cos\theta\right)}\Biggr).\end{split}

This converges to zero as
r\to\infty
since
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
and
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
.
Note that
M
depends on
r
but that it is bounded because it is periodic with respect to
r
.

Putting
x=r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\overline{U}_{r}^{+}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{(2z-1)r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})}\times r\sqrt{-1}}{\sinh(r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1}))}\,dy\right|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r\left|e^{(2z-1)r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})}\right|}{\left|\sinh(r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1}))\right|}\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2re^{2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-r\cos\theta}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}\left(e^{r\cos\theta}-e^{-r\cos\theta}\right)}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&=\frac{2e^{2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2r\cos\theta}}{r^{m-1}\cos^{m}\theta\left(1-e^{-2r\cos\theta}\right)}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&\to 0\quad(r\to\infty)\end{split}

since
\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
, noting that the last integral becomes either
1
(if
z
is real) or
\frac{1-e^{-2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}
(otherwise).

Similarly, putting
x=r(-e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\overline{U}_{r}^{-}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-(2z-1)r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1})}\times r\sqrt{-1}}{\sinh(r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1}))}\,dy\right|\\
&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r\left|e^{-(2z-1)r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1})}\right|}{\left|\sinh(r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1}))\right|}\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2re^{-2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)+r\cos\theta}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}\left(e^{r\cos\theta}-e^{-r\cos\theta}\right)}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&=\frac{2e^{-2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{r^{m-1}\cos^{m}\theta\left(1-e^{-2r\cos\theta}\right)}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{-2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&\to 0\quad(r\to\infty)\end{split}

since
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})
.
Therefore from (
6.1
) we have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dx=2\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\frac{e^{2kz\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k},

which converges to
-2\log(1-e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z})
as
r\to\infty
when
\left|e^{2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right|<1
, or
e^{2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\neq 1
(
z\in\mathbb{R}
), that is, when
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
(Recall that we assume
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
).
We also have

\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\,dx=-\frac{2\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\frac{e^{2kz\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k^{2}},

which converges to
-\frac{2\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\operatorname{Li}_{2}(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}z})
as
r\to\infty
when
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
from Lemma
6.3
below.

This completes the case where
\operatorname{Im}{z}\geq 0
.

Next we assume that
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0
.

For
r>1
, let
\underline{U}_{r}^{\pm}
be the vertical segment connecting
\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}
and
\pm re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1}
, and
\underline{H}_{r}
be the segment connecting
-re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1}
and
re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1}
.
We orient
\underline{U}_{r}^{\pm}
upward and
\underline{H}_{r}
from left to right.
Note that the distance between the origin and the line containing
\underline{U}_{r}^{\pm}
is
r\cos\theta
, and that the distance between the origin and the line containing
\underline{H}_{r}
is also
r\cos\theta
.

For
m=1,2
, we have

(6.2)

\begin{split}&-\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx+\int_{\underline{H}_{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
&+\int_{\underline{U}_{r}^{+}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx-\int_{\underline{U}_{r}^{-}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\\
=&2\pi\sqrt{-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)};x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}\right).\end{split}

Since the order of the pole
x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}
of
\frac{e^{(2z-1)}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}
(
m=1,2
) for
k=1,2,3,\dots
is one, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)};x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}\right)=\lim_{x\to-k\pi\sqrt{-1}}\frac{(x+k\pi\sqrt{-1})e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}=\frac{-e^{-2k\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k\pi\sqrt{-1}}

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)};x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}\right)=\lim_{x\to-k\sqrt{-1}}\frac{(x+k\sqrt{-1})e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}=\frac{-e^{-2k\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k^{2}\pi^{2}}.

Since
e^{(2z-1)x}=1+(2z-1)x+\frac{(2z-1)^{2}x^{2}}{2}+\cdots
and
\frac{1}{\sinh(x)}=\frac{1}{x}-\frac{x}{6}+\cdots
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)};x=0\right)

\displaystyle=2z-1

and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)};x=0\right)

\displaystyle=2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}.

We can prove the integrals along
\underline{H}_{r}
and
\underline{U}_{r}^{\pm}
converge to zero as
r\to\infty
in similar ways to the cases of
\overline{H}_{r}
and
\overline{U}_{r}^{\pm}
.

Putting
x=ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1}
and assuming
r>1
, we have

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\underline{H}_{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|\\
\leq&\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left|\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{e^{(2z-1)(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1})}\times e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}}{\sinh(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1})}\,dy\right|\\
\leq&\frac{e^{2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\int_{-r}^{r}\frac{e^{y\operatorname{Re}\left((2z-1)e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{\left|\sinh(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1})\right|}\,dy\\
&\text{\small($M:=\max_{-1\leq y\leq 1}\frac{e^{y\operatorname{Re}\left((2z-1)e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{\left|\sinh(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-r\sqrt{-1})\right|}>0$)}\\
\leq&\frac{e^{2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\\
&\quad\times\left(2M+\int_{-r}^{-1}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-y\cos\theta}}{e^{-y\cos\theta}-e^{y\cos\theta}}\,dy+\int_{1}^{r}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-y\cos\theta}}{e^{y\cos\theta}-e^{-y\cos\theta}}\,dy\right),\end{split}

which converges to zero as
r\to\infty
.

Putting
x=r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1})
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\underline{U}_{r}^{+}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{(2z-1)r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1})}\times(-r\sqrt{-1})}{\sinh(r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}-y\sqrt{-1}))}\,dy\right|\\
&\leq\frac{2re^{2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-r\cos\theta}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}(e^{r\cos\theta}-e^{-r\cos\theta})}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&=\frac{2e^{2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)-2r\cos\theta}}{r^{m-1}\cos^{m}\theta(1-e^{-2r\cos\theta})}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy,\end{split}

since
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})<\cos\theta
, noting that the last integral becomes either
1
(if
z
is real) or
\frac{e^{2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}-1}{2r\operatorname{Im}{z}}
(otherwise).

Similarly, putting
x=-r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})
, we have

\begin{split}\left|\int_{\underline{U}_{r}^{-}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{m}\sinh(x)}\,dx\right|&\leq\frac{1}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}}\left|\int_{0}^{1}\frac{e^{-r(2z-1)(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1})}\times(-r\sqrt{-1})}{\sinh(-r(e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}+y\sqrt{-1}))}\,dy\right|\\
&\leq\frac{2re^{-2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)+r\cos\theta}}{(r\cos\theta)^{m}(e^{r\cos\theta}-e^{-r\cos\theta})}\int_{0}^{1}e^{2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&=\frac{2e^{-2r\operatorname{Re}\left(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}\right)}}{r^{m-1}\cos^{m}\theta(1-e^{-2r\cos\theta})}\times\int_{0}^{1}e^{2ry\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,dy\\
&\to 0\quad(r\to\infty)\end{split}

since
0<\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})
.
So from (
6.2
) we have

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dx&=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)};x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}\right).\\
&=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}(2z-1)+2\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\frac{e^{-2kz\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k}.\end{split}

Since this series converges to
-\log\left(1-e^{-2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)
as
r\to\infty
if
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0
from Lemma
6.3
, we finally have

\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)}\,dt=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}(2z-1)-2\log\left(1-e^{-2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right),

completing the proof when
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0
.

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{C_{\theta}^{r}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\,dx&=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\operatorname{Res}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)};x=-k\pi\sqrt{-1}\right).\\
&=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)+\frac{2\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\lim_{r\to\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{\lfloor{r}\rfloor}\frac{e^{-2kz\pi\sqrt{-1}}}{k^{2}}.\end{split}

The series converges to
\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right)
and so

\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\,dt=-2\pi\sqrt{-1}\left(2z^{2}-2z+\frac{1}{3}\right)+\frac{2\sqrt{-1}}{\pi}\operatorname{Li}_{2}\left(e^{-2z\pi\sqrt{-1}}\right),

completing the proof when
\operatorname{Im}{z}<0
.
∎

We give a proof for the following well-known lemma.

Lemma 6.3
.

For a complex number
w
with
|w|\leq 1
, the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k}}{k^{2}}
converges to
\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w)
.
Here we use
\operatorname{Li}_{2}(w):=-\int_{0}^{w}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}\,dt
as the definition of the dilogarithm.

Proof.

Put
a_{k}:=\frac{1}{k^{2}}
.

Then we have

\left|\frac{a_{k+1}}{a_{k}}\right|=\left(\frac{k}{k+1}\right)^{2}\to 1

as
k\to\infty
.
Therefore from d’Alembert’s ratio test, the radius of convergence of the power series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}w^{k}
is
1
.
So we can differentiate it term by term if
|w|<1
, and we obtain

\frac{d}{d\,w}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k}}{k^{2}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k-1}}{k}=-\frac{\log(1-w)}{w}.

Therefore we conclude that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k}}{k^{2}}=-\int_{0}^{w}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}\,dt
.

If
w=1
, the series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}
converges by the integral test.

Finally, we assume that
|w|=1
with
w\neq 1
, and apply Abel’s test.
Since the sequence
\{a_{k}\}
is positive and monotonically decreasing with
\lim_{k\to\infty}a_{k}=0
, the power series
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}w^{k}
converges if
|w|=1
(
w\neq 1
).
We can also apply Abel’s theorem to conclude that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}z^{k}
converges to
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}a_{k}w^{k}
provided that
z
approaches
w
along the radius.
Note that this includes the case
w=1
.
Therefore we also conclude that
\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{w^{k}}{k^{2}}=-\int_{0}^{w}\frac{\log(1-t)}{t}\,dt
for the case
|w|=1
by choosing the integral path as the radius connecting
0
and
w
.
∎

We prove that
\frac{1}{N}T_{N}(z)
uniformly converges to
\frac{1}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)
.

Proof of Proposition
2.8
.

We have

\begin{split}\left|T_{N}(z)-\frac{N}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)\right|&=\frac{1}{4}\left|\int_{C_{\theta}}\left(\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x\sinh(x)\sinh(\gamma x)}-\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\gamma x^{2}\sinh(x)}\right)\,dx\right|\\
&=\frac{1}{4}\left|\int_{C_{\theta}}\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\gamma x^{2}\sinh(x)}\left(\frac{\gamma x}{\sinh(\gamma x)}-1\right)\,dx\right|\\
&\leq\frac{\pi N}{2|\xi|}\int_{C_{\theta}}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{x^{2}\sinh(x)}\right|\left|\frac{\gamma x}{\sinh(\gamma x)}-1\right|\,dx.\end{split}

Since
\frac{\gamma x}{\sinh(\gamma x)}=1-\frac{(\gamma x)^{2}}{6}+O((\gamma x)^{4})
,
\left|\frac{\gamma x}{\sinh(\gamma x)}-1\right|<\frac{C|x|^{2}}{N^{2}}
for a positive constant
C
since
\gamma=\frac{\xi}{2N\pi\sqrt{-1}}
.
So we have

\left|T_{N}(z)-\frac{N}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)\right|\leq\frac{C^{\prime}}{N}\int_{C_{\theta}}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\right|\,dx,

where
C^{\prime}:=\frac{C\pi}{2|\xi|}
.
Put

\displaystyle I_{+}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}}^{re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\right|\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{-}

\displaystyle:=\lim_{r\to\infty}\int_{-re^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}}^{-e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\right|\,dx,

\displaystyle I_{0}

\displaystyle:=\int_{|x|=1,\theta\leq\arg{x}\leq\pi+\theta}\left|\frac{e^{(2z-1)x}}{\sinh(x)}\right|\,dx.

Putting
x:=e^{\sqrt{-1}\theta}y
, we have

\begin{split}I_{+}&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\left|e^{(2z-1)ye^{\theta\sqrt{-1}}}\right|}{|\sinh(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})|}\,dy\\
&\leq\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})-y\cos\theta}}{e^{y\cos\theta}-e^{-y\cos\theta}}\,dy\\
&=\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{2e^{2y\left(\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})-\cos\theta\right)}}{1-e^{-2y\cos\theta}}\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\cos\theta}}\int_{1}^{\infty}e^{-2y\nu}\,dy\\
&=\frac{e^{-2\nu}}{(1-e^{-2\cos\theta})\nu}.\end{split}

Here we use the assumption
\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})\leq\cos\theta-\nu
.

Similarly, we have

\begin{split}I_{-}&=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{\left|e^{(2z-1)ye^{\theta\sqrt{-1}}}\right|}{|\sinh(ye^{\sqrt{-1}\theta})|}\,dy\\
&\leq\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})-y\cos\theta}}{e^{-y\cos\theta}-e^{y\cos\theta}}\,dy\\
&=\int_{-\infty}^{-1}\frac{2e^{2y\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})}}{1-e^{2y\cos\theta}}\,dy\\
&\leq\frac{2}{1-e^{-2\cos\theta}}\int_{-\infty}^{-1}e^{2y\nu}\,dy\\
&=\frac{e^{2\nu}}{(1-e^{-2\cos\theta})\nu}.\end{split}

Here we use the assumption
\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\theta\sqrt{-1}})\geq\nu
.

Finally, putting
x=e^{\tau\sqrt{-1}}
(
\theta\leq\tau\leq\theta+\pi
) and
L:=\min_{\theta\leq\tau\leq\theta+\pi}|\sinh(e^{\sqrt{-1}\tau})|>0
, we have

\begin{split}I_{0}&=\int_{\theta}^{\theta+\pi}\frac{\left|e^{(2z-1)e^{\tau\sqrt{-1}}}\right|}{|\sinh(e^{\sqrt{-1}\tau})|}|\sqrt{-1}e^{\tau\sqrt{-1}}|\,d\tau\\
&\leq\frac{1}{L}\int_{\theta}^{\theta+\pi}e^{2\operatorname{Re}(ze^{\tau\sqrt{-1}})-\cos\tau}\,d\tau\\
&=\frac{1}{L}\int_{\theta}^{\theta+\pi}e^{(2\operatorname{Re}{z}-1)\cos\tau-2\sin\tau\operatorname{Im}{z}}\,d\tau,\end{split}

which is bounded from the above because both
\operatorname{Re}{z}
and
\operatorname{Im}{z}
are bounded.

Therefore we conclude that
\left|T_{N}(z)-\frac{N}{\xi}\mathcal{L}_{2}(z)\right|\leq\frac{C^{\prime\prime}}{N}
for some constant
C^{\prime\prime}
that does not depend on
z
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.1
.

Recall that
\gamma=\frac{\xi}{2N\pi\sqrt{-1}}
.

By the definition of
T_{N}(z)
, we have

\begin{split}&T_{N}\left(\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(1+\frac{1}{2N})\right)-T_{N}\left(\frac{\xi}{2\pi\sqrt{-1}}(1-\frac{1}{2N})\right)\\

which equals
\pi\sqrt{-1}-\xi-\log(1-e^{-\xi})
from Lemma

.

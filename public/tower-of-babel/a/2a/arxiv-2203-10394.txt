Abstract entropy and expansiveness
M. Achigar

Abstract
. We present a general definition of entropy in the setting of pre-ordered semigroups, extending the notion of topological entropy. From our definition, we obtain the basic properties exhibited by various entropy-like theories encountered in the literature, many of them being particular cases of our scheme. We show how to derive from the properties of the abstract entropy corresponding properties in the concrete cases. We also introduce a notion of expansiveness in this general context, extending the concept of expansive dynamical system, which is related to the entropy in a similar fashion as in the case of topological dynamics. Finally, as an application, we suggest some definitions of new entropies and expansiveness in concrete cases.



1.
Introduction

In his 1965 seminal paper
[
3
]
, Adler, Konheim and McAdrew introduced the notion of
topological entropy
,
h(T)
, for a continuous map
T\colon X\to X
on a compact topological space
X
. Some of the key ingredients in the definition of this invariant are the open covers of
X
(denoted here as
\alpha,\beta,\ldots
), preimages
T^{-1}(\alpha)
under
T
of open covers, and the meet
\alpha\wedge\beta
of open covers (called
join
and denoted
\vee
in
[
3
]
). In that article several useful properties were developed, such as the
logarithmic law

h(T^{n})=nh(T)
, and many others. The proofs of these properties relies, among other things, on the following basic fact:

(1)

T^{-1}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=T^{-1}(\alpha)\wedge T^{-1}(\beta).

That is, if we consider the set
\mathscr{C}
of all open covers, and the map
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
given by
\lambda(\alpha)=T^{-1}(\alpha)
, then
\lambda
preserves the operation
\wedge
. In fact,
\mathscr{C}
is a semigroup with this operation so that
\lambda
is a semigroup homomorphism, and the topological entropy can be defined in terms of
\lambda
.

There are also other ingredients and basic facts supporting the definition and properties of
h(T)
. For example, the refinement relation
\alpha\prec\beta
between open covers, the quantity
H(\alpha)
of
[
3
]
*Definition 1, and the monotonicity of
\lambda
and
H
, but we prefer to stress only property (
1
) to keep this introduction simple.

Another theory very similar to that of topological entropy is the theory of
mean dimension
. The mean dimension,
\mdim(T)
, of a continuous map
T
as before, is introduced in
[
13
]
. To obtain this definition we simply need to replace the quantity
H(\alpha)
in the definition of
h(T)
by the quantity
D(\alpha)
of
[
13
]
*Definition 2.1. As
H
and
D
share the relevant basic properties, both theories also share various properties (and proofs). This is also the case of the theory of
algebraic entropy
, introduced in
[
17
]
. To get the definition of the algebraic entropy of an endomorphism
\varphi
of an abelian group
G
, we need to replace
\mathscr{C}
by the set of all finite subgroups of
G
,
\wedge
by the sum
+
of subgroups,
\prec
by the relation
\supseteq
,
\lambda
by the map taking a finite subgroup to its image under
\varphi
, and the quantity
H
by the logarithm of the cardinality of the subgroups. In this two examples and many others the map
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
always preserves the semigroup opertaion of
\mathscr{C}
. However, this is not the case in some natural contexts.

For example, suppose that we want to define a forward version of the topological entropy, that is, to consider the map
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
given by
\lambda(\alpha)=T(\alpha)
. In first place, it is necessary to assume that
T\colon X\to X
is an open onto map, to guarantee that
\lambda
carries open covers to open covers. With these changes one can define the
forward topological entropy
of such a map
T
following the same method as for the topological entropy. However, this time
\lambda
does not necessarily preserves the operation
\wedge
, but instead we can ensure that

(2)

T(\alpha\wedge\beta)\prec T(\alpha)\wedge T(\beta).

That is,
\lambda
is not a semigroup homomorfism anymore, but it satisfies a weaker condition resembling subadditivity. We will call this type of maps
lower maps
. Surprisingly, it is still possible to prove various of the topological entropy theory properties in this context. To show that is one of the main contributions of the present work.

In Section
2
we consider the situation above from an abstract viewpoint. Starting with a lower map
\lambda
acting on a general semigroup
\mathscr{C}
, we define his entropy and then we derive the properties. Later, in Section
4
, we give few examples of known entropy-like theories as particular cases of our abstract entropy, and show how to obtain some of their properties from the abstract ones.
In
[
8
]
, Dikranjan and Giordano Bruno made a similar delevopement assuming that the map
\lambda
does preserves the operation of
\mathscr{C}
. In §
4.4
and §
4.5
we also suggest some possibly useful new entropy theories which are particular cases of our abstract entropy.

The second main contribution of this article is the introduction of the concept of expansivity in the abstract setting of a general semigroup
\mathscr{C}
. The usual notion of expansivity of a dynamical system on a compact metric space, is a particular case of our abstract expansivity, and corresponds to the case when
\mathscr{C}
is the set of all open covers of the space, as in the beginning of this introduction.

Moreover, abstract expansivity and entropy interacts in the same way as in the theory of topological dynamical systems. A useful tool for computing topological entropy for an expansive dynamical system is the following fact: the entropy of the system coincides with the entropy of size
\varepsilon>0
, if this size is smaller than an expansivity constant. We will prove an analogue of this result at the abstract level of a general semigroup
\mathscr{C}
. These topics are covered in Section
3
.

Finally, in Section
4.5
we consider the special case of our abstract expansivity when the semigroup
\mathscr{C}
is the corresponding to the theory of algebraic entropy, obtaining a possibly useful notion of expansivity for group endomorphisms, which of course interacts with the entropy in the correct way.

2.
Abstract entropy

In this section we introduce an abstract version of entropy, inspired in the definition of topological entropy of a continuous map given in
[
3
]
, and we prove several of its properties. The entropy theory we develop here is similar to the
semigroup entropy
of
[
8
]
*§2 and §3, although there are some important differences. In
[
8
]
the semigroup entropy is studied mainly for (contractive) endomorphisms of (normed) semigroups, while here we consider (normed) semigroups with a preorder which plays a central role and allow us to study entropy for a more general class of (contractive) maps called
lower maps
.

2.1.
Entropy spaces

The first thing to abstract from the definition of topological entropy of
[
3
]
is the role played by the collection
\mathscr{C}_{X}
of all open covers of a (compact) topological space
X
. In
\mathscr{C}_{X}
we have a pre-order
\prec
and an operation
\wedge
given by the refinement relation and the meet of open covers,

1

Note that, according to §
1.2
, our
\prec
has the opposite meaning than in
[
3
]
*Definition 3 and that our

is denoted
\vee
and called
join
in
[
3
]
*Definition 2.
and both verifies a series of basic properties such as
[
3
]
*Property 00, 0 and 1. We also have a non-negative real value

associated to each cover
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
, called the
entropy
of
\alpha
, in
[
3
]
*Definition 1, that relates to
\prec
and

through properties like

3
]
*Property 2 and 4. Inspired on this we introduce the following definitions.

Definition 2.1.1
.

A
cover space
is a 3-tuple
(\mathscr{C},\prec,{}\wedge)
where
\mathscr{C}
is a non-empty set,
\prec
a pre-order in
\mathscr{C}
(a transitive and reflexive relation) and
\wedge
an associative binary operation on
\mathscr{C}
satisfying the following conditions:

[c
1
]

\alpha\wedge\beta\prec\alpha
and
\alpha\wedge\beta\prec\beta
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
.

[c
2
]

\alpha\wedge\beta\prec\alpha^{\prime}\wedge\beta^{\prime}
if
\alpha\prec\alpha^{\prime}
and
\beta\prec\beta^{\prime}
, where
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime},\beta,\beta^{\prime}\in\mathscr{C}
.

The structure
(\mathscr{C},\prec,{}\wedge)
, also called
pre-ordered semigroup
, will be denoted simply as
\mathscr{C}
. In this context an element of
\mathscr{C}
is called a
cover
,
\prec
is the
refinement relation
and
\wedge
the
meet operation
. Given
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\alpha\prec\beta
(also written
\beta\succ\alpha
) we say that
\alpha

is finer than
/
is a refinement of
/
refines

\beta
.

Remark 2.1.2
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a cover space and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathscr{C}
, conditions
[c1]
and
[c2]
implies that adding factors to the product
\twedge_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}=\alpha_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge\alpha_{n}
at any place will result in a finer cover. For example,
\alpha_{1}\wedge\beta\wedge\alpha_{2}\prec\alpha_{1}\wedge\alpha_{2}
if
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\beta\in\mathscr{C}
.

Definition 2.1.3
.

Given a cover space
\mathscr{C}
consider the equivalence relation
\sim
on
\mathscr{C}
defined for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
as
\alpha\sim\beta
iff
\alpha\prec\beta
and
\alpha\succ\beta
. We say that
\mathscr{C}
(or
\wedge
) is
commutative
iff
\alpha\wedge\beta\sim\beta\wedge\alpha
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
. We call
\mathscr{C}
a
meet cover space
, also called
pre-lower semilattice
, iff for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
the cover
\alpha\wedge\beta
is a
greatest lower bound
for
\{\alpha,\beta\}
, that is, if
\gamma\prec\alpha
and
\gamma\prec\beta
for some
\gamma\in\mathscr{C}
then
\gamma\prec\alpha\wedge\beta
.

Remark 2.1.4
.

For a cover space
\mathscr{C}
it is not difficult to show that to be a meet cover space is equivalent to the condition:
\alpha\wedge\alpha\sim\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, which in turn is equivalent to the property: if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
and
\alpha\prec\beta
then
\alpha\sim\alpha\wedge\beta
. It is also clear that a meet cover space is automatically commutative. Hence, in a product of covers of a meet cover space we can cancel repeated factors or in general factors refined by other factors of the product, for example
\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge\alpha\wedge\gamma\sim\alpha\wedge\beta
if
\beta\prec\gamma
.

Remark 2.1.5
.

If a cover space
\mathscr{C}
has a
unit

{\uno}\in\mathscr{C}
, that is,
\alpha\wedge{\uno}\sim{\uno}\wedge\alpha\sim\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, then by condition

we can replace condition

in Definition

by the simpler one:
\alpha\prec{\uno}
for all

. This structure will be called

or

. If a cover space
\mathscr{C}
has no unit element (or even if it has) we can add one, say
{\uno}\not\in\mathscr{C}
, to it and get a unital cover space
\mathscr{C}\cup\{{\uno}\}
extending the relation
\prec
and the binary operation
\wedge
in the obvious way.

Remark 2.1.6
.

Given a cover space
\mathscr{C}
let
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\mathscr{C}/{\sim}
be the quotient space by the equivalence relation
\sim
of Definition
2.1.3
, and let
[\alpha]\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
denote the equivalence class of
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
. Define the relation
\prec_{1}
on
\mathscr{C}_{1}
by
[\alpha]\prec_{1}[\beta]
iff
\alpha\prec\beta
, for
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
, and consider the binary operation
\wedge_{1}
on
\mathscr{C}_{1}
given by
[\alpha]\wedge_{1}[\beta]=[\alpha\wedge\beta]
if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
. As can be easily checked

is a partial order (a transitive, reflexive and antisymmetric relation) and

is a cover space. Then, without loss of generality, we could have assumed that

Let

.

An

is a 4-tuple

where

The function

(or

The concept of entropy function, which is inspired by the quantity

Remark 2.1.9
.

Note that from conditions

[h1]

Remark 2.1.10

If

2.2.
Morphisms

If
T\colon X\to X
is a continuous function on a (compact) topological space
X
, then the definition of the topological entropy of
T
given in
[
3
]
*p. 310 is formulated in terms of taking preimages under
T
of open covers of
X
. That is, in terms of the map
\lambda_{T}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
defined as
\lambda_{T}\alpha=T^{-1}\alpha
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
, where

is the collection of all open covers of

. This map verifies some properties such as

*Property 5, 6 and 7, in particular

preserves the meet operation on open covers. In the next definition we consider properties of this type at the abstract level of entropy spaces introducing various classes of maps, some of them allowing non

Definition 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{1},\mathscr{C}_{2}

(or

[l
1
]

\lambda(\alpha\wedge\beta)\prec\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda\beta
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
, and

[l
2
]

h(\lambda\alpha)\leq h(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
.

We say that
\lambda
is an
upper map
(

[u
1
]

\lambda(\alpha\wedge\beta)\succ\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda\beta
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
, and

[u
2
]

h(\lambda\alpha)\geq h(\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
.

We call

a

iff

[m
1
]

\lambda(\alpha\wedge\beta)\sim\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda\beta
for all
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
, and

[m
2
]

for all

Remark 2.2.2
.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
be a lower map and
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
. Then condition
[c2]
implies that condition
[l1]
generalizes to
\lambda\twedge_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\prec\twedge_{k=1}^{n}\lambda\alpha_{i}
. Analogously,
\lambda\twedge_{k=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}\succ\twedge_{k=1}^{n}\lambda\alpha_{i}
if

Remark 2.2.3
.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
be a
bi-monotone
map between entropy spaces, that is,
\lambda
is monotone, bijective and
\lambda^{-1}
is monotone. Then,
\lambda
is a lower map iff
\lambda^{-1}
is an upper map. In particular, if
\lambda
is a homomorphism then it is an isomorphism.

Remark 2.2.4
.

Compositions of l-maps are l-maps because l-maps are monotone. Then, with the entropy spaces as objects and the l-maps as arrows we obtain a category. Similarly, if we take as arrows the l-morphisms, u-maps, u-morphisms or homomorphisms we also get categories. Note that in these five categories the category isomorphisms are precisely the isomorphisms introduced in Definition
2.2.1
. Compositions of morphisms are morphisms as well, again by monotonicity.

Remark 2.2.5
.

By Remark
2.2.4
if
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is an l-map, l-morphism, u-map, u-morphism, homomorphism or a morphism, then
\lambda^{n}
is of the same class for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. If
\lambda
is an isomorphism then
\lambda^{n}
is an isomorphism for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

2.3.
Entropy

In this section we define the abstract entropy and show some basic facts that will be used later. At the end we show a result not stressed in the literature as far as we know, that could be useful in further developments.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cover space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a map,
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n\leq m
. We define
\alpha_{n}^{m}[\lambda]=\alpha_{n}^{m}=\twedge_{k=n}^{m}\lambda^{k}\alpha
. If
\lambda
is a bijective map the above definition makes sense for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Recall that the order of the factors matters in Definition
2.3.1
because a general cover space is not assumed to be commutative.

Definition 2.3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a map and
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
. We define the
entropy of
\lambda
relative to the cover
\alpha
and the
entropy of
\lambda
as

h(\lambda,\alpha)=\textstyle\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})\quad\text{and}\quad h(\lambda)=\textstyle\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}h(\lambda,\alpha),

respectively, where the
\limsup_{n}
and
\sup_{\alpha}
functions can take values in
\mathbb{R}^{+}=[0,\infty]
.

Remark 2.3.3
.

In the context of Definition
2.3.2
we have that the
\mathbb{R}^{+}
-valued sequence
\bigl(h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})\bigr)
is increasing. Indeed, if
n\geq 1
, as
\alpha_{0}^{n}\prec\alpha_{0}^{n-1}
by condition
[c1]
, we get
h(\alpha_{0}^{n})\geq h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})
by condition
[h1]
. Hence, for a non-finite entropy space we always have
h(\lambda)=\infty
, because
h(\lambda,\alpha)=\infty
if
\alpha
is a cover such that
h(\alpha)=\infty
.

Lemma 2.3.4

(
[
3
]
*Property 10)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a monotone map, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\alpha\prec\beta
, then
h(\lambda,\alpha)\geq h(\lambda,\beta)
.

Proof.

As
\alpha\prec\beta
then
\lambda^{k}\alpha\prec\lambda^{k}\beta
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
because
\lambda
is monotone. Then, for all
n\geq 1
, we have
\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\prec\beta_{0}^{n-1}
by condition
[c2]
, and hence
h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})\geq h(\beta_{0}^{n-1})
by condition
[h1]
. Dividing by
n
and taking
\limsup_{n}
we get
h(\lambda,\alpha)\geq h(\lambda,\beta)
.
∎

As a consequence of Lemma
2.3.4
, to compute
h(\lambda)
we will be interested in the asymptotic behaviour of
h(\lambda,\alpha)
as the covers
\alpha
become finer. Note that in this direction (relation
\succ
) every cover space
\mathscr{C}
is a
directed set
by condition
[c1]
, that is, for every
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
there exists
\gamma\in\mathscr{C}
(namely
\gamma=\alpha\wedge\beta
) such that
\gamma\prec\alpha
and
\gamma\prec\beta
. We will consider
\mathscr{C}
as a directed set in this way when taking limits. Accordingly we will use the term
cofinal
in the sense of the following definition.

Definition 2.3.5
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cover space. A subset
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is called
cofinal
iff for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
there exists
\beta\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}
such that
\beta\prec\alpha
. A map
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
between cover spaces is called
cofinal map
iff its image
\lambda(\mathscr{C}_{1})
is a cofinal subset of
\mathscr{C}_{2}
.

The next easy consequence of Lemma
2.3.4
allows us to replace the “
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}
” of Definition
2.3.2
by a “
\lim_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}
” for a cofinal subset
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
in the monotone case.

Lemma 2.3.6

(
[
3
]
*Property 12)
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a monotone map and
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
cofinal. Then
h(\lambda)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}h(\lambda,\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}h(\lambda,\alpha)
.

Next we generalize
[
3
]
*Property 8 and the subadditive property in its proof.

Lemma 2.3.7
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a lower map and
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
. Then the sequence
(a_{n})
given by
a_{n}=h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})
if
n\geq 1
is subadditive, that is,
a_{n+m}\leq a_{n}+a_{m}
for all
n,m\geq 1
.

Proof.

For
n,m\geq 1
let
s=n+m
denote their sum. Then we can estimate

\begin{split}a_{n+m}&=h\bigl(\twedge_{k=0}^{s-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\bigr)\leq h\bigl(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\bigr)+h\bigl(\twedge_{i=n}^{s-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\bigr)\\
&\leq a_{n}+h\bigl(\lambda^{n}\twedge_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\bigr)\leq a_{n}+h\bigl(\twedge_{k=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\bigr)=a_{n}+a_{m},\end{split}

where the first inequality comes from condition
[h2]
, the second one from conditions
[l1]
and
[h1]
, and the final from condition
[l2]
. Therefore
(a_{n})
is subadditive.
∎

By
[
16
]
*Theorem 4.9, if
(a_{n})
is a subadditive sequence of non-negative real numbers then the limit
\lim_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{n}=\inf_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{n}
exists and is finite. Therefore, by Lemma
2.3.7
and Remark
2.3.3
we see that we can replace the “
\limsup_{n}
” in Definition
2.3.2
by a “
\lim_{n}
” in the case of a lower map. We record this in the following statement.

Lemma 2.3.8
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a lower map and
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
. Then
h(\lambda,\alpha)=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})=\inf_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})
, and
h(\lambda,\alpha)
is finite if
h
is finite.

We end this section with a related result we could not find in the literature.

Lemma 2.3.9
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a commutative entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is a morphism, and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}
, then
h(\lambda,\alpha\wedge\beta)\leq h(\lambda,\alpha)+h(\lambda,\beta)
.

Proof.

Given
n\geq 1
we have
(\alpha\wedge\beta)_{0}^{n-1}\sim\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\wedge\beta_{0}^{n-1}
by commutativity and condition
[m1]
. Then
h\bigl((\alpha\wedge\beta)_{0}^{n-1}\bigr)=h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\wedge\beta_{0}^{n-1})\leq h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})+h(\beta_{0}^{n-1})
by condition
[h2]
. Form this the claim follows dividing by
n
and taking
\limsup_{n}
.
∎

Remark 2.3.10
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a commutative entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is a morphism, from Lemma
2.3.4
and Lemma
2.3.9
we deduce that the function
h^{\lambda}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
given by
h^{\lambda}(\alpha)=h(\lambda,\alpha)
for
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, is actually an entropy function for the cover space
\mathscr{C}
. We do not develop any application of this remark in this work but we point out that it gives a source of new entropy functions on a given entropy space. For example, as can be easily checked, any lower map
\mu
for
(\mathscr{C},h)
such that
\mu\lambda\prec\lambda\mu
(see condition
[l3]
in Definition
2.5.1
) is a lower map for
(\mathscr{C},h^{\lambda})
too. Maybe it could be useful to study pairs
(\lambda,\mu)
of commuting morphisms comparing the quantities
h^{\lambda}(\mu)
and
h^{\mu}(\lambda)
for the concrete entropy theories generalized here.

2.4.
The logarithmic law

In this section we extend
[
3
]
*Theorem 2 and its corollary to our abstract setting in Proposition
2.4.2
and Proposition
2.4.4
. We also show, in Lemma
2.4.3
, the “bilateral formula” for the entropy relative to a cover in the invertible case.

Lemma 2.4.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be an entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a map,
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
m\geq 1
. Then we have 1.
mh(\lambda,\alpha)\geq h(\lambda^{m},\alpha)
. If in addition
\lambda
is a lower map then we also have 2.
mh(\lambda,\alpha)\leq h(\lambda^{m},\alpha_{0}^{m-1}[\lambda])
, and 3.
h(\lambda,\lambda^{m}\alpha)\leq h(\lambda,\alpha)
.

Proof.

Firstly, we estimate
\alpha_{0}^{nm-1}[\lambda]=\twedge_{k=0}^{nm-1}\lambda^{k}\alpha\prec\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{mk}\alpha=\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda^{m}]
, where we applied Remark
2.1.2
. Therefore, by condition
[h1]
we have

\textstyle h(\lambda,\alpha)\geq\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{nm}h(\alpha_{0}^{nm-1}[\lambda])\geq\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{nm}h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda^{m}])=\tfrac{1}{m}h(\lambda^{m},\alpha),

and we are done. For the second assertion let
\beta=\alpha_{0}^{m-1}[\lambda]
. Then, if
n\geq 1
we have

\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda^{m}]=\dwedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{mk}\beta=\dwedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{mk}\dwedge_{l=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{l}\alpha\\
\prec\dwedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\dwedge_{l=0}^{m-1}\lambda^{mk+l}\alpha=\alpha_{0}^{nm-1}[\lambda],

where we applied conditions
[l1]
and
[c2]
. Hence, as
\lambda
and
\lambda^{m}
are lower maps (see Remark
2.2.5
) we can apply Lemma
2.3.8
and condition
[h1]
to get

\textstyle h(\lambda^{m},\beta)=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda^{m}])\geq\lim_{n}\tfrac{m}{nm}h(\alpha_{0}^{nm-1}[\lambda])=mh(\lambda,\alpha),

which ends the proof. For the last statement let
\beta=\lambda^{m}\alpha
and
n\geq 1
. Then we have
\beta_{0}^{n-1}=\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda^{k+m}\alpha\succ\lambda^{m}\alpha_{0}^{n-1}
by condition
[l1]
. Hence, by conditions
[h1]
and
[l2]
we obtain
h(\beta_{0}^{n-1})\leq h(\lambda^{m}\alpha_{0}^{n-1})\leq h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1})
, and the result follows.
∎

Proposition 2.4.2
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is an entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a map then for
m\geq 1
we have
h(\lambda^{m})\leq mh(\lambda)
. If
\lambda
is a lower map then
h(\lambda^{m})=mh(\lambda)
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

For all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, by Lemma
2.4.1
(1), we know that
mh(\lambda,\alpha)\geq h(\lambda^{m},\alpha)
. Then taking
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}
we get the first claim. For the second, firstly suppose
m\geq 1
. By Lemma
2.4.1
(2) we have
mh(\lambda,\alpha)\leq h(\lambda^{m},\alpha_{0}^{m-1}[\lambda])\leq h(\lambda^{m})
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
. Taking
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}
we obtain
h(\lambda^{m})\geq mh(\lambda)
and hence
h(\lambda^{m})=mh(\lambda)
. Finally, for
m=0
we have
\lambda^{0}=\id_{\mathscr{C}}
. As
\id_{\mathscr{C}}=\id_{\mathscr{C}}^{2}
by what we already proved
h(\id_{\mathscr{C}})=2h(\id_{\mathscr{C}})
, hence
h(\id_{\mathscr{C}})=0
or
h(\id_{\mathscr{C}})=\infty
. In the first case we are done, and in the second what we need to prove is
\infty=0\cdot\infty
which is true according to our convention.
∎

Lemma 2.4.3
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is an entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
an isomorphism, then we have
h(\lambda,\alpha)=\textstyle\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{2n+1}h(\alpha_{-n}^{n})
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
.

Proof.

Given
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
n\geq 1
, by condition
[m1]
we have
\alpha_{-n}^{n}\sim\lambda^{-n}\alpha_{0}^{2n}
. Therefore, by conditions
[h1]
and
[m2]
we obtain
h(\alpha_{-n}^{n})=h(\lambda^{-n}\alpha_{0}^{2n})=h(\alpha_{0}^{2n})
. Then we deduce
\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{2n+1}h(\alpha_{-n}^{n})=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{2n+1}h(\alpha_{0}^{2n})=h(\lambda,\alpha)
as claimed.
∎

Proposition 2.4.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a commutative entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
an isomorphism, then
h(\lambda)=h(\lambda^{-1})
and moreover
h(\lambda^{m})=|m|h(\lambda)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Given
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
n\geq 1
we have
\alpha_{-n}^{n}[\lambda]\sim\alpha_{-n}^{n}[\lambda^{-1}]
by commutativity, and hence
h(\alpha_{-n}^{n}[\lambda])=h(\alpha_{-n}^{n}[\lambda^{-1}])
by condition
[h1]
. Thus, by Lemma
2.4.3
we obtain
h(\lambda,\alpha)=h(\lambda^{-1},\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and therefore
h(\lambda)=h(\lambda^{-1})
. To complete the proof it is enough to apply Proposition
2.4.2
.
∎

2.5.
Comparison of entropies

In this section we introduce the
connections
between maps in order to compare their entropies. The tools developed will be useful to deduce properties of the concrete entropy theories from properties of the abstract entropy in §
4
.

Definition 2.5.1
.

For
i=1,2
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a map. We say that
\mu
is a
map from
\lambda_{1}
to
\lambda_{2}
, and denote
\mu\colon\lambda_{1}\to\lambda_{2}
or
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
, iff
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
is a map. Let
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
a map. We call
\mu
a
lower connection
iff
\mu
is a lower map (conditions
[l1]
and
[l2]
) verifying

[l
3
]

\mu\lambda_{1}\alpha\prec\lambda_{2}\mu\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
.

Analogously, if
\mu
is an upper map (conditions
[u1]
and
[u2]
) and

[u
3
]

\mu\lambda_{1}\alpha\succ\lambda_{2}\mu\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
,

then
\mu
is called
upper connection
. We say that
\mu
is a
connection
iff
\mu
is a homomorphism (conditions
[m1]
and
[m2]
) and

[m
3
]

\mu\lambda_{1}\alpha\sim\lambda_{2}\mu\alpha
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
.

A connection which is an isomorphism is called a
conjugation
. If there exists a conjugation
\lambda_{1}\to\lambda_{2}
we say that
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are
conjugate
.

Remark 2.5.2
.

For
i=1,2,3
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a map. If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{1}}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{2}}}{{\to}}\lambda_{3}
are lower connections, upper connections or connections, then their composition is again a lower connection, upper connection or connection
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{2}\mu_{1}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda_{3}
. If
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\to\mathscr{C}_{2}
is a bi-monotone map (see Remark
2.2.3
) we have that
\mu\colon\lambda_{1}\to\lambda_{2}
is a lower/

upper connection iff
\mu^{-1}\colon\lambda_{2}\to\lambda_{1}
is an upper/

lower connection. In particular, a bi-monotone connection is a conjugation. Finally, note that if
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a lower connection, upper connection or a connection and
\lambda_{2}
is monotone, then
\lambda_{1}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}^{k}
is a lower connection, upper connection or a connection for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 2.5.3
.

For
i=1,2
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space,
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a map such that
\lambda_{2}
is monotone and
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is an upper connection then
h(\lambda_{1},\alpha)\leq h(\lambda_{2},\mu\alpha)
.

2.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a lower connection then
h(\lambda_{1},\alpha)\geq h(\lambda_{2},\mu\alpha)
.

3.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a connection then
h(\lambda_{1},\alpha)=h(\lambda_{2},\mu\alpha)
.

Proof.

For the first assertion consider
n\geq 1
and let
\beta=\mu\alpha
. Then we have

\mu\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{1}]=\mu\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{1}^{k}\alpha\succ\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\mu\lambda_{1}^{k}\alpha\succ\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}\lambda_{2}^{k}\mu\alpha=\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{2}],

where we applied condition
[u1]
in the first inequality and the fact that
\lambda_{1}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}^{k}
is an upper connection (see Remark
2.5.2
) together with conditions
[u3]
and
[c2]
in the second. Hence, using conditions
[u2]
and
[h1]
we estimate
h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{1}])\leq h(\mu\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{1}])\leq h(\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{2}])
, and we conclude that
h(\lambda_{1},\alpha)\leq h(\lambda_{2},\beta)
as desired.

For the second statement, note that the proof given before works with the inequalities reversed, but this time arguing with conditions
[l1]
,
[l2]
and
[l3]
instead of
[u1]
,
[u2]
and
[u3]
, showing that
h(\lambda_{1},\alpha)\geq h(\lambda_{2},\mu\alpha)
for all
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
. Finally, the last assertion follows directly from the first two because to be a connection is the same as to be a lower and upper connection simultaneously.
∎

Proposition 2.5.4
.

For
i=1,2
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a map such that
\lambda_{2}
is monotone. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is an upper connection then
h(\lambda_{1})\leq h(\lambda_{2})
.

2.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a cofinal lower connection then
h(\lambda_{1})\geq h(\lambda_{2})
.

3.

If
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a cofinal connection then
h(\lambda_{1})=h(\lambda_{2})
.

4.

If
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are conjugate then
h(\lambda_{1})=h(\lambda_{2})
.

Proof.

For the first assertion simply take
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}}
in the inequality of Lemma
2.5.3
(1). The second follows similarly form Lemma
2.5.3
(2) applying Lemma
2.3.6
when taking
\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}}
. The last two claims are consequence of the first two.
∎

2.6.
Products, direct limits and coproducts

In this section we consider products, direct limits and coproducts of entropy spaces and compute the entropy of induced maps.

Construction 2.6.1
.

Let
I
be a non-empty finite set, for
i\in I
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space and let
(\alpha_{i})
and
(\beta_{i})
denote generic elements of the Cartesian product
\mathscr{C}=\tprod_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}
. We introduce an entropy space structure in
\mathscr{C}
as follows: we consider the product pre-order, the binary operation and the entropy function defined as
(\alpha_{i})\prec(\beta_{i})
iff
\alpha_{i}\prec\beta_{i}
for all
i\in I
,
(\alpha_{i})\wedge(\beta_{i})=(\alpha_{i}\wedge\beta_{i})
, and
h\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
h\bigl((\alpha_{i})\bigr)=\sum_{i\in I}h(\alpha_{i})
, respectively. Then
\mathscr{C}=\tprod_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}
is said to be the
product entropy space
of the family
(\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
and the lower morphisms
\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
given by
(\alpha_{i})\mapsto\alpha_{i}
are called
canonical maps
.

If
(\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
is family of maps then the map
\tprod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
given by
(\alpha_{i})\mapsto(\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i})
is called the
induced product map
.

If in Construction
2.6.1
the maps
\lambda_{i}
are monotone, l-maps, l-morphisms, u-maps, u-morphisms, homomorphisms, morphisms or isomorphisms, then so is
\tprod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}
.

Proposition 2.6.2
.

Let
I
be a non-empty finite set, for
i\in I
let
\mathscr{C}_{i}
be an entropy space and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a map. Then
h(\tprod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})\leq\sum_{i\in I}h(\lambda_{i})
. If in addition
\lambda_{i}
is lower map for all
i\in I
then
h(\tprod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})=\sum_{i\in I}h(\lambda_{i})
.

Proof.

Denote
\lambda=\tprod_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}
. Given
\alpha=(\alpha_{i})\in\tprod_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}
and
n\geq 1
it can be shown that
\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda]=(\alpha_{i}{}_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{i}])
, hence
h(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda])=\sum_{i\in I}h(\alpha_{i}{}_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{i}])
. Dividing by
n
, taking
\limsup_{n}
and then
\sup_{\alpha}
we get the inequality. If the maps
\lambda_{i}
are lower maps, by Lemma
2.3.8
we can take
\lim_{n}
instead of
\limsup_{n}
getting an equality.
∎

Construction 2.6.3
.

We briefly describe a construction of direct limits for entropy spaces. Given a non-empty directed set
(I,\leq)
, a family
(\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
of entropy spaces and
\Phi=(\varphi_{ij}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{j})_{i\leq j}
a
coherent
family of homomorphisms, that is
\varphi_{jk}\varphi_{ij}=\varphi_{ik}
if
i\leq j\leq k
, consider
\bigsqcup_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}
, the disjoint union of the
\mathscr{C}_{i}
’s, and let
\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
and
\beta_{j}\in\mathscr{C}_{j}
denote generic elements of this set. Let
\mathscr{C}=\nicefrac{{\bigsqcup_{i}\!\!\mathscr{C}_{i}}}{{\approx}}
be the quotient space by the equivalence relation
\approx
defined as
\alpha_{i}\approx\beta_{j}
iff there exists
k\geq i,j
such that
\varphi_{ik}\alpha_{i}=\varphi_{jk}\beta_{j}
, and let
[\alpha_{i}]
denote the equivalence class of
\alpha_{i}
. Define a pre-order in
\mathscr{C}
by
[\alpha_{i}]\prec[\beta_{j}]
iff
\varphi_{ik}\alpha_{i}\prec\varphi_{jk}\beta_{j}
for some
k\geq i,j
, a binary operation
[\alpha_{i}]\wedge[\beta_{j}]=[\varphi_{ik}\alpha_{i}\wedge\varphi_{jk}\beta_{j}]
where
k\geq i,j
, and an entropy function
h([\alpha_{i}])=h(\alpha_{i})
. Then
\mathscr{C}
is an entropy space denoted
\mathscr{C}=\lim_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}
and called
direct limit entropy space
of the
\mathscr{C}_{i}
’s. The
canonical maps

\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}
,
\alpha_{i}\mapsto[\alpha_{i}]
, are homomorphisms.

If
(\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of maps
compatible
with
\Phi
, that is,
\varphi_{ij}\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}\varphi_{ij}
if
i\leq j
, then the
induced limit map

\lim_{i}\lambda_{i}\colon\lim_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\lim_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}
is
[\alpha_{i}]\mapsto[\lambda_{i}\alpha_{i}]
.

If in Construction
2.6.3
the maps
\lambda_{i}
are monotone, l-maps, l-morphisms, u-maps, u-morphisms, homomorphisms, morphisms or isomorphisms, then so is
\lim_{i}\lambda_{i}
.

In the proof of the next result we have a first application of the tools of §
2.5
.

Proposition 2.6.4
.

For a directed family of monotone maps between entropy spaces
(\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
compatible with a coherent family of homomorphisms
(\varphi_{ij}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{j})_{i\leq j}
we have
h(\lim_{i}\lambda_{i})=\sup_{i}h(\lambda_{i})=\lim_{i}h(\lambda_{i})
.

Proof.

Denote
\mathscr{C}=\lim_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
\lambda=\lim_{i}\lambda_{i}
and for
i\in I
let
\mu_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}
be the canonical map. For
i\in I
,
\lambda_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{i}}}{{\to}}\lambda
is a connection, then by Lemma
2.5.3
(3) we know that that
h(\lambda_{i},\alpha_{i})=h(\lambda,\mu_{i}\alpha_{i})
for all
\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
. As
\mathscr{C}=\bigcup_{i\in I}\mu_{i}\mathscr{C}_{i}
we have

\begin{split}h(\lambda)&=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}h(\lambda,\alpha)=\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}}h(\lambda,\mu_{i}\alpha_{i})=\sup_{i\in I}\sup_{\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}}h(\lambda_{i},\alpha_{i})=\sup_{i\in I}h(\lambda_{i}).\end{split}

Finally, note that
\lambda_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varphi_{ij}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\lambda_{j}
is a connection for all
i\leq j
, then
h(\lambda_{i})\leq h(\lambda_{j})
by Proposition
2.5.4
(1). Thus the net
\bigl(h(\lambda_{i})\bigr)_{i\in I}
is increasing and we are done.
∎

Construction 2.6.5
.

Let
(\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
be a family of unital entropy spaces (see Remark
2.1.10
) with units denoted
{\uno}
. The
coproduct entropy space
of the
\mathscr{C}_{i}
’s is the set

\toplus_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}=\bigl\{(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}\in\tprod_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}:\alpha_{i}\neq{\uno}\text{ only for finitely many }i\text{'s}\bigr\},

with the entropy space structure given by the pre-order, the binary operation, and the entropy function defined for elements
(\alpha_{i})
and
(\beta_{i})
as
(\alpha_{i})\prec(\beta_{i})
iff
\alpha_{i}\prec\beta_{i}
for all
i\in I
,
(\alpha_{i})\wedge(\beta_{i})=(\alpha_{i}\wedge\beta_{i})
, and
h\bigl((\alpha_{i})\bigr)=\sum_{i\in I}h(\alpha_{i})
. The
canonical maps

\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}
,
\alpha_{i}\mapsto(\alpha_{ij})_{j\in I}
, where
\alpha_{ij}=\alpha_{i}
if

or
{\uno}

.

If
(\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
is a family of

, that is

for all

, the

,

.

If in Construction
2.6.5
the maps

are monotone, l-maps, l-morphisms, u-maps, u-morphisms, homomorphisms, morphisms or isomorphisms, then so is

.

Corollary 2.6.6
.

For a family of unital entropy spaces
(\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
and a family of monotone unital maps
(\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i})_{i\in I}
we have
h(\toplus_{i\in I}\lambda_{i})\leq\sum_{i\in I}h(\lambda_{i})
with equality if
\lambda_{i}
is a lower map for all

.

Proof.

The coproduct
\toplus_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}_{i}
is (isomorphic to) the direct limit
\lim_{F}\tprod_{i\in F}\mathscr{C}_{i}
, where
F
runs over the directed set
J
of finite subsets of

\tprod_{i\in F}\mathscr{C}_{i}
is the product entropy space as in Construction
2.6.1
, and the coherent homomorphisms are
\varphi_{EF}\colon\tprod_{i\in E}\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\tprod_{i\in F}\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
\varphi_{EF}\bigl((\alpha_{i})_{i\in E}\bigr)=(\alpha_{Ej})_{j\in F}
, for finite subsets

of

and
\alpha_{Ej}=\alpha_{j}
if

or
\alpha_{Ej}={\uno}
otherwise. The monotone map
\toplus_{i\in I}\lambda_{i}
is (conjugate to) the one induced in the direct limit by the compatible family of monotone maps
(\tprod_{i\in F}\lambda_{i})_{F\in J}

is the product of the maps
\lambda_{i}
for

as in Construction
2.6.1
. By Proposition
2.6.2
we know that

. Then applying Proposition

(4) and Proposition

as desired. The case of lower maps follows similarly because Proposition

∎

Construction 2.6.7
.

Let

, we define the

for

. Given maps
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
for

, the

is
\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2}\colon\mathscr{C}_{1}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{2}\to\mathscr{C}_{1}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{2}

.

If in Construction
2.6.7
the maps
\lambda_{i}
are monotone, l-maps, l-morphisms, u-maps, u-morphisms, homomorphisms, morphisms or isomorphisms, then so is

.

Lemma 2.6.8
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{+}\!\!\times\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the monotone and subadditive map given by
f(a,b)=\log(e^{a}+e^{b})
, if
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. For
i=1,2
let
(\mathscr{C}_{i},h_{i})
be an entropy space and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
a lower map. Then the induced product map on
\mathscr{C}_{1}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{2}
as in Construction
2.6.7
verifies
h_{f}(\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2})=\max\{h_{1}(\lambda_{1}),h_{2}(\lambda_{2})\}
.

Proof.

Given
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
and
\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{2}
define
a_{n}=h_{1}(\alpha_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{1}])
and
b_{n}=h_{2}(\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{2}])
for
n\geq 1
. As
\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2}
is a lower map because
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are lower maps, if
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathscr{C}_{1}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{2}
, by Lemma
2.3.8
we have

\textstyle h_{f}(\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2},\gamma)=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h_{f}(\gamma_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2}])=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}f(a_{n},b_{n}).

Therefore, as
\lim_{n}\frac{a_{n}}{n}=h_{1}(\lambda_{1},\alpha)

, by

h_{f}(\lambda_{1}\times\lambda_{2},\gamma)=\max\{h_{1}(\lambda_{1},\alpha),h_{2}(\lambda_{2},\beta)\}

3.
Abstract expansiveness

3.1.
Generators and expansiveness

In this section we extend the notion of expansiveness from the theory of topological dynamical systems to our context (see §
4.3.1
for more details). Here we will be interested mainly in morphisms on meet entropy spaces.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a cover space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a map. A cover
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
is called
positive generator
(or
positive expansivity cover
) for
\lambda
iff for all
\beta\in\mathscr{C}
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{0}^{m}[\lambda]\prec\beta
. If a positive generator
\alpha
for
\lambda
exists we call
\lambda

positively expansive
, or
positively
\alpha
-expansive
. If
\lambda
is bijective, a cover
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
is said to be a
generator
(or
expansivity cover
) for
\lambda
iff for every
\beta\in\mathscr{C}
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{-m}^{m}[\lambda]\prec\beta
. If a generator
\alpha
for
\lambda
exists we say that
\lambda
is
expansive
, or
\alpha
-expansive
.

In our motivating example, that is, a topological dynamical system
(X,T)
where
T
is a continuous map on a compact space
X
, we have that the associated map
\lambda_{T}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
(see the first paragraph of §
2.2
) is positively expansive according to Definition
3.1.1
iff
T
is
positively refinement expansive
as in
[
1
]
*Definition 3.17, which in turn is equivalent to the usual
positive expansivity
of
T
on a metric space (see
[
4
]
*p. 40) when
X
has the Hausdorff separation property. If
T
is a homeomorphism an analogous statement relating expansivity of
\lambda_{T}
, refinement expansivity of
T
and the usual expansivity on metric spaces holds, as explained in
[
2
]
*§1 and
[
1
]
*Theorem 3.13 and 2.7. In
[
2
]
*Definition 2.4 a notion of expansivity coherent with the previous ones is given at the intermediate level of lattices between expansivity in cover spaces and refinement expansivity.

In
[
12
]
*Definition 2.4 a definition of
topological generator
is introduced in the context of compact Hausdorff spaces, showing that the existence of a topological generator is equivalent to expansivity on metric spaces in
[
12
]
*Theorem 3.2. For a general compact space, that notion of generator is more restrictive than the one corresponding to Definition
3.1.1
at the topological level, the latter being the notion of
refinement expansivity cover
of
[
1
]
, which in turn corresponds to the
expansivity covers
of
[
2
]
at the lattice level.

In the following proposition, we present some basic properties that we have borrowed from the theory of expansive dynamical systems. To avoid introducing new terminology when discussing maps between cover spaces, we will use the same nomenclature as in §
2
, assuming that the entropy functions are trivially constant and equal to zero. Therefore, we should disregard any conditions related to the entropy functions.

Proposition 3.1.2
.

Let
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
i=1,2
, be maps between cover spaces. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
\lambda_{1}
is positively expansive,
\lambda_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}
is a cofinal upper connection and
\lambda_{2}
is monotone, then
\lambda_{2}
is positively expansive.

2.

If
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}
are monotone and conjugate then
\lambda_{1}
is positively expansive iff
\lambda_{2}
is positively expansive.

3.

The coproduct
\lambda_{1}\oplus\lambda_{2}
is positively expansive iff
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are positively expansive.

4.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
n>0
. If
\lambda^{n}
is positively expansive then
\lambda
is positively expansive. If
\lambda
is a lower map the converse statement also holds.

Statements analogous to 2., 3. and 4. hold for the property of expansiveness for isomorphisms of commutative cover spaces (in 4. we can take
n\in\mathbb{Z}
)

Proof.

For the first assertion, let
\alpha_{1}
be a positive generator for
\lambda_{1}
. We will show that
\alpha_{2}=\mu\alpha_{1}
is a positive generator for
\lambda_{2}
. Given a cover
\beta_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{2}
, as
\mu
is cofinal, there exists
\beta_{1}\in\mathscr{C}_{1}
such that
\mu\beta_{1}\prec\beta_{2}
. We know that
\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{1}^{k}\alpha_{1}\prec\beta_{1}
for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
because
\alpha_{1}
is a positive generator. Then

\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{2}^{k}\alpha_{2}=\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{2}^{k}\mu\alpha_{1}\prec\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\mu\lambda_{1}^{k}\alpha_{1}\prec\mu\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\lambda_{1}^{k}\alpha_{1}\prec\mu\beta_{1}\prec\beta_{2},

where in the first inequality we used that
\lambda_{1}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{2}^{k}
is an upper connection (see Remark
2.5.2
) together with conditions
[u3]
and
[c2]
, in the second inequality we applied condition
[u1]
, and in the third inequality we utilized monotonicity of
\mu
. This proves that
\alpha_{2}
is a positive generator as claimed.

The second statement follows form the first. For the third statement we simply indicate that it is easily checked that
(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})
is a positive generator for
\lambda_{1}\oplus\lambda_{2}
iff
\alpha_{i}
is a positive generator for
\lambda_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Finally, the fourth assertion can be shown as in
[
2
]
*Proposition 2.7, which corresponds to the invertible case.
∎

Next we turn our attention to the relationship between expansivity and entropy.

Lemma 3.1.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a meet entropy space,
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a morphism,
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
h(\lambda,\alpha_{0}^{m})=h(\lambda,\alpha)
, and
h(\lambda,\alpha_{-m}^{m})=h(\lambda,\alpha)
if
\lambda
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Cancelling repeated factors (see Remark
2.1.4
) we get
(\alpha_{0}^{m})_{0}^{n-1}\sim\alpha_{0}^{m+n-1}
for all
n\geq 1
. Then,
h(\lambda,\alpha_{0}^{m})=\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}h\bigl((\alpha_{0}^{m})_{0}^{n-1}\bigr)=\limsup_{n}\frac{1}{n}h(\alpha_{0}^{m+n-1})=\limsup_{n}\frac{m+n}{n}\frac{1}{m+n}h(\alpha_{0}^{m+n-1})=h(\lambda,\alpha)
. For the last statement, note that we have
(\alpha_{-m}^{m})_{0}^{n-1}\sim\lambda^{-m}\alpha_{0}^{2m+n-1}
, then
h\bigl((\alpha_{-m}^{m})_{0}^{n-1}\bigr)=h(\lambda^{-m}\alpha_{0}^{2m+n-1})=h(\alpha_{0}^{2m+n-1})
by condition
[m2]
, and the result follows similarly.
∎

The following result, which generalizes
[
12
]
*Theorem 2.6, indicates that it would be interesting to discover new examples of expansivity in the existing entropy theories in order to compute entropy. In
[
5
]
the authors start a work in this direction in the category of commutative rings.

Proposition 3.1.4
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a meet entropy space. If
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is a positively expansive morphism with positive generator
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, then
h(\lambda)=h(\lambda,\alpha)
, and this quantity is finite if
h
is finite and
\lambda
is a lower morphism. If
\lambda
is an expansive isomorphism with generator
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
then
h(\lambda)=h(\lambda,\alpha)
, and is finite if
h
is finite.

Proof.

For a positive generator
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
the subset
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}=\{\alpha_{0}^{m}[\lambda]:m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathscr{C}
is cofinal. Then by Lemma
2.3.6
and Lemma
3.1.3
we see that
h(\lambda)=h(\lambda,\alpha)
. The finiteness claim follows from Lemma
2.3.8
. The statement about expansive isomorphisms is obtained similarly by Lemma
2.4.3
.
∎

The next result improves
[

]
*Lemma 3.19 and
[

]
*Lemma 4.2, where the dynamical system is supposed to be invertible, whereas here we assume a weaker hypothesis. The statement says that, under certain circumstances, positive expansivity implies that a condition much stronger than the definition holds. It is a primitive version of the main theorem of
[
9
]
, which we extend in Proposition

Proposition 3.1.7
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a meet cover space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a positively expansive cofinal morphism. Then there exists
\beta\in\mathscr{C}
with the following properties: 1.
\lambda^{n}\beta\succ\lambda^{n+1}\beta
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, 2.  for all
\gamma\in\mathscr{C}
there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lambda^{n}\beta\prec\gamma
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
be a positive generator for
\lambda
. As
\lambda
is cofinal, there exists
\gamma\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\lambda\gamma\prec\alpha
. Then, as
\alpha
is a positive generator, we can find
m\geq 1
such that
\alpha\wedge\lambda\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{m-1}\alpha\prec\gamma
. Let
\beta=\alpha\wedge\lambda\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{m-1}\alpha
. Applying
\lambda
to the last inequality we get
\lambda\beta\prec\lambda\gamma\prec\alpha
. Then, by Remark
2.1.4
, we have

\alpha\wedge\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda^{2}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{m}\alpha=\alpha\wedge\lambda\beta\sim\lambda\beta=\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda^{2}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{m}\alpha,

and in general, applying
\lambda^{k}
to this equivalence for
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we get

\lambda^{k}\alpha\wedge\lambda^{k+1}\alpha\wedge\lambda^{k+2}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{k+m}\alpha\sim\lambda^{k+1}\alpha\wedge\lambda^{k+2}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{k+m}\alpha.

Then we see that in a product of
m+1
consecutive iterates of
\alpha
we can cancel the first factor. Therefore, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, cancelling repeatedly a first factor, we have

\alpha\wedge\lambda\alpha\wedge\lambda^{2}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{n+m-1}\alpha\sim\lambda^{n}\alpha\wedge\lambda^{n+1}\alpha\wedge\cdots\wedge\lambda^{n+m-1}\alpha=\lambda^{n}\beta.

From this, it is clear that
\lambda^{n}\beta\succ\lambda^{n+1}\beta
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and also that
\lambda^{n}\beta
will refine any given cover for a sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, because
\alpha
is a positive generator.
∎

Corollary 3.1.8
.

If
\mathscr{C}
is a meet entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
is a positively expansive cofinal lower morphism, then
h(\lambda)=0
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\in\mathscr{C}
as in Proposition
3.1.7
. As
\lambda^{n}\beta\succ\lambda^{n+1}\beta
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have
h(\lambda^{n}\beta)\leq h(\lambda^{n+1}\beta)
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by
[h1]
. On the other hand, by
[l2]
, the reverse inequality also holds, and then we obtain
h(\lambda^{n}\beta)=h(\beta)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. From the property
\lambda^{n}\beta\succ\lambda^{n+1}\beta
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by Remark
2.1.4
we also get
\beta_{0}^{n}[\lambda]\sim\lambda^{n}\beta
, for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
h(\beta_{0}^{n}[\lambda])=h(\lambda^{n}\beta)=h(\beta)
if
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and therefore

\textstyle h(\lambda,\beta)=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\beta_{0}^{n-1}[\lambda])=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}h(\beta)=0.

Now, applying Lemma
2.4.1
(3) we deduce
h(\lambda,\lambda^{n}\beta)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Finally, as by Proposition
3.1.7
(2) the set
\{\lambda^{n}\beta:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is cofinal, we see that
h(\lambda)=0
, by Lemma
2.3.6
.
∎

3.2.
Shifts

In this section we consider shift maps in the setting of entropy spaces and compute their entropies in Proposition
3.2.3
.

Definition 3.2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a unital cover space and
I=\mathbb{N}
or
\mathbb{Z}
. Consider the coproduct cover space
\mathscr{C}^{I}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathscr{C}
, and define the map
s^{I}\colon\mathscr{C}^{I}\to\mathscr{C}^{I}
given by
s^{I}(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}=(\alpha_{i-1})_{i\in I}
, where we agree that
\alpha_{-1}={\uno}
for the case
I=\mathbb{N}
. Either of these two maps is called
forward shift
. Given a unital map
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
we also consider a map
s_{\lambda}^{I}\colon\mathscr{C}^{I}\to\mathscr{C}^{I}
, which coincides with
s^{I}
if
\lambda=\id_{\mathscr{C}}
, defined by
s_{\lambda}^{I}(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}=(\lambda\alpha_{i-1})_{i\in I}
, that is,
s_{\lambda}^{I}=s^{I}\lambda^{I}=\lambda^{I}s^{I}
, where
\lambda^{I}\colon\mathscr{C}^{I}\to\mathscr{C}^{I}
is the coproduct map
\lambda^{I}=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\lambda
,
\lambda^{I}(\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}=(\lambda\alpha_{i})_{i\in I}
. Finally, given a cover
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
, define
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}}=(\alpha_{0i})_{i\in I}\in\mathscr{C}^{I}
where
\alpha_{0i}=\alpha
if
i=0
or
\alpha_{0i}={\uno}
otherwise.

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a unital cover space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a unital cofinal monotone map. Then
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}}
is positively
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
-expansive in the sense of Definition
3.1.5
, where
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}=\{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{N}}:\alpha\in\mathscr{C}\}
. If
\lambda
is a unital bi-monotone bijection then
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}
-expansive, where
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}=\{\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{Z}}:\alpha\in\mathscr{C}\}
.

Proof.

Given
(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\alpha_{i}={\uno}
if
i>m
. As
\lambda
is monotone and cofinal then so is
\lambda^{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence there exist
\beta_{k}\in\mathscr{C}
such that
\lambda^{k}\beta_{k}\prec\alpha_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
\beta=\twedge_{k=0}^{m}\beta_{k}
, so that
\lambda^{k}\beta\prec\alpha_{k}
for
0\leq k\leq m
. Then we have
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.32811pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\beta$}}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
and
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.32811pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\beta$}}_{0}^{m}[s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}}]=(\beta,\lambda\beta,\ldots,\lambda^{m}\beta,{\uno},{\uno},\ldots)\prec(\alpha_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
, showing that
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}}
is
\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}
-expansive. The statement about
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
follows similarly.
∎

For a meet entropy space
\mathscr{C}
we denote
h(\mathscr{C})=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}h(\alpha)=h(\id_{\mathscr{C}})
.

Proposition 3.2.3
.

Let
\mathscr{C}
be a unital meet entropy space and
\lambda\colon\mathscr{C}\to\mathscr{C}
a unital cofinal lower morphism. Then
h(s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}})\leq h(\mathscr{C})
, with equality if
\lambda
is also a homomorphism. If
\lambda
is a unital isomorphism then
h(s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}})=h(\mathscr{C})
. In particular for the forward shifts we have
h(s^{\mathbb{N}})=h(s^{\mathbb{Z}})=h(\mathscr{C})
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}
and
\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}}\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}\subseteq\mathscr{C}^{\mathbb{N}}
as in Lemma
3.2.2
. For
n\geq 1
we have

h(\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}}_{0}^{n-1}[s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}}])=h\bigl((\alpha,\lambda\alpha,\ldots,\lambda^{n-1}\alpha,{\uno},{\uno},\ldots)\bigr)=\textstyle\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h(\lambda^{k}\alpha)\leq nh(\alpha),

where the last inequality, given by condition
[l2]
, is an equality if
\lambda
is a homomorphism, by condition
[m2]
. Dividing by
n
and taking
\limsup_{n}
we get
h(s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}},\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}})\leq h(\alpha)
. As
\lambda
is a morphism one can check that
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}}
is a morphism too. Then, by Lemma
3.2.2
and Corollary
3.1.6
we have
h(s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}})=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}h(s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{N}},\hbox to0.0pt{\hskip 0.6985pt\hbox{$\overline{\hbox{}}$}\hss}{\hbox{$\alpha$}})\leq\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}}h(\alpha)
, with equality if
\lambda
is a homomorphism. The assertion on
s_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
follows similarly.
∎

4.
Examples and applications

In what follows, unless stated otherwise, we use the following notation:
X
denotes a topological space,
T\colon X\to X
is a continuous map, and
\prec
and
\wedge
denote the refinement relation and the meet operation, respectively, on families of sets, as described in §
1.2
.

4.1.
Topological entropy

Let us recall the definition of
topological entropy
introduced in
[
3
]
.

Definition 4.1.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an open cover of
X
. We define

\begin{gathered}N(\alpha)=\min\{|\beta|:\beta\subseteq\alpha\text{ and }X\subseteq\tcup\beta\},\qquad H(\alpha)=\log N(\alpha),\\
\textstyle h_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{-k}\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad h_{\textup{top}}(T)=\sup_{\alpha}h_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha),\end{gathered}

where the
\sup_{\alpha}
is taken over all open covers
\alpha
of
X
. Then
h_{\textup{top}}(T)
is the
topological entropy of

T
. We call
h_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)
the topological entropy of
T

of size
\alpha
.

Definition 4.1.2
.

Let
\mathscr{C}_{X}
be the set of all open covers of
X
,
h_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
given by
h_{X}(\alpha)=H(\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
, and
\lambda_{T}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
the map
\lambda_{T}\alpha=T^{-1}\alpha
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
.

In the next easy to prove statement, we record that the topological entropy is a particular instance of the abstract entropy developed in §
2
.

Proposition 4.1.3
.

The
4
-tuple
(\mathscr{C}_{X},\prec,\wedge,h_{X})
is a meet entropy space,
\lambda_{T}
is a lower morphism, and
h_{X}(\lambda_{T})=h_{\textup{top}}(T)
. Moreover,
h_{X}
is finite iff
X
is compact.

As a consequence of Proposition
4.1.3
we can derive various of the know properties of the topological entropy from the development made in §
2
. Some properties are obtained immediately. For example, a direct application of Lemma
2.3.7
shows that, for any open cover
\alpha
of
X
, the sequence
n\mapsto H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{-k}\alpha)
in the definition of
h_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)
is subadditive (see the proof of
[
3
]
*Property 8). Other properties need some little work to be obtained. For example, if
m\in\mathbb{N}
, from Proposition
2.4.2
we have
h_{X}\big((\lambda_{T})^{m}\big)=mh_{X}(\lambda_{T})
. Then, noticing that
(\lambda_{T})^{m}=\lambda_{T^{m}}
, we get
h_{\textup{top}}(T^{m})=mh_{\textup{top}}(T)
, which is
[
3
]
*Theorem 2. Similarly, when
T
is a homeomorphism, from Propositon
2.4.4
we obtain
h_{\textup{top}}(T^{m})=|m|h_{\textup{top}}(T)
for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, as in
[
3
]
*Corollary, p. 312.

On the other hand, some properties demand more elaborate arguments to be derived. To deal with these situations we use the tools for comparing entropies of §
2.5
. As a first example we consider the following result and proof, where
S\colon Y\to Y
denotes a continuous map on a topological space
Y
and
T\times S\colon X\times Y\to X\times Y
is the continuous map given by
(T\times S)(x,y)=(Tx,Sy)
if
(x,y)\in X\times Y
.

Proposition 4.1.4
.

If
X
and
Y
are compact then
h_{\textup{top}}(T\times S)\leq h_{\textup{top}}(T)+h_{\textup{top}}(S)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{Y}\to\mathscr{C}_{X\times Y}
be the map given by
\mu(\alpha,\beta)=\alpha\otimes\beta
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
and
\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{Y}
, where
\alpha\otimes\beta=\{U\times V:U\in\alpha,V\in\beta\}
. As can be easily checked,
\mu
is a lower morphism, that is,
\mu
is monotone, preserves the operation
\wedge
, and
h_{X\times Y}(\mu\gamma)=h_{X\times Y}(\alpha\otimes\beta)\leq h_{X}(\alpha)+h_{Y}(\beta)=h(\gamma)
if
\gamma=(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathscr{C}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{Y}
, where
h
denotes the entropy function of
\mathscr{C}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{Y}
as in Construction
2.6.5
.

On the other hand, we have
\mu\circ(\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S})=\lambda_{T\times S}\circ\mu
, therefore in particular
\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{T\times S}
is a lower connection. Moreover, as
X
and
Y
are assumed to be compact, it can be shown that every open cover of
X\times Y
is refined by some open cover of the form
\alpha\otimes\beta
, where
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
and
\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{Y}
(see the proof of
[
3
]
*Theorem 3), that is,
\mu
is a cofinal map. Therefore, by Proposition
2.5.4
(2), we have
h_{X\times Y}(\lambda_{T\times S})\leq h(\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S})=h_{X}(\lambda_{T})+h_{Y}(\lambda_{S})
, where the last equality comes from Corollary
2.6.6
. Thus,
h_{\textup{top}}(T\times S)\leq h_{\textup{top}}(T)+h_{\textup{top}}(S)
.
∎

Remark 4.1.5
.

Proposition
4.1.4
and its proof can be extended to the product of arbitrary many continuous maps.

Remark 4.1.6
.

Proposition
4.1.4
corresponds to
[
3
]
*Theorem 3 where an equality is claimed. However, as noted by Goodwyn in
[
10
]
, the proof given in
[
3
]
only shows the inequality we stated. In
[
10
]
*Theorem 2 the equality is proved assuming both
X
and
Y
are compact Hausdorff spaces.

Another example, showing how to use the results in §
2.5
comparing entropies, is the next property, which is an alternative formulation of
[
3
]
*Theorem 5.

Proposition 4.1.7
.

If
Y
is a topological space,
\pi\colon X\to Y
a continuous onto map, and
S\colon Y\to Y
a continuous map such that
\pi T=S\pi
, then
h_{\textup{top}}(S)\leq h_{\textup{top}}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{Y}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
the map given by
\mu\alpha=\pi^{-1}\alpha
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{Y}
. As can be easily checked,
\mu
is monotone, preserves the operation
\wedge
,
h_{X}(\mu\alpha)=h_{Y}(\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{Y}
, and
\mu\lambda_{S}=\lambda_{T}\mu
, that is,
\lambda_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{T}
is a connection. Then, by Proposition
2.5.4
(1), we conclude that
h_{Y}(\lambda_{S})\leq h_{X}(\lambda_{T})
, or equivalently,
h_{\textup{top}}(S)\leq h_{\textup{top}}(T)
.
∎

For the next application, we recall from
[
11
]
*§1 that a subspace
Y\subseteq X
is called
extension closed
iff for every open cover
\beta
of
Y
there exists an open cover
\alpha
of
X
such that
\beta=Y\wedge\alpha
. As noted in
[
11
]
*§1, closed subspaces are extension closed, and the converse holds if
X
is a Hausdorff space. Now we are ready to state the following minor extension of
[
3
]
*Theorem 4.

Proposition 4.1.8
.

If
X_{1},X_{2}
are extension closed subspaces of
X
such that
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
T(X_{i})\subseteq X_{i}
for
i=1,2
, then
h_{\textup{top}}(T)=\max\{h_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{1}}),h_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{2}})\}
.

Proof.

Let
i\in\{1,2\}
. To simplify the nomenclature let us denote the restriction of
T
to
X_{i}
as
T_{i}=T|_{X_{i}}
. Following the notation of Definition
4.1.2
, let
\mu_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X_{i}}
be the map
\mu_{i}\alpha=\alpha\wedge X_{i}
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. As can be easily checked,
\mu_{i}
is a morphism,
\mu_{i}\lambda_{T}=\lambda_{T_{i}}\mu_{i}
and
h_{X_{i}}(\mu_{i}\alpha)\leq h_{X}(\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. Hence,
\lambda_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu_{i}}}{{\to}}\lambda_{T_{i}}
is a lower connection. Moreover, as
X_{i}
is extension closed, we see that
\mu_{i}
is an onto map, and in particular a cofinal map. Then, by Proposition
2.5.4
(2), we conclude that
h_{X}(\lambda_{T})\geq h_{X_{i}}(\lambda_{T_{i}})
for
i=1,2
. Consequently, by Proposition
4.1.3
, we obtain
h_{\textup{top}}(T)\geq\max\{h_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{1}}),h_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{2}})\}
.

For the reverse inequality, consider the
f
-product
\mathscr{C}_{X_{1}}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{X_{2}}
as in Lemma
2.6.8
, and the map
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X_{1}}\times_{f}\mathscr{C}_{X_{2}}
given by
\mu\alpha=(\mu_{1}\alpha,\mu_{2}\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. This map is a morphism and
\mu\lambda_{T}=(\lambda_{T_{1}}\times\lambda_{T_{2}})\mu
. Given
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
it can be easily shown that
N(\alpha\wedge X_{1})+N(\alpha\wedge X_{2})\geq N(\alpha)
, where the quantities
N
are as in Definition
4.1.1
. Taking logarithms, we obtain
h_{f}(\mu\alpha)\geq h_{X}(\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
, and therefore
\lambda_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{T_{1}}\times\lambda_{T_{2}}
is an upper connection. Thus, by Proposition
2.5.4
(1) and Lemma
2.6.8
, we have
h_{X}(\lambda_{T})\leq h_{f}(\lambda_{T_{1}}\times\lambda_{T_{2}})=\max\{h_{X_{1}}(\lambda_{T_{1}}),h_{X_{2}}(\lambda_{T_{2}})\}
. Finally, an application of Proposition
4.1.3
ends the proof.
∎

We end this section obtaining part of
[
10
]
*Theorem 1, a result involving the topological entropy of inverse limits of dynamical systems. Following
[
10
]
*§3, given a directed set
(I,\leq)
, a family of continuous maps
(T_{i}\colon X_{i}\to X_{i})_{i\in I}
, where
X_{i}
is a topological space if
i\in I
, and a collection of continuous maps
(S_{ij}\colon X_{i}\to X_{j})_{i\geq j}
such that
S_{ij}\circ T_{i}=T_{j}\circ S_{ij}
for all
i,j\in I
,
i\geq j
, and
S_{jk}S_{ij}=S_{ik}
for all

,
i\geq j\geq k
, we define the

of this system as the continuous map
T\colon X\to X
, where
X=\{(x_{i})_{i\in I}\in\tprod_{i\in I}X_{i}:S_{ij}x_{i}=x_{j}\text{ for all }i,j\in I,i\geq j\}
with the relative product topology, and

Proposition 4.1.9
.

In the context of the preceding paragraph, if the spaces
X_{i}
are compact and the maps
S_{ij}
are onto, then
h_{\textup{top}}(T)=\sup_{i}h_{\textup{top}}(T_{i})=\lim_{i}h_{\textup{top}}(T_{i})
.

Proof.

For each
i\in I
define
(\mathscr{C}_{i},h_{i})=(\mathscr{C}_{X_{i}},h_{X_{i}})
, the entropy space associated to the space
X_{i}
, and
\lambda_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{i}
,
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{T_{i}}
, the lower morphism associated to
T_{i}
, as in Definition
4.1.2
. For
i\in I
let
\pi_{i}\colon X\to X_{i}
be the restriction to
X
of the canonical projection of
\tprod_{i\in I}X_{i}
on
X_{i}
, and
\mu_{i}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
the map
\mu_{i}\alpha_{i}=\pi_{i}^{-1}\alpha_{i}
if
\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
. As the maps
S_{ij}
are assumed to be onto maps, the maps
\pi_{i}
are onto maps too, and then the maps
\mu_{i}
are homomorphisms. For
i,j\in I
,
i\leq j
, consider the map
\varphi_{ij}\colon\mathscr{C}_{i}\to\mathscr{C}_{j}
given by
\varphi_{ij}\alpha_{i}=S_{ji}^{-1}\alpha_{i}
if
\alpha_{i}\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
.

As can be easily checked,
\Phi=(\varphi_{ij})_{i\leq j}
is a coherent family of homomorphisms and the family
(\lambda_{i})_{i\in I}
is compatible with
\Phi
as in Construction
2.6.3
. Then we can take the direct limits

\alpha_{i}\mapsto\mu_{i}\alpha_{i}

*Lemma 3. Moreover, as

.

4.2.
Topological mean dimension

Let us recall the definition of
mean dimension
introduced in
[
13
]
*Definition 2.6.

Definition 4.2.1
.

Let
\alpha
be an open cover of
X
. We define

\begin{gathered}\ord(\alpha)=\max\{|\beta|:\beta\subseteq\alpha\text{ and }\tcap\beta\neq\varnothing\}-1,\\
D(\alpha)=\min\{\ord(\beta):\beta\prec\alpha\text{ and }\tcup\beta=X\},\\
\textstyle\mdim(T,\alpha)=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}D(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{-k}\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad\mdim(T)=\sup_{\alpha}\mdim(T,\alpha),\end{gathered}

where the
\sup_{\alpha}
is taken over all open covers
\alpha
of
X
. Then
\mdim(T)
is the
mean dimension of

T
as in
[
13
]
. We call
\mdim(T,\alpha)
the mean dimension of
T

of size
\alpha
.

Consider
\mathscr{C}_{X}
and
\lambda_{T}
as in Definition
4.1.2
but this time define
h_{X}
as follows.

Definition 4.2.2
.

Let
h_{X}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
be the map
h_{X}(\alpha)=D(\alpha)
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
.

The next statement says that mean dimension is a particular instance of the abstract entropy developed in §
2
.

Proposition 4.2.3
.

The
4
-tuple
(\mathscr{C}_{X},\prec,\wedge,h_{X})
is a meet entropy space,
\lambda_{T}
is a lower morphism, and
h_{X}(\lambda_{T})=\mdim(T)
. Moreover,
h_{X}
is finite if
X
is compact.

Proof.

The only difficult fact to check is the property
D(\alpha\wedge\beta)\leq D(\alpha)+D(\beta)
if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. This is proved in
[
13
]
*Corollary 2.5.
∎

As in the case of topological entropy, form Proposition
4.2.3
and the results in §
2
, we can derive a series of properties of the mean dimension. For example, subadditivity of the sequence

, the logarithmic law

(see
[

*Proposition 2.7, where the property is stated only for

[

Next we introduce a result, not stated in

Proposition 4.2.4
.

If

is a homeomorphism the following statements hold.

1.

\mdim(T,\alpha)=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{2n+1}D(\twedge_{|k|\leq n}T^{k}\alpha)
, if

.

2.

\mdim(T^{n})=|n|\mdim(T)

Proof.

As

2.4.4

∎

4.3.
Topological expansiveness

4.3.1.
Classical expansive systems

The notion of

was introduced by Utz

then

. When

is compact this property does not depend on the choice of

[

]
.

If

is a topological space and
T
is a homeomorphism, we say that an open cover
\alpha

]
*Definition 2.4, where a strong generator is called simply generator. We added the word “strong” to distinguish this concept from the one corresponding to Definition
3.1.1

*Theorem 3.2 that if

If

\alpha

4.3.2.
Positively expansive embeddings

In the next application, we will use the concept of extension closed subspace already discussed before Proposition
4.1.8
. Recall that

is closed for every

. We say that

Proposition 4.3.1
.

If

is a positively refinement expansive embedding and

is extension closed, then

is finite.

Proof.

Given an open cover
\alpha
of

is an open cover of

. Then, as

is extension closed, there exists an open cover
\beta
of

such that

. We also have

. Then we showed that the morphism

there exists an open cover

of

[

]
*Theorem 3.20.
∎

Remark 4.3.2
.

Proposition

improves

4.3.3.
Topological generator systems

Applying Definition
3.1.5
to the map

of Definition

T

Definition 4.3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{A}
be a collection of open covers of

\mathscr{A}
is called a
positive generator system
for

\beta

such that

\mathscr{A}
is called a
generator system
for

\beta

This definition extends the concepts of generator and positive generator discussed in §

has only one element. The next result generalizes to this context the well known fact, also mentioned in §

, that

if

is a generator or a positive generator for

. Note that this generalization take into account not only the topological entropy but also the mean dimension.

Proposition 4.3.4
.

If

is a generator system or a positive generator system for

then
h_{\textup{top}}(T)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}h_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)
and
\mdim(T)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{A}}\mdim(T,\alpha)
.

Proof.

The statement is a consequence of Corollary
3.1.6
applied to the map
\lambda_{T}
acting on the entropy space
(\mathscr{C}_{X},h_{X})
, where

is either the entropy function associated to the topological entropy theory as Definition

, or the one associated to the mean dimension theory as in Definition
4.2.2
.
∎

Example 4.3.5

(Shifts)
.

Let
I=\mathbb{N}

\pi_{I}\bigl((x_{i})_{i\in I}\bigr)=x_{0}

In the next result, where

[

*Proposition 3.1.

Proposition 4.3.6
.

We have
\mdim(\sigma_{\mathbb{Z}})\leq\dim(X)
and
\mdim(\sigma_{\mathbb{N}})\leq\dim(X)
.

Proof.

First, let us consider the case of
\sigma_{\mathbb{N}}
. By Proposition
4.3.4
and Example
4.3.5
we have
\mdim(\sigma_{\mathbb{N}})=\sup_{\beta\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}}\mdim(\sigma_{\mathbb{N}},\beta)
. Then it is enough to show that
\mdim(\sigma_{\mathbb{N}},\beta)\leq\dim(X)
for every
\beta\in\mathscr{A}_{\mathbb{N}}

\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n}
of topological spaces

denote
\alpha_{0}\otimes\cdots\otimes\alpha_{n}=\{U_{0}\times\cdots\times U_{n}:U_{i}\in\alpha_{i}\text{ for }i=0,\ldots,n\}

Given

\beta=\alpha\otimes\{\tprod_{k=1}^{\infty}X\}

4.4.
Forward topological entropy

In this section, we consider an open onto map
T\colon X\to X
on a topological space
X
. We introduce the concept of
forward topological entropy
for such maps and establish some basic properties using the general theory presented in §
2
. We conclude by providing a few examples. Further developments on this topic, such as obtaining a definition
à la Bowen
, will be discussed in a subsequent paper.

Definition 4.4.1
.

Consider the quantity
H
of Definition
4.1.1
and let
\alpha
be an open cover of
X
. We define

\begin{gathered}\textstyle h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}\alpha)\quad\text{and}\quad h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)=\sup_{\alpha}h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha),\end{gathered}

where the
\sup_{\alpha}
is taken over all open covers
\alpha
of
X
. Then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)
is called the
forward topological entropy of

T
.

Consider
\mathscr{C}_{X}
and
h_{X}
as in Definition
4.1.2
but this time define
\lambda_{T}
as follows.

Definition 4.4.2
.

Let
\lambda_{T}\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{X}
be the map
\lambda_{T}\alpha=T\alpha
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
.

The following statement is easy to prove and records that the forward topological entropy is a specific instance of the abstract entropy developed in §
2
.

Proposition 4.4.3
.

The
4
-tuple
(\mathscr{C}_{X},\prec,\wedge,h_{X})
is a meet entropy space,
\lambda_{T}
is a lower map, and
h_{X}(\lambda_{T})=h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)
. Moreover,
h_{X}
is finite iff
X
is compact.

Remark 4.4.4
.

If
T
is a homeomorphism, then we have
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)=h_{\textup{top}}(T)
. This is because the definition of
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)
in this case is identical to the definition of
h_{\textup{top}}(T^{-1})
, and it is known that
h_{\textup{top}}(T^{-1})=h_{\textup{top}}(T)
(see
[
3
]
*Corollary, p. 312).

Proposition 4.4.5
.

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
\alpha\prec\beta
then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)\geq h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\beta)
.

2.

If
\mathscr{C}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{C}_{X}
is cofinal,
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)=\sup_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)=\lim_{\alpha\in\mathscr{C}^{\prime}}h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)
.

3.

The sequence
n\mapsto H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}\alpha)
is subadditive.

4.

We have
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)=\lim_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}\alpha)=\inf_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}H(\twedge_{k=0}^{n-1}T^{k}\alpha)
.

5.

If
X
is compact then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)
is finite.

Proof.

As
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T,\alpha)=h_{X}(\lambda_{X},\alpha)
and similarly for
\beta
, by Proposition
4.4.3
and Lemma
2.3.4
it follows (1). Analogously, (2) is a consequence of Lemma
2.3.6
, (3) follows from Lemma
2.3.7
, and from Lemma
2.3.8
we obtain (4) and (5).
∎

For an open onto map
S\colon Y\to Y
on a topological space
Y
, note that the product map
T\times S\colon X\times Y\to X\times Y
,
(T\times S)(x,y)=(Tx,Sy)
if
(x,y)\in X\times Y
, is an open onto map as well. Recall the concept of extension closed subspace discussed before Proposition
4.1.8
.

Theorem 4.4.6
.

Let
Y
be a topological space,
S\colon Y\to Y
an open onto map,
\pi\colon X\to Y
a continuous open onto map, and
X_{1}
,
X_{2}
extension closed subspaces of
X
such that
X=X_{1}\cup X_{2}
and
T^{-1}(X_{i})\subseteq X_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then the following statements hold.

1.

If
m\in\mathbb{N}
then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T^{m})=mh^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)
.

2.

If
X
and
Y
are compact then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T\times S)\leq h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)+h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(S)
.

3.

If
\pi T=S\pi
then
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(S)\leq h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)
.

4.

We have
h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T)=\max\{h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{1}}),h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T|_{X_{2}})\}
.

Proof.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}
it is easily checked that
\lambda_{T^{m}}=(\lambda_{T})^{m}
. Then by Proposition
4.4.3
and Proposition
2.4.2
we obtain

h^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T^{m})=h_{X}(\lambda_{T^{m}})=h_{X}\bigl((\lambda_{T})^{m}\bigr)=mh_{X}(\lambda_{T})=mh^{*}_{\textup{top}}(T),

from which the first assertion follows.

To show (2), consider the cofinal lower morphism
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{Y}\to\mathscr{C}_{X\times Y}
defined in the proof of Proposition
4.1.4
. It is easily checked that
\mu\circ(\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S})=\lambda_{T\times S}\circ\mu
. (Note that here
\lambda_{T}
,
\lambda_{S}
,
\lambda_{T\times S}
have a different meaning than in Proposition
4.1.4
.) Then, in particular
\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{T\times S}
is a cofinal lower connection. Hence, we have

h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(T\times S)=h_{X\times Y}(\lambda_{T\times S})\leq h(\lambda_{T}\oplus\lambda_{S})=h_{X}(\lambda_{T})+h_{Y}(\lambda_{S})=h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(T)+h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(S),

where the inequality comes from Proposition
2.5.4
(2), the middle equality is given by Corollary
2.6.6
, and

is the entropy function of
\mathscr{C}_{X}\oplus\mathscr{C}_{Y}
. This proves (2).

For the proof of (3), define
\mu\colon\mathscr{C}_{X}\to\mathscr{C}_{Y}
as
\mu\alpha=\pi\alpha
if
\alpha\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
, which is a well defined map because
\pi
is open and onto. It can be checked that
\mu
is a lower map, that is,
\mu
is monotone,
\mu(\alpha\wedge\beta)\prec\mu\alpha\wedge\mu\beta
and
h_{Y}(\mu\alpha)\leq h_{X}(\alpha)
, if
\alpha,\beta\in\mathscr{C}_{X}
. As

we obtain that
\mu\lambda_{T}=\lambda_{S}\mu
, then, in particular,
\lambda_{T}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mu}}{{\to}}\lambda_{S}
is a lower connection. Finally, since
\pi
is also assumed to be continuous, we have that
\mu
is onto and, in particular, a cofinal map. Therefore, from Proposition
2.5.4
(2) we obtain
h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(T)=h_{X}(\lambda_{T})\geq h_{Y}(\lambda_{S})=h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(S)
, and we are done.

The proof of (4) is identical, word for word, to the proof of Proposition
4.1.8
, but instead of using Proposition
4.1.3
, we argue with Proposition
4.4.3
. Note that in this context, the meanings of
\lambda_{T}
,
\lambda_{T_{1}}
, and
\lambda_{T_{2}}
are different from those in the cited proof. Here, the equality
\mu_{i}\lambda_{T}=\lambda_{T_{i}}\mu_{i}
, for

, is guaranteed by the assumption that
T^{-1}(X_{i})\subseteq X_{i}
for

. From this, we also have
\mu\lambda_{T}=(\lambda_{T_{1}}\times\lambda_{T_{2}})\mu
.
∎

4.4.7
we have
h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(T)=\max\{h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(\sigma_{\mathbb{N},X}),h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(\sigma_{\mathbb{Z},Y})\}=\max\{0,\log|Y|\}=\log|Y|
, and similarly
h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(S)=\log|Z|
. Therefore
h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(T)\neq h_{\textup{top}}^{*}(S)
. On the other hand, by Proposition
4.1.8
, we obtain
h_{\textup{top}}(T)=h_{\textup{top}}(S)=\infty
.

4.5.
Algebraic entropies and expansiveness

In this section, we will provide an overview of some algebraic entropy theories that fit into the framework of our abstract entropy. Additionally, we will propose a few new definitions of entropy and expansiveness within the algebraic context, with the aim of suggesting potential lines for further research. Although we do not have any particular applications or examples in mind, we hope that sharing these ideas will be helpful to others interested in exploring this direction.

4.5.1.
Algebraic entropies

The notion of
algebraic entropy
for endomorphisms of abelian groups is introduced in
[
3
]
*§5 and first studied in
[
17
]
. Given an abelian group
G
let
\mathscr{C}_{G}
be the set of all finite subgroups of
G
, and consider on
\mathscr{C}_{G}
the relation
\prec
and the binary operation
\wedge
defined for
E,F\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
as:
E\prec F
iff
F\subseteq E
, and
E\wedge F=E+F
(the subgroup generated by
E\cup F
), respectively. Also define
h_{G}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
by
h(F)=\log|F|
if
F\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
. Then
(\mathscr{C}_{G},\prec,\wedge,h_{G})
is a meet entropy space (where actually
\prec
is a partial order). If in addition
\varphi\colon G\to G
is an endomorphism let
\lambda_{\varphi}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}\to\mathscr{C}_{G}
be the map given by
\lambda_{\varphi}F=\varphi F
if
F\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
. Then
\lambda_{\varphi}
is a lower morphism (see
[
17
]
*Proposition 1.1) and the abstract entropy
h_{G}(\lambda_{\varphi})
is precisely the algebraic entropy
h(\varphi)
defined in
[
17
]
*Definition 1.1. We call this entropy
Weiss’ algebraic entropy
.

Later, the above definition was modified in
[
14
]
for automorphisms and in
[
7
]
for endomorphisms, where
\mathscr{C}_{G}
was replaced by the set of all finite
subsets
of
G
. The resulting notion of entropy is referred to as
Peters’ algebraic entropy
.

To mention a last algebraic example, we consider the
adjoint algebraic entropy
for group endomorphisms introduced in
[
6
]
. This entropy is obtained by taking
\mathscr{C}_{G}
as the set of all subgroups of finite index of an abelian group
G
,
\prec
and
\wedge
the relation and the operation on
\mathscr{C}_{G}
given by
E\prec F
iff
E\subset F
and
E\wedge F=E\cap F
, respectively, and
\lambda_{\varphi}
as the map taking the elements of
\mathscr{C}_{G}
to its preimage under the endomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to G
. Again,
(\mathscr{C}_{G},\prec,\wedge,h_{G})
is a meet entropy space and
\lambda_{\varphi}
is a lower morphism. The abstract entropy
h_{G}(\lambda_{\varphi})
obtained with the above choices is the adjoint algebraic entropy
\textrm{ent}^{\star}(\varphi)
defined in
[
6
]
*p. 5.

We refer the reader to
[
8
]
for more examples of purely algebraic nature.

4.5.2.
Backward algebraic entropy

In all of the examples discussed in §
4.5.1
, the function
\lambda
preserves the operation
\wedge
, making them particular instances of the semigroup entropy of
[
8
]
. Next, we propose a modification to Weiss’ algebraic entropy where
\lambda
no longer preserves
\wedge
, while still remaining a lower map.

Consider an
injective
group endomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to G
, and let
\mathscr{C}_{G}
,
\prec
,
\wedge
, and
h_{G}
be defined as in the Weiss’ algebraic entropy case. However, in this case, we define
\lambda_{\varphi}\colon\mathscr{C}_{G}\to\mathscr{C}_{G}
by
\lambda_{\varphi}F=\varphi^{-1}F
if
F\in\mathscr{C}_{G}
. While
(\mathscr{C}_{G},\prec,\wedge,h_{G})
remains a meet entropy space as before,
\lambda_{\varphi}
is now merely a lower map. The resulting entropy, which could be called
backward Weiss’ algebraic entropy
, will share properties with the abstract entropy because it fits within our context.

This “procedure of reversing the direction of entropies” is analogous to what we have done with the topological entropy obtaining a forward version of it in §
4.4
, and could potentially be applied to other entropies as well, such as Peters’ or adjoint entropies.

This ”procedure of reversing the direction of entropies” is similar to what we did with the topological entropy by obtaining a forward version of it in §
4.4
. It may also be possible to apply this procedure to other entropies, such as Peters’ or adjoint entropies.

4.5.3.
Algebraic expansivity

Given a group endomorphism
\varphi\colon G\to G
, if we apply Definition
3.1.1
to the map
\lambda_{G}
associated to the Weiss’ algebraic entropy as in §
4.5.1
, we obtain a definition of
positively expansive group endomorphism
. The endomorphism
\varphi
is positively expansive iff there exists a finite subgroup
F
of
G
, a
positive generator
, such that for every finite subgruop
E
of
G
there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that
E\subseteq\sum_{k=0}^{m}\varphi^{k}F
. If
G
is a torsion group this amounts to
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{k}F=G
.

While the Bernoulli shifts,
\sigma\colon H^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}\to H^{\oplus\mathbb{N}}
,
\sigma(h_{0},h_{1},\ldots)=(0,h_{0},h_{1},\ldots)
, where
H
is a finite abelian group, are positively expansive, Anna Giordano Bruno sketched a proof to me that if
\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\varphi^{k}F=G
, then it implies that
\varphi
is essentially a shift of that type. Then the way we defined positively expansive endomorphisms may not be interesting. However, it could be possible to obtain interesting notions of “algebraic expansivity” by applying Definition
3.1.1
in the context of other entropies.

An Upper Bound on the Weisfeiler-Leman Dimension The research leading to these results has received funding from the European Research Council (ERC) under the European Union’s Horizon 2020 research and innovation programme (EngageS: grant agreement No. 820148).
Thomas Schneider, Pascal Schweitzer

Abstract
The Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) dimension is a standard measure in descriptive complexity theory for the structural complexity of a graph. We prove that the WL-dimension of a graph on n n vertices is at most 3 / 20 ⋅ n + o ⁡ ( n ) = 0.15 ⋅ n + o ⁡ ( n ) 3/20\cdot n+o(n)=0.15\cdot n+o(n) . The proof develops various techniques to analyze the structure of coherent configurations. This includes sufficient conditions under which a fiber can be restored up to isomorphism if it is removed, a recursive proof exploiting a degree reduction and treewidth bounds, as well as an analysis of interspaces involving small fibers. As a base case, we also analyze the dimension of coherent configurations with small fiber size and thereby graphs with small color class size.

Introduction

In recent years, the Weisfeiler-Leman (WL) dimension has evolved to become a standard measure for the structural complexity of a graph. Initially coined in the context of isomorphism questions
[
Bab79
,
Wei76
,
IL90
]
, a plethora of equivalent reformulations in seemingly unrelated areas has surfaced (e.g.
[
CFI92
,
Dvo10
,
AM13
,
GO15
,
DGR18
,
AMR+19
]
). The concept in particular has applications in machine learning on graphs (see
[
MLM+23
]
for a survey).

In its initial formulation, the WL-dimension of a graph
G
is characterized as the minimum
k
required so that the
k
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm distinguishes
G
from every non-isomorphic graph.
By a central result of Cai, Fürer, and Immerman
[
CFI92
]
, the dimension plus one is also the least number of variables required to identify
G
in a particular logic (fixed-point logic with counting) and also the number of pebbles required in a particular combinatorial pebble game (the bijective pebble game).

In some sense, the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension measures how difficult it is to test isomorphism of one graph to others using a fairly general class of combinatorial algorithms.
Crucially, isomorphism between graphs of bounded Weisfeiler-Leman dimension can be decided in polynomial time. More precisely, if the WL-dimension is at most
k
, then the problem can be solved in time
O(n^{k+1}\log n)
.
While group theoretic techniques can circumvent the structural complexity given by high WL-dimension, the currently fastest theoretical algorithm, which runs in quasi-polynomial time
[
Bab16
]
, uses a
O(\log(n))
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm as a subroutine.

Many graph classes are known to have bounded Weisfeiler-Leman dimension (e.g., all classes with a forbidden minor
[
Gro17
]
and bounded clique width graphs
[
GN23
]
), giving a polynomial time isomorphism algorithm for these classes.

Initial hopes to find a general bound on the WL-dimension
of graphs, however, were dispelled by the seminal construction of Cai, Fürer, and Immerman
[
CFI92
]
which constructs graphs of order
n
and Weisfeiler-Leman dimension
\Omega(n)
.

In this paper we investigate explicit bounds for the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension. A calculation of the precise constant in
[
CFI92
]
yields a lower bound of
0.00465\cdot n
as demonstrated in
[
PVV06
,
PV09
]
. This constitutes the current best lower bound in the literature. The best explicit upper bound is
0.5n+1.5

[
PVV06
,
PV09
]
, which follows from upper bounds that apply in a more general context (more specifically from bounds on the “non-counting version” of the WL-algorithm).

Results.

As main result in this paper, we prove that for all graphs
\WLdim\left(G\right)\leq 3/20\cdot n+o(n)=0.15\cdot n+o(n)
.
As a side note, we also observe that for all orders there are graphs with a Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of at least
0.0105027\cdot n-o(n)
. As usual, these improved bounds can both be recast in logical terms, bounding the number of variables required for graph identification in fixed point-logic with counting.

Techniques.

On a macroscopic scale the idea for the upper bound proof is as follows. To facilitate recursion, we first generalize the problem to the realm of coherent configurations. These naturally generalize various highly regular graph families, such as strongly regular graphs and distance-regular graphs. They can be understood as the stable colorings under the 2-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm.
Increasing the dimension by at most 2, we can at any point in time, even during recursion, assume that all our objects are coherent configurations. The color classes of graphs translate into
fibers
of the coherent configuration.

The proof strategy is to reduce the number of vertices, with a focus on reducing those contained in large fibers. We repeatedly use individualizations (artificially assigning single vertices a different color) to simplify the coherent configuration. A vertex individualization decreases the dimension by at most 1.
We argue that, at a sublinear cost, we can ensure that the maximum color class size is sublinear (Section
8
).

With small fibers in mind, we investigate situations in which a color class can be removed without decreasing the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension. We call configurations
critical
if no fiber can be removed this way. Fibers of size at most
3
, which we call
tiny
, can always be removed.
For fibers that are not tiny, we develop a technique to determine from the combinatorial structure that a fiber is
restorable
and thus can be removed (Section
3
). The technique is based on extendability of automorphisms of induced subconfigurations.

Our base case of the recursion is the situation in which all fibers have size at most 7. We call such fibers
small
. We analyze the structure of the configurations that small fibers can induce, as well as the possible connections between small fibers (Section
4
). These connections are called
interspaces
. The
quotient graph
captures the structural information given by fibers and interspaces. Several reductions lead us to a quotient graph of maximum degree at most 3, at which point we can use bounds on the treewidth for cubic graphs to bound the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension.
Overall we show that a coherent configuration with fiber size at most
7
has Weisfeiler-Leman dimension at most
n/20+O(1)
(Section
7
).

For the recursion we also need to understand the possible interspaces between small and large fibers (Section
5
).
We define a potential function (Section
9
) that measures the progress we make towards the base case. It gives us the possibility to trade individualizations for a reduction of the potential. We then define a sequence of local reductions that, for various subconfigurations and types of interspace, provide a positive trade-off (Section
10
). We can thus inductively assume that these subconfigurations and interspaces are not present in our configuration.

To finally reach the base case, we employ a global argument. We introduce the concept of a
t
-reduced
configuration (Section
11
) and show that configurations to which none of the local reductions are applicable are
t
-reduced.
For reduced structures, the global argument allows us to separate the graph into pieces whose underlying structure either has small treewidth or which consist only of small fibers (Section
12
).
Overall this recursive approach proves the main theorem (Section
13
).

In contrast to the upper bounds, the lower bound simply follows straightforwardly by combining three known results on expansion, treewidth, and the CFI-construction in the evident fashion. An intermediate step in that argument is that random cubic graphs asymptotically almost surely have treewidth at least
0.04201\cdot n
(Section
14
).

Related Work.

A concrete classification of graphs with Weisfeiler-Leman dimension 1 is known
[
AKRV17
,
KSS22
]
.
Fuhlbrück, Köbler, and Verbitsky analyze the structure of

]

A recent generalization of their complexity results regarding the WL-dimension can be found in
[
LRS24
]

A survey on descriptive complexity in particular with bounds related to the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension can be found in
[
PV09
]
. We previously supervised a Bachelor’s thesis by Simon Lutz
[
Lut20

.

The term Weisfeiler-Leman dimension was coined by Grohe in his monograph
[
Gro17
]
. The main result implies that for non-trivial minor-closed graphs classes the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension is bounded. As remarked above, this is also true for graphs of bounded rank-width
[
GN23
]
. Recently, for several graph classes explicit bounds on the dimension have been proven, including planar graphs
[
KPS19
]
, distance hereditary graphs
[
GNP23
]
, and permutation graphs
[
GGP23
]
.

In independent work, Kiefer and Neuen
[
KN24

recently prove an upper bound on the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of
\lceil\frac{n}{4}\rceil+o(n)

Isomorphisms.

Weisfeiler-Leman Algorithm.

Given
k\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}
, the
k
-dimensional Weisfeiler-Leman algorithm
k-WL
is part of family of incomplete deciders for the
isomorphism problem
, which, given two uncolored graphs
G
and
H
, asks whether
G\cong H
holds.
Given the colored graph
(G,\chi)
, the algorithm
k-WL
determines for every
(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\in\Omega(G)^{k}
an initial coloring
\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{0}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})\coloneqq(\vartheta(v_{1}v_{1}),\vartheta(v_{1}v_{2}),\dots,\vartheta(v_{k}v_{k}))
where

\vartheta(vw)\coloneqq\begin{cases}(0,\chi(vw))&\text{if }v=w,\\
(1,\chi(vw))&\text{if }vw\in A(G),\\
(2,0)&\text{if }vw\notin A(G).\end{cases}

Next, it iteratively computes
\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i+1}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k})
defined by

(\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k}),\{\!\!\{(\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i}(w,v_{2},\dots,v_{k}),\dots,\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i}(v_{1},\dots,v_{k-1},w))\mid w\in\Omega(G)\}\!\!\})

for all
(v_{1},\dots v_{k})\in\Omega(G)^{k}
.
This process stops if
\pi(\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i})
is
stable under
k-WL
, that is
\pi(\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i})=\pi(\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i+1})
.
For the
i
at which the process stops, we define
\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{\infty}\coloneqq\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{i}
.

If the initially given graph is uncolored, we start the algorithm on the monochromatic version of it.
The algorithm
k-WL

distinguishes
graphs
(G,\chi_{G})
and
(H,\chi_{H})
if
\{\!\!\{\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{\infty}(\overline{v})\mid\overline{v}\in\Omega(G)^{k}\}\!\!\}\neq\{\!\!\{\chi^{\texttt{k-WL}}_{\infty}(\overline{v})\mid\overline{v}\in\Omega(H)^{k}\}\!\!\}
. The notation
(G,\chi_{G})\simeq_{k}(H,\chi_{H})
indicates that the graphs are not distinguished by
k-WL
.
The algorithm
k-WL

identifies

(G,\chi_{G})
if it distinguishes the graph
(G,\chi_{G})
from all non-isomorphic graphs. The
Weisfeiler-Leman dimension

\WLdim\left((G,\chi_{G})\right)
of a graph
(G,\chi_{G})
is the minimal
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that
k-WL
identifies
G
. Note that for a colored graph
(G,\chi_{G})
, the WL-dimension only depends on
\pi(\chi_{G})
and not on
\chi_{G}
itself.

Intuitively

works as follows:
in each iteration, the algorithm counts for each arc
(vw)
in

to
w

Many invariants are computed by

. It in particular it measures distances between vertices in

and is able to identify cycles and cliques.
It is known that
2-WL
identifies all uncolored graphs with order smaller than
16
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration.
If
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
is connected.

Proof sketch.

Since
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)=\max\{\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)[C]\mid\text{$C$ a connected component of\penalty\ $\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})$}\}
,
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
must consist of exactly one connected component.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration.
If
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then there are no distinct fibers
R,B\in\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
with
\Deg(B,R)=1
, that is, fibers with
|R|K_{1,\frac{|B|}{|R|}}\in\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
.

Proof sketch.

We argue that
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}-R\right)\geq\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
.
Recall that we can interpret coherent configuration as complete colored graphs.
Suppose that
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\simeq_{k}\mathfrak{X}
.
We can assume that the two configurations are defined on the same vertex set and their vertex colorings agree.
Then
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}[\Omega(\mathfrak{X})-B]\simeq_{k}\mathfrak{X}[\Omega(\mathfrak{X})-B]
.

It suffices now to observe that an isomorphism
\varphi
from
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}[\Omega(\mathfrak{X})-B]
to
\mathfrak{X}[\Omega(\mathfrak{X})-B]
extends to an isomorphism
\widehat{\varphi}
from
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
to
\mathfrak{X}
. The extension is defined as follows.
Let
U
be a basis relation in
\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
such that
(R\cup B,U)
is isomorphic to
|R|K_{1,\nicefrac{{|B|}}{{|R|}}}
.
For vertex
r\in R
choose a neighbor
b
in
B
with respect to
U
.
Set
\widehat{\varphi}(r)
to be the unique neighbor with respect to
U
of
\varphi(b)
. Since criticality requires
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\geq 2
, it follows from coherence that
\widehat{\varphi}(r)
is an isomorphism.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration.
If
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then there are no fibers
R,B\in\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
with
|R|=|B|
odd such that
\Deg(R,B)=2
.
In particular, if
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then there is no interspace

for positive integers

with

Proof.

Recall that
2-WL
is able to measure lengths of paths of particular colors between two vertices, and in particular, color-alternating paths between two vertices. Under the assumptions, for all

there is a unique

such that there are two color-alternating paths between

and

of equal length.
By Property

, the arcs

\mathfrak{X}
, and thus
|R|K_{1,1}\in\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
.
This contradicts Lemma

.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration.
If
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then there is no restorable union of fibers that is non-dominating.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a non-dominating restorable union of fibers in a
critical coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
. Let
\mathcal{B}
be the union of fibers that are not in
\mathcal{R}

\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})

\mathcal{R}

Recall that we can interpret coherent configuration as complete colored graphs.
So let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be a second coherent configuration for which

\mathcal{B}^{\prime}\coloneqq\chi^{\prime-1}{\chi(\mathcal{B})}

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
in classes corresponding to
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{B}

By the choice of

\varphi
from
\mathfrak{X}-\mathcal{R}

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}-\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}[\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\cup\mathcal{B}^{\prime}]

of
\mathfrak{X}[\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{B}]

.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration that is not homogeneous.
If
\mathfrak{X}
is critical, then there is no small fiber that can be properly partitioned into at most 3 modules.

Proof.

Let

.
Towards a contradiction, assume that there is a small fiber

which partitions into
i

By Property
(CC3)

.
Furthermore, due to their small size, we have
K_{i}\in\mathfrak{X}[M_{j}]
for all

Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be a copy of
\mathfrak{X}

where for all

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a coherent configuration.
Suppose
(R,B,Y)
is an induced path in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
,
r\in R
,
y\in Y
,
U\in\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
, and
U^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{X}[Y,B]
.
Then
\Deg\left(U\right)\cdot\Deg\left(U^{\prime}\right)=|B|\cdot|rU\cap yU^{\prime}|
.

In particular
\lcm\{|B|,\Deg\left(U\right)\}>1
and
\lcm\{|B|,\Deg\left(U^{\prime}\right)\}>1
. In particular
|B|
is not prime.

Proof.

Choose
y\in Y
and consider
yU^{\prime}
.
Since
\mathfrak{X}[R,Y]
is homogeneous, the number
|rU\cap yU^{\prime}|
is independent of the choice of
r\in R
or
y\in Y
.
The probability that for
r\in R
and
b\in B
chosen independently uniformly at random we have
rb\in U
is the same as the probability that for
r\in R
and
b\in yU^{\prime}
chosen independently uniformly at random we have
rb\in U
(because all vertices in
B
have the same degree with respect to
U^{\star}
).
On top of that, both probabilities are the same if we first fix an arbitrary
r
and then choose
b
only from
B
or
yU^{\prime}
, respectively.
Thus
\frac{\Deg\left(U\right)}{|B|}=\frac{|vU\cap yU^{\prime}|}{\Deg\left(U^{\prime}\right)}
.

Observe that
\Deg\left(U^{\prime}\right)>|rU\cap yU^{\prime}|\geq 1
and that
\Deg\left(U\right)>|rU\cap yU^{\prime}|\geq 1
. This the implies the second part of the theorem.
∎

5
Interspaces between large and small fibers

To study interspaces between large and small fibers we will introduce a specialized notation that concisely captures the attachment structure that vertices in the large fiber have in the small fiber.
The challenge is to capture the entire information for all constituents simultaneously.

For a relation
A
, we define
\ul(A)
to be
A\cup A^{\star}
.
We extend this notation to graphs and coherent configurations to obtain their
underlying undirected structure
.
For a coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
, we set
\ul(\mathfrak{X})
to be
(\Omega(\mathfrak{X}),\{\ul(A)\mid A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathfrak{X})\})

Observe that
\ul(\mathfrak{X})
is not necessarily coherent.
For a graph

\ul(G)
to be
(\Omega(G),\ul(A(G)))
and call it the
underlying undirected graph
of
G

Let

be distinct fibers of a coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
.
We say the interspace
\mathfrak{X}[L,S]
contains the
interspace pattern

(G^{1},d^{1}_{1},d^{1}_{2},\dots,d^{1}_{t_{1}};\dots;G^{k},d^{k}_{1},d^{k}_{2},\dots,d^{k}_{t_{k}})

\sum_{j=1}^{k}t_{j}=|\mathfrak{X}[L,S]|-1

we have
\ul(S,A^{i})\cong G^{i}
, and there are distinct

such that for all
\ell\in L
and

the set

induces a
d^{i}_{j}
-clique in
\ul(S,A^{i})
. We should emphasize that the sum of the

is

and thus we omit exactly one of the interspaces in the pattern. In fact, in general the missing interspace will not satisfy a clique condition. Also, it is a priori not clear that an interspace always has a pattern of this form we just defined.

Considering

, if for all

that induce a

-clique in

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a critical coherent configuration.

all fibers have the same size, or

(2)

Proof.

This follows directly from Lemmas
3.2

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a critical coherent configuration such that all fibers of
\mathfrak{X}
have size
4
.
A fiber
R
of
\mathfrak{X}
and all fibers adjacent to it in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
split entirely into tiny fibers in
\mathfrak{X}_{r}
where

\ColorDeg\left(R\right)\geq 4

\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)=2
or there is a critical coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
for which
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq 1+\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}_{r}\right)=1+\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\right)
with
|\Omega(\mathfrak{X})|\leq|\Omega(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})|-20
.

Proof.

Let
r\in R
and
\{B_{1},\dots,B_{\ColorDeg\left(R\right)}\}
be the neighborhood of
R
in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
.
Since
|R|=4
, fiber
R
splits into tiny fibers in
\mathfrak{X}_{r}
.
By Lemma
4.2
, all interspaces incident to
R
in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
contain a constituent isomorphic to
C_{8}
or
2K_{2,2}
.
Hence in
\mathfrak{X}_{r}
all fibers adjacent to
R
in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
split in half.
This means

has WL-dimension at least
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)-1

If
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)>2

\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that

Observe that

The lemma essentially allows us to reduce the degree of the quotient graph. For graphs with maximum degree at most
3
, we can make use of a bound by Fomin and Høie on the pathwidth of the graph.

Theorem 7.3

(see
[
FH06
]
)
.

For every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that every graph of maximum degree at most
3
with at at least
n
vertices satisfies
\treewidth(G)\leq\pathwidth(G)\leq\frac{n}{6}+\epsilon
.

We use the theorem to bound the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension for certain graphs as follows.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a critical coherent configuration on
n
vertices such that all fibers of
\mathfrak{X}
have size
4
and there is no interspace containing a constituent isomorphic to
C_{8}
.
If all fibers of
\mathfrak{X}
have at most
3
neighbors in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
, then
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq\frac{n}{24}+\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Proof Sketch.

If there is a dominating fiber
R
for which
\ColorDeg\left(R\right)\leq 3
, then
n\leq 16
.
Thus
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq 3
.
So assume there is no dominating fiber in
\mathfrak{X}
.
Since there are no interspace containing constituents isomorphic to
C_{8}
, Lemma
6.2
implies
|\ul(\mathfrak{X}[R])|=4
for all
R\in\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
.
By Lemma
6.4
, the quotient graph
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
is
3
-regular.
Furthermore, whenever we individualize a vertex
r
in a fiber
R
,
R
splits into singletons in
\mathfrak{X}_{r}
.

By Lemma
7.3
we have
\treewidth(\Quotient(\mathfrak{X}))\leq\frac{|\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)|}{6}+\varepsilon
for constant
\varepsilon>0
.
Let
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
be another coherent configuration satisfying the same assumptions.
Thus in the cops and robbers game played on the quotient graphs
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
and
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
there is a winning strategy for
t
cops, where
t\leq\frac{n}{6}+\varepsilon
.
We mimic this winning strategy now on the corresponding coherent configurations
\mathfrak{X}
and
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
in the bijective pebble game as follows (see e.g.
[
OS14
]
for more information on the connection between the two games).
Whenever we place a pebble on fiber
R
in
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X})
(respectively
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
) we now place the corresponding pebble on some vertex
r
of the corresponding fiber
R
in
\mathfrak{X}
(respectively
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
).
By the reasoning above this fiber splits into singletons after applying the coherent closure.
Therefore, Spoiler has a winning strategy if we add two additional pebbles to the game.

We conclude
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq\frac{|\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)|}{6}+2+\varepsilon\leq\frac{n}{24}+2+\varepsilon
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a critical coherent configuration on
n
vertices such that all fibers of
\mathfrak{X}
have size
4
.
Then
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq\frac{n}{20}+\mathcal{O}(1)
.

Proof.

First, we apply Lemma
7.2
repeatedly and restore the criticality after each application until the preconditions of Lemma
7.2
are no longer satisfied. Say this happens
k
times.
We then have a critical coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
with
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq k+\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)
and whose quotient graph
\Quotient(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})
has maximum degree
3
.
Observe that
|\Omega(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})|\leq n-20\cdot k
. Note that in the proof Lemma
7.2
we repeatedly take the coherent closure after individualizations and restore criticality, thus all fibers of
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
have size exactly
4
.

If there are distinct fibers
R,R^{\prime}
in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
such that
C_{8}\in\mathfrak{X}[R,R^{\prime}]
, then by Lemma
6.3
the set
\{R,R^{\prime}\}
is dominating.
Thus
|\Omega(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})|\leq 24
and
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\right)\leq 4\in O(1)
.

Now assume there is no interspace in
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
containing a constituent isomorphic to
C_{8}
.
Then we apply Theorem
7.4
and obtain
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\right)\leq\frac{|\Omega(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})|}{24}+\mathcal{O}(1)
.
Altogether we have

\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq 2+k+\max\{4,\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\right)\}\leq 2+k+\max\{4,\frac{n-20\cdot k}{24}+\mathcal{O}(1)\}.

Observe that

. Thus
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq\frac{n}{20}+\mathcal{O}(1)
.
∎

8
Limiting fiber sizes

For each interspace
\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
we arbitrarily choose a basis relation whose degree is maximal. A
non-maximal
basis relation is basis relation different than the chosen one.
Similarly for each fiber
R
we chose a constituent with maximal degree. A non-maximal constituent is one that is not the chosen one. Define
\overline{\Deg(B,R)}
to be the maximum degree among all degrees of non-maximal basis relations in
\mathfrak{X}[R,B]
. Set
\overline{\Deg(B,R)}=0
if
|\mathfrak{X}[R,B]|=1
.

Define the
max-modules
of a coherent configuration to be the connected components of the graph in which the edges are formed by all non-maximal basis relations. (The max-modules are sections in the sense of
[
Gol83
]
and colored modules in the sense of
[
Sch17
]
.)
It is not difficult to see that the Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of a coherent configuration is at most the maximum of
2
and the maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of its max-modules.

Lemma 8.1

(Color valence limit, (Zemlyachenko, see
[
Bab81
]
))
.

Let
\mathfrak{X}
be a critical coherent configuration, let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer.
There is a set of vertices
S
of size at most
\frac{2n}{d}
so that in the components of

\mathfrak{X}_{S}
we have
\overline{\Deg(B,R)}\leq d

for all (not necessarily distinct) fibers
R,B\in\Fibers\left(\mathfrak{X}_{S}\right)
.

Proof sketch.

Let us observe that it suffices to argue that we can decrease the degree bound from
2d^{\prime}
to
d^{\prime}
by individualizing
n/d^{\prime}
vertices as follows. Indeed, if this is possible then to reduce from a degree bound of
n
to
d
we use at most
n/d+n/(2d)+n/(4d)\ldots\leq 2n/d
individualizations.

Now to reduce from
2d^{\prime}
to
d^{\prime}
suppose
\overline{\Deg(R,B)}>d
.
We individualize a vertex in
B
and refine.
Then some subset of vertices in
R
of size more than
d
but size at most
2d
is separated from
R
. Thus the number of vertices contained in fibers of size more than
2d
reduces by more than
d
. This can only happen at most
n/d
times.
∎

Lemma 8.2

(Fiber size limit)

9
A potential function

For a coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
let
\parameters(\mathfrak{X})
be the triple
(n_{\ell},k_{\ell},n_{s})
, where
n_{\ell}
is the number of vertices in large fibers,
k_{\ell}
is the number of large fibers and
n_{s}
is the number of small fibers.

Let
\widehat{f}\colon\mathbb{N}^{3}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}
be the function that assigns to the triple
(n_{\ell},k_{\ell},n_{s})
the maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of a coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
with
\parameters(\mathfrak{X})=(n_{\ell},k_{\ell},n_{s})
.

Define the function
\tau\colon\mathbb{N}^{3}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
(n_{\ell},k_{\ell},n_{s})\mapsto\frac{3n_{\ell}+n_{s}-8k_{\ell}}{20}
.

Note that the parameters and function
\tau
are additive in the sense that we can compute the parameters separately for a configuration induced by an arbitrary partition of the fibers and then add them up.

We now define
f
to be the function that assigns to a coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}
the maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension among all coherent configurations
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
for which
\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}))\leq\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}))
. (In some sense
f
is a monotonization of
\widehat{f}
.)

Using this notation, note that the choice of potential function guarantees that if
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
is finer than
\mathfrak{X}
then
f(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})\leq f(\mathfrak{X})
.

We define
\widetilde{f}\colon\mathbb{R}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
with
r\mapsto\max\{f(\mathfrak{X})\mid\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}))\leq r\}
.

Our goal in the upcoming sections will be to show that
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}))+o(n)
. A key technique will be reductions that show that
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq t+f(\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})))
for some coherent configuration
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
obtained from
\mathfrak{X}
in a controlled manner, which will give a recursive bound of the form
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq t+\widetilde{f}(\tau(\parameters(\mathfrak{X}))-t)
.

10
Local reductions

In this section we provide local reductions,
trading progress in the potential with individualizations. This eventually allows us to restrict the structure of the coherent configuration in three ways:
First, it limits the number of neighbors a large fibers can have in the quotient graph.
Second, we eliminate certain interspace patterns. For some other interspace patterns we show that there can be at most one small neighboring fiber attached with this pattern.
Finally we reduce the quotient graph degree of fibers of size 4 or 6.

Each reduction proceeds as follows.
We examine the local structure of neighbors of a fiber. In particular, we describe the interspace patterns and the color degree.
We individualize one or two carefully chosen vertices, take the coherent closure, and restore criticality.
Afterwards we weigh the number of individualized vertices against the progress in the terms of the potential function
\tau
we made due to fibers that split.

Corollary 12.4
.

Let

.

Proof.

By the previous theorem we have
\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq r_{1}+r_{2}+\widetilde{f}(\tau(0,0,n_{s}-r_{1}\cdot 3-r_{2}\cdot 4))+3t

Using our bound for graphs with only small fibers (Theorem

\WLdim\left(\mathfrak{X}\right)\leq r_{1}+r_{2}+\frac{1}{20}(n_{s}-r_{1}\cdot 3-r_{2}\cdot 4)+3t

We maximize the function

∎

Theorem 13.1

If

14
Lower bound

In this section we assemble several known results to obtain an improved lower bound for the maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension of graphs in terms of their order.

Theorem 14.1
.

A random cubic graph asymptotically almost surely has a treewidth of at least
0.042011151\cdot n
.

Proof.

By
[
KW14
]
a random cubic graph asymptotically almost surely has vertex expansion (i.e., vertex-isoperimetric number) at least
\alpha\geq 0.144208556
(this is
A_{3}(1/2)
in
[
KW14
]
).
This implies that a random cubic graph asymptotically almost surely has treewidth at least
\frac{\alpha}{3(1+\alpha)}n\geq 0.042011151\cdot n

[
DN16
, Corollary 7]
.
∎

It is well known that cubic graphs of high treewidth yield graphs with high Weisfeiler-Leman dimension via the CFI-construction.
Specifically, we have the following relation.

Theorem 14.2

(Consequence of
[
DR07
, Theorem 3]
)
.

For a graph
G
, the CFI-graph
CFI(G)
with base graph
G
satisfies
\WLdim\left(G\right)\geq\treewidth(G)
.

We remark that there are two versions of the CFI-constructions used in the literature. One where each vertex is replaced by a gadget of order 10 and one where each vertex is replaced by a gadget of order 4. (See
[
Für01
,
NS17
,
Lic23
]
for more information.)
These versions are very similar and the theorem, as well as many other theorems, hold for either of them.
The difference between the constructions is that the former produces CFI-graphs of order
10|G|
, while the latter produces graphs of order
4|G|
. In the terminology of our current paper, the first version produces coherent configurations with fibers of size 2, so a non-critical configuration. Removal of the tiny fibers yields the other construction.

The two theorems combine as follows.

Corollary 14.3
.

The maximum Weisfeiler-Leman dimension for cubic graphs of order
n
is at least
0.0105027\cdot n-o(n)
.

In the light of our discussions in Section
4
we remark that, due to the nature of the CFI-construction, the statement is also true for graphs of color class size 4.

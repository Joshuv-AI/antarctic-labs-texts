High-order isospin-dependent surface tension contribution to the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei
Bao-Jun Cai, Rui Wang, Zhen Zhang, Lie-Wen Chen

Abstract
The relation between the fourth-order symmetry energy E sym,4 ​ ( ρ 0 ) E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0}) of nuclear matter at saturation density ρ 0 \rho_{0} and its counterpart a sym,4 ​ ( A ) a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A) of finite nuclei in a semiempirical nuclear mass formula is revisited by considering the high-order isospin-dependent surface tension contribution to the latter. We derive the full expression of a sym,4 ​ ( A ) a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A) , which includes explicitly the high-order isospin-dependent surface tension effects, and find that the value of E sym,4 ​ ( ρ 0 ) E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0}) cannot be extracted from the measured a sym,4 ​ ( A ) a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A) before the high-order surface tension is well constrained. Our results imply that a large a sym,4 ​ ( A ) a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A) value of several MeVs obtained from analyzing nuclear masses can nicely agree with the empirical constraint of E sym,4 ​ ( ρ 0 ) ≲ 2 E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})\lesssim 2 MeV from mean-field models and does not necessarily lead to a large E sym,4 ​ ( ρ 0 ) E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0}) value of ≈ 20 \approx 20 MeV obtained previously without considering the high-order surface tension. Furthermore, we also give the expression for the sixth-order symmetry energy a sym,6 ​ ( A ) a_{\textrm{sym,6}}(A) of finite nuclei, which involves more nuclear matter bulk parameters and the higher-order isospin-dependent surface tension.

II
Symmetry Energies of Finite Nuclei: Relevant Status Review

We start our discussions by reviewing the status of the symmetry energy of finite nuclei.
For a finite nucleus with
N
neutrons and
Z
protons, the total difference
\Delta=N-Z
could be decomposed into two parts as
\Delta_{\textrm{v}}=N_{\textrm{v}}-Z_{\textrm{v}}
and
\Delta_{\textrm{s}}=N_{\textrm{s}}-Z_{\textrm{s}}
, representing the isospin difference in the bulk
of the nucleus and that distributed on its surface.
Naturally one has
\Delta=\Delta_{\textrm{v}}+\Delta_{\textrm{s}}
. As more isospin asymmetry moves to the surface, physically the nucleus becomes looser, indicating that the surface tension of the nucleus becomes smaller compared with the one in which no isospin asymmetry is distributed on the surface. Moreover, as either neutrons or protons are distributed more on the nucleus surface, the surface tension
\sigma=a_{\textrm{sur}}/(4\pi r_{0}^{2})
, where
r_{0}
is the nuclear matter radius parameter defined by
4\pi\rho_{0}r_{0}^{3}/3=1
, always decreases. Consequently, the surface tension with some isospin asymmetry distributed on the nucleus surface could be written as
Dan03

\sigma=\sigma_{0}-\gamma\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}=\sigma_{0}\left[1-(\gamma/\sigma_{0})\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}\right],

(3)

where
\mu_{\textrm{a}}=(\mu_{\textrm{n}}-\mu_{\textrm{p}})/2
is the chemical potential difference between neutrons and protons, and
\gamma
is a parameter.
The leading non-trivial contribution starting at order
\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}
reflects the aforementioned symmetry between neutrons and protons.

Based on the relation (
3
), Ref.
Dan03
derived a closed expression for the symmetry energy of finite nuclei incorporating the surface effects, see formula (
2
) where
\alpha\equiv a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{v}}
is the coefficient in front of
(N_{\textrm{v}}-Z_{\textrm{v}})^{2}/A
, and
\beta\equiv 1/16\pi r_{0}^{2}\gamma\equiv a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{s}}
.
The
a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{v}}
and

are the volume (bulk) symmetry energy and the surface symmetry energy

;

In the limit of large

In addition, the relation is further established between the coefficient

III
Fourth-order Symmetry Energy of Finite Nuclei: Full Expression

Generally, one can obtain the following equations to determine the bulk and surface isospin asymmetries as well as the chemical potential difference in finite nuclei
Dan03
,

\Delta_{\textrm{v}}+\Delta_{\textrm{s}}=\Delta,~~2\alpha\Delta_{\textrm{v}}/A-\mu_{\textrm{a}}=0,~~\Delta_{\textrm{s}}/S+\mathrm{d}\sigma/\mathrm{d}\mu_{\textrm{a}}=0,

(6)

where
S=4\pi r_{0}^{2}A^{2/3}
is the surface area of the nucleus via the relation
R=r_{0}A^{1/3}

It should be pointed out that the equations shown in (
6
) are not complete in the sense that as higher order terms like the
\gamma^{\prime}\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{4}
would induce a high order symmetry energy in finite nuclei related to the surface properties, one needs to add the bulk term
4\alpha_{4}(\Delta_{\textrm{v}}/A)^{3}\equiv 4E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})(\Delta_{\textrm{v}}/A)^{3}
originating from the fourth-order symmetry energy to the second equation and to solve self-consistently. However, since this part was already obtained in Ref.
WangRui17
, for the purpose of the present work, we will not give the detailed derivations here and only focus on the surface contribution.

III.1
Effective Symmetry Energies for Finite Nuclei

By introducing the function
f=\sigma/\sigma_{0}
, one obtains in the situation
f=1-\theta\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}\equiv 1+y
(with
\theta\equiv\gamma/\sigma_{0}

\Delta_{\textrm{v}},\Delta_{\textrm{s}}

\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{v}}}{\Delta}=\frac{1}{1+\phi},~~\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{s}}}{\Delta}=\frac{\phi}{1+\phi},~~\mu_{\textrm{a}}=\frac{2\alpha}{A}\frac{\Delta}{1+\phi},

The effective symmetry energy (appearing in the mass formula in the form of

a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)=\left.\left[\frac{\alpha\Delta_{\textrm{v}}^{2}}{A}+\mu_{\textrm{a}}\Delta_{\textrm{s}}+\sigma_{0}S(f-1)\right]\right/AI^{2},

which includes the effects from higher order symmetry energies as
a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)\approx a_{\textrm{sym}}(A)+a^{\textrm{s}}_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A)I^{2}+\cdots

In particular, for
f=1-\theta\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}
the effective symmetry energy
a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)
reduces to
a_{\textrm{sym}}(A)=\alpha/(1+\alpha/\beta A^{1/3})

Moreover, in this simple model there is only one effective parameter
\theta
, and it is determined uniquely by the surface symmetry energy coefficient
\beta
and the surface tension
\sigma_{0}
.
Similarly,
the effective fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei is defined as

a_{\textrm{a,4}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)=[{a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)-a_{\textrm{sym}}(A)}]/{I^{2}},

(9)

which contains even higher order contributions, e.g., the sixth-order symmetry energy.

As long as the function
f
is depending on the combination
y=-\theta\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}
, i.e.,
f=f(y)
, it could be proved straightforwardly that the bulk and the surface isospin asymmetries are given, by generalizing the first two relations of (

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{v}}}{\Delta}=

\displaystyle\left(1+\phi\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}y}\right)^{-1},~~\frac{\Delta_{\textrm{s}}}{\Delta}=\phi\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}y}\left(1+\phi\frac{\mathrm{d}f}{\mathrm{d}y}\right)^{-1}.

(10)

In this situation, the effective symmetry energy of finite nuclei can be obtained as

\displaystyle a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)=

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(1+2\phi{\mathrm{d}f}/{\mathrm{d}y})}{(1+\phi{\mathrm{d}f}/{\mathrm{d}y})^{2}}+\frac{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}}{I^{2}A^{1/3}}\left[f(y)-1\right],

(11)

and the

should be self-consistently obtained by solving the three equations of (
6

As

N-Z\to\infty

III.2
Surface Fourth-order Symmetry Energy

Formula (
11
) itself could be used to derive the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei.
We start from formula (
11
) by assuming that the function
f
takes the form
f\approx 1+y+\kappa y^{2}
where
\kappa=-\gamma^{\prime}\sigma_{0}/\gamma^{2}
is an effective parameter characterizing the fourth-order contribution to the isospin splitting of the
f=\sigma/\sigma_{0}
[see Eq. (
4
)]. Consequently one has
f^{\prime}\equiv\mathrm{d}f/\mathrm{d}y=1+2y\kappa
, and the first term in Eq. (
11
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha(1+2\phi f^{\prime})}{(1+\phi f^{\prime})^{2}}\approx\frac{\alpha(1+2\phi)}{(1+\phi)^{2}}-\frac{4\phi^{2}y\alpha}{(1+\phi)^{3}}\kappa,

(12)

to order
\kappa
.
Similarly the second term in Eq. (
11
) is expanded as
{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}(y+y^{2}\kappa)}/{I^{2}A^{1/3}}
,
where the first term here gives

\frac{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}y}{I^{2}A^{1/3}}\approx-\frac{\alpha\phi}{(1+\phi)^{2}}-\frac{16\alpha^{3}\phi^{2}\theta I^{2}\kappa}{(1+\phi)^{5}}.

(13)

Summing the first term of Eq. (
12
) and the first term of Eq. (
13
) gives the familiar formula for symmetry energy of finite nuclei, i.e.,
a_{\textrm{sym}}(A)=\alpha/(1+\phi)
.

Since we assume
\kappa
is small, the
y

4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}y^{2}\kappa/I^{2}A^{1/3}
could be approximated as

y\approx-{4\theta\alpha^{2}I^{2}}/{(1+\phi)^{2}}
(recalling that
\Delta/A=I
),
and moreover
\mu_{\textrm{a}}\approx{2\alpha I}/({1+\phi})

One then obtains

\displaystyle-\frac{4\alpha\phi^{2}y}{(1+\phi)^{3}}\approx

\displaystyle\frac{16\alpha^{3}\phi^{2}\theta}{(1+\phi)^{5}}I^{2},~~\frac{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}y^{2}}{I^{2}A^{1/3}}\approx\frac{4\alpha^{3}\phi\theta}{(1+\phi)^{4}}I^{2},

(14)

where the relation

{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}y}/{I^{2}A^{1/3}}\approx-{\alpha\phi}/{(1+\phi)^{2}}

is used for obtaining
{4\pi r_{0}^{2}\sigma_{0}y^{2}}/{I^{2}A^{1/3}}

Combining all these terms gives the effective symmetry energy of finite nuclei to order

a_{\textrm{a}}^{\textrm{eff}}(N,Z)\approx\frac{\alpha}{1+\phi}+\frac{4\alpha^{3}\phi\theta\kappa I^{2}}{(1+\phi)^{4}},

the coefficient in front of

of the second term gives

a^{\textrm{s}}_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A)=\frac{4\alpha^{3}\theta\kappa\phi}{(1+\phi)^{4}}=\left.\frac{4\alpha^{4}\theta\kappa}{\beta A^{1/3}}\right/\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{\beta A^{1/3}}\right)^{4}.

(16)

\displaystyle a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A)=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{0})}{\beta A^{1/3}}\right)^{-4}

\displaystyle\times\left(E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})-\frac{L^{2}}{2K_{0}}+\frac{4\theta\kappa E_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}(\rho_{0})}{\beta A^{1/3}}\right).

III.3
Implications of the Smallness of
E_{\textmd{sym},4}(\rho_{0})

Since the derivation of the term
4\theta\kappa E_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}(\rho_{0})/\beta A^{1/3}
is independent of the bulk contribution
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})-L^{2}/2K_{0}
, the two terms are additive. Here the
\theta\kappa
-term in Eq. (
III.2
) approaches to zero as
A\to\infty
since the surface disappears for infinite matter. On the other hand, the bulk term approaches to a constant (independent of
A
), i.e.,
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})-L^{2}/2K_{0}
as
A\to\infty
.
It is now clear that one could still have a
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})\lesssim 2\,\textrm{MeV}
to be consistent with microscopic calculations, irrespective of the value of
a^{\textrm{s}}_{\textrm{sym},4}(A)
since the surface contribution only affects the finite nuclei.
Solving Eq. (
III.2
) for
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})
gives the expression for
E_{\textrm{sym},4}(\rho_{0})

a_{\textrm{a,4}}
(as obtained from nucleus mass formula fitting) should not necessarily lead to a large
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})
, and the balance strongly depends on the higher order coefficient
\kappa
, which has very little influence on nuclear structure quantities such as the surface tension of certain typical finite nuclei.

V
Summary and conclusions

We have shown that for the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei, it could be naturally decomposed into two terms characterizing the bulk and the surfaces contributions, respectively.
The surface contribution
4\theta\kappa E_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}(\rho_{0})/\beta A^{1/3}/(1+E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{0})/\beta A^{1/3})^{4}\sim 4\theta\kappa E_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}(\rho_{0})/\beta A^{1/3}
characterized by the product of
\theta\kappa
, to the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei is obtained by introducing the next leading order contribution to the isospin dependence of the nuclear surface tension coefficient through
\sigma/\sigma_{0}\approx 1-\theta\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}+\kappa\theta^{2}\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{4}
, where
\kappa
is an effective parameter characterizing the high order effects.
Although the
\kappa
parameter may induce a sizable
4\theta\kappa E_{\textrm{sym}}^{4}(\rho_{0})/\beta A^{1/3}
, it has very little impact on the nuclear structure quantities of interest such as the surface tension itself (since
y=-\theta\mu_{\textrm{a}}^{2}
is generally small than unity).
Since this term is independent of the
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})
, it characterizes the coupling between the symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym}}(\rho_{0})
and the high order coefficient
\kappa
, i.e., it is induced by some high-order isospin dependent surface tension effects.
Although both the surface contribution and the bulk term to the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei could be large, they contribute little to the nuclear structure quantities, since generally one has
I^{4}\lesssim 0.003
for finite nuclei. This means that the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei is usually hard to “probe” and special observables are needed (see, e.g., Refs.
Jia14
;
Jia15
;
Tia16
).

On the other hand, all the microscopic many-body theories and phenomenological model predictions give the consistent constraint that
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})\lesssim 2
MeV, indicating the
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})
could not be large although its counterpart of finite nuclei could be sizable.
One needs to consider the total fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei composed of both the bulk and the surface contributions. In this sense the surface contribution characterized by
\kappa
to the fourth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei is fundamental, i.e., it is essential for explaining a reasonable
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})\lesssim 2\,\textrm{MeV}
and a sizable
a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A)
simultaneously.
Essentially, it is hard to constrain the parameter
\kappa
via finite-nucleus information.
In the future, unless certain quantities/processes determining the coefficient
\kappa
to within some narrow range are available, it seems that one can hardly constrain the fourth-order symmetry energy
E_{\textrm{sym,4}}(\rho_{0})
of nuclear matter from the nuclear mass formula fitting on
a_{\textrm{sym,4}}(A)
, since a finite nucleus has a surface while the infinite matter does not.

Finally, we also present the expression for the sixth-order symmetry energy of finite nuclei, which is related to more nuclear matter bulk parameters and the higher-order isospin-dependent surface tension.
We would like to mention that
although the higher-order symmetry energies of finite nuclei are difficult to measure in terrestrial nuclei, they could be potentially useful for understanding the properties of neutron star crust or supernova explosions where extremely neutron-rich clusters may exist.

A syntactic approach to continuity of T-definable functionals
Chuangjie Xu

Abstract
. We give a new proof of the well-known fact that all functions ( ℕ → ℕ ) → ℕ (\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N} which are definable in Gödel’s System T \mathrm{T} are continuous via a syntactic approach. Differing from the usual syntactic method, we firstly perform a translation of System T \mathrm{T} into itself in which natural numbers are translated to functions ( ℕ → ℕ ) → ℕ (\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N} . Then we inductively define a continuity predicate on the translated elements and show that the translation of any term in System T \mathrm{T} satisfies the continuity predicate. We obtain the desired result by relating terms and their translations via a parametrized logical relation. Our constructions and proofs have been formalized in the Agda proof assistant. Because Agda is also a programming language, we can execute our proof to compute moduli of continuity of T \mathrm{T} -definable functions.

1.
Introduction

The usual syntactic method for proving properties of terms in Gödel’s System
\mathrm{T}
works as follows: (1) Define a predicate
P_{\rho}\subseteq\rho
for the designated property by induction on the finite type
\rho
,
i.e.

\begin{array}[]{rll}P_{\mathbb{N}}(n)&:\equiv&\cdots\\[2.0pt]
P_{\sigma\to\tau}(g)&:\equiv&\forall x^{\sigma}\left(P_{\sigma}(x)\to P_{\tau}(g(x))\right).\end{array}

(2) Prove that each term
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
satisfies
P_{\rho}
by induction on
t
. Examples of properties for which such syntactic methods work include totality
[
18
]
and majorizability
[
11
]
. Our goal is to recover the well-known fact that all
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
are continuous
[
2
]
. But the above syntactic method does not seem to work directly, because we do not know how to define the base case
P_{\mathbb{N}}
such that
P_{(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}}(f)
expresses the continuity of
f
.

Our idea is, as a step (0), to perform a translation
(t\mapsto t^{\mathrm{b}}):\rho\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
of System
\mathrm{T}
into itself where
\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}:\equiv(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.
natural numbers are translated to functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
. Further, any function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
is pointwise equal to
f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)
, where
f^{\mathrm{b}}:(\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}})\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
is the translation of
f
and
\Omega:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
is a
\mathrm{T}
-definable generic element in the sense of
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
. Then our step (1) is to inductively define a continuity predicate
\mathrm{C}_{\rho}\subseteq\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
: the base case
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}}(f)
states the continuity of
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
and the one for function spaces is defined in the usual way as above. And our step (2) is to prove
\mathrm{C}_{\rho}(t^{\mathrm{b}})
for all terms
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
by induction on
t
. Moreover, the generic element
\Omega
also satisfies the continuity predicate
\mathrm{C}
. We thus have
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}}(f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega))
,
i.e.

f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)
is continuous, for any
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
. Because continuity is preserved under pointwise equality, we know that
f
is also continuous. As pointed out by one of the anonymous referees, our results that any term is related to its translation (Lemma
1
) and that the translation of any term satisfies the continuity predicate (Lemma
4
) are instances of the
fundamental theorem of logical relations

[
19
]
.

Our development is constructive and has been formalized in the Agda proof assistant
[
1
]
. The main purpose of this formalization is to execute our Agda proof which is also a computer program to compute moduli of continuity, rather than merely certify the correctness of our work. Some sample computations of moduli of continuity are provided in our Agda development
[
25
]
.

Motivation

Oliva and Steila
[
13
]
give a direct proof of Schwichtenberg’s theorem that the terms of Gödel’s System
\mathrm{T}
are closed under the rule of Spector’s bar recursion of types 0 and 1
[
16
]
. By induction on terms, they explicitly construct a functional of their notion of
general bar recursion
for each term in
\mathrm{T}
, and then turn it into a functional of Spector’s bar recursion. The author implemented their work in Agda
[
24
]
and recognized that some part of their correctness proof
[
13
, Theorem 3.4]
can be adapted to compute moduli of continuity. For each term
t
in
\mathrm{T}
, a bar is constructed to control the behavior of the general-bar-recursion functional for
t
. This bar essentially contains the continuity information of
t
. The Agda code of this part of the correctness proof was then separated, refined and further developed under the inspiration of Escardó’s Agda development of dialogue trees
[
5
]
, which led to the first version of the Agda implementation of this paper
[
25
]
.

Related work

Kohlenbach
[
10
]
obtains the uniform continuity of terms
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in System
\mathrm{T}
as an application of his pointwise version of strong majorization. In particular, his logical relation for majorization on functions from natural numbers is given in a pointwise way. He shows that every term
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
is pointwise strongly majorized by some
t^{*}:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
by induction on
t
. For any
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
, one can show in the intuitionistic system WE-HA
ω
that it is extensional. From such a proof, Kohlenbach extracts a term via the Dialectica interpretation and then uses the majorant of this term to construct a modulus of uniform continuity of
f
.

Coquand and Jaber’s approach to continuity
[
3
,
4
]
is also syntactic. The difference is that they obtain continuity information by an operational method: they extend dependent type theory with a new constant
\mathsf{f}
for a generic element, and then decorate its operational semantics with forcing information to keep track of approximation information about
\mathsf{f}
as the computations proceed. The continuity information of a function
F
is extracted from the computation of
F(\mathsf{f})
. They tackle uniform continuity of functions from the Cantor space
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2}
, where
\mathbbm{2}:\equiv\{0,1\}
, and also discuss how to adapt their argument for continuity of functions from the Baire space
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}

[
4
, Section 1.2.3]
. They also provide a Haskell implementation for System
\mathrm{T}
using a monad combined by the list monad and the state monad as an appendix in
[
4
]
. The algorithm to extract continuity information in their operational method restricted to System
\mathrm{T}
can be represented as a monadic translation of System
\mathrm{T}
into itself which is an instance of
[
14
, Section 4]
.

Inspired by Coquand and Jaber’s work, Escardó also employs a generic element but in his dialogue tree model to prove (uniform) continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
[
5
]
. He has a concrete notion of generic element given by a function
\tilde{\mathbb{N}}\to\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
, where
\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
is the set of dialogue trees. Suppose a function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is denoted by a term
t
whose dialogue interpretation is a function
\tilde{f}:(\tilde{\mathbb{N}}\to\tilde{\mathbb{N}})\to\tilde{\mathbb{N}}
. Applying
\tilde{f}
to the generic element, a dialogue tree which contains the (uniform) continuity information of
f
is obtained. In one version of the Agda implementation of
[
5
]
, Escardó uses Church encodings of dialogue trees to turn his semantic interpretation into a compositional translation of System
\mathrm{T}
into itself. In this way, he extracts from the Church encoding of the dialogue tree interpreting
t
a term
m
in
\mathrm{T}
which internalizes the modulus of continuity of
f
. We provide a direct and explicit construction of terms in
\mathrm{T}
internalizing moduli of continuity in Section
4.1
.

Our method is also related to the sheaf model
[
26
,
6
,
22
]
introduced by Escardó and the author. To prove uniform continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions, it is sufficient to work with the subcategory of concrete sheaves which admit a more intuitive description as what we call
\mathrm{C}
-spaces. A
\mathrm{C}
-space is a set
X
equipped with a certain collection of maps
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\to X
which are called probes on
X
, where
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}:\equiv\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2}
. A
\mathrm{C}
-continuous map of
\mathrm{C}
-spaces is a function whose composition with a probe is again a probe. For instance, the set
\mathbb{N}
with all uniformly continuous maps
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\to\mathbb{N}
forms a
\mathrm{C}
-space; so does
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}
with all uniformly continuous maps
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\to{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}
. A crucial tool in this semantic method is the Yoneda Lemma which says that a function
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\to X
into a
\mathrm{C}
-space
X
is
\mathrm{C}
-continuous iff it is a probe on
X
. Each term
t:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
is interpreted as a
\mathrm{C}
-continuous map
{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}\to\mathbb{N}
in the category of
\mathrm{C}
-spaces and thus, by the Yoneda Lemma, a probe on
\mathbb{N}
,
i.e.
a uniformly continuous function. Interestingly, the proof of one direction of the Yoneda Lemma is essentially the same proof of that
\Omega
satisfies a certain uniform-continuity predicate studied in Section
4.2
. To investigate the relationship between our syntactic method and sheaf models
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
21
]
is left as one of our future tasks.

Another different approach to continuity is to use computational effects such as exceptions
[
12
,
15
]
. Suppose a function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
and a sequence
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
are given. We can find a number
m
such that the value of
f(\alpha)
depends only on the first
m
positions of
\alpha
(
i.e.
a modulus of continuity of
f
at
\alpha
) as follows: An exception is thrown if
f
attempts to compute
\alpha(n)
for
n\geq k
where
k
is a variable parameter. We start with computing
f(\alpha)
with
k=0
. Once an exception is caught, we try
k+1
. At some point no exception happens and the current value of
k
is a modulus of continuity of
f
.

Organization

Section
2
introduces the
\mathrm{b}
-translation of System
\mathrm{T}
as a preliminary for the syntactic method. Section
3
employs the syntactic method on the
\mathrm{b}
-translation of System
\mathrm{T}
to prove continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
. Section
4
strengthens the result by constructing terms in System

which internalize moduli of continuity, and studies uniform continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}
. The last section discusses how to generalize the method for proving properties of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions of arbitrary finite types.

2.
Gödel’s System
\mathrm{T}
and the
\mathrm{b}
-translation

We work with Gödel’s System
\mathrm{T}
in its lambda-calculus form. Recall that the term language of
\mathrm{T}
is (equivalent to) a simply typed lambda calculus extended with natural numbers and a primitive recursor. The constants and equations (
i.e.
computational rules) associated to the ground type
\mathbb{N}
include

•

the natural number
0:\mathbb{N}
,

•

the successor function
\mathrm{succ}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, and

•

the primitive recursor
\mathrm{rec}:\rho\to(\mathbb{N}\to\rho\to\rho)\to\mathbb{N}\to\rho
with

\mathrm{rec}(a)(f)(0)=a\qquad\mathrm{rec}(a)(f)(\mathrm{succ}\ n)=f(n)(\mathrm{rec}(a)(f)(n))

for every finite type
\rho
.

A function is called
\mathrm{T}
-
definable
if there exists a closed term in
\mathrm{T}
denoting it. In the paper, we do not distinguish
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions and their corresponding
\mathrm{T}
-terms. Moreover, we may write
n+1
rather that
\mathrm{succ}(n)
, and
\alpha_{i}
rather than
\alpha(i)
for
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
and
i:\mathbb{N}
.

As discussed earlier, it does not seem possible to directly apply the syntactic method to prove continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
. Hence we ‘precook’
\mathrm{T}
-terms so that continuity becomes the base case of a predicate which all ‘precooked’ terms will satisfy. We call this procedure the
\mathrm{b}
-
translation
where
\mathrm{b}
stands for the Baire type/space, because natural numbers are translated to functionals from the Baire type
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.

{defi}

[
\mathrm{b}
-translation]

For each finite type
\rho
we associate a finite type
\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
inductively as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}\\[2.0pt]
(\sigma\to\tau)^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}\to\tau^{\mathrm{b}}.\end{array}

Assume a given mapping of variables
x:\rho
to variables
x^{\mathrm{b}}:\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
. For any term
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
, we define
t^{\mathrm{b}}:\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
inductively as follows:

\begin{array}[]{rll}(x)^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&x^{\mathrm{b}}\\[2.0pt]
(\lambda x.u)^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&\lambda x^{\mathrm{b}}.u^{\mathrm{b}}\\[2.0pt]
(fa)^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&f^{\mathrm{b}}a^{\mathrm{b}}\\[2.0pt]
0^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&\lambda\alpha.0\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{succ}^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&\lambda f\alpha.\mathrm{succ}(f\alpha)\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}&:\equiv&\lambda af.\mathrm{ke}(\mathrm{rec}(a)(\lambda k.f(\lambda\alpha.k)))\end{array}

where
\mathrm{ke}_{\rho}:(\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}})\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
is inductively defined by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathrm{ke}_{\mathbb{N}}(g)(f)&:\equiv&\lambda\alpha.g(f\alpha)(\alpha)\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{ke}_{\sigma\to\tau}(g)(f)&:\equiv&\lambda x.\mathrm{ke}_{\tau}(\lambda k.g(k)(x))(f).\end{array}

In the above definition, the only difficulty arrises when translating the primitive recursor: To be a sound translation,
\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}:\rho^{\mathrm{b}}\to(\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}})\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
has to preserve the computational rules of
\mathrm{rec}
,
i.e.

\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}
should satisfy

\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}(a)(f)(0^{\mathrm{b}})=a\qquad\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}(a)(f)(\mathrm{succ}\ n)^{\mathrm{b}}=f(n^{\mathrm{b}})(\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}(a)(f)(n^{\mathrm{b}}))

where
k^{\mathrm{b}}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
is the constant function
\lambda\alpha.k
for any
k:\mathbb{N}
.
One suitable candidate for such
\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}(a)(f):\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
is
\mathrm{rec}(a)(\lambda k.f(k^{\mathrm{b}}))
but it has type
\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
. In general, we can extend a function
g:\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
to
g^{*}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
such that
g^{*}(i^{\mathrm{b}})=g(i)
for all
i:\mathbb{N}
, by induction on the finite type
\rho
. We write
\mathrm{ke}
to denote the extension function (
g\mapsto g^{*}
) as it behaves like a
Kleisli extension
for functions
\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
. However, in general, our
\mathrm{b}
-translation does not seem to be a monad, let alone a functor.

Our first goal is to prove certain “equality” between any closed term
f:{(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}}
and its translation
f^{\mathrm{b}}
so that once
f^{\mathrm{b}}
satisfies a predicate for some property such as continuity in Section
3
then so does
f
. We firstly relate terms and their
\mathrm{b}
-translations using the following
parametrized logical relation
that was introduced in a version of the Agda implementation of
[
5
]
.

{defi}

For any
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, we define a logical relation
\mathrm{R}_{\rho}^{\alpha}\subseteq\rho^{\mathrm{b}}\times\rho
by

\begin{array}[]{rll}f\ \mathrm{R}_{\mathbb{N}}^{\alpha}\ n&:\equiv&f(\alpha)=n\\[2.0pt]
g\ \mathrm{R}_{\sigma\to\tau}^{\alpha}\ h&:\equiv&\forall x^{\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}},y^{\sigma}\left(x\ \mathrm{R}_{\sigma}^{\alpha}\ y\to g(x)\ \mathrm{R}_{\tau}^{\alpha}\ h(y)\right).\end{array}

We may omit the subscript and simply write
\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}
if it can be inferred from the context.

Lemma 1
.

For any term
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
, we have

t^{\mathrm{b}}\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ t

for all
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, assuming
x^{\mathrm{b}}\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ x
for all
x\in\mathrm{FV}(t)
.

Proof 2.1
.

Let
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be given. We carry out the proof by structural induction over
t
. As the others are trivial, here we prove only the case
t\equiv\mathrm{rec}
with the following claims:

(1)

The Kleisli extension
\mathrm{ke}
preserves the relation
\mathrm{R}^{\alpha}
, i.e.

\mathrm{ke}(g)\ \mathrm{R}_{\mathbb{N}\to\rho}^{\alpha}\ h

for any
g:\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
and
h:\mathbb{N}\to\rho
with
g(i)\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ h(i)
for all
i:\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 2.2
.

By induction on
\rho
.

(2)

Given
x:\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
and
y:\rho
with
x\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ y
, and
f:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
and
g:\mathbb{N}\to\rho\to\rho
with
f\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ g
,

\mathrm{rec}(x)(\lambda k.f(\lambda\alpha.k))(i)\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \mathrm{rec}(y)(g)(i)

for all
i:\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 2.3
.

By induction on
i
.

By applying (1) to (2), we get a proof of
\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}}\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \mathrm{rec}
.

Hence we have
f^{\mathrm{b}}\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ f
for any closed
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
. Unfolding it according to Definition
2
, we can see that a term
\Omega:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \alpha
is needed in order to get the equality
f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)(\alpha)=f(\alpha)
. Such a term
\Omega
can be viewed as a
generic element

[
3
,
4
]
or
generic sequence

[
5
]
, and can be easily defined by unfolding
\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \alpha
: because
\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \alpha
is unfolded to

\forall f^{(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}},n^{\mathbb{N}}\left(f(\alpha)=n\to\Omega(f)(\alpha)=\alpha(n)\right)

by replacing
n
by
f\alpha
as they are equal by assumption, we define
\Omega:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
by

\Omega(f)(\alpha)\ :\equiv\ \alpha(f\alpha).

Then the following lemma is trivial but is necessary for deriving our first result.

Lemma 2
.

For any
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, we have

\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \alpha.

Our first result follows directly from Lemmas
1
and
2
.

Theorem 3
.

For any closed term
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
,

f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)(\alpha)=f(\alpha)

for all
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
.

3.
Continuity of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functionals

After “precooking”
\mathrm{T}
as above, we can now carry out the steps of the usual syntactic method. The crucial difference is that the predicate to work with is defined instead on elements of the
\mathrm{b}
-translated types, so that continuity of functions
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
becomes the base case of the predicate. With a proof by induction on terms, we show that the
\mathrm{b}
-translation of any term in
\mathrm{T}
satisfies the predicate. The case for closed terms of type
(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
will bring us the desired result.

Recall that a function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is
continuous
if for any sequence
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
there exists
m:\mathbb{N}
, called a
modulus of continuity
of
f
at the point
\alpha
, such that any sequence
\beta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
which is equal to
\alpha
up to the first
m
positions gives the same result. In
e.g.

\mathrm{HA}^{\omega}
, the continuity of
f
can be formulated as

\forall\alpha^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}\ \exists m^{\mathbb{N}}\ \forall\beta^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}\ \left(\alpha=_{m}\beta\to f(\alpha)=f(\beta)\right)

where
\alpha=_{m}\beta
stands for
\forall i<m\ \alpha_{i}=\beta_{i}
. It is obvious that continuity is preserved under pointwise equality in the sense that if
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is continuous then so is any function that is pointwise equal to
f
.

We define a continuity predicate on elements of the
\mathrm{b}
-translated types and show that the
\mathrm{b}
-translation of any term in
\mathrm{T}
satisfies the predicate.

{defi}

We define a unary predicate
\mathrm{C}_{\rho}\subseteq\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
inductively on
\rho
by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}}(f)&:\equiv&\text{$f$ is continuous}\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{C}_{\sigma\to\tau}(g)&:\equiv&\forall x^{\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}}\left(\mathrm{C}_{\sigma}(x)\to\mathrm{C}_{\tau}(g(x))\right).\end{array}

Lemma 4
.

For any term
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
, we have

\mathrm{C}_{\rho}(t^{\mathrm{b}})

assuming
\mathrm{C}(x^{\mathrm{b}})
for all
x\in\mathrm{FV}(t)
.

Proof 3.1
.

By induction on the term
t
. Here we prove only the case
t\equiv\mathrm{rec}
with two claims.

(1)

The Kleisli extension
\mathrm{ke}
preserves the predicate
\mathrm{C}
, i.e.

\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}\to\rho}(\mathrm{ke}(g))

for any
g:\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
such that
\mathrm{C}_{\rho}(g(i))
for all
i:\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 3.2
.

By induction on
\rho
. (i)
\rho=\mathbb{N}
. Given
g:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
with
g(i)
continuous for all
i:\mathbb{N}
and
f:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
continuous, we need to show that
\mathrm{ke}(g)(f)
is continuous. Given
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, let
m
be the modulus of
g(f\alpha)
at
\alpha
and let
n
be the modulus of
f
at
\alpha
. Take
k:\equiv\max(m,n)
. Given
\beta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\alpha=_{k}\beta
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\mathrm{ke}(g)(f)(\alpha)&=&g(f\alpha)(\alpha)\\
&=&g(f\alpha)(\beta)&&(\text{by the continuity of }g(f\alpha))\\
&=&g(f\beta)(\beta)&&(f\alpha=f\beta\text{ by the continuity of }f)\\
&=&\mathrm{ke}(g)(f)(\beta).\end{array}

(ii)
\rho=\sigma\to\tau
. Given
g:\mathbb{N}\to\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}\to\tau^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\mathrm{C}_{\sigma\to\tau}(g(i))
for all
i:\mathbb{N}
,
f:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}}(f)
and
a:\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\mathrm{C}_{\sigma}(a)
, we have to show
\mathrm{C}_{\tau}(\mathrm{ke}(g)(f)(a))
. Define
h:\mathbb{N}\to\tau^{\mathrm{b}}
by
h(i):\equiv g(i)(a)
. Then we have
\mathrm{ke}(h)(f)=\mathrm{ke}(g)(f)(a)
by definition and
\mathrm{C}_{\tau}(\mathrm{ke}(h)(f))
by the induction hypothesis.

(2)

Given
a:\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\mathrm{C}_{\rho}(a)
and
f:\mathbb{N}^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}\to\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
with
\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}\to\rho\to\rho}(f)
, we have

\mathrm{C}_{\rho}(\mathrm{rec}(a)(\lambda k.f(\lambda\alpha.k))(i))

for all
i:\mathbb{N}
.

Proof 3.3
.

By induction on
i
.

By applying (1) to (2), we get a proof of
\mathrm{C}(\mathrm{rec}^{\mathrm{b}})
.

Another important fact is that the generic sequence also satisfies the predicate
\mathrm{C}
.

Lemma 5
.

We have

\mathrm{C}_{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}(\Omega).

Proof 3.4
.

Suppose a continuous
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is given. The goal is to show that
\Omega(f)
is also continuous. Let
\alpha:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
be given. By the continuity of
f
, we have a modulus
m
of
f
at the point
\alpha
. Take
n:\equiv\max(m,f\alpha+1)
. Given
\beta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
with
\alpha=_{n}\beta
, we have

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\Omega(f)(\alpha)&=&\alpha(f\alpha)\\
&=&\beta(f\alpha)&&(\alpha=_{n}\beta\text{ and }f\alpha<f\alpha+1\leq n)\\
&=&\beta(f\beta)&&(f\alpha=f\beta\text{ by the continuity of $f$})\\
&=&\Omega(f)(\beta)\end{array}

and hence
\Omega(f)
is continuous.

Theorem 6
.

Every
\mathrm{T}
-definable function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is continuous.

Proof 3.5
.

By Lemmas
4
and
5
, we know
f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)
is continuous. Then
f
is also continuous, because
f
and
f^{\mathrm{b}}(\Omega)
are pointwise equal by Theorem
3
.

4.
Other notions of continuity

4.1.
T-definable moduli of continuity

It is also well-known that any
\mathrm{T}
-definable function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
has a
\mathrm{T}
-definable
modulus of continuity

[
20
, Theorem 2.7.8]
, that is, a function
M:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall\alpha^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}},\beta^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}\left(\alpha=_{M(\alpha)}\beta\to f(\alpha)=f(\beta)\right).

Here we present two ways to recover this fact.

The first approach was suggested by Ulrich Kohlenbach. During a workshop
1
1

1

Workshop “Proofs and Computation”, July 2-6 2018, Hausdorff Research Institute of Mathematics, Bonn, Germany. Website:
https://www.him.uni-bonn.de/application/types-sets-constructions/workshop-proofs-and-computation/
.
in Bonn, he pointed out that our Theorem
6
can be precisely formulated as

for each closed term
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
,
\mathrm{HA}^{\omega}
proves that
f
is continuous

whose proof remains the same. From the
\mathrm{HA}^{\omega}
proof of the continuity of
f
, we can extract a closed term
M:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
and a proof in
\mathrm{HA}^{\omega}
that
M
is a modulus of continuity of
f
, via the modified realizability
[
11
, §5]
.

We can also “manually” extract moduli of continuity by combining the construction with the
\mathrm{b}
-translation similarly to the construction of general-bar-recursion functionals in
[
13
]
. For this, we extend System
\mathrm{T}
with product type
\sigma\times\tau
(and a pairing function
\langle\,\text{-}\,\mathord{;}\,\text{-}\,\rangle
and projections), and then adapt Definition
2
to the following: The type translation
(\rho\mapsto\rho^{\mathbf{{b}}})
becomes

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{{b}}}&:\equiv&\left((\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}\right)\times\left((\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}\right)\\[2.0pt]
(\sigma\to\tau)^{\mathbf{{b}}}&:\equiv&\sigma^{\mathbf{{b}}}\to\tau^{\mathbf{{b}}}.\end{array}

For simplicity, we omit the trivial translation of product types and related constants. Note that each term
w
of type
\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{{b}}}
denotes a pair of functionals. We write

•

\mathrm{V}_{w}:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
for the first component of
w
, and

•

\mathrm{M}_{w}:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
for the second,

and hence have
w=\langle\mathrm{V}_{w};\mathrm{M}_{w}\rangle
. The idea is that
\mathrm{V}_{w}
is the
value
(
i.e.
the
\mathrm{b}
-translation) of some term while
\mathrm{M}_{w}
is a
modulus of continuity
of
\mathrm{V}_{w}
. Because function types are translated in the same way as in Definition
2
, we need to change the term translation
(t\mapsto t^{\mathbf{{b}}})
only for the constants of
\mathbb{N}
:

\begin{array}[]{rll}0^{\mathbf{{b}}}&:\equiv&\langle\lambda\alpha.0;\lambda\alpha.0\rangle\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{succ}^{\mathbf{{b}}}&:\equiv&\lambda x.\langle\lambda\alpha.\mathrm{succ}(\mathrm{V}_{x}(\alpha));\mathrm{M}_{x}\rangle\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{rec}^{\mathbf{{b}}}&:\equiv&\lambda af.\mathbf{ke}(\mathrm{rec}(a)(\lambda k.f\langle\lambda\alpha.k;\lambda\alpha.0\rangle))\end{array}

where the Kleisli extension
\mathbf{ke}_{\rho}:(\mathbb{N}\to\rho^{\mathbf{{b}}})\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{{b}}}\to\rho^{\mathbf{{b}}}
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathbf{ke}_{\mathbb{N}}(g)(f)&:\equiv&\langle\lambda\alpha.\mathrm{V}_{g(\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha))}(\alpha);\lambda\alpha.\max(\mathrm{M}_{g(\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha))}(\alpha),\mathrm{M}_{f}(\alpha))\rangle\\[2.0pt]
\mathbf{ke}_{\sigma\to\tau}(g)(f)&:\equiv&\lambda x.\mathbf{ke}_{\tau}(\lambda k.g(k)(x))(f).\end{array}

Note that the maximum function
\max:\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is primitive recursive and can be defined using
\mathrm{rec}
in
\mathrm{T}
. The “value” part of the above translation is exactly the
\mathrm{b}
-translation. In the “modulus” part of the Kleisli extension
\mathbf{ke}_{\mathbb{N}}(g)(f)
, there are two potential moduli of continuity at
\alpha
: one is given by
g(\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha))
and the other by
f
. We of course take the greater one to be the modulus of continuity.

The generic sequence
\mathbf{\Omega}:\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{{b}}}\to\mathbb{N}^{\mathbf{{b}}}
is defined by

\mathbf{\Omega}(f)\ :\equiv\ \langle\lambda\alpha.\alpha(\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha));\lambda\alpha.\max(\mathrm{M}_{f}(\alpha),\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha)+1)\rangle.

For the “modulus” part, we again take the greater of the two potential moduli of continuity: one is given by the input
f
, and the other is
\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha)+1
because
\alpha
is applied to
\mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha)
.

We can show the following variant of Theorem
3

any closed
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
is pointwise equal to
\mathrm{V}_{f^{\mathbf{{b}}}(\mathbf{\Omega})}

using a parametrized logical relation
\mathbf{R}_{\rho}^{\alpha}\subseteq\rho^{\mathbf{{b}}}\times\rho
that is almost the same as Definition
2
except that the base case is defined by

f\ \mathbf{R}_{\mathbb{N}}^{\alpha}\ n\ :\equiv\ \mathrm{V}_{f}(\alpha)=n.

We also modify Definition
3
to get a predicate
\mathbf{C}_{\rho}\subseteq\rho^{\mathbf{{b}}}
with the following base case

\mathbf{C}_{\mathbb{N}}(f)\ :\equiv\ \mathrm{M}_{f}\text{ is a modulus of continuity of }\mathrm{V}_{f}.

Similarly to the proof of Theorem
6
, by showing (i)
\mathbf{C}_{\rho}(t^{\mathbf{{b}}})
for all
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
and (ii)
\mathbf{C}(\mathbf{\Omega})
, we can conclude that for any
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
, the term
\mathrm{M}_{f^{\mathbf{{b}}}(\mathbf{\Omega})}
is a modulus of continuity of
f
. Note that
\mathrm{M}_{f^{\mathbf{{b}}}(\mathbf{\Omega})}
is exactly the term which is extracted from the proof of Theorem
6
via modified realizability.

Theorem 7
.

If
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is
\mathrm{T}
-definable, then it has a
\mathrm{T}
-definable modulus of continuity.

4.2.
Uniform continuity

Because each
\mathrm{T}
-definable function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is continuous, so is its restriction
f|_{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}
. Using our syntactic method, we can show that these restrictions are actually uniformly continuous.

Recall that a function
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}
is
uniformly continuous
if there exists
m:\mathbb{N}
, called a
modulus of uniform continuity
of
f
, such that any binary sequences
\alpha,\beta:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2}
that agree at the first
m
positions give the same result. The uniform continuity of
f
can be formulated in
\mathrm{HA}^{\omega}
as

\exists m^{\mathbb{N}}\,\forall\alpha^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2}},\beta^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2}}\left(\alpha=_{m}\beta\to f(\alpha)=f(\beta)\right).

The following lemma of uniform continuity will play an important role in our proof.

Lemma 8
.

For any
m:\mathbb{N}
and
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}
, if
m
is a modulus of uniform continuity of
f
, then
f
has a maximum image.

Proof 4.1
.

By induction on
m
.

We sketch out our proof of uniform continuity only with the key steps. Firstly we define a predicate
\mathrm{UC}_{\rho}\subseteq\rho^{\mathrm{b}}
by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathrm{UC}_{\mathbb{N}}(f)&:\equiv&\text{$f|_{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}$ is uniformly continuous}\\[2.0pt]
\mathrm{UC}_{\sigma\to\tau}(g)&:\equiv&\forall x^{\sigma^{\mathrm{b}}}\left(\mathrm{UC}_{\sigma}(x)\to\mathrm{UC}_{\tau}(g(x))\right).\end{array}

Then we show

•

\mathrm{UC}_{\rho}(t^{\mathrm{b}})
for all
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
, assuming
\mathrm{UC}(x^{\mathrm{b}})
for all
x\in\mathrm{FV}(t)
; and

•

\mathrm{UC}_{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}(\Omega)
.

Both are proved using Lemma
8
. They together lead to the desired result.

Theorem 9
.

If
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
is
\mathrm{T}
-definable, then its restriction
f|_{\mathbbm{2}^{\mathbb{N}}}:{(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbbm{2})\to\mathbb{N}}
is uniformly continuous.

Using the Dialectica interpretation and his pointwise version of strong majorization, Kohlenbach
[
10
]
obtains a more general result: for each term
f:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
, he constructs a term
\Phi:(\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N})\to\mathbb{N}
in
\mathrm{T}
such that
\Phi(\gamma)
is a modulus of uniform continuity of
f
on
\{\alpha^{\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}}\,|\,\forall i^{\mathbb{N}}\,\alpha_{i}\leq\gamma_{i}\}
. Our syntactic method can also provide a construction of such moduli of uniform continuity (see our Agda implementation
[
23
]
).

5.
Generalization

The key step of our syntactic approach to continuity is the
\mathrm{b}
-translation: natural numbers are translated to functionals from the Baire type
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
so that continuity becomes the base case of the predicate to work with. The translation can be generalized by replacing the Baire type by arbitrary finite type
X
. In specific, the type translation
\rho\mapsto\rho^{X}
is defined by

\begin{array}[]{rll}\mathbb{N}^{X}&:\equiv&X\to\mathbb{N}\\[2.0pt]
(\sigma\to\tau)^{X}&:\equiv&\sigma^{X}\to\tau^{X}\end{array}

while the term translation remains the same as in Definition
2
(with the superscribed
\mathrm{b}
replaced by
X
). The parametrized logical relation
\mathrm{R}_{\rho}^{\alpha}\subseteq\rho^{X}\times\rho
for the “
X
-translation” is defined exactly the same as in Definition
2
except that the parameter
\alpha
is of type
X
instead of
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, and similarly Lemma
1
for it can be shown with the same proof. The generic sequence
\Omega
which in this case has type
X^{X}
(
i.e.
the
X
-translation of
X
) can be defined by unfolding
\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{\alpha}\ \alpha
when
X
is given concretely. For instance, when
X\equiv\mathbb{N}
we define
\Omega:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
by
\Omega(n):\equiv n
, as
\Omega\ \mathrm{R}^{n}\ n
is unfolded to
\Omega(n)=n
.

Therefore, our method can be generalized for proving various properties of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions of
arbitrary
finite type: Suppose the goal is to prove a certain property of
\mathrm{T}
-definable functions
X\to\mathbb{N}
. As discussed above, we already have the “
X
-translation” of
\mathrm{T}
. Then we define a predicate
P_{\rho}\subseteq\rho^{X}
whose base case
P_{\mathbb{N}}(f)
expresses the targeting property of
f:X\to\mathbb{N}
. Once we show
P_{\rho}(t^{X})
for all
t:\rho
in
\mathrm{T}
, we achieve the goal. For instance, when taking
X\equiv\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
(or
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
), the above method is essentially Oliva and Steila’s technique of proving the closure property of System
\mathrm{T}
terms under the rule of Spector’s bar recursion of type 0 (respectively 1)
[
13
]
. When taking
X\equiv\mathbb{N}
, we should be able to use the above method to prove Schwichtenberg’s theorem that every
\mathrm{T}
-definable function
\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
is eventually bounded by the slow growing hierarchy
[
17
, Theorem 4.8]
(but this is left as a future task). We have not found applications when
X
is higher than 1.

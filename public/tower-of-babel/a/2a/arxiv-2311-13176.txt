Sergiy Maksymenko

Abstract
. Let M M be a smooth manifold and ℱ \mathcal{F} a Morse-Bott foliation with a compact critical manifold Σ ⊂ M \Sigma\subset M . Denote by 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}) the group of diffeomorphisms of M M leaving invariant each leaf of ℱ \mathcal{F} . Under certain assumptions on ℱ \mathcal{F} it is shown that the computation of the homotopy type of 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}) reduces to three rather independent groups: the group of diffeomorphisms of Σ \Sigma , the group of vector bundle automorphisms of some regular neighborhood of Σ \Sigma , and the subgroup of 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}) consisting of diffeomorphisms fixed near Σ \Sigma . Examples of computations of homotopy types of groups 𝒟 ⁡ ( ℱ ) \mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F}) for such foliations are also presented.

Introduction

The paper continues a series of works
[
30

,
32
,
33
,
42
,

devoted to computations of the homotopy types of groups of leaf-preserving diffeomorphisms for certain classes of foliations with singularities including Morse-Bott ones.

The general problem of determining homotopy types of different kinds of spaces of maps and groups of automorphisms (especially for smooth maps between manifolds) is extremely hard, and explicit computations are made in not so many cases.
On the other hand, the importance of the information about homotopy types even to real world problems can be observed from the facts that usually real objects and processes are ‘‘invariant with respect to small deformations’’, and therefore their integral numeric characteristics (like degree of a map) should be ‘‘stable under such deformations’’ and thus represented by some homotopy invariants.
Let us just recall that the homotopy types of diffeomorphism or homeomorphism groups are known for all manifolds of dimension
\leqslant 2
, for many
3
-manifolds, and for certain specific manifolds of dimensions
\geqslant 4
, see e.g.
[
6
,
9
,
18
,
56
,
53
,
25
]
and references therein.
For groups of diffeomorphisms preserving some ‘‘geometric’’ structures there is essentially less information, see
[
13
,
12
,
39
,
40
]
especially for computing homotopy types of diffeomorphism groups preserving several classes of foliations on
3
-manifolds and
1
-dimensional foliations with singularities (flow lines).

In
[
32
]
it was shown that given a manifold
M
, a submanifold
\Sigma\subset M
, and its regular neighborhood
{p}:E\to\Sigma
, one can deform by isotopy each diffeomorphism
h
of
M
preserving
\Sigma
to a diffeomorphism which coincides near
\Sigma
with some (uniquely determined) vector bundle automorphism
\hat{h}
of
E
given by
\hat{h}(v)=\lim\limits_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t}h(tv)
.
Such a statement can be regarded mutually as a parametrized, compactly supported, and even foliated extension of a theorem on isotopies of regular neighborhoods.
It is convenient to mention this procedure as a ‘‘linearization’’ of

.
Note that the correspondence
{\mathsf{t}}:h\mapsto\hat{h}
can be regarded as a homomorphism from the group
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{inv}}(M,\Sigma)
of diffeomorphisms of

\Sigma

of all vector bundle automorphisms of

That linearization allowed to compute in

,
41

the homotopy types of the group

of leaf preserving diffeomorphisms for certain Morse-Bott foliations
\mathcal{F}
with only center singularities on the solid torus and all lens spaces as well as on their non-orientable counterparts: solid Klein bottle and twisted

bundle over
S^{1}
.
Such foliations and flows preserving them are studied in
[

,
51
,

,

It is also proved in
[

]
that

The main result of the present paper, see Theorems

A. Hatcher

2.3.
Partitions with scalability properties

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a partition of a manifold

The elements of the partition
\mathcal{F}
of

will be called

.
A subset

\mathcal{F}
-saturated
, whenever

is a union of leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.
In this case by the
restriction of
\mathcal{F}
onto

we will mean a partition
\mathcal{F}|_{A}
of
A
into the leaves of
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Suppose
\Sigma
is an
\mathcal{F}
-saturated submanifold of
M
and
{p}\colon E\to\Sigma
is a regular neighborhood of
\Sigma
.
Say that an open neighborhood
U
of
\Sigma
is

•

\mathcal{F}
-isolating
(for
\Sigma
) if
U\cap(\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega)\subset\Sigma
for each
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
;

•

\mathcal{F}
-scalable
(with respect to
{p}
) if
U\subset E
and the following condition holds:

-

let
x,{y}\in U
be any points belonging to the same leaf of
\mathcal{F}
and
\tau>0
be any number such that
\tau x,\tau{y}\in U
;
then
\tau x,\tau{y}
also belong to the same (but possibly another) leaf of
\mathcal{F}
;

•

star-convex
if
U\subset E
and
tU\subset U
for all

.

The following lemma gives an important example of such neighborhoods.

Lemma 2.3.2

(
[
32
]
)
.

Let
{p}\colon E\to\Sigma
be a vector bundle over a smooth manifold
\Sigma
(not necessarily compact) and

be a continuous function such that

(1)

is homogeneous of some (not necessarily integer) degree

and

(2)

for some

and

the path components of

and

are closed in

.

Let also
\mathcal{F}_{g}
be the partition of

whose elements are path components of
\Sigma
and path components of the sets

for all

.

\overline{\omega}\setminus\omega\subset\Sigma

-isolating and

.

2.4.
Distributions of automorphism groups

Let
{p}\colon E\to\Sigma
be a vector bundle over a compact manifold
\Sigma
.
Denote by
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
the
\mathbb{R}
-algebra of all
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
vector bundle morphisms
h\colon E\to E
fixed on
\Sigma
, i.e. satisfying
{p}\circ h={p}
.
Then
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)\subset\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
is the multiplicative subgroup consisting of all vector bundle automorphisms.
Notice that the vector bundle projection
{p}\colon E\to\Sigma
is the zero element of the algebra
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
, while
\mathrm{id}_{E}
is the unit of
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
.

Evidently, each
h\in\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
is uniquely determined by its restriction to any open neighborhood of
\Sigma
.
In particular, if we fix some smooth compact tubular neighborhood
U\subset E
of
\Sigma
, then one can regard
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
as a subset of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(U,E\right)
, and therefore we can endow
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
with the induced strong
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
Whitney topology.
It is easy to see that this topology does not depend on a particular choice of
U
and turns
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
into a Fréchet space.
Then
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
is an open subset of
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
, and in particular, it is locally contractible.

For each
{y}\in\Sigma
let
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
be the group of linear automorphisms of the fiber
E_{{y}}:={p}^{-1}({y})
.
If
\dim E_{{y}}=n
, then every local trivialization chart
\phi\colon{p}^{-1}(U)\to U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
U
is a neighborhood of
{y}
in
\Sigma
, gives a bijection
\phi_{{y}}\colon\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Hence, we can endow
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
with a Lie group structure such that
\phi_{{y}}
is an isomorphism of Lie groups, and it is easy to see that this structure does not depend on a choice of a local chart at
{y}
.

Definition 2.4.1
.

By a
distribution of automorphism groups over a subset
Y\subset\Sigma
or simply a
distribution over
Y
we will mean an arbitrary collection of subgroups
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{{y}}\subset\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})\}_{{y}\in Y}

of the corresponding groups of automorphisms of fibers over the points of
Y
.
The set
Y
will be called the
support
of
\mathcal{G}
.
Also,
\mathcal{G}
will be called a
Lie groups distribution
if each
G_{{y}}
is closed in
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
, and therefore is a Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
, e.g.
[
35
, Theorem 20.12]
.

Thus, for each
{y}\in Y
one chooses some subgroup
G_{{y}}
of
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
, and does not require any kind of continuity of such family.
Notice that then the distribution
\overline{\mathcal{G}}=\{\overline{G_{{y}}}\}_{{y}\in Y}
consisting of closures of groups
G_{{y}}
is always a Lie groups distribution.
Also, one can always extend
\mathcal{G}
to have the support
\Sigma
by setting
G_{{y}}:=\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
for
{y}\in\Sigma\setminus Y
.

Lemma 2.4.2
.

Let
f\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function, and for each
{y}\in\Sigma
let

G_{{y}}=\{A\in\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})\mid f(A(v))=f(v),\ v\in E_{{y}}\}

be the group of linear automorphisms preserving the restriction of
f
on
E_{{y}}
.
Then each
G_{{y}}
is closed, so
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{{y}}\}_{{y}\in\Sigma}
is a
Lie groups distribution over all of
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Evidently, we have a continuous action
\mu\colon\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})\times E_{{y}}\to E_{{y}}
,
\mu(A,v)=Av
.
Now, for each
v\in E_{{y}}
define the following continuous function

f_{v}\colon\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})\to\mathbb{R},\qquad f_{v}(A)=f(A(v))-f(v).

Then
G_{{y}}=\mathop{\cap}\limits_{v\in E_{{y}}}f_{v}^{-1}(0)
, and so its is closed as an intersection of closed sets.
∎

Let
\mathcal{G}=\{G_{{y}}\mid{y}\in Y\}
be a distribution of groups over a subset
Y\subset\Sigma
.
Say that a bundle automorphism
{A}\in\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)

belongs to
\mathcal{G}
if
{A}|_{E_{{y}}}\in G_{{y}}
for every
{y}\in Y
.
The set of all
{A}\in\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
belonging to
\mathcal{G}
will be denoted by
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.
Evidently,
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
and for every
{y}\in Y
we have the following
continuous
homomorphism

{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}\colon\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\to G_{{y}},\qquad{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}({A})={A}|_{E_{{y}}}.

Let us mention that in general
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
might have a very complicated local structure.
We will now consider an example of distributions with ‘‘good’’ local structure.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie groups distribution over a subset
Y\subset\Sigma
and
Y_{0}=\{{y}\in Y\mid\dim G_{{y}}=0\}
be the subset of
Y
consisting points
{y}
for which the group
G_{{y}}
is zero-dimensional.
Then the following set

(2.7)

\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G}):=\mathop{\cap}\limits_{{y}\in Y_{0}}{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}})\equiv\mathop{\cap}\limits_{{y}\in Y_{0}}\ker({\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}),

is a closed normal subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.
We will call
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
the
0
-kernel
of
\mathcal{G}
.

Notice that if
\dim G_{{y}}=0
, then the one-point set
\{\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}\}
is open in
G_{{y}}
, and therefore
{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.
However, if
Y_{0}
is infinite, then in general
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
(being thus an intersection of infinitely many open sets) might be not open in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.

Lemma 2.4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie groups distribution over a subset
Y\subset\Sigma
.

(1)

The following conditions are equivalent:

(1a)

\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is open (and thus open closed) in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
;

(1b)

\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
contains some (not necessarily open) neighborhood of
\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
;

(1c)

there exists an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
such that for every
{y}\in Y
with
\dim G_{{y}}=0
and every
{A}\in{\mathcal{L}}
we have that
{A}|_{E_{{y}}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}
.

In the case
(1)(1c)
one can choose
{\mathcal{L}}\subset\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
;

(2)

Each of the following conditions implies that
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
:

(2a)

Y_{0}
is finite, i.e.
\dim G_{{y}}=0
only for finitely many
{y}\in Y
;

(2b)

the quotient group
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})/\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is finite;

(2c)

there exists a
connected
neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
;

(2d)

there exists a
contractible
neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.

Moreover, in the cases
(2)(2c)
and
(2)(2d)
, one can choose
{\mathcal{L}}\subset\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.

Proof.

Statement
(1)
directly follows form the definitions.

(2)(2a)

If
Y_{0}
is finite, then by (
2.7
),
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is an intersection of finitely many open sets
{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}^{-1}(\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}})
.

(2)(2b)

Suppose
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})/\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is finite, and let
K_{1}=\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G}),K_{2},\ldots,K_{n}
be corresponding adjacent classes of
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.
Since
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is closed, we have that each
K_{i}
is also closed, and therefore
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})=\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\setminus\mathop{\cup}\limits_{i=2}^{n}K_{i}
is open.

(2)(2c)

If
{\mathcal{L}}
is connected, then for each
{y}\in Y
with
\dim G_{{y}}=0
the image
{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}({\mathcal{L}})
is contained in the path component
\{\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}\}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}
in
G_{{y}}
.
In other words,
{A}|_{E_{{y}}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}
for all
{A}\in{\mathcal{L}}
.
Thus,
{\mathcal{L}}\subset\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.

Finally,
(2)(2d)
implies
(2)(2c)
and therefore openness of
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})

∎

(1)(1c)

Lemma 5.2

Lemma 5.4
.

The quotient group
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}})/\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{F}})
is finite.

Proof.

Let
Y_{0}=\{{y}\in\Sigma\mid\dim G_{{y}}=0\}
.
Recall that for every
{y}\in\Sigma
we have the natural homomorphism
{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}:\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\to G_{{y}}
,
{\mathsf{t}}_{{y}}({A})={A}|_{E_{{y}}}
, and

\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G}):=\{{A}\in\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\mid{A}|_{E_{{y}}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}\ \text{for all}\ {y}\in Y_{0}\}.

By Lemma
5.3
the dimension of the group

depends on the dimension of the fiber over
{y}
.
Hence, if
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a connected component of
\Sigma
, then all fibers over points

have the same dimension, and therefore
\dim G_{{y}}
is also the same for all
{y}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
.
In particular,
Y_{0}
is a union of connected components, say
\Sigma_{1},\ldots,\Sigma_{l}
of
\Sigma
.

Moreover, let

be the connected component of

containing
\Sigma_{i}
, so
{p}|_{E_{i}}\colon E_{i}\to\Sigma_{i}

\Sigma_{i}
.

, so
\dim G_{{y}}=0
.

.

, and we will denote that common value of all

Let

7.
Groups of vector bundle automorphisms

7.1.
Exponential map
\exp\colon\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)\to\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)

Let
{p}\colon E\to\Sigma
be a vector bundle over a manifold
\Sigma
.
As mentioned above,
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
is locally contractible.
For the proof of Theorem
3.1.1
we need to define a specific contraction ‘‘along fiberwise one-parameter subgroups’’ of some neighborhood
{\mathcal{M}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
, see Corollary

below.

First we will define a fiberwise analogue of the exponential map
\exp\colon\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathbb{R})\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})

Such a map is usually studied for principal
G
-bundles, c.f.
[
34
]
.
Though this construction is rather natural, the author was not able to find it in the available literature for this specific case.
For the convenience of the reader we will provide its explicit description.

Let
\phi\colon{p}^{-1}(U)\to U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a local trivialization of
{p}
over some open subset
U\subset\Sigma
.
Then for each
{B}\in\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
we have that
{p}\circ{B}={p}
, so in particular,
{B}({p}^{-1}(U))={p}^{-1}(U)
.
Therefore, we get the following commutative diagram from the left:

\textstyle{{p}^{-1}(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{{B}}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{{p}^{-1}(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\phi}
\textstyle{U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

\textstyle{U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}}

\textstyle{{p}^{-1}(U)\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

Lemma 7.1.1
.

Let
\psi\colon U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to{p}^{-1}(U)
be another trivialization of
{p}
over
U
.
Then the following maps coincide:

(7.1)

\phi^{-1}\circ\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\phi}}\circ\phi=\psi^{-1}\circ\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\psi}}\circ\psi\colon{p}^{-1}(U)\to{p}^{-1}(U).

Proof.

Let
\gamma=\psi\circ\phi^{-1}\colon U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to U\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
({y},u)=\bigl({y},{C}({y})u\bigr)
, be the transition map between those charts, where
{C}\colon U\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is some
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map.
Then (
7.1
) is equivalent to the identity
\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\psi}}=\gamma\circ\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\phi}}\circ\gamma^{-1}
.

As
\psi^{-1}\circ{B}\circ\psi=\gamma\circ(\phi^{-1}\circ{B}\circ\phi)\circ\gamma^{-1}
, we have that
{B}_{\psi}({y})={C}({y}){B}_{\phi}({y}){C}^{-1}({y})
, whence

e^{{B}_{\psi}({y})}=e^{{C}({y}){B}_{\phi}({y}){C}^{-1}({y})}={C}({y})e^{{B}_{\phi}({y})}{C}^{-1}({y}),

which implies that
\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\psi}}=\gamma\circ\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\phi}}\circ\gamma^{-1}
.
∎

Hence, for each

, one can define the map

of

\mathbf{e}^{{B}}|_{{p}^{-1}(U)}:=\phi\circ\mathbf{e}^{{B}_{\phi}}\circ\phi^{-1}.

Due to Lemma

.

.

Suppose that

Then the following statements hold.

Also note that the image

7.2.
Local contractibility of
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})

Say that a topological space
X
is
locally contractible at a point
x\in X
, if
x
has a local base of the topology of
X
consisting of (open) contractible neighborhoods.
Also,
X
is
locally contractible
if it is locally contractible at each
x\in X
.

Lemma 7.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a Lie groups distribution over
Y\subset\Sigma
.
Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is open;

(2)

\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
is locally contractible at
\mathrm{id}_{E}
;

(3)

\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
is locally contractible.

Proof.

The implication
(2)
\Rightarrow
(1)
follows from the implication

(2)(2d)
\Rightarrow
(1)(1a)
of Lemma
2.4.3
.

The implication
(3)
\Rightarrow
(2)
is evident, while the inverse
(2)
\Rightarrow
(3)
follows from the fact that
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
is a topological group, and therefore it is homogeneous, so it has ‘‘the same topology at each point’’.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2)
.
We have that each
G_{{y}}
is a Lie subgroup of
\mathrm{Aut}(E_{{y}})
and
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
.
It is necessary to prove that every open neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
contains some contractible neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}
.

Since
\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
is open in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
one can assume that

{\mathcal{L}}=\mathcal{U}\cap\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\subset\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})

for some open subset
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
.

By Lemma
7.1.2

, the map
\exp:\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)\to\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
induces a homeomorphism of some neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}}
of
{p}
in
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
onto an open neighborhood
{\mathcal{M}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma)
.
Then one can choose an open star-convex neighborhood
{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}\subset{\mathcal{N}}
of
{p}
in
\mathrm{End}(E,\Sigma)
such that
\exp({\mathcal{N}^{\prime}})\subset{\mathcal{M}}\cap\mathcal{U}
.
We claim that the following neighborhood
{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}
of
\mathrm{id}_{E}
in
\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
:

{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}:=\exp({\mathcal{N}^{\prime}})\cap\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\subset{\mathcal{M}}\cap\mathcal{U}\cap\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})\subset{\mathcal{M}}\cap\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})

is invariant under the contraction
{\mathbf{G}}
from Lemma
7.1.2
c)
, i.e.
{\mathbf{G}}\bigl({\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\times[0;1]\bigr)\subset{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}
.
In other words, for every
{B}\in\exp^{-1}({\mathcal{L}^{\prime}})
, we have that
\exp(t{B})\in\mathrm{GL}(E,\Sigma;\mathcal{G})
for all
t\in[0;1]
.

Indeed, let
{y}\in\Sigma
, so
\exp({B})|_{E_{{y}}}\in G_{{y}}
.
If
\dim G_{{y}}\geqslant 1
, then
\exp(t{B})|_{E_{{y}}}\in G_{{y}}
for all

due to Lemma
7.1.2

.

On the other hand, if

, since
\exp({B})\in{\mathcal{L}^{\prime}}\subset\ker_{0}(\mathcal{G})
.
Hence,
\exp(t{B})|_{E_{{y}}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{{y}}}\in G_{{y}}
for all

as well.
∎

\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map
\Phi\colon\Sigma\to\mathcal{L}(g)
such that
{A}(v,{y})=\bigl(\Phi({y})v,{y}\bigr)
.

Indeed, by definition
{A}
is a
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
map
{A}\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma
having the following properties.

a)

{A}
preserves the fibers
E_{{y}}=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{{y}\}
for all
{y}\in\Sigma
, and its restriction to
E_{{y}}
is a linear isomorphism.

b)

{A}
also preserves the leaves of
\hat{\mathcal{F}}
, i.e. sets of the form
\omega\times\Sigma^{\prime}
, where
\omega
is a path component of some level set of
g
, and
\Sigma^{\prime}
is a connected component of
\Sigma
.
Hence,
{A}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\{{y}\}}=\Phi({y})
preserves
\omega\times\{{y}\}
, and thus belongs to
\mathcal{L}(g)
.

The first property implies that
{A}(v,{y})=(\Phi({y})v,{y})
, where
\Phi({y})\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is some non-degenerate matrix depending on
{y}
, so we get a map
\Phi\colon\Sigma\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
.
Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, so
e_{i}=(0,\ldots,1,\ldots,0)
, where
1
stands at position
i
and all other coordinates are zeros.
Then for each
i=1,\ldots,n
the map

{A}_{i}:={A}|_{e_{i}\times\Sigma}\colon\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
(since
{A}
is so), and the coordinate functions of
{A}_{i}({y})
constitute the
i
-th column of the matrix
\Phi({y})
.
Hence,
\Phi=({A}_{1},\ldots,{A}_{n})\colon\Sigma\to\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
as well.
Furthermore, by the second condition, the image of
\Phi
is contained in the Lie subgroup
\mathcal{L}(g)
of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{R})
, whence, the induced map
\Phi\colon\Sigma\to\mathcal{L}(g)
is also
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
.

We leave to the reader the verification that for every
\Phi\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(\Sigma,\mathcal{L}(g)\right)
the map
\gamma(\Phi)\in\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})
, and that (due to compactness of
\Sigma
) the map
\gamma
is a homeomorphism with respect to the corresponding Whitney topologies.

2) Note that we have a natural restriction of
\Sigma
homomorphism
{\mathsf{r}}\colon\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})\to\mathcal{D}(\Sigma)
,
{\mathsf{r}}({A})={A}|_{\Sigma}
, with kernel
\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}},\Sigma)
.
Since
{p}
is a trivial vector bundle,
{\mathsf{r}}
admits the following global section
s:\mathcal{D}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})
,
s(h)(v,{y})=(v,h({y}))
, being also a homomorphism.
Now by Lemma
2.1.1
(3)
, this gives the homeomorphism
\sigma
.
∎

Thus, in this case the computation of the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},\Sigma)
reduces to two independent problems of computing the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(\Sigma)
and of the space of smooth maps
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(\Sigma,\mathcal{L}(g)\right)
.
Moreover, if
g
is a non-degenerate definite quadratic form, so
\mathcal{F}
is Morse-Bott, then we will get a more detailed statement, see Corollary
9.2.4
.

Before formulating it let us prove a simple auxiliary lemma concerning maps from and to the circle.
Let
1\in S^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
be the unit number in the circle.

Lemma 9.2.3
.

1)
Let
G
be a compact Lie group with the identity path component
G_{0}
and unit
e
, and
\Omega^{\infty}(G_{0})=\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left((S^{1},1),(G_{0},e)\right)
be the space of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
maps
\phi:S^{1}\to G
such that
\phi(1)=e
, i.e. the space of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
loops in
G_{0}
at
e
.
Then we have a homeomorphism

\theta\colon\Omega^{\infty}(G_{0})\times G\to\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(S^{1},G\right),\qquad\theta(\phi,g)(a)=\phi(x)g.

Let
\Omega(G_{0})=\mathcal{C}^{0}\!\left((S^{1},1),(G_{0},e)\right)
be the space of
continuous
loop in
G_{0}
at
e
.
Then the inclusion
\Omega^{\infty}(G_{0})\subset\Omega(G_{0})
is a weak homotopy equivalence.
In particular, we have the following homotopy equivalence:
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(S^{1},G\right)\simeq\Omega(G_{0})\times G
.

2)
Also, let
A
be a connected smooth manifold,
a\in A
any point,
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(A,S^{1}\right)
be the subspace of
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(A,S^{1}\right)
consisting of null-homotopic maps.
Then the ‘‘evaluation at
a
’’ homomorphism

\delta\colon\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(A,S^{1}\right)\to S^{1},\qquad\delta(\phi)=\phi(a),

is a homotopy equivalence.

Proof.

1) The fact that
\theta
is a homeomorphism is straightforward.
It is also well-known that inclusion
\Omega^{\infty}(G_{0})\subset\Omega(G_{0})
is a weak homotopy equivalence, see
[
31
]
for discussions.

2) It suffices to show that
\delta
admits a global section and its kernel is contractible.
Then by Lemma
2.1.1
(3)
,
\delta
will be a homotopy equivalence.

Notice that
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(A,S^{1}\right)
is a group with respect to pointwise multiplication and
\delta
is a surjective homomorphism.
Evidently, it admits a global section
S^{1}\to\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(A,S^{1}\right)
associating to each
\tau\in S^{1}
the constant map
A\to\{\tau\}\subset S^{1}
into the point
\tau
.

Further,
\ker(\delta)=\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left((A,a),(S^{1},1)\right)
, i.e. it is the subset of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\left(A,S^{1}\right)
consisting of maps
\phi\colon A\to S^{1}
such that
\phi(a)=1
.
Let also
{p}\colon\mathbb{R}\to S^{1}
,
{p}(t)=e^{2\pi it}
, be the universal covering map.
Then for each
\phi\in\ker(\delta)
there exists a unique lifting
\hat{\phi}\colon A\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\hat{\phi}(a)=0
.
It is easy to see that the correspondence
\phi\mapsto\hat{\phi}
is a homeomorphism
\ker(\delta)\cong\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left((A,a),(\mathbb{R},0)\right)
, and the latter space is contractible.
∎

Corollary 9.2.4
.

Let
{p}\colon E=\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma\to\Sigma
be a trivial vector bundle, and
f\colon\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Sigma\to\mathbb{R}
be the Morse-Bott function given by
f(v,{y})=(v,v)=\|v\|^{2}
.
Then the homotopy types of
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})
and
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},\Sigma)
for different values of
n
are presented in the following table:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr&\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},\Sigma)\simeq\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}},\Sigma)&\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\mathrm{GL}(\hat{\mathcal{F}})\\
\hline\cr n=1&\text{point}&\mathcal{D}(\Sigma)\\
\hline\cr n\geqslant 2&\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(\Sigma,\mathrm{O}(n)\right)&\mathcal{C}^{\infty}\!\left(\Sigma,\mathrm{O}(n)\right)\times\mathcal{D}(\Sigma)\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

If
\Sigma=S^{1}
, then by Lemma
9.2.3
, for
n\geqslant 2
the above homotopy equivalences imply the following
homotopy equivalences
:

(9.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fix}}(\mathcal{F},\Sigma)\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}(n))\times\mathrm{O}(n),

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\Omega^{\infty}(\mathrm{SO}(n))\times\mathrm{O}(n)\times\mathrm{O}(2).

Proof.

Consider the following function
g:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0;+\infty)
,
g(v)=(v,v)=\|v\|^{2}
.
Then
f(v,{y})=g(v)=\|v\|^{2}
, whence we have homotopy equivalences (
9.2
).
Recall also that
\mathcal{L}(g)
is the group of linear automorphisms of
\mathbb{R}
fixing every connected component of each level set of
g
.
Then for
n=1
each level set
g^{-1}(t)
,
t>0
, consists of two points
\pm\sqrt{t}
, whence
\mathcal{L}(g)=\{\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{R}}\}
, which implies the first row of the table.
On the other hand, if
n\geqslant 2
, then each level set of
g
is the
(n-1)
-sphere
S^{n-1}
, whence
\mathcal{L}(g)=\mathrm{O}(n)
is the group of linear automorphisms preserving
g
.
This gives the second row of the table.
∎

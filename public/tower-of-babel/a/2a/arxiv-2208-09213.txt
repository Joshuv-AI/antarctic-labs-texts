2
Relaxation Superoperator

2.1
Derivation

A spin Hamiltonian for interaction
\Lambda
may be written as a tensor product between a time-dependent spatial tensor and time-independent spin tensor. A convenient set of tensors to use are the
irreducible spherical tensor operators
, since these are eigenoperators of the Zeeman Hamiltonian commutation superoperator, leading to a simple expression in the corresponding interaction representation. For a rank-
l
interaction, we have,
1

H^{\Lambda}_{l}(t)=c^{\Lambda}\sum_{m=-l}^{+l}A^{\Lambda*}_{lm}(t)X^{\Lambda}_{lm},

(1)

where
c^{\Lambda}
is a real constant specific to interaction
\Lambda
. Being Hermitian, we may also write,

\begin{split}H^{\Lambda}_{l}(t)&=H^{\Lambda\dagger}_{l}\\
&=c^{\Lambda}\sum_{m=-l}^{+l}A^{\Lambda}_{lm}(t)X^{\Lambda\dagger}_{lm}.\end{split}

(2)

Useful relations for components
A_{lm}
and
X_{lm}
are,

A^{*}_{lm}=(-1)^{m}A_{l-m},

(3)

and,

X^{\dagger}_{lm}=(-1)^{m}X_{l-m}.

(4)

Then, using the eigenoperator relation,

\big[I_{z},X_{lm}\big]=mX_{lm},

(5)

the tensor components may be written in the interaction representation of the Zeeman interaction as,

\begin{split}\tilde{X}_{lm}(t)&=e^{i\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Z}}t}X_{lm}\\
&=X_{lm}e^{im\omega_{0}t},\end{split}

(6)

where
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Z}}
is the Zeeman Hamiltonian commutation superoperator,
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{Z}}=\big[H_{\mathrm{Z}},\cdots\big]=\omega_{0}\big[I_{\mathrm{z}},\cdots\big]
.
In a
Wangsness-Bloch-Redfield
(WBR) formalism, the relaxation superoperator takes the form,

\hat{\Gamma}=-\frac{1}{2}\int^{+\infty}_{-\infty}d\tau\overline{\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\mathrm{fluc}}(t),\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\mathrm{fluc}}(t+\tau)}.

(7)

From here, we simplify the expression. First, we have the liberty of setting
t=0
by assuming the noise in the system is stationary. This removes an exponential term in the interaction representation. Also, by neglecting the small dynamic frequency shifts, the correlation function has time-reversal symmetry and the integral may be taken from
-\infty
to
0
while introducing a factor of two. We now write,

\hat{\Gamma}=-\int^{0}_{-\infty}d\tau\overline{\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\mathrm{fluc}}(0)\hat{\tilde{H}}_{\mathrm{fluc}}(\tau)}.

(8)

\hat{\Gamma}
may be decomposed as a sum of auto- and cross-correlated relaxation superoperators. Simply,

\hat{\Gamma}=\sum_{\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{l,l^{\prime}}\hat{\Gamma}^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}},

(9)

and using eq. (
1
) while utilising (
2
),
\hat{\Gamma}^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}}
takes the form,

\hat{\Gamma}^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}}=-c^{\Lambda}c^{\Lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{mm^{\prime}}J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}mm^{\prime}}(\omega_{0})\Big[\hat{X}^{\Lambda}_{lm}\Big]^{L}\Big[\hat{X}^{\Lambda^{\prime}\dagger}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}\Big]^{L},

(10)

where the spectral density function is given by,

J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}mm^{\prime}}(\omega_{0})=\mathrm{Re}\int^{0}_{-\infty}d\tau\overline{\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{lm}(0)\Big]^{L}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\tau)\Big]^{L}}e^{-im^{\prime}\omega_{0}|\tau|}.

(11)

Here, the square brackets with the superscript
L
denote the laboratory frame. This is important since the spatial functions and spin tensors must be expressed in the same frame to legitimise the Hamiltonian they are associated with. However, the spatial functions are known in the molecule-fixed principal axis (
P
-) frame of the interaction in question, whereas the spin tensors are known in the space-fixed
L
-frame. Thus, we transform the spatial functions in the
L
-frame as a linear combination of functions in the diffusion (
D
-) frame using the properties of Wigner functions, before themselves being transformed as a linear combination of functions in the
P
-frame.

The relation to use is,

\Big[A_{lm}(t)\Big]^{L}=\sum_{n}\Big[A_{ln}\Big]^{D}D^{(l)}_{nm}(\Omega_{DL}(t)),

(12)

and the ensemble-averaged product in eq. (
11
) for the system becomes,

\begin{split}\overline{\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{lm}(0)\Big]^{L}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{l^{\prime}m^{\prime}}(\tau)\Big]^{L}}=\sum_{nn^{\prime}}&\overline{D^{(l)}_{mn}(\Omega_{LD}(0))D^{(l^{\prime})*}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(\Omega_{LD}(\tau))}\\
&\times\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{ln}\Big]^{D}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{l^{\prime}n^{\prime}}\Big]^{D},\end{split}

(13)

where the relation
D^{(l)}_{nm}(\Omega)=D^{(l)*}_{mn}(\Omega^{-1})
is used, and
\Omega^{-1}_{DL}\equiv\Omega_{LD}
. The time-dependence of the spatial functions in the
L
-frame has been absorbed into the Wigner functions, since the
D
-frame is molecule-fixed. We then define the time-correlation function as that between the Wigner functions only, as,

\begin{split}G^{ll^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}(\tau)&=\overline{D^{(l)}_{mn}(\Omega_{LD}(0))D^{(l^{\prime})*}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(\Omega_{LD}(\tau))}\\
&=\int\int d\Omega(0)d\Omega(\tau)D^{(l)}_{mn}(\Omega(0))D^{(l^{\prime})*}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(\Omega(\tau))\times P(\Omega(0))P(\Omega(\tau)|\Omega(0)),\end{split}

(14)

where
\Omega^{-1}_{DL}
have been denoted simply by
\Omega
in the second line for brevity,
P(\Omega(0))=1/(8\pi^{2})
and is the probability that the molecule hosting the spin system will be at orientation
\Omega
at time
t=0
, and
P(\Omega(\tau)|\Omega(0))
is the
conditional probability
that the molecule will be at orientation
\Omega(\tau)
at time
t^{\prime}=t+\tau=\tau
given that it was at orientation
\Omega(0)
at time
t=0
. From here, the notation and derivation is in similar vein of Huntress
2
,
3
.

The time-derivative of the probability that the molecule will be at orientation
\Omega(\tau)
at time
\tau
, in the limit of a rigid molecule reorienting in random steps of small angular displacement, is given by the
Favro equation

4
,

\frac{\partial}{\partial\tau}P(\Omega(\tau))=-H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}P(\Omega(\tau)),

(15)

where
H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}
is the
rotational-diffusion Hamiltonian
, which may be written in the form,

H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}=\mathbf{L}\bullet\mathbf{D}\bullet\mathbf{L}

(16)

where
\mathbf{L}
and
\mathbf{D}
are the quantum mechanical angular momentum operator and diffusion tensor, respectively. That is, the diffusion tensor describes the spatial aspect of the Hamiltonian in this case.

Favro shows that the solution to equation (
15
) is
4
,

P(\Omega(\tau))=\int d\Omega(0)P(\Omega(0))P(\Omega(0)|\Omega(\tau)),

(17)

for which the conditional probability is,

P(\Omega(0)|\Omega(\tau))=\sum_{\nu}\psi^{*}_{\nu}(\Omega(0))\psi_{\nu}(\Omega(\tau))e^{-E_{\nu}\tau},

(18)

noindent where
\psi(\Omega)
are eigenfunctions of
H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}
with the associated eigenvalue
E_{\nu}
. This solution is reliant on the boundary condition
P(\Omega(0)|\Omega(\tau\rightarrow 0))=\delta(\Omega(0),\Omega(\tau))
, where
\delta(\Omega(0),\Omega(\tau))
is the Dirac delta function.

If eq. (
16
) is written in the
D
-frame, it takes the form
H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}=\sum_{i}D_{i}L^{2}_{i}
in which
i\in\{x,y,z\}
and
L_{i}
have become the Cartesian angular momentum operators. This takes the form of the rigid-rotor Hamiltonian when considering the substitution
D_{i}\rightarrow\hbar^{2}/2I_{i}
, where
I_{i}
is the moment of inertia about principal axis
i
. Thus, (
18
) may be expanded in asymmetric-rotor eigenfunctions,

P(\Omega(\tau)|\Omega(0))=\sum_{\nu,J,M}\psi^{J*}_{\nu,M}(0)\psi^{J}_{\nu,M}(\tau)e^{-E^{J}_{\nu}\tau},

(19)

where,

\psi^{J}_{\nu,M}(t)=\sum_{K}a^{J}_{\nu,K}\phi^{J}_{K,M}

(20)

and
\phi^{J}_{K,M}
are symmetric-rotor eigenfunctions which may be expressed in terms of Wigner functions and take the form,

\phi^{J}_{K,M}=(-1)^{M-K}\sqrt{\frac{2J+1}{8\pi^{2}}}D^{(J)}_{-M-K}(\Omega).

(21)

We then have all we need to obtain the time-correlation function and subsequently the spectral density function and relaxation superoperator. Inserting these eigenfunctions and probabilities into (
33
) and setting
\Omega\equiv\Omega_{LD}
in (
21
),

\begin{split}G^{ll^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}(\tau)=&\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}}\sum_{\nu,J,M}(2J+1)e^{-E^{J}_{\nu}\tau}\\
&\times\int\int d\Omega_{LD}(0)d\Omega_{LD}(\tau)D^{(l)}_{mn}(\Omega_{LD}(0))D^{(l^{\prime})*}_{m^{\prime}n^{\prime}}(\Omega_{LD}(\tau))\\
&\times\bigg\{\sum_{K}a^{J*}_{\nu,K}(-1)^{M-K}D^{(J)*}_{-M-K}(\Omega_{LD}(0))\bigg\}\\
&\times\bigg\{\sum_{K^{\prime}}a^{J}_{\nu,K^{\prime}}(-1)^{M-K^{\prime}}D^{(J)}_{-M-K^{\prime}}(\Omega_{LD}(\tau))\bigg\},\end{split}

(22)

and rearranging the expression, while using the orthogonality relation,

\int d\Omega D^{j}_{pq}(\Omega)D^{j^{\prime}}_{p^{\prime}q^{\prime}}(\Omega)=\frac{8\pi^{2}}{2j+1}\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\delta_{pp^{\prime}}\delta_{qq^{\prime}},

(23)

the time correlation function simplifies to,

\begin{split}G^{l}_{mnn^{\prime}}(\tau)&=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}G^{ll^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}(\tau)\\
&=\frac{(-1)^{n+n^{\prime}}}{2l+1}\sum_{\nu}a^{l*}_{\nu,n}a^{l}_{\nu,-n^{\prime}}e^{-E^{l}_{\nu}\tau},\end{split}

(24)

where
l=l^{\prime}=J
,
m=m^{\prime}=-M
,
n=-K
and
n^{\prime}=-K^{\prime}
.

From this, the spectral density becomes,

\begin{split}J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{lm}(\omega_{0})&=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}mm^{\prime}}(\omega_{0})\\
&=\mathrm{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\tau\overline{\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{lm}(0)\Big]^{L}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{lm}(\tau)\Big]^{L}}e^{-im\omega_{0}|\tau|}\\
&\begin{split}=\sum_{nn^{\prime}}\frac{(-1)^{n+n^{\prime}}}{2l+1}\sum_{\nu}&a^{l}_{\nu,n}a^{l}_{\nu,-n^{\prime}}\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{ln}\Big]^{D}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ln^{\prime}}\Big]^{D}\\
&\times\mathrm{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{0}d\tau e^{-E^{l}_{\nu}\tau}e^{-im\omega_{0}|\tau|}.\end{split}\end{split}

(25)

Performing the integral and inserting back into the relaxation superoperator, we have,

\begin{split}\hat{\Gamma}^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{l}&=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\hat{\Gamma}^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ll^{\prime}}\\
&=-c^{\Lambda}c^{\Lambda^{\prime}}\sum_{m}J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{lm}(\omega_{0})\Big[\hat{X}^{\Lambda}_{lm}\Big]^{L}\Big[\hat{X}^{\Lambda^{\prime}\dagger}_{lm}\Big]^{L},\end{split}

(26)

with,

\begin{split}J^{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}_{lm}(\omega_{0})=\sum_{nn^{\prime}}&\frac{(-1)^{n+n^{\prime}}}{2l+1}\Big[A^{\Lambda*}_{ln}\Big]^{D}\Big[A^{\Lambda^{\prime}}_{ln^{\prime}}\Big]^{D}\\
&\times\sum_{\nu}a^{l}_{\nu,n}a^{l}_{\nu,-n^{\prime}}\frac{E^{l}_{\nu}}{E^{(l)2}_{\nu}+m^{2}\omega^{2}_{0}}.\end{split}

(27)

This is the general case of an asymmetric-top molecule. To derive the case for a symmetric rotor with the
P
- and
D
-frames coincident (as in our model), we refer back to eq. (
16
), where we may write it in the
D
-frame as,

H_{\mathrm{rot-diff}}=D_{\perp}\left(L^{2}_{x}+L^{2}_{y}\right)+D_{\parallel}L^{2}_{z}

(28)

where
D_{\perp}
and
D_{\parallel}
are rotational diffusion constants associated with axes perpendicular and parallel, respectively, with the spin chain.

Eq. (
28
) is of the same form as the rigid-rotor Hamiltonian for a symmetric-top. As such, the eigenfunctions and eigenvalues in eq. (
18
) are those of a quantum mechanical rigid-rotor
5
,
6
:

\psi_{\nu}(t)\xrightarrow{}\phi^{J}_{K,M}(t)\equiv(-1)^{M-K}\sqrt{\frac{2J+1}{8\pi^{2}}}D^{(J)}_{-M-K}(\Omega(t))

(29)

E_{\nu}\xrightarrow{}E^{J}_{K}\equiv D_{\perp}J(J+1)+K^{2}(D_{\parallel}-D_{\perp}).

(30)

For our specific system,
J\equiv l=2
and
K=0
. To see this, note that the only non-zero component for the dipole-dipole (DD) interaction is
\left[A^{\mathrm{DD}}_{20}\right]^{P}=\sqrt{6}
in the
P
-frame. The
z
-principal axis is defined as a vector connecting the two nuclei in question. We then assume this is coincident with the
z
-axis in the
D
-frame, with the
x
- and
y
-axes arbitrary. Writing,
D^{(l)}_{mn}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)=e^{-im\alpha}d^{(l)}_{mn}(\beta)e^{-in\gamma}
and assuming coincidence of the
P
- and
D
-frames (i.e.
\{\alpha,\beta,\gamma\}=\{0,0,0\}
), eq. (
12
) may be written,

\begin{split}\Big[A^{\mathrm{DD}}_{20}\Big]^{P}&=\sum_{n}\Big[A^{\mathrm{DD}}_{2n}\Big]^{D}d^{(2)}_{n0}(\beta=0)\\
&=\Big[A^{\mathrm{DD}}_{20}\Big]^{D},\end{split}

(31)

where
d^{(2)}_{00}(\beta)=\frac{3\mathrm{cos}^{2}\beta-1}{2}
is the only non-vanishing reduced Wigner function, and equates to unity for
\beta=0
.

In general, the CSA
P
-frame will not be coincident with the DD
P
-frame. To simplify analytical expressions in the main text, however, they are assumed to be so for our system. Also, assuming cylindrical symmetry of the rigid-rod, the biaxiality parameters may be approximated as 0. Then the same relation holds for the CSA interaction; i.e. the only non-vanishing component of the spatial tensor is,

\Big[A^{\mathrm{CSA}}_{20}\Big]^{P}=\Big[A^{\mathrm{CSA}}_{20}\Big]^{D}.

(32)

Since
n=K
, the only non-vanishing term in the correlation function is then
E^{(2)}_{0}=6D_{\perp}
, and related to the rotational correlation time
\tau_{\perp}\equiv\big(6D_{\perp}\big)^{-1}
. We then see that rotational motion about the principal axis of inertia parallel to the rod does not modulate the interactions responsible for relaxation, and this is a consequence of the coincidence of the
P
- and
D
-frames, as well as the assumption that the molecule tumbles rigidly. The secularised time-correlation function for our system becomes,

G^{ll^{\prime}}_{mm^{\prime}nn^{\prime}}(\tau)=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\frac{(-1)^{n+n^{\prime}}}{2l+1}e^{-\tau/\tau_{\perp}}.

(33)

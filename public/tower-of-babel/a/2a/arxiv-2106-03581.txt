Abstract
. We investigate the nonnegative solutions to the nonlinear integral inequality u ≥ I α ∗ ( ( I β ∗ u p ) ​ u q ) u\geq I_{\alpha}\ast\big((I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\big) a.e. in ℝ N {\mathbb{R}}^{N} , where α , β ∈ ( 0 , N ) \alpha,\beta\in(0,N) , p , q > 0 p,q>0 and I α , I β I_{\alpha},I_{\beta} denote the Riesz potentials of order α \alpha and β \beta respectively. Our approach relies on a nonlocal positivity principle which allows us to derive optimal ranges for the parameters α \alpha , β \beta , p p and q q to describe the existence and the nonexistence of a solution. The optimal decay at infinity for such solutions is also discussed.

1.
Introduction

We study nonnegative solutions of the following integral inequality with double Riesz potentials

(1)

u\geq I_{\alpha}\ast\big((I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\big)\quad\text{a.e.~in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

in the range
N\geq 2
,
p,q>0
,
\alpha,\beta\in(0,N)
and
I_{\gamma}(x):=A_{\gamma}|x|^{-(N-\gamma)}
is the Riesz potential of order
\gamma\in(0,N)
where
*
denotes the standard convolution in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. The choice of the normalisation constant
A_{\gamma}:=\frac{\Gamma((N-\gamma)/2)}{\pi^{N/2}2^{\gamma}\Gamma(\gamma/2)}
ensures that
I_{\gamma}(x)
could be interpreted as the Green function of
(-\Delta)^{\gamma/2}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, and that the semigroup property
I_{\alpha+\beta}=I_{\alpha}*I_{\beta}
holds for all
\alpha,\beta\in(0,N)
such that
\alpha+\beta<N
, see for example
[
7
]
*p. 45.

By a nonnegative
solution
of (
1
) we understand a function
u\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
u\geq 0
, such that the right hand side of (
1
) is well-defined, i.e.

(2)

I_{\alpha}\ast\big((I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\big)<+\infty\quad\text{a.e.~in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N}

and the inequality (
1
) holds a.e. in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
. Condition (
2
) above is equivalent (see Lemma
2.1
below) to

(3)

(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big).

Integral inequalities and equations featuring a single Riesz potential have been extensively investigated in the past two decades. The prototype model

u=I_{\alpha}\ast u^{p}\quad\text{ in }\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

has been largely investigated starting with the seminal works in
[
3
,
4
]
. More recently, various techniques have been devised to deal with systems of equations or inequalities that incorporate anisotropic or more general potentials
[
2
,
6
,
9
,
15
,
14
]
.

Our aim in this paper is to provide an optimal description for the existence and nonexistence of positive solutions to the integral inequality (
1
).

For any
\alpha>0
, the fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
is defined by means of the Fourier transform

\widehat{(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u}(\xi):=|\xi|^{\alpha}\hat{u}(\xi)

for all
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
such that
|\xi|^{\alpha}u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, here

\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
stands for the space of tempered distributions on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
which is the dual of the Schwartz space
\mathcal{S}
.

Since for
\alpha\in(0,N)
the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}
can be interpreted as the inverse of
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
(cf.
[
13
]
*Sect.5.1 or
[
2
, Section 2.1]
), under some extra integrability conditions on
u\geq 0
, inequality (
1
) is equivalent to the elliptic inequality

(4)

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u\geq(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\quad\text{a.e. in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}$},

provided that both (
1
) and (
4
) are well-defined.
This is the case, for instance, if (
3
) holds and
u
belongs to the
homogeneous Sobolev space
\dot{H}^{\alpha/2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
, so that (
4
) is understood in the weak sense. Pointwise interpretations of the inequality (
4
) for non-integer
\alpha/2
are also possible, cf.
[
2
, Theorem 2.13]
. For a comparison of different definitions of the higher order fractional Laplacian
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
see
[
1
]
.

Inequality (
4
) is a Choquard type inequality. Equations and inequalities of such structure originate from mathematical physics and have attracted considerable interest of mathematicians in the past decade, see
[
12
]
for a survey. In the 2nd order elliptic case
\alpha=2
optimal regimes for the existence and nonexistence of positive solutions to inequality (
4
) were fully investigated in
[
11
]
.
The higher–order polyharmonic case
\alpha/2=m\in\mathbb{N}
was recently studied in
[
5
]
, where (amongst other results) optimal existence and nonexistence regimes for the equation (
4
) were obtained for the exponents
p\geq 1
and
q>1
, see
[
5
, Theorem 1.4]
.

In this work we extend earlier results in
[
11
]
and
[
5
]
to the full admissible range
\alpha\in(0,N)
and exponents
p,q>0
. Our approach is different from the techniques in
[
5
]
, which were based on the poly–superharmonic properties of
(-\Delta)^{m}
in the elliptic framework of equation (
4
). Instead, we work entirely with the double–nonlocal inequality (
1
). Our analysis of (
1
) employs only direct Riesz kernel estimates, and a new version of the nonlocal positivity principle in Lemma
3.1
, inspired by
[
11
, Proposition 3.2]
. This has the advantage of incorporating the fractional case of noninteger
\alpha/2
in a seemingly effortless way, and does not rely on comparison principles or Harnack type inequalities, which are commonly used for similar Liouville type results in the elliptic framework, but which are generally speaking not available in the case of the higher–order fractional Laplacians
(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}
with
\alpha>2
.

The main result of this work related to the existence of positive solutions to (
1
) reads as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
p,q>0
. Then, inequality (
1
) has a nontrivial nonnegative solution
u\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
which satisfies (
2
) if and only if

\begin{cases}\displaystyle p>\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}&\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle p+q>\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}&\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle q>\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}&\quad\mbox{ if }\beta>N-\alpha,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle q\geq 1&\quad\mbox{ if }\beta=N-\alpha,\\[7.22743pt]
\displaystyle q>1-\frac{N-\alpha-\beta}{N}p&\quad\mbox{ if }\beta<N-\alpha.\end{cases}

The necessary part of the proof follows directly from Propositions
2.3
,
4.1
-
4.5
below.
The sufficiency follows from Propositions
5.4
-
5.7
,
where we construct explicitly smooth positive radial solutions to (
1
).
In the case
\alpha=2
our results are fully consistent with the results established in
[
11
, Theorem 1]
for the 2nd order elliptic inequality (
4
).
The nonexistence of positive solutions to double–nonlocal inequality (
1
) with
p>1
,
q>0
and
p+q\leq\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}
was established by different methods in
[
8
, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 1.2
.

In Section
5
we also discuss the optimal decay of solutions to (
1
) in terms of the parameters
\alpha
,
\beta
,
p
and
q
.
Clearly (see (

)), if
u\geq 0
is a nontrivial solution of (

) then

\liminf_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)|x|^{N-\alpha}>0
. In particular, for
R\gg 1
we have

(5)

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\geq cR^{\alpha}.

In Proposition

we establish an integral lower bound

(6)

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\geq cR^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-Nq}{1-q}},

which is stronger than (

) when

and

.
In Propositions

and

we construct positive radial solutions

. When

the bounds in (

and

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect some useful facts for our approach.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a nonnegative measurable function. Then, the Riesz potential
I_{\alpha}*f
of order
\alpha\in(0,N)
is well defined, in the sense that

(7)

I_{\alpha}*f<+\infty\quad\text{a.e. in ${\mathbb{R}}^{N}$,}

if and only if

(8)

f\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big).

Moreover, if (
7
) fails then
I_{\alpha}*f=+\infty
everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
, see
[
7
]
*p.61-62.
We present the proof of the lemma for completeness.

Proof.

Assume first that (
7
) holds. Then, for any
x,y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
x\neq 0
we have

|x-y|^{N-\alpha}\leq c(|x|^{N-\alpha}+|y|^{N-\alpha})\leq c\max\{1,|x|^{N-\alpha}\}(1+|y|^{N-\alpha})\leq c\max\{1,|x|^{N-\alpha}\}(1+|y|)^{N-\alpha}.

Thus, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that (
7
) holds, we have

\infty>(I_{\alpha}\ast f)(x)\geq\frac{1}{c\max\{1,|x|^{N-\alpha}\}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}\frac{f(y)dy}{(1+|y|)^{N-\alpha}},

which yields (
8
).

Conversely, assume now that (
8
) holds and let
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus\{0\}
. From (
8
) we have
f\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
. Then

\displaystyle(I_{\alpha}\ast f)(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{|y|\leq 2|x|}\frac{f(y)dy}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}+\int_{|y|>2|x|}\frac{f(y)dy}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|y|\leq 2|x|}\frac{f(y)dy}{|x-y|^{N-\alpha}}+2^{N-\alpha}\int_{|y|>2|x|}\frac{f(y)dy}{|y|^{N-\alpha}}

\displaystyle<\infty.

∎

In the same spirit to the above proof, if
f\geq 0
and (
7
) (or, equivalently (
8
)) holds, then

(9)

I_{\alpha}*f(x)\geq\frac{c}{|x|^{N-\alpha}}\int_{B_{|x|}(0)}f(y)\,dy.

One of the elementary yet important for our approach consequences of (
9
) is the following estimate, which we will be using frequently, and which to some extent is the counterpart of the Harnack inequalities on the annuli in the study of (
4
).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,N)
,
\theta>0
and
0\leq f\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big)
. Then for all
R>0
we have

(10)

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}\big(I_{\alpha}*f\big)^{\theta}\geq CR^{N-(N-\alpha)\theta}\Big(\int_{B_{R}}f\Big)^{\theta}.

Proof.

Follows from (
9
) by integration.
∎

An obvious implication of (
9
) is that
I_{\alpha}*f
can not decay faster than
I_{\alpha}
at infinity, even if the function
f
is compactly supported.
Recall also that if
f\geq 0
then
an elementary estimate shows that for every
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,

(11)

\ I_{\alpha}*f(x)\geq\frac{A_{\alpha}}{R^{N-\alpha}}\int_{B_{R}(x)}f(y)dy.

As a consequence, if
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
) that is positive on a set of positive measure, then
u
is everywhere strictly positive on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
and the following lower bounds must hold:

(12)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle\geq c(1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)},

(13)

\displaystyle I_{\beta}\ast u^{p}(x)

\displaystyle\geq c(1+|x|)^{-(N-\beta)}.

On the other hand, (
2
) requires

(14)

\displaystyle u^{p}

\displaystyle\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\beta)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big),

(15)

\displaystyle(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}

\displaystyle\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big).

Combining the competing upper and lower bounds immediately leads to the following nonexistence result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that either
p\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
, or
\alpha+\beta>N
and
q\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}-1
.
If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
) then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

First we note that (
12
) and (
14
) are incompatible when
p\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
. Then we observe that (
12
), (
13
) and (
15
) are incompatible when
0<q\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}-1
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

We will see in Proposition
4.3
below that
q\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}-1
is suboptimal for the nonexistence and could be refined.

3.
Nonlocal positivity principle

The nonexistence result in Proposition
2.3
”decouples” the values of
p
and
q
. In order to deduce an estimate which involves the quantity
p+q
which appears in Theorem
1.1
, we need the following lemma, inspired by
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
and
[
11
]
*Section 3.

Lemma 3.1

(Nonlocal positivity principle)
.

Let
\alpha\in(0,N)
and
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\to[0,\infty)
be a measurable function.
Assume that there exists
u\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
such that
u>0
a.e. in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
Vu\in L^{1}\big((1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}dx,{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\big)
and

(16)

u\geq I_{\alpha}\ast(Vu)\quad\text{a.e.~in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Then for every
R>0
and
0\leq\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
,

(17)

\int_{B_{R}}\varphi^{2}\geq CR^{\alpha-N}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{V}\varphi\right)^{2}.

Proof.

Take
\psi:=\frac{\varphi^{2}}{u}
as a test function in (
16
). Then

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}I_{\alpha}(|x-y|)V(y)u(y)\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)}{u(x)}dy\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}I_{\alpha}(|x-y|)\left(V(x)u(x)\frac{\varphi^{2}(y)}{u(y)}+V(y)u(y)\frac{\varphi^{2}(x)}{u(x)}\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}I_{\alpha}(|x-y|)\sqrt{V(x)V(y)}\varphi(y)\varphi(x)dxdy

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2}\iint_{B_{R}\times B_{R}}I_{\alpha}(|x-y|)u(x)u(y)\left(\sqrt{V(x)}\frac{\varphi(y)}{u(y)}-\sqrt{V(y)}\frac{\varphi(x)}{u(x)}\right)^{2}dxdy

\displaystyle\geq\frac{A_{\alpha}}{2^{N-\alpha}R^{N-\alpha}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\sqrt{V(x)}\varphi(x)dx\right)^{2},

since

(18)

I_{\alpha}(x-y)\geq\frac{A_{\alpha}}{2^{N-\alpha}R^{N-\alpha}}\qquad(x,y\in B_{R}),

which completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

Nonlocal inequality (
16
) can be interpreted as the “inversion” of the fractional Schrödinger inequality

(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}u-Vu\geq 0\quad\text{in}\quad{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

In this context Lemma
3.1
can be seen as a higher–order version of the fractional
Agmon–Allegretto–Piepenbrink’s positivity principle: if (
16
) has a positive supersolution then a certain variational inequality which involves the potential
V
must hold.
We will see that Lemma
3.1
alongside with the standard integral estimate (
10
) of the Riesz potentials are sufficient for the complete analysis of the existence and nonexistence of positive solutions of the nonlinear inequality (
1
).

Using Lemma
3.1
we establish the following estimate.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
p,q>0
and
u>0
be a solution of (
1
).
Then, for every
R>0
and every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
,

(19)

\int_{B_{R}}\varphi^{2}\geq CR^{\alpha+\beta-2N}\left(\int_{B_{R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\varphi\right)^{2}.

Proof.

For every
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(B_{R})
, by Lemma
3.1
with
V=(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q-1}
, and using a similar inequality to (
18
) for
I_{\beta}\ast u^{p}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\varphi^{2}

\displaystyle\geq CR^{\alpha-N}\left(\int_{B_{R}}\big((I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q-1}\big)^{1/2}\varphi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime}R^{\alpha+\beta-2N}\left(\int_{B_{R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\varphi\right)^{2}.\qed

One of the principal tools in the subsequent analysis is the following decay estimate on the solutions of (
1
), which is an adaptation of (
19
). Note that for
q<1
our estimate contains a lower bound on the solution, since the 2nd integral involves a negative power of
u
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
p,q>0
and
u>0
be a solution of (
1
).
Then for every
R>0
,

(20)

\left(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq CR^{3N-\alpha-\beta}.

Proof.

Take
\varphi_{R}(x)=\varphi(x/R)
, where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{c}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
is such that
\mathrm{supp}(\varphi)\subset\overline{B}_{4}\setminus B_{1/2}
,
\varphi\equiv 1
on
\overline{B}_{2}\setminus B_{1}
and
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
. Then,by (
19
) we find

\displaystyle cR^{N}=\int_{B_{4R}}\varphi_{R}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime}R^{\alpha+\beta-2N}\left(\int_{B_{4R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{4R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\varphi_{R}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq C^{\prime}R^{\alpha+\beta-2N}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}.\qed

4.
Nonexistence

In this section we derive several nonexistence result for (
1
). Our approach is inspired by

[
11
]
which studied the inequality (
4
) in the semilinear the case
\alpha=2
, yet with substantial modifications. In particular, in this work we completely avoid the use of the comparison principle and Harnack’s inequalities, which are not applicable in the framework of the double–nonlocal inequality (
1
). It turns out that Harnack inequality estimates in the context of (
1
) can be replaced by the estimate (
10
).

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that
p+q<1
.
If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
) then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Since
u^{p}\in L^{1}((1+|x|)^{-(N-\beta)}dx)
and
\beta<N
, by Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}=o\big(R^{N-\beta}\big)=o\big(R^{N}\big)\quad\mbox{ as }R\to\infty.

Since
p+q<1
, we may apply Hölder and then the estimate (
20
) to obtain

\displaystyle cR^{N}=\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{p(1-q)}{2p+1-q}}\;u^{\frac{(q-1)p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1-q}{2p+1-q}}\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1-q-p}{2p+1-q}}\biggl[\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]^{\frac{p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1-q-p}{2p+1-q}}\Bigl(CR^{3N-\alpha-\beta}\Bigr)^{\frac{p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq o(1)R^{N\frac{1-q-p}{2p+1-q}}\bigl(R^{3N}\bigr)^{\frac{p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle=o(1)R^{N},

which raises a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that
1\leq p+q\leq\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}
.
If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
) then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Assume first
p+q<\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}
.
By the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality,

\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}\geq cR^{-N}\left(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2},

and so (
20
) implies

(21)

CR^{4N-\alpha-\beta}\geq\left(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}\right)^{2}\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}.

Using (
21
) and the Cauchy–Schwarz again together with
u\geq c|x|^{-(N-\alpha)}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}
(that follows from (
12
)), we obtain

(22)

\displaystyle CR^{4N-\alpha-\beta}

\displaystyle\geq\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{\frac{p}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{p+q-1}{4}}\Big)^{4}

\displaystyle\geq cR^{4N-(N-\alpha)(p+q-1)},

which is a contradiction since
0<p+q-1<\frac{\alpha+\beta}{N-\alpha}
.

Assume now
p+q=\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}
. By Hölder’s inequality we find

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}I_{\beta}(x-y)u^{p}(y)u^{q}(x)\Big)\Big(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}I_{\beta}(x-y)u^{p}(x)u^{q}(y)\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\Big(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}I_{\beta}(x-y)u(y)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}\,dx\,dy\Big)^{2},

so that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\geq\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}u(x)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}I_{\beta}(x-y)u(y)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}.

Using the lower bound (
12
) and the fact that
\frac{p+q}{2}=\frac{N+\beta}{2(N-\alpha)}>0
we deduce

\begin{split}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}&\geq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}u(x)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}I_{\beta}(x-y)u(y)^{\frac{p+q}{2}}\,dx\,dy\\
&\geq c\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{\frac{N+\beta}{2}}}I_{\beta}(x-y)\frac{1}{|y|^{\frac{N+\beta}{2}}}\,dx\,dy=\infty.\end{split}

Hence,

(23)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{B_{R}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}=\infty.

Since
u
satisfies (
1
), from Lemma
2.2
with
\theta=\frac{p+q-1}{4}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4(N-\alpha)}
and
f=(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}
we find

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{p+q-1}{4}}\geq\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}(I_{\alpha}\ast f)^{\frac{p+q-1}{4}}\geq CR^{N-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}}\Big(\int_{B_{R}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\Big)^{\frac{p+q-1}{4}}.

From the above estimate and (
23
) we deduce

\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{1}{R^{N-\frac{\alpha+\beta}{4}}}\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{p+q-1}{4}}=\infty,

which contradicts the upper bound in (
22
).
∎

If

we give precise lower bounds on
\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{q-1}
to obtain a further nonexistence result.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that
\alpha+\beta>N
and
1<q\leq\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
.
If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (

) then

.

Proof.

Assume that
u>0
on a set of positive measure.
From (

) we obtain

(24)

\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}\geq cR^{2N-(N-\alpha)(q-1)}.

On the other hand, by Corollary
3.4
,

(25)

\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{CR^{3N-\beta-\alpha}}{\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}}\leq C^{\prime}R^{3N-\beta-\alpha}.

This yields a contradiction if
q<\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
.

In the critical case
q=\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
using (
9
), (
13
) we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}

\displaystyle\geq c\left(\int_{B_{1}}u^{p}\right)\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{u(x)^{q}}{|x|^{N-\beta}}dx

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}\int_{B_{R}\setminus B_{1}}\frac{1}{|x|^{N-\beta+(N-\alpha)q}}\,dx=c^{\prime\prime}\log(R),

since
(N-\alpha)q=\beta
.
By Lemma
2.2
with
\theta=\frac{q-1}{2}>0
,

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}\Big(I_{\alpha}*\big((I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\big)\Big)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq cR^{N-(N-\alpha)\frac{q-1}{2}}\left(\int_{B_{R}}(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\right)^{\frac{q-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq c^{\prime}R^{\frac{2N-(N-\alpha)(q-1)}{2}}\log^{\frac{q-1}{2}}(R)

\displaystyle=c^{\prime}R^{\frac{3N-\alpha-\beta}{2}}\log^{\frac{q-1}{2}}(R),

which contradicts (

).
∎

The transitional locally linear case

Proposition 4.4
.

Let

and assume that
\alpha+\beta>N
and

Proof.

Using Corollary

, for any

In the sublinear case

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that
p+q\geq 1
,

and

q\leq 1-\frac{N-\alpha-\beta}{N}p.

If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (

) then

.

Proof.

Since

, by Hölder’s inequality we deduce

(27)

\displaystyle cR^{N}=\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1

\displaystyle=\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p\frac{1-q}{2p+1-q}}\,u^{\frac{q-1}{2}\frac{2p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)^{\frac{1-q}{2p+1-q}}\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2p}{2p+1-q}}

\displaystyle=\biggl[\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\biggr]^{\frac{1-q}{2p+1-q}}\biggl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\biggr)^{2\frac{p+q-1}{2p+1-q}}.

By Corollary
3.4
we have

\left(\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}\right)\left(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\right)^{2}\leq CR^{3N-\alpha-\beta},

which yields

(28)

\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\Bigr)\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\leq CR^{3N-\alpha-\beta},

and on the other hand

(29)

\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Bigr)^{2}\leq C\frac{R^{3N-\beta-\alpha}}{\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}}\leq C^{\prime}R^{3N-\alpha-\beta}.

If
q<1-\frac{N-\beta-\alpha}{N}p
, we use (
28
)-(
29
) in (

) to raise a contradiction since

.

If

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{p}\geq\frac{\Bigl(\displaystyle{\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1}\Bigr)^{1+\frac{2p}{1-q}}}{\displaystyle{\Bigl(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{1-q}{2}}}\Bigr)^{\frac{2p}{1-q}}}\geq cR^{N-\frac{p}{1-q}(N-\alpha-\beta)}=c.

This shows that

and

Using this fact in (

\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{\frac{q-1}{2}}\Big)^{2}=o(R^{3N-\alpha-\beta})\quad\mbox{ as }R\to\infty.

We now use this last estimate and (
28
) in (

) to conclude.

5.
Optimal decay and existence

If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
), then either
u\equiv 0
or

u
must obey the ”natural” lower bound (
12
), which implies in particular, the integral lower bound

(30)

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\geq cR^{\alpha}.

In the region
q<1
the estimate (
20
) of Corollary
3.4
leads to an integral lower bound which improves upon (
30
) when
\alpha+\beta<N
and
q<\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
p,q>0
and assume that
\alpha+\beta<N
and
q<\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}{<1}
.
If
u\geq 0
is a solution of (
1
), then either
u\equiv 0
or

(31)

\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\geq cR^{\frac{\alpha+\beta-Nq}{1-q}}.

As pointed out in Remark
1.2
, since
\alpha+\beta<N
and
q<\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}<1
, the exponent of
R
in (
31
) is greater than
\alpha
.

Proof.

From Corollary
3.4
we have

\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{-\frac{1-q}{2}}\Big)^{2}\leq c\frac{R^{3N-\alpha-\beta}}{\displaystyle\int_{B_{2R}}u^{p}}\leq c^{\prime}R^{3N-\alpha-\beta}.

Further, by Hölder’s inequality (since
0<q<1
) we have

\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u^{-\frac{1-q}{2}}\Big)^{2}\geq\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\Big)^{-(1-q)}\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}1\Big)^{3-q}.

Now, the above estimates yield

\Big(\int_{B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}}u\Big)^{-(1-q)}\leq R^{Nq-\alpha-\beta},

which leads to (
31
).
∎

Our next step is to construct explicit solutions with the decay which match or near-match the lower bounds in (
30
) and (
31
). Before we do this, we recall the following simple estimates, cf.
[
11
, Lemma A.1 and A.2]
which are frequently used in the proofs below.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
v\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
\gamma\in(0,N)
and
s>\gamma
.
If

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}v(x)|x|^{s}<\infty,

then

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)|x|^{s-\gamma}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $\gamma<s<N$},

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)\frac{|x|^{N-\gamma}}{\log|x|}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $s=N$},\vskip 12.0pt plus 4.0pt minus 4.0pt

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)|x|^{N-\gamma}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $s>N$}.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
v\in L^{1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
,
\gamma\in(0,N)
and
\sigma\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
If

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}v(x)\frac{|x|^{N}}{(\log|x|)^{\sigma}}<\infty,

then

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)|x|^{N-\gamma}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $\sigma<-1$},

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)\frac{|x|^{N-\gamma}}{(\log(\log|x|))}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $\sigma=-1$},

\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}(I_{\gamma}\ast v)(x)\frac{|x|^{N-\gamma}}{(\log|x|)^{\sigma+1}}

\displaystyle<\infty

\displaystyle\text{if $\sigma>-1$}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Assume that

(32)

p>\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha},\quad p+q>\frac{N+\beta}{N-\alpha}\quad\text{and}\quad q>\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}.

Then, (
1
) admits a positive radial solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{N})
which satisfies

(33)

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)|x|^{N-\alpha}<\infty.

Proof.

Let
0<\varepsilon<q(N-\alpha)-\beta
and take
u(x)=(1+|x|)^{-(N-\alpha)}
. Since
p(N-\alpha)>\beta
we can apply the estimates in Lemma
5.2
to deduce

(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})(x)\leq c_{1}\begin{cases}(1+|x|)^{\beta-p(N-\alpha)}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)<N\\
(1+|x|)^{\beta-N}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)>N\\
(1+|x|)^{\beta-N}\log(|x|+e)&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)=N\\
\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

for some constant
c_{1}>0
. Thus,

\Big[(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\Big](x)\leq c_{1}\begin{cases}(1+|x|)^{\beta-(p+q)(N-\alpha)}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)<N\\
(1+|x|)^{\beta-N-q(N-\alpha)}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)>N\\
(1+|x|)^{\beta-N-q(N-\alpha)}\log(|x|+e)&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)=N\\
\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

In particular, one may further estimate as

\Big[(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\Big](x)\leq c_{1}\begin{cases}(1+|x|)^{\beta-(p+q)(N-\alpha)}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)<N\\
(1+|x|)^{\beta-N-q(N-\alpha)+\varepsilon}&\quad\mbox{ if }p(N-\alpha)\geq N\\
\end{cases}\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Since
(p+q)(N-\alpha)-\beta>N
and
N-\beta+q(N-\alpha)-\varepsilon>N
it follows from the third estimate in Lemma
5.2
that

I_{\alpha}\ast\Big[(I_{\beta}\ast u^{p})u^{q}\Big](x)\leq c_{2}(1+|x|)^{\alpha-N}=c_{2}u(x)\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

where
c_{2}>0
is a constant.
Thus, the continuous function
U(x)=c_{2}^{-1/(p+q-1)}u(x)
is a solution of (
1
) which satisfies (
33
).
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Assume that

1-\frac{N-\alpha-\beta}{N}p<q<\frac{\beta}{N-\alpha}<1.

Then, (
1
) admits a positive radial solution
u\in C({\mathbb{R}}^{N})

\limsup_{|x|\to\infty}u(x)|x|^{\frac{N-\alpha-\beta}{1-q}}<\infty.

Let

Since

, we have

5.2

I_{\beta}*u^{p}\leq c_{1}(1+|x|)^{\beta-N}\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N},

Since

, we have

(I_{\beta}*u^{p})u^{q}\leq c_{2}(1+|x|)^{-\frac{N-\beta-\alpha q}{1-q}}\quad\mbox{ in }{\mathbb{R}}^{N}.

Hence, by the first estimate of Lemma
5.2

∎

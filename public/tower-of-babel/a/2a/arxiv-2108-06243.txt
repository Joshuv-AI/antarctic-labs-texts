MINIMAL BOSONIZATION OF DOUBLE-GRADED QUANTUM MECHANICS
C. QUESNE

Abstract
The superalgebra of ℤ 2 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2} -graded supersymmetric quantum mechanics is shown to be realizable in terms of a single bosonic degree of freedom. Such an approach is directly inspired by a description of the corresponding ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -graded superalgebra in the framework of a Calogero-Vasiliev algebra or, more generally, of a generalized deformed oscillator algebra. In the case of the ℤ 2 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2} -graded superalgebra, the central element Z Z has the property of distinguishing between degenerate eigenstates of the Hamiltonian.

2
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded SUSYQM

In
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded SUSYQM, we are going to deal with a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded vector space (over
\mathbb{C}
) that is the direct sum of four homogeneous subspaces, labelled by
\overrightarrow{a}=(a_{1},a_{2})
, where
a_{1}
,
a_{2}\in\{0,1\}
,

g=g_{(0,0)}\oplus g_{(1,0)}\oplus g_{(0,1)}\oplus g_{(1,1)}.

(1)

Homogeneous elements of
g_{\overrightarrow{a}}
are denoted by
X_{\overrightarrow{a}}
,
Y_{\overrightarrow{a}}
, …. The vector space
g
becomes a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded Lie superalgebra if it admits a bilinear form, denoted by
[[\cdot,\cdot]]
, satisfying the identities

\displaystyle[[X_{\overrightarrow{a}},Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}]]\in g_{\overrightarrow{a}+\overrightarrow{b}},

(2)

\displaystyle[[X_{\overrightarrow{a}},Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}]]=-(-1)^{\overrightarrow{a}.\overrightarrow{b}}[[Y_{\overrightarrow{b}},X_{\overrightarrow{a}}]],

(3)

\displaystyle(-1)^{\overrightarrow{a}.\overrightarrow{c}}[[X_{\overrightarrow{a}},[[Y_{\overrightarrow{b}},Z_{\overrightarrow{c}}]]\,]]+(-1)^{\overrightarrow{b}.\overrightarrow{a}}[[Y_{\overrightarrow{b}},[[Z_{\overrightarrow{c}},X_{\overrightarrow{a}}]]\,]]

\displaystyle\quad{}+(-1)^{\overrightarrow{c}.\overrightarrow{b}}[[Z_{\overrightarrow{c}},[[X_{\overrightarrow{a}},Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}]]\,]]=0,

(4)

where

\overrightarrow{a}+\overrightarrow{b}=(a_{1}+b_{1},a_{2}+b_{2}),\qquad\overrightarrow{a}.\overrightarrow{b}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}a_{k}b_{k}

(5)

are calculated modulo 2. Equation (
4
) is called the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded Jacobi relation. In practice, the general Lie bracket is given by

[[X_{\overrightarrow{a}},Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}]]=X_{\overrightarrow{a}}Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}-(-1)^{\overrightarrow{a}.\overrightarrow{b}}Y_{\overrightarrow{b}}X_{\overrightarrow{a}}

(6)

and therefore coincides with either a commutator or an anticommutator.

In the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded version of SUSYQM, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded superalgebra has four generators
H_{00}
,
Q_{10}
,
Q_{01}
, and
Z_{11}
,

which are Hermitian operators acting in a Hilbert space
{\cal H}={\cal H}_{00}\oplus{\cal H}_{10}\oplus{\cal H}_{01}\oplus{\cal H}_{11}
and satisfying the relations

\{Q_{10},Q_{10}\}=\{Q_{01},Q_{01}\}=2H_{00},\qquad[Q_{10},Q_{01}]=2{\rm i}Z_{11}.

(7)

As a consequence of the latter, we also have

[H_{00},Q_{10}]=[H_{00},Q_{01}]=[H_{00},Z_{11}]=0,\qquad\{Z_{11},Q_{10}\}=\{Z_{11},Q_{01}\}=0.

(8)

The operator
H_{00}
is the Hamiltonian,
Q_{10}
and
Q_{01}
are two supercharges, and
Z_{11}
is a central element. From now on, we will abbreviate
H_{00}
and
Z_{11}
in
H
and
Z
, respectively.

Instead of
Q_{10}
and
Q_{01}
, we may consider their linear combinations

Q^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{2}(Q_{10}+{\rm i}Q_{01}),\qquad Q=\frac{1}{2}(Q_{10}-{\rm i}Q_{01}),

(9)

from which we get

Q_{10}=Q^{\dagger}+Q,\qquad Q_{01}=-{\rm i}(Q^{\dagger}-Q).

(10)

On inserting these relations in Eq. (
7

\displaystyle\{Q^{\dagger},Q\}=H,

(11)

\displaystyle(Q^{\dagger})^{2}+Q^{2}=0,

(12)

\displaystyle[Q^{\dagger},Q]=Z.

(13)

Furthermore, Eq. (

[H,Q^{\dagger}]=[H,Q]=[H,Z]=0,\qquad\{Z,Q^{\dagger}\}=\{Z,Q\}=0.

(14)

This reformulation of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}

In the next section, we will proceed to consider the simplest approach of SUSYQM in terms of the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra, leaving another one in terms of a more general GDOA for Sec. 4.

3
Bosonization in Terms of a Calogero-Vasiliev Algebra

Let us first recall that the SUSYQM superalgebra, generated by
H
,
Q^{\dagger}
, and
Q
, such that

\{Q^{\dagger},Q\}=H,\qquad(Q^{\dagger})^{2}=Q^{2}=0,\qquad[H,Q^{\dagger}]=[H,Q]=0,

(15)

can be realized in terms of the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra, defined by
[
23
]

[N,a^{\dagger}]=a^{\dagger},\qquad[N,a]=-a,\qquad[a,a^{\dagger}]=1+\kappa T,

(16)

where
N=N^{\dagger}
,
a=(a^{\dagger})^{\dagger}
,
T=e^{{\rm i}\pi N}=(-1)^{N}
, and
\kappa
is some real deformation parameter. The operator
T
is known as the Klein operator. On assuming the existence of a Fock-type vacuum state
|0\rangle
, such that

N|0\rangle=a|0\rangle=0,\qquad T|0\rangle=|0\rangle,

(17)

the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra admits a Fock space representation, whose carrier space is spanned by the orthonormalized vectors

|n\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{[n]_{\kappa}!}}(a^{\dagger})^{n}|0\rangle,\qquad\langle n^{\prime}|n\rangle=\delta_{n^{\prime},n},

(18)

where
[n]_{\kappa}!=\prod_{i=1}^{n}[i]_{\kappa}
,
[i]_{\kappa}=i+\frac{\kappa}{2}\bigl(1-(-1)^{i}\bigr)
. These vectors are such that
N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle
,
a|n\rangle=\sqrt{[n]_{\kappa}}|n-1\rangle
, and
a^{\dagger}|n\rangle=\sqrt{[n+1]_{\kappa}}|n+1\rangle
. One may write

a^{\dagger}a=N+\kappa P_{1},\qquad aa^{\dagger}=N+1+\kappa P_{0},\qquad N=\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger},a\}-\frac{1}{2}(\kappa+1),

(19)

where
P_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(1+T)
and
P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-T)
are the projectors on even and odd number states, respectively.

The superalgebra (
15
) may be realized in terms of the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra in two different ways
[
18
]
, namely

Q^{\dagger}_{0}=aP_{0},\qquad Q_{0}=a^{\dagger}P_{1},\qquad H_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger},a\}-\frac{1}{2}T[a,a^{\dagger}]=N+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2}T,

(20)

and

Q^{\dagger}_{1}=aP_{1},\qquad Q_{1}=a^{\dagger}P_{0},\qquad H_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\{a^{\dagger},a\}+\frac{1}{2}T[a,a^{\dagger}]=N+\kappa+\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}T.

(21)

The corresponding (linear) spectrum of
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
is given by

{\cal E}^{(0)}_{n}=2\left[\frac{n+1}{2}\right]_{\kappa},\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(22)

and

{\cal E}^{(1)}_{n}=2\left[\frac{n}{2}\right]_{\kappa}+1+\kappa,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(23)

respectively. This means that
{\cal E}^{(0)}_{0}=0
,
{\cal E}^{(0)}_{2k+1}={\cal E}^{(0)}_{2k+2}=2k+2
,
k=0
, 1, 2, …, whereas
{\cal E}^{(1)}_{2k}={\cal E}^{(1)}_{2k+1}=2k+1+\kappa
,
k=0
, 1, 2, …. The former case corresponds to unbroken SUSYQM, while the latter is associated with broken SUSYQM. This has led to the conclusion
[
18
]
that in the framework of Calogero-Vasiliev algebra, SUSYQM is both minimally bosonized and fully reducible.

On comparing Eq. (
15
) with Eqs. (
11
) and (
12
), it is obvious that the sets
Q^{\dagger}_{0}
,
Q_{0}
,
H_{0}
and
Q^{\dagger}_{1}
,
Q_{1}
,
H_{1}
of Eqs. (
20
) and (
21
) also provide a realization of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded superalgebra. It only remains to use Eq. (
13
) to determine the corresponding
Z
operator. The results read

Z_{0}=-TH_{0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad Z_{1}=TH_{1},

(24)

respectively. As a consequence, in both scenarii, the central element distinguishes between the degenerate eigenstates of
H
in the Fock space representation, since its eigenvalues are given by

{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{0}=0,\qquad{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{2k+1}=-{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{2k+2}=2k+2,\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(25)

and

{\cal Z}^{(1)}_{2k}=-{\cal Z}^{(1)}_{2k+1}=2k+1+\kappa,\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(26)

respectively.

4
Bosonization in Terms of a GDOA

A more general realization of the SUSYQM superalgebra (
15
) can be obtained by replacing the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra by a GDOA, defined by
[
21
,
22
]

[N,a^{\dagger}]=a^{\dagger},\qquad[N,a]=-a,\qquad[a,a^{\dagger}]=G(N),

(27)

where
N=N^{\dagger}
,
a=(a^{\dagger})^{\dagger}
, and
G(N)=[G(N)]^{\dagger}
is some Hermitian function of
N
. We restrict ourselves to GDOAs possessing a bosonic Fock space representation. In the latter, we may write
a^{\dagger}a=F(N)
,
aa^{\dagger}=F(N+1)
, where the structure function
F(N)=[F(N)]^{\dagger}
is such that

G(N)=F(N+1)-F(N)

(28)

and is assumed to satisfy the conditions

F(0)=0,\qquad F(n)>0\qquad\text{if $n=1,2,3,\ldots.$}

(29)

The carrier space
{\cal F}
of such a representation can be constructed from a vacuum state
|0\rangle
(such that
a|0\rangle=N|0\rangle=0
) by successive applications of
a^{\dagger}
and its orthonormalized basis states

|n\rangle=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{n}F(i)\right)^{-1/2}(a^{\dagger})^{n}|0\rangle,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots,

(30)

satisfy the relations
N|n\rangle=n|n\rangle
,
a^{\dagger}|n\rangle=\sqrt{F(n+1)}\,|n+1\rangle
, and
a|n\rangle=\sqrt{F(n)}\,|n-1\rangle
.

A
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading structure can be imposed on
\cal F
by introducing a grading operator
T=e^{{\rm i}\pi N}=(-1)^{N}
with the two eigenvalues
(-1)^{\mu}
,
\mu=0
, 1. The two corresponding eigenspaces
{\cal F}_{0}
and
{\cal F}_{1}
, made of even or odd number states, respectively, are such that
{\cal F}={\cal F}_{0}\oplus{\cal F}_{1}
, the corresponding projectors being
P_{0}=\frac{1}{2}(1+T)
and
P_{1}=\frac{1}{2}(1-T)
.

On starting from the general results obtained for order-
p
parasupersymmetric quantum mechanics in
[
24
]
and setting
p=1
, which gives back SUSYQM, we arrive at two different realizations of superalgebra (
15
) in terms of the GDOA (
27
), namely

Q^{\dagger}_{0}=f(N)a^{\dagger}P_{1},\quad Q_{0}=f(N+1)aP_{0},\quad H_{0}=f^{2}(N)F(N)P_{0}+f^{2}(N+1)F(N+1)P_{1},

(31)

and

Q^{\dagger}_{1}=f(N)a^{\dagger}P_{0},\quad Q_{1}=f(N+1)aP_{1},\quad H_{0}=f^{2}(N)F(N)P_{1}+f^{2}(N+1)F(N+1)P_{0}.

(32)

Here
f(N)
is an arbitrary real function of
N
defined on the set
\{1,2,3,\ldots\}
. The eigenvalues of
H_{0}
and
H_{1}
are given by

{\cal E}^{(0)}_{0}=0,\qquad{\cal E}^{(0)}_{2k+1}={\cal E}^{(0)}_{2k+2}=f^{2}(2k+2)F(2k+2),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(33)

and

{\cal E}^{(1)}_{2k}={\cal E}^{(1)}_{2k+1}=f^{2}(2k+1)F(2k+1),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(34)

respectively. and therefore correspond to either unbroken or broken SUSYQM again. This time, however, the spectra are in general nonlinear.

As already observed in Sec. 3, the operators (
31
) and (
32
) also provide realizations of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded superalgebra equations (
11
) and (
12
). The calculation of
Z
, given in Eq. (
13
), now leads to

Z_{0}=-f^{2}(N+1)F(N+1)P_{1}+f^{2}(N)F(N)P_{0}

(35)

and

Z_{1}=-f^{2}(N+1)F(N+1)P_{0}+f^{2}(N)F(N)P_{1}.

(36)

By comparing Eqs. (
35
) and (
36
) with (
31
and (
32
), it is then straightforward to show that

Z_{\mu}=(-1)^{\mu}TH_{\mu},\qquad\mu=0,1.

(37)

Hence, the eigenvalues of
Z_{0}
and
Z_{1}
are given by

{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{0}=0,\qquad{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{2k+1}=-{\cal Z}^{(0)}_{2k+2}=-f^{2}(2k+2)F(2k+2),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(38)

and

{\cal Z}^{(1)}_{2k}=-{\cal Z}^{(1)}_{2k+1}=-f^{2}(2k+1)F(2k+1),\qquad k=0,1,2,\ldots,

(39)

respectively. We conclude that the central element distinguishes between degenerate eigenstates of
H
again.

Whenever the GDOA reduces to the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra, i.e.,
G(N)=1+\kappa T
, Eq. (
19
) leads to
F(N)=N+\kappa P_{1}
and
F(N+1)=N+1+\kappa P_{0}
. On choosing in addition
f(N)=1
, Eqs. (
31
), (
32
), and (
37
) give back Eqs. (
20
), (
21
), and (
24
) with
Q\leftrightarrow Q^{\dagger}
and
Z\leftrightarrow-Z
. Such discrepancies are of course irrelevant.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have shown that the minimal bosonization of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-graded SUSYQM superalgebra in terms of the Calogero-Vasiliev algebra or, more generally, of a GDOA can be extended to the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2}
-graded one in a straightforward way. For the latter, the central element
Z
has the property of distinguishing between degenerate eigenstates of the Hamiltonian.

An interesting open question for future investigation would be the search for a bosonization of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{3}
-graded SUSYQM superalgebra in the framework of GDOAs.

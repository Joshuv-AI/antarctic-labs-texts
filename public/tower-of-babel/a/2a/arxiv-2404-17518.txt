Manin pairs and moment maps revisited
Eckhard Meinrenken, Selim Tawfik

Abstract
. The notion of quasi-Poisson G G -spaces with D / G D/G -valued moment maps was introduced by Alekseev and Kosmann-Schwarzbach in 1999. Our main result is a Lifting Theorem , establishing a bijective correspondence between the categories of quasi-Poisson G G -spaces with D / G D/G -valued moment maps and of quasi-Poisson G × G G\times G -spaces with D D -valued moment maps. Using this result, we give simple constructions of fusion and conjugation for these spaces, and new examples coming from moduli spaces.

Introduction

A Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})

\mathfrak{d}

\operatorname{ad}
-invariant metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle

\mathfrak{g}

The choice of a Lagrangian subspace

In their article

-manifold

-manifold

(1)

\Phi\colon M\to D/G

for such a quasi-Poisson
G
-action, similar to Lu’s
G^{*}
-valued moment map for Poisson Lie group actions
[
29
]

and generalizing the group-valued moment maps of
[
5
]

[

12

expressed

The present work was motivated by Ševera’s construction

by the

(2)

\textstyle{\widehat{M}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\widehat{\Phi}}
\textstyle{D\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}

2.
Moment maps for Manin pairs

We begin by recalling the definition of Alekseev and Kosmann-Schwarzbach of quasi-Poisson spaces, using the reformulation in Dirac geometric terms. See the appendix for background information on
Courant algebroids, Dirac structures, and their morphisms. Note that what we call Dirac morphisms are also referred to as
strong Dirac morphisms
or
morphisms of Manin pairs
.

2.1.
Quasi-Poisson manifolds

A
Manin pair
is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
, equipped with a nondegenerate
\operatorname{ad}
-invariant symmetric bilinear form (from now on referred to as
metric
)
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, together with a Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
which is
Lagrangian
, i.e., maximal isotropic for the metric. Given a Lie group
G
integrating
\mathfrak{g}
, with an action on
\mathfrak{d}
by metric-preserving automorphisms, extending the adjoint action on
\mathfrak{g}
and with infinitesimal action the adjoint action of
\mathfrak{g}
on
\mathfrak{d}
, we speak of a
G
-equivariant Manin pair
.

Definition 2.1
.

A
quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-manifold
for a Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a manifold
M
together with a Dirac morphism

R_{0}\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g}).

Remarks 2.2
.

(a)

In the language of
[
15
,
23
]
,
M
is a Hamiltonian space for the Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
.

(b)

The Dirac-geometric approach to quasi-Poisson manifolds was developed by Bursztyn and Crainic

[
11
,
12
]
.
The equivalence of the original definitions
[
3
]
with Definition
2.1
was proved by
Bursztyn, Iglesias-Ponte, and Ševera
[
15
]
(see also
[
13
]
).

(c)

The Dirac morphism determines a
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
M
. If
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a
G
-equivariant Manin pair,
and
R_{0}
is equivariant for a
G
-action on
M
integrating this
\mathfrak{g}
-action, we say that
M
is a
quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold
.

(d)

The original setting of
[
3
]
involves the choice of a Lagrangian subspace
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
complementary to
\mathfrak{g}
.
The backward image of
\mathfrak{m}
under
R_{0}
is a Lagrangian subbundle complementary to
TM
, which therefore is the graph of a bivector field
\pi
. The properties of
R_{0}
being a Dirac morphism then translate into the conditions

[\pi,\pi]=\chi_{M},\ \ \ \mathcal{L}_{\xi_{M}}\pi=-(F(\xi))_{M},

where
\chi\in\wedge^{3}\mathfrak{g}
and
F\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{*}
are defined in terms of
\mathfrak{m}
, and

\wedge\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(\wedge TM),\ \phi\mapsto\phi_{M}
is the algebra morphism extending the action map.
The triple
(\mathfrak{g},\chi,F)
defines a
quasi-Lie-bialgebra. If
\mathfrak{m}
is a Lagrangian Lie subalgebra, then
\pi
is a Poisson structure, and the definition amounts to a
Lie bialgebra action as in the work of Lu
[
29
]
.

The Courant morphism
R_{0}
may be regarded as a Dirac structure

(3)

E\subseteq\mathfrak{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}M}

over
M
. This was first described as a Lie algebroid by Bursztyn-Crainic
[
11
, Theorem 2.5]
, and recognized as a Dirac structure in
[
12
]
(see also
[
25
, Section 6.2.3]
). Since it is a Lie algebroid, the
range of its anchor map defines a singular foliation of
M
. We shall call this the
quasi-symplectic foliation
.

Remark 2.3
.

After choice of a Lagrangian complement
\mathfrak{m}
, defining a bivector field
\pi
, the quasi-symplectic foliation is spanned by
vector fields

\{\xi_{M}|\,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\}+\{\pi^{\sharp}(\mu)|\,\mu\in\Gamma(T^{*}M)\}.

If
\mathfrak{m}
is a Lagrangian Lie subalgebra, so that
\pi
is Poisson, one also has the symplectic foliation defined by the Poisson structure. The quasi-symplectic foliation need not coincide with the latter.

Definition 2.4
.

A quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-space
M
is called
quasi-symplectic
if the Lie algebroid
E
is transitive.

If
M
is a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-manifold, then every leaf
\iota\colon N\to M
of its quasi-symplectic foliation becomes a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-manifold, by restriction. The corresponding Courant morphism is the composition of
R_{0}
with
\mathbb{T}\iota\colon\mathbb{T}N\dasharrow\mathbb{T}M
.

Example 2.5
.

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
be a Manin pair, and
M
a manifold with a
\mathfrak{d}
-action

\mathsf{a}\colon M\times\mathfrak{d}\to TM

with co-isotropic stabilizers. Equivalently, the range of the dual map
\mathsf{a}^{*}\colon T^{*}M\to M\times\mathfrak{d}
is isotropic.
Then
M
acquires the structure of a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-manifold, with

R_{0}=\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{a}|_{M\times\mathfrak{g}})+\operatorname{gr}(\mathsf{a}^{*})\subseteq\mathfrak{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}M}

given as the sum of graphs of the restricted action map

\mathsf{a}|_{M\times\mathfrak{g}}\colon M\times\mathfrak{g}\to TM
and the dual map
\mathsf{a}^{*}\colon T^{*}M\to M\times\mathfrak{d}
. (Note that the range of
\mathsf{a}^{*}
is isotropic.) Its quasi-symplectic leaves are the
\mathfrak{g}
-orbits. Given a Lagrangian complement
\mathfrak{m}
to
\mathfrak{g}
in
\mathfrak{d}
, and denoting by
p\colon\mathfrak{d}\to\mathfrak{g}
be the projection along
\mathfrak{m}
, the resulting bivector field is given by
\pi^{\sharp}=-\mathsf{a}\circ p\circ\mathsf{a}^{*}
. (See
[
2
, Theorem 2.9]
.)

By the construction of
[
1
]
, a
\mathfrak{d}
-action with coisotropic stabilizers also determines an
action Courant algebroid

\mathbb{A}_{M}=M\times\mathfrak{d}
over
M
, with
E_{M}=M\times\mathfrak{g}
as a Dirac structure. A choice of a complementary Lagrangian subbundle
F_{M}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{M}
(given for example by
M\times\mathfrak{m}
) defines a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TM)
, which is Poisson whenever
F_{M}
is integrable (e.g.,

\mathfrak{m}
a Lie subalgebra). As shown in
[
1
]
, many well-known Poisson structures are obtained from this mechanism.

Example 2.6
.

[
4
]

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra with an invariant metric, and denote by
\overline{\mathfrak{g}}
the same Lie algebra with the opposite metric.
Consider the Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})=(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta})
(where
\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}\cong\mathfrak{g}
is embedded diagonally).
If
G
is a Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{g}
, the
\mathfrak{d}
-action on
G

\mathfrak{d}\to\Gamma(TG),\ \ (\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\mapsto-\xi_{1}^{R}+\xi_{2}^{L}

(where the superscripts indicate the left-invariant and right-invariant vector fields) has Lagrangian stabilizers.
It gives
G
the structure of a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold, with quasi-symplectic leaves the conjugacy classes.

2.2.
Moment maps

The theory of Alekseev and Kosmann-Schwarzbach
[
3
]
considers moment maps valued in the homogeneous space
D/G
, where
(D,G)
is a group pair
integrating the given Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
. In the Dirac-geometric setting, it is natural to allow for more general
targets. See
[
27
, Definition 3.1]
.

Definition 2.7

(Moment map targets)
.

A
moment map target
for a Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a manifold
{\mathcal{Q}}
together
with a transitive action of
\mathfrak{d}
with Lagrangian stabilizers. A
G
-equivariant moment map target
for a
G
-equivariant Manin pair is a moment map target together
with a
G
-action integrating the
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
-action. Given a moment map target
{\mathcal{Q}}
for
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
, we shall denote by
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\operatorname{op}}
the same space regarded as a moment map target for
(\overline{\mathfrak{d}},\mathfrak{g})
.

Moment map targets satisfy
\dim{\mathcal{Q}}=\dim\mathfrak{g}
. We obtain a Dirac structure

(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})=({\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d},\ {\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{g});

for
G
-equivariant moment map targets, this Dirac structure is
G
-equivariant. By dimension count, the Courant algebroid

\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}}
is
exact
.

Definition 2.8
.

For a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-space
M
, with Dirac morphism
R_{0}
, we define a
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment map
to be a Dirac morphism

R\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})

whose composition with
(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})\dasharrow(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
equals
R_{0}
. For a quasi-Poisson
G
-space
M
,
with a
G
-equivariant moment map target, we require
R
to be
G
-equivariant.

When a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-space admits a moment map, then the Dirac morphism
R
is uniquely determined
by
R_{0}
together with
\Phi
. Indeed,
R\subseteq({\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d})\times\overline{\mathbb{T}M}
, as a subbundle along
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)\subseteq{\mathcal{Q}}\times M
, agrees
with
R_{0}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}M}
under the identification
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)\cong M
.

Example 2.9
.

As a special case of Example
2.5
, the moment map target
{\mathcal{Q}}
is itself a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-space,
with quasi-symplectic foliation given by the
\mathfrak{g}
-orbits. The identity map serves as a moment map. Similarly, the
\mathfrak{g}
-orbits
\mathcal{O}\subseteq{\mathcal{Q}}
are quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-spaces, with the
inclusion map as a moment map.

If
\mathfrak{m}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is a Lagrangian complement to
\mathfrak{g}
, then the backward image of
\mathfrak{m}
under
R_{0}
is a Lagrangian subbundle complementary to
TM
, hence of the form
\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{M})
for a bivector field
\pi_{M}
on
M
.
In particular, we obtain such a bivector field on
{\mathcal{Q}}
itself. The latter is given
by (see, e.g.,
[
1
, Section 3.2]
)

(4)

\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}={\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{a}(e_{a})_{\mathcal{Q}}\wedge(f^{a})_{\mathcal{Q}}

where
e_{a}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
f^{a}
the dual basis of
\mathfrak{m}
. Given a
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment map, the Dirac morphism
R
relates the Lagrangian splittings, and hence
\Phi
relates the bivector fields:

\pi_{M}\sim_{\Phi}\pi_{\mathcal{Q}}.

2.3.
Examples

Example 2.10

(Classical moment maps)
.

For any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, one has the Manin pair

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
where

\mathfrak{d}=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rtimes\mathfrak{g}
, with metric defined by the pairing between
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
acts on

\mathfrak{g}^{*}
, with the first factor acting by translation and the second factor by the coadjoint representation. The stabilizers for this action are Lagrangian. This defines the standard moment map target

\mathfrak{g}^{*}
for the classical theory of Kostant and Souriau
[
36
]
.

Example 2.11

(
D/G
-valued moment maps)
.

Suppose
(D,G)
is a Lie group pair integrating the Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
. The
\mathfrak{d}
-action on
D/G

(obtained by differentiating the natural
D
-action) has Lagrangian stabilizers, given at
dG\in D/G
by
\operatorname{Ad}_{d}(\mathfrak{g})
.
These are the moment map targets from the Alekseev-Kosmann-Schwarzbach theory
[
3
]
. For
D=\mathfrak{g}^{*}\rtimes G
, one recovers the classical theory.

Example 2.12

(
G
-valued moment maps)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
carries an invariant metric. By Example
2.6
, the
G\times G
-action on
G
has coisotropic stabilizers; hence
{\mathcal{Q}}=G
is a moment map target for the Manin pair

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})=(\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}_{\Delta}).

This is a special case of Example
2.11
, with the group pair
(D,G)=(G\times G,G_{\Delta})
.
Note that in this case, the anti-diagonal
\mathfrak{m}=\{(\xi,-\xi)|\xi\in\mathfrak{g}\}
is an invariant Lagrangian complement,
leading to a description of the theory in terms of bivector fields and 2-forms. This is the theory of

G
-valued moment maps

[
4
,
5
]
.

Example 2.13
.

Suppose
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a Manin pair,
D
a Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{d}
, and

U\subseteq D
any closed Lagrangian Lie subgroup. Then
{\mathcal{Q}}=D/U
defines a
moment map target for
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
(just as for the
D/G
example). For instance,
if
G
is a semisimple real Lie group, with Iwasawa decomposition

G=KAN
, one may take
D=G\times G
(with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}=\overline{\mathfrak{g}}\oplus\mathfrak{g}
) and

U=A_{\Delta}(N\times N)
. The resulting moment map target is
D/U=K\times K\times A
.

Example 2.14

(
G^{*}
-valued moment maps)
.

According to Drinfeld’s theory
[
19
]
, multiplicative Poisson structures on a Lie group
G
are classified by Manin triples
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
. That is,
\mathfrak{h}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is a Lagrangian Lie subalgebra
complementary to
\mathfrak{g}
. The metric on
\mathfrak{d}
identifies
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. Let
H=G^{*}
be the 1-connected Lie group integrating
\mathfrak{h}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{*}
. The
(right) dressing action
of
\mathfrak{d}
on
{\mathcal{Q}}=G^{*}
has generating vector fields
\zeta_{H},\ \zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
given
in terms of their contraction with the right-invariant Maurer-Cartan form by

(5)

\iota_{\zeta_{H}}\theta^{R}|_{h}=\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{h}\zeta)

where
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{h}}
is projection to
\mathfrak{h}
along
\mathfrak{g}
. See, e.g.,
[
26
, Section 5.1]
.
The stabilizer algebras for this action are given by

\mathfrak{d}_{h}=\operatorname{Ad}_{h^{-1}}\mathfrak{g}
, and in particular are Lagrangian. This defines the moment map target
G^{*}
for
Lu’s moment map theory
[
29
]
. In certain examples,
G,\ G^{*}
are realized as closed subgroups of
D
,
in such a way that the group multiplication
G\times G^{*}\to D
is a global diffeomorphism. In this case, one has

D/G\cong G^{*}
.

Example 2.15

(Dirac Lie groups)
.

Suppose
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a Manin pair such that

\mathfrak{g}
admits a complement
\mathfrak{h}
that is a Lie subalgebra, but not necessarily Lagrangian. Let
H
be a 1-connected integration of

\mathfrak{h}
. The right dressing action
of
\mathfrak{d}

3.
The quasi-symplectic case

The quasi-symplectic
\mathfrak{g}
-spaces with
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment maps correspond to the special case that the
Dirac morphism
R\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}},E_{\mathcal{Q}})
is
exact
. (See Lemma
3.6
below.) This allows for a description of such spaces
in terms of differential forms, once a
splitting
of the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
has been chosen.

3.1.
Splittings

In Appendix
A
, we recall how (isotropic) splittings
j\colon TM\to\mathbb{A}
of exact Courant algebroids give an isomorphism
\mathbb{A}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M
for a closed 3-form
\eta
. The space of splittings is an affine space over the vector space of
2-forms; changing
j
by a 2-form
\varpi
(as in Equation (
41
) below) replaces

\eta
with
\eta^{\prime}=\eta+{\mbox{d}}\varpi
.

For a moment map target, a splitting
j
of the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}

may be regarded as a 1-form

(6)

\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{d})

with the properties

(\alpha(v)|_{q})_{\mathcal{Q}}=v,\ \ \langle\alpha(v),\alpha(v)\rangle=0,

for all
v\in T_{q}{\mathcal{Q}}
. Modifying the splitting by a 2-form
\varpi
replaces
\alpha
with
\alpha^{\varpi}
, given by

(7)

\langle\alpha^{\varpi},\zeta\rangle=\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle-\iota(\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}})\varpi.

In terms of
\alpha
,
the isomorphism

(8)

s\colon{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{T}_{\eta}{\mathcal{Q}}

is explicitly given on constant sections by

s(\zeta)=\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}+\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle,\ \ \zeta\in\mathfrak{d}.

In particular,
\langle s(\zeta_{1}),s(\zeta_{2})\rangle=\langle\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle
and
[\![s(\zeta_{1}),s(\zeta_{2})]\!]=s([\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}])
.
One has the following formula for the 3-form
\eta
, obtained by the same calculation as in the proof of
Bursztyn-Crainic
[
12
, Lemma 3.5]
(for the case
{\mathcal{Q}}=D/G
):

(9)

\eta=-{\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\mbox{d}}\alpha,\alpha\rangle-\frac{1}{6}\langle[\alpha,\alpha],\alpha\rangle.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
acts on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}
by infinitesimal Courant automorphisms, and hence acts on splittings.
The 1-form part of the equation
[\![s(\zeta_{1}),s(\zeta_{2})]\!]=s([\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}])
gives

(10)

\left\langle\mathcal{L}_{(\zeta_{1})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\alpha+[\zeta_{1},\alpha],\zeta_{2}\right\rangle=\iota_{(\zeta_{2})_{\mathcal{Q}}}({\mbox{d}}\langle\alpha,\zeta_{1}\rangle+\iota_{(\zeta_{1})_{\mathcal{Q}}}\eta).

From (
10
), we see that the splitting is invariant under the action of a given element
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
if and only if

(11)

{\mbox{d}}\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle+\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\eta=0.

If
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a
G
-equivariant moment map target,
the diagonal
G
-action on
{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}
is by Courant algebroid automorphisms. Hence,
G
acts on the affine space of splittings; the
pullback of the splitting defined by
\alpha
under an element
g\in G
is the splitting defined by

g^{-1}\cdot\alpha=\operatorname{Ad}_{g^{-1}}\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\alpha
, where
\mathcal{A}_{g}\in\operatorname{Diff}({\mathcal{Q}})
is the action on the base.
We hence obtain a map

(12)

\beta\colon G\to\Omega^{2}({\mathcal{Q}})

where
\beta(g)
is the 2-form (as in (
41
))
relating the two splittings:

\langle\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\alpha,\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\zeta\rangle-\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle=-\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\beta(g).

Note that
\beta
has the cocycle property,
\beta(hg)=\beta(g)+\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\beta(h)
, and that

(13)

{\mbox{d}}\beta(g)=-\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\eta+\eta,

as a special case of (
41
). For later reference, we also note the
formula for the infinitesimal cocycle:

Lemma 3.1
.

For all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(14)

\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\beta(\exp(t\xi))={\mbox{d}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle+\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\eta

Proof.

Taking the derivative of (
12
), we obtain

\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\frac{d}{dt}\big|_{t=0}\beta(\exp(t\xi))=\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\langle\alpha,\zeta\rangle-\langle\alpha,[\xi,\zeta]\rangle.

Using the 1-form part of the identity
[\![s(\xi),s(\zeta)]\!]=s([\xi,\zeta])
,
we see that the right hand side may be written
\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}({\mbox{d}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle+\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\eta)
.
∎

3.2.
The quasi-symplectic case

Recall from Appendix
A
the notion of
exact
(or
full
) Courant morphisms between exact Courant algebroids. Given
splittings, these may be described in terms of 2-forms.

Lemma 3.6
.

For a quasi-Poisson
\mathfrak{g}
-space
M
with
{{\mathcal{Q}}}
-valued moment map
\Phi
, the associated Dirac morphism

R\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})
is exact if and only if
M
is quasi-symplectic.

Proof.

The Dirac morphism
R
is exact if and only if the anchor map restricts to a surjection
R\to\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)
.
In terms of the identification
\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)\cong TM
, the range of the anchor map of
R

equals that of
R_{0}\colon\mathbb{T}M\dasharrow\mathfrak{d}
. By definition,
M
is quasi-symplectic
if and only if the latter map is surjective.
∎

By spelling out the description of exact Dirac morphisms, see Appendix
A
, Equation (
), we obtain:

Proposition 3.7
.

Given a splitting
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{d})
, with corresponding 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}({\mathcal{Q}})
, a quasi-symplectic
\mathfrak{g}
-space
M
with
{{\mathcal{Q}}}
-valued moment map
is described by a
2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
and a
\mathfrak{g}
-equivariant map
\Phi\colon M\to{{\mathcal{Q}}}
satisfying the properties

(i)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\Phi^{*}\eta
,

(ii)

\iota_{\xi_{M}}\omega=-\Phi^{*}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle,\ \ \xi\in\mathfrak{g}
,

(iii)

\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)\cap\operatorname{ker}(\omega)=0
.

Remarks 3.8
.

(a)

Changing the splitting by
\varpi
replaces
\omega
with
\omega+\Phi^{*}\varpi
.

(b)

As a consequence of (i),(ii), the 2-form
\omega
satisfies

\mathcal{L}_{\xi_{M}}\omega=-\Phi^{*}\big({\mbox{d}}\langle\alpha,\xi\rangle+\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\eta\big)

for
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.
The right-hand side vanishes precisely when the splitting is
\mathfrak{g}
-invariant. See Equation (
11
).

(c)

If the
\mathfrak{g}
-action on
M
integrates to a
G
-action, one describes a

quasi-symplectic
G
-space with
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment map
, by the additional requirement that

\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\omega-\omega=\Phi^{*}\beta(g)

with the cocycle (
12
).
This property is automatic if
G
is connected.

Lemma 3.9
.

Condition (iii) may be replaced with

(iii)^{\prime}\ \ \operatorname{ker}(\omega_{m})=\{\xi_{M}(m)|\ \xi\in\mathfrak{g},\ \langle\alpha_{\Phi(m)},\xi\rangle=0\},\ \ m\in M.

Proof.

Let
q=\Phi(m)
, with stabilizer
\mathfrak{d}_{q}
.
The range of
\alpha|_{q}
is a complement to
\mathfrak{d}_{q}
, and hence is non-singularly paired with
\mathfrak{d}_{q}
. It follows that the map

\mathfrak{d}_{q}\to T^{*}_{q}{\mathcal{Q}},\ \zeta\mapsto\langle\alpha|_{q},\zeta\rangle

is an isomorphism.
Using this observation, we see that
Condition (iii)’ implies (iii). Conversely, suppose (iii) holds, and let
v\in\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{m})
.
Let

w=T_{m}\Phi(v),\ \ \xi=\iota_{w}\alpha_{q}.

The moment map condition (ii) shows that for all

\xi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
,

\langle\xi,\xi^{\prime}\rangle=\langle\iota_{w}\alpha_{q},\xi^{\prime}\rangle=\omega(\xi^{\prime}_{M}|_{m},v)=0.

Hence,
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.
We have
\langle\alpha_{q},\xi\rangle=0
, since
\xi\in\operatorname{ran}(\alpha_{q})=\operatorname{ran}(\alpha_{q})^{\perp}
.
Using (ii) again, it follows that

\xi_{M}|_{m}\in\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{m})
.
The difference
v-\xi_{M}|_{m}
lies in
\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{m})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T_{m}\Phi)
, and is hence equal to
0
. This proves (iii)’.
∎

As a direct consequence,
\omega
is nondegenerate at all those points
m\in M
for which the range of

\alpha_{\Phi(m)}
is a complement to
\mathfrak{g}
. Since
\alpha_{\Phi(m)}\colon T_{m}{\mathcal{Q}}\to\mathfrak{d}
defines a splitting of the
\mathfrak{d}
-action, this holds in particular at all points where the stabilizer
\mathfrak{d}_{\Phi(m)}
is equal to
\mathfrak{g}
.

3.3.
Symplectic reduction

Suppose
M
is a quasi-symplectic
G
-space with
moment map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
, with the associated
G
-equivariant exact
Dirac morphism

R\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}}).

(The choice of splitting defines a 2-form
\omega
,
but the following results do not depend on such a choice.)

Proposition 3.10
.

On the pre-image of the set of regular values of
\Phi
, the
G
-action is locally free.

Proof.

Let
m\in M
be such that
T\Phi|_{m}
is surjective, and let
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}_{m}
(the infinitesimal stabilizer of
m
).
Then
0_{m}\sim_{R}\xi
. By surjectivity there exists, for every given
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
, some
v\in TM|_{m}
with

T\Phi|_{m}(v)=\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}|_{\Phi(m)}
. Since
R
is an exact Courant morphism, the pair

(\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}|_{\Phi(m)},v)\in\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)

lifts to an element

(\zeta^{\prime},v+\mu)\in R

where
\mu\in T^{*}M|_{m}
and with
\zeta-\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{d}_{\Phi(m)}
. Since
R
is Lagrangian, the pairing of this element with
(\xi,0)\in R
is zero. This gives

\langle\zeta^{\prime},\xi\rangle=0
. But also
\langle\zeta-\zeta^{\prime},\xi\rangle=0
, since
\zeta-\zeta^{\prime},\xi
are both in the Lagrangian subspace
\mathfrak{d}_{\Phi(m)}
.
We conclude
\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle=0
for all
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
, and hence
\xi=0
.
∎

Let
\lambda\in{\mathcal{Q}}
be a regular value of
\Phi
, and
G_{\lambda}
its stabilizer.
By Proposition
3.10
, the
G_{\lambda}
-action on
\Phi^{-1}(\lambda)
is locally free. If the action is free and proper, the quotient is a manifold.

Proposition 3.11

(Symplectic reduction)
.

Suppose
\lambda
is a regular value of
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
, and that the

G_{\lambda}
-action on
\Phi^{-1}(\lambda)
is free and proper. Then

M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\lambda}\,G=\Phi^{-1}(\lambda)/G_{\lambda}

acquires a symplectic 2-form.

Proof.

Let
Z=\Phi^{-1}(\lambda)
.
Denote by

i_{Z}\colon Z\to M
and
i_{\lambda}\colon\{\lambda\}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
the inclusions.
The pullback Courant algebroids are

i_{Z}^{!}\mathbb{T}M=\mathbb{T}Z
and
i_{\lambda}^{!}\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}=0
, and
R
defines a
G_{\lambda}
-equivariant exact Courant morphism

R_{Z}\colon\mathbb{T}Z\dasharrow 0.

Explicitly, for
v+\nu\in\mathbb{T}Z
we have

(18)

v+\nu\sim_{R_{Z}}0\Leftrightarrow\exists\mu\in T^{*}M|_{Z},\ \zeta\in\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}\colon\nu=i_{Z}^{*}\mu,\ v+\mu\sim_{R}\zeta.

We claim that the intersection
\operatorname{ker}(R_{Z})\cap TZ
consists exactly of the
G_{\lambda}
-orbit directions. By (
18
), a tangent vector
v\in TZ
satisfies
v\sim_{R_{Z}}0
if and only if there exists
\xi\in\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}

with
v\sim_{R}\xi
. Since
R
is a Dirac morphism, the forward image of
TM|_{m}
under
R
equals
\mathfrak{g}
; hence

\xi\in\mathfrak{d}_{\lambda}\cap\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{\lambda}
, and by the uniqueness condition for Dirac morphisms we have

v=\xi_{M}|_{m}
. This proves the claim.
Since
\mathbb{T}Z
has a distinguished splitting, the Courant morphism

R_{Z}
is equivalent to a
G_{\lambda}
-invariant, closed 2-form
\omega_{Z}\in\Omega^{2}(Z)
, with kernel
\operatorname{ker}(\omega_{Z})=\operatorname{ker}(R_{Z})\cap TZ
. It descends to a symplectic 2-form on
Z/G_{\lambda}=M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\lambda}\,G
.
∎

Remark 3.12
.

The assumption that

\lambda
is a regular value of the moment map is equivalent to
\Phi
being transverse to the orbit
\mathcal{O}=G.\lambda
. The reduced space

M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\lambda}\,G
is canonically identified with

M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\mathcal{O}}\,G=\Phi^{-1}(\mathcal{O})/G.

Remark 3.13
.

The proposition (and its proof) generalizes, as follows: Suppose
(\mathbb{B},F)
is a Dirac structure over another manifold
N
, and

R\colon(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})\times(\mathbb{B},F)

is a
G
-equivariant Dirac morphism with base map
(\Phi,\Psi)\colon M\to{\mathcal{Q}}\times N
(where the
G
-action on
\mathbb{B}\to N

is trivial). By the same argument as above, the action on the pre-image of the set of regular values of
\Phi
is locally free. If
\lambda
is a regular value of
\Phi
, and the action of
G_{\lambda}
on
\Phi^{-1}(\lambda)
is free and proper,
then
R
descends to a Dirac morphism

R/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\lambda}\,G\colon(\mathbb{T}(M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\lambda}G),\ T(M/\kern-3.01385pt/_{\!\lambda}\,G))\dasharrow(\mathbb{B},F).

For instance, given two Manin pairs
(\mathfrak{d}_{i},\mathfrak{g}_{i})
with moment map targets
{\mathcal{Q}}_{i}
, and
given a quasi-symplectic

-space with

-space.

4.
Integration of moment map targets

4.1.
Quasi-symplectic groupoids

The notion of a quasi-symplectic groupoid integrating an exact Dirac structure was introduced in
[
14
]
and

[
38
]
, independently, using splittings of the Courant algebroid. (Reference
[
14
]
uses the terminology of
pre-symplectic groupoid
.)
The following formulation in terms of Dirac morphisms may be found in
[
15
]
; see also

[
25
, Remark 6.2.1]
and
[
22
]
.

Let
(\mathbb{A},E)
be a Dirac structure (not necessarily exact) over a manifold
M
.
A
quasi-Poisson groupoid
integrating this Dirac structure is a Lie groupoid
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
integrating the Lie algebroid
E\Rightarrow M
, together with a multiplicative Dirac morphism

(19)

K\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G},T\mathcal{G})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A},E)\times(\mathbb{A},E)^{\operatorname{op}},

with base map
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})\colon\mathcal{G}\to M\times M
. Here, multiplicativity means that

K\subseteq\mathbb{A}\times\overline{\mathbb{A}}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}}
is a subgroupoid along the graph of the base map
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
, for the standard groupoid structure
\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows TM\oplus\operatorname{Lie}(\mathcal{G})^{*}
and
the pair groupoid structure
\mathbb{A}\times\overline{\mathbb{A}}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{A}
. We speak of a
quasi-symplectic groupoid
if
\mathbb{A}
and the
morphism
K
are exact.

Example 4.1
.

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
be a
G
-equivariant Manin pair, regarded as a Dirac structure over
M=\operatorname{pt}
.
A quasi-Poisson groupoid integrating this Dirac structure is given by the morphism
K\colon\mathbb{T}G\dasharrow(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})\times(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})^{\operatorname{op}}
,
where

K\subseteq\mathfrak{d}\times\overline{\mathfrak{d}}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}G}

is spanned by all
(\xi,\xi^{\prime},(\xi^{\prime})^{L}-\xi^{R})
with
\xi,\xi^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{g}
, together with all
(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}\zeta,\zeta,-\theta^{L}_{G}\cdot\zeta)
with
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
.

We are mainly interested in the special case that the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}
and the morphism
K
are
exact
.
Given a splitting of
\mathbb{A}
, with associated 3-form
\eta
, such a morphism is described by a

multiplicative
2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(\mathcal{G})
with
{\mbox{d}}\omega=\mathsf{s}^{*}\eta-\mathsf{t}^{*}\eta
,
satisfying the minimal degeneracy condition

(20)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{t})\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\mathsf{s})=0

and a moment map property. The theory in
[
14
]
shows that whenever the Lie algebroid
E
admits a source-simply connected integration
\mathcal{G}
, then the latter has a canonical structure as quasi-symplectic groupoid.

4.2.
Integration of moment map targets
{\mathcal{Q}}

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be a moment map target for the Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
.
Given a splitting
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{d})
, let

(21)

\chi\in C^{\infty}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{d}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{d}^{*}),\ \ \chi(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})=\langle\iota_{(\zeta_{1})_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\alpha,\zeta_{2}\rangle.

This satisfies

\chi(\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})+\chi(\zeta_{2},\zeta_{1})=\langle\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2}\rangle;

in particular, the restriction to a bilinear form on
\mathfrak{g}
is skew-symmetric.
Let

\beta
be the cocycle (
12
) regarded as a 2-form of bidegree
(0,2)
on
G\times{\mathcal{Q}}
.
We denote by

\mathcal{A}_{g}\in\operatorname{Diff}({\mathcal{Q}})
the action of
g\in G
; pullbacks of differential forms under
\mathcal{A}_{g}
may be regarded as families of
forms on
{\mathcal{Q}}
(depending on

g\in G
as a parameter), hence as forms on
G\times\mathcal{Q}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}
be a
G
-equivariant moment map target for the Manin pair
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
. Then the action groupoid

the quasi-symplectic 2-form on

is given by

\omega=\mathcal{A}_{g}^{*}\left(-\langle\alpha,\theta^{R}\rangle+{\frac{1}{2}}\chi(\theta^{R},\theta^{R})\right)+\beta.

For the case that

is 1-connected and the splitting is

-equivariant, this is proved in

. The subsequent
[

explains how to generalize the

5.
Quotients and lifts of quasi-Poisson spaces

5.1.
Quotients of moment map targets

Let
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
be a Manin pair. Suppose

\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is a Lie subalgebra containing
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the orthogonal space

\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{c}^{\perp}
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{c}
, hence also in
\mathfrak{g}
, and
we obtain a reduced Manin pair

(\mathfrak{d}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=(\mathfrak{c}/\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{k}).

For reduction of
G
-equivariant
Manin pairs
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
, we assume that
\mathfrak{k}
integrates to a closed normal
subgroup
K\subseteq G
. (Normality is automatic if
K
is connected.) Then

\mathfrak{k}
and
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
are

\operatorname{Ad}_{G}
-invariant, and hence
(\mathfrak{d}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
is a
G^{\prime}=G/K
-equivariant Manin pair.
Suppose
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a
G
-equivariant moment map target for
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
,
and that the action of
K\subseteq G
on
{\mathcal{Q}}
is free and proper.
Then the
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
-action on
{\mathcal{Q}}
descends to a
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
-action on
the quotient

{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{Q}}/K.

Lemma 5.1
.

The
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
-action on
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is transitive, with Lagrangian stabilizers.

Proof.

By definition, the
\mathfrak{d}
-action on
{\mathcal{Q}}
is transitive, with Lagrangian stabilizers. Since the
K
-action is free,
we have
\mathfrak{d}_{q}\cap\mathfrak{k}=0
for all
q\in{\mathcal{Q}}
. Taking orthogonals, this shows that
\mathfrak{d}_{q}+\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{d}
, so that already the
action of
\mathfrak{c}
is transitive. It follows that the induced
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
-action on
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is transitive.
The stabilizer
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}_{qK}
contains the Lagrangian subspace

(\mathfrak{d}_{q}\cap\mathfrak{c})/(\mathfrak{d}_{q}\cap\mathfrak{k})
, and in fact has to be equal for dimension reasons.
∎

In conclusion,
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
becomes a
G^{\prime}=G/K
-equivariant moment map target
for
(\mathfrak{d}^{\prime},\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})
.
As before, we denote

(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}})=({\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d},{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{g})

and similarly for
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The Dirac structure
(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}},E_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}})
is the reduction of
(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}},E_{\mathcal{Q}})

under the action of
K
.

Proof.

The
K
-action on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
has isotropic generators

\varrho\colon\mathfrak{k}\to\Gamma(\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}})

given by the constant sections,
\xi\mapsto\xi
. Thus,

\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)^{\perp}={\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{c}
, and the reduced Courant algebroid is

\frac{\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)^{\perp}}{\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)}\Big/K=\frac{{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{c}}{{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{k}}\Big/K={\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}=\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}.

The constant sections of
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}
defined by elements

\zeta^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
are the images of the constant sections
of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
defined by any choice of lift
\zeta
; hence the Courant bracket of constant sections is simply the given Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
. We have
\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)\subseteq E_{\mathcal{Q}}
, hence
the image of
E_{\mathcal{Q}}\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)^{\perp}
under the reduction is
E_{{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}}
.
∎

The choice of a principal connection gives a method for descending splittings.

Proposition 5.3
.

Suppose
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{d})
is a
K
-invariant splitting of
{\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}
, and
\theta\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}},\mathfrak{k})
a principal connection. Let
\alpha^{\varpi}
be the twist of
\alpha
by the 2-form

\varpi=-\langle\alpha,\theta\rangle+{\frac{1}{2}}\chi(\theta,\theta)

where
\chi
is defined in (
21
).
Then
\alpha^{\varpi}
takes values in
\mathfrak{c}
, and its projection
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\varpi}
descends to a splitting
\alpha^{\prime}\in\Omega^{1}({\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime},\mathfrak{d}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Contraction of
\varpi
with
\zeta_{\mathcal{Q}}
for

\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
gives

\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\varpi=\langle\alpha,\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta\rangle-\chi(\zeta-\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta,\theta).

By definition of the twisted splitting (see (
7
)), this gives

\langle\alpha^{\varpi},\zeta\rangle=\langle\alpha,\zeta-\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta\rangle+\chi(\zeta-\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta,\theta)

Note that this expression vanishes if
\zeta\in\mathfrak{k}
; hence
\alpha^{\varpi}
takes values in
\mathfrak{c}=\mathfrak{k}^{\perp}
as required.
Since
\alpha^{\varpi}
is
K
-equivariant, the
\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}
-values 1-form
\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\varpi}
is
K
-invariant. To show that it descends, we must show that it is
K
-basic. Thus let
\xi\in\mathfrak{k}
.
For
\zeta\in\mathfrak{c}
, we see

\displaystyle\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\langle\alpha^{\varpi},\zeta\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\alpha,\zeta\rangle-\langle\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\alpha,\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta\rangle+\chi(\zeta,\xi)-\chi(\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle=\chi(\xi,\zeta)+\chi(\zeta,\xi)-\chi(\xi,\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta)-\chi(\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta,\xi)

\displaystyle=\langle\zeta,\xi\rangle-\langle\iota_{\zeta_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\theta,\xi\rangle=0.

It follows that
\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\alpha^{\varpi}
takes values in
\mathfrak{c}^{\perp}=\mathfrak{k}
, and so

\iota_{\xi_{{\mathcal{Q}}}}\operatorname{pr}_{\mathfrak{d}^{\prime}}\alpha^{\varpi}=0
.
∎

5.2.
Lifting theorem

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{Q}}/K
as above. If
M
is a manifold with a
G
-equivariant map
\Phi\colon M\to{\mathcal{Q}}
, then the action of
K
on
M
is again free and proper (by equivariance of
\Phi
), and hence
M^{\prime}=M/K
with
G^{\prime}
-equivariant map
\Phi^{\prime}\colon M^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
is defined. Conversely, given
a
G^{\prime}
-equivariant map
\Phi^{\prime}\colon M^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
, we recover
M
by pulling back the principal
K
-bundle

{\mathcal{Q}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
. It is possible to incorporate Dirac geometry in this construction:

Theorem 5.5

(Lifting theorem)
.

Let
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}={\mathcal{Q}}/K
and
G^{\prime}=G/K
as above.
There is a 1-1 correspondence between

•

quasi-Poisson
G
-manifolds
M
with
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment maps,

•

quasi-Poisson
G^{\prime}
-manifolds

with
{\mathcal{Q}}^{\prime}
-valued moment maps.

Furthermore,
M^{\prime}=M/K
is quasi-symplectic if and only if
M
is quasi-symplectic.

Proof.

The theorem is a straightforward application of a general result on reductions of Dirac morphisms, see
[
16
]
as well as
Proposition
B.4
in the appendix. By definition, the structure of a quasi-Poisson
G
-manifold

with

{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment map

is described by a

This Dirac morphism intertwines the generators for the

-action, given for

by the generating vector fields
for the action on

and for
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
by the constant sections of
\mathbb{A}_{\mathcal{Q}}
:

(\xi_{M},0)\sim_{{R}}\varrho(\xi).

In particular,

intertwines the generators for the

-action. Since
\mathbb{T}M^{\prime}
is the reduction by

of the Courant algebroid

, Proposition

gives a 1-1 correspondence between

-equivariant Dirac morphisms

-equivariant Dirac morphism

Moreover,
R
is exact if and only if
R^{\prime}
is exact.
∎

{R}_{1}\circledast{R}_{2}\colon\big(\mathbb{T}(M_{1}\circledast M_{2}),\ T(M_{1}\circledast M_{2})\big)\dasharrow\left(\mathbb{A}_{D},\ L_{D}\right).\qed

It is clear from the construction that the fusion product is associative, in the sense that

(M_{1}\circledast M_{2})\circledast M_{3}\cong M_{1}\circledast(M_{2}\circledast M_{3})

canonically. On the other hand, there is no
G\times G
-equivariant isomorphism between
M_{1}\circledast M_{2}
and
M_{2}\circledast M_{1}
, in general. (See
[
37
, Section 4.2.2]
for a concrete counter-example.)

Remark 6.2
.

By combining Theorem
6.1
with the Lifting Theorem
5.5
, one obtains a fusion operation for
quasi-Poisson
G

D/G
-valued moment maps
\Phi_{i}\colon M_{i}\to D/G

with

\widehat{M}_{1}\circledast\widehat{M}_{2}

is the fiber product of

with

over

The fusion operation generalizes in various directions, with straightforward generalizations of the proof:

•

Internal fusion
: If

quasi-Poisson
(G\times G)\times(G\times G)
-manifold with

•

Given another

-equivariant moment map target

for a Manin pair

one has internal fusion of quasi-Poisson

-valued moment map, producing a space with

-valued moment map.

•

Given Lagrangian subalgebras
\mathfrak{g}_{1},\mathfrak{g}_{2}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
with corresponding groups

-spaces with

-valued moment map, by using the

-spaces and quasi-Poisson
G_{2}\times G_{3}

-valued moment maps.

•

Suppose
\mathfrak{c}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is a coisotropic subalgebra, and suppose that
\mathfrak{k}=\mathfrak{c}^{\perp}

generalizes to give a

6.2.
Conjugation

The conjugate
M^{*}
of a Poisson
G
-space
(M,\pi)
with moment map
\Phi\colon M\to\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is obtained by changing the sign of
\pi
and of the moment map. For Poisson Lie group actions with
G^{*}
-valued moment maps, conjugation involves replacing
\Phi
with
\operatorname{Inv}_{G^{*}}\circ\Phi
; similarly for
G
-valued moment maps.

A conjugation for quasi-Poisson spaces with
D/G
-valued moment maps was introduced by Bursztyn-Crainic
[
12
, Proposition 4.3]
and Ševera
[
35
, Section 6]
, independently.

Using the Lifting Theorem, it may be understood in terms of a natural conjugation for quasi-Poisson spaces with
G\times G
-valued moment map
\Phi\colon M\to D
. Denote by
{M}^{*}
the same
space with the new action

(g_{1},g_{2})\cdot_{\rm new}{m}=(g_{2},g_{1})\cdot{m}

obtained by switching the two factors in
G\times G
. Then
{\Phi}^{*}=\operatorname{inv}_{D}\circ\ {\Phi}\colon{M}^{*}\to D

is equivariant for the new
G\times G
-action.

Theorem 6.3
.

The space
{M}^{*}
is naturally a quasi-Poisson
G\times G
-space with
D
-valued moment map
{\Phi}^{*}
.

Proof.

Let
{R}\colon\mathbb{T}{M}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{D}^{\operatorname{op}}
denote the Dirac morphism for the space
{M}
.
It remains a Dirac morphism, if we change the sign of the metric for both Courant algebroids.
We define
{R}^{*}
as the following composition,

\mathbb{T}{M}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\overline{\mathbb{T}{M}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle{R}}}{{\dasharrow}}D\times\overline{\mathfrak{d}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{d}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{A}_{D}^{\operatorname{op}}.

Here the first isomorphism is the involution
x=v+\mu\mapsto v-\mu
of
\mathbb{T}{M}
; this preserves the Courant
bracket, anchor, and the Dirac structure
TM
, but changes the sign of the metric.
The last isomorphism is the involution of
\mathbb{A}_{D}^{\operatorname{op}}

given by groupoid inversion
(d,\zeta^{\prime},\zeta)\mapsto(d^{-1},\zeta,\zeta^{\prime})
; this
preserves the Courant bracket and anchor, as well as the Dirac structure
L_{D}
, but once again changes the
sign of the metric. Thus,
{R}^{*}
is a Courant morphism, with base map
{\Phi}^{*}
, and equivariant for the

G\times G
-action on
{M}^{*}
.
∎

It is clear from the construction that
({M}^{*})^{*}\cong{M}
; furthermore,

({M}_{1}\circledast{M}_{2})^{*}\cong{M}_{2}^{*}\circledast{M}_{1}^{*}.

Remark 6.4
.

The quasi-Poisson
G
-spaces
M/(\{e\}\times G)
and
M^{*}/(\{e\}\times G)
are usually non-isomorphic as
G
-manifolds. See
[
37
, Section 4.2.2]
for a concrete example.

6.3.
The quasi-symplectic case

In Section
3
we discussed the differential form approach to quasi-symplectic
G
-spaces with
{\mathcal{Q}}
-valued moment maps, for a given choice of splitting
\alpha
of
\mathbb{A}_{{\mathcal{Q}}}={\mathcal{Q}}\times\mathfrak{d}
. For quasi-symplectic
G\times G
-spaces with
D
-valued moment maps, there is a canonical
D\times D
-equivariant splitting given by

\alpha={\frac{1}{2}}(-\theta^{R},\theta^{L})
, where
\theta^{L},\,\theta^{R}\in\Omega^{1}(D,\mathfrak{d})
are the Maurer-Cartan forms on
D
, and with the 3-form

\eta=\frac{1}{12}\langle\theta^{L},[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]\rangle
.
The resulting isomorphism
\mathbb{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{T}_{\eta}D
reads as
[
2
]

(d,\zeta_{1},\zeta_{2})\mapsto\left(\zeta_{2}^{L}-\zeta_{1}^{R}+{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\langle\theta^{L},\,\zeta_{2}\rangle+\langle\theta^{R},\,\zeta_{1}\rangle\right)\right)\Big|_{d}.

Using this identification, one may describe the conditions for a Hamiltonian quasi-symplectic
G
-spaces
with
D/G
-valued moment map in terms of a
G\times G
-invariant 2-form
{\omega}\in\Omega^{2}({M})
satisfying the following axioms:

(i)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\frac{1}{12}{\Phi}^{*}\langle\theta^{L},[\theta^{L},\theta^{L}]\rangle
,

(ii)

\iota((\xi_{1},\xi_{2})_{{M}}){\omega}=-{\frac{1}{2}}{\Phi}^{*}(\langle\theta^{L},\xi_{2}\rangle+\langle\theta^{R},\xi_{1}\rangle),\ \ \ \xi_{1},\xi_{2}\in\mathfrak{g}
.

(iii)

\operatorname{ker}(T{\Phi})\cap\operatorname{ker}({\omega})=\{0\}
.

The quasi-symplectic form on the conjugate space
M^{*}
is simply the opposite form
-\omega
.
Given two such spaces
(M_{i},\omega_{i},\Phi_{i})
, the quasi-symplectic 2-form
{\omega}
on

{M}_{1}\circledast{M}_{2}
is given by

(29)

\pi^{*}{\omega}={\omega}_{1}+{\omega}_{2}-{\frac{1}{2}}\langle{\Phi}_{1}^{*}\theta^{L},\,{\Phi}_{2}^{*}\theta^{R}\rangle

where
\pi\colon{M}_{1}\times{M}_{2}\to{M}_{1}\circledast{M}_{2}
is the quotient map for the
G_{\Delta}
-action. This follows from
the description of the ‘multiplication morphism’
T

for the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}_{D}
in terms of the splitting, see
[
2
]
.
One may verify directly that the right-hand side of (
29
) is
G_{\Delta}
-basic, and that the resulting 2-form

{\omega}
satisfies the axioms (i),(ii).
A direct proof of the minimal degeneracy condition (iii) seems less straightforward; here the Dirac geometric approach is more convenient.

7.
Examples from moduli spaces

In this section, we show that many examples of quasi-symplectic
G
-spaces with
D/G
-valued moment maps arise as moduli spaces of flat bundles over surfaces. In most cases, these are described in terms of their lifts to spaces with

D
-valued moment maps. These lifted spaces may be seen as special instances of the very general constructions of Li-Bland and Ševera
[
27
,
34
]
. See also the recent paper by Álvarez
[
8
]
for related constructions.

7.1.
Ševera formalism

As before, we denote by
\theta^{L},\theta^{R}
the Maurer-Cartan forms, and by
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(D)
the Cartan 3-form. The 2-form

\beta={\frac{1}{2}}\langle d_{1}^{*}\theta^{L},d_{2}^{*}\theta^{R}\rangle\in\Omega^{2}(D\times D)

satisfies
{\mbox{d}}\beta=d_{1}^{*}\eta+d_{2}^{*}\eta-(d_{1}d_{2})^{*}\eta
under
group multiplication. Here elements of
D^{n}
are denoted
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
, and
d_{i}
is also used to denote projection to the
i
-th factor; thus
d_{1}\cdots d_{i}\colon D^{n}\to D
is the product map. It will be convenient to introduce
d_{0}=(d_{1}d_{2})^{-1}
; hence the exterior differential of
\beta
may be written

{\mbox{d}}\beta=\sum_{i=0}^{2}d_{i}^{*}\eta.

The contractions with the vector fields on
D^{2}
, for
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}
,

\zeta_{(0)}=(-\zeta^{R},0),\ \zeta_{(1)}=(\zeta^{L},-\zeta^{R}),\ \zeta_{(2)}=(0,\zeta^{L})

are given by

\iota(\zeta_{(i)})\beta={\frac{1}{2}}\langle d_{i}^{*}\theta^{L}+d_{i+1}^{*}\theta^{R},\zeta\rangle,\ \ \ i=0,1,2,

with the convention
d_{3}=d_{0}
. Note that these formulas become more symmetric if
D^{2}
is regarded as the
submanifold of
D^{3}
given by
d_{0}d_{1}d_{2}
. In particular,
\beta
is unchanged under cyclic permutations of indices:
\beta={\frac{1}{2}}\langle d_{0}^{*}\theta^{L},d_{1}^{*}\theta^{R}\rangle={\frac{1}{2}}\langle d_{2}^{*}\theta^{L},d_{0}^{*}\theta^{R}\rangle
.

We will use the following construction due to Ševera

[
34
]
. For any manifold
M
, define a
product on
C^{\infty}(M,D)\times\Omega^{2}(M)
by

(\Phi_{1},\omega_{1})\bullet(\Phi_{2},\omega_{2})=\left(\Phi_{1}\Phi_{2},\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}-(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2})^{*}\beta\right).

One checks that
\bullet
is a group structure, with inverse
(\Phi,\omega)^{-1}=(\Phi^{-1},-\omega)
.
Furthermore,
\{e\}\times\Omega^{2}(M)
is a central subgroup, and

C^{\infty}(M,D)\times\Omega^{2}(M)\to\Omega^{3}(M),\ (\Phi,\omega)\mapsto{\mbox{d}}\omega-\Phi^{*}\eta
is a group homomorphism for this group structure.

7.2.
Representation spaces for surfaces with boundary

Let
\Sigma
be a compact, connected, oriented surface with non-empty boundary, with a finite set of marked points

\mathcal{\mathcal{V}}\subseteq\partial\Sigma
, referred to as
vertices
. We require at least one vertex on each boundary component.
The vertices subdivide each boundary component into a finite union of oriented edges; let
\mathcal{E}
be the set of such edges.

Denote by
\Pi(\Sigma)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{V}
the fundamental groupoid, consisting of homotopy classes of paths
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\Sigma
with endpoints in
\mathcal{V}
. The source and target maps are

\mathsf{s}([\gamma])=\gamma(0),\ \mathsf{t}([\gamma])=\gamma(1)

and the groupoid multiplication is given by concatenation of paths.
Similarly, let
\Pi(\partial\Sigma)\rightrightarrows\mathcal{V}
be the fundamental groupoid of the boundary.
The inclusion of the boundary defines a groupoid morphism

\Pi(\partial\Sigma)\to\Pi(\Sigma)
. Given a Lie group

D
, let

M_{D}(\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\Sigma),D)

be the set of groupoid homomorphisms
\kappa\colon\Pi(\Sigma)\to D,\ [\gamma]\mapsto\kappa_{\gamma}
. This space is a smooth manifold: a choice of
generators of
\Pi(\Sigma)
gives a diffeomorphism to a product of several copies of
D
.
Similarly, we may define a space
M_{D}(\partial\Sigma)=\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\partial\Sigma),D)
. Taking
\mathcal{E}\subseteq\Pi(\partial\Sigma)
as generators,
this space is identified with
D^{\mathcal{E}}=\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{E},D)
.
Restriction
\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\Sigma),D)\to\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\partial\Sigma),D)
defines a smooth map

(30)

\Phi\colon M_{D}(\Sigma)\to M_{D}(\partial\Sigma)=D^{\mathcal{E}}.

This map is equivariant under the action of

D^{\mathcal{V}}=\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{V},D)
, where
d\colon\mathcal{V}\to D,\ {\mathsf{v}}\mapsto d_{\mathsf{v}}
acts as
on
\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\Sigma),D)
as

(31)

(d\cdot\kappa)_{\gamma}=d_{\mathsf{t}(\gamma)}\ \kappa_{\gamma}\ d_{\mathsf{s}(\gamma)}^{-1},

and similarly on
\operatorname{Hom}(\Pi(\partial\Sigma),D)
.
Let us now assume that the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
of
D
comes equipped with an invariant metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Denote by
\theta^{L},\ \theta^{R}
the Maurer-Cartan forms and by
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(D)
the Cartan 3-form.
Let
\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}=\operatorname{Map}(\mathcal{V},\mathfrak{d})
denote the Lie algebra of
D^{\mathcal{V}}
; for
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}
denote by

\zeta_{\mathsf{v}}\in\mathfrak{d}
its components.

Theorem 7.1
.

The space
M_{D}(\Sigma)
is a quasi-symplectic
D^{\mathcal{V}}
-manifold with
D^{\mathcal{E}}
-valued moment map. That is,
it carries a
D^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant 2-form
\omega
with the following properties:

(a)

{\mbox{d}}\omega=-\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\eta
.

(b)

For
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}
,

\iota(\zeta_{M_{D}(\Sigma)})\omega=-{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}}\left(\langle\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\theta^{L},\ \zeta_{\mathsf{s}(e)}\rangle+\langle\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}^{*}\theta^{R},\,\zeta_{\mathsf{t}(e)}\rangle\right).

(c)

\operatorname{ker}(\omega)\cap\operatorname{ker}(T\Phi)=\{0\}
.

In terms of a formulation with bivector fields, the moduli spaces
M_{D}(\Sigma)
are studied in the work of
Li-Bland and Ševera
[
27
]
. One way of constructing the 2-form starts from the Atiyah-Bott symplectic 2-form
\omega_{AB}
on a moduli space of flat
D
-bundles over
\Sigma
, with trivializations at the vertices, and up to gauge transformations that are trivial along the edges.
The method of
[
5
,
16
]
shows how to descend
\omega_{AB}
to a 2-form (no longer closed) on the space of holonomies. In particular
[
16
]
explains the nondegeneracy condition (
c
) in terms of reduction of Dirac morphisms.

Consider first the case that

\Sigma
is a
disk
. Choose an initial vertex
v\in\mathcal{V}
, and label the edges clockwise as
{\mathsf{e}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathsf{e}}_{r}
(so,
\mathsf{s}({\mathsf{e}}_{i+1})=\mathsf{t}({\mathsf{e}}_{i})
).

Then
M_{D}(\Sigma)\subseteq D^{r}
is the submanifold given by
\prod d_{i}=e
,
with moment map
\Phi=(\Phi_{1},\ldots,\Phi_{r})
the inclusion. Using Ševera’s formalism,
the 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M_{D}(\Sigma))
is given by

(32)

(e,\omega)=(\Phi_{1},0)\bullet\cdots\bullet(\Phi_{r},0).

The case of a general surface
\Sigma
can be obtained from that of a disk by making boundary identifications.
To this end, cut the surface along paths, with end points in
\mathcal{V}
, until it becomes a disk (equivalently, polyhedron)

\Sigma^{c}
. The quotient map
\Sigma^{c}\to\Sigma

restricts to a surjective map
\mathcal{V}\to\mathcal{V}^{c}
of vertices, and defines a surjective groupoid morphism
\Pi(\Sigma^{c})\to\Pi(\Sigma)
. This defines an embedding

(33)

j\colon M_{D}(\Sigma)\to M_{D}(\Sigma^{c}).

The 2-form
\omega\in M_{D}(\Sigma)
is simply the pullback of the 2-form on
M_{D}(\Sigma^{c})
under this embedding.
In other words, it is given by the same formula (
32
), by letting

\Phi_{i}=\Phi_{j}^{-1}
for any two paired edges
\mathsf{e}_{i},\mathsf{e}_{j}
– the moment map components
\Phi_{\mathsf{e}}
are the components
\Phi_{i}
for non-paired edges. The 2-form
\omega
does not depend on the choice of cutting used in the construction of
\Sigma^{c}
.
One possible proof of this fact uses (
32
) to check that ‘elementary moves’ (cutting the polygon along diagonals separating paired edges
\mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{2}
, and re-gluing along these paired edges) do not change the 2-form.

Alternatively, the 2-form may be described using a Dirac-geometric approach – see Appendix
D
. In particular, the non-degeneracy property (
c
) is automatic from that approach. In a nutshell, when
\Sigma
is a disk, one may regard
M_{D}(\Sigma)
as an orbit
for a Dirac structure
E^{(r)}
inside
(D\times(\overline{\mathfrak{d}}\oplus\overline{\mathfrak{d}}))^{r}
.
In the general case one obtains
M_{D}(\Sigma)
as a Dirac-geometric cross section.

7.3.
Surfaces with colored boundary

Suppose now that
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
is a Lagrangian Lie subalgebra of
\mathfrak{d}
, integrating to a closed Lie subgroup
G\subseteq D
.
The pullback of the Cartan 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(D)
to
G
vanishes, since
\mathfrak{g}
is Lagrangian.
Suppose
\Sigma
(as in the previous section) has an even number of edges for each boundary component, alternatingly labeled as
‘free’ or ‘colored’:

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{col}}\sqcup\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}.

A picture of such a surface is given in the introduction.
Every vertex is either the source or the target of a uniquely defined free edge. That is,

(34)

\mathcal{V}=\mathsf{t}(\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}})\sqcup\mathsf{s}(\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}).

The submanifold
N\subseteq D^{\mathcal{E}}

of maps
\mathcal{E}\to D
taking colored edges to
G
is transverse to the
D^{\mathcal{V}}
-orbits, and hence
is transverse to the moment map
\Phi\colon M_{D}(\Sigma)\to D^{\mathcal{E}}
. Since
N
is
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant,
it follows that the pre-image

M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)=\Phi^{-1}(N)

is a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant submanifold

(35)

i\colon M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)\to M_{D}(\Sigma).

Thus,
M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)
consists
of all those homomorphims
\kappa\colon\Pi(\Sigma)\to D
such that
\kappa(\mathsf{e})\in G
for each colored edge
\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{col}}
. This space carries a
G^{\mathcal{V}}
-invariant 2-form
\omega^{\operatorname{col}}
given by pullback of
\omega
, and
a map

(36)

\Phi^{\operatorname{col}}\colon M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma)\to D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}

taking
\kappa
to its values on the
free
edges. By (
34
),
G^{\mathcal{V}}=(G\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
.

Theorem 7.2
.

The space
(M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma),\omega^{\operatorname{col}})
is a quasi-symplectic
(G\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-space with
D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-valued moment map (
36
). Equivalently, there is an exact
(G\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-equivariant morphism of Manin pairs

R^{\operatorname{col}}\colon\left(\mathbb{T}M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma),TM^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)\right)\dasharrow\left(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}},D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\right).

Hence, the quotient by
(\{e\}\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
is a quasi-symplectic
G^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-space with
(D/G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-valued moment map.

Proof.

Our proof will use the Dirac-theoretic formulation, similar to Theorem
D.1
.
We obtain
R^{\operatorname{col}}
as a composition of the Dirac morphisms

R^{\operatorname{col}}=T\circ R\circ\mathbb{T}i

where
\mathbb{T}i
is defined by the inclusion (
35
),
R
is the Dirac morphism (
48
) for the uncolored surface, and

(37)

T\colon(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}},D^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}})\dasharrow\left(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}},D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\right)

is defined as the product over all
T^{\mathsf{e}}
for
\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}
, given as identity morphism of
\mathbb{A}^{\mathsf{e}}

for each free edge and as the morphism

T^{\mathsf{e}}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\mathsf{e}}\dasharrow 0
defined by

L_{D}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
for each colored edge. To see that that this is indeed a Dirac morphism,
recall that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\cong\mathfrak{g}^{\mathcal{V}}
, since every vertex is either the source or the target of a
unique free edge. Using this observation, we see that every element of
\{\pi(d)\}\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
is

T
-related to a unique element of
\{d\}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}
.
∎

7.4.
Examples

We now discuss various examples of the spaces
(M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma),\omega^{\operatorname{col}})
introduced above.

Example 7.3

(2-gon)
.

Suppose first that
\Sigma
is a disk, whose boundary is subdivided into two edges
\mathsf{e}_{1},\mathsf{e}_{2}
, where
\mathsf{e}_{1}
is free and
\mathsf{e}_{2}
is colored.

We obtain a quasi-symplectic
G\times G
-space
M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma)
consisting of
pairs
(d,g)
with
d\in D,\ g\in G
and
dg=e
; the moment map takes such a pair to
d
.
It follows that

M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma)=G,\ \ \omega^{\operatorname{col}}=0

with
G\times G
-action on
d\in G
given by
(a,a^{\prime})\cdot d=ad(a^{\prime})^{-1}
and
moment map the inclusion

G\hookrightarrow D
. The corresponding
D/G
-valued space is
G/G=\operatorname{pt}
.

Example 7.4

(4-gon)
.

Appendix A
Background on Dirac geometry

A.1.
Dirac structures

A
Courant algebroid

[
17
,
28
]
is a vector bundle

\mathbb{A}\to M
with a (non-degenerate) fiber metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\colon\mathbb{A}\times_{M}\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{R}
, an anchor map
\mathsf{a}\colon\mathbb{A}\to TM
, and a bracket
[\![\cdot,\cdot]\!]\colon\Gamma(\mathbb{A})\times\Gamma(\mathbb{A})\to\Gamma(\mathbb{A})
on the space of sections.
These are required to satisfy the following axioms, for all sections
\sigma_{i}\in\Gamma(\mathbb{A})
:

(a)

[\![\sigma_{1},[\![\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!]+[\![\sigma_{2},[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3}]\!]]\!]
,

(b)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathsf{a}(\sigma_{1})}\langle\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3}\rangle=\langle[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\!],\sigma_{3}\rangle+\langle\sigma_{2},[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{3}]\!]\rangle
,

(c)

[\![\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}]\!]+[\![\sigma_{2},\sigma_{1}]\!]=\mathsf{a}^{*}{\mbox{d}}\langle\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\rangle
.

Here the last line uses the metric to identify
\mathbb{A}^{*}\cong\mathbb{A}
. A
Dirac structure

(\mathbb{A},E)
over
M
is a Courant algebroid

\mathbb{A}\to M
together with a Lagrangian subbundle
E\to M
whose space of sections is closed under the Courant bracket.
(If
\mathbb{A}
is given, one refers to
E
as a Dirac structure.)
A Courant algebroid over
M=\operatorname{pt}
is just a metrized Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
, and a Dirac structure over
M=\operatorname{pt}
is a Manin pair

(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
. For this reason, Dirac structures
(\mathbb{A},E)
are also called
Manin pairs

[
28
]
.

Let
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(M)
be a closed 3-form on
M
.
The
standard (
\eta
-twisted) Courant algebroid
is the direct sum

\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M=TM\oplus T^{*}M
, with metric given by the pairing, with anchor the projection to the first summand, and with
Courant bracket on sections given by

(38)

[\![X_{1}+\alpha_{1},X_{2}+\alpha_{2}]\!]=[X_{1},X_{2}]+\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\alpha_{2}-\iota_{X_{2}}{\mbox{d}}\alpha_{1}+\iota_{X_{1}}\iota_{X_{2}}\eta.

For
\eta=0
we also write
\mathbb{T}M
.
The graph
\operatorname{gr}(\pi)
of a Poisson bivector field is an example of a Dirac structure in
\mathbb{T}M
, as is the graph of any closed 2-form.

Another type of examples are the
action Courant algebroids

[
1
]
, for any action of a metrized Lie algebra
\mathfrak{d}
on
M
for which all stabilizers are coisotropic. As a vector bundle, the action Courant algebroid is
M\times\mathfrak{d}
, the metric and bracket extend the given ones on constant sections
\mathfrak{d}\subseteq\Gamma(M\times\mathfrak{d})
, and the anchor is the action map
M\times\mathfrak{d}\to TM
. Given a Lagrangian Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathfrak{d}
, one obtains a Dirac structure

(M\times\mathfrak{d},\ M\times\mathfrak{g})
.

A.2.
Morphisms

Definition A.1
.

[
6
]
(See also
[
2
,
15
,
26
]
.)

(i)

Let
\mathbb{A}_{1},\mathbb{A}_{2}
be Courant algebroids over manifolds
M_{1},M_{2}
. A
Courant morphism

R\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2}

is a smooth map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
together with a Lagrangian subbundle
R\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{2}\times\overline{\mathbb{A}}_{1}

along
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)\subseteq M_{2}\times M_{1}
, with the property that the image of
R
under the anchor is tangent to
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)
, and such that
the space of sections of
\mathbb{A}_{2}\times\overline{\mathbb{A}}_{1}
whose restrictions to
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)
takes values in
R
, is closed under
Courant bracket. Here
\overline{\mathbb{A}}
denotes the Courant algebroid obtained from
\mathbb{A}
by changing the sign of the metric.

(ii)

Let
(\mathbb{A}_{i},E_{i}),\ i=1,2
be Dirac structures over manifolds
M_{i}
. A
Dirac morphism

or
morphism of Manin pairs

R\colon(\mathbb{A}_{1},E_{1})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{2},E_{2})

is a Courant morphism
R\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2}
, with the property that
R\cap(E_{2}\times E_{1})
is the graph of a
bundle map
\Phi^{*}E_{2}\to E_{1}
.

The dotted arrow is used since
R
is not a bundle map, but only a relation. We shall often write
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
in place of
(x_{2},x_{1})\in R
.

Remark A.2
.

As a consequence of
\mathsf{a}(R)\subseteq\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)
, one obtains

\mathsf{a}_{1}^{*}(\mu_{1})\sim_{R}\mathsf{a}_{2}^{*}(\mu_{2})

for all
\mu_{i}\in TM_{i}^{*}|_{m_{i}}
with
m_{2}=\Phi(m_{1}),\ (T\Phi|_{m_{1}})^{*}\mu_{2}=\mu_{1}
.

Remark A.3
.

Condition

(ii)
for a Dirac morphism says that for a given point
(m_{2},m_{1})\in\operatorname{gr}(\Phi)
, if

x_{2}\in(E_{2})_{m_{2}}
then there is a
unique
element
x_{1}\in(E_{1})_{m}
with
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
.
We stress that we are working with the
strong
version of Dirac morphisms, as opposed to the weak version where uniqueness is not required.

Remark A.4
.

A vector bundle morphism
\Phi^{*}E_{2}\to E_{1}
as in (ii) is the same as a vector bundle
comorphism

E_{1}\dasharrow E_{2}
.
For a Dirac morphism, the compatibility of
R
with brackets implies that it is a
Lie algebroid comorphism
: In particular,
the pullback map on sections
\Gamma(E_{2})\to\Gamma(E_{1})
preserves Lie brackets.

Suppose
M_{i},\ i=1,2
are manifolds with closed 3-forms

\eta_{i}
, defining standard Courant algebroid
\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{i}}M_{i}
.
Given a map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
and a 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M_{1})
satisfying

{\mbox{d}}\omega=\eta_{1}-\Phi^{*}\eta_{2}
, we define the
standard Courant morphism

\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi\colon\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{1}}M_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}M_{2}

by

v_{1}+\mu_{1}\sim_{\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi}v_{2}+\mu_{2}\ \ \Leftrightarrow\ \ v_{2}=(T\Phi|_{m_{1}})(v_{1}),\ \mu_{1}=(T\Phi|_{m_{1}})^{*}\mu_{2}+\iota_{v_{1}}\omega.

For
\omega=0
we use the notation
\mathbb{T}\Phi
.
Given Poisson structures

\pi_{i}
on
M_{i}
, the map
\Phi
is a Poisson map if and only if it defines a Dirac morphism
\mathbb{T}\Phi\colon(\mathbb{T}M_{1},\operatorname{gr}({\pi_{1}}))\dasharrow(\mathbb{T}M_{2},\operatorname{gr}(\pi_{2}))
.

If
\mathcal{O}\subseteq M
is an orbit of a Dirac structure
(\mathbb{A},E)
(that is, a leaf of the singular foliation defined by
\mathsf{a}(\Gamma(E))\subseteq\Gamma(TM)
), there is a canonical Dirac morphism

(39)

R\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{O},T\mathcal{O})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A},E).

As a subbundle of
\mathbb{A}\times\overline{\mathbb{T}\mathcal{O}}
along the graph of the inclusion
\iota_{\mathcal{O}}
, it is spanned by the restrictions of
(\sigma,\mathsf{a}(\sigma))
for
\sigma\in\Gamma(E)
, together with the restrictions of
(\mathsf{a}^{*}\mu,\iota_{\mathcal{O}}^{*}\mu)
for
\mu\in\Omega^{1}(M)
.

As another example, if
(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
is a Manin pair,
an equivariant map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
of
\mathfrak{d}
-manifolds
with coisotropic stabilizers gives a Dirac morphism

(40)

(M_{1}\times\mathfrak{d},\ M_{1}\times\mathfrak{g})\dasharrow(M_{2}\times\mathfrak{d},\ M_{2}\times\mathfrak{g}).

In particular, given a
\mathfrak{d}
-action on
M
with coisotropic stabilizers, we have the Dirac morphism
(M\times\mathfrak{d},\,M\times\mathfrak{g})\dasharrow(\mathfrak{d},\mathfrak{g})
defined by the map
M\to\operatorname{pt}
. Other examples arise from the theory of Dirac Lie groups and Dirac homogeneous spaces (cf.
[
26
,
31
]
).

A.3.
Exact Courant algebroids

A Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}\to M
is called
exact

[
33
]
if the sequence
0\to T^{*}M\to\mathbb{A}\to TM\to 0
is exact.
Equivalently,
\operatorname{rank}(\mathbb{A})=2\dim M
and the
anchor
\mathsf{a}\colon\mathbb{A}\to TM
is onto.
In this case, the choice of a Lagrangian splitting
j\colon TM\to\mathbb{A}
of the anchor gives an identification with the standard Courant algebroid

\mathbb{A}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M

for a closed 3-form
\eta\in\Omega^{3}(M)
. Here
\eta
is given in terms of
j
by

\iota_{X_{1}}\iota_{X_{2}}\iota_{X_{3}}\eta=\langle j(X_{1}),[\![j(X_{2}),j(X_{3})]\!]\rangle.

The space of all splittings is an affine space
over the space of 2-forms
\varpi\in\Omega^{2}(M)
; any two splittings are related by

(41)

j^{\prime}(X)=j(X)-\mathsf{a}^{*}(\iota(X)\varpi);

the resulting 3-form changes to

(42)

\eta^{\prime}=\eta+{\mbox{d}}\varpi.

A morphism
R\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2}
of exact Courant algebroids,
with base map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
, is called
exact

[
16
,
27
]

if the sequence

0\to\operatorname{gr}(T^{*}\Phi)\to R\to\operatorname{gr}(T\Phi)\to 0

is exact. Equivalently, the anchor identifies
\mathsf{a}(R)=T\Phi
as a relation

\mathsf{a}(R)\colon TM_{1}\dasharrow TM_{2}
. The choice of splittings
\mathbb{A}_{1}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{1}}M_{1},\ \ \mathbb{A}_{2}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta_{2}}M_{2}

describes the exact Courant morphisms as
R=\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi
.

Example A.5
.

An exact Courant morphism
\mathbb{T}_{\eta}M\dasharrow 0
is the same as a
2-form
\omega
satisfying
{\mbox{d}}\omega=\eta
; one obtains

\operatorname{gr}(\omega)
as the backward image of
0
. An exact Dirac morphism

(\mathbb{T}M,TM)\dasharrow(0,0)
amounts to having a symplectic 2-form on
M
.

Remark A.6
.

Given a Dirac structure
E\subseteq\mathbb{A}
in an exact Courant algebroid, with a source-simply connected
integration
\mathcal{G}\rightrightarrows M
of the Lie algebroid

E\Rightarrow M
, there is a canonically defined exact Dirac morphism

R\colon(\mathbb{T}\mathcal{G},T\mathcal{G})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A},E)\times(\mathbb{A},E)^{\operatorname{op}}

with base map
(\mathsf{t},\mathsf{s})
.
See
[
14
]
,
[
15
, Section 4.2]
and
[
25
, Remark 6.2.1]
.

B.1.
Reduction of Dirac structures

Suppose
\mathbb{A}\to M
is a Courant algebroid, on which a Lie group
G
acts by Courant morphisms. We assume that the action
on
M
is a principal action, and that the action on
\mathbb{A}
has
isotropic generators

\varrho\colon M\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{A}.

That is,
\varrho
is
G
-equivariant (where the action on
\mathfrak{g}
is the adjoint action), and for all
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
we have

\langle\varrho(\xi),\varrho(\xi)\rangle=0
and

[\![\varrho(\xi),\sigma]\!]=\mathcal{L}_{\xi}\sigma

for all
\sigma\in\Gamma(\mathbb{A})
.
Then the quotient

\mathbb{A}_{\operatorname{red}}=\frac{\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)^{\perp}}{\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)}\Big/G\to M/G

is a Courant algebroid
[
10
]
. The quotient procedure comes with a Courant morphism
S\colon\mathbb{A}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{\operatorname{red}}
, where

x\sim_{S}y
if and only if
x\in\operatorname{ran}(\varrho^{\perp})
, with
y
its image under the quotient map.
If
E\subseteq\mathbb{A}
is a
G
-invariant Dirac structure satisfying

\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)\subseteq E
, then

E_{\operatorname{red}}=\frac{E}{\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)}\Big/G

is a Dirac structure in
\mathbb{A}_{\operatorname{red}}
. (Note that
E\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)^{\perp}
.)
This sets up a 1-1 correspondence between
G
-equivariant Dirac structures in
\mathbb{A}
containing the generators, and Dirac structures in
\mathbb{A}_{\operatorname{red}}
.

Remark B.1
.

Note that
S\colon\mathbb{A}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{\operatorname{red}}
is only a weak Dirac morphism for the Dirac structures
E
and
E_{\operatorname{red}}
, since
for a given
y\in E_{\operatorname{red}}|_{\pi(m)}
, the element
x\in E|_{m}
with
x\sim_{S}y
is not unique: it is only unique up to elements of
\operatorname{ran}(\varrho)
. On the other hand,
E
is the backward image of
E_{\operatorname{red}}
: if
x\in\mathbb{A}
with
x\sim_{S}y\in E_{\operatorname{red}}

then
x\in E
.

Remark B.2
.

If a second Lie group
H
acts on
\mathbb{A}
by automorphisms, commuting with the action of
G
and preserving the generators for the
G
-action, then this action descends to the reduction.

B.2.
Reduction of Dirac morphisms

Let
\mathbb{A}_{i}\to M_{i}
be
G
-equivariant exact Courant algebroids,
with isotropic generators
\varrho_{i}\colon M_{i}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathbb{A}_{i}
.
We assume that the base actions are principal, hence the reductions

\mathbb{A}_{i,\operatorname{red}}\to M_{i}/G
are defined.

Proposition B.3
.

[
16
, Theorem 3.8]

There is a 1-1 correspondence between

•

Courant morphisms

R_{\operatorname{red}}\colon\mathbb{A}_{1,\operatorname{red}}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2,\operatorname{red}}

•

G
-equivariant Courant morphisms

R\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2}
intertwining the generators:

\varrho_{1}(\xi)\sim_{R}\varrho_{2}(\xi),\ \ \ \xi\in\mathfrak{g}.

Furthermore,
R_{\operatorname{red}}
is exact if and only if
R
is exact.

In more detail, the correspondence is as follows. Let
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
be the base map of
R
.
Let
S_{i}\colon\mathbb{A}_{i}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{i,\operatorname{red}}
be the reduction morphisms.
In one direction, given
R
, we have
y_{1}\sim_{R_{\operatorname{red}}}y_{2}
if and only if
there are elements
x_{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{i}
such that

x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2},\ \ x_{i}\sim_{S_{i}}y_{i}.

In the opposite direction, given
R_{red}
, we have
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
if and only if there are elements
y_{i}\in\mathbb{A}_{i,\operatorname{red}}
and
\mu_{i}\in\ T^{*}M_{i}
such that the following conditions hold:

•

y_{1}\sim_{R_{\operatorname{red}}}y_{2}
,

•

x_{i}-\mathsf{a}_{i}^{*}(\mu_{i})\sim_{S_{i}}y_{i}
.

•

(T\Phi)(\mathsf{a}_{1}(x_{1}))=\mathsf{a}_{2}(x_{2})
,

•

\mu_{1}=(T\Phi)^{*}(\mu_{2})
,

The
\Rightarrow
direction of the following result is also proved in
[
16
]
. The other direction is not explicitly stated there; we will therefore provide a proof.

Proposition B.4
.

Let
R\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}_{2}
be as in Proposition
B.3
. Let
E_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{i}
be
G
-invariant Dirac structures
containing the generators
\operatorname{ran}(\varrho_{i})
, with reductions
E_{i,\operatorname{red}}
. Then

R
defines a Dirac morphism

R\colon(\mathbb{A}_{1},E_{1})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{2},E_{2})

if and only if
R_{\operatorname{red}}
defines a Dirac morphism

R_{\operatorname{red}}\colon(\mathbb{A}_{1,\operatorname{red}},E_{1,\operatorname{red}})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{2,\operatorname{red}},E_{2,\operatorname{red}}).

Proof.

Let
\pi_{i}\colon M_{i}\to M_{i}/G
be the quotient maps. Suppose
R
is a Dirac morphism. To see that
R_{\operatorname{red}}
is a Dirac morphism, let

y_{2}\in E_{2,\operatorname{red}}

be given, with base point
\pi_{2}(m_{2})
, where
m_{2}=\Phi(m_{1})
.
Choose
x_{2}\in E_{2}|_{m_{2}}
with
x_{2}\sim_{S_{2}}y_{2}
. Since
R
is a Dirac morphism, there is a unique
x_{1}\in E|_{m_{1}}
with
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
. Its image
y_{1}\in E_{1}
satisfies
y_{1}\sim_{R_{\operatorname{red}}}y_{2}
.
To verify the uniqueness condition of Dirac morphisms, let

, there exist elements

.
But since

is a Dirac morphism,

shows that

, hence

.

Conversely, suppose

is a Dirac morphism. To show that

is a Dirac morphism, let

be given, where
m_{2}=\Phi(m_{1})
.
Let
y_{2}\in E_{2,\operatorname{red}}|_{\pi_{2}(m_{2})}
be the image of

, and let
y_{1}\in E_{1,\operatorname{red}}|_{\pi_{1}(m_{1})}
be the unique element such that

. By definition of
R_{\operatorname{red}}
, there are elements
x_{1}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{1}|_{m_{1}},\ x_{2}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{A}_{2}|_{m_{2}}
with
with

. Since both

are
S_{2}
-related to
y_{2}
, there is a
(unique) element

. Then
x_{1}=x_{1}^{\prime}-\varrho_{1}(\xi)|_{m_{1}}\in E_{1}|_{m_{1}}
satisfies

. To verify the uniqueness condition, let

with

. Then its image

satisfies

is a Dirac morphism, it follows that

for some

. But then

Since the action is principal and

is

B.3.
Dirac Cross Section Theorem

If the anchor
\mathsf{a}
of the Courant algebroid
\mathbb{A}\to M
is transverse to a closed submanifold
\iota\colon N\to M
, then the pull-back Courant algebroid

\iota^{!}\mathbb{A}=\frac{\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)}{\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)^{\perp}}\to N

is defined (see e.g.
[
1
]
).
Similarly, given a Dirac structure

(\mathbb{A},E)
such that
\mathsf{a}|_{E}
is transverse to
N
, the pullback Dirac structure

\iota^{!}E=E\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)\subseteq\iota^{!}\mathbb{A}

is defined
[
1
]
. (Observe that
E|_{N}+\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)=\mathbb{A}|_{N}
implies

E\cap\,\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)^{\perp}=\{0\}
.)
We shall write
\iota^{!}(\mathbb{A},E)=(\iota^{!}\mathbb{A},\iota^{!}E)
. As a special case,

(43)

\iota^{!}(\mathbb{T}M,TM)=(\mathbb{T}N,TN).

The following result may be seen as a Dirac-geometric analogue of the Symplectic Cross Section Theorem of Guillemin-Sternberg
[
21
]
. Several variations and special cases have appeared in the literature (e.g.,
[
4
,
9
,
18
]
).

Theorem B.7

(Dirac Cross Section Theorem)
.

Let
R\colon(\mathbb{A}_{1},E_{1})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}_{2},E_{2})
be a Dirac morphism, with base map
\Phi\colon M_{1}\to M_{2}
. Suppose
N_{2}\subseteq M_{2}
is a closed submanifold transverse to
\mathsf{a}_{2}|_{E_{2}}
:

(44)

TM_{2}|_{N_{2}}=\mathsf{a}_{2}(E_{2})|_{N_{2}}+TN_{2}.

Then

\Phi
is transverse to
N_{2}
, and the pre-image
N_{1}=\Phi^{-1}(N_{2})\subseteq M_{1}
is transverse to
\mathsf{a}_{1}|_{E_{1}}
.
Furthermore, letting
\iota_{i}\colon N_{i}\to M_{i}
be the embeddings,

R
induces a Dirac morphism

(45)

\iota^{!}R\colon\iota_{1}^{!}(\mathbb{A}_{1},E_{1})\dasharrow\iota_{2}^{!}(\mathbb{A}_{2},E_{2}),

with base map
\Phi|_{N_{1}}\colon N_{1}\to N_{2}
.

Proof.

Consider
m_{2}=\Phi(m_{1})
. Since
R
is a Dirac morphism, every
x_{2}\in E_{2}|_{m_{2}}
determines a unique
x_{1}\in E_{1}|_{m_{1}}
with
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
. Applying the anchor, it follows that

\mathsf{a}_{2}(x_{2})=(T\Phi|_{m_{1}})(\mathsf{a}(x_{1}))
. This shows
\mathsf{a}_{2}(E_{2}|_{m_{2}})\subseteq\operatorname{ran}(T\Phi|_{m_{1}})
, hence transversality of

\mathsf{a}_{2}|_{E_{2}}
to
N_{2}
implies transversality of
\Phi
to
N_{2}
. It follows that

N_{1}=\Phi^{-1}(N_{2})
is a submanifold.

The normal bundle functor gives an isomorphism
\nu(M_{1},N_{1})|_{m_{1}}\to\nu(M_{2},N_{2})|_{m_{2}}
.
By assumption, every element of
\nu(M_{2},N_{2})|_{m_{2}}
is realized as
\mathsf{a}(x_{2})
for some
x_{2}\in E_{2}|_{m_{2}}
; the unique

x_{1}\in E_{1}|_{m_{1}}
with
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
realizes the corresponding element of
\nu(M_{1},N_{1})_{m_{1}}
. This shows
that
N_{1}
is transverse to
\mathsf{a}_{1}|_{E_{1}}
. In particular, the Dirac structures
\iota_{i}^{!}(\mathbb{A}_{i},E_{i}),\ i=1,2
are defined.

Write
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{A}_{2}\times\overline{\mathbb{A}_{1}}
, with anchor
\mathsf{a}=(\mathsf{a}_{2},\mathsf{a}_{1})
, and let

N=N_{2}\times N_{1},\ M=M_{2}\times M_{1}
, with inclusion
\iota=(\iota_{2},\iota_{1})\colon N\to M
.
Let
\Phi_{N}=\Phi|_{N_{1}}\colon N_{1}\to N_{2}
be the restriction of
\Phi
.

We claim that

R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
restricts to a subbundle of
\mathbb{A}
along
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})
. This then implies that
R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)^{\perp}
is a subbundle
as well, and hence so is the quotient

{\iota^{!}R}=\frac{R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)}{R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)^{\perp}}\subseteq\iota^{!}\mathbb{A}.

To prove the claim, it is convenient to work with a decomposition of
R
. Since

TM_{2}|_{N_{2}}=\mathsf{a}_{2}(E_{2}|_{N_{2}})+TN_{2}
we have
\mathbb{A}_{2}|_{N_{2}}=E_{2}|_{N_{2}}+\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})
.
Choose a Lagrangian subbundle
F_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{2}
with
\mathbb{A}_{2}=E_{2}\oplus F_{2}
and such that

F_{2}|_{N_{2}}\subseteq\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2}).

The backward image

F_{1}=F_{2}\circ R\subseteq\mathbb{A}_{1}

is a smooth Lagrangian subbundle with
\mathbb{A}_{1}=E_{1}\oplus F_{1}
,
and it comes with a bundle map

F_{1}\to F_{2}
taking
y_{1}\in F_{1}
to the
unique

y_{2}\in F_{2}
such that
y_{1}\sim_{R}y_{2}
(see e.g.
[
2
]
).
This determines a direct sum decomposition

R\cong F_{1}\oplus\Phi^{*}E_{2},

where the two summands are embedded as graphs of the bundle maps
F_{1}\to F_{2}

and
\Phi^{*}E_{2}\to E_{1}
.
Since
(T\Phi)\big(\mathsf{a}_{1}(y_{1})\big)=\mathsf{a}_{2}(y_{2})
for
y_{i}\in F_{i},\ y_{1}\sim_{R}y_{2}
,
and since
F_{2}|_{N_{2}}\subseteq\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})
,
we have that

F_{1}|_{N_{1}}\subseteq\mathsf{a}_{1}^{-1}(TN_{1}).

That is,
R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
contains the graph of the bundle map

F_{1}|_{N_{1}}\to F_{2}|_{N_{2}}
, and we obtain

(46)

R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)\cong F_{1}|_{N_{1}}\oplus(\Phi|_{N_{1}})^{*}(E_{2}\cap\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})).

Since
E_{2}\cap\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})
has constant rank, so does
R|_{\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})}\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
. This proves that
{\iota^{!}R}
is a well-defined
Lagrangian subbundle along
\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})
, and that its image under the anchor is tangent to

\operatorname{gr}(\Phi_{N})
. It is involutive by reduction: the bracket of two sections of
\mathbb{A}
that restrict to sections of
R
again restricts to a section of
R
, and similarly for sections that restrict to sections of
\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)
.

It remains to show that
{\iota^{!}R}
defines a Dirac morphism. Consider

m_{2}=\Phi(m_{1})
for
m_{1}\in N_{1}
, and let
[x_{2}]\in\iota_{2}^{!}E_{2}|_{m_{2}}
be an element represented by

x_{2}\in E_{2}\cap\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})
. Let
x_{1}\in E_{1}|_{m_{1}}
be the unique element
with
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2}
. Then
x_{1}\in E_{1}\cap\mathsf{a}_{1}^{-1}(TN_{1})
, and

[x_{1}]\sim_{\iota^{!}R}[x_{2}]
.

For the uniqueness property, suppose we are given
w_{1}\in\iota_{1}^{!}E_{1}
with
w_{1}\sim_{\iota^{!}R}0
.
By definition of
\iota^{!}R
, this lifts to a relation
z_{1}\sim_{R}z_{2}
, where

(z_{2},z_{1})\in R\cap\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TN)

with
[z_{1}]=w_{1}
and
[z_{2}]=0
. Using the direct sum decomposition (
46
) we can write
z_{i}=x_{i}+y_{i}
with
x_{i}\in E_{i},\ y_{i}\in F_{i}
and
x_{1}\sim_{R}x_{2},\ y_{1}\sim_{R}y_{2}
. From

[z_{2}]=0
, it follows that
z_{2}\in\mathsf{a}_{2}^{-1}(TN_{2})^{\perp}\subseteq F_{2}|_{N_{2}}
, hence
x_{2}=0
.
But then
x_{1}\sim_{R}0
implies that
x_{1}=0
as well, since
R
is a Dirac morphism. Similarly,
[z_{1}]=w_{1}\in\iota_{1}^{!}E_{1}
shows that

y_{1}=z_{1}\in E_{1}|_{m_{1}}+\mathsf{a}_{1}^{-1}(TN_{1})^{\perp}
, hence
y_{1}\in\mathsf{a}_{1}^{-1}(TN_{1})^{\perp}
and finally
w_{1}=[y_{1}]=0
.
∎

Remark B.8

(Exact case)
.

Consider the special case that

, hence
\tilde{S}_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}|_{Q_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}}\subseteq\mathsf{a}^{-1}(TQ_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}})
.
By reduction we obtain a Dirac structure

S_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}\subseteq\iota_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}^{!}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}),

defining a Courant morphism
\iota_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}^{!}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A})\dasharrow 0
to the zero Courant algebroid over a point. Taking a product over all pairs of glued edges, together with the
identity morphisms for non-glued edges, we obtain a Courant morphism

S\colon\iota^{!}\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}^{c}}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}}

with base map
\pi
. We claim that it defines a Dirac morphism

(50)

S\colon\iota^{!}(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}^{c}},\ D^{\mathcal{E}^{c}}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}^{c}})\dasharrow(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}},\ D^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}).

To see this, let
d^{c}\in Q\subseteq D^{\mathcal{E}^{c}}
with image
d=\pi(d^{c})\in D^{\mathcal{E}}
.
Given
\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}
, let
\zeta^{c}\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}^{c}}
be the quotient map
\mathcal{V}^{c}\to\mathcal{V}
followed by

\zeta\colon\mathcal{V}\to\mathfrak{d}
. Then
(d^{c},\zeta^{c})\sim_{S}(d,\zeta)
. Conversely, suppose
\zeta^{c}\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}^{c}}
satisfies
(d^{c},\zeta^{c})\sim_{S}(d,\zeta)
for
some

\zeta\in\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}
. Then the definition of
S
shows that
\zeta^{c}
is the quotient map

\mathcal{V}^{c}\to\mathcal{V}
followed by
\zeta
. In particular, if
(d^{c},\zeta^{c})\sim_{S}(d,0)
then
\zeta^{c}=0
. Hence, (
50
) is a Dirac morphism as claimed. By composition, we obtain the desired Dirac morphism

R=S\circ\iota^{!}R^{c}
with base map
\pi\circ\Phi^{c}\circ\iota_{M}=\Phi
.

For the last part of the theorem, use the standard splittings to identify
\mathbb{A}\cong\mathbb{T}_{\eta}D
. Note that the pullback of

\operatorname{pr}_{1}^{*}\eta+\operatorname{pr}_{2}^{*}\eta
to the anti-diagonal
Q_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}\subseteq D\times D
vanishes. Hence,

\iota_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}^{!}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A})=\mathbb{T}Q_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}
is just a standard (untwisted) Courant algebroid. Furthermore,
in terms of this identification,
S_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}=TQ_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}
, and so the Courant morphism
\iota_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}^{!}(\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A})\dasharrow 0
is just the standard Courant morphism
\mathbb{T}Q_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}\dasharrow 0
defined by the map
Q_{\mathsf{e}^{\prime},\mathsf{e}}\to\operatorname{pt}
. It follows that

S=\mathbb{T}\pi
. On the other hand,
R^{c}=\mathbb{T}_{\omega^{c}}\Phi^{c}
where
\omega^{c}\in\Omega^{2}(M(\Sigma^{c}))
is the 2-form for the
cut surface, and so
\iota^{!}R^{c}=\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\Phi^{c}\circ\iota)
with
\omega=\iota^{*}\omega^{c}
. Finally,

R=\mathbb{T}\pi\circ\mathbb{T}_{\omega}(\Phi^{c}\circ\iota)=\mathbb{T}_{\omega}\Phi.\qed

We may now consider the case of colored surfaces, as in Section
7.3
.

Theorem D.2
.

The space
(M_{D}^{\operatorname{col}}(\Sigma),\omega^{\operatorname{col}})
is a quasi-symplectic
(G\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-space with
D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-valued moment map (
36
). Equivalently, there is an exact
(G\times G)^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
-equivariant morphism of Manin pairs

R^{\operatorname{col}}\colon\left(\mathbb{T}M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma),TM^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)\right)\dasharrow\left(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}},D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\right).

In terms of the standard splitting of
\mathbb{A}
, we have
R^{\operatorname{col}}=\mathbb{T}_{\omega^{\operatorname{col}}}\Phi^{\operatorname{col}}
.

Proof.

The argument is similar to the proof of Theorem
D.1
, hence we will be brief. Let
R
be the Dirac morphism (
48
) for the uncolored surface, with base map
\Phi\colon M(\Sigma)\to D^{\mathcal{E}}
.
Let
Q\hookrightarrow D^{\mathcal{E}}
be the submanifold, consisting of maps
d\colon\mathcal{E}\to D
taking colored edges into
G
.
Then
M^{c}(\Sigma)=\Phi^{-1}(Q)
. Let
\iota_{M}\colon M^{c}(\Sigma)\to M(\Sigma)
and

\iota_{Q}\colon Q\to D^{\mathcal{E}}
be the embeddings. The Dirac Cross Section Theorem
B.7
gives a Dirac morphism

\iota_{Q}^{!}R\colon\left(\mathbb{T}M^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma),TM^{\operatorname{col}}_{D}(\Sigma)\right)\dasharrow\iota_{Q}^{!}(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}},D^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}}),

with base map
\Phi\circ\iota_{M}
. For each colored edge, let
\iota_{\mathsf{e}}\colon G\to D
be the inclusion. Then

\iota_{Q}^{!}\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}^{c}}=\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\times\prod_{\mathsf{e}\in\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{col}}}\iota_{\mathsf{e}}^{!}\mathbb{A}.

The image of the Dirac structure
\tilde{S}_{\mathsf{e}}=D\times\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g}\subseteq\mathbb{A}
under the
anchor is tangent to
G
, hence it descends, by reduction, to a Dirac structure
S_{\mathsf{e}}\subseteq\iota_{\mathsf{e}}^{!}\mathbb{A}
, which we may regard as defining a Courant morphism
\iota_{\mathsf{e}}^{!}\mathbb{A}\dasharrow 0
.
Taking the product over all colored edges, together with the identity morphism for the free edges, we obtain a Courant morphism

S\colon\iota_{Q}^{!}\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}}\dasharrow\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}
. In terms of the standard splitting, this is just
S=\mathbb{T}\pi
for the projection

Q\to D^{\operatorname{free}}
. By an argument parallel to that in the proof of Theorem
D.1
, this
is a Dirac morphism

S\colon\iota_{Q}^{!}(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}},D^{\mathcal{E}}\times\mathfrak{d}^{\mathcal{V}})\dasharrow\left(\mathbb{A}^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}},D^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\times(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g})^{\mathcal{E}_{\operatorname{free}}}\right).

We obtain
R^{\operatorname{col}}
as a composition of the Dirac morphisms,
R^{\operatorname{col}}=S\circ\iota^{!}R
.

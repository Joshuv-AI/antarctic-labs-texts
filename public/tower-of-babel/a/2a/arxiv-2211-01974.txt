Discrete approximations to Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians on a half-space and norm resolvent convergence
Horia Cornean, Henrik Garde, Arne Jensen

Abstract
We extend recent results on discrete approximations of the Laplacian in 𝐑 d \mathbf{R}^{d} with norm resolvent convergence to the corresponding results for Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians on a half-space. The resolvents of the discrete Dirichlet/Neumann Laplacians are embedded into the continuum using natural discretization and embedding operators. Norm resolvent convergence to their continuous counterparts is proven with a quadratic rate in the mesh size. These results generalize with a limited rate to also include operators with a real, bounded, and Hölder continuous potential, as well as certain functions of the Dirichlet/Neumann Laplacians, including any positive real power.



Keywords:
norm resolvent convergence, Dirichlet Laplacian, Neumann Laplacian, lattice.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification:
47A10, 47A58, 47B39.

1
Introduction

Let
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
be the Dirichlet Laplacian and let
H_{0}^{\textup{N}}
be the Neumann Laplacian on the half-space
\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}
, and let
\mathcal{H}^{+}=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})
. Let
H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}
and
H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}
be the standard finite difference discretizations of
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
and
H_{0}^{\textup{N}}
, defined on
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}=\ell^{2}(h\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+})
with a mesh size
h>0
; see section
2.3
for the precise definitions.

Using suitable embedding operators
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}},J_{h}^{\textup{re}}:\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}^{+}
and discretization operators
K_{h}^{\textup{ro}},K_{h}^{\textup{re}}:\mathcal{H}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
(see section
2.2
), we prove the following type of norm resolvent convergence with an explicit rate in the mesh size.

Theorem
.

Let
K\subset\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
be compact. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{2},

and

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{2},

for
0<h\leq 1
and
z\in K
.

Norm resolvent convergence was first shown for discrete approximations of the Laplacian on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
in
[
10
]
and was extended to classes of Fourier multipliers in
[
2
]
. Recently norm resolvent convergence of discrete approximations to other operators have been considered as well, such as discrete Dirac operators in
[
3
]
and quantum graph Hamiltonians in
[
4
]
.

We prove the above result as Theorem
3.2
in section
3
, and also prove several extensions to this result. In section
3.1
we add a real, bounded, and Hölder continuous potential
V
to
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
and
H_{0}^{\textup{N}}
, and add a discrete potential
V_{h}(n)=V(hn)
with
n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+}
to
H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}
and
H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}
. The norm resolvent estimates with a potential are given in Theorem
3.6
with a rate that now depends explicitly on the Hölder exponent for
V
. Such norm resolvent convergence implies much improved spectral results compared to e.g. strong resolvent convergence. This includes convergence of the spectrum in a local Hausdorff distance
[
2
, Section 5]
.

Finally in section
3.2
we prove norm resolvent estimates between
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})
and
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
, and between
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}})
and
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
, defined via the functional calculus for certain functions
\Psi
that have also been considered in
[
2
]
for estimates on the full space
\mathbf{R}^{d}
. The results are given in Theorem
3.10
. As an example, this includes
\Psi(\lambda)=\lambda^{s/2}
for any positive real power
s
. This example leads to norm resolvent estimates with a rate of
h^{\min\{s,2\}}
. Fractional Laplacians on a half-space (or general domains) with Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions have been considered by several authors. See e.g.
[
1
,
5
,
7
,
8
]
for some recent results. However, results are scarce for discrete approximations of such operators.

2
Preliminaries

We give the results in dimensions
d\geq 2
. The case
d=1
is obtained by a simple modification of the arguments below.

Let
d\geq 2
and
d^{\prime}=d-1
. For
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
we write
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})
with
x_{1}\in\mathbf{R}
and
x^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{d^{\prime}}
.
The half-space is denoted by
\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d}=(0,\infty)\times\mathbf{R}^{d^{\prime}}
.
For
x=(x_{1},x^{\prime})\in\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d}
the reflection of
x
in the hyperplane

\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{d^{\prime}}
is denoted by

\breve{x}=(-x_{1},x^{\prime}).

For
n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}
we write
n=(n_{1},n^{\prime})
with
n_{1}\in\mathbf{Z}
and
n^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
. We write

\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+}=\{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}\mid n_{1}\geq 1\}

for the discrete half-space. We denote the reflection in the discrete hyperplane
\{0\}\times\mathbf{Z}^{d^{\prime}}
by

\breve{n}=(-n_{1},n^{\prime}).

2.1
Extension and restriction operators

The continuous Hilbert spaces are denoted by

\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}^{+}=L^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}).

In analogy with the even-odd decomposition of functions in dimension one we introduce the reflection-even and reflection-odd functions in
\mathcal{H}
by defining

\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}\mid f(x)=f(\breve{x}),\;x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\}

and

\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}=\{f\in\mathcal{H}\mid f(x)=-f(\breve{x}),\;x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}\},

such that
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}
as an orthogonal direct sum.

The discrete Hilbert spaces are given by

\mathcal{H}_{h}=\ell^{2}(h\mathbf{Z}^{d})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}=\ell^{2}(h\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+})

with norms

\lVert{v_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{h}}=h^{d}\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}\lvert{v_{h}(n)}\rvert^{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}}=h^{d}\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+}}\lvert{u_{h}(n)}\rvert^{2}.

Notice that we use the index
n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}^{d}
in the notation for
v_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}
and
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
. The dependence on the mesh size is given by the subscript
h
.

The reflection-even and reflection-odd sequences are defined by

\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}_{h}=\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}\mid v_{h}(n)=v_{h}(\breve{n}),\;n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}\}

and

\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}_{h}=\{v_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}\mid v_{h}(n)=-v_{h}(\breve{n}),\;n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}\}.

We have
\mathcal{H}_{h}=\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}_{h}\oplus\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}_{h}
as an orthogonal direct sum.

The reflection-odd extension operator
\mathcal{O}\colon\mathcal{H}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}
and reflection-even extension operator
\mathcal{E}\colon\mathcal{H}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}
are given by

\mathcal{O}f(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+},\\
-f(\breve{x}),&\breve{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+},\end{cases}\qquad\mathcal{E}f(x)=\begin{cases}f(x),&x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+},\\
f(\breve{x}),&\breve{x}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}.\end{cases}

In the discrete case the reflection-odd extension operator
\mathcal{O}_{h}\colon\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}\to\mathcal{H}_{h}
is given by

\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}u_{h}(n),&n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+},\\
0,&\text{$n_{1}=0$, $n^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{d^{\prime}}$,}\\
-u_{h}(\breve{n}),&\breve{n}\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+}.\end{cases}

The discrete reflection-even extension operator

\mathcal{E}_{h}\colon\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}_{h}
is defined by

\mathcal{E}_{h}u_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}u_{h}(n),&n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+},\\
u_{h}(1,n^{\prime}),&\text{$n_{1}=0$, $n^{\prime}\in\mathbf{Z}^{d^{\prime}}$,}\\
u_{h}(\breve{n}),&\breve{n}\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}_{+}.\end{cases}

The natural restriction operators onto the half-spaces are denoted by

\mathcal{R}\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}^{+}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{R}_{h}\colon\mathcal{H}_{h}\to\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}.

Obviously we have
\mathcal{R}\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{R}\mathcal{E}=I^{+}
and
\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}=\mathcal{R}_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}=I^{+}_{h}
, where we also introduced the notation for the identity operators on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}
, respectively.

2.2
Embedding and discretization operators

In
[
2
]
embedding and discretization operators were defined using a pair of biorthogonal Riesz sequences. Here we consider only the special case of an orthogonal sequence, as in
[
10
]
, but with the additional assumption that the generating function is reflection-even.

Assumption 2.1
.

Assume
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}
such that
\{\varphi_{0}(\,\cdot\,-n)\}_{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}
is an orthonormal sequence in
\mathcal{H}
.

Define

\varphi_{h,n}(x)=\varphi_{0}((x-hn)/h),\quad h>0,\>n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d},\;x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Since
\varphi_{0}
is assumed reflection-even we have the
important property

\varphi_{h,n}(\breve{x})=\varphi_{h,\breve{n}}(x),\quad h>0,\>n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d},\;x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

(2.1)

Define the embedding operators
J_{h}\colon\mathcal{H}_{h}\to\mathcal{H}
by

J_{h}v_{h}(x)=\sum_{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}v_{h}(n)\varphi_{h,n}(x),\quad v_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}.

From Assumption
2.1
it follows that
\{h^{-d/2}\varphi_{h,n}\}_{n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}}

is an orthonormal sequence, hence that
J_{h}
is isometric.

The discretization operators are given by
K_{h}=(J_{h})^{\ast}
. With the convention that inner products are linear in the second entrance, we explicitly have

K_{h}g(n)=\frac{1}{h^{d}}\langle{\varphi_{h,n}},{g}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}},\quad g\in\mathcal{H}.

Let us note that (
2.1
) implies
J_{h}\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}
,
J_{h}\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}_{h}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}
,
K_{h}\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}_{h}
, and
K_{h}\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{\textup{re}}_{h}
.

The half-space embedding operators
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}},J_{h}^{\textup{re}}\colon\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}\to\mathcal{H}^{+}
are defined as

J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}=\mathcal{R}J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h},\qquad J_{h}^{\textup{re}}=\mathcal{R}J_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}.

The operators
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
and
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
are isometric, as can be seen from the following computation. Let
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
and use that
J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{\textup{ro}}
,

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}u_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert{J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert{\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{h}}=\lVert{u_{h}}\rVert^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}}.

A similar computation holds for
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
.

The half-space discretization operators

K_{h}^{\textup{ro}},K_{h}^{\textup{re}}\colon\mathcal{H}^{+}\to\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}

are defined as

K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}=\mathcal{R}_{h}K_{h}\mathcal{O},\qquad K_{h}^{\textup{re}}=\mathcal{R}_{h}K_{h}\mathcal{E}.

Note that
K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}=K_{h}^{\textup{re}}J_{h}^{\textup{re}}=I^{+}_{h}
.
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\ran J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
in
\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}
is the orthogonal projection onto
\ran J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
in
\mathcal{H}^{+}
.

2.3
Laplacians

Let
H_{0}=-\Delta
be the Laplacian in
\mathcal{H}
with domain
\mathcal{D}(H_{0})=H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
.

The Dirichlet Laplacian
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
is defined as the positive self-adjoint operator given by the Friedrichs extension of
-\Delta|_{C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d})}
. Equivalently,
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
is the variational operator associated with the triple
(\mathcal{H}^{+},H_{0}^{1}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d}),q)
, where the sesquilinear form
q
is

q(u,v)=\int_{\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d}}\overline{\nabla u}\cdot\nabla v\,\,{\rm d}x.

By
[
6
, Theorem 9.11]
the domain of
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
on a half-space simplifies to

\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})=H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})\cap H_{0}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})=\{u\in H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})\mid\gamma_{0}u=0\},

where
\gamma_{0}
is the Dirichlet trace operator.

Next we define the Neumann Laplacian
H_{0}^{\textup{N}}
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
as the positive self-adjoint variational operator associated with the triple
(\mathcal{H}^{+},H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}),q)
. On a half-space its domain simplifies via
[
6
, Theorem 9.20]
to

\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})=\{u\in H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})\mid\gamma_{1}u=0\},

where
\gamma_{1}
is the Neumann trace operator.

From
[
6
, Theorem 9.2]
the trace maps satisfy
\gamma_{j}\in\mathcal{B}(H^{m}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}),H^{m-j-\frac{1}{2}}(\partial\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}))
for
m\in\mathbf{N}
and
j\leq m-1
.

We need the following lemma. The result is a consequence of e.g.
[
9
, Proposition 2.2]
. We give a shorter proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 2.2
.

(i)

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
. Then
\mathcal{O}f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
and
\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}f=H_{0}\mathcal{O}f
. Furthermore, for
z\in\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{+}
we have

\mathcal{O}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}g=(H_{0}-zI)^{-1}\mathcal{O}g.

(2.2)

(ii)

Let
f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
. Then
\mathcal{E}f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
and
\mathcal{E}H_{0}^{\textup{N}}f=H_{0}\mathcal{E}f
. Furthermore, for
z\in\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
and all
g\in\mathcal{H}^{+}
we have

\mathcal{E}(H_{0}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}g=(H_{0}-zI)^{-1}\mathcal{E}g.

(2.3)

Proof.

(i): Let
f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
.
Since
C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d})
is a core for
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}
, we can
find a sequence
\psi_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}_{+}^{d})
such that

\psi_{n}\to f
and
H_{0}^{\textup{D}}\psi_{n}\to H_{0}^{\textup{D}}f
in
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, as
n\to\infty
.
We have
\mathcal{O}\psi_{n}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
, such that
\mathcal{O}\psi_{n}\to\mathcal{O}f
and
\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}\psi_{n}\to\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}f
in
\mathcal{H}
, as
n\to\infty
. Note that

\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}\psi_{n}=\mathcal{O}(-\Delta\psi_{n})=-\Delta\mathcal{O}\psi_{n},

since
-\Delta
commutes with orthogonal coordinate transformations and since
\psi_{n}
is supported in
\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}
, i.e. away from the hyperplane
\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{d^{\prime}}
. Thus

\mathcal{O}\psi_{n}\to\mathcal{O}f\quad\text{and}\quad-\Delta\mathcal{O}\psi_{n}\to\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}f

in
\mathcal{H}
. Since
H_{0}
is a closed operator we conclude that

\mathcal{O}f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
and
H_{0}\mathcal{O}f=\mathcal{O}H_{0}^{\textup{D}}f
. The second part of the statement follows by using
\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})=\ran((H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1})
for
z\in\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
.

(ii): Let
f\in\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
. Restrictions of
\mathcal{E}f
to either side of
\{0\}\times\mathbf{R}^{d^{\prime}}
has coinciding Dirichlet and Neumann traces, so at least
\mathcal{E}f\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. We can approximate
f
in
H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})
by a sequence
\psi_{n}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}})
with
\gamma_{1}\psi_{n}=0
. Now
\mathcal{E}\psi_{n}\in C^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
implies the identity

\partial_{1}\mathcal{E}f=\mathcal{O}\partial_{1}f.

However we have that
\partial_{1}f\in H_{0}^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+})
, which has a zero-extension
E_{0}(\partial_{1}f)\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
. Since

\partial_{1}\mathcal{E}f(x)=\mathcal{O}\partial_{1}f(x)=E_{0}(\partial_{1}f)(x)-E_{0}(\partial_{1}f)(\breve{x}),

then
\partial_{1}\mathcal{E}f\in H^{1}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
and as a consequence
\mathcal{E}f\in H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})=\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
, since there was no contention regarding the square integrability of the other partial derivatives. The rest of the proof follows by using that
-\Delta
on
H^{2}(\mathbf{R}^{d})
commutes with orthogonal coordinate transformations, and that
\mathcal{D}(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})=\ran((H_{0}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1})
for
z\in\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
.
∎

The discrete Laplacian
H_{0,h}
on
\mathcal{H}_{h}
is given by

H_{0,h}v_{h}(n)=\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{j})-v_{h}(n-e_{j})\bigr),\quad v_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h},\;n\in\mathbf{Z}^{d}.

Here
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{d}
denotes the canonical basis for
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
The discrete Dirichlet Laplacian on
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
is given by

H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}v_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{j})-v_{h}(n-e_{j})\bigr)&\\
\quad+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{1})\bigr),&n_{1}=1,\\
\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{j})-v_{h}(n-e_{j})\bigr),&n_{1}\geq 2.\end{cases}

Let
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
. Then using the definitions
one can verify that
\mathcal{O}_{h}H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}u_{h}=H_{0,h}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}
and then

\mathcal{O}_{h}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}u_{h}=(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}.

(2.4)

The discrete Neumann Laplacian on
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
is given by

H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}v_{h}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=2}^{d}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{j})-v_{h}(n-e_{j})\bigr)&\\
\quad+\frac{1}{h^{2}}\bigl(v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{1})\bigr),&n_{1}=1,\\
\frac{1}{h^{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d}\bigl(2v_{h}(n)-v_{h}(n+e_{j})-v_{h}(n-e_{j})\bigr),&n_{1}\geq 2.\end{cases}

Let
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
. Similar to the above,
\mathcal{E}_{h}H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}u_{h}=H_{0,h}\mathcal{E}_{h}u_{h}
and then

\mathcal{E}_{h}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}u_{h}=(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{h}u_{h}.

(2.5)

Remark 2.3
.

Since we use homogeneous Dirichlet and Neumann conditions, the discrete Laplacians have a very similar finite difference structure. The discrete Neumann Laplacian only differs from the discrete Dirichlet Laplacian at the indices where
n_{1}=1
. Here the contributions from the boundary conditions either mean that
v_{h}(n-e_{1})=0
(Dirichlet case) or that
v_{h}(n-e_{1})=v_{h}(n)
(Neumann case). This subtle difference also implies the connections to odd and even reflections in (
2.4
) and (
2.5
).

3
Results

Additional assumptions on the function
\varphi_{0}
are needed to obtain our results, cf.
[
2
, Assumption 2.8]

or
[
10
, Assumption B]
. Let
\widehat{\varphi}_{0}
denote the Fourier transform of
\varphi_{0}
, defined as

\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi)=(2\pi)^{-d/2}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{d}}{\rm e}^{-{\rm i}x\cdot\xi}\varphi_{0}(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
\varphi_{0}
satisfy Assumption
2.1
and assume that

\widehat{\varphi}_{0}
is essentially bounded.
Assume there exists
c_{0}>0
such that

\supp(\widehat{\varphi}_{0})\subseteq[-3\pi/2,3\pi/2]^{d},\quad\text{and}\quad\lvert{\widehat{\varphi}_{0}(\xi)}\rvert\geq c_{0},\quad\xi\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]^{d}.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
,
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
,
K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
, and
K_{h}^{\textup{re}}
be as above, with
\varphi_{0}
satisfying
Assumption
3.1
.
Let
K\subset\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
be compact. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{2},

(3.1)

and

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{2},

(3.2)

for
0<h\leq 1
and
z\in K
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{+}
. Then

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f-(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}f}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}^{2}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\lVert{\mathcal{O}J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f-\mathcal{O}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}f}\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We have
\mathcal{O}J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}=\mathcal{O}\mathcal{R}J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}=J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}
, since
J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}
is a reflection-odd function for all
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
.
Thus using (
2.4
) we get

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f

\displaystyle=J_{h}\mathcal{O}_{h}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f

\displaystyle=J_{h}(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{h}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f.

Now
\mathcal{O}_{h}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f=\mathcal{O}_{h}\mathcal{R}_{h}K_{h}\mathcal{O}f=K_{h}\mathcal{O}f
, since
K_{h}\mathcal{O}f
is a reflection-odd sequence. Thus we have shown

\mathcal{O}J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}f=J_{h}(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}\mathcal{O}f.

Using this result together with (
2.2
) we have shown that

\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}\bigr)f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}^{2}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}-(H_{0}-zI)^{-1}\bigr)\mathcal{O}f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

Thus we can use the results in
[
2
]
or
[
10
]
to obtain (
3.1
).

To prove (
3.2
) note that
\mathcal{E}J_{h}^{\textup{re}}=J_{h}\mathcal{E}_{h}
and
\mathcal{E}_{h}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}=K_{h}\mathcal{E}
, and then use
(
2.3
) instead of (
2.2
) and (
2.5
) instead of (
2.4
). This leads to:

\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(H_{0}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}\bigr)f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}^{2}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}(H_{0,h}-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}-(H_{0}-zI)^{-1}\bigr)\mathcal{E}f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

which together with the results in
[
2
]
or
[
10
]
completes the proof of (
3.2
).
∎

3.1
Adding a potential

Next we add a potential. To obtain the results we introduce two assumptions.

Assumption 3.3
.

Let
\tau>d
. Assume that there exists
C>0
such that

\lvert{\varphi_{0}(x)}\rvert\leq C(1+\lvert{x}\rvert)^{-\tau},\quad x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Assumption 3.4
.

Let
V\colon\overline{\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}}\to\mathbf{R}
be a bounded function which is uniformly Hölder continuous of order
\theta\in(0,1]
.

Note that
\overline{\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}}
denotes the closed half-space, so the conditions hold up to the boundary.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
V
satisfy Assumption
3.4
. Then
\mathcal{E}V
is bounded and uniformly Hölder continuous of order
\theta
on
\mathbf{R}^{d}
.

Proof.

Boundedness is clear, and for the Hölder continuity we only need to consider points
x,y\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
such that
x,\breve{y}\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}
. Now Assumption
3.4
and
\lvert{x-\breve{y}}\rvert\leq\lvert{x-y}\rvert
imply

\lvert{\mathcal{E}V(x)-\mathcal{E}V(y)}\rvert=\lvert{V(x)-V(\breve{y})}\rvert\leq C\lvert{x-\breve{y}}\rvert^{\theta}\leq C\lvert{x-y}\rvert^{\theta}.\qed

We define the discretized potential as
V_{h}(n)=V(hn)
,
h>0
,
n\in\mathbf{Z}_{+}^{d}
. Then we define
H^{\textup{D}}=H_{0}^{\textup{D}}+V
and
H^{\textup{N}}=H_{0}^{\textup{N}}+V
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, and

H_{h}^{\textup{D}}=H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}}+V_{h}
and
H_{h}^{\textup{N}}=H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}}+V_{h}
on
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
,
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
,
K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
, and
K_{h}^{\textup{re}}
be as above, with
\varphi_{0}
satisfying
Assumptions
3.1
and
3.3
. Let
V
satisfy Assumption
3.4
. Define

\frac{1}{\theta^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{\theta}+\frac{1}{\tau-d}.

Let
K\subset\mathbf{C}\setminus\mathbf{R}
be compact. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(H^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{\theta^{\prime}},

(3.3)

and

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(H_{h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(H^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{\theta^{\prime}},

(3.4)

for
0<h\leq 1
and
z\in K
.

Proof.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}_{+}
. Then

\mathcal{O}(Vf)(x)=V(x)f(x)=(\mathcal{E}V)(x)(\mathcal{O}f)(x),

and

\mathcal{O}(Vf)(\breve{x})=-(Vf)(x)=V(x)(-f(x))=(\mathcal{E}V)(\breve{x})(\mathcal{O}f)(\breve{x}).

Thus we have
\mathcal{O}V=(\mathcal{E}V)\mathcal{O}
as operators from
\mathcal{H}^{+}
to
\mathcal{H}
,
where
\mathcal{E}V
denotes the operator of multiplication in
\mathcal{H}
by

(\mathcal{E}V)(x)
,
x\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
.
Let
H=H_{0}+\mathcal{E}V
on

\mathcal{H}
. Then combining the above result with
the arguments leading to (
2.2
) we get for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{+}

\mathcal{O}(H^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}f=(H-zI)^{-1}\mathcal{O}f.

We can repeat these arguments in the discrete case, leading to

\mathcal{O}_{h}(H_{h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}u_{h}=(H_{h}-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{h}u_{h}

for
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}_{h}^{+}
. Here we have defined
H_{h}=H_{0,h}+\mathcal{E}_{h}V_{h}
on

\mathcal{H}_{h}
. Note that
\mathcal{E}_{h}V_{h}
and
(\mathcal{E}V)_{h}
may differ only at
n_{1}=0
. Thus replacing
\mathcal{E}_{h}V_{h}
by
(\mathcal{E}V)_{h}
introduces an error of order
h^{\theta}
, due to Assumption
3.4
, and this error can be absorbed in the final estimate below.

Repeating the computations in the proof of Theorem
3.2
we get
for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{+}

\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(H_{h}^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(H^{\textup{D}}-zI^{+})^{-1}\bigr)f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}^{2}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}(H_{h}-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}-(H-zI)^{-1}\bigr)\mathcal{O}f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}.

We can then use
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
to complete the proof.

The proof for the Neumann Laplacian is analogous, using instead that
\mathcal{E}(Vf)=(\mathcal{E}V)(\mathcal{E}f)
, which for
f\in\mathcal{H}^{+}
and
u_{h}\in\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(H^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}f

\displaystyle=(H-zI)^{-1}\mathcal{E}f,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{h}(H_{h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}u_{h}

\displaystyle=(H_{h}-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{E}_{h}u_{h}.

This leads to

\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(H_{h}^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(H^{\textup{N}}-zI^{+})^{-1}\bigr)f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}^{+}}^{2}\\
=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl\lVert{\bigl(J_{h}(H_{h}-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}-(H-zI)^{-1}\bigr)\mathcal{E}f}\bigr\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2},

which can also be estimated by
[
2
, Theorem 4.4]
.
∎

3.2
Functions of Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians

Now we extend the approximation results given in Theorem
3.2
to functions of the Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians on the half-space. Let
\Psi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
be a Borel function. Using the functional calculus we can define the operators
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
,
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})
,
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
, and
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}})
.

We need the following lemma, which is an immediate consequence of
[
11
, Proposition 5.15]
; see also
[
9
]
. For operators
S
and
T
the notation
S\subset T
means that
T
is an extension of
S
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For
j=1,2
assume that
A_{j}
is a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{j}
. Assume that
B\colon\mathcal{H}_{1}\to\mathcal{H}_{2}
is a bounded operator such that

BA_{1}\subset A_{2}B.

Let
\Psi
be a Borel function on
\mathbf{R}
. Then we have

B\Psi(A_{1})\subset\Psi(A_{2})B.

(3.5)

If
\Psi
is a bounded function then equality holds in (
3.5
).

In the following assumption the parameters are chosen to be compatible with the ones in
[
2
, Assumption 3.1]
.

Assumption 3.8
.

Assume

\alpha>\tfrac{1}{2},\quad\beta>-\tfrac{1}{2},\quad\text{and}\quad\alpha\leq 1+\beta<2\alpha\leq 3+\beta.

Let
\Psi\colon[0,\infty)\to\mathbf{R}
be a continuous function which is continuously differentiable on
(0,\infty)
and satisfies the following conditions:

(1)

\Psi(0)=0
,

(2)

there exist
c_{0}>0
and
c_{1}>0
such that

\Psi(\lambda)\geq c_{0}\lambda^{\alpha/2}
for
\lambda\geq c_{1}
.

(3)

there exists
c>0
such that
\lvert{\Psi^{\prime}(\lambda)}\rvert\leq c\lambda^{(\beta-1)/2}
for
\lambda>0
.

We omit the straightforward proof of the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
\Psi
satisfy Assumption
3.8
with parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. Define
G_{0}(\xi)=\Psi(\lvert{\xi}\rvert^{2})
,
\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}
. Then
G_{0}
satisfies Assumption
3.1
in
[
2
]
with the same parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Next we define

G_{0,h}(\xi)=G_{0}(\tfrac{2}{h}\sin(\tfrac{h}{2}\xi_{1}),\tfrac{2}{h}\sin(\tfrac{h}{2}\xi_{2}),\ldots,\tfrac{2}{h}\sin(\tfrac{h}{2}\xi_{d})),\quad h>0,\quad\xi\in\mathbf{R}^{d}.

Using these definitions it follows that
\Psi(H_{0})
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
G_{0}
on
\mathcal{H}
, and
\Psi(H_{0,h})
is the Fourier multiplier with symbol
G_{0,h}
on
\mathcal{H}_{h}
. The operators
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
and
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, and the operators

\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})
and
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}})
on
\mathcal{H}^{+}_{h}
, are defined using the functional calculus.

We have the following extension of Theorem
3.2
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
\Psi
satisfy Assumption
3.8
with parameters
\alpha
and
\beta
. Let

\gamma=\min\{2\alpha-1,2\alpha-\beta-1\}.

Let
J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
,
J_{h}^{\textup{re}}
,
K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}
, and
K_{h}^{\textup{re}}
be as above, with
\varphi_{0}
satisfying Assumption
3.1
. Let
K\subset\mathbf{C}\setminus[0,\infty)
be compact. Then there exists
C>0
such that

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{ro}}(\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{ro}}-(\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{\gamma},

(3.6)

and

\lVert{J_{h}^{\textup{re}}(\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}})-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}K_{h}^{\textup{re}}-(\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})-zI^{+})^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}^{+})}\leq Ch^{\gamma},

(3.7)

for
0<h\leq 1
and
z\in K
.

Proof.

We prove the result for the Dirichlet Laplacians.
Assumption
3.8
and Lemma
3.9
together with
[
2
, Proposition 3.5]
imply that we have the estimate

\lVert{J_{h}(\Psi(H_{0,h})-zI_{h})^{-1}K_{h}-(\Psi(H_{0})-zI)^{-1}}\rVert_{\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})}\leq Ch^{\gamma}

(3.8)

for
0<h\leq 1
and
z\in K
, with
K
satisfying the assumption in the theorem.

Combine Lemma
2.2
with Lemma
3.7
to get the result

\mathcal{O}(\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})-zI^{+})^{-1}=(\Psi(H_{0})-zI)^{-1}\mathcal{O},\quad z\in K.

(3.9)

Analogously, using (
2.4
) and Lemma
3.7
we get

\mathcal{O}_{h}(\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})-zI^{+}_{h})^{-1}=(\Psi(H_{0,h})-zI_{h})^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{h},\quad z\in K.

(3.10)

Using the results (
3.8
)–(
3.10
) we can repeat the arguments in the proof of Theorem
3.2
to get the result in the Dirichlet case. The proof in the Neumann case is almost the same, so we omit it.
∎

Remark 3.11
.

By repeating the proof of Theorem
3.6
, we may also add a potential
V
to the operators
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
and
\Psi(H_{0}^{\textup{N}})
and add a discrete potential
V_{h}
to the operators
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{D}})
and
\Psi(H_{0,h}^{\textup{N}})
. The resulting estimates, replacing those in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), will have the rate
h^{\min\{\gamma,\theta^{\prime}\}}
.

Of particular interest are the functions
\Psi
that give the powers of the Laplacian
H_{0}
.
Let
s>0
and define
\Psi_{s}(\lambda)=\lambda^{s/2}
,
\lambda\geq 0
. Then
G_{0}(\xi)=\lvert{\xi}\rvert^{s}
and
\Psi_{s}(H_{0})=(-\Delta)^{s/2}
. For
s\geq 2
we can take
\alpha=(s+2)/2
and
\beta=s-1
to satisfy the conditions in Assumption
3.8
. Then the estimate (
3.8
) holds with
\gamma=2
.

For
\frac{1}{2}<s<2
the conditions in Assumption
3.8
are satisfied with
\alpha=s
and
\beta=s-1
. We get
\gamma=s
for
1\leq s<2
. For
0<s<1
we can use the result in
[
2
, Proposition 3.11]
which yields the estimate (
3.8
) for
\Psi_{s}(H_{0})
and
\Psi_{s}(H_{0,h})
with
\gamma=s
.

We summarize the results above as a Corollary to both Theorem
3.10
and the results in
[
2
]
.

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
\Psi_{s}(\lambda)=\lambda^{s/2}
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
s>0
. Then the estimates (
3.6
) and (
3.7
) hold for
\gamma=\min\{s,2\}
.

Remark 3.13
.

The operators
\Psi_{s}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})
defined here do not agree with the fractional Dirichlet Laplacians on a half-space defined in
[
7
,
8
]
.
Let
u\in\mathcal{D}(\Psi_{s}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}}))
, then Lemmas
2.2
and
3.7
imply
\mathcal{O}\Psi_{s}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})u=\Psi_{s}(H_{0})\mathcal{O}u
, such that

\Psi_{s}(H_{0}^{\textup{D}})u=\mathcal{R}\Psi_{s}(H_{0})\mathcal{O}u
. Whereas in
[
7
,
8
]
the definition is based on the operator
\mathcal{R}\Psi_{s}(H_{0})E_{0}
applied to suitable functions in
\mathcal{H}^{+}
, where
E_{0}
is the operator for extension by zero. Hence the two approaches differ by the type of extension operator that is used.

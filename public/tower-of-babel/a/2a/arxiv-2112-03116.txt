Non-uniqueness of Leray solutions of the forced Navier-Stokes equations
Dallas Albritton, Elia Brué, Maria Colombo

Abstract
. In the seminal work [ 39 ] , Leray demonstrated the existence of global weak solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations in three dimensions. We exhibit two distinct Leray solutions with zero initial velocity and identical body force. Our approach is to construct a ‘background’ solution which is unstable for the Navier-Stokes dynamics in similarity variables; its similarity profile is a smooth, compactly supported vortex ring whose cross-section is a modification of the unstable two-dimensional vortex constructed by Vishik in [ 43 , 44 ] . The second solution is a trajectory on the unstable manifold associated to the background solution, in accordance with the predictions of Jia and Šverák in [ 32 , 33 ] . Our solutions live precisely on the borderline of the known well-posedness theory.

Our central task is therefore to
find a smooth and decaying unstable steady state of the forced Euler equations in three dimensions

Rather, unstable solutions are typically sought among explicit solutions with many symmetries and subsequently analyzed by ODE techniques. In three dimensions, the most natural explicit steady states are

Comparison with existing literature

We now review the above cited literature more in detail and point out certain connections with the present work.

Self-similarity, instability, and non-uniqueness in the Navier-Stokes equations: Jia, Šverák, and Guillod’s program

Open problems
. As mentioned above, the main task of this paper is to find an unstable (forced) Euler steady state. The steady state we choose is a perturbation of a particular (unforced) unstable vortex of Vishik whose construction is far from simple. Given the flexibility afforded by a force, it would be interesting to know how generic this instability is and to develop a general method for finding these unstable (forced) solutions. It is, moreover, an open problem to remove the force.

2.
Linear instability: from two dimensions to axisymmetry

2.1.
Spectral preliminaries

Let
H
be a Hilbert space and
\bm{L}:D(\bm{L})\subset H\to H
be closed and densely defined. Then
\bm{D}(L)
is a Hilbert space with the graph norm. The basic theory of these operators is reviewed in
[
41
, VIII: Unbounded Operators]
. A simple criterion for self-adjointness is that
\ker\bm{L}+i
and
\ker\bm{L}-i
are trivial.

The
resolvent set

\rho(\bm{L})
consists of those
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
such that
\lambda-\bm{L}:D(\bm{L})\to H
is invertible. Its inverse
R(\lambda,\bm{L}):H\to D(\bm{L})
is known as the
resolvent
, and it is bounded by the open mapping theorem. The
spectrum

\sigma(\bm{L})
is the complement of the resolvent set.
In our convention, the
essential spectrum

\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L})
is the set of spectral values for which
\lambda-\bm{L}
is not Fredholm of index zero.
7
7

7

The essential spectrum is sometimes defined otherwise, as the set where
\lambda-\bm{L}
is not semi-Fredholm
[
34
]
, or as the set where
\lambda-\bm{L}
is not Fredholm
[
21
]
.
The essential spectrum is closed. Moreover,
[
34
, Theorem 5.28]
(see also the discussion in
[
34
, Chapter 4, Section 6]
) can be used to show that, in each connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L})
,
(i)
\lambda-\bm{L}
is not invertible everywhere, or (ii)
\lambda-\bm{L}
is invertible except at isolated points.

An operator
\bm{T}:D(\bm{L})\to H
is
relatively compact with respect to
\bm{L}
if
\bm{T}
is compact when

is considered with the graph norm. A key property is that relatively compact perturbations do not disturb the essential spectrum:
\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}+\bm{T})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L})
, see
[
34
, Theorem 5.26]
.

We frequently use the following reasoning: Consider an operator
\bm{L}
as above whose essential spectrum is known to be contained in a half space
\{\Re\lambda\leq\mu\}
. We perturb
\bm{L}
by a relatively compact perturbation
\bm{T}
, so that
\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}+\bm{T})=\sigma_{\rm ess}(\bm{L})
. Furthermore, we typically know that the perturbed operator
\bm{L}+\bm{T}
is invertible for
\Re\lambda\gg 1
. Hence, the spectrum in
\{\Re\lambda>\mu\}
consists of isolated points.

2.2.
Two-dimensional instability

To begin, we recall the unstable vortex constructed in
[
44
]
, see also
[
1
]
.

For integer
m\geq 2
, we define the following space of
m
-fold rotationally symmetric functions:

(2.1)

L^{2}_{m}:=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}):f(R_{\frac{2\pi}{m}}x)=f(x)\,\mbox{for a.e. }x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\},

where
R_{\frac{2\pi}{m}}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is counterclockwise rotation by
2\pi/m
.

Typically, the two-dimensional Biot-Savart law
{\rm BS}_{2d}[f]:=\nabla^{\perp}\Delta^{-1}f=K_{2}\ast f
, considered on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, is only well defined
up to constants
and satisfies
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\to\dot{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\mathbb{R}^{2})
. An important point is that the Biot-Savart law is well defined on
L^{2}_{m}

within the class of
m
-fold rotationally symmetric functions
, since the
m
-fold rotational symmetry sets the mean velocity on
B_{R}
to zero for all
R>0
.

For
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, using polar coordinates
(x,y)=(\varrho\cos\varphi,\varrho\sin\varphi)
and expanding in Fourier series, we can write

(2.2)

f(x,y)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{k}(\varrho)e^{ik\varphi}\,,\qquad f_{k}(\varrho):=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(\varrho,\varphi)e^{-ik\varphi}d\varphi\,.

The Plancherel identity gives

(2.3)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}^{2}=2\pi\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\,\varrho\,d\varrho)}^{2}\,.

It is immediate to check that
f\in L^{2}_{m}
if and only if
f_{k}=0
whenever
m
does not divide
k
. It is convenient to introduce the following decomposition of
L^{2}_{m}
:

(2.4)

L^{2}_{m}=\oplus_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}U_{k}\,,\qquad U_{k}:=\{f(x)=g(\varrho)e^{ikm\varphi}\,:\,g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\,\varrho\,d\varrho)\}\,,

where
U_{k}
are closed, mutually orthogonal subspaces of
L^{2}_{m}
.

Let us consider a smooth, divergence-free vector field
\bar{u}(x)=\zeta(|x|)x^{\perp}
that decays at infinity, where
x^{\perp}=(-x_{2},x_{1})
. We set

(2.5)

\bar{\omega}=\curl\bar{u}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bar{u}\,,

which is a radial function.

We then define

(2.6)

\bm{A}:D(\bm{A})\subset L^{2}_{m}\to L^{2}_{m},\quad D(\bm{A}):=\{\omega\in L^{2}_{m}:\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\omega\in L^{2}_{m}\},

(2.7)

-\bm{A}\omega=\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\omega+u\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega},

where
u={\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]
.
It is verified in
[
43
,
44
,
1
]
that
\bm{K}\omega:=u\cdot\nabla\bar{\omega}
is a compact operator
\bm{K}:L^{2}_{m}\to L^{2}_{m}
. The operator
\bm{A}
is closed and densely defined. By regarding it as a compact perturbation of the skew-adjoint operator
\omega\mapsto\bar{u}\cdot\nabla\omega
, we have that the essential spectrum of
\bm{A}
belongs to the imaginary axis, and the remainder of the spectrum is discrete, consisting of countably many eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.

We now state a version of Vishik’s theorem in
[
44
]
, see also
[
1
, Theorem 3.0.1]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

There exists
m\geq 2
and a smooth, radially symmetric vorticity profile
\bar{\omega}=\bar{\omega}(\varrho)
satisfying

(2.8)

|\bar{\omega}|+\varrho|\partial_{\varrho}\bar{\omega}|\lesssim\langle\varrho\rangle^{-2}

such that
\bm{A}\colon D(\bm{A})\subset L^{2}_{m}\to L^{2}_{m}
has an unstable eigenvalue
\lambda
(that is,
\Re{\lambda}>0
).

The profile can be chosen to satisfy many additional properties, which we do not require here. Recall that
\langle\varrho\rangle=(1+\varrho^{2})^{1/2}
is the Japanese bracket notation. The decay rate
\langle\varrho\rangle^{-2}
is not essential; any power-law decay will suffice. One may multiply the vortex by a prefactor
\beta\gg 1
to increase
\Re\lambda
.

We now demonstrate that appropriate truncations of unstable vortices remain unstable.
Let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{1})
be radially symmetric with
\phi\equiv 1
on
B_{1/2}
. For
R>0
, consider
\phi_{R}(x):=\phi(x/R)
and the truncation

(2.9)

\bar{u}_{R}=\phi_{R}\bar{u},\quad\bar{\omega}_{R}=\curl\bar{u}_{R}=\nabla^{\perp}\cdot\bar{u}_{R}.

We consider also operators
\bm{A}_{R}
defined analogously to
\bm{A}
in (
2.6
)-(
2.7
) with
\bar{u}_{R}
replacing
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\omega}_{R}
replacing
\bar{\omega}
.

Proposition 2.2

(Truncated unstable vortex)
.

Let
\bar{\omega}
be an unstable vortex as in Theorem
2.1
. Let
\lambda_{\infty}
be an unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{A}
. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\Re\lambda_{\infty})
, there exists
R_{0}>0
, depending on
\varepsilon
,
\lambda_{\infty}
, and
\bar{\omega}
, such that for all
R\geq R_{0}
,
\bm{A}_{R}
has an unstable eigenvalue
\lambda_{R}
with
|\lambda_{R}-\lambda_{\infty}|<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let us begin by observing (separation of variables) that the subspaces
U_{k}
defined in (
2.4
) are invariant under the operators
\bm{A}
and
\bm{A}_{R}
. Moreover, the restrictions

(2.10)

\bm{A}^{(k)}=\bm{A}|_{U_{k}}\,,\qquad\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}=\bm{A}_{R}|_{U_{k}}\,,

are continuous.
Indeed, for
\omega(x)=g(\varrho)e^{imk\varphi}
we have

(2.11)

\bm{A}^{(k)}\omega=\left(-\zeta imkg+\frac{1}{\varrho}imkf\bar{\omega}^{\prime}\right)e^{imk\varphi},

(2.12)

\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}\omega=\left(-\phi_{R}\zeta imkg+\frac{1}{\varrho}imkf\bar{\omega}_{R}^{\prime}\right)e^{imk\varphi},

where

(2.13)

\left(\partial_{\varrho\varrho}+\frac{1}{\varrho}\partial_{\varrho}-\frac{(mk)^{2}}{\varrho^{2}}\right)f=g\,.

By employing
[
1
, Lemma 4.12]
, one can see that

(2.14)

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\varrho\,d\varrho)}\lesssim_{mk}\|g\|_{L^{2}(\varrho\,d\varrho)}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{m}}\,.

We now demonstrate that

(2.15)

\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}\overset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}\bm{A}^{(k)}

in operator norm.
Let
n:=km\neq 0
, since the operators
\bm{A}^{(0)}
and
\bm{A}^{(0)}_{R}
are zero. For
\omega(x)=g(\varrho)e^{in\varphi}
and
f(\varrho)
as above, the difference between
\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}
and
\bm{A}^{(k)}
is

(2.16)

\left(\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}-\bm{A}^{(k)}\right)\omega=\left(-\zeta(\phi_{R}-1)ing+\frac{1}{\varrho}inf(\bar{\omega}_{R}^{\prime}-\bar{\omega}^{\prime})\right)e^{in\varphi}\,.

Since

(2.17)

\|\bar{u}^{\varphi}(\phi_{R}-1)\|_{L^{\infty}(\varrho\,d\varrho)}+\|\frac{1}{\varrho}(\bar{\omega}_{R}^{\prime}-\bar{\omega}^{\prime})\|_{L^{2}(\varrho\,d\varrho)}\overset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,

due to the cutoff
\phi_{R}
and the decay properties of
\bar{u}
and
\bar{\omega}
, the claimed conclusion (
2.15
) follows from (
2.14
) and Hölder’s inequality.

The unstable eigenvalue
\lambda_{\infty}
can be associated to a non-trivial eigenfunction
\eta
belonging to
U_{k_{0}}
for some
k_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
\bm{A}^{(0)}
is trivial, we must have that
k_{0}\neq 0
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\rho(\bm{A})\cap\{\Re\lambda>0\}
. For all
\lambda\in K
, we have

(2.18)

\displaystyle\lambda-\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}

\displaystyle=\lambda-\bm{A}^{(k)}+(\bm{A}^{(k)}-\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\bm{A}^{(k)})[I+R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)})(\bm{A}^{(k)}-\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})].

Due to (
2.15
), there exists
R_{0}>0
(which depends on
K
) such that, for all
R\geq R_{0}
,
\lambda-\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R}
is invertible, its inverse is

(2.19)

R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})=[I+R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)})(\bm{A}^{(k)}-\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})]^{-1}R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)}),

and

(2.20)

R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})\overset{R\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)})

in operator norm, uniformly on
K
.

Let
\vec{c}\subset\rho(\bm{A}^{(k_{0})})\cap\{\Re\lambda>0\}
be a counterclockwise, smooth, simple, closed curve containing
\lambda_{\infty}
in its interior and no other element of
\sigma(\bm{A}^{(k_{0})})
. Let
K=\vec{c}
. We define the spectral projection
\Pr_{R}^{(k_{0})}
onto
\lambda_{\infty}
according to

(2.21)

\Pr_{R}^{(k_{0})}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\vec{c}}R(\lambda,\bm{A}^{(k)}_{R})\,d\lambda,

and we define
\Pr^{(k_{0})}
similarly. The projection
\Pr^{(k_{0})}
is non-trivial. Therefore, since
\Pr^{(k_{0})}_{R}\to\Pr^{(k_{0})}
in operator norm as
R\to+\infty
, we must have that for all
R
sufficiently large,
\Pr^{(k_{0})}_{R}
is also non-trivial. Therefore,
\bm{A}^{(k_{0})}_{R}
must have an unstable eigenvalue, and so must
\bm{A}_{R}
. This completes the proof.
∎

For
\varepsilon=\Re\lambda_{\infty}/2
, we choose
\bar{R}\geq R_{0}
and define the truncated vortex
\widetilde{u}=\bar{u}_{\bar{R}}
and
\widetilde{\omega}=\curl\widetilde{u}
. Let us introduce the weighted space
L^{2}_{\gamma}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2};\gamma\,dr\,dz)
, without imposing
m
-fold symmetry, where
\gamma\geq 0
is a smooth weight satisfying

(2.22)

\gamma\equiv 1\text{ on }B_{\bar{R}},\quad\langle r,z\rangle^{100}\lesssim|\gamma|\lesssim\langle r,z\rangle^{100}\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{2}\,,

where
\langle\gamma,z\rangle^{2}:=\gamma^{2}+z^{2}+1
.
We now consider the operator
\bm{L}_{\infty}\colon D(\bm{L}_{\infty})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}
defined by

(2.23)

-\bm{L}_{\infty}:=\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla\omega+u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\omega}\,,

where
u={\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]
. Notice that
{\rm BS}_{2d}:L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2,\infty}\cap\dot{H}^{1}
is well defined and continuous since
L^{2}_{\gamma}
embeds into
L^{1}
. The domain of
\bm{L}_{\infty}
is

(2.24)

D(\bm{L}_{\infty}):=\{\omega\in L^{2}_{\gamma}:\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla\omega\in L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})\}\,.

Using that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\widetilde{u},\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}{\widetilde{\omega}}\subset B_{\bar{R}}
, it is not difficult to verify that the operator
\omega\mapsto u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\omega}
is compact. Hence, for the same reasons as before, the essential spectrum of
\bm{L}_{\infty}
is on the imaginary axis, and the remainder of the spectrum is discrete, consisting of countably many eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.

Corollary 2.3

(Instability in weighted space)
.

The operator
\bm{L}_{\infty}
has an unstable eigenvalue.

Proof.

Let
\eta
be a non-trivial unstable eigenfunction of
\bm{A}_{\bar{R}}
. Since
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}(\bm{A}_{\bar{R}}\eta)\subset B_{\bar{R}}
and
\lambda\eta+\bm{A}_{\bar{R}}\eta=0
, it follows that
\eta
is supported in
B_{\bar{R}}
. In particular
\eta\in L_{\gamma}
and
\lambda\eta+\bm{L}_{\infty}\eta=0
.
∎

2.3.
Axisymmetric instability without swirl

We now pass to the axisymmetric situation. We artificially place the two-dimensional vortex
\widetilde{u}(x,y)=\widetilde{u}^{x}e_{x}+\widetilde{u}^{y}e_{y}
into the axisymmetric-without-swirl coordinates
(r^{\prime},z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
. More often, we work with the variable
r=r^{\prime}-\ell\in\mathbb{R}_{>-\ell}
, where
\ell\geq 2\bar{R}
. We define

(2.25)

\widetilde{u}(r,z)=\widetilde{u}^{r}e_{r}+\widetilde{u}^{z}e_{z},

where, as functions on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\widetilde{u}^{r}(r,z)=\widetilde{u}^{x}(r,z)
and
\widetilde{u}^{z}(r,z)=\widetilde{u}^{y}(r,z)
.
So far,
\widetilde{u}
does not correspond to a
divergence-free
axisymmetric-no-swirl vector field, since the physical divergence of
\widetilde{u}
is

(2.26)

\div_{\ell}\widetilde{u}:=\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r+\ell}\right)\widetilde{u}^{r}+\partial_{z}\widetilde{u}^{z}=(r+\ell)^{-1}(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})\cdot\left[(r+\ell)\widetilde{u}\right].

Since
(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})\cdot\widetilde{u}=0
, there is an error of
(r+\ell)^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{r}
, which we correct below:

Lemma 2.4

(Correcting the divergence)
.

For each
\ell\geq 2\bar{R}
, there exists
v_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{\bar{R}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfying

(2.27)

\div_{\ell}\left(\widetilde{u}+v_{\ell}\right)=0

and

(2.28)

v_{\ell}\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\text{ in }C^{k}(B_{\bar{R}})\text{ for all }k\geq 0.

Proof.

We multiply (
2.27
) through by
r+\ell
to find that the correction
v_{\ell}
must solve

(2.29)

(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})\cdot[(r+\ell)v_{\ell}]=-\widetilde{u}^{r}.

The right-hand side belongs to
C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{\bar{R}})
and has zero mean due to the radial symmetry of
\widetilde{u}
:
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\widetilde{u}(x,y)\,dx\,dy=0
. Bogovskii’s operator
[
4
]
(see also Galdi’s book
[
22
, Section III.3]
) gives us a solution
(r+\ell)v_{\ell}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(B_{\bar{R}};\mathbb{R}^{2})
which is independent of
\ell
. The function
v_{\ell}
and its quantitative bounds are recovered after dividing by
r+\ell
. ∎

We define the corrected background as

(2.30)

\widetilde{u}_{\ell}=\widetilde{u}+v_{\ell},\quad\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}=\curl_{\ell}\widetilde{u}:=-\partial_{z}\widetilde{u}^{r}+\partial_{r}\widetilde{u}^{z}\,.

Here, we identify the
3d
curl of
\widetilde{u}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3})
with a scalar function
\curl_{\ell}\widetilde{u}
. This is possible because, as
\widetilde{u}
is axisymmetric without swirl, it holds

(2.31)

\curl_{3d}\widetilde{u}=-\curl_{\ell}\widetilde{u}\,e_{\theta}\,.

Notice the sign convention for the swirl component we introduced above (
1.13
).

Consider the linearized axisymmetric-no-swirl Euler operator around the background
\widetilde{u}_{\ell}
:

(2.32)

-\bm{L}_{\ell}\omega:=\widetilde{u}_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\omega+u\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}-\frac{\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{r}}{r+\ell}\omega-\frac{u^{r}}{r+\ell}\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell},\quad u={\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega],

where here and in this subsection
\nabla=(\partial_{r},\partial_{z})
and
u={\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]
is defined by

(2.33)

\partial_{r}^{2}\psi+\frac{1}{r+\ell}\partial_{r}\psi-\frac{1}{(r+\ell)^{2}}\psi+\partial_{z}^{2}\psi=\omega\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}_{>-\ell},\quad\partial_{n}\psi|_{r=-\ell}=0

(2.34)

u=-\partial_{z}\psi\,e_{r}+\left(\partial_{r}+\frac{1}{r+\ell}\right)\psi\,e_{z}.

We emphasize here that
\omega
is regarded as minus the swirl component of the vorticity and
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\widetilde{u}_{\ell},\,\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\subset B_{\bar{R}}
and

2.3.1.
Functional analytic set-up

We consider the space

(2.35)

L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{r>-\ell};\gamma\,dr\,dz)\,,

where
\gamma
is defined in (
2.22
).
We define the operator
\bm{L}_{\ell}\colon D(\bm{L}_{\ell})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\to L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}
with domain

(2.36)

D(\bm{L}_{\ell}):=\{\omega\in L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}:\widetilde{u}_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\omega\in L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})\}

according to

(2.37)

\bm{L}_{\ell}=\bm{M}_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\ell}+\bm{S}_{\ell}\,,

where

(2.38)

-\bm{M}_{\ell}\omega:=\widetilde{u}_{\ell}\cdot\nabla\omega+\frac{1}{2}(\div_{2d}v_{\ell})\omega\,,

(2.39)

-\bm{K}_{\ell}\omega:={\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\,,

(2.40)

-\bm{S}_{\ell}\omega:=-(r+\ell)^{-1}{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]^{r}\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}-(r+\ell)^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{r}_{\ell}\omega-\frac{1}{2}(\div_{2d}v_{\ell})\omega\,.

It is not hard to see (Lemma
2.5
below) the mapping properties

(2.41)

\bm{M}_{\ell}:D(\bm{L}_{\ell})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\to L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\,,

and

(2.42)

\bm{K}_{\ell}\,,\bm{S}_{\ell}:L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\to L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\,.

The naming convention is as follows:
\bm{M}_{\ell}
is the ‘main term’, which is also skew-adjoint with the above choice of domain;
\bm{K}_{\ell}
is ‘compact’; and
\bm{S}_{\ell}
is ‘small’.
To show that
\bm{M}_{\ell}
is skew-adjoint we use that
\gamma=1
on the support of
\widetilde{u}_{\ell}
and
\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}
.

Lemma 2.5

(Basic properties)
.

The operators
\bm{K}_{\ell},\bm{S}_{\ell}:L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\to L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}
are bounded.
Moreover,
\bm{K}_{\ell}
is compact, and

(2.43)

\|\bm{S}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}}\to 0\text{ as }\ell\to+\infty\,.

Since
\bar{R}
is fixed, it will be convenient to omit the dependence of the implied constants on
\bar{R}
in the remainder of the paper.

Proof.

Let us begin by proving (
2.43
).
Let
\omega
be a function supported on
{\{r>-\ell\}}
.
By (
2.29
), we have that

\div_{2d}v_{\ell}=-(r+\ell)^{-1}v_{\ell}^{r}-(r+\ell)^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{r}
and, by the boundedness of
\widetilde{u}^{r}
and since
v_{\ell}^{r}
goes to
0
in
L^{\infty}
, we deduce that

\div_{2d}v_{\ell}
goes to
0
in
L^{\infty}
.
Using also that
\widetilde{u}^{r}_{\ell}
is supported in
B_{\bar{R}}
, we estimate

(2.44)

\begin{split}\left\|(r+\ell)^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{r}_{\ell}\omega+\frac{1}{2}(\div_{2d}v_{\ell})\omega\right\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}}&=\left\|(r+\ell)^{-1}\widetilde{u}^{r}_{\ell}\omega+\frac{1}{2}(\div_{2d}v_{\ell})\omega\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\\
&\leq\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{\ell-\bar{R}}\|\widetilde{u}^{r}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|\div_{2d}v_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)}_{\to 0\text{ as }\ell\to+\infty}\|{\omega}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}}\,.\end{split}

The standard estimate for the Biot-Savart kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, read on axisymmetric functions, gives us that

(2.45)

\|{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{6}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\lesssim\|\omega\|_{L^{2}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\lesssim\ell^{1/2}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}(\gamma\,dr\,dz)}\,.

Hence, we estimate

(2.46)

\begin{split}\|(r+\ell)^{-1}{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]^{r}\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}}&=\|(r+\ell)^{-1}{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]^{r}\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell-\bar{R}}\|{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{6}(B_{\bar{R}})}\|\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{3}(B_{\bar{R}})}\\
&\lesssim\ell^{-5/6}\|{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{6}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\|\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{3}(B_{\bar{R}})}\\
&\lesssim\ell^{-1/3}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}}\|\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{3}(B_{\bar{R}})}\,.\end{split}

Recalling that
\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}
is bounded independently on
\ell
, putting together (
2.44
), (
2.46
) and (
2.45
), we obtain (
2.43
).

To prove that
\bm{K}_{\ell}
is compact, it is enough to check that

(2.47)

\|{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{6}(B_{\bar{R}})}+\|\nabla{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\lesssim_{\ell}1\,.

The
\dot{H}^{1}
estimate follows from the fact that
\omega\in L^{2}
, while the
L^{6}
estimate is a consequence of (
2.45
).
∎

With these properties in mind, we can say the following: The essential spectrum of
\bm{L}_{\ell}
is contained in a half-plane
\{\Re\lambda<o_{\ell\to+\infty}(1)\}
, outside of which the spectrum is discrete, consisting of countably many eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity. We always consider
\ell
large enough so that the
o(1)
term above is
\leq 1/2
.

2.3.2.
Main result

We are now ready to state our main instability result for the operator
\bm{L}_{\ell}
. The proof is given at the end of this section.

Proposition 2.6

(Axisymmetric instability)
.

Let
\lambda_{\infty}
be an unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{L}_{\infty}
.
For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\Re\lambda_{\infty})
, there exists
\ell_{0}\geq 2\bar{R}
, depending on
\widetilde{u}
,
\varepsilon
, and
\lambda_{\infty}
, such that for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the operator
\bm{L}_{\ell}
has an unstable eigenvalue
\lambda_{\ell}
with
|\lambda_{\ell}-\lambda_{\infty}|<\varepsilon
.

It will be convenient to consider all operators on the single function space
L^{2}_{\gamma}
rather than
L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}
. Define the projection operator
P_{\ell}:L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}
by restriction:

(2.48)

P_{\ell}\omega=\omega|_{r>-\ell}\,.

We consider the operator

(2.49)

\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}=\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell}:L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}\,,

and observe that
for
\ell\geq 2\bar{R}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
,

(2.50)

\lambda\in\sigma(\bm{L}_{\ell})\qquad\mbox{if and only if}\qquad\lambda\in\sigma(\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}).

Indeed, if we make the identification

(2.51)

L^{2}_{\gamma}=L^{2}(\{r<-\ell\}\times\mathbb{R},\,\gamma\,dr\,dz)\oplus L^{2}_{\gamma,\ell}\,,\quad\omega=((I-P_{\ell})\omega,P_{\ell}\omega)=(f,g)\,,

the operator
\lambda+\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}
acts as
(f,g)\mapsto(\lambda f,\lambda g+\bm{L}_{\ell}g)
. Hence,
\lambda+\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}
is invertible if and only if
\lambda+\bm{L}_{\ell}
is invertible. This implies (
2.50
).

We introduce the operators

(2.52)

\bm{K}_{\infty}:L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}\,,\qquad-\bm{K}_{\infty}\omega:={\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]\cdot\nabla\widetilde{\omega}\,,

(2.53)

\bm{M}_{\infty}:D(\bm{M}_{\infty})\subset L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}\,,\qquad-\bm{M}_{\infty}\omega:=\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla\omega\,,

where
D(\bm{M}_{\infty})=\{\omega\in L^{2}_{\gamma}:\widetilde{u}\cdot\nabla\omega\in L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})\}
.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
K
be a compact subset of
\rho(\bm{M}_{\infty}+\bm{K}_{\infty})\cap\{\Re\lambda>0\}
. There exists
\ell_{0}\geq 2\bar{R}
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, the operator
\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty}
, acting on
L^{2}_{\gamma}
, is invertible for all
\lambda\in K
, and

(2.54)

R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\infty})f\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty}+\bm{K}_{\infty})f\quad\text{ in }L^{2}_{\gamma}\,,

for all
f\in L^{2}_{\gamma}
, uniformly on
K
.

Proof.

First, we show that

(2.55)

R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})f\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})f\quad\text{ for all }f\in L^{2}_{\gamma}

locally uniformly in
\{\Re\lambda>0\}
.
To do so, we employ the identity

(2.56)

[R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})]f(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda t}(f(X^{\ell}_{t}(x))-f(X_{t}(x)))dt\,,

where
X^{\ell}
and
X
are the flow maps associated to, respectively,
\widetilde{u}_{\ell}
and
\widetilde{u}
. Since
X_{t}^{\ell}(x)=X_{t}(x)=x
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus B_{\bar{R}}
, it holds

(2.57)

\|[R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})]f\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\Re\lambda t}\|f(X_{t}^{\ell})-f(X_{t})\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}dt\,.

The claimed conclusion (
2.55
) follows from a simple approximation argument and the fact that

X_{t}^{\ell}\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}X_{t}
in
C^{1}
as a consequence of Lemma
2.4
.

We now show that

(2.58)

I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})\bm{K}_{\infty}

in operator norm,
locally uniformly in
\{\Re\lambda>0\}
.

To this aim we use that
\bm{K}_{\infty}
is a compact operator on the separable Hilbert space
L^{2}_{\gamma}
; hence, it can be approximated in operator norm by a sequence
(F_{k})
of finite-rank operators on
L^{2}_{\gamma}
.
Due to (
2.55
), we have that

(2.59)

R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})F_{k}\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})F_{k},

in operator norm,
locally uniformly in
\{\Re\lambda>0\}
,
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Therefore, (
2.58
) follows from a simple diagonal argument.

From (
2.58
) it follows that there exists
\ell_{0}\geq 2\bar{R}
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, we have that
\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty}
is invertible, and

(2.60)

R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\infty})=[I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}]^{-1}R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\,.

Indeed, the identity

(2.61)

\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty}=(\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})[I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}]\,,

tells us that
\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty}
is invertible if and only if
I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}
is invertible. When
\lambda\in K
, the operator on the right-hand side of (
2.58
) is invertible, and its inverse depends continuously on
\lambda
; therefore, when
\ell
is sufficently large, we have that
I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}
is invertible,

To conclude the proof of (
2.55
) it remains only to show that

(2.62)

[I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\bm{K}_{\infty}]^{-1}\overset{\ell\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}[I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})\bm{K}_{\infty}]^{-1}\,,

which again is a consequence of (
2.58
) and the invertibility of
I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty})\bm{K}_{\infty}
.

∎

Proof of Proposition
2.6
.

Thanks to (
2.50
) and the fact that
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\ell})
in
\{\Re\lambda>o_{\ell\to+\infty}(1)\}
consists of eigenvalues with finite multiplicity, it is enough to show that
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\cap\{\Re\lambda>o_{\ell\to+\infty}(1)\}
is non-empty.

For any
\lambda\in\rho(\bm{M}_{\infty}+\bm{K}_{\infty})\cap\{\Re\lambda>0\}
we decompose the operator
\lambda-\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}
as

(2.63)

\displaystyle\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty}-\underbrace{\bm{K}_{\infty}(P_{\ell}-I)}_{=:{\rm I}_{\ell}}-\underbrace{(\bm{K}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty})P_{\ell}}_{=:{\rm II}_{\ell}}-\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell}

\displaystyle=(\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty})[I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\infty})({\rm I}_{\ell}+{\rm II}_{\ell}+\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell})]

on
D(\bm{L}_{\ell})
when
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
, see Lemma
2.7
. We argue below that
{\rm I}_{\ell}
and
{\rm II}_{\ell}
converge to zero as
\ell\to+\infty
(it is already known that
\bm{S}_{\ell}
does so, see Lemma
2.5
). The consequence is that
I-R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}+\bm{K}_{\infty})({\rm I}_{\ell}+{\rm II}_{\ell}+\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell})
, hence,
\lambda-\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell}
, will be invertible when
\ell
is large enough.

1. Term
{\rm I}_{\ell}
. Let us show that

(2.64)

\|\bm{K}_{\infty}(P_{\ell}-I)\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}}\to 0\qquad\mbox{as }\ell\to+\infty\,.

For any
\omega\in L^{2}_{\gamma}
, it holds

\displaystyle\|\bm{K}_{\infty}(P_{\ell}-I)\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\|\bm{K}_{\infty}(P_{\ell}-I)\omega\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}

\displaystyle\leq\|{\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega 1_{\{r<-\ell\}}]\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\|\nabla\widetilde{\omega}_{\ell}\|_{L^{\infty}}\,.

The second factor is bounded since
\widetilde{\omega}
is smooth with bounded support and
\curl v_{\ell}
and its derivatives are bounded by Lemma
2.4
. As regards the first term, since
{\rm BS}_{2d}
sends
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
to
L^{q^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
q\in(1,2)
, by Hölder’s inequality, we have for any fixed
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
that

(2.65)

\begin{split}\|{\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega 1_{\{r<-\ell\}}]\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|\omega 1_{\{r<-\ell\}}\|_{L^{1+\varepsilon}}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}(\gamma\,dr\,dz)}\|\langle r,z\rangle^{-50(1+\varepsilon)}1_{\{r<-\ell\}}\|_{L^{\frac{2}{1-\varepsilon}}}^{\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}}\,.\end{split}

Notice that the second factor on the right-hand side is
o_{\ell\to+\infty}(1)
.

2. Term
{\rm II}_{\ell}
.
We show that

(2.66)

\|(\bm{K}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\infty})P_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}}\to 0\quad\text{ as }\ell\to+\infty.

Let
\omega
be a function supported on
{\{r>-\ell\}}
.
It is enough to prove that

(2.67)

\|{\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]-{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\leq C(\ell)\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,,

where
C(\ell)=o_{\ell\to+\infty}(1)
.

Recall that

(2.68)

{\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]=-\partial_{z}\psi\,e_{r}+\partial_{r}\psi\,e_{z}\,,

(2.69)

{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]=-\partial_{z}\psi_{\ell}\,e_{r}+\partial_{r}\psi_{\ell}\,e_{z}+\frac{1}{r+\ell}\psi_{\ell}\,e_{z}\,,

where
\psi
,
\psi_{\ell}
are the stream functions associated to
\omega
; they satisfy

(2.70)

\Delta_{\ell}\psi_{\ell}=\left(\partial_{r}^{2}+\frac{1}{r+\ell}\partial_{r}-\frac{1}{(r+\ell)^{2}}+\partial_{z}^{2}\right)\psi_{\ell}=\omega\,,\qquad\partial_{n}\psi_{\ell}|_{r=-\ell}=0\,,

(2.71)

\Delta\psi=(\partial_{r}^{2}+\partial_{z}^{2})\psi=\omega\,.

In particular, we have

(2.72)

\|{\rm BS}_{2d}[\omega]-{\rm BS}_{\ell}[\omega]\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\leq\|(r+\ell)^{-1}\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}+\|\nabla(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}=:I+II\,.

We now estimate
I
and
II
separately.

Let us begin by studying
I
.
We recall the standard Young convolution inequality applied to the Biot-Savart kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
gives

(2.73)

\begin{split}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{q}(({r+\ell})\,dr\,dz)}+&\|(\partial_{r}+(r+\ell)^{-1},\partial_{z})\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{p^{*}}((r+\ell)drdz)}\\
&\lesssim_{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\lesssim_{p}\ell^{1/p}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

for any
p\in(1,3/2)
, where
1/p^{*}=1/p-1/3
and
1/q=1/p-2/3
.

To bound
I
, it is enough to use Hölder’s inequality and (
2.73
) with
p=6/5
and
q=6
. Indeed,

(2.74)

\begin{split}\|(r+\ell)^{-1}\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}&\lesssim\|(r+\ell)^{-1}\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{6}(B_{\bar{R}})}\lesssim\ell^{-1}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{6}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\\
&\lesssim\ell^{-1/6}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

Let us now study
II
.

(2.75)

\|\psi\|_{\dot{C}^{2-2/p}}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{p^{*}}(dr\,dz)}\lesssim_{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{p}(dr\,dz)}\lesssim_{p}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}

for any
p\in(1,2)
, where
1/p^{*}=1/p-1/2
.
Notice that
\psi
is only defined up to a constant; here and in the following, we assume that
\psi(0)=0
, which implies

(2.76)

\|\psi\|_{L^{q}(B_{R})}\lesssim_{p}R^{2+\frac{2}{q}-\frac{2}{p}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,\qquad\text{for any $R>1$, $q\geq 1$, $p\in(1,2)$}\,.

Let us denote by
\chi_{\ell}
a standard cutoff which is
1
on
B_{\ell/4}
and
0
outside
B_{\ell/2}
, with
|\nabla\chi_{\ell}|\lesssim\ell^{-1}
and
|\nabla^{2}\chi_{\ell}|\lesssim\ell^{-2}
. Here,
\ell\geq 8\bar{R}
.
We write an equation for
(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\chi_{\ell}
by using the equations for
\psi_{\ell}
and
\psi
. We obtain

(2.77)

\Delta[(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\chi_{\ell}]=\frac{1}{(r+\ell)^{2}}\psi_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}-\frac{1}{r+\ell}\partial_{r}\psi_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}+2\nabla(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\ell}+(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\Delta\chi_{\ell}\,.

By using Hölder’s inequality and the Sobolev embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we deduce

(2.78)

\|\nabla(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\|_{L^{2}(B_{\bar{R}})}\lesssim\|\nabla[(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\chi_{\ell}]\|_{L^{6}}\lesssim\|\Delta[(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\chi_{\ell}]\|_{L^{3/2}}\,,

and (
2.77
) implies

(2.79)

\begin{split}&\|\Delta[(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\chi_{\ell}]\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\\
&=\|(r+\ell)^{-2}\psi_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}-(r+\ell)^{-1}\partial_{r}\psi_{\ell}\chi_{\ell}+2\nabla(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\cdot\nabla\chi_{\ell}+(\psi_{\ell}-\psi)\Delta\chi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(B_{\ell/2})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\big(\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}+\|\psi\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\big)\\
&\qquad\qquad+\frac{1}{\ell}\big(\|(\partial_{r}+(r+\ell)^{-1},\partial_{z})\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(B_{\ell/2})}+\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(B_{\ell/2})}\big)\,.\end{split}

By using Hölder’s inequality and (
2.73
) we estimate the first term:

(2.80)

\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell}\|\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{4}((r+\ell)\,dr\,dz)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{1}{12}}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.

To estimate the second term we employ (
2.76
):

(2.81)

\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{1/2}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.

We apply (
2.73
) to the third term: For
\varepsilon>0
small enough, it holds

(2.82)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\ell}\|(\partial_{r}+(r+\ell)^{-1},\partial_{z})\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}}(B_{\ell/2})}&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{9/10}}\|(\partial_{r}+(r+\ell)^{-1},\partial_{z})\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(B_{\ell/2})}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{4/3}}\|(\partial_{r}+(r+\ell)^{-1},\partial_{z})\psi_{\ell}\|_{L^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}((r+\ell)drdz)}\\
&\lesssim_{\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\ell^{1/3}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.\end{split}

To estimate the last term, we use Hölder’s inequality and (
2.75
):

(2.83)

\frac{1}{\ell}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{3/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{7}{15}}}\|\nabla\psi\|_{L^{5/2}(B_{\ell/2})}\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{\frac{7}{15}}}\|\omega\|_{L^{2}_{\gamma}}\,.

3. Conclusion
. From the above arguments and Lemma
2.7
, we conclude the following. Let
K
be a compact subset of
\rho(\bm{M}_{\infty}+\bm{K}_{\infty})\cap\{\Re\lambda>0\}
. There exists
\ell_{1}\geq\ell_{0}
such that, for all
\ell\geq\ell_{1}
, the operator
\lambda-\bm{M}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{K}_{\ell}P_{\ell}-\bm{S}_{\ell}P_{\ell}
is invertible on
L^{2}_{\gamma}
, and its inverse converges to
R(\lambda,\bm{M}_{\infty}+\bm{K}_{\infty})
pointwise, uniformly on
K
, as
\ell\to+\infty
.

Let
\vec{c}\subset B_{\varepsilon}(\ell_{\infty})
be a counterclockwise, smooth, simple, closed curve containing
\lambda_{\infty}
in its interior and no other element of
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\infty})
. Let
K=\vec{c}
. When
\ell\geq\ell_{1}
, we may define the spectral projections
\Pr_{\ell}:L^{2}_{\gamma}\to L^{2}_{\gamma}
according to

(2.84)

\Pr_{\ell}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{\vec{c}}R(\lambda,\bm{L}_{\ell}P_{\ell})\,d\lambda,

and analogously
\Pr_{\infty}
. In particular, the integrand is well defined when
\ell\geq\ell_{1}
. Then
\Pr_{\ell}f\to\Pr_{\infty}f
for all
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as
k\to+\infty
. In particular, since
\Pr_{\infty}
is non-trivial, we must have that
\Pr_{\ell}
is non-trivial for all sufficiently large
\ell
. ∎

3.
Linear instability: from Euler to Navier-Stokes equations

For sufficiently large
\ell
(which will be fixed momentarily), we define
\bar{U}
to be the axisymmetric-no-swirl velocity field on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
associated with
\widetilde{u}_{\ell}
:

(3.1)

\bar{U}(x)=\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{r}(r+\ell,z)e_{r}+\widetilde{u}_{\ell}^{z}(r+\ell,z)e_{z},

where
x=(x^{\prime},z)
and
r=|x^{\prime}|
. Let
L^{2}_{\rm aps}
denote the set of
L^{2}
-integrable ‘axisymmetric pure-swirl’ vector fields
\Omega=\Omega^{\theta}(r,z)e_{\theta}
.

In this section, we concern ourselves with instability for the operator

(3.2)

\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}:D(\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor})\subset L^{2}_{\rm aps}\to L^{2}_{\rm aps}

(3.3)

-\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}=(-1-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi})\Omega-\Delta\Omega+\beta[\bar{U},\Omega]+\beta[U,\bar{\Omega}],

where
U={\rm BS}_{3d}[\Omega]
. Its domain is

(3.4)

D(\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}):=\{\Omega\in L^{2}_{\rm aps}:\Omega\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\Omega\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}\,.

We further introduce the operators comprising
\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}
:

(3.5)

-\bm{D}\Omega:=-\frac{3}{4}\Omega-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\Omega-\Delta\Omega\,

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\bm{M}\Omega

\displaystyle:=\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Omega

(3.7)

-\bm{S}\Omega:=-\bar{\Omega}\cdot\nabla U-\Omega\cdot\nabla\bar{U}

(3.8)

\displaystyle-\bm{K}\Omega

\displaystyle:=({\rm BS}_{3d}[\Omega]\cdot\nabla)\bar{\Omega}\,.

The naming convention is as follows:
\bm{D}
is the ‘dissipation’,
\bm{M}
is the ‘main term’,
\bm{S}
arises from the ‘stretching’ and is ‘small’, and
\bm{K}
is easily verified to be ‘compact’. Notice that
\bm{D}-\Delta
is a skew-symmetric operator; this will play a role in Lemma
3.3
.

Finally, we define

(3.9)

\bm{T}_{\beta}:=\frac{1}{\beta}\bm{D}+\bm{M}+\bm{S}+\bm{K}=\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}-\frac{1}{4}\right).

In analogy with
\bm{T}_{\beta}
we introduce

(3.10)

\bm{T}_{\infty}:=\bm{M}+\bm{S}+\bm{K}\,,

whose domain is
D(\bm{T}_{\infty})=\{\Omega\in L^{2}_{\rm aps}:\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Omega\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}
.

Let

(3.11)

a:=\sup_{\lambda\in\sigma(\bm{T}_{\infty})}\Re\lambda,\quad\mu:=\|S\|_{L^{2}_{\rm aps}\to L^{2}}.

The above constants depend on
\ell
, which we fix momentarily to ensure that

(3.12)

a>\mu.

From Proposition
2.6
, we know that
a>\text{const}.
for all sufficiently large
\ell
. On the other hand, we now justify that
\mu\to 0
as
\ell\to+\infty
. Using the axisymmetric-pure-swirl structure to estimate
\bm{S}
, we have

(3.13)

(\bm{S}\Omega)^{\theta}=\frac{1}{r}\bar{\Omega}^{\theta}U^{r}+\frac{1}{r}\Omega^{\theta}\bar{U}^{r}.

Since
\bar{U}
is compactly supported and, specifically,
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\bar{U}\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|r-\ell|^{2}+|z|^{2}\leq\bar{R}^{2}\}
, we have by the Calderón–Zygmund estimates that

(3.14)

\begin{split}\|\bm{S}\Omega\|_{L^{2}}&\leq\left\|\frac{1}{r}\bar{\Omega}^{\theta}U^{r}\right\|_{L^{2}(r\,dr\,dz)}+\left\|\frac{1}{r}\Omega^{\theta}\bar{U}^{r}\right\|_{L^{2}(r\,dr\,dz)}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell}\|U^{r}\|_{L^{6}(r\,dr\,dz)}\|\bar{\Omega}^{\theta}\|_{L^{3}(r\,dr\,dz)}+\frac{1}{\ell}\|\bar{U}^{r}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Omega^{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(r\,dr\,dz)}\\
&\lesssim\frac{1}{\ell^{2/3}}\|\Omega^{\theta}\|_{L^{2}(r\,dr\,dz)},\end{split}

where the implicit constant may depend on
\|\bar{\Omega}^{\theta}\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

From now on, we consider
\ell
and the background
\bar{U}
as fixed and satisfying (
3.12
).

Theorem 3.1

(Self-similar Navier-Stokes instability: vorticity)
.

Let
\lambda_{\infty}
be an unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{T}_{\infty}
with
\Re\lambda_{\infty}>\mu
. For all
\varepsilon\in(0,\Re\lambda_{\infty}-\mu)
, there exists
\beta_{0}>0
such that, for all
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
,

\bm{T}_{\beta}
has an unstable eigenvalue
\lambda_{\beta}
satisfying
|\lambda_{\beta}-\lambda_{\infty}|<\varepsilon
, and
\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vor}
has an unstable eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\beta}
defined by

(3.15)

\widetilde{\lambda}_{\beta}=\beta\lambda_{\beta}+\frac{1}{4}.

To prove nonlinear instability in Section
4
, it will be more convenient to work with the velocity formulation. Therefore, we introduce

(3.16)

\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}:D(\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel})\subset L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\to L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})

(3.17)

-\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}U:=(-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi})U-\Delta U+\mathbb{P}(\bar{U}\cdot\nabla U+U\cdot\nabla\bar{U})

whose domain is

(3.18)

D(\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}):=\{U\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3}):U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}U\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}\,.

Here,
L^{p}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is the space of divergence-free
L^{p}
-integrable vector fields on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.
Notice that we do not impose axisymmetry on
\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}
.

Corollary 3.2

(Self-similar Navier-Stokes instability: velocity)
.

For
\beta\geq\beta_{0}
, the unstable eigenvalue
\widetilde{\lambda}_{\beta}
in Theorem
3.1
is also an unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}
.

The operator
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
in the next section will be defined as
\bm{L}^{(\beta)}_{\rm vel}
for sufficiently large
\beta
.

Before we prove Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, we prove two preliminary lemmas.

Lemma 3.3
.

For all
\beta>0
and
\lambda\in\{\Re\lambda>\mu\}
, we have

(3.19)

\|R(\lambda,\beta^{-1}\bm{D}+\bm{M}+\bm{S})\|_{L^{2}_{\rm aps}\to L^{2}_{\rm aps}}\leq(\Re\lambda-\mu)^{-1},

and, for all
\Omega\in L^{2}_{\rm aps}
, we have

(3.20)

R(\lambda,\beta^{-1}\bm{D}+\bm{M}+\bm{S})\Omega\overset{\beta\to+\infty}{\longrightarrow}R(\lambda,\bm{M}+\bm{S})\Omega\text{ in }L^{2}

locally uniformly in
\{\Re\lambda>\mu\}
.

From (
3.19
) and the compactness of
\bm{K}
, we know that the essential spectrum of
\bm{T}_{\beta}
is contained in the left half-plane
\{\Re\lambda\leq\mu\}
, and the remainder of its spectrum is discrete, consisting of isolated eigenvalues of finite algebraic multiplicity.

Proof.

Consider the advection-diffusion equation

(3.21)

\partial_{\tau}\Omega^{\beta}-\frac{1}{\beta}(\Delta+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi})\Omega^{\beta}+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Omega^{\beta}-\bm{S}\Omega^{\beta}=0

and its inviscid counterpart

(3.22)

\partial_{\tau}\Omega+\bar{U}\cdot\nabla\Omega-\bm{S}\Omega=0

supplemented with the initial condition

\Omega^{\beta}(\cdot,0)=\Omega(\cdot,0)=\Omega_{0}\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{2}_{\rm aps}
. The basic energy estimate for solutions to (

(3.23)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|\Omega^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}\int|\nabla\Omega^{\beta}|^{2}\,dx\leq\|\bm{S}\|_{L^{2}\to L^{2}}\|\Omega^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\mu\|\Omega^{\beta}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

Therefore, we have

(3.24)

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{d\tau}\|e^{\tau\mu}\Omega^{\beta}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\beta}\int|\nabla e^{\tau\mu}\Omega^{\beta}(\cdot,\tau)|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

Hence,

(3.25)

The growth estimate (
3.25

R(\lambda,\beta^{-1}\bm{D}+\bm{M}+\bm{S})\Omega_{0}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s\lambda}\Omega^{\beta}(\cdot,s)\,ds

is well defined and satisfies the desired estimate (
3.19

\Re\lambda>\mu

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}F:=\frac{1}{\beta}(\Delta+\frac{3}{4}+\frac{\xi}{2}\cdot\nabla_{\xi})\Omega,

4.
Nonlinear instability

In this section, we demonstrate how to use the linear instability proven in Corollary
3.2
to construct non-unique solutions to the forced Navier-Stokes equations.

We employ the uppercase/lowercase notation introduced in (
1.8
) for functions in similarity/physical variables, respectively. Recall that
L^{p}_{\sigma}
is the space of divergence-free vector fields in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Theorem 4.1

(Non-uniqueness criterion)
.

Let
\bar{U}
be a compactly supported, smooth, divergence-free vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. Suppose that
(A)
in Theorem
1.3
holds, that is, the linearized operator
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}\colon D(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\subset L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{\sigma}
defined by

(4.1)

-\bm{L}_{\rm ss}U=-\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\xi\cdot\nabla_{\xi}\right)U-\Delta U+\mathbb{P}(\bar{U}\cdot\nabla U+U\cdot\nabla\bar{U})\,,

where
D(\bm{L}_{\rm ss}):=\{U\in L^{2}_{\sigma}:U\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\xi\cdot\nabla U\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\}
,
has an unstable eigenvalue
\lambda
. Then
\lambda
can be chosen to be maximally unstable, that is,
\Re\lambda=\sup_{z\in\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})}\Re z>0
, and moreover,
(B)
in Theorem
1.3
holds.

As mentioned above (
1.11
), a second solution to (
NS
) is sought as a trajectory on the unstable manifold of
\bar{U}
associated to the most unstable eigenvalues of
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
. We make the following ansatz:

(4.2)

U=\bar{U}+U^{\rm lin}+U^{\rm per},

where

(4.3)

U^{\rm lin}(\cdot,\tau)=\Re(e^{\lambda\tau}\eta)\,

is a solution of the linearized PDE
\partial_{\tau}U^{\rm lin}=\bm{L}_{\rm ss}U^{\rm lin}
,

\lambda=a+ib
is an unstable eigenvalue of
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
with maximal growth rate
a>0
, and
\eta\in L^{2}_{\sigma}
is a non-trivial eigenfunction associated to
\lambda
. The remainder
U^{\rm per}
, whose equation is (
4.17
) below, decays in
L^{2}
as
o(e^{\tau a})
when
\tau\to-\infty
. The additional decay ensures that
U
is not identical to
\bar{U}
and, moreover, guarantees non-uniqueness.

In Section
4.2
, we employ a fixed point argument to solve (
4.17
) and construct

. First, we introduce the semigroup generated by
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
.

4.1.
The linear operator

In this section we study
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
, the spectrum of the closed, densely defined operator
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}:D(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\subset L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{\sigma}
, and the associated semigroup
e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}:L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{\sigma}
.
The latter is well defined and strongly continuous, as proven in
[
33
, Section 2]
.

We define the
spectral bound
of
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}
as

(4.4)

s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss}):=\sup\{\Re\lambda\,:\,\lambda\in\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\}\,,

which is bounded by the
growth bound

(4.5)

\omega_{0}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss}):=\inf\{\omega\in\mathbb{R}\,:\,\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\|_{L^{2}_{\sigma}\to L^{2}_{\sigma}}\leq M(\omega)e^{\tau\omega}\}

of the semigroup. We refer the reader to
[
21
, Proposition 2.2]
for the proof of the inequality
s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\leq\omega_{0}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
. As a consequence of
[
34
, Corollary 2.11]
, in our generality, we have the following.

Lemma 4.2
.

It holds
\omega_{0}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})=\max\{\omega_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss}),s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\}
, where

(4.6)

\omega_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss}):=\inf_{\tau>0}\frac{1}{\tau}\log\left(\inf\{\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}-K\|_{O}\,:\,K\,\,{\rm is\,\,compact}\}\right).

Moreover, for every
w>\omega_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
, the set
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\cap\{\Re\lambda>w\}
is finite, and the corresponding spectral projection has finite rank.

In other words, the identity
s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})=\omega_{0}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
holds up to taking into account contributions coming from the essential spectrum.

Applying
[
33
, Lemma 2.7]
, we have that
\omega_{\rm ess}(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\leq-1/4
, and hence the following result holds.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume
a:=s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})>0
. Then
a<\infty
, and there exist
\lambda=a+ib\in\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
and
\eta=\eta_{1}+i\eta_{2}
such that
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in D(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})
and
\bm{L}_{\rm ss}\eta=\lambda\eta
.

Moreover, for any
\delta>0
, it holds

(4.7)

\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\leq M(\delta)e^{\tau(a+\delta)}\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\,,\qquad\forall\,U_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\,.

A more elementary way to prove that
\sigma(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})\cap\{\Re\lambda\geq\mu\}
is countable for all

was already mentioned in Section
2.1
, since
U\mapsto\mathbb{P}(\bar{U}\cdot\nabla U+U\cdot\nabla\bar{U})
is relatively compact with respect to

[
33
, Section 2]
. Proposition

contains more information, and in particular, the growth estimate (

We conclude this section by proving that

enjoys parabolic regularity estimates with sharp growth at infinity.

Lemma 4.4
.

Assume
a=s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})>0
. Then, for any
\sigma_{2}\geq\sigma_{1}\geq 0
and
\delta>0
, it holds

(4.8)

\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}U_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma_{2}}}\leq\frac{M(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\delta)}{\tau^{(\sigma_{2}-\sigma_{1})/2}}e^{\tau(a+\delta)}\|U_{0}\|_{H^{\sigma_{1}}}\,,

for any
U_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap H^{\sigma_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

Proof.

It suffices to treat the case when
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}
are non-negative integers. The general case follows by interpolation.

We divide the proof into two steps. First, we show smoothing estimates (
4.8
) for small times, say,
0<\tau\leq 2
, and afterward we address the long-time behaviour.

Step 1
. For any
0\leq m\leq k
, it holds

(4.9)

\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}U_{0}\|_{H^{k}}\leq M(k,m)\tau^{-\frac{k-m}{2}}\|U_{0}\|_{H^{m}}\,,\quad\forall\,U_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}\cap H^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),\,\,\tau\in(0,2)\,.

To ease notation, we set
U(\cdot,\tau):=e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}U_{0}
.
We study the problem in physical variables: Setting

(4.10)

u(x,t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}}U\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t+1}},\log(t+1)\right)\,,\quad\bar{u}(x,t):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t+1}}\bar{U}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{t+1}}\right)\,,

we have the equation

(4.11)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}u-\Delta u&=-\mathbb{P}(\bar{u}\cdot\nabla u+u\cdot\nabla\bar{u})&&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,e^{2}-1)\\

Using that

\|u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}+t^{1/2}\|\nabla u(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\bar{U})\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\,,\qquad t\in(0,10)\,,

which gives

(4.13)

\|U(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}}+\tau^{1/2}\|\nabla U(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\bar{U})\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\,,\qquad\tau\in(0,2)\,.

The latter implies (

Step 2
. For any
\delta>0

\|e^{\tau\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}U_{0}\|_{H^{k}}\leq M(k,\delta)e^{\tau(a+\delta)}\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\,,\quad\forall\,U_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma},\,\,\tau\geq 2\,.

Remark 4.6
.

In principle, one could infer the existence, uniqueness, and smoothness of the unstable manifold from abstract theory for semilinear parabolic PDEs, see

4.2.1.
Functional setting

From now on, as in Proposition
4.5
, we suppose
a=s(\bm{L}_{\rm ss})>0
. Let
N>5/2
be an integer. It will be enough to choose
\varepsilon_{0}=a/2

T\in\mathbb{R}
, to be determined. We suppress dependence of the constants below on
\bar{U}
and

, which are now fixed.

We consider the norm

(4.20)

\|U\|_{X}:=\sup_{\tau<T}e^{-(a+\varepsilon_{0})\tau}\|U(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N}}\,,

and the associated Banach space

(4.21)

X:=\{U\in C((-\infty,T];H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3};\mathbb{R}^{3}))\,:\,\|U\|_{X}<\infty\}\,.

We study the functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}(U)(\cdot,\tau)

\displaystyle=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\circ\mathbb{P}\left[(U\cdot\nabla)U+(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U+(U\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\right]ds

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\circ\mathbb{P}\left[(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\right]ds\,.

By Duhamel’s formula and parabolic regularity theory, any

such that
\mathcal{T}(U)=U
is a solution to (
4.17
) satisfying the statement of Proposition
4.5

To find the sought fixed point we apply the contraction mapping principle.

Proposition 4.7
.

There exist
T=T(\bar{U},U^{\rm lin},N)\in\mathbb{R}
and
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(a)
such that

(4.22)

\mathcal{T}:\{U\in X:\|U\|_{X}\leq 1\}\to\{U\in X:\|U\|_{X}\leq 1\}\,,

Proof.

We first prove a claim which, under suitable assumptions, builds a fixed point of an operator with our structure.

4.2.2.
Abstract claim

Let
X
be a Banach space and

(4.23)

\mathcal{T}(U):=B(U,U)+LU+G\,,\quad U\in X\,,

where
G\in X
,
L:X\to X
is a bounded linear operator and
B:X\times X\to X
is a bounded bilinear form. If

(4.24)

\|L\|+2\|B\|+\|G\|_{X}<1\,,

then

(4.25)

\mathcal{T}:\{U\in X:\|U\|_{X}\leq 1\}\to\{U\in X:\|U\|_{X}\leq 1\}\,,

is a contraction.

Indeed, if
\|U\|_{X}\leq 1
, then

(4.26)

\|\mathcal{T}U\|_{X}\leq\|B(U,U)\|_{X}+\|LU\|_{X}+\|G\|_{X}\leq\|B\|\|U\|_{X}^{2}+\|L\|\|U\|_{X}+\|G\|_{X}<1\,.

Let us now consider
U,V\in X
such that
\|U\|_{X}\leq 1
,
\|V\|_{X}\leq 1
. It holds

(4.27)

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{T}(U)-\mathcal{T}(V)\|_{X}

\displaystyle\leq\|B(U,U)-B(V,V)\|_{X}+\|L(U-V)\|_{X}

(4.28)

\displaystyle\leq\|B(U-V,U)\|_{X}+\|B(V,U-V)\|_{X}+\|L\|\|U-V\|_{X}

(4.29)

\displaystyle\leq(2\|B\|+\|L\|)\|U-V\|_{X}\,,

since
2\|B\|+\|L\|<1
, we conclude that
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction.

4.2.3.
Application of the claim to Proposition
4.7

We now turn to verify the assumptions of the previous elementary claim for our operators:

(4.30)

B(U,V)(\cdot,\tau):=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\circ\mathbb{P}\left[(U\cdot\nabla)V\right]ds\,,

(4.31)

LU(\cdot,t):=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\circ\mathbb{P}\left[(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U+(U\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\right]ds\,,

(4.32)

G(\cdot,\tau):=-\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)\bm{L}_{\rm ss}}\circ\mathbb{P}\left[(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\right]ds\,.

More precisely, we prove below that

(4.33)

\displaystyle\|B(U,U)\|_{X}+\|LU\|_{X}+\|G\|_{X}\leq C(N,\delta,a)e^{T(a-\varepsilon_{0})}\,,

provided
\delta<a
. Choosing
\delta=\varepsilon_{0}/2=a/4
and
T
negative enough we conclude that the right-hand side in (
4.33
) is less than
1/2
. Applying the previous abstract claim, the proof of Proposition
4.7
is concluded provided we show (
4.33
).

To this end, we will use the following observation: For
N>5/2
, the space
H^{N-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is an algebra, and hence for every
f,g\in H^{N}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

(4.34)

\|f\nabla g\|_{H^{N-1}}\leq C(N)\|f\|_{H^{N-1}}\|\nabla g\|_{H^{N-1}}\leq C(N)\|f\|_{H^{N}}\|g\|_{H^{N}}\,.

4.2.4.
Estimate on
B(U,U)

We show that

(4.35)

\|B(U,U)\|_{X}\leq C(N,\delta,d)e^{(a+\varepsilon_{0})T}\|U\|_{X}^{2}\,.

We apply Lemma
4.4
to get

(4.36)

\lVert B(U,U)(\cdot,\tau)\rVert_{H^{N+1/2}}\leq M(N,\delta)\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\frac{e^{(\tau-s)(a+\delta)}}{(\tau-s)^{3/4}}\lVert(U\cdot\nabla)U(\cdot,s)\rVert_{H^{N-1}}ds\,,

for any
\tau\in(-\infty,T)
.
Thanks to (
4.34
), we deduce

(4.37)

\lVert(U\cdot\nabla)U(\cdot,s)\rVert_{H^{N-1}}\leq C(N)\lVert U(\cdot,s)\rVert_{H^{N}}^{2}\leq C(N)e^{2(a+\varepsilon_{0})s}\lVert U\rVert_{X}^{2}\,,

and, using that
a+2\varepsilon_{0}-\delta>0
, we obtain

(4.38)

\displaystyle\lVert B(U,U)(\cdot,\tau)\rVert_{H^{N+1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C(N,\delta)\lVert U\rVert_{X}^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\frac{e^{(\tau-s)(a+\delta)}e^{2(a+\varepsilon_{0})s}}{(\tau-s)^{3/4}}ds

(4.39)

\displaystyle\leq C(N,\delta)e^{2(a+\varepsilon_{0})\tau}\lVert U\rVert_{X}^{2}\,,

which implies (
4.35
). The additional gain of
1/2
regularity is not necessary for the existence; we use it to bootstrap the solution to smoothness in Section
4.2.7
.

4.2.5.
Estimate on
G

We show that

(4.40)

\|G\|_{X}\leq C(N,\delta,a)e^{T(a-\varepsilon_{0})}\,,

provided
\delta<a
.

As a consequence of Lemma
4.4
and (
4.34
), we have

(4.41)

\|G(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N}}\leq M(N,\delta)\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)(a+\delta)}\|U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla U^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{N}}ds\,,

(4.42)

\|U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla U^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{N}}\leq C(N)\|U^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{N+1}}^{2}\leq C(N)e^{2as}\,.

Hence,

(4.43)

\displaystyle\|G(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N}}\leq C(N,\delta)\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}e^{(\tau-s)(a+\delta)}e^{2sa}ds\leq C(N,\delta,a)e^{2\tau a}\,,

provided
\delta<a
.
This implies (
4.40
).

4.2.6.
Estimate on
LU

We show that

(4.44)

\|LU\|_{X}\leq C(N,\delta,a)e^{aT}\|U\|_{X}\,.

As a consequence of Lemma
4.4
we get

(4.45)

\displaystyle\|LU

\displaystyle(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N+1/2}}

(4.46)

\displaystyle\leq M(N,\delta)\int_{-\infty}^{\tau}\frac{e^{(\tau-s)(a+\delta)}}{(\tau-s)^{3/4}}(\|(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U\|_{H^{N-1}}+\|(U\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{N-1}})ds\,,

for
\tau\in(-\infty,T)
.

By employing (
4.16
) and (
4.34
) we deduce

(4.47)

\|(U^{\rm lin}\cdot\nabla)U\|_{H^{N-1}}+\|(U\cdot\nabla)U^{\rm lin}\|_{H^{N-1}}\leq C(N,\delta)e^{(2a+\varepsilon_{0})s}\|U\|_{X}\,.

This implies

(4.48)

\|LU(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N+1/2}}\leq C(N,\delta,a)e^{(2a+\varepsilon_{0})s}\|U\|_{X}\,,

and (
4.44
) easily follows.

Collecting the estimates (
4.35
), (
4.40
), (
4.44
), we obtain (
4.33
). ∎

4.2.7.
Refined decay estimates

Once the solution
U^{\rm per}
of Proposition
4.5
is known to exist, we can bootstrap it to satisfy
O(e^{2\tau a})
decay in all
H^{k}
,
k\geq 0
, as claimed in Theorem
1.3
. First, the estimate (
4.43
) on
G
is already satisfied for all
N
. Second, the estimates on
B(U^{\rm per},U^{\rm per})
and
LU^{\rm per}
above gain
1/2
derivative above the regularity of the solution. By Duhamel’s formula, we see that if
N>5/2
(not necessarily integral) and

(4.49)

\sup_{\tau\in(-\infty,T]}e^{-(a+\varepsilon_{0})\tau}\|U^{\rm per}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N}}<+\infty\,,

then

(4.50)

\sup_{\tau\in(-\infty,T]}e^{-2a\tau}\|U^{\rm per}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{N+1/2}}<+\infty\,.

By induction, we conclude that (
4.50
) holds for all
N>5/2
.

Remark 4.8

(General backgrounds)
.

It is possible to treat more general unstable backgrounds
satisfying the natural decay conditions

(4.51)

|\nabla^{k}\bar{U}(\xi)|\lesssim_{k}\langle\xi\rangle^{-k-1}

and
\bar{u}(x,t)\to\bar{u}_{0}
in
L^{2}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
as
t\to 0^{+}
.
Let
0\leq\chi\in C^{\infty}(B_{2})
with
\chi\equiv 1
on
B_{1}
. For
\alpha\in(0,1/2)
, we define

(4.52)

\widetilde{u}(x,t)=\bar{u}(x)\chi\left(\frac{x}{t^{\alpha}}\right).

That is, the cut-off shrinks as
t\to 0^{+}
but ‘more slowly’ than the self-similar rate (in similarity variables, the tails of the cut-off are escaping to
|\xi|=+\infty
as
\tau\to-\infty
). The ansatz is instead
u=\widetilde{u}+u^{\rm lin}+u^{\rm per}
. The cut-off introduces additional forcing and lower-order terms, which are treated perturbatively. (With this forcing, the refined decay
O(e^{2\tau a})
is not expected, but it is not necessary for non-uniqueness.)

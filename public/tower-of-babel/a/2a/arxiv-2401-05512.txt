By the classical Bézout theorem (see

17

the system is from the simplest situation where it can be solved by composition in order to

Lemma 2.5

Let

(1)

In particular when

\sum_{j\in T}P(j)K_{T,j}=0
.

(2)

On the other side, for

(14)

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in T}P(j)K_{T,j}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{coeff}_{X^{c}}(P)+\mathrm{coeff}_{X^{c+1}}(P)\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{c+1}K_{T,t_{j}}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\mathrm{coeff}_{X^{\deg(P)}}(P)\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{\deg(P)}K_{T,t_{j}}.

Proof.

Let

be the Vandermonde matrix related to

, that is to say

\displaystyle V_{T}=\begin{pmatrix}1&1&\cdots&1\\
t_{1}&t_{2}&\cdots&t_{c+1}\\
\vdots&\vdots&&\vdots\\
t_{1}^{c}&t_{2}^{c}&\cdots&t_{c+1}^{c}.\end{pmatrix}

Let us denote by

its determinant. We have

v_{T}=\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq c+1}(t_{j}-t_{i}),

Let us first prove
(1)
. For this let

be the row matrix of coefficients of
P
, completed at its right with

Notice that

\displaystyle C\cdot V_{T}=\left(P(t_{1}),P(t_{2}),\ldots,P(t_{c+1})\right).

Let

be the last column of the inverse matrix of

. We have

\displaystyle\text{coeff}_{X^{c}}(P)=C\cdot V_{T}\cdot V^{-1}_{T,c+1}=\left(P(t_{1}),P(t_{2}),\ldots,P(t_{c+1})\right)\cdot V^{-1}_{T,c+1}.

Consequently, it is sufficient to take for

, which is by Cramer’s formula

(15)

\displaystyle K_{T,t_{p}}=\frac{(-1)^{c+p+1}}{v_{T}}\det(V_{T\setminus\{t_{p}\}})=\frac{1}{\prod_{i\neq p}(t_{p}-t_{i})}.

This define the sequence

, and proves

.

To prove

, assuming

, we consider

\widetilde{V}_{T}
the square matrix of size

, defined as the matrix

first completed at its bottom by the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}t_{1}^{c+1}&\cdots&t_{c+1}^{c+1}\\
\vdots&&\vdots\\
t_{1}^{\deg(P)}&\cdots&t_{c+1}^{\deg(P)}\end{pmatrix},

and then at its right by the matrix with
\deg(P)+1

Let us also consider
\widetilde{V}^{-1}_{T,c+1}
the last column of the inverse matrix of

, but this time completed at its bottom by

\widetilde{V}_{T}\cdot\widetilde{V}_{T,c+1}^{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
\vdots\\
0\\

\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{c+1}K_{T,t_{j}}\\
\vdots\\
\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{\deg(P)}K_{T,t_{j}}\end{pmatrix},

where the unit appears at row

and

instead of

and

\displaystyle\sum_{j\in T}P(j)K_{T,j}=

\displaystyle\textrm{coeff}_{X^{c}}(P)+\textrm{coeff}_{X^{c+1}}(P)\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{c+1}K_{T,t_{j}}

\displaystyle+\cdots+\textrm{coeff}_{X^{\deg(P)}}(P)\sum_{j=1}^{c+1}t_{j}^{\deg(P)}K_{T,t_{j}}.\qed

Lemma 2.13
.

Let
N,R,p\in\mathbb{N}
with
p\leq R<N
and

f_{p}^{R,N}:\llbracket 0,R\rrbracket\to\mathbb{N}
be the function defined by

f_{p}^{R,N}(n)=\sigma_{p}(1,2,\ldots,R-n,N-n,\ldots,N)
, with the notation of Proposition
2.12
. There exists
a polynomial
P_{p}^{R,N}
of degree
p
such that
f_{p}^{R,N}(n)=P_{p}^{R,N}(n)
, for all
n\in\llbracket 0,R\rrbracket
.

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
p\geq 0
. Notice first
that
f_{0}^{R,N}\equiv 1
is a constant function.
Then for
p\geq 1
a direct computation shows that for all
n\in\llbracket 0,R-1\rrbracket

f_{p}^{R,N}(n+1)-f_{p}^{R,N}(n)=(N-R-1)\sigma_{p-1}(1,2,\ldots,R-1-n,N-n,\ldots,N).

Since by induction hypothesis the right hand side of the above equation equals the polynomial
(N-R-1)P_{p-1}^{R-1,N}
of degree
p-1
on
\llbracket 0,R-1\rrbracket
, so does
f_{p}^{R,N}(n+1)-f_{p}^{R,N}(n)
.

We just need to show this implies there exists a polynomial
P_{p}^{R,N}
which equals
f_{p}^{R,N}
on
\llbracket 0,R\rrbracket
. To do that, we follow for instance
[
9
, Proposition I.7.3 (b)]
. Let us consider the family of Newton polynomials
(\binom{X}{r})_{r\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by
\binom{X}{r}=\frac{1}{r!}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}(X-i)
. We can easily notice that
(\binom{X}{r})_{r\leq d}
is a basis of polynomials of degree at most
d
. Moreover, a simple calculation shows that for
r>0
,
\binom{X+1}{r}-\binom{X}{r}=\binom{X}{r-1}
.
Writing
(N-R-1)P_{p-1}^{R-1,N}
in this basis, we obtain the coefficients
(c_{p-1},\ldots,c_{0})

(N-R-1)P_{p-1}^{R-1,N}(X)=c_{p-1}\binom{X}{p-1}+c_{p-2}\binom{X}{p-2}+\cdots+c_{0}.

Let us define

P_{p}^{R,N}(X)=c_{p-1}\binom{X}{p}+c_{p-2}\binom{X}{p-1}+\cdots+c_{0}\binom{X}{1}+K

where the constant
K
is chosen to get
P_{p}^{R,N}(R)=f_{p}^{R,N}(R)
.
It follows that for any
n\in\llbracket 0,R-1\rrbracket
,
P_{p}^{R,N}(n+1)-P_{p}^{R,N}(n)=f_{p}^{R,N}(n+1)-f_{p}^{R,N}(n)
. An easy induction finishes the proof.
∎

Definition 5.3

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{b}\lvert v_{i}(\lambda)\rvert r^{i}+\sum_{j\geq b+1}\lvert v_{j}(\lambda)\rvert r^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\mu\sum_{i=0}^{b}\frac{r^{i}}{(\alpha r)^{i}}+\sum_{j\geq b+1}\sum_{k\in S}\lvert\gamma_{j}^{k}\rvert\lvert v_{k}(\lambda)\rvert R^{j}\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mu}{\alpha^{b}}\frac{1-\alpha^{b+1}}{1-\alpha}+\sum_{j\geq b+1}\sum_{k\in S}c\left(R^{j}\max_{i=1,\ldots,m}\lvert c_{j}^{i}\rvert\right)\lvert v_{k}(\lambda)\rvert\left(\frac{r}{R}\right)^{j}

Gray-categories model algebraic tricategories
Giovanni Ferrer

Abstract
Lack described a Quillen model structure on the category 𝖦𝗋𝖺𝗒𝖢𝖺𝗍 \GrayCat of 𝖦𝗋𝖺𝗒 \Gray -categories and 𝖦𝗋𝖺𝗒 \Gray -functors, for which the weak equivalences are the weak 3-equivalences. In this note, we adapt the technique of Gurski, Johnson, and Osorno to show the localization of 𝖦𝗋𝖺𝗒𝖢𝖺𝗍 \GrayCat at the weak equivalences is equivalent to the category of algebraic tricategories and pseudo-natural equivalence classes of weak 3-functors.



1
Introduction

Let
\TwoCat
be the category of strict 2-categories and strict 2-functors.
The symmetric monoidal category
\Gray
is the category
\TwoCat
equipped with the Gray monoidal structure
[
Gur13
]
.
A
\Gray
-category is then a category enriched in
\Gray
in the sense of
[
Kel05
]
.
We denote the 1-category of
\Gray
-categories and
\Gray
-functors by
\GrayCat
. A more explicit definition of
\Gray
-category can be found in Appendix
A
.

We now discuss the model category structure on
\GrayCat
from
[
Lac11
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
\Gray
-functor
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
between
\Gray
-categories is called:

•

a
weak equivalence
if it is a weak 3-equivalence of
{\mathcal{A}}
and
{\mathcal{B}}
as weak 3-categories,

•

a
fibration
if it is a fibration on each hom-2-category and satisfies a so-called biadjoint-biequivalence-lifting property (from which one sees all
\Gray
-categories are fibrant),

•

in particular, a
trivial fibration
if it is surjective at all levels and (fully) faithful at the top level, and

•

a
cofibration
if it has the left lifting property against trivial fibrations.
More specifically,
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is a cofibration if and only if for every trivial fibration
F^{\prime}:{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
and
\Gray
-functors
a:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
and
b:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}
such that the following square commutes,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces a}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces b}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell}

there exists a
\Gray
-functor lift
\ell:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}
such that the two triangles commute.

Definition 1.2
.

We denote by
{\mathcal{W}}
the collection of weak equivalences in
\GrayCat
. The category
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
is the localization of
\GrayCat
at the weak equivalences, which is determined (up to unique isomorphism) by the following universal property
[
BD69
]
:

•

For any functor
F:\GrayCat\to{\mathcal{D}}
of categories which maps weak equivalences to isomorphisms,
F
uniquely factors through
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
, i.e., there exists a unique
\Phi:\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]\to{\mathcal{D}}
such that the following diagram commutes on the nose:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GrayCat}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{D}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\exists!\,\Phi}

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
BD69
, Appendix]
, an explicit construction is provided in which an object of
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
is simply a
\Gray
-category and a morphism from
{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
in
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
is a “zigzag”, i.e., a finite chain of
\Gray
-categories and
\Gray
-functors

{\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{w_{1}}X_{1}\xrightarrow{f_{1}}X_{2}\xleftarrow{w_{2}}X_{3}\xrightarrow{f_{2}}\cdots\xleftarrow{w_{n}}X_{2n-1}\xrightarrow{f_{n}}{\mathcal{B}}

where the
\leftarrow
and
\rightarrow
alternate and each morphism
w_{i}
“in the wrong direction” is a weak equivalence. These zigzags are considered equal up to a certain equivalence relation. We refer the reader to the previous citation for more details.

Definition 1.4
.

We denote by
\hoTriCat
the 1-category whose objects are
\Gray
-categories and morphisms are pseudo-natural equivalence classes of weak 3-functors. Since each weak 3-category (algebraic tricategory) is equivalent a
\Gray
-category
[
Gur13
, §10.4]
,
\hoTriCat
is equivalent to the 1-category of weak 3-categories and pseudo-natural equivalence classes of weak 3-functors.

The main result of this note shows that Lack’s model structure models algebraic tricategories in the following sense.

Theorem

(Theorem
3.3
)
.

The categories
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
and
\hoTriCat
are isomorphic.

The proof is a straightforward adaptation of
[
GJO19
, Prop. 3.31]
using cofibrant replacement and path objects.

Proposition 2.1

(Cofibrant replacement in

)

For every

3
The main theorem

In this section we implement the technique of
[
GJO19
]
. There is an obvious functor
Q:\GrayCat\to\hoTriCat
which maps each
\Gray
-category to itself and maps
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
to its equivalence class
[F]
in
\hoTriCat
.
Clearly this functor maps equivalences to isomorphisms, and thus uniquely factors through
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
.
We will denote this factorization by
\Phi:\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]\to\hoTriCat
.

Definition 3.1
.

Define
\Psi:\hoTriCat\to\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
to be the identity on objects and, for a weak 3-functor
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
, define

\Psi([F])\coloneqq{\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

\Psi
is a well-defined functor.

Proof.

Suppose
F,G:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
are pseudo-naturally equivalent.
Then

\displaystyle\Psi([F])

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{S\circ\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}

(P3)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{S}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}

(
\widehat{5}
)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xrightarrow{S}{\mathcal{B}}

(
\widehat{2}
)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xleftarrow{C}{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{C}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xrightarrow{S}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xleftarrow{C}{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{C}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xrightarrow{T}{\mathcal{B}}

(P1)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xrightarrow{T}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{T}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}

(
\widehat{2}
)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{T\circ\langle F,G\rangle}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}

(
\widehat{5}
)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}=\Psi([G]).

(P3)

Hence
\Psi
is well-defined. Observe

\displaystyle\Psi([\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{A}}])

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{A}}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}

(
\widehat{3}
)

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{id}_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}=\mathrm{id}_{\Psi({\mathcal{A}})}.

Since
\Psi
is well-defined, for composable weak 3-functors
{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{F}{\mathcal{B}}\xrightarrow{G}{\mathcal{C}}
,

\displaystyle\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}\underset{\hyperref@@ii[fact:hat]{(\widehat{2})}}{=}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}}}\,\,\widehat{\!\!\widehat{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\widehat{G}\circ\widehat{F}}}\,\,\widehat{\!\!\widehat{{{\mathcal{C}}}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}

\displaystyle=\Psi([\widehat{G}\circ\widehat{F}])

(
\widehat{4}
)

\displaystyle=\Psi([\widehat{G\circ F}])=\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}}}\,\,\widehat{\!\!\widehat{{{\mathcal{A}}}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{\widehat{G\circ F}}}\,\,\widehat{\!\!\widehat{{{\mathcal{C}}}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}\underset{\hyperref@@ii[fact:hat]{(\widehat{2})}}{=}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G\circ F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}.

Hence

\displaystyle\Psi([G])\circ\Psi([F])

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{C}}}}{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{C}}}}{\mathcal{C}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{G\circ F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{C}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{C}}}}{\mathcal{C}}=\Psi([G\circ F]),

and we conclude
\Psi
is a functor.
∎

We are now ready to prove the main result of this note.

Theorem 3.3
.

The functors
\Phi
and
\Psi
exhibit an isomorphism of categories.

Proof.

Since both functors are the identity on objects, it suffices to show that
\Phi
and
\Psi
are mutual inverses on hom sets.
The fact that
\Phi
is surjective on hom sets follows from
(
\widehat{1}
)
.
We now show
(\Psi\circ\Phi)([F])=[F]
and conclude
\Phi
is an isomorphism with inverse
\Psi
.
Indeed, consider the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\GrayCat}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\hoTriCat}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Q}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi}

whose left triangle commutes by the universal property of
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
.
Note that for every
\Gray
-functor
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
,

(\Psi\circ Q)(F)=\Psi([F])=A\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}}{\mathcal{B}}\underset{\hyperref@@ii[fact:hat]{(\widehat{2})}}{=}{\mathcal{A}}\xleftarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{F}{\mathcal{B}}={\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{F}{\mathcal{B}}=\pi(F).

Thus
\Psi\circ\Phi\circ\pi=\Psi\circ Q=\pi
. By the uniqueness of factorizations through
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
, it is easy to see that
\pi
is epic.
From this we conclude
\Psi\circ\Phi=\mathrm{id}
.
∎

4
Corollaries

We obtain the following two corollaries which correspond to the surjectivity and injectivity/well-definedness respectively of the above bijection induced by
\Phi
.

Corollary 4.1
.

If
{\mathcal{A}}
is a cofibrant
\Gray
-category and
F:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
is a weak 3-functor, then
F
is pseudonaturally equivalent to a
\Gray
-functor.

Proof.

Since
\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}:\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a trivial fibration, if
\varnothing\to{\mathcal{A}}
is a cofibration, then there exists a
\Gray
-functor lift
\ell:{\mathcal{A}}\to\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
which makes the following triangles commute
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varnothing}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{A}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces!}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces!}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{{\mathcal{A}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ell}

Thus
\ell
is a section of
\ev_{\mathcal{A}}
and
F=F\circ\ev_{\mathcal{A}}\circ\,\ell\underset{\hyperref@@ii[fact:hat]{(\widehat{1})}}{\cong}\ev_{{\mathcal{B}}}\circ\widehat{F}\circ\ell\text{, a $\Gray$-functor}.

∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Suppose
{\mathcal{A}}
is a cofibrant
\Gray
-category and
F,G:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}
are
\Gray
-functors. Then
F
is pseudonaturally equivalent to
G
if and only if
F
and
G
are homotopic in Lack’s model structure.

Proof.

First suppose
F
is pseudo-natually equivalent to
G
, i.e.
\Phi(F)=[F]=[G]=\Phi(G)
. By the injectivity of
\Phi
,
F=G
in
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
. Since
\ev_{\mathcal{A}},\ev_{\mathcal{B}}\in{\mathcal{W}}
, it follows by
(
\widehat{2}
)
that
\widehat{F}=\widehat{G}
in
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
. By the general theory of model categories,
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
is equivalent to the category of fibrant-cofibrant
\Gray
-categories together with homotopy classes of
\Gray
-functors (see
[
Hir03
, Thm. 8.3.9]
). Through this equivalence we obtain that
\widehat{F}
is homotopic to
\widehat{G}
since
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
are fibrant-cofibrant. More specifically, there exists a
\Gray
-functor
H:\widehat{A}\rightarrow\widehat{B}^{I}
such that
S\circ H=\widehat{F}
and
T\circ H=\widehat{G}
.

By
(P2)
, there exists a map
\ev_{\mathcal{B}}^{I}:\widehat{B}^{I}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{I}
such that the following diagrams commute.
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{\mathcal{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\ev_{\mathcal{B}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces T}

As before, since
{\mathcal{A}}
is cofibrant, there exists a
\Gray
-functor section
\ell:{\mathcal{A}}\to\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}
for
\ev_{\mathcal{A}}
. By defining the
\Gray
-functor
H^{\prime}:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{I}
by
H^{\prime}\coloneqq\ev^{I}_{\mathcal{B}}\circ H\circ\ell
we see that

\displaystyle S\circ H^{\prime}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\ell}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{H}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}^{I}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\mathcal{B}}^{I}}{\mathcal{B}}^{I}\xrightarrow{S}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\ell}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{H}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}^{I}\xrightarrow{S}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle={\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\ell}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\widehat{F}}\widehat{{\mathcal{B}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle\underset{\hyperref@@ii[fact:hat]{(\widehat{2})}}{=}{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{\ell}\widehat{{\mathcal{A}}}\xrightarrow{\ev_{\mathcal{A}}}{\mathcal{A}}\xrightarrow{F}{\mathcal{B}}=F

and similarly
T\circ H^{\prime}=G
. Thus
F,G
are homotopic. Conversely, suppose
F,G
are homotopic so there exists a
\Gray
-functor
H:{\mathcal{A}}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{I}
with
S\circ H=F
and
T\circ H=G
. By
(P1)
,
(C:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathcal{B}}^{I})\in{\mathcal{W}}
with
S\circ C=T\circ C
, so
S=T
in
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}].
This implies
F=S\circ H=T\circ H=G
in
\GrayCat[{\mathcal{W}}^{-1}]
and by the well-definedness of
\Phi
we have that
[F]=\Phi(F)=\Phi(G)=[G]
. Thus
F
is pseudo-naturally equivalent to
G
.
∎

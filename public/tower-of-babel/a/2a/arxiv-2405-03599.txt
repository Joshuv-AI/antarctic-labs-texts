Proof of the geometric Langlands conjecture I: construction of the functor
Dennis Gaitsgory and Sam Raskin

Abstract
. In this paper we construct the geometric Langlands functor in one direction (from the automorphic to the spectral side) in characteristic zero settings (i.e., de Rham and Betti). We prove that various forms of the conjecture (de Rham vs Betti, restricted vs. non-restricted, tempered vs. non-tempered) are equivalent. We also discuss structural properties of Hecke eigensheaves.

0.1.4.

The construction of

0.1.5.

The functor

idea is incarnated by the fact that the functor

•

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

•

It makes the triangle

\textstyle{\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})}
\textstyle{{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})}
\textstyle{\operatorname{Vect}}
\scriptstyle{\operatorname{coeff}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}}
\scriptstyle{\Gamma(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}},-)}

0.1.6.

0.2.
The Betti setting

We now consider GLC in the Betti setting.

0.2.1.

In this case our algebraic geometry happens over the ground field
{\mathbb{C}}

(so
X
and
G
are over
{\mathbb{C}}
), and we work with sheaves of
{\mathsf{e}}
-vector spaces, where
{\mathsf{e}}
is an arbitrary
field of characteristic
0
.

So,
{\check{G}}
is an reductive group over
{\mathsf{e}}
, and
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}

is an algebraic stack over
{\mathsf{e}}
.

0.2.2.

A historical and conceptual difference between the de Rham and Betti situations is that while it has “always”
(i.e., since the inception of the subject by A. Beilinson and V. Drinfeld) been
understood what the automorphic side in the de Rham setting was (i.e., the entire category of half-twisted D-modules),
in the Betti setting it was a relatively recent discovery, due to D. Ben-Zvi and D. Nadler, see
[
BZN
]
.

Namely, the entire category
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

of (twisted) Betti sheaves on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
is too big to be equivalent
to something on the spectral side. What Ben-Zvi and Nadler discovered was that there is a reasonable automorphic candidate:
namely, this is the full category

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

of Betti sheaves with singular support in the global nilpotent cone.

0.2.3.

The spectral side in the Betti setting must also be modified as compared to the de Rham setting,
but that modification is evident: one should replace the stack
\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}
of de Rham local
systems on
X
(i.e., the stack classifying
{\check{G}}
-bundles with a connection) by the stack
\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}}

of Betti
{\check{G}}
-local systems (i.e., if we ignore the derived structure, this is the stack of homomorphisms

\pi_{1}(X)\to{\check{G}}
, divided by the action of
{\check{G}}
by conjugation).

In retrospect, knowing the spectral side, one can convince oneself that
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
is the right candidate for the
automorphic category as follows: this is the largest subcategory of
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, on
which the Hecke action gives rise to an action of
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{Betti}})
, see
[
AGKRRV
, Theorem 18.1.4]
.

0.2.4.

With the candidates for the automorphic and spectral sides in place, the construction of the Langlands functor
in the Betti setting

(0.4)

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}:\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})

proceeds along the same lines as in the de Rham setting, with one modification:

The functor
\operatorname{coeff}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}

in the de Rham setting appealed to the exponential D-module on
{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
. There is (obviously) no Betti analog
of the exponential D-module, but one finds a
substitute using an additional group of symmetries (given by the torus
T
) acting in our situation, see
Sect.
3.3
.

0.2.5.

The geometric Langlands conjecture (GLC) in the Betti setting says that the functor (

is an equivalence.

0.3.
Comparison of the different versions of GLC

In addition to the de Rham (resp., Betti) versions of GLC, which we will refer to as
full
GLC, in both settings
there exist other versions, which we refer to as
restricted
and
tempered
, respectively. And there exist
also the restricted tempered versions.

However, it turns out that all these versions are logically equivalent in the sense
that any one implies the others.

0.3.1.

The restricted version of GLC, in either de Rham or Betti versions, was introduced
4
4

4

Without specifying the functor in either
direction.
in
[
AGKRRV

On the automorphic side of the restricted GLC in the de Rham setting, we have the full subcategory

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

that consists of (twisted) D-modules with singular support in the nilpotent cone
{\operatorname{Nilp}}\subset T^{*}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.
Objects of this category are (obviously) ind-holonomic. However, one can show that actually
have regular singularities (this is a non-trivial result
[
AGKRRV
, Corollary 16.5.6]
).

On the automorphic side of the restricted GLC in the Betti setting, we have the full subcategory

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

In fact, both categories

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\text{ and }\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

fall into the paradigm of
[
AGKRRV
, Sect. 14.1]
, where the restricted automorphic category is defined
starting from a

as in
[

.
The two sheaf theories in question are

\operatorname{Shv}(-):=\operatorname{D-mod}^{\operatorname{RS}}(-)\text{ and }\operatorname{Shv}(-):=\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}(-),

respectively.

0.3.2.

On the spectral side of the restricted GLC we have the categories

(0.5)

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}})\text{ and }{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}_{\check{G}}),

0.3.3.

Starting from the full geometric Langlands functors
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
and
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{Betti}}
one produces their
restricted versions

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\overset{{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{restr}}}{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}})

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\overset{{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}}{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}_{\check{G}}).

by applying the operations

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})_{\operatorname{restr}}\subset{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})\text{ and }{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})_{\operatorname{restr}}\subset{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})

It is clear that if
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
and
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{Betti}}

{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{restr}}
and

{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}

0.3.4.

Thus, we have:

\text{Full de Rham GLC}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\text{Restricted de Rham GLC}

and

\text{Full Betti GLC}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\text{Restricted Betti GLC}.

However, Riemann-Hilbert implies that we also have

\text{Restricted de Rham GLC}\Leftrightarrow\,\text{Restricted Betti GLC}.

As a result, we deduce that

\text{Full de Rham GLC}\,\Leftrightarrow\,\text{Full Betti GLC}.

In the rest of this series of papers we will focus on the de Rham version of GLC.

This is because it is
in this context that one can use Kac-Moody localization, which provides a powerful tool

0.3.6.

Let us now comment on how we deduce

\text{Restricted de Rham GLC}\,\Rightarrow\,\text{Full de Rham GLC}

(the Betti case is similar).

To do so, we introduce yet another version of GLC: the tempered one. Namely, the category
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

admits a localization

{\mathbf{u}}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})_{\operatorname{temp}}\rightleftarrows\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}):{\mathbf{u}}^{R},

The tempered versions of the Langlands functor map

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})_{\operatorname{temp}}\overset{{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}}{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})_{\operatorname{temp}}\overset{{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}}{\longrightarrow}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{\check{G}}),

The tempered (resp., tempered restricted) version of GLC says that the functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
(resp.,
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{restr}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}

is an equivalence.

0.3.7.

It is clear that we have the implications

\text{Full GLC}\,\Rightarrow\,\text{Tempered GLC}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\text{Restricted GLC}\,\Rightarrow\,\text{Tempered restricted GLC}

1.
Construction of the Langlands functor (de Rham context)

In this section we will first review the ingredients that are needed for the construction of the Langlands functor,
and then carry out the construction in question, all in the context of D-modules.

The “coarse” version of the functor will be automatic from the spectral action (see Sect.
1.4
).
To upgrade it to the actual Langlands functor, we will need some cohomological estimates, which are
provided by Theorem
1.6.2
.

1.1.
The automorphic category

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
of
G

Let

where

Note also that up to the (constant) line

According to

gives rise to a square root of
\operatorname{det}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}

Let

Let

The category

1.1.5.

Note, however, that by Sect.

Remark 1.1.6

It is crucial, however, to consider the twisted version, when we study the Hecke functors.
Otherwise, instead of the usual category

one has to consider its twist by a

-gerbe, see
[
GLys

1.2.
The spectral action

An ingredient, whch is crucial for both for the construction of the Langlands functor and to the eventual proof
that it is an equivalence, is the fact that the action of Hecke functors on
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

gives rise to a monoidal action of the category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
.

1.2.1.

Let
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
be the de Rham version of the category
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})
spread over
the Ran space, see
[
Ga2
, Sect. 4.2]

The (naive) geometric Satake functor and the Hecke action
define an action of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
on
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

1.2.2.

Recall now that we have a (symmetric) monoidal localization functor

(1.2)

\operatorname{Loc}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{spec}}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}),

see
[
Ga2
, Sect. 4.3]

The category
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
is compactly generated, and the functor
\operatorname{Loc}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{spec}}

preserves compactness (being a symmetric monoidal functor between rigid symmetric monoidal

It is a basic fact that this right adjoint is fully faithful.
I.e., the functor
\operatorname{Loc}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{spec}}
is a localization.

1.2.3.

We have the following result (see
[
Ga2
, Corollary 4.5.5]

Theorem 1.2.4
.

The action of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
on
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

factors via the functor (
1.2
).

1.2.5.

Thanks to Theorem

, we obtain a canonically defined action of the monoidal category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

on
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

We refer to it as the

.

1.3.
The vacuum Poincaré sheaf

The second ingredient for the construction of the Langlands functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
is the object of

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
, which is the image of
{\mathcal{O}}_{\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}}
under the
would-be left adjoint
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{L}
of

This object is the
vacuum
Poincaré sheaf, introduced in this subsection. This particular

automorphic functions, that the Langlands correspondence is normalized by the Whittaker model.

1.3.1.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}_{T}
be any
T
-bundle. Consider the stack

\operatorname{Bun}_{N,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}}\simeq\operatorname{Bun}_{B}\underset{\operatorname{Bun}_{T}}{\times}\operatorname{pt},

where the map
\operatorname{pt}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{T}

{\mathcal{P}}_{T}

Note that the pullback of
\operatorname{det}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}

Denote the resulting line by

1.3.2.

The Langlands functor depends on the choice of the square root
\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}}

of the canonical line bundle on
X
, which we fix from now on. With this choice we claim:

Proposition 1.3.3
.

The line
{\mathfrak{l}}_{G,N_{{\mathcal{P}}_{T}}}
admits a canonical square root.

Proof.

We claim more generally that for any parabolic
P

M

M

\operatorname{det}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}|_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}\otimes\operatorname{det}^{\otimes-1}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{M}}|_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}

Let
P

By definition,

\operatorname{det}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}|_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}\otimes\operatorname{det}^{\otimes-1}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{M}}|_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}\simeq\\
\simeq\operatorname{det}(\Gamma(X,({\mathfrak{n}}_{P})_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}})\otimes\operatorname{det}(\Gamma(X,({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{P})_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}))^{\otimes-1}\otimes\operatorname{det}(\Gamma(X,{\mathfrak{n}}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}))^{\otimes-1}\otimes\operatorname{det}(\Gamma(X,{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}_{P}\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X})).

We claim that the lines

(1.4)

•

{\mathcal{L}}
is a line bundle on

Hence, the ratio of the tensor product of (

\operatorname{Weil}(\operatorname{det}(({\mathfrak{n}}_{P})_{{\mathcal{P}}_{M}}),\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})\otimes\operatorname{Weil}(\operatorname{det}({\mathfrak{n}}_{P})\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X},\omega_{X}^{\frac{1}{2}})^{\otimes-1}\otimes\\

1.4.
The coarse version of the functor

In this subsection we will construct a
coarse
version of the Langlands functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
,
denoted
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
.

The difference between the two versions is that
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
is supposed to
take values in the category
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
, while
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
takes values
in the usual category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
.

1.4.1.

Note that by Sect.
1.2.5
, a choice of an object in
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

defines a
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
-linear functor
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

1.4.2.

We define the functor

(1.10)

{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}:{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})\to\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

to correspond to the object

(1.11)

\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{G,!}\in\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

Since the object
\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{G,!}
is compact and the monoidal category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

is compactly generated and rigid, the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
preserves compactness.

Remark 1.4.3
.

The notation
{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
has to do with the fact that the essential image
of this functor lands in the subcategory

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})_{\operatorname{temp}}\subset\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}),

see Sect.
5.1.4
.

Remark 1.4.4
.

Once GLC (Conjecture
1.6.7
) is proved, it would follow that the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}

is actually an equivalence

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})\overset{\sim}{\to}\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})_{\operatorname{temp}},

see Remark
5.1.7
.

1.4.5.

We let

{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

be the right adjoint of
{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
.

Since the category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
is compactly generated and the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
preserves compactness,
the functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
is continuous.
By rigidity,
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
is automatically
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
-linear.

1.4.6.

One can equivalently describe
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
as follows. It is uniquely characterized by the following
two pieces of structure:

•

{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
is
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
-linear;

•

\Gamma(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}},{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}(-))\simeq\operatorname{coeff}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}
.

1.5.
The case
G=T

1.5.1.

Let
G=T
be a torus. Consider the Fourier-Mukai equivalence

\operatorname{FM}:{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}}),

given by the Poincaré line bundle

{\mathcal{L}}_{\operatorname{Poinc}}\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}}),

as a kernel, where
{\mathcal{L}}_{\operatorname{Poinc}}
, viewed as a map
\operatorname{Bun}_{T}\times\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}}\to B{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
, is
given by the Weil pairing.

1.5.2.

It is known that
\operatorname{FM}
can be enhanced to an equivalence

\operatorname{FM}^{\operatorname{enh}}:\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}}),

that makes the following diagram commute:

\begin{CD}\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})@>{\operatorname{FM}^{\operatorname{enh}}}>{}>{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}})\\
@V{}V{}V@V{}V{}V\\
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})@>{\operatorname{FM}}>{}>{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}}),\end{CD}

where:

•

The functor
\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})\to\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{T})
is
{\mathbf{oblv}}^{r}
, the forgetful functor for “right” D-modules;

•

The functor
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}})
is direct image along the projection

\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{\check{T}}.

1.5.3.

Unwinding the definitions, we obtain that the functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{T,\operatorname{coarse}}
identifies with

\operatorname{FM}^{\operatorname{enh}}\circ\tau_{T},

where
\tau_{T}
is the Cartan involution, i.e., the inversion automorphism, of
T
.

1.5.4.

Note that in the case of
G=T
, the subset
{\operatorname{Nilp}}\subset\operatorname{Sing}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}})
is the
0
-section. Hence

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}})={\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{T}}).

We set

{\mathbb{L}}_{T}:={\mathbb{L}}_{T,\operatorname{coarse}}.

So in this case there is no difference between the coarse Langlands functor and the sought-for
Langlands functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{T}
.

1.6.
Statement of the main result

In this subsection we will formulate Theorem
1.6.2
, which would allow us to upgrade
{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}

to the actual Langlands functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
.

1.6.1.

The main technical result of this paper pertaining to the Langlands functor in the de Rham context reads:

Theorem 1.6.2
.

The functor

{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

sends compact objects in
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to bounded below (i.e., eventually coconnective)
objects in
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
.

The proof will be given in Sect.
2
. We now proceed to the consequences of Theorem
1.6.2

that pertain to the geometric Langlands functor.

1.6.3.

Let
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
be the category of
[
AG
, Sect. 11.1.5]
. It is equipped with a forgetful functor

(1.12)

\Psi_{{\operatorname{Nilp}},\{0\}}:{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})

is t-exact and induces an equivalence

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})^{>-\infty}\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})^{>-\infty},

see
[
AG
, Proposition 4.4.5]
.

1.6.4.

Combining this with Theorem
1.6.2
and the fact that
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

is compactly generated (see
[
DG1
, Theorem 0.1.2]
), we obtain:

Corollary 1.6.5
.

There exists a (colimit-preserving) functor

{\mathbb{L}}_{G}:\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}}),

uniquely characterized by the following properties:

•

The functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
sends compact objects in

\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to eventually coconnective objects
in
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})
, i.e., to
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})^{>-\infty}
.

•

\Psi_{{\operatorname{Nilp}},\{0\}}\circ{\mathbb{L}}_{G}\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
;

1.6.6.

The functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
is
the
geometric Langlands functor in the de
Rham context.

We can now state the geometric Langlands conjecture (GLC) in the de Rham context:

Conjecture 1.6.7
.

The functor
{\mathbb{L}}_{G}
is an equivalence.

Remark 1.6.8
.

By the same logic as in Corollary
1.6.5
, using Theorem
1.6.2
, we can lift

Note, however, that Proposition

says that its cohomological amplitude on the right

2.4.
Proof of Proposition
2.3.2

2.4.1.

Let

be a smooth cover of
{\mathcal{Y}}
by an affine scheme. Since

we can assume that
{\mathcal{Y}}=Y
is an affine scheme.

2.4.2.

Let
Z\overset{i_{Z}}{\hookrightarrow}Y
be an
irreducible subvariety and let

\eta_{Z}\overset{j_{Z}}{\hookrightarrow}Z

be the embedding of its generic point.

Let

{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)_{Z}\overset{(\widehat{i}_{Z})_{!}}{\hookrightarrow}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)

supported on
Z

(\widehat{i}_{Z})^{!}
denote the right adjoint of
(\widehat{i}_{Z})_{!}

Let

be the localization of
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)_{Z}
at the generic point.

Applying Cousin decomposition, we obtain that
{\mathcal{M}}\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)^{\geq-d}
if and
only if for every
Z
as above,

(2.2)

j_{Z}^{*}\circ(\widehat{i}_{Z})^{!}({\mathcal{M}})\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(Y)^{\geq-d}_{\eta_{Z}}.

2.4.3.

We can represent the formal completion of

in

\underset{n}{\operatorname{colim}}\,Z_{n},

where

[

We have

Hence, in order to establish (
2.2

above, we have

(2.3)

j_{Z}^{*}\circ(i_{Z_{n}})^{!}({\mathcal{M}})\in{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\eta_{Z_{n}})^{\geq-d},

where
\eta_{Z_{n}}
is the localization of
Z_{n}
at
\eta_{Z}
.

2.4.4.

Let
K
be the field of rational functions on

\eta_{Z_{n}}=\operatorname{Spec}(A_{Z_{n}})
. Note, however, that since

is eventually,
coconnective,

Hence, in order to establish (

2.4.5.

Denote

Y^{\prime}:=Y\underset{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}{\times}\operatorname{Spec}(K_{Z}),\,\,Z^{\prime}:=Z\underset{\operatorname{Spec}(k)}{\times}\operatorname{Spec}(K_{Z});

{\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}

{\mathcal{M}}
to

Note that

z_{\operatorname{can}}

the corresponding morphism
\operatorname{Spec}(K)\to Z^{\prime}

The morphism

\operatorname{Spec}(K)\overset{i_{\operatorname{can}}}{\longrightarrow}Z^{\prime}\to Z.

Hence,

j_{Z}^{*}\circ i_{Z}^{!}({\mathcal{M}})\simeq(i_{\operatorname{can}})^{*}\circ(i^{\prime}_{Z})^{!}({\mathcal{M}}^{\prime}).

Note, however, that

Hence, we can further rewrite

2.4.7.

Now, the

(i^{\prime}_{Z}\circ i_{\operatorname{can}})^{!}({\mathcal{M}})\in\operatorname{Vect}_{K}^{\geq 0}.

Hence,

(i^{\prime}_{Z}\circ i_{\operatorname{can}})^{!}({\mathcal{M}})[\dim(Z)]\in\operatorname{Vect}_{K}^{\geq-d},

∎[Proposition
2.3.2
]

Proof of Proposition

2.5.1.

We have the following counterpart of Proposition

3.
Geometric Langlands functor in the Betti context

In this section we take our ground field
k
to be
{\mathbb{C}}
. Throughout this section we will work with the “big” category of Betti
sheaves of
{\mathsf{e}}
-vector spaces (see
[
AGKRRV
, Appendix G]
), where
{\mathsf{e}}
is an arbitrary field of coefficients
(assumed of characteristic zero).

We will construct the Langlands functor in the Betti setting.

Once the functor is constructed, we will formulate a theorem to the effect that this functor is an equvalence
if and only if its de Rham counterpart is.

3.1.
The category with nilpotent singular support

In this subsection we will introduce (following
[
BZN
]
) the automorphic category in the Betti context.

3.1.1.

Note that since
\operatorname{det}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}
is a gerbe with respect to
\mu_{2}\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
, it gives rise to an étale
{\mathsf{e}}^{\times}
-gerbe
for any field
{\mathsf{e}}
of characteristic different from 2.

Hence, it makes sense to consider the category
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
of sheaves of
{\mathsf{e}}
-vector spaces in the classical
topology on
\operatorname{Bun}_{G}
, twisted by
\operatorname{det}^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\operatorname{Bun}_{G}}
.

Note, however, that by Sect.
1.1.2
, we can identify

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\simeq\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

3.1.2.

Let

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\overset{{\mathbf{i}}}{\hookrightarrow}\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

be the full subcategory consisting of sheaves with singular support contained in
{\operatorname{Nilp}}
.

According to
[
AGKRRV
, Sect. 18.2.6]
, the functor
{\mathbf{i}}
admits a left adjoint, to be denoted
{\mathbf{i}}^{L}
.

3.1.3.

The functor
{\mathbf{i}}^{L}
allows us to construct compact objects in
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

For a stack
{\mathcal{Y}}
and a rational point
y
, let
\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}({\mathcal{Y}})
be the corresponding

\delta
-function object, i.e.,
(i_{y})_{!}({\mathsf{e}})
, where
i_{y}
be the morphism

\operatorname{pt}\to{\mathcal{Y}}

corresponding to
y
.

According to
[
AGKRRV
, Proposition G.3.5]
, objects of the form

{\mathbf{i}}^{L}(\delta_{y}),\quad y\in\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

form a set of compact generators of

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

Remark 3.1.4
.

Note, however, that objects
\delta_{y}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
are
not

compact, and that the category
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
itself is
not
compactly generated.

3.2.
The Hecke action in the Betti context

3.2.1.

Let
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
be the Betti version of the category
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})_{\operatorname{Ran}}
, defined as in

[
AGKRRV
, Remark 11.1.10]
.

As in the de Rham setting, we have a localization functor

(3.1)

\operatorname{Loc}_{\check{G}}^{\operatorname{spec}}:{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}}),

which admits a fully faithful continuous right adjoint.

3.2.2.

We have a canonically defined monoidal action of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
on
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
,
which preserves the subcategory

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
.

The following is a key observation from
[
NY
]
(see also
[
AGKRRV
, Theorem 18.1.4]
):

Proposition 3.2.3
.

The action of
{\mathop{\operatorname{\rm Rep}}}({\check{G}})^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\operatorname{Ran}}
on
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

factors via the localization (
3.1
).

As a result, we obtain that the category
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
carries
a canonically defined action of the monoidal category
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
.

3.3.
The vacuum Poincaré sheaf in the Betti context

Our current goal is to construct the object

(3.2)

\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

The slight hiccup is that the exponential sheaf is not defined as an object of

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}({\mathbb{G}}_{a})
. In order to circumvent this, we will first apply the procedure of
{\mathbb{G}}_{m}
-averaging.

3.3.1.

Define the object

\operatorname{exp}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}({\mathbb{G}}_{a}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m})

to be the *-extension of

{\mathsf{e}}[-1]\in\operatorname{Vect}=\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{pt})

along the open embedding

\operatorname{pt}\simeq({\mathbb{G}}_{a}-\{0\})/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{G}}_{a}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

Remark 3.3.2
.

Note that when
{\mathsf{e}}={\mathbb{C}}
, under the Riemann-Hilbert equivalence, the object
\operatorname{exp}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}

corresponds to the !-direct image of
\operatorname{exp}
along the projection

{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\to{\mathbb{G}}_{a}/{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

3.3.3.

We will now consider a variant of this construction. Consider the stack

\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a}/T,

where
T
acts on
\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
by means of

T\to T/Z_{G}\overset{\text{simple roots}}{\simeq}\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{m}.

Consider the open embedding

(3.3)

\operatorname{pt}/Z_{G}\simeq\left(\underset{i}{\Pi}\,({\mathbb{G}}_{a}-0)\right)/T\hookrightarrow(\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})/T.

Let

(\underset{i}{\boxtimes}\,\operatorname{exp})/T\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}\left((\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})/T\right)

be the *-direct image along (
3.3
) of

R_{Z_{G}}[-r]\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{pt}/Z_{G}),

where:

•

R_{Z_{G}}
is the !-direct image of
{\mathsf{e}}\in\operatorname{Vect}=\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{pt})

along
\operatorname{pt}\to\operatorname{pt}/Z_{G}
;

•

r
is the semi-simple rank of
G
.

Remark 3.3.4
.

It follows from Remark
3.3.2
and the projection formula that when
{\mathsf{e}}={\mathbb{C}}
, under the Riemann-Hilbert equivalence, the object

(\underset{i}{\boxtimes}\,\operatorname{exp})/T
is the !-direct image along the projection

\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\to(\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})/T

of

\operatorname{add}^{*}(\operatorname{exp})\simeq\underset{i}{\boxtimes}\,\operatorname{exp}.

3.3.5.

Note that the map

{\mathfrak{p}}:\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{G}

factors as

\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T\overset{{\mathfrak{p}}/T}{\to}\operatorname{Bun}_{G}.

By construction, the map
\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\overset{\chi}{\to}{\mathbb{G}}_{a}
factors as

\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\overset{\underline{\chi}}{\to}\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a}\overset{\operatorname{sum}}{\to}{\mathbb{G}}_{a}.

Consider the resulting map

\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T\overset{\underline{\chi}/T}{\longrightarrow}(\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})/T.

Let

\operatorname{exp}_{\chi}/T\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T)

be the pullback of the object
(\underset{i}{\boxtimes}\,\operatorname{exp})/T
along the map
\underline{\chi}/T
.

Remark 3.3.6
.

It follows from Remark
3.3.4
that when
{\mathsf{e}}={\mathbb{C}}
, under the Riemann-Hilbert equivalence, the object
\operatorname{exp}_{\chi}/T

corresponds to the !-direct image along the projection

\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\to\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T

of the object

\operatorname{exp}_{\chi}:=\chi^{*}(\operatorname{exp})\in\operatorname{D-mod}(\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}).

3.3.7.

Let

\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!}:=({\mathfrak{p}}/T)_{!}(\operatorname{exp}_{\chi}/T)\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

Finally, set

\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}:={\mathbf{i}}^{L}(\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!}).

3.3.8.

We claim:

Proposition 3.3.9
.

The object
\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

is compact.

Proof.

Note that the object
(\underset{i}{\boxtimes}\,\operatorname{exp})/T
is a finite extension of

\delta
-functions.

The map

\underline{\chi}:\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}\to\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})

is a unipotent gerbe. Hence, the map

\underline{\chi}/T:\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T\to(\underset{i}{\Pi}\,{\mathbb{G}}_{a})/T

has a similar property.

It follows that the object
\operatorname{exp}_{\chi}/T\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{N,\rho(\omega_{X})}/T)

is a finite extension of
\delta
-functions. Hence, so is
\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!}
.

The assertion of the proposition follows now from Sect.
3.1.3
.

∎

3.4.
Construction of the functor

Having the object
\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}
at our disposal, the construction of the Langlands functor
in the Betti setting mimics its de Rham counterpart.

3.4.1.

As in the de Rham context, we define the functor

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti},L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}:{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})\to\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

to be given by acting on the object

\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}\in\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G}).

Since the object
\operatorname{Poinc}^{\operatorname{Vac,glob}}_{!,{\operatorname{Nilp}}}
is compact and
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
is compactly generated and
rigid, the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti},L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
preserves compactness.

3.4.2.

Let

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}:\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})

denote the right adjoint of
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti},L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
.

Since
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
is compactly generated and

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti},L}_{G,\operatorname{temp}}
preserves compactness, the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
is continuous.
By rigidity, it is automatically
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
-linear.

3.4.3.

We have the following assertion, which is parallel to Theorem
1.6.2
:

Theorem 3.4.4
.

The functor

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}:\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})

sends compact objects in
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to bounded below
objects in
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
.

The proof will be given in Sect.
4
.

3.4.5.

Assuming Theorem
3.4.4
, as in the de Rham context, we obtain that there exists a uniquely
defined continuous functor

(3.4)

{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}:\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}}),

characterized by the requirements that:

•

The functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}
sends compact objects in
\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
to eventually coconnective
objects in
{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
;

•

\Psi_{{\operatorname{Nilp}},\{0\}}\circ{\mathbb{L}}_{G}\simeq{\mathbb{L}}_{G,\operatorname{coarse}}
.

Moreover, the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}
is automatically
{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})
-linear.

3.4.6.

The geometric Langlands conjecture in the Betti context, originally formulated by D. Ben-Zvi and D. Nadler in
[
BZN
]
, says:

Conjecture 3.4.7
.

The functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}
of (
3.4
) is an equivalence.

3.5.
Comparsion of de Rham and Betti versions of GLC

Although the Betti and de Rham versions of the automorphic category (i.e.,

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
and
\operatorname{D-mod}_{\frac{1}{2}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})
) look very different, it turns
out that the respective versions of GLC in the two contexts are logically equivalent.

The passage is realized by showing that
either version is equivalent to its
restricted
variant, and the latter can be compared via Riemann-Hilbert.

The material in this subsection relies heavily on that of
[
AGKRRV
]
.

3.5.1.

We are going to prove:

Theorem 3.5.2
.

The de Rham version of GLC implies the Betti version.

In fact, we will prove a more precise version of Theorem
3.5.2
, see
Theorem
3.5.6
below.

3.5.3.

Let

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

be the ind-constructible category, as defined in
[
AGKRRV
, Sect. E.5]
.

Recall that according to
[
AGKRRV
, Proposition 18.3.2]
, the forgetful functor

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

is fully faithful.

Moreover, by
[
AGKRRV
, Theorem 18.3.6]
, the subcategory

(3.5)

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\subset\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})

equals

\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\underset{{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})}{\otimes}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})_{\operatorname{restr}}.

Note also that we have an equivalence

(3.6)

{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})\underset{{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}_{\check{G}})}{\otimes}{\operatorname{QCoh}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{\check{G}})_{\operatorname{restr}}\simeq{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}_{\check{G}}).

Hence, the functor
{\mathbb{L}}^{\operatorname{Betti}}_{G}
induces a functor, to be denoted

{\mathbb{L}}_{G}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}:\operatorname{Shv}^{\operatorname{Betti,constr}}_{\frac{1}{2},{\operatorname{Nilp}}}(\operatorname{Bun}_{G})\to{\operatorname{IndCoh}}_{\operatorname{Nilp}}(\operatorname{LS}^{\operatorname{Betti,restr}}_{\check{G}}).

3.5.4.

Horocycle dynamics in rank one invariant subvarieties I: weak measure classification and equidistribution
Jon Chaika, Barak Weiss, Florent Ygouf

Abstract
. Let ℳ \mathcal{M} be an invariant subvariety in the moduli space of translation surfaces. We contribute to the study of the dynamical properties of the horocycle flow on ℳ \mathcal{M} . In the context of dynamics on the moduli space of translation surfaces, we introduce the notion of a ‘weak classification of horocycle invariant measures’ and we study its consequences. Among them, we prove genericity of orbits and related uniform equidistribution results, asymptotic equidistribution of sequences of pushed measures, and counting of saddle connection holonomies. As an example, we show that invariant varieties of rank one, Rel-dimension one and related spaces obtained by adding marked points satisfy the ‘weak classification of horocycle invariant measures’. Our results extend prior results obtained by Eskin-Masur-Schmoll, Eskin-Marklof-Morris, and Bainbridge-Smillie-Weiss.



1.
Introduction

Any stratum of translation surfaces
\mathcal{H}
is endowed with an action of the group
G{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, and the restriction of this action to the subgroup

(1)

U=\{u_{s}:s\in\mathbb{R}\},\ \ \ \text{ where }\ u_{s}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left(\begin{matrix}1&s\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),

is called the
horocycle flow
. It is a longstanding open problem to understand the invariant measures for the
U
-action on
\mathcal{H}
. It has been known for a while, that a ‘sufficiently nice’ classification of such measures would have many interesting dynamical and geometrical consequences. See
[
Zor06
,
HMSZ06
]
, and see
[
CSW20
]
for a survey of recent results. This paper contributes to the investigation of this problem in two ways. Firstly, we obtain a partial classification of horocycle invariant ergodic measures, which we call
weak classification
, in the setting of
rank-one invariant subvarieties of Rel-dimension one
, or spaces obtained from them by adding marked points. All of these notions will be defined in §
2
. Secondly, we explore the implications of a weak classification result in rank-one invariant subvarieties (of arbitrary Rel-dimension) and show that it implies genericity of orbits, equidistribution of circle averages, and many additional equidistribution results.

We do not know whether the weak classification result holds in general rank-one invariant subvarieties; our approach crucially uses the Rel-dimension one assumption — see Remark
3.4
. Nevertheless, in a follow-up paper
[
CWY
]
, we extend the classification of
U
-orbit closures to general rank-one invariant subvarietes (omitting the assumption of one-dimensional Rel). We do so without obtaining a weak measure classification, but rather by an induction on the dimension of Rel. Although the current paper uses completely different techniques, it serves as the base of the induction used in
[
CWY
]
.

In the remainder of this introduction we give an initial description of the main results and discuss the proofs. Precise statements of results require some preparations and will be given in the body of the text. Let
\mathcal{M}
be an orbit-closure for the action of
G
. As was shown in a celebrated paper of Eskin, Mirzakhani and Mohammadi
[
EMM15
]
, such orbit-closures are (iso-area subloci of) affine orbifolds in period coordinates, and carry a wealth of additional structure. Following
[
AW
]
, we refer to these orbit closures as
invariant subvarieties
. A fundamental invariant of an invariant subvariety is its
rank
, introduced in
[
Wri15a
]
. We will be interested in the rank-one case, which is characterized by the fact that the
G
-action and the Rel foliation (see §
2.3
) are complementary foliations, in the sense that a neighborhood of a point can be described in terms of the
G
-action and the Rel-foliation. A further invariant is the
Rel dimension
(the complex dimension of leaves of the Rel foliation). Now suppose
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is an invariant subvariety. It was shown in
[
EM18
]
that
\mathcal{N}
is the support of a unique
\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
-invariant Radon probability measure, which has a geometric description in terms of the affine structure of
\mathcal{N}
. We denote this measure by
m_{\mathcal{N}}
. It is well-known that
m_{\mathcal{N}}
is ergodic for the action of
U
and, as first observed by Calta
[
Cal04
]
and explained in §
2.5
, such a measure can be pushed by real Rel to create more
U
-invariant ergodic measures. We say that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the
weak classification of
U
-invariant measures
if any ergodic
U
-invariant probability Radon measure measure on
\mathcal{M}
, that assigns measure zero to the set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connections, is obtained by this construction. In Theorem
3.3
we prove that invariant subvarieties of rank one and with Rel-dimension one satisfy weak classification. We remark that all invariant subvarieties modeled on a complex vector space of dimension 3 are of rank one with Rel-dimension one and thus satisfy the hypotheses of Theorem
3.3
; explicit examples are given in Corollaries
3.5
,
3.8
and
3.9
.

Theorem
6.1
gives severe restrictions on the collection of weak-* limits of measures obtained by averaging along a
U
-orbit in a rank-one invariant subvariety. This theorem uses the rank-one structure and is false in general, as shown in
[
CSW20
]
.
We then explore the implications of weak classification in the setting of rank-one invariant subvarieties (omitting the assumption that the Rel-dimension is one). Under these conditions, we can derive the following results. In Theorem

6.2
we prove that the
U
-orbit of any surface without horizontal saddle connections is generic for some
U
-ergodic invariant measure. Moreover, for a given test function
f
and a given error
\varepsilon
, genericity holds uniformly outside certain compact ‘singular sets’, see Theorem
6.4
. This result is an analogue of
[
DM93
, Thm. 3]
. We show in Theorem
7.3
that for any
U
-invariant ergodic measure
\mu
, the pushforwards
g_{t\ast}\mu
converge to
m_{\mathcal{N}}
for some invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}
, as
t\to\infty
and to
m_{\mathcal{N}^{\prime}}
for some invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{N}
, as
t\to-\infty
(unless
\mu
assigns positive measure to the set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connections, in which case they diverge in the space of probability measures). Here

(2)

\displaystyle g_{t}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathrm{diag}(e^{t},e^{-t})

denotes the geodesic flow. In Theorem
7.1
we obtain a similar result for pushing a
G
-invariant measure by a real Rel flow. Finally we show in Theorem
7.4
that in a rank one locus satisfying the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures, pushed circle averages
\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}g_{t\ast}r_{\theta}\delta_{q}
converge as
t\to\infty
, to the
G
-invariant measure on
\mathcal{N}=\overline{Gq}
. Here
r_{\theta}
is the rotation matrix by angle
\theta
. As a consequence, every surface satisfies quadratic growth of saddle connection holonomies. Our technique revisits and adapts arguments of Ratner
[
Rat91
]
for proving the measure classification of unipotent flows on homogeneous spaces, and subsequent work of Dani and Margulis
[
DM93
]
for harnessing this in order to obtain equidistribution results. The first papers in which these ideas were adapted to translation surfaces are
[
EMS03
,
EMWM06
]
, in which a weak classification of
U
-invariant measures was proved, and equidistribution for circle averages was deduced, in the context of branched covers of Veech surfaces.
In
[
CW10
]
and
[
BSW16
]
, measure classification results were obtained in eigenform loci in genus two, which are particular cases of a rank-one invariant subvariety with Rel-dimension one. Specifically, the paper
[
BSW16
]
proves a complete measure classification result, which is stronger than our ‘weak classification’, as it also classifies measures for which typical surfaces do have horizontal saddle connections. From this strong measure classification, equidistribution results are deduced, namely a strong version of Theorem
6.2
, and analogues of Theorems
7.3
,
7.1
and
7.4
. Our work adapts the techniques in
[
BSW16
]
to our more general setting and establishes that most of these equidistribution results, and additional ones, can be proved assuming a weaker measure classification result. A key technical input is Proposition
5.7
, which improves
[
BSW16
, Proof of Thm. 11.1, Step 3]
and uniformly bounds the time horocycle trajectories starting in a given compact set can spend close to certain singular sets.

2.
Strata of translation surfaces

In this section we collect some standard definitions and set our
notations. See
[
MT02
,
Zor06
,
FM14
,
Wri15b
,
BSW16
]
for
helpful surveys and additional
introductory material.

2.1.
Translation surfaces and developing map

Let
S
be a compact and oriented topological surface of genus
g
,
let
\Sigma
be a finite subset of
S
and let
\kappa=(k_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Sigma}
be an integer partition of
2g-2
. That is,
\sum_{\xi\in\Sigma}k_{\xi}=2g-2
and
k_{\xi}
are non-negative integers. We emphasize that
\kappa_{\xi}=0
is allowed, in which case
\xi
is called a
marked point
. A
singularity-labeled translation surface of type
\kappa
is a triple
q=(X_{q},\omega_{q},\mathfrak{z}_{q})
where
X_{q}
is a compact and connected Riemann surface of genus
g
,
\omega_{q}
is a non-zero holomorphic
1
-form on
X_{q}
whose set of zeroes is denoted by
\Sigma_{q}
and
\mathfrak{z}_{q}:\Sigma\to X_{q}
is an injective map whose image contains
\Sigma_{q}
and such that
\mathfrak{z}_{q}(\xi)
has order
k_{\xi}
. Where it will not cause any confusion we will omit the words ‘singularity-labeled’ and refer to
q
simply as a
translation surface of type
\kappa
. A
marking
on a translation surface
q
is a homeomorphism
\varphi:S\to X_{q}
such that
\varphi(\xi)=\mathfrak{z}_{q}(\xi)
for
\xi\in\Sigma
, and a pair
(q,\varphi)

is called a
marked translation surface with labeled singularities
. Two marked translation surfaces
(q_{1},\varphi_{1})
and
(q_{2},\varphi_{2})
are isomorphic if there is a biholomorphism
\phi:X_{q_{1}}\to X_{q_{2}}

such that
\phi^{\ast}\omega_{q_{2}}=\omega_{q_{1}}
,
\phi\circ\mathfrak{z}_{q_{1}}=\mathfrak{z}_{q_{2}}
and
\varphi_{2}^{-1}\circ\phi\circ\varphi_{1}
is isotopic to the identity rel
\Sigma
. Let

\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
be the space of isomorphism classes of marked translation surfaces of type
\kappa
with labeled singularities and let
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)

be the mapping class group of
S
that preserves
\Sigma

point-wise. It acts by precomposition on the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
and we
denote by
\mathcal{H}
the quotient and by
\pi:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathcal{H}
the
quotient map. The space
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}(\kappa)
is the

moduli space of translation surfaces of type
\kappa
with
labeled singularities
. There is a structure of linear
manifold on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
that turns the following map into a
local linear isomorphism.

\mathrm{dev}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}\to H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2}),\ \ \ \mathrm{dev}\left((q,\varphi)\right){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left(\gamma\mapsto\int_{\varphi(\gamma)}\omega_{q}\right).

Each homeomorphism of
S
preserving
\Sigma
acts on homology and
this induces an action of
the group
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
on
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
For this action, the map
\mathrm{dev}
is

\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
-equivariant. The space
\mathcal{H}
can be endowed
with the quotient topology and inherits an orbifold structure from the
one on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
.

The
area
of a translation surface is the integral of the volume
form induced by the 1-form, i.e.

\mathrm{Area}(q)=\frac{1}{2\mathbf{i}}\int_{S}\omega_{q}\wedge\overline{\omega}_{q}.

We denote the subset of area-one surfaces in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
and

\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
respectively.

Associated with a translation surface is a translation atlas on
X_{q}
,
obtained by locally integrating
\omega_{q}
.
A
saddle connection
on a translation surface
q
is a path

\gamma:[0,1]\to X_{q}
such that
\gamma(0),\gamma(1)\in\Sigma_{q},\gamma(t)\notin\Sigma_{q}
for
t\in(0,1)
, and
t\mapsto\gamma(t)
is linear in planar charts. We refer to the vector

\int_{\gamma}\omega_{q}\in\mathbb{C}\cong\mathbb{R}^{2}
as the
holonomy
of
\gamma

with respect to
q
, and denote it by
\mathrm{hol}_{q}(\gamma)
.

2.2.
G
-action, invariant subvarieties

For the remainder of this paper we set
G{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\operatorname{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
.
The space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
is endowed with a
G
-action, for which the developing map is equivariant with respect to the natural
action of
G
by post-composition on
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})
. More precisely, for any
(q,\varphi)
and any
g\in G
, we have

\mathrm{dev}(g(q,\varphi))=g\mathrm{dev}(q,\varphi),

and thus, for any saddle connection
\delta
on
q
and any
g\in G
,

(3)

g\mathrm{hol}_{q}(\delta)=\mathrm{hol}_{gq}(\delta).

The
G
-action preserves the area of a translation surface. Besides the group
U=\{u_{s}:s\in\mathbb{R}\}
defined in (
1
), we will consider

(4)

A{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\{g_{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}\text{ and }B{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}AU,

where
g_{t}
is as in (
2
). The action of
A
will be referred to as the
geodesic flow.

Definition 2.1

(Invariant subvariety)
.

An invariant subvariety is a subset
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
such that
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
is a locally finite union of connected equilinear manifolds, each of which projects to
\mathcal{M}
under
\pi
.

We recall from
[
SSWY
]
that an equilinear manifold is a properly embedded connected submanifold
X\subset\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
that is modeled via the developing map on a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace
V\subset H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{C})\cong H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})
defined over
\mathbb{R}
, and such that if
\gamma\in\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
preserves
X
, then
\gamma|_{V}
has determinant
1
. By the work of Eskin, Mirzakhani and Mohammadi (
[
EM18
]
and
[
EMM15
]
), the orbit-closures for the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
-action are exactly the invariant subvarieties, closed invariant sets are always finite unions of invariant subvarieties, and the orbit-closures and invariant measures for
G
coincide with the orbit-closures and invariant measures for its subgroup
P
. What we call ‘invariant subvariety’ was called ‘affine invariant manifold’ in
[
EMM15
]
; our choice follows
[
AW
]
. Note also that Definition
2.1
differs slightly from the definitions given in
[
EMM15
]
but will be more convenient for us, see
[
SSWY
]
for a more detailed discussion of this definition.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an invariant subvariety. A connected equilinear manifold
X
contained in
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\pi(X)=\mathcal{M}
is called an
irreducible component of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
. It is easy to see that
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
is the union of its irreducible components and that
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
acts transitively on the set of irreducible components. In particular, since the action of
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
is smooth, the irreducible components all have the same dimension. This allows us to define the dimension of
\mathcal{M}
, which we denote by
\dim(\mathcal{M})
, as the dimension of any one of the irreducible components.

Let
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
be the area-one locus in
\mathcal{M}
. Using Lebesgue measures on the irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
and a ‘cone construction’, one can construct a natural smooth
G
-invariant Radon probability measure of full support on
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
. For more details on this construction, using the language above, see
[
SSWY
]
. We will denote this measure by
m_{\mathcal{M}}
. It was shown in
[
EM18
]
that all
G
-invariant ergodic measures on strata arise in this way, and that
m_{\mathcal{M}}
is the unique
G
-invariant ergodic measure with
\mathrm{supp}\,m_{\mathcal{M}}=\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
.

2.3.
Rel foliation, Rel vector fields

We recall the following construction (see
[
Zor06
,
MW14
,
McM14
,
BSW16
]
). Let
{\mathrm{Res}}:H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})\to H^{1}(S;\mathbb{R}^{2})
be the canonical cocycle restriction map and denote its kernel by
\mathfrak{R}
. Clearly
\mathfrak{R}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace defined over
\mathbb{R}
. It will be useful to have an explicit set of generators for
\mathfrak{R}.

For any

\xi\in\Sigma
, denote by
\xi^{*}
the element of
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})
that is dual (with respect to the intersection form) to the cycle of
H_{1}(S\smallsetminus\Sigma;\mathbb{Z})
represented by a contractible loop on
S
that circles counterclockwise the point
\xi
. The elements
\{\xi^{*}:\xi\in\Sigma\}
are a set of generators for
\mathfrak{R}
, and they satisfy one relation
\sum_{\xi\in\Sigma}\xi^{*}=0.
In particular
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{R})=n-1
, where
n
is the cardinality of
\Sigma
.

There is a foliation of
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})
by translates of
\mathfrak{R}
which can be pulled back by the developing map to give a foliation of
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
. Since the map
\mathrm{dev}\circ{\mathrm{Res}}
is
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
-equivariant, there is an induced foliation of
\mathcal{H}
. The area of two translation surfaces which are in the same leaf for this foliation are the same and thus the foliation restricts to give a foliation of
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
. We call this foliation (on any one of the spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}},\mathcal{H},
or
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
) the
Rel foliation
; it has also been called the kernel foliation, the isoperiodic foliation, and the absolute period foliation.

Recall our convention that the singularities of surfaces in
\mathcal{H}
are labeled. This implies that elements of

\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
do not permute the singularities and act trivially on
\mathfrak{R}.

For any
v\in\mathfrak{R}
, the constant vector field
v
can be lifted to a vector field on
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
by
\mathrm{dev}
and since singularities are not permuted by
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
, this vector field descends to a well-defined vector field on
\mathcal{H}
, which is called the Rel vector field associated to
v
and denoted
\vec{v}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
q\in\mathcal{H}
and let
v\in\mathfrak{R}
. We say that
\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(q)
is well-defined and equal to
q^{\prime}
if there is a smooth path
\gamma:I\to\mathcal{H}
, where
I
is an interval that contains
0
and
1
, and such that
\gamma(0)=q
,
\gamma(1)=q^{\prime}
and
\forall t\in I,\ \frac{d}{dt}\gamma(t)=\vec{v}
. We denote by
\Delta
the subset of
\mathcal{H}\times\mathfrak{R}
consisting of pairs
(q,v)
for which
\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(q)
is well-defined.

We summarize some properties of this partially defined map, see
[
BSW16
, §4]
for proofs.

The set
\Delta
is open and the map

\displaystyle\Delta\to\mathcal{H},\ \ \ \ \ (q,v)\mapsto\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(q)

is continuous. The group
G
acts on
\mathfrak{R}
by restriction of its action by post-composition on
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{C})
, and for any
g\in G
,

(5)

(q,v)\in\Delta\iff(gq,gv)\in\Delta,\ \ \text{ and }\ \mathrm{Rel}_{gv}(gq)=g\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(q).

The set
\Delta
is completely described in
[
MW14
,
BSW16
]
in terms of saddle connections. In particular, if
n=2
and
q
has no saddle connection whose holonomy is parallel to
v
, then
(q,v)\in\Delta
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
be an invariant subvariety, let
X
be an irreducible component of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
and let
V
be the
\mathbb{C}
-linear subspace of
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{C})
defined over
\mathbb{R}
, on which
X
is modeled. We define

\mathfrak{R}_{X}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathfrak{R}\cap V.

Since
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
acts transitively on the set of irreducible components of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathrm{Mod}(S,\Sigma)
acts trivially on
\mathfrak{R}
, it is easy to see that if
Y
is another irreducible component then
\mathfrak{R}_{X}=\mathfrak{R}_{Y}
. We thus denote by
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
this common subspace. By construction, for any
q\in\mathcal{M}
and
v\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
we have
\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(q)\in\mathcal{M}
whenever the latter is well-defined, and
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the biggest linear subspace of
\mathfrak{R}
with this property. Whenever
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
is an inclusion of invariant subvarieties, we have
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{N}}\subset\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}.

We refer to the number
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}})
as the
Rel dimension of
\mathcal{M}
.

Let
V\subset H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{C})
be the local model of an irreducible component
X
of
\pi^{-1}(\mathcal{M})
. It follows from
[
AEM17
, Theorem 1.4]
that
{\mathrm{Res}}(V)
is a symplectic subspace of
H^{1}(S;\mathbb{C})
with respect to the symplectic structure induced by the cup product, and in particular, that
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}({\mathrm{Res}}(V))
is even. In particular this implies that for any affine subvariety
\mathcal{M}
,
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M})-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}})
is an even number.

Definition 2.3

(Rank)
.

The rank of
\mathcal{M}
is defined to be

\frac{1}{2}\left(\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M})-\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}})\right)
.

The rank is a fundamental invariant of an invariant subvariety. See
[
Wri15a
]
for additional characterizations. In this paper we will be particularly interested in the case
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{M})=1
. A simple dimension computation implies that in this case, the Rel leaves and the foliation into
G
-orbits are complementary foliations on
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
; that is, in the rank one case, for any
q\in\mathcal{M}
there are neighborhoods
\mathcal{U}
in
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
and neighborhoods
\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{V}_{2}
of the identity in
G
and of
0
in
\mathfrak{R}
, such that the map

\mathcal{V}_{1}\times\mathcal{V}_{2}\to\mathcal{U},\ \ \ \ \ (g,v)\mapsto\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(gq)

is a homeomorphism. It follows from the discussion preceding Definition
2.3
that if
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M})=2
then necessarily
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
is a closed
G
-orbit, and that being a rank-one invariant subvariety of Rel-dimension one is equivalent to the condition
\dim_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{M})=3
.

For fixed
v\in\mathfrak{R}
, we denote by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{v}
the set of surfaces
q\in\mathcal{H}
such that
(q,v)\in\Delta
; and for any
v\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
, we denote by
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}_{v}
the set of surfaces
q\in\mathcal{M}
such that
(q,v)\in\Delta
.

2.4.
Real Rel vector fields and real Rel flow

Following
[
BSW16
]
, we write
\mathbb{R}^{2}=\mathbb{R}_{x}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{y}
for the canonical splitting into
x
and
y
coordinates. This induces the splitting

(6)

\displaystyle H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}^{2})=H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})\oplus H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{y}),

where
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})\cong H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R})\cong H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{y})
. The subspace
H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})
shall be referred to as the
horizontal space
. If
v\in H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})
, it will be convenient to denote by
\mathbf{i}\cdot v
the corresponding vector in

; that is, with respect to the splitting (

(x,0)
projects to
x\in H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})
and
(0,x)
projects to
\mathbf{i}\cdot x\in H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{y})
. We denote

Z{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathfrak{R}\cap H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})\ \ \ \text{ and }Z_{\mathcal{M}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}\cap H^{1}(S,\Sigma;\mathbb{R}_{x})

where
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
is an invariant subvariety. If

, the vector field
\vec{v}
is called a
real Rel vector field
. For any

, we denote by
\mathcal{M}_{r}
the set of surfaces in
\mathcal{M}
which do not have a horizontal saddle connection strictly shorter than
r
, and by
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
the set of surfaces in
\mathcal{M}
that do not have horizontal saddle connections. With respect to the action of
G
by post-composition on
\mathfrak{R}
, the space
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
is invariant, and the subgroup
B\subset G
defined in (
4

invariant. We can use this action to define semi-direct products, as follows

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
be an invariant subvariety. We define

\displaystyle N_{\mathcal{M}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}B\ltimes Z_{\mathcal{M}}\ \text{ and }\ L_{\mathcal{M}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}G\ltimes\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}.

The set
\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
is a dense
G_{\delta}
subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Since
B
preserves horizontal saddle connections and real Rel is defined on surfaces without horizontal saddle connections, the map

N_{\mathcal{M}}\times\mathcal{M}_{\infty}\ni(b,z,q)\mapsto\mathrm{Rel}_{z}(bq)

is well-defined. Moreover, it satisfies the axioms of a group action (see
[
MW14
]
). In particular, for

, the map

defines a

on

need not satisfy the axioms of a group action, even if well-defined. In order to stress this point, for general
q\in\mathcal{M}
and

for which

is well-defined, we will write
gq\pluscirc v{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathrm{Rel}_{v}(gq).

2.5.
Real Rel pushes of ergodic
U
-invariant measures

For fixed
q\in\mathcal{H}
, let
Z^{(q)}
denote the set of
z\in Z
for which
\mathrm{Rel}_{z}(q)
is well-defined. By (
5
),
Z^{(q)}=Z^{(uq)}
for any

and
q\in\mathcal{H}
, and hence, for any ergodic
U
-invariant probability measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{H}
, there is a set
Z^{(\mu)}\subset Z
such that
Z^{(q)}=Z^{(\mu)}
for
\mu
-a.e.
q\in\mathcal{H}
. Let
z\in Z^{(\mu)}
. Denoting by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M})
the Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
\mathcal{M}
, we define a measure by

(7)

\displaystyle\forall X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}),\ \ \ {\mathrm{Rel}_{z}}_{\ast}\mu(X)=\mu(\mathrm{Rel}_{-z}(\mathcal{H}_{-z}^{\prime}\cap X)).

The measure
{\mathrm{Rel}_{z}}_{\ast}\mu
is also
U
-invariant and ergodic. In particular, let
\mathcal{M}\subset\mathcal{H}
be an invariant subvariety. If
\mu
is an ergodic
U
-invariant measure supported on
\mathcal{M}
and

, then
{\mathrm{Rel}_{z}}_{\ast}\mu
is also an ergodic
U
-invariant measure supported on
\mathcal{M}
. We finally denote

(8)

\displaystyle N_{\mu}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\left\{(b,z)\in N_{\mathcal{M}}:z\in Z^{(b_{\ast}\mu)}\ \text{and}\ {\mathrm{Rel}_{z}}_{\ast}(b_{\ast}\mu)=\mu\right\}.

It follows from
[
BSW16
, Cor. 4.20]
that

is a closed subgroup of
N_{\mathcal{M}}
, and thus

, the connected component of the identity in

q\in\mathcal{H}
is generic for an ergodic
U
-invariant measure
\mu
if for any compactly supported continuous function

,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}f(u_{s}q)\,ds\underset{T\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\int_{\mathcal{H}}f\ d\mu.

The set of generic points of
\mu
, which we denote by

, has full measure for
\mu
. We will need the following:

Proposition 2.5

(
[
BSW16
]
, Cor. 4.24, Prop. 4.27 & Cor. 4.28)
.

Let
\mu
be an ergodic
U
-invariant measure.
Then:

•

Z^{(\mu)}\subset Z^{(q)}
for any

.

•

If

and

and

.

•

If

with

, and

, then

for

We will also need the following result, which is proved in
[
CW10
, §6]
for eigenform loci in genus 2, and is a special case of a general result proved in
[

]
:

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a rank-one invariant subvariety and let

be a

-invariant ergodic probability measure on

. Suppose that

.

Notational conventions.
We will be interested in the horocycle dynamics on invariant subvarieties of rank one. Since the horocycle flow preserves the area of a surface, it is sufficient to consider the area one locus in an invariant subvariety.
From now on, to simplify notation, we will restrict attention to area-one surfaces and we will write
\mathcal{H}
for
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
,
\mathcal{M}
for
\mathcal{M}^{(1)}
, etc.
Also, although some statements are valid in greater generality,

will denote a rank one invariant subvariety in

.

3.
Horocycle dynamics in rank-one, Rel-one invariant subvarieties and compact extension

This section is concerned with the classification of ergodic
U
-invariant measures supported on invariant subvarieties of rank one and Rel-dimension one and related compact extensions where marked points are added.
Throughout this section we assume that, in addition to being of rank one,
\mathcal{M}
is of Rel-dimension one.

3.1.
The drift argument

The following key argument is modeled on Ratner’s work and was adapted to the present context in
[
EMM06
,
BSW16
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mu
be an ergodic
U
-invariant probability measure on
\mathcal{M}
. If
\mu
is not supported on a closed
U
-orbit then
\dim(N^{\circ}_{\mu})\geq 2
.

Remark 3.2
.

Contrary to
[
BSW16
, Prop 9.4]
, we do not need to assume that
\mu
does not assign positive measure to the set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connection.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and let
K\subset\Omega_{\mu}
be a compact subset such that
\mu(K)>1-\varepsilon
. Such a compact set exists by inner regularity of the measure
\mu
. By combining Birkhoff’s ergodic theorem and Egorov’s theorem, one obtains that there is another compact set
K_{0}\subset\mathcal{M}
and
T_{0}
large enough so that for any
T>T_{0}
and any
q\in K_{0}
,

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q\in K\}|>1-2\varepsilon.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
denote the Lie algebra of
G
, which we identify with the set of
2\times 2
matrices with vanishing trace, endowed with the Lie bracket
[A,B]=AB-BA
, and let
\exp:\mathfrak{g}\to G
be the exponential map. We also choose a
G
-equivariant linear isomorphism
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{2}
where the action of
G
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}
is the usual linear action. Under this isomorphism, we have
Z_{\mathcal{M}}\simeq\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
. In the remainder of this proof, we shall abuse slightly notations and write elements of
\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
as elements of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

If
\mu
is not supported on a closed orbit,
there is a point
q\in K_{0}
and a sequence of points
q_{n}\in K_{0}\smallsetminus Uq
that converges to
q
. For
n
large enough, we can find
V_{n}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
W_{n}\in\mathfrak{R}_{\mathcal{M}}
such that

(\exp(V_{n}),W_{n})\in L_{\mathcal{M}}\smallsetminus U,\ \ \ (V_{n},W_{n})\to(0,0)\ \ \ \text{ and }\ q_{n}=\exp(V_{n})q\pluscirc W_{n}.

Write

\displaystyle V_{n}=\begin{pmatrix}a_{n}&&u_{n}\\
v_{n}&&-a_{n}\end{pmatrix}\ \ \text{ and }\ W_{n}=(x_{y},y_{n}).

Let
P_{n}(s)=-v_{n}s^{2}+(1-2a_{n})s+u_{n}
. A computation using (
5
) shows that for any
s>0
,

\displaystyle u_{s}q_{n}=\exp\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a_{n}+sv_{n}&&0\\
v_{n}&&-(a_{n}+sv_{n})\end{smallmatrix}\right)u_{P_{n}(s)}q\pluscirc(x_{n}+sy_{n},y_{n}).

Let
0<\eta<1
and define

\displaystyle I_{n}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\{s\in\mathbb{R}:\max(|a_{n}+sv_{n}|,|x_{n}+sy_{n}|)<\eta\varepsilon\}.

If
n
is large enough, the set
I_{n}
contains
0
and we denote by
J_{n}
the connected component of
I_{n}
that contains
0
. For any
s>0
, we have
P_{n}^{\prime}(s)=-2(sv_{n}+a_{n})+1
and thus if
s\in I_{n}
we get
|P_{n}^{\prime}(s)|<1+2\eta\varepsilon<1+2\varepsilon
. We want to find
s\in J_{n}
such that

(10)

u_{s}q_{n}\text{ and }u_{P_{n}(s)}q\text{ are both in }K

and

(11)

\frac{\eta\varepsilon}{4}\leq\max(|a_{n}+sv_{n}|,|x_{n}+sy_{n}|)\leq\eta\varepsilon.

Notice that if
n
is large enough then
S_{n}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\sup(J_{n})
is larger than
T_{0}
and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{S_{n}}\left|\left\{s\in[0,S_{n}]:\max(|a_{n}+sv_{n}|,|x_{n}+sy_{n}|)<\frac{\eta\varepsilon}{4}\right\}\right|<\frac{1}{2}.

Finally, the set of times
s\in[0,S_{n}]
for which
u_{s}q_{n}
is in
K
has measure at least
(1-2\varepsilon)S_{n}
and likewise for
u_{s}q
. Since
P_{n}^{\prime}(s)
is bounded by
1+2\varepsilon
on
J_{n}
, the set of times
s\in[0,S_{n}]
for which
u_{P_{n}(s)}q
belongs to
K
has measure at least
(1-4\varepsilon)S_{n}
. Consequently, whenever
\varepsilon
is small enough, for all
n
large enough (depending on
T_{0}
and hence on
\varepsilon
),
s=s_{n}
satisfies the desired properties (
10
) and (
11
). We choose
\eta
small enough to that
\exp(v)p\pluscirc w
is well defined for any
v
,
w
with coefficients smaller that
\eta\varepsilon
and
p\in K
. Passing to a converging subsequence in
u_{s_{n}}q
and
u_{p_{n}(s)}q
and invoking Proposition 4.6 in
[
BSW16
]
, we obtain a pair of generic points
p
and
p^{\prime}
in
K
such that
p^{\prime}=\exp(V)p\pluscirc W
with

\displaystyle V=\begin{pmatrix}a&&0\\
0&&-a\end{pmatrix}\ \mathrm{and}\ W=(x,0),

and
\eta\varepsilon/4\leq\max(a,x)\leq\varepsilon
. From Proposition
2.5
that
\exp(V,W)\in N_{\mu}
. Since
\varepsilon
was arbitrary, this shows that
N_{\mu}\smallsetminus U
contains elements arbitrarily close to the identity. Since
N_{\mu}
is a Lie subgroup of
N_{\mathcal{N}}
, this implies
\dim N_{\mu}^{\circ}>1
.
∎

3.2.
Saddle connection free
U
-invariant ergodic measures

The set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connections is invariant under
U
. In particular, if
\mu
is an ergodic
U
-invariant measure, the measure of this set is either 1 or 0. In the second case, we say
\mu

is a
saddle connection free measure.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mu
be a saddle connection free ergodic
U
-invariant probability measure on
\mathcal{M}
. Then there is some invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
and some
x\in Z_{\mathcal{M}}
such that
\mu=\mathrm{Rel}_{x\ast}m_{\mathcal{N}}
.

Proof.

It is well-known that the normalized length measure on a periodic
U
-orbit is not saddle connection free. Thus, by Proposition
3.1
there are
a\geq 0
,
w\in Z_{M}
and a homomorphism
\rho:\mathbb{R}\to N^{\circ}_{\mu}\smallsetminus U
such that

(12)

\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\rho(t)=\Bigl(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&&0\\
0&&-a\end{smallmatrix}\right),w\Bigr),\ \ \text{ where }(a,w)\neq(0,0).

If
a=0
, we have that
\rho(t)=(\mathrm{Id},tw)
and thus
\rho(t)_{\ast}\mu={\mathrm{Rel}_{tw}}_{\ast}\mu=\mu
. Therefore
\mu
is invariant under both
U
and
\mathrm{Rel}_{tw}
for all
t
. Since
\mathcal{M}
is of Rel-dimension 1, we deduce via Proposition
2.6
that
\mu=m_{\mathcal{M}}
. If
a\neq 0
we have

\displaystyle\rho(t)=\Bigl(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}e^{at}&&0\\
0&&e^{-at}\end{smallmatrix}\right),e^{at}w-w\Bigr)

and it follows via (
5
) that the measure
\mu_{0}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}{\mathrm{Rel}_{-w}}_{\ast}\mu
is invariant under the upper triangular subgroup
B
. Here we have used the assumption that
\mu
assigns zero measure to surfaces without horizontal saddle connections, since it implies
Z_{\mathcal{M}}\subset Z^{(\mu)}
, that is
\mathrm{Rel}_{-w}(q)
is well-defined for every
w\in Z_{\mathcal{M}}
, for
\mu
-a.e.
q
. It follows from
[
EM18
]
that there is an invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that
\mu_{0}=m_{\mathcal{N}}
and thus
\mu=\mathrm{Rel}_{w\ast}m_{\mathcal{N}}
.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

The drift argument relies on a fine analysis of the displacement between two horocycle trajectories of nearby points. In order to remove the assumption that
\mathcal{M}
is of Rel-dimension one, it is tempting to argue inductively, replacing the horocycle flow by a well-chosen flow in the group
N_{\mu}
in order to pick up additional invariance of the measure. However, to compute the displacement between two nearby trajectories under this new flow, one would need the ‘group-action property’
(
5
), with
G
replaced by larger groups
G\ltimes\mathfrak{R}_{0},
for
\mathfrak{R}_{0}\subset\mathfrak{R}
(even the weaker property
\mathrm{Rel}_{x_{1}+x_{2}}(q)=\mathrm{Rel}_{x_{1}}(\mathrm{Rel}_{x_{2}}(q))
for suitable
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathfrak{R}
might suffice). These relations are not true in general.

We now list some examples to which our results apply. For relevant definitions, see
[
McM06
]
. Let
D\equiv 0\mod 4
or
D\equiv 1\mod 4
, let
\mathcal{O}_{D}
be the quadratic order of discriminant
D
and let
\Omega E_{D}
be the space of Prym eigenforms for real multiplication by
\mathcal{O}_{D}
. Let
\Omega E_{D}^{odd}(2,2)
and
\Omega E_{D}(2,1,1)
be the intersection of
\Omega E_{D}
with the strata
\mathcal{H}^{odd}(2,2)
and
\mathcal{H}(2,1,1)
. It follows from
[
LN18
]
that these spaces are not empty whenever
D\not\equiv 5\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
, in which case there are at most two connected components. Any such connected component is a rank 1 invariant subvariety of Rel-dimension 1, see
[
LN16
, Lemma 3.1]
. Thus, Theorem
3.3
has the following corollary:

Corollary 3.5
.

Let
D\equiv 0,1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
,
D\not\equiv 5\ \mathrm{mod}\ 8
be a positive integer that is not a square and let
\mathcal{M}
be a connected component of
\Omega E_{D}^{odd}(2,2)
or
\Omega E_{D}(2,1,1)
. Then
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a connected component of
\Omega E_{D}
. In the case where
D
is a square,
\mathcal{M}
consists of branched covers of flat tori and the fact that
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures follows from the work of Eskin, Markloff and Morris
[
EMWM06
, Theorem 2.1]
. When
D
is not a square,
\mathcal{M}
is a nonarithmetic invariant subvariety, which means that its field of definition is a non trivial algebraic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. The examples mentioned in Corollary
3.5
do not arise via a covering construction and thus provide the first non-trivial examples in genus 3 of invariant subvarieties in which a classification of
U
-invariant measures is known. It is worth mentioning that any nonarithmetic rank 1 invariant subvariety of Rel-dimension 1 in
\mathcal{H}^{odd}(2,2)
arises in this way, see
[
Ygo20
]
. The other Prym eigenform loci in genus 3 are
\Omega(\kappa){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\Omega E_{D}\cap\mathcal{H}(\kappa)
for
\kappa\in\{4^{odd},4^{hyp},(2,2)^{hyp},(1,1,1,1)\}
. The first three are a finite union of Teichmüller curves (
i.e.,
satisfy
dim(\mathcal{M})=2
), see
[
LN18
, Prop 2.3]
for a proof in the case
(2,2)^{hyp}
, and the latter has Rel-dimension 2. In genus 4 and 5, no explicit description of the connected components of the Prym eigenform loci is known besides the case
\mathcal{H}(6)
that is addressed in
[
LN20
]
. Nevertheless, we can still use Prym eigenforms to produce more examples of rank one invariant subvarieties of Rel-dimension 1. We recall the following that lists all the potential rank one invariant subvarieties of Rel-dimension 1 arising from Prym eigenforms:

Lemma 3.6

(
[
LN16
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
D\equiv 0,1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
, let
g=4,5
, let
\kappa
be an integer partition of
2g-2
such that
\Omega E_{D}(\kappa)\neq\emptyset
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a connected component of
\Omega E_{D}(\kappa)
. Then
\mathcal{M}
is a rank one invariant subvariety and its Rel-dimension is 1 if, and only if,
\kappa
belongs to the following list:

(3,3),(2,2,2)^{even},(1,1,4),(1,1,2,2),(4,4)^{even}.

Proposition 3.7
.

For any
\kappa
as in Lemma
3.6
, there is
D\equiv 0,1\ \mathrm{mod}\ 4
such that
D
is not a square and
\Omega E_{D}(\kappa)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

It is explained in
[
LN16
]
that each
\kappa
as in Lemma
3.6
is associated with a rank 2 invariant subvariety denoted by
Prym(\kappa)
, and the latter can easily be verified to be arithmetic. It thus follows from
[
EFW18
, Theorem 1.7]
that this affine variety itself contains a nonarithmetic rank one invariant subvariety
\mathcal{M}
. It is not hard to see that
\mathcal{M}
must contain a surface

that contains a hyperbolic matrix

in its Veech group. See for instance
[
Wri14
, Remark 4.2]
. It follows from
[

, Theorem 3.5]
that

with
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D})=\mathbb{Q}(tr(A))
. If

is rational then it follows from
[

, Theorem 9.8]
that

covers a torus, in contradiction with the fact that
\mathcal{M}
is nonarithmetic.
∎

Using Theorem
3.3
, we get:

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a connected component of
\Omega E_{D}(\kappa)
where

and

. Then

-invariant measures.

The Pentagon variety described in
[
EMMW20
]
yields another infinite family of quadratic rank one invariant subvarieties of Rel-dimension 1 contained in

. It is explained in
[

that the intersection of the Pentagon variety with

, we deduce the existence of infinitely many quadratic rank one invariant subvarieties of Rel-dimension 1.

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be one of the infinitely many quadratic rank one invariant subvarieties contained in the intersection of the Pentagon variety with

. Then
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of

-invariant measures.

4.
Adding marked points

Let
\Sigma
be a collection of singularities and let
\kappa=(k_{\xi})_{\xi\in\Sigma}
be a partition of
2g-2
. We will be interested in the case that some of the
k_{\xi}
are zero (and thus correspond to marked points). Let

(13)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{0}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\{\xi\in\Sigma:k_{\sigma}\neq 0\}

be the subset of ‘non-removable’ singularities,
let
\mathfrak{S}\subset\Sigma
be a subset containing
\Sigma_{0}
,
and
\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}(k_{\xi})_{\xi\in\mathfrak{S}}
. By definition of
\Sigma_{0}
,
\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}}
is also an integer partition of
2g-2
. Let
\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathcal{H}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}})
be the moduli space of singularity-labeled translation surfaces of type
\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}}
. We also consider the space
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}})
of marked surfaces of type
\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}}
and we denote by
\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}})\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}}
the canonical projection that forgets the marking. We define the forgetful map

(14)

\displaystyle p_{\mathfrak{S}}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathfrak{S}},\ \ q\mapsto(X_{q},\omega_{q},{\mathfrak{z}_{q}}|_{\mathfrak{S}}),

and the corresponding map at the level of marked surfaces

(15)

\displaystyle\tilde{p}_{\mathfrak{S}}:\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}\to\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}(\kappa_{\mathfrak{S}}),\ \ (q,f)\mapsto((X_{q},\omega_{q},{\mathfrak{z}_{q}}|_{\mathfrak{S}}),f).

These two maps satisfy

(16)

\displaystyle\pi_{\mathfrak{S}}\circ\tilde{p}_{\mathfrak{S}}=p_{\mathfrak{S}}\circ\pi,

and we have:

Proposition 4.1
.

The fibers of
\tilde{p}_{\mathfrak{S}}
are connected.

Proof.

Let
(q_{1},f_{1})
and
(q_{2},f_{2})
be two marked surfaces in
\mathcal{H}_{\mathrm{m}}
that have the same image under
\tilde{p}_{\mathfrak{S}}
. By definition, this means that there is biholomorphism
\varphi:X_{q_{1}}\to X_{q_{2}}
such that

Remark 4.4
.

The special case of Theorem
4.3
in which

is a closed
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})
-orbit is the main result of
[

]
. In the same paper, the authors deduce that the orbit closure of a translation surface that is a translation branched cover of a Veech surface satisfies the weak classification of

-invariant measures. We recall that a translation cover of

is a pair

where

is a holomorphic map such that

and that
q_{0}
is a Veech surface if its orbit is closed. Analogously, it should be possible to deduce from Theorem
4.3

that the orbit-closure of a translation branched cover of a surface in a rank one invariant subvariety of Rel dimension one, satisfies weak classification of
U
-invariant measures. This would require pinning down the relationship, as dynamical systems for the
U
-action, between the spaces
p_{\mathfrak{S}}^{-1}(\mathcal{N})
of Proposition
4.2
(adding marked points to surfaces in an invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}
), and the space of branched translation covers of a fixed topological type of surfaces in
\mathcal{N}
.
See
[
SW08
, Section 3]
for a related discussion of the moduli space of a branched covers.

For

as in Proposition
4.2
, the invariant subvarieties

such that

correspond to finite

-equivariant configurations of marked points over

. Such configurations have been classified, see
[
EFW18
,
AW21
]
.

As a corollary we obtain that adding marked points to invariant subvarieties that satisfy themselves the weak classification of

From

Proposition 5.3

6.
From measure classification to equidistribution of orbits

For any rank-one invariant subvariety
\mathcal{M}
, we denote by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
the set of surfaces
q\in\mathcal{M}
without horizontal saddle connections for which there is a proper invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{M}
and
x\in Z_{\mathcal{N}}^{\perp}
such that
\mathrm{Rel}_{x}(q)\in\mathcal{N}
. By Proposition 1.3 in
[

]
, there are at most countably many invariant subvarieties of
\mathcal{M}
. We assume there are countably many, and denote them by
\left(\mathcal{N}^{(i)}\right)_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
(the reader will have no difficulty in adapting our arguments to the case in which there are finitely many). With this notation,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}=\underset{(i,j)\in\mathbb{N}\times\mathbb{N}}{\bigcup}Z\left(\mathcal{N}^{(i)},j\right);

in particular, the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
is measurable.

The following result is independent of the weak classification of
U
-invariant measure and holds in arbitrary rank-one invariant subvarieties. It contrasts with a surprising phenomenon in
\mathcal{H}(1,1)
discovered in
[
CSW20
, Theorem 1.2]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a rank-one invariant subvariety, let

and

\ast
limit of

U
-invariant Radon probability measure that assigns zero measure to
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
and to
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}

Proof.

Let
\nu
be a weak-
\ast
limit of a convergent subsequence

.

Step 1.
We show that
\nu(\mathcal{M})=1
. It is enough to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
, there is a compact

such that
\nu(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
. We take

as in item (II) in Theorem
5.1
for

. By assumption

does not have any horizontal saddle connections and in particular, no horizontal saddle connection shorter than

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q\notin K\}|<\varepsilon.

It follows that for

, we have
\mu_{T_{n}}(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Since

is compact, by a well-known property of weak-* convergence, we obtain
\nu(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

Step 2.
We now show that
\nu(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=0
. For any
i>0
, let
K_{i}\subset\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
be the set of surfaces whose saddle connections have length bounded below by

, and which have horizontal saddle connections, all of whose lengths are bounded above by
i

exhaust
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
and thus it is enough to show that for any
i>0
and any

By a well-known property of weak-* convergence, it suffices to show that for each
\varepsilon>0
and each
i

that contains

, such that

large enough. Once again, let

be as in item (II) in Theorem

for

. Since the lengths of all saddle connection are bounded below on the compact set
K
, there is

such that

. We denote by
\mathcal{U}
the set

and let

. Since

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{t}\notin K\}|<\varepsilon.

Now, let

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{U}}(u_{s}q)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{K^{c}}(g_{-t}u_{s}q)\,ds

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Te^{-2t}}\left|\left\{s^{\prime}\in\left[0,Te^{-2t}\right]:u_{s^{\prime}}q_{t}\notin K\right\}\right|<\varepsilon.

We now show that for any invariant subvariety

that contains

5.7
, applied to

.

Theorem 6.2

(Genericity)
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures. The orbit of any surface

without horizontal saddle connections is generic for some measure as in §

, an invariant subvariety

and

and

\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s\ast}\delta_{q}\,ds\underset{T\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\mathrm{Rel}_{x\ast}m_{\mathcal{N}}\ \ \ \ \text{ (w.r.t. the weak-* topology).}

Remark 6.3
.

It is instructive to compare Theorem
6.2
with
[
CSW20
, Thm. 1.4]
, which gives examples of surfaces in the stratum
\mathcal{H}(1,1)
which are not generic for any measure. Note that
\mathcal{H}(1,1)
is not of rank one. It should also be noted that the genericity result in
[
BSW16
, Thm. 11.1]
is stronger than Theorem
6.2
, as it does not assume that

has no horizontal saddle connections. This is possible since it relies on a stronger measure classification result.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be the invariant subvariety of smallest dimension amongst the collection of all invariant subvarieties
\mathcal{O}
such that there is
\xi\in Z_{\mathcal{M}}
satisfying
\mathrm{Rel}_{\xi}(q)\in\mathcal{O}
. Such a pair
(\mathcal{N},\xi)
is unique by Proposition
5.3
. For any
T>0
, we define

\displaystyle\nu_{T}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}u_{s\ast}\delta_{\mathrm{Rel}_{\xi}(q)}\,ds.

The measure
\nu_{T}
is supported on
\mathcal{N}
. By definition of
\mathcal{N}
, we have that
\mathrm{Rel}_{\xi}(q)\notin\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
and since
q
does not have horizontal saddle connections we have that
\mathrm{Rel}_{\xi}(q)\in\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
. It follows from Theorem
6.1
(applied to
\mathcal{N}
and
\mathrm{Rel}_{\xi}(q)
) that any limit
\nu
of a convergent subsequence of
\nu_{T}
is a Radon probability measure that assigns zero measure to the set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connections, as well as to the singular set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{N}}
. We decompose the measure
\nu
into
U

\displaystyle\nu=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{p}\ d\nu(p)

where there is a
\nu
-conull set
E
comprised of surfaces

for which

is an ergodic

-invariant probability Radon measure that assigns zero measure to

. Let

\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of

by an element of real Rel. Since
\nu_{p}

and

and

. By construction,

contains

and satisfies the following property: for any compact subset

of
\mathcal{M}_{R_{n}}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{i\leq n}Z\left(\mathcal{N}^{(i)},R_{n}\right)
, there is

such that

(46)

\displaystyle\forall q\in L,\ \forall T>S_{n},\ \frac{1}{T}|\{t\in[0,T]:u_{s}q\in\mathcal{U}_{n}\}|\leq\frac{1}{n}.

Now, let

, let

and let

be as in the contradicting assumption for

and

. For any

, the set

is a compact subset of

, the surface

satisfies (

, this concludes the construction of

Now define a sequence of measures by

\displaystyle\mu_{n}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\frac{1}{T_{n}}\int_{0}^{T_{n}}\delta_{u_{s}q_{n}}\,ds.

Our goal is to show that this sequence of measures weak-* converges to

converges to some measure

Step 1
. We show that

\varepsilon>0
, there is a compact

such that

and

for

and

. For any

Step 2.
We now want to show that
\nu(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=0
. By construction, the
(L_{i})_{i>0}
exhaust
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
and thus it is enough to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
and
i>0
, there is an open set
\mathcal{U}
that contains

and such that
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})<\varepsilon
for any

and set

. By (i) and the definition of

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})=\frac{1}{T_{n}}|\{t\in[0,T_{n}]:u_{s}q_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{N}\}|\leq\frac{1}{N}<\varepsilon.

Step 3.
We now prove that if
\mathcal{N}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{M}
is an invariant subvariety and

, then

. Let

such that
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{(i)}
. We recall that we had an exhaustion by nested compact sets

of

. It is enough to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
and

, there is a open set
\mathcal{U}
that contains

and such that

for any

and set

. By (i) and the definition of

,
\mathcal{U}_{N}
contains

and for any

) that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})=\frac{1}{T_{n}}|\{t\in[0,T_{n}]:u_{s}q_{n}\in\mathcal{U}_{N}\}|\leq\frac{1}{N+1}<\varepsilon.

Step 4.
Since for any
n>0
, we have
T_{n}>n
, the measure
\nu
is a
U
-invariant Radon measure. It follows from Step 1 that it is a probability measure, from Step 2 that it assigns measure zero to the set of surfaces that have a horizontal saddle connection and from Step 3 that it assigns measure zero to the set
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
. Let

\displaystyle\nu=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\nu_{p}\ d\nu(p)

be the decomposition of

into

-invariant ergodic measures. There is a
\nu
-conull set

for which

-invariant probability Radon measure that assigns measure zero to the set of surfaces with horizontal saddle connections and such that

-invariant measure, for every

∎

7.
Equidistribution results for sequences of measures

In this section we state and prove several equidistribution results regarding limits of sequences of measures. The first result extends
[
BSW16
, Thm. 1.5]
.

Theorem 7.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures, let
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{M}
be an invariant subvariety and let
x\in Z_{\mathcal{M}}
. There are an invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
that contains
\mathcal{L}
and
x\in Z_{\mathcal{N}}
such that, in the weak-* topology,
{\mathrm{Rel}_{tx}}_{\ast}m_{\mathcal{L}}\to m_{\mathcal{N}}
as
t\to\infty
.

For the proof of Theorem
7.1
we will need the following:

Lemma 7.2
.

Suppose
\mathcal{N}_{1},\mathcal{N}_{2}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{M}
are two invariant subvarieties. If there is
r>0
such that
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}(Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},r))>0
then
\mathcal{N}_{1}\subset\mathcal{N}_{2}
.

Proof.

Since
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
is
U
-invariant and ergodic, we have that
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}(Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},r))=1
. For any
t
, since
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
is
g_{t}
-invariant and
g_{-t}(Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},r))=Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},e^{-t}r)
, we have
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}(Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},e^{-t}r))=1.
It follows that
(\mathcal{N}_{2})_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n>0}Z(\mathcal{N}_{2},re^{-n})
also has full measure for
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
. Since
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
assigns zero measure to surfaces with horizontal saddle connections, we deduce that
m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}(\mathcal{N}_{2})=1
. This implies that
\mathcal{N}_{2}
contains
\mathcal{N}_{1}=\mathrm{supp}\,m_{\mathcal{N}_{1}}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{L},x)
be the collection of invariant subvarieties
\mathcal{N}
of
\mathcal{M}
that contain
\mathcal{L}
and such that

, and let

be of smallest dimension.
We will show that

converges to

as

.

It is enough to show that if
\mu_{n}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mu_{t_{n}}
is a convergent subsequence then its limit is

. Denote by
\nu
the limit of
\mu_{n}
. By construction,
\mathrm{supp}\,\nu\subset\mathcal{N}
.

Step 1.
We show that
\nu
is a probability measure. It is enough to show that for any
\varepsilon>0
there is compact
K\subset\mathcal{M}
such that
\mu_{n}(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
for
n
large
enough. We choose
K
as in Theorem
5.1
, item (II) for
r=1
. The measure

assigns full measure to surfaces without horizontal saddle connections, and since this set is invariant under

we have
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=1
. Therefore there is

that is Birkhoff generic for the indicator function

with respect to

. By the choice of

, for any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{n}\in K\}|>1-\varepsilon.

By Birkhoff genericity, the left-hand side tends to

as

and thus
\mu_{n}(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

Step 2.
We now show that
\nu(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=0
. For any
i>0
, let
K_{i}
be the closure of the set of surfaces whose saddle connection lengths are bounded below by
\frac{1}{i}
, and which have at least one horizontal saddle connection, with the lengths of all horizontal saddle connections bounded above by
i
. By construction the
(K_{i})_{i>0}
exhaust
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
and thus it is enough to show that for any
i>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is an open set
\mathcal{U}
that contains
K_{i}
and such that
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})\leq\varepsilon
for any

large enough. Once again, let
K
be as in item (II) in Theorem
5.1
for
r
as above. Since the length of saddle connections is bounded below on compact sets and
K
is compact, there is a

such that

, so that

K_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}g_{t}(K^{c}).

Let

be large enough so that
t_{n}>t
. We have
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{re^{t_{n}}})=0
and thus there is a surface
q_{n}
without horizontal saddle connections shorter than

that is Birkhoff generic for
\mathbf{1}_{K^{c}}
with respect to
\mu_{n}
. The surfaces

do not have horizontal saddle connections shorter that

and thus there is

such that for any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{n}^{\prime}\notin K\}|<\varepsilon.

Now, let

be large enough so that

. For any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{U}}(u_{s}q_{n})\,ds=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{K^{c}}(g_{-t}u_{s}q_{n})\ ds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Te^{-2t}}\left|\left\{s\in\left[0,Te^{-2t}\right]:u_{s}q_{n}^{\prime}\notin K\right\}\right|<\varepsilon.

By Birkhoff genericity the quantity on the left-hand side converges to

as

and thus
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})\leq\varepsilon
.

Step 3.
Let
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{N}
be an invariant subvariety and let

. We want to show that
\nu(Z(\mathcal{N}^{\prime},r)=0
. It is enough to show that for any compact subset

and

there is an open set

that contains

such that

for

large enough. Let
\mathcal{U}
and

be as in Proposition

.

Suppose first that

is not contained in
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. If

then

and thus by Lemma

, we get that

, which is a contradiction. It follows that

and thus by Birkhoff’s Theorem, one can find a point

such that

\displaystyle\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})=\underset{T\to\infty}{\lim}\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{n}\in\mathcal{U}\}|.

Applying Proposition
5.7
to

shows that for all

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{n}\in\mathcal{U}\}|<\varepsilon.

This shows that
\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})\leq\varepsilon
.

Suppose now that
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
. By definition of
\mathcal{N}
, we know that

. It thus follows from Proposition

that the map

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{N}_{\infty}^{\prime}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{N},\ \ \ \ \ \Phi(q,x){\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\mathrm{Rel}_{tx}(q)

is injective. This means that for any

large enough such that

, the surface

does not belong to

and thus applying once more Proposition

to

shows that

for

To deduce now that

, we use the ergodic decomposition argument which we used in Step 4 of the Proof of Theorem

.
∎

Theorem 7.3
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of
U
-invariant measures and let
\mu
be a
U
-invariant ergodic Radon probability measure on
\mathcal{M}
. Then there is an invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\subset\mathcal{M}
such that
{g_{t}}_{\ast}\mu
converges to
m_{\mathcal{N}}
as

. If we furthermore assume that
\mu(\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=1
, then there is an invariant subvariety
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{N}
such that
{g_{t}}_{\ast}\mu
converges to
m_{\mathcal{L}}
as

.

Remark
. If
\mu
is an ergodic
U
-invariant measure with
\mu(\mathcal{M}_{\infty})<1
then by ergodicity
\mu
-a.e

has a horizontal saddle connection and
{g_{t}}_{\ast}\mu
converges to the zero measure as

.

Proof of Theorem
7.3
.

We first prove the first assertion. We assume first that for any invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{M}
, we have
\mu(\mathcal{N})=0
. In that case, we are going to show that
{g_{t}}_{\ast}\mu
converges to
m_{\mathcal{M}}
as
t\to\infty
. Let
\mu_{n}={g_{t_{n}}}_{\ast}\mu
be a convergent subsequence when

and let
\nu
be its limit. If generic surfaces for
\mu
have some horizontal saddle connections, let
r
be the length of the shortest of them. Otherwise, let

.

Step 1.
We show that
\nu(\mathcal{M})=1
. Let
\varepsilon>0
. It is enough to show that there is a compact
K
such that
\mu_{n}(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
. Let
K
be a compact
as in item (II) of Theorem
5.1
with

as above. The measure
\mu_{n}
assigns measure zero to surfaces with a saddle connection shorter than
r
and thus there is a surface

that is Birkhoff generic for the indicator function
\mathbf{1}_{K}
with respect to
\mu_{n}
. By definition of

, for any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}|\{s\in[0,T]:u_{s}q_{n}\in K\}|\geq 1-\varepsilon.

By Birkhoff genericity, the quantity on the left-hand side converges to
\mu_{n}(K)
as

and thus
\mu_{n}(K)\geq 1-\varepsilon
.

Step 2.
We now show that
\nu(\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty})=0
. For any
i>0
, let
K_{i}
be the closure of the set of surfaces whose saddle connection lengths are bounded below by
\frac{1}{i}
, and which have at least one horizontal saddle connection, with the lengths of all horizontal saddle connections bounded above by
i
. By construction the
(K_{i})_{i>0}
exhaust
\mathcal{M}\smallsetminus\mathcal{M}_{\infty}
and thus it is enough to show that for any
i>0
and any
\varepsilon>0
, there is an open set
\mathcal{U}
that contains
K_{i}
and such that

for any

large enough. Once again, let

be as in item (II) in Theorem

for

as above. Since the length of saddle connections is bounded below on compact sets and

is compact, there is a

such that

, so that

K_{i}\subset\mathcal{U}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}g_{\tau}(K^{c}).

Let

be large enough so that

. We have

and thus there is a surface

without horizontal saddle connections shorter than

with respect to

. The surfaces

do not have horizontal saddle connections shorter that

and thus there is

Now let

. For any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{U}}(u_{s}q_{n})\,ds=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{K^{c}}(g_{-\tau}u_{s}q_{n})\ ds

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Te^{-2\tau}}\left|\left\{s\in\left[0,Te^{-2\tau}\right]:u_{s}q_{n}^{\prime}\notin K\right\}\right|<\varepsilon.

By Birkhoff genericity the quantity on the left-hand side converges to

as

\mu_{n}(\mathcal{U})\leq\varepsilon
.

Step 3.
We now show that for any invariant subvariety
\mathcal{N}\varsubsetneq\mathcal{M}
and

\mathcal{N}_{\infty}
and

, there is

,

that

.

General case
. Suppose now

. Since

.

Theorem 7.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{M}
satisfies the weak classification of horocycle invariant measures. Let

,
\mathcal{N}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\overline{Gq}\subset\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle\mu_{q}{\,\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{def}}}{{=}}\,}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}{r_{\theta}}_{\ast}\delta_{q}\ d\theta,

and let
\mu_{t,q}

\mu_{t,q}\underset{t\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}m_{\mathcal{N}}.

Proof.

Up to replacing

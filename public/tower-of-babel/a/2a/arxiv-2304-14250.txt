Discrete Rubio de Francia extrapolation theorem via factorization of weights and iterated algorithms
S. H. Saker 1 , A. I. Saied 2 and R. P. Agarwal 3

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove a discrete Rubio de Francia extrapolation theorem via factorization of discrete Muckenhoupt weights and discrete iterated Rubio de Francia algorithm and its duality. Precisely, we will prove that if for some p 0 > 1 p_{0}>1 and w ∈ 𝒜 p 0 w\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}} the ℓ p 0 ​ ( w ) \ell^{p_{0}}(w) inequality ‖ 𝒯 ​ f ‖ ℓ p 0 ​ ( w ) ≤ C ​ ‖ f ‖ ℓ p 0 ​ ( w ) \left\|\mathcal{T}f\right\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(w)}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{p_{0}}(w)} holds for pairs of the form ( OPEN 𝒯 ​ f , f ) \mathcal{T}f,f) , then for all p p and w ∈ 𝒜 p w\in\mathcal{A}_{p} the ℓ p ​ ( w ) \ell^{p}(w) inequality ‖ 𝒯 ​ f ‖ ℓ p ​ ( w ) ≤ C ​ ‖ f ‖ ℓ p ​ ( w ) \left\|\mathcal{T}f\right\|_{\ell^{p}(w)}\leq C\left\|f\right\|_{\ell^{p}(w)} also holds for such pairs, where 𝒯 \mathcal{T} is a given operator. The new approach, among other things, allows us to obtain explicit estimates of the constants and do not assume that the operator 𝒯 \mathcal{T} is linear or even sublinear. Key words and phrases: Extrapolation theorem, Muckenhoupt classes, Bounded operator and Rubio de Francia algorithm.

Introduction

The study of boundedness of operators is one of the main subjects in modern
harmonic analysis. The ideas related to the boundedness of operators and the
weighted inequalities appeared with the birth of singular integrals but a
better understanding of the subject was only in the 1970s. In 1972
Muckenhoupt
[
18
]
introduced the
A_{p}-
weights (now often referred to
as Muckenhoupt weights): for a given exponent
p>1
a weight
w
is said to
belongs to
A_{p}
if there exists a real constant
\mathcal{C}>1
such
that,

(1.1)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}(x)dx\right)^{p-1}\leq\mathcal{C},

holds for every interval
I\subseteq I_{0}
. The infimum of all such
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
[w]_{A_{p}}
and is called the
A_{p}\emph{-}
norm. The full characterization of the weights
w,
for which the
Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator

(1.2)

\mathcal{M}f(x):=\sup_{x\in I}\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}f(y)dy,

is bounded on
L_{w}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
by means of the so-called
A_{p}
-condition
was achieved by Muckenhoupt and the weighted norm
inequality

(1.3)

\int_{0}^{\infty}w(t)\left(\mathcal{M}f(t)\right)^{p}dt\leq C\int_{0}^{\infty}w(t)f^{p}(t)dt.

has been proved for a nonnegative function
f\in L_{w}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{+})

and the constant
C
depends of the norm
[w]_{A_{p}}
of the weight
w.

The weight
w
is in the Muckenhoupt class
A_{1}
if there exists a
constant
\mathcal{C}>1
such that

(1.4)

\mathcal{M}w(x)\leq\mathcal{C}w(x),\text{ \ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}^{+},

where the operator
f\rightarrow\mathcal{M}f
is the Hardy-Littlewood
maximal operator. The infimum of all such
\mathcal{C}
is denoted by
[w]_{A_{1}}
and is called the
A_{1}\emph{-}
norm.

Muckenhoupt’s result became a landmark in the theory of weighted norm
inequalities because most of the previously known results for classical
operators had been obtained for special classes of weights (like power
weights) and has been extended to cover several operators like Hardy’s
operator, Hilbert’s operator, Calderón-Zygmund’s singular operator,
fractional integral operator. For more details about the properties of
Muckenhoupt weights and their applications, we refer the reader to the
papers
[
14
,
19
,
1
,
2
]
and the references cited therein. By Hölder’s inequality it is easy to prove that if
w
satisfies the
A_{p}

condition (
1.1
) then
w
satisfies the Muckenhoupt condition

(1.5)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w(x)dx\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w^{-\frac{1}{p_{1}-1}}(x)dx\right)^{p_{1}-1}\leq\mathcal{C},\text{ for }p_{1}>p.

The main problem that has been solved in
[
18
]
by Muckenhoupt is the
(backward propagation) self-improving property of the Muckenhoupt class of
weights which sates that: If
w\in A_{p}(\mathcal{C)}
then there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(p,\mathcal{C})>0
such that

(1.6)

\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w(t)dt\right)\left(\frac{1}{\left|I\right|}\int_{I}w^{-\frac{1}{q-1}}(t)dt\right)^{q-1}\leq\mathcal{C}_{1},

for all
q=p-\epsilon
, where the constant
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}_{1}(p,\mathcal{C}).
In other words, Muckenhoupt result for self-improving
property shows that if
w\in A_{q}(\mathcal{C})
then there exists
\epsilon>0
such that
w\in A_{q-\epsilon}(\mathcal{C}_{1}),
and then

(1.7)

A_{q}(\mathcal{C})\subset A_{q-\epsilon}(\mathcal{C}_{1}).

In 1982 Rubio de Francia
[
20
]
discovered a very different perspective
theorem on the theory of weighted norm inequalities. This theorem called the extrapolation theorem which studies the boundedness of operators in
different spaces via the Muckenhoupt weights. In particular, it has been
proved that for a given sublinear operator
\mathcal{T},
for some
p_{0}

such that
1\leq p_{0}<\infty,
and every
w\in A_{p_{0}},
there exists a
constant
\mathcal{C}>0
depends on
[w]_{A_{p_{0}}}
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{T}f(x)\right|^{p_{0}}w(x)dx\leq\mathcal{C(}[w]_{A_{p_{0}}})\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(x)\right|^{p_{0}}w(x)dx,

then for every
1<p<\infty
and every
w\in A_{p},
there exists a constant

\mathcal{C}_{1}
depends on
[w]_{A_{p}}
such that

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{T}f(x)\right|^{p}w(x)dx\leq\mathcal{C}_{1}([w]_{A_{p}})\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|f(x)\right|^{p}w(x)dx.

The original proof of Rubio de Francia extrapolation theorem was quite
complex and depends on a connection between vector-valued estimates and a
weighted norm inequalities. A more direct proof that depend only on
weighted norm inequalities was given by Garciá-Cuerva
[
13
]
.
However this approach requires two complicated lemmas on the structure of
A_{p}-
weights.

In
[
10

Francia algorithm. In
[
12

Duoandikoetxea proved the extrapolation

The proof has two steps: first prove the
desired inequality holds for

and

self-improving property of
A_{p}
weights that: if

In

weights, instead of the

weights, for pairs of positive decreasing functions defined on

. A weight

\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{w(x)}{x^{p}}dx\leq\frac{C}{t^{p}}\int_{0}^{t}w(x)dx\text{, \ for all }t>0.

The smallest constant

The proof also requires the very deep self-improving property of
B_{p}
weights that: if
w\in B_{p}
then there exists
\epsilon>0
such
that
w\in B_{p-\epsilon}.

The theory of Rubio de Francia extrapolation has found a host of

,
\ w\in A_{2}.

A discrete nonnegative weight
w
defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{1,2,\ldots\}

belongs to the discrete Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{1}(C)
for

and

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}{w(k)}\right){\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p-1}}\leq C,

\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}:=\sup_{n\geq 1}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(n)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{1}{1-p}}(n)\right)^{p-1}.

The

In this paper, we give a new proof of the discrete extrapolation theorem
via Muckenhoupt weights
\mathcal{A}_{p}
with explicit constants
.
The

]

intermediate steps and uses the iteration algorithm

\mathcal{N}\left(h(n)\right):=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}^{s}},

where for

\mathcal{M}^{s}h=\mathcal{M\circ}...

\mathcal{\circ M}h
denotes
s
iterations of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and
\mathcal{M}^{0}h=h,
and the dual iteration algorithm

(1.22)

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n):=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}},

with

(1.23)

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h(n)=\frac{\mathcal{M}\left(wh\right)(n)}{w(n)},\text{
\ }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\text{,}

where
\mathcal{M}h
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and
\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\mathcal{\circ}...

2.
Preliminaries and Basic Lemmas

In
[
26
]
the authors proved that the Hardy-Littlewood maximal
operator
\mathcal{M}
is bounded in
\ell_{p}(w)
if
w
is a Muckenhoupt
weight. In
[
21
]
the authors used this result and proved the
following results which will play crucial roles in proving the main results
in Section 3.

Theorem 2.1
.

Assume that
0<\gamma\leq 1
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
for
1<p<\infty.
For a nonnegative sequence
g,
we define

Gg:=\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}\left(g^{1/\gamma}w\right)}{w}\right)^{\gamma}.

Then the operator
G
is bounded in
\ell_{p^{\prime}/\gamma}(w),

p^{\prime}=p/\left(p-1\right)
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\left\|Gg\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}/\gamma}(w)}\leq C\left\|g\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}/\gamma}(w)}.

Lemma 2.1
.

(Discrete Rubio de Francia algorithm) Fix
1<p<\infty
and assume
that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}.
For any nonnegative sequence
h\in\ell_{p}(w)
, we define

\mathcal{N}h(n):=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\mathcal{M}^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}^{s}},

where for
s>0,

\mathcal{M}^{s}h=\mathcal{M\circ}...

\mathcal{\circ M}h
denotes
s
iterations of the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and
\mathcal{M}^{0}h=h.
Then

(i)

h(n)\leq\mathcal{N}h(n),

(ii)

\left\|\mathcal{N}h\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}\leq 2\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)},

(iii)

\mathcal{N}h\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}.

Now, we define the dual Discrete Rubio de Francia algorithm and prove the
properties of it.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p},
for
1<p<\infty.
For a
nonnegative sequence
h,
we define a general Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator by

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h:=\frac{\mathcal{M}\left(wh\right)}{w}.

Then the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is bounded in
\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w),

p^{\prime}=p/\left(p-1\right)
and there exists a constant
C>0

such that

(2.1)

\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}.

Proof.

Applying Theorem
2.1
with
g=h
and
\gamma=1,
we see that the
operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
which is defined by

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h:=\frac{\mathcal{M}\left(wh\right)}{w},

is bounded in
\ell_{{}^{p^{\prime}}}(w)
and satisfies that

\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\leq C\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)},

which is the desired inequality (
2.1
). The proof is complete.

Lemma 2.3
.

(Discrete Dual Rubio de Francia algorithm) Fix
1<p<\infty
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}.
For any nonnegative sequence
h\in\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w),

p^{\prime}=p/(p-1),
we define

(2.2)

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n):=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}},

with

(2.3)

\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h(n)=\frac{\mathcal{M}\left(wh\right)(n)}{w(n)},\text{
\ }n\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\text{,}

where
\mathcal{M}h
is the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator and
\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\mathcal{\circ}...

\mathcal{\circ M}^{\prime}h
denotes
s
iterations of the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime},
and
\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{0}h=h.
Then

\,(a).

h(n)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n),

(b).

\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\leq 2\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)},

(c).

\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\left[\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}.

Proof.

From (
2.2
), we have (where
\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{0}h=h
) that

\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n)=h(n)+\sum_{s=1}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}},

and then

h(n)\leq\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n),

which proves the property
(a).
Applying Minkowski’s inequality, on the
term
\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)},
we see that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w(n)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n)\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w(n)\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}w(n)\left(\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)\right)^{p^{\prime}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\left\|\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}.

From Lemma
2.2
, since the operator
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
is bounded,
the inequality (
2.4
) becomes

\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\leq\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}}\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}=2\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)},

which proves the property
(b).
Since the operator
h\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\prime}h
is sublinear, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\left(\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\left(\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\right)=\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s+1}h(n)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{\left(\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right)^{s}h(n)}{2^{s}\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{s}}\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(n),

and then by using (
2.3
), we have that

\mathcal{M}\left(w\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)(n)\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\left(w\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)(n).

This proves that
w\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
with the norm

\left[\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\leq 2\left\|\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)},

which proves the property
(c).
The proof is complete.

Lemma 2.4
.

Assume that
w,

h
are positive weights and
1<p<p_{0}<\infty.
If
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p},
then
w\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}
and

\left[w\mathcal{N}h^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}}\leq\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}},

where the operator
\mathcal{N}
is defined as in Lemma
2.1
.

Proof.

From the property
(iii)
of Lemma
2.1
, we see that
\mathcal{N}h\in\mathcal{A}_{1},
and then

\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\leq\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\mathcal{N}h(k),

and thus we have for
p_{0}>p
that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\mathcal{N}h^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}.

Applying Hölder’s inequality on the term

\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left[w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right]^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1},

with
\gamma=\left(p_{0}-1\right)/\left(p-1\right)>1
and
\nu=\left(p_{0}-1\right)/\left(p_{0}-p\right)
(
1/\gamma+1/\nu=1
), we get that

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left[w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right]^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p_{0}-1}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-p}{p_{0}-1}}(k)\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p_{0}-1}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p-1}.

From (
2.5
) and (
2.6
), since
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p},

1<p<\infty,

we have that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left[w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right]^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\mathcal{N}h(k)\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\right)\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p-1}.

Taking supremum, we have that
w\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}
with the constant

\displaystyle\left[w\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left[w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}h\right)^{-\left(p_{0}-p\right)}(k)\right]^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\left[\mathcal{N}h\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}},

which is the desired inequality. The proof is complete.

Lemma 2.5
.

Assume that
w,

h
are positive sequences on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}\
and
1<p_{0}<p<\infty.
If
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p},
then
w\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}

and

\left[w\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}}\leq\left[\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}},

where the operator
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
is defined as in Lemma
2.3
.

Proof.

Since
p>p_{0}>1,

p_{0}-1>0,

p-p_{0}>0,
we have that

(2.7)

\frac{p-1}{p-p_{0}}=\frac{\left(p-p_{0}\right)+\left(p_{0}-1\right)}{p-p_{0}}=1+\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-p_{0}}>1.

Then by applying the weighted Hölder inequality

(2.8)

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)f(k)g(k)\leq\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)f^{\gamma}(k)\right]^{\frac{1}{\gamma}}\left[\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)g^{\nu}(k)\right]^{\frac{1}{\nu}},

on the term

\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k),

with indices
\gamma=\left(p-1\right)/\left(p-p_{0}\right)>1
and
\nu=\left(p-1\right)/\left(p_{0}-1\right)
(where
1/\gamma+1/\nu=1
), we
see that

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k).1.\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}},

and then

(2.9)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\right)^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w(k)\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}.

Also, we note that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}w^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}(k)\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(k)\right)^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}w^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}w^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}(k)w^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}(k)\right)^{p_{0}-1}

(2.10)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{n}\right)^{p_{0}-1}\left(\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h(k)\right)^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}w^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}(k)w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p_{0}-1}.

From the property
(c)
in Lemma
2.3
, we observe that
\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\in\mathcal{A}_{1},
and then

(2.11)

\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)(k)w(k)\leq\left[\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)(k)w(k).

Substituting (
2.11
) into (
2.10
), we have for
p>p_{0}>1,

\left(p-p_{0}\right)/\left[\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)\right]>0,

that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{-1}{p_{0}-1}}\right)^{p_{0}-1}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)(k)w(k)\right)^{\frac{-\left(p-p_{0}\right)}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}\left[\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{1}}^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p_{0}-1\right)\left(p-1\right)}}w^{\frac{-1}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{p_{0}-1}

(2.12)

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)f^{p_{0}}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

From (
3.23
) and (
3.29
), we have that

(3.30)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{0}}}.

Applying the weighted Hölder inequality on the term

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k),

with
\gamma=p/p_{0}>1
and
\nu=p/\left(p-p_{0}\right),
we see that

(3.31)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{p_{0}}{p}}.

Substituting (
3.31
) into (
3.30
), we obtain

(3.32)

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{pp_{0}}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

From the property
(b)
in Lemma
2.3
, we have for
p>p_{0}>1
that

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{pp_{0}}}=\left\|\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}\leq\left(2\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}.

Since
\left\|h\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w)}=1
, we have that

(3.33)

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}h\right)^{\frac{p}{p-1}}(k)\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{pp_{0}}}\leq 2^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}.

Substituting (
3.33
) into (
3.32
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|f\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

i.e.

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)f^{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell^{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

which satisfies (
3.18
). The proof is complete.

By combining Theorem
3.1
and Theorem
3.2
, we get the following
theorem.

Theorem 3.3
.

Assume that for some
p_{0},

1\leq p_{0}<\infty,
there exists
a positive increasing function
\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}
on
[1,\infty)

such that for every
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}},

(3.34)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)f^{p_{0}}(k)\leq\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left([w_{0}]_{\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k).

Then for all
p,

1\leq p<\infty
and for all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p},

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)f^{p}(k)\leq\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k).

If
p<p_{0},
then

\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)=2^{\frac{p_{0}-p}{p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right),

and if
p>p_{0},
then

\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)=2^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}^{p^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right),

with
p^{\prime}=p/\left(p-1\right).

Now, we are ready to state and prove the main theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}
be a given operator defined on
\mathbb{Z}_{+}

and there exists a positive an increasing function
\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}
on
[1,\infty)
such that for every
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{T}f(k)\right|^{p_{0}}w_{0}(k)\leq\varphi_{p_{0}}\left(\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|f(k)\right|^{p_{0}}w_{0}(k),

for some
p_{0},

1\leq p_{0}<\infty.
Then

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|\mathcal{T}f(k)\right|^{p_{0}}w(k)\leq\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\left|f(k)\right|^{p_{0}}w(k),

for every
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
such that
1\leq p<\infty
.

If
p<p_{0},
then

\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)=2^{\frac{p_{0}-p}{p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p}(w)}\right)^{p_{0}-p}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right).

If
p>p_{0},
then

\mathcal{\varphi}_{p}\left(p_{0},p,[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}\right)=2^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{\left(p-1\right)p_{0}}}\mathcal{\varphi}_{p_{0}}\left(\left(2\left\|\mathcal{M}\right\|_{\ell_{p^{\prime}}(w^{1-p^{\prime}})}\right)^{\frac{p-p_{0}}{p-1}}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}^{\frac{p_{0}-1}{p-1}}\right),

where
p^{\prime}=p/\left(p-1\right).

Proof.

The proof is obtained from Theorem
3.3
by considering the pair
(\mathcal{T}f,f)
instead of
(f,g).
The proof is complete.

In the following, we prove two corollaries of Theorem
3.3
. These
results further illustrate the value of the extrapolation of families of
pairs of functions. To prove these results we need the following inclusion
properties of Muckenhoupt classes that has been proved in
[
28
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
w
be a positive weight,
p,

q
be nonnegative real
numbers. Then the following inclusion relations hold:

(1)
.
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subset\mathcal{A}_{p}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
, for all
1<p<\infty,

(2)
.
\mathcal{A}_{p}\subset\mathcal{A}_{q}
for all
1<p\leq q,

(3).

\mathcal{A}_{\infty}=\bigcup\limits_{1<p}\mathcal{A}_{p}
with
\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{\infty}}=\lim_{p\rightarrow\infty}\left[w\right]_{\mathcal{A}_{p}}
and
\mathcal{A}_{1}\subset\bigcap{}_{p>1}\mathcal{A}_{p}.

Our first theorem yields
\ell_{p}
inequalities with weights in
\mathcal{A}_{p/r}
,
p\geq r>0.
Hereafter, by
\Omega
we mean a family of pairs
(f\,,g)
of nonnegative sequences.

Corollary 3.1
.

Suppose that for some
r>0
, and some
p_{0}
,
r\leq p_{0}<\infty,
and for every
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}/r},

(3.35)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)f^{p_{0}}(k)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)\text{.}

Then for all
p,

r\leq p<\infty
and all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p/r},

(3.36)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)f^{p}(k)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k).

Proof.

Define a new family
\Omega_{r}
consisting of the pairs
(f^{r},g^{r})
,
where
(f,g)\in\Omega.
Then for
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{0}/r}
, by (
3.35
)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)\left(f^{r}(k)\right)^{p_{0}/r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)f^{p_{0}}(k)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)\left(g^{r}(k)\right)^{p_{0}/r}.

Therefore, we can apply Theorem
3.3
with this as our initial hypothesis
and conclude that for all
q>1
and all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{q},

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(f^{r}(k)\right)^{q}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(g^{r}(k)\right)^{q}\text{.}

By putting
q=p/r
for some
p>r
and this is equivalent to (
3.36
). The
proof is complete.

Corollary 3.2
.

Suppose that for some
p_{0}
,
0<p_{0}<\infty,
and for every
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty},

(3.37)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)f^{p_{0}}(k)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)\text{.}

Then for all
p,

0<p<\infty
and all
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty},

(3.38)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)f^{p}(k)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)g^{p}(k).

Proof.

Fix
r,

1<r<\infty
and define a new family
\Omega_{r}
consisting of
the pairs
(f^{p_{0}/r},g^{p_{0}/r})
, with
(f,g)\in\Omega.
Then by
inequality (
3.37
)
,
for for every
w_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{r}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
and
(f^{p_{0}/r},g^{p_{0}/r})\in\Omega_{r}

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)\left(f^{p_{0}/r}(k)\right)^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)\left(f^{p_{0}}(k)\right)\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)g^{p_{0}}(k)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w_{0}(k)\left(g^{p_{0}/r}(k)\right)^{r}.

This gives the initial estimate (
3.34
) for
\Omega_{r}
and with
r

in place of
p_{0}.
Therefore by Theorem
3.3
, we have that for every
s,

1<s<\infty
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{s}

(3.39)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(f^{p_{0}/r}(k)\right)^{s}\leq C\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}w(k)\left(g^{p_{0}/r}(k)\right)^{s}.

Fix
p>1
and
w\in\mathcal{A}_{\infty}.
By Theorem
3.5
, since
\mathcal{A}_{p}
are nested, we may assume without loss of generality that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{s}\subset\mathcal{A}_{\infty}
for
s>p/p_{0}.

Therefore, we can choose
r>1
such that
\left(p_{0}/r\right)s=p
and
then the inequality (
3.39
) yields the inequality (
3.38
). The
proof is complete.

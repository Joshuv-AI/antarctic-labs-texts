The matter Lagrangian of a non-perfect fluid
Sergio Mendoza, Sarahí Silva

Abstract
We show that the matter Lagrangian of a non-perfect fluid takes the negative or positive value of the total energy density of the fluid, which is composed of the the rest plus internal energy densities. The ambiguity of the sign depends on the definition of the matter action and the chosen signature.



I
Introduction

Since the mid 1950’s it has become important to compute the value of
the matter Lagrangian of an ideal fluid
(see e.g.
Mendoza and Silva 2021
, and references
therein)
. Over the years, different approaches have
shown that this value can be either (up to a sign) the pressure or its
full energy density, this last one composed of the addition between the
rest energy density plus the thermodynamical internal energy density of
the fluid.
Mendoza and Silva 2021
showed that the only possible value
that the matter Lagrangian of an ideal fluid can only be the complete
energy density of the fluid (up to a sign depending on definitions of
the matter action and the signature of the metric).

Using calculus of variations, in this article we show that the
matter Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}
of a non-perfect fluid depends on the total
energy density of the fluid
e
in the form
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}=\mp e
, where the sign
of the matter Lagrangian depends on the definition of the matter action
and on the signature of the metric. In other words, the matter Lagrangian
of a non-perfect fluid consists of the rest mass energy density plus the
purely thermodynamical internal energy density of the fluid.
In Section
II
we
present the matter action and the definition of the energy-momentum through
the variations of this action. In Section
III
we introduce some
basic thermodynamic relations and the energy momentum tensor of a non
perfect fluid in the Eckart frame. In Section
IV

we use the mass continuity equation and the energy equation in the
Eckart frame in order to find the value of the matter Lagrangian of a
non-perfect fluid. Finally in Section
V
we discuss our results
and show that the same result for the matter Lagrangian case is also valid
in the Landau frame of the fluid.

II
Action

The matter action
S_{\text{matt}}
is defined as
(
Landau 2013
, see
e.g.)
:

S_{\text{matt}}=\pm\frac{1}{c}\int{\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}\,\sqrt{-g}\,\mathrm{d}^{4}x},

(1)

where
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}
represents the matter Lagrangian,
c

is the speed of light an
g
is the non-positive determinant of the
metric tensor
g_{\alpha\beta}
. The choice of a
+
or
-
sign in
equation (
1
) is a definition convention. In this work we use a
signature (
+,-,-,-
) for the metric. Greek space-time indices vary
from
0
to
4
and spatial Latin indices vary from
1

to
3
and we use the Einstein’s summation convention over repeated
indices.

The metric, Hilbert or Belifante-Rosenfeld energy-momentum tensor
T_{\alpha\beta}
is defined through the variations
\delta S
of the matter
action (
1
). Assuming that the matter
Lagrangian is a function of the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
only
and not of its
derivatives
\partial g^{\alpha\beta}/\partial x^{\lambda}
, the metric energy-momentum tensor is defined as
(
Landau 2013
;
Harko and Lobo 2018
;
Franklin 2017
;
Năstase 2019
, see
e.g.)
:

T_{\alpha\beta}=\pm\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta\left(\sqrt{-g}\,\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}\right)}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}},

(2)

or, equivalently
(
Mendoza and Silva 2021
, see e.g.)
:

T_{\alpha\beta}=\pm 2\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}\mp g_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}},

(3)

and in contravariant representation:

T^{\alpha\beta}=\mp 2\frac{\delta\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}}{\delta g_{\alpha\beta}}\mp g^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}.

(4)

III
Non-Perfect Fluids

The first law of thermodynamics for relativistic fluids can be written
as
(
Landau and Lifshitz 2013
, see e.g.)
:

\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{e}{\rho}\right)=T\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{\sigma}{\rho}\right)-p\mathrm{d}\left(\frac{1}{\rho}\right),

(5)

where
\sigma=\rho s
is the entropy density of the fluid with

s
the entropy per unit mass and
\rho
the fluid or gas (baryonic) mass
density. The total energy density
e=\rho c^{2}+\xi
contains the
rest energy density
\rho c^{2}
and a pure internal energy density
term
\xi
. The pressure and temperature are represented by
p

and
T
, respectively.

In the Eckart frame
(
Eckart 1948
)
for relativistic
fluids, the energy-momentum tensor of a non-perfect fluid is written as

(
Rezzolla and Zanotti 2013
, see e.g.)
:

T^{\alpha\beta}=T^{\alpha\beta}_{\text{pf}}+T^{\alpha\beta}_{\text{npf}},

(6)

where
T^{\alpha\beta}_{\text{pf}}
and
T^{\alpha\beta}_{\text{npf}}
refer to the perfect and non-perfect fluid
contribution, respectively. Equation (
6
) can be written
explicitly as
(
Rezzolla and Zanotti 2013
, see e.g.)
:

T^{\alpha\beta}=eu^{\alpha}u^{\beta}+(p+\Pi)h^{\alpha\beta}+q^{\alpha}u^{\beta}+q^{\beta}u^{\alpha}+\pi^{\alpha\beta},

(7)

where
\Pi,q^{\alpha}
and
\pi^{\alpha\beta}
are, the
viscous bulk pressure, the heat flux and the anisotropic stress
tensor respectively. In equation (
7
) the projection tensor
is represented by
h^{\alpha\beta}=u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}-g^{\alpha\beta}
. The covariant divergence of the complete energy-momentum
tensor (
6
) is null, i.e.:

\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\beta}=0.

(8)

IV
Matter Lagrangian for a Non-Perfect Fluid

In the Eckart frame the mass density current
\rho u^{\alpha}

is parallel to the four velocity and in the continuity
equation takes the same form as for a perfect fluid, i.e.
(
Rezzolla and Zanotti 2013
, see
e.g.)
:

\nabla_{\alpha}\left(\rho u^{\alpha}\right)=0,

(9)

For this reason the variation of the mass density
\rho

satisfies
(
Mendoza and Silva 2021
, see e.g.)
:

\frac{\delta\rho}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho\left(g_{\alpha\beta}-u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\right),

(10)

for a non-perfect fluid in the Eckart frame.

In order to obtain the variation of the entropy per unit mass
s

in terms of the variation of the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
let us
multiply equation (
8
) by
u_{\beta}
so that the
following energy equation is obtained:

\displaystyle u_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}T^{\alpha\beta}=\nabla_{\alpha}((e+p)u^{\alpha})-u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}p+\Pi\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\nabla_{\alpha}q^{\alpha}+u^{\alpha}u_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}q^{\beta}+u_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\pi^{\alpha\beta}.

(11)

Since the relativistic enthalpy
\omega
is defined as:

\omega:=e+p=\rho(\mu+Ts),

(12)

where
\mu:=\omega/\rho-Ts
is the relativistic chemical
potential, it follows from the first law of
thermodynamics (
5
) that:

\mathrm{d}\mu=\frac{1}{\rho}\mathrm{d}p-s\mathrm{d}T.

(13)

Using this, we can rewrite the energy equation (
11
) as:

\begin{split}u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}s=&-\frac{\Pi}{T\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}u^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{T\rho}\nabla_{\alpha}q^{\alpha}-\frac{1}{T\rho}u_{\beta}u^{\alpha}\nabla_{\alpha}q^{\beta}\\
&-\frac{1}{T\rho}u_{\beta}\nabla_{\alpha}\pi^{\alpha\beta}\end{split}

(14)

Let us now assume that the specific entropy
s
, the four velocity

u^{\alpha}
and thermodynamic fluxes
q^{\alpha}
and
\pi^{\alpha\beta}

depend only on the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
and not on its
derivatives
\partial g^{\alpha\beta}/\partial x^{\lambda}
, so that:

\frac{\partial}{\partial x^{\mu}}=\frac{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}{\partial x^{\mu}}\frac{\partial}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}.

(15)

Using this operator (
15
) and the following relations:

u_{\mu}\frac{\partial u^{\mu}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{2}u_{\alpha}u_{\beta},

(16)

\frac{\partial\sqrt{-g}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}=-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-g}g_{\alpha\beta},

(17)

\frac{\partial g^{\mu\nu}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}\delta^{\nu}_{\beta}+\delta^{\mu}_{\beta}\delta^{\nu}_{\alpha}\right),

(18)

\frac{\partial g_{\mu\nu}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}=-\frac{1}{2}\left(g_{\mu\alpha}g_{\nu\beta}+g_{\mu\beta}g_{\nu\alpha}\right),

(19)

u_{\mu}\frac{\partial q^{\mu}}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{1}{4}\left(q_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+q_{\beta}u_{\alpha}\right),

(20)

in equation (
14
) it follows that:

\begin{split}\frac{\delta s}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial g^{\alpha\beta}}&=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}-u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{\rho T}\left(q_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+u_{\alpha}q_{\beta}+\pi_{\alpha\beta}\right),\end{split}

(21)

for a non-perfect fluid.

Let us now choose the specific entropy
s
and the mass density
\rho
as the dependent variables for any given thermodynamical quantity,
so that:

\frac{\delta}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}=\frac{\delta\rho}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}\evaluated{\frac{\partial}{\partial\rho}}_{s}+\frac{\delta s}{\delta g^{\alpha\beta}}\evaluated{\frac{\partial}{\partial s}}_{\rho}.

(22)

Substitution of the operator (
22
) into equation (
3
)
and using relations (
10
) and (
21
), it follows that:

\begin{split}&T_{\alpha\beta}=\pm\left\{\rho\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial\rho}}_{s}+\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\right\}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}-u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\right)\\
&\mp\frac{1}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\left(q_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+u_{\alpha}q_{\beta}+\pi_{\alpha\beta}\right)\mp g_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}},\end{split}

(23)

for a non-perfect fluid. Direct comparison of
equations (
7
) and (
23
) imply that:

\begin{split}&eu_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+(p+\Pi)h_{\alpha\beta}+q_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+q_{\beta}u_{\alpha}+\pi_{\alpha\beta}=\\
&\pm\left\{\rho\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial\rho}}_{s}+\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\right\}\left(g_{\alpha\beta}-u_{\alpha}u_{\beta}\right)\\
&\mp\frac{1}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\left(q_{\alpha}u_{\beta}+u_{\alpha}q_{\beta}+\pi_{\alpha\beta}\right)\mp g_{\alpha\beta}\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}.\end{split}

(24)

The previous equation (
24
) can be solved for
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}
in two different ways:

(i)

By multiplication of equation (
24
) by
u^{\alpha}u^{\beta}
to obtain:

\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}=\mp e.

(25)

(ii)

In order to obtain a system of equations for
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}
, let us
first multiply equation (
24
) by the metric tensor
g^{\alpha\beta}
to obtain:

e-3\left(p+\Pi\right)=\pm 3\rho\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial\rho}}_{s}\pm 3\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\mp 4\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}.

(26)

and multiply equation (
24
) by the projection tensor

h^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}:=u^{\alpha}u_{\lambda}-\delta^{\alpha}{}_{\lambda}
to obtain:

\begin{split}&-(p+\Pi)(u_{\lambda}u_{\beta}-g_{\lambda\beta})-(q_{\lambda}u_{\beta}+\pi_{\lambda\beta})=\\
&\mp\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}-\rho\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial\rho}}_{s}-\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\right)(u_{\lambda}u_{\beta}-g_{\lambda\beta})\\
&\pm\frac{1}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\left(q_{\lambda}u_{\beta}+\pi_{\lambda\beta}\right),\end{split}

(27)

Equating terms with
(u_{\lambda}u_{\beta}-g_{\lambda\beta)}

and the ones with
(q_{\lambda}u_{\beta}+\pi_{\lambda\beta})
in equation (
27
), the following two relations are
obtained:

\displaystyle p+\Pi=\pm\left(\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}-\rho\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial\rho}}_{s}-\frac{\Pi}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}\right),

(28)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho T}\evaluated{\frac{\partial\lmatt}{\partial s}}_{\rho}=\mp 1.

(29)

The solution to the system of equations (
26
), (
28
) and (
29
)
recovers the same value of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}=\mp e
given in relation (
25
).

With the value of
\mathcal{L}_{\text{matt}}=\mp e
the set of
equations (
28
)-(
29
) can be written as:

\evaluated{\frac{\partial e}{\partial\rho}}_{s}=\frac{e+p}{\rho},

(30)

and

\evaluated{\frac{\partial e}{\partial s}}_{\rho}=\rho T,

(31)

which are consistent with the first law of
thermodynamics (
5
).

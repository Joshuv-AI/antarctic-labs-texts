To be more specific, consider the generalized spectral projections
\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}
defined initially for

by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}F(x)=\mid\mathbf{c}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}(\mathcal{F}F(\lambda,.)(x),\quad\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast},

(1.1)

Conjecture

There exists a positive constant

\displaystyle C^{-1}\parallel F\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}\leq\sup_{R>0,y\in X}\,\int_{\mathfrak{a}_{+}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{R^{r}}\int_{B(y,R)}\mid F_{\lambda}(x)\mid^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\lambda\leq C\parallel F\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(X)},

(1.2)

and

\displaystyle\parallel F\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(X)}=\gamma_{r}\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{\mathfrak{a}_{+}^{\ast}}\frac{1}{R^{r}}\int_{B(y,R)}\mid F_{\lambda}(x)\mid^{2}\,{\rm d}x\,{\rm d}\lambda.

(1.3)

Conversely, if
F_{\lambda}
is any family of joint eigenfunctions for which the right hand side of (

Here

The strichartz conjecture has been recently settled by Kaizuka, see

Most of the proof consists in proving a uniform estimate for the Poisson transform.
More precisely, the following was proved by Kaizuka [

\displaystyle\sup_{R>1}\frac{1}{R^{r}}\int_{B(0,R)}\mid F(x)\mid^{2}\,{\rm d}x<\infty.

Moreover there exists a positive constant
C
independent of such

,

C^{-1}\mid\mathbf{c}(\lambda)\mid^{2}\parallel f\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(K/M)}\leq\sup_{R>1}\,\frac{1}{R^{r}}\int_{B(0,R)}\mid\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}f(x)\mid^{2}\,{\rm d}x\leq C\mid\mathbf{c}(\lambda)\mid^{2}\parallel f\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(K/M)}.

\displaystyle F(gk)=\tau_{\nu}(k)^{-1}F(g),\quad\forall g\in G,\quad\forall k\in K.

We denote by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}f(g)=\int_{K}{\rm e}^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa(g^{-1}k))f(k)\,{\rm d}k

Our first main result is an image characterization of the Poisson transform

of

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

(i)

There exists a positive constant

independent of

such that for

(1.5)

with

\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)=2^{\rho-i\lambda}\frac{\Gamma(\rho-1)\Gamma(i\lambda)}{\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\nu+\rho}{2})\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\rho-\nu-2}{2})}.

Furthermore we have the following Plancherel type formula for the Poisson transform

\lim_{R\rightarrow+\infty}\dfrac{1}{R}\int_{B(R)}\|\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\nu}f(g)\|_{\nu}^{2}\,{\rm d}g_{K}=2\mid\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{L^{2}(K,\sigma_{\nu})}^{2}.

(1.6)

ii)

is a topological isomorphism from

onto
\mathcal{E}^{2}_{\lambda}(G,\tau_{\nu})
.

This generalizes the result of Kaizuka [
[

trivial.

Consequence

For

\mathcal{E}^{\ast}_{\lambda}(G,\tau_{\nu})=\{F\in\mathcal{E}_{\lambda}(G,\tau_{\nu}):M(F)<\infty\},

Next, let us introduce our second main result on the

-range of the generalized spectral projections.

For

\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\,F(\lambda,k)=\int_{G}{\rm e}^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa(g^{-1}k)^{-1})F(g)\,{\rm d}g\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},

Then the following inversion formula holds (see section 4)

\begin{split}\quad F(g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}&\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{K}{\rm e}^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa(g^{-1}k))\mathcal{F}_{\nu}F(\lambda,k)\,\mid\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}{\rm d}\lambda\,{\rm d}k\\
&+\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in D_{\nu}}d_{\nu}(\lambda_{j})\int_{K}{\rm e}^{-(i\lambda_{j}+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa(g^{-1}k))\mathcal{F}_{\nu}F(\lambda_{j},k)\,{\rm d}k.\end{split}

(1.7)

In above

Our aim here is to study the operator
\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{\lambda}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{\lambda}F(g)=\mid\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}[\mathcal{F}_{\nu}\,F(\lambda,.)](g),

More precisely, following Strichartz idea, we are interested in the following question:

Characterize those

(

(i)

There exists a positive constant

\displaystyle C^{-1}\parallel F\parallel_{L^{2}(G,\tau_{\nu})}\leq\parallel\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{\lambda}F\parallel_{+}\leq C\parallel F\parallel_{L^{2}(G,\tau_{\nu})}

Furthermore we have

\displaystyle\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{R}\int_{B(R)}\parallel\mathcal{Q}^{\nu}_{\lambda}F(g)\parallel^{2}_{\nu}\,{\rm d}g_{K}\,{\rm d}\lambda=2\parallel F\parallel^{2}_{L^{2}(G,\tau_{\nu})}

(ii)

The linear map

is a topological isomorphism from

This extends Kaizuka result [
[

Preliminaries

2.1

The quaternionic hyperbolic space

Let
G=Sp(n,1)
be the group of all linear transformations of the right
\mathbb{H}
-vector space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
which preserve the quadratic form
\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mid u_{j}\mid^{2}-\mid u_{n+1}\mid^{2}
.
Let
K=Sp(n)\times Sp(1)
be the subgroup of
G
consisting of pairs
(a,d)
of unitaries. Then
K
is a maximal compact subgroup of
G
. The quaternionic hyperbolic space is the rank one symmetric space
G/K
of the noncompact type. It can be realized as the unit ball
\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{H}^{n})=\{x\in\mathbb{H}^{n};\mid x\mid<1\}
. The group
G
acts on
\mathbb{B}(\mathbb{H}^{n})
by the fractional linear mappings
x\mapsto g.x=(ax+b)(cx+d)^{-1}
, if
g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
, with
a\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times n},b\in\mathbb{H}^{n\times 1},c\in\mathbb{H}^{1\times n}
and
d\in\mathbb{H}
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{g}
the Lie algebra of
G
;
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{p}
the Cartan decomposition of
\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{p}
is a vector space of matrices of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{pmatrix}0&x\\
x^{\ast}&0\\
\end{pmatrix},x\in\mathbb{H}^{n}\right\}
,
and

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}=\left\{\begin{pmatrix}X&0\\
0&q\\
\end{pmatrix},X^{\ast}+X=0,q+\overline{q}=0\right\}
,
where
X^{\ast}
is the conjugate transpose of the matrix
X
and
q\in\mathbb{H}
.

Let
H=\begin{pmatrix}0_{n}&e_{1}\\
{}^{t}e_{1}&0\\
\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{p}
with
{}^{t}e_{1}=(1,0,\cdots,0)
.
Then
\textbf{a}=\mathbb{R}\,H
is a Cartan subspace in
\mathfrak{p}
, and the corresponding analytic subgroup
A=\{a_{t}=\exp t\,H;t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, where

\displaystyle a_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}cht&0&sht\\
0&0_{n-1}&0\\
sht&0&cht\\
\end{pmatrix}.

With
A
determined we then have that

M=\left\{g=\begin{pmatrix}q&0&0\\
0&m&0\\
0&0&q\end{pmatrix},m\in Sp(n-1),\mid q\mid=1\right\}\simeq Sp(n-1)\times Sp(1).

Let
\alpha\in\mathfrak{a}^{\ast}
be defined by
\alpha(H)=1
. Then a system
\Sigma
of restricted roots of the pair
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{a})
is
\Sigma=\{\pm\alpha,\pm 2\alpha\}
if
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma=\{\pm 2\alpha\}
if
n=1
, with Weyl group
W\simeq\{\pm Id\}
. A positive subsystem of roots corresponding to the positive Weyl chamber
\mathfrak{a}^{+}\simeq(0,\infty)
in
\mathfrak{a}
is
\Sigma^{+}=\{\alpha,2\alpha\}
if
n\geq 2
and
\Sigma^{+}=\{2\alpha\}
if
n=1
.

Let
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}+\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}
be the direct sum of the positive root subspaces, with
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{\alpha}=4(n-1)
and
\dim\mathfrak{g}_{2\alpha}=3
and
N
the corresponding analytic subgroup of
G
. Then
the half sum of the positive restricted roots with multiplicities counted
\rho
equals to
(2n+1)\alpha
, and shall be viewed as a real number
\rho=2n+1
by the identification
\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{c}^{\ast}\simeq\mathbb{C}
via
\displaystyle\lambda\alpha\leftrightarrow\lambda
.

Let
\displaystyle\overline{A^{+}}=\{a_{t}\in A;\quad t\geq 0\}
. Then we have the Cartan decomposition
G=K\overline{A^{+}}K
, that is any
g\in G
can be written
g=k_{1}(g)\,{\rm e}^{A^{+}(g)}\,k_{2}(g),\quad k_{1}(g),k_{2}(g)\in K
and
A^{+}(g)\in\overline{\mathfrak{a}^{+}}
.

If we write
g\in G
in
(n+1)\times(n+1)
block notation as

\displaystyle g=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}
. Then a straightforward computation gives

\displaystyle\cosh A^{+}(g)=\mid d\mid\quad\textit{and}\quad H(g)=\log\mid ce_{1}+d\mid.

(2.1)

We normalize the invariant measure
{\rm d}g_{K}
on
G/K
so that the following integral formula holds: for all
h\in L^{1}(G/K)
,

\displaystyle\int_{G/K}h(gK){\rm d}g_{K}=\int_{G}h(g.0){\rm d}g=\int_{K}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(k\,a_{t})\Delta(t)\,{\rm d}k\,{\rm d}t,

(2.2)

where
{\rm d}t
is the Lebesgue measure,

\Delta(t)=(2\sinh t)^{4n-1}(2\cosh t)^{3}
, and
{\rm d}k
is the Haar measure of
K
with
\displaystyle\int_{K}{\rm d}k=1
.

2.2

The vector Poisson transform

In this subsection we define the Poisson transform associated to the vector bundles
G\times_{K}V_{\nu}
over
Sp(n,1)/Sp(n)\times Sp(1)
and derive some results referring to
[
23
]
,
[

]
, and
[

]

Let
\sigma_{\nu}
denote the restriction of
\tau_{\nu}
to
M
. For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
we consider the representation
\sigma_{\nu,\lambda}
of
P=MAN

defined by
\sigma_{\nu,\lambda}(man)=a^{\rho-i\lambda}\sigma_{\nu}(m)
. Then

\displaystyle H^{\nu,\lambda}:=\{f:G\rightarrow V_{\nu}\mid f(gman)=\sigma_{\nu,\lambda}^{-1}(man)f(g)\,\forall man\in MAN,f_{\mid K}\in L^{2}\},

We shall denote by

. This yields, the so-called compact picture of

\displaystyle\pi_{\sigma_{\nu},\lambda}(g)f(k)={\rm e}^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}f(\kappa(g^{-1}k)).

By

we denote the space of its hyperfunctions vectors.

A Poisson transform is the continuous, linear,

\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}^{\nu}
from

to
C^{\infty}(G,\tau_{\nu})
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}\,f(g)=\int_{K}\tau_{\nu}(k)f(gk)\,{\rm d}k.

In the compact picture the Poisson transform is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}\,f(g)=\int_{K}{\rm e}^{-(i\lambda+\rho)H(g^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa(g^{-1}k))\,f(k)\,{\rm d}k.

Let

denote the algebra of left invariant differential operators on

. Let

.

Proposition 2.1
.

(i)
\mathbb{D}(G,\tau_{\nu})
is the algebra generated by the Casimir operator
\Omega
of
\mathfrak{g}
.

(ii) For
\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\nu\in\mathbb{N}
, the Poisson transform
\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}
maps

to
\mathcal{E}_{\nu,\lambda}(G)
.

Proof.

(i) Let

be the universal enveloping algebra of the complexification of

. Since the restriction of
\tau_{\nu}
to

is irreducible, then
\mathbb{D}(G,\tau_{\nu})\simeq U(\mathfrak{a})^{W}
. As

is one dimensional, then
\mathbb{D}(G,\tau_{\nu})\simeq\mathbb{C}[s^{2}]
, symmetric functions of one variable . Thus
\mathbb{D}(G,\tau_{\nu})
is generated by the Casimir element
\Omega
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, viewed as a differential operator acting on
C^{\infty}(G,\tau_{\nu})
.

(ii) Since
\sigma_{\nu}
is irreducible, the image of
\mathcal{P}^{\nu}_{\lambda}
consists of joint eigenfunctions with respect to the action of
\Omega
. Moreover
\Omega
acts by the infinitesimal character of the the principal series representations
\pi_{\sigma_{\nu},\lambda}
. It follows from Proposition 8.22 and Lemma 12.28 in

]
, that

\displaystyle\pi_{\sigma_{\nu},\lambda}(\Omega)=-(\lambda^{2}+\rho^{2}-c(\sigma_{\nu}))Id\quad\textit{on}\quad C^{-\omega}(G,\sigma_{\nu,\lambda}),

(2.3)

where

is the Casimir value of

given by

.

∎

Let

be the

-spherical function associated to

According to

Notice that our sign on "

[

In order to state the next theorem, we introduce the finite set in

\displaystyle D_{\nu}=\{\lambda_{j}=i(\nu-\rho+2-2j),j=0,1,\cdots,\nu-\rho+2-2j>0\}.

[

\displaystyle d_{\nu}(\lambda_{j})=\frac{2^{-2(\rho-\nu-1)}(\nu-\rho-2j+2)(\rho-2+j)!(\nu-j)!}{\Gamma^{2}(\rho-1)j!(\nu-\rho-j+2)!},\quad\lambda_{j}\in D_{\nu}

Theorem 3.1
.

(i) For

(3.2)

(ii) The Fourier transform

.

The first part of Theorem

can be easily deduced from the inversion and Plancherel formulas for the spherical transform.

Let

denote the space of smooth compactly supported

-radial functions. The spherical transform of

The inversion and the Plancherel formulas for the
\tau
-spherical transform have been given explicitly in
[
12
]
. For the convenience of the reader we give an elementary proof by using the Jacobi transform.

Theorem 3.2
.

For
F\in C_{c}^{\infty}(G,\tau_{\nu},\tau_{\nu})
we have the following inversion and Plancherel formulas

\begin{split}F(g)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\Phi_{\nu,\lambda}(g)\mathcal{H}_{\nu}F(\lambda)\mid\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}{\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in D_{\nu}}\Phi_{\nu,\lambda_{j}}(g)\mathcal{H}_{\nu}f(\lambda_{j})\,d_{\nu}(\lambda_{j}),\end{split}

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{G}\parallel F(g)\parallel_{HS}^{2}{\rm d}g=\frac{\nu+1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\mid\mathcal{H}_{\nu}F((\lambda)\mid^{2}\mid\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}{\rm d}\lambda+(\nu+1)\sum_{\lambda_{j}\in D_{\nu}}d_{\nu}(\lambda_{j})\mid\mathcal{H}_{\nu}F((\lambda_{j})\mid^{2},\end{split}

(3.4)

In above

(3.5)

We refer to (

Now the theorem follows from the inversion and the Plancherel formulas for the Jacobi transform (

For the proof of the surjectivity statement in Theorem

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
F\in C^{\infty}_{c}(G,\tau_{\nu})
and
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(G,\tau_{\nu},\tau_{\nu})

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(F\ast\Phi)(\lambda,k)=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}\Phi(\lambda)\mathcal{F}_{\nu}F(\lambda,k),\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},k\in K,

where the convolution is defined by

\displaystyle(\Phi\ast F)(g)=\int_{G}\Phi_{\nu,\lambda}(x^{-1}g)F(x)\,{\rm d}x.

Proof.

Let
\Phi\in C^{\infty}(G,\tau_{\nu},\tau_{\nu})
,
v\in V_{\nu}
, and set
F_{v}=\Phi(.\,)v
. Then we have the following relation between the Fourier transform and the spherical transform

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}F_{v}(\lambda,k)=\mathcal{H}_{\nu}\Phi(\lambda)\tau(k^{-1})v.

(3.6)

By definition

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(F\ast\Phi)(\lambda,k)&=\int_{G}\,\int_{G}e_{\lambda}^{\nu}(k^{-1}g)\Phi(x^{-1}g)F(x){\rm d}x{\rm d}g\\
&=\int_{G}{\rm d}x\int_{G}e_{\lambda}^{\nu}(k^{-1}xy)\Phi(y)F(x){\rm d}y\end{split}

Using the following cocycle relations for the Iwasawa function
H(x)

\displaystyle H(xy)=H(x\kappa(y))+H(y),

and

\displaystyle\kappa(xy)=\kappa(x\kappa(y)),

for all

, we get the following identity

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}^{\nu}(k^{-1}xy)={\rm e}^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}e_{\lambda}^{\nu}(\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k)y),

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(\Phi\ast F)(\lambda,k)=\int_{G}{\rm e}^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}\left(\int_{G}e_{\lambda,\nu}(\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k)y)\Phi(y)F(x)\,{\rm d}y\right){\rm d}x.

Next, put
\displaystyle h_{v}(y)=\Phi(y)v,v\in V_{\tau_{\nu}}
. Then (
3.6
) implies

\displaystyle\int_{G}e_{\lambda,\nu}(\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k)y)\Phi(y)F(x)\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(h_{F(x)})(\lambda,\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k))

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}(\Phi)(\lambda)\tau_{\nu}(\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k))F(x),

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}_{\nu}(\Phi\ast F)(\lambda,k)=\mathcal{H}(\Phi)(\lambda)\int_{G}{\rm e}^{(i\lambda-\rho)H(x^{-1}k)}\tau_{\nu}(\kappa^{-1}(x^{-1}k))F(x){\rm d}x,

and the proposition follows.
∎

.

Proof.

(i) We may follow the same method as in

4

Fourier restriction estimate

The main result of this section is the following uniform continuity estimate for the Fourier-Helgason restriction operator.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. There exists a positive constant
C_{\nu}
such that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
and
R>1
, we have

\displaystyle\bigg(\int_{K}\|\mathcal{F}_{\nu}F(\lambda,k)\|_{\nu}^{2}dk\bigg)^{1/2}\leq C_{\nu}|c_{\nu}(\lambda)|R^{1/2}\bigg(\int_{G/K}\|F(g)\|_{\nu}^{2}\,{\rm d}g_{K}\bigg)^{1/2},

(4.1)

for every
F\in L^{2}(G,\tau_{\nu})
with
\textit{supp}F\subset B(R)
.

To prove this result we shall need estimates of the Harish-Chandra
c
-function. To this end we introduce the function
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)
defined on
\mathbb{R}
by

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)=\begin{cases}\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\quad\textit{if}\quad\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}\\
\lambda\,\mathbf{c}_{\nu}(\lambda)\quad\textit{if}\quad\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{+}\end{cases}\,

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume
\nu>\rho-2
.

(i)

The function
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)
has no zero in
\mathbb{R}
.

(ii)

There exists a positive constant
C
such that for
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle C^{-1}(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{2\rho-4-\varepsilon(\nu)}{4}}\leq\mid\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-1}\leq C(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{2\rho-4-\varepsilon(\nu)}{4}},

(4.2)

with
\varepsilon(\nu)=\pm 1
according to
\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{+}
or
\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}

Proof.

(i)

If
\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, then
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{\rho+\nu-i\lambda}\Gamma(\rho-1)\Gamma(i\lambda+1)}{\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\rho+\nu}{2})\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\rho-\nu-2}{2})}
, and clearly
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)
has no zero on
\mathbb{R}
.

If
\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
then
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)
a priori can have zero and pole at
\lambda=0
. This is not the case, since

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)=(-1)^{\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}}\frac{2^{\rho+\nu}\Gamma(\rho-1)(\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2})!}{\Gamma(\frac{\rho+\nu}{2})}.

(ii)

To prove the estimate (
4.2
) we shall use the following property of the
\Gamma
-function

\displaystyle\lim_{\mid z\mid\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\Gamma(z+a)}{\Gamma(z)}z^{-a}=1,\,\,\mid\arg(z)\mid<\pi-\delta,

(4.3)

where
a
is any complex number, and
\log
is the principal value of the logarithm and
\delta>0
.

Assume first that
\frac{\nu-\rho+2}{2}\notin\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Using the duplicata formula for the function gamma

\displaystyle\Gamma(2z)=\frac{2^{2z-2}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\Gamma(z)\Gamma(z+\frac{1}{2}),

we rewrite
\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)
as

\displaystyle\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)=\frac{2^{\rho+\nu-1}}{\sqrt{\pi}}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+1}{2})\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+2}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\rho+\nu}{2})\Gamma(\frac{i\lambda+\rho-\nu-2}{2})}.

It follows from (
4.3
) that for every
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle\mid\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid\leq C(1+\lambda^{2})^{-\frac{2\rho-5}{4}}

and

\displaystyle\mid\mathbf{b}_{\nu}(\lambda)\mid^{-1}\leq C(1+\lambda^{2})^{\frac{2\rho-5}{4}}.

The proof for the case

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}{\rm e}^{(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}-i\lambda)t}\phi_{\lambda}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)=\mathbf{c}_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda).

(A4)

Let
D_{e}(\mathbb{R})
denote the space of even smooth function with compact support on
\mathbb{R}
. For
f\in D_{e}(\mathbb{R})
, the Fourier-Jacobi transform
\mathcal{J}^{\alpha,\beta}f
(
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}
) is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\alpha,\beta}f(\lambda)=\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\phi_{\lambda}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}(t)\,{\rm d}t,

(A5)

where
\Delta_{\alpha,\beta}(t)=(2\sinh t)^{2\alpha+1}(2\cosh t)^{2\beta+1}
.

In the sequel, we assume that
\alpha>-1,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.
Then the meromorphic function
\mathbf{c}_{\alpha,\beta}(-\lambda)^{-1}
has only simple poles for
\Im\lambda\geq 0
which occur in the set

\displaystyle D_{\alpha,\beta}=\{\lambda_{k}=i(\mid\beta\mid-\alpha-1-2k);k=0,1,\cdots,\mid\beta\mid-\alpha-1-2k>0\}.

(If
\mid\beta\mid\leq\alpha+1
, then
D_{\alpha,\beta}
is empty).

The following inversion and Plancherel formulas for the Jacobi transform hold for every
f\in D_{e}(\mathbb{R})
:

\displaystyle f(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha,\beta}f)(\lambda)\,\phi_{\lambda}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t)\mid\mathbf{c}_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)\mid^{-2}\,{\rm d}\lambda+\sum_{\lambda_{k}\in D_{\alpha,\beta}}d_{k}(\mathcal{J}^{\alpha,\beta}f)(\lambda_{k})\,\phi_{\lambda_{k}}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(t),

(A6)

(A6’)

where
d_{k}=-i\,\textit{Res}_{\lambda=\lambda_{k}}(\mathbf{c}_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda)\mathbf{c}_{\alpha,\beta}(-\lambda))^{-1}

\displaystyle d_{k}=(\beta-\alpha-2k-1)\frac{2^{-2(\alpha+\beta)}\Gamma(\alpha+k+1)\Gamma(\beta-k)}{\Gamma^{2}(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\beta-\alpha-k)k!}.

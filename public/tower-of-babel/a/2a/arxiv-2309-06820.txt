Liouville theorem for V V -harmonic maps under non-negative ( m , V ) (m,V) -Ricci curvature for non-positive m m
Kazuhiro Kuwae, Songzi Li, Xiang-Dong Li, Yohei Sakurai

Abstract
Let V V be a C 1 C^{1} -vector field on an n n -dimensional complete Riemannian manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) . We prove a Liouville theorem for V V -harmonic maps satisfying various growth conditions from complete Riemannian manifolds with non-negative ( m , V ) (m,V) -Ricci curvature for m ∈ [ − ∞ , 0 ] ∪ [ n , + ∞ ] m\in\,[\,-\infty,\,0\,]\,\cup\,[\,n,\,+\infty\,] into Cartan-Hadamard manifolds, which extends Cheng’s Liouville theorem proved in [ 11 ] for sublinear growth harmonic maps from complete Riemannian manifolds with non-negative Ricci curvature into Cartan-Hadamard manifolds. We also prove a Liouville theorem for V V -harmonic maps from complete Riemannian manifolds with non-negative ( m , V ) (m,V) -Ricci curvature for m ∈ [ − ∞ , 0 ] ∪ [ n , + ∞ ] m\in\,[\,-\infty,\,0\,]\,\cup\,[\,n,\,+\infty\,] into regular geodesic balls of Riemannian manifolds with positive upper sectional curvature bound, which extends the results of Hildebrandt-Jost-Wideman [ 17 ] and Choi [ 12 ] . Our probabilistic proof of Liouville theorem for several growth V V -harmonic maps into Hadamard manifolds enhances an incomplete argument in [ 59 ] . Our results extend the results due to Chen-Jost-Qiu [ 9 ] and Qiu [ 53 ] in the case of m = + ∞ m=+\infty on the Liouville theorem for bounded V V -harmonic maps from complete Riemannian manifolds with non-negative ( ∞ , V ) (\infty,V) -Ricci curvature into regular geodesic balls of Riemannian manifolds with positive sectional curvature upper bound. Finally, we establish a connection between the Liouville property of V V -harmonic maps and the recurrence property of Δ V \Delta_{V} -diffusion processes on manifolds. Our results are new even in the case V = ∇ f V=\nabla f for f ∈ C 2 ​ ( M ) f\in C^{2}(M) .

Construction of
\Delta_{V}
-diffusion processes and
Kendall’s expression of radial processes

In this section, we summarize the construction of

\Delta_{V}
-diffusion processes on
M
and note that the Kendall’s expression for radial processes
remains valid for
\Delta_{V}
-diffusion process.

Let
V
be a
C^{1}
-vector field and
\Delta_{V}:=\Delta-\langle V,\nabla\cdot\rangle
denote the
V
-Laplacian. The construction of

\Delta_{V}
-diffusion process can be done by way of the
horizontal diffusion process

(U_{t})_{t\geq 0}
generated by
\Delta_{O(M)}-{\bf H}_{V}
on
O(M)
by solving the Stratonovich stochastic differential equation (SDE)

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}U_{t}=\sqrt{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{e_{i}}(U_{t})\circ\mathrm{d}B_{t}^{i}-{\bf H}_{V}(U_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\quad U_{0}=U\in O(M),

where
B_{t}:=(B_{t}^{1},B_{t}^{2},\cdots,B_{t}^{n})
is the
n

(\Omega,(\mathscr{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
.
Here
(e_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
is the canonical orthonormal basis in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
(H_{e_{i}})_{i=1}^{n}
the corresponding family of horizontal vector fields in the frame bundle
\mathscr{F}(M)
, and

{\bf H}_{V}(U):=H_{U^{-1}V}(U)
,
U\in O(M)
denotes the horizontal lift of
V
, where
U^{-1}V
is the unique vector
e\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
V_{\pi(U)}=Ue
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\Delta_{O(M)}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}H_{e_{i}}^{2}

is the
horizontal Laplace operator
.
Since

{\bf H}_{V}
is
C^{1}
, it is well known that the equation has a unique solution up to the life time
\zeta:=\lim_{k\to\infty}\zeta_{k}
, where

\displaystyle\zeta_{k}:=\inf\{t>0\mid{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}(\pi(U),\pi(U_{t}))\geq k\},\quad k\in\mathbb{N}

and
\pi:O(M)\to M
is the projection from the orthonormal frame to its point on
M
.
Let
X_{t}:=\pi(U_{t})
. Then
X_{t}
solves the equation

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}X_{t}=\sqrt{2}U_{t}\circ\mathrm{d}B_{t}-V(X_{t})\mathrm{d}t,\quad X_{0}=x:=\pi(u_{0})

up to the life time
\zeta
. By Itô’s formula, for any

\displaystyle m_{t}^{f}:=f(X_{t})-f(X_{0})-\int_{0}^{t}\Delta_{V}f(X_{s})\mathrm{d}s=\sqrt{2}\int_{0}^{t}\langle U_{s}^{-1}\nabla f(X_{s}),\mathrm{d}B_{s}\rangle

In this case,

is no longer a martingale, but for any relatively compact open set

From now on, the probability measure

Bochner Identity

In this section, based on the
Bochner identity for smooth map
u:M\to N
in terms of usual Laplace-Bertrami operator
\Delta
, we establish the
Bochner identity for
u:M\to N
in terms of
\Delta_{V}
.
We denote the Levi-Civita connection on
M
by
\nabla^{M}
.
Let
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
be the canonical ONB of
TM
, i.e.,
e_{i}=\left(\partial/\partial x^{i}\right)_{x}
, and
u^{-1}TN
the induced
vector bundle from
TN
by
u
, i.e., the fibre bundle over
M
with fibre
T_{u(x)}N
for
x\in M
. Moreover
\nabla^{u^{-1}TN}
denotes the induced connection from the Levi-Civita
connection
\nabla^{N}
on
N
. Let
\nabla
be the connection over
TM^{*}\otimes u^{-1}TN
induced from
the dual connection

\nabla^{*}
over
TM^{*}
and the connection
\nabla^{u^{-1}TN}
over

u^{-1}TN
(see
[
48
, (3.23)]
for the definition of
\nabla
).
The
Hessian
of
u:M\to N
is the symmetric bilinear map

{\rm Hess}\,u:TM\times TM\to TN
defined by

{\rm Hess}\,u(X,Y):=(\nabla_{X}\mathrm{d}u)(Y)=\nabla_{X}^{u^{-1}TN}(\mathrm{d}u(Y))-\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{X}^{M}Y)
for
X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)

(see
[
48
, pp. 93–94]
for definitions
\nabla^{\prime}:=\nabla^{N}
and
{}^{\prime}\nabla:=\nabla^{u^{-1}TN}
).
The tension field of
u
is given by

\tau(u):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Hess}\,u(e_{i},e_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(\nabla_{e_{i}}\mathrm{d}u)(e_{i})
and the energy density of
u
is given by
e(u):=\frac{1}{2}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\rangle
.

Lemma 4.1

(Bochner Identity)
.

For any smooth map
u:M\to N
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\tau_{V}(u))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\Ric}_{V}^{\infty}(e_{i},e_{j})\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

(4.1)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle{}^{N}{\rm Riem}(\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}))\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle.

Here
{}^{N}{\rm Riem}
is the Riemannian curvature tensor for
(N,h)
.

Proof.

The assertion seems to be known for experts.
For completeness we give a proof.
The proof of (
4.1
) is based on
the following Bochner identity for smooth map
u

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\tau(u))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\Ric}_{g}(e_{i},e_{j})\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

(4.2)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle{}^{N}{\rm Riem}(\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}))\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle.

The Bochner identity (
4.2
) for smooth map
u:M\to N
is well-known for experts (see
[
1
, Proposition 1.5]
,
[
41
, Corollary 2.10]
). From (
4.2
), we can deduce (
4.1
) as follows:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\langle V,\nabla|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip-2.84544pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{\tiny\eqref{eq:BochnerWeitzen}}}}{{=}}|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\tau_{V}(u))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\Ric}_{V}^{\infty}(e_{i},e_{j})\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle{}^{N}{\rm Riem}(\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}))\mathrm{d}u(e_{j}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\mathrm{d}u(V))\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left(\left\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V,e_{j}\right\rangle+\left\langle\nabla_{e_{j}}^{M}V,e_{i}\right\rangle\right)\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt-\frac{1}{2}\langle V,\nabla|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}\rangle.

So it suffices to prove that the last three terms vanish. Observe

\displaystyle|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})|^{2}.

By definition of the connection
\nabla
in
TM^{*}\otimes u^{-1}TN
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}V|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle\nabla_{V}\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla\mathrm{d}u(V,e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{V}^{u^{-1}TN}\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})-\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle\qquad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Nishikawa}{}{}, (3.25)]})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{V}^{u^{-1}TN}\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle.

Then the sum of last three terms becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\mathrm{d}u(V))\right\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{V}^{u^{-1}TN}\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V,e_{j}\right\rangle\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\nabla_{e_{i}}^{u^{-1}TN}(\mathrm{d}u(V))-\nabla_{V}^{u^{-1}TN}\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V,e_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u([e_{i},V])\right\rangle\qquad(\text{by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Nishikawa}{}{}, Lemma~3.7]}})

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle-\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\left\langle\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V,e_{j}\right\rangle\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V-\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{V}^{M}e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{i})\right\rangle-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(\nabla_{e_{i}}^{M}V)\right\rangle

\displaystyle=0.

∎

We need the following lemma to prove Corollary
4.3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
H
be a real separable Hilbert space and suppose that
h_{ij}\in H
satisfies
h_{ij}=h_{ji}
for
i,j=1,2,\cdots,n
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|h_{ij}\|_{H}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{ii}\right\|_{H}^{2}.

If further the symmetric matrix
(h_{ij})\in H^{n}\otimes H^{n}
has a null eigenvalue

0_{H}\in H
with multiplicity
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
, then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\|h_{ij}\|_{H}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n-k}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}h_{ii}\right\|_{H}^{2}.

Proof.

The first assertion can be easily confirmed for the case
H=\mathbb{R}
. In fact, in this case any real symmetric matrix
A=(a_{ij})
satisfies
\|A\|^{2}={\rm Tr}(A^{2})\geq\frac{{\rm Tr}(A)^{2}}{n}
. For general case, letting
\{e_{\ell}\}_{\ell=1}^{\infty}
be the complete orthonormal base in
H
, the assertion follows from the Parseval’s identity
\|h\|_{H}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\langle h,e_{\ell}\rangle_{H}^{2}
and the assertion for
H=\mathbb{R}
. Next we prove the second assertion.
In view of the Fourier expansion
h=\sum_{\ell=1}^{\infty}\langle h,e_{\ell}\rangle_{H}e_{\ell}
in
H
, we can prove

\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(h_{ij})=\bigcap_{\ell=1}^{\infty}{\rm Ker}(\langle h_{ij},e_{\ell}\rangle_{H}),

where

\displaystyle{\rm Ker}(h_{ij}):=\{\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(h_{ij})\phi=0_{H^{n}}\},\quad{\rm Ker}(\langle h_{ij},e_{\ell}\rangle_{H}):=\{\phi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\mid(\langle h_{ij},e_{\ell}\rangle_{H})\phi=0_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\}.

Therefore, if
(h_{ij})
has a null eigenvalue
0_{H}
with multiplicity
k
, then
the real symmetric matrix
(\langle h_{ij},e_{\ell}\rangle_{H})
has the null eigenvalue
0

with multiplicity
k
for all
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. The converse also holds.
The rest of the proof is similar to the proof of the first assertion.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

Suppose that
{\Ric}_{V}^{m}\geq 0
with
m\in\,[-\infty,\,0\,]\,\cup\,[\,n,+\infty\,]
and
{\rm Sect}_{N}\leq 0
. Then
any
V
-harmonic map
u:M\to N
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}\geq\frac{2m}{n(m-n)}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}\geq 0.

(4.3)

If further the symmetric matrix
({\rm Hess}\,u(e_{i},e_{j}))_{ij}
for
V
-harmonic map
u:M\to N
admits a null eigenvalue with multiplicity
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
independent of the choice of fibre, then

\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}\geq\frac{2(m-k)}{(n-k)(m-n)}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}\geq 0
under

{\Ric}_{V}^{m}\geq 0
with
m\in]-\infty,\,k\,]
and
{\rm Sect}_{N}\leq 0
.

Proof.

We may assume
m\in]-\infty,\,0\,]
by (
1.7
).
By Lemma
4.2
,

\displaystyle|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}|{\rm Hess}\,u(e_{i},e_{j})|^{2}\geq\frac{1}{n}\left|\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\rm Hess}\,u(e_{i},e_{i})\right|^{2}=\frac{|\tau(u)|^{2}}{n}.

Since
u:M\to N
is
V
-harmonic,
\Ric_{V}^{m}\geq 0
and
{\rm Sect}_{N}\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\Ric}_{V}^{\infty}(e_{i},e_{j})\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle=|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}{\Ric}_{V}^{m}(e_{i},e_{j})\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt+\frac{1}{m-n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle\langle V,e_{j}\rangle\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}+\frac{1}{m-n}\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle\langle V,e_{j}\rangle\langle\mathrm{d}u(e_{i}),\mathrm{d}u(e_{j})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\tau(u)|^{2}}{n}+\frac{1}{m-n}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}}{n}+\frac{1}{m-n}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}\qquad(\text{by }\tau_{V}(u)=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n(m-n)}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}\geq 0.

In the second inequality above, we use the fact that any
n\times n
symmetric non-negative definite real matrix
A=(a_{ij})
satisfies
\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}a_{ij}\langle h_{i},h_{j}\rangle_{H}\geq 0
for any real Hilbert space
(H,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)

and
h_{i}\in H
(
1\leq i\leq n
). If further
({\rm Hess}\,u(e_{i},e_{j}))_{ij}

for
V
-harmonic map
u:M\to N
admits a null eigenvalue with multiplicity
k\in\{1,2,\cdots,n-1\}
independent of the choice of fibre, then

|{\rm Hess}\,u|^{2}\geq\frac{|\tau(u)|^{2}}{n-k}
, consequently

\Delta_{V}|\mathrm{d}u|^{2}\geq\frac{2(m-k)}{(n-k)(m-n)}|\mathrm{d}u(V)|^{2}\geq 0
under
m\in]-\infty,\,k\,]
.
∎

{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(\zeta=+\infty)=1
for
x\in M
(see
[
16
, (3.4) Lemma]
).
First note that any smooth bounded
V
-subharmonic function is finely
V
-subharmonic. This is easily confirmed by way of Itô’s formula.
Let
f
be a bounded finely
V
-subharmonic (resp.
V
-superharmonic) function, then
g:=\|f\|_{\infty}-f
(resp.
g=\|f\|_{\infty}+f
) is a non-negative
finely
V
-superharmonic function.
Let
\{G_{n}\}
be the increasing sequence of relatively compact open sets
satisfying
\overline{G_{n}}\subset G_{n+1}\subset M
for
n\in N
and
M=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}G_{n}
.
Then
t\mapsto g(X_{t\land\tau_{G_{n}}})
is a bounded supermartingle, hence
{\mathbb{E}}_{x}[g(X_{t}):t<\tau_{G_{n}}]\leq g(x)
for any
t>0
and
x\in M
.
Letting
k\to\infty
, we have
{\mathbb{E}}_{x}[g(X_{t})]\leq g(x)
for any
t>0
and
x\in M
.
Since
g
is finely continuous,
g
is an excessive function with respect to
{\bf X}^{V}
. Therefore we obtain the first conclusion. Conversely suppose that
any bounded finely
V
-subharmonic function is constant.
Let
g
be a bounded excessive function with respect to
{\bf X}^{V}
.
Then

\mathbb{E}_{x}[g(X_{t\land\tau_{G_{n}}})]\leq g(x)
for all
t>0
and
x\in M
by

[
5
, Chapter II, (2.8) Proposition]
.
From this, we can deduce that

t\mapsto g(X_{t\land\tau_{G_{n}}})
is a bounded supermartingale, that is,

g
is finely
V
-superharmonic.
By assumption,
g
is constant. For general excessive function
g
,
for each
\ell>0
,
g\land\ell
is a bounded excessive function and it is a constant. If
g
takes different values
g(x_{1})
and
g(x_{2})
with
g(x_{1})<g(x_{2})
, then
g(x)\land\left(\frac{g(x_{1})+g(x_{2})}{2}\right)
has the constant value
c
.
Then one can obtain
c=g(x_{1})=\frac{g(x_{1})+g(x_{2})}{2}
so that
g(x_{1})=g(x_{2})
.
This is a contradiction. Therefore
g
is constant, hence
{\bf X}^{V}
is recurrent.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.12
.

By Lemma
8.2
,
\Phi(u,o)
is constant.
Thanks to the concrete expression of
\Phi
shown in
[
24
, Theorem 3]
,
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(u,o)
is constant. Then
one can conclude that
u(M)\subset\partial B_{r}(o)\subset B_{R}(o)
for some
r>0
.
Fix an
x_{0}\in M
.
Since
B_{R}(o)\cap{\rm Cut}(o)=\emptyset
,
u(x_{0})
and
o
can be joined by a unique minimal geodesic in
B_{R}(o)
. On this geodesic arc, we can choose a point
\hat{o}\in B_{R}(o)
which is different from
o
such that we can find another geodesic ball
B_{\hat{r}}(\hat{o})
satisfying
B_{r}(o)\subset B_{\hat{r}}(\hat{o})\subset B_{R}(o)
.
Applying Lemma
8.1
to
\Phi(u,\hat{o})
, it is constant
so that
{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(u,\hat{o})\equiv\tilde{r}
, that is,
u(M)\subset\partial B_{\tilde{r}}(\hat{o})
for some
\tilde{r}>0
. Then we see

\tilde{r}={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(u(x_{0}),\hat{o})=r-{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(o,\hat{o})
. Obviously,
u(x_{0})\in\partial B_{r}(o)\cap\partial B_{\tilde{r}}(\hat{o})
. Suppose there exists a different

u(x_{1})\neq u(x_{0})
such that
u(x_{1})\in\partial B_{r}(o)\cap\partial B_{\tilde{r}}(\hat{o})
. Since
u(x_{1})\neq u(x_{0})
,
u(x_{1})
does not lie on the minimal geodesic joining
o
and
u(x_{0})
. Then
r={\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(u(x_{1}),o)<{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(u(x_{1}),\hat{o})+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(\hat{o},o)=\tilde{r}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}{{\sf d}}}_{N}(\hat{o},o)
. This is a contradiction. Hence
\partial B_{r}(o)\cap\partial B_{\tilde{r}}(\hat{o})=\{u(x_{0})\}
,
consequently,
u(M)\subset\partial B_{r}(o)\cap\partial B_{\tilde{r}}(\hat{o})=\{u(x_{0})\}
implies
u(M)=\{u(x_{0})\}
.
∎

A computational approach to almost-inner derivations Thanks: The authors thank Boris Kunyavskii for proposing this question to us (at the Monash Prato Workshop ‘Lie Theory: frontiers, algorithms, and applications’), and suggesting to look at the example of [ 14 ] .
Heiko Dietrich, Willem A. de Graaf

Abstract
. We present a computational approach to determine the space of almost-inner derivations of a finite dimensional Lie algebra given by a structure constant table. We also present an example of a Lie algebra for which the quotient algebra of the almost-inner derivations modulo the inner derivations is non-abelian. This answers a question of Kunyavskii and Ostapenko.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a finite dimensional Lie algebra over a field
F
. A linear map
\delta\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a derivation if for all
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
it satisfies

\delta([a,b])=[\delta(a),b]+[a,\delta(b)].

The set of all derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
forms an
F
-vector space, denoted
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
, and a short calculation shows that
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie-subalgebra of
\mathfrak{\mathop{gl}}(\mathfrak{g})
. For every
a\in\mathfrak{g}
the adjoint homomorphism
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
,
b\mapsto[a,b]
, is a derivation; these are the inner derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
and they form a subalgebra
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
of
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
; note that
[\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a),\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(b)](x)=\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}([a,b])(x)
. In particular, the map
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
is a surjective homomorphism whose kernel is the centre
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
of
\mathfrak{g}
, and
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
is spanned by
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(b)
where
b
runs over an
F
-basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. If
\delta
is a derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
[\delta,\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)]=\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(\delta(a))
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
, which shows that
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal of
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
. A derivation
\delta
of
\mathfrak{g}
is almost-inner if there exists a map
A_{\delta}\colon\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that for every
a\in\mathfrak{g}
it satisfies

\delta(a)=[A_{\delta}(a),a],

that is,
\delta(a)\in[\mathfrak{g},a]
for all
a\in\mathfrak{g}
. The map
A_{\delta}
is neither unique nor linear in general; for example, one can modify each image of
A_{\delta}
by adding a different central element of
\mathfrak{g}
. The
F
-space of all almost-inner derivations on
\mathfrak{g}
is denoted
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
. An almost-inner derivation
\delta
is central almost-inner if there is some
a\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\delta-\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)
maps
\mathfrak{g}
into the centre of
\mathfrak{g}
. We follow the convention in
[
4
]
and denote the space of central almost inner derivation of
\mathfrak{g}
by
{\rm CAID}(\mathfrak{g})
. Since
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)(b)=[a,b]
for every
a,b\in\mathfrak{g}
, the inner derivation
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)
is an almost-inner derivation with constant map
A_{\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(a)}=a
. More generally, in
[
4
, Proposition 2.3]
the following inclusion of Lie subalgebras of
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is shown

{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})\leqslant{\rm CAID}(\mathfrak{g})\leqslant{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})\leqslant{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g}).

Recall that
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})={\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
for every semisimple Lie algebra over a field of characteristic 0, see for example
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
.

Clearly,
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal in each of these subalgebras. It is shown in
[
4
, Proposition 2.4]
that
{\rm CAID}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal in
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
, but it remains open whether or not
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal in
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
: this is conjectured to be true in
[
4
, Remark 2.5]
. For more details on known results, we refer to
[
4
,
5
]
; for example, it is known that
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})={\rm CAID}(\mathfrak{g})={\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
for every complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension at most
4
, see
[
4
, Proposition 2.8]
.

Almost-inner derivations have first been considered by Gordon and Wilson
[
8
]
in a differential-geometric context. They have recently been studied by Saeedi and collaborators (see, e.g.
[
15
,
1
]
) and Burde, Dekimpe, and Verbeke (see, e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
16
]
). Most recently, Kunyavskii and Ostapenko
[
12
]
used
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
to define an algebra-theoretic analog of the Tate-Shafarevich group, the Tate-Shafarevich algebra of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
,

\Sha(\mathfrak{g})={\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})/{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g}),

see
[
12
, Section 2]
. They point out that algebras with nonzero
\Sha(\mathfrak{g})
reveal important geometric phenomena. One of their main results is the proof that
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal of
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
for nilpotent
\mathfrak{g}
, partially answering the aforementioned conjecture affirmatively. This also implies that
\Sha(\mathfrak{g})
is an ideal of
{\rm Out}(\mathfrak{g})={\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})/{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
for nilpotent
\mathfrak{g}
, see
[
12
, Theorem 2.5]
.

The first author of
[
12
]
asked us for computational methods to determine the subalgebra
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
, and whether there is a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
for which
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})/{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
is non-abelian, see
[
12
, Question 4.1(i)]
. We develop such an approach in Section
2
, and then discuss some computational examples (including an affirmative answer to the question) in Sections
3
and
4
.

2.
Computational approach

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra over a field
F
, with basis
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\}
and corresponding structure constants
\sigma_{i,j}^{k}
, that is, for each
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
we have
[b_{i},b_{j}]=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\sigma_{i,j}^{k}b_{k}
. Let
\delta
be an endomorphism of
\mathfrak{g}
, represented by an
n\times n
matrix with entries
d_{j,k}
, such that
\delta(b_{j})=\sum_{k=1}^{n}d_{j,k}b_{k}
for each
i
. Since
\delta
is a derivation if and only if
\delta([b_{i},b_{j}])=[\delta(b_{i}),b_{j}]+[b_{i},\delta(b_{j})]
for all
i,j
, this translates to the following equations for each
i,j,\ell\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
:

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{n}(\sigma_{i,j}^{k}d_{k,\ell}-\sigma_{k,j}^{\ell}d_{i,k}-\sigma_{i,k}^{\ell}d_{j,k})=0.

Since
[b_{i},b_{j}]=-[b_{j},b_{i}]
, it suffices to consider
i>j
. This shows that a basis for the derivation algebra
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
can be computed by solving a system of
n^{2}(n+1)/2
linear equations with
n^{2}
unknowns, see also
[
9
, Section 1.9]
. A basis of
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
as a subalgebra can readily be computed by determining the matrices (with respect to
\mathcal{B})
of the adjoints
\mathrm{\mathop{ad}}(b_{i})
for every
i\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
.

In conclusion, linear algebra methods can be used to compute
{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
and
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
, and therefore also a complement subspace
U\leqslant{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})
such that

{\rm Der}(\mathfrak{g})={\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus U.

In the sequel we fix one such space
U
. Since
{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})\leqslant{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
, to determine
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
, it suffices to compute the space
U\cap{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 2.1
.

For
z_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
let
D_{z_{0}}
be the subspace of
U
that consists of all derivations
\delta
that act as an inner derivation on the
1
-dimensional subspace spanned by
z_{0}
; note that
\delta
is not inner since
U\cap{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})=\{0\}
. In other words,
\delta\in D_{z_{0}}
if and only if
\delta\in U
and
\delta(z_{0})=[z_{0},x]
for some
x\in\mathfrak{g}
.

If
z_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
and
\psi_{z_{0}}
denotes the linear map

\psi_{z_{0}}\colon U\oplus\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g},\quad(\delta,x)\mapsto\delta(z_{0})-[z_{0},x],

then
D_{z_{0}}
is the image of the kernel
\ker\psi_{z_{0}}
under the projection
U\oplus\mathfrak{g}\to U
. This shows that we can compute
D_{z_{0}}
by solving a linear equation system. In particular,

U\cap{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigcap\nolimits_{z_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}}D_{z_{0}}.

Clearly, a finite intersection suffices to construct
U\cap{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
in this way. However, it is not clear how to chose suitable elements
z_{0}
, and how to establish that the intersection is as small as possible.

We fix the previous notation and, throughout, let
V
be a subspace of
U
, for example, the intersection of multiple spaces
D_{z_{0}}
for arbitrarily chosen elements
z_{0}\in\mathfrak{g}
, so that

{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})\leqslant{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus V.

Let
\{\delta_{1},\ldots,\delta_{s}\}
be a basis of
V
. How to decide whether
\delta=\sum_{i=1}^{s}d_{i}\delta_{i}\in V
lies in
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
? The following proposition provides an answer, but we need some notation before we can state it.

For a vector
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in F^{n}
define the
n\times n
matrix
M(z)
as

M(z)=(m_{k,j}(z))_{k,j}\quad\text{where each}\quad m_{k,j}(z)=\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\sigma_{i,j}^{k};

recall that the
\sigma_{i,j}^{k}
are the structure constants with respect to the basis
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
. Also define
b_{z}\in\mathfrak{g}
by

b_{z}=z_{1}b_{1}+\cdots+z_{n}b_{n}.

For
\delta\in V
write
\delta(b_{z})=c_{1}(z)b_{1}+\cdots+c_{n}(z)b_{n}
with each
c_{i}(z)\in F
, and define the column vector
v_{\delta}(z)
as

v_{\delta}(z)=(c_{1}(z),\ldots,c_{n}(z))^{\intercal}.

Lastly, denote by

M_{\delta}(z)=[M(z)|v_{\delta}(z)]

the augmented matrix
M(z)
with additional column
v_{\delta}(z)
.

Proposition 2.2
.

The derivation
\delta\in V
lies in
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if for all
z\in F^{n}

\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M(z))=\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M_{\delta}(z)).

Proof.

The derivation
\delta
is almost-inner if and only if for every
z\in F^{n}
there exists some
x\in\mathfrak{g}
such that
\delta(b_{z})=[b_{z},x]
. Writing
x=x_{1}b_{1}+\ldots+x_{n}b_{n}
we have

[b_{z},x]=\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{j}[b_{i},b_{j}]=\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{n}(\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{j}\sigma_{i,j}^{k})b_{k}.

Using the definition of
v_{\delta}(z)
and its components
c_{k}(z)
, the derivation
\delta
lies in
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if for all
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}\in F
, there exist
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in F
such that for each
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}

c_{k}(z)=(\sum\nolimits_{i,j=1}^{n}z_{i}x_{j}\sigma_{i,j}^{k})=\sum\nolimits_{j=1}^{n}(\sum\nolimits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\sigma_{i,j}^{k})x_{j}.

The latter holds if and only if

(2.1)

\displaystyle M(z)\cdot(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\intercal}=v_{\delta}(z)

where
M(z)
and
v_{\delta}(z)
are as defined prior to the proposition. We have that (
2.1
) has a solution if and only if
v_{\delta}(z)
lies in the column space of
M(z)
. The claim follows.
∎

Note that
m_{k,j}(z)
and
c_{i}(z)
are (linear) polynomials in
z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}
. Now let
z=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})
be the vector of indeterminates of
F[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
and consider the corresponding matrices
M(z)
and
M_{\delta}(z)
.
The rank of a matrix can be defined as the largest integer
r
such that there exists a nonzero
r\times r
minor, that is, a nonzero determinant of an
r\times r
submatrix. For a fixed
r
, denote by
K_{r}(z)
and
K_{\delta,r}(z)
the set of all
r\times r
minors of
M(z)
and of
M_{\delta}(z)
, respectively; so the elements in
K_{r}(z)
and
K_{\delta,r}(z)
are polynomials in
F[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n}]
. Let
\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)
and
\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
be the ideals generated by
K_{r}(z)
and
K_{\delta,r}(z)
, respectively. Recall that the radical of an ideal
I
in a ring
R
is the ideal
\sqrt{I}=\{r\in R:r^{n}\in I\text{ for some $n\in\mathbb{N}$}\}
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
\delta\in V
be a derivation. If
\delta
is not an almost-inner derivation, then there exists some
r
and
w\in\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
with
w\notin\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
. Conversely, if the field is algebraically closed and there exist some
r
and
w\in\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
such that
w\notin\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
, then
\delta
is not an almost-inner derivation.

Proof.

If
\delta
is not an almost-inner derivation, then there exists
\tilde{z}\in F^{n}
such that
r=\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M_{\delta}(\tilde{z}))
is larger than
\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M(\tilde{z}))
, that is, there is some
r\times r
minor
w(\tilde{z})
of
M_{\delta}(\tilde{z})
that does not vanish, but all
r\times r
minors of
M(\tilde{z})
are
0
. Thus,
\tilde{z}
is a common root of all elements in
\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)
, but not of all elements in
\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
. In particular,
w(z)
lies in
\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
, but not in
\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
.

Conversely, suppose that there exists some
r
and
w\in\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
such that
w\notin\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
. Over an algebraically closed field, Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz
[
7
, Theorem 4.1.2]
says that
\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
is exactly the set of all polynomials that vanish on all the common roots of the elements in
\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)
. This means that there is a common root
\tilde{z}
for all the elements in
\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)
, but
\tilde{z}
is not a root of
w(z)
. This implies that
\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M_{\delta,r}(\tilde{z}))
is greater than
\mathrm{\mathop{rank}}(M_{r}(\tilde{z}))
, and therefore
\delta
is not an almost-inner derivation by Proposition
2.2
.
∎

We note that deciding membership in the radical can be achieved without computing the radical, see
[
7
, Proposition 4.2.8]
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\delta\in V
be a derivation.

a)

Over an algebraically closed field,
\delta\in{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
if and only if
\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)\subseteq\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
for every
r>1
.

b)

If
\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)\subseteq\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
for each
r>1
, then
\delta\in{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})
(even for non-algebraically closed fields).

Remark 2.5
.

If the latter condition in part a) of the corollary does not hold, then there exists some
r\times r
minor
w(z)\in\mathcal{I}_{\delta,r}(z)
that does not lie in
\sqrt{\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)}
; in particular, there exists a point
\tilde{z}
such that
w(\tilde{z})
is not zero, but
\tilde{z}
is a common root of all the elements in
\mathcal{I}_{r}(z)
. One can attempt to find
\tilde{z}
by working in the polynomial ring
F[z_{1},\ldots,z_{n},y]
and finding a common zero of the polynomials in the ideal generated by
K_{r}(z)
and
w(z)y-1
: such a common zero annihilates every generator in
K_{r}(z)
, but due to
w(\tilde{z})y=1
, it cannot be a zero of
w(z)
. Once such an element
\tilde{z}\in F^{n}
is found, one can reduce
V
to
V\cap D_{b_{\tilde{z}}}
. Note that the latter intersection is smaller than
V
since
\delta\in V
, but
\delta\notin D_{b_{\tilde{z}}}
. Eventually, one can iterate this method to reduce
V
to a smaller subspace such that one can verify that each generator is an almost-inner derivation. However, it is still a computational challenge to find these suitable points
\tilde{z}
. In particular, for fields that are not algebraically closed, it is not even known whether the problem of finding such points is decidable, c.f. Hilbert’s 10th Problem over
\mathbb{Q}
, see
[
13
, Section 2.6.4]
.

3.
Computational examples

Our methods can quickly deal with small-dimensional Lie algebras. For example,
we went through the classification of
8
-dimensional filiform Lie algebras given in
[
2
]
. For the cases where the Lie algebra depends on a parameter
we set it equal to 1. Our methods readily compute the quotient

{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})/{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
, and it has dimension
0
,
1
, or
2
in all cases. When the dimension of the Lie algebra
increases we can still quickly get a good idea of
{\rm AID}(\mathfrak{g})/{\rm Inn}(\mathfrak{g})
by
computing the intersection of many spaces
D_{z_{0}}
for arbitrarily chosen
z_{0}
. However, proving
that a given derivation is almost-inner by the minors-method described above quickly
becomes cumbersome due to the large number of minors that has to be considered.

We now illustrate our method with some computations in the algebra system Magma
[
3
]
.

.

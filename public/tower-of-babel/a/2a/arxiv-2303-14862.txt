New derivation of Time-Independent Perturbation Theory
A. N. Kvinikhidze, B. Blankleider

Abstract
We propose a new derivation of Time-Independent Perturbation Theory (PT) that has a fundamental advantage over the usual derivations presented in textbooks on Quantum Mechanics (QM): it is simpler and much shorter. As such, it can provide an easier and quicker way for students to learn PT, than afforded by current methods. In spite of that, our approach does not require the potentials to be energy independent or the inverse free Green function G 0 − 1 ​ ( E ) G_{0}^{-1}(E) to be a linear function of energy E E , as is the case in QM, and can be applied directly to various extensions of QM including Relativistic QM, the Bethe-Salpeter equation, and all kinds of quasipotential approaches in Quantum Field Theory.



I
Introduction

In Quantum Mechanics (QM), almost none of the physically interesting problems can be solved exactly. This makes Time-Independent Perturbation Theory (PT) a fundamental part of QM, as it allows one to approximate, to any level of accuracy, the energies and wave functions of any potential
K
that differs not too greatly from a potential
K_{0}
whose energies and wave function are known. For this reason, PT features as a major topic in all textbooks on QM. What is noteworthy, is that the approach used to derive the expressions of PT is standard: one starts with the Schrödinger equation for potential
K
expressed as
K_{0}
plus a “perturbation”
K_{1}
(thus
K=K_{0}+K_{1}
), one then introduces a small parameter
\lambda
into
K_{1}
(thus
K=K_{0}+\lambda K_{1}
), and then one expands all
\lambda
-dependent terms into a power series in
\lambda
. By utilising the orthonormality and completeness of eigenstates, the coefficients of the power series
can then be determined by various means.
At its core, this approach relies on the potentials
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
being energy independent, thereby resulting in a complete set of orthonormal eigenstates that are essential for the derivation.

In this paper we would like to introduce a different way of deriving PT that has a significant advantage over the standard one just described: it is simpler and much shorter. Moreover, our formulation remains valid for extensions of QM, like Relativistic QM (see
Rizov et al. 1985
and reference therein), the Bethe-Salpeter (BS) equation
Salpeter and Bethe 1951
;
Gell-Mann and Low 1951
, and all kinds of quasipotential approaches in Quantum Field Theory
Logunov and Tavkhelidze 1963
;
Blankenbecler and Sugar 1966
;
Gross 1969
;
*Gross:1982nz
;
*Gross:1982ny
, where the potentials can be energy dependent and where the inverse free Green function
G_{0}^{-1}(E)
is not restricted to be the operator
E-H_{0}
as in QM, but can be any function of energy
E
.

II
New derivation

II.1
Preliminaries

Textbooks on QM derive PT in a variety of ways, however they all share the same overall approach (see for example
Schiff 1968
;
Shankar 1994
;
Merzbacher 1997
;
Gasiorowicz 2003
;
Griffiths and Schroeter 2018
;
Sakurai and Napolitano 2020
;
Zettili 2022
). For completeness, we provide a quick summary of this standard approach in Appendix A. Here we describe an alternative approach. The method is much simpler than the one we previously proposed
Kvinikhidze and Blankleider 2003
.

We consider a system subject to a potential
K=K_{0}+K_{1}
where
K_{1}
is considered to be a perturbation to a potential
K_{0}
for which the bound state problem has been completely solved. Otherwise we make no assumptions about the energy dependence of
K_{0}
,
K_{1}
, or of the Green function
G_{0}(E)
describing the free propagation of particles in this system. In particular, we would like to find the solution of the bound state equation for the perturbed system,

[G^{-1}_{0}(E_{n})-K_{0}-K_{1}]|\psi_{n}\rangle=0,

(1)

when the unperturbed system has a bound state described by the bound state equation

[G^{-1}_{0}({\cal E}_{n})-K_{0}]|\phi_{n}\rangle=0

(2)

where not only
{\cal E}_{n}
and
|\phi_{n}\rangle
are known, but so is the unperturbed Green function
G_{u}
defined as the solution of the inhomogeneous equation,

G_{u}=G_{0}+G_{0}K_{0}G_{u}.

(3)

In the case of QM, this statement of the PT problem is equivalent to the one given in the standard approach described in Appendix A. Indeed, Eq. (
1
) is just a rearranged version of Eq. (
21
), and Eq. (
2
) is just a rearranged version of Eq. (
18
), and knowing
all
the solutions of the unperturbed Schrödinger equation, Eq. (
18
), as is assumed in the standard approach, implies that
G_{u}
is known, since in that case,

G_{u}(E)=\sum_{m}\frac{|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|}{E-{\cal E}_{m}},

(4)

which follows from the definition of
G_{u}
and the completeness relation Eq. (
20b
). However, in the general case applicable to various extensions of QM, our statement of the PT problem is less restrictive, it allows
K_{0}
and
K_{1}
to be energy dependent and is applicable to any functional form of
G^{-1}_{0}(E)
. Of course in the general case (for example in the BS approach), Eq. (
4
) does not apply and
G_{u}(E)
would need to be found by solving Eq. (
3
).

Being defined by Eq. (
3
),
G_{u}
is an interacting Green functions driven by potential
K_{0}
. A general feature of interacting Green functions is that they exhibit poles at energies corresponding to bound states or resonances produced by their driving potentials, with the residue consisting of an outer product of their corresponding bound state wave functions. Thus, if the unperturbed potential
K_{0}
gives rise to a bound state at energy
{\cal E}_{n}
, one can express
G_{u}(E)
as

G_{u}(E)=\frac{|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|}{E-{\cal E}_{n}}+G_{u}^{b}(E)

(5)

where
|\phi_{n}\rangle
is the corresponding bound state wave function and
G_{u}^{b}(E)
is a “background” term.
Note that Eq. (
5
) implies the normalisation condition
\langle\phi_{n}|\partial(G^{-1}_{0}-K_{0})/\partial E|\phi_{n}\rangle=1
which in the case of QM, reduces to
\langle\phi_{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle=1
.
In writing Eq. (
5
) we have assumed the simplest case of no degeneracy for energy
{\cal E}_{n}
, but our presentation is easily extended to the case of degeneracy simply by replacing the outer product
|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|
with ths sum
\sum_{j}|\phi^{j}_{n}\rangle\langle\phi^{j}_{n}|
where
|\phi^{j}_{n}\rangle
is the
j
’th degenerate state. In QM, Eq. (
5
) follows from Eq. (
4
) and is a consequence of completeness, while in Quantum Field Theory it is usually related the vacuum expectation of the time-ordered product of fields,
G_{u}(E)=\langle 0|T\psi\psi\bar{\psi}\bar{\psi}|0\rangle
.

II.2
Derivation

Using Eq. (
5
) as representative of the general case where the potentials can be energy dependent, the derivation of the perturbed bound state energy
E_{n}
and corresponding bound state function
|\psi_{n}\rangle
, is remarkably short. The key idea is to
use Eq. (
3
) and Eq. (
5
) to write Eq. (
1
) in the form
1
1

1

G^{b}\equiv[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{-1}
should be understood as the solution of the equation
G^{b}=G^{b}_{u}+G^{b}_{u}K_{1}G^{b}
or as the infinite series of Eq. (
10
).

|\psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|}{E_{n}-{\cal E}_{n}}K_{1}(E_{n})|\psi_{n}\rangle,

(6)

which is a bound state equation with a separable kernel, and which can therefore be solved algebraically. Indeed, the shortness of the derivation is due directly to the trick of reducing the PT problem to that of an equation with a separable kernel, a trick that does not appear to have been noticed previously.
In particular, Eq. (
6
) can be used to write the equation for the scalar combination
\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\psi_{n}\rangle
,

\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\psi_{n}\rangle=\frac{\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}(1-G^{b}_{u}K_{1})^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{E_{n}-{\cal E}_{n}}\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\psi_{n}\rangle.

(7)

This is an algebraic equation for
\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\psi_{n}\rangle
which has a nontrivial solution only if

E_{n}={\cal E}_{n}+\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}(E_{n})[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle.

(8)

To solve Eq. (
6
) for
|\psi_{n}\rangle
unambiguously, a normalisation condition is needed, which we choose to correspond to the solution

|\psi_{n}\rangle=[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle,

(9)

as this choice simplifies the PT expressions. There is no problem to renormalise
|\psi_{n}\rangle
in order to construct amplitudes for scattering processes where the bound states are involved
Kvinikhidze and Blankleider 2003
. Note also that in QM, Eq. (
9
) implies
\langle\phi_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle=1
, in agreement with the normalisation chosen in standard derivations of PT
Griffiths and Schroeter 2018
;
Sakurai and Napolitano 2020
.

The simplicity and the shortness of the derivation of Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) constitute the main results of this paper. They provide exact expressions for
E_{n}
and
|\psi_{n}\rangle
, albeit as functions of the yet undetermined exact energy
E_{n}
. As such, it is possible that they could be solved numerically by iteration, starting with
{\cal E}_{n}
as the first estimate of
E_{n}
. However, they can also be used to generate
E_{n}
and
|\psi_{n}\rangle
at each order of
K_{1}
in perturbation theory, by introducing the expansion parameter
\lambda
via the replacement
K_{1}\rightarrow\lambda K_{1}
and expanding Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) in powers of
\lambda
. To pursue this procedure it may be convenient to expand Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) in powers of
K_{1}
beforehand, using

[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{-1}=\sum_{s=0}[G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}(E_{n})]^{s}.

(10)

III
Discussion

We have presented a new derivation of PT that applies not only to QM, but also to various extensions of QM for which the unperturbed potential
K_{0}
and the perturbation potential
K_{1}
can be energy dependent, and for which the inverse free Green function
G_{0}^{-1}(E)
can be a nonlinear function of
E
. However, the main feature of the new approach is its simplicity and the shortness of its derivation, resulting in the expressions Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) for the perturbed energy and wavefunction
E_{n}
and
|\psi_{n}\rangle
, respectively.

Although Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) were derived for the case where the only difference between the perturbed and unperturbed systems is the presence or absence of the perturbation potential
K_{1}
, our approach enables the use of PT for a yet more general case, where in addition to
K_{1}
, the perturbed and unperturbed systems differ by having different free Green functions. Denoting by
G_{1}(E)
and
G_{0}(E)
the free Green functions of the perturbed and unperturbed systems, respectively, it is clear that in the derivation of Sec. II, all that needs to be done to describe this more general case, is to replace Eq. (
1
) with

\displaystyle[G^{-1}_{1}(E_{n})-K_{0}-K_{1}]|\psi_{n}\rangle=0,

(11)

which can of course be written as

\displaystyle[G^{-1}_{0}(E_{n})-K_{0}-\tilde{K}_{1}]|\psi_{n}\rangle=0,

(12)

where
\tilde{K}_{1}=K_{1}+\Delta
and

\Delta=G^{-1}_{0}(E_{n})-G^{-1}_{1}(E_{n})

(13)

is an energy-dependent addition to the perturbation potential
K_{1}
. Thus, to obtain a PT for this more general case, all one needs to do is to use
\tilde{K}_{1}
instead of
K_{1}
in Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
). Such a PT would be useful, for example, to describe a relativistic perturbation where the inverse free Green function
G^{-1}_{1}(E)
of the perturbed system is a non-linear function of
E
, while the completely solved unperturbed system uses the standard linear inverse free Green function
G^{-1}_{0}(E)=E-H_{0}
of nonrelativistic QM.

It is noteworthy that in the case where
K_{0}
is energy independent, so that the unperturbed Green function
G_{u}
is given by Eq. (
4
), but where
K_{1}(E)
is still energy dependent, our perturbed wave function can be written in the form given by Sakurai and Napolitano for QM (see Eq. (5.34) of Ref.
Sakurai and Napolitano 2020
),

|\psi_{n}\rangle=[1-G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})\left(K_{1}(E_{n})-E_{n}+{\cal E}_{n}\right)]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle.

(14)

The distinctive feature of this expression is that the argument of
G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})
is the unperturbed energy
{\cal E}_{n}
, not
E_{n}
. Equation (
14
) is derived in Appendix B. In this case the perturbed energy, Eq. (
8
), can similarly be written as

E_{n}={\cal E}_{n}+\langle\phi|K_{1}(E_{n})\left[1-G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})\left(K_{1}(E_{n})-E_{n}+{\cal E}_{n}\right)\right]^{-1}|\phi\rangle.

(15)

To conclude, we note that the efficiency of our approach can be demonstrated explicitly for the case of QM for which

G^{b}_{u}(E)=\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|}{E-{\cal E}_{m}}.

(16)

Substituting this expression into Eq. (
10
), the first few term of Eq. (
8
) and Eq. (
9
) become

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{n}+\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{|\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle|^{2}}{E_{n}-{\cal E}_{m}}+\dots

(17a)

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\phi_{n}\rangle+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{E_{n}-{\cal E}_{m}}|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq n}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{(E_{n}-{\cal E}_{m})(E_{n}-{\cal E}_{k})}|\phi_{m}\rangle+\dots

(17b)

which immediately gives the perturbation series Eq. (
26
) of the standard approach.
This constitutes a much easier and shorter derivation of PT, and its main results, than that of the standard approach used in all current textbooks on QM. As such, our derivation may present students of QM a simpler and quicker way to learn PT.

Appendix A
Standard approach to PT in QM

In QM, the standard way of framing the task of PT is as follows (see for example Griffiths and Schroeter
Griffiths and Schroeter 2018
). Suppose one knows
all
the solutions of the Schrödinger equation

H_{u}|\phi_{n}\rangle={\cal E}_{n}|\phi_{n}\rangle

(18)

where
H_{u}
is some “unperturbed” Hamiltonian

H_{u}=H_{0}+K_{0},

(19)

H_{0}
being the kinetic energy operator and
K_{0}
being the potential. In almost all presentations it is assumed that the energy levels are discrete, so that
n
is an integer and the solutions
|\phi_{n}\rangle
satisfy the orthonormality and completeness conditions

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{n}|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle=\delta_{nm},

(20a)

\displaystyle\sum_{m}|\phi_{m}\rangle\langle\phi_{m}|

\displaystyle=I.

(20b)

For simplicity, we shall also make this assumption while recognising that in many practical applications one also needs to include scattering states
Colladay and Goff 2020
.
Now suppose a perturbation is introduced into the system by adding a potential
K_{1}
. The problem then is to determine the solutions of

H|\psi_{n}\rangle=E_{n}|\psi_{n}\rangle

(21)

where
H
is the Hamiltonian with the perturbation included:

H=H_{u}+K_{1}.

(22)

The standard way to solve this problem is to introduce a parameter
\lambda
such that

H(\lambda)=H_{u}+\lambda K_{1}.

(23)

One then solves the Schrödinger equation

(H_{u}+\lambda K_{1})|\psi_{n}(\lambda)\rangle=E_{n}(\lambda)|\psi_{n}(\lambda)\rangle

(24)

by making a power expansion in
\lambda
,

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}(\lambda)\rangle

\displaystyle=|\phi_{n}\rangle+\lambda|\psi^{1}_{n}\rangle+\lambda^{2}|\psi^{2}_{n}\rangle+\dots,

(25a)

\displaystyle E_{n}(\lambda)

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{n}+\lambda E_{n}^{1}+\lambda^{2}E_{n}^{2}+\dots.

(25b)

The coefficients of these power series are then determined by various means. After a substantial amount of algebra, and assuming there is no degeneracy, one obtains the perturbation series
Griffiths and Schroeter 2018
;
Sakurai and Napolitano 2020
(shown explicitly up to second order in
K_{1}
)

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle={\cal E}_{n}+\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{|\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle|^{2}}{{\cal E}_{n}-{\cal E}_{m}}+\dots

(26a)

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=|\phi_{n}\rangle+\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{{\cal E}_{n}-{\cal E}_{m}}|\phi_{m}\rangle-\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle\langle\phi_{n}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{({\cal E}_{n}-{\cal E}_{m})^{2}}|\phi_{m}\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq n}\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\langle\phi_{m}|K_{1}|\phi_{k}\rangle\langle\phi_{k}|K_{1}|\phi_{n}\rangle}{({\cal E}_{n}-{\cal E}_{m})({\cal E}_{n}-{\cal E}_{k})}|\phi_{m}\rangle+\dots

(26b)

Appendix B
Derivation of Eq. (
14
)

Assuming that
K_{0}
(but not
K_{1}
) is energy independent, the unperturbed Green function
G_{u}(E)
is given by Eq. (
4
) and its background part,
G^{b}_{u}(E)
, is given by Eq. (
16
). Defining

\Delta_{n}=E_{n}-{\cal E}_{n},

(27)

one can write down the Taylor expansion of
G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})
around the point
{\cal E}_{n}
as

G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})=G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})-\Delta_{n}[G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})]^{2}+\Delta^{2}_{n}[G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})]^{3}+\dots

(28)

Writing
K_{1}\equiv K_{1}(E_{n})
, Eq. (
9
) then becomes

\displaystyle|\psi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1-G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1+G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}+G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}G^{b}_{u}(E_{n})K_{1}+\dots]|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1+G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})K_{1}-\Delta_{n}[G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})]^{2}K_{1}+G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})K_{1}G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})K_{1}+\dots]|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1+G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})K_{1}+G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})(K_{1}-\Delta_{n})G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})K_{1}+\dots]|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1-G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})(K_{1}{-\Delta_{n}})+G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})(K_{1}-\Delta_{n})G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})(K_{1}{-\Delta_{n}})+\dots]|\phi_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=[1-G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})(K_{1}-\Delta_{n})]^{-1}|\phi_{n}\rangle,

(29)

where we have used the fact that
G^{b}_{u}({\cal E}_{n})|\phi_{n}\rangle=0
.

The centre of the algebra
\Lambda(f)

It follows from the direct sum

A_{1}=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}K[x^{p},\partial^{p}]x^{i}\partial^{j}

and the commutation relation

of the Weyl algebra is equal to

K(x^{p},\partial^{p})
be the field of fractions of the polynomial algebra

The localization
{\cal A}_{1}
of the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
by the Ore set

K[x^{p},\partial^{p}]\backslash\{0\}
is a simple
p^{2}
-dimensional algebra

{\cal A}_{1}=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}K(x^{p},\partial^{p})x^{i}\partial^{j}.

This follows from the relation
[\partial,x]=1
. So,
Z({\cal A}_{1})=K(x^{p},\partial^{p})
. Hence,
every nonzero ideal of the Weyl algebra

meets the centre of

A_{1}
.

The polynomial algebra
K[x]
is a left
A_{1}
-module where
\partial
acts as the derivation
\frac{d}{dx}
. Furthermore, the kernel of the corresponding algebra homomorphism

A_{1}\rightarrow{\rm End}_{K}(K[x]),\;\;x\mapsto x,\;\;\partial\mapsto\frac{d}{dx}

(5)

is generated by the central element

. So,
A_{1}/(\partial^{p})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}K[x]\partial^{i}\subseteq{\rm End}_{K}(K[x])
. The factor algebra

is a subalgebra of the algebra

of differential operators on the polynomial algebra

and the Weyl algebra

is not. This is in sharp contrast with the characteristic zero case where

Theorem 2.1

Let
\delta=f\frac{d}{dx}\in{\rm Der}_{K}(K[x])
where
f\in K[x]\backslash\{0\}
, and
g^{\prime}:=\frac{dg}{dx}
where
g\in K[x]

1.

\delta^{p}=(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}\delta\in{\rm Der}_{K}(K[x])
.

2.

In the algebra
\Lambda(f)
,
y^{p}=f^{p}\partial^{p}+(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}y
. In particular, in the Weyl algebra
A_{1}
,
(f\partial)^{p}=f^{p}\partial^{p}+(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}f\partial
.

3.

The centre
Z(\Lambda(f))
of the algebra
\Lambda(f)
is the polynomial algebra

K[x^{p},y^{p}-(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}y]=K[x^{p},f^{p}\partial^{p}]

.

4.

The algebra
\Lambda(f)=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}Z(\Lambda(f))x^{i}y^{j}
is a free

-module of rank

.

5.

The localization of the algebra
\Lambda(f)
at the Ore set
Z(\Lambda(f))\backslash\{0\}
is
{\cal A}_{1}
.

Proof
. 1. Since
\delta^{p}\in{\rm Der}_{K}(K[x])
, we have that
\delta^{p}=g\frac{d}{dx}
where
g=\delta^{p}(x)=\delta^{p-1}(f)=(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}f
. Therefore,

\delta^{p}=(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}f\frac{d}{dx}=(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}\delta.

2. Notice that
y^{p}=(f\partial)^{p}=f^{p}\partial^{p}+\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}\partial^{i}
for some elements
a_{i}\in K[x]
. Recall that
A_{1}/(\partial^{p})=\bigoplus_{i=0}^{p-1}K[x]\partial^{i}\subseteq{\rm End}_{K}(K[x])
. By statement 1,

(f\partial)^{p}\equiv\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}a_{i}\partial^{i}\equiv(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}\delta\equiv(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}f\partial\equiv(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}y\mod(\partial^{p}),

and so
a_{1}=(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}f
and
a_{2}=\cdots=a_{p-1}=0
.

3–5. By statement 2,
f^{p}\partial^{p}=y^{p}-(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}y\in Z(\Lambda(f))
. Hence,

Z^{\prime}:=K[x^{p},y^{p}-(\delta^{p-2}(f))^{\prime}y]=K[x^{p},f^{p}\partial^{p}]\subseteq Z(\Lambda(f))

and
\Lambda(f)=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}Z^{\prime}x^{i}y^{j}
. Now,

(Z^{\prime}\backslash\{0\})^{-1}\Lambda(f)=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}K(x^{p},\partial^{p})x^{i}y^{j}=\bigoplus_{i,j=0}^{p-1}K(x^{p},\partial^{p})x^{i}\partial^{j}={\cal A}_{1},

and so statement 5 is obvious and statements 3–4 follow.
\Box

Algorithm of finding the eigengroup
G_{f}(\overline{K})
and the eigenform of
f
.

The algorithm consists of finitely many steps and is based on Prpoposition
4.8
, Theorem
4.18
, Theorem
4.20
, Theorem
4.22
and Theorem
4.23
.
We assume that
K=\overline{K}
.

Step 1.
If
|{\cal R}_{d}(f)|=1
then apply Proposition
4.8
to find
G_{f}
.

From this moment on we assume that
|{\cal R}_{d}(f)|\geq 2
.

Step 2.
Use Theorem
4.23
to check whether
G_{f}=\{e\}
or
G_{f}\neq\{e\}
.

From this moment on we assume that
G_{f}\neq\{e\}
.

Step 3.
By Proposition
4.12
.(1), the group
\widetilde{G}_{f}={\rm Sh}_{V}
can be found.

Step 4.
Suppose that
\widetilde{G}_{f}=\{e\}
. Then necessarily
\overline{G}_{f}\neq\{e\}
, and using Theorem
4.20
the group
\overline{G}_{f}
is found. In more detail, we know that
\overline{G}_{f}=\langle\sigma_{\lambda_{n},(1-\lambda_{n})\nu}\rangle
and that
f(x)=(x-\nu)^{i}g((x-\nu)^{n})
for a unique
\nu\in{\cal R}_{d}(f)\cup{\cal R}_{d}(f_{1}^{\prime})
and
n\geq 2
such that if
\nu
is a root of
f(x)
then
n={\rm gcd}_{p}(x^{-i}f(x+\nu))
, and if
\nu
is not a root of
f(x)
then
n={\rm gcd}_{p}(f(x+\nu))
.

From this moment on we assume that
\widetilde{G}_{f}={\rm Sh}_{V}\neq\{e\}
, the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subspace
V
of the field
K
is non-zero.
Let
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}
be the multiplier field of
V
. It can be easily found since the multiplier field
\mathbb{F}_{p^{e}}
is the largest among finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}
such that
\mathbb{F}_{p^{m}}V\subseteq V
and
m\leq{\rm dim}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}(V)
.

Step 5.
Now, we check whether the conditions of Theorem
4.22
hold or not. If they do then
\overline{G}_{f}=\{e\}
.

If they do not then necessarily
\overline{G}_{f}\neq\{e\}
and hence the conditions of Theorem Theorem
4.18
hold. Using Theorem
4.18
the group
\overline{G}_{f}
and the eigenform of
f
are found in finitely many steps.
\Box

Step 2.

Error Feedback Reloaded: From Quadratic to Arithmetic Mean of Smoothness Constants Thanks: King Abdullah University of Science and Technology
Peter Richtárik, Elnur Gasanov, Konstantin Burlachenko

Abstract
Error Feedback ( EF ) is a highly popular and immensely effective mechanism for fixing convergence issues which arise in distributed training methods (such as distributed GD or SGD ) when these are enhanced with greedy communication compression techniques such as TopK. While EF was proposed almost a decade ago ( Seide et al. 2014 ) , and despite concentrated effort by the community to advance the theoretical understanding of this mechanism, there is still a lot to explore. In this work we study a modern form of error feedback called EF21 ( Richtárik et al. 2021 ) which offers the currently best-known theoretical guarantees, under the weakest assumptions, and also works well in practice. In particular, while the theoretical communication complexity of EF21 depends on the quadratic mean of certain smoothness parameters, we improve this dependence to their arithmetic mean , which is always smaller, and can be substantially smaller, especially in heterogeneous data regimes. We take the reader on a journey of our discovery process. Starting with the idea of applying EF21 to an equivalent reformulation of the underlying problem which (unfortunately) requires (often impractical) machine cloning , we continue to the discovery of a new weighted version of EF21 which can (fortunately) be executed without any cloning, and finally circle back to an improved analysis of the original EF21 method. While this development applies to the simplest form of EF21 , our approach naturally extends to more elaborate variants involving stochastic gradients and partial participation. Further, our technique improves the best-known theory of EF21 in the rare features regime ( Richtárik et al. 2023 ) . Finally, we validate our theoretical findings with suitable experiments.

Distributed training problems are typically formulated as optimization problems of the form

\textstyle\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{f(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}(x)\right\},

(1)

{\text{communication complexity}=\text{\# communication rounds}\times\text{cost of 1 communication round}}.

(2)

This simple formula clarifies the rationale behind two orthogonal approaches to alleviating the communication bottleneck. i) The first approach aims to minimize the first factor in (
2

Alistarh et al. 2017
;
Khirirat et al. 2018
;

1.1
Communication compression

Vector compression can be achieved through the application of a compression operator. Below, we outline two primary classes of these operators: unbiased (with conically bounded variance) and contractive.

Definition 1

(Compressors)
.

A randomized mapping
{\cal C}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called i) an
unbiased compressor
if for some
\omega>0
it satisfies

\textstyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\cal C}(x)\right]=x,\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\cal C}(x)-x\|^{2}\right]\leq\omega\|x\|^{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(3)

and ii) a
contractive compressor
if for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
it satisfies

\textstyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|{\cal C}(x)-x\|^{2}\right]\leq(1-\alpha)\|x\|^{2},\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(4)

It is well known that whenever a compressor
{\cal C}
satisfies (
3
), then the scaled compressor
{\cal C}/(\omega+1)
satisfies (
4
) with
\alpha=1-(\omega+1)^{-1}
. In this sense, the class of contractive compressors includes all unbiased compressors as well. However, it is also strictly larger. For example, the Top
K
compressor, which retains the
K
largest elements in absolute value of the vector it is applied to and replaces the rest by zeros, and happens to be very powerful in practice
(
Alistarh et al. 2018
)
, satisfies (
4
) with
\alpha=\frac{K}{d}
, but does not satisfy (
3
). From now on, we write
\mathbb{C}(\alpha)
to denote the class of compressors satisfying (
4
).

It will be convenient to define the following functions of the contraction parameter

\textstyle\theta=\theta(\alpha)\coloneqq 1-\sqrt{1-\alpha};\quad\beta=\beta(\alpha)\coloneqq\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\sqrt{1-\alpha}};\quad\xi=\xi(\alpha)\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\beta(\alpha)}{\theta(\alpha)}}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-\alpha}}{\alpha}-1.

(5)

Note that
0\leq\xi(\alpha)<\frac{2}{\alpha}-1
.
The behavior of distributed algorithms utilizing unbiased compressors for solving (
1

)
. However, it is well known that the class of contractive compressors includes some practically very powerful operators, such as the greedy sparsifier Top

However, our theoretical understanding of algorithms based on contractive compressors in general, and these powerful biased compressors in particular, is very weak. Indeed, while the SOTA theory involving unbiased compressors offers significant and often several-degrees-of-magnitude improvements over the baseline methods that do not use compression

To the best of our knowledge, the only exception to this is the very recent work of

EF21 Reloaded: Our Discovery Story

We now take the reader along on a ride of our discovery process.

2.1
Step 1: Cloning the client with the worse smoothness constant

The starting point of our journey is a simple observation described in the following example.

Example 1
.

Let
n=4
and
f(x)=\frac{1}{4}(f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)+f_{3}(x)+f_{4}(x))
. Assume the smoothness constants
L_{1},L_{2},L_{3}
of

are equal to 1, and

is equal to

\textstyle T_{1}\coloneqq{\cal O}\left({\left(L+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}\xi(\alpha)\right)}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)={\cal O}\left({\left(L+\sqrt{2501.5}\xi(\alpha)\right)}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)

iterations. Now, envision the existence of an additional machine capable of downloading the data from the fourth “problematic” machine. By rescaling local loss functions, we maintain the overall loss function as:

\textstyle f(x)=\frac{1}{4}(f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(x)+f_{3}(x)+f_{4}(x))=\frac{1}{5}\left(\frac{5}{4}f_{1}(x)+\frac{5}{4}f_{2}(x)+\frac{5}{4}f_{3}(x)+\frac{5}{8}f_{4}(x)+\frac{5}{8}f_{4}(x)\right)\coloneqq\tilde{f}(x).

Rescaling of the functions modifies the smoothness constants to
\hat{L}_{i}=\frac{5}{4}L_{i}
for
i=1,2,3
, and
\hat{L}_{i}=\frac{5}{8}L_{4}
for

EF21

\textstyle T_{2}\coloneqq{\cal O}\left({\left(L+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\tilde{L}_{\rm QM}}\xi(\alpha)\right)}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\approx{\cal O}\left({\left(L+\sqrt{1564}\xi(\alpha)\right)}{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)

{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\tilde{L}_{\rm QM}}
is the quadratic mean of the new smoothness constants
\hat{L}_{1},\dots,\hat{L}_{5}

This simple observation highlights that the addition of just one more client significantly enhances the convergence rate. Indeed,

requires approximately
\frac{\xi(\alpha)}{\varepsilon}(\sqrt{2501.5}-\sqrt{1564})\approx 10\frac{\xi(\alpha)}{\varepsilon}
fewer iterations. We will generalize this client cloning idea in the next section.

Step 2: Generalizing the cloning idea

We will now take the above motivating example further, allowing each client

Notice that we scaled the functions as before, and that

Analysis of the convergence rate.
The performance of

\textstyle M(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{i}}L_{ij}^{2}}=\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{N}{n^{2}N_{i}}L_{i}^{2}}=\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{N_{i}/N}}.

(10)

Note that if

Optimal choice of cloning frequencies.
Our goal is to find integer values

In our work, we address an optimization problem of the form

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{j}\geq 0\ \forall j\in[n];\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}},

Zhao & Zhang 2015
applies importance sampling to reduce the variance of a stochastic gradient estimator by adjusting sampling probabilities, our method involves adjusting client cloning weights without sampling. 2.3
Step 3: From client cloning to update weighting

It is evident that employing client cloning improves the convergence rate. Nevertheless, there are obvious drawbacks associated with this approach. Firstly, it necessitates a larger number of computational devices, rendering its implementation less appealing from a resource allocation perspective. Secondly, the utilization of
EF21
with cloned machines results in a departure from the principles of Federated Learning, as it inherently compromises user privacy – transferring data from one device to another is prohibited in FL.

However, a simpler approach to implementing the cloning idea emerges when we assume the compressors used to be
deterministic
. To illustrate this, let us initially examine how we would typically implement
EF21
with cloned machines:

\displaystyle\textstyle x^{t+1}=x^{t}-\gamma\frac{1}{N}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{N_{i}}g_{ij}^{t},

(11)

Appendix A
Basic Results and Lemmas

In this section, we offer a few results that serve as essential prerequisites for establishing the main findings in the paper.

A.1
Optimal client cloning frequencies

Lemma 1

(Optimal weights)
.

Let
a_{i}>0
for
i\in[n]
. Then

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1}>0,\dots,w_{n}>0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=1\end{subarray}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\right)^{2},

(17)

which is achieved when
w^{\ast}_{i}=\frac{a_{i}}{\sum_{j}a_{j}}
. This means that

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1}>0,\dots,w_{n}>0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

(18)

We now show that the cloning frequencies given by
N^{\star}_{i}=\left\lceil\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\right\rceil
form a
\sqrt{2}
-approximation for the optimization problem of finding the optimal integer client frequencies.

Lemma 2

(
\sqrt{2}
-approximation)
.

If we let
N^{\star}_{i}=\left\lceil\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\right\rceil
for all
i\in[n]
, then

{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}\leq\min_{N_{1}\in\mathbb{N},\dots,N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}M(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})\leq M(N^{\star}_{1},\dots,N^{\star}_{n})\leq\sqrt{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}.

Proof.

Recall that

M(N_{1},\dots,N_{n})\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{N_{i}/N}}.

The first inequality in the claim follows by relaxing the integrality constraints, which gives us the bound

\min\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}w_{1}>0,\dots,w_{n}>0\\
\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}=1\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}}}\leq\min_{N_{1}\in\mathbb{N},\dots,N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{N_{i}/N}},

and subsequently applying Lemma
17
.

Next, we argue that the quantity
N^{\star}\coloneqq\sum_{i}N^{\star}_{i}
is at most
2n
. Indeed,

N^{\star}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}N^{\star}_{i}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\right\rceil\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}+1\right)=2n.

(19)

We will now use this to bound
M(N^{\star}_{1},\dots,N^{\star}_{n})
from above:

\displaystyle M(N^{\star}_{1},\dots,N^{\star}_{n})=\frac{1}{n}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{N^{\star}_{i}/N^{\star}}}\overset{\eqref{eq:98y08fd=-=f-d}}{=}\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{N^{\star}_{i}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}}{N^{\star}_{i}}L_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}.

Since
\frac{\frac{L_{i}}{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}}{N^{\star}_{i}}\leq 1
for all
i\in[n]
, the proof is finished as follows:

\displaystyle M(N^{\star}_{1},\dots,N^{\star}_{n})\leq\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\sqrt{n}}\sqrt{{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}}=\sqrt{2}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}.

∎

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|g_{i}^{t+1}-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}\right\|^{2}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|g_{i}^{t}+{\cal C}_{i}^{t}\left(\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}-g_{i}^{t}\right)-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}\right\|^{2}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:compressor_contraction}}{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\left\|\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}-g_{i}^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\left\|\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})}{nw_{i}}+\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})}{nw_{i}}-g_{i}^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:young}}{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)(1+s)\left\|\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})}{nw_{i}}-g_{i}^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+(1-\alpha)\left(1+s^{-1}\right)\frac{1}{n^{2}w_{i}^{2}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right\|^{2},

with the final inequality holding for any positive scalar
s>0
. Consequently, we have successfully established the first part of the lemma.

By employing (
24
) and the preceding inequality, we can derive the subsequent bound for the conditional expectation of
G^{t+1}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\mid W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_def_grad_distortion}}{=}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}G_{i}^{t+1}\mid W^{t}\right]

(29)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_def_grad_distortion}}{=}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|g_{i}^{t+1}-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})}{nw_{i}}\right\|^{2}\mid W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_ind_grad_dist_evolution}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\|g_{i}^{t}-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})}{nw_{i}}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\beta(\alpha,s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}}{w_{i}^{2}n^{2}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right\|^{2}.

Applying
2
and (
25
), we further proceed to:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\mid W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_aux_1}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\|g_{i}^{t}-\frac{\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})}{nw_{i}}\right\|^{2}+\beta(\alpha,s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{w_{i}}{w_{i}^{2}n^{2}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right\|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_def_grad_distortion}}{=}

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}n^{2}}\left\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:L_i}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}n^{2}}\right)\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weight_definition}}{=}

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{\frac{L_{i}}{\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}}n^{2}}\right)\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}\sum_{j=1}^{n}L_{j}}{n^{2}}\right)\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}.

Using the tower property, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\mid W^{t}\right]\right]\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_aux_2}}{\leq}\left(1-\theta(\alpha,s)\right)\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t}\right]+\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}\right],

and this finalizes the proof.
∎

Proof.

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weighted_def_grad_distortion}}{=}

Subtracting
f^{\ast}
from both sides and taking expectation, we get

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[f(x^{t+1})-f^{\ast}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}\right]-\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(x^{t})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\frac{\gamma}{2}\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t}\right].

(33)

\delta^{t}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\left[f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}\right]

and

\displaystyle\delta^{t+1}+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta(\alpha,s)}s^{t+1}

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta(\alpha,s)}\left(\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}r^{t}+(1-\theta(\alpha,s))s^{t}\right)

\displaystyle\delta^{t}+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta(\alpha,s)}s^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x^{t})\right\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2\theta(\alpha,s)}\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\right)r^{t}

\displaystyle\delta^{t}+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta(\alpha,s)}s^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\left\|\nabla f(x^{t})\right\|^{2}.

The last inequality is a result of the bound
\gamma^{2}\frac{\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}}{\theta(\alpha,s)}+L\gamma\leq 1,
which is satisfied for the stepsize

\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}\xi(\alpha,s)},

where
\xi(\alpha,s)\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{\theta(\alpha,s)}}
.
Maximizing the stepsize bound over the choice of
s
using
Lemma

6
, we obtain the final stepsize.
By summing up inequalities for
t=0,\ldots,T-1,
we get

0\leq\delta^{T}+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta}s^{T}\leq\delta^{0}+\frac{\gamma}{2\theta}s^{0}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(x^{t})\right\|^{2}\right].

Multiplying both sides by
\frac{2}{\gamma T}
, after rearranging we get

\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\frac{1}{T}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(x^{t})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2\delta^{0}}{\gamma T}+\frac{s^{0}}{\theta T}.

It remains to notice that the left hand side can be interpreted as
\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\nabla f(\hat{x}^{T})\right\|^{2}\right]
, where
\hat{x}^{T}
is chosen from
\{x^{0},x^{1},\ldots,x^{T-1}\}
uniformly at random.
∎

Appendix D
Proof of
Theorem

4
(Improved Theory for EF21)

We commence by redefining gradient distortion as follows:

G^{t}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})-g^{t}_{i}\|^{2}.

(36)

We recall that the gradient update step for standard
EF21
(
Algorithm

1
) takes the following form:

\displaystyle g_{i}^{t+1}=g_{i}^{t}+{\cal C}_{i}^{t}(\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-g_{i}^{t}),

(37)

\displaystyle g^{t+1}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{t+1}.

(38)

D.1
Two lemmas

Once more, we start our proof with the contraction lemma.

Lemma 8
.

Let
{\cal C}_{i}^{t}\in\mathbb{C}(\alpha)
for all
i\in[n]
and
t\geq 0
. Define
W^{t}\coloneqq\{g_{1}^{t},g_{2}^{t},\dots,g_{n}^{t},x^{t},x^{t+1}\}
. Let
2
hold. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\;|\;W^{t}\right]\leq(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2},

(39)

where
\theta(\alpha,s)\coloneqq 1-(1-\alpha)(1+s)
and
\beta(\alpha,s)\coloneqq(1-\alpha)(1+s^{-1})
for any
s>0
.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma starts as the similar lemma in the standard analysis of
EF21
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:new_def_distortion}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-g^{t+1}_{i}\|^{2}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:standard_ind_grad_upd}}{=}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\mathbb{E}\left[\|g_{i}^{t}+{\cal C}_{i}^{t}(\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-g_{i}^{t})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})\|^{2}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:compressor_contraction}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-\alpha}{w_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-g_{i}^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-\alpha}{w_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})+\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})-g_{i}^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:young}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-\alpha}{w_{i}}\left((1+s^{-1})\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t}))\|^{2}+(1+s)\|g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}\right)

for all
s>0
. We proceed the proof as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:aux1}}{\leq}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-\alpha}{w_{i}}\left((1+s^{-1})\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t}))\|^{2}+(1+s)\|g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}\right)

(41)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))\frac{1}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\|g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t}))\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:new_def_distortion}}{=}

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t+1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:L_i}}{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}.

Note that this is the exact place where the current analysis differs from the standard one. It fully coincides with it when
w_{i}=\frac{1}{n}
, i.e., when we assign the same weight for each individual gradient distortion
\|g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}
. However, applying weights according to “importance” of each function, we proceed as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[G^{t+1}\;|\;W^{t}\right]

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:aux2}}{\leq}

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{w_{i}}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:weight_definition}}{=}

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{L_{i}^{2}}{L_{i}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\frac{\beta(\alpha,s)}{n^{2}}\sum_{j}L_{j}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-\theta(\alpha,s))G^{t}+\beta(\alpha,s){\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2},

what finishes the proof.
∎

To prove the main convergence theorem, we also need the following lemma.

Lemma 9
.

For the variable
g^{t}
from
Algorithm

1
, the following inequality holds:

\|g^{t}-\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}\leq G^{t}.

(42)

Proof.

The proof is straightforward:

\displaystyle\|g^{t}-\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:standard_average_grad}}{=}

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\left(g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\frac{1}{w_{i}n}\left(g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}w_{i}\left\|\frac{1}{w_{i}n}\left(g_{i}^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\right)\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{w_{i}n^{2}}\|g^{t}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|^{2}\quad\overset{\eqref{eq:new_def_distortion}}{=}\quad G^{t},

where the only inequality in this series of equations is derived using Jensen’s inequality.
∎

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{\gamma}{\theta(\alpha,s)}\left((1-\theta(\alpha,s))s^{t}+\beta\left(\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\right)^{2}r^{t}\right)

\displaystyle(1-\gamma\mu)\delta^{t}+\frac{\gamma}{\theta(\alpha,s)}\left(1-\frac{\theta(\alpha,s)}{2}\right)s^{t}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\beta{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\gamma}{\theta(\alpha,s)}\right)r^{t}.

It remains to unroll the recurrence which finishes the prove.

\displaystyle\|g^{t}-\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

H.4
Main result

And now we are ready to formulate the main result.

Theorem 11
.

Let Assumptions
1
,
2
and
3
hold. Let
g_{i}^{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{i}
for all
i\in[n]
,

\alpha=\min\left\{\min\limits_{i\in[n]}\frac{K_{i}}{|\mathcal{J}_{i}|},1\right\},\qquad 0<\gamma\leq\frac{1}{L+\frac{c}{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}\xi(\alpha)}.

Under these conditions, the iterates of
Algorithm

6
satisfy

\frac{1}{T}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}\leq\frac{2(f(x^{0})-f^{\ast})}{\gamma T}+\frac{c}{n}\frac{G^{0}}{\theta(\alpha)T}.

(84)

Proof.

Let us define the Lyapunov function:

\Psi^{t}\coloneqq f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}G^{t}.

(85)

We start the proof as follows:

\displaystyle\Psi^{t+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sparse_Lyapunov}}{=}

\displaystyle f(x^{t+1})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}G^{t+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:descent_lemma}}{\leq}

\displaystyle f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\|g^{t}-\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}G^{t+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:grad_est_distortion}}{\leq}

\displaystyle f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{c}{n}G^{t}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}G^{t+1}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq:sparse_grad_est_evolution}}{\leq}

\displaystyle f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{c}{n}G^{t}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}\left((1-\theta)G^{t}+\beta\frac{c}{n}{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x^{t})-f^{\ast}+\frac{\gamma c}{2\theta n}G^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}-\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\beta}{\theta}\frac{c^{2}}{n^{2}}\cdot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\right)\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Psi^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}-\underbrace{\left(\frac{1}{2\gamma}-\frac{L}{2}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\frac{\beta}{\theta}\frac{c^{2}}{n^{2}}\cdot{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\right)}_{\geq 0}\|x^{t+1}-x^{t}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Psi^{t}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2}.

Unrolling the inequality above, we get

\displaystyle 0\leq\Psi^{T}\leq\Psi^{T-1}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\|\nabla f(x^{T-1})\|^{2}\leq\Psi^{0}-\frac{\gamma}{2}\sum\limits_{t=0}^{T-1}\|\nabla f(x^{t})\|^{2},

from what the main result follows.
∎

I.2
Comments on the improvement

The standard
EF21

\displaystyle\gamma=\left(L+{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta(\alpha)}{\theta(\alpha)}}\right)^{-1},\qquad\theta(\alpha)=1-\sqrt{1-\alpha},\qquad\beta(\alpha)=\frac{1-\alpha}{1-\sqrt{1-\alpha}}.

Our analysis allows us to replace the quantity
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}
with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}
. This improvement has an important consequence. The replaced quantity affects the step size by a factor of
\xi(\alpha)=\sqrt{\frac{\beta(\alpha)}{\theta(\alpha)}}
. This factor can be arbitrarily large as
d
increases, as shown in Figure
4
. If
d
is increasing and the parameter
k
of
TopK
compressor is fixed, then even a small improvement in the constant term can have a significant impact in an absolute sense to the computed step size if
\xi(\alpha)\gg L
.

I.3
When improved analysis leads to more aggressive steps

The quantity
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}\coloneqq\sqrt{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}^{2}}
plays essential role in
EF21
analysis. As we saw with special consideration this quantity for
EF21
and its extensions is improvable. The improved analysis allows us to replace it with
{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}.
Clearly, by the arithmetic-quadratic mean inequality,

L_{\rm var}\coloneqq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}^{2}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}\geq 0.

The difference
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}
can be expressed as follows:

\displaystyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}})\left(\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}^{2}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\quad=\quad\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\cdot{\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(L_{i}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}L_{i}\right)^{2}}.

The coefficient
\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}
in the last equation can be bounded from below and above as follows:

\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}}=\frac{1}{2\cdot\max({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}})}\leq\frac{1}{{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}+{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}\leq\frac{1}{2\cdot\min({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}},{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}})}\leq\frac{1}{2{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}}.

As a consequence, difference
{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}}
is bound above by the estimated variance of
L_{i}
divided by the mean of
L_{i}
, also known as
Index of Dispersion
in statistics. From this consideration, we can more easily observe that
EF21-W
can have an arbitrarily better stepsize than vanilla
EF21
if the variance of
L_{i}
is increasing faster than the mean of
L_{i}
.

Dataset generation for synthetic experiment

First, we assume that the user provides two parameters:
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
L\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. These parameters define the construction of strongly convex function
f_{i}(x)
, which are modified by meta-parameters
q\in[-1,1]
and

, described next.

1.

Each client initially has

f_{i}(x)\coloneqq\frac{1}{n_{i}}\left\|\mathbf{A}_{i}x-{b_{i}}\right\|^{2},

where
\mathbf{A}_{i}
is initialized in such way that
f_{i}
is
L_{i}
smooth and
\mu_{f_{i}}
strongly convex. Parameters are defined in the following way:

L_{i}=\frac{i}{n}\cdot(L-\mu)+\mu,\qquad\mu_{f_{i}}=\mu.

2.

The scalar value

informally plays the role of meta parameter to change the distribution of
L_{i}
and make values of
L_{i}
close to one of the following: (i)
\mu
; (ii)
L
; (iii)
(L+\mu)/2
. The exact modification of

.

•

Case

. In this case for first
n/2
(i.e.,

) compute the value

, where

\mathrm{lerp}(a,b,t)=a(1-t)+bt.

The last
n/2
(

L_{i,q}=\mathrm{lerp}(L_{i},L,q)
. For example, if
q=0

{n}/{2}

L_{i,q}=L

{n}/{2}
clients.

•

Case
q\in[-1,0]
. In this for all
n
clients the new value

. In this case for example if
q=0

L_{i,q}=L_{i}
and if

.

The process firstly fills the
\mathbf{A}_{i}
in such form that

3.

We use these new values

4.

Next obtained values are used to generate

5.

I.5
Dataset shuffling strategy for LIBSVM dataset

Our dataset shuffling strategy heuristically splits data points so that
L_{\rm var}
is maximized. It consists of the following steps:

1.

Sort data points from the whole dataset according to
L
constants.
Sort all data points according to the smoothness constants of the loss function for each single data point.

2.

Assign a single data point to each client
Assume that there are total
m
data points in the datasets, and the total number of clients is
n
. At the beginning each client
i
holds a single data point
\lfloor(i-1+1/2)\cdot\frac{m}{n}\rfloor
.

3.

Pass through all points.
Initialize set
F=\{\}
. Next, we pass through all points except those assigned from the previous step. For each point we find the best client
i^{\prime}\in[n]\backslash F
to assign the point in a way that assignment of point to client
i^{\prime}
maximize
L_{\rm var}\coloneqq{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}L_{\rm QM}^{2}}-{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}L_{\rm AM}^{2}}
. Once the client already has
\lceil\frac{m}{n}\rceil
data points assigned to it, the client is added to the set
F
.

The set
F
in the last step guarantees each client will have
\frac{m}{n}
data points. In general, this is a heuristic greedy strategy that approximately maximizes
L_{\rm var}
under the constraint that each client has the same amount of data points equal to
\lfloor\frac{m}{n}\rfloor
. Due to its heuristic nature, the Algorithm does not provide deep guarantees, but it was good enough for our experiments.

On D. Peterson’s presentation of quantum cohomology of G / P G/P
Chi Hong Chow

Abstract
. We prove in full generality that the T T -equivariant quantum cohomology of any flag variety G / P G/P is isomorphic to the coordinate ring of a stratum of the Peterson scheme associated to the Langlands dual group scheme G ∨ G^{\vee} . This result was discovered by Dale Peterson but remains unpublished. Our isomorphism is constructed using Yun-Zhu’s isomorphism and Peterson-Lam-Shimozono’s homomorphism.



1.
introduction

1.1.
Main results

Let
G
be a simple simply-connected complex algebraic group and
P
a parabolic subgroup of
G
. We prove the following results expressing the
T
-equivariant quantum cohomology
QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)
and its localization in terms of the Langlands dual group scheme
G^{\vee}
. Our coefficient ring is
\mathbb{Z}[\ell_{G}^{-1}]
where
\ell_{G}
is equal to 1 for type ADE, 2 for type BCF and 3 for type G.

Theorem A
.

There exists an isomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
-algebras

\Phi:\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})\xrightarrow{\sim}QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)

which induces an injective homomorphism

\Phi^{*}:\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*})\rightarrow QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}].

The latter is also an isomorphism if we set all equivariant parameters to zero.

The schemes
\mathcal{Y}_{P}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*}
are defined as follows. Define first
e^{T}:=e+f\in\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}\otimes H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
where

e:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\quad\text{ and }\quad f:\alpha^{\vee}_{i}\mapsto|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}\alpha_{i},

and

\mathcal{Y}:=\left\{(gB_{-}^{\vee},h)\in G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)\left|~g^{-1}\cdot e^{T}(h)\in\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}\oplus\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\right.\right\}.

Here
e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}
is a generator of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}
and
|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}=1
or
\ell_{G}
depending on whether
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is short or long. Then

\mathcal{Y}_{P}:=\mathcal{Y}\times_{G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}}U^{\vee}_{P^{\vee}_{-}}\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}

and

\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*}:=\mathcal{Y}_{P}\times_{G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}}U^{\vee}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}.

The regular functions

\overline{q}_{i}:(u\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee},h)\mapsto|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{-2}\langle e^{*}_{-w_{0}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}},(u\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}\cdot e^{T}(h)\rangle

make
\mathcal{Y}_{P}
and
\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*}
schemes over
\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
. The homomorphisms
\Phi
and
\Phi^{*}
stated in Theorem
A
are required to send these regular functions to the quantum parameters
q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}
.

In general,
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*})
is not isomorphic to
QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}]
. A counterexample arises already when
G=\mathbf{SL}_{2}
. See Section
1.3
. To obtain a presentation of
QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}]
, we consider another scheme. Define
B_{e^{T}}^{\vee}
to be the centralizer group scheme of
e^{T}
in
B^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
. This group scheme was introduced by Yun and Zhu
[
49
]
who proved that
\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee})
is isomorphic to the
T
-equivariant homology
H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
of the affine Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}r
of
G
.

Theorem B
.

There exists an isomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
-algebras

\Phi_{loc}:\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee}\times_{G^{\vee}}U^{\vee}_{-}(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}B_{-}^{\vee})\xrightarrow{\sim}QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}]

under which the quantum parameters
q_{i}
correspond to the regular functions

\overline{q}_{loc,i}:(u_{1}(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}tu_{2},h)\mapsto\alpha^{\vee}_{i}(t).

Theorem
B
has an immediate application to mirror symmetry. Let us take the coefficient ring to be
\mathbb{C}
. Motivated by earlier work
[
2
,
20
,
25
,
38
]
, Rietsch
[
41
]
constructed a Landau-Ginzburg model
(X_{P}^{\vee},W,\pi,p)
and conjectured that it is the
T
-equivariant mirror of
G/P
. She gave evidence by proving the following mirror symmetry prediction.

Theorem C
.

(
[
41
, Corollary 4.2]
) There exists an isomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
-algebras

\jac(X_{P}^{\vee},W,\pi,p)\simeq QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}].

Here,
\jac(X_{P}^{\vee},W,\pi,p)
is the Jacobi ring which, in the non-equivariant case, is the coordinate ring of the fiberwise critical locus of the superpotential
W
.
\square

However, her proof relies essentially on a version of the Peterson variety presentation which is equivalent to our Theorem
B
, and which, to the best of our knowledge, has remained conjectural since Peterson’s announcement (see Section
1.2
). Therefore, our work completes the last step of the proof of Theorem
C
.

We will not review the Rietsch mirror in this paper. For more discussion, see our sequel paper
[
12
]
where we enhance the isomorphism from Theorem
C
to an isomorphism of
D
-modules.

1.2.
History

In 1997, Peterson
[
38
]
gave a lecture course at MIT where he announced the following four results about the quantum cohomology of flag varieties:

(1)

Quantum Chevalley formula
, an explicit formula expressing the quantum product of a Schubert class and a divisor class in terms of other Schubert classes.

(2)

Comparison formula
, an explicit formula expressing the Schubert structure constants of
QH^{\bullet}(G/P)
in terms of those of
QH^{\bullet}(G/B)
.

(3)

“Quantum equals affine” and its generalization to the parabolic case
, which states that the affine Schubert calculus determines the quantum Schubert calculus for
G/P
completely, and vice versa for
P=B
, through an explicit ring homomorphism from the Pontryagin homology
H_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
of the affine Grassmannian
\mathcal{G}r
of
G
to
QH^{\bullet}(G/P)[q^{-1}_{1},\ldots,q^{-1}_{k}]
.

(4)

Peterson variety presentation
, which is the non-equivariant version of our Theorem
A
.

However, he did not publish any of these results. Some years later, complete proofs of the first three results were given by other people: (1) was proved by Fulton and Woodward
[
18
]
and generalized later by Mihalcea
[
36
]
to the equivariant settings; (2) was proved by Woodward
[
48
]
; and (3) was proved by Lam and Shimozono
[
33
]
. The proof in
[
33
]
is combinatorial. Recently, the author of the present paper
[
11
]
has given a new proof of (3) which is geometric. Both proofs work for the equivariant settings.

It is (4) which has not been proved in full generality so our work provides the final piece of the puzzle. The case
P=B
is complete. The quantum cohomology ring
QH^{\bullet}(G/B)
has another presentation formulated in terms of the Toda lattice. It was established by Ciocan-Fontanine
[
13
]
, Givental and Kim
[
21
]
for type A, and Kim
[
27
]
for the general type. Although it is different from Peterson’s presentation, they are equivalent after a change of variables due to Kostant
[
29
]
.

Beyond the Borel case, again using known presentations of quantum cohomology
[
1
,
14
,
26
,
30
,
31
,
46
,
47
]
, Rietsch
[
39
]
proved (4) for Grassmannians and later for all type A flag varieties
[
40
]
; and Cheong
[
10
]
for Lagrangian and orthogonal Grassmannians. For minuscule flag varieties, Lam and Templier
[
34
]
proved the
D
-module mirror conjecture and used it to establish the equivariant version of (4) by taking the semi-classical limit and applying a result of Rietsch
[
41
]
.

1.3.
Example: the rank-one case

As a warm up, let us prove by hand Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
for the case
G=\mathbf{SL}_{2}
and
P=B
. We have
G/P\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
and
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h]
. The Langlands dual group scheme
G^{\vee}
is isomorphic to
\mathbf{PGL}_{2}
. Take
T^{\vee}\subset G^{\vee}
to be the diagonal torus and
B^{\vee}
(resp.
B_{-}^{\vee}
) to be the subgroups of upper (resp. lower) triangular matrices. Then
G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
. Denote by
U^{\vee}
(resp.
U^{\vee}_{-}
) the unipotent radical of
B^{\vee}
(resp.
B_{-}^{\vee}
). Let
\alpha
(resp.
\alpha^{\vee}
) be the unique positive root (resp. coroot) determined by
B^{\vee}
. Define

e_{\alpha^{\vee}}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
0&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}}\quad\text{ and }\quad e_{-\alpha^{\vee}}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&0\\
1&0\end{matrix}\right)\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{-\alpha^{\vee}}.

We have

e^{T}(h)=h\alpha+e_{\alpha^{\vee}}.

The
T
-equivariant quantum cohomology
QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
is generated by the equivariant parameter
h
, the quantum parameter
q
and the unique Schubert class
\sigma
which is not equal to 1, subject to

\sigma^{2}=2h\sigma+q.

(The presence of the factor “2” in the above expression is due to the fact that we are using the maximal torus of
\mathbf{SL}_{2}
instead of
\mathbf{PGL}_{2}
.) In other words,

QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,q,\sigma]/\langle\sigma^{2}-2h\sigma-q\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,\sigma].

Observe that the condition for the definition of
\mathcal{Y}
is always satisfied, and hence

\mathcal{Y}=G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)\simeq\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}.

The projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}
is covered by two open subschemes

U^{\vee}(e):=U^{\vee}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}\simeq U^{\vee}\quad\text{ and }\quad U^{\vee}(w_{0}):=U^{\vee}_{-}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}\simeq U^{\vee}_{-}

where
w_{0}\in W\simeq S_{2}
is the longest element. Then we have

\mathcal{Y}_{P}\simeq U^{\vee}(w_{0})\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*}\simeq\left(U^{\vee}(w_{0})\cap U^{\vee}(e)\right)\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{\mathbb{Z}},

and hence

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,y_{-}]\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*})\simeq\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})[y_{-}^{-1}]\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,y_{-}^{\pm 1}]

where
y_{-}:=\chi_{-}(u_{-})
is the composition of the
U^{\vee}(w_{0})
-coordinate
u_{-}
of
\mathcal{Y}_{P}
and the unique isomorphism
\chi_{-}:U^{\vee}_{-}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{G}_{a}
satisfying
\lie(\chi_{-})(e_{-\alpha^{\vee}})=1
. The homomorphism

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Phi&:&\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})&\rightarrow&QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})\\[5.0pt]
&&y_{-}&\mapsto&-\sigma\end{array}

is obviously an isomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
-algebras.

We also need to show that
\Phi
is
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-linear. Let
e_{\alpha^{\vee}}^{*}\in(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee})^{*}
be the unique element satisfying
\langle e_{\alpha^{\vee}}^{*},e_{\alpha^{\vee}}\rangle=1
and
e_{\alpha^{\vee}}^{*}|_{\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}}=0
. Define

\dot{w}_{0}:=\left(\begin{matrix}0&1\\
-1&0\end{matrix}\right)

considered as a
\mathbb{Z}
-point of
N_{G^{\vee}}(T^{\vee})
. It represents
w_{0}
in the Weyl group. We have

\displaystyle\overline{q}

\displaystyle=\langle e_{\alpha^{\vee}}^{*},(u_{-}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}\cdot(h\alpha+e_{\alpha^{\vee}})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle e_{\alpha^{\vee}}^{*},\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\cdot[-(y_{-}^{2}+2hy_{-})e_{-\alpha^{\vee}}+(y_{-}+h)\alpha+e_{\alpha^{\vee}}]\rangle

\displaystyle=y_{-}^{2}+2hy_{-}.

It follows that
\Phi(\overline{q})=\sigma^{2}-2h\sigma=q
as desired.

Next we look at
\Phi^{*}
. Define

\Phi^{*}:=\Phi[y_{-}^{-1}]:\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{*})\xrightarrow{\sim}QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})[\sigma^{-1}].

Observe that
\sigma^{-1}=q^{-1}(\sigma-2h)
, and hence

QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})[\sigma^{-1}]\subseteq QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})[q^{-1}].

But this inclusion is proper unless we set
h=0
. In fact, if
\Bbbk
is an integral domain such that
2\neq 0
, then
\Bbbk[h,\sigma^{\pm 1}]
and
\Bbbk[h,\sigma,(\sigma^{2}-2h\sigma)^{-1}]
are not isomorphic as
\Bbbk
-algebras. Indeed, after inverting 2, they are isomorphic to
\Bbbk_{1}[X,Y^{\pm 1}]
and
\Bbbk_{1}[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1}]
respectively, where
\Bbbk_{1}:=\Bbbk[2^{-1}]
. But

\Bbbk_{1}[X,Y^{\pm 1}]^{\times}/\Bbbk_{1}^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{Z}\quad\text{ while }\quad\Bbbk_{1}[X^{\pm 1},Y^{\pm 1}]^{\times}/\Bbbk_{1}^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}.

We now look at
\Phi_{loc}
. Let
t
and
u_{+}
be the
T^{\vee}
- and
U^{\vee}
-coordinates of
B^{\vee}\simeq T^{\vee}\times U^{\vee}
respectively. Then

(tu_{+})\cdot e^{T}(h)=h\alpha+\alpha^{\vee}(t)(1-2h\chi_{+}(u_{+}))e_{\alpha^{\vee}}

where
\chi_{+}:U^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{G}_{a}
is the unique isomorphism satisfying
\lie(\chi_{+})(e_{\alpha^{\vee}})=1
. Put
z:=\alpha^{\vee}(t)
and
y_{+}:=\chi_{+}(u_{+})
. It follows that

\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,y_{+},z^{\pm 1}]/\langle z(1-2hy_{+})-1\rangle\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,y_{+},(1-2hy_{+})^{-1}].

Observe that

t\chi_{+}^{-1}(y)=\chi_{-}^{-1}(\alpha^{\vee}(t)^{-1}y^{-1})\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}(t^{-1}\alpha(-y^{-1}))\chi_{-}^{-1}(y^{-1})

for any
y\in\mathbb{G}_{m}\subset\mathbb{G}_{a}
and
t\in T^{\vee}
. It follows that

\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee}\times_{G^{\vee}}U^{\vee}_{-}\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}B_{-}^{\vee})\simeq\mathbb{Z}[h,y_{+}^{\pm 1},(1-2hy_{+})^{-1}]

and
\overline{q}_{loc}=z^{-1}y_{+}^{-2}=y_{+}^{-2}-2hy_{+}^{-1}
. It is straightforward to show that the homomorphism

\begin{array}[]{ccccc}\Phi_{loc}&:&\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee}\times_{G^{\vee}}U^{\vee}_{-}\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}B_{-}^{\vee})&\rightarrow&QH_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})[q^{-1}]\\[5.0pt]
&&y_{+}&\mapsto&\sigma^{-1}=q^{-1}(\sigma-2h)\end{array}

is an isomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
-algebras. Finally,

\Phi_{loc}(\overline{q}_{loc})=\Phi_{loc}(y_{+}^{-2}-2hy_{+}^{-1})=\sigma^{2}-2h\sigma=q.

Therefore,
\Phi_{loc}
is
\mathbb{Z}[q]
-linear.

Remark 1.1
.

The isomorphisms
\Phi
and
\Phi_{loc}
are in fact intertwined by the homomorphism
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})\rightarrow\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee}\times_{G^{\vee}}U^{\vee}_{-}\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}B_{-}^{\vee})
induced by the morphism
B^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)\rightarrow G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
defined by
(b,h)\mapsto(b^{-1}B_{-}^{\vee},h)
.

1.4.
Organization

In Section
2
, we establish notation and recall facts from equivariant quantum cohomology, affine Grassmannian, geometric Satake equivalence and scheme of Borel subgroups over
\spec\mathbb{Z}
. In Section
3
, we recall Peterson-Lam-Shimozono’s theorem and compute the map with input different from affine Schubert classes, based on our recent new proof of the theorem. In Section
4
, we recall Yun-Zhu’s theorem and a result of Baumann, Kamnitzer and Knuston which describes the isomorphism in terms of Mirković-Vilonen cycles. In Section
5
, we introduce the Peterson schemes and verify several properties. In Section
6
, we prove our main results. In Appendix
A
, we prove a number of lemmas which are used in the preceding sections but not proved immediately after stated. In Appendix
B
, we settle sign issues which arise when we identify specific regular functions on the Peterson schemes with the quantum parameters.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Notation

Let
G
be a complex reductive group. Fix a maximal torus
T
and a Borel subgroup
B
containing
T
. Let
B_{-}
be the unique Borel subgroup such that
B\cap B_{-}=T
. Denote by
R
the set of roots and by
R^{+}
the set of positive roots determined by
B
. The simple roots are denoted by
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}
. For any
\alpha\in R
, denote by
\alpha^{\vee}
the corresponding coroot. Let
W
be the Weyl group and
w_{0}
the longest element of
W
. Let
\mathbb{X}^{\bullet}(T)
(resp.
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
) be the lattice of the characters (resp. cocharacters) of
T
. Let
\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
be the subset of
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
consisting of elements which are dominant. Let
Q^{\vee}
be the coroot lattice. In general,
Q^{\vee}\subseteq\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
, and the equality holds precisely when
G
is simply-connected. Define

2\rho:=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\alpha\in\mathbb{X}^{\bullet}(T)\quad\text{ and }\quad 2\rho^{\vee}:=\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\alpha^{\vee}\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T).

Fix a parabolic subgroup
P
of
G
containing
B
. Let
L
be its Levi subgroup. Denote by
R_{P}\subseteq R
(resp.
R^{+}_{P}\subseteq R^{+}
) the set of (resp. positive) roots of
L
. WLOG, we assume

R_{P}\cap\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}=\{\alpha_{k+1},\ldots,\alpha_{r}\}

for some integer
0\leqslant k\leqslant r
. Let
W_{P}\subseteq W
be the Weyl group of
L
and
w_{P}\in W_{P}
the longest element of
W_{P}
. Define
Q^{\vee}_{P}
to be the sublattice of
Q^{\vee}
generated by
\alpha^{\vee}
with
\alpha\in R_{P}
.

2.2.
Equivariant quantum cohomology of flag variety

Assume
G
is simply-connected. Let
P
be the standard parabolic subgroup of
G
fixed in Section
2.1
. Denote by
W^{P}
the set of minimal length coset representatives in
W/W_{P}
. For each
v\in W^{P}
, define

\displaystyle\sigma_{v}

\displaystyle:=\pd\left[\overline{B_{-}\dot{v}P/P}\right]\in H_{T}^{2\ell(v)}(G/P)

\displaystyle\sigma^{v}

\displaystyle:=\pd\left[\overline{B\dot{v}P/P}\right]\in H_{T}^{\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(G/P)-2\ell(v)}(G/P).

The families
\{\sigma_{v}\}_{v\in W^{P}}
and
\{\sigma^{v}\}_{v\in W^{P}}
are dual bases of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)
with respect to
\int_{G/P}-\cup-
.

Let
\ec\subseteq\pi_{2}(G/P)
be the semi-group of effective curve classes in
G/P
and
\mathbb{Z}[\ec]
the associated group ring. Since
\ec
is identified with
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}\cdot[\alpha^{\vee}_{i}]\subseteq Q^{\vee}/Q^{\vee}_{P}
under the canonical isomorphism
\pi_{2}(G/P)\simeq Q^{\vee}/Q^{\vee}_{P}
, the ring
\mathbb{Z}[\ec]
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
\mathbb{Z}[q_{1},\ldots,q_{k}]
where
q_{i}:=q^{[\alpha^{\vee}_{i}]}
. Define the
T
-equivariant quantum cohomology of
G/P

QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P):=H_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)\otimes\mathbb{Z}[\ec].

We grade
QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P)
by declaring each
q_{i}
to have degree
2\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}\setminus R^{+}_{P}}\alpha(\alpha^{\vee}_{i})
. The
T
-equivariant quantum cup product
\star
is a deformation of the
T
-equivariant cup product, defined by

\sigma_{u}\star\sigma_{v}:=\sum_{w\in W^{P}}\sum_{\beta\in\ec}q^{\beta}\left(\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(G/P,\beta)}\ev_{1}^{*}\sigma_{u}\cup\ev_{2}^{*}\sigma_{v}\cup\ev_{3}^{*}\sigma^{w}\right)\sigma_{w},

where

(i)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(G/P,\beta)
is the moduli of genus-zero stable maps to
G/P
of degree
\beta
which have three marked points in the domain curve and

\ev_{1},\ev_{2},\ev_{3}:\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(G/P,\beta)\rightarrow G/P

are the evaluation morphisms at these marked points; and

(ii)

the integral
\int_{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(G/P,\beta)}
is the
T
-equivariant integral.

It is well-known (e.g.
[
6
,
17
]
) that

(1)

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,3}(G/P,\beta)
is a smooth projective Deligne-Mumford stack of expected dimension. In particular, the above integral is well-defined.

(2)

(QH_{T}^{\bullet}(G/P),\star)
is a graded commutative
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
-algebra.

2.3.
Affine Grassmannian

Let
\mathcal{G}r:=\mathcal{G}r_{G}:=G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})
be the affine Grassmannian of
G
, where
\mathcal{K}:=\mathbb{C}((z))
and
\mathcal{O}:=\mathbb{C}[[z]]
. Every
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
is naturally an element of
G(\mathcal{K})
and so gives rise to a point
t^{\lambda}
of
\mathcal{G}r
. The set
\{t^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)}
is the set of
T
-fixed points of
\mathcal{G}r
. Suppose
\lambda
is dominant. Define
\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}:=G(\mathcal{O})\cdot t^{\lambda}
which is an affine bundle on
G\cdot t^{\lambda}
. Its closure
\overline{\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}}
is called a spherical affine Schubert variety. It is known that
\overline{\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}}=\bigcup_{\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)\ni\mu\leqslant\lambda}\mathcal{G}r^{\mu}
where
\mu\leqslant\lambda
if and only if
\lambda-\mu
is a sum of positive coroots. Because of this equality, we will also denote the closure by
\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}
.

The Cartan decomposition tells us
\mathcal{G}r=\bigcup_{\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)}\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
. We can further decompose each
\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
into smaller strata. Define
\mathcal{B}:=\ev^{-1}_{z=0}(B_{-})
where
\ev_{z=0}:G(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow G
is the evaluation map at
z=0
. For any
\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
not necessarily dominant, the orbit
\mathcal{B}\cdot t^{\mu}
is isomorphic to an affine space and called an affine Schubert cell. Its closure
\overline{\mathcal{B}\cdot t^{\mu}}
is called an affine Schubert variety. We have
\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}=\bigcup_{\mu\in W\cdot\lambda}\mathcal{B}\cdot t^{\mu}
.

Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
such that
T\cap K
is a maximal torus of
K
. It is known that
\mathcal{G}r
is homeomorphic, as
T\cap K
-spaces, to the polynomial based loop group
\Omega K
. The group structure on
\Omega K
thus induces an
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
-algebra structure on
H_{\bullet}^{T\cap K}(\Omega K)\simeq H^{T}_{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
. This product is called the Pontryagin product and denoted by
\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}
. For any

, we have

[t^{\mu_{1}}]\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}[t^{\mu_{2}}]=[t^{\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}}].

2.4.
Geometric Satake equivalence

We give a brief review on the geometric Satake equivalence proved by Mirković and Vilonen
[
37
]
. See also the work
[
7
,
19
,
35
]
. Let
\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
be the category of
G(\mathcal{O})
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\mathcal{G}r
with finite dimensional support. The coefficient ring here is
\mathbb{Z}
. It is an abelian category equipped with a tensor structure given by the convolution product and a fiber functor given by the hypercohomology
H^{\bullet}(-)
. There exists a functorial isomorphism

(2.1)

H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{F})\simeq\bigoplus_{\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)}H^{2\rho(\mu)}_{S_{\mu}^{-}}(\mathcal{F})

for any object
\mathcal{F}
, where

S^{-}_{\mu}:=\left\{y\in\mathcal{G}r\left|~\lim_{s\to\infty}2\rho^{\vee}(s)\cdot y=t^{\mu}\right.\right\}.

Using the information about
\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
, Mirković and Vilonen constructed a group scheme
\widetilde{G}
over
\spec\mathbb{Z}
which contains the dual split torus
T^{\vee}:=\spec\mathbb{Z}[\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)]
as a maximal torus and satisfies

(2.2)

\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)\simeq\rep(\widetilde{G})

as tensor categories, where
\rep(\widetilde{G})
is the category of representations of
\widetilde{G}
which are finitely generated over
\mathbb{Z}
. Moreover,
H^{\bullet}(-)
(resp.
H_{S^{-}_{\mu}}^{2\rho(\mu)}(-)
) corresponds to the forgetful functor (resp.
\mu
-weight functor). They showed that
\widetilde{G}
is split reductive and the root datum associated to
(\widetilde{G},T^{\vee})
is dual to that associated to
(G,T)
, and hence
\widetilde{G}\simeq G^{\vee}
, the Langlands dual group scheme of
G
.

We will consider a particular class of objects of
\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
called costandard sheaves. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
, define

(2.3)

\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}:={}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{0}((j_{\lambda})_{*}\mathbb{Z}[2\rho(\lambda)])

where
{}^{p}\mathcal{H}^{0}
is the zeroth perverse cohomology functor and
j_{\lambda}:\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}
is the inclusion map.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
37
, Proposition 3.10]
) For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
and
\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
, there exists an isomorphism between
H^{2\rho(\mu)}_{S^{-}_{\mu}}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda})
and the free abelian group generated by the irreducible components of
S^{-}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
.
\square

2.5.
Scheme of Borel subgroups

The main reference for this subsection is
[

Definition 2.2

Let

For any

For any

The following arguments can be found in

Consider the Weyl group of

Lemma 2.4
.

For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
and

Lemma 2.5
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cdot 2\rho^{\vee}
such that
\mathcal{L}(\lambda_{0})
is very ample.

Proof.

Put
X:=G^{\vee}/B^{\vee}_{-}
and let
f:G^{\vee}\rightarrow X
be the quotient morphism. By
[
16
, Remark 2.3.7 & Theorem 5.2.11]
, the determinant
\det(\mathcal{T}_{X})
of the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}_{X}:=\mathcal{T}_{X/\spec\mathbb{Z}}
is ample. Hence it suffices to show
\det(\mathcal{T}_{X})\simeq\mathcal{L}(2\rho^{\vee})
. By descent, it amounts to showing

f^{*}\det(\mathcal{T}_{X})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{G^{\vee}}\otimes M_{2\rho^{\vee}}

as
B_{-}^{\vee}
-equivariant sheaves.

Since
f
is smooth, we have a short exact sequence

(2.8)

0\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{G^{\vee}/X}\rightarrow\mathcal{T}_{G^{\vee}}\rightarrow f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X}\rightarrow 0.

Let us determine the second arrow. Recall it is induced by the canonical morphism

(2.9)

G^{\vee}\times_{X}G^{\vee}\rightarrow G^{\vee}\times G^{\vee}.

Observe that
(g_{1},g_{2})\mapsto(g_{1},g^{-1}_{1}g_{2})
gives rise to isomorphisms

G^{\vee}\times_{X}G^{\vee}\simeq G^{\vee}\times B_{-}^{\vee}\quad\text{ and }\quad G^{\vee}\times G^{\vee}\simeq G^{\vee}\times G^{\vee}

under which (

) is identified with

where
\iota:B_{-}^{\vee}\hookrightarrow G^{\vee}
is the inclusion, and the diagonal morphisms become
g\mapsto(g,e)
. It follows that the second arrow in (

\mathcal{O}_{G^{\vee}}\otimes\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{G^{\vee}}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
induced by the inclusion
\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
, and hence

f^{*}\det(\mathcal{T}_{X})\simeq\det(f^{*}\mathcal{T}_{X})\simeq\det(\mathcal{O}_{G^{\vee}}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{G^{\vee}}\otimes\det(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}).

The result will follow if we can show that

as

-modules. It is clear that they are isomorphic as

-modules. By definition,
M_{2\rho^{\vee}}
is a trivial
U^{\vee}_{-}
-module. The same holds for
\det(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-})
as well because every graded piece associated to the

-filtration on
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}
is a trivial
U^{\vee}_{-}
-module. The proof is complete.
∎

T
-fixed point
\dot{v}P\in G/P
, the degree of the corresponding section is equal to
[v^{-1}(\mu)]
. The rest is clear.
∎

Proposition 3.9
.

For any dominant
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
,
\Phi_{PLS}
vanishes on
H^{T}_{>2\rho(\lambda-w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda))}(\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda})
.

Proof.

Let
y\in H^{T}_{>2\rho(\lambda-w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda))}(\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda})
. We may assume
y
is an affine Schubert class
\left[~\overline{\mathcal{B}\cdot t^{\mu}}~\right]
for some
\mu\in W\cdot\lambda
. (If not, replace
\lambda
by another
\lambda^{\prime}
with
\lambda^{\prime}<\lambda
.) Then
y=(r_{\lambda})_{*}x
for some
x
so that we can apply Lemma
3.7
. By Lemma
3.3
, for any
v\in W^{P}
and
\eta\in Q^{\vee}/Q^{\vee}_{P}
, the homology degree of
(\Pi_{r_{\lambda},\eta})_{*}\ev_{r_{\lambda},\eta}^{*}\sigma^{v}
is greater than or equal to
4\rho(\lambda)+2\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}\setminus R^{+}_{P}}\alpha(\eta)
which is greater than or equal to
4\rho(\lambda)+2\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}\setminus R^{+}_{P}}\alpha(w_{0}(\lambda))
if
\overline{\mathcal{M}}(r_{\lambda},\eta)\neq\emptyset
, by Lemma
3.8
. The last expression is equal to
4\rho(\lambda)-2\rho(\lambda-w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda))
. It follows that

\deg\left((\Pi_{r_{\lambda},\eta})_{*}\ev_{r_{\lambda},\eta}^{*}\sigma^{v}\right)+\deg x>4\rho(\lambda)=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}\Gamma_{\lambda},

and hence the intersection product in (
3.1
) is zero. The result now follows from Theorem
3.5
.
∎

Lemma 3.10
.

For any dominant
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
, the restriction of
\Pi_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}
to
\ev_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}^{-1}(\dot{e}P)
is an isomorphism onto
r_{\lambda}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)})
where
L
is the Levi subgroup of
P
and

\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}:=\overline{L(\mathcal{O})\cdot t^{w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}.

Proof.

Observe that the structure group of the
G
-torsor
\mathcal{E}^{G}_{\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}
is reduced to
L
. In fact, the associated
L
-torsor is nothing but
\mathcal{E}^{L}_{\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}
. Hence

\left.\mathcal{E}^{G}_{\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}}(G/P)\right|_{\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}\simeq\mathcal{E}^{L}_{\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}(G/P).

The
L
-equivariant morphism
\{\dot{e}P\}\hookrightarrow G/P
induces a morphism

(3.2)

\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}\simeq\mathcal{E}^{L}_{\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}(\{\dot{e}P\})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{L}_{\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}}(G/P).

In other words, every point in
\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}
gives rise to a section of
\mathcal{E}^{G}_{\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}}(G/P)
lying over the same point. It is not difficult to see that the degrees of these sections are equal to
[w_{0}(\lambda)]
. Thus, (
3.2
) defines a closed immersion

(3.3)

r_{\lambda}^{-1}(\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)})\hookrightarrow\ev^{-1}_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}(\dot{e}P).

By a dimension argument and the smoothness of
\ev^{-1}_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}(\dot{e}P)
, the image of (
3.3
) is a connected component of
\ev^{-1}_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}(\dot{e}P)
.

It remains to show that
\ev^{-1}_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}(\dot{e}P)
has no other connected component. Every other component has a
T
-fixed point, and by Lemma
3.8
every
T
-fixed point is represented by a
T
-invariant section of
\mathcal{E}^{G}_{t^{\mu}}(G/P)
for some
\mu\in Q^{\vee}\cap\conv(W\cdot\lambda)
. Since the marked point on this section (over
\infty
) is required to hit
\dot{e}P\in G/P
, its degree is equal to
[\mu]
. It follows that
[\mu]=[w_{0}(\lambda)]
, and hence
\mu\in Q^{\vee}\cap\conv(W_{P}\cdot w_{0}(\lambda))
or equivalently
t^{\mu}\in\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}
. Thus, this section, viewed as a point of the component of
\ev^{-1}_{r_{\lambda},[w_{0}(\lambda)]}(\dot{e}P)
we are looking at, belongs to the component we have already determined, a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 3.11
.

Let
\lambda\in Q^{\vee}
be dominant. For any
x\in H^{T}_{2\rho(\lambda-w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda))}(\Gamma_{\lambda})
, we have

\Phi_{PLS}((r_{\lambda})_{*}x)=q^{[w_{0}(\lambda)]}x\bullet\left[r_{\lambda}^{-1}\left(\mathcal{G}r_{L}^{\leqslant w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}\right)\right].

Proof.

By the degree argument in the proof of Proposition
3.9
, the summation (
3.1
) contains only one possibly non-zero summand, namely the term corresponding to
v=e
and
\eta=[w_{0}(\lambda)]
. The assertion then follows from Theorem
3.5
, Lemma
3.7
and Lemma
3.10
.
∎

4.
Yun-Zhu’s theorem

4.1.
Statement

Recall the notation introduced in Section
2.4
. Assume
G
is simple.

Consider the
T
-equivariant hypercohomology functor
H^{\bullet}_{T}(-)
on
\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
. By
[
49
, Lemma 2.2 & Lemma 2.4]
,
H^{\bullet}_{T}(-)\simeq H^{\bullet}(-)\otimes H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
as tensor functors. Define a natural transformation
\sigma_{can}
from the functor
H^{\bullet}_{T}(-)\otimes_{H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)}H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
to itself

\sigma_{can}(v\otimes h):=\sum_{i}(h^{i}\cup v)\otimes(h_{i}\mathchoice{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\displaystyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\textstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}{\mathbin{\vbox{\hbox{\scalebox{.5}{$\scriptscriptstyle\bullet$}}}}}h)

for any
v\in H^{\bullet}_{T}(\mathcal{F})
with
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
and
h\in H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
, where
\{h_{i}\}
and
\{h^{i}\}
are dual bases of
H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
and
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)
respectively. By
[
49
, Lemma 3.1]
,
\sigma_{can}
is a tensor automorphism, and hence it induces, via the Tannakian formalism, a homomorphism of
H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
-algebras

\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}:\mathcal{O}(G^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt))\rightarrow H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)

satisfying the property that for any
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{P}_{G(\mathcal{O})}(\mathcal{G}r)
,
v\in H^{\bullet}_{T}(\mathcal{F})
and
v^{*}\in H^{\bullet}_{T}(\mathcal{F})^{*}
,

(4.1)

\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}(\varphi(\mathcal{F},v,v^{*}))=\sum_{i}\langle v^{*},h^{i}\cup v\rangle h_{i}

where
\varphi(\mathcal{F},v,v^{*})\in\mathcal{O}(G^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt))
is defined by

\varphi(\mathcal{F},v,v^{*})(g,h):=\langle v^{*},g\cdot v\rangle.

\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}
is graded if we grade
\mathcal{O}(G^{\vee})
using the conjugate action of
2\rho:\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow T^{\vee}
. Moreover, by showing that
\sigma_{can}
preserves the weight filtration induced by the
T
-equivariant analogue of the decomposition (
2.1
),
\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}
factors through
\mathcal{O}(B^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt))
.

Consider an element
c\in H^{2}_{T}(\mathcal{G}r;\mathbb{Q})
which is the unique primitive
T
-equivariant lift of the positive generator of
H^{2}(\mathcal{G}r;\mathbb{Z})
. By the facts that
c
is primitive and
\sigma_{can}
commutes with the cup product
c\cup-
,
\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}
further factors through the coordinate ring
\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee})
of the centralizer group scheme in
B^{\vee}\times\spec H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
of an element
e^{T}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)\otimes\mathbb{Q}
which is induced by the natural transformation

c\cup-:H^{\bullet}_{T}(-)\rightarrow H^{\bullet+2}_{T}(-)

via the Tannakian formalism. By
[
49
, Proposition 5.6 & Proposition 5.7]
,

(4.2)

e^{T}=e+f

where

e:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\quad\text{ and }\quad f:=2\frac{\langle-,-\rangle_{Kil}}{\langle\alpha^{\vee}_{0},\alpha^{\vee}_{0}\rangle_{Kil}}.

Here,

(1)

|\cdot|
is a
W
-invariant norm on
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)_{\mathbb{R}}
, normalized such that
|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}=1
if
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is short;

(2)

e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}
is a generator of
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
; and

(3)

\langle-,-\rangle_{Kil}
is the Killing form regarded as an element of
\mathbb{X}^{\bullet}(T)\otimes\mathbb{X}^{\bullet}(T)\subset\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}\otimes H_{T}^{\bullet}(\pt)
.

Observe that
f
, regarded as an element of
\home_{\mathbb{Q}}(\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}},\mathbb{X}^{\bullet}(T)_{\mathbb{Q}})
, sends each
\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
to
|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{2}\alpha_{i}
. Suppose
G
is simply-connected. Then
\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)=Q^{\vee}
. Since
Q^{\vee}
is generated by the simple coroots over
\mathbb{Z}
, we conclude that
f
is defined over
\mathbb{Z}
.

Theorem 4.1
.

(
[
49
, Theorem 6.1]
)
Assume
G
is simple and simply-connected. The homomorphism

\Phi_{YZ}:\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee})\rightarrow H^{T}_{-\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)

induced by
\widetilde{\Phi}_{YZ}
is bijective after inverting
\ell_{G}\in\mathbb{Z}
, the square of the ratio of the lengths of the long and short coroots of
G
.
\square

Remark 4.2
.

In
[
8
]
, Bezrukavnikov, Finkelberg and Mirković constructed the same isomorphism for the case where the coefficient ring is
\mathbb{C}
. Their method does not rely on the geometric Satake equivalence.

4.2.
Description in terms of Mirković-Vilonen cycles

We describe Yun-Zhu’s isomorphism
\Phi_{YZ}
from Theorem
4.1
in terms of Mirković-Vilonen cycles (MV cycles) in
\mathcal{G}r
. When the coefficient ring is
\mathbb{C}
, this was done in
[
3
, Section 10.2]
. The integral case is similar. For the convenience of the reader, we provide the details.

Let
\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
. Recall

S^{-}_{\mu}:=\left\{y\in\mathcal{G}r\left|~\lim_{s\to\infty}2\rho^{\vee}(s)\cdot y=t^{\mu}\right.\right\}.

It is a subset of
\mathcal{G}r
whose dimension and codimension are both infinite.

Lemma 4.3
.

(
[
37
, Proposition 3.1]
)

\overline{S^{-}_{\mu}}=\bigcup_{\nu\geqslant\mu}S_{\nu}^{-}
.
\square

Lemma 4.4
.

(
[
37
, Theorem 3.2]
)
Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
and
\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
. Every irreducible component of
S^{-}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
has dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
.
\square

Definition 4.5
.

The closure of any irreducible component of
S^{-}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
is called an MV cycle of type
\lambda
and weight
\mu
.

The following lemma is well-known. We provide the proof for the convenience of the reader.

Lemma 4.6
.

The following two sets are equal:

(1)

The set of MV cycles of type
\lambda
and weight
\mu
.

(2)

The set of irreducible components of
\overline{S^{-}_{\mu}}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}
which have dimension
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
.

Proof.

Denote by
A
and
B
the sets in (1) and (2) respectively. By
\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}=\bigcup_{\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)\ni\nu\leqslant\lambda}\mathcal{G}r^{\nu}
, Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.4
, the dimension of every irreducible component of
\overline{S^{-}_{\mu}}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}
is less than or equal to
\rho(\lambda-\mu)
. This shows
A\subseteq B
. Conversely, let
Z\in B
. Put
Z^{\prime}:=Z\cap S^{-}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
. By the same reasoning,
Z^{\prime}
is a non-empty open dense subset of
Z
. It follows that
Z^{\prime}
is irreducible, and hence it is contained in an irreducible component
Z^{\prime\prime}
of
S^{-}_{\mu}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}
. By Lemma
4.4
again, we have
Z=\overline{Z^{\prime\prime}}
, and hence
Z\in A
.
∎

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
. Consider the costandard sheaf
\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}
defined in (
2.3
). Put
S(\lambda):=H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda})
. Let
v_{\lambda}^{*}\in S(\lambda)^{*}
be the element from Definition
2.2
. For any
v\in S(\lambda)
, define
\theta(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda},v,v_{\lambda}^{*})\in\mathcal{O}(B_{e^{T}}^{\vee})
by

\theta(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda},v,v_{\lambda}^{*})(b,h):=\langle v_{\lambda}^{*},b\cdot v\rangle.

Proposition 4.7
.

(C.f.
[
3
, Lemma 10.5]
) Let
\mu\in\mathbb{X}_{\bullet}(T)
and
v_{Z}\in S(\lambda)_{\mu}
be the element corresponding to an MV cycle
Z
via the isomorphism from Lemma
2.1
. We have

\Phi_{YZ}(\theta(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda},v_{Z},v_{\lambda}^{*}))=[Z]\in H^{T}_{2\rho(\lambda-\mu)}(\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda};\mathbb{Z}).

Proof.

For a space
X
, denote by
\mathbb{D}_{X}
its dualizing complex. Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the Verdier dual functor. By the perversity condition on
\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda})
and excision, we have

(4.3)

H_{T}^{-2\rho(\lambda)}(\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}))\simeq H_{T}^{-2\rho(\lambda)}(\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda};\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{\lambda})|_{\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda}})\simeq H_{T}^{0}(\mathcal{G}r^{\lambda};\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}.

By duality, the element of the LHS of (
4.3
) corresponding to
1\in\mathbb{Z}
induces a morphism
f_{\lambda}:\mathcal{J}_{\lambda}\rightarrow\mathbb{D}_{\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}}[-2\rho(\lambda)]
in the
T
-equivariant derived category.

Let
\iota:\overline{S_{\mu}^{-}}\cap\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{G}r^{\leqslant\lambda}
denote the inclusion. The morphism
f_{\lambda}
and the counit
\cu:\iota_{!}\iota^{!}\rightarrow\id
give rise to the commutative diagram

Lemma 5.5
.

Suppose
\Bbbk
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic not dividing
\ell_{G}
. Then

|\mathcal{Y}|_{\Bbbk}=\bigcup_{Q}|\mathcal{Y}_{Q}|_{\Bbbk}

where
|\cdot|_{\Bbbk}
denotes the set of
\Bbbk
-points and
Q
runs over all standard parabolic subgroups of
G
.

Proof.

By flowing every point of
\mathcal{Y}
to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-fixed point and the Bruhat decomposition, it suffices to show

(\dot{w}B_{-}^{\vee},0)\in\mathcal{Y}\implies w=w_{Q}w_{0}\text{ for some }Q.

By the definition of
\mathcal{Y}
,
(\dot{w}B_{-}^{\vee},0)\in\mathcal{Y}
implies that for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
,
w^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is either negative or equal to a simple coroot. Denote by
I_{Q}
the set of
i\in I:=\{1,\ldots,r\}
for which the second case holds, and by
Q
the associated standard parabolic subgroup. Define
w^{\prime}:=w_{0}w^{-1}w_{Q}
. We have to show
w^{\prime}=e
. It suffices to show that
w^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is positive for any
i\in I
.

Case 1:
i\in I_{Q}
.
We have
w_{Q}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}=-\alpha^{\vee}_{j}
for some
j\in I_{Q}
, and hence
w^{-1}w_{Q}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is equal to minus a simple coroot, and hence
w^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is positive.

Case 2:
i\in I\setminus I_{Q}
.
Suppose to the contrary that
w^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is negative. Write
w_{Q}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}=\sum_{j\in I}c_{j}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}
. Then
c_{j}\geqslant 0
for each

and is non-zero for some
j_{0}\in I\setminus I_{Q}
. Since
w^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is negative, the sum
\sum_{j\in I}c_{j}w_{0}w^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}=w^{\prime}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}
is a negative coroot. By the definition of
I_{Q}
,
w_{0}w^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}
is positive (resp. minus a simple coroot) for
j\in I\setminus I_{Q}
(resp.

). It follows that
w_{0}w^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j_{0}}
lies in the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of
w_{0}w^{-1}\alpha^{\vee}_{j}
with

, or equivalently,
\alpha^{\vee}_{j_{0}}
lies in the
\mathbb{Z}
-span of
\alpha^{\vee}_{j}
for these

, a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 5.6
.

Suppose
\Bbbk
is an algebraically closed field of characteristic not dividing
\ell_{G}
. The fiber

of
\mathcal{Y}_{P}
over

\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee}_{-}:=\lie(\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-})=\mathfrak{b}^{\vee}_{-}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in-w_{0}R^{+}_{P}}\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}}.

\{(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee},0)\}

Proof.

We have
\mathcal{T}_{G^{\vee}/\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}}\simeq G^{\vee}\times_{\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}/\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee}_{-})
so the morphism
g\mapsto g^{-1}\cdot e^{T}(0)\bmod\overline{\mathfrak{p}}^{\vee}_{-}
defines a section

. Observe that
U^{\vee}_{P^{\vee}_{-}}\simeq U^{\vee}_{P^{\vee}_{-}}\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}/\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}
is an open subscheme of
G^{\vee}/\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}
. We first show that
(\mathcal{Y}_{P})_{x}
is isomorphic to the zero locus of the restriction of

to this subscheme. The latter scheme is by definition the closed subscheme of

cut by the polynomials
\langle e^{*}_{\alpha^{\vee}},(u\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}\cdot e^{T}(0)\rangle
for

which are part of the defining polynomials of

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that the set of closed points of the zero locus of
s_{e^{T}(0)}
is equal to
\{\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}\}
. Observe that the former set is

-invariant. Hence it suffices to show that
\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}
is the only
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-fixed point of

at which

vanishes. Clearly,

vanishes at
\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}
. Pick a point

in the dominant chamber such that
\alpha_{i}(b)=0
if
\alpha_{i}\in-w_{0}R^{+}_{P}
and
\alpha_{i}(b)>0
otherwise. Let

. If
w\not\in w_{P}w_{0}W_{\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}}=w_{0}W_{\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-}}

is away from the anti-dominant chamber. It follows that there exists

such that
\alpha_{i}(w(b))>0
. This implies

, and hence
s_{e^{T}(0)}(\dot{w}\overline{P}^{\vee}_{-})\neq 0

∎

Proof.

It suffices to verify the flatness at every closed point of
\mathcal{Y}_{P}

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{\spec S,x}
is regular;

(2)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{P},y}
is Cohen-Macaulay; and

(3)

\dim\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{Y}_{P},y}=\dim_{y}(\mathcal{Y}_{P})_{x}+\dim\mathcal{O}_{\spec S,x}
(in general LHS

(1) is obvious. Observe that
\mathcal{Y}_{P}
is a closed subscheme of

(

\langle e^{*}_{\alpha^{\vee}},(u\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})^{-1}\cdot e^{T}(h)\rangle

To complete the proof, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{Y}_{P}

\mathcal{Y}_{P}

\spec S

\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{\prime}

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}_{P}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset

\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{\prime}

\mathbb{G}_{m}

\overline{\mathcal{Y}}^{\prime}_{P}\setminus\mathcal{Y}_{P}^{\prime}\subseteq\left(\overline{U^{\vee}_{P^{\vee}_{-}}\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}}\setminus U^{\vee}_{P^{\vee}_{-}}\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}B_{-}^{\vee}/B_{-}^{\vee}\right)\times\spec S

via the isomorphism in Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
and
Z
be an MV cycle of type
\lambda
and weight
\mu
. Consider the
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
-action on
S(\lambda)
. Write

e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\cdot v_{Z}=\sum_{Z^{\prime}}c_{Z^{\prime}}v_{Z^{\prime}}

where each
c_{Z^{\prime}}\in\mathbb{Z}
and the sum is taken over all MV cycles
Z^{\prime}
of type
\lambda
. Then
c_{Z^{\prime}}\geqslant 0
for all
Z^{\prime}
.

Proof.

We may assume the coefficient ring is
\mathbb{C}
. The result follows immediately from
[
3
, Theorem 5.4]
which says that each
Z^{\prime}
for which
c_{Z^{\prime}}\neq 0
must be an irreducible component of the support of a Cartier divisor
D
on
Z
, and in this case
c_{Z^{\prime}}
is equal to
|\alpha^{\vee}_{i}|^{-2}
times the intersection multiplicity of
Z^{\prime}
along
D
.
∎

We now construct specific
\dot{w}_{0}
and
\dot{w}_{P}
. The following approach is standard. Let
\alpha\in R
. By
[
16
, Theorem 4.1.4]
, there is a unique homomorphism

\exp_{\alpha^{\vee}}:\spec\sym^{\bullet}(\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}})^{*}\rightarrow G^{\vee}

of group schemes such that
\lie(\exp_{\alpha^{\vee}})
is the inclusion
\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}}\hookrightarrow\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}
. By
[
16
, Theorem 4.2.6 & Corollary 5.1.12]
, there is a perfect pairing

\beta_{\alpha^{\vee}}:\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{-\alpha^{\vee}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}

satisfying

[X,Y]=\beta_{\alpha^{\vee}}(X,Y)H_{\alpha^{\vee}}

for any
X\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{\alpha^{\vee}}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{-\alpha^{\vee}}
, where
H_{\alpha^{\vee}}:=\lie(\alpha)(1)\in\lie(T^{\vee})
. For each
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, denote by
e^{-1}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\in\mathfrak{g}^{\vee}_{-\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}
the unique element such that
\beta_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}(e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}},e^{-1}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}})=1
.

Definition B.2
.

(1)

Let
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
. Define

\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}:=\exp_{-\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}(-e^{-1}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}})\exp_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}(e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}})\exp_{-\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}(-e^{-1}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}).

(2)

Let
w\in W
. Define

\dot{w}:=\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{1}}}\cdots\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{\ell(w)}}}

where
w=s_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\alpha_{i_{\ell(w)}}}
is a reduced word decomposition of
w
.

Lemma B.3
.

Each
\dot{w}
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-point of the normalizer
N_{G^{\vee}}(T^{\vee})
and represents
w
in the quotient
N_{G^{\vee}}(T^{\vee})/T^{\vee}\simeq W
. It is independent of the choice of the reduced word decomposition of
w
. In particular, if
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
satisfy
\ell(w_{1}w_{2})=\ell(w_{1})+\ell(w_{2})
, then
\dot{(w_{1}w_{2})}=\dot{w}_{1}\dot{w}_{2}
.

Proof.

The first assertion follows from
[
16
, Corollary 5.1.9]
. For any two reduced word decompositions of
w
, the corresponding two
\dot{w}
are related by a
\mathbb{Z}
-point of
T^{\vee}
which must be the unit by Lemma
B.5
below. This proves the second assertion. The last assertion follows from the second assertion.
∎

Lemma B.4
.

We have

\dot{w}_{0}^{2}=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}}\alpha\right)(-1)\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{w}_{P}^{2}=\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R^{+}_{P}}\alpha\right)(-1).

In particular,
\dot{w}^{2}_{0}=e
if
G^{\vee}
is of adjoint type.

Proof.

Let
w_{0}=s_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}\cdots s_{\alpha_{i_{\ell(w_{0})}}}
be a reduced word decomposition of
w_{0}
. Then
w_{0}=w_{0}^{-1}=s_{\alpha_{i_{\ell(w_{0})}}}\cdots s_{\alpha_{i_{1}}}
is also a reduced word decomposition of the same element. By Lemma
B.3
,

\dot{w}_{0}^{2}=\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{1}}}\cdots\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{\ell(w_{0})}}}\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{\ell(w_{0})}}}\cdots\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i_{1}}}.

The first equality then follows from the identity
\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}^{2}=\alpha_{i}(-1)
(see
[
16
, Corollary 5.1.11]
) and a straightforward computation. The second equality is proved similarly.
∎

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
and
w\in W
. Denote by
v_{w}\in S(\lambda)_{w(\lambda)}\simeq\mathbb{Z}
the generator corresponding to the unique MV cycle of type
\lambda
and weight
w(\lambda)
via the isomorphism in Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma B.5
.

We have
\dot{w}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=v_{ww_{0}}\in S(\lambda)_{ww_{0}(\lambda)}
.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\cdot v_{u}=v_{s_{\alpha_{i}}u}
for any
1\leqslant i\leqslant r
and
u\in W
such that
\ell(s_{\alpha_{i}}u)=\ell(u)-1
. Clearly, we have
\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}\cdot v_{u}=\pm v_{s_{\alpha_{i}}u}
. To determine the sign, we may work over
\mathbb{C}
so that we can expand the exponential maps involved in
\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}
(see Definition
B.2
). But by the assumption
\ell(s_{\alpha_{i}}u)=\ell(u)-1
, only the middle factor
\exp_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}(e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{i}})
contributes to the sign, and hence the sign is positive by Lemma
B.1
.
∎

Finally, the following propositions will settle the sign issues occurring in the proofs of Lemma
5.2
and Lemma
6.9
.

Proposition B.6
.

For any
k+1\leqslant i\leqslant r
, we have

(B.1)

\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot e_{-w_{0}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}=e_{-w_{P}\alpha^{\vee}_{i}}.

Proof.

Clearly, both sides of (
B.1
) are equal up to sign so it suffices to show that the sign is
+1
. We may assume
G^{\vee}
is simply-connected so that we can consider the representation
S(\omega^{\vee}_{i})
where
\omega^{\vee}_{i}
is the fundamental coweight corresponding to the simple root
\alpha_{i}
. Put
\alpha_{j}:=-w_{0}\alpha_{i}
. We have

e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}}\cdot v_{w_{0}}\in S(\omega_{i}^{\vee})_{w_{0}(\omega_{i}^{\vee})+\alpha^{\vee}_{j}},

and hence

(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot e_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}})\cdot(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{w_{0}})=\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}}\cdot v_{w_{0}}.

Write

\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=m_{1}v_{w_{P}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=m_{2}v_{w_{P}w_{0}s_{\alpha_{j}}w_{0}}

where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\{\pm 1\}
. By Lemma
B.1
, the result will follow if we can show
m_{1}=m_{2}
. Let
u
denote
e
or
s_{\alpha_{j}}
and put
u^{\prime}:=w_{P}w_{0}u
. By Lemma
B.3
, we have

\dot{(u^{\prime})}=\dot{(w_{P}w_{0})}\dot{u}=\dot{w}_{P}^{-1}\dot{w}_{0}\dot{u}=\dot{w}_{P}^{-2}(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\dot{u}).

By Lemma
B.5
, we have
v_{u^{\prime}w_{0}}=\dot{(u^{\prime})}\cdot v_{w_{0}}
. It follows that

v_{w_{P}}=\dot{w}_{P}^{-2}(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0})\cdot v_{w_{0}}\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{w_{P}w_{0}s_{\alpha_{j}}w_{0}}=\dot{w}_{P}^{-2}(\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\dot{s}_{\alpha^{\vee}_{j}})\cdot v_{w_{0}},

and hence, by Lemma
B.4
,

m_{1}/m_{2}=(-1)^{\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R_{P}^{+}}\alpha\right)[w_{P}(\omega^{\vee}_{i})-w_{P}s_{\alpha_{i}}(\omega^{\vee}_{i})]}=(-1)^{\left(\sum_{\alpha\in R_{P}^{+}}\alpha\right)(w_{P}(\alpha^{\vee}_{i}))}=(-1)^{-2}=1.

∎

Proposition B.7
.

Suppose
G^{\vee}
is of adjoint type. For any
\lambda\in\mathbb{X}^{+}_{\bullet}(T)
, we have

v_{w_{P}w_{0}}=\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{e}\in S(\lambda)_{w_{P}w_{0}(\lambda)}.

Proof.

By Lemma
B.5
,

v_{w_{P}w_{0}}=\dot{w}_{P}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\dot{w}_{0}\cdot v_{w_{0}}=\dot{w}_{P}\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}\cdot v_{e}.

Since
G^{\vee}
is of adjoint type, we have, by Lemma
B.4
,
\dot{w}_{0}^{-1}=\dot{w}_{0}
. The result follows.
∎

Linear Layouts of Complete Graphs
Stefan Felsner, Laura Merker, Torsten Ueckerdt, Pavel Valtr

Abstract
A page ( queue ) with respect to a vertex ordering of a graph is a set of edges such that no two edges cross (nest), i.e., have their endpoints ordered in an abab -pattern ( abba -pattern). A union page ( union queue ) is a vertex-disjoint union of pages (queues). The union page number ( union queue number ) of a graph is the smallest k k such that there is a vertex ordering and a partition of the edges into k k union pages (union queues). The local page number ( local queue number ) is the smallest k k for which there is a vertex ordering and a partition of the edges into pages (queues) such that each vertex has incident edges in at most k k pages (queues). We present upper and lower bounds on these four parameters for the complete graph K n K_{n} on n n vertices. In three cases we obtain the exact result up to an additive constant. In particular, the local page number of K n K_{n} is n / 3 ± 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) n/3\pm\mathcal{O}(1) , while its local and union queue number is ( 1 − 1 / 2 ) ​ n ± 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) (1-1/\sqrt{2})n\pm\mathcal{O}(1) . The union page number of K n K_{n} is between n / 3 − 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) n/3-\mathcal{O}(1) and 4 ​ n / 9 + 𝒪 ⁡ ( 1 ) 4n/9+\mathcal{O}(1) .

Both queue layouts and book embeddings were intensively investigated in the past decades, where complete graphs are one of the very first considered graph classes

Queue layouts and book embeddings model how the edges of a graph can be assigned to and processed by queues, respectively stacks.
It was first asked by Heath, Leighton, and Rosenberg

]
whether queues or stacks are more powerful in this context.
Recently, Dujmović et al.
[

partly answered this question by presenting a class of graphs with bounded queue number that needs an unbounded number of stacks, showing that stacks are not more powerful than queues for representing graphs.
In the classical variant in this question, the total number of necessary queues or stacks serves as a measure for the power of queues and stacks.
Local and union variants relax the setting by allowing more queues or stacks, as long as each vertex is touched by a small number of queues, respectively stacks, or if they operate on vertex-disjoint subgraphs.

Local and union variants of queue layouts and book embeddings were recently introduced by the second and third author

Local and union variants have been considered for numerous graph decomposition parameter, such as boxicity, interval numbers, planar thickness, poset dimension, several arboricities, and many more.

In this paper, we continue the investigation of local and union variants of linear layouts, that is, queue numbers and page numbers.

\displaystyle\,\{(\,x\,,k+1-\gamma)\in T_{n}\mid x\leqslant n-3k+2-\gamma\}

\displaystyle\cup

\displaystyle\,\{\begin{aligned} (\,x,y\,)\in T_{n}\mid&\ y-x\leqslant 4k-n-1\text{ and }x-y\leqslant 4k-n-1\text{ and }\\

.

\displaystyle\leqslant 7k-2n-1

\displaystyle(1+1/\sqrt{2})n+(2-1/\sqrt{2})

\displaystyle\leqslant(3+2\sqrt{2})k

\displaystyle\kern 4.0pt\Longleftarrow

\displaystyle(1+1/\sqrt{2})n+(2-1/\sqrt{2})

4
Local and Union Page Numbers

For book embeddings, it is convenient to think of the spine as being
circularly closed. The placement of the vertices together with straight-line
edges yields a convex drawing of
K_{n}
. A page assignment is a partition of
the edges into non-crossing subsets, i.e., into outerplanar subdrawings of
this drawing of
K_{n}
.

First, we analyze the outerplanar subgraphs on each page of a book embedding and thereby show the lower bound of
Theorem

1.2
.
The proof gives insight into how book embeddings for a matching upper bound on the local or union page number should look like.
This bound is also the best lower bound we obtain for the union page number.
We then prove upper bounds both on the local and union page number.

Lemma 6

For any
n
we have

\displaystyle\lpn(K_{n})>\frac{1}{3}n-1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a page assignment of

which minimizes the

\lpn(\mathcal{P})\leqslant n/3
,
otherwise we are done.
Let

. We shall show that

, which in particular proves that

\lpn(\mathcal{P})>n/3-1
. Later we will
use that

k\leqslant\frac{n}{3}.

(3)

Note that every edge of
K_{n}
belongs to exactly one page of

\mathcal{P}
. Now for each page

we consider an outerplanar

consisting of all edges of

of

•

•

are inner edges of

Observe that every edge

of

for exactly one page, while

may be red for any number of

For a vertex

containing

forward edge

\fwdP(v)
at

be the edge of

which leaves

in clockwise direction. Let

be the number of pages for which the forward edge of

be the

(4)

Now for each page
P
and each edge

of

with
u

clockwise followed by

, let

be the distance along

when going clockwise from

to
v

and

and

.
Since

is a cycle, we have
\sum_{e\in C_{P}}\len(e)=n
. Thus

\displaystyle|\mathcal{P}|

\displaystyle\cdot n=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\Big(\sum_{e\in C_{P}}\len(e)\Big)=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\Big(\sum_{v\in V_{P}}\len(\fwdP(v))\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{v}\Big(\sum_{P:v\in V_{P}}\len(\fwdP(v))\Big)\overset{(\diamond)}{\geqslant}\sum_{v}\Big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{b_{v}}\ell\Big)\geqslant\sum_{v}\frac{b_{v}^{2}}{2}\overset{(\ast)}{\geqslant}\frac{1}{2n}\Big(\sum_{v}b_{v}\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2n}B^{2}\overset{\eqref{eq:kn=R+B}}{=}\frac{1}{2n}(kn-R)^{2}\geqslant\frac{1}{2n}(k^{2}n^{2}-2knR)=\frac{k^{2}}{2}n-kR\overset{\eqref{eq:k-leqn/3}}{\geqslant}\frac{k^{2}}{2}n-\frac{n}{3}R.

For

ignore red forward edges at

and use that the black
forward edges at

are pairwise distinct and for
(\ast)
use the
Cauchy-Schwarz inequality with the vectors
(b_{v_{1}},\ldots,b_{v_{n}})
and

(1,\ldots,1)

Dividing both sides of the above by

|\mathcal{P}|\geqslant\frac{k^{2}}{2}-\frac{R}{3}.

(5)

Now consider the green edges in
K_{n}
.
Since

is outerplanar and the green edges of

are the inner edges
there are at most

.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\#\text{green edges}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sum_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(|V(P)|-3)=kn-3|\mathcal{P}|\overset{\eqref{eq:num-pages-lower-bound}}{\leqslant}kn-\frac{3k^{2}}{2}+R\text{ and}

(6)

\displaystyle\#\text{green edges}

\displaystyle=|E(K_{n})|-\#\text{black edges}=\binom{n}{2}-B\overset{\eqref{eq:kn=R+B}}{=}\binom{n}{2}-kn+R.

(7)

Combining (
6
) and
(
7

\displaystyle kn-\frac{3k^{2}}{2}+R

\displaystyle\geqslant\binom{n}{2}-kn+R

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{3k^{2}}{2}-2kn+\binom{n}{2}

\displaystyle\iff

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle\geqslant k^{2}-\frac{4n}{3}k+\frac{n(n-1)}{3}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2n}{3}-\sqrt{\left(\frac{2n}{3}\right)^{2}-\frac{n(n-1)}{3}}

\displaystyle\implies

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2n}{3}-\sqrt{\frac{n^{2}}{9}+\frac{n}{3}}>\frac{2n}{3}-\sqrt{\frac{(n+3)^{2}}{9}}=\frac{n}{3}-1

Thus we have

∎

To observe that
\mathcal{P}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{P}_{n/3}
are indeed union pages, we list for each vertex

by which vertices of

it is hit.
For ease of presentation, we assume that

For odd

In particular, each vertex that is contained in some

None of the graphs

We cover these edges with two additional union pages, each containing a perfect matching.

Finally, we define union pages consisting of disjoint unions of stars to cover the remaining edge lengths
1,\dots,k-1
and
3k+1,\dots,4k

For this, we define stars

The union pages

Comparing the presented construction with the lower bound of

6
, we remark that we have
n^{2}/18+\Theta(n)
connected components and
\Theta(n^{2})

To obtain an upper bound of

In this case, each union page uses all vertices and each connected component is a maximal outerplanar graph, i.e., there are no red edges.
It remains open whether such a book embedding exists.
Note that the book embedding constructed for

Partitioning these pages into union pages containing

for the union page number of

.

5
Conclusions

We have shown bounds on the local page number, the local queue number, and the union queue number of complete graphs that are tight up to a constant additive term.
However, there remains a gap between the lower bound of
n/3-\mathcal{O}(1)
and the upper bound of
4n/9+\mathcal{O}(1)
on the union page number of
K_{n}
.

Question 1

What is the union page number of complete graphs?

Comparing queues and stacks, we find that both in the local and in the union setting, queues are more powerful than stacks for representing complete graphs as both the local and the union queue number is smaller than the respective variant of the page number.

Finally, we point out complete bipartite graphs as another dense graph class.
Heath and Rosenberg
[
9
]
proved
\qn(K_{m,n})=\lceil m/2\rceil
, where
m\leqslant n

For the page number, it is known that

\pn(K_{m,n})=m
if
n\geqslant m^{2}-m+1

[
2
]
,

\pn(K_{n,n})\leqslant\lfloor 2n/3\rfloor+1
,

\pn(K_{\lfloor n^{2}/4\rfloor,n})\leqslant n-1

[
6

\pn(K_{m,n})\leqslant\lceil(m+2n)/4\rceil

[
15
]
.
In light of the unclear situation for the page number, we ask for the local and union variants of queue number and page number of complete bipartite graphs.

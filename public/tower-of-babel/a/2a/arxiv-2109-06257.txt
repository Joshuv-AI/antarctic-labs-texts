Hadrons as QCD Bound States Talk at the (virtual) Quark Confinement and the Hadron Spectrum 2021 conference on 2 – 6 August 2021 in Stavanger, Norway.
Paul Hoyer

Abstract
Bound state perturbation theory is well established for QED atoms. Today the hyperfine splitting of Positronium is known to 𝒪 ⁡ ( α 7 ​ log ⁡ α ) {\cal O}\left(\alpha^{7}\log\alpha\right) . Whereas standard expansions of scattering amplitudes start from free states, bound states are expanded around eigenstates of the Hamiltonian including a binding potential. The eigenstate wave functions have all powers of α \alpha , requiring a choice in the ordering of the perturbative expansion. Temporal ( A 0 = 0 A^{0}=0 ) gauge permits an expansion starting from valence Fock states, bound by their instantaneous gauge field. This formulation is applicable in any frame and seems promising even for hadrons in QCD. The 𝒪 ⁡ ( α s 0 ) {\cal O}\left(\alpha_{s}^{0}\right) confining potential is determined (up to a universal scale) by a homogeneous solution of Gauss’ law.



I
1. Motivations

Bound states – even QED atoms
Bodwin et al. 1985
– are inherently non-perturbative. Atomic wave functions have all powers of
\alpha
and describe exponentially suppressed processes such as tunneling. No Feynman diagram has a bound state pole. The propagators contribute inverse powers of
\alpha
at the Bohr momentum scale
\alpha m
, thus reordering the perturbative expansion. The sum of Feynman “ladder” diagrams diverge at the bound state energies for arbitrarily small values of
\alpha
. Atoms are at the borderline between hard perturbative and soft classical physics, featuring elementary quanta in a classical potential. The good news is that the truly hard as well as the soft, classical regime is familiar and under theoretical control.

The omission of basic bound state methods from modern field theory (QFT) textbooks seems unwarranted
Blum 2017
. Quantum Mechanics is traditionally introduced via the Schrödinger equation, which in a field theory context should be derived from the QED action. Bound states give insights into QFT that are complementary to those of scattering amplitudes.

The non-perturbative aspects of atoms and hadrons may be related, as suggested by the successful quark model classification of the hadron spectrum. The QCD coupling
\alpha_{s}(Q)
is
Q
-independent in a classical gluon field. There are indications that the coupling “freezes” at hadronic scales,
\alpha_{s}(Q\lesssim 1\,{{\mathrm{GeV}}})\simeq 0.5

Dokshitzer 1998
;
Dokshitzer et al. 1999
;
Deur et al. 2016
. Then the strong binding of hadrons is not due to a large
\alpha_{s}
, and a perturbative expansion can be relevant.

II
2. Bound state basics

The accuracy of QED applied to atoms is demonstrated by the hyperfine splitting of Positronium, the mass difference
\Delta E_{b}
between the ground states of Orthopositronium
(J^{PC}=1^{--})
and Parapositronium
(0^{-+})

Penin 2014
;
Adkins 2015
;
Adkins 2018

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta E_{b}}{m_{e}}=

\displaystyle\frac{7}{12}\alpha^{4}-\Big(\frac{8}{9}+\frac{\ln 2}{2}\Big)\frac{\alpha^{5}}{\pi}-\frac{5}{24}\alpha^{6}\ln\alpha+\bigg[\frac{1367}{648}-\frac{5197}{3456}\pi^{2}+\Big(\frac{221}{144}\pi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Big)\ln 2-\frac{53}{32}\zeta(3)\bigg]\frac{\alpha^{6}}{\pi^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{7\alpha^{7}}{8\pi}\ln^{2}\alpha+\Big(\frac{17}{3}\ln 2-\frac{217}{90}\Big)\frac{\alpha^{7}}{\pi}\ln\alpha+{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{7}\right)

(1)

The
\log\alpha
dependence arises from the quenching of infrared singularities: Soft photons decouple from the neutral atom when their wavelength is larger than its Bohr radius
r=2/\alpha m_{e}
.

Despite impressive achievements such as (
II
) the principles of bound state perturbation theory are not formulated at a level similar to the perturbative
S
-matrix in the Interaction Picture. The Poincaré invariance of interacting, spatially extended states is non-trivial.
To illustrate, atoms in motion are often depicted as ellipses due to Lorentz contraction, with no discussion of how the contraction is realized in QED. The effective binding energy
\Delta E(P)
of a Positronium atom of mass
M=2m_{e}+E_{b}
, where
E_{b}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}\alpha^{2}m_{e}+{\cal O}(\alpha^{4})
, is inversely proportional to its momentum
P
in the limit of relativistic motion,
P\gg 2m_{e}
:

\displaystyle\Delta E(P)\equiv\sqrt{P^{2}+(2m_{e}+E_{b})^{2}}-\sqrt{P^{2}+4m_{e}^{2}}=\frac{2m_{e}E_{b}}{P}+{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{4}\right)

(2)

The Coulomb energy
V({\bm{r}})=-\alpha/|{\bm{r}}|
is independent of
P
for the uncontracted orthogonal separations,
{\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{P}}=0
. This seems to contradict the
P
-dependent energy difference (
2
). An explanation was given in
Järvinen 2005
using the Bethe-Salpeter (B-S) equation
Salpeter and Bethe 1951
;
Itzykson and Zuber 1980
. For
P>0
transverse photon exchange contributes at leading
{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{2}\right)
to the binding energy. This cancels the Coulomb energy (see Eq. (33) of
Järvinen 2005
), leaving an interaction that ensures the correct dependence of the Positronium energy on its momentum,
E(P)=\sqrt{M^{2}+P^{2}}
. The valence Fock state wave function
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}}\right\rangle
contracts as expected, whereas
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}\gamma}\right\rangle
behaves differently.

Feynman diagrams arise in an expansion around free states, which lack overlap with bound states. Consequently, no Feynman diagram for
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}
has a pole at the Positronium energies. Bound state binding may be recovered by expanding the electron propagator in a photon field
A^{\mu}
in a geometric series with free propagators,

\displaystyle\frac{i}{i\not{\partial}-m-e{\not{A}}}=\frac{i}{i\not{\partial}-m}-\frac{i}{i\not{\partial}-m}ie{\not{A}}\frac{i}{i\not{\partial}-m}+\ldots

(3)

In the non-relativistic limit Positronium dynamics is equivalent to that of the
e^{-}
in a fixed
A^{0}
field. This allows to obtain the Schrödinger equation through a sum of Feynman ladder diagrams analogous to (
3
) (see,
e.g.
, Section III of
Hoyer 2021
). The Positronium poles of
e^{+}e^{-}\to e^{+}e^{-}
arise through the divergence of the sum of ladder diagrams.

Relativistic bound state dynamics cannot be reduced to scattering in an external field. Higher order corrections to Positronia were first evaluated
Murota 1988
using the Bethe-Salpeter equation. It is based on the Dyson-Schwinger (D-S) identity for a
2\to 2
Green function
G_{T}
, whose external propagators are truncated,

\displaystyle G_{T}=K+G_{T}\,S\,K

(4)

Here
S
is a two-particle propagator and
K
a two-particle irreducible kernel. Any Feynman diagram contributes equally to the two-sides of (
4
). The D-S equation is thus formally exact when
S
and
K
are expanded to all orders in
\alpha
. The B-S equation for bound state wave functions
\Phi_{T}
is given by the residues of the poles in
G_{T}
,

\displaystyle\Phi_{T}=\Phi_{T}\,S\,K

(5)

Being based on Feynman diagrams the B-S equation is Poincaré covariant, but notoriously difficult to solve. There are retardation effects even at lowest order, with free Dirac propagators in
S
and a single photon exchange kernel
K
: The
A^{\mu}
field at
e^{-}(t,{\bm{x}})
depends on the distribution of
e^{+}(t-t^{\prime},{\bm{x}}^{\prime})
at earlier times, due to the propagation time
t^{\prime}
of transverse photons. No analytic solution of the B-S equation is known.

The rhs. of the D-S equation (
4
) involves a double perturbative expansion in
S
and
K
, whereas
G_{T}
has a unique expansion in
\alpha

Caswell and Lepage 1978
;
Lepage 1978
. This allows to fix
S
and let the expansion of
K
be determined by that of
G_{T}
. The B-S equation can thus at lowest order in the rest frame be reduced to the Schrödinger equation, and the higher order corrections systematically determined. More intuitively: There are many equivalent expansions in
\alpha
when the first approximation of the wave function already has all powers of
\alpha
. All reorderings must give the same result for physically measurable quantities such as the binding energies in (
II
).

The realization that the B-S equation is not unique led to Non-Relativistic QED
Caswell and Lepage 1986
;
Kinoshita 1998
. NRQED expands in powers of
|{\bm{p}}|/m_{e}
, the velocity of the
e^{\pm}
in the rest frame. This reordering of the original B-S expansion is motivated by the nearly non-relativistic motion of atomic electrons. The expansion is uniquely defined when the Schrödinger equation is chosen as starting point. The higher order terms in (
II
) were derived using NRQED.

III
3. The instantaneity of gauge interactions

In the Schrödinger approximation Positronium is described by its valence
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}}\right\rangle
Fock state, which is an eigenstate of the QED Hamiltonian with the instantaneous Coulomb potential. The tranverse photon interaction terms in
\mathcal{H}_{QED}
create Fock states such as
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}\gamma}\right\rangle
, which contribute to the binding energy at higher orders in
\alpha
. This suggests
Hoyer 2021
a “Bound Fock expansion”, where the constituents of each Fock state propagate in their instantaneous gauge field. Transitions between Fock states are given by the transverse photon interactions of
\mathcal{H}_{QED}
, and are suppressed by the coupling
e
. With all Fock states and the full Hamiltonian taken into account the method is formally exact and valid in any frame.

It is sometimes held that the concept of an instantaneous potential is valid only in a non-relativistic limit. However, gauge theories have instantaneous interactions even with relativistic dynamics. This seems surprising since the action is local and no constituent moves faster than light. The exception arises because the gauge can be fixed non-locally. Thus all instantaneous interactions are gauge dependent.

The QED action

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{QED}

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\big[-{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}+\bar{\psi}(i\not{\partial}-m-e\not{A})\psi\big]

\displaystyle F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{\mu}

(6)

has no term involving
\partial_{0}A^{0}
since
F_{00}=0
. Similarly,
F_{ij}
gives no space derivative of the longitudinal gauge field, which is defined by a scalar potential,
{\bm{A}}_{L}^{j}=\partial_{j}\,\phi
. Hence
A^{0}
and
{\bm{A}}_{L}
do not propagate: Their values are fixed by the gauge.

Feynman gauge is convenient for scattering amplitudes, as the gauge fixing term
\mathcal{S}_{GF}=-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\int d^{4}x\,(\partial_{\mu}A^{\mu})^{2}
preserves full Poincaré invariance.
\mathcal{S}_{GF}
adds the “missing” derivatives

\partial_{0}A^{0}
and
\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{A}}_{L}
to the action, allowing the gauge dependent fields to propagate similarly to the gauge invariant ones. For bound states, on the other hand, instantaneous interactions are welcome due to their lack of retardation. It is then advantageous to use Coulomb gauge (
\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{A}}_{L}=0
) or temporal gauge (
A^{0}=0
), which non-locally fix
{\bm{A}}_{L}
and
A^{0}
, respectively.

The Hamiltonian generates evolution in time, leaving only space rotations and translations as explicit (“kinetic”) symmetries. Time translations and boosts involve interactions and are thus “dynamic” symmetries. Both the Coulomb and temporal gauge conditions preserve the kinetic symmetries of the Hamiltonian.

The Hamiltonian approach defines a conjugate field
\pi_{\alpha}
for each field
\varphi_{\alpha}
in the action, and imposes canonical commutation relations between them,

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(t,{\bm{x}})\equiv\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}(\varphi,\partial\varphi)}{\delta[\partial_{0}\varphi_{\alpha}(t,{\bm{x}})]}\hskip 56.9055pt\left[{\varphi_{\alpha}(t,{\bm{x}})},{\pi_{\beta}(t,{\bm{y}})}\right]_{\pm}=i\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta^{3}({\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}})

(7)

In QED the conjugate field of
A^{0}
vanishes. Quantization in Coulomb gauge can be realized by means of constraints
Weinberg 2005
, which are cumbersome especially in QCD
Christ and Lee 1980
. Temporal gauge eliminates
A^{0}
and thus avoids the problem with its lack of conjugate field. The canonical commutation relations can then be imposed for all space components
{\bm{A}}
, with the longitudinal electric field
{\bm{E}}_{L}=-\partial_{0}{\bm{A}}_{L}
being conjugate to
{\bm{A}}_{L}
.

The classical nature of the instantaneous
{\bm{E}}_{L}
field is apparent in temporal gauge. The gauge condition
A^{0}=0
needs to be supplemented with a condition on the remaining gauge degrees of freedom
Willemsen 1978
;
Bjorken 1979
;
Leibbrandt 1987
. Time independent gauge transformations which preserve
A^{0}=0
are generated by Gauss’ operator,

\displaystyle G(x)\equiv\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}_{QED}}{\delta{A^{0}(x)}}=\partial_{i}E^{i}_{L}(x)-e\psi^{\dagger}\psi(x)

(8)

Since
A^{0}
is fixed
G(x)=0
is not an operator equation of motion, but a condition which is imposed on physical states,
G(x)\left|{phys}\right\rangle=0
(similarly as
{\bm{A}}_{L}\left|{phys}\right\rangle=0
in the Gupta-Bleuler formalism). This ensures that the
\left|{phys}\right\rangle
states are invariant under time independent gauge transformations and determines the value of
{\bm{E}}_{L}
,

\displaystyle{\bm{E}}_{L}(t,{\bm{x}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\bm{\nabla}_{x}\int d{\bm{y}}\,\frac{e}{4\pi|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}|}\,\psi^{\dagger}\psi(t,{\bm{y}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle

(9)

This is not an operator constraint,
e.g.
, it should be understood that
\psi^{\dagger}\psi(t,{\bm{y}})\left|{0}\right\rangle=0
.
The electric field
{\bm{E}}_{L}
is classical, generated by the charged constituents and contributes an instantaneous (“
V
”) potential to the QED Hamiltonian in temporal gauge,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QED}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{x}}\,\big[E^{i}\partial_{0}A_{i}+i\psi^{\dagger}\partial_{0}\psi-\mathcal{L}_{QED}\big]=\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(V)}+\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(KI)}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(V)}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{x}}\,{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{E}}^{2}_{L}={\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\int d{\bm{x}}d{\bm{y}}\,\frac{\alpha}{|{\bm{x}}-{\bm{y}}|}\big[\psi^{\dagger}\psi(t,{\bm{x}})\big]\big[\psi^{\dagger}\psi(t,{\bm{y}})\big]

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(KI)}

\displaystyle=\int d{\bm{x}}\,\big[{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{E}}^{2}_{T}+{\textstyle\frac{1}{4}}F^{ij}F^{ij}+\psi^{\dagger}\big[(-{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot(i\bm{\nabla}+e{\bm{A}})+m\gamma^{0})\big]\,\psi\big]

(10)

where
{\bm{\alpha}}\equiv\gamma^{0}\bm{\gamma}
. The kinetic energies of the electron and photon fields, as well as their
-e\int d{\bm{x}}\,\psi^{\dagger}{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\bm{A}}\,\psi
interaction term, are in the “
KI
” part of the Hamiltonian.

IV
4. Application to Positronium

The valence
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}}\right\rangle
Fock state of Positronium is defined in terms of the electron field

as

\displaystyle\left|{e^{-}e^{+};{\bm{P}}}\right\rangle\equiv\int d{\bm{x}}_{1}d{\bm{x}}_{2}\,\bar{\psi}({\bm{x}}_{1})e^{i{\bm{P}}\cdot({\bm{x}}_{1}+{\bm{x}}_{2})/2}\,\Phi^{({\bm{P}})}({\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2})\psi({\bm{x}}_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle

where

. The perturbative vacuum
\left|{0}\right\rangle
projects on the creation operators

in
\bar{\psi}
and

has

and on the quantum numbers of the Positronium.

The action of
\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(V)}
(
III
) on the state (
11

\big[{\psi^{\dagger}\psi({\bm{x}})},{\bar{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bm{x}}_{1})}\big]=\bar{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bm{x}}_{1})\delta({\bm{x}}-{\bm{x}}_{1})

\big[{\psi^{\dagger}\psi({\bm{y}})},{\psi_{\beta}({\bm{x}}_{2})}\big]=-\psi_{\beta}({\bm{x}}_{2})\delta({\bm{y}}-{\bm{x}}_{2})
. Together with the

contribution this gives the Coulomb energy (the infinite “self-energies” with

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(V)}\,\bar{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bm{x}}_{1})\psi_{\beta}({\bm{x}}_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle=-\frac{\alpha}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}\,\bar{\psi}_{\alpha}({\bm{x}}_{1})\psi_{\beta}({\bm{x}}_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle

In the rest frame (
{\bm{P}}=0
) only the kinetic energy of the electrons in
\mathcal{H}_{QED}^{(KI)}
contributes at leading order, since the radiation of a transverse photon is suppressed by the
e^{\pm}
Bohr momentum
\alpha m/2
. The eigenstate condition gives the bound state equation (BSE),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QED}\,\left|{e^{-}e^{+};{\bm{P}}=0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=M\,\left|{e^{-}e^{+};{\bm{P}}=0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\big(i{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\overset{\rightarrow}{\bm{\nabla}}}+m\gamma^{0}\big)\Phi^{(0)}({\bm{x}})+\Phi^{(0)}({\bm{x}})

\displaystyle\big(i{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\overset{\leftarrow}{\bm{\nabla}}}-m\gamma^{0}\big)=\big[M-V({\bm{x}})\big]\Phi^{(0)}({\bm{x}})

(13)

where
V({\bm{x}})=-\alpha/|{\bm{x}}|
. This BSE reduces to the Schrödinger equation in the non-relativistic limit, which may be taken at
{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{2}\right)
in the binding energy
E_{b}=M-2m
.

For Positronium in motion
(P>0)
the

have momenta
{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{P}}+{\cal O}\left(\alpha m\right)
, so the
ee\gamma
vertices are no longer suppressed by
\alpha
. Including the
\left|{ee\gamma}\right\rangle
Fock state

gives agreement with the B-S analysis
Järvinen 2005
: The
\left|{e^{+}e^{-}}\right\rangle
wave function Lorentz contracts and the bound state energy

as required by Poincaré invariance.

Transverse photon exchange as well as annihilation

give the Positronium hyperfine splitting (

) at
{\cal O}\left(\alpha^{4}\right)
. First checks of bound state scattering were also made for form factors and deep inelastic scattering

. The bound Fock expansion outlined here may seem cumbersome, but should remove the “art” (noted on p. 493 of

V
5. Application to hadrons

The properties of charmonium and bottomonium states are successfully described using the Schrödinger equation with the “Cornell potential”
Eichten et al. 1980
;
Eichten et al. 2008
,

\displaystyle V(r)=V^{\prime}r-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{r}\ \ \ \text{with}\ \ V^{\prime}\simeq 0.18\ \text{GeV}^{2},\ \ \alpha_{s}\simeq 0.39

(14)

The confining potential
V^{\prime}r
in (
14
) involves a scale
V^{\prime}
which is not present in the QCD action, and thus also not in Gauss’ operator (
8
). The only way this scale can appear in a classical solution of Gauss’ law is through a boundary condition. Analogous scenarios have been discussed before, notably in the context of the MIT Bag Model
Chodos et al. 1974
. Can a non-trivial boundary condition arise in the bound state approach discussed above, applied to QCD in temporal gauge?

Gauss’ operator in QCD is

\displaystyle G_{a}(x)\equiv\frac{\delta\mathcal{S}_{QCD}}{\delta{A_{a}^{0}(x)}}=\partial_{i}E_{a}^{i}(x)+gf_{abc}A_{b}^{i}E_{c}^{i}-g\psi^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi(x)

(15)

As in QED, the color electric field
{\bm{E}}_{L}^{a}
is constrained by
G_{a}(x)\left|{phys}\right\rangle=0

Willemsen 1978
;
Bjorken 1979
;
Leibbrandt 1987
,

\displaystyle\bm{\nabla}\cdot{\bm{E}}_{L}^{a}({\bm{x}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle=g\big[-f_{abc}A_{b}^{i}E_{c}^{i}+\psi^{\dagger}T^{a}\psi({\bm{x}})\big]\left|{phys}\right\rangle\equiv g\,\mathcal{E}_{a}({\bm{x}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle

(16)

In QED the
\bar{\psi}({\bm{x}}_{1})\psi({\bm{x}}_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle
component of the state (
11
) generates a dipole electric field, giving rise to the Coulomb potential
-\alpha/|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|
in (
12
). The analogous color potential
-{\textstyle\frac{4}{3}}\alpha_{s}/r
in (
14
) arises from the color
A
quark components
\left|{q_{A}({\bm{x}}_{1})\bar{q}_{A}({\bm{x}}_{2})}\right\rangle
of a meson state. However, a color singlet meson cannot generate a classical color octet electric field
E_{L}^{a}({\bm{x}})

at any point
{\bm{x}}
. Color symmetry ensures that the electric field cancels in the sum over quark colors.

The QED solution (
9
) for
{\bm{E}}_{L}
was obtained with the obvious boundary condition
\lim_{|{\bm{x}}|\to\infty}{\bm{E}}_{L}({\bm{x}})=0
. The situation is less obvious in QCD, where the color electric field in any case vanishes at all
{\bm{x}}
for color singlets. Confinement motivates to consider alternative boundary conditions,
i.e.
, homogeneous solutions of (
16
).

A homogenous solution should maintain the kinetic symmetries of space rotations and translations. The only possibility appears to be

\displaystyle{\bm{E}}^{a}_{H}({\bm{x}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\kappa\,\bm{\nabla}_{x}\int d{\bm{y}}\,{\bm{x}}\cdot{\bm{y}}\,\mathcal{E}_{a}({\bm{y}})\left|{phys}\right\rangle

(17)

where
\mathcal{E}_{a}({\bm{y}})
is defined in (
16
).
{\bm{E}}^{a}_{H}
trivially satisfies
\bm{\nabla}_{x}\cdot{\bm{E}}_{H}^{a}=0
when the normalization
\kappa
is independent of
{\bm{x}}
. Adding the homogeneous
{\bm{E}}^{a}_{H}
to a particular solution of (
16
) (analogous to (
9

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QCD}^{(V)}

\displaystyle\equiv{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\int d{\bm{x}}\,({\bm{E}}_{a,L})^{2}=\int d{\bm{y}}d{\bm{z}}\,\Big\{\,{\bm{y}}\cdot{\bm{z}}\Big[{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\kappa^{2}{\textstyle\int}d{\bm{x}}+g\kappa\Big]+{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{|{\bm{y}}-{\bm{z}}|}\Big\}\,\mathcal{E}_{a}({\bm{y}})\mathcal{E}_{a}({\bm{z}})

(18)

The term of
{\cal O}\left(\kappa^{2}\right)
is
\propto\hskip-2.84526pt\int d{\bm{x}}
(the volume of space) because the homogeneous solution generates an
{\bm{x}}
-independent field energy density. This (infinite) contribution is irrelevant provided it is universal,
i.e.
, the same for all components of all bound states. This determines the normalization
\kappa
in (
17
) for each state
\left|{phys}\right\rangle

Applying
\mathcal{H}_{QCD}^{(V)}
to a meson Fock component
\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle\equiv\sum_{A}\bar{\psi}_{A}^{\alpha}({\bm{x}}_{1})\psi_{A}^{\beta}({\bm{x}}_{2})\left|{0}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{QCD}^{(V)}\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle=\Big[\big({\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}\kappa^{2}{\textstyle\int}d{\bm{x}}+g\kappa\big)C_{F}\,({\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2})^{2}-C_{F}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}\Big]\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\kappa_{q\bar{q}}=\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{gC_{F}}\frac{1}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}

The potential energy defined by

\displaystyle V_{q\bar{q}}({\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2})=\Lambda^{2}|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|-C_{F}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}

\displaystyle V_{qqq}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2},{\bm{x}}_{3})

\displaystyle=\Lambda^{2}\,d_{qqq}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2},{\bm{x}}_{3})-\frac{2}{3}\,\alpha_{s}\Big(\frac{1}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}+\frac{1}{|{\bm{x}}_{2}-{\bm{x}}_{3}|}+\frac{1}{|{\bm{x}}_{3}-{\bm{x}}_{1}|}\Big)

(22)

When two of the quarks in the baryon are close to each other the potential should (for
N_{c}=3

(

{\bm{x}}_{2}={\bm{x}}_{3}

\displaystyle V_{qqq}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2},{\bm{x}}_{2})=\Lambda^{2}|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\alpha_{s}}{|{\bm{x}}_{1}-{\bm{x}}_{2}|}=V_{q\bar{q}}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2})

(23)

Corresponding potentials are obtained for color singlet
\left|{q\bar{q}g}\right\rangle
and
\left|{gg}\right\rangle
Fock states
Hoyer 2021
.

The above potentials depend only on the instantaneous positions
{\bm{x}}_{i}
of the charges. They are independent of the bound state wave function and the CM momentum
{\bm{P}}
. In a general frame, for a
q\bar{q}
state of the form (
11
), the bound state equation is

\displaystyle i\bm{\nabla}\cdot\big\{{{\bm{\alpha}}},{\Phi^{(P)}({\bm{x}})}\big\}-{\textstyle\frac{1}{2}}{\bm{P}}\cdot\big[{{\bm{\alpha}}},{\Phi^{(P)}({\bm{x}})}\big]+m\big[{\gamma^{0}},{\Phi^{(P)}({\bm{x}})}\big]

\displaystyle=\big[E-V({\bm{x}})\big]\Phi^{(P)}({\bm{x}})

(24)

where
E=\sqrt{M^{2}+{\bm{P}}^{2}}
and the potential
V({\bm{x}})
is as in (

P>0
to take into account also transverse gluon exchange,
i.e.
, the
{\cal O}\left(g\right)

\left|{q\bar{q}g}\right\rangle
Fock state created by
\mathcal{H}_{QCD}^{(KI)}
(
cf.
(

{\cal O}\left(\alpha_{s}^{0}\right)
the
\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle
state must satisfy covariance on its own, as any contribution from higher Fock states is of higher order in
\alpha_{s}

q\bar{q}
potential is indeed boost covariant by itself. The
{\bm{P}}
-dependence of the wave function is not given by standard Lorentz contraction, however.

More generally, the
{\cal O}(\alpha_{s}^{0})

\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle

q\bar{q}

{x_{Bj}}

{\cal O}\left(\alpha_{s}^{0}\right)

\left|{q\bar{q}g}\right\rangle

For small quark masses

the BSE (
24

{\cal O}\left(\alpha_{s}^{0}\right)
gives
\left|{q\bar{q}}\right\rangle

m=0

P^{\mu}=0

For quark separations
{\bm{x}}

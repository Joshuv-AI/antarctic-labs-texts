Frames With Several Generators Associated with Weyl-Heisenberg Group and Extended Affine Group
Divya Jindal, Lalit Kumar Vashisht

Abstract
. We study the construction of Gabor frames and wavelet frames for Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group by using contraction between the affine group and the Weyl-Heisenberg group due to Subag, Baruch, Birman and Mann. Firstly, we give construction of Gabor frames with several generators from a unitary irreducible representation associated to the Weyl-Heisenberg group. Wavelet frames with several generators associated with an extended affine group have been obtained. A relation between frames for Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group is also discussed. Finally, we show that frames of Gabor and wavelet structure are stable under small perturbations.

1.
Introduction

Wavelet and Gabor analyses are perfect illustrations of the deep analogy between quantum physics and signal processing, see
[
1
,
10
]
. Gabor analysis yields a time frequency representation of signals while wavelet provide a time scale representation. More precisely, both Gabor analysis and wavelet analysis are integral part of time-frequency analysis which gives uniform time and frequency resolution. In recent years, reconstruction of signals and its decomposition by using wavelet and Gabor systems have been extensively studied, see
[
2
,
4
,
6
,
9
,
12
,
13
,
14
,
18
,
25
,
26
]
and many references therein. The wavelet and Gabor analyses are both induced by coherent states that are associated with a unitary irreducible representation of a Lie group, see
[
1
,
12
,
14
,
20
]
for details. Wavelet analysis corresponds to unitary irreducible representations of the affine group while the Gabor analysis corresponds to that of the Weyl-Heisenberg group. Let
\mathcal{H}
be a nonzero separable Hilbert space and
G
be a locally compact group. A unitary representation of
G
is a homomorphism
\pi
from
G
into the group
\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
of unitary operators on
\mathcal{H}
that is continuous with respect to the strong operator topology, that is, a map
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
that satisfies
\pi(xy)=\pi(x)\pi(y)
and
\pi(x^{-1})=\pi(x)^{-1}=\pi(x)^{*}
, and for which
x\to\pi(x)u
is continuous from
G
to
\mathcal{H}
for any
u\in\mathcal{H}
. Also,
\pi
is said to be irreducible if
\pi
admits only trivial (that is,
=\{0\}
or
\mathcal{H}
) invariant subspaces. A Lie group can be obtained as a certain limit of another group with the help of group contraction, see
[
21
]
and references therein. Let
G_{0}
and
\widetilde{G_{0}}
be two Lie groups. We say that
\widetilde{G_{0}}
is a
contraction
of
G_{0}
if for every
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, there exists a diffeomorphism
F_{\epsilon}:\widetilde{G_{0}}\to G_{0}
such that
F_{\epsilon}(e_{0})=e_{1}
where
e_{0}
,
e_{1}
are the identity elements of
\widetilde{G_{0}}
and
G_{0}
, respectively, and
x.y=\lim_{\epsilon\ to0^{+}}F_{\epsilon}^{-1}(F_{\epsilon}(x).F_{\epsilon}(y))
for every
x,y\in\widetilde{G_{0}}
. Very recently, Subag, Baruch, Birman, and Mann, in
[
21
]
, studied relation between Gabor and wavelet analyses by using “contraction” between affine group and Weyl-Heisenberg group. They obtained unitary irreducible representations of the Heisenberg group as contraction of representations of the extended affine group. Using this technique, Subag et al., in
[
21
]
, relates two analyses, where they contracted coherent states, tight frames and resolutions of the identity. In the direction of frame conditions, Subag et al. constructed a family of time localized wavelet frames that contract to a Gabor frame. In
[
21
]
, Subag et al., also showed that how a family of frequency localized wavelet frames can be contracted to non-standard Gabor frames. They also gave deformation of Gabor frames to wavelet frames. To connect wavelet and Gabor systems by special type of groups, the authors of
[
5
,
22
,
23
]
gave some relations between the Gabor analysis and wavelet analysis by exhibiting both the affine group and the Weyl-Heisenberg group as subgroups of the four dimensional affine Weyl-Heisenberg group.

Motivated by above works which relates fundamental properties of frames from wavelet systems to Gabor systems via contraction, we study construction of frames with several generators for Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group. Subag et al., in
[
21
]
, gave tight frames with Gabor and wavelet structure associated with Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group. They obtain unitary irreducible representation of Weyl-Heisenberg group from unitary irreducible representation of extended affine group using group contraction. Using the representations given by Subag et al., in
[
21
]
, we give existence of Gabor frames and wavelet frames with several generators associated with Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group, respectively.

2.
Preliminaries

Symbols
\mathbb{N}
,
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\mathbb{R}
stand for a set of the natural numbers, integers, positive real numbers, non-zero real numbers and real numbers, respectively. To make notations compatible with quantum physics, in this paper, a vector
\phi
of a Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
is also denoted by
|\phi\rangle
(ket vector or state in
\mathbb{H}
). The inner product on
\mathbb{H}
is denoted by
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
. The inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle
is conjugate-linear in the first variable and linear in the first variable, that is,

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\alpha\psi+\beta\varphi\rangle=\alpha\langle\phi|\psi\rangle+\beta\langle\phi|\varphi\rangle\ \ \text{and}\ \ \langle\alpha\phi|\psi\rangle=\overline{\alpha}\langle\phi|\psi\rangle,

for all vectors
|\phi\rangle
,
|\psi\rangle
and
|\varphi\rangle
in
\mathbb{H}
and for all scalars
\alpha
,
\beta
. Here,
\overline{\alpha}
denote the complex-conjugate of the scalar
\alpha
. The norm on
\mathbb{H}
, denoted by
\|\cdot\|
, is defined as
\|\phi\|=\langle\phi|\phi\rangle
for
|\phi\rangle\in\mathbb{H}
. As is standard, the space of equivalence classes of square integrable (Lebesgue integrable) functions is denoted by
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. Here
dx
is the Lebesgue measure. Engineers and physicists pronounced
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
as the “
signal space
”. We consider the following standard inner product on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
:

(2.1)

\displaystyle\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{\phi(x)}\psi(x)dx,\ \ \phi,\,\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx).

The space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
becomes a Hilbert space with respect to the inner product defined in (
2.1
).

To define Gabor and wavelet system, the following classes of operators, which acts unitarily on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, are used: Let
a
,
b
be positive real numbers,
c
be a non-zero real number and let
\phi
be a non-zero vector in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

\displaystyle\ T_{a}:\phi(x)\mapsto\phi(x-a)\ \quad(\text{Translation by}\ a);

\displaystyle E_{b}:\phi(x)\mapsto e^{2\pi ib\cdot x}\phi(x)\quad(\text{Modulation by}\ b);

\displaystyle D_{c}:\phi(x)\mapsto|c|^{\frac{1}{2}}\phi(cx)\ \ \,\quad(\text{Dilation by}\ c).

A collection of vectors of the form
\mathcal{G}(a,b,\phi):=\{E_{mb}T_{na}\phi\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{e^{2\pi imbx}\phi(x-a)\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
Gabor system
; and the collection
\mathcal{W}(a,b,\phi):\{T_{kba^{j}}D_{a^{j}}\phi\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}=\{a^{\frac{j}{2}}\phi(a^{j}x-kb)\}_{j,k\in\mathbb{Z}}
is called a
wavelet system
.

2.1.
Hilbert Frames

Dennis Gabor, in
[
11
]
, introduced a fundamental approach to signal decomposition in terms of elementary signal, for example, the Gaussian. Duffin and Schaeffer
[
8
]
while studying some intense problems related to non-harmonic Fourier series, abstracted the fundamental notion of Gabor to introduce the notion of a frame for the signal space
L^{2}((-\beta,\beta),dx)
. To be precise, they defined frames of exponentials for the space
L^{2}((-\beta,\beta),dx)
. Gabor frames or Weyl-Heisenberg frames originate in quantum mechanics by Neumann
[
19
]
, and in communication theory by the idea of Gabor, see
[
11
]
. A countable collection of vectors
\{|w_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}
in a separable Hilbert space
\mathbb{H}
is a
Hilbert frame
(or simply,
frame
) for
\mathbb{H}
if there exist positive real numbers
\gamma_{o}
,
\delta_{o}
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\gamma_{o}\|w\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}|\langle w,w_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq\delta_{o}\|w\|^{2}

holds for all
|w\rangle
in
\mathbb{H}
.
The scalars
\gamma_{o}
,
\delta_{o}
are called lower and upper frame bounds of the frame
\{w_{k}\}_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}
. The frame
\{|w_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}

frame
is tight if it is possible to choose
\alpha_{o}=\delta_{o}
, and
normalized tight
(or
Parseval
, if
\alpha_{o}=\delta_{o}=1
. If only upper inequality holds in (
2.2
), then we say that
\{|w_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}
is a
Bessel sequence
with
Bessel bound

\delta_{o}
. If
\{|w_{k}\rangle\}_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}
is a frame for
\mathbb{H}
, then the map
\mathrm{S}:\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, known as the
frame operator
, given by

\mathrm{S}:|w\rangle\mapsto\sum_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}\langle w,w_{k}\rangle w_{k}

is linear, bounded and invertible on
\mathbb{H}
. Thus, a frame for
\mathbb{H}
provides a decomposition, not necessarily unique, for every vector
|w\rangle
of
\mathbb{H}
:

\displaystyle|w\rangle=\mathrm{S}\mathrm{S}^{-1}|w\rangle=\sum_{k\in\mathfrak{I}}\big(\langle\mathrm{S}^{-1}w,|w_{k}\rangle\big)\,|w_{k}\rangle.

This decomposition turns out to be a powerful tool for deriving many fundamental results in abstract frame theory. Further, it plays central role both in both pure mathematics and engineering science, for technical details about this, we refer to excellent texts by Casazza and Kutyniok
[
2
]
and Heil
[
13
]
.
Nowadays the theory of wavelets, distributed signal processing, quantum mechanics, iterated function systems and many more topics are emerging in important applications in frame theory, see
[
7
,
15
,
16
,
24
]
and many references therein. A frame of the form
\mathcal{G}(a,b,\phi)
is called the Gabor frame and wavelet frame is a frame of the form
\mathcal{W}(a,b,\phi)
.

2.2.
The Weyl-Heisenberg group

The Weyl-Heisenberg group is the semidirect product
\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{R}
, where
\psi:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow Aut(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is given by
\psi_{\lambda}((x_{1},x_{2}))=(x_{1}+\lambda x_{2},x_{2})
for every
\lambda,x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
; and denote
\mathcal{W}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\rtimes_{\psi}\mathbb{R}
with multiplication law

\displaystyle((x_{1},x_{2}),\lambda)((y_{1},y_{2}),\nu)=(\psi_{\lambda}(y_{1},y_{2})+(x_{1},x_{2}),\lambda+\nu).

Recall that for every
P\in\mathbb{R}^{*},Q\in\mathbb{R}
, the unitary irreducible representation of this group acting on
\mathcal{H}
,
\kappa^{P,Q}:\mathcal{W}\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx))
is given by

\displaystyle(\kappa^{P,Q}(((u_{1},u_{2}),z))f)(x)=e^{i[P(u_{1}+xu_{2})+Qu_{2}]}f(z+x).

For
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, where
N
is some fixed natural number, let
q_{0}^{(l)}
and
p_{0}^{(l)}
be real numbers such that
|q_{0}^{(l)}p_{0}^{(l)}|<2\pi
. Consider the discrete subset of
\mathcal{W}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{q_{0}^{(l)},p_{0}^{(l)}}=\bigg\{\bigg(\bigg(\frac{mnq_{0}^{(l)}p_{0}^{(l)}}{2},mp_{0}^{(l)}\bigg),nq_{0}^{(l)}\bigg)|n,m\in\mathbb{Z}\bigg\}.

For
0\neq\phi_{l}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
where
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, the sequence

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{(m,n,l)}^{P,Q}\big>=\kappa^{P,Q}\bigg(\bigg(\frac{mnq_{0}^{(l)}p_{0}^{(l)}}{2},mp_{0}^{(l)}\bigg),nq_{0}^{(l)}\bigg)\phi_{l},

where
n,m\in\mathbb{Z}
is dense in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, see
[
21
]
for technical details.

2.3.
Extended Affine Group

The affine group
A
realized as the upper half plane

A=\{(a,b):a\in\mathbb{R}^{+},b\in\mathbb{R}\}
,

and the multiplication law in
A
is given by

(a_{1},b_{1})(a_{2},b_{2})=(a_{1}a_{2},a_{1}b_{2}+b_{1})
,

for all points
(a_{1},b_{1}),(a_{2},b_{2})\in A
.

The support of a function
f
, denoted by supp
f
, is defined as supp
f:=
closure of the set
\{x:f(x)\neq 0\}
. Let
V
be a subspace of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
given by

(2.3)

\displaystyle V=\Big\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx):\mbox{supp}(\hat{f})\subseteq[0,\infty)\Big\}

where
\hat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f
given by

\displaystyle\hat{f}(w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)e^{-iwx}dx.

Recall that for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R},
the unitary irreducible representation of the affine group on the subspace
V
of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, as given in (
2.3
), is the map
\eta^{\alpha}:A\to\mathcal{U}(V)
given by

(\eta^{\alpha}(a,b)f)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}f(\frac{x+\alpha b}{a}),\,x\in\mathbb{R}
;

and correspondingly define the sequence

\phi^{\alpha}_{a,b}(x)=(\eta^{\alpha}(a,b)\psi)(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}\psi(\frac{x+\alpha b}{a})
.

The extended affine group, denoted by
EA
, is given by the direct product of the affine group
A
with additive group of real numbers. That is,
EA=A\oplus\mathbb{R}
. The multiplication law on
EA
is given by

(a,b,y)(c,d,z)=(ac,ad+b,y+z)
.

For
\beta\in\mathbb{R},
let
\chi_{\beta}(x)=e^{i\beta x}
be unitary character of
\mathbb{R}.
For every
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R^{*}}
, the unitary irreducible representation of
EA
acting on
V
is the map
\eta^{\alpha}\otimes\chi_{\beta}:EA\to\mu(V)
given by

(\eta^{\alpha}\otimes\chi_{\beta}(a,b,y))f)(x)=e^{i\beta y}\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}f(\frac{x+\alpha b}{a})
.

For finding the relation between frames for
EA
and frames for
W
, we need a different realization which is better suited. This realization is given by three linear invertible maps
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
S_{3}(\epsilon)
defined by

(i)

S_{1}:V\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx)
,
S_{1}(f)(x)=\hat{f}(x)|_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}
.

(ii)

S_{2}:L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{1}{x}dx)
,
S_{2}(f)(x)=\sqrt{x}f(x)
.

(iii)

S_{3}(\epsilon):L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\frac{1}{x}dx)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{+},dx)
,
S_{3}(\epsilon)(f)(x)=f\circ\psi_{\epsilon}(x)
,
where
\psi_{\epsilon}(x)=e^{-\epsilon x}
.

Set
U_{\epsilon}=S_{3}(\epsilon)\circ S_{2}\circ S_{1}
. For each
\epsilon\in(0,1]
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, interwine
\eta^{\alpha}\otimes\chi_{\beta}
with
U_{\epsilon}
to get the equivalent representation

\zeta^{\beta,\alpha}_{\epsilon}:EA\to\mathcal{U}(V)

\displaystyle\begin{split}(\zeta^{\beta,\alpha}_{\epsilon}(a,b,y)f)|_{x}&=U_{\epsilon}\circ(\eta^{\alpha}\otimes\chi_{\beta})(a,b,y)\circ U_{\epsilon})(f)(x)\\
&=e^{i\beta y}e^{i\alpha be^{-\epsilon x}}f(\frac{-\log a}{\epsilon}+x).\end{split}

3.
Main Results

We begin this section with the following definition.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
N
be a fixed natural number. A collection of vectors
\{\big|\phi_{(m,n,l)}\big>\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
in
\mathcal{H}
is said to be a
frame
for
\mathcal{H}
if there exist positive real constants
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta_{o}
such that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{o}\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi_{(m,n,l)}\rangle\|^{2}\leq\beta_{o}\|f\|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathcal{H}.

The scalars
\alpha_{o}
,
\beta_{o}
, obviously not unique, are called
lower frame bound
and
upper frame bound
, respectively, for
\{\big|\phi_{(m,n,l)}\big>\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
.

3.1.
Frames for Weyl-Heisenberg Group

The following theorem gives sufficient conditions for a sequence of functions obtained from a unitary irreducible representation associated to the Weyl-Heisenberg group to be a Gabor frame with several generators.

Theorem 3.2
.

For each
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
, let
\phi_{l}
be a non-zero function in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
such that support of each
\phi_{l}
is contained in an interval of length
\mu_{l}
.
Assume that

(i)

p_{0}^{(l)}=\frac{2\pi}{P\mu_{l}}
,

(ii)

|q_{0}^{(l)}p_{0}^{(l)}|<2\pi
, that is,
|q_{0}^{(l)}|<|P|\mu_{l}
for some fixed
q_{0}^{(l)}
,

(iii)

For each
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\leq\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\big|\phi_{l}\big(x-nq_{0}^{(l)}\big)\big|^{2}\leq\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}},\ \ \text{for almost all}\ x\in\mathbb{R}.

Then, the collection
\{\big|\phi_{(m,n,l)}^{P,Q}\big>\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
is a multivariate frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds
\alpha_{o}
,
\beta_{o}
.

Proof.

Let
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
be arbitrary. By invoking the Parseval’s theorem, we compute

\displaystyle{}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi_{(m,n,l)}^{P,Q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(x)}e^{iPx\frac{2\pi}{P\mu_{l}}m}\phi_{l}(x+nq_{0}^{(l)})dx\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(y-nq_{0}^{(l)})}e^{i(y-nq_{0}^{(l)})\frac{2\pi}{\mu_{l}}m}\phi_{l}(y)dy\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(y-nq_{0}^{(l)})}e^{iy\frac{2\pi}{\mu_{l}}m}\phi_{l}(y)dy\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(y-nq_{0}^{(1)})\phi_{1}(x)|^{2}dx+\mu_{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(y-nq_{0}^{(2)})\phi_{2}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\dots+\mu_{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(y-nq_{0}^{(N)})\phi_{N}(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}|\phi_{1}(x+nq_{0}^{(1)})|^{2}dx+\mu_{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}|\phi_{2}(x+nq_{0}^{(2)})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\dots+\mu_{N}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}|\phi_{N}(x+nq_{0}^{(N)})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\mu_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\phi_{1}(x+nq_{0}^{(1)})|^{2}dx+\mu_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\phi_{2}(x+nq_{0}^{(2)})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\dots+\mu_{N}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\phi_{N}(x+nq_{0}^{(N)})|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\mu_{2}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle+\dots+\mu_{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\geq\mu_{1}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\mu_{2}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\dots+\mu_{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\dots+\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\alpha_{o}}{N\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle=\alpha_{o}\|f\|^{2}.

Similarly, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi_{(m,n,l)}^{P,Q}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\mu_{2}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\dots+\mu_{N}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\mu_{1}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\mu_{2}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\dots+\mu_{N}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}+\dots+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}\frac{\beta_{o}}{N\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq N}\mu_{i}}\|f\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\beta_{o}\|f\|^{2}.

Inequalities

3.2.
Frames for Extended Affine Group

In this section, we give existence of wavelet frames with several generators associated with extended affine group. Here, we fix a representation
\kappa^{P,Q}
of the Weyl-Heisenberg group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
; and let
u(\epsilon)=u_{0}+\frac{P}{\epsilon}
and
v(\epsilon)=v_{0}-\frac{P}{\epsilon}
be such that
u_{0}+v_{0}=Q
. Define a sequence on extended affine group,
EA
, by

\displaystyle\big|\phi_{(m,n,l)}^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}\big>=\zeta_{\epsilon}^{u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}(a,b,y)\phi_{l},

where
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
and
l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}
and the symbol
\zeta_{\epsilon}^{\bullet,\bullet}(.,.,.)
is defined in Section
2.3
. The following result gives multivariate wavelet frames for signal space
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, for any
\epsilon\in(0,1]
, let
EA_{q_{0}(l),p_{0}(l)}(\epsilon)
be a discrete subset of
EA
given by

\displaystyle\Big\{Q_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon),b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon),y_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)|\ m,n\in\mathbb{Z},l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}\Big\},

where

\displaystyle a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=e^{-\epsilon nq_{0}^{(l)}},

\displaystyle b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)m\beta_{o}}{\alpha_{o}v(\epsilon)(ln(\epsilon+c)-lnc)c\mu_{l}},

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta_{o}e^{-\epsilon nq_{0}^{(l)}}\pi m}{\alpha_{o}v(\epsilon)(ln(\epsilon+c)-lnc)c\mu_{l}}

\displaystyle y_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)

\displaystyle=b_{mn}(\epsilon)\frac{l_{n}a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}{a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)-1}.

Then, the collection
\big\{\big|\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\big>\big\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}=\Big\{\zeta_{\epsilon}^{u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}(a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon),\beta_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon),\gamma_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon))(T_{\epsilon}\phi)\Big\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
,
where
T_{\epsilon}(x)=e^{\frac{-1}{2}\epsilon x}
, forms a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{o}^{2}c}{\beta_{o}}\Big(\frac{ln(\epsilon+c)-lnc}{\epsilon}\Big)\ \quad\text{and}\ \quad\alpha_{o}c\Big(\frac{ln(\epsilon+c)-lnc}{\epsilon}\Big).

Proof.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we compute

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\big\langle f|\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\big\rangle\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(x)}e^{iv(\epsilon)b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)e^{-\epsilon x}}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(x-\frac{lna_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}{\epsilon})}\phi_{l}(x-\frac{lna_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}{\epsilon})dx\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\overline{f(x)}e^{iv(\epsilon)b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)e^{-\epsilon x}}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(x+nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(x+nq_{0}^{(l)})dx\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon})}e^{iv(\epsilon)b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)s}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon}+nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon}+nq_{0}^{(l)})\frac{ds}{\epsilon s}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon})}e^{iv(\epsilon)b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)s}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon}+nq_{0}^{(l)})\frac{ds}{\epsilon\sqrt{s}}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{E_{l}}\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{t(a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon))}^{-1}}{\epsilon})}e^{iv(\epsilon)b_{mn}^{(l)}(\epsilon)\frac{t}{a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{t}}{\epsilon})\frac{dt}{\epsilon\sqrt{a_{n}(\epsilon)t}}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\int_{E_{l}}\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{t(a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon))}^{-1}}{\epsilon})}e^{\frac{iB_{o}\pi m}{A_{o}(\ln{\epsilon+c}-\ln{c})\mu_{l}c}}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{t}}{\epsilon})\frac{dt}{\epsilon\sqrt{a_{n}(\epsilon)t}}\bigg|^{2}

\displaystyle=(\star\star),

where
E_{l}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with finite measure depending on
\mu_{l}

(l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\})
.

By invoking the Parseval’s theorem, we get that

\displaystyle(\star\star)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}c\mu_{l}(\ln{\epsilon+c}-\ln{c})}{B_{o}}\underset{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\int_{E_{l}}\bigg|\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{t(a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon))}^{-1}}{\epsilon})}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{t}}{\epsilon})\frac{1}{\epsilon\sqrt{a_{n}(\epsilon)t}}\bigg|^{2}dt

(3.5)

\displaystyle=\sum\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}c\mu_{l}(\ln{\epsilon+c}-\ln{c})}{\beta_{o}\epsilon}\underset{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\bigg|\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{t(a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon))}^{-1}}{\epsilon})}e^{\frac{-\epsilon}{2}(nq_{0}^{(l)})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{t}}{\epsilon})\bigg|^{2}\frac{1}{\epsilon t}dt.

Change of variables
s=\frac{t}{a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}
and then
x=\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon}
, in Eq. (
3.2
), gives

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\big|\big\langle f|\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\big\rangle\big|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}(\ln{(\epsilon+c})-\ln{c})c\mu_{l}}{\beta_{o}\epsilon}\underset{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\bigg|\overline{f(\frac{-\ln{s}}{\epsilon})}\phi_{l}(\frac{-\ln{s}a_{n}^{(l)}(\epsilon)}{\epsilon})\bigg|^{2}\frac{1}{\epsilon s}ds

(3.6)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{N}\frac{\alpha_{o}(\ln{(\epsilon+c})-\ln{c})c\mu_{l}}{B_{o}\epsilon}\underset{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{+}}\big|\overline{f(x)}\phi_{l}(x+nq_{0}^{(l)})\big|^{2}dx.

Using Eq. (
3.2
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{o}^{2}(ln{(\epsilon+c)}-\ln{c})c||f||^{2}}{\beta_{o}\epsilon}\leq\sum_{l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}\bigg|\big\langle f|\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\big\rangle\bigg|^{2}\leq\frac{\alpha_{o}(ln{(\epsilon+c)}-\ln{c})c||f||^{2}}{\epsilon},

for all

\{\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z},l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
is a frame for

with the desired frame bounds.
∎

Next theorem gives us the relation between frames with several generators for Weyl-Heisenberg group and extended affine group.

Theorem 3.8
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.2
, for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}|\langle f,\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}|\langle f,\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\leq\beta_{o}||f||^{2}

Proof.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}|\langle f,\phi^{\epsilon,u(\epsilon),v(\epsilon)}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\alpha_{o}(\ln{(\epsilon+c)}-\ln{c})c||f||^{2}}{\epsilon}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{\alpha_{o}c}{\epsilon+c}||f||^{2}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{o}||f||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\underset{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\sum}|\langle f,\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\beta_{o}||f||^{2}.

This concludes the proof.
∎

4.
A Perturbation Result

The importance of perturbation theory is well-known and has been extensively studied, see
[
17
]
. For a variety of perturbation results for different types of frames, we refer to
[
2
,
9
,
13
]
. In this section, we give Paley-Wiener type perturbation results for multivariate frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. The following result shows that frames for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
associated with the Weyl-Heisenberg group are stable under small perturbation.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\big\{\big|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\big>\big\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
be a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds
\alpha_{o}
and
\beta_{o}
and let
\big\{\big|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\big>\big\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. For non-negative real number
\lambda
and
M
with
\lambda<\alpha_{o}
, assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}-\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

(4.1)

\displaystyle\leq M\min\Big\{\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2},\ \sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\Big\}+\lambda||f||^{2},

for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
. Then,
\big\{\big|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\big>\big\}_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}\atop l\in\{1,2,\dots,N\}}
is a frame for
L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
with frame bounds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(M+1)}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_{o}}\Big)\alpha_{o}\quad\text{and}\quad\Big(2\beta_{o}(M+1)+\lambda\Big).

Proof.

By hypothesis (
4.1
), for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}-\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}+2\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2M\min\Big(\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2},\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\Big)+\lambda||f||^{2}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(2M+2)\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}+\lambda||f||^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2(M+1)\beta_{o}||f||^{2}+\lambda||f||^{2}

(4.2)

\displaystyle=\big(2\beta_{o}(M+1)+\lambda\big)||f||^{2}.

Similarly, using (
4.1
), for any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx)
, we can show that

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}-\lambda||f||^{2}\leq(2M+2)\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2},

which entails

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_{o}}\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\phi^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\leq 2(M+1)\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}.

That is

(4.3)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2(M+1)}\big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\alpha_{o}}\big)\alpha_{o}||f||^{2}\leq\sum_{l=1}^{N}\sum_{m,n\in\mathbb{Z}}|\langle f|\tilde{\phi}^{P,Q}_{(m,n,l)}\rangle|^{2}\ \text{for all}\ f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},dx).

Hence, conclusion follows from (
4
) and (
4.3
).
∎

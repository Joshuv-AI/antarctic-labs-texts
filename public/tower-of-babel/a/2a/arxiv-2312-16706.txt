A canonical generator for congruence ideals of Hida families
Alexandre Maksoud

Abstract
. We construct canonical adjoint p p -adic L L -functions generating the congruence ideal attached to Hida families using Ohta’s pairing. We show that these p p -adic L L -functions, suitably modified by certain Euler factors, are interpolated by a regular element of Hida’s universal ordinary Hecke algebra. We also relate them to characteristic series of primitive adjoint Selmer groups.

11R27.

1.
Introduction

In the context of
L
-functions, the notion of period often refers to Deligne’s period for the value of a motivic
L
-function at a critical integer. When the
L
-function has an automorphic origin, its special values can sometimes be related to automorphic periods. Consider an elliptic cuspidal newform
f
of weight
k\geq 2
and level
N
, and let
L(\ad f,s)
be the
L
-function attached to the adjoint lift of the automorphic representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})
generated by
f
. A theorem of Sturm
[
Stu80
]
implies that the ratio

(1)

\dfrac{L(\ad f,j)}{\pi^{2j+k-1}\langle f,f\rangle}

is an algebraic number for all odd integers
1\leq j\leq k-1
, where
\langle f,f\rangle
is the Petersson norm of
f
for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
. The same automorphic period
\langle f,f\rangle
is involved in algebraicity statements on special values of the Rankin-Selberg
L
-function
L(f\times g,s)
, where
g
is another newform of weight smaller than
k
.

Let
p\geq 5
be a prime number. When
f
is
p
-ordinary, Schmidt
[
Sch88
]
constructed a
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\ad f,s)
interpolating (up to simple fudge factors) quantities like (
1
) or twisted variants thereof. Hida showed in
[
Hid90
]
that this construction could be realized for the primitive Hida family
F
of
f
, yielding a two-variable
p
-adic
L
-function
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\ad F,\kappa,s)
,
\kappa
being the weight variable.
However, Schmidt-Hida’s construction does not quite fit into Coates-Perrin-Riou’s framework of
p
-adic
L
-functions of motives developed in
[
CPR89
]
. Indeed, the motivic periods for
\ad f
and its critical twists should involve the product
\Omega_{f}^{+}\Omega_{f}^{-}
of
p
-normalized Shimura periods of
f
rather than
\langle f,f\rangle
.
This problem of normalizations of periods is perhaps better directly seen on (
1
) for
j=1
, which turns out to be an explicit number depending on
f
in an elementary way and does not seem to capture deep algebraic invariants such as the size of a Bloch-Kato Selmer group.
This observation suggests that
\mathcal{L}_{p}(\ad F,\kappa,s)
is the ratio of two ”genuine“ (or ”motivic“)
p
-adic
L
-functions
L_{p}(\ad F,\kappa,s)
and
L_{p}(\ad F,\kappa)
interpolating (up to simple fudge factors)

(2)

\dfrac{L(\ad f,j)}{\pi^{2j+k-1}\Omega_{f}^{+}\Omega_{f}^{-}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dfrac{\langle f,f\rangle}{\Omega_{f}^{+}\Omega_{f}^{-}}

respectively.
The second one should also generate the congruence ideal of
F
in most cases. By Hida’s formula mentioned above,
\pi^{k+1}\langle f,f\rangle
is essentially equal to
L(\ad f,1)
, and it is therefore reasonable to call
L_{p}(\ad F,\kappa)
the (weight-variable) adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function of
F
.
These expectations are not new; a similar picture for Rankin-Selberg
L
-series and their
p
-adic counterparts is drawn by Hida in his monograph
[
Hid96
]
where many evidences are provided in the CM case.

This article focuses on the construction of
L_{p}(\ad F,\kappa)
and the study of its arithmetic properties.
Hida
[
Hid88a
]
gave first evidences for the existence of
L_{p}(\ad F,\kappa)
by showing in certain cases that any generator of the congruence ideal of
F
, evaluated at
f
, is essentially equal to the second ratio of (
2
) (see also
[
Hid16
, Corollary 6.29]
).
A construction of an adjoint
p
-adic
L
-function (or, rather, an invertible sheaf) on the Eigencurve of tame level
N
was carried out by Bellaïche
[
Bel21
]
using Kim’s scalar product on overconvergent modular symbols.
Its vanishing locus is intimately related to the ramification locus of the weight map, which constitutes a geometric counterpart to the support of the congruence module.
Note that Bellaïche’s interpolation formula is only given at crystalline points of trivial Nebentype. Locally on the Eigencurve, the Shimura periods can be normalized in a coherent way at all arithmetic points, with a controllable
p
-adic error term. This approach has been generalized to other settings in recent works by Balasubramanyam-Longo, Lee, Wu and Lee-Wu
[
BL20
,
Lee21
,
Wu21
,
LW23
]
.

Theorem A

(=Theorem
2.3.7
)
.

Assume
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{F}

In the above formula,

and
\beta
are the roots of the

Let
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{F}

To

Under (

-free of rank 1, where the superscript

The choice of a
\mathbb{T}
-basis

of

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. Shimura periods for the newform

In order to construct

Since members of

contribute to ordinary cohomology instead of anti-ordinary cohomology, we make use of the Atkin-Lehner involution, which is known by Hida to
transports integral structures between ordinary and anti-ordinary cohomology (see Proposition

Theorem B
.

There exists a regular element

We next relate adjoint
p
-adic
L
-functions to their algebraic counterparts called Selmer groups. Let

be a primitive Hida family whose residual Galois representation satisfies (

) and let

is defined as the kernel of the global-to-local restriction map

\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{Q},D_{\ad})\longrightarrow\prod_{\ell\neq p}\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{\ell},D_{\ad})\times\mathrm{H}^{1}(I_{p},D^{-}_{\ad}),

where

denotes the inertia subgroup at a prime

is a finitely generated

-module. Since

-module

which we denote by

In the next theorem, we make a slightly stronger assumption than (

) on residual Galois representations:

(CR’)

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
is

-distinguished, and
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{|G_{\mathbb{Q}(\mu_{p})}}

We also need a certain finite set of exceptional primes, defined as

(3)

\Sigma_{e}:=\left\{\ell\colon\pi_{f,\ell}\textrm{ is supercuspidal and }\pi_{f,\ell}\simeq\pi_{f,\ell}\otimes\tau_{\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}^{2}}\right\},

where
\pi_{f,\ell}
is the automorphic representation of

of any arithmetic specialization

Theorem C
.

Assume that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{F}
satisfies (

). The Selmer group

is a torsion

\textrm{char}_{\mathbb{I}}\Sel(\ad F)^{\vee}=\prod_{\ell\in\Sigma_{e}}(1+\ell^{-1})^{-1}L_{p}(\ad F).\mathbb{I}

Recall that

Note that

isn’t, so twist-minimality is a necessary hypothesis in Theorem

However, the relation between adjoint

[

Under assumption (

turns out to be the universal ring

for

\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\rho\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

2.1.
Adjoint
L

We fix in this paragraph an integer
M\geq 3
. For
\Gamma=\Gamma_{1}(M)
, we let
S_{k}(M)
be the space of modular cusp forms of weight
k\geq 1
and level
\Gamma
. Recall the usual slash operator in weight
k
defined by

(f|s)(z)=\det(s)^{k-1}(cz+d)^{-k}f(\frac{az+b}{cz+d})

for every function
f\colon\mathscr{H}\to\mathbb{C}
on the upper half-plane and every
s=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in S^{+}=M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})^{+}
.

The abstract Hecke ring
\mathcal{H}(S^{+},\Gamma)
consisting of double cosets
[\Gamma s\Gamma]
,
s\in S^{+}
acts on
S_{k}(M)
. For
n,d\geq 1
with
(d,M)=1
, we denote by
T_{n}
,
T^{*}_{n}
,
\langle d\rangle
the usual Hecke operators, adjoint Hecke operators and diamond operators respectively. The Atkin-Lehner involution is
W_{M}=[\Gamma\tau_{M}\Gamma]
, where

\tau_{M}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
M&0\end{pmatrix}.

Note that
\tau_{M}
normalizes
\Gamma
, and
T_{n}W_{M}=W_{M}T^{*}_{n}
,
T^{*}_{n}W_{M}=W_{M}T_{n}
and
\langle d\rangle W_{M}=W_{M}\langle d\rangle^{-1}
.

The Petersson scalar product is defined by the formula

\langle f,g\rangle_{\Gamma}=\int_{X(\mathbb{C})}f(z)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug(z)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muy^{k-2}dxdy\qquad(z=x+iy),

where
f,g\in S_{k}(M)
and
X=X_{1}(M)_{/\mathbb{C}}
is the compactified modular curve of level
\Gamma
. The adjoint of
[\Gamma s\Gamma]
for
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\Gamma}
is
[\Gamma s^{\prime}\Gamma]
, where
s^{\prime}=\det(s)s^{-1}
,
i.e.
,

s^{\prime}=\begin{pmatrix}d&-b\\
-c&a\end{pmatrix}\qquad\mbox{if }s=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

The operators
T_{n}
and
T_{n}^{*}
(resp.
\langle d\rangle
, and
\langle d\rangle^{-1}
) are adjoint to each other under
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\Gamma}
.

The Nebetype action decomposes
S_{k}(M)
as a direct sum
\oplus S_{k}(M,\chi)
indexed on the set of Dirichlet characters
\chi\colon(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
and which is Hecke-compatible.

Let
f(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in S_{k}(M,\chi)
be a normalized eigenform (
i.e.
,
a_{1}=1
and
f|T_{n}=a_{n}f
for all
n\geq 1
). For every prime
\ell
, let
\alpha_{\ell}
and
\beta_{\ell}
be such that

(1-\alpha_{\ell}X)(1-\beta_{\ell}X)=1-a_{\ell}X+\chi(\ell)\ell^{k-1}X^{2},

where
\chi(\ell)=0
if
\ell|M
. Define

L^{\text{imp}}(s,\ad f)=\prod_{\ell}(1-\frac{\alpha_{\ell}^{2}}{\chi(\ell)\ell^{k-1}}\ell^{-s})(1-\frac{\alpha_{\ell}\beta_{\ell}}{\chi(\ell)\ell^{k-1}}\ell^{-s})(1-\frac{\beta_{\ell}^{2}}{\chi(\ell)\ell^{k-1}}\ell^{-s}),

the product being taken over all prime numbers
\ell
. The product converges for
\Re(s)>>0
and it has a meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
. When
f
is new,
L^{\textrm{imp}}(s,\ad f)
coincides, up to Euler factors at certain primes

, with the
L
-function
L(\ad f,s)
attached to the adjoint lift of the automorphic representation of
\GL_{2}(\mathbb{A})
generated by
f
.

Proposition 2.1.1
.

Assume

is a newform of level

\chi
, and let

This follows from
[

\displaystyle\langle f,f\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\dfrac{(k-1)!M\varphi(M)}{2^{2k}\pi^{k+1}}\prod_{\ell\in\Sigma_{e}}(1+\ell^{-1})L(\ad f,1)

\displaystyle=M\varphi(M)2^{1-k}\prod_{\ell\in\Sigma_{e}}(1+\ell^{-1})\Gamma(\ad f,1)L(\ad f,1),

where

is the set of exceptional primes for

of the introduction and
\Gamma(\ad f,s)=\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s+k-1)\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)
(and
\Gamma_{\mathbb{C}}(s)=2(2\pi)^{-s}\Gamma(s)
,
\Gamma_{\mathbb{R}}(s)=\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)
) is the Gamma factor of the motive attached to

(see
[

, Lemma 2.12]
).

We recall the definition of Shimura periods attached to Hecke eigenforms. For a subring

of
\mathbb{C}
, consider the local system

on

associated with the

-module
\textrm{TSym}^{k-2}(A^{\oplus 2})
. This latter module consists of homogeneous polynomials of degree

of the form

with
a_{i}\in A
for all
0\leq i\leq k-2
. For such a polynomial

, we let
s\in S=M_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cap\GL_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
act on

via
(P|s)(X,Y)=P((X\ Y)(s^{\prime})^{\textrm{tr}})
,
i.e.
,

(P|s)(X,Y)=P(dX-bY,-cX+aY)\qquad\mbox{if }s=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

As
\Gamma
is torsion-free,
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),V_{k}(A))
can be identified with the parabolic cohomology group
\mathrm{H}_{P}^{1}(\Gamma,\textrm{TSym}^{k-2}(A^{\oplus 2}))
. It comes equipped with an action of the abstract Hecke algebra
\mathcal{H}(S,\Gamma)
(
[
Hid93
, §6.3]
).

For a given

, we define classes in
\mathrm{H}^{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),V_{k}(\mathbb{C}))
by the formulae

\delta(f)(\gamma)=\int_{z_{0}}^{\gamma z_{0}}(X-zY)^{k-2}f(z)\diff\!z,\ \ \delta^{c}(f)=-\int_{z_{0}}^{\gamma z_{0}}(X-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muY)^{k-2}\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muf^{c}(z)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\diff\!\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu,

where
z_{0}\in X(\mathbb{C})
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
and
f^{c}(z)=\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muf(-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu
. The cohomology classes do not depend on the choice of
z_{0}
. The action of the complex conjugation
[\Gamma\iota\Gamma]
, where
\iota=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, sends
\delta(f)
to
\delta^{c}(f)
. The Eichler-Shimura period map is the
\mathbb{C}
-linear isomorphism

(4)

\begin{array}[]{ccc}S_{k}(M)\oplus S_{k}(M)&\overset{\sim}{\longrightarrow}&\mathrm{H}^{1}(X(\mathbb{C}),V_{k}(\mathbb{C}))\\
(f,g)&\mapsto&\delta(f)+\delta^{c}(g).\end{array}

Take

and
s=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in S^{+}
. From

(X-(s\cdot z)Y)^{k-2}|s^{\prime}=\left(\frac{\det s}{cz+d}\right)^{k-2}(X-zY)^{k-2}\quad\mbox{and}\quad f^{c}|s=(f|\iota s\iota)^{c},

one sees that

\delta(f)|[\Gamma s\Gamma]=\delta(f|[\Gamma s\Gamma])\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\delta^{c}(f)|[\Gamma s\Gamma]=\delta^{c}(f|[\Gamma\iota s\iota\Gamma]).

In particular, (

) is equivariant with respect to the actions of

and
\langle d\rangle
for
n,d\geq 1
,
(M,d)=1
.

For any ring

, we put

and we consider the Hecke algebras

.

-algebras. If

. For

, the map

identifies

with
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)_{A}^{\pm}
, where we have put

\delta^{\pm}(f)=\delta(f)\pm\delta^{c}(f).

As

admits a basis consisting of modular forms with Fourier coefficients in

, for every subring

we have

as
\mathfrak{h}_{k}(M)_{\mathbb{C}}=\mathbb{C}\otimes_{A}\mathfrak{h}_{k}(M)_{A}
-modules, where

S_{k}(M)_{A}=\{f\in S_{k}(M)\ \colon\ f(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in A\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}[[q]]\}.

Recall that
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu\subset\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}

the system of Hecke eigenvalues of

given by

and
\lambda_{f}(\langle d\rangle)=\chi(d)
for all

,

.

For any field

-eigenspace of

.
Using the maps

in (

Let

be the completion of

inside
\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}\simeq\mathbb{C}
. The image of
\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}

-lattice, which we still denote by

with a slight abuse of notations. As long as we deal with ordinary eigenforms, this won’t cause any problem as the ordinary part of

Definition 2.1.3
.

Let

be a normalized eigenform. Let

-basis of

\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}\cap\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)^{\pm}_{\mathbb{C}}[\lambda_{f}].

Remark 2.1.4

One can actually replace the coefficient ring

in the above definition with any PID

(see e.g.

We now recall the cohomological interpretation of the Petersson norm of normalized eigenforms. Let

be a ring, and consider the nondegenerate

-linear pairing
[\cdot,\cdot]
on

defined in

, (4.2.1)]
. It is characterized by the identity

[(uX+vY)^{k-2},(u^{\prime}X+v^{\prime}Y)^{k-2}]=(uv^{\prime}-u^{\prime}v)^{k-2}\qquad(u,u^{\prime},v,v^{\prime}\in A).

For
s\in S
and
P,Q\in\textrm{TSym}^{k-2}(A^{\oplus 2})
, one checks that
[P|s,Q|s]=(\det s)^{k-2}[P,Q]
. Combining
[\cdot,\cdot]
with the cup-product in cohomology we obtain a new

-linear pairing

(8)

(\cdot,\cdot)_{\pi}\colon\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)_{A}\times\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(M)_{A}\to\mathrm{H}^{2}(X(\mathbb{C}),A)\simeq A,

where the last isomorphism is the cap-product with the fundamental class of

, endowed with its natural orientation of complex manifold. For

, the adjoint of
[\Gamma s\Gamma]
under

is
[\Gamma s^{\prime}\Gamma]
, so for the modified anti-symmetric pairing

(9)

(x,y)\mapsto(x,y|W_{M})_{\pi},

the Hecke algebras
\mathfrak{h}_{k}(M)_{A}
and
\mathfrak{h}^{*}_{k}(M)_{A}
become autoadjoint.

Proposition 2.1.5
.

Let
f,g\in S_{k}(M)
. We have

\displaystyle(\delta(f),\delta^{c}(g))_{\pi}

\displaystyle=(2i)^{k-1}\langle f,g^{c}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle(\delta^{+}(f),\delta^{-}(g)|W_{M})_{\pi}

\displaystyle=-2(2i)^{k-1}\langle f,g^{c}|W_{M}\rangle_{\Gamma}.

Proof.

We start with the first equality. Over
\mathbb{C}
, the cap-product with the fundamental class of
X(\mathbb{C})
is given by the integration over
X(\mathbb{C})
of cohomology classes seen as
2
-differential forms. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\delta(f),\delta^{c}(g))_{\pi}

\displaystyle=-\int_{X(\mathbb{C})}f(z)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug^{c}(z)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu[(X-zY)^{k-2},(X-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muY)^{k-2}]dz\wedge d\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu

\displaystyle=2i\int_{X(\mathbb{C})}f(z)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug^{c}(z)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu(z-\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5muz\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu)^{k-2}dxdy

\displaystyle=(2i)^{k-1}\int_{X(\mathbb{C})}f(z)\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mug^{c}(z)\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5muy^{k-2}dxdy.

This proves the first formula. A similar computation as above yields

\displaystyle(\delta(h),\delta(h^{\prime}))_{\pi}

\displaystyle=0=(\delta^{c}(h),\delta^{c}(h^{\prime}))_{\pi},

\displaystyle(\delta(h),\delta^{c}(h^{\prime}))_{\pi}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}(\delta^{c}(h^{\prime}),\delta(h))_{\pi},

for any
h,h^{\prime}\in S_{k}(M)
, and from (
5
) we find
\delta^{-}(g)|W_{M}=\delta(g|W_{M})+(-1)^{k-1}\delta^{c}(g|W_{M})
. Hence, by linearity,

\displaystyle(\delta^{+}(f),\delta^{-}(g)|W_{M})_{\pi}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{k-1}\left((\delta(f),\delta^{c}(g|W_{M}))_{\pi}+(\delta(g|W_{M}),\delta^{c}(f))_{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle=(-2i)^{k-1}\left(\langle f,(g|W_{M})^{c}\rangle_{\Gamma}+\langle g|W_{M},f^{c}\rangle_{\Gamma}\right)

\displaystyle=2(-2i)^{k-1}\langle f,(g|W_{M})^{c}\rangle_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=-2(2i)^{k-1}\langle f,g^{c}|W_{M}\rangle_{\Gamma},

since
(g|W_{M})^{c}=(-1)^{k}g^{c}|W_{M}
. This proves the second formula.
∎

2.2.
Hida families

Let
p
be an odd prime and let
M
be of the form
Np^{r}
with
(N,p)=1
and
r\geq 1
. We let
X_{r}=X(\Gamma_{1}(Np^{r}))_{/\mathbb{C}}
. We shall consider the ordinary and anti-ordinary parts of the (co-)homology of

, defined with the help of Hida’s projectors

e=\lim_{r}(T_{p})^{r!},\qquad e^{*}=\lim_{r}(T^{*}_{p})^{r!}.

In what follows, we drop for convenience the subscript
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
when we work with cohomology with
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-coefficients.

Proposition 2.2.1

(Hida)
.

The Atkin-Lehner map

\cdot|W_{Np^{r}}\colon e\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(Np^{r})\to e^{*}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(Np^{r})

is a
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-linear isomorphism, and
e\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(Np^{r})
and
e^{*}\mathrm{H}^{1}_{k}(Np^{r})
are
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
-free.

Proof.

See
[
Hid88a
, Corollary 3.2]
and
[
Hid88a
, (2.4) p.347]
.
∎

In particular, we have
e\mathfrak{h}(Np^{r})=e^{*}\mathfrak{h}^{*}(Np^{r})
via
T_{n}\leftrightarrow T^{*}_{n}
.
Poincaré duality yields a canonical identification

\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{r}(\mathbb{C}),{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{r}(\mathbb{C}),{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}),

which is compatible with the Hecke action, in the sense that the action of
T_{n}
and
\langle d\rangle
on the left hand side corresponds to that of
T_{n}^{*}
and
\langle d\rangle^{-1}
on the right hand side respectively. By functoriality of (co)homology, we obtain

\varprojlim_{r}e\mathrm{H}_{1}(X_{r}(\mathbb{C}),{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=\varprojlim_{r}e^{*}\mathrm{H}^{1}(X_{r}(\mathbb{C}),{\mathbb{Z}_{p}})=:e^{*}\mathrm{H}^{1}(Np^{\infty}),

as Hecke-modules, where the transition maps on cohomology are the trace maps. Since the adjoint Hecke operators are compatible with trace maps in cohomology,
e^{*}\mathrm{H}^{1}(Np^{\infty})
inherits a structure of module over

e^{*}\mathfrak{h}^{*}(Np^{\infty})=\varprojlim_{r}e^{*}\mathfrak{h}_{2}^{*}(Np^{r}).

We will primarily be interested in
e^{*}\mathfrak{h}^{*}(Np^{\infty})
, but note that it is canonically isomorphic to
e\mathfrak{h}(Np^{\infty})=\varprojlim_{r}e\mathfrak{h}_{2}(Np^{r})
.

The action of inverse diamond operators endows the ring
e^{*}\mathfrak{h}(Np^{\infty})
with a structure of algebra over
\tilde{\Lambda}=\varprojlim_{r}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[(\mathbb{Z}/Np^{r})^{\times}]
. Letting
U_{r}=1+p^{r}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
for all
r\geq 1
, we have
\tilde{\Lambda}=\Lambda[(\mathbb{Z}/Np)^{\times}]
where we put

\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[[U_{1}]]=\varprojlim_{r}{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}[U_{1}/U_{r}],

and
e^{*}\mathfrak{h}(Np^{\infty})
is known to be finite free over
\Lambda
by
[
Hid86
, Thm. 3.1]
.

Let
\mathbb{I}
be a finite flat extension of
\Lambda
and let
\gamma
be a topological generator of
U_{1}
. We identify via
\mathbb{C}\simeq\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}
Dirichlet characters of
p
-power order and conductor with elements of
\widehat{U_{1}}:=\Hom_{\textrm{cts}}(U_{1},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p}^{\times})=\Hom_{{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}-\textrm{alg}}(\Lambda,\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})
. The set of arithmetic points of
\mathbb{I}
is defined as

\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{I})=\left\{\nu\in\Hom_{\Lambda}(\mathbb{I},\mkern 1.5mu\overline{\mkern-1.5mu\mathbb{Q}\mkern-1.5mu}\mkern 1.5mu_{p})\ |\ \exists k\geq 2,\ \exists\varepsilon\in\widehat{U_{1}},\ \nu(\gamma)=\gamma^{k-2}\epsilon(\gamma)\right\}.

We refer to the pair
(k,\epsilon)
in the definition of
\nu\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{I})
as the weight of
\nu
, and we define a finite flat extension of
{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
by letting
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}=\nu(\mathbb{I})
. We further denote by
S(N,\mathbb{I})
the space of ordinary
\mathbb{I}
-adic cusp forms of tame level
N
. Recall that
S(N,\mathbb{I})
is spanned over
\mathbb{I}
by formal
q
-expansions
F(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}q^{n}\in\mathbb{I}[[q]]
with the property that there exists a Dirichlet character
\theta
of level dividing
Np
such that, for all
\nu\in\mathcal{X}(\mathbb{I})
of weight
(k,\varepsilon)
, the specialization of

at
\nu
yields

Here,
\omega
is the Teichmüller character,

(and
C(\varepsilon)
is the conductor of
\varepsilon
), and
\mathcal{O}_{\nu}
is seen inside
\mathbb{C}
.

On

there is an action of Hecke operators

which is compatible with specialization maps. More precisely,

\displaystyle\simeq e\mathfrak{h}_{k}(Np^{r}),

by Hida’s control theorem

.

A primitive Hida family is a

-expansion

as above which is a normalized eigenform (
i.e.
,

,

for all

) and such that

is a primitive newform for at least one

. The character

. Moreover, given

. Otherwise,

,

. The kernel

, where

Let

. Then

. Let

.

We keep the notations of the preceding sections. For any

\Lambda
-module of
\mathcal{T}_{K}=e_{1}\mathcal{T}_{K}\oplus e_{2}\mathcal{T}_{K}
. The projection
\mathcal{T}_{K}\twoheadrightarrow e_{1}\mathcal{T}_{K}
is
\lambda\otimes K
, so we have
\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}=\im(\mathcal{T}\to e_{1}\mathcal{T}_{K})=\im(\lambda)=\Lambda
as
\Lambda
-modules. Hence,
M_{\lambda}
is
\Lambda
-free of rank
1
.

Since
\lambda_{|\Lambda}=\id
, we have
\mathcal{T}=\im(\lambda)\oplus\ker(\lambda)
,
i.e.
,
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{T}_{\lambda}\oplus(\mathcal{T}\cap e_{2}\mathcal{T}_{K})
inside
\mathcal{T}_{K}
. Therefore, we obtain
M=M_{\lambda}\oplus(M\cap e_{2}M_{K})
.
∎

Suppose we are given a
\Lambda
-linear pairing
(\cdot,\cdot)\colon M\times N\to\Lambda
which is perfect (
i.e.
, it induces isomorphisms
N\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
and
M\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(N,\Lambda)
) and such that

(29)

\forall t\in\mathcal{T},\ \forall(m,n)\in M\times N,\qquad(tm,n)=(m,tn).

Note that the orthogonal of
e_{1}M_{K}
(resp. of
e_{2}M_{K}
) is
e_{2}N_{K}
(resp.
e_{1}N_{K}
). As a last piece of notation, we define for any
M^{\prime}\subseteq M_{K}

(M^{\prime})^{*}:=\{n\in N_{K}\ \colon\ \forall m\in M^{\prime},\ (m,n)\in\Lambda\},

and we define similarly
(N^{\prime})^{*}
for
N^{\prime}\subseteq N_{K}
.

Proposition A.1
.

We have
(M_{\lambda})^{*}=N^{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
, and
(M^{\lambda})^{*}=N_{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
. Moreover,
(\cdot,\cdot)
induces an isomorphism
M^{\lambda}\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(N_{\lambda},\Lambda)
(and also
N^{\lambda}\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(M_{\lambda},\Lambda)
).

Proof.

We first check that
(M_{\lambda})^{*}\supseteq N^{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
. Take
n_{1}\in N
,
n_{2}\in N_{K}
and let
n=e_{1}n_{1}+e_{2}n_{2}
. For any
m\in M_{\lambda}
, we have
m=e_{1}m
, so
(m,e_{2}n_{2})=(m,e_{1}e_{2}n_{2})=0
by (
29
). Hence,

(m,n)=(m,e_{1}n_{1})=(e_{1}m,n_{1})=(m,n_{1})\in\Lambda,

as
m\in M
and
n_{1}\in N
. This shows that
n\in(M_{\lambda})^{*}
.

We now prove
(M_{\lambda})^{*}\subseteq N^{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
and we take
n\in(M_{\lambda})^{*}
. The map
m\mapsto(m,n)
defines a
\Lambda
-linear form
M_{\lambda}\to\Lambda
, which can be extended to a linear form
M\to\Lambda
by Lemma
A.1
. By perfectness of
(\cdot,\cdot)
, there exists
p\in N
such that
(\cdot,n)=(\cdot,p)_{|M_{\lambda}}
. Moreover, as
\Lambda\simeq M_{\lambda}\subset e_{1}M_{K}\simeq K
by Lemma
A.1
, we have
M_{\lambda}\otimes_{\Lambda}K=e_{1}M_{K}
. In particular,
n-p
is orthogonal to
e_{1}M_{K}
by linearity of the pairing, that is,
n-p\in e_{2}N_{K}
. Therefore,
n\in e_{1}p+e_{2}N_{K}\subset N^{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
, as wanted.

Since
e_{1}N_{\lambda}=N_{\lambda}\subset N
, we have (as sets)
(M^{\lambda},N_{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K})=(M^{\lambda},N_{\lambda})=(e_{1}M,N_{\lambda})=(M,N_{\lambda})\subset\Lambda
, which implies
(M^{\lambda})^{*}\supseteq N_{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}N_{K}
. We check the reverse inclusion and we let
n\in(M^{\lambda})^{*}
. To prove that
e_{1}n\in N
, we remark that, for all
m\in M
, we have
(m,e_{1}n)=(e_{1}m,n)\in\Lambda
, so the map
m\mapsto(m,e_{1}n)
belongs to
\Hom_{\Lambda}(M,\Lambda)
. By the perfectness of
(\cdot,\cdot)
, this means that
e_{1}n\in N
, as claimed.

It remains to prove that
m\mapsto(n\mapsto(m,n))
yields an isomorphism
M^{\lambda}\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(N_{\lambda},\Lambda)
.
We already know that
N_{\lambda}\otimes_{\Lambda}K=e_{1}N_{K}
, and it is clear from the above arguments that
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricts to a perfect pairing
e_{1}M_{K}\times e_{1}N_{K}\to K
. We thus only have to check that the inverse image of
\Hom_{\Lambda}(N_{\lambda},\Lambda)
under
e_{1}M_{K}\simeq\Hom_{\Lambda}(e_{1}N_{K},K)
, say
M^{\prime}
, is equal to
M^{\lambda}
. But we have
M^{\prime}=(N_{\lambda})^{*}\cap e_{1}M_{K}
by definition, so we conclude that
M^{\prime}=(M^{\lambda}\oplus e_{2}M_{K})\cap e_{1}M_{K}=M_{\lambda}
.
∎

Corollary A.1
.

Let
m_{0}
and
n_{0}
be generators of
M_{\lambda}
and
N_{\lambda}
over
\Lambda
respectively (see Lemma
A.1
). The
\Lambda
-linear map

\varphi\colon\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}M^{\lambda}&\to&\Lambda\\
m&\mapsto&(m,n_{0})\end{array}\right.

is an isomorphism. Consequently,
C_{0}(\lambda)\simeq C_{0}^{M}(\lambda)\simeq\Lambda/(m_{0},n_{0})\Lambda
.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition
A.1
shows that
(\cdot,\cdot)
restricts to a non-degenerate pairing
M^{\lambda}\times N_{\lambda}\to\Lambda
, so
\varphi
is well-defined and injective. Again by Proposition
A.1
, the element of
\Hom_{\Lambda}(N_{\lambda},\Lambda)
sending
n_{0}
to
1
is of the form
(m,\cdot)
for some
m\in M^{\lambda}
, and this element thus satisfies
\varphi(m)=1
. This shows that
\varphi
is an isomorphism. It is then clear that
\varphi
induces an isomorphism
M^{\lambda}/M_{\lambda}\simeq\Lambda/\varphi(m_{0})\Lambda=\Lambda/(m_{0},n_{0})\Lambda
.
∎

Abstract
. A connected and nonempty graph A A is defined as generalized t t -edge distance-balanced, while for each edge f = α ​ β f=\alpha\beta the number of edges nearer to α \alpha than β \beta are equal to t t -times of edges nearer to β \beta than to α \alpha , for t ∈ ℕ t\in\mathbb{N} , or vice versa. We determine some classes of such graphs. Moreover, we investigate edge-szeged index of the generalized t t -edge distance-balanced graphs. Also, it is discussed about their cartesian and lexicographic products.



1.
Introduction

The notion of graph is a pivotal tool to make use of the modelling of the phenomena and it is taken into consideration in many studies in a recent decades. One of the optimal uses of graphs theory is to classify graphs based on discriminating quality. This phenomenon can be best observed in distance-balanced graphs has been determined by [13]. Also, it is investigated in some papers, we refer the reader to ([1],[2],[3],[4],[5],[6],[10],[11],[14]-[19]) and references therein.

We consider
A
is a connected, finite and undirected graph throughout of this paper, in which its vertex set is
V(A)
and its edge set is
E(A)
. In graph
A
, the interval among vertices
\alpha,\beta\in V(A)
is introduced the number of edges in the least interval joining them and it is indicated by
d_{A}(\alpha,\beta)
(see [4, 18]). For every two desired vertices
\alpha,\beta
of

we indicate
n_{\alpha}^{A}(f)=|W_{a,\beta}^{A}|=|\{a\in V(A)|d_{A}(a,\alpha)<d_{A}(a,\beta)\}|
. In the same way, we would define
n_{\beta}^{A}(f)=|W_{\beta,\alpha}^{A}|
. We name
A
distance-balanced (
DB
) while for adjacent vertices
\alpha
and
\beta
of
A
, we have

|W_{\alpha,\beta}^{A}|=|W_{\beta,\alpha}^{A}|
.

For each two desired edges
f=\alpha\beta
,
\acute{f}=\acute{\alpha}\acute{\beta}
, the distance between
f
and
\acute{f}
is introduced via:

d_{A}(f,\acute{f})=\min\{d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{f}),d_{A}(\beta,\acute{f})\}=\min\{d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{\alpha}),d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{\beta}),d_{A}(\beta,\acute{\alpha}),d_{A}(\beta,\acute{\beta})\}
.

Set
M_{\alpha}^{A}(f)=\{\acute{f}\in E(A)|d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{f})<d_{A}(\beta,\acute{f})\}
and
m_{\alpha}^{A}(f)=|M_{\alpha}^{A}(f)|
,

M_{\beta}^{A}(f)=\{\acute{f}\in E(A)|d_{A}(\beta,\acute{f})<d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{f})\}
and
m_{\beta}^{A}(f)=|M_{\beta}^{A}(f)|
,

and
M_{0}^{A}(f)=\{\acute{f}\in E(A)|d_{A}(\alpha,\acute{f})=d_{A}(\beta,\acute{f})\}
and
m_{0}^{A}(f)=|M_{0}^{A}(f)|
.

Persume that
f=\alpha\beta\in E(A)
. For every two integers
i,j
we consider:

\acute{D}_{j}^{i}(f)=\{\acute{f}\in E(A)|d_{A}(\acute{f},\alpha)=i,d_{A}(\acute{f},\beta)=j\}
.

A ”distance partition” of

is concluded by sets
\acute{D}_{j}^{i}(f)
due to the edge

f=\alpha\beta
. Merely the sets
\acute{D}_{i}^{i-1}(f)
,
\acute{D}_{i}^{i}(f)
and,
\acute{D}_{i-1}^{i}(f)
, for each
(1\leqslant i\leqslant d)
might be nonempty according to the triangle inequality (The diameter of
the graph

is

.

A graph

-edge distance-balanced graph and also

t
-edge distance-balanced. In the same way, we define generalized

We also remind that the sets

become visible as the well-known Szeged index and edge-Szeged index of a graph

A graph

According to the stated definition, clearly if graph be

Proposition 2.4

A bipartite graph

t(\acute{\gamma}_{A\square B}+\gamma_{A\square B})
,

in which

\gamma_{A\square B}=n_{a_{1}}^{A}(a_{1}a_{2}).|E(B)|=n_{a_{2}}^{A}(a_{1}a_{2}).|E(B)|
,

and

\acute{\gamma}_{A\square B}=m_{a_{1}}^{A}(a_{1}a_{2}).|V(B)|=m_{a_{2}}^{A}(a_{1}a_{2}).|V(B)|
.

We conclude that from (7) that
A
and

Gt-NEDB
and
Gt-NDB

|V(B)|.\acute{\gamma}_{A}+|E(B)|.\gamma_{A}=|V(A)|.\acute{\gamma}_{B}+|E(A)|.\gamma_{B}

Example 4.4
.

A class of

.

Proof.

Lemma 4.5
.

Consider that
A
is a
Gt-SEDB
graph with diameter 2. Then
A
is a
Gt-EDB
graph for
t\in\mathbb{N}
and
t>1
.

Proof.

According to (1), we know that the graph
A
is
Gt-EDB
if and only if for each arbitrary edge
f=\alpha\beta\in E(A)
and each
i\in[1,d-1]
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\acute{D}_{i}^{i-1}(f)|=t\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\acute{D}_{i-1}^{i}(f)|+(t-1)
.

Let now
A
be a
Gt-SEDB
graph. Then for each
f=\alpha\beta\in E(A)
and each
i\in[1,d-1]
, it is obtained

|\acute{D}_{i}^{i-1}(f)|=t|\acute{D}_{i-1}^{i}(f)|+(t-1)
.

Thus,

\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\acute{D}_{i}^{i-1}(f)|=t\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}|\acute{D}_{i-1}^{i}(f)|+(t-1)(d-1)

If

|\acute{D}_{2}^{1}(f)|=t|\acute{D}_{1}^{2}(f)|+(t-1)

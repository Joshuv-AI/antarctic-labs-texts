D. Lukkassen, L.E. Persson, G. Tephnadze, G. Tutberidze

Abstract
. In this paper we derive a new strong convergence theorem of Riesz logarithmic means of the one-dimensional Vilenkin-Fourier (Walsh-Fourier) series. The corresponding inequality is pointed out and it is also proved that the inequality is in a sense sharp, at least for the case with Walsh-Fourier series.

26D10, 26D20, 42C10.

Key words and phrases:
inequalities, Vilenkin systems, Walsh system, Riesz logarithmic means, martingale Hardy space, strong convergence.

The maximal operator of Fejér means

is bounded from the Hardy space

to the space

In

\overline{\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}}\frac{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-2}\log^{2\left[1/2+p\right]}\left(n+1\right)}{\varphi\left(n\right)}=+\infty,

there exists a martingale

Theorem W1 implies that

\frac{1}{n^{2p-1}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma^{\psi}_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}^{p},\text{ \ \ \ }1/2<p<\infty,\ n=1,2,\dots.

\frac{1}{\log^{\left[1/2+p\right]}n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|\sigma^{\psi}_{k}f\right\|_{p}^{p}}{k^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}^{p},\ 0<p\leq 1/2,\ \ n=2,3,\dots.

\widetilde{R}_{p}^{\psi,\ast}:=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{\left|R_{n}^{\psi}\right|\log(n+1)}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-2}\log^{2\left[1/2+p\right]}\left(n+1\right)},

\varphi:\mathbb{N}_{+}\rightarrow[1,\infty)

(3)

there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}(G_{m}),
such that

\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left\|\frac{R^{\psi}_{n}f}{\varphi\left(n\right)}\right\|_{p}=\infty.

The main aim of this paper is to derive a new strong convergence theorem of Riesz logarithmic means of one-dimensional Vilenkin-Fourier (Walsh-Fourier)
series (see Theorem 1). The corresponding inequality is pointed out. The sharpness is proved in Theorem 2, at least for the case with Walsh-Fourier series.

The paper is organized as follows: In Section 2 some definitions and notations are presented. The main results are presented and proved in Section 3. Section 4 is reserved for some concluding remarks and open problems.

2.
Definitions and Notations

Let
\mathbb{N}_{+}
denote the set of positive integers,
\mathbb{N}:=\mathbb{N}_{+}\cup\{0\}.

Let
m:=(m_{0},m_{1},...)
denote a sequence of positive integers not
less than 2.

Denote by

Z_{m_{k}}:=\{0,1,...m_{k}-1\}

the additive group of integers modulo
m_{k}.

Define the group
G_{m}
as the complete direct product of the group
Z_{m_{j}}
with the product of the discrete topologies of
Z_{m_{j}}
’s.

The direct product
\mu
of the measures

\mu_{k}\left(\{j\}\right):=1/m_{k}\text{\qquad}(j\in Z_{m_{k}})

is a Haar measure on
G_{m}
with
\mu\left(G_{m}\right)=1.

If
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}m_{n}<\infty
, then we call
G_{m}
a bounded Vilenkin group.
If the generating sequence
m
is not bounded, then
G_{m}
is said to be an
unbounded Vilenkin group. In this paper we discuss only bounded Vilenkin groups.

The elements of
G_{m}
are represented by sequences

x:=(x_{0},x_{1},...,x_{j},...)\qquad\left(\text{ }x_{k}\in Z_{m_{k}}\right).

It is easy to give a base for the neighborhood of
G_{m}
namely

I_{0}\left(x\right):=G_{m},

I_{n}(x):=\{y\in G_{m}\mid y_{0}=x_{0},...y_{n-1}=x_{n-1}\},\text{ \ }(x\in G_{m},\text{ }n\in\mathbb{N}).

Denote
I_{n}:=I_{n}\left(0\right)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\overline{I_{n}}:=G_{m}

\backslash

I_{n}
.

Let

e_{n}:=\left(0,0,...,x_{n}=1,0,...\right)\in G_{m}\qquad\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

It is evident that

(4)

\overline{I_{M}}=\left(\overset{M-2}{\underset{k=0}{\bigcup}}\overset{m_{k}-1}{\underset{x_{k}=1}{\bigcup}}\overset{M-1}{\underset{l=k+1}{\bigcup}}\overset{m_{l}-1}{\underset{x_{l}=1}{\bigcup}}I_{l+1}\left(x_{k}e_{k}+x_{l}e_{l}\right)\right)\bigcup\left(\underset{k=1}{\bigcup\limits^{M-1}}\overset{m_{k}-1}{\underset{x_{k}=1}{\bigcup}}I_{M}\left(x_{k}e_{k}\right)\right).

If we define the so-called generalized number system based on
m
in the
following way :

M_{0}:=1,\text{ \qquad}M_{k+1}:=m_{k}M_{k\text{ }}\ \qquad(k\in\mathbb{N}),

then every
n\in\mathbb{N}
can be uniquely expressed as
n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{j}M_{j},
where
n_{j}\in Z_{m_{j}}

~(j\in\mathbb{N})
and only a finite number of
n_{j}`
s differ from zero. Let
\left|n\right|:=\max\{j\in\mathbb{N};\ \ n_{j}\neq 0\}.

The norm (or quasi-norm when
p<1
) of the space
L_{p}(G_{m})
is defined by

\left\|f\right\|_{p}:=\left(\int_{G_{m}}\left|f\right|^{p}d\mu\right)^{1/p}\qquad\left(0<p<\infty\right).

The space
weak-L_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)
consists of all measurable
functions
f
for which

\left\|f\right\|_{weak-L_{p}(G_{m})}:=\underset{\lambda>0}{\sup}\lambda^{p}\mu\left(f>\lambda\right)<+\infty.

Next, we introduce on
G_{m}
an orthonormal system which is called the
Vilenkin system.

Let us define the complex valued function
r_{k}\left(x\right):G_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{C},
the generalized Rademacher functions as

r_{k}\left(x\right):=\exp\left(2\pi ix_{k}/m_{k}\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(i^{2}=-1,\ \ \ x\in G_{m},\ \ \ k\in\mathbb{N}\right).

Now, define the Vilenkin system
\psi:=(\psi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N})
on
G_{m}
as:

\psi_{n}(x):=\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\Pi}}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)\text{ \qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).

The Vilenkin systems are orthonormal and complete in
L_{2}\left(G_{m}\right)
(for details see e.g
[
1
]
).

Specifically, we call this system the Walsh-Paley one
if
m_{k}=2,\ \text{for all}\ k\in\mathbb{N}.
In this case we have dyadic group
G_{2}=\prod_{j=0}^{\infty}Z_{2},
which is called the Walsh group and Vilenkin system coincides Walsh functions defined by (for details see e.g.
[
17
]
and
[
27
]
)

w_{n}(x):=\underset{k=0}{\overset{\infty}{\prod}}r_{k}^{n_{k}}\left(x\right)=r_{\left|n\right|}\left(x\right)\left(-1\right)^{\underset{k=0}{\overset{\left|n\right|-1}{\sum}}n_{k}x_{k}}\text{\qquad}\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right),

where
n_{k}=0\vee 1
and
x_{k}=0\vee 1.

Now, we introduce analogues of the usual definitions in Fourier-analysis.

If
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right),
then we can establish the Fourier
coefficients, the partial sums of the Fourier series, the Fejér means, the
Dirichlet and Fejér kernels with respect to the Vilenkin system
\psi
(Walsh system
w
) in
the usual manner:

\displaystyle\widehat{f}^{\alpha}\left(k\right)

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\int_{G_{m}}f\overline{\alpha}_{k}d\mu,\text{\ \ \ \ \ \ }(\alpha_{k}=w_{k}\text{ or }\psi_{k}),\qquad\left(k\in\mathbb{N}\right),

\displaystyle S^{\alpha}_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{f}\left(k\right)\alpha_{k},\qquad(\alpha_{k}=w_{k}\text{ or }\psi_{k}),\text{ \ \ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},\ S^{\alpha}_{0}f:=0\right),

\displaystyle\sigma^{\alpha}_{n}f

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}S^{\alpha}_{k}f,\text{ \ \ \ \ \ \ }(\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right),

\displaystyle D^{\alpha}_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\alpha_{k},\text{ \qquad\ \ \ \ \ }(\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right),

\displaystyle K^{\alpha}_{n}

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\overset{n-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}D^{\alpha}_{k},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),\text{ \qquad\ \ }\left(n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\right).

It is well-known that (see e.g.
[
1
]
)

(5)

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|K^{\alpha}_{n}\right|d\mu\leq c<\infty,\ \ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ \ \alpha=w\text{ or }\psi.

The
\sigma
-algebra generated by the intervals
\left\{I_{n}\left(x\right):x\in G_{m}\right\}
will be denoted by
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right).
Denote by
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
a martingale with respect to
\digamma_{n}

\left(n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
(for details see e.g.
[
5
]
,
[
23
]
,
[
45
]
). The maximal
function of a martingale
f
is defend by

f^{\ast}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|f^{\left(n\right)}\right|.

In the case
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}),
the maximal functions are also given by

f^{\ast}\left(x\right)=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\frac{1}{\left|I_{n}\left(x\right)\right|}\left|\int_{I_{n}\left(x\right)}f\left(u\right)\mu\left(u\right)\right|.

For
0<p<\infty
the Hardy martingale spaces
H_{p}\left(G_{m}\right)

consist of all martingales for which

\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}:=\left\|f^{\ast}\right\|_{p}<\infty.

If
f\in L_{1}(G_{m}),
then it is easy to show that the sequence
\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale. If
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is a martingale, then the Vilenkin-Fourier (Walsh-Fourier) coefficients must be defined in a slightly different
manner, namely

\widehat{f}\left(i\right):=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\int_{G_{m}}f^{\left(k\right)}\left(x\right)\overline{\alpha}_{i}\left(x\right)d\mu\left(x\right),\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ \alpha=w\text{ \ or \ }\psi.

The Vilenkin-Fourier coefficients of
f\in L_{1}\left(G_{m}\right)
are
the same as those of the martingale

\left(S_{M_{n}}f:n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
obtained from
f.

In the literature, there is the notion of Riesz logarithmic means of the
Fourier series. The
n
-th Riesz logarithmic means of the Fourier series
of an integrable function
f
is defined by

R^{\alpha}_{n}f:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{S^{\alpha}_{k}f}{k},\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ \alpha=w\ \text{or }\psi,

with

l_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{1}{k}.

The kernels of Riesz‘s logarithmic means are defined by

L^{\alpha}_{n}:=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{D^{\alpha}_{k}}{k},\ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi).

For the martingale
f
we consider the following maximal operators:

\displaystyle\sigma^{\alpha,\ast}f:\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left|\sigma^{\alpha}_{n}f\right|,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),

\displaystyle R^{\ast}f:=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\left|R^{\alpha}_{n}f\right|,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{R}^{\alpha,\ast}f:=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\left|R^{\alpha}_{n}f\right|}{\log\left(n+1\right)},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi),

\displaystyle\overset{\sim}{R}_{p}^{\alpha,\ast}f:=\underset{n\in\mathbb{N}}{\sup}\frac{\log\left(n+1\right)\left|R^{\alpha}_{n}f\right|}{\left(n+1\right)^{1/p-2}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ (\alpha=w\text{ or }\psi).

A bounded measurable function
a
is a
p
-atom, if there exist an
interval
I
, such that

\int_{I}ad\mu=0,\text{ \ }\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq\mu\left(I\right)^{-1/p},\text{\ \ supp}\left(a\right)\subset I.

In order to prove our main results we need the following lemma of Weisz (for details see e.g. Weisz
[
48
]
):

Proposition 1
.

A martingale
f=\left(f^{\left(n\right)},n\in\mathbb{N}\right)
is in
H_{p}(G_{m})\left(0<p\leq 1\right)
if and only if there exist
a sequence
\left(a_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of p-atoms and a sequence
\left(\mu_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right)
of a real numbers such that for
every
n\in\mathbb{N}

(6)

\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\mu_{k}S_{M_{n}}a_{k}=f^{\left(n\right)},

and

\qquad\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|^{p}<\infty.

Moreover,
\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}\backsim\inf\left(\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\left|\mu_{k}\right|^{p}\right)^{1/p},
where the infimum is taken over all decompositions of
f

of the form (
6
).

By using atomic characterization (see Proposition
1
) it can be easily proved that the following
statement holds (see e.g. Weisz
[
47
]

.

Suppose that an operator
T
is sub-linear and for some

\int\limits_{\overset{-}{I}}\left|Ta\right|^{p_{0}}d\mu\leq c_{p}<\infty

for every
p_{0}
-atom
a
, where
I
denotes the support of the atom. If
T
is bounded from
L_{p_{1}}
to
L_{p_{1}},

\left(1<p_{1}\leq\infty\right)
, then

\left\|Tf\right\|_{p_{0}}\leq c_{p_{0}}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p_{0}}(G_{m})}.

3.
Main Results

Our first main result reads:

Theorem 1
.

Let
0<p<1/2
and
f\in H_{p}(G_{m}).
Then there exists an absolute constant
c_{p},
depending only on
p,
such that the inequality

(8)

\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\frac{\log^{p}n\left\|R^{\psi}_{n}f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}^{p}}{n^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}\left\|f\right\|_{H_{p}(G_{m})}^{p}

holds, where
R^{\psi}_{n}f
denotes the
n
-th Reisz logarithmic mean with respect to the Vilenkin-Fourier series of
f.

For the proof of Theorem
1
we will use the following lemmas:

Lemma 1
.

(see
[
37
]
) Let
x\in I_{N}\left(x_{k}e_{k}+x_{l}e_{l}\right),

\ 1\leq x_{k}\leq m_{k}-1,

1\leq x_{l}\leq m_{l}-1,

k=0,...,N-2,

\ l=k+1,...,N-1.
Then

\int_{I_{N}}\left|K^{\psi}_{n}\left(x-t\right)\right|d\mu\left(t\right)\leq\frac{cM_{l}M_{k}}{nM_{N}},\qquad\text{when }n\geq M_{N}.

Let
x\in I_{N}\left(x_{k}e_{k}\right),\ \ 1\leq x_{k}\leq m_{k}-1,

k=0,...,N-1.
Then

\int_{I_{N}}\left|K^{\psi}_{n}\left(x-t\right)\right|d\mu\left(t\right)\leq\frac{cM_{k}}{M_{N}},\qquad\text{when }n\geq M_{N}.

Lemma 2
.

(see
[
38
]
)
Let
x\in I_{N}\left(x_{k}e_{k}+x_{l}e_{l}\right),

\ 1\leq x_{k}\leq m_{k}-1,

1\leq x_{l}\leq m_{l}-1,

\ k=0,...,N-2,

l=k+1,...,N-1.
Then

\int_{I_{N}}\text{ }\underset{j=M_{N}+1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{\left|K^{\psi}_{j}\left(x-t\right)\right|}{j+1}d\mu\left(t\right)\leq\frac{cM_{k}M_{l}}{M_{N}^{2}}.

Let
x\in I_{N}\left(x_{k}e_{k}\right),

1\leq x_{k}\leq m_{k}-1,

\ k=0,...,N-1.
Then

\int_{I_{N}}\underset{j=M_{N}+1}{\overset{n}{\sum}}\frac{\left|K^{\psi}_{j}\left(x-t\right)\right|}{j+1}d\mu\left(t\right)\leq\frac{cM_{k}}{M_{N}}l_{n}.

Proof.

By using Abel transformation, the kernels of the Riesz
logarithmic means can be rewritten as (see also
[
38
]
)

(9)

L^{\psi}_{n}=\frac{1}{l_{n}}\overset{n-1}{\underset{j=1}{\sum}}\frac{K^{\psi}_{j}}{j+1}+\frac{K^{\psi}_{n}}{l_{n}}.

Hence, according to (
5
) we get that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\int_{G_{m}}\left|L^{\alpha}_{n}\right|d\mu\leq c<\infty,\ \ \ \ \text{where}\ \ \ \ \alpha=w\text{ or }\psi

and it follows that
R^{\psi}_{n}
is bounded from
L_{\infty}
to
L_{\infty}.

By Proposition
2
, the proof of Theorem
1
will be complete, if we show that

(10)

\overset{\infty}{\underset{n=1}{\sum}}\frac{\log^{p}n\int\limits_{\overset{-}{I}}\left|R^{\psi}_{n}a\right|^{p}d\mu}{n^{2-2p}}\leq c_{p}<\infty,\ \ \text{for}\ \ 0<p<1/2,

for every
p
-atom
a,
where
I
denotes the support of the atom.

Let
a
be an arbitrary p-atom with support
I
and
\mu\left(I\right)=M_{N}^{-1}.
We may assume that
I=I_{N}.
It is easy to see that
R^{\psi}_{n}a=\sigma^{\psi}_{n}\left(a\right)=0,
when
n\leq M_{N}
.
Therefore we suppose that
n>M_{N}.

Since
\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\leq cM_{N}^{2}
if we apply (
9
), then we can conclude that

(11)

\displaystyle\left|R^{\psi}_{n}a\left(x\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{I_{N}}\left|a\left(t\right)\right|\left|L^{\psi}_{n}\left(x-t\right)\right|d\mu(t)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\|a\right\|_{\infty}\int_{I_{N}}\left|L^{\psi}_{n}\left(x-t\right)\right|d\mu\left(t\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{cM_{N}^{1/p}}{l_{n}}\underset{I_{N}}{\int}\text{ }\underset{j=M_{N}+1}{\overset{n-1}{\sum}}\frac{\left|K^{\psi}_{j}\left(x-t\right)\right|}{j+1}d\mu\left(t\right)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\overline{I_{N}}}\left|R^{\psi}_{n}a\left(x\right)\right|^{p}d\mu\left(x\right)

\displaystyle\overset{N-2}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}\overset{N-1}{\underset{l=k+1}{\sum}}\sum\limits_{x_{j}=0,j\in\{l+1,...,N-1}^{m_{j-1}}\int_{I_{N}^{k,l}}\left|R^{\psi}_{n}a\right|^{p}d\mu+\overset{N-1}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}\int_{I_{N}^{k,N}}\left|R^{\psi}_{n}a\right|^{p}d\mu

Our next main result shows in particular that the inequality in Theorem

is in a special sense sharp at least in the case of Walsh-Fourier series (c.f. also problem 2 in the next section).

Theorem 2
.

Let
0<p<1/2
and
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
be any non-decreasing function, satisfying the condition

(16)

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(n\right)=+\infty.

Then there exists a martingale
f\in H_{p}\left(G_{2}\right)
such that

(17)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{\log^{p}n\left\|R^{w}_{n}f\right\|_{p}^{p}\Phi\left(n\right)}{n^{2-2p}}=\infty,

where
R^{w}_{n}f
denotes the
n
-th Reisz logarithmic means with respect to Walsh-Fourier series of

f.

Proof.

It is evident that if we assume that
\Phi\left(n\right)\geq cn,
where
c
is some positive constant then

\frac{\log^{p}n\Phi\left(n\right)}{n^{2-2p}}\geq n^{1-2p}\log^{p}n\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{as}\ n\rightarrow\infty,

and also (
17
) holds. So, without lost the generality we may assume that there exists an increasing sequence of positive integers

\left\{\alpha^{\prime}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
such that

(18)

\Phi\left(\alpha^{\prime}_{k}\right)=o(\alpha^{\prime}_{k}),\ \text{as}\ k\rightarrow\infty.

Let
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}\subseteq\left\{\alpha^{\prime}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
be an increasing sequence of positive integers such that
\alpha_{0}\geq 2
and

(19)

\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\Phi^{1/2}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}<\infty,

(20)

\sum_{\eta=0}^{k-1}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}/p}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{\eta}})}\leq\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k-1}/p+1}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k-1}})},

(21)

\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k-1}/p+1}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k-1}})}\leq\frac{1}{128\alpha_{k}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-2)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}.

We note that under condition (
18
) we can conclude that

\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}/p}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{\eta}})}\geq{\left(\frac{2^{2\alpha_{\eta}}}{\Phi(2^{2\alpha_{\eta}})}\right)}^{1/2p}\rightarrow\infty,\ \text{as}\ \eta\rightarrow\infty

and it immediately follows that such an increasing sequence
\left\{\alpha_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\right\},
which satisfies conditions (
19
)-(
21
), can be constructed.

Let

f^{\left(A\right)}\left(x\right):=\sum_{\left\{k;\text{ }2\alpha_{k}<A\right\}}\lambda_{k}a_{k},

where

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}

and

From (

) and Lemma

we can conclude that

It is easy to show that

(22)

\widehat{f}^{w}(j)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})},\,\,\text{ if \thinspace\thinspace}j\in\left\{2^{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\},\text{ }k\in\mathbb{N},\\
0,\text{ \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace if \thinspace\thinspace\thinspace}j\notin\bigcup\limits_{k=1}^{\infty}\left\{2^{2\alpha_{k}},...,\text{ ~}2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1\right\}.\end{array}\right.

For
n=\sum_{i=1}^{s}2^{n_{i}},

n_{1}<n_{2}<...<n_{s}
we denote

\mathbb{A}_{0,2}:=\left\{n\in\mathbb{N}:\text{ }n=2^{0}+2^{2}+\sum_{i=3}^{s_{n}}2^{n_{i}}\right\}.

Let
2^{2\alpha_{k}}\leq j\leq 2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1
and

Then

(23)

\displaystyle R^{w}_{j}f

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{j}}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{S_{n}f}{n}+\frac{1}{l_{j}}\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}}}^{j}\frac{S_{n}f}{n}:=I+II.

Let
n<2^{2\alpha_{k}}.
Then from (

Consequently,

(24)

\displaystyle\left|I\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{j}}\underset{n=1}{\overset{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}{\sum}}\frac{\left|S^{w}_{n}f\left(x\right)\right|}{n}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{2^{2\alpha_{k}}}}\frac{1}{128\alpha_{k}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-2)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\sum_{n=1}^{2^{2\alpha_{k}}-1}\frac{1}{n}\leq\frac{1}{128\alpha_{k}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-2)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}.

Let

According to well-known equalities for Dirichlet kernels (for details see e.g.
[

]
and
[

]
):

(27)

D^{w}_{n}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}r_{k}D^{w}_{2^{k}}=w_{n}\overset{\infty}{\underset{k=0}{\sum}}n_{k}\left(D^{w}_{2^{k+1}}-D^{w}_{2^{k}}\right),\text{ for \ }n=\overset{\infty}{\underset{i=0}{\sum}}n_{i}2^{i},

number,}\\
0,&\text{if}\,\,n\ \ \text{is even number.}\end{array}\right.

Since

and if we apply (

II_{1}=0

\displaystyle II_{2}=\frac{1}{l_{j}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{(j-1)/2}\frac{w_{2n+1}}{2n+1}=\frac{1}{l_{j}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}r_{1}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{(j-1)/2}\frac{w_{2n}}{2n+1}.

Let
x\in I_{2}(e_{0}+e_{1}).
Then, by the definition of Walsh functions, we get that

w_{4n+2}=r_{1}w_{4n}=-w_{4n}

(29)

\displaystyle\left|II_{2}\right|=\frac{1}{l_{j}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\left|\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-1}}^{(j-1)/2}\frac{w_{2n}}{2n+1}\right|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{j}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\left|\frac{w_{j-1}}{j}+\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{(j-1)/4}\left(\frac{w_{4n-4}}{4n-3}+\frac{w_{4n-2}}{4n-1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\frac{1}{l_{j}}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\left|\frac{w_{j-1}}{j}+\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{(j-1)/4}\left(\frac{w_{4n-4}}{4n-3}-\frac{w_{4n-2}}{4n-1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\frac{c}{\log({2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}})}\frac{2^{2\alpha_{k}(1/p-1)}}{\Phi^{1/2p}(2^{2\alpha_{k}})}\left(\left|\frac{w_{j-1}}{j}\right|-\sum_{n=2^{2\alpha_{k}-2}+1}^{(j-1)/4}\left|{w_{4n-4}}\right|\left(\frac{1}{4n-3}-\frac{1}{4n-1}\right)\right)

2^{2\alpha_{k}}\leq j\leq 2^{2\alpha_{k}+1}-1,

\displaystyle\left|II_{2}\right|

Problem 1
.

For any
f\in{H_{1/2}},
is it possible to find strong convergence theorems for Reisz means
R^{w}_{m},
where
\alpha=w\text{ or }\alpha=\psi
?

Remark 1
.

[
2
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
42
]
(see also

]

]
). Our method and estimations of Reisz and Fejér kernels (see Lemmas 1 and 2) do not give possibility to prove even similar strong convergence result as for the case of Fejer means. In particular, for any

\frac{1}{\log n}\overset{n}{\underset{k=1}{\sum}}\frac{\left\|R^{\alpha}_{k}f\right\|_{1/2}^{1/2}}{k}\leq c\left\|f\right\|_{H_{1/2}}^{1/2},\ \text{where}\ \alpha=w\text{ or }\alpha=\psi?

for Vilenkin systems:

Problem 2
.

For
0<p<1/2
and any non-decreasing function
\Phi:\mathbb{N}\rightarrow[1,\infty)
satisfying the conditions

\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\lim}\Phi\left(n\right)=+\infty,

-th Reisz logarithmic means with respect to Vilenkin-Fourier series of

Problem 3
.

Is it possible to find a martingale
f\in{H_{1/2}},
such that

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left\|R^{\alpha}_{n}f\right\|_{1/2}=\infty,

where
\alpha=w\text{ or }\alpha=\psi
?

Remark 2

On the eternal non-Markovianity of non-unital quantum channels
Shrikant Utagi, Subhashish Banerjee, R. Srikanth

Abstract
The eternally non-Markovian Pauli channel is an example of a unital channel characterized by a negative decay rate for all time t > 0 t>0 . Here we consider the problem of constructing an analogous non-unital channel, and show in particular that a d d -dimensional generalized amplitude damping (GAD) channel cannot be eternally non-Markovian when the non-Markovianity originates solely from the non-unital part of the channel. We study specific ramifications of this result for qubit GAD. Specifically, we construct a quasi-eternally non-Markovian qubit GAD channel, characterized by a time t ∗ > 0 t^{\ast}>0 , such that the channel is non-Markovian only and for all time t > t ∗ t>t^{\ast} . We further point out that our negative result for the qudit GAD channel, namely the impossibility of the eternal non-Markovian property, does not hold for a general qubit or higher-dimensional non-unital channel.

Before embarking on our results, we present a notion of non-unital non-Markovianity as below.

Definition 1

When non-Markovianity originates solely from the non-unital part, i.e., from

Given this notion, we ask: is eternal non-unital non-Markovianity, as per the Definition (
1

However, we will later show that for a class of phase-covariant channels and higher dimensional non-unital channels, eternal non-Markovianity is still possible, nonetheless such a non-Markovianity originates from the unital part of the channel.

\displaystyle=\ket{l}\!\bra{l}+\sqrt{1-\lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\

We have the following result.

Theorem 1

A non-unital GAD channel in any finite dimension

that is eternally CP-indivisible in the non-unital part is impossible.

Proof:

In the Markovian case, any initial state is driven monotonically towards the fixed point
\mathfrak{F}
, fulfilling Eq. (
4

\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}

If this CP-divisibility condition is violated at time

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|[\mathbb{I}\otimes\mathcal{E}(t)](\rho_{1}-\rho_{2})\|_{t=0}>0.

With the idea of maximizing the l.h.s in Eq. (

\rho_{1}\equiv\mathbb{I}\otimes\rho_{S}

It can be shown that the action of the GAD channel Eq. (
12

\mathcal{E}(\rho_{S})[t]=\lambda(t)\mathfrak{F}+\{(1-\lambda(t))\mathfrak{F}+\sqrt{1-\lambda(t)}\mathfrak{G}\},

is a matrix consisting only of off-diagonal terms dependent on the initial state. In the Markovian case,

\|\mathcal{E}(\rho_{S})[t]-\mathfrak{F}\|=\sqrt{1-\lambda(t)}\|\mathfrak{G}\|.

Given that GAD constitutes a CP map,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\mathcal{E}({\rho}_{S})-\mathfrak{F}\|_{t=0}\leq 0,

contrary to the implication of Eq. (

\square

In the following, the above result for the general case of qudits will be elucidated in the case of qubits.

Non-unital channels and eternal non-Markovianity

The impossibility of ENMity of GAD provokes the question of whether this is generic for all qubit non-unital channels. Yet, a simple example suffices to show that this is not the case. Consider the non-unital channel generated by linearly combining the generators the above Pauli ENM channel and an amplitude damping (AD) channel. An example would be:

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}(\rho)

It could be asked whether this no-go result is specific to two-level systems. It is straightforward to show that these results hold in general to arbitrary finite dimensions, by taking a trivial extension of the qubit dynamics, by embedding the qubit in two dimensions and padding the additional dimensions with an identity operation.

V
Conclusions

The ENM Pauli channel
[
14
]
presented the rather counter-intuitive idea of a quantum noisy channel that is
recoherent
for all
t>0
. Here we address the question of the possibility of a non-unital channel that is ENM, and show that it is impossible for the well-known class of GAD channels of qubits.

We point out that this impossibility of eternal non-Markovianity does not hold for arbitrary non-unital channels. Specifically, as evident from examples discussed above, one can construct an ENM non-unital channel by a linear combination of the generators of an ENM unital channel and an arbitrary non-unital one. However, we conjecture that for a non-unital channel with no such unital component (in the above sense) to be ENM, is impossible.

A consequence of our above negative result is that the best that a qubit GAD channel can approach the ENM property is to be non-Markovian for all times above a non-zero threshold time
t^{\ast}
, i.e., strictly
t^{\ast}>0
. By way of illustration, we provided an example of a “quasi-ENM” GAD channel, which is non-Markovian for all time
t\geq t^{\ast}>0
.

Combinatorics on Number Walls and the p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -adic Littlewood Conjecture
Steven Robertson

Abstract
In 2004, de Mathan and Teulié stated the p p -adic Littlewood Conjecture ( p p -LC) in analogy with the classical Littlewood Conjecture. Let 𝕂 \mathbb{K} be a field and p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) be an irreducible polynomial with coefficients in 𝕂 \mathbb{K} . This paper deals with the analogue of p p -LC over the field of formal Laurent series over 𝕂 \mathbb{K} , known as the p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -adic Littlewood Conjecture ( p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC). Firstly, it is shown that any counterexample to p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC for the case p ⁡ ( t ) = t p(t)=t induces a counterexample to p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC when p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) is any irreducible polynomial. Since Adiceam, Nesharim and Lunnon (2021) disproved p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC when p ⁡ ( t ) = t p(t)=t and 𝕂 \mathbb{K} has characteristic three, one obtains a disproof of p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC over any such field in full generality (i.e for any choice of irreducible polynomial p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) ). The remainder of the paper is dedicated to proving two metric results on p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC with an additional monotonic growth function f f . The first - a Khintchine-type theorem for p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -adic multiplicative approximation - enables one to determine the measure of the set of counterexamples to p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC for any choice of f f . The second complements this by showing that the Hausdorff dimension of the same set is maximal when p ⁡ ( t ) = t p(t)=t in the critical case where f = log 2 f=\log^{2} . These results are in agreement with the corresponding theory of multiplicative Diophantine approximation over the reals. Beyond the originality of the results, the main novelty of the work comes from the methodology used. Classically, Diophantine approximation employs methods from either Number Theory or Ergodic Theory. This paper provides a third option - combinatorics. Specifically, an extensive combinatorial theory is developed relating p ⁡ ( t ) p(t) -LC to the properties of the so-called number wall of a sequence. This is an infinite array containing the determinant of every finite Toeplitz matrix generated by that sequence. In full generality, the paper creates a dictionary allowing one to transfer statements in Diophantine approximation in positive characteristic to combinatorics through the concept of a number wall, and conversely.

\displaystyle=\inf\penalty\ \left|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot t^{j}N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}t^{j}N_{j}(p(t))\right|.

(2.0.7)

Above, and throughout the rest of the proof, the infimum is taken over natural numbers
k\geq 0
and all polynomials
N_{0}(p(t)),\dots,N_{m-1}(p(t))\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[p(t)]
, ignoring the case where they are all zero. It is clear that
\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle
has no nonzero coefficient for
t^{-1},\dots,t^{-m+1}
, which implies that for any
0\leq j\leq m-1
,

\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot t^{j}N(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle=t^{j}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle.

Hence,

(\ref{inf1})=\inf\penalty\ \left|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}t^{j}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|\cdot\left|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}t^{j}N_{j}(p(t))\right|\cdotp

(2.0.8)

If the degree of
\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle
is
-nm
, then for any
0\leq j\leq m-1
the degree of
t^{j}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle
is
-nm+j\equiv j\mod m
. This implies that the degree of each of the terms in
\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}t^{j}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle
are all different and therefore the degree of the sum is equal to the greatest degree of its terms. The same is true for
\left|\sum^{m-1}_{j=0}t^{j}N_{j}(p(t))\right|
. As a consequence,

\displaystyle(\ref{inf3})

\displaystyle=\inf\penalty\ \max_{j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}\left|t^{j}\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|\max_{i\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}\left|t^{i}N_{i}(p(t))\right|

\displaystyle=\inf\penalty\ \max_{j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}q^{j}\left|\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|\max_{i\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}q^{i}\left|N_{i}(p(t))\right|.

(2.0.9)

Let
j^{\prime}
and
i^{\prime}
be the values of
j
and
i
, respectively, that attain the maxima in (
2.0.9
). Then,

\displaystyle\max_{j\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}q^{j}\left|\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{j}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|\penalty\ \max_{i\in\{0,\dots,m-1\}}q^{i}\left|N_{i}(p(t))\right|

\displaystyle\geq\min_{\penalty\ h\in\{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}\}}q^{h}\left|\left\langle\Theta(p(t))\cdot N_{h}(p(t))\cdot p(t)^{k}\right\rangle\right|q^{h}\left|N_{h}(p(t))\right|

\displaystyle\underset{(\ref{inf5})}{\geq}q^{-ml+2h}.

This provides a lower bound for the value of (
2.0.9
), namely the case where
h=0
. The fact that
\mathbb{F}_{q}[p(t)]\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
and the equation (

) show that this lower bound is attained, hence

\displaystyle(\ref{inf4})=q^{-lm}.

This completes the proof of Theorem
1.0.3
.

3.1
Fundamentals of Number Walls

This section serves as an introduction to number walls. Only the theorems crucial for this paper are mentioned, and the proofs can be found in the references. For a more comprehensive look at number walls, see

Definition 3.1.1
.

A matrix
(s_{i,j})
for

is called

(

(
s_{i,j}=s_{i+1,j-1}
, respectively) for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that this entry is defined.

Given a doubly infinite sequence
S:=(s_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{Z}}
, natural numbers
m
and
v

n

(m+1)\times(v+1)
Toeplitz matrix
T_{S}(n,m,v):=(s_{i-j+n})_{0\leq i\leq m,0\leq j\leq v}
and a Hankel matrix of the same size as
H_{S}(n,m,v):=(s_{i+j+n})_{0\leq i\leq m,0\leq j\leq v}

\displaystyle T_{S}(n,m,v):=\begin{pmatrix}s_{n}&s_{n+1}&\dots&s_{n+v}\\
s_{n-1}&s_{n}&\dots&s_{n+v-1}\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
s_{n-m}&s_{n-m+1}&\dots&s_{n-m+v}\end{pmatrix},\penalty\ H_{S}(n,m,v):=\begin{pmatrix}s_{n}&s_{n+1}&\dots&s_{n+v}\\
s_{n+1}&s_{n+2}&\dots&s_{n+v+1}\\
\vdots&&&\vdots\\
s_{n+m}&s_{n+m+1}&\dots&s_{n+m+v}\end{pmatrix}.

If
v=m
, these are shortened to
T_{S}(n,m)
and
H_{S}(n,m)
, respectively. The Laurent series
\Theta(t)=\sum^{\infty}_{i=1}s_{i}t^{-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}((t^{-1}))

By elementary row operations, it holds that.

\det(H_{S}(n,m))=(-1)^{\frac{m(m+1)}{2}}\det(T_{S}(n+m,m)).

(3.1.1)

Definition 3.1.2
.

Let

be a doubly infinite sequence over a finite field

In keeping with standard matrix notation,

Proof of Theorem

1\Rightarrow 2:
The proof is by contraposition. Let
N(t)=M(t)\cdot t^{n}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[t]
be a nonzero polynomial with

\displaystyle f(|N(t)|)\cdot|N(t)|\cdot|N(t)|_{t}\cdot|\langle\Theta(t)\cdot N(t)\rangle|

\displaystyle=f(q^{m+n})\cdot q^{m}\cdot|\langle\Theta(t)\cdot M(t)\cdot t^{n}\rangle|.

(3.2.1)

This quantity is strictly less than
q^{-l}
if and only if

|\langle\Theta(t)\cdot M(t)\cdot t^{n}\rangle|<q^{-l-m-b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)},

(3.2.2)

where
b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)=\lfloor\log_{q}(f(q^{m+n}))\rfloor
. This is equivalent to the coefficients of

in

These coefficients are given by

\sum_{j=0}^{m}a_{j}s_{i+j+n},

and hence the inequality (
3.2.2
) can be written as the matrix equation

H_{\Theta}(1+n,l+m+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)-1,m)\cdot\begin{pmatrix}a_{0}\\
\vdots\\
a_{m}\end{pmatrix}=\mathbf{0}.

(3.2.3)

This implies that
H_{\Theta}(n+1,l+m+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)-1,m)
has less than maximal rank, meaning the square matrix
H_{\Theta}(n+1,l+m+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)-1)
has zero determinant. In particular,
H_{\Theta}(n+1,i)
is singular for
m\leq i\leq l+m+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)-1
, since each of these matrices satisfies the same linear recurrence relation in the first
m+1

Relation (
3.1.1
) shows that the determinant of
H_{\Theta}(n+1,i)
is, up to a nonzero multiplicative constant, equal to the determinant of
T_{\Theta}(n+1+i,i)
. Therefore, there is a diagonal of zeros from entry
(n+m+1,m)
to entry
(n+m+l+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n),m+l+b_{f}^{(q)}(m+n)-1).
The Square Window Theorem (Theorem

.

2\Rightarrow 1:
Proceeding again by contraposition, assume that there is a window of size at least

. The diagonal of this window corresponds to a sequence of nested singular square Toeplitz matrices,

. Hence, the matrices

for

implies that the first

has less than maximal rank.

Let then

f(|N(t)|)\cdot|N(t)|\cdot|N(t)|_{t}\cdot|\langle\Theta(t)\cdot N(t)\cdot t^{n}\rangle|<q^{-l}.

Hence, a polynomial

is equivalent to there being a window of size

Definition 3.3.3

Given a finite number wall generated by a sequence of length

Lemma 3.3.4

(Free-Determined Lemma)
.

Let
W(S)=(W_{m,n}(S))_{n,m\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the finite number wall generated by the sequence
S=(s_{i})_{0\leq i\leq r}
. Assume that
s_{r+1}
is added to the end of
S
. Then, for
0\leq i\leq\left\lfloor\frac{r}{2}\right\rfloor
,
W_{i,r+1-i}(S)
is determined if and only if
W_{i-1,r-i}(S)=0
. Furthermore, if
W_{i,r+1-i}(S)
is not determined, then it is free. In particular, picking a value for any non-determined entry on the
(r+1)^{\textup{th}}
diagonal uniquely decides the value for every remaining non-determined entry on the
(r+1)^{\textup{th}}
diagonal.

Proof.

The value of
W_{i,r+1-i}(S)
is calculated from the definition of a number wall:

W_{i,r+1-i}(S)=\det(T_{S}(r+1-i,i))=\det\begin{pmatrix}s_{r+1-i}&\dots&s_{r+1}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
s_{r+1-2i}&\dots&s_{r+1-i}\end{pmatrix}.

Due to the Toeplitz structure,
s_{r+1}
only occurs in the top right corner of
T_{S}(r+1-i,i)
. By expanding along the top row,

W_{i,r+1-i}(S)=s_{r+1}\cdot\det(T_{S}(r-i,i-1))+Y,

where
Y
is a number that depends only on
\{s_{i}:1\leq i\leq r\}
. Hence, the value of
W_{i,r+1-i}(S)
is independent of
s_{r+1}
if and only if
\det(T_{S}(r-i,i-1))=W_{i-1,r-i}(S)=0
. If
W_{i-1,r-i}(S)\neq 0
, then for any chosen value of
W_{i,r+1-i}(S)
there exists a unique choice of
s_{r+1}
that achieves it. Namely,

s_{r+1}=(W_{i,r+1-i}(S)-Y)\cdot\det(T_{S}(r-i,i-1))^{-1}.

(3.3.1)

This, in turn, fixes the value of every non-determined entry on diagonal
r+1.

∎

W_{j,r+1-j}(S^{\prime\prime})

W_{j,r+1-j}(S^{\prime})=W_{j,r+1-j}(S^{\prime\prime})

Proof.

Clearly, if

∎

Remark 3.3.6
.

That
t
-LC (and hence
p(t)
-LC from Theorem
1.0.3
) are false over infinite fields
\mathbb{K}
(originally established by Mathan and Teulié in
[

3.3.4
. Indeed, if the sequence
(s_{i})_{1\leq i\leq r}
over
\mathbb{K}
has no windows in its number wall, every entry on the

diagonal is free. Now, Lemma
3.3.4
shows there are infinite choices for

that do not create any windows. A simple induction beginning with any nonzero sequence of length one generates a counterexample to
t
-LC.

Lemma
3.3.4
explains when a finite sequence can be extended by one digit to obtain a particular value in the number wall. In practice, finite sequences are not extended one digit at a time, but instead by arbitrarily many digits at once. The next subsection proves results about how many ways this can be done whilst preserving certain properties of the number wall.

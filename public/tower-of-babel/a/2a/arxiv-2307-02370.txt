A generalization of formal multiple zeta values related to multiple Eisenstein series and multiple q q -zeta values Thanks: The author was partially funded by the Deutsche Forschungsgemeinschaft (DFG, German Research Foundation) – SFB-TRR 358/1 2023 — 491392403.
Annika Burmester

Abstract
. We introduce the algebra 𝒢 f \mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}} , which is equipped with natural maps into the algebra of (combinatorial) multiple Eisenstein series and that of multiple q q -zeta values. Similar to Racinet’s approach to formal multiple zeta values, we consider an affine scheme BM \operatorname{BM} corresponding to the algebra 𝒢 f \mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}} . We show that Racinet’s affine scheme DM \operatorname{DM} represented by the algebra of formal multiple zeta values embeds into this affine scheme BM \operatorname{BM} . This leads to a projection from the algebra 𝒢 f \mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}} onto the algebra of formal multiple zeta values. Via the above natural maps, this projection corresponds to taking the constant terms of multiple Eisenstein series or the limit q → 1 q\rightarrow 1 of multiple q q -zeta values.

1.
Introduction

Multiple zeta values
are real numbers defined for integers
k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
by

\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}.

We refer to the number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}
as the
weight
and to the number
d
as the
depth
. Multiple zeta values were first introduced in depth two by C. Goldbach and L. Euler more than two centuries ago. In recent years these values were studied intensively due to their rich structure and their occurrence in various areas of mathematics, such as number theory, algebraic geometry, knot theory, quantum field theory, and also in mathematical physics. A survey on achievements in the theory of multiple zeta values can be found in
[
BGF
]
and
[
Zh20
]
, and all articles related to multiple zeta values are listed in
[
Hof
]
.

The product of multiple zeta values can be expressed in two different ways, one is called the
stuffle product
and comes from the definition of multiple zeta values as infinite nested sums, and the other one is called the
shuffle product
and comes from the representation of multiple zeta values as iterated integrals. Both products possess a description in terms of quasi-shuffle algebras (introduced in
[
Hof00
]
,
[
HI17
]
). Comparing these two product formulas together with some regularization process (given in
[
IKZ06
]
) yields the
extended double shuffle relations
among multiple zeta values. A main conjecture in the theory of multiple zeta values is that the extended double shuffle relations give all algebraic relations in the algebra

\mathcal{Z}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\mid d\geq 0,k_{1}\geq 2,k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\}.

This motivates to consider the algebra
\mathcal{Z}^{f}
of
formal multiple zeta values
spanned by the elements
\zeta^{f}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
,
k_{1}\geq 2,k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
, which exactly satisfy the extended double shuffle relations. By construction, we have a realization of
\mathcal{Z}^{f}
into
\mathcal{Z}
. In other words, there is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{f}

\displaystyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z},

\displaystyle\zeta^{f}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}),

which should be an isomorphism by the previously mentioned main conjecture.

In his thesis (
[
Rac00
]
), Racinet studied the algebraic structure of formal multiple zeta values by considering an affine scheme
\operatorname{DM}_{0}
represented by the quotient algebra
\mathcal{Z}^{f}/(\zeta^{f}(2))
. This means, for each commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
with unit we have natural bijections

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\operatorname{-alg}}\Big({\mathchoice{\raisebox{4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\mathcal{Z}^{f}\!}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.49501pt}{$\displaystyle{\!(\zeta^{f}(2))}$}}{\raisebox{4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{\mathcal{Z}^{f}\!}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-4.24501pt}{$\textstyle{\!(\zeta^{f}(2))}$}}{\raisebox{3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{\mathcal{Z}^{f}\!}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.24501pt}{$\scriptstyle{\!(\zeta^{f}(2))}$}}{\raisebox{2.4913pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\mathcal{Z}^{f}\!}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.4913pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\!(\zeta^{f}(2))}$}}},R\Big)\simeq\operatorname{DM}_{0}(R).

(1.1)

Then, he showed that
\operatorname{DM}_{0}
is actually an affine group scheme. This yields an isomorphism of algebras

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}^{f}\simeq\mathbb{Q}[\zeta^{f}(2)]\otimes_{\mathbb{Q}}\mathcal{U}(\mathfrak{dm}_{0})^{\vee},

where
\mathfrak{dm}_{0}
is the corresponding Lie algebra to
\operatorname{DM}_{0}
.
In particular,
\mathcal{Z}^{f}
is a free polynomial algebra. The Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{dm}_{0}
induces a coproduct on the quotient
\mathcal{Z}^{f}/(\zeta^{f}(2))
, which is usually referred to as Goncharov’s coproduct (
[
Gon05
]
). This coproduct equips
\mathcal{Z}^{f}/(\zeta^{f}(2))
with a weight-graded Hopf algebra structure. The purpose of this article is to advocate a generalization of (
1.1
).

A kind of generalization of multiple zeta values are
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values
, these are elements in
\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket
degenerating to multiple zeta values for a suitable limit
q\rightarrow 1
. To get nice algebraic structures, we have to restrict to a particular kind of
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values. An example for such
q
-analogs are the
Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values
first studied in
[
Schl01
]
,
[
Zu03
]
, and then in their extended version in
[
EMS16
]
. Those are defined for integers
s_{1}\geq 1,s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
as

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{l}>0}\frac{q^{n_{1}s_{1}}}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{q^{n_{l}s_{l}}}{(1-q^{n_{l}})^{s_{l}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

We call the number
s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}
the
weight
and the number
l-\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}
the
depth
. Denote

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})\mid l\geq 0,\ s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0\},

where
\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(\emptyset)=1
. Since the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values are defined as infinite nested sums, they also satisfy a stuffle product. For example, we have for
s_{1},s_{2}\geq 1

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1})\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{2})=\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},s_{2})+\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{2},s_{1})+\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1}+s_{2}).

(1.2)

In particular, the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is an algebra. The Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values are invariant under a certain involution
\tau
(see (
3.3
)), we have for
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0

\displaystyle\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(k_{1},\{0\}^{m_{1}},\ldots,k_{d},\{0\}^{m_{d}})=\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(m_{d}+1,\{0\}^{k_{d}-1},\ldots,m_{1}+1,\{0\}^{k_{1}-1}).

(1.3)

An alternative description of the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is given by generating series of polynomials evaluated at partitions. With this point of view, the involution
\tau
corresponds to the conjugation of partitions. Express any partition
\lambda
of some positive integer
|\lambda|=n
in its Stanley coordinates, i.e., we write
\lambda=((u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}))
with
u_{1}>\dots>u_{d}>0
and
n=u_{1}v_{1}+\dots+u_{d}v_{d}
. Denote the set of all these partitions of length
d
by
\mathcal{P}_{d}
. Then the elements

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda=((u_{1},\ldots,u_{d}),(v_{1},\ldots,v_{d}))\in\mathcal{P}_{d}}u_{1}^{m_{1}}v_{1}^{l_{1}}\cdots u_{d}^{m_{d}}v_{d}^{l_{d}}q^{|\lambda|},\qquad l_{1},m_{1},\ldots,l_{d},m_{d}\geq 0,

(1.4)

also span the space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, since these elements are essentially equal to the bi-brackets (studied in
[
Ba19
]
,
[
BK20
]
). The elements in (
1.4
) are evidently invariant under conjugation of partitions. The exact translation of this invariance into the spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
consisting of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values is (
1.3
).

Conjecture
.

All algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
are a consequence of the stuffle product formula (
1.2
) and the
\tau
-invariance (
1.3
) of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values.

This conjecture was first stated by Bachmann in the context of bi-brackets.

On easily verifies that the
\tau
-invariance in (
1.3
) is homogeneous in weight, but the stuffle product in (
1.2
) is not. Whenever there is an entry
s_{i}=0
in one of the factors, then the stuffle product produces terms of lower weight. In analogy to the multiple zeta values and to apply similar techniques as in Racinet’s approach to formal multiple zeta values, we are more interested in weight-homogeneous relations. Precisely, we want to replace the stuffle product of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values with the associated weight-graded product. This is our motivation to introduce the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
. In
[
Bu23-2
]
, a spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is constructed, which indeed satisfies the associated weight-graded product and the
\tau
-invariance of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values. The elements of this spanning set are called
balanced multiple
q
-zeta values
.

Another family of objects related to multiple zeta values are the
multiple Eisenstein series
introduced in
[
GKZ06
]
. For integers
k_{1}\geq 3,k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 2
those are given by

\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda_{1}\succ\cdots\succ\lambda_{d}\succ 0\\
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}z+\mathbb{Z}\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots\lambda_{d}^{k_{d}}}\qquad(z\in\mathbb{H}).

Here we set
m_{1}z+n_{1}\succ m_{2}z+n_{2}
if
m_{1}>m_{2}
or
(m_{1}=m_{2}\wedge n_{1}>n_{2})
. Every multiple Eisenstein series
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(z)
possesses a Fourier expansion, where the constant term is exactly the multiple zeta value
\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
. More precisely, the building blocks of the Fourier expansion are multiple zeta values and so-called
brackets
studied in
[
BK16
]
. The brackets are contained in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, thus with the identification
q=e^{2\pi iz}
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(z)\in\mathcal{Z}_{q}\otimes\mathcal{Z}[\pi i].

There exist stuffle and shuffle regularized multiple zeta values for all positive multi indices
(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, and an extension of the brackets, called
bi-brackets
(see
[
Ba19
]
). Those two extended families of objects allow to define stuffle and shuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series
\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(z)
for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
(see
[
Ba22
]
,
[
BT17
]
). The shuffle regularized version is related to Goncharov’s coproduct for multiple zeta values. Computer experiments by Bachmann and Tasaka (see
[
BT17
]
) suggest that the relations between multiple Eisenstein series are equal to the relations between brackets modulo lower weight.

In
[
BB22
]
a spanning set of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is constructed, which conjecturally satisfies exactly the relations of the bi-brackets modulo lower weight, those are called
combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series

G\binom{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}{m_{1},\ldots.m_{d}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket,\qquad k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0.

These elements are built from the bi-brackets and a rational solution to the extended double shuffle equations. In
[
BK20
]
, it is shown that the bi-brackets also form a spanning set for
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, and it is conjectured by Bachmann that the brackets are enough to span
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. The algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
introduced in the following, is a formalization of the expected relations between the combinatorial bi-multiple Eisenstein series, and hence also should describe the relations between multiple Eisenstein series.

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
and let
\ast_{b}
be the quasi-shuffle product on the non-commutative free algebra
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
recursively given by
\mathbf{1}\ast_{b}w=w\ast_{b}\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle b_{i}u\ast_{b}b_{j}v=b_{i}(u\ast_{b}b_{j}v)+b_{j}(b_{i}u\ast_{b}v)+\begin{cases}b_{i+j}(u\ast_{b}v),&\quad i,j\geq 1,\\
0&\quad\text{else},\end{cases}

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
. We refer to
\ast_{b}
as the
balanced quasi-shuffle product
.

Proposition

(Proposition
5.3
)
.

The tuple
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})
is a graded commutative Hopf algebra. The dual completed Hopf algebra is
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
, where
R
is some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra and the coproduct
\Delta_{b}
is on the generators given by

\Delta_{b}(b_{i})=\mathbf{1}\otimes b_{i}+b_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}b_{j}\otimes b_{i-j},\qquad i\geq 0.

On the subspace

\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}=\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\backslash b_{0}\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,

we define the involution
\tau
by

\displaystyle\tau(b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}}\cdots b_{k_{d}}b_{0}^{m_{d}})=b_{m_{d}+1}b_{0}^{k_{d}-1}\cdots b_{m_{1}+1}b_{0}^{k_{1}-1}

for all
k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1,\ m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\geq 0
.

Definition
.

We set

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}={\mathchoice{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\displaystyle{(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\displaystyle{\operatorname{Rel}_{\tau,0}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\textstyle{(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-3.47221pt}{$\textstyle{\operatorname{Rel}_{\tau,0}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptstyle{(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-2.43054pt}{$\scriptstyle{\operatorname{Rel}_{\tau,0}}$}}{\raisebox{3.75pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b})}$}\mkern-5.0mu\diagup\mkern-4.0mu\raisebox{-1.7361pt}{$\scriptscriptstyle{\operatorname{Rel}_{\tau,0}}$}}},

where
\operatorname{Rel}_{\tau,0}
is the ideal generated by the elements
b_{0}
and
\tau(w)-w
for each
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle^{0}
. We denote the class of an element
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
by
\operatorname{f}(w)
. A
realization
of
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
is a surjective algebra morphism
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow R
into some
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
.

Theorem

(Theorem
4.3
)
.

There is a realization of
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
into the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}

\displaystyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{q},

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(w)

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{reg}}(w).

The elements
\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{reg}}(w)
are the
regularized balanced multiple
q
-zeta values
. We expect the realization
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}_{q}
in Theorem
4.3
to be an isomorphism, this means all algebraic relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
come from the balanced quasi-shuffle product formula and the
\tau
-invariance of the balanced multiple q-zeta values.

Denote by
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f},0}
the subalgebra generated by the elements
\operatorname{f}(w)
, where
w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
is a word consisting of the letters
b_{i}
,
i\geq 1
. Then, a candidate for a realization into the algebra
\mathcal{E}
of multiple Eisenstein series is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f},0}

\displaystyle\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{E},

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}}\cdots b_{k_{d}})

\displaystyle\longmapsto\mathbb{G}_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}}(z).

Here the images are the stuffle regularized multiple Eisenstein series. According to the previously mentioned conjecture by Bachmann (see also Conjecture
3.14
), the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f},0}
should coincide with
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
.

Inspired by Racinet’s ideas for formal multiple zeta values (
[
Rac00
]
), we introduce an affine scheme
\operatorname{BM}:\mathbb{Q}\operatorname{-Alg}\rightarrow\operatorname{Sets}
having values in the complete Hopf algebra
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
(given in Proposition
5.3
), and show the following.

Theorem

(Theorem
6.2
)
.

The affine scheme
\operatorname{BM}
is represented by the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
, i.e., there are natural bijections

\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\operatorname{-alg}}(\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}},R)\simeq\operatorname{BM}(R)

for each commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
with unit.

The affine scheme
\operatorname{BM}
generalizes Racinet’s affine scheme
\operatorname{DM}
. Precisely, we have an injective morphism of affine schemes

\displaystyle\theta:\operatorname{DM}\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\operatorname{BM}.

Applying Yoneda’s Lemma to the morphism
\theta
gives the following main result.

Theorem

(Theorem
7.4
)
.

There is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\relbar\joinrel\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}.

In other words, there is a realization of the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
into the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values. The morphism
p
should be seen as a formal version of the limit
q\rightarrow 1
(as computed in
[
Bu23-2
, Proposition 11.5, Remark 11.6]
) or, equivalently, as a formal version of taking the constant term of multiple Eisenstein series.

The map
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
allows to recover the extended double shuffle relations in
\mathcal{Z}^{f}
from the relations satisfied in the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
. So we obtain a new point of view on the extended double shuffle relations, which might give a possibility to make some progress towards the main conjecture for multiple zeta values (Conjecture
2.2
).

On the other hand, Theorem
7.4
gives evidence that there should be an approach to the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
similar to the one given by Racinet for the algebra
\mathcal{Z}^{f}
of formal multiple zeta values (see Section
2
). In particular, this paper gives the first step towards the expectation that
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
is a free polynomial algebra and is equipped with a Hopf algebra structure, where the coproduct is a generalization of Goncharov’s coproduct.

Structure of the paper.

In Section
2
we shortly recall (formal) multiple zeta values and illustrate Racinet’s approach to them via the affine group scheme
\operatorname{DM}_{0}
and the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{dm}_{0}
. In Section
3
we describe the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
and its relation to
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values, and multiple Eisenstein series. Then in Section
4
we define the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
and explain the realization into
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
given by the balanced multiple
q
-zeta values. In particular, we interpret
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
as a formalization of
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
. In Section
5
, we introduce the balanced quasi-shuffle Hopf algebra and its completed dual, which will be necessary for defining the affine scheme
\operatorname{BM}
. Then in Section
6
, we give the affine scheme
\operatorname{BM}
, which is represented by the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
. Finally, in Section
7
we prove that we have an embedding
\operatorname{DM}\hookrightarrow\operatorname{BM}
. This leads to the surjective algebra morphism
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
.

2.
Racinet’s approach to multiple zeta values

We recall Racinet’s approach to the algebra of formal multiple zeta values via affine group schemes and Lie algebras. In particular, we associate an affine group scheme
\operatorname{DM}_{0}
to the algebra of formal multiple zeta values having values in a completed Hopf algebra. Then, we shortly illustrate the structural results for the algebra of formal multiple zeta values obtained from this approach. This will motivate our approach to the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
in the following sections. We start with a short basic introduction to the theory of multiple zeta values, for details we refer to
[
BGF
, Chapter 1]
.

Definition 2.1
.

To integers
k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
, associate the
multiple zeta value

\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{d}>0}\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{d}^{k_{d}}}\in\mathbb{R}.

The number
k_{1}+\dots+k_{d}
is called the
weight
and the number
d
is called the
depth
. Denote the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space spanned by all multiple zeta values by

\mathcal{Z}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})\mid d\geq 0,\ k_{1}\geq 2,\ k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1\},

where
\zeta(\emptyset)=1
.

There are two ways of expressing the product of multiple zeta values, both of them have a description in terms of quasi-shuffle algebras (
[
Hof00
]
,
[
HI17
]
).

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{Y}=\{y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},\ldots\}
. Let
\mathcal{Y}^{*}
be the set of all words with letters in
\mathcal{Y}
, and denote by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
the free non-commutative polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\mathcal{Y}
. We denote the empty word by
\mathbf{1}
. Define the
stuffle product

\ast
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
recursively by
\mathbf{1}\ast w=w\ast\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle y_{i}u\ast y_{j}v=y_{i}(u\ast y_{j}v)+y_{j}(y_{i}u\ast v)+y_{i+j}(u\ast v)

for all
u,v,w\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle
. Then there is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{Y}\rangle,\ast)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z},

(2.1)

\displaystyle y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{d}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}).

(2.2)

The elements
\zeta_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
are the
stuffle regularized multiple zeta values
, they are uniquely determined by
\zeta_{\ast}(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
for
k_{1}\geq 2
,
k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
and
\zeta_{\ast}(1)=0
.

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{X}=\{x_{0},x_{1}\}
. Denote by
\mathcal{X}^{*}
the set of all words with letters in
\mathcal{X}
, and let
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
be the free non-commutative polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\mathcal{X}
. The
shuffle product
on
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle
is recursively defined by
\mathbf{1}\shuffle w=w\shuffle\mathbf{1}=w
and

\displaystyle x_{i}u\shuffle x_{j}v=x_{i}(u\shuffle x_{j}v)+x_{j}(x_{i}u\shuffle v)

for all

. Using the iterated integral expression of the multiple zeta values, one obtains a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle,\shuffle)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z},

(2.3)

\displaystyle x_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{n}}

\displaystyle\longmapsto\zeta_{\shuffle}(x_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{n}}).

(2.4)

The elements
\zeta_{\shuffle}(x_{\varepsilon_{1}}\cdots x_{\varepsilon_{n}})
are the
shuffle regularized multiple zeta values
, they are uniquely determined by
\zeta_{\shuffle}(x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1})=\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})
for all
k_{1}\geq 2
,
k_{2},\ldots,k_{d}\geq 1
and
\zeta_{\shuffle}(x_{0})=\zeta_{\shuffle}(x_{1})=0
.

There is an explicit connection between the shuffle and stuffle regularized multiple zeta values (see
[
IKZ06
]
) given by

\displaystyle\sum_{w\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}}\zeta_{\ast}(w)w=\exp\left(\sum_{n\geq 2}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n}\zeta(n)y_{1}^{n}\right)\sum_{w\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}\zeta_{\shuffle}(w)\Pi_{\mathcal{Y}}(w).

(2.5)

Here the map

is the canonical projection sending each word ending with
x_{0}
to
0
and
x_{0}^{k_{1}-1}x_{1}\cdots x_{0}^{k_{d}-1}x_{1}
to
y_{k_{1}}\cdots y_{k_{d}}
. Comparing the stuffle product formula for the stuffle regularized multiple zeta values (
2.1
) and the shuffle product formula for the shuffle regularized multiple zeta values (
2.3
) by using (

) gives the
extended double shuffle relations
among multiple zeta values.

Conjecture 2.2
.

(
[
IKZ06

) All algebraic relations in the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values are a consequence of the extended double shuffle relations.

In particular, Conjecture

\mathcal{Z}
is graded by weight, since the stuffle and shuffle product are both homogeneous in weight. Conjecture

Definition 2.3

R

\mathbb{Q}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\shuffle}(x_{i})=x_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes x_{i},\qquad i\in\{0,1\}.

The duality pairing is

\displaystyle R\langle\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle\rangle\times\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{X}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow R,

\displaystyle(f,w)

Similarly, the dual completed, cocommutative Hopf algebra to

\displaystyle\Delta_{\ast}(y_{i})=y_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\mathbf{1}\otimes y_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}y_{j}\otimes y_{i-j},\qquad i\geq 1.

An element

if and only if

3.
The algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}

We present the algebra
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
, which combines
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values, (combinatorial) multiple Eisenstein series, and also generating series of certain functions on partitions. We illustrate the strong connections to the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values.

3.1.
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values.

A
q
-analog of some expression is a generalization involving the variable
q
, which returns the original expression for the limit
q\rightarrow 1
. For example, a
q
-analog of some positive integer
n
is given by

\displaystyle[n]_{q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=1+q+q^{2}+\dots+q^{n-1}.

We are interested in
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values.

Definition 3.1
.

(
[
Schl01
]
,
[
Zu03
]
,
[
EMS16
]
) For integers
s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0
, the
Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta value
is

\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{l}>0}\frac{q^{n_{1}s_{1}}}{(1-q^{n_{1}})^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{q^{n_{l}s_{l}}}{(1-q^{n_{l}})^{s_{l}}}\in\mathbb{Q}\llbracket q\rrbracket.

The number
s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}
is called its
weight
and the number
d
its
depth
.

For integers
s_{1}\geq 2,s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 1
, one easily verifies

\displaystyle\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}(1-q)^{s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}}\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})=\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}\sum_{n_{1}>\dots>n_{l}>0}\frac{q^{n_{1}s_{1}}}{[n_{1}]_{q}^{s_{1}}}\cdots\frac{q^{n_{l}s_{l}}}{[n_{l}]_{q}^{s_{l}}}=\zeta(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l}).

So the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values are (sometimes called modified)
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values. More generally, in
[
BI22
]
it is shown that the regularized limit of all Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values lies in
\mathcal{Z}
.

Definition 3.2
.

We denote

\mathcal{Z}_{q}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}}\{\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(s_{1},\ldots,s_{l})\mid l\geq 0,\ s_{1}\geq 1,\ s_{2},\ldots,s_{l}\geq 0\},

where
\zeta_{q}^{\operatorname{SZ}}(\emptyset)=1
.

The space
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
is an algebra. To describe the product of the Schlesinger-Zudilin multiple
q
-zeta values, one usually uses quasi-shuffle products.

Consider the alphabet
\mathcal{B}=\{b_{0},b_{1},b_{2},\ldots\}
. Let
\mathcal{B}^{*}
the set of all words with letters in
\mathcal{B}
, and denote by
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
the free non-commutative polynomial algebra over
\mathbb{Q}
generated by
\mathcal{B}
. We define the
weight
and

of a word in
\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
as

\displaystyle\operatorname{wt}(b_{s_{1}}\cdots b_{s_{l}})=s_{1}+\dots+s_{l}+\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\},\qquad\operatorname{dep}(b_{s_{1}}\cdots b_{s_{l}})=l-\#\{i\mid s_{i}=0\}.

The SZ-stuffle product on

\mathbf{1}\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}w=w\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}\mathbf{1}=w

\displaystyle b_{i}u\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}b_{j}v=b_{i}(u\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}b_{j}v)+b_{j}(b_{i}u\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}v)+b_{i+j}(u\ast_{\operatorname{SZ}}v)

Remark 3.4

Any polynomial

3.3.
Multiple Eisenstein series.

Let
\mathbb{H}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\mid\operatorname{Im}(z)>0\}
be the upper half plane. For each

are given (up to some normalization) by

\displaystyle\mathbb{G}_{k}(z)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>0\\
\vee(m=0\wedge n>0)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{(mz+n)^{k}}=\zeta(k)+\frac{(-2\pi i)^{k}}{(k-1)!}\sum_{m,d>0}d^{k-1}q^{md}\qquad(q=e^{2\pi iz},\ z\in\mathbb{H}).

To obtain a multiple version of the Eisenstein series, define an order on the lattice

(

One observes immediately that multiple Eisenstein series are holomorphic functions on the upper half plane

(3.9)

Proposition 3.10

(

A very natural question is how to determine the algebraic relations between multiple Eisenstein series. Since the multiple Eisenstein series satisfy the stuffle product formula and in their Fourier expansion the multiple zeta values occur, one could expect that they also satisfy a variant or subset of the extended double shuffle relations.

3.4.
Combinatorial multiple Eisenstein series.

To attack the above question, we will restrict to
q
-series with rational coefficients. Precisely, this means that we take a rational solution to the extended double shuffle equations
\beta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{d})

\displaystyle\beta(k)=\frac{\zeta(k)}{(2\pi i)^{k}}=-\frac{B_{k}}{2k!},\quad k\text{ even},\qquad\qquad\beta(k)=0,\quad k\text{ odd}.

Moreover, we will use the
q

given in (

.

(
[
BB22
]

Example 3.13

\Delta_{b}:R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle\rightarrow R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle\otimes R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle
by

\displaystyle\Delta_{b}(b_{i})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}\otimes b_{i}+b_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}b_{j}\otimes b_{i-j},\qquad i\geq 0,

and extend this with respect to the concatenation product.

Proposition 5.3
.

The tuple
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
is a complete, cocommutative Hopf algebra. The pairing

\displaystyle R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle\otimes\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow R,

\displaystyle\Phi\otimes w

\displaystyle\longmapsto(\Phi\mid w),

where
(\Phi\mid w)
denotes the coefficient of
w
in
\Phi
, gives a duality between the weight-graded Hopf algebra
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})
and the complete Hopf algebra
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
.

Proof.

We prove the duality of
(\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle,\ast_{b},\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}})
and
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
with respect to the given pairing. Then it is an immediate consequence that
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
is a cocommutative Hopf algebra. It is well-known that
\Delta_{\operatorname{dec}}
and
\operatorname{conc}
are dual maps. For
u,v\in\mathbb{Q}\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle
one obtains

\displaystyle\big(\Delta_{b}(b_{i})\mid u\otimes v\big)

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbf{1}\otimes b_{i}+b_{i}\otimes\mathbf{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}b_{i}\otimes b_{i-j}\ \Big|\ u\otimes v\Big)=\big(b_{i}\mid u\ast_{b}v\big)

The last equality holds, since the word
b_{i}
appears in the product
u\ast_{b}v
if and only if
u=\mathbf{1},\ v=b_{i}
or
u=b_{i},\ v=\mathbf{1}
or
u=b_{j},\ v=b_{i-j}
for some
j=1,\ldots,i-1
.
Since
\Delta_{b}
is compatible with the concatenation product by definition and the letters
b_{i}
generate the algebra
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc}
), we deduce that the maps
\ast_{b}
and
\Delta_{b}
are dual.
∎

The antipode
S:R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle\rightarrow R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle
of the Hopf algebra
(R\langle\langle\mathcal{B}\rangle\rangle,\operatorname{conc},\Delta_{b})
is the anti-automorphism given by

\displaystyle S(b_{0})

\displaystyle=-b_{0},

\displaystyle S(b_{a})

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=1}^{a}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1}+\dots+j_{r}=a\\
j_{1},\ldots,j_{r}\geq 1\end{subarray}}(-1)^{r}b_{j_{1}}\cdots b_{j_{r}},\qquad a\geq 1.

Relation of the affine schemes

Remark 7.3
.

(i) Since the set
\operatorname{DM}(R)
is non-empty for any commutative
\mathbb{Q}
-algebra
R
with unit, the existence of the injective map in Theorem
7.2
shows that
\operatorname{BM}(R)
is non-empty.

(ii) By applying the defining conditions of
\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(R)
, one obtains that each
\phi\in\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(R)
satisfies

(\phi\mid x_{0}^{3}x_{1})=(\phi_{\ast}\mid y_{4})=\frac{2}{5}\lambda^{2},\qquad(\phi\mid x_{0}^{5}x_{1})=(\phi_{\ast}\mid y_{6})=\frac{8}{35}\lambda^{3}.

These numbers come from Euler’s formula for the even zeta values, precisely one computes
\frac{\zeta(4)}{\zeta(2)^{2}}=\frac{2}{5}
and
\frac{\zeta(6)}{\zeta(2)^{3}}=\frac{8}{35}
. Therefore, the map
\theta
in Theorem
7.2
restricts to an embedding

\theta:\operatorname{DM}_{\lambda}(R)\lhook\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\operatorname{BM}_{\big(\lambda,\frac{2}{5}\lambda^{2},\frac{8}{35}\lambda^{3}\big)}(R).

Since
\theta:\operatorname{DM}\rightarrow\operatorname{BM}
is an injective natural transformation of affine schemes, we obtain by applying Yoneda’s Lemma a surjective morphism between the representing algebras.

Theorem 7.4
.

There is a surjective algebra morphism

\displaystyle p:\hskip 11.38092pt\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\hskip 36.98866pt\mathcal{Z}^{f},

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(w)\

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=uv\\
u\in\{b_{0},b_{1}\}^{*},\ v\in\{b_{i}\mid i\geq 1\}^{*}\end{subarray}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}\left((\theta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\operatorname{anti}})^{-1}(u)\right)\zeta^{f}_{\ast}\left(\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(v)\right)\qquad(w\in\mathcal{B}^{*}).

Here
\zeta^{f}_{\ast}(u)
denotes the stuffle regularized formal multiple zeta values and
\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(v)
denotes the shuffle regularized formal multiple zeta values.

In other words, Theorem
7.4
shows that there is a realization of the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
into the algebra
\mathcal{Z}
of multiple zeta values, given by the composition of the maps
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{f}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z},\ \zeta_{\shuffle}^{f}(w)\mapstochar\rightarrow\zeta_{\shuffle}(w)
.

Proof.

The element
\operatorname{id}_{\mathcal{Z}^{f}}\in\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb{Q}\operatorname{-alg}}(\mathcal{Z}^{f},\mathcal{Z}^{f})
corresponds to the element
\sum\limits_{w\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(w)w
in
\operatorname{DM}(\mathcal{Z}^{f})
under the bijection given in (
2.6
). We obtain

\displaystyle\theta\Bigg(\sum_{w\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(w)w\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\theta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\operatorname{anti}}\Bigg(\sum_{u\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(u)u\Bigg)\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}\Bigg(\sum_{v\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\ast}(v)v\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathcal{X}^{*},\ v\in\mathcal{Y}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(u)\zeta^{f}_{\ast}(v)\theta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\operatorname{anti}}(u)\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}(v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\{b_{0},b_{1}\}^{*},\ v\in\{b_{i}\mid i\geq 1\}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}\Big((\theta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\operatorname{anti}})^{-1}(u)\Big)\zeta^{f}_{\ast}\Big(\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(v)\Big)uv.

So under the bijection given in Theorem
6.2
the element
\theta\Big(\sum\limits_{w\in\mathcal{X}^{*}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}(w)w\Big)\in\operatorname{BM}(\mathcal{Z}^{f})
corresponds to the algebra morphism

\displaystyle p:\hskip 11.38092pt\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\hskip 34.14322pt\mathcal{Z}^{f},

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(w)\

\displaystyle\longmapsto\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}w=uv\\
u\in\{b_{0},b_{1}\}^{*},\ v\in\{b_{i}\mid i\geq 1\}^{*}\end{subarray}}\zeta^{f}_{\shuffle}\left((\theta_{\mathcal{X}}^{\operatorname{anti}})^{-1}(u)\right)\zeta^{f}_{\ast}\left(\theta_{\mathcal{Y}}^{-1}(v)\right)\qquad(w\in\mathcal{B}^{*}).

By Yoneda’s Lemma, this is exactly the algebra morphism induced by the natural transformation
\theta:\operatorname{DM}\rightarrow\operatorname{BM}
of affine schemes.
∎

The map
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
can be seen as a formal limit
q\rightarrow 1
. For example, one computes

p(\operatorname{f}(b_{2}b_{3}))=\zeta_{\shuffle}^{f}(\mathbf{1})\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(y_{2}y_{3})=\zeta^{f}(2,3),

and similarly

\lim_{q\rightarrow 1}(1-q)^{5}\zeta_{q}(2,3)=\zeta(2,3).

In
[
BI22
, Theorem 4.18]
it is proven in a slightly different context that this holds in general. Interpreting the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
as a formalization of multiple Eisenstein series (see Section
3
), the map
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
can also be seen as a formal version of taking the constant term.

The algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
and (extended) double shuffle relations.

We indicate how to obtain a variant of the double shuffle relations in the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
, which reduce to the double shuffle relations under the surjective algebra morphism
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
.

On the one hand, we can multiply the elements
\operatorname{f}(w)
in the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
with respect to the balanced quasi-shuffle product
\ast_{b}
. On the other hand, we can first apply the
\tau
-invariance to both factors, then multiply with respect to the balanced quasi-shuffle product, and finally again apply the
\tau
-invariance to all terms.

Example 7.5
.

The previous described procedure yields for all
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 1,\ m_{1},m_{2}\geq 0

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}})\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{2}}b_{0}^{m_{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m_{1}+m_{2}}\left(\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-j}{m_{2}}\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{j}b_{k_{2}}b_{0}^{m_{1}+m_{2}-j})+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}-j}{m_{1}}\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{2}}b_{0}^{j}b_{k_{1}}b_{0}^{m_{1}+m_{2}-j})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\binom{m_{1}+m_{2}}{m_{1}}\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}+k_{2}}b_{0}^{m_{1}+m_{2}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}\operatorname{f}(b_{j}b_{0}^{m_{1}}b_{k_{1}+k_{2}-j}b_{0}^{m_{2}})+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\operatorname{f}(b_{j}b_{0}^{m_{2}}b_{k_{1}+k_{2}-j}b_{0}^{m_{1}})\right)

\displaystyle\hskip 19.91684pt+\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{k_{1}-1}\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}b_{0}^{m_{1}+m_{2}+1}).

In particular, we get in the case
m_{1}=m_{2}=0
that

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}})\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{2}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}}b_{k_{2}})+\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{2}}b_{k_{1}})+\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}+k_{2}})

(7.1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\right)\operatorname{f}(b_{j}b_{k_{1}+k_{2}-j})+\binom{k_{1}+k_{2}-2}{k_{1}-1}\operatorname{f}(b_{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}b_{0}).

(7.2)

Applying the algebra morphism
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
from Theorem
7.4
yields for
k_{1},k_{2}\geq 2

\displaystyle\zeta^{f}(k_{1})\zeta^{f}(k_{2})

\displaystyle=\zeta^{f}(k_{1},k_{2})+\zeta^{f}(k_{2},k_{1})+\zeta^{f}(k_{1}+k_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=2}^{k_{1}+k_{2}-1}\left(\binom{j-1}{k_{1}-1}+\binom{j-1}{k_{2}-1}\right)\zeta^{f}(j,k_{1}+k_{2}-j).

So we recover the double shuffle relations in depth
2
. We also obtain relations between formal multiple zeta values coming from regularization. For example, (
7.1
) in the case
k_{1}=1,\ k_{2}=2
reads

\displaystyle\operatorname{f}(b_{1})\operatorname{f}(b_{2})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{f}(b_{1}b_{2})+\operatorname{f}(b_{2}b_{1})+\operatorname{f}(b_{3})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{f}(b_{1}b_{2})+2\operatorname{f}(b_{2}b_{1})+\operatorname{f}(b_{2}b_{0}),

and applying the surjection
p:\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}\rightarrow\mathcal{Z}^{f}
gives

\displaystyle\Big(\zeta_{\shuffle}^{f}(1)+\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(1)\Big)\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(2)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(1,2)+\zeta_{\shuffle}^{f}(1)\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(2)+\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(2,1)+\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(3)

\displaystyle=\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(1,2)+\zeta_{\shuffle}^{f}(1)\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(2)+2\zeta_{\ast}^{f}(2,1)+0.

Then cancellation yields Euler’s famous relation
\zeta^{f}(3)=\zeta^{f}(2,1)
.

There are several examples in the literature of how to deduce relations between multiple zeta values from the relations in
\mathcal{Z}_{q}
(or more generally, from relations between
q
-analogs of multiple zeta values), see for example
[
BK16
, Section 7.6]
,
[
Bra05
]
, or
[
Si15
, Section 3.4]
. The approach given here allows a very algebraic view on the extended double shuffle relations of multiple zeta values.

Outlook.

By linearizing the defining equations of
\operatorname{BM}_{0}
one obtains a vector space
\mathfrak{bm}_{0}
. Since we expect
\operatorname{BM}_{0}
to be an affine group scheme, the linearized space
\mathfrak{bm}_{0}
should be equipped with a Lie algebra structure. In
[
Bu23
]
, the
q
-Ihara bracket is introduced, which is experimentally shown to preserve the space
\mathfrak{bm}_{0}
in small weights. In particular, the
q
-Ihara bracket should give rise to the group multiplication on
\operatorname{BM}_{0}
and an exponential map
\exp:\widehat{\mathfrak{bm}_{0}}\rightarrow\operatorname{BM}_{0}
. Consequences of this would be that the algebra
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}
is a free polynomial algebra and that the quotient
\mathcal{G}^{\operatorname{f}}/(\operatorname{f}(b_{2}),\operatorname{f}(b_{4}),\operatorname{f}(b_{6}))
is a Hopf algebra, where the coproduct should be seen as a generalization of Goncharov’s coproduct. This is illustrated in more detail in
[
Bu23
]
and will be part of forthcoming work.
